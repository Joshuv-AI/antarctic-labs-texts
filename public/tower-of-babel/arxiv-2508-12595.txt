Abstract
. We consider nonhomogeneous fractional p p -Laplace equations defined on a bounded nonsmooth domain which goes beyond the Lipschitz category. Under a sufficient flatness assumption on the domain in the sense of Reifenberg, we establish several fine boundary regularity results for solutions, and their gradient, near the boundary. To the best of our knowledge, each of our results is new even in the linear case.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35R09, 35B65, 35J60

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with nonlocal problems of the type

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{2}$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{2}\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with possibly nonsmooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
is the
s
-fractional
p
-Laplace operator defined by

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)\coloneqq p.v.\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

with
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. In this paper, we are interested in boundary regularity for solutions, and their gradient, under a considerably mild regularity assumption on the domain
\Omega
.

Boundary regularity theory for partial differential equations presents nontrivial and challenging issues, particularly when the underlying domain lacks a graph structure and classical techniques such as flattening or reflection are no longer applicable. A central question in this direction is whether boundary regularity results can still be achieved in the absence of smoothness, or even Lipschitz continuity, of the domain.
In this respect, it was shown in
[
BW04
,
BR13
]
that sharp global gradient estimates for local elliptic problems remain valid under the Reifenberg flatness assumption (see Definition
1.1
).
The notion of Reifenberg flatness, which was introduced by Reifenberg
[
Rei60
]
in his study of Plateau-type problems, is characterized by a scaling-invariant flatness that accommodates substantial boundary roughness. This geometric framework enables one to recover key analytic tools necessary for establishing boundary regularity, see
[
Tor97
]
for a well-written summary.
We also refer to
[
KT99
,
LN12
]
and references therein for more on Reifenberg flatness in the context of harmonic measures and free boundary regularity results for local elliptic equations.

We now turn to regularity theory for nonlocal problems.
Despite its nonlocal nature, the fractional
p
-Laplacian shares key variational and structural features with its local counterpart and has attracted significant attention in recent years. Interior regularity for such nonlocal problems have been systematically studied. For instance, without being exhaustive, basic regularity results for problems with measurable kernels are well established in
[
Coz17
,
DKP16
,
DKP14
,
Kas09
,
KKP16
,
KLL23
,
ABES19
,
KMS15a
,
KMS15
,
NOS24
,
Sch16
]
and references therein. For higher regularity results under better regularity assumptions on the data, see also
[
BT25
,
BDLMS25
,
BL17
,
BLS18
,
BK25
,
DKLN25
,
DKLN24
,
DN25
,
GL24
,
Fal20
,
KNS22
,
BDLM25
,
BDLMS24
,
MSY21
,
CS11
]
.

However, on the contrary to interior ones, boundary regularity issues for nonlocal problems exhibit several peculiarities, even in the case of linear equations in sufficiently regular domains; see
[
FR24
]
for an overview.
For instance, while any solution
u
to (
1.1
) with
p=2
and
f=\mathrm{const.}
is locally
C^{\infty}
-regular, one cannot expect more than boundary
C^{s}
-regularity for
u
due to explicit examples.
Such a phenomenon suggests that boundary regularity for nonlocal problems needs substantially different approaches compared to the case of corresponding local problems, in which the interior and boundary regularity often coincide via the reflection argument.

Specifically, in view of higher regularity near the boundary, it was observed in
[
Gru15
,
RS14
,
RS17
]
that the function
u/d^{s}
, where
d
denotes the distance function to
\partial\Omega
(see (
1.4
)), enjoys better boundary regularity than
u
itself.
Heuristically, the limit of
u/d^{s}
as
s\nearrow 1
can be considered as the normal derivative of
u
at a boundary point.
There are several recent results for linear nonlocal problems in this direction, concerned with various regularity assumptions on the domain and the kernel.
We refer to
[
Pra25a
,
Pra25
,
BN23
]
for higher Hölder and Sobolev regularity results for the solution itself, and
[
KW24
,
RW24
,
Gru24
,
AG23
,
BKK25
]
for higher regularity results for
u/d^{s}
.
Note in particular that in
[
Pra25a
,
Pra25
]
, global
C^{s-\varepsilon}
-regularity was proved for linear nonlocal problems in Reifenberg flat domains.
We also refer to
[
CKR23
,
DR24
,
AFLY23
]
for other kinds of global Calderón-Zygmund type estimates.

Up to now, there are few boundary regularity results for fractional
p
-Laplacian type problems in the literature.
In
[
IMS16
]
, global
C^{\alpha}
-regularity for some
\alpha\in(0,s]
of weak solutions
u
was obtained via comparison principles and barrier arguments.
Such results, combined with the interior higher regularity results in
[
BLS18
,
GL24
]
, in turn lead to global
C^{s}
-regularity of
u
.
Later in
[
IM24
,
IMS20
]
, global Hölder regularity for
u/d^{s}
was obtained. We remark that all the papers
[
IMS16
,
IMS20
,
IM24
]
considered
C^{1,1}
-domains.
We also refer to
[
BLN24
]
for boundary higher differentiability results for solutions in Lipschitz domains.
However, to our knowledge, there is no boundary regularity result for nonlocal problems with
p
-growth in nonsmooth domains.

The aim of this paper is to develop a boundary regularity theory for nonlinear nonlocal problems of the type (
1.1
) in nonsmooth domains. Specifically, we establish several fine regularity results near the boundary, provided the domain is sufficiently flat.
This opens new paths for studying nonlocal problems in rough domains, and the methods develop in this paper may extend to more general nonlocal operators with nonstandard growth.

1.1.
Assumptions and main results

We first recall the notion of
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flatness.
We refer to the next section for more on basic properties of Reifenberg flat domains.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and
R_{0}>0
be fixed. We say that
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat if for any
z\in\partial\Omega
and
r\leq R_{0}
, there is a coordinate system
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
, which may depend on
r
and
z
, such that
z=(z^{\prime},0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
in this coordinate system and

B_{r}(z^{\prime},0)\cap\{y_{n}>\delta r\}\subset B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega\subset B_{r}(z^{\prime},0)\cap\{y_{n}>-\delta r\}.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

Note that any Lipschitz domain with a small Lipschitz constant is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat for some sufficiently small constants
\delta
and
R_{0}
(see
[
Tor97
]
).

(ii)

A notable consequence of Reifenberg flatness is the following measure density condition (see for instance
[
BW04
, (2.12) in Lemma 2.7]
): if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
\delta<1/2
and
R_{0}>0
, then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\rho<R_{0}}\sup_{z\in\Omega}\frac{|B_{\rho}|}{|B_{\rho}(z)\cap\Omega|}\leq 4^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{0<\rho<R_{0}}\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega}\frac{|B_{\rho}(z)\setminus\Omega|}{|B_{\rho}|}\geq 4^{-n}.

Therefore, we always consider a sufficiently flat domain
\Omega
to ensure that it satisfies the measure density condition (
1.2
).

Before we introduce a notion of weak solution to (
1.1
), we recall function spaces; see for instance
[
BL17
,
DKP16
]
for more details.
We consider the following fractional space

\displaystyle X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,:\,u\equiv 0\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We also consider the tail space

\displaystyle L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\coloneqq\left\{u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,dy<\infty\right\}.

Accordingly, for any
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and any ball
B_{r}(z)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we denote the nonlocal tail of
u
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{r}(z))\coloneqq\left(r^{sp}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(z)}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y-z|^{n+sp}}\,dy\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

We are now ready to state a definition of weak solution to (
1.1
).

Definition 1.3
.

We say that
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{p_{*}}(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))
with

(1.3)

p_{*}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{np}{np-n+sp}&\text{if}\;\;sp<n,\\
&\text{any number in }(1,\infty)&\text{if}\;\;sp\geq n,\end{aligned}\right.

if it satisfies

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}{\,dx\,dy}=\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0})}f\varphi\,dx

for any
\varphi\in X^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega\cap B_{R}(x_{0}))
.

We now introduce our main results.
The first result is concerned with integrability estimates for the function
u/d^{s}
, where the function
d
is defined by

(1.4)

\displaystyle d(x)\equiv d_{\Omega}(x)\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega).

We also consider the following functional

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(u;B_{r}(z))\coloneqq\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega}|u|^{p-1}\,dx\right)^{1/(p-1)}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{r}(z)).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{2})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
)
with
f\in L^{p_{*}}(\Omega\cap B_{2})
. For any
q\in[p_{*},n/s)
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta=\delta(n,s,p,q)>0
such that if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
R_{0}>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\|u/d^{s}\|_{L^{\frac{nq(p-1)}{n-sq}}(\Omega\cap B_{1})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{2})}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,q,R_{0})
.

Remark 1.5
.

(i)

When
p=2
and
\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
, the same estimate is proved in
[
BKK25
, Theorem 1.4]
(see also
[
BKK25
, Remark 1.6]
for similar results in this direction). Moreover, the sharpness of Theorem
1.4
is shown in
[
BKK25
, Appendix A]
.

(ii)

Theorem
1.4
extends the previous result in
[
BKK25
]
to degenerate/singular nonlocal problems in nonsmooth domains. To our knowledge, such a result is new even for Lipschitz domains.

We next present a boundary higher Sobolev regularity result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{2})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
),
where
f\in L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{2})
with
q>n/s
. Let us fix
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
with

s+\frac{1}{\gamma}<\min\left\{1,\left(sp-\frac{n}{q}\right)\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}.

For any
\sigma<s+1/\gamma
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta=\delta(n,s,p,q,\gamma,\sigma)>0
such that if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
R_{0}>0
, then we have

\|u\|_{W^{\sigma,\gamma}(B_{1})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{2})}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,R_{0},q,\gamma,\sigma)
.

Remark 1.7
.

(i)

The result in Theorem
1.6
is sharp in the sense that we cannot take
\sigma=s+1/\gamma
.
Indeed, we can see that the function
v(x)=(x_{n})_{+}^{s}
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) with
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and
f=0
, which satisfies
v\in W^{s+1/\gamma-\varepsilon,\gamma}(B_{1/2})

for any
\varepsilon>0
, but
v\notin W^{s+1/\gamma,\gamma}(B_{1/2})
.

(ii)

We would like to point out that in
[
BLN24
,
BN23
]
, Besov-type regularity estimates for (
1.1
), such as
u\in B^{s+1/\max\{p,2\}}_{p,\infty}(\Omega)
, are obtained when
\Omega
is Lipschitz and
f
belongs to a suitable Besov space. Theorem
1.6
improves such results by obtaining the differentiability order arbitrarily close to
s+1/\gamma
, which is bigger than
s+1/p
provided
s<(p-1)/p
, when
\Omega
is Reifenberg flat and
f
is sufficiently regular.

(iii)

We also note that Theorem
1.6
does not follow from the results in
[
BLN24
,
BN23
]
, as a
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat domain need not be Lipschitz, even if
\delta
is sufficiently small (see
[
Tor97
]
).

In particular, we also have a sharp boundary Hölder regularity result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{2})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
),
where
f\in L^{n/s}(\Omega\cap B_{2})
. For any
\alpha\in(0,s)
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta=\delta(n,s,p,\alpha)>0
such that if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
R_{0}>0
, then we have
u\in C^{\alpha}(B_{1})
with the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{1})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{n/s}(\Omega\cap B_{2})}\right),

where
c=c(n,s,p,\alpha)
.

Remark 1.9
.

(i)

When
p=2
and
f\in L^{\infty}
, the same kind of results are proved in
[
Pra25a
,
Pra25
]
. We extend those results by not only weakening the regularity assumption on the right-hand side, but also considering the fractional
p
-Laplace operator which can be degenerate or singular.

(ii)

On the other hand, in
[
LZLH20
, Theorem 3.11]
, boundary
C^{\beta}
-regularity for some
\beta\in(0,2s-n/q]
of solutions is proved when
p=2
and
f\in L^{q}
with
q\in(n/(2s),n/s)
.

In the linear case, we further present a boundary Calderón-Zygmund type estimate.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let us fix
q\in[2_{*},n/(2s))
. Let
u\in W^{s,2}(B_{1})\cap L^{1}_{2s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{2}$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{2}\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{2})
. Then for any
\gamma<nq/(n-2sq)
and
\sigma\in(s,\min\{s+1/\gamma,1\})
with
\sigma-n/\gamma<2s-n/q
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta=\delta(n,s,q,\gamma,\sigma)>0
such that if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
R_{0}>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\sigma,\gamma}(B_{1})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2})+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{2})}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,q,\gamma,\sigma)
.

Remark 1.11
.

(i)

To our knowledge, similar results available in the literature are only known for
C^{1,\alpha}
-domains, whose proof relies on sharp two-sided Green function estimates (see
[
AFLY23
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
KW24
, Remark 1.4]
). On the other hand, our approach can cover nonsmooth domains, for which sharp Green function estimates are not known.

(ii)

Indeed, the proof of Theorem
1.10
employs comparison estimates in
L^{\gamma}
space for sufficiently large
\gamma>2
(see Remark
6.4
), whose validity for nonlinear equations is not clear. This is why we confine ourselves to linear equations in Theorem
1.10
, and our approach is not directly applied to fractional
p
-Laplace equations with
p\neq 2
.

The last result is concerned with sharp gradient integrability near the boundary.
We believe that the following theorem will be very useful for the investigation of Calderón-Zygmund type gradient estimates on nonsmooth domains.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{2})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to (
1.1
),
where
s>(p-1)/p
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap B_{2})
. For any
q<1/(1-s)
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta=\delta(n,s,p,q)>0
such that if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat with
R_{0}>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap B_{2})}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,R_{0},q)
.

1.2.
Techniques

Here we briefly sketch the idea of proof of our main results. Regarding the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we only explain the case when
f=0
to skip some technical parts.
To this end, we first find a regular solution
v
which is close to
u
in the sense that the
L^{p-1}
-norm of
(u-v)/d^{s}
is sufficiently small, when the domain is sufficiently flat. As in the local case
[
BR13
,
BW04
]
, this procedure is based on a compactness argument. More precisely, we assume that there is a sequence of weak solutions
\{u_{i}\}\subset W^{s,p}(B_{1})\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}_{i}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{i}\cap B_{1}$},\\
{u}_{i}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1}\setminus\Omega_{i}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega_{i}
is
(1/i,1)
-Reifenberg flat, such that
u_{i}
is not locally close to any regular function
v
. A difficulty here is that we do not know any global Sobolev regularity of
u_{i}
, which allows us to use a compactness argument in the whole domain. Thus, by using a localization argument, we consider a weak solution
w_{i}\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{w}_{i}&=g_{i}&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{i}\cap B_{1/2}$},\\
{w_{i}}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1/2}\setminus\Omega_{i}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
g_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i}\cap B_{1/2})
,
w_{i}=u_{i}
in
B_{1/2}
and
w_{i}\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{1}
. Now, due to the fact that
w_{i}\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we find a limiting function
w_{\infty}\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
weakly solving

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{w}_{\infty}&=g_{\infty}&&\qquad\mbox{in $B^{+}_{1/2}$},\\
{w}_{\infty}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1/2}^{-}$}\end{aligned}\right.

with
g_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{1/2})
, which enjoys
C^{s}
-regularity up to the boundary.
In addition, we obtain that the
L^{p-1}
norm of
(u_{i}-w_{\infty})/d^{s}
is small when
i
is sufficiently large. This gives a contradiction and thus we are always able to find a regular solution which is close to
u
when the domain is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
We highlight that we do not use any Liouville type theorem which is frequently used to obtain comparison estimates beyond the solution level for linear nonlocal equations. With this comparison estimate and perturbation arguments based on
[
CP98
]
, we obtain the desired higher integrability result for the function
u/d^{s}
.

Next, using the estimate given in Theorem
1.4
and a interior higher Hölder regularity for the fractional
p
-Laplace equations, we prove boundary higher Sobolev and Hölder regularity in nonsmooth domains. In particular, we investigate the integral

I\coloneqq\sup_{0<|h|\ll 1}\int_{B_{1/8}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{\gamma}}{|h|^{\sigma\gamma}}\,dx,

where
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
and
\sigma<s+1/\gamma
, depending on whether the points
x
and
x+h
are close to the boundary or not. When both points are near the boundary, we want to obtain an estimate of the form

\displaystyle I\leq c\int_{B_{1/4}}\frac{|u(x)|^{\gamma}}{d^{\sigma\gamma}(x)}\,dx,

whose right-hand side is readily seen to be finite when
\sigma\leq s
; however, this is not clear when
\sigma>s
.
To ensure the finiteness of the integral on the right-hand side, we prove that the distance function
d
satisfies
d^{-\varepsilon}\in L^{1}
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Note that when
\Omega
is either a Lipschitz domain or a half-space, this is a direct consequence, as
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega)<\infty
and the distance function behaves like
d_{\Omega}(x)\eqsim x_{n}
. However, these two properties are not true when
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat even though
\delta
is sufficiently small (see
[
LNV13
, Remark 2.2]
). Thus, we instead find a suitable covering of level sets of the distance function to derive a good decay estimate of the size of level sets in terms of the flatness of the domain (see Lemma
2.3
), which leads to
d^{-\varepsilon}\in L^{1}
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
(see Lemma
6.2
). Using this, we obtain
u/d^{\sigma}\in L^{\gamma}
for any
\sigma<s+1/\gamma
, which enables us to control the term
I
in the boundary case. On the other hand, when at least one of the points is far from the boundary, we use the interior higher Hölder estimates for solutions, established in
[
BLS18
,
GL24
]
(see the proof of Theorem
1.6
below for more details). Consequently, in any case, we derive
I<\infty
and then apply an embedding lemma to get the desired higher Sobolev and Hölder estimates near the boundary.

Subsequently, using Theorem
1.6
and a refined difference quotient technique together with a comparison estimate in
L^{\gamma}
space for sufficiently large
\gamma
(see Lemma
6.3
and and Remark
6.4
), we can prove Calderón-Zygmund type estimates below the gradient level for linear nonlocal equations on nonsmooth domains. Note that this kind of difference quotient technique was first introduced in
[
KM05
,
Min07
]
in the context of local problems.

Lastly, we prove sharp boundary gradient estimates by analyzing the term
I
with
\sigma=1
. More precisely, we employ the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.6
along with the interior Lipschitz estimates established in the recent paper
[
BT25
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper, we denote by
c
an universal constant which is bigger than or equal to 1. Moreover, we use parentheses to indicate the relevant dependence of a constant; for instance,
c=c(n,s,p)
means that
c
depends only on
n,s,
and
p
.

We denote by
B_{r}(z)\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|x-z|<r\}
the
n
-dimensional ball with center
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and radius
r>0
. In particular, we write
B_{r}\equiv B_{r}(0)
.
Moreover, we write
x\coloneqq(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and

B^{\pm}_{r}(z)\coloneqq\{x\in B_{r}(z)\,:\,\pm(x_{n}-z_{n})>0\}.

We also recall the definition of the distance function
d_{\Omega}(x)\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)
given in (
1.4
). When the domain is clear from context, we omit the subscript denoting the domain
\Omega
, and simply write
d(x)\equiv d_{\Omega}(x)
.

2.2.
Basic properties of Reifenberg flat domains

We observe the following scaling property of a localized equation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{0})$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

and define

u_{r,x_{0}}(x)\coloneq\frac{u(rx+x_{0})}{r^{s}},\quad f_{r,x_{0}}(x)\coloneqq{r^{s}f(rx+x_{0})}{}.

Then for any
\lambda>0
,
u_{r,x_{0}}/\lambda
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}({u}_{r,x_{0}}/\lambda)&=f_{r,x_{0}}/\lambda^{p-1}&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{r,x_{0}}\cap B_{1}$},\\
{u}_{r,x_{0}}/\lambda&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1}\setminus\Omega_{r,x_{0}}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\Omega_{r,x_{0}}\coloneqq(\Omega-x_{0})/r\coloneqq\{(x-x_{0})/r\,:\,x\in\Omega\}.

In addition, if
\Omega
is
(\delta,R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat, then
\Omega_{r,x_{0}}
is
(\delta,R_{0}/r)
-Reifenberg flat.

The next lemma is concerned with the scaling property of the distance function.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an orthogonal transformation. For
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
, define
\Psi_{r,x_{0}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)\coloneqq\Psi((x-x_{0})/r),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Then we have

\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)=r\mathrm{dist}(\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x),{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\Omega))\quad\text{for any }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle d_{\Omega}(x)=rd_{\Omega_{r,x_{0}}}((x-x_{0})/r).

Proof.

Note that
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
if and only if
\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\Omega)
. Thus, it suffices to prove that if
x\in\Omega
, then

(2.1)

\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)=r\mathrm{dist}(\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x),{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\Omega)).

Let
\overline{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
satisfy
|x-\overline{x}|=\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)
. Then we have
\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\overline{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\Omega)
with

|\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)-\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\overline{x})|=|x-\overline{x}|/r=|x-\overline{x}|/r,

where we have used the fact that
\Psi
is orthogonal. Moreover, for any
\overline{x_{1}}=\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\Omega)
, we have

|\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)-\overline{x_{1}}|=|\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)-\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x_{1})|=|x-\overline{x}|/r\geq|x-x_{0}|/r=|\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(x)-\Psi_{r,x_{0}}(\overline{x})|,

which implies (
2.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

We now provide a decay type estimate for the size of an
r
-neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
in terms of the flatness of
\Omega
, which will be used in Section
6
. Our argument is based on
[
FR24
, Lemma B.3.5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
r\in(0,2^{-10}]
, consider

\displaystyle D_{r}\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega\,:d_{\Omega}(x)\leq r\}.

If
\Omega
is
(\delta,1)
-Reifenberg flat for
\delta\in(0,2^{-n}]
, then we have

\displaystyle|D_{\delta r}\cap B_{1}|\leq c\left(\delta|D_{r}\cap B_{1}|+r\right)

for some constant
c=c(n)\geq 1
.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that if the domain is flat, then we directly have

\displaystyle|D_{\delta r}\cap B_{1}|\leq\delta|D_{r}\cap B_{1}|.

Proof.

Let us fix
r\in(0,2^{-10}]
and
\delta\in(0,2^{-n}]
.
Recall from (
1.1
) that for each
z_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, there is a new coordinate system
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
such that
z_{0}=(z_{0}^{\prime},0)
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{5r}(z_{0})\cap\{y_{n}>5r\delta\}\subset B_{5r}(z_{0})\cap\Omega\subset B_{5r}(z_{0})\cap\{y_{n}>-5r\delta\}.

Now we are going to construct a suitable covering of
D_{\delta r}\cap B_{1}
. To this end, we first use the Vitali covering lemma to have a countable collection of mutually disjoint balls
\{B_{r}(z_{i})\}_{i\in I}
, where
z_{i}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1+2r}
, satisfying

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{z\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1+2r}}B_{r}(z)\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{5r}(z_{i}).

In addition, we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}(B_{r/4}(z_{i})\cap\Omega)\subset D_{r}\quad\text{and}\quad(D_{r}\cap B_{1})\subset\bigcup_{z\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1+2r}}B_{r}(z)\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{5r}(z_{i}).

Note that either

\displaystyle B_{5r}(z_{i})\subset B_{1+12r}\setminus B_{1-8r}\quad\text{or}\quad B_{5r}(z_{i})\subset B_{1}

must hold; we accordingly set

\displaystyle I_{1}\coloneqq\{i\in I\,:\,B_{5r}(z_{i})\subset B_{1+12r}\setminus B_{1-8r}\}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{2}\coloneqq\{i\in I\,:\,B_{5r}(z_{i})\subset B_{1}\}.

If
i\in I_{1}
, then we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}}|B_{5r}(z_{i})|\leq 5^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{1}}|B_{r}(z_{i})|\leq 5^{n}|B_{1+12r}\setminus B_{1-8r}|\leq cr,

where we have used the fact that
\{B_{r}(z_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is mutually disjoint.
For each
i\in{I}_{2}
, we define

U_{r}(z_{i})\coloneqq B_{5r}(z_{i})\cap\{-10r\delta<y_{n}<10r\delta\}

on the coordinate system
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
(see (
2.2
)). Then we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle|U_{r}(z_{i})|\leq c\delta|B_{5r}(z_{i})|\leq c\delta|B_{r/4}(z_{i})|

for some constant
c=c(n)
. Now we want to prove

(2.8)

D_{\delta r}\cap B_{1}\subset\left(\bigcup_{i\in I_{1}}B_{5r}(z_{i})\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{i\in I_{2}}U_{r}(z_{i})\right).

Suppose that
x\in\Omega\cap B_{1}
with
d_{\Omega}(x)\leq\delta r
, then there is a point
\overline{x}\in\partial\Omega
such that
d_{\Omega}(x)=|x-\overline{x}|
.
By (
2.3
),
B_{r}(\overline{x})\subset B_{5r}(z_{i})
for some
i\in I
. If
i\in I_{1}
, then (
2.8
) directly follows from the fact that
x\in B_{\delta r}(\overline{x})\subset B_{r}(\overline{x})\subset B_{5r}(z_{i})
. We next assume
i\in I_{2}
. Note that, on the coordinate system
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
, (
2.2
) and the fact that
d_{\Omega}(x)\leq\delta r
imply

\overline{x}\in B_{5r}(z_{i})\cap\partial\Omega\subset B_{5r}(z_{i})\cap\{-6r\delta\leq y_{n}\leq 5r\delta\}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle x\in B_{5r}(z_{i})\cap\{-7r\delta<y_{n}<7r\delta\}\subset U_{r}(z_{i}),

which implies (
2.8
).
Now we obtain the desired estimate as follows:

\displaystyle|D_{\delta r}\cap B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{1}}|B_{5r}(z_{i})|+\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|U_{r}(z_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq c\left[r+\delta\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|B_{r/4}(z_{i})|\right]\leq c\left[r+\delta\sum_{i\in I_{2}}|B_{r/4}(z_{i})\cap\Omega|\right]\leq c(r+\delta|D_{r}\cap B_{1}|)

for some constant
c=c(n)
, where we have used (
2.6
), (
2.8
), (
2.7
), (
2.5
), (
2.4
), (
1.2
) and the fact that
\{B_{r}(z_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is mutually disjoint.
∎

2.3.
Supremum estimates near the boundary

With the measure density condition (
1.2
), we now provide supremum estimates near the boundary for fractional
p
-Laplace equations. To shorten notations, we consider the
s
-fractional gradient of
u
defined by

d_{s}u(x,y)\coloneqq\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\quad\text{for any }x\neq y.

First, we give an energy estimate near the boundary.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{p_{*}}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)
and
\Omega
satisfies (
1.2
) with
R_{0}=r
. Then there is a constant

such that

\displaystyle\leq c\widetilde{E}(u/r^{s};B_{r}(x_{0}))^{p}+c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}|r^{s}f|^{p_{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}(p-1)}}.

By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to consider the case that
B_{r}(x_{0})=B_{1}

\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{(\rho+R)/2})

0\leq\psi\leq 1

\psi\equiv 1

B_{\rho}
and

2.9
) with

[
DKP16

(2.10)

and the fractional Sobolev-Poincaré inequality given in

Plugging this last display into (

for some constant

Note in particular that
p-1<p<p_{1}
. Accordingly, we have the following interpolation inequality:

\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p-1}\,dx\right)^{\theta/(p-1)}\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p_{1}}\,dx\right)^{(1-\theta)/p_{1}},

where the constant
\theta=\theta(n,s,p)\in(0,1)
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p-1}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{1}}.

Using this and Young’s inequality, along with the fact that
\psi\equiv 1
in
B_{\rho}
and
7/8\leq\rho<R\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|u|^{p_{1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{{p}_{1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{c}{(R-\rho)^{\frac{np}{\theta}}}\left[\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p-1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{R})^{p}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{R}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p_{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}(p-1)}}\right]

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
.
After a few simple computations, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}|u|^{p_{1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{{p}_{1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{1}}}+\frac{c}{(R-\rho)^{\frac{np}{\theta}}}\left[\widetilde{E}(u;B_{1})^{p}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p_{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}(p-1)}}\right]

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
, whenever
7/8\leq\rho<R\leq 1
. By using
[
Giu03
, Lemma 6.1]
, we get

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{7/8}}|u|^{p}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{B_{7/8}}|u|^{p_{1}}\,dx\right)^{p/p_{1}}\leq c\left[\widetilde{E}(u;B_{1})^{p}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{1}\cap\Omega}|f|^{p_{*}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{*}(p-1)}}\right],

where we have also used Hölder’s inequality for the first inequality. On the other hand, by (
2.12
) with this time
\rho=3/4
and
R=7/8
, we have

\displaystyle[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{3/4})}\leq c\left(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{7/8})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{7/8})^{p}+\|f\|^{p/(p-1)}_{L^{p_{*}}(B_{7/8}\cap\Omega)}\right).

Combining this last display and (
2.13
) leads to the desired estimate.
∎

We next give a boundedness result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{r}(x_{0}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)
and
\Omega
satisfies (
1.2
) with
R_{0}\leq r
. Then there is a constant
c=c(n,s,p)
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7r/8}(x_{0}))}\leq c\widetilde{E}(u;B_{r}(x_{0}))+c\|r^{sp}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of
[
KKP16
, Theorem 5]
, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7r/8}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}+c\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{r}(x_{0}))^{p}+c\|r^{sp}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}

by using Lemma
2.5
and a standard De Giorgi type iteration as in
[
Coz17
, Proposition 6.1]
. We now follow the arguments given in
[
KMS15
, Corollary 2.1]
to get the desired estimate.
∎

We end this section with a boundary version of the localization argument.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{4R}(x_{0}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{4R}(x_{0})$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{4R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let us fix a cut-off function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{11R/4}(x_{0}))
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi\equiv 1
in
B_{5R/2}(x_{0})
. Then
v\coloneqq u\psi\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a weak solution to

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{v}&=f+\mu&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{2R}(x_{0})$},\\
{v}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where

(2.15)

\mu(x)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{5R/2}(x_{0})}\frac{(|d_{s}v|^{p-2}d_{s}v)(x,y)-(|d_{s}u|^{p-2}d_{s}u)(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,dy,\quad x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})

satisfies

\displaystyle|\mu(x)|\leq cR^{-sp}\left(|u(x)|^{p-1}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{5R/2}(x_{0}))^{p-1}\right).

Proof.

By following the proof of
[
DKLN25
, Lemma 3.1]
, we see that
v
is a weak solution to (
2.14
) with the right-hand side
\mu=\mu(x)
determined in (
2.15
). Due to the facts that
|v(x)|\leq|u(x)|
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and that

\displaystyle|x-y|\geq|y-x_{0}|-|x-x_{0}|\geq|y-x_{0}|/4

for any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{5R/2}(x_{0})
and
x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle|\mu(x)|\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{5R/2}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
. After a few simple computations, we get the desired estimate.
∎

3.
Regularity in flat domains

In this section, we prove a supremum estimate for
v/d^{s}
, where
v
is a weak solution to a nonhomogeneous fractional
p
-Laplace equation in a flat domain, by employing a barrier constructed in
[
IMS16
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v\in W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{v}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}^{+}(z)$},\\
{v}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}^{-}(z)$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}(z))
. Then there exists a constant
c=c(n,s,p)
such that

\|v/d_{B^{+}_{r}(z)}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{r/2}(z))}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(v/r^{s};B_{r}(z))+\|r^{s}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}(z))}\right).

Proof.

Since
v_{r,z}(x)\coloneqq v(rx+z)/r^{s}
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{v}_{r,z}&=f_{r,z}&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1}^{+}$},\\
{v}_{r,z}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1}^{-}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f_{r,z}(x)\coloneqq r^{s}f(rx+z)
, by Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to consider the case that
B_{r}(z)=B_{1}
.
First, we note from Lemma
2.6
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7/8})}\leq c_{0}\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)

for some constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,s,p)
. Next, let us choose
x_{0}=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,n})\in B_{1/2}^{+}
and define
v_{0}(x)\coloneqq v(x+(x_{0}^{\prime},0))
to see that
v_{0}
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{v_{0}}&=f_{0}&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1/4}^{+}$},\\
{v_{0}}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1/4}^{-}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
f_{0}(x)\coloneqq f(x+(x_{0}^{\prime},0))
.
We now consider a cut-off function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3/8})
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi\equiv 1
in
B_{1/4}
. Then we see that
h\coloneqq v_{0}\psi
is a weak solution to

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{h}&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{1/8}^{+}$},\\
{h}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{3/8})\cup{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{-}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle g(x)\coloneqq

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{1/4}}\frac{|v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|^{p-2}(v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{1/4}}\frac{|v_{0}(x)-h(y)|^{p-2}(v_{0}(x)-h(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy+f_{0}(x)\quad\text{in }B^{+}_{1/8}.

A simple computation, together with (
3.1
) and the definition of
v_{0}
, shows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{1/8})}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{1/4}}\frac{|v_{0}(x)|^{p-1}+|v_{0}(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{n+sp}}\,dy+c\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{1/8})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}^{1/(p-1)}\right)^{p-1}

for some constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n,s,p)
.
By
[
IMS16
, Lemma 4.3]
, there is a barrier
w\in C^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}&{w}\geq a&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r_{0}}(e_{n})\setminus\overline{B}_{1}$},\\
c_{2}^{-1}(|x|-1)_{+}^{s}\leq&w\leq c_{2}(|x|-1)_{+}^{s}&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}$}\end{aligned}\right.

for some
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,s,p)>0
,
a=a(n,s,p)\in(0,1)
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(n,s,p)\geq 1
. Note that we may assume
r_{0}\leq 1/8
, as
w
also weakly solves

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}w\geq a\quad\text{in }B_{\min\{r_{0},1/8\}}(e_{n})\setminus\overline{B}_{1}.

Now, for
\widetilde{w}(x)\coloneqq w(x+e_{n})
and any constant
M\geq 1
, we observe that

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}&{(M\widetilde{w})}\geq M^{p-1}a&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r_{0}}\setminus\overline{B_{1}(-e_{n})}$ },\\
c_{2}^{-1}M(|x+e_{n}|-1)_{+}^{s}\leq&M\widetilde{w}\leq c_{2}M(|x+e_{n}|-1)_{+}^{s}&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}$}.\end{aligned}\right.

By taking

(3.5)

M\coloneqq\left[(c_{1}/a)^{1/(p-1)}+c_{0}c_{2}(4n/r_{0})^{s}\right]\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)

and using (
3.3
), we have

(3.6)

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}h\leq(-\Delta_{p})^{s}(M\widetilde{w})\quad\text{in }B_{r_{0}}^{+}.

In light of (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we observe

(3.7)

\displaystyle h=0\leq M\widetilde{w}\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}_{-}^{n}\cup({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus B_{3/8}).

We also observe that, for any
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}^{+}
, either

\displaystyle|x^{\prime}|\geq r_{0}/(4n)\quad\text{or}\quad x_{n}\geq r_{0}/(4n)

holds, which implies

(3.8)

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}M(r_{0}/4n)^{s}\leq c_{2}^{-1}M(|x+e_{n}|-1)_{+}^{s}\quad\text{for any }x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{0}}^{+}.

Therefore, using (
3.1
) together with the fact that
|h(x)|=|(v_{0}\psi)(x)|\leq|v_{0}(x)|
, (
3.5
), (
3.4
) and (
3.8
), we have

(3.9)

h(x)\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7/8})}\leq c_{0}\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)\leq c_{2}^{-1}M(r_{0}/4n)^{s}\leq M\widetilde{w}(x)

for any
x\in B_{3/4}^{+}\setminus B_{r_{0}}^{+}
.
Therefore, by the comparison principle given in
[
IMS16
, Proposition 2.10]
, (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.9
) imply

h\leq M\widetilde{w}\quad\text{in }B_{r_{0}}^{+},

which gives

|h(x)|\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)(|x+e_{n}|-1)_{+}^{s}\quad\text{in }B_{r_{0}}^{+}.

When
x_{0,n}\leq r_{0}
,
since
v_{0}\equiv h
in
B_{1/4}
, we have

|v(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,n})|=|v_{0}(0,x_{0,n})|=|h(0,x_{0,n})|\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)d_{B^{+}_{1}}^{s}(x_{0}).

When instead
x_{0,n}\geq r_{0}
, since
r_{0}<1
depends only on
n,s
and
p
, we have
x_{0,n}\leq cd_{B^{+}_{1}}(x_{0})
and so

|v(x_{0})|=|v(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{0,n})|\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7/8})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)d_{B^{+}_{1}}^{s}(x_{0})

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
.
Since we have arbitrarily chosen
x_{0}\in B^{+}_{1/2}
, we conclude with

\displaystyle\|v/d^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{+}_{1/2})}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(v;B_{1})+\|f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
.
∎

4.
Comparison estimates

In this section, we deal with a weak solution
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{5r}(z))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{u}&=f&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{5r}(z)$},\\
{u}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{5r}(z)\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
d_{\Omega}(z)\leq r
and
\Omega
is
(\delta,5r)
-Reifenberg flat with
\delta\in(0,1/2)
to ensure (
1.2
).
We start with a nonlocal Hardy-type inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p>1
, assume that
u\in W^{\alpha,p}(B_{r}(z))
satisfies
u\equiv 0
in
B_{r}(z)\setminus\Omega
.
Then for any
\rho\in[1/2,1)
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{\rho r}(z)\cap\Omega}\left|\frac{u}{d_{\Omega}^{\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq c[u]_{W^{\alpha,p}(B_{r}(z))}

where
c=c(n,\alpha,p,1/(1-\rho))
.

Proof.

The proof is based on that of
[
KW24
, Lemma 2.4]
together with a localization argument. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{(\rho+3)r/4}(z))
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\psi\equiv 1
in
B_{(\rho+1)r/2}(z)
and
|\nabla\psi|\leq\frac{c}{(1-\rho)r}
. We first observe that
w\coloneqq u\psi
satisfies

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r/2}(z)\cap\Omega}\left|\frac{w}{d_{\Omega}^{\alpha}}\right|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq c[w]_{W^{\alpha,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

for some constant
c=c(n,\alpha,p)
. Note that the domain
\Omega
is
(\delta,5r)
-Reifenberg so that (
1.2
) holds with
R_{0}=5r
.
Thus, by following the proof of
[
KW24
, Lemma 2.4]
together with the facts that
d_{\Omega}(x)\leq 2r
for any
x\in B_{r/2}(z)\cap\Omega
and that (
1.2
) holds with
R_{0}=5r
, we get (
4.3
). Since
w\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{(\rho+3)r/4}(z)
and
|w(x)|\leq|u(x)|
, we have

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{p}\leq[w]_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))}^{p}+c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(z)}\int_{B_{(\rho+3)r/4}(z)}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}.

Using
[
Coz17
, Lemma 4.7]
together with the facts that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and that
|\nabla\psi|\leq\frac{c}{(1-\rho)r}
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))}^{p}+\int_{B_{r}(z)}\int_{B_{r}(z)}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp-p}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))}^{p}+\frac{c}{(1-\rho)^{p}}\int_{B_{r}(z)}|u(x)/r^{s}|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{c}{(1-\rho)^{p}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))}

for some
c=c(n,s,p)
.
Using
[
Coz17
, Lemma 4.7]
together with the fact that
u\equiv 0
in
B_{r}(z)\setminus\Omega
and (
1.2
), we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(z)}\int_{B_{(\rho+3)r/4}(z)}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|z-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(1-\rho)^{p}}\int_{B_{r}(z)}|u(x)/r^{s}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{(1-\rho)^{p}}[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{r}(z))}

for some
c=c(n,s,p)
. Thus, (
4.2
) follows from (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and the fact that
u=w
in
B_{r/2}(z)
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the first comparison estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
4.1
). Then there is a weak solution
w\in W^{s,p}(B_{4r}(z))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta_{p})^{s}{w}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega\cap B_{4r}(z)$},\\

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{z\in\Omega\cap B_{1/4}\,:\,M_{2^{-6}}^{\Omega}(|u/d_{\Omega}^{s}|^{p-1})(z)>\lambda^{p-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cup\left\{z\in\Omega\cap B_{1/4}\,:\,M^{\Omega}_{sp_{*},2^{-6}}(|f|^{p_{*}})(z)>(\delta\lambda)^{{p_{*}}{(p-1)}}\right\}.

With these notations, we start with the following measure density lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
.
For any

and

\displaystyle\Omega\cap B_{\rho/8}(z)\not\subset\mathcal{D},

where

Proof.

Assume that
\Omega\cap B_{\rho/8}(z)\not\subset\mathcal{D}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r\leq 2^{-6}}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}|u/d_{\Omega}^{s}|^{p-1}\,dx\leq\lambda^{p-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{0<r\leq 2^{-6}}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}r^{sp_{*}}|f|^{p_{*}}\,dx\leq(\delta\lambda)^{{p_{*}}{(p-1)}}.

With

being any point with

, we first show

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(u;B_{2^{6}\rho}(y))\leq c\lambda

To this end, we observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(u;B_{2^{6}\rho}(y))

for some

, we get

(6.20)

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(z)}|w|^{\frac{nq}{n-2sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-2sq}{nq}}\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(z)}r^{2sq}|f_{+}|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
c=c(n,s,q)
. Since the Green function of
(-\Delta)^{s}
with respect to
B_{r}(z)
is nonnegative (see
[
CKS10
]
) and
f_{+}\geq 0
, we have
w\geq 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus, we see that

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}{(v-w)}&\leq 0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega$},\\
{v-w}&\leq 0&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus(B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega)$}.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, since
B_{r/2}(z)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
, by (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle|B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega|\geq|B_{r}(z)|/c

for some constant
c=c(n)
.
Using this and the maximum principle given in
[
FR24
, Lemma 2.3.3]
, we have
v\leq w
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus, (
6.20
) and the fact that
v=f_{+}=0
in
B_{r}(z)\setminus\Omega
imply

(6.21)

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega}|v_{+}|^{\frac{nq}{n-2sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-2sq}{nq}}\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega}r^{2sq}|f_{+}|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for some constant
c=c(n,s,q)
. Since (
6.21
) also holds with
v_{+}
and
f_{+}
replaced by
v_{-}
and
f_{-}
, respectively, we have the desired estimate.
∎

.

We now revise the estimate given in Theorem

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
v
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}{v}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{4r}(z)\cap\Omega$},\\
{v}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
z\in\partial\Omega
and
\Omega
is
(\delta,4r)
-Reifenberg flat. Let us fix
\gamma\geq 1
and
\sigma<\min\{s+1/\gamma,1\}
. If
\delta=\delta(n,s,\gamma,\sigma)>0
is sufficiently small, then we have

\displaystyle r^{\sigma-n/\gamma}[v]_{W^{\sigma,\gamma}(B_{3r}(z))}\leq c\widetilde{E}(v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)};B_{4r}(z))

for some constant
c=c(n,s,\gamma,\sigma)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
1.6
with
f=0
, we have

(6.22)

\displaystyle r^{\sigma-n/\gamma}[v]_{W^{\sigma,\gamma}(B_{3r}(z))}\leq c\widetilde{E}(v;B_{4r}(z))

for some constant
c=c(n,s,\gamma,\sigma)
, whenever
\delta=\delta(n,s,\gamma,\sigma)>0
is sufficiently small.
Since
z\in\partial\Omega
, (
1.2
) implies

(6.23)

\displaystyle|B_{4r}(z)|/4^{n}\leq|B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega|.

Using (
6.23
) along with the facts that
v\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and that

\displaystyle|(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega}|\leq\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega}|v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}|\,dx,

we estimate

\displaystyle|(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}|=\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{4r}(z)}|v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{4r}(z)}|v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}|\,dx+|(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)\setminus\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq c\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{4r}(z)}|v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}|\,dx

for some constant
c=c(n)
, which with Hölder’s inequality further gives

(6.24)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(v;B_{4r}(z))

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{E}(v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)};B_{4r}(z))+c(v)_{B_{4r}(z)}\leq c\widetilde{E}(v-(v)_{B_{4r}(z)};B_{4r}(z))

for some constant

.
Connecting (
6.24
) to (
6.22
) yields the desired result.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

We fix
\gamma<nq/(n-2sq)
and

\sigma\in(s,\min\{s+1/\gamma,1\})\quad\text{with}\quad\sigma<2s+n/\gamma-n/q.

We next choose

\sigma_{1}\coloneqq(\sigma+\min\{s+1/\gamma,1,2s+n/\gamma-n/q\})/2\in(\sigma,\min\{s+1/\gamma,1\})

to see that
\sigma_{1}-n/\gamma<2s-n/q
. Then we set

(6.25)

\beta\coloneqq\frac{\sigma_{1}\gamma}{2s\gamma+n(1-\gamma/q)}\in(0,1)

Let us set
1\leq\rho<r\leq 2

and fix
h\in B_{2^{-10}(r-\rho)^{1/\beta}}\setminus\{0\}
. By
[
DKLN24
, Lemma 2.11]
, there exist a constant
c=c(n)
, a finite index set
I
and a sequence
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset B_{\rho}
such that

(6.26)

\displaystyle B_{\rho}\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\subset B_{r},\quad\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{i\in I}{\mbox{\Large$\chi$}}_{B_{2^{k}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}(x)\leq c2^{nk},\quad|I|\leq c/|h|^{n\beta},

where
|I|
denotes the number of elements in the set
I
.
Accordingly, we introduce the finite difference operator
\delta_{h}
defined by

\delta_{h}u(x)\coloneqq(\delta_{h}u)(x)\coloneqq u(x+h)-u(x),\qquad x\in B_{\rho}.

We are going to prove that, for any
h\in B_{2^{-10}(r-\rho)^{1/\beta}}\setminus\{0\}
,

(6.27)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|\delta_{h}u|^{\gamma}\,dx\leq c|h|^{\gamma(\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)+\alpha\beta)}[u]^{\gamma}_{W^{\alpha,\gamma}(B_{r})}+c|h|^{\sigma_{1}\gamma}\left[\widetilde{E}(u;B_{r})^{\gamma}+\left(\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}|f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\gamma/q}\right],

holds for some constant
c=c(n,s,\gamma,q,\sigma,r-\rho)
, whenever
\delta=\delta(n,s,q,\gamma,\sigma)>0
is sufficiently small and
u\in W^{\alpha,\gamma}(B_{r})
for some
\alpha\in[0,\sigma_{1})
. In particular when
\alpha=0
, we identify
W^{0,\gamma}\equiv L^{\gamma}
and
[u]_{W^{0,\gamma}(B_{r})}\equiv\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{r})}
.
Now, with
i\in I
being fixed, we estimate

\displaystyle J\coloneqq\int_{B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|\delta_{h}u|^{\gamma}\,dx,

depending on the location of the ball
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})
.

Step 1: The exterior case.
We first assume that
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega=\emptyset
. In this case, since
|h|\leq|h|^{\beta}
for
|h|\leq 1
and
\beta<1
, we have
x,x+h\in B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})
when
x\in B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})
. Thus, we get

(6.28)

\displaystyle J=\int_{B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|\delta_{h}u|^{\gamma}\,dx=0.

Step 2: The interior case.
We next assume that
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)=\emptyset
. From
[
BL17
, Proposition 2.6]
and the fact that
0<|h|<|h|^{\beta}/2
, we note

(6.29)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\delta_{h}u}{|h|^{\sigma_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))}\leq c\left([u]_{W^{\sigma_{1},\gamma}(B_{3|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}+\frac{\|u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{3|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}}{|h|^{\beta\sigma_{1}}}\right).

Now, using
[
DN25
, Theorem 1.4]
together with the fact that
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\Subset\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle|h|^{-n\beta/\gamma}[u]_{W^{\sigma_{1},\gamma}(B_{3|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{-\sigma_{1}\beta}\left(\widetilde{E}(u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))+|h|^{-n\beta/q}\||h|^{2s\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{-\sigma_{1}\beta}|h|^{-n\beta/\gamma}\|u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{2|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}

\displaystyle\quad+c|h|^{-\sigma_{1}\beta}\left(\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))+|h|^{-n\beta/q}\||h|^{2s\beta}f\|_{L^{q}(B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))}\right).

Thus, we estimate
J
as

(6.30)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\left(\int_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx+|h|^{n\beta}\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})})^{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle+c|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)+n\beta(1-\gamma/q)+2s\beta\gamma}\left(\int_{B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\gamma/q}.

Step 3: The boundary case.
We finally assume that
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
and
B_{2|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
v_{i}
be the weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}{v}_{i}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega$},\\
{v}_{i}&=u&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus(B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega)$}.\end{aligned}\right.

We then see that
u-v_{i}
is the weak solution to (
6.19
) with
u
and
B_{r}(z)
replaced by
u-v_{i}
and
B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})
, respectively.
Thus, by Hölder’s inequality and Remark
6.4
, we get

(6.31)

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-v|^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-v|^{\frac{nq}{n-2sq}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-2sq}{nq}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}||h|^{2s\beta}f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}

for some constant
c=c(n,s,q)
.
Using this, we have

(6.32)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}{|\delta_{h}(u-v)|^{\gamma}}\,dx+c\int_{B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}{|\delta_{h}v|^{\gamma}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{2s\beta\gamma+n\beta(1-\gamma/q)}\left(\int_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega}|f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\gamma/q}+\underbrace{c\int_{B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}{|\delta_{h}v|^{\gamma}}\,dx}_{\eqqcolon J_{1}}.

To estimate

, we note that there is a point

such that

B_{|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\subset B_{3|h|^{\beta}}(\overline{z_{i}})\subset B_{4|h|^{\beta}}(\overline{z_{i}})\subset B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}).

Thus, we see that
v_{i}
is also a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}{v}_{i}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{4|h|^{\beta}}(\overline{z_{i}})\cap\Omega$},\\
{v}_{i}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $B_{4|h|^{\beta}}(\overline{z_{i}})\setminus\Omega$}.\end{aligned}\right.

By (
6.29
) with

and Lemma

together with the fact that

\displaystyle J_{1}^{1/\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\sigma_{1}}\left([v]_{W^{\sigma_{1},\gamma}(B_{4|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}+|h|^{(1-\beta)\sigma_{1}}\|v-(v)_{B_{4|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{4|h|^{\beta}/2}(z_{i}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{(1-\beta)\sigma_{1}}\|v-(v)_{B_{5|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\|_{L^{\gamma}(B_{5|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))}

when

is sufficiently small.
We now use (
6.31
) to further estimate

as

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\left(\int_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx+|h|^{n\beta}\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))^{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c|h|^{2s\beta\gamma+\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)+n\beta(1-{\gamma}/{q})}\left(\int_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})\cap\Omega}|f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\gamma/q}.

), we get

(6.33)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\left(\int_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx+|h|^{n\beta}\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))^{\gamma}\right)

Step 4: Tail estimates.

By (
6.28
), (
6.30
) and (

), we derive

(6.34)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\left(\int_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx+|h|^{n\beta}\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))^{\gamma}\right)

where

(6.35)

(6.36)

\sigma_{2}\coloneqq(\sigma_{1}+2s)/2\in(\sigma_{1},2s),

and using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|h|^{n\beta}\mathrm{Tail}(u-(u)_{B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})};B_{8|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i}))^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{3/4}}|\delta_{h}u|^{\gamma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i\in I}|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\sum_{j=0}^{l}2^{-(\sigma_{2}\gamma+n)j}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{2^{3+j}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx

\displaystyle+c\sum_{i\in I}|h|^{n\beta+2s\beta\gamma}\widetilde{E}(u;B_{r})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle+c|h|^{2s\beta\gamma+n\beta(1-\gamma/q)}\left[\widetilde{E}(u;B_{r})^{\gamma}+\left(\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}|f|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\gamma/q}\right].

Step 5: Fractional bootstrap.

We now use a bootstrap argument together with a suitable choice of parameter in order to further estimate the term given in (

). Assume that

for some

. Then, by the fractional Poincaré inequality, (

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\sum_{j=1}^{l}2^{-(\sigma_{2}\gamma+n)j}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|u-(u)_{B_{2^{3+j}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}|^{\gamma}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i\in I}|h|^{\gamma\sigma_{1}(1-\beta)}\sum_{j=1}^{l}2^{-(\sigma_{2}\gamma+n)j}(2^{j+3}|h|^{\beta})^{\alpha\gamma}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\int_{B_{2^{j+3}|h|^{\beta}}(z_{i})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{\gamma}}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha\gamma}}\,dx\,dy

Plugging this into (
6.39
) and then using the fact that

6.27

\displaystyle 2s\beta\gamma+n\beta(1-\gamma/q)\geq\sigma_{1}\gamma.

Next, we consider the two sequences

and

6.3.
Sharp gradient estimates

In this subsection, we prove sharp boundary gradient estimates for fractional
p
-Laplace equations.

When

, Lipschitz estimates for the
s
-fractional
p
-Laplace equation are established in the very recent paper
[

]
. We slightly modify those results in a form suitable for our analysis.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{2R}(z_{0}))\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=f\quad\text{in }B_{2R}(z_{0}),

where

and

Then we have

(6.43)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(z_{0}))}\leq\frac{c}{R}\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2R}(z_{0}))+\|R^{sp}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R}(z_{0}))}\right),

for some
c=c(n,s,p)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have

(6.44)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3R/2}(z_{0}))}\leq c\left(\widetilde{E}(u;B_{2R}(z_{0}))+\|R^{sp}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R}(z_{0}))}\right)

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
. Note from
[
KKL19
]
that a weak solution
u
to (
6.42
) is also a viscosity solution to (
6.42
). Moreover,
u_{R}(x)\coloneqq u(Rx+z_{0})/R
is a viscosity solution to

where

.
Thus, using
[
BT25
, Theorem 2.1]
and scaling back, we get

(6.45)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(z_{0}))}\leq\frac{c}{R}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3R/2}(z_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;B_{3R/2}(z_{0}))+\|R^{sp}f\|^{1/(p-1)}_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R}(z_{0}))}\right),

where

. Combining (

) and (

) yields (

∎

Using this estimate, we now prove Theorem

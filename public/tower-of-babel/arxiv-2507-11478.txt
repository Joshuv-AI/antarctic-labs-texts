The Integral Chow Rings of the Moduli Stacks of Hyperelliptic Prym Pairs II
Alessio Cela, Alberto Landi

Abstract
This paper is the second in a series devoted to describing the integral Chow ring of the moduli stacks ℛ ​ ℋ g \mathcal{RH}_{g} of hyperelliptic Prym pairs. For fixed genus g g , the stack ℛ ​ ℋ g \mathcal{RH}_{g} is the disjoint union of ⌊ ( g + 1 ) / 2 ⌋ \lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor components ℛ ​ ℋ g n \mathcal{RH}_{g}^{n} for n = 1 , … , ⌊ ( g + 1 ) / 2 ⌋ n=1,\ldots,\lfloor(g+1)/2\rfloor . In this paper, we give presentations and compute the integral Chow rings of the components ℛ ​ ℋ g ( g + 1 ) / 2 \mathcal{RH}_{g}^{(g+1)/2} for odd g g . As an application, we also obtain presentations and Chow rings for all irreducible components of the moduli stack of hyperelliptic Spin curves of odd genus. An intermediate result of independent interest is the computation of the integral Chow ring of the moduli stack of unordered pairs of divisors of the same even degree in ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} .

Introduction

This paper concerns the study of the geometry of the stack parametrizing hyperelliptic Prym pairs and the computation of its integral Chow ring.

The computation of Chow rings of moduli stacks has attracted significant interest in algebraic geometry, starting from the seminal work of Mumford
[
Mum83
]
, where he studies the rational Chow ring of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{g}
parametrizing smooth genus
g
curves, as well as its Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Since then, numerous computations have been carried out. As for
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
the rational Chow ring of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is known for
g\leq 9
(
[
Mum83
,
Fab90a
,
Fab90b
,
Iza95
,
PV15
,
CL23a
]
). On the other hand, the integral case is considerably more difficult, as reflected by the comparatively smaller number of known results. The integral Chow ring of the stack of
n
-pointed smooth genus
g
curves
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is currently known only for the following few values of
(g,n)
:
(0,n)
all
n
(
[
Kee92
]
),
(1,n)
for
n\leq 10
(
[
EG98
,
Inc22
,
AOA23
,
Bis24
]
),
(2,n)
for
n\leq 2
(
[
Vis98
,
Per22
,
Lan24
]
). When
(g,n)=(3,0)
the almost integral Chow ring of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is computed in
[
Per23
]
.

Other computations of Chow rings of moduli spaces related to curves involve stable maps
[
CF23
]
, admissible covers
[
CL23b
,
CL22
]
, or geometrically significant substacks of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, namely the hyperelliptic locus
[
EF09
,
FV11
,
DL18
,
Lan23
,
Lan24
,
EH22
]
.

While substantial progress has been made and many conjectures formulated regarding the intersection theory of moduli stacks of curves, relatively little is known about the intersection theory of the moduli stack of Prym pairs. The program to compute the Chow rings of the moduli stack of Prym pairs was initiated in
[
CIL24
]
, where the genus 2 case was addressed, and further developed in
[
CL25
]
, where the computation of the integral Chow ring of the hyperelliptic locus in arbitrary genus was begun. This paper is the second in this ongoing series of works.

A Prym pair of genus
g\geq 2
is essentially an étale double cover
\pi:D\to C
between smooth curves, with
C
of genus
g
. In this sense, these stacks fit naturally into the framework of moduli stacks of covers of curves. Moreover, such a cover defines a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
g-1
, known as the associated Prym variety. The close relationship between Prym pairs and abelian varieties has been extensively studied; see, for example,
[
Mum74
,
FR70
,
Bea77a
,
Bea77b
,
DS81
]
. In particular, moduli stacks of Prym pairs play a significant role due to their rich interactions with several other important moduli stacks.

Main definitions

[

\mu_{2}

The decomposition (

1.2.2
The Chow rings
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{RH}_{g}^{(g+1)/2})
and
\mathrm{CH}^{*}([D_{g+1,g+1}/\mu_{2}])

The difficulty in computing

1.2.3
Hyperelliptic Spin Pairs of odd genus

An interesting application of our result concerns hyperelliptic Spin curves of odd genus.

Fix a genus
g\geq 2
. The moduli stack of Spin curves
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is defined similarly to
\mathcal{R}_{g}
, as the stackification of the prestack whose objects are triples
(C/S,\theta,\alpha)
, where
C\to S
is a smooth genus
g
curve,
\theta\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
is a line bundle, and
\alpha:\theta^{\otimes 2}\to\omega_{C/S}
is an isomorphism. Morphisms
(C/S,\theta,\alpha)\to(C^{\prime}/S^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
in this prestack are given by cartesian diagrams as in (
1
), for which there exists a morphism
\tau:\varphi^{*}\theta^{\prime}\to\theta
such that the diagram corresponding to (
2
) commutes, with
\mathcal{O}_{C^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{C}
replaced respectively by
\omega_{C^{\prime}/S^{\prime}}
and
\omega_{C/S}
. We refer to
[
Cor89
]
for an introduction to Spin curves, often also called theta characteristics.

There is a natural (representable) finite and étale morphism
\mathcal{S}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
of degree
2^{2g}
. We denote by
\mathcal{SH}_{g}
the restriction of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
to
\mathcal{H}_{g}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{g}
be the stack parametrizing smooth genus
g
curves together with line bundles of degree
0
, with morphisms given by maps between the curves and the corresponding line bundles. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{g}
carries a natural
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-2-structure, whose rigidification we denote by
\mathcal{J}_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{g}[2]\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{g}
be the closed substack where the line bundle is
2
-torsion. There is an open and closed decomposition (see
[
CIL24
, Proposition 11]
)

\mathcal{J}_{g}[2]=\mathcal{R}_{g}\sqcup\mathcal{M}_{g},

(6)

where
\mathcal{M}_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{g}[2]
is the substack corresponding to the section given by the structure sheaf.

Furthermore, there is a natural free and transitive group action

\mathcal{J}_{g}[2]\curvearrowright\mathcal{S}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}

over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, obtained by twisting a Spin curve with a 2-torsion line bundle.

Proposition 1.8
.

When
g
is odd, the forgetful map
\mathcal{SH}_{g}\to\mathcal{H}_{g}
has a section. In particular, we have a decomposition

\mathcal{SH}_{g}\cong\mathcal{H}_{g}\sqcup\mathcal{RH}_{g}.

The proof is given in §

. As a consequence, combining Theorems
1.4
and
1.7
with
[

\mathcal{SH}_{g}
when
g
is odd.

Convention

We work over an algebraically closed field

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We start with recalling that, by
[
CL25
, Equation 4]
, we have

\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}\cong\bigg[\frac{(\chi^{(1)})^{-1}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\times(\chi^{(2)})^{-1}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\smallsetminus\Delta}{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(1)}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(2)}\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\bigg]

where
\Delta
denotes the locus of pairs of polynomials
(f,g)
such that the product
fg
is singular. The groups
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(2)}
are both equal to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, with the superscripts indicating that each acts only on the corresponding factor in the product. Similarly, the notation
\chi^{(i)}
refers to the same character
\chi
, where the superscript specifies which
\mathbb{G}_{m}
factor is acting.

There is a natural map

\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}:\Bigg[\frac{V^{\vee}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\smallsetminus\Delta}{G\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\Bigg]^{\mathrm{pre}}\longrightarrow\left[\frac{\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}}{\mu_{2}}\right],

defined as follows. On objects, it assigns to a pair of polynomials
(f,g)
over a scheme
S
their respective zero loci in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}
.

Let
(f_{1},g_{1})\to(f_{2},g_{2})
be a morphism in the quotient prestack, given by a morphism
\varphi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
and an element
g=(A,[B])\in G\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(S_{1})
such that

\varphi^{*}(f_{2},g_{2})=g\cdot(f_{1},g_{1}).

Let
F_{i}
and
G_{i}
denote the zero divisors of
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S_{i}}
for
i=1,2
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S_{1}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S_{2}}
be the morphism induced by
[B]
and
\varphi
. Then, as Cartier divisors, we have either
\phi^{*}F_{2}=F_{1}
and
\phi^{*}G_{2}=G_{1}
, or
\phi^{*}F_{2}=G_{1}
and
\phi^{*}G_{2}=F_{1}
. This defines a morphism

(F_{1},G_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S_{1}}\to S_{1})\longrightarrow(F_{2},G_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S_{2}}\to S_{2})

in the stack
[\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}/\mu_{2}]
, and thereby defines
\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}
on morphisms.

We denote by
\tau
the stackification of
\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}
. Since the
\mu_{2}
-torsor
\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}\to[\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}/\mu_{2}]
factors through
\tau
via the natural
\mu_{2}
-torsor

\mathcal{D}_{g+1,g+1}\cong\bigg[\frac{(\chi^{(1)})^{-1}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\times(\chi^{(2)})^{-1}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\smallsetminus\Delta}{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(1)}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{(2)}\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\bigg]\longrightarrow\Bigg[\frac{V^{\vee}\otimes W_{\frac{g+1}{2}}\smallsetminus\Delta}{G\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\Bigg],

the morphism
\tau
must be an isomorphism.

∎

For every

(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{2}})^{\times 2}

Let

\phi:BH_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\rightarrow B(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{1}}\rtimes\mu_{2})

1\leq j\leq\ell_{2}

\pi_{j}:BH_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\rightarrow B\mathrm{GL}_{2}
the morphism induced by the composite
H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\rightarrow G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, where the first morphism is the projection to the
j
-th component of
(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{2}})^{\times 2}\rtimes\mu_{2}
.

Recall also that
\beta_{2}\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B\mathrm{GL}_{2})
denotes the second Chern class of the standard
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-representation

Proposition 3.1
.

Every element
\alpha\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BH_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}})
can be written as
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
, where
\alpha_{1}\in\mathrm{im}(\psi_{*})
, and
\alpha_{2}
is sum of products of classes of the form
\pi_{j}^{*}(\beta_{2}^{i})
for
1\leq j\leq l_{2}
and
i\geq 0
, and classes of the form
\phi^{*}(\epsilon)
for
\epsilon\in\mathrm{CH}^{*}(B(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{1}}\rtimes\mu_{2}))
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
\ell_{2}
, where the case
\ell_{2}=0
is obvious. Then, assume
\ell_{2}\geq 1
and that the statement is true for every
\ell_{2}^{\prime}\leq\ell_{2}
. For

let

be the the

-representation
E_{n}=\pi_{\ell_{2}}^{*}((V^{\vee})^{n+1})\cong\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
and let
B_{n}:=\{0\}\times\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\cup\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\times\{0\}\subseteq E_{n}
. Note that the surjective ring homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BH_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{E_{n}\smallsetminus B_{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}}\bigg]\bigg)\cong\mathrm{CH}^{*}\bigg(\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]\bigg)}

is an isomorphism in degree

; see
[

, Lemma 4.3]
for the last isomorphism. It therefore suffices to show that for all
n\geq 0
the group
\mathrm{CH}^{*}_{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n})
is generated in degrees
\leq n
by the classes appearing in the statement (viewed in this ring via the map above). Consider the open/closed decomposition

\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{A}^{n}\times\mathbb{A}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]\subset\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]\supset\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\cup\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]:=\mathcal{W},

where
\mathbb{A}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the open subscheme where the last coordinate is different from
0
. Notice that the morphism

\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]\sqcup\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}\sqcup\phi_{2}}\mathcal{W}

given by the normalization map and the natural inclusion, induces a surjection on Chow rings. Moreover, we have an isomorphism

\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{1}}\times(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{2}-1})^{\times 2}}\bigg]\cong\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\sqcup\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]

induced by the inclusion on thee first factor. Putting everything together, we get an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{1}}\times(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{2}})^{\times 2}}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1})\oplus\mathrm{CH}_{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}^{*}((\mathbb{P}^{n-1})^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}_{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}^{*}((\mathbb{P}^{n})^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BH_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

By the inductive hypothesis and the exact sequence above, it suffices to show that every element in the image of the first homomorphism of groups is of the form described in the statement. We prove this by induction on
n
, with the case

being immediate. The map
\phi_{1}
factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{1}}\times(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\ell_{2}-1})^{\times 2}}\bigg]}

implying that the image of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}}{H_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}-1}}\bigg]}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\ell_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}

Corollary 3.2
.

The group
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(BH_{1,1})
is given by

\mathrm{CH}^{2}(BH_{1,1})=\mathbb{Z}\langle\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta_{2})\rangle\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle\psi_{*}(tx_{1})\rangle\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle\psi_{*}(x_{1}^{2})\rangle\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle\phi^{*}(c_{2})\rangle\oplus\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}\langle\phi^{*}(\gamma^{2})\rangle.

Moreover, the kernel of the homomorphism of groups
\psi^{*}:\mathrm{CH}^{2}(BH_{1,1})\to\mathrm{CH}^{2}(B\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\times 2})
is the subgroup
\langle\gamma^{2}\rangle
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, we know that
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(BH_{1,1})
is additively generated by the image of
\psi_{*}
and the classes
\phi^{*}(\gamma^{2})
,
\phi^{*}(c_{2})
and
\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta_{2})
. See
[
Lar21
, Theorem 5.2]
for the Chow ring of
G=H_{0,1}
. Since
\mathrm{CH}(B(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}))\cong\mathbb{Z}[t,x_{1},x_{2}]
, where
t
,
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are the first Chern classes of
\chi
associated to the first, second and third projection respectively, we know that the image of
\psi_{*}
is additively generated by the classes

\psi_{*}(t^{2}),\quad\psi_{*}(tx_{1}),\quad\psi_{*}(tx_{2}),\quad\psi_{*}(x_{1}^{2}),\quad\psi_{*}(x_{2}^{2}),\quad\psi_{*}(x_{1}x_{2}).

First, notice that
\psi_{*}(x_{1}x_{2})=\psi_{*}(\psi^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}\beta_{2})=2\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}\langle\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta_{2})\rangle
. Moreover, by
\mu_{2}
-invariance, we have
\psi_{*}(x_{1}^{2})=\psi_{*}(x_{2}^{2})
and
\psi_{*}(tx_{1})=-\psi_{*}(tx_{2})
. Also,
\psi^{*}(\phi^{*}(c_{2}))=t^{2}
, hence
\psi_{*}(t^{2})=2\phi^{*}(c_{2})
. It follows that
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(BH_{1,1})
is additively generated by the classes in the statement and it is enough to prove that the only relation between them is
2\gamma^{2}=0
( which we already know to hold). To prove it, we compute the pullback along
\psi
, showing that the only element that is sent to
0
to zero is
\gamma^{2}
. Notice that this would leave open the possibility of
\gamma^{2}
being 0, which is ruled out by considering its the pullback along
B(\mathbb{G}_{m}\rtimes\mu_{2})\rightarrow BH_{1,1}
. Clearly,
\psi^{*}(\gamma^{2})=0
. By Lemma
3.3
below, we have that
\psi^{*}\psi_{*}(tx_{1})=t(x_{1}-x_{2})
and
\psi^{*}\psi_{*}(x_{1}^{2})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
. Finally, we have that
\psi^{*}\phi^{*}(c_{2})=t^{2}
and
\psi^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}(\beta_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}
. As these four classes are linearly independent in
\mathrm{CH}^{2}(B(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}))
, the result follows.
∎

In the previous proof we have used the next well known result, which we record here also for ease of reference later in the paper.

.

Let

-torsor of quotient stacks over a field

It follows immediately from

.

Let

be a scheme of finite type over

-action, and let

-counterpart of it. Then, there exists a morphism

\mathrm{GL}_{3}
-counterpart to

.

Consider the
\mu_{2}\times\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-action on

where
\mu_{2}
exchanges the two factors and
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
acts diagonally. Then,

\mu_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{3}
-counterpart to

.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from the proof of
[

, Theorem 1.4]
. For the second part, by construction we have cartesian cube

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times_{\mathcal{S}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left[\frac{X\times X}{\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\right]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left[\frac{X}{\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\right]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left[\frac{X}{\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}\right]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{0}}

where the maps from the left to the right side of the cube are

-torsors. One concludes by noticing that the

One can prove a similar statement for products of morphism, disjoint unions and so forth. As we will not make extensive use of these techniques, we will limit ourselves in stating each time what is the

3.3
Chow-Künneth Property

In this subsection we study the Chow Künneth property for quotient stacks, and prove that it is satisfied by
B\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
,
B\mathrm{SL}_{n}
and
B\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for every
n
. Some of the results are probably known to experts, but we report them here due to a lack of a reference.

Every stack is assumed to be of finite type over a field
k
.
We start by recalling some definitions.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quotient stack. For every quotient stack
\mathcal{T}
, consider the Chow-Kunneth morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathrm{CH}_{*}(\mathcal{X})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CH}_{*}(\mathcal{T})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CH}_{*}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{T})}

•

We say that
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the
Chow-Künneth property
, CKP for short, if
\varphi_{\mathcal{T}}
is an isomorphism for every quotient stack
\mathcal{T}
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the
Chow-Künneth generation property
, CKgP for short, if
\varphi_{\mathcal{T}}
is surjective for every quotient stack
\mathcal{T}
.

The above definition was already given and studied in
[
BS23
, Section 2.3]
,
[
CL24
, Section 3]
with rational coefficient.

We start by stating two lemmas whose proof is similar to that of
[
CL24
, Section 3]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

1.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n}
be quotient stacks satisfying CKgP. Then, the
n
-fold product
\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{X}_{n}
satisfies CKgP.

2.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quotient stack satisfying CKgP. Suppose that
f:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is either

a)

a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor, or

b)

an affine bundle, or

c)

an open immersion, or

d)

a Grassmann bundle.

Then,
\mathcal{Y}
satisfies CKgP. In the case b), also the converse holds.

3.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}=[X/G]
,
\mathcal{Z}=[Z/G]
, and suppose given a
G
-equivariant Chow envelope
g:X\rightarrow Z
. If
\mathcal{X}
satisfies CKgP then also
\mathcal{Z}
does.

4.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a quotient stack with a finite stratification (see
[
EH16
, Page 27]
for the definition) whose strata are quotient stacks that satisfies CKgP, then
\mathcal{X}
satisfies CKgP.

Proof.

The proofs of points 1), 3), 4) and parts b), c) and d) of point 2) are the same as the proofs for
[
CL24
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5, 3.7]
. Part a) of point 2) follows from points b) and c) noticing that a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor is the complement of the 0-section of the associated line bundle
\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

1.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{n}
be quotient stacks satisfying CKP. Then, the
n
-fold product
\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{X}_{n}
satisfies CKP.

2.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a quotient stack satisfying CKP. Suppose that
f:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is either

a)

a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor, or

b)

an affine bundle, or

c)

an open immersion whose reduced closed complement satisfies CKgP, or

d)

a Grassmann bundle.

Then,
\mathcal{Y}
satisfies CKP. In the case b), also the converse holds.

Proof.

Point 1) follows directly from the definitions. Part b) of
2
follows because the pullback
\mathrm{CH}_{*}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathrm{CH}_{*}(\mathcal{Y})
is an isomorphism. Part c) follows from the excision formula, part a) follows from part b) and c). Finally, part d) follows from the formula
[
Ful98
, Proposition 14.6.5]
for Chow groups of Grassmann bundles.
∎

Lemma 4.18

For every

, we have

Proof.

Let

be the multiplication map

and let

We have a stratification of

where

is the locus of polynomials that are divisible by the square of a polynomial of degree

. Equivalently, it is the image of the natural map

. Moreover, by

, the pushforwards of the fundamental classes

lie in the ideal generated by two classes:

•

(4g+2)\xi_{2g+2}
whose pullback under

is equal to

•

is

In particular,

for all

. A point

is in

if and only if there exists polynomials

,
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
,

,

and

with

,

,
d_{2}:=\deg h_{1}=\deg h_{2}
, satisfying

, and such that
(f,g)=(uh^{2}h_{1}^{2}f_{1},uh_{2}^{2}g_{1})
or
(f,g)=(uh_{1}^{2}f_{1},uh^{2}h_{2}^{2}g_{1})

\underline{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})

the locus of points as above. If

is the image of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}}\times(\mathbb{P}^{d_{3}})^{2}\times(\mathbb{P}^{g+1-k+d_{2}})^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{g+1}\times\mathbb{P}^{g+1},}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(u,(h_{1},h_{2}),(f_{1},g_{1}))\mapsto(uh_{1}^{2}f_{1},uh_{2}^{2}g_{1}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\underline{d}}}

If

, then

is the image of the morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{P}^{d_{2}}\times(\mathbb{P}^{d_{3}})^{2}\times((\mathbb{P}^{g+1-k-d_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}^{g+1-k+d_{2}})\sqcup(\mathbb{P}^{g+1-k+d_{2}}\times\mathbb{P}^{g+1-k-d_{2}}))}

5
Application: Hyperelliptic Prym pairs of odd genus

In this section we prove Proposition
1.8
, providing an identification of the irreducible components of the moduli stack
\mathcal{SH}_{g}
of hyperelliptic Spin curves with those of
\mathcal{RH}_{g}
and
\mathcal{H}_{g}
.

Before starting the proof, let us recall a useful fact. Given a (representable) finite, étale morphism
\phi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
of algebraic stacks and a universal homeomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
, there exists a section
s:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
to
\phi
if and only if there exists
\rho:\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
such that
\phi\circ\rho=\psi
. Notice that we are not asking
\psi
to be representable. The set of such
\rho
is in bijection with the set of sections
s^{\prime}
to the finite, étale cover
\phi^{\prime}
in the following cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

As
s^{\prime}
is a section to a finite, étale morphism, it is an open and closed immersion, hence
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\sqcup(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{Y}^{\prime})
, where we identified
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
with its image under
s^{\prime}
. As
\psi^{\prime}
is an homeomorphism,
\mathcal{X}\cong\mathcal{X}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{X}_{2}
with
\mathcal{X}_{1}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
. It follows that
\mathcal{X}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is finite, étale and of degree 1, hence an isomorphism, thus yielding an inverse.

Proof of Proposition
1.8
.

Consider the universal commutative diagram over
\mathcal{H}_{g}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{H}_{g}
is a Brauer–Severi scheme of relative dimension
1
. There exists a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe
\psi:\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{g}
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}:=\mathcal{P}\times_{\mathcal{H}_{g}}\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{g}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
is a trivial Brauer-Severi scheme, that is, there exists a rank-2 vector bundle
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}
over
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\cong\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\mathcal{E}})
. Such a gerbe
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
may be constructed by taking the image the Brauer class
[\mathcal{P}]\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{H}_{g},\mathrm{PGL}_{2})

\widehat{f}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)

Remark 5.1
.

We can explicitly describe the section to
\mathcal{SH}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{g}
as follows. If
C
is an hyperelliptic curve over an algebraically closed field, and
f:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a double cover with Weierstrass divisor
W
, then every theta characteristic is of the form

f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\otimes(\frac{g-1}{2}+n)}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(-w_{1}-\ldots-w_{2n})

for some
0\leq n\leq(g-1)/2
and
w_{i}\in W
distinct. Then, the statement of Proposition
1.8
is essentially equivalent to saying that the number
n
is a deformation invariant, and the section is given by the ‘isolated’ theta characteristic
f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}((g-1)/2)
. Given a family
C\xrightarrow{f}P\rightarrow S
of hyperelliptic curves realized as a double cover of a Brauer-Severi scheme
P\rightarrow S
of relative dimension 1, to construct the map
S\rightarrow\mathcal{SH}_{g}
one can work étale-locally on
S
, to trivialize
P
and construct the root. The first step is replaced by a global construction in the proof of Proposition
1.8
, and it is not necessary if
g
is congruent to 1 modulo 4, as the candidate section can be taken to be
f^{*}\omega_{P/S}^{-\frac{g-1}{4}}
in this case.

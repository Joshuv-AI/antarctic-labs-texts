Contrasting effects of mineral, farmyard manure, and integrated fertilization on soybean productivity, seed quality, and soil biochemical properties
Authors: Emrah Ramazanoglu, Vedat Beyyavas, Suat Cun, Zehra Bozdag Karakeci, Songül Akdag, Zemzem Firat, Cevher İlhan Cevheri, Erdal Sakin, Ahmet Çelik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMineral and organic fertilizers differ in nutrient-release patterns and effects on soil biological processes, yet their combined influence on soybean productivity, seed composition, and soil biochemical functioning remains unclear. This study evaluated soybean and soil responses to mineral, farmyard manure (FYM), and combined fertilization over two growing seasons. A two-year field experiment was conducted in a randomized complete block design with three replicates. Eight fertilizer treatments, i.e., unfertilized control, triple superphosphate, 20–20 (20% N and 20% P₂O₅), 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and FYM at 40, 80, or 120 t ha⁻¹ were included in the study. Agronomic traits, chlorophyll index (SPAD), normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), seed protein and oil contents, 100-seed weight, seed yield, soil enzyme activities, and soil NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N were recorded. Data were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models, and Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons were used to compare means. Fertilizer treatment significantly affected all plant and soil variables. The 20–20 + urea treatment produced the highest number of pods and nodes plant− 1 and the highest urease activity. FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest seed yields, followed by FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹. Likewise, FYM + urea produced the highest seed protein content, whereas TSP, 20–20, and FYM-only treatments generally favored seed oil content. FYM at 40–120 t ha⁻¹ increased catalase activity, and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest dehydrogenase activity and soil NO₃⁻-N concentration. Yield was strongly associated with 100-seed weight and dehydrogenase activity (r = 0.83 for both), SPAD (r = 0.78), and catalase activity (r = 0.73), whereas seed protein and oil contents were negatively correlated (r = − 0.87). PCA associated FYM at 80–120 t ha⁻¹ with yield, seed weight, mineral N, and soil enzyme activity, whereas FYM + urea and 20–20 + urea were more closely related to vegetative growth, NDVI, pod production, and seed protein content. The fertilizer treatments exhibited different responses among the measured plant and soil variables. FYM + urea and the higher FYM rates resulted in high soybean yield, greater seed weight, improved canopy performance, and enhanced soil biological activity, whereas 20–20 + urea mainly favored reproductive traits, urease activity, and soil NH₄⁺-N. Thus, integrated and organic nutrient inputs can improve both productivity and short-term soil biochemical functioning, but the responses should be interpreted as treatment-package effects, as the nutrient inputs were not equivalent.Keywords: Glycine max, Farmyard manure, Integrated nutrient management, Mineral nitrogen, Seed quality, Soil enzyme activity

Introduction

Soybean [Glycine max (L.) Merr.] is a strategic crop for human nutrition, the feed industry, and vegetable oil production due to its high protein and oil content [1, 2]. Moreover, it contributes to soil fertility in crop rotation systems through its capacity for biological nitrogen fixation [3, 4]. However, yield and quality of soybean are strongly associated with symbiotic nitrogen fixation, as well as with environmental conditions and the soil’s existing nutrient status [5, 6]. Nutrient uptake and remobilization significantly shape protein accumulation and final product quality in modern cultivars, particularly through nitrogen supply during the grain-filling period [7, 8]. On the other hand, increasing nitrogen (N) use in agriculture is associated with environmental losses, including nitrate leaching and N₂O emissions [9]. Therefore, nutrient management approaches that simultaneously optimize yield and ensure environmental safety have become critical in sustainable production systems [10, 11].

Mineral, organic, and combined fertilization strategies affect short-term nutrient availability and long-term soil health through different mechanisms. While mineral fertilizers support early vegetative growth by providing rapidly available N and phosphorus (P) [7, 12], organic inputs can provide more persistent gains by increasing soil organic matter and improving water retention and nutrient buffering capacity [13, 14]. For this reason, integrated nutrient management aims to strengthen both yield and soil functionality by combining the rapid effect of mineral sources with the time-distributed contribution of organic materials [15–18]. However, the C/N ratio and application rates of organic sources can substantially influence short-term N-availability and soil nutrient concentrations by determining the mineralization–immobilization balance [19].

Growth and yield-related traits of soybean, including the number of pods and seeds plant-1, and seed size, exhibit varying sensitivity to nutrient and water conditions. Phosphorus supply is known to have significant effects on root structure and vegetative development [20, 21]. Morphological characteristics such as first pod height and plant height have been reported to directly affect harvest efficiency depending on production systems [22, 23]. Sustainability depends not only on crop productivity but also on how fertilizer management influences soil biological functioning and N-transformation processes that support the long-term resilience of the production system. Soil enzymes are regarded as sensitive indicators of microbial metabolism and nutrient cycles. Urease and dehydrogenase activities are closely associated with nitrogen transformation and organic matter decomposition [24, 25].

This study hypothesized that combining mineral fertilizer with farmyard manure would provide complementary nutrient-release patterns, with mineral fertilizer supplying readily available nutrients during early crop development and manure contributing to sustained nutrient availability through gradual mineralization [15, 18]. A two-year field experiment compared mineral, organic, and combined fertilization strategies with respect to soybean growth, yield components, harvest-related morphological traits, soil enzyme activities, and mineral nitrogen fractions to test this hypothesis. The integrated assessment was designed to determine whether improvements in crop performance were accompanied by favorable changes in soil biological functioning and nitrogen dynamics.

Materials and methods

Study area and climatic data

The field experiment was conducted over a two-year period, in 2021 and 2022. The trial was established on a farmer’s field located in Külünçe village, Harran district, Şanlıurfa province (Fig. 1) at 38°03′26″ N latitude and 38°52′25″ E longitude. Continental climatic conditions prevail in Şanlıurfa province, with hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters. In addition, the difference between day and night temperatures in the region is considerably high (Fig. 1).

Study area and long-term climatic data of the study site

The experiment was established independently in a different part of the experimental site in each year. New plots were prepared, and treatments were re-randomized according to the experimental design; therefore, no physical plot was measured repeatedly across years.

Soil properties

Soil samples were collected from different places (0–20 cm depth) in the experimental site each year before initiating the experiments. A composite soil sample was prepared each year by mixing the sub-samples. Soil samples were air-dried and sieved to a 2 mm mesh before analysis. Particle size distribution was recorded using the hydrometer method [26]. Soil pH was analyzed in a 1:2.5 soil-to-deionized water solution using a calibrated glass electrode pH meter (WTW InoLab pH 720, Germany) as per ISO 10,390 (2005). The suspension was stabilized for 30 min with occasional stirring before evaluation at 25 °C.

Electrical conductivity (EC) was measured in a 1:5 soil-to-deionized water extract using a conductivity meter (WTW Cond 720, Germany) [27]. Cation exchange capacity (CEC) was determined using the 1 M ammonium acetate extraction method at pH 7.0. The adsorbed ammonium ions were subsequently displaced and quantified, and CEC was expressed as cmol kg⁻¹ soil. The Walkley–Black dichromate oxidation method [28] was used to determine organic matter as a percentage. The calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) concentration was recorded using a Scheibler calcimeter [29] by converting CO₂ produced after 1 N HCl treatment to a percentage of CaCO₃ equivalent. The total nitrogen (N) concentration was quantified using high-temperature dry combustion utilizing the LECO TruSpec CN Analyzer (LECO Corp., St. Joseph, MI, USA) [30]. Plant-available phosphorus (P) was extracted by the Olsen technique [31] with 0.5 M NaHCO₃ (pH 8.5) at a soil-to-extractant ratio of 1:20. Suspensions were stirred for 30 min, filtered, and then evaluated calorimetrically at 880 nm using the molybdenum blue–ascorbic acid technique on a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-1800, Japan). Exchangeable potassium (K) from the NH₄OAc extract was quantified directly by inductively coupled plasma–optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) (PerkinElmer Optima 8000, USA) [32].

The soil characteristics of the experimental area were generally similar in 2021 and 2022. The soil was clay-textured, containing 59.11–59.38% clay, 28.41–29.53% silt, and 11.36–12.21% sand. It had a slightly alkaline reaction, with pH values of 7.94 and 7.98, and was non-saline, with electrical conductivity values of 0.79 and 0.81 dS m⁻¹ in 2021 and 2022, respectively. The soil had a relatively high cation exchange capacity, increasing from 34.10 to 36.84 cmol kg⁻¹, and a high calcium carbonate content of 21.64–29.12%. Organic matter content was low, ranging from 0.72 to 0.80%, whereas total nitrogen content varied from 0.42 to 0.44%. Available potassium decreased from 178.2 mg kg⁻¹ in 2021 to 165.9 mg kg⁻¹ in 2022, while available phosphorus remained low and relatively stable, with values of 4.86 and 4.71 mg kg⁻¹, respectively.

Crop sowing and management

The ‘GAPSOY-16’ soybean cultivar was used in the current study. It is well adapted to regional conditions and widely preferred by producers. The seeds were obtained from the GAP Agricultural Research Institute. The study was conducted using a randomized complete block design with three replications (n = 3). Each experimental plot consisted of four rows, each 6 m in length. Rows were spaced 70 cm apart, while plants within rows were established at a target spacing of 4 cm, resulting in a plant population of approximately 357,143 plants ha⁻¹. A distance of 3 m was left between blocks and 3 m between plots, and the experiment consisted of a total of 24 plots.

Soybean was sown on 17 June and harvested on 16 October in 2021, whereas sowing and harvest were conducted on 19 June and 18 October, respectively, in 2022. Pre-sowing irrigation was applied to ensure adequate soil moisture for seedbed preparation and crop establishment. Following emergence, eight additional drip-irrigation events were applied during each growing season, resulting in a total of nine irrigations. Irrigation timing was adjusted based on crop development, prevailing weather conditions, and visual assessments of soil moisture. Approximately 600 mm of irrigation water (equivalent to 6000 m³ ha⁻¹) was supplied during each growing season. Irrigation was reduced as the crop approached physiological maturity. All fertilizer treatments, including the unfertilized control, received the same irrigation schedule and equal amounts of water.

Following harvest of the preceding wheat crop, crop residues were removed, and the soil was prepared by disking and cultivation, followed by leveling with a land roller. Pre-sowing irrigation was applied to ensure adequate soil moisture for seedbed preparation and crop establishment. Soybean was then sown with a pneumatic seeder under suitable soil and weather conditions. All plots were irrigated uniformly using a drip irrigation system supplied by a nearby deep well. Mechanical hoeing was carried out with a tractor at the V5 growth stage to control weeds, improve soil aeration, and break the surface crust. Harvesting was performed at physiological maturity, when most pods had turned yellow, and seed moisture had declined to a level suitable for harvest.

Fertilizer applications and types and amounts of fertilizers used

Seven fertilized treatments and an unfertilized control were included in the study. A control (F0) treatment was included in the study to compare the effects of the fertilizer treatments. No basal or top-dressing fertilization was done in the control plots. The fertilizer types used in the experiment, the amounts of N and P₂O₅ contained in these fertilizers, and the basal and top-dressing application rates are presented in detail in Table 1.

FYM farmyard manure, TSP triple superphosphate

The farmyard manure (FYM) used in the experiment was obtained from the NAMET Livestock Farm in Şanlıurfa, Türkiye, and consisted of cattle manure fermented for six months before application. The FYM contained 1.43% total N, 0.18% P₂O₅, 0.33% K, 0.12% Ca, and 0.10% Mg on a dry-weight basis. The moisture content, dry-matter content, organic C concentration, and C ratio of the FYM were not measured; therefore, nutrient-release and mineralization patterns could not be evaluated precisely.

The FYM was applied at 40, 80, and 120 t ha⁻¹ to establish a broad experimental gradient for evaluating soybean and soil responses to increasing organic inputs under the low-organic-matter conditions of the experimental soil. These rates were not intended to represent nutrient-equivalent comparisons with mineral fertilizer treatments or recommended commercial application rates. The nutrient inputs presented in Table 1 were calculated from the total nutrient concentrations of the applied fertilizers and manure. Therefore, the reported N and P values represent total amounts applied rather than immediately plant-available nutrient inputs. No mineralization or nutrient-availability coefficient was applied when calculating these values.

FYM was applied 2 weeks before sowing, uniformly distributed across the assigned plots, and incorporated into the upper 20 cm of soil during seedbed preparation. The basal mineral fertilizers, including TSP and 20–20 fertilizer, were applied at sowing and incorporated into the soil to a depth of ~ 20 cm. Urea was applied as a top dressing in two equal doses. The first half was applied at the V4 stage, when four fully developed trifoliate leaves were present on the main stem, and the remaining half was applied at the V6 stage, when six fully developed trifoliate leaves were present. Each application was followed by irrigation to facilitate nitrogen movement into the root zone and reduce surface losses. No fertilizer was applied to the control plots.

Agronomic and biochemical traits

Agronomic and yield-related traits were measured using standard procedures. Seed yield was determined by harvesting plants from the two central rows of each plot. The harvested material was mechanically threshed, and the seeds were cleaned and weighed. Plot-level seed yield was then converted to kilograms per hectare (kg ha⁻¹). Plant height was measured at harvest maturity from 20 randomly selected plants in the two central rows of each plot as the distance from the soil surface to the uppermost point of the main stem. Number of pods plant-1 was determined by counting all pods on the same plants, while number of seeds plant-1 was determined by counting the seeds produced by each sampled plant. The measurements were averaged to obtain a plot-level value for each trait.

The 100-seed weight was determined by randomly selecting and weighing four subsamples of 100 seeds from each plot, and the measurements were then averaged to obtain a plot-level value. Number of branches plant-1 was recorded on 20 mature plants per plot by counting the fruit-bearing branches arising from the main stem. First pod height was measured on the same plants as the vertical distance from the soil surface to the lowest pod. Number of nodes plant-1 was determined by counting the nodes on 20 randomly selected plants from each plot. For each trait, individual plant measurements were averaged to obtain a single plot-level value.

Seed oil content was determined by Soxhlet extraction. For each plot, a 5-g subsample of dried and finely ground seed material was extracted with n-hexane at approximately 70 °C for 6 h, corresponding to approximately 10 siphon cycles. Three independent analytical replicates were analyzed per biological sample. After extraction, the solvent was removed, and the recovered oil was dried to constant mass and expressed as a percentage of seed dry weight [33].

Seed protein content was estimated from total seed nitrogen concentration, determined by the Kjeldahl wet-digestion method. The measured nitrogen concentration was multiplied by a conversion factor of 6.25, and the resulting protein content was expressed as a percentage [34].

Leaf chlorophyll index was measured non-destructively at the full-bloom stage (R2; peak flowering) using a Konica Minolta SPAD-502 m. Measurements were conducted between 08:00 and 10:00 on 20 randomly selected plants from the two central rows of each plot. Three readings were taken from the central portion of the uppermost fully expanded trifoliolate leaf of each plant, avoiding the midrib and leaf margins. The 60 readings obtained from each plot were averaged to calculate one plot-level SPAD value [35].

The normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) was measured at the same growth stage (i.e., R2) and during the same morning period using a GreenSeeker Handheld Crop Sensor. The sensor was held horizontally at ~ 80 cm above the canopy and moved at a constant walking speed along each of the two central rows. Two continuous passes were completed per plot. Measurements recorded while entering and leaving the plot were excluded, and all valid readings from the two passes were averaged to obtain one plot-level NDVI value [36].

Soil enzyme activities and nitrogen forms

Soil dehydrogenase activity was measured based on the determination of triphenyl formazan (TPF) formed after incubating soil samples amended with triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) at 25 °C for 24 h. The formed TPF was evaluated spectrophotometrically at a wavelength of 485 nm [37]. Urease enzyme activity was determined by measuring the amount of ammonium released through urea hydrolysis after incubating soil samples at 37 °C for 1 h. Measurements were performed at 578 nm, and analyses were conducted according to the method of Tabatabai [37]. Soil catalase activity was determined based on the volumetric measurement of oxygen released as a result of hydrogen peroxide decomposition [38].

Soil samples were collected at harvest in each growing season. From each plot, 10 soil cores were collected from the 0–30 cm soil layer within the central plot area, avoiding plot borders and fertilizer application bands. The subsamples from each plot were thoroughly mixed to produce a single composite sample, yielding three independent composite samples per treatment each year. Samples were placed in polyethylene bags, transported to the laboratory in a cooled container, and passed through a 2-mm sieve after visible roots, stones, and plant residues had been removed. Fresh samples were stored at 4 °C and analyzed within 10 days. A separate subsample was oven-dried at 105 °C to determine gravimetric moisture content, and all mineral N concentrations were corrected to a dry-soil basis.

Extractable NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N were determined using 2 M KCl at a soil-to-extractant ratio of 1:4 (w/v). Samples were shaken for 1 h and filtered. Ammonium was determined by reaction with buffer, salicylate–nitroprusside, and hypochlorite solutions, followed by spectrophotometric measurement at 650 nm after 45 min [39]. Nitrate was determined from the KCl extract following reaction with TRI solution and concentrated H₂SO₄, subsequent addition of NaOH, and spectrophotometric measurement at 410 nm [40]. The results were expressed as mg NH₄⁺-N kg⁻¹ dry soil and mg NO₃⁻-N kg⁻¹ dry soil.

Statistical analyses

Data were analyzed using linear mixed-effects models implemented in the ‘lmerTest’ package [41] in R (version 4.5.2) [42]. The statistical model was:

where Yi is the fixed effect of year, Tⱼ is the fixed effect of fertilizer treatment, (Y × T)iⱼ is their interaction, Bₖ(i) is the random effect of block nested within year, and εiⱼₖ is the residual error. Type III analysis of variance was performed using the Kenward–Roger approximation for denominator degrees of freedom to test the significance of year, fertilizer treatment, and year × treatment interaction effects.

Estimated marginal means were calculated using the ‘emmeans package’ [43], and pairwise comparisons were conducted using Tukey adjustment for means comparison. When the year × treatment interaction was significant, fertilizer treatments were compared separately within each year. Nevertheless, treatment means were averaged across years before multiple comparisons when the interaction was non-significant.

Model assumptions were assessed by inspecting residual-versus-fitted and normal quantile–quantile plots. Residual normality was additionally evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test [44]. Pearson correlation analysis was used to examine relationships among agronomic, physiological, yield, and soil variables. Principal component analysis was performed using standardized variables to summarize multivariate treatment responses.

Results

Fertilizer treatments significantly affected all measured soybean and soil variables (Table 2; P < 0.05). The year effect was significant for plant height, first pod height, and seed oil content, whereas it was non-significant for the remaining variables. Significant year × treatment interactions were recorded for plant height, number of pods, seeds and nodes plant⁻¹, first pod height, NDVI, seed protein and oil contents, 100-seed weight, urease and dehydrogenase activities, and soil NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N concentrations. In contrast, the interaction was non-significant for number of branches plant⁻¹, SPAD, seed yield, and catalase activity, indicating consistent treatment responses across the two growing seasons; these variables were therefore evaluated using pooled treatment means.

Agronomic traits

Fertilizer application significantly influenced agronomic performance of soybean, although the response varied among traits and growing seasons (Fig. 2). Plant height increased in response to fertilizer application compared with the unfertilized control in both years (Fig. 2a). The longest plant height was recorded under the 20–20 + urea treatment during 2021, with comparable values observed for 20–20, FYM + urea, and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹. Similarly, 20–20 + urea was the highest-performing treatment in 2022, while FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ also maintained better plant height relative to the control.

The number of seeds plant⁻¹ was strongly affected by fertilizer treatment in both growing seasons (Fig. 2b). FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest seed number during 2021, followed by FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ and 20–20 + urea, whereas the control consistently recorded the lowest values. Nevertheless, FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ resulted in the greatest seed production during 2022, followed by FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹, 20–20 + urea, and FYM + urea.

First pod height responded similarly to fertilizer application (Fig. 2c). 20–20 + urea produced the highest first pod height during 2021, whereas FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ exhibited comparable responses. 20–20 + urea, FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹, and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ had the highest first pod heights in 2022, while the unfertilized control consistently showed the lowest values.

Pod production increased significantly with fertilizer application in both years (Fig. 2d). 20–20 + urea consistently produced the highest number of pods plant⁻¹, whereas the control and TSP treatments generally produced fewer pods. The remaining fertilizer treatments showed intermediate responses, indicating that integrated nutrient management enhanced reproductive development.

The year × treatment interaction was non-significant for the number of branches plant⁻¹; therefore, treatment means were evaluated across years (Fig. 2e). Fertilizer application slightly increased the number of branches compared with the control, although treatment differences were relatively small. TSP and 20–20 + urea produced the highest number of branches, whereas the control produced the lowest.

The number of nodes plant⁻¹ increased following fertilizer application in both growing seasons (Fig. 2f). 20–20 + urea produced the greatest number of nodes, followed by FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹, while the unfertilized control recorded the lowest values. The 20–20 + urea treatment and the higher FYM rates were associated with relatively high values for several vegetative and reproductive traits.

Biochemical and yield parameters

Fertilizer treatments produced significant differences in soybean physiological performance, seed quality, and yield (Fig. 3). The highest SPAD values were recorded for FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹, followed by 20–20 + urea and FYM + urea during both years, whereas the unfertilized control showed the lowest chlorophyll index (Fig. 3a).

Seed protein content varied between treatments in both growing seasons (Fig. 3b). FYM + urea produced the highest protein content and was statistically comparable to 20–20 + urea during 2021. Similarly, FYM + urea recorded the highest protein concentration during 2022, followed by 20–20 + urea. The lowest protein values were generally observed under 20–20 and FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹.

The 100-seed weight was improved by both mineral and organic fertilizer treatments (Fig. 3c). The highest 100-seed weight was recorded for 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ in 2021. Similarly, the heaviest 100-seed weights were recorded under FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ during 2022, while 20–20 + urea and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ produced comparable values. The unfertilized control consistently had the lowest seed weight.

The highest NDVI was recorded for 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and the two higher FYM rates in 2021 (Fig. 3d). In 2022, 20–20, 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹, and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ formed the highest statistical group, whereas the control, TSP, and FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ showed lower canopy vegetation indices.

Seed oil content responded differently from protein content (Fig. 3e). The highest oil concentrations were obtained with TSP, 20–20, and FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ during 2021, while FYM + urea produced the lowest value. On the other hand, TSP, 20–20, and all FYM-only treatments resulted in the highest oil contents during 2022, whereas lower oil contents were recorded in the control, 20–20 + urea, and FYM + urea treatments.

The highest seed yield was recorded under FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ across years, with FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ producing a statistically comparable yield (Fig. 3f). The 20–20 + urea and FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ treatments produced intermediate yields, whereas the unfertilized control had the lowest seed yield. Overall, the combined FYM–urea treatment and higher FYM rates yielded the most favorable responses in seed yield, protein content, seed weight, and canopy performance, although these treatments were not consistently associated with higher seed oil content.

Urease activity varied between years but followed a generally similar treatment pattern (Fig. 4b). The highest urease activity was recorded for 20–20 + urea during both years. The higher FYM rates, particularly FYM at 80 and 120 t ha⁻¹, also maintained relatively high urease activity, while the control consistently produced the lowest values.

Dehydrogenase activity was highest under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ in both growing seasons (Fig. 4c). The 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and the lower FYM rates produced intermediate activities, whereas the control, TSP, and 20–20 treatments generally remained in the lowest statistical group. Overall, the FYM-only treatments favored catalase and dehydrogenase activities, while urease activity responded most strongly to the mineral N-containing 20–20 + urea treatment.

Fertilizer treatments significantly altered soil NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N concentrations in both years (Fig. 5). The highest soil NH₄⁺-N was recorded for 20–20 + urea in both years, reaching ~ 14.7 and 16.0 mg N kg⁻¹ dry soil, respectively (Fig. 5a). FYM at 40 and 120 t ha⁻¹ produced the next highest concentrations during 2021, whereas FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ showed a lower value. FYM + urea ranked second during 2022, while the FYM-only treatments produced intermediate and statistically comparable NH₄⁺-N concentrations. Overall, the mineral N-rich 20–20 + urea treatment favored NH₄⁺-N accumulation, whereas the highest FYM rate promoted markedly greater NO₃⁻-N accumulation.

Seed yield was most strongly and positively correlated with 100-seed weight and dehydrogenase activity (r = 0.83 for both), followed by SPAD (r = 0.78), catalase activity (r = 0.73), urease activity (r = 0.69), soil NO₃⁻-N (r = 0.67), and NDVI (r = 0.66). Discussion

Agronomic traits

The fertilizer treatments exhibited varying effects on soybean vegetative and reproductive development, and most responses varied between growing seasons. Significant year × treatment interactions for plant height, number of seeds, pods, and nodes plant− 1, and first pod height indicate that treatment performance was partly dependent on seasonal conditions. In contrast, the non-significant interaction for number of branches plant− 1 suggests a comparatively consistent treatment response across years. These results demonstrate that fertilizer effects cannot be interpreted through a single response pattern because readily available nutrients from mineral fertilizers and the gradual mineralization of FYM may influence different stages of crop development [13, 18, 45, 46]. However, the treatments also differed substantially in total N and P inputs; therefore, the observed responses represent the effects of the complete fertilizer packages rather than fertilizer source alone.

The longest plants were generally recorded under 20–20 + urea, although 20–20, FYM + urea, and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced comparable values in 2021, and FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced taller plants in 2022. This pattern indicates that soybean stem growth responded strongly to treatments supplying readily available mineral N, either alone or together with FYM. Mineral N can promote leaf expansion, photosynthetic capacity, and stem elongation during early vegetative development [47, 48]. Nevertheless, the absence of a proportional increase in plant height with increasing FYM rate suggests that total nutrient input alone did not determine this trait. Nutrient mineralization, temporary microbial immobilization, soil moisture, and seasonal temperature may have influenced the availability of manure-derived N during crop development [49–52].

The higher pod number recorded under the 20–20 + urea treatment suggests that readily available mineral N may have contributed to reproductive development; however, this response should be interpreted carefully because the treatments differed in total nutrient inputs. The 20–20 + urea treatment produced the highest number of pods plant− 1 in both years, whereas the unfertilized control and TSP generally produced fewer pods. The response suggests that the combined supply of N and P, followed by top-dressed urea, supported reproductive development more effectively than P application alone. However, because soybean can meet part of its N demand through biological fixation, excessive mineral N does not necessarily result in proportional reproductive gains, and responses may depend on initial soil fertility, nodulation, cultivar, and seasonal conditions [53–55].

The number of seeds plant− 1 followed a different treatment pattern than the number of pods plant− 1. FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest number of seeds plant− 1 in 2021, whereas FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest value in 2022, followed by FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹, 20–20 + urea, and FYM + urea. The relatively favorable seed production under FYM treatments may reflect improved nutrient availability during reproductive development, together with possible effects of organic matter on soil water retention and microbial nutrient cycling [56]. Nevertheless, the response was not linear with manure rate or year, indicating that increasing FYM quantity did not consistently increase the number of seeds plant− 1. Differences in total nutrient input and year-specific mineralization conditions must therefore be considered when interpreting the advantage of the organic treatments.

TSP and 20–20 + urea produced the highest number of branches plant− 1 (pooled across years), although the differences among treatments were relatively small. This response is consistent with previous studies showing that soybean branching can vary across different nutrient management regimes [21, 57]. Since the treatments differed in total N and P inputs, the observed variation cannot be attributed to P supply or fertilizer source. First pod height was generally greater under 20–20 + urea and FYM applied at 80 or 120 t ha⁻¹. These differences should also be interpreted carefully owing to unequal nutrient inputs. Nevertheless, greater first pod height may have practical relevance for mechanical harvesting because pods positioned close to the soil surface are more likely to remain below the cutter bar and contribute to harvest losses [22, 23].

The higher number of nodes plant− 1 recorded under 20–20 + urea suggests that readily available mineral N may have influenced soybean shoot development. Mineral N can stimulate shoot growth and stem elongation during early developmental stages, thereby affecting plant morphology and node formation [3, 12]. It may also support leaf development, photosynthetic capacity, and biomass accumulation, which can further modify shoot architecture and vegetative structure [7, 58]. However, this response should be interpreted cautiously because the fertilizer treatments differed substantially in total nutrient inputs.

Collectively, the agronomic results indicate that the fertilizer treatments produced different response patterns across growth and yield-related traits. The 20–20 + urea treatment was associated with relatively high plant height, number of pods and nodes plant− 1, and first pod height, whereas FYM treatments generally produced higher number of seeds plant− 1. These contrasting responses show that the tested fertilizer packages influenced vegetative structure, reproductive development, and seed formation in different ways.

Physiological, seed-quality, and yield responses

Fertilizer management also affected canopy characteristics, seed composition, seed weight, and final yield. Significant year × treatment interactions for NDVI, protein content, oil content, and 100-seed weight indicate that these responses were influenced by seasonal conditions. In contrast, SPAD and seed yield showed consistent treatment patterns across years and were therefore interpreted using pooled means.

The highest SPAD value (pooled across years) was noted under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹, followed by 20–20 + urea and FYM + urea, whereas the unfertilized control produced the lowest value. Since SPAD is closely associated with leaf chlorophyll and N status, the high values under these treatments suggest greater maintenance of canopy greenness [59, 60]. High organic inputs may not increase SPAD values in the short term because nutrient release from organic materials depends on their decomposability and C: N characteristics. Early microbial immobilization may further delay the availability of manure-derived N to plants [58]. The relatively high SPAD value recorded under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ in the current study suggests greater maintenance of leaf chlorophyll content. Nevertheless, this response may partly reflect the greater total N and P inputs supplied by this treatment and cannot be attributed solely to the organic fertilizer source.

The highest NDVI values were noted under 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and the two higher FYM rates in 2021. In 2022, 20–20 was also included in the highest statistical group. NDVI reflects canopy greenness and ground cover rather than photosynthetic rate directly; therefore, the high values suggest greater canopy development but should not be interpreted as direct evidence of enhanced photosynthetic efficiency or production stability [61, 62]. Similarly, combined fertilization strategies have been reported to stabilize canopy development monitored through NDVI and to reduce yield fluctuations [63]. The year-dependent response further indicates that canopy expression under each fertilizer treatment was influenced by seasonal growing conditions [64, 65].

The highest seed protein content was recorded under FYM + urea in both years, with 20–20 + urea producing a statistically comparable value in 2021 and the second-highest value in 2022. This pattern is consistent with the importance of N availability during seed development, when absorbed N and remobilized vegetative N contribute to storage-protein synthesis [66, 67]. High organic inputs do not necessarily increase seed protein content, as the response depends on genotype, climatic conditions, and initial soil N availability [21]. The relatively high protein content recorded under FYM + urea in the current study may reflect the availability of mineral N from urea during early growth together with a later contribution from manure mineralization. Similar combined fertilization strategies have been reported to support N uptake and protein accumulation during seed development [68]. Nevertheless, nutrient-release dynamics were not measured directly, and the response should therefore be interpreted as an effect of the complete treatment package rather than evidence of synchronized N supply.

Seed oil content showed a contrasting response to treatments. TSP, 20–20, and FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹ produced relatively high oil contents in 2021, whereas TSP, 20–20, and all FYM-only treatments formed the higher statistical group in 2022. Phosphorus nutrition may support lipid biosynthesis through its roles in ATP production and triose-phosphate transport [69]. The lower oil content observed under FYM + urea, together with its higher protein content, is consistent with the commonly reported inverse relationship between seed protein and oil accumulation [70, 71]. Nevertheless, simultaneous increases in protein and oil have also been reported when N and P are supplied in an appropriate balance [56, 57]. Since nutrients differed among treatments, the oil response cannot be attributed solely to phosphorus supply or fertilizer source.

The highest 100-seed weights were recorded under 20–20 + urea, FYM + urea, and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ in 2021. FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest values in 2022, while 20–20 + urea and FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ were statistically comparable. These treatments may have supported assimilate supply and nutrient availability during seed filling, resulting in heavier seeds [12, 55]. However, the absence of a consistent dose-dependent response to FYM indicates that increasing manure application beyond 80 t ha⁻¹ did not consistently improve seed weight [72]. The differences in total nutrient supply among treatments are owed to the observed differences in 100-seed weight.

Seed yield was pooled across years because the year × treatment interaction was non-significant. FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest yields, while FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ was statistically comparable. These treatments also maintained relatively high SPAD and NDVI values, consistent with studies reporting positive associations between canopy indicators and soybean yield [73, 74]. Previous meta-analyses have shown that combined organic and mineral fertilization can increase mean crop yield and, under some conditions, reduce interannual yield variability [75, 76]. However, mineral fertilizers may produce stronger yield responses in the season of application because of their greater immediate nutrient availability, whereas longer-term outcomes are partly influenced by cumulative changes in soil organic matter, microbial biomass, microbial community structure, and enzyme activities [77, 78]. The combined approach has been shown to provide a balance between short-term yield gains and long-term soil functionality [46, 79]. FYM + urea was among the higher-yielding treatments in the current study, but it was not significantly higher, as the FYM treatments at 80 and 120 t ha⁻¹ produced comparable yields. Therefore, the results indicate a favorable short-term yield response to both combined and high-FYM treatments but do not provide direct evidence of long-term yield stability or improved soil functionality.

Relationships among plant and soil variables

Correlation analysis provided further insight into the traits associated with yield variation. Seed yield was strongly and positively correlated with 100-seed weight and dehydrogenase activity (r = 0.83 for both), SPAD (r = 0.78), catalase activity (r = 0.73), urease activity (r = 0.69), soil NO₃⁻-N (r = 0.67), and NDVI (r = 0.66). These relationships indicate that higher yields were observed in treatments with heavier seeds, greater canopy greenness, and greater soil biochemical activity. However, the correlations do not establish that any of these variables directly determined yield. Instead, they probably represent interconnected plant and soil responses to the fertilizer treatments.

The inverse correlation between seed protein and oil contents (r = − 0.87) confirms a pronounced quality trade-off within the dataset. Treatments favoring N accumulation and protein synthesis did not generally maximize oil concentration. This result highlights the need to consider the intended end use of soybean when selecting a fertilizer strategy, because treatments associated with higher yield or protein content may not also produce higher oil content.

The first two PCA axes explained 63.4% of the total variation. FYM at 80–120 t ha⁻¹ was associated with yield, seed weight, soil mineral N, and enzyme activities, whereas FYM + urea and 20–20 + urea were more closely related to vegetative growth, NDVI, pod production, and seed protein content. Conversely, the control, TSP, and 20–20 treatments were positioned opposite most growth–yield and soil-biochemical variables. These multivariate patterns support the univariate results by showing that high-FYM and combined treatments produced different response profiles rather than a common improvement across all traits.

Soil enzyme activities

The statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) observed in soil enzyme activities demonstrate that fertilizer treatments influenced soil enzyme activities and nutrient transformation processes. Recent studies have reported that organic and combined fertilizer applications enhance enzymatic activity by increasing carbon inputs and microbial substrate availability in soil [80, 81].

The highest catalase activities (pooled across years) were recorded for FYM at 40, 80, and 120 t ha⁻¹, whereas FYM + urea showed an intermediate response, and the control and 20–20 + urea showed the lowest values [25]. The higher activity under FYM-only treatments may therefore reflect the provision of organic substrates that supported microbial activity. However, this explanation remains inferential because microbial biomass and community composition were not measured directly [82]. Furthermore, the unequal nutrient and organic-matter inputs among treatments prevent separation of the effects of fertilizer source, nutrient quantity, and carbon addition.

Urease activity was highest under 20–20 + urea in both years. FYM at 80 and 120 t ha⁻¹ also maintained relatively high urease activity, whereas the control resulted in the lowest values. Since urease catalyzes the hydrolysis of urea to ammonia, its high activity under 20–20 + urea is consistent with the large quantity of urea applied in this treatment. Organic N must undergo microbial decomposition and mineralization before entering the soil mineral N pool, which may delay its availability relative to urea-derived N [49, 58]. Combined organic and mineral fertilization can therefore provide N pools with different release patterns. FYM + urea showed intermediate urease activity in the current study, which may reflect contributions from both urea hydrolysis and gradual mineralization of manure-derived N [49, 51]. As N transformation rates were not measured directly, this proposed mechanism remains inferential.

Dehydrogenase activity was highest under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ in both years, whereas FYM at 40 and 80 t ha⁻¹, FYM + urea, and 20–20 + urea produced intermediate responses. Since dehydrogenase is an intracellular enzyme involved in microbial respiration, its activity is commonly used as an indicator of overall microbial metabolic activity in soil [25]. Organic amendments may increase dehydrogenase activity by supplying readily decomposable carbon and other substrates for heterotrophic microorganisms [83]. Accordingly, the high activity observed under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ may reflect the greater organic-material input. Nevertheless, this treatment also supplied substantially greater amounts of N and P; therefore, the response cannot be attributed exclusively to the addition of organic matter or to the fertilizer source.

Soil mineral nitrogen

The differences in soil NH₄⁺-N and NO₃⁻-N indicate that the fertilizer treatments altered mineral N pools and associated transformation processes. Organic N must first be mineralized to NH₄⁺, which can subsequently be oxidized to NO₃⁻ through nitrification. The relative abundance of these forms is influenced by fertilizer composition, application timing, soil temperature, moisture, aeration, and sampling time [84].

The highest NH₄⁺-N concentration was noted for 20–20 + urea in both years, reaching ~ 14.7 mg N kg⁻¹ dry soil in 2021 and 16.0 mg N kg⁻¹ dry soil in 2022. This result is consistent with the rapid hydrolysis of urea and the resulting formation of NH₄⁺ in soil [10, 84]. FYM at 40 and 120 t ha⁻¹ produced the next-highest concentrations in 2021, whereas FYM + urea ranked second in 2022. These year-specific differences suggest that mineralization, nitrification, crop uptake, and environmental conditions influenced the persistence of NH₄⁺ after fertilizer application [85].

Soil NO₃⁻-N exhibited a different pattern. FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest concentration in both years, reaching ~ 180 mg N kg⁻¹ dry soil in 2021 and 156 mg N kg⁻¹ dry soil in 2022. FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ ranked second in 2022, followed by FYM at 40 t ha⁻¹, whereas mineral fertilizer treatments generally produced lower NO₃⁻-N concentrations than the higher FYM rates. The large NO₃⁻ pool under FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ indicates substantial mineralization and nitrification of manure-derived N, but it should not be interpreted solely as a beneficial increase in nutrient availability. Residual NO₃⁻ beyond crop demand may be vulnerable to leaching or denitrification, particularly under high rainfall or irrigation [10, 86].

Limitations

The principal limitation of this study is that the treatments were not nutrient-equivalent. Total N and P inputs differed among mineral, organic, and combined treatments, and only a fraction of manure-derived nutrients would have been available during the two-year experimental period. Consequently, differences cannot be attributed exclusively to fertilizer source or to mineral–organic interactions. The findings should instead be interpreted as comparisons among the fertilizer treatments tested. In addition, the two-year duration supports conclusions regarding short-term soil biochemical responses but does not establish long-term improvement in soil health or sustainability. Nutrient-equivalent, multi-year experiments incorporating soil N balance, gaseous and leaching losses, microbial biomass, and biological N fixation are needed to distinguish fertilizer-source effects and identify agronomically effective rates with lower environmental risk.

Conclusion

This study indicated that the tested mineral, organic, and combined fertilizer treatments produced distinct responses in soybean growth, seed composition, yield, and soil biochemical properties. No single treatment maximized all measured variables. The 20–20 + urea treatment was effective for plant height, number of pods and nodes plant− 1, first pod height, urease activity, and soil NH₄⁺-N, whereas FYM treatments were more strongly associated with the number of seeds plant− 1, 100-seed weight, catalase and dehydrogenase activities, and soil NO₃⁻-N. FYM + urea and FYM at 80 t ha⁻¹ produced the highest seed yields, while FYM at 120 t ha⁻¹ achieved a statistically comparable yield. Yield was positively associated with 100-seed weight, SPAD, NDVI, soil enzyme activities, and mineral N availability, indicating that crop performance was linked to both canopy development and soil biochemical functioning; however, these relationships should not be interpreted as causal. Overall, FYM + urea and the higher FYM rates provided the favorable balance between soybean productivity and short-term soil biological activity, whereas the highest FYM rate also led to substantial accumulation of soil NO₃⁻-N, which may increase the risk of N losses. Since the treatments did not have equivalent total N and P inputs, the results represent comparisons among fertilizer treatments rather than isolated fertilizer-source effects. Nutrient-equivalent, longer-term studies are therefore needed to identify rates that maintain yield benefits while limiting residual mineral N and associated environmental risks.

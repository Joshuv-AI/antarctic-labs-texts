Prevention on Offense: How the Drug-Free Communities Program Drives National Strategy to Solidify a Drug-Free America
Authors: Sara Carter
Published: 2026

The United States is at an historic turning point in our fight against the drug crisis. For the first time since before the pandemic, we are seeing a sustained, unprecedented decline in national overdose deaths—a 13.2% drop to 69,147 annual losses for the 12 months ending in January 2026. This progress is the direct result of the relentless, whole-of-government offense launched under the leadership of President Trump. Yet, although we are finally bending the curve, the chemistry of the drug threat is growing more complex and lethal. Synthetic opioids, primarily illicit fentanyl, still drive 54% of these tragedies, followed closely by psychostimulants like methamphetamine at 37%. We cannot relent. Although our administration aggressively attacks the illicit drug supply at every point of entry, a whole-of-society public health effort remains our most vital tool to collapse domestic demand and save American lives before addiction takes root.

For nearly 30 years, the Drug-Free Communities (DFC) Support Program has been the vanguard of this mission. Administered by the Office of National Drug Control Policy with support from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the DFC program represents a deliberate fusion of Federal oversight and local execution. Under this administration, we have supercharged this model, ensuring that Federal counterdrug resources directly empower grassroots coalitions to launch a proactive prevention offense on the home front. The 2026 National Drug Control Strategy recognizes that reducing the demand for illicit drugs is inseparable from slamming the door on their supply. President Trump has launched a decisive offensive against the narcoterrorists poisoning Americans, designating major cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations, and targeting them through Homeland Security Task Forces and Department of War support. But matching that supply-side pressure requires an equally relentless domestic effort. The Strategy makes it clear that primary prevention is not a passive public health placeholder; it is an operational cornerstone. Because more than 83% of Americans do not use illicit drugs, a drug-free life is already the prevailing national baseline. Our strategic goal is to aggressively promote and lock in that drug-free reality as the absolute social norm.

The DFC model succeeds because it rejects a Washington-knows-best approach. Local coalitions bring together parents, schools, law enforcement, healthcare providers, and crucially, faith-based organizations to deploy targeted, data-driven strategies unique to their zip codes. The Office of National Drug Control Policy does not just monitor these efforts from afar; we provide aggressive policy direction and real-time intelligence on emerging drug threats to ensure local coalitions are equipped to push back against nimble adversaries.

The Strategy is not a passive document—it is our order of battle, and the DFC program is one of its most direct expressions on the ground. By translating national directives into localized defense, DFC coalitions do not merely implement policy; they weaponize it. They are the frontline shield protecting our children from a chemical assault that is being pushed via encrypted dark web marketplaces and localized digital delivery apps.

This administration demands strict accountability, and the DFC program delivers it. We do not fund coalitions to write passive reports; we fund them to drive measurable outcomes. This is prevention on offense: proactive, evidence-based, and relentless. When foreign adversaries and predatory networks attempt to exploit vulnerabilities in our communities, DFC coalitions are positioned to push them back.

Our latest program reporting shows just how aggressively these coalitions are leaning into the fight. In 2025, approximately 75% of DFC-funded coalitions directly targeted opioids, with 86% of those focusing their crosshairs squarely on fentanyl. This sharp focus mirrors our national advisory on deadly new poly-drug threats like medetomidine and cychlorphine, proving that DFC coalitions are rapidly adapting to meet the shifting chemistry of the street-level supply.

Youth engagement is central to this effort. We do not treat young people as passive targets for boilerplate antidrug messages; we draft them as active partners in the Strategy. By leading outreach and shaping peer-to-peer communication, youth leaders build a durable, resilient culture that flatly rejects drug use. This engagement builds lasting community leadership and public health awareness that protect our communities long after a federal grant cycle ends.

Beyond immediate program outcomes, youth engagement helps build long-term community resilience. Students who participate in coalition activities develop leadership skills, gain exposure to public health and civic processes, and contribute to a culture that rejects drug use. These are durable benefits that extend beyond the life of any single grant.

Senator Chuck Grassley, coauthor and champion of the Drug-Free Communities Act, helped Congress establish this program in 1997 because he saw the need for local solutions to the illicit drug crisis. “Local substance abuse trends require local solutions. That's why I helped create the Drug-Free Communities program nearly 30 years ago—modeled after Iowa's successful ‘Face It Together' anti-drug initiative,” Grassley said. “Thanks to the steadfast partnership of community leaders and the White House Office of National Drug Control Policy, the Drug Free Communities program has saved countless lives from drug use and addiction. I'm proud to see this program continue to benefit our nation's next generation, and I applaud Director Carter for her ongoing work to ensure American communities have the tools they need to combat drug abuse.”

Looking ahead, our adversaries will continue to innovate, using physical and digital perimeters to target younger populations. Staying ahead of them requires sustained investment in proven community infrastructure, a willingness to confront threats on every front, and leaders who resolutely refuse to accept the normalization of youth drug use in America.

Primary prevention is not a secondary effort. It is foundational to our national security and the long-term viability of our country. The DFC program has proven for nearly 3 decades that when American communities are properly resourced, trusted, and held to the highest standards, they deliver results. Under President Trump's administration, we will continue to wage this offensive on 2 equally important fronts—dismantling the supply abroad and eradicating the demand at home—protecting our next generation and ensuring a safer, drug-free America.

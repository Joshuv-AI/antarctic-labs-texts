Approximations of Extremal Eigenspace and Orthonormal Polar Factor
Ren-Cang Li

Abstract
This paper is concerned with two extremal problems from matrix analysis. One is about approximating the top eigenspaces of a Hermitian matrix and the other one about approximating the orthonormal polar factor of a general matrix. Tight error bounds on the quality of the approximations are obtained. Keywords: eigenspace; Hermitian matrix; orthonormal polar factor; error bound Mathematics Subject Classification 15A18, 15A42, 5B57, 65F15.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with two matrix extremal problems related to approximate the top eigenspaces of a Hermitian
matrix and the orthonormal polar factor of a matrix. Both problems will be stated in terms of certain matrix trace approximations.
The first problem is well-known in matrix analysis while the second one isn’t that much and only came up recently in
[
9
]
.
They played key roles on optimization on Stiefel manifolds
[
9
]
, where the results
on the problems are used for the convergence analysis of two newly established frameworks for numerical solutions
of most common optimization problems on Stiefel manifolds arising from various data science tasks.

Specifically, given a Hermitian matrix
H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and an orthonormal matrix
P\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
,
our first problem is to bound the error in the column subspace of
P
as an approximate eigenspace associated with
the
k
largest eigenvalues of
H
, in terms of the difference between trace
\tr(P^{\HH}HP)
and the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues.
There have been some results on this problem in the literature already
[
4
, Theorem 1]
,

[
17
, Chapter 3]
,
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
. Our second problem is about the error analysis of
approximating the orthonormal polar factor of a matrix
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
via approximating its
trace norm, also known as the nuclear norm, by
\tr(P^{\HH}B)
where
P\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
is orthonormal.
This second problem seems to appear for the first time in
[
9
]
.
Both problems are of interest in their own right.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In
section

2
, we introduce the canonical angles between
two subspaces of equal dimension.
Section

3
is about approximating the extremal eigenspaces of
a Hermitian matrix while
section

4
is about approximating the orthonormal polar factor of a tall matrix
by the means of approximating its nuclear norm. Finally, in
section

5
we draw our conclusions.

4
Orthonormal Polar Factor

In this section, we will bound the error in an approximation to

\arg\max_{P\in{\rm St}(k,n)}\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B)),

(4.1)

which will be shown to be an orthonormal polar factor of
B
,
where
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
and
k\leq n
, and
\Re(\cdot)
extracts the real part of a complex number.
For notation, we will denote the singular values of
B
by
\sigma_{i}(B)
for
1\leq i\leq k
and arranged in the descending order, i.e.,

\sigma_{1}(B)\geq\sigma_{1}(B)\geq\cdots\sigma_{k}(B),

the smallest singular value
\sigma_{\min}(B)=\sigma_{k}(B)
, and the trace norm
\|B\|_{\tr}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(B)
, also known as the nuclear norm.
The following von Neumann’s trace inequality is needed later in our proofs.

Lemma 4.1

(von Neumann’s trace inequality
[
14
]
,
[
3
, p.183]
)
.

For
B,\,C\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
, we have

|\tr(B^{\HH}C)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(B)\,\sigma_{i}(C).

The polar decomposition of
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}

refers to
B=P\Lambda
where
P\in{\rm St}(k,n)
and
0\preceq\Lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times k}
. It is known
that
\Lambda
is unique and in fact
\Lambda=(B^{\HH}B)^{1/2}
(the positive semidefinite square root of
B^{\HH}B
) but the orthonormal polar factor
P
is unique if and only if
\rank(B)=k

[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
, in which case
P=B(B^{\HH}B)^{-1/2}
.
The polar decomposition can be computed by the thin SVD:
B=U\Sigma V^{\HH}
, as

P=UV^{\HH}
and
\Lambda=V\Sigma V^{\HH}
,
where
U\in{\rm St}(k,n)
,
V\in{\rm St}(k,k)
, and

\Sigma=\diag(\sigma_{1}(B),\ldots,\sigma_{k}(B))\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
is diagonal with the singular values on the diagonal.

The next lemma are likely well-known. For example, its results are implied in the discussion in
[
15
]
before

[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
for the real number case (see also
[
9
, Lemma B.11]
,
[
10
]
).
We provide a proof here for self-containedness and also because it is stated for the complex number field.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
and
k\leq n
.

(a)

\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))\leq\|B\|_{\tr}
for any
P\in{\rm St}(k,n)
;

(b)

\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))=\|B\|_{\tr}
where
P\in{\rm St}(k,n)
if and only if
B=P\Lambda
with
\Lambda\succeq 0
, a polar decomposition;

(c)

We have

\max_{P\in{\rm St}(k,n)}\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))=\|B\|_{\tr}

and the optimal value
\|B\|_{\tr}
is achieved by
P=P_{*}
, an orthonormal polar factor of
B
.

Proof.

Item (a) is a consequence of
Lemma

4.1
:

\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))\leq|\tr(P^{\HH}B)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(P)\sigma_{i}(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}(B)=\|B\|_{\tr},

where we have used
\sigma_{i}(P)=1
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

For item (b), if
B=P\Lambda
with
\Lambda\succeq 0
,
then
\tr(P^{\HH}B)=\tr(\Lambda)=\|B\|_{\tr}
. On the other hand, if
\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))=\|B\|_{\tr}
, then
immediately
0\leq\tr(P^{\HH}B)\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\rank(B)=r\leq k
and let the thin SVD of
B
be

B=U\Sigma V^{\HH}=U_{1}\Sigma_{1}V_{1}^{\HH},

where
U_{1}\in{\rm St}(r,n)
,
V_{1}\in{\rm St}(r,k)
, and
\Sigma_{1}=\diag(\sigma_{1}(B),\ldots,\sigma_{r}(B))
.
Write

\widehat{P}:=PV\equiv[\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{k}]\in{\rm St}(k,n),\quad U\equiv[\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{k}],

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
are unit column vectors.
We have

\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))=\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}U\Sigma V^{\HH}))=\Re(\tr([V^{\HH}P^{\HH}U]\Sigma))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Re(\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}^{\HH}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\sigma_{i}(B),

yielding
\sum_{i=1}^{r}[1-\Re(\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}^{\HH}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})]\sigma_{i}(B)=0
by
\Re(\tr(P^{\HH}B))=\|B\|_{\tr}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}(B)
. Hence

\Re(\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}^{\HH}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})=1\quad\mbox{for $1\leq i\leq r$}

because
1-\Re(\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}^{\HH}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\geq 0
and
\sigma_{i}(B)>0
for
1\leq i\leq r
. By the condition under which the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
becomes an equality:

1=\Re(\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}^{\HH}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\leq\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\|_{2}=1,

we conclude that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{p}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. We can then write
\widehat{P}=[U_{1},\widehat{P}_{2}]
where

\widehat{P}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(k-r)}
. Finally, we get

P=\widehat{P}V^{\HH}=U_{1}V_{1}^{\HH}+\widehat{P}_{2}V_{2}^{\HH}\in{\rm St}(k,n)

for which
P(V_{1}\Sigma_{1}V_{1}^{\HH})=U_{1}\Sigma_{1}V_{1}^{\HH}=B
, a polar decomposition of
B
, as was to be shown.

Item (c) is a consequence of item (a) and item (b).
∎

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we investigated two matrix extremal problems concerning the eigenvalue problem of a Hermitian matrix and the
polar decomposition of a general matrix. Both problems are stated in terms of maximizing certain matrix trace functions.
The results have been used in
[
9
]
to analyze the convergence of two newly established frameworks
for numerical solutions of most common optimization problems on Stiefel manifolds. A notable difference is that
in
[
9
]
they are presented in the real number field whereas here it is for the complex number field.
Because of that, some subtleties arise. For example, we have to use
\Re(\cdot)
to extract the real part of a complex number at various places here.

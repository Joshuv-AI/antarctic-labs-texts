Meijia Yang, Yong Xia

Abstract
The generalized trace ratio problem (GTRP) is to maximize a quadratic fractional objective function in trace formulation over the Stiefel manifold. In this paper, based on a newly developed matrix S-lemma, we show that (GTRP), if a redundant constraint is added and well scaled, has zero Lagrangian duality gap. However, this is not always true without the technique of scaling or adding the redundant constraint.

Introduction

Consider the generalized trace ratio problem (GTRP):

\displaystyle{\rm(GTRP)}~

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\Phi(X)=\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle X^{T}X=I_{p},

where
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
n\geq p
,
B\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
means that
B
is a symmetric matrix in the real space with dimension
n\times n
. Throughout this paper, we assume that both
G\in\mathbb{S}^{p\times p}
and
A\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
are positive definite matrices so that
tr(GX^{T}AX)>0
always holds for
\forall X\neq 0
.

(GTRP) is presented in
[
22
]
, where the authors established the first-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for the global maximizer of (GTRP), the authors also proved that (GTRP) has no local non-global maximizer and revealed the hidden convexity of (GTRP).

(GTRP) plays an important role in pattern recognition, computer vision and machine learning
[
15
]
. (GTRP) contains many famous problems as its special cases. For example,
the problem of maximizing
\Phi(X)
with
X
being a permutation matrix is a fractional quadratic assignment problem (FQAP), where
G
denotes the distance matrix,
A
and
B
denote the flow matrix. Then (GTRP) can be seen as the orthogonal relaxation of (FQAP). In (GTRP), when
G=I_{p}
, it reduces to the trace ratio problem (TRP), which is well studied in
[
23
,
24
]
; when
n=p
and
A=I_{n}
(or
tr(GX^{T}AX)\equiv 1
), it reduces to the orthogonal constrained homogeneous quadratic programming problem, denoted by (GP). (GP) is well studied in
[
2
,
3
]
; when
A=I_{n}
(or
tr(GX^{T}AX)\equiv 1
), it reduces to the Brockett cost function optimization, which is an orthogonal relaxation of the Koopmans-Beckmann quadratic assignment problem
[
1
,
6
,
13
]
, we denote this case by (GTP), which is well studied in
[
7
]
; when
G=I_{p},~A=I_{n}
holds together, it reduces to
\max_{X^{T}X=I_{p}}tr(X^{T}BX)
, whose optimal value is the sum of the first
p
largest eigenvalues of
B

[
7
]
; when
p=1
, it reduces to
\max_{x^{T}x=1}\frac{x^{T}Bx}{x^{T}Ax}
, which is a generalized Rayleigh quotient problem, denoted by (GRQ1); when
p=1
and
A=I_{n}
, it reduces to
\max_{x^{T}x=1}\frac{x^{T}Bx}{x^{T}x}
, denoted by (RQ), in this case,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a vector, so (RQ) is the classical Rayleigh quotient problem, whose optimal value is the largest eigenvalue of
B

[
10
]
.

Lagrangian dual plays an important role in optimization. If there is no Lagrangian duality gap, we can turn to solve the dual problem. Usually we can obtain the Lagrangian dual from the primal problem directly. But sometimes there might be duality gap. For example, as a special case of (GTRP), (GRQ1) enjoys the strong Lagrangian duality property, which we will give an explanation in Example
1
. However, in this paper, we will show that for the general case of (GTRP), there might be a Lagrangian duality gap.

There are two useful techniques in closing the duality gap of some nonconvex optimization problems
[
18
]
. The first one is adding redundant constraint. For example, in
[
2
,
3
]
, the authors studied (GP), which is (GTRP) with
n=p
and
A=I_{n}
(or
tr(GX^{T}AX)\equiv 1
). The authors showed that strong duality holds for (GP) if the seemingly redundant constraints
XX^{T}=I_{n}
(
[
3
]
) or
XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
(
[
2
]
) is added before taking the Lagrangian dual.
In
[
7
]
, the authors studied (GTP), which is (GTRP) with
A=I_{n}
. The authors showed that strong duality holds for (GTP) if the seemingly redundant constraints
XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
is added before taking the Lagrangian dual. (The expression
XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
means that
XX^{T}-I_{n}
is negative semidefinite). So we add
XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
to (GTRP) and obtain (GR):

\displaystyle{\rm(GR)}~

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle X^{T}X=I_{p},

\displaystyle XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}.

However, we will prove in this paper that for the general case of (GR), it does not enjoy the strong Lagrangian duality property.

The second skill is scaling, which is very useful in fractional programming. In
[
21
]
, the authors studied the two-sided quadratic constraint quadratic fractional programming problem, denoted by (QF), in which if the two-sided constraint reduces to the equality constraint and the quadratic terms are homogeneous, it reduces to (GRQ1), a special case of (GTRP) by letting
p=1
. The authors showed that (QF), if well scaled, has strong Lagrangian duality property, which inspires us to make a scale on (GTRP) and obtain (GS):

\displaystyle{\rm(GS)}~

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{X\neq 0}

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\frac{X^{T}X}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}=\frac{I_{p}}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}.

However, in this paper, we will show that for the general case of (GS), it does not enjoy the strong Lagrangian duality property.

Since for the general case of (GTRP), (GR), (GS), all of them have the Lagrangian duality gap. So we combine the skills together and obtain (GRS):

\displaystyle{\rm(GRS)}~

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{X\neq 0}

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\frac{X^{T}X}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}=\frac{I_{p}}{tr(GX^{T}AX)},

\displaystyle\frac{XX^{T}}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}\preceq\frac{I_{n}}{tr(GX^{T}AX)},

we will prove in this paper that (GRS) has no Lagrangian duality gap.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we establish a matrix S-lemma based on which we show that (GRS) enjoys the strong Lagrangian duality property, which means that we close the duality gap of (GTRP) if the seemingly redundant constraint is added and well scaled before we take the Lagrangian dual. In section 3, we show that all of (GTRP), (GR), (GS) have duality gap if we take the Lagrangian dual directly (without any other equivalent transformation). Conclusion are made in Section 4.

Strong Lagrangian duality for (GRS)

In this section, we show that there is no Lagrangian duality gap for (GRS), which is equivalent to (GTRP).
We first review an extension of the Farkas lemma, which will be used in the following proof. The Farkas lemma
[

is the well-known alternative theorem for linear system. It has been extended to the convex system, which is known as convex Farkas lemma

Lemma 1
(Convex Farkas lemma)

Let
f,g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be convex functions and
\Omega
be a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume Slater’s condition holds for
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
,
i.e., there exists an
\widetilde{x}\in relint(\Omega)
(the set of relative interior points of
\Omega
) such that for
i=1,\ldots,m
,
g_{i}(\widetilde{x})<0
if
g_{i}(x)
is nonlinear, and
g_{i}(\widetilde{x})\leq 0

if
g_{i}(x)
is linear. The following two
statements are equivalent:

(i)

The system
\{f(x)<0,~g_{i}(x)\leq 0,~i=1,\ldots,m,~x\in\Omega\}
is not solvable.

(ii)

There exist
\lambda_{i}\geq 0~(i=1,\ldots,m)
such that

f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x)\geq 0,~\forall x\in\Omega.

Based on Lemma
1
, we obtain the following special case of convex Farkas lemma, which is named as nonhomogeneous Farkas lemma and will be used in the proof of the main theorem (matrix S-lemma). We give a proof of nonhomogeneous Farkas lemma to improve the readability.

Lemma 2
(Nonhomogeneous Farkas Lemma)

Let
\tilde{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
,
\tilde{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\tilde{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
. Then the following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

The linear system
\tilde{A}^{T}\tilde{y}\leq\tilde{c},~\tilde{b}^{T}\tilde{y}<0,~\tilde{y}(\in\mathbb{R}^{p})\geq 0
has no solution.

(ii)

There exists
\tilde{x}(\in\mathbb{R}^{q})\geq 0
such that
\tilde{A}\tilde{x}+\tilde{b}\geq 0
and
\tilde{c}^{T}\tilde{x}\leq 0
.

Proof

Let
\tilde{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
,
\tilde{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\tilde{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. In (i) of Lemma
1
, let
f(\tilde{y})=\tilde{b}^{T}\tilde{y}
,
g_{i}(\tilde{y})=-\tilde{y}_{i},~i=1,...,p
,
g_{p+1}(\tilde{y})=\tilde{A}^{T}\tilde{y}-\tilde{c}
,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
the system

\{f(\tilde{y})<0,~g_{i}(\tilde{y})\leq 0,~i=1,\ldots,p+1\}
is not solvable is equivalent to the following statement that there exist
\tilde{\lambda}(\in\mathbb{R}^{p})\geq 0
and
\tilde{x}(\in\mathbb{R}^{p})\geq 0
such that

\tilde{b}^{T}\tilde{y}-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\tilde{y}_{i}+\tilde{x}^{T}(\tilde{A}^{T}\tilde{y}-\tilde{c})\geq 0,~\forall\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

hold. It can be further reformulated as

(\tilde{b}-\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{A}\tilde{x})^{T}\tilde{y}-\tilde{c}^{T}\tilde{x}\geq 0,~\forall\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
which is equivalent to the system

\{\tilde{b}-\tilde{\lambda}+\tilde{A}\tilde{x}=0,~\tilde{c}^{T}\tilde{x}\leq 0\}.

Since
\tilde{\lambda}\geq 0
, the above system can be further equivalent to

\{\tilde{b}+\tilde{A}\tilde{x}\geq 0,~\tilde{c}^{T}\tilde{x}\leq 0\}
. The proof is complete.

We provide the following lemma for completeness.

Lemma 3

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
with
p\leq n
, then
X^{T}X=I_{p}
implies
XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
.

Proof

Since
X^{T}X=I_{p}
, then
XX^{T}=X(X^{T}X)X^{T}=XX^{T}XX^{T}
. Let
Y=XX^{T}
, then we have
Y=Y^{2}
(so
Y
is an idempotent matrix).

Let
\lambda
be any eigenvalue of
Y
and the corresponding eigenvector is
x(\in\mathbb{R}^{n})\neq 0
, then according to
Yx=\lambda x
, we have
Y^{2}x=Y\lambda x=\lambda^{2}x
. Combined with
Y=Y^{2}
, we obtain
\lambda x=\lambda^{2}x
, hence
\lambda=0~or~1
since
x\neq 0
. So the eigenvalues of
Y
are either 0 or 1.

So
0_{n}\preceq Y\preceq I_{n}
, i.e.,
(0_{n}\preceq)XX^{T}\preceq I_{n}
. The proof is complete.

Now we are ready to propose the following alternative theorem. We name it as Matrix S-lemma:

Theorem 2.1
(Matrix S-lemma)

Let
H\in\mathbb{S}^{p\times p}
and
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
. The following two statements are equivalent:

(i)
\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}:~tr(HX^{T}QX)<0,~X^{T}X=I_{p}\right\}=\emptyset
.

(ii) There exist
M\in\mathbb{S}^{p\times p}
and

\displaystyle tr\left(HX^{T}QX\right)+tr\left(M(X^{T}X-I_{p})\right)+tr\left(W(XX^{T}-I_{n})\right)\geq 0,\forall X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}.

(7)

Proof

We first prove that (ii) implies (i)
.
We give this proof on the contrary. Assume that there exists
\tilde{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
such that
tr(H\tilde{X}^{T}Q\tilde{X})<0
and
\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}=I_{p}
hold. According to Lemma
3
, it also holds that
\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^{T}-I_{n}\preceq 0
. As a result,

tr\left(H\tilde{X}^{T}Q\tilde{X}\right)+tr\left(M(\tilde{X}^{T}\tilde{X}-I_{p})\right)+tr\left(W(\tilde{X}\tilde{X}^{T}-I_{n})\right)<0

and
\forall W(\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n})\succeq 0
. It means that there does not exist

and

such that (
7

3
A positive Lagrangian duality gap for (GTRP), (GR) and (GS)

In this section, we study the Lagrangian duality gap for (GTRP), (GR) and (GS). Before we come to the main result, we recall the following two famous lemmas, which will be used in the following subsections.

Lemma 5
(Rayleigh Quotient Theorem
[
10
]
)

Let
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
Q
,
u_{1},...,u_{n}
be orthogonal and such that
Qu_{i}=\lambda_{i}u_{i},~i=1,...,n
. Then
\lambda_{1}\geq x^{T}Qx\geq\lambda_{n}
for any unit vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with equality in the left-hand (respectively, right-hand) if and only if
Qx=\lambda_{1}x
(respectively,
Qx=\lambda_{n}x
); moreover,

\lambda_{1}=\sup_{x\neq 0}\frac{x^{T}Qx}{x^{T}x}(=\max_{x^{T}x=1}x^{T}Qx),~and~\lambda_{n}=\inf_{x\neq 0}\frac{x^{T}Qx}{x^{T}x}(=\min_{x^{T}x=1}x^{T}Qx).

Lemma 6
(
[
7
,
9
,
19
]
)

Let
H\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
,
Q\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
,
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq...\geq\lambda_{n},~\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq...\geq\sigma_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
H
and
Q
, respectively. For any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfying
X^{T}X=I_{n}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sigma_{n-i}\leq tr(HX^{T}QX)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sigma_{i}.

Based on Lemma
5
, we further obtain the following lemma. We give a proof to improve the readability.

Lemma 7
(
[
10
]
)

Let
B\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
,
G(\in\mathbb{S}^{p\times p})\succ 0
,
A(\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n})\succ 0
,
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
X\neq 0
. Define

R(vec(X))=\frac{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes B)vec(X)}{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes A)vec(X)}.

Then
\sup\limits_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},X\neq 0}R(vec(X))=\lambda_{max}(A^{-1}B)
.

Proof

Let
y=(G\otimes A)^{\frac{1}{2}}vec(X)
, where
(G\otimes A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the square root of the positive definite matrix
G\otimes A
. Then we can insert
vec(X)=(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y
into
R(vec(X))
and obtain

R(y)=\frac{y^{T}Sy}{y^{T}y},~S=((G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{T}(G\otimes B)(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then according to Lemma
5
, we know that the maximum value of
R(y)
is
\lambda_{\max}(S)
. Denote the optimal solution by
\tilde{y}
. It holds that
S\tilde{y}=\lambda_{\max}(S)\tilde{y}
, where
\tilde{y}
is the eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{\max}(S)
.

Consider the secular function

((G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{T}(G\otimes B)(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y=\lambda y,

on whose left-hand side multiplied by
(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

((G\otimes A)^{-1})^{T}(G\otimes B)(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y=\lambda(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y,

Since
vec(X)=(G\otimes A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}y
, the above equation can be further written as

((G\otimes A)^{-1})^{T}(G\otimes B)vec(X)=\lambda vec(X).

So in order to find the largest eigenvalue of the matrix
S
and the corresponding eigenvector
\tilde{y}
, we can turn to find the largest eigenvalue of the matrix

((G\otimes A)^{-1})^{T}(G\otimes B)=(G^{-1}\otimes A^{-1})(G\otimes B)=(G^{-1}G)\otimes(A^{-1}B)=I_{p}\otimes(A^{-1}B),

which corresponds to the Rayleigh quotient problem:

\sup_{vec(X)\neq 0}\frac{vec(X)^{T}(I_{p}\otimes(A^{-1}B))vec(X)}{vec(X)^{T}vec(X)},

whose optimal value is
\lambda_{\max}(I_{p}\otimes(A^{-1}B))=\lambda_{\max}(A^{-1}B)
due to Lemma
5

. The proof is complete.

3.1

In this subsection, we show that (GTRP), or even (GR), which is obtained by adding a redundant constraint to (GTRP), could have a positive Lagrangian duality gap. Unlike the effect on (GTP)

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\frac{X^{T}X}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}=\frac{I_{p}}{tr(GX^{T}AX)},

also has Lagrangian duality gap by an example. We first derive the Lagrangian dual of (GS) and denote it as (DGS):

\displaystyle{\rm(DGS)}

\displaystyle\inf_{S}\sup_{X\neq 0}\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}+\frac{tr(S(X^{T}X-I_{p}))}{tr(GX^{T}AX)}

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{S}\sup_{X\neq 0}\frac{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes B+S\otimes I_{n})vec(X)-tr(S)}{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes A)vec(X)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{S}\left\{\inf_{\rho\in\mathbb{R}}\rho:\rho\geq\frac{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes B+S\otimes I_{n})vec(X)-tr(S)}{vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes A)vec(X)},\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\forall X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},~X\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{S}\left\{\rho:vec(X)^{T}(G\otimes B+S\otimes I_{n}-\rho G\otimes A)vec(X)-tr(S)\leq 0,\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\forall X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},~X\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{S}~~\rho

\displaystyle~s.t.~tr(S)\geq 0

\displaystyle~~~~~~G\otimes B+S\otimes I_{n}-\rho G\otimes A\preceq 0,

(23)

where
S\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
. Next, we show that there is a gap between (GS) and its Lagrangian dual (GRS) by an example.

Example 2

Remark 1

Consider the following problem:

\displaystyle{\rm(NGTRP)}~

\displaystyle\max\limits_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}BX)+\beta}{tr(GX^{T}AX)+\alpha}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle X^{T}X=I_{p}.

Although
(NGTRP)
is non-homogeneous, we can make the following equivalent transformation:

Since
X^{T}X=I_{p}
, we can obtain

1=\frac{1}{p}tr(X^{T}X)=\frac{1}{ptr(G)}tr(GX^{T}X),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle tr(GX^{T}BX)+\beta=tr\left(GX^{T}(B+\frac{\beta}{ptr(G)}I_{n})X\right),

\displaystyle tr(GX^{T}AX)+\alpha=tr\left(GX^{T}(A+\frac{\alpha}{ptr(G)}I_{n})X\right).

So
(NGTRP)
is equivalent to the following
(GTRP)
-like problem:

\displaystyle{\rm(NGTRP^{\prime})}~

\displaystyle\max\limits_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}

\displaystyle\frac{tr(GX^{T}\tilde{B}X)}{tr(GX^{T}\tilde{A}X)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle X^{T}X=I_{p},

where
\tilde{B}=B+\frac{\beta}{ptr(G)}I_{n}
,
\tilde{A}=A+\frac{\alpha}{ptr(G)}I_{n}
. So the theory about
(GTRP)
can also be applied to the non-homogeneous problem
(NGTRP)
.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the Lagrangian duality gap of (GTRP). We close the duality gap of (GTRP) by equivalently reformulating it as (GRS), which is obtained by adding a redundant constraint and make a scaling. And we prove that (GRS) has no Lagrangian duality gap by establishing a matrix S-lemma. Then we show that the following three cases: (GTRP) itself, (GR), which is obtained by adding a redundant to (GTRP), (GS), which is obtained by making a scaling on (GTRP), might have positive Lagrangian duality gap. We will consider the following more general problem

\displaystyle\max\limits_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}}

\displaystyle\frac{tr(G_{2}X^{T}BX)}{tr(G_{1}X^{T}AX)}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle X^{T}X=I_{p}

and study its properties and global algorithms in the future.

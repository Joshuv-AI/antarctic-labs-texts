LQG inspired spacetimes as solutions of the Einstein equations
Marcos V. de S. Silva, Carlos F. S. Pereira, G. Alencar, Celio R. Muniz

Abstract
Black bounces are compact objects with a wormhole structure hidden behind an event horizon. This type of metric can be obtained through general relativity by considering the presence of exotic matter. Such spacetimes can also arise within the framework of effective theories inspired by loop quantum gravity. In this work, we verify the possibility of obtaining black bounce models inspired by loop quantum gravity as solutions of general relativity. For this, we examine which sources can generate these solutions and the consequences of using these types of sources. We find that the sources can be expressed as a combination of a phantom scalar field and nonlinear electrodynamics. Once we obtain the sources in terms of fields, we analyze the energy conditions for each field separately to verify which of the fields is responsible for the violation of the energy conditions.

II
Field source to a general spacetime

As the main point of this work is to find sources that can generate LQG inspired spacetimes in GR, we will consider the line element that can be written as

ds^{2}=g(r)dt^{2}-f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}-r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2}\right).

(1)

The components of the Einstein tensor,
G_{\mu\nu}
, for this type of line element are written as

\displaystyle{G^{0}}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{f}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}},

(2)

\displaystyle{G^{1}}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{fg^{\prime}}{rg}-\frac{f}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}},

(3)

\displaystyle{G^{2}}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{G^{3}}_{3}=-\frac{f^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{4g}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r}-\frac{fg^{\prime\prime}}{2g}+\frac{fg^{\prime 2}}{4g^{2}}-\frac{fg^{\prime}}{2rg}.

(4)

In the context of GR, such a general solution could not be described only by a scalar field or NED. This can be seen through the symmetries of the stress-energy tensor for each field source. In the case of the scalar field, there is the symmetry
{T^{0}}_{0}={T^{2}}_{2}
and in the electromagnetic case, we have the symmetry
{T^{0}}_{0}={T^{1}}_{1}
.

This type of problem also arises in the case of BB spacetimes, such as the Simpson-Visser model. As is usually done in the Simpson-Visser model, we will check whether the coupling of gravitational theory together with NED and a phantom scalar field is capable of generating this spacetime.

The action that describes this type of theory is given by

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-2\epsilon g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+2V\left(\phi\right)+L(F)\right],

(5)

with
R
being the Ricci scalar,
g
is the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
,
V\left(\phi\right)
is the potential related to the scalar field
\phi
and the electromagnetic Lagrangian
L(F)
is an arbitrary function of the electromagnetic scalar
F=F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}
. According to the notation we are following in this work, if
\epsilon=1
we have a usual scalar field and if
\epsilon=-1
we have a phantom scalar field.

From the action (
5
), we find the following field equations

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left[L_{F}F^{\mu\nu}\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left[\sqrt{-g}L_{F}F^{\mu\nu}\right]=0,

(6)

\displaystyle 2\epsilon\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi=-\frac{dV(\phi)}{d\phi},

(7)

\displaystyle{R^{\mu}}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}R={T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[\phi]+{T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[F],

(8)

where
L_{F}=\partial L/\partial F
and
R_{\mu\nu}
is the Ricci tensor. The stress-energy tensors
{T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[\phi]
and
{T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[F]
are given by

\displaystyle{T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[\phi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\epsilon\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\left(\epsilon g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-V(\phi)\right),

(9)

\displaystyle{T^{\mu}}_{\nu}[F]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}L(F)-2L_{F}{F}^{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu\alpha}.

(10)

Depending on the type of source chosen, the Maxwell-Faraday tensor may have a nonzero component
F_{23}
, for the magnetic case, or
F_{01}
, for the electric case.

II.1
Magnetic case

For magnetic charged solutions, the only nonzero component of the Maxwell-Faraday tensor,

, is

F_{23}=q\sin\theta,

and the electromagnetic scalar is

F(r)=\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}}.

From (

12

6

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{f}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}=V+\frac{L}{2}+\epsilon f\phi^{\prime 2},

\displaystyle f\left(\epsilon\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\frac{rg^{\prime}}{g}+1}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{L}{2}+V,

\displaystyle\frac{-r^{3}g\left(rf^{\prime}g^{\prime}+2f\left(rg^{\prime\prime}+g^{\prime}\right)\right)-2r^{3}g^{2}f^{\prime}+r^{4}fg^{\prime 2}}{4r^{4}g^{2}}=\frac{L}{2}-\frac{2q^{2}L_{F}}{r^{4}}+V+\epsilon f\phi^{\prime 2},

\displaystyle-\epsilon f^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+\epsilon f\left(\left(-\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}-\frac{4}{r}\right)\phi^{\prime}-2\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)=-\frac{V^{\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}.

For quantities associated with the scalar field, it is not yet possible to write the functions

\displaystyle\frac{i\sqrt{gf^{\prime}-fg^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{2r\epsilon fg}}.

II.2
Electric case

If we consider an electric charged source, only the component
F_{01}=-F_{10}
of the Maxwell-Faraday tensor is nonzero. From the modified Maxwell equations, (
6
), we find

F^{10}=\frac{q}{L_{F}r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{f}{g}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad F=-\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}L_{F}^{2}}.

(21)

For an electrically charged source, the Einstein equations undergo modifications only in the electromagnetic contribution, and thus are written as:

\displaystyle-\frac{f^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{f}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}L_{F}}+\epsilon f\phi^{\prime 2}+V,

\displaystyle-\frac{f\left(\frac{rg^{\prime}}{g}+1\right)}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}L_{F}}-\epsilon f\phi^{\prime 2}+V,

\displaystyle\frac{g\left(rf^{\prime}g^{\prime}+2f\left(rg^{\prime\prime}+g^{\prime}\right)\right)+2g^{2}f^{\prime}-rfg^{\prime 2}}{4rg^{2}}=-\epsilon f\phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{L}{2}-V.

As the part of the equations of motion related to the scalar field remains the same, only the electromagnetic functions undergo modifications and are given by:

\displaystyle L(r)=\frac{-g\left(rf^{\prime}g^{\prime}+2f\left(rg^{\prime\prime}+g^{\prime}\right)\right)-2g^{2}f^{\prime}+rfg^{\prime 2}}{2rg^{2}}+2f\phi^{\prime 2}-2V,

\displaystyle L_{F}(r)=\frac{8q^{2}g^{2}}{r^{2}\left(rg\left(rf^{\prime}g^{\prime}-2f\left(g^{\prime}-rg^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)+2g^{2}\left(rf^{\prime}+f\left(-4r^{2}\phi^{\prime 2}-2\right)+2\right)-r^{2}fg^{\prime 2}\right)},

(26)

which are different from equations (
17
) and (
18

Although obtained independently through the Einstein equations, the electromagnetic functions

L_{F}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial F}=L_{F}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}\right)^{-1}=0.

As we shall see later, for electrically charged solutions, it becomes more complicated, but not always impossible, to analytically construct the Lagrangian

P=P^{\mu\nu}P_{\mu\nu}=L_{F}^{2}F=-\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}}.

(28)

In general, the above function is much easier to invert than the scalar

21

If an electrodynamics theory tends to Maxwell in the weak-field limit, we have

L(F)\approx F\approx P.

(29)

Thus, in the weak-field limit, the two representations are identical. However, for BHs, the associated electrodynamics often does not reduce to Maxwell’s theory in the limit

IV
Holonomy corrected Schwarzschild BH

The Simpson-Visser case was made as a way to test the validity of the method. Now let us apply it to the Holonomy corrected Schwarzschild spacetime, and see if this proposal can be interpreted as a solution to the Einstein equations.

\displaystyle L(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{0}(9m+4r)}{6r^{4}},\quad L_{F}(r)=\frac{r_{0}(3m+r)}{4q^{2}},\quad F(r)=\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}}.

We found that, despite originally appearing in a different context, the Holonomy corrected Schwarzschild BH can be obtained through Einstein equations when coupled with NED and a phantom scalar field.

Just like we did in the Simpson-Visser case, let us also check how the source looks when considering an electrically charged solution. In this case, the scalar field remains the same, and the electromagnetic functions are written as:

V
Polymerized BH

Another spacetime inspired by LQG is the polymerized BH. This spacetime is regular and, like the previous cases, has a throat hidden by an event horizon, thus being a model of a BB. The line element that describes this spacetime is written as

L(P)

L(P)=\frac{9k^{2}P\left(2\ 2^{3/4}m\sqrt[4]{-P}-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{4-\frac{2\sqrt{2}k^{2}\sqrt{-P}}{q}}\right)}{20q^{5/2}}+\frac{8\sqrt{2-\frac{\sqrt{2}k^{2}\sqrt{-P}}{q}}\left(k^{2}\sqrt{-P}+2\sqrt{2}q\right)}{15k^{2}q}.

(50)

Thus, we have shown that we can consistently reconstruct LQG inspired BHs as solutions of GR with a phantom scalar field and NED, where this electrodynamics can be with either an electric or magnetic source.

VI
Energy Conditions

To determine whether the material content of a solution is usual or exotic, we must analyze the energy conditions associated with the stress-energy tensor. The energy conditions we will consider are the null (NEC), weak (WEC), dominant (DEC), and strong (SEC) energy conditions. Typically, models of BBs violate all energy conditions associated with the total stress-energy tensor in at least some regions of spacetime because they violate the NEC. However, here we will consider the conditions for each field separately.
The standard energy conditions are written as follows:

\displaystyle NEC_{1,2}=WEC_{1,2}=SEC_{1,2}\Longleftrightarrow\rho+p_{1,2}\geq 0,

(51)

\displaystyle SEC_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho+p_{1}+2p_{2}\geq 0,

(52)

\displaystyle DEC_{1,2}\Longrightarrow\rho-p_{1,2}\geq 0,

(53)

\displaystyle DEC_{3}=WEC_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho\geq 0.

(54)

The fluid quantities are related to the components of the stress-energy tensor as

\displaystyle T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}={\rm diag}\left[\rho,-p_{1},-p_{2},-p_{2}\right]\,\quad\mbox{if}\quad f(r)>0,

(55)

\displaystyle T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}={\rm diag}\left[-p_{1},\rho,-p_{2},-p_{2}\right]\,\quad\mbox{if}\quad f(r)<0.

(56)

Here,
\rho
is the energy density,
p_{1}
is the radial pressure, and
p_{2}
is the tangential pressure.

The fluid quantities for the electromagnetic field do not change inside or outside an event horizon. These are then written as

\rho[E]=\frac{L}{2}=-p_{1}[E],\qquad p_{2}[E]=-\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2q^{2}L_{F}}{r^{4}}.

(57)

For the scalar field, the expressions depend on the presence of the event horizon. In this case, the fluid quantities are given by

\displaystyle\rho[\phi]=V-f\phi^{\prime 2}=-p_{2}[\phi],\qquad p_{1}[\phi]=-V-f\phi^{\prime 2},\quad\mbox{if}\quad f(r)>0,

(58)

\displaystyle\rho[\phi]=V+f\phi^{\prime 2},\qquad p_{1}[\phi]=-V+f\phi^{\prime 2}=p_{2}[\phi].\quad\mbox{if}\quad f(r)<0.

(59)

The energy conditions for the Simpson-Visser case have already been analyzed for each field in the literature
[
30
]
. Therefore, we now need to check for solutions inspired by LQG.

It is important to note that NEC is also included in the other conditions. This reveals to us that if NEC is violated, the others will also be violated. However, if the null energy condition is satisfied, this does not guarantee that the others will also be satisfied.

VI.1
Holonomy corrected

Considering the functions related to the electromagnetic field and the scalar field, obtained in the previous section, we find the expressions to the energy conditions for the Holonomy corrected Schwarzschild BH. For the electromagnetic sector, the expressions are written as

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[E]\Longleftrightarrow 0,\quad NEC_{2}[E]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(3m+r)}{2r^{4}}\geq 0,\quad SEC_{3}[E]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(9m+2r)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0,

(60)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[E]=2WEC_{3}[E]\Longrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(9m+4r)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0,\qquad DEC_{2}[E]\Longrightarrow\frac{r_{0}}{6r^{3}}\geq 0.

(61)

This reveals that NED does not violate the energy conditions and this is valid for the entire spacetime.

For the scalar field, in regions where
f(r)>0
, the energy conditions are

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow-\frac{r_{0}}{r^{3}}\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\geq 0,\qquad NEC_{2}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow 0,

(62)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[\phi]=-SEC_{3}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(3m+2r)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0,\quad DEC_{2}[\phi]=2WEC_{3}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(15m-4r)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0,

(63)

and for regions where
f(r)<0
, we have

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[\phi]=NEC_{2}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{r_{0}}{r^{3}}\left(1-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\geq 0,

(64)

\displaystyle SEC_{3}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(10r-27m)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0,\quad WEC_{3}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(8r-9m)}{12r^{4}}\geq 0,

(65)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[\phi]=DEC_{2}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{r_{0}(3m+2r)}{6r^{4}}\geq 0.

(66)

Since at least one of the inequalities of NEC is always violated, all energy conditions will have at least one of their inequalities violated for the phantom scalar field.

Therefore, even if the electromagnetic field does not violate the energy conditions, the phantom scalar field will violate them.

VI.2
Polymerized solution

Let us now analyze the energy conditions for the polymerized solution case. We will start with the electromagnetic part, as it is valid for the entire spacetime. These are given by:

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[E]\Longleftrightarrow 0,\quad NEC_{2}[E]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{6k^{2}m+2r^{3}}{2r^{5}}-\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\frac{\left(3k^{2}+2r^{2}\right)}{2r^{4}}\geq 0,

(67)

\displaystyle SEC_{3}[E]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{18k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}-\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(27k^{4}+16k^{2}r^{2}+32r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}\geq 0,

(68)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[E]=2WEC_{3}[E]\Longrightarrow\frac{2\left(6k^{2}m+5r^{3}\right)}{5r^{5}}+\frac{2\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(-9k^{4}-7k^{2}r^{2}+16r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}\geq 0,

(69)

\displaystyle DEC_{2}[E]\Longrightarrow-\frac{3k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}+\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(9k^{4}+2k^{2}r^{2}+64r^{4}\right)}{30k^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\geq 0.

(70)

For the scalar field, in regions where
f(r)>0
, the energy conditions are

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow-\frac{2k^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\geq 0,\qquad NEC_{2}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow 0,

(71)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[\phi]=-SEC_{3}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{8k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}+\frac{4\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(-3k^{4}r-4k^{2}r^{3}-8r^{5}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{5}}\geq 0,

(72)

\displaystyle DEC_{2}[\phi]=2WEC_{3}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{28k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}-\frac{2\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(21k^{4}+8k^{2}r^{2}+16r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}\geq 0.

(73)

and for regions where
f(r)<0
, we have

\displaystyle NEC_{1}[\phi]=NEC_{2}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{2k^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}-\frac{2m}{r}\right)\geq 0,

(74)

\displaystyle SEC_{3}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{8\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(9k^{4}+2k^{2}r^{2}+4r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}-\frac{48k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}\geq 0,

(75)

\displaystyle WEC_{3}[\phi]\Longleftrightarrow\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(9k^{4}-8k^{2}r^{2}-16r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}-\frac{6k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}\geq 0,

(76)

\displaystyle DEC_{1}[\phi]=DEC_{2}[\phi]\Longrightarrow\frac{8k^{2}m}{5r^{5}}-\frac{4\sqrt{1-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}}\left(3k^{4}+4k^{2}r^{2}+8r^{4}\right)}{15k^{2}r^{4}}\geq 0.

(77)

For the case of the scalar field, it is obvious that the
NEC_{1}[\phi]
inequality is always violated in such a way that the NEC is never satisfied. This result is expected since the scalar field is phantom-like.

For the case of the electromagnetic field, it is not very clear whether the NEC is satisfied or not. However, if we expand the inequality
NEC_{2}[E]
for points far from the BH, we obtain
NEC_{2}[E]\Longleftrightarrow-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{4}}\geq 0
, which is clearly not satisfied. This shows us that there are points outside the event horizon where the NEC will be violated.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we show that spherically symmetric and static BB spacetimes inspired by LQG can be interpreted as solutions of GR. For this, we constructed the formalism considering the coupling between the gravitational theory with NED and a non-free scalar field. The equations of motion were written in the most general form possible for the chosen line element, and we considered both magnetically charged and electrically charged cases.

The combination of the scalar field and NED is necessary since, for general BB cases, the components of the Einstein tensor do not exhibit the symmetries
{G^{0}}_{0}={G^{1}}_{1}
or
{G^{0}}_{0}={G^{2}}_{2}
, which means that the fields individually cannot satisfy the Einstein equations. In general, the form of the functions related to the scalar field does not depend on whether the charge is electric or magnetic. Thus, changing the choice of the source only modifies the functions
L

L_{F}
, and
F

\phi
and
V(\phi)
remain unchanged, regardless of the source. For all cases, we consider
\epsilon=-1
, ensuring that the scalar field is a phantom field for all solutions. This requirement is necessary to guarantee that the scalar field remains real.

The first model we studied was the Simpson-Visser spacetime. This model was chosen because it has been extensively studied in the literature and served as a consistency test for our method. The results obtained, both for magnetic and electric charges, differ from those presented in
[
30
,
79
]
. However, this difference arises because of the chosen coordinate system, so despite being different, our results are consistent with those found in the literature.

As the second model, we chose the Schwarzschild solution with holonomic corrections. The causal structure of this solution is quite similar to that in the case of RBH in the Simpson-Visser model. However, unlike Simpson-Visser, we do not have traversable WH cases in both directions, since
0<r_{0}<2m

For the third case, the functions related to the source fields are much more complex than in the previous cases. For example, the function

Once the fields have been separated, we analyzed the energy conditions for each field individually. Since the Simpson-Visser case has been extensively studied in the literature, we did not address it here. For the second model, we found that all the energy conditions related to the electromagnetic field are always satisfied, whereas the scalar field can violate all the energy conditions, both inside and outside the event horizon, which is consistent with a phantom scalar field. For the third model, some of the analytical expressions are not very clear. However, we observe that, for points far from the event horizon, the inequality related to

In summary, NEC violations are consistent with the behavior expected for BB solutions, such as those discussed here

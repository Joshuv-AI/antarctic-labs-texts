Center of affine 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 \mathfrak{sl}_{2|1} at the critical level
Dražen Adamović, Shigenori Nakatsuka

Abstract
. In this article, we shall describe the center of the universal affine vertex superalgebra V κ c ​ ( 𝔤 ) V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}) associated with 𝔤 = 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 , 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1},\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1} at the critical level κ c \kappa_{c} and prove the conjecture of A. Molev and E. Ragoucy [ 24 ] in this case. The center 𝔷 ⁡ ( V κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) ) \mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})) turns out to be isomorphic to the large level limit ℓ → ∞ \ell\rightarrow\infty of a vertex subalgebra, called the parafermion vertex algebra K ℓ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) K^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) , of the affine vertex algebra V ℓ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) V^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) . The key ingredient of the proof is to understand the principal 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -superalgebra 𝒲 κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) \mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}) at the critical level. It relates the center 𝔷 ⁡ ( V κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) ) \mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})) to V ∞ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) V^{\infty}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) via the Kazama–Suzuki duality while it has a surprising coincidence with V κ c ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 1 | 1 ) V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}) , whose center has been recently described. Moreover, the centers 𝔷 ⁡ ( V κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) ) \mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})) and 𝔷 ⁡ ( 𝒲 κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 | 1 ) ) \mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})) are proven to coincide as a byproduct. A general conjecture is proposed which describes the center 𝔷 ⁡ ( V κ c ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n | m ) ) \mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m})) with n > m n>m as a large level limit of “the dual side”, i.e., the parafermion-type subalgebras of 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -algebras 𝒲 ℓ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n , 𝕆 [ n − m , 1 m ] ) \mathcal{W}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]}) associated with hook-type partitions [ n − m , 1 m ] [n-m,1^{m}] , known also as vertex algebras at the corner.



1.
Introduction

Let
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
be the universal affine vertex superalgebra associated with a basic-classical simple Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a complex number
k
, called a level.
The center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{k}(\mathfrak{g}))
is known to be trivial except for one level, called the
critical level

k=\kappa_{c}
.
When
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie algebra, the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
at the critical level was described by B. Feigin and E. Frenkel in
[
9
,
10
]
. It is isomorphic to the principal
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
and thus the ring of functions over the space of certain connections over the formal disc with values in the Langlands dual Lie algebra by the Feigin–Frenkel duality. This provides a fundamental fact in the geometric Langlands program, see e.g.
[
14
]
.
Recently, new explicit formulas for the generators of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
was given by A. Molev in
[
22
]
.

However,
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
for Lie superalgebras remains mysterious for decades and is much more difficult to study since
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
does not need to be finitely generated.
A. Molev and E. Ragoucy started in
[
24
]
a systematic study on generators of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n|m}
. They constructed a family of higher rank Segal–Sugawara vectors which belong to
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
and conjectured that they generate the center (see also §
6.1
for a review). Their conjecture has only been proved in the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
by A. Molev and E. Mukhin
[
23
]
.

The alternative description of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))
was obtained
[
2
]
by realizing
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
as an orbifold of the vertex algebra
M\otimes\mathcal{A}
, where
M
is the commutative vertex algebra of differential polynomials in two variables and
\mathcal{A}
is the fermionic vertex algebra (denoted by
F
in
[
2
]
). Using this explicit realization (which is parallel to the realization of

L_{1}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})
from
[
18
]
), it was proved in
[
2
]
that
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))\cong M_{0}
, where
M_{0}
is an orbifold of
M
which has the same Hilbert–Poincaré series as of the center determined in
[
23
]
(see §
3
, for more details on this construction).

In this paper, we consider the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1},\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}
and completely describe
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
. Our method is based on finding a relation between the centers of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
and
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
through the center of the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra

\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
, which is obtained as a quantum Hamiltonian reduction of
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})

[
16
]
. The main theorem of the paper is the following.

Main Theorem

(Theorem
6.5
/ Corollary
6.7
)
.

There are isomorphisms of (commutative) vertex algebras

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq K^{\infty}(\check{\mathfrak{g}}).

where we set
\check{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
, respectively.

The vertex algera
K^{\infty}(\check{\mathfrak{g}})
appearing in the right-most hand side is the large level limit (
\ell\rightarrow\infty
) of the
parafermion vertex algebra

K^{\ell}(\check{\mathfrak{g}})
(cf.
[
11
,
12
]
) obtained as the coset subalgebra inside
V^{\ell}(\check{\mathfrak{g}})
by the Heisenberg vertex subalgebra associated with the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

The first isomorphism for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}
implies a conjecture by A. Molev and E. Ragoucy
[
24
]
in this case, which describes a generating set of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))
and also a conjecture by A. Molev and E. Mukhin
[
23
]
in this case, which asserts its associated graded algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of affine supersymmetric polynomials assocaited with the superspace
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
. On the other hand, the second isomorphism comes from a “correct” form of the Kazama–Suzuki duality
[
19
]
, which is known except for this level. It may be regarded as a variant of the Feigin–Frenkel duality
[
9
,
10
]
for the principal
\mathcal{W}
-algebras, which plays a crutial role to understand the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
for Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let us briefly describe the proof of Main Theorem.
The key ingredient is a rather surprising isomorphism
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\simeq V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
available only at this level (Proposition
4.1
).
This isomophism, in particular, identifies their centers.
We utilize the Wakimoto-type free field realizations of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
(§
5
), which provides an upper bound of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
by
\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
, i.e., the known center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))

[
23
]
.
This upper bound will be shown to be the lower bound of
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
by using higher-rank Segal–Sugawara vector and a structure of algebras of affine supersymmetric functions. The second isomorphism is derived from the Kazama–Suzuki duality together with the description of the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))
in
[
2
]
.

Although the first isomorphism will be obtained first by the map induced by the restriction of the quantum Hamiltonian reduction, we provide in §
7
a yet another one in the spirit of
inverse Hamiltonian reduction
, which might be useful to construct various
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules with arbitrary central characters.

As already mentioned above, the Kazama–Suzuki duality for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
is a key ingredient to describe the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
and plays a role of the Feigin–Frenkel duality for principal
\mathcal{W}
-algebras
[
9
,
10
]
.
Indeed, the Feigin–Frenkel duality has a variant for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|1})
, called the Feigin–Semikhatov duality — which includes the Kazama–Suzuki duality —
[
6
,
8
,
13
]
and a more general conjecture has recently been proposed in
[
5
]
for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m})
(
n>m
).
The latter relates
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m})
to the
\mathcal{W}
-algebra
\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]})
associated with the nilpontent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]}
corresponding to a hook-type partition
[n-m,1^{m}]
.
In this setting, the parafermion
K^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
in the case
n=m=1
is generalized to the coset subalgebra
C^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]})
of
\mathcal{W}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]})
with respect to the affine vertex subalgebra for
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
. We note that it is known also as the vertex algebras at the corner
[
8
,
15
]
.
Base on this, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture
.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m},\mathfrak{gl}_{n|m}
with
n>m
, there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{O}_{[n-m,1^{m}]})

where we set
\check{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, respectively.

The structure of
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
, espacially in the case
m=1
, seems to be simple enough to understand the algebraic structure among higher rank Segal–Sugawara vectors in
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
in the super setting.
We hope to come back to this point in our forthcoming paper.

2.
Affine vertex superalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
be the general linear Lie superalgebra associated with the vector superspace
V=\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
and
e_{i,j}
(
i,j=1,\dots,m+n
) be the
(i,j)
-th elementary matrix.
The even supersymmetric invariant bilinear forms on
\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n}
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa=k_{1}\kappa_{V}+k_{2}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}

where
\kappa_{V}
and
\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
are the supertrace on
V
and the Killing form, that is,

\displaystyle\kappa_{V}(u,v)=\operatorname{str}_{V}(uv),\quad\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{str}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\operatorname{ad}_{u}\operatorname{ad}_{v}).

The affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the central extension of the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t^{\pm 1}]

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{C}K\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}[t^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow 0

with Lie bracket

\displaystyle[u_{(p)},v_{(q)}]:=[u,v]_{(p+q)}+\kappa(u,v)p\delta_{p+q,0}K,\quad[K,\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}]=0

where
u_{(p)}=ut^{p}
.
The (universal) affine vertex superalgebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}
at level
\kappa
is defined as the parabolic induction

\displaystyle V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}):=U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)}\mathbb{C}

induced from the
(\mathfrak{g}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}K)
-module
\mathbb{C}
where
\mathfrak{g}[t]
acts by
0
and
K
by the identity, equipped with a unique vertex algebra structure with the unit
\mathbf{1}=1\otimes 1
, the translation operator
\partial
satisfying
[\partial,u_{(p)}]=-pu_{(p-1)}
, and the state-field correspondence
Y(\cdot,z)
satisfying
Y(u,z)=\sum u_{(p)}z^{-p-1}
.
Here
u
is identified with
u_{(-1)}\otimes\mathbf{1}
.

Note that for
n\neq m
, the decomposition
\mathfrak{gl}_{n|m}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n|m}\oplus\mathbb{C}h
with
h=\sum e_{i,i}
induces an isomorphism of vertex superalgebras

\displaystyle V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})\simeq V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n})\otimes\pi^{\kappa}_{h}.

Here
\pi^{\kappa}_{h}
is the Heisenberg vertex algebra
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1})
with
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}=\mathbb{C}h
.
It is straightforward to check the following formula of the
\lambda
-bracket

\displaystyle[u{}_{\lambda}v]=[u,v]+k\kappa_{V}(u,v),\quad[h{}_{\lambda}h]=(m-n)k_{1}\lambda

for
u,v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{m|n}
where we set
k=k_{1}+2(m-n)k_{2}
.

The level
\kappa=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
, denoted by
\kappa_{c}
is called
the critical level
; the bilinear form is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}(e_{i,j},e_{p,q})=\delta_{i,j}\delta_{p,q}(-1)^{p(i)+p(q)}-(m-n)\delta_{i,j}\delta_{p,q}(-1)^{p(i)}

where
p(i)
is the parity, which is 0 (resp. 1) if
i\leqslant m
(resp.
i>m
).

Given a vertex superalgebra
V
and a subalgebra
W
, the coset
\operatorname{Com}(W,V)
is the subalgebra defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Com}(W,V):=\{u\in V;[Y(u,z),Y(v,w)]=0\ (\forall v\in W)\}.

In particular, the coset subalgebra
\mathfrak{z}(V):=\operatorname{Com}(V,V)

is called the center of
V
.
For a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
is known to be trivial
\mathbb{C}
at
\kappa\neq\kappa_{c}
, whereas non-trivial at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
.
It is known as the Feigin–Frenkel center and explicitly described by using the notion of
opers
associated with the Langlands dual of
\mathfrak{g}

[
9
]
.
On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, the center for the basic-classical simple Lie superalgebras, the Feigin-Frenkel center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
is not understood except for the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}

[
2
,
23
]
.
In this paper, we determine the Feigin-Frenkel center for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}
or equivalently
\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}
at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
.

3.
Feigin-Frenkel center of affine
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}

The Feigin-Frenkel center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{g}))
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}
is determined in
[
2
,
23
]
.
We recall the presentation which will play a key role, following
[
2
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
denote the
bc
-system vertex superalgebra generated by odd fields
\phi,\phi^{*}
satisfying the
\lambda
-bracket

\displaystyle[\phi{}_{\lambda}\phi^{*}]=1,\quad[\phi{}_{\lambda}\phi]=[\phi^{*}{}_{\lambda}\phi^{*}]=0.

Let
x:=\xi_{+}\xi_{-}
where we omit the symbol of normally ordered product. Then the operator
x_{(0)}
define on
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
the following
\mathbb{Z}
–gradation

\mathcal{A}_{\phi}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathcal{A}_{\phi})_{\ell},

where

(\mathcal{A}_{\phi})_{\ell}=\{v\in\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\ |\ x_{(0)}v=\ell v\}.

Let

M=\mathbb{C}[\partial^{n}\xi_{+},\partial^{n}\xi_{-}\mid n\geqslant 0]

denote the commutative vertex algebra of differential polynomials in variables
\xi_{+},\xi_{-}
.
We introduce the weight decomposition

\displaystyle M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{n}

by setting
\operatorname{wt}(\xi_{\pm})=\pm 1
with
\operatorname{wt}(\partial A)=\operatorname{wt}(A)
and
\operatorname{wt}(AB)=\operatorname{wt}(A)+\operatorname{wt}(B)
.
The subspace
M_{0}\subset M
is a vertex subalgebra admitting the following generating sets

(3.1)

\displaystyle M_{0}=\langle(\partial^{n}\xi_{+})(\partial^{m}\xi_{-})\mid n,m\geqslant 0\rangle=\langle(\partial^{n}\xi_{+})\xi_{-}\mid n\geqslant 0\rangle

as a differential algebra.

By
[
2
, Theorem 3.2 (1)]
, there is an injective homomorphism of vertex superalgebras

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}q\colon&V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})&\hookrightarrow&\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\otimes M\\
&e_{1,2},\ e_{2,1}&\mapsto&\phi\xi_{+},\ \phi^{*}\xi_{-}\\
&e_{1,1},\ e_{2,2}&\mapsto&\phi\phi^{*},\ \xi_{+}\xi_{-}-\phi\phi^{*}.\end{array}

Moreover, it was proved that
the image of
q
is identified with the following
U(1)
–orbifold of
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\otimes M
:

V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\otimes M)^{U(1)}:=\bigoplus_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathcal{A}_{\phi})_{\ell}\otimes M_{-\ell}

which then easily identifies the center of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})
with the vertex algebra
M_{0}
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

The map
q
induces an isomorphism of vertex algebras

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))\simeq M_{0}.

4.
Center of
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
at the critical level

4.1.
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra

To understand the Feigin–Frenkel center
\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))
, we first consider the center of the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
associated with the regular nilpotent orbit (the orbit containing
f=e_{2,1}
) at the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}

[
16
]
.

The vertex superalgebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
is, by definition, the vertex superalgebra obtained as the cohomology of the following BRST complex

(4.1)

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{DS}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})):=V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\otimes\bigwedge{}^{\bullet}

equipped with the differential

(4.2)

\displaystyle d=\int Y(Q,z)\mathrm{d}z,\quad Q=(e_{1,2}+1)\varphi_{2}^{*}-e_{1,3}\varphi_{3}^{*}.

Here,
\bigwedge{}^{\bullet}
is the charged fermion vertex superalgebra, strongly generated by
\varphi_{i},\varphi_{i}^{*}
(
i=2,3
) which have parity opposite to
e_{1,i}
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}
and satisfy the
\lambda
-bracket

\displaystyle[\varphi_{i}{}_{\lambda}\varphi_{j}^{*}]=\delta_{i,j},\quad[\varphi_{i}{}_{\lambda}\varphi_{j}]=[\varphi_{i}^{*}{}_{\lambda}\varphi_{j}^{*}]=0.

By replacing
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
with
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
-modules
M
in (
4.1
), we obtain the BRST complex
C^{\bullet}_{DS}(M)
, whose cohomology

H_{DS}^{\bullet}(M):=H^{\bullet}(C^{\bullet}_{DS}(M),d)

is naturally a module over the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}):=H_{DS}^{\bullet}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}).

By
[
17
]
, the complex decomposes into a tensor product

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{DS}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\simeq C^{\bullet}_{+}\otimes C^{\bullet}_{0}

where
C^{\bullet}_{+}
is the subcomplex generated by
e_{1,2},e_{1,3},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
and
C^{\bullet}_{0}
generated by

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{1,1}=e_{1,1}+\varphi_{2}\varphi_{2}^{*}-\varphi_{3}\varphi_{3}^{*},

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{2,2}=e_{2,2}-\varphi_{2}\varphi_{2}^{*},\quad\mathbf{e}_{3,3}=e_{3,3}+\varphi_{3}\varphi_{3}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{2,1}=e_{2,1},\quad\mathbf{e}_{2,3}=e_{2,3}+\varphi_{3}\varphi_{2}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{3,1}=e_{3,1},\quad\mathbf{e}_{3,2}=e_{3,2}+\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}^{*}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}^{*},\quad\varphi_{3}^{*}.

We have the cohomology vanishing

\displaystyle H^{n}\left(C^{\bullet}_{DS}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\right)\simeq\delta_{n,0}\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}),\quad H^{n}\left(C^{\bullet}_{+}\right)\simeq\delta_{n,0}\mathbb{C}

and then
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
is realized as the kernel
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\simeq\operatorname{Ker}d\subset C_{0}^{\bullet}
.
Moreover, it admits a set of strong generators given by a basis of the centralizer

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})^{f}=\operatorname{Span}\{h_{0},\ h_{1}+2h_{2},\ e_{2,1},\ e_{2,3},\ e_{3,1}\}

where we set

\displaystyle h_{0}=e_{1,1}+e_{2,2}+e_{3,3},\quad h_{1}=e_{1,1}-e_{2,2},\quad h_{2}=e_{2,2}+e_{3,3}.

Indeed, we find the following strong generators:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&h=\mathbf{h}_{0},\quad J=-(\mathbf{h}_{1}+2\mathbf{h}_{2}),\\
&S=\mathbf{f}+(\mathbf{h}_{1}+\mathbf{h}_{2})\mathbf{h}_{2}-\frac{k+1}{2}\partial\mathbf{h}_{1}-\partial\mathbf{h}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{2,3}\mathbf{e}_{3,2},\\
&G^{+}=\mathbf{e}_{2,3},\quad G^{-}=\mathbf{e}_{3,1}+k\partial\mathbf{e}_{3,2}+(\mathbf{h}_{1}+\mathbf{h}_{2})\mathbf{e}_{3,2}.\end{split}

Note that the decomposition

V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\simeq V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\pi^{\kappa}_{h}
induces

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\simeq\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\pi^{\kappa}_{h}.

Here, the
\mathcal{W}
-superalgebra
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
is defined by replacing
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
with
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
in (
4.1
) and explicitly generated by
J,S,G^{\pm}
in
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
.
It is straightforward to check that these generators satisfy the following
\lambda
-brackets:

\displaystyle[J{}_{\lambda}J]=-(2k+1)\lambda,\quad[J{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]=\pm G^{\pm},

\displaystyle[S{}_{\lambda}S]=-(k+1)\left(\frac{3}{2}(k+1)(2k+1)\lambda^{3}+2S\lambda+\partial S\right),

\displaystyle[S{}_{\lambda}J]=-(k+1)(J\lambda+\partial J),\quad[S{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]=-(k+1)\left(\frac{3}{2}G^{\pm}\lambda+\partial G^{\pm}{}\right),

\displaystyle[G^{+}{}_{\lambda}G^{+}]=[G^{-}{}_{\lambda}G^{-}]=0,

\displaystyle[G^{+}{}_{\lambda}G^{-}]=(k+1)(2k+1)\lambda^{2}-(k+1)J\lambda+\left(S-\frac{k+1}{2}\partial J\right).

At the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
i.e.
k=-1
, we have

\displaystyle[J{}_{\lambda}J]=\lambda,\quad[J{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]=\pm G^{\pm},\quad[G^{+}{}_{\lambda}G^{-}]=S

with all the remaining
\lambda
-brackets to be zero.
Then, we observe the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is an isomorphism of vertex superalgebras:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\eta\colon&\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})&\xrightarrow{\simeq}&V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})\\
&G^{+},G^{-}&\mapsto&e_{1,2},e_{2,1}\\

\operatorname{gr}V=\oplus_{p}F^{p}V/F^{p+1}V
has a structure of Poisson vertex (super)algebra, see
[
4
,
20
]
.
Moreover, given a homomorphism
f\colon V\rightarrow W
of vertex superalgebras, the above association induces a homomorphism
\overline{f}\colon\operatorname{gr}V\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}W
of Poisson vertex superalgebras.
Let us call this construction the semi-classical limit of
f\colon V\rightarrow W
. We apply this limit to the Wakimoto realization (Proposition
5.1
) and obtain the homomorphism

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\colon\operatorname{gr}V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\pi^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}_{\mathfrak{h}})\simeq\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{A}\otimes\operatorname{gr}\pi^{\kappa-\kappa_{c}}_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Proposition 6.4
.

There is an embedding

\Lambda^{2|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}\subset\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})).

Proof.

Note that one has an isomorphism
R_{\infty}^{2|1}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{gr}\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}
which maps

\displaystyle u_{1},u_{2}\mapsto e_{1,1},e_{2,2},\quad v_{1}\mapsto e_{3,3}.

Hence, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Im}\overline{\rho}
contains the power sums
s_{p}
(
p\geqslant 0
) as
\overline{\rho}
is an embedding and restricts to

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\colon\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{gr}\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}

by Lemma
5.2
.
By the formula in Proposition
5.1
, the image of the Segal–Sugawara vector
s_{p,p}
agrees with

\displaystyle\operatorname{str}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}e_{1,1}&&\\
&e_{2,2}&\\
&&-e_{3,3}\end{array}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=e_{1,1}^{p}+e_{2,2}^{p}-(-e_{3,3})^{p}

\displaystyle=u_{1}^{p}+u_{2}^{p}-(-1)^{p}v_{1}^{p}=s_{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.3.
Center

Now, we are ready to establish the main result of the paper.

Theorem 6.5
.

(1)
The natural homomorphism
\iota
is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\iota\colon\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})).

(2)
There is an isomorphism of differential algebras

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\simeq\Lambda^{2|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
and
5.6
, we have embeddings

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\subset\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}.

On the other hand, we have
\Lambda^{2|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}\subset\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))
by Proposition
6.4
.
Hence, it suffices to show

(6.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\subset\Lambda^{2|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}.

For this purpose, we take the semi-classical limit of the Wakimoto realization of
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})
(Proposition
5.4
) and obtain the embedding
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\subset\operatorname{gr}\overline{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\operatorname{gr}\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}
, which restricts to

\displaystyle\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\subset\operatorname{gr}\pi^{0}_{\mathfrak{h}}\simeq R^{2|1}_{\infty}

by Lemma
5.5
.
On the other hand, we have the isomorphism

\operatorname{gr}\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1})\simeq\operatorname{gr}\pi_{h}\otimes\operatorname{gr}V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1})

by Proposition
4.1
.
By
[
23
]
, there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))\simeq\Lambda^{1|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}

and
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{1|1}))
is generated by the elements

\overline{h}_{p,p}=e_{1,1}^{p-1}(e_{1,1}+e_{2,2})+(p-1)e_{1,1}^{p-2}e_{2,1}e_{1,2}

for
p\geqslant 1
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))
is generated by
h=u_{1}+u_{2}+v_{1}
and the elements corresponding to
\overline{h}_{p,p}
(
p\geqslant 1
), that is,

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-1}(u_{1}+u_{2}+2v_{1})^{p-1}(u_{1}+v_{1})(u_{2}+v_{1}).

Hence, we have
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2|1}))\subset\Lambda^{2|1}_{\mathrm{aff}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction

In this section, we relate the centers of
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
and
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
by realizing
V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
in terms of
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
, namely the inverse Hamiltonian reduction first considered by Semikhatov
[
25
]
for non-critical levels.
For this realization, we use the
bc
-system vertex superalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
(see §
3
) and the so-called half-lattice vertex algebra
\Pi(0)
introduced in
[
3
]
.
The algebra
\Pi(0)
is, by definition, the subalgebra

\Pi(0)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{u,v}_{n(u+v)}\subset V_{L}

inside the lattice vertex superalgebra
V_{L}
associated with the integer lattice
L=\mathbb{Z}u\oplus\mathbb{Z}v
equipped with bilinear form such that
(u,u)=1=-(v,v)
. It will be convenient to introduce

c=u+v,\quad d=u-v.

As the highest weights in
\Pi(0)
are
n(u+v)=nc
which have norm zero, we may extend
\Pi(0)
to

\Pi^{\frac{1}{m}}(0)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{u,v}_{\frac{n}{m}(u+v)}

along a larger lattice
\frac{1}{m}\mathbb{Z}c\supset\mathbb{Z}c
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. By setting

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{k}{2}c+d,\quad\nu=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}c-d\right),

we have the following realization of
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
.

Proposition 7.1
.

(1) (cf.
[
25
]
)
There is an embedding of vertex superalgebras

\Phi^{\kappa}\colon V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\otimes\Pi^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)

such that

\displaystyle h_{1}\mapsto\mu,\quad h_{2}\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}(J-\phi\phi^{*}-\mu),

\displaystyle e_{1,2}\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{{c}},\quad e_{1,3}\mapsto\phi\mathrm{e}^{{c/2}},\quad e_{3,2}\mapsto\phi^{*}\mathrm{e}^{{c/2}},

\displaystyle e_{2,1}\mapsto(\mathcal{T}-\nu^{2}+(k+1)\partial\nu)\mathrm{e}^{{-c}},

\displaystyle e_{3,1}\mapsto-\left(G^{+}+\phi^{*}\nu-(k+\tfrac{1}{2})\partial\phi^{*}-\tfrac{1}{2}J\phi^{*}\right)\mathrm{e}^{{-c/2}},

\displaystyle e_{2,3}\mapsto+\left(G^{-}+\phi\nu-(k+\tfrac{1}{2})\partial\phi+\tfrac{1}{2}J\phi\right)\mathrm{e}^{{-c/2}},

where

\mathcal{T}=-S+G^{+}\phi+G^{-}\phi^{*}+\tfrac{1}{4}(J+\phi\phi^{*})^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(k+1)(\phi\phi^{*})^{2}.

(2)
At the critical level
\kappa=\kappa_{c}
, the map
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}
restricts to an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}\colon\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})).

Proof.

(1) The map
\Phi^{\kappa}
essentially agrees with the one in
[
25
]
at non-critical levels
\kappa\neq\kappa_{c}
. We reformulate it regarding our choice of strong generators, which works for all levels
\kappa
. (One may check that the formula
\Phi^{\kappa}
preserves the OPEs and thus defines a homomorphism of vertex superalgebras directly.)
To show that
\Phi^{\kappa}
is an embedding, we consider the lexicographic order of the monomial basis for
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\otimes\Pi(0)^{1/2}
by introducing the following order for the generators

\partial^{\bullet}S>\partial^{\bullet}J>\partial^{\bullet}G^{+}>\partial^{\bullet}G^{-}>\partial^{\bullet}\phi>\partial^{\bullet}\phi^{*}>\partial^{\bullet}c>\partial^{\bullet}d>\mathrm{e}^{{pc/2}}

with
\partial^{n+1}A>\partial^{n}A
and
\mathrm{e}^{{(p+1)c/2}}>\mathrm{e}^{{pc/2}}
.
Then the leading terms of the images of the strong generators of
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
are

\displaystyle h_{1}\sim\mu,\quad h_{2}\sim\tfrac{1}{2}J,

\displaystyle e_{1,2}\sim\mathrm{e}^{{c}},\quad e_{1,3}\sim\phi\mathrm{e}^{{c/2}},\quad e_{3,2}\sim\phi^{*}\mathrm{e}^{{c/2}},

\displaystyle e_{2,1}\sim-S\mathrm{e}^{{-c}},\quad e_{3,1}\sim-G^{+}\mathrm{e}^{{-c/2}},\quad e_{2,3}\sim+G^{-}\mathrm{e}^{{-c/2}}.

Now, it is clear that
\Phi^{\kappa}
is injective.

(2) As
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}
is an embedding, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}\left(\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\right)

\displaystyle\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))

thanks to Theorem
6.5
(1).
This is achieved by using a standard technique developed to show the almost simplicity of inverse Hamiltonian reductions (see e.g. the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 8.1]
).
Set
\mathfrak{Z}=\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}\left(\mathfrak{z}(V^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))\right)
.
Since
\mathfrak{Z}
commutes with
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(h_{1})
and
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(\mathrm{e}^{{c}})
, we have

\mathfrak{Z}\subset\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
.
Secondly,
\mathfrak{Z}
commutes with
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(e_{1,3})
and
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(e_{3,2})
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}\subset\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\subset\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\phi}.

Finally,
\mathfrak{Z}
commutes with
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(h_{2})
,
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(e_{3,1})
and
\Phi^{\kappa_{c}}(e_{2,3})
implies that
\mathfrak{Z}\subset\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1})
commutes with
J
,
G^{\pm}
. As
S
is a central element, we conclude
\mathfrak{Z}\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{W}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2|1}))
. This completes the proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we study the existence of weak solutions to the following nonlocal problem

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}u=\lambda P(x)|u|^{k-2}u+\theta|u|^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-2}u\,\mbox{ in }\,\Omega,\quad u=0\,\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a general open set,
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
,
1<q<k<p<N/s_{1}
, while
p_{s_{1}}^{*}=Np/(N-ps_{1})
is the critical exponent and parameters
\lambda,\,\theta>0
. Naturally, the condition
u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega
disappears when
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
To prove the existence of weak solutions for problem (
1.1
), we assume that the weight function
P:\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is nontrivial and satisfies the following condition:

(P_{0})

P\in L^{r}(\Omega),\quad r=\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}
.

The nonlocal fractional operators
(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}
and
(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}
are defined along any function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}\varphi(x)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{R^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\dfrac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p-2}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dy,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}\varphi(x)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{R^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\dfrac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{q-2}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dy,

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
denotes the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of radius
\varepsilon>0
at the center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Recently, problem (
1.1
) has been studied in
[
3
]
under a sublinear and a superlinear settings, namely with
k\in(1,q)
and with
k\in(p,p_{s_{1}}^{*})
respectively. The aim of the present paper is to look for weak solutions of (
1.1
) with negative energy under a sandwich type situation, that is with
k\in(q,p)
. The sandwich type case for (
1.1
) is more interesting and delicate, since it is strictly related to the double
(p,q)
-growth of the main operator in (
1.1
). The local version of (
1.1
) with
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
has been studied in
[
2
,
12
]
under a sandwich type situation, mainly considering either
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\Omega
bounded. In order to overcome the lack of compactness of energy functional, arising from the presence of a critical Sobolev term in (
1.1
), the authors of
[
2
,
12
]
exploit a Lions’ concentration compactness argument. Here, instead, we introduce a tricky step analysis which allows us to study the compactness property of energy functional of (
1.1
), considering
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
a general open set. We also recall
[
7
]
, where the authors studied the vectorial version of problem (
1.1
) in the bounded domain, namely the system

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}u=\lambda|u|^{k-2}u+\theta\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha-2}u|v|^{\beta}&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{p}v+(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{q}v=\mu|v|^{k-2}v+\theta\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\alpha+\beta}|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta-2}v&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u=v=0&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

with
\alpha>1
,
\beta>1
and
\alpha+\beta=p_{s_{1}}^{*}
, under the sandwich situation
k\in(q,p)
. In
[
7
]
, the authors establish the existence of a mountain pass weak solution of (
1.2
) with positive energy, when
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
,
\mu>\mu^{*}
and
\theta\in(0,\theta^{*})
. However, we observe there is a conflict between the parameters given in
[
7
]
, since the thresholds

\lambda^{*}=\lambda^{*}(\theta)>0,\quad\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(\theta)>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta^{*}=\theta^{*}(\lambda,\mu)>0

are interconnected. As far as we know, the existence of a weak solution for (
1.1
), (
1.2
) with positive energy is still an open problem under the sandwich situation.

Now, we are ready to state the main results of the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. Let
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
and
1<q<k<p<N/s_{1}
. Assume that
P
is nontrivial and satisfies
(P_{0})
.

Then, there exists a sequence
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j}
with
\theta_{j}>\theta_{j+1}>0
, such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and with
\theta\in(0,\theta_{j})
, there exist
\lambda_{*}
,
\lambda^{*}>0
such that problem (
1.1
) admits at least
j
distinct weak solutions with negative energy for any
\lambda\in(\lambda_{*},\lambda^{*})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a careful combination of variational and topological tools, such as truncation techniques and genus theory, similarly to
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
under the sublinear situation. However, the sandwich growth
k\in(q,p)
does not allow us to provide the existence of infinitely many solutions for (
1.1
). For this, as in the classical case
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, in Theorem
1.1
we can actually prove the existence of a finite number of solutions for (
1.1
), at very least. Indeed, as in
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, we can construct a monotone non-decreasing sequence
\{c_{j}\}_{j}
of critical values by Krasnoselskii’s genus theory. However, we can guarantee that the values
c_{1}\leq c_{2}\leq\ldots\leq c_{j}
are negative just when
\theta<\theta_{j}
and
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
, with possibly
\theta_{j}\to 0
and
\lambda_{*}=\lambda_{*}(\theta_{j})\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
. This is the crucial difference with respect to the sublinear case
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, where the
\{c_{j}\}_{j}
are all negative when
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
is small. In any case, with respect to
[
2
, Theorem 1]
and
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, we are able to set our (
1.1
) on
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
general, and not just
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\Omega
bounded.

In the next result, we study (
1.1
) whenever
\lambda
is sufficiently large, a situation which is not covered by Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set. Let
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
and
1<q<k<p<N/s_{1}
. Assume that
P
is nontrivial, nonnegative and satisfy
(P_{0})
.

Then, there exists
\overline{\lambda}>0
such that for any
\lambda>\overline{\lambda}
, there exists
\theta^{*}=\theta^{*}(\lambda)>0
such that problem (
1.1
) admits a nonnegative weak solution with negative energy for any
\theta\in(0,\theta^{*})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on a minimization argument combined with the Ekeland variational principle. Theorem
1.2
generalizes the local result stated in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
in a fractional setting, allowing
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be a general open set. Indeed, in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
they mainly assume
\Omega
bounded, in order to get the trivial intersection
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, with
q<p
. In this way, they minimize the energy functional on the ball

B_{\varrho}(0)=\left\{u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega):\,\,\|\nabla u\|_{p}\leq\varrho\right\},

that is neglecting the norm
\|\nabla u\|_{q}
. In Theorem
1.2
, we set our variational problem on a nontrivial intersection space
E
, endowed by norm
\|u\|_{E}=[u]_{s_{1},p}+[u]_{s_{2},q}
, as explained in Section
2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we discuss the variational formulation of (
1.1
), proving the compactness property of the energy functional. In Sections
3
and
4
, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, respectively.

2.
Fractional Sobolev Spaces and Functional Framework

Let
\Omega
be any open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
s\in(0,1)
and
m\in(1,\infty)
. We denote with
\|\cdot\|_{m}
the norm of the space
L^{m}(\Omega)
.

We recall the fractional Sobolev space of parameters
s
and
m
as

W^{s,m}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{m}(\Omega):\,\,\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{m}+s}}\in L^{m}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\}.

We define here the density space
Z^{s,m}(\Omega)
, given as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

[u]_{s,m}=\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{m}}{|x-y|^{N+sm}}dxdy\right)^{1/m}.

Even if
Z^{s,m}(\Omega)
is not a real space of functions, but a density one, the choice of it is an improvement with respect to the space

X_{0}^{s,m}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{s,m}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\,\,u=0\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\},

fairly popular in recent papers devoted to nonlocal variational problems.
Indeed, the density result proved in
[
10
, Theorem 6]
does not hold true for
X_{0}^{s,m}(\Omega)
, without assuming more restrictive conditions on the open
bounded set
\Omega
and on its boundary
\partial\Omega
;
see in particular
[
10
, Remark 7]
. In conclusion, if
\Omega
is an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
Z^{s,m}(\Omega)\subset X_{0}^{s,m}(\Omega)
, with possibly
Z^{s,m}(\Omega)\not=X_{0}^{s,m}(\Omega)
.

Furthermore, we recall the fractional Beppo-Levi space of parameters
s
and
m
as

(2.1)

D^{s,m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{m_{s}^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\,\,[u]_{s,m}<\infty\right\}

with
m_{s}^{*}=Nm/(N-sm)
when
N>sm
. It is worth noting that if
\Omega
is any open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\widetilde{u}
denotes the natural extension of any
u\in Z^{s,m}(\Omega)
, then

\widetilde{u}\in D^{s,m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In other words,

(2.2)

Z^{s,m}(\Omega)\subset\left\{u\in L^{m^{*}_{s}}(\Omega):\,\,\widetilde{u}\in D^{s,m}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}

and equality holds when either
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\partial\Omega
is continuous, as shown in
[
11
, Theorem 1.4.2.2]
. In what follows, with abuse of notation, we continue to write
u
in place of
\widetilde{u}
, since the context is clear.

Due to the presence of a fractional
(p,q)
-Laplacian in problem (
1.1
), the solution space is given by

E=Z^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)\cap Z^{s_{2},q}(\Omega),

which is a reflexive Banach space endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{E}=[u]_{s_{1},p}+[u]_{s_{2},q}.

We point out that when
\Omega
is bounded, we can reduce
E=Z^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)
thanks to
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
, since
s_{1}>s_{2}
and
p>q
. While, when
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, we obtain that

E=Z^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap Z^{s_{2},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=D^{s_{1},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap D^{s_{2},q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),

thanks to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

Furthermore, in order to handle the critical Sobolev term in (
1.1
), we set the fractional Sobolev constant as

(2.3)

S:=\inf_{u\in Z^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\dfrac{[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}}{\|u\|^{p}_{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}},

which is strictly positive and well defined thanks to
[
6
, Theorem 5.6]
.

From now on, we assume that
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
,
1<q<k<p<N/s_{1}
,
(P_{0})
and
(Q_{0})
hold true, without further mentioning.
Thus, problem (
1.1
) has a variational structure with the energy functional
I_{\lambda,\theta}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\begin{split}I_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}[u]_{s_{2},q}^{q}-\frac{\lambda}{k}\int_{\Omega}P(x)|u|^{k}dx-\frac{\theta}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\|u\|^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}_{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}.\end{split}

The functional
I_{\lambda,\theta}
is well defined and
I_{\lambda,\theta}\in C^{1}(E)
following a similar argument as in
[
2
, Lemma 1 and Lemma 2]
.
We see that
u\in E
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
\langle I^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u),v\rangle=0
for any
v\in E
, where

\begin{split}\langle I^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u),v\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle_{s_{1},p}+\langle u,v\rangle_{s_{2},q}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}P(x)|u|^{k-2}uvdx-\theta\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-2}uvdx.\end{split}

with

\begin{split}\langle u,v\rangle_{s_{1},p}&=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dxdy,\\
\langle u,v\rangle_{s_{2},q}&=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dxdy.\end{split}

We first study the compactness property for
I_{\lambda,\theta}
, that is the Palais-Smale condition. For this, we say that sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
I_{\lambda,\theta}
at level
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
if

(2.4)

I_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n})\to c\,\mbox{ and }\,I^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n})\to 0\,\mbox{ in }\,E^{*}\,\mbox{ as }\,n\to\infty.

We say that
I_{\lambda,\theta}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at a level
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
(PS)_{c}
for short, if any Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
admits a convergent subsequence.

Let us set
\overline{c}=\overline{c}(\lambda,\theta)
as

(2.5)

\overline{c}:=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)\left[\frac{S^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}}{\theta^{\frac{p}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}}-\lambda^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}\theta^{\frac{-k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}\|P\|_{r}^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}\cdot\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{p-k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}\right)^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}\right]

with
S
given in (
2.3
). We are able to prove the Palais-Smale condition under the threshold given in (
2.5
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\theta>0
. Then, functional
I_{\lambda,\theta}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition for any
c<\overline{c}
, with
\overline{c}
such as in (
2.5
).

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
,
\theta>0
and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
be a
(PS)_{c}
sequence of
I_{\lambda,\theta}
. By
(P_{0})
and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\langle I^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle&=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}\\
&\quad-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)\int_{\Omega}P(x)|u_{n}|^{k}dx\\
&\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}\\
&\quad-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)S^{\frac{-k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}.\end{split}

Thus, by (
2.4
) there exists
\beta_{\lambda,\theta}
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}c+\beta_{\lambda,\theta}\|u_{n}\|_{E}+o(1)&\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}\\
&\quad-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)S^{\frac{-k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k},\end{split}

as
n\to\infty
.

We claim that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
E
. Assume for contradiction that
\|u_{n}\|_{E}\rightarrow\infty
. Then, passing if necessary to a subsequence, still labeled by
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
, at least one component has norm diverging as
n
diverges. Hence we distinguish three situations.

•

Case 1.

[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}\rightarrow\infty
and
[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}
is bounded.

Dividing (
2.6
) by
[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}
, we get

\frac{c}{[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}}+\beta_{\lambda,\theta}\left(\frac{1}{[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k-1}}+\frac{[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}}{[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}}\right)+o(1)\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p-k}-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)S^{\frac{-k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}

which is a contradiction since
p>k
.

•

Case 2.

[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}\rightarrow\infty
and
[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}
is bounded.

Dividing (
2.6
) by
[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}
, we get

\frac{c}{[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}}+\beta_{\lambda,\theta}\left(\frac{[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}}{[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}}+\frac{1}{[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q-1}}\right)+o(1)\geq\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)S^{\frac{-k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}\frac{[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}}{[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}}

which is a contradiction since
p_{s_{1}}^{*}>q
.

•

Case 3.

[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}\rightarrow\infty
and
[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}\rightarrow\infty
.

We can rewrite (
2.6
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}c+\beta_{\lambda,\theta}[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}-\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{2},q}^{q}+o(1)&\geq\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}\\
&\quad-\lambda\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{1}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\right)S^{\frac{-k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}^{k}-\beta_{\lambda,\theta}[u_{n}]_{s_{1},p}\end{split}

which is a contradiction since, simultaneously, we have
q>1
and
p>k>1
.

Thus,
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
E
and by the reflexivity of
E
, considering that
Z^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{k}(\Omega,P)
compactly thanks to
(P_{0})
and
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
, and a function
u\in E

such that

(2.7)

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\text{ in }E,&u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\mbox{ in }L^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}(\Omega),\\
u_{n}\rightarrow u\mbox{ in }L^{k}(\Omega,P),&u_{n}\to u\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\end{array}

as
n\to\infty
.

Furthermore, as shown in the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
,
by (
2.7
) the sequence
\left\{\mathcal{U}_{n}\right\}_{n}
, defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus\mbox{Diag}\,\mathbb{R}^{2N}
by

(x,y)\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{n}(x,y)=\frac{|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N+ps_{1}}{p^{\prime}}}},

is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
as well as
\mathcal{U}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, where

\mathcal{U}(x,y)=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{\frac{N+ps_{1}}{p^{\prime}}}}

and
p^{\prime}
is conjugate of

.
Thus, up to a subsequence, we get
\mathcal{U}_{n}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{U}
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
, and so as

(2.8)

\langle u_{n},\varphi\rangle_{s_{1},p}\rightarrow\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{s_{1},p}

for any

,
since
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\cdot|x-y|^{-\frac{N+ps_{1}}{p}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
.
Similarly, we get as
n\to\infty

(2.9)

\langle u_{n},\varphi\rangle_{s_{2},q}\rightarrow\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{s_{2},q}\

for any
\varphi\in E
.
Thanks to (
2.7

Definition 1

1

) we have

(3.14)

\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(t^{*}_{j}u)\leq(t^{*}_{j})^{q}h_{\lambda}(t^{*}_{j})=:-\varepsilon_{j}<0.

Let us set

\mathbb{S}_{j}:=\left\{u\in E_{j}:\,\,[u]_{s_{1},p}=t^{*}_{j}\right\}.

It is clear that
\mathbb{S}_{j}
is homeomorphic to the
(j-1)
-dimensional sphere
S^{j-1}
. Hence, from Proposition
3.2
we know that
\gamma(\mathbb{S}_{j})=j
. While, by (
3.14
) we have that
\mathbb{S}_{j}\subset\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}}
, with

\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}}:=\left\{u\in E:\,\,\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\leq{-\varepsilon_{j}}\right\},

and so by Proposition
3.2
again, we have

\gamma(\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}})\geq\gamma(\mathbb{S}_{j})=j.

Since
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(0)=0
we have that
0\not\in\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}}
, so that considering
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
is even and continuous, we know that
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}}\in\Sigma_{j}
. Finally, we get

c_{j}=\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{j}}\sup_{u\in A}\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\leq\sup_{u\in\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}^{\,-\varepsilon_{j}}}\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\leq-\varepsilon_{j}<0,

concluding the proof.
∎

Clearly, once
j\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed, the truncated functional
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
verifies Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
if
\lambda\in(\lambda_{j},\lambda^{*})
, with
\lambda^{*}
and
\lambda_{j}
given in (
3.4
) and (
3.13
), respectively. In this direction, we observe that inequality
\lambda_{j}<\lambda^{*}
can be rewritten as

\delta_{j}^{\frac{q(p-k)}{p-q}+k}\cdot\frac{k(p-q)}{(p-k)^{\frac{p-k}{p-q}}(k-q)^{\frac{k-q}{p-q}}}\cdot q^{-\frac{p-k}{p-q}}\cdot p^{\frac{q-k}{p-q}}<\dfrac{1}{\theta^{\frac{p-k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}}\frac{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p)k}{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k)p}\left[\frac{(p-k)p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k)p}\right]^{\frac{p-k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}\frac{S^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}}{\|P\|_{r}},

which holds true if
\theta<\theta_{j}
, with

(3.15)

\theta_{j}:=\delta_{j}^{-\frac{q(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p)}{p-q}-\frac{k(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p)}{p-k}}\cdot\frac{(p-k)p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k)p}\cdot\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}{p-q}}\cdot\left[\frac{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p)(k-q)^{\frac{k-q}{p-q}}}{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k)(p-q)\|P\|_{r}}\right]^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}{p-k}}\cdot S^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}{p-k}}\cdot(p-k)^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}{p-q}}.

From the monotonicity of
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j}
, given in (
3.11
), we deduce that
\theta_{j}>\theta_{j+1}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and let us set
\lambda_{j}
and
\theta_{j}
as in (
3.13
) and (
3.15
), respectively. Let
\theta\in(0,\theta_{j})
, so that
\lambda_{j}<\lambda^{*}
, with
\lambda^{*}
given in (
3.4
). Then, let
\lambda\in(\lambda_{j},\lambda^{*})
and let us consider the minimax sequence
\{c_{j}\}_{j}
given in (
3.10
). By Lemma
3.3
, we know that

-\infty<c_{i}<0,\quad\mbox{for\, any }\,i=1,\ldots,j,

so that Lemma
3.1
(ii)
yields that
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c_{i}}
condition, for any
i=1,\ldots,j
.
Thus,
c_{i}
are critical values for
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
, see
[
13
]
, and setting

K_{c}:=\left\{u\in E\setminus\{0\}:\,\,\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=c\,\mbox{ and }\,\widehat{I}\,^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=0\right\},

we infer that
K_{c_{i}}
are compact, for any
i=1,\ldots,j
.

Now, we distinguish two situations. Either
\{c_{i}:\,\,i=1,\ldots,j\}
are
j
distinct critical values
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
or
c_{n}=c_{n+1}=\ldots=c_{j}=c
for some
n\in\{1,\ldots,j-1\}
. In the second situation, since
K_{c}
is compact, by a deformation lemma, Proposition
3.2
and arguing as in
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
, we get

\gamma(K_{c})\geq j-n+1\geq 2.

Thus,
K_{c}
has infinitely many points, see
[
13
, Remark 7.3]
, which are infinitely many critical values for
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
by Lemma
3.1
(ii)
.

Consequently,
\widehat{I}_{\lambda,\theta}
admits at least
j
negative critical values, which represent at least
j
negative critical values for
I_{\lambda,\theta}
thanks to Lemma
3.1
(i)
.
∎

4.
The existence of a nonnegative solution

We observe that since
P
is a nonnegative nontrivial function satisfying
(P_{0})
, then the set

\mathcal{A}_{+}:=\{\varphi\in E:\,\,\int_{\Omega}P(x)\varphi_{+}^{k}dx>0\}\neq\emptyset.

Indeed, using the hypothesis on
P
and the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, there exists a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}P(x)dx=P(x_{0})>0.

Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\int_{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}P(x)dx>0
. Now choose
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(x_{0},2\varepsilon))
with
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi\equiv 1
in
B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
. Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}P(x)\varphi^{k}dx=\int_{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}P(x)\varphi^{k}dx+\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)\setminus B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}P(x)\varphi^{k}dx\geq\int_{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}P(x)dx>0.

Hence,
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
. Therefore, we can define

(4.1)

\overline{\lambda}:=\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{+}}C_{0}(p,q,k)\left(\frac{[\varphi]^{p}_{s_{1},p}}{p}\right)^{\frac{k-q}{p-q}}\left(\frac{[\varphi]^{q}_{s_{2},q}}{q}\right)^{\frac{p-k}{p-q}}\frac{k}{\int_{\Omega}P(x)\varphi_{+}^{k}dx},

where
C_{0}(p,q,k):=(\frac{p-q}{k-q})^{\frac{k-q}{p-q}}(\frac{p-q}{p-k})^{\frac{p-k}{p-q}}
.

Since we look for a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
), we define the energy functional
J_{\lambda,\theta}:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that

J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}[u]_{s_{2},q}^{q}-\frac{\lambda}{k}\int_{\Omega}P(x)(u_{+})^{k}dx-\frac{\theta}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\int_{\Omega}(u_{+})^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}dx.

Then, dealing with
\overline{\lambda}
given above, we study functional
J_{\lambda,\theta}
on a suitable ball

B_{\varrho}(0)=\left\{u\in E:\,\,\|u\|_{E}\leq\varrho\right\},

proving the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda>\overline{\lambda}
, with
\overline{\lambda}
as defined in (
4.1
). Then, there exist
\varrho>0
,
\alpha>0
and
\widehat{\theta}>0
such that

\inf_{u\in B_{\varrho}(0)}J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)<0<\alpha\leq\inf_{u\in\partial B_{\varrho}(0)}J_{\lambda,\theta}(u),

whenever
\theta\in\left(0,\widehat{\theta}\right)
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>\overline{\lambda}
. Thus, there exists
\varphi\in E
such that

(4.2)

C_{0}(p,q,k)\left(\frac{[\varphi]^{p}_{s_{1},p}}{p}\right)^{\frac{k-q}{p-q}}\left(\frac{[\varphi]^{q}_{s_{2},q}}{q}\right)^{\frac{p-k}{p-q}}\frac{k}{\int_{\Omega}P(x)\varphi_{+}^{k}dx}<\lambda.

Now, for any
t>0
, we have

J_{\lambda,\theta}(t\varphi)=t^{q}f_{\lambda}(t)-\frac{\theta t^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{+}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}dx,

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&f_{\lambda}(t):=d_{1}+d_{2}t^{p-q}-\lambda d_{3}t^{k-q},\quad\mbox{with}\\
&d_{1}:=\frac{[\varphi]_{s_{2},q}^{q}}{q},\quad d_{2}:=\frac{[\varphi]_{s_{1},p}^{p}}{p}\quad\mbox{and}\quad d_{3}:=\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Omega}P(x)\varphi_{+}^{k}dx.\end{split}

We observe that there exists
t^{*}=t^{*}(\lambda)
as

t^{*}:=\left[\lambda\cdot\frac{(k-q)\,d_{3}}{(p-q)\,d_{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-k}},

such that

\min_{t\geq 0}f_{\lambda}(t)=f_{\lambda}\left(t^{*}\right)=d_{1}-\lambda^{\frac{p-q}{p-k}}\cdot\frac{p-k}{p-q}\cdot\left(\frac{k-q}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{k-q}{p-k}}\cdot(d_{2})^{\frac{q-k}{p-k}}\cdot(d_{3})^{\frac{p-q}{p-k}}.

Thus, by (
4.2
) we get

\min_{t\geq 0}f_{\lambda}(t)=f_{\lambda}\left(t^{*}\right)<0,

which yields

J_{\lambda,\theta}(t^{*}\varphi)=\left(t^{*}\right)^{q}f_{\lambda}(t^{*})-\frac{\theta\left(t^{*}\right)^{p^{*}_{s_{1}}}}{p^{*}_{s_{1}}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{+}^{p^{*}_{s_{1}}}dx<0.

Let
\overline{C}_{0}:=\left[\displaystyle\frac{2p}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-k}}
, if we fix
\varrho=\varrho(\lambda)
as

(4.3)

\varrho:=\max\left\{1+t^{*}\|\varphi\|_{E}+2\overline{C}_{0}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-k}},\left[\frac{\lambda q2^{q+1}}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}\left(\overline{C}_{0}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-k}}\right)^{k}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\},

clearly
\|t^{*}\varphi\|_{E}\leq\varrho
implying
t^{*}\varphi\in B_{\varrho}(0)
.
Therefore, we get

\inf_{u\in B_{\varrho}(0)}J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\leq J_{\lambda,\theta}\left(t^{*}\varphi\right)<0.

On the other hand, we claim that there exists
d>0
such that

(4.4)

\inf_{u\in\partial B_{\varrho}(0)}J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\geq d,

for
\theta
sufficiently small.
By
(P_{0})
and (
2.3
), for any
u\in E
we have

(4.5)

J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\geq\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}[u]_{s_{2},q}^{q}-\lambda\frac{S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}}{k}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{k}-\frac{\theta S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}.

Let
u\in E
with
\|u\|_{E}=\varrho
. In order to prove (
4.4
), we distinguish the following two cases.

•

Case 1.

Let
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}\leq\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u]^{k}_{s_{1},p}
, that is
[u]_{s_{1},p}\leq\overline{C}_{0}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-k}}
.

By (
4.3
), we have

[u]_{s_{1},p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varrho=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{E}=\frac{1}{2}\left([u]_{s_{1},p}+[u]_{s_{2},q}\right),

from which
[u]_{s_{1},p}\leq[u]_{s_{2},q}
and so

2[u]_{s_{2},q}\geq\|u\|_{E}.

Furthermore, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u]^{k}_{s_{1},p}\leq\frac{\lambda}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}\left(\overline{C}_{0}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-k}}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{1}{q2^{q+1}}\varrho^{q}=\frac{1}{q2^{q+1}}\|u\|_{E}^{q}.

Using the estimates above, we obtain from (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)\geq\frac{1}{q2^{q}}\|u\|_{E}^{q}-\frac{1}{q2^{q+1}}\|u\|_{E}^{q}-\frac{\theta S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\|u\|_{E}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}=\frac{S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}\varrho^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\left(\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{q2^{q+1}S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}-\theta\right).

Thus, setting

\theta_{a}:=\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{q2^{q+1}S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{a}:=\frac{S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}\varrho^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\left(\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{q2^{q+1}S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}-\theta\right),

we get (
4.4
) with
d=\alpha_{a}
, whenever
\theta\in(0,\theta_{a})
.

•

Case 2.

Let
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}>\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda}{k}S^{-\frac{k}{p}}\|P\|_{r}[u]^{k}_{s_{1},p}
, that is
[u]_{s_{1},p}>\overline{C}_{0}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-k}}
.

In this case, we derive from (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2p}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}[u]_{s_{2},q}^{q}-\frac{\theta S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\|u\|_{E}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2p}\left(\overline{C}_{0}^{p-q}\lambda^{\frac{p-q}{p-k}}[u]_{s_{1},p}^{q}+[u]_{s_{2},q}^{q}\right)-\frac{\theta S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\|u\|_{E}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\overline{C}_{1}}{2^{q}p}\|u\|_{E}^{q}-\frac{\theta S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\|u\|_{E}^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}\varrho^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\left(\frac{\overline{C}_{1}p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{2^{q}pS^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}-\theta\right),

with
\overline{C}_{1}:=\overline{C}_{1}(\lambda)=\min\left\{\overline{C}_{0}^{\,p-q}\lambda^{\frac{p-q}{p-k}},1\right\}
.
Hence, setting

\theta_{b}:=\frac{\overline{C}_{1}p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{2^{q}pS^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{b}:=\frac{S^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}\varrho^{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}\left(\frac{\overline{C}_{1}p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{2^{q}pS^{-\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p}}}\varrho^{q-p_{s_{1}}^{*}}-\theta\right),

we get (
4.4
) with
d=\alpha_{b}
, whenever
\theta\in(0,\theta_{b})
.

It is enough to set
\widehat{\theta}:=\min\{\theta_{a},\theta_{b}\}
and
\alpha:=\min\{\alpha_{a},\alpha_{b}\}
in order to complete the proof.
∎

Proof of the Theorem
1.2
.

We first observe that
\overline{c}
given in (
2.5
) is positive whenever
\theta<\widetilde{\theta}
, with
\widetilde{\theta}=\widetilde{\theta}(\lambda)
set as

(4.6)

\widetilde{\theta}:=\left[\frac{S^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}}}{\left(\lambda\|P\|_{r}\right)^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}}\cdot\frac{k}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}\cdot\left(\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p}{p-k}\right)^{\frac{p_{s_{1}}^{*}}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k}}\right]^{\frac{(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-p)(p_{s_{1}}^{*}-k)}{p_{s_{1}}^{*}(p-k)}}.

Thus, let us fix
\lambda>\overline{\lambda}
, with
\overline{\lambda}
given in (
4.1
). Let us fix
\theta\in\left(0,\theta^{*}\right)
with
\theta^{*}:=\min\left\{\widehat{\theta},\widetilde{\theta}\right\}
, where
\widehat{\theta}
and
\widetilde{\theta}
are as in Lemma
4.1
and (
4.6
) respectively.

Now, let us set the minimum

c_{\varrho}:=\inf_{u\in B_{\varrho}(0)}J_{\lambda,\theta}(u).

Thanks to Lemma
4.1
, we can apply the Ekeland variational principle to
J_{\lambda,\theta}
which provides a minimizing sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset B_{\varrho}(0)
, such that
J_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n})\rightarrow c_{\varrho}
and
J^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
.
However, since
c_{\varrho}<0<\overline{c}
by Lemma
4.1
, we can apply Lemma
2.1
for the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
, so that there exists
u\in E
such that
u_{n}\to u
. Hence
J^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=0
and
J_{\lambda,\theta}(u)=c_{\varrho}<0
. Thus,
u
is a nontrivial weak solution of the problem (
1.1
) with negative energy. Next, we prove that
u
is nonnegative. Indeed, we have

\begin{split}0=\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda,\theta}(u),u_{-}\rangle=&\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dxdy\\
&\quad+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}(u(x)-u(y))(u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dxdy\\
&\geq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps_{1}}}dxdy+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2N}}\dfrac{|u_{-}(x)-u_{-}(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs_{2}}}dxdy\\
&=[u_{-}]_{s_{1},p}^{p}+[u_{-}]_{s_{2},q}^{q},\end{split}

which implies that
u_{-}=0
. Hence,
u\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

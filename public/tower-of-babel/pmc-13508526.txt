The first complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of Veratrum dolichopetalum O.Loes. (Melanthiaceae)
Authors: Hyeonjin Kim, Tae-Soo Jang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractVeratrum dolichopetalum O.Loes. (1926) is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Melanthiaceae that is geographically restricted to northern Korea and the Ussuri River basin of Russia. Here, we present the first complete chloroplast genome of V. dolichopetalum, which is 153,673 bp in length and contains 82 protein-coding gene copies, 38 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. The genome exhibits the typical quadripartite structure, comprising a large single copy region (83,352 bp), a small single copy region (17,603 bp), and a pair of inverted repeats (26,359 bp each). Phylogenetic analysis based on 77 orthologous chloroplast protein-coding genes indicated that V. dolichopetalum is closely related to V. oxysepalum Turcz. The complete chloroplast genome reported here provides essential genomic resources for further evolutionary studies of Veratrum.Keywords: Veratrum, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Veratrum L. (Melanthiaceae Batsch) is a perennial herbaceous genus distributed in woodlands, wetlands, and alpine regions of the Northern Hemisphere (Zomlefer et al. 2003). Species within the genus are characterized by erect stems, plicate and prominently veined leaves, a terminal panicle, and an andromonoecious reproductive system (Chen and Takahashi 2000). Molecular phylogenetic studies (Zomlefer et al. 2003; Kim et al. 2014; Zhang et al. 2022) and micromorphological analyses (Hu et al. 2020) have improved our understanding of evolutionary relationships within the genus. Among Veratrum species, V. dolichopetalum O.Loes. is distinguished by densely flowered inflorescences with closely appressed lateral branches and narrow tepals (Loesener 1927). To our knowledge, no complete chloroplast genome has yet been reported for V. dolichopetalum, limiting comparative and systematic investigations involving this taxon.

Complete chloroplast genome sequences are widely used in plant systematics and comparative phylogenomics because they are generally structurally conserved, usually uniparentally inherited, often maternally in angiosperms, and informative for resolving relationships among closely related taxa (Birky 1995; Yang et al. 2013; Greiner et al. 2015; Luo et al. 2021). Recent population-level and comparative phylogenomic studies have further demonstrated the utility of chloroplast genome data for assessing genome diversity, maternal lineage structure, and potential marker regions (Kan et al. 2024; Dong et al. 2025). However, chloroplast genome-based relationships should be interpreted cautiously because the chloroplast genome is usually inherited as a single non-recombining unit and may be affected by introgression, hybridization, or incomplete lineage sorting. Several Veratrum chloroplast genomes have been reported and used to clarify interspecific relationships (Han et al. 2019; Chen et al. 2021; Men et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2022). Nevertheless, a complete chloroplast genome for V. dolichopetalum has not yet been reported. Here, we report the complete chloroplast genome of V. dolichopetalum and assess its phylogenetic placement within Veratrum.

Materials and methods

Veratrum dolichopetalum was collected in June 2024 from Mandae-ri, Haean-myeon, Yanggu-gun, Gangwon-do, South Korea (N 38.249008 E 128.115303; Figure 1). The voucher specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of Chungnam National University (CNUK; Tae-Soo Jang, jangts@cnu.ac.kr) under voucher number PUN01-1. Total genomic DNA was extracted from the dried leaves material using the AccuPrep® Plant Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (Bioneer, Daejeon, Korea). Library preparation and Illumina paired-end sequencing were conducted by Macrogen Inc. (Seoul, Republic of Korea), generating 48,810,246 reads. After trimming low-quality reads, chloroplast genome assembly was performed in Geneious Prime 2026.0.2 using a reference-guided approach based on V. oxysepalum (NC057090). A separate de novo assembly was not used as the final assembly pipeline. To validate the assembly, raw reads were mapped back to the assembled chloroplast genome, and read coverage, quadripartite structure and LSC/IRb, IRb/SSC, SSC/IRa, and IRa/LSC junction regions were inspected. Genome annotation was performed in Geneious Prime 2026.0.2, and the circular genome map was generated using OGDRAW v1.3.1 (Greiner et al. 2019). Simple sequence repeats (SSRs) were identified using MISA (Beier et al. 2017) with thresholds of 10, 5, 4, 3, 3 and 3 repeat units for mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, penta- and hexa-nucleotide repeats, respectively. The complete chloroplast genome of V. dolichopetalum was deposited in NCBI GenBank (accession no. PZ033175.1).

Photographs of Veratrum dolichopetalum O.Loes. (A) habit showing a terminal, densely flowered panicle with closely appressed lateral branches, (B) flowers with narrow tepals, and (C) strongly plicate, prominently veined leaves. Photographs were taken by Hyeonjin Kim in Yanggu-gun, South Korea.

Publicly available complete chloroplast genomes of Veratrum were checked in NCBI GenBank on 23 June 2026, and one representative accession per accepted species or taxon was selected to avoid redundant conspecific or duplicated sampling (Table S1). The phylogenetic analysis included 14 Veratrum taxa, using Chionographis japonica (PV786082) and Helonias koreana (NC027159) as outgroups. Protein-coding sequences were extracted from GenBank files, and duplicated CDS copies in the IR regions were represented by a single copy. In total, 77 orthologous chloroplast protein-coding genes shared by all 16 taxa were retained. Pseudogenes (infA and a partial ycf1 copy) were excluded from the phylogenetic matrix, whereas the functional ycf1 CDS was retained. rps16 was absent from the common annotated CDS dataset. The 77 genes were aligned separately using MAFFT v.7.505 (Katoh and Standley 2013) and concatenated using PhyloSuite v.2.0 (Zhao et al. 2025). The final alignment was 69,121 bp, and gene boundaries, alignment lengths and substitution models are provided in Table S2. Maximum likelihood analysis was performed using IQ-TREE v3.0.1 (Nguyen et al. 2015), with model selection by ModelFinder and branch support evaluated using 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates (UFBoot2).

Results

The chloroplast genome assembly of Veratrum dolichopetalum was supported by 1,588,768 mapped reads, with an average depth of 1556× (Figure S1). Read coverage was inspected across the chloroplast genome, including the junction regions, and no major structural rearrangement was detected relative to the V. oxysepalum chloroplast genome. The genome was 153,673 bp in length and showed a typical quadripartite structure consisting of a large single copy (LSC: 83,352 bp), a small single copy (SSC: 17,603 bp) and a pair of inverted repeats (IRs: 26,359 bp) (Figure 2). A total of 130 genes were annotated, comprising 82 protein-coding gene copies, 38 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, 8 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes and two pseudogenes (infA and a partial ycf1 copy) (Figure 2). The overall GC content was 37.7%, and the average GC contents of protein-coding genes, rRNA genes, and tRNA genes were 38.0%, 55.2%, and 53.4%, respectively. Duplicated CDS copies were detected for ndhB, rpl2, rpl23, rps7 and ycf2. Cis-splicing genes included atpF, clpP, ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, and rpoC1 (Figure S2), and rps12 was identified as a trans-splicing gene (Figure S3). A total of 70 SSRs were identified, with mono-nucleotide repeats being the most abundant.

Based on 77 orthologous chloroplast protein-coding genes from 14 Veratrum taxa, the phylogenetic analysis recovered two strongly supported clades corresponding to section Veratrum and section Fuscoveratrum (ultrafast bootstrap = 100; Figure 3). Veratrum dolichopetalum was placed in section Veratrum and clustered with V. oxysepalum. The corresponding topology including the two outgroups is provided in Figure S4.

Discussion and conclusion

This study reported the complete chloroplast genome of V. dolichopetalum. Its genome structure, GC content, gene content and SSR composition were generally similar to those of other Veratrum species (Chen et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2022), and comparative features across the 14 sampled Veratrum taxa are summarized in Table S3. The chloroplast genome of V. dolichopetalum was similar in size and gene content to those of other members of section Veratrum, particularly V. oxysepalum, V. grandiflorum and V. album. The 70 SSRs identified in V. dolichopetalum may provide candidate chloroplast markers for future population-level, phylogeographic and conservation genetic studies, although their polymorphism and diagnostic utility require testing with multiple individuals and broader taxon sampling.

The phylogenetic analysis supported the separation of section Veratrum and section Fuscoveratrum, consistent with previous molecular studies (Kim et al. 2014; Zhang et al. 2022). In the chloroplast genome-based phylogeny, V. dolichopetalum was resolved as closely related to V. oxysepalum. However, this result should be interpreted as chloroplast genome evidence rather than definitive evidence of species-level relationships, because introgression, chloroplast capture and incomplete lineage sorting may affect chloroplast genome-based inference. Despite the close relationship suggested by chloroplast genome data, V. dolichopetalum remains morphologically distinguishable by its densely flowered inflorescence with closely appressed lateral branches and narrow tepals (Loesener 1927). Because the present study did not include formal marker-discrimination tests and taxon sampling remains incomplete relative to the full diversity of Veratrum, broader taxon sampling and additional nuclear genomic evidence will be necessary to test species boundaries and relationships within Veratrum more rigorously.

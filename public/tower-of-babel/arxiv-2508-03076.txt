Cohomologies and linear deformations of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras
Sylvain Attan and Nabil Oro Djibril

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to introduce an algebraic cohomology theory of left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras. We use the cohomological approach to study linear deformations of these algebras. We also introduce the notion of Nijenhuis operators on pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and we show that the deformation generated by a Nijenhuis operator is trivial.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
17C50,16W10,17B56.

Keywords:
Pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras, cohomologies, linear deformations.

1.
Introduction

Left (resp. right) pre-Jacobi Jordan algebras are algebras whose anti-associator is left (resp. right) skew-symmetric. This justifies the name " left(resp. right) skew-symmetric algebras" given for these algebras, first studied in
[
4
]
. There is a close relationship between pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and Jacobi-Jordan algebras: a pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\cdot)
gives rise to a Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\ast)
via the anticommutator
multiplication, i.e., pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras are Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebras. Moreover, it is observed that they form a particular class of Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebras in
[
5
]
, where various constructions of these algebras are discussed, in particular an inductive description is provided. If A is left
pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra, then the algebra defined on the same vector space
A
with "opposite” multiplication
x\bullet y:=y\cdot x
is a right pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra and vice-versa. Hence, all the statements for left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras have their
corresponding statements for right pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras. Thus, we will only consider the left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra case that we call pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra for short.

The deformation theory was first started mainly in geometry and physics. More precisely, in quantum mechanics, the non-commutative associative product of operators is viewed as a formal associative deformation of the pointwise product of the algebraic structure on symbols of these operators. Therefore, in several works
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
, Murray Gerstenhaber introduced algebraic formal deformations of associative algebras and proved that this theory is intimately linked to the cohomology of considered algebras. For further information on the extension of such approach for other algebraic structures, one may refer to, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
7
]
and their references.

Even though there are important applications of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras, cohomologies of these algebras have not been developed due perhaps to the dfficulty.
The purpose of this paper is to fill this gap. Precisely, we introduce the cohomology theory of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and give its applications. As Jacobi-Jordan algebtras case
[
3
]
, the complex provided here is defined by two sequences of operators

d^{i}
and
\delta^{i}
which satisfy
d^{p+1}\circ\delta^{p}=0
for any integer
p\geq 0
. It is called a zigzag cohomology since it deals with two types of cochains and two sequences of operators. Next, we study linear deformations of Jacobi-Jordan algebras and prove that these deformations are in one-to-one correspondence with the elements of the second zigzag cohomology group. This shows that the zigzag cohomology fits perfectly and provides the expected results.

The outline of the paper is as follows: In the second section, some basic notions
and concepts used through the paper, namely some properties of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and
their representations are given.
In section 3, we introduce a cohomology theory of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras. Next, we compute the first and second cohomology groups and give interpretations of these groups.
In Section 4, we study linear deformation theory of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and show that the cohomology theory introduced in Section 3 fits. In section 5, we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra and show that it generates a trivial deformation of this pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

Throughout this paper, all vector spaces and algebras are meant over a commutative ground field
\mathbb{K}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
6
]

A Jacobi-Jordan algebra is an algebra
(A,\ast)
such that

\displaystyle x\ast y=y\ast x\mbox{ ( commutativity), }

(1)

\displaystyle J(x,y,z):=\circlearrowleft_{(x,y,z)}(x\ast y)\ast z)=0\mbox{ \ \ $\forall x,y,z\in A$,}

where
\circlearrowleft_{(x,y,z)}
is the sum over cyclic permutation of
x,y,z.
The map

J
is called the Jacobian of
(A,\cdot)
.

In
[
6
]
, it is proved that an algebra is a commutative algebra such that
x^{3}=0,\ \forall x\in A
if and only if it is a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. Moreover, it is found
[
4
]
that every Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\cdot)
is a nilpotent Jordan algebra such that
x^{3}=0,\ \forall x\in A.

Definition 2.2
.

[
3
]
[
12
]

A representation of a Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(A,\ast)
is a couple
(V,\rho)
where
V
is a vector space and
\rho:A\to gl(V)
is a linear map such that

(2)

\displaystyle\rho(x\ast y)=-\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)-\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)\mbox{ \ \ $\forall x,y\in A$.}

Let prove the following elementary result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}:=(A_{1},\ast_{1})
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}:=(A_{2},\ast_{2})
be two Jacobi-Jordan algebras and
f:\mathcal{A}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}
be a morphism of Jacobi-Jordan algebras. Then
A_{2}^{f}:=(A_{2},\rho)
is a representation of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
where

\rho(a)b:=f(a)\ast_{2}b
for all
(a,b)\in A_{1}\times A_{2}.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in A_{1}
and
b\in A_{2}
, we have :

\begin{array}[]{lll}\rho(x\ast_{1}y)b+\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)b+\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)b\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{3})}}{{=}}\biggl(f(x)\ast_{2}f(y)\biggr)\ast_{2}b+\biggl(f(y)\ast_{2}b\biggr)\ast_{2}f(x)\\
+\biggl(b\ast_{2}f(x)\biggr)\ast_{2}f(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{3})}}{{=}}0.\end{array}

Hence,
\rho(x\ast_{1}y)=-\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x).

∎

Definition 2.6
.

An algebra
(A,\cdot)
is called

(1)

a left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra if

(5)

\displaystyle Aasso(x,y,z)+Aasso(y,x,z)=0,

\displaystyle\forall x,y,z\in A,

or equivalently

(6)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)+(y\cdot x)\cdot z+y\cdot(x\cdot z)=0\mbox{ for all $x,y,z\in A.$}

(2)

a right pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras if

(7)

\displaystyle Aasso(x,y,z)+Aasso(x,z,y)=0,

\displaystyle\forall x,y,z\in A,

or equivalently

(8)

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)+(x\cdot z)\cdot y+x\cdot(z\cdot y)=0\mbox{ for all $x,y,z\in A.$}

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an algebra and
\bullet
be the bilinear map defined by

a\bullet b=b\cdot a\mbox{ $\forall a,b\in A.$}

Then,
(A,\cdot)
is a left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra if and only if
(A,\bullet)
is a right pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in A
we have :

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)+(y\cdot x)\cdot z+y\cdot(x\cdot z)

\displaystyle=z\bullet(y\bullet x)+(z\bullet y)\bullet x+z\bullet(x\bullet y)+(z\bullet x)\bullet y.

Hence,

(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)+(y\cdot x)\cdot z+y\cdot(x\cdot z)=0

if and only if

z\bullet(y\bullet x)+(z\bullet y)\bullet x+z\bullet(x\bullet y)+(z\bullet x)\bullet y=0.

∎

Proposition 2.12

4.
Linear deformations of pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras

In this section, we study a deformation theory for pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras and show that the cohomology introduced in the previous section fits with formal deformations of left pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\omega(x,y)\cdot z+\omega(y,x)\cdot z+\omega(x\cdot y,z)+\omega(y\cdot x,z)+\omega(x,y\cdot z)+\omega(y,x\cdot z)\\

(44)

\displaystyle\omega(\omega(x,y),z)+\omega(x,\omega(y,z))+\omega(\omega(y,x),z)+\omega(y,\omega(x,z))=0.

Note that Eq.(

Definition 4.3

(A,\cdot)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\bigg(x\cdot N(y)+N(x)\cdot y+\omega^{\prime}(x,y)-\omega(x,y)-N(x\cdot y)\bigg)t\\

(45)

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(x,y)-\omega(x,y)=-N(x)\cdot y-x\cdot N(y)+N(x\cdot y),

Proposition 4.4

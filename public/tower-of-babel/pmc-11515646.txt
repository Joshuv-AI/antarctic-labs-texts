A nomogram for predicting postpartum post-traumatic stress disorder: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Xiaoting Wang, Jiaxin Ren, Linping Kang, Jing Lu, Hongxia Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostpartumpost-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), as a psychological stress disorder, has long-term and widespread harm. Still, compared with other postpartum psychiatric disorders, postpartum PTSD has received relatively littleattention in China. This study aims to investigate the risk factors of postpartum PTSD and to develop a convenient and rapid nomogram screening tool to help clinical staff identify high-risk pregnant womenin time and take preventative and management measures.MethodsRecruited pregnant women hospitalized for delivery in Qingdao Municipal Hospital and Jinzhou Maternal and Child Health Hospital from November 2022 to October 2023 as convenient samples for the questionnaire survey. Telephone follow-up was conducted 42 days after delivery. After univariate analysis, multicollinearity analysis, and logistic regression analysis, the risk factors of postnatal PTSD were obtained, a prediction model was established, and a nomogram was drawn by R software. G*power3.1.9.7 calculated the effectiveness of the test. The model was validated internally using the Bootstrap approach, and external validation was carried out using a verification group. The accuracy of the model's predictions and its clinical application value were evaluated by the area under the curve, calibration plot, and decision curve analysis.ResultsA total of 602 women were recruited in this study, and the incidence of postpartum PTSD was 11.1% (67/602). Multifactorial logistic regression analysis showed that poor self-assessment of sleep status in late pregnancy (OR = 5.336), cesarean section (OR = 2.825), instrumental delivery (OR = 5.994), having fear of labor (OR = 4.857), and a high score of Five Factors Inventory Neuroticism subscale (OR = 1.244) were independent risk factors for developing postpartum PTSD. A high Quality of Relationship Index score (OR = 0.891) was a protective factor for postpartum PTSD. In the training and validation sets, the nomogram model's area under the ROC curve was 0.928 and 0.907, respectively. The calibration curves showed that the nomogram model was well-fitted, and the Decision Curve Analysis indicated that the nomogram model had good value for clinical application.ConclusionsWith its strong predictive capacity, the prediction model built using postpartum PTSD risk factors can help clinical caregivers identify high-risk pregnant women early on and implement timely preventive intervention strategies.Keywords: Post-traumatic stress disorder, Childbirth, Risk factors, Nomogram

Background

The birth of a new baby is usually joyful for the family, but the birth itself can be a traumatic experience for the mother [1]. It has been reported that 19.74% to 45.03% of women consider childbirth to be a traumatic experience, of which 1.52% to 6.37% will develop postpartum post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) [2, 3]. Postpartum PTSD is a delayed-onset psychological stress disorder that occurs in postpartum women after childbirth [4]. Postpartum PTSD is mainly characterized by negative emotions during re-experiencing childbirth, avoidance of things related to childbirth, heightened vigilance, and a state of over-stress [4]. Global epidemiology shows that the prevalence of postpartum PTSD is between 1 and 30%, and is increasing year by year [5]. The prevalence of postpartum PTSD in China ranges from approximately 1.13% to 16.78%, showing a trend higher than the international average [6, 7]. Early identification, monitoring, and prevention of postpartum PTSD risk are extremely important. As an emotional stress disorder, postpartum PTSD has lasting and widespread harm, which has been compared to a "ripple effect" [8]. Postpartum PTSD can affect women's physical and mental health, leading to fear of pregnancy and childbirth, and even affecting their birth plans for future pregnancies [9]. In addition, postpartum PTSD can impair sexual relationships, cause disagreement between couples about life events and blame for childbirth events, and have a huge impact on the cognitive and behavioral development of infants and children [10, 11]. If left untreated, it will evolve into chronic PTSD, leading to an increased incidence of psychiatric disorders such as maternal postpartum depression and generalized anxiety disorder, and even causing serious consequences such as postpartum maternal suicide or infanticide [12]. Therefore, to provide effective preventative and early intervention measures, it is imperative that postpartum PTSD be the center of attention and that the risk factors for postpartum PTSD be understood.

Different studies vary in their results due to socio-cultural differences, the population surveyed, and the type and content of the scale. The two-dimensional predictor model of posttraumatic stress disorder constructed by Slade et al. summarized the influencing factors of posttraumatic stress disorder from the two dimensions of time and environment, which were mainly divided into four categories [13]. (1) Prenatal susceptibility factors: common ones include prior mental health problems, age, economics, multiple births, etc. [14]. Sabrina et al. conducted a cross-sectional study investigating 685 women in labor and showed that young age and low education were risk factors for postnatal PTSD [15]. Fear of childbirth is a subjective negative emotion. Takegata et al. investigated 464 pregnant women after 32 weeks of gestation, 3 days, and 1 month after delivery and showed that fear of childbirth was a risk factor for postpartum PTSD [16]. Fadime et al. showed that low income characterized women at higher risk of developing PTSD [17]. In addition, maternal employment status and health insurance status are also factors that influence the level of postpartum PTSD in women [7]. (2) Risk factors at the time of delivery: For some psychologically vulnerable women, the match between the mode of delivery and expectations can affect their psychological stress level, which can lead to PTSD symptoms [18]. The results of a cross-sectional study of 1531 Spanish women by Antonio et al. [19] confirmed this idea, with instrumental delivery, cesarean section, and third/fourth-degree perineal tear as risk factors for postpartum PTSD. (3) Stress-related factors: Azijada's study showed that neurotic personality was a positive predictor of postpartum PTSD, with higher neurotic personality tendencies being associated with a greater likelihood of postpartum PTSD occurring [20]. This may be because a neurotic personality is a susceptible personality to stress, which can lead to maternal emotional instability and nervousness [21]. When faced with stress, women in labor tend to adopt negative coping methods such as avoidance and withdrawal and react too sensitively and strongly to various stimuli in the environment and life [21]. (4) Other influencing factors: Previous research has shown that adequate social support, good sleep, and high-quality intimate relationships can effectively alleviate PTSD symptoms [22–24]. In the face of various negative stressful events related to labor and delivery, mothers may experience different degrees of psychological problems, which in turn may lead to perinatal psychosomatic disorders.

With the introduction of the "biopsychosocial" model of medicine, more attention is being paid to maternal mental health in addition to physical health. However, current research on maternal postpartum mood disorders in China focuses on postpartum anxiety and postpartum depression. Postpartum PTSD, as a typical subtype of postpartum mood disorders, has only received attention from a few researchers [24]. Currently, postpartum PTSD mainly relies on scale diagnosis, which requires that the patient's PTSD symptoms last for 30 days and cannot be applied promptly, and there is a lack of research tools for postpartum PTSD risk prediction in China. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a convenient and fast early warning tool. Nomogram, as an emerging predictive model, has been widely used in the prediction of risk events, which can integrate various risk factors in the same plane and present them in an intuitive graphical form [25]. Compared with the Logistic model, the nomogram is simple, easy to operate, and more suitable for personalized prediction. In this study, we analyzed the risk factors of postpartum PTSD based on the "biopsychosocial" medical model and established and validated the relevant nomogram risk prediction model. We facilitated the development of an early postpartum PTSD risk assessment tool to inform and guide the prevention and early intervention of PTSD in postpartum women.

Materials and methods

Design

This study was a prospective cohort study in which women who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled in the obstetrics departments of two tertiary-level hospitals in Qingdao City and Jinzhou City from November 2022 to October 2023 and were followed up by telephone 42 days after their discharge from the hospitals for the assessment of postpartum PTSD. According to the hospitals, the mothers were divided into a training set (Qingdao Municipal Hospital) and a validation set (Jinzhou Maternity and Infant Hospital). According to the requirement of the ratio of constructed (70%) and validated (30%) models recommended by Poldrack et al. [26], 602 samples were finally included in this study, of which 420 cases (70%) were in the training set and 182 cases (30%) were in the validation set. The model was developed and internally validated using the training set, and it was externally validated using the validation set. Based on whether PTSD occurred in 42 days postpartum, the enrolled mothers were categorized into PTSD and non‐PTSD groups in this study.

Participants

Inclusion criteria: ① maternal age ≥ 20 years old (reaching the legal age for marriage in China); ② delivery between 37 and 42 weeks of gestation; ③ normal communication and understanding of the questionnaire; ④ consenting to fill out the survey and signing the informed consent document. Exclusion criteria: ① history of major physical trauma, cognitive dysfunction, or mental illness; ②abnormal postpartum outcomes; ③ positive postnatal depression with an EPDS score of > 13; ④ non-single births; ⑤ mothers with incomplete clinical data and no follow-up. The study was carried out by the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by Jinzhou Medical University's Medical Ethics Committee (JZMULL2023064).

Measures

General information questionnaire

In this study, we determined the general factors by searching the literature and experts' opinions, combined with the "biopsychosocial" medical model. Based on the pre-survey, a postnatal PTSD questionnaire with 17 relevant factors was developed with due consideration of its feasibility and validity. (1) General information: age, education level, the only child, income, employment, living with parents, separation from husband, and having medical insurance. (2) Clinical information: history of trauma, parity, planned pregnancy, method of conception, having adverse maternity history, having pregnancy complications, experiencing discomfort during pregnancy, mode of birth, and self-assessment of sleep in late pregnancy.

Perinatal Post-traumatic Stress Disorder Questionnaire (PPQ)

Created in 1996 by De Mier et al. [27] and based on the DSM-IV, the PPQ is a self-report measure designed to help identify women who exhibit symptoms of postpartum depression between one and eighteen months after giving birth. The scale consists of fourteen entries, scored according to Likert five, with a total score of zero to fifty-six and a total score of ≥ nineteen to diagnose postpartum PTSD [24]. This study used the Chinese version of the PPQ scale [28], which has good validity and reliability with a content validity index of 0.990 and a Cronbach's α coefficient of 0.840. In this investigation, the Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.899.

Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS)

Cox et al. [29] created the EPDS in 1987 to screen for and measure postpartum depression. The scale consists of ten entries. Each entry is scored from zero to three in order from "none" to "always", with a total score of zero to thirty. The higher the score, the more severe the depression, and when the score is ≥ 13, it is recognized as the presence of depression [30]. This study employed the Chinese version of the EPDS, which has a content validity score of 0.843 and a Cronbach's α coefficient of 0.901 [31].

Quality of Relationship Index (QRI)

Patrick et al. [32] created and updated the QRI to gauge people's opinions and assessments of their existing relationships with their partners. The scale consists of six items, each of which is scored from one to seven, ranging from "totally disagree" to "strongly agree", with a total score of six to forty-two, with higher scores indicating greater satisfaction with the current relationship. This study used the Chinese version of the QRI, and the scale's Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.910. In this investigation, the QRI's Cronbach's α coefficient was 0.949.

Five Factors Inventory Neuroticism Subscale (FFI‐N)

Costa et al. [33] created the FFI‐N to measure people's emotional stability and capacity to control their emotions. A total of sixty entries in the Five Factors Inventory. The neuroticism subscale used in this study has twelve entries, of which questions one, four, seven, and ten are reverse scored, with each entry scored one to five in order of "very much not in agreement" to "very much in agreement". There were twelve to sixty total points available, with a higher score indicating more pronounced neuroticism. In this study, the Chinese version of the FFI‐N was adopted to measure neuroticism levels, and the Cronbach's α coefficient for this scale was 0.840 [34].

Childbirth Attitudes Questionnaires (CAQ)

Lowe et al. [35] created the CAQ to gauge the level of fear that expectant mothers have about giving delivery. The scale has sixteen items spread across four dimensions, and each entry is assigned a score between zero and four, ranging from "none" to "severe". The total score ranges from sixteen to sixty—four, with higher scores denoting a greater degree of fear of childbirth and a total score of ≥ twenty-eight points denoting the existence of birthing fear [36]. In this study, we applied the Chinese version of CAQ [36], which has a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.910, retest reliability of 0.803, and a content validity index of 0.924.

Discriminative criteria for self-assessment of sleep in late pregnancy

Sleep quality is a composite measure of sleep duration, sleep depth, and sleep performance [37]. The American Sleep Foundation recommends an optimal length of sleep of seven to eight hours per day for adults, with seven hours being the minimum daily sleep limit [38]. Women in late pregnancy have a high prevalence of sleep disorders and often experience sleep problems such as difficulty falling asleep, frequent night awakenings, daytime sleepiness, and restless legs syndrome [39, 40]. Based on the literature review and expert consultation, we determined the self-assessed sleep status in late pregnancy as follows: ①Sleep duration < 7h/d, difficulty in falling asleep, frequent night awakenings, increased insomnia, and daytime sleepiness are considered poor sleep. ② Sleep duration ≥ 7h/d, ability to fall asleep in a short period, no frequent night waking, and no fatigue after waking are considered good sleep conditions.

Data collection

Data collection was conducted in three stages. Stage one: General information, QRI, FFI‐N, and CAQ were collected after the pregnant women were processed for admission to the hospital. Stage two: The EPDS was administered to the mothers before discharge from the hospital, and those with an EPDS < 13, i.e., negative for postpartum depression, were included in the study. Stage three: Telephone follow-up of mothers in 42 days postpartum to assess and screen for PTSD. Before the study's commencement, the project team members were uniformly trained and assessed. At the beginning of the study, members of the project team verified the completeness and validity of the questionnaires on the spot, after they had been completed by the pregnant women. Communication with the mother in advance of her discharge from the hospital to agree on the time and manner of the follow-up visit, and ensure that she understands the purpose and benefits of the visit and is willing to accept the telephone follow-up visit. The researcher herself supervised the members of the project team to check the content and collection of the questionnaires again before the end of each day's work, to ensure that the collection of all parts of the questionnaires and the follow-up visits were completed on time. After 654 surveys were sent out, 602 valid surveys were gathered, yielding a 92.04% valid recovery rate.

Data analysis

Data was statistically analyzed using SPSS 26.0, R 4.3.1, and G*power 3.1.9.7. Measures that were not normally distributed were expressed as medians and quartiles, and the Mann–Whitney U test was used for between-group comparisons. The count data was expressed as frequencies and percentages, and the χ2test was used for comparison between groups. To facilitate statistical analysis and interpretation of results, CAQ and PPQ scores were converted to categorical variables. Univariate analyses were conducted for general maternal conditions and postpartum PTSD. Multifactorial analysis was performed for variables that were statistically significant in the univariate analysis. First, the independent variables with P< 0.05 were analyzed for multicollinearity, and if the variance inflation factor (VIF) was < 5, it indicated that there as no multicollinearity among the independent variables [41]. Next, statistically significant variables were included in the binary logistic regression analysis to determine the entry level = 0.05 and the exclusion level = 0.01 to obtain independent risk factors and logistic regression equations for postpartum PTSD. The odds ratios (OR) of factors associated with postpartum PTSD and their 95% confidence intervals (CI) were also estimated. The predictive model was developed using the "rms" package in R. Bootstrap autonomous sampling method was used for internal validation of the training set data and external validation of the validation set data. The area under the subject work characteristic curve was used to assess the discrimination of the model, the calibration curve to evaluate the calibration of the model, and the clinical decision curve to evaluate the clinical validity of the model, with α = 0.05 as the test level.

Results

Participant characteristics

In this study, 656 cases in total were included; 25 cases were lost to follow-up. 29 cases in all were removed for noncompliance, 20 of which had EPDS scores greater than 13 and 9 of which had more than 20% of the total number of entries missing. See Fig. 1for a flowchart of the maternity inclusion–exclusion process. A total of 620 maternity were included in this study, 46 of the 420 maternity in the training set developed PTSD, with an incidence rate of 10.95%; 21 of the 182 maternity in the verification set developed PTSD, with an incidence rate of 11.53%; the total incidence of postpartum PTSD was 11.1% (67/602). The percentage of women with a bachelor's degree was 64.5%, 86.0% of women had health insurance, and more than half of women gave birth by vaginal delivery. Univariate analysis of the occurrence of PTSD in postpartum women

Women in the training group were divided into PTSD and non-PTSD groups according to whether or not they developed PTSD on the 42nd day postpartum, and univariate analysis was performed. The results showed that the two groups' employment (P= 0.041), income (P= 0.013), mode of birth (P< 0.001), self-assessment of sleep in late pregnancy (P= 0.002), fear of childbirth (P< 0.001), FFI-N score (P< 0.001), QRI score (P< 0.001), EPDS score (P< 0.001). The difference was statistically significant (P< 0.05). The age differences (P= 0.865), education level (P= 0.276), having medical insurance (P= 0.489), parity (P= 0.919), and method of conception (P= 0.302) between the two groups were not statistically significant (P> 0.05). Construction and internal validation of a prediction model for the risk of PTSD in postpartum women

According to the results of logistic regression analysis, the logistic regression equation was established as follows: Logit (P) = -6.792 + 1.675 × Self-assessment of sleep status in late pregnancy + 1.580 × Having fear of childbirth + 1.039 × Cesarean section + 1.791 × instrumental delivery + 0.218 × FFI‐N score -0.116 × QRI score. Based on the establishment of logistic regression equations, the nomogram, a risk prediction model for postpartum PTSD, was constructed using R 4.3.1 software. Based on the scores corresponding to the vertex axis of each factor in the nomogram, the total score was calculated by summing the scores, and the probability of postpartum PTSD could be obtained by drawing a straight line from the corresponding total score point, see Fig. 2. The predictive model Hosmer‐Lemeshow test χ2= 12.658, P= 0.124, calibration graph in Fig. 3. The area under the ROC curve was 0.928, 95% CI was 0.885–0.971, the maximum value of the Jordon index was 0.755, the best cutoff value was 0.122, the sensitivity was 0.891, and the specificity was 0.864 (Fig. 4). DCA is shown in Fig. 5. Of the 420 women who gave birth, the prediction model judged that PTSD would occur in 30 cases and would not occur in 365 cases, and PTSD actually occurred in 46 cases, and the model misjudged 16 cases, with a positive correct rate of 65.2%, a negative correct rate of 97.6%, and an overall correct rate of 94.0%.

Validation of predictive models

The validation set is selected for external validation of the model. The results showed that the Hosmer‐Lemeshow test χ2= 9.697,P= 0.287, and the calibration plot is shown in Fig. 3. Discussion

The results of this study showed that the incidence of PTSD at 42 days postpartum was 11.1%. This is similar to Chen's finding (12.1%) [30] but higher than that reported in the study by Sergio, who also chose PPQ as a measurement tool [43]. This may be due to factors such as the sample included in the study, the different economic and cultural backgrounds of China and the West, and the different distribution of healthcare resources. For example, Sergio's study only included women who delivered vaginally, whereas the present study included women who delivered by cesarean section as well as vaginally assisted delivery. In addition, this study assessed postpartum PTSD in women at 42 days postpartum, which may also account for the difference in results. Global epidemiology shows that the prevalence of postpartum PTSD is in the range of 1% to 30% [5], and the prevalence of postpartum PTSD in China is about 1.13% to 16.78%, which shows a trend higher than the international average [6, 7]. Therefore, it is particularly important to establish a risk prediction model to identify the risk of postpartum PTSD early and prevent it. In this study, five factors were screened by logistic regression to establish a visual nomogram prediction model. Its risk prediction ability was 0.928 and 0.907 on the training set and validation set, respectively. There was no significant difference between the calibration curve prediction value and the measured value, indicating that the model has sufficient discriminative ability and clinical practical value.

The obstetrics medical staff assigns a value to each of the five variables in the nomogram prediction model, calculating the total score. Distinguish between high and low-risk populations based on the best cutoff value of 0.122. This not only reduces the computational burden on the user but also saves evaluation time. For example, a woman delivered by cesarean Sect. (12.5 points), had poor self-assessed sleep in late pregnancy (23.5 points), had fear of birth (20 points), and had a QRI score of 30 (15 points) NEO-FFI score of 25 (42.5 points). The total score for the woman is 12.5 + 23.5 + 20 + 15 + 42.5 = 113.5 by analyzing the nomogram. The likelihood of this woman developing postpartum PTSD is about 20%, and the optimal critical value for this model is 0.122, with 0.2 > 0.122, so she is at high risk and should be intervened as early as possible.

In this study, the mode of delivery for women with postpartum PTSD was more often cesarean section and instrumental delivery compared to women without postpartum PTSD. The cesarean section and instrumental delivery are risk factors for the development of postpartum PTSD, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies [44, 45]. This may be because, while it can save the life of the mother and baby, compared with vaginal delivery, cesarean section causes increased blood loss, vulnerable organs around the uterus, and postoperative pelvic adhesion, which can cause physical and psychological trauma to the mother [46]. In addition, vaginal-assisted delivery is a high-risk and unanticipated sudden situation for mothers in the process of labor and delivery. This may cause mothers to experience greater pain and may even pose a threat to their lives and health [43, 44]. Therefore, Healthcare professionals should advocate normal delivery, strictly control the indications for cesarean delivery, and do a good job of maternal health education and psychological care. For women who need vaginal assisted delivery, they should explain the operation process and risks, actively communicate and meet the needs of women, and instruct their families to increase the accompanying time to reduce the traumatic feelings and negative emotions of women.

Previous studies have shown that unemployment and low monthly household income are risk factors for the development of postpartum PTSD [47]. However, this finding is not supported by the present study. These differences may be related to the implementation of comprehensive health insurance, maternal maternity subsidies, and other beneficial policies in China. The comprehensive health insurance policy may allow mothers to enjoy better medical services, increase their sense of security, and reduce their worries about the childbirth process. Policies such as the maternal childbirth subsidy may reduce the financial pressure on mothers, making it easier for them to give birth during childbirth, and reducing anxiety and stress.

In our study, the incidence of postpartum PTSD was higher in participants who were afraid of labor than in maternity who were not afraid of labor fear, which is similar to the results reported by Malaju et al. [48]. Severe fear of delivery affects the physical and mental health of pregnant women and predisposes them to an increased incidence of preterm birth, low birth weight babies, and pregnant women's unindicated cesarean section claims [49–51]. This phenomenon may be a function of the persistence of negative maternal emotions such as sleep disturbances, difficulty concentrating, and anxiety due to fear of childbirth. Women in late pregnancy have a high prevalence of sleep disorders and often have sleep problems such as difficulty falling asleep, frequent awakenings, light sleep, and restless leg syndrome [52]. The main reason may be due to the combination of many unfavorable factors such as severe fear of childbirth, changes in sex hormones, uncomfortable sleeping positions, increased nocturia, etc. [52]. Nearly half of the women we surveyed had poor sleep in late pregnancy. Theodore et al. [53] found that sleep problems mediated the relationship between PTSD and negative physical and mental health outcomes and that the poorer the quality of sleep, the more severe the PTSD symptoms, which is consistent with our results. Medical personnel should focus on sleep problems in women in late pregnancy by creating a comfortable sleep environment, instructing pregnant women to maintain proper sleeping positions, and providing dietary guidance to improve sleep quality.

We observed that the high quality of couple intimacy during labor and delivery was a protective factor against postpartum PTSD, a finding that is consistent with the findings of Lauren [54]. Many studies to date have focused primarily on the professional support of healthcare professionals during labor and delivery [55]. The relationship between PTSD and the quality of intimacy is bi-directional, with PTSD not only affecting intimacy, but also the quality of intimacy predicts the course of PTSD, and good intimacy reduces the occurrence of PTSD symptoms [44]. Women during pregnancy may feel the need for more attention and support, while their partners may not be able to fulfill these needs because they are busy with work or other things. These mismatched needs and expectations may lead to tension and dissatisfaction in the intimate relationship between couples. Strengthening intimacy between couples has a positive effect on enhancing the quality of the relationship and improving the psychological state, which is beneficial for promoting physical and mental health [56]. Therefore, it is necessary to help pregnant women enhance their experience of intimacy through effective ways, which in turn reduces the level of postpartum PTSD in this population.

Strengths and limitations

As far as we are aware, this is the first study conducted in China that creates a nomogram for the early risk of PTSD following childbirth. We prospectively collected high-quality and reliable study data. According to multifactorial logistic analysis, mode of delivery, self-assessment of sleep status in late pregnancy, fear of childbirth, FFI‐N score, and QRI score were associated with postpartum PTSD. The nomogram constructed based on these five predictors had good diagnostic accuracy, consistency, and clinical application value. In addition, the variables are easy to obtain, do not increase the excessive workload of healthcare professionals, have clinical ease of use, and can be used as a tool for early risk assessment of postpartum PTSD. Based on the results of this risk prediction model, healthcare professionals can take timely preventive measures to reduce the incidence of postpartum PTSD.

There are several limitations to our study as well. Firstly, this study did not carefully divide the category of cesarean section, and future studies should distinguish between emergency cesarean section and elective cesarean section to further optimize the prediction model. Second, due to the sensitive nature of obstetric/family/partner violence in the Chinese socio-cultural context, mothers may be reluctant to talk openly about such experiences, making data collection difficult. Due to feasibility and validity, this factor was not included in this study for the time being. In the future, we will endeavor to explore more appropriate methods and approaches to explore the relationship between obstetric/family/partner violence and postpartum PTSD, to provide more comprehensive research results and effective interventions.

Conclusion

Based on mother physiological‐psychosocial parameters, a preliminary nomogram model with good differentiation, calibration, and predictive ability was built into this study. The chance of developing postpartum PTSD increases with a higher overall score. Based on the nomogram model, medical personnel can predict the probability of postpartum PTSD, and make early judgments and interventions for postpartum PTSD.

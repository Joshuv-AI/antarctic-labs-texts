Embedding separable Banach spaces into ( ℓ ∞ ∖ c ) ∪ { 0 } (\ell^{\infty}\setminus c)\cup\{0\}
Geivison Ribeiro

Abstract
. We prove that every separable Banach space admits an isometric embedding into ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} intersecting c c only at the origin. Moreover, we prove that any finite-or countable-dimensional, or more generally separable, subspace of ( ℓ ∞ ∖ c ) ∪ { 0 } (\ell_{\infty}\setminus c)\cup\{0\} can be extended to a subspace containing an isometric copy of an arbitrary separable Banach space, while still avoiding c c .

1.  Introduction

The classical Banach–Mazur theorem asserts that every separable Banach space is isometric to a subspace of
C[0,1]
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
).
It is also a standard fact (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
) that every separable Banach space embeds isometrically into
\ell^{\infty}
.
In this context, Lindenstrauss
[
2
]
and Rosenthal
[
3
]
showed that every separable subspace of
\ell^{\infty}
is quasi-complemented,
and criteria due to Wilansky
[
4
]
and Kalton
[
5
]
became fundamental in the study of large closed subspaces of sequence spaces.

A refinement of the Banach–Mazur theorem was obtained by Rodríguez–Piazza
[
7
]
, who proved that such embeddings can be realized inside the set of nowhere differentiable functions.
Later, Hencl
[
8
]
pushed this further, embedding every separable Banach space into functions lacking approximate differentiability and even Hölder continuity;
see also Fonf, Gurariy and Kadets
[
6
]
for earlier constructions of nowhere differentiable subspaces of
C[0,1]
.

Our contributions.

In this note we investigate analogous phenomena in the sequence space
\ell^{\infty}
.
Our main results can be summarized as follows:

•

Every separable Banach space admits an isometric embedding into
\ell^{\infty}
whose image intersects
c
only at the origin (Theorem
2.1
).

•

If
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
is finite-dimensional with
D\cap c=\{0\}
, then
D
can be extended to a closed subspace containing an isometric copy of any separable Banach space while still avoiding
c
(Theorem
2.2
).

•

More generally, if
D
has a countable Hamel basis or if
D
is separable, then analogous extensions are possible, although the sum
D+T(E)
need not be closed (Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
).

These results highlight the abundance of linear structures in
\ell^{\infty}\setminus c
and complement both the classical geometric results

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
and more recent contributions to lineability and spaceability theory

[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
12
,
16
]
.

Organization of the paper.

In Section
2
we prove the isometric embedding theorem (Theorem
2.1
).
and we extend the result to the finite-dimensional case (Theorem
2.2
).
Section
3
treats the countable-dimensional and separable cases (Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
)
and formulates an open problem.
Finally, throughout we interpret our results in the framework of
(\alpha,\beta)
-lineability and spaceability.

2.  Main results

In this section we present the two main contributions of this work.

Theorem 2.1
.

Every separable Banach space
E
is isometric to a subspace
Y\subset\ell^{\infty}
such that

Y\cap c=\{0\}.

This theorem establishes the following: every separable Banach space admits an isometric copy inside
\ell^{\infty}
that avoids
c
except at the origin.
Our next result shows that the construction can be made more flexible by incorporating additional finite-dimensional subspaces.

Theorem 2.2

(Finite-dimensional extension)
.

Let
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
be a finite-dimensional subspace with
D\cap c=\{0\}
.
For any separable Banach space
E
, there exists a closed subspace
Z\subset\ell^{\infty}
containing
D
and a subspace isometric to
E
, such that
Z\cap c=\{0\}
.

In other words, any finite-dimensional subspace of
(\ell^{\infty}\setminus c)\cup\{0\}
can be enlarged to a closed subspace that also contains an isometric copy of an arbitrary separable Banach space, while still intersecting
c
only at the origin.

Remark.

Theorem
2.1
is a particular case of Theorem
2.2
, obtained by choosing
D=\{0\}
.
For clarity of exposition, however, we prefer to present them in this order: first the embedding result, and then its refinement through finite-dimensional extensions.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
E
be separable and fix a dense sequence
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{S}_{E}
.
By Hahn–Banach, for each
k
there exists
\phi_{k}\in\mathbb{S}_{E^{*}}
with
\phi_{k}(u_{k})=1
.

Define
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
by

T(x):=\big(\psi_{n}(x)\big)_{n=1}^{\infty},\qquad\psi_{2k-1}:=\phi_{k},\ \ \psi_{2k}:=-\phi_{k}.

For every
x\in E
,

\|T(x)\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n}|\psi_{n}(x)|\leq\|x\|.

Conversely, let
x\neq 0
and set
v:=x/\|x\|
. Since
(u_{k})
is dense in
\mathbb{S}_{E}
, pick a subsequence
(u_{k_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
u_{k_{j}}\to v
.
As
\|\phi_{k_{j}}\|=1
,

|\phi_{k_{j}}(v)-\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{k_{j}})|\leq\|v-u_{k_{j}}\|\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{k_{j}})=1
, we get
\phi_{k_{j}}(v)\to 1
, hence
\phi_{k_{j}}(x)=\|x\|\,\phi_{k_{j}}(v)\to\|x\|
.
Therefore

\|T(x)\|_{\infty}\geq\limsup_{j}|\phi_{k_{j}}(x)|=\|x\|,

and thus

\|T(x)\|_{\infty}=\|x\|.

For
x\neq 0
, from the previous step
\phi_{k_{j}}(x)\to\|x\|
, and by construction

\psi_{2k_{j}}(x)=-\phi_{k_{j}}(x)\to-\|x\|.

Hence
T(x)
has subsequences converging to
\|x\|
and to
-\|x\|
, so it does not converge.
Therefore
T(x)\notin c
for
x\neq 0
, i.e.,
T(E)\cap c=\{0\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Fix a basis
w^{1},\dots,w^{r}
of the finite-dimensional subspace
D\subset\ell_{\infty}
and set

z_{n}:=(w^{1}_{n},\dots,w^{r}_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{r}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}),

where
w^{i}=(w_{n}^{i})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Since each
w^{i}
is bounded,
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\mathbb{K}^{r}
. By Bolzano–Weierstrass, there exists a subsequence
(z_{n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
converging to some
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{K}^{r}
. Let

I:=\{n_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\},\qquad I^{+}:=\{n_{2j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\},\qquad I^{-}:=\{n_{2j-1}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Then for every
d=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}w^{i}\in D
,

d_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}w_{n}^{i}\xrightarrow[n\in I]{}\langle a,\alpha\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i},

hence
(d_{n})_{n\in I^{+}}\to\langle a,\alpha\rangle
and
(d_{n})_{n\in I^{-}}\to\langle a,\alpha\rangle
.

Let
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset S_{E}
be dense (since
E
is separable). For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by Hahn–Banach pick
\phi_{k}\in S_{E^{*}}
with
\phi_{k}(u_{k})=1
.
Fix bijections
\eta^{+}:\mathbb{N}\to I^{+}
and
\eta^{-}:\mathbb{N}\to I^{-}
. Define functionals
(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset E^{*}
by

\varphi_{n}:=\begin{cases}\ \ \ \phi_{\ (\eta^{+})^{-1}(n)},&n\in I^{+},\\[2.0pt]
-\phi_{\ (\eta^{-})^{-1}(n)},&n\in I^{-},\\[2.0pt]
\ 0,&n\notin I.\end{cases}

Set
T:E\to\ell_{\infty}
by
T(x):=(\varphi_{n}(x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Then
T
is linear and
\|T(x)\|_{\infty}\leq\|x\|
for all
x
, since
\|\varphi_{n}\|\leq 1
.

Step 1.
T
is an isometry.

Let
x\neq 0
and put
v:=x/\|x\|\in S_{E}
. Choose a subsequence
(u_{k_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
u_{k_{j}}\to v
.
As
\|\phi_{k_{j}}\|=1
, we have

|\phi_{k_{j}}(v)-\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{k_{j}})|\leq\|v-u_{k_{j}}\|\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{k_{j}})=1
, it follows that
\phi_{k_{j}}(v)\to 1
, hence

\phi_{k_{j}}(x)=\|x\|\phi_{k_{j}}(v)\longrightarrow\|x\|.

Let
(n_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
n_{j}:=\eta^{+}(k_{j})\in I^{+}
. Then
\varphi_{n_{j}}=\phi_{k_{j}}
, so (passing to an increasing subsequence if needed)
\varphi_{n_{j}}(x)\to\|x\|
, which yields

\|T(x)\|_{\infty}\ \geq\ \limsup_{j\to\infty}|\varphi_{n_{j}}(x)|\ =\ \|x\|.

Together with the reverse inequality, we conclude
\|T(x)\|_{\infty}=\|x\|
for all
x
. Thus
T
is an isometric embedding.

Avoiding
D+c
.

We show
T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
. Suppose
x\neq 0
and
T(x)=d+y
with
d\in D
and
y\in c
.
Write
d=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}w^{i}
and let
L:=\langle a,\alpha\rangle
. As above,
(d_{n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}\to L
along both
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
. On the other hand, defining
(p_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
p_{j}:=\eta^{-}(k_{j})\in I^{-}
we have
\varphi_{p_{j}}=-\phi_{k_{j}}
, hence

\varphi_{n_{j}}(x)-d_{n_{j}}\ \longrightarrow\ \|x\|-L,\qquad\varphi_{p_{j}}(x)-d_{p_{j}}\ \longrightarrow\ -\|x\|-L.

Since
\|x\|>0
, these limits are distinct; thus
(T(x)-d)_{n}
has two different subsequential limits (along
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
), so it cannot converge. Therefore
y=T(x)-d\notin c
, a contradiction. Hence
T(x)\notin D+c
for all
x\neq 0
, proving
T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
.

Conclusion.

Let
F:=T(E)
and
G:=D+F
. Since
T
is an isometry and
E
is complete,
F
is closed in
\ell_{\infty}
. The sum of a finite-dimensional subspace with a closed subspace is closed, hence
G
is closed. If
z\in G\cap c
, write
z=d+T(x)
with
d\in D
,
x\in E
. Then
T(x)=z-d\in c+D
, so
T(x)\in T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
, which gives
x=0
and
z=d\in D\cap c=\{0\}
. Therefore
G\cap c=\{0\}
,
D\subset G
, and
G
contains the isometric copy
F
of
E
, as required.
∎

Open question.

In Theorem
2.2
we showed that, given a finite-dimensional subspace
D\subset(\ell^{\infty}\setminus c)\cup\{0\}
and a separable Banach space
E
, one can construct an isometry
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
and a separable closed subspace
Z\subset\ell^{\infty}
containing
D
such that

T(E)\subset Z\quad\text{and}\quad Z\cap c=\{0\}.

A natural question is whether one can go further and obtain
nonseparable
extensions:

Given
D\subset(\ell^{\infty}\setminus c)\cup\{0\}
finite-dimensional and
E
an arbitrary separable Banach space, does there always exist an isometry
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
and a closed nonseparable subspace
Z\subset\ell^{\infty}
containing
D
such that

T(E)\subset Z,\qquad Z\cap c=\{0\}?

3.  Extensions beyond the finite-dimensional case

In Theorem
2.2
we established that if
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
is finite-dimensional with
D\cap c=\{0\}
, then
for every separable Banach space
E
there exists an isometric embedding
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
such that

T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}.

Moreover, in this situation the extension
Z:=D+T(E)
is closed, since the sum of a closed subspace with a finite-dimensional subspace is always closed.
In the framework of
(\alpha,\beta)
-structures (see
[
13
,
16
]
), this already yields that
\ell^{\infty}\setminus c
is
(r,\mathfrak{c})
-
spaceable
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Reminder:
(\alpha,\beta)
-lineability/spaceability.

Let
A\subset V
where
V
is a (topological) vector space, and let
\alpha\leq\beta
be cardinals.
We say that:

•

A
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-lineable
if
A
contains an
\alpha
-dimensional subspace and, moreover, for every
\alpha
-dimensional subspace
W_{\alpha}\subset A\cup\{0\}
there exists a
\beta
-dimensional subspace
W_{\beta}
with

W_{\alpha}\subset W_{\beta}\subset A\cup\{0\}.

•

A
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-spaceable
if, in addition,
V
is topological and
W_{\beta}
can be chosen closed.

A natural question is whether the same extension property persists for larger classes of subspaces
D
.
We address here two intermediate cases: (i) when
D
has a countable Hamel basis, and (ii) when
D
is separable (but possibly without a countable Hamel basis).
In both situations we construct the required isometry
T
and retain the separation property
T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Extension with countable-dimensional
D
)
.

Let
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
be a subspace with a countable Hamel basis
W=\{w^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and assume
D\cap c=\{0\}
.
Let
E
be a separable Banach space. Then there exists an isometry
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
such that

T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}.

In particular, there exists
Z)
such thata
D\subset Z
,
Z
contains an isometric copy of
E
, and
Z\cap c=\{0\}
.
(Closedness of
Z
is not guaranteed in general.)

Proof.

Step 1. A common subsequence for
D
.

For each
i\geq 1
write
w^{i}=(w^{i}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{\infty}
. Each coordinate sequence
(w^{i}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.
By a diagonal argument, we construct a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that

\forall i\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}w^{i}_{\,n_{j}}=:\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{K}.

Set
I:=\{n_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
and split it into two infinite subsequences

I^{+}:=\{n_{2j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\},\qquad I^{-}:=\{n_{2j-1}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Thus, for any
d=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}w^{i}\in D
, we have

(d_{n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}\ \longrightarrow\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\alpha_{i}=:L(d),

so
d_{n_{j}}\to L(d)
along both
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
.

From here the argument is identical to the proof of Theorem
2.2
: we construct
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
using functionals
\pm\phi_{k}
placed alternately on
I^{\pm}
, show that
T
is an isometry, and use the limits
L(d)
to deduce
T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
. The assertions about
Z:=D+T(E)
follow immediately.
∎

(\alpha,\beta)
-interpretation.

Since every countable-dimensional
D
embeds as above, we obtain that
\ell^{\infty}\setminus c
is
(\aleph_{0},\mathfrak{c})
-
lineable
.

Proposition 3.2

(Extension with separable
D
)
.

Let
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
be a separable subspace with
D\cap c=\{0\}
, and let
E
be a separable Banach space.
Then there exists an isometry
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}
such that

T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}.

In particular, there exists
Z
such that
D\subset Z
,
Z
contains an isometric copy of
E
, and
Z\cap c=\{0\}
.
(Closedness of
Z
is not guaranteed in general.)

Proof.

Step 1. A common subsequence for a dense sequence.

Choose a dense sequence
(v^{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset D
in
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Each
v^{j}=(v^{j}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded.
By diagonalization, there exists a strictly increasing sequence
(n_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that

\forall j\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}v^{j}_{\,n_{j}}=:\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{K}.

Set
I:=\{n_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
and split into subsequences
I^{+}:=\{n_{2j}\}
,
I^{-}:=\{n_{2j-1}\}
.

Step 2. Extension of limits to all
d\in D
.

If
d\in D
and
\varepsilon>0
, pick
j
with
\|d-v^{j}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
. Then

|d_{n_{j}}-v^{j}_{\,n_{j}}|\leq\|d-v^{j}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }j.

Since
(v^{j}_{\,n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
converges,
(d_{n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is Cauchy and hence convergent; define

L(d):=\lim_{j\to\infty}d_{n_{j}}.

Thus every
d\in D
has a limit along
I
and, consequently, along
I^{\pm}
.

As in Proposition
3.1
, we now construct
T
exactly as in Theorem
2.2
, obtaining an isometry and the separation property
T(E)\cap(D+c)=\{0\}
. The conclusions about
Z:=D+T(E)
follow.
∎

(\alpha,\beta)
-interpretation.

If
\alpha=\dim D
(possibly infinite), the result shows that
\ell^{\infty}\setminus c
is
(\alpha,\mathfrak{c})
-
lineable
.

Open question.

Let
D\subset\ell^{\infty}
be an arbitrary (possibly nonseparable) subspace with
D\cap c=\{0\}

E

Does there always exist an isometry
T:E\to\ell^{\infty}

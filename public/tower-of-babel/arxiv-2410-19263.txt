Ultrafast selective mid-infrared sublattice manipulation in the ferrimagnet FeCr 2 S 4
Davide Soranzio, Matteo Savoini, Fabian Graf, Rafael T. Winkler, Abhishek Nag, Hiroki Ueda, Kenya Ohgushi, Yoshinori Tokura, Steven L. Johnson

Abstract
FeCr 2 S 4 is a ferrimagnet with two oppositely ordered spin sublattices (Fe and Cr), connected via superexchange interaction, giving a non-zero net magnetic moment. We show, using time-resolved measurements of the magneto-optic Kerr effect, how the magnetic dynamics of the sublattices can be selectively manipulated by resonantly perturbing the Fe sublattice with ultrashort laser pulses. The mid-infrared excitation through intra-atomic Fe d d - d d transitions triggers markedly slower dynamics in comparison to an off-resonant pumping affecting both of the two sublattices simultaneously. By changing probe wavelength to move in and out of resonance with the Fe d d - d d transitions, we also show the specific contributions of the Fe sublattice to these dynamics.



Ferrimagnets are an interesting class of magnetic materials that combine some features of antiferromagnets with those of ferromagnets.
They are characterized by a finite net magnetic moment given by sublattices with opposite spin orientation but unequal magnetic moments.
The relative orientation of the magnetic sublattices is typically determined by the exchange interaction
Blundell 2001
. This leads to intriguing potential applications in spintronics
Kim et al. 2022
, spin textures such as domain walls and skyrmions
Wu et al. 2020
, and in some cases the phenomenon of all-optical switching
Stanciu et al. 2007
.
For light with photon energies near intra-atomic electronic transitions involving partially filled shells, strong element-specific enhancement of the magneto-optical Kerr effect (MOKE) response can be observed
Ahrenkiel et al. 1973
;
Ohgushi et al. 2005
;
Ohgushi et al. 2008
;
Kocsis et al. 2018
.
In principle, exciting and monitoring the material resonantly to such transitions via light pulses in a time-resolved approach (TR-MOKE,
Sato and Ishibashi 2022
;
Koopmans et al. 2003
) offers a unique pathway for selective manipulation of a single sublattice, provided that other contributions of mixed atomic character are distinctively separated in the energy spectrum.
Past studies have shown that resonant pumping can improve the efficiency of processes such as spin-wave excitation and switching, while resonant probing can help to distinguish the sublattice dynamics
Khorsand et al. 2013
;
Stupakiewicz et al. 2017
;
Mikhaylovskiy et al. 2020
;
Mathias et al. 2012
. To our knowledge, however, no studies so far have reported how the individual sublattice dynamics vary as a result of resonant pumping.

We now turn our attention to the case of probe photon energy resonant to the Fe

-

Starting from the 7 ps window shown in Fig.

For the 1.55 eV case, a small pre-pulse, arriving 19 ps before and estimated to be eight times smaller than the main pulse, was present in the pump. For degenerate pump and probe photon energies at 0.30 eV, it was not possible to fully remove the contribution of interference effects, which created coherent artifacts, localized mainly around the first few hundreds of fs after time zero (Fig.

Using the mid-infrared pump, although limited by the coherent artifacts at time zero, we do not observe a slow decrease of the out-of-equilibrium signal as in the case of the visible probe (Fig.
2

Over the 150 ps range (bottom row, Fig.
3

A comparison of the data shown in Fig.
3

One major contribution to the differences among the time dynamics at nearby fluences likely stems from the strong differences in attenuation length. This may explain, for example, why, for a 1.55 eV excitation with a given fluence, the 0.30 eV probe reveals dynamics which resembles those measured at lower fluences for the 3.10 eV probe (Figs.
2
(e),
3
(c)). A similar argument applies for the differences among distinct pump photon energies, but same probe, even after taking into account their reflectivities.
However, the dynamics of a material is determined not simply by the absorbed energy, but also by the orbitals and quasi-particles involved.
An example is the TR-MOKE dynamics in Fig.
2
(a)-(c). Considering the average absorbed excitation energy density, the highest fluence for the 3.10 eV pump in panel (a) corresponds to 9.8 µJ/m
3
, whereas it is 4.3 µJ/m
3
for 1.55 eV excitation in panel (b) and 2.2 µJ/m
3
for 0.30 eV excitation in panel (c). Despite this, even decreasing the pulse energy by a factor of 10 for the 1.55 eV pump does not reproduce the slower dynamics seen for the 0.30 eV pump.
As shown for antiferromagnetic iron oxides
Mikhaylovskiy et al. 2020
, resonant pumping of Fe
d
-
d
transitions can weaken the magnetic interactions by altering the electronic configuration of the sublattices. This can transiently make a different alignment more favorable. The data of Fig.
2
(a)-(c) show that, using a 3.10 eV probe to monitor the response sensitive to modifications that occur in both sublattices
Sarkar et al. 2009
;
Ohgushi et al. 2005
;
Ohgushi et al. 2008
;
Ogasawara et al. 2006
, a 0.30 eV excitation leads to a slower TR-MOKE dynamics in comparison to other excitation photon energies that are not resonant with the intra-atomic Fe
d
-
d
transitions. This dependence of the speed of the initial response on the pump photon energy does not occur when probing resonantly to the Fe sublattice (Fig.
3
(a),(b)), where a fast drop was observed regardless of the selected pump.
This suggests that the slower response in Fig.
2
(c) is connected to a change in the superexchange interaction between the excited Fe states and the Cr sublattice, triggered by the selective modification of the Fe electronic configuration. In fact, excitations at either 1.55 or 3.10 eV photon energies perturb directly both the two sublattices, not relying mainly on an indirect Cr spin reorganization via superexchange
Sarkar et al. 2009
;
Ohgushi et al. 2008
. In the simplified semiclassical picture of the Heisenberg model, these pump-induced changes can be viewed as variations of the effective magnetic fields generated by each sublattice, which depend on the exchange interaction and spin orientation
Blundell 2001
;
Ogasawara et al. 2006
. Interaction with the phonon bath is expected to occur on a longer time scale
Xu et al. 2021
.

The 24 GHz oscillation was previously reported by Ogasawara et al., and assigned to the precession of Cr spins
Ogasawara et al. 2006
. This was motivated by the lack of oscillations at 0.5 eV probe photon energy (pump at 1.55 eV), ruling out a significant role of the Fe spins, limited by the relatively high magnetocrystalline anisotropy of this material
Stapele et al. 1971
. Despite this, our data in Fig.
3
(c),(d) clearly show oscillations at 24 GHz when probing resonantly to the Fe sublattice (0.30 eV).
These experimental results can be reconciled by noting that a photon energy of 0.5 eV is at the very edge of the MOKE resonance associated with the Fe
d
-
d
transitions
Ohgushi et al. 2005
;
Ogasawara et al. 2006
;
Ohgushi et al. 2008
. Thus, MOKE data at this photon energy may have other contributions that are not specific to the Fe sublattice. Moreover, in Ogasawara et al.
Ogasawara et al. 2006
, a slight oscillation appears to be in fact present in the datasets at 70 K and 90 K. The crystalline anisotropy, although predicted to be on the order of a few Tesla at 4 K
Ogasawara et al. 2006
, is heavily influenced by temperature
Stapele et al. 1971
. This is also evident from the coercive field growth while cooling
Liu et al. 2024
.
Therefore, we conclude that under our conditions (
T
=75 K), the magnetocrystalline anisotropy, while present, is considerably reduced. Since the oscillation frequency is close to that observed when probing at low fluences for 3.10 eV (Fig.
2
(d)-(f)) and 1.9 eV
Ogasawara et al. 2006
, we conclude that the two sublattices are coupled in their precession motion by the superexchange interaction.
No further oscillatory contributions were observed (SM, Sect. IV

In summary, we have shown that, by selectively pumping the Fe sublattice in the mid-infrared, we are able to modify dramatically the time scale of the TR-MOKE response connected to both the Fe and Cr sublattices, compared to non-resonant case. We propose that the possible mechanism consists of a distinct modification of the superexchange interaction, based on which electronic levels are perturbed by the pump.
Moreover, our work highlights the importance of resonant probing to distinguish the dynamics of the sublattices and to understand whether their spins are involved in specific collective modes. This approach can be extended to other magnetic materials provided that a clear spectral separation among the localized energy levels of the atoms is present.

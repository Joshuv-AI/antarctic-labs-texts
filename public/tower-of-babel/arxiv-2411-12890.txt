Abstract
. We give explicit product formulas for the mod 2 motivic Steenrod algebra in the conjugated motivic Milnor basis. This provides an efficient tool for computing the E 2 E_{2} -page of the motivic Adams spectral sequence.

Primary 14F42, 55S10

1.
Introduction

In recent years, motivic methods have seen significant progress and applications in stable homotopy theory. The Steenrod algebra and the Adams spectral sequence have been extended to the motivic context
[
10
,
1
,
3
]
, which provides new tools for computing motivic stable homotopy groups.

Despite this progress, a fully explicit product formula for the conjugated motivic Milnor basis of the motivic Steenrod algebra has not yet been achieved over a general base field. It turns out that the motivic computation of such a formula is much more complex than the classical case studied by
[
6
]
, for several reasons.

(1)

The motivic cohomology of a point can be complicated, and contains extra gradings.

(2)

The motivic dual Steenrod algebra is in general a Hopf algebroid; therefore, there is an asymmetry in the action of the unit map.

(3)

The motivic dual Steenrod algebra is not a free polynomial algebra.

Previous work by Kylling
[
4
]
gives some recursive formulas in specific cases. In this paper, we work out general product formulas for the conjugated motivic Milnor basis of the mod 2 motivic Steenrod algebra. This formula also provides the foundation for machine-assisted computations of the
\mathbb{R}
-motivic Adams spectral sequence, extending the work of Lin
[
5
]
.

Background

We work in the motivic homotopy category over a field
k
, with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
coefficients throughout.

We recall some results of the motivic dual Steenrod algebra; these results can be found in

.

Let
\mathbb{M}_{2}:=H_{**}(\Spec k;\mathbb{Z}/2)
denote the motivic mod
2
cohomology of a point.
The Milnor conjecture
[
9
]
shows that

(\mathbb{M}_{2})_{-n,-n+*}\simeq K^{M}_{n}(k)/2[\tau],~~|\tau|=(0,-1).

Here
K^{M}_{*}(k)
denote the Milnor
K
-theory, which by definition has
K^{M}_{1}(k)=k^{\times}/(k^{\times})^{2}.

In particular, there is an element called
\rho:=[-1]\in K^{M}_{1}(k)\subset(\mathbb{M}_{2})_{-1,-1}
; the element
\rho
is possibly zero if
-1
is a square in
k
.

The motivic dual Steenrod algebra is

(1)

A_{**}=\mathbb{M}_{2}[\tau_{i},\xi_{j},i\geq 0,j\geq 1]/(\tau_{i}^{2}=\tau\xi_{i+1}+\rho\tau_{0}\xi_{i+1}+\rho\tau_{i+1}).

with gradings given by

|\tau|=(0,-1),~~|\rho|=(-1,-1),~~|\tau_{i}|=(2^{i+1}-1,2^{i}-1),\text{ and }|\xi_{i}|=(2^{i+1}-2,2^{i}-1).

We adopt the notation that
\xi_{0}=1
.

The dual motivic Steenrod algebra is a Hopf algebroid (see, e.g.,
[
7
, A.1.1.1]
for the definition of a Hopf algebroid). In particular, it has an antipode map
\chi.

Let
\bar{\tau}_{i}=\chi\tau_{i}
,
\bar{\xi}_{j}=\chi\xi_{j}
be the conjugates of
\tau_{i}
,
\xi_{j}
respectively. Note that
\eta_{R}(\tau)=\tau+\rho\tau_{0}
. Then (
1
) can be rewritten as

A_{**}=\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\tau}_{i},\bar{\xi}_{j},i\geq 0,j\geq 1]/(\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}=\tau\bar{\xi}_{i+1}+\rho\bar{\tau}_{i+1}).

Here each relation has one fewer term, which makes reductions of powers of
\bar{\tau}_{i}
possible.

The unit map and the coproduct map in
A_{**}
, in terms of
\bar{\tau}_{i}
and
\bar{\xi}_{j}
, have the following formulas.

(1)

The left and right unit maps:
\eta_{L}(\tau)=\tau
,
\eta_{R}(\tau)=\tau+\rho\bar{\tau}_{0}
,
\eta_{L}(\rho)=\eta_{R}(\rho)=\rho.

(2)

The coproduct map
A_{**}\to A_{**}\otimes_{\eta_{R},\mathbb{M}_{2},\eta_{L}}A_{**}
:

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\tau}_{k})

\displaystyle=1\otimes\bar{\tau}_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bar{\tau}_{i}\otimes\bar{\xi}_{k-i}^{2^{i}},

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\xi}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bar{\xi}_{i}\otimes\bar{\xi}_{k-i}^{2^{i}}.

We recall that this coproduct is defined so that when we consider the motivic Steenrod algebra
A^{**}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{M}_{2}}(A_{**},\mathbb{M}_{2})
where
\mathbb{M}_{2}
acts on
A_{**}
via
\eta_{L}
, the pairing satisfies

\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,a\otimes b\rangle=\langle\alpha,a\langle\beta,b\rangle\rangle

for any
\alpha,\beta\in A_{**}
,
a,b\in A^{*,*}
.

Notation 2.1
.

We introduce the following notation about sequences.

(1)

Let
\mathrm{Seq}
denote the set of all sequences of nonnegative integers of the form
(a_{0},a_{1},\dots)
such that
a_{i}
is non-zero for only finitely many
i
.

(2)

Let
\mathscr{E}\subset\mathrm{Seq}
denote the subset of sequences
(e_{0},e_{1},\dots)
where
e_{i}\in\{0,1\}
.

(3)

Let
\mathscr{R}\subset\mathrm{Seq}
denote the subset of sequences
(0,r_{1},r_{2},\dots)
where the first entry is zero.

(4)

Let
(n)_{i}\in\mathrm{Seq}
denote the sequence whose
i
th entry is
n
and all entries are
0
.

(5)

It is clear that

\{\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(R)=\bar{\tau}_{0}^{e_{0}}\bar{\tau}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\cdot\bar{\xi}_{1}^{r_{1}}\bar{\xi}_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots~|~E=(e_{i})\in\mathscr{E},R=(r_{j})\in\mathscr{R}\}

is an
{\mathbb{M}}_{2}
-basis of
A_{**}
. Let
\bar{Q}(E)
,
\bar{P}(R)
and
\bar{M}(E,R)
denote the duals of
\bar{\tau}(E)
,
\bar{\xi}(R)
and
\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(R)
respectively.

Remark 2.2
.

The relationship between the conjugated Milnor basis elements
\bar{Q}(E),\bar{P}(R)
and the standard Milnor basis elements
Q(E),P(R)
are determined by the antipode map
\chi
on
A_{**}
. For example, we can obtain

P(0,2,0,\dots)=\bar{P}(0,2,0,\dots)+\tau\bar{Q}(1,1,0,\dots)

by checking that both sides evaluate to the same value on
\bar{\xi}_{1}^{2}=\xi_{1}^{2}
,
\bar{\tau}_{0}\bar{\tau}_{1}=\tau_{0}\tau_{1}+\tau\xi_{1}^{2}+\rho\tau_{1}\xi_{1}+\rho\tau_{0}\xi_{1}^{2}
,
\bar{\tau}_{0}\bar{\xi}_{1}=\tau_{0}\xi_{1}
and
\bar{\tau}_{1}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{0}\xi_{1}
.

We refer to the basis
\bar{M}(E,R)\in A^{*,*}
,
E\in\mathscr{E},R\in\mathscr{R}
as the
conjugated motivic Milnor basis
. The goal of this article is to determine the product formula for

\bar{M}(E_{1},R_{1})\cdot\tau^{n}\bar{M}(E_{2},R_{2})

where
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E}
and
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathscr{R}
.
One of the difficulties for finding the product formula comes from the relations
\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}=\tau\bar{\xi}_{i+1}+\rho\bar{\tau}_{i+1}
, which will be resolved in the next section.

3.
Reduction of
\bar{\tau}(S)

For some
S\in\mathrm{Seq}
where
s_{i}
could be bigger than
1
, we want to reduce
\bar{\tau}(S)=\bar{\tau}_{0}^{s_{0}}\bar{\tau}_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots
to a linear combination
\Sigma c_{i}\bar{\tau}(E_{i})\bar{\xi}(R_{i})
where each
c_{i}\in\mathbb{M}_{2}
,
E_{i}\in\mathscr{E}
and
R_{i}\in\mathscr{R}
.

Example 3.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}_{0}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{1}=\tau\bar{\tau}_{1}\bar{\xi}_{1}+\rho\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}=\tau\bar{\tau}_{1}\bar{\xi}_{1}+\rho\tau\bar{\xi}_{2}+\rho^{2}\bar{\tau}_{2},

and

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}_{0}^{4}

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{2}=\tau^{2}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{2}+\rho^{2}\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}=\tau^{2}\bar{\xi}_{1}^{2}+\rho^{2}\tau\bar{\xi}_{2}+\rho^{3}\bar{\tau}_{2}.

Construction 3.2
.

We construct a binary tree to encode the reduction of
\bar{\tau}(S)
for
S\in\mathrm{Seq}
to a linear combination of
\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(R)
for
E\in\mathscr{E}
,
R\in\mathscr{R}
.

(1)

Every node is labeled by two sequences
S^{\prime}|R^{\prime}
, representing
\bar{\tau}(S^{\prime})\bar{\xi}(R^{\prime})
. Note that different nodes can share the same label.

(2)

The sum of the monomials represented by child nodes equals the monomial represented by the parent node.

(3)

The branches from a parent node to its child nodes correspond to rewriting a
\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}
factor (where
i
is as small as possible) into
\tau\bar{\xi}_{i+1}+\rho\bar{\tau}_{i+1}
.

We start from
\bar{\tau}(S)
.

(1)

The root node is labeled by
S|0
.

(2)

A node labeled
S^{\prime}|R^{\prime}
with
S^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seq}\backslash\mathscr{E}
has a left child node labeled

S^{\prime}-(2)_{i}+(1)_{i+1}|R^{\prime},

and a right child node labeled

S^{\prime}-(2)_{i}|R^{\prime}+(1)_{i+1},

where
i
is the smallest index with
s_{i}\geq 2
.

(3)

A node labeled
E|R
with
E\in\mathscr{E}
,
R\in\mathscr{R}
is a leaf node.

Once we construct the tree, we can reduce
\bar{\tau}(S)
to

\bar{\tau}(S)=\sum c\cdot\tau^{k}\rho^{l}\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(R),

where
c
is the number of occurrences of the leaf nodes labeled
E|R
, and the powers of
\tau
and
\rho
can be determined by the degrees.

For example, the reduction of
\bar{\tau}_{0}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{1}
labeled
(21)|(0)
and
\bar{\tau}_{0}^{4}
labeled
(4)|(0)

Notation 3.3
.

For
S\in Seq
,
R\in\mathscr{R}
we introduce the following notations.

(1)

\Sigma_{2}(S)=\sum s_{i}2^{i}.

(2)

\Sigma(S)=\sum s_{i}.

(3)

c(S,R)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\binom{\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n}(s_{i}-r_{i})\rfloor}{r_{n}}.

Here we take
\binom{m}{n}=0
if
m<n
.

Theorem 3.4
.

(2)

\bar{\tau}(S)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathscr{E},R\in\mathscr{R}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E)+\Sigma_{2}(R)=\Sigma_{2}(S)\end{subarray}}c(S,R)\tau^{\Sigma(R)}\rho^{\Sigma(S)-\Sigma(E)-2\Sigma(R)}\bar{\tau}(E){\bar{\xi}(R)}

Proof.

Note that

\Sigma_{2}(E)+\Sigma_{2}(R)=\Sigma_{2}(S)

because of the rewriting rule: every two in
s_{i}
contributes to one in
e_{i+1}
or one in
r_{i+1}
. The sequence
E
is actually determined uniquely by the equation. The power of
\tau
and
\rho
can be verified by comparing degrees and it remains to show that the number of occurrences of leaf nodes labeled
E|R
in the rewriting tree of
\bar{\tau}(S)
is
c(S,R)
.

We use a bottom-up approach to count all leaf nodes labeled
E|R
with given
E=(e_{0},e_{1},\dots)\in\mathscr{E}
and
R=(0,r_{1},r_{2},\dots)\in\mathscr{R}
. Let
n
be the largest index such that
r_{n}\neq 0
. Given any
m<n
, since we always rewrite
\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}
from the left-most re-writable entry, before any
\bar{\tau}_{m}^{2}
is rewritten from
\bar{\tau}(S)
its descendants labeled
S^{\prime}|R^{\prime}
always satisfy

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}2^{k}{s^{\prime}_{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{k}{r^{\prime}_{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}2^{k}{s_{k}}.

Hence a node labeled
S_{n}|R_{n}=E|R
is a descendant of a node labeled

S_{n-1}|R_{n-1}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{n-2},\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}2^{i-n+1}(s_{i}-e_{i}-r_{i})+s_{n-1}-r_{n-1},s_{n},\dots)|(0,r_{1},\dots,r_{n-1},0,\dots)

and we have

\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}2^{i-n+1}(s_{i}-e_{i}-r_{i})+s_{n-1}-r_{n-1}=\left\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n+1}(s_{i}-r_{i})\right\rfloor

because
\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}2^{i-n+1}e_{i}<1
.

On the other hand, a node labeled
S_{n-1}|R_{n-1}
has exactly

\binom{\lfloor\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n+1}(s_{i}-r_{i})\rfloor/2\rfloor}{r_{n}}=\binom{\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n}(s_{i}-r_{i})\rfloor}{r_{n}}

descendants labeled
E_{n}|R_{n}
because

\bar{\tau}_{n-1}^{\left\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n+1}(s_{i}-r_{i})\right\rfloor}=\bar{\tau}_{n-1}^{e_{n-1}}(\tau\bar{\xi}_{n}+\rho\bar{\tau}_{n})^{\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}2^{i-n}(s_{i}-r_{i})\rfloor}.

Next, we count the occurrences of
S_{n-1}|R_{n-1}
in descendants of a node labeled

S_{n-2}|R_{n-2}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{n-3},\sum_{i=0}^{n-3}2^{i-n+2}(s_{i}-e_{i}-r_{i})+s_{n-2}-r_{n-2},s_{n-1},\dots)|(0,r_{1},\dots,r_{n-2},0,\dots)

and repeat the same calculation, until we get to the root node

S_{0}|R_{0}=S|(0).

We conclude that the number of occurrences of leaf nodes labeled
E|R
is

c(S,R)=\prod_{m=1}^{n}\binom{\lfloor\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}2^{i-m}(s_{i}-r_{i})\rfloor}{r_{m}}

∎

4.
Coproduct formula for
\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(T)

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be the set of matrices of the following form.

\begin{pmatrix}x_{0,0}&x_{0,1}&x_{0,2}&\cdots\\
x_{1,0}&x_{1,1}&x_{1,2}&\cdots\\
x_{2,0}&x_{2,1}&x_{2,2}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\end{pmatrix}

(1)

We define
\mathscr{X}_{0}\subset\mathscr{X}
as the subset of
X\in\mathscr{X}
such that
x_{0,0}=0
.

(2)

For
X\in\mathscr{X}
, define
T,S,R:\mathscr{X}\to\mathrm{Seq}
by

T(X)_{k}=\sum_{i}x_{i,k-i},~~S(X)_{k}=\sum_{j}x_{k,j},~~R(X)_{k}=\sum_{i}2^{i}x_{i,k}.

We define
T_{0},S_{0},R_{0}:\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{R}
by setting
F_{0}(X)=(0,F(X)_{1},F(X)_{2},\dots)
for
F=T,S,R
.

(3)

For
X\in\mathscr{X}
, define

b(X)=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{(x_{i,0}+x_{i-1,1}+\cdots+x_{0,i})!}{x_{i,0}!x_{i-1,1}!\cdots x_{0,i}!}(\text{mod }2).

(4)

Assume
R,R^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seq}
. We write
R\leq R^{\prime}
if
r_{i}\leq r_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
E\in\mathscr{E},T\in\mathrm{Seq}
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\tau}(E))=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X}\\
T(Y)\leq E\end{subarray}}b(Y)\cdot\bar{\tau}(S(Y))\otimes\bar{\tau}(E-T(Y))\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(Y)).

(2)

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\xi}(T))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\mathscr{X}_{0}\\
T_{0}(X)=T\end{subarray}}b(X)\cdot\bar{\xi}(S_{0}(X))\otimes\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(X)).

Proof.

The proof of (2) is the same as the classical case in
[
6
]
.

The equation in (1) can be computed by the following.

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\tau}(E))=

\displaystyle\prod_{k}(1\otimes\bar{\tau}_{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bar{\tau}_{i}\otimes\bar{\xi}_{k-i}^{2^{i}})^{e_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{E^{\prime}+E^{\prime\prime}=E}\prod_{k}(1\otimes\bar{\tau}_{k})^{e_{k}^{\prime}}\cdot(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\bar{\tau}_{i}\otimes\bar{\xi}_{k-i}^{2^{i}})^{e^{\prime\prime}_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E^{\prime}+E^{\prime\prime}=E\\
Y\in\mathscr{X},T(Y)=E^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}b(Y)\cdot\bar{\tau}(S(Y))\otimes\bar{\tau}(E^{\prime})\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(Y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X},T(Y)\leq E\end{subarray}}b(Y)\cdot\bar{\tau}(S(Y))\otimes\bar{\tau}(E-T(Y))\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(Y)).

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For any
E\in\mathscr{E},T\in\mathscr{R},
we have

\displaystyle\psi(\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X},T(Y)\leq E\\
X\in\mathscr{X}_{0},T_{0}(X)=T\end{subarray}}b(Y)b(X)\cdot\bar{\tau}(S(Y))\bar{\xi}(S_{0}(X))\otimes\bar{\tau}(E-T(Y))\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(Y+X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X},T(Y)\leq E\\
X\in\mathscr{X}_{0},T_{0}(X)=T\end{subarray}}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E},R^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})+\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(S(Y))\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr b(Y)b(X)c(S(Y),R^{\prime})\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R^{\prime})}\rho^{\Sigma(S(Y))-\Sigma(E^{\prime})-2\Sigma(R^{\prime})}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{\tau}(E^{\prime})\bar{\xi}(R^{\prime}+S_{0}(X))\otimes\bar{\tau}(E-T(Y))\bar{\xi}(R_{0}(Y+X))\\
\hline\cr\end{array}.

(We write the long product in a multi-line box to improve readability.)

Proof.

It follows from
Lemma

4.2
and
Theorem

3.4
.

5.
Product formula for the conjugated motivic Milnor basis

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E}
and
R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathscr{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{M}(E_{1},R_{1})\cdot\bar{M}(E_{2},R_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X},X\in\mathscr{X}_{0}\\
R_{0}(Y+X)=R_{2}\\
S_{0}(X)\leq R_{1}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E_{1})+\Sigma_{2}(R_{1}-S_{0}(X))=\Sigma_{2}(S(Y))\\
E_{2}+T(Y)\in\mathscr{E}\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr b(Y)b(X)c(S(Y),R_{1}-S_{0}(X))\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R_{1}-S_{0}(X))}\rho^{\Sigma(S(Y))-\Sigma(E_{1})-2\Sigma(R_{1}-S_{0}(X))}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{M}(E_{2}+T(Y),T_{0}(X))\\
\hline\cr\end{array}.

Proof.

This is the dualization of
Corollary

4.3
with substitutions
E^{\prime}=E_{1}
,
R^{\prime}+S_{0}(X)=R_{1}
,
E-T(Y)=E_{2}
and
R_{0}(Y+X)=R_{2}
.
∎

Lastly, we deal with the case when the second factor has a coefficient
\tau^{n}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

For
E\in\mathscr{E},R\in\mathscr{R},
we have

\bar{M}(E,R)\cdot\tau^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq n\\
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E},R^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E)+\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})+m\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr\binom{n}{m}c(E^{\prime}+(m)_{0},R^{\prime})\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R^{\prime})+n-m}\rho^{\Sigma(E^{\prime})-\Sigma(E)-2\Sigma(R^{\prime})+2m}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{M}(E^{\prime},R-R^{\prime})\\
\hline\cr\end{array}.

Proof.

Note that
for
b\in A_{**}
and
a\in A^{*,*}
, we have

\langle b,a\tau^{n}\rangle=\langle b,a\langle\tau^{n},1\rangle\rangle=\langle b\otimes\tau^{n},a\otimes 1\rangle=\langle(\tau+\rho\bar{\tau}_{0})^{n}b,a\rangle.

For
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
and
T\in\mathscr{R}
, we have

\displaystyle(\tau+\rho\bar{\tau}_{0})^{n}\bar{\tau}(E^{\prime})\bar{\xi}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\binom{n}{m}\tau^{n-m}\rho^{m}\tau\left(E^{\prime}+(m)_{0}\right)\bar{\xi}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathscr{E},R^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Seq}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E)+\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})+m\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr\binom{n}{m}c(E^{\prime}+(m)_{0},R^{\prime})\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R^{\prime})+n-m}\rho^{\Sigma(E^{\prime})-\Sigma(E)-2\Sigma(R^{\prime})+2m}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{\tau}(E)\bar{\xi}(R^{\prime}+T)\\
\hline\cr\end{array}.

The conclusion is dual to the above with substitution
R=R^{\prime}+T
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

For
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E},R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathrm{Seq},
we have

\displaystyle\bar{M}(E_{1},R_{1})\cdot\tau^{n}\bar{M}(E_{2},R_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq n\\
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E},R^{\prime}\in\mathscr{R}\\
\Sigma_{2}(E_{1})+\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})+m\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr\binom{n}{m}c(E^{\prime}+(m)_{0},R^{\prime})\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R^{\prime})+n-m}\rho^{\Sigma(E^{\prime})-\Sigma(E_{1})-2\Sigma(R^{\prime})+2m}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{M}(E^{\prime},R_{1}-R^{\prime})\cdot\bar{M}(E_{2},R_{2})\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq m\leq n\\

Corollary 5.4
.

Consider
E\in\mathscr{E}
and
R\in\mathscr{R}
. We have

(1)

\bar{M}(E,R)=\bar{P}(R)\bar{Q}(E)
,

(2)

\bar{Q}(e_{0},e_{1},\cdots)=\prod_{e_{i}\neq 0}\bar{Q}_{i},\text{ where }e_{i}\in\{0,1\},

(3)

\bar{Q}_{i}\bar{Q}_{j}=\bar{Q}_{j}\bar{Q}_{i}
,

(4)

\bar{Q}_{i}^{2}=0
.

Proof.

(1) By Theorem
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{P}(R)\bar{Q}(E)=\bar{M}(0,R)\cdot\bar{M}(E,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X},X\in\mathscr{X}_{0}\\
R_{0}(Y+X)=0\\
S_{0}(X)\leq R\\
\Sigma_{2}(R-S_{0}(X))=\Sigma_{2}(S(Y))\\
E+T(Y)\in\mathscr{E}\end{subarray}}\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr b(Y)b(X)c(S(Y),R-S_{0}(X))\\
\cdot\\
\tau^{\Sigma(R-S_{0}(X))}\rho^{\Sigma(S(Y))-2\Sigma(R-S_{0}(X))}\\
\cdot\\
\bar{M}(E_{2}+T(Y),T_{0}(X))\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})\\
E+E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}\end{subarray}}c(E^{\prime},R^{\prime})\cdot\tau^{\Sigma(R^{\prime})}\rho^{\Sigma(E^{\prime})-2\Sigma(R^{\prime})}\cdot\bar{M}(E+E^{\prime},R-R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{M}(E,R)

The third equality holds because
R_{0}(Y+X)=0
implies that
Y
and
X
are nonzero only in the zeroth column. The fourth equality holds because
\Sigma_{2}(R^{\prime})=\Sigma_{2}(E^{\prime})
and
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
implies
\Sigma(R^{\prime})\geq\Sigma(E^{\prime})
. On the other hand nonzero terms always have
2\Sigma(R^{\prime})\leq\Sigma(E^{\prime})
and then we must have
R^{\prime}=E^{\prime}=0
. Hence there is only one nontrivial term.

(2) It is sufficient to prove when
E+(1)_{j}\in\mathscr{E}

\bar{M}(E+(1)_{j},0)=\bar{M}(E,0)\bar{Q}_{j}
.

5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{M}(E,0)\bar{Q}_{j}=\bar{M}(E,0)\cdot\bar{M}((1)_{j},0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\mathscr{X}\\
R_{0}(Y)=0\\
\Sigma_{2}(E)=\Sigma_{2}(S(Y))\\
(1)_{j}+T(Y)\in\mathscr{E}\end{subarray}}b(Y)\cdot\rho^{\Sigma(S(Y))-\Sigma(E)}\cdot\bar{M}((1)_{j}+T(Y),0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma_{2}(E)=\Sigma_{2}(S)\\
(1)_{j}+S\in\mathscr{E}\end{subarray}}\rho^{\Sigma(S)-\Sigma(E)}\cdot\bar{M}((1)_{j}+S,0)

\displaystyle\bar{M}(E+(1)_{j},0)

(1)_{j}+S\in\mathscr{E}
implies
S\in\mathscr{E}
. Hence
\Sigma_{2}(E)=\Sigma_{2}(S)
forces

.

(3) This is a direct consequence of (2).

(4) By Theorem
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{i}^{2}=\bar{M}((1)_{i},0)\cdot\bar{M}((1)_{i},0)

\displaystyle 0

Example 5.5
.

We use our formula to compute an example and compare it to
[
4

P(0,2,0,\dots)\cdot Q(0,1,0,\dots)=Q(0,1,0,\dots)P(0,2,0,\dots)+Q(0,0,1,0,\dots)+\rho Q(1,1,0,\dots)P(0,1,0,\dots).

(Note that

is denoted by
P^{2}

by

in

4
]

We can recover this result using our formula. As mentioned in Remark
2.2
,

P(0,2,0,\dots)=\bar{P}(0,2,0,\dots)+\tau\bar{Q}(1,1,0,\dots)

and

Q(0,1,0,\dots)=\bar{Q}(0,1,0,\dots).

Therefore, by
Corollary

\displaystyle P(0,2,0,\dots)\cdot Q(0,1,0,\dots)

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{P}(0,2,0,\dots)+\tau\bar{Q}(1,1,0,\dots)\right)\cdot\bar{Q}(0,1,0,\dots)

\displaystyle=\bar{P}(0,2,0,\dots)\bar{Q}(0,1,0,\dots)

\displaystyle=\bar{M}((0,1,0,\dots),(0,2,0,\dots)).

It remains to show that
\bar{M}((0,1,0,\dots),(0,2,0,\dots))
agrees with the right-hand side of the above equation by Kylling.
This can be checked by the following conversion between classical basis and conjugated basis of
A_{*,*}
in relative degrees.

\displaystyle\chi(\tau_{1}\xi_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle=(\tau_{1}+\tau_{0}\xi_{1})\xi_{1}^{2}=\tau_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\tau_{0}\xi_{1}^{3},

\displaystyle\chi(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle=\tau_{2}+\tau_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\tau_{0}\xi_{2}+\tau_{0}\xi_{1}^{3},

\displaystyle\chi(\tau_{0}\tau_{1}\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{0}\xi_{1})\xi_{1}=\tau_{0}\tau_{1}\xi_{1}+\tau_{0}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}\tau_{1}\xi_{1}+(\tau\xi_{1}+\rho\tau_{1}+\rho\tau_{0}\xi_{1})\xi_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau_{0}\tau_{1}\xi_{1}+\tau\xi_{1}^{3}+\rho\tau_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\rho\tau_{0}\xi_{1}^{3}.

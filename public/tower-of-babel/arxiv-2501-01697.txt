Sets preserved by a large subgroup of the special linear group
Le Quang Hung, Thang Pham, Kaloyan Slavov

Abstract
Let E E be a subset of the affine plane over a finite field 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . We bound the size of the subgroup of SL 2 ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) \SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) that preserves E E . As a consequence, we show that if E E has size ≪ q α \ll q^{\alpha} and is preserved by ≫ q β \gg q^{\beta} elements of SL 2 ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) \SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) with β ≥ 3 ​ α / 2 \beta\geq 3\alpha/2 , then E E is contained in a line. This result is sharp in general, and will be proved by using combinatorial arguments and applying a point-line incidence bound in 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{3} due to Mockenhaupt and Tao (2004).

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field of order
q
, let
E
be a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, and let
R
be a subset of
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. In a recent work
[
2
]
on packing sets over finite fields, Hegyvári, Hung, Iosevich, and Pham give lower bounds for the size of

R(E):=\bigcup_{\theta\in R}\theta(E)=\left\{\theta(x)\colon x\in E,\theta\in R\right\}

in terms of
|E|,|R|,q
(and other quantities determined by
R
and
E
). However, in some cases, only the trivial bound
|R(E)|\geq|E|
is available (see Proposition 2.1, Theorem 2.2, and Theorem 2.3 in
[
2
]
).

In the present paper, we ask for a description of extremal configurations, that is, sets
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
which “stay packed” under
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. More precisely, our goal is to study sets
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
such that
R(E)=E
for a large subset
R
of
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

Throughout the paper,
X\ll Y
means
X\leq CY
for a constant
C
;
X\gg Y
means
X\geq CY
for a constant
C
, and
X\approx Y
means
X\ll Y
and
Y\ll X
.

For a subset
E
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, let

R_{E}=\{\theta\in\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\ |\ \theta(E)=E\}.

Example 1
.

When
E
is empty,
\{(0,0)\}
, all of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, or
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}-\{(0,0)\}
, we have that
R_{E}=\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
has size
\approx q^{3}
.

Example 2
.

If
E
is a line, then
|E|=q
and
|R_{E}|=q^{2}-q\approx q^{2}
.

Note that for any
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, denoting the complement
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}-E
by
E^{c}
, we have
R_{E}=R_{E^{c}}
. So Example
2
also shows that when
E
is the complement of a line, we have
|R_{E}|\approx q^{2}.

Example 3
.

For positive divisor
c
of
q-1
, there exists a subgroup
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
with
|S|=(q-1)/c
. Let

E=\left\{(0,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\colon y\in S\right\}.

Then

R_{E}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
d&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\colon a\in S,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

Here again
|E|=|S|=(q-1)/c\approx q
and
|R|=q|S|=q(q-1)/c\approx q^{2}
.

Motivated by these examples, we ask the following

Question 1.1
.

Let
E
be a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
that is preserved under “many” elements in
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Does it follow (under an assumption on
|E|
) that
E
is contained in a line?

Remark 1.2
.

There is a significant difference between the cases when the ground field is a prime field versus a general finite field. In this paper, we treat these cases separately. More precisely, while tools from group theory would be enough to study the prime field setting, for the arbitrary field setting we use a combination of combinatorial arguments and a point-line incidence bound due to Mockenhaupt and Tao
[
5
]
.

We answer in the affirmative.

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
c_{1}>0
, there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
with the following property. Let
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
be such that
\beta\geq 3\alpha/2
. Let
E
be a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
with
|E|\leq c_{1}q^{\alpha}
. Suppose the set
R_{E}
of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\theta(E)=E
satisfies
|R_{E}|\geq c_{2}q^{\beta}
. Then
E
is contained in a line.

Note that the assumption involves merely counting data for
|E|
and
|R_{E}|
while the conclusion is a geometric structure on
E
.
In the next corollary, we spell out some illustrative special cases.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
E
be a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Let
R_{E}
denote the set of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\theta(E)=E
.

(i)

If
|E|\approx q^{1/2}
and
|R_{E}|\gg q^{3/4}
, then
E
is contained in a line;

(ii)

If
|E|\approx q^{2/3}
and
|R_{E}|\gg q
, then
E
is contained in a line;

(iii)

If
|E|\approx q
and
|R_{E}|\gg q^{3/2}
, then
E
is contained in a line;

(iv)

If
|E|\approx q^{4/3}
and
|R_{E}|\geq q^{2}
, then
E
is contained in a line (in particular
|E|\leq q
).

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the next result, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
. Let
R_{E}
be the set of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\theta(E)=E
. Suppose at least
2
lines through the origin intersect
E-\{(0,0)\}
.
Then
|R_{E}|\ll|E|^{3/2}
.

The main difficulty in proving this theorem arises when the number of lines depends on
q
.

The exponent
3/2
in Theorem
1.5

is sharp, as the example below illustrates.

Example 4
.

For
p
a prime and
q=p^{2}
, let
E=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Then
R_{E}\supset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. So
|E|=q
and
|R_{E}|\geq p^{3}-p\approx q^{3/2}=|E|^{3/2}
.

In fact, Example
4
shows that Theorem
1.3
is sharp when
\alpha=1
in the following sense: in the statement of Corollary
1.4
(i), if one keeps the assumption
|E|\ll q
but replaces
|R_{E}|\gg q^{3/2}
by
|R_{E}|\gg q^{3/2-\varepsilon}
with any
\varepsilon>0
, the statement would be false.

The example below shows that Theorem
1.3
is in fact sharp in the sense described above for all
\alpha
of the form
\alpha=1/s
or
\alpha=2/s
for a positive integer
s
.

Example 5
.

Let
\alpha
be of the form
\alpha=1/s
or
\alpha=2/s
for a positive integer
s
. Then there exist sufficiently large
q
and subsets
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
of size
|E|\approx q^{\alpha}
such that
|R_{E}|\approx q^{3\alpha/2}

and
E
is not contained in a line. Indeed, pick any positive integer
r^{\prime}
, set
r=(2/\alpha)r^{\prime}
, and consider
E={\mathbb{F}}^{2}_{p^{r^{\prime}}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, where
q=p^{r}
. Then
|E|=p^{2r^{\prime}}=q^{\alpha}
while
R_{E}
contains
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{r^{\prime}}})
,
so
|R_{E}|\geq p^{3r^{\prime}}-p^{r^{\prime}}\approx q^{3\alpha/2}
.

The result below addresses in particular the case of a prime ground field.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
q=p^{r}
, where
r\geq 1
and
p
is prime.
Suppose
E
is not
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}-\{(0,0)\}
or all of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
.
Suppose at least
2
lines through the origin intersect
E-\{(0,0)\}
. Then
|R_{E}|\leq p^{r-1}|E|
.

When
|E|\approx q^{\alpha}
, Proposition
1.6
gives the bound
|R_{E}|\leq q^{\alpha+(r-1)/r}
. Compared to Theorem
1.5
, this result is only interesting when
\alpha\geq 2-2/r
, which will be a very small range when
r
is large.

Remark 1.7
.

Given
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
, one can obtain an upper bound for
R_{E}
as follows.
Let
P=E\times E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
. Then, we know from
[
2
]
that

\left|I(P,R_{E})-\frac{\left|P\right|\left|R_{E}\right|}{q^{2}}\right|\ll q\sqrt{\left|R_{E}\right|\left|P\right|}+|R_{E}|.

If
1\ll|E|
and
1\ll|E^{c}|
, using the fact that
I(P,R_{E})=|R_{E}||E|
, one has
|R_{E}|\ll q^{2}
(suppose first
|E|<q^{2}/2
; then apply the same for the complement
E^{c}
).
However, the bound from Theorem
1.5
is better as long as
|E|\ll q^{4/3}
.

It would be interesting to study analogues of Theorem
1.3
in higher dimensions.

Based on Example
5
, we conjecture that
Theorem
1.3
is sharp for each specific
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

In light of results on the packing set problems over the reals (see, for example,
[
3
]
and references therein) Theorem
1.3
suggests that it is reasonable to state the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.8
.

Let
E
be a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\dim_{H}(E)=\alpha
. Assume that there exists a set
R
of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\theta(E)=E
for each
\theta\in R
, with

\dim_{H}(R)>3\alpha/2
. Then the set

is contained in a line. Here
\dim_{H}
means the Hausdorff dimension.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
P
be a set of points and
L
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. We denote the number of incidences between
P
and
L
by
I(P,L)
. The proof of Theorem
1.3
makes use of an incidence bound
I(P,L)
. A modification of the proof of Theorem 8.6 in
[
5
]
gives the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(Mockenhaupt–Tao)
.

Let
P
be a set of points in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
and
L
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
. Assume that any plane contains at most
M
lines from
L
, then we have

I(P,L)\leq c\left(|P|^{1/2}|L|^{3/4}M^{1/4}+|P|+|L|\right),

with an absolute constant
c
.

The lemma below will allow us to apply the point–line incidence bound of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
such that for
(m_{1},m_{2})\in A
with
m_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1}),m_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2})
we have
u_{1},u_{2}\neq 0
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\neq(0,0)
.
Assume the image of
A
under the first projection to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
does not contain two distinct points on the same line through the origin.
Then the map
f\colon\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
,
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto(a,d,c)
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

For any
(m_{1},m_{2})\in A
, the set

\ell_{m_{1}\rightarrow m_{2}}:=f\left(\left\{\theta\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\colon\theta m_{1}=m_{2}\right\}\right)

is a line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
;

(ii)

for
(m_{1},m_{2})\neq(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})
, we have
\ell_{m_{1}\rightarrow m_{2}}\neq\ell_{m_{1}^{\prime}\rightarrow m_{2}^{\prime}}
;

(iii)

f
is injective on the set
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{\theta\colon\theta(e_{1})=e_{1}\}
, here
e_{1}
is the line defined by
y=0
.

Proof.

Let
(m_{1},m_{2})\in A
. Write
m_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1})
and
m_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2})
. The set of all
\theta=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)
such that
\theta m_{1}=m_{2}
is described as the solution set of the following system of three equations

\begin{cases}au_{1}+bv_{1}=u_{2},\\
cu_{1}+dv_{1}=v_{2},\\
ad-bc=1.\end{cases}

Solving this system, we have

\begin{bmatrix}a\\
d\\
c\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{u_{2}}{u_{1}}\\
\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\\
\frac{v_{2}}{u_{1}}-\frac{v_{1}}{u_{2}}\end{bmatrix}+b\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{v_{1}}{u_{1}}\\
\frac{v_{2}}{u_{2}}\\
-\frac{v_{1}v_{2}}{u_{1}u_{2}}\end{bmatrix},

for
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Now one checks that
f
satisfies the required properties.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Suppose there are exactly
2
lines through the origin that intersect
E-\{(0,0)\}
. Then

|R_{E}|\leq|E|
.

Proof.

Since
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
acts doubly transitive on the set of lines through the origin, we can assume without loss of generality that the two lines are the two coordinate axes
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. Denote by
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
the intersections of
E-\{(0,0)\}
with
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, respectively. Fix a point
m=(x,0)
on
E_{1}
.

A matrix
\theta\in\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
that sends
e_{1}\mapsto e_{1}
and
e_{2}\mapsto e_{2}
has the form
\theta=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}
.
Fixing the image
\theta m
on
E_{1}
uniquely determines
a
, hence also
d
. Thus, the number of
\theta
’s that preserve
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is at most
|E_{1}|
.

Next, a matrix
\theta\in\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
that swaps

e_{1}
and
e_{2}
has the form
\theta=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Fixing the image
\theta m
on
E_{2}
uniquely determines
c
, hence also
b
. Thus, the number of
\theta
’s that swap
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is at most
|E_{2}|
.

Altogether,
|R_{E}|\leq|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|=|E|
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a set of
m\geq 3
lines through the origin in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
. Then the number of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
such that
\theta(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}
is

\leq 2\cdot m^{3}(m-1)^{2}
.

Proof.

As in the previous lemma, we can assume that
\mathcal{L}
contains the two coordinate lines
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
. Consider a matrix
\theta=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
in
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
that acts on
\mathcal{L}
. Deciding which element in
\mathcal{L}
goes under
\theta
to
e_{1}
amounts to fixing the ratio
c/d
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
. Similarly, deciding which element in
\mathcal{L}
goes under
\theta
to
e_{2}
amounts to fixing the ratio
a/b
. Deciding where
\theta
sends
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
determines the ratios
a/c
and
b/d
.

Therefore, unless
a=d=0
or
b=c=0
, the ratio
a/d
is also determined by the choices we have made; then
\theta=\lambda\theta_{0}
for a fixed matrix
\theta_{0}
and a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The condition
\det(\theta)=1
now gives at most two possibilities for
\lambda
. If
a=d=0
or
b=c=0
, we decide where
\theta
sends an element in
\mathcal{L}-\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
; this will determine
a/d
, and we finish as in the previous case.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
. Let
R_{E}
be the set of matrices
\theta
in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that
\theta(E)=E
.
Let
m_{0}
be the number of lines passing through the origin that intersect

E-\{(0,0)\}
at exactly
m_{1}
points (for a fixed
m_{1}\geq 1
). With the constant
c
taken from Theorem
2.1
, assume that
m_{0}>8+4c
. Then

|R_{E}|\leq 16c^{2}(m_{0}m_{1})^{3/2}.

In particular,
|R_{E}|\leq 16c^{2}|E|^{3/2}
.

Proof.

Replace
E
by
E-\{(0,0)\}
to assume without loss of generality that
(0,0)\notin E
.
Let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{m_{0}}
be all the lines through the origin such that
|\ell_{j}\cap E|=m_{1}
. Let
E_{j}=\ell_{j}\cap E
for
j=1,\dots,m_{0}
.

Let
S
be the set of matrices
\theta\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\theta(E_{j})\in\left\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{m_{0}}\right\},\,\forall j=1,\ldots,m_{0}
. Then
R_{E}\subset S
. We will estimate
|S|
.

For
j=1,\dots,m_{0}
, fix a point
a_{j}\in E_{j}
. Let

B=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m_{0}}\}-(e_{1}\cup e_{2})
and
C=\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{0}}E_{j}\right)-(e_{1}\cup e_{2})
.

Let

\Omega=\{(u_{1},u_{2},\theta)\in B\times C\times S\ |\ \theta u_{1}=u_{2}\}.

Let
\theta\in S
. For any
u_{1}\in B
such that
\theta u_{1}\notin e_{1}\cup e_{1}
, we have

(u_{1},\theta u_{1},\theta)\in\Omega
. Therefore

|\Omega|\geq(m_{0}-4)|S|.

(1)

Set
S_{1}=\{\theta\in S\ |\ \theta e_{1}\neq e_{1}\}
and

S_{2}=\{\theta\in S\ |\ \theta e_{1}=e_{1}\}
. For a fixed
(u_{1},u_{2})\in B\times C
, there exists at most one
\theta=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in S_{2}
such that
\theta u_{1}=u_{2}

|\Omega\cap(B\times C\times S_{2})|\leq|B||C|\leq m_{0}^{2}m_{1}
.

We now estimate
|\Omega\cap(B\times C\times S_{1})|
. Apply Lemma

2.2
with
A=B\times C
. Let

L=\{\ell_{u_{1}\to u_{2}}\ |\ u_{1}\in B,u_{2}\in C\};

this is a set of lines in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{3}
with
|L|\leq m_{0}^{2}m_{1}
.
Notice that
|\Omega\cap(B\times C\times S_{1})|
equals the number of incidences between
the lines in
L
and the points in
f(S_{1})

We will see below that any plane in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
contains at most
2m_{0}
lines from

Once this is established, Theorem

I(L,f(S_{1}))\leq c\left(|L|^{3/4}|S_{1}|^{1/2}(2m_{0})^{1/4}+|L|+|f(S_{1})|\right),

|\Omega|\leq 2c\left(m_{0}^{7/4}m_{1}^{3/4}|S|^{1/2}+m_{0}^{2}m_{1}+|S|\right).

(2)

Comparing the lower and upper bounds (

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}

2m_{0}
lines from

First, we observe that
\ell_{u\rightarrow v}
and
\ell_{u\rightarrow w}
(elements of
L

\ell_{u\rightarrow v}=\left\{\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}},\frac{x_{0}}{x_{1}},\frac{y_{1}}{x_{0}}-\frac{y_{0}}{x_{1}}\right)+t\left(-\frac{y_{0}}{x_{0}},\frac{y_{1}}{x_{1}},-\frac{y_{0}y_{1}}{x_{0}x_{1}}\right)\colon t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\},

W

We denote the set of lines passing through the origin and intersecting the set

If

Proof of Proposition
1.6

Suppose
|R_{E}|>p^{r-1}|E|
. By changing coordinates, we may assume that
e\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
is in
E
. The stabilizer of
e
under the action of
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
is the Borel subgroup

B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{Stab}_{\text{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(e)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\ :\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}

of
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. In particular,
|B|=q=p^{r}
.

Let
x\in E-\{(0,0)\}
. Applying the orbit-stabilizer theorem to the action of
R_{E}
on
E
, we can write

p^{r-1}|E|<|R_{E}|=|R_{E}x|\cdot|\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(x)|\leq|E|\cdot|\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(x)|

to conclude
p^{r-1}<|\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(x)|
.
However,
\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(x)
is a subgroup of
\text{Stab}_{\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(x)
, which is a conjugate of
B
and hence of size
p^{r}
. Therefore
\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(x)=\text{Stab}_{\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(x)
.

By the assumption on
E
, there exists a point
y=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}
in
E
with
v\neq 0
. Since
B\subset R_{E}
, all points

\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u+\alpha v\\
v\end{pmatrix}

as
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
belong to
E
. The lines through the origin that contain those points are all distinct. Therefore,
E
intersects all lines through the origin in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
except possibly the
y
-axis.

For
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, set
e_{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\beta\end{pmatrix}
and pick a point
y_{\beta}\in E\cap\text{span}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(e_{\beta})
with

y_{\beta}\neq\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(y_{\beta})=\text{Stab}_{\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(y_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\text{Stab}_{\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})}(e_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\beta&1\end{pmatrix}B\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\beta&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\beta&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\beta&1\end{pmatrix}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha\beta&\alpha\\
-\beta^{2}\alpha&\alpha\beta+1\end{pmatrix}:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}.

Therefore, for each
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha\beta&\alpha\\
-\beta^{2}\alpha&\alpha\beta+1\end{pmatrix}
belongs to
R_{E}
.
Therefore, the point

\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha\beta&\alpha\\
-\beta^{2}\alpha&\alpha\beta+1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\alpha\beta\\
-\beta^{2}\alpha\end{pmatrix}

belongs to the
R_{E}
-orbit
R_{E}e
.
These points are all distinct for
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
. Hence
|R_{E}e|\geq(q-1)^{2}
.

We conclude

|R_{E}|=|R_{E}e|\cdot|\text{Stab}_{R_{E}}(e)|\geq(q-1)^{2}q>\frac{|\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|}{2}

(for

). Therefore,

. But
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
acts transitively on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}-\{(0,0)\}
; this would force
E
to contain all of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}-\{(0,0)\}
, contradicting the assumption on
E
.

Some discussions

One might ask if we can get stronger results when we replace Theorem
2.1
by other point-line incidence bounds in the literature. In this section, we discuss this question. We first recall the following three incidence bounds.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
1
,
6
]
)
.

Let
P
be a set of points and
L
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. Then the number of incidences between
P
and
L
satisfies

\left|I(P,L)-\frac{|P||L|}{q^{2}}\right|\ll q\sqrt{|P||L|}.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
P
be a set of
n
distinct points and
L
be a set of
m
distinct lines in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. Assume that no plane contains more than
c\sqrt{m}
lines from
L
for some constant
c
. Then we have

I(P,L)\ll|L||P|^{2/5}+|P|^{6/5}.

By a generic projection, one can show that the number of incidences between
P
and
L
is at most the number of incidences between a point set and a line set in two dimensions over an arbitrary fields, which is quite weak in general. Therefore, the main result in
[
9
]
implies the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
P
be a set of points and
L
be a set of lines in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
. Assume that
|P|^{7/8}<|L|<|P|^{8/7}
and
|P|^{-2}|L|^{13}\ll p^{15}
, then we have

I(P,L)\ll|P|^{11/15}|L|^{11/15}.

If we just plug these incidence bounds in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, without checking conditions on the sets and the underlying fields, one has

•

Theorem
4.1
implies
|S|\leq q^{2}m_{1}
.

•

Theorem
4.2
implies
|S|\leq(m_{0}m_{1})^{5/3}
.

•

Theorem
4.3
implies
|S|\leq m_{0}^{7/4}m_{1}^{11/4}
.

All of these bounds are not better than that of Theorem
1.3
.

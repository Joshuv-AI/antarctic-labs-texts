Periodic propagation of singularities for heat equations with time delay
Gengsheng Wang, Huaiqiang Yu, Yubiao Zhang

Abstract
This paper presents two remarkable phenomena associated with the heat equation with a time delay: namely, the propagation of singularities and periodicity. These are manifested through a distinctive mode of propagation of singularities in the solutions. Precisely, the singularities of the solutions propagate periodically in a bidirectional fashion along the time axis. Furthermore, this propagation occurs in a stepwise manner. More specifically, when propagating in the positive time direction, the order of the joint derivatives of the solution increases by 2 for each period; conversely, when propagating in the reverse time direction, the order of the joint derivatives decreases by 2 per period. Additionally, we elucidate the way in which the initial data and historical values impact such a propagation of singularities. The phenomena we have discerned not only corroborate the pronounced differences between heat equations with and without time delay but also vividly illustrate the substantial divergence between the heat equation with a time delay and the wave equation, especially when viewed from the point of view of singularity propagation.



Keywords.

Heat equations with time delay,
propagation of singularities, periodicity, discreteness.

History, motivation and aim

Differential equations with time delay constitute an important subject in the domain of differential equations. Numerous natural phenomena emerging from physics, chemistry, biology, economics, and other fields hinge not merely on the current state but also on certain past events (see, for example,

To the best of our knowledge, the related research originated from the study of the well-posedness of linear ordinary differential equations with delays during the 1960s and 1970s. Its foundation lies in the state space theory (also known as the so-called semigroup theory, or the evolution equation approach) that was developed in works such as

The state space theory is convenient for analyzing the global regularity and dynamic behavior of equations with delay because this theory can transform equations with delay into a system of linear evolution equations without delay. Then, we can apply the theory of a one-parameter semigroup to obtain many useful results for the equations with delay.

The motivation for this research is as follows.

(m1)

We can recognize that equation (
1.1
) may have some kind of propagation of singularity from the following three aspects.

First,
we consider the following ODE with time-delay:

\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)=y(t-1),\penalty\ t>0;\penalty\ \penalty\ y(0)\in\mathbb{R},\penalty\ y|_{(-1,0)}\in L^{2}((-1,0)).

Second, employing the state space method, we are able to rewrite equation (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}y(t)-\Delta_{D}y(t)=az(t,1),\penalty\ t>0,\\

where

Third, it has been widely recognized for a long time that heat equations with integral-type memory exhibit certain wavelike characteristics (see, for instance,

and the references therein). Notably, recently in

The above considerations prompt us to explore the following issues: Firstly, what sort of singularity propagation does equation (
1.1

(m2)

Although extensive research has been conducted on partial differential equations with time delay, in comparison to general partial differential equations, our comprehension of those with time delay remains considerably restricted. Particularly, to the best of our knowledge, quantitative investigations regarding the propagation of singularities for equation (
1.1

Given that the propagation of singularities is a characteristic typically associated with hyperbolic equations (specifically, the wave equation and the transport equation), its manifestation in the heat equation with delay is, in general, even more deserving of scrutiny.

(m3)

The propagation of singularities is intrinsically of great significance. This can be expounded upon from several perspectives as follows.
Firstly, the exploration of the propagation of singularities represents an essential subject matter (even one of the central themes) within the realm of the theory of partial differential equations/operators. Phenomena like unique continuation, as well as the propagation of wavefront sets. Secondly, in a multitude of specific application scenarios, if our objective is to attain a comprehensive solution, we will invariably have to delve into certain facets of the propagation of singularities for the corresponding equations. For instance, this holds true when handling the controllability or observability of linear control systems (see

The purpose of this paper is as follows.

(p1)

We are going to disclose novel phenomena pertaining to equation (

(p2)

When propagating in the positive time direction, the order of the joint derivative of the solution increases by 2 for each period; conversely, when propagating in the reverse time direction, the order of the joint derivative decreases by 2 per period. (ii)

The propagation of singularities in Theorem

(1)

Singularities propagate along the time direction with zero velocity.

(2)

(3)

While as time progresses along the negative direction, the singularities increase, accompanied by a reduction of two orders in joint derivatives per period.

To offer an intuitive image of the above propagation of singularities, we consider this scenario: a fading light that blinks at regular intervals, with its brightness gradually decreasing. In the context presented here, the light symbolizes the singularities of a solution; its blinking reflects the recurrence of these singularities, and the brightness of the light represents the degree of these singularities.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}:=\left\{f=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}a_{i}e_{i}:\{a_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\subset\mathbb{R},\;\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|a_{i}|^{2}\lambda_{i}^{s}<+\infty\right\},

with the inner product:

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}a_{i}b_{i}\lambda_{i}^{s}\penalty\ \text{when}\penalty\ f=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}a_{i}e_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ g=\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}b_{i}e_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}.

for the heat semigroup, that is, the

-semigroup on

-semigroup on

The main results in this section are presented below.
The first one concerns the connection between the local regularities of nonhomogeneous terms and the corresponding solutions of the heat equations (which will be used in the proof of Theorem

Propositon 3.1

(ii)

The second main result in this section is stated in the following, which gives the relations between the local regularities of an initial data and corresponding solutions of the heat equations (which will be used in the proof of Theorem

4
Explicit expression of solutions

This section presents an explicit expression for solutions of equation (

) in terms of the semigroup
\{e^{t\Delta_{D}}\}_{t\geq 0}
. It plays an important role in the proofs of our main results.
Recall that
\Delta_{D}
and
\mathcal{H}^{s}
are introduced in (

) and (

), respectively. The expression aforementioned is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Let

and

Then,
y(t;y_{0},\phi)|_{[0,+\infty)}

where

\varPhi(t):=e^{t\Delta_{D}}+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{a^{j}}{j!}(t-j\tau)^{j}\chi_{[j\tau,+\infty)}(t)e^{(t-j\tau)^{+}\Delta_{D}},\penalty\ t\geq 0.

(4.2)

Remark 4.2
.

(i)
Although it may be possible to derive the above expression indirectly from
[

we obtain it directly using a method developed in
[
32
]
.

(ii)
By (

), we have that for each

,

y(t;y_{0},0)=\varPhi(t)y_{0},\penalty\ \forall\,t\geq 0.

Hence,
\{\varPhi(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is the flow generated by equation (
1.1
) with
\phi=0
. Moreover,
(
4.2
) provides a way to calculate this flow.

(iii)
The expression (

) gives the exact difference between the solution of the heat equations with and without time delay (i.e.,
a=0
). The sum on the right side of (
4.2
) is caused by the delay term
ay(t-\tau,x)
in equation (

). This sum is independent of the historical value
\phi
.

(iv)
With
\tilde{\phi}
the zero extension of
\phi
over
\mathbb{R}
, (

) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle y(t;y_{0},\phi)=\varPhi(t)y_{0}+a\int_{-\infty}^{t-\tau}\varPhi((t-\tau)-\gamma)\tilde{\phi}(\gamma)d\gamma,\penalty\ t\geq 0.

This is quite similar to the classical variation-of-constants formula for ODEs.

(v)
Theorem

can be extended to be true for any

by very small modifications of its proof.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We write

\displaystyle(f*g)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(t-\eta)g(\eta)d\eta,\penalty\ t\geq 0\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;f,g\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}^{s-1}).

The proof is divided into the following steps.

Step 1. To transform (
1.1
) into an integral equation

We start with writing a delay function in the convolution form. For this purpose, we introduce the following:
given

,
the function
\delta_{\tau}*f
is also in

, and has the expression

\delta_{\tau}*f(t)=\begin{cases}f(t-\tau),&t\geq\tau,\\
0,&0\leq t<\tau.\end{cases}

(The convolution here can also be understood in the distribution sense.)

Next, we write
\widetilde{\phi}
for the zero extension of
\phi
over
\mathbb{R}
. (It is treated as a function of the time variable.) It is clear that
\widetilde{\phi}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s-1})
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle y(t-\tau;y_{0},\phi)=\delta_{\tau}*(y(\cdot;y_{0},\phi)|_{[0,+\infty)})(t)+\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi}(t),\penalty\ t\geq 0.

Then, it follows from the variation of constants formula that
y(\cdot;y_{0},\phi)|_{[0,+\infty)}
satisfies the following integral equation :

y_{+}(t)-a(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau})*y_{+}(t)=e^{t\Delta_{D}}y_{0}+a(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})(t),\;\;t\geq 0,

(4.3)

with the unknown

in
C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
.

Step 2. Analysis of the integral equation (

)

First, we aim to put equation (
4.3
) in a functional analysis framework. To this end, we arbitrarily fix

and then define a linear operator

\mathcal{G}_{T}
on
C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
in the following manner:
given

(\mathcal{G}_{T}f)(t):=a(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau})*f(t),\;\;t\in[0,T].

(4.4)

Then, equation (

) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle(y_{+}|_{[0,T]})-\mathcal{G}_{T}(y_{+}|_{[0,T]})=F|_{[0,T]}\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ C([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)),

(4.5)

where the function

\displaystyle F(t):=e^{t\Delta_{D}}y_{0}+e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*(a\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})(t),\penalty\ t\geq 0.

Notice that by

and

Next, we claim that for each

(I_{m}-\mathcal{G}_{T})^{-1}=I_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathcal{L}(C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m})),

(4.7)

where

is the identity operator over
C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m})

To this end, we fix an
m\geq 0
and let

\displaystyle K(t,\tau):=a(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau})(t-\tau),\penalty\ t\geq\tau\geq 0.

Then, by the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{T}
(see (
4.4
)), we obtain that for each

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}f\|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m})}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t_{1}\leq T}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{j}}\|K(t_{1},t_{2})K(t_{2},t_{3})\cdots K(t_{j},t_{j+1})f(t_{j+1})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m}}dt_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t\leq T}\|K(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{m})}^{j}\Big)\Big(\sup_{0\leq t_{1}\leq T}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}dt_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{j}}dt_{j+1}\Big)\|f\|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a^{j}(T^{j}/j!)\|f\|_{C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m})},

which implies

\|\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}\|_{\mathcal{L}(C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m}))}\leq(aT)^{j}/j!\penalty\ \text{for each}\penalty\ j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

Therefore, the series

absolutely converges in
\mathcal{L}(C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{m}))
. Furthermore, one can directly check that

(I_{m}-\mathcal{G}_{T})\bigg(I_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}\bigg)=I_{m},

which leads to (
4.7
).

Step 3. To solve the integral equation (

) in

Since

(see (
4.6

\displaystyle y_{+}|_{[0,T]}=F|_{[0,T]}+\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}(F|_{[0,T]})\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{s}).

(4.8)

At the same time, it follows from (

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}(F|_{[0,T]})=

\displaystyle\underset{j}{\underbrace{(ae^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau})*\cdots*(ae^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau})}}*\left(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}y_{0}+a(e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{j}\underset{j}{\underbrace{\delta_{\tau}*\cdots*\delta_{\tau}}}*\underset{j+1}{\underbrace{e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\cdots*e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}}}y_{0}+a^{j}\underset{j}{\underbrace{\delta_{\tau}*\cdots*\delta_{\tau}}}*\underset{j+1}{\underbrace{e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\cdots*e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}}}*(a\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{j}\delta_{j\tau}*\underset{j+1}{\underbrace{e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\cdots*e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}}}y_{0}+a^{j}\delta_{j\tau}*\underset{j+1}{\underbrace{e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}*\cdots*e^{\cdot\Delta_{D}}}}*(a\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{j}\delta_{j\tau}*\Big(\frac{t^{j}}{j!}e^{t\Delta_{D}}\Big)y_{0}+a^{j}\delta_{j\tau}*\Big(\frac{t^{j}}{j!}e^{t\Delta_{D}}\Big)*(a\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi}),

which implies that for each

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{T}^{j}(F|_{[0,T]})=\varPhi_{j}y_{0}+\varPhi_{j}*(a\delta_{\tau}*\widetilde{\phi})\penalty\ \text{over}\penalty\ [0,T],

(4.9)

where

\varPhi_{j}(t):=\frac{1}{j!}(t-j\tau)^{j}\chi_{[j\tau,+\infty)}(t)e^{(t-j\tau)^{+}\Delta_{D}},\penalty\ t\geq 0.

(4.10)

Now, by the definition of
\varPhi
in (
4.2

4.10

\displaystyle\varPhi(t)=e^{t\Delta_{D}}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\varPhi_{j}(t),\penalty\ t\geq 0.

Since
T>0
was arbitrarily taken, The above, together with (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.6

4.3

\displaystyle y_{+}=\varPhi y_{0}+\varPhi*(a\delta_{\tau}*\tilde{\phi})\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ C([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H}^{s}).

(4.11)

Step 4. To verify (
4.1
)

Since
y(\cdot,\cdot;y_{0},\phi)|{[0,+\infty)}
solves (
4.3
) which has a unique solution,
we obtain (
4.1
) from (
4.11

In summary, the proof is completed.

∎

7.3
Two lemmas on regularity

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\Omega)
be the space of distributions compactly supported over
\Omega
. Then, for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle H^{s}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{s}\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(7.1)

Proof.

We arbitrarily fix a compact non-empty subset
K\Subset\Omega
.
For each
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle H^{s}_{K}(\Omega):=\big\{f\in H^{s}_{loc}(\Omega)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \text{supp}\,f\subset K\big\}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \mathcal{H}^{s}_{K}:=

\displaystyle\big\{f\in\mathcal{H}^{s}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \text{supp}\,f\subset K\big\}.

To prove (
7.1
), it suffices to show that

\displaystyle H^{s}_{K}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{s}_{K},\penalty\ \forall\,s\in\mathbb{R}.

(7.2)

The rest of the proof is divided into the following two steps.

Step 1. We prove that for each
s\geq 0
, the identity operator is an isomorphism from
H^{s}_{K}(\Omega)

to
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{K}
.

Note that when
s_{2}\geq s_{1}\geq 0

\displaystyle H^{s_{2}}_{K}(\Omega)\subset H^{s_{1}}_{K}(\Omega)\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \mathcal{H}^{s_{2}}_{K}\subset\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}_{K}.

By this and the interpolation theorem (see
[
23

, the identity operator is an isomorphism from
H^{2m}_{K}(\Omega)
to
\mathcal{H}^{2m}_{K}
.

Let

. We first show that

\displaystyle H^{2m}_{K}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{H}^{2m}_{K}.

(7.3)

To this end, we arbitrarily fix

. Then we have

\displaystyle\text{supp}\,f\subset K\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \Delta_{D}^{m}f=\Delta^{m}f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

, which leads to (

Next, we will prove

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2m}_{K}\subset H^{2m}_{K}(\Omega).

(7.4)

Let

\displaystyle\text{supp}\,f\subset K\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \Delta^{m}f=\Delta_{D}^{m}f\in L^{2}(\Omega).

This indicates that

Finally, from (

Step 2. We show (

Lemma 7.2
.

Let

and

(7.5)

\displaystyle=

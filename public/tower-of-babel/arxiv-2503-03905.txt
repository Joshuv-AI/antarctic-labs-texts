On the minimum Hamming distance between vectorial Boolean and affine functions Thanks: Support provided from the Program of Excellence TKP2021-NVA-02 at the Budapest University of Technology and Economics.
Gábor P. Nagy

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Hamming distance between vectorial Boolean functions and affine functions. This parameter is known to be related to the non-linearity and differential uniformity of vectorial functions, while the calculation of it is in general difficult. In 2017, Liu, Mesnager and Chen conjectured an upper bound for this metric. We prove this bound for two classes of vectorial bent functions, obtained from finite quasigroups in characteristic two, and we improve the known bounds for two classes of monomial functions of differential uniformity two or four. For many of the known APN functions of dimension at most nine, we compute the exact distance to affine functions.

06E30,94A60,05B25

1.
Introduction

Vectorial Boolean functions
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
, also called
substitution boxes,
play a central role in symmetric key block ciphers. Because they are the only non-linear components of the ciphers, they provide
confusion.
The study of the nonlinear properties of vectorial Boolean functions is fundamental for the evaluation of the resistance of the block cipher against the main attacks, such as the differential attack and the linear attack; see
[
15
,
1
]
. The two main metrics of these nonlinear properties are the
differential uniformity
\delta_{f}
(the lower is the less linear), and the
nonlinearity
\mathrm{nl}(f)
.
The
Walsh-Hadamard transform
provides an effective tool for computation with nonlinearity
\mathrm{nl}(f)
.

In this paper, we deal with a third metric, which is called by different names in the literature:
vectorial nonlinearity

\mathrm{NL}_{\boldmath{v}}(f)
by the author of this paper
[
17
]
,
distance to affine functions

d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
by Carlet
[
7
]
,
second type of nonlinearity

\mathcal{N}_{\text{v}}
by Liu, Mesnager, and Chen
[
14
]
. This metric should not be confused with the notion of
r
th-order nonlinearity
of Boolean functions, which is the minimum Hamming distance of
f
from the set of all
n
-variable Boolean functions having an algebraic degree at most
r
, see
[
19
]
.

In this paper, we use the terminology “distance to affine function”. For a map
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
, we define
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
as the Hamming distance of
f
to the set
\mathcal{A}
of affine maps
\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
. The computation of
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
is generally difficult. In addition, it is difficult to give non-trivial lower and upper bounds for
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
. The distance to affine functions is linked to the nonlinearity by the inequality

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\geq\mathrm{nl}(f),

that holds for all
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
; see
[
14
]
. For recent results on bounds involving nonlinearity and differential uniformity, see
[
9
,
25
]
. Using the concept of Sidon sets and
t
-thin sets, the lower bound

(1)

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\geq 2^{n}-\sqrt{\delta_{f}}\cdot 2^{n/2}-\frac{1}{2}

has been shown in
[
17
]
by the author of this paper, and independently by Ryabov
[
18
]
. Concerning upper bounds, we have the trivial inequality

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\leq 2^{n}-n-1.

The function
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
is called an
(n,m)
-bent function, if its nonlinearity achieves the the covering radius upper bound:

\displaystyle\mathrm{nl}(f)=2^{n-1}-2^{n/2-1}.

For the class of
(n,m)
-bent functions, Carlet, Ding and Yuan
[
8
]
proved

(2)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)\big(2^{n}-2^{n/2}\big)\leq d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)\big(2^{n}+2^{n/2}\big).

In 2017, Liu, Mesnager and Chen formulated a conjecture:

Problem

(Liu-Mesnager-Chen Conjecture
[
14
]
)
.

For any map
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
,

(3)

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)\big(2^{n}-2^{n/2}\big)

holds. In particular, if
n=m
, then

(4)

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})\leq 2^{n}-2^{n/2}-1.

The covering radius bound implies the conjecture for
m=1
. For
(n,m)
-bent functions, the conjecture implies the exact value of the distance from affine functions:

(5)

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)\big(2^{n}-2^{n/2}\big).

Theorem 1
.

Let
t\leq m
be positive integers and
(\mathsf{GF}(2^{m}),+,\star)
be a left pre-quasifield. Let the maps
\gamma,\tau,h:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})
and
\sigma:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
be such that
\gamma
is balanced,
\tau
is surjective linear,
\sigma
is invertible, and
h
is arbitrary. Define the
(2m,t)
-bent functions
f_{1},f_{2}
as

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\star\frac{x}{y}\right),

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(\sigma(y)\star{x}\right)+h(y).

Then

d_{H}(f_{1},\mathcal{A})=d_{H}(f_{2},\mathcal{A})=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{t}}\right)\big(2^{2m}-2^{m}\big).

Theorem 2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.

(i)

If
n
is even and
h_{1}(x)=x^{2^{n}-2}
, then

\displaystyle 2^{n}-\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{n/2}-\frac{3}{2}\leq d_{H}(h_{1},\mathcal{A})\leq 2^{n}-2^{n/2}-2.

(ii)

If
n
is divisible by
4
,
d=2^{n/2-1}+1
, and
h_{2}(x)=x^{d}
, then

\displaystyle 2^{n}-\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{n/2}-\frac{1}{2}\leq d_{H}(h_{2},\mathcal{A})\leq 2^{n}-2^{n/2}-2.

In particular, the Liu-Mesnager-Chen Conjecture holds for both functions
h_{1}(x)
,
h_{2}(x)
.

Notice that
h_{1}(x)
is Nyberg’s Field Inverse Function, used in the AES cryptosystem with
n=8
. In even dimension, the Field Inverse Function has differential uniformity
4
. The function
h_{2}(x)
is a monomial APN function of Gold type, and the exponent is
d=2^{k}+1
, where
k=n/2-1
. As
n/2
is even,
k
is odd and
\gcd(k,n)=\gcd(n/2-1,n)=1
. The APN property of
h_{2}(x)
follows.

At the end of the paper, we will present new results for the minimum Hamming distance between affine and APN functions of dimension
n=6,7,8,9
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
A,B
be finite sets. The function
f:A\to B
is
balanced,
if the preimage
f^{-1}(b)
has size
|A|/|B|
for all
b\in B
. The
Hamming distance

d_{H}(f,g)
between two functions
f,g:A\to B
is
defined as the number of inputs at which they differ:

d_{H}(f,g)=|\{a\in A:f(a)\neq g(a)\}|.

We say that a function
f
is
linear,
if its definition domain is a vector space over
\mathsf{GF}(2)
, and the function is
\mathsf{GF}(2)
-linear. The sum of a linear function and a constant is called an
affine function.
The space of affine functions
\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{n,m}
, or simply
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
q
be a prime power, and
t
a positive integer. For
x,y\in\mathsf{GF}(q)^{t}
,
x\cdot y
denotes the (non-degenerate)
inner product

x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{t}y_{t}
. If we identify the
\mathsf{GF}(q)
-vector spaces
\mathsf{GF}(q^{t})
and
\mathsf{GF}(q)^{t}
, then the inner product can be written as
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(q^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(q)}(xy)
. The map

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(q^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(q)}(x)=x+x^{q}+\cdots+x^{q^{t-1}}

is the
trace map
of the field extension
\mathsf{GF}(q^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(q)
.

The
nonlinearity
of a Boolean function
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)
is defined as the minimum Hamming distance between
f
and all affine Boolean functions in
n
variables, denoted by
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the set of all affine functions on
n
variables. The nonlinearity of a Boolean function can be expressed as:

\mathrm{nl}(f)=d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\max_{a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}|W_{f}(a)|,

where
W_{f}(a)
is the
Walsh-Hadamard transform
of
f
:

W_{f}(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)+a\cdot x},\qquad a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}.

Parseval’s relation
\sum_{a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}W_{f}^{2}(a)=2^{2n}
implies

\mathrm{nl}(f)\leq 2^{n-1}-2^{n/2-1}

for every
n
-variable Boolean function
f
. This bound, tight for every even
n
, is called the
covering radius bound.
Functions achieving this bound are called
n
-variable bent functions.
The
Maiorana-McFarland class
is the most important direct construction of bent functions. Let
\pi
be an arbitrary permutation on the set
\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n/2}
, and let
h
be an arbitrary Boolean function in
n/2
variables,
x,y\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n/2}
. Then

f(x,y)=x\cdot\pi(y)+h(y)

is a bent function in
n
variables.

Vectorial Boolean functions,
also called
(n,m)
-functions,
are functions from
\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
. If
v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\setminus\{0\}
, then the Boolean function
v\cdot f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)
,
(v\cdot f)(x)=v\cdot f(x)
is called a
component Boolean function
of
f
. The
nonlinearity
of
f
is the minimum distance between its component Boolean functions and affine Boolean functions:

\mathrm{nl}(f)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\setminus\{0\}\\
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n,1}\end{subarray}}d_{H}(v\cdot f,\alpha).

By the covering radius bound,
\mathrm{nl}(f)\leq 2^{n-1}-2^{n/2-1}
. The functions achieving this bound are called
(n,m)
-bent functions,
or
vectorial bent functions,
or
bent vectorial functions.
It is known that
(n,m)
-bent functions exist only if
n
is even and
m\leq n/2
.

The
Walsh transform
of an
(n,m)
-function
f
is defined as

W_{f}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}(-1)^{b\cdot f(x)+a\cdot x},

where
a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}
,
b\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
. For the nonlinearity, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{nl}(f)

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\\
b\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\setminus\{0\}\end{subarray}}|W_{f}(a,b)|

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,b)\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\times\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\\
(a,b)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}|W_{f}(a,b)|.

For any vectorial Boolean function
f:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
,
a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and
b\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
, we set

\delta(a,b)=|\{x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\mid f(x+a)+f(x)=b\}|.

The
differential uniformity

\delta_{f}
of
f
is defined as

\delta_{f}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\setminus\{0\}\\
b\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\end{subarray}}\delta(a,b).

We also say that
f
is
differentially
\delta
-uniform,
if
\delta_{f}\leq\delta
.
\delta_{f}
is a positive even integer. When
\delta_{f}=2
, the possible smallest value,
f
is said to be
almost perfect nonlinear
(APN for short). APN functions are a special class of vectorial Boolean functions with the highest possible resistance to differential cryptanalysis. They play an important role in the design of S-boxes for symmetric-key cryptosystems, and they have many desirable cryptographic properties. However, their implementation can be more complex than other types of functions, due to their larger S-boxes.

There are
6
known infinite classes of pairs
(n,d)
such that the power function
f(x)=x^{d}
is APN over the finite field
\mathsf{GF}(2^{n})
. An
APN Gold function
is a power function of the form
f(x)=x^{2^{k}+1}
over
\mathsf{GF}(2^{n})
, where
\gcd(n,k)=1
. APN Gold functions take a special place among APN functions.

The main notions of equivalence of Boolean and vectorial Boolean functions are as follows:

(1)

Two
(n,m)
-functions
f,g
are
linearly equivalent,
if there are invertible linear function
L:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}
,
L^{\prime}:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
such that
g=L^{\prime}\circ f\circ L
.

(2)

Two
(n,m)
-functions
f,g
are
affine equivalent,
if there are invertible affine functions
L:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}
,
L^{\prime}:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
such that
g=L^{\prime}\circ f\circ L
.

(3)

Two
(n,m)
-functions
f,g
are
extended affine (EA) equivalent,
if
g=L^{\prime}\circ f\circ L+L^{\prime\prime}
holds with invertible affine functions
L:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}
,
L^{\prime}:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
, and arbitrary affine
(n,m)
-function
L^{\prime\prime}
.

(4)

Two
(n,m)
-functions
f,g
are
CCZ-equivalent,
if there is an invertible affine map
LL:\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\times\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\to\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\times\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}
which maps the graph

\mathcal{G}_{f}=\{(x,f(x))\mid x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\}

of
f
to the graph

\mathcal{G}_{g}=\{(x,g(x))\mid x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\}

of
g
.

With fixed
n,m
, each equivalence relation above is a strict particular case of the next one, see page 29 in
[
6
]
. Therefore, any EA-invariant property or parameter is linearly and affine invariant. The differential uniformity, the nonlinearity of Boolean functions, and the nonlinearity of vectorial Boolean functions are CCZ-invariant parameters. There are some classes of
(n,m)
-functions, where CCZ-equivalence reduces to EA-equivalence, see Yoshiara
[
26
]
for quadratic APN functions, and Theorem 1 in
[
4
]
by Budaghyan and Carlet for
(n,m)
-bent functions.

Problem
.

Is the parameter
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
of
(n,m)
-functions CCZ-invariant?

The following useful formula has been communicated to us by Claude Carlet.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
be an
(n,m)
-function, and let
\mathcal{L}_{n,m}
denote the space of linear
(n,m)
-functions. Then

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=2^{n}-2^{-m}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\\
L^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{m,n}\end{subarray}}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}}(-1)^{v\cdot b}\,W_{f}(L^{*}(v),v).

Proof.

Let
A(x)=L(x)+b
be an affine
(n,m)
-function with
L\in\mathcal{L}_{n,m}
. Define the linear
(m,n)
-function
L^{*}
by
L^{*}(v)\cdot x=v\cdot L(x)
for all
x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle d_{H}(f,A)

\displaystyle=2^{n}-2^{-m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}\\
v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{v\cdot(f(x)+A(x))}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-2^{-m}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}}(-1)^{v\cdot b}\sum_{x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}(-1)^{v\cdot f(x)+v\cdot L(x)}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-2^{-m}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}}(-1)^{v\cdot b}\sum_{x\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{n}}(-1)^{v\cdot f(x)+L^{*}(v)\cdot x}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-2^{-m}\sum_{v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}}(-1)^{v\cdot b}\,W_{f}(L^{*}(v),v).

The lemma follows from the definition of
d_{H}(f,\mathcal{A})
.
∎

3.
The distance of
(2m,t)
-bent functions to affine functions

We recall that
(n,m)
-functions achieving the covering radius bound

\mathrm{nl}(f)\leq 2^{n-1}-2^{n/2-1}

are called
(n,m)
-bent functions.
It is known that
(n,m)
-bent functions exist only if
n
is even and
m\leq n/2
. The function
f
is
(n,m)
-bent if and only if all its non-trivial component functions
v\cdot f
are bent (
v\in\mathsf{GF}(2)^{m}\setminus\{0\}
). Equivalently,
W_{f}(a,b)=\pm 2^{n/2}
for
b\neq 0
.

We present three infinite classes of
(2m,t)
-bent functions. To the best knowledge of the author, the first two are commonly known, and the third one is new.

3.1.
Maiorana-McFarland type
(n,m)
-bent functions

The first class is a generalization of the Maiorana-McFarland construction. We take maps
\tau:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})
,
\pi:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
, and
h:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})
such that
\tau
is surjective linear,
\pi
is invertible, and
h
is arbitrary. We write

(6)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\tau(x\pi(y))+h(y)

with
x,y\in\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
. Then, the component functions are all Maiorana-McFarland bent functions of the form

f^{\prime}(x,y)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(x\pi^{\prime}(y))+h^{\prime}(y).

Indeed, for fixed
y
, the map

\alpha_{y}:x\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(\tau(x\pi(y)))

is a linear Boolean function. Hence, there is a unique element
\pi^{\prime}(y)\in\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
such that
\alpha_{y}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(x\pi^{\prime}(y))
. It is easy to see that
\pi^{\prime}
is a permutation of
\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
. This implies that
f(x,y)
is
(2m,t)
-bent with
t\leq m
.

3.2.
Dillon type
(2m,t)
-bent functions

To define the two other classes of
(n,m)
-bent functions, we need the notion of left pre-quasifields.

Definition 4
.

Let
(Q,+)
be a finite abelian group and
\star:Q\times Q\to Q
a binary operation with the following properties:

(1)

x\star 0=0\star x=0
for all
x\in Q
.

(2)

x\star(y+z)=x\star y+x\star z
for all
x,y,z\in Q
.

(3)

For all
0\neq a\in Q
the mappings
x\mapsto x\star a
and
x\mapsto a\star x
are bijective.

Then
Q=(Q,+,\star)
is called a
(left) pre-quasifield
or a weak (left) quasifield.

One knows that
(Q,+)
has to be an elementary abelian
p
-group,
p
a prime, i.e.
Q
is a vector space over
\mathsf{GF}(p)
, and
|Q|=q=p^{m}
for some positive integer
m
. One usually identifies
(Q,+)
with the additive group of the finite field
\mathsf{GF}(q)
.

The
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{S}
class of bent functions was introduced by Dillon in his PhD thesis
[
11
]
in 1974. A subclass of the
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{S}
class has been presented in algebraic terms by Wu
[
24
]
and by Wan and Xu
[
22
]
. The right division operation of
(Q,+,\star)
is defined as

\star\frac{x}{y}=\begin{cases}a&\text{if $y\neq 0$ and $x=a\star y$},\\
0&\text{if $y=0$.}\end{cases}

Theorem 5

(Theorem 3.1 of
[
24
]
and Corollary 17 of
[
22
]
)
.

Let
n=2m
and
(\mathsf{GF}(2^{m}),+,\star)
be a left pre-quasifield. Assume
g
is a balanced Boolean function in
m
variables with
g(0)=0
. Then the function

(7)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=g\left(\star\frac{x}{y}\right)

is a
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{S}^{-}
bent function.

The generalization to
(2m,t)
-bent functions (
t\leq m
) is straightforward.

Proposition 6
.

Let
t\leq m
be positive integers and
(\mathsf{GF}(2^{m}),+,\star)
be a left pre-quasifield. Assume
\gamma
is a balanced
(m,t)
-function. Then

(8)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\gamma\left(\star\frac{x}{y}\right)

is a
(2m,t)
-bent function.

Proof.

Since the composition
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}\circ\gamma
is balanced, all component functions of
f
are bent functions of type (
7
).
∎

3.3.
Quasifield type
(2m,t)
-bent functions

The following lemma is a well-known property of pre-quasifields.

Lemma 7
.

Let
m
be a positive integer,
p
a prime, and
(Q,+,\star)
a left pre-quasifield with
|Q|=p^{m}
. If
y_{1},y_{2}\in Q
are different elements, then the map

\beta:Q\to Q,\qquad x\mapsto y_{1}\star x-y_{2}\star x

is a
\mathsf{GF}(p)
-linear isomorphism.

Proof.

If
x\neq 0
is in
\ker(\beta)
, then
y_{1}\star x=y_{2}\star x
contradicts the defining property (3) of a left pre-quasifield. Hence,
\beta
is bijective. The
\mathsf{GF}(p)
-linearity is clear.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
t\leq m
be positive integers and
(\mathsf{GF}(2^{m}),+,\star)
be a left pre-quasifield. Let the maps
\tau:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})
,
\sigma:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
and
h:\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})\to\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})
be such that
\tau
is surjective linear,
\sigma
is invertible, and
h
is arbitrary. Then

(9)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\tau\left(\sigma(y)\star{x}\right)+h(y)

is a
(2m,t)
-bent function.

Proof.

Any component function of
f
has the shape
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(uf(x,y))
for some
u\in\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})^{*}
. For fixed
u
and
y
, the map

\alpha_{y}:x\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(u\tau(\sigma(y)\star x)+uh(y))

is an affine Boolean function in
m
variables. Hence, there is a unique element
\sigma^{\prime}(y)\in\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
such that

\alpha_{y}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(x\sigma^{\prime}(y))+h^{\prime}(y),

where

.

We show that

is bijective on
\mathsf{GF}(2^{m})
. Let us fix

,
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
. There is an element

such that

As

is surjective, there is a

with

. By Lemma

, there is

. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{y_{1}}(x)+\alpha_{y_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(u\tau(y_{1}\star x+y_{2}\star x)+uh(y_{1})+uh(y_{2}))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(u\tau(z))+h^{\prime}(y_{1})+h^{\prime}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathsf{GF}(2^{t})/\mathsf{GF}(2)}(uv)+h^{\prime}(y_{1})+h^{\prime}(y_{2})

\displaystyle\neq 0.

This implies
\alpha_{y_{1}}(x)\neq\alpha_{y_{2}}(x)
and
\sigma^{\prime}(y_{1})\neq\sigma^{\prime}(y_{2})

are of Maiorana-McFarland type bent function. This finishes the proof of the theorem.

4.

In this section, we prove Theorem

Definition 9

Let

be a finite abelian group. We say that

The behavior of Sidon sets depends very much on the structure of

-group

. Moreover, the Sidon property is affine invariant, hence, we can speak of Sidon sets in affine subspaces. (Affine subspaces are translates of
\mathsf{GF}(2)
-linear subspaces.)

Lemma 10

(Obvious upper bound)
.

For a Sidon

Proof.

See Lemma 4 and 5 of
[

∎

The following lemma is folklore:

The
(n,n)
-function is APN if and only if its graph

Lemma 12

If

Lemma 13
.

Let

be even,

Proof of Theorem

.

Since the Hamming distance of two functions

Conclusion

In this paper, we have made significant progress on the study of vectorial Boolean functions and their nonlinear properties. We proved that for
(2m,t)
-bent functions constructed using pre-quasifields, the distance to affine functions can be precisely determined (Theorem
1
). Furthermore, we obtained tight bounds on the distance to affine functions for specific APN functions, including Nyberg’s Field Inverse Function and a monomial APN function of Gold type (Theorem
2
). These results provide strong evidence for the Liu-Mesnager-Chen Conjecture in the case of vectorial bent functions, and demonstrate its validity for certain classes APN of functions. Our findings have important implications for cryptography, particularly in the design of secure cryptographic primitives. Future research directions may include extending our results to other classes of functions and exploring the connections between vectorial Boolean functions, Sidon sets, and other areas of mathematics.

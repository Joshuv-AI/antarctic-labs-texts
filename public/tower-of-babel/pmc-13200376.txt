A risk assessment nomogram for cardiac remodeling in the multimorbid geriatric patients: a retrospective study
Authors: Bo Liu, Jian Xiao, Fei Li, Lan Hu, Ya Ting Liu, Dan Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMyocardial remodeling is a key precursor to heart failure and adverse cardiovascular outcomes. While comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes, and chronic kidney disease are prevalent in the geriatric population. Early identification of risk factors for myocardial remodeling could improve clinical outcomes in this population.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed data from older patients (≥ 60 years) at Xiangya Hospital (n = 306). Clinical, echocardiographic, and biochemical data were collected, and myocardial remodeling was defined using standardized criteria. Multivariate logistic regression identified associated factors, which were incorporated into nomograms for left and right heart remodeling. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis.ResultsThe following factors were associated with myocardial remodeling: myocardiopathy, arrhythmia, valvular disease, PCI, diabetes mellitus, smoking history, male sex, renal function (including eGFR, urea, and uric acid), thyroid dysfunction, and albumin levels.ConclusionsThis study developed nomograms that provide a practical tool for early risk stratification, enabling targeted monitoring and intervention in the geriatric population.Trial registrationThe Ethics Committees at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, accepted the study protocol with a waiver of informed consent (No. 2025060932).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-026-07462-2.Keywords: Cardiac remodeling, Geriatric comorbidities, Assessment nomogram

Introduction

Myocardial remodeling is defined as alterations in cardiac size, morphology, and function in response to endogenous or exogenous injury. It is commonly assessed through measurable changes in chamber dimensions, wall thickness, volume, mass, and ejection fraction [1]. Extensive evidence has established the association of cardiovascular diseases—such as coronary atherosclerotic disease (CAD), hypertension, and cardiomyopathy—with this remodeling process, which ultimately contributes to adverse outcomes like heart failure [2]. Beyond these typical cardiovascular conditions, older adults often present with a complex comorbidity profile, including cerebrovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, respiratory disease, and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Although these links are recognized, there remains a gap in clinically applicable, quantitative tools that can stratify remodeling risk based on readily available clinical and echocardiographic data—particularly for the growing population of older patients with multiple, overlapping comorbidities. A practical tool enabling early identification of high-risk individuals would facilitate targeted monitoring and timely intervention, thereby potentially improving survival and quality of life in this vulnerable group.

Methods

Study design and population

This study retrospectively analyzed data from older patients managed at Xiangya Hospital. The Ethics Committees at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, accepted the study protocol with a waiver of informed consent (No. 2025060932).

We enrolled consecutive patients aged ≥ 60 years who were admitted to the geriatric cardiovascular ward between January 1 and March 31, 2025, and who had complete clinical data and echocardiography required for the study. After excluding 152 patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria or had incomplete records, 306 patients were included in the final analysis (Figure S1). To generate a standard curve for the nomogram, 214 individuals were randomly selected for the study. The validation group consisted of the remaining 92 people. The age requirement was 60 years or older. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) Age < 60 years, (b). deceased patients, (c). Incomplete documents.

Data collection

The data collected included the general situation of older patients, echocardiographic data, blood biochemical indexes, and medical history.

Definitions

According to the echocardiographic measurement guidelines of the Chinese Medical Association [3] and in combination with the operational standards of Xiangya Hospital’s cardiac color ultrasound. In accordance with clinical practice, echocardiography was performed upon admission for patients in NYHA functional classes Ⅰ-Ⅲ. For patients with NYHA class Ⅳ, imaging was deferred until after their condition stabilized following initiation of guideline-directed medical therapy. The specific criteria for myocardial remodeling were established as follows: left atrial (LA) diameter > 35 mm, left ventricular (LV) diameter > 55 mm(male) or > 50 mm(female), left ventricular mass index (LVMI) > 125(male) or > 120(female), relative wall thickness(RWT) > 0.42, ejection fraction (EF) < 50, right atrium (RA) diameter > 40 mm, right ventricular (RV) diameter > 25 mm, and pulmonary hypertension (PAH) >30mmHg, as indicated in Supplementary Table S1. Supplementary Table S2 lists the thresholds for the blood biochemical index.

Development and assessment of the nomogram

To evaluate cardiac remodeling in older patients, a multivariate logistic regression model was constructed, and the results are presented as a nomogram. Associated factor (P < 0.05) identified by multivariate logistic regression were incorporated into the nomogram. The significance level was selected at P < 0.01 because of the low prognostic value of LV remodeling variables. Associated factor values were given points by drawing vertical lines to the top axis for nomogram interpretation. To evaluate the likelihood of cardiac remodeling, these points were added up and plotted on the “Total Points” axis. A downward line was then projected to the bottom scale. After that, the model underwent external validation. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test and Youden index were used to evaluate the model’s performance. Assessment accuracy and calibration are measured by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, the calibration curve, the concordance index (C-index), and the area under the ROC curve (AUC). Clinical net benefit is calculated with the decision curve analysis (DCA).

Statistical analysis

All accessible samples were included in the analysis, and the sample size was determined using PASS software. The rms, pROC, ggplot2, and dca packages were used in the R program to perform statistical analyses. For properly distributed data, continuous variables were expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD). Every statistical test was two-sided, and a P-value of less than 0.05 (or less than 0.01 if indicated) was deemed statistically significant.

Results

General characteristics

306 patients were included in the final analysis (Figure S1). The average age of the participants in this study was 73.56 years. To create a standard curve for the nomogram, 214 participants were chosen at random. The validation group consisted of the remaining 92 individuals. All recruited patients’ baseline characteristics, including the 43 assessed criteria, are compiled in Supplementary Table S3.

Identification of risk factors for cardiac remodeling

Univariate logistic regression was utilized to conduct the first screening for significant factors (Supplementary Table S4). For identifying variables for every aspect of cardiac remodeling, multivariate logistic regression analysis was used (Table 1). Arrhythmia (OR = 1.909, 95% CI:1.029–3.562, P = 0.041), myocardiopathy (OR = 17.306, 95% CI:2.945-329.855, P = 0.009), and thyroid dysfunction (OR = 2.072, 95% CI:1.006–4.251, P = 0.047) were significant related factors of LA remodeling. Male gender (OR = 0.070, 95% CI:0.004–0.355, P = 0.011), high uric acid (OR = 2.564, 95% CI:0.988–6.609, P = 0.050), decreased glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) (OR = 0.340, 95% CI:0.112–1.088, P = 0.060), and diabetes mellitus (OR = 2.483, 95% CI:0.934–7.506, P = 0.083) all associated with LV remodeling. Strong correlations were observed between the LVMI and myocardiopathy (OR = 32.221, 95% CI:5.721-216.414, P < 0.001), valvular disease (OR = 18.556, 95% CI:2.985–121.880, P = 0.001), and smoking history (OR = 6.811, 95% CI:1.850-34.213, P = 0.008). Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) (OR = 3.237, 95% CI:0.963–10.171, P = 0.047) and myocardiopathy (OR = 5.852, 95% CI:1.043–27.603, P = 0.030) either had an impact on RWT. Factor associated with EF were myocardiopathy (OR = 8.633, 95% CI:1.909–46.953, P = 0.007), valvular disease (OR = 7.973, 95% CI:2.126–34.184, P = 0.003), Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) (OR = 2.085, 95% CI:1.013–4.279, P = 0.045), and uric acid (OR = 2.307, 95% CI:1.101–4.833, P = 0.026). Arrhythmia (OR = 2.429, 95% CI:1.139–5.334, P = 0.023) and valvular disease (OR = 5.253, 95% CI:1.425–20.053, P = 0.012) were linked to RA remodeling. whereas arrhythmia (OR = 2.831, 95% CI:1.295–6.493, P = 0.011) and myocardiopathy (OR = 6.847, 95% CI:1.650-30.262, P = 0.008) were attributed to RV remodeling. PAH demonstrated a positive correlation with urea (OR = 4.647, 95% CI:1.359–14.832, P = 0.010) and an inverse correlation with albumin (OR = 0.206, 95% CI:0.048–1.005, P = 0.039). Summary of factors associated with cardiac remodeling

Risk factor nomogram development

We constructed nomograms using the risk parameters listed in Table 1: one for left heart remodeling (Fig. 2) and another for right heart remodeling (Fig. 3). Older patients who score higher overall on these nomograms are at higher risk of myocardial remodeling. For instance, an older male patient with stage 3 CKD, hyperuricemia, and diabetes mellitus would accrue roughly 100, 36, 34, and 42 points, respectively, for a total of 212 points, which would indicate a 60% estimated likelihood of LV remodeling (Fig. 2). High risk element performance was indicated by the related nomogram’s C-index of 0.914 (95% CI: 0.836–0.972) and Hosmer-Lemeshow test (P = 0.572) (Table S5).

Nomogram accuracy and clinical net benefit

Figures S2-S3 show the ROC curves (Figure S2) and calibration curves (Figure S3) for each indicator in the training cohorts. The nomogram’s high accuracy and consistency were demonstrated by the training cohorts’ AUC values, which varied from 0.659 to 0.826 (Figures S2A-H), and the calibration curves near resemblance to the ideal diagonal line (Figures S3A-H). Furthermore, the DCA curves showed that the risk assessment model had a great clinical net benefit (Figures S4A-H).

92 older patients were used for external validation; the validation model’s accuracy was good, as shown by the AUC values, which ranged from 0.479 to 0.914 (Figures S2I-P). The excellent consistency of the validation model was demonstrated by the majority of the indicators’ validation curves (Figures S3I-P) being near the diagonal line. Additionally, as demonstrated by the DCA results, several association models demonstrated noteworthy clinical net gains in the validation cohort (Figures S4I-P). Furthermore, extensive performance metrics for all myocardial remodeling component nomograms are provided in Supplementary Table S5, including the C-index, Hosmer-Lemeshow test scores, and Youden index. The findings indicate that the left heart model has a greater ability to assess risk than the right heart model.

These findings support the potential utility of our created nomogram model for clinical decision-making; however, more study population expansion is still needed.

Discussion

The accelerating global spread of population aging is highlighted in the United Nations’ World Population Prospects 2024 report. The World Health Organization has put out the idea of healthy aging. Older adults frequently have comorbid conditions like diabetes, CAD, CKD, cerebrovascular diseases, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and are more susceptible to heart failure, which has a major impact on survival and quality of life.

Atrial and ventricular chamber dilatation, diminished ventricular wall motion, and a decreased ejection fraction are the hallmarks of myocardial remodeling. These pathological alterations cause heart failure to worsen and may even result in sudden cardiac death. In the aged population, heart failure incidence can be effectively decreased and health outcomes improved by early identification of high-risk individuals for myocardial remodeling, followed by careful surveillance and prompt management.

It has been established that the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, inflammatory reactions, and elevated sympathetic nerve activity are the factors behind myocardial remodeling. Few studies have examined the relationship between cardiac remodeling and geriatric comorbidities; the majority of predictive factors are based on biochemical markers and signaling pathways. This study aimed to develop a column-chart tool, based on routine clinical data, for risk stratification of specific cardiac remodeling phenotypes in older patients. Our research offers fresh perspectives to direct professional judgment and support early intervention for patients at high risk.

Myocardiopathy, arrhythmia, valvular disease, PCI, diabetes mellitus, smoking history, male gender, renal function (eGFR, urea, uric acid), thyroid dysfunction, and albumin were found to be risk factor of myocardial remodeling in this study. Among them, men, thyroid dysfunction, uric acid, urea nitrogen, and albumin are newly discovered risk factor in this research.

1 case of alcoholic cardiomyopathy (7.7%), 5 cases of non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (38.5%), and 7 cases of dilated cardiomyopathy (53.8%) made up the 13 cardiomyopathy patients in the study. Cardiomyopathy is characterized by myocardial remodeling, such as atrioventricular dilation, ventricular wall thickening, and decreased EF. This study identified that cardiomyopathy is associated with distinct cardiac remodeling phenotypes, including variations in LA size, LVMI, LV systolic function, and RV enlargement. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy patients have more obvious involvement of the left heart [4]. It is possible to reverse cardiac remodeling in the early phases of active and successful pharmacological treatment [5]. Future studies enrolling larger cohorts of older patients with cardiomyopathy may further elucidate the relationship between specific cardiomyopathy subtypes and their corresponding remodeling patterns. We recommend enhancing echocardiographic screening for patients with a family history and aggressively starting anti-remodeling therapy at an early stage of diagnosis.

According to this study, one of the related factors of LA, RA, and RV remodeling is arrhythmia. Atrial fibrillation (AF) (45.5%), premature ventricular contractions (21.4%), sick sinus syndrome (17.9%), atrioventricular block (12.4%), and supraventricular tachycardia (2.8%) were included in the study. The incidence of AF is high among the older population. LA enlargement is strongly associated with the development of AF. AF also cause significant left and right atrial remodeling [6, 7]. Long-term AF and LA enlargement sustain each other in a vicious cycle, leading to progressive cardiac remodeling. Correcting AF can improve LA remodeling [8]. The second-highest percentage of older patients suffered premature ventricular contractions. Both the RA and the RV may undergo remodeling as a result of frequent premature ventricular contractions [9]. The above findings optimize the follow-up strategies for patients with arrhythmia and provide evidence-based support for the rational use of β-blockers such as metoprolol and bisoprolol, as well as new drugs like dronedarone.

The medical burden of valve heart disease is rising as the population ages more quickly, with the primary effects of severe valvular lesions being cardiac remodeling and dysfunction [10]. Various valvular diseases can cause distinct patterns of cardiac remodeling. For example, atrial remodeling is the primary outcome of aortic and mitral stenosis [11], whereas adverse LV remodeling is closely linked to aortic stenosis and primary mitral regurgitation [12, 13]. Significant remodeling of the right heart can also result from severe functional tricuspid regurgitation [14, 15]. In our study, valvular heart disease can estimate the probability of RA enlargement, decreased LV ejection fraction, and LV hypertrophy. In the older population, aortic valve lesions are more common. As transcatheter aortic valve replacement technology advances, this study offers support for the selection of interventional treatment timing and indications, which can enhance the prognosis of high-risk patients.

PCI is an essential method of revascularization for CAD. It offers a clear benefit in treating acute myocardial infarction (AMI), but little effect on the long-term prognosis [16], even in cases of severe coronary stenosis, there is no significant difference in long-term prognosis between Optimizing Medication Therapy (OMT) and PCI, OMT should be the primary treatment strategy for patients with stable CAD [17]. One significant finding of this study is that PCI is a factor involved in a lower EF. It implies that PCI indications should be closely monitored in order to prevent negative clinical outcomes. OMT should serve as the cornerstone of treatment and PCI should be performed only on patients with high-risk characteristics or substantial symptoms.

Diabetes is a known high-risk factor for cardiovascular disease. The structure and function of the LV can be sustained with subclinical damage by diabetes [18]. We found that diabetes increases the risk of LV eccentric dilatation but has no relationship with myocardial hypertrophy. It is possible that the early stage of cardiac hypertrophy progressed to a late stage of remodeling due to their long-term diabetes in the geriatric patients. We firmly believe that diabetes patients should have echocardiograms on a frequent basis in order to detect early indications of myocardial remodeling and enable therapies that would effectively lower the risk of unfavorable cardiovascular events.

Both conventional tobacco and new tobacco products, such as e-cigarettes, can cause LV remodeling [19–21]. Notably, active smoking and cumulative smoking exposure are substantially linked to elevated LVMI in older persons [22]. Based on this study, smoking is an associated factor of higher LVMI in older individuals with comorbidities. In addition to its contribution to atherosclerosis, smoking also induces myocardial remodeling. This finding gives fresh, evidence-based evidence for clinical smoking cessation strategies.

The process of cardiac remodeling varies significantly by gender. Males are more likely to experience eccentric remodeling and heart failure, whereas females mostly show concentric remodeling [23, 24]. These variations may be connected to sex hormone levels and sex chromosomes [25, 26]. This study discovered that male gender is a risk factor of LV enlargement in the senior population. We advise older males to undergo more echocardiographic screening in order to identify ventricular remodeling earlier. This finding serves as a crucial foundation for gender-specific therapeutic management approaches.

The notion of “cardiorenal syndrome” describes the interplay between the heart and kidneys. Urea, uric acid, and creatinine are used to evaluate renal function. According to our research, both an eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m² and hyperuricemia (> 420 µmol/L) are associated with LV remodeling, with the latter also linked to a decreased EF. For more than 75% of the CKD patients, LV hypertrophy is observed [27]. Patients with CKD stages 3–4 have a markedly raised LVMI [28]. Hyperuricemia harms cardiomyocytes and encourages LV hypertrophy by triggering oxidative stress and inflammatory reactions [29–31]. High uric acid can predict cardiovascular events in the older population and is a significant risk factor for heart failure [32, 33]. But in our research, eGFR and uric acid are correlated with dilatation of the LV. We hypothesize this might be a dynamic history of cardiac remodeling in older patients, where ventricular dilatation eventually develops from long-term LV hypertrophy. Moreover, the accumulation of uremic toxins can directly promote pulmonary vascular remodeling [34, 35]. Our study discovered that elevated blood urea (> 9.5 mmol/L) is an associated factor for PAH. The conclusion suggests that renal failure and cardiac remodeling are related in multiple ways. Given that a number of pathogenic variables impact renal function in the aged, we advise early, rigorous blood pressure, blood glucose, and uric acid management in addition to optimizing pharmaceutical therapy regimens.

Excessive or insufficient thyroid hormone can have negative consequences for the cardiovascular system. Hypothyroidism can cause LA dilatation [36]. TSH is independently linked to LV remodeling following AMI [37] and the risk of heart failure [38]; thyroid hormone replacement can prevent cardiac remodeling after MI [39]. The incidence of subclinical hypothyroidism and hypothyroidism is higher in the older population. In our study, two diseases accounted for 60% and 22%, respectively. Abnormal thyroid function is associated with LA remodeling, and subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH ≥ 4.95mIU/L) is associated with LV wall thickening. Thyroid hormone supplements for older people are more conservative because they frequently have underlying conditions such as CAD. More research and discussion are needed to determine whether a more proactive low-dose thyroxine intervention strategy should be used for older patients.

The TOPCAT trial discovered a strong correlation between hypoalbuminemia and the incidence of PAH [40]. Decreased albumin concentration is linked to a higher mortality rate with PAH [41]. Our investigation finds that hypoalbuminemia is a related factor of PAH. The incidence of hypoalbuminemia is much higher in the aged population because of a deterioration in digestive and absorptive abilities, as well as a decreased ability for protein synthesis. We advise older patients to make sure they increase high-quality protein to enhance their clinical prognosis. The effect of nutritional therapies on the long-term results of patients with PAH requires further investigation.

LV usually exhibits hypertrophy in hypertension patient [42, 43], but hypertension does not associate with myocardial remodeling in our research. The following elements could be connected to this outcome: (1) some antihypertensive drugs could stop the myocardial remodeling process brought on by hypertension; and (2) the majority of patients reach target blood pressure. To better investigate the relationship between hypertension and myocardial remodeling, future research should concentrate on older hypertension patients who are monitored in outpatient clinics.

AMI frequently results in LV remodeling [44]. However, MI history is not an indicator of ventricular remodeling in older patients with comorbidities. This could be linked to the pathophysiological condition: diabetes mellitus causes myocardial fibrosis and microvascular disease; CKD causes abnormal calcium and phosphorus metabolism, which encourages vascular calcification; and COPD causes persistent hypoxia. These disorders impair the myocardium’s ability to compensate, which lessens the usual post-infarction remodeling response. Furthermore, the process of ventricular remodeling is somewhat inhibited by the long-term use of cardioprotective drugs.

Respiratory conditions such asthma, bronchiectasis, and COPD produce right heart dilatation [45]. Nevertheless, pulmonary conditions are not a factor of right heart remodeling in our study. Fifty patients with respiratory diseases had mild illness or were stable after treatment with inhalants. Besides, the internal validation revealed a relatively low AUC for some indicators (e.g., RV/PH). This is likely due to the cohort’s limited size (n = 92) and low disease prevalence. To improve the model’s assessment efficacy for right heart remodeling, future work must involve enrolling more older outpatients and inpatients from respiratory departments for in-depth analysis, explore the effect of respiratory disorders on right ventricular remodeling.

To sum up, this study formalizes known associations into a quantitative, visual tool, enabling individualized risk assessment for older patients with multiple comorbidities. Higher total scores indicated a higher likelihood of cardiac remodeling for each older patient. A straightforward and user-friendly tool for early valuation of myocardial remodeling in older patients, this risk assessment model offers clinical value for enhancing quality of life and lowering mortality rates in this population.

Limitations

This study assessed cardiac remodeling using conventional echocardiography. While this approach is widely accessible and clinically applied, it lacks the sensitivity of advanced techniques such as speckle tracking strain analysis or cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, which can detect subclinical myocardial changes earlier and more accurately.

This single-center retrospective study is limited by a relatively small sample size. Future validation should involve larger, multi-center prospective cohorts to enhance the model’s accuracy.

This study did not capture the chronicity and severity of major comorbidities (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, arrhythmia), whose progressive nature likely contributes to cardiac remodeling. Collecting such longitudinal exposure data in future prospective studies would mitigate this limitation and add a critical temporal dimension to association models.

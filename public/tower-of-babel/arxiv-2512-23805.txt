Fitted Q Evaluation Without Bellman Completeness via Stationary Weighting
Lars van der Laan, Nathan Kallus

Abstract
Fitted Q Q -evaluation (FQE) is a central method for off-policy evaluation in reinforcement learning, but it generally requires Bellman completeness: that the hypothesis class is closed under the (evaluation) Bellman operator, which is challenging because enlarging the hypothesis class can worsen completeness. We show that the need for this assumption stems from a fundamental norm mismatch : the Bellman operator is γ \gamma -contractive under the stationary distribution of the target policy, whereas FQE minimizes Bellman error under the behavior distribution. We propose a simple fix: reweight each regression step using an estimate of the stationary density ratio, thereby aligning FQE with the norm in which the Bellman operator contracts. This enables strong evaluation guarantees in the absence of realizability or Bellman completeness, avoiding the geometric error blow-up of standard FQE in this setting, while maintaining the practicality of regression-based evaluation.

A norm-mismatch perspective.

The core instability of standard FQE arises from a mismatch between the
regression norm and the Bellman contraction geometry.
For policy evaluation,
the Bellman operator
\mathcal{T}
is
\gamma
-contractive in the stationary
distribution of the target policy
(
14
;
30
)
,
yet FQE minimizes Bellman error in the behavior norm, where no contraction need
hold. Classical guarantees sidestep this mismatch only by assuming realizability
or completeness.

We show that a single modification aligns FQE with the correct geometry.
Reweighting each regression step by an estimate of the stationary density ratio
restores contraction of the fitted Bellman update, even under misspecification.
The resulting
stationary-weighted FQE
preserves the simplicity of fitted
Bellman iteration and requires only an admissible weighting function—such as
the stationary density ratio. Under classical Bellman completeness, any weighting suffices, so the choice of
weights is irrelevant.

Targeting the stationary norm also provides a conceptual advantage: it depends
only on
(\pi,P)
and is invariant to the behavior policy and the initial-state
distribution. Predictors aligned with this geometry capture the long-run
dynamics of the target policy rather than artifacts of data collection.

2.1
Preliminaries

We consider a discounted Markov decision process (MDP) with state space

A target policy

The associated policy evaluation Bellman operator is

We observe i.i.d. transitions

For any policy

For any distribution

\|f\|_{2,\mu}:=\Bigl(\int_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}f(s,a)^{2}\,\mu(a,ds)\Bigr)^{1/2}.

3.1
Algorithm

Let
\mu

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}^{(k+1)}\in\arg\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{w}_{\mu}(S_{i},A_{i})\Bigl\{R_{i}

Why the stationary norm?

The stationary-distribution norm is the canonical space in which the Bellman
operator is guaranteed to be a
\gamma
-contraction for any MDP under policy

\pi
. This makes
L^{2}(\mu)
the natural geometry for analyzing and
stabilizing Bellman updates. Beyond contraction, it also weights prediction
error by the state–action pairs that the target policy visits in the long
run. Because
\mu
depends only on
(\pi,P)
, this norm is invariant to the
behavior policy and the initial-state distribution. As a result, predictors
that are accurate in
L^{2}(\mu)
tend to generalize across datasets or
deployments that share the same environment but differ in how data were
collected. Under standard ergodicity assumptions, empirical visitation
frequencies under
\pi
converge to
\mu
, so error in this norm captures
long-run prediction performance
(
54
;
38
)
.

Computational complexity.

Stationary-weighted FQE adds negligible overhead to standard FQE: density
ratios are estimated once, and each subsequent regression iteration has the
same computational cost as in the unweighted case. The overall complexity
remains
K
supervised regressions over
\mathcal{F}
, with the ratio-estimation
step comparable to a single additional regression pass.

Section
4
formalizes the stationary-weighted update as an
inexact
\gamma
-contractive Picard iteration and provides the resulting
finite-sample guarantees.

3.2
Estimation of Stationary Ratios

A key component of stationary-weighted FQE is the estimation of the
stationary density ratio. This problem is well studied, with many optimized
variants in the DICE family

(
47
;
76
;
36
;
72
)
.
A widely used approach encodes stationarity via a convex–concave
saddle-point formulation, as in the DICE estimators of

47
;
76
. The stationary ratio

w_{\mu}=d\mu/d\nu_{b}
is characterized by the fixed-point
relation
w_{\mu}=\mathcal{K}w_{\mu}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is the adjoint of

\pi P
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. The key variational identity is that, for all measurable

f
,

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{b}}\!\left[w_{\mu}(S,A)\,\bigl(f(S,A)-(\mathcal{K}f)(S,A)\bigr)\right]=0,

which holds if and only if
w_{\mu}
is stationary for
(\pi,P)
.
This yields the empirical min–max problem

\displaystyle\arg\min_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\;\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\Biggl\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(S_{i},A_{i})\,\bigl(h(S_{i},A_{i})-h(S_{i}^{\prime},A_{i}^{\prime})\bigr)-\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(S_{i},A_{i})^{2}\Biggr\}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(S_{i},A_{i})-1\right)^{2},

where
A_{i}^{\prime}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid S_{i}^{\prime})
, and the final term enforces a
normalization constraint to prevent the trivial solution. A practical implementation uses a linear
class or RKHS for the critic, reducing the inner maximization to a
regularized least-squares problem that computes approximate balancing weights

(
72
)
. A fixed-point estimation alternative is given in
Appendix
B
.

Estimating stationary ratios can be challenging in general, but in our
setting the ratio enters FQE solely as a regression weight. Consequently,
the weighted Bellman objective continues to target the correct

Q
-function when
Q^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}
, and even coarse or
misspecified ratio estimates can still improve stability by upweighting
regions where the stationary distribution concentrates.

Admissible weighting functions.

Our theory does not require the exact stationary ratio. It suffices to assume
the existence of a weighting function that induces the correct projection

We define the

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{F}}\|\mathcal{T}(Q)-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}.

Lemma 3

(Regret for inexact Picard iteration)
.

Assume
C1
-
C8
. There exists a constant

such that, for all

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{(k)})-

\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{(k+1)}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\;C\Bigl(\delta_{n}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\log(1/\eta)}{n}}\Bigr)+\;C\Bigl(\Bigl\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Bigr\|_{2,\mu}^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\Bigl\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Bigr\|_{2,\mu}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}^{\alpha}\Bigr).

Taking
\alpha=0
in

makes the exponent

and sets the misspecification factor to
1

\big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big\|_{2,\mu}
.
For

is small. As

(provided, e.g.,
\hat{w}_{\mu}<2w_{\mu}
) the dominant
contribution becomes

revealing a double-robust structure: the term vanishes if either the weight
estimator is accurate or the Bellman projection error is small. Intuitively, under (approximate) Bellman completeness the Bellman projection is
essentially invariant to the choice of weighting, so errors in the weights have
little effect on the resulting projection.

For
s

(

.
For classes with VC-subgraph dimension

(

.
For Hölder or Sobolev classes of smoothness

.
For multivariate functions of bounded Hardy–Krause variation, the rate

Theorem 2

(Estimation Error for Stationary-Weighted FQE)

Assume

\displaystyle\|\hat{Q}^{(K)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\leq

\displaystyle\gamma^{K}\,\|\hat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}+\;\frac{C}{1-\gamma}\Bigl(\delta_{n}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\log(K/\eta)}{n}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{C}{1-\gamma}\Bigl(\Bigl\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Bigr\|_{2,\mu}^{\tfrac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\Bigl\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Bigr\|_{2,\mu}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Bell}}^{\alpha}\Bigr).

Theorem
2
provides Stationary-Weighted FQE with an analogue
of the classical sup-norm bounds of
44

stationary

geometry and without requiring Bellman completeness.
The structure of the bound closely parallels the classical result and differs
only through the density-ratio term

\|\hat{w}_{\mu}/w_{\mu}-1\|_{2,\mu}/(1-\gamma)
in the worst case
(obtained by taking

and absorbing

into the constant). When

shows that

approximate Bellman completeness.

Following

;

extends to general function approximators by

application of Lemma

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
, one may analyze contraction on the convex hull of

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
(or set
\mathcal{F}=L^{2}(\mu)
) and then bound the
resulting approximation error in the per-iteration regret analysis.

Discussion.

Classical analyses of fitted value and
Q
-evaluation, most prominently

44
, control the iterates in the behavior (sampling) norm,
under which
\mathcal{T}
need not be contractive; moreover, the Bellman target
may lie outside the function class, introducing misspecification error at every
iteration. These per-iteration errors can accumulate geometrically, yielding
bounds of the form
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\gamma^{K-1-k}\epsilon_{k}
. When

\mathcal{T}Q\notin\mathcal{F}
, one typically has

\epsilon_{k}\approx\epsilon_{k-1}+c
, giving

\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\gamma^{K-1-k}k\asymp(1-\gamma)^{-2}
and, in the worst
case, quadratic or exponential error blow-up

(
18
;
23
;
73
;
6
)
.

In contrast, stationary-weighted FQE performs regression in the stationary

L^{2}(\mu)
geometry, where
\mathcal{T}
is a
\gamma
-contraction. This
restores an inexact Picard structure: statistical and density-ratio errors
enter additively and incur only a
(1-\gamma)^{-1}
factor, with no geometric
error accumulation. Theorem
1
further shows that
the approximation error of
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
scales as
(1-\gamma)^{-1}

relative to the best projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}
.

The weighting requirement itself is minimal. The weighted stationary
orthogonality condition in
C3
is strictly weaker than Bellman
completeness—under realizability the residual

\mathcal{T}Q-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}Q
vanishes, making any weighting
admissible, while under stationary–behavior overlap the stationary density
ratio satisfies the condition automatically. When completeness fails, stationary
weighting restores contraction in the stationary norm, thereby preventing the
instability caused by the norm mismatch.

Weighted bellman projection orthogonality is also weaker than the assumptions required
by minimax/DICE-style approaches for
Q
-function estimation

(
60
;
61
;
62
)
, which
typically require (i) discounted-occupancy coverage and (ii) strong structural
conditions such as realizability or dual representability. Full stationary
overlap parallels discounted-occupancy coverage in long-horizon settings, since
discounted occupancies converge to the stationary distribution as

\gamma\to 1
under standard ergodicity
(
43
)
. In contrast,
our guarantees require neither primal nor dual realizability.

Contraction under approximate weights.

Even when the estimated stationary weights
\hat{w}_{\mu}
are inconsistent, the
resulting update can remain stable. Reweighting replaces the regression norm
with
L^{2}(\hat{\mu})
, where
\hat{\mu}:=\nu_{b}\hat{w}_{\mu}
, under which the
Bellman operator need not contract at rate
\gamma
. Nevertheless, even coarse
or misspecified weights can induce a geometry that is
strictly more
contractive
than the behavior norm used by standard FQE.

When completeness fails, approximate weights satisfying the multiplicative
accuracy condition

(1-\varepsilon)\,w_{\mu}(s,a)\;\leq\;\hat{w}_{\mu}(s,a)\;\leq\;(1+\varepsilon)\,w_{\mu}(s,a)

ensure that the
L^{2}(\mu)
and
L^{2}(\hat{\mu})
norms remain equivalent. In this
case,
\mathcal{T}
is still a
\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}
-contraction in

L^{2}(\hat{\mu})
with

\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\gamma\,\sqrt{(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)}
.
Thus, small multiplicative errors preserve contraction, while larger errors
recover the norm-mismatch behavior of standard FQE. Roughly accurate weights
can therefore substantially improve stability even when
\hat{w}_{\mu}
is far
from consistent.

On-policy stability and ergodic limits.

Setting
\hat{w}_{\mu}=1
shows that Theorem
2
recovers
the classical on-policy stability of FQE: when data are drawn from (or
close to) the stationary distribution of
(\pi,P)
, the regression norm
matches
L^{2}(\mu)
and the update contracts toward
Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
.
The effect of sampling mismatch is captured entirely by the density-ratio
term
\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\|_{2,\mu}
, yielding graceful
degradation at a rate governed by the
\chi^{2}
-divergence between the
behavior and stationary distributions. In particular, for an ergodic
Markov chain the behavior distribution converges geometrically to
\mu
at
a rate determined by the spectral gap

(
38
;
43
)
, so standard unweighted FQE
eventually becomes self-stabilizing as the norm mismatch vanishes.

4.4
Reward Misspecification Error

Reward misspecification is common when rewards must be estimated from behavior, especially in robotics and simulator-based control

(
1
;
35
;
10
;
25
)
,
and in policy-evaluation settings for inverse reinforcement learning

(
55
;
49
;
50
;
77
;
22
;
32
;
65
)
.

As a concrete example, suppose the transition kernel
P
—and therefore the stationary
distribution
\mu
of
\pi P
—is known. This setting occurs when transitions
can be queried or generated directly, as in many simulator-based environments.
In this case we may independently sample

(\tilde{S}_{i},\tilde{A}_{i},\tilde{S}_{i}^{\prime})\sim\mu\otimes P
directly, eliminating
the need for stationary reweighting.
Let
\hat{r}(s,a)
be an estimated reward function obtained from the behavior
data
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Running FQE on the dataset

\{(\tilde{S}_{i},\tilde{A}_{i},\hat{r}(\tilde{S}_{i},\tilde{A}_{i}),\tilde{S}_{i}^{\prime})\}_{i=1}^{n}

is then equivalent to applying Algorithm
1
with
w_{\mu}\equiv 1
.
We analyze the misspecification error of the resulting estimator
\hat{Q}^{(K)}
.

Define the reward-estimated Bellman operator

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\mathcal{T}},\qquad\hat{\mathcal{T}}Q:=\hat{r}+\gamma\,\pi PQ.

Let
\hat{Q}^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}
denote the fixed point of

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}
, which is the implicit target of the
estimator
\hat{Q}^{(K)}
.

By the triangle inequality,

\|\hat{Q}^{(K)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\|\hat{Q}^{(K)}-\hat{Q}^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\|\hat{Q}^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}.

A high-probability bound for the first term follows directly from
Theorem
2
. The second term reflects the
misspecification error induced by reward estimation; the next theorem
bounds this quantity.

Theorem 3

(Reward Misspecification)
.

Under
C1
-
C2
,

\|\hat{Q}^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\|\hat{r}-r\|_{2,\mu}.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
is a linear subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)
, then

\|\hat{Q}^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r)\|_{2,\mu}.

For linear classes
\mathcal{F}
, only the component
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r)
of the reward
error lying in
\mathcal{F}
affects the fixed point. Thus, for
linear models, the reward estimator
\hat{r}
may be trained in the same class

\mathcal{F}
as FQE—provided the regression is stationary-weighted—without
requiring reward realizability.

6
Conclusion

We identify a simple but underemphasized source of instability in fitted

Q
-evaluation: standard FQE minimizes Bellman error under the
behavior

distribution, whereas the policy-evaluation Bellman operator is contractive
only in the
stationary
distribution of the target policy. This norm
mismatch breaks the fixed-point geometry underlying dynamic programming and
allows misspecification errors to accumulate across iterations.

A single modification resolves this issue. Reweighting each regression step by
the stationary density ratio aligns the empirical loss with the contraction
geometry of the evaluation operator. The resulting update behaves as an inexact
Picard iteration of the projected Bellman operator in
L^{2}(\mu)
and yields
stable iterates
without
requiring realizability, Bellman completeness, or
adversarial critics. Even coarse ratio estimates are beneficial, as any
approximate weighting that shifts the regression toward the stationary measure
improves stability.

It is also natural to ask how stationary weighting extends to the control
setting. Unlike policy evaluation, where both the Bellman operator and its
stationary contraction geometry are fixed, control involves nonlinear Bellman
optimality operators and an unknown optimal policy. In a companion
paper
67
, we show that an iterative form of stationary
weighting extends to this setting, yielding local linear convergence for soft
fitted
Q
-iteration.

Overall, stationary-weighted FQE provides a simple, plug-in refinement that
stabilizes fitted value methods under misspecification and highlights the
central role of geometry in value-function approximation.

Appendix B
Fixed-Point Estimation of Stationary Density Ratios

The stationary ratio
w_{\mu}=d\mu/d\nu_{b}
is identified by the
fixed-point equation implied by stationarity
\mu=\mu P_{\pi}
:

w_{\mu}(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(\mathcal{K}w_{\mu})(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}),

where the linear operator
\mathcal{K}
is

(\mathcal{K}g)(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})=\frac{\pi(a^{\prime}\mid s^{\prime})}{\mu_{b}(a^{\prime}\mid s^{\prime})}\,\mathbb{E}_{(S,A)\sim\nu_{b}}\!\left[g(S,A)\,k(S,A,s^{\prime})\right],

and

k(s,a,s^{\prime})=\frac{P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)}{\rho_{b,\mathrm{next}}(s^{\prime})},\qquad\rho_{b,\mathrm{next}}(s^{\prime}):=\int P(s^{\prime}\mid s,a)\,\nu_{b}(ds,da)

is the transition density ratio, with
\rho_{b,\mathrm{next}}
the marginal
density of
S^{\prime}
induced by the behavior dynamics.
The quantity
k
can be estimated directly using the empirical squared-loss
objective of
31
:

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k(S_{i},A_{i},S_{j}^{\prime})^{2}\;-\;\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k(S_{i},A_{i},S_{i}^{\prime}).

However,
\mathcal{K}
is the adjoint of
\pi P
in
L^{2}(\mu)
and satisfies

\|\mathcal{K}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=1
, so direct Picard iteration of

w_{\mu}=\mathcal{K}w_{\mu}
is not contractive. A standard workaround is the
discounted resolvent equation

w_{\gamma^{\prime}}=(1-\gamma^{\prime})\tfrac{\pi}{\mu_{b}}+\gamma^{\prime}\,\mathcal{K}w_{\gamma^{\prime}},

whose solution
w_{\gamma^{\prime}}
is the
\gamma^{\prime}
-discounted occupancy ratio.
The resolvent replaces exact stationarity by a
\gamma^{\prime}
-discounted flow
balance, which yields a
\gamma^{\prime}
-contractive update. Since
\mathcal{K}
is
nonexpansive in
L^{2}(\mu)
, Picard iteration applies and

w_{\gamma^{\prime}}
can be computed analogously to fitted value iteration.

Under standard ergodicity assumptions—irreducibility, aperiodicity, and geometric
mixing—the discounted ratios satisfy
w_{\gamma^{\prime}}\to w_{\mu}
as
\gamma^{\prime}\uparrow 1

(
43
;
28
)
. The resolvent formulation induces a
familiar bias–variance trade-off: smaller
1-\gamma^{\prime}
improves numerical
stability but introduces bias of order
O(1-\gamma^{\prime})
, whereas letting

\gamma^{\prime}\uparrow 1
removes the bias at the expense of variance scaling like

(1-\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}
due to the effective horizon of the discounted occupancy
measure.

Contraction and Error Propagation Results

C.1
Contraction Under Stationary Measures

Lemma 4

(Bellman contraction for
Q
-functions under a stationary measure)
.

If
\mu
is stationary for
\pi P
, then
\pi P
is nonexpansive in
L^{2}(\mu)
:

\|\pi P(Q_{1}-Q_{2})\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}.

Therefore the Bellman operator
\mathcal{T}
is a
\gamma
-contraction:

\|\mathcal{T}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}.

Proof.

Let
h:=Q_{1}-Q_{2}
.
For any
(s,a)
, Jensen’s inequality gives

\bigl(\pi Ph\bigr)(s,a)^{2}\;\leq\;\bigl(\pi P(h^{2})\bigr)(s,a),

since
\pi P
is a conditional expectation operator.

Integrating with respect to
\mu
and using stationarity

\mu=\mu(\pi P)
,

\|\pi Ph\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\int(\pi Ph)^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int\pi P(h^{2})\,d\mu=\int h^{2}\,d\mu=\|h\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

Thus
\pi P
is nonexpansive:

\|\pi Ph\|_{2,\mu}\leq\|h\|_{2,\mu}.

For the Bellman operator,

\mathcal{T}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}Q_{2}=\gamma\,\pi P(Q_{1}-Q_{2})=\gamma\,\pi Ph.

Taking norms and applying nonexpansiveness proves

\|\mathcal{T}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\gamma\|h\|_{2,\mu},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5

(Contraction of the projected Bellman operator)
.

If
\mu
is stationary for
\pi P
, then
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\gamma
-contraction on
L^{2}(\mu)
; that is, for all
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in L^{2}(\mu)

\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}\,\leq\,\gamma\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4
,

\|\mathcal{T}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}\leq\gamma\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}.

Since
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
is the
L^{2}(\mu)
orthogonal projection onto a closed
convex set, it is nonexpansive:

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}f-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}g\|_{2,\mu}\leq\|f-g\|_{2,\mu}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{T}Q_{1})-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{T}Q_{2})\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}Q_{1}-\mathcal{T}Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\,\|Q_{1}-Q_{2}\|_{2,\mu}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
1
.

The first claim follows directly from Lemma
4
.
The second claim follows from Lemma
5
.

C.2
Misspecification error bound

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Write

Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}=\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}\bigr)+\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}-\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr).

Taking
L^{2}(\mu)
norms and applying the triangle inequality

\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}-\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}.

By the contraction property of Lemma
5
,

\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu},

and by definition of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}Q^{\star}-\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}-\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}=-(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}.

Combining these two displays yields

\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\gamma\,\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr\|_{2,\mu}.

Rearranging, we obtain

(1-\gamma)\,\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr\|_{2,\mu},

which proves the first inequality:

\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr\|_{2,\mu}.

For the second inequality, note that

\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}
is the best
L^{2}(\mu)

approximation to
\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}
in
\mathcal{F}
, so

\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr\|_{2,\mu}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}-f\|_{2,\mu}.

Since
Q^{\star}
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
, we have

\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}=Q^{\star}
, and hence

\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}})\,\mathcal{T}Q^{\star}\bigr\|_{2,\mu}=\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|Q^{\star}-f\|_{2,\mu}.

Substituting this into the previous display yields

\|Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu},

which completes the proof.
∎

C.3
Approximate Q Iteration and Error Propagation

We now prove the error–propagation bound for approximate
Q iteration with
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
under the stationary measure.
It is the natural analogue of the classical perturbed Picard iteration
inequality for inexact fixed-point iterations of a contraction mapping.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

By Lemma
5
,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a

\gamma
-contraction on
L^{2}(\mu)
. Let

a_{k}\;:=\;\|\hat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu},\qquad k\geq 0,

with
\hat{Q}^{(0)}=\hat{Q}
. For any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{Q}^{(k)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{Q}^{(k)}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{(k-1)})\|_{2,\mu}+\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{(k-1)})-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{k}+\gamma\,\|\hat{Q}^{(k-1)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}=\eta_{k}+\gamma\,a_{k-1},

where we used the assumed approximation bound in the first term, the fixed-point

in the second line,

Thus the sequence

Unrolling this recursion yields

(2)

Lemma 6

(Local maximal inequality)
.

\displaystyle P_{0}\Big[\hat{w}_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\Big]

\displaystyle=P_{0}\Big[w_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\bigr)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+P_{0}\Big[(\hat{w}_{\mu}-w_{\mu})\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\Big].

The first-order optimality conditions for
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})

as a population minimizer (Lemma
8
) imply, for all

\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}}\in\mathcal{F}
,

P_{0}\big[w_{\mu}\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\bigr)\,\{\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})\}\big]\;\geq\;0.

Thus

\displaystyle P_{0}\Big[w_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\bigr)\Big]

\displaystyle=P_{0}\Big[w_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\bigr)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+P_{0}\Big[w_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\bigl(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})\bigr)\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}^{2}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle P_{0}\big[(\hat{w}_{\mu}-w_{\mu})\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle\quad=P_{0}\big[w_{\mu}\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle\quad\geq-\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\Big\|_{2,\mu}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}},

where
\|f\|_{2,w_{\mu}}^{2}:=P_{0}\big[w_{\mu}f^{2}\big]
.

Combining the above, we obtain the local quadratic lower bound

\displaystyle P_{0}\big[\hat{w}_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}.

Next, applying this lower bound to (
3
) and rearranging,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq(P_{0}-P_{n})\big[\hat{w}_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle+\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}.

The empirical process term satisfies

\displaystyle(P_{0}-P_{n})\big[\hat{w}_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle=(P_{0}-P_{n})\big[w_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

\displaystyle+(P_{0}-P_{n})\big[(\hat{w}_{\mu}-w_{\mu})\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]

We now apply Lemma
9
to each term on the
right-hand side with
w=w_{\mu}
and

w=\hat{w}_{\mu}-w_{\mu}
, both of which are uniformly bounded
by assumption. We use that

\|w_{\mu}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\|\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}

by boundedness of
w_{\mu}
. We also have the loose bound:

\displaystyle\big\|(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1)(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\min\Bigl\{\big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big\|_{\infty}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}},

\displaystyle\hskip 65.00009pt\big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{\infty}\Bigr\}.

We use that
\big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big\|_{\infty}<\infty
by
Assumption
C4
. Applying the lemma twice yields a constant

C>0
such that, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least

1-\eta
,

\displaystyle\left|(P_{0}-P_{n})\big[\hat{w}_{\mu}\,(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\,\{R+\gamma(\pi\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\}\big]\right|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C\Bigl(\delta_{n}^{2}+\delta_{n}\,\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\;C\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt{\log(1/\eta)}\,\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}}{\sqrt{n}}+\;\frac{\log(1/\eta)}{n}\Biggr).

Combining the high-probability bound above with (

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}^{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\leq\;C\Bigl(\delta_{n}^{2}+\delta_{n}\,\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\;\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n})\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\,\big\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}.

The inequality above implies that, with probability at least

\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\;\leq\;C\Biggl(\delta_{n}+\Big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\eta)}{n}}\Biggr),

for some constant

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\big)\big(\mathcal{T}(\hat{Q}^{\mathrm{init}})-\hat{Q}_{n}\big)\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\;\lesssim\;\Big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Big\|_{2,w_{\mu}}\,

Proof of Theorem

\varepsilon_{k}:=C\Biggl(\delta_{n}+\Big\|\tfrac{\hat{w}_{\mu}}{w_{\mu}}-1\Big\|_{2,\mu}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(K/\eta)}{n}}\Biggr),

\|\hat{Q}^{(k)}-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{(k-1)})\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{k}\qquad\text{for all }k\in[K].

2

\|\hat{Q}^{(K)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\gamma^{K}\,\|\hat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\sum_{j=1}^{K}\gamma^{\,K-j}\,\varepsilon_{j}.

\varepsilon_{j}
does not depend on
j
, write
\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon
for all

. Hence,

\|\hat{Q}^{(K)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\gamma^{K}\,\|\hat{Q}^{(0)}-Q^{\star}_{\mathcal{F}}\|_{2,\mu}\;+\;\varepsilon\,\frac{1-\gamma^{K}}{1-\gamma}.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By Lemma
5
and stationarity of
\mu
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\gamma
-contraction on
L^{2}(\mu)
.

By definition of the fixed points,

Q^{\star}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star}),\qquad\hat{Q}^{\star}=\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star}).

Write

\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}=\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star}),

and add and subtract
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})
:

\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}=\underbrace{\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})\bigr)}_{(I)}\;+\;\underbrace{\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})\bigr)}_{(II)}.

Taking
L^{2}(\mu)
-norms and using the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})\|_{2,\mu}.

The second term is controlled by the contraction property:

\|\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q^{\star})\|_{2,\mu}\leq\gamma\,\|\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}.

Hence

(1-\gamma)\,\|\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})\|_{2,\mu}.

(8)

Next, observe that for any
Q
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}\qquad\text{(nonexpansiveness of $\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}$)}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{r}-r\|_{2,\mu},

since
\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}(Q)=\hat{r}-r
does not depend on
Q
.
Substituting into (
8
) yields

\|\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\|\hat{r}-r\|_{2,\mu}.

Stronger guarantee under linear function classes.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a linear subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)
, then

\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}
is a linear orthogonal projection, and we obtain a sharper
reward misspecification bound. In this case,
for any
Q
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{T}(Q)\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}(Q)\bigr)\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r)\|_{2,\mu},

since
\hat{\mathcal{T}}(Q)-\mathcal{T}(Q)=\hat{r}-r
does not depend on
Q
.
In particular,

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r),

so

\|\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{Q}^{\star})\|_{2,\mu}=\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r)\|_{2,\mu}.

Substituting this into (
8
) yields

\|\hat{Q}^{\star}-Q^{\star}\|_{2,\mu}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\|\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\hat{r}-r)\|_{2,\mu},

so only the component of the reward error lying in
\mathcal{F}
affects
the fixed point.

∎

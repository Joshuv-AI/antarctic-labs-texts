Hall-on-Toric: Descendant Laughlin state in the chiral ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} toric code
Robin Schäfer, Claudio Chamon, Chris R. Laumann

Abstract
We demonstrate that the chiral ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} toric code – the quintessential model of topological order — hosts additional, emergent topological phases when perturbed: descendant fractional quantum Hall-like states, which we term Hall-on-Toric . These hierarchical states feature fractionalized ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} charges and increased topological ground-state degeneracy. The Hall-on-Toric phases appear in the vicinity of the transitions between deconfined ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} phases with different background charge per unit cell, in a fixed non-trivial flux background. We confirm their existence through extensive infinite density matrix renormalization group (iDMRG) simulations, analyzing the topological entanglement entropy, entanglement spectra, and a generalized Hall conductance. Remarkably, the Hall-on-Toric states remain robust even in the absence of U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) symmetry. Our findings reinforce the foundational interpretation of star and plaquette defects as magnetic and electric excitations, and reveal that this perspective extends to a much deeper level.

In what sense are the emergent electric charges in a toric code like familiar electrons? For example, in a magnetic field due to a background of emergent magnetic charges, would they undergo cyclotron motion? If one could engineer a large “density” of the charges, would further interactions then produce quantum Hall physics?

Here, we answer these questions in the affirmative using a surprisingly simple canonical model for topological phases — the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
toric code illustrated in

A major distinction between the Hall-on-Toric states and the usual fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states

Conclusion.

We have demonstrated that the chiral toric code in a small field hosts hierarchical topological states in which the usual deconfined charges form a Laughlin-like Hall state of their own.
We refer to these as Hall-on-Toric states.
Our understanding is grounded in a perturbative mapping between the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
model and the — number-conserving — Bosonic Hofstadter problem.
We confirm that the Hall-on-Toric states remain stable even without particle conservation.

Our results are quite surprising, as the Hall-on-Toric state arises in such a small perturbation of a well-known exactly solvable model.
On the other hand, this means that the Hall-on-Toric states we find are within sight of well-known bosonic Laughlin states, in which charge non-conservation is only a weak correction to spectral properties.
It would be very interesting to find “strong” Hall-on-Toric states which have no analogues in the conventional Hall hierarchies.
Such states would presumably take advantage of the modular nature of number conservation in a fundamental way, similarly to the parafermionic states discovered in the context of superconducting proximity effects
Read and Rezayi 1999
;
Xia et al. 2004
;
Nayak et al. 2008
.
We leave the search for other Hall-on-Toric states, either weak or strong, to future investigations.

The generalized
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
Pauli operators satisfy the clock algebra,

\displaystyle XZ=\omega ZX=e^{2\pi i/p}ZX

(20)

and the physical Hilbert space is
\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{p})^{\otimes N_{e}}
with
N_{e}
the number of edges.
Here, the boundary
\partial
and coboundary
\delta
operators are oriented and we define
X_{-e}=X^{\dagger}_{e}
and similarly for
Z
.
The orientation only matters for
p>2
.
We note that
X_{e},Z_{e},A_{s},B_{p}
are all unitary but, for
p>2
, not Hermitian (rather they are
p
’th roots of the identity).

For
t_{e}=0
, we have that both
[A_{s},H_{\mathrm{TC}}]=0
and
[B_{p},H_{\mathrm{TC}}]=0
for all
s,p
.
For
t_{e}>0
, the plaquette operators still commute with
H_{\mathrm{TC}}
, but not the star operators.
Assuming that
\Gamma_{m}
is large enough, the ground state should live in the
B_{p}
sector with
B_{p}=\omega^{g}
for some
g
which minimizes the
B_{p}
terms.
Furthermore, the Wilson loops
W_{Z}[\ell]=\prod_{e\in\ell}Z_{e}
for non-trivial cycles
\ell
are also conserved and take values among the powers of
\omega
.

To make further progress, we extend the Hilbert space to have separate gauge and matter fields by introducing additional
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
clock degrees of freedom
C_{s}
on the sites of the lattice with conjugate “rotating” operators
R_{s}
:

\displaystyle RC=\omega CR\,.

(21)

We impose the constraint that
C_{s}=A_{s}
, or, equivalently, that
G_{s}=C_{s}^{\dagger}A_{s}\equiv 1
to recover the physical Hilbert space.
In the extended system, we can rewrite the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{e}e^{-i\Phi_{e}}\sum_{s}C_{s}-\Gamma_{m}e^{-i\Phi_{m}}\sum_{p}B_{p}+\text{h.c.}

(22)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-t_{e}\sum_{e}R^{\dagger}_{\partial e_{0}}Z_{e}R^{\,}_{\partial e_{1}}+\text{h.c.}

(23)

where
\partial e_{0}
and
\partial e_{1}
are the sites at the beginning and end of edge
e
.
Recalling the system is on a torus, the global gauge constraint directly constrains the matter variables:

\displaystyle\prod_{s}G_{s}=\prod_{s}C_{s}^{\dagger}\prod_{e\in\delta s}X_{e}=\prod_{s}C_{s}^{\dagger}\equiv 1\,.

(24)

In the second equality, we have observed that every edge appears twice, with opposite orientation in the product, and thus the
X_{e}
operators cancel. To wit, the matter degrees of freedom must be global
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
charge neutral.

This extended representation helps to solve the perturbed model as now
[Z_{e},H_{\mathrm{TC}}]=0
.
We may work in any fixed
Z_{e}=\tau_{e}
sector we like, so long as we apply the gauge projection to obtain physical states.
The
B_{p}
eigenvalues of a flux sector of interest are given by

\displaystyle B_{p}

\displaystyle=\prod_{e\in\partial p}\tau_{e}

(25)

where again
\tau_{-e}=\bar{\tau}_{e}
.
In our numerical computations, we typically work in Landau gauge for a uniform flux background (
g
fluxes per unit cell),

\displaystyle\tau_{\langle ij\rangle}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\langle ij\rangle\textrm{\penalty\ horizontal}\\
\omega^{gi_{x}}&\langle ij\rangle\textrm{\penalty\ vertical}\end{cases}

(26)

where
i_{x}
is the
x
coordinates of site
i
.

In a fixed gauge sector, we are left with the matter Hamiltonian (cf.
Eq. (7)
)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{M}}=-\Gamma_{e}e^{-i\Phi_{e}}\sum_{s}C_{s}-t_{e}\sum_{e}\tau_{e}R^{\dagger}_{\partial e_{0}}R_{\partial e_{1}}+\text{h.c.}

(27)

which has global
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
symmetry:

\displaystyle[\prod_{s}C_{s},H_{\mathrm{M}}]=0

(28)

and which we must solve in the charge neutral sector
\prod_{s}C_{s}=1
.

With periodic boundary conditions, there are in fact
p^{2}
globally distinct gauge sectors of the model with the same background
B_{p}
flux patterns.
These are distinguished by the non-trivial Wilson loop operators
Wilson 1974
,

\displaystyle W_{x/y}

\displaystyle=\prod_{e\in\ell_{x/y}}Z_{e}=\prod_{e\in\ell_{x/y}}\tau_{e}\,

(29)

where
\ell_{x}
and
\ell_{y}
are fixed paths wrapping the
x-
and
y-
directions, respectively.
The eigenvalues of the loop operators are
p
’th roots of unity, which can be used to label the sectors.
In the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-deconfined phase, the
p^{2}
ground states across the sectors correspond to the topological degeneracy of the phase.
Each sector has an additional 2-fold topological ground state degeneracy in the Hall-on-Toric state.

A.2
Global symmetries

Space group—

In terms of the physical toric code variables, we define the unitary action of the square lattice space group
G
in the usual way for an edge field.
To wit, any spatial transformation
g\in G
which maps sites
i\to g(i)
, maps edges
e=\langle ij\rangle\to g(e)=\langle g(i)g(j)\rangle
.
We then define

\displaystyle g:\begin{cases}X_{e}\to X_{g^{-1}(e)}\\
Z_{e}\to Z_{g^{-1}(e)}\\
\end{cases}\,.

(30)

Recall that edges
e=\langle ij\rangle=-\langle ji\rangle
have an orientation and that
X_{e}=X^{-1}_{-e}=X^{\dagger}_{-e}
(and similarly for
Z_{e}
).

Under these transformations,
A_{s}
transforms like a scalar:

\displaystyle g:A_{s}

\displaystyle\to A_{g^{-1}(s)}

\displaystyle\forall g\in G

(31)

which is to say that
g^{-1}(s)
has the same orientation as
s
.
On the other hand, since plaquettes
p
reverse orientation under improper transformations, so too does
B_{p}

\displaystyle\sigma:B_{p}

\displaystyle\to B^{\dagger}_{|g^{-1}(p)|}

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in G\penalty\ \textrm{improper}

(32)

where
|g^{-1}(p)|
is the transformed plaquette but with a positive orientation.
In short, this implies that improper transformation swap
B_{p}
and
B^{\dagger}_{p}
.

Reflection symmetry—

With these definitions, we find that
H_{\mathrm{TC}}
is symmetric under all proper transformations of the square lattice, but that the reflections
P
transform the Hamiltonian by

\displaystyle P:H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},-\Phi_{m})\,.

(33)

There are two immediate consequences: (i)
\Phi_{m}\neq 0,\pi
explicitly breaks reflection symmetry; (ii) the phase diagram of
H_{\mathrm{TC}}
has a symmetry
\Phi_{m}\to-\Phi_{m}
.

Charge conjugation—

We define charge conjugation
C
by mapping the clock (
Z
) basis
|s\rangle\to|-s\penalty\ \textrm{mod}\penalty\ p\rangle
on every edge. This induces

\displaystyle C:\begin{cases}X_{e}\to X_{e}^{-1}\\
Z_{e}\to Z_{e}^{-1}\\
A_{s}\to A_{s}^{-1}\\
B_{p}\to B_{p}^{-1}\end{cases}

(34)

Thus, we obtain another unitary symmetry of the phase diagram:

\displaystyle C:H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{TC}}(-\Phi_{e},-\Phi_{m})

(35)

Or, combined with a reflection
\sigma
:

\displaystyle C\sigma:H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{TC}}(-\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})

(36)

Time-reversal—

We define time-reversal
\mathcal{T}=K
to be complex conjugation with the clock basis
|s\rangle
real.
Accordingly,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:\begin{cases}i\rightarrow-i\\
X_{e}\rightarrow X_{e}\\
Z_{e}\rightarrow Z^{\dagger}_{e}\\
A_{s}\rightarrow A_{s}\\
B_{p}\rightarrow B_{p}^{\dagger}\end{cases}\,.

(37)

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{TC}}(-\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\,.

(38)

Putting all of the discrete transformations together, we find that, no matter the couplings
\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m}
,
H_{\mathrm{TC}}
has
CP\mathcal{T}
symmetry.

Symmetry under global charge/flux shifts—

The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
model has several further useful unitary transformations associated with globally rotating the charge/flux ferris wheels by
2\pi/p
on every site/plaquette.

Let us assume for simplicity that
p|L_{x}
(similar constructions exist so long as
p|L_{y}
).
Consider the unitary

\displaystyle U=\prod_{i_{x}=1}^{L_{x}}\prod_{i_{y}=1}^{L_{y}}Z_{\langle(i_{x},i_{y}),(i_{x}+1,i_{y})\rangle}^{i_{x}}\,.

(39)

This transformation clearly commutes with the plaquette and field terms.
However, it rotates the charge
A_{s}
on every site
s
:

\displaystyle U:A_{s}\to\omega^{-1}A_{s}

(40)

Thus,

\displaystyle U:H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e},\Phi_{m})\to H_{\mathrm{TC}}(\Phi_{e}+2\pi/p,\Phi_{m})

(41)

which induces a periodicity in the phase diagram of
H_{\mathrm{TC}}
.

A similar construction permits us to shift the flux
B_{p}
globally.
The corresponding unitary is

\displaystyle V=\prod_{i_{x}=1}^{L_{x}}\prod_{i_{y}=1}^{L_{y}}X_{\langle(i_{x},i_{y}),(i_{x},i_{y}+1)\rangle}^{i_{x}}\,

(42)

which transforms

\displaystyle V:B_{p}\to\omega^{-1}B_{p}\,.

(43)

Unlike the charge shifting operator
U
, the
V
operator also modifies the field term proportional to
t_{e}
.
Thus, the unperturbed phase diagram (
Fig. 2
in the main text) is periodic in both
\Phi_{e}
(by
U
) and
\Phi_{m}
(by
V
) by
2\pi/p
, but when
t_{e}\neq 0
, the shift in
\Phi_{m}
need not be a symmetry of the phase diagram.
Indeed, the Hall-on-Toric states do not appear in the absence of background flux (e.g., near
\Phi_{m}=0
).

Weights for different charge channels in the

and
L_{y}=2p
. The adiabatic evolution from
\Theta_{y}=0
to
\Theta_{y}=4\pi
was performed using 201 steps for

\chi=1024
. In the

We generalize the charge pumping
Laughlin 1981
for fractional quantum Hall states to the non-number-conserving matter model. The ground state is represented as an infinite matrix product state, and the Schmidt values at a bipartition can be organized according to the underlying symmetries of the system. In the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
case, the defining symmetry corresponds to particle number conservation modulo
p
, while it is the
U(1)
symmetry in the conventional setting of fractional quantum Hall states.

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle:=\sum_{c}\sum_{i=1}^{\chi_{c}}\Lambda_{(c,i)}|\psi_{(c,i)}^{L}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{(c,i)}^{R}\rangle\,.

(44)

We have dropped the summation over
k_{y}
. Here,
c
refers to the charge difference between the two sides of the cylinder.
c
can take any integer in the
U(1)
case and is restricted to integers modulo
p
for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
case:
\{0,...,p-1\}
. Note that additional charges can be placed on one side of the infinite cylinder such
that the weights can be shifted.

We define the weight associated with each charge channel by:

\displaystyle W_{c}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\chi_{c}}\Lambda_{(c,i)}^{2}\,.

(45)

The sum over all the weights is one due to the normalization of the wavefunction:

\displaystyle\sum_{c}W_{c}=\innerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}=1\,.

(46)

Starting from the ground state of the respective models, we adiabatically thread
\Theta_{y}
through the periodic direction. The flux is inserted via the vertical edges of the lattice. In the
U(1)
case, we simply fix the particle number to match
\nu=1/2
filling and compute the ground state of the hardcore Boson model. In the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
case, we select parameters that place the system within the Hall-on-Toric phase.

In the number-conserving model, weights associated with each charge sector undergo a uniform shift during the adiabatic evolution:

\displaystyle W_{c}(\Theta_{y}=0)=W_{c+1}(\Theta_{y}=4\pi)\,.

(47)

Hence, the pumping can be understood as a one-dimensional, discrete transport process. It is illustrated in
Fig. 9
(a)
, which displays a subset of weights and their evolution after threading
\Theta_{y}=4\pi
through the system. For example, the green weight initially associated with
W_{-2}
at
\Theta_{y}=0
shifts to
W_{-1}
at
\Theta_{y}=4\pi
.

In the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
case, we find that weights are shifted modulo
p
:

\displaystyle W_{c}(\Theta_{y}=0)=W_{c+1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p}(\Theta_{y}=4\pi)\,.

(48)

This behavior is illustrated in
Fig. 9
(b)
for
p=5
. The weight associated with
W_{0}
(yellow) shifts to
W_{1}
after threading
\Theta_{y}=4\pi
. However, in contrast to the
U(1)
case, the weight of
W_{4}
(dark blue) wraps around and shifts to
W_{0}
.

We show numerical values of the weights for both models in
Table 1
. Starting from the ground state, we adiabatically evolve the system up to
\Theta_{y}=4\pi
. To probe the uniform shift in the
U(1)
case,
Eq. (47)
, and a cyclic shift in the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
case,
Eq. (48)
, we compute the following quantities:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{c}\left(W_{c}(0)-W_{c+1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p}(4\pi)\right)^{2}}\,.

(49)

\displaystyle\Delta_{U(1)}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sum_{c}\left(W_{c}(0)-W_{c+1}(4\pi)\right)^{2}}\,.

(50)

In both models, we observe excellent agreement:
\Delta_{U(1)}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
vanish within numerical accuracy for
p=4,5,6

U(1)
model, we observed that the weights are concentrated within
p
consecutive charge sectors (values below
10^{-4}
have been discarded in
Table 1
). Moreover, the numerical values of the weights for both models closely match within this threshold, suggesting that they share the same ground state structure.

1.
Introduction

Stiefel-Whitney Classes (SWCs) are interesting natural cohomological invariants of orthogonal representations. In this paper we present a formula for SWCs for the finite symplectic groups in odd characteristic.

This paper is part of a project to understand SWCs for finite groups of Lie type. Let
q
be an odd prime power throughout. A formula determining SWCs for
G=\GL(n,q)
was discovered in
[
8
]
, for
G=\SL(2,q)
in
[
15
]
and for
G=\SL(2n+1,q)
in
[
16
]
. In this paper we find similar expressions when
G=\Sp(2n,q)
.

Write
\hc^{*}(G)
for the mod
2
cohomology
\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and
\hc^{*}_{\SW}(G)
for the subalgebra of
\hc^{*}(G)
generated by SWCs of orthogonal representations. The diagonal matrices in
G
with eigenvalues
\pm 1
form a subgroup we denote by
Z_{X}
. (It is the center of another subgroup
X
which we will encounter later.) The mod
2
cohomology of
Z_{X}
is a polynomial algebra:

\hc^{*}(Z_{X})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}],

where each
v_{i}
is the first SWC of a certain linear character of
Z_{X}
; in particular it has degree 1.
Let
\mathfrak{e}_{i}=v_{i}^{4}
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{k}
be the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial in the
\mathfrak{e}_{i}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The restriction map
\hc^{*}_{\SW}(G)\to\hc^{*}(Z_{X})
is injective, and its image is contained in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n}]
.

Since no information is lost by restriction, we will express SWCs in terms of the
\mathcal{E}_{k}
. For
0\leq i\leq n
, let
g_{i}\in Z_{X}
with
-1
having multiplicity
2i
as an eigenvalue, and
1
having multiplicity
2(n-i)
. For example
g_{0}=\mathbbm{1}
, the identity matrix. Given a representation
\pi
, write
\chi_{\pi}
for its character. Here is our “universal” formula for the 4th and 8th SWCs:

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\pi
is an orthogonal representation of
\Sp(2n,q)
, then

w_{4}(\pi)=\frac{1}{8}\left(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(g_{1})\right)\mathcal{E}_{1}\quad\quad\quad\text{ for all $n$,}\\

and

w_{8}(\pi)=r_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}+\left(\binom{r_{1}}{2}+\binom{r_{2}}{2}\right)\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\quad\text{ for $n\geq 2$}.\\

Here

r_{1}=\frac{1}{16}(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(g_{2}))

and

r_{2}=\frac{1}{16}(\deg\pi-2\chi_{\pi}(g_{1})+\chi_{\pi}(g_{2})).

A general formula for the total SWCs
w(\pi)
is found in Theorem
3.6
, although it takes some computation to extract individual SWCs
w_{k}(\pi)
from this. For example:

Theorem 1.3
.

The total SWC of an orthogonal representation
\pi
of
\Sp(4,q)
is

w(\pi)=((1+\mathfrak{e}_{1})(1+\mathfrak{e}_{2}))^{r_{\pi}}(1+\mathfrak{e}_{1}+\mathfrak{e}_{2})^{s_{\pi}},

where

\displaystyle r_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\Big(\chi_{\pi}(\mathbbm{1})-\chi_{\pi}(-\mathbbm{1})\Big)\quad\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle s_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\Big(\chi_{\pi}(\mathbbm{1})+\chi_{\pi}(-\mathbbm{1})-2\chi_{\pi}(g_{1})\Big).

We use this theorem to compute:

Corollary 1.3.1
.

The subalgebra

\hc^{*}_{\SW}(\Sp(4,q))\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1}+\mathfrak{e}_{2},\mathfrak{e}_{1}\mathfrak{e}_{2}].

One can in principle produce universal formulas for all
w_{k}
, akin to those of Theorem
1.2
by the following.
Let
G_{n}=\Sp(2n,q)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
m\geq n
. The restriction map
\iota_{n}^{*}:\hc^{i}(G_{m})\to\hc^{i}(G_{n-1})
is injective for
i<4n-1
.

(Compare
[
19
, Corollary 6.7]
and
[
8
, Theorems 3, 8]
.)

A representation
\pi
of a group
G
has a total Chern class
c(\pi)\in\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. Under the coefficient map to
\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
, these map to
w(\pi\oplus\pi^{\vee})
. Hence this “mod
2
Chern class of
\pi
” is computable for
G=\Sp(2n,q)
by our formulas.

The paper is laid out as follows. Preliminaries are reviewed and developed in Section
2
.
Section
3
contains the heart of the paper; we prove Theorem
1.1
, and give the product formula for the total SWC,
as Theorem
3.6
. Some simplifications come when the representation is irreducible, by a formula of Gow.
We develop our formulas for
\Sp(4,q)
and
\Sp(8,q)
in Section
4
, and prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.3.1
. We also illustrate application of our formula by computing the mod
2
Chern class of the Weil representations.
Finally, in Section
5
we establish Theorem
1.4
, and deduce Theorem
1.2
from this.
Certain technical arguments belonging to vector bundle theory are sketched in the Appendix.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

As this paper is a continuation of
[
15
]
, we use the same notations and conventions, which we now review.

2.1.
Representations

Let
G
be a finite group. All the representations
(\pi,V)
considered in this paper (before the Appendix) are complex finite dimensional. Let
\irr(G)
be the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
G
. Write
(\pi^{\vee},V^{\vee})
for the dual representation. If
H
is a subgroup of
G
, write
\res^{G}_{H}\pi
or
\pi|_{H}
for the restriction of
\pi
to
H
. A
linear character

\chi
of
G
is a degree 1 representation. We call
\chi

quadratic
when
\chi^{2}=1
.

We say
\pi
is
orthogonal
(resp.,
symplectic
), provided there exists a non-degenerate
G
-invariant symmetric (resp., antisymmetric) bilinear form
B:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
.
When
\pi
is self-dual and irreducible, it is either orthogonal or symplectic. In this
case, the Frobenius-Schur Indicator
\varepsilon(\pi)
is a sign defined as
1
when
\pi
is orthogonal,
and
-1
when
\pi
is symplectic. Whereas it is
0
, when
\pi
is not self-dual.

One can symmetrize a general
(\pi,V)
by defining
S(\pi):=\pi\oplus\pi^{\vee}
on the vector space
V\oplus V^{\vee}
. Under the symmetric
G
-invariant bilinear map
B
on
V\oplus V^{\vee}
as
B((v,\alpha),(w,\beta))=\langle\alpha,w\rangle+\langle\beta,v\rangle
, the representation
S(\pi)
is orthogonal. We call
S(\pi)
the
symmetrization
of
\pi
. (In
[
20
, Definition 4.3 ]
it is called the
hyperbolic space
on
V
.)

Every orthogonal representation
\Pi
of
G
can be decomposed as

(1)

\Pi\cong\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{j}S(\varphi_{j}),

such that each
\pi_{i}
is irreducible orthogonal and
\varphi_{j}
are irreducible non-orthogonal representations of
G
.

A representation
\pi
of
G
is said to be an
orthogonally irreducible
representation (OIR), provided
\pi
is orthogonal, and can not be decomposed into a direct sum of orthogonal representations. An irreducible representation
\pi
is orthogonally irreducible if and only if
\pi
is orthogonal. Moreover, for
\varphi
irreducible and non-orthogonal, its symmetrization
S(\varphi)
is an OIR.

2.2.
Detection

As in the earlier work, we make use of
detection
. To recall this notion, let
i:H\hookrightarrow G
be a subgroup. We say
H

detects the mod
2
cohomology of

G
, when the restriction map

i^{*}:\hc^{*}(G)\rightarrow\hc^{*}(H)

is injective, and we say
H

detects SWCs
of
G
when the restriction of
i^{*}
to
\hc^{*}_{\SW}(G)
is injective. Often the cohomology of
H
admits an easy description, e.g., when it is polynomial. Then it is convenient to give our formulas there, since no information is lost.
Let
N_{G}(H)
be the normalizer of
H
in
G
, which acts on
H
by conjugation. This induces an action of
N_{G}(H)
on
\hc^{*}(H)
, and generally the image of
i^{*}
is contained in the subalgebra

\hc^{*}(H)^{N_{G}(H)}
fixed under this action.

2.3.
Characteristic Classes

Let
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of degree
d
. Associated to
\pi
are cohomological invariants

w_{i}(\pi)\in\hc^{i}(G)\quad;\quad i=0,1,2,\ldots,d

known as the
i
th
Stiefel-Whitney Class
(SWC) of
\pi
. Their sum
w(\pi)=w_{0}(\pi)+w_{1}(\pi)+\ldots
is called the
total SWC
of
\pi
. We refer the reader to
[
15
, Section 2.3]
for detailed description.

Also, associated to a complex representation
\pi
of
G
are cohomology classes
c_{i}(\pi)\in\hc^{2i}(G,\mathbb{Z}),

called
Chern classes
(CCs). Their sum

c(\pi)=c_{0}(\pi)+c_{1}(\pi)+c_{2}(\pi)+\ldots\in\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z})

is called the
total Chern class
of
\pi
. We have

(2)

w(S(\pi))=\kappa(c(\pi)),

where
\kappa:\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
is the
coefficient homomorphism
of cohomology. (See
[
7
, Lemma 1]
, based on
[
18
, Problem 14-B]
for proof.) So we interpret
w(S(\pi))
as the “mod
2
Chern class” of
\pi
.

For
n,i\geq 0
, there are additive homomorphisms on cohomology, called
Steenrod Squares
,

\Sq^{i}:\hc^{n}(G)\rightarrow\hc^{n+i}(G).

These operations are
functorial
, meaning for a group homomorphism
\varphi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
, we have

\varphi^{*}(\Sq^{i}y)=\Sq^{i}(\varphi^{*}(y))\text{ for all }y\in\hc^{i}(G_{2}).

They satisfy
\Sq^{i}(x)=x\cup x
for
i=\deg(x)
, and
\Sq^{i}(x)=0
for
i>\deg(x)
. There is the
Cartan Formula
: For
x,y\in\hc^{*}(G)
,

(3)

\Sq^{n}(x\cup y)=\sum_{i+j=n}(\Sq^{i}x)\cup(\Sq^{j}y).

The well-known
Wu’s formula
states:

Proposition 2.1

(
[
17
]
, Chapter 23, Section 6)
.

Let
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of
G
. The cohomology class
\Sq^{i}(w_{j}(\pi))
can be expressed as a polynomial in
w_{1}(\pi),\ldots,w_{i+j}(\pi)
:

\Sq^{i}(w_{j}(\pi))=\sum_{t=0}^{i}{j+t-i-1\choose t}w_{i-t}(\pi)w_{j+t}(\pi).

Corollary 2.1.1
.

Suppose
w_{1}(\pi)=w_{2}(\pi)=0
. Then if
w_{i}(\pi)\neq 0
, then
i
is a multiple of
4
.

Proof.

This is clear.
∎

2.4.
Symmetric Functions

The elementary symmetric functions
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbf{x})
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
are defined for
k\leq n
as,

\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{k}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

Whereas for
k>n
, these are defined to be
0
.
For example,
\mathcal{E}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+x_{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}
. Of course,

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+x_{i})=1+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\ldots+\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{x}).

2.5.
Cyclic Groups

Let
k
be even, and
G=C_{k}
the cyclic group of order
k
. Let
g
be a generator of
G
. We write
g^{k/2}=-1
, the unique order
2
element of
G
. We say a linear character
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is
odd
, when
\chi(-1)=-1
, and
even
, when
\chi(-1)=1
.
Let ‘sgn’ denote the unique non-trivial quadratic character of
G
.

It is known
[
13
]
that for
k\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,

\hc^{*}(C_{k})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[v]
where
v=w_{1}(\sgn)
. In this case, (see
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
for instance) for a representation
\pi
,

w(\pi)=(1+v)^{b_{\pi}}

with
b_{\pi}=\cfrac{1}{2}\left(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(-1)\right)
.

Let
C_{2}^{n}
be the
n
-fold product of
C_{2}
, with projection maps
\pr_{i}:C_{2}^{n}\to C_{2}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. By Künneth, we have

(4)

\hc^{*}(C_{2}^{n})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}],

where we put
v_{i}=w_{1}(\sgn\circ\pr_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

2.6.
OIRs of a Direct Product of Groups

Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
be finite groups and
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}
be their direct product.
Given
G_{i}
-representations
(\pi_{i},V_{i})
, one can form their
external tensor product

\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{n}
from the action of the product group
G
on the tensor space
V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}
as,

(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})=g_{1}v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}v_{n}

for
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in G
and
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\in V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{n}.
(See
[
6
, Exercise 2.36, page 24]
.)
If each
\pi_{i}
is irreducible, then so is

\pi
. All irreducible representations of
G
decompose in this manner.
The Frobenius-Schur indicator of
\pi
is given by

(5)

\begin{split}\varepsilon(\pi)&=\varepsilon(\pi_{1})\varepsilon(\pi_{2})\ldots\varepsilon(\pi_{n}).\end{split}

Write
F(\pi)
for the multiset
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\ldots,\pi_{n}\}
. Then from (
5
), we can describe the OIRs of
G
in terms of irreducible representations of
G_{i}
as follows:

(1)

Irreducible orthogonal representations
of the form
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{n},
where

\pi_{i}\in\irr(G_{i})
are self-dual for each
i
and an even number of representations in
F(\pi)
are symplectic.

(2)

Symmetrization of irreducible non-orthogonal representations

\pi=S(\varphi)=S(\varphi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\varphi_{n}),
where
\varphi_{i}\in\irr(G_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and exactly one of the following holds:

(a)

At least one of
\varphi_{i}
is not self-dual.

(b)

Each
\varphi_{i}
is self-dual and there is an odd number of symplectic representations
in
F(\varphi)
.

2.7.
Quaternion Group

Let
Q
be the quaternion group of order 8. There are four linear characters of
Q
, which we denote by
1
,
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}
. Each one is quadratic. The group also possesses a unique irreducible representation
\rho
of degree
2
. It is symplectic.

Let
Q^{n}
be the
n
-fold product of
Q
. From Section
2.6
above, any irreducible representation
\pi
of
Q^{n}
has the form

\pi\cong\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{n}

where each
\pi_{i}\in\irr(Q)
. Since every representation of
Q
is self-dual, the same is true for
Q^{n}
.

Put
I_{\pi}=\{i:\pi_{i}\cong\rho\}
, and
r(\pi)=|I_{\pi}|
.
From (
5
) we obtain:

Lemma 2.2
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
Q^{n}
is orthogonal if and only if
r(\pi)
is even.

Let
Z
be the center of
Q
, which is
\{\pm 1\}
. For a linear character
\theta
of
Z^{n}
, write
[\theta,\pi]
for the multiplicity of
\theta
in
\pi|_{Z^{n}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of
Q^{n}
. Let
\theta
be a non-trivial linear character of
Z^{n}
. Then

[\theta,\pi]
is a multiple of
4
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\pi
is an OIR. Let
r=r(\pi)
.

If
r=0
, then
\pi|_{Z}
is trivial, since
Z
is in the kernel of all linear characters of
Q
.
So take
r>0
.
Define a linear character
\theta_{\pi}
of
Z^{n}
by
\theta_{\pi}=\boxtimes_{i}\theta_{i}
, with

\theta_{i}=\begin{cases}\sgn,&i\in I_{\pi}\\
1&i\notin I_{\pi}.\\
\end{cases}

Then
\pi|_{Z^{n}}\cong 2^{r}\theta_{\pi}
,
since
\rho|_{Z}=\sgn\oplus\sgn
.
If
\pi
is irreducible orthogonal, then
r
is even by Lemma
2.2
and therefore
4
divides
[\theta_{\pi},\pi]
, and other
[\theta,\pi]=0
.

Otherwise
\pi=S(\phi)
for an irreducible symplectic representation
\phi
.
In this case,

[\theta_{\pi},S(\phi)]=2^{r}+2^{r}=2^{r+1},

which is again divisible by
4
for
r>0
.
∎

2.8.
S_{n}
-invariant Representations of Elementary Abelian
2
-groups

Let
E
be an elementary abelian
2
-group of rank
n
. View
E
as an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space, say with basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
. For
e\in E,
put
|e|=\#\{i:c_{i}=1\}
when
e=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}e_{i}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Put
\mathcal{O}_{k}=\{e\in E:|e|=k\}.

Let
E^{\vee}=\Hom(E,\mathbb{F}_{2})
and consider the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
of
E^{\vee}
dual to the
e_{i}
. For
v\in E^{\vee},
put
|v|=\#\{i:c_{i}=1\}
when
v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}v_{i}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
. The representation

\sigma_{k}=\bigoplus_{|v|=k}v\quad;\quad k=0,1,\ldots,n

is
S_{n}
-invariant, of degree
\binom{n}{k}
.
For a polynomial
f
, write
[f]_{i}
for the coefficient of the degree
i
term of
f
; in other words so that
f(x)=\sum_{i}[f]_{i}x^{i}
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
8
]
, Proposition 2)
.

For
e\in\mathcal{O}_{k}
, we have

\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(e)=\left[(1-x)^{k}(1+x)^{n-k}\right]_{i}.

Moreover, any
S_{n}
-invariant representation
\sigma
of
E
is a direct sum of
\sigma_{k}
’s:

(6)

\sigma=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}m_{k}(\sigma)\sigma_{k}

where
m_{k}(\sigma)
are certain non-negative integers.
The coefficients
m_{k}(\sigma)
can be expressed in terms of character values of
\sigma
at the elements of
E
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, put

\vartheta_{k}=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{k}
. (In particular,
\vartheta_{0}=0
.)
Then,

(7)

m_{k}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(\vartheta_{k})\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{i}).

From Lemma
2.4
, the character value
\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(\vartheta_{k})
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in the expression
(1-x)^{k}(1+x)^{n-k}
. Following
[
8
, Theorem 7]
we have

(8)

w(\sigma)=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\prod\limits_{|v|=k}(1+v)\Big)^{m_{k}(\sigma)}.

For later use, we state the following lemma; its proof is immediate.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\sigma
be an
S_{n}
-invariant representation of
E
such that

\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{i})=\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{n-i})
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. Then, we have

m_{k}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ when }k\text{ is odd}\\[5.0pt]
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(\vartheta_{k})\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{i})&\text{ when }k\text{ is even, }n\text{ is odd}\\[15.0pt]
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(\vartheta_{k})\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{i})+\cfrac{1}{2^{n}}\big(\chi_{\sigma_{\frac{n}{2}}}(\vartheta_{k})\chi_{\sigma}(\vartheta_{\frac{n}{2}})\big)&\text{ when }k,n\text{ both are even}\end{cases}

for
1\leq k\leq n.

2.9.
Dickson Invariants

With
E
as the elementary abelian
2
-group above, write
\Sym(E^{\vee})
for the symmetric algebra of
E^{\vee}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
In this algebra, we have an element

\mathcal{D}(E)=\prod_{v\in E^{\vee}}(1+v)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}(E),

for certain
\GL(E)
-invariant polynomials
d_{i}
of degree
2^{n}-2^{n-i}
, known as
Dickson invariants
.
Note that
w(\reg(E))=\mathcal{D}(E)
, where
\reg(E)
is the regular representation (as mentioned in
[
21
]
).

Certain factors of
\mathcal{D}(E)
enter into our work, which we explain here. Consider the basis

{\bm{v}}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

of
E^{\vee}
. Identifying
E^{\vee}
with
\hc^{1}(E)
, we may write

\hc^{*}(E)=\Sym(E^{\vee})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}].

Put

\mathcal{D}^{[k]}({\bm{v}})=\prod_{|v|=k}(1+v)\in\Sym(E^{\vee}),

so that
\mathcal{D}(E)=\prod_{k}\mathcal{D}^{[k]}({\bm{v}})
. Clearly,
\mathcal{D}^{[k]}({\bm{v}})
is a symmetric polynomial. We have

\mathcal{D}^{[n]}({\bm{v}})=1+\mathcal{E}_{1}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\mathcal{D}^{[1]}({\bm{v}})=1+\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2}+\ldots+\mathcal{E}_{n},

For
n=4
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{[2]}({\bm{v}})&=1+\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{3}+(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{3})+(\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{3})+(\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}\mathcal{E}_{3}+\mathcal{E}_{3}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{4})\text{ and }\\

Thus we can rewrite (

Proposition 2.6

For an

-invariant representation

3.1.
Subgroups of

Let
\mathcal{S}=\SL(2,q)
. Let

be the center of
\mathcal{S}
.
Set

0&&0&&\ldots&&-1&&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&\iddots&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&&1&&\ldots&&0&&0\\
-1&&0&&\ldots&&0&&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

and put

Let

\vdots&&&c_{1}&d_{1}&&&\vdots\\
&&c_{2}&&&d_{2}&&\\

c_{n}&0&0&\ldots&\ldots&0&0&d_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix},

meaning the nonzero entries of

lie either on the diagonal or the antidiagonal.
Let

be the center of

; it is the subgroup of diagonal matrices in

which have

or
-1
on the diagonal.

Note that each

maps

isomorphically to the

\mathcal{S}^{n}
. It also maps

to

.

Let

be the subgroup of diagonal

block matrices of

; then

\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\

maps

isomorphically to
\GL(n,q)
. The subgroup of

permutation matrices in

thus gives a copy of

in

; this subgroup normalizes

by the given permutation.

3.2.
Detecting SWCs

In this Section, we prove Theorem

is a detecting subgroup of

, and that

detects the SWCs of

. Along the way, we must determine

It is well known (see

\hc^{*}(\mathcal{S})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}]\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{b}]/\langle\mathfrak{b}^{2}\rangle

with
\deg(\mathfrak{b})=3
,

In
[

, we specified an orthogonal representation

of

with the property that

.
We also studied the restriction map

and computed that

maps to

and leads to:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

The center
Z
detects SWCs of
\mathcal{S}
. We have
\hc^{*}_{\SW}(\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}]

From above, consider the subgroup

of

, isomorphic to

.
There are projections

\hc^{*}(X)\cong\hc^{*}(\mathcal{S}^{n})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n}]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{b}_{n}]/(\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{2},\ldots,\mathfrak{b}_{n}^{2}),

where
\mathfrak{e}_{j}=\text{pr}^{*}_{j}(\mathfrak{e})
and
\mathfrak{b}_{j}=\text{pr}^{*}_{j}(\mathfrak{b})

\mathfrak{e}_{j}=w_{4}(\eta_{j})
with

for each

Lemma 3.2

(
[
1
]
, Chapter VII, Lemma 6.2)
.

The subgroup
X
detects the mod

G
.

From before, the subgroup
Z_{X}
of

is normalized by

and its mod

by (

Consider the case

. Then by (
10
) we have
\hc^{k}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})=0
when

, and

\hc^{k}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\mathfrak{e}_{2}]

. We have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

The Steenrod square
\Sq^{2}:\hc^{k}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})\to\hc^{k+2}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})
is trivial when

is a multiple of

.

Proof.

By the above, it is enough to show that
\Sq^{2}
vanishes on
\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\mathfrak{e}_{2}]
.

Since
\hc^{5}(\mathcal{S})=\hc^{6}(\mathcal{S})=\{0\}
, we necessarily have
\Sq^{1}(\mathfrak{e})=\Sq^{2}(\mathfrak{e})=0
. By naturality of
\Sq
we also have

\Sq^{1}(\mathfrak{e}_{i})=\Sq^{2}(\mathfrak{e}_{i})=0
for

. By Cartan’s formula (

), we deduce
\Sq^{2}(\mathfrak{e}_{1}^{s}\mathfrak{e}_{2}^{t})=0
for all nonnegative

.
The conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

We have

\hc^{*}_{\SW}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\mathfrak{e}_{2}].

Proof.

Since
w_{4}(\eta_{i})=\mathfrak{e}_{i}
for

, the right side is contained in the left side.

Now let
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
. By (
10
) again it is enough to see that
w_{k}(\pi)=0
whenever
k\equiv 2,3
(mod
4
).

For

(mod
4
), we use Wu’s formula (Proposition
2.1
) with

and

, giving

\begin{split}\Sq^{2}(w_{k-2}(\pi))&\small={k-5\choose 0}w_{2}(\pi)w_{k-2}(\pi)+{k-4\choose 1}w_{1}(\pi)w_{k-1}(\pi)+{k-3\choose 2}w_{0}(\pi)w_{k}(\pi)\\
&=\frac{(k-3)(k-4)}{2}w_{k}(\pi).\end{split}

The second equality holds because
\hc^{i}(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S})=\{0\}
for

. Also

is odd for

(mod

A similar argument works for

.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

We have

\hc^{*}_{\SW}(\mathcal{S}^{n})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n}].

Proof.

Again we have
\mathfrak{e}_{i}=w_{4}(\eta_{i})\in\hc^{*}_{\SW}(\mathcal{S}^{n})
, so the left hand side contains the right hand side.
For the other direction, it is enough to show that the SWCs of an OIR of

lie in
\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n}]

First we treat the case of “decomposable” orthogonal representations. For

with

be symplectic representations of
\mathcal{S}
, and

\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{s}
orthogonal representations of
\mathcal{S}
, with

, as in Section

. Note that each of

Next we treat symmetrizations of decomposable representations.

of

, put

Then,

lies in the subalgebra of

By the classification in Section

∎

3.3.
Product Formula for Total SWCs

Let

, and
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of

, we work with

(with

as in Section

. But we can say more about the coefficients

is coming from a representation of the bigger group

Let

is isomorphic to

isomorphic to

. Since

detects SWCs of

, we infer that

also detects the SWCs of

Let
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of

. Clearly

and is

-invariant. Now, Lemma

gives all

are divisible by

We can now obtain

as its image in
\hc^{*}(X)
by identifying

with
\mathfrak{e}_{i}\in\hc^{*}(X)

Theorem 3.6
.

The total SWC of an orthogonal representation

of

is given by

where

.

Note that the character values

are given by Lemma

.

Example 3.7
.

For the regular representation

3.4.
Gow’s Formula

In this section, let
G=\Sp(2n,q)
.

Theorem 3.8

(
[
11
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible self-dual representation of
G
with central character
\omega_{\pi}
and Frobenius-Schur Indicator
\varepsilon(\pi)
. Then, we have

(12)

\varepsilon(\pi)=\omega_{\pi}(-\mathbbm{1}).

We simply call this equality
Gow’s formula.

For irreducible orthogonal
\pi
of
G
, it means
\chi_{\pi}(\mathbbm{1})=\chi_{\pi}(-\mathbbm{1})
.
This is same as
\chi_{\pi}(g_{0})=\chi_{\pi}(g_{n})
, which leads to

(13)

\chi_{\pi}(g_{i})=\chi_{\pi}(g_{n-i})\quad;\quad 0\leq i\leq n.

Now, Lemma
2.5
simplifies the exponents
m_{k}(\pi)
in Theorem
3.6
when
\pi
is either irreducible orthogonal, or the symmetrization of an irreducible symplectic:

Corollary 3.8.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible orthogonal representation of
G
. Then the total SWC of
\pi
is

w(\pi)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\mathcal{D}^{[k]}(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n})\Big)^{m_{k}(\pi)/4}

where

m_{k}(\pi)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ when }k\text{ is odd}\\
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(g_{k})\chi_{\pi}(g_{i})&\text{ when }k\text{ is even, }n\text{ is odd}\\[10.0pt]
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(g_{k})\chi_{\pi}(g_{i})+\cfrac{1}{2^{n}}\big(\chi_{\sigma_{\frac{n}{2}}}(g_{k})\chi_{\pi}(g_{\frac{n}{2}})\big)&\text{ when }k,n\text{ both are even}.\end{cases}

Corollary 3.8.2
.

Let
\varphi
be an irreducible symplectic representation of
G
. Then we have

w(S(\varphi))=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\mathcal{D}^{[k]}(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n})\Big)^{m_{k}(\varphi)/2}

where

m_{k}(\varphi)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ when }k\text{ is even and }n\text{ is odd}\\
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}\chi_{\sigma_{i}}(g_{k})\chi_{\varphi}(g_{i})&\text{ when }k\text{ is odd, }\\[10.0pt]
\cfrac{1}{2^{n}}\chi_{\sigma_{\frac{n}{2}}}(g_{k})\chi_{\varphi}(g_{\frac{n}{2}})&\text{ when }k,n\text{ both are even}.\end{cases}

In the formula above,
\left\lceil\cdot\right\rceil
denotes the ceiling function; in particular

\left\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\right\rceil=\begin{cases}\frac{n-2}{2},&\text{if $n$ is even},\\[6.0pt]
\frac{n-1}{2},&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

is odd when
q\equiv 1
mod (
4
), and
m_{1}(\Pi_{2})/4
is odd when
q\equiv 3
mod (
4
).

Corresponding to the cases, we have

\displaystyle w(\Pi_{i})

\displaystyle=(1+\mathfrak{e}_{1}+\mathfrak{e}_{2}+\mathfrak{e}_{1}\mathfrak{e}_{2})^{m_{1}(\Pi_{i})/4}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{m_{1}(\Pi_{i})}{4}\left(\mathfrak{e}_{1}+\mathfrak{e}_{2}+\mathfrak{e}_{1}\mathfrak{e}_{2}\right)+\ldots

hence
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2}\in\hc^{*}_{\SW}(G)
.

∎

4.3.
The case
n=4

Let
\underline{\mathfrak{e}}=(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\mathfrak{e}_{2},\mathfrak{e}_{3},\mathfrak{e}_{4})
.
We apply Theorem
3.6
for
n=4
to have the total SWC of an orthogonal representation
\pi
of
G
:

(18)

w(\pi)=\mathcal{D}^{[1]}(\underline{\mathfrak{e}})^{m_{1}(\pi)/4}\mathcal{D}^{[2]}(\underline{\mathfrak{e}})^{m_{2}(\pi)/4}\mathcal{D}^{[3]}(\underline{\mathfrak{e}})^{m_{3}(\pi)/4}\mathcal{D}^{[4]}(\underline{\mathfrak{e}})^{m_{4}(\pi)/4};

please see Section
2.9
for calculation of these
\mathcal{D}^{[i]}(\underline{\mathfrak{e}})
.

Also, Gow’s formula through Corollary
3.8.1
allows simplification for irreducible orthogonal
\pi
:

w(\pi)=\Big(1+\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{3}+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{3}+\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{3}+\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}\mathcal{E}_{3}+\mathcal{E}_{3}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{E}_{4}\Big)^{m_{2}(\pi)/4}(1+\mathcal{E}_{1})^{m_{4}(\pi)/4}

where, with the help of Lemma
2.4
once again, gives

\begin{split}m_{2}(\pi)&=\cfrac{1}{8}\big(\deg(\pi)-\chi_{\pi}(g_{2})\big),\\
m_{4}(\pi)&=\cfrac{1}{8}\big(\deg(\pi)-4\chi_{\pi}(g_{1})+3\chi_{\pi}(g_{2})\big).\end{split}

Again,
g_{1}=\diag(1,1,1,-1,-1,1,1,1)
and

g_{2}=\diag(1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1,1,1)
.

4.4.
Weil Representations

For a fixed nontrivial linear character
\lambda
of
(\mathbb{F}_{q},+)
, the prescription

x\mapsto\lambda_{x}\quad;\quad\lambda_{x}(y)=\lambda(xy)

defines a group isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q}\overset{\sim}{\to}\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
\alpha
be a non-square, and
1
be the identity in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. According to Gérardin
[
9
, Theorem 2.4(d)]
, the symplectic group
G=\Sp(2n,q)
has two nonisomorphic
Weil representations

\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{1}}
,
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{\alpha}}
associated to the characters
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{\alpha}
. Set

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{1}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}_{\lambda_{\alpha}}
. Both
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
are complex representations of degree
q^{n}
, and decompose into two irreducible representations as

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{l}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{s}\quad,\quad\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{l}\oplus\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{s}

where
\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{l}
are the components with larger degrees
(q^{n}+1)/2
, and
\mathcal{W}_{s}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{s}
have degrees
(q^{n}-1)/2
. When
q\equiv 3
(mod
4
), none of these four irreducible representations are self-dual. In fact,
\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{l}
are dual to each other, and similarly
\mathcal{W}_{s}
is the dual of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{s}
. Whereas when
q\equiv 1
(mod
4
), all representations of
G
are self-dual. In this case, exactly one of
\mathcal{W}_{l}
,
\mathcal{W}_{s}
is orthogonal, and the other is symplectic. The same is true for the components of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
. So, neither
\mathcal{W}
nor
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is orthogonal. Here we compute

w(S(\mathcal{W}))=c(\mathcal{W})\mod 2.

From
[
9
, Corollary 4.8.1]
, the character values of
\mathcal{W}
at
g_{i}
are given by:

\chi_{\mathcal{W}}(g_{i})=(-1)^{i\frac{q-1}{2}}q^{N(g_{i})},\text{ with }\quad N(g)=\frac{1}{2}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\ker(g-\mathbbm{1}).

One sees that

\chi_{\mathcal{W}}(g_{i})=(-1)^{i\frac{q-1}{2}}q^{n-i}.

For each
1\leq k\leq n
, Equation (
7
) gives

\displaystyle m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}[f_{k}]_{i}\chi_{\mathcal{W}}(g_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}[f_{k}]_{i}(-1)^{i\frac{q-1}{2}}q^{n-i}

where
[f_{k}]_{i}
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
f_{k}(x)=(1-x)^{k}(1+x)^{n-k}
. Note that
[f_{k}]_{n-i}=(-1)^{k}[f_{k}]_{i}
for each
i=0,\ldots,n
, so that

m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}[f_{k}]_{n-i}(-1)^{i\frac{q-1}{2}}q^{n-i}.

Put
F_{k}(x):=(x-1)^{k}(x+1)^{n-k}=(-1)^{k}f_{k}(x)
. Then, for
q\equiv 1
(mod
4
), it is clear that

m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}F_{k}(q)=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}(q-1)^{k}(q+1)^{n-k}.

Whereas for
q\equiv 3
(mod
4
), with a few manipulations, we get

\displaystyle m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}[F_{k}]_{n-i}q^{n-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}[F_{k}]_{j}q^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n-1}}F_{k}(-q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}(q-1)^{n-k}(q+1)^{k}.

Corollary 4.0.1
.

For the Weil representations
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
of
G
, we have

w(S(\mathcal{W}))=w(S(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}))=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}^{[k]}(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n})^{m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))/4}

where

m_{k}(S(\mathcal{W}))=\begin{cases}\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}(q-1)^{k}(q+1)^{n-k},&q\equiv 1\;(\Mod 4)\\
\cfrac{1}{2^{n-1}}(q-1)^{n-k}(q+1)^{k},&q\equiv 3\;(\Mod 4).\end{cases}

Proof.

We have already shown this for
\mathcal{W}
. In
[
9
]
, we see that
\chi_{\mathcal{W}}(g_{i})=\chi_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}(g_{i})
for each
i
. Thus, the formula also holds for
w(S(\mathcal{W}^{\prime}))
.
∎

5.
Universal SWCs

Let us write
X_{n}
for the subgroup of
G_{n}=\Sp(2n,q)
isomorphic to
\mathcal{S}^{n}
(previously just written as
X
).
The restriction map
\hc^{*}(G_{n})
to
\hc^{*}(X_{n})
has image in the
S_{n}
-invariants
\hc^{*}(X_{n})^{S_{n}}
, which contains the elements

\mathcal{E}_{j}=\mathcal{E}_{j}(\mathfrak{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{e}_{n})
but also

\mathcal{F}_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\ldots<i_{j}\\
1\leq k\leq j\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{e}_{i_{1}}\ldots\hat{\mathfrak{e}}_{i_{k}}\ldots\mathfrak{e}_{i_{j}}b_{i_{k}}.

Note that
\deg(\mathcal{E}_{j})=4j
and
\deg(\mathcal{F}_{j})=4j-1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
be formal indeterminates. The homomorphism

\mathbb{F}_{2}[E_{1},\ldots,E_{n},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}]\to\hc^{*}(X_{n})^{S_{n}}

defined by sending
E_{i}
to
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and
F_{i}
to
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is surjective with kernel equal to the ideal
(F_{1}^{2},\ldots,F_{n}^{2})
.

Proof.

This is implicit in
[
5
, Theorem 6.1, page 283]
.
∎

Let
m\geq n
and
\iota_{n}:G_{n-1}\to G_{m}
be the following inclusion: For
A\in G_{n-1}
,

\iota_{n}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{m-n+1}&&\\
&A&\\
&&\mathbbm{1}_{m-n+1}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Lemma
3.2
, it is enough to show that the restriction

\hc^{i}(X_{m})^{S_{m}}\to\hc^{i}(X_{n-1})^{S_{n-1}}
is injective. Write
R_{n}=P_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}Q_{n}
, where

P_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n}]
is the subalgebra generated by
\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n}
and
Q_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n}]
is the subalgebra generated by
\mathcal{F}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n}
in
\hc^{*}(\mathcal{S}^{n})
. From above, we need to show that the restriction of the map
P_{m}\otimes Q_{m}\to P_{n-1}\otimes Q_{n-1}
to the degree
k
part is injective for all
k<4n-1
.

Any monomial of degree
k
is of the form
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{r_{1}}\ldots\mathcal{E}_{m}^{r_{m}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{s_{1}}\ldots\mathcal{F}_{m}^{s_{m}}
with
r_{i}\geq 0
,
s_{i}\in\{0,1\}
. We may write this as
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}
, with
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
. By Theorem
5.1
above, these monomials are linearly independent.
The condition

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(4i)r_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(4j-1)s_{j}=k<4n-1

ensures that if
i\geq n
, then
r_{i}=0
and
s_{i}=0
.

An element
\alpha=\sum_{(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s})}c_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}\in\hc^{k}(G_{m})
under
\iota^{*}_{n}
gets mapped to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n-1})\\
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1})\end{subarray}}c_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}\in\hc^{k}(G_{n-1}).

If
i^{*}_{n}(\alpha)=0
, then all
c_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}}
are zero since
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{s}}
are linearly independent. This implies
\alpha=0
, and hence we have the injectivity of
\iota^{*}_{n}
for
k<4n-1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Theorem
1.4
, the map
i^{*}_{2}:\hc^{4}(G_{m})\to\hc^{4}(G_{1})=\hc^{4}(\mathcal{S})
is injective for all
m\geq 1
. So the formula for
w_{4}(\pi)
follows from (
14
). Similarly, since
i^{*}_{3}:\hc^{8}(G_{m})\to\hc^{8}(G_{2})
is injective for all
m\geq 3
, we have
w_{8}(\pi)
from Equation (
17
).
∎

Appendix A

In this Appendix we discuss the formula for CCs and SWCs of external tensor products of representations. This formula involves an interesting family of polynomials which we now define.

A.1.
The Polynomials
\PP_{m,n}

Given non-negative integers
m,n
, we define
q_{m,n}
be the following polynomial in
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
:

(19)

q_{m,n}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+x_{i}+y_{j}).

It is symmetric in the
x_{i}
and the
y_{j}
separately, so by the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials
[
3
, Chapter 4, Section 6, Theorem 1]
, there is a unique polynomial
\PP_{m,n}\in\Lambda
such that

(20)

q_{m,n}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\PP_{m,n}(\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{m}(\mathbf{x}),\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{y}),\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbf{y})).

For example, we have
\PP_{m,0}({\bf x},{\bf y})=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
.

A.2.
Characteristic Classes of External Tensor Products

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be finite groups, and let
p_{i}:G_{1}\times G_{2}\to G_{i}
be the projection maps. Given complex
G_{i}
-representations
(\pi_{i},V_{i})
, we consider their external tensor product
(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2},V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose
(\pi_{1},V_{1}),

(\pi_{2},V_{2})
have respective degrees
m,n
. Then,

c(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})=\PP_{m,n}(p_{1}^{*}c_{1}(\pi_{1}),\ldots,p_{1}^{*}c_{m}(\pi_{1}),p_{2}^{*}c_{1}(\pi_{2}),\ldots,p_{2}^{*}c_{n}(\pi_{2})),

where
\PP_{m,n}
is as defined above. When
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are orthogonal, then

w(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})=\PP_{m,n}(p_{1}^{*}w_{1}(\pi_{1}),\ldots,p_{1}^{*}w_{m}(\pi_{1}),p_{2}^{*}w_{1}(\pi_{2}),\ldots,p_{2}^{*}w_{n}(\pi_{2})).

We will sketch the proof for SWCs in this Appendix; the case of CCs is similar.
Iterating Proposition
A.1
gives:

Corollary A.1.1
.

Let
\Pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{n}
be a (complex) representation of
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}
. Then
c_{k}(\Pi)
is in the subalgebra of
\hc^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z})
generated by
\{p_{j}^{*}c_{i}(\pi_{j}):1\leq j\leq n,i\geq 0\}
. When each
\pi_{i}
is orthogonal, the class

w_{k}(\Pi)
is in the subalgebra of
\hc^{*}(G)
generated by
\{p_{j}^{*}w_{i}(\pi_{j}):1\leq j\leq n,i\geq 0\}
.

A.3.
Products of Vector Bundles

The results in the previous section are valid for vector bundles over paracompact spaces. Here, we will prove them in this more general setting.
All our base spaces are paracompact.

Let
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be two real vector bundles over a base space
B
. Their internal tensor product
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
is again a real vector bundle over
B
.

Proposition A.2

(
[
18
]
, Chapter 7, Problem 7-C)
.

Let
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be real vector bundles over
B
with respective ranks
m,n
. Then,

w(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})=\PP_{m,n}(w_{1}(E_{1}),\ldots,w_{m}(E_{1}),w_{1}(E_{2}),\ldots,w_{n}(E_{2})).

Let
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be real vector bundles over base spaces
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
respectively with
\Pi_{i}:E_{i}\to B_{i}
. We can form their external tensor product
E_{1}\boxtimes E_{2}
over
B_{1}\times B_{2}
as follows.

Consider the projection maps
p_{i}:B_{1}\times B_{2}\to B_{i}
. Let
p_{i}^{*}E_{i}
be the pullback of
E_{i}
by
p_{i}
consisting of elements
((b_{1},b_{2}),e_{i})\in(B_{1}\times B_{2})\times E_{i}
such that
\Pi_{i}(e_{i})=p_{i}(b_{1},b_{2})=b_{i}
. These are vector bundles over the same base space
B_{1}\times B_{2}
. Thus, we construct their internal tensor product, and put
E_{1}\boxtimes E_{2}:=p_{1}^{*}E_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}E_{2}
, which is again a vector bundle over
B_{1}\times B_{2}.

This with the naturality of SWCs and Proposition
A.2
gives:

Proposition A.3
.

Let
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be real vector bundles over respective base spaces
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
with
\rank(E_{1})=m
and
\rank(E_{2})=n
. Then,

w(E_{1}\boxtimes E_{2})=\PP_{m,n}(p_{1}^{*}w_{1}(E_{1}),\ldots,p_{1}^{*}w_{m}(E_{1}),p_{2}^{*}w_{1}(E_{2}),\ldots,p_{2}^{*}w_{n}(E_{2})).

A.4.
Vector Bundles From Representations

Let
G
be a finite group, and
(\rho,U)
be a real representation of
G
. Associated to
G
is a classifying space
BG
with a contractible right principal
G
-bundle
EG
. From
(\rho,U)
one can form the associated real vector bundle
EG[U]
over
BG
. To define
EG[U]
, first form the product
EG\times U
; it is
G
-space under the action
g\cdot(x,u)=(x\cdot g^{-1},\rho(g)u)
. Then
EG[U]
is the quotient of
EG\times U
by this action. Then put

w_{i}^{\mathbb{R}}(\rho)=w_{i}(EG[U])
.
(See for instance
[
2
]
or
[
12
]
.) The singular cohomology
\hc^{*}(BG,A)
is naturally isomorphic to the group cohomology
\hc^{*}(G,A)
for any abelian group
A
.

Let
(\pi,V)
be a complex orthogonal representation of
G
. There is a representation
(\rho,U)
, with
U
a real vector space, so that
\rho\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\pi
(see
[
4
, Chapter II, Section 6]
for instance). Such a representation is called a
real form
of
\pi
; it is unique up to isomorphism. We now can define
w(\pi):=w^{\mathbb{R}}(\rho)
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be finite groups with orthogonal complex representations
(\pi_{1},V_{1})
and
(\pi_{2},V_{2})
. Let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
. We can form their external tensor product
(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2},V_{1}\otimes V_{2})
, an orthogonal representation of
G
. Let
(\rho_{i},U_{i})
be real forms of
\pi_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}
is a real form of
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
. From above, one has

\begin{split}w(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})&=w^{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2})\\
&=w(EG[U_{1}\otimes U_{2}])\\
&=w(\mathcal{U}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{U}_{2})\in\hc^{*}(BG_{1}\times BG_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}),\\
\end{split}

where

\mathcal{U}_{i}=EG_{i}[U_{i}]
.
We may identify
BG_{1}\times BG_{2}
with
BG
. Now, Proposition
A.1
for SWCs follows from Proposition
A.3
with
E_{i}=\mathcal{U}_{i}
.

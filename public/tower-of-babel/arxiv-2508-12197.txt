Implicit-Explicit Scheme with Multiscale Vanka Two-Grid Solver for Heterogeneous Unsaturated Poroelasticity
Maria Vasilyeva, Ben S. Southworth, Yunhui He, Min Wang

Abstract
We consider a coupled nonlinear system of equations that describe unsaturated flow in heterogeneous poroelastic media. For the numerical solution, we use a finite element approximation in space and present an efficient multiscale two-grid solver for solving the coupled system of equations. The proposed two-grid solver contains two main parts: (i) accurate coarse grid approximation based on local spectral spaces and (ii) coupled smoothing iterations based on an overlapping multiscale Vanka method. A Vanka smoother and local spectral coarse grids come with significant computational cost in the setup phase. To avoid constructing a new solver for each time step and/or nonlinear iteration, we utilize an implicit-explicit integration scheme in time, where we partition the nonlinear operator as a sum of linear and nonlinear parts. In particular, we construct an implicit linear approximation of the stiff components that remains fixed across all time, while treating the remaining nonlinear residual explicitly. This allows us to construct a robust two-grid solver offline and utilize it for fast and efficient online time integration. A linear stability analysis of the proposed novel coupled scheme is presented based on the representation of the system as a two-step scheme. We show that the careful decomposition of linear and nonlinear parts guarantees a linearly stable scheme. A numerical study is presented for a two-dimensional nonlinear coupled test problem of unsaturated flow in heterogeneous poroelastic media. We demonstrate the robustness of the two-grid solver, particularly the efficacy of block smoothing compared with simple pointwise smoothing, and illustrate the accuracy and stability of implicit-explicit time integration.

Introduction

Accurately simulating unsaturated flow plays a crucial role in hydrology, soil science, agricultural engineering, reservoir simulation, and environmental engineering. Unsaturated flow describes water movement through porous medium where both air and water coexist in the pores. The mathematical model is described by nonlinear partial differential equations that contain Darcy’s flow model and the continuity equation
[
1
]
.
The model includes a highly nonlinear relationship between moisture content, pressure, and hydraulic conductivity that is usually described by empirical functions such as the Van Genuchten or Brooks-Corey models
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
Including mechanics in unsaturated flow modeling is crucial because fluid transport in porous media is inherently coupled with mechanical deformations
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Changes in water content significantly affect both permeability and mechanical properties, leading to complex interactions between flow and deformation. The elastic properties of the porous matrix are water-dependent, with soils and rocks softening as they absorb water, leading to increased deformations
[
9
]
. Therefore, a fully coupled poroelastic approach is essential for capturing the realistic behavior of unsaturated porous media.
The numerical modeling of such coupled processes in heterogeneous media is complex due to strong nonlinearity, multiphysics interactions, and scale dependencies. Advances in numerical methods lead to enhanced accuracy and efficiency of simulations in geotechnical engineering, reservoir modeling, and environmental hydrology, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
6
,
13
,
14
]
.

The nonlinear, coupled nature of time-dependent equations in unsaturated poroelastic flow presents significant computational challenges.
Classical numerical schemes for solving time-dependent problems are based on explicit or implicit time approximation.
Explicit methods are straightforward in implementation, but can require very small time steps for stability, making them impractical for problems with heterogeneous properties.
Implicit schemes enhance stability for stiff equations, but require expensive linear and nonlinear solves.
An alternative approach involves combining two methods using an Implicit-Explicit (ImEx) time-stepping scheme, which applies an explicit scheme to non-stiff components and an implicit scheme to stiff components that require improved stability, e.g.,
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Typical ImEx schemes are based on a linear additive partitioning of the operator, which must be tailored to the specific application and problem being addressed.
In
[
18
]
,
the authors propose implicit-explicit-Null (EIN) methods, where linear terms are added and subtracted to ensure the stability of the ImEx integration scheme, with an added linear damping term treated implicitly. A similar approach was introduced earlier in
[
19
]
for removing the stiffness from interfacial flows with surface tension by reformulating high-order, nonlinear, and non-local terms into linear.

Even in ImEx schemes, the bulk of computational cost is in solving the implicit equations. When applicable, multigrid and multilevel methods are among the most efficient solvers.
Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of using the multiscale method as a preconditioner for high-contrast problems
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, and various multigrid methods have been developed for and demonstrated on porelastic models, e.g.,
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. For challenging problems, robust multigrid methods can require more advanced smoothers than simple pointwise methods such as Jacobi or Gauss-Seidel. This has led to renewed interest in block- or patch-based smoothers along the lines of Schwarz or Vanka methods
[
26
]
. Such methods can be overlapping or nonoverlapping with respect to patches or “subdomains” that are locally solved, and additive or multiplicative in nature. For geometric multigrid and systems of equations like Stokes, the patches are typically relatively small and local, e.g.,
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
, while for two- and multilevel domain-decomposition type methods the subdomains can be quite large and span a large number of fine degrees of freedom, e.g.,
[
27
,
28
]
. Multi-color smoothers, e.g.,
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, combine ideas from additive and multiplicative methods are often used for improved parallelization over multiplivative methods and improved performance over additive methods.

In this work, we consider both the discretization and numerical solver for unsaturated flow in heterogeneous poroelastic media.
Specifically, we consider nonlinear poroelasticity systems in heterogeneous media, which cause high spatial variations in conductivity and elastic properties of the soils. Moreover, unsaturated flow causes sharp wetting fronts requiring a small-scale resolution of the meshes to track changes in the solution and resolve heterogeneity at the grid level.
We start with an unsaturated poroelastic media that is nonlinear in both permeability and elastic properties, as well as in the coefficient for time derivative.
First, we utilize an EIN formulation to develop a
stable coupled implicit-explicit scheme
that divides operators into a linear part that is fixed for all time, and a corresponding nonlinear residual, where we treat the linear part implicitly and the nonlinear part explicitly. We show that our careful choice of operator splitting ensures the unconditional linear stability of the scheme and provides a basis for the construction of an efficient solver for the linearized problems.
Second, we propose an
efficient multiscale two-grid solver
that contains two main components: a coarse-grid solver based on generalized multiscale finite element and a
multiscale Vanka smoother
. The coarse grid solver uses local spectral basis functions to systematically enrich a linear piecewise coarse space for flow and mechanics. For the multiscale Vanka smoother, we consider (i) overlapping subdomains defined by the support of the multiscale basis, (ii) subdomains associated with the coarse
cells
, which overlap only at coarse mesh facets, and (iii) subdomains built by extending coarse cell-based subdomains to have a small overlap of
\mathcal{O}(1)
fine-grid cells. This forms our multiscale Vanka relaxation, and within each choice of subdomains we consider multicolor additive implementations.
We numerically show the high efficiency of our proposed approach for the nonlinear poroelasticity problems arising from unsaturated flow problems. The multiscale basis functions and Vanka smoothing introduce a significant computational cost to multigrid methods in computing dense inverses or eigenvalue decompositions, but by combining with our proposed implicit-explicit formulation, we are able to precompute these components a priori or “offline,” and reuse for a nonlinear simulation.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the problem formulation for unsaturated flow in poroelastic media.
In Section 3, we discuss finite element approximation in space and propose time approximation schemes based on the implicit-explicit method. We then give a stability analysis, proving an unconditionally stable scheme for our proposed additive partitioning of the operator.
In Section
5
, we propose the construction of a multiscale two-grid solver based on multiscale Vanka approach, and describe the construction of the local spectral multiscale basis functions for the flow and mechanics parts of the system.
In Section
6
, we investigate the performance of the classic pointwise smoothers based on Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel iterations and proposed coupled local smoothing iterations. Numerical results show the efficiency of our proposed two-grid solver for the two-dimensional nonlinear poroelastic problem in heterogeneous media. Finally, the conclusion is presented in Section
7
.

2
Problem formulation

We consider the unsaturated poroelasticity model that describes the coupled behavior of fluid flow and mechanical deformation in partially saturated porous media. Specifically, we utilize the model from
[
32
,
33
,
10
]
with an additional nonlinearity to account for elastic properties
[
9
]
.

The Richards equation describes an unsaturated flow in porous media, which combines Darcy’s law for variably saturated flow with the mass conservation equation
[
1
]

c_{w}(x,h_{w})\frac{\partial h_{w}}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot(k_{w}(x,h_{w})(\nabla h_{w}+\nabla\zeta))=f_{w},

(1)

with

\theta
is the water content,

h_{w}
is the pressure head,

\zeta
is the elevation head,

f_{w}
is a fluid source/sink term and

k_{w}
is the hydraulic conductivity and
c_{w}(x,h_{w})=\partial\theta/\partial h_{w}
.

The nonlinear conductivity coefficient and water contentare depends on the pressure head
h_{w}
and defined by the van Genuchten model
[
2
]

\theta(h_{w})=\theta_{r}+(\theta_{s}-\theta_{r})\left[1+(\beta|h_{w}|)^{n_{\theta}}\right]^{-m_{\theta}},\quad h_{w}<0,

(2)

where
\theta_{s}
is the saturated water content,
\theta_{r}
is the residual water content,
\beta
is an empirical parameter related to the inverse of the air entry pressure, and
n_{\theta}
and
m_{\theta}
are fitting parameters with
m_{\theta}=1-\frac{1}{n_{\theta}}
. The unsaturated hydraulic conductivity function is defined as

k_{w}(x,h_{w})=k_{s}(x)(S_{e}(h_{w}))^{\eta}\left[1-\Big(1-\big(S_{e}(h_{w})\big)^{1/m_{\theta}}\Big)^{m_{\theta}}\right]^{2},\quad S_{e}(h_{w})=\frac{\theta(h_{w})-\theta_{r}}{\theta_{s}-\theta_{r}},

(3)

where

k_{s}
is the saturated hydraulic conductivity,

\eta
is a pore connectivity parameter and

S_{e}
is the effective saturation.

For the mechanics, we consider the momentum balance for the porous medium’s displacement under the quasi-static and small deformations assumptions
[
32
,
33
,
10
]

\begin{split}&-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{T}=\rho_{b}g,\quad\sigma_{T}=\sigma-\alpha Sp\mathcal{I},\\
&\sigma(u)=2\mu\varepsilon(u)+\lambda(\nabla\cdot u)\mathcal{I},\quad\varepsilon(u)=0.5(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}).\end{split}

(4)

where

u
is the displacement vector,

p
is the water pressure,

S
is the water saturation,

\alpha
is the Biot coefficient,

\sigma_{T}
is the total stress tensor,

\sigma
is the stress tensor,
\varepsilon
is the strain tensor,

\rho_{b}=\big(\phi S\rho_{w}+(1-\phi)\rho_{s}\big)
is the bulk density,

g
is the gravity vector,

\phi
is the porosity,

\rho_{w}
is the water density,

\rho_{s}
is the solid phase density,

\mathcal{I}
is the identity tensor
and
\lambda
and
\mu
are the Lame’s coefficients.

Additionally, we consider nonlinear elastic properties that describe the effect of the water content changes on mechanical properties
[
9
]

\lambda(x,h_{w})=\frac{E(x,h_{w})\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)},\quad\mu(x,h_{w})=\frac{E(x,h_{w})}{2(1+\nu)},

where
\nu
is the Poisson’s ratio and
E(x,h_{w})
is the Young’s modulus given by

E(x,h_{w})=E_{d}(x)+(E_{w}(x)-E_{d}(x))(S_{e}(h_{w}))^{\zeta}.

(5)

The elastic properties of the porous matrix are water-dependent, meaning that soils and rocks tend to soften as they absorb water, leading to increased deformations. Here
E_{d}
and
E_{w}
are Young’s modulus of the dry and wet states, respectively, and
\zeta
is the empirical fitting parameter
[
9
]
.

We note that the relationships (
2
)-(
3
) are expressed in terms of water content and pressure, therefore we adapt the original van Genuchten model to water pressure and saturation representation by using the following relations

S=\theta/\phi,\quad p=\rho_{w}gh_{w}.

Here, instead of formulation (
1
) for
(\theta,h_{w})
, we write a mass conservation equation in terms of
(S,p)

[
32
,
33
,
10
]
. A full derivation from physical principles can be found in the Appendix
A
.
We let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{R}^{d}
be a computational domain and consider the following coupled nonlinear poroelasticity system in
\Omega

\begin{split}&c(x,p)\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\alpha S(p)\frac{\partial(\nabla\cdot u)}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot\big(\kappa(x,p)\nabla p\big)=\tilde{f},\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
&-\nabla\cdot\sigma(u)+\alpha\nabla\big(S(p)p\big)=\rho_{b}g,\quad\quad x\in\Omega.\end{split}

(6)

with
\tilde{f}=f+\nabla\cdot\big(\kappa(x,p)\nabla(\rho_{w}g)\big)
and

c(x,p)=\big(\phi C_{w}+(\alpha-\phi)C_{s}S\big)S+\big(\phi+(\alpha-\phi)C_{s}Sp\big)S^{\prime},\quad\kappa(x,p)=k(x)\frac{k_{rw}(S)}{\mu_{w}}.

Here
k
is the intrinsic permeability tensor,
\mu_{w}
is the water dynamic viscosity, and
k_{rw}
is the relative permeability,
C_{w}
is the water compressibility,
C_{s}
is the compressibility of the solid grains,
S=S(p)
and
S^{\prime}=\partial S/\partial p
.

We supplement (
6
) with the following initial conditions

p=p^{0},\quad u=u^{0},\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t=0,

(7)

and boundary conditions

\begin{split}&-\kappa\nabla p\cdot n=\gamma(p-p_{1}),\quad x\in\Gamma_{p},\quad-\kappa\nabla p\cdot n=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega/\Gamma_{p},\quad t>0,\\
&u=0,\quad x\in\Gamma_{u},\quad\sigma\cdot n=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega/\Gamma_{u},\quad t>0,\end{split}

(8)

where
p_{1}
is the given pressure on the boundary
\Gamma_{p}
and
\gamma
is exchange coefficient in Robin-type boundary condition.

3
Spatial discretization and semi-implicit time integration

Let

V=\{v\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}:v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{u}\},\quad Q=H^{1}(\Omega).

We define a variational formulation of unsaturated poroelasticity problem (
6
)-(
8
) as follows:
find
(p,u)\in Q\times V
such that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\alpha S(p)\frac{\partial(\nabla\cdot u)}{\partial t}\ r\ dx+\int_{\Omega}c(x,p)\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}\ r\ dx&+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x,p)\nabla p\cdot\nabla r\ dx+\int_{\Gamma_{p}}\gamma\ p\ r\ ds\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}\ r\ dx+\int_{\Gamma_{p}}\gamma\ p_{1}\ r\ ds,\quad\forall r\in Q,\\
\int_{\Omega}\sigma(u):\varepsilon(v)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\alpha\nabla\big(S(p)p\big)\cdot\ v\ dx&=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{b}\ g\ v\ dx,\quad\forall v\in V.\end{split}

(9)

We use a finite element method for spatial discretization. Let
\mathcal{T}^{h}
be a partition of the domain
\Omega
,
\mathcal{E}^{b}
be a set of all boundary interfaces and
\mathcal{E}^{b}_{p}
be a subset of the boundary interfaces
\mathcal{E}^{b}_{p}=\mathcal{E}^{b}\cap\Gamma_{p}
. We consider linear elements
Q_{h}\subset Q
and
V_{h}\subset V
for pressure and elasticity, respectively. Note that an additional regularization can be added to the pressure equation to avoid oscillation in the solution and stabilize the discretization for some special cases of parameters
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
.
Consider the first-order backward Euler implicit scheme with constant time-step size
\tau
.
Then we obtain the following discrete system in a matrix form for
(p^{n+1}_{h},u^{n+1}_{h})\in Q_{h}\times V_{h}
:

\frac{1}{\tau}\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n+1}&\alpha D_{h}^{n+1}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}-p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{n+1}&0\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n+1}&K_{h}^{n+1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}F^{n+1}_{p,h}\\
F^{n+1}_{u,h}\end{bmatrix},

(10)

where
c^{n+1}=c(p^{n+1})
,
k^{n+1}=k(x,p^{n+1})
and

\begin{split}&A_{h}^{n+1}=\left[a^{n+1}_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}\kappa^{n+1}\nabla\phi_{i}\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}\ dx\right],\\
&K_{h}^{n+1}=\left[b^{n+1}_{ln}=\int_{\Omega}(2\mu^{n+1}\varepsilon(\Phi_{l}):\varepsilon(\Phi_{n})dx+\lambda^{n+1}\nabla\cdot\Phi_{l}\ \nabla\cdot\Phi_{n})dx\right],\\
&D_{h}^{n+1}=\left[d_{il}^{n+1}=\int_{\Omega}S(p^{n+1})\ \phi_{i}\nabla\cdot\Phi_{l}dx\right],\\
&G_{h}^{n+1}=\left[g_{li}^{n+1}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\big(S(p^{n+1})\phi_{i}\big)\cdot\Phi_{l}\ dx\right],\\
&F_{p,h}^{n+1}=\left[f^{n+1}_{p,i}=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}^{n+1}\ \phi_{i}\ dx+\int_{\Gamma_{p}}\gamma p_{1}\phi_{i}\ ds\right],\\
&F_{u,h}^{n+1}=\left[f_{u,l}^{n+1}],\quad f_{u,l}^{n+1}=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{b}^{n+1}\ g\ \Phi_{l}\ dx\right],\quad M^{n+1}_{h}=\left[m^{n+1}_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}c^{n+1}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\ dx\right],\end{split}

with

u_{h}^{n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{v}}u_{l}^{n+1}\Phi_{l},\quad p_{h}^{n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{v}}p_{i}^{n+1}\phi_{i},

where

\{\Phi_{l}\}
are the linear basis functions for displacements,
\{\phi_{i}\}
are the linear basis functions for pressure, and
N_{v}
is the number of vertices on the grid
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Each implicit time step requires solving a nonlinear system. To linearize, we will use a Picard iteration in the form

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\tau}\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n+1,(m)}&\alpha D_{h}^{n+1,(m)}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}&\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}-p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}-u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}\\
&+\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{n+1,(m)}&0\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n+1,(m)}&K_{h}^{n+1,(m)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}\\
u^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}F^{n+1,(m)}_{p,h}\\
F^{n+1,(m)}_{u,h}\end{bmatrix},\end{split}

(11)

where
(m)
denotes nonlinear iteration, and the process continues until a desired tolerance between successive iterates is obtained,
||p^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}-p^{n+1,(m)}_{h}||\leq e_{p}
and
||u^{n+1,(m+1)}_{h}-u^{n+1,(m)}_{h}||\leq e_{u}
.

Using an analogous linearization, we may also linearize (
8
) on the level of the time integration scheme using the solution from the previous time step to evaluate certain nonlinear quantities:

\frac{1}{\tau}\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n}&\alpha D_{h}^{n}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}-p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{n}&0\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n}&K_{h}^{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}F^{n}_{p,h}\\
F^{n}_{u,h}\end{bmatrix}.

(12)

This is a first-order semi-implicit scheme, which falls into the broader class of semi-implicit
[
39
]
integrators and nonlinear partitions
[
40
,
41
]
. Such an approach is a simple way to reduce computational costs by only solving a single linear system each time step, rather than resolving a fully nonlinear system, albeit typically at a cost of reduced stability and/or larger error constant.

4
Implicit-explicit scheme for time approximation

To approximate the nonlinear flow problem in time, we construct an additive EIN splitting, with implicit linear term that is fixed for all time.
We follow our previous work
[
38
]
, in which we developed a splitting scheme for the poroelasticity equations to construct stable solvers and decouple the displacement and pressure equations. While the present approach is still based on additive splitting, it is conceptually different. Here, we propose to use additive splitting to derive an EIN scheme.

4.1
Additive scheme

In the poroelasticity system (
6
), the second equation does not contain a time derivative, and the system is in the form of differential algebraic equations. To construct an additive scheme and analyze the stability of the resulting linearized formulation, we begin by differentiating the displacement equation with respect to time

\begin{split}&c(x,p)\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\alpha S(p)\frac{\partial(\nabla\cdot u)}{\partial t}-\nabla\cdot\kappa(x,p)\nabla p)=\tilde{f},\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\\
&-\frac{\partial(\nabla\cdot\sigma(u))}{\partial t}+\alpha\frac{\partial\big(S(p)\nabla p\big)}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial(\rho_{b}g)}{\partial t},\quad\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,\end{split}

(13)

with initial conditions
p=p^{0}
and
u=u^{0}
such that

-\nabla\cdot\sigma(u^{0})+\alpha\nabla p^{0}=\rho_{b}g,\quad x\in\Omega,\quad t=0.

Then for a semi-discrete system, we have the following matrix form for
U=(p_{h},u_{h})\in Q_{h}\times V_{h}
:

\mathbb{C}\frac{\partial U}{\partial t}+\mathbb{L}U=F,

(14)

with

\mathbb{C}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}&\alpha D_{h}\\
\alpha G_{h}&K_{h}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbb{L}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad F=\begin{bmatrix}F_{p,h}\\
\delta F_{u,h}\end{bmatrix}.

The resulting semi-discrete system can be viewed as an initial value problem for a system of ordinary differential equations
[
42
,
38
,
43
]
.

The standard implicit approximation for the problem (
14
) has the following form
for
U^{n+1}=(p^{n+1}_{h},u^{n+1}_{h})

\mathbb{C}^{n}\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{L}^{n}U^{n+1}=F^{n},

\mathbb{C}^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n+1}&\alpha D_{h}^{n+1}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n+1}&K_{h}^{n+1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbb{L}^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{n+1}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

To eliminate the need for reassembling the problem operator at each time step, we define the following additive representation of the problem operators
[
44
,
45
]

\begin{split}&\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{C}^{(lin)}+\mathbb{C}^{n,(nl)}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{(lin)}&\alpha D_{h}^{(lin)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{(lin)}&K_{h}^{(lin)}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n,(nl)}&\alpha D_{h}^{n,(nl)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n,(nl)}&K_{h}^{n,(nl)}\end{bmatrix},\\
&\mathbb{L}^{n}=\mathbb{L}^{(lin)}+\mathbb{L}^{n,(nl)}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{(lin)}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}A_{h}^{n,(nl)}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

where
M^{(lin)}
,
D^{(lin)}
,
G^{(lin)}
,
A^{(lin)}
and
K^{(lin)}
are linear operators and
M^{(nl)}
,
D^{(nl)}
,
G^{(nl)}
,
A^{(nl)}
and
K^{(nl)}
is the nonlinear residual, which depends on current solution
(p^{n}_{h},u^{n}_{h})
and should be updated at each iteration, i.e.,

\begin{split}&M^{n,(nl)}_{h}=M^{n}_{h}-M^{(lin)}_{h},\quad D^{n,(nl)}_{h}=D^{n}_{h}-D^{(lin)}_{h},\quad G^{n,(nl)}_{h}=G^{n}_{h}-G^{(lin)}_{h},\\
&A^{n,(nl)}_{h}=A^{n}_{h}-A^{(lin)}_{h},\quad K^{n,(nl)}_{h}=K^{n}_{h}-K^{(lin)}_{h}.\end{split}

Such additive representation separates the nonlinear parts, and by approximating it explicitly, we obtain a linear system without updating the operator at each time layer.

Then, the proposed implicit-explicit scheme can be written as a three-step scheme

\mathbb{C}^{(lin)}\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\tau}+\mathbb{C}^{n,(nl)}\frac{U^{n}-U^{n-1}}{\tau}+\mathbb{L}^{(lin)}U^{n+1}+\mathbb{L}^{n,(nl)}U^{n}=F^{n},

or

\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{(lin)}&\alpha D_{h}^{(lin)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{(lin)}&K_{h}^{(lin)}\end{bmatrix}&\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}-p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}-u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n,(nl)}&\alpha D_{h}^{n,(nl)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{n,(nl)}&K_{h}^{n,(nl)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n}_{h}-p^{n-1}_{h}\\
u^{n}_{h}-u^{n-1}_{h}\end{bmatrix}\\
&+\begin{bmatrix}\tau A_{h}^{(lin)}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\tau A_{h}^{n,(nl)}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tau F_{p,h}^{n}\\
F_{u,h}^{n}-F_{u,h}^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(15)

The implementation of the scheme (
15
) at each time iteration can be written as follows

\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{(lin)}+\tau A_{h}^{(lin)}&\alpha D_{h}^{(lin)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{(lin)}&K_{h}^{(lin)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n+1}_{h}\\
u^{n+1}_{h}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}b^{n}_{p,h}\\
b^{n}_{u,h}\end{bmatrix}

(16)

with

\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix}b^{n}_{p,h}\\
b^{n}_{u,h}\end{bmatrix}&=\begin{bmatrix}\tau F_{p,h}\\
F_{u,h}^{n}-F_{u,h}^{n-1}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{(lin)}&\alpha D_{h}^{(lin)}\\
\alpha G_{h}^{(lin)}&K_{h}^{(lin)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{n}_{h}\\
u^{n}_{h}\end{bmatrix}\\
&-\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{n,(nl)}&\alpha D_{h}^{n,(nl)}\\

Here, at each iteration, we have a linear system, where the matrix does not depend on the current solution. Therefore, the matrix is constructed only once, and by pre-inverting/pre-factorizing or pre-initializing a solver, we can significantly accelerate the simulations.
The main question in this implicit-explicit approximation is how to split the operators to ensure the unconditional stability of the scheme.

4.2
Stability analysis

Traditionally, splitting schemes for the poroelasticity system have been developed and analyzed by separating the displacement and pressure calculations, allowing each variable to be solved independently. Common approaches include the undrained split, fixed-stress split, drained split, and fixed-strain split methods, whose stability properties have been studied extensively in the literature
[

47

It has been shown that when the coupling terms are treated explicitly, whether through implicit–explicit methods or sequential implicit schemes, the method can suffer from stability issues, with stability only under certain conditions on the model parameters, and an additional stabilization term is required in some cases. Our proposed approach differs from traditional splitting methods for poroelasticity and is specifically designed to address nonlinearities, where we treat part of the coupling implicitly to avoid these stability problems.

5
Two-grid solver

This section outlines the construction of the two-grid solver for the unsaturated poroelasticity problem, focusing on its two main components: (1)
the construction of the coarse grid approximation and (2) the design of the coupled smoothing iterations.

Among existing solvers, the multigrid method is one of the most efficient solvers for large linear systems of equations. The key idea of the multigrid method is to accelerate convergence by solving the system on a coarse grid, aiming for convergence rates that are independent of the grid size. The performance of the multigrid method highly depends on the ability of the coarse grid solver to represent solution error that is not attenuated by smoothing iterations. Construction of the multigrid method for coupled nonlinear multiphysics problems in heterogeneous settings of the parameters is not trivial.
Although a full grid hierarchy is ideal for computational efficiency, the two-grid setting serves as the foundation for full multigrid techniques and still offers significant speedup over single-level methods.

We choose eigenvectors

and

Multiscale basis functions for displacements.

We solve the following local spectral problem for

in the local domain with a fine-scale resolution

where

\begin{split}&K_{h}^{(lin),\omega_{l}}=[b_{ij}],\quad b_{ij}=\int_{\omega_{l}}\bar{\sigma}(\Phi_{i}):\varepsilon(\Phi_{j})dx,\\
&S_{h}^{K,\omega_{l}}=[s^{K}_{ij}],\quad s^{K}_{ij}=\int_{\omega_{l}}(\bar{\lambda}+2\bar{\mu})\Phi_{i}\Phi_{j}dx.\end{split}

We choose eigenvectors

&P_{u}=(\Psi^{\omega_{1}}_{1}\ldots\Psi^{\omega_{1}}_{M^{1,u}}\ldots\Psi^{\omega_{N^{H}_{v}}}_{1}\ldots\Psi^{\omega_{N^{H}_{v}}}_{M^{N^{H}_{v},u}}).\end{split}

(25)

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}L_{H}^{(lin)}=P^{T}L_{h}^{(lin)}P&=\begin{bmatrix}P_{p}^{T}&0\\
0&P_{u}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}M_{h}^{(lin)}+\tau A_{h}^{(lin)}&\alpha D_{h}^{(lin)}\\

We will use a direct solver to solve the coarse-scale problem.
For smoothing iterations, we consider the classical pointwise smoothers Jacobi and Gauss-Seidel, and further propose a coupled blockwise smoother.

5.2
Smoothing iterations

In this work, we construct coupled blockwise smoothing iterations where the matrix blocks are related to the coarse grid. This approach is based on our previous work on online multiscale space enrichment or local-residual-based online multiscale correction
[
55
,
57
,
61
]
. It has been shown that the residual-based online calculations incorporate the distant information and improve multiscale space solution.
The online correction approach is related to the domain decomposition method with a subdomain partition aligned to the coarse grid cells or multiscale basis function’s supports
[
62
,
63
]
. This method is also closely related to the family of overlapping Schwarz and Vanka-type smoothers with an element-wise patch and a vertex-wise patch, but on the coarse grid
[
64
]
.

We start with defining subdomains based on the coarse grid that effectively balances computational efficiency with inter-subdomain communication, and reduces errors in a coupled way for our poroelasticity problem.
In our approach, a coarse grid is aligned with coarse facets, such that
K_{i}\cap K_{j}=E_{ij}
. Then the computational domain

K_{i}
by adding
o

(

\omega_{l}

7
Conclusion

In this work, we consider a coupled nonlinear system of equations that describe an unsaturated flow problem in heterogeneous poroelastic media. We derive a standard implicit scheme for the coupled system and then propose an implicit-explicit formulation, in which the stiff components of the problem are represented by a single linear operator that is treated implicitly. Careful construction of the additive splitting approach is presented for the unsaturated poroelasticity problem with the van Genuchten nonlinearity model and power-low for elastic modulus. We show that the proposed implicit-explicit scheme is unconditionally stable in the linear stability sense and enables an efficient implementation. Next, based on the proposed linearization, we develop a multiscale two-grid solver with coupled overlapping Schwarz smoothing iterations. Accurate coarse grid approximation is constructed based on an enrichment of the linear coarse space with local spectral basis functions for both pressure and elasticity equations. A coupled smoothing iteration is proposed based on the overlapping Vanka approach and defining blocks relative to a coarse grid (oversampled coarse cell and local support of multiscale basis functions). Although the block smoothers present a significant computational cost, similar to the multiscale basis functions, by combining with the implicit-explicit formulation we are able to precompute these quantities once “offline” for a full nonlinear simulation. A numerical study is presented for a two-dimensional nonlinear coupled test problem of unsaturated flow in heterogeneous poroelastic media.

For the conservation of the solid phase, we have
[

\begin{split}0&=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big((1-\phi)\rho_{s}\big)+\nabla\cdot\big((1-\phi)\rho_{s}q_{s}\big)\approx(1-\phi)\frac{\partial\rho_{s}}{\partial t}-\rho_{s}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+(1-\phi)\rho_{s}\nabla\cdot q_{s}\\

&=-C_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{C_{m}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{v}}{\partial t}-\alpha p\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}-\alpha S\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\phi p\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}+\phi S\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}\Big)+\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{v}}{\partial t}\\

We note that classic smoothers are pointwise, and the proposed approach is in a class of blockwise smoothers where we can apply subdomain coloring and perform fractional stepping in the group of non-overlapped subdomains corresponding to each color.

We observe that the classic pointwise smoothers perform poorly for the considered coupled system, especially for a smaller number of basis functions
M=1,2
. Jacobi diverges or fails to converge within 500 iterations in almost all cases. Gauss-Seidel improves upon this but still exhibits high iteration counts and solve times.
We see that the proposed Vanka-type smoothers outperform pointwise smoothers across all configurations, particularly with multicoloring subiterations. Among Vanka variants, VK2 frequently achieves the lowest iteration counts and solve times.
We also note that multicoloring significantly enhances convergence performance, especially for VK-type smoothers. Transitioning from 1-color (additive) to 2- or 4-color schemes consistently reduces iteration counts and wall-clock time. The benefit is most noticeable for smaller coarse spaces corresponding to lower values of
M
, where standard smoothers struggle.
Moreover, as
M
increases, both the number of iterations and the solve times decrease across most smoothing strategies.
We observe a clear dependence of the solution time on a number of iterations. By enlarging coarse space with a larger number of spectral basis functions, we expect a more accurate solution that directly affects the number of iterations.

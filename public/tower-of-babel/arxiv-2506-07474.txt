Erdős-Kac type theorem for the number of scattering geodesics on modular surface
Sudhir Pujahari, Punya Plaban Satpathy

Abstract
. In 1917, Hardy and Ramanujan showed that if ω ⁡ ( n ) \omega(n) is the number of distinct prime factors of a randomly chosen positive integer n , n, then the normal order of ω ⁡ ( n ) \omega(n) is log ⁡ log ⁡ n . \log\log\,n. This led Erdős and Kac to prove their celebrated result showing a Gaussian behaviour for ω ⁡ ( n ) . \omega(n). In this article we prove an Erdős-Kac kind result for the number of scattering geodesics on the modular surface with a common sojourn time.

1.
Introduction and main results

In 1917, Hardy and Ramanujan
[
5
]
showed that if we randomly choose a natural number
n
, it has “roughly”
\log\log n
many distinct prime factors. Motivated by the result of Hardy and Ramanujan, Erdős and Kac
[
2
]
showed that if
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
n
, then
\omega(n)
is normally distributed with mean and variance
\log\log n
.
In other words, they showed that the random variable

\frac{\omega(n)-\log\log n}{\sqrt{\log\log n}}

has a normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
1
. This result is famously known in the literature as the Erdős-Kac Theorem.
The above studies ignited the development of a new branch of mathematics, known as Probabilistic number theory. Since the proof of the Erdős-Kac Theorem, a significant number of similar results have been obtained for different mathematical objects.
In
[
7
]
, Murty, Murty and Pujahari, proved an all-purpose Erdős-Kac theorem. For a more detailed account, the reader is referred to
[
7
]
. In this article, we prove an Erdős-Kac type theorem for the number of scattering geodesics on the modular surface with a common sojourn time.
To state our main result, we recall the following notions:

The object in which we are interested is the modular surface defined as the quotient space

It is a standard result that

Consider the natural projection map

\mathcal{M}^{T_{0}}_{c}

\mathcal{M}

1.2.
Scattering geodesics in
\mathcal{M}

We recall the following definition of scattering geodesics from
[
4
]
;

Definition 1.1
.

A geodesic
\gamma:=\gamma(t)
in
\mathcal{M}
is called a scattering geodesic if it is contained in
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}^{T_{0}}
for both large positive and large negative times
t

Further, the associated sojourn time
l_{\gamma}
is defined as the total time elapsed from the time the geodesic
\gamma
first enters the compact core
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{T_{0}}
to the time it leaves
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{T_{0}}
permanently.

It follows from the work of Guillemin [Corollary 2,Theorem B1,
[
4
]
] that
for a fixed
T_{0}\gg 0
, there are a countable number of scattering geodesics in
\mathcal{M}
.
Recently, we

\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{G}

Construction of the Set
\mathcal{G}

For convenience of the reader, we recall the construction of a subset
\mathcal{G}\subset[0,1)
of the rational numbers from
[
8
, §1.4]
. We start as follows.

For each integer
q\geq 2
, define

I_{q}:=\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\mid 1\leq p<q,\ \gcd(p,q)=1\},

and let
\phi(q)=|I_{q}|
denote Euler’s totient function. Next, define the set

(1.2)

S_{q}:=\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\mid 1\leq p<q,\ p^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{q}\},

and let
s_{q}:=|S_{q}|
denote its cardinality.

For each
p\in I_{q}
, let
y_{p,q}\in I_{q}
be the unique element satisfying
py_{p,q}\equiv-1\pmod{q}
. Define

C_{q}:=\{p\in I_{q}\mid p\neq y_{p,q}\}.

The elements of
C_{q}
form unordered pairs
\boxed{p_{1},p_{2}}
such that
p_{1}p_{2}\equiv-1\pmod{q}
.

Define the subset

(1.3)

C^{*}_{q}:=\left\{\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\mid\boxed{p_{1},p_{2}}\text{ is a pair in }C_{q}\right\},

which has cardinality

(1.4)

|C^{*}_{q}|=\frac{1}{2}(\phi(q)-s_{q}).

Set
\mathcal{G}_{1}:=\{0\}
. For
q\geq 2
, define

(1.5)

\mathcal{G}_{q}:=\left\{\frac{p}{q}\mid p\in C^{*}_{q}\sqcup S_{q}\right\}.

The number of elements in
\mathcal{G}_{q}
is given by

|\mathcal{G}_{q}|=\frac{1}{2}(\phi(q)+s_{q}).

Finally, with all the above notations, we define the set
\mathcal{G}\subset[0,1)
of rational numbers by the disjoint union

(1.6)

\mathcal{G}:=\bigsqcup_{q=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{q}.

\bar{\gamma}_{w_{1}},\bar{\gamma}_{w_{2}}

(1)

q_{1}=q_{2}=q

(2)

q

p_{1}p_{2}+1

Given a scattering geodesic
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}
, it can be lifted to a geodesic
\bar{\gamma}
in
\mathbb{H}
connecting a rational point
w
to
i\infty

can then be used to establish a bijection between the set of scattering geodesics

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of scattering geodesics in
\mathcal{M}
. For each integer

\mathcal{S}_{q}\subset\mathcal{S}
consisting of

n_{q}:=\frac{\phi(q)+s_{q}}{2}

s_{1}=1

s_{q}
denotes the cardinality of the set

\left\{p\mid 1\leq p<q,\ p^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{q}\right\}.

Moreover, the full set
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}=\bigsqcup_{q=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{q}.

We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2

For any

\lim_{x\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{x}\bigg|\bigg\{1\leq q\leq x\mid\omega(n_{q})-\frac{1}{2}(\log\log x)^{2}\leq\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}(\log\log x)^{3/2}\bigg\}\bigg|=\Phi(a).

Our first result involves understanding the asymptotics of

Proposition 2.2

Consider the L-function

We can rewrite

Note that
\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal{O}}\frac{1}{q^{s}}
and therefore

converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
and consequently a holomorphic function in the region
\Re(s)>1

. In order to understand the nature of this singularity at

\displaystyle(F(s))^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+2^{-s})^{2}\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}(1-p^{-s})^{-2}

\displaystyle=(1+2^{-s})^{2}\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}\frac{1-p^{-2s}}{(1-p^{-s})^{2}}\bigg(\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}(1-p^{-2s})^{-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(1+2^{-s})^{2}\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}\frac{1+p^{-s}}{1-p^{-s}}\bigg(\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}(1-p^{-2s})^{-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(1+2^{-s})\prod_{p}(1+p^{-s})\bigg(\prod_{p\equiv 1\bmod{4}}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{p\equiv 3\bmod{4}}(1+p^{-s})^{-1}\bigg)G(s)

\displaystyle=(1+2^{-s})L(s,\chi)\prod_{p}\frac{1-p^{-2s}}{1-p^{-s}}G(s)

We know that

Next we state the Erdős-Kac theorem for the Euler’s
\phi
-function proved by Erdős and Pomerance in
[
3
]
. We use this in the next section to prove the main theorem.

Proposition 2.4
.

(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
)
For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
we have,

\lim_{x\longrightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{x}\bigg|\bigg\{1\leq q\leq x\mid\omega(\phi(q))-\frac{1}{2}(\log\log x)^{2}\leq\frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}(\log\log x)^{3/2}\bigg\}\bigg|=\Phi(a).

where
\displaystyle\Phi(a):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{a}e^{-y^{2}/2}dy

3.
Proofs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. To prove the claim, for sufficiently large
x
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\displaystyle I(q,x,a)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle and\ I_{1}(q,x,a)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\frac{\omega(n_{q})-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

f(x):=\tfrac{1}{2}(\log\log x)^{2},

and

g(x):=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\log\log x)^{3/2}
.

We get the required result by comparing the following sums;

S(x,a)=\sum_{q\leqslant x}I(q,x,a),\ and\ S_{1}(x,a)=\sum_{q\leqslant x}I_{1}(q,x,a).

By Proposition
2.4
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{S(x,a)}{x}=\Phi(a)\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathbb{R}.

It therefore suffices to show

(3.1)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{S_{1}(x,a)}{x}=\Phi(a),

which is equivalent to show

(3.2)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{T(x,a)}{x}=0,\ \text{where}\ T(x,a):=S(x,a)-S_{1}(x,a).

In order to estimate
T(x,a)
, we split its underlying sum into two parts, one corresponding to
q\in E(x)
and the other corresponding to
q\notin E(x)
.

Case (I): Contribution from
q\in E(x)
.
Recall that

E(x)=\{1\leqslant q\leqslant x:|\omega(n_{q})-\omega(\phi(q))|>1\},

and by Proposition
2.3
we have
|E(x)|=o(x)
as
x\to\infty
. Define

T_{1}(x,a):=\sum_{{q\leqslant x}\atop{q\in E(x)}}\bigl(I(q,x,a)-I_{1}(q,x,a)\bigr).

Since
|I(q,x,a)-I_{1}(q,x,a)|\leqslant 1
for every
q,
we have

|T_{1}(x,a)|\leqslant|E(x)|=o(x)\mbox{ as }x\longrightarrow\infty.

Case (II): Contribution from
q\notin E(x)
.
Let

T_{2}(x,a)=\sum_{{q\leqslant x}\atop{q\notin E(x)}}\bigl(I(q,x,a)-I_{1}(q,x,a)\bigr).

For
q\notin E(x)
we have
|\omega(n_{q})-\omega(\phi(q))|\leqslant 1
, so

\left|\frac{\omega(n_{q})-f(x)}{g(x)}-\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{g(x)}.

Hence, for
q\leq x,q\notin E(x)
we have

(3.3)

\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}-\frac{1}{g(x)}\leq\frac{\omega(n_{q})-f(x)}{g(x)}\leq\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}+\frac{1}{g(x)}

Now

T_{2}(x,a)=\sum_{q\in E_{1}(x,a)}(I-I_{1})+\sum_{q\in E_{2}(x,a)}(I-I_{1}),

where

\displaystyle E_{1}(x,a)

\displaystyle:=\{q\leqslant x:\,q\notin E(x),\,I(q,x,a)=1,\,I_{1}(q,x,a)=0\},

\displaystyle E_{2}(x,a)

\displaystyle:=\{q\leqslant x:\,q\notin E(x),\,I(q,x,a)=0,\,I_{1}(q,x,a)=1\}.

We now justify that both
E_{1}(x,a)
and
E_{2}(x,a)
lie within the following set defined by

\left\{q\leqslant x:a-\frac{1}{g(x)}<\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a+\frac{1}{g(x)}\right\}.

Indeed, suppose
q\in E_{1}(x,a)
. Then
I(q,x,a)=1
and
I_{1}(q,x,a)=0
, which implies

\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\omega(n_{q})-f(x)}{g(x)}>a.

These two inequalities along with (
3.3
) tell us that
q\in E_{1}(x,a)
implies

a-\frac{1}{g(x)}<\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a.

A similar argument for
q\in E_{2}(x,a)
gives

a<\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a+\frac{1}{g(x)}.

Hence, in both cases, we have

a-\frac{1}{g(x)}<\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a+\frac{1}{g(x)},

and thus both
E_{1}(x,a)
and
E_{2}(x,a)
are contained in

\left\{q\leqslant x:a-\frac{1}{g(x)}<\frac{\omega(\phi(q))-f(x)}{g(x)}\leqslant a+\frac{1}{g(x)}\right\}.

Using Proposition
2.4
, we have

|E_{1}(x,a)|+|E_{2}(x,a)|=o(x)\ \text{as $x\to\infty$},

and thus
T_{2}(x,a)=o(x)\ \text{as $x\to\infty$}
.

Combining case (I) and case (II) yields

T(x,a)=T_{1}(x,a)+T_{2}(x,a)=o(x)\ \text{as $x\to\infty$}.

This establishes (
3.2
) and completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
. ∎

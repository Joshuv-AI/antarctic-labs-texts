Topology of projective Tate-Shafarevich twists
David Zhiyuan Bai

Abstract
. A Tate-Shafarevich twist X ϕ → B X^{\phi}\to B of a fibration X → B X\to B modifies it by a 1 1 -cocycle of flows of vector fields relative to the base, locally in the analytic topology. Saccà conjectured that the total spaces of two projective Lagrangian fibrations related by such a twist are deformation-equivalent. Assuming that the class of the twist is torsion (which is often equivalent to the twist being realizable in the étale topology), we show that there is an isomorphism H ∗ ​ ( X , ℚ ) ≅ H ∗ ​ ( X ϕ , ℚ ) H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{\ast}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q}) of graded vector spaces that respects (1) the Hodge structures and (2) the Hodge-Riemann pairing. Consequently, the rational Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki lattices of these two spaces are Hodge-similar. Assuming further that B B is smooth, that the fibers of the fibrations are reduced outside of a locus of codimension 2 2 in B B , and that the integral homology classes of a general fiber in both spaces are primitive, we show Saccà’s conjecture using a recent result of Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky. We also show that Beauville-Mukai systems for primitive classes satisfy the last condition.



0.
Introduction

We work over
\mathbb{C}
and, unless otherwise stated, endow the spaces we consider with the analytic (Euclidean) topology.

0.1.
A conjecture of Saccà

Given a fibration
\pi:X\to B
and a
1
-cocycle
\phi
of relative automorphisms that are locally given by flows of vertical vector fields (
i.e.
sections of
\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B}
), one can re-glue the fibration with transition functions modified by
\phi
.
This is called the
Tate-Shafarevich twist
of
X
by
\phi
.
See §
1.1
for a precise formulation and discussion of this construction.

In general, there is no reason
X^{\phi}
should even be homeomorphic to
X
(Example
1.2
), even though the fibrations have isomorphic fibers.
Nonetheless, when
X
is an irreducible hyper-Kähler variety (in which case its fibrations are called Lagrangian fibrations), the situation becomes very interesting:
It turns out that any twist of a fibration on
X
is still holomorphic symplectic, hence hyper-Kähler (in fact, irreducible hyper-Kähler) as soon as it is Kähler.

There is only a small set of known deformation types of irreducible hyper-Kähler varieties.
One then wonders if it would ever be possible to reach new deformation types by performing a Tate-Shafarevich twist on a Lagrangian fibration.

Conjecture 1
.

Total spaces of projective Lagrangian fibrations that are related by a Tate-Shafarevich twist are deformation-equivalent.

Saccà considered this in the following context:
In
[
33
, Theorem 6.13]
, she started with a Lagrangian fibration with integral fibers and constructed the relative compactified Albanese of it, which is a Tate-Shafarevich twist of the original fibration that admits a section.
From there, she conjectured:

Conjecture 2
.

The relative compactified Albanese of a Lagrangian fibration with integral fibers stays in the same deformation class.

She provided evidence for this by showing that the singular cohomology groups of these two spaces coincide as
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures by applying Ngô’s support theorem
[
30
]
.

The statement of Conjecture
1
is a generalization of this that relaxes both the condition on fibers (allowing non-integral fibers) and the topology (analytic instead of étale) in question.

0.2.
Cohomology groups

We generalize the observation of Saccà on singular cohomology groups to arbitrary (projective) Tate-Shafarevich twists.

From now on the base
B
will always be assumed to be normal and projective.

Theorem 0.1
.

Suppose
X\to B
is a projective fibration and
X^{\phi}\to B
is a Tate-Shafarevich twist of it.
Suppose in addition that
X^{\phi}
is also projective.
Then we have
H^{k}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{k}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q})
as
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures for each
k
.

This is proved in §
1.2
.
We do not need the fibration to be Lagrangian.

Note that this theorem is false if the projectivity assumption on
X^{\phi}
is dropped.
Indeed, in
[
2
]
, it was observed that the second Betti number can be different for non-Kähler twists of a Lagrangian fibration.
An explicit example given by the Bogomolov-Guan manifold is described in
[
2
, Remark 5.3.13]
.

Theorem
0.1
holds under the projectivity assumption because it allows us to use the decomposition theorem for perverse sheaves.
We in fact show:

Theorem 0.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
0.1
, there exists natural isomorphisms
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})
of mixed Hodge modules for each
k
.

This yields a non-canonical isomorphism
\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbf{R}\pi^{\phi}_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
, from which the previous theorem follows.

0.3.
Lagrangian fibrations

Let us now impose two conditions for the rest of our theorems.
Firstly, we would like the original fibration to be projective and Lagrangian,
i.e.
the total space to be an irreducible hyper-Kähler variety.
A consequence of this is that a general fiber of the fibration would be an abelian variety, thus the sheaf
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0}
is actually a sheaf of commutative groups.
It then makes sense to define the Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sha_{X/B}=H^{1}(B,\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B})
which parameterizes Tate-Shafarevich twists.

The second condition we impose is that the twist
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}
is required to be torsion.
This is expected to be the case for when the twist is algebraic,
i.e.
it can be achieved in the étale topology.
See §
4.2
for a discussion of this condition.

As a consequence, results in
[
2
]
would show that the twist
X^{\phi}
is also a projective Lagrangian fibration.

In this instance, we obtain the invariance of more structures on the singular cohomology.

Theorem 0.3
.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
is a projective Lagrangian fibration and
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}
is torsion.
Then there exists ample classes
\omega,\omega^{\phi}
on
X,X^{\phi}
respectively such that the isomorphism in Theorem
0.1
can be chosen to preserve the Hodge-Riemann bilinear form

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\omega}=\int(1+\omega+\omega^{2}+\cdots)\smile\alpha\smile\beta

associated to
\omega
and
\omega_{\phi}
.

This is obtained by studying, instead of the cohomology ring, the associated graded ring with respect to the perverse filtration.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
is a fibration.
The perverse filtration on the singular cohomology of
X
is defined as the images of

P_{k}H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{Im}(H^{l}(B,{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\tau_{\leq k+\dim B}\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{l}(B,\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

Perverse filtrations for generically abelian fibrations have been studied intensively in the last decade.
Among many other examples, they are used in the study of the Hitchin fibration in relation to the
P=W
conjecture
[
12
,
29
,
19
,
28
]
, moduli spaces of one-dimensional sheaves on del Pezzo surfaces
[
25
,
24
,
31
]
, Lagrangian fibrations of compact hyper-Kähler manifolds
[
35
,
18
,
16
]
, and universal compactified Jacobians
[
4
]
.

The perverse filtration is called multiplicative if the cup product takes
P_{k}H^{l}\times P_{k^{\prime}}H^{l^{\prime}}
to
P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{l+l^{\prime}}
.
If this were the case, then the cup product descends to a product on the bigraded vector space

\mathbb{H}_{\pi}=\bigoplus_{k,l}\operatorname{Gr}_{k}^{P}H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})

making it a bigraded ring.
We call this the associated graded ring.

In general the associated graded ring is far from being isomorphic to the original cohomology ring.
However, the miracle in the case of a Lagrangian fibration is that not only is the perverse filtration always multiplicative, but there is actually a multiplicative splitting of the perverse filtration,
i.e.
we have an isomorphism

H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{H}_{\pi}

of graded rings (only the cohomology grading is considered on the latter).
This is the result of
[
35
, Appendix A]
.

Our strategy is then to show Theorem
0.3
for the analogous construction on
\mathbb{H}_{\pi}
.
This is easier because of the following:
The cup product on the level of complexes

\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}

is impossible to control knowing only local information (generally, maps between complexes are not sheafy,
i.e.
determined by values on an open cover).
However, if we fix a splitting

\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\bigoplus\mathcal{H}_{i}[-i]

with
\mathcal{H}_{i}\in\operatorname{Perv}(B)[-\dim B]
, then the cup product would induce maps
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{H}_{k+k^{\prime}}
.
We show that after taking global cohomology, this recovers the product

\operatorname{Gr}_{k}^{P}H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})\times\operatorname{Gr}_{k^{\prime}}^{P}H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Gr}_{k+k^{\prime}}^{P}H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})

on
\mathbb{H}_{\pi}
.

Using a construction in
[
2
]
, we show:

Theorem 0.4
.

There exists splittings of the respective perverse filtrations such that the maps
\mathcal{H}_{k}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{H}_{k+k^{\prime}}
coincide for
X
and
X^{\phi}
(with the identifications given by Theorem
0.2
) provided that one of
\mathcal{H}_{k},\mathcal{H}_{k^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{k+k^{\prime}}
is a local system.

This is shown in §
2.2
for a class of fibrations and twists satisfying certain conditions.
We explain in §
3.1
why our setup (projective Lagrangian fibrations under torsion twists) is in this class.

Consequently, the intersection pairing as well as the action of
H^{\ast}(B;\mathbb{Q})=H^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
are preserved under the isomorphism
\mathbb{H}_{\pi}\cong\mathbb{H}_{\pi^{\phi}}
of vector spaces.

We show in §
2.3
that the certain actions by relatively ample classes are also preserved under this isomorphism.
When combined, they show Theorem
0.3
as explained in §
3.2
.

As a consequence of Theorem
0.3
, we see that

Corollary 0.5
.

There is a Hodge similitude
(H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q}),q_{X})\to(H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q}),q_{X^{\phi}})
where
q
is the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF) form.

A natural question is whether we can compare the integral BBF lattices.
This turns out to be hard:
Indeed, the map in the corollary cannot preserve the integral lattices in general.
Nevertheless, we discuss in §
3.3
some partial results in this front.

0.4.
Fibrations of Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky type

Let us add further topological constraints on our fibration.
From now on, we assume in addition that:

•

the base
B
is smooth;

•

the fibers are reduced outside of a locus of codimension
2
in
B
;

•

the integral homology classes of a general fiber in
X
and
X^{\phi}
are primitive.

Theorem 0.6
.

Under these further constraints, Conjecture
1
is true.

The main ingredient is a recent theorem of Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky
[
9
]
on degenerate twistor deformations.
Their theorem says that given any Lagrangian fibration, there is a member in its degenerate twistor family that admits a meromorphic section.

Starting from our setup, we perform such a deformation on
X
and
X^{\phi}
respectively.
We show that the resulting spaces are still projective Lagrangian fibrations related by a torsion twist, which in the situation in question implies that the twist can be realized in the étale topology.
Since the new spaces now have rational sections, we can produce open subschemes in them that are isomorphic by comparing the abelian schemes over a suitable open subscheme of the base.
As birational hyper-Kähler varieties are deformation-equivalent, we obtain Theorem
0.6
.
The details can be found in §
4.3
.

The last item in the assumption (primitivity of a fiber class) is a property interesting on its own.
For now, we are not able to decide whether every Lagrangian fibration with integral fibers satisfies this property.
If this were true, then Conjecture
2
follows from Theorem
0.6
.

We discuss in §
4.4
situations where this property holds for a Beauville-Mukai system.
They are fibrations of the form

M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to|L|

where
S
is a K3 surface,
L
is a big and nef line bundle on
S
, and
\chi
is an integer so that
(0,L,\chi)\in\tilde{H}(S;\mathbb{Z})
is primitive.
The space
M_{S}(0,L,\chi)
is the moduli space of stable sheaves on
S
with Mukai vector
(0,L,\chi)
, and the morphism is given by taking the Fitting support.

We assume
\dim|L|>2
.

Theorem 0.7
.

Suppose
L
is primitive (e.g. when
\dim|L|-1
is square-free), then a general fiber of the Beauville-Mukai system
M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to|L|
has primitive integral homology class.

Let us note that Conjecture
2
is already known for a large class of K3
[n]
-type Lagrangian fibrations including Beauville-Mukai systems.
See Remark
4.17
.

1.
Tate-Shafarevich twists and the decomposition theorem

1.1.
Recollections on Tate-Shafarevich twists

We are going to be interested in analytic Tate-Shafarevich twists as formulated in
[
2
]
.

For us, a fibration is a proper holomorphic surjection
\pi:X\to B
with connected fibers, where
X
is a connected complex manifold,
B
is a normal quasiprojective variety, and
0<\dim B<\dim X
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a fibration
\pi:X\to B
, the sheaf
T_{X/B}
of vertical vector fields is defined to be the kernel of
T_{X}\to\pi^{\ast}T_{B}/\operatorname{Tors}(\pi^{\ast}T_{B})
, where
\operatorname{Tors}(\pi^{\ast}T_{B})
is the torsion subsheaf of
\pi^{\ast}T_{B}
.

Denote by
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0}
the image of the exponential map
\exp:\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}
.

So
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0}
consists of those automorphisms that are flows of vertical vector fields.
Note also that
\operatorname{Tors}(\pi^{\ast}T_{B})=0
if
B
is smooth.

Given a
1
-cocycle
\phi=\{\phi_{ij}\}
with values in
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B}
over an open cover
\{U_{i}\}
(in the analytic topology) of
B
, one can form a new fibration
\pi^{\phi}:X^{\phi}\to B
by re-gluing
X_{i}=\pi^{-1}U_{i}
along the automorphisms
\phi_{ij}
.

Clearly,
\pi^{\phi}
is independent of modifications by a coboundary.

Definition 1.2
.

We call
\pi^{\phi}:X^{\phi}\to B
the Tate-Shafarevich twist of
\pi
by
\phi
.

Example 1.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}\to B
is a family of integral curves, then one can form the compactified Jacobian fibrations
\overline{\operatorname{Pic}}^{d}(\mathcal{C}/B)\to B
of different degrees
d
.
Let us assume that these spaces
\overline{\operatorname{Pic}}^{d}(\mathcal{C}/B)
are smooth.

All of them are related to each other via a Tate-Shafarevich twist.
Indeed, these families of compactified Jacobians of different degrees are isomorphic as soon as the curve family admits a section.
On the other hand, an arbitrary curve family of such kind always admits analytic local sections.
So these compactified Jacobians are isomorphic when restricted to an analytic open covering
\{U_{i}\}
of the base.

On double intersections
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, the difference between the choices of an analytic section on
U_{i}
versus that on
U_{j}
is measured precisely by an action of
\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathcal{C}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}/U_{i}\cap U_{j}}^{0}
, which lies in
\Gamma(U_{i}\cap U_{j},\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0})
.

Example 1.2
.

Consider
X=B\times\mathbb{P}^{r-1}\to B
.
Note that in this case
\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}
is simply the exponential map
\mathfrak{pgl}_{r}\to\operatorname{PGL}_{r}
, and is therefore surjective.
So any
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}\to B
is a Tate-Shafarevich twist of
X\to B
.

Note that
\mathbb{P}
is generally not even homeomorphic to
X
(by looking at the singular cohomology ring, for example).
Nevertheless, they do share the same Betti numbers.
We will further discuss this phenomenon soon.

Definition 1.3
.

The Tate-Shafarevich group for the fibration
\pi:X\to B
is
\Sha_{X/B}=H^{1}(B,\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B})
.

Remark 1.1
.

As shown by Example
1.2
,
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B}
can be noncommutative, in which case
\Sha_{X/B}
is to be understood in the framework of nonabelian cohomology, and is not a group in general.
That said,
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B}
is commutative whenever a generic fiber of the fibration is a complex torus, which is the case for our main situation of interest (namely, Lagrangian fibrations).

Example 1.3
.

Suppose
S\to B=\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an elliptic K3 surface with integral fibers and let
X=\overline{\operatorname{Pic}}^{0}(S/B)
.
Then the map
H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})\to H^{1}(B,\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0})=\Sha_{X/B}
is actually surjective.
In fact, as computed in
[
22
, Ch. 11]
,
\Sha_{X/B}\cong\operatorname{Br}(X)\cong\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}^{22-\rho(X)}
where
\rho(X)
is the Picard rank of
X
.

As an aside:
Take any
p\in B
and write
B^{0}=B\setminus\{p\}
and
X^{0}=\pi^{-1}B^{0}
.
Then
H^{1}(B^{0},\pi_{\ast}T_{X^{0}/B^{0}})=0
, which means that
X^{0}\cong(\pi^{\phi})^{-1}(B^{0})
as complex manifolds for any
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}
.
But of course a Tate-Shafarevich twist of
X
is generally not birational to
X
:
As exhibited in Example
1.1
, one can reach
\overline{\operatorname{Pic}}^{d}(S/B)
for any
d
, and they are usually not all birational to each other (unless
S\to B
has a section).

This gives a simple example of a non-proper complex manifold that admits multiple non-isomorphic algebraic structures.

Remark 1.2
.

Suppose
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B}
is commutative.
For any
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}
, we can naturally identify
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0}
with
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X^{\phi}/B}^{0}
, which gives an identification of
\Sha_{X/B}
with
\Sha_{X^{\phi}/B}
.
Under these isomorphisms, for any
\psi\in\Sha_{X/B}
, we have
(X^{\phi})^{\psi}=X^{\phi+\psi}
.

1.2.
Perverse sheaves

In
[
2
]
, in the occasion of a Lagrangian fibration, the second Betti number of
X
and
X^{\phi}
are compared.
An observation made there is that even when
X
is a Lagrangian fibration,
X^{\phi}
can have a second Betti number different from that of
X
, as a result of a mismatch between the differentials in the Leray spectral sequence.
We refer the reader to
[
2
, Remark 5.3.13]
for an explicit example.

Our purpose in this section is to point out that the situation improves drastically when a projectivity assumption is placed on both the fibration and its twist.
The difference it makes is the decomposition theorem, which shows that the
perverse
Leray spectral sequence takes a very simple form in this case – namely, it degenerates at
E_{2}
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
8
]
,
[
34
]
)
.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
is a projective morphism between varieties, then there exists a (non-canonical) splitting

\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\operatorname{IC}_{X}=\bigoplus_{k=-r}^{r}{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\operatorname{IC}_{X})[-k]

in the category of mixed Hodge modules.
Here,
r=r_{\pi}=\dim X\times_{B}X-\dim X
is the defect of semismallness of
\pi
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Suppose
\pi
and
\pi^{\phi}
are both projective.
Then for each
k
, there are isomorphisms

\Psi_{k}:{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})

of perverse sheaves.
Moreover, after restricting to each
U_{i}
, this isomorphism is the cohomology of

\psi^{i}:\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}}\cong\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}}

induced by the isomorphism
\pi^{-1}U_{i}\cong(\pi^{\phi})^{-1}U_{i}
relative to
U_{i}
.
In particular,
\Psi_{k}
is also an isomorphism of mixed Hodge modules.

Proof.

Since perverse sheaves satisfy smooth descent (see
[
3
, §3.7]
), it suffices to show that the local isomorphisms
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}})\to{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}})
glue, meaning that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}})|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{j}})_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{i}})_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}|_{U_{j}})|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}}

commutes.

Denote by
\bar{\pi}:\pi^{-1}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})=(\pi^{\phi})^{-1}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\to U_{i}\cap U_{j}
the common restriction of
\pi
and
\pi^{\phi}
.
The natural isomorphism
\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\to(\phi_{ij})_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
induces an isomorphism
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\bar{\pi}_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\to{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\bar{\pi}_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})
.
The commutativity of the diagram above amount to the claim that this induced isomorphism is the identity.

Write
\phi_{ij}=\exp(v)
for some vertical vector field
v\in\Gamma(U_{i}\cap U_{j},\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})
.
We then have a representation
\mathbb{R}\to\operatorname{End}({}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\bar{\pi}_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}}))
sending
t
to the map induced by
\exp(tv)
.
The codomain being a finitely generated abelian group, the map must be constant.
So
\phi_{ij}
induces the same isomorphism as the identity.
∎

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
X
and
X^{\phi}
are both projective.
Then there is an isomorphism
H^{k}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{k}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q})
of Hodge structures for each
k
.

Proof.

The preceding proposition shows that the factors in Theorem
1.3
are the same for the fibration and its twist.
Taking global cohomology shows the claim.
∎

2.
Cohomology rings of twists

2.1.
Good twists

We turn our focus to specific kinds of Tate-Shafarevich twists.
As before we are interested in a pair of data
(\pi:X\to B,\phi)
consisting of a fibration
\pi:X\to B
and a
1
-cocycle
\phi=(\phi_{ij})
with values in
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{X/B}^{0}
over an open cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
B
.
In this section we will only consider the case where
X
is proper.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(\pi:X\to B,\phi)
is called
good
if:

(i) the morphism
\pi
(hence
\pi^{\phi}
) is equidimensional;

(ii) there exists relatively ample line bundles
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
and
L^{\phi}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X^{\phi})
such that
L|_{\pi^{-1}U_{i}}\cong L^{\phi}|_{(\pi^{\phi})^{-1}U_{i}}
for each
i
(in particular, both
\pi
and
\pi^{\phi}
are projective);

(iii) the perverse filtrations associated to
\pi
and
\pi^{\phi}
are multiplicative.

Under the assumptions, we have
r_{\pi}=\dim X-\dim B
.

Let us explain the last condition.
Recall that given an equidimensional projective fibration
\pi:X\to B
, we get a natural map
{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\tau_{\leq k}\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}
for each
k
, which induces an increasing filtration

P_{k}H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{Im}(H^{l}(B,{}^{\mathfrak{p}}\tau_{\leq k+\dim B}\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{l}(B,\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}}))

on
H^{l}(B,\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, known as the perverse filtration associated to
\pi
.
This filtration is said to be
multiplicative
if

P_{k}H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})\smile P_{k^{\prime}}H^{l^{\prime}}(X;\mathbb{Q})\subset P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{l+l^{\prime}}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

As a consequence of being multiplicative, the associated graded module of the perverse filtration on cohomology inherits a product structure from the cup product.
We therefore obtain a bigraded ring, with one grading given by the perverse filtration and the other given by the cohomology grading.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose the perverse filtration associated to a(n equidimensional projective) fibration
\pi:X\to B
is multiplicative.
The associated bigraded ring is defined by

\mathbb{H}_{\pi}=\bigoplus_{k,l}\operatorname{Gr}_{k}^{P}H^{l}(X;\mathbb{Q})

with its product, which we will denote by
\smile^{\mathbb{H}}
, induced by the cup product.

Remark 2.1
.

It should be noted that the ring
\mathbb{H}_{\pi}
may in general retain very little information about the cup product on
H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.
In particular, they are far from being isomorphic in general.
Indeed, this can already be seen when the fibration is the projectivization of a vector bundle (over a smooth variety), as the Chern classes of the vector bundle can no longer be obtained from this associated graded ring.

Such phenomenon also happens for generically abelian fibrations.
In
[
5
]
, the authors considered the compactified Jacobian over a family of integral curves with at worst simple nodes, and showed that the perverse filtration (which is still multiplicative in this case) does not admit a multiplicative splitting.

In these kinds of contexts, the associated graded ring
\mathbb{H}_{\pi}
usually behaves better than the cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.
For example, in the case of universal compactified Jacobians over
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
(
n\geq 1
), it was shown in
[
4
]
that the associated graded ring does not depend on the degree nor the stability condition, even though the cohomology ring does.

Nonetheless, there are situations where we can recover the cup product.
See Theorem
3.5
.

3.
Lagrangian fibrations

3.1.
Recollections on Lagrangian fibrations

Let us now specialize to the situation where
X
is a irreducible hyper-Kähler variety.

Definition 3.1
.

A compact Kähler manifold
X
is irreducible hyper-Kähler if it is simply connected, holomorphic symplectic, and
h^{2,0}(X)=1
.
Fibrations whose total space is irreducible hyper-Kähler are called Lagrangian fibrations.

Remark 3.1
.

To check that a holomorphic symplectic compact Kähler manifold is simply connected, it is (necessary and) sufficient to check that
h^{1}(X)=0
(see
[
38

).

For the rest of this article, we fix a projective Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
and a torsion class
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}

Torsion Tate-Shafarevich twists usually coincide with Tate-Shafarevich twists in the étale topology (see §
4.2
).

Let us show that
(\pi:X\to B,\phi)
is a good pair.

Write
m
for the order of
\phi
.
As observed in
[
2
, Lemma 3.0.2]
, one can choose
\phi
so that

kills the local cocycles already.
Based on this observation, it was proved that:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
2
, §3]
)
.

For any line bundle

on
X
, possibly after replacing
L
by a positive power, there exists a
1

unique up to
\mathcal{O}_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}^{\times}
.
In particular, they glue to a line bundle

on

, well-defined up to

.

This establishes an isomorphism

\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}/\pi^{\ast}\operatorname{Pic}(B)_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\operatorname{Pic}(X^{\phi})_{\mathbb{Q}}/(\pi^{\phi})^{\ast}\operatorname{Pic}(B)_{\mathbb{Q}}

sending
\pi
-ample line bundles to
\pi^{\phi}
-ample line bundles.

Remark 3.3
.

The ambiguity of
\operatorname{Pic}(B)_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be removed:
Call

the composition

L|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\to(\phi_{ij})_{\ast}L|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\to\cdots\to(\phi_{ij}^{m})_{\ast}L|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}=L_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}

which an element of

Then the maps

, possibly after replacing

This additional requirement allows an isomorphism

Corollary 3.4
.

The twist

is an irreducible hyper-Kähler variety, and one can find relatively ample

that are such that

Lagrangian fibrations are equidimensional by
[

.
One particularly nice property of Lagrangian fibrations is that the perverse filtration associated to it is as nice as possible as far as the cup product is concerned.

Theorem 3.5

(
[

The perverse filtration associated to a Lagrangian fibration is multiplicative.

Hence

3.2.
Topological invariants under twists

The results we obtained in the previous section specializes, in the case of a Lagrangian fibration, to the invariance of certain topological information of the total space under a torsion twist.

Proposition 3.6
.

There exists ample classes

on

Since

These means that we can assume, without loss of generality, that our choice of relatively ample line bundles

Generally, it can be impossible to find an isomorphism
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q})
coming from a splitting of the perverse filtrations that preserves both a multiple of the respective BBF forms and the integral cohomology, since any such isomorphism is Hodge.

Example 3.2
.

Recall in Example
1.3
that there are non-birational K3 surfaces related by a Tate-Shafarevich twist.
The corresponding Tate-Shafarevich class is torsion, for example by Theorem
4.4
.
So the integral BBF lattices cannot be Hodge isometric by the global Torelli theorem
[
32
]
.

So the present method does not seem to reach the full extent of the integral structure.
Let us briefly discuss the limited information that one can get.

As before, write
\kappa\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
for the pullback of an ample class on
B
.

Definition 3.3
.

The local BBF lattice for the Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
is the sublattice
\Lambda_{\pi}=\kappa^{\perp}\cap H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
equipped with the restriction of the BBF form.
The reduced local BBF lattice
\Lambda_{\pi}^{\prime}
has the same underlying
\mathbb{Z}
-module as
\Lambda_{\pi}
, but is equipped with the form
\lambda_{\pi}^{-1}q_{X}
with
\lambda_{\pi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
chosen so that
\Lambda_{\pi}^{\prime}
is integral and indivisible.

Lemma 3.10
.

We have
\mathbb{Q}\kappa=P_{0}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
and
\kappa^{\perp}=P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

The first statement is clear.
As for the second statement, note first that
q_{X}(\kappa)=0
.
Also, from Theorem
3.5
we know that the perverse filtration associated to a Lagrangian fibration is multiplicative, so for any
\alpha\in P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, we have

c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}q_{X}(\alpha+\beta)=\int(\alpha+\beta)^{2n}=\int\alpha^{2n}=c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}q_{X}(\alpha),

hence
q_{X}(\alpha,\beta)=0
.
Therefore
P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\subset\kappa^{\perp}
.

On the other hand, if the inclusion were strict, then since
P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
has codimension
1
in
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
we must have
\kappa^{\perp}=H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.
This however contradicts the nondegeneracy of
q_{X}
.
∎

In particular,
\Lambda_{\pi}
has corank
1
in
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
Now let

\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\mathbb{Z}}={}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})[-\dim B]={}^{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathbf{R}\pi_{\ast}^{\phi}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})[-\dim B]

with the identification given by Proposition
1.4
.
Similar to the argument of
[
3
, Lemma 3.8.3]
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{k}=0
if
k<0
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose
H^{3}(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}})
are torsion-free.
Then there is a Hodge isometry
\Lambda_{\pi}^{\prime}\cong\Lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}^{\prime}
, and we have
\lambda_{\pi}^{n}c_{X}=\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}^{n}c_{X^{\phi}}
.

Proof.

Consider the perverse Leray sequence for perverse sheaves over
\mathbb{Z}
which takes the form

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{q}^{\mathbb{Z}})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X;\mathbb{Z}).

After base-change to
\mathbb{Q}
, this recovers the corresponding Leray sequence for
\mathbb{Q}
coefficients, which we know from Theorem
1.3
is degenerate at
E_{2}
.
Hence all differentials in
E_{2}^{p,q}
have torsion images.

The spectral sequence converges to a filtration of
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
Let us denote it by
P_{k}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
for
k\leq 2
which, after extending the coefficient to
\mathbb{Q}
, recovers the perverse filtration on
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.

Since
H^{0}(\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion-free,
\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{2}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free and hence isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.
Consequently, we have

P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})=P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cap H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})=\kappa^{\perp}\cap H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})

by the preceding lemma.

As in the proof of the preceding lemma,
q_{X}(\kappa,-)
is zero on
P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, hence the form
q^{\omega}
descends to a quadratic form
\bar{q}^{\omega}
on
\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.

On the other hand, the isomorphism in Corollary
3.8
respects the perverse filtration by construction.
The isomorphism on the graded piece
\operatorname{Gr}_{1}^{P}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong\operatorname{Gr}_{1}^{P}H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Q})
comes from extending the isomorphism in Proposition
1.4
to
\mathbb{Q}
.
In particular, it preserves
H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}})^{\rm tf}
.

Since
H^{3}(\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion-free, it must be zero.
Therefore
\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})^{\rm tf}=H^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}})^{\rm tf}
.
We thus have a Hodge isometry

(\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})^{\rm tf},\bar{q}^{\omega})\cong(\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{1}H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Z})^{\rm tf},\bar{q}^{\omega^{\phi}}).

Now observe that there is a Hodge isometry
(\operatorname{Gr}^{P}_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})^{\rm tf},\bar{q}^{\omega})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(0)\cong(P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),q^{\omega})
, where
\mathbb{Z}(0)
is the rank
1
lattice with zero pairing.
Indeed, the
\mathbb{Z}(0)
factor is precisely given by
\mathbb{Q}\kappa\cap H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
So we actually have a Hodge isometry

(P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),q^{\omega})\cong(P_{1}H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Z}),q^{\omega^{\phi}}).

There is then a constant
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
such that
\mu q^{\omega}
and
\mu q^{\omega^{\phi}}
are both integral and indivisible on
P_{1}H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
and
P_{1}H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Z})
, respectively.
This induces a Hodge isometry
\Lambda_{\pi}^{\prime}\cong\Lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}^{\prime}
.

Moreover, we know that
\lambda_{\pi}\mu q^{\omega}=q_{X}
.
So

\langle 1,1\rangle_{\omega}=c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}q_{X}(\omega,\omega)^{n}=c_{X}\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}\lambda_{\pi}^{n}\mu^{n}q^{\omega}(\omega,\omega)^{n}.

But we also have
q_{\omega}(\omega,\omega)=\langle 1,1\rangle_{\omega}=\langle 1,1\rangle_{\omega^{\phi}}
.
So
\lambda_{\pi}^{n}c_{X}=\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}^{n}c_{X^{\phi}}
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

By
[
14
]
, a hyper-Kähler variety
X
has type K3
[2]
if and only if
c_{X}=1
and there exists classes
\ell,m\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
with
q_{X}(\ell)=0
and
q_{X}(\ell,m)=1
.

Suppose that our Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
satisfies the torsion-free conditions in the preceding proposition.
If
X
is of K3
[2]
type and the aforementioned classes
\ell,m
can already be found in
\Lambda_{\pi}
, then
X^{\phi}
must also be of K3
[2]
-type.

Indeed, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}=c_{X^{\phi}}=1
.
We have
\lambda_{\pi}=c_{X}=1
by assumptions, so
\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}^{n}c_{X^{\phi}}=1
.
Thus

3\left(\frac{q_{X^{\phi}}(\alpha)}{\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}}\right)^{2}=\int\alpha^{4}\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
\alpha\in H^{2}(X^{\phi};\mathbb{Z})
.
Since the BBF lattice is indivisible by definition, the greatest common factor of all
q_{X^{\phi}}(\alpha)
is
2
.
Therefore
\lambda_{\pi^{\phi}}=1
, hence
c_{X^{\phi}}=1
.

4.
Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky fibrations

4.1.
The identity component of
\Sha

From now on, we assume that the base
B
is smooth.
This forces
B
to be isomorphic to a projective space.

There is one part of the group
\Sha
where twists by its elements are already a deformation of the original fibration.

Definition 4.1
.

We denote by
\Sha^{0}_{X/B}\subset\Sha_{X/B}
the image of the map

H^{1}(\exp):H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})\to H^{1}(B,\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B})=\Sha.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
1
, Corollary 2.6, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

There is a holomorphic family (the “Tate-Shafarevich family”) of fibrations over
H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})
, such that its fiber over
t\in H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})
is the twisted fibration
\pi^{\phi}:X^{\phi}\to B
where
\phi=H^{1}(\exp)(t)
.

This holds true without the Lagrangian assumption.
In the Lagrangian case, this is a one-dimensional family:
We have an isomorphism
H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})\cong H^{1,1}(B)\cong\mathbb{C}
by
[
1
, Corollary 2.6]
.

There is another natural
\mathbb{C}
-family of Lagrangian fibrations starting from a given one (satisfying our stated assumptions), called a degenerate twistor deformation.

We will not need a technical definition of degenerate twistor deformations.
Interested reader may confer
[
39
]
.
Roughly, given a choice of a holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma
on
X
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
, one can associate an integrable complex structure
I_{t}
with the
\mathbb{C}
-valued
2
-form
\sigma_{t}=\sigma+t\pi^{\ast}\kappa
(where
\kappa
is the class of a hyperplane in
B
), so that
\sigma_{t}
is holomorphic symplectic with respect to
I_{t}
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
39
]
)
.

The map
\pi:(X,I_{t})\to B
is holomorphic and Lagrangian with respect to
\sigma_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}
.
And the biholomorphism classes of its fibers are independent of
t
.

Moreover, one can put an integrable complex
I_{\rm tw}
structure on
X\times\mathbb{C}
such that:

(i) the projection to
\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic;

(ii) the restriction of the complex structure to the fiber over
t\in\mathbb{C}
is
I_{t}
.

The family
(X\times\mathbb{C},I_{\rm tw})
is called the degenerate twistor family.
The complex manifolds in this family are all Kähler (
[
36
]
).

Since we have chosen
\kappa
to be a hyperplane class on
B
, the base for the degenerate twistor family can be naturally identified with
H^{1,1}(B)
.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Under the isomorphism
H^{1}(B,\pi_{\ast}T_{X/B})\cong H^{1,1}(B)
, the degenerate twistor family is isomorphic to the Tate-Shafarevich family.

4.2.
Torsions in
\Sha
and algebraic twists

Let us discuss the role of torsion elements in
\Sha
.
We have already mentioned Theorem
3.2
that says, among other things, that twists of projective Lagrangian fibrations by torsion elements are projective.
There is a partial converse to this.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
2
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
\Sha^{\prime}\subset\Sha
be the subgroup consisting of elements those image in
\Sha/\Sha^{0}
is torsion.
Then for
\phi\in\Sha^{\prime}
,
X^{\phi}
is projective if and only if
\phi
is torsion.

So torsion points should have some kind of interpretation within an algebraic context.
For a start, one need to make sense of what an algebraic twist actually is.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
23
]
; see also
[
33
]
)
.

Suppose fibers of the Lagrangian fibration are non-multiple,
i.e.
they each has at least one reduced component.
Then there is a connected group scheme
P=P_{\pi}\to B
whose analytification represents
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}^{0}_{X/B}
.

Note that this is
P^{\circ\circ}
in the notation of
[
23
]
.

This group scheme allows one to make sense of Tate-Shafarevich twists in the étale topology.
The construction is the same as before, except that instead of performing a holomorphic gluing in the analytic topology, we apply étale descent to the local data.
This gives us an algebraic space fibered over
B
.

Definition 4.2
.

The étale Tate-Shafarevich group for the fibration is
\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}=H_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{1}(X,P)
.

One immediately gets a natural map (given by analytification)
\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}\to\Sha
, and an étale twist by an element in the former group is ismorphic to an analytic twist by an element in the latter group.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
23
, Proposition 6.34]
)
.

This natural map
\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}\to\Sha
is injective and induces a bijection between the respective torsion subgroups.

Remark 4.7
.

If the fibers are all integral, we actually know that
\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}
is torsion (
[
23
, Proposition 6.32]
).

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose
\phi\in\Sha^{\prime}
is such that
X^{\phi}
is projective, then
\phi\in\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}
.

4.3.
Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky’s theorem

In this section, we explain why Saccà’s conjecture is true given the following extra condition on the fibration and its twist:

Definition 4.3
.

A Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
satisfies the Bogomolov-Kamenova-Verbitsky (BKV) conditions if

•

the fibers of
\pi
is reduced outside of a locus of codimension
2
in
B
;

•

the integral homology class of a general fiber is primitive.

Note that the fibration cannot have any multiple fiber as soon as it satisfies the BKV conditions.

Theorem 4.9

(
[
9
]
)
.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
satisfies the BKV conditions.
Then there is a degenerate twistor deformation of
X
that admits a meromorphic section.

Corollary 4.10
.

Suppose
\pi:X\to B
is projective and satisfies the BKV conditions.
Then there is some torsion
\psi\in\Sha^{0}
such that
X^{\psi}
admits a rational section.

Proof.

By the preceding theorem and Theorem
4.3
, we can find
\psi\in\Sha^{0}
such that
X^{\psi}
has a meromorphic section.
But then
X^{\psi}
must be projective (see
[
1
, Lemma 5.17]
), so
\psi
is torsion by Theorem
4.4
.
∎

Let us now gives a positive answer to Saccà’s conjecture under some assumptions.

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose we have a projective Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
and a torsion class
\phi\in\Sha_{X/B}
, such that both
X,X^{\phi}
satisfy the BKV conditions.
Then
X
is deformation-equivalent to
X^{\phi}
.

The proof relies on the following observation:

Proposition 4.12
.

Suppose we have a projective Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\to B
with a rational section and an étale Tate-Shafarevich class
\phi\in\Sha_{\text{\'{e}t}}
such that
\pi^{\phi}:X^{\phi}\to B
too has a rational section, then
X
is birational to
X^{\phi}
.

Proof.

Remove from the base the (common) discriminant locus of the fibrations, as well as the indeterminacy loci of the rational sections.
Denote by
B^{0}
the resulting open locus, and
A,A^{\phi}
the preimages of
B^{0}
under
\pi,\pi^{\phi}
respectively.

Then
A,A^{\phi}
are related by an étale Tate-Shafarevich twist over
B
.
Since
A
has a section, it can be given a canonical structure of an abelian scheme
\mu:A\times_{B^{0}}A\to A
over
B^{0}
with the section being the identity.
But then
\underline{\operatorname{Aut}}_{A/B^{0}}^{0}\cong(A,\mu)^{\rm an}
where the latter acts by translation.
If we let
P\to B
be the group scheme associated to
X\to B
as in Theorem
4.5
, then this shows that
P|_{B^{0}}\cong(A,\mu)
.
Therefore
A^{\phi}\to B^{0}
is a twist of
A\to B^{0}
by the element

\phi|_{B^{0}}\in H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(B^{0},P|_{B^{0}})=H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(B^{0},(A,\mu)).

The group law
\mu:A\times_{B^{0}}A\to A
gives rise to an action
A\times_{B^{0}}A^{\phi}\to A^{\phi}
by étale descent, making
A^{\phi}
a torsor for
(A,\mu)
.
But
A^{\phi}\to B^{0}
has a section, so this action gives an isomorphism
A\cong A^{\phi}
as
B^{0}
-schemes.
Therefore
X
is birational to
X^{\phi}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.11
.

By Corollary
4.10
, we can find torsion
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\Sha
such that
X
is deformation-equivalent to
X^{\psi}
,
X^{\phi}
is deformation-equivalent to
(X^{\phi})^{\psi^{\prime}}=X^{\phi+\psi^{\prime}}
(note also Remark
1.2
), and both
X^{\psi}
and
X^{\phi+\psi^{\prime}}
are projective and admit rational sections.

Now we have
X^{\phi+\psi^{\prime}}=(X^{\psi})^{\phi+\psi^{\prime}-\psi}
.
Since
\phi+\psi^{\prime}-\psi
is torsion, the preceding proposition shows that
X^{\psi}
is birational to
X^{\phi+\psi^{\prime}}
.
Since they are both irreducible hyper-Kähler varieties, this means that they are deformation-equivalent by
[
21
, Theorem 4.6]
.
Therefore
X
and
X^{\phi}
are deformation-equivalent.
∎

4.4.
Beauville-Mukai systems and primitivity of general fibers

Let us examine the condition for a general fiber to have primitive integral homology class.
Clearly, any fibration with a rational section has this property.

We focus our attention on a class of fibrations whose total space is of
K3^{[n]}
-type.
For a K3 surface
S
and a primitive Mukai vector
v\in\tilde{H}(S;\mathbb{Z})
, we denote by
M_{S}(v)
the moduli space of stable sheaves with Mukai vector
v
, where the stability is with respect to a generic polarization.
It is an irreducible hyper-Kähler variety of dimension
v^{2}+2
.

Suppose
L
is a big and nef line bundle on
S
, and
\chi\in\mathbb{Z}
is any integer such that
(0,L,\chi)\in\tilde{H}(S;\mathbb{Z})
is primitive.
The Beauville-Mukai system associated to these data is given by the support map

M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to|L|

given by sending such a sheaf to its Fitting support.
The dimension of the total space is
2g
, where
L^{2}=2g-2
.
We will assume that
g>2
.

Note that a generic Lagrangian fibration whose total space is of K3
[n]
-type is bimeromorphic to a fibration on the degenerate twistor line of a Beauville-Mukai system (cf.
[
26
]
).

Our main result here is:

Proposition 4.13
.

Suppose
L
is primitive, then the general fiber of the Beauville-Mukai system
M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to|L|
has primitive integral homology class.

Note that if
g-1
is square-free, then
L
is automatically primitive.

Remark 4.14
.

If
L
is not primitive, then the Beauville-Mukai system may have multiple (
i.e.
nowhere reduced) fibers (see
[
20
]
), which implies that the general fiber cannot have primitive integral homology class (see also
[
11
]
).

The technique is to deform any such Beauville-Mukai system to a situation with a section.
Take an elliptic K3 surface
\rho:S_{0}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with integral fibers and a section
s
, such that
\operatorname{Pic}(S_{0})=\mathbb{Z}c_{1}(s)\oplus\mathbb{Z}f
where
f\in H^{2}(S_{0},\mathbb{Z})
the class of a general fiber.

There is a fibration
\rho^{[g]}:S_{0}^{[g]}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(g)}=\mathbb{P}^{g}
given by composing the Hilbert-Chow map
S_{0}^{[g]}\to S_{0}^{(g)}
with the functorial map on the symmetric product
S_{0}^{(g)}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(g)}
.

Let
H_{0}=s+gf
which is ample by Nakai-Moishezon.
Then we can naturally identify
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(g)}\cong H_{0}
by sending
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{g}\in(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(g)}
to the effective Cartier divisor
s+\rho^{-1}(p_{1})+\cdots+\rho^{-1}(p_{g})
.

Proposition 4.15
.

There is an isomorphism
M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi)\cong S_{0}^{[g]}
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S_{0}^{[g]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|H_{0}|}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{(g)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

commutes.

Proof.

Let us first construct this for
\chi>1
.
This is a consequence of
[
40
, Theorem 3.15]
, which says that the relative Fourier-Mukai transform on the elliptic surface gives an isomorphism

M_{S_{0}}(1,(\chi-1)f,1-g)\cong M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi)

for any
\chi>1
.

Note now that
M(1,(\chi-1)f,1-g)
consists precisely of sheaves of the form
\mathcal{I}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{O}((\chi-1)f)
where
Z
is a length
Z
subscheme.
By the calculation in
[
40
, (3.15)]
(see also
[
6
]
), the image of such a sheaf under this isomorphism is supported on the Cartier divisor
s+\rho^{-1}(p_{1})+\cdots+\rho^{-1}(p_{g})
, where
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{g}=\rho^{[g]}(Z)
.

Hence the stated isomorphism is the desired one.
For
\chi\leq 1
, simply note that the operation
-\otimes\mathcal{O}(H_{0})
establishes an isomorphism

M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi)\to M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi+2g-2)

that clearly preserves the respective support maps.
∎

Corollary 4.16
.

The Beauville-Mukai system
M_{S_{0}}(0,H_{0},\chi)\to|H_{0}|
has a section.
In particular, the integral homology class of a general fiber is primitive.

There is a smooth connected Deligne-Mumford stack parameterizing pairs
(S,L)
where
S
is a K3 surface, and
L
is a quasi-polarization,
i.e.
a big and nef line bundle on
S
whose class is primitive and satisfies
L^{2}=2g-2
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{g}
be a cover of this moduli space by a smooth connected variety.

Proof of Proposition
4.13
.

The construction of Beauville-Mukai systems is functorial, so we obtain a family of fibrations

u:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}_{g}\times\mathbb{P}^{g}

such that the fiber over any
(S,L)\in\mathcal{H}_{g}
is the Beauville-Mukai system

M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to\mathbb{P}^{g}.

We claim that if
(S,L)\in\mathcal{H}_{g}
satisfies the condition that a general fiber of the system
M_{S}(0,L,\chi)\to\mathbb{P}^{g}
has primitive homology, then so does any
(S^{\prime},L^{\prime})
contained in a contractible open
U\subset\mathcal{H}_{g}
containing
(S,L)
.
Indeed, let us consider the restriction

\mathcal{M}_{U}\to U\times\mathbb{P}^{g}

as fibration.
All general fibers of this are then homologous in
H^{2g}(\mathcal{M}_{U};\mathbb{Z})
.

On the other hand, for any
(S^{\prime},L^{\prime})\in U
, the inclusion map

H^{2g}(M_{S^{\prime}}(0,L^{\prime},\chi);\mathbb{Z})\to H^{2g}(\mathcal{M}_{U};\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism as
U
is contractible.
But the class of a general fiber is primitive in
H^{2g}(M_{S}(0,L,\chi);\mathbb{Z})
, so it would have to be primitive in
H^{2g}(M_{S^{\prime}}(0,L^{\prime},\chi);\mathbb{Z})
as well.

As
\mathcal{H}_{g}
can be covered by contractible opens, it remains to find one
(S,L)\in\mathcal{H}_{g}

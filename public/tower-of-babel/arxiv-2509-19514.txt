Nulling baryonic feedback in weak lensing surveys using cross-correlations with fast radio bursts Preprint: APS/123-QED
Calvin Leung, Josh Borrow, Kiyoshi W. Masui, Shion Andrew, Kai-Feng Chen, Joop Schaye, Matthieu Schaller

Abstract
Baryonic feedback is a leading contaminant in studying dark matter and cosmology using cosmic shear. This has meant omitting much of the data during cosmological inference, or forward-modeling the spatial distribution of gas around dark matter halos using analytical or hydrodynamical models for baryonic feedback, which introduces nuisance parameters and model dependence. We propose a novel method of “baryon nulling” using cross-correlations between shear maps and fast radio burst (FRB) dispersion measures. By directly subtracting the dark matter–dispersion measure cross-correlation, the sensitivity of our nulled power spectra to feedback effects can be significantly reduced without any explicit feedback modeling. Using the FLAMINGO suite of hydrodynamic simulations, whose power spectra span a wide yet realistic range of feedback variations, we demonstrate that our method reduces sensitivity to feedback modeling at k ≈ 1 k\approx 1 Mpc -1 by about an order of magnitude. This points toward a strong synergy between the next generation of sensitive FRB surveys such as CHORD and the DSA-2000, and cosmic shear surveys such as Rubin, Euclid, and Roman.

In this paper, we propose to carry out weak lensing cosmology using a new observable that is insensitive to the large-scale baryon distribution.
Our “baryon-nulled” power spectrum uses cross-correlations between cosmic shear and fast radio burst dispersion measures to nullify the baryonic contributions to the total matter power spectrum.

Here, we explore the sensitivity of

Collectively, the feedback variations in FLAMINGO span a wide range of baryonic feedback models as allowed by the observed galaxy stellar mass function and galaxy cluster gas fractions.

FLAMINGO provides an ideal testbed for evaluating baryon-nulling methods against feedback scenarios which span the range consistent with observations.
For instance, the fiducial variant of FLAMINGO is calibrated to be consistent with observational constraints on stellar and AGN feedback: the former via the observed galaxy stellar mass function; and the latter via gas fractions inferred from X-ray observations of groups and clusters

In conclusion, we have introduced baryon nulling: a first-principles method which uses FRBs to null the impact of baryonic feedback on cosmic shear power spectra, and have shown that it performs well at

(25)

Appendix A
Window function conventions

In this Appendix we write out the window functions used to compute the angular power spectra in the main text. The cosmic shear auto-power spectrum is

C^{\gamma}_{\ell}=\int_{\chi_{min}}^{\infty}P_{mm}(k=\ell/\chi,z)W_{\gamma}(\chi)^{2}/\chi^{2}~d\chi.

(26)

Here,
W_{\gamma}
is defined as

W_{\gamma}(\chi)=\dfrac{3}{2}\dfrac{\Omega_{m}H_{0}^{2}}{c^{2}}(1+z)\chi g(\chi)

(27)

where
g
is the lensing efficiency, defined by
g(\chi)=\int_{\chi}^{\chi_{\infty}}d\chi^{\prime}\dfrac{\chi^{\prime}-\chi}{\chi^{\prime}}p_{G}(\chi^{\prime})
, and
p_{G}
is the redshift distribution of the distorted background galaxies normalized such that
\int p_{G}(\chi^{\prime})~d\chi^{\prime}=1

(
Prat and Bacon 2025
)
. We take
\chi_{min}=$1\text{\,}\mathrm{Mpc}$
to remove the divergence due to the Limber approximation as
\chi\to 0
, but our results are insensitive to this choice (it is the same as cutting off the
k
integral at some
k_{max}=\ell/\chi_{min}
). In the limit that all the galaxies are at the same distance
\chi_{G}
(i.e.
p_{G}(\chi^{\prime})
is a delta function),
W_{\gamma}
reduces to the familiar parabola which upweights matter fluctuations halfway between the source and
observer and which is defined to be zero behind the background galaxies:

W_{\gamma}(\chi)\rightarrow\dfrac{3H_{0}^{2}\Omega_{m}(1+z)}{2c^{2}}\dfrac{\chi(\chi_{G}-\chi)}{\chi_{G}}\qquad\chi<\chi_{G}

(28)

FRB dispersion measures, like lensing shear/convergence, is also a line of sight probe. Let
p_{F}(\chi^{\prime})
be the normalized redshift distribution of FRB sources. The quantity analogous to the lensing efficiency
g(\chi)
is the normalized cumulative distribution function of FRB sources. We notate it as
g_{F}(\chi)=\int_{\chi}^{\infty}p_{F}(\chi^{\prime})~d\chi^{\prime}
. The DM of a single source is

\textrm{DM}(\chi,\hat{n})=\int_{0}^{\chi_{F}}\overline{n}_{e,0}F(z)(1+\delta_{e}(\chi,\hat{n}))~d\chi.

(29)

where the slowly-varying function
F(z)=f_{\mathrm{diffuse}}(z)[X_{e,H}(z)(1-Y_{He})+X_{e,He}(z)(Y_{He})
which captures the evolution of the Universe’s baryons into neutral gas and stars and the global history of reionization. At
z<3
, we have
X_{e,H}=X_{e,He}=1
and
f_{\mathrm{diffuse}}(z)=F(z)=0.844
. We neglect uncertainties in
F(z)
in the remainder of this work but point out that
F(z)
can be calibrated with the dispersion-measure redshift relation.

The analogous window function for FRB dispersion measures is
(
Reischke et al. 2023
, see Eq. 2.15 in)

W_{D}(\chi)=\overline{n}_{e,0}F(z)(1+z(\chi))g_{F}(\chi)

(30)

where
n_{e,0}
is the
z=0
of the comoving average number density of free electrons in the Universe:

n_{e,0}=\dfrac{\rho_{crit}\Omega_{b}}{m_{p}}.

(31)

In the limit that all sources are at the same distance
\chi_{F}
, it reduces to a step function with a
1+z
weighting:

W_{D}(\chi)\to n_{e,0}H(\chi<\chi_{F})(1+z)

(32)

The dispersion auto-power is

C^{D}_{\ell}=\int_{\chi_{min}}^{\infty}P_{e}(k=\ell/\chi,z)W_{D}^{2}(\chi)/\chi^{2}~d\chi.

(33)

Finally, the lensing-dispersion cross-power is

\displaystyle C^{\gamma D}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\int_{\chi_{min}}^{\infty}W_{D}(\chi)W_{\gamma}(\chi)P_{me}(k=\ell/\chi,z)/\chi^{2}~d\chi..

(34)

Using a combination of these equations, we obtain the angular power spectra
C^{\gamma D}_{\ell}
,
C^{\gamma}_{\ell}
, and
C^{D}_{\ell}
in the main text at
\langle z\rangle=0.5
and
1.0

2.
General Properties of Dedieu–Shub measures and Application to Lyapunov Exponents

We detail some properties of Dedieu–Shub measures and we use these to prove (known) Dedieu–Shub inequalities.

2.1.
General properties

2.1.1.
Dedieu–Shub measures are well defined

2.1
.

Let
Y
and
X
be compact metric spaces which come with uniform distributions
\mu_{Y}
and
\mu_{X}
respectively. Let
m:Y\to\text{Prob}(X)
be Borel measurable. For all
B\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, the function

g\in Y\mapsto m(g)(B)\in\mathbb{R}

is Borel measurable.

Proof.

This is Lemma 2.2 in
[
4
]
.
∎

2.1.2.
Fubini–Like property

For a
B\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, let
f=\mathbbm{1}_{B}
. Then Property
1.1
.2 becomes

\int_{K}\int_{X}f(x)\ dm_{kg}(x)d\nu(k)=\int_{X}f\ d\mu.

As this holds for all measurable
B\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, we have the following.

2.2
.

For every bounded and measurable
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{K}\int_{X}f(x)\ dm_{kg}(x)d\nu(k)=\int_{X}f\ d\mu.

Proof.

Our comment above implies the result when
f
is a simple functions. Now, a bounded measurable function can be approximated by an increasing sequence of simple functions
\{f_{n}\}
such that
f_{n}\to f
pointwise and
|f_{n}(x)|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
for all
x
. Now apply dominated convergence theorem.

∎

2.1.3.
Projecting Dedieu–Shub measures

2.3
.

Let
\Pi:X\to Y
be a continuous onto map. Dedine a
G
–action on
Y
such that
g(\Pi(x)):=\Pi(g(x))
. Suppose
G
acts on X preserving fibers of
\Pi
, that is

\text{if }\Pi x_{1}=\Pi x_{2}\implies\Pi(gx_{1})=\Pi(gx_{2})

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and
g\in G
.
Then, if
m
is a Dedieu – Shub measure for
(K,G,X)
, then
\tilde{m}_{g}:=\Pi_{*}(m_{g})
is a Dedieu–Shub measure for
(K,G,Y)
.

Proof.

1.

(Invariance)
We wish to show for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
, we have
\tilde{m}_{g}(g^{-1}A)=\tilde{m}_{g}(A)
. We have

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{g}(g^{-1}A)=m_{g}(\Pi^{-1}(g^{-1}A))=m_{g}((\Pi^{-1}\circ g^{-1})(A))=m_{g}(g^{-1}(\Pi^{-1}(A)))

\displaystyle=m_{g}(g^{-1}(\Pi^{-1}(A)))=m_{g}(\Pi^{-1}(A))=\tilde{m}_{g}(A).

2.

(Average)
We need to argue that for every
g\in G
, we have
\int_{k\in K}\tilde{m}_{kg}\ d\nu(k)=\Pi_{*}(\mu)
. Suppose not – then for some Borel
B\in\mathcal{B}(Y)
,

\int_{k\in K}\tilde{m}_{kg}(B)\ d\nu(k)\neq\Pi_{*}(\mu)(B).

However, this would imply that for

\int_{k\in K}m_{kg}(\Pi^{-1}B)\ d\nu(k)\neq\mu(\Pi^{-1}B),

contradicting that
m
was originally Dedieu–Shub.

∎

The

The

\text{LE}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\gamma)\times X}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|Df^{n}(x)\|\ d\gamma(f)\times d\mu(x).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

3.1.
Proof strategy

Producing a Dedieu–Shub measure over
GL(2,\mathbb{R})
acting on
\mathbb{RP}^{1}
splits into two cases – the positive determinant and negative determinant case. They are different because of Cayley–Hamilton theorem: a
2\times 2
matrix with positive determinant can have both real and imaginary eigenvalues, but a
2\times 2
matrix with negative determinant can only have real eigenvalues. Hence in the case of positive determinant, we can have elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic phenomenon, whereas in the negative determinant case, we can have only hyperbolic phenomenon. The main difference is that in the hyperbolic case, invariant measures are supported on (convex combinations of) Dirac masses, whereas in the parabolic and elliptic cases, invariant measures are fully supported.

The positive determinant case is proven in
[
12
]
and
[
10
]
. We present the proof of
[
12
]
. For the negative determinant case, we explicitly compute densities and normalize.

3.1.1.

An immediate simplification we can make is that we only need to consider diagonal matrices
A
in the proofs of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
,

1.4
. Any
d\times d
matrix
A
admits a
singular value decomposition
of the form
A=U\Sigma V
, where
U
and
V
are unitary and
\Sigma
is diagonal with values in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. This simplifies much of our analysis for Dedieu–Shub measures.

3.1
.

Let
A\in GL(d,\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C})
admit singular value decomposition
A=V\Sigma W
. Then

1.

For every
k\in\{1,...,d\}
,

\int_{U(d)}\delta_{\lambda_{k}(UA)}\ d\nu(U)=\int_{U(d)}\delta_{\lambda_{k}(U\Sigma)}\ d\nu(U);

2.

For every
k\in\{1,...,d\}

\int_{\mathrm{Grass}(d,k)}\delta_{\det A|g_{k}}\ d\mu(g_{k})=\int_{\mathrm{Grass}(d,k}\delta_{\det\Sigma|g_{k}}\ d\mu(g_{k}).

Proof.

In case 1, let
A=V\Sigma W
be the singular value decomposition and let
U\in U(d)
be Haar random. We have that
UA=UV\Sigma W=WUV\Sigma
. Haar measure is translationally invariant, and so
WUV
is also Haar distributed. In case 2,
\mu
on
\text{Grass}(k,d)
is by definition, stationary under
U(d)
action and so
Wg_{k}V
is distributed in
g_{k}
. In the real case, under
O(d)
action, the situation is the same.
∎

Hence, throughout our paper when considering Dedieu–Shub measures, it is enough for us to consider diagonal matrices with non negative matrices, essentially presupposing our matrix was processed first through a singular value decomposition machine. Secondly, we can always prove results for matrices with determinany
\pm 1
. This is because we can divide out by the determinant.

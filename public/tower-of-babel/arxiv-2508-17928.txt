Arithmetic properties of t t -Schur overpartitions
Mohammed L. Nadji, Manjil P. Saikia, James A. Sellers

Abstract
. In a recent work, Nadji and Ahmia introduced the t t -Schur overpartitions as an overpartition analogue for t t -Schur partitions, which generalizes the classical Schur’s partitions into parts congruent to 1 1 or 5 5 modulo 6 6 . We continue the study of this new class of overpartitions and prove several arithmetic results for the cases t = 3 , 9 t=3,9 and t t being a power of 2 2 or a power of 3 3 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11P83, 05A15, 05A17.

1.
Introduction

A
partition
of a positive integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{m})

such that
n=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}
. Each summand
\lambda_{i}
is called a
part
. An
overpartition
of
n
is a partition of
n
in which the first occurrence of parts of each size may be overlined. Let
\overline{p}(n)
denote the number of overpartitions of
n
. Corteel and Lovejoy
[
19
]
gave the generating function for the number of overpartitions, which is

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{1+q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}.

Here and throughout we will use the standard
q
-series notation

(a;q)_{n}:=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{i}),&\text{if $n\geq 0$;}\\
1,&\text{if $n=0$.}\end{cases}

Moreover,
(a;q)_{\infty}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}
, for
|q|<1
and
f_{k}=(q^{k},q^{k})_{\infty}
.

The Rogers-Ramanujan identities
[
37
]
are two remarkable identities that establish deep connections between partition theory, combinatorics, modular forms, and representation theory. These identities were discovered independently by Rogers
[
36
]
in 1894 and later by Ramanujan in 1913 and Schur
[
40
]
in 1917. Following the discovery of these identities, in 1926, Schur
[
41
]
presented his famous Rogers-Ramanujan type identity.

Theorem 1.1

(Schur, 1926)
.

Let
A(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
\pm 1
modulo
6
,
B(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into distinct parts congruent to
\pm 1
modulo
3
, and
C(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts differ by at least
3
where no consecutive multiples of
3
appear. Then for all
n
,

A(n)=B(n)=C(n).

As expected, Schur’s theorem also became a highly influential partition identity, and several different proofs were provided using a variety of techniques. These techniques include bijections by Bressoud
[
16
]
and Bessenrodt
[
15
]
, the method of weighted words by Alladi and Gordon
[
4
]
, and recurrences by Andrews
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
.

In 1968 and 1969, Andrews
[
7
,
8
]
proved two partition theorems of the Rogers-Ramanujan type which generalize Schur’s partition identity. Andrews’ two generalizations of Schur’s theorem went on to become two of the most influential results in the theory of partitions, finding applications in combinatorics
[
3
,
20
,
47
]
, representation theory
[
11
]
and quantum algebra
[
33
]
. Notably, Lovejoy
[
27
]
presented the overpartition analogue of Schur’s theorem in the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Lovejoy, 2005)
.

Let
A(n,k)
denote the number of overpartitions of
n
into parts congruent to
1
or
2
modulo
3
with
k
non-overlined parts. Let
B(n,k)
denote the number of overpartitions
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}
of
n
, having
k
non-overlined parts and satisfying the difference conditions

\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\geq\begin{cases}0+3\chi(\overline{\lambda_{i+1}})&\text{if}\quad\lambda_{i+1}\equiv 1,2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),\\
1+3\chi(\overline{\lambda_{i+1}})&\text{if}\quad\lambda_{i+1}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),\end{cases}

where
\chi(\overline{\lambda_{i+1}})=1
if
\lambda_{i+1}
is overlined and
0
otherwise. Then for all
k,n\geq 0
,

A(k,n)=B(k,n).

Recently, Nadji and Ahmia
[
30
]
studied the partitions into parts that are simultaneously regular and distinct from both combinatorial and arithmetic perspectives, where the authors defined the
t
-Schur partitions of a positive integer
n
.

Definition 1.3

(Nadji & Ahmia, 2025)
.

For any odd positive integer
t\geq 3
, a
t
-Schur partition of
n
is a partition into parts congruent to
i
modulo
2t
, where
i\in I(t)
, and
I(t)=\{1,3,5,7,\dots,(2t-1)\}\backslash\{t\}
.

For
t=3
, this definition yields the classical Schur’s theorem
[
32
,
A003105
]
(or
3
-Schur partitions), where
I(3)=\{1,5\}
. The number of
t
-Schur partitions of
n
, denoted by
S_{t}(n)
, satisfies the generating function

\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{t}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{(1-q^{t(2n-1)})}{(1-q^{2n-1})}=\dfrac{f_{2}f_{t}}{f_{1}f_{2t}}.

Moreover, the number
S_{t}(n)
also counts the number of
(2,t)
-biregular partitions of
n
, which are partitions of
n
into parts not divisible by
2
and
t
.

Building upon this, Nadji and Ahmia
[
30
]
introduced the number of
t
-Schur overpartitions of a positive integer
n
, denoted by
\overline{S}_{t}(n)
, as a generalization of the
t
-Schur partitions. The generating function for
\overline{S}_{t}(n)
is given by

(1)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{S}_{t}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{(1+q^{2n-1})(1-q^{t(2n-1)})}{(1+q^{t(2n-1)})(1-q^{2n-1})}=\dfrac{f_{2}^{3}f_{t}^{2}f_{4t}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{4}f_{2t}^{3}}=\frac{\varphi(-q^{2})\varphi(-q^{t})}{\varphi(-q)\varphi(-q^{2t})},

where
\varphi(-q)
is Ramanujan’s theta function and is defined as

\varphi(-q):=1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}.

The
t
-Schur partitions also belong to the broader family of
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
-biregular overpartitions (for
\ell_{1}=2
and
\ell_{2}\geq 3
odd), which has been studied by Nadji, Ahmia and Ramírez
[

and Alanazi, Munagi, and Saikia
[

]
recently.

The authors in
[

(Nadji & Ahmia, 2025)

For any odd

For instance, the coefficients

For all

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{3}(6n+5)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4),

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{3}(12n+7)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 8),

2.2.
Results from the theory of modular forms

We define the matrix groups

\displaystyle\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\infty}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\

\displaystyle\Gamma(N):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\

Theorem 5.6

\displaystyle A_{g}=\frac{1}{n-2}\bigg(\operatorname{Ric}_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{2(n-1)}g\bigg),

\displaystyle\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ is an open, convex, connected symmetric cone with vertex at 0},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0~\forall~1\leq i\leq n\}\subseteq\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{1}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}>0\},

\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Gamma})\text{ is concave, homogeneous of degree one and symmetric in the }\lambda_{i},

\displaystyle f>0\text{ in }\Gamma,\hskip 10.00002ptf=0\text{ on }\partial\Gamma,\hskip 10.00002ptf_{\lambda_{i}}>0\text{ in }\Gamma\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n.

\displaystyle f(e)=1,\hskip 10.00002pte=(1,\dots,1).

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{k}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq k\}.

The fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem is a type of uniformisation problem within a fixed conformal class, well-motivated from both a geometric and a PDE perspective. Indeed, the Schouten tensor arises as the trace part of the Riemann curvature tensor in the so-called Ricci decomposition:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Riem}_{g}=W_{g}+A_{g}\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}g.

Let us now discuss some previous results on the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem to put Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
into context. The
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem was first studied for

by Mazzeo & Pacard in
[
54
]
, where they established perturbative existence results and studied links with the existence of Poincaré-Einstein metrics. The first general existence result for the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem was obtained by González, Li & Nguyen in
[
19
]
, where they showed (among other results) that any smooth bounded Euclidean domain
\Omega
admits a complete conformally flat locally Lipschitz metric solving (
1.6
) in the viscosity sense, and moreover the solution is unique in the class of continuous viscosity solutions. In later work of Li & Nguyen
[
48
]
and Li, Nguyen & Xiong
[
50
]
, it was shown that the solution of
[
19
]
is not
C^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}
if
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{2}^{+}
and
\partial\Omega
contains more than one connected component; this is in stark contrast to the case
f=c\sigma_{1}
, where solutions are always smooth in the interior. Recently, Wu
[
58
]
showed that solutions to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem are smooth away from a set of measure zero if
k=n
, or if
k>\frac{n}{2}
and
g_{0}
is Euclidean.

In the Riemannian setting, Guan
[
23
]
proved existence of smooth solutions to a related problem in which the Schouten tensor is replaced by the Ricci tensor, under the assumption that there exists a smooth metric
g\in[g_{0}]
satisfying
\lambda(-g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_{g})\in\Gamma
on
M
. Such equations involving the Ricci tensor can be recast as special instances of (
1.6
) in which
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
(see Remark
3.2
). Guan’s existence results were subsequently extended by Yuan
[
59
]
, who proved the existence of a smooth complete metric
g_{w}=w^{-2}g_{0}
solving (
1.6
), assuming
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
and the existence of
g\in[g_{0}]
satisfying
\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g})\in\Gamma
. In
[
59
]
, Yuan also proves uniqueness of solutions and the asymptotics in (
1.7
) under a further technical assumption on
\Gamma
. We point out that the condition
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
fails in many cases of interest, in particular the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
k\geq 2
.

To gain a more complete understanding of the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem, it is therefore of significant interest to determine whether the aforementioned assumptions on conformal flatness, the cone
\Gamma
and/or the existence of certain metrics
g\in[g_{0}]
can be removed. In this direction, Gursky, Streets & Warren
[
29
]
obtained the same existence result of Guan
[
23
]
without assuming the existence of
g\in[g_{0}]
satisfying
\lambda(-g^{-1}\operatorname{Ric}_{g})\in\Gamma
; they also proved uniqueness and asymptotics of solutions. In
[
14
]
, the present authors proved the existence of a maximal Lipschitz viscosity solution to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem provided
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}>1
, without any assumption on the existence of
g\in[g_{0}]
satisfying
\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g})\in\Gamma
. Therein, we also proved asymptotics of solutions, and showed that solutions are smooth and unique (in the class of continuous viscosity solutions) when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
. We point out that some of the estimates in
[
14
]
do not hold when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
(see our recent work
[

for a further discussion). Using Morse-theoretic methods, Yuan
[

independently proved the existence of a smooth metric

satisfying

in the following three cases: (1)

and

Proof of Theorem

.

This follows immediately from Theorem

∎

Remark 1.8
.

Uniqueness of the Lipschitz viscosity solution to (

[

. It is not currently known whether such a comparison principle holds in the non-Euclidean setting when

Remark 1.9
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.7
and the aforementioned existence result of Yuan
[
60
]
when
n=3
, the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem is now solved when
n=3
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.7
, the existence of a smooth solution to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
is achieved by first obtaining for each constant
\delta>0
a solution
u_{\delta}
to (
1.6
) satisfying
u_{\delta}=\delta
on
\partial M
, and then taking a limit as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Our locally Lipschitz viscosity solution to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\partial\Gamma
is then obtained as a limit of solutions to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem on cones satisfying
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
. For this purpose, it is important to have interior
C^{1}
estimates (depending on two-sided

bounds) which are stable under perturbations of
(f,\Gamma)
and include both the cases
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
and
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\partial\Gamma
. Such an estimate has recently been established in a general setting by Chu, Li & Li
[
10
]
. The validity of such a local gradient estimate also allows us to apply our estimates near the boundary in
[
14
, Section 4]
verbatim, yielding the desired asymptotics of solutions. We refer the reader to Section
3.5
for the details.

As indicated in the previous paragraph, the existence of a locally Lipschitz viscosity solution to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\partial\Gamma
does not require the existence of solutions to (
1.6
) with positive boundary data when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\partial\Gamma
. However, the existence of solutions to (
1.6
) with positive boundary data when
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\partial\Gamma
is of independent interest, and requires us to establish a new boundary gradient estimate (see Proposition
3.7
). The existence result is as follows:

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume the same hypotheses as in Theorem
1.7
and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
be positive. Then (

) admits a Lipschitz viscosity solution
g_{u}=u^{-2}g_{0}
satisfying

on
\partial M
. Moreover, if
(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Gamma
, then
u
is smooth and it is the unique solution in the class of continuous viscosity solutions satisfying

on
\partial M
.

The existence of solutions to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem on general compact Riemannian manifolds with non-empty boundary remains an interesting open problem for values of
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}
closer to zero when

.

2.1
A key lemma and admissible metrics when

In this subsection, we prove that
\mu(M,[g])\leq 1-\eta<1
for an arbitrary smooth compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with non-empty boundary, where

depends on an upper bound for the Schouten tensor of

and the

norm of the metric and inverse metric components with respect to a given open covering of charts on

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth compact manifold of dimension

with smooth non-empty boundary
\partial M
and let

be any smooth finite open covering of charts on

. Then for any
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, there exists
\eta=\eta(M,\mathcal{U},C_{1},C_{2})>0
such that if

is any Riemannian metric on
M
with components

in local coordinates determined by
\mathcal{U}
satisfying

\displaystyle|g_{ab}|+|g^{ab}|+|\partial_{a}g_{bc}|\leq C_{1}

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle A_{g}\leq C_{2}g,

(2.3)

then

.

Before giving the proof of Lemma

, we note that it has the following immediate consequence:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension

with non-empty boundary. Then
\mu(M,[g_{0}])<1
. In particular, if
\Gamma
satisfies (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\geq 1
, then there exists a smooth metric

such that
\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g})\in\Gamma
on
M
.

We recall that for

[
14
]
and Yuan
[
60
]
. Our proof of Lemma
2.1
is inspired by the approach of Yuan. In the course of the proof, we make use of a good term that was dropped in Yuan’s argument – see the sentence following (
2.1
) below.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We start by fixing a smooth function

on
M
with no critical points, so that

on
M
– note that such a function exists since
M
is a smooth compact manifold with non-empty boundary. In what follows, our constants may depend on
v
, although such dependence is implicit through dependence on
M
. From this point onwards, all computations are carried out in a single chart where
\delta
is used as a local reference metric, and
|X|_{\delta}
denotes the norm of a vector field
X
with respect to
\delta
. Note that by (
2.2
), there exists a constant
C_{0}\geq 1
depending only on

and
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle C_{0}^{-1}(\delta_{ab})\leq(g_{ab})\leq C_{0}(\delta_{ab})

(2.4)

in the sense of matrices. Note that (
2.4
) is equivalent to
C_{0}^{-1}|X|_{\delta}^{2}\leq|X|_{g}^{2}\leq C_{0}|X|_{\delta}^{2}
for all vector fields
X
. Additionally, after enlarging
C_{0}
if necessary, the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma_{ab}^{c}
of the Levi-Civita connection for

satisfy

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{ab}^{c}|\leq C_{0},

(2.5)

which follows from (
2.2
) and the fact that
\Gamma_{ab}^{c}
can be expressed in terms of the inverse metric components and first order partial derivatives of the metric components.

For
N>0
, denote
g^{N}=e^{2e^{Nv}}g
. We will show that there exists
N=N(M,\mathcal{U},C_{1},C_{2})
such that if
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\geq 1-\frac{1}{e^{N\max_{M}v}}
, then
\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g^{N}})\in\Gamma
. To this end, we first observe

\displaystyle-g^{-1}A_{g^{N}}=Ne^{Nv}g^{-1}\nabla_{g}^{2}v+N^{2}e^{Nv}(1-e^{Nv})g^{-1}(dv\otimes dv)+\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\operatorname{Id}-g^{-1}A_{g}.

(2.6)

Now,

\displaystyle\lambda\bigg(

\displaystyle N^{2}e^{Nv}(1-e^{Nv})g^{-1}(dv\otimes dv)+\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\operatorname{Id}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\bigg(-1+\frac{2}{e^{Nv}}\,,\,1\,,\,\dots\,,\,1\bigg)

(2.7)

and (interpreting inequalities of vectors componentwise)

\displaystyle\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g})

\displaystyle\geq-(C_{2},\dots,C_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\bigg(\frac{2C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}},\dots,\frac{2C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\bigg(\frac{2C_{0}C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}},\dots,\frac{2C_{0}C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}\bigg).

(2.8)

We point out that the term
\frac{2}{e^{Nv}}
in the first component on the bottom line of (
2.1
) was dropped at this point in Yuan’s argument
[
60
]
.

We next consider
Ne^{Nv}\lambda(g^{-1}\nabla_{g}^{2}v)
, which is the only remaining term coming from (
2.6
). Recalling

\displaystyle(\nabla_{g}^{2}v)_{ab}=\partial_{a}\partial_{b}v-\Gamma_{ab}^{c}\,\partial_{c}v

and (
2.5
), we see that there exists a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(M,\mathcal{U},C_{1})>0
such that

\displaystyle Ne^{Nv}\lambda(g^{-1}\nabla_{g}^{2}v)

\displaystyle\geq-Ne^{Nv}(C_{3},\dots,C_{3})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\bigg(\frac{2C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}},\dots,\frac{2C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}\bigg(\frac{2C_{0}C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}},\dots,\frac{2C_{0}C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}\bigg).

(2.9)

By (
2.6
)–(
2.1
), we therefore have

\displaystyle\lambda(-g^{-1}A_{g^{N}})\geq\frac{1}{2}N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{g}^{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\dots,\chi_{2})

where

\displaystyle\chi_{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2C_{0}C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{0}C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{2}{e^{Nv}}-\frac{2C_{0}C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}-\frac{2C_{0}C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\chi_{2}+\frac{2}{e^{Nv}}-\frac{4C_{0}C_{2}}{N^{2}e^{2Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}-\frac{4C_{0}C_{3}}{Ne^{Nv}|dv|_{\delta}^{2}}.

Now observe that we may fix

such that

and

. Therefore, if

and hence

.

We begin by introducing the space

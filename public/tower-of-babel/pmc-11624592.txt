Examination of homozygosity runs and selection signatures in native goat breeds of Henan, China
Authors: Weifeng Peng, Yiyuan Zhang, Lei Gao, Shuping Wang, Mengting Liu, Enrui Sun, Kaixin Lu, Yunxia Zhang, Bing Li, Guoyin Li, Jingya Cao, Mingsheng Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Understanding the genomic characteristics of livestock is crucial for improving breeding efficiency and conservation efforts. However, there is a relative lack of information on the genetic makeup of local goat breeds in Henan, China. In this study, we identified runs of homozygosity (ROH), genomic inbreeding coefficients (FROH), and selection signatures in four breeds including Funiu White (FNW), Huai (HG), Lushan Bullleg (LS), and Taihang black (THB). The genomic analysis utilized a dataset of 46,278 SNP markers and 102 animals. A total of 342, 567, 1285, and 180 ROH segments were detected in FNW, HG, LS, and THB, respectively, with an average of 15.55, 29.84, 32.95, and 8.18 segments per individual. The lengths of ROH segments varied from 69.36 Mb in THB to 417.06 Mb in LS, with the most common lengths being 2-4 Mb and 4-8 Mb. The highest number of longest ROH segments (> 16 Mb) were found in LS (328) and the highest average FROH value was observed in LS (0.173), followed by HG (0.128), while the lowest FROH values were in THB (0.029) and FNW (0.070). Furthermore, the analysis of ROH islands and Composite Likelihood Ratio (CLR) identified a total of 175 significant genes. Among these, 25 genes were found to overlap, detected by both methods. These genes were associated with a diverse range of traits including reproductive ability (GPRIN3), weight (CCSER1), immune response (HERC5 and TIGD2), embryo development (NAP1L5), environmental adaptation (KLHL3, TRHDE, and IFNGR1), and milk characteristics (FAM13A). Significant Gene Ontology (GO) terms related to embryo skeletal system morphogenesis, brain ventricle development, and growth were also identified. This study helps reveal the genetic architecture of Henan goat breeds and provides valuable insights for the effective conservation and breeding programs of local goat breeds in Henan.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-024-11098-0.

Introduction

Due to their adaptability and extensive geographical range, goats were one of the earliest animals to be domesticated, eventually becoming the most widely distributed of all domesticated animals [1]. With close to 58 indigenous goat breeds, China holds a key position in biodiversity due to their beneficial characteristics like high fecundity, robust adaptability, and resistance to stress and diseases, especially in demanding ecological conditions [2, 3]. The indigenous goat breeds in China have experienced substantial natural and artificial selection processes, resulting in significant variations in production traits. These goats have a strong bond with humans and offer diverse products such as meat, milk, wool, cashmere, and fur [4].

Henan Province, situated in the middle and lower stretches of the Yellow River, boasts a rich reservoir of genetic resources for goats. With a high goat population, its favorable climate and ample forage resources create an ecological environment conducive to the thriving growth and reproduction of goats in numerous areas [5]. Due to the effects of long-term natural selection and meticulous breeding efforts undertaken by local farmers, Henan Province has cultivated notable local goat breeds, including the Lushan Bullleg Goat, Zhoukou Huai Goat, Taihang Black Goat, and Funiu White Goat [5]. The province's wide array of goat breeds, encompassing primitive breeds, positions it as a crucial genetic repository, functioning as a valuable gene pool in China. Huai goat is a longstanding and valuable indigenous breed in China, renowned for its versatility in providing skin, meat, and wool. Originating from Huaidian Town in Shenqiu of Hean, this breed holds a significant position in the agricultural history of China [6–8]. The Taihang black goat, a distinguished indigenous breed in China, has its distribution in Xiuwu County, Jiaozuo City, Henan Province [5]. It is primarily distributed in the counties located on the eastern border of the Taihang Mountain in the northwest of Henan Province. Taihang black goats exhibit robust adaptability to their breeding environment, displaying strong disease resistance, as well as tolerance to cold, heat, and rough feeding conditions [9]. They can thrive in environments ranging from 0 to 40 ℃ in their production areas. Moreover, they boast a high reproductive rate, typically birthing two offspring annually, each of which can further produce two lambs. Both grazing and captive breeding methods are suitable for their rearing [9]. The Funiu White Goat, indigenous to Henan Province, is prized for its superior skin quality and meat yield. This breed exhibits remarkable adaptability, quick maturation, genetic stability, prolific reproductive capacity, robust resistance, and cost-effective breeding [5]. Its primary habitat is the Funiu Mountain region in the western and southwestern sectors of Henan Province [10]. Lushan Bullleg Goat, primarily found in Lushan County, Henan Province, is alternatively referred to as the Lushan Bullleg Goat, owing to its robust and thick limbs. Its adaptation to a distinctive habitat results in a larger physique, straight and broad back and waist, well-developed hind legs, and prominent gluteal muscles, resembling certain physical attributes of meat goats [5]. The goat breeds in Henan Province exhibit exceptional traits, including robust adaptability, tolerance to roughage, strong disease resistance, high reproductive capacity, and distinctive production performance. These breeds constitute an essential component of China's goat genetic resource gene bank, serving as the foundation for the enhancement and breeding of indigenous goats in the country. However, due to the relentless pursuit of economic gains and indiscriminate crossbreeding, the numbers and quality of superior local goat breeds in the province are on a steady decline [5].

The recent progress in genomic resources for goat breeds, alongside the development of next generation sequencing method, has facilitated an extensive research into the genetic composition of indigenous breeds [11–13]. This advancement plays a pivotal role in strengthening conservation and breeding efforts aimed at preserving genetic diversity and improving desirable traits within these breeds [14, 15]. The concern about local breeds stems from their small population sizes and the absence of strong breeding strategies. Consequently, consanguineous mating, known as inbreeding, becomes widespread, exacerbating the decrease in fitness attributed to heightened homozygosity—an occurrence commonly referred to as inbreeding depression [16, 17]. Hence, a diverse set of genome-wide approaches have been recommended for detecting inbreeding in various goat breeds, with the evaluation of the genomic inbreeding coefficient (FROH) being underscored as an essential technique [18–20].

Domestication and selective breeding are pivotal forces in shaping the remarkable diversity seen in goat breeds, giving rise to discernible genomic imprints known as "selection signatures" [13]. These signatures are associated with specific phenotypes of economic significance, reflecting the genetic adaptations and traits favored by human intervention [21]. Selection signatures are characterized by the reduction, elimination, or alteration of genetic diversity within genomic regions near causative variants, as a result of either natural or artificial selection pressures [22]. These variants typically impact multiple traits and play a role in the development of a breed [22, 23]. In recent years, two primary methodologies have garnered extensive usage in identifying signatures of selection across various goat breeds: Runs of homozygosity (ROH) [19, 20] and complex likelihood ratio (CLR) [8]. Runs of homozygosity occur when there are extended genomic segments with a homozygous genotype, which happens when two haplotypes have a recent common ancestor [23]. These ROH regions are observed in individuals undergoing the selection process [20, 24]. The analysis of runs of homozygosity is valuable for understanding population-wide inbreeding patterns and pinpointing locations with selection signals [20]. The composite likelihoods test has a high detection effect on the variants near fixation, which quantifies significant deviations from the neutral site frequency spectrum (SFS) [25, 26]. However, its practical implementation with empirical data may pose computational challenges and reliance on assumptions regarding mutation and recombination rates. The identification of crucial genomic characteristics within indigenous breeds holds significant importance in comprehending their adaptive capabilities and addressing inbreeding concerns [13]. This methodology has seen broad application, utilizing SNP arrays across diverse livestock species, including cattle [27], sheep [28], and goats [13, 29]. To date, there remains a lack of information concerning indigenous goat breeds in Henan, China. This research seeks to identify runs of homozygosity and selection signatures in four local goat breeds from Henan, China, utilizing ROH and CLR analyses. This investigation aims to enhance comprehension of the genomic landscape and evolutionary trajectory of these breeds, potentially informing future goat breeding initiatives.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

All goat were handled following the guidelines established by the Council for Animal Welfare of China. The protocols for sample collection and animal handling have been approved by the Faculty of Animal Policy and Welfare Committee of Zhoukou Normal University (ZKNU, Protocol number, ZKNM2023032). In addition, the project does not include euthanasia of the experimental animals. The sample size was decided based on the availability of similar animals with similar physical and physiological status. Additionally, an informed consent was granted for animals from the owners in the study.

Blood samples were gathered from Funiu White Goat (FNW, n = 22), Huai Goat (HG, n = 19), Lushan Bullleg Goat (LS, n = 39), and Taihang Black Goat (THB, n = 22) within Henan's primary goat breeding areas. Samples information are summarized in Table S1. Selection criteria ensured that only animals conforming to established breed standards were included.

Genotyping, quality control and genetic structure analyses

Genomic DNA was extracted using a phenol/chloroform method, followed by genotyping of all goats with the Illumina Goat SNP50 BeadChip from Illumina Inc. in San Diego, CA, which contains 53,347 SNPs. All genotyped individuals in the complete dataset underwent quality control filtering using PLINK v1.9 [30]. The filtering process involved applying the following parameters [19]: (i) With a genotype call rate below 0.99 for markers and less than 90% for individuals, exclusion will occur; (ii) SNPs located on unknown or identical chromosomal positions, as well as those on sex chromosomes, will be omitted from further analyses. It should be noted that deviations from Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE) were not considered as a filtering criterion in this case, considering the possible effects of inbreeding on such deviations [31]. LD pruning was not conducted in this study because LD is closely associated with different evolutionary processes, such as inbreeding, artificial selection, and natural selection, all of which are investigated through the analysis of ROH [19]. Similarly, the present study did not include MAF filtering in order to avoid excluding many homozygous regions. Plink and R software [30, 32] was employed to perform principal component analysis (PCA) based on filtered SNPs. The Bayesian clustering analysis was conducted using STRUCTURE v2.3.4 [33], with 50,000 iterations and a burn-in phase. Different K values from 1 to 5 were examined. The effective population size (Ne) was estimated by utilizing the ped and map files in SNeP v1.1 [34].

Detection of runs of homozygosity (ROH)

ROHs were identified in every goat by utilizing the consecutive runs method integrated into the R package detectRUNS [35]. The parameters required for analysis were: (1) at least 20 consecutive SNPs within a run of homozygosity (ROH), (2) a minimum ROH length of 250 kb, (3) no more than a 1 Mb gap between consecutive homozygous SNPs, (4) a maximum of 1 opposite genotype in the run, and (5) a maximum of 1 allowed missing genotype. After calculating the total ROH values per goat, they were categorized into five groups based on ROH estimates: 0–2 Mb, 2–4 Mb, 4–8 Mb, 8–16 Mb, and above 16 Mb [19]. The study focused on determining the length categories of ROH and the distribution of ROH across each autosome when assessing each breed. It was important to use the consecutive method rather than the sliding window approach to prevent the detection of ROH that were inaccurately shorter than the designated 20 SNP window [36]. The study analyzed the total number of runs of homozygosity (ROH), the average ROH count per person, the average length of ROH, the distribution of ROH per breed on each chromosome, and the frequency of ROH based on length classification within each breed.

Calculation of the genomic inbreeding coefficient (FROH)

The genomic inbreeding coefficient (FROH) quantifies the genomic segments in runs of homozygosity (ROH), indicating regions where chromosome copies are identical by descent owing to recent ancestral coalescence. This coefficient ranges from 0 to 1 [37].

The coefficient of inbreeding (FROH) for each goat was estimated based ROH as described by [38]: FROH = LROH/Lauto, LROH is a measure that represents the combined length of runs of homozygosity (ROHs) found in an individual's genetic makeup, while Lauto denotes the aggregate length of autosomal regions in the goat genome that are encompassed by single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which has been determined to be 2.46 Gb [39].

Detection of selection signatures

Investigation of selection signatures for each goat breed involved estimating the occurrence of runs of homozygosity (ROH) throughout the genome. In order to identify genomic regions with a high frequency of Runs of Homozygosity (ROH), The proportion of SNP appearances was calculated for each breed. These findings were then visually displayed on chromosomes using Manhattan plots generated using the qqman package of R [40]. The determination of selected regions for each breed was guided by the ROH value, focusing on peaks that represent the top 1% of SNP markers. Moreover, the investigation employed the composite likelihood ratio (CLR) method [26] to pinpoint selection signatures resulting from artificial selection and genetic adaptation to the local environment. Computational analysis of CLR values for genomic sites in non-overlapping 50 kb windows was conducted using SweepFinder2 [41]. The top 1% of windows identified by this method are considered as potential signal candidates [8].

Functional annotation and GO enrichment

For every ROH region and CLR analysis that surpassed the designated threshold, gene functional annotations were obtained from BioMart on the Ensembl Genome Browser (https://www.ensembl.org/biomart/martview) [42], using the goat reference genome (ARS1.2) [43]. Gene functions and protein domains were then identified using literature database of PMC. The Metascape database [44] was utilized to identify enriched Gene Ontology (GO) terms, with a significance threshold established at P < 0.01.

Results

Quality control fltering and population structure

The filtering procedure led to the removal of 3 animals and 8121 SNPs, leaving behind 45,349 variants and 102 animals. In the PCA analysis, the first and second principal components (Fig. 1A) explained 17.08% and 8.36% of the genetic variability among individuals from the four breeds, respectively. The PCA analysis demonstrated clear distinctions among the studied breeds, with each breed forming a unique cluster. In addition, an investigation into ancestral admixture among populations was carried out through a model-based clustering analysis using STRUCTURE software. Hypothetical ancestral clusters (K) ranging from 2 to 4 were utilized to categorize populations according to their geographic regions (Fig. 1B). The findings indicate that LS and HG demonstrate comparable cluster affinities, as do THB and FNW, aligning with the outcomes of the PCA analysis (Fig. 1A). ROH statistics and effective population size

The total number of ROH segments found in FNW, HG, LS, and THB were 342, 567, 1285, and 180, respectively, with average counts per individual of 15.54, 29.84, 32.94, and 8.18, respectively (Table S1). In all studied breeds, the most common ROH segments were those of lengths 2–4 Mb and 4–8 Mb, as shown in the length category distribution (Fig. 2A). Conversely, ROH segments of lengths 0–2 Mb exhibited a lower frequency. Long ROH segments (8–16 Mb) and the longest ROH segments (> 16 Mb) were present in all four breeds. Mean total ROH lengths varied from 417.06 Mb in LS to 69.36 Mb in THB (Table S1). Individual analysis of ROH segments indicated substantial genomic coverage diversity within LS and comparatively lower ROH genomic coverage in THB (Fig. 2B). The mean FROH values for FNW, HG, LS, and THB breeds were 0.070, 0.128, 0.173, and 0.029, respectively (Table S1). The estimation of inbreeding per chromosome, derived from the proportion of each chromosome covered by runs of homozygosity (ROH), is illustrated in Fig. 2C for the 29 autosomes within the goat population under investigation. The effective population size (Ne) of four native goat breeds was assessed over 913 generations. Selection sweep and gene annotation

In order to identify regions characterized by a high frequency of ROH (referred to as ROH hotspots) that play a crucial role in selection, the percentage of SNP occurrences was assessed for each breed and mapped against their positions on autosomes. Manhattan plots were used to visualize the distribution of ROH in the four goat breeds (Fig. 3). The analysis revealed that FNW had the highest peak on chromosome 6 (Fig. 3A), while HG showed the highest peak on chromosome 19 (Fig. 3B). Moreover, LS exhibited the highest peak on chromosome 18 (Fig. 3C), and THB had higher peaks on chromosomes 12, 5, and 9 (Fig. 3D). The application of the top 1% SNP threshold led to the identification of 7 genes for FNW, 55 genes for HG, 22 genes for LS, and 28 genes for THB (Table S2). In addition, complex likelihood ratio (CLR) were used to detect genomic regions associated with selection in the four goat breeds (Fig. 4), and we selected the top 1% of SNP as candidate regions. In the FNW breed, 25 genes were identified across 6 selection regions (Fig. 4A and Table S3), while the HG breed had 18 genes identified in 6 selection regions (Fig. 4B and Table S3). The LS breed had 21 genes spread across 3 selection regions (Fig. 4C and Table S3), and the THB breed had 23 genes located in 8 selection regions (Fig. 4D and Table S3). Additionally, a total of 7 genes (CCSER1, MMRN1, GPRIN3, TIGD2, FAM13A, HERC5, and NAP1L5) were identified in the FNW breed using both methods. 19 overlap genes (ZDHHC7, KIAA0513, CIBAR2, GSE1, GINS2, C16orf74, IRF8, EMC8, FOXF1, MTHFSD, FOXC2, FOXL1, FBXO31, ZCCHC14, JPH3, KLHDC4, SLC7A5, CA5A, and BANP) were detected in the LS breed through the ROH and CLR tests (Table S2 and Table S3). Discussion

The identification of selection footprints in the genomic region can be utilized to pinpoint genes and mutations linked to economically significant phenotypic traits in livestock species. In this research, we analyzed genome-wide runs of homozygosity, population structure, effective population size, and signs of positive selection in four goat populations from Henan, China, employing goat SNP 50 K chips.

Four breeds were found to be genetically distinct and exhibited a separate genetic structure in the PCA results, which was attributed to the implementation of specific breeding programs (Fig. 1A). For example, The LS breed is a new meat-type goat breed developed over more than 30 years under the influence of long-term natural economic and agricultural production conditions, suitable for grazing and feeding in hilly areas of Henan province. Compared with other goat breeds, this breed has larger body, sturdy limbs, commonly known as " Bullleg Goat " goats [5]. Furthermore, the structural analysis of k = 4 revealed distinctions between the FNW breed and other populations, potentially attributable to its geographical location or human intervention, thereby impacting gene flow [5]. The Funiu White Goat is predominantly distributed in the western and southwestern regions of Henan Province, specifically in the Funiu Mountain area. This region serves as a prime example of an ecological transition zone, leading to notable distinctions between the Southern Foothill and Northern Foothill types of the Funiu White Goat [10]. These variations are distinctly evident along the northern and southern foothills of the Funiu Mountains.

Moreover, LS and HG breeds, which undergo intensive selection for crucial traits, demonstrated higher levels of inbreeding, with FROH values of 0.173 and 0.128, respectively (Table S1). Conversely, breeds not specifically managed for breeding programs (THB and FNW) showed lower inbreeding levels, with FROH values of 0.029 and 0.070, respectively (Table S1). This indicates that it is feasible to conserve local breeds with small population sizes by implementing a breeding strategy that emphasizes reduced selection pressure for production traits, thus facilitating increased genetic diversity within their genomes [8]. In addition, the length of runs of homozygosity (ROH) is a key determinant, with longer ROHs reflecting recent inbreeding or artificial selection, and shorter ROHs indicating ancient inbreeding [45]. All four breeds demonstrated the longest ROH category (> 16 Mb) (Fig. 2A), indicating recent inbreeding and a potential escalation in the prevalence of inbreeding depression in the future. Moreover, the average cumulative length of runs of homozygosity (ROH) in the LS and HG breeds was found to be higher (417.06 Mb and 307.97 Mb, respectively) compared to Turkish breeds (210.64 Mb), Central Asian breeds (260.64 Mb) [32], and Russian local breeds (79.42–183.94 Mb) [46]. In contrast, FNW and THB breeds exhibited lower ROH lengths, measuring 169.66 Mb and 69.36 Mb, respectively (Table S1), than Turkish and Central Asian breeds. This suggests that FNW and THB goats exhibit greater genetic variability compared to LS, and HG, thus indicating a higher potential for conservation as a genetic resource. Moreover, in the current study, all four breeds exhibited a reduction in effective population size (Fig. 2D), suggesting a decline in genetic diversity. This decline could be attributed to factors such as population decrease, inbreeding, or geographical variations [12, 47].

Following the analysis of top ROH hotspots and CLR, seven genes (CCSER1, MMRN1, GPRIN3, TIGD2, FAM13A, HERC5, and NAP1L5) were found to overlap in FNW goats (Table S2 and Table S3). These genes play roles in growth traits (CCSER1, MMRN1, and TIGD2) [48–50], prolificacy (GPRIN3) [51], milk production (FAM13A) [52], immune response (HERC5) [53], and embryo differentiation (NAP1L5) [54]. In addition, 19 genes were identified in LS goat by both ROH hotspots and CLR methods (Table S2 and Table S3). Such as related to feed intake (KIAA0513, ZDHHC7) [55, 56], reproduction (GSE1) [57], litter size (MTHFSD, FOXL1) [58, 59], Mammary Gland (ZCCHC14) [60], and longevity (CA5A) [61]. In addition, the identification of 11 genes (ZDHHC7, GINS2, IRF8, EMC8, FOXF1, FOXC2, FBXO31, JPH3, KLHDC4, SLC7A5, and BANP) associated with growth traits and local adaptation in LS breeds represents a novel discovery, previously recognized in cattle and chicken populations [62, 63] (Table S2 and Table S3). In both the HG and THB breeds, no overlapping genes were detected through the two methods employed. Nonetheless, key genes linked to embryo development (HOXB1-9, and HOXB13) [15], prolificacy (CA12, USP3) [64, 65], litter size (ZFAT) [66], milk production (RBM5) [67], and host immune function (MON1A and MST1R) [68] were identified specifically in the HG breeds. Likewise, genes connected to energy and digestive metabolism (TRHDE) [69], milk traits (IL20RA and IL22RA2) [70, 71], and reproduction traits (ESR2) [72] were detected in THB breeds (Table S2 and Table S3). Moreover, difference in the genes detected by these methods stems from differences in their computational algorithms. ROH is employed as a selection signal by calculating the percentage of SNP occurrences to pinpoint genomic regions with a high frequency of ROH. The composite likelihoods test has a high detection effect on the variants near fixation, which quantifies significant deviations from the neutral site frequency spectrum (SFS).

For the GO enrichment analysis, there 76 GO terms were identified using the candidate genes (Table S4). The most significant GO term were related to embryonic skeletal system morphogenesis, which including 10 genes (FOXC2, and HOXB1-9) (Table S4 and Fig. 5). The homeobox gene family plays a crucial role in vertebrate embryonic development. HOXB1 is associated with determining teat number in pigs [73] and is also involved in spermatogenesis and male fertility in rats [74]. HOXB2 and HOXB4 are implicated in sternum development, with a high mortality rate in Hoxb2 homozygous mutant mice due to a split sternum [75]. HOXB3 is essential for skeletal development in chickens [76], while HOXB5 is critical for establishing limb position along the anteroposterior axis in vertebrates [77]. HOXB6 regulates skeletal patterns in mice [78] and is associated with human infertility, along with HOXB7. HOXB7 and HOXB8 govern abdominal and thoracic segments, leading to significant morphological abnormalities [79, 80]. Additionally, HOXB9 may play a role in regulating oocyte maturation [81]. Furthermore, this study identified seven genes (FOXC2, GAS2, GNAT1, SEMA3F, SPRY2, B4GALNT2, and GJE1) associated with the GO term for growth. Previous research has linked the B4GALNT2 gene to growth characteristics such as lamb count, reproductive traits, and litter size in sheep and goats [82]. The remaining six genes were newly discovered in this study within goat breeds, specifically in relation to growth traits.

Our study involved an examination of the runs of homozygosity (ROH), effective population size, and population structure of the Henan goats. Based on the findings of this study, future genetic management and sustainable utilization efforts can consider the following improvement measures: developing a scientific breeding plan, avoiding inbreeding, and selecting individuals with high genetic diversity for mating. Collecting genetic information of Henan goats to provide data support for population management. While maintaining genetic diversity, guiding population evolution through artificial selection. Enhancing breeders' awareness and capabilities in genetic diversity conservation and breeding management to ensure the effective implementation of management measures. In addition, the investigation into regions of homozygosity (ROH) hotspots and the composite likelihood ratio (CLR) test has unveiled candidate genes and pathways affected by natural or artificial selection, particularly in the contexts of environmental adaptation, immunity, growth attributes, and reproduction within Henan breeds. This study represents a significant resource for forthcoming quantitative trait loci mapping in goat breeding.

Conclusion

Multiple selection signatures were detected in four goat breeds in Henan using runs of homozygosity (ROH) and complex likelihood ratio (CLR) analyses. The genomic regions identified harbor candidate genes linked to various traits including prolificacy, feed efficiency, immune response, local adaptation, and milk production. These findings imply that the genetic composition of Henan local goat breeds has been shaped by adaptation to specific environmental conditions. Our investigation also unveiled signs of recent genomic inbreeding within the local Henan goat population, likely stemming from closed breeding practices in small populations, potentially leading to inbreeding depression in the future. To preserve the valuable genetic diversity of Henan goat breeds, it is imperative to devise scientifically informed conservation strategies and efficient management protocols. These results deepen our comprehension of the genomic landscape and evolutionary trajectory of local Henan goat breeds, providing valuable insights for forthcoming breeding endeavors in local goat populations.

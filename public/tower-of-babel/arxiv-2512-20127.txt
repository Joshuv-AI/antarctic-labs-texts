Poisson structure on bi-graded spaces and Koszul duality, I. The classical case
Ruobing Chen, Sirui Yu

Abstract
Let ℝ m | n \mathbb{R}^{m|n} be the usual super space. It is known that the algebraic functions on ℝ m | n \mathbb{R}^{m|n} is a Koszul algebra, whose Koszul dual algebra, however, is not the set of functions on ℝ n | m \mathbb{R}^{n|m} , due to the anti-commutativity of the corresponding variables. In this paper, we show that these two algebras are isomorphic to the algebraic functions of two ℤ × ℤ \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z} -graded spaces. We then study the Poisson structures of these two spaces, and show that the quadratic Poisson structures are preserved under Koszul duality. Based on it, we obtain two isomorphic differential calculus structures, and if furthermore the Poisson structures are unimodular, then the associated Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra structures that arise on the Poisson cohomologies of these two ℤ × ℤ \mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z} -graded spaces are isomorphic as well.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{m|n}
be the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-graded super space over field over
k
, whose algebraic functions are the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
polynomial algebra

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})=\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathbb{R}}\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\},

(1)

where the gradings

|x_{i}|=0
and
|y_{j}|=1
, for

i=1,\cdots,m
and
j=1,\cdots n
.
In the special case where
n=0
or
m=0
, the function algebras on
\mathbb{R}^{m|0}
and
\mathbb{R}^{0|n}
reduce to the ordinary polynomial algebra and the exterior algebra, respectively.
If furthermore
m=n
, these two are well known to be Koszul dual to each other.

However, the situation becomes tricky when both
m
and
n
are nonzero.
Although the algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})
remains to be Koszul, its Koszul dual algebra is not the algebra of functions on any graded space.
The point is that the generators
x_{i}
commute with
y_{j}
, while their Koszul dual counterparts must anticommute.
As a result, there is no naive geometric space whose function algebra realizes the Koszul dual of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})
; in particular,
\mathbb{R}^{n|n}
is not self-dual.

This causes some problems in practice that.
In physics, both
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})
and its Koszul dual appear naturally as algebras of observables of some quantum field theory, and the duality corresponds the Boson–Fermion correspondence.
This implies that there should exist a phase space such that the Koszul dual algebra of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})
is the algebra of observables on such a space (see, for example,
[
14
]
).

The purpose of this paper is to address this issue by realizing
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n})
and its Koszul dual as functions on two
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
-graded super spaces, and study in particular the Poisson structures on them.

1.1
Koszul duality of bi-graded
spaces

We first
identify
\mathbb{R}^{m|n}

as a
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
-graded space; in
other words, the functions
on it is
again (
1
):

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m|n}):=\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathbb{R}}\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\},

(2)

but
with

\mathrm{deg}(x_{i})=(0,0)

and
\mathrm{deg}(y_{j})=(1,0)
.
We call the first argument in
the degree
the
parity
and the second
argument the
weight
.
Following the sign convention
proposed by Deligne-Morgan in

[
5
]
,
the commutativity
of the variables is given by

xy=(-1)^{p(x)p(y)+w(x)w(y)}yx,

(3)

where
p(-)
and
w(-)
denote the parity and weight respectively. The Koszul dual algebra
of (
2
)
is given by

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}):=\mathrm{Sym}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\},

(4)

where
\mathrm{deg}(\xi_{i})=(0,-1)

and
\mathrm{deg}(\eta_{j})=(-1,-1)
,
for
i=1,\cdots,m
and
j=1,\cdots,n
.
Again, the sign
for the commutativity follows
from (
3
).
From the construction,
we directly see that if we
forget the weights, (
2
)
is isomorphic to (
1
)
as graded algebras. However,
to distinguish with the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
space, we denote the underlying

\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
-graded spaces of
(
2
)
and (
4
)
by
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}

respectively. In summary, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem

2.6
)
.

With the above notations,
(
2
) and
(
4
) are Koszul
dual to each other
(in the sense of Definition

2.5
below);
in other words,

\mathbb{R}^{m||n}
and

\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}

are Koszul dual spaces.

As an application,
we study the Poisson
structures on
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}
.
They are given similarly to the
usual case. Among all the
Poisson structures on these spaces,

quadratic
Poisson structures
are of special interest.
On
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m||n})
, denote the generators
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}
by
\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{m+n}\}
;
on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m})
, denote the generators
\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\\
\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\}
by
\{\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{m+n}\}
.
A Poisson
structure on
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

is called
quadratic

if it takes the form

\pi=\sum_{i,j,p,q}c_{ij}^{pq}z_{i}z_{j}\partial_{p}\wedge\partial_{q}.

(5)

There is a map
\varphi
exchanging the variables in the following way:

\varphi:x_{i}\leftrightarrow\partial_{\xi_{i}},\quad y_{j}\leftrightarrow\partial_{\eta_{j}},\quad\partial_{x_{p}}\leftrightarrow\xi_{p},\quad\partial_{y_{q}}\leftrightarrow\eta_{q},

(6)

then we get a quadratic Poisson
structure
\pi^{!}

on
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}
,
which is called
the Koszul dual
of
\pi
.
It is straightforward to check (cf. Theorem
4.4
) that
a quadratic bivector
\pi

in the above form
(
5
) is Poisson if and only if
\pi^{!}
is Poisson.

1.2
Poisson structure
and differential calculus

We next study some algebraic
structures on the Poisson cohomology.
Recall that on a smooth
manifold, the space of
polyvector fields, together
with the wedge product
and the Schouten-Nijenhuis
bracket, forms a Gerstenhaber
algebra (see below for the
definition), while
the space of differential forms
is a graded module over
this Gerstenhaber algebra, via
the contraction (interior product)
and the Lie derivative;
and moreover, there is a compatibility
between these two action given
by the Cartan’s formula. Tamarkin
and Tsygan showed in
[
17
]

that such a phenomenon
also occurs in noncommutative
geometry, where the polyvectors
and differential forms
are now replaced by the Hochschild
cohomology and homology of an associative algebra.
They encapsulated these algebraic
structures in one structure, called

differential calculus
.

In the Poisson case, on
\mathbb{R}^{m|0}

or more generally a smooth manifold,
its Poisson cochain complex
and Poisson chain complex form
a differential calculus (see

[
9
, Chapter 3]
).
Later, such a concept
is generalized to the Frobenius Poisson
algebras by Zhu, Van Oystaeyen and Zhang in
[
21
]
. A finite dimensional
Poisson algebra is called
Frobenius

if it admits a non-degenerate
symmetric bilinear pairing. It is presented
by an element in its dual space, also
called
the volume form.
The result is, on
\mathbb{R}^{0|m}

or more generally on a Frobenius Poisson algebra, its Poisson cochain
complexes with values in itself
and with values in its linear dual space form a differential calculus
(see
[
21
]
).

The following result generalizes
the above two results to the (mixed)
general case.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
6.4
)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}

be as above.
Denoted by
A
and

A^{!}
the two
sets of algebra functions (
2
)
and (
4
)
on them respectively.
Then:

(1)

(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A))
is a differential calculus.

(2)
Similarly,

(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}))
is a differential calculus.

(3)
Under the correspondence of Table (
2
),
the above two differential calculus
structures are isomorphic.

In the above theorem,
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(-)

denotes the Poisson
cohomology, and
\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(-)
is given later, see
§
5
below
for the precise definition.

1.3
Unimodularity
and the Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra

For an oriented manifold, its space of polyvectors
is isomorphic to its space of differential forms,
via the contraction with a volume form.
However, this isomorphism may not necessarily
lead to a quasi-isomorphism between
the Poisson cochain complex and chain complex,
if the manifold is Poisson.
There is an obstruction
for them from being a quasi-isomorphism,
which arises from the fact
that the volume may not necessarily be
a Poisson cycle. If the obstruction vanishes,
then the Poisson structure is called

unimodular
, a notion
introduced by Weinstein (see
[
18
]
).
One also similarly introduce
this notion for Frobenius
Poisson algebra (see
[
21
]
).
In both cases, the corresponding
differential calculus structure
is called
differential
calculus with duality
, introduced by
Lambre in
[
8
]
.

One of the main corollaries
for unimodular Poisson structures,
or more generally, differential calculus
with duality,
is that in this case, the corresponding
cohomology has a Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebra structure.
This is an interesting result,
since Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras characterizes
the genus zero algebraic structures of
a topological conformal field theory; see,
for example,
[
6
]
.

It turns out that the above two differential
calculus structures are isomorphic
in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{m|0}

and
\mathbb{R}^{0|m}
, when equipped
with Koszul dual quadratic Poisson
structures (see
[
2
]
).
The following theorem
generalizes this result to the general case.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
7.13
)
.

Suppose
A=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{m||n})
,
A^{!}=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m})
.
Let quadratic Poisson algebras
(A,\pi)

and
(A^{!},\pi^{!})
be as above.
Given the mixed volume forms (
28
)
and (
29
) on
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}
respectively,
then:

(1)
The Poisson structure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

is unimodular if and only if
so is

\pi^{!}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}
.

(2)
In the above case,

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\cong\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})

is an isomorphism of Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebras.

In summary, although

\mathbb{R}^{m||n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}

are different from each other,
such a difference is not big:
they share many algebraic
properties, and
if they are Koszul
dual as Poisson
spaces, such properties
are even isomorphic.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proofs
of above theorems. It is organized as follows:
In §
2
we first
study the Koszul duality theory
for bi-graded spaces,
this is kind of standard
(c.f. Loday-Vallette

[
11
]
),
except that here will consider
bi-graded spaces. Theorem
1.1

is proved in this section.
In §
3
we study polyvectors and differential forms
on bi-graded algebra.
As an application, we study the Poisson
structures on
\mathbb{R}^{m||n}

and
\mathbb{R}^{n\wedge m}
in §
4

and thereafter; and in particular,
we discuss
in some detail Koszul duality for quadratic Poisson structures.
In §
5

we study the Poisson cohomology
and homology of Poisson algebras
and in §
6

we first show
the differential calculus
structure on the Poisson cohomology
and then prove Theorem
1.2
.
In §
7

we study unimodular Poisson
structures and then prove
Theorem
1.3
.

2
Koszul duality of bi-graded
algebras

In this section, we go over
Koszul duality theory for
bi-graded

algebras. The notion of Koszul algebras
is first introduced by Priddy in
[
19
]
;
for a comprehensive discussion of them,
the reader may refer to Loday-Vallette
[
11
]
,
although our notations
are slightly different
from theirs.

Throughout this paper, all vector spaces are endowed with a

\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
Recall that a

bi-graded vector space

V

is a direct sum of vector spaces indexed by
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
;
that is,
V=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}}V_{i,j}
.
An element
a\in V_{i,j}

is called
homogeneous of
degree

(i,j)
; we call
i
and
j
the
parity
and

weight
of
a
,
and denote them by
p(a)
and
w(a)

respectively.
A linear map
f:V\to W
between bi-graded
vector spaces is said to be degree
(i,j)
, if for any
(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,

f(V_{s,t})\subseteq W_{s+i,t+j}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An associative algebra
A
is called
a
bi-graded algebra
if it admits a
direct sum
A=\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}}A_{i,j}
of vector spaces with
a product mapping
A_{i_{1},j_{1}}\times A_{i_{2},j_{2}}
to

A_{i_{1}+i_{2},j_{1}+j_{2}}
.

Example 2.2
.

The
tensor algebra

generated by a
vector space
V
is the space

T(V):=k1\oplus V\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{\otimes n}\oplus\cdots

equipped with the
concatenation
product

T(V)\times T(V)\to T(V)
given by

(v_{1}\cdots v_{p},v_{p+1}\cdots v_{p+q})\mapsto v_{1}\cdots v_{p}v_{p+1}\cdots v_{p+q}.

(7)

This operation is clearly associative and
1\in V^{\otimes 0}:=k1
is
the unit. For an element
x\in V^{\otimes n}
, the integer

n
is called the
order
of
x
.

Suppose that
V
is a
finite-dimensional
vector space.
Let
R\subset V\otimes V
be a
subspace spanned
by homogeneous elements.
We call the pair
(V,R)
a
quadratic datum
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
quadratic algebra

associated to the quadratic datum
(V,R)
is

A=A(V,R):=T(V)/(R),

where
(R)
is
the two sided ideal generated by
R
in
T(V)
; that is,

A=T(V)/(R)=k\oplus V\oplus\frac{V^{\otimes 2}}{R}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{V^{\otimes n}}{\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}V^{\otimes i}\otimes R\otimes V^{\otimes n-2-i}}\oplus\cdots.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose that
A
is the quadratic
algebra associated to
(V,R)
.

(1) The
quadratic dual algebra

of
A
,
denoted by
A^{!}
,
is the quadratic algebra
associated to

(sV^{*},s\otimes s\circ R^{\perp})
, where

V^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}(V,k)
is the

k
-linear dual of
V
,

s
is the degree shifting functor
which preserves the parity and shifts
the weight down by
1
,
and
R^{\perp}
is the annihilator
of
R
.

(2) The
quadratic dual coalgebra
of
A
,
denoted by
A^{\textup{!`}}
, is
the linear dual space of
A^{!}
.

In the above definition,
it is easy to check
that in fact

A^{\textup{!`}}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}^{\textup{!`}}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-2}(s^{-1}V)^{\otimes i}\otimes(s^{-1}\otimes s^{-1}(R))\otimes(s^{-1}V)^{n-i-2},

where it is understood that
A_{0}^{\textup{!`}}=k
and
A_{1}^{\textup{!`}}=V
.
Thus
A^{\textup{!`}}
has an induced
coalgebra structure which comes
from the co-concatenation
coproduct on

T(s^{-1}V)

converse to (
7
).
Also, from the above definition,
one has
(A^{!})^{!}=A
.

Choose a set of basis
\{e_{i}\}
for
V
,
and let
\{e^{*}_{i}\}
be the
dual basis in
V^{*}
.
Consider the following sequence
of maps, there is a chain complex associated to
A
,
called the
(left) Koszul complex
:

\displaystyle\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\otimes A^{\textup{!`}}_{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\otimes A^{\textup{!`}}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\longrightarrow A\otimes A^{\textup{!`}}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}k,

(8)

where
for any
r\otimes f\in A\otimes A^{\textup{!`}}
,

d(r\otimes f)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{w(r)}re_{i}\otimes se_{i}^{*}f,

and

d:A\otimes A^{\textup{!`}}_{0}=A\otimes k\cong A\to k
is the augmentation map.

It is straightforward to see that
d^{2}=0
. By swapping the
components in the tensor products in the above complex,
we also get the
right Koszul complex
by analogy.
Take left Koszul complex of
A
as a left twisted tensor product

A\otimes_{\kappa}A^{\textup{!`}}
,
\kappa:=A^{\textup{!`}}\twoheadrightarrow s^{-1}V\xrightarrow{s}V\rightarrowtail A
is a twisting morphism.
Similarly, the right Koszul complex of
A
is also expressed as

A^{\textup{!`}}\otimes_{\kappa}A
.

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose that
A
is a quadratic algebra.
Then
A
is called a
Koszul algebra

if its Koszul complex (
8
) is acyclic.

One immediately gets that if
A=A(V,R)
is Koszul, then
A^{!}
is also Koszul. In fact,
by
[
11
, Theorem 2.3.1]
, the left
Koszul complex is acyclic is equivalent to the right Koszul
complex is acyclic.
The Koszul complex of
A^{!}
is

\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{!}\otimes(A^{!})^{\textup{!`}}_{i+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{!}\otimes(A^{!})^{\textup{!`}}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\longrightarrow A^{!}\otimes(A^{!})^{\textup{!`}}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}k,

and its linear dual is exactly the right Koszul complex of

A
, up to a degree shifting.
Suppose
A
is a Koszul algebra, in what follows we call
the quadratic dual algebra
A^{!}
and coalgebra
A^{\textup{!`}}

its
Koszul dual algebra
and
coalgebra

respectively.

2.1
Two main examples

In this paper, we mainly concern
the following two bi-graded algebras:

\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}):=\mathrm{Sym}\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\},

(9)

with

\mathrm{deg}(x_{i})=(0,0)

and

\mathrm{deg}(y_{j})=(1,0)
and

\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}):=\mathrm{Sym}\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\},

(10)

with

\mathrm{deg}(\xi_{i})=(0,-1)

and

\mathrm{deg}(\eta_{1})=(-1,-1)
.
We have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6

(Theorem

1.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})

and
\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})

be as above.
Then both of them are Koszul algebras
and Koszul dual to each other.

Proof.

From its
definition (
9
),
it is direct to see that

\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
is a quadratic algebra
T(V)/(R)
, where

V=\mathrm{span}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}

(11)

and

R=\mathrm{span}\left\{x_{i}x_{j}-x_{j}x_{i},y_{u}y_{v}+y_{v}y_{u},x_{i}y_{u}-y_{u}x_{i}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}i,j=1,\cdots,m,\\
u,v=1,\cdots,n\end{array}\right.\right\}.

By the definition of quadratic dual
algebra, we have

\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{!}=T(sV^{*})/(s\otimes s\circ R^{\bot}),

where

V^{*}=\mathrm{span}\{x_{1}^{*},\dots,x_{m}^{*},y_{1}^{*},\dots,y_{n}^{*}\},

and

R^{\bot}=\mathrm{span}\left\{x_{i}^{*}x_{j}^{*}+x_{j}^{*}x_{i}^{*},y_{u}^{*}y_{v}^{*}-y_{v}^{*}y_{u}^{*},x_{i}^{*}y_{u}^{*}+y_{u}^{*}x_{i}^{*}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}i,j=1,\cdots,m,\\
u,v=1,\cdots,n\end{array}\right.\right\}.

So we have

s\otimes s\circ R^{\bot}=\mathrm{span}\left\{sx_{i}^{*}sx_{j}^{*}+sx_{j}^{*}sx_{i}^{*},sy_{u}^{*}sy_{v}^{*}-sy_{v}^{*}sy_{u}^{*},sx_{i}^{*}sy_{u}^{*}+sy_{u}^{*}sx_{i}^{*}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}i,j=1,\cdots,m,\\
u,v=1,\cdots,n\end{array}\right.\right\}.

On the other hand, by its definition (
10
), the
algebra
\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})

also admits a quadratic algebra presentation

T(\tilde{V})/(\tilde{R})
, where

\tilde{V}=\mathrm{span}\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\}

and

\tilde{R}=\mathrm{span}\left\{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}+\xi_{j}\xi_{i},\eta_{u}\eta_{v}-\eta_{v}\eta_{u},\xi_{i}\eta_{u}+\eta_{u}\xi_{i}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}i,j=1,\cdots,m,\\
u,v=1,\cdots,n\end{array}\right.\right\}.

Under the identifications

\displaystyle sx_{i}^{*}\leftrightarrow\xi_{i},\quad sy_{j}^{*}\leftrightarrow\eta_{j},\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,m,\quad j=1,\cdots,n,

we have
\tilde{V}\simeq sV^{*}
and

\tilde{R}\simeq s\otimes s\circ R^{\bot}
.
It induces the isomorphism of the two algebras
\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})

and
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{!}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
and

\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})
are quadratic
dual to each other.

We next show that
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
is Koszul.
Notice that a homogeneous element

v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q}\in\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})_{q}
has the property

v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q}=(-1)^{p(v_{i})p(v_{i+1})+w(v_{i})w(v_{i+1})}v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i+1}\wedge v_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q}

for
i=1,\cdots,q
.
Similarly, a homogeneous element

u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}\in{\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{\textup{!`}}}_{p}

has the property

u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}=(-1)^{p(u_{i})p(u_{i+1})+w(u_{i})w(u_{i+1})}u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{i+1}\wedge u_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}

for
i=1,\cdots,q
.
The Koszul boundary map

\displaystyle d:\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})_{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{\textup{!`}}}_{p}\to\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})_{q+1}\otimes{\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{\textup{!`}}}_{p-1}

is given by

\displaystyle(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q},u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{\epsilon}(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q}\wedge su_{j},u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{u_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}),

where
\epsilon=\sum_{s=1}^{j-1}(p(u_{j})p(u_{s})+w(u_{j})w(u_{s}))
.
Consider the map

\displaystyle h:\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})_{q}\otimes{\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{\textup{!`}}}_{p}\to\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})_{q-1}\otimes{\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})^{\textup{!`}}}_{p+1}

defined on homogeneous elements by

\displaystyle(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q},u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p})\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{\varepsilon}(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{v_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q},s^{-1}v_{j}\wedge u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}),

where
\varepsilon=\sum_{t=j+1}^{q}(p(v_{j})p(v_{t})+w(v_{j})w(v_{t}))
.
It is direct to see
that
d\circ h+h\circ d=(p+q)id
,
and
the identity map is homotopic to
0
.
Therefore, the Koszul complex is acyclic,
and thus
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
is a Koszul algebra.

Since
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
is quadratic dual to

\mathcal{O}(k^{m\wedge n})
, we have
\mathcal{O}(k^{m\wedge n})
is also Koszul. It follows that they are Koszul dual to each other.
∎

Recall that
the algebra
\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})

of the graded space

k^{m|n}

(see, for example,
[
5
]
)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
commutative
algebra
generated by

\{\tilde{x}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{x}_{m},\tilde{y}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{y}_{n}\},

where

\deg(\tilde{x}_{i})=0
and

\deg(\tilde{y}_{j})=1
.
Consider the following map
of associative algebras

\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})\to\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}):\tilde{x}_{i}\mapsto x_{i},\,\tilde{y}_{j}\mapsto y_{j},

for
i=1,\cdots,m,\ j=1,\cdots,n
.
It is straightforward to see that
this is in fact an isomorphism
of bi-graded commutative
algebras. In summary, we have
the following.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})
and
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
be as given above.
Then

\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})\cong\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})
as
bi-graded commutative algebras.

Another result relating
the bi-graded super space
and the usual super space is the following,
whose proof is straightforward and hence omitted:

Proposition 2.8

There are canonical isomorphisms

k^{m||0}\cong k^{m}(\cong k^{m|0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad k^{0\wedge n}\cong k^{0|n},

3
Differential forms and polyvectors

Many algebraic structures of bi-graded
Koszul algebras do not differ much from
those of usual graded Koszul algebras.
From now on, we study an example where
some phenomena do not exist in the usual Koszul
algebra case and
introduce bi-graded Koszul algebras which
lead to some interesting results.

As we have mentioned in §
1
, the
graded algebra

\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})
for nonzero
m
and
n

is Koszul but its Koszul dual algebra is
not graded symmetric. Thus, even though there
are many Poisson structures on

, it does not make sense
to talk about their Koszul dual Poisson
structures.
However, in §

we have shown
that
\mathcal{O}(k^{m|n})

is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
as bi-graded
commutative algebras
(see Proposition
2.7
),
and the Koszul dual algebra of latter,

\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})
,
is again bi-graded commutative.
In this section, we
study the differential
forms and polyvectors of a bi-graded
commutative algebra.

3.1
Kähler differential
and differential forms

Definition 3.1

(Kähler differential)
.

Suppose

is a bi-graded commutative algebra.
The
A

denoted by

-module

\underline{d}x

where

From the Kähler differentials,
one obtains that the space of 1-forms
\Omega^{1}(A)

of

\underline{\Omega}(A)
but with
the weights on the elements shifted by 1.
The image of
\underline{d}x
, for any

under this weight shifting is
denoted by

.
One may think of

as a
k
-linear operator
of degree
(0,1)

that vanishes on

and
has the (bi-graded) Leibniz

In what follows, we shall use both

\underline{\Omega}(A)

and

More generally, we have the following.

Definition 3.2

(Differential forms)
.

For

, the

-module of

The

Extend the degree

to

Example 3.3
.

Suppose

Definition 3.4

(Derivation)
.

Suppose
A
is a bi-graded algebra.
A

of

with values in
A
-module
M

a
k
-linear map

such that
\gamma(ab)=\gamma(a)b+a\gamma(b)

for all elements
a,b\in A
and

\gamma(c)=0
for
c\in k

The set of derivations is denoted by

\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)
.
If

, we write
\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)

simply by
\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For a bi-graded commutative algebra
A
and an

A
-module
M
, there is an isomorphism

\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\underline{\Omega}(A),M).

Proof.

Let

\phi:\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\underline{\Omega}(A),M)

be the map

\phi(D)(\underline{d}x):=D(x)
for any derivation
D\in\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)

and
x\in A
.
We first have to show
\phi

is well-defined. In fact,

(1)

\phi(D)(\underline{d}(xy))=D(xy)=D(x)y+xD(y)=\phi(D)(\underline{d}x)y+x\phi(D)(\underline{d}y)
;

(2)

\phi(D)(\underline{d}x+\underline{d}y)=\phi(D)(\underline{d}(x+y))=D(x+y)=D(x)+D(y)=\phi(D)(\underline{d}x)+\phi(D)(\underline{d}y)
,

for any
x,y\in A
.
This means

\phi(D)\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\underline{\Omega}(A),M)
.

Now suppose
D,\tilde{D}\in\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)
, such that

\phi(D)=\phi(\tilde{D})
. Then, for all
x\in A
,

\phi(D)(\underline{d}x)=D(x)=\tilde{D}(x)=\phi(\tilde{D})(\underline{d}x),

which implies
D=\tilde{D}
. Hence,
\phi
is injective.

Finally, for any
D\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\underline{\Omega}(A),M)
, define a derivation

\tilde{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)

such
that

\tilde{D}(x)=D(\underline{d}x)
, for any
x\in A
.
Then

\tilde{D}(xy)=D(\underline{d}(xy))=D(\underline{d}x)y+xD(\underline{d}y)=\tilde{D}(x)y+x\tilde{D}(y).

Thus
\tilde{D}\in\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A,M)
.
From our construction,
we have
\phi(\tilde{D})=D
,
which proves that
\phi
is surjective.

Since
\phi
is both injective and surjective, it is a bijection.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
The Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket

In this subsection, we study the
Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
defined on polyvectors.
For an element

, we write
the corresponding derivation
in
\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A)

as
\overline{D}
, and vice versa;
sometimes, we also use

for such an identification.
In the following, for simplify the notations, we write

|x|:=\mathrm{deg}(x),|x|+|y|:=(p(x)+p(y),w(x)+w(y)),|x||y|:=p(x)p(y)+w(x)w(y)\ \mathrm{and}\ \partial_{i}:=\partial_{z_{i}}.

Now suppose
A
is a bi-graded commutative
algebra. For
any
\overline{X},\overline{Y}\in\overline{\mathfrak{X}}(A)
,
their commutator

[\overline{X},\overline{Y}](f):=\overline{X}(\overline{Y}(f))-(-1)^{|\overline{X}||\overline{Y}|}\overline{Y}(\overline{X}(f)),\quad\mbox{for any}\,f\in A,

defines a derivation on
A
.
This leads to the following.

Definition 3.8

(Schouten-Nijenhuis
bracket)
.

For any two polyvectors

Y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{p}

and
Z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Z_{q}

in
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A)
,
their Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
is given by

\displaystyle[Y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{p}\ ,\ Z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Z_{q}]

(12)

\displaystyle:=\sum^{p,q}_{s=1,t=1}(-1)^{\varepsilon}Y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{Y}_{s}\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_{p}\wedge s([\overline{Y}_{s},\overline{Z}_{t}])\wedge Z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{Z}_{t}\wedge\cdots\wedge Z_{q},

(13)

where
\varepsilon=|Z_{t}|\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|Z_{i}|+|Y_{s}|\sum_{j=s+1}^{p}|Y_{j}|
.

From the above definition, the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
has the following properties:

(1)

the bi-graded skew commutativity:

\displaystyle[P,Q]=-(-1)^{(|P|-|s|)(|Q|-|s|)}[Q,P];

(2)

the bi-graded Jacobi identity:

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|P|-|s|)(|S|-|s|)}\big[[P,Q],S\big]+(-1)^{(|P|-|s|)(|Q|-|s|)}\big[[Q,S],P\big]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(|Q|-|s|)(|S|-|s|)}\big[[S,P],Q\big]=0;

(3)

the bi-graded Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[P,\,Q\wedge S]=[P,Q]\wedge S+(-1)^{|Q|(|P|-|s|)}Q\wedge[P,S],

for any homogeneous elements
P,Q,S\in\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A)
.

which acts on generators by

\tilde{d}(z_{i})=dz_{i},i\leq m\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\tilde{d}(\partial_{j})=z_{j}^{*},j\geq m+1.

Then

\displaystyle\tilde{d}:\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)\to\tilde{\Omega}^{1}(A):=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}dz_{i}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{l}f_{j}z_{j}^{*}\mid f_{i},f_{j}\in\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)\Big\}.

Definition 3.14

(Mixed differential form)
.

For
p\geq 0
, the
\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
-module of

mixed differential
p
-forms
, denoted by
\tilde{\Omega}^{p}(A)
,
is a bi-graded symmetric
\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
-module generated by

\tilde{\Omega}^{1}(A)
; that is,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{p}(A)=\mathrm{Sym}^{p}_{\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)}(\tilde{\Omega}^{1}(A)).

The
total space of mixed differential forms
of
A
, is defined to be

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A):=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\tilde{\Omega}^{p}(A)=\mathrm{Sym}\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{m},\partial_{m+1},\cdots,\partial_{l},dz_{1},\cdots,dz_{m},z_{m+1}^{*},\cdots,z_{l}^{*}\}.

Extend the degree
(0,1)
map
\tilde{d}
to
\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)

by derivation and by letting
\tilde{d}^{2}=0
.
The map
\tilde{d}
is a differential of degree
(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\tilde{d}(\omega\wedge\eta)=\tilde{d}\omega\wedge\eta+(-1)^{|\omega||\tilde{d}|}\omega\wedge\tilde{d}\eta,

for any
\omega,\eta\in\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)
.

Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be the pairing between the generators of

\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A)
and
\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)
:

\langle dz_{i},\partial_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij},i\leq m\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\langle z_{s}^{*},z_{t}\rangle=\delta_{st},s\geq m+1.

Definition 3.15

(Mixed vector field)
.

Denote the algebra of
mixed vector field
of
A
by
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
,

\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A):=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}\partial_{i}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{l}g_{j}z_{j}\mid g_{i},g_{j}\in\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)\Big\}\subset{\mathfrak{X}}^{\bullet}(A).

For any
p\geq 1
, the
mixed
p
-vector fields
, denoted by
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{p}(A)
, is a bi-graded symmetric
\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
-module generated by
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
; that is,

\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{p}(A):=\mathrm{Sym}_{\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)}^{p}(\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A))\\
\subset\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A).

A vector
X\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
defines a map

\tilde{\iota}_{X}
on
\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)
that shares the same degree with
X
.
The operator
\tilde{\iota}_{X}
acts on mixed 1-form is

\tilde{\iota}_{X}\omega=\omega(X)=\langle\omega,X\rangle
.
Then it induces a natural action of
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
on

\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)
.

Definition 3.16

(Mixed contraction)
.

For any vector
X\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{X}:\tilde{\Omega}^{p}(A)\to\tilde{\Omega}^{p-1}(A)

is the
\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
-linear map defined by the property that

\displaystyle(\tilde{\iota}_{X}\omega)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1})=\omega(X,X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1})

(16)

for any vectors
X_{1},\cdots,X_{p-1}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
.
When
\omega
is a mixed
0
-form,
\iota_{X}\omega=0
by convention.
The differential form
\tilde{\iota}_{X}\omega
is called the

mixed contraction
of
\omega
with
X
.

The mixed contraction map satisfies the Leibniz rule: for any homogeneous
elements
\alpha,\beta\in\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{X}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=(\tilde{\iota}_{X}\alpha)\wedge\beta+(-1)^{|\alpha||X|}\alpha\wedge(\tilde{\iota}_{X}\beta).

(17)

And we extend the mixed contraction to
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A)
by

\tilde{\iota}_{X\wedge Y}:=\tilde{\iota}_{X}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Y}.

Proposition 3.17

(Mixed Cartan’s formula)
.

Given a bi-graded space
\Sigma
and
A=\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)
as above,
for any homogeneous mixed polyvector field
P,Q
,

\displaystyle[[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Q}]=\tilde{\iota}_{[P,Q]}.

(18)

Proof.

First, we need to prove that
[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\omega)=\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}\omega
for any
X,Y\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
and any homogeneous elements

\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}^{s}(A)
,
s\geq 0
.
We proceed by induction on
p
.
When
s=0
and
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
,
[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\omega)=0=\tilde{\iota}_{[P,Q]}\omega.

Then we check
[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\tilde{d}f)=0=\tilde{\iota}_{[P,Q]}(\tilde{d}f)
, for any
f\in\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
.
By the definition of Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket, we have

\displaystyle[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\tilde{d}f)=

\displaystyle(\tilde{\iota}_{X}\circ\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Y}-(-1)^{|\tilde{d}||X|}\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{X}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Y}-(-1)^{(|X|+|\tilde{d}|)|Y|}\tilde{\iota}_{Y}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{X}\circ\tilde{d}

(19)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(|X|+|\tilde{d}|)|Y|+|\tilde{d}||X|}\tilde{\iota}_{Y}\circ\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{X})(\tilde{d}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tilde{\iota}_{X}\circ\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Y}+(-1)^{(|X|+|\tilde{d}|)|Y|+|\tilde{d}||X|}\tilde{\iota}_{Y}\circ\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{X})(\tilde{d}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}(\tilde{d}f).

Now we assume that (
18
) holds for
\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}^{s}(A)
,

s>0
. Consider
\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}
acting on
\tilde{d}f\wedge\omega
,

f\in\tilde{\Omega}^{0}(A)
.
By (
17
), (
19
) and the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}(\tilde{d}f\wedge\omega)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}(\tilde{d}f)\wedge\omega+(-1)^{|[X,Y]||\tilde{d}f|}\tilde{d}f\wedge\tilde{\iota}_{[X,Y]}(\omega)

\displaystyle=[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\iota_{Y}](\tilde{d}f)\wedge\omega+(-1)^{|[X,Y]||\tilde{d}f|}\tilde{d}f\wedge[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\omega)

\displaystyle=[[\tilde{\iota}_{X},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Y}](\tilde{d}f\wedge\omega).

This completes the induction on
s
.

Now we prove that
[[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{Q}]=\tilde{\iota}_{[P,Q]}
for
any
P\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
,
Q\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{q}(A)
.
Again, we proceed it by induction on
q
.
When
q=1
, we already proved this.
Assumed that it holds for
Q\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{q}(A)
,
q>1
, consider

\tilde{\iota}_{[P,S\wedge Q]}
for
S\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{1}(A)
.
By the Leibniz rule of mixed contraction map and the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{[P,S\wedge Q]}=

\displaystyle\tilde{\iota}_{[P,S]\wedge Q}+(-1)^{|P||S|+|S||s|}\tilde{\iota}_{S\wedge[P,Q]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]\circ\tilde{\iota}_{S}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Q}-(-1)^{|S|(|P|+|\tilde{d}|)}\tilde{\iota}_{S}\circ[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Q}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|P||S|+|S||s|}\tilde{\iota}_{S}\circ[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Q}-(-1)^{|P||S|+|S||s|+|Q|(|P|+|\tilde{d}|)}\tilde{\iota}_{S}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Q}\circ[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]\circ\tilde{\iota}_{S\wedge Q}-(-1)^{(|S|+|Q|)(|P|+|\tilde{d}|)}\tilde{\iota}_{S\wedge Q}\circ[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}],\tilde{\iota}_{S\wedge Q}].

This completes the induction on
q
.

Now we verify that (
18
) holds for any
P\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{p}(A)
,
Q\in\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{q}(A)
.
We proceed by induction on
p
.
Due to the skew-symmetry of the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket and the commutator, (
18
) holds for
P
by following the same procedure as
Q
, thus completing the claim.
∎

4
Poisson
structures

From now on, we give some
applications of the Koszul
duality theory developed
in the previous sections. Our
primary interest is the Poisson
structures on
k^{m||n}
and

k^{n\wedge m}
.
Shoikhet observed in
[
15
]

that,
for

k^{m|0}

and
k^{0|m}
,
a bi-vector with quadratic
coefficients
\pi

on
k^{m|0}
is Poisson
if and only if the corresponding dual
bi-vector
\pi^{!}
on
k^{0|m}

is Poisson.
He then showed that for
k=\mathbb{C}
,
Kontsevich’s
deformation quantizations, via Tamarkin’s
approach, of these
two Poisson structures remain to be
Koszul dual to each other.
The rest of the paper may be viewed
as a generalization of Shoikhet’s results
to bi-graded super spaces, while
in the current paper, we only study
the invariance of
some algebraic structures arising
from the Poisson structures
under Koszul
duality, while leaving the deformation
quantization problem to the future.

Definition 4.1

(Poisson structure)
.

Given a bi-graded commutative algebra
A
with its bivector field
space

\mathfrak{X}^{2}(A)
.
A polyvector
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(A)
is said to be

Poisson bivector field
(
Poisson structure
) if
[\pi,\pi]=0
.

Given a bivector field
\pi
on
A
, it defines a bilinear operation

\{-,-\}:A\otimes A\to A
by

\displaystyle\{f,g\}:=\pi(df,dg),\ \mathrm{for\ any}\ f,g\in A.

Since
\pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(A)
, the skew commutativity and Leibniz rule are satisfied.
By direct computation, the Jacobi identity is equivalent to the condition

[\pi,\pi]=0
.
So we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\pi
be a Poisson bivetor field on
A
.
Then, for any homogeneous elements
f,g,h\in A
, the bracket
\{-,-\}

satisfies the following properties:

(1)

the bi-graded skew commutativity:

\{f,g\}=-(-1)^{|f||g|}\{g,f\};

(2)

the bi-graded Jacobi identity:

\displaystyle(-1)^{|f||h|}\{f,\{g,h\}\}+(-1)^{|g||h|}\{h,\{f,g\}\}+(-1)^{|g||f|}\{g,\{h,f\}\}=0;

(3)

the bi-graded Leibniz rule:

\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+(-1)^{|g||f|}\{f,h\}g.

A bracket
\{-,-\}
defined by a Poisson bivector field

\pi
is called a
Poisson bracket
on
A
.
Such a bi-graded algebra
A
equipped with a Poisson
structure is called a
bi-graded Poisson algebra
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a bi-graded space, and let
\{z_{1},\cdots z_{l}\}

be the coordinates of
\Sigma
. A Poisson structure
\pi
on it is
called
quadratic
, if it is of the form

\pi=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}}C^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}{\partial_{{j_{1}}}}\wedge{\partial_{{j_{2}}}},

where
C^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}\in k
for any
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}=1,\cdots,l.

In the following,
to simplify the notations,
for
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})=\mathrm{Sym}\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}

and

\mathcal{O}(k^{m\wedge n})=\mathrm{Sym}\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n}\},

we write their
generators as
z_{i}
and

\zeta_{i}
respectively.
We give a correspondence
between the sets of polyvectors on
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
and
\mathcal{O}(k^{m\wedge n})
,
which exchanges the variables as follows:

z_{i}\leftrightarrow{\partial_{\zeta_{i}}},\quad{\partial_{z_{i}}}\leftrightarrow\zeta_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,m+n.

Via this correspondence,
a quadratic bivector field
\pi=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}}C^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}{\partial_{{j_{1}}}}\wedge{\partial_{{j_{2}}}}
on
\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
corresponds to a quadratic bivector field
\pi^{!}
on
\mathcal{O}(k^{m\wedge n})
, and vice versa.

Theorem 4.4
.

Under the above correspondence, a quadratic bivector field

\pi
on
k^{m||n}
is Poisson if and only if the corresponding quadratic bivector field
\pi^{!}
on
k^{n\wedge m}
is Poisson.

Proof.

We denote the Poisson bracket defined by
\pi=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}}C^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}{\partial_{{j_{1}}}}\wedge{\partial_{{j_{2}}}}
acting on

as

\omega^{ij}:=\{z_{i},z_{j}\}=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}}C^{i_{1},i_{2}}_{i,j}z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}.

The Jacobi identity is satisfied if

\displaystyle(-1)^{|z_{j}||z_{t}|}\omega^{ji}\partial_{z_{i}}\omega^{st}+(-1)^{|z_{s}||z_{j}|}\omega^{si}\partial_{z_{i}}\omega^{tj}+(-1)^{|z_{t}||z_{s}|}\omega^{ti}\partial_{z_{i}}\omega^{js}=0,

(20)

for any
i,j,s,t=1,\cdots,m+n
.

Fixing

, for any

, we consider
the coefficients of

on (
20
).
It consists of three parts: the coefficients of

and
z_{c}z_{a}z_{b}

Combining above the three coefficients, (

\displaystyle\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }z_{a}z_{b}z_{c}+(-1)^{|z_{a}||z_{b}|+|z_{a}||z_{c}|}\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }z_{b}z_{c}z_{a}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|z_{a}||z_{c}|+|z_{b}||z_{c}|}\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }z_{c}z_{a}z_{b}=0.

(21)

We denote the dual Poisson bracket acts on
\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j}
by
\theta^{ij}:=\{\zeta_{i},\zeta_{j}\}

the Jacobi identity is satisfied if

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\zeta_{a}||\zeta_{c}|}\theta^{ai}\partial_{\zeta_{i}}\theta^{bc}+(-1)^{|\zeta_{b}||\zeta_{a}|}\theta^{bi}\partial_{\zeta_{i}}\theta^{ca}+(-1)^{|\zeta_{c}||\zeta_{b}|}\theta^{ci}\partial_{\zeta_{i}}\theta^{ab}=0,

(22)

for any

Fixing

\zeta_{j}\zeta_{s}\zeta_{t}
on
(

It consists of three parts: the coefficients of
\zeta_{j}\zeta_{s}\zeta_{t}
,

\zeta_{s}\zeta_{t}\zeta_{j}
and
\zeta_{t}\zeta_{j}\zeta_{s}
.
Combining above the three coefficients, (
22

\displaystyle\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }\zeta_{j}\zeta_{s}\zeta_{t}+(-1)^{|z_{j}||z_{s}|+|z_{j}||z_{t}|}\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }\zeta_{s}\zeta_{t}\zeta_{j}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|z_{j}||z_{t}|+|z_{s}||z_{t}|}\mathrm{coefficients\ of\ }\zeta_{t}\zeta_{j}\zeta_{s}=0.

(23)

After fixing

4

(
4

Then, we let

5
Poisson cohomology and homology

In this section, we first study
the Poisson cohomologies of

(k^{m||n},\pi)

and
(k^{n\wedge m},\pi^{!})
,
where
\pi

and
\pi^{!}
are Koszul dual Poisson
structures, and show that they are
in fact isomorphic.
After that, we go to the Poincaré
dual sides of the Poisson
cohomologies of
(k^{m||n},\pi)

and
(k^{n\wedge m},\pi^{!})

respectively.

It turns out
there are two different types
of homologies which serve
as the Poincaré dual for each
of these Poisson cohomologies.
They arise
from the Poincaré duality
of the Poisson cohomology of

k^{n|0}
and

k^{0|m}
,
and are mixed with each other
in our case. Nevertheless, we
show that for the Koszul
dual pair
(k^{m||n},\pi)

and
(k^{n\wedge m},\pi^{!})
,
these two Poisson complexes
are quasi-isomorphic.

5.1
Poisson cohomology

The notion of Poisson cohomology was introduced by Lichnerowicz.
In our case, we consider the Poisson cohomology on bi-graded
commutative algebras

A=\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n})
and
A^{!}=\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose that
(A,\pi)
is a bi-graded Poisson algebra.
The
Poisson cochain complex

(\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\delta^{\pi}_{\bullet,\bullet})
is defined as follows. For any

(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}(A):=\mathfrak{X}^{s,t}(A)=\{P\in\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(A)\mid\mathrm{deg}(P)=(-s,-t)\}

and the coboundary map
\delta^{\pi}
is defined by

\displaystyle\delta^{\pi}_{s,t}:=[-,\pi]:\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}(A)\to\bigoplus_{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{CP}^{i,j}(A).

The associated cohomology

\mathrm{HP}^{s,t}(A)

is called
Poisson cohomology
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)
is a bi-graded Poisson algebra.
Regarding the degree of coboundary map, for the quadratic Poisson
bivector field on
k^{m||n}
, we only consider the
k
-linear
combination of the following types:

\displaystyle x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{x_{a}}\wedge\partial_{x_{b}},\quad y_{u}y_{v}\partial_{y_{s}}\wedge\partial_{y_{t}},\quad x_{i}y_{s}\partial_{x_{a}}\wedge\partial_{y_{t}},

(24)

where
i,j,a,b=1,\cdots,m
and
s,t,u,v=1,\cdots,n
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^{!})
is the
bi-graded Poisson algebra.
For the quadratic Poisson bivector field on
k^{n\wedge m}
,
we only consider
the
k
-linear combination of the following types:

\displaystyle\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\partial_{\xi_{a}}\wedge\partial_{\xi_{b}},\quad\eta_{u}\eta_{v}\partial_{\eta_{s}}\wedge\partial_{\eta_{t}},\quad\xi_{i}\eta_{s}\partial_{\xi_{a}}\wedge\partial_{\eta_{t}}

(25)

where
i,j,a,b=1,\cdots,m
and
s,t,u,v=1,\cdots,n
.

When
\pi
is the linear combination of the forms (
24
) and
(
25
), the coboundary map
\delta^{\pi}
is

\delta^{\pi}_{s,t}:\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}(A)\to\mathrm{CP}^{s,t-1}(A).

The next theorem gives an isomorphism
of the above two Poisson cohomologies
under Koszul duality.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)
be a quadratic Poisson algebra
and
A^{!}=(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^{!})
be the Koszul dual
Poisson algebra of
A
.
Then there is an isomorphism

\quad\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\cong\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}).

Proof.

From the above definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet,\bullet}\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},\partial_{y_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{y_{n}},\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{x_{m}},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet,\bullet}\{\partial_{\xi_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{\xi_{m}},\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m},\partial_{\eta_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{\eta_{n}}\},

where
\mathrm{Sym}^{i,j}\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{l}\}:=\{f\in\mathrm{Sym}\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{l}\}\mid\mathrm{deg}(f)=(i,j)\}
.
Under the correspondence given in Table
2
,
we obtain an isomorphism of cochain complexes

\Psi:\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\cong\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}).

The coboundary map
\delta^{\pi}=[-,\pi]
under the
correspondence is exactly the dual coboundary map

\delta^{\pi^{!}}=[-,\pi^{!}]
,
which induces an isomorphism on the cohomologies.
∎

5.2
Poisson
chain complexes on
k^{m||n}
and
k^{n\wedge m}

In this and the subsequent subsections,
we introduce (mixed) complexes associated
to the Poisson structures on
k^{m||n}
and
k^{n\wedge m}
respectively.
Since they are new in this form, we give some
backgrounds about our construction of them.

Consider
k^{m||0}
with
a Poisson structure
\pi
. Then the set
of
differential forms on
k^{m||0}
,

\Omega^{\bullet}(k^{m||0})

together with the Poisson boundary map, is usually
called the Poisson chain complex, computes
the Poisson homology. Moreover,
there is a volume form

dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}\in\Omega^{n}(k^{m||0})
,
such that the contraction
of elements in the Poisson cochain complex
with the volume form
gives an isomorphism between
the polyvectors and differential forms.
However, this is in general
not an isomorphism of complexes,
since the volume form may not be a Poisson
cycle. If the volume form is a Poisson
cycle, then the Poisson structure
is called
unimodular
.

On the dual side, for
k^{0||m}
,
suppose it is equipped with
a Poisson structure
\pi
. In this case,
we consider a Poisson
complex whose underlying
space is the linear dual
space of differential forms
on
k^{0|m}
, or equivalently,
the space of polyvectors
with values in
the linear dual space
of
\mathcal{O}(k^{0|m})
.
The reason we consider
that a complex is that,
in this case,
we would not have a natural
isomorphism from the space
of polyvectors
to the space of
differential forms, but to
the linear dual of them.
Like the
k^{m|0}

case, the above isomorphism
may not be an isomorphism
of Poisson complexes;
and if it is, the Poisson
structure is again
called unimodular.

Now let us turn to
the
k^{m||n}

and
k^{n\wedge m}

cases. In these two cases,
we construct Poisson
complexes which come
from combining the above
types of Poisson complexes.
For
k^{m||n}
,
the underlying
space is the tensor product
of differential forms
on
k^{m||0}

and polyvectors on

k^{0||n}
;
while for
k^{n\wedge m}
,
the underlying space is the tensor
product of polyvectors
on
k^{0\wedge m}

and differential forms
on
k^{n\wedge 0}
,
with appropriate coefficients
taken into account.

5.2.1
Poisson complex on
k^{m||n}

Let the bi-graded space

or

Define a linear map
\partial_{\pi}:=[\tilde{\iota}_{\pi},\tilde{d}]:\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet}(A)\to\tilde{\Omega}^{\bullet-1}(A)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A=(\mathcal{O}(\Sigma),\pi)
be a bi-graded Poisson algebra.
And
\pi

24
) and
(

\partial_{\pi}

\tilde{d}
,
\tilde{\iota}_{\pi}

Proof.

Due to mixed Cartan’s formula (

For the commutator with

\displaystyle[\partial_{\pi},\tilde{d}]

6
The differential
calculus structure

In this section, we study some algebraic structures relating the Poisson cohomology and
homology, which are summarized into a notion called
differential calculus
, introduced by
Tamarkin and Tsygan in
[
17
]
.

As mentioned in the introduction,
the differential calculus structure
exists on the Poisson cohomology
and homology of Poisson algebras (or
Poisson manifolds); see, for example,

[
9
]
.
Recently, inspired by Shoikhet’s
observation, Chen et. al.

[
3
]

showed that, for Koszul dual Poisson
spaces
\mathbb{R}^{m|0}
and
\mathbb{R}^{0|m}
,
the differential calculus
structures on the corresponding
Poisson cohomolgies and homologies
are preserved by Koszul duality.
The goal of this section is to show
a similar result for
k^{m||n}
and

k^{n\wedge m}
.

Definition 6.1

(Tamarkin-Tsygan)
.

Let
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}
and
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}

be bi-graded vector spaces.
A
differential calculus
is the sextuple

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\iota,[-,-],d)

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,[-,-])
is a
bi-graded Gerstenhaber
algebra; that is,

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup)
is a bi-graded commutative algebra,

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},[-,-])
is a degree
(0,1)
(or
(0,-1)
)
Lie algebra,
and
the product and Lie bracket are compatible in the following sense

[P\cup Q,R]=P\cup[Q,R]+(-1)^{|P||Q|+w(Q)}Q\cup[P,R],

for homogeneous elements
P,Q,R
;

(2)

\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}
is a bi-graded (left)
module over

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup)
via the map

\iota:\mathrm{H}^{m,n}\otimes\mathrm{H}_{s,t}\to\mathrm{H}_{s-m,t-n},\;f\otimes p\mapsto\iota_{f}p,

for any
f\in\mathrm{H}^{m,n}
and
p\in\mathrm{H}_{s,t}
;

(3)

There is a map
d:\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}\to\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet+1}

satisfying
d^{2}=0
, and moreover, if we set

{L}_{f}:=[\iota_{f},d]=\iota_{f}d-(-1)^{w(f)}d\iota_{f}
,
then

\displaystyle\iota_{[f,g]}=[L_{f},\iota_{g}]:={L}_{f}\iota_{g}-(-1)^{p(g)p(f)+w(g)(w(f)+1)}\iota_{g}{L}_{f}.

(27)

In the following, if
\cup
,
\iota
,
[-,-]
and
d

are clear from the context, we will simply write
a differential calculus by
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet})
for short.

6.1
Differential
calculus on
k^{m||n}
and
k^{n\wedge m}

In this subsection, we study the differential calculus structure on
A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)
and
A^{!}=(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^{!})
.

Firstly, we define the wedge product and the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
on Poisson cohomology
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
.
By the bi-graded Leibniz rule and the bi-graded Jacobi identity for the
Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket (due to (
12
)),

\displaystyle\delta^{\pi}(P\wedge Q)

\displaystyle=\delta^{\pi}(P)\wedge Q+(-1)^{w(P)}P\wedge\delta^{\pi}Q,

\displaystyle\delta^{\pi}([P,Q])

\displaystyle=-[P,\delta^{\pi}(Q)]-(-1)^{(|P|-|s|)(|Q|-|s|)}[\delta^{\pi}(P),Q],

for any homogeneous elements
P,Q\in\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
.
It follows that these formulas that if
P
and
Q
are Poisson
cocycles, then
P\wedge Q
and
[P,Q]
are Poisson cocycles.
This means that the wedge product and the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
can be restricted to Poisson cocycles.

Next, it follows from the same formula that either product of a Poisson
cocycle with a Poisson coboundary is a Poisson coboundary.
This means that, for Poisson cocycles
P
and
Q
, the cohomology class of

P\wedge Q
and
[P,Q]
are independent of the representatives
P
and
Q

of their cohomology classes.
Thus, we have two well-defined operators in Poisson cohomology.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose that
A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)
is a Poisson algebra.
Then

\big(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\wedge,\tilde{\iota},[-,-],\tilde{d}\big)

is a differential calculus.

Proof.

(1) We first show
that
(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\wedge,[-,-])
is a
Gerstenhaber algebra.
By the above analysis, we have the well-defined wedge product on

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
, which is bi-graded commutative.
And we have the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket on

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
, which is also a Lie bracket of degree

(0,1)
, so
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
is a Gerstenhaber algebra.

(2) The map
\tilde{\iota}
:
\mathrm{HP}^{s,t}(A)\otimes\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{u,v}(A)\to\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{u-s,v-t}(A)
is also induced from
(
16
).
Now we check that
\tilde{\iota}
is well-defined on

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
and

\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
.
For any
P\in\mathrm{CP}^{s,t}(A)
, by (
18
),
we have
[\partial_{\pi},\tilde{\iota}_{P}]=\tilde{\iota}_{[P,\pi]},

it means
that when
P
is a Poisson cocycle,

\tilde{\iota}_{P}
commutes with
\partial_{\pi}
.
Since the wedge product is well-defined,
\tilde{\iota}
is also
independent of the representative of cohomology class.

\tilde{\iota}
and
\partial_{\pi}
induce indeed actions of the Poisson
cohomology
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
on the Poisson homology

\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
.

(3) Finally, for the differential,
we know that
\tilde{d}
satisfies
\tilde{d}^{2}=0
in
§
3.5
.
In the proof of Proposition
5.3
, we have already checked
that
\tilde{d}
commutes with
\partial_{\pi}
.
Since
\tilde{d}
is a derivation,
\tilde{d}
acts on a product of a
mixed Poisson cycle with a Poisson boundary is still a Poisson boundary.
It means that
\tilde{d}
is a well-defined operator in mixed Poisson homology.
Moreover, in
\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
,
if we set
L_{P}:=[\tilde{\iota}_{P},\tilde{d}]=\tilde{\iota}_{P}\circ\tilde{d}-(-1)^{|P||\tilde{d}|}\tilde{d}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{P}
,
then by (
18
), we have:

\tilde{\iota}_{[P,Q]}=[L_{P},\tilde{\iota}_{Q}]:={L}_{P}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{Q}-(-1)^{|L_{P}||Q|}\tilde{\iota}_{Q}\circ{L}_{P}.\qed

By completely the same argument, we have the following.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
A^{!}=(\mathcal{O}(k^{n\wedge m}),\pi^{!})
is a Poisson algebra.
Then

\big(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\wedge,\tilde{\iota},[-,-],\tilde{d}\big)

is a differential calculus.

6.2
Koszul duality

We are now ready to show
Theorem
1.2
, which is rephrased
as follows for reader’s convenience.

Theorem 6.4

(Theorem
1.2
)
.

Let
(k^{m||n},\pi)

and
(k^{n\wedge m},\pi^{!})
be two
bi-graded Poisson
spaces.
Then:

(1)

(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A))
is a differential calculus.

(2)

Similarly,

(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}))
is a differential calculus.

(3)

If furthermore
\pi

and
\pi^{!}
are Koszul dual to each other,
then under the correspondence
given in Table
2
,
the above two differential calculus
structures are isomorphic.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are proved in
Theorems

6.2

and

6.3

respectively.
It is left to show (3).

In fact, due to Theorem
5.2
, we have

\Psi:\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\cong\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})

For any
P,Q\in\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)

2

\Psi(P),\Psi(Q)\in\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})

\Psi(P\wedge Q)=\Psi(P)\wedge\Psi(Q)

\Psi([P,Q])=[\Psi(P),\Psi(Q)]

Since we already checked that the wedge product and the Schouten-Nijenhuis

\Psi
commutes
with the wedge product and the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket.

, there is an isomorphism between

\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A)

\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})
.

Since the contraction
\tilde{\iota}
and the

\tilde{d}

\big(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\big)\ \mathrm{and}\ \big(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})\big)

7
Unimodularity and the Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebra

Recall that a Poisson
structure is called
unimodular

if there exists a volume form which
is Poisson-closed.
As is shown by Xu (
[
20
]
),
the existence of a unimodular

Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebra structure on its Poisson

Later, it was observed by Lambre

[
8
]

that all these known Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebras arising from mathematics
share some common property; namely,
they come from differential calculus
with some extra datum, which
he called
differential calculus with
duality
.

In this section, we study the
unimodular Poisson structures
on
k^{m||n}
and

k^{n\wedge m}
. Again
this is inspired by the work

[
3
]
, where
the case of
k^{m|0}

and
k^{0|m}
are considered.
We then finish the proof of
Theorem
1.3
.

Differential

We now introduced Lambre’s

An important corollary is that

7.2.1
Differential
calculus with duality

To start with, we introduce the notion of differential
calculus with duality. Then we prove that

(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A))
with a unimodular Poisson
structure
forms a differential calculus with duality.
The same holds for
(\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}),\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!}))
.

Definition 7.3

(Lambre)
.

A differential calculus
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\iota,[-,-],d)
is
called a
differential calculus with duality
if
there exists integers
u,v
and an element
\gamma\in\mathrm{H}_{u,v}

such that

(a)

\iota_{1}\gamma=\gamma
,
where
1\in\mathrm{H}^{0,0}
is the unit,
d(\gamma)=0
, and

(b)

for any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathrm{PD}(-):=\iota_{(-)}\gamma:\mathrm{H}^{i,j}\to\mathrm{H}_{u-i,v-j}

is an isomorphism.

Such an isomorphism
\mathrm{PD}
is called the

Van den Bergh duality
(also called
the
noncommutative Poincaré duality
).

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
A=(\mathcal{O}(k^{m||n}),\pi)
be a bi-graded unimodular
Poisson algebra with the mixed volume form (
28
),
the Poisson structure is the linear combination of (
24
).
Then there exists an isomorphism

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\cong\widetilde{\mathrm{HP}}_{-n-\bullet,m-\bullet}(A).

Proof.

Given a
k
-linear map

\tilde{\iota}_{(-)}\gamma:\mathrm{CP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)\to\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}_{-n-\bullet,m-\bullet}(A),

where
\gamma=dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{m}\wedge y_{1}^{*}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n}^{*}
, we prove the map
\tilde{\iota}_{(-)}\gamma
is well-defined.
For any
P\in\mathrm{HP}^{i,j}(A)
, the degree of
\tilde{\iota}_{P}\gamma

is
(-n-i,m-j)
, so
\tilde{\iota}_{P}\gamma\in\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}_{-n-i,m-j}(A)
.

For any
P,Q\in\mathrm{CP}^{i,j}(A)
, if

\tilde{\iota}_{P}\gamma=\tilde{\iota}_{Q}\gamma
, then

\tilde{\iota}_{(P-Q)}\gamma=0
which implies that
P-Q=0
,

\tilde{\iota}_{(-)}\gamma
is injective.
Next we show that
\tilde{\iota}_{(-)}\gamma
is surjective.
For any
\alpha\in\widetilde{\mathrm{CP}}_{-n-i,m-j}(A)
,
without loss of generality, we consider
\alpha
is a monomial,

\alpha=fdx_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i_{s}}\wedge y^{*}_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge y^{*}_{j_{t}},

7.2.2
The Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra

Next, we
show that for unimodular quadratic Poisson polynomial algebras,
the horizontal isomorphisms in (
33
)
naturally induce a Batalin–Vilkovisky algebra structure on

\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A)
and
\mathrm{HP}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A^{!})
,
and the vertical isomorphisms in (
33
)
are isomorphisms of Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras.

Definition 7.8

(Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra)
.

Suppose that
A

is a bi-graded commutative algebra. A
Batalin-Vilkovisky
algebra
structure on
A
is
(A,\Delta)

such that

(1)

\Delta:A^{i,j}\to A^{i,j+1}
is
a differential of degree
(0,1)
,
that is,
\Delta^{2}=0
;

(2)

\Delta
is second order operator,
that is,

\displaystyle\Delta(abc)=

\displaystyle\Delta(ab)c+(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}a\Delta(bc)+(-1)^{|b|(|a|+|\Delta|)}b\Delta(ac)

\displaystyle-(\Delta a)bc-(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}a(\Delta b)c-(-1)^{|\Delta|(|a|+|b|)}ab(\Delta c).

The following proposition generalizes the result
of Getzler in
[
6
, Proposition 1.2]
.

Proposition 7.9
.

A bi-graded Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra
A
is a
Gerstenhaber algebra equipped with an operator

\Delta:A_{\bullet,\bullet}\to A_{\bullet,\bullet+1}

such that
\Delta^{2}=0

and that

\displaystyle[a,b]=(-1)^{|\Delta||a|}\Delta(ab)-(-1)^{|\Delta||a|}\Delta(a)b-a\Delta(b),

(34)

for any homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
.
Moreover, in a bi-graded Batalin-Vilovisky algebra,

\Delta
satisfies the identity

\Delta[a,b]=[\Delta a,b]+(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}[a,\Delta b].

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that
[-,-]
given by (
34
) is skew symmetric:

\displaystyle[a,b]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta(ab)-(-1)^{|\Delta||a|}\Delta(a)b-a\Delta(b)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta(ba)-(-1)^{|\Delta||a|+|b|(|a|+|\Delta|)}b\Delta(a)-(-1)^{|a|(|\Delta|+|b|)}\Delta(b)a

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)}[b,a]

for any homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
.
Secondly, we show
[-,-]
satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle[\Delta a,b]+(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}[a,\Delta b]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta((\Delta a)b)-(\Delta a)(\Delta b)-\Delta(a(\Delta b))+(\Delta a)(\Delta b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta((\Delta a)b)-\Delta(a(\Delta b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta[a,b],

since
\Delta
is a differential.

Next we verify
[-,-]
is a derivation of degree
(0,1)

with respect to the product. In fact,

\displaystyle[a,bc]-[a,b]c-(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|b|}b[a,c]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}\Delta(abc)-(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}(\Delta a)bc-a\Delta(bc)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}\Delta(ab)c+(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}(\Delta a)bc+a\Delta(b)c

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)|b|}((-1)^{|a||\Delta|}b\Delta(ac)-(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}b(\Delta a)c-ba\Delta(c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}\Delta(abc)-(-1)^{|a||\Delta|}\Delta(abc)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

for any homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
.

Finally, we check the Jacobi identity for
[-,-]
.
In fact,

\displaystyle[[a,b],c]+(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)}[b,[a,c]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|a|+|b|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta([a,b]c)-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta([a,b])c-[a,b]\Delta(c)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)}((-1)^{|b||\Delta|}\Delta(b[a,c])-(-1)^{|b||\Delta|}\Delta(b)[a,c]-b\Delta([a,c]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|a|+|b|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}\Delta([a,bc])-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|+|\Delta|)|\Delta|}[\Delta a,b]c-(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)}b[\Delta a,c])

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|b||\Delta|}[a,\Delta b]c-(-1)^{|b||\Delta|+(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)}\Delta(b)[a,c]

\displaystyle-[a,b]\Delta(c)-(-1)^{(|a|+|\Delta|)(|b|+|\Delta|)+|\Delta|(|\Delta|+|a|)}b[a,\Delta c])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,[b,c]].

Thus we proved the claim.
∎

In other words, a Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra is a
Gerstenhaber algebra
in the sense of Definition
6.1

with a differential
\Delta:V^{i,j}\to V^{i,j+1}

such that

[a,b]=\Delta(ab)-\Delta(a)b-(-1)^{|\Delta||a|}a\Delta(b),

for any
a,b\in V
.

\Delta
is also called the Batalin-Vilkovisky operator,
or the generator (of the
Gerstenhaber bracket).
If there exists a degree
(0,1)
operator
\Delta
with

\Delta^{2}=0
on a Gerstenhaber algebra
(A,[-,-])
such that
(
34
) holds, then
A
is a BV algebra.

Now suppose that
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\iota,[-,-],d,\gamma)

is a differential calculus with duality,
define the linear operator

\Delta:\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}\to\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet+1}
by

\Delta(a)=(-1)^{w(a)+1}\mathrm{PD}^{-1}\circ d\circ\mathrm{PD}(a)

Lemma
7.10
and Theorem

present below are

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\iota,[-,-],d,\gamma)

be a differential calculus with duality.
Let
\mathrm{PD}^{-1}=(\iota_{(-)}\gamma)^{-1}
be the inverse of the duality
isomorphism.
And let
P,Q\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}
and
\omega\in\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet}
be homogeneous elements.
Then, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle[P,Q]\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{w(P)+1}P\cup\Delta(Q\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))+\Delta(P\cup Q\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+(w(Q)+1)(w(P)+1)}Q\cup P\cup\Delta(\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+w(Q)w(P)}Q\cup\Delta(P\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

(35)

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that

\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{P}\omega)=P\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega).

Since
\mathrm{PD}^{-1}
is an isomorphism, there exists
R\in\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}
, such that
\iota_{R}\gamma=\omega
, i.e.,

\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega)=R
.
Then

\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{P}\omega)=\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{P\cup R}\gamma)=P\cup R=P\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega).

By the above equation, we have
\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{[P,Q]}\omega)=[P,Q]\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega).

Furthermore, by the relation (
27
),

\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{[P,Q]}\omega)
is written as
\mathrm{PD}^{-1}([[\iota_{P},d],\iota_{Q}]\omega)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\iota_{[P,Q]}\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{PD}^{-1}\circ\iota_{P}\circ d\circ\iota_{Q}(\omega)-(-1)^{w(P)}\mathrm{PD}^{-1}\circ d\circ\iota_{P}\circ\iota_{Q}(\omega)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+w(Q)(w(P)+1)}\mathrm{PD}^{-1}\circ\iota_{Q}\circ\iota_{P}\circ d(\omega)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+w(Q)w(P)+w(P)+w(Q)}\mathrm{PD}^{-1}\circ\iota_{Q}\circ d\circ\iota_{P}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{w(P)+1}P\cup\Delta(Q\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))+\Delta(P\cup Q\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+w(Q)w(P)+w(Q)+w(P)}Q\cup P\cup\Delta(\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

\displaystyle-(-1)^{p(P)p(Q)+w(Q)w(P)}Q\cup\Delta(P\cup\mathrm{PD}^{-1}(\omega))

This calculation completes the claim.
∎

Theorem 7.11

(Lambre)
.

Let
(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\mathrm{H}_{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\iota,[-,-],d,\gamma)

(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet},\cup,\Delta)

Proof.

We apply the formula (

\omega=\gamma
. Thanks to

\Delta(1)=0

which shows that
\Delta
is a generator of Gerstenhaber bracket of

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet,\bullet}

Proposition
7.9
.

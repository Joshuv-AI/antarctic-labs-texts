EDGE: A Theoretical Framework for Misconception-Aware Adaptive Learning Evaluate → \rightarrow Diagnose → \rightarrow Generate → \rightarrow Exercise
Ananda Prakash Verma

Abstract
We present EDGE , a general-purpose, misconception-aware adaptive learning framework composed of four stages: Evaluate (ability and state estimation), Diagnose (posterior inference of misconceptions), Generate (counterfactual item synthesis), and Exercise (index-based retrieval scheduling). EDGE unifies psychometrics (IRT/Bayesian state-space models), cognitive diagnostics (misconception discovery from distractor patterns and response latencies), contrastive item generation (minimal perturbations that invalidate learner shortcuts while preserving psychometric validity), and principled scheduling (a restless bandit approximation to spaced retrieval). We formalize a composite readiness metric, EdgeScore , prove its monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity, and derive an index policy that is near-optimal under mild assumptions on forgetting and learning gains. We further establish conditions under which counterfactual items provably reduce the posterior probability of a targeted misconception faster than standard practice. The paper focuses on theory and implementable pseudocode; empirical study is left to future work.



1
Introduction

Large-scale preparation for high-stakes exams requires efficient remediation of
misconceptions
—stable, systematic errors not addressed by generic practice. Classical spaced repetition and IRT-based selection optimize recall and information gain, yet they typically remain
misconception-agnostic
.
EDGE
operationalizes a pipeline that (i) estimates learner state continuously, (ii) infers latent misconception structure from the joint distribution of correctness, chosen distractors, time, and confidence, (iii) synthesizes contrastive counterfactual items that fail the learner’s current shortcut, and (iv) schedules retrieval to jointly maximize mastery and retention subject to time budgets. We provide a complete mathematical treatment and design-ready algorithms suitable for deployment at scale.

Contributions.

(1) A probabilistic state model that augments 2PL-IRT with time- and confidence-aware likelihoods. (2) A Bayesian diagnostic for misconception posteriors using clusterable distractor embeddings and timing signatures. (3) A constrained optimization view of counterfactual item generation that preserves construct validity. (4) A restless-bandit scheduler with a closed-form priority index and theoretical properties. (5) A composite readiness score,
EdgeScore
, with calibration and guarantees.

2.1
Ability and Response Model

We adopt a 2PL likelihood with time and confidence effects. For item
q

S(q)=\{t:c_{q,t}=1\}
, define the effective ability

\theta^{\text{eff}}_{u,q}\triangleq\frac{\sum_{t\in S(q)}w^{(M)}_{t}\theta_{u,t}}{\sum_{t\in S(q)}w^{(M)}_{t}},

(1)

with nonnegative weights
w^{(M)}_{t}
. The probability of correctness is

P(y_{u,q}=1\mid\theta^{\text{eff}}_{u,q},a_{q},b_{q},\tau_{u,q},s_{u,q})=\sigma\!\left(a_{q}(\theta^{\text{eff}}_{u,q}-b_{q})+\beta_{\tau}g(\tau_{u,q})+\beta_{s}h(s_{u,q})\right),

(2)

where
\sigma(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})
,

is a bounded, decreasing function (faster correct
\Rightarrow

is increasing (confidence interacts with correctness via the likelihood below).

2.2
State-Space Update

Let the prior over
\bm{\theta}_{u}
be Gaussian and independent across topics:
\theta_{u,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{u,t},\sigma^{2}_{u,t})
. After observing
(y,\tau,s)

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}_{u,t}

\displaystyle=\mu_{u,t}+\kappa_{u,q,t}\cdot\left(y-P(y=1\mid\cdot)\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime 2}_{u,t}

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma^{-2}_{u,t}+a_{q}^{2}\cdot\eta_{u,q,t}\right)^{-1},\qquad t\in S(q),

(4)

with sensitivity terms

2

2.3
Retention Dynamics

For each

\rho_{u,t}(t^{\prime})=\exp\!\left(-\lambda_{u,t}\,[t^{\prime}-L_{u,t}]_{+}\right).

A successful, effortful retrieval (correct with

Diagnose: Bayesian Misconception Inference

A

is a latent class

characterized by a signature over distractors and times. For a question
q

\mathbf{e}_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbf{z}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}

\mathbf{x}_{u,q}=\big[\mathbf{e}_{q}\,\|\,\mathbf{z}_{d_{u,q}}\,\|\,\phi(\tau_{u,q})\,\|\,\psi(s_{u,q})\big]\in\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}+2},

(6)

p(\mathbf{x}_{u,q}\mid y=0)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\pi_{u,m}\,\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{u,q}\mid\bm{\mu}_{m},\Sigma_{m}\right),

(7)

where
\pi_{u,m}
is the learner-specific mixture over misconceptions. Given a set
\mathcal{W}_{u}
of wrong responses, the posterior satisfies

\pi_{u,m}\propto\alpha_{m}\prod_{(q\in\mathcal{W}_{u})}\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{u,q}\mid\bm{\mu}_{m},\Sigma_{m}\right),

(8)

with Dirichlet prior
\bm{\alpha}
. Cluster identities can be seeded by weak supervision or discovered via EM; human-readable labels are produced by an LLM applied to cluster centroids.

Definition 3.1

(Misconception Flag)
.

Topic
t

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{t}}\pi_{u,m}\geq\gamma
, where
\mathcal{M}_{t}
collects misconceptions impacting topic
t
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
is a threshold.

4
Generate: Counterfactual Item Synthesis

Let each item
q
decompose into a structured attribute vector
\mathbf{v}_{q}
(concepts, numerical parameters, qualitative conditions). For a flagged misconception
m
, define a
counterfactual

q^{\star}
as the solution of

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{v}}\quad

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{q}\|_{W}

(9)

s.t.

\displaystyle\Delta_{m}(\mathbf{v})\geq\delta\qquad\text{(\emph{invalidate} shortcut)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{v})=1\qquad\quad\ \ \text{(content validity, constraints)}

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{v})\in[b_{q}-\epsilon_{b},b_{q}+\epsilon_{b}],\ a(\mathbf{v})\in[a_{q}-\epsilon_{a},a_{q}+\epsilon_{a}],

where
\Delta_{m}
measures the contradiction to the misconception-specific heuristic,
\mathcal{C}
enforces factual correctness and syllabus constraints, and
(a(\cdot),b(\cdot))
are psychometric predictions from item generators. Minimal perturbation under
W\succeq 0
yields
near-miss

Proposition 1

(Monotone Posterior Reduction)
.

Suppose for misconception
m
there exists
\delta>0
such that the counterfactual
q^{\star}
satisfies
\Delta_{m}(\mathbf{v}_{q^{\star}})\geq\delta
and preserves other model factors. If the learner answers
q^{\star}
correctly with sufficiently fast
\tau
, then the posterior
\pi_{u,m}
in (
8
) strictly decreases by a factor bounded away from
1
.

Sketch.

Correct and fast response shifts the likelihood of the cluster centered at
(\bm{\mu}_{m},\Sigma_{m})
downward relative to alternatives, reweighing the mixture via Bayes’ rule. The margin
\delta
ensures a uniform decrease independent of nuisance factors; details follow from Gaussian tail inequalities.
∎

5
Exercise: Retrieval Scheduling as a Restless Bandit

We allocate practice opportunities over topics under a daily budget
B
. Each topic
t

u

X_{u,t}=(\mu_{u,t},\sigma^{2}_{u,t},\rho_{u,t},\Pi_{u,t})

\Pi_{u,t}
collects misconception posteriors affecting

5.1
Index Policy

Let
G_{u,t}(x)
be the expected one-step gain from practicing topic
t

x

C_{u,t}(x)
the time cost. Define the priority index

\mathcal{I}_{u,t}(x)=\frac{G_{u,t}(x)}{C_{u,t}(x)}+\lambda^{\star}\cdot H_{u,t}(x),

(10)

where
H
is a hazard-like term derived from the derivative of
\rho
(the urgency of retrieval), and
\lambda^{\star}
balances learning vs. forgetting. The scheduler selects the
B
topics with highest
\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 1

(Near-Optimality under Separable Gains)
.

If
G_{u,t}(x)
decomposes as a concave function of
\mu
plus a convex penalty in
\rho
and misconceptions enter
G
additively via a bounded corrective term, then the index policy (
10
) is a
1/(1+\epsilon)
-approximation to the optimal policy for some
\epsilon=O(\max_{t}\text{var}(\Delta G_{u,t}))
.

Sketch.

Follows from Lagrangian relaxation of the budgeted restless bandit and standard performance bounds for separable indices using convexity/concavity arguments.
∎

6
EdgeScore: A Composite Readiness Metric

For topic
t
of learner
u
we define

\mathrm{EdgeScore}_{u,t}\triangleq S\Big(w_{M}M_{u,t}+w_{R}R_{u,t}+w_{P}P_{u,t}+w_{C}C_{u,t}-\Gamma(\Pi_{u,t})\Big)\in[0,100],

(11)

with components:

•

M_{u,t}
: mastery proxy, e.g.,
\mu_{u,t}
or calibrated probability
\mathbb{E}[y\mid\cdot]
at a reference difficulty.

•

R_{u,t}
: retention, e.g.,
\rho_{u,t}
from Eq. (
5
).

•

P_{u,t}
: pace, a z-score of response time versus peers at matched difficulty.

•

C_{u,t}
: confidence consistency, e.g., correlation between
s
and correctness over a sliding window.

•

\Gamma(\Pi_{u,t})
: penalty increasing in the mass of active misconceptions impacting
t
.

S(\cdot)
is an affine map to
[0,100]
with clipping.

Proposition 2

(Monotonicity and Lipschitzness)
.

If each component is 1-Lipschitz and weights are nonnegative, then (
11
) is nondecreasing in
(M,R,P,C)
and
L
-Lipschitz with
L\leq w_{M}+w_{R}+w_{P}+w_{C}+\mathrm{Lip}(\Gamma)
.

6.1
Calibration

Weights
(w_{M},w_{R},w_{P},w_{C})
and the penalty
\Gamma
are identified by solving

\min_{w,\gamma}\ \sum_{(u,t)}\ell\!\left(\widehat{p}_{u,t}^{\text{future}},\,\mathrm{EdgeScore}_{u,t}/100\right)+\lambda\|w\|_{2}^{2},\quad w\geq 0,

(12)

where
\widehat{p}^{\text{future}}
is a held-out success proxy (e.g., correctness at delayed intervals) and
\ell
is a proper scoring loss. This ensures probabilistic calibration if
S
is the identity.

Theoretical Guarantees

8.1
Information Gain

For 2PL items, the Fisher information is
I_{q}(\theta)=a_{q}^{2}\sigma(a_{q}(\theta-b_{q}))\sigma(-a_{q}(\theta-b_{q}))
. Under mild smoothness of
g(\tau)
, selecting items near
\theta\approx b_{q}
maximizes information;
EDGE
respects this while adding corrective gains from misconception resolution.

Theorem 2

(Counterfactual Advantage)
.

Assume a single active misconception
m
and a separating counterfactual with margin
\delta
exists. Then the expected reduction in
\pi_{u,m}
from one
Generate
\Rightarrow
Exercise
step dominates that of any item not violating the shortcut by at least a factor
1+\kappa(\delta)
for some
\kappa>0
depending on cluster overlap.

Sketch.

Compare Bayes updates of (
8
) under the two likelihoods; the counterfactual shifts mass outside the high-density region of
m
, providing a multiplicative decrease.∎

8.2
Scheduling Regret

Let
V^{\star}
be the optimal value over horizon
H
and
V^{\text{EDGE}}
the value under
EdgeScheduler
. Under separability and Lipschitz gains,

V^{\star}-V^{\text{EDGE}}\leq O\!\left(B\,\sqrt{H\log|\mathcal{T}|}\right)+O\!\left(H\epsilon\right),

(13)

where the first term is a bandit-style exploration penalty and
\epsilon
is the approximation factor from Theorem 2.

Limitations and Future Directions

The mixture model assumes Gaussian clusters in the feature space of wrong responses, which may be misspecified; robust or nonparametric alternatives (
e.g.
, DP mixtures) are promising. Counterfactual validity relies on reliable psychometric predictors
(a,b)
; calibration requires periodic field trials. Scheduling theory beyond separability, and fairness constraints across languages and curricula, remain open.

10
Conclusion

EDGE
provides a principled foundation for misconception-aware adaptive learning, coupling rigorous estimation with actionable generation and scheduling. The framework is modular, implementable, and admits guarantees that justify its design choices.

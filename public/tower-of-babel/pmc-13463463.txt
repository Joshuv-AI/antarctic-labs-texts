Transthoracic drainage of lung abscesses (TOLA): protocol for a national multicenter randomized open-label trial
Authors: Katrine Kristensen, Uffe Bodtger, Søren Helbo Skaarup, Christian B. Laursen, Ulla Møller Weinreich, Iben Rahbek Andersen, Marianne Kragh Thomsen, Zaigham Saghir, Jens-Ulrik Stæhr Jensen, Elisabeth Bendstrup, Søren Sperling, Andreas Fløe

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionLung abscess (LA) is a rare but severe necrotizing pulmonary infection associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Current management relies on prolonged systemic antibiotic therapy without systematic use of abscess drainage. However, small case series and retrospective studies suggest that transthoracic drainage may be a safe and effective adjunctive intervention improving treatment response. Historically, drainage has been avoided for fear of fistulation, but evidence supporting this is weak. High-quality evidence from randomized trials regarding interventional treatment of LA is lacking. This trial aims to determine whether transthoracic drainage combined with standard antibiotic therapy reduces hospital stay, compared with standard antibiotics alone in patients with LA.MethodsThis is a national, multicentre, randomized open-label trial. Adults diagnosed with an LA ≥ 4 cm in diameter, containing fluid, and in contact with the outer third of the lung, will be eligible for the study. A total of 84 patients will be randomized 1:1 to either (1) transthoracic drainage combined with standard antibiotic treatment or (2) standard antibiotic treatment alone. Patients will undergo clinical assessments, laboratory testing, microbiological analyses, CT imaging, and standardized patient-reported outcome measures. Follow-up is scheduled at 1, 4, and 12 weeks post‑discharge. The primary outcome is length of hospital stay. Recruitment started February 2026 with an anticipated inclusion period of 4 years.PerspectiveThis study will provide the first randomized evidence on the role of transthoracic drainage in LA management. The results may inform national and international clinical guidelines and improve outcomes in patients with this severe condition.Trial registrationClinicalTrial.gov (NCT07247461)

Introduction

Lung abscess (LA) is a severe necrotizing pulmonary infection characterized by the formation of pus-filled cavities within the lung parenchyma [1,2]. LA most often develops as a complication of pneumonia, particularly in vulnerable patients with underlying chronic pulmonary diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Other risk factors include alcohol misuse, poor dental hygiene, the use of sedatives, immune deficiencies, and patients with an increased risk of aspiration [3,4]. Despite advances in antimicrobial therapy, LA continues to be associated with considerable morbidity, mortality, and long-term hospitalization [3].

First-line management of LA is antibiotic treatment. Several studies have failed to determine the optimal antibiotic strategy, and no national or international guidelines are available to guide the clinicians in the treatment of LA [5–7]. Treatment often depends on local practice rather than solid evidence, leading to the use of prolonged broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, which extends hospital stays and increases the risk of antimicrobial resistance [8]. In this context, strategies that could accelerate clinical resolution and reduce antibiotic exposure are of high clinical relevance.

Transthoracic drainage (TTD) is a safe and effective adjunctive treatment strategy for LA to improve treatment response and shorten hospital stay [9–13]. Historically, TTD has been limited to refractory cases due to concerns regarding complications such as empyema, pneumothorax, bronchopleural fistula, and haemothorax; however, recent reviews find that TTD has lower complication rates than previously reported [14–16]. Nevertheless, available evidence is limited to small retrospective cohorts, case series, and small prospective studies [9–13]. No large randomized controlled trials have evaluated the timing or efficacy of TTD in the initial management of LA. These findings highlight the lack of consensus and the need for prospective data to guide clinical decision-making [17,18]

Aim of the study

This study aims to investigate whether up-front transthoracic drainage of lung abscess, in combination with standard antibiotic treatment, can reduce hospital stay, compared with standard conservative approach with antibiotic treatment alone. We hypothesize that transthoracic drainage is a safe procedure that reduces the length of hospital stay compared with the conservative approach.

Methods

Trial design, setting, and time frame

The study is a multicentre, national, randomized controlled, open-label trial involving University Hospitals across all regions of Denmark. Patient enrolment began in early 2026 and is expected to continue for a 4-year period, concluding in late 2029.

Eligibility criteria

Patients are eligible for inclusion in the study if they are above 18 years of age and admitted in a hospital with a lung abscess ≥ 4 cm in size containing fluid and in contact with the outer 1/3 of the lung. In our study, a lung abscess is defined as a cavitary intrapulmonary lesion with fluid content, with or without an air-fluid level, identified on chest computed tomography (CT) and considered consistent with a pulmonary abscess by a radiologist or senior pulmonologist.

Patients may be enrolled regardless of prior admission and antibiotic treatment, provided that all eligibility criteria are met at the time of lung abscess diagnosis and study inclusion.

Patients can be screened for eligibility at any hospital in Denmark. If the patient is eligible for inclusion and accepts participation in the study, the patient will be transferred to the respiratory department at the regional university hospital where randomization and inclusion will be performed.

Intervention – drainage

TTD will be initiated as soon as possible in the intervention group and within 72 h of inclusion and randomization. Chest tube insertion will be performed by experienced interventional radiologists or pulmonologists with expertise in image-guided percutaneous thoracic procedures. To ensure procedural consistency, the intervention will be undertaken by a limited number of operators at each participating center. Insertion of a drainage tube will be guided by ultrasound or computed tomography, as decided by the operator case-by-case. Discontinuation of anticoagulant treatment and application of local anesthetics will be performed according to local guidelines. The choice of tube size and insertion technique (Seldinger/one-step) will be decided by the operator. In cases where multiple LAs are present, a tube will be placed in the largest LA accessible for drainage. If the tube is accidentally removed, indication for reinsertion will be decided by the treating physician.

The drain will be assessed daily and flushed three times daily with saline to maintain patency and to irrigate the abscess cavity. The volume of saline will be determined by treating physician based on abscess volume, and irrigation will be continued until the return fluid is clear. Daily monitoring and documentation of chest tube input and output will be conducted, supplemented by a brief assessment of symptoms related to drainage (Appendix).

Duration of drainage will be decided by the treating physician. Protocol violation with rescue drainage is expected in patients allocated to the control group in case of clinical deterioration, persistent uncontrolled infection, or other safety concerns raised by the treating physician. The antibiotic strategy in the intervention group is identical to the standard antibiotic treatment in the comparator group.

Comparator – standard treatment

Patients will be treated with 4 g/0.5 g piperacillin/tazobactam intravenously (IV) four times daily, or alternatively as a continuous infusion of 12 g/1.5 g or 16 g/2 g daily administered via an infusion pump, for a minimum of 7 days. Antibiotic dose will be adjusted according to kidney function and weight.

The total duration of antibiotic treatment is decided by the treating physician, depending on clinical improvement, paraclinical response, and radiological assessment. In cases of disease progression, allergy to specific antibiotics or microbiological findings, the treating physician can adjust the choice of antibiotics and the need for drainage or surgery in both groups.

Standard supportive care is provided to all patients according to national guidelines such as pain‑relief medication, thrombosis prophylaxis, nutritional support, and respiratory physiotherapy.

Microbiology

Abscess bacterial flora will be assessed from the abscess aspirate following drainage in the intervention group. Respiratory tract samples will be collected from all enrolled patients according to the study protocol, by either expectorated sputum or aspirate collected by tracheal suction. Oral swab samples will be obtained from all patients to determine oral bacterial flora and bacterial load. Samples will be collected as soon as possible and preferable before initiation of antibiotics.

Microbiological samples will be analyzed using conventional culture as well as microbiome characterization by 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing. Microbiological cultures will be analyzed promptly, and the results will be made available to the treating physician. Antibiotic therapy may be adjusted in the event of detected resistance to the therapeutic agent.

Safety

Safety will be evaluated with an ongoing focus on device adverse reactions and complications to TTD, including pleural empyema, pneumothorax, bronchopleural fistula, hemothorax, and procedure-related mortality during admission and clinical follow-up. Treating physicians are responsible for patient safety and may deviate from the protocol when considered necessary to ensure optimal patient care.

All serious adverse events potentially related to the intervention will be recorded by the study steering group. An independent Safety Management Board consisting of pulmonologists and infectious disease specialists from three separate regions in Denmark will review safety data upon request from the steering group or in the event of any serious adverse events related to the intervention. Should unexpected serious safety concerns arise during the trial, including procedure-related mortality or an unacceptable frequency of severe complications, the Safety Management Board will provide recommendations regarding study continuation, protocol modifications, or study termination. The final decision regarding study continuation will be made by the steering group.

Sample size

The power calculation is based on clinical experience, national data, and international studies. A previous study reported a mean length of stay of 18 days (SD 6.5) among patients treated with antibiotics alone [20]. As no randomized controlled trials have evaluated transthoracic drainage for lung abscesses, robust data on the expected effect size are lacking. Based on clinical experience and observational reports suggesting more rapid source control and clinical improvement following drainage, we considered a 25% reduction in length of hospital stay to be clinically meaningful and realistically achievable. This corresponds to an absolute reduction of approximately 4–5 hospital days compared with standard treatment.

As the distribution of length of hospital stay is expected to be skewed, a logarithmic transformation is performed prior to the calculation of sample size.

Assuming a power of 80%, a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and an expected dropout rate of 10%, 42 patients will be enrolled in each arm, yielding a total sample size of 84 participants.

Randomization will be carried out by a designated physician at each site in REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) in blocks of 2/4/6 stratified for each region to ensure equal distribution of patients and interventions.

Patients and treating healthcare staff will not be blinded. Statisticians who perform the statistical analysis will be blinded to treatment allocation.

Data collection and follow-up

During hospitalization, all data related to blood tests, radiological procedures, medical treatment, microbiological samples, and TTD will be systematically recorded during the study. A standardized template will be employed during medical rounds to ensure consistency in data collection both across consecutive days and between participating hospitals (Appendix). Upon inclusion, an audit of each patient’s medical record will provide information on comorbidities, prior lung function test, previous radiological procedures, and relevant medical history.

All TTD procedural data will be systematically collected for each patient, including insertion technique, imaging guidance used, drain size and type, and any immediate complications (e.g. pneumothorax, bleeding, malposition). Post-procedure monitoring will include daily clinical assessment, radiological evaluation, and recording of drain output.

Protocol adherence will be closely monitored. Key parameters include timing of drain removal, adherence to antibiotic regimen, and completion of all scheduled imaging and laboratory assessments. Any deviations from the protocol will be documented, including reasons for deviations and measures taken. This systematic documentation ensures both patient safety and high-quality data regarding the intervention and its outcomes.

Additionally, patients will complete questionnaires including COPD assessment test (CAT) and the EuroQol − 5 dimensions, 5‑level instrument (EQ-5D-5 L) to evaluate health-related quality of life [21,22].

After hospital discharge, all patients will be followed in outpatient clinics at the participating study sites. Follow-up assessments will be conducted 1, 4, and 12 weeks after discharge, focusing primarily on clinical status, biochemical parameters, and radiological resolution. One year after discharge, patients’ medical records will be reviewed to evaluate morbidity and mortality. The patient flowchart and timeline are presented in Table 2 and Figure 2. Study data will be collected and managed using REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted at Aarhus University. REDCap is a secure, web-based software platform designed to support data capture for research studies, providing 1) an intuitive interface for validated data capture; 2) audit trails for tracking data manipulation and export procedures; 3) automated export procedures for seamless data downloads to common statistical packages; and 4) procedures for data integration and interoperability with external sources [23,24]. Any physical data will be stored at the Department of Respiratory Disease and Allergy at Aarhus University Hospital.

Statistical methods

Results will be reported in accordance with CONSORT guidelines. Data is extracted from REDCap and data analysis is performed using the statistical software R (current version 4.5.2).

Data will be reported in each group as median with interquartile ranges (IQR). The primary endpoint, length of hospital stay, is defined as the number of days from admission to discharge with hospital at home included as hospital admission. As length of hospital stay may be influenced by local discharge practices, sensitivity analyses adjusting for study centers will be conducted. Furthermore, fulfilment of predefined discharge criteria will be assessed through medical record review, allowing additional analyses to explore the impact of discharge practice on the primary outcome.

Comparison between groups will be performed using a t-test on the log-transformed length of hospital stay to account for skewed distribution. The results will be presented as median ratios with 95% confidence intervals.

Key secondary outcome is days alive and out of hospital (DAOH) at 30 days (Table 2).

Time-to-discharge models (Cox proportional hazards) will be used to assess the impact of deaths and censored observations (patient hospitalized at end-of-study or lost to follow-up). Analysis will adjust for age, gender, and pulmonary comorbidities.

Secondary outcomes will be reported as median with IQR for continuous variables and n (%) for binary outcomes.

Continuous secondary outcomes will be compared using t-test or Wilcoxon rank‑sum test depending on distribution of data. Binary outcomes will be compared using Chi-square test for unadjusted analyses. If adjustment is required, logistic regression will be applied, and results will be reported as odds ratio with 95% confidence intervals. Repeated measurements (e.g. patient‑reported outcomes) will be analyzed using linear mixed model with treatment groups and time as fixed effects and patients as a random effect.

The intervention will primarily be analyzed according to intention-to-treat principles. In addition, a per-protocol analysis will be conducted.

Ethics, approvals and monitoring

This study is approved by the Danish Research Ethics Committee (1-10-72-146-25) and Central Region Denmark (1-16-02-539-25). Information about the subjects is protected under the EU’s General Data Protections Regulation (GDPR), the Danish Personal Data Processing Act and Danish Health Act. The study will be conducted in accordance with the Helsinki II Declaration and is registered at ClinicalTrial.gov (NCT07247461)

Since TTD is a recognized treatment for selected cases of LA, the study is not categorized as test of medical equipment and monitoring by the Good Clinical Practice Unit (GCP) is not legally required. However, the study will be conducted according to GCP principles.

Patients will provide written informed consent according to Danish legislation prior to enrolment.

Discussion

LA remains a serious, necrotizing pulmonary infection with considerable morbidity and mortality despite advances in antimicrobial therapy [3]. Current management of this disease relies predominantly on prolonged antibiotic treatment, frequently resulting in extended hospital admissions and delayed recovery. To date, no large randomized controlled trials have evaluated the optimal timing or role of TTD compared to standard care with antibiotics in the management of LA, and no international guideline currently provides evidence-based recommendations. This trial has been designed to address this important knowledge gap.

This national, multicentre, randomized, controlled, open-label trial will assess whether early TTD, combined with standard antibiotic therapy, can reduce the length of hospital stay compared with standard therapy alone. In addition, in a prospective randomized design, the study will provide valuable insights into treatment success rates and frequency of complications. Bye focusing on a pragmatic, real-world setting that includes University Hospitals across all regions in Denmark, the study aims to generate clinically applicable data that can easily be implemented in daily practice.

Strengths and limitations

The TOLA trial has several notable strengths. Its national, multicentre design enhances generalizability and ensures representation of different clinical settings and patient populations. The randomized, controlled structure provides a high level of internal validity, minimizing confounding factors and risk of bias.

The trial also includes clinically meaningful endpoints, particularly the length of hospital stay and days alive and out of hospital, which has direct implications for patient quality of life and healthcare resource. Secondary outcomes such as complication rates, radiological resolution, antibiotic use, and mortality will provide a comprehensive evaluation of efficacy and safety and enhance antibiotic stewardship.

Some limitations must be acknowledged. The open-label design introduces potential bias, as treating clinicians and patients are aware of the intervention. This may influence clinical decision-making, particularly regarding discharge timing; however, the use of objective discharge criteria and blinded outcome analysis where possible will mitigate this risk. Another challenge is heterogeneity in abscess aetiology and patient comorbidities, such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, aspiration risk, and immunosuppression. These factors may affect outcomes, although randomization and stratification will help balance these variables between groups.

The trial is powered for the primary endpoint, length of hospital stay, and may not be sufficiently large to detect rare but serious complications such as bronchopleural fistula, pneumothorax, or empyema. Finally, as a single-country study, external validity to other healthcare systems may be limited, though Denmark’s uniform healthcare structure and national coverage represent a strong foundation for generalizable results.

Ethical and clinical perspectives

Early drainage may shorten recovery and hospitalization but also introduces procedural risk. The trial design reflects a balance between potential benefits and harms, ensuring that participants receive best available standard care alongside close safety monitoring. Retrospective studies indicate that these severe complications occur fare less frequently than anticipated earlier [9–13]. Importantly, by exploring whether early intervention can reduce antibiotic treatment duration and overall exposure, the study contributes to the principles of antimicrobial stewardship, an increasingly important goal in infection management [8].

Conclusion

If early TTD proves effective and safe, the TOLA trial could lead to a paradigm shift in the management of lung abscesses, offering clinicians an evidence-based interventional strategy to reduce hospital burden and improve patient outcomes. Beyond its immediate clinical implications, the study may form the foundation for future research into microbiological mechanisms, radiological healing patterns, and long-term respiratory sequelae following abscess resolution.

In summary, the TOLA trial will provide much-needed high-quality evidence to clarify the role of transthoracic drainage in the management of lung abscesses. The results have the potential to inform future guideline development, optimize antibiotic use, and improve care for a patient group that remains both clinically challenging and underserved in current literature.

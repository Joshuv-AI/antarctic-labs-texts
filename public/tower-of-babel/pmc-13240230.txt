Twin Pregnancy Following Interval Laparoscopic Transabdominal Cervicoisthmic Cerclage After Failed Transvaginal Cerclage: A Case Report
Authors: Sophia Malik, Minhal Nadeem, Muhammad Hassaan Javaid, Raghabendra Kumar Mahato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe procedure of interval LTCC may be an option for women who have established history of cervical incompetence and a failed TVC, including those with pregnancy conceived after IVF treatment with twins. This should still be on a case‐to‐case basis considering that evidence is not abundant for this situation.Keywords: cervical insufficiency, in vitro fertilization, laparoscopic transabdominal cervicoisthmic cerclage (LTCC), preterm birth, transvaginal cerclage, twin pregnancy

Introduction

It is described as the inability of the cervical region of the uterus to maintain pregnancy without contracting or going into labor. Cervical insufficiency occurs in about 0.1%–2% of pregnancies, and it has been found to be responsible for recurrent miscarriage in the second trimester of pregnancy, which usually occurs from 14 to 24 weeks [1]. This condition assumes importance especially during multiple pregnancies, wherein mechanical loading on the cervix is much greater, and cervical shortening is more prevalent.

Transvaginal cerclage (TVC), which comprises both McDonald and Shirodkar types, has been regarded as the gold‐standard treatment modality for cervical incompetence over the past many years. It has proven to be effective in cases where there is the coexistence of previous spontaneous preterm birth and ultrasonography‐based cervical incompetence in singleton pregnancies. On the contrary, results associated with TVC are not very promising in cases involving multiple pregnancies; indeed, various randomized studies and meta‐analyses suggest that TVC may even cause harm [2].

Transabdominal cervical cerclage (TAC) and its less invasive form LTCC have been recommended as a possible option for cases where the use of TVC has failed or the anatomy prevents its use. The LTCC procedure offers a higher and more effective point of fixating the sutures in the cervicoisthmic junction region when compared to vaginal procedures. It is recommended that LTCC be performed before pregnancy, as it is associated with better safety and easier access [3,5]. From available studies, it seems that LTCC may have lower blood loss, a shorter duration of hospitalization, and similar obstetric performance when compared to open TAC [8].

Although substantial scientific data support the use of TAC and LTCC in pregnant women with singleton pregnancies and cervical incompetence, especially after failure of TVC, there is very little information available regarding the application of the procedure in twin pregnancies, and those few studies that have been published are primarily case reports and series [4, 11, 12, 13]. In this paper, we present a case where the procedure performed at an interval before conception resulted in twin pregnancy conceived through IVF and successful delivery.

Case Presentation

The patient was a 29‐year‐old woman (G2P1) with primary infertility and diagnosed with cervical insufficiency presenting to seek preconception counseling. She had conceived twins through in vitro fertilization in her previous pregnancy, in which case a preventive McDonald transvaginal cerclage was done at 14 weeks. At that time, her cervix measured 36 mm, which was within the normal range, and it was based on an indicated procedure because of the anticipated twin pregnancy along with her clinical diagnosis of cervical insufficiency. Nevertheless, both fetuses were lost due to intrauterine fetal demise at 24 weeks. This was associated with advanced cervical effacement and dilatation due to cervical insufficiency as confirmed through transvaginal ultrasonography, as seen in Figure 1.

Investigations

After the miscarriage, two more single embryo transfer procedures were conducted without any success of embryo implantation leading to a clinical pregnancy. A detailed evaluation, which included pelvic ultrasound, hysteroscopy, and microbiological investigations, ruled out any factors like intrauterine adhesion, uterine abnormality, pathological condition of the uterus, and infection of the pelvis.

Management

Following extensive multidisciplinary counseling in regard to the scanty literature available about the use of abdominal cerclage in cases of multiple pregnancies, along with the inherent dangers involved with the procedure, the patient opted to undergo interval LTCC prior to her next pregnancy. Interval LTCC was done through a laparoscope via a four‐port approach (one 10 mm port at the umbilicus and three 5 mm accessory ports). Placement of the 5 mm Mersilene tape (Ethicon, Somerville, NJ) at the cervicoisthmic junction under direct visualization during laparoscopy, with retraction of the bladder anteriorly while placing the tape posterior to the broad ligaments, was done. Operative time was 85 min, with an estimated blood loss of 30 mL. No intraoperative problems were encountered; pelvic anatomy was normal, without pelvic adhesions, and the previous vaginal cerclage scar could be seen at the vaginal apex.

Follow Up and Outcomes

The patient was discharged the following postoperative day. Pelvic rest was advised for 6 weeks, along with 5 days of analgesia using paracetamol and ibuprofen. Fertility treatment was restarted after 3 months.

Subsequent IVF transfer involved two day‐5 embryos at the blastocyst stage. Vaginal micronized progesterone (400 mg twice daily) was prescribed starting from embryo transfer until week 12 of pregnancy. Measurements of cervical length throughout pregnancy were always stable at more than 35 mm, as shown in Table 1. There were no occurrences of preterm labor, antepartum bleeding, or infection among other complications. The cesarean delivery was performed at 37 completed weeks of gestation under regional anesthesia. Twins were successfully delivered: Twin A had a birth weight of 2680 g (Apgar scores 8 and 9 at 1 and 5 min, respectively) and Twin B had a birth weight of 2540 g (Apgar scores 8 and 9 at 1 and 5 min, respectively).

Discussion

The clinical management of cervical insufficiency in cases of multiple gestations can be especially difficult. In twin pregnancies, there is increased mechanical stress on the cervical uterine tissues, as well as an increase in cervical shortening and an elevated incidence of pre‐term birth, regardless of any pre‐existing conditions involving the cervix. Where both cervical insufficiency and multiple gestation occur together, either spontaneously or with the aid of assisted reproductive technologies, there is a much greater risk of fetal loss during the second trimester and very pre‐term delivery.

Transvaginal cerclage has been shown to be highly effective in singleton pregnancies with a history of spontaneous preterm births and short cervical lengths on ultrasound examination. A multi‐center meta‐analysis study concluded that there was a statistically significant decreased incidence of preterm deliveries less than 35 weeks of gestation following cerclage placement in singleton pregnancies (28% vs. 41%; p = 0.02) [6]. However, it is important to note that this effect is not observed in cases involving multiple gestations. According to a study conducted by Berghella et al., a combined analysis of patient level data showed that TVC in twins with indications from ultrasound examination had statistically significantly higher rates of delivery less than 35 weeks when compared to expectant management [7]. Such observations have resulted in recommendations against the use of transvaginal cerclages in multiple pregnancies, which serve as the clinical basis for our topic.

The transabdominal cerclage presents an optimal mechanical solution for those cases where the TVC method was unsuccessful or anatomically impossible. LTCC provides the cervicoisthmic location of the cerclage, which ensures the fixation on the area above the attachment of the cardinal and uterosacral ligaments, thus presenting greater structural stability and making it less likely that such a technique will be affected by the factors causing cervical effacement. Compared to the conventional open abdominal cerclage, laparoscopy involves decreased blood loss and quicker recovery time along with a similar obstetric profile [8]. Interval LTCC, done prior to pregnancy, is considered to be preferable compared to intrapregnancy placement due to the risk of membrane rupture and induced premature labor [3].

The case series of 100 cases of simplified laparoscopic cerclage presented by Huang et al. showed that more than 90% of patients delivered past 34 weeks in pregnancies complicated with previous unsuccessful vaginal cerclage, confirming its effectiveness in difficult‐to‐treat cervical incompetence [9]. The findings of Tulandi et al., which compared both techniques in their study subjects, proved the superiority of LTCC over TAC regarding intraoperative blood loss and postoperative recovery time, with no statistically significant difference in obstetric results [8].

Specific data on LTCC during twin pregnancy are currently available only in terms of several case reports or observational studies. In 2020, Debiève et al. described a series of seven cases of transabdominal cerclage performed in twin pregnancies, where the outcome proved to be good [17]. Similarly, a study carried out by Son et al. showed good gestational prolongation after transabdominal cerclage in twin pregnancies with previous unsuccessful TVC [13,14]. Huang et al. published a systematic review and guideline regarding this issue in 2019, highlighting the lack of strong evidence in favor of the technique [12].

The first description of successful interval LTCC before twin gestation was described by Pereira et al. [4]. However, this woman had a septate uterus who also received concomitant hysteroscopic metroplasty, thus constituting a potential confounding factor, as it is unknown if the reason for recurrent miscarriage was cervical insufficiency or the congenital uterine malformation. The current report is unique in terms of excluding a uterine malformation, leaving only cervical insufficiency as the primary cause of miscarriages, which were confirmed on the basis of comprehensive evaluation and exclusion of other etiologies such as hysteroscopy and microbiology. The difference between the two cases is crucial from a clinical perspective, as it demonstrates the importance of cervical insufficiency as the main condition to be treated by LTCC. As far as we know, this is one of the few reports of successful interval LTCC before twin gestation conceived by IVF in a woman without any uterine abnormalities.

However, it is necessary to understand that results in twins conceived through IVF treatment depend on various aspects other than cervical competency. Obstetric and neonatal complications in pregnancies that have been conceived through ART procedures have a high risk, owing to the multifetal status of the pregnancies. Placental characteristics such as chorionicity, placentation effectiveness, and fetoplacental circulation play a role in the progression of twin pregnancies, irrespective of whether a cervical procedure is performed [15,16]. As such, in the current scenario, there can be no direct correlation between the interval LTCC procedure and the positive result achieved. The success in delivering a healthy baby at term might be a combination of the effects of the cerclage procedure and appropriate prenatal management. It should not be considered as evidence for its efficacy in twin pregnancies [10].

Limitations

The case report was generated following the CARE guidelines for the generation of case reports. The major weakness of the study is that it does not have the capacity to provide causative evidence due to the limitations inherent in a single case report. It cannot provide any conclusive information on the effectiveness of interval LTCC in twin pregnancies based on the results of one case report. There is a need for future prospective multicenter registries and eventually randomized clinical trials wherever feasible to prove the role of abdominal cerclage in multiples.

Conclusion

This study adds to the existing literature regarding LTCC in multiple pregnancies and suggests that LTCC should be considered in a well‐chosen group of patients with cervical insufficiency that is refractory to other treatment options, including vaginal cerclage. It is important to remember that the use of the technique should not be standardized and used in all women. Comparative studies are required in order to establish the proper indication for LTCC.

Deep hybrid architecture for multi-class detection of network layer attacks in WSN
Authors: Anshika Sharma, Shalli Rani, Mohammad Shabaz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe growth of Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) in essential fields such as health care, defense, and environmental monitoring poses severe cybersecurity threats, specifically to the network layer. Network layer attacks such as Blackhole, Flooding, and Selective Forwarding are common and tend to have a serious impact on the data integrity, the reliability, and the lifetime of the network. To counter these vulnerabilities, this study proposes the development of a hybrid deep learning (DL) model combining the Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) structures for the detection of multi-class WSNs attacks. The proposed model combines the temporal learning capability of GRU with the long-term dependency learning strength of LSTM to accurately detect malicious attacks. Using WSNBFSF dataset, different types of experiments have been conducted to evaluate the performance of the developed model in accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score metric measures. Experimental results indicate that the GRU+LSTM hybrid model offers improved classification performance when compared to traditional DL models, i.e., Deep Neural Network (DNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), Artificial Neural Network (ANN), Attention, LSTM, and GRU models for multi-class classification tasks. The model achieved an overall accuracy of 97.41% (without SMOTE) and 98.74% using a Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) technique and k-fold validation along with macro F1 and weighted F1 of 0.9874 across multiple attack classes. These results indicate that the proposed architecture effectively captures complex sequential attack patterns and provides a reliable solution for intelligent intrusion detection in WSN environments.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

WSNs represent a crucial aspect of intelligent systems, which enable error-free communication and information gathering in a wide range of applications such as environmental monitoring, smart cities, health surveillance, military missions, and automation1,2. Such networks consist of many sensor nodes (SN) that have been installed in variously distributed, and sometimes unattended, conditions for gathering, processing, and relaying data to a central processing unit3. Even though WSNs come with numerous benefits, it also come with their inherent features which comprise limited energy supply, low computational capabilities, decentralized control, and open communication links. Such features make WSNs extremely vulnerable to security threats, and other malicious activities. Perhaps the greatest challenge for a WSN is layering attacks since they directly threaten the data routing and communication reliability4,5. These attacks include Blackhole, where malicious nodes discard packets rather than forwarding them, Flooding attacks which flood the network with duplicate requests, and Forwarding attacks where specific packets are discarded6. The effects of these attacks could risk the network performance, data loss, or even system failures7,8. Hence, some research significance has been attributed to guaranteeing strong and smart intrusion detection in WSNs to preserve data integrity and network dependability9,10.

Conventional security measures like cryptographic methods and rule-based IDS are limited in their capacity to protect because they rely on preconfigured signatures and assumptions of static actions11,12. These methods are ineffective in detecting new or evolving attack patterns, which may be dynamic and nonlinear in nature13. More recently, ML and DL-based intrusion detection methodologies have received attention because they have inherent capabilities for automatic learning and adaptation to complex data patterns14. In particular, DL models have demonstrated impressive performance in identifying unseen attack behaviors by learning hierarchical and temporal features from network traffic data15,16.

While intrusion detection systems based on ML and DL have are more advanced, there are still some problems that need to be solved to make WSN environments secure17. Multiple existing current methods use single models like CNN, RNN, or LSTM that can learn either spatial or temporal features, but they frequently fail to do an adequate task of capturing both short-term changes and long-term dependencies in network traffic data. Additionally, many existing IDS only focus on binary classification, which makes it more difficult for them to find more than one form of attack at the same time in complicated WSN scenarios. An additional challenge is that many of the proposed models fails to fully use the complementing strengths of different recurrent architectures. This makes it harder to generalize when trying to find different types of network-layer attacks. To make IoT-enabled WSN systems safer and smarter, it is important to fix these problems18. To overcome these limitations, this work presents a hybrid DL model by integrating GRU and LSTM networks to detect multi-class network layer attacks in WSNs. In the proposed GRU-LSTM hybrid model, fast learning and efficiency of GRUs are combined with the memory retention and contextual depth enabled by LSTMs. It can discover both the short-term variations and long-term relations present in the WSN data. This synergy improves its ability to detect various attack behaviors to provide increased accuracy in detection and resilience over simple architectures or individual models. The hybrid model is further tested using the WSNBFSF dataset consisting of a variety of attack types at the network level including Blackhole, Flooding, and Forwarding, with normal traffic cases.

Literature review

Dhanalakshmi et al.19 have proposed efficient DL-based classification models. First, the data are generated using the Cluster Head (CH) selection procedure with Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). The encryption and decryption procedures for data security are accessible after the key is produced using the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) algorithm in hybrid mode and Elliptic Curve Crypto systems (ECC). For the classification of attacks and non-attacks on WSN, a Modified Deep Convolutional Neural Network-Bi-Directional LSTM with Attention Mechanism model is developed. Salmi et al.20 applied DL techniques to detect and categorize several DoS attacks. The current research is focused on evaluating a series of DL-based methods that provide a precise, lightweight, and effective way to identify DoS attacks in WSNs. The algorithms were evaluated and trained using WSN-DS, a WSN-specific dataset. The four DoS attack types Blackhole, Grayhole, Scheduling, and Flooding attacks and instances without attack are part of the WSN-DS dataset. They also examine earlier efforts in creating ML-based intrusion detection systems for WSNs.

Al et al.8 suggested a predictive approach designed to avoid cyberattacks on WSNs in Industry 4.0. Using a multi-criteria approach, the suggested architecture improves the cybersecurity of WSNs in Industry 4.0. Using ML and DL techniques, it detects intrusions in Industry 4.0 WSNs and prevents them by prioritizing threats according to the context and type of attacks. As suggested algorithms in the framework, they used three models including Decision Tree, MLP, and Autoencoder. They used MLP and DT models for multidimensional cybersecurity intrusion detection and categorization. To protect Wi-Fi-based WSNs from common cyberthreats as flooding, injection attacks, and impersonation, Sadia et al.21 presented an advanced NIDS. To provide a targeted and effective study, 76 features are first chosen from a pool of 154 features, and these are then further reduced to 13 key features. This research train suggested a CNN based strategy aiming for optimal intrusion detection and prevention across multiclass classifications within WSN systems, using conventional scaler functions for feature scaling and preprocessing, also compared with DNN and LSTM. The goal of this study was to identify wormhole attacks by examining network node connectivity information by Alshehri et al.22. To tackle these issues with wormhole attack detection in sensor networks, ML approaches were suggested. To categorise traffic data and detect malicious nodes in the network, the base station (BS) uses two models SVM and DNN. These algorithms’ efficacy is examined against real-world circumstances and verified using the traffic produced by the NS3.37 simulator. The performance of the suggested models is evaluated using metrics such as average recall, false positive rates, latency, end-to-end delay, reaction time, throughput, energy consumption, and CPU utilization.

Soltani et al.23 proposed a new hybrid approach that merges adaptive ML with DLT to improve the security and reliability of WSN. Specifically, the adaptive ML models detect replica nodes based on network properties like location, signal strength, and transmission rate, while the approach utilizes DLT for vote record protection and incentive management. Three ML-based approaches were proposed and evaluated for replication attack detection: RFM, AWRFM-PRN, and AWRFM-PGRN. Weight updates in iterations based on previous predictions were utilized by AWRFM-PRN and AWRFM-PGRN models in order to enhance the detection accuracy. Arunachalam et al.24 proposed a new model for vampire attack detection based on DL. The overall steps of the developed DL-based vampire attack detection model were data collection, detection of an attack, mitigation, and selection of an optimal path. The first step is to gather the data properties for each sensor node in the WSN system. Additionally, a Weighted WRNN detects vampire attacks; in this case, utilizing Enhanced Golf Optimization algorithm, the weight values are optimized. The identified vampire nodes were successfully divided according to several node attributes, such as node PRR, node energy, and node broadcast count. The vampire nodes were removed from the network to carry out the attack mitigation process; the remaining nodes are taken into account for the routing procedure. Dougan et al.25 proposed a lightweight ML framework to identify attacks and malicious traffic in SCADA systems based on IoT. The model is based on network logs provided by Zeek, statistical traffic features and the Isolation Forest method, which provides high detection accuracy with low computing cost, making it suited for resource-constrained SCADA systems and real-time cybersecurity monitoring.

To solve the problem of intrusion detection in WSN systems, Alkhatib et al.26 suggested a new improved bidirectional long-short-term-memory (Bi-LSTM) algorithm, which is an extension of the LSTM model and consists of four steps. As part of the preparation phase, the original raw data can be utilized to train learning models using data gathered from the network. The second phase involved detecting the attacks after the analyzed data had been categorized based on the categories of network traffic. In the following stage, the suggested enhanced learning models outperformed conventional detection techniques in terms of accuracy. This paper researched the performance of DL algorithms on two different datasets, namely WSN-DS and KDDCup99, selected for the detection and classification of different types of DoS attacks. John et al.27 proposed the development of Cluster-Based WSN and Variable Selection Ensemble ML Algorithms (CBWSN_VSEMLA) as a framework for a security threats detection system for detecting DoS attacks. In the design of CBWSN, FCM clustering has been used, whereas VSEMLA is a detection system that makes use of PCA for feature selection and a number of ensemble ML algorithms for detecting flooding, scheduling, grayhole, and blackhole attacks. Polat et al.28 presented a comparison of ML based DDoS attack detection in SDN networks across a wide range of application areas including IoT, SCADA, 5G/mobile networks and VANETs. Unlike the prior studies, it provides a holistic review of SDN security problems, threats and detection methodologies in numerous real-world deployment scenarios.

Sinha et al.2 present a DL-based IDS by combining CNN and RNN with an adversarial-aware optimization model. Based on their structure, diversity, and requirements of DL, different benchmark datasets such as NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and CTU-13 have been investigated. A compound objective is put forward that maximizes the detection accuracy while minimizing adversarial susceptibility and guarantees model generalizability. This is addressed using SMOTE-based synthetic oversampling. The performance of the proposed framework is comparatively better in terms of robustness and transferability while evaluating cross-dataset and intra-dataset tests. Hussain et al.29 have proposed MultiNet-IDS architecture that incorporates DRL, LSTM networks, and a two-stage EFS method for reliable intrusion detection. To select an optimal subset and improve the model efficiency without any loss of accuracy, the EFS method first performs filtering of features based on Pearson correlation and mutual information and then performs a thorough combinatorial search together with K-fold cross-validation. Temporal dependencies in traffic flows are captured by LSTM, and DRL enables adaptive learning from shifting attack behaviors. Research gaps

While numerous studies have examined ML and DL methods for intrusion detection in WSNs, still there are some gaps exists. Most of the existing studies only focus at the DL architecture, such CNN, RNN, LSTM, or the ML algorithm. These methods often have challenges capturing both short-term and long-term temporal relationships in network traffic patterns. Multiple models focus on either long-term sequence learning or computing efficiency, but they do not often do both well in the same architecture. Moreover, several existing studies predominantly focus on binary intrusion detection or constrained attack classifications, hence limiting their relevance in real-world multi-class attack scenarios. Another gap exists in existing research is the inadequate examination of hybrid DL architectures that can simultaneously utilize the complimentary characteristics of various recurrent models to enhance attack detection. Moreover, several detection methods do not explicitly address network-layer attacks, including Blackhole, Flooding, and Forwarding, which directly affect routing dependability and data delivery in WSNs. To fill these gaps, this study suggests a hybrid GRU–LSTM DL architecture that can capture both short-term changes in WSN traffic data and long-term contextual dependencies which improve the performance of multi-class detection for network-layer attacks.

Architecture of wireless sensor networks

WSN is a distributed network composed of spatially deployed SN that collaboratively monitor physical or environmental parameters such as temperature, pressure, humidity, or network activity. The architecture of a WSN typically consists of four main components: SN, CH, BS, and the end-user interface. All the components are critical for data sensing, aggregation, forwarding, and decision-making. SN at the lowest level are tiny devices with limited power and endowments in sensing, computation, and communication. They perceive environmental information and relay it either directly or via multi-hop to a neighboring CH. CH operate as middlemen that preprocess and aggregate data from several SN to eliminate redundancy and minimize network energy expenditure. The aggregated data is subsequently sent to a BS (sink node), the central point where data collection, analysis, and forwarding to the end-user interface or control center for decision-making takes place. The information in WSNs is exchanged mainly through wireless links, using protocols optimized for energy consumption, scalability, and reliability. The structure of the network may be flat, hierarchical, or hybrid depending on the needs of the applications. WSNs are extremely susceptible to a range of security breaches and attacks, including at the network layer – Blackhole, Flooding, Grayhole, and Selective Forwarding attacks. These attacks interfere with data transmission, reduce network performance, and weaken system reliability, thus requiring intelligent and interpretable detection mechanisms like ML and DL-based techniques.

The architecture of WSN is typically structured into multiple layers, each performing specific network functions, ensuring efficient communication and data management with each layer being susceptible to different types of attacks that can compromise network performance and security as shown in Fig. 1.

Layer-wise classification of attacks in wireless sensor networks.

Physical layer

It is the backbone of the WSN architecture that transfers raw data bits wirelessly over wireless channels through radio frequency or infrared signals. It performs operations like modulation, signal detection, and frequency selection while ensuring energy efficiency and good signal transmission. But this layer is vulnerable to Jamming and Tampering Attacks, where an attacker knowingly transmits radio signals on the same frequency to jam communication, leading to packet loss and decreased network performance.

Data link layer

This Layer ensures reliable data transmission across the physical medium by providing access control over the common wireless channel. It involves data error detection, correction, and medium access to avoid collisions. Although it facilitates reliable communication, this layer can be targeted by Collision, Exhaustion, and Denial-of-Sleep Attacks where an attacker node initiates continuous retransmissions or overconsumption of energy, resulting in decreased node lifespan and overall network instability.

Network layer

This layer is responsible for forwarding data packets from SN to the BS. It handles routing paths, network topology, and packet forwarding for effective communication. It is, however, specifically susceptible to attacks like Blackhole, Flooding, and Selective Forwarding, in which malicious nodes interfere with regular routing by dropping, misforwarding, or forwarding packets excessively. These attacks deteriorate network performance, decrease packet delivery ratios, and violate the dependability of data transmission.

Transport layer

This layer provides end-to-end communication reliability at nodes by managing congestion control, packet retransmission, and session management. It provides consistency of communication even in unreliable network environments. However, it is vulnerable to Session Hijacking and De-synchronization Attacks, where attackers flood the network with duplicate requests or interfere with existing communication sessions, thus depleting resources and lowering data delivery reliability.

Application layer

This layer offers a linkage between the sensor network and the end-user application. It helps in data gathering, query processing, and network observation and specifies how the data sensed is packaged and delivered. Attacks like Data Aggregation and Path-Based DoS can take place at this layer, where the hackers tamper with or compromise the gathered data to mislead decision-making or interfere with regular service provision.

The WSN structure is structured into layers of functionalities to ensure effective communication, energy saving, and secure transmission of data. Each layer has inherent vulnerabilities that can be attacked by hackers. Among them, the most important one is the Network Layer in light of its functionality for routing data and vulnerability to routing attacks like Blackhole, Flooding, and Selective Forwarding. Therefore, this research is aimed at identifying these network-layer attacks with the help of a hybrid GRU-LSTM DL model, with the goal of improving detection accuracy, minimizing false alarms, and facilitating secure and reliable WSN communication.

Data preprocessing

Data preprocessing is a crucial step in preparing the dataset for effective model training and evaluation. In this study, the WSNBFSF dataset has been used, which contains multiple network traffic features along with four classes–Blackhole, Flooding, Normal, and Forwarding representing different types of network layer attacks. Initially, the dataset has been examined for missing, redundant, and irrelevant values to ensure data consistency and reliability. The categorical target labels then encoded into numerical values (0, 1, 2, and 3) to facilitate multi-class classification. All numerical features are then normalized using Min–Max scaling to bring them within a uniform range, preventing bias due to feature magnitude differences and accelerating model convergence. The dataset has been subsequently divided into training and testing subsets to allow fair model evaluation. These preprocessing steps ensured the data is clean and suitable for training DL models effectively. The WSNBFSF dataset exhibits a significant class imbalance where the number of normal traffic samples is substantially larger than the attack samples. To mitigate the potential bias caused by this imbalance, SMOTE has been applied to the training data to generate synthetic samples for minority attack classes.

Proposed hybrid model architecture

In the proposed research, a hybrid DL model has been put forward that uses both GRU and LSTM networks to identify network-layer attacks in WSN32. The motivation behind this architecture is to utilize the efficacy of GRU in capturing short-term dependencies and the resilience of LSTM in keeping long-term temporal patterns. By combining these two models, the model becomes capable of utilizing their complementary strengths, offering both computational efficacy and powerful representational capacity.

The hybrid architecture follows a sequential approach where the GRU layers first take the input sequences to acquire compact temporal features. The sequential ordering of GRU followed by LSTM has been selected to utilize their complementary temporal learning capabilities. GRU networks are computationally lighter and capable of rapidly capturing short-term sequential variations in network traffic patterns. These short-term features are then forwarded to the LSTM layer, which is better suited for learning long-term contextual dependencies through its memory cell and gating mechanisms. This hierarchical feature extraction strategy allows the model to first learn local temporal dynamics and subsequently refine them into deeper long-range patterns associated with complex routing attacks. Empirical testing with alternative configurations such as LSTM–GRU and parallel GRU–LSTM architectures demonstrated that the GRU–LSTM sequential configuration provided more stable convergence and improved detection performance for WSN network-layer attacks.

Hyperparameter configuration and ablation analysis

Various hyperparameter settings of the proposed GRU-LSTM architecture were experimentally tuned to provide stable convergence and better detection performance. During testing, important hyperparameters were evaluated experimentally, such as hidden units, learning rate, batch size and optimizer selection. The final model configuration included 32 hidden units for GRU and LSTM layers, Adam optimizer with learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 512 and Cross-Entropy loss function as shown in Table 4. To avoid redundant training and increase convergence stability, an early stopping technique with a patience value of 10 was used based on validation loss monitoring. An ablation study also performed to further support the architectural design by analyzing individual GRU and LSTM models independently and assessing them with the proposed hybrid GRU–LSTM framework. The experimental results show that the independent GRU model is computationally efficient but has limited long-term context-based learning ability while the independent LSTM model can model long-range dependencies well but with higher computational complexity. The suggested Hybrid model successfully integrates the advantages of both architectures and provides greater efficiency in terms of accuracy, recall and F1-score on different classes of attack.

Accuracy

Accuracy evaluates the overall correctness of the model’s predictions. It is defined as the ratio of correctly classified instances whether attack or normal to the total number of instances in the dataset. A higher accuracy indicates that the model effectively distinguishes between attack and normal traffic across all classes. However, in imbalanced datasets, accuracy alone may not fully represent the model’s effectiveness, hence the need for additional metrics like precision and recall.

Precision

Precision calculates the ratio of true positive attack cases over all those predicted to be attacks. It indicates how well the model can prevent false positives, i.e., how reliable the model is when it labels a sample as an attack, meaning that it actually is an attack. High precision means that the positive predictions of the model are very trustworthy, and thus it is crucial in situations where false alarms can cause useless consumption of resources or automatic countermeasures.

Recall

Recall measures the model’s ability to detect true attack occurrences out of all actual attack instances available in the dataset. It seeks to reduce false negatives so that as many malicious activities as possible are caught. High recall indicates that the model is able to catch real threats, which is very important when applying it in security-critical tasks such as intrusion detection.

F1-score

The F1-score is the harmonic mean of recall and precision, and it gives a balanced figure between the two. It is particularly helpful in cases when there is an imbalanced distribution of classes because it takes into account false positives and false negatives at the same time. A higher F1-score signifies that the model maintains a strong trade-off between detecting attacks accurately and avoiding false alarms, thus offering a more comprehensive assessment of model performance.

Each baseline model used in this study exhibits certain inherent limitations that hamper its individual effectiveness for network layer attack detection in WSNs. Traditional feedforward architectures such as ANN and DNN fail to capture temporal correlations within traffic patterns, as they treat input features independently. Recurrent networks such as RNNs are plagued by vanishing gradients, limiting their capacity to learn long-term dependencies, while LSTMs break this constraint at the expense of higher computational complexity less appealing in resource-scarce WSN settings. GRUs offer a less heavy alternative but can fail to preserve deep contextual information when confronted with multi-stage attack behaviors. Attention-only models, though able to concentrate on discriminative features, are devoid of intrinsic temporal modeling, leading to incorrect interpretation of sequential attack signatures. These shortcomings in combination encourage a hybrid model capable of learning both short-term efficiency and long-term dependency simultaneously, for which the present proposed GRU+LSTM architecture is developed.

Statistical analysis

To further validate the robustness and reliability of the experimental findings, a Friedman test has been conducted to statistically compare the performance of the evaluated models, including LSTM, GRU and the proposed hybrid model. The Friedman test is a widely used non-parametric statistical method for comparing multiple algorithms across repeated experimental runs including as k-fold validation. The test produced a Friedman statistic value of 10.0 with a corresponding p-value of 0.0067, which is lower than the significance threshold of 0.05. Therefore, the null hypothesis that all models perform equally is rejected. This result indicates that there is a statistically significant difference among the compared models. The analysis confirms that the proposed hybrid GRU–LSTM model consistently achieves superior performance compared to the baseline models, thereby demonstrating the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed approach for WSN intrusion detection.

Limitation and future work

While the suggested hybrid model shows great accuracy and resilience in identifying network-layer attacks in WSN, it still has some limitations. The model needs a great deal of labeled data to successfully train, which may not always be present in practical WSN deployments. Moreover, while the hybrid structure is effective, it still has moderate computational overhead, so it is not very appropriate for extremely resource-limited SN. Subsequent work can include creating light or adaptive hybrid frameworks optimized for edge or fog computing platforms. Although the proposed model demonstrated strong performance on the WSNBFSF dataset, the evaluation has been conducted using a single dataset that focuses on network-layer attacks. Future research could extend the evaluation by testing the model on additional WSN security datasets such as WSN-DS or IoT-based intrusion detection datasets. Cross-dataset validation would further confirm the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed hybrid architecture across different network environments and attack scenarios. Additionally, incorporating Explainable AI (XAI) methods can improve model transparency, allowing improved interpretability of attack patterns and better support for more credible intrusion detection systems.

Conclusion

This study presented a hybrid DL architecture combining GRU and LSTM networks for the detection of network-layer attacks in WSN. The proposed model has been designed to capture both short-term sequential variations and long-term contextual dependencies present in WSN network traffic. Using the WSNBFSF dataset, the model has been evaluated against several baseline DL models including ANN, DNN, RNN, LSTM, GRU, and Attention-based architectures. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed hybrid model achieved the highest overall accuracy of 97.41% (without SMOTE) and 98.74%(with SMOTE) technique and k-fold validation along with macro F1 and weighted F1 of 0.9874 across multiple attack classes. These results indicate that integrating GRU and LSTM enables more effective modeling of complex sequential patterns associated with network-layer attacks. However, further evaluation on additional datasets and real-world network environments is necessary to fully validate the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed model. Despite the promising results, the study has certain limitations such as evaluation on a single dataset and moderate computational complexity for extremely resource-constrained nodes. Future research can focus on developing lightweight hybrid architectures optimized for edge computing environments and evaluating the model on multiple datasets to further validate its robustness. Additionally, integrating explainable AI techniques could enhance the interpretability of the model and support more transparent intrusion detection systems for WSN security. In addition, future research will investigate the generalization capability of the proposed model using multiple benchmark and real-world WSN datasets with adaptive and self-learning intrusion detection techniques for zero-day and unknown threats as evolving attacks remain a serious concern.

Effective thermal conduction of a Signorini-type problem in composites with rough interface
Sara Monsurrò *†, Carmen Perugia, Federica Raimondi *†

Abstract
We analyse the effect of a Signorini-type interface condition on the asymptotic behaviour, as ε \varepsilon tends to zero, of a problem posed in an open bounded cylinder of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , divided in two connected components by an imperfect rough surface. The Signorini-type condition is expressed by means of two complementary equalities involving the jump of the solution on the interface and its conormal derivative via a parameter γ \gamma . Different limit problems are obtained according to the values of γ \gamma and the amplitude of the interface oscillations. † † Keywords: Homogenization, nonlinear elliptic problems, rough interface, variational inequality, Signorini-type condition. † † MSC: 35B27, 35J60, 35J66, 35J20, 35R35.

Introduction

In this paper we are interested in a free boundary-value problem posed in a two-component domain.
A free boundary-value problem consists in a partial differential equation combined with boundary conditions often expressed via a set of equalities and inequalities. As a consequence, the unknowns are both the solution and regions of the boundary of the domain. Unlike classical cases, the weak formulation of such problems may involve variational inequalities, whose mathematical and historical backgrounds can be found in
[
4
,
15
,
24
,
26
]
.

In particular, we are interested in Signorini-type conditions given on a rough surface separating the two parts of the composite.
These kinds of problems are widely used to model phenomena arising from engineering and material sciences, such as lubrication of rough surfaces
[
34
]
, frictional forces at the contact area of two different materials
[
5
,
18
]
, turbulence flows over rough walls
[
1
,
23
]
and heat and mass transfer on surfaces with roughness elements between solid and fluid interfaces
[
21
]
.

The homogenization of problems presenting Signorini boundary conditions in domains with oscillating boundaries, in both linear and nonlinear cases, has been treated in
[
19
,
20
,
27
]
. In
[
7
,
10
,
33
]
the geometrical setting is given by perforated domains, whilst in
[
31
]
domains with inclusions have been considered. Cracks and layers were taken into account in the works
[
9
,
22
]
.

Here we consider an open and bounded cylinder

in
\mathbb{R}^{N},

N\geq 2
, where
\omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and

. Denoted by
\varepsilon
a small positive parameter tending to zero, the domain
Q
is made up of two connected components
Q_{\varepsilon}^{+}
and
Q_{\varepsilon}^{-}
separated by a rough interface
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, which is the graph of a Lipschitz-continuous and quickly oscillating function,
\varepsilon
-periodic in the first
N-1
variables, see Figure
1
. The amplitude of the oscillations is of order
\varepsilon^{k}
, with

. Therefore, as

(cf. Figure

Our aim is to study the asymptotic behaviour of the following Signorini-type problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f&\hbox{ in }Q\setminus\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{-}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}=(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}&\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle[u_{\varepsilon}]\geq 0,\quad(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]\geq 0&\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle[u_{\varepsilon}]((A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}])=0&\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=0&\hbox{ on }\partial Q,\end{array}\right.

where
A^{\varepsilon}
and

are
\varepsilon

,

From a physical point of view, problem (

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
of perfect conduction (absence of jump) and to regions characterized by an imperfect contact transmission condition. This phenomenon is well described by our Signorini-type conditions, which mean that one can distinguish two a priori unknown subsets of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
where the two alternative equalities

(1.2)

\displaystyle[u_{\varepsilon}]=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}h^{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]=0

are satisfied.

The above imperfect transmission condition is physically justified in

(see also

,

Problems posed in the same domain

We prove that the effective thermal properties of the composite vary according to the amplitude of the oscillations

In cases A) and B), the two alternative conditions in (

split in two components

and

\varepsilon

In case B), the limit problem suggests that

[

]

Finally, in case C), we find a homogenized Dirichlet elliptic problem in the whole cylinder

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
where the two temperature fields differ is such small that it does not affect the asymptotic behaviour. Roughly speaking, the limit problem behaves as in presence, at
\varepsilon

[u_{\varepsilon}]=0

We remark that one can also assume
h^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
, obtaining just one limit problem for all values of

4.3

Let us mention that the results of the paper can be generalized adding in the first equation of problem (
1.1
) a lower order term of the type
h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})
, being
h_{1}
an increasing and Lipschitz-continuous function, vanishing in zero, see Remark

The key point of this work is the choice of appropriate test functions allowing to identify the different limit problems. Moreover, in cases A) and B) we need to handle products of two weakly convergent sequences. To this aim, we take advantage of lower semi-continuity arguments, suitable in the framework of variational inequalities.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we describe the geometrical setting and introduce the functional framework, along with some preliminary results. In Section 3, once established the assumptions on the data, we prove the unique solvability of the problem at
\varepsilon
-level, together with some uniform a-priori estimates. Section 4 is devoted to determine the effective thermal conduction of the problem.

\varepsilon

Given

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=f&\hbox{ in }Q\setminus\Gamma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]

\displaystyle K^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}=\{v\in W_{0}^{\varepsilon}\ |\ [v]\geq 0\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\}.

Case B):

Proof.

be the weak solution to problem (

and the a priori estimate (

)

(4.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u&\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(Q)\text{ and a.e. in }Q,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\chi_{Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\chi_{Q^{+}}\nabla u&\text{ weakly in }(L^{2}(Q))^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\chi_{Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\chi_{Q^{-}}\nabla u&\text{ weakly in }(L^{2}(Q))^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Consequently, one has

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\chi_{Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\chi_{Q^{+}}u&\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(Q),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\chi_{Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\chi_{Q^{-}}u&\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(Q),\end{array}\right.

in view of the almost everywhere convergence (
2.4
).

Also, by arguing as in
[
14
, Proposition 3.1]
, we get

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle i)\ \chi_{Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\chi_{Q^{+}}A^{0}\nabla u&\text{ weakly in }(L^{2}(Q))^{N},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle ii)\ \chi_{Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\chi_{Q^{-}}A^{0}\nabla u&\text{ weakly in }(L^{2}(Q))^{N}.\end{array}\right.

The cases A) and B)

Let us take
\varphi\in W^{0}_{0}
such that
\varphi^{+}(x^{\prime},x_{N})\geq\varphi^{-}(x^{\prime},-x_{N})
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. Let

\psi_{2}

\varphi^{-}

\varphi^{+}=\psi_{1|Q^{+}}
and
\varphi^{-}=\psi_{2|Q^{-}}

Set
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{1}+\chi_{Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{2}
, one has that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in K^{\varepsilon}_{\gamma}
, that is

(4.12)

\displaystyle[\varphi_{\varepsilon}]\geq 0\quad\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon}.

Indeed, it results

(4.13)

\displaystyle[\varphi_{\varepsilon}]=\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{-}=\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}=\varphi^{+}(x^{\prime},x_{N})-\varphi^{-}(x^{\prime},-x_{N})\geq 0\quad\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{\varepsilon}.

Therefore, by definition of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
and Proposition
2.2
, we can pass to the limit in (
4.12
) and get

(4.14)

[\varphi]_{0}=\varphi^{+}-\varphi^{-}\geq 0\quad\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{0}.

Hence, we can define the closed convex subset of

as follows:

(4.15)

\displaystyle K^{0}=\{v\in W_{0}^{0}\ |\ [v]_{0}\geq 0\hbox{ on }\Gamma_{0}\}.

Moreover, by construction and using (
2.4
), it holds

(4.16)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\varphi\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(Q).

Let us take
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
as test function in the variational inequality (

), we want to pass to the limit in

\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon})dx+\varepsilon^{\gamma}\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}h^{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]([\varphi_{\varepsilon}]-[u_{\varepsilon}])d\sigma\geq\int_{Q}f(\varphi_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})dx.

Observe that first convergence in (

) and convergence (

\displaystyle\int_{Q}f(\varphi_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})\,dx\ \rightarrow\ \int_{Q}f(\varphi-u)\,dx.

Concerning the interface term, we recall that, in the coordinates

, it can be written as follows:

(4.19)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\gamma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}h^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime})[u_{\varepsilon}]([\varphi_{\varepsilon}]-[u_{\varepsilon}])\,d\sigma}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\gamma}\int_{\omega}h\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\left(u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right)\times\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\left(\left(\varphi^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-\varphi^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right)-\left(u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(x^{\prime},\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right)\right)\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\times\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2(k-1)}(\left|\nabla_{y^{\prime}}g(y^{\prime})\right|^{2})_{|y^{\prime}=x^{\prime}/\varepsilon}}\,dx^{\prime}.\end{array}

In view of (
3.9
)
i
and the construction of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, by Proposition

(4.20)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\ \rightarrow\ u^{\pm}(\cdot,0)\,\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\omega),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\varphi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\ \rightarrow\ \varphi^{\pm}(\cdot,0)\,\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\omega).\end{array}\right.

Hence, we can pass to the limit in (

In case A), taking into account Proposition

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\gamma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}h^{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]([\varphi_{\varepsilon}]-[u_{\varepsilon}])\,d\sigma}\rightarrow h_{\gamma,k}\int_{w}[u(x^{\prime},0)]_{0}([\varphi(x^{\prime},0)]_{0}-[u(x^{\prime},0)]_{0})\,dx^{\prime},

The case C)

In this case, one can prove that the limit function

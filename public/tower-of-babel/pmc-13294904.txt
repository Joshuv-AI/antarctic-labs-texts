Pharmacological Characterization of SLL-022CCP, a Selective κ‑Opioid Receptor Agonist with Potent Antinociceptive Effects and Low Central Side Effects
Authors: Jiang-Wen Gui, Jia-Wen Luo, Deng-Gao Zhang, Zhi-Wen Wang, Chao Zhang, Meng Sun, Jia-Qi Tan, Si-Yuan Tang, Shao-Liang Duan, Yu-Liang Lin, Min Liu, Li-Jin Feng, Li-Ming Shao, Jing-Gen Liu, Pan-Wen Liu, Wei Li, Yu-Jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The κ-opioid receptor (KOR) is recognized as a promising therapeutic target for the development of nonaddictive analgesics. However, the clinical utility of typical KOR agonists is limited by central adverse effects. SLL-022CCP is a novel KOR-selective ligand derived from a northebaine-based 4,5-epoxymorphinan scaffold, and we have previously demonstrated its high binding affinity for KOR. In this study, we performed a systematic evaluation of SLL-022CCP through in vitro and in vivo assays. In vitro, SLL-022CCP acted as an extremely potent full agonist at the KOR, inhibiting cAMP production with an EC50 of 0.002 nM, and exhibited high receptor subtype selectivity over the μ-opioid receptor (MOR, EC50 = 1.6 nM) and δ-opioid receptor (DOR, EC50 = 30.0 nM). SLL-022CCP recruited β-arrestin at the KOR with an EC50 of 15.5 nM and at the MOR with an EC50 of 668.0 nM. Molecular docking data for SLL-022CCP revealed that it maintains canonical interactions with the KOR orthosteric site while extending its terminal phenyl moiety into a hydrophobic subpocket. In vivo, SLL-022CCP produced potent antinociception in the hot plate test (ED50 = 1.816 mg/kg), acetic acid-induced writhing test (ED50 = 0.113 mg/kg), and significant antipruritic effects in the histamine-induced itch test (ED50 = 0.079 mg/kg) and CFA-induced itch test (ED50 = 0.105 mg/kg). The antinociceptive effects of SLL-022CCP were antagonized by the KOR-selective antagonist Nor-BNI, but not by the MOR antagonist CTAP (1 mg/kg, i.p.), supporting primarily KOR-mediated activity. Importantly, at the antinociceptive dose, SLL-022CCP did not elicit significant motor incoordination or conditioned place aversion (CPA) under current experimental conditions, while eliciting transient sedation, distinguishing it from the typical arylacetamide derivatives, like U50,488H. Collectively, these results identify SLL-022CCP as a highly potent and selective KOR agonist with a unique pharmacological profile that exhibits robust antinociceptive and antipruritic effects with low central side effects.

Introduction

The κ, μ and δ opioid receptors constitute the primary opioid receptor family. Classical opioid analgesics, such as morphine, primarily target the μ opioid receptor (MOR), are highly effective for moderate to severe pain. However, their clinical utility is limited by significant adverse effects, including respiratory depression and high addiction liability. , There is urgent need to develop novel analgesics that provide potent pain relief without these risks. κ-Opioid receptor (KOR) agonists present a promising alternative. KOR agonists produce robust analgesic and antipruritic effects, and the analgesic potency is comparable to MOR agonists, , however, the clinical use of KOR agonists are limited by side effects such as psychotomimetic effect, dysphoria, and sedation. ,, Currently, only three selective KOR-targeting therapeutics are approved for clinical use: the central-acting agonist nalfurafine (TRK820), the peripherally restricted agonists difelikefalin (CR845) and anrikefon (HSK21542) (Figure ). Nalfurafine was used in Japan, South Korea and China to treat systemic pruritus in patients undergoing hemodialysis or with chronic liver disease. − Difelikefalin was approved in the USA for the treatment of pruritus associated with chronic kidney disease undergoing hemodialysis, , while anrikefon is approved in China for pain relief. − Noteworthily, clinical reports have shown that nalfurafine did not produce dysphoria or psychotomimetic effects at therapeutic doses. ,− Preclinical studies in mice also confirmed that nalfurafine exerted antinociceptive and antipruritic effects without sedation and conditioned place aversion (CPA), only a small motor incoordination was observed at high doses. Primarily due to the risk of such centrally mediated adverse effects, to date, no other centrally acting, KOR-selective agonist has been approved for the clinical management of pain in humans.

Structural formula of marketed selective KOR agonists.

Our collaborative research team has long been committed to the development of opioid receptor ligands based on the 4,5-epoxymorphinans, with the aim of discovering candidate molecules that combine high selectivity, potent analgesia and favorable safety profiles. Based on exploration of the 7α-benzoyl-6,14-endoetheno-tetrahydrothebaine scaffold, after structural optimization we yielded the first highly selective KOR agonist, SLL-020ACP. However, significant sedation was observed at its effective antinociceptive doses. , To address this limitation, we synthesized its 7α-methyl epimer derivative, SLL-627, which retained potent KOR agonist activity while demonstrating no significant sedation or aversion at therapeutic doses, thereby achieving an initial separation of efficacy from adverse effects. Building on this progress, systematic modifications of substituents led to the identification of several structurally diverse and highly selective KOR agonists, including SLL-039, SLL-1206, and SLL-1324, and recently reported G protein-biased KOR agonist SLL-1380 ,− (Figure ).

SLL-022CCP is a novel synthetic compound and initial competitive radioligand displacement assay and [35S]­GTPγS binding assay show its high affinity and potent functional activity for KOR, but its comprehensive pharmacological profile remain unknown. In the present study, we perform a systematic evaluation of SLL-022CCP through in vitro and in vivo assays. We further assessed functional activity through cAMP inhibition assays to measure G-protein coupling efficiency, and Tango assays to measure β-arrestin2 recruitment. The antinociceptive and antipruritic potency of SLL-022CCP was quantitatively assessed using the hot plate test, acetic acid-induced writhing test, and histamine- and CFA-induced itch models. The pain-depressed behavior test was detected by lactic acid-depressed nesting. Potential sedative effects and motor impairment were investigated using locomotor activity test and the rotarod performance test. Finally, its dysphoria-like aversion liability was assessed by conditioned place aversion (CPA) experiment.

Materials and Methods

Reagents

SLL-022CCP was obtained from Department of Medicinal Chemistry, School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Fudan University (Shanghai, China). Nor-BNI, and U69593 were purchased from Abcam Public Limited Company. U50,488H was purchased from Shanghai WeiHuan Biotech Limited Company (Shanghai, China). DAMGO and DPDPE were purchased from Tocris Bioscience. CTAP was purchased from Selleck Chemicals Limited Liability Company. Morphine Hydrochloride was obtained from Shenyang No.1 Pharmaceutical Factory. Histamine and Complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

Drug Administration

In vitro, SLL-022CCP was dissolved in culture medium containing 1% DMSO and 0.1%BSA. Nor-BNI, U69593, DAMGO, CTAP, and DPDPE were dissolved in culture medium with 0.1% BSA. In vivo, all reagents were intraperitoneally injected (i.p.) by 10 mg/mL. SLL-022CCP was dissolved in saline solution containing 1% DMSO and 0.1% BSA. Nor-BNI, CTAP, Morphine, U50,488H, and histamine were dissolved in saline.

Cell Line Culture

The HEK293T cell line and the HTLA cell line (a HEK293 cell line stably expressing TEV protease-fused β-arrestin2) were purchased from ATCC (American Type Culture Collection) and were maintained in culture medium consisting of Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco, Life Technologies) supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS), 2 μg/mL puromycin, and 100 μg/mL hygromycin B. All cells were cultured under standardized conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2).

cAMP Assay

For the cAMP inhibition assay, HEK293T cells underwent cotransfection with either human MOR or DOR constructs alongside the pGloSensor-22F cAMP plasmid (Promega) at a 1:1 molar ratio. In experiments involving the KOR, a HEK293-hKOR-GloSensor stable cell line was utilized. Following a 16 h-transfection period, transiently transfected cells were seeded into 96-well white clear-bottom plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells per well in 200 μL of DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and incubated at 37 °C under 5% CO2 overnight. The HEK293-hKOR-GloSensor stable line was plated at a lower density (6 × 104 cells/200 μL/well). After overnight incubation, 3-fold concentrated drug solutions were prepared in drug buffer (20 mM HEPES, 1× HBSS, 0.3% BSA, pH 7.4). Plates were equilibrated with GloSensor cAMP reagent (Promega, 40 μL/well, final 2% v/v) for 15 min at ambient temperature. Subsequently, 20 μL of drug solutions were dispensed into designated wells, followed by a 15 min dark incubation at room temperature. Chemiluminescent signals, reflecting intracellular cAMP levels, were quantified using a TECAN Infinite M200 Pro microplate reader. Dose–response curves were generated by plotting normalized luminescence against logarithmic drug concentrations, with EC50 values derived via nonlinear regression analysis (log­[agonist] vs response model) in GraphPad Prism (v.10.1.2).

Tango β-Arrestin Recruitment Assay

For transient transfection experiments, HTLA cells were transfected with the human KOR and MOR Tango plasmids via Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Following a 16 h incubation, transfected cells were seeded into 96-well white clear-bottom plates at a density of 2.5 × 105 cells per well in 100 μL complete medium and equilibrated overnight under the aforementioned culture conditions. Before drug stimulation, 3-fold concentrated agonist solutions were prepared in filter-sterilized assay buffer (DMEM + 10% heat-inactivated FBS) and administered to designated wells (50 μL/well). After 17 h of ligand exposure, residual medium and drug solutions were aspirated, followed by the addition of 50 μL/well Bright-Glo solution (Promega) diluted 1:20 in assay buffer. Following a 20 min incubation at ambient temperature (22–25 °C), luminescent signals were quantified using a TECAN Infinite M200 Pro microplate reader. Dose–response curves were generated by plotting normalized luminescence against logarithmic drug concentrations, with EC50 values derived via nonlinear regression analysis (log­[agonist] vs response model) in GraphPad Prism (v.10.1.2).

Experimental Methods for Molecular Modeling

The crystal structure of the KOR (PDB ID: 6B73) in its active conformation was obtained from the Protein Data Bank (PDB; https://www.rcsb.org). The reported crystal structure of KOR in complex with nalfurafine (PDB ID: 7YIT) was used directly for comparative analysis. The three-dimensional structures of SLL-020ACP and SLL-022CCP were constructed using SYBYL (Tripos, St. Louis, MO, USA). All ligand structures were energy-minimized using the Tripos force field with Gasteiger–Hückel charges, applying a convergence criterion of an energy gradient below 0.05 kcal·mol–1·Å–1 over a maximum of 1,000 iterations. Default parameters were used for all other optimization settings. Molecular docking studies were performed using GOLD Suite (Cambridge Crystallographic Data Center, Cambridge, UK) to dock ligands into the binding pockets of KOR and MOR. The binding site was defined as a sphere with a radius of 20 Å centered on the Cβ atom of the conserved Asp1383.32 residue. For each ligand, multiple binding poses were generated and evaluated based on GOLD docking scores and conformational clustering analysis. The most representative binding mode was selected by considering both the lowest docking score and the highest cluster population. The final ligand–receptor complexes were visualized and analyzed using PyMOL (The PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, DeLano Scientific, Palo Alto, CA).

Animals

Kunming mice and ICR mice (18–22 g, 6–8 weeks) were obtained from Beijing HFK Biotechnology Limited Company (Beijing, China). Mice were housed in a controlled environment with regulated temperature and humidity, with free access to food and water within a 12 h light/dark cycle. The number of experimental animals employed, and the intensity of noxious stimuli utilized were strictly limited to the minimum requirements necessary to demonstrate the consistent pharmacological effects of the administered treatments. All animal experimental procedures were reviewed and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Shanghai Institute of Materia Medica, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Approval Number: 2023-10-WYJ-08).

Hot Plate Test

The antinociceptive effect in female Kunming mice (n = 8) was performed using the hot plate test according to a previously reported method. The thermal nociceptive threshold was maintained at a constant temperature of 55 °C, and the paw withdrawal latency was recorded as the time elapsed from placement to the first occurrence of hind paw licking. A 60 s observation period was set as the maximum cutoff to avoid thermal injury. The nociceptive response of each mouse was assessed twice, with the average used as the baseline latency, and mice whose latency is shorter than 5 s or longer than 30 s were excluded. Mice were intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected with either saline, vehicle, SLL-022CCP (1, 3, 5, or 8 mg/kg) or morphine (10 mg/kg). For the antagonist study, mice were pretreated with the KOR antagonist Nor-BNI (10 mg/kg, −24 h, i.p.) or the MOR antagonist CTAP (1 mg/kg, −1 h, i.p.), ,,,,− then intraperitoneally injected with SLL-022CCP (8 mg/kg, −3 h) or vehicle (−3 h). Antinociception was evaluated by calculating the percentage of antinociception, employing the formula: (test latency – basal latency)/ (cutoff time – basal latency) × 100%. The antinociceptive ED50 value represents the dose required to achieve 50% antinociception.

Acetic Acid-Induced Writhing Test

The antinociceptive activity in male ICR mice was conducted using the abdominal constriction test according to the method previously described. An abdominal constriction was defined as a wave of contraction of the abdominal musculature followed by extension of the hind limbs. Following administration of graded doses of test compounds, each mice received an intraperitoneal injection of 0.6% acetic acid (10 mL/kg body weight), and the number of writhing signs displayed by each mouse was counted for 15 min. Mice were injected i.p. with either vehicle, SLL-022CCP (0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, or 0.5 mg/kg, −3 h) or morphine (0.5 mg/kg, −0.5 h). For the antagonist study, mice were pretreated i.p. with the Nor-BNI (10 mg/kg, −24 h), CTAP (1 mg/kg, −1 h), or vehicle, then injected i.p. with SLL-022CCP (0.25 mg/kg) or vehicle. Antinociceptive activity was evaluated using the percentage antinociception, expressed as 100 × (mean number of abdominal constrictions in control mice – mean number of abdominal constrictions in test mice)/mean number of abdominal constrictions in control mice. The antinociceptive ED50 value of each compound was obtained as the dose that produced 50% antinociception.

Lactic Acid-Induced Depression of Nesting Behavior

The lactic acid-induced depression was conducted according to the method previously described. , Briefly, after being individually housed for 24 h, male ICR mice were injected with SLL-022CCP (0.1 or 0.32 mg/kg, i.p.) 3 h before the test. Morphine (0.1 or 0.32 mg/kg, i.p.) or saline (i.p.) was administered 0.5 h before the test. Subsequently, 0.32% lactic acid was injected intraperitoneally. Five minutes later, new cotton balls of equal size (approximately 2.5 cm in diameter) were placed at the six corners of the cage. The mice were returned to their cages for a 100 min observation period, during which nesting behavior was quantified. The quantification standard was as follows: cotton balls transferred and used for nest building were scored, with a maximum score of 5 and a minimum score of 0.

Pruritogen-Induced Scratching Test

The antipruritic activity of male ICR mice was evaluated using the pruritogen-induced scratching test as described previously. Acute scratching behavior was induced by intradermal injection of 100 μL of the histamine (10 mg/mL in 1% DMSO) or 50 μL of the Complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA) into the nape of the neck of mice. Mice were injected i.p. with either vehicle (−3 h), SLL-022CCP (0.0625, 0.125, or 0.25 mg/kg, −3 h), or U50,488H (3 mg/kg, −0.25 h). The number of scratching bouts was assessed over a 30 min period after histamine or CFA injection.

Rotarod Test

Motor coordination performance were assessed in male Kunming mice (n = 7–8) utilizing a rotarod apparatus, equipped with an automated recording feature, following a protocol previously delineated. The rotarod system equipped with an automatic recording system was used to detect whether the compound had a sedative effect and whether it affected the motor coordination ability of mice. Mice were placed on the rotarod, with their positions set at a high level to prevent animals from jumping off the rod. Mice were trained three times (240 s per trial) per day for two consecutive days to adapt the device, and mice with baseline >160 s were screened for the following test. On the day of testing, following baseline determination from two trials, mice were intraperitoneally injected with either saline, U50,488H (5.0 mg/kg), or different doses of SLL-022CCP (1.8 and 8 mg/kg). The latency for each mouse to fall was recorded and was calculated as test latency/basal latency × 100%.

Locomotor Activity

Locomotor activity in mice was quantified using an automated video-based tracking system for comprehensive analysis of movement patterns. Male Kunming mice were administered i.p. vehicle controls (saline or 1% DMSO), U50,488H (5 mg/kg, −0.25 h), or different doses of SLL-022CCP (1.8 or 8 mg/kg, −0.25 h or −2.75 h). Mice were placed into locomotor chambers (30 × 30 × 40 cm), and the total distance traveled was recorded for 30 min.

Conditioned Place Aversion

The CPA experiment was performed with male ICR mice (n = 8) according to our previously published work. Briefly, the equipment consists of a rectangular Plexiglas container with two equal compartments separated by a removable partition. Each compartment is illuminated by a 5 W colored light: one has white floors/walls with red light, the other checkered floors/walls with blue light. The experiment included three phases: pretest (Day 1), conditioning (Days 3–8), and posttest (Day 10). On Day 1, mice freely explored the entire apparatus for 15 min, with time spent in each compartment recorded. During Days 3–8, two daily conditioning sessions (≥5 h apart) were conducted: morning sessions involved saline injection followed by 30 min in the unfavored compartment; afternoon sessions involved injection of SLL-022CCP (1.8 mg/kg or 8 mg/kg, i.p.) or U50,488H (5 mg/kg, i.p.), then 30 min in the preferred compartment 15 min postinjection. On Day 10, mice freely explored again for 15 min, with compartment time documented. The CPA score calculated by subtracting pretest time in the drug-paired compartment from test-phase time in the same compartment.

Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± SEM. The statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (Version 10.0), employing the appropriate methods as indicated in the figure legends. The group differences were analyzed with one way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by post hoc comparison using Bonferroni’s test. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 was considered significant.

To further characterize the efficacy and potency of SLL-022CCP, a cAMP inhibition assay was conducted. As shown in Figure A–C and Table , SLL-022CCP demonstrated potent and selective agonism at the KOR, inhibiting cAMP production with an EC50 of 0.002 nM. This represents >770-fold and >14,000-fold selectivity over the MOR (EC50 = 1.6 nM) and DOR (EC50 = 30.0 nM), respectively. SLL-022CCP-induced cAMP inhibition reached 76.5%, 77.7%, and 119.2% of the maximal stimulation obtained with U69593, DAMGO, and DPDPE, respectively. KOR antagonist nor-BNI and MOR antagonist CTAP concentration-dependently reversed cAMP inhibition induced by U69593 and DAMGO, while SLL-022CCP did not exhibit KOR or MOR antagonist activity (Figure D, E). Together, these in vitro functional data indicate that SLL-022CCP acts as a full agonist at the KOR. The β-arrestin recruitment activity of SLL-022CCP was further evaluated using the Tango assay. SLL-022CCP recruited β-arrestin at the KOR with an EC50 of 15.5 nM and at the MOR with an EC50 of 668.0 nM (Table and Figure F, G).

Molecular Modeling Studies

The modeled complex of SLL-022CCP bound to KOR was constructed and compared with the KOR-bound complexes of SLL-020ACP and nalfurafine. As shown in Figure A, the 4,5-epoxymorphinan scaffold of SLL-022CCP retained the canonical interactions within the orthosteric binding site of KOR, including a salt bridge between the protonated morphinan nitrogen and Asp1383.32, hydrophobic contacts between the phenanthrene core and Val2305.42 and Ile2946.55, and a weak hydrogen bond between the 6-methoxy group and Tyr1393.33. In addition, the carbonyl oxygen of the linker and the ortho-amino group of SLL-022CCP were oriented toward the extracellular, hydrophilic surface of the receptor, where no direct protein–ligand interactions were observed. In contrast, the terminal phenyl moiety extended into a hydrophobic side subpocket formed by Leu1353.29, Val1343.28, Trp124EL1, and Val1182.63. As illustrated in Figure B, SLL-022CCP, SLL-020ACP, and nalfurafine adopted highly similar binding conformations, displaying excellent superposition of both the 4,5-epoxymorphinan core and the terminal aryl groups. Notably, the carbonyl linkers of SLL-022CCP and SLL-020ACP showed close overlap in both position and orientation. Although the 6-carbonylamido spacer of nalfurafine exhibited poorer spatial overlap with the carbonyl linker and ortho-amino group of SLL-022CCP, these structural elements shared a comparable overall orientation and were consistently directed toward the hydrophilic surface of the KOR binding pocket.

SLL-022CCP-Induced Potent Antinociceptive and Antipruritic Effects

The antinociceptive properties of SLL-022CCP were assessed via the hot plate test and the acetic acid-induced writhing test. As shown in Figure A, in the hot plate test, SLL-022CCP-induced antinociception was observed at 3 h postinjection and then gradually declined and returned to the basal level within 6 h. SLL-022CCP demonstrated an ED50 of 1.816 mg/kg (Figure B and Table ). Consistent with these results, SLL-022CCP also produced potent, dose-dependent antinociception in the acetic acid-induced writhing test, exhibiting an ED50 of 0.113 mg/kg (Figure C and Table ). Its antinociception was more potent than morphine and classical KOR agonist U50,488H. In pruritus models, SLL-022CCP reduced scratching behaviors in a dose-dependent manner. The ED50 was 0.079 mg/kg in the histamine-induced itch (Figure A and Table ) and 0.105 mg/kg in the CFA-induced itch (Figure B and Table ).

SLL-022CCP-Induced Antinociception Was Mediated by KOR Activation

To identify the mechanism underlying SLL-022CCP’s antinociceptive effects, animals were pretreated with the KOR-selective antagonist Nor-BNI or the MOR-selective antagonist CTAP. SLL-022CCP Caused Transient Sedation at Its Therapeutic Dose

A novelty-induced locomotion assay was used to evaluate sedation. U50,488H, at its antinociceptive ED50 (5 mg/kg) caused significant sedation (Figure A). Discussion

In the present study, we found that SLL-022CCP acts as a highly potent KOR full agonist. It produced potent antinociceptive effects in models of thermal and visceral pain, and potent antipruritic effects against both histamine- and CFA-induced itch. Importantly, there effects were achieved without significant aversion and motor incoordination, but causing transient sedation, at therapeutic dose. These data suggest that SLL-022CCP may hold promise as a lead compound for the treatment of both pain and itch, with a potentially improved side effect profile.

In vitro, comparison of EC50 values across functional assays reveals distinct differences in potency. SLL-022CCP activates KOR-mediated G proteins with high potency in both the [35S]­GTPγS (EC50 = 9.1 nM) and cAMP (EC50 = 0.002 nM) assays. In contrast, its potency for β-arrestin recruitment (EC50 = 15.5 nM) is significantly lower, particularly relative to its picomolar potency in the cAMP assay. Even within the same G protein signaling pathway, the EC50 values for SLL-022CCP differed by approximately 4500-fold between the [35S]­GTPγS assay (9.1 nM) and the cAMP GloSensor assay (0.002 nM). This striking discrepancy likely arises from multiple factors. First, the assays were performed in different cell backgrounds (CHO for [35S]­GTPγS vs HEK293 for cAMP), with HEK293 cells typically exhibiting higher receptor reserve and more efficient signal amplification. Second, [35S]­GTPγS binding measures the most proximal event of G protein activation with minimal amplification, whereas cAMP levels represent a downstream, multistep cascade that can produce substantial signal amplification, particularly for high-efficacy agonists. Third, the GloSensor platform is inherently more sensitive than conventional membrane-based [35S]­GTPγS binding. We have verified that the picomolar cAMP potency is reproducible across independent replicates (n ≥ 3) and that similar potency are observed for reference compounds (U69593), suggesting assay-specific rather than compound-specific effects. SLL-022CCP displayed substantially higher potency in the cAMP assay (0.002 nM) than in the β-arrestin 2 Tango recruitment assay (15.5 nM), showing an approximately 7750-fold difference in apparent potency. The mechanistic basis for such large potency difference requires further investigation. Our structural modeling analysis suggests that SLL-022CCP maintains canonical interactions with KOR’s orthosteric site (e.g., salt bridge with Asp1383.32) and extends its terminal phenyl moiety into a hydrophobic subpocket formed by Leu1353.29, Val1343.28, Trp124EL1, and Val1182.63. Whether this specific interaction contributes to the observed differential potency requires future exploration.

In vivo, it was found that SLL-022CCP produced potent antinociceptive effects in both the hot plate test and acetic acid-induced writhing test. In the hot plate test, its antinociceptive ED50 was approximately 2.4-fold higher than that of U50,488H and 3.8-fold higher than that of morphine. Similarly, in the acetic acid test, SLL-022CCP exhibited an ED50 approximately 7.8-fold higher than U50,488H and 7.4-fold higher than morphine. Thus, SLL-022CCP produced greater potency than U50,488H and morphine. Although functional cAMP inhibition assays showed strong agonist activity at KOR (EC50 = 0.002 nM), it still displayed considerable activity at MOR (EC50 = 1.6 nM). Surprisingly, antagonism of KOR, not MOR, significantly attenuated SLL-022CCP-induced antinociception in both the hot plate and acetic acid-induced writhing test. The results demonstrate that the analgesic effects of SLL-022CCP are mediated primarily through KOR activation. A methodological limitation of this study is the use of CTAP, a peptide MOR antagonist. While published evidence support central MOR antagonism by CTAP at 1 mg/kg i.p., we cannot definitively rule out incomplete CNS exposure. , Future studies using centrally administered MOR antagonists (intracerebroventricular CTAP or nonpeptide antagonists such as β-FNA, if commercially available) would be required to conclusively establish MOR noninvolvement. Notably, Nor-BNI pretreatment did not fully abolish SLL-022CCP-induced antinociception, with approximately 35% of the effect remaining in the hot-plate test and approximately 25% remaining in the acetic acid-induced writhing test, suggesting the possible involvement of additional targets beyond KOR.

KOR agonists, like U50,488H, are known to cause CPA, motor incoordination and sedation in rodents. , In this study, we found that SLL-022CCP at its antinociceptive ED50 induced no CPA or motor incoordination under our experimental conditions, but did elicit transient sedation. At 8 mg/kg, 4-fold higher dose, it still did not induce CPA under the same conditions, but caused motor incoordination. Furthermore, a clear temporal dissociation between the antinociception, sedation and motor impairment was observed. Its antinociceptive effect peaked 3 h postadministration, sedation and motor incoordination (at the higher dose) both peaked at 0.5 h. This distinct, time-dependent divergence aligns with our prior findings for SLL-1324, which shows a lack of synchronization between the antinociceptive, motor incoordination and aversive effects. At 6-fold its antinociceptive ED50, compound SLL-1324 caused motor impairment at 4 h-post injection, significantly earlier than its antinociceptive peak at 8 h, while still failing to elicit CPA. Collectively, these data support that antinociception, motor incoordination and CPA mediated by KOR activation, may be attributed to distinct signaling pathways. Nevertheless, we cannot exclude the contribution of unique pharmacokinetic properties of SLL-022CCP. For instance, differential kinetics of blood-brain barrier penetration could result in region-specific accumulation or the formation of active metabolites with distinct temporal profiles and pharmacological activities. Additionally, SLL-022CCP may provide crucial insights into rational drug design strategies aimed at enhancing this temporal dissociation. Continued exploration of these aspects could potentially lead to more effective and targeted antinociceptive therapies with minimized side effects.

As shown in Figure , we have listed several close analogs of SLL-022CCP, including SLL-020ACP, SLL-627, SLL-039, SLL-1206, SLL-1324, and SLL-1380, which have been reported as KOR-selective agonists. In our previous studies, we have already provided parallel comparisons of the chemical structures and biological activities among SLL-039, SLL-1206, and SLL-132425, as well as between SLL-039 and SLL-138026. In the present work, we have now included a direct parallel comparison among SLL-020ACP, SLL-022CCP, and SLL-627, with the results summarized in Table . Compared with SLL-020ACP, the introduction of an additional ortho-amino substituent in SLL-022CCP is associated with increased MOR affinity and decreased KOR affinity, consistent with the observed binding data. Despite some differences in in vitro activity, both SLL-020ACP and SLL-022CCP exhibit comparable antinociceptive effects in rodent models and show apparent sedative effects at higher doses. By contrast, SLL-627, a 7β-epimeric analog of SLL-020ACP and SLL-022CCP, displays similar in vitro binding affinities, produces more potent antinociceptive activity in the hot plate test (but not in the abdominal constriction test), and does not induce apparent sedative effects at high doses in either the locomotor activity or rotarod assays. Considering that the structural difference between the 17,18-etheno and 17,18-ethano moieties generally has a minimal impact on biological activity, as exemplified by etorphine versus dihydroetorphine, we attribute the observed in vivo differences primarily to the 7α-configuration within this series of compounds.

The nociceptin/orphanin FQ peptide (NOP) receptor is recognized as the fourth member of the opioid receptor family due to its sequence homology with the classical opioid receptors. One limitation of the present work is the binding affinity and functional activity of SLL-022CCP at the NOP receptor was not evaluated. Given the substantial variability and heterogeneity reported for NOP receptor interactions among 4,5-epoxymorphinan-based ligands, it is difficult to reliably predict NOP affinity based solely on chemical structure. Nevertheless, a common observation across reported 4,5-epoxymorphinan derivatives and related bifunctional MOR/NOP ligands is that their affinity for the NOP receptor is generally lower than that for MOR. − If SLL-022CCP follows this established trend, its putative affinity for the NOP receptor (if any) would be expected to be weaker than its affinity for classical opioid receptors and therefore unlikely to play a dominant role in its pharmacological activities. Concerning off-target activity at other GPCRs or nonopioid systems, this remains an important consideration for future characterization.

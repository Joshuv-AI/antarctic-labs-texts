Germ expansion for 𝑺 ​ 𝑳 ​ ( 𝟐 ) \bm{SL(2)} in arbitrary characteristics
Jean-Pierre Labesse

Abstract
. Let F F be a local field of characteristic p p and G G be a connected reductive group over F F . Recall that Shalika’s germ expansion [ Sh ] of orbital integrals of regular semisimple elements near the identity, when it exists, is a sum indexed by the set 𝒰 G \mathcal{U}^{G} of unipotent conjugacy classes in G ⁡ ( F ) G(F) . Observe that if G = S ​ L ​ ( 2 ) G=SL(2) the set 𝒰 G \mathcal{U}^{G} is always compact; it is finite if p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 while it is uncountable if p = 2 p=2 . As a consequence, Shalika’s germ expansion for elliptic elements does not make sense if p = 2 p=2 . On the other hand the endoscopic expansion of elliptic orbital integrals always exists and yields a germ expansion equivalent if p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 (up to a Fourier transform) to Shalika’s germ expansion but is new if p = 2 p=2 .



1.
Introduction

The asymptotic behaviour of orbital integrals of semisimple elements in a connected reductive group over
F

is controlled by Shalika’s germs when
F
is a local field of characteritic zero and more generally whenever
the number of unipotent orbits is finite and no inseparability occurs. The simplest case where these
hypothesis fail is
SL(2,F)
with
F
of characteristic 2 since, for such a group, there is an uncountable
set of unipotent orbits.

Shalika’s germ expansion is a very useful tool in local harmonic analysis
and in particular one would need a substitute for them to extend, in positive characteristic, the proof
by Kottwitz of Weil’s conjecture on Tamagawa numbers for global fields of characteristic zero.
Observe that the case of quasi-split groups
can readily be treated using Langlands-Lai technique since the spectral decomposition is available,
thanks to Morris, in arbitrary characteristic. But inner forms are, for the time being out of reach,
via Kottwitz’ techniques, in small characteristics. We understand there is a proof by completely different
methods for positive characteristics but one would like to have a uniform approach independent of
the characteristic.

This is our main motivation for looking more carefully at a very simple special case. It turns out that
a natural substitute valid in any characteristic can be given, for
SL(2)
, using endoscopy.
This appeared, with detailed proofs, in the lecture notes
[
L
]
where local and global
aspects of the contribution of unipotent elements to harmonic analysis for
SL(2)

in arbitrary characteristics are discussed. It seems reasonable to expect that
similar techniques will provide, for arbitrary reductive groups, the substitue one is looking for.
This will be stated as a conjecture. But the state
of art, in (small with respect to the order of the Weyl group)
positive characteristic, does not allow to go much farther for the time being.

3.
Stable conjugacy

Two elements in
G(F)
(resp.
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
)
will be said to be
stably conjugate
if they are conjugate in
G(\overline{F})
(resp.
{\widetilde{G}}(\overline{F})
).
Observe that, in the literature, stable conjugacy is only defined for semisimple
elements and even for them it is slightly more subtle for non strongly regular elements in arbitrary reductive group
(see for example
[
MW
, section I.4.4]
).
But this simple definition works nicely for all elements
in groups under consideration here.

The set
C(x)
of conjugacy orbits under
G(F)
inside the set
of rational points in the geometric orbit is parametrized by

C(x)\simeq\mathrm{Coker}[H^{0}_{f}(F,G)\to H^{0}_{f}(F,I_{x}\backslash G)]\simeq\ker[H^{1}_{f}(F,I_{x})\to H^{1}_{f}(F,G)]

where the
H^{i}_{f}
are flat cohomology sets. We have to use flat topology since, in characteristic 2,
the scheme
I_{x}
has an infinitesimal factor when
x=zu
with
z\in Z(F)
and
u

a non trivial unipotent.
For all other elements one could use étale
cohomology as well since étale and flat cohomology coincide
for smooth connected groups and one may forget about the index
f
.
Since
H^{1}_{f}(F,G)
is trivial we have

C(x)\simeq H^{1}_{f}(F,I_{x})~.

Then standard cohomology results show that
C(x)
is trivial if
x
belongs to a split torus, it has two elements
if
x
is regular semisimple in a non split torus and finally

C(x)\simeq Q_{F}

when
x
has a non-trivial unipotent factor in his Jordan decomposition.
In particular it is an uncountable set when
F
is of characteristic 2.
We also observe that if
T
is a torus in
G

H^{0}(F,T\backslash G)={\widetilde{T}}(F)\backslash{\widetilde{G}}(F)~.

Lemma 3.1
.

In
G(F)
(resp.
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
) two elements are stably conjugate if and only if they are
conjugate under
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
.

Proof.

Firstly one observes that since
G(\overline{F}).{\widetilde{Z}}(\overline{F})={\widetilde{G}}(\overline{F})

conjugacy under
G(\overline{F})
and
{\widetilde{G}}(\overline{F})
coincide.
Then it remains to show that conjugacy and stable conjugacy coincide
in
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
. Observe that
{\widetilde{I}}_{x}

is smooth and connected for all
x\in{\widetilde{G}}(F)
. In fact

{\widetilde{I}}_{x}=Res_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}
for some separable quadratic extension
if
x
is regular semisimple,
{\widetilde{I}}_{x}={\widetilde{G}}
if
x
is central while

{\widetilde{I}}_{x}=\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{a}
if the Jordan decomposition of
x
has a non trivial unipotent component.
The set
{\widetilde{C}}(x)
of conjugacy orbits under
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
inside the set
of rational points in the geometric orbit is parametrized by

{\widetilde{C}}(x)\simeq\mathrm{Coker}[H^{0}_{f}(F,{\widetilde{G}})\to H^{0}_{f}(F,I_{x}\backslash{\widetilde{G}})]\simeq\ker[H^{1}_{f}(F,{\widetilde{I}}_{x})\to H^{1}_{f}(F,{\widetilde{G}})]~.

It remain to observe, that

is trivial for any

∎

4.
Endoscopic expansion

For our
G
an endoscopic character
\kappa
is an element of
\mathcal{K}

(the Pontryagin dual of
Q_{F}
)
viewed as a character of
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
thanks to the isomorphism

{\widetilde{Z}}(F)G(F)\backslash{\widetilde{G}}(F)\simeq Q_{F}=(F^{\times})^{2}\backslash F^{\times}

induced by the determinant.
This allows to define
\kappa
-orbital integrals by

\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(x,f)=\int_{Q_{F}}\kappa(q)\mathcal{O}_{G}(x^{q},f)\,dq=\int_{{\widetilde{Z}}(F)I_{x}(F)\backslash{\widetilde{G}}(F)}\kappa(\det{\widetilde{g}})f({\widetilde{g}}^{-1}x{\widetilde{g}})\,d\dot{\widetilde{g}}

where, by abuse of notation,
x^{q}={\widetilde{g}}^{-1}x{\widetilde{g}}

and
{\widetilde{g}}
is any element in
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
that maps to
q
via the determinant.
This makes sense since
\mathcal{O}_{G}(x^{q},f)
is independent of the choice of
{\widetilde{g}}
.
The compact group
Q_{F}
is endowed with the canonical
Haar measure:
\mathrm{vol}\,(Q_{F})=1
.

When
\kappa=1
integral
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{1}(x,f)
will also be denoted
\mathcal{SO}_{G}(x,f)

and is called the stable orbital integral. For
\kappa\not=1
they are called unstable.
By Fourier inversion, we have

\mathcal{O}_{G}(x,f)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(x,f)~.

The series converges since all but a finite number, depending on
f
, of
\kappa
-orbital integrals

\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(x,f)
do vanish. Let us discuss the various cases. For
z\in Z(F)
we have

f(z)=\mathcal{O}_{G}(z,f)=\mathcal{SO}_{G}(z,f)\quad\hbox{while}\quad\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(z,f)=0\quad\hbox{for all}\quad\kappa\not=1~.

Now consider a torus
T
in
G
and
t\in T(F)
regular.
Since

{\widetilde{g}}\mapsto f({\widetilde{g}}^{-1}t{\widetilde{g}})

is left-invariant under

{\widetilde{T}}(F)
, the centralizer of
t
in
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
,
the
\kappa
-orbital integral
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)
vanishes unless

\kappa
is trivial on
{\widetilde{T}}(F)
which implies

\kappa=1
or
\kappa={\varepsilon_{E/F}}
where
E/F
is the quadratic extension
defined by
T
. In such a case we have
1

1

We observe that the open inclusion

T(F)\backslash G(F)\subset{\widetilde{T}}(F)\backslash{\widetilde{G}}(F)

\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)=\int_{H^{0}(F,T\backslash G)}\kappa(\det{\widetilde{g}})f({\widetilde{g}}^{-1}t{\widetilde{g}})d\dot{\widetilde{g}}=\int_{{\widetilde{T}}(F)\backslash{\widetilde{G}}(F)}\kappa(\det{\widetilde{g}})f({\widetilde{g}}^{-1}t{\widetilde{g}})d\dot{\widetilde{g}}~.

For the group
{\widetilde{G}}
, lemma

shows that
orbital integrals are automatically stable. The same is trivially true for tori
viewed as reductive groups since conjugacy classes are singletons.

5.
Germ expansion for
p\not=2

Consider the unipotent element

{{u_{0}}}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\cr 0&1\cr\end{pmatrix}~.

The map
\nu:U(F)\to F
:

\begin{pmatrix}1&\eta\cr 0&1\cr\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\eta

induces a bijection between the set
\mathcal{U}^{G}
of rational conjugacy classes of
non trivial unipotent elements in
G(F)

and the compact group
Q_{F}
where
{u_{0}}
corresponds to
1\in Q_{F}
.
Assume now
p\not=2
; then the set
\mathcal{U}^{G}
is finite
and one has Shalika’s germ expansion when
t
tends to
z\in Z(F)
:

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\Gamma^{G}_{1}(t)f(z)+\sum_{\bm{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{G}}\Gamma^{G}_{\bm{u}}(t)\mathcal{O}_{G}(zu,f)

where we denote by
u
a representative of the conjugacy class
\bm{u}
.
By Fourier inversion we see that, if we put
<\kappa,\bm{u}>:=\kappa(\nu(\bm{u}))
, then

\mathcal{O}_{G}(z{u},f)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}<\kappa,\bm{u}>\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(z{{u_{0}}},f)~.

Shalika’s germ expansion can be rewritten

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\Gamma^{G}_{1}(t)f(z)+\sum_{\bm{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{G}}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}<\kappa,\bm{u}>\Gamma^{G}_{\bm{u}}(t)\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}({z{u_{0}}},f)~.

It is then natural to introduce
\kappa
-germs:

\Gamma^{G,\kappa}_{z{u_{0}}}(t):=\sum_{\bm{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{G}}<\kappa,\bm{u}>\Gamma^{G}_{\bm{u}}(t)~.

This yields an expansion with
\kappa
-germs

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\Gamma^{G}_{1}(t)f(z)+\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}\Gamma^{G,\kappa}_{z\bm{u_{0}}}(t)\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}({z{u_{0}}},f)~.

Combined with endoscopic transfer this yields another form of germ expansion
that will be shown to make sense in arbitrary characteristic
for
G=SL(2)
.

6.
Endoscopic transfer

We now introduce objects for local endoscopy. An endoscopic pair
\mathcal{E}
is a pair
\{H,\kappa\}

where
H
is a quasi-split connected reductive group over
F
said to be an endoscopic group and
\kappa

is an endoscopic character. For
G=SL(2)
endoscopic pairs are of the form

\{G,1\}
or
\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}
where
E/F
is a separable quadratic extension.
Let
\mathcal{E}=\{H,\kappa\}
be an endoscopic pair and
T
a torus in
G
.
Assume there is an embedding

\iota:T(F)\to H(F)~.

This is always possible if
H=G
while, if
H=T_{E/F}
, this implies that
E/F

is the quadratic extension defined by
T
. In such a case we denote by
\overline{\iota}

the compositum of
\iota
with the Galois automorphism,
so that
\iota(t)
and
\overline{\iota}(t)
can be seen as the eigenvalues of
t
.
Moreover we fix a regular element
\tau\in T(F)
.
We may now define transfer factors.

Definition 6.1
.

For
\mathcal{E}=\{G,1\}
the transfert factor is the scalar

\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)=1~.

For
\mathcal{E}=\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}
and
T
isomorphic to
T_{E/F}
the transfert factor is the scalar

\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)=c\,{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\Big(\frac{\iota(t)-\overline{\iota}(t)}{\iota(\tau)-\overline{\iota}(\tau)}\Big){|{\iota(t)-\overline{\iota}(t)}|_{E}}

where
c
is a constant.

We shall not discuss natural choices for the constant
c
since this is irrelevant for what follows.

Definition 6.2
.

Given an endoscopic pair
\mathcal{E}=\{H,\kappa\}
,
a function

f^{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(H(F))

will be called an endoscopic transfer of
f
if,
given a torus
T
in
G
together with an injection
\iota:T\to H
then, for any regular

t\in T(F)
, there is an identity of the form

\mathcal{SO}_{H}(\iota(t),f^{\mathcal{E}})=\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)~.

Theorem 6.3
.

Given
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(G(F))

an endoscopic transfer
f^{\mathcal{E}}
always exists. Moreover

f^{\mathcal{E}}(\overline{\iota}(t))=f^{\mathcal{E}}({\iota(t)})~.

Proof.

The theorem is trivial when
\mathcal{E}=\{G,1\}
. When
\mathcal{E}=\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}

a proof is given in
[
LL
, Lemma 2.1]
. It
is an explicit computation based on an argument borrowed from the proof of
[
JL
, lemma 7.3.2]
.
One should observe that
[
LL
]
assumes that the characteristic of
F
is zero,
but this assumption is seldom used
and, in particular, plays no role in the proof of
[
LL
, Lemma 2.1]
.
One can also find a detailed proof in
[
L
]
.
∎

One should notice that the transfer depends on various choices, namely:
of Haar measures, of
c
and
\tau
.

7.
Germ expansion for
SL(2)
in arbitrary characteristic

Consider first stable orbital integrals
\mathcal{SO}_{G}(t,f)
.
Let us denote by
\bm{\tilde{u}_{0}}
the orbit under
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
conjugacy of
{u_{0}}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The stable orbital integrals
\mathcal{SO}_{G}(t,f)
has a germ expansion when
t\in T(F)
is regular and tends to
z\in Z(F)
.
Namely, if
t
is regular and close enough to
z
, one has the identity

\mathcal{SO}_{G}(t,f)=\mathcal{S}\Gamma_{z}^{G}(t)f(z)+\mathcal{S}\Gamma_{{z\bm{\tilde{u}_{0}}}}^{G}(t)\mathcal{SO}_{G}({z{u_{0}}},f)

where
\mathcal{S}\Gamma^{G}_{\star}:=\Gamma^{\widetilde{G}}_{\star}
are Shalika’s germs for
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
.

Proof.

We already observe that a stable orbital integral is nothing but
an orbital integral on
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
:

\mathcal{SO}_{G}(t,f)=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{G}}(t,\widetilde{f})

where
\widetilde{f}
is any element in
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\widetilde{G}}(F))
such that
\widetilde{f}|_{G(F)}=f

since, for
t\in G(F)
, the restriction of
\widetilde{f}
to
G(F)
only matters for the computation of the orbital integral.
Assumptions of Shalika’s theorem
[
Sh
]

are fulfilled for
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
in any characteristic
and hence stable orbital integrals for
G
, viewed as orbital integrals for
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
,
do have a germ expansion indexed by stable unipotent orbits in
G(F)
, i.e.
usual unipotent orbits in
{\widetilde{G}}(F)
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
T
be a torus isomorphic via
\iota
to
T_{E/F}
and let
\mathcal{E}=\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}
.
The
\kappa
-orbital integral
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)
where
\kappa={\varepsilon_{E/F}}
,
has a germ expansion when
t\in T(F)
is regular and tends to
z\in Z(F)
. Namely,
if
t
is regular and close enough to
z
one has the identity

\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\varepsilon_{E/F}}(t,f)=\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{-1}f^{\mathcal{E}}(z)~.

Proof.

This follows from
6.3
.
∎

These two lemmas together with the Fourier expansion

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)

yield a germ expansion valid in arbitrary characteristics.
Let us make explict the various cases.
When
E
is the quadratic field attached to an elliptic torus
T
and

\mathcal{E}=\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}
then, for
t\in T(F)
regular but close enough to
z\in Z(F)
, one has

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\mathcal{S}\Gamma_{z}^{G}(t)f(z)+\mathcal{S}\Gamma_{{z\bm{\tilde{u}_{0}}}}^{G}(t)\mathcal{SO}_{G}({z{u_{0}}},f)+\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{-1}f^{\mathcal{E}}(z)~.

When
T
is a split torus and
E=F\oplus F
we have two ways for looking
at
\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)
for
t\in T(F)

since, in such a case,

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\mathcal{SO}_{G}(t,f)=\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\varepsilon_{E/F}}(t,f)~.

The split transfer yields readily an asymptotic expansion since,
for
t
close enough to
z\in Z(F)
, one has

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{-1}f^{\mathcal{E}}({z})

where

is nothing but the stable orbital integral
\mathcal{SO}_{G}(z{u_{0}},f)
.
Hence for
T
split,
t\in T(F)
regular and close enough to

one also has

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{-1}\mathcal{SO}_{G}(z{u_{0}},f)~.

We may now summarize the previous observations.
We say that
\mathcal{E}=\{H,\kappa\}
is an elliptic data if
\mathcal{E}=\{G,1\}
or
\mathcal{E}=\{T_{E/F},{\varepsilon_{E/F}}\}

where
\kappa={\varepsilon_{E/F}}
is non trivial.
We denote by
\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{ell}
the set of elliptic data. Elliptic data are in bijection with
\mathcal{K}
.
Consider a torus
T
and
t
semisimple regular in
T(F)
. Recall we have choosen
an isomorphism
\iota
if
T
is isomorphic to
T_{E/F}
. Now let

\Phi^{\mathcal{E}}(t)=\begin{cases}\Delta^{\mathcal{E}}(t)^{-1}\mathcal{SO}_{H}(\iota(t),f^{\mathcal{E}})&\quad\hbox{if there is an embedding of $T$ in $H$}\cr\quad 0&\quad\hbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

Theorem 7.3
.

When
t\in T(F)
is regular but close enough to
z\in Z(F)

the orbital integral
\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)
is a sum over elliptic endoscopic data:

[formula]

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\sum_{\mathcal{E}=\{H,\kappa\}\in\bm{\mathcal{E}}_{ell}}\Phi^{\mathcal{E}}(t)~.

The stable orbital integral
\mathcal{SO}_{H}(f^{\mathcal{E}}(\iota(t)))
has a germ expansion indexed by
the set
\mathcal{SU}^{H}
of
stable unipotent conjugacy classes
in

:

[formula]

\mathcal{SO}_{H}(f^{\mathcal{E}}(\iota(t)))=\sum_{\bm{\tilde{u}}\in\mathcal{SU}^{H}}\mathcal{S}\Gamma^{H}_{\iota(z){u}}(\iota(t))\mathcal{SO}_{H}(\iota(z){u},f^{\mathcal{E}})

where
{u}
is a representatives of
\bm{\tilde{u}}
.

Proof.

We first recall that

\mathcal{O}_{G}(t,f)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\kappa}(t,f)

and we conclude thanks to

,
7.1
and

8.
A conjecture

In this section
G
is an arbitrary connected quasi-split reductive groupe over a non archimedean local field
F
.
A naive conjecture would be that theorem

An even more serious difficulty is that the vector space

Photochemical Macrolactonization of Hydroxyaldehydes via C–H Bromination
Authors: Sakura Kodaki, Haru Ando, Hiroyoshi Takamura, Isao Kadota, Kenta Tanaka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Macrolactones are structurally important motifs that are found in a variety of natural products. While conventional approaches to their synthesis involve the use of seco acids with condensing agents or activators, methods based on hydroxyaldehydes as substrates remain relatively unexplored. Furthermore, the development of macrolactonization reactions that proceed via radical processes is still in its infancy, and to date, examples that use hydroxyaldehydes have not yet been reported. In this study, a photochemical macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes via in-situ-generated acyl bromide intermediates has been developed. Lactones with ring sizes ranging from 7–21 were successfully obtained in a good yield. The present photochemical radical macrolactonization therefore represents a promising tool for the synthesis of natural products.

Introduction

Macrolactones are critical structural motifs commonly found in a wide range of natural products. Classical methods for the synthesis of macrolactones have typically been based on seco acid substrates with condensing agents or activators (Scheme a). The macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes is also a valuable method for synthesizing macrolactones. In 2012, Lee and co-workers achieved the macrolactonization of a hydroxyaldehyde using an N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) catalyst with a stoichiometric amount of an oxidant (Scheme b). More recently, the Lee method has been further developed to enable enantioselective macrolactonization reactions, thus providing access to planar chiral macrocycles. However, since the macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes is limited to only this method, the development of new approaches is crucial for advancing the field of natural-product synthesis.

Macrolactonization of Seco Acids and Hydroxyaldehydes

Photochemical transformations involving radical processes have attracted significant attention in organic chemistry of late. However, photochemical macrolactonization via radical processes remains largely unexplored and there are, as of today, no reported examples using hydroxyaldehydes as substrates. Since macrolactonizations that proceed via radical processes represent a valuable strategy for the synthesis of natural products, the development of new types of this methodology is highly desirable. Recently, we have developed a direct photochemical oxidative esterification of aldehydes with alcohols via an in-situ-generated acyl bromide. In this context, bromotrichloromethane (BrCCl3) selectively facilitates the cleavage of the aldehyde C–H bond to generate an acyl bromide intermediate, which subsequently reacts with the alcohol to afford the corresponding ester. Since typical halogenating agents such as thionyl chloride, oxalyl chloride, and N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) readily react with alcohols, their application to macrolactonization remains challenging. In contrast, as BrCCl3 effectively promotes direct esterification without oxidizing the alcohol, we hypothesized that the photochemical C–H-bromination method could also be applied to intramolecular cyclization, specifically the macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes. To expand the utility of macrolactonization reactions, we report herein the photochemical macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes via a C–H-bromination method (Scheme ).

Results and Discussion

We initially investigated the optimization of the reaction conditions for the photochemical macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehyde 1 using BrCCl3 under purple LED irradiation (Table ). When polar solvents, such as DMF and MeCN, were used, the reaction did not proceed (Table , entries 1–2). While THF was not suitable, the reaction did proceed in hexane and toluene to give the desired product (Table , entries 3–5). Since BrCCl3 efficiently activates C–H bonds, solvents such as THF and DMF, could undergo C–H activation, thereby interfering with macrolactonization. Subsequently, changing the solvent to CH2Cl2 improved the yield of 2 to 75%. (Table , entry 6). When CBr4 and N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) were used instead of BrCCl3, the yield of the product decreased (Table , entries 7 and 8). As CCl4 was not suitable, the presence of a bromo group is essential for the reaction to proceed (Table , entry 9). When the reaction was conducted in the presence of H2O, the yield was significantly reduced, suggesting that the acyl bromide intermediate may undergo hydrolysis in the reaction condition (Table , entry 10). Furthermore, performing the reaction in air proved unsuitable (Table , entry 11). Control experiments, wherein BrCCl3 or the light source were omitted, did not proceed, and it can therefore be concluded that both elements are crucial for a successful reaction (Table , entries 12 and 13).

With the optimal conditions in hand, we screened various hydroxyaldehydes, as shown in Table . For example, 21-, 19-, 17-, and 16-membered ring macrolactones were obtained in good to high yields (3–5, respectively). In addition, a hydroxyaldehyde containing a benzene ring and an ether functional group was also converted to the corresponding macrolactone under the reaction conditions (6). Furthermore, lactones with 15- to 12-membered rings were obtained in moderate yield (7–11). Of note, a secondary alcohol could also be utilized in this reaction (10). Since these macrolactones are found in various natural products, this reaction provides an efficient synthetic route for their synthesis. , Subsequently, we investigated the synthesis of lactones with medium-sized rings and found that lactones with 11- to 7-membered rings, which are also found in various natural products, could be synthesized in moderate to high yield (12–16). Benzodioxepinone, a structurally important motif with few reported synthetic methods, was efficiently obtained in moderate yield (17). , These results demonstrate that our newly developed reaction can be applied to the synthesis of lactones over a broad range of ring sizes and uses.

To investigate the reaction mechanism, a radical scavenger was employed (Scheme ). When the reaction was conducted in the presence of TEMPO (3.0 equiv), no product was obtained. Instead, an acyl radical trapped by TEMPO (19) was detected using mass spectrometry. Thus, the reaction may proceed via an acyl-radical intermediate. Furthermore, when 1-octene was treated with BrCCl3 under light irradiation, Br and CCl3-substituted product was obtained in 75% yield, suggesting that the CCl3 radical was efficiently generated in the reaction (see Supporting Information).

Mechanistic Study Using a Radical Trapping Agent

A feasible reaction mechanism is proposed in Scheme . Homolytic cleavage of BrCCl3 under purple LED irradiation generates a trichloromethyl radical and a bromo radical. According to Scheme , the trichloromethyl radical causes the dissociation of the C–H bond of the aldehyde to form acyl radical A, which then reacts with another equivalent of BrCCl3 to produce acyl bromide B. According to the literature, , as acyl bromide was detected in the reaction of the aldehyde and the alcohol, the present reaction may also generate acyl bromide B. Finally, the hydroxyl group of the alcohol can react with the acyl bromide to furnish the desired macrolactone.

Conclusions

In summary, we have developed a photochemical macrolactonization of hydroxyaldehydes that proceeds via an in-situ-generated acyl-bromide intermediate formed under irradiation from purple LEDs. While typical halogenating agents are unsuitable for macrolactonization due to their high reactivity with alcohols, BrCCl3 effectively promotes the desired transformation. The macrolactonization can be used for the synthesis of macrolactones with 12- to 21-membered rings. The reaction can also be used to successfully synthesize a benzodioxepinone derivative and lactones with 7- to 11-membered rings. Hydroxyaldehydes substituted with a benzene ring, an ether, or a secondary alcohol are tolerated in this reaction. When the reaction is conducted in the presence of TEMPO, an acyl-radical is trapped, suggesting that the reaction may produce an acyl-radical intermediate. We believe that this photochemical macrolactonization provides an efficient route for the synthesis of natural products via a radical process.

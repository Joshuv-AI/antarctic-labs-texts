Steady-state phase transition in one-dimensional quantum contact process
Lin Shang, Shuai Geng, Xingli Li, Jiasen Jin

Abstract
We investigate the steady-state phases of the one-dimensional quantum contact process model with local dissipation. Exploiting the single-site and cluster mean-field approximations, we show the bistability of the absorbing and active phases in the system with strong interaction between neighboring sites, accompanied by the closing of the Liouvillian gap in the thermodynamic limit. Moreover we find that, near the transition point, the system may evolve first to the long-lived metastable state before reaching the eventual steady state, suggesting us to prolong the time-evolution in the numerical simulation to find the true steady state. We also present the extrapolated transition point by systematically including the correlations in the system.

We provide the evidence of the existence of metastability near the transition point through the Liouvillian spectrum analysis, this alerts us the necessity of the longer simulation time in the numerical simulation of the dynamics.

II
The Model

To model the one-dimensional quantum contact process, we consider a quantum spin-
\frac{1}{2}
chain incorporating the local dissipation at each spin.
For each spin, it can be either in the spin-up state
|\uparrow\rangle
, representing the occupied site, or the spin-down state
|\downarrow\rangle
, representing the empty site. The dissipation always incoherently flips the spins down to the
z
-direction modelling the self-destruction. In quantum contact process, the branching and coagulation work coherently. They are modelled by the following Hamiltonian (set
\hbar=1
hereinafter),

H=\Omega\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}(\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}\hat{n}_{j+1}+\hat{n}_{j}\hat{\sigma}_{j+1}^{x}),

(1)

where
\hat{\sigma}^{\alpha}_{j}
(
\alpha=x,y,z
) are the Pauli operators of the
j
th spin, and
\hat{n}_{j}=\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{j}
denotes the number operator with
\hat{\sigma}^{\pm}_{j}=(\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{x}\pm i\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{y})/2
being the raising and lowering operators, respectively. The parameter
\Omega
denotes the interaction strength and
L
is the number of spins in the chain.

Under Markovian approximation, the time-evolution of the density matrix
\rho
of the system is described by the Lindblad master equation
\dot{\rho}(t)={\cal L}[\rho]
with the following Liouvillian superoperator,

{\cal L}[\rho]=-i[\hat{H},\rho]+{\cal D}[\rho]

(2)

The first term on the right-hand side denotes the coherent evolution governed by the Hamiltonian (
1
). The state of a given site evolves coherently conditioned on the population of its nearest neighboring sites, i.e. the state coherently flips only when one of its neighboring sites is occupied. While the second term
{\cal D}[\rho]=\Gamma\sum_{j=1}^{L}{(\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{-}\rho\hat{\sigma}_{j}^{+}-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{n}_{j},\rho\})}
denotes the dissipation on each site, and the
\Gamma
is the decay rate. In the rest of this paper, we work in units of
\Gamma
.

We are interested in the steady state of the system in the thermodynamic limit, i.e. the state for
L\rightarrow\infty
in the long-time limit
\rho_{\text{ss}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}{\rho(t,L)}
. It should be note that the two limits do not commute. The interaction strength plays a crucial role in determining the property of
\rho_{\text{ss}}
. Define the averaged population of the steady-state as
\langle\bar{n}\rangle_{\text{ss}}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}{\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\text{ss}}}/L
. There exists a critical value
\Omega_{c}
, for
\Omega<\Omega_{c}
all the sites are in the spin-down state, which is referred to as the absorbing phase with
\langle\bar{n}\rangle_{\text{ss}}=0
. Oppositely, for
\Omega>\Omega_{c}
the system has the probability to evolve into a steady state with
\langle\bar{n}\rangle_{\text{ss}}\neq 0
, referred to as the active phase. As the interaction strength varies, the steady-state of the system may undergo a transition from the absorbing to the active phases. We investigate the critical value
\Omega_{c}
and whether the phase transition is continuous or discontinuous in the next sections.

III
The stable steady states under mean-field approximation

Solving Eq. (
2
) is intractable due to both the non-unitarity of the dynamics and the complexity of many-body interaction. The (single-site) mean-field approximation is a commonly used method for investigating the steady-state property of open quantum many-body system. Such approximation is based on the Gutzwiller factorization of the density matrix of the total system
\rho=\bigotimes_{j}\rho_{j}
. Within this framework, the total density matrix is factorized as a tensor product of the density matrices
\rho_{j}
.

After substituting the factorized total density matrix into Eq. (
2
), one yields the following mean-filed master equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho_{j}=-i[\hat{H}_{j}^{\text{mf}},\rho_{j}]+{\cal D}[\rho_{j}],

(3)

where
\hat{H}^{\text{mf}}_{j}=\mathfrak{z}\Omega(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle\hat{n}+\langle\hat{n}\rangle\hat{\sigma}^{x}
) is the mean-field Hamiltonian and
\mathfrak{z}=2
is the coordinate number for one dimensional system. In deriving Eq. (
3
), the density matrices of each site are assumed to be identical, that is
\langle\hat{O}_{j}\rangle=\langle\hat{O}\rangle
for any local observable on the
j
-th site.

\langle n\rangle_{\text{ss}}
as functions of

\Omega
. The blue (green) circles represent the stable (unstable) steady-state with nonzero

By virtue of Eq. (
3
), the system of mean-field Bloch equations is given as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle,

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle^{2}-2\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle+1)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle,

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle+1)-(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle+1).

(8)

The steady-state solutions
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss}}=(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle_{\text{ss}},\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle_{\text{ss}},\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle_{\text{ss}})
can be obtained by setting
d\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle/dt=0
. Considering that
|\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle|\leq 1
, one obtains three sets of physical steady-state solutions
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss},0}=(0,0,-1)
and
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss},\pm}=\left(0,\frac{1}{2\Omega},-\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{2\Omega^{2}}}\right)
. The latter solutions, indicating the active state, only exist for
\Omega/\Gamma>1/\sqrt{2}
. Moreover, the stability of
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss}}
is determined by the following Jacobian matrix of Eq. (
8
),

M=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-4\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle_{\text{ss}}-1&-4\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle_{\text{ss}}&0\\
8\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}\rangle_{\text{ss}}&-1&-8\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle_{\text{ss}}-4\Omega\\
0&4\Omega(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{z}\rangle_{\text{ss}}+1)&4\Omega\langle\hat{\sigma}^{y}\rangle_{\text{ss}}-2\\
\end{array}\right),

(9)

Denote the maximal real part of the eigenvalues of
M
by
\text{Re}(\lambda)
. The negative
\text{Re}(\lambda)
indicates that the steady-state solution
\langle\hat{\bm{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss}}
is stable, otherwise the
\langle\hat{\bm{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss}}
is unstable.

IV
Liouvillian spectrum

Although the appearance of the active steady state is revealed by the single-site mean-field approximation, actually it survives even if in the full quantum analysis. This is reflected by the closing of the Liouvillian gap associated to Eq. (
2
) in the thermodynamic limit. The real part of the Liouvillian eigenvalue
\mu_{i}
characterizes the time-scale of the decay and are non-positive. In particular the eigenstate associated to the zero eigenvalue is the steady state of the quantum master equation. While the maximum of negative real part is defined as the Liouvillian gap,
\mu_{0}=\max{[\text{Re}(\mu_{i})]}
. The close of the Liouvillian gap indicates the phase transition or a bistability of steady state.

In Fig.
2
, it is shown the Liouvillian gap as a function of
\Omega
for various length of the spin chain. The extrapolation of the Liouvillian gap of finite-size chain indicates the gap closing at
\Omega\gtrsim 5.83
, i.e. the coexistence of the active states and the absorbing state.

Liouvillian gap. The circles denote the extrapolated Liouvillian gap in the limit of
L\rightarrow\infty
. The Liouvillian gap closes at
\Omega\approx 5.83
.

V
Metastable state

Apart from the Liouvillian gap
\mu_{0}
, the structure of Liouvillian eigenvalues also reflects the dynamics of the open system, in particular the time-scale of relaxation towards the steady states. This is quite relevant to the consuming time in a practical numerical simulation. In Fig.
3
, it is shown the first few largest negative real parts of Liouvillian eigenvalues for
\Omega/\Gamma=5.8
which is located near the transition point. One can see that the first largest negative real part (blue circle) becomes more close to zero as
1/L\rightarrow 0
, as already shown by the vertical dashed line along
\Omega/\Gamma=5.8
in Fig.
2

Moreover the second largest negative real part (red circles) is constant
-0.5
and it bounds the real parts of all the rest eigenvalues. Namely, the eigenvalues with zero and the largest negative real parts are isolated from the other eigenvalues when the system size becomes larger and larger, signaling a metastability during the time-evolution

VI
The effects of short-range correlations

The existence of the metastable state, which is not observed in the (single-site) mean-field approximation, is a consequence of the inclusion of short-range correlations in the system. It reminds us that one should prolong the evolving time in the numerical simulation to escape from the metastable state.
In order to incorporate the effects of correlations in the analysis, we employ the cluster mean-field (CMF) approximation. The idea of CMF method is to factorize the density matrix of the total system as
\rho=\bigotimes_{C}{\rho_{C}}
. Here
\rho_{C}
is the density matrix of a cluster in the lattice (or chain) of the total system. Substituting the factorized density matrix in to Eq. (
2
) and tracing over the clusters other than
C
, one obtains the following CMF master equation,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\rho_{C}}=-i[\hat{H}_{\text{CMF}},\rho_{C}]+{\cal D}[\rho_{C}],

(10)

where
\hat{H}_{\text{CMF}}=\hat{H}_{C}+\hat{H}_{\partial C}(t)
is the CMF Hamiltonian, with
\partial C
being the boundary of
C
. The on-cluster Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{C}=\Omega\sum_{j\in C}{(\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j}\hat{n}_{j+1}+\hat{n}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j+1})}
describes the interactions between the sites inside the cluster
C
which is treated in a full quantum manner. While the inter-site Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\partial C}=\Omega\sum_{j\in\partial C,j^{\prime}\in\partial C^{\prime}}{(\langle\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle\hat{n}_{j}+\langle\hat{n}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j})}
with
j,j^{\prime}=1
and
L
being the nearest neighbour but belonging to two adjacent clusters, describes the interactions between cluster
C
with the neighboring cluster
C^{\prime}
via the time-dependent effective fields. Due to the translational invariance of the system, the steady-state solution of Eq. (
10
) can be determined by the self-consistent conditions
\langle\hat{O}_{j^{\prime}}(t\rightarrow\infty)\rangle=\text{tr}[\hat{O}_{j}\rho_{C}(t\rightarrow\infty)]
for
\hat{O}=\hat{n}
and
\hat{\sigma}^{x}
.

Here, in order to have a more comprehensive description on the steady-state solutions to Eq. (
10
), we implement in an alternative way. Let us reexpress the inter-site Hamiltonian as
\hat{H}_{\partial C}=\Omega\sum_{j=1,L}(F^{n}\hat{n}_{j}+F^{x}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j})
, where the
F^{n}
and
F^{x}
play the role of the effective fields induced by
C^{\prime}
and thus parameterized the steady-state density matrix as
\rho_{C,\text{ss}}(F^{n},F^{x})
. Note that the
x
-component in the
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss},0}
and
\langle\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\rangle_{\text{ss},\pm}
is always zero, only
F^{n}
appears in the
\hat{H}_{\partial C}
, because the solution with
\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle\neq 0
is physically unacceptable
Buchhold2017
.
The self-consistence condition then requires that
\langle\hat{n}_{j}\rangle_{\text{ss}}=\text{tr}[\hat{n}_{j}\rho_{C,\text{ss}}(F^{n})]
. In this way one can find all the possible self-consistent steady-state solution even if it is not stable.

In Fig.
4
(a) the steady-state population of the boundary site
\langle\hat{n}-1\rangle_{\text{ss}}
is shown for various
\Omega
in the CMF approximation with
L=7
. One can see that for
\Omega/\Gamma=4
the curve of
\langle\hat{n}\rangle_{\text{ss}}
only intersects with the straight line
F^{n}
at the origin indicating the absorbing state. While for
\Omega/\Gamma=6
apart from the origin there are two other intersections at
F^{n}=0.0725
and
0.3085
, the diamonds in Fig.
4
(a). The former (with derivative greater than unity) indicates the unstable fixed point and the latter indicates the physical steady state.

VII
Summary

In this work we investigate the non-equilibrium steady-state phases of quantum contact process in one dimension. By means of the single-site mean-field approximation, apart from the trivial absorbing state, the active state with non-zero population is shown to exist for the system with larger interaction strength. This is also reflected by the closing of the Liouvillian gap. The Liouvillian spectrum shows the isolation of the eigenmodes corresponding to the eigenvalues with the largest two real parts from others, indicating the existence of the metastability in the system near the transition point. This suggest us to prolong the evolution time in the numerical simulation of the dynamics. Moreover another branch of steady state, although is unstable, is also revealed by both the single-site and the cluster mean-field approximations with an alternative effective field scenario. The comprehensive view of the steady states illustrates that the emergence of both stable and unstable active phases at finite transition point
\Omega_{c}
, but the merging of unstable active branch and absorbing state at
\Omega\rightarrow\infty
. This features the absorbing phase transition without symmetry breaking. We obtain the transition point with a systematically increasing the correlation up to
L=13
in CMF simulation.

The steady-state phase transitions in contact process with both quantum and classical branching and coagulation in the framework of cluster mean-field approximation are intriguing future directions. In particular it is interesting to extract the critical exponent of the continuous absorbing state phase transition in such hybrid contact process. Along this line, the coherent anomaly analysis basing on the CMF results would play an important role
Suzuki1986
;
Jin2021
.

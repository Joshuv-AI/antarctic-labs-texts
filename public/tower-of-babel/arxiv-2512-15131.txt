The period–index problem for hyperkähler varieties: Lower and upper bounds
Alessio Bottini & Daniel Huybrechts

Abstract
. It is expected that a stronger form of the period-index conjecture holds for hyperkähler varieties. Following ideas of Hotchkiss, we provide further evidence for this expectation by proving a version in which the index is replaced by the Hodge-theoretic index. We also show that the hyperkähler period-index conjecture is optimal. As an application, we prove that Mumford–Tate general hyperkähler varieties cannot be covered by families of elliptic curves passing through a fixed point. By extending work of Hotchkiss, Maulik, Shen, Yin, and Zhang, we prove the hyperkähler period-index conjecture for non-special coprime Brauer class on hyperkähler varieties of K3 [ n ] {\rm K3}^{[n]} -type without any restriction on the Picard number.

1.
Introduction

Before turning to the period-index conjecture for hyperkähler varieties and outlining the results proved in this paper, we briefly review the general conjecture.

1.1.
Period-index

With any Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
on a smooth projective variety
X
one associates
two numerical invariants, its period
{\rm per}(\alpha)
and its index
{\rm ind}(\alpha)
.
The period is simply the order of
\alpha
as an element of the abelian group
{\rm Br}(X)
while
the index is the minimal
m
such that the restriction of
\alpha
to some non-empty Zariski
open subset
U\subset X
can be represented by a Brauer–Severi variety
P\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces U
of relative dimension
m-1
. According to a classical result going back to Albert
[
2
]
, one knows that
{\rm per}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{e(\alpha)}
for some integer
e(\alpha)
which a priori depends on the specific class

\alpha
.

It is generally believed that the exponent
e(\alpha)
can be chosen to only depend on
X
. This is made
more precise by the following, cf.
[
8
]
:

Conjecture 1.1

(Colliot-Thélène)
.

All Brauer classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
on a smooth projective variety
X
over an algebraically closed field
satisfy

(1.1)

{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)-1}.

The conjecture is wide open in general, but a few things are known. For example, the ‘discriminant avoidance’ result of de Jong and
Starr
[
12
]
shows that once Conjecture
1.1
is established the upper bound (
1.1
)
also holds for all ‘ramified’ Brauer classes, i.e. classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(K(X))
over the function field, cf. the recent more geometric account
[
14
]
. Also, due to joint work with Mattei
[
20
]
, it is known that a uniform upper bound exists. More precisely, there exists an integer
e(X)
only depending on
X
with
{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{e(X)}

for all ‘unramified’
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
. A similar result was established by de Jong and Perry
[
11
]
conditionally on the standard Lefschetz conjecture in degree two. At the moment,
lacking a sufficiently strong discriminant avoidance result, the existing uniform bounds do not extend to ramified classes. Famously, (
1.1
) is known for curves by Tsen’s theorem and
for surfaces by a result of de Jong, see
[
10
,
12
,
24
]
. It is also known that if true the conjecture is optimal. Examples proving optimality, especially of unramified classes, are not easy to come by. The first one is due to Gabber, see the appendix to
[
9
]
; it has recently been generalised in
[
11
,
16
]
.

The best evidence for Conjecture
1.1
and the first conceptual (geometric) explanation of the
particular
shape of the upper bound is provided by recent results of Hotchkiss
[
16
]
, see
also
[
11
]
. He defines the Hodge-theoretic index
{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)
for any unramified Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, see §​
2.6
, and shows

(1.2)

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)-1}

for all
classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
for which
{\rm per}(\alpha)
and
(\dim(X)-1)!
are coprime. Moreover,
he argues that if the integral Hodge conjecture holds for a Brauer–Severi variety
P\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces X
representing
\alpha
, then also the original (
1.1
) holds. Indeed, in this case the general

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}(\alpha)
becomes an equality. A similar strategy has been applied
by de Jong and Perry
[
11
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

In a number of recent papers
[
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
, certain results are not established for all classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)

but only for those with
{\rm per}(\alpha)
coprime to a fixed positive integer
N_{X}
. For short, we say that in this case
\alpha
is coprime to
N_{X}
. The integer
N_{X}
only depends on
X

and often only on certain numerical invariants of
X
,
e.g. in the result of Hotchkiss one chooses
N_{X}=(\dim(X)-1)!
.
The exact value of
N_{X}
is usually not important and rarely optimal. In order to avoid
having to specify
N_{X}
, we often simply say that a certain result holds for all
coprime classes

\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
.

1.2.
Period-index for hyperkähler varieties

Although the upper bound (
1.1
) is known to be optimal in general, it is conceivable that better bounds
exist for particular classes of varieties. Of course, this is only relevant if the Brauer group is not trivial altogether. One particular class of varieties with an interesting Brauer group is provided by K3 surfaces and higher-dimensional generalisations thereof, hyperkähler varieties. In this paper, a
hyperkähler variety
will always refer to a simply connected, smooth, projective, complex variety
X
with
H^{2,0}(X)
spanned by an
everywhere non-degenerate two-form. For these, the following stronger version of Conjecture
1.1
was put forward in
[
19
]
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

All Brauer classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
on a hyperkähler variety
X
satisfy

(1.3)

{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}.

The conjecture is known to hold for all hyperkähler varieties in various dense countable unions of
codimension one loci in the moduli space of hyperkähler varieties. We summarise the previously established cases:

\bullet

If
X
admits a Lagrangian fibration
X\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then (
1.3
)
holds for all coprime classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, see
[
19
, Thm. 0.3]
.

\bullet

If
X
is the Hilbert scheme
S^{[n]}
of a K3 surface
S
, then (
1.3
)
holds for all classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, see
[
19
, Thm. 0.4]
.

\bullet

If
X
is a hyperkähler variety of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type of Picard number
at least two, then (
1.3
) holds for all non-special coprime classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, see
[
17
, Thm. 0.5]
.

Hotchkiss et al
[
17
]
also establish the uniform bound
e(X)=\dim(X)
for coprime Brauer classes on arbitrary hyperkähler varieties of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type. The additional assumption of
\alpha
being non-special is a condition on the square of the
B
-field representing
\alpha
,
see Remark
3.3
.

Further examples providing evidence for Conjecture
1.3
can be obtained
by considering surface decomposable hyperkähler varieties, cf.
[
19
, §​ 3.5]
.
According to results of Voisin, see
[
34
, Thm. 3.3]
, the Fano variety of lines on a cubic fourfold, which is of
{\rm K3}^{[2]}
-type, has a surface decomposition. Hence,
(
1.3
) holds for coprime classes on Fano varieties of lines, but see Theorem
D
below for a more general result. More interestingly, also the LSV compactification of the intermediate Jacobian
fibration associated with a cubic fourfold
[
23
]
, a hyperkähler variety of OG10-type, is surface decomposable and, therefore,
(
1.3
) holds for all coprime classes. There are also examples of surface
decomposable hyperkähler varieties of OG6-type, see
[
28
]
.

In the same sense that Hotchkiss’s result
{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)-1}
for an arbitrary smooth projective variety
X
is
the strongest evidence yet for the period-index conjecture (
1.1
), we view our first theorem as convincing
evidence for the hyperkähler version (
1.3
). The proof will be given in §​
4.1
.

Theorem A
.

For all coprime classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
on a hyperkähler variety
X

one has

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}.

In addition, if
\alpha
is represented by a Brauer–Severi variety
P\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.55554pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.55554pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.55554pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces X
and the integral Hodge conjecture holds for

, then Conjecture
1.3
holds.

In fact, in §​
4.1
we will prove that all Hodge-theoretic indices defined in terms of the various Hodge structures

,

, and
{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}
naturally associated with a hyperkähler

1.3.
Optimal period-index

The next result shows that Conjecture
1.3
is optimal. More is true, we cannot
only exclude a stronger upper bound to hold for all Brauer classes, but we show in fact that it cannot even exist
when one restricts to coprime classes (with respect to any integer
N_{X}
). The next result will be proven in §​
4.4
.

Theorem B
.

Assume
X
is a general hyperkähler variety of Picard rank
\rho(X)\leq b_{2}(X)-6
. Then for any non-special coprime class

\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
one has

(1.4)

{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}\mid{\rm ind}(\alpha).

In §​
4
we will make the assumption more precise; ‘general’ is specified as ‘Mumford–Tate general’. Note that for the known deformation types both assumptions
hold away from a countable union of proper closed subsets in the moduli space.
A fairly complete picture emerges in the case of hyperkähler varieties admitting a Lagrangian fibration and hyperkähler varieties of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type, cf. Corollary
1.4
and Corollary
4.3
.

1.4.
Hodge-theoretic indices

The key to the above two theorems is the additional symmetry of the Hodge structure of
a hyperkähler variety which gives rise to refinements of Hotchkiss’s Hodge index
{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)
.
The Mukai lattice
\widetilde{H}
, its symmetric power
{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}
, and
the Verbitsky component
SH(X)\subset H^{\ast}(X)
, i.e. the sub-algebra of the cohomology generated by

H^{2}
, all lead to numerical invariants

{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)
,
{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)
,
{\rm ind}_{H^{\ast}}(\alpha)
, and
{\rm ind}_{SH}(\alpha)

of any topologically trivial Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, see §​
2
for precise definitions.
They are linked to each other as follows, see §​
2
for the verifications.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler variety of dimension
2n
. Then
{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}(\alpha)
,

\displaystyle{\rm ind}_{H^{\ast}}(\alpha)\mid\gcd\left((2n)!\cdot{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha),{\rm ind}_{SH}(\alpha)\right),

\displaystyle~\text{\rm and }~{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid\text{\rm gcd}\left(n!\cdot{\rm ind}_{SH}(\alpha),{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)^{n}\right).

For coprime Brauer classes
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
one has

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{H^{\ast}}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{SH}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)^{n}.

Moreover,
{\rm per}(\alpha)
divides all four indices.

1.5.
Prime Brauer classes on
{\rm K3}^{[n]}

Combining the arguments in
[
17
]
, which
crucially rely on results of Markman
[
26
]
, with work of O’Grady
[
29
]
, we give further evidence for the period-index conjecture by the following result.

Theorem D
.

Assume
X
is a hyperkähler variety of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type.
If
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
is a non-special coprime Brauer class, then

(1.5)

{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}.

For a special coprime Brauer class
\alpha

(
1.5
)
holds true
if its period
{\rm per}(\alpha)
has a reduced prime factor decomposition
{\rm per}(\alpha)=\prod p_{i}
,
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
.

The theorem extends
[
17
, Thm. 0.5]
from the case of Picard number at least two to all hyperkähler varieties of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type. Its proof can be found in §§​
5.3
and
5.4
.

We also note the following combination of a version of Theorem
B
,
see Remark
4.1
, and Theorem
D
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume
X
is a Mumford–Tate general hyperkähler variety of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type of Picard number one. Then

{\rm per}(\alpha)^{n}={\rm ind}(\alpha)

and
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
is a non-special coprime Brauer class.∎

1.6.
Covering families

In
[
35
, Question 1.2]
Voisin asks whether a very general hyperkähler variety is covered
by elliptic curves. The motivation comes from recent studies of birational invariants of projective varieties like covering gonality and degree of irrationality. Combining the results in
[
19
,
20
]
with Theorem
B
we show that the period-index problem affects this question, see §​
6.3
for the proof.

Theorem E
.

Assume
X
is a Mumford–Tate general hyperkähler variety and
\{{\cal C}_{t}\}
is a dominating family
of curves in
X
all passing through one fixed point
x\in X
. Then the geometric genus satisfies

g({\cal C}_{t})\geq\dim(X)/2.

In particular, general hyperkähler varieties of dimension at least four do not admit covering families
of elliptic curves all passing through one fixed point.

2.
Hodge-theoretic indices

With a hyperkähler variety
X
various Hodge structures are naturally associated. We will concentrate on the following ones (all to be recalled below):

(2.1)

H^{2\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z}),~SH(X,\mathbb{Z}),~\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\text{ and }~{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Here,
SH(X,\mathbb{Z})
is the Verbitsky component and
\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\oplus U
is the Hodge–Mukai lattice.
Using Tate twists, they will all be considered as Hodge structures of weight zero and torsion
will be ignored throughout. There are variants of the above where instead of integral cohomology
one uses topological
K
-theory. This is only really relevant for the first two.

The underlying idea to define the index of a Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
with respect to any of these Hodge structures is always the same:

\bullet
Firstly, one uses the (topologically trivial) Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
to ‘twist’ any of the four integral Hodge structures
V
in (
2.1
) to obtain a new integral Hodge structure
V_{\alpha}

such that
V
and
V_{\alpha}
are commensurable, i.e.
V\otimes\mathbb{Q}\simeq V_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
as Hodge structures.

\bullet
Secondly, for any integral Hodge structure
V
of weight zero together with a given rank function
{\rm rk}\colon V\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}
one defines
{\rm ind}(V)
as the positive generator of the image of
{\rm rk}
restricted to the subgroup of Hodge classes
V_{\rm Hdg}\coloneqq V\cap V^{0,0}\subset V
, i.e.
{\rm rk}(V_{\rm Hdg})={\rm ind}(V)\cdot\mathbb{Z}
.
The dependence of
{\rm ind}(V)
on the rank function is suppressed in the notation.

\bullet
Thirdly, if an integral Hodge structure
V
comes with a rank function, then
a natural rank function on the twisted Hodge structure
V_{\alpha}
is given via the isomorphism

V\otimes\mathbb{Q}\simeq V_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. One then defines the (a priori rational but often integral) number

{\rm ind}_{V}(\alpha)\coloneqq{\rm ind}(V_{\alpha}).

2.1.
Twisting the Hodge–Mukai lattice

We consider
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of a hyperkähler variety
X

together with its Hodge structure of weight two and the BBF-form
q
. However, the following is purely algebraic and could be set up without a hyperkähler variety in the background.

The Hodge–Mukai lattice is defined as the orthogonal sum

\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z})\coloneqq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})(1)\oplus U.

Here,
U
is the hyperbolic plane, for which we fix a basis
e,f
of isotropic vectors with
(e.f)=-1

and for
b\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
we often write
(r,b,s)
instead of
r\,e+b+s\,f
. The quadratic form on the Mukai lattice shall also be denoted
q
. The weight zero Hodge structure of
the Tate twist
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})(1)
is extended by declaring
U
to be of Hodge type
(0,0)
.
A natural rank function
{\rm rk}\colon\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z})\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}
is given by the projection
to
\mathbb{Z}e
or, alternatively, as
{\rm rk}=-q(~,f)
.

Next, to a fixed topologically trivial Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
, we associate a twist

\widetilde{H}(X,\alpha,\mathbb{Z})
of the Hodge–Mukai lattice. For this, one has to fix a
B
-field lift of
\alpha
, i.e. a class
B\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
that under the exponential map is mapped to

\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)\subset H^{2}(X,{\cal O}_{X}^{\ast})
. One then defines
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
as the Mukai lattice
with the Hodge structure determined by the following conditions:

H^{1,-1}(X,B)
is spanned by
(0,\sigma,-q(\sigma,B))=\sigma-q(\sigma,B)\,f\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{C}f
and
H^{0,0}(X,B)
is the orthogonal complement of

H^{1,-1}(X,B)\oplus H^{-1,1}(X,B)
. Here,
\sigma\in H^{2,0}(X)
is an arbitrary generator. It is known that the isomorphism type
of
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
only depends on
\alpha
, see
[
21
]
for more details and motivation.

As the lattice underlying
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
is just the untwisted
\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{Z})
,
we can keep the rank function

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 17.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern-3.0pt\lower 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 17.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})/{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is contained in
T^{\prime}(X)\coloneqq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})/{\rm NS}(X)\subset T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. If

is as above,
then clearly
rB
has this property and, therefore,
{\rm per}(\alpha)\mid r
.

The very last assertion is a consequence of Lemma

.
∎

3.2.
Period versus index for the Hodge–Mukai lattice

Consider the finite index inclusion

{\rm NS}(X)\oplus T(X)\subset H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

For any given topologically trivial Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
one can choose
a
B
-field lift
B=(1/\ell)\,b\in T(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with
b\in T(X)
primitive and
\ell
a positive integer.
Then the period of
\alpha
divides
\ell
and in many cases, namely in the non-special case, equals it, cf. Remark
3.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
X
is a hyperkähler variety.
Then for any
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
we have

{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)={\rm per}(\alpha).

Proof.

Clearly,
{\rm per}(\alpha)\mid\ell
, but in general this is not an equality. Indeed, the period
has the property that for
\ell^{\prime}\coloneqq\ell/{\rm per}(\alpha)
the class
(1/\ell^{\prime})\,b\in T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}=T^{\prime}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}

is contained in
T^{\prime}(X)\subset T(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where the notation is as above. In other words, there exists
a class
g\in{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
a\coloneqq(1/\ell^{\prime})\,b+g\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Then
({\rm per}(\alpha),-a,0)\in\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
is a Hodge class. Hence,
{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)
and the
other direction was already proved in the previous lemma.
∎

Note that the lemma is consistent with the spirit of the result of de Jong
[
10
]
for surfaces,
which in this light can be seen as a twisted Lefschetz
(1,1)
theorem, cf. Proposition
2.1
.

Remark 3.3
.

Recall that a topologically trivial class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
is called
non-special
if it is associated with a transcendental
B
-field
B=(1/\ell)\,b

with
\gcd(\ell,q(b))=1
, see
[
17
]
. Note that the condition is independent of the choice of the transcendental
B
-field.

Furthermore, in this case,
{\rm per}(\alpha)=\ell
. Indeed, with the notation as in the last proof, we have
(1/\ell^{\prime})\,q(b)=q(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}

and, therefore,
\ell^{\prime}\mid q(b)
. For a non-special
\alpha
, this immediately gives
\ell^{\prime}=1
, i.e.
{\rm per}(\alpha)=\ell
.

3.3.
Symmetric products

We come back to the divisibility
{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)^{n}
,
see Remark
2.2
. This is not an equality in general, but we will
provide many examples when it is. (The main reason for its failure is the fact that the index of the inclusion (
3.1
) below heavily depends on
{\rm per}(\alpha)
.)

In the following,
N(X,B)
denotes the space of all integral Hodge classes in
\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
and the transcendental lattice
T(X,B)
is defined as its orthogonal complement. Then the natural inclusion

(3.1)

N(X,B)\oplus T(X,B)\subset\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})

is of finite index.

The symmetric product
{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
is again a
Hodge structure of weight zero, cf. Section
2.2
, and its lattice of Hodge classes contains
{S}^{n}N(X,B)\subset{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
. However, further Hodge class may exist. For example, for
j
even the quadratic form
q_{T}
leads to a Hodge class in
{S}^{j}T(X,B)_{\rm Hdg}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
, which we will
denote by
q^{j/2}_{T}
. This class has the property that
q^{(j/2)}(\gamma^{(j)},q^{j/2}_{T})=q(\gamma)^{j/2}
for the symmetric power
\gamma^{(j)}\in{S}^{n}T(X,B)
of any class
\gamma\in T(X,B)
.

We introduce the following shorthands:
N\coloneqq N(X,B)
,
T\coloneqq T(X,B)
, and
\widetilde{H}\coloneqq\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})
. Then the space of all rational Hodge classes in
H_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes n}
decomposes as

(3.2)

\bigoplus_{j}(N^{\otimes n-j}\otimes\mathbb{Q})\otimes(T^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\rm Hdg}\simeq(H^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\rm Hdg}.

This leads to the finite index inclusion on the level of the lattices:

\bigoplus_{j}N^{\otimes n-j}\otimes T^{\otimes j}_{\rm Hdg}\subset H^{\otimes n}_{\rm Hdg}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume the transcendental lattice
T(X)
is of rank at least six and

(3.3)

{S}^{j}T(X)_{\rm Hdg}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\begin{cases}0&\text{\rm if }j\text{ \rm odd}\\
q_{T}^{j/2}\mathbb{Q}&\text{\rm if }j\text{ \rm is even.}\end{cases}

Then for any non-special coprime Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
one has

{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)={\rm per}(\alpha)^{n}.

Proof.

Consider a
B
-field lift
B=(1/\ell)\,b\in T(X)
of
\alpha
, so
b\in T(X)
and
\gcd(\ell,q(b))=1
. Then
\ell={\rm per}(\alpha)
, see Remark
3.3
.

First observe that
\tilde{e}\coloneqq(\ell,-b,0)
is contained in
N(X,B)
, as it is orthogonal to the generator
(0,\sigma,-q(\sigma,b/\ell))\in T(X,B)^{2,0}
. Hence, the symmetric power
\tilde{e}^{(n)}=\ell^{n}e^{(n)}+\ell^{(n-1)}\cdot b+\cdots+b^{(n)}
is contained in
{S}^{n}N(X,B)\subset{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})_{\rm Hdg}
and, therefore,
{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid\ell^{n}
for all
\ell
(without any coprimality assumption).

We now have to prove that conversely
\ell^{n}\mid{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)
for all
\ell

coprime to a certain
N_{X}
. To this end, assume that
v\in{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})_{\rm Hdg}
and write

v=\mu\,\tilde{e}^{(n)}+v^{\prime}
with
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}
and
{\rm rk}(v^{\prime})=0
. Then
\mu\,\ell^{n}={\rm rk}(v)
. It suffices to show that
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To illustrate the idea let us first discuss the easier case that
v\in{S}^{n}N(X,B)
, in which case we set
N_{X}=q(b)
. Then

(3.4)

q^{(n)}(b^{(n)},v)=q^{(n)}(b^{(n)},\mu\,\tilde{e}^{(n)})=\mu\,q(b)^{n}

and, therefore,
\mu\,q(b)^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The first equality follows from the decomposition, cf. the arguments in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,

(3.5)

N(X,B)=\mathbb{Z}\tilde{e}\oplus{\rm NS}(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}(0,0,1).

and the fact that the transcendental class
b
is orthogonal to
{\rm NS}(X)
.
On the other hand,
\mu\,\ell^{n}={\rm rk}(v)\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(\ell,q(b))=1
if
\ell
is coprime to
N_{X}=q(b)
. Hence,
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
, which implies

\ell^{n}\mid{\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)
.

In the general situation, the above argument breaks down in (
3.4
),
for
v
might now have a transcendental part, which eventually forces us to increase
N_{X}
.
So we decompose the given Hodge class
v
with respect to (
3.2
) as

v=\sum u_{n-i}\otimes w_{i}.

Note that the classes
u_{n-i}\in N^{\otimes n-i}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
w_{i}\in(T^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\rm Hdg}
, are a priori only rational(!) Hodge classes and that
{\rm rk}(u_{n})={\rm rk}(v)=\mu\,\ell^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Also note that by assumption (
3.3
),
w_{i}=0
for
i
odd and
w_{i}=\lambda_{i}\,q_{T}^{i/2}
for
i
even and some scalar
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Hence, normalising the
u_{n-i}
, we can write

v=\sum^{m}_{j=0}(u_{n-2j}\otimes q^{j}_{T}),

where
m
is determined by
n=2m+1
resp.
n=2m
.

We claim that there exists a class
w\in{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}(X,B,\mathbb{Z})

with the following property

(3.6)

q^{(n)}(w,v)=\varepsilon\,q^{(n)}\!\left(b^{(n)},u_{n}\right).

Here,
\varepsilon
is any non-zero integer, which will be fixed below.
This would immediately lead to

\varepsilon\,\mu\,q(b)^{n}=\varepsilon\,q^{(n)}\!\left(b^{(n)},u_{n}\right)=q^{(n)}\!\left(w,v\right)\in\mathbb{Z}.

If now
\ell
is assumed to be coprime to
N_{X}\coloneqq 2\varepsilon\,{\rm disc}(T(X))\,q(b)
, then
\mu\,\ell^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varepsilon\,\mu\,q(b)^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
together imply

\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
.

It remains to construct
w
. For this we first pick
\eta\in T(X)\cap b^{\perp}\subset T(X,B)
with
0<q(\eta)

and set
\varepsilon=q(\eta)^{m}
. Below in Lemma
3.5
we show that
under the assumption on the rank of
T(X)
we can choose
\eta
with
q(\eta)\mid 8q(b)^{2}\,{\rm disc}(T(X))^{2}
.
Then we define

(3.7)

w\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{2k}\,q(\eta)^{m-k}\,q(b)^{k}\,(b^{(n-2k)}\otimes\eta^{(2k)}).

Setting
\gamma\coloneqq\sqrt{q(\eta)}\,b+i\sqrt{q(b)}\,\eta\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\subset\widetilde{H}(X,\mathbb{C})
, we have that
w=\operatorname{Re}(\gamma^{(n)})
if
n
is even, and
w=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q(\eta)}}\operatorname{Re}(\gamma^{(n)})
if
n
is odd.
The verification of (
3.6
) is straightforward.
First, recall the following formula, which holds for every
u\in{S}^{k}\widetilde{H},\ v\in{S}^{n-k}\widetilde{H}

(3.8)

q^{(n)}\left(\gamma^{(n)},u\otimes v\right)=\binom{n}{k}\,q^{(k)}\left(\gamma^{(k)},u\right)q^{(n-k)}\left(\gamma^{(n-k)},v\right).

Therefore,

q^{(n)}\left(\gamma^{(n)},u_{n-2i}\otimes q_{T}^{i}\right)=\binom{n}{2i}\,q^{(n-2i)}\left(\gamma^{(n-2i)},u_{n-2i}\right)q^{(2i)}\left(\gamma^{(2i)},q_{T}^{i}\right)

which vanishes for
i>0
because
q(\gamma)=0
.
It follows that

q^{(n)}\left(w,v\right)=q^{(n)}\left(w,u_{n}\right)=q^{(n)}\left(q(\eta)^{m}b^{(n)},u_{n}\right)

where the first equality is obtained by taking the real part from the above, and for the second equality we used that
\eta\in T(X)\cap b^{\perp}
.

For the last assertion just observe that
{\rm per}(\alpha)\mid\ell
if
\alpha
has a
B
-field lift of
the form
(1/\ell)\,b
with
b
an integral class.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
T(X)
is of rank at least six, then for any

with
q(b)\neq 0
the orthogonal

By Meyer’s theorem the indefinite lattice

and consider the class

, where

. Then

.

Then use that

. For the first divisibility
use that

and also

.
For the second use the formula

.

4.
Upper and lower bounds

In this section we prove Theorems
A
and
B
.

The upper bound of the Hodge theoretic index provided by Theorem
A
is
an almost immediate consequence of the previous sections, see §​
4.1
.
We also comment on further Hodge theoretic obstructions which we currently do not control, see §​
4.2
.

The interest in the lower bound in Theorem
B
is motivated by the following question: In the theory of hyperkähler varieties, the second cohomology
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
with its K3 Hodge structure
and the quadratic form
q_{X}
replaces
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
of a (K3) surface and, indeed, in many respects
hyperkähler varieties often behave more like surfaces than higher-dimensional Kähler manifolds. In this spirit one could wonder whether possibly the period-index conjecture advocated for in
[
19
]
,
see Conjecture
1.3
, is not optimal. Maybe one should rather expect
{\rm per}(\alpha)={\rm ind}(\alpha)
as for surfaces? Theorem
B
shows that this is not the case.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
A

As before,
n
is fixed such that
\dim(X)=2n
. Then, according to Proposition
2.5
, we already know

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{H^{\ast}}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{SH}(\alpha)={\rm ind}_{{S}^{n}\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)\mid{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)^{n}

for all coprime classes
\alpha
. Combined with Lemma
3.2
,
which shows
{\rm ind}_{\widetilde{H}}(\alpha)={\rm per}(\alpha)
, this proves the claimed

{\rm ind}_{H}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}
.

We conclude the proof by applying Proposition
2.1
.∎

4.2.
Other Hodge theoretic obstructions

Theorem
A
says that there is no a priori Hodge theoretic obstruction
to Conjecture
1.3
. But this is not quite the full picture. Adapting an approach first discussed by de Jong and Perry
[
11
]
from the case of general projective varieties to
the hyperkähler situation, there are (at least) two other potential Hodge theoretic obstructions:
Assume
\pi\colon P\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces X
represents a topologically trivial Brauer class
\alpha\in{\rm Br}(X)
such that its index satisfies
{\rm ind}(\alpha)\mid{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}
. This gives rise to two integral Hodge classes:

(i)

There exists a closed subvariety

which is generically finite over
X
of degree
{\rm per}(\alpha)^{\dim(X)/2}
.
We denote its cycle class by
\delta\in H^{r,r}(P,\mathbb{Z})

is the relative

(ii)

Geometrically, passing from (i) to (ii) is realised by taking fibrewise the linear span of the fibre

for the passage to

In the general situation, so with the exponent

. In contrast, the existence of the class

, cf.
[

and

In the hyperkähler situation, the existence of both classes,

(i) According to

For

4.3.
Mumford–Tate general hyperkähler varieties

The Mumford–Tate group of

5.1.
Setup and strategy

The basic idea to prove results about (twisted) bundles over a hyperkähler variety

of
{\rm K3}^{[n]}
-type is to first consider
the problem for a hyperkähler variety that is actually isomorphic to a Hilbert scheme

In this case, one can often use the classical K3 surface theory to produce sufficiently many
(twisted) bundles. Then, as a second step, one tries to establish the same result for the original

one usually needs the (twisted) bundles on

to be (projectively) hyperholomorphic.

There are various construction methods on Hilbert schemes available. The one used by Hotchkiss et al
[
17
]
is based on work of Markman
[
26
]
, see also
[
27
]
,
and exploits semi-rigid bundles on K3 surfaces. For the majority of cases, however,
we appeal to results of O’Grady
[
29
]
which focus on rigid bundles
on K3 surfaces. (Alternatively, one could try to use twisted
bundles obtained by means of Lagrangian fibration as in
[
19
, §​ 2]

will be approached via
[

,
26
]
, see §​

, while dealing
with non-special Brauer classes of prime period requires the existence results proved in
[

]
, see §​

5.2.
Lattice theory

We start by proving a technical result in lattice theory, which
will be at the heart of the argument in both cases, special and non-special Brauer classes.

As we are only dealing with Brauer classes

of prime power period, say

not divisible by

The lattice theory for both cases is similar and covered by the following technical result. The difference between special and non-special is reflected by the choice of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

Now choose a class

Assume first that

Let now

As

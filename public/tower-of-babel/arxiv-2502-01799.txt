Strictly equivalent a posteriori error estimators for quasi-optimal nonconforming methods
Christian Kreuzer, Matthias Rott, Andreas Veeser, Pietro Zanotti

Abstract
. We devise a posteriori error estimators for quasi-optimal nonconforming finite element methods approximating symmetric elliptic problems of second and fourth order. These estimators are defined for all source terms that are admissible to the underlying weak formulations. More importantly, they are equivalent to the error in a strict sense. In particular, their data oscillation part is bounded by the error and, furthermore, can be designed to be bounded by classical data oscillations. The estimators are computable, except for the data oscillation part. Since even the computation of some bound of the oscillation part is not possible in general, we advocate to handle it on a case-by-case basis. We illustrate the practical use of two estimators obtained for the Crouzeix-Raviart method applied to the Poisson problem with a source term that is not a function and its singular part with respect to the Lebesgues measure is not aligned with the mesh.

1.
Introduction

Nonconforming finite element methods are a well-established technique for the approximate solution of partial differential equations (PDEs). Classical nonconforming elements, like the ones of Morley
[
Mor68
]
and Crouzeix-Raviart
[
CR73
]
, were originally proposed as valuable alternatives to conforming ones for the biharmonic and the Stokes equations. Later on, other nonconforming techniques like discontinuous Galerkin and
C^{0}
interior penalty
methods have proved to be competitive for a wide range of problems.

Nonconforming methods weaken the coupling between elements so that at least some discrete functions are not admissible to the weak formulation of the PDE of interest. On the one hand, this increases flexibility for approximation and accommodating desired structural properties, but on the other hand it complicates the theoretical analysis and, without suitable measures, excludes data that is admissible in the weak PDE.

An a posteriori error analysis aims at devising a quantity, called estimator, that, ideally, is computable and equivalent to the error of the approximate solution. Such an estimator can be used to asses the quality of the approximate solution and, if it splits into local contributions, to guide adaptive mesh refinement.
To outline the structure of most available results, consider a weak formulation of the form

u\in V\text{ such that }\forall v\in V\;a(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle,

where the bilinear form
a
is symmetric and
V
-coercive, and apply some nonconforming method based upon a discrete space
V_{h}\not\subset V
. Then the error
\|u-u_{h}\|
and the estimator
\Est_{h}
are related by the following equivalence, cf.
[
DDP95
,
KP03
,
Car05
,
Voh07
,
AR08
,
Bre15
,
CN22
,
CGN24
]
:

(1.1)

\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2}\eqsim\Est_{h}^{2}=\Ncf_{h}^{2}+\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2},

where
\Ncf_{h}
is an approximation of the distance of
u_{h}
to
V
,
\eta_{h}
is a PDE-specific part of the estimator depending on
u_{h}
, and
\Osc_{h}
is the so-called oscillation, typically involving only data. Notice the presence of
\Osc_{h}
on both sides, which spoils the ideal equivalence of error and estimator. This flaw is due to the fact that in general the error cannot bound the oscillation
\Osc_{h}
on a given mesh and this may even persist under adaptive refinement; cf. Remark
3.17
below. Often and related,
\eta_{h}
cannot be bounded by the error alone.

In contrast to (
1.1
), this article establishes the strict equivalence

(1.2)

\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}\eqsim\Est_{h}^{2}=\Ncf_{h}^{2}+\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2},

where
\Osc_{h}
and often also
\eta_{h}
differ from their counterparts in (
1.1
) and are defined for all
f\in V^{\prime}
. Moreover, the oscillation
\Osc_{h}
in (
1.2
) can be designed to be bounded by the one in (
1.1
). Observe that now the error dominates both oscillation
\Osc_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
. We achieve this improvement by generalizing the new approach of
[
KV21
]
, used also in the recent survey
[
BCNV24
]
, to nonconforming methods. To shed some light on the proof, let us outline our approach.

We start by identifying a residual
\Res_{h}
and, similarly to existing approaches, obtain

\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}=\|\Res_{h}\|_{(V+V_{h})^{\prime}}^{2}=\inf_{v\in V}\|u_{h}-v\|^{2}+\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}

with the conforming part
\Res_{h}^{\texttt{C}}:=\Res_{h}{}_{|V}
of the residual. Note that the infinite dimension of
V
obstructs the computation of both terms on the right-hand side. For the first term, averaging operators allow deriving indicators
\Ncf_{h}
that are strictly equivalent, computable, and readily split into local contributions.

The second term with the conforming residual
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}
is more delicate in view of the dual norm and the fact that data, say only the source
f
, is typically taken from an infinite-dimensional space. Considering just for a moment a simplifying special case, we address these two issues by the following two steps:

(1.3)

\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}\eqsim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\eqsim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2},

where
\mathcal{V}
are the vertices of the underlying mesh,
V_{z}
are local counterparts of
V
, and
\mathcal{P}_{z}
are local projections onto finite-dimensional counterparts
D_{z}
of
V_{z}^{\prime}
.

The general version of the first step in (
1.3
) is given in Lemma
3.7
. It hinges on (near) orthogonality properties of the conforming residual
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}
, which originate in the nonconforming discrete test functions. If the nonconforming method is quasi-optimal in the sense of

(1.4)

\|u_{h}-u\|\leq C_{\mathtt{qo}}\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|u-v_{h}\|,

they are verified in Lemma
3.6
, else they have to be measured by an additional indicator in the spirit of Remark
4.14
.

The second step in (
1.3
) further decomposes the residual, adapting the ideas in
[
KV21
]
to new features arising from nonconformity. Thanks to
\dim D_{z}<\infty
, classical techniques allow deriving strictly equivalent and computable indicators
\eta_{h}
for the sum with the approximate residuals
\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}
, while the computability issue due to infinite dimensional data is isolated in the second sum, which coincides with
\Osc_{h}^{2}
in (
1.2
). The latter decomposition is also useful for adaptivity; see Remark
3.19
below.
It is also the crucial step for the difference between the two equivalences (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). For the latter, the projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}
have to be stable in a
V^{\prime}
-like manner, while classical techniques (implicitly) use projections that are stable only in proper subspaces; see Remark
3.17
.

We advocate to first apply the outlined approach and then address the computability of the oscillation
\Osc_{h}
on a case-by-case basis, exploiting all the available information on the structure and regularity of the given data. We postpone a more detailed discussion to the Remarks
3.15
,
3.16
, and
3.18
and only mention here that an a posteriori analysis based upon a ‘more computable’ oscillation may lead to indicators
\eta_{h}
that cannot be bounded by the error alone, as in the case of several classical techniques.

To illustrate the generality of the outlined approach, we apply it to various quasi-optimal nonconforming methods covering arbitrary approximation order as well as second- and fourth-order problems. The approach also allows for estimator simplifications thanks to nonconformity as in
[
DDP95
,
DDPV96
]
. We restrict ourselves to equations with data-free symmetric principal terms in order to avoid the technical complications which are addressed in
[
BCNV24
, Section 4]
in the case of conforming methods. Furthermore, for the sake of the simplicity, we do not cover estimators based on flux equilibration; the related changes are however illustrated for conforming methods in
[
KV21
, Section 4.4]
(lowest-order case) and in
[
BCNV24
, Section 4.9]
(higher-order cases).

2.
Quasi-optimal nonconforming methods

In this section we summarize the framework for quasi-optimal nonconforming methods introduced in
[
VZ18a
]
. We report only the notions and results that are useful to our subsequent developments.

Let
V
be a Hilbert space with scalar product
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{V}:=\sqrt{a(\cdot,\cdot)}
. As in
[
VZ18a
]
, we focus on PDEs whose weak formulation reads

(2.1)

\text{for $f\in V^{\prime}$, find $u\in V$ such that }\forall v\in V\quad a(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle.

Thanks to the Riesz representation theorem, the operator
A:V\to V^{\prime}
induced by
a
and defined
as
Av:=a(v,\cdot)
is an isometry. Hence, for any source term
f\in V^{\prime}
, there exists a unique solution
u=A^{-1}f
of (
2.1
) with

\|u\|=\|f\|_{V^{\prime}}
.

Let
V_{h}
be a finite-dimensional, linear space, which is possibly
nonconforming
, i.e. not necessarily a subspace of
V
. We assume
that
a
extends to a scalar product
\widetilde{a}
on the space
\widetilde{V}:=V+V_{h}
. The norm induced by
\widetilde{a}
extends the one induced by
a
, therefore we still denote it by
\|\cdot\|
. Consequently, the operator
\widetilde{A}:\widetilde{V}\to\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
extending
A
via
\widetilde{A}\tilde{v}=\widetilde{a}(\tilde{v},\cdot)
is an isometry as well. Writing
\Pi_{V}:\widetilde{V}\to V
for the
\widetilde{a}
-orthogonal projection onto
V
, the
distance to conformity
of an element
\widetilde{v}\in\widetilde{V}
is given by the distance to its projection

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{v}-\Pi_{V}\widetilde{v}\|=\inf_{v\in V}\|\widetilde{v}-v\|.

To discretize (
2.1
), we need a nondegenerate bilinear form
a_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and a functional
f_{h}\in V_{h}^{\prime}
, which approximate their counterparts
a
and
f
in a sense to be specified. According to
[
VZ18a
, Theorem 4.7]
, a necessary condition for the quasi-optimality (
1.4
) is
full stability
, i.e. the mapping
V^{\prime}\ni f\mapsto f_{h}\in V_{h}^{\prime}
induced by the discretization of the source term must be bounded. This, in turn, implies that we necessarily have
f_{h}=\mathcal{E}^{*}_{h}f
, where
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{h}:V^{\prime}\to V_{h}^{\prime}
is the adjoint of some linear
smoothing
operator
\mathcal{E}_{h}:V_{h}\to V
. Therefore, we restrict our attention to nonconforming discretizations of (
2.1
) which read as follows:

(2.3)

\text{for $f\in V^{\prime}$},\text{find $u_{h}\in V_{h}$ such that}~\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\langle f,\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle.

Since
a_{h}
is nondegenerate and
\dim V_{h}<\infty
, the operator
A_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}^{\prime}
, defined by
A_{h}(v_{h}):=a_{h}(v_{h},\cdot)
, is invertible. Then, for any source term
f\in V^{\prime}
, there exists a unique solution of the discrete problem (
2.3
) with
u_{h}=A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{*}_{h}f=:M_{h}f
. Henceforth, we assume that the evaluations of
a_{h}
and
f
in the discrete problem are computationally realizable, so that
u_{h}
is computable (in exact arithmetic). This entails that the evaluations of
f
for all test functions in
\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h})
are known. We call the linear operator
M_{h}:V^{\prime}\to V_{h}
the
nonconforming method
, i.e. the mapping of the source term
f
to the approximation
u_{h}
of
u
, whereas
P_{h}:V\to V_{h}
defined as

(2.4)

P_{h}:=M_{h}A=A_{h}^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{h}^{*}A

is the associated approximation operator, i.e. the mapping of
u
to
u_{h}
. Figure
2.1
summarizes the functional framework.

The definitions (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) of exact and approximate solution directly lead to the
generalized Galerkin orthogonality

(2.5)

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=a(u,\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}).

Note the presence of the smoothing operator
\mathcal{E}_{h}
and the related possible difference between the test functions in the bilinear forms
a
and
a_{h}
.

In view of
[
VZ18a
, Theorem 4.14]
, the compatibility condition

(2.6)

\forall u\in V\cap V_{h},~v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad a_{h}(u,v_{h})=a(u,\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}),

called
full algebraic consistency
, is necessary and sufficient for the quasi-optimality of a fully stable nonconforming method. More precisely, the following result holds true.

Proposition 2.1

(Quasi-optimality)
.

For the fully stable method
M_{h}
, induced by the discretization (
2.3
), the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

M_{h}
is quasi-optimal, i.e. (
1.4
) is verified for some constant
C_{\mathtt{qo}}\geq 1
.

(2)

M_{h}
is fully consistent, i.e. (
2.6
) is verified.

(3)

There is a constant
\delta_{h}\geq 0
verifying

\forall w_{h}\in V_{h}\quad\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h})-a(\Pi_{V}w_{h},\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h})}{\|A_{h}^{*}v_{h}\|_{V_{h}^{\prime}}}\leq\delta_{h}\|w_{h}-\Pi_{V}w_{h}\|.

Moreover, if (
1
)-(
3
) are verified, then the constant
C_{\mathtt{qo}}
from (
1.4
) is such that

\max\{C_{\textsf{stab}},\delta_{h}\}\leq C_{\mathtt{qo}}\leq\sqrt{C_{\textsf{stab}}^{2}+\delta_{h}^{2}}

with

C_{\textsf{stab}}:=\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\dfrac{\|\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\|}{\|A_{h}^{*}v_{h}\|_{V_{h}^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

See
[
VZ18a
, Theorem 4.14]
and, for the gap between the barriers for
C_{\mathtt{qo}}
,
[
VZ18a
, Remark 3.5]
. ∎

We refer to the constants
\delta_{h}
and
C_{\textsf{stab}}
in Proposition
2.1
as the
consistency measure
and the
stability constant
of the method
M_{h}
, respectively.

Remark 2.2

(Overconsistency)
.

Quasi-optimal methods satisfying
\delta_{h}=0
are called
overconsistent
in
[
VZ19
]
, hinting at the fact that this condition does not need to be satisfied for consistent quasi-optimality (or convergence for a suitable sequence of discrete spaces
V_{h}
). Such methods are closer to conforming methods in the following sense. First, their quasi-optimality constant equals the stability constant,
C_{\mathtt{qo}}=C_{\textsf{stab}}
. Second, the error function is
\widetilde{a}
-orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h})
because the generalized Galerkin orthogonality (
2.5
) can be rewritten as

(2.7)

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad\widetilde{a}(u-u_{h},\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h})=0.

3.
Abstract a posteriori analysis

The goal of an a posteriori analysis is to devise a quantity, called
error estimator
, that is equivalent to the typically unknown error, that splits into local contributions, so-called
indicators
, and that is computable. Such an estimator allows assessing the quality of the current approximate solution and its indicators may be used as input for adaptive mesh refinement.

This section proposes a framework for the a posteriori analysis of
quasi-optimal nonconforming methods. Conceptually speaking, it
proceeds in three steps. The first step determines the

residual
, along with a convenient decomposition, and relates it
to the error by means of a dual norm. This complements the previous
frameworks used in
[
KP03
,
Voh07
]
and is an
alternative to
[
Car05
]
.
The second step provides a

localization
, i.e. it splits the global residual norm into
local contributions. Finally, the third step addresses computability,
generalizing the approach
[
KV21
]
to nonconforming
methods.

3.1.
The residual of nonconforming errors

We start by looking for a quantity that is equivalent to the error and given in terms of available information, viz. problem data
f
and approximate solution
u_{h}
in the setting of Section
2
.

In the special case of a conforming Galerkin discretization, i.e. for
V_{h}\subseteq V
,
a_{h}=a_{|V_{h}\times V_{h}}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathrm{id}_{V}
in (
2.3
), the residual
f-Au_{h}\in V^{\prime}
satisfies

(3.1)

\|u-u_{h}\|=\sup_{v\in V,\|v\|\leq 1}a(u-u_{h},v)=\|f-Au_{h}\|_{V^{\prime}}

thanks to (
2.1
) and the fact that
A
is a linear isometry.

In the general case, the possibility of
u_{h}\not\in V
and the identity

(3.2)

\|u-u_{h}\|=\sup_{\tilde{v}\in\widetilde{V},\|\tilde{v}\|\leq 1}\widetilde{a}(u-u_{h},\tilde{v})

thus suggest to suitably extend the residual
f-Au_{h}\in V^{\prime}
in (
3.1
) to a functional in
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
allowing for
u_{h}\not\in V
. While
\widetilde{A}u_{h}\in\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
is such an extension for the term
Au_{h}\in V^{\prime}
, the extension for
f\in V^{\prime}
is less obvious. Mimicking the role of (
2.1
) in (
3.1
) for the given nonconforming situation, we observe

(3.3)

\forall\tilde{v}\in\widetilde{V}\quad\widetilde{a}(u,\tilde{v})=\widetilde{a}(u,\Pi_{V}\tilde{v})=\langle f,\Pi_{V}\tilde{v}\rangle.

Note that these conditions are meaningful for
u\in\widetilde{V}
and the original condition
u\in V
is encoded in the new conditions

\forall v^{\perp}\in V^{\perp}\;\;\widetilde{a}(u,v^{\perp})=0

where
V^{\perp}
denotes the
\widetilde{a}
-orthogonal complement of
V
in
\widetilde{V}
. These observations suggest to take
\langle f,\Pi_{V}\cdot\rangle\in\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
as extension for
f\in V^{\prime}
and motivate the following residual notions: given the solution
u_{h}
of the discrete problem (
2.3
), we define its residual as

(3.4)

\Res_{h}:=\Res_{h}(f,u_{h}):=\langle f,\Pi_{V}\cdot\rangle-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\in\widetilde{V}^{\prime},

which can be decomposed into a conforming and nonconforming component by

(3.5a)

\displaystyle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}

\displaystyle:=\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}(f,u_{h}):=\Res_{h}{}_{|V}=f-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\in V^{\prime},

(3.5b)

\displaystyle\Res_{h}^{\texttt{NC}}

\displaystyle:=\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}(u_{h}):=\Res_{h}{}_{|V^{\perp}}=-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\in\big(V^{\perp}\big)^{\prime}.

Thus, we have
\Res_{h}=\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},\Pi_{V}\cdot\rangle+\langle\Res_{h}^{\texttt{NC}},(\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}}-\Pi_{V})(\cdot)\rangle
and, upon identifying a component with its respective term in this relationship, it may be viewed as an element of
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
.

The following lemma states a well-known orthogonal decomposition of the error, which is usually formulated not completely in terms of residuals.

Lemma 3.1

(Error, residual and their decompositions)
.

For the respective solutions
u
and
u_{h}
of (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}=\|\Res_{h}\|_{\widetilde{V}^{\prime}}^{2}=\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}+\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}\|_{(V^{\perp})^{\prime}}^{2},

\displaystyle\|u-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|=\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}},\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|=\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}\|_{(V^{\perp})^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Let us write
e_{h}:=u-u_{h}
for the error function and observe
\Pi_{V}u=u
. In light of (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\|u-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|=\sup_{v\in V,\|v\|\leq 1}\widetilde{a}(e_{h},v),\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|=\sup_{v^{\perp}\in V^{\perp},\|v^{\perp}\|\leq 1}\widetilde{a}(e_{h},v^{\perp}),

the identities for these errors follow from (
3.3
) and the fact that
\widetilde{A}
is a linear isometry. The missing identity is then a consequence of orthogonality

\widetilde{a}(u-\Pi_{V}u_{h},u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h})=\widetilde{a}(\Pi_{V}e_{h},e_{h}-\Pi_{V}e_{h})=0,

which expresses also the orthogonality of the residual components in
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
endowed with the scalar product
\widetilde{a}(\widetilde{A}^{-1}\cdot,\widetilde{A}^{-1}\cdot)
.
∎

Remark 3.2

(Extended weak formulation)
.

An equivalent formulation of the weak formulation (
2.1
) in the extended space
\widetilde{V}
reads as follows: for
f\in V^{\prime}
, find
u\in\widetilde{V}
such that

\forall v\in V\quad a(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\forall v^{\perp}\in V^{\perp}\quad\widetilde{a}(u,v^{\perp})=0.

This formulation is an alternative departure point for the definition of the residual
\Res_{h}
and its components
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}
and
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}
.

Remark 3.3

(Decomposition features)
.

Orthogonal decompositions are ‘constant-free’ and therefore desirable
in an a posteriori error analysis. The orthogonal decompositions in
Lemma
3.1
are essentially characterized by the
property that one component coincides with the conforming
subcase. This prepares the ground for using techniques available
for conforming methods and will be exploited in the following steps
concerning localization and computability.

In what follows, we shall refer to the two parts
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}
and
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}
of the residual, respectively, as
conforming
and
nonconforming residual
for short and handle them differently.

3.2.
Indicators for the nonconforming residual

We start with the nonconforming residual norm
\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{NC}}\|_{(V^{\perp})^{\prime}}
, which, according to Lemma
3.1
, equals the distance to conformity
\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|
of the approximate solution
u_{h}
.

In applications, the abstract space
V
is a space of functions over some domain
\Omega
and the extended bilinear form
\widetilde{a}
is defined in terms of an integral over
\Omega
. Thus, given a partition
\mathcal{T}
of
\Omega
, the extended energy norm can be written as

(3.6)

\|\cdot\|^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}|\cdot|_{T}^{2},

where the local contributions
|\cdot|_{T}
are in general only seminorms.

The distance to conformity
\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|
therefore readily splits into the local quantities
|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}|_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}
. Note however that the presence of the projection
\Pi_{V}
and the infinite dimension of
V
typically entail that these quantities are not computable and depend on
u_{h}
in a global manner. We therefore look for an equivalent alternative. For this purpose, the following proposition individuates a simple algebraic condition. It coincides with
[
CN22
, Lemma 2.2]
, which is proved by a different argument and seems to be used in the context of the smoothing operator
\mathcal{E}_{h}
in the discrete problem (
2.3
); for such a usage, it is instructive to take Remark
4.21
into account.

Proposition 3.4

(Approximating the distance to conformity)
.

If

(H1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}:V_{h}\to V

\displaystyle\text{ is a bounded linear operator with }

\displaystyle\forall w_{h}\in V\cap V_{h}\quad\mathcal{A}_{h}w_{h}=w_{h},

then there exists a constant
C_{\mathtt{av}}>0
such that

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}\quad C_{\mathtt{av}}\|v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}\|\leq\|v_{h}-\Pi_{V}v_{h}\|\leq\|v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}\|.

Proof.

The upper bound is an immediate consequence of
\mathcal{A}v_{h}\in V
, while the lower bound essentially follows from the proof of
[
VZ18a
, Theorem 3.2]
about the quasi-optimality of a nonconforming method. We provide a sketch for the sake of completeness. Thanks to the compatibility condition in (
H1
), the following extension of
\mathcal{A}_{h}
to
\widetilde{V}
is well-defined:

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\widetilde{v}:=v+\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h},\quad\widetilde{v}=v+v_{h}\text{ with }v\in V,v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Since
V
and
V_{h}
are closed subspaces of
\widetilde{V}
, the extension
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
is bounded, too; cf.
[
VZ18a
, Lemma 3.1]
, swapping the roles of
V
and
V_{h}
. Given
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, we can thus write

v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}=(\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h})(v_{h}-v)

with an arbitrary
v\in V
and immediately get

\|v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}v_{h}\|\leq\|\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\|\inf_{v\in V}\|v_{h}-v\|=\|\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\|\|v_{h}-\Pi_{V}v_{h}\|.

Notably,
[
VZ18a
, Lemma 3.4]
shows that
\|\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}\|^{-1}
is actually the largest constant
C_{\mathtt{av}}
such that the claimed lower bound holds.
∎

Observe that the upper bound in Proposition
3.4

comes with constant
1
, while the concrete value of
C_{\mathtt{av}}
in the
lower bound is left unspecified. We will provide more information on

C_{\mathtt{av}}
in the applications.

Remark 3.5

(Computable realization of the distance to conformity)
.

An operator
\mathcal{A}_{h}
verifying (
H1
) provides a computable realization of the distance to conformity whenever it is itself computable and each
|\cdot|_{T}
can be computationally evaluated on
V_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}(V_{h})
. Note that, even if
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is a local operator, the indicators are still of global nature due to their dependence on
u_{h}
. In the applications below,
\mathcal{A}_{h}
will be an
averaging operator
.

3.3.
Localization of the conforming residual

Our next goal is to split the conforming residual norm
\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}=\sup_{v\in V,\|v\|\leq 1}\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v\rangle
into an equivalent ‘sum’ of local counterparts. This step is underlying most techniques. It is complicated by the fact that functions in
V
and the norm
\|\cdot\|
typically involve derivatives.
Indeed, first, the partition of unity with characteristic functions underlying (
3.6
) cannot be directly applied. Instead, as for conforming methods, we use sufficiently smooth partitions of unity to split the test function
v
into local contributions.
Second, the effect of the cut-off in the norm
\|\cdot\|
has to be stabilized with the help of some orthogonality property of the residual.

For nonconforming methods, it is convenient to formulate such orthogonality properties with the help of the smoothing operator
\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Indeed, the overconsistency of Remark
2.2
then leads to exact orthogonality, while, in general, quasi-optimality turns out to be a useful ingredient.

Lemma 3.6

(Near orthogonality of conforming residual)
.

If the discretization (
2.3
) induces a quasi-optimal method
M_{h}
, then we have, for all
v\in V
and
K>0
,

(3.7)

\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v\rangle\leq\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h},\|v_{h}\|\leq K\|v\|}\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v-\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle+K\delta_{h}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{A_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|\|v\|.

Proof.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
be such that
\|v_{h}\|\leq K\|v\|
. We add and subtract
\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}
in the action of the conforming residual

\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v\rangle=\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v-\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle+\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle.

For the second term on the right-hand side, we use the method (
2.3
), the
\widetilde{a}
-orthogonal projection
\Pi_{V}
, and the quasi-optimality of the method through Proposition
2.1
(
3
), to derive

\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle=a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})-a(\Pi_{V}u_{h},\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h})\leq\delta_{h}\|A_{h}^{*}v_{h}\|_{V_{h}^{\prime}}\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|.

We thus conclude by recalling that the operator norm of the adjoint
A_{h}^{*}
equals the operator norm of
A_{h}
.
∎

Concerning the partition of unity, we assume the following properties:

(H2)

There are a finite index set
\mathcal{Z}
, functions
(\Phi_{z})_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
,
subspaces
(V_{z})_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
of
V
, a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}:V\to V_{h}
and constants
C_{\mathtt{loc}},C_{\mathtt{col}}>0
such that

(i)

for
z\in\mathcal{Z}
and
v\in V
, we have
\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}v\Phi_{z}=v
with
v\Phi_{z}\in V_{z}
,

(ii)

for
v_{z}\in V_{z}
, we have

\|\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}v_{z}\|^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|v_{z}\|^{2}
, and

(iii)

for
v\in V
, we have

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|(v-\mathcal{E}_{h}\mathcal{I}_{h}v)\Phi_{z}\|^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}\|v\|^{2}
.

The term ‘localization’ in the title of this subsection hints to the fact that in applications the functions
\Phi_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, have compact supports. We therefore shall refer to the subspaces
V_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, as
local test spaces
. Properties (ii) and (iii) prescribe some sort of stability of the partition of unity, where the form of (iii) allows countering the effect of the cut-off.

Lemma 3.7

(Localizing the conforming residual norm)
.

If the discretization (
2.3
) induces a quasi-optimal method
M_{h}
, then (
H2
) implies that

\frac{1}{C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}_{z}}^{2}\leq\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}\leq 2C_{\mathtt{ort}}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2}\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|^{2}+2C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}_{z}}^{2}

holds for some constant
C_{\mathtt{ort}}\leq\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{A_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert
arising through near orthogonality properties of the residual.

Recall that the distance to conformity of the approximate solution
u_{h}
is localized in Section
3.2
. Furthermore, for overconsistent methods, we have
\delta_{h}=0
meaning that the upper bound does not mix with the nonconforming residual; in this case, the factor 2 in the constant
2C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}
of the upper bound can be removed.

Proof.

We begin by verifying the lower bound. Given
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, let
v_{z}\in V_{z}
be the Riesz representer of
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}{}_{|{V_{z}}}
in
V_{z}
entailing
\|v_{z}\|=\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}
and
\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v_{z}\rangle=\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
. Defining
v:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}v_{z}\in V
, we infer

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v_{z}\rangle=\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v\rangle\leq\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}\|v\|.

Thus, by invoking (ii) in (
H2
), we obtain the lower bound

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}.

To prove the upper bound, let
v\in V
with
\|v\|\leq 1
and set
v_{h}:=\mathcal{I}_{h}v\in V_{h}
. The assumptions (i) and (iii) in (
H2
) provide

\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},v-\mathcal{E}_{h}v_{h}\rangle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},(v-\mathcal{E}_{h}\mathcal{I}_{h}v)\Phi_{z}\rangle\leq C_{\mathtt{loc}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We use this estimate in Lemma
3.6
with
K=\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert
, then take the supremum over
v
and conclude the upper bound

\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}\leq 2\delta_{h}^{2}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{A_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert^{2}\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert^{2}\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|^{2}+2C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}.\qed

3.4.
Computability and conforming residual norm

Lemma
3.7
may suggest to employ the local
quantities
\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}=\|f-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}
,
z\in V_{z}
, as indicators. However,
these quantities neither are computable nor can be bounded by a
computable expression if we only know that
f
is taken from
the infinite-dimensional space
V^{\prime}
; cf.
[
KV21
, Lemma 2]
. We therefore adapt the approach in

[
KV21
]
to the nonconforming methods in
Section
2
, thus isolating this computability problem
in data terms.

More precisely, for each
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, using a suitable
local
projection

\mathcal{P}_{z}
, we split the local conforming residual

(f-\widetilde{A}u_{h})_{|V_{z}}
into two parts with the following properties:
The first part
(\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}})_{|V_{z}}
is a
local finite-dimensional approximation of the conforming residual, while
the second part
(\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}-\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}})_{|V_{z}}=(f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f)_{|V_{z}}
only depends on data and is of
oscillatory nature. Doing so,
\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}
is equivalent to a computable quantity and, therefore, the above computability problem gets
isolated in the second part and may be handled on a case-by-case basis,
depending on the knowledge of
f
. A further advantage of this
splitting is outlined in Remark
3.19
below.

To construct a local projection
\mathcal{P}_{z}
, we discretize the local
test and dual spaces
V_{z}
and
V_{z}^{\prime}
by respective local,
finite-dimensional spaces
S_{z}
and
D_{z}
. We shall refer to the
elements of these discrete spaces as
simple (test)
functions
and
simple functionals
, respectively. The simple test functions
may be locally nonconforming, i.e.
S_{z}\not\subseteq V_{z}
is
allowed, but have to be globally conforming, i.e.
S_{z}\subseteq V
. Similar to
[
DDP95
,
DDPV96
]
,
this additional freedom is exploited in
Theorem
4.9
below to provide an estimator,
which is simpler than the one in
Theorem
4.5
based on locally conforming
simple test functions. In view of possibly nonconforming simple test
functions, we consider the local space
\widetilde{V}_{z}:=V_{z}+S_{z}
and
require
D_{z}\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
. Thus, the simple functionals are
locally
nonconforming only if there are locally nonconforming simple test functions.
The operator
\mathcal{P}_{z}
will then project
\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
onto
D_{z}

orthogonal to
S_{z}
and we shall build on the following assumption:

(H3)

There are subspaces
(D_{z})_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
,
(S_{z})_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}
, and constants
C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}},C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}>0
, such that, for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,

(i)

S_{z}\subseteq V
and
D_{z}\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
, where
\widetilde{V}_{z}:=V_{z}+S_{z}
,

(ii)

\widetilde{A}(V_{h})_{|\widetilde{V}_{z}}\subseteq D_{z}
,

(iii)

\dim S_{z}\leq\dim D_{z}<\infty
,

(iv)

for
\chi\in D_{z}
, we have

\|\chi\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}\leq C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}\|\chi\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}
,

(v)

for
g\in V^{\prime}
, we have

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
.

If we consider the case of locally conforming simple test functions,
i.e.
S_{z}\subseteq V_{z}
, the conditions (i) and (ii) are equivalent
to

(3.8)

\widetilde{A}(V_{h})_{|V_{z}}\subseteq D_{z}\subseteq V_{z}^{\prime}

and (v) holds with
C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}=1
since
\|g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\|g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
.

In any case, the simple functionals have to cover locally the part of
the residual depending on the approximate solution. This is a
crucial structural condition and the remaining conditions (iii)-(v) concern the relationship between simple functionals and simple
test functions in order to ensure well-posedness and stability of the
aforementioned operators
\mathcal{P}_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Condition (iv) is
equivalent to the inf-sup condition

(3.9)

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{\chi\in D_{z}}{\sup}_{s\in S_{z}}\dfrac{\langle\chi,s\rangle}{\|\chi\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}\|s\|}\geq\dfrac{1}{C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}}>0

and implies
\dim D_{z}\leq\dim S_{z}
. Consequently, a necessary condition for (
H3
) is
\dim S_{z}=\dim D_{z}
. In Sections
4
and
5
below, we exemplify how to choose simple functionals and local test functions in concrete settings.

The next lemma establishes the local projections, along with their stability properties and their interplay with the extended operator
\widetilde{A}
and the discrete space
V_{h}
.

Proposition 3.8

(Local projections)
.

Suppose assumption (
H3
) holds. Then the variational equations

(3.10)

\forall s\in S_{z}\qquad\langle\mathcal{P}_{z}g,s\rangle=\langle g,s\rangle,\quad z\in\mathcal{Z},

define linear projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}:\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}\to D_{z}
, satisfying the invariances

(3.11a)

\displaystyle\forall z\in\mathcal{Z},v_{h}\in V_{h}\qquad\big(\mathcal{P}_{z}\widetilde{A}v_{h}\big)_{|V_{z}}=\big(\widetilde{A}v_{h}\big)_{|V_{z}}

and the collective stability bound

(3.11b)

\displaystyle\forall g\in V^{\prime}\qquad\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}.

Hereafter, we also write simply
\mathcal{P}_{z}g
instead of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(g_{|\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}})
for
g\in V^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Fix any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Thanks to the rank-nullity theorem and condition (iii) in (
H3
), the well-posedness of the linear variational problem for
\mathcal{P}_{z}
follows from its uniqueness. To show the latter, let
\chi\in D_{z}
with
\langle\chi,s\rangle=0
for all
s\in S_{z}
, that is
\chi_{|S_{z}}=0
. Condition (iv) in (
H3
) then implies
\chi_{|V_{z}}=0
. By its linearity,
\chi
vanishes on the sum
V_{z}+S_{z}
, meaning
\chi=0
in the light of (i) in (
H3
). Consequently, (
3.10
) has a unique solution in
D_{z}
and
\mathcal{P}_{z}
is the linear projection from
\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
onto
D_{z}
with the orthogonality property

(3.12)

\forall s\in S_{z}\qquad\langle g-\mathcal{P}_{z}g,s\rangle=0.

The invariance (
3.11a
) then follows from the observation that, for
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, the difference
\mathcal{P}_{z}\widetilde{A}v_{h}-(\widetilde{A}v_{h})_{|\widetilde{V}_{z}}
is in the image
D_{z}
of
\mathcal{P}_{z}
thanks to (ii) in (
H3
).

Finally, given any
g\in V^{\prime}
, the collective stability bound (
3.11b
) readily follows by combining (iv) and (v) in (
H3
) and the orthogonality (
3.12
) of
\mathcal{P}_{z}
:

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}=C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}.\qed

The use of local projections is motivated by difficulties with the computability of the local quantities
\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}
. The next remark is therefore of primary interest.

Remark 3.9

(Computing the local projections)
.

Let
z\in\mathcal{Z}
and
g\in V^{\prime}
. From an algebraic viewpoint,
the projection
\mathcal{P}_{z}g
from (
3.10
) can
be computed by solving a linear system of equations. Moreover, we have
for the
S_{z}^{\prime}
-norm of
\mathcal{P}_{z}g
that

\|\mathcal{P}_{z}g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}=\mathbf{g}_{z}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{z}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{z},

where
\mathbf{g}_{z}
is the column vector representing the action of
g
on some basis of
S_{z}
and
\mathbf{A}_{z}
is the matrix representing the extended scalar product
\widetilde{a}
on
S_{z}\times S_{z}
with respect to the same basis.

The reader may skip the following two remarks as they are not used in what follows. They, however, give instructive information on the approximation qualities of the local projections and how they are achieved.

Remark 3.10

(Near-best approximation of the local projections)
.

The local projections provide the collective near-best approximation

(3.13)

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g-\mathcal{P}_{z}g\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq(2+2C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2})\inf_{\chi\in\widetilde{A}(V_{h})}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|g-\chi\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}

for all
g\in V^{\prime}
. This follows from the local invariances (
3.11a
) for any fixed
\chi\in\widetilde{A}(V_{h})
, the linearity of the local projections, and their collective stability (
3.11b
).

Remark 3.11

(Local projections as nonconforming approximations)
.

Each projection
\mathcal{P}_{z}
can be viewed as a
nonconforming Petrov-Galerkin approximation
of the identity
\mathrm{id}_{V_{z}^{\prime}}
on
V_{z}^{\prime}
. Equally, in Section
2
,
M_{h}
is such an approximation of
A^{-1}
. It is therefore in order to compare the approach based upon (
H3
) with the one in
[
VZ18a
]
used in Section
2
. To this end, we note that (ii) of (
H3
) is not of interest in this context, and ignore that
[
VZ18a
]
does not properly cover the collective or product setting with different continuous trial and test spaces of the local projections.

A common feature of both approaches is that they invoke the same type of extended spaces. Indeed, the model
\widetilde{V}=V+V_{h}
is repeated in the context of (
H3
), on the test side, by
\widetilde{V}_{z}=V_{z}+S_{z}
, which we may write also as
\widetilde{V}_{z}=V_{z}\oplus S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}
, where
S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}
is a complement of
S_{z}\cap V_{z}
in
S_{z}
, i.e. we have
S_{z}=(S_{z}\cap V_{z})\oplus S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}
.
Similarly, for the more involved trial side, we can view
\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
as the direct sum of
V_{z}^{\prime}
and the nonconforming part
(D_{z}{}_{|S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}})
of
D_{z}
. In fact, we have
\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}=(V_{z}\oplus S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}})^{\prime}\eqsim V_{z}^{\prime}\oplus(S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}})^{\prime}\eqsim V_{z}^{\prime}\oplus(D_{z}{}_{|S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}})
and
D_{z}\eqsim(D_{z}{}_{|V_{z}})\oplus(D_{z}{}_{|S_{z}^{\mathrm{NC}}})
.

There is, however, an important and, at the first look, perhaps striking difference. The nonconforming discrete problems (
3.10
) do not invoke smoothers but exploit simple restriction. Recall that classical nonconforming methods relying on simple restriction cannot be quasi-optimal, i.e. cannot provide near-best approximation; cf.
[
VZ18a
]
. Nevertheless, the use of restriction in (
3.10
) does not obstruct the collective near-best approximation (
3.13
) thanks to the fact that all simple test functions are globally conforming. In addition, restriction is preferable over smoothing in (
3.10
) as it simplifies the implementation of the local projections. The global conformity of all simple test functions implies also that each extended space
\widetilde{V}_{z}^{\prime}
, like its counterpart
V_{z}^{\prime}
, can be viewed as a subspace of
V^{\prime}
, meaning that the simple functionals are globally conforming. Hence, the extension of the local projections to the extended local spaces is ‘built-in’, while the extension of the approximation operator
P_{h}
in (
2.4
) has to be constructed.

The next lemma applies the local projections in order to obtain the
announced splitting of the conforming residual. Therein, an indicator

\eta_{h}
is
computationally
C
-quantifiable
whenever it is
equivalent to a computable quantity
\bar{\eta}_{h}
up to a
multiplicative constant
C>0
, i.e., for all
f\in V^{\prime}
, we have
C^{-1}\bar{\eta}_{h}\leq\eta_{h}\leq C\bar{\eta}_{h}
.

Lemma 3.12

(Conforming residual splitting)
.

Assumption (
H3
) ensures

C_{\mathtt{splt}}^{2}\left(\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2}\right)\leq\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}_{z}}^{2}\leq 2\left(\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2}\right)

with some
C_{\mathtt{splt}}\geq(1+2C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2})^{-1/2}
and the indicators

(3.14)

\eta_{h}^{2}:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Osc_{h}^{2}:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2},

where the indicator
\eta_{h}
is computationally quantifiable up to
\max\{C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}},C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{-1}\}
whenever
\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}=f-\widetilde{A}u_{h}
can be computationally evaluated for all simple test functions.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from the triangle inequality and the special case
(\mathcal{P}_{z}\widetilde{A}u_{h}){}_{|V_{z}}=\widetilde{A}u_{h}{}_{|V_{z}}
of the invariance (
3.11a
):

(3.15)

\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}+\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}-\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}=\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}+\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}.

The lower bound follows from using the last equality in the opposite direction and applying the collective stability (
3.11b
) of the local projections,

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}+\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq(2+3C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}^{2}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}^{2})\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}_{z}}^{2}.

To show the quantifiability of
\eta_{h}
, observe that
\bar{\eta}_{h}^{2}:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
is computable thanks to Remark
3.9
and the assumed knowledge on
\Res_{h}^{\texttt{C}}
. Furthermore, it is equivalent to
\eta_{h}
thanks to (iv) and (v) of (
H3
).
∎

It is important to note that, thanks to the structural condition (ii) in (
H3
), the oscillation
\Osc_{h}
is not just of oscillatory nature, but a
data oscillation
, i.e., in the context of (
2.1
), it depends only on the data
f
and not on the discrete solution
u_{h}
.

Remark 3.13

(Improving
C_{\mathtt{splt}}
)
.

In the analysis
[
BCNV24
, Section 4]
for a conforming method, one has the counterpart of the improved inequality

C_{\mathtt{splt}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}C_{\mathtt{\overline{com}}}C_{\mathtt{\underline{com}}}}

thanks to
[
Szy06
, Lemma 2.1]
. For the corresponding case when
all simple test functions are locally conforming, i.e.
S_{z}\subseteq V_{z}
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, this improvement follows from applying
the cited lemma on each projection
\mathcal{P}_{z}
. Similarly, it follows
also if the local projections arise from a global one, viz. there is
a projection on
P_{h}
on
V^{\prime}
such that
P_{z}{}_{|V_{z}}=P_{h}{}_{|V_{z}}
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. In fact, we can then apply the
cited lemma on
P_{h}
, endowing
V^{\prime}
with the square root of

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\cdot\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
.

3.5.
Deriving a strictly equivalent error estimator

We now combine the various ingredients and previous results to obtain
the main result of our abstract a posteriori analysis: a guideline to
derive an estimator bounding the error from above and below without
any additional terms.

Theorem 3.14

(Abstract estimator)
.

Let
M_{h}
be a quasi-optimal method, induced by the discretization
(
2.3
). Suppose that (
H1
),
(
H2
), and (
H3
) hold
and, given tuning constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, define an abstract
estimator by

\Est_{h}^{2}:=(1+C_{1}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})\Ncf_{h}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}(\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2}),\text{ where }\\
\begin{aligned} \Ncf_{h}^{2}&:=\|u_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{h}u_{h}\|^{2},&&\text{(approximate distance to conformity)}\\
\eta_{h}^{2}&:=\textstyle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}f-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2},&&\text{(approximate conforming residual)}\\
\Osc_{h}^{2}&:=\textstyle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2},&&\text{(data oscillation)}\end{aligned}

and the projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}
are defined by (
3.10
). Then this estimator quantifies the error by

\underline{C}\Est_{h}\leq\|u-u_{h}\|\leq\overline{C}\Est_{h}

where the equivalence constants
\underline{C}
,
\overline{C}
depend only on the norm
\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{A_{h}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert
of the discrete operator
A_{h}
, the constants arising from the assumptions (
H1
), (
H2
), and (
H3
), and the tuning constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
.

Proof.

We first show the upper bound. Combining Lemmas
3.1
,
3.7
,
3.12
, and Proposition
3.4
leads to

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\Res_{h}\|_{\widetilde{V}^{\prime}}^{2}=\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|^{2}+\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2C_{\mathtt{ort}}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})\Ncf_{h}^{2}+2C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2C_{\mathtt{ort}}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})\Ncf_{h}^{2}+4C_{\mathtt{loc}}^{2}(\eta_{h}^{2}+\Osc_{h}^{2}).

This implies the upper bound for some

\overline{C}\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt{2}C_{\mathtt{ort}}}{C_{1}},\frac{2C_{\mathtt{loc}}}{C_{2}}\right\}.

Regarding the lower bound, we apply the same auxiliary results to derive

\displaystyle\Est_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{1}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})C_{\mathtt{av}}^{-2}\|u_{h}-\Pi_{V}u_{h}\|^{2}+C_{2}^{2}C_{\mathtt{splt}}^{-2}C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\big\{(1+C_{1}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})C_{\mathtt{av}}^{-2},C_{2}^{2}C_{\mathtt{splt}}^{-2}C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}\big\}\|u-u_{h}\|^{2},

whence the lower bound holds for some

\underline{C}\geq\max\big\{(1+C_{1}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})C_{\mathtt{av}}^{-2},C_{2}^{2}C_{\mathtt{splt}}^{-2}C_{\mathtt{col}}^{2}\big\}^{-1}.\qed

Several remarks about the properties of the estimator
\Est_{h}
in
Theorem
3.14
are in order. We start by discussing
the computability of
\Est_{h}
, compare
with typical results from literature, highlight its features for
adaptivity, and conclude with a remark about how to choose the simple
functionals.

Remark 3.15

(On computability)
.

In Remark
3.5
and
Lemma
3.12
, we have already observed that,
under mild assumptions, the indicators
\Ncf_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
are
computable (in exact arithmetic), exploiting their finite-dimensional
nature. The remaining oscillation indicator
\Osc_{h}
cannot be
computationally bounded, not to mention computed, in general, viz. knowing only that the data
f
is from the infinite-dimensional space

V^{\prime}
; cf.
[
KV21
, Lemma 2 and Corollary 5]
. We
may neglect it (see Remark
3.16
for a
discussion of this option) or resort to some approximation of
\Osc_{h}
,
which is derived case-by-case, possibly exploiting additional
information on the regularity and/or structure of
f
. The
significance of Theorem
3.14
then of course hinges
on the quality of those approximations. In particular, to facilitate
the approximation, it may be convenient to waive the lower bound with
the data oscillation
\Osc_{h}
and replace it by a so-called

surrogate oscillation
, i.e. a quantity that provides an upper
bound of
\Osc_{h}
and can be considered computable; cf.
[
CDN12
, Section 7.1]
.

Remark 3.16

(Neglecting data oscillation?)
.

Let us compare the splitting of the conforming residual in
Lemma
3.12
to the following formula in
numerical integration:

\int_{0}^{1}v=v(\tfrac{1}{2})+\int_{0}^{1}v(x)-v(\tfrac{1}{2})\,dx.

Similarly to Lemma
3.12
combined with
Remark
3.9
, but with an equality
instead of an equivalence, this formula relates an exact
non-computable value, a computable approximation and a representation
of the error of the approximation. Since in numerical integration the
error is often not considered on a computational level, one may be
tempted to also neglect the oscillation
\Osc_{h}
. This approach
amounts to use in computations simply the estimator

\widetilde{\mathrm{est}}{}_{h}^{2}:=(1+C_{1}^{2}\delta_{h}^{2})\Ncf_{h}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\eta_{h}^{2}.

Note that this estimator depends on the data
f
only through the discrete solution
u_{h}
and the local projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. It therefore cannot see components of
f
orthogonal to
\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h})+\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}S_{z}
, which may constitute the main part of the error; cf.
[
BCNV24
, Section 5.3.1]
. Hence, a reliable use of
\widetilde{\mathrm{est}}{}_{h}

requires to verify a smallness assumption of the type
\Osc_{h}\ll\widetilde{\mathrm{est}}_{h}
. Apart from the fact that such a smallness
condition would a priori restrict the admissible data, it is not clear
to us that its verification is a simpler task than the alternatives
outlined in Remark
3.15
. Notice also that, in an
adaptive context, the estimator
\widetilde{\mathrm{est}}{}_{h}
does
not provide any information to adapt to the aforementioned orthogonal
components of
f
and the smallness assumption will be needed for the
initial and all subsequent meshes.

Remark 3.17

(Strict equivalence and error-dominated oscillation)
.

The strict equivalence between error and the estimator
\Est_{h}
and
the related error-dominated oscillation
\Osc_{h}
are key features of
Theorem
3.14
. In fact, if we derive a posteriori
bounds for (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) by means of
classical techniques, the equivalence between error and estimator is
spoiled.

To explain this defect in more detail, denote the classical estimator and oscillation
by
\widehat{\Est}_{h}
and
\widehat{\Osc}_{h}
, respectively. The classical oscillation requires
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\subsetneq V^{\prime}
or other regularity assumptions beyond
V^{\prime}
. The
modified equivalence then reads

(3.16)

\|u-u_{h}\|\leq C_{1}\big(\widehat{\Est}_{h}+\widehat{\Osc}_{h}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\Est}_{h}\leq C_{1}\big(\|u-u_{h}\|+\widehat{\Osc}_{h}\big),

where, depending on the estimator type,
\widehat{\Osc}_{h}
may be not
present in one of the bounds. Since the classical oscillation

\widehat{\Osc}_{h}
does not vanish whenever the error does, a bound of
the type
\widehat{\Osc}_{h}\leq C\|u-u_{h}\|
does not
hold. Furthermore, the fact that the classical oscillation
\widehat{\Osc}_{h}
is
formally of higher order does not exclude that, under adaptive refinement,
it converges slower than the error in certain cases; cf.
[
CDN12
, Section 6.4]
and

[
KV21
, Section 3.8]
. Consequently, a strict
equivalence cannot be obtained from (
3.16
) and
the defect may not mitigate under adaptive refinement. In contrast to
this, Theorem
3.14
ensures
\Osc_{h}\leq C\|u-u_{h}\|
for some constant
C
.

The crucial step for this improvement is the splitting of the
conforming residual by means of the triangle inequality in
(
3.15
). Therein, the splitting is realized with the
local projections that are collectively stable without changing the
involved norm thanks to conditions (iv) and (v) of
(
H3
). Classical techniques split,
explicitly or implicitly, the conforming residual in the upper and/or
lower bound in a less stable manner, passing to some norm that is strictly stronger than
\|\cdot\|_{V^{\prime}}
.
This difference in the stability of the splitting of the
conforming residual is the new twist. In view of the discussion in

[
BCNV24
, Remark 4.19]
and

[
KVZ
]
, it is also a necessary ingredient
for the strict equivalence.

Remark 3.18

(Classical oscillation as surrogate)
.

A classical oscillation
\widehat{\Osc}_{h}
from
Remark
3.17
may be used as a surrogate
oscillation, as defined in Remark
3.15
; see Theorems
4.5
,
4.9
,
4.19
,
5.8
, and
5.15
below. In this context, it is
worth mentioning that the application of
Theorem
3.14
is nevertheless advantageous. Indeed,
Theorem
3.14
ensures the lower bound
\eta_{h}\leq C\|u-u_{h}\|
with some constant
C
, while, for certain classical
techniques, the corresponding lower bound gets spoiled, i.e. one has
only
\widehat{\eta}_{h}\leq C(\|u-u_{h}\|+\widehat{\Osc}_{h})
for
the counterpart
\widehat{\eta}_{h}
of
\eta_{h}
, with the disadvantages
outlined in Remark
3.17
;
cf.
[
BCNV24
, Remark 4.43]
.

Remark 3.19

(Conforming residual splitting and adaptivity)
.

We briefly discuss a further advantage of the splitting of the conforming residual in an adaptive context. The quantity
\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\|f-\widetilde{A}u_{h}\|_{V_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}
is of infinite-dimensional and, although localized, of global nature. The infinite dimension arises through the data
f
, while the global dependence through the approximate solution
u_{h}
. These features lead to complications in adaptivity. Roughly speaking, infinite dimension in absence of additional information entails that a given pattern for local refinement cannot be expected to uniformly reduce the error, while global dependence complicates the behavior of indicators under local refinement. After the splitting in Lemma
3.12
, the indicator
\eta_{h}
is of finite-dimensional but global nature and the data oscillation
\Osc_{h}
of local, but infinite-dimensional nature. In other words: the two difficulties get separated by this splitting. This separation, which relies on the structural condition (ii) in (
H3
), can be exploited in the design and analysis of adaptive algorithms; cf., e.g.,
[
BCNV24
, Section 5]
.

Remark 3.20

(Choosing the simple functionals)
.

The crucial structural condition (ii) of (
H3
) prescribes a minimal size for the sets
D_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, of simple functionals. Furthermore, in the light of the computational issues with
\Osc_{h}
, it appears convenient that its formal convergence order is larger than the one for the error and that it is smaller than available classical oscillations. It will then play, at least asymptotically for sufficiently smooth sources
f
, a less important role. Of course, the larger the sets
D_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, the greater the computational cost of the estimator. All this suggests to take the simple functionals not much larger than necessary for the aforementioned three conditions.

4.
Estimators for the Poisson problem

In this section, we first exemplify the application of the guidelines developed Section
3
in a simple case and then start to illustrate its generality. For the first purpose, we consider the Poisson problem

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in}~\Omega\qquad\text{and}\qquad u=0\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,

together with a first-order Crouzeix-Raviart method, which is quasi-optimal and overconsistent. Covering the use of locally conforming and locally nonconforming simple test functions, we derive and discuss two different strictly equivalent a posteriori error estimators, highlighting various aspects of the abstract framework.

To illustrate the generality of the guidelines, we then consider quasi-optimal discontinuous Galerkin methods of arbitrary but fixed order for the second-order problem (
4.1
). This will be complemented in Section
5
by dealing with nonconforming methods for a fourth-order problem.

4.1.
Domain, mesh, and polynomials

Let
\Omega
be a polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. We denote by
\mathsf{n}
the outward pointing unit normal vector field of
\partial\Omega
and, correspondingly,
\partial_{\mathsf{n}}v:=\nabla v\cdot\mathsf{n}
stands for the normal derivative of a sufficiently smooth function
v
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a simplicial face-to-face (conforming) mesh of the domain
\Omega
. We denote, respectively, the set of its faces, of its interior faces, of its vertices, and of its interior vertices by
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}^{i}
,
\mathcal{V}
, and
\mathcal{V}^{i}
. The mesh-size function
h:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
of
\mathcal{T}
is given by

h_{|\mathrm{int}(T)}:=h_{T}\quad\text{for $T\in\mathcal{T}$},\qquad\text{and}\qquad h_{|F}:=h_{F},\quad\text{for $F\in\mathcal{F}$},

where
h_{T}
and
h_{F}
are the respective diameters of
T
and
F
. We extend the normal field
\mathsf{n}
on the skeleton
\Sigma:=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F
by setting

\mathsf{n}_{|F}:=n_{F}\quad\text{for $F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}$},

where
n_{F}
is an arbitrary but fixed unit vector normal to the
interior face
F
. The patches around a simplex
T\in\mathcal{T}
, a face

F\in\mathcal{F}
and a vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}
are the respective sets

\begin{gathered}\omega_{T}:=\bigcup_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T},T^{\prime}\cap T\neq\emptyset}T^{\prime},\qquad\omega_{F}:=\bigcup_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T},F\subseteq\partial T^{\prime}}T^{\prime},\quad\omega_{z}:=\bigcup_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T},z\in T^{\prime}}T^{\prime},\\
\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{z}^{+}:=\bigcup_{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T},T^{\prime}\cap\omega_{z}\neq\emptyset}T^{\prime}.\end{gathered}

For convenience, we introduce a dedicated notation also for diameter, induced mesh and set of interior faces of the above patches around a vertex, namely

\begin{gathered}h_{z}:=\mathrm{diam}(\omega_{z}),\qquad\mathcal{T}_{z}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid z\in T\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}\mid z\in F\},\\
\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid T\cap\omega_{z}\neq\emptyset\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i+}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}\mid F\cap\omega_{z}\neq\emptyset\}.\end{gathered}

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(T)
and
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(F)

the sets of polynomials with total degree not larger than
\ell
on a
simplex
T\in\mathcal{T}
and a face
F\in\mathcal{F}
, respectively. For

\ell<0
, we use the convention
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(T):=\{0\}=:\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(F)
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we consider the following
sets of piecewise polynomials on
\mathcal{T}

S^{k}_{\ell}:=\{v\in H^{k}(\Omega)\mid\forall T\in\mathcal{T}\quad v_{|T}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(T)\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathring{S}^{k}_{\ell}:=S^{k}_{\ell}\cap\mathring{H}^{k}(\Omega),

with the convention
H^{0}(\Omega):=L^{2}(\Omega)=:\mathring{H}^{0}(\Omega)
. We
make use of the Courant basis functions
\{\Psi_{z}\}_{z\in\mathcal{V}}
of the first-order space
S^{1}_{1}
, which are determined by the condition

(4.2)

\Psi_{z}(y)=\delta_{zy},\quad y,z\in\mathcal{V},

and of the bubble functions

(4.3)

\Psi_{T}:=\prod_{z\in\mathcal{V},z\in T}\Psi_{z}\in\mathring{S}^{1}_{d+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi_{F}:=\prod_{z\in\mathcal{V},z\in F}\Psi_{z}\in S^{1}_{d}

associated with each element
T\in\mathcal{T}
and face
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

For a piecewise smooth function
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, the jump

\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
of
v
is defined on the skeleton
\Sigma
of

\mathcal{T}
as

\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{|F}:=v_{|T_{1}}-v_{|T_{2}}\quad\text{for $F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}$},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket_{|F}:=v_{|T}\quad\text{for $F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{i}$},

where, for
F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, the simplices
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
are such that
T_{1}\cap T_{2}=F
and
\mathsf{n}_{F}
points from
T_{1}
to
T_{2}
, and for
F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{i}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}
is such that
T\supset F
.
Similarly, the average
\{\kern-2.24998pt\{v\}\kern-2.24998pt\}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
of
v
is
defined as

\{\kern-2.24998pt\{v\}\kern-2.24998pt\}_{|F}:=\frac{v_{|T_{1}}+v_{|T_{2}}}{2}\quad\text{on $F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}$},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{\kern-2.24998pt\{v\}\kern-2.24998pt\}_{|F}:=v_{|T}\quad\text{on $F\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{i}$}.

Finally, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a positive constant
c
such that

a\leq c\,b
and
a\eqsim b
when in addition
b\lesssim a
.
The hidden constants possibly depend on the space dimension
d
, the
polynomial degree
\ell
and the shape constant

(4.4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}:=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\frac{h_{T}}{\rho_{T}},

of the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, where
\rho_{T}
is the diameter of the largest ball in
T
.

4.2.
Crouzeix-Raviart method as model example

This section is devoted to the a posteriori analysis of the quasi-optimal Crouzeix-Raviart method from
[
VZ19
, section 3.2]
. Exemplifying the guidelines in Section
3
, two strictly equivalent error estimators of hierarchical type are derived in Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
. The first estimator involves face-bubble functions, while the second one does not, in line with the results in
[
DDP95
,
DDPV96
]
.

We start by recalling the aforementioned Crouzeix-Raviart method. The lowest-order finite element space of
Crouzeix-Raviart
[
CR73
]
on
\mathcal{T}
is defined as

(4.5)

{C\!R}_{1}:=\left\{v\in S^{0}_{1}\mid\forall F\in\mathcal{F}~\int_{F}\left\llbracket v\right\rrbracket=0\right\},

and is not contained in
\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, the trial and test space of the standard weak formulation of the Poisson problem (
4.1
). The corresponding discrete problem reads

(4.6)

\begin{multlined}\text{for $f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)$},~\text{find $u_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}$ such that}\\
\forall v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}\quad\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}=\langle f,\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h}\rangle.\end{multlined}

Hereafter,
\nabla_{h}
denotes the broken gradient given by

(\nabla_{h}v)_{|T}:=\nabla(v_{|T})\quad\text{for }T\in\mathcal{T}\text{ and suitable }v.

The
smoothing operator

\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}:{C\!R}_{1}\to\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
is defined
in
[
VZ19
, Proposition 3.3]
and satisfies the following properties: for all
v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}
, we have

(4.7a)

\displaystyle\forall F

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\int_{F}\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h}=\int_{F}v_{h},

(4.7b)

\displaystyle\forall T

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})},

which are related to consistency and stability of the method, respectively; see also below. Note that the bound (
4.7b
) entails that
\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}
is a local operator but may enlarge the support of its argument.

In order to apply Section
3
, we first note that the Poisson problem (
4.1
) and the method (
4.6
) fit into the abstract framework of Section
2
with

(4.8)

\begin{gathered}\widetilde{V}=\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)+{C\!R}_{1}\quad\text{with}\quad V=\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega),\quad V_{h}={C\!R}_{1},\\
\widetilde{a}(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v\cdot\nabla_{h}w,\quad\|v\|=\|\nabla_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\\
a_{h}=\widetilde{a}_{|{C\!R}_{1}\times{C\!R}_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}.\end{gathered}

The conservation (
4.7a
) of face means by the
smoother
\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}
and the fact that both, discrete and continuous
bilinear forms, are restrictions of
\widetilde{a}
imply

(4.9)

\displaystyle\forall v_{h},w_{h}

\displaystyle\in{C\!R}_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}w_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h})=0.

Consequently, the consistency measure vanishes, i.e.
\delta_{h}=0
, and the method is overconsistent; cf. Remark
2.2
. Hence,
Proposition
2.1
and the smoother stability (
4.7b
) ensure that the method is quasi-optimal with the constant
C_{\mathtt{qo}}=\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert
depending on the shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
and the dimension
d
.

We next verify the main assumptions of Section
3
for the setting (
4.8
). The derivations of the two estimators will differ only in the verification of (
H3
) for the construction of the local projections. Let us start with (
H1
) concerning the distance to conformity. According to Proposition
3.4
, we need a linear operator that is invariant on the intersection

\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap{C\!R}_{1}=\mathring{S}^{1}_{1}
. In line with Remark
3.5
, a possible choice is the averaging operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}:{C\!R}_{1}\to\mathring{S}^{1}_{1}
defined by

(4.10)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h}(z):=\dfrac{1}{\#\mathcal{T}_{z}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}v_{h|T}(z),\qquad z\in\mathcal{V}^{i},\;v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1},

Lemma 4.1

(Approximate
\|\nabla_{h}\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
-distance to
\mathring{H}^{1}
by averaging)
.

In the setting (
4.8
), the operator

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}
in (
4.10
) satisfies assumption
(
H1
) and
C_{\mathtt{av}}\gtrsim 1
. In particular,
for
all
v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}
we have that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\inf_{v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}
satisfies (
H1
) and so the second bound of the claimed equivalence holds. For its first bound, which is equivalent to
C_{\mathtt{av}}\gtrsim 1
, we have

\|\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Sigma}\dfrac{|\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket|^{2}}{h};

see, e.g.,
[
KP03
, Theorem 2.2]
. To conclude, we adapt ideas from
[
ABC03
, Theorem 10]
to prove that the jumps are bounded by the distance to
\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
be any face and, for any element
T\in\mathcal{T}
with
\partial T\supset F
, define
\phi_{T}\in H^{1}(T)
as the weak solution of the Neumann problem

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi_{T}=0~\text{in}~T,\qquad\partial_{\mathsf{n}}\phi_{T}=\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket~\text{on}~F,\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{\mathsf{n}}\phi_{T}=0~\text{on}~\partial T\setminus F,

with
\int_{T}\phi_{T}=0
. Note that the existence of
\phi_{T}
hinges on
\int_{F}\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket=0
and thus on
v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}
. For all
v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{F}|\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket|^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T},T\supset F}\int_{T}\nabla\phi_{T}\cdot\nabla(v_{h}-v)\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T},T\supset F}\|\nabla\phi_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\|\nabla(v_{h}-v)\|_{L^{2}(T)}.

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}
with
\partial T\supset F
, the norm of
\phi_{T}
can be further estimated by

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}=\int_{F}\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket\phi_{T}\lesssim h_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket\|_{L^{2}(F)}\|\nabla\phi_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}

with the help of the scaled trace theorem, a Poincaré inequality thanks to
\int_{T}\phi_{T}=0
, and
h_{T}\lesssim h_{F}
. Inserting this bound into the previous one shows

\int_{F}\dfrac{|\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket|^{2}}{h}\lesssim\inf_{v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{h}(v_{h}-v)\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}^{2}.

We conclude by summing over all faces, because the number of patches
\omega_{F}
containing a given simplex is bounded by
d+1
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
4.1
reveals that properly scaled jumps of
v_{h}
on the mesh skeleton can be considered as an alternative indicator for the distance to
\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.3

(Partition of unity in

for
\mathsf{CR}
)
.

In the setting (

), the choices (

) satisfy assumption (

) with constants

.

Proof.

Property (i) in (

(d+1)
stars

,

Theorem 4.5

(Estimator for
\mathsf{CR}
@Poisson)
.

Let
u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
be the weak solution of the Poisson problem (
4.1
) and
u_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}
its quasi-optimal Crouzeix-Raviart approximation from (
4.6
). Given tuning constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, define

\Est_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}:=\Ncf_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}+C_{1}^{2}\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2},\text{ where }\\
\begin{aligned} \Ncf_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}&:=\|\nabla_{h}(u_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\\
\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}&:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\eta_{\mathsf{CR},T}^{2}\text{ with }\eta_{\mathsf{CR},T}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{T}}\frac{|\langle f,\Psi_{K}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla\Psi_{K}|}{\|\nabla\Psi_{K}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},\\
\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}&:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}^{2},\end{aligned}

with the averaging operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}
from (
4.10
),
\mathcal{K}_{T}=\{T\}\cup\{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}\mid F\subset T\}
, the bubble functions
\Psi_{T}
and
\Psi_{F}
from (
4.3
), and the local projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}
from Lemma
4.4
.
This estimator quantifies the error by

\underline{C}\Est_{\mathsf{CR}}\leq\|\nabla_{h}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\overline{C}\Est_{\mathsf{CR}}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{\mathsf{CR},T}\leq\|\nabla_{h}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{T})}

where
\widetilde{\omega}_{T}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i},F\subset\partial T}\omega_{F}
and the equivalence constants
\underline{C}
and
\overline{C}

depend only on the dimension
d
, the shape constant

\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
, and the tuning constants
C_{1},C_{2}
.

Furthermore, if
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the oscillation indicator is bounded in terms of the classical
L^{2}
-oscillation:

\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h_{T}^{2}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-c\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Thanks to Lemmas
4.1
,

4.3
, and
4.4
, we
can apply Theorem
3.14
with
(
4.8
) and
(
4.11
). The overconsistency of the
Crouzeix-Raviart method (

), i.e.

simplifies the abstract estimator therein and the claimed

(4.15)

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|\mathcal{P}_{z}\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}^{2}\eqsim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}^{2}\eqsim\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2},

(

the arguments in
[

To verify the claimed lower bound, let

\chi\in D_{z}

v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})
, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\chi,v\rangle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}}\int_{T}r_{T}v+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}}\int_{F}r_{F}v

for some
r_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}
and
r_{F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}
. Fixing a local test function
v
, we choose the simple test function

s=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}}s_{T}\Psi_{T}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}}s_{F}\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F}^{\mathsf{CR}}\in S_{z}

where
s_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}
, and
s_{F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}
, solve the problems

\displaystyle\forall p_{F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)\qquad\int_{F}s_{F}p_{F}\Psi_{F}^{\mathsf{CR}}=\int_{F}vp_{F},

\displaystyle\forall p_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T)\qquad\int_{T}s_{T}p_{T}\Psi_{T}=\int_{T}vp_{T}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}}\int_{T}s_{F}p_{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F}^{\mathsf{CR}}.

These problems are uniquely solvable because
\Psi_{F}^{\mathsf{CR}}
and
\Psi_{T}
are strictly positive in the interior of
F
and
T
, respectively. We then have

\langle\chi,v\rangle=\langle\chi,s\rangle
and

\|\nabla s\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z}^{+})}\lesssim h_{z}^{-1}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}}\|s_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{z}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}}\|s_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z})},

where the first inequality follows from (
4.7b
) and the scaling properties of the basis functions of
S_{z}
and, for the second one, we use in addition norm equivalences with bubble functions
[
Ver13
, Proposition 1.4]
, trace and Poincaré inequalities. Hence, we arrive
at

\dfrac{\left\langle\chi,v\right\rangle}{\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z})}}\lesssim\dfrac{\left\langle\chi,s\right\rangle}{\|\nabla s\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z}^{+})}}\leq\|\chi\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}

and, taking the supremum over
v
, we obtain the desired condition (iv).

Finally, for verifying condition (v) in (
H3
), assume
g\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Since we have
S_{z}\subset\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z}^{+})
and so
\|g\|_{S_{z}^{\prime}}\leq\|g\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z}^{+})}
for all vertices
z\in\mathcal{V}
, the desired bound follows from

(4.20)

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|g\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z}^{+})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|g\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}^{2}.

Given any vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}
and
v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z}^{+})
, we can write
v=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+}}v\Psi_{y}
and have the stability bounds
\|\nabla(v\Psi_{y})\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}\lesssim\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z}^{+})}
thanks to scaling properties of the Courant basis and the Poincaré inequality with zero boundary values on
\omega_{z}^{+}
. Hence, similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we derive

(4.21)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\langle g,v\right\rangle}{\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{z}^{+})}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+}}\dfrac{\left\langle g,v\Psi_{y}\right\rangle}{\|\nabla(v\Psi_{y})\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{y})}}\lesssim\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+}}\|g\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{y})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+}}\|g\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{y})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where in the last inequality we used that
\#(\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+})
is bounded in terms of the shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d
. We take the supremum over
v
, square and sum over all vertices
z
. This verifies (
4.20
)
because the number of patches
\omega_{z}^{+}
containing a vertex
y
is
again bounded in terms of shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
and
d
.
∎

Theorem 4.9

(Simplified estimator for
\mathsf{CR}
@Poisson)

Let

\begin{aligned} \Ncf_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}^{2}&:=\|\nabla_{h}(u_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{CR}}u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\\
\eta_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}^{2}&:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}},T}^{2}\text{ with }\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}},T}:=\frac{|\langle f,\Psi_{T}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla\Psi_{T}|}{\|\nabla\Psi_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},\qquad\qquad\\

\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h_{T}^{2}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|f-c\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the one of Theorem

To discuss Theorem

, two remarks address the design and the generality of the approach behind it and three remarks compare its estimator with alternatives.

Remark 4.10

(Nonconforming element-bubble functions)
.

One may be tempted to build the second approach on the simpler pairs

(4.23)

S_{z}=\mathrm{span}\{\Psi_{T}\mid T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi^{\mathsf{CR}}_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}\},\quad D_{z}=\widehat{D}_{z}{}_{|(\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})+S_{z})},

which just replace the face-bubble functions in (
4.14b
) by the smoothed Crouzeix-Raviart basis functions, thus avoiding the nonconforming element-bubble functions in
S_{z}
along with their companions in
D_{z}
. In this case, the estimator equivalence in Theorem
4.9
still holds, but the oscillation bound is not valid in general. Indeed, assuming (
4.23
), testing (
3.10
) first with the element-bubble functions
\Psi_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}
, and then with the smoothed Crouzeix-Raviart basis functions
\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F}^{\mathsf{CR}}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}
reveals that
\mathcal{P}_{z}\bar{f}=\bar{f}
holds only under additional assumptions on
\bar{f}_{|(\omega_{z}^{+}\setminus\omega_{z})}
outside of the star
\omega_{z}
. This drawback is overcome by including the nonconforming element-bubble functions
\Psi_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{z}
, and their companions
\chi_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{z}
, ensuring (
4.22
), which in turn implies
(\mathcal{P}_{z}\bar{f})_{|\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})}=\bar{f}_{|\omega_{z}}
.

On the other hand, if we set the oscillation bound in Theorem
4.9
aside, we can use the even simpler pair

S_{z}=\mathrm{span}\{\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi^{\mathsf{CR}}_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}\},\quad D_{z}=\widehat{D}_{z}{}_{|(\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})+S_{z})},

and obtain another strictly equivalent estimator, which consists only
of an approximate distance to conformity and a data oscillation term
and which is a lower bound of the estimators in

[
Bre15
, Section 2.3]
and
[
CGN24
, Section
3.2.3]
, respectively.

Remark 4.11

(Simplification for some higher-order Crouzeix-Raviart methods)
.

For
d=2
and odd polynomial degree
\ell
, a similarly simplified estimator as in Theorem
4.9
can be derived for the corresponding Crouzeix-Raviart method in
[
VZ19
, Section 3.3]
. In this case,
[
SB06
, Lemma 1]
shows that point evaluations at the order
\ell
Legendre nodes on the element edges together with the order
\ell
Lagrange points in the interior of a given element determine
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}
. The smoothed basis functions corresponding to the Legendre nodes on the edges are then orthogonal to the residual. Thus, the higher order counterpart of (
4.14
) can be modified to a higher order counterpart of (
4.18
) in order to obtain a generalization of Theorem
4.9
.

Remark 4.12

(Comparison of strictly equivalent estimators with quasi-optimality)
.

Let us compare the two estimators in Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
. The differing indicators are related as follows:

(4.24)

\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}\geq\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}},\quad\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}\not\lesssim\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}},\quad\textit{and}\quad\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}\eqsim\Osc_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}}.

These properties are proved below.

Clearly, the implementation of the indicator
\eta_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
is easier than the one of
\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}
, while the one of
\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}
is slightly easier that the one of
\Osc_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}}
because the latter in addition involves some smoothing; recall that smoothing itself has to be implemented in any case for assembling the discrete problem (
2.3
) and that oscillations may be replaced by surrogates. In terms of computational costs, the situation is quite similar in the sense that ‘easier’ is replaced with ‘less costly’. To sum up, we may view
\Est_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
as a simplification of
\Est_{\mathsf{CR}}
.

We finally inspect the (hidden) constants in the proof. Since the derivations of the two upper bounds differ only in the second equivalence of (
4.15
), the constants in front of the oscillations
\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}
and
\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
coincide and we expect that those in front of
\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}
and
\eta_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
are different. In light of the first inequality in (
4.24
), the latter should turn out to be larger. For the global lower bounds, the constants for
\eta_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
and
\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
are expected to be larger in view of the increased overlapping in the simple functions, as suggested by the given proofs for (v) in (
H3
).

Proof of (
4.24
).

We use the superscript ‘
\sim
’ to distinguish the spaces and operators related to the estimator
\Est_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
from those related to
\Est_{\mathsf{CR}}
.

1
The first claimed inequality readily follows by the respective definitions. Furthermore, the invariance and stability properties of
\mathcal{P}_{z}
as well as
\widetilde{D}_{z}{}_{|\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})}=D_{z}
imply

\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}=\|(\operatorname{id}-\mathcal{P}_{z})(f-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}f)\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}\lesssim\|f-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}.

Hence, summing over
z\in\mathcal{V}
, gives the oscillation bound
\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}\lesssim\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}
.

2
To prepare the proof of inequality
\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}\lesssim\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}
, we introduce a further local projection acting over the extended star
\omega_{z}^{+}
: for any vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}
, the pair

\displaystyle S_{z}^{+}:=\mathrm{span}\{\Psi_{T}\mid T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}\}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{\Psi_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i+}\},

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{z}^{+}:=\Big\{\chi\in H^{-1}(\omega_{z}^{+})&\mid\langle\chi,v\rangle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}}\int_{T}r_{T}v+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i+}}\int_{F}r_{F}v\\
&\phantom{\mid}\text{with}~r_{T}\in\mathbb{R},\,T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+},\text{ and }r_{F}\in\mathbb{R},\,F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i+}\Big\},\end{aligned}

induces through Proposition
3.8
a local projection
\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}:H^{-1}(\omega_{z}^{+})\to D_{z}^{+}
. For a given vertex
y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}
, combining
D_{z}^{+}{}_{|\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{y})}=D_{y}
,
S_{y}\subset S_{z}^{+}
, and the uniqueness of (
3.10
) for (
4.14
) yield that, for any
g\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
,

(4.25)

(\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}g)_{|\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{y})}=\mathcal{P}_{y}g.

Also, there are constants
r_{T}
,
T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}
, and
r_{F}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}
, such that, for all
v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})
and
s\in\widetilde{S}_{z}
, we have

\langle\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}g,v+s\rangle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{z}^{+}}\int_{T}r_{T}(v+s)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{i}}\int_{F}r_{F}(v+s)

thanks to (
4.19
). In other words,
(\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}g)_{|(\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})+\widetilde{S}_{z})}\in\widetilde{D}_{z}
and the invariance of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}
ensure

(4.26)

\big(\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}g\big)_{|(\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})+\widetilde{S}_{z})}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}(\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}g).

Note that this property hinges on the companions of the nonconforming
element-bubble functions in Remark
4.10
.

3
We turn to the proper proof of

\Osc_{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}\lesssim\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}
. Employing
(
4.26
) restricted to
\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z})
, the
stability properties of
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}
,
\widetilde{S}_{z}\subset\mathring{H}^{1}(\omega_{z}^{+})
, (
4.21
) and
(
4.25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}

\displaystyle=\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}f-\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{z}(f-\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}f)\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}^{+}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{z}^{+})}\lesssim\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{V}\cap\omega_{z}^{+}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{y}f\|_{H^{-1}(\omega_{y})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and so squaring, and summing over all vertices, yields the desired bound.

4
Finally, we verify that
\eta_{{{\mathsf{CR}}}}
cannot be bounded by
\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}}
. For
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
T\in\mathcal{T}
, an integration by parts reveals

\langle f,\Psi_{T}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla\Psi_{T}=\langle f,\Psi_{T}\rangle.

Moreover, for
F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, the orthogonality property of the interpolant
\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{CR}}
in (
4.11
), see
[
Bre15
, Section 2.1]
, and (
4.6
) entail that

\langle f,\Psi_{F}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla\Psi_{F}=\langle f,\Psi_{F}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F}\rangle.

The construction of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}
in
[
VZ19
, Proposition 3.3]
reveals that there is a face
F
such that
(\Psi_{F}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F})_{|F}
is not the zero function. Therefore, fixing that face and taking the source
f
so that
\langle f,v\rangle=\int_{F}(\Psi_{F}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{CR}}\Psi_{F})v
for
v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have
\eta_{{{\mathsf{CR}}}}\neq 0
, whereas
\eta_{{\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}}}=0
.
∎

Remark 4.13

(Comparison with classical estimators)
.

We compare the estimators in Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
with the classical ones in
[
DDPV96
]
, for the classical Crouzeix-Raviart method

(4.27)

\text{find $u_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}$ such that\;}\forall v_{h}\in{C\!R}_{1}\quad\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle.

For
*\in\{\mathsf{CR},\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}\}
, the classical estimators are given by

(4.28)

\displaystyle\Est_{\mathrm{*}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\Ncf_{\mathrm{cl,*}}^{2}+\eta_{\mathrm{cl,*}}^{2}+\Osc_{\mathrm{cl,*}}^{2},\quad\text{where}

\displaystyle\Ncf_{\mathrm{cl,*}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Sigma}\dfrac{|\left\llbracket u_{h}\right\rrbracket|^{2}}{h},\qquad\Osc_{\mathrm{cl},*}^{2}:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{T}h^{2}|f-c|^{2}

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{cl,*}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\eta_{\mathrm{cl,*}}(T)^{2}\quad\text{with}

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{cl,*}}(T)^{2}:=\begin{cases}\int_{T}h^{2}|f|^{2}+\sum_{F\subseteq\partial T}\int_{F}h|\left\llbracket\nabla u_{h}\right\rrbracket\cdot n|^{2},&\text{if }\mathrm{*}=\mathsf{CR},\\
\int_{T}h^{2}|f|^{2},&\text{if }\mathrm{*}=\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}.\end{cases}

Note that both, the classical method (
4.27
) and the estimators (
4.28
), are not defined for all
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
. More crucially, there are no continuous extensions of their definitions to
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, the classical method (
4.27
)
is not quasi-optimal and the estimators cannot be strictly equivalent; cf.
[
VZ18a
, Remark 4.9]
and
[
KVZ
]
. It therefore comes as no surprise that, if
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

(4.29)

\Est_{*}\lesssim\Est_{\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{*}}\quad*\in\{\mathsf{CR},\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}\}.

In fact, the respective inequalities for the oscillations indicators are already shown in Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
, while those for the indicators of the approximate
\mathring{H}^{1}
-distance and the conforming residual follow from Corollary
4.2
and standard residual techniques. The fact that the dependence of
\Est_{\mathrm{cl,*}}
on
f
does not continuously extend to
H^{-1}(\Omega)
entails that there is a sequence
(f_{k})_{k}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
such that the right-hand side of (
4.29
) tends to
\infty
, while the left-hand side remains bounded or even tends to
0
.

Remark 4.14

(Quasi-optimality and strict estimator equivalence)
.

The quasi-optimality of the nonconforming method is a useful
but not necessary ingredient for the strict equivalence of error
estimators. In fact, the usefulness is clear from
Lemma
3.6
and, to show the non-necessity, we
outline the derivation of a strictly equivalent error estimator for
the classical Crouzeix-Raviart method (
4.27
). Since
the quasi-optimality enters our approach only in Lemma

3.6
, we can generalize the bounds therein by
accepting the additional indicator

\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h})^{\prime}}:=\sup_{w_{h}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h}),\|w_{h}\|\leq 1}\langle\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}},w_{h}\rangle.

This indicator can be computed by solving a discrete problem or quantified by using the correction of a solver with strict error reduction or by a hierarchical approach as illustrated in
[
FV06
]
. Combing this idea, e.g., with Theorem
4.5
and using the notation therein shows that the estimator

\left(\Ncf_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}+\eta_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}+\|\Res_{h}^{\mathtt{C}}\|_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{CR}}({C\!R}_{1})^{\prime}}^{2}+\Osc_{\mathsf{CR}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

is strictly equivalent to the error
\|\nabla_{h}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
of the classical Crouzeix-Raviart method (
4.27
).

4.3.
Discontinuous Galerkin methods of fixed arbitrary order

In this section we apply the abstract framework of Sections
2
and
3
to derive strictly equivalent error estimators for quasi-optimal discontinuous Galerkin methods of fixed arbitrary order. We shall restrict to the symmetric and non-symmetric interior penalty methods in
[
VZ18b
, Section 3.2]
for the ease of presentation, see also Remark
4.21
. To recall them, we use the notation of Section
4.1
.

Given a polynomial degree
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and a stabilization parameter
\sigma
, we define a discrete bilinear form on the possibly discontinuous piecewise polynomials
S_{\ell}^{0}
by

(4.30)

\begin{multlined}a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h}+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\sigma}{h}\left\llbracket u_{h}\right\rrbracket\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket\\
{}+\int_{\Sigma}\theta\left\llbracket u_{h}\right\rrbracket\{\kern-2.24998pt\{\partial_{n}v_{h}\}\kern-2.24998pt\}-\{\kern-2.24998pt\{\partial_{n}u_{h}\}\kern-2.24998pt\}\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket\end{multlined}

with
\theta=1
for the non-symmetric and
\theta=-1
for the symmetric case. In order to ensure coercivity of the bilinear
form, the former case requires
\sigma>0
and the latter
\sigma>\sigma^{*}
for some sufficiently large
\sigma^{*}
depending on the dimension
d
, the shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
and the polynomial degree
\ell
; compare, e.g., with
[
VZ18b
, Lemma 3.5 and (3.23)]
. The discrete problem then reads

(4.31)

\text{for $f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)$},~\text{find $u_{h}\in S_{\ell}^{0}$ such that }\forall v_{h}\in S_{\ell}^{0}\quad a_{h}(u_{h},\,v_{h})=\langle f,\mathcal{E}_{\ell}v_{h}\rangle,

where the smoothing operator
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}:S^{0}_{\ell}\to\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
is defined in
[
VZ18b
, Proposition 3.4]
and satisfies for the following
moment conditions
and
stability properties
: for all
v_{h}\in S^{0}_{\ell}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
,
m_{F}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell-1}(F)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}
,
m_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell-2}(T)

(4.32a)

\displaystyle\int_{F}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v_{h})m_{F}=\int_{F}\{\kern-2.24998pt\{v_{h}\}\kern-2.24998pt\}m_{F},\qquad\int_{T}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v_{h})m_{T}=\int_{T}v_{h}m_{T},

(4.32b)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} h_{T}^{-2}\|v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\ell}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}&+\|\nabla\mathcal{E}_{\ell}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap T\neq\emptyset}\int_{F}\frac{1}{h}|\!\left\llbracket v_{h}\right\rrbracket\!|^{2}.\end{aligned}

Together with the Poisson problem (
4.1
), method (
4.31
) fits into the framework of Section
2

5.
Estimators for the biharmonic problem

In this section, we continue to illustrate the generality of the guidelines in Section
3
for an a posteriori analysis of nonconforming methods. To this end, we consider the fourth-order problem of the two-dimensional biharmonic equation
with clamped boundary conditions:

(5.1)

\Delta\Delta u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad u=\partial_{\mathsf{n}}u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Generally speaking, fourth-order problems are challenging test cases for nonconforming techniques because, in contrast to second-order problems, the conforming subspaces of the underlying discrete spaces may be so small that they are unusable for theoretical or practical purposes. To illustrate how to handle this adverse feature within the aforementioned guidelines, we derive strictly equivalent error estimators for a quasi-optimal
C^{0}
-interior penalty
method of lowest order and a quasi-optimal
Morely
method. For convenience of the reader, we recall the weak formulation of (
5.1
) underlying these methods:

\text{ for }f\in H^{-2}(\Omega)\text{, find $u\in\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)$ such that }\forall v\in\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)\;\int_{\Omega}D^{2}u:D^{2}v=\langle f,v\rangle,

where, in addition to the assumptions and notation of Section
4.1
,

\displaystyle\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)=\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\mid v=\partial_{\mathsf{n}}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},\quad H^{-2}(\Omega)=\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)^{\prime},

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad D^{2}u:D^{2}v=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

5.1.
\mathring{H}^{2}
-conforming quadratics and
H\!C\!T
elements

The piecewise quadratics
S^{0}_{2}
underlie both the Morley method
[
Mor68
]
and the
C^{0}
-interior penalty method
[
BS05
]
of lowest order. In this section, we recall that their
\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)
-conforming subspace
S^{0}_{2}\cap\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)
may be trivial, whence other conforming discrete functions have to be used in the a posteriori error analysis (and in a quasi-optimal method). Furthermore, for the later purpose, we recall properties of the
H^{2}
-conforming
H\!C\!T
elements.

The following example illustrates the well-known fact from
[
dBD83
, Theorem 3]
that smoothness requirements for the approximate may adversely affect their approximation properties in multidimensions.

Example 5.1

(No
\mathring{H}^{2}
-conforming quadratics)
.

Subdivide
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
for a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
into
n^{2}
equal sized squares and obtain a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
by inserting in each square the diagonal parallel to the line
\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then
S_{2}^{0}\cap\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)=\{0\}
. To see this, let
v\in S_{2}^{0}\cap\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)
and consider first the square containing the origin, denoting by
T_{i}
,
i=1,2
, its two triangles. Factorizing
v_{|T_{i}}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}
by means of the boundary conditions yields
v=c_{i}a_{i}^{2}
on
T_{i}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}
,
i=1,2
. The
C^{1}
-transition along the diagonal
\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}\}
implies
c_{1}=c_{2}
and
\nabla a_{1}=\nabla a_{2}
and therefore
v_{|(T_{1}\cup T_{2})}=0
. Inductively repeating this argument for the other squares provides the desired identity
v=0
.

In the Sections
4.2
and
4.3
for the Poisson problem, the operator
\mathcal{A}_{h}
to approximate the distance of conformity

has values in
\mathring{S}^{1}_{\ell}=V_{h}\cap V
. This is algorithmically convenient as there is no need to implement additional shape functions. However, in situations with
V_{h}\cap\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)=\{0\}
as in Example
5.1
, the crucial constant
C_{\mathtt{av}}
in Proposition
3.4
vanishes for
h\to 0
whenever the discrete spaces can approximate a nontrivial exact solution. Indeed, under these assumptions, we have

\displaystyle C_{\mathtt{av}}\|u\|=\lim_{h\to 0}C_{\mathtt{av}}\|\Pi_{V_{h}}u\|=\lim_{h\to 0}C_{\mathtt{av}}\|(\mathcal{A}_{h}-\mathrm{id}_{\widetilde{V}})\Pi_{V_{h}}u\|\leq\lim_{h\to 0}\|u-\Pi_{V_{h}}u\|=0.

Thus, a generic approach for nonconforming quadratics has to employ additional shape functions with a sufficiently large
\mathring{H}^{2}
-conforming subspace.

Conforming discrete functions also play a role in bounding the conforming residual
\Res_{h}^{\texttt{C}}
, namely trough
\mathcal{E}_{h}(V_{h})
in the near orthogonality of Lemma
3.6
. Of course, Example
5.1
excludes also that the smoothing operator

of quasi-optimal methods with piecewise quadratics has values in

. In fact, the smoothers of the quasi-optimal methods in

,
VZ19
]

elements. Since we shall also build here on these elements, we recall them and then prove some useful properties of their nodal basis.

Let

(5.2)

\displaystyle H\!C\!T:=\left\{w_{h}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\mid{w_{h}}_{|T}\in S_{3}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{T}),~T\in\mathcal{T}\right\}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)

-conforming

[

Lemma 5.2

(Properties of

We have

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\Upsilon^{0}_{z}=1\text{ in }\Omega,
where, for all

\operatorname{supp}\Upsilon_{z}^{0}=\omega_{z},\quad h_{z}^{2}\|D^{2}\Upsilon_{z}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\eqsim h_{z}\|\nabla\Upsilon_{z}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\eqsim\|\Upsilon_{z}^{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\eqsim 1.

Furthermore, for all
z\in\mathcal{V}
,
j=1,2
, all

with

, as well as all

,

with

\operatorname{supp}\Upsilon_{z}^{j}=\omega_{z},\quad\|D^{2}\Upsilon_{z}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim 1,\qquad\operatorname{supp}\Upsilon_{F}=\omega_{F},\quad\|D^{2}\Upsilon_{F}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim 1.

All hidden constants only depend on the shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}

Proof.

The functions
\Upsilon_{z}^{0}
,
z\in\mathcal{V}
, form a partition of unity because the function

that equals

in

satisfies
1_{\Omega}\in H\!C\!T
and

in
\Omega

\Upsilon_{z}^{0}
,
z\in\mathcal{V}
, while those for
\Upsilon_{z}^{j}
,
z\in\mathcal{V}
,
j=1,2
, and
\Upsilon_{F}
,

The first two equivalences for

For the third or last equivalence for

with

for

with

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{z}^{0}=\tilde{\Upsilon}_{z}+\frac{3}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T),z\in F}\frac{(z-y_{F})}{|z-y_{F}|^{2}}\cdot(m_{T}-m_{F})\tilde{\Upsilon}_{F}.

Hence the desired equivalence

{}-\int_{\Sigma}\{\kern-2.24998pt\{\partial_{\mathsf{n}}^{2}v_{h}\}\kern-2.24998pt\}\left\llbracket\partial_{\mathsf{n}}w_{h}\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\partial_{\mathsf{n}}v_{h}\right\rrbracket\{\kern-2.24998pt\{\partial^{2}_{\mathsf{n}}w_{h}\}\kern-2.24998pt\}.\end{multlined}

Lemma 5.5

Theorem 5.8

Let

\Est_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}:=\Ncf_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}+C_{1}^{2}\eta_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}+C_{2}^{2}\Osc_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2},\text{ where }\\
\begin{aligned} \Ncf_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}&:=\|u_{h}-\mathcal{A}_{\mathtt{C0}}u_{h}\|_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2},\\
\eta_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}&:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\eta_{\mathtt{C0},T}^{2}\text{ with }\eta_{\mathtt{C0},T}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{V}_{T}^{i}\cup\mathcal{F}_{T}^{i}}\frac{|\langle f,\Upsilon_{K}\rangle-\int_{\Omega}D^{2}_{h}u_{h}:D^{2}\Upsilon_{K}|}{\|D^{2}\Upsilon_{K}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},\\
\Osc_{\mathtt{C0}}^{2}&:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}}\|f-\mathcal{P}_{z}f\|_{H^{-2}(\omega_{z})}^{2},\end{aligned}

swith the averaging operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathtt{C0}}
from (

\mathcal{V}_{T}^{i}=\mathcal{V}^{i}\cap T
,

, the

and

This estimator quantifies the error by

\underline{C}\Est_{\mathtt{C0}}\leq\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathtt{C0}}\leq\overline{C}\Est_{\mathtt{C0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{\mathtt{C0},T}\leq\|D^{2}_{h}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\omega}_{T})}

and the equivalence

,

, the stabilization constant

.

Finally, if

, we have

where the latter is formally of higher order.

5.3.
A quasi-optimal Morley method

In this section, we derive a strictly equivalent error estimator for the quasi-optimal Morley method from
[
VZ19
, Section 4,3]
approximating the two-dimensional biharmonic problem (
5.2
). The estimator is simplified in the spirit of Theorem
4.9
, reducing this time to an approximate distance to
\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)
and data oscillation that is classically bounded and formally of higher order. Again, the simplification is obtained by locally nonconforming simple test functions that are orthogonal to the residual.

In the notation of Section
4.1
, the Morley space
[
Mor68
]
is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {M\!R}:=\bigg\{v_{h}\in S^{0}_{2}&\mid v_{h}\text{ is continuous in $\mathcal{V}^{i}$, }v_{h}=0\text{
in }\mathcal{V}\setminus\mathcal{V}^{i},\\
&\quad\text{and}~\forall F\in\mathcal{F}~\int_{F}\left\llbracket\partial_{\mathsf{n}}v_{h}\right\rrbracket=0\bigg\}\end{aligned}

and the discrete problem reads

(5.17)

\begin{multlined}\text{for $f\in H^{-2}(\Omega)$},~\text{find $u_{h}\in{M\!R}$ such that}\\
\forall v_{h}\in{M\!R}\quad\int_{\Omega}D^{2}_{h}u_{h}:D_{h}^{2}v_{h}=\langle f,\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}\rangle.\end{multlined}

The smoothing operator
\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}:{M\!R}\to\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)
has the following properties:
for all
v_{h}\in{M\!R}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}
,
z\in\mathcal{V}
, and
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

(5.18a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}(z)=v_{h}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{F}\partial_{\mathsf{n}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}=\int_{F}\partial_{\mathsf{n}}v_{h},

(5.18b)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{aligned} h_{T}^{-4}\|v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+h_{T}^{-2}\|\nabla(v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\\
{}+\|D^{2}\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|D^{2}_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{T})}^{2};\end{aligned}\end{aligned}

see
[
VZ19
, Propostition 3.17]
.

We note that the weak formulation of the biharmonic problem (
5.2
) and this method fit into the abstract framework of Section
2
with

(5.19)

\begin{gathered}\widetilde{V}=\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega)+{M\!R}\quad\text{with}\quad V=\mathring{H}^{2}(\Omega),\quad V_{h}={M\!R},\\
\widetilde{a}(v,w)=\int_{\Omega}D^{2}_{h}v:D_{h}^{2}w,\quad\|v\|=\|D^{2}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\\
a_{h}=\widetilde{a}_{|{M\!R}\times{M\!R}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}.\end{gathered}

The conservation (
5.18a
) of point values and face means of the normal derivatives, together with element-wise integration by parts, implies

(5.20)

\displaystyle\forall v_{h},w_{h}\in{M\!R}\quad\int_{\Omega}D_{h}^{2}w_{h}:D_{h}(v_{h}-\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h})=0.

Since both discrete and continuous bilinear forms are restrictions of
\widetilde{a}
, the method is thus overconsistent, i.e. its consistency measure vanishes,
\delta_{h}=0
. Hence, Proposition
2.1
and the stability (
5.18b
) ensure that the method is quasi-optimal with the constant
C_{\mathtt{qo}}=\lvert\!\lvert\!\lvert{\mathcal{E}_{\mathsf{MR}}}\rvert\!\rvert\!\rvert
depending on the shape constant
\gamma_{\mathcal{T}}
.

To apply the abstract a posteriori analysis in Section
3
, we establish its main assumptions. As we proceed similarly to Section
5.2
, we focus on the differences and start with (
H1
) regarding the distance to conformity. Motivated by Section
5.1
, we define a linear and bounded operator

with the help of
H\!C\!T
elements by

(5.21)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{MR}}v_{h}:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{i}}\Big(v_{h}(z)\Upsilon^{0}_{z}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}({v_{h}}_{|T_{z}})(z)\Upsilon^{j}_{z}\Big)+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{i}}\partial_{\mathsf{n}}{v_{h}}(m_{F})\Upsilon_{F},

which in contrast to the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathtt{C0}}
in (

interior penalty method exploits continuity of the normal derivatives in edge midpoints. The next lemma specifies Proposition
3.4
for
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{MR}}

Lemma 5.10

(Approximate
\|D^{2}_{h}\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
-distance to
\mathring{H}^{2}
by averaging)
.

In the setting (

Proof.

The operator

maps

into

\displaystyle\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\llbracket\nabla_{h}v_{h}\right\rrbracket\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\llbracket D^{2}_{h}v_{h}\right\rrbracket\mathsf{t}_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

As in the proof of

\displaystyle\|\left\llbracket D^{2}_{h}v_{h}\right\rrbracket\mathsf{t}_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}=\int_{F}\left\llbracket D^{2}_{h}v_{h}\right\rrbracket\mathsf{t}_{F}\cdot\phi_{F}=\int_{\omega_{F}}D^{2}_{h}(v_{h}-v):\operatorname{Curl}\phi_{F}

,

) and the local projections
\mathcal{P}_{z}
from Lemma

This estimator quantifies the error by

\displaystyle\underline{C}\Est_{\mathsf{MR}}\leq\|D_{h}^{2}(u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\overline{C}\Est_{\mathsf{MR}}.

The equivalence constants

, and

and the tuning constant

Finally, if

, we have

\Osc_{\mathsf{MR}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}h_{T}^{4}\|f\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2},

where the latter is formally of higher order.

6.
A numerical benchmark with rough source term

This section presents some numerical results obtained from the discretization of the Poisson problem (
4.1
), on an open polygon
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with Crouzeix-Raviart finite elements. Our implementation is realized with the library ALBERTA 3.1; see
[
HKK+
,
SS05
]
.

Recall that the Crouzeix-Raviart discretization (
4.27
) without smoothing is defined only for sufficiently smooth source terms like, e.g.,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. This assumption, in turn, implies that the solution of the Poisson problem is in
H^{2}(\Omega)
, provided that
\Omega
is convex. Hence, adaptive mesh refinement can provide a higher error decay rate than uniform refinement only in presence of re-entrant corners, as for the so-called L-shaped or slit domains.

In contrast, the quasi-optimal discretization (
4.6
) is defined for general sources
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
, entailing that
H^{2}(\Omega)
-regularity of the solution cannot be expected in general. Therefore, adaptive mesh refinement can asymptotically outperform the uniform refinement also for convex
\Omega
. We propose one such benchmark and test the ability of the estimators in Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
to quantify the error and to drive adaptive mesh refinement.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
be the unit square. For
\lambda\in(0,1)
, consider the manufactured weak solution of (
4.1
) defined as

(6.1a)

u(x_{1},x_{2}):=\begin{cases}x_{1}(\lambda-x_{1})x_{2}(1-x_{2}),&\text{for $x_{1}\in(0,\lambda)$},\\
(1-x_{1})(x_{1}-\lambda)x_{2}(1-x_{2}),&\text{for $x_{1}\in(\lambda,1)$}.\end{cases}

This function is in
\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and the gradient is discontinuous along the ‘critical’ line
\Lambda=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\mid x_{1}=\lambda\}
. Thus, the corresponding source
f=-\Delta u
in (
4.1
) consists of a regular and a singular component and acts on
v\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
as follows

(6.1b)

\langle f,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\mathrm{reg}}v+\int_{\Lambda}w(x_{2})v(\lambda,x_{2})\mathrm{d}x_{2},

where
w(x_{2}):=x_{2}(1-x_{2})
and
f_{\mathrm{reg}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

(6.1c)

f_{\mathrm{reg}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}2x_{1}(\lambda-x_{1})+2x_{2}(1-x_{2}),&\text{for $x_{1}\in(0,\lambda)$},\\
2(1-x_{1})(x_{1}-\lambda)+2x_{2}(1-x_{2}),&\text{for $x_{1}\in(\lambda,1)$}.\end{cases}

To obtain the initial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, we subdivide
\Omega
into four triangles, by drawing the two main diagonals, cf. Figure
6.2
(A). Each successive mesh
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2},\dots
is obtained from the previous one subdividing each marked triangle into four triangles by bisection and performing completion to preserve conformity. We set
\lambda=\frac{2}{3}
in (
6.1
), to make sure that the critical line
\Lambda
is not contained in the skeleton of any mesh
\mathcal{T}_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, cf. Remark
6.1
below. According to Remarks
3.15
and
3.18
, we replace the data oscillation in the estimators from Theorems
4.5
and
4.9
by the surrogate

(6.2)

\mathrm{sur}_{*,k}^{2}:=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{T}^{2}\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\|f_{\mathrm{reg}}-c\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{k},T\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset}h_{T}^{2}\left(\max_{T\cap\Lambda}w\right)^{2}

where
*\in\{\mathsf{CR},\widetilde{\mathsf{CR}}\}
. The second summand is inspired by
[
BCNV24
, Lemma 7.19]
. For both estimators, we make use of the tuning constants
C_{1}=1
and
C_{2}=0.3
.

Remark 6.1

(Critical line)
.

The position of the line
\Lambda
is crucial in this benchmark. Indeed, if
\Lambda
was contained in the skeleton of, e.g., the initial mesh, then we would have

for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
,

, and we would observe the error decay rate
(\#\mathcal{T}_{k})^{-0.5}
also with uniform refinement. Moreover, we should modify the surrogate oscillation, because the simple functionals in (
4.18b
) would approximate the singular component of the source term on
\Lambda
more accurately than predicted by the latter term in (
6.2
).

Prototype Training with Dual Pseudo-Inverse and Optimized Hidden Activations
Mauro Tucci

Abstract
We present Proto-PINV+H, a fast training paradigm that combines closed-form weight computation with gradient-based optimisation of a small set of synthetic inputs, soft labels, and—crucially—hidden activations. At each iteration we recompute all weight matrices in closed form via two (or more) ridge-regularised pseudo-inverse solves, while updating only the prototypes with Adam. The trainable degrees of freedom are thus shifted from weight space to data/activation space. On MNIST (60k train, 10k test) and Fashion-MNIST (60k train, 10k test), our method reaches 97.8% and 89.3% test accuracy on the official 10k test sets , respectively, in 3.9–4.5 s using approximately 130k trainable parameters and only 250 epochs on an RTX 5060 (16 GB). We provide a multi-layer extension (optimised activations at each hidden stage), learnable ridge parameters, optional PCA/PLS projections, and theory linking the condition number of prototype matrices to generalisation. The approach yields favourable accuracy–speed–size trade-offs against ELM, random-feature ridge, and shallow MLPs trained by back-propagation.



1
Introduction

Back-propagation (BP) is the de facto standard for neural network training but requires full-graph gradient computation and storage of intermediate activations, which increases training time and memory usage. Analytic or semi-analytic methods (e.g. Extreme Learning Machines) reduce runtime by solving linear systems but typically forgo accuracy on modern benchmarks.

Key idea.

We relocate trainable degrees of freedom from the weight space to a compact set of
prototypes
:
synthetic inputs
\mathbf{X}_{p}
, hidden activations
\mathbf{H}_{p}
, and soft labels
\mathbf{Y}_{p}
.
At every iteration, we analytically recompute the weights via ridge-regularised pseudo-inverses, and only update
(\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p},\mathbf{Y}_{p})
with gradient descent. Optimising
hidden activations
is a central novelty that improves expressive power beyond methods that optimise only input prototypes and labels.

Contributions.

•

A dual pseudo-inverse procedure with
optimised hidden activations
(Proto-PINV+H), enabling closed-form weights at every step.

•

A multi-layer extension with analytic inter-layer weights and learnable prototype activations at each hidden stage.

•

Learnable ridge parameters and optional PCA/PLS projections; analysis of numerical stability and complexity independent of dataset size.

•

A theoretical connection between prototype matrix conditioning and generalisation, plus differentiability of the ridge solutions.

•

Strong accuracy–speed–size trade-offs on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with 250-epoch runs on a single consumer GPU (RTX 5060, 16 GB).

2
Background and Preliminaries

2.1
Ridge-regularised least squares

For a design matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and targets
B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
, ridge-regularised least squares solves

\min_{W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}}\ \frac{1}{2}\left\lVert AW-B\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{F}^{2},

whose solution is
W=(A^{\top}A+\lambda I)^{-1}A^{\top}B
. This is numerically stable for
\lambda>0
and differentiable with respect to
A,B,\lambda
via the implicit function theorem. In practice we compute
W
by solving
(A^{\top}A+\lambda I)W=A^{\top}B
with a Cholesky-based solver.

2.2
Differentiability through linear solves

Let
G(\theta)=A(\theta)^{\top}A(\theta)+\lambda(\theta)I
and
R(\theta)=A(\theta)^{\top}B(\theta)
.
Then
W(\theta)=G(\theta)^{-1}R(\theta)
. For a scalar loss
L(W)
,

\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}=-\Tr\!\bigl((G^{-1}R\,\nabla_{W}L^{\top})\,\partial_{\theta}G\bigr)+\Tr\!\bigl(G^{-1}\partial_{\theta}R\,\nabla_{W}L^{\top}\bigr),

where
\nabla_{W}L
is supplied by reverse-mode AD. Modern autodiff libraries expose this without manual Jacobians.

3
Method

3.1
Setup and variables

Given labelled data
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
y_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
we synthesise
N_{p}\ll N
prototypes:

\mathbf{X}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times(d+1)},\quad\mathbf{H}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times h},\quad\mathbf{Y}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times k},

where
\mathbf{X}_{p}
includes a bias column and
\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p})\bigr]=[\mathbf{1},\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p})]
augments the nonlinearity with a bias column.

3.2
Dual pseudo-inverse with optimised activations

Closed-form weights.

For
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{p}+\lambda_{1}I)^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{p},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p})\bigr]^{\!\top}\,\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p})\bigr]+\lambda_{2}I\bigr)^{-1}\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p})\bigr]^{\!\top}\,\mathbf{Y}_{p}.

(2)

Given a real input
x
, predictions are

f_{\theta}(x)=\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\,\sigma\bigl([\mathbf{1},x]\mathbf{W}_{1}\bigr)\,)\bigr]\,\mathbf{W}_{2}.

Objective.

Let
F=[\mathbf{1},X]
be the bias-augmented training data. We minimise

\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{CE}\bigl(\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\,\sigma(F\mathbf{W}_{1})\,)\bigr]\mathbf{W}_{2},\ y\bigr)+\lambda_{3}\bigl(\left\lVert\mathbf{W}_{1}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{W}_{2}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\bigr),

(3)

updating only
\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p},\mathbf{Y}_{p}
(and optionally
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
via
\lambda=\softplus(\rho)
).

3.3
Multi-layer extension

For depths
L\geq 1
with widths
h_{1},\ldots,h_{L}
, introduce prototype activations
\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(l)}
and define

\mathbf{W}_{1}=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{+}\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(1)},\quad\mathbf{W}_{2}=\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(1)})\bigr]^{\!+}\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(2)},\ \ldots,\ \mathbf{W}_{L+1}=\bigl[\mathbf{1},\,\sigma(\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(L)})\bigr]^{\!+}\mathbf{Y}_{p},

where
A^{+}=(A^{\top}A+\lambda I)^{-1}A^{\top}
. Only
\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(l)},\mathbf{Y}_{p}
are trainable; all
\mathbf{W}_{l}
are recomputed in closed form.

3.4
Regularisation, soft labels, and initialisation

Weight penalties.
We use an explicit Frobenius penalty on
\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2}
via
\lambda_{3}
, distinct from ridge terms
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
.

Soft labels.

\mathbf{Y}_{p}
is optimised directly; optionally a temperature
T>0
enforces
\tilde{Y}_{p}=\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{Y}_{p}/T)
.

Initialisation.
Balanced one-hot on
\mathbf{Y}_{p}
often accelerates early training;
\mathbf{X}_{p}
can be random normal or stratified from training samples.
\mathbf{H}_{p}
is random normal.

Decoupled weight decay.
Separate decays for
\mathbf{X}_{p}
and
\mathbf{H}_{p}
give finer control; decay on
\mathbf{Y}_{p}
is often unnecessary with soft labels.

3.5
Dimensionality reduction (optional)

PCA or PLS projects
x\mapsto U^{\top}(x-\mu)
to
d^{\prime}\ll d
before prototype optimisation. After training, we back-project
\mathbf{W}_{1}
to original pixels; the mean shift is absorbed by the bias.

3.6
Complexity and stability

Each epoch solves small ridge systems on
N_{p}
prototypes and backpropagates only through
\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p},\mathbf{Y}_{p}
. The cost scales as
\mathcal{O}(N_{p}h^{2})
plus
\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
for linear solves, independent of
N
. Cholesky-based solvers with positive
\lambda
ensure numerical stability; SVD can be used as a fallback in highly rank-deficient regimes.

4
Theory

4.1
Stability of ridge solutions and conditioning

Lemma 1

(Ridge sensitivity)
.

Let
W_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p})=(\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{p}+\lambda_{1}I)^{-1}\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{p}
.
Then, for small perturbations
\Delta\mathbf{X}_{p},\Delta\mathbf{H}_{p}
,

\left\lVert\Delta W_{1}\right\rVert_{F}\ \leq\ C_{1}(\lambda_{1},\mathbf{X}_{p},\mathbf{H}_{p})\,\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{X}_{p}\right\rVert_{F}+C_{2}(\lambda_{1},\mathbf{X}_{p})\,\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{H}_{p}\right\rVert_{F},

where
C_{1}
scales with
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{X}_{p})/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}_{p})
and
C_{2}
with
1/\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}_{p})
.

Proposition 1

(Generalisation via condition number)
.

Assume the loss is 1-Lipschitz in
\mathbf{W}_{1}
. Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\mathcal{E}(\hat{f})\ \leq\ \hat{\mathcal{E}}(\hat{f})+\frac{\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{X}_{p})}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{X}_{p})}\cdot\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2N}},

where
C
depends on label norms and activation Lipschitz constants.

Sketch.
The ridge sensitivity factors bound deviations in
\mathbf{W}_{1}
through
\kappa(\mathbf{X}_{p})
. A standard Rademacher argument yields the sample term. Optimising prototypes to reduce
\kappa(\mathbf{X}_{p})
thus tightens the bound.

4.2
Differentiating through the closed-form

Let
G=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{p}+\lambda_{1}I
and
R=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{p}
. With
W_{1}=G^{-1}R
, for any scalar loss
L
,

\partial L=\Tr\!\bigl((\partial R)^{\top}U\bigr)-\Tr\!\bigl((\partial G)^{\top}V\bigr),\quad U=G^{-\top}\nabla_{W_{1}}L,\ V=G^{-\top}(R\nabla_{W_{1}}L^{\top}),

which backprop engines realise via linear solves. Analogous expressions hold for
W_{2}
.

5.4
Ablations and practical findings

Prototype count.
150 prototypes suffice for MNIST; Fashion benefits modestly from more prototypes due to class similarity.

Hidden width.
Accuracy improves up to
h\approx 768
, then saturates.

Learnable ridge.
Learning
\lambda_{2}
improves Fashion by 0.2–0.3 pp;
\lambda_{1}
is often more stable fixed.

Schedulers.
Cosine with 20-epoch warm-up reduces time to best by roughly half compared with a constant LR.

Dropout on
\mathbf{H}_{p}
.
Small
p
may smooth early training but often reduces final accuracy.

Weight decay.
Separate decays for
\mathbf{X}_{p}
and
\mathbf{H}_{p}
are preferable; decay on
\mathbf{Y}_{p}
generally unnecessary with soft labels.

Prototype allocation.
Starting from balanced one-hot labels, optimisation reallocates capacity to hard classes; observed counts align with class ambiguity.

7
Discussion and Limitations

Proto-PINV+H offers a favourable speed–size–accuracy Pareto frontier for shallow architectures. It is particularly attractive when rapid re-training or on-device constraints matter. Limitations include vectorised inputs (convolutional stems remain future work), potential error accumulation in very deep analytic stacks, and the need for careful regularisation choices when
N_{p}
is very small.

8
Conclusion

Optimising hidden activations together with prototype inputs and labels, while keeping weights in closed form, yields fast and accurate shallow networks. Future directions include convolutional stems, continual learning with expandable prototype banks, extensions to regression and multi-label settings, and tighter generalisation analyses.

Appendix A
Proof Sketches and Technical Notes

Stability of
W_{1}
.

Let
G=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{p}+\lambda_{1}I
and
R=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{p}
. Then
W_{1}=G^{-1}R
. By first-order perturbation

\Delta W_{1}\approx-G^{-1}(\Delta G)W_{1}+G^{-1}\Delta R.

Bounding
\left\lVert G^{-1}\right\rVert\leq 1/\sigma_{\min}(G)
and expanding
\Delta G=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\Delta\mathbf{X}_{p}+(\Delta\mathbf{X}_{p})^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{p}
,
\Delta R=\mathbf{X}_{p}^{\top}\Delta\mathbf{H}_{p}+(\Delta\mathbf{X}_{p})^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{p}
, yields the stated dependence on
\kappa(\mathbf{X}_{p})
.

Differentiability w.r.t.
\lambda
.

Write
W_{1}(\lambda_{1})=G(\lambda_{1})^{-1}R
. Then

\frac{dW_{1}}{d\lambda_{1}}=-G^{-1}\frac{dG}{d\lambda_{1}}G^{-1}R=-G^{-1}G^{-1}R.

With
\lambda=\softplus(\rho)
, positivity and differentiability are guaranteed.

Back-projection under PCA/PLS.

If
x\mapsto z=U^{\top}(x-\mu)
and we train on
z
with bias, the learned
W_{1}^{(z)}
lifts back as

\mathbf{W}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\mu&U\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\text{bias row of }W_{1}^{(z)}\\
\text{non-bias rows of }W_{1}^{(z)}\end{bmatrix},

so that
[\mathbf{1},x]\mathbf{W}_{1}=[\mathbf{1},z]\mathbf{W}_{1}^{(z)}
. The mean is absorbed by the bias.

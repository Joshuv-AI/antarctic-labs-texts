Knowledge, attitude, and practice toward delayed onset of lactogenesis among postpartum women experiencing mother-infant separation: a multi-center cross-sectional study
Authors: Xi Wang, Jing He, Xianmei Cui, Yifang Zheng, Yiwei Li, Yajie Shi, Ru Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDelayed onset of lactogenesis (DOL) is a significant barrier to successful breastfeeding, particularly for women experiencing mother–infant separation due to preterm birth or neonatal complications. This separation disrupts crucial early breastfeeding triggers. Understanding maternal knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) toward DOL is essential for improving outcomes. However, few studies have explored this relationship, especially in China where such separation is common. This study aimed to investigate the KAP toward DOL among postpartum women experiencing mother–infant separation and to examine the associations between these dimensions.MethodsThis multicenter cross-sectional survey was conducted in 11 tertiary hospitals in China from June to December 2023. We included 528 mothers over 20 years old who intended to breastfeed and experienced mother–infant separation requiring NICU admission. We assessed KAP toward DOL using a validated, self-designed questionnaire. Data were collected at or beyond 72 h postpartum, allowing participants to reflect on their early lactation experience.ResultsA total of 528 valid questionnaires were analyzed (mean age: 30.08 ± 5.14 years), with 321 individuals (60.8%) suffering from DOL. The mean scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were 14.02 ± 5.04 (range: 0–26), 15.38 ± 5.02 (range: 6–30), and 27.73 ± 8.98 (range: 9–45), respectively. Multivariate logistic regression showed that a positive attitude (OR 1.37; 95% CI 1.29, 1.45), premature birth (OR 1.79; 95% CI 1.13, 2.83), and mother–infant separation for 1–3 days (OR 2.39; 95% CI 1.31, 4.37) or 4–7 days (OR 2.49; 95% CI 1.36, 4.54) were independently associated with breastfeeding practice. A structural equation model (SEM) confirmed that attitude significantly influenced practice (β = 0.90, P < 0.001).ConclusionsOur findings underscore the importance of fostering positive maternal attitudes through structured lactation support from healthcare providers. Background

Breastfeeding is a crucial public health strategy that offers significant benefits for both maternal and infant health, which are essential for reducing infant morbidity and mortality from various infectious diseases [1–4]. The early initiation and establishment of breastfeeding postpartum are also vital for successful breastfeeding outcomes [2, 3]. Optimal breastfeeding practice includes the early initiation of breastfeeding within one hour of birth, exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months, and continued breastfeeding for at least two years [2, 4].

Lactogenesis II refers to the onset of copious milk production, typically occurring within 24–72 h after delivery. Delayed onset of lactogenesis (DOL) occurs when this process is delayed beyond 72 h postpartum, and it is often characterized by maternal perceptions of breast hardness, fullness, or colostrum leakage [5]. Recognizing and addressing challenges during this period is crucial for promoting sustained and successful breastfeeding. A meta-analysis involving 14 observational studies and 17,610 females found that the prevalence of DOL is approximately 31% [6, 7]. It is associated with shorter breastfeeding duration [1]. Common strategies to prevent or manage DOL include initiating breastfeeding early, increasing breastfeeding frequency, and minimizing mother-infant separation. Separating the mother and infant not only reduces breastfeeding rates but also deprives them of the benefits of stimulating hormones and bonding, posing risks of malnutrition and even death [1, 8, 9].

The Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice (KAP) model, a cornerstone in public health research, posits that individual behaviors are influenced by one’s knowledge and attitude [10, 11]. Understanding the perspectives and challenges of these women is vital for enhancing medical care strategies, increasing the success rate of early lactogenesis, and providing comprehensive support, thus ensuring their active participation in the breastfeeding process. In China, mother–infant separation is still common for infants admitted to the NICU, which may increase the risk of DOL [12]. However, little is known about the KAP of mothers who experience such separation, and this study applied the KAP model to explore their breastfeeding-related behaviors. Maternal-infant separation, often resulting from NICU hospitalization or perinatal complications, is regarded as a significant adverse life event that disrupts maternal bonding, delays lactation, and increases emotional distress [8, 9]. This population faces distinct challenges in initiating and maintaining breastfeeding, particularly due to the disruption of early mother-infant contact. Existing research often overlooks the impact of NICU-related separation on lactation outcomes. In China, where NICU admission for preterm or ill neonates is common, breastfeeding practices may be further complicated by limited postpartum support and inconsistent hospital-based lactation guidance. These challenges highlight a critical need to explore how knowledge, attitudes, and practices relate to DOL in this specific context. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the levels of KAP toward DOL among postpartum women experiencing mother-infant separation due to NICU admission. In addition to identifying key influencing factors, the study further explored the interrelationships among the three KAP dimensions, including the association between knowledge and attitude, knowledge and practice, and attitude and practice.

Methods

Study design and participants

This multicenter cross-sectional study was conducted at multiple tertiary hospitals in China, including Tongji Hospital (Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology), Peking University Third Hospital, Peking University Hospital, Chongqing First Affiliated Hospital (Chongqing Medical University), Wuhan Union Hospital, Shanxi Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital, The First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University, Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, Shanghai Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital, West China Hospital, and Shanxi Bethune Hospital between June and December, 2023. This study specifically focused on cases of mother–infant separation due to preterm birth or neonatal complications requiring NICU admission, which was defined as the physical separation of the mother and infant resulting from the infant’s transfer to the NICU immediately after birth. All recruited mothers had their preterm infants (gestational age between 28 weeks and 36 weeks + 6 days) admitted to NICU, leading to unavoidable separation. In some cases, separation occurred immediately after birth due to maternal complications (e.g., preeclampsia, eclampsia, or postpartum shock) or neonatal conditions (e.g., respiratory distress, infection, or severe jaundice), preventing any initial contact or breastfeeding. In other cases, newborns were temporarily roomed-in with their mothers before being transferred to the NICU, allowing brief opportunities for skin-to-skin contact and breastfeeding initiation. Although we did not collect data on parity status (i.e., whether mothers were primiparous or multiparous), all participants were experiencing mother-infant separation for the first time, and their knowledge, attitudes, and practices were assessed specifically in this context. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age above 20 years; (2) singleton pregnancy; (3) willingness to breastfeed and actual initiation of breastfeeding postpartum; (4) first-time experience of mother–infant separation due to infant’s NICU admission; (5) with basic literacy and normal cognition. The exclusion criteria were: (1) conception achieved through assisted reproductive technology (e.g., in vitro fertilization or other non-natural conception methods); (2) twin or multiple pregnancies; (3) complications associated with pregnancy or childbirth, such as amniotic fluid embolism, uterine rupture, postpartum shock, postpartum disseminated intravascular coagulation, etc.; (4) presence of contraindications to breastfeeding; (5) presence of pregnancy complications, comorbidities, or concurrent infectious diseases; (6) presence of physical or mental disorders; (7) newborn malformations or other illnesses. Ethical approval for the research was granted by the Shanxi Bethune Hospital Ethics Committee (YXLL-2024-042). All mothers were fully informed about the purpose of the study and the potential impact of mother-infant separation on lactation. The informed consent process included detailed information about: (1) the medical necessity of NICU admission for preterm infants, (2) potential challenges in establishing breastfeeding during separation, and (3) available support services for maintaining milk production. Participation was entirely voluntary and participants could withdraw at any time. All procedures were performed in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments.

NICU setting and antenatal education

The NICUs in participating hospitals are designed as enclosed negative-pressure wards, managed by multidisciplinary teams including neonatologists and nurses who are all specially trained to work in the NICU. These units follow strict environmental controls regarding temperature, humidity, and infection prevention, using a “three zones and two channels” model to ensure optimal care for premature, low-birth-weight, or seriously ill neonates.

While most NICU areas are closed to mothers to minimize infection risks, some units have recently established transitional maternal-infant wards for newborns scheduled to be discharged within a week. In these areas, mothers are allowed to stay and participate in adaptive training related to infant care and breastfeeding preparation.

In addition, all maternity hospitals in the study region provide standardized antenatal education through hospital-affiliated “maternity schools.” Upon registration, pregnant women are invited to join WeChat or QQ groups where they receive regular notifications to attend courses. These sessions cover topics such as breast development, breastfeeding techniques, timing, posture, and the importance of early initiation. Similar sessions are also offered to spouses to enhance family support and breastfeeding success.

Because the development of milk banks in China is still nascent and faces significant challenges, including high operational costs, inconsistent donation volumes, and a lack of nationwide support policies. Consequently, infant formula is the default feeding option for newborns in this study when maternal milk is not yet available, especially in NICU settings.

Questionnaire development

Preceding the primary investigation, a pilot test involving a cohort of 108 participants was administered, yielding a reliability coefficient of 0.827, indicating good internal consistency. Each item was evaluated based on item-total correlation and internal consistency analysis. Items with low item-total correlation or ambiguity in participant responses were revised or removed prior to the final questionnaire.

The questionnaire was developed through a systematic process including literature review, expert consultation, and pilot testing. The initial item pool was generated based on comprehensive literature review and validated by a panel of five experts in maternal health and lactation. Content validity was established through expert review, with a content validity index of 0.85.

The questionnaire was first pilot-tested with 108 participants, showing good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.827).

The final questionnaire, presented in Chinese, encompasses four dimensions: demographic characteristics, knowledge, attitude, and practice dimensions: (1) the demographic characteristics part included variables such as age, gender, height, weight, residence, education, employment, marital status, delivery method, premature birth or not, pregnancy-induced hypertension, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), mother-infant separation duration, gestational weight gain, and the occurrence of DOL; (2) the knowledge dimension assessed the understanding of concepts related to mother-infant separation and DOL, comprised of 13 questions, where responses indicating being “very familiar” were assigned 2 points, “heard about” responses were assigned 1 point, and “not clear” responses were assigned 0 points, thereby encompassing a potential score range from 0 to 26 points; (3) the attitude dimension comprised of 6 items, assessing attitude toward mitigating DOL, associated anxiety and other negative emotions, and perspectives on intervention strategies. Responses are scored on a five-point Likert scale, ranging from “Strongly disagree” (0 points) to “Strongly agree” (5 points), with a total score range of 6–30 points; (4) the practice dimension, including 9 questions, evaluates consistency in practice like expressing milk, breast massage, monitoring pumping volume, dietary attention, and ensuring adequate sleep. This section uses a five-point Likert scale, ranging from “always” (5 points) to “never” (1 point), and has a total score range of 9–45 points. Adequate knowledge, positive attitude, and proactive practice were defined for this study as scores exceeding 50% of the maximum possible score in each respective section [13]. For interpretive purposes, practice scores were further described qualitatively (e.g., “moderate” or “good” adherence) based on overall score distribution and central tendency. Specifically, scores around the median or within the middle tertile range were described as “moderate” while higher scores approaching the maximum were referred to as “good” adherence.

DOL was defined and assessed in this study as the absence of the onset of copious milk production within 72 h postpartum. This was measured using a self-reported questionnaire item: “Did you experience the onset of copious milk production (breast fullness and increased milk volume) within 72 hours postpartum?” (Item 16). Participants were asked to report the timing of first experiencing breast fullness and the initial noticeable increase in milk volume. Based on the responses, DOL was coded as “yes” if the onset occurred after 72 h, and “no” otherwise [6, 7].

Questionnaire data collection

After the questionnaire was finalized, data collection was conducted by trained research nurses who had received standardized training on questionnaire administration and maternal–infant separation care. Data were collected through a combination of face-to-face interviews and digital platforms, including WeChat and QQ groups. Each participant was approached during their hospital stay or follow-up visits, and questionnaires were administered either through face-to-face interviews or via secure online platforms (WeChat and QQ groups) for those who had been discharged. All questionnaires were completed at or beyond 72 h postpartum, once participants had stabilized and were able to reflect on their early breastfeeding experience.

Maternal-infant separation definition and survey implementation

The study population included two groups: mothers whose newborns were directly transferred to the neonatal department for continued treatment due to premature birth or certain diseases during hospitalization, and mothers who experienced maternal-infant separation in the past and were surveyed remotely. All in-hospital questionnaires were completed under the direct supervision of certified breastfeeding instructor nurses. For mothers who participated remotely after discharge, instructions were provided in advance via phone or secure messaging platforms, and they completed the questionnaires independently. Proxy completion by family members was not permitted in any case. To ensure that all participants had passed the initial postpartum phase and could accurately reflect on lactation-related experiences, data collection was conducted at or beyond 72 h after birth. All mothers, regardless of the timing or cause of separation, received individualized breastfeeding education and counseling during their hospital stay. This included training on breast stimulation, manual expression, breast pump usage, and milk storage, tailored to their clinical conditions and the care needs of their infants.

This survey was conducted through collaboration with multiple tertiary-level hospitals across China. Participating institutions included tertiary hospitals, which typically admit pregnant women with high-risk or complicated conditions, as well as specialized maternal and child health hospitals that primarily serve women with normal deliveries. All selected hospitals were required to have certified obstetrics departments, neonatal intensive care units (NICUs), and established breastfeeding counseling services. Shanxi Bethune Hospital served as the lead coordinating institution.

Statistical analysis

The statistical software used is SPSS 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous data were presented as mean and standard deviation (SD), while categorical data were expressed as n (%). Differences in knowledge (K), attitude (A), and practice (P) scores among subjects with different demographic characteristics were compared using independent sample one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis H test was employed for inter-group comparisons. Spearman correlation analysis was utilized to explore the correlation between knowledge, attitude, and practice scores. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis were employed to investigate the risk factors for Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice. Structural equation model (SEM) was utilized to elucidate the interrelationships between the Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice. The study explored the following relationships: (1) the association between knowledge levels and attitudes toward DOL, (2) the correlation between knowledge and breastfeeding practices, and (3) the relationship between attitudes and actual practices among postpartum women experiencing mother-infant separation. A two-sided P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 528 valid questionnaires were included in the final analysis (mean age: 30.08 ± 5.14 years), and 321 individuals (60.8%) experienced DOL. All participants completed the questionnaire at or beyond 72 h postpartum to ensure the accuracy of recall regarding lactation onset and related experiences. Regarding feeding practices during mother-infant separation, all mothers initiated breastfeeding through indirect methods such as breast pumping or hand expression. They were instructed to express milk every 2–3 h to stimulate lactation. Once DOL began and breast engorgement occurred, mothers adjusted the expression frequency according to their comfort and milk production status. Infants admitted to the NICU typically received formula milk initially. Once maternal milk supply was established and reviewed by neonatologists (including maternal medical status and infant tolerance), expressed breast milk was transported daily to the NICU for infant feeding. Direct breastfeeding was not possible during separation but was encouraged after discharge. Approximately one-third of the infants were full-term newborns transferred to the NICU due to conditions such as jaundice or infection; the remaining two-thirds were preterm infants admitted immediately after birth. The mean BMI was 22.77 ± 4.11 kg/m2, 230 (43.6%) lived in urban area, 254 (48.1%) had bachelor’s degree, 245 (46.4%) were employed, 443 (83.9%) were married, 270 (51.1%) were caesarean section, 287 (54.4%) had premature birth, 324 (61.4%) had pregnancy-induced hypertension, 295 (55.9%) had GDM, 218 (41.3%) were separated from their baby for over 4 days, 254 (67.0%) gained 20 kg or less during pregnancy (Table 1).

Knowledge, attitude and practice dimensions

The overall KAP assessment showed varying levels of understanding of and adherence to practices related to DOL among participants. The mean knowledge score was 14.02 ± 5.04 (possible range: 0–26), representing moderate level of understanding about DOL. The mean attitude score was 15.38 ± 5.02 (possible range: 6–30), indicating generally positive attitudes toward DOL management. The mean practice score was 27.73 ± 8.98 (possible range: 9–45), suggesting moderate adherence based on score distribution relative to the maximum possible score. Knowledge scores were significantly higher among participants who lived in urban areas (P = 0.016), had premature births (P = 0.022), and experienced shorter separation periods (1–3 days) from their infants (P = 0.029). Attitude scores showed significant association with marital status, with married participants demonstrating more positive attitudes (P = 0.032). Practice scores were notably higher among participants who underwent cesarean section (P = 0.014) (Table 1).

In the knowledge dimension, participants showed highest familiarity with “Factors contributing to DOL include maternal physiological factors such as excessive weight gain during pregnancy, obesity, or complications during pregnancy” (K7, 42.99% “very familiar”). The highest level of partial knowledge was observed for “Traditional Chinese nursing techniques such as acupuncture massage, breast massage, and ear acupuncture can help clear mammary ducts” (K13, 44.13% “heard about”). Notably, 34.85% were “not clear” about “Using breast pumps, hand expression, and breast massage helps increase milk production” (K12). Attitudes toward DOL showed considerable variation. Regarding maternal role perception (A1), 23.11% felt DOL would affect their maternal duty performance, 25.19% were neutral, and 21.78% disagreed. A significant proportion (26.7%) questioned the effectiveness of standardized antenatal check-ups for managing DOL (A3). However, most participants recognized the importance of breastfeeding and colostrum (A5), with 26.33% strongly agreeing and 29.73% agreeing. Practice patterns revealed varying levels of adherence to recommended behaviors. While 31.06% regularly consulted healthcare professionals about DOL (P1), and 25.57% sought professional assistance for breast massage (P5), other essential practices showed lower adherence. Only 24.62% maintained adequate sleep (P7), 26.7% performed occasional breast self-massage during separation (P4), and notably, 26.52% never maintained records of milk expression frequency and volume (P6) (Supplementary Table 1).

Associations between Knowledge, Attitude, and practice components

Before examining correlations between KAP components, we stratified analyses by DOL status to identify potential differences in relationships. Among all participants, attitude was positively associated with practice (r = 0.687, P < 0.001), indicating that women with more favorable attitudes toward DOL management tended to engage in more proactive breastfeeding practices. When stratified by DOL status, a surprising negative association was found: in both groups (with and without DOL), higher attitude scores correlated with lower reported practice scores (r = -0.251, P < 0.001). Knowledge showed no significant correlation with either attitudes or practices across all analyses (Table 2).

Predictors of Knowledge, Attitude, and practice toward DOL

Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified several predictors for KAP toward DOL. For the knowledge dimension, urban residence was positively associated with higher knowledge scores (OR 1.650; 95% CI 1.157, 2.351), while being separated from the infant for more than 7 days was negatively associated with knowledge (OR 0.347; 95% CI 0.196, 0.617). For attitude, separation from the infant for 4–7 days (OR 0.510; 95% CI 0.314, 0.828) was negatively associated with attitude scores, whereas experiencing DOL was positively associated with more favorable attitudes toward lactation management (OR 1.518; 95% CI 1.043, 2.209). For practice, multiple factors showed significant associations. Positive attitude had a strong positive association with improved practice (OR 1.368; 95% CI 1.294, 1.447). Additionally, premature birth (OR 1.788; 95% CI 1.130, 2.830), separation for 1–3 days (OR 2.392; 95% CI 1.308, 4.372), and separation for 4–7 days (OR 2.488; 95% CI 1.363, 4.539) were each positively associated with higher reported practice scores (Table 3).

Discussion

The study revealed a notable alignment between sufficient knowledge, positive attitude, and proactive practice toward DOL among postpartum women facing mother-infant separation, and attitude had effect on practice. Clinical interventions should focus on improving hospital practices to support lactation among women experiencing mother–infant separation. These may include providing education on early and frequent breast expression using high-quality breast pumps or skilled hand expression techniques, and reducing separation by allowing mothers to have earlier and more frequent access to their infants in the NICU.

To improve KAP outcomes in the NICU setting, targeted interventions should be considered. These include structured education sessions focusing on breast milk expression techniques (such as hand expression and use of breast pumps), personalized lactation counseling during hospitalization, and the provision of emotional support to reduce the psychological burden caused by separation. In addition, enhancing maternal-infant contact through transition rooms, flexible visitation policies, and the routine use of breast milk expression records can help close the gap between maternal intention and actual feeding practice. Adapting NICU protocols to support more family-centered and responsive care may promote maternal engagement and lead to more favorable breastfeeding outcomes.

This study found disparities in KAP based on socio-demographic variables such as residence, marital status, and delivery mode. The observed association between marital status and attitude scores echoes findings from studies emphasizing the role of social support networks in breastfeeding duration [14]. Interestingly, mothers who underwent cesarean section demonstrated higher practice scores in our study, which contrasts with previous findings reporting that cesarean delivery often delays the initiation and reduces the duration of breastfeeding [15]. This unexpected pattern may reflect increased medical supervision and structured postoperative care following cesarean delivery, during which mothers receive more targeted lactation guidance and support from healthcare professionals. Such additional support and education during hospitalization may contribute to better adherence to recommended breastfeeding practices, despite the typical physiological delays associated with surgical delivery. The logistic regression analysis further substantiates these disparities, highlighting urban residence and prolonged infant separation as key predictors of improved knowledge and practice outcomes. This resonates with findings in broader literature, previous studies emphasize the influence of socio-demographic factors on maternal health knowledge and practice [16, 17]. In response to the disparities identified across different residential areas, marital statuses, and delivery methods, community-level interventions are recommended, especially in rural and non-urban areas. Face-to-face educational and counseling services could be provided through mobile health services and community centers. Government and non-governmental organizations should fund the establishment of mobile maternal-child health education units in remote and rural areas, offering customized health information and support to postpartum women in these communities.

Interestingly, some findings in our study appeared to be counterintuitive. Mothers who experienced preterm births demonstrated higher knowledge scores, while those who reported DOL showed more positive attitudes toward lactation management. Similarly, women who encountered adverse events such as preterm birth or prolonged separation tended to have higher practice scores. These results may reflect the effect of increased exposure to medical care and breastfeeding counseling among high-risk mothers. Mothers of preterm infants often receive intensive medical attention and targeted lactation guidance during their extended hospital stays, which may enhance their awareness and knowledge of breastfeeding management. Likewise, mothers who experience DOL may develop stronger motivation and resilience through problem-focused coping, leading to more favorable attitudes. Furthermore, repeated professional counseling and hands-on lactation training during NICU hospitalization could explain the higher practice scores observed among mothers with preterm births or prolonged separation. Collectively, these findings suggest that enhanced medical engagement and experience with lactation challenges may paradoxically strengthen maternal competence and practice despite adverse circumstances.

These counterintuitive patterns may also highlight the compensatory role of clinical support and psychological adaptation. Mothers facing early complications often receive more intensive professional guidance and emotional counseling, which may transform stress into proactive coping behaviors. In contrast, women without such challenges may have fewer opportunities for targeted lactation education. Thus, what appears as a paradox likely reflects a system-level compensatory effect, where greater adversity triggers stronger medical and educational intervention, leading to improved practical outcomes despite initial disadvantage.

The correlation analysis revealed an intriguing pattern in the relationship between knowledge, attitude, and practice. This unusual correlational pattern may reflect the complex moderating role of DOL in maternal lactation behavior. Women experiencing DOL may develop compensatory psychological responses to lactation challenges, resulting in more positive attitudes despite practical difficulties, thus creating a negative attitude-behavior association. Conversely, women without DOL may maintain more conservative attitudes, more cautious or traditional views toward breastfeeding and lactation management, while experiencing fewer practical barriers. The SEM results corroborate this, indicating that while knowledge does not directly influence practice, attitude does. Given the positive correlation between attitude and practice, medical service providers should adopt positive communication strategies, focusing on attitude change to improve practice outcomes. This can be achieved through motivational interviewing techniques, which have been shown to effectively alter health-related attitudes and behaviors [18], and to improve breastfeeding outcomes in postpartum women [19]. Training programs for healthcare professionals should incorporate motivational interviewing techniques, aimed at enhancing maternal attitude towards breastfeeding and lactation management, particularly in the context of mother-infant separation [20, 21]. However, the contrasting correlations observed in stratified analyses suggest that healthcare providers should adopt a more nuanced approach. While maintaining positive communication strategies, there is a clear need to focus on practical support and expectation management, particularly for mothers experiencing or at risk of DOL. This comprehensive approach should emphasize hands-on practical training, realistic goal-setting, and enhanced support systems to bridge the gap between positive attitudes and actual practice capabilities. The finding that knowledge alone shows no significant correlation with practice further reinforces the importance of focusing on practical skills and emotional support rather than solely on knowledge transmission.

The distribution of knowledge responses points to a widespread lack of clarity and understanding among postpartum women regarding critical aspects of lactogenesis. This deficiency in knowledge is a significant barrier to effective lactation management and mirrors findings in the literature, previous studies highlighted the need for improved lactation education among new mothers [22, 23]. To address the evident knowledge gaps, the creation of targeted educational content is essential. This content should be clear, concise, and specifically address common misconceptions about lactogenesis. Develop a series of short, informative videos and infographics that demystify lactogenesis, making them accessible via online platforms and in healthcare settings. These resources should use layman’s terms and include practical tips for recognizing and managing DOL.

The varied responses in the attitude dimension signal the necessity for more nuanced, individualized approaches to modifying maternal attitude toward lactation and breastfeeding. This need for personalized strategies is supported by previous research, which found that tailored messaging significantly influences maternal attitude and decisions [24, 25]. Considering the varied attitude towards lactogenesis, personalized counseling sessions are recommended. These sessions should address individual concerns and misconceptions, fostering more positive attitude toward lactation management. Healthcare providers should be trained in delivering personalized counseling, focusing on individual beliefs and cultural practice, to effectively change attitude toward DOL and delayed breastfeeding [26, 27].

The responses in the practice dimension reveal inconsistent adoption of beneficial lactation practice among postpartum women, indicating potential barriers such as lack of skill, knowledge, or motivation. The inconsistent adoption of beneficial lactation practice calls for practical skill-building workshops. These workshops should teach techniques such as breast massage, hand expression, and the effective use of breast pumps. Organize hands-on training sessions led by experienced lactation consultants in both hospital settings and community centers, ensuring that postpartum women have the skills and confidence to manage lactation effectively, particularly during periods of mother-infant separation [28].

This study illuminates the challenges faced by postpartum women experiencing mother-infant separation, specifically in terms of their KAP related to the DOL. Our findings reveal a complex relationship between knowledge, attitudes, and practices, particularly when considering DOL status. The negative correlation between attitudes and practices among both DOL and non-DOL mothers suggests that positive attitudes alone may not overcome practical barriers to breastfeeding during separation. This finding challenges the traditional assumption of a straightforward positive relationship between favorable attitudes and better practices, highlighting the need for more nuanced interventions that address both psychological and practical aspects of breastfeeding support during mother-infant separation. These findings are pivotal, as they underscore a substantial gap in maternal health education and support. Other studies have similarly identified the critical role of targeted educational interventions in improving maternal knowledge and practice concerning postpartum care [7, 29]. Considering the identified deficiencies in knowledge, attitude, and practice among postpartum women experiencing mother-infant separation, it is recommended that healthcare institutions develop specialized educational toolkits. These should include easy-to-understand informational booklets, interactive online courses, and focused workshops addressing the specific needs of these women. Additionally, establishing support groups can provide a platform for experience sharing and mutual learning. Healthcare institutions should collaborate with lactation experts and experienced breastfeeding consultants to develop educational materials and seminars aimed at preventing or reducing DOL in cases of mother–infant separation, by minimizing separation and providing skilled support to encourage early and frequent milk expression [30]. These recommendations are supported by prior systematic reviews and global guidelines demonstrating that structured education, professional counseling, and peer support interventions significantly improve breastfeeding initiation and continuation rates [31, 32].

Prolonged mother–infant separation also warrants further attention. Extended separation periods, although sometimes medically necessary, may disrupt key physiological and hormonal changes essential for lactation establishment — particularly the oxytocin- and prolactin-mediated hormonal responses that trigger milk synthesis and ejection, as well as maternal–infant sensory and emotional bonding cues that reinforce these neuroendocrine processes. Our findings that prolonged separation was associated with both improved knowledge and practice may reflect increased clinical engagement and targeted education in such high-risk contexts. However, these results also underscore the need for systemic strategies aimed at minimizing unnecessary separation whenever possible. Hospitals should strengthen family-centered care policies to allow earlier and more frequent maternal access to the infant in the NICU, provide high-quality breast pumps, and ensure individualized lactation support to mitigate the negative physiological and psychological effects of separation. Such measures could reduce the incidence of DOL and improve long-term breastfeeding outcomes.

However, there were still several limitations in this study. Firstly, data on parity (primiparous vs. multiparous status) were not collected, which may have confounded the results, as DOL is more common among primiparous women and this could not be adjusted for in the analysis. Secondly, it relied on self-reported data through questionnaires, which may introduce response bias or recall bias, affecting the accuracy of the results. The self-reported nature of DOL status might have led to classification errors, as some mothers may have had difficulty accurately recognizing the onset of lactogenesis. Additionally, our questionnaire, while showing good internal consistency, would benefit from further validation studies to establish its reliability across different populations and settings. Moreover, detailed quantitative data on infant feeding methods during separation (e.g., use of formula, expressed milk, or mixed feeding) were not collected, which may limit the ability to fully contextualize maternal practices. Thirdly, the study was conducted in a specific region, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to a broader population. Lastly, the cross-sectional design does not allow for establishing causal relationships between variables. Despite these limitations, the strengths of this paper lie in its comprehensive examination of knowledge, attitude, and practice related to lactogenesis in postpartum women facing mother-infant separation, which adds valuable insights to the existing literature. Additionally, the large sample size and use of statistical analyses, including multivariate logistic regression and SEM model, enhance the robustness of the study’ s findings, providing a foundation for further research and potential interventions in this area.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study highlights important disparities and interactions among KAP regarding DOL in postpartum women who experienced mother-infant separation. While participants generally demonstrated moderate knowledge and positive attitudes, actual breastfeeding practices varied. Attitude was found to be a key driver of practice, whereas knowledge alone did not directly influence behavioral outcomes. Factors such as urban residence, cesarean section delivery, and duration of separation were significantly associated with differences in KAP scores. These findings suggest that healthcare professionals should prioritize strengthening maternal attitudes and providing practical support, particularly for mothers facing NICU-related separation, in order to improve breastfeeding outcomes and reduce the incidence of DOL.

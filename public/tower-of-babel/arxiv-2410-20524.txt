A note on semiprime skew left braces and related semidirect products
Marco CASTELLI

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on semiprime skew left braces provided by semidirect products. We show that if a semidirect product B 1 ⋊ B 2 B_{1}\rtimes B_{2} is semiprime and B 1 B_{1} is Artinian, then B 1 B_{1} must be semiprime. Moreover, we prove that the semidirect product of strongly semiprime skew left braces is strongly semiprime. Finally, addressing Question 1 1 in Smoktunowicz, More on skew braces and their ideals (2024), we provide examples of skew left braces of abelian type that are non-simple and strongly prime.

Introduction

Motivated by the search of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, which are of great impact in the study of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation (see
[
4
,
27
]
for more details), several algebraic structures were introduced. In particular, in
[
12
]
Guarnieri and Vendramin introduced the notion of
skew left brace
. A triple
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be a skew left brace if
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are groups, and the equality

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c

holds for all
a,b,c\in B
. This definition extends the one given by Rump in
[
20
]
where
(B,+)
is an abelian group. Skew left braces are powerful tools in the study of the quantum Yang-Baxter equation, since they allow to provide set-theoretic solutions belonging to the class of non-degenerate ones. On the other hand, these algebraic structures have gained increased interest in recent years because of several links with various topics - such as graph theory
[
17
]
, Hopf-Galois structures
[
9
]
, triply factorized groups
[
3
]
, and regular subgroups of the affine group
[
6
]
- that have been discovered.

It is well-known that groups and rings provide skew left braces. In fact, every group
(G,\cdotp)
has two natural skew left brace structures given by
(G,\cdotp,\cdotp)
and
(G,\cdotp,\cdotp^{op})
(where
\cdotp^{op}
is the opposite operation); while a radical ring
(A,+,\cdotp)
is a skew left brace if we replace the operation
\cdotp
with the adjoint operation
\circ
given by
a\circ b:=a+a\cdotp b+b
for all
a,b\in A
. For this reason, to study skew left braces, many papers have introduced several notions and tools inspired by group theory and ring theory, such as sub-skew left braces, left ideals, ideals
[
12
]
, matched product
[
1
]
, and nilpotency
[
8
]
. The concept of a solvable skew left brace was introduced in
[
2
]
for skew left braces of abelian type, and extended in
[
16
]
for arbitrary skew left braces. In analogy to what happens with groups, it is known that if
B
is a skew left brace and
I
is one of its ideals, then
B
is solvable if and only if
B/I
and
I
are solvable (see
[
16
, Lemma
6.4
]
,
[
15
, Lemma 8.1.5]
, and
[
2
, Proposition
2.4
]
). In
[
16
,
23
]
, the notions of (strongly) prime and (strongly) semiprime ideals were introduced, and suitable versions of celebrated theorems of ring theory were given for skew left braces (see
[
16
, Sections
5-6
]
).

To concretely provide new skew left braces, in
[
21
,
24
]
a suitable version of skew left braces semidirect product was developed. This construction has recently appeared in several contexts. Indeed, in
[
10
, Lemma 4.5]
it was shown that the permutation skew left braces of some set-theoretical solutions arise as semidirect products of two skew left braces, while Corollary
21
of
[
5
]
shows that a particular class of set-theoretic solutions can be recovered by semidirect products of skew left braces. In the last year, this construction was studied in the context of digroups
[
11

.
As a further step in the investigation of prime and semiprime skew left braces, in
[
7
, Example 5.3]
Cedò, Jespers and Okniński constructed the first example of a prime non-simple skew left brace of abelian type using semidirect products. In
[
14
]
, Kinnear showed that the class of semiprime skew left brace is closed under semidirect products. The goal of this paper is to continue the investigation of semidirect products and their links with (strongly) prime and semiprime skew left braces.

In the first part of the paper, following
[
14
, Question 3.4]
and
[
15
, Question 8.2.11]
, we discuss a possible converse to Kinnear’s result on the semidirect product of semiprime skew left braces. In this context, we show that if a skew left braces semidirect product
B_{1}\rtimes B_{2}
is semiprime, then so is
B_{1}
, provided that
B_{1}
is Artinian
[
13
]
(this is the case if
B_{1}
is finite). On the other hand, we show that an analogous result for
B_{2}
does not follow (even in the finite case), therefore we cannot provide a characterization similar to that of solvable skew left braces. Moreover, we show a “strongly" version of Kinnear’s result, i.e. we prove that the semidirect product of strongly semiprime skew left braces is strongly semiprime. In Section
4
, we focus on strongly prime skew left braces. As noted in
[
23
]
, finding finite examples of non-simple strongly prime skew left braces of abelian type seems to be quite difficult (see
[

, Question

]

]
allows to construct strongly prime skew left braces of abelian type: in particular, in

, Example
5.3

a concrete remarkable example was provided without computer calculations. However, this example has
92.160
elements. As a personal contribution in this context, we provide a family of semidirect products of strongly prime non-simple skew left braces (not necessarily of abelian type) that includes the one constructed in
[

1
Basic definitions, notations and results

In this section, we give basic definitions and results involving skew left braces. We start with the definition of a skew left brace.

Definition 1

(Definition
1.1
,
[
12
]
)
.

A triple
(B,+,\circ)
with two binary operations is said to be a
skew left brace
if
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are groups and
a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c
for all
a,b,c\in B
.
(B,+)
will be called the
additive group
, and
(B,\circ)
will be called the
multiplicative group
. Moreover, the inverse of an element
a\in B
in the additive (resp. multiplicative) group will be indicated by
-a
(resp.
a^{-}
).

Skew left braces
of abelian type
, i.e. with an abelian additive group, were previously introduced by Rump in
[
20
]
.

Example 1
.

1)

Let
(B,+)
be a group and
\circ
be the binary operation on
B
given by
a\circ b:=a+b
for all
a,b\in B
. Then, the triple
(B,+,\circ)
is a skew left brace. In the following, we will refer to this skew left brace as the
trivial
skew left brace on
(B,+)
.

2)

Let
B:=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z},+)
and
\circ
be the binary operation on
B
given by
a\circ b:=a+b+p\cdotp a\cdotp b
(where
\cdotp
is the ring-multiplication of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
). Then,
(B,+,\circ)
is a skew left brace.

Clearly, there are several skew left braces different from the ones provided in
Example

1
. The skew left braces up to size
168
(with some exceptions) were calculated in
[
26
]
. The database
\SmallBrace
collects all the skew left braces of abelian type, while the database
\SmallSkewbrace
collects all the skew left braces. Since throughout the paper we will ofter refer to these databases, we adopt the following convention.

Convention
.

We will indicate by
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
the skew left brace of abelian type having size
n
and located at position
k
in the database
\SmallBrace
. Moreover, we will indicate by
\mathcal{SB}_{n,k}
the skew left brace having size
n
and located at position
k
in the database
\SmallSkewbrace
.

Given a skew left brace
B
and
a\in B
, let us denote by
\lambda_{a}:B\longrightarrow B
the map from
B
into itself defined by
\lambda_{a}(b):=-a+a\circ b,
for all
b\in B
. Then,
\lambda_{a}\in\Aut(B,+)
, for every
a\in B
; and the map
\lambda
from
B
to
Aut(B,+)
given by
\lambda(a):=\lambda_{a}
is an action of
(B,\circ)
on
(B,+)
by automorphisms (see
[
12
]
for further details). As for other algebraic structures, one can define several sub-structures, such as left ideals and ideals.

Definition 2
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace. A subset
I
of
B
is said to be a
left ideal
if it is a subgroup of the additive group and
\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
, for every
a\in B
. Moreover, an
ideal
is a left ideal which is also a normal subgroup of the additive and multiplicative groups.

Of course, the subsets
\{0\}
and
B
are ideals, which we will call
trivial.
If there are no other ideals,
B
will be called
simple
. In a standard manner, ideals give rise to
quotient skew left braces
. Ideals of a quotient skew left brace
B/I
correspond bijectively to ideals of
B
that contain
I
. As one can expect, the intersection and the sum of a family (not necessarily finite) of ideals are again ideals. In particular, if
\mathcal{I}:=\{I_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a family of ideals, we denote the sum of the ideals in
\mathcal{I}
by
\Sigma_{j\in J}I_{j}
and by
I_{1}+...+I_{n}
if
|J|<+\infty
. A map
\alpha
between two skew left braces
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
is a
homomorphism
if
\alpha(a+b)=\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)
and
\alpha(a\circ b)=\alpha(a)\circ\alpha(b)
for all
a,b\in B_{1}
, and its
kernel
, defined as in the context of rings, is an ideal of
B_{1}
.

Examples of skew left braces can be constructed by a suitable version of the semidirect product, provided in
[
24
]
(and in
[
21
]
for skew left braces of abelian type).
If
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are skew left braces and

is a group homomorphism from
(B_{2},\circ)
to

, the

and

via

is given by

(a_{1},a_{2})+(b_{1},b_{2}):=(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})

(a_{1},a_{2})\circ(b_{1},b_{2}):=(a_{1}\circ\alpha_{a_{2}}(b_{1}),a_{2}\circ b_{2})

for all
(a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2})\in B_{1}\times B_{2}
. From now on, we will indicate this skew left brace by
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
. In analogy to the groups semidirect product, the set
B_{1}\times\{0\}
is an ideal of

.

Before presenting the next result, whose proof is straightforward, we fix some notations. If
X,Y
are nonempty sets, we denote by
\pi_{1}
(resp.
\pi_{2}
) the map from
X\times Y
to
X
(resp. from
X\times Y
to

) given by
\pi_{1}(x,y):=x
(resp.
\pi_{2}(x,y):=y
) for all
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be skew left braces, and
\alpha
be a homomorphism from
B_{2}
to

. Let

. Then:

1)

if

is an ideal of

, then

is an ideal of

2)

if

In order to study skew left braces using tools that resemble the ones used in ring-theory, one has to consider a third operation

for a skew left brace

a*b:=-a+a\circ b-b
, for all

. From now on, if

is a skew left brace and

the additive subgroup of

By the operation

, in

Definition 3
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace.

1.

B
is
semiprime
if for each non-zero ideal

one has
I*I\neq\{0\}
.

2.

B
is

if for any non-zero ideals

one has

.

3.

is

if for each non-zero ideal

one has that every

is non-zero.

4.

is

if every
*
-product of any number of non-zero ideals is non-zero.

(Corollary 2.8 of
[

]
)

Let

be semiprime skew left braces. Then, a semidirect product

is a semiprime skew left brace.

In
[

]

[

]

Question 4

(Question 3.4 of
[

]
- Question

of
[

]
)
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be skew left braces, and suppose that their semidirect product via
\alpha

B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is semiprime. Are

and

semiprime?

The focus of the next section will be the discussion of

2
Semiprime skew left braces in semidirect products

In this section, we discuss a possible converse to
Theorem

3
. In the main result, we show that if
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is a semiprime skew left brace, then so is
B_{1}
whenever it is an Artinian skew left brace (in particular, this is the case if
B_{1}
has finite order). Under this hypothesis, our result answers affirmatively to
Question

. We finish the section showing by a suitable example that an analogous result for
B_{2}
does not follow, even if we restrict to the finite case.

At first, we have to provide some technical results. Following the notation adopted in
[

, Chapter

]
, if
B
is a skew left brace,

, and

, we indicate by

the set

and by

the set

Lemma 5
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace,
n
a natural number and
J,I_{1},...,I_{n}
distinct minimal ideals of

. Then
J*(I_{1}+...+I_{n})=(I_{1}+...+I_{n})*J=0
.

Proof.

At first, we show the equality
J*(I_{1}+...+I_{n})=0
by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then
J*I_{1}\subseteq I_{1}\cap J=\{0\}
. Now, if
n>1
,
j\in J
, and
i_{1}\in I_{1},...,i_{n}\in I_{n}
then

\displaystyle j*(i_{1}+...+i_{n})=\lambda_{j}(i_{1}+...+i_{n})-(i_{1}+...+i_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(i_{1})-i_{1}+i_{1}+\lambda_{j}(i_{2}+...+i_{n})-(i_{2}+...+i_{n})-i_{1}

the last member belongs to
J*I_{1}+(J*(I_{2}+...+I_{n}))^{i_{1}}
, which is
0
by the inductive hypotesis.
Using induction again on
n
, we show the equality
(I_{1}+...+I_{n})*J
. As in the first part,
I_{1}*J\subseteq I_{1}\cap J=\{0\}
. Now, if
n>1
,
j\in J
, and
i_{1}\in I_{1},...,i_{n}\in I_{n}
then

\displaystyle(i_{1}+...+i_{n})*j=\lambda_{i_{1}+...+i_{n}}(j)-j

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i_{1}}(\lambda_{\lambda_{i_{1}^{-}}(i_{2}+...+i_{n})}(j)-j)+\lambda_{i_{1}}(j)-j

the last member belongs to
\lambda_{i_{1}}((I_{2}+...+I_{n})*J)+I_{1}*J
, which is equal to
0
by the inductive hypothesis. Therefore, the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
B

n
be a natural number with

(i_{1}+...+i_{n})*(j_{1}+...+j_{n})\in(I_{1}*I_{1})+(I_{2}*I_{2})^{I_{1}}+...+(I_{n}*I_{n})^{I_{1}+...+I_{n-1}}

\displaystyle(i_{1}+...+i_{n})*(j_{1}+...+j_{n})=\lambda_{i_{1}+...+i_{n}}(j_{1}+...+j_{n})-(j_{1}+...+j_{n})

\displaystyle\lambda_{i_{1}}(\lambda_{\lambda_{i_{1}^{-}}(i_{2}+...+i_{n})}(j_{1})-j_{1})+\lambda_{i_{1}}(j_{1})+\lambda_{i_{2}+...+i_{n}}(\lambda_{\lambda_{(i_{2}+...+i_{n})^{-}}(o_{1})}(j_{2}+...+j_{n})+

Theorem 9

Let

be skew left braces and

be the skew left braces semidirect product via

, and suppose that

is Artinian. If

is semiprime, then so is

.

Proof.

Suppose that

is not semiprime, and let
K
be a nonzero ideal of
B_{1}
such that

. Since

is Artinian,

contains a minimal ideal, which we denote by

. By a standard calculation, one can show that

is an ideal of

and is invariant under the action

2

J\times\{0\}
is an ideal of

. We check that

for all

. One has

and

for suitable elements

,
b_{1},...,b_{n},b_{1}^{\prime},...,b_{m}^{\prime}\in B_{2}
and

where

. Up to enlarging
n
and to taking some of the
i_{s}
’s and
i_{s}^{\prime}
’s as zero, we may suppose
n=m
and
b_{s}=b_{s}^{\prime}
. Let
I_{s}:=\alpha_{b_{s}}(I)
for all
s\in\{1,...,n\}
, then
I_{s}*I_{s}=0
follows from
Lemma

7
, and
(I_{1}+....+I_{n})*(I_{1}+....+I_{n})=0
follows from
Lemma

8
. Therefore
j*j^{\prime}=0
. This proves
J*J=0
, whence one also has

(J\times\{0\})*(J\times\{0\})=(J*J)\times\{0\}=\{(0,0)\}

but this contradicts the semiprimality of
B
.
∎

Since finite skew left braces are Artinian, the following is immediate.

Corollary 10
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be finite skew left braces and
B:=B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
the skew left braces semidirect product via
\alpha
. If
B
is semiprime, then so is
B_{1}
.

In general, an analog of
Theorem

9
for
B_{2}
does not hold even if we only consider the finite case, as the following example shows.

Example 11
.

Let
B_{1}
be the skew left brace given by
B_{1}:=\mathcal{SB}_{12,22}
and
B_{2}
be the trivial skew left brace on the cyclic group of size
2
. By computer calculations, we obtain that
Aut(B_{1},+,\circ)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},+)
and the unique non-trivial semidirect product
B_{1}\rtimes B_{2}
is semiprime.

3
Semidirect product of strongly semiprime skew left braces

In this small section, we show the “strongly" version of
Theorem

3
. At first, we give a preliminary lemma.

Lemma 12
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace,
I
be an ideal of
B
, and
X,Y
be subgroups of
(B,+)
. Then, we have
(X+I)*(Y+I)\subseteq(X*Y)+I
.

Proof.

Since
I
is an ideal, we have that
(x+i)*(y+j)-x*y\in I
for all
x\in X
,
y\in Y
, and
i,j\in I
; therefore
(x+i)*(y+j)\in(X*Y)+I
, for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. Moreover, since
(X*Y)+I
is a subgroup of
(B,+)
and since
(X+I)*(Y+I)
is generated by the elements of the form
(x+i)*(y+j)
, the statement follows.
∎

Theorem 13
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace and
I
an ideal of
B
. If
I
and
B/I
are strongly semiprime skew left braces, then so is
B
.

Proof.

Let
J
be an ideal of
B
. If there is a
*
-product of copies of
J
that is zero, by repeatedly using
Lemma

12
, we obtain that there exists a
*
-product of copies of the ideal
(J+I)/I
of
B/I
that is zero. Since
B/I
is semiprime, it follows that
(J+I)/I=I/I
and hence
J\subseteq I
. By semiprimality of
I
the equality
J=0
follows. Therefore,
B
is strongly semiprime.
∎

Remark 1
.

Lemma

12
is a more general version of the idea used in
[
14
, Section
2
]
to show
Theorem

3
, where the inclusion
(J+I)*(J+I)\subseteq J*J+I
for two arbitrary ideals
I,J
was used. In this context, since we have to consider an arbitrary
*
-product and, in general, the set
X*Y
is not an ideal even if
X
and
Y
are, we have to weaken the hypothesis.

As a corollary of the previous theorem, the main result of the section follows.

Corollary 14
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be strongly semiprime skew left braces and
B:=B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
be a semidirect product of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
via a homomorphism
\alpha.
Then,
B
is a strongly semiprime skew left brace.

Proof.

If we set
I:=B_{1}\times\{0\}
, it follows that
B/I\cong B_{2}
, hence the statement follows by
Theorem

13
.
∎

As in the semiprime case, if
B:=B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is a strongly semiprime skew left brace,
B_{2}
in full generality is not a strongly semiprime skew left brace. Indeed,
Example

11
is a strongly semiprime skew left brace and
B_{2}*B_{2}=0
. However, the question remains open for
B_{1}
.

Question 15
.

If
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are skew left braces such that their semidirect product via
\alpha

B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is strongly semiprime, is
B_{1}
strongly semiprime?

4
Strongly prime skew left braces by semidirect products

In this section, we study semidirect products of simple skew left braces. First, we show the possible ideals of these skew left braces. Later, we apply this result to provide sufficient conditions that allow to construct strongly prime non-simple skew left braces. All these examples answer in negative sense to
[
23
, Question 1]
.

From now on, a homomorphism
\alpha:G\longrightarrow H
is said to be
non-trivial
if the kernel of
\alpha
is a proper subgroup of
G
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be simple skew left braces and
\alpha:B_{2}\longrightarrow Aut(B_{1},+,\circ)
be a non-trivial homomorphism. Suppose that
\bar{J}
is an ideal of the skew left braces semidirect product
B:=B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
. Then,
\bar{J}
is either a trivial ideal, or
\bar{J}=B_{1}\times\{0\}
or
\bar{J}
is such that
\bar{J}\cong B_{2}
(as skew left braces) and
B
is isomorphic, as a skew left brace, to the skew left braces direct product
(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})\times\bar{J}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\bar{J}\cap(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})
is an ideal of
B_{1}\rtimes\{0\}
, therefore, since
B_{1}
is simple, this ideal is either
\{(0,0)\}
or
B_{1}\rtimes\{0\}
. If it is equal to
B_{1}\rtimes\{0\}
, by the correspondence theorem we have that
\bar{J}=B_{1}\rtimes\{0\}
or
\bar{J}=B_{1}\rtimes B_{2}
. Now, suppose that
\bar{J}\cap(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})=\{(0,0)\}
. Thus, either
\bar{J}=\{(0,0)\}
or
\bar{J}
is a proper ideal of
B
. In the latter case, we obtain that
(B_{1}\times\{0\})+\bar{J}/B_{1}\times\{0\}\cong\bar{J}/(B_{1}\times\{0\})\cap\bar{J}\cong\bar{J}
, and since
B_{2}\cong B/(B_{1}\times\{0\})
, we have that
B_{2}
contains an ideal isomorphic to
\bar{J}
. Since
\bar{J}\neq\{(0,0)\}
, we obtain that
\bar{J}\cong B_{2}
. Finally, since
B_{2}
is simple and the ideals containing
B_{1}\times\{0\}
correspond to the ideals of
B/(B_{1}\times\{0\})
, the unique ideals of
B
containing
B_{1}\times\{0\}
are
B_{1}\times\{0\}
and
B
, hence
\bar{J}+(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})=\bar{J}\circ(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})=B
, and the statement follows by the equality
\bar{J}\cap(B_{1}\rtimes\{0\})=\{(0,0)\}
.
∎

Now, we want to provide sufficient conditions to ensure that
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
has a unique non-trivial ideal. If
(G,\cdotp)
is a group and
g\in G
, we denote by
i_{g}
the inner automorphism of
(G,\cdotp)
given by
i_{g}(h):=g^{-1}\cdotp h\cdotp g
for all
h\in G
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be simple skew left braces and
\alpha:B_{2}\longrightarrow Aut(B_{1},+,\circ)
be a non-trivial homomorphism. Suppose that there is a non-trivial ideal
J
of
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
different from
B_{1}\times\{0\}
. Then, for every
(a_{1},a_{2})\in J
we have
\alpha_{a_{2}}=i_{a_{1}}
.

Proof.

If
(a_{1},a_{2})\in J
, by
Proposition

16
we have
(a_{1},a_{2})\circ(b_{1},0)=(b_{1},0)\circ(a_{1},a_{2})
for all
b_{1}\in B_{1}
, which implies the equality
(a_{1}\circ\alpha_{a_{2}}(b_{1}),a_{2})=(b_{1}\circ a_{1},a_{2})
for all
b_{1}\in B_{1}
, therefore
\alpha_{a_{2}}=i_{a_{1}}
and the statement follows.
∎

Now, we give two corollaries of
Proposition

17
that are useful for our purposes.

Corollary 18
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be simple skew left braces and
\alpha:B_{2}\longrightarrow Aut(B_{1},+,\circ)
be a non-trivial homomorphism. Moreover, suppose that
\alpha
is not injective and
(B_{1},\circ)
has trivial center. Then, the unique non-trivial ideal of the skew left braces semidirect product
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is
B_{1}\times\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
J
is a non-trivial ideal of
B
different from
B_{1}\times\{0\}
. By
Lemma

2
and
Proposition

16
, we have that
\pi_{2}(J)=B_{2}
, therefore for every
a_{2}\in B_{2}
there exists
a_{1}\in B_{1}
such that
(a_{1},a_{2})\in J
. Then, by
Proposition

17
, for every
(a_{1},a_{2})\in J
we have
\alpha_{a_{2}}=i_{a_{1}}
. If we take
a_{2}\in Ker(\alpha)\setminus\{0\}
, being the center of
(B_{1},\circ)
trivial, it follows that
(0,a_{2})\in J
. Thus, we have that
J\cap(\{0\}\times B_{2})
is a non-trivial ideal of
\{0\}\times B_{2}
, hence
J=\{0\}\times B_{2}
, thus
\alpha_{a_{2}}=i_{0}=id_{B_{2}}
for all
a_{2}\in B_{2}
; but this contradicts that
\alpha
is a non-trivial homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 19
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
be simple skew left braces and
\alpha:B_{2}\longrightarrow Aut(B_{1},+,\circ)
be a non-trivial homomorphism. Moreover, suppose that
\alpha_{a}
is not an inner automorphism of
(B_{1},\circ)
, for every
a\in B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, the unique non-trivial ideal of the skew left braces semidirect product
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is
B_{1}\times\{0\}
.

Proof.

If
J
is a non-trivial ideal of
B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
different from
B_{1}\times\{0\}
, then by
Proposition

17
every non-zero element of
J
has the form
(a_{1},a_{2})\in B_{1}\times B_{2}
with
\alpha_{a_{2}}=i_{a_{1}}
. Since
\alpha_{a_{2}}
is not an inner automorphism for
a_{2}\neq 0
, we must have
a_{2}=0
and hence
J\subseteq B_{1}\times\{0\}
. Therefore,
J=B_{1}\times\{0\}
because
B_{1}
is simple.
∎

We remark that
Corollary

19
uses the main idea of
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
, where skew left braces with a unique non-trivial ideal were constructed by an outer automorphism of the multiplicative group of
B_{1}
.

Finally, we can construct the desired family of skew left braces. First, we give a simple but fundamental lemma.

Lemma 20
.

Let
B
be a skew left brace, and suppose that
B
has a unique non-trivial ideal
J
, that is simple and non-trivial as a skew left brace. Then,
B
is strongly prime.

Proof.

Every product
I_{1}*I_{2}
of non-zero ideals of
B
contains
J*J
, which is an ideal of
J
(see
[
16
, Section 4]
) and hence equal to
J
by hypothesis. It follows that an arbitrary product of non-zero ideals in
B
contains
J
. Thus, the statement follows.
∎

Theorem 21
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}
and
\alpha
be as in the statement of
Corollary

18
(resp.
Corollary

19
). Moreover, suppose that
B_{1}
is not a trivial skew left brace. Then, the skew left braces semidirect product
B:=B_{1}\rtimes_{\alpha}B_{2}
is strongly prime and non-simple.

Proof.

By
Corollary

18
(resp.
Corollary

19
), the unique non-trivial ideal is
B_{1}\rtimes\{0\}\cong B_{1}
, which is a simple non-trivial skew left brace by hypothesis. Thus, the statement follows by
Lemma

20
.
∎

All the examples obtained by
Theorem

21
are non-simple and strongly prime. Note that if we take
B_{1},B_{2}
and
\alpha
as in
Corollary

19
with
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of abelian type,
B_{1}
non-trivial, and
B_{2}
having a prime number of elements, we find the skew left braces of
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
.

5
Examples and counterexamples

In this section, we collect suitable examples and counterexamples involving semiprime skew left braces.

By definition, we know the following implications: “strongly prime
\Rightarrow
prime
\Rightarrow
semiprime" and “strongly semiprime
\Rightarrow
semiprime". Apart from some special cases (see for example
[
25
, Section
5
]
), these notions do not coincide, even when we consider skew left braces of abelian type. In
[
16
, Example 5.7]
and
[
18
]
, examples that show “semiprime
\nRightarrow
prime" and “prime
\nRightarrow
strongly prime" were provided. Note that some skew left braces constructed in
[
18
]
also show “semiprime
\nRightarrow
strongly semiprime" and “prime
\nRightarrow
strongly semiprime". For example, let
B:=\mathcal{B}_{81,804}
. Then,
B
has only
B*B
as non-trivial ideal, which is not trivial as skew left brace. Therefore

B
is prime. However, by
[
8
, Proposition
4.4
]
,
B
is not strongly semiprime.

Now, we use
Theorem

21
to show that there exist examples of strongly prime non-simple skew left braces. Since these examples are of abelian type, they respond negatively to
[
23
, Question 1]
.

Proposition 22
.

There exists a strongly prime skew left brace of abelian type that is not a simple skew left brace.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Section
7.1
]
, there exists a simple skew left brace
B_{1}
of abelian type having
24
elements. Let
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
be the Sylow subgroups of
(B_{1},+)
. By
[
22
, Section
3
]
, we know that
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
are left ideals of
(B_{1},\circ)
. If
A_{2}
is normal in
(B_{1},\circ)
, by
[
8
, Lemma
1.8
and
1.9
]
we have that it is an ideal of
B_{1}
, which is a contradiction. In the same way, one can show that
A_{3}
cannot be normal in
(B_{2},\circ)
. By a standard exercise, we have that the unique group of order
24
without normal Sylow subgroups is
\mathbb{S}_{4}
, therefore
(B_{1},\circ)\cong\mathbb{S}_{4}
. Now, by
[
22
, Example
4
]
there exists a unique element

, which has multiplicative order
2
, such that
i_{b}(A_{2})=A_{2}
and
i_{b}(A_{3})=A_{3}
. By
[
22
, Examples
3
and
4
]
, we obtain the following statements:
b
does not act trivially on
A_{3}
by the map
\lambda_{b}
; the map
i_{b}
induces two skew left braces automorphisms on
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
, regarded as skew left braces. Using these facts, by a standard calculation, we obtain that
i_{b}
is an automorphism of
(B_{1},+,\circ)
of order
2
.

Now, let
B_{2}
be an isomorphic copy of
B_{1}
. If
\alpha
is the non-trivial homomorphism from
(B_{2},\circ)
to

that sends a transposition of

to

and with

, by

18
and

we obtain that

is a non-simple strongly prime skew left brace.
∎

We highlight that, by

20
, the example given in

is also a non-simple strongly prime skew left braces of abelian type, and it was obtained without computer calculations. However, it is a skew left brace with

Anyway, using the GAP package

.
Another attempt is the inspection of the skew left braces of abelian type having small size by computer

. In this regard, the following result shows that there are strongly prime skew left braces that are different from the ones considered in this paper.

Proposition 23
.

There exists a strongly prime skew left brace of abelian type that is not simple, and is different from the ones provided up to now. In particular, it is not a semidirect product of skew left braces, and is the minimal example of a strongly prime non-simple skew left brace of abelian type.

Proof.

Let
B
be the skew left brace given by

. Then,
B
has only one non-trivial ideal

of size

, which is isomorphic as a skew left brace to

. By

,

is a strongly prime skew left brace. Now, if
B
is a semidirect product of skew left braces, it must be constructed as a semidirect product of the form

, where

is the trivial skew left brace of size

, we obtain that there is a unique semidirect product of the form

Structure constants of Peterson Schubert calculus
Tao Gui, Yuqi Jia, Xinkai Yu, Zhexi Zhang, Yuchen Zhu

Abstract
. We give an explicit, positive, type-uniform formula for all (equivariant) structure constants of the Peterson Schubert calculus in arbitrary Lie types, using only information from the Cartan matrix of the corresponding root system Φ \Phi . As an application, we derive a type-uniform formula for the mixed Φ \Phi -Eulerian numbers.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14N15, 14N10, 14M15, 05E14

1.
Introduction

The Peterson variety is a remarkable subvariety of the flag variety, introduced by Dale Peterson
[
32
]
to realize the quantum cohomology rings of all (Langlands dual) partial flag varieties geometrically. It can also be used to describe the homology of the affine Grassmannian (see
[
28
]
) and it is closely related to the wonderful compactification of a complex semisimple algebraic group
[
5
]
.
The Peterson varieties in type
A
are closely related to the Toeplitz matrices
[
34
]
.

Now, let
G
be a complex semisimple linear algebraic group with a Borel subgroup
B\subset G
. Recall that the cohomology ring
H^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
of the flag variety
G/B
has a distinguished basis consisting of Schubert classes
[
8
]
. Studying the product structure of these Schubert classes (sometimes also encompassing the study of analogous questions in generalized cohomology theories or equivariant settings) is the central problem in modern Schubert calculus. By Kleiman’s transversality theorem
[
23
]
, the Schubert structure constants are non-negative integers. In type
A
, the Schubert polynomials in
[
29
]
are polynomial representatives of Schubert classes in the classical Borel presentation
[
9
]
of the cohomology ring of the complete flag variety. It is an outstanding problem in combinatorics and enumerative algebraic geometry to give a positive formula or a combinatorial model for the structure constants of multiplying Schubert polynomials.

There are also interesting works studying the structure constants of the cohomology ring of Peterson varieties (the so-called “Peterson Schubert calculus”). Let
\Pet_{G}
denote the Peterson variety inside
G/B
(see (
2.3
) for a precise definition). It admits an action of a
one-dimensional sub-torus
S
of the maximal torus
T\subset G
. Let
\sigma_{v_{I}}\in H_{S}^{*}(G/B)
be a Schubert class indexed by some Coxeter element
v_{I}\in W
for
I\subset\Delta
, where
W
is the Weyl group and
\Delta
is the set of simple roots. Let
\iota^{*}\left(\sigma_{v_{I}}\right)
be the (equivariant) pullback of
\sigma_{v_{I}}
along the inclusion
\iota:\Pet_{G}\rightarrow G/B
. While class
\iota^{*}\left(\sigma_{v_{I}}\right)
depends on the choice of
v_{I}
, the class
p_{I}:=\frac{\iota^{*}\left(\sigma_{v_{I}}\right)}{m\left(v_{I}\right)}
is independent of this choice after dividing by a certain intersection multiplicity
m\left(v_{I}\right)
, known as the (integral equivariant) Peterson Schubert class in the literature. In
[
16
]
,
Goldin, Mihalcea, and Singh show that
\left\{p_{I}\right\}_{I\subset\Delta}
is a
c_{I,J}^{K}\in H_{S}^{*}(pt)
-basis of
H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
, and that this basis is dual to the equivariant Borel–Moore homology basis constituted by the fundamental classes of Peterson Schubert cycles (see Theorem
2.5
).
Furthermore, their main result shows that the structure constants of multiplication
c_{I,J}^{K}\in H_{S}^{*}(pt)\cong\mathbb{Z}[t]
, with respect to the basis
\left\{p_{I}\right\}_{I\subset\Delta}
, defined by

(1.1)

p_{I}\cdot p_{J}=\sum_{K}c_{I,J}^{K}p_{K},

are polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients.

A natural open question (asked in
[
20
, P.43]
for Lie type
A
) is the following

Question 1.1
.

Is there a positive formula for all structure constants
c_{I,J}^{K}
in (
1.1
)?

For Lie type
A
, Goldin–Gorbutt
[
15
]
found positive combinatorial formulae for the structure constants in a successive way, while Abe–Horiguchi–Kuwata–Zeng
[
3
]
found a different combinatorial model computing these structure constants in non-equivariant cohomology based on a geometric viewpoint. Some years earlier, in type
A
, Harada–Tymoczko
[
20
]
gave a positive equivariant Monk formula for the product of a cohomology-degree-2 Peterson Schubert class (that is,
p_{\{i\}}
for some
i\in\Delta
) with an arbitrary Peterson Schubert class, and Bayegan–Harada
[
6
]
gave an equivariant Giambelli formula to express Peterson Schubert class
p_{I}
in terms of the ring generators
p_{\{i\}}
’s. Drellich
[
13
]
gave the equivariant Monk rule and Giambelli formula for all Lie types.

While the positivity of structure constants in Peterson Schubert calculus was recently established using the aforementioned geometric and combinatorial methods, the above Question
1.1
remains unresolved in general, and it is not known whether these constants can be calculated directly and uniformly from the intrinsic Lie-theoretic data of the associated root system. This paper addresses this problem. In this paper, we give a simple, explicit, positive, type-uniform formula for all structure constants in (
1.1
) of the Peterson Schubert calculus in arbitrary Lie types, which completely solves the above question in a type-uniform way. Our formula is a concise algebraic formula, determined only by the Cartan matrix. As far as we know, this is the first example of an explicit, complete, and type-uniform positive algebraic formula in general Schubert calculus. We hope that our method can shed some new light on related structure constants problems in Schubert calculus. We derive this formula from an algebraic viewpoint, which is different from the above-mentioned work. We now present our main results.

Let
C_{\Delta}:=\left(\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle\right)_{i,j\in\Delta}=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in\Delta}
be the Cartan matrix associated with the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
. Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda
[
19
]
gave an explicit and beautiful Borel-type presentation of the equivariant cohomology ring of Peterson varieties in all Lie types; see Theorem
2.4
.
The following is our first result.

Theorem 1.2

(Peterson Schubert monomials)
.

Under the isomorphism (
2.6
), the Peterson Schubert class
p_{I}
,
I\subset\Delta
, is represented by the monomial

(1.2)

\frac{\operatorname{det}\left(C_{I}\right)}{\left|W_{I}\right|}\prod_{i\in I}\varpi_{i},

where
\operatorname{det}\left(C_{I}\right)
is the determinant of the Cartan sub-matrix
C_{I}
determined by
I\subset\Delta
and
\left|W_{I}\right|
is the order of the parabolic subgroup
W_{I}
of the Weyl group
W
determined by
I\subset\Delta
.

Using the above Peterson Schubert monomials and the Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda presentation (
2.6
), we can expand the product of two monomials into the monomial basis representing integral equivariant Peterson Schubert classes, and compute all the structure constants in (
1.1
).

To state our main theorem, we first need to introduce some notations. For any
K\subset\Delta
, we use
[C_{K}^{-1}]_{j,k}
to denote the
(j,k)
-entry of the matrix
C_{K}^{-1}
, which is the inverse of the Cartan sub-matrix
C_{K}
determined by
K
.
Suppose
|\Delta|=n
, we use the notation
(\quad)_{J,K}
to denote a
2^{n}\times 2^{n}
matrix, with rows indexed by
J\subset\Delta
and columns indexed by
K\subset\Delta
, and the ordering of the subsets of
\Delta
is the lexicographical order. The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(Structure constants of equivariant Peterson Schubert calculus)
.

With the notations as above. Suppose
I=\{i_{1},...,i_{l}\}\subset\Delta
. Then the structure constant
c_{I,J}^{K}
, defined in (
1.1
), is equal to
\frac{\operatorname{det}\left(C_{I}\right)\operatorname{det}\left(C_{J}\right)\left|W_{K}\right|}{\left|W_{I}\right|\left|W_{J}\right|\operatorname{det}\left(C_{K}\right)}
multiplied to the
(J,K)
-entry of the product of matrices
(d^{K}_{i_{1},J})_{J,K}\cdots(d^{K}_{i_{l},J})_{J,K}
, where

(1.3)

d^{K}_{i,J}=\begin{cases}\frac{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,s}}{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s}},&\text{ if }i\in K,|K|=|J|+1,\text{ and }K\setminus J=\{s\},\\
2t\sum_{k\in K}[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,k},&\text{ if }i\in K\text{ and }K=J,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

By letting
t=0
in (
1.3
), we get a simple formula for all non-equivariant structure constants in Peterson Schubert calculus for
H^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
I=\{i_{1},...,i_{l}\}\subset\Delta
. Then the non-equivariant structure constant
n_{I,J}^{K}
, defined to be
c_{I,J}^{K}
with
t=0
, is equal to
\frac{\operatorname{det}\left(C_{I}\right)\operatorname{det}\left(C_{J}\right)\left|W_{K}\right|}{\left|W_{I}\right|\left|W_{J}\right|\operatorname{det}\left(C_{K}\right)}
multiplied to the
(J,K)
-entry of the product of matrices
(m^{K}_{i_{1},J})_{J,K}\cdots(m^{K}_{i_{l},J})_{J,K}
, where

(1.4)

m^{K}_{i,J}=\begin{cases}\frac{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,s}}{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s}},&\text{ if }i\in K,|K|=|J|+1,\text{ and }K\setminus J=\{s\},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

To prove the above theorems, we transform the structure constants in the second case of (
1.3
) into an infinite matrix series (see the proof of Theorem
3.5
) related to the
Coxeter adjacency matrix

A_{K}
, defined as
A_{K}:=2E-C_{K}
, where
E
is the identity matrix. We use the classical
Perron–Frobenius theorem
to prove that this matrix series is convergent and related to an inverse of a matrix; see Lemma
3.7
. Finally, we use the
Sherman–Morrison formula
to compute matrix inverse, which is simplified to the final formula; see Lemma
3.8
.

It is well known that the entries of the inverse of any Cartan matrix are all non-negative (see, for example,
[
22
, Exercise 13.8]
), hence the entries of all the matrices
(d^{K}_{i,J})_{J,K}
in Theorem
1.3
are polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients.
Since the entries in the product of such matrices remain polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients, our formula is indeed a positive formula, which provides a new purely algebraic proof of the positivity of equivariant structure constants of Peterson Schubert calculus. We also give a simple criterion for when these structure constants are non-zero; see Corollary
3.13
and Remark
3.14
.

The relationship between the (non-equivariant) Peterson Schubert calculus and mixed Eulerian numbers (which
were introduced and studied by Postnikov

[
33
]
) has been actively explored very recently; see
[
7
]
,
[
31
]
, and
[
21
]
. These numbers compute the mixed volumes of
\Phi
-hypersimplices, see Section
4
for the precise definitions. The origins of this connection lie in the realization of the Peterson variety as a flat degeneration of a smooth projective toric variety, the
W
-permutohedral variety; see, for example,

[
1
,
24
,
25
]
. In
[
21
]
, Horiguchi provided a combinatorial model introduced in
[
3
]
for the computation of mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers when
\Phi
is of type
A
and derived a type-by-type computation for the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers for general Lie types by iteratively applying the (non-equivariant version of) Monk formula from Drellich in
[
13
]
. As an application of our main theorem, we derive a type-uniform formula for the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers in arbitrary Lie type.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system of rank
n
. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
be non-negative integers with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}=n
.
The mixed
{\Phi}
-Euler number
A_{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}^{\Phi}
can be computed using the following formula:

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}=\frac{|W_{\Phi}|}{\det(C_{\Phi})}\left[\left((m_{1,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{1}}\cdots\left((m_{n,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{n}}\right]_{(\emptyset,\Delta)}.

where
(m_{i,J}^{K})_{J,K}
is the matrix defined in (
1.4
).
The notation

[\quad]_{(\emptyset,\Delta)}

denotes the entry in the row indexed by
\emptyset
and the column indexed by
\Delta
.

Compared with the computations in
[
21
]
, our formula for the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers is quite simple and direct, which avoids the need to discuss the changes in the Lie types case-by-case when considering sub-root systems as in
[
21
, Section 7]
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notations and some preliminary results which we use throughout this paper. In Section
3
, we prove our main theorems. In Section
4
, we give the application to mixed Eulerian numbers.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect our notations and preliminary results for later use.

2.1.
Flag varieties and Schubert varieties

Let
G
be a complex connected semisimple algebraic group of rank
n
,
B\subset G
be a Borel subgroup, and
B^{-}\subset G
be the opposite Borel. Write
T:=B\cap B^{-}
for the maximal torus common to both. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(G)
,
\mathfrak{b}=\Lie(B)
, and
\mathfrak{h}=\Lie(T)
be the corresponding Lie algebras. Denote by
\Phi
the root system determined by
(G,T)
, and by

\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\}
the simple roots specified by
(B,T)
. We abuse the notation by letting
\Delta
also denote the index set of simple roots. The Weyl group
W:=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in\Delta\rangle
is a finite Coxeter group. For any subset
I\subset\Delta
, we have the standard parabolic subgroup
P_{I}\subset G
,
whose Weyl group is
W_{I}=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
, a parabolic subgroup of
W
.

Recall that the group
G
admits the Bruhat decomposition

G=\coprod_{w\in W}BwB,

a disjoint union of
B
-double cosets indexed by the Weyl group elements.
Passing to the flag variety, we obtain

(2.1)

G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}BwB/B,

where each
BwB/B
is isomorphic to an affine space, which is called the
Schubert cell

X^{\circ}_{w}:=BwB/B
.

Similarly, we have the opposite Bruhat decomposition

(2.2)

G/B=\coprod_{w\in W}B^{-}wB/B,

where each
B^{-}wB/B
is again isomorphic to an affine space, called the
opposite Schubert cell

\Omega^{\circ}_{w}:=B^{-}wB/B
.

For every
w\in W
, we set

X_{w}:=\overline{X^{\circ}_{w}}=\overline{BwB/B}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{w}:=\overline{\Omega^{\circ}_{w}}=\overline{B^{-}wB/B},

the Schubert and opposite Schubert varieties in
G/B
, respectively. It follows from (
2.2
) that the equivariant cohomology classes
\sigma_{w}\in H_{T}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
, which are Poincaré dual to the fundamental class
\left[\Omega_{w}\right]
, form a basis
\left\{\sigma_{w}\mid w\in W\right\}
of
H_{T}^{*}(G/B;\mathbb{Z})
as a module over
H_{T}^{*}(pt)
, called
equivariant Schubert classes
.

2.2.
Peterson varieties and some geometric constructions

We recall the definition of the Peterson variety in any Lie type and provide some related geometric constructions from the existing literature.

Recall that we have the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into root spaces

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Fix a regular nilpotent element
e\in\mathfrak{b}
. The
Peterson variety

\Pet_{G}
is a closed subvariety of the flag variety
G/B
, defined by

(2.3)

\Pet_{G}:=\Bigl\{gB\in G/B\;\Big|\;\text{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(e)\in\mathfrak{b}\oplus{\textstyle\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\Bigr\}.

It is known that the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
is always irreducible with complex dimension
n:=\rank G
(see
[
32
]
or
[
27
, Proposition 6.2]
), but it is singular and even non-normal in general
[
26
]
. Peterson varieties also form a special class of a larger family of subvarieties of the flag variety, namely the
Hessenberg varieties
introduced in
[
12
]
; see
[
2
]
for a very nice survey.

Example 2.1

(Type
A_{n-1}
Peterson variety)
.

In Lie type
A
, the Peterson variety can be easily described in a concrete way. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let

\mathrm{Fl}_{n}:=\bigl\{V_{\bullet}=(0=V_{0}\subsetneq V_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq V_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n})\mid\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V_{i}=i\bigr\}

denote the full flag variety of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Fix the regular nilpotent matrix

N:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}.

The Peterson variety
\mathrm{Pet}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
is defined by

\mathrm{Pet}_{n}:=\bigl\{V_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Fl}_{n}\mid NV_{i}\subset V_{i+1}\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq n-1\bigr\},

where
NV_{i}
denotes the image of
V_{i}
under the linear map
N\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
In this setting, Peterson varieties are also known as the
regular nilpotent
Hessenberg varieties with
Hessenberg function

h(i)=i+1
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
.

It is known that there is a one dimensional sub-torus
S\subset T
acting on the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
and we set
H_{S}^{*}(pt)\cong\mathbb{Z}[t]
, where
t
is a certain character of
S
; see
[
16
, Section 3.2]
. By intersecting the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
with Schubert cells and opposite Schubert cells in the Bruhat decomposition (
2.1
) and opposite Bruhat decomposition (
2.2
) of the flag variety
G/B
, we have the following

Proposition 2.2

(See
[
16
, Proposition 3.3]
and
[
27
, Proposition 6.2]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

\Pet_{G}\cap X_{w}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

\Pet_{G}\cap\Omega_{w}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
;

(3)

w=w_{I}
for some
I\subset\Delta
,
where
w_{I}
is the longest element of
W_{I}
.

The geometry of the intersection
\Pet_{G}\cap\Omega_{w_{I}}^{\circ}
encodes the (small) quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}(G^{\vee}/P_{I}^{\vee})
of the Langlands dual partial flag variety
G^{\vee}/P_{I}^{\vee}
, which was Dale Peterson’s original motivation in his unpublished work
[
32
]
to introduce and study these remarkable varieties. Furthermore, it is known that the set-theoretic intersection
\Pet_{G,I}^{\circ}:=\Pet_{G}\cap X_{w_{I}}^{\circ}
is an affine space of dimension
|I|
(see
[
36
]
or
[
5
]
), called a
Peterson cell
. The Bruhat decomposition (
2.1
) induces an
S
-stable affine paving

(2.4)

\Pet_{G}=\bigsqcup_{I\subset\Delta}\Pet_{G,I}^{\circ}.

For
I\subset\Delta
, set

(2.5)

\Pet_{G,I}:=\overline{\Pet_{G,I}^{\circ}}=\Pet_{G}\cap X_{w_{I}},\qquad\Omega_{I}:=\Pet_{G}\cap\Omega_{w_{I}}.

\Pet_{G,I}
is known as the
Peterson Schubert variety
in the literature.
By the above affine paving (
2.4
), we have the following theorem; see
[
32
]
and
[
36
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
I\subset\Delta
, we have the fundamental class
[\Pet_{G,I}]
in
H^{S}_{2|I|}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
, and the collection

\{[\Pet_{G,I}]\mid I\subset\Delta\}
forms a basis of the total equivariant homology
H^{S}_{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
.

2.3.
Cohomology ring of Peterson varieties and Peterson Schubert classes

Write
B=T\ltimes U
with
U
the unipotent radical. The canonical projection
\pi\colon B\twoheadrightarrow T
sends
b=tu\mapsto t
. Any weight
\mu\colon T\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
extends to a character of
B
by pulling back along
\pi
. Denote the associated one-dimensional
B
-module by
\mathbb{C}_{\mu}
and consider the line bundle

L_{\mu}:=G\times^{B}\mathbb{C}_{-\mu}.

Sending
\mu
to the equivariant first Chern class
c_{1}(L_{\mu})
gives the homomorphism

\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\varpi_{i}\rightarrow H_{T}^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Z}),\qquad\mu\longmapsto c_{1}(L_{\mu}),

where
\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\varpi_{i}
is the weight lattice. We identify all weights
\mu
with
c_{1}(L_{\mu})
in cohomology by abusing notation.

Consider the restriction of the line bundle
L_{\mu}
on the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
, which will also be denoted by
L_{\mu}
when there is no confusion. Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda
[
19
]
gave the following explicit and beautiful Borel-type presentation of the equivariant cohomology ring of Peterson varieties in all Lie types.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
19
]
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Suppose that rank of
G
is
n
. The cohomology ring of the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
has the following presentation

(2.6)

H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Q}[\varpi_{1},\ldots,\varpi_{n},t]}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle\varpi_{i}\varpi_{j}-2t\varpi_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\right)}.

For the above
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficient, see their final remark in
[
19
]
. In the above Harada–Horiguchi–Masuda presentation (
2.6
), the variable
\varpi_{i}
corresponds to
the equivariant first Chern class
c_{1}(L_{\varpi_{i}})\in H_{S}^{2}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})
of the line bundle
L_{\varpi_{i}}
on
\Pet_{G}
associated with the fundamental weight
\varpi_{i}
; see, for example,
[
4
, Section 6.5]
.

For
I\subset\Delta
, recall that an element
v\in W
is called a
Coxeter element
for
I
if
v=s_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots s_{\alpha_{k}}
for some enumeration
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
of
I
. Let
\iota:\Pet_{G}\hookrightarrow G/B
denote the closed embedding from the Peterson variety to the flag variety. In
[
16
]
, a basis
\left\{p_{I}\right\}_{I\subset\Delta}
of
H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
dual to the basis
\left\{\left[\Pet_{G,I}\right]\right\}_{I\subset\Delta}
of
H^{S}_{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
is constructed. We call
p_{I}
an (integral equivariant)
Peterson Schubert class
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
16
, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4]
)
.

For each
I\subset\Delta
, fix a Coxeter element
v_{I}
, and consider the pull-back of the equivariant Schubert class
\iota^{*}\sigma_{v_{I}}\in H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})
. Then the classes

(2.7)

\left\{\left.p_{I}:=\frac{\iota^{*}\sigma_{v_{I}}}{m(v_{I})}\in H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})\right\rvert\,I\subset\Delta\right\}

form a
H_{S}^{*}(pt)\cong\mathbb{Z}[t]
-basis of
H_{S}^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
, which is dual to the basis
\left\{\left[\Pet_{G,I}\right]\right\}_{I\subset\Delta}
of
H^{S}_{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Z})
under the standard pairing between equivariant homology and equivariant cohomology, where
m\left(v_{I}\right)
is the multiplicity (which is a positive integer) of the unique point
w_{I}
in the intersection
\Omega_{w_{I}}\cap\Pet_{G,I}
.

It follows that while class
\iota^{*}\left(\sigma_{v_{I}}\right)
depends on the choice of
v_{I}
, the class
p_{I}
is independent of this choice. In
[
16
, Remark 7.7]
, a type-independent formula for the intersection multiplicity
m\left(v_{I}\right)
was conjectured, which was proved in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Intersection multiplicity formula,
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Suppose I is a connected Dynkin diagram. Let
v_{I}
be a Coxeter element of
I
, and let
R\left(v_{I}\right)
denote the number of reduced expressions for
v_{I}
. We have

(2.8)

m\left(v_{I}\right)=\frac{R\left(v_{I}\right)\left|W_{I}\right|}{|I|!\operatorname{det}\left(C_{I}\right)},

where
W_{I}
is the Weyl group determined by
I
, and
C_{I}
is the Cartan matrix determined by

A subset

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
K\subset\Delta

have absolute values less than 1. As a consequence, the matrix

B_{K}^{\widehat{s}}\left(E-B_{K}^{\widehat{s}}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(B_{K}^{\widehat{s}}\right)^{m}.

Proof.

It is not hard to see that we suffice to prove the lemma for connected

is block diagonal. Now suppose

is a connected subset of
\Delta
, it is shown in
[

where

is defined as

We can decompose this procedure into two steps: the first step is to multiply by

, and the second step is to set the entries in the row indexed by

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{K}\right)^{-1}+\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{K}\right)^{-1}e_{s}\left(\frac{1}{2}x_{s}-e_{s}\right)^{\text{T}}\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{K}\right)^{-1}}{1-\left(\frac{1}{2}x_{s}-e_{s}\right)^{\text{T}}\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{K}\right)^{-1}e_{s}},

\displaystyle=1-2[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s},

\displaystyle=(E-B_{K}^{\widehat{s}})^{-1}e_{s}

\displaystyle=2C_{K}^{-1}e_{s}+\frac{y_{s}e_{s}^{\text{T}}e_{s}-2y_{s}(y^{\prime}_{s})^{\text{T}}e_{s}}{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s}}.

\displaystyle\left[B_{K}^{\widehat{s}}(E-B_{K}^{\widehat{s}})^{-1}\right]_{i,s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(e_{i}^{\text{T}}-\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime}_{i})^{\text{T}}\right)\cdot\left(2C_{K}^{-1}e_{s}+\frac{y_{s}e_{s}^{\text{T}}e_{s}-2y_{s}(y^{\prime}_{s})^{\text{T}}e_{s}}{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s}}\right)

\displaystyle 2e_{i}^{\text{T}}C_{K}^{-1}e_{s}-(x^{\prime}_{i})^{\text{T}}C_{K}^{-1}e_{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{e_{i}^{\text{T}}y_{s}e_{s}^{\text{T}}e_{s}-2e_{i}^{\text{T}}y_{s}(y^{\prime}_{s})^{\text{T}}e_{s}-\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime}_{i})^{\text{T}}y_{s}e_{s}^{\text{T}}e_{s}+(x^{\prime}_{i})^{\text{T}}y_{s}(y^{\prime}_{s})^{\text{T}}e_{s}}{[C_{K}^{-1}]_{s,s}}

be the corresponding Cartan matrix. Then we have
[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,j}=\frac{\det(C(i,j))}{det(C_{K})}
.

We have the following corollary. In particular, it means that our formulas provide an algebraic proof of the positivity of the equivariant structure constants of Peterson Schubert calculus.

Corollary 3.12
.

We have
d_{I,J}^{K}\geq 0
for all
I,J,K\subset\Delta
.

Proof.

It is well known that the entries of the inverse of any Cartan matrix are all non-negative (see, for example,
[
22
, Exercise 13.8]
), hence by (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.8
, all matrices
(d^{K}_{i,J})_{J,K}
in Theorem
3.5
are polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients.
Since the entries in the product of such matrices remain polynomials in
t
with non-negative coefficients, this corollary follows directly.
∎

We have the following corollary, which gives a simple criterion for when the structure constants are non-zero.

Corollary 3.13
.

The structure constant
c_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
(equivalently,
d_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
) if and only if

K\supset I\cup J
, and

For each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
, we have
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
, the structure constant
c_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
is equivalent to the structure constant
d_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
.

When
|I|=1
, we have
I=\{i\}
, and we consider the value of
d_{i,J}^{K}
. Then by (
3.4
),
d_{i,J}^{K}\neq 0
if and only if we have
K=J\bigsqcup\{i\}
, or
i\in J
,
|K|=|J|+1
,
K\setminus J=\{s\}\neq\{i\}
and
b_{i,s}^{K,\widehat{s}}\neq 0
, or
i\in J
,
K=J
and
\sum_{k\in K}[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,k}\neq 0
. In any case, we have
K\supset\{i\}\cup J
, so in necessity, it remains to prove
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
in each case.

For the case
K=J\bigsqcup\{i\}
, since
i
can only be contained in exactly one connected component of
K
, it is easy to see that
|K_{k}|=|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
.

Now consider the case
i\in J
,
|K|=|J|+1
,
K\setminus J=\{s\}\neq\{i\}
and
b_{i,s}^{K,\widehat{s}}\neq 0
. In this case, it is not hard to see that
|K_{k}|=|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
is equivalent to
i
and
s
being in the same component. By
[
30
]
, the latter is equivalent to
[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,s}\neq 0
. By Lemma
3.8
, this case is proved.

Then consider the case
i\in J
,
K=J
and
\sum_{k\in K}[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,k}\neq 0
. Note that
\sum_{k\in K}[C_{K}^{-1}]_{i,k}\neq 0
always holds, since all entries of
C_{K}^{-1}
are non-negative. It is easy to see that
i\in J
and
K=J
imply
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
.

As for the sufficiency, we have
K\supset\{i\}\cup J
and for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
,
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
. If
|K_{k}|=|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
, from the discussion above, we have
K\setminus J=\{s\}
,
s=i
or
s
and
i
are in the same connected component, which means
d_{i,J}^{K}\neq 0
. If
|K_{k}|<|K_{k}\cap\{i\}|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for some
K_{k}
, by
K\supset\{i\}\cup J
, we have
K=J
, which also means
d_{i,J}^{K}\neq 0
.

For the general
I
, choose any
i\in I
and suppose
I=I^{\prime}\bigsqcup\{i\}
. By Theorem
3.5
, we have

d_{I,J}^{K}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K^{\prime}\subset\Delta\end{subarray}}d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}.

Since all the terms in the summation are polynomials in
t
with nonnegative coefficients by Corollary
3.12
,
d_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
if and only if there is a
K^{\prime}\subset\Delta
such that
d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}\neq 0
and
d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}\neq 0
. We need to prove that they are further equivalent to
K\supset I\cup J
and
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
.

We prove the necessity by induction on the cardinality of
I
. So suppose
d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}\neq 0
and
d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}\neq 0
for some
K^{\prime}
. Because
d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}\neq 0
, we have
i\in K^{\prime}
and
K^{\prime}=K
, or
K\supset\{i\}\cup K^{\prime}
and
|K^{\prime}|=|K|-1
.

For the case of
i\in K^{\prime}
and
K^{\prime}=K
, according to the induction hypothesis for
d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|K_{k}|

\displaystyle=|K_{k}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}|+|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap J|

\displaystyle=|K_{k}\cap I^{\prime}|+|K_{k}\cap J|

\displaystyle\leq|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|

for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
,
which completes the proof of this case.

For the case of
K\supset\{i\}\cup K^{\prime}
and
|K^{\prime}|=|K|-1
, according to the induction hypothesis for
d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}\neq 0
,
K^{\prime}\supset I^{\prime}\cup J
and for each connected component
K_{k}^{\prime}
of
K^{\prime}
, we have

(3.10)

|K_{k}^{\prime}|\leq|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}|+|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap J|.

It follows that
K\supset I\cup J
.
Let
K=K_{1}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup K_{t}
be the decomposition of
K
into connected components. Without loss of generality, we assume
i\in K_{1}
and
K\setminus K^{\prime}=\{s\}
(here
s
may or may not equal
i
). Note that
K^{\prime}\supset I^{\prime}\cup J
, we have
s\notin I^{\prime}
and
s\notin J
. Since
d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}\neq 0
, by the proof of the
|I|=1
case, it is not hard to see
s\in K_{1}
.

Assume
K_{1}\setminus\{s\}=K_{1,1}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup K_{1,r}
is the decomposition of
K_{1}\setminus\{s\}
into connected components. Here
r=0
means
K_{1}=\{i\}=\{s\}
, and
r=1
means
K_{1}\setminus\{s\}
is still connected.

By definition, the decomposition of
K^{\prime}
into connected components is:

K^{\prime}=K_{1,1}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup K_{1,r}\bigsqcup K_{2}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup K_{t}.

Define
K_{1}^{\prime}:=K_{1}\setminus\{s\}=K_{1,1}\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup K_{1,r}.

Since
i\notin K_{2},\dots,K_{t}
, when
2\leq k\leq t
, we have
K_{k}=K_{k}^{\prime}
, the connected component of
K^{\prime}
. By (
3.10
), we have
|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|,
for
k=2,...,t
.

As for
K_{1}
(the component containing
i
), we have

\displaystyle|K_{1}\cap I|+|K_{1}\cap J|=

\displaystyle|K_{1}\cap(I^{\prime}\bigsqcup\{i\})|+|K_{1}\cap J|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{1}\cap(I^{\prime}\bigsqcup\{i\})|+|K_{1}^{\prime}\cap J|\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{1}\cap I^{\prime}|+1+|K_{1}^{\prime}\cap J|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{1}^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}|+1+|K_{1}^{\prime}\cap J|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|K_{1}^{\prime}|+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{1}|,

where the second equality is because
s\notin J
, the fourth equality is because
s\notin I^{\prime}
, the fifth inequality is by (
3.10
). The necessity is proved.

As for the sufficiency, assume
K\supset I\cup J
, and for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
, we have

|K_{k}|\leq|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|.

Recall that
I=\{i\}\bigsqcup I^{\prime}
, and now we define
K^{\prime}=K\setminus\{i\}
. By (
3.4
), we have
d_{i,K^{\prime}}^{K}=1
.
By definition,
K^{\prime}\supseteq I^{\prime}\cup J
, so we have
i\notin J
. Using the same arguments as above, we know that each connected component
K_{k}^{\prime}
of
K^{\prime}
satisfies

|K_{k}^{\prime}|\leq|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}|+|K_{k}^{\prime}\cap J|.

Applying the induction hypothesis to
I^{\prime}
, we have
d_{I^{\prime},J}^{K^{\prime}}\neq 0.

This finishes the proof of the sufficiency.
The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

By tracing the above proof carefully, one can see that the non-equivariant structure constant
m_{I,J}^{K}\neq 0
if and only if
K\supset I\cup J
and

|K_{k}|=|K_{k}\cap I|+|K_{k}\cap J|
for each connected component
K_{k}
of
K
.

4.
Applications to mixed Eulerian numbers

We would like to introduce the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers for arbitrary Lie types as in
[
33
]
and their connections to the structure constants of the Peterson Schubert calculus. We mainly follow the notations as in
[
21
]
.

Recall that
\Phi
is a crystallographic root system of rank
n
. Let
\Lambda
be the associated integral weight lattice and
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{R}
be the weight space. The associated Weyl group
W
acts on the weight space
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
as a finite real reflection group. Taking a weight
\chi\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
, the
weight polytope

P_{\Phi}(\chi)
is defined as the convex hull of the Weyl group orbit of
\chi
:

P_{\Phi}(\chi):=\mathrm{ConvexHull}\{w(\chi)\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\mid w\in W\}.

Example 4.1

(Type
A_{n-1}
weight polytope—Permutohedron)
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. The permutation group
S_{n}
in
n
letters acts naturally on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by permuting coordinates. For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, the
permutohedron

P_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
is defined to be the convex hull of points in the
S_{n}
-orbit of
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

P_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right):=\mathrm{ConvexHull}\left\{\left(a_{w(1)},\ldots,a_{w(n)}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid w\in S_{n}\right\}.

It is at most
(n-1)
-dimensional, sitting inside an affine hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}
are distinct, then
P_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
is
(n-1)
-dimensional, and Postnikov gave a formula
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
of its volume using
divided symmetrization
. This formula can be interpreted as a computation in the equivariant cohomology of the type
A
permutohedral variety using the localization technique; see
[
21
, Section 3]
.

Recall that
\Delta:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
denotes the set of simple roots in
\Phi
. In
[
33
]
, Postnikov gave a formula for the volume of the weight polytope
P_{\Phi}(\chi)
, with the normalization that the volume of the parallelepiped generated by the simple roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
is 1.

Let
\varpi_{1},\ldots,\varpi_{n}
be the fundamental weights. Suppose
\chi=u_{1}\varpi_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}\varpi_{n}
and consider the associated weight polytope
P_{\Phi}(\chi)
. Its volume is a homogeneous polynomial
V_{\Phi}
of degree
n
in variables
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
:

(4.1)

V_{\Phi}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}):=\text{volume of }P_{\Phi}(u_{1}\varpi_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}\varpi_{n}).

Postnikov
[
33
]
defined the
mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}
, for
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\geq 0
with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}=n
, as the coefficients of the volume polynomial (
4.1
):

(4.2)

V_{\Phi}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})=\sum_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}\frac{u_{1}^{c_{1}}}{c_{1}!}\cdots\frac{u_{n}^{c_{n}}}{c_{n}!}.

By this definition, the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian number
A_{c_{1},\cdots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}
is exactly the
mixed volume
of
c_{1}
copies of
P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{1}\right)
,
c_{2}
copies of
P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{2}\right),\cdots
, and
c_{n}
copies of
P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{n}\right)
, multiplied by
n!
. Here, the weight polytopes
P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{1}\right),P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{2}\right),\ldots,P_{\Phi}\left(\varpi_{n}\right)
are called the
\Phi
-hypersimplices
. The mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers are known to be non-negative integers; see
[
33
]
for more details. When
\Phi
is of type
A
, these numbers are simply called
mixed Eulerian numbers
.

Postnikov provided in
[
33
]
a combinatorial formula for the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers in terms of certain binary trees. In
[
7
]
, Berget–Spink–Tseng studied the log-concavity of matroid
h
-vectors in relation to mixed Eulerian numbers, using the fact that the cohomology ring of the type
A
Permutohedral variety is exactly the Chow ring of the Boolean matroid. In
[
31
]
, Nadeau–Tawari found a beautiful relation between mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers and intersection numbers of Schubert varieties and the permutohedral variety for arbitrary Lie types. In
[
21
]
, Horiguchi showed that mixed Eulerian numbers can be written as intersection numbers of Schubert divisors in Peterson variety for an arbitrary Lie type as follows.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system of rank
n
. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
be non-negative integers with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}=n
.
Then the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian number
A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}
is equal to

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}=\int_{\Pet_{G}}\varpi_{1}^{c_{1}}\varpi_{2}^{c_{2}}\cdots\varpi_{n}^{c_{n}},

where, as before,
\Pet_{G}
denotes the Peterson variety associated with the simple algebraic group
G
,
\varpi_{i}\in H^{2}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})
denotes the first Chern class of the line bundle
L_{\varpi_{i}}
on
\Pet_{G}
, which is also the image of the Schubert class
\sigma_{s_{i}}\in H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Q})
under the restriction map
H^{2}(G/B;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})
.

Remark 4.3
.

From (
4.2
) and the above theorem, it follows that the volume polynomial in (
4.1
) has the following expression

(4.3)

\operatorname{Vol}P_{\Phi}\left(u_{1}\varpi_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}\varpi_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\Pet_{G}}\left(u_{1}\varpi_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}\varpi_{n}\right)^{n},

which is the volume polynomials of the nef divisors
\varpi_{1},\cdots,\varpi_{n}
on the Peterson variety
\Pet_{G}
. While the Peterson variety is singular in general
[
26
, Theorem 6]
, its rational cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Pet_{G};\mathbb{Q})
in (
2.6
) admits the structure of the cohomology ring of a (rational smooth) toric orbifold (see, for example,
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
), thus satisfying all the Kähler package—the Poincaré duality, the hard Lefschetz theorem, and the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relation. This gives a different proof that
\operatorname{Vol}P_{\Phi}\left(u_{1}\varpi_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}\varpi_{n}\right)
is
Lorentzian
in the sense of
[
10
]
.

Using the above theorem, Horiguchi gave a combinatorial model introduced in
[
3
]
for the computation of mixed Eulerian numbers, and derived a type-by-type computation for mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers for general Lie types by iteratively applying the Monk formula of Drellich in
[
13
]
. As an application of our main theorem, we now derive a type-uniform formula for the mixed
\Phi
-Eulerian numbers in arbitrary Lie types.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system. Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
be non-negative integers with
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}=n
.
The mixed
{\Phi}
-Euler number
A_{c_{1}\cdots c_{n}}^{\Phi}
can be computed using the following formula:

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}=\frac{|W_{\Phi}|}{\det(C_{\Phi})}\left[\left((m_{1,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{1}}\cdots\left((m_{n,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{n}}\right]_{(\emptyset,\Delta)}.

where
(m_{i,J}^{K})_{J,K}
is the matrix defined in (
1.4
).
The notation

[\quad]_{(\emptyset,\Delta)}

denotes the entry in the row indexed by
\emptyset
and the column indexed by
\Delta
of a matrix.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.2
, we have:

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}=\int_{\Pet_{G}}\varpi_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\varpi_{n}^{c_{n}}.

Using Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
3.4
, it follows that:

\varpi_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\varpi_{n}^{c_{n}}=a_{\Delta}\,\varpi_{1}\cdots\varpi_{n},

where

a_{\Delta}=\left[\left((m_{1,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{1}}\cdots\left((m_{n,J}^{K})_{J,K}\right)^{c_{n}}\right]_{(\emptyset,\Delta)}.

By
[
21

, we conclude:

A_{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}}^{\Phi}=a_{\Delta}\int_{\Pet_{G}}\varpi_{1}\cdots\varpi_{n}=\frac{|W_{\Phi}|}{\det(C_{\Phi})}a_{\Delta}.

The proof is completed.
∎

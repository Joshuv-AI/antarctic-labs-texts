Global carbohydrate metabolism is increased and beta hydroxybutyrate decreased in sepsis vulnerable children compared to sepsis tolerant children; a cohort study
Authors: Ruth A Carcillo, Rebecca I Caldino Bohn, Aditya Sriram, Kate F Kernan, Ivan Saraiva, Lina Ghaloul-Gonzalez, Dana Y Fuhrman, Christopher M Horvat, Jeremy R Herrman, Yong Y Han, Trung C Nguyen, Brian Ingram, Robert A Berg, Kathy L Meert, Mark W Hall, Christopher JL Newth, Hernando Gomez, Jerry Vockley, Hyun Jung Park, Joseph A Carcillo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Background: Sepsis investigators propose tolerance is conferred when the dormancy response increases beta hydroxybutyrate to protect organs; whereas, vulnerability occurs when a ‘metabolic switch’ to aerobic glycolysis decreases beta hydroxybutyrate promoting persistent immune cell activation without organ protection. We previously identified tolerant (PedSep A 2% mortality), susceptible (PedSep B 12% mortality and PedSep C 10% mortality) and vulnerable (PedSep D 34% mortality) phenotypes in the PHENOtyping pediatric sepsis induced Multiple organ failure Study (PHENOMS). We hypothesized that carbohydrate metabolism was increased and beta hydroxybutyrate decreased in PedSep D compared to PedSep A in this study. Methods: Metabolomics were assayed in biobanked plasma samples previously collected at 24-48 hours of sepsis in 375 children ages 44 weeks post gestation to 17 years, using the Metabolon Discovery Panel. Welch’s t-tests identified differences between PedSep B, C, and D with A. Results Compared to PedSep A (n =130) PedSep B had similar carbohydrate metabolism and beta hydroxybutyrate but dysregulated branch chain amino acid metabolism (n = 92; p < 0.05); PedSep C had similar carbohydrate metabolism but decreased beta hydroxybutyrate (n = 100; p< 0.05); and, PedSep D had increased carbohydrate metabolism with decreased beta hydroxybutyrate (n = 53; p < 0.05). Causal inference analysis found direct associations of increased glycolysis with decreased beta hydroxybutyrate, and death. Conclusions Only PedSep D had increased carbohydrate metabolism with decreased beta hydroxybutyrate providing rationale for designing clinical trials of low carbohydrate ketogenic nutrition to decrease glycolysis, restore dormancy, and improve outcome in these high-risk children.

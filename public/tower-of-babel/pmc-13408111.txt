Enhanced color image denoising using fuzzy metric-like space and peer group
Authors: Maliha Rashid, Faryad Ali, Nadia Batool, Akbar Azam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBy using a fuzzy metric-like space to redefine the idea of peer group, this study offers a novel chromatic filtering technique for removing outliers. The technique uses features of space to enable effective corrupted pixel detection. The suggested filtering method is intended to preserve fine image features while successfully restoring color images that have been deteriorated by excessive amounts of impulsive noise. Additionally, a switching technique is shown that, based on the results of noisy pixel detection, intelligently switches between the identification operation and Enhanced Average Filtering Technique.Keywords: Fuzzy metric-like space, Peer group, Filtering outliers, Noisy imageSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Digital images frequently degrade in quality during acquisition, transmission, or storage due to distortions such as noise contamination, blurring and geometric deformations. Impulsive noise is especially harmful because it causes sudden intensity variations which decrease visual quality and have a negative influence on automated image processing activities. As a result, effective noise reduction approaches that decrease impulsive noise while keeping fundamental image structures remain critical in digital image processing. These approaches consider color pixels as multidimensional vectors using correlations between the red, green and blue channels rather than handling each channel separately. Non-linear vector filters based on robust statistics1, in particular, have risen in popularity due to their ability to limit the influence of extreme values, making them suited for impulsive noise suppression while maintaining color accuracy.

Examples of such filters are the Vector Median Filter 2,3, the Basic Vector Directional Filter 4 and the Directional Distance Filter 5. Although these algorithms make good use of inter-channel interactions, they often use fixed smoothing strategies that do not account for local image statistics. As a result, they frequently change noise-free pixels resulting in the loss of fine features particularly in textured or edge-rich regions.

To address these constraints, adaptive filtering algorithms have been proposed6–12, which dynamically modify filtering strength based on local image properties. Peer group based techniques13–15 for example use spatial and intensity similarities within a small region to identify noise-free pixels. While traditional peer group filters often use Euclidean distance measurements, their performance suffers under high noise levels due to restricted flexibility in similarity modeling. To boost robustness fuzzy metric-based peer group filters have been developed16–18 which improve adaptability and noise detection capability.

In recent years deep learning methods19–21 such as CNNs, GANs, zero-shot techniques and transformers have shown very good results in image denoising. CNNs can learn important image features but they need large labeled datasets and high computing power. GANs can improve image quality but they may create unwanted artifacts and require complex training. Transformer models can understand global image information but they require a lot of memory and computation. Overall these deep learning methods work well but they often need training data and high computational cost which makes real-time use difficult. In comparison classical methods like fuzzy metric–based peer group techniques are simpler, do not require training data and have lower computational complexity.

More recently, fuzzy measures have been employed to further generalize the peer group concept enabling perceptually meaningful similarity assessment by incorporating uncertainty and gradual transitions between pixel intensities. However, existing fuzzy metric–based methods are often constrained by strict self-distance assumptions which limit their effectiveness in highly corrupted images. Motivated by these observations, this work proposes a novel peer group filtering framework based on fuzzy metric-like spaces, providing a more flexible similarity model for effective impulsive noise detection and detail preservation. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: section “Fuzzy metric-like space” introduces the fuzzy metric-like space, section “Construction of peer group in the context of fuzzy metric-like space” presents the proposed filtering algorithm, section “Comparision of experimental results and algorithm efficiency” discusses experimental results and comparisons, and section “Statistical analysis” discusses the statistical analysis, section “Computational complexity analysis” presents the complexity analysis and section “Conclusion” concludes the study.

Conclusion

This proposed a new filtering method called the fuzzy-like peer group filter () based on fuzzy metric-like space () for removing impulsive noise from color images. By redefining how similar pixels are grouped together, the proposed method can better detect noisy pixels while keeping important image details and colors.

The experimental results show that the proposed filter performs better than several existing methods in terms of and . The statistical tests also confirm that the improvements are significant and consistent across different test images.

In terms of computation, the proposed method has similar overall complexity to standard peer group filters. Although the fuzzy-based distance calculation adds a small extra cost, the method remains practical and efficient for normal image sizes and window settings.

However, some challenges still remain. The current method mainly focuses on salt-and-pepper noise, while real images may contain mixed or more complex types of noise. Also, some parameters (such as and ) are selected experimentally, and future research should focus on automatically selecting these parameters. Another important direction is combining fuzzy-based methods with modern learning-based techniques to further improve performance.

In the future, we plan to extend this method to handle mixed noise types and to develop more adaptive and robust versions for different real-world imaging applications.

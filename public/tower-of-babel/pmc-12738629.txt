Two Groins-One Hand Syndrome: is it an Underdiagnosed Condition?
Authors: Marta Szepietowska, Andrzej K Jaworek, Jacek C Szepietowski
Published: 2025

A 52 year-old female patient presented with itchy lesions located in the groins and on one hand. She had been suffering from hypothyroidism and insulin resistance for more than 20 years and had been continuously taking levothyroxine 125 µg daily and metformin 750 mg daily. The first skin lesions appeared about 18–20 years ago in the groins. Approximately 5 years ago, she noticed lesions on the left hand. The lesions were pruritic, and due to increasing itch intensity in the groins, the patient decided to visit a dermatologist.

On admission, she presented with large, well-demarcated, slightly scaly plaques located in both inguinal folds, extending to the inner parts of the thighs. Bilaterally raised borders were observed. The vulva, perineum, intergluteal cleft, and surrounding buttocks were also involved (Fig. 1A-C). The lesions were very itchy, with the worst itch during the 24 h prior to examination assessed on the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) as 8 points. In the past, she had periodically scratched the affected skin and could not resist the urge. Additionally, erythematous, hyperkeratotic, scaly lesions were located exclusively on the left palm, especially involving the skin over the thenar eminence. Within the plaque, several small fissures were present. Hyperkeratotic scaly lesions were also found on the flexural surface of the wrist (Fig. 2A-B). The worst-itch NRS within last 24 h was reported as 5 points. It is noteworthy that the patient is left-handed.

Direct mycological examination of the samples obtained both from the groins and the left palm was negative. The molecular identification of the Trichophyton rubrum and Candida albicans from both sites was performed by the external commercial laboratory using EUROArray Dermatomycosis kit from Euroimmune AG®. According to the manufacturer’s documentation, the kit includes ready-to-use PCR master mixes and a microarray-based hybridization step following amplification, allowing simultaneous detection of 23 dermatophyte species and 3 yeast/mould species.

The patient was started on oral terbinafine 250 mg daily and topical treatment with a combination of isoconazole and diflucortolone valerate twice daily. After two weeks of treatment, the lesions in the groins and anogenital area improved markedly, and the itch disappeared completely at all affected sites. An additional two weeks of the aforementioned treatment resulted in complete resolution of all symptoms and signs. A two-month follow-up revealed no recurrence.

Two feet–one hand syndrome (TFOHS) is a well-known condition in which both feet and one hand are infected with dermatophytes. Usually, infection of the hand occurs due to autoinoculation from the feet. The most common causative agent of this clinical manifestation of dermatophytosis is Trichophyton rubrum.

Here, we report a similar clinical situation in a patient with chronic tinea cruris, with the coexisting infection of the left hand. In the majority of reported TFOHS cases, the dominant hand was affected. Our patient is left-handed, which is consistent with previous observations. In our patient, the hand infection appeared after 13–15 years of persistent tinea cruris. Similarly to the majority of TFOHS cases, Trichophyton rubrum was identified as the causative agent. Based on this case, we propose to call the condition a “two groins–one hand syndrome”. It is likely that such a condition is not rare but rather underdiagnosed and underreported. We therefore recommend careful examination of the hands in all patients with chronic dermatophytosis, including not only those with tinea pedis, but also with tinea cruris.

Non-commutative crepant resolutions for (almost) simplicial toric algebras
Aimeric Malter 1, Artan Sheshmani 1,2

Abstract
. Given a rational convex polyhedral Gorenstein cone constructed as cone over a lattice polytope P P , we establish that toric non-commutative crepant resolutions (NCCRs) of its associated toric algebra descend to toric NCCRs of the algebras associated to faces of the polytope P P . As consequence, we present two new, short proofs to the existence of toric NCCRs for simplicial affine toric Gorenstein algebras and for almost simplicial affine toric Gorenstein algebras, i.e. those associated to cones σ \sigma with dim σ + 1 \dim\sigma+1 extremal rays.



1.
Introduction

Resolutions of singularities have long been an intense area of study in algebraic geometry. Oftentimes, the underlying geometric objects become more complicated after resolving them (for instance, most singular affine schemes do not have affine resolutions). The classical approach to deal with this fact of life is to simply expand the class of geometric objects one considers. A second approach that emerged is to abstracise and distill from the resolutions some algebraic properties which in turn can be seen as defining characteristics of resolutions. For instance, Bondal-Orlov
[
4
]
and Kawamata
[
17
]
conjectured that given a normal algebraic variety
X
with two crepant resolutions
\pi_{i}:Y_{i}\rightarrow X
,
i=1,2
, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Y_{1})\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Y_{2})
linear over
X
.

Verifying this conjecture for a simple flop of three-dimensional varieties, Van den Bergh
[
25
]
used tilting theory to construct two Morita equivalent algebras
\Lambda_{i}
such that
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda_{i})\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Y_{i})
. In a certain sense thus, the algebras
\Lambda_{i}
themselves constitute crepant resolutions. Van den Bergh
[
24
]
formalises this notion, introducing
non-commutative (crepant) resolutions
, abbreviated as NC(C)Rs. For a normal noetherian domain
R
, an NCR is an algebra of the form
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
for
M\in\operatorname{ref}R
such that
\operatorname{gl}\dim\Lambda<\infty
. If further
R
is Gorenstein, we call
\Lambda
crepant if it is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. In general, the existence of such resolutions is thus far an open question even for the case of affine toric Gorenstein algebras, i.e. the algebras associated to strictly convex rational polyhedral Gorenstein cones. However, certain classes of cones have been shown to possess NCCRs, using a plethora a methods of algebraic and combinatorial nature (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
22
,
10
,
23
]
).

All Gorenstein cones
\sigma
can be written (after change of basis) as
\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
, where
P
is a lattice polytope. In
[
19
]
, we conjecture that for a lattice polytope
Q
lattice equivalent to a face of
P
, if the coordinate ring of
\sigma_{P}=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
admits an NCCR, so does the coordinate ring of
\sigma_{Q}=\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})
. The main result of the present paper provides a proof of this conjecture for the special case of
toric
NCCR, which arise when the rank of the reflexive
R
-module
M
is 1.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
3.1
)
Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a lattice polytope and let
\sigma
be the cone
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
together with its toric algebra
R_{\sigma}=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma}]
, where
M_{\sigma}
is the character lattice of the toric variety defined by
\sigma
. Suppose
Q
is lattice equivalent to a face
F
of a lattice polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the cone
\sigma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Denoting the corresponding character lattice by
M^{\prime}
, let
R^{\prime}=k[(\sigma^{\prime})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]
be the toric algebra associated to
\sigma^{\prime}
. Suppose
R^{\prime}
has a toric NCCR
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Then
R
has a toric NCCR.

With this theorem, we develop a unified approach to providing toric NCCRs for Gorenstein toric algebras, by expressing a Gorenstein cone
\sigma
as a facet of another Gorenstein cone
\sigma^{\prime}
and descending the corresponding NCCR.
In this way, we obtain a new, short proof of the known result that all Gorenstein simplicial toric algebras admit an NCCR.

Corollary
.

(Corollary
4.3
)
Let
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be a simplicial Gorenstein cone. Then
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
has an NCCR.

Furthermore, we can use our approach prove the existence of toric NCCRs for the case of
almost simplicial
Gorenstein cones (i.e. Gorenstein cones
\sigma
with
\dim\sigma+1
extremal rays), remarking that this result has recently been proven by Tomonaga
[
23
]
using different, more algebraic methods.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
4.5
)
Let
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be an almost simplicial Gorenstein cone, i.e.
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
with
P
a lattice polytope with
\dim P+2
vertices. Then
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
has a toric NCCR.

Our proof works by constructing, given a lattice polytope
P
, another polytope
Q
in one dimension higher such that a simplicial subdivision of the associated face fan yields a smooth toric DM stack with a tilting bundle (obtained by applying methods of Borisov-Hua
[
6
]
). This tilting bundle further fulfills some cohomological vanishing conditions which, by results from our previous work
[
19
]
, guarantee the existence of a toric NCCR for
k[\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})^{\vee}\cap(M\oplus\mathbb{Z})]
, descending to give an NCCR of
R
.

1.1.
Structure and Notation

We give a brief overview of toric Deligne-Mumford stacks and non-commutative crepant resolutions in §
2
. Then, in §
3
, we introduce and prove the main technical result of this paper in Theorem
3.1
. The theorem states that a toric NCCR for an affine toric Gorenstein algebra associated to a cone
\sigma
descends to a toric NCCR for cones lattice equivalent to its faces
\tau\prec\sigma
. We apply this theorem in §
4
to give altenative proofs for the existence of NCCRs for toric algebras associated to simplicial and almost simplicial Gorenstein cones.

Let us here fix some notation used throughout this paper. Given a normal noetherian domain
R
, we say that a finitely generated
R
-module is
reflexive
if the canonical map
M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)
is an isomorphism. Such modules form a category, which we denote by
\operatorname{ref}R
. For a reflexive
R
-algebra
\Lambda
, denote by
\operatorname{ref}(\Lambda)
the category of
\Lambda
-modules which are reflexive as
R
-modules. Under the additional assumption that
R
is commutative, an
R
-module
M
is said to be
maximal Cohen-Macaulay
if
M_{m}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay for every maximal ideal
m
. We denote the category of such modules by
\operatorname{CM}R
and will omit the word maximal in this paper. As detailed in
[
15
,
16
]

M\in\operatorname{CM}R\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all
i>0
.
Given a toric variety
X
, we usually denote by
M
its character lattice and by
N
the cocharacter lattice, dual to
M
.The pairing
M\times N\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
naturally extends to a pairing of
M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. As such, the fan
\Sigma
of
X
lives inside
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For a fan
\Sigma
with ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, we denote by
D_{\rho}
the torus invariant Weil divisor associated to
\rho
.
Finally, we fix an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic 0.

2.
Toric geometry and non-commutative resolutions

In this section, we review some notions and facts from toric geometry and define non-commutative resolutions.

2.1.
Cox stacks and cohomology computations

Recall that a
Gorenstein
cone is a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
with primitive ray generators
u_{\rho}\in N
for
\rho\in\sigma(1)
such that there is an element
m_{\sigma}\in M
with
\langle m_{\sigma},u_{\rho}\rangle=1
for all
\rho\in\sigma(1)
.
Fix now a fan
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
where
\dim N=n
. To the fan
\Sigma
we can not only associate a toric variety, but also a quotient stack
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
, using a standard construction by Cox. Let
\nu
be the set of primitive ray generators of
\Sigma(1)
, i.e.
\nu:=\{u_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\Sigma(1)\}
. Consider the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{|\nu|}
with elementary
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
e_{\rho}
, indexed by the rays
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. The
Cox fan
of
\Sigma
is now defined to be

\operatorname{Cox}(\Sigma):=\{\operatorname{Cone}(e_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma)|\sigma\in\Sigma\}.

This fan is a subfan of the standard fan for
\mathbb{A}^{|\nu|}
, and thus we can view the associated toric variety as an open subset of
\mathbb{A}^{|\nu|}
, denoted by
U_{\Sigma}:=X_{\operatorname{Cox}(\Sigma)}
. There is a well-known right-exact sequence

M\xrightarrow{f_{\Sigma}}\mathbb{Z}^{|\nu|}\xrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{coker}(f_{\Sigma})\rightarrow 0,

(2.1)

where
m\mapsto\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\langle u_{\rho},m\rangle e_{\rho}
. Applying to this sequence the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbb{G}_{m})
yields

1\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{coker}(f_{\Sigma}),\mathbb{G}_{m})\xrightarrow{\hat{\pi}}\mathbb{G}_{m}^{|\nu|}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}.

From this sequence we extract the group
S_{\Sigma}:=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{coker}(f_{\Sigma}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
, which acts on
U_{\Sigma}
and allows us to define the
Cox stack
as the following quotient stack.

Definition 2.1
.

Define the
Cox stack
associated to
\Sigma
to be

\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}:=[U_{\Sigma}/S_{\Sigma}]

Considering the Cox stack associated to a fan has an advantage over simply considering the associated toric variety in that the stack can be smooth even when the variety is not. A toric variety is smooth if and only if the corresponding fan is a smooth fan, whereas a Cox stack merely requires the fan to be simplicial (which happens for orbifold singularities in the toric variety).

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 4.12 in
[
11
]
)
.

If
\Sigma
is simplicial, then
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack with coarse moduli space
X_{\Sigma}
. When
\Sigma
is smooth (equivalently, the variety
X_{\Sigma}
is smooth),
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}\cong X_{\Sigma}
.

Let us now recall a way to compute the cohomology of line bundles on such stacks. These computations are standard, and our recollection here is based on work by Borisov-Hua
[
6
]
and Efimov
[
9
]
; the results below can be found within these two references. From hereon, we fix
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
to be a complete simplicial fan, keeping
\dim N=n
. For any
I\subseteq\Sigma(1)
, we denote by
C_{I}
the simplicial complex with vertex set
I
, consisting of those subsets
J\subseteq I
forming the set of rays of some cone in
\Sigma
. For example,
|C_{\emptyset}|=\emptyset
and
|C_{\Sigma(1)}|=S^{n-1}
. Given a vector
\mathbf{r}=\sum r_{\rho}e_{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|\Sigma(1)|}\subset\mathbb{R}^{|\Sigma(1)|}
, we denote by
\operatorname{Supp}(\mathbf{r})
the set of rays
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
r_{\rho}<0
. Using a standard computation via toric Čech complexes, one obtains the following result on the cohomology of line bundles on
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
. Then

H^{i}(\mathcal{L})=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|\Sigma(1)|},\\
\mathcal{O}(\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}r_{\rho}D_{\rho})\cong\mathcal{L}\end{subarray}}\tilde{H}_{i-1}(|C_{\operatorname{Supp}(\mathbf{r})}|).

For the purpose of effectively determining the cohomology of line bundles, it is thus required to understand when the reduced homology of the simplicial complexes
C_{I}
vanishes.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a subset
I\subseteq\Sigma(1)
of non-vanishing homology
\tilde{H}_{\bullet}(|C_{I}|)\neq 0
, define the
forbidden point

q_{I}=-\sum_{\rho\not\in I}D_{\rho}\in\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}).

Then define the
forbidden cone

F_{I}\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma})
to be the set

F_{I}=q_{I}+\sum_{\rho\in I}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}D_{\rho}-\sum_{\rho\not\in I}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}D_{\rho}.

We note the following immediate Corollary of the Proposition
2.3
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

H^{>0}(\mathcal{L})=0
;

(2)

\mathcal{L}
does not belong to any forbidden cone
F_{I}
,
I\neq\emptyset
.

To classify which sets
I
give complexes
C_{I}
of non-vanishing reduced homology, we consider
primitive collections
.

Definition 2.6
.

A non-empty subset
I\subseteq\Sigma(1)
is called a
primitive collection
if it is not a set of boundary cones of any cone in
\Sigma
, but each proper subset
J\subseteq I
is.

Efimov
[
9
]
proves the following result.

Lemma 2.7

(Lemma 4.4 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
I\subseteq\Sigma(1)
be a non-empty subset such that
\tilde{H}^{\bullet}(|C_{I}|)\neq 0
. Then
I
is a union of primitive collections.

2.2.
Non-commutative crepant resolutions

Consider now a Gorenstein cone
\sigma
with associated toric variety
X_{\sigma}
. It is known that subdividing the cone
\sigma
gives a crepant toric morphism if and only if the added rays lie in the Gorenstein plane
\{\langle m_{\sigma},n\rangle=1\}
. Thus, any simplicial fan
\Sigma
with support
|\Sigma|=\sigma
such that for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, the primitive ray generator
u_{\rho}
lies in this Gorenstein plane, gives a crepant resolution via the smooth toric DM stack
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
. These resolutions are of a geometric nature, and it remains a challenge to translate this into the language of
non-commutative crepant resolutions
.

Let
R
be a normal noetherian domain.

Definition 2.8
.

A
non-commutative resolution
of
R
is an
R
-algebra
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
for
M\in\operatorname{ref}R
such that
\operatorname{gl}\dim\Lambda<\infty
. Further assuming that
R
is Gorenstein, we call
\Lambda

crepant
if additionally
\Lambda\in\operatorname{CM}R
.

Usually, we will abbreviate non-commutative crepant resolutions as NCCRs.
Note that the notion of NCCRs does capture in an algebraic manner the geometric concept of crepant resolutions by passing from an affine variety to its associated coordinate ring. Details of this correspondence are detailed in
[
24
, §4]
. The definition of NC(C)Rs can be understood for more general schemes by reducing to affine patches.

Whilst the notion of NCCRs encapsulates the nature of crepant resolutions, it is still unclear to what extent they exist. A natural first class of variety to consider is the class of affine Gorenstein toric varieties. Given a cone
\sigma
, we refer to the associated coordinate ring as
toric algebra
.

Conjecture 2.9
.

A Gorenstein toric algebra always has an NCCR.

Most known results in this context give a special type of NCCR, called
toric NCCR
.

Definition 2.10
.

An NCCR
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
,
M\in\operatorname{ref}R
is called
toric
if
M
is isomorphic to a sum of ideals.

Constructions giving toric NCCRs often exploit links to tilting theory, expressing the NCCR as endormorphism algebra of a
tilting bundle
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
Y
be a Noetherian scheme. A
partial tilting complex

\mathcal{T}
on
Y
is a perfect complex such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{Y}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})=0
for
i\neq 0
. We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
tilting
if it generates the derived category
\operatorname{D}_{Qcoh}(Y)
in the sense that it has trivial right orthgonal, i.e.
\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=0
implies
\mathcal{F}=0
. If the perfect complex is in fact a bundle, we refer to it as
(partial) tilting bundle
.

This definition naturally generalises to algebraic stacks.

Consider a noetherian scheme
Y
with tilting complex
\mathcal{T}
and let
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{Y}(\mathcal{T})
. The functor
\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}(\mathcal{T},-)
defines an equivalence between
\operatorname{D}_{Qch}(Y)
and
\operatorname{D}(\Lambda^{\circ})

[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
. Assuming further that
Y
is regular and
\Lambda
is right noetherian, then
\Lambda
has finite global dimension and we obtain an equivalence of categories
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Y)\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda)
. This generalises to the context of smooth DM stacks
\mathcal{Y}
. It is this derived equivalence between the endomorphism algebra of tilting objects and the derived category of coherent sheaves of a bonafide crepant resolutions that motivates the notion of NCCR.

Using tilting theory, NCCRs have been obtained for a variety of cases, see for instance
[
21
,
20
,
19
,
22
]
. One key result in this context from previous work of Malter-Sheshmani we would like to point out is the following.

Theorem 2.12

(Theorem 4.11 in
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete simplicial fan such that
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
has a tilting complex
\mathcal{T}
. Let
p:\operatorname{tot}V\rightarrow X_{\Sigma}
be a toric vector bundle on
X_{\Sigma}
with fan
\mathcal{V}
. Let
f_{\Sigma}:\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}\rightarrow X_{\Sigma}
be the good moduli space. If
H^{i}(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{T}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{T}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}(f_{\Sigma}^{\ast}V^{\vee}))=0
for all
i\neq 0
, then there is a tilting complex on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{V}}
. If
V=\omega_{X_{\Sigma}}
, the ring
R=k[|\mathcal{V}|^{\vee}\cap M]
then has an NCCR.

Other, more algebraic, tools to construct NCCRs include dimer models and conic/divisorial modules. Using dimer models, Broomhead
[
7
]
proved the existence of NCCRs for three-dimensional Gorenstein toric algebras, whereas conic modules have been used to construct NCCRs for all simplicial toric algebras by Faber, Muller and Smith
[
10
]
. Recently Tomonaga
[
23
]
used divisorial modules and the combinatorics of upper sets to construct NCCRs for Gorenstein toric algebras coming from cones
\sigma
with
|\sigma(1)|=\dim\sigma+1
.

3.
Descending NCCRs

The main technical result of this paper concerns the descent of toric NCCRs in the following sense.
Up to a change of basis, all Gorenstein cones can be expressed as
\sigma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})
for some lattice polytope
P
. Given an NCCR for the toric algebra of such a cone
\sigma^{\prime}
and a face
P
of
Q
, geometrically we expect an NCCR for the toric algebra associated to
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
to exist. Note here that the two cones lie in distinct spaces
N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, having different dimensions. The geometric expectation comes from the fact that if we obtain the NCCR via a tilting object associated to a simplicial subdivision
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\sigma^{\prime}
, the regular triangulation of
Q
giving that subdivision descends to a regular subdivision of
P
. The toric stack coming from the restriction of
\Sigma^{\prime}
to
\sigma
is thus a smooth toric DM stack that provides a crepant resolution of
X_{\sigma}
. Unfortunately, tilting is not a local property and we cannot simply presume the existence of a tilting object. However, for the case of a toric NCCR, we are able to prove the desired descent in the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a lattice polytope and let
\sigma
be the cone
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
together with its toric algebra
R_{\sigma}=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M_{\sigma}]
, where
M_{\sigma}
is the character lattice of the toric variety defined by
\sigma
. Suppose
Q
is lattice equivalent to a face
F
of a lattice polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the cone
\sigma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Denoting the corresponding character lattice by
M^{\prime}
, let
R^{\prime}=k[(\sigma^{\prime})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]
be the toric algebra associated to
\sigma^{\prime}
. Suppose
R^{\prime}
has a toric NCCR
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Then
R
has a toric NCCR.

Proof.

Observe that the toric algebra
R_{F}=k[\sigma_{F}^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]
associated to the cone
\sigma_{F}=\operatorname{Cone}(F\times\{1\})
is obtained by localising the algebra
R^{\prime}
on some multiplicative set
S
, i.e.
R_{F}=S^{-1}R^{\prime}
. Specifically, this is the content of
[
8
, Proposition 1.3.16]
:
R_{F}
is the localisation of
R^{\prime}
at
\chi^{m}\in R^{\prime}
, where
m\in(\sigma^{\prime})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}
is the lattice element such that
\sigma_{F}=\sigma^{\prime}\cap H_{m}
(
H_{m}
is the hyperplane
\{u\in(N^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\}\subseteq(N^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
). The proof of Theorem
3.1
now splits into two parts. Firstly, we show that
R_{F}
has an NCCR and then we relate
R_{F}
to
R
and show that from there we can deduce the existence of an NCCR for
R
.

Write the NCCR
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
R^{\prime}
as
\Lambda^{\prime}=\operatorname{End}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})
with
M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}R
. Then
\Lambda^{\prime}\in\operatorname{CM}R
and
\operatorname{gl}\dim\Lambda^{\prime}<\infty
. For the first part of the proof, we shall show that
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}=\operatorname{End}_{R_{F}}(S^{-1}M)
where
S^{-1}M\in\operatorname{ref}R_{F}
,
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}\in\operatorname{CM}R_{F}
and
\operatorname{gl}\dim(S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime})<\infty
.
We begin by noting that the functor
S^{-1}
is an exact functor
[
1
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Since
\Lambda^{\prime}
is Noetherian, any
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
-module arises as module of the form
S^{-1}M
for some
\Lambda^{\prime}
-module
M
. Consider a projective resolution

0\rightarrow P^{m}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow P^{1}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

of
M
as
\Lambda^{\prime}
-module. Since
d:=\operatorname{gl}\dim(\Lambda^{\prime})<\infty
, we have
m<d
.

Apply the functor
S^{-1}
to it, which is exact and thus gives an exact sequence

0\rightarrow S^{-1}P^{m}\rightarrow S^{-1}P^{m-1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow S^{-1}P^{1}\rightarrow S^{-1}M\rightarrow 0.

Each of the modules
S^{-1}P^{i}
is projective as
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
-module
[
2
, Lemma 10.109.13]
.

Now, let us show that
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
is a trivial Azumaya algebra, i.e. of the form
\operatorname{End}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime\prime})
for some
M^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
. The NCCR
\Lambda^{\prime}
itself is of the form
\operatorname{End}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})
with
M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}R^{\prime}
. Note that as the module
M^{\prime}
is finitely generated and
R^{\prime}
is Noetherian,
M^{\prime}
is finitely presented. For a multiplicative set
S
over the Noetherian ring
R^{\prime}
, if
M^{\prime}
is finitely presented, localisation commutes with the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)
.

Hence,

S^{-1}\operatorname{End}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})=\operatorname{End}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}(S^{-1}M^{\prime}).

It remains to show that
M^{\prime\prime}:=S^{-1}M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}S^{-1}R^{\prime}
, noting that the rank of a module is preserved under localisation. Since
R^{\prime}
is Noetherian, the dual of a finitely generated module is finitely presented, and so
M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}R^{\prime}
implies that
\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime},R^{\prime})
is finitely presented. Being finitely presented localises (as the localisation functor is exact) and so we have

\displaystyle S^{-1}M^{\prime}

\displaystyle=S^{-1}\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime},R^{\prime}),R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}(S^{-1}\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime},R^{\prime}),S^{-1}R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Hom}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}(S^{-1}M^{\prime},S^{-1}R^{\prime}),S^{-1}R^{\prime}).

Hence,
M^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{ref}S^{-1}R^{\prime}
.
Finally, we aim to show that
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}\in\operatorname{CM}S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
. The algebra
S^{-1}R^{\prime}
is commutative Noetherian, as
R^{\prime}
is. Since the functor
S^{-1}
is exact, for any complex
C^{\bullet}
, we have
S^{-1}H_{i}(C^{\bullet})=H_{i}(S^{-1}C^{\bullet})
.

Applying this, we obtain

\operatorname{Ext}_{R^{\prime}}^{i}(\Lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime})=\operatorname{Ext}_{S^{-1}R^{\prime}}^{i}(S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime},S^{-1}R^{\prime}).

Since
\Lambda^{\prime}\in\operatorname{CM}R^{\prime}
, the left-hand side vanishes for
i>0
, and thus so does the right-hand side. In turn, this implies that
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}\in\operatorname{CM}S^{-1}R^{\prime}
, as required. So
S^{-1}\Lambda^{\prime}
is a toric NCCR for
R_{F}=S^{-1}R^{\prime}
when
\Lambda^{\prime}
is for
R^{\prime}
.

Next, let us relate
R_{F}
and
R
, following closely the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1.3.12]
. Denote by
N_{1}
the smallest saturated sublattice of
N^{\prime}
(the dual lattice to
M^{\prime}
) containing the generators of the cone
\sigma_{F}
. The quotient
N^{\prime}/N_{1}
is torsion-free and so
N^{\prime}=N_{1}\oplus N_{2}
, giving
M^{\prime}=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
on the level of dual lattices. Let
\sigma_{F,N_{1}}
be the cone
\sigma_{F}
considered within the lattice
N_{1}
. Then for the relevant semigroups we have
(\sigma_{F})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}=(\sigma_{F,N_{1}})^{\vee}\cap M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
, giving

R_{F}\simeq k[(\sigma_{F,N_{1}})^{\vee}\cap M_{1}]\otimes_{k}k[M_{2}].

But now by the lattice equivalence of the face
F
of
P
to
Q
, we see that
R\simeq k[(\sigma_{F,N_{1}})^{\vee}\cap M_{1}]
, and so
R_{F}\simeq R\otimes_{k}k[M_{2}]
. Note that the algebra
k[M_{2}]
is the coordinate ring of a torus of dimension
n-k
. Since both
R
and
k[M_{2}]
are integral domains, we have, by a result of Ischebeck’s
[
14
, Theorem 1.7]
, the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}R)\oplus\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}k[M_{2}])\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}(R\otimes k[M_{2}]))\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(k(\operatorname{Spec}R)\otimes k(\operatorname{Spec}k[M_{2}])).

Since the torus has trivial class-group, this reduces to the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\sigma})\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\sigma}\times(k^{\times})^{n-k})\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(k(X_{\sigma})\otimes k((k^{\times})^{n-k})).

In particular, the tensor product
k(X_{\sigma})\otimes k((k^{\times})^{n-k})
can be written as fraction ring
T^{-1}A
of the ring
A=k(X_{\sigma})[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-k}]
over the field
k(X_{\sigma})
and is thus a UFD, with trivial Picard group.

Thus,
\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\sigma}\times(k^{\times})^{n-k})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\sigma})\oplus\operatorname{Pic}((k^{\times})^{n-k})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\sigma})
. In particular, all rank 1 modules of
R\otimes k[M_{2}]
decompose as tensor products of rank 1 modules over
R
and
k[M_{2}]
, respectively. Since
\operatorname{Pic}(k[M_{2}])=0
, we have that the unique rank 1 module (up to isomorphism) is simply
k[M_{2}]
itself. Thus, we have
M^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of modules of the form
M_{R}\otimes k[M_{2}]
, where
M_{R}
is a rank 1 module over
R
. Furthermore,
M_{R}\in\operatorname{ref}R
due to the flatness of
-\otimes k[M_{2}]
.

The flatness and finite presentation of
M_{R}
also gives

\operatorname{End}_{R\otimes k[M_{2}]}(M_{R}\otimes k[M_{2}])\cong\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{R})\otimes k[M_{2}].

A Künneth-type result now yields

\operatorname{RHom}_{R}(M_{R},R)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{RHom}_{k[M_{2}]}(k[M_{2}],k[M_{2}])\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_{R\otimes k[M_{2}]}(p_{1}^{\ast}M_{R}\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}k[M_{2}],p_{1}^{\ast}R\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}k[M_{2}]),

where
p_{1},p_{2}
are the natural projections from
R\otimes k[M_{2}]
to
R
and
k[M_{2}]
.
Taking
H^{i}
on both sides gives

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R\otimes k[M_{2}]}(\operatorname{End}(M_{R})\otimes k[M_{2}],R\otimes k[M_{2}])\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\operatorname{Ext}^{p}_{R}(\operatorname{End}_{R}((M_{R}),R))\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{q}(k[M_{2}],k[M_{2}]).

Since the left-hand side vanishes for
i>0
, the equality to the right-hand side implies
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{R}),R)=0
for
i>0
and thus
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{R})\in\operatorname{CM}R
.

Finally, we note that
\operatorname{gl}\dim\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{R})<\infty
as
\operatorname{End}_{R\otimes k[M_{2}]}(M_{R}\otimes k[M_{2}])
is finite. Hence,
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{R})
is a toric NCCR of
R
.

∎

The proof above relied on descending rank 1 reflexive modules on
R\otimes k[M_{2}]
to reflexive modules on
R
, which does not happen in the same way for higher rank. Hence, the proof from that point onwards needs adjustment when considering NCCRs of a non-toric nature. We do still believe the statement to hold and thus reiterate the following conjecture.

Conjecture 3.2
.

The statement of Theorem
3.1
holds also if
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a non-toric NCCR.

4.
Application to (almost) simplicial toric algebras

Heuristically, as detailed in
[
19
]
, Theorem
3.1
and Conjecture
3.2
allows us to reduce the Conjecture
2.9
to the case of reflexive Gorenstein cones. Indeed, to do so we note the following well-known fact of toric geometry.

Proposition 4.1

(Proposition 2.2 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
P
be a lattice polytope. Then
P
is lattice equivalent to a face of some reflexive polytope
Q
.

In
[
19
]
, the authors prove the following result (the word toric was omitted but the proof shows the toric nature of the NCCR as it derives from a full strong exceptional collection).

Theorem 4.2

(Theorem 5.1 in
[
19
]
)
.

Let
P\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simplicial, reflexive polytope with
\leq n+2
vertices. Consider the cone
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
. Then
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
has a toric NCCR.

An immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
4.1
is the following short re-proof of the fact that all simplicial Gorenstein cones admit toric NCCRs. The existence of such NCCRs for all simplicial cones has been proven in
[
10
]
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
be a simplicial Gorenstein cone. Then
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
has an NCCR.

Proof.

Write
\sigma
as
\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
for a simplicial polytope
P
. By Proposition
4.1
there exists a reflexive polytope
Q
with a face that is lattice equivalent to
P
. Thus, it is sufficient to prove that for
\sigma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})
, there is an NCCR of
k[(\sigma^{\prime})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]
, where
M^{\prime}
is the corresponding character lattice of the affine toric variety
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. The proof of Proposition
4.1
constructs
Q
from
P
by repeated application of the wedge construction over a codimension 1 facet. Since
P
is simplicial, the number of vertices at each step increases by 1 and so
Q
is simplicial (see cf.
[
12
, Chapter 15]
). Then
k[(\sigma^{\prime})^{\vee}\cap M^{\prime}]
admits a toric NCCR by Theorem
4.2
, and Theorem
3.1
guarantees the existence of an NCCR of
R
, as desired.
∎

Corollary
4.3
also follows as a consequence of the work by Ballard et al.
[
3
]
and can be considered well-established in the literature. The next case to be considered is that of toric varieties associated to cones
\sigma
with Picard rank 1, i.e. where the cone has
\dim\sigma+1
extremal rays. We refer to such cones as
almost simplicial
.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a cone
\sigma
is
almost simplicial
if
|\sigma(1)|=\dim\sigma+1
.

Using Theorem
3.1
, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\sigma\subset(N\oplus\mathbb{Z})_{\mathbb{R}}
be an almost simplicial Gorenstein cone, i.e.
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
with
P\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
a lattice polytope with
\dim P+2
vertices. Then the toric algebra
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap(M\oplus\mathbb{Z})]
associated to the cone
\sigma
has a toric NCCR.

This result has also recently been proven by Tomonaga
[
23
]
. Tomonaga’s proof uses a partial order on upper sets, and is algebraic in nature. The proof we will give below resembles the proof we give of Corollary
4.3

. Given a polytope

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\Sigma

I_{+}:=\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}

giving forbidden cones:

Proof.

Using the above list of maximal cones, it is immediately clear that these two sets are primitive collections, so it suffices to show no others exist. Since

containing both

\rho_{1}\in I\subset\Sigma(1)

\rho_{2}\not\in I
and so

is not a primitive collection. Similar argumentation shows that no primitive collection

and its complement

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a collection of positive numbers

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}r_{i}v_{i}=0

\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}r_{i}=1

\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}\alpha_{i}v_{i}=0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}\alpha_{i}=0

Begin by noting that

directly.
To find the collection

, note that the fan
\Sigma

\sum_{i=1}^{n+3}r_{i}>0

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
,
\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{n+2}<0

\vartheta
is a point inside the interior of the line segment
\theta_{+}^{+}
. Then one verifies that
\vartheta-\sum_{\rho\in I_{+}}D_{\rho}\in\theta_{+}^{-}
. For any non-empty, proper subset
J\subsetneq I_{+}
then
\vartheta-\sum_{\rho\in J}D_{\rho}
lies in the interior of
\Delta
.
Similarly, if
\vartheta\in\theta_{-}^{+}
, then
\vartheta+\sum_{\rho\in I_{-}}D_{\rho}\in\theta_{-}^{-}
and for any
\emptyset\neq J\subsetneq I_{-}
, the point
\vartheta+\sum_{\rho\in J}D_{\rho}
lies in the interior of the parallelogram
\Delta
.

By
[
5
, Corollary 4.8]
,
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma})
is generated by line bundles and so it suffices to show that the category
\mathcal{D}
generated by the line bundles in
\mathcal{S}
contains all line bundles. Move the polytope
p+\Delta
by varying
p
in line with constant
f(p)
until a new line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}(E)
appears. The argumentation for both possible choices of direction is analogous, so we assume that we move in the direction of increasing
\alpha(x)
, i.e. the new line bundle first crosses into the parallelogram
p+\Delta
via the line segments
p+\theta_{\pm}^{+}
. Note that the image of
\mathcal{L}
has to lie in either the interior of
p+\theta_{+}^{+}
or the interior of
p+\theta_{-}^{+}
as their intersection point moves along a non-rational line due to the generic choice of
r_{i},p
. Both cases are analogous, so we may assume
\mathcal{L}\in p+\theta_{+}^{+}
.

We claim that the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
lies in
\mathcal{D}
. For any line non-empty
J\subsetneq I_{+}
the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(E-\sum_{\rho\in J}D_{\rho})
is in
\mathcal{D}
as its image lies inside the polytope
p+\Delta
(and the corresponding line bundles are all in
\mathcal{D}
). Consider now the Koszul complex on
\mathbb{C}^{n+3}
given by
x_{\rho}
,
\rho\in I_{+}
. This complex resolves the structure sheaf of a coordinate subspace outside of
U_{\Sigma}
, yielding a long exact sequence of sheaves on
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
(see also
[
5
]
).

Twisting by
\mathcal{L}
, we obtain

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(E-\sum_{\rho\in I_{+}}D_{\rho})\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\rho\in I_{+}}\mathcal{O}(E-D_{\rho})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\rightarrow 0.

Since all terms in this long exact sequence are in
\mathcal{D}
apart from the last, we also have
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Thus, so long as the image
q
of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
satisfies
|f(q-p)|\leq\frac{1}{2},
we have shown
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{D}
. A similar argument works for moving the parallelogram in the direction of constant
\alpha
by using the Koszul resolutions for
I_{+}
(or equivalently
I_{-}
), noting that
-1<f(-\sum_{\rho\in J}D_{\rho})<0
for any non-empty subset
J\subsetneq I_{+}
. So we have shown that all line bundles lie in
\mathcal{D}
and thus
\mathcal{S}
is a full strong exceptional collection.
∎

Using the explicit full, strong exceptional collection
\mathcal{S}
constructed above now suffices to prove the Theorem
4.5
.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Consider the tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{L}
built from the full, strong exceptional collection
\mathcal{S}
. In view of Theorem
2.12
, we aim to verify the following vanishing condition:

H^{i}\left(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}\left(l\cdot\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}\right)\right)=0\text{ for }i,l>0.

The components of
\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\vee}
are of the form
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2}^{\vee}
for
\mathcal{L}_{i}\in S
and so
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\in\Delta
. Tensoring with
\mathcal{O}\left(l\cdot\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}\right)
moves
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma})
on a line of constant
\alpha
, away from the forbidden cone
F_{\emptyset}
and parallel to the supporting hyperplanes of the forbidden cones
F_{I_{\pm}}
. Thus, for any
l>0
, the line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}\left(l\cdot\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}\right)
remains outside the three forbidden cones and hence is acyclic, as required. Theorem
2.12
thus shows that the toric algebra associated to
\operatorname{Cone}(Q\times\{1\})
has a toric NCCR. Since
P\times\{1\}
is (up to lattice equivalence) a face of
Q
, Theorem
3.1
shows that
R=k[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
has a toric NCCR, as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Whilst Corollary
4.3
and Theorem
4.5
are not new results, having been proven in
[
10
]
and
[
23
]
respectively, the descent of toric NCCRs via Theorem
3.1
, we provides a unified approach that works in both cases.
Affine toric Gorenstein varieties of higher Picard rank no longer necessarily admit toric NCCRs, as a counterexample by Špenko and Van den Bergh
[
21
, Example 6.1]
shows. The toric algebra is associated to a four-dimensional cone
\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}(P\times\{1\})
with 6 extremal rays, for which no toric NCCRs exist. Consequentially, any reflexive polytope containing the polytope
P
as face itself cannot admit a toric NCCR. Thus, any smooth toric DM stack
\mathcal{X}_{\Sigma}
where
\Sigma
simplicially subdivides the face fan of
P
cannot admit a tilting bundle that fulfills the required cohomology vanishing condition to give a tilting bundle on
[\operatorname{tot}\omega_{X_{\Sigma}}]
. However, the authors hope that proving Conjecture
3.2
can help give a general proof to Conjecture
2.9
in a similar manner as was done here for the cases of (almost) simplicial toric algebras.

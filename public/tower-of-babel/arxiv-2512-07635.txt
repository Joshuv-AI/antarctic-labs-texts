Factorization envelopes and enveloping vertex algebras
Yusuke Nishinaka

Abstract
. We construct a factorization algebra, via the factorization envelope, starting from a Lie conformal algebra, and prove that the associated vertex algebra is isomorphic to its enveloping vertex algebra. Our construction generalizes the Kac–Moody factorization algebra of Costello–Gwilliam and the Virasoro factorization algebra of Williams. Moreover, by considering the super analogue of this construction, we obtain new factorization algebras corresponding to vertex superalgebras, such as the Neveu–Schwarz vertex superalgebra and the N = 2 N=2 vertex superalgebra.



Introduction

Factorization algebras, introduced by Costello and Gwilliam
[
CG17
,
CG21
]
, provide a mathematical framework for describing the observables of a quantum field theory. Using the renormalization techniques developed in
[
C11
]
, they constructed the observables of a perturbative quantum field theory and proved that the resulting structure indeed forms a factorization algebra.

Vertex algebras, on the other hand, offer an algebraic formulation of two-dimensional chiral conformal field theory. It is therefore natural to ask how factorization algebras on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
relate to vertex algebras.
In
[
CG17
, Chapter 5]
, Costello and Gwilliam developed a general procedure for extracting a vertex algebra from a factorization algebra on
\mathbb{C}
. Conversely, they constructed two factorization algebras—one associated with the
\beta
-
\gamma
vertex algebra and the other with the affine vertex algebra—for which this procedure applies, and proved that the resulting vertex algebra is isomorphic to the original one in both cases. Their construction uses an important method for building factorization algebras, called
factorization envelopes
. Moreover, Williams
[
W17
]
constructed a factorization algebra from the Virasoro vertex algebra using the same method. For general vertex algebras, Bruegmann
[
B20a
,
B20b
]
constructed a factorization algebra associated to an arbitrary vertex algebra. However, there are several unsatisfactory aspects in the present situation:

•

To extract a vertex algebra from a factorization algebra, one needs to impose an analytic structure on factorization algebras. Costello and Gwilliam start from a factorization algebra taking values in the category of chain complexes of differentiable vector spaces. The notion of differentiable vector spaces is convenient for BV field theory, since many topological vector spaces can be realized as differentiable vector spaces, and they form a Grothendiek abelian category, so that the standard tools of homological algebra apply immediately (see
[
CG17
, Appendix B]
for more details). However, the notion is somewhat unintuitive, as a differentiable vector space is a sheaf on the site of smooth manifolds. Therefore, the construction of Costello and Gwilliam requires some familiarity with this concept.

•

In
[
B20b
]
, Bruegmann constructed a geometric vertex algebra—whose categorical equivalence with vertex algebras was proved in
[
B20a
]
—from a factorization algebra taking values in bornological vector spaces. The notion of bornological vector spaces is an axiomatization of bounded sets in a topological vector space, so the concept is more familiar than that of differentiable vector spaces. However, the relationship with the setting of Costello and Gwilliam—namely, the construction from a factorization algebra taking values in chain complexes—has not been discussed.

•

Bruegmann also constructed, in
[
B20b
]
, a factorization algebra from an arbitrary geometric vertex algebra. However, the relationship with those constructed via factorization envelopes by Costello–Gwilliam and Williams has not been established. Moreover, although he proved that this construction is a right inverse to the previous construction of geometric vertex algebras, it has not been shown whether it gives rise to a categorical equivalence between factorization algebras and vertex algebras.

We now remark that there is another concept of factorization algebras introduced by Beilinson and Drinfeld
[
BD04
]
. Beilinson–Drinfeld factorization algebras are essentially the same as chiral algebras, which provide an algebro-geometric formulation of two-dimensional conformal field theory. It is known that there is a categorical equivalence between chiral algebras on the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and vertex algebras
[
BD04
, §0.15]
(see also
[
B+19
, Corollary A.2]
). Thus, for Beilinson–Drinfeld factorization algebras, a categorical equivalence with vertex algebras has already been established.

Costello–Gwilliam factorization algebras can be viewed as an analytic version of the concept of factorization algebras. In
[
CG17
, §1.4.1]
, Costello and Gwilliam heuristically explain the connection between these two notions of factorization algebras. In order to turn this explanation into a rigorous mathematical theorem, a deeper understanding of the relationship between Costello–Gwilliam factorization algebras and vertex algebras is of significant importance.

The original motivation for the author’s preprint
[
N1
]
was to construct factorization algebras from vertex algebras in a manner compatible with the construction via factorization envelopes, and to identify the class of factorization algebras that is categorically equivalent to vertex algebras. However, this goal has not yet been achieved.

Since the
\beta
-
\gamma
vertex algebra, the affine vertex algebra, and the Virasoro vertex algebra can be realized in a unified way as the
enveloping vertex algebras
of Lie conformal algebras, it is natural to seek a generalization of the construction via factorization envelopes due to Costello–Gwilliam and Williams. This note provides such a generalization, namely the construction of a factorization algebra starting from a Lie conformal algebra.

Main results

We first establish a general procedure for extracting a vertex algebra from a factorization algebra on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. We work with factorization algebras taking values in bornological vector spaces, rather than in chain complexes of differentiable vector spaces as in
[
CG17
]
, because the notion of differentiable vector spaces is less familiar and not needed for our purposes. Moreover, we construct a vertex algebra directly, without passing through a geometric vertex algebra as in
[
B20a
,
B20b
]
.

A prefactorization algebra is a precosheaf equipped with a family of morphisms called the factorization products. Thus, on
\mathbb{C}
, it assigns an object
\mathcal{F}(U)
to each open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, and a morphism
\mathcal{F}^{U}_{V}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{F}(V)
for each inclusion
U\subset V
of open subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
To extract a vertex algebra from a prefactorization algebra, we need to impose an analytic structure on
\mathcal{F}(U)
. In this note, we adopt bornology, so each
\mathcal{F}(U)
is a bornological vector space. We further assume that
\mathcal{F}(U)
is a convenient vector space, i.e., a complete and separated convex bornological vector space, so that we can apply complex analysis to functions with values in
\mathcal{F}(U)
.
Moreover, our factorization algebra
\mathcal{F}
needs to be equivariant under the natural action of the group
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
on
\mathbb{C}
as isometric affine transformations. This means that
\mathcal{F}
is equipped with a morphism of convenient vector spaces

\sigma_{(q,z),U}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{F}((q,z)U)

for
(q,z)\in S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
and open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, satisfying certain compatibility conditions.
Thus, we consider an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{C}
taking values in convenient vector spaces.

Since the open disk
D_{R}
centered at
0
with radius
R
is invariant under the action of
S^{1}
, for each
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, one can define a weight space by

\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})\mid\forall q\in S^{1},\,\sigma_{(q,0),D_{R}}(a)=q^{\Delta}a\}.

The compatibility condition of
\sigma_{(q,z),U}
implies that, for
0<r<R\leq\infty
, the image of
\mathcal{F}(D_{r})_{\Delta}
under the linear map
\mathcal{F}^{D_{r}}_{D_{R}}
lies in
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}
.
We call
\mathcal{F}
an
amenably holomorphic prefactorization algebra
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

The
\mathbb{C}
-action makes
\mathcal{F}
a holomorphically translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra (
section

3.1
).

(ii)

For
0<R\leq\infty
, we have
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}=0
(
\Delta\ll 0
).

(iii)

For
0<r<R\leq\infty
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, the map
\mathcal{F}^{D_{r}}_{D_{R}}\colon\mathcal{F}(D_{r})_{\Delta}\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}
is a linear isomorphism.

Note that the analytic structure on
\mathcal{F}
is needed to state the condition (i).

Theorem 1A

(
3.2.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra on
\mathbb{C}
taking values in convenient vector spaces.
If
\mathcal{F}
is amenably holomorphic, then the linear space

\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}

admits the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex algebra.

In
section

3.2
, we also compare
theorem

1A
with the construction of Costello and Gwilliam (
section

3.2
). We note that the term amenably holomorphic was first used in
[
GGW20
]
to refer prefactorization algebras satisfying the conditions in
[
CG17
, Theorem 5.3.3]
.
section

3.2
justifies our usage of this term, although the conditions in
section

3.2
are slightly modified from those in
[
CG17
, Theorem 5.3.3]
for our purposes (see
section

3.2
for instance).

Moreover, in
section

3.3
, we introduce the notion of convenient vector superspaces and extend
theorem

1A
to the super setting, that is, we provide a construction of a vertex superalgebra from a prefactorization algebra with values in convenient vector superspaces.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra on
\mathbb{C}
taking values in convenient vector superspaces. In the super case, for each
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define a weight space by

\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta+i/2}\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\overline{i}}\mid\forall q\in S^{1},\,\sigma_{(q,0),D_{R}}(a)=q^{\Delta}a\}\quad(i\in\{0,1\}),

where
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\overline{i}}
denotes the parity-
\overline{i}
component of
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})
. The notion of amenably holomorphic prefactorization algebras remains valid in the super case, with only minor modifications.

Theorem 1B

(
3.3.1
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra on
\mathbb{C}
taking values in convenient vector superspaces. If
\mathcal{F}
is amenably holomorphic, then the linear superspace

\mathbf{V}_{\!R}^{\mathrm{super}}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta},\hskip 5.69046pt\text{where}\hskip 6.25963pt\mathbf{V}_{\!R}^{\mathrm{super}}(\mathcal{F})_{\overline{i}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta+i/2}\quad(i\in\{0,1\})

admits the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded vertex superalgebra.

The second result concerns the construction of a prefactorization algebra from a Lie conformal algebra via the factorization envelope.

A Lie conformal algebra consists of a linear space
L
, a linear map
T\colon L\to L
called the translation operator, and a linear map
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]\colon L\otimes L\to L[\lambda]
called the
\lambda
-bracket. Here
L[\lambda]
denotes the linear space of polynomials in the variable
\lambda
with coefficients in
L
.
The notion of Lie conformal algebras can be regarded as the “polar part” of a vertex algebra, in the sense that it governs the operator product expansion of vertex operators. Thus, every vertex algebra naturally gives rise to a Lie conformal algebra. This assignment defines a functor from the category of vertex algebras to that of Lie conformal algebras, which admits a left adjoint functor

\{\text{Lie conformal algebras}\}\to\{\text{vertex algebras}\},\quad L\mapsto V(L).

The vertex algebra
V(L)
obtained in this way is called the enveloping vertex algebra.
The construction of
V(L)
can be describes roughly as follows:
For a Lie conformal algebra
L
, one can define a Lie algebra
\Lie(L)
, called the current Lie algebra or the Borcherds Lie algebra. This Lie algebra contains a Lie subalgebra denoted by
\Lie_{+}(L)
. Then the induced representation

V(L)\coloneqq U(\Lie L)\otimes_{U(\Lie_{+}(L))}\mathbb{C},

where
\mathbb{C}
is regarded as the one-dimensional trivial representation of
\Lie_{+}(L)
, carries the structure of a vertex algebra.

Let
X
be a complex manifold, and consider the Dolbeault complex
\mathcal{A}^{0,\bullet}_{X}(U)
on an open subset
U\subset X
. Varying open subsets
U\subset X
, it forms a sheaf on
X
. For a Lie conformal algebra
L
and a morphism
D\colon\mathcal{A}^{0,\bullet}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}^{0,\bullet}_{X}
of sheaves satisfying

D_{U}(\xi\wedge\eta)=D_{U}\xi\wedge\eta+\xi\wedge D_{U}\eta\quad(\xi\in\mathcal{A}^{0,k}_{X}(U),\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{0,l}_{X}(U)),

we construct, in
section

4.2
, a prefactorization algebra
L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
on
X
taking values in dg Lie algebras internal to the category of convenient vector spaces.

A factorization envelope is a construction of a prefactorization algebra from one valued in dg Lie algebras, obtained by applying the symmetric monoidal functor
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}
that takes the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex. Thus, a factorization envelope yields a prefactorization algebra
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
on
X
with values in chain complexes of convenient vector spaces. Moreover, by composing this with the symmetric monoidal functor
H_{\bullet}
of taking homology, we obtain a prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{X,D}\coloneqq H_{\bullet}C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
on
X
with values in convenient vector spaces.

Now we focus on the case where
X=\mathbb{C}
and
D=\partial_{z}
. In this case, for an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie conformal algebra
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{N}}L_{\Delta}
, we can equip
L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
with an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant structure, which in turn induces an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant structure on
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
. To verify that
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
is amenably holomorphic, we assume the following technical condition:

(*)

There exists a graded linear subspace
E\subset L
such that

\mathbb{C}[T]\otimes E\to L,\quad p(T)\otimes x\mapsto p(T)x

is a linear isomorphism. Here
T\colon L\to L
denotes the translation operator of
L
.

Many interesting Lie conformal algebras, such as the Virasoro conformal algebra (
section

1.2.2
) and the current Lie conformal algebra (
section

1.2.2
), are one-dimensional central extensions
L_{\omega_{\lambda}}=L\oplus\mathbb{C}C
of a Lie conformal algebra
L
satisfying the condition
(*)
, associated with a
2
-cocycle
\omega_{\lambda}\colon L\otimes L\to\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
.

For a 2-cocycle
\omega_{\lambda}
of a Lie conformal algebra
L
, one defines a
(-1)
-shifted
2
-cocycle
\omega
of the prefactorization algebra
L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
. Thus, the twisted version of the factorization envelope yields an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet\,\omega}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
.

Theorem 2A

(
4.3.3
,
4.3.11
)
.

Let
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{N}}L_{\Delta}
be an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie conformal algebra satisfying the condition
(*)
above.

(1)

Then the
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
is amenably holomorphic, and its associated vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}})
is isomorphic to the enveloping vertex algebra
V(L)
as an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra.

(2)

For a
2
-cocycle
\omega_{\lambda}\colon L\otimes L\to\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
of
L
, the
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet\,\omega}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
is amenably holomorphic, and its associated vertex algebra
\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet\,\omega}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}})
is isomorphic to the enveloping vertex algebra
V(L_{\omega_{\lambda}})
as an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra.

theorem

2A
(2) provides a generalization of the constructions of Costello–Gwilliam
[
CG17
, §5.5]
and Williams
[
W17
]
.

To prove
theorem

2A
, we examine the value of the prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
on annuli. We find that it contains the universal enveloping algebra
U(\Lie(L))
of the current Lie algebra
\Lie(L)
(
section

4.3.2
). Thus, in the case of general complex manifold, we can view
L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
as a higher-dimensional analogue of the current Lie algebra.

The construction of
L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
can be extended to the super setting with minor modifications. That is, for a Lie conformal superalgebra
L
, one can construct a prefactorization algebra
L^{\bullet}_{X,D}
on
X
taking values in dg Lie algebras internal to the category of convenient vector superspaces. In the case where
X=\mathbb{C}
,
D=\partial_{z}
, and
L
is an
\mathbb{N}/2
-graded Lie conformal superalgebra, one can equip
L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
with an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant structure. Thus, the factorization envelope yields an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}}
on
\mathbb{C}
with values in convenient vector superspaces.

Theorem 2B

(
4.3.13
)
.

Let
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{N}/2}L_{\Delta}
be an
\mathbb{N}
/2-graded Lie conformal superalgebra satisfying the following condition: There exists a graded linear subsuperspace
E\subset L
such that

,

is a linear isomorphism.

(1)

Then the

-equivariant prefactorization algebra

as an
\mathbb{N}/2
-graded vertex superalgebra.

(2)

For a
2
-cocycle

of

-equivariant prefactorization algebra

\mathbf{V}_{\!R}^{\mathrm{super}}(H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet\,\omega}L^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C},\partial_{z}})

), the

), and the

). Therefore, we obtain new factorization algebras corresponding to vertex superalgebras.

It is difficult and subtle to choose an appropriate analytic structure on factorization algebras to work with. Differentiable vector spaces provide a comprehensive framework and offer one solution to this choice. However, this framework sacrifices intuition, and many natural examples arising in quantum field theory in fact remain within the setting of convenient vector spaces. Throughout this note, we consistently use convenient vector spaces and demonstrate their advantages when addressing problems relating factorization algebras and vertex algebras.

1.
Preliminaries: factorization algebras and vertex algebras

This section provides a preliminary introduction to factorization algebras and vertex algebras.

1.1.
Factorization algebras

In this subsection, we review the basic definitions and constructions of prefactorization algebras. The main reference is
[
CG17
, Chapter 3]
.

1.1.1.
Definition of factorization algebras

Recall that a precosheaf
\mathcal{F}
on a topological space
X
with values in a category
\mathsf{A}
is simply a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{A}
, where
\mathfrak{U}_{X}
denotes the poset of all open subsets of
X
ordered by inclusion. Moreover, a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
of precosheaves is just a natural transformation from the functor
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{G}
.

Fix a topological space
X
and a symmetric monoidal category
\mathsf{M}
. We denote by
\otimes
the tensor product of
\mathsf{M}
, and by
1_{\mathsf{M}}
its unit object.

Definition 1.1.1

(
[
CG17
, §3.1.1]
)
.

1.1.3.
Factorization envelopes

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a symmetric monoidal linear category with all colimits, such that the tensor product preserves colimits in each variable. The category
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
of complexes in
\mathsf{M}
naturally inherits a symmetric monoidal linear structure:

•

For
X,Y\in\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
, the tensor product
X\otimes Y
is defined as follows: For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the object in degree
n
is

(X\otimes Y)^{n}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{l+m=n}X^{l}\otimes Y^{m},

and the differential in degree
n
is

d_{X\otimes Y}^{n}\colon(X\otimes Y)^{n}\to(X\otimes Y)^{n+1},\quad x\otimes y\mapsto d_{X}^{n}(x)\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes d_{Y}(y).

Here
|\cdot|
denotes the degree of
X
.

•

The unit object is
1_{\mathsf{M}}
, regarded as a complex concentrated in degree
0
.

•

The braiding is given by

(X\otimes Y)^{n}\to(Y\otimes X)^{n},\quad x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x

for each
X,Y\in\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
.

Moreover, the category
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
has all colimits, and its tensor product preserves colimits separately in each variable.

For
X\in\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
, we denote by

T(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}X^{\otimes p},\quad\Sym(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\Sym^{p}(X),\quad{\bigwedge}(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge^{p}(X)

the tensor algebra, the symmetric algebra, and the exterior algebra, respectively.
There is an isomorphism
X[1]^{\otimes p}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}X^{\otimes p}[p]
defined by

(X[1]^{\otimes p})^{n}\to X^{\otimes p}[p]^{n},\quad\otimes_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}\mapsto\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{(p-i)(|x_{i}|-1)}\bigr)\otimes_{i=1}^{p}x_{i},

which induces an isomorphism

\Sym^{p}(X[1])\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}(\bigwedge^{p}X)[p]

in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
. Here
|\cdot|
denotes the degree of
X
.
From now on, we identify
\Sym^{p}(X[1])
with
\bigwedge^{p}(X)[p]
via this isomorphism.

A
dg Lie algebra
in
\mathsf{M}
is just a Lie algebra object in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
. More explicitly, a dg Lie algebra is an object
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
endowed with a morphism
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
, called the Lie bracket, satisfying the usual skew-symmetry and Jacobi identity. A morphism of dg Lie algebras is a morphism in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
that respects the Lie brackets. We denote by
\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})
the category of dg Lie algebras in
\mathsf{M}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a dg Lie algebra in
\mathsf{M}
, and denote by
|\cdot|
the degree of
\mathfrak{g}
. For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a morphism
\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes p}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes(p-1)}
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
defined by

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes p})^{n}

\displaystyle\to(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes(p-1)})^{n},

\displaystyle\otimes_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq p}(-1)^{|x_{i}|(|x_{i+1}|+\cdots+|x_{j-1}|)+i}x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\check{x_{i}}\otimes\cdots\otimes[x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{p},

which induces a morphism
\bigwedge^{p}(\mathfrak{g})\to\bigwedge^{p-1}(\mathfrak{g})
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
. Here
\check{x_{i}}
indicates to remove
x_{i}
. Thus, we obtain a morphism

\Sym^{p}(\mathfrak{g}[1])=(\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{g})[p]\to(\bigwedge^{p-1}\mathfrak{g})[p]=\Sym^{p-1}(\mathfrak{g}[1])[1],

and hence a morphism

d_{[\cdot,\cdot]}\colon\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])\to\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])[1]

in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
. Since
d_{[\cdot,\cdot]}
satisfies

d_{[\cdot,\cdot]}^{n+1}\circ d^{n}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}=0,\quad d^{n+1}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}\circ d^{n}_{\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])}=-d^{n+1}_{\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])}\circ d_{[\cdot,\cdot]}^{n},

the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded object
\{\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
becomes a complex with differential
\{d^{n}_{\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])}+d^{n}_{[\cdot,\cdot]}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This complex is called the
Chevalley–Eilenberg chain complex
of the dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and we denote it by
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
:

C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])^{-n},\quad d_{n}^{\mathrm{CE}}\coloneqq d_{\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])}^{-n}+d_{[\cdot,\cdot]}^{-n}.

For a morphism
f\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
in
\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})
,

C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(f)\coloneqq\Sym(f[1])\colon C_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g})\to C_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{h})

is a morphism of complexes. The assignment

C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\colon\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M}),\quad\mathfrak{g}\mapsto C_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g})

gives rise to a functor by
f\mapsto C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(f)
. Note that the natural morphism
\mu_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}\colon\Sym(\mathfrak{g}[1])\otimes\Sym(\mathfrak{h}[1])\to\Sym((\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h})[1])
is compatible with the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential
d^{\mathrm{CE}}
. Hence

\mu_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}\colon C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{h})\to C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h})

is a morphism of complexes. We find that
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\colon\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
becomes a symmetric monoidal functor via
\mu_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}
and the canonical morphism of the coproduct
1_{\mathsf{M}}=\Sym^{0}(0_{\mathsf{M}}[1])\to C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}(0_{\mathsf{M}})
.

Let
X
be a topological space. For a prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})
, composing it with the symmetric monoidal functor
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}
yields a new prefactorization algebra

C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M}).

We call
C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}
the
factorization envelope
of
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover, since the functor
H_{\bullet}\colon\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})\to\mathsf{M}
of taking homology is naturally symmetric monoidal, we have a prefactorization algebra

H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\coloneqq H_{\bullet}C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{M}.

We also call
H^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}
the factorization envelope of
\mathcal{F}
.

There is a twisted version of the factorization envelopes. To describe it, recall the notion of 2-cocycles for a dg Lie algebra. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a dg Lie algebra in
\mathsf{M}
. A
k
-shifted
2
-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
is a morphism
\omega\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to 1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
satisfying:

(i)

\omega(y\otimes x)=-(-1)^{|x||y|}\omega(x\otimes y)
,

(ii)

\omega(x\otimes[y,z])=\omega([x,y]\otimes z)+(-1)^{|x||y|}\omega(y,[x,z])
.

Given a
k
-shifted
2
-cocycle
\omega
of
\mathfrak{g}
, the object
\mathfrak{g}_{\omega}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}\oplus 1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]
in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})
carries the structure of a dg Lie algebra whose bracket is defined by

[x,y]^{\sim}\coloneqq[x,y]+\omega(x,y)\quad(x,y\in\mathfrak{g}),\quad[\mathfrak{g},1_{\mathsf{M}}]\coloneqq 0.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})
be a prefactorization algebra. A
k
-shifted
2

\mathcal{F}
is a family of
k

\omega_{U}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\otimes\mathcal{F}(U)\to 1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]

of dg Lie algebras such that

\omega_{U}=\omega_{V}\circ(\mathcal{F}^{U}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{U}_{V})

for every inclusion of open subsets
U\subset V
of
X
. Given a
k
-shifted cocycle
\omega=\{\omega_{U}\}_{U\in\mathfrak{U}_{X}}
of
\mathcal{F}
, the assignment

\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M}),\quad U\mapsto\mathcal{F}(U)_{\omega_{U}}=\mathcal{F}(U)\oplus 1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]

has the structure of a prefactorization algebra as follows:

•

The extension morphism is given by

(\mathcal{F}_{\omega})^{U}_{V}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{U}_{V}\oplus\id_{1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]}\colon\mathcal{F}_{\omega}(U)\to\mathcal{F}_{\omega}(V)

for each open subsets
U,V\subset X
with
U\subset V
.

•

The factorization product is given by

(\mathcal{F}_{\omega})^{U,V}_{W}|_{\mathcal{F}(U)\oplus\mathcal{F}(V)}=\mathcal{F}^{U,V}_{W},\quad(\mathcal{F}_{\omega})^{U,V}_{W}|_{1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]}=\id_{1_{\mathsf{M}}[k]},

for open subsets
U,V,W\subset X
with
U\sqcup V\subset W
.

•

The unit is given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1_{\mathsf{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(\varnothing)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\omega}(\varnothing).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1_{\mathcal{F}}}

For a prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{dgLie}(\mathsf{M})
and a

-shifted

-cocycle
\omega
of

C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet\,\omega}\mathcal{F}\coloneqq C^{\mathrm{CE}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}_{\omega}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{X}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{M})

is called the
twisted factorization envelope
of

by

. Moreover, the prefactorization algebra

by

1.2.1.
Definition of vertex algebras

We briefly recall the definitions and basic examples of vertex algebras.

An (algebraic) field on a linear space
V
is a formal series
a(z)\in(\End V)\llbracket z^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
such that
a(z)v\in V(\!(z)\!)
for all
v\in V
.
Here, writing
a(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}a_{n}
with
a_{n}\in\End V
, we denote
a(z)v\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}a_{n}(v)
.

Definition 1.2.1
.

A
vertex algebra
is a tuple
(V,\ket{0},T,Y)
consisting of

•

a linear space
V
, called the state space,

•

an element
\ket{0}\in V
, called the vacuum,

•

a linear map
T\colon V\to V
, called the translation operator,

•

a linear map

Y\colon V\to\Fld V,\quad a\mapsto Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)},

called the state-field correspondence, or the vertex operators,

which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

(vacuum axiom)
We have
Y(\ket{0},z)=\id_{V}
. Furthermore, for
a\in V
, we have
Y(a,z)\ket{0}\in V\llbracket z\rrbracket
, and
Y(a,z)\ket{0}|_{z=0}=a
.

(ii)

(translation invariance)
We have
T\ket{0}=0
and
[T,Y(a,z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,z)
for
a\in V
.

(iii)

(locality)
For
a,b\in V
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(z-w)^{N}[Y(a,z),Y(b,w)]=0

in
(\End V)\llbracket z^{\pm 1},w^{\pm 1}\rrbracket
.

Let
V
and
W
be vertex algebras. A
morphism of vertex algebras
is a linear map
f\colon V\to W
which satisfies

f(\ket{0})=\ket{0},\quad f\circ T=T\circ f,\quad f(Y(a,z)b)=Y(f(a),z)f(b)\quad(a,b\in V).

We present two basic examples of vertex algebras: the Virasoro vertex algebra and the affine vertex algebra. The constructions of these vertex algebras go back to the work of Frenkel and Zhu
[
FZ92
]
.

Example 1.2.2

Consider the Virasoro algebra

.

-graded vertex algebras. A

1.2.2.
Enveloping vertex algebras of Lie conformal algebras

We recall the notion of Lie conformal algebras and the construction of enveloping vertex algebras.

Definition 1.2.7
.

A
Lie conformal algebra
(or
vertex Lie algebra
) is a tuple
(L,T,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
consisting of

•

a linear space
L
,

•

a linear map
T\colon L\to L
,

•

a linear map

[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]\colon L\otimes L\to L[\lambda],\quad a\otimes b\mapsto[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n!}\lambda^{n}a_{(n)}b,

called the
\lambda
-bracket,

which satisfies the following conditions for
a,b,c\in L
:

(i)

(sesquilinearity)

[Ta_{\lambda}b]=-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b]
.

(ii)

(skew-symmetry)

[b_{\lambda}a]=-[a_{-\lambda-T}b]
.

(iii)

(Jacobi identity)

[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]=[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]]
.

Let
K
and
L
be Lie conformal algebras. A
morphism of Lie conformal algebras
is a linear map
f\colon K\to L
which satisfies

f\circ T=T\circ f,\quad f([a_{\lambda}b])=[f(a)_{\lambda}f(b)]\quad(a,b\in K).

Definition 1.2.8
.

Let
L
be a Lie conformal algebra. A
2
-cocycle of
L
is a linear map

\omega_{\lambda}\colon L\otimes L\to\mathbb{C}[\lambda],\quad a\otimes b\mapsto\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n!}\lambda^{n}\omega_{(n)}(a,b),

which satisfies the following conditions for
a,b,c\in L
:

(i)

(sesquilinearity)

\omega_{\lambda}(Ta,b)=-\lambda\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)
.

(ii)

(skew-symmetry)

\omega_{\lambda}(b,a)=-\omega_{-\lambda}(a,b)
.

(iii)

(Jacobi identity)

\omega_{\lambda}(a,[b_{\mu}c])=\omega_{\lambda+\mu}([a_{\lambda}b],c)+\omega_{\mu}(b,[a_{\lambda}c])
.

For a Lie conformal algebra
L
and a
2
-cocycle
\omega_{\lambda}
of
L
, one can endow
L_{\omega_{\lambda}}\coloneqq L\oplus\mathbb{C}C
with the structure of a Lie conformal algebra as follows:

•

The translation operator
\widetilde{T}\colon L_{\omega_{\lambda}}\to L_{\omega_{\lambda}}
is defined by

\widetilde{T}(a)\coloneqq T(a)\quad(a\in L),\quad\widetilde{T}(C)\coloneqq 0.

•

The
\lambda
-bracket
[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]^{\sim}
is defined by

[a_{\lambda}b]^{\sim}\coloneqq[a_{\lambda}b]+\omega_{\lambda}(a,b)C,\quad[a_{\lambda}C]^{\sim}=[C_{\lambda}a]^{\sim}=[C_{\lambda}C]^{\sim}\coloneqq 0\quad(a,b\in L).

We call
L_{\omega_{\lambda}}
the
one-dimensional central extension of
L
associated with
\omega_{\lambda}
.

Example 1.2.9
.

The linear space
\mathbb{C}[T]L
has the structure of a Lie conformal algebra defined by

[L_{\lambda}L]\coloneqq(T+2\lambda)L.

This Lie conformal algebra admits a one-dimensional central extension
\Conf\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[T]L\oplus\mathbb{C}C
associated with the
2
-cocycle

(1.1)

\omega_{\lambda}(L,L)\coloneqq\frac{1}{12}\lambda^{3}.

We call
\Conf
the
Virasoro conformal algebra
. The
\lambda
-bracket of
\Conf
is given by

[L_{\lambda}L]=(T+2\lambda)L+\frac{1}{12}\lambda^{3}C,\quad[L_{\lambda}C]=[C_{\lambda}L]=0.

Example 1.2.10
.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the linear space
\mathbb{C}[T]\otimes\mathfrak{g}
has the structure of a Lie conformal algebra defined by

[a_{\lambda}b]\coloneqq[a,b]\quad(a,b\in\mathfrak{g}).

If a symmetric invariant form
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
is given, this Lie conformal algebra admits a one-dimensional central extension
\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[T]\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{C}K
associated with the
2
-cocycle

(1.2)

\kappa_{\lambda}(a,b)\coloneqq\lambda\kappa(a,b).

We call
\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
the current Lie conformal algebra. The
\lambda
-bracket of
\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
is given by

[a_{\lambda}b]=[a,b]+\lambda\kappa(a,b)K,\quad[a_{\lambda}K]=[K_{\lambda}a]=0\quad(a,b\in\mathfrak{g}).

For a vertex algebra
V
, define the
\lambda
-bracket by

[a_{\lambda}b]\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\lambda^{n}a_{(n)}b\quad(a,b\in V).

Then, one can verify that
V
, equipped with the translation operator
T\colon V\to V
and this
\lambda
-bracket, is a Lie conformal algebra. Hence, we have a functor from the category of vertex algebras to that of Lie conformal algebras.
This functor admits a left adjoint denoted

\{\text{Lie conformal algebras}\}\to\{\text{vertex algebras}\},\quad L\mapsto V(L).

The vertex algebra
V(L)
is called the
enveloping vertex algebra
of
L
. We now describe the construction of
V(L)
.

For a Lie conformal algebra
L
, define

\Lie(L)\coloneqq(\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\otimes L)/\Img(\partial_{t}\otimes\id+\id\otimes T).

It becomes a Lie algebra with the bracket

[\overline{f\otimes a},\overline{g\otimes b}]\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\overline{(\partial^{n}_{t}f)g\otimes a_{(n)}b}\quad(f,g\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}],a,b\in L).

The Lie algebra
\Lie(L)
is called the
current Lie algebra
(or
Borcherds Lie algebra
) of
L
. For
a\in L
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, write
a_{[n]}\coloneqq\overline{t^{n}\otimes a}
, then we have

\Lie(L)=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{a_{[n]}\mid a\in L,n\in\mathbb{Z}\},\quad[a_{[m]},b_{[n]}]=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{m}{j}(a_{(j)}b)_{[m+n-j]}.

Note that
\Lie_{+}(L)\coloneqq\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{a_{[n]}\mid a\in L,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Lie(L)
is a Lie subalgebra. The linear space

V(L)\coloneqq U(\Lie(L))\otimes_{U(\Lie_{+}(L))}\mathbb{C},

where
\mathbb{C}
is regarded as the one-dimensional trivial representation of
\Lie_{+}(L)
, carries the structure of a vertex algebra such that:

•

The vacuum is given by
\ket{0}\coloneqq 1_{U(\Lie(L))}\otimes 1
.

•

The translation operator
T\colon V(L)\to V(L)
is determined by

T\ket{0}\coloneqq 0,\quad[T,a_{[n]}]=-na_{[n-1]}\quad(a\in L,n\in\mathbb{Z}).

•

The state-field correspondence
Y\colon V(L)\to\Fld(V(L))
is determined by

Y(a_{[-1]}\ket{0},z)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{[n]}.

Before moving on to examples of enveloping vertex algebras, we recall the notions of vertex algebra ideals and quotient vertex algebras. Let
V
be a vertex algebra. A
vertex algebra ideal
of
V
is a linear subspace
I\subset V
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

I
is invariant under the translation operator:
TI\subset I
.

(ii)

For
a\in V
and
b\in I
, we have
Y(a,z)b\in I(\!(z)\!)
.

For a vertex algebra ideal
I\subset V
, the quotient linear space
V/I
naturally inherits the structure of a vertex algebra.

Example 1.2.11
.

Consider the enveloping vertex algebra
V(\Conf)
of the Virasoro conformal algebra. The linear map

\Vir\to\Lie(\Conf),\quad L_{n}\mapsto L_{[n+1]},\quad C\mapsto C_{[-1]}

gives an isomorphism of Lie algebras. Moreover, for
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the linear subspace
(C-c)V(\Conf)
is a vertex algebra ideal of
V(\Conf)
, and the quotient vertex algebra
V(\Conf)/(C-c)V(\Conf)
is isomorphic to the Virasoro vertex algebra
V^{c}
:

V(\Conf)/(C-c)V(\Conf)\cong V^{c}.

Example 1.2.12
.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a symmetric invariant form
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
, consider the enveloping vertex algebra
V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
of the current Lie conformal algebra. The linear map

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\to\Lie(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})),\quad t^{n}\otimes a\mapsto a_{[n]},\quad K\mapsto K_{[-1]}

gives an isomorphism of Lie algebras. Moreover, for
k\in\mathbb{C}
, the linear subspace
(K-k)V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
is a vertex algebra ideal of
V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
, and the quotient vertex algebra
V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))/(K-k)V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))
is isomorphic to the affine vertex algebra
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
:

V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))/(K-k)V(\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g}).

Definition 1.2.13
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebra
is a Lie conformal algebra
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{\Delta}
with a direct sum decomposition as a linear space, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
T(L_{\Delta})\subset L_{\Delta+1}
.

(ii)

For
a\in L_{\Delta}
,
b\in L_{\Delta^{\prime}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
a_{(n)}b\in L_{\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}-n-1}
.

Moreover, a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebra
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{\Delta}
is said to be
\mathbb{N}
-graded if
V_{\Delta}=\{0\}
for all
\Delta<0
.

For an element
a
of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebra
L
, if
a\in L_{\Delta}\backslash\{0\}
, we say that
a
is a homogeneous element of
L
, and denote
\Delta(a)\coloneqq\Delta
.

Let
K
and
L
be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebras. A
morphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebras
is a morphism of Lie conformal algebras
f\colon K\to L
satisfying
f(K_{\Delta})\subset L_{\Delta}
for all
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 1.2.14
.

The Virasoro conformal algebra
\Conf
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded by declaring

\Delta(L)\coloneqq 2,\quad\Delta(C)\coloneqq 0.

Example 1.2.15
.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a symmetric invariant form
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
, the current Lie conformal algebra
\Cur_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded by declaring

\Delta(a)\coloneqq 1\quad(a\in\mathfrak{g}),\quad\Delta(K)\coloneqq 0.

Clearly, every
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex algebra naturally becomes a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebra. Conversely, let
L=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{\Delta}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie conformal algebra. Define
V(L)_{\Delta}
to be the subspace of
V(L)
spanned by elements of the form

a^{1}_{[-n_{1}-1]}\cdots a^{r}_{[-n_{r}-1]}\ket{0}\quad(a^{i}\in L,\,r,n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\Delta(a^{i})+n_{i})=\Delta),

then
V(L)=\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}V(L)_{\Delta}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex algebra. Moreover, the Lie algebra
\Lie(L)
is also
\mathbb{Z}
-graded by declaring

\Lie(L)_{\Delta}\coloneqq\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{a_{[m]}\mid a\in L,\,m\in\mathbb{Z},\,\Delta(a)-m-1=\Delta\},

and its enveloping algebra

carries the induced

1.2.3.
Vertex superalgebras and Lie conformal superalgebras

There are super analogues of the notions of vertex algebras and Lie conformal algebras, which arise in two-dimensional supersymmetric conformal field theory. We call them
vertex superalgebras
and
Lie conformal superalgebras
, respectively. In this section, we recall several examples of these structures. For precise definitions of the super analogues, we refer the reader to
[
K98
]
.

Many vertex superalgebras and Lie conformal superalgebras naturally come equipped with a
\mathbb{N}/2
-grading, defined in a manner similar to
section

1.2.1
and
section

1.2.2
.

Example 1.2.16

(Neveu–Schwarz vertex superalgebra)
.

The linear superspace
\mathbb{C}[T]L\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G
, where
L
is even and
G
is odd, has the structure of a Lie conformal superalgebra defined by

[L_{\lambda}L]\coloneqq(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[L_{\lambda}G]\coloneqq\bigl(T+\frac{3}{2}\lambda\bigr)G,\quad[G_{\lambda}G]\coloneqq 2L.

This Lie conformal superalgebra admits a one-dimensional central extension

L^{N=1}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[T]L\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G\oplus\mathbb{C}C,

where
C
is even, associated with the
2
-cocycle

(1.3)

\omega_{\lambda}(L,L)\coloneqq\frac{1}{12}\lambda^{3},\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G,G)\coloneqq\frac{1}{3}\lambda^{2}.

The Lie conformal superalgebra
L^{N=1}
is
\mathbb{N}/2
-graded by declaring

\Delta(L)\coloneqq 2,\quad\Delta(G)\coloneqq\frac{3}{2},\quad\Delta(C)\coloneqq 0.

We call
L^{N=1}
the
Neveu–Schwarz Lie conformal superalgebra
or the
N=1
Lie conformal superalgebra
.
Consider the enveloping vertex superalgebra
V(L^{N=1})
. For
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the linear subspace
(C-c)V(L^{N=1})
is an ideal of
V(L^{N=1})
. The quotient vertex superalgebra
V^{N=1}_{c}\coloneqq V(L^{N=1})/(C-c)V(L^{N=1})
is called the
Neveu–Schwarz vertex superalgebra
or the
N=1
vertex superalgebra
.

Example 1.2.17

(
N=2
vertex superalgebra)
.

The linear superspace

M\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[T]L\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]J\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G^{-},

where
L
,
J
are even and
G^{\pm}
are odd, has the structure of a Lie conformal superalgebra defined by

\displaystyle[L_{\lambda}L]\coloneqq(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[L_{\lambda}J]\coloneqq(T+\lambda)J,\quad[L_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq\bigl(T+\frac{3}{2}\lambda\bigr)G^{\pm},

\displaystyle[J_{\lambda}J]\coloneqq 0,\quad[J_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]=\pm G^{\pm},\quad[G^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[G^{+}{}_{\lambda}G^{-}]\coloneqq L+\bigl(\frac{1}{2}T+\lambda\bigr)J.

This Lie conformal superalgebra admits a one-dimensional central extension

L^{N=2}\coloneqq M\oplus\mathbb{C}C,

where
C
is even, associated with the
2
-cocycle

(1.4)

\omega_{\lambda}(L,L)\coloneqq\frac{1}{12}\lambda^{3},\quad\omega_{\lambda}(L,J)\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(J,J)\coloneqq\frac{1}{3}\lambda,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G^{\pm},G^{\pm})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G^{+},G^{-})\coloneqq\frac{1}{6}\lambda^{2}.

The Lie conformal superalgebra
L^{N=2}
is
\mathbb{N}/2
-graded by declaring

\Delta(L)\coloneqq 2,\quad\Delta(J)\coloneqq 1,\quad\Delta(G^{\pm})\coloneqq\frac{3}{2},\quad\Delta(C)\coloneqq 0.

We call
L^{N=2}
the
N=2
Lie conformal superalgebra
. Consider the enveloping vertex superalgebra
V(L^{N=2})
. For
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the linear subspace
(C-c)V(L^{N=2})
is an ideal of
V(L^{N=2})
. The quotient vertex superalgebra
V^{N=2}_{c}\coloneqq V(L^{N=2})/(C-c)V(L^{N=2})
is called the
N=2
vertex superalgebra
.

Example 1.2.18

(
N=4
vertex superalgebra)
.

The linear superspace

M\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[T]L\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]J^{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]J^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]J^{-}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]G^{-}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]\overline{G}^{+}\oplus\mathbb{C}[T]\overline{G}^{-},

where
L
,
J^{0}
,
J^{\pm}
are even and
G^{\pm}
,
\overline{G}^{\pm}
are odd, has the structure of a Lie conformal superalgebra defined by

\displaystyle[L_{\lambda}L]\coloneqq(T+2\lambda)L,\quad[L_{\lambda}J^{0}]\coloneqq(T+\lambda)J^{0},\quad[L_{\lambda}J^{\pm}]\coloneqq(T+\lambda)J^{\pm},

\displaystyle[L_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq\bigl(T+\frac{3}{2}\lambda\bigr)G^{\pm},\quad[L_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\pm}]\coloneqq\bigl(T+\frac{3}{2}\lambda\bigr)\overline{G}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[J^{0}{}_{\lambda}J^{0}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[J^{0}{}_{\lambda}J^{\pm}]\coloneqq\pm 2J^{\pm},\quad[J^{0}{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq\pm G^{\pm},\quad[J^{0}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\pm}]\coloneqq\pm\overline{G}^{\pm}

\displaystyle[J^{+}{}_{\lambda}J^{-}]\coloneqq J^{0},\quad[J^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[J^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}G^{\mp}]\coloneqq G^{\pm},\quad[J^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\pm}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[J^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\mp}]\coloneqq-\overline{G}^{\pm},

\displaystyle[G^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}G^{\pm}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[G^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}G^{\mp}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[G^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\pm}]\coloneqq(T+2\lambda)J^{\pm},\quad[G^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\mp}]\coloneqq L\pm\frac{1}{2}(T+2\lambda)J^{0},

\displaystyle[\overline{G}^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\pm}]\coloneqq 0,\quad[\overline{G}^{\pm}{}_{\lambda}\overline{G}^{\mp}]\coloneqq 0.

This Lie conformal superalgebra admits a one-dimensional central extension

L^{N=4}\coloneqq M\oplus\mathbb{C}C,

where
C
is even, associated with the 2-cocycle

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\omega_{\lambda}(L,L)\coloneqq\frac{1}{12}\lambda^{3},\quad\omega_{\lambda}(L,J^{0})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(L,J^{\pm})\coloneqq 0,\\
&\omega_{\lambda}(J^{0},J^{0})\coloneqq\frac{1}{3}\lambda,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(J^{0},J^{\pm})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(J^{+},J^{-})\coloneqq\frac{1}{6}\lambda\\
&\omega_{\lambda}(G^{\pm},G^{\pm})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G^{\pm},G^{\mp})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G^{\pm},\overline{G}^{\pm})\coloneqq 0,\quad\omega_{\lambda}(G^{\pm},\overline{G}^{\mp})\coloneqq\frac{1}{6}\lambda^{2}.\end{split}

The Lie conformal superalgebra
L^{N=4}
is
\mathbb{N}/2
-graded by declaring

\Delta(L)\coloneqq 2,\quad\Delta(J^{0})\coloneqq 1,\quad\Delta(J^{\pm})\coloneqq 1,\quad\Delta(G^{\pm})\coloneqq\frac{3}{2},\quad\Delta(\overline{G}^{\pm})\coloneqq\frac{3}{2}.

We call
L^{N=4}
the
N=4
Lie conformal superalgebra
.
Consider the enveloping vertex superalgebra
V(L^{N=4})
. For
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the linear subspace
(C-c)V(L^{N=4})
is an ideal of
V(L^{n=4})
. The quotient vertex superalgebra
V^{N=4}_{c}\coloneqq V(L^{N=4})/(C-c)V(L^{N=4})
is called the
N=4
vertex superalgebra
.

2.
Preliminaries: bornological vector spaces

This section provides a preliminary introduction to bornological vector spaces. The main reference is
[
H77
]
. We refer the reader to
[
J81
]
for terminology on topological vector spaces.

2.1.
Definition of bornological vector spaces

A
bornological space
is a set
X
equipped with a collection
\mathfrak{B}_{X}
of subsets of
X
, called the
bornology
, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For any
x\in X
, we have
\{x\}\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
.

(ii)

If
A,B\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
, then
A\cup B\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
.

(iii)

If
A\subset B\subset X
and
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
, then
A\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
.

Each element
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
is called a
bounded set
of
X
. A
basis of the bornology
is a subset
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathfrak{B}_{X}
such that for every
A\in\mathfrak{B}_{X}
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
A\subset B
.

We say that a map
f\colon X\to Y
between bornological spaces is
bounded
if
f(B)\subset Y
is bounded for every bounded set
B\subset X
. Clearly, for a given basis
\mathfrak{B}
of the bornology of
X
, a map
f\colon X\to Y
is bounded if and only if
f(B)\subset Y
is bounded for all
B\in\mathfrak{B}
.

Example 2.1.1
.

(1)

The set
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers has a natural bornology whose bounded sets are those subsets
B\subset\mathbb{C}
for which there exists
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
B\subset D_{R}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<R\}
. Clearly, the set
\{D_{R}\mid R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
forms a basis of this bornology.

(2)

More generally, any topological vector space
X
has a natural bornology, called the
von Neumann bornology
, whose bounded sets are those subsets
B\subset X
for which the following holds: for every neighborhood
N
of
0\in X
, there exists
a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
B\subset aN
.
Note that the natural bornology on
\mathbb{C}
in (1) coincides with the von Neumann bornology.
It is easy to check that any continuous linear map
f\colon X\to Y
between topological vector spaces is bounded with respect to the von Neumann bornology. Henceforth, when we regard a topological vector space as a bornological space, we always equip it with the von Neumann bornology.

If a collection
\mathfrak{B}
of subsets of a set
X
satisfies the conditions

(i)

for any
x\in X
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
x\in B
,

(ii)

for any
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
A_{1}\cup A_{2}\subset B
,

then
X
admits a unique bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis. This bornology is called the
bornology generated by
\mathfrak{B}
. Note that the von Neumann bornology on
\mathbb{C}
is the one generated by
\{D_{R}\mid R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
.

Now, we collect some basic constructions of bornological spaces:

Subspace bornology
.
For a bornological space
X
and a subset
E\subset X
, the set
\{B\cap E\mid\text{$B\subset X$ is bounded}\}
is a bornology on
E
. We call it the
subspace bornology
on
E
. Note that if
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis of
X
, then
\{B\cap E\mid B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
forms a basis of
E
. Cleary, the inclusion
\iota\colon E\hookrightarrow X
is bounded. Moreover, a map
f\colon Y\to E
, where
Y
is another bornological space, is bounded if and only if
\iota\circ f\colon Y\to X
is bounded.

Quotient bornology
.
Let
X
be a bornological space and
R
an equivalence relation on
X
. Consider the quotient set
X/R
, and denote by
\pi\colon X\twoheadrightarrow X/R
the projection. The collection
\{\pi(B)\mid\text{$B\subset X$ is bounded}\}
generates the bornology on
X/R
, called the
quotient bornology
. Note that if
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis of
X
, then
\{\pi(B)\mid B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
forms a basis of
X/R
. Cleary, the projection
\pi\colon X\twoheadrightarrow X/R
is bounded. Moreover, a map
f\colon X/R\to Y
, where
Y
is another bornological space, is bounded if and only if
f\circ\pi\colon X\to Y
is bounded.

Product bornology
.
Let
\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of bornological spaces. Consider the product set
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, and denote by
\pi_{\lambda}\colon\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\lambda}
the projections. The collection

\Bigl\{B\subset\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\Bigm|\text{$\pi_{\lambda}(B)\subset X_{\lambda}$ is bounded for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$}\Bigr\}

is a bornology on
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, called the
product bornology
. Note that if
\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}
is a basis of
X_{\lambda}
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then
\{\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B_{\lambda}\mid B_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}\}
forms a basis of
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
. Cleary, the projections
\pi_{\lambda}\colon\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\lambda}
are all bounded. Moreover, a map
f\colon Y\to\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, where
Y
is another bornological space, is bounded if and only if
\pi_{\lambda}\circ f\colon Y\to X_{\lambda}
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The bornological space
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is actually a product in the category of bornological spaces with bounded maps.

Projective limit bornology
.
Let
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\colon X_{\mu}\to X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
be a projective system in the category of bornological spaces with bounded maps. Consider the projective limit

\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}=\Bigl\{(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\Bigm|\forall\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,\,\lambda\leq\mu\Longrightarrow f_{\lambda,\mu}(x_{\mu})=x_{\lambda}\Bigr\}

in the category of sets, and denote by
\pi_{\lambda}\colon\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\lambda}
the canonical morphisms. The collection

\Bigl\{B\subset\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\Bigm|\text{$\pi_{\lambda}(B)\subset X_{\lambda}$ is bounded for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$}\Bigr\}

is a bornology on
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, called the
projective limit bornology
. It is clear that the canonical morphisms
\pi_{\lambda}\colon\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to X_{\lambda}
are all bounded. Moreover, a map
f\colon Y\to\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, where
Y
is another bornological space, is bounded if and only if
\pi_{\lambda}\circ f\colon Y\to X_{\lambda}
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The bornological space
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is actually a projective limit in the category of bornological spaces with bounded maps. Note that the projective limit bornology on
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
coincides with the subspace bornology induced by the product
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

A
bornological vector space
is a linear space
X
equipped with a bornology such that the maps

X\times X\to X,\quad(x,y)\mapsto x+y,\qquad\mathbb{C}\times X\to X,\quad(a,x)\mapsto ax

are both bounded. Here,
\mathbb{C}
is equipped with the von Neumann bornology, and
X\times X
and
\mathbb{C}\times X
are both equipped with the product bornology. Such a bornology on a linear space is called a
vector bornology
.

Example 2.1.3
.

The von Neumann bornology on a topological vector space is a vector bornology, since the von Neumann bornology associated with the product topology is larger than the product bornology of the von Neumann bornologies.

Recall that a subset
A
of a linear space is
balanced
if
aA\subset A
for all
a\in\mathbb{C}
with
|a|\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Let
X
be a bornological vector space. For any bounded set
A\subset X
, there exists a bounded balanced set
B\subset X
with
A\subset B
. Hence the set of all bounded balanced sets forms a basis of
X
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset X
be a bounded set, and fix
R>1
. Then
B\coloneqq D_{R}\cdot A
is a bounded set of
X
satisfying
A\subset B
. Moreover, for any
a\in\mathbb{C}
with
|a|\leq 1
and
x=bx^{\prime}\in B
(
b\in D_{R}
,
x^{\prime}\in A
), we have
ax=abx^{\prime}\in B
, since
ab\in D_{R}
. Hence
B
is balanced.
∎

The above constructions of bornological spaces are compatible with the linear structure:

Subspace bornology
.
For a bornological vector space
X
and a linear subspace
E\subset X
, the subspace bornology on
E
is a vector bornology.

Quotient bornology
.
For a bornological vector space
X
and a linear subspace
E\subset X
, the quotient bornology on
X/E
is a vector bornology.

Product bornology
.
For a family
\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of bornological vector spaces, the product bornology on
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a vector bornology. The bornological vector space
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a product in the category of bornological vector spaces with bounded linear maps.

Projective limit bornology
.
For a projective system
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\colon X_{\mu}\to X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
in the category of bornological vector spaces with bounded linear maps, the projective limit bornology on
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a vector bornology. The bornological vector space
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a projective limit in the category of bornological vector spaces with bounded linear maps.

Most bornological vector spaces we are interested in satisfy an additional condition, called convexity. Recall that a subset
A
of a linear space is
convex
if
tx+(1-t)y\in A
for all
x,y\in A
and
0\leq t\leq 1
. For a subset
A
of a linear space, we denote by
\conv A
the convex hull of
A
, which is the smallest convex set containing
A
. Moreover, a balanced convex set is called a
disked set
. For a subset
A
of a linear space, we denote by
\disk A
the disked hull of
A
, which is the smallest disked set containing
A
.

Definition 2.1.5
.

A
convex bornological vector space
is a bornological vector space
X
whose bornology admits a basis consisting of convex sets of
X
. Such a bornology is called a
convex vector bornology
.

Example 2.1.6
.

The von Neumann bornology on a locally convex space
X
is a convex vector bornology. Indeed, for a bounded set
B\subset X
, the disked hull
\disk B
is also bounded, and hence
X
has a basis consisting of convex sets.

Lemma 2.1.7
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. If
A\subset X
is bounded, then both
\conv A
and
\disk A
are bounded in
X
. In particular, the set of all bounded disked set forms a basis of
X
.

Proof.

By the definition of convex bornological vector spaces, there exists a bounded convex set
B\subset X
such that
A\subset B
. Since
\conv A\subset\conv B=B
, we see that
\conv A\subset X
is bounded.

Moreover, by
section

2.1
, there exists a bounded balanced set
B\subset X
such that
A\subset B
. Since
\disk A\subset\disk B=\conv B
, we see that
\disk A\subset X
is bounded.
∎

Lemma 2.1.8
.

Let
X
be a linear space and
\mathfrak{B}
a collection of subsets of
X
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

Each element of
\mathfrak{B}
is a nonempty disked set of
X
.

(ii)

For any
x\in X
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
x\in B
.

(iii)

For any
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
A_{1}+A_{2}\subset B
.

(iv)

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
A\in\mathfrak{B}
, there exists
B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
tA\subset B
.

Then
X
admits a unique convex vector bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis.

Proof.

Since every nonempty balanced set contains
0
, we have
A_{1}\cup A_{2}\subset A_{1}+A_{2}
for
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
. This, together with condition (ii), yields that
\mathfrak{B}
generates a bornology. Hence, it suffices to show that the resulting bornology is a vector bornology. The boundedness of addition follows directly from condition (iii). Moreover, for
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
A\in\mathfrak{B}
, condition (i) implies that
D_{r}\cdot A\subset rA
. Since
rA\subset X
is bounded by condition (iv), we see that
D_{r}\cdot A\subset X
is bounded. Thus, the boundedness of scalar multiplication follows.
∎

Definition 2.1.9
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. A net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
is said to
converge bornologically
to
x\in X
, or equivalently,
x
is a
bornological limit
of
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
if there exist a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}B
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.1.10
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a bounded linear map between convex bornological vector spaces. If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
bornologically converges to
x\in X
, then the net
\{f(x_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
bornologically converges to
f(x)
in
Y
.

Proof.

Take a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}B
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then
f(B)\subset Y
is bounded, and we have
f(x_{\lambda})-f(x)\in t_{\lambda}f(B)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
∎

We next establish the relationship between bornological convergence and the usual convergence in topological space.
Let
X
be a linear space. For a nonempty disked set
A\subset X
, define

p_{A}\colon\Span_{\mathbb{C}}A\to\mathbb{R},\quad x\mapsto\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid x\in tA\}.

Then
p_{A}
is a seminorm, since
A
is an absorbing set of
\Span_{\mathbb{C}}A
. We denote
\Span_{\mathbb{C}}A
by
X_{A}
regarded as a seminormed space via
p_{A}
. Note that
\{tA\mid t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
forms a basis of the von Neumann bornology of
X_{A}
. Moreover, for nonempty disked sets
A,B\subset X
with
A\subset B
, we have
p_{A}(x)\geq p_{B}(x)
for all
x\in X_{A}
, so the inclusion
X_{A}\hookrightarrow X_{B}
is continuous.

Lemma 2.1.11
.

Let
X
,
Y
be linear spaces and
f\colon X\to Y
a linear map. For a nonempty disked set
A\subset X
, the subset
f(A)\subset Y
is a nonempty disked set such that
Y_{f(A)}
is isometric to the quotient seminormed space
X_{A}/(\Ker f\cap X_{A})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
f(A)\subset Y
is a nonempty disked set. Since
f|_{X_{A}}\colon X_{A}\to Y_{f(A)}
is a surjective linear map with
N\coloneqq\Ker(f|_{X_{A}})=\Ker f\cap X_{A}
, it induces a linear isomorphism
X_{A}/N\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}Y_{f(A)}
. Recall that the seminorm on
X/N
is defined by
\overline{p}_{A}(\overline{x})\coloneqq\inf_{y\in N}p_{A}(x-y)
. For
x\in X_{A}
, it is then easy to verify that
p_{f(A)}(f(x))=\overline{p}_{A}(\overline{x})
, which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.1.12
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. For a nonempty bounded disked set
A\subset X
, the inclusion
X_{A}\hookrightarrow X
is bounded.

Proof.

This is immediate since
\{tA\mid t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
forms a basis of
X_{A}
, and each
tA\subset X
is bounded.
∎

Lemma 2.1.13
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space and
B\subset X
a nonempty bounded disked set. If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X_{B}
converges to
x\in X_{B}
(in usual sense), then there exists a net
\{t_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}B
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>1
, and set
t_{\lambda}\coloneqq\delta p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Since
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to
x
in
X_{B}
, we have
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
. Moreover, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, since
t_{\lambda}\geq p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x)
, it follows that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}B
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.14
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. For a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}\subset X
and
x\in X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges bornologically to
x
in
X
.

(ii)

There exist a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}B
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

(iii)

There exists a nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
such that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X_{B}
,
x\in X_{B}
and
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to
x
in
X_{B}
.

Proof.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is trivial, and (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) follows from
section

2.1
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): Take a bounded set
A\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}t_{\lambda}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}A
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. By
section

2.1
, the subset
B\coloneqq\disk(A\cup\{x\})\subset X
is nonempty, bounded, and disked. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we have

x_{\lambda}=(x_{\lambda}-x)+x\in t_{\lambda}A+B\subset X_{B}.

Moreover, for
\lambda\in\Lambda
, since
B
is balanced, we have

x_{\lambda}-x\in t_{\lambda}A\subset t_{\lambda}B=|t_{\lambda}|B.

Thus, we obtain
p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x)\leq|t_{\lambda}|
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, which shows that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to
x
in
X_{B}
.
∎

Based on the notion of bornological convergence, we introduce the concept of closed subsets in a convex bornological space.

Definition 2.1.15
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. A subset
A\subset X
is called
bornologically closed
if, whenever a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset A
converges bornologically to some
x\in X
, we have
x\in A
.

For a subset
A
of a convex bornological vector space
X
, there exists the smallest bornologically closed subset of
X
containing
A
. We call it the bornological closure of
A
in
X
and denote it by
\overline{A}
.

Lemma 2.1.16
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. A subset
A\subset X
is bornologically closed if and only if, for every nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
, the set
A\cap X_{B}
is closed in
X_{B}
.

Proof.

Necessary part: If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset A\cap X_{B}
converges to
x\in X_{B}
, then by
section

2.1
, the net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges bornologically to
x
. Since
A
is bornologically closed, we have
x\in A\cap X_{B}
.

Sufficient part: If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset A
converges bornologically to
x\in X
, then by
section

2.1
, there exists a nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
such that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to
x
in
X_{B}
. Since
A\cap X_{B}
is closed in
X_{B}
, we have
x\in A
.
∎

Finally, we discuss two important properties of bornological spaces: separation and completeness.

Definition 2.1.17
.

A convex bornological vector space
X
is said to be
separated
(or, more precisely,
bornologically separeted
) if
\{0\}
is the only bounded linear subspace of
X
.

Proposition 2.1.18
.

For a convex bornological vector space
X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is bornologically separated.

(ii)

\{0\}
is bornologically closed in
X
.

(iii)

For any nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
, the seminormed space
X_{B}
is Hausdorff, i.e., its seminorm
p_{B}
is a norm.

(iv)

Every bornologically convergent net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
has a unique bornological limit.

(v)

Every bornologically convergent sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
has a unique bornological limit.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): If
x\in X_{B}
satisfies
p_{B}(x)=0
, then
\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
is bounded in
X_{B}
. Hence
\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
is also bounded in
X
by
section

2.1
. This implies
x=0
.

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i): If
E\subset X
is a bounded linear subspace, then

(ii)

(iii)

Example 2.1.19
.

The von Neumann bornology on a Hausdorff locally convex space

is separated. Indeed, for every nonempty bounded disked set

is continuous, and hence

To define completeness, we need the notion of Cauchy nets.

Definition 2.1.20
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. A net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
is called a
bornological Cauchy net
if there exist a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}B
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
. Here the set
\Lambda\times\Lambda
is directed by

(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1})\leq(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})\ratio\Longleftrightarrow\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\,\text{ and }\,\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}.

Lemma 2.1.21
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a bounded linear map between convex bornological vector spaces. If
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
is a bornological Cauchy net, then
\{f(x_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a bornological Cauchy net in
Y
.

Proof.

Take a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}B
. Then
f(B)\subset Y
is bounded, and we have
f(x_{\lambda})-f(x_{\mu})\in t_{\lambda,\mu}f(B)
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
.
∎

Lemma 2.1.22
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space and
B\subset X
a nonempty bounded disked set. If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X_{B}
is Cauchy (in the usual sense), then there exists a net
\{t_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}B
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>1
, and set
t_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq\delta p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for each
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
. Since
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a Cauchy net in
X_{B}
, we have
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
. Moreover, for every
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
, since
t_{\lambda,\mu}\geq p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu})
, it follows that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}B
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.23
.

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space. For a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a bornological Cauchy net in
X
.

(ii)

There exists a bounded set
B\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}B
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
.

(iii)

There exists a nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
such that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X_{B}
and
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a Cauchy net in
X_{B}
.

Proof.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) is trivial, and (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) follows from
section

2.1
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): Take a bounded set
A\subset X
and a net
\{t_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\lim_{(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda}t_{\lambda,\mu}=0
such that
x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}A
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
. Fix
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda
, and set
B\coloneqq\disk(A\cup\{x_{\lambda_{0}}\})
. Then

is a nonempty bounded disked set. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we have

x_{\lambda}=(x_{\lambda}-x_{\lambda_{0}})+x_{\lambda_{0}}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}A+B\subset X_{B}.

Moreover, for
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
, since

is balanced, we have

x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu}\in t_{\lambda,\mu}A\subset t_{\lambda,\mu}B=|t_{\lambda,\mu}|B.

Thus, we obtain
p_{B}(x_{\lambda}-x_{\mu})\leq|t_{\lambda,\mu}|
for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
, which shows that
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a Cauchy net in
X_{B}
.
∎

Definition 2.1.24
.

A convex bornological vector space
X
is said to be
complete
(or, more precisely,
bornologically complete
) if every bornological Cauchy net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X
converges bornologically to some point
x\in X
.

A nonempty disked set
A
of a linear space
X
is called a
completant disked set
if
X_{A}
is a Banach space.

Proposition 2.1.25
.

For a separated convex bornological vector space
X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

X
is bornologically complete.

(ii)

Every bornological Cauchy sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X
converges bornologically to some point
x\in X
.

(iii)

Every nonempty bornologically closed bounded disked set

is completant.

(iv)

There exists a basis
\mathfrak{B}
of
X
such that every
B\in\mathfrak{B}
is a completant disked set of
X
.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) and (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv) are trivial.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii): Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{B}
be a Cauchy sequence. Then, by
section

2.1
, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bornologically Cauchy in
X
, and hence converges bornologically to some
x\in X
. Since
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
X_{B}
, for arbitrary
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

m,n\geq N\,\Longrightarrow\,x_{m}-x_{n}\in\varepsilon B
.
Taking the bornological limit for

and using the bornological closedness of

, we obtain

. This shows that

and

converges to

in
X_{B}
.

\Longrightarrow
(i): Let
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset X

. Moreover, by the condition (iv), there exists a bounded completant disked set

such that

. Since the inclusion

is also Cauchy in

, and hence
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to some

. Thus, by

, the net

converges bornologically to

in

.
∎

Example 2.1.26
.

The von Neumann bornology on a complete Hausdorff locally convex space
X
is bornologically complete, since every nonempty closed bounded disked set
B\subset X
is completant.

2.2.
Basic constructions of bornological vector spaces

We denote by
\mathsf{BVS}
the category of convex bornological vector spaces with bounded linear maps.

2.2.1.
Subspace and quotient

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space and
E\subset X
a linear subspace.

The subspace bornology on
E
is a convex vector bornology, since
\{B\cap E\mid B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
forms a basis of
E
for any basis
\mathfrak{B}
of
X
. Clearly, if
X
is separated, then so is
E
. Moreover, if
X
is complete and
E
is bornologically closed in
X
, then
E
is also complete.

The quotient bornology on
X/E
is a convex vector bornology, since
\{\pi(B)\mid B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
forms a basis of
X/E
for any basis
\mathfrak{B}
of
X
. Here
\pi\colon X\to X/E
denotes the projection.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

For a convex bornological vector space
X
and a linear subspace
E\subset X
, the convex bornological vector space
X/E
is separated if and only if
E
is bornologically closed in
X
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X/E
is separated. If a net
\{x_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset E
converges bornologically to
x\in X
, then by
section

2.1
, the net
\{\pi(x_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges bornologically to
\pi(x)
in
X/E
. Since
\pi(x_{\lambda})=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the net
\{\pi(x_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
also converges bornologically to
0
in
X/E
. Thus, by
section

2.1
, we have
x\in E
.

Conversely, suppose that
E
is bornologically closed in
X
. To prove that
X/E
is separated, it suffices to show that
(X/E)_{\pi(B)}
is a normed space for each nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
. Note that every element of
(X/E)_{\pi(B)}
can be written in the form
\pi(x)
for some
x\in X_{B}
. For
x\in X_{B}
, if
p_{\pi(B)}(\pi(x))=0
, then we have
x\in\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(tB+E)
. Hence, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, there exists
x_{n}\in E
such that
x_{n}-x\in(1/n)B
. In particular, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges bornologically to
x
. Thus, bornological closedness of
E
implies that
x\in E
.
∎

Proposition 2.2.2
.

For a complete and separated convex bornological vector space
X
and a bornologically closed linear subspace
E\subset X
, the convex bornological vector space
X/E
is complete and separated.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
X/E
is complete. By
section

2.1
, there exists a basis
\mathfrak{B}
of
X
consisting of completant disked sets. Note that
\{\pi(B)\mid B\in\mathfrak{B}\}
forms a basis of
X/E
. Moreover, for each
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, by
section

2.1
, the nonempty disked set
\pi(B)\subset X/E
is completant. Hence the claim follows from
section

2.1
.
∎

2.2.2.
Separation

For a convex bornological vector space
X
, by
section

2.2.1
, the quotient bornology on
X/\overline{\{0\}}
is separated. We call
X/\overline{\{0\}}
the
separation
of
X
and denote it by
X^{\mathrm{s}}
. The separation has the following universal property: for any separated convex bornological vector space
Y
and any bounded linear map
f\colon X\to Y
, there exists a unique bounded linear map
\widetilde{f}\colon X^{\mathrm{s}}\to Y
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\mathrm{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{f}}

commutes. Note that if
X
is already separated, then
X^{\mathrm{s}}
is canonically isomorphic to
X
as a bornological vector space. Moreover, for a convex bornological vector space
X
and a linear subspace
E\subset X
, we have a canonical isomorphism
X/\overline{E}\cong(X/E)^{\mathrm{s}}
as bornological vector spaces.

2.2.3.
Product and coproduct

Let
\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a family of convex bornological vector spaces.

The product bornology on
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a convex vector bornology, since
\{\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B_{\lambda}\mid B_{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}\}
forms a basis of
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
for any basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}
of
X_{\lambda}
. Thus, the convex bornological vector space
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a product in
\mathsf{BVS}
.
One can check that
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is separated (resp. complete) if and only if
X_{\lambda}
is separated (resp. complete) for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Consider the direct sum
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, and denote by
\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
the canonical injections. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be the collection of subsets of
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
of the form
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\iota_{\lambda}(B_{\lambda})
, where each
B_{\lambda}\subset X_{\lambda}
is a nonempty bounded disked set, and all but finitely many
B_{\lambda}
are zero. Then, the collection
\mathfrak{B}
satisfies the conditions in
section

2.1
. Hence
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
admits the convex vector bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis. This bornology is called the
direct sum bornology
.
It is clear that
\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
are all bounded.
Moreover, a linear map
f\colon\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to Y
, where
Y
is another convex bornological vector space, is bounded if and only if
f\circ\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to Y
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Thus, the convex bornological vector space
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a coproduct in
\mathsf{BVS}
.
One can check that
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is separated (resp. complete) if and only if
X_{\lambda}
is separated (resp. complete) for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

2.2.4.
Projective limits and inductive limits

For a projective system
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
in
\mathsf{BVS}
, the projective limit bornology on
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a convex vector bornology, since
\disk B
is bounded in
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
whenever
B\subset\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is bounded. Thus, the convex bornological vector space
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is a projective limit in
\mathsf{BVS}
. One can check that if
X_{\lambda}
is separated for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then so is
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
. Moreover, if
X_{\lambda}
is complete and separated for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then so is
\varprojlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
.

Let
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
be an inductive system in
\mathsf{BVS}
. Consider the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
in the category of linear spaces, and denote by
\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
the canonical morphisms. Then, the collection

\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\{\iota_{\lambda}(B)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda,\,\text{$B\subset X_{\lambda}$ is a nonempty bounded disked set}\}

satisfies the conditions in
section

2.1
. Hence
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
admits the convex vector bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis. This bornology is called the
inductive limit boronology
. It is clear that
\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
are all bounded. Moreover, a linear map
f\colon\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\to Y
, where
Y
is another convex bornological vector space, is bounded if and only if
f\circ\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to Y
is bounded for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Thus, the convex bornological vector space
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is an inductive limit in
\mathsf{BVS}
.

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
be an inductive system in
\mathsf{BVS}
, such that
f_{\lambda,\mu}\colon X_{\lambda}\to X_{\mu}
is injective for all
\lambda\leq\mu
.

(1)

If
X_{\lambda}
is separated for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then so is
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
.

(2)

If
X_{\lambda}
is complete and separated for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then so is
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
E\subset\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
be a bounded linear subspace. Then there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda
and a bounded subset
B\subset X_{\lambda}
such that
E\subset\iota_{\lambda}(B)
. Since
\iota_{\lambda}\colon X_{\lambda}\to\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
is injective, we have
\iota_{\lambda}^{-1}(E)\subset B
. Thus, it follows that
\iota_{\lambda}^{-1}(E)=\{0\}
and hence
E=\{0\}
.

(2) For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, by
section

2.1
, there exists a basis
\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}
of
X_{\lambda}
consisting of completant disked sets. Since
\{\iota_{\lambda}(B)\mid\lambda\in\Lambda,\,B\in\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}\}
forms a basis of
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, it suffices to show that
\iota_{\lambda}(B)
is a completant disked set for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}
. This follows immediately from
section

2.1
.
∎

In general, the inductive limit
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
in
\mathsf{BVS}
need not be separated even if all
X_{\lambda}
are separated.
For an inductive system
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
in
\mathsf{BVS}
, we denote by
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\mathrm{s}}X_{\lambda}
the separation of
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
, and call it the
separated inductive limit
. We refer to the the composition of bounded linear maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\mathrm{s}}X_{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\lambda}}

as the canonical morphism of the separated inductive limit.

Proposition 2.2.4
.

Let
(\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{f_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda\leq\mu})
be an inductive system in
\mathsf{BVS}
. If
X_{\lambda}
is complete and separated for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then so is
\varinjlim_{\lambda\in\Lambda}^{\mathrm{s}}X_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
section

2.2.4
(2).
∎

2.2.5.
Completion

Let
X
be a convex bornological vector space, and write

\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\{B\subset X\mid\text{$B\subset X$ is a nonempty bounded disked set}\}

For
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, denote by
\widehat{X}_{B}
the Hausdorff completion of the seminormed space
X_{B}
. If
A,B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
A\subset B
, the inclusion
X_{A}\hookrightarrow X_{B}
induces a continuous linear map
\widehat{X}_{A}\to\widehat{X}_{B}
. Hence the family
\{\widehat{X}_{B}\}_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}
forms an inductive system in
\mathsf{BVS}
. By
section

2.2.4
, the separated inductive limit

\widetilde{X}\coloneqq\textstyle\varinjlim_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}^{\mathrm{s}}\widehat{X}_{B}

is complete and separated. We call
\widetilde{X}
the
completion of
X
(or, more precisely, the
bornological completion of
X
). For each
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, denote by
\iota_{B}
the composition of canonical morphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{B}\colon\,X_{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{X}_{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X},}

then
\{\iota_{B}\}_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}
induces a bounded linear map
\iota\colon X\to\widetilde{X}
. We refer to
\iota
as the canonical morphism of the completion. The completion
\widetilde{X}
has the following universal property: for any complete and separated convex bornological vector space
Y
and any bounded linear map
f\colon X\to Y
, there exists a unique bounded linear map
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X}\to Y
such that
\widetilde{f}\circ\iota=f
.

2.2.6.
Tensor product and internal hom

Let
X
and
Y
be convex bornological vector spaces.

Consider the (algebraic) tensor product
X\otimes Y
, and denote by
\otimes\colon X\times Y\to X\otimes Y
the canonical bilinear map. Then, the collection

\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\{\disk(\otimes(A\times B))\mid\text{$A\subset X$, $B\subset Y$ are nonempty bounded sets}\}

satisfies the conditions in
section

2.1
. Hence
X\otimes Y
admits the convex vector bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis. This bornology is called the
tensor product bornology
. It is clear that
\otimes\colon X\times Y\to X\otimes Y
is bounded. Moreover, a linear map
f\colon X\otimes Y\to Z
, where
Z
is another convex bornological vector space, is bounded if and only if
f\circ\otimes\colon X\times Y\to Z
is bounded. The convex bornological vector space
X\otimes Y
has the following universal property: for any convex bornological vector space
Z
and any bounded bilinear map
\varphi\colon X\times Y\to Z
, there exists a unique bounded linear map
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon X\otimes Y\to Z
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\otimes=\varphi
. One can check that
X\otimes Y
is separated if and only if
X
and
Y
are both separated.

We denote by
X\totimes Y
the completion of
X\otimes Y
, and call it the
completed tensor product
. We also denote by
\widetilde{\otimes}\colon X\times Y\to X\totimes Y
the composition of
\otimes\colon X\times Y\to X\otimes Y
with the canonical morphism
X\otimes Y\to X\totimes Y
of the completion, and call it the canonical bilinear map. The completed tensor product has the following universal property: for any complete and separated convex bornological vector space
Z
and any bounded bilinear map
\varphi\colon X\times Y\to Z
, there exists a unique bounded linear map
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon X\totimes Y\to Z
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}\circ\totimes=\varphi
.

Consider the linear space
\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,Y)
of bounded linear maps. Let
\mathfrak{B}
be the collection of nonempty disked sets
F\subset\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,Y)
such that
\bigcup_{f\in F}f(A)\subset Y
is bounded for all bounded set
A\subset X
. Then
\mathfrak{B}
satisfies the conditions in
section

2.1
. Hence
\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,Y)
admits the convex vector bornology for which
\mathfrak{B}
is a basis. One can check that if
Y
is separated, then so is
\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,Y)
. Moreover, if
Y
is complete and separated, then so is
\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,Y)
.

So far, we obtain functors

(-)\otimes(-)\colon\mathsf{BVS}\times\mathsf{BVS}\to\mathsf{BVS},\quad\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(-,-)\colon\mathsf{BVS}\times\mathsf{BVS}\to\mathsf{BVS}.

For a convex bornological vector space
X
, one can verify that the functor
X\otimes(-)\colon\mathsf{BVS}\to\mathsf{BVS}
is left adjoint to
\Hom_{\mathsf{BVS}}(X,-)\colon\mathsf{BVS}\to\mathsf{BVS}
.

2.2.7.
Fine bornology on a linear space

Any linear space
V
carries a natural bornology, called the
fine bonorlogy
, whose bounded sets are those subsets
B\subset V
for which the following holds: there exists a finite-dimensional linear subspace
E\subset V
such that
B\subset E
and
B
is bounded in
E
, where
E
is equipped with the von Neumann bornology associated with the Hausdorff vector topology of
E
. It is easy to verify that the fine bornology is a separated convex vector bornology.

Proposition 2.2.5
.

Let
V
be a linear space.

(1)

The fine bornology on
V
is the smallest separated convex bornology on
V
.

(2)

For a basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
V
, the linear isomorphism

\mathbb{C}^{\oplus I}\to V,\quad\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}v_{i}

is an isomorphism of bornological vector spaces, where
\mathbb{C}^{\oplus I}
is equipped with the direct sum bornology, and
V
with the fine bornology.

Proof.

(1) Since separated bornologies on a finite-dimensional linear space are unique (
[
H77
, Theorem 3.3.1]
), it follows that for a finite-dimensional linear subspace
E\subset V
, the inclusion
E\hookrightarrow V
is bounded with respect to any separated convex bornology on
V
. This proves (1).

(2) Denote by
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{\oplus I}\to V
the claimed linear isomorphism. By the construction of the direct sum bornology, it is clear that
f
is bounded. Moreover, by (1), the inverse map
f^{-1}
is also bounded.
∎

Corollary 2.2.6
.

The fine bornology on a linear space is complete and separated convex bornology.

Proof.

This follows from
section

2.2.7
(2).
∎

Corollary 2.2.7
.

For a complete convex bornological vector space
X
and a linear space
V
equipped with the fine bornology, the tensor product bornology on
X\otimes V
is complete.

Proof.

Since
X\otimes(-)\colon\mathsf{BVS}\to\mathsf{BVS}
is a left adjoint functor, it preserves colimits. Hence the corollary follows from
section

2.2.7
(2).
∎

2.3.
Holomorphic functions with values in a bornological vector space

Fix a separated convex bornological vector space
X
.
For an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
a\in U
, we direct the set
U\backslash\{a\}
by

z\preceq w\ratio\Longleftrightarrow|z-a|\geq|w-a|.

For a map
f\colon U\backslash\{a\}\to X
, if the net
\{f(z)\}_{z\in U\backslash\{a\}}
converges bornologically, we denote its bornological limit by
\blim_{z\to a}f(z)
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset and
f\colon U\to X
a map.
We say that
f
is
bornologically complex differentiable at
a\in U
if there exists
v\in X^{n}
such that

\blim_{z\to a}\frac{f(z)-f(a)-(z-a)\cdot v}{|z-a|}=0.

Here, writing
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
,
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, and
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, we denote
(z-a)\cdot v\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}-a_{i})v_{i}
.
Moreover, we say that
f
is
bornologically holomorphic
on
U
if
f
is complex differentiable at every
a\in U
.

Recall that
X_{B}
is a normed space for each nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
, since
X
is separated (
section

2.1
).

Proposition 2.3.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset. For a map
f\colon U\to X
and
a\in U
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f
is bornologically complex differentiable at
a
.

(ii)

There exists a nonempty bounded disked set
B\subset X
such that
f(U)\subset X_{B}
and the map
f\colon U\to X_{B}
is complex differentiable at
a
(in the usual sense).

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii): Take
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in X^{n}
satisfying the condition in
section

2.3
. By
section

2.1
, there exists a nonempty bounded disked set
A\subset X
such that

\lim_{z\to a}\frac{f(z)-f(a)-(z-a)\cdot v}{|z-a|}=0\quad\text{in $X_{A}$}.

Set
B\coloneqq\disk(A\cup\{f(a),v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\})
, then
B\subset X
is a nonempty bounded disked set such that
f(U)\subset X_{B}
and
f\colon U\to X_{B}
is differentiable at
a
.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i): This is immediate from
section

2.1
.
∎

As we can see from the proof of
section

2.3
, if a map
f\colon U\to X
is bornologically complex differentiable at
a\in U
, then the element
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in X^{n}
satisfying the condition in
section

2.3
is unique. We denote

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}(a)=\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}f(z)\Bigr|_{z=a}\coloneqq v_{i}.

Suppose that
X
is complete. By
section

2.3
and
section

2.1
, a function
f\colon U\to X
is bornologically holomorphic if and only if there exists a bounded completant disked set
B\subset X
such that
f\colon U\to X_{B}
is holomorphic. Therefore, the standard complex analysis can be applied to bornologically holomorphic functions with values in a complete and separated convex bornological vector space.

3.
From factorization algebras to vertex algebras

In this section, we present the general method for constructing vertex algebras from prefactorization algebras, developed by Costello and Gwilliam. To extract vertex operators from factorization products, one needs to impose a certain analytic structure on prefactorization algebras. We adopt bornological vector spaces here, and give a concrete description of the construction of vertex algebras.

3.1.
Holomorphically translation-equivariant prefactorization algebras

We briefly review the notion of holomorphically translation-equivariant prefactorization algebras introduced in
[
CG17
, §5.2]
, presenting it in a form suitable for our purposes.

A complete and separated convex bornological vector space is called a
convenient vector space
. We denote by
\mathsf{CVS}
the category of convenient vector spaces with bounded linear maps.
From the discussion in
section

2.2
, we see that
\mathsf{CVS}
is a complete and cocomplete closed symmetric monoidal linear category:

•

For a family
\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of objects in
\mathsf{CVS}
, the product
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\,\mathbb{C}
, equipped with the product bornology, is a product in
\mathsf{CVS}
, and the coproduct
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
in
\mathsf{Mod}\,\mathbb{C}
, equipped with the direct sum bornology, is a coproduct in
\mathsf{CVS}
.

•

For a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathsf{CVS}
, the linear subspace
\Ker f=\{x\in X\mid f(x)=0\}
of
X
, equipped with the subspace bornology, is its kernel in
\mathsf{CVS}
, and the quotient linear space
Y/\overline{\Img f}
, equipped with the quotient bornology, is its cokernel in
\mathsf{CVS}
. Here
\overline{\Img f}
denotes the bornological closure of
\Img f=f(X)
.

•

The symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathsf{CVS}
is given by the completed bornological tensor product
\widetilde{\otimes}
.

We denote by
H^{n}
(resp.
H_{n}
) the cohomology (resp. homology) taken in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{BVS})
, and by
H^{n}_{\mathrm{sep}}
(resp.
H_{n}^{\mathrm{sep}}
) the cohomology (resp. homology) taken in
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})
.

For
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},R\leq\infty
, set

\Disks(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R)\coloneqq\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid\overline{D}_{r_{1}}(z_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{D}_{r_{n}}(z_{n})\subset D_{R}\},

which is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Here we use the convention that
D_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}
, and
\overline{D}_{r_{i}}(z_{i})
denotes the closed disk.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be a symmetric monoidal category, and
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{M}
a translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra, i.e., a prefactorization algebra equivariant under the action of the additive group
\mathbb{C}
by translations. For
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},R\leq\infty
and
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Disks(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R)
, define a morphism
\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}
in
\mathsf{M}
as the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}\,\colon\,\displaystyle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}}(z_{i}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(D_{R})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{z_{i},D_{r_{i}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{\{D_{r_{i}}(z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}}_{D_{R}}}

Here
\sigma_{z}
denotes the translation-equivariant structure of
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

A translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
is called
holomorphically translation-equivariant
if it satisfies the following condition: For
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},R\leq\infty
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})
, the map

\Disks(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R)\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

is bornologically holomorphic.

We now give a systematic method for producing holomorphically translation-equivariant prefactorization algebras from ones valued in chain complexes. This method will be used in
section

4.3
.

First, for an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a convenient vector space
X
, one can define the notion of bornologically differentiable functions on
U
with values in
X
. By an argument similar to
section

2.3
, a function
f\colon U\to X
is bornologically differentiable on
U
if and only if there exists a bounded completant disked set
B\subset X
such that
f\colon U\to X_{B}
is differentiable on
U
in the usual sense. For a bornologically differentiable function
f\colon U\to X
, we denote its derivative at
a\in U
by

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(a)\quad\text{or}\quad\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f\Bigr|_{x=a}.

Based on bornological differentiability, one is led to the notion of bornologically smooth functions. For an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and a bornologically smooth function
f\colon U\to X
, the Cauchy–Riemann equation holds, namely, the function
f
is bornologically holomorphic if and only if

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z_{i}}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2i-1}}+\sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2i}}\Bigr)=0\quad\text{for all }\ i=1,\ldots,n.

Here
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})
denotes the real coordinates given by
z_{i}=x_{2i-1}+\sqrt{-1}x_{2i}
.

Definition 3.1.2
.

A translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})
is called smoothly translation-equivariant if it satisfies the following condition: For
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{m},R\leq\infty
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})_{n_{i}}
, the map

\Disks(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R)\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}},\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})

is bornologically smooth.

For a translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})
, note that by composing
\mathcal{F}
with the symmetric monoidal functor
H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\colon\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})\to\mathsf{CVS}
of taking homology, we obtain a translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
.

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})
be a smoothly translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For
0<R\leq\infty
, we have
H_{n}^{\mathrm{sep}}(\mathcal{F}(D_{R}))=0
whenever
n\neq 0
.

(ii)

There exists a family of morphism

\delta_{R}\colon\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\bullet}\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\bullet+1}\quad(0<R\leq\infty)

of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded objects such that for
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{m},R\leq\infty
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})_{n_{i}}
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}=0
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})=d\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\delta_{r_{i}}(a_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m}),

where
d
denotes the differential of the complex
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})
.

Proof.

Let
0<r_{1},\ldots,r_{m},R\leq\infty
and
[a_{i}]\in H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{n_{i}}(\mathcal{F}(D_{R}))
. Note that we have

(H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F})^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}:R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}([a_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[a_{m}])=[\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})].

If
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\neq 0
, then by the condition (i),

(H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F})^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}:R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}([a_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[a_{m}])=0.

Moreover, if
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}=0
, then by condition (ii)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}(H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F})^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}:R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}([a_{1}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[a_{m}])=\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m})\Bigr]=0.\qed

3.2.
Construction of vertex algebras from factorization algebras

In this subsection we construct vertex algebras from
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebras on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, called amenably holomorphic.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
be an
S^{1}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra, where the unit circle
S^{1}
acts on
\mathbb{C}
by rotations.
For
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
and an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
that is invariant under the action of
S^{1}
, define

\mathcal{F}(U)_{\Delta}\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{F}(U)\mid\forall q\in S^{1},\,\sigma_{q,U}(a)=q^{\Delta}a\},

where
\sigma_{q}
denotes the
S^{1}
-equivariant structure of
\mathcal{F}
.

Consider the action of the group
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
on
\mathbb{C}
as isometric affine transformations. Given an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
, note that
\mathcal{F}
naturally inherits both an
S^{1}
-equivariant structure and a translation-equivariant structure. We use the convention that
D_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
be an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

The
\mathbb{C}
-action makes
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
a holomorphically translation-equivariant prefactorization algebra.

(ii)

For
0<R\leq\infty
, we have
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}=0
(
\Delta\ll 0
).

(iii)

For
0<r<R\leq\infty
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, the map
\mathcal{F}^{D_{r}}_{D_{R}}\colon\mathcal{F}(D_{r})_{\Delta}\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}
is a linear isomorphism.

Then, the linear space

\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta}

admits the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex algebra. We call such
\mathcal{F}
an
amenably holomorphic prefactorization algebra
.

In the remainder of this subsection, we prove
3.2.1
. Let
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
be an amenably holomorphic prefactorization algebra. We denote by

\sigma_{(q,z),U}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{F}((q,z)U)\quad((q,z)\in S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C},\,U\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}})

the
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant structure on
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover, for an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, we write

\Conf_{n}(U)\coloneqq\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in U^{n}\mid z_{i}\neq z_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\},

which is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

First, for
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Conf_{n}(D_{R})
, by the condition (iii) in
3.2.1
, one can define a linear map
\mu_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{R}
as the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{R}\colon\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})^{\otimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigotimes_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathbf{V}_{\!r_{i}}(\mathcal{F})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigotimes_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\tbotimes_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathcal{F}(D_{r_{i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n};R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}}

where
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are chosen so that
\overline{D}_{r_{1}}(z_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{D}_{r_{n}}(z_{n})\subset D_{R}
. Note that the definition of
\mu_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{R}
is independent of the choices of
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Conf_{n}(D_{R})
. For
q\in S^{1}
and homogeneous
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
, we have

\sigma_{(q,0),D_{R}}\,\mu_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{R}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=q^{\Delta(a_{1})+\cdots+\Delta(a_{n})}\mu_{qz_{1},\ldots,qz_{n}}^{R}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}).

Proof.

Take
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\overline{D}_{r}(z_{1})\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\overline{D}_{r}(z_{n})\subset D_{R}
. For
q\in S^{1}
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{r})
, we have

\sigma_{(q,0),D_{R}}\mathcal{F}^{r,\ldots,r;R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=\mathcal{F}^{\{D_{r}(z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}}_{D_{R}}\bigl(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{(q,0),D_{r}(z_{i})}\sigma_{(1,z_{i}),D_{r}}(a_{i})\bigr).

Since
(q,0)(1,z_{i})=(1,qz_{i})(0,q)
in
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
, we obtain

\sigma_{(q,0),D_{R}}\mathcal{F}^{r,\ldots,r;R}_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=\mathcal{F}^{r,\ldots,r;R}_{qz_{1},\ldots,qz_{n}}(\sigma_{(q,0),D_{r}}(a_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{(q,0),D_{r}}(a_{n})).

This proves the lemma.
∎

For
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
, note by the condition (i) in
3.2.1
that the function

\Conf_{n}(D_{R})\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\mapsto\mu_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}}^{R}(a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

is bornologically holomorphic.

Now we define a vertex algebra structure on
\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
.

•

The vacuum
.
Let
\ket{0}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})
be the image under the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(\varnothing)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1_{\mathcal{F}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{\varnothing}_{D_{R}}}

then we find that
\ket{0}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta=0}
. Thus, we obtain
\ket{0}\in\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
.

•

The translation operator
.
Let
a\in\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
. Since the function

D_{R}\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),\quad z\mapsto\mu_{z}^{R}(a)

is bornologically holomorphic, one can define

Ta\coloneqq\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mu_{z}^{R}(a)\Bigr|_{z=0}\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R}).

Then, by
section

3.2
, we find that
Ta\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta(a)+1}
. Hence, we obtain a linear map

T\colon\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F}),\quad a\mapsto Ta.

•

The state-field correspondence
.
Let
a,b\in\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
. Since the function

D_{R}^{\times}\to\mathcal{F}(D_{R}),\quad z\mapsto\mu_{z,0}^{R}(a\otimes b)

is bornologically holomorphic, it can be expanded in the form of Laurent series

\mu_{z,0}^{R}(a\otimes b)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)}b\quad(z\in D_{R}^{\times}),

where the series on the right-hand side converges bornologically in
\mathcal{F}(D_{R})
.
Then, by
section

3.2
, we find that
a_{(n)}b\in\mathcal{F}(D_{R})_{\Delta(a)+\Delta(b)-n-1}
. It follows from the condition (ii) in

that
a_{(n)}b=0
for

. Hence, we obtain a linear map

Y\colon\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})(\!(z)\!),\quad a\otimes b\mapsto Y(a,z)b\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)}b.

We prove that
\mathbf{V}_{\!R}(\mathcal{F})
, equipped with the above data, indeed forms a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vertex algebra. By construction, it suffices to verify that these data satisfy the conditions in
section

1.2.1
. Moreover, by the condition (iii) in

, we may assume that

Proof of vacuum axiom.

Let
a\in\mathbf{V}_{\!\infty}(\mathcal{F})
. For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we have
\mu_{z,0}^{\infty}(\ket{0}\otimes a)=a
. Hence, we obtain

\ket{0}_{(n)}a=\begin{cases}0&(n\neq-1)\\
a&(n=-1),\end{cases}

which proves that
Y(\ket{0},z)a=a
. Moreover, for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we have

\mu^{\infty}_{z,0}(a\otimes\ket{0})=\mu^{\infty}_{z}(a).

Since the function

is bornologically holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}
, it follows that
a_{(n)}\ket{0}=0
for all
n\geq 0
. Furthermore,

a_{(-1)}\ket{0}=\mu_{z}^{\infty}(a)|_{z=0}=a.

Thus, we obtain
Y(a,z)\ket{0}\in\mathbf{V}_{\!\infty}(\mathcal{F})\llbracket z\rrbracket
and
Y(a,z)\ket{0}|_{z=0}=a
.
∎

Lemma 3.2.3
.

For
(z,w)\in\Conf_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and

, we have

where the series on the right-hand side converges bornologically in
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C})

Proof.

Take
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

, then we have

Here we use
section

2.1
for the second equality, which guarantees that the series on the right-hand side converges bornologically.
∎

Proof of translation invariance.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have
\mu_{z}^{\infty}(\ket{0})=\ket{0}
, which proves
T\ket{0}=0
. Let
a,b\in\mathbf{V}_{\!\infty}(\mathcal{F})
. For

3.2
, we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}T(a_{(n)}b)=\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mu_{z+w,w}^{\infty}(a\otimes b)\Bigr|_{w=0}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mu_{z,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b)+\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mu_{z,w}^{\infty}(a\otimes b)\Bigr|_{w=0}.

such that

\Bigl.\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\mu_{z,w}^{\infty}(a\otimes b)\Bigr|_{w=0}=\mu_{z,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes Tb).

It follows that

Lemma 3.2.4
.

For
(z,w)\in\Conf_{2}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and

\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(z-w)^{-n-1}\mu_{w,0}^{\infty}(a_{(n)}b\otimes c)&\text{if}\hskip 5.69046pt|z-w|<|w|,\\

where both series on the right-hand side converge bornologically in

If

Here we use

2.1
for the second equality, which guarantees that the series on the right-hand side converges bornologically.
Similarly, one can show the equation for the case

Proof of locality.

Let
a,b\in\mathbf{V}_{\!\infty}(\mathcal{F})
, and take
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{(n)}b=b_{(n)}a=0
for
n\geq N
. It suffices to prove that

\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}\binom{N}{j}a_{(m+N-j)}(b_{(n+j)}c)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}\binom{N}{j}b_{(n+j)}(a_{(m+N-j)}c)

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c\in\mathbf{V}_{\!\infty}(\mathcal{F})
. By
section

3.2
, the function

(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\backslash\{z=w\}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C}),\quad(z,w)\mapsto(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}(a\otimes b\otimes c)

extends to a bornologically holomorphic function on
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
. Hence, we have

\Res_{z=0}\Res_{w=0}z^{m}w^{n}(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\Res_{w=0}\Res_{z=0}z^{m}w^{n}(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c).

Here we choose a bounded completant disked set
B\subset\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C})
so that the function

(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C})_{B},\quad(z,w)\mapsto(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)

is holomorphic. The residues are taken in the Banach space
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C})_{B}
, so the residue operations
\Res_{z=0}
and
\Res_{w=0}
commute.
Now, for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, fix

such that
\overline{D}_{R}(z)\cap\overline{D}_{R}=\varnothing
. Then, for

\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\mathcal{F}^{R,R;\infty}_{z,0}(a\otimes\mu_{w,0}^{R}(b\otimes c))=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w^{-n-1}\mu_{z,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b_{(n)}c).

Hence, we obtain

\Res_{z=0}\Res_{w=0}z^{m}w^{n}(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}\binom{N}{j}a_{(m+N-j)}(b_{(n+j)}c).

Similarly, one can show that

\Res_{w=0}\Res_{z=0}z^{m}w^{n}(z-w)^{N}\mu_{z,w,0}^{\infty}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}\binom{N}{j}b_{(n+k)}(a_{(m+N-j)}c).

Thus, the claim follows.

We conclude this section by explaining the relationship between
3.2.1
and the construction of Costello and Gwilliam.

In
[
CG17
, §5.3]
, they start with an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra taking values in the category of complexes of differentiable vector spaces. For concreteness, we consider an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})
. To bring this into our setting, one simply composes
\mathcal{F}
with the symmetric monoidal functor
H_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{sep}}\colon\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})\to\mathsf{CVS}
of taking homology, yielding an
S^{1}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
-equivariant prefactorization algebra
H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{CVS}
. However, for an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
that is invariant under the action of
S^{1}
, there are two possible ways to endow a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading: one way is
(H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F})(U)_{\Delta}
as before, and another is
H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}(U))
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}(U)=\{\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}(U)_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a subcomplex of
\mathcal{F}(U)
defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}(U)_{n}\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{F}(U)_{n}\mid\forall q\in S^{1},\,\sigma_{(q,0),U}(a)=q^{\Delta}a\}.

To extract a vertex algebra structure from
\mathcal{F}
, Costello and Gwilliam impose a technical condition on the
S^{1}
-action of
\mathcal{F}
, called tameness, which ensures the identification
(H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}\mathcal{F})(U)_{\Delta}=H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}(U))
. We now recall this notion.

Consider the space of distributions
\mathcal{D}(S^{1})\coloneqq C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})^{\prime}
, that is, the continuous dual of
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})
equipped with the canonical LF-topology. The linear space
\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
is an associative algebra under convolution: for
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
, their convolution
\varphi*\psi\in\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
is defined by

\varphi*\psi\colon C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},\quad f\mapsto\varphi(\psi*f),

where
\psi*f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})
denotes the convolution with a function

\psi*f\colon S^{1}\to\mathbb{C},\quad q\mapsto\psi(f(q\cdot-)).

We have an element
\delta_{q}\in\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
for each
q\in S^{1}
, called the delta-distribution at
q
. This is defined by

\delta_{q}\colon C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},\quad f\mapsto f(q).

Note that the map
\delta\colon S^{1}\to\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
,
q\mapsto\delta_{q}
is an injective monoid homomorphism. We also have an element
\pi_{\Delta}\in\mathcal{D}(S^{1})
for each
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\pi_{\Delta}\colon C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C},\quad f\mapsto\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\int_{S^{1}}z^{-\Delta-1}f(z)\,dz.

A direct calculation shows that

(3.1)

\delta_{q}*\pi_{\Delta}=q^{\Delta}\pi_{\Delta}=\pi_{\Delta}*\delta_{q}

for all
q\in S^{1}
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We equip
\mathcal{D}(S^{1})=C_{\mathrm{c}}(S^{1},\mathbb{C})^{\prime}
with the strong dual topology, i.e., the topology of uniform convergence on bounded subsets.

Let
X^{\bullet}
be a complex of convenient vector spaces endowed with an
S^{1}
-action

\sigma\colon S^{1}\to\Aut_{\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{CVS})}(X^{\bullet}),\quad q\mapsto\sigma_{q}.

For each
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, define a subcomplex
X_{\Delta}^{\bullet}
of
X^{\bullet}
by

X_{\Delta}^{n}\coloneqq\{x\in X^{n}\mid\forall q\in S^{1},\,\sigma_{q}(x)=q^{\Delta}x\}.

.

Let

be a complex of convenient vector spaces endowed with an

We now make several remarks on the topology of

4.
Factorization envelopes and enveloping vertex algebras

In this section, we present the main results of this note. We construct an amenably holomorphic prefactorization algebra via the factorization envelope, starting from a Lie conformal algebra, and prove that the associated vertex algebra is isomorphic to the enveloping vertex algebra. This construction generalizes those of the Kac–Moody factorization algebra
[
CG17
, §5.5]
and the Virasoro factorization algebra
[
W17
]
. Moreover, the super analogue of this construction yields new factorization algebras that correspond to vertex superalgebras, such as the Neveu–Schwarz vertex superalgebra, the
N=2
vertex superalgebra, and the
N=4
vertex superalgebra.

4.1.
The compactly supported Dolbeault complex

This subsection provides some recollections on the compactly supported Dolbeault complex. We use the following notations:

•

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we denote
[m]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

•

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote

\mathbb{N}^{m}_{n}\coloneqq\{\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\mid\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{m}=n\}.

Let
X
be a complex manifold. For an open subset
U\subset X
, consider the
\mathbb{C}
-linear space
\mathcal{A}^{0,k}_{X}(U)
of
C^{\infty}
-class complex
(0,k)
-differentials on
U
. Take a local coordinate system
\mathcal{S}=\{(U_{\alpha},z_{1}^{\alpha},\ldots,z_{m}^{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A}
of
U

\Lambda_{k}(U,\mathcal{S})
the set of tuples
\lambda=(\alpha,K,n,I)
consisting of

•

\alpha\in A
,

•

a compact set

,

•

n\in\mathbb{N}
,

•

I\subset[m]
such that
\#I=k
.

equipped with the locally convex topology defined by the set of semi-norms

is a Fréchet space. This topology is independent of the choice of the local coordinate system of

(2)

of

(1) Take

. For

Lemma 4.3.6

Let

For each

g(t)\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{-1}(\tau)\,d\tau-\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_{1}(\tau)\,d\tau,

\displaystyle=(\overline{\xi^{f_{0}}_{m}\otimes x})(\overline{\overline{\partial}g^{\times}(z)z^{n}\otimes y})+[\overline{\xi^{f_{0}}_{m}\otimes x},\,\overline{g^{\times}(z)z^{n}\otimes y}]

\displaystyle\Phi_{I}([x_{[m]},y_{[n]}])

Partnering RemoTe monitoring of Implanted Cardiac devices with Intelligent PATient Engagement – PARTICIPATE trial: a protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Brodie Sheahen, Liliana Laranjo, Gopal Sivagangabalan, Tim Shaw, Aravinda Thiagalingam, Clara K Chow
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCardiac implantable electronic devices (CIED) can transfer data to the healthcare team, remotely. National and international cardiac organisations recommend all patients use this technology, however patient engagement is suboptimal. Previously, in cardiovascular patients, SMS messaging services have shown improvements in patient engagement and subsequent health outcomes. This paper describes the protocol and intervention of a randomised controlled trial (RCT) to assess the feasibility of a novel CIED remote monitoring SMS patient support programme on self-efficacy in managing CIED and cardiovascular health following CIED implantation.Methods/analysisA two-arm RCT will be conducted of 100 participants with 1:1 allocation to intervention or control. Participants awaiting-CIED or immediately post-CIED implantation from sites throughout Australia will be invited to partake. The intervention group will receive regular SMS communication with a range of educational and self-efficacy resources, in conjunction with engagement initiatives following CIED clinical issue detection. The control group will receive CIED remote monitoring education and clinical issue management as per standard practice at their respective sites. The primary outcome will assess the patient’s capacity to manage their CIED as measured by the ‘Self-Efficacy Expectations After Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator Implantation Scale’. Secondary outcomes will assess participant’s ability to manage their cardiovascular condition, CIED remote monitoring, quality of life, impact on health service utilisation, cardiovascular behavioural risk factor change and motivation to improve cardiovascular health. A sample size of 100 will have a 90% power to detect a minimum difference of 1.07 in the ‘Self-Efficacy Expectations After Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator Implantation Scale’ between the intervention and control group with an alpha value of 0.05.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval for this study has been obtained from the Western Sydney Local Health District Human Research Ethics Committee. Background

Cardiac implanted electronic devices (CIED) are sophisticated and expensive technologies, usually implanted in people with advanced heart disease, heart failure or arrhythmias, to pace the heart or treat potentially life-threatening tachyarrhythmias. It is estimated that 1.4 million CIEDs are implanted globally each year,1 with an increasing prevalence across multiple countries,2 3 which can largely be attributed to people living longer and broadened indications for implant.4 Essential to caring for CIED patients is to engage them in shared decision-making, both prior to and after implant. The US Federal Department of Health’s Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services has recently mandated that patients requiring a CIED are to be engaged in shared decision-making of their management.5

Since 2008, new CIEDs are capable of remote monitoring (RM), that is, they can continuously monitor the heart and send a subset of this information in a daily summary from the home or remote locations to their healthcare team.6 This has translated to improvements in the efficiency of follow-up of patients with CIEDs that are cost-effective.7 Consequently, professional cardiac organisations such as the Heart Rhythm Society,8 European Heart Rhythm Association8 and Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand9 recommend that all patients be offered the RM utility when feasible. In addition to improved follow-up efficiency, RM and regular monitoring of CIED data has opened opportunities to add value to the care of patients with CIEDs in heart failure management10 and atrial fibrillation detection and stroke prevention.11 Additionally, RM and CIED data can be leveraged to improve patient engagement with their care.12

Two recent areas of focus for CIED patient engagement include (1) patient interaction with their RM data and (2) patient education in post-implantation care. There is an increasing desire for patients to interact with their RM data, especially data points that can be actionable.12 CIED RM can detect a wide range of clinical issues which, in theory, could prompt patients to modify behaviour and improve condition understanding; however, this is not routinely implemented in clinical practice. Some small studies have evaluated the value of patient engagement initiatives that allow patients access to their CIED data. These include small qualitative and mixed-method studies using semi-structured interviews, surveys and user engagement data to examine the impact on patient outcomes and clinical care.13,15 These studies have demonstrated that providing patients with access to CIED data has the potential to improve satisfaction,14 appropriately react to data,13 increase shared decision-making without increasing workload14 and may enhance clinical and economic outcomes by allowing patients to have an active role in their health management.15 16 While these findings are promising, they are limited by study size and design and none have included randomised controlled trials. The impact of CIED data sharing on multiple outcomes, including clinical outcomes, patient-reported outcomes and experience need to be assessed in randomised controlled trials.

Patient education following a CIED implant is important to optimise care and ensure adherence to management advice. Despite needing to be an active member in their management, patients often believe that their CIED knowledge is insufficient. In a survey conducted with over 300 patients with CIEDs, respondents were found to have a substantial deficit in their CIED knowledge, while simultaneously having a strong desire to receive more information.17 Common topics patients have reported to lack of knowledge in were in regard to restrictions on activities of daily living and how to deal with an emergency, for example, implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD) shock occurrence while swimming.18 Recently, an evaluation of patients post-CIED implant demonstrated that lack of knowledge about restrictions on daily activities led to unnecessarily restraining from activities.19 Furthermore, it was shown that patients with a greater understanding of their CIED and post-implantation restrains had a greater quality of life.19 Typically, clinicians provide patients with detailed information regarding their CIED management post implant; however, a downfall to this method is that patients often forget the conveyed information, have limited time to discuss it with the clinician and are apprehensive to ask too many questions.18 Despite there being a need for improved patient education and delivery methods post-CIED implant, to date, there has not been a CIED, and RM patient education programme developed and assessed within a randomised controlled study to assess the impact on clinical outcomes and patient acceptability.

There is a clear need for increased patient support and engagement post-CIED implantation, particularly regarding appropriate CIED education delivery and patient interaction with their RM data. Therefore, this paper describes the protocol and intervention of PARTCIPATE—a study that aims to examine the feasibility and efficacy of a novel patient-centred CIED and RM patient support programme on patient-reported outcomes.

Methods

Study design

The PARTICIPATE intervention is to be evaluated in a multicentre randomised controlled trial (1:1 allocation ratio) of 100 patients with a recently implanted CIED with RM capacity complemented by a mixed-methods process evaluation. Intervention participants will receive the PARTICIPATE programme that will monitor and provide semi-personalised support and as required engagement with their healthcare team post CIED implant. The control group will receive usual care (figure 1). Due to the nature of the intervention, participants cannot be blinded, however, staff recruiting participants and researchers analysing the data will be blinded to treatment allocation.

The trial has been registered in the Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry.

Patient population

The study population will be patients with recently implanted CIEDs with atrial sensing capacity and RM capability. This study will be conducted in multiple centres throughout Australia. Initial sites include Westmead Hospital, Western Sydney and Wollongong Hospital, Illawarra region, New South Wales. Western Sydney serves a financially and culturally diverse population with 46.8% of residents born overseas.20 The Illawarra region, is an ageing population with 9.8% aged 75 years and over, forecasted to increase to 13.6% by 2041.21

Recruitment and consent

Recruitment

Study participants will be recruited from both the public and private health services in Australia. Centres that implant and manage CIEDs will be identified through clinical colleagues and networks. The participating cardiologist investigators will notify the research team when they have a potentially eligible patient. The research team staff will contact the patient to discuss the PARTICIPATE trial, confirm eligibility and enrol the patient into the trial if agreeable. Patients will be contacted by either phone call, SMS or, in-person for enrolment and consent.

With respect to the qualitative evaluation following the main study implementation, healthcare workers (cardiologists, cardiac technicians and nurses) and Westmead Applied Research Centre (WARC) research team members involved in the implementation of the trial will be invited to participate.

Consent informed consent will be obtained either in person or over the phone. Participants will be provided with a copy of the participant information sheet and consent form (PICF) in person or if consented over the phone, an electronic PICF will be used which will be provided to the patient via email or a hard copy as per the participants preference.

Randomisation

There will be 1:1 randomisation to intervention and control stratified by CIED type and site of recruitment. Once the patient has consented, the baseline electronic survey will (see online supplemental appendix 1) be initiated usually via an electronic tablet. Following this, participants will be randomised and allocated to either the intervention group, receiving the PARTICIPATE intervention of CIED RM and education, or to a control group in which they will receive standard care of CIED RM and education, for a duration of 6 months. Randomisation will occur centrally via a computer-generated sequence, using the randomise R library of R statistical software (V.4.2.3) and the sequence imported into the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) platform.

PARTICIPATE intervention

PARTICIPATE overview

A multistep co-design process involving multiple stakeholders was used to develop the PARTICIPATE programme. The goal was to develop a programme that effectively delivered relevant, important and accurate CIED and cardiovascular disease (CVD) information to patients following CIED implantation. The PARTICIPATE programme is composed of two main components: (1) post-CIED implant support that provides information on their CIED and improves their sense of involvement and confidence in managing their CIED and cardiovascular condition and, (2) provision of remote monitored clinical issue detection triggering as needed health provider and patient support and engagement (figure 2). The TextCare platform will be used to programme customisation algorithms, manage content, document delivery of SMS messages and interactions between participants and healthcare team. TextCare is a software developed at the University of Sydney and has been successfully used in multiple other health programmes and studies.22,26

Post-CIED implant support

Regular SMS messages will be used to deliver important CIED and CVD information to participants following CIED implantation. SMS messages will be semi-personalised based on factors including: CIED type, employment status, driving status, vegetarian status, presence of cardiovascular comorbidities and risk factors. Message content is supplemented by additional information from websites, videos, images that have been curated by the clinical steering group. The delivery structure of messages has been developed to address two phases. The initial phase content (Week 0–6) focuses on CIED and RM. The content development is described below, but the content has been co-designed with cardiologists, cardiac technicians, health professions and patients and curated resources drawn from industry partners and the National Heart Foundation. The timing and ordering of delivery of the information has been guided by health professional and consumers. In phase two (Week 7–26) content focuses more broadly on cardiovascular health and healthy lifestyle behaviours. The timing and delivery of content are not as strictly structured.

Participants will be informed that they have access to a two-way communication portal, allowing direct communication between the participant and the PARTICIPATE central monitoring unit (CMU). The CMU is composed of a cardiologist, doctor-in-training, health counsellor, research assistant and CIED technical support officer. The communication portal will be monitored daily by the health counsellor who will review the messages received by the participants and then follow a predefined response manual to issue an appropriate response. If an appropriate response is not available using the predefined manual, the participant’s question will be escalated to the relevant CMU team member (doctor-in-training, cardiologist or CIED technical support officer). Once the appropriate response has been formed, the health counsellor will reply to the participant via the communication portal.

Remote monitored clinical issue detection and engagement

Selected algorithms will be implemented based on CIED device type that will trigger additional patient support if a clinical issue is detected. Clinical issues that will be detected include: new-onset atrial fibrillation (AF), change in AF burden, increased likelihood of heart failure deterioration, ICD shock and non-transmission of CIED data. If these are detected participants will be provided additional informational and educational messages about the clinical issue over a 1–5-day period following the clinical issue detection. Additional information could provide an explanation of the clinical issue, and/or tips on how to improve their cardiovascular health. The PARTICIPATE CMU team will also ensure that the home clinical team for the participant is aware of these issues, though they are likely to be aware of these through their own internal monitoring practices.

Control

The control group will not receive the intervention, rather receive current standard care of CIED RM and education for a duration of 6 months. Standard of care is heterogenous among device clinics. A description of standard care for each site will be obtained. The control group will only receive SMS messages from the PARTICIPATE team to complete the follow-up surveys.

Stakeholder involvement in intervention co-design

The intervention underwent development via a multistage process that engaged key stakeholders, including clinicians (cardiologists, cardiac technicians and cardiac nurses), CIED industry partners, researchers and consumers. Initial workshops were conducted that highlighted current gaps in patient care following CIED implantation and RM engagement. Subsequently, semi-structured interviews with CIED remote monitored patients highlighted aspects of information that patients found valuable post-CIED implantation and additional information they desired. Furthermore, semi-structured interviews were conducted with cardiac technicians, cardiac nurses and cardiologists, separately, to understand the current standard of practice of CIED and RM information delivery and the use of CIED data to detect and act on clinical issues. The PARTICIPATE intervention concept was subsequently derived encompassing two main components described above, (1) post-CIED implant support package and (2) remote monitored clinical issue detection and patient engagement package. Industry partners and digital health professionals at WARC, provided guidance on the capabilities of both the CIED RM software and TextCare platform, respectively. Clinicians were involved in identifying which clinical alerts were to be included in the PARTICIPATE programme and the content delivered in the SMS messages. Researchers experienced in similar cardiovascular SMS programmes provided guidance on the frequency, message length, timing and readability of the messages. The final developed messages were then reviewed and approved by consumers, clinicians and industry partners prior to study commencement.

Study outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is patient self-efficacy in managing their CIED (belief in their confidence and capacity to manage their CIED) post-implantation. CIED self-efficacy will be measured using the ‘Self-Efficacy Expectations After Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator Implantation Scale’,27 at a 7-week follow-up. There will be 16 questions, requiring a rating from 1 to 10. Each question will be tallied and divided by 16 providing an overall average number out of 10.

Sample size

This trial is designed to test the study hypotheses with a conventional two-sided α value of 0.05 and a statistical power (1−β) of 90%. Event rates are based on the estimated event rates of the trial by Lau et al.28 An average ‘Self-Efficacy Expectations After Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator Implantation’ Score of 7.98 for patients in the control group was estimated based on the trial by Lau et al.28 Accounting for the possibility of dropout and missing data to 15% during the study, a number of 100 patients will be enrolled such that the study will be powered to detect a minimum difference of 1.07 points in ‘Self-Efficacy Expectations After Implantable Cardioverter Defibrillator Implantation’ score from 7.98 to 9.05 with an SD of 1.5.

Statistical analysis

Our analysis will be conducted in accordance with a prespecified statistical analysis plan that will be finalised prior to study completion, data lock and unblinding. Continuous data will be checked for normality before performing parametric tests (two-tailed t-tests). Appropriate non-parametric tests (eg, Mann-Whitney U test) will be used where data are not normally distributed. Continuous variables will be presented as means and SDs unless they are skewed, in which case medians and IQRs will be used. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages. Effect estimates will be reported with 95% CIs. The analysis will follow the principles of intention-to-treat, with participants analysed in the arm they have been allocated. The primary outcome (self-efficacy managing CIED), groups will be compared using analysis of covariance adjusted for appropriate baseline values. Similarly, other continuous variables secondary outcomes (CIED RM self-efficacy, CVD management self-efficacy, health service utilisation events, quality of life, cardiovascular behavioural risk factor change and motivation to improve cardiovascular health/CIED self-management) will be adjusted using appropriate baseline values. P value<0.05 will be considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses will be performed using R statistical software (V.4.2.3)

Process evaluation

An implementation process evaluation will be conducted to assess intervention engagement, feasibility, implementation efficacy and user experiences. This evaluation will use semi-structured interviews with a sample of intervention participants and site research members, in conjunction with participant surveys and engagement metrics of the TextCare platform. The implementation evaluation will be structured using the Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation and Maintenance framework29 at the completion of the intervention.

Interview data will be analysed using thematic analysis30 of transcribed audio recordings in NVivo software (V.14). The designated researcher will perform line-by-line coding of imported data and inductively delineate key concepts and then group-related concepts into themes. The coding hierarchy will be reviewed by a second investigator for quality assurance and rigour, and themes will be discussed between investigators to ensure the breadth and depth of content have been appropriately captured. Reporting will follow the COREQ (Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research) checklist.31

Discussion

Patient engagement following CIED implant is increasingly recommended by cardiac organisations, however, is underprovided in clinical practice. Currently, the provision of patient education is lacking and the utilisation of CIED RM data is underused to improve patient management. Providing a post-CIED implant support programme may address these issues and enhance the patient experience and involvement in their care and have other beneficial outcomes on patient self-efficacy, health behaviour modification and health service utilisation. The proposed study evaluates the efficacy of novel CIED RM management programme composed of regular semi-personalised SMS messages on CIED and cardiovascular information, two-way communication portal and RM clinical issue detection and subsequent participant advice and support in a multicentre randomised control trial.

Anticipated findings

This study presents a novel way to use CIED RM data and improve patient engagement post CIED implant. Our primary objective is to assess the impact of patient self-efficacy in managing their CIED post implantation. However, important learnings will be gained from secondary patient benefits and a detailed evaluation of the implementation, feasibility and acceptability of this management programme. Appropriately designed post-CIED implant management programmes can meet the request for improved patient engagement in care; yet the development of such programmes and efficacy analysis is lacking.

Strengths and limitations

To the best of our knowledge this will be the first evaluation of a carefully designed and literature-informed CIED patient support programme using CIED RM data to support patients with their CIED and cardiovascular management. The PARTICIPATE programme has been co-designed with cardiologists, non-specialist doctors, cardiac technicians, researchers, CIED industry partners and patients. The trial will be implemented at multiple sites in Australia and will include patients from both the public and private sectors to ensure findings are applicable to future real-world implementation.

The limitations of the study include the trial being conducted for a 6-month duration and not being powered to assess the impact of health service utilisation and other secondary outcomes. Additionally, the trial is limited by the inclusion criteria requiring participants to own and be able to operate a mobile phone, thereby potentially restricting larger enrolment numbers. An alternative could have been to include a caregiver who owns and is able to operate a mobile phone.

Potential implications

Post-CIED patient support programmes and utilisation of RM data to improve patient engagement is of high interest to CIED device clinics in both the private and public sectors and cardiac device companies. If successful in this trial, patient CIED RM support programmes may be able to complement routine CIED management post implantation and have the potential to be significantly scalable. Furthermore, the delivery of curated information from a trusted source to patients at times when most relevant is of high yield and likely to be highly socially accepted.

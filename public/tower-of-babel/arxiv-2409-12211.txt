Analysis of “Revaluation of the lower critical field in superconducting H 3 ​ S H_{3}S and L ​ a ​ H 10 LaH_{10} (Nature Comm. 13, 3194, 2022)” by V. S. Minkov et al
J. E. Hirsch a and M. van Kampen b

Abstract
In Nat Comm. 13, 3194, 2022 e2021p and an “ Author Correction ” to it correction , Minkov et al. presented magnetization data versus applied magnetic field for H 3 ​ S H_{3}S and L ​ a ​ H 10 LaH_{10} under pressure, argued that the data provide evidence that these materials are superconducting at high temperatures, and extracted from the reported data the behavior of lower critical fields versus temperature. In several papers unpulling ; bending ; hysteresis ; ma analyzing Refs. e2021p ; correction it was shown that the published magnetization data could not have been obtained from the reported measured data through the processes described in Refs. e2021p ; correction . Recently, Minkov et al performed a revaluation of their experimental results reval and argued that the results derived from their new analysis are consistent with the results reported earlier e2021p . In addition, they made public the underlying data data from which the data published in Ref. e2021p were derived. In this paper we analyze those underlying data and conclude that (a) the data published in Ref. e2021p are incompatible with the underlying measured data, and (b) the revaluation analysis presented in Ref. reval does not support the conclusions drawn by the authors in Ref. reval nor Ref. e2021p .

Instead, for a superconducting sample with very high upper critical field (
\sim 90T

these samples to be consistent with other experiments, one expects the diamagnetic
response to persist well above magnetic field 1T for all temperatures smaller than
the critical temperature. That expectation is not met by the data shown in Fig.
10. If the slope of the subtracted background is assumed to be somewhat smaller
than what the authors assumed the moment would turn upward at lower field and again
downward at higher field, also not expected for a superconductor.

The authors provide no rationale for their scaling of the cut-off value with the
(average) uncertainty on magnetic moment measurements, except that they
believe that

above the chosen cut-off value. However, scaling

number of Z-scans and hence averages. By scaling

In their re-analysis the authors use a 10x larger

IV.4
Mismatch in datasets

A revaluation can be expected to re-analyze the same data in a different
manner. In Sect. II it was found that the data published in the OSF repository
mostly does not match the the data published in
e2021p
. For every dataset
the virgin curve is different for fields
>30
mT. This may be explained by
undisclosed unconventional smoothing of the (raw) data shown in Figs. 3(e), 3(f),
S10, S11, and S12 of the original publication and its correction. However, also
many curves are found to differ in the low-noise and presumably unsmoothed
\leq 30

3

10

there is no overlap at all between the virgin data analyzed in the original
publication and in its revaluation.

Presenting an analysis of mostly different datasets as a revaluation is a
surprising choice.

IV.6
Conclusions on the validity of the revaluation procedure

The lower critical field is determined from the point where
M(H)
deviates by
a certain cut-off from linear. In their revaluation the authors use a fitting
procedure that is principally reproducible and takes away some of the
arbitrariness involved in the earlier ‘by eye’ determination. However, we
believe that the large noise on the
M(H)
data precludes any objective
determination of a deviation from linear. The good correspondence between the original
and re-evaluated values appears to be the result of adjusting the cut-off value
and the fitting range, sub-optimal fitting, and possibly the choice of datasets.
Especially the per
M(H)
curve selection of cut-off value based on noise
level is most puzzling. It goes against the fixed-value approach of the original
publication
talan
and results in an obvious bias towards higher
H_{p}

values for the noisier low-temperature curves.

V
Conclusions

In this paper we have analyzed the relation between the results published in
Ref.
e2021p
and the underlying data reported in
data
. We also
evaluated the consistency of the revaluation procedure reported in Ref.
reval

that claims to lead to results consistent with those of Ref.
e2021p
. We
have found that:

(1) The data reported in Ref.
e2021p
are incompatible with the
underlying measured data of
data
in a variety of ways.

(2) The revaluation procedure
reval
uses arbitrary criteria for
choosing the parameters in the fitting procedure, and the large noise of the data precludes any objective
determination of where they deviate from linearity.

(3) As a consequence of (1) and (2), the revaluation analysis presented in Ref.
reval
does not
support the conclusions drawn by the authors in Ref.
reval
nor Ref.
e2021p
.

(4) Questions about the consistency of a variety of statements in Refs.
e2021p
,

correction
,
reval
with reality remain open.

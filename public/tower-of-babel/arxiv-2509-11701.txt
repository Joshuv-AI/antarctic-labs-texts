Abstract
. The rectangle condition for a genus g g Heegaard splitting of a 3-manifold, defined by Casson and Gordon in [ 2 ] , provides a sufficient criterion for the Heegaard splitting to be strongly irreducible. However it is unknown whether there exists a strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting which does not satisfy the rectangle condition. In this paper we provide a counterexample of a genus 2 Heegaard splitting of a 3-manifold which is strongly irreducible but fails to satisfy the rectangle condition. The way of constructing such an example is to take a double branched cover of a 3-bridge decomposition of a knot in S 3 S^{3} which is strongly irreducible but does not meet the rectangle condition. This implies that the rectangle condition does not detect the strong irreducibility. As our next goal, we expect that this result provides the weaker version of the rectangle condition which detects the strong irreducibility.

Introduction and main results

A genus
g
Heegaard splitting of a 3-manifold

For instance, if a 3-manifold admits a strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting, then it is irreducible and

Furthermore, if a genus 2 Heegaard splitting is strongly irreducible, then the Heegaard genus of the 3-manifold is 2.
This is because if a 3-manifold

has a Heegaard genus 0 or 1, then

is either

or a lens space,
in which case any genus

) Heegaard splitting of

or a lens space is weakly reducible.

\beta=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\}
be systems of bridge arcs for
T_{1}
and

respectively that satisfy the rectangle condition. Then

is in normal form with respect to

Proof.

For a contradiction, assume that

are two adjacent intersections in

\alpha_{i}
with endpoints

to reduce its intersection number with

Thus,

separates the other two bridge arcs different from

.

\Sigma

Now, we prove the ‘only if’ part. Applying Lemma

1.1
Fractional linear differential equations

Though fractional differential equations have a variety of applications in modelling physical processes, their theory is not as well developed as that of ordinary differential equations.

1.2
Fractional integrals and derivatives

Let
0<T\leq\infty
and
L(0,T)
be the class of all integrable functions on
(0,T)
. The Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative of order
\alpha>0
for
\varphi\in L(0,T)
is defined as

D^{\alpha}\varphi(x)=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\{I^{n-\alpha}\varphi(x)\},\quad\alpha\in(n-1,n],\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where

I^{\alpha}\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{\varphi(t)\,dt}{(x-t)^{1-\alpha}}

is the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha>0
for
\varphi
,
\Gamma(\alpha)
is the Gamma function. In particular, if
0<\alpha<1
, then

D^{\alpha}\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dx}\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{\varphi(t)\,dt}{(x-t)^{\alpha}},

provided that
I^{1-\alpha}\varphi
is absolutely continuous on
(0,T)
.

The fractional derivative and integral have the following property (
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle I^{\alpha}x^{\beta-1}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\beta+\alpha)}x^{\beta+\alpha-1},\;D^{\alpha}x^{\beta-1}=\frac{\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\beta-\alpha)}x^{\beta-\alpha-1},\quad\alpha,\beta>0,\alpha\neq\beta,

(1.2)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}1=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}x^{-\alpha},\;D^{\alpha}x^{\alpha-j}=0,\quad\alpha>0,j\in\{1,2,\dots,[\alpha]+1\}.

(1.3)

We can see that, on one hand, Riemann-Liouville fractional differentiation can produce a singularity and, on the other hand, it can be defined on functions with a singularity at the origin. Despite this the
Caputo-Djrbashian, or regularized fractional derivative

\displaystyle(\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}\varphi)(x)=D^{\alpha}\left(\varphi(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(0)}{k!}x^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=D^{\alpha}\varphi(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(0)}{\Gamma(k+1-\alpha)}x^{k-\alpha},\quad n-1<\alpha\leq n,

(1.4)

is defined on functions that are continuous with their derivatives up to order
n-1
and vanishes on constants, which is more natural for physical applications.

Though the operator
I^{\alpha}
is associative and commutative with respect to the index, i.e.
I^{\alpha}\circ I^{\beta}=I^{\beta}\circ I^{\alpha}=I^{\alpha+\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta>0

1.3
Wiman-Valiron theory

For
r\in[0,+\infty)
and an entire function
f
of the form (
1.9
) we denote
M(r,f)=\max\{|f(z)|:|z|=r\}
. We define the maximal term as
\mu(r,f)=\max\{|a_{n}|r^{n}:n\geq 0\}
and the central index of the series as
\nu(r,f)=\max\{n\geq 0:|a_{n}|r^{n}=\mu(r,f)\}
. Note that
\nu(r,f)
is non-decreasing, and an entire function

is transcendental if and only if
\nu(r,f)\to+\infty
as
r\to+\infty
.

For a non-constant entire function
f
, the order
\sigma(f)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma(f):=\varlimsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log M(r,f)}{\log r}=\varlimsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\nu(r,f)}{\log r}.

(1.11)

Let
V
be the class of positive continuous nondecreasing functions
v
on
[0,+\infty)
such that
\frac{x^{2}}{v(x)\ln v(x)}
increases to
+\infty
on

,
x_{0}>0
, and
\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{v(x)}<+\infty
. For example, the functions
v_{0}(x)=x^{\beta}

(x\geq 0)
,
1<\beta<2
, and
v_{1}(x)=x\ln^{\alpha+1}x
,
(x\geq e)
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, belong to
V
.

A measurable set
E\subset[0,\infty)
is called of finite logarithmic measure if
\int_{E\cap[1,\infty)}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty
.

The main result of the Wiman-Valiron theory can be formulated as follows (cf.
[
21
, Lemma 3.8]
)

Theorem A
.

Let
v\in V
and
\varkappa(t)=4\sqrt{v(t)\ln v(t)}
. Suppose that
f
is an entire function,
|z_{0}|=r
, and

|f(z_{0})|\geq M(r,f)v^{-2}(\nu(r,f)),

holds. There exists a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
of finite logarithmic measure such that if

r\left(1-\frac{1}{40\varkappa(\nu)}\right)<\rho<r\left(1+\frac{1}{40\varkappa(\nu)}\right),\quad r\not\in E,\nu=\nu(r,f),

and

, then we have for
|z|=\rho

\left(\frac{r}{\nu}\right)^{q}f^{(q)}(z)=f(z)+O\left(\frac{\varkappa(\nu)}{\nu}\right)M(\rho,f).

In particular, if

then

M(\rho,f^{(q)})=\left(\frac{\nu}{\rho}\right)^{q}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{\varkappa(\nu)}{\nu}\right)\right\}M(\rho,f)=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{\nu}{r}\right)^{q}M(r,f)

as
r\rightarrow+\infty
,

.

The main result of

literally repeats Theorem

for arbitrary

and

|f(z_{0})|\geq M(r,f)v^{-2}(\nu(r,f))

holds. Then there exists a set

of finite logarithmic measure such that if

r\left(1-\frac{1}{40\varkappa(\nu)}\right)<\rho<r\left(1+\frac{1}{40\varkappa(\nu)}\right),\quad r\not\in E,\nu=\nu(r,f),

\alpha>0
and

2.2

Suppose that

According to Faá di Bruno’s formula

(f\circ g)^{(n)}=\sum_{j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+nj_{n}=n}\frac{n!}{j_{1}!\cdots j_{n}!}f^{(j_{1}+\dots+j_{n})}(g)\prod_{s=1}^{n}\left(\frac{g^{(s)}}{s!}\right)^{j_{s}}.

Noting that

We write

.
To estimate the Bell polynomial we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5

For
\alpha>0
,
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq k

, we have

(2.4)

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Since
g^{(j)}(w)=\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-j+1)w^{\alpha-j}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{n,n}(g^{\prime})=\alpha^{n}w^{n\alpha-n},\;n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle B_{n,n-1}(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\alpha^{n-1}(\alpha-1)w^{(n-1)\alpha-n},\;n\geq 2.

Thus, the assertion of the lemma holds for
k\in\{n-1,n\}

To show that this is true for
1\leq k<n-1
we use the induction in
k
.

For
k=1

B_{n,1}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=z_{n}
, so
(w^{\alpha})^{(n)}=\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-n+1)w^{\alpha-n}
, and the assertion follows.

Assume that (
2.4
) holds for
n\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq n
, and
k\leq m-1

is already considered. Then

\displaystyle(f\circ g)^{(m+1)}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{(k)}(g)B_{m,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+1)})\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(f^{(k+1)}(g)g^{\prime}B_{m,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+1)})+f^{(k)}(g)(B_{m,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+1)}))^{\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{m+1}f^{(k)}(g)g^{\prime}B_{m,k-1}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+2)})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}f^{(k)}(g)(B_{m,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+1)}))^{\prime}.

Combining this with (

\displaystyle B^{*}_{m+1,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+2)})=|g^{\prime}|B^{*}_{m,k-1}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+2)})+

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(m-k+1)j_{m-k+1}=m\\
j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{m-k+1}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{m!}{j_{1}!\cdots j_{m-k+1}!}\sum_{s=1}^{m-k+1}j_{s}\frac{|g^{(s+1)}|}{|g^{(s)}|}\prod_{l=1}^{m-k+1}\left(\frac{|g^{(l)}|}{l!}\right)^{j_{l}}

(2.5)

Evidently,
\frac{|g^{(s+1)}|}{|g^{(s)}|}=\frac{|\alpha-s|}{|w|}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{m-k+1}j_{s}\frac{|g^{(s+1)}|}{|g^{(s)}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|w|}(\alpha(j_{1}+\dots+j_{m-k+1})+j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(m-k+j)j_{m-k+j})=\frac{\alpha k+m}{|w|}.

We rewrite (

\displaystyle B^{*}_{m+1,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+2)})

\displaystyle\leq|g^{\prime}|B^{*}_{m,k-1}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+2)})+\frac{\alpha k+m}{|w|}B^{*}_{m,k}(g^{\prime},\dots,g^{(m-k+1)})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha|w|^{\alpha-1}(m-1)!\alpha^{k-1}(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+m-k+1)|w|^{(k-1)\alpha-m}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha k+m}{|w|}(m-1)!\alpha^{k}(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+m-k)|w|^{k\alpha-m}

\displaystyle=(m-1)!|w|^{k\alpha-m-1}\alpha^{k}(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+m-k)\left(\alpha+m-k+1+\alpha k+m\right)

\displaystyle\leq m!|w|^{k\alpha-m-1}\alpha^{k}(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+m-k)(\alpha+m-k+1)

as long as

This inequality is equivalent to

, while the right-hand side is nonpositive for

. The induction step is proved. The assertion of the lemma follows.

In our notation we have the representation for the fractional derivative of a formal solution due to Remark
2

\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}^{k}y(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m+k}\frac{\Gamma((m+k)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(m\alpha+1)}x^{m\alpha}.

(4.2)

This yields
\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}^{k}y(0)=a_{k}\Gamma(k\alpha+1)
. Hence,
a_{k}=b_{k}/\Gamma(k\alpha+1)
,
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.
Substituting (
4.2
) into (
1.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m+n}\frac{\Gamma((m+n)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(m\alpha+1)}x^{m\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}p_{mk}x^{m\alpha}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m+k}\frac{\Gamma((m+k)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(m\alpha+1)}x^{m\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}x^{m\alpha}\sum_{s=0}^{m}a_{s+k}\frac{\Gamma((s+k)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(s\alpha+1)}p_{m-s,k}.

(4.3)

Equating the coefficients of the same degree in (
4.3
), we write

a_{m+n}\frac{\Gamma((m+n)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(m\alpha+1)}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=0}^{m}a_{s+k}p_{m-s,k}\frac{\Gamma((s+k)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(s\alpha+1)},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(4.4)

Let
r\in(0,\rho)
. Then there exists
M>0
such that

|p_{j,k}|\leq\frac{M}{r^{j\alpha}},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\;k\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}.

(4.5)

Lemma 6
.

Under the above conditions the following estimate for the coefficients is valid

|a_{p}|\leq\beta_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)

(4.6)

for some positive sequence
(\beta_{p})
such that
\beta_{p}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
, where
\prod\limits_{j\in\varnothing}c_{j}:=1
.

In particular,
\lim\limits_{p\to\infty}\sqrt[p]{|a_{p}|}\leq r^{-\alpha}
.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

It follows from properties of the Gamma function that

\frac{\Gamma(m\alpha+1)}{\Gamma((m+n)\alpha+1)}\leq\frac{M_{1}}{(m\alpha+1)^{n\alpha}}=:\gamma_{m},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},n\in\mathbb{N}

(4.7)

and

\frac{\Gamma((s+k)\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(s\alpha+1)}\leq{M_{2}}{((s+k)\alpha+1)^{k\alpha}}=:\delta_{s,k},\quad s,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.8)

The values
\beta_{0}
, …,
\beta_{n-1}
are chosen recursively so that the equality in (
4.6
) holds, i.e.

\beta_{0}=|a_{0}|,\;\beta_{1}=|a_{1}|,\;|a_{2}|=\beta_{2}(r^{-\alpha}+\beta_{1}),\dots,|a_{n-1}|=\beta_{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right).

We then write

\beta_{m+n}=\frac{nMM_{1}M_{2}C}{(m\alpha+1)^{\alpha}}/{\min_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\prod_{j=m+k+1}^{m+n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)},\quad m\geq 0,

where the constant
C
will be specified later. It is an elementary exercise to prove that
\beta_{p}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
.

We prove (
4.6
) by induction. Since the induction base holds by the choice of
\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{n-1}
, it is sufficient to prove the induction step.

Let
m\geq 0
, and (
4.6
) hold for
0\leq p\leq m+n-1
. Then by (
4.4
)

\displaystyle|a_{m+n}|\leq\gamma_{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\beta_{k+s}\prod_{j=1}^{k+s-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\frac{M}{r^{\alpha(m-s)}}\delta_{s,k}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{m}M\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{m,k}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\frac{\beta_{k+s}}{r^{\alpha(m-s)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k+s-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right).

(4.9)

Consider the internal sum. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{m}\frac{\beta_{k+s}}{r^{\alpha(m-s)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k+s-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\beta_{k}}{r^{\alpha m}}+\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{r^{\alpha(m-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{k}\right)+\dots+\beta_{k+m}\prod_{j=k}^{k+m-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle<\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha(m+1)}}+\frac{\beta_{k}}{r^{\alpha m}}+\frac{\beta_{k+1}}{r^{\alpha(m-1)}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{k}\right)+\dots\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\beta_{k+m}\prod_{j=k}^{k+m-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\prod_{j=k}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right).

Substituting this estimate into (
4.9
) we get

\displaystyle|a_{m+n}|\leq\gamma_{m}M\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{m,k}\prod_{j=1}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq MM_{1}M_{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{((m+k)\alpha+1)^{k\alpha}}{(m\alpha+1)^{n\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq MM_{1}M_{2}\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{C}{(m\alpha+1)^{(n-k)\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{nMM_{1}M_{2}C}{(m\alpha+1)^{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\prod_{j=m+1}^{k+m}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{nMM_{1}M_{2}C}{(m\alpha+1)^{\alpha}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m+n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)}{\min_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\prod_{j=m+k+1}^{m+n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right)}=\beta_{m+n}\prod_{j=1}^{m+n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}+\beta_{j}\right),

where
C=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\left(\frac{(m+n-1)\alpha+1}{m\alpha+1}\right)^{n-1}
.
Since the product in the denominator is uniformly in
m
bounded from above and separated from zero, the induction step is proved.
∎

Theorem 3
.

If all coefficients
P_{j}
of (
1.1
) are polynomilals, then all
\alpha
-analytic soultions have the form
v(t^{\alpha})
where
v
is an entire function of finite order of the growth.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

By Theorem
2
, there is an entire function
v
such that
y(t)=v(t^{\alpha})
is the unique solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), (
4.1
). By Theorem
A
, there exists a set
E\subset[1,\infty)
of finite logarithmic measure such that

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{j}v(z)=(\nu(r,v)\alpha)^{j\alpha}\frac{v(z)}{z^{j}}(1+o(1)),\quad|z|\not\in E,

(4.10)

where
z
satisfies
M(|z|,v)=|v(z)|
.

Let
c_{j}z^{d_{j}}
be the leading coefficient of
P_{j}(z)
,
j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
. Substituting (
4.10
) into (
1.1
) and dividing by
v(z)
we obtain
(\nu=\nu(|z|,v))

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\frac{(\nu\alpha)^{n\alpha}}{z^{n}}+(c_{n-1}+o(1))z^{d_{n-1}}\frac{(\nu\alpha)^{(n-1)\alpha}}{z^{n-1}}+\dots

\displaystyle+(c_{1}+o(1))z^{d_{1}}\frac{(\nu\alpha)^{\alpha}}{z}+(c_{0}+o(1))z^{d_{0}}=0

or

\displaystyle{(\nu\alpha)^{n\alpha}}+(c_{n-1}+o(1))z^{d_{n-1}+1}{(\nu\alpha)^{(n-1)\alpha}}+\dots

\displaystyle+(c_{1}+o(1))z^{d_{1}+n-1}{(\nu\alpha)^{\alpha}}+(c_{0}+o(1))z^{d_{0}+n}=0.

(4.11)

Considering the term

(\nu\alpha)^{\alpha}\leq 1+\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}|c_{k}+o(1)|r^{d_{k}+n-k},\quad r\not\in E.

To finish the proof of Theorem

Lemma 7

(
[

)

Let

Theorem 4
.

Let
P_{j}
be polynomials of degree
d_{j}=\deg P_{j}
,
j\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
,
p_{0}\not\equiv 0
, and
\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\frac{d_{k}}{n-k}=\frac{d_{0}}{n}
. Then all non-trivial
\alpha
-analytic solutions

of the equation (

) has the form
y(t)=f(t^{\alpha})
,

, where
the order of an entire function
f
is
\rho(f)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{d_{0}}{n}\right)
.

Corollary
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
P
be a nontrivial polynomial of degree
d_{0}
. All non-trivial
\alpha
-analytic solutions
y
of the equation

\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}^{n}y+P(x^{\alpha})y=0

has the form
y(t)=f(t^{\alpha})
,
t\geq 0
, where
the order of an entire function
f
is
\rho(f)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{d_{0}}{n}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

First, we show that

\sigma(f)\leq\sigma_{0}:=\frac{1}{\alpha}\max_{0\leq k\leq n-1}\left(\frac{d_{k}}{n-k}+1\right).

Suppose the contrary. Then, by (
1.11
) there exists
\eta>0
and a sequence of positive numbers
(r_{n})
tending to
+\infty
such that
1<r_{m}<r_{m+1}/2
with
\nu(r_{m})\geq r_{m}^{\sigma_{0}+\eta}
. Let
F=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}[r_{m},2r_{m}]
. Clearly,
F
has infinite logarithmic measure. Moreover, for
r\in F
, we have that
r\in[r_{m},2r_{m}]
for some

. Since
\nu(r)
is non-decreasing,

\nu(r)\geq\nu(r_{m})\geq r_{m}^{\sigma_{0}+\eta}\geq\frac{r^{\sigma_{0}+\eta}}{2^{\sigma_{0}+\eta}},\quad r\in F.

(4.12)

Therefore, for

, which is of infinite logarithmic measure, and, in particular, unbounded, we have that (

) holds. Note that for every

and

the following estimates are valid

\displaystyle(c_{j}+o(1))|z|^{d_{j}+n-j}(\nu\alpha)^{\alpha j}\leq(c_{j}\alpha^{j\alpha}+o(1))r^{d_{j}+n-j+\alpha j(\sigma_{0}+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq(c_{j}\alpha^{j\alpha}+o(1))r^{\frac{\alpha(n-j)}{\alpha}\left(\frac{d_{j}}{n-j}+1\right)+\alpha j(\sigma_{0}+\varepsilon)}\leq(c_{j}\alpha^{j\alpha}+o(1))r^{\alpha\sigma_{0}\left(n+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma_{0}j}\right)}.

That is, (

(\nu\alpha)^{n\alpha}+O(r^{\alpha\sigma_{0}\left(n+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma_{0}j}\right)})=0,\quad r\in F\setminus E,

n-k+d_{k}+ks<d_{0}+ns,\quad k\in\{1,\dots,n\},

Relationship between spiritual health and happiness among elderly individuals with chronic illness
Authors: Fatemeh Zahra Gholipour Chari, Reza Norouzadeh, Nasrin Alaee, Zahra Fotokian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe significance of spiritual well-being and happiness in promoting overall health among older adults with chronic illnesses cannot be ignored. This research aimed to explore the correlation between spiritual health and happiness in elderly patients suffering from chronic disease who were admitted to the Comprehensive Health Center in Babol City in 2023.MethodsThis study employed a descriptive and cross-sectional research design. A sample of 225 elderly individuals with chronic diseases was selected through convenience sampling from four comprehensive health centers in both urban and rural areas. Data were collected using the Spiritual Well-Being Questionnaire (SWB) and the Oxford Happiness Questionnaire. Statistical analyses were performed using descriptive and inferential statistics, including the Pearson correlation coefficient, independent t-test, analysis of variance, and regression analysis, utilizing SPSS version 20.ResultsThe results indicated that the average scores for spiritual health (109.98 ± 12.58) and happiness (79.89 ± 11.82) were significantly high. A strong and statistically significant correlation was found between spiritual health and happiness (P < 0.001, r = 0.967). Regression analysis revealed that spiritual health accounts for 93.5% of the variance in happiness among the elderly.ConclusionEnhancing spiritual health in older adults is linked to higher levels of happiness. It is essential to incorporate spiritual health considerations into health interventions designed for the elderly population.

Introduction

Aging is a basic biological process that affects all living things [1]. As the population ages, we’ve seen a rise in chronic diseases like diabetes, high blood pressure, and obesity, along with mental health issues like depression, which pose serious public health challenges [2]. The decline in the quality of life for older folks dealing with chronic illnesses can be linked to various factors, such as how long they’ve been sick, the severity of complications, the financial strain of treatment, retirement-related concerns, and the impact of family and social dynamics [3]. With the elderly population growing rapidly, making sure they stay healthy and well is becoming more and more important [4]. The United Nations estimates that by 2025, the global population of older adults will hit 1.2 billion, effectively doubling in size. As people get older, The risk of chronic diseases increase significantly, with most individuals over 60 facing at least one such condition [5]. Chronic diseases require long-term management and usually don’t have clear cures, leading to financial strain and time use that’s about 20 to 30 times higher than what’s needed for acute illnesses. As a result, those living with chronic conditions carry a heavy disease burden, which negatively impacts their overall health related quality of life [6].

Spirituality involves the exploration of existential meaning, particularly in stressful life situations. Given the burden of chronic illnesses, older adults often turn to spirituality as a coping mechanism [7], which may influence their overall happiness. Similarly, explicitly connect spirituality and happiness early on to clarify their interrelated roles in aging.The core of spiritual health is how well a person can have meaningful communication with himself/herself, a higher power, other people, and the world around them [8]. This kind of engagement helps blend together the physical, mental, and social sides of health, making it easier to handle illness and life’s challenges [1]. In the realm of spiritual health, people often find a deep purpose that fits with their way of life and work. This realization, along with a sense of inner peace and a strong commitment to doing their best, fosters a transformative mindset that aims to improve overall quality of life and encourage holistic health [6]. Moreover, spiritual health is a complex idea that enhances overall well-being and quality of life through a profound love for a higher power, along with faith, trust, a commitment to ethics, and a feeling of closeness to God [9]. Research indicates that a significant portion of the elderly population specifically, 90% actively participate in religious and spiritual activities as a means to manage the stress associated with aging, with many expressing that their faith provides them solace [1]. Additionally, another study revealed that individuals engaged in religious ceremonies more than once a week experienced an average increase in lifespan of seven years. Furthermore, older adults suffering from depression demonstrated a more rapid recovery when they maintained their religious convictions [10].

Happiness plays a crucial role in promoting healthy aging [11]. It is characterized as a favorable internal state that arises from both cognitive processes and emotional assessments of one’s life [12]. This concept encompasses a feeling of life satisfaction and the experience of heightened positive emotions. The emotional aspect of happiness pertains to the enjoyment derived from life, whereas the cognitive element relates to an individual’s overall contentment with their existence. As individuals age, their physical, mental, and social changes can lead to a decline in their sense of well-being. Cultivating a positive perspective on life, fostering self-esteem, maintaining cognitive health, achieving emotional stability, and nurturing optimism are essential. Additionally, caring for oneself and others, establishing balanced social connections, steering clear of resentment, avoiding procrastination, enhancing one’s sense of achievement, ensuring quality sleep, being willing to assist others, and making sound decisions are all vital strategies for attaining happiness [13]. The significance of happiness and its influence on enhancing individuals’ personal and social experiences has garnered considerable attention from psychologists, who are exploring its correlation with various factors. Happiness as an essential aspect of well-being, encompass emotional states, personal fulfillment, and the quality of interpersonal relationships. Research indicates that older adults tend to experience greater life satisfaction when they lead happier lives, which is also linked to a reduction in mortality rates [14]. A study by Adib-Hajbagheri et al. (2015) revealed that many elderly individuals, both in community settings and nursing homes, report low levels of happiness [15]. Furthermore, Park (2019) observed in Malaysia that happiness tends to decline with advancing age, particularly among those aged 50 and above [16]. Additionally, Luchesi et al. (2018) found a significant association between unhappiness in older adults and decreased life satisfaction, as well as increased levels of depression and social anxiety [17].

Living with a chronic illness frequently causes emotional problems, such as depression, worry, irritability, and fear [18]. Nevertheless, it is important to recognize the paradox that happiness and disability can coexist; individuals may find joy even in the face of illness [14]. Numerous studies have demonstrated the beneficial impact of happiness on the health of older adults. For instance, happiness has been linked to increased longevity, and there appears to be a correlation between mental well-being, life satisfaction, and happiness among this population [19, 20]. Variations in happiness levels among older adults can be attributed to factors such as spiritual health and social support [1]. Additionally, some studies have explored the relation between spiritual health and other variables, revealing an inverse relationship between spiritual health and loneliness in older adults [13], as well as a significant positive correlation between spiritual health and life satisfaction among Iranian older adults [21]. A study indicated a significant positive correlation between spiritual health and happiness among caregivers of elderly individuals residing in nursing homes [4]. Additionally, Mortazavi and et al. (2024) demonstrated that the spiritual health of elderly individuals in Iran is generally at a commendable level. The survey further highlighted that their spiritual beliefs and trust in God surpass their levels of altruism and spiritual practices [22].

Given the mutual impact of spiritual health and happiness on mental health and the different correlations between these two variables it is important to address these two factors in order to gain insight into individuals’ mental health [23]. Due to the global increase in the senior population with chronic diseases, there is an increased necessity in improving health and overall well-being for this group; however, there is limited research on the determinants of happiness in healthy older people [1], particularly in developing countries such as Iran (a country with a growing number of the elderly population). Given the growing elderly population in Iran, particularly those living with chronic diseases, addressing health-related issues has become increasingly critical. Ensuring the well-being of this demographic group requires focused attention on their healthcare needs. Our understanding of the spiritual health of older adults remains insufficient, and it is uncertain how spiritual health may affect the happiness of this population, particularly those living with chronic illnesses. This study highlight the potential of spiritual health as a modifiable factor to improve mental health, happiness among older adults facing chronic health challenges.The hypothesis of this investigation is that spiritual health is positively correlated with happiness among older adults living with chronic illnesses.

Methods

Study population and design

This research is defined as descriptive and cross-sectional, taking place from May to August 2023 in the urban and rural comprehensive health centers of Babol City, Iran. The researcher obtained permission from Babol University of Medical Sciences to access three urban health centers and one rural health center among the available options. Visits to these designated centers were scheduled on non-official holidays, in accordance with their operational hours, which are from 7:30 am to 1:30 pm. A convenience sampling method was employed, resulting in the selection of 225 elderly participants. The sample size was determined based on the study conducted by Bastani et al. (2012), which included a 10% attrition rate in its calculations [24]. N = Z2s2/d2=(1.96)2 × (11/51)2/(0.05)2=225.

The study’s inclusion criteria targeted individuals aged 60 years and older who demonstrated the physical ability to answer questions, exhibited cognitive health with a score of 7 or above on the abbreviated mental test (AMT), and had at least one chronic illness recorded in their medical history. Cognitive health was assessed using the Abbreviated Mental Test (AMT), and only individuals scoring 7 or above [25] were included to ensure their capacity to provide informed consent. The researchers maintained strict confidentiality throughout the study, and all data were anonymized before analysis.

Data collection

Data collection utilized several instruments, including a demographic information sheet, the Spiritual Well-Being Scale (SWBS), and the Oxford Happiness Questionnaire. The demographic variables evaluated encompassed age, gender, marital status, living arrangements, number of children, educational level, employment status, smoking habits, income, duration of illness, number of chronic conditions, and participation in physical activities.

The Spiritual Well-Being Scale, created by Paloutzian and Ellison, comprises 20 items rated on a 6-point Likert scale, with responses ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree.” A religious well-being scale (RWBS) and an existential well-being scale (EWBS) each comprise ten questions, allowing for a scoring range of 10 to 60 for each individual scale. The cumulative score is derived by adding the results of both subscales, resulting in a total score that can range from a minimum of 20 to a maximum of 120. These total scores are divided into three specific categories: scores between 20 and 40 denote low spiritual health, scores from 41 to 99 represent moderate spiritual health, and scores from 100 to 120 indicate high spiritual health [26]. Allahbakhshian et al. (2010) performed a validation study on the spiritual health scale, resulting in a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 82% [27].

In a different investigation, Argyle and his team (1989) introduced the Oxford Happiness Questionnaire [28], which comprises 29 items assessed on a scale ranging from 0 to 3. In this scale, a score of 3 indicates strong agreement, 2 represents partial agreement, 1 denotes partial disagreement, and 0 reflects strong disagreement. The highest possible score on this questionnaire is 87, indicating the utmost level of happiness, while a score of zero represents a total lack of happiness. The standard scoring range for this tool is between 40 and 42 [24]. Scores are classified as follows: below 22 indicates low happiness, 22 to 44 suggests moderate happiness, 44 to 68 indicates high happiness, and 68 to 87 signifies very high happiness. It is crucial to recognize that responses to items 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, and 28 necessitate reverse. The Oxford questionnaire was translated into Persian by Alipour and Noor Bala and obtained a reliability of 0.79 [29].

The Abbreviated Mental Test (AMT) was first developed by Hodkinson in 1972 and consists of 10 questions that assess concentration, short and long term memory. It is useful for screening for cognitive problems such as delirium and dementia in the elderly [30].

Statistical analyses

Statistics used included mean and standard deviation, frequencies and percentages, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, and linear regression. Given the normal distribution of the data, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, Pearson’s correlation, independent t-test and one-way ANOVA were implemented. A significance level of less than 0.05 was set for the research. The data analysis was performed using SPSS version 20.

All procedures adhered to the ethical standards outlined in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Prior to participation, all eligible individuals were provided with a detailed explanation of the study’s objectives, procedures, and voluntary nature. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant after they were ensured that their responses would remain confidential and that they could withdraw from the study at any time without consequences. Participants were also informed that the study involved completing questionnaires and posed no physical risks or complications.

Results

Among the participants, 60.4% (n = 136) were in the age range of 60–65 years. Discussion

Research shows that older adults dealing with chronic illnesses tend to have higher levels of spiritual well-being and happiness, with a strong correlation between the two variables. In the current study, it was found that 91.1% of the elderly population reported experiencing a high level of happiness. The results of a research showed that happiness is affected by spirituality, in other words, spirituality contributes as a protective factor in increasing the happiness of the elderly [31]. Shah and et al. found 79/2% of elderly Malaysians were happy; they stated that receiving emotional support and active social engagement amongst the elderly population can enhance their happiness level [32]. In contrast, Adib-Hajbaghery et al. (2015) showed that most elderly individuals living in nursing homes had low levels of happiness, likely due to a lack of social support and limited communication with their families [20].

This finding aligns with the results of Zarepour Moghadami and Norouzadeh (2019), who similarly observed that elderly diabetic patients exhibited elevated levels of spiritual well-being [33]. Likewise, a study by Shirbaigy et al. (2017) found that both active and inactive older adults showed significant spiritual health [34]. A study in the United States revealed that 90% of older adults look for comfort in religion and spirituality to cope with the challenges of ageing. Moreover, those who participated in religious activities at least once a week were more likely to exercise, quit smoking, feel satisfied with life, manage challenges effectively, and stick to their medication regimens [35].

The findings indicate that there is no statistically significant difference in spiritual health and happiness levels based on age or the presence of chronic diseases. Chabok et al. (2017) propose that spiritual health varies among different older age groups, which contradicts the assertion that spiritual health and happiness remain constant across the elderly population [36]. Furthermore, Park and Joshanloo (2021) highlight that aging, particularly beyond the age of 50, is associated with a significant decline in overall subjective happiness levels [16]. Zareipour et al. (2017) demonstrated that elderly women exhibit higher levels of spiritual health compared to men, which is consistent with the findings of the present study [4]. The findings of this study are consistent with previous research indicating that married elderly individuals demonstrate higher levels of spiritual health compared to their unmarried counterparts. Furthermore, participants with adequate family income reported significantly better spiritual well-being, while those with lower educational attainment exhibited substantially reduced spiritual health scores [36]. These results suggest that marital status, socioeconomic factors, and educational background may serve as important determinants of spiritual health in older adult populations. Consistent with the current study’s findings, Stepto and Fancourt (2019) show that married elderly individuals report higher levels of happiness [35]. Shirbighi et al. (2020) also stated that physically and mentally active elderly people have higher spiritual health scores [34]. Park and Joshanloo (2021) show that happiness in the elderly is related to high income in the family, being employed, having a high education, and female gender [16].Also, Zarepour Moghadami and Norouzadeh (2019) showed that sufficient financial resources are significantly associated with higher levels of subjective well-being in older adults [33]. Ezati et al. (2023) found that happiness levels were higher among married elderly individuals and among men [1]. In accordance with these findings, researchers concluded that health-related factors and the absence of disease not only influence self-reported happiness but are also reciprocally influenced by happiness levels [37]. Furthermore, studies investigating the prevalence of successful aging in the United States found that being male and having higher social and economic status were significantly associated with both successful aging outcomes and greater happiness levels [38]. Previous research shows that built environment and household structure differently affect seniors’ happiness through physiological and social factors like gender roles, cultural norms, and household labor division [39]. In the present study, the majority of participants were non-smokers who demonstrated positive spiritual health and higher happiness levels. This observation aligns with the findings of Mirzaei et al. (2017), which indicated that older adults who do not smoke tend to experience a better quality of life and overall health [40]. The most striking point of the different findings is gender and happiness. Andrade et al. (2018) show that older men are happier than women [41], while Park and Joshanloo (2021) show the opposite [16]. This discrepancy may be attributed to methodological differences across studies, including variations in sample selection, happiness measurement tools, and cultural contexts. The definition and measurement of happiness varies culturally, and different populations with diverse socioeconomic backgrounds were likely studied. Additionally, cultural differences in gender roles may influence outcomes. In some societies men may have greater social status or economic security in old age, while in others women may benefit from stronger social support networks or different happiness expectations. Park and Joshanloo (2021) found that women reported higher happiness levels, which was associated with greater income and education. This suggests that socioeconomic factors may explain the relationship between gender and happiness [16].

This study was geographically constrained to Babol City, where participants were recruited from comprehensive health centers. This sampling approach limited the inclusion of elderly individuals from the broader city population and surrounding rural areas, thereby restricting the generalizability of findings. A key limitation was the use of self-reported data, which may have been influenced by participants’ reluctance to provide honest responses or tendency to answer in socially desirable ways, potentially affecting result accuracy. To address this limitation, the researcher provided participants with clear instructions and emphasized the importance of providing honest and accurate responses throughout the data collection process. Moreover, the cross-sectional study design inherently precludes the establishment of causal inferences between variables. Therefore, subsequent studies are recommended to evaluate the impact of spiritual health interventions on happiness outcomes in older adults living with chronic diseases. Future research should examine spiritual health and happiness in elderly individuals from diverse ethnic backgrounds to improve the generalizability of findings.

Conclusions

The present study revealed that elderly participants with chronic diseases exhibited favorable levels of both spiritual health and happiness, with a positive correlation observed between enhanced spiritual health and increased happiness. The findings offer valuable insights for healthcare professionals, including health planners, nurses, and hospital administrators. It is recommended that healthcare systems incorporate spiritual care protocols into standard medical practice for elderly patients with chronic conditions through professional counseling services, community-based spiritual activities, and educational programs focused on stress-reduction interventions such as mindfulness and meditation practices. Such approaches may optimize holistic patient care and improve quality of life outcomes for elderly populations.

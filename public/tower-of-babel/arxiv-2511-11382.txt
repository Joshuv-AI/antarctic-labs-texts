Self-gravitating baryonic tubes supported by π \pi - and ω \omega -mesons and its flat limit
Gonzalo Barriga, Carla Henríquez Baez, Leonardo Sanhueza, Aldo Vera

Abstract
In this paper, we construct self-gravitating topological solitons in the S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Einstein non-linear sigma model coupled to ω \omega -vector-mesons in D = 4 D=4 space-time dimensions. These solutions represent tube-like configurations free of curvature singularities and possess a non-vanishing topological charge identified as the baryon number. We show that, using the maximal embedding Ansatz of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) into S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) in the exponential representation, the tubes can be constructed for arbitrary values of the flavor number, N N , with the topological charge being proportional to this number. The flat limit of this solution, which corresponds to an array of baryonic tubes in a finite volume, is analyzed in detail. Remarkably, although the energy of the solitons at a finite volume is an increasing function of N N , the binding energy decreases as the number of flavors present in the theory increases, reaffirming that the inclusion of more than two flavors improves the predictions of the model.



I
Introduction

The non-linear sigma model (NLSM) is one of the most relevant effective field theories, first introduced in particle physics to describe pion dynamics at low energies
Nair
,
ChPT
. This theory has a wide range of applications that range from condensed matter physics to string theory
MantonBook
,
BalaBook
. Although phenomenologically and theoretically relevant, T. Skyrme realized that for the NLSM to have energetically stable topological soliton solutions, the action of the model must be supplemented with higher-order derivative terms
skyr
. This is in agreement with Derrick’s scaling argument
D
, and the simplest option is to add to the NLSM action the so-called Skyrme term. However, there also exist other options to circumvent Derrick’s argument, such as coupling the NLSM to spin-1 matter fields or considering other geometries that go beyond spherical symmetry. Using any of these mechanisms, it is in principle possible to obtain a non-zero topological charge, which, in the context of chiral effective field theories of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), corresponds to the baryon number
Witten1
,
Witten2
(see also Refs.
Bala1
and
Bala2
).

Now, although the NLSM and the Skyrme model provide an accurate description of the static properties and interacting states of hadrons
Hooft
-
Weinberg:1990rz
, experimental data reveal that these theories lack accuracy in certain relevant predictions. This difference becomes significant in predicting the nuclear binding energy. In fact, experimental data show that the repulsive energy between baryons is smaller than that predicted by the chiral models. Fortunately, there exists a way to resolve this problem. Hadrons can be stabilized, and the binding energy can be reduced by introducing
\omega
-vector-mesons into the theory. This fact was first noticed by Adkins and Nappi in Ref.
Adkins1
, where the authors report that the presence of the
\omega
-mesons stabilizes the solitons and reduces the binding energy without the need to consider the Skyrme term (see
Meissner1
-
Bijnens:1997ni
, and references therein). Subsequently, these results were expanded to more general effective models, emphasizing the relevance of including vector mesons
Park:2003sd
-
Leask:2024ith
.

Recently, in Ref.
Barriga:2023jam
, analytical solutions of the
SU(2)-
NLSM coupled to
\omega
-mesons were constructed (see also Ref.
Barriga:2022izc
). These solutions represent ordered arrays of baryonic tubes and layers with non-trivial topological charge, where the presence of
\omega
-mesons reduces the repulsive energy between baryons and leads to a kind of nuclear linguine phase in a finite volume. These solutions were constructed using techniques previously developed in the context of the Skyrme model
crystal1
-
Canfora:2023zmt
.

Usually, effective field theories are studied in the two-flavor case—that is, when the internal global symmetry group is
SU(2)
—in order to simplify the set of coupled non-linear differential equations. Indeed, in the general case, i.e., when the internal symmetry group is
SU(N)
, the system involves
(N^{2}-1)
coupled partial differential equations, which makes it very difficult to extract relevant information from configurations with
N
greater than two. In this context, a useful strategy is to use the maximal embedding Ansatz of
SU(2)
into
SU(N)
. It was introduced in Refs.
Bertini:2005rc
,
Cacciatori:2012qi
, and it has the advantage of being able to reduce the degrees of freedom of the matter field, allowing us to explore exactly what the role that the flavor number plays in the observables of the model. For instance, this formalism has recently been used to study phase transitions between nuclear pasta states in the context of the Skyrme model
SU(N)1
-
CacciatoriScotti
.

Now, solutions from the effective chiral theories mentioned above can also be promoted to self-gravitating configurations by coupling the corresponding matter fields to general relativity. Indeed, relevant exact solutions in these models describing black holes, gravitating solitons, and extended objects have been constructed
CanforaMaeda
-
Canfora:2025roy
.

In this article, we will construct solutions that describe self-gravitating solitons in the Einstein-NLSM coupled to
\omega
-mesons. These solutions describe regular baryonic tubes and generalize the solutions presented in Ref.
Barriga:2023jam
in flat space-time. Furthermore, using the maximal embedding Ansatz in the exponential representation, we will show that the solutions are robust to including an arbitrary number of flavors. To achieve this, we will rely on the following articles:
gravtube1
and
gravtube2
, where the authors managed to construct self-gravitating superconducting tubes and parallel waves in the
SU(2)
Einstein-NLSM. Finally, we will study the flat limit of these solutions, showing that the binding energy is a decreasing function of the flavor number, emphasizing that the predictions of the model are improved when we consider more than two flavors.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section II, we introduce the
SU(N)
Einstein-NLSM coupled to
\omega
-mesons. In Section III, we construct analytical solutions describing self-gravitating tubes and discuss their main physical properties. In section IV, we show the flat limit of our solutions and how the inclusion of the
\omega
-mesons together with a flavor number greater than two allows for a reduction of the binding energy. Finally, in Section V, we present the conclusions and perspectives.

II
General relativity in the presence of
\pi
- and
\omega
-mesons

As a starting point, we consider the
SU(N)
Einstein-NLSM coupled to
\omega
-vector-mesons, which is described by the following action principle

\displaystyle I[g,U,\omega]=\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2\kappa}+\frac{K}{4}\operatorname{Tr}\left[L^{\mu}L_{\mu}\right]-\frac{1}{4}S_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}M_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}-\gamma\rho_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}\right)\ ,

(1)

\displaystyle U(x)\in SU(N)\ ,\quad L_{\mu}=L_{\mu}^{j}t_{j}=U^{-1}\nabla_{\mu}U\ ,\quad S_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\omega_{\mu}\ .

Here,
\mathcal{R}
is the Ricci scalar,
U(x)
is the pionic complex scalar field,
\omega_{\mu}
is a fourth-vector representing the
\omega
-mesons potential, and

is the topological current defined in Eq. (
5

\kappa=8\pi G
, where
G
is the gravitational constant,

M_{\omega}
is the mass of the
\omega

K
and
\gamma
are experimentally fixed positive constants.
We use the convention where
c=\hbar=1

In our formulation, we introduce vector mesons through the action and pseudoscalar mesons through the extension of the symmetry group; thus baryons emerge due to the nonlinear interaction between mesons.

The complete set of field equations of the system is obtained by varying the action with respect to the fields
U
,
\omega_{\mu}
and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}L^{\mu}-\frac{6\gamma}{K}\nabla_{\nu}(\epsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\omega_{\mu}L_{\lambda}L_{\rho})=0\ ,

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu\nu}-M_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{\nu}=\gamma\rho^{\nu}\ ,

(3)

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}\ .

(4)

The
\omega

\rho^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(U^{-1}\partial_{\nu}U\right)\left(U^{-1}\partial_{\lambda}U\right)\left(U^{-1}\partial_{\rho}U\right)\right]\ ,

(5)

being

This tensor is written in terms of the Levi-Civita symbol,

The integral over a space-like hyper-surface of the topological current leads to the topological charge, which is interpreted as the baryon number of the system

Here

On the other hand, the energy-momentum tensor of the theory, obtained by varying Eq. (

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{K}{2}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left[L_{\mu}L_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}L^{\alpha}L_{\alpha}\right]+S_{\mu\alpha}S_{\nu}{}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4}S_{\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}

(7)

\displaystyle+M_{\omega}^{2}\left(\omega_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}\right)+\gamma\left(\rho_{\mu}\omega_{\nu}+\rho_{\nu}\omega_{\mu}-g_{\mu\nu}\rho_{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}\right)\ .

III
Self-gravitating hadronic tubes

Self-gravitating hadronic tubes can be constructed as solutions of the Einstein-NLSM by considering a pionic matter field that leads to field configurations with non-vanishing topological charge in a space-time described by the Weyl-Lewis-Papapetrou (WLP) metric. This fact was first noticed in Ref.
gravtube1
(see also Ref.
gravtube2
) in the context of the
SU(2)
Einstein-NLSM coupled to Maxwell electrodynamics. In that paper, the pionic matter field is the one that, in flat space-time, leads to an array of baryonic tubes. The matter field in Ref.
gravtube1
was proven to be compatible with the WLP metric in the sense that the matter equations naturally decouple from the Einstein equations. Moreover, it was shown that by choosing an appropriate Ansatz for the Maxwell potential, the NLSM equations reduce to the same first-order equations as in the flat space-time case in Refs.
crystal1
and
crystal2
. Here we use a similar strategy, choosing a suitable Ansatz for the
\omega
-meson potential that allows the equations to decouple. We will show that a metric of the WLP family in null coordinates is compatible with our matter fields.

III.1
The Ansatz

In the study of the NLSM, the case of two flavors (that is,
N=2
) is usually considered in the literature, mainly due to its simplicity. However, in the general case
U(x)\in SU(N)
, it is necessary to deal with a system of
(N^{2}-1)
coupled non-linear differential equations. Naturally, this is, in general, a very difficult task to achieve unless an appropriate Ansatz is used that allows, first, reducing the number of degrees of freedom of the system and, second, does not simply reduce to the two-flavor case.

Here, in order to construct solutions where the parameter
N
appears explicitly in the relevant physical quantities, we will use the maximal embedding Ansatz of
SU(2)
into
SU(N)
in the exponential representation, following the conventions introduced in Refs.
SU(N)1
and
SU(N)2
in the context of the Skyrme model.

In the exponential representation, the pionic field can be written as

\displaystyle U=e^{\alpha(x^{\mu})(\vec{n}\,\cdot\,\vec{T})}\ ,

(8)

\displaystyle\vec{n}=\left(\sin\Theta\sin\Phi,\sin\Theta\cos\Phi,\cos\Theta\right)\ .

In the maximal embedding Ansatz introduced in Refs.
Bertini:2005rc
,
Cacciatori:2012qi
, the functions
\alpha(x^{\mu})
,
\Theta(x^{\mu})
, and
\Phi(x^{\mu})
are the three degrees of freedom of the pionic field, with
\alpha
, in this context, the soliton profile. The
T_{i}
matrices (with
i=1,2,3
) are the generators of a
3
-dimensional sub-algebra of
\mathfrak{su}(N)
, which are explicitly given by

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\sqrt{(j-1)(N-j+1)}(E_{j-1,j}+E_{j,j-1})\ ,

(9)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\sqrt{(j-1)(N-j+1)}(E_{j-1,j}-E_{j,j-1})\ ,

(10)

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\frac{N+1}{2}-j)E_{j,j}\ ,

(11)

where
(E_{i,j})_{mn}=\delta_{im}\delta_{jn}
, and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. The matrices in Eqs. (
9
), (
10
) and (
11
) satisfy the following commutation relation:

\left[T_{a},T_{b}\right]\ =\ \epsilon_{abc}T_{c}\ .

(12)

The advantage of this Ansatz is that it allows one to extract important information about how the solutions behave for an arbitrary number of flavors without having to increase the number of degrees of freedom, and therefore the number of equations to be solved, as we will see below.

A clear way to prove that configurations constructed using this formalism are non-trivial embeddings of
SU(2)
into
SU(N)
is by computing the trace of the generators. This quantity turns out to be

\text{Tr}\left(T_{b}T_{c}\right)\ =\ -\frac{1}{2}\bar{N}\delta_{bc}\ ,\qquad\bar{N}=\frac{N\left(N^{2}-1\right)}{6}\ .

(13)

We see that the trace depends explicitly on the
N
parameter, and the result is proportional to the tetrahedral numbers. This implies that different values of the flavor number lead to different matter fields and, therefore, different solutions (labeled by a different spin, as can be seen in Refs.
Bertini:2005rc
-
CacciatoriScotti
). We will see below that this fact is fundamental because both the energy density and the topological charge depend on the number
\bar{N}
defined in Eq. (
13
).

Following the steps on the construction of hadronic tubes in flat space-time in Refs.
crystal1
and
crystal2
, we choose the pionic field in Eq. (
8
) with the following form for the degrees of freedom:

\alpha(x^{\mu})=2\alpha(X)\ ,\qquad\Theta(x^{\mu})=q\theta\ ,\qquad\Phi(x^{\mu})=pu\ ,

(14)

being
p
and
q
real numbers.

The Ansatz in Eq. (
14
) will be defined on a WLP metric, as this is a compatible space-time metric in the sense discussed above (see Ref.
gravtube1
),

ds^{2}=-(1+H)\,du^{2}\,-dudv+e^{-2R}\left(dX^{2}+d\theta^{2}\right)\ .

(15)

In Eq. (
15
), the coordinate dependence of the functions to be found is
H=H(X,\theta)
and
R=R(X)
. Here we are using null coordinates;

u=t-z\ ,\qquad v=t+z\ ,

(16)

and the range of the coordinates goes as
t\in\left(-\infty,\infty\right)
,

,

and

. It is important to note that the

gravtube1

gravtube2

For the
\omega
-mesons potential, we use

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}=(S,0,0,0)\ ,

(17)

where
S=S(X,\theta)
. The above expression for the vector-mesons potential is exactly what allows the NLSM equations to be decoupled from those of the
\omega
-mesons. Furthermore, as we will see below, the
\omega
-mesons equations can be easily solved because the soliton profile can be obtained analytically.

Solving the system

Using the Ansatz presented in Eqs. (

Second, the

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\partial^{2}_{X}\mathcal{O}+\partial^{2}_{\theta}\mathcal{O}

III.3
Characterization of the solutions: Regularity, energy density and baryon charge

It is possible to check the regularity of the solutions presented above by computing the curvature invariants

,
Harvey

gravtube1

R

\mathcal{R}=2e^{2R}\partial_{X}^{2}R\ .

As an example, the Kretschmann scalar, the square of the Ricci tensor, and the Weyl tensor invariants are the following:

\mathcal{K}\equiv R^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}{}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\mathcal{R}^{2}\ ,\quad R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}\ ,\qquad C_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}C^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}=\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{R}^{2}\ .

IV
The flat limit

In this section, we discuss the flat limit of the above solutions by taking the limit

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}=0\ ,

\displaystyle\Delta H=0\ ,

(33)

K\kappa\bar{N}\,[(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}-q^{2}\sin\left(\alpha\right)^{2}]=0\ .

Now, Eqs. (

) and (

) lead directly to

R(X)=C_{2}X+C_{3}\ ,\qquad H=0\ .

(34)

At this point, there are two possible paths to follow in the study of the flat limit, and these are determined by the cases
C_{2}=0
and
C_{2}\neq 0
.

When
C_{2}
is nonzero, the geometry is reduced to that of a cylinder in which, to avoid the appearance of a conic singularity, the integration constant must necessarily be
C_{2}=-1
. However, this is not the only issue to deal with. One can notice that in this case, the solution becomes singular because the energy density diverges at the origin. The only possible option in this scenario would be to take
q=0
to avoid the divergence, but this condition is not compatible with the constraints that emerge for
q
in the topological charge calculation presented in the previous section. Thus, the option to consider in the study of the flat limit is that with
C_{2}=0
. A detailed analysis of this procedure can be found in Ref.
gravtube1
, Sec. VI.

Indeed, considering the case where
C_{2}=0
we reach the “genuine flat-limit” of these solutions which, as expected, turns out to be regular. These configurations live in a flat space-time metric

ds^{2}=-d\tilde{t}^{2}+d\tilde{z}^{2}+dX^{2}+d\theta^{2}\ ,

(35)

where
\tilde{t}=\sqrt{2}t-\sqrt{1/2}z
, and
\tilde{z}=\sqrt{1/2}z
. These configurations describe baryonic tubes confined in a finite volume. In these coordinates, the
\omega
-mesons potential and the degrees of freedom of the pionic field take the form

\displaystyle\alpha(x^{\mu})=2\alpha(x)\ ,\quad\Theta(x^{\mu})=qy\ ,\quad\Phi(x^{\mu})=p(t-z)\ ,

(36)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}=(-S,0,0,S)\ ,\qquad S=S(x,y)\ .

(37)

This kind of solution was obtained and studied in detail in Refs.
Barriga:2022izc
and
Barriga:2023jam
(see also Refs.
crystal1
and
crystal2

Now, the present solution generalizes those reported in Refs.
Barriga:2022izc
and
Barriga:2023jam
by including arbitrary flavors. In fact, the parameter
N
appears explicitly in the equation for

(38)

(where we have chosen, without loss of generality,
C_{3}=0

\varepsilon=(\nabla S)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}K\bar{N}\{(q^{2}+2p^{2}\sin^{2}(q\theta))\sin^{2}(\alpha)+(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}\}+24\gamma\bar{N}pq\sin(q\theta)\sin^{2}(\alpha)S\alpha^{\prime}+M_{\omega}^{2}S^{2}=0\ .

V
Conclusions and perspectives

In this paper, we have shown that the Einstein-NLSM in
D=4
space-time dimensions coupled to
\omega
-mesons admits topological soliton solutions describing self-gravitating baryon tubes. Furthermore, by using the maximal embedding Ansatz of
SU(2)
into
SU(N)
in the exponential representation, the solutions have been proven to be robust to an arbitrary number of flavors. These baryon tubes do not present singularities, and their topological charge is proportional to the number of flavors in the theory. The flat limit of these solutions is particularly interesting, since the complete system of equations reduces to a single equation for the
\omega
-mesons potential, which can be solved in terms of Green functions. These new solutions generalize those previously found in Refs.
Barriga:2022izc
and
Barriga:2023jam
for the two-flavor case. As is well known, the coupling of
\omega
-mesons to the NLSM allows for the reduction of the repulsion energy between baryons, resulting in theoretical estimates that are closer to experimental data. Here, we have demonstrated this phenomenon by comparing the energy of hadron tubes with and without the presence of
\omega
-mesons. We have also seen that as the number of flavors in the theory increases, the binding energy decreases, indicating that models with higher flavor number provide more accurate and physically realistic predictions.

This study leaves open promising directions for further investigation. For example, the inclusion of large
N_{c}
corrections and the coupling of the theory to Maxwell electrodynamics. It will also be important to analyze how these solutions are modified in the presence of a non-zero cosmological constant. Additionally, it is known that the families of WLP solutions are related to the Kasner parameter
Peraza:2025
; it would be interesting to explore how the number of flavors could influence this parameter.

Appendix A
About the radial coordinate

As we have pointed out previously, the coordinate
X
in Eq. (
15
) is not strictly a radial coordinate, since it can, in principle, take both positive and negative values. An appropriate radial coordinate can now be defined as follows:

r(X)=\int_{-\infty}^{r}e^{-R(y)}dy=\int_{-\infty}^{r}\frac{e^{-C2y-C_{3}}}{\left(\cosh(qy+C_{1})\right)^{K\kappa}}dy\ ,

(41)

where
R(y)
is given by the solution presented in Eq. (
26
).
One of the advantages of the function defined above is that
r(X)
increases as
X
increases; this is

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{dX}=\frac{e^{-C_{2}X-C_{3}}}{\left(\cosh(qX+C_{1})\right)^{K\kappa}}>0\ .

After we perform the integration of the expression on (
41
), we see that to have
r
well-defined, that is, to be positive, it is necessary to impose the following condition on the integration constant

\displaystyle C_{2}<\kappa KN|q|\ .

When the above condition is considered, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{X\rightarrow-\infty}r(X)=0\ ,

and therefore

\displaystyle-\infty<X<\infty\Rightarrow 0<r<\infty\ .

Under that transformation, the metric takes the form

ds^{2}=-\left(1+H\right)du^{2}-dudv+dr^{2}+e^{-2\tilde{R}}d\theta^{2}\,\ ,

(42)

where the function becomes
\tilde{R}(r)=R\left(X(r)\right)
.

It is important to determine whether our solution exhibits an angular deficit. Since the transverse section of our metric is identical to one used in Ref.
gravtube1
, it is possible to see that for a suitable value of the constant
C_{3}
in the function
\tilde{R}(r)
, the solution is free of angular deficit when
r
is close to zero, but develops an angular deficit at large
r
. Thus, our solution behaves asymptotically as a cosmic string.

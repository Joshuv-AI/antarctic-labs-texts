Creation of chaos for interacting Brownian particles
Armand Bernou, Mitia Duerinckx, Matthieu Ménard

Abstract
. We consider a system of N N Brownian particles, with or without inertia, interacting in the mean-field regime via a weak, smooth, long-range potential, and starting initially from an arbitrary exchangeable N N -particle distribution. In this model framework, we establish a fine version of the so-called creation-of-chaos phenomenon: in weak norms, the mean-field approximation for a typical particle is shown to hold with an accuracy O ⁡ ( N − 1 ) O(N^{-1}) up to an error due solely to initial pair correlations, which is damped exponentially over time. Corresponding higher-order results are also derived in the form of higher-order correlation estimates. The approach is new and easily adaptable: we start from suboptimal correlation estimates obtained from an elementary use of Itô’s calculus on moments of the empirical measure, together with ergodic properties of the mean-field dynamics, and these bounds are then made optimal after combination with PDE estimates on the BBKY hierarchy. MSC-class: Primary 60K35; Secondary 35Q70, 82C22, 82C31, 35Q84.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
General overview

We consider the Langevin dynamics for a system of
N
Brownian particles with mean-field interactions, moving in a confining potential in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, as described by the following system of coupled SDEs: for
1\leq i\leq N,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{d}X^{i,N}_{t}=V^{i,N}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,&\\[5.69054pt]
\mathrm{d}V^{i,N}_{t}=-\tfrac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\nabla W(X^{i,N}_{t}-X_{t}^{j,N})\,\mathrm{d}t-\tfrac{\beta}{2}V^{i,N}_{t}\mathrm{d}t-\nabla A(X^{i,N}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{i},&\quad t\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

where
\{Z^{i,N}:=(X^{i,N},V^{i,N})\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
is the set of positions and velocities of the particles in the phase space
\mathbb{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a long-range interaction potential with the action-reaction condition
W(x)=W(-x)
, where
A
is a uniformly convex confining potential, where
\{B^{i}\}_{i}
are i.i.d.
d
-dimensional Brownian motions, and where
\kappa,\beta>0
are given constants.
For simplicity, we choose the confining potential
A
to be quadratic,

(1.3)

A(x)\,:=\,\tfrac{1}{2}a|x|^{2},\qquad\text{for some $a>0$},

although perturbations can be considered as well (see Section
1.3
).
Turning to a statistical description of the system, in terms of a probability density
F^{N}
on the
N
-particle phase space
\mathbb{X}^{N}
, the Langevin dynamics (
1.1
) is equivalent to the corresponding Liouville equation

(1.4)

\partial_{t}F^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x_{i}}F^{N}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{v_{i}}((\nabla_{v_{i}}+\beta v_{i})F^{N})\\[-8.53581pt]
+\tfrac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\nabla W(x_{i}-x_{j})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}F^{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla A(x_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}F^{N},\qquad t\geq 0.

In this contribution, we investigate the behavior of the system in the regime of a large number
N\gg 1
of particles in the general setting of initial data
\{Z^{i,N}|_{t=0}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
that are only assumed to be exchangeable (that is, their law
F^{N}|_{t=0}
is invariant under permutation of the particles), but that may be strongly correlated.
Let us recall two standard types of results for the system:

—

Propagation of chaos:

If particles have weak correlations initially, then propagation of chaos is expected to hold, which means that only little correlations can build over time. As a consequence, the distribution of a typical particle is approximated by the solution
\mu
of the mean-field Vlasov–Fokker–Planck equation

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\mu+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mu=\tfrac{1}{2}{\operatorname{div}}_{v}((\nabla_{v}+\beta v)\mu)+(\nabla A+\kappa\nabla W\ast\mu)\cdot\nabla_{v}\mu,\qquad t\geq 0,\\[2.84526pt]
\mu|_{t=0}=\mu_{\circ},\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{\circ}
stands for the initial single-particle distribution. Such a result has been established in various settings and we refer e.g. to the recent work
[
5
,
4
]
in case of singular interactions.
More precisely, for all
1\leq m\leq N
, denoting by
F^{N,m}
the distribution of
m
typical particles in the system, as given by the
m
-th marginal of
F^{N}
,

F^{N,m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{N-m}}F^{N}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\,\mathrm{d}z_{m+1}\ldots\mathrm{d}z_{N},\qquad 1\leq m\leq N,

we find that
F^{N,m}
converges to
\mu^{\otimes m}
as
N\uparrow\infty
for any fixed
m
.
Beyond this qualitative convergence, an optimal
O(N^{-1})
error estimate for the convergence of marginals was first obtained in
[
10
]
for smooth interactions, and later in
[
15
]
for bounded non-smooth interactions in a better metric: if correlations vanish initially, then there holds

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|F^{N,m}_{t}-\mu^{\otimes m}_{t}\|_{TV}\,\leq\,Ce^{Ct}mN^{-1},\qquad\text{for all $1\leq m\leq N$ and $t\geq 0$}.

If in addition the intensity
\kappa
of interaction forces is small enough, which ensures the uniqueness of the mean-field equilibrium, then this error estimate (
1.6
) holds uniformly in time without the exponential factor
[
16
,
1
]
(see also previous work using coupling methods but only producing a suboptimal convergence rate
[
3
,
13
]
).

—

Relaxation to equilibrium:

If interaction forces are weak enough, even if particles are strongly correlated initially, their distribution is known to relax to Gibbs equilibrium on long times.
For Lipschitz forces, the following uniform-in-
N
exponential estimate was established in
[
3
]
,

\mathcal{W}_{2}(F_{t}^{N,m},M^{N,m})\,\leq\,C_{m}e^{-\frac{1}{C}t},\qquad\text{for all $1\leq m\leq N$ and $t\geq 0$},

where
M^{N,m}
stands for the
m
-th marginal of the
N
-particle Gibbs equilibrium
M^{N}
.
As the latter exhibits weak correlations, this entails in particular that initial correlations between the particles must be exponentially damped over time.

Both uniform-in-time propagation of chaos and convergence to Gibbs equilibrium rely essentially on the same phenomenon: due to the diffusion, correlations cannot accumulate too much over time and must somehow be damped.
This leads to the natural related question of
creation of chaos
as first raised by Del Moral and Tugaut
[
7
]
(see also “generation of chaos” in
[
17
,
19
]
):
for general exchangeable initial data, is it possible to show that the mean-field approximation holds to order
O(N^{-1})
up to an error coming from initial correlations, which is damped exponentially over time?

Such a result can in fact be deduced immediately from the coupling argument of
[
3
]
, but only with a suboptimal convergence rate
O(N^{-1/2})
: more precisely, for weak Lipschitz forces,
it follows from
[
3
, Theorem 2 and Remark 12]
that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(F^{N,m}_{t},\mu_{t}^{\otimes m})\leq C_{m}\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+C_{m}\,e^{-\frac{1}{C}t}\,\mathcal{W}_{2}(F^{N,m}_{0},\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes m}),\quad\hbox{ for all }1\leq m\leq N\hbox{ and }t\geq 0.

Our first objective is to achieve the optimal rate
O(N^{-1})
in this creation-of-chaos result, which to our knowledge is not possible via the same coupling methods. More precisely, we look for bounds of the following form, in some suitable metric,

(1.8)

\|F_{t}^{N,m}-\mu_{t}^{\otimes m}\|\,\leq\,C_{m}N^{-1}+C_{m}e^{-\frac{1}{C}t}\|F_{\circ}^{N,m}-\mu_{\circ}^{\otimes m}\|,\quad\text{for all $1\leq m\leq N$ and $t\geq 0$}.

In the sequel, we establish estimates of this kind for weak smooth interactions, in negative Sobolev norms instead of Wasserstein distance; see Theorem
1.1
below.

While virtually any proof of uniform-in-time propagation of chaos comes with some associated creation-of-chaos result showing the damping of initial correlations, we emphasize that clean estimates of the form (
1.8
) are more difficult to establish. In particular, there has been a lot of recent progress based on hierarchical approaches to uniform-in-time propagation of chaos
[
15
,
5
,
16
,
1
,
4
]
,
starting from the BBGKY hierarchy of equations for marginals,
but such methods require to deal with information on the
whole
hierarchy
\{F^{N,m}-\mu^{\otimes m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
at once and can thus a priori not be used to obtain estimates of the form (
1.8
) for any
fixed

m
. For overdamped systems (see Section
1.3.1
below), modulated energy methods can also be used to establish creation of chaos, see
[
19
]
, but they require much stronger initial information in the form of the relative entropy of the
N
-particle distribution with respect to a tensorized state, which again cannot lead to clean estimates of the form (
1.8
) for marginals.

Next to this question on the creation and propagation of chaos, we further refine our analysis to corresponding correlation estimates.
Many-particle correlation functions provide finer information on the propagation of chaos in the system and we shall see that creation of chaos holds more generally at the level of arbitrary high-order correlations.
Recall that the two-particle correlation function is defined as

G^{N,2}\,:=\,F^{N,2}-(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 2},

which captures the defect to propagation of chaos (
1.6
) at the level of two-particle statistics.
This quantity alone does not allow to reconstruct the full particle density
F^{N}
:
we define higher-order correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{2\leq m\leq N}
as suitable polynomial combinations of marginals of
F^{N}
in such a way that the full particle distribution
F^{N}
is recovered in form of a cluster expansion,

(1.9)

F^{N}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket N\rrbracket}\prod_{B\in\pi}G^{N,\sharp B}(z_{B}),

where
\pi
runs through the list of all partitions of the index set
\llbracket N\rrbracket:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, where
B
runs through the list of blocks of the partition
\pi
, where
\sharp B
is the cardinality of
B
, where for
B=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}\}\subset\llbracket N\rrbracket
we write
z_{B}=(z_{i_{1}},\ldots,z_{i_{l}})
, and where we have set
G^{N,1}:=F^{N,1}
. As is easily checked, correlation functions are fully determined by prescribing (
1.9
) together with the requirement
\int_{\mathbb{X}}G^{N,m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\,\mathrm{d}z_{l}=0
for
2\leq m\leq N
and
1\leq l\leq m
.
If initial correlations vanish, standard propagation of chaos leads to
G^{N,2}_{t}=O(N^{-1})
,
and a formal analysis of the BBGKY hierarchy further leads to expect

(1.10)

G_{t}^{N,m}\,=\,O(N^{1-m}),\qquad 2\leq m\leq N.

This is referred to as
higher-order propagation of chaos
and is a key tool to describe deviations from mean-field theory, see e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and in particular
[
2
, Corollary 1.3]
for the derivation of the so-called Bogolyubov correction to mean field. Such estimates have been obtained in several settings with an exponential time growth: non-Brownian particle systems with smooth interactions were first addressed in
[
10
]
, while in
[
14
]
the overdamped dynamics
was further covered for bounded non-smooth interactions. If interactions are weak enough, we proved in our previous work
[
2
]
that (
1.10
) actually holds uniformly in time, and the same was obtained later in
[
21
]
for the overdamped dynamics with bounded non-smooth interactions. All these results have been obtained only on the condition that initial correlations vanish.
Yet, uniform-in-time versions of (
1.10
) hint towards a corresponding ‘higher-order’ creation-of-chaos phenomenon at the level of correlation functions:

for general exchangeable initial data, is it possible to show that the
m
-particle correlation
G^{N,m}
is of order
O(N^{1-m})
only up to an error coming from initial
m
-particle correlations (or at least, from a finite number of high-order correlations), which is damped exponentially over time?

For two-particle correlations, it already follows from (
1.8
) that

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|G^{N,2}_{t}\|\,\leq\,CN^{-1}+Ce^{-\frac{1}{C}t}\|G^{N,2}_{\circ}\|.

In the sequel, we obtain similar estimates for all higher-order correlations, see Theorem
1.2
below. The only other result of this kind that we are aware of is the recent work of Lukkarinen and Vuoksenmaa
[
17
]
regarding energy cumulants for the stochastic Kac model.
In that work, however, the authors appeal to hierarchical techniques, which require to deal with the whole hierarchy
\{G^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
at once and do not yield simpler estimates of the form (
1.11
) for correlations of a fixed order.

1.3.
Extensions

We briefly discuss some simple extensions of the above results. The adaptation to the Kac model, based on ergodic estimates for the space-homogeneous Boltzmann equation and the use of a similar BBGKY hierarchy, is a bit more demanding and is postponed to a separate work, improving the recent result of Lukkarinen and Vuoksenmaa in
[
17
]
.

1.3.1.
Overdamped dynamics

Our results also apply to the overdamped Langevin dynamics, as described by the following system of coupled SDEs: for
1\leq i\leq N
,

(1.20)

dY^{i,N}_{t}=\tfrac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K(Y^{i,N}_{t}-Y^{j,N}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-\nabla A(Y^{i,N}_{t})+dB^{i}_{t},\qquad t\geq 0,

where
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a smooth force kernel with the action-reaction condition
K(x)=-K(-x)
(we may take
K
of the form
K=-\nabla W
, but this is not necessary).
In terms of a probability density
\tilde{F}^{N}
on the
N
-particle phase space
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
, this particle dynamics is equivalent to the following Liouville equation,

(1.21)

\partial_{t}\tilde{F}^{N}=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\triangle_{x_{i}}\tilde{F}^{N}_{t}-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{i}}\big(K(x_{i}-x_{j})\tilde{F}^{N}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\operatorname{div}}_{x_{i}}\big(\nabla A(x_{i})\tilde{F}^{N}\big).

The associated mean-field equation is now the McKean–Vlasov equation

(1.22)

\partial_{t}\tilde{\mu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\triangle\tilde{\mu}-\kappa\,{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde{\mu}(K\ast\tilde{\mu}))+{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde{\mu}\nabla A).

In this setting, by standard parabolic theory, ergodic estimates for the linearized mean-field equation hold in the
unweighted
spaces
W^{-k,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
k\geq 0
, see
[
8
,
2
]
. This simplifies our proof substantially: while working in
weighted
Sobolev spaces in the kinetic setting forces us to use fine concentration estimates for moments of the particle dynamics, this is no longer needed here. In addition, when adapting the proof to the present unweighted framework, we find that the smallness requirement on
\kappa
in correlation estimates is no longer required to depend on the order of the correlation.

1.4.
Strategy and plan of the paper

Our starting point is the derivation of new estimates for quantities of the form

A^{m}(t)\,:=\,\sup_{\varphi}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu_{t})\Big|^{m}\bigg],

where the supremum runs over a suitable class of smooth test functions
\varphi
. Analyzing the dynamics of those quantities and appealing to the ergodic estimates of the linearized mean-field equation, we are led to proving roughly that for all
t\geq 0
,

(1.27)

A^{m}(t)\,\leq\,\Big(\frac{Cm}{N}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}+C^{m}e^{-\lambda_{0}mt}A^{m}(0),

see Proposition
2.3
.
For the Langevin dynamics, as ergodic estimates only hold in weighted Sobolev spaces, we lose moments of the particle dynamics in the estimates, which we manage to control by means of suitable concentration estimates.

When reformulating
A^{m}
in terms of marginals or correlations, this bound (
1.27
) leads only to suboptimal estimates. In particular, as
t\uparrow\infty
, we only deduce a bound
O(N^{-1/2})
instead of
O(N^{-1})
for the error in the propagation of chaos, and a bound
O(N^{-m/2})
instead of
O(N^{1-m})
for the
m
-particle correlation.
In order to recover the optimal scalings, our next main ingredient is the BBGKY hierarchy: more precisely, appealing again to ergodic properties of the linearized mean-field equation, we show that the optimal scalings can be rigorously extracted from the BBGKY hierarchy, noting that the latter can be truncated using the suboptimal estimates obtained from (
1.27
).

We emphasize that relatively few ingredients are used in this approach: the basic estimate (
1.27
) relies solely on ergodic estimates for the linearized mean-field equation, on concentration properties, and on some regularity of the interaction kernel. The strategy appears versatile and should be applicable to other mean-field systems, including the Kac model mentioned above, which is postponed to a future work.

Plan of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to the statement of ergodic estimates, the proof of concentration bounds for moments of the particle dynamics, and most importantly the proof of (
1.27
), cf. Proposition
2.3
. Next, Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to the proof of Theorems

and
1.2
as a consequence of (

) together with BBGKY estimates.

3.
Creation of chaos

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
using the preliminary results above. Our argument shows how the estimates of Proposition
2.3
can be turned into suboptimal mean-field estimates for marginals, which in turn can be made optimal in
N
after combination with direct estimates on the BBGKY hierarchy. A similar idea will be used again for the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the next section.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We split the proof into three steps.
Let
\lambda_{0},p,q,\kappa
be as in Proposition
2.3
, and assume
p\leq\frac{1}{6}
. In the sequel of this proof, all multiplicative constants are allowed to depend on
d,\beta,a,p,q,

Q_{2}(\mu_{\circ})
,
Q_{2}(F_{\circ}^{N,1})
, and
\|W\|_{W^{d+3,\infty}\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for some
s>\frac{d}{2}+5
. A subscript ‘
m
’ to constants is used to indicate further dependence on
m
and on
Q_{mp}(F_{\circ}^{N,1})
.

Step 1:
Reformulation of Proposition
2.3
: proof of the suboptimal mean-field estimate

(3.1)

\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2}+\|G_{t}^{N,2}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\\
\,\lesssim\,N^{-1}+e^{-2\lambda_{0}pt}\Big(\|F_{\circ}^{N,1}-\mu_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2}+\|G_{\circ}^{N,2}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\Big).

Noting that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi\,(\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu_{t})\Big)\bigg]\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(F^{N,1}_{t}-\mu_{t}),

and that

\displaystyle A^{N,2}_{p,q}(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t})\Big)^{2}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X}),\penalty\ \|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-p}\varphi\|_{W^{2,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\leq 1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2},

Proposition
2.3
implies for

,

(3.2)

\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2}\,\lesssim\,N^{-1}+e^{-2\lambda_{0}pt}A_{3p,q}^{N,2}(0).

Also note that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi\,(\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu_{t})\Big)^{2}\bigg]\,=\,\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(F^{N,1}_{t}-\mu_{t})\Big)^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi^{\otimes 2}G^{N,2}_{t}+\tfrac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi^{2}F^{N,1}_{t}-\tfrac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi^{\otimes 2}F^{N,2}_{t},

we get

\Big|A_{p,q}^{N,2}(t)-\|G_{t}^{N,2}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\Big|\,\leq\,\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|^{2}_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}+CN^{-1}Q_{2p}(F_{t}^{N,1}).

in form of

,
this means

\Big|A_{p,q}^{N,2}(t)-\|G_{t}^{N,2}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\Big|\,\lesssim\,\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|^{2}_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}+CN^{-1}.

Combining with the result of Proposition

Step 2:
Proof of the improved mean-field bound

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|F_{t}^{N,1}-\mu_{t}\|_{W^{-3,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-1}+e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}pt}\Big(\|F_{\circ}^{N,1}-\mu_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}+\|F_{\circ}^{N,1}-\mu_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2}+\|G^{N,2}_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\Big)

\displaystyle N^{-1}+e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}pt}\Big(\|F_{\circ}^{N,1}-\mu_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})}+\|G^{N,2}_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3p}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes 2}}\Big).

+\kappa\int_{\mathbb{X}}\nabla W(\cdot-x_{*})\cdot\nabla_{v}(G^{N,2}-\tfrac{1}{N}F^{N,2})(\cdot,z_{*})\,\mathrm{d}z_{*},

Given

Let us examine the three right-hand side terms separately.

2.1

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}}\nabla_{v}(U_{s,t}\varphi)\cdot\nabla W\ast(F^{N,1}_{s}-\mu_{s})\,F^{N,1}_{s}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle Q_{p}(F^{N,1}_{s})\,\|\langle z\rangle^{-p}\nabla_{v}(U_{s,t}\varphi)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}}\nabla W(x-\cdot)\,(F^{N,1}_{s}-\mu_{s})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle Q_{p}(F^{N,1}_{s})\,e^{-\lambda_{0}p(t-s)}\,\|\langle z\rangle^{-p}\varphi\|_{W^{2,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\,\|F^{N,1}_{s}-\mu_{s}\|_{W^{-3,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})},

and thus, further using the moment bounds of Lemma

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}}\nabla_{v}(U_{s,t}\varphi)\cdot\nabla W\ast(F^{N,1}_{s}-\mu_{s})\,F^{N,1}_{s}\Big|\,\lesssim\,e^{-\lambda_{0}p(t-s)}\,\|\langle z\rangle^{-p}\varphi\|_{W^{2,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\,\|F^{N,1}_{s}-\mu_{s}\|_{W^{-3,q}_{p}(\mathbb{X})}.

For the last term in (

as a superposition of Fourier modes, which indeed allows to reduce to a tensorized test function. More precisely, by polarization, using that

is symmetric in its two variables, and recalling the short-hand notation

, we can bound

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{2}}\nabla_{v}(U_{s,t}\varphi)(z)\cdot\nabla W(x-x_{\ast})\,(G^{N,2}_{s}-\tfrac{1}{N}F^{N,2}_{s})(z,z_{\ast})\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}z_{\ast}\Big|$\mbox{}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi\widehat{W}(\xi)|\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{2}}e_{\xi}(x_{*})e_{-\xi}(x)\nabla_{v}(U_{s,t}\varphi)(z)\,(G^{N,2}_{s}-\tfrac{1}{N}F^{N,2}_{s})(z,z_{\ast})\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}z_{\ast}\Big|\mathrm{d}\xi

4.
Correlation estimates

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Similarly as for Theorem
1.1
, our argument starts from the estimates of Proposition
2.3
: we show how these can be used to deduce suboptimal estimates on correlations, which in turn can be made optimal in
N
after combination with direct estimates on the BBGKY hierarchy. Note that this time we will need to iterate BBGKY estimates multiple times to achieve optimality.
We split the proof into the next three subsections.

While in the introduction we defined correlation functions so as to satisfy the cluster expansion (
1.9
), we recall that they can be defined more explicitly as polynomial combinations of marginals:

\displaystyle G^{N,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{N,1},

\displaystyle G^{N,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{N,2}-F^{N,1}\otimes F^{N,1},

\displaystyle G^{N,3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{sym}}\big(F^{N,3}-3F^{N,2}\otimes F^{N,1}+2(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 3}\big),

\displaystyle G^{N,4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{sym}}\big(F^{N,4}-4F^{N,3}\otimes F^{N,1}-3F^{N,2}\otimes F^{N,2}+12F^{N,2}\otimes(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 2}-6(F^{N,1})^{\otimes 4}\big),

and so on, where the symbol ‘
{\operatorname{sym}}
’ stands for the symmetrization of coordinates. More generally, we can write for all
2\leq m\leq N
,

(4.1)

G^{N,m}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}(\sharp\pi-1)!(-1)^{\sharp\pi-1}\prod_{B\in\pi}F^{N,\sharp B}(z_{B}),

where we use a similar notation as in (
1.9
) for sums over partitions and where
\sharp\pi
stands for the number of blocks in a partition
\pi
.
We also use the following abbreviation for functional spaces: for all
r,p,q,m

W_{p,m}^{-r,q}\,:=\,W_{p}^{-r,q}(\mathbb{X})^{\otimes m}.

4.1.
From moment mean-field estimates to correlations

From now on, we let
\mu
be the weak solution of the mean-field equation (
1.5
) with initial condition

\mu_{\circ}\,:=\,F^{N,1}_{\circ}.

In this section, we examine how the correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{2\leq m\leq N}
can be related to the moments of
\mu_{t}^{N}-\mu_{t}
in terms of the

2.3
implies the following hierarchy of estimates on correlations with suboptimal
N
-dependence (
N^{-m/2}
instead of

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda_{0},p,q,\kappa

2.3

\mu_{\circ}=F^{N,1}_{\circ}
.
Then we have for all

,

+e^{-\lambda_{0}pmt}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m/2}\sum_{\ell\leq K\leq L\leq m}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{K}\geq 1\atop j_{1}+\ldots j_{K}=L}N^{L-K-m+\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{K}\|G^{N,j_{i}}_{\circ}\|_{W^{-2,q}_{3mp,j_{i}}}&:&\text{$m$ even},\\

Proof.

As a starting point, we recall the standard link between cumulants of the empirical measure and correlation functions.
We first used this link in
[
10

and we further included a self-contained statement and proof in
[
2

. It can be stated as follows: for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
,

(4.2)

\bigg|\kappa^{m}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi\mu^{N}_{t}\Big]-\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}\varphi^{\otimes m}G^{N,m}_{t}\bigg|\\

where
\kappa^{m}[\cdot]

For

\kappa^{m}[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi\mu_{t}^{N}]=\kappa^{m}[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu_{t}^{N}-\mu_{t})]

(4.3)

\kappa^{m}[X]\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}(-1)^{\sharp\pi-1}(\sharp\pi-1)!\prod_{B\in\pi}\mathbb{E}[X^{\sharp B}]\,\lesssim_{m}\,\mathbb{E}[|X|^{m}].

Hence, from the above, we deduce in particular for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
,
2\leq m\leq N
, and

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{m}}\varphi^{\otimes m}G^{N,m}_{t}\Big|\,\lesssim_{m}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu_{t})\Big|^{m}\bigg]\\
+\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket\atop\sharp\pi<m}\sum_{\rho\vdash\pi}N^{\sharp\pi-\sharp\rho-m+1}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{X}^{\sharp\pi}}\Big(\bigotimes_{B\in\pi}\varphi^{\sharp B}\Big)\Big(\bigotimes_{D\in\rho}G^{N,\sharp D}_{t}(z_{D})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}z_{\pi}\bigg|.

For
r\geq 2

, and

\varphi

, noting that for

A_{p_{0},q}^{N,m}\leq A_{p,q}^{N,m}

(4.4)

\|\langle z\rangle^{-p_{0}\ell}\varphi^{\ell}\|_{W^{r,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,\|\langle z\rangle^{-p_{0}}\varphi\|_{W^{r,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}^{\ell},\qquad\text{for\penalty\ $\ell\geq 1$,}

we deduce for all
2\leq m\leq N
and
t\geq 0

\|G^{N,m}_{t}\|_{W^{-r,q}_{p_{0},m}}\,\lesssim_{m}\,A_{p,q}^{N,m}(t)+\sum_{1\leq k\leq l\leq m-1}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\geq 1\atop j_{1}+\ldots+j_{k}=l}N^{l-k-m+1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|G_{t}^{N,j_{i}}\|_{W^{-r,q}_{mp_{0},j_{i}}}.

Hence, by Proposition
2.3
, for suitable
\lambda_{0},p,q,\kappa
,

\|G^{N,m}_{t}\|_{W^{-r,q}_{p_{0},m}}\,\lesssim_{m}\,N^{-\frac{m}{2}}+e^{-\lambda_{0}pmt}A_{3p,q}^{N,m}(0)+\sum_{1\leq k\leq l\leq m-1}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\geq 1\atop j_{1}+\ldots+j_{k}=l}N^{l-k-m+1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|G_{t}^{N,j_{i}}\|_{W^{-r,q}_{mp_{0},j_{i}}}.

It remains to express
A_{3p,q}^{N,m}(0)
in the right-hand side in terms of initial correlations.
For that purpose, we first appeal to the cluster expansion of moments in terms of cumulants: given
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X})
, by definition of cumulants, we can expand for all
m\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu^{N}_{0}-\mu_{\circ})\Big)^{m}\bigg]\,=\,\sum_{\pi\vdash\llbracket m\rrbracket}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa^{\sharp B}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu^{N}_{0}-\mu_{\circ})\Big].

\pi
such that
\sharp B>1
for all

4.2
) at

\sup\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\varphi(\mu^{N}_{0}-\mu_{\circ})\Big)^{m}\bigg]\penalty\ :\penalty\ \varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{X}),\penalty\ \|\langle z\rangle^{-3p}\varphi\|_{W^{2,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X})}\leq 1\bigg\}\\[-2.84526pt]

For

4.2.
BBGKY hierarchical estimates

Starting from the BBGKY hierarchy for marginals of
F^{N}
, and recalling how marginals can be expanded in terms of correlations (and vice versa), we can derive a corresponding BBGKY hierarchy of equations for correlations.
Before stating it, we start by introducing some useful notation.

Definition 4.2
.

Consider a collection
\{h^{m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
of functions
h^{m}:\mathbb{X}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
m
the function
h^{m}
is symmetric in its
m
entries (such as
\{F^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
or
\{G^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
).

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
with
P\neq\varnothing
, we define
h^{P}:\mathbb{X}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
as

h^{P}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket})\,:=\,h^{\sharp P}(z_{P}),

and for
P=\varnothing
we set
h^{\varnothing}:=0
.

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
with
P\neq\llbracket N\rrbracket
, we define
h^{P\cup\{*\}}:\mathbb{X}^{m+1}\to\mathbb{R}
as

h^{P\cup\{*\}}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket},z_{*})\,:=\,h^{\sharp P+1}(z_{P},z_{*}),

and for
P=\llbracket N\rrbracket
we set
h^{\llbracket N\rrbracket\cup\{*\}}=0
.

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
and
k,\ell\in P
, we define

S_{k,\ell}h^{P}\,:=\,\nabla W(x_{k}-x_{\ell})\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}h^{P}.

—

For
P\subset\llbracket m\rrbracket
and
k\in P
, we define

H_{k}h^{P\cup\{*\}}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket})\,:=\,\int_{\mathbb{X}}\nabla W(x_{k}-x_{*})\cdot\nabla_{v_{k}}h^{P\cup\{*\}}(z_{\llbracket m\rrbracket},z_{*})\,\mathrm{d}x_{*}.

With this notation, we may now formulate the BBGKY hierarchy of equations satisfied by correlation functions. To the best of our knowledge, such hierarchies for correlations were first written down by Ernst and Cohen
[
12
]
in the context of Boltzmann-type systems.
We refer to
[
14
, Section 4]
for the derivation in the case of the overdamped dynamics (
1.20
): lengthy but straightforward algebraic manipulations are needed to collect the different factors in the correlation hierarchy.
For convenience, we shall write
A-B=A\setminus B
for set difference, with for instance
A-B-C=A\setminus(B\cup C)
and
A\cup B-C=(A\cup B)\setminus C
.

Lemma 4.3

(Correlation hierarchy, e.g.
[
14
]
)
.

For fixed
N
, correlation functions
\{G^{N,m}\}_{1\leq m\leq N}
satisfy the following hierarchy of equations: for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G^{N,m}=

Normalized Standing Waves for the Focusing Inhomogeneous Schrödinger Equation with Spatially Growing Nonlinearity
Mohamed Majdoub ID, Tarek Saanouni ID

Abstract
. We study the focusing inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u = − | x | b ​ | u | p − 1 ​ u in ​ ℝ N , i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=-|x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}u\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}, with b > 0 b>0 and p > 1 p>1 . Due to the spatial growth of the nonlinearity, standard compactness arguments do not apply and new difficulties arise. We first characterize ground state standing waves via a variational approach on the Nehari manifold and we establish some sharp stability and instability properties. In the L 2 L^{2} -subcritical regime, we prove the existence of normalized ground states by solving a constrained energy minimization problem in the radial energy space, and we show that the resulting set of minimizers is orbitally stable under the flow. In contrast, in the L 2 L^{2} -critical and supercritical regimes, ground state standing waves are shown to be strongly unstable by finite-time blow-up. Our results extend classical stability and instability theory for nonlinear Schrödinger equations to the case of spatially growing inhomogeneous nonlinearities.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following focusing inhomogeneous nonlinear
Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=-|x|^{b}|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function,
b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p>1
.

Equation (
1.1
) is a particular case of a broader class of
inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equations in which the spatial weight

-|x|^{b}
is replaced by a general function
K(x)
. Such models naturally
arise in several physical contexts where the medium is spatially inhomogeneous.
In nonlinear optics, for example, spatially varying refractive indices or
inhomogeneous gain/loss profiles lead to effective nonlinear Schrödinger
equations with spatial weights representing nonuniform media

[
22
,
21
]
. Related models appear in the study of
optical beam propagation in nonuniform plasmas
[
22
]
and in
Bose–Einstein condensates under spatially dependent interactions

[
36
,
21
]
. From a mathematical point of view, the presence of a spatially dependent coefficient
in the nonlinear term profoundly modifies both the variational structure and the
qualitative behavior of solutions. In particular, the weighted nonlinearity affects
the existence and stability of standing waves and plays a decisive role in the long-time
dynamics, which has motivated a substantial literature on well-posedness, scattering,
and blow-up for inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equations

[
7
,
9
,
18
]
. Moreover, several classical tools available in the
homogeneous case; such as arguments based on translation invariance or uniform coercivity;
are no longer applicable, and the analysis becomes strongly dependent on the behavior
of the weight at infinity.

The Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) with
b<0
, corresponding to spatially decaying
nonlinearities, has attracted considerable attention over the past decades (see,
for instance,
[
21
,
23
,
14
,
1
,
26
,
13
,
18
,
3
,
19
,
20
,
5
,
15
,
32
,
6
,
17
,
33
]
and the references therein).
In this regime, the decay of
|x|^{b}
allows one to exploit weighted Sobolev
embeddings and concentration–compactness techniques to study well-posedness,
scattering, and blow-up phenomena. We also refer to
[
2
]
for recent
results on blow-up and strong instability of ground states for (
1.1
).

The case
b>0
, which corresponds to spatially growing nonlinearities, is
substantially more delicate. Indeed, the growth of the weight
|x|^{b}
at infinity
prevents a direct control of the potential energy by means of standard Sobolev
embeddings or Hardy-type inequalities in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
As a result, both the local and global analysis of (
1.1
) require refined
estimates that are adapted to the weighted structure of the nonlinearity and,
in particular, to the radial framework.

Equation (
1.1
) enjoys the following scaling invariance:

u_{\lambda}(t,x)\coloneqq\lambda^{\frac{2+b}{p-1}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\quad\lambda>0.

A direct computation yields

\|u_{\lambda}(0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\lambda^{s+\frac{2+b}{p-1}-\frac{N}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

so that the scaling critical regularity is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
, where

s_{c}\coloneqq\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2+b}{p-1}.

We denote by

(1.2)

p_{c}:=1+\frac{4+2b}{N}

the mass-critical exponent, and by

(1.3)

p^{c}:=\begin{cases}1+\frac{4+2b}{N-2},&N\geq 3,\\[6.0pt]
\infty,&N=1,2.\end{cases}

The case
s_{c}=0
corresponds to
p=p_{c}
(mass-critical), while
s_{c}=1

corresponds to
p=p^{c}
when
N\geq 3
(energy-critical). When
0<s_{c}<1
, that is,
when
p_{c}<p<p^{c}
for
N\geq 3
, we say that (
1.1
) is intercritical
(mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical).

Using a Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality together with energy methods (see

[
7
, Theorem 3.3.9, p. 71]
), Chen and Guo

[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
proved local well-posedness in

H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
under the assumptions

(1.4)

\displaystyle N\geq 2,\quad b>0,\quad p>1+\frac{2b}{N-1},\quad p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}+\frac{2b}{N-1}\text{ if }N\geq 3.

As later observed in
[
16
]
, the above theory leaves uncovered the range

\frac{N+2}{N-2}+\frac{2b}{N-1}\leq p\leq\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2},

which is not addressed in

[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. This gap was subsequently filled in

[
16
]
, where the authors also developed a long-time dynamical theory for
(
1.1
), including global existence, energy scattering, and finite-time
blow-up.

The main purpose of the present paper is to investigate the existence and the
stability/instability properties of ground state standing waves for (
1.1
)
in the case
b>0
.
By a standing wave, we mean a solution of
(
1.1
) of the form

u(t,x)={\rm e}^{i\omega t}\phi(x),

where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is a real frequency and

\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a nontrivial solution of the stationary equation

(1.5)

-\Delta\phi+\omega\phi=|x|^{b}\,|\phi|^{p-1}\phi.

Define the following
C^{2}
functionals on
H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
:

\displaystyle S_{\omega}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}dx+\frac{\omega}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\phi(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}dx,

\displaystyle I_{\omega}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}dx+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\phi(x)|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}dx,

\displaystyle P(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{N(p-1)-2b}{2(p+1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}dx.

Note that if
\omega\leq 0
then (
1.5
) has no nontrivial solution, see Remark
2.3
. For
\omega>0
, it was shown in
[
16
, Proposition 2.3.]
that (
1.5
) has a unique positive radial solution
Q_{\omega}\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
N\geq 2,b>0
and
1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
denote the set of nontrivial solutions of (
1.5
), namely

(1.6)

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}:\;\;\;\phi\;\;\;\mbox{solves}\;\;\;\eqref{GS}\,\bigg\}.

From
[
35
]
we know that
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\neq\emptyset
for
1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}.
The set of ground states is defined as

(1.7)

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}:\;\;\;S_{\omega}(\phi)\leq S_{\omega}(v),\;\;\forall\;\;v\in\mathcal{A}_{\omega}\,\bigg\}.

Define the Nehari manifold

(1.8)

\mathcal{N}_{\omega}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}:\;\;\;I_{\omega}(\phi)=0\,\bigg\}.

Consider the minimizing problem

(1.9)

d(\omega)=\inf\bigg\{\,S_{\omega}(\phi):\;\;\;\phi\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega}\,\bigg\}.

We also define

(1.10)

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega}:\;\;\;S_{\omega}(\phi)=d(\omega)\,\bigg\}.

Our first result gives a complete description of
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
and can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 2,b>0,\omega>0
, and
1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p^{c}
. Then

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}=\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{G}_{\omega}=\bigg\{\,{\rm e}^{i\theta}\,Q_{\omega},\;\;\;\theta\in\mathbb{R}\,\bigg\}
where
Q_{\omega}\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the unique positive solution of (
1.5
).

Remark 1.1.

(i)

Theorem
1.1
shows that the variational characterization on the Nehari manifold
is
exactly
the ground state characterization for the stationary problem (
1.5
).
In particular, every minimizer of (
1.9
) is a solution of (
1.5
). and conversely,
every ground state must minimize the action on
\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
.
This identification is crucial for the dynamical arguments in the potential-well analysis.

(ii)

Part (iii) implies that the ground state set
G_{\omega}
consists of a single orbit under
the gauge symmetry
u\mapsto e^{i\theta}u
.
This reduces the stability/instability analysis to perturbations of a unique positive
radial profile
Q_{\omega}
.

(iii)

The radial assumption is essential in the present setting
b>0
,
since the weight
|x|^{b}
destroys translation invariance and prevents the use of the
usual concentration–compactness arguments in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Compactness is recovered here through the compact embedding

H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{b}\,dx)
.

For
\omega>0
, we define the following subsets of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\{\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;\;S_{\omega}(\phi)<S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;P(\phi)\geq 0\,\bigg\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{-}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\{\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;\;S_{\omega}(\phi)<S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;P(\phi)<0\,\bigg\}.

We now establish a sharp dichotomy between global existence and finite-time blow-up
for energy solutions of (
1.1
), in the spirit of the potential-well theory
introduced by Payne and Sattinger
[
34
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 2,b>0
and
\omega>0
.

(i)

The sets
\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\omega}
and
\mathcal{K}^{-}_{\omega}
are invariant under the flow generated by (
1.1
).

(ii)

If
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\omega}
, then the corresponding maximal solution
u
exists globally in time.

(iii)

If
p_{c}<p<p^{c}
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{-}_{\omega}\cap\Sigma
, then the corresponding maximal solution
u
blows up in finite time. Here
\Sigma=H^{1}\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2}\,dx)
.

Remark 1.2.

(i)

Theorem
1.2
is a potential-well dichotomy in the spirit of Payne–Sattinger
[
34
]
.
The functionals
S_{\omega}
and
P
play the role of a Lyapunov threshold and a
virial-type sign functional, respectively.
The invariant decomposition
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}\cup\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{-}
gives a sharp separation
between global existence and blow-up below the ground state level.

(ii)

In Part (iii), the assumption
u_{0}\in\Sigma
is used to justify the virial identity
and obtain finite-time blow-up.
Since the solutions considered are radial, the condition can often be relaxed using localized virial estimates.
In particular, we also have blow-up for
u_{0}\notin\Sigma
under the extra assumption
p\leq 5
.

(iii)

The restriction
p>p_{c}
in the blow-up statement corresponds to the mass-supercritical
regime, where the virial functional has the correct sign structure.
In the mass-subcritical regime
p<p_{c}
, the same argument cannot yield blow-up,
and indeed the dynamics are dominated by global existence and stability phenomena.

Next, we give the definition of stability/instability of standing waves.

Definition 1.1.

Let
\omega>0
.

(i)

A standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is said to be (orbitally) stable if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that if
u_{0}\in H^{1}
satisfies
\|u_{0}-\phi\|<\eta
, then the corresponding maximal solution
u(t)
to (
1.1
) exists globally in time with

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\,\|u(t)-{\rm e}^{i\theta}\phi\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon.

(ii)

A standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is said to be unstable if it is not stable.

(iii)

A standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is said to be strongly unstable if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
\|u_{0}-\phi\|<\varepsilon
and the corresponding maximal solution
u(t)
to (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

Remark 1.3.

The stability notion in Definition
1.1
is the classical
orbital stability

in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is the natural energy space associated with (
1.1
).
The minimization over the phase parameter,

\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t)-e^{i\theta}\phi\|_{H^{1}},

accounts for the gauge invariance of (
1.1
) under the transformation

u\mapsto e^{i\theta}u
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we derive the following
stability/instability criterion.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\geq 2,\omega>0,
and
b>0
.

(i)

If
p_{c}<p<p^{c}
, then for each
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
, the standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is strongly unstable.

(ii)

If
p=p_{c}
,
N\geq 3
and
0<b\leq N-2
, then for each
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
, the standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is strongly unstable.

(iii)

If
1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p_{c}
, then for each
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
, the standing wave
{\rm e}^{iwt}\phi(x)
is stable.

Remark 1.4.

(i)

The assumption

1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p_{c}

implies, in particular, that

N>1+\frac{b}{2}.

This condition imposes a restriction on the admissible values of
N
(and consequently on
b
) required to ensure stability.

(ii)

Theorem
1.3
provides a sharp stability/instability trichotomy according to the
mass-critical exponent
p_{c}
.
This extends the classical theory for homogeneous NLS (
b=0
) to the spatially growing
inhomogeneous case
b>0
, where standard compactness and scaling arguments require
significant modifications.

(iii)

The strong instability statements in Parts (i)–(ii) are proved by constructing
arbitrarily small
H^{1}
perturbations of ground states that lie in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{-}
,
and then invoking the blow-up mechanism from Theorem
1.2
.
This approach is robust and avoids delicate spectral computations.

We are also interested in
normalized standing waves
for (
1.1
),
i.e.
,
standing waves whose mass is prescribed. This question is naturally motivated by the
fact that the mass
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is conserved along the flow of (
1.1
).
As a first observation, a scaling argument shows that one can always construct a solution
to (
1.5
) with a given mass whenever

1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p^{c},\qquad p\neq p_{c},

where the exponents
p_{c}
and
p^{c}
are given by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), respectively.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 2,b>0,

1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p^{c}
and
p\neq p_{c}.
Then for any
c>0
, there exists a solution
(\phi_{c},\omega_{c})\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}\times(0,\infty)
to (
1.5
) such that

(1.11)

\|\phi_{c}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c.

Moreover, we have

(1.12)

\omega_{c}=\left(c\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}\right)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{N(p_{c}-p)}},\quad\phi_{c}(x)=\omega_{c}^{\frac{2+b}{2(p-1)}}Q\left(\sqrt{\omega_{c}}x\right),

where
Q
is the unique positive radial solution of (
1.5
) with
\omega=1
.

Remark 1.5.

(i)

Proposition
1.4
shows that normalized standing waves for (
1.1
) can always be
produced by scaling ground states of (
1.5
) whenever
p\neq p_{c}
.
The mass-critical case
p=p_{c}
is exceptional because the scaling preserves the
L^{2}

norm, so one cannot freely prescribe the mass by dilation.

(ii)

The monotonicity of
\omega_{c}
as
c\to 0
or
c\to\infty
reflects the competition
between dispersion and the spatially growing focusing nonlinearity.
In particular, in the mass-subcritical regime
p<p_{c}
, large mass forces large
frequency, while in the mass-supercritical regime
p>p_{c}
the behavior is reversed.

Now, we consider for each
c>0
,

(1.13)

\mathbf{m}(c)=\inf\bigg\{\,E(\phi):\;\;\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)\,\bigg\}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p<p_{c},

and

(1.14)

\mathbf{m}(c)=\inf\bigg\{\,E(\phi):\;\;\phi\in\mathcal{V}(c)\,\bigg\}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p>p_{c},

where

(1.15)

E(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}\,|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx,

(1.16)

\mathcal{S}(c)=\bigg\{\,\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\;\;\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c\,\bigg\}.

and

(1.17)

\mathcal{V}(c)=\bigg\{\,\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)\quad\mbox{such that}\quad P(\phi)=0\,\bigg\}.

Let us denote

(1.18)

\mathbb{M}_{c}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c):\;\;E(\phi)=\mathbf{m}(c)\,\bigg\}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p<p_{c},

and

(1.19)

\mathbb{M}_{c}=\bigg\{\,\phi\in\mathcal{V}(c):\;\;E(\phi)=\mathbf{m}(c)\,\bigg\}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p>p_{c}.

We will prove that
\mathbf{m}(c)>-\infty
in the
L^{2}
-subcritical regime (see
Section
6
below). In contrast, a simple scaling argument shows that

\mathbf{m}(c)=-\infty
in the
L^{2}
-supercritical regime, namely whenever

\max\left(1+\frac{2b}{N-1},\,1+\frac{4+2b}{N}\right)<p<\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}.

Indeed, let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)
and
\mu>0
, and define

\phi_{\mu}(x):=\mu^{N/2}\,\phi(\mu x).

Then
\phi_{\mu}\in\mathcal{S}(c)
and

E(\phi_{\mu})=\mu^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\mu^{\kappa}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\bigg),

where

\kappa:=\frac{N}{2}(p+1)-N-b-2=\frac{N}{2}(p-p_{c})>0\qquad\text{since }p>p_{c}.

Consequently,

E(\phi_{\mu})\to-\infty\quad\mbox{as}\quad\mu\to\infty,

which yields
\mathbf{m}(c)=-\infty
. This divergence justifies the modified
definition of
\mathbf{m}(c)
in the
L^{2}
-supercritical regime given in
(
1.14
). See also
[
7
,
8
,
31
]
.

Remark 1.6.

Note that the assumption

1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<1+\frac{4+2b}{N}

necessarily requires

N>1+\frac{b}{2}.

Moreover, we have

\max\!\left\{1+\frac{2b}{N-1},\,1+\frac{4+2b}{N}\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\frac{4+2b}{N},&\text{if }N\geq 1+\frac{b}{2},\\[6.0pt]
1+\frac{2b}{N-1},&\text{if }N\leq 1+\frac{b}{2}.\end{array}\right.

We introduce the following notion of orbital stability for normalized standing waves.

Definition 1.2.

Let
c>0
. We say that
\mathbb{M}_{c}
is orbitally stable if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any initial data
u_{0}
satisfying

(1.20)

\inf_{\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}}\,\|u_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}}<\delta,

the corresponding solution
u(t)
to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
satisfies

(1.21)

\inf_{\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}}\,\|u(t)-\phi\|_{H^{1}}<\epsilon,\quad\forall\;\;t>0.

Remark 1.7.

Definition
1.2
provides the natural notion of orbital stability for
normalized standing waves, since the elements of
\mathbb{M}_{c}
arise as minimizers of
a variational problem under the mass constraint. The distance is taken with respect to
the whole set
\mathbb{M}_{c}
, which accommodates the possible lack of uniqueness of
constrained minimizers.

Our goal is to establish the existence and orbital stability of normalized standing waves
for (
1.1
) in the mass-subcritical, mass-critical, and mass-supercritical regimes.
Our first result in this direction is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
b>0
,
N>1+\frac{b}{2}
,
c>0
, and
1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p_{c}
. Then,

(i)

\mathbb{M}_{c}\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

\mathbb{M}_{c}
is orbitally stable in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

Remark 1.8.

(i)

The homogeneous case
b=0
with
p<p_{c}
was studied in
[
8
]
, where the proof relies on some compactness arguments due to Lions
[
27
,
28
]
.

(ii)

The inhomogeneous case remains less understood. The results of
[
29
]
apply, in particular, to the regime
b<0
.

(iii)

To the best of our knowledge, the case
b>0
has not been previously investigated. It appears to be more delicate, primarily due to the lack of suitable compactness arguments.

(iv)

The embedding (
2.2
) is clearly not compact in the limiting cases, namely when

p=1+\frac{2b}{N-1}\quad\text{or}\quad p=\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}\quad\text{if }N\geq 3.

A natural question is whether this lack of compactness can be characterized, even in the homogeneous case
b=0
.

(v)

Theorem
1.5
provides a variational construction of normalized ground states
in the radial energy space.
The key point is that in the mass-subcritical regime
p<p_{c}
the constrained energy
level
\mathbf{m}(c)
is finite and negative, which yields compact minimizing sequences and
strong convergence.

(vi)

Orbital stability of
\mathbb{M}_{c}
is obtained by a standard contradiction argument:
if stability fails, one constructs a minimizing sequence for
\mathbf{m}(c)
along the flow,
and then uses compactness of minimizing sequences to recover proximity to
\mathbb{M}_{c}
.
This is a constrained version of the classical Cazenave–Lions stability method
[
8
]
.

(vii)

The restriction
N>1+\frac{b}{2}
appears naturally in the mass-subcritical range

1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p_{c}
, and ensures that the admissible interval of exponents is nonempty.
This is consistent with the dimensional restriction already pointed out in Remark
1.1
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we gather a number of
preliminary tools, including functional inequalities and auxiliary results that
will be used repeatedly in the sequel. Section
3
is devoted to the
variational analysis of the stationary problem (
1.5
), where we prove the
existence and characterization of ground states and, consequently, establish the
corresponding results for standing waves. In Section
4

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
b>0
,
p>1+\frac{2b}{N-1}
, and
p<\frac{N+2+2b}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
. Let
\phi\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a non-trivial solution to (

). Then

(2.3)

\left(\frac{2}{N}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx=\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{2(N+b)}{N(p+1)}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx.

(i)

\bigl(N+2+2b-(N-2)p\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx=\omega\bigl(N(p-1)-2b\bigr)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\phi(x)|^{2}\,dx.

(ii)

The Pohozaev identities link the kinetic, mass, and potential terms for stationary

Lemma 3.1

Proof of Theorem
1.2

For
a,c\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
, and
v\in H^{1}_{\bf r}
, we consider the scaling

v^{\lambda}_{a,c}(x):=\lambda^{a}\,v\!\left(\frac{x}{\lambda^{c}}\right).

We then define the associated scaling derivative of the action functional by

\displaystyle K_{a,c}(v)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\Big(S_{\omega}\big(v^{\lambda}_{a,c}\big)\Big)\Big|_{\lambda=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2a+Nc)\|v\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(2a+(N-2)c\big)\|\nabla v\|^{2}-\Big(a+\frac{c(b+N)}{1+p}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|v|^{1+p}\,dx.

We further introduce the constant
B=B(N,b,p)
defined by

(4.1)

B:=\frac{N(p-1)-2b}{2}.

\bullet

Stability of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{\pm}
under the flow of (
1.1
).

Let us show that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
is invariant under the flow of (
1.1
).
Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
, that is,

S_{\omega}(u_{0})<d(\omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(u_{0})\geq 0.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

u(t_{0})\notin\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
. By the conservation laws and a standard
continuity argument, there then exists a time
t_{1}\in(0,T^{*})
such that

S_{\omega}(u(t_{1}))<d(\omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(u(t_{1}))=0.

Now, arguing as in
[
24
]
, for any pair
(a,c)
satisfying

\min\Big\{2a+Nc,\;2a+(N-2)c\Big\}\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\max\Big\{2a+Nc,\;2a+(N-2)c\Big\}>0,

one has the variational characterization

d(\omega)=\inf\Big\{S_{\omega}(v):\ K_{a,c}(v)=0\Big\}.

In particular, since
K_{1,-\frac{2}{N}}=\frac{2}{N}P
, we obtain

d(\omega)=\inf\Big\{S_{\omega}(v):\ P(v)=0\Big\}.

Applying this with
v=u(t_{1})
yields

d(\omega)\leq S_{\omega}(u(t_{1}))=S_{\omega}(u_{0})<d(\omega),

which is a contradiction. This proves that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
is invariant
under the flow of (
1.1
). The invariance of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{-}
is
proved in the same way.

\bullet

Global existence for data in
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
. Then, by the invariance of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}

under the flow, we have

P(u(t))\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T^{*}).

Recalling the definition of
P
, this yields

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{B}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|u(t)|^{1+p}\,dx.

Moreover, using the identity

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|u(t)|^{1+p}\,dx,

we infer that

\frac{1}{1+p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|u(t)|^{1+p}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-E(u(t)).

Consequently,

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq B\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-E(u(t))\Big),

and therefore

\Big(1-\frac{B}{2}\Big)\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq-B\,E(u(t)).

Since
E(u(t))=E(u_{0})
is conserved, we obtain the uniform bound

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2B}{B-2}\,E(u_{0}).

In particular,
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
remains bounded on
[0,T^{*})
, and thus the
maximal existence time satisfies
T^{*}=\infty
.

.

.

One has

d(\omega)=\inf_{0\neq u\in H^{1}}\Big\{\big(S_{\omega}-\tfrac{1}{2}P\big)(u):\ P(u)\leq 0\Big\}.

denote the right-hand side. It suffices to prove that

and consider the

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\Big(\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\frac{N(p-1)-2b}{2(p+1)}\lambda^{B-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx\Big),

Lemma 5.1

Lemma 5.2

Lemma 5.3

E(\phi_{n})<0,\;\;\;\forall\;n\geq 1.

Proof.

A direct computation yields

(5.4)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\,\|\phi\|_{L^{2}},

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\,\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}},

(5.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi_{n}(x)|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\frac{(N+2)(p+1)}{2}-b-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx.

In particular, (
5.2
) follows from Brezis–Lieb’s lemma
[
4
]
.

To prove (
5.3
), we use (
5.5
)–(
5.6
) to obtain

(5.7)

E(\phi_{n})=\frac{\lambda_{n}^{4}}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{(N+2)(p+1)}{2}-b-N}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx.

Using (
2.5
) and the fact that
p=1+\frac{4+2b}{N}
, we can rewrite
(
5.7
) as

(5.8)

E(\phi_{n})=\frac{\lambda_{n}^{4}}{p+1}\left(1-\lambda_{n}^{\frac{4+2b}{N}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx.

Since
\lambda_{n}>1
, this implies
E(\phi_{n})<0
for all
n\geq 1
, proving (
5.3
).
∎

We are now in a position to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Part (i).

Let
\phi\in G_{\omega}
. By Lemma
5.2
, we have

\phi^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{-}\cap\Sigma
for every
\lambda>0
.
Therefore, by Part (iii) of Theorem
1.2
, the corresponding solution to
(
1.1
) with initial datum
\phi^{\lambda}
blows up in finite time.
Since
\|\phi^{\lambda}-\phi\|_{H^{1}}\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
, we conclude that the
standing wave
{\rm e}^{i\omega t}\phi(x)
is strongly unstable.

Proof of Part (ii).

This follows by combining Lemma
5.3
with
[
16
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Proof of Part (iii).

The proof relies on Lemma
5.1
together with an adaptation of the
variational argument in
[
2
, Proof of Theorem 1.5(i), p. 117]
.

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We begin with the proof of Proposition
1.4
, which provides a scaling
construction of normalized solutions to (
1.5
).

Proof.

Let
Q
be the unique positive radial solution to (
1.5
) with
\omega=1

(see
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
). For
\lambda>0
(to be fixed later), define

\phi(x)=\lambda^{\frac{2+b}{p-1}}\,Q(\lambda x).

A straightforward computation shows that
\phi
solves

-\Delta\phi+\lambda^{2}\phi=|x|^{b}\phi^{p},

and moreover

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\lambda^{\frac{N}{p-1}(p_{c}-p)}\,\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Choosing

\lambda:=\left(c\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{N(p_{c}-p)}},

we obtain a function

\phi_{c}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{2+b}{p-1}}\,Q(\lambda x)

which solves (
1.5
) with

\omega=\omega_{c}=\left(c\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}\right)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{N(p_{c}-p)}},\qquad\text{and satisfies}\qquad\|\phi_{c}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c.

The remaining assertions follow immediately.
∎

Throughout the rest of this section, we assume that

b>0,\qquad N>1+\frac{b}{2},\qquad c>0,\qquad 1+\frac{2b}{N-1}<p<p_{c}.

•

Proof of Part (i) of Theorem
1.5
.

The proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1. We claim that

(6.1)

-\infty<\mathbf{m}(c)<0.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)
and
\mu>0
, and set
\phi_{\mu}(x)=\mu^{\frac{N}{2}}\phi(\mu x)
.
Then
\phi_{\mu}\in\mathcal{S}(c)
and

E(\phi_{\mu})=\mu^{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\mu^{\kappa}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\bigg),

where
\kappa:=\frac{N}{2}(p+1)-N-b-2=\frac{N}{2}(p-p_{c})<0
since
p<p_{c}
.
Hence
E(\phi_{\mu})<0
for
\mu>0
small enough, and therefore
\mathbf{m}(c)<0
.

Next, using the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), we have for
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi(x)|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4+2b-(N-2)(p-1)}{2}}\,\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{N(p-1)-2b}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{N(p-1)-2b}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K,

where
0<K<\infty
depends only on
b,c,N,p
. Here we used Young’s inequality

xy\leq\frac{x^{q}}{4}+\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(\frac{q}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\,y^{\frac{q}{q-1}},\qquad x,y\geq 0,\quad q>1,

together with the assumption
p<1+\frac{4+2b}{N}
. It follows that

E(\phi)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-K\geq-K,

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(c)
, and thus
\mathbf{m}(c)>-\infty
.
This proves (
6.1
).

Step 2.
Existence of a minimizer for
\mathbf{m}(c)
.

Let
(\phi_{n})\subset H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a minimizing sequence for
\mathbf{m}(c)
, namely

(6.2)

\phi_{n}\in S(c),\qquad E(\phi_{n})\to\mathbf{m}(c).

By Step 1,
(\phi_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, up to a subsequence,

\phi_{n}\rightharpoonup\phi\quad\text{weakly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad\phi_{n}(x)\to\phi(x)\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.3
, the embedding

H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{b}\,dx)

is compact. Therefore,

(6.3)

\phi_{n}\to\phi\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{b}\,dx),

and in particular

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi_{n}|^{p+1}\,dx\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi|^{p+1}\,dx.

We now show that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c
. By weak lower semicontinuity,

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c.

Assume by contradiction that
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=:c_{1}<c
. Then
\phi\in S(c_{1})
and hence

E(\phi)\geq\mathbf{m}(c_{1})
.

We claim that
\mathbf{m}(c)
is strictly decreasing in
c
. Indeed, let
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
and

u\in S(c_{1})
. Set
\alpha=\sqrt{c_{2}/c_{1}}>1
, so that
\alpha u\in S(c_{2})
. Since
p+1>2
,

E(\alpha u)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{p+1}}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|u|^{p+1}\,dx<\alpha^{2}E(u).

Taking the infimum over
u\in S(c_{1})
yields

\mathbf{m}(c_{2})<\alpha^{2}\mathbf{m}(c_{1}).

Since
\mathbf{m}(c_{1})<0
by Step 1 and
\alpha^{2}>1
, it follows that

\mathbf{m}(c_{2})<\mathbf{m}(c_{1})
.

Applying this with
c_{1}<c
, we obtain
\mathbf{m}(c)<\mathbf{m}(c_{1})
.
On the other hand, by (
6.3
) and weak lower semicontinuity of the gradient norm,

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and therefore

E(\phi)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(\phi_{n})=\mathbf{m}(c).

Hence,

\mathbf{m}(c)\geq E(\phi)\geq\mathbf{m}(c_{1}),

which contradicts
\mathbf{m}(c)<\mathbf{m}(c_{1})
. Thus
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c
.

Consequently,
\phi\in S(c)
and

\mathbf{m}(c)\leq E(\phi)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(\phi_{n})=\mathbf{m}(c),

so
E(\phi)=\mathbf{m}(c)
and
\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}
.

Finally, since
\|\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\to\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
and
\phi_{n}\rightharpoonup\phi
in
L^{2}
,
we have
\phi_{n}\to\phi
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Using (
6.3
) and

E(\phi_{n})\to E(\phi)
, we also get

\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2E(\phi_{n})+\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{b}|\phi_{n}|^{p+1}\,dx\longrightarrow\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

hence
\phi_{n}\to\phi
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This completes the proof of
Part (i) of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Part (ii) of Theorem
1.5
.

Assume, by contradiction, that
\mathbb{M}_{c}
is not orbitally stable in the sense of
Definition
1.2
. Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, a sequence of initial data

(u_{0,n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and a sequence of times
t_{n}>0
such that

(6.4)

\inf_{\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}}\|u_{0,n}-\phi\|_{H^{1}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

but

(6.5)

\inf_{\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}}\|u_{n}(t_{n})-\phi\|_{H^{1}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{for all }n,

where
u_{n}(t)
denotes the solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0,n}
.

By conservation of mass and energy along the flow of (
1.1
), we have

\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u_{0,n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad E(u_{n}(t_{n}))=E(u_{0,n}).

From (
6.4
) and the fact that every element of
\mathbb{M}_{c}
has mass
c
and energy

\mathbf{m}(c)
, it follows that

(6.6)

\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\longrightarrow c,\qquad E(u_{n}(t_{n}))\longrightarrow\mathbf{m}(c).

Define the normalization factor

\alpha_{n}:=\sqrt{\frac{c}{\|u_{n}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}},

and set

v_{n}:=\alpha_{n}\,u_{n}(t_{n}).

Then
\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c
for all
n
, and by (
6.6
) we have
\alpha_{n}\to 1
as

n\to\infty
. Moreover, since the map
\alpha\mapsto E(\alpha u)
is continuous for fixed

u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we obtain

E(v_{n})-E(u_{n}(t_{n}))\longrightarrow 0.

Combining this with (
6.6
), we conclude that

E(v_{n})\longrightarrow\mathbf{m}(c),

so that
(v_{n})\subset S(c)
is a minimizing sequence for
\mathbf{m}(c)
.

By Part (i) of Theorem
1.5
, there exist a subsequence (still denoted
(v_{n})
)
and
\phi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}
such that

v_{n}\longrightarrow\phi\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Since
\alpha_{n}\to 1
, it follows that

u_{n}(t_{n})=\alpha_{n}^{-1}v_{n}\longrightarrow\phi\quad\text{strongly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Hence,

\inf_{\psi\in\mathbb{M}_{c}}\|u_{n}(t_{n})-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|u_{n}(t_{n})-\phi\|_{H^{1}}\longrightarrow 0,

which contradicts (
6.5
). Therefore
\mathbb{M}_{c}
is orbitally stable, and the proof
of Part (ii) of Theorem
1.5
is complete.

7.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we studied the focusing inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(
1.1
) with spatially growing nonlinearity (
b>0
), with particular emphasis on
the variational construction of ground state standing waves and their role as
threshold objects for the dynamics. A central difficulty in the case
b>0
is that
the weight
|x|^{b}
destroys the usual translation-invariant structure of the
homogeneous NLS, and this substantially modifies both the variational framework and
the dynamical analysis. In the radial setting, however, the growth of the weight at
infinity yields a compactness mechanism which is crucial in the minimization
procedures and in the characterization of ground states.

At fixed frequency
\omega>0
, we established existence and qualitative properties
of ground states in
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
, and we derived a sharp dichotomy between
global existence and finite-time blow-up in the spirit of the potential-well theory.
More precisely, the sets
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{+}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}^{-}
provide
a natural partition of the phase space below the ground state level
d(\omega)
,
leading respectively to global existence or blow-up under the corresponding
assumptions. This yields a coherent description of the dynamics near the ground
state manifold, and highlights the decisive influence of the exponent
p
through
the mass-subcritical, mass-critical, and mass-supercritical regimes.

We also investigated normalized standing waves, that is, standing waves with a
prescribed mass
c>0
. In the mass-subcritical range, we proved that the constrained
variational problem at fixed mass admits minimizers and that the associated set

\mathbb{M}_{c}
is orbitally stable. The proof relies on a constrained compactness
argument for minimizing sequences in the radial energy space, together with a
standard stability-by-contradiction strategy based on the conservation laws of
(
1.1
). In contrast, in the mass-supercritical regime, the energy becomes
unbounded from below on the
L^{2}
-sphere, which forces one to adopt a modified
constraint in order to recover meaningful variational information.

Several natural directions remain open. It would be interesting to understand the
endpoint and limiting regimes where compactness deteriorates, as well as to extend
the analysis beyond the radial framework, where the lack of compactness becomes more
severe. Another challenging question concerns normalized standing waves in the
mass-critical and mass-supercritical cases, where the constrained minimization
problem requires additional structure and where the stability picture is expected to
be more subtle. Finally, the approach developed here suggests that similar questions
could be addressed for more general inhomogeneous coefficients
K(x)
, and it would
be worthwhile to investigate how different growth or decay behaviors at infinity
affect the existence and dynamical properties of standing waves.

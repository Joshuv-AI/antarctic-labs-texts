Letter to the Editor of Heliyon: Could internal limiting membrane peeling before Voretigen neparvovec-ryzl subretinal injection prevent focal chorioretinal atrophy?
Authors: Mirjana Bjeloš, Ana Ćurić, Mladen Bušić
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We commend the authors for their insightful examination of focal chorioretinal atrophy following subretinal injection of voretigene neparvovec published in the article by Dormegny et al. [1]. The discussion on potential preventive measures, such as internal limiting membrane peeling, is particularly relevant and could offer valuable insights into mitigating this complication.

However, we would like to raise a question regarding the role of immunomodulatory therapy in the observed chorioretinal atrophy patients. The article does not specify whether the treated eyes were the first or second in the sequence of injections, nor does it detail the specific immunomodulatory therapy administered. Additionally, we lack information on the dosage received, the tolerance to it, and whether it was initiated prior to the operation as recommended by the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) [2]. Given these uncertainties, we question whether the timing and effectiveness of the immunosuppressants might have influenced the outcomes, particularly if the interval between the treatments for each eye was a factor.

If the second eye, which received treatment after a longer period of immunosuppressive therapy, did not develop atrophy while the first eye did, this observation could suggest that the stability of immunosuppressants in the bloodstream might affect the incidence of atrophy. This could indicate that a longer preoperative period of immunosuppression, exceeding the current standard of three days, might be beneficial. This consideration is especially pertinent for pediatric patients, who developed chorioretinal atrophy, whereas the young adult did not. The immature immune system in children might necessitate a more extended course of immunosuppression to better control inflammatory responses.

Further investigation into the optimal duration of immunosuppressive therapy could provide critical insights into improving patient outcomes and reducing the risk of chorioretinal atrophy following gene therapy. We look forward to future research exploring these aspects.

Thank you for considering this perspective.

Sincerely,

Mirjana Bjeloš, Ana Ćurić, Mladen Bušić, Damir Bosnar, Borna Šarić, Benedict Rak, and Biljana Kuzmanović Elabjer

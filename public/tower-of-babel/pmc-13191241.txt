Protocol for purifying and sequencing cell subpopulations based on RNA FISH signal in planaria
Authors: Mary Cathleen McKinney, Jason A Morrison, Frederick G Mann, Dung M Vuu, Carolyn E Brewster, Kate E Hall, Blair W Benham-Pyle, Alejandro Sánchez Alvarado
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIsolation and characterization of rare cell types in non-model organisms present a challenge. Here, we provide a protocol to purify specific cells from planaria for downstream RNA sequencing. We employ a one-step dissociation and fixation method that avoids RNA-degrading formaldehyde. We label cells using RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (RNA FISH) and flow sort to enrich sequencing reads from cells of interest for deeper biological insight.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Mann et al.1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Developmental biology, Model Organisms, Stem Cells

Before you begin

This protocol modifies the Probe-Seq technique2 but modified for non-model organisms and RNA preservation. Although designed for planaria worms, it applies to many non-standard organisms where mRNA probes can be generated. The first hurdle involves finding an RNA sequence that will specifically identify the cell type of interest. Since the organism will be completely dissociated, any other cells expressing the same transcript will also be sorted into the positive population. For a full comparison of marker selection process, see Pullin et al. 2024.3 A highly expressed gene provides a high signal-to-background, improving cytometric sorting accuracy. We choose HCR because of its ease of use, high signal-to-noise ratio, specificity for the transcript of interest, and relatively fast protocol (2 overnights). To improve sequencing outcomes, researchers minimize processing time and maintain low temperatures to prevent RNA degradation. Other protocols of RNA FISH (traditional protocols), SABER-FISH,4 ViewRNA (Thermo Fisher) can also be used, but here we focus on HCR.

Innovation

Starting with the published concept of sorting single cells based upon RNA FISH signal, we applied a tissue dissociation and fixation technique that improves RNA quality. We also integrate a widely adopted RNA FISH technique that incorporates a dual probe design to limit nonspecific signal and produces high signal-to-noise via amplification for accurate single cell flow cytometry.

Experimental design

The probes for HCR can be designed manually5,6 or ordered directly from Molecular Instruments. This protocol is designed for HCR v3 and downstream bulk RNA-seq, but is applicable to the newer version, HCR Gold. Probes designed to highly expressed genes specific to your tissue of interest are ideal for sorting, but anything that is clearly higher than background should work. Even combinations of different genes labeled in the same color can work by increasing the fluorophores in cells of interest.7 The optimal fluorophore should be determined by examining the autofluorescence of your sample in a wholemount or cyrosection HCR experiment. Choose a fluorophore that avoids the most autofluorescent spectra in your tissue. In this work, planaria are highly autofluorescent when excited by a 488 nm laser. Therefore, hairpins labeled with Alexa Fluor 647 were chosen to create the highest signal-to-background. Wholemount or cryosection experiments are also useful to troubleshoot accurate and bright labeling of the specific cells of interest. Any modifications to the protocol (such as altering the probe concentration during hybridization), empirically determined to improve labeling the cells of interest in tissue sections of whole mount samples, should be carried over during the dissociated cell protocol.

The quality of the RNA resulting from the protocol will be lower than live cells sorted directly into lysis buffer. The RNA quality can be assessed by RNA Integrity Numbers (RIN) or RNA integrity and Quality (IQ) scores, qPCR for known genes or single-cell quantification of HCR images compared to live cells processed directly for RNA or HCR labeling. The final values deemed to be ‘too poor’ for moving forward will have to be determined for individual samples. Dissociation, fixation, labeling, and sorting time can all contribute to RNA degradation. Proper measures to control RNase activity should be employed to maintain the highest RNA quality throughout the protocol. In general, RNase inhibitors, lower temperatures and reduced protocol times will assist in RNA integrity. Low RNA quality will negatively impact cDNA generation, library complexity, and subsequent downstream sequencing analyses.

In addition to the potential decrease in RNA quality, there is a significant chance of sample loss during this protocol because of the high number of centrifugation steps. Be prepared to start with approximately twice as many organisms as you would expect to need for an experiment sorting live cells for downstream sequencing. Fortunately, cDNA and sequencing chemistries have improved to the point of routine single cell sequencing. However, more cells input per cDNA reaction will produce more insightful results. The SMART-Seq mRNA kit (Takara Bio) can prepare cDNA from 1 to 1000 cells. At least 3 biological replicates are recommended for computational processing. The transcriptome from 1000 randomly collected unlabeled cells from the same samples can serve as a comparison to cells of interest. Since the RNA quality may be lower, more than 1000 cells can be added to the reaction, but the exact amount will need to be empirically determined.

Expected outcomes

This protocol enables the dissociation and fixation of tissues while preserving RNA integrity. Single cells labeled by RNA FISH and purified by flow cytometry are suitable for many downstream applications, including bulk RNA-seq. An example of this is shown in Figures 3A–3D. RNA-FACS-Seq is used to identify genes expressed in mmp-1+ cells in planarians.1 Dissociating 50 worms (approximately 100 μL of tissue) and labeling the single cell suspension with Alexa 647, 1000 positively labeled cells are sorted with flow cytometry. More cells are available to sort in some of the biological replicates, but the Takara Biosciences kit will prepare up to 1000 cells so we cap each of the three biological replicates at 1000 cells. A gating scheme is determined by using three controls to identify single cells andnegative populations, while the mmp-1+ labeled cells form a distinct positive population (Figure 3A). Isolated cells are subjected to standard bulk RNA-seq, which shows high replicate-to-replicate reproducibility (Figure 3B) indicating we retrieve similar quality and quantity of RNA from each sample. Differential expression analysis shows genes enriched in the mmp-1+ population relative to the mmp-1- population (Figure 3C). Double fluorescent in situ hybridization is performed to validate coexpression of these newly identified genes with mmp-1.1 Two of the top genes, nas-4 and prss27, mark similar cell populations to mmp-1: large cells arranged in a punctate pattern surrounding the planarian pharynx (Figure 3D). Together, these data illustrate that RNA-FACS-Seq data are of high-quality and can be used to discover new molecules present in target cell populations of interest.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Intact worm RNA-FACS-seq data from Mann et al., 2025 was mapped to the Sánchez Alvarado lab transcriptome and quantified using STAR. Differential expression analysis was performed using the R library edgeR.

Limitations

We have presented a protocol for the bulk RNA sequencing of individual flow sorted cells based upon HCR v3 RNA FISH labeling, but suggest this protocol is more widely generalizable to other RNA FISH labeling methods and subsequent downstream analyses on an enriched population of cells. We chose HCR v3 for its robust success in many model research organisms and high signal-to-noise ratio, but anticipate that other RNA FISH methods, such as RNAscope, View RNA, SABER-FISH,4 STARmap,8 etc., may also be successful provided the resulting signal is sufficiently bright and steps are taken to control RNA degradation during labeling.

We pooled individual cells with positive RNA FISH signal for bulk RNA-seq but suggest this protocol for other sequencing modalities and protein analyses. Whole, single cells could be sorted into individual wells for plate-based scRNA-seq, resuspended for droplet-based scRNA-seq or employed for DNA-seq or multi-omic approaches. Furthermore, this protocol retains sufficient biomacromolecules for downstream proteomic analysis via mass spectrometry.

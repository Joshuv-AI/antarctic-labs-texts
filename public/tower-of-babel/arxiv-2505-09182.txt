Composition operators in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Michał Borowski & Andrea Cianchi

Abstract
. The continuity of the Nemytskii operator between Orlicz-Sobolev spaces is investigated. Natural Orlicz-Sobolev versions of classical results for standard Sobolev are established. The results presented not only extend the latter, but also improve them in borderline situations. Anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are included in our analysis. The results offered for this class of spaces are new even for customary anisotropic Sobolev spaces.

46E35, 47H30 46E30

1.
Introduction

Given an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the composition operator
T_{f}
is defined as

T_{f}(u)(x)=f(u(x))\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

for a function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

The nonlinear operator
T_{f}
is also called the superposition operator or Nemytskii operator in the literature. Its action on various function spaces has been investigated, for instance, in
[
24
,
23
,
7
,
17
,
3
,
6
,
4
,
2
,
5
,
26
,
15
]
. In particular, information on the continuity of
T_{f}
in Sobolev type spaces is of use in the theory of Partial Differential Equations and the Calculus of Variations.

A classical result from
[
23
,
24
]
asserts that, if
f
is Lipschitz continuous and
|\Omega|<\infty
, then
T_{f}
maps the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
into itself, and the operator

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{f}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)

is continuous for every
p\in[1,\infty]
. Here,
|\Omega|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
. The same conclusion holds if
|\Omega|=\infty
, provided that
f(0)=0
.

A version of this result holds under the weaker assumption that
f
is just locally Lipschitz continuous, and its derivative admits a power-type estimate. However, in this case, only sufficiently regular domains
\Omega
are admissible, and the operator

(1.2)

\displaystyle T_{f}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,q}(\Omega)

is continuous for some range of exponents
q<p
, depending on
p,n
and on the power which bounds the growth of
|f^{\prime}|
. The latter result rests upon the Sobolev embedding theorem for the space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, which dictates the regularity required for
\Omega
. For the same reason, such regularity can be dispensed with when dealing with the homogeneous Sobolev space
V^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of functions vanishing on
\partial\Omega
, equipped with the gradient norm in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

In the present paper, we are concerned with the continuity of the operator
T_{f}
in the more general class of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
. They are built on arbitrary Young functions
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
, of which the powers
t^{p}
, for
p\geq 1
, are special instances. In analogy with (
1.1
), the continuity of the operator

(1.3)

\displaystyle T_{f}:W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,A}(\Omega)

is established for any Young function
A
. This is our first result, which is the subject of Theorem
3.1
. A sharp Orlicz-Sobolev counterpart of (
1.2
) is also offered. Namely, under a bound for
|f^{\prime}|
in terms of a non-decreasing continuous function
{E}
, the operator

(1.4)

\displaystyle T_{f}:W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,B}(\Omega)

is shown to be continuous for functions
B
fulfilling a proper growth condition, depending on
A,{E}
, and
n
.
This condition is prescribed by the optimal embedding theorem for Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and takes a different form, depening on whether
|\Omega|<\infty
or
|\Omega|=\infty
. These two cases are dealt with in Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.7
, respectively. The regularity assumptions on
\Omega
needed for (
1.4
) are the same as for (
1.2
), and, as shown in Theorem
3.12
, can be dropped if the spaces
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,B}(\Omega)
are replaced with their homogeneous versions
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
and
V^{1,B}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The continuity of
T_{f}
between homogeneous anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
is analyzed as well. They are defined via
n
-dimensional Young functions
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to[0,\infty]
. Hence, the norm of a function in
V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
depends on the full gradient and not only on its length. In Theorem
4.1
, the continuity of the operator

(1.5)

\displaystyle T_{f}:V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)

is proved for every
n
-dimensional Young function
\Phi
. Moreover, the operator

(1.6)

\displaystyle T_{f}:V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to V^{1,\Psi}_{0}(\Omega)

is shown to be continuous under a bound on
\Psi
, depending on
\Phi
,
n
and the growth of
|f^{\prime}|
, which extends the assumption required for (
1.4
). This is the content of Theorem
4.2
.

Importantly, our conclusions in the anisotropic setting are new, even for classical orthotropic functions of the form

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}}
,
where
p_{i}\in[1,\infty)
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
. This special instance is discussed in Example
4.5
. The more general class of functions given by

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}(|\xi_{i}|)
,
where
A_{i}
is a Young function, for
i=1,\dots,n
, is considered in
Corollary
4.4
.

The lack of homogeneity of Young functions prevents some standard properties of Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, such as some classical convergence theorems, from holding in Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, unless additional assumptions are imposed on the defining Young functions. By contrast, the missing properties can typically be restored if the norm topology in the relevant spaces is replaced with the so-called modular topology. The latter is defined through integrals instead of norms and is not equivalent to the norm topology for Young functions that do not satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, a property which is not assumed in our main results. This is not a serious issue in the applications we alluded to above, where integrals of functions rather than their norms come into play.
In the same spirit, we deal with the continuity of the operator
T_{f}
in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces in the modular topology. This is not just a technical choice, inasmuch as the conclusions of our results can fail in the norm topology in the absence of the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, as demonstrated by Example
3.19
.

Let us mention that the continuity of composition of operators in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces was earlier addressed in the paper
[
17
]
, whose results are improved and extended by our contribution in several respects. Primarily, the use of sharp Sobolev embedding theorems from
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
9
]
enables us to derive a natural optimal balance between the functions
A
and
B
in (
1.4
), and
\Phi
and
\Psi
in (
1.6
). This is not achieved in
[
17
]
, whose approach relies upon an older non-optimal (in general) Orlicz-Sobolev embedding of
[
16
]
. Importantly, the results of
[
17
]
also require unnecessary extra hypotheses, including the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, on the Young functions defining the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Moreover, domains
\Omega
with infinite measure are excluded from the discussion in
[
17
]
, and anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are not considered.

Our approach builds upon methods employed in
[
7
]
and
[
24
]
in the case of classical Sobolev spaces. However, dealing with Orlicz-Sobolev spaces entails facing additional difficulties, including the lack of homogeneity and of the
\Delta_{2}
-property of the Young functions defining the spaces in question, the non-equivalence of the norm and the modular topologies, and the unconventional structure of optimal Orlicz-Sobolev inequalities in integral form. To overcome these problems, specific arguments tailored for Orlicz spaces are developed in this work.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the necessary background on Young functions, Orlicz, Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, and their anisotropic versions. Our main results and their implementation in special instances are presented in the subsequent two sections. Specifically, Section
3
is devoted to
the isotropic regime, whereas the anisotropic realm is the subject of Section
4
. The proofs of the main results are accomplished in Section
6
, which is preceded by Section
5
where some technical lemmas are collected.

2.
Background

Although the isotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are a special case of their anisotropic generalizations, we provide a separate treatment for ease of presentation. The pertaining basic notions about Young functions and Orlicz spaces, and of their vectorial counterparts, are also recalled. We refer to the monographs
[
18
,
20
,
28
]
for an exhaustive treatment of this topic.

2.1.
Young functions, Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

A function

A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]

is said to be a
Young function
if it is convex, left-continuous, satisfies
A(0)=0
, and is non-constant in
(0,\infty)
. As a consequence,
one has that the function

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{A(t)}{t}\quad\text{is non-decreasing in}\,\,(0,\infty).

Moreover,

(2.2)

\displaystyle A(\lambda t)\leq\lambda A(t)\quad\text{if $\lambda\geq 1$ and $t\geq 0$.}

We shall say that the Young function
A
is non-degenerate if

(2.3)

\displaystyle A(t)>0\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,t>0.

A Young function
A
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition (briefly,
A\in\Delta_{2}
) globally if there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that

(2.4)

A(2t)\leq cA(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

The function
A

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition near infinity if it is finite-valued and (
2.4
) is only fulfilled for sufficiently large
t
. Similarly,
A
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition near zero if (
2.4
) is fulfilled only for sufficiently small
t\geq 0
.
Notice that, if
A\in\Delta_{2}
globally or near zero, then
A
is non-degenerate.

Given any non-decreasing function
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
, the generalized right-continuous inverse
A^{-1}:[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]
is defined as

(2.5)

A^{-1}(t)=\inf\{s\geq 0:A(s)>t\}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

with the convention that
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. Notice that
t\leq A^{-1}(A(t))
for
t\geq 0.

Two Young functions
A
and
B
are said to be equivalent globally/near infinity/near zero if there exists a positive constant
c
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle A(t/c)\leq B(t)\leq A(ct)

for
t\geq 0
/for
t\geq t_{0}
and for some
t_{0}>0
/for
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
and for some
t_{0}>0
, respectively. We shall write

(2.7)

\displaystyle A\simeq B

to denote the equivalence between
A
and
B
in the sense of (
2.6
).
These notions will also be employed for nonnegative increasing functions, which are not necessarily Young functions.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable set in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
the set of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The Orlicz space
L^{A}(\Omega)
is defined as

L^{A}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty\,\,\text{for some $\lambda>0$}\bigg\}\,,

and is equipped with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x\leq 1\bigg\}\,.

We denote by
E^{A}(\Omega)
the subspace of
L^{A}(\Omega)
defined as

E^{A}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty\,\,\text{for every $\lambda>0$}\bigg\}\,.

The norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{B}(\Omega)}
are equivalent if and only if either
|\Omega|<\infty
and

A
and
B
are equivalent near infinity, or
|\Omega|=\infty
and
A
and
B
are equivalent globally.

Assume that
|\Omega|<\infty
and that either
p\geq 1
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, or
p=1
and
\alpha\geq 0
. We denote by

L^{p}(\log L)^{\alpha}(\Omega)
the Orlicz space, also called Zygmund space, associated with a Young function equivalent to
t^{p}(\log(1+t))^{\alpha}
near infinity. Accordingly, the notation
L^{p}(\log\log L)^{\alpha}(\Omega)
stands for the Orlicz space associated with a Young function equivalent to
t^{p}\left(\log\log(1+t)\right)^{\alpha}
near infinity. Also, given
\alpha>0
, we denote by
\exp L^{\alpha}(\Omega)
the Orlicz space built upon a Young function equivalent to
e^{t^{\alpha}}
near infinity.

A sequence of functions
\{u_{k}\}
in
L^{A}(\Omega)
is said to converge modularly to a function
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
if

(2.8)

\text{there exists $\lambda>0$ such that}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=0\,.

We say that
u_{k}
converges to
u
modularly with constant
\lambda
if the limit in (
2.8
) holds for such a
\lambda
.

If
A
is a non-degenerate Young function, modular convergence in
L^{A}(\Omega)
implies convergence in measure. Moreover, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then modular convergence in
L^{A}(\Omega)
implies convergence in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Recall that

(2.9)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=0\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0\,$.}

Thus,
u_{k}\to u
in norm if and only if
u_{k}\to u
modularly with every constant
\lambda>0
. In particular, norm and modular convergence in
L^{A}(\Omega)
are equivalent if
A\in\Delta_{2}
.

Assume that
|\Omega|<\infty
and
A
and
B
are Young functions equivalent near infinity. Then

(2.10)

u_{k}\to u
modularly in
L^{A}(\Omega)
if and only if
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
L^{B}(\Omega)
.

This is a consequence of Vitali Convergence Theorem.

Assume now that
\Omega
is an open set. The Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
is defined as

(2.11)

W^{1,A}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{A}(\Omega):\,\text{$u$ is weakly differentiable and $\nabla u\in L^{A}(\Omega)$}\},

and is endowed with the norm given by

\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)\,.

The alternate notation
W^{1}L^{A}(\Omega)
instead of
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
will also be employed when convenient.

The homogeneous Orlicz-Sobolev space
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
of those functions whose gradient belongs to
L^{A}(\Omega)
and vanish on
\partial\Omega

is suitably defined as follows. Given any function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\widehat{u}:{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)\quad&\text{if $x\in\Omega$}\\
0\quad&\text{if $x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\setminus\Omega$.}\end{cases}

Then we set

(2.13)

\displaystyle V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)=\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega):\text{
$\widehat{u}\in W^{1,1}_{loc}({{\mathbb{R}^{n}}})$, $\nabla\widehat{u}\in L^{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})$, $|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|\widehat{u}(x)|>t\}|<\infty$ \, for $t>0$
\lx@text@rbrace}\,.

Clearly,
\widehat{u}=u
in (
2.13
) if
\Omega={\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. The space
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is equipped with the norm given by

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }u\in V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\,.

Modular convergence in the spaces
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
and
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined
in analogy to (
2.8
).
The Sobolev type spaces
W^{1}E^{A}(\Omega)
and
V^{1}_{0}E^{A}(\Omega)
are defined as in (
2.11
) and (
2.13
), with the space
L^{A}(\Omega)
replaced with
E^{A}(\Omega)
.

Let us notice that, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then thanks to a Poincaré type inequality in Orlicz spaces,
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}(\Omega)
for every Young function
A
.

Sobolev type inequalities and corresponding embeddings play a critical role in our results. They take a different form according to whether
n=1
or
n\geq 2
.

Consider first the case when
n=1
. Let
\Omega
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}
and let
A
be a Young function. If
|\Omega|<\infty
, then

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|)\,{\,{\rm d}}x\bigg)

for
u\in V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
.

If
\Omega
is any open set and
A(t)\simeq t
near zero, then there exists a constant
c=c(A)
such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|)\,{\,{\rm d}}x

for
u\in V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In both cases,

(2.16)

\displaystyle V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}).

Assume that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is an open interval and
A
is any Young function. Then,

(2.17)

\displaystyle W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),

and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}

for
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, where
c=c(A,\Omega)
if
\Omega
is bounded and
c=c(A)
otherwise.

The proof of inequalities (
2.14
), (
2.15
), and (
2.18
) is quite elementary. We provide a proof in Lemma
5.7
, Section
5
, for completeness.

Assume now that
n\geq 2
. Then optimal embeddings in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces can be described as follows.

Let
A
be a Young function such that

(2.19)

\int_{0}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t<\infty\,.

The Sobolev conjugate
A_{n}
of
A
is given by

(2.20)

\displaystyle A_{n}(t)=A(H_{n}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$\,,}

where

(2.21)

H_{n}(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t\right)^{1/n^{\prime}}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$\,,}

and
H_{n}^{-1}
denotes the generalized left-continuous inverse of
H
.

The Poincaré-Sobolev inequality of
[
11
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
10
]
for an equivalent version) in Orlicz spaces tells us that

(2.22)

\displaystyle V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{n}}(\Omega)\,,

and

(2.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\Bigg(\frac{|u(y)|}{c(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}x)^{1/n}}\Bigg){\,{\rm d}}y\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}x

for some constant
c=c(n)
and for every
u\in V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
.

When
|\Omega|<\infty
, the embedding (
2.22
)
continues to hold even if
A
does not satisfy the assumption (
2.19
), provided that
A_{n}
is defined with
A
replaced with a Young function equivalent to
A
near infinity, which fulfills (
2.19
). In what follows, when (
2.19
) is not explicitly assumed, the function
A_{n}
is defined via this replacement whenever needed, without further mentioning.

Notice that, if

(2.24)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t<\infty\,,

then

\lim_{s\to\infty}H_{n}(s)<\infty
,
and hence
A_{n}(t)=\infty
for large
t
. Therefore,
V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, this embedding fails in the complementary regime when

(2.25)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t=\infty\,.

Under the assumption (
2.25
), one also has that

(2.26)

\displaystyle V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)\to E^{A_{n}}(\Omega)\,,

see
[
11
, Comments after Theorem 3]
.

Let
\sigma\geq n
. An open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
with
|\Omega|<\infty
is said to satisfy a relative isoperimetric inequality with exponent
1/{\sigma^{\prime}}
if there exists a positive constant
c
such that

(2.27)

c\min\{|G|,|\Omega\setminus G|\}^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}}}\leq P(G;\Omega)

for every measurable set
G\subseteq\Omega
. Here,
P(G;\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of
E
relative to
\Omega
in the sense of geometric measure theory. Recall that

P(G;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial G\cap\Omega)

whenever
\partial G\cap\Omega
is sufficiently smooth, where
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
stands for the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. The assumption
\sigma\geq n
is due to the fact that the inequality (
2.27
) cannot hold if
\sigma<n
, whatever is
\Omega
. This can be shown by testing the inequality when
G
is a ball and letting its radius tend to
0
.

We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{1/{\sigma^{\prime}}}
the class of domains in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
satisfying a relative isoperimetric inequality (
2.27
) with exponent
1/{\sigma^{\prime}}
. These classes were introduced in
[
25
]
, where membership of a domain
\Omega
in
\mathcal{G}_{1/{\sigma^{\prime}}}
was shown to be equivalent to the validity of a Sobolev-Poincaré inequality between the norm of functions in
L^{\sigma^{\prime}}(\Omega)

and the norm of their gradient in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Notice that, in particular, every set
\Omega\in\mathcal{G}_{1/{\sigma^{\prime}}}
is connected.

Several customary families of domains are contained in
\mathcal{G}_{1/{n^{\prime}}}
. This is the case of
bounded Lipschitz domains, domains satisfying the cone condition, or John domains.

Let
\sigma\geq n
and let
A_{\sigma}
be the Young function defined as in (
2.20
)–(
2.21
), with
n
replaced with
\sigma
. Namely,

(2.28)

\displaystyle A_{\sigma}(t)=A(H_{\sigma}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$\,,}

where

(2.29)

H_{\sigma}(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(\sigma-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t\right)^{1/\sigma^{\prime}}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$\,.}

Assume that
\Omega\in\mathcal{G}_{1/{\sigma^{\prime}}}
for some
\sigma\geq n
. Then,

(2.30)

\displaystyle W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{\sigma}}(\Omega)\,,

and, by
[
12
, Remarks 3.11 and 3.12]
,

(2.31)

\int_{\Omega}A_{\sigma}\left(\frac{|u-u_{\Omega}|}{c\left(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{1/\sigma}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}x

for some constant
c=c(\Omega)
and
for every
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
. Here,

u_{\Omega}=\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}u{\,{\rm d}}y
, the average of
u
over
\Omega
.

In particular, if

(2.32)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{1/(\sigma-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t<\infty\,,

then

(2.33)

\displaystyle W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

On the other hand, if (
2.32
) fails, then

(2.34)

\displaystyle W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to E^{A_{\sigma}}(\Omega)\,.

In analogy with (
2.22
), since we are assuming that
|\Omega|<\infty
, the embedding (
2.30
) continues to hold with a suitable replacement of
A
in the definition of
A_{\sigma}
, even if the integral in (
2.29
) does not converge. In what follows, such a replacement will implicitly be performed whenever needed in the definition of
A_{\sigma}
.

Let us now drop the assumption that
|\Omega|<\infty
, and suppose that
\Omega
is just an extension domain in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, i.e., an open set admitting a linear operator
\mathcal{E}
mapping functions defined in
\Omega
to functions defined in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}u(x)

\displaystyle=u(x)\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega\,$,}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:W^{1,1}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

\displaystyle\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}:W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\to W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\,.

Under these assumptions, one has that

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

for every Young function
A
– see
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
. Since the norm of the operator (
2.35
) is independent of
A
, one also has that

(2.36)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}A(|\mathcal{E}u|)+A(|\nabla\mathcal{E}u|){\,{\rm d}}x\leq c\int_{\Omega}A(|u|)+A(|\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}x

for every
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
making the right-hand side finite.

Such an extension operator is known to exist, for instance, for strongly Lipschitz domains in the sense of
[
29
, Chapter VI]
and in
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domains defined in
[
19
]
. On the other hand, a domain from the class
\mathcal{G}_{1/{n^{\prime}}}
need not be an extension domain.

Let

\widehat{A}_{n}
be a Young function such that

(2.37)

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{n}(t)\simeq\begin{cases}A_{n}(t)&\quad\text{near infinity}\\
A(t)&\quad\text{near zero,}\end{cases}

where
A_{n}
is defined with
A
replaced, if necessary, with a Young function
\widehat{A}
equivalent to
A
near infinity and satisfying the condition (
2.19
).
Then, there exists a constant
c

such that

(2.38)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\widehat{A}_{n}}(\Omega)}\leq c\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)\,.

An equivalent form of this result is established in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
; the present version is contained in
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
. Notice that different replacements of
A
near zero result in equivalent Young functions
A_{n}
near infinity, and hence in Young functions
\widehat{A}_{n}
, which are globally equivalent.

In particular, under the assumption (
2.24
), we have that

(2.39)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for }u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)\,.

2.2.
n
-dimensional Young functions, vector-valued Orlicz spaces, and anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

An
n
-dimensional Young function
is a function

\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty]

which is
convex, even, lower semicontinuous, finite in a neighborhood of
0
, and such that

(2.40)

\Phi(0)=0
and
\lim_{|\xi|\to\infty}\Phi(\xi)=\infty\,
.

The
n
-dimensional Young function
\Phi
will be called non-degenerate if

(2.41)

\displaystyle\Phi(\xi)>0\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\xi\neq 0.

Equivalence between
n
-dimensional Young functions is defined analogously to (
2.6
).

Given a measurable set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we denote by

L^{\Phi}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
the Orlicz space of measurable vector-valued functions
{U}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
equipped with the norm

\|{U}\|_{L^{\Phi}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\Phi\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}{U}(x)\Big){\,{\rm d}}x\leq 1\right\}.

The anisotropic homogeneous Orlicz-Sobolev space associated with
\Phi
is defined as

(2.42)

\displaystyle V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)=\{u\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega):\text{
$\widehat{u}\in W^{1,1}_{loc}({{\mathbb{R}^{n}}},{\mathbb{R}^{n}})$, $\nabla\widehat{u}\in L^{\Phi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})$, $|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|\widehat{u}(x)|>t\}|<\infty$ \, for $t>0$
\lx@text@rbrace}\,,

where
\widehat{u}
is given by (
2.12
).
The space
V_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{V_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\Phi}(\Omega)}\,.

Modular convergence in
L^{\Phi}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and in
V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined analogously to (
2.8
).

Observe that, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then
V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to V^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}(\Omega)
for every
n
-dimensional Young function
\Phi
.

Denote by

\Phi_{\circ}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
the Young function obeying

(2.43)

|\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\Phi_{\circ}(|\xi|)\leq t\}|=|\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\Phi(\xi)\leq t\}|\quad\hbox{for $t\geq 0\,$.}

The function
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ni\xi\mapsto\Phi_{\circ}(|\xi|)
is a kind of “average in measure”  of
\Phi
.

Under the assumption

(2.44)

\int_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\Phi_{\circ}(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t<\infty\,,

the Sobolev conjugate
\Phi_{n}
of
\Phi
is defined as in (
2.20
)–(
2.21
), with
A
replaced with
\Phi_{\circ}
. Namely,

(2.45)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(t)=\Phi(H_{\Phi}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for $t\geq 0\,$,}

where

(2.46)

H_{\Phi}(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{t}{\Phi_{\circ}(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t\right)^{1/n^{\prime}}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0\,$,}

The anisotropic Sobolev embedding reads

(2.47)

\displaystyle V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{\Phi_{n}}(\Omega)\,,

and the corresponding
Poincaré-Sobolev inequality takes the form

(2.48)

\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}\left(\frac{|u|}{c\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi(\nabla u){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{1/n}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi(\nabla u){\,{\rm d}}x

for some constant
c=c(n)
and for every
u\in V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)
, see
[
9
]
.

As in the isotropic framework, the embedding (
2.47
) continues to hold, when
|\Omega|<\infty
, even if condition (
2.44
) fails, provided that
\Phi_{n}
is defined with
\Phi
suitably modified near zero. This modification will implicitly be adopted, whenever needed, without explicit mention throughout.
In particular, the embedding

(2.49)

\displaystyle V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)

holds under the assumption

(2.50)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\Phi_{\circ}(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t<\infty\,,

whereas it fails if

(2.51)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{\Phi_{\circ}(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t=\infty\,.

Isotropic Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces are recovered as special instances of their anisotropic counterparts with the choice

\Phi(\xi)=A(|\xi|)\qquad\text{for $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$}.

Genuinely anisotropic instances of
n
-dimensional Young functions, sometimes called orthotropic in the literature, have the form

(2.52)

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}(|\xi_{i}|)\qquad\hbox{for $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$,}

where
A_{i}
are Young functions and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots\xi_{n})
.
A customary example of functions of this kind is

(2.53)

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}|^{p_{i}}\qquad\hbox{for $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$,}

where
1\leq p_{i}<\infty
, for
i=1,\dots,n
.

The example (
2.52
) can be further generalized as

(2.54)

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\left(\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,k}(i)\xi_{k}\Big)^{2}}\right)\qquad\hbox{for $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}$,}

where
A_{i}
are Young functions,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and, for each
i=1,\dots,n
, the matrix
(\alpha_{j,k}(i))\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is such that
{\rm det}(\alpha_{jk}(i))\neq 0
. A possible instance, for
n=2
, is

(2.55)

\Phi(\xi)=|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{p}+|\xi_{1}|^{q}\log(c+|\xi_{1}|)^{\alpha}\quad\hbox{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$,}

where either
q>1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, or
q=1
and
\alpha\geq 0
, the exponent
p\geq 1
, and
c
is a sufficiently large constant for
\Phi
to be convex. Another example amounts to the function

(2.56)

\Phi(\xi)=|\xi_{1}+3\xi_{2}|^{p}+e^{|2\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{\beta}}-1\quad\hbox{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$, }

with
p\geq 1
and
\beta>1
.

Functions as in (
2.54
),
composed with another Young function, result in
n
-dimensional Young functions as well.
One can also consider
n
-dimensional Young functions of the form

(2.57)

\Phi(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{h}B_{i}\Big({\sum_{j=1}^{n}}A_{{i,j}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{{j,k}}(i)\xi_{k}\Big|\Big)\Big)\qquad\hbox{for $\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}$,}

where
B_{i}
and
A_{ij}
are Young functions,
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\rm det}(\alpha_{{j,k}}(i))\neq 0
for
i=1,2,\dots,h
,
j=1,\dots n
. An example is given by the function

\Phi(\xi)=(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|+|\xi_{2}|^{2})^{3}+(|2\xi_{3}-\xi_{4}|^{3}+|\xi_{3}+3\xi_{4}|^{2})^{2}\quad\hbox{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$. }

Let us stress that there exist
n
-dimensional Young functions that do not split as in (
2.57
) – see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.

The Sobolev conjugate of orthotropic functions as in (
2.52
) takes, up to equivalence, a quite explicit form. Indeed, one has that

(2.58)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\circ}(t)\simeq\overline{A}(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$\,,}

where
\overline{A}
is the Young function obeying

(2.59)

\displaystyle\overline{A}^{\,-1}(t)=\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{-1}(t)\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0\,$.}

Hence, if we denote by
\overline{A}_{n}
the function defined as

(2.60)

\displaystyle\overline{A}_{n}(t)=\overline{A}(\overline{H}_{n}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for $t\geq 0\,$,}

where

(2.61)

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{n}(s)=\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left(\frac{t}{\overline{A}(t)}\right)^{1/(n-1)}{\,{\rm d}}t\right)^{1/n^{\prime}}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0\,$,}

then

(2.62)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(t)\simeq\overline{A}_{n}(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Remark 3.4
.

Under the assumption that

,

, and

in (

, as shown in Example

.

On the other hand, the norm continuity can be restored for any Young function

if the spaces

and

and

and

,
respectively. This can be verified via a close inspection of the proofs of Theorems

and

.

We next focus on the continuity of
T_{f}
in non-homogeneous Orlicz-Sobolev spaces when

is not necessarily Lipschitz continuous but just locally Lipschitz with a derivative subject to some growth condition. In this case, one has to require regularity assumptions on the open set
\Omega
under which a sharp Sobolev type embedding holds, allowing for different Orlicz-Sobolev domain and target spaces for

. Namely, we consider

(3.5)

\displaystyle T_{f}:W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,B}(\Omega)

in the modular topology, where

is a locally Lipschitz continuous function subject to the condition

(3.6)

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(t)|\leq\kappa{E}(\kappa|t|)\quad\text{for a.e. $t\in{\mathbb{R}}$,}

for some non-decreasing, continuous function

which is not identically equal to

, and
some constant

. Note that the case when

everywhere is trivial, as then

is a constant, and hence

agrees with the same constant. Here, and in what follows,

denotes a Borel representative of the derivative of

.

Example 3.19
.

Assume that
\Omega=(0,1)^{n}
, with
n\geq 1
,
A(t)=t\exp t
, and
f(t)=\max(0,|t|-1)
. Consider the function

u(x)=1+x_{1}(\log(x_{1})-1),\quad\text{where $x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})$}

and the sequence
\{u_{k}\}
defined as

u_{k}(x)=u(x)+\frac{1}{k}(\log(k)+1)\,.

We claim that
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
,
u_{k}\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
, but the sequence
\{f(u_{k})\}
does not converge to
f(u)
in the norm topology of
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
.

To verify these claims, observe that

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}(x)=\log(x_{1})<0
,
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}=0
for
i=2,\dots n
, and
u(x)\in(0,1)
. Clearly,
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
, as
u
is bounded. Also,
A(|\nabla u(x)|/2)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|x_{1}|^{-1/2}\log(|x_{1}|)
, whence
\nabla u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
. Next, the function
u_{k}
is bounded for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and therefore
u_{k}\in L^{A}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\nabla u_{k}=\nabla u
,
and hence
u_{k}\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
as well.
Since
u_{k}\to u
uniformly in
\Omega
and

\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u=0
, one has that
\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
.

On the other hand, observe that for
x
such that
x_{1}<1/k
, we have
u_{k}(x)>1
and
u(x)<1
. Furthermore,
f^{\prime}(t)=1
for
t>1
and
f^{\prime}(t)=0
for
t\in(0,1)
. Therefore, if
x_{1}<1/k
, then

(f(u_{k}))^{\prime}(x)-(f(u))^{\prime}(x)=f^{\prime}(u_{k}(x))\nabla u_{k}(x)-f^{\prime}(u(x))\nabla u(x)=\nabla u_{k}(x)=\nabla u(x)\,.

Consequently,

\int_{\Omega}A(|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|){\,{\rm d}}x\geq\int_{(0,1/k)\times(0,1)^{n-1}}A(|\nabla u(x)|){\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{0}^{1/k}t^{-1}|\log(t)|=\infty\,.

This shows that the sequence
\{f(u_{k})\}
does not converge to
f(u)
in norm topology of
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
.

5.
Technical lemmas

5.1.
Isotropic spaces

We begin with some properties of Young functions related to the assumptions of our main results.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
, let
A
and
B
be Young functions, and let
{E}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a
non-decreasing function.
Assume that
A
fulfills the condition (
2.25
) and the inequality (
3.7
)
holds.
Then, there exists a constant
c
such that

(5.1)

B\left({E}(s)t/2\right)\leq c+A_{n}(s)+A(t)\quad\text{for $t,s>0$.}

Proof.

As the condition (
2.25
) ensures that
A
is finite valued, the inequality (
3.7
) implies that
B
is also finite valued and

(5.2)

A^{-1}(t){E}(A_{n}^{-1}(t))\leq B^{-1}(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq A(t_{0})$.}

Therefore,

(5.3)

A^{-1}(t){E}(A_{n}^{-1}(t))\leq B^{-1}(t)+c^{\prime}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where
c^{\prime}=t_{0}{E}(A_{n}^{-1}(A(t_{0})))
.
The inequality (
5.1
) can be deduced from (
5.3
) via the following adaptation of an argument from
the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
. Let
s,t\geq 0
. If
A_{n}(s)\geq A(t)
, then

(5.4)

\displaystyle E(s)t\leq E(A_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}(s)))A^{-1}(A(t))\leq E(A_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}(s)))A^{-1}(A_{n}(s))\leq B^{-1}(A_{n}(s))+c^{\prime},

whence

(5.5)

\displaystyle B(E(s)t/2)\leq B((B^{-1}(A_{n}(s))+c^{\prime})/2)\leq A_{n}(s)+B(c^{\prime}).

If
A(t)\geq A_{n}(s)
, then

(5.6)

\displaystyle E(s)t\leq E(A_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}(s)))A^{-1}(A(t))\leq E(A_{n}^{-1}(A(t)))A^{-1}(A(t))\leq B^{-1}(A(t))+c^{\prime},

whence

(5.7)

\displaystyle B(E(s)t/2)\leq B((B^{-1}(A(t))+c)/2)\leq A(t)+B(c^{\prime}).

The inequality (
5.1
) follows from (
5.5
) and (
5.7
) with
c=B(c^{\prime})
.
∎

An analogous (and simpler) argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
yields the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
,
B
, and
{F}
be Young functions, and let
{E}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a
non-decreasing function. Assume that
A
and
F
are finite-valued, and

(5.8)

B(t{E}({F}^{-1}(A(t))))\leq A(t)\quad\text{ for }\quad\text{$t\geq 0$.}

Then

(5.9)

B({E}(s)t)\leq{F}(s)+A(t)\quad\text{ for}\,\,\,s,t\geq 0.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
A
be a Young function satisfying the condition (
2.19
). Then

(5.10)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{A_{n}(\lambda t)}{A(t)}<\infty\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0$.}

Proof.

By the property (
2.1
),

\frac{A(t)}{t}\leq\frac{A(1)}{1}\quad\text{for $t\in(0,1)$.}

Hence, there exists a constant
c>0
such
that

H_{n}(t)\geq ct^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for $t\in(0,1)$.}

Therefore,

H_{n}^{-1}(t)\leq(t/c)^{n^{\prime}}\quad\text{near zero.}

Fixing
\lambda>0
, we have that
\lambda t\leq 1
near zero. Thus,

H_{n}^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{n^{\prime}}t^{\frac{1}{n-1}}t\leq t\quad\text{near zero.}

Thus,

\frac{A_{n}(\lambda t)}{A(t)}=\frac{A(H_{n}^{-1}(\lambda t))}{A(t)}\leq\frac{A(t)}{A(t)}=1\quad\text{near zero,}

whence (
5.10
) follows.
∎

The following results concern the convergence properties of sequences in Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, with
n\geq 1
, and let
A
be a finite-valued non-degenerate Young function. Assume that the sequence
\{u_{k}\}\subset L^{A}(\Omega)
and the function
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
satisfy

\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{0}}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for some
\lambda_{0}>0
. Let
\lambda_{1}>0
be such that

\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u|}{\lambda_{1}}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty.

Then,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}A\Big(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x

for every
\lambda\geq 2\max(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\}
.

In particular, if
\{u_{k}\}\subset E^{A}(\Omega)
,
u\in E^{A}(\Omega)
and
\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}\to 0
, then (
5.11
) holds for every
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

First, assume that
|\Omega|<\infty
. By Vitali Convergence Theorem, the sequence

\Big\{A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{0}}\Big)\Big\}
is uniformly integrable and converges to
0
in measure as
k\to\infty
. By the convexity of
A
,

\displaystyle A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\Big)\leq A\Big(\frac{2|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda}\Big)+A\Big(\frac{2|u|}{\lambda}\Big)\quad\text{for $\lambda\geq 2\max\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\}$.}

Hence, the sequence
\Big\{A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\Big)\Big\}
is also uniformly integrable.

On the other hand, the convergence in measure of
\Big\{A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{0}}\Big)\Big\}
to
0
implies that
u_{k}\to u
in measure. In turn, this implies that the sequence
\Big\{A\Big(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\Big)\Big\}
converges in measure to
\Big\{A\Big(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\Big)\Big\}
for
\lambda>0
. The limit (
5.11
) follows from an application of Vitali Convergence Theorem again.

Now, assume that
\Omega
is an arbitrary open set. Given
R>0
, denote by
B_{R}
the ball centered at
0
, with radius
R
. An application of (
5.11
) to
\Omega\cap B(0,R)
enables us to deduce that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x-\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\right|\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left|\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,R)}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x-\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,R)}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,R)}A\left(\frac{2|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+2\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,R)}A\left(\frac{2|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=2\int_{\Omega\setminus B(0,R)}A\left(\frac{2|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x.

Hence, we get (
5.11
) by passing to the limit as
R\to\infty
.

The assertion concerning sequences in
L^{E}(\Omega)

is a consequence of (
5.11
) and of property (
2.9
).

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
and
\Omega\in\mathcal{G}_{1/n^{\prime}}
. Let
A
be a non-degenerate Young function satisfying the condition (
2.25
).
Assume that the sequence
\{u_{k}\}\subset W^{1,A}(\Omega)
and the function
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
are such that
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
.
Then
{\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{A_{n}}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{c}>0
be such that

\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Owing to the property (
2.9
), the conclusion will follow if we show that

(5.12)

\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0$.}

Fix any
\lambda>0
. Let

c
be the constant appearing in (
2.31
). By Vitali convergence Theorem, there exists
\delta>0
such that

(5.13)

\sup_{k}\int_{G}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{4c\lambda_{c}}\right)^{n},

for every measurable set
G\subset\Omega
such that
|G|<\delta
.
Note that
u_{k}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and in measure. Therefore, given any
s>0
, we have that
|\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}|<\delta
for sufficiently large
k
. Hence, by (
5.13
),

(5.14)

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{4c\lambda_{c}}\right)^{n}\,.

Now, observe that

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|<2s\}}A_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{\min(2s,|u-u_{k}|)}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,,

and the integrand in the last integral can be bounded by
A_{n}(2s/\lambda)
, independently of
k
. Thus, by Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, the right-hand side of the last inequality tends to
0
. Consequently,

(5.15)

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|<2s\}}A_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Now, define the function
g_{s}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

(5.16)

g_{s}(t)=\begin{cases}t+s&\quad\text{if $t<-s;$}\\
0&\quad\text{if $-s\leq t\leq s;$}\\
t-s&\quad\text{if $s<t$.}\end{cases}

Since
g_{s}
is Lipschitz continuous, a standard result in the theory of Sobolev spaces ensures that for any weakly differentiable function
v
in
\Omega
, one has

|g_{s}(v)|=\max(0,|v|-s)\,\quad\text{and}\quad\,\nabla(g_{s}(v))={\chi_{\{|v|\geq s\}}}\nabla v\quad\text{a.e. in $\Omega$.}

Here,
\chi_{Z}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
Z\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
The following chain holds:

(5.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq 2s\}}A_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq 2s\}}A_{n}\left(\frac{2(|u-u_{k}|-s)}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{2|g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{4|g_{s}(u-u_{k})-\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{4\left|\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y\right|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,.

Observe that

\left|\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y\right|\leq\frac{\|u-u_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}{|\Omega|}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Therefore,

(5.18)

\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{4\left|\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y\right|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

By (
5.14
),

\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla(g_{s}(u-u_{k}))|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{4c\lambda_{c}}\right)^{n}\,,

for sufficiently large
k
.
Hence, via (
2.31
), we infer that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A_{n}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4|g_{s}(u-u_{k})-\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{|g_{s}(u-u_{k})-\,\ThisStyle{\hbox{\hskip 2.08334pt\hskip-2.08334pt\hbox{$\hbox{$\SavedStyle\phantom{\int}$}$}\hskip-2.08334pt\hskip-1.66666pt\raisebox{-1.65277pt}{\hbox{$\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\rule{0.2pt}{0.0pt}\hbox{$\SavedStyle-$}$}}\hskip-1.66666pt\hskip 2.08334pt}\kern-5.83331pt}\int_{\Omega}g_{s}(u-u_{k}){\,{\rm d}}y|}{c\lambda_{c}\left(\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla(g_{s}(u-u_{k}))|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}y\right)^{1/n}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla(g_{s}(u-u_{k}))|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Combining equations (
5.19
), (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) implies that

(5.20)

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq 2s\}}A_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Equation (
5.12
) follows from (
5.15
) and (
5.20
).
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\Omega
be an extension domain in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, with
n\geq 2
, and let
A
and
{F}
be Young functions. Assume that
A
is non-degenerate and satisfies the condition (
2.25
),

(5.21)

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{{F}(t/\lambda)}{A(t)}<\infty\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0$\,\, and there exists $t_{1}>0$ such that\,\,}{F}(t)\leq{A_{n}(t)}\text{ for $t\geq t_{1}$.}

Then,

(5.22)

\displaystyle W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to E^{{F}}(\Omega)\,.

Moreover,
if
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
and
\{u_{k}\}\subset W^{1,A}(\Omega)
are such that
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, then

\|u-u_{k}\|_{L^{F}(\Omega)}\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,,

and

(5.23)

\int_{\Omega}F\Big(\frac{|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}F\Big(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\Big){\,{\rm d}}x

for every
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

Since
\Omega
is an extension domain, there exists a linear extension operator
\mathcal{E}
satisfying the property (
2.36
). Let us still denote by
u
and
u_{k}

the functions
\mathcal{E}u
and
\mathcal{E}u_{k}
. Thanks to (
2.36
), we have that
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
W^{1,A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Pick any
\lambda_{c}>0
such that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}A(|u_{k}-u|/\lambda_{c})\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}A(|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u|/\lambda_{c})\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0.

Let us fix
\lambda>0
. Note that the limsup in (
5.21
) implies the existence of a constant
c_{d}
such that
{F}(t/\lambda)\leq c_{d}A(t/\lambda_{c})
if
t/\lambda\leq t_{1}
. Therefore,

(5.25)

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\leq t_{1}\lambda\}}{F}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq c_{d}\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\leq t_{1}\lambda\}}A\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Now, let
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{A}_{n}
be as in (
2.37
). By replacing, if necessary,
F
with an equivalent Young function, we may assume that

F(t)\leq\widehat{A}_{n}(t)
for
t\geq t_{1}
. Set
s=t_{1}\lambda/2
and let
g_{s}
be the function defined as in (
5.16
).
Observe that

(5.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq t_{1}\lambda\}}{F}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq t_{1}\lambda\}}\widehat{A}_{n}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq t_{1}\lambda\}}\widehat{A}_{n}\left(\frac{2(|u-u_{k}|-s)}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}_{n}\left(\frac{2|g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x.

Choose
t_{0}>0
such that
A(t)=\widehat{A}(t)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Thereby,
\widehat{A}(t)\leq A(t)+A(t_{0})
for
t\geq 0
. Hence,

(5.27)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}}\widehat{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}}A(t_{0}){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}}A\left(\frac{|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq|\{|u-u_{k}|\geq s\}|A(t_{0})+\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u-\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,,

where the last limit follows from convergence of
u_{k}
to
u
in measure and (
5.24
). Owing to (
5.27
), one has that

(5.28)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\left(\frac{\lambda}{2C\lambda_{c}}\right)^{n}

for sufficiently large
k
,
where
c
is the constant appearing in (
2.23
). Coupling the inequality (
2.23
) with (
5.28
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}_{n}\left(\frac{2|g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}_{n}\left(\frac{|g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{C\lambda_{c}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}(|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|/\lambda_{c})\right)^{1/n}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla g_{s}(u-u_{k})|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0,

whence, by (
5.26
), we deduce that

\int_{\{|u-u_{k}|\geq t_{1}\lambda\}}{F}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

The latter limit combined with (
5.25
) tells us that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}{F}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
. Hence,

(5.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{F}\left(\frac{|u-u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0$.}

By the arbitrariness of
\lambda
, the property (
2.9
) ensures that

\|u-u_{k}\|_{L^{F}(\Omega)}\to 0
.

The limit (
5.23
) now follows from (
5.29
), via
Lemma
5.4
.

Finally, consider any
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
and
\lambda>0
, and set
u_{k}=\min\{k,\max\{-k,u\}\}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By equation (
5.29
), there exists
k_{0}
such that
\int_{\Omega}F(2|u-u_{k_{0}}|/\lambda)<\infty
. Moreover, the assumption (
5.21
) ensures that there exists a constant
c=c(k_{0},\lambda,u)>0
such that
F(2t/\lambda)\leq cA(t/\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)})
for

. Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}F\left(\frac{2|u-u_{k_{0}}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega}F\left(\frac{2\min(|u|,k_{0})}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}F\left(\frac{2|u-u_{k_{0}}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+c\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}}\right)<\infty\,.

By the arbitrariness of
\lambda
, the embedding (
5.22
) follows.
∎

2.14
), (
2.15
), and (

).

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}
and let
A
be a Young function.

i)

Assume that
|\Omega|<\infty
.
Then, the inequality (
2.14
) holds.

ii)

Assume that
A(t)\simeq t
near
0
. Then, the inequality (
2.15
) holds.

iii)

Assume that
\Omega
is an interval. Then, the inequality (
2.18
) holds.

Proof.

Part
(i)
. Given
u\in V^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have that

(5.30)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|{\,{\rm d}}y\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega\,$.}

Thus, by Jensen’s inequality,

(5.31)

\displaystyle A\bigg(\frac{|u(x)|}{|\Omega|}\bigg)\leq A\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|{\,{\rm d}}y\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|){\,{\rm d}}y\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

whence (
2.14
) follows.

Part
(ii)
. Our assumption on
A
ensures that there exists a constant
c
such that

cA(t)\geq t
for
t\geq 0
. Therefore,

|u(x)|\leq\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|\,{\,{\rm d}}y\leq c\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|)\,{\,{\rm d}}y\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega$,}

namely (
2.15
).

Part
(iii)
. First, assume that
\Omega
is bounded. Let
u
be such that
\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. A counterpart of (
5.30
) and Jensen’s inequality again imply that

(5.32)

\displaystyle|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\inf|u|+\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|{\,{\rm d}}y\leq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}|u|{\,{\rm d}}y+\int_{\Omega}|u^{\prime}|{\,{\rm d}}y

\displaystyle\leq A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}A(|u|){\,{\rm d}}y\bigg)+|\Omega|A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|){\,{\rm d}}y\bigg)

(5.33)

\displaystyle\leq A^{-1}(1/|\Omega|)+|\Omega|A^{-1}(1/|\Omega|)\quad\text{for $x\in\Omega\,$.}

An application of the inequality (
5.32
) to the function
\frac{u}{\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}}
yields (
2.18
) for an arbitrary function
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
.

Next, if
\Omega
is unbounded, then it is either
{\mathbb{R}}
or a half-line. Assume first that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose, for the time being, that

(5.34)

\displaystyle A(1)=1\,.

Hence,
A(t)\geq t
for
t\geq 1
.
Let
u\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R})}=1
. Denote by
g
the function defined as in (
5.16
), with
s=1
. Set
S=\{|u|>1\}
. Then,
S
is an open set with
|S|<\infty
and

g(u)\in V^{1,A}_{0}(S)
. From the inequality (
2.14
) we deduce that

(5.35)

\displaystyle\|g(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq|S|A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}A(|g(u)^{\prime}|){\,{\rm d}}x\bigg)\leq|S|A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|){\,{\rm d}}x\bigg)\,.

On the other hand,

(5.36)

\displaystyle|S|\leq\int_{S}|u|{\,{\rm d}}y\leq\int_{S}A(|u|){\,{\rm d}}y\leq 1.

Owing to the property (
2.1
), we deduce from (
5.35
) and (
5.36
) that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\|g(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq A^{-1}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}A(|u^{\prime}|){\,{\rm d}}x\bigg)\leq A^{-1}(1)=1.

Hence,

(5.38)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|(|u|-1)\chi_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\|g(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2.

The inequality (
2.18
) thus holds with
c=2
.

When
A
is an arbitrary Young function, then by scaling and normalization, one can produce an equivalent Young function satisfying (
5.34
). This results in an equivalent norm in
W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R})
, whence (
2.18
)
holds also in this case, with a constant depending on
A
.

Finally, if
\Omega
is a half-line, then the extension operator to
\mathbb{R}
of any function
u\in W^{1,A}(\Omega)
by even reflection about the endpoint of
\Omega
maps
u
into a function
\overline{u}\in W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
\|\overline{u}\|_{W^{1,A}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}
. Hence, the inequality (
2.18
)
follows from its version in
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Anisotropic spaces

Recall that, as mentioned in the previous sections,
if the condition (

, and hence

given by (

), are defined with
\Phi
modified near zero, if necessary, so that (
2.44
) is fulfilled. The specific choice of this replacement does not affect the assumptions of the statements below.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
n\geq 2
, let
\Psi,\Phi:{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\to[0,\infty)
be
n
-dimensional Young functions, and let

{E}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing function.
Assume that
\Phi
satisfies the condition (
2.51
) and

(5.39)

\Psi(\xi)\leq c+\Phi\left(\frac{\xi}{{E}(\vartheta(\xi))}\right)\quad\text{for $\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}$, }

for some constant
c
, where
\vartheta
is the function defined by
(
4.3
). Then,

(5.40)

\Psi({E}(s)\xi)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi(\xi)\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$ and $\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}$.}

Proof.

Since the function
\Phi_{n}
is increasing, and for each
\xi
, the function
s\mapsto\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\Big)
is non-increasing, by the assumption (
5.39
), we have that

(5.41)

\displaystyle\Psi(\xi)\leq c+\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(\vartheta(\xi))}\Big)\leq c+\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\Big)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\Big)\quad\text{if $s\leq\vartheta(\xi)$,}

and

(5.42)

\displaystyle\Psi(\xi)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(\vartheta(\xi))\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\Big)\quad\text{if $s\geq\vartheta(\xi)$.}

From (
5.41
) and (
5.42
) we deduce that

\Psi(\xi)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi\left(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\right)\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$ and $\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}$.}

whence (
5.40
) follows.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Assume that
\Phi(\xi)=A(|\xi|)
and
\Psi(\xi)=B(|\xi|)
for some Young functions
A
and
B
, with
A
satisfying (
2.19
) and (
2.25
). Let
{E}
be as in (
3.6
).
Then the condition (
4.4
) for
\Phi
and
\Psi
implies the condition (
3.7
) for
B
and a Young function equivalent to
A
.

Proof.

For functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
as in the statement, the function
\vartheta(\xi)
only depends on
|\xi|
. Let
\widehat{\vartheta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function such that
\vartheta(\xi)=\widehat{\vartheta}(|\xi|)
for
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
Equation (
4.3
) then takes the form

(5.43)

A_{n}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t))=A\left(\frac{t}{{E}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t))}\right)\quad\text{ for $t\geq 0$.}

Namely,

A(H_{n}^{-1}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t)))=A\left(\frac{t}{{E}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t))}\right),

and hence

(5.44)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{-1}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t)){E}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t))=t\quad\text{ for $t\geq 0$.}

The latter identity ensures that
\widehat{\vartheta}
is strictly monotone and invertible, and

(5.45)

\displaystyle\widehat{\vartheta}^{-1}(s)=H_{n}^{-1}(s)E(s)\quad\text{ for $s\geq 0$.}

Now, the condition (

\displaystyle B(t)\leq c+A\left(\frac{t}{{E}(\widehat{\vartheta}(t))}\right)\quad\text{ for $t\geq 0$,}

and the latter is in turn equivalent to

(5.46)

\displaystyle B(\widehat{\vartheta}^{-1}(s))\leq c+A\left(\frac{\widehat{\vartheta}^{-1}(s)}{{E}(s)}\right)\quad\text{ for $s\geq 0$,}

Coupling (

(5.47)

\displaystyle B(H_{n}^{-1}(s)E(s))\leq c+A(H_{n}^{-1}(s))\quad\text{ for $s\geq 0$.}

Thus, there exist constants

and

such that

(5.48)

\displaystyle B(t{E}(H_{n}(t)))\leq A(c^{\prime}t)\quad\text{ for $t\geq t_{0}.$}

Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

6.
Proofs of the main results

6.1.
Isotropic spaces

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We begin by proving that, if
\nabla u\in L^{{A}}(\Omega)
, then
\nabla(f(u))
belongs to
L^{{A}}(\Omega)
. Since
u
is weakly differentiable and
f
is Lipschitz continuous,
f(u)
is also weakly differentiable, and
\nabla(f(u))=f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Let
C_{f}>0
be the Lipschitz constant of the function
f
. Hence,
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{f}
and therefore

(6.1)

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{C_{f}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty\,,

for sufficiently large
\lambda
. Furthermore, if
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
, then
f(u)\in L^{A}(\Omega)
. Indeed,

(6.2)

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|f(u)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{C_{f}|u|+|f(0)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{2C_{f}|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{2|f(0)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,.

As
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
, we have that
\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(2C_{f}|u|/\lambda\right){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty
for sufficiently large
\lambda
. Also, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then
\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(2|f(0)|/\lambda\right){\,{\rm d}}x=|\Omega|{A}\left(2|f(0)|/\lambda\right)<\infty
. If
|\Omega|=\infty
and
f(0)=0
, then
\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(2|f(0)|/\lambda\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,=0
. Thereby, the rightmost side of (
6.2
) is finite. Altogether, we have shown that
f(u)\in{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
for any
u\in{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
.

We next focus
on the continuity of the operator
T_{f}
. Consider any sequence
\{u_{k}\}
, converging modularly to some function
u
in
{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
. Namely, there exists
\lambda_{c}\geq\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}
such that

(6.3)

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Plainly,
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
modularly in
L^{A}(\Omega)
. Indeed, if
\lambda>C_{f}\lambda_{c}
, then

(6.4)

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|f(u_{k})-f(u)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{C_{f}|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

It remains to prove that
\nabla(f(u_{k}))\to\nabla(f(u))
modularly in
L^{A}(\Omega)
, namely that

(6.5)

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u_{k}))-\nabla(f(u))|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for sufficiently large
\lambda>0
.

Assume, for the time being, that
\Omega
is bounded. For any
\varepsilon>0
let us set

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:\nabla u(x)=0\}\,,

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:\varepsilon>|\nabla u(x)|>0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:|\nabla u(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}\,.

The first limit in (
6.3
) implies that
\int_{G_{0}}{A}(|\nabla u_{k}|/\lambda_{c})\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
. Hence,

(6.6)

\int_{G_{0}}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u_{k}))-\nabla(f(u))|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{G_{0}}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{G_{0}}{A}\left(\frac{C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda>C_{f}\lambda_{c}
.

Moreover, since
|G_{\varepsilon}|\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
, from (
6.3
) we deduce that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u_{k}))-\nabla(f(u))|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{2C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{2C_{f}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}2\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{2C_{f}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0

for
\lambda>4C_{f}\lambda_{c}
. Note that the convergence in
k
rests upon Lemma
5.4
.

Thanks to (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), it only remains to show that for every
\lambda_{0}>12C_{f}\lambda_{c}
and
\varepsilon>0
one has

(6.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda_{0}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Owing to Lusin’s Theorem, for any
\delta>0
, there exists an open set

{S_{\delta}}
, and a continuous function
g_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|{S_{\delta}}|<\delta
,
g_{\delta}=f^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus S_{\delta}
, and
|g_{\delta}|\leq C_{f}
.

Clearly,

(6.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

for
\lambda>0
.
Owing to equation (
6.3
) and the continuity of
g_{\delta}
,
every subsequence of
\left\{{A}\left(\frac{3(g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u)}{\lambda}\right)\right\}
has a subsequence converging to
0
a.e.. Thus,
{A}\left(\frac{3(g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u)}{\lambda}\right)\to 0
in measure. Moreover,

{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\leq{A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right)+{A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\,,

and the right-hand side is equiintegrable by Lemma
5.4
, provided that
\lambda>12C_{f}\lambda_{c}
. This piece of information, combined with Vitali Convergence Theorem, implies that

(6.10)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda>12C_{f}\lambda_{c}
.

Next, we have that

(6.11)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k}){A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,.

Let us set
V_{k,\varepsilon}=\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap\{x\in\Omega:|\nabla u_{k}(x)|\geq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\}
. As
\nabla u_{k}\to\nabla u
in measure,

|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.4
and Vitali Convergence Theorem,

(6.12)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k}){A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda>12C_{f}\lambda_{c}
.
Also,

(6.13)

|V_{k,\varepsilon}\cap u_{k}^{-1}({S_{\delta}})|=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{V_{k,\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\,{\rm d}}x\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|{\,{\rm d}}x\,.

As a consequence of
[
14
, Theorem 1]
and
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
, one has that, if

Z
is any measurable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
, then the functional

(6.14)

W^{1,1}(\Omega)\ni v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\chi_{Z}(u)|\nabla v|{\,{\rm d}}x\quad\text{ is continuous on $W^{1,1}(\Omega)\,$.}

Note that as
\Omega
is bounded,
W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, and modular convergence in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
implies convergence in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Hence, thanks to the property (
6.14
),

(6.15)

\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|{\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u)|\nabla u|{\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{{S_{\delta}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{u=t\}){\,{\rm d}}t\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0,

where the last equality holds thanks to the coarea formula
[
21

. Consequently, by (
6.13
) and (
6.15
),

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\Big(\limsup_{k}|V_{k,\varepsilon}\cap u_{k}^{-1}({S_{\delta}})|\Big)=0,

whence

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\bigg(\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{V_{k,\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k}){A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\bigg)=0.

Combining the latter limit
with equations (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) yields

(6.16)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

To estimate the last integral on the right-hand side of the inequality (

(6.17)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u){A}\left(\frac{6C_{f}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,.

A chain analogous to (
6.13
) and an application of the coarea formula as in (

) enables showing that

. Thus,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{A}\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0\,,

whence, via (
6.9
), (
6.10
) and (
6.16
), we infer that
the limit (
6.8
) holds
for every
\lambda_{0}>12C_{f}\lambda_{c}
.
This concludes the proof of (
6.5
) for bounded
\Omega
.

It remains to remove this additional assumption. Let
\Omega
be any open set in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Given any bounded open set

, we already know that

\int_{G}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda_{0}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,,

for

Fix any such
\lambda_{0}
and any

\int_{\Omega\setminus G}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right)<\varepsilon

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus U}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda_{0}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus U}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega\setminus U}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}2\int_{\Omega\setminus U}{A}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq 2\varepsilon\,,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda_{0}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq 2\varepsilon\,.

\int_{\Omega}{A}\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda_{0}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,,

whence (

) holds for

.

Proof of Theorem

Here, and in what follows, we shall repeatedly make use of the fact that, if

is any weakly differentiable function, and

is a locally Lipschitz continuous function, then

is weakly differentiable if and only if the function

is locally integrable in
\Omega
. Moreover, if this is the case, then
\nabla(f(u))=f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u

a.e. in
\Omega
.
We refer to
[
22
]
for this and related results.

Part (i).
Observe that as
A
satisfies (
2.24
), and
\Omega\in\mathcal{G}_{1/n^{\prime}}
, by (
2.33
) the space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Therefore, if
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, there exists
M>0
such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M
and
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M
for every
k
. Therefore, the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
yields (
3.1
).

Part (ii). As a first step, we show that, if
u\in{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
, then
f(u)\in{W^{1,B}}(\Omega)
. Our assumptions on
f
ensure that

(6.19)

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq{\kappa}|t|{E}({\kappa}|t|)+|f(0)|\quad\text{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Let
c
be the constant appearing in Lemma
5.1
. Thus,

(6.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f(u)|}{4\lambda{\kappa}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f(0)|}{2\lambda{\kappa}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|u|{E}({\kappa}|u|)}{2\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|B\left(\frac{|f(0)|}{2\lambda{\kappa}}\right)+c|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}A_{n}({\kappa}|u|){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\,.

The rightmost side of the chain (
6.20
) is finite
for sufficiently large
\lambda>0
, inasmuch as
|\Omega|<\infty
,
u\in L^{A}(\Omega)
, and the embedding (
2.34
) holds with
\sigma=n
. This tells us that
f(u)\in L^{B}(\Omega)
.

Furthermore, by (
5.1
),

(6.21)

\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u)||\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{{\kappa}{E}({\kappa}|u|)|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(c+A\left(\frac{2{\kappa}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x<\infty\,,

for
\lambda\geq 2{\kappa}\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}
, whence
|f^{\prime}(u)||\nabla u|\in L^{B}
. Altogether, we have that
f(u)\in{W^{1,B}}(\Omega)
.

As far as the continuity of the operator
T_{f}
is concerned, consider any sequence
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
. Thus, we may pick
\lambda_{c}\geq\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}
such that

(6.22)

\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u_{k}-u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

To prove that
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
modularly in
L^{B}
, note that

(6.23)

\displaystyle B\left(\frac{|f(u_{k}(x))-f(u(x))|}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\leq B\left(\frac{2|f(u(x))|}{\lambda}\right)+B\left(\frac{2|f(u_{k}(x))|}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\leq B\left(\frac{2|f(u(x))|}{\lambda}\right)+B\left(\frac{4|f(0)|}{\lambda}\right)+B\left(\frac{4{\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|{E}({\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|)}{\lambda}\right)

for
\lambda>0
and
x\in\Omega
.
By Lemma
5.1
,

(6.24)

B\left(\frac{4{\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|{E}({\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|)}{\lambda}\right)\leq c+A\left(\frac{8{\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|}{\lambda}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}(x)|)\quad\text{for $\lambda>0$ and $x\in\Omega$.}

From Lemmas
5.5
and
5.4
we deduce that
\int_{\Omega}A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}A_{n}({\kappa}|u|){\,{\rm d}}x
. Thanks to Lemma
5.4
again, this piece of information ensures that the right-hand side of (
6.24
) is equiintegrable for
\lambda>16{\kappa}\lambda_{c}
. As every subsequence of
\{f(u_{k}(x))\}
has a subsequence that converges pointwise a.e. to
f(u(x))
, the sequence
\{f(u_{k})\}
converges to
f(u)
in measure. Thereby, the estimates (
6.23
) and (
6.24
), via Vitali Convergence Theorem, imply that

(6.25)

B\left(\frac{|f(u_{k}(x))-f(u(x))|}{\lambda}\right)\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,

for
\lambda>16{\kappa}\lambda_{c}
. Hence,
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
modularly in
L^{B}(\Omega)
.

It remains to prove the modular convergence of
\nabla(f(u_{k}))
to
\nabla(f(u))
in
L^{B}(\Omega)
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, let us set:

\displaystyle G_{0}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:\nabla u(x)=0\}\,,

\displaystyle G_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:\varepsilon>|\nabla u(x)|>0\}\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:|\nabla u(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}\,.

By (
6.22
),
\int_{G_{0}}A(|\nabla u_{k}|/\lambda_{c})\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
. Moreover, since any subsequence of
\{u_{k}\}
admits a subsequence which converges a.e. to
u
, the sequence
f^{\prime}(u_{k})
is bounded a.e. in
\Omega
, and
f^{\prime}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|\to 0
in measure in
G_{0}
. By Lemma
5.1
,

B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)\leq c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)\,.

Lemma
5.5
ensures that the right-hand side of the last inequality converges in
L^{1}(G_{0})
. Hence, owing to Vitali Convergence Theorem,

(6.26)

\int_{G_{0}}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Next, note that
|G_{\varepsilon}|\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0
. Moreover,

(6.27)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u_{k}))-\nabla(f(u))|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}\left(c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u))|}{2{\kappa}\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}\left(c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{G_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u))|}{2{\kappa}\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}0\,,

for
\lambda\geq 8\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
, where the limits hold thanks to
Lemma
5.4
and equation (
6.21
).

Our last task is to show that

(6.28)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda\geq 24{\kappa}\lambda_{c}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
By Lusin’s Theorem, for any
\delta>0
, there exists an open set
{S_{\delta}}
and a continuous function
g_{\delta}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|{S_{\delta}}|<\delta
and
g_{\delta}=f^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{S_{\delta}}
. We may also assume that
|g_{\delta}(t)|\leq{\kappa}{E}(t)
, since the function

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\min\{{\kappa}{E}({\kappa}|t|),\max\{(-{\kappa}{E}({\kappa}|t|),g_{\delta}(t))\}\}

is also continuous and agree with
f^{\prime}
in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{S_{\delta}}
, as
|f^{\prime}|\leq{\kappa}{E}({\kappa}|t|)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Consequently,

(6.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

for
\lambda>0
.
Every subsequence of
\Big\{B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\Big\}
has a subsequence that converges to
0
a.e. in
\Omega
. This ensures that
B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\to 0
in measure. Moreover,

\displaystyle B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\leq c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)+B\left(\frac{{E}({\kappa}|u|)|\nabla u|}{4\lambda_{c}}\right)

for
\lambda\geq 24\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
. Hence, via Lemma
5.4
, equation (
6.21
), and Vitali Convergence Theorem one infers that

(6.30)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

Next, observe that
|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap u^{-1}({S_{\delta}})|\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0
. Indeed, the coarea formula
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
and the fact that
|S_{\delta}|<\delta
imply

|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap u^{-1}(S_{\delta})|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u)|\nabla u|{\,{\rm d}}x={\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\int_{{S_{\delta}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{u=t\}){\,{\rm d}}t\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0\,.

Thereby,

(6.31)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u)B\left(\frac{3|g_{\delta}(u)\nabla u-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u)\left(c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0

for
\lambda\geq 24\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
.

Now, set
V_{k,\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}:|\nabla u_{k}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\}
. We have

(6.32)

|V_{k,\varepsilon}\cap u_{k}^{-1}({S_{\delta}})|=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{V_{k,\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\,{\rm d}}x\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|{\,{\rm d}}x\,.

By the property (
6.14
), the operator
v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(v)|\nabla v|{\,{\rm d}}x
is continuous on
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Therefore,

(6.33)

\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|{\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u)|\nabla u|{\,{\rm d}}x=\int_{{S_{\delta}}}H^{n-1}(\{u=t\})\,{\,{\rm d}}t\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0\,.

Coupling (
6.32
) with (
6.33
) tells us that
{\lim_{k}}|V_{k,\varepsilon}\cap u_{k}^{-1}({S_{\delta}})|\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k}\int_{V_{k,\varepsilon}}B\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

(6.34)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k}\int_{V_{k,\varepsilon}}\chi_{{S_{\delta}}}(u_{k})\left(c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0

for
\lambda\geq 24\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
, where
the limit in
\delta
relies upon the uniform integrability of
A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)
and
A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)
.

Since
\nabla u_{k}\to\nabla u
in measure, we have that
|\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}|\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0
. By uniform integrability, this implies

(6.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle B\left(\frac{3|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-g_{\delta}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus V_{k,\varepsilon}}2\left(c+A\left(\frac{|\nabla u_{k}|}{2\lambda_{c}}\right)+A_{n}({\kappa}|u_{k}|)\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda\geq 24\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
.

Equation (
6.28
) follows from (
6.29
), (
6.30
), (
6.31
), and (
6.35
).

Thanks to the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, from equations (
6.26
)
, (
6.27
), and (
6.28
) we deduce that

(6.36)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for
\lambda\geq 24\lambda_{c}{\kappa}
. The modular convergence
\nabla(f(u_{k}))\to\nabla(f(u))
in
L^{B}(\Omega)
is thus established. The proof of the modular convergence
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
in
{W^{1,B}}(\Omega)
is complete.
∎

Proof Theorem

.

Part (i).
Thanks to the inequality (

3.5
.

Part (ii). Since the assumptions imposed on the function
{F}

far from zero, that

3.8
) and (
3.7
), we may suppose that

A^{-1}(t){E}({F}^{-1}(t))\leq B^{-1}(t)\quad\text{ for $t\geq 0$.}

The assumptions of Lemma
5.2
are thus satisfied.

We begin by
proving that
f(u)\in W^{1,B}(\Omega)
for any

. Lemma
5.2

(6.37)

\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f(u)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{{\kappa}|u|{E}({\kappa}|u|)}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left({F}({\kappa}|u|)+A\left(\frac{{\kappa}|u|}{\lambda}\right)\right){\,{\rm d}}x

.

.

Similarly,

\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{{\kappa}|\nabla u|{E}({\kappa}|u|)}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left({F}({\kappa}|u|)+A\left(\frac{{\kappa}|\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)\right){\,{\rm d}}x

for

. As the last integral is finite

, we have that
\nabla(f(u))\in L^{B}(\Omega)
. In conclusion,
f(u)\in W^{1,B}(\Omega)
.

Let us now establish the continuity of the operator
T_{f}
from
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
into
W^{1,B}(\Omega)
. Assume that
\{u_{k}\}\subset{W^{1,A}}(\Omega)
and

are such that
u_{k}\to u
modularly in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
. Choose
\lambda_{c}\geq\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}
such that

Consider any open set
G\subset\Omega
which is a finite union of domains from the class
\mathcal{G}_{1/n^{\prime}}
, bounded connected Lipschitz domains, for instance. Such a set

can be chosen in such a way that

is arbitrarily small. As (
3.7
) is in force,
Theorem
3.5
and Remark
3.6
ensure that

(6.38)

. Next, notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus G}B\left(\frac{|f(u_{k})-f(u)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x

5.4
and
5.6
for convergence. The integrand in the last integral in (

. As a consequence, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
G
as above such that this integral does not exceed
\varepsilon
. By the arbitrariness of

and (

\int_{\Omega}B(|f(u_{k})-f(u)|/\lambda){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus G}B\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u_{k}-f^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|}{\lambda}\right)

Proof of Proposition
3.9
, sketched.

The strengthening of the conclusions of Parts (ii) of Theorems
3.5
and
3.7
provided by this proposition amounts to the fact that if
u_{k}\to u
in the modular topology of
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, then

\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|f(u_{k})-f(u)|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}B\left(\frac{|\nabla(f(u_{k}))-\nabla(f(u))|}{\lambda}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0

for every
\lambda>0
, and not just for sufficiently large
\lambda
. The latter restriction on
\lambda
arises in the proof of
Theorem
3.5
from an inequality of the form

(6.41)

B\left(\frac{C{E}({\kappa}|v|)|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right)\leq c+A_{n}({\kappa}|v|)+A\left(\frac{C|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right),

which is exploited several times. The proof of Theorem
3.5
uses inequality (
6.41
) with several constants
C>0
, but never exceeding
6{\kappa}
.

As now
E
is Young function, given constants
C
and
\lambda
, thanks
to the property (
2.2
) and Lemma
5.1
, we can make use of the inequality

(6.42)

B\left(\frac{C{E}({\kappa}|v|)|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right)\leq B\left(\frac{C{E}({\kappa}\tfrac{\Lambda}{\lambda}|v|)|\nabla v|}{\Lambda}\right)\leq c+A_{n}\left({\kappa}\tfrac{\Lambda}{\lambda}|v|\right)+A\left(\frac{2C|\nabla v|}{\Lambda}\right)\,,

where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
One can argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, starting with any
\lambda>0
instead of choosing a particular
\lambda
. Then, one can take
\Lambda=24{\kappa}\max(\lambda,\lambda_{c},\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)})
and replace every use of the inequality (
6.41
) with the inequality (
6.42
). This affects the constants inside the functions
A
and
A_{n}
in the estimates. Nonetheless, the choice of
\Lambda
shall guarantee the equiintegrability of the families
\left\{A_{n}\left(\kappa\frac{\Lambda|u_{k}|}{\lambda}\right)\right\}
and
\left\{A\left(\frac{2C|\nabla u_{k}|}{\Lambda}\right)\right\}
for any
C\leq 6\kappa
, via Lemmas
5.5
and
5.4
, respectively. The rest of the proof follows along the same lines.

Similarly, if in the proof of Theorem
3.7
, one replaces the inequality

B\left(\frac{C{E}({\kappa}|v|)|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right)\leq{F}({\kappa}|v|)+A\left(\frac{C|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right)

with

B\left(\frac{C{E}({\kappa}|v|)|\nabla v|}{\lambda}\right)\leq{F}\left(\frac{{\kappa}\Lambda|v|}{\lambda}\right)+A\left(\frac{C|\nabla v|}{\Lambda}\right)\,,

then one obtains the desired conclusion.
∎

Anisotropic spaces

The outline of the proofs of the results of Section

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Part
(i)
.
The proof follows along the same lines as the proof of Theorem
3.1
, with
A
replaced by
\Phi
. The inequalities involved in the proof hold thanks to the convexity of
\Phi
and the monotonicity of a function
t\mapsto\Phi(t\xi)
for any
\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}
. Since membership in the space
V_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)
only depends on the gradient of functions, the lines (
6.2
) and (
6.4
) have to be skipped, whereas
\lambda_{c}
in (
6.3
) must be chosen so that
\lambda_{c}\geq\|u\|_{V^{1,\Phi}_{0}(\Omega)}
and

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{\nabla u_{k}-\nabla u}{\lambda_{c}}\right){\,{\rm d}}x\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}0\,.

The embedding

V_{0}^{1,\Phi}(\Omega)\to W^{1,1}(\Omega)
if

Proof of Corollary
4.4
.

Part
(i)
. Using the assumption (
4.8
) and arguing as in Lemma
5.1
enable us to deduce that there exists a constant
c
such that

B_{i}({E}(s)t/2)\leq c+\Phi_{n}(s)+A_{i}(t)\quad\text{for $s,t\geq 0$.}

Hence,

\Psi({E}(s)\xi/2)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}({E}(s)|\xi_{i}|/2)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(c+\Phi_{n}(s)+A_{i}(|\xi_{i}|)\right)=nc+n\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi(\xi)

for
\xi\in{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}
and
s\geq 0
.
Therefore,

\Psi(\xi/2)\leq nc+n\Phi_{n}(s)+\Phi\Big(\frac{\xi}{{E}(s)}\Big)\,,

whence, in particular,

\Psi(\xi/2)\leq nc+n\Phi_{n}(\vartheta(\xi))+\Phi\left(\frac{\xi}{{E}(\vartheta(\xi))}\right)=nc+(n+1)\Phi\left(\frac{\xi}{{E}(\vartheta(\xi))}\right).

In conclusion, we have shown that
the condition (
4.4
) holds with
\Psi(\xi)
replaced with the equivalent
n

\frac{1}{n+1}\Psi(\xi/2)
.
The conclusion follows via an application of Theorem
4.2
.

(ii)

(i)

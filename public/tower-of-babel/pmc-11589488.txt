Targeted review of the maximum residue levels (MRLs) for dimethomorph
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the European Commission requested EFSA to perform a targeted risk assessment for dimethomorph considering the residue definition and the toxicological reference values (TRVs) derived during the renewal of the approval of dimethomorph and the most recent version of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) (rev.3.1). EFSA confirmed that the current TRVs are sufficiently protective for consumers, including the identified hazard indicative of endocrine disruption. Furthermore, EFSA identified all the codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) and import tolerances that are covered by existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) and performed chronic and acute dietary risk assessment to support risk managers during the decision‐making. A screening of all existing MRLs was also performed. The exposure calculations performed by EFSA showed that the existing MRLs, including the ones based on CXLs or import tolerances, do not lead to consumers intake concerns.

BACKGROUND AND TERMS OF REFERENCE

Dimethomorph is an active substance which belongs to the class of morpholine fungicides such as tridemorph and fenpropimorph. In the framework of the procedure on the renewal of the approval of dimethomorph under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 1 EFSA in their conclusions on the peer review (EFSA, 2023), identified several areas of concerns: dimethomorph is classified according to Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 2 as toxic to reproduction, category 1B (H360F ‘may damage fertility’),meets the criteria for endocrine disruptors for humans and wild mammals as non‐target organisms.

In addition, due to several data gaps identified, only a preliminary consumer risk assessment was performed.

Based on the EFSA conclusions, dimethomorph was non‐renewed by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1207. 3 The grace period will expire on 20 May 2025. After the expiry of the grace period, when the EU authorisations will have been withdrawn, the maximum residue levels based on former EU uses will be lowered to the limit of quantification. However, Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) or import tolerances exist for a range of commodities for which maximum residue levels (MRLs) could be maintained provided that it was proven that the levels are safe for consumers.

EFSA provided a reasoned opinion on 30 August 2011 on the review of the existing maximum residue limits (MRLs) for the active substance dimethomorph in compliance with Article 12(1) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 4 (EFSA, 2011a). In the MRL review, the risk assessment was performed using revision 2 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) and the calculated exposures were compared with the toxicological reference values (TRVs) for dimethomorph valid at that time. The MRLs resulting from this review were implemented by Regulation (EU) No 668/2013. 5 In addition, several MRLs were implemented by Regulation (EU) 2016/567 6 based on Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) and by Commission Regulation (EU) No 51/2014, 7 Commission Regulation (EU) 2016/1902 8 and Commission Regulation (EU) 2020/1633 9 based on MRL applications submitted under Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, including the assessment of the Art.12 confirmatory data (see Table 1).

Taking all this into consideration, the Commission requested EFSA to carry out a targeted review of the maximum residue levels for dimethomorph limited to existing MRLs based on CXLs or import tolerances, considering the newest version of PRIMo and the residue definition and toxicological reference values as derived in the context of the renewal of approval procedure.

In addition, as dimethomorph meets the criteria for endocrine disruption, EFSA was asked to confirm whether the toxicological reference values derived in 2023 are expected to be protective also in this regard, or if new values should be derived. If new toxicological reference values are needed and can be established, the consumer risk assessment should be performed considering the new toxicological reference values and safe MRLs should be derived accordingly.

Specifically, EFSA was requested, according to Article 43 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 to: confirm that the toxicological reference values derived by EFSA in 2023 are expected to be protective for endocrine disruption or if new values should be derived;perform an assessment of the chronic and acute risk to consumers limited to existing MRLs based on CXLs or import tolerances, considering the latest toxicological reference values and the newest version of PRIMo;perform a screening of the existing MRLs to assess if they can still be considered safe for consumers in view of the decision that needs to be taken by risk managers whether transitional periods according to Article 49(2) of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 can be granted;carry out a Member States consultation on the draft Statement.

ASSESSMENT

Under the remit of the current mandate, the conclusions on endocrine disruption (ED) properties issued by EFSA in 2023 have been considered with the aim to confirm if the derived toxicological reference values (TRVs) (EFSA, 2023) are expected to be protective for endocrine disruption as outlined in the term of reference 1.

In 2023 the following TRVs (acceptable daily intake (ADI) and acute reference dose (ARfD)) were derived: – acceptable daily intake (ADI) is 0.05 mg/kg bw per day, based on the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of 5 mg/kg bw per day for increase relative/absolute liver and statistically significant relative testes weight at 15 mg/kg bw per day in the 1‐year study in dogs and applying an uncertainty factor of 100;– acute reference dose (ARfD) is 0.6 mg/kg bw based on the NOAEL of 60 mg/kg bw per day for the decreased body weight gain, decreased food consumption and total litter loss observed at 160 mg/kg bw per day in the developmental toxicity study in rats. An uncertainty factor of 100 was applied.

The current TRVs are set based on the critical effects identified in the mammalian toxicity studies including the reproductive toxicity endpoints that lead to the classification of the substance as Repr. Cat. 1B (EFSA, 2023).

Dimethomorph meets the criteria for endocrine disruptors for the endocrine active substance (EAS)‐modalities (Scenario 1b of the ECHA/EFSA ED Guidance, ECHA and EFSA, 2018) and the observed pattern of EAS‐mediated adversity suggests an anti‐androgenic mode of action (MoA).

The effects indicative of the pattern of EAS‐mediated adversity i.e. decrease in prostate and seminal vesicle weight, decreased anogenital distance (AGD) and delayed preputial separation, were observed at the average dose of 63 mg/kg bw per day (44.3–100.3 mg/kg bw per day) with a NOAEL set at the average dose of 23.4 mg/kg bw per day (16.5–37.7 mg/kg bw per day) in the extended one generation study conducted in the rat. In the 90‐day and in the 1‐year dog studies, a decreased prostate weight and an increase in prostatic interstitial fibrosis were found at 43 and 47 mg/kg bw per day. The NOAEL for these effects was set at 15 mg/kg bw per day. At the same dose of 15 mg/kg bw per day, a statistically significant increase of relative testes weight accompanied by a non‐statistically significant increase in absolute testes weight was observed in the 1‐year dog study; however, the effect direction is considered unlikely to be associated to an anti‐androgenic effect which is the postulated MoA for dimethomorph based on the available evidence for adversity and activity.

Taking into account the current ADI value (0.05 mg/kg bw per day) and its basis and considering that hazards indicative of endocrine disruption were not manifested after acute exposure to the active substance, it is concluded that the current TRVs are sufficiently protective for consumers, including the identified hazard indicative of endocrine disruption.

To address term of reference 2, EFSA identified all the CXLs and import tolerances that are covered by the existing EU MRLs. In the framework of this targeted assessment, the import tolerance uses of dimethomorph reported in EFSA assessments (EFSA, 2012, 2016b) and the uses of dimethomorph previously assessed by the JMPR (FAO and WHO, 2007, 2014, 2016) were considered. It is highlighted that as the European Union expressed a reservation on the Codex MRLs for fruiting vegetables other than cucurbits because of different extrapolation policies used by the JMPR and in EU and for beans with pods due to concerns on the maturity stage of the commodity under concern (CAC, 2015), these CXLs were not considered further in the assessment.

Chronic and acute exposure calculations were performed using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018a, 2019). For the risk assessment EFSA considered the following residue definition: dimethomorph (sum of isomers) derived in the framework of the first approval and confirmed during the renewal. It is noted that for ruminant commodities metabolite M550F021 has been proposed by the renewal to be included in the residue definition for risk assessment, pending further assessment of its genotoxic potential 10 (EFSA, 2023). Nevertheless, as no significant residues are expected in livestock, consideration of this metabolite in the risk assessment is not relevant. The calculated exposure levels were compared with the toxicological reference values for dimethomorph derived by EFSA in the framework of the renewal for the approval of the active substance and confirmed in the present assessment. A screenshot of the report sheet of the PRIMo 3.1 is presented in Appendix A. Input values as derived in the previous MRL assessments and by the JMPR, were considered for the exposure calculations and are summarised in Appendix B.

The highest chronic exposure was calculated for the Dutch toddler, representing 17% of the ADI. The highest acute exposure was calculated for lettuces, accounting for up to 68% of the ARfD for children. Therefore, no consumers intake concerns were identified for the MRLs based on CXLs and/or import tolerances.

In addition, to assist risk managers in deciding on transitional measures and as requested by term of reference 3, a screening of existing MRLs was performed by using EFSA PRIMo ver. 3.1, considering the highest residue (HR) as derived in previous EFSA outputs (EFSA, 2011a, 2011b, 2012, 2013, 2016a, 2016b) and JMPR evaluations (FAO and WHO, 2007, 2014). Considering the scope of the screening, this calculation was limited to the acute exposure. According to the results of this calculation, the highest acute exposure was calculated for lettuces (68% of ARfD). Therefore, no acute concerns were identified for the existing MRLs. A screenshot of the report sheet of the PRIMo 3.1 is presented in Appendix A.

In order to address term of reference 4, the draft statement was shared with Member States for consultation in September 2024. All comments received were collated in the Member States Consultation report (EFSA, 2024). The Member States consultation report and the chronic and acute exposure calculations performed using the EFSA PRIMo 3.1 are made publicly available as supporting documents to this statement.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION

EFSA confirmed that the toxicological reference values derived in 2023 are expected to be protective for endocrine disruption. Overall, the exposure calculations performed by EFSA showed that the existing EU MRLs, including the ones based on CXLs or import tolerances do not lead to consumers intake concerns.

Abstract
. We study the dynamics of multidimensional contracted rotations and address a problem posed by Y. Bugeaud and J-P. Conze in Acta Arithmetica in 1999. More precisely, we show that if A A is an invertible linear contraction of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , then the map f : [ 0 , 1 ) d → [ 0 , 1 ) d f:[0,1)^{d}\to[0,1)^{d} defined by f ⁡ ( x ) = A ​ x + b ⁡ ( mod ​ ℤ d ) f(x)=Ax+b\,\,(\textrm{mod}\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}) is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost all b ∈ ℝ d b\in\mathbb{R}^{d} .



.

1.
Introduction and Main Result

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the multidimensional contracted rotation is the map
f\colon[0,1)^{d}\to[0,1)^{d}
which assigns to every
x\in[0,1)^{d}
the vector

(1)

f(x):=Ax+b\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},

where
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}
(i.e.
A
is an invertible
d\times d
matrix),
\|A\|<1
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here
\|A\|=\sup_{x\neq 0}\|Ax\|\big/\|x\|
and
\|x\|=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|x_{j}|
for all
x=(x_{j})
.

The
\omega
-limit set
of
f
is defined by

(2)

\omega(f):=\bigcup_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}\omega(f,x),\quad\textrm{where}\quad\omega(f,x):=\bigcap_{m\geq 1}\overline{\bigcup_{n\geq m}{\left\{f^{n}(x)\right\}}}

denotes the
\omega
-limit set of a point
x\in[0,1)^{d}
under
f
.

We say that
f
is
asymptotically periodic
if
\omega(f)
is the union of finitely many periodic orbits of
f
.

In this article, we address a problem stated by Y. Bugeaud and J-P. Conze in
[
4
, p. 218]
. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}
with
\|A\|<1
.
For Lebesgue almost every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the map
f
defined by (
1
) is asymptotically periodic.

The claim of Theorem
1
and much more is known to be true in the one-dimensional case
(d=1)
, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
. Regarding the multidimensional case
(d>1)
, no results are currently known, as progress on this problem depends on advances on the understanding of multidimensional piecewise contractions, which were obtained only very recently, see
[
7
]
. Although a few other results on multidimensional piecewise contractions are available, see
[
1
,
6
,
8
,
9
]
, they cannot be applied to the parametrized families of piecewise contractions that arise in multidimensional contracted rotations.

2.
Proofs

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on some results provided in
[
7
]
. More precisely, we need a version of
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
for the particular case in which the family of maps is derived from (
1
). We will introduce all the background necessary to put the current problem into the framework of
[
7
]
.

Throughout this section, fix
A=(a_{j\ell})\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}
with
\|A\|=\max_{j}\sum_{\ell}|a_{j\ell}|<1
. We will consider
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a parameter.
In this way, the map
f
in (
1
) becomes the
b
-parameter
family of maps

f_{b}:[0,1)^{d}\to[0,1)^{d}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined by

(3)

f_{b}(x):=Ax+b\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

Denoting by
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
the component-wise floor function and by
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the component-wise fractional part, then (
3
) reads

(4)

f_{b}(x)=\{Ax+b\}=Ax+b-\lfloor Ax+b\rfloor,\quad\forall x\in[0,1)^{d}.

Figure 1.
Example taking
A=\begin{pmatrix}0.8&0.1\\
0.5&0.4\end{pmatrix}
and
b=\begin{pmatrix}0.3\\
0.4\end{pmatrix}
. In the left-hand-side the image of
f_{b}
and in the right-hand-side the discontinuity set of
f_{b}
.

The
code
of
f_{b}
is the function
e_{b}\colon[0,1)^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
defined by

(5)

e_{b}(x):=\lfloor Ax+b\rfloor,\quad\forall\,x\in[0,1)^{d}.

The following properties are elementary to check.

Lemma 1
.

For every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1)

f_{b+p}=f_{b}
for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(2)

f_{b}
is injective,

(3)

e_{\{b\}}([0,1)^{d})\subset\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
.

Proof.

We only give a proof of (2) and (3) as (1) is obvious.
Let
x,y\in[0,1)^{d}
be such that
f(x)=f(y)
. By (
4
),
Ax+b-e_{b}(x)=Ay+b-e_{b}(y)
, which gives
A(y-x)=e_{b}(y)-e_{b}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. But,

\|e_{b}(y)-e_{b}(x)\|=\|A(y-x)\|\leq\|A\|\|y-x\|<1,
for every
x,y\in[0,1)^{d}
. Thus,
e_{b}(y)=e_{b}(x)
, which gives
x=y
. This shows (2). Regarding (3), since
\|Ax\|<1
for every
x\in[0,1)^{d}
, we have
Ax+\{b\}\in(-1,2)^{d}
. Thus,
\lfloor Ax+\{b\}\rfloor\subset\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
, which shows (3).
∎

Set

E_{b}(p):=e_{b}^{-1}(p)=\{x\in[0,1)^{d}\colon e_{b}(x)=p\},\quad p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Clearly, these sets are disjoint and for each
p=(p_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

E_{b}(p)=\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}E^{(j)}_{b}(p),\quad E^{(j)}_{b}(p):=\{x\in[0,1)^{d}\colon p_{j}\leq(Ax+b)_{j}<p_{j}+1\}.

Whenever
E_{b}(p)\neq\emptyset
, we have
f(x)=Ax+b-p
for every
x\in E_{p}(b)
. The regions
E_{b}(p)
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},
are called the
domains of continuity
of
f_{b}
.

Notice that, by (3) of Lemma
1
,
E_{\{b\}}(p)=\emptyset
for all
p\notin\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
. In particular, this shows that
\#\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon E_{b}(p)\neq\emptyset\}\leq 3^{d}
. However, as we shall see in the following lemma, the number of non-empty regions
E_{b}(p)
is much smaller. For instance, in the example of Figure
1
,
E_{b}(p)\neq\emptyset
iff
p\in\{0,1\}^{2}
.

Let
\chi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the function

(6)

\chi(b):=\min_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}e_{b}(x)=\min_{x\in[0,1)^{d}}\lfloor Ax+b\rfloor,

where the minimum is taken component-wise. If
A
has non-negative entries, then
\chi(b)=\lfloor b\rfloor
.

Lemma 2
.

(1)

\chi(b)=\chi(\{b\})+\lfloor b\rfloor
for every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2)

\chi(b)\in\{-1,0\}^{d}
for every
b\in[0,1)^{d}
,

(3)

[0,1)^{d}=\bigcup_{p\in\{0,1\}^{d}}E_{b}(\chi(b)+p)
for every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(4)

For every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

E_{b}^{(j)}(\chi(b)+p)=\begin{cases}\{x\in[0,1)^{d}\colon(Ax+b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+1\},&\textrm{if}\quad p_{j}=0\\
\{x\in[0,1)^{d}\colon(Ax+b)_{j}\geq(\chi(b))_{j}+1\},&\textrm{if}\quad p_{j}=1\end{cases}

Proof.

Properties (1) and (2) are obvious. Let us prove (3) and (4).
For every
x,y\in[0,1]^{d}
,
|(Ax+b)_{j}-(Ay+b)_{j}|\leq\|A\|\|x-y\|\leq\|A\|<1
. This implies that, for every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the set
\{(Ax+b)_{j}\colon x\in[0,1)^{d}\}
contains at most one integer. Thus, by (
6
), we conclude that
(\chi(b))_{j}\leq(Ax+b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+2
for every
x\in[0,1)^{d}
. This shows (4) and also shows (3) since, for every
x\in[0,1)^{d}
, we have
x\in E_{b}(\chi(b)+p)
for some
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Lemma
2
shows that the discontinuities of
f_{b}
partition
[0,1)^{d}
into at most
2^{d}
regions, i.e.
\#\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon E_{b}(p)\neq\emptyset\}\leq 2^{d}
. Notice that, we are not claiming that all
E_{b}(\chi(b)+p)
are non-empty. In fact, it is easy to construct examples of
A
and
b
where
f_{b}
has a single domain of continuity.

2.1.
Change of variables

In order to prove Theorem
1
, it is convenient to change variables using the linear translation
h_{b}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

(7)

h_{b}(y):=y+(I-A)^{-1}b,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
D_{b}:=h_{b}^{-1}([0,1)^{d})
denote the translated unit hypercube. The map
f_{b}
in the new variables
y=h_{b}^{-1}(x)
is
g_{b}\colon D_{b}\to D_{b}
defined by
g_{b}:=h_{b}^{-1}\circ f_{b}\circ h_{b}
. Obviously,
f_{b}
is asymptotically periodic iff
g_{b}
is as well.

Lemma 3
.

g_{b}(y)=Ay-e_{b}(h_{b}(y))
for every
y\in D_{b}
.

Proof.

Clearly, for each
y\in D_{b}
,

\displaystyle g_{b}(y)

\displaystyle=f_{b}(h_{b}(y))-(I-A)^{-1}b

\displaystyle=Ah_{b}(y)+b-\lfloor Ah_{b}(y)+b\rfloor-(I-A)^{-1}b

\displaystyle=Ay+A(I-A)^{-1}b+b-(I-A)^{-1}b-\lfloor Ah_{b}(y)+b\rfloor

\displaystyle=Ay-\lfloor Ah_{b}(y)+b\rfloor,

since
A(I-A)^{-1}b+b-(I-A)^{-1}b=(A+(I-A)-I)(I-A)^{-1}b=0
.
∎

The domains of continuity of
g_{b}
are

(8)

C_{b}^{p}:=h_{b}^{-1}(E_{b}(\chi(b)+p)),\quad p\in\{0,1\}^{d}.

Whenever
C_{b}^{p}\neq\emptyset
, we have
g_{b}(y)=Ay-(\chi(b)+p)
for every
y\in C_{b}^{p}
.

2.2.
Change of parameters

In order to describe the domains
C_{b}^{p}
as in the setup of
[
7
]
, we introduce the following change of parameters.

Definition 1
.

Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the map
b\mapsto\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d})
where

\mu_{j}=\frac{(\chi(b)-(I-A)^{-1}b)_{j}+1}{\|A^{({j})}\|},\quad 1\leq j\leq d,

where
A^{(j)}
denotes the
j
-th row of
A
.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, consider the following family of hyperplanes,

(9)

H_{j}(\mu):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle v^{(j)},x\rangle=\mu_{j}\right\},\quad 1\leq j\leq d

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean inner product and
v^{(j)}:=\frac{A^{(j)}}{\|A^{(j)}\|}
are unit vectors. Associated to this family of hyperplanes we have
the
label map

\sigma_{\mu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}
defined by
\sigma_{\mu}(x)=(s_{1}(x),\ldots,s_{d}(x))
where

s_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{if }\langle v^{(j)},x\rangle<\mu_{j}\\
-1,&\text{if }\langle v^{(j)},x\rangle\geq\mu_{j}\end{cases},

and
\mathcal{A}=\{-1,+1\}^{d}
.
For simplicity, set
(-1)^{p}=((-1)^{p_{1}},\ldots,(-1)^{p_{d}})\in\mathcal{A}
for any
p=(p_{j})\in\{0,1\}^{d}
.

Lemma 4
.

For every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
,

C_{b}^{p}=D_{b}\cap\sigma^{-1}_{\rho(b)}((-1)^{p}).

Proof.

Suppose that
p_{j}=0
for some
1\leq j\leq d
. By (
8
) and by (4) of Lemma
2
we get,

\displaystyle h_{b}^{-1}(E_{b}^{(j)}(\chi(b)+p))

\displaystyle=\{h_{b}^{-1}(x)\colon x\in[0,1)^{d},\,(Ax+b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+1\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in D_{b}\colon(Ah_{b}(y)+b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+1\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in D_{b}\colon(Ay+(A(I-A)^{-1}+I)b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+1\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in D_{b}\colon(Ay+(I-A)^{-1}b)_{j}<(\chi(b))_{j}+1\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in D_{b}\colon\langle A^{(j)},y\rangle<(\chi(b)-(I-A)^{-1}b)_{j}+1\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in D_{b}\colon\langle v^{(j)},y\rangle<\mu_{j}\}

\displaystyle=D_{b}\cap s_{j}^{-1}((-1)^{p_{j}}).

Similarly, for
p_{j}=1
, we get
h_{b}^{-1}(E_{b}^{(j)}(\chi(b)+p))=D_{b}\cap s_{j}^{-1}((-1)^{p_{j}})
.

∎

The next step is to extend
g_{b}
to a piecewise-affine contraction map defined on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The extension is the obvious one.

2.3.
Extension map
G_{\mu}
.

Throughout this subsection we fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Recall that
\mathcal{A}=\{-1,+1\}^{d}
.

Let
\{\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
be the family of affine contractions

\varphi_{i}(y)=Ay-(k+p),\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that
(-1)^{p}=i
.

Define

r:=\frac{(1+\|A\|)(1+\|k\|)}{(1-\|A\|)^{2}}.

Lemma 5
.

\|y\|\leq 2r\Longrightarrow\|\varphi_{i}(y)\|<2r
for all
i\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Each
\varphi_{i}
has a unique fixed point
z_{i}=-(I-A)^{-1}(k+p)
. Notice that
\|z_{i}\|\leq\frac{1+\|k\|}{1-\|A\|}
for all
i\in\mathcal{A}
. Then, given
\|y\|\leq 2r
, we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{i}(y)\|

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{i}(y)-\varphi_{i}(z_{i})+z_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(y-z_{i})\|+\|z_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|A\|r+(1+\|A\|)\|z_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|A\|r+\frac{(1+\|A\|)(1+\|k\|)}{1-\|A\|}

\displaystyle=2\|A\|r+(1-\|A\|)r

\displaystyle=(1+\|A\|)r

\displaystyle<2r.

∎

Let

X=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\|y\|\leq 2r\}.

By Lemma
5
,
\varphi_{i}(X)\subset\mathrm{int}(X)
. Therefore,
given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can define
G_{\mu}\colon X\to X
by

G_{\mu}(y)=\varphi_{\sigma_{\mu}(y)}(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The map
G_{\mu}
is a piecewise-affine contraction as defined in
[
7
, Equation (8)]
. In order to state the result we need from
[
7
]
, we have to introduce the notions of regular point and asymptotic periodicity on a forward invariant set.

Definition 2

(regular point)
.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
a point
y\in X
is called
a regular point of

G_{\mu}
if

G_{\mu}^{n}(y)\in\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\mathrm{int}(\sigma_{\mu}^{-1}(\alpha)),\quad\forall\,n\geq 0.

The set of regular points of
G_{\mu}
is denoted by
Z_{\mu}
.

It is an elementary fact that
Z_{\mu}
is forward invariant by
G_{\mu}
.

Definition 3

(asymptotic periodicity on a set)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

Z\subset X
be a set forward invariant by
G_{\mu}
. We say that
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic on
Z
if
\bigcup_{z\in Z}\omega(G_{\mu},z)
is the union of finitely many periodic orbits of
G_{\mu}
.

Notice that if
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic on
Z=X
, then
G_{\mu}
is called asymptotically periodic, as it has been defined in the Introduction.

Theorem 2

(Theorem 2.2 of
[
7
]
)
.

The map
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic on
Z_{\mu}
for Lebesgue almost every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now, we generalise the arguments used in the proof of
[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
to obtain the following stronger result.

Theorem 3
.

The map
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
, there is a full Lebesgue measure set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the map
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic on
Z_{\mu}
for every
\mu\in W
. So, since
Z_{\mu}
is forward invariant by
G_{\mu}
, it remains to show that for every
y\in X
, there is
n\geq 0
such that
G_{\mu}^{n}(y)\in Z_{\mu}
. To prove this, we need to introduce some more terminology.

Given
\alpha=(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1})\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, the map
\varphi^{\alpha}:=\varphi_{i_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{i_{0}}
satisfies

\varphi^{\alpha}(y)=A^{n}y+r_{\alpha},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
r_{\alpha}=\varphi^{\alpha}(0)
. Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\geq 1
, define the collection of hyperplanes

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(n)}:=\{(\varphi^{\alpha})^{-1}(H_{j}(\mu))\colon\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{k},\,0\leq k<n,\,1\leq j\leq d\},

where
\varphi^{0}=\mathrm{id}
is the identity map and
\mathcal{A}^{0}=\{0\}
. By
[
7
, Lemma 5.8]
,

(10)

\forall\,\mu\in W,\quad\sup_{n\geq 1}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\#\{H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{(n)}\colon x\in H\}<+\infty.

Let

\Lambda=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda\textrm{ is a real eingenvalue of $A^{n}$}\}.

Because
\|A\|<1
, we have that

\Lambda\subset(-1,1)
.
Given integers
1\leq m<n
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{A}^{n},

let

N_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\exists\,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\,\exists\,\lambda\in\Lambda\mid\mu_{j}=\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\langle r_{\alpha}-\lambda r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle\right\},

where
N_{\alpha,\beta}=\emptyset
if
A
has no real eigenvalues. The set
N_{\alpha,\beta}
is a union of finitely many hyperplanes. Thus,
N_{\alpha,\beta}
has zero Lebesgue measure.
Set

W_{1}=W{\bigg\backslash}\left(\bigcup_{m\geq 1}\bigcup_{n>m}\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{m}}\bigcup_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}N_{\alpha,\beta}\right).

Then,
W_{1}
has full Lebesgue measure.

Now, the rest of the proof goes by contradiction. Let
\mu\in W_{1}
and suppose that there is
y\in X
such that
G_{\mu}^{n}(y)\notin Z_{\mu}
for all
n\geq 0
. Since
G_{\mu}(X)\subset\mathrm{int}(X)
, the orbit
\{G^{n}_{\mu}(y)\}_{n\geq 0}
is contained in the union of hyperplanes
\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}(\mu)
. By the pigeon-hole principle, there must exist
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
\{G^{n}_{\mu}(y)\}_{n\geq 0}
intersects the hyperplane
H_{j}(\mu)
infinitely many times, i.e.,
G^{n}_{\mu}(y)\in H_{j}(\mu)
for infinitely many
n\geq 1
. Equivalently, there exist infinitely many
n\geq 1
such that
y\in(\varphi^{\alpha})^{-1}(H_{j}(\mu))
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
. By (
10
), there exist positive integers
m<n
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
such that
y
belongs to the coincident hyperplanes

(11)

(\varphi^{\beta})^{-1}(H_{j}(\mu))=(\varphi^{\alpha})^{-1}(H_{j}(\mu)).

In terms of vectors, we have that for some
\lambda\neq 0
,

(A^{n})^{t}v^{(j)}=\lambda(A^{m})^{t}v^{(j)}.

Since
A
is invertible, we have that

(A^{n-m})^{t}v^{(j)}=\lambda v^{(j)}
, thus
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Moreover, because
y
belongs to both hyperplanes in (
11
), we have that

\displaystyle\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=\langle A^{n}y+r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A^{n}y,v^{(j)}\rangle+\langle r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,(A^{n})^{t}v^{(j)}\rangle+\langle r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,\lambda(A^{m})^{t}v^{(j)}\rangle+\langle r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda\langle A^{m}y,v^{(j)}\rangle+\langle r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda(\mu_{j}-\langle r_{\alpha},v^{(j)}\rangle)+\langle r_{\beta},v^{(j)}\rangle,

which gives
(1-\lambda)\mu_{j}=\langle r_{\beta}-\lambda r_{\alpha},v^{(j)}\rangle
. Thus,
\mu\in N_{\alpha,\beta}
, yielding a contraction.
∎

2.4.
Proof of Theorem
1

By (1) of Lemma
1
, we may suppose that
b\in[0,1)^{d}
.
By (2) of Lemma
2
, the function
\chi
is piecewise constant in
[0,1)^{d}
taking values in
\{-1,0\}^{d}
. Therefore, given any
k\in\{-1,0\}^{d}
, let
U_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the pre-image of
k
under
\chi
, i.e.
U_{k}:=\chi^{-1}(k)
. In order to prove Theorem
1
, since
[0,1)^{d}=\bigcup_{k\in\{-1,0\}^{d}}U_{k}
, it is enough to show that
f_{b}
is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost every
b\in U_{k}
. Changing variables, we just need to show that
g_{b}
is asymptotically periodic for Lebesgue almost every
b\in U_{k}
(see Section
2.1
).

Now, we show that
G_{\mu}
is an extension of
g_{b}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
b\in[0,1)^{d}
. Then
D_{b}\subset X
and
G_{\mu}|_{D_{b}}=g_{b}
provided
k=\chi(b)
and
\mu=\rho(b)
.

Proof.

First, we show the inclusion
D_{b}\subset X
. Recall the definition of
D_{b}=h_{b}^{-1}([0,1)^{d})
. Given any
y\in D_{b}
we have
y=x-(I-A)^{-1}b
for some
x\in[0,1)^{d}
. This gives
\|y\|\leq 1+\frac{\|b\|}{1-\|A\|}\leq\frac{2-\|A\|}{1-\|A\|}
. But, a simple computation shows that

\frac{2-\|A\|}{1-\|A\|}<2\frac{(1+\|A\|)(1+\|k\|)}{(1-\|A\|)^{2}},\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Thus,
\|y\|<2r
and
D_{b}\subset X
.

Now, we show that
G_{\mu}
is an extension of
g_{b}
. Let
y\in D_{b}
. Since
\{C_{b}^{p}\}_{p\in\{0,1\}^{d}}
is a partition of
D_{b}
, there must exist
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
such that
y\in C_{b}^{p}
. for some
p\in\{0,1\}^{d}
. Then, by Lemma
4
,
\sigma_{\rho(b)}(y)=(-1)^{p}
, which gives

G_{\rho(b)}(y)=\varphi_{\sigma_{\rho(b)}(y)}(y)=\varphi_{(-1)^{p}}(y)=Ay-(\chi(b)+p)=g_{b}(y),

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3
.
∎

Using this lemma, for any
b\in U_{k}
, we have
k=\chi(b)
and
g_{b}
equals the restriction of
G_{\rho(b)}
on
D_{b}
. But, by Theorem
3
, there is a set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of full Lebesgue measure such that
G_{\mu}
is asymptotically periodic for every
\mu\in W
. Therefore, since
(\rho(b))_{j}=\frac{(k-(I-A)^{-1}b)_{j}+1}{\|A^{(j)}\|}
for
b\in U_{k}
, we see that
\rho|_{U_{k}}
is an injective affine transformation. Thus,
(\rho|_{U_{k}})^{-1}(W)
has full Lebesgue measure in
U_{k}
and we have proven Theorem
1
.∎

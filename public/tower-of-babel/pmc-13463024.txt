SDEF-BEV: spatial-aware dual-expert radar-camera fusion for robust BEV 3D object detection
Authors: Jinhao Li, Xueying Bai, Quanyi Liu, Shenghua Xiong, Haibin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bird’s-eye view (BEV) radar-camera fusion is an economical and robust solution for 3D perception in autonomous driving. However, existing methods struggle with spatial misalignment between modalities and lack the ability to dynamically adjust the contribution of each modality across different spatial regions in complex scenarios. To address these issues, we propose SDEF-BEV, a novel spatial-aware dual-expert fusion network. At its core, SDEF-BEV adopts a parallel dual-path fusion architecture. The Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module adaptively learns optimal feature fusion strategies via two specialized experts (semantic-enhanced and geometry-enhanced) and a spatial gating network, generating location-wise fusion weights. Meanwhile, a parallel Channel and Spatial Fusion (CSF) path preserves rich spatial context through convolutional inductive biases. The outputs of these two paths are aggregated to form unified and information-rich fused features. Experiments on the nuScenes dataset demonstrate that SDEF-BEV achieves an NDS of 57.1% and an mAP of 45.7%, showing competitive performance. Extensive ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of the parallel SDEF architecture, and the overall approach exhibits strong robustness, particularly under challenging adverse weather conditions.

Introduction

The perception module of autonomous driving systems relies on multi-sensor fusion to achieve an accurate and reliable understanding of the surrounding environment. Among various sensors, LiDAR has become the preferred choice for high-performance systems due to its precise depth measurement capabilities, but its high cost limits widespread adoption1. In contrast, millimeter-wave radar offers low cost, precise distance and velocity information, and stable performance in adverse weather conditions; cameras provide rich semantic and textural information. Integrating radar and camera data thus presents an ideal solution combining affordability and robustness2,3.

In recent years, converting multi-view camera features into bird’s-eye view (BEV) representations has become the mainstream paradigm for perception tasks4–6. The BEV space provides a unified, obstruction-free representation for cameras and radars from different viewpoints, greatly facilitating feature fusion. Works such as RCBEVDet7 demonstrate the immense potential of radar-camera BEV fusion. However, current fusion methods still face two core challenges: (1) Spatial misalignment: The inherent sparsity and azimuth measurement errors in radar point clouds make it difficult to precisely spatially align the generated BEV features with image BEV features; (2) Static fusion: Widely adopted fusion strategies such as serial concatenation, summation, or squeeze-and-excitation (SE)8 are essentially static and passive. They cannot dynamically adjust the contribution weights of radar and camera features based on specific scene content (e.g., visibility, target density, weather conditions), thereby limiting the model’s adaptability and performance in complex scenarios.

Recently, dynamic fusion strategies9 have enhanced adaptability to multimodal information by introducing gating mechanisms. However, these approaches generally follow a sequential processing paradigm (align-first, then weight-and-fuse). This paradigm carries two potential limitations: (1) Error accumulation risk: Minor deviations during the alignment phase may be amplified in subsequent fusion stages; (2) Information bottlenecks: Features processed through a single path after alignment may fail to simultaneously capture both global semantic relationships and local spatial details. Inspired by the need for fine-grained, spatially adaptive fusion, we propose SDEF-BEV. Our core idea is to feed aligned bimodal features in parallel into a Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module and a Channel-Spatial Fusion (CSF) module. The SDEF module enables the model to perform location-wise adaptive fusion by employing two heterogeneous expert networks (semantic and geometric) and a lightweight spatial gating network that generates pixel-wise weighting maps. This allows the model to emphasize camera semantics in clear, near-range regions and rely more on radar geometry in distant or adverse-weather areas. Meanwhile, the CSF path preserves critical spatial details via convolutional strong inductive biases. This parallel design enables the model to process content-adaptive expert selection and local detail enhancement concurrently, thereby achieving more robust and context-sensitive feature fusion. Related work

Radar-camera 3D object detection

Three-dimensional object detection is a core task in autonomous driving perception systems, with its development reflecting a technological evolution from reliance on single sensors to the adoption of multi-sensor fusion. Early research primarily relied on high-precision 3D point cloud data from LiDAR. Representative works include the columnar encoding scheme proposed by PointPillars11 and the voxel feature extraction method adopted by VoxelNet12, which achieved remarkable performance on standard datasets such as KITTI and nuScenes13,14. Tao et al.15 further improved PointPillars’ detection accuracy on the KITTI dataset by introducing an attention mechanism and an anchor-free detection head, providing an optimization approach for pure point cloud detection. However, cost constraints for LiDAR sensors in mass production applications have driven researchers to explore alternative sensing solutions1.

With the advancement of deep learning technology, vision-based 3D detection methods have gradually matured. Monocular 3D detection approaches such as FCOS3D16 and MonoPair17 focus on inferring depth information from a single image; multi-view 3D detection methods enhance spatial perception capabilities by fusing multi-camera perspective information. Pioneering works like BEVDet4 and BEVFormer18 project image features into bird’s-eye view space through perspective transformations, laying crucial foundations for subsequent research. BEVDepth6 further improves performance by introducing explicit depth supervision, while SOLOFusion19 and StreamPETR20 effectively leverage temporal information, continually pushing the boundaries of pure vision-based 3D detection.

While exploring visual solutions, pure millimeter-wave radar detection has also gradually gained attention. Radar sensors possess unique advantages such as high measurement accuracy, relatively low cost, and insensitivity to weather conditions21,22. However, radar point cloud data is sparse and lacks rich semantic information, posing significant challenges for high-precision detection tasks. Although existing research has proposed detection methods specifically designed for radar characteristics, such as RadarPillars23, their performance still struggles to fully meet the stringent accuracy demands of autonomous driving scenarios. Consequently, researchers have increasingly turned to low-cost fusion solutions combining cameras and millimeter-wave radar. Cameras provide rich texture and semantic information but suffer from inherent limitations in depth estimation and are susceptible to lighting and weather conditions24,25. Radar and cameras exhibit complementary sensing capabilities: radar excels in reliable ranging, velocity measurement, and weather resilience, while cameras demonstrate superiority in semantic understanding and scene interpretation. Integrating information from these two modalities to balance cost-effectiveness and robustness has become a critical research direction for autonomous driving perception systems.

In radar-camera fusion detection, researchers have proposed multiple innovative architectures. MVFusion26 achieves fusion of radar and camera features through semantic alignment, facilitating cross-modal information exchange at the feature level. CRN5 generates enhanced images using depth information provided by radar, effectively improving the accuracy of multi-view transformations. RCBEVDet7 designed a dedicated dual-stream radar backbone network and an RCS-aware BEV encoder, significantly enhancing radar feature extraction capabilities. These approaches explore the complementary advantages of radar and camera characteristics through different technical pathways, providing important references for multimodal fusion detection.

Radar-camera fusion strategies

Radar-camera fusion strategies can be broadly categorized into result-level fusion and feature-level fusion based on the level at which fusion occurs. Result-level fusion, as the early mainstream paradigm, typically employs a staged processing approach: first generating preliminary detection results based on a single modality (usually images), then associating radar detection information with these results for refinement. Representative work CenterFusion27 first generates detection results in images via a keypoint detection network. It then employs a histogram-based radar association module to match radar points with detected objects, ultimately optimizing target attributes such as velocity and position. While relatively straightforward to implement, this approach heavily relies heavily on the accuracy of the front-end detector and operates at a coarse fusion granularity, limiting its ability to fully leverage the complementary potential of multimodal information.

Feature-level fusion integrates information during the feature extraction phase rather than the decision-making phase, enabling deeper exploration of intrinsic correlations among multimodal data. With the rise of bird’s-eye view (BEV) representations, feature-level fusion has gained an ideal implementation platform. BEVFusion8 projects LiDAR and camera features onto BEV space and employs feature stitching for fusion, pioneering a new direction for multimodal fusion in BEV space. RCBEVDet7 introduces a deformable cross-attention mechanism to achieve dynamic alignment between radar and camera features, significantly enhancing fusion quality. CRN5 addresses the issue at the data level by generating enhanced images using radar depth information, thereby improving multi-view transformation accuracy. DAD-Fusion28 operates at the feature level, utilizing a dynamic alignment module and diffusion model to optimize BEV features, thereby enhancing the robustness of multimodal fusion.

In recent years, researchers have begun exploring more refined and adaptive fusion mechanisms. ACAGN9 achieves adaptive feature weighting by introducing a gating network on top of a deformable cross-attention mechanism. CRAFT14 proposes a Spatial Context Fusion Transformer for fine-grained cross-modal information exchange at the proposal level. RCFusion29 designs a weighted fusion module for soft weighting of radar and camera BEV features. These studies offer novel perspectives for multimodal fusion. However, existing methods predominantly adopt a serial architecture of “align first, then weight,” which still faces challenges in flexibility and error control. Recently, dynamic networks and spatially adaptive mechanisms have demonstrated strong potential30,31. In addition, recent studies in broader vision and imaging domains suggest that upstream representation enhancement, such as super-resolution or hybrid enhancement, can improve downstream fusion or recognition under degraded input conditions32–34. Inspired by the need for finer spatial control, we propose the SDEF-BEV framework. By adopting a parallel architecture that incorporates a spatial gating mechanism and two dedicated experts, it preserves the spatial detail extraction capability of traditional convolutional networks while gaining the flexibility to dynamically adjust fusion strategies at each BEV grid location. This approach further enhances the robustness and adaptability of fusion results.

Methodology

This section presents our proposed SDEF-BEV model in detail. The core concept of this model is to effectively address the challenges of feature alignment and adaptive fusion between radar and camera in BEV space through a parallel dual-path fusion mechanism. The overall framework follows a sequence of feature encoding, feature alignment, feature fusion, and object detection, with the design of the fusion module representing the key innovation of our work.

Overall architecture

The overall architecture of SDEF-BEV inherits the image and radar branches from RCBEVDet (as shown in Fig. 2) and primarily comprises four tightly coupled components. First, during the multimodal feature encoding stage, the image branch extracts multi-view image features through a 2D backbone network with shared weights. These features are then transformed into a BEV-space representation using an LSS-based view transformation module. Simultaneously, the radar branch processes radar point cloud data using a dual-stream backbone network. One stream, based on PointNet++, captures fine-grained local features, while the other, based on Transformers, focuses on modeling global dependencies among points. Information exchange occurs through an injection-extraction module, ultimately generating radar BEV features via an RCS-aware BEV encoder.

Subsequently, during the feature alignment phase, we introduce a Deformable Cross-Attention module to dynamically establish spatial correspondences between the two modal features, thereby mitigating spatial misalignment issues caused by radar point cloud sparsity and noise. The subsequent feature fusion stage forms the core of our work. We designed a parallel fusion architecture that simultaneously feeds the aligned features into both the Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) branch and the Channel and Spatial Fusion (CSF) branch. The former employs a spatial gating mechanism and two specialized experts for location-wise adaptive fusion, while the latter preserves spatial details through convolutional operations. This parallel design overcomes potential error accumulation and information loss inherent in serial fusion approaches, enabling both paths to independently and fully extract distinct feature characteristics. The outputs from both paths are ultimately aggregated through summation to form a unified and information-rich fused feature. Finally, the aggregated fused feature is fed into a standard 3D detection head to complete tasks such as bounding box regression and class prediction.

Spatial-aware dual-expert fusion module

To fully leverage the strengths of both modalities at different spatial locations within the BEV representation, we design the Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module. Parallel fusion and aggregation

To fully leverage the strengths of the Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module while preserving crucial local spatial details, we design a parallel dual-path fusion architecture, as illustrated in Fig. 4. The core idea is that the SDEF path and the Channel and Spatial Fusion (CSF) path process the aligned features in parallel, with complementary functions, and their outputs are subsequently aggregated.

Fig. 4The structure of parallel fusion and aggregation. The aligned bimodal features are simultaneously fed into both the SDEF and CSF paths. The outputs from both paths are aggregated through element-wise summation to generate unified and information-rich fusion features.

The structure of parallel fusion and aggregation. The aligned bimodal features are simultaneously fed into both the SDEF and CSF paths. The outputs from both paths are aggregated through element-wise summation to generate unified and information-rich fusion features.

The Channel and Spatial Fusion (CSF) path aims to enhance local spatial consistency and contextual information through the strong inductive bias of convolutional networks. This path consists of a series of basic Convolution-BatchNorm-ReLU (CBR) blocks, with the first block incorporating a residual connection for training stability. Instead of performing complex cross-modal weighting, the CSF path focuses on extracting and reinforcing shared local patterns and spatial structures from the aligned features. We denote the output of the CSF path as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{CSF}$$\end{document}. This design ensures that the model maintains a stable foundation for spatial perception even in highly dynamic or noisy scenes.

The parallel design allows the SDEF and CSF paths to work synergistically with distinct responsibilities. The SDEF path performs location-wise, content-adaptive modality selection and enhancement through its spatial gating and heterogeneous expert networks. In contrast, the CSF path provides a detail-preserving and spatially smooth foundation. This architecture overcomes the potential information bottlenecks and error accumulation inherent in single serial fusion pipelines. Finally, the outputs of the two paths are aggregated via element-wise summation to form a unified and information-rich fused feature:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{fused}={F}_{SDEF}+{F}_{CSF}$$\end{document}

where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{SDEF}$$\end{document} is the spatially adaptive fused feature from the SDEF module, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{F}_{CSF}$$\end{document} is the spatially enhanced feature from the CSF path. This aggregation method is simple yet effective: it preserves the adaptive fusion advantages brought by the SDEF path while incorporating the local spatial consistency reinforced by the CSF path, ultimately providing a more robust and discriminative BEV representation for the downstream 3D detection head.

Experiments

Dataset and evaluation metrics

We comprehensively evaluated the proposed SDEF-BEV model on the widely used autonomous driving multimodal dataset nuScenes10. The dataset comprises 1,000 driving scenarios, each lasting approximately 20 s, covering diverse weather conditions (e.g., sunny, rainy, nighttime) and complex traffic environments. The dataset provides images from six cameras, 32-line LiDAR point clouds, millimeter-wave radar point clouds, and highly annotated 3D bounding boxes for ten object categories (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians, bicycles). Following the official partition, we used 700 scenes for training and 150 scenes for validation.

Regarding evaluation metrics, we adopt the nuScenes Detection Score (NDS) and mean Average Precision (mAP) as the primary performance indicators, as officially recommended by nuScenes. NDS is a comprehensive metric calculated using the following formula:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathrm{NDS}=\frac{1}{10}\left[5\cdot\:\mathrm{m}\mathrm{AP}+{\sum\:}_{\mathrm{mTP}\in\:\mathcal{T}}\left(1-\mathrm{min}\left(1,\mathrm{mTP}\right)\right)\right]$$\end{document}

Among these, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathcal{T}=\{\mathrm{mATE},\mathrm{mASE},\mathrm{mAOE},\mathrm{mAVE},\mathrm{mAAE}\}$$\end{document} represents five True Positive metrics, defined as follows: mATE: Mean Average Translation Error, mASE: Mean Average Scale Error, mAOE: Mean Average Orientation Error, mAVE: Mean Average Velocity Error, mAAE: Mean Average Attribute Error. These metrics collectively evaluate the accuracy of detection results in terms of localization, scale, orientation, velocity, and attribute classification. A higher NDS indicates superior overall performance, while a higher mAP signifies greater detection precision.

Implementation details

Our implementation is based on the open-source code repository RCBEVDet7. All experiments were conducted on eight NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPUs (24 GB VRAM per GPU). The model was trained for 15 epochs with a total batch size of 8. We employed the AdamW optimizer36 with a learning rate of 2e-4 and weight decay of 0.01, utilizing a cyclic learning rate scheduler37 to promote more stable convergence.

Following the common practice of current BEV-based detection methods4,6, we set the point cloud range to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\left[-51.2\mathrm{m},-51.2\mathrm{m},-5.0\mathrm{m}\right]\times\:\left[51.2\mathrm{m},51.2\mathrm{m},3.0\mathrm{m}\right]$$\end{document} to comprehensively cover the perceptible 3D space around the vehicle. The generated BEV feature map has a spatial size of 128 × 128. To prevent overfitting, we employed multiple data augmentation techniques, including random scaling [-0.06, 0.11], random rotation [-5.4°, 5.4°], and random horizontal flipping with a probability of 0.5. Additionally, global rotation and scaling were specifically applied to radar point clouds. Concurrently, a multi-scan radar fusion strategy was implemented, integrating information from up to eight consecutive sweeps per frame to enrich temporal context.

In terms of network architecture, we employ ResNet-5038 as the image backbone network, maintaining an input image resolution of 256 × 704. The radar stream follows the design of RadarBEVNet from RCBEVDet7, comprising a dual-stream backbone network and an RCS-aware BEV encoder. Our core contribution—the Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module—is embedded after the Deformable Cross-Attention Alignment module. The SDEF module consists of a spatial gating network and two specialized expert networks. The spatial gating network, implemented as a 3 × 3 convolution followed by a Softmax activation, generates two normalized weight maps that adaptively assign fusion importance to the camera and radar modalities at each BEV grid location. The two experts are structurally heterogeneous: the Semantic-Enhanced Expert for camera features employs a 5 × 5 convolution, a Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention module, and a 3 × 3 convolution; the Geometry-Enhanced Expert for radar features employs a 3 × 3 deformable convolution followed by a 1 × 1 convolution. The Channel-Spatial Fusion (CSF) path consists of multiple CBR (Conv 3 × 3, BatchNorm, ReLU) blocks, with the first block incorporating a residual connection.

Consistent with the baseline method7, we adopt a two-stage training strategy. In the first stage, we independently train the camera stream to obtain a robust image-based feature extractor6. Subsequently, in the second stage, we load the pre-trained weights of the camera stream and freeze its parameters, then jointly train the radar backbone network and our proposed dual-path fusion module. The loss function employs Gaussian Focal Loss39 for classification and L1 loss11 for bounding box regression.

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed SDEF-BEV model, we compare it with multiple state-of-the-art methods on the nuScenes validation dataset. As shown in Table 1, our approach achieves competitive performance across several key metrics.

Specifically, we primarily benchmark against the robust radar-camera fusion baseline model RCBEVDet7. This demonstrates the effectiveness of our introduced SDEF-BEV, which enables more intelligent integration of multimodal information. Concurrently, the core detection accuracy metric mAP also improved from 45.3% to 45.7%.

Conclusion and future work

In this paper, we proposed SDEF-BEV, a novel radar-camera BEV fusion method based on a parallel dual-path architecture. It employs a Spatial-Aware Dual-Expert Fusion (SDEF) module for location-wise adaptive weighting and enhancement of multimodal features and incorporates a Channel and Spatial Fusion (CSF) path to enhance local details. This approach effectively overcomes the spatial misalignment and rigid fusion strategy issues inherent in traditional methods. Experiments on the nuScenes dataset demonstrate that SDEF-BEV achieves a leading NDS of 57.1% and a competitive mAP of 45.7%, surpassing strong radar-camera fusion baselines such as RCBEVDet. It exhibits superior robustness and generalization capabilities, particularly in challenging scenarios like rainy and nighttime conditions. While this study validates the effectiveness of the dual-expert architecture with spatial gating, future work will explore extending the spatial-aware gating principle to more than two experts or tasks, and investigate more efficient expert designs for real-time applications. Future work will also investigate whether resolution-aware preprocessing or super-resolution-assisted camera enhancement can further strengthen adaptive BEV fusion, especially for small or distant objects under degraded sensing conditions.

1-D Schrödinger operator on a star graph with nondefinite weight function
Edison Leguizamón, Carsten Trunk, Mitsuru Wilson, Monika Winklmeier

Abstract
We investigate the indefinite Kirchhoff Laplacian on a star graph G G with n = n + + n − n=n_{+}+n_{-} edges of unit length, where the differential operator acts as − d 2 d ​ x 2 -\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}} on n + n_{+} edges and d 2 d ​ x 2 \frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}} on n − n_{-} edges. The operator is subject to Dirichlet conditions at the outer vertices and Kirchhoff conditions at the central vertex. We establish that this operator is similar to a self-adjoint operator in the Hilbert space L 2 ​ ( G ) L^{2}(G) and that its eigenfunctions constitute a Riesz basis. Additionally, we provide a complete characterization of its point spectrum.

Introduction

The study of differential operators on metric graphs has attracted considerable attention over the past decades. Much of the existing literature has focused on selfadjoint Schrödinger operators (see the monographs
[
5
,
6
,
26
]
and the seminal paper of V. Kostrykin and R. Schrader
[

The present work is motivated by the growing interest in spectral theory on network structures and by the renewed relevance of non-selfadjoint operators in quantum mechanics (see, e.g.,
[
21
]
). There have been recent attempts to formulate “quasi-Hermitian quantum mechanics”, where physical observables are represented by non-selfadjoint operators

(1.1)

for a bounded, boundedly invertible operator

[

In

on

in

defined on the Sobolev space

. This operator serves as one of the pioneering examples of a simple differential operator that, while not selfadjoint in the standard

-space over
\mathbb{R}
, is nevertheless spectrally well understood as it satisfies (
1.1
). From the view point of graphs, this operator is the (indefinite) Kirchhoff Laplacian defined on a star graph with two infinite edges (namely
\mathbb{R}^{-}
and
\mathbb{R}^{+}

[
10
]
for a broader class of such differential operators and to
[
17
]
for an overview, see also
[
9
]

The present paper can be viewed as a generalization of these results to graphs. It follows the way paved by the seminal paper of B. Ćurgus and H. Langer

10

Specifically, we study here the spectral properties of the indefinite Kirchhoff Laplacian on a star graph
G

n

We will work in the space of square integrable functions on

L^{2}(G):=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}L^{2}(I_{j})=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}L^{2}((0,1)).

(1.2)

We denote

for a function

Our goal is to investigate the spectral properties of the indefinite Kirchhoff Laplacian

Let us denote

\displaystyle(Bf)_{j}=\begin{cases}-f_{j}^{\prime\prime}\quad&\text{if }\ 1\leq j\leq n_{+},\\

Using boundary triple techniques and the associated Weyl function (see the next section below) we show in Section

In particular one can recover from the spectrum parts of the topology of the graph

The last result was already shown in

Preliminaries: Non-negative operators in Krein spaces

A Krein space is a complex vector space
{\mathcal{K}}
equipped with a sesquilinear form
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
such that there exist subspaces
{\mathcal{H}}_{+}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{-}
of
{\mathcal{K}}
such that both
({\mathcal{H}}_{-},\,-[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
and
({\mathcal{H}}_{+},\,[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
are Hilbert spaces.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{K}}
admits the decomposition

{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{H}}_{+}[\dot{+}]{\mathcal{H}}_{-},

(2.1)

as a direct sum and orthogonal with respect to
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
. This decomposition is called a
fundamental decomposition
of the Krein space
{\mathcal{K}}
. Let
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
be projections associated with the direct sum
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{H}}_{+}\dot{+}{\mathcal{H}}_{-}
. The operator

J:=P_{+}-P_{-}

is called a
fundamental symmetry
of the Krein space
{\mathcal{K}}
. Equipped with the inner product

\langle x,\,y\rangle=[Jx,y],\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{K}},

(2.2)

the space
{\mathcal{K}}
becomes a Hilbert space. Throughout, the topological structure of the Krein space
{\mathcal{K}}
is given with respect to the topology induced by this Hilbert space inner product. For the general theory of Krein spaces and operators acting in them, we refer to the monographs
[
3
,
7
,
18
]
.

Let
A
be a linear operator in a Krein space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
with a dense domain
\mathcal{D}(A)
. The
adjoint
of
A
with respect to the inner product
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
is denoted by
A^{+}
. The operator
A
is called
symmetric
in
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
if
A^{+}
is an extension of
A
and
A
is called
selfadjoint
in
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
if
A=A^{+}
. The operator
A
is called
non-negative
(
positive
) in
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
if
[Af,f]\geq 0

(resp.
[Af,f]>0
)
for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(A)
(for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(A)\setminus\{0\}
, respectively). Let
A
be a selfadjoint non-negative operator with a nonempty resolvent set
\rho(A)
. In this case, its spectrum is real (see
[
2
]
or
[
31
, Theorem II.3.1]
). Additionally, the operator
JA
is non-negative in the Hilbert space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle\bigr)
and has a well-defined square root
(JA)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

The decomposition of the underlying space
{\mathcal{K}}
in (
2.1
) is not unique, consequently neither is the inner product in (
2.2
). However, any two Banach space norms such that
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
is continuous are equivalent (see, e.g.,
[
31
]
). This naturally leads to the following question: When is a given selfadjoint operator
A=A^{+}
in a Krein space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
selfadjoint with respect to one of the possible Hilbert space inner products defined by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
)? This question can be formulated more generally: When is a given selfadjoint operator
A=A^{+}
in a Krein space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
similar to a selfadjoint operator in a Hilbert space?
Recall that
A
is similar to a densely defined operator
B
if

A=VBV^{-1}

for some bounded linear operator
V
with a bounded everywhere-defined inverse.
This is one of the big questions in operator theory in Krein spaces, with many significant positive results; for an overview, we mention
[
17
]
, see also
[
9
]
. For our purposes, it suffices to study non-negative operators
A
with
0\in\rho(A)
. We recall a result from Branko Ćurgus
[
8
]
in the version presented in
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be a non-negative operator in a Krein space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
with
0\in\rho(A)
. Then, the following two statements are equivalent.

(i)

There exists a bounded operator
W
, positive with respect to the Krein space inner product
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
with a bounded everywhere defined inverse such that

W\mathcal{D}\left((JA)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\subset\mathcal{D}\left((JA)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

(ii)

The operator
A
is similar to a selfadjoint operator in the Hilbert space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle\bigr)
, where
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle
is given by (
2.2
).

If the spectrum of
A
is discrete, the above statements
(i)
and
(ii)
are equivalent to the following statement.

(iii)

There exists an Riesz basis in
{\mathcal{K}}
consisting of eigenfunctions of
A
.

The aforementioned Riesz basis is an orthonormal basis with respect to some Hilbert space inner product, which is equivalent to
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle
in (
2.2
)
[
19
, Chapter VI §2]
.

We conclude this section by recalling some well-known facts from extension theory.
Let
S
be a closed densely defined symmetric operator in a Krein space
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
.
As in the Hilbert space case, we define the set of points of regular type of
S
by, see
[
1
]
,

\widehat{\rho}(S)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\text{exists }c_{z}>0\text{ with }\|(S-z)x\|>c_{z}\|x\|\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{D}(S)\}.

The
defect subspace
of the operator
S
in a point of regular type
z
is defined as follows

\mathfrak{N}_{z}:=\ran(S-\overline{z})^{[\perp]},\quad z\in\widehat{\rho}(S).

The numbers
n_{\pm}(S):=\dim(\mathfrak{N}_{z})
are called the
defect numbers

of
S
and they are constant for all
z\in\widehat{\rho}(S)
in the open upper (lower) half plane.
Here
[\perp]
stands for the orthogonal complement with respect to
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
.

In what follows we assume that the operator
S
admits a selfadjoint extension
\widetilde{S}
in
\bigl({\mathcal{K}},[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]\bigr)
with a nonempty resolvent set
\rho(\widetilde{S})
. Then, for all
z\in\rho(\widetilde{S})
, we have the direct sum decomposition

\dom(S^{+})=\dom(\widetilde{S})\dotplus\mathfrak{N}_{z}.

(2.3)

This implies that the dimension
\dim(\mathfrak{N}_{z})
is constant for all
z\in\rho(\widetilde{S})
and consequently that
n_{+}(S)=n_{-}(S)
. In what follows, we consider only the case
n_{+}(S)=n_{-}(S)=1
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
be linear mappings from
\dom(S^{+})
to
\mathbb{C}
such that

(i)

the mapping
\Gamma:\dom(S^{+})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
,

f\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{0}f\\
\Gamma_{1}f\end{pmatrix}
,
is surjective and

(ii)

the abstract Green’s identity holds for all
f,~g\in\dom(S^{+})
:

\bigl[S^{+}f,g\bigr]-\bigl[f,S^{+}g\bigr]=(\Gamma_{1}{f})\overline{{(\Gamma_{0}{g})}}\,-(\Gamma_{0}{f})\overline{{(\Gamma_{1}{g})}}.

(2.4)

Then the triple
\bigl(\mathbb{C},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\bigr)
is called a
boundary triple
for
S^{+}
, cf.
[
14
,
16
,
20
]
for more generality.

It follows from (
2.4
) that the extensions
S_{0}
,
S_{1}
of
S
defined as restrictions of
S^{+}
to the domains

\dom(S_{0}):=\ker(\Gamma_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dom(S_{1}):=\ker(\Gamma_{1})

are selfadjoint extensions of
S
. Moreover, one can always choose a boundary triple
\bigl(\mathbb{C},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\bigr)
for
S
such that
S_{0}=\widetilde{S}
,
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
. In this case for every
z\in\rho(S_{0})
the decomposition (
2.3
) holds with
\widetilde{S}=S_{0}
and the mapping
\Gamma_{0}|_{\mathfrak{N}_{z}}:\mathfrak{N}_{z}\to\mathbb{C}
is invertible for every
z\in\rho(S_{0})
.
This leads to the definition of the following functions.

Definition 2.3
.

The functions
z\mapsto M(z)
and
z\mapsto\gamma(z)
, defined as

M(z)\Gamma_{0}f_{z}=\Gamma_{1}f_{z},\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(z)=\left(\Gamma_{0}|_{\mathfrak{N}_{z}}\right)^{-1},\quad f_{z}\in\mathfrak{N}_{z},\,z\in\rho(S_{0}),

are called the
abstract Weyl function
of
S
and the
\gamma
-field
associated to the boundary triple
\bigl(\mathbb{C},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\bigr)
, respectively.

The concept of the abstract Weyl function was introduced in
[
16
]
for symmetric operators in Hilbert spaces and later extended to symmetric operators in Krein spaces in
[
14
]
.

Operators on the star graph
G

Let
G
be a star graph with
n
edges, each of unit length and joined at
0
. An element
f
from the space
L^{2}(G)
in (
1.2
) is of the form
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})
where
f_{j}\in L^{2}(I_{j})
. The inner product on
L^{2}(G)
is induced by the usual one on the intervals, that is,

\langle f,\,g\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}f_{j}(t)\overline{g_{j}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad f,g\in L^{2}(G).

Let
n_{+}\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
n_{-}:=n-n_{+}
.
On
L^{2}(G)
we define the indefinite inner product

[f,\,g]:=\langle f,\,Jg\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{+}}\int_{0}^{1}f_{j}(t)\overline{g_{j}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{j=n_{+}+1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}f_{j}(t)\overline{g_{j}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.1)

whose fundamental symmetry
J:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
is given by

(Jf)_{j}:=\begin{cases}f_{j},\quad&1\leq j\leq n_{+},\\[4.30554pt]
-f_{j},\quad&n_{+}+1\leq j\leq n,\end{cases}

which clearly satisfies
J^{*}=J^{-1}=J
for
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
. The inner product in (
3.1
) endows
(L^{2}(G),[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
with a Krein space structure, see Section
2
.

Recall the definition of
\widehat{H}^{1}_{D}(G)
in (

In order to study the spectral properties of the operator

) and (

S\subseteq A\subseteq T

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(T)

\displaystyle=\Big\{f\in\widehat{H}^{1}_{D}(G)\ :\ f_{1}=\dots=f_{n}(0),\,f_{j}\in H^{2}(I_{j})\text{ for all }j\ \Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A)

\displaystyle=\{f\in\mathcal{D}(T)\ :\ f_{j}(0)=0\ \text{ for all }j\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{D}(T)\ :\ f_{j}(0)=0\ \text{ for all }j\ \text{and}\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}(0)=0\Big\}

\displaystyle Tf

(i)

Proposition 3.3

4.2
Similarity of

For stating and proving the main result in this section, we start with two lemmas. Lemma

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
\delta\in(0,1/2)
there exists a positive bounded and boundedly invertible operator
X:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
in the Hilbert space
(L^{2}(G),\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle)
such that
for every
f=(f_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in L^{2}(G)
the following is true.

(i)

(f-Xf)_{j}(x)=0
for
x\in(1-\delta,1)\subseteq I_{j}
;

(ii)

if
f_{j}
has a continuous extension to
0
, then so does
(Xf)_{j}
and
(Xf)_{j}(0)=0
.

Proof.

Let
0<\delta<1/2
and choose
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,1])
,
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
, which is equal to
1
on
[0,\delta/2)
and vanishes in
[\delta,1]
. Define the linear operator
Y=Y_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Y_{n}
in
L^{2}(G)
as

Y_{j}u_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}\left(\alpha_{j}s_{j}u_{j}(s_{j}x)+\beta_{j}t_{j}u_{j}(t_{j}x)\right)\varphi(x)\quad&\text{for }\ x\in[0,\delta],\\[4.30554pt]
0\quad&\text{for }\ x\in(\delta,1],\end{cases}

(4.5)

where
s_{j},t_{j}\in(1,\,2)
,
s_{j}\neq t_{j}
and
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
for
j=1,\dots,n
.
Below in (
4.7
) we will determine an appropriate choice for the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
.
It is easy to see that
Y
is bounded and that for all
j=1,\dots,n

\displaystyle(Y^{*}u)_{j}(x)=Y_{j}^{*}u_{j}(x)=\alpha_{j}u_{j}\left(\frac{x}{s_{j}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{x}{s_{j}}\right)+\beta_{j}u_{j}\left(\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{x}{t_{j}}\right),

(4.6)

Moreover, if
u_{j}
admits a continuous extension at
0
, then so do
(Yu)_{j}
and
(Y^{*}u)_{j}
. Note that by construction,
(Yu)_{j}
and
(Y^{*}u)_{j}
are zero in
[\delta,1)\subseteq I_{j}
for each
j=1,\dots,n
and
u\in L^{2}(G)
.
Next we want to choose the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
such that

(Yu)_{j}(0)=u_{j}(0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(Y^{*}u)_{j}(0)=-u_{j}(0)

(4.7)

whenever
u_{j}
has a continuous extension to
0
. By construction of

, this is clearly equivalent to

\alpha_{j}s_{j}+\beta_{j}t_{j}=1,\qquad\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}=-1,\qquad\qquad j=1,\dots,n.

(4.8)

This system of linear equations has the unique solution

\alpha_{j}=\frac{1+t_{j}}{s_{j}-t_{j}}\quad\mbox{and }\quad\beta_{j}=-\frac{1+s_{j}}{s_{j}-t_{j}}.

From all the above it follows that the operator
X:=1+Y^{*}Y
has the desired properties.

The next lemma describes the form domain of
JB
. Since
JB
is a positive operator in the Hilbert space
H
by (

), its form domain
\mathcal{D}[JB]
is defined and, according to the Second Representation Theorem, it is equal to

(see, e.g.,

Lemma 4.4
.

The form domain
\mathcal{D}[JB]
of

is equal to

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[JB]=\left\{f\in\widehat{H}^{1}_{D}(G):f_{1}(0)=\dots=f_{n}(0)\right\}.

(4.9)

Proof.

The claim can be shown with the general results from
[
5
, Theorem 1.4.11]
, but we give a direct proof as our setup is relatively simple. It follows from (
4.2
) that
\mathcal{D}[JB]
is the closure of
\mathcal{D}(JB)
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{1}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(G)}

(see, e.g.,
[
23
, VI-§ 2.1]
). Let us denote the right hand side of (
4.9
) by
\mathcal{D}
. We first show
\mathcal{D}[JB]\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Let us fix
f\in\mathcal{D}[JB]
and choose a sequence
(g_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(JB)
such that

with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, Then
(g_{t,j})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to

in

on each edge

Theorem 4.5
.

The operator
B
is similar to a selfadjoint operator in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(G)
. In particular, its eigenfunctions form a Riesz basis.

Proof.

Since
B
is nonnegative with
0\in\rho(B)
it remains to show that there exists a positive, bounded and boundedly invertible operator

W:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)\quad\mbox{such that}\quad W(\mathcal{D}((JB)^{\frac{1}{2}})))\subseteq\mathcal{D}((JB)^{\frac{1}{2}}),

cf. Theorem
2.1
.
We claim that the operator
W=JX
with
X
from Lemma
4.3
is such an operator.
Clearly,
X
is bounded and boundedly invertible in
L^{2}(G)
, hence
W
is bounded and boundedly invertible.
Moreover, the positivity of
X
in the Hilbert space
(L^{2}(G),\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle)
gives the positivity of
W
in the Krein space
(L^{2}(G),[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
.
By construction,

Xf\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{1}(I_{j})\quad\mbox{if}\quad f\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{1}(I_{j}),

hence by Lemma

X(\mathcal{D}[JB])\subseteq X\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{1}(I_{j})\Big)\subseteq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{1}(I_{j}).

If
f\in\mathcal{D}((JB)^{\frac{1}{2}}))
, then
f_{j}(1)=0
for all
j=1,\dots,n
,
hence
(i)
from Lemma

shows that

f_{1}(1)=\dots=f_{n}(1)=0

and
(ii)
from the same lemma gives

f_{1}(0)=\dots=f_{n}(0)=0.

In summary,

Xf\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H_{0}^{1}(I_{j}).

Clearly

J\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H_{0}^{1}(I_{j})\Big)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H_{0}^{1}(I_{j})\subseteq\mathcal{D}[JB].

Therefore

W(\mathcal{D}[JB])=JX(\mathcal{D}[JB])\subseteq J\Big(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H_{0}^{1}(I_{j})\Big)\subseteq\mathcal{D}[JB].\qed

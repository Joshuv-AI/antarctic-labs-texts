The Riemann Hypothesis in Oaxaca
Carlos Segovia

Abstract
An equivalence of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) enables a direct bridge to the Young lattice. In specific, the classical threshold lim n → ∞ σ ⁡ ( n ) / ( n ​ log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) = e γ ≈ 1.78107 \lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma(n)/(n\log\log n)=e^{\gamma}\approx 1.78107 , derived from the asymptotic behavior of the sum-of-divisors function, admits a combinatorial interpretation via limiting proportions associated to specific families of integer partitions.



Riemann
[
7
]
introduced the zeta function
\zeta(s)
with the assertion that all of the nontrivial zeros have real part equal to
1/2
. Later, Robin
[
8
]
established an equivalence between the RH and the inequality:

\frac{\sigma(n)}{n}<e^{\gamma}\log\log n,\quad\textrm{ for }n>5040\,,

(1)

with
\sigma(n)
the sum of divisors and
\gamma
the Euler–Mascheroni constant.
Lagarias
[
5
]
provided an elementary equivalence of the RH with the inequality:

\sigma(n)\leq H_{n}+e^{H_{n}}\log H_{n}\,,

(2)

where
H_{n}
is the
n
-Harmonic number. Recently, Espinosa published an equivalence of the RH in
[
3
]
. In what follows I present an sketch of his proof:

A(n):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k!}\geq\sigma(n)\,,

(3)

for
H_{k}-\gamma\leq a_{k}\leq\log(k)+\frac{1}{k}
,
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Take the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{H_{k}-\gamma}{k!}\right)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}-\gamma e^{x}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle=e^{x}(\log x+\gamma+E_{1}(x))-\gamma e^{x}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle=e^{x}(\log x+E_{1}(x))\,,

(6)

where we use

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{x}-e^{xt}}{1-t}dt\,,

(7)

\displaystyle=e^{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}dt\,,

(8)

which is equal to
e^{x}(\log x+\gamma+E_{1}(x))
by
[
1
, 5.1.39]
. By Lagarias
[
5
]
, for
n\geq 3
,

e^{\gamma}n\log\log n\leq e^{H_{n}}\log H_{n}\,.

(9)

Suppose the RH, by Robin
[
8
]
, for
n>5040
, we have

\sigma(n)\leq e^{\gamma}n\log\log n\,,

(10)

For
1\leq n\leq 5040
we can verify by computer that
\sigma(n)\leq A(n)
.

Suppose the inequality
\sigma(n)\leq A(n)
. If the RH is false, by Robin
[
8
]
, there exists constants
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
and
C>0
such that the inequality

\sigma(n)\geq e^{\gamma}n\log\log n+\frac{Cn\log\log n}{(\log n)^{\beta}}\,,

(11)

holds for infinitely many
n
. In addition, by Lagarias
[
5
]
, for
n\geq 3
, we have the inequality

e^{H_{n}}\log H_{n}\leq e^{\gamma}n\log\log n+\frac{3e^{\gamma}n}{2\log n}\,.

(12)

Because of (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Cn\log\log n}{(\log n)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3e^{\gamma}n}{2\log(n)}+e^{H_{n}}E_{1}(H_{n})\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3e^{\gamma}n}{2\log n}+\frac{e^{\gamma}(n+1)}{\log n}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\gamma}}{\log n}\left(\frac{5}{2}n+1\right)\,,

(15)

where
e^{H_{n}}E_{1}(H_{n})\leq e^{H_{n}}/H_{n}\leq e^{\gamma}(n+1)/\log n
since
\log n<H_{n}<\log(n+1)+\gamma
for
n\geq 1
.

Therefore, we have the inequality:

\frac{C}{e^{\gamma}}\log\log n\leq(\log n)^{\beta-1}\left(\frac{5}{2}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\,.

(16)

Taking limits with
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the left-side tend to infinity, while the right-side tend to zero, which is a contradiction. We can increase the coefficients
H_{k}-\gamma\leq a_{k}\leq\log k+1/k
providing the following inequality (see de Appendix):

A(n)\leq e^{H_{n}}\log H_{n}+\frac{2e^{\gamma}(n+1)}{\log n}\,.

(17)

This inequality implies we do not exceed the threshold
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A(n)/n\log\log n=e^{\gamma}
.
∎

The Young lattice is one of the most important and beautiful structures in mathematics. The connection with the RH arises through the interpretation of
A(n)
in terms of integer partitions:

\exp(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu\vdash j}\frac{m_{\mu}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{n}\right)}{\mu_{1}!\,\mu_{2}!\,\ldots\,\mu_{\ell(\mu)}!}\,,

(18)

for
m_{\mu}
, the monomial symmetric polynomial associated with the partition
\mu=[\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{l(\mu)}]
. Consider partitions of
j
with order
i
and define the sums:

E_{i,j}(n):=\sum_{\mu\vdash j,\,\ell(\mu)=i}\frac{m_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})}{\mu_{1}!\mu_{2}!\dots\mu_{i}!}\,.

(19)

Recently, Espinosa in
[
4
]
, provides an interesting identity summing appropriately the monomials in
E_{i,j}(n)
called the assembly theorem. The following is an improvement adding the coefficients in order to obtain complete sums.

We denote the
r
-sum as follows:

A_{r}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(i+r)E_{i,i+r-1}(n)\,,

(20)

and we obtain
A(n)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}A_{r}(n)
with
x_{i}=1/i
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

The case
r=1
is a well understood logarithmic structure

A_{1}(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\log(m+1)e_{m}(n)\,,

(21)

where the
e_{m}
are the elementary symmetric polynomials with

e_{m}(n):=e_{m}\left(1,\frac{1}{2},\ldots,\frac{1}{n}\right)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+1}{m+1}/n!\,,

(22)

the numerator is the sign-less Stirling number of first kind, see
[
2
]
. Denote by
C_{n}
the number of cycles of a permutation of
n
elements. The elementary symmetric polynomials have the following probabilistic interpretation:

e_{m}(n)=(n+1)\mathbb{P}(C_{m+1}=m+1)\,,

(23)

where
\mathbb{P}(C_{n+1}=m+1)
denotes the probability that a permutation of
n+1
elements, chosen uniformly at random, has exactly
m+1
cycles; see
[
2
]
.

Consequently,

A_{1}(n)=(n+1)\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\log k\cdot\mathbb{P}(C_{n+1}=k)=(n+1)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\log C_{n+1}]\,.

(24)

For
r\geq 2
, we found the following determinantal formulas:

p_{k}=\begin{vmatrix}e_{1}&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
2e_{2}&e_{1}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
3e_{3}&e_{2}&e_{1}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
ke_{k}&e_{k-1}&e_{k-2}&\cdots&e_{2}&e_{1}\end{vmatrix}\,,\hskip 28.45274ptm_{[r,1^{l}]}=\begin{vmatrix}e_{1}&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
2e_{2}&e_{1}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
3e_{3}&e_{2}&e_{1}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(r-1)e_{r-1}&e_{r-2}&e_{r-3}&\cdots&e_{1}&1\\
(l+r)e_{l+r}&e_{l+r-1}&e_{l+r-2}&\cdots&e_{l+2}&e_{l+1}\end{vmatrix}\,,

(25)

where the first one can be found in
[
6
]
. Recall the
p_{k}
and
e_{i}
denote the power and elementary symmetric polynomials, respectively, and
m_{[r,1^{l}]}
are the monomial symmetric polynomials for the partitions
[r]
,
[r,1]
,
[r,1,1]
,
\ldots
(denoted by
[r,1^{l}]
for shortly).

We denote the sub-summand of
A_{r}(n)
:

\tilde{A}_{r}(n)=\frac{1}{r!}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\log(l+r+1)\,m_{[r,1^{l}]}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\,.

(26)

The use of the previous determinantal formulas give the expresion:

\tilde{A}_{r}(n)=\frac{1}{r!}\det\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}(n)&1&0&\cdots&0\\
2e_{2}(n)&e_{1}(n)&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(r-1)e_{r-1}(n)&e_{r-2}(n)&\cdots&e_{1}&1\\
S_{1}^{(r)}&S_{2}^{(r)}&\cdots&S_{r-1}^{(r)}&S_{r}^{(r)}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(27)

where

S^{(r)}_{1}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\log(m+r)\cdot(m+r-1)e_{m+r-1}(n)

(28)

and

S^{(r)}_{j}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\log(m+r)\cdot e_{m+r-j}(n),\quad j=2,\ldots,r\,.

(29)

Remember
e_{m}(n)=(n+1)\mathbb{P}(C=m+1)
,
m\geq 0
. For
C:=C_{n+1}
, denote the following:

E_{1}=\mathbb{E}[(C-1)\log C]\textrm{ and }E_{j}=\mathbb{E}[\log(C+j-1)],\textrm{ for }j\geq 2\,.

(30)

Consequently, we write

S_{1}^{(r)}=(n+1)E_{1}-(n+1)\sum_{t=1}^{r}\log t\cdot(t-1)\,\mathbb{P}(C=t)=(n+1)E_{1}-\sum_{t=1}^{r}\log t\cdot(t-1)\,e_{t-1}(n)

(31)

and for
j\geq 2
,

S_{j}^{(r)}=(n+1)E_{j}-(n+1)\sum_{t=1}^{r-j+1}\log(t+j-1)\,\mathbb{P}(C=t)=(n+1)E_{j}-\sum_{t=1}^{r-j+1}\log(t+j-1)\,e_{t-1}(n),\quad j\geq 2\,.

(32)

where
e_{t-1}(n)=(n+1)\mathbb{P}(C=t)
states our previous notation.

Set
\Delta_{1}=-\sum_{t=1}^{r}\log t\cdot(t-1)e_{t-1}
and
\Delta_{j}=-\sum_{t=1}^{r-j+1}\log(t+j-1)e_{t-1}
, for
j\geq 2
. Because the matrix below has zero determinant:

\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
2e_{2}&e_{1}&1&0&\cdots&0\\
3e_{3}&e_{2}&e_{1}&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(r-1)e_{r-1}&e_{r-2}&e_{r-3}&\cdots&e_{1}&1\\
\Delta_{1}&\Delta_{2}&\Delta_{3}&\cdots&\Delta_{r-1}&\Delta_{r}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(33)

therefore, we obtain the formula, for
r\geq 2
:

\tilde{A}_{r}(n)=\frac{n+1}{r!}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}p_{j-1}(n)E_{j}\,.

(34)

In order to find expressions for
E_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq r
, we have the following:

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\log(C_{n}+a)\bigr]=\log\bigl(H_{n}+a\bigr)+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\frac{\mathbb{E}[(C_{n}-H_{n})^{m}]}{(H_{n}+a)^{m}},

(35)

and

\mathbb{E}[C_{n}\log C_{n}]=H_{n}\log H_{n}-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m(m-1)}\frac{\mathbb{E}[(C_{n}-H_{n})^{m}]}{H_{n}^{m-1}}\,.

(36)

The central moments
\mu_{m}:=\mathbb{E}[(C-H_{n})^{m}]
are given in terms of the cumulants of the number of cycles of permutations
C_{n}
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\kappa_{1}=H_{n}=p_{1}(n)\,,\\
\kappa_{2}=p_{1}(n)-p_{2}(n)\,,\\
\kappa_{3}=p_{1}(n)-3p_{2}(n)+2p_{3}(n)\,,\\
\vdots\\
\kappa_{m}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(-1)^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!\;S(m,\ell)\;p_{\ell}\,,\end{array}

(37)

where
S(m,\ell)
is the Stirling of second kind.
The central moments
\mu_{m}=B_{m}(0,\kappa_{2},\ldots,\kappa_{m})
are the complete Bell polynomials with positive integer coefficients evaluated in the cumulants:

\mu_{1}=0,\quad\mu_{2}=\kappa_{2},\quad\mu_{3}=\kappa_{3},\quad\mu_{4}=\kappa_{4}+3\kappa_{2}^{2},\quad\mu_{5}=\kappa_{5}+10\kappa_{3}\kappa_{2},\quad\mu_{6}=\kappa_{6}+15\kappa_{4}\kappa_{2}+10\kappa_{3}^{2}+15\kappa_{2}^{3},\ldots

(38)

It is not difficult to convince ourself that
B_{m}(0,\kappa_{2},\ldots,\kappa_{m})\sim c(\log n)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
for
c
a constant. In addition, we use
H_{n+1}=\log n+\gamma+O(1/n)
and
\log H_{n+1}=\log\log n+\gamma/\log n+o(1/\log n)
and we obtain:

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[C\log C]-\mathbb{E}[\log C]\,,

(39)

\displaystyle=\left[H_{n+1}\log H_{n+1}+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2H_{n+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right]-\left[\log H_{n+1}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right]\,,

(40)

\displaystyle=\log H_{n+1}(H_{n+1}-1)+\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2H_{n+1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\,,

(41)

\displaystyle=\left(\log\log n+\frac{\gamma}{\log n}+o\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\right)\left(\log n+\gamma-1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)+O(1)\,,

(42)

\displaystyle=\log n\log\log n+(\gamma-1)\log\log n+\gamma+O(1)\,,

(43)

and

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(\log(C+j-1))\,,

(44)

\displaystyle=\left[\log H_{n+1}+\frac{j-1}{H_{n+1}}+o\left(\frac{1}{H_{n+1}^{2}}\right)\right]+O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)\,,

(45)

\displaystyle=\log\log n+O(1)\,,

(46)

for
j\geq 2
. As a consequence, we have the following:

H_{n}E_{2}-E_{1}=\log\log n-\gamma+O(1)\,.

(47)

We deduce the following limits:

\rho_{1}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A_{1}(n)}{n\log\log n}=1,\quad\rho_{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A_{2}(n)}{n\log\log n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n+1)}{2n}\frac{H_{n}E-E_{1}}{\log\log n}=\frac{1}{2}\,,

(48)

and for
r\geq 2
, we have:

\rho_{r}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tilde{A}_{r}(n)}{n\log\log n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n+1)(-1^{r})}{r!n}\left(\frac{-E_{1}+p_{1}(n)E_{2}-p_{2}(n)E_{3}+\ldots+(-1)^{r}p_{r-1}(n)E_{r}}{\log\log n}\right)\,.

(49)

Therefore, we obtain

\rho_{r}=\frac{1}{r!}\left[(-1)^{r}+\sum_{j=3}^{r}(-1)^{r+j}\zeta(j-1)\right]\,.

(50)

Take the coefficient
a_{k}=\log(k)+\frac{1}{k}
we have the inequality:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\log k+\frac{1}{k}\right)\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{H_{n}}\log H_{n}+\frac{2e^{\gamma}(n+1)}{\log n}\,,

(55)

for
n>1
.

Proof.

We have the following sequence of identities and inequalities:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\log(k)+\frac{1}{k}\right)\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\log(k)\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k\cdot k!}\,,

(56)

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(H_{k}-\gamma)\frac{(H_{n})^{k}}{k!}+E_{i}(H_{n})-\gamma-\log(H_{n})\,,

(57)

\displaystyle=e^{H_{n}}\log(H_{n})+e^{H_{n}}E_{1}(H_{n})+E_{i}(H_{n})-\log(H_{n})\,.

(58)

where we used
[
1
, 5.1.10]
together with
\log(k)<H_{k}-\gamma
, for
k\geq 1
, and (
6
). Consequently, it rest to show

e^{H_{n}}E_{1}(H_{n})+E_{i}(H_{n})\leq\log(H_{n})+\frac{2e^{\gamma}(n+1)}{\log(n)}\,.

(59)

We have the well-known inequality
e^{x}E_{1}(x)\leq 1/x
and, we can show, for
x>2
, the inequality
E_{i}(x)\leq 2e^{x}/x
. In addition,
e^{H_{n}}/H_{n}\leq e^{\gamma}(n+1)/\log(n)
since
\log(n)<H_{n}<\log(n+1)+\gamma
for
n\geq 1
. In addition,
\log H_{n}>1/\log n
, for
n>3
. Thus (
59
) follows for
H_{n}>2
, the lower cases can be verified.
∎

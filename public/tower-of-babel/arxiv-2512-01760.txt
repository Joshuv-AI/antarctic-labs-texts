Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

n

\mathbb{F}_{2}

\mathrm{PG}(n-1,2)
denote the corresponding
(n-1)

We identify the points of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,2)

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

A line in
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,2)
then corresponds to a triple
\{x,y,x+y\}

These lines define a
3

of the binary projective space.
This is the least number of colors needed to color the points of

Note that a color class contains no three collinear points; that is, each color class is a (projective)

Equivalently,

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

It can be easily shown that

It is a standard exercise in the context of Property B and minimal non-2-colorable hypergraphs (see, for example,

The next case is

As a corollary of our

In this paper

General upper bounds on the chromatic number

We use the convention that all dimensions are projective.
Recall that we obtain a projective space
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,\mathbb{F})
from the
n
-dimensional vector space
V
over the field
\mathbb{F}
in the following way:
The points, lines,
\ldots
,
(d-1)
-spaces of
PG(n-1,\mathbb{F})
are the
1
-dimensional,
2
-dimensional,
\dots
,
d
-dimensional vector subspaces of
V
.
For
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, we write

[v]=[v_{1}:\dots:v_{n}]\in\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)

for the point corresponding to the
1
-dimensional subspace
\langle v\rangle\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Thus,
[v]=[\lambda v]
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Fix a prime power
q
and an integer
t\geq 2
.
For each integer
n\geq 1
, let
\chi_{q}(t;n)
denote the smallest
k
such that the points of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
can be colored with
k
colors in such a way that no
(t-1)
-dimensional projective subspace is monochromatic.
Equivalently, this is the chromatic number of the hypergraph whose vertices are the points of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
and whose hyperedges are the sets of points of each
(t-1)
-dimensional projective subspace.

For
q=2
and
t=2
, a color class is a cap (no three collinear), so a proper coloring is the same as a partition into caps.
For
q\geq 3
and
t=2
, a color class must avoid a full
(q+1)
-point line.
In other words, its complement is a blocking set
[
3
]
.
We denote
\chi_{q}(2;n)
as
\chi_{q}(n)
.
In particular, partitions into caps give upper bounds on
\chi_{q}(n)
, but the true value of
\chi_{q}(n)
can be smaller.

We can prove the following upper bounds.
Let
S
be a projective subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
of dimension
n-t
.
Then
S\cong\mathrm{PG}(n-t,q)
, so we can color
S
properly
with
\chi_{q}(t;n-t+1)
colors.
Color all points outside
S
with one new color. Then no
(t-1)
-dimensional projective subspace is monochromatic.
Hence, for all prime powers
q\geq 2
and for
n\geq t\geq 2
,

\chi_{q}(t;n)\ \leq\ \chi_{q}(t;n-t+1)+1.

(4)

In fact, we can do better.
We have the following recursion for
\chi_{q}(t;n)
that gives an improved upper bound in general.

Lemma 1
.

For all prime powers
q\geq 2
, for all integers
t\geq 2
, and for all integers
1\leq d\leq n-1
,

\chi_{q}(t;n)\leq\chi_{q}(t;n-d)+\chi_{q}(t;d).

(5)

Proof.

Represent the points of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
as
[v_{1}:\dots:v_{n}]
.
Define

A\;=\;\bigl\{[v_{1}:\dots:v_{n}]:v_{n-d+1}=\dots=v_{n}=0\bigr\},

B\;=\;\bigl\{[v_{1}:\dots:v_{n}]:v_{1}=\dots=v_{n-d}=0\bigr\}.

Then
A\cong\mathrm{PG}(n-d-1,q)
and
B\cong\mathrm{PG}(d-1,q)
.
If
v=[v_{1}:\dots:v_{n}]\in\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
is a point, then we write

v=[a:b].

The point
[a:b]
lies in
A
if and only if
b=0
.

Let
c_{A}
be a proper coloring of
A
with
\chi_{q}(t;n-d)
colors, and let
c_{B}
be a proper coloring of
B
with
\chi_{q}(t;d)
colors.
Use disjoint color palettes for
c_{A}
and
c_{B}
.
Define a coloring
c
of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
by

c([a:b])\;=\;\begin{cases}c_{A}([a:0]),&\text{if }b=0,\\[3.0pt]
c_{B}([b]),&\text{if }b\neq 0.\end{cases}

Thus,
c
uses
\chi_{q}(t;n-d)+\chi_{q}(t;d)
colors.

We show that no
(t-1)
-dimensional projective subspace is monochromatic.
Let
S\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
be a
(t-1)
-dimensional projective subspace.
Then
S
must satisfy one of the three cases depending on the dimension of
S\cap A
.

•

Suppose that
S\cap A=\emptyset
.
Then every point of
S
has the form
[a:b]
with
b\neq 0
, and hence
c([a:b])=c_{B}([b])
.
Consider the projection
\varphi([a:b])=b
, which maps points of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
to points of
\mathrm{PG}(d-1,q)
.
We show that
\varphi\big|_{S}
is injective, which would imply that
\varphi(S)
is a monochromatic
(t-1)
-space in
B
; a contradiction.

Say,
\varphi\big|_{S}
is not injective, i.e., there exists
[a_{1}:b_{1}]\neq[a_{2}:b_{2}]
on
S
such that
\varphi([a_{1}:b_{1}])=\varphi([a_{2}:b_{2}])
, and hence
b_{1}=\lambda b_{2}
, for some
\lambda\neq 0
.
Then, the point with coordinates
[a_{1}:b_{1}]-\lambda[a_{2}:b_{2}]=[a_{1}-\lambda a_{2}:0]
lies in
S\cap A
, which is a contradiction.

•

If
S
contains a point
x=[a_{1}:b_{1}]
with
b_{1}=0
and a point
y=[a_{2}:b_{2}]
with
b_{2}\neq 0
, then
c(x)
is a color from the palette of
c_{A}
and
c(y)
is a color from the palette of
c_{B}
.
Since the palettes are disjoint,
c(x)\neq c(y)
.

•

If
S\subseteq A
, then the coloring
c
on
S
coincides with
c_{A}
.
Since
c_{A}
is proper for
(t-1)
-spaces in
A
,
S
is not monochromatic.

In all cases
S
is not monochromatic.
Therefore,
c
is a proper coloring of
\mathrm{PG}(n-1,q)
, and this gives

\chi_{q}(t;n)\ \leq\ \chi_{q}(t;n-d)+\chi_{q}(t;d).

∎

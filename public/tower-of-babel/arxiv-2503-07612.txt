Fuzzy Variational Calculus in Linearly Correlated Space: Part I
Gastão S. F. Frederico, Estevão Esmi and Laécio C. Barros

Abstract




Abstract

This article is the first part of series of articles that aim to present the foundations for fuzzy variational calculus for functions taking values in the space of linearly correlated fuzzy numbers
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.
Recall that the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is composed by all sums of a real number
r
and a fuzzy number
qA
, where
A
is a given asymmetric fuzzy number. Two advantages of this space are that it can be equipped with a Banach space structure and, similar to additive models in Statistics, its elements can be interpreted as the sum of a deterministic expected/predictable value with an uncertain/noise component.
The foundation of variational calculus theory requires the definition and establishement of many concepts and results. This aritcle presents a total order relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
for wihch the notions of local minimal and maximal of a
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued function
f
can be derived. We present a fuzzy version of the first and second optimality conditions in terms of derivatives of
f
.
Finally, we present a generalized fuzzy version of du Bois–Reymond lemma which is essential in variational calculus theory.

keyword:
Fuzzy Variational Calculus; Linearly correlated fuzzy numbers;
Order relation; Optimality conditions; Lagrange lemma; du Bois-Reymond lemma.

1
Introduction

Nowadays, there are several approach about calculus for fuzzy number-valued functions and all them extends the classical calculus for real functions. As a consequence, this can also be seen in fuzzy approach to calculus of variations by considering fuzzy functions and their derivatives into the variational integrals to be extremized. This may occur naturally in many problems of physics and mechanics. Recently, Farhadinia
[
17
]
studied necessary optimality conditions for fuzzy variational problems using the fuzzy differentiability concept due to Buckley and Feuring
[
5
]
. In
[
16
]
, Fard and Zadeh use the concept of
\alpha
–differentiability to obtain an extended fuzzy Euler-Lagrange condition. Subsequently, Fard
et al.
present the fuzzy Euler–Lagrange conditions for fuzzy constrained and unconstrained variational problems under the generalized Hukuhara (
gH
) differentiability
[
15
]
.
Base on the notion of
gH
-differentiability, Zhang
et al.
study Euler-Lagrange equations for fuzzy fractional variational problems under gH-Atangana-Baleanu Differentiability
[
36
]
.
However, their approach does not bring light in the concept of extremals (i.e., the concepts of minimal and maximal), since there is not a consensual (total) order relation on fuzzy numbers.

In the literature, there are many proposals of order relation on fuzzy numbers, for instance, see
[
18
,
26
,
32
,
37
,
35
]
and the references therein. Despite all these order relations, one of them has been usual in optimization problems that is defined in terms of endpoints of the
\alpha
-levels
[
20
,
27
,
28
]
. In
[
33
]
, authors investigate optimizations problems in terms of other order relation that is provided in
[
18
]
. Recently, Antczak presents optimality conditions for fuzzy optimization problems under granular differentiable fuzzy objective functions
[
1
]
.

Our study do not deal with the general class of fuzzy number (
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
) nor does it consider the fuzzy calculus from generalized Hukuhara derivative. Instead, here we use the subclass
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
which is a subset composed by linearly correlated fuzzy numbers or linearly interactive fuzzy numbers (in fact, the precise term it should be linearly
interactive fuzzy variables, the interested reader can see
[
9
,
8
]
.
Here, we present a different and new total order relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
for which we state necessary and sufficient optimality conditions.

In optimization problems, the objective function often involves squaring operations.
Similarly, this can also be the case when we are dealing with fuzzy optimization problems.
To this propose, it is reasonable to expected that the square of a fuzzy number is greater or equal than zero.
However, in general, such a property does not hold true if we define the square of a fuzzy number in terms of the usual multiplication operation. Moreover,
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is not closed under the usual multiplication operation.
In contrast, Longo
et al.
defined the
\Psi
-cross product on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
for which
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is closed under it
[
24
]
.
In this work, we define a total order on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
such that the
\Psi
-cross product of any element of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
by itself is greater or equal zero.
These results are important to establish the fuzzy version of the Langrange du Bois-Reymond lemmas that are two fundamental results that have major implications in several areas of mathematics including variational calculus.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that these lemmas are established in the fuzzy context.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents some basic notions of fuzzy set theory and properties of the subclass of fuzzy number
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.
A brief recall of the calculus theory for
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued functions is provided in this section.
In Section 3, we present an order relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
and states some of its properties with respect to the
\Psi
-cross product.
Sufficient and necessary optimality conditions regarding the proposed order relation is provided in Section 4. In Section 5, we present the main results of this first article which includes a fuzzy version of the well-known Lagrange and du Bois-Reymond lemmas that will serve as a basis for establishing a theory of fuzzy variational calculus in subsequent articles of this series of articles.
Finally, we conclude this paper present some final comments and concluding remarks in the last section.

2
Mathematical Background on Fuzzy Sets and Calculus on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}

A fuzzy (sub)set
A
of the universe
X\neq\emptyset
is described by its membership function

\mu_{{}_{A}}:X\longrightarrow[0,1]
, where
\mu_{A}(x)
means the degree to which
x
belongs to
A
. For notational convenience, we write
A(x)
instead of
\mu_{A}(x)
. The
\alpha
-levels of the fuzzy subset
A
are classical subsets defined as follows:

[A]_{\alpha}=\{x\in X:A(x)\geq\alpha\}
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
and, when
X
is a topological space,

[A]_{0}=\overline{\{x\in X:A(x)>0\}}
, where
\overline{Y}
stands for the closure of
Y\subseteq X
.
Here, we focus on a particular classes of fuzzy sets of
\mathbb{R}
called fuzzy numbers, which will be denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 1

(Fuzzy number)
.

[
2
,
12
]
The fuzzy subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}
is a fuzzy number if

i.

all their
\alpha
-levels are closed and nonempty intervals of
\mathbb{R}
, and

ii.

the support of
A
,
supp(A)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:A(x)>0\}
, is bounded.

For every
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we denote the
\alpha
-level of
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
by
[A]_{\alpha}:=[\underline{a}_{\alpha},\overline{a}_{\alpha}]
.
Note that every real number
r
can be regarded as a fuzzy number such that its
\alpha
-level is given by
[r,r]
for all
\alpha\in[0,1].
Therefore, the set of real numbers can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
and we simply denote it by the symbol
\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

For every
C,D\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the usual sum and product of
C
and
D
and the product of
\lambda
and
C
are defined level-wise respecatively by:

[C+D]_{\alpha}=[\underline{c}_{\alpha}+\underline{d}_{\alpha},\overline{c}_{\alpha}+\overline{d}_{\alpha}],

(1)

[C\cdot D]_{\alpha}=[\min P_{\alpha},\max P_{\alpha}],

(2)

and

[\lambda C]_{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left[\lambda\underline{c}_{\alpha},\lambda\overline{c}_{\alpha}\right]&,\mbox{ if }\lambda\geq 0,\\
\left[\lambda\overline{c}_{\alpha},\lambda\underline{c}_{\alpha}\right]&,\mbox{ if }\lambda<0.\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
P_{\alpha}=\{\underline{c}_{\alpha}\underline{d}_{\alpha},\underline{c}_{\alpha}\overline{d}_{\alpha},\overline{c}_{\alpha}\underline{d}_{\alpha},\overline{c}_{\alpha}\overline{d}_{\alpha}\}.

The class of fuzzy numbers with these operations does not form a vector space because every

B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathbb{R}
does not have an opposite (i.e. an additive inverse) and the distributive
equality
(\lambda+\mu)B=\lambda B+\mu B
,
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, holds true only if
\mu\lambda\geq 0
. Below, we recall the subclass
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
for which, under weak conditions, forms a vector space with a different notion of addition.

2.1
The Space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}

For every fuzzy number
A
, we can consider the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
defined as follows:

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}=\{r+qA\mid r,q\in\mathbb{R}\}.

(4)

In
[
13
]
, authors have proved that the mapping
\Psi
given by
(r,q)\mapsto\Psi(r,q)=r+qA
is injective if, and only if,
A
satisfies the property of asymmetry.
A fuzzy number
A
is said to be asymmetric (or non-symmetric) if for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
A(x-y)\neq A(x+y)
. The following theorem states that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
forms a vector space with the addition operation induced by the mapping
\Psi
.

Theorem 1
.

[
13
]

Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number. The set
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a real vector space with the usual scalar product and the addition operation given as follows.
For every
B=(r_{B}+q_{B}A)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
and
C=(r_{C}+q_{C}A)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
, the sum of
B
and
C

is an element
B\oplus C
of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
defined by

B\oplus C=\Psi\left(\Psi^{-1}(B)+\Psi^{-1}(C)\right)=(r_{B}+r_{C})+(q_{B}+q_{C})A.

(5)

Moreover,
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the isomorphism
(r,q)\mapsto r+qA
.

Theorem
1
ensures that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a 2-dimension vector space.
Furthermore, it is worth noting that one can easily verify that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
we have
\lambda B=\Psi\left(\lambda\Psi^{-1}(B)\right).

In other words, the usual scalar product restrict to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
coincides with the scalar product induced by
\Psi
.

For the propose of this work, we only focus on the case the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
forms a real vector space.
Therefore, from now on, unless otherwise stated, we assume that the fuzzy number
A
that generates the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is asymmetric. It is worth noting that, from a practical point of view, the assumption of asymmetry is not a strong condition but indeed a weaker one since the set of asymmetric fuzzy numbers is open and dense in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
w.r.t. the Hausdorff metric
[
10
]
.
In addition, as usual in vector spaces, for every
C,B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
, the additive inverse (or the opposite) of
B
is given by
-B=(-1)B
and the difference of
C
and
B
is defined
by
C\ominus B=C\oplus(-B)
.

The space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is not closed under the usual multiplication operation on fuzzy multiplication.
In order to equip
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
with a multiplication operation,
Longo
et al.
defined the
\Psi
-cross product between two elements of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
whenever the
1
-level of
A
is an unit set
[
24
]
.

Definition 2
.

[
24
]

For every
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
, with
[A]_{1}=\{a\}
, and
B,C\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
with
[B]_{1}=\{b\}
and
[C]_{1}=\{c\}
. The
\Psi
-cross product of
B
and
C
is defined by

B\odot C=cB+bC-bc.

(6)

The
\Psi
-cross product of two elements of
B,C\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
with
[B]_{1}=\{b\}
and

[C]_{1}=\{c\}
can be seen as extension to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
of the linear approximation of the product function around the pair
(b,c)
. More precise, let
f(x,y)=xy
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, the linear approximation of
f
at
(b,c)
is the first-order Taylor polynomial of
f
around
(b,c)
given by the function
g(x,y)=bc+c(x-b)+b(y-c)=cx+by-bc
.
If
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
,
B=r_{B}+q_{B}A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
, and
C=r_{C}+q_{C}A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
, then
the
\Psi
-cross product of
B
and
C
can be rewritten as follows
[
24
]
:

B\odot C=(r_{B}r_{C}-a_{m}^{2}q_{B}q_{C})+(r_{B}q_{C}+r_{C}q_{B}+2a_{m}q_{B}q_{C})A.

(7)

From now on, unless stated otherwise, we assume that the asymmetric fuzzy number
A
that generates the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is such that
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
. In other words, we assume that the
\Psi
-cross product is well-defined on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.

We finish this section, pointing out that, from a modelling point of view, a fuzzy number
A
such that
a_{m}=0
can be interpreted a fuzzy noise centered at zero. In this case, one can understand an element
B=(r+qA)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
as fuzzy number “around
r
” since
B
is the sum the deterministic value
r
and the fuzzy noise
qA
, where
q
is a factor that modulates the length of the (fuzzy) noise around
r
. This kind of interpretation has a clear parallel with the additive models found in statistics.
The assumption that
A
is centered at zero
is not a strong assumption, since for every
\tilde{A}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
the fuzzy number

A=-\tilde{a}_{m}+\tilde{A}
, with
\tilde{a}_{m}=0.5(\underline{\tilde{a}}_{1}+\overline{\tilde{a}}_{1})
, is centered at zero and belongs to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F(\tilde{A})}}
with
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}=\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F(\tilde{A})}}

[
13
]
.

2.2
Calculus on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued Functions

In what follows, we shall equip the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
with a notion of norm that can be related with the notion of order propose in the next section (see Lemma
2
). The following theorem provides another notion of norm on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
from that presented in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number and
a_{m}=0.5(\underline{a}_{1}+\overline{a}_{1})
. The space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a normed space with the norm
\|\cdot\|:\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\to\mathbb{R}
given for every
B=(r+qA)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
as follows:

\|B\|=|q|+|r+qa_{m}|.

(8)

Proof.

First of all, Equation (
8
) is well-defined, since for every
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
there exist unique
r
and
q
such that
B=r+qA
.

By definition, we have that
\|B\|\geq 0
for all
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
. Moreover, if
\|B\|=0
, then

|q|=0
and
|r+qa_{m}|=0
. The first equality (
|q|=0
) implies that
q=0
. This also implies that
r=0
because the second equality becomes
|r|=0
. Therefore,
\|B\|=0\Leftrightarrow B=0
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda B\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\lambda r)+(\lambda q)A\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda q|+|\lambda r+\lambda qa_{m}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda|(|q|+|r+qa_{m}|=|\lambda|\|B\|.

Let
C=s+pA
. It follows that

\displaystyle\|B\oplus C\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(r+s)+(q+p)A\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|q+p|+|(r+s)+(q+p)a_{m}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|q|+|p|+|r+qa_{m}|+|s+pa_{m}|=\|B\|+\|C\|.

∎

Since the concept of norm induces a metric on the 2-dimension Banach space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
, the concepts and notations of convergence, limit, and continuity on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
are defined as usual for any Banach space.

Below, we recall the definition of derivative for
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued functions.

Definition 3
.

Given
f:I\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
with
I=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
. We say that
f
is differentiable at
t\in I
if there exists
f^{\prime}(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
such that

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(t+h)\ominus f(t)}{h}=f^{\prime}(t).

(9)

In this case,
f^{\prime}(t)
is said to be the derivative of
f
at
t
. If
f
is differentiable at all points of
I
, then we say that
f
is differentiable in
I
.

A well-known fact is that every norm in a finite dimension Banach space are equivalent.
Thus, the norm defined in Equation (
8
) is equivalent to the norm provided in
[
13
]
. It means that the convergence of a sequence or a function at some point w.r.t. one of these two norm implies in the converge w.r.t. the other norm. Moreover, it converges to the same limit in both norms. As a consequence all results obtained with any other norm on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
also hold for the proposed norm. For example, from this equivalence and from Corollary 5 in
[
11
]
, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in I
, where
r,q:I\to\mathbb{R}
and
I=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
. The function
f
is differentiable at
t\in I
if, and only if,
r
and
q
are differentiable at
t
. Moreover, we have that

f^{\prime}(t)=r^{\prime}(t)+q^{\prime}(t)A.

(10)

The notions and notations of partial derivates and derivative of higher orders for functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
can be naturally defined as in the classical case.
Since
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a 2-dimmension Banach space, we can also consider the well-establised notion of Riemman integral for functions from an interval of
\mathbb{R}
to a Banach space
[
19
]
. This integral regards as an operator on the space of integral functions satisfies many interesting properties such as linearity, continuidade, etc
[
19
]
.
In the particular context of the functions from
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
,
the precise definiton of Riemman integral can be found in

[
29
,
31
]
.
An interesting and useful result is that the existence of this integral is caracterized by the existence of two integrals of real-valued funcitons. The following proposition clarifies this fact.

Proposition 2
.

[
29
]

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in[a,b]
, where
r,q:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
. The functin
f
is integrable on
[a,b]
if, and only if,
r
and
q
are integrable on
[a,b]
. Moreover, we have that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)dt=\left(\int_{a}^{b}r(t)dt\right)+\left(\int_{a}^{b}q(t)dt\right)A.

(11)

Similarly to the crisp case, the symbols
\textnormal{C}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
,

\textnormal{C}{}^{k}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
,

\textnormal{AC}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
, and

\textnormal{Lip}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)

stands to the classes of functions from
[a,b]
to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
that are
continuous,
k
times continuously differentiable, absolute continuous, and Lipschitz, respectively.

Based on Propositions
1
and
2
, one can easily prove the
linearity properties for the derivative and integral operators and the fuzzy version of the fundamental calculus theorems. Here, as in classical case, the arithmetic operations between
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued functions are defined pointwise.

Theorem 3
.

[
29
,
30
]

Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number,
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
The following statements are valid:

a)

If
f,g
are differentiable in
(a,b)
, then

(f\oplus\lambda g)^{\prime}(t)=f^{\prime}(t)\oplus\lambda g^{\prime}(t),\qquad\forall t\in(a,b).

b)

If
f,g
are integrable in
[a,b]
, then

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\oplus\lambda g(t)dt=\int_{a}^{b}f(t)dt\oplus\lambda\int_{a}^{b}g(t)dt.

c)

If
f
is integrable in
[a,b]
, then the function
F(t)=\int_{a}^{t}f(s)ds
is continuous
and
F^{\prime}(t)=f(t)
almost everywhere in
[a,b]
.

d)

If
f
is differentiable in
(a,b)
and
f^{\prime}
is integrable in
[a,b]
, then

\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)dt=f(b)\ominus f(a)=f(y)\Big|_{a}^{b}.

The following theorem presents the Product rule (or Leibniz rule) and
Integration by parts with respect to
\Psi
-cross product.

Theorem 4
.

[
31
]

Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number and
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.
The following statements are valid:

•

Product Rule:
If
f,g
are differentiable in
(a,b)
, then

(f\odot g)^{\prime}(t)=f(t)\odot g^{\prime}(t)\oplus f^{\prime}(t)\odot g(t),\quad\forall t\in(a,b).

•

Integration by parts:
If
f,g
are differentiable in
(a,b)
with
f,g,f^{\prime},g^{\prime}
integrable in
[a,b]
, then

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot g^{\prime}(t)dt=f(t)\odot g(t)\Big|_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)\odot g(t)dt.

3
Order Relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}

Minimal and maximal elements are basic concepts in optmization/variational problems that arise from a given order relation. The concept of order relation on a nonempty set
X
is nothing more than a (binary) relation on
X
, that is, a subset of
X\times X
, which satisfies the conditions of the
definition below. In addition, an order relation is usually denoted by
\leq
and the symbol
x\leq y
means that
(x,y)
belongs to the relation
\leq
and it is read as “
x
is less or equal to
y
”. If
(x,y)\not\in\leq
, we say that “
x
is not less or equal to
y
” and this fact is denoted by
x\not\leq y
.

Definition 4
.

[
4
]

An order relation on a nonempty set
X
is a relation on
X
that satisfies the following three properties for all
x,y,z\in X
:

i)

x\leq x
;

ii)

if
x\leq y
and
y\leq x
, then
x=y
;

iii)

x\leq y
and
y\leq z
, then
x\leq z
.

If for every
x,y\in X
we have
x\leq y
or
y\leq x
, then
X
is said to be totally ordered or a chain, otherwise,
X
is said to be partially ordered or, for short, a poset.

Well-known examples of chains are the set of real numbers with the usual order and the power set of a given set with the inclusion order. The following proposition provides a order relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
for which
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
becomes a totally ordered set.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number and
a_{m}=0.5(\underline{a}_{1}+\overline{a}_{1})
. The space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a totally ordered set with the order relation given for every
B=(r_{B}+q_{B}A)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
and
C=(r_{C}+q_{C}A)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
by

B\leq C\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}<r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m},&\mbox{(}I)\\
\mbox{ or }&\\
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}\mbox{ and }|q_{B}|<|q_{C}|,&\mbox{(}II)\\
\mbox{ or }&\\
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}\mbox{ and }|q_{B}|=|q_{C}|\mbox{ and }q_{B}\leq q_{C}&\mbox{(}III).\end{array}\right.

(12)

Proof.

Let
B,C,D\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be such that
B=r_{B}+q_{B}A
,
C=r_{C}+q_{C}A
, and
C=r_{C}+q_{C}A
.
Note that
B\leq B
since the third condition of right side of (
12
) is satisfied.

Now, let us analyse Item ii) of Definition
4
.
Suppose that
B\leq C
. Note that if the first condition of the right side of (
12
) is satisfied, then, we have that
C\not\leq B
. Hence, this case is not possible if the hypothesis of Item ii) of Definition
4
is satisfied. Now, if the second condition of the right side of (
12
) is satisfied, then,
C\not\leq B
. Again, in this case, the hypothesis of Item ii) can not be satisfied.
Thus, the only possibility is that the third condition is satisfied if the hypothesis of Item ii) holds true.
Using a similar reasoning for
C\leq B
, we conclude that
the hypothesis that
B\leq C
and
C\leq B
occurs if, and only if, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}\mbox{ and }|q_{B}|=|q_{C}|\mbox{ and }q_{B}\leq q_{C}\\
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}\mbox{ and }|q_{B}|=|q_{C}|\mbox{ and }q_{C}\leq q_{B}.\end{array}\right.

This implies that
q_{B}=q_{C}
and
r_{B}=r_{C}
. Therefore, we obtain that
B=C
.

Suppose that
B\leq C
and
C\leq D
.
Consider the case where
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}<r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}
. Since
C\leq D
, we have that

r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}\leq r_{D}+q_{D}a_{m}
. Thus, we
B\leq D

r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}<r_{D}+q_{D}a_{m}
.
Similarly, if
r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}<r_{D}+q_{D}a_{m}
, we obtain that
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}<r_{D}+q_{D}a_{m}
and, consequently,

B\leq D
.
The remain case is the case where

r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}=r_{D}+q_{D}a_{m}
. From the second and third conditions in
the right side of (
12
), we have that
|q_{B}|\leq|q_{C}|\leq|q_{D}|
.
Thus, if
|q_{B}|<|q_{C}|
or
|q_{C}|<|q_{D}|
, from definition, we obtain that
B\leq D
.
Finally, if
|q_{B}|=|q_{C}|=|q_{D}|
, then
q_{B}\leq q_{C}\leq q_{D}
, which also implies that

B\leq D
.

To prove that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is totally order with respect to this order relation, we have to show that one of the inequalities

or
C\leq B
holds.
If
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}<r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}
(or
r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}<r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}

B\leq C
(or
C\leq B
).
If
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}
and
|q_{B}|<|q_{C}|
(or
|q_{C}|<|q_{B}|
), then
B\leq C
(or
C\leq B
).
For
r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m}=r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}
and
|q_{B}|=|q_{C}|
, we have that
B\leq C
if
q_{B}\leq q_{C}
, or

if
q_{C}\leq q_{B}
.
∎

In optimization problems, the objective function often involves squaring operations.
Similarly, this can also be the case when we are dealing with fuzzy optimization problems.
To this propose, it is reasonable to expected that the square of a fuzzy number is greater or equal than zero.
However, in general, such a property does not hold true if we define the square of a fuzzy number in terms of the usual multiplication operation.
Based on the notion of
\Psi
-cross product, one can define the square of an element
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
as the fuzzy number
B^{2}=B\odot B.

The following proposition states
0\leq B^{2}
for all
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
be an asymmetric fuzzy number with
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
.
For every
B=(r+qA)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}

0\leq B^{2}
.
Moreover,
B^{2}=0
if, and only if,
r+qa_{m}=0

Proof.

From Definition
2
, for
B=r+qA\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
we have

B^{2}=B\odot B=(r^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q^{2})+(2rq+2a_{m}q^{2})A.

(13)

From Corollary
1
, we have that
0\leq B^{2}
if, and only if,

0\leq(r^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q^{2})+(2rq+2a_{m}q^{2})a_{m}=(r+a_{m}q)^{2}.

Since
(r+a_{m}q)^{2}\geq 0
for every
r,q\in\mathbb{R}
, we conclude that
0\leq B^{2}.

Moreover, if
B^{2}=0
, then, from equation above, we obtain
r+a_{m}q=0
. Now, if
r+a_{m}q=0
, then
r=-a_{m}q
.
This implies
B^{2}=0
because
(r^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q^{2})=0
and
(2rq+2a_{m}q^{2})=2q(r+a_{m}q)=0
.

∎

\Psi
-cross product of two elements taking into account these subsets.

Proposition 5
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}}
be an asymmetric fuzzy number with

First of all, we have

B\odot C=(r_{B}r_{C}-a_{m}^{2}q_{B}q_{C})+(r_{B}q_{C}+r_{C}q_{B}+2a_{m}q_{B}q_{C})A.

where

Consider the value
\gamma

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=¨

\displaystyle(r_{B}r_{C}-a_{m}^{2}q_{B}q_{C})+(r_{B}q_{C}+r_{C}q_{B}+2a_{m}q_{B}q_{C})a_{m}

\displaystyle=¨

\displaystyle r_{B}r_{C}+r_{B}q_{C}a_{m}+r_{C}q_{B}a_{m}+a_{m}^{2}q_{B}q_{C}

\displaystyle(r_{B}+q_{B}a_{m})(r_{C}+q_{C}a_{m}).

In view of Proposition

, we can establish the following theorem which states that the integral of the square of integral function is always greater or equal than zero.

f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A

(17)

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)^{2}\textnormal{d}t=0

r(t)+q(t)a_{m}=0
almost everywhere in
[a,b]
.

Let
r,q:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in[a,b]
.
Note that
r
and
q
are also integral in
[a,b]
,

f(t)^{2}=(r(t)^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q(t)^{2})+(2r(t)q(t)+2a_{m}q(t)^{2})A

and

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)^{2}dt=\left(\int_{a}^{b}(r(t)^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q(t)^{2})\textnormal{d}t\right)+\left(\int_{a}^{b}(2r(t)q(t)+2a_{m}q(t)^{2})\textnormal{d}t\right)A.

,

\displaystyle 0

4
Preliminary Results for Fuzzy Calculus of Variations

As in the classical calculus of variations, we will provide the basis for studying necessary and
sufficient conditions for general fuzzy variational problems based on the Banach space

\left(\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)},\|\cdot\|\right).

The following lemma shows that we can interchange integration and differentiation while working with
fuzzy functions.

Lemma 1
.

Given
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
and
X=\left[a,b\right]\times\left[c,d\right]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
, with
a<b
and
c<d
. Let
g:X\longrightarrow Y
be the function defined by
g(t,\epsilon)=r(t,\epsilon)+q(t,\epsilon)A
,
\forall(t,\epsilon)\in X
, such that
r,q\in\textnormal{C}^{1}\left(X,\mathbb{R}\right)
(i.e.,
r
and
q
have continuous partial derivatives in
X
). For all
\epsilon\in\left[c,d\right]
, we have

\dfrac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}\epsilon}\int_{a}^{b}g(t,\epsilon)\textnormal{d}t=\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}g(t,\epsilon)\textnormal{d}t.

(18)

Proof.

It follows from the results of the classical calculus theory for the functions
r
and
q
together with Propositions
1
and
2
.
∎

The notion of local minimum or maximum of a function
g\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is analogous to the classical case, taking into account the order relation defined in Equation (
12
). More precisely, a point
t^{*}\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
is
a local minimizer (or maximizer) of a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
if
f(t^{*})\leq f(z)
(or
f(z)\leq f(t^{*})
) for all

z
in a neighborhood of
t^{*}
in
I
. In this case,
f(t^{*})
is said to be a local minimum (or maximum) of
f
.
A natural question is whether or not it is possible to establish a necessary and sufficient conditions for a point
t^{*}
to be a local minimizer or maximizer of a
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-value function in terms of its derivative when it exists. The following lemmas address this issue.

Lemma 2
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval,
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be asymmetric with
a_{m}=0.5(\underline{a}_{1}+\overline{a}_{1})
, and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be a differentiable function in
I
such that

f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
,
\forall t\in I
.
If
t^{*}\in I
is a local minimizer or maximizer of
f
, then
r^{\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}=0
.

Proof.

First of all, let us suppose w.l.g. that
t^{*}
is minimizer since the proof is similar for the case where
t^{*}
is a maximizer.

From Proposition
1
, we have that
r
and
q
are differentiable in
I
.
Let
(t_{n})
be an arbitrary decreasing sequence such that
t_{n}\to t^{*}
and
f(t^{*})\leq f(t_{n})
for all
n
.
It is always possible to extract a subsequence
(t_{n_{k}})
such that either (I) in (
12
) is satisfied for all
k
or one of the conditions (II) and (III) in (
12
) is satisfied for all
k
. Thus,
we have that one of the following cases occurs:

(a)

r(t^{*})+q(t^{*})a_{m}<r(t_{n_{k}})+q(t_{n_{k}})a_{m}
for all
k
;

(b)

r(t^{*})+q(t^{*})a_{m}=r(t_{n_{k}})+q(t_{n_{k}})a_{m}
for all
k
;

If Item (a) happens, then we have

0<\frac{(r(t_{n_{k}})-r(t^{*}))+a_{m}(q(t_{n_{k}})-q(t^{*}))}{t_{n_{k}}-t^{*}}

\Rightarrow 0\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(r(t_{n_{k}})-r(t^{*}))+a_{m}(q(t_{n_{k}})-q(t^{*}))}{t_{n_{k}}-t^{*}}=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+a_{m}q^{\prime}(t^{*}).

Now, if (b) is satisfied, then, using the same manipulations above, we conclude that
0=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+a_{m}q^{\prime}(t^{*})
.
Therefore, in both cases the inequality
0\leq r^{\prime}(t^{*})+a_{m}q^{\prime}(t^{*})
holds true.

In the same way, we can conclude that
0\geq r^{\prime}(t^{*})+a_{m}q^{\prime}(t^{*})
following the same steps above, but with the sequence
(t_{n})
being increasing.

Therefore, from these inequalities, we obtain that
0=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+a_{m}q^{\prime}(t^{*})
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be open interval,
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be asymmetric with
a_{m}=0.5(\underline{a}_{1}+\overline{a}_{1})
, and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be a function three times continuously differentiable in
I
such that

f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in I
.

a)

If
0=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}
and
0<r^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}
, then
f(t^{*})
is a local minimum of
f
;

b)

If
0=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}
and
0>r^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}
, then
f(t^{*})
is a local maximum of
f
.

Proof.

Here, we only prove Item a) since the proof of Item b) follows similarly.

Consider the function
g(t)=r(t)+q(t)a_{m}
for all
t\in I
. Note that
g
is three times continuously differentiable in
I
because
r
and
q
are so by Proposition
1
.
By hypothesis, we have that
g^{\prime}(t^{*})=r^{\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}=0
and

g^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})=r^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})+q^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})a_{m}>0.

Using Taylor Theorem, we have

g(t^{*}+h)=g(t^{*})+g^{\prime}(t^{*})h+0.5g^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})h^{2}+\epsilon(h^{3})=g(t^{*})+0.5g^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})h^{2}+\epsilon(h^{3})

where
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\epsilon(h^{3})}{h^{2}}=0
.
From the hypothesis that
I
is open and the property of the remainder
\epsilon
,
there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
|h|<\delta
we have
t^{*}+h\in I
and
\Big|\frac{\epsilon(h^{3})}{h^{2}}\Big|\leq\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})}{2}.

This implies that

\frac{2}{h^{2}}(g(t^{*}+h)-g(t^{*}))\geq 0.5g^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})>0\Rightarrow g(t^{*}+h)>g(t^{*}).

Thus, we conclude that
f(t^{*})\leq f(t^{*}+h)
for all
h\in(-\delta,\delta)
since the condition (I) in (
12
) is satisfied:

g(t^{*})=r(t^{*})+q(t^{*})a_{m}<g(t^{*}+h)=r(t^{*}+h)+q(t^{*}+h)a_{m}.

∎

5
The Fuzzy Lagrange and du Bois-Reymond Lemmas

As we know, Lagrange and du Bois-Reymond lemmas play a very important role in classical variational calculus and in the theory of ordinary differential equations
[
6
,
7
,
14
,
21
,
23
]
. In this section, we study the generalizations of the classical Lagrange and du Bois-Reymond lemmas for the class of functions on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
. We start with an extension of Lagrange classic lemma to a general fuzzy setting.

Lemma 4
.

(Lagrange)
Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number such that
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
.

(i)

If
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is an integrable function such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}

(19)

for any
\eta\in C^{l}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta^{(j)}(a)=\eta^{(j)}(b)=0
,
j=0,1,\dots,l
, then

f(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere in
[a,b].

(ii)

If
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is an integrable function such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t=0

(20)

for any
\eta\in C^{l}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta^{(j)}(a)=\eta^{(j)}(b)=0
,
j=0,1,\dots,l
, then

f(t)=0
almost everywhere in
[a,b].

Proof.

First of all, there exist integrable functions
r,q:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in[a,b]
. Since
r
and
q
are integrable functions, they are continuous almost everywhere in
[a,b]
, that is, they are discontinuous in subsets with zero measure. Thus, there exist a open set
I\subset[a,b]
such that
r
and
q
are continuous in
I
and the closure of
I
is
[a,b]
.
Let
\epsilon>0
, consider the functions
\delta_{k}:[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\delta_{k}(x)=c_{k}^{-1}\left[\frac{\cos\left(\pi x\epsilon^{-1}\right)+1}{2}\right]^{(l+1)k}

where
c_{k}=\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\left[\frac{\cos\left(\pi s\epsilon^{-1}\right)+1}{2}\right]^{(l+1)k}\textnormal{d}s
. Note that
(\delta_{k})
is Dirac sequence and each
\delta_{k}
is
l
times continuous differentiable.

(i)

If there exists
t\in I
such that
f(t)\not\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
, then
r(t)+q(t)a_{m}\neq 0
.
Since
I
is open, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)\subset I
and
f
is continuous in
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
.
Using the functions
\delta_{k}
, we define the functions
\eta_{k}(z)=r_{k}(z)+q_{k}(z)A
,
\forall z\in[a,b]
, with

r_{k}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}r(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)&,\mbox{ if }|z-t|<\epsilon\\
0&,\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and

q_{k}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}q(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)&,\mbox{ if }|z-t|<\epsilon\\
0&,\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for all
z\in[a,b].

Let
B_{k}=\int_{a}^{b}f(z)\odot\eta_{k}(z)\textnormal{d}z
and
[B_{k}]_{1}=\{b_{k}\}
with

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{a}^{b}r(z)r_{k}(z)-a_{m}^{2}q(z)q_{k}(z)\textnormal{d}z\right)+

\displaystyle\,+\left(\int_{a}^{b}r(z)q_{k}(z)+r_{k}(z)q(z)+2a_{m}q(z)q_{k}(z)\textnormal{d}z\right)a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}(r(z)^{2}-a_{m}^{2}q(z)^{2})\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\right)+

\displaystyle\,+\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}(2r(z)q(z)+2a_{m}q(z)^{2})\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\right)a_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}(r(z)^{2}+a_{m}q(z))^{2}\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z.

Since
(\delta_{k})
is a Dirac sequence, we have that
b_{k}\to(r(t)^{2}+a_{m}q(t))^{2}>0
when
k\to\infty
. Thus, for
k
sufficient large we have that
b_{k}>0
, which implies that
B_{k}\not\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
. This contradicts the hypothesis that
\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta_{k}(t)\textnormal{d}t\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
.
Therefore, we must have that
f(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere.

(ii)

For every
t\in I
, since
I
is open, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)\subset I
and
f
is continuous in
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
.
Using the functions
\delta_{k}
, we define the functions
\tilde{\eta}_{k}(z)=\tilde{r}_{k}(z)
,
\forall z\in[a,b]
, with

\tilde{r}_{k}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\delta_{k}(z-t)&,\mbox{ if }|z-t|<\epsilon\\
0&,\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for all
z\in[a,b].

From our hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{a}^{b}r(z)\tilde{r}_{k}(z)\textnormal{d}z\right)+\left(\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{r}_{k}(z)q(z)\textnormal{d}z\right)A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}r(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\right)+\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}q(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\right)A.

Using the above equality and the fact that
(\delta_{k})
is Dirac sequence, we obtain

\Rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0=\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}r(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}r(t)\\
0=\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}q(z)\delta_{k}(z-t)\textnormal{d}z\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}q(t)\end{array}\right..

Therefore, we conclude that
f(t)=0
almost everywhere in
[a,b].

∎

Corollary 4
.

(du Bois-Reymond’s lemma I)
Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number such that
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
.

(i)

If
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a differentiable function almost everywhere in
[a,b]
such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}

(21)

for any
\eta\in C^{1}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
, then

f(t)-u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere in
[a,b]
for some
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.

(ii)

If
f:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a differentiable function almost everywhere in
[a,b]
such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t=0

(22)

for any
\eta\in C^{1}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
, then

f(t)=u
almost everywhere for some
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.

Proof.

Let
r,q:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in[a,b]
.
Using the facts that
f
and
\eta
are differentiable almost everywhere and therefore continuous almost everywhere, we can apply the integration by parts to obtain:

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t=f(t)\odot\eta(t)\Big|_{a}^{b}\ominus\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t=-\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t.

Note that
f(t)\odot\eta(t)\Big|_{a}^{b}=0
because
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0.

(i)

Using the hypotheses of the corollary and the equality above, we have

\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}

for all
\eta
satisfying the conditions of the enunciate. From Item (i) of Lemma
4
,
we have that
f^{\prime}(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere. This implies that

r^{\prime}(t)+q^{\prime}(t)a_{m}=0
almost everywhere and therefore

r(t)+q(t)a_{m}=u_{m}
almost everywhere, for some
u_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For every
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
such that
u_{m}=0.5(\underline{u}_{1}+\overline{u}_{1})
, the claim of Item (i) holds true.

(ii)

Again, from our hypotheses, we have

\int_{a}^{b}f^{\prime}(t)\odot\eta(t)\textnormal{d}t=0

for all
\eta\in C^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)})
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
.
From Item (ii) of Lemma
4
, we conclude that
f^{\prime}(t)=0
, that is,
r(t)=q^{\prime}(t)=0
almost everywhere.
This implies that
f(t)=u
almost everywhere, for some
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
.

∎

Lemma 5
.

(du Bois-Reymond’s lemma II)
Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number such that
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
.
If
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
is a integrable function such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t=0

(23)

for any
\eta\in C^{1}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
,
then
f(t)\ominus u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere in
[a,b]
, where
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}.

Proof.

Let
u=v+wA=(b-a)^{-1}\int^{b}_{a}f(t)\,\textnormal{d}t
and let
r,q:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be
such that
f(t)=r(t)+q(t)A
for all
t\in[a,b].

Since
f
is integrable, the functions
r
and
q
are also integrable. This implies that
r
and
q
are continuous almost everywhere and the functions
\tilde{r},\tilde{q}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
given below are continuous in
[a,b]
:

\tilde{r}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lim_{s\to t^{-}}r(s)&,\mbox{ if }t\in(a,b]\\
\lim_{s\to a^{+}}r(s)&,\mbox{ if }t=a\end{array}\right.

and

\tilde{q}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lim_{s\to t^{-}}q(s)&,\mbox{ if }t\in(a,b]\\
\lim_{s\to a^{+}}q(s)&,\mbox{ if }t=a\end{array}\right..

Consider the function
\tilde{f}(t)=\tilde{r}(t)+\tilde{q}(t)A
and

\eta(t)=\int_{a}^{t}(\tilde{f}(s)\ominus u)ds

for all
t\in[a,b].

Note that
\eta
is continuously differentiable in
[a,b]
with

\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
and
\eta^{\prime}(t)=\tilde{f}(s)\ominus u=f(s)\ominus u

almost everywhere because
r(t)=\tilde{r}(t)
and
q(t)=\tilde{q}(t)
almost everywhere.
From Theorem
5
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(f(t)\ominus u)\odot(f(t)\ominus u)\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(f(t)\ominus u)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t\ominus u\odot\int_{a}^{b}\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t=0.

The last equality follows from the hypothesis that
\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t=0
and
u\odot\int_{a}^{b}\eta^{\prime}(t)=u\odot\eta(t)\Big|_{a}^{b}=0
because

\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
.
Again, from the second part of Theorem
5
, we conclude that

f(t)\ominus u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere.
∎

In what follows we present a generalization of du Bois–Reymond lemma for
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
-valued functions.

Theorem 6
.

(Fuzzy du Bois–Reymond lemma)
Let
A
be an asymmetric fuzzy number such that
[A]_{1}=\{a_{m}\}
and let
f,g:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
be two functions defined respectively by
f(t)=r_{f}(t)+q_{f}(t)A
and
g(t)=r_{g}(t)+q_{g}(t)A
. The following statements hold:

(i)

If
f,g
are integrable functions and

\int_{a}^{b}\left[f(t)\odot\eta(t)\oplus g(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\right]\,\textnormal{d}t=0

(24)

for any
\eta\in C^{1}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
, then there exists a continuous
function
\tilde{g}
such that
\tilde{g}(t)\ominus g(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
and

\tilde{g}^{\prime}=f
almost everywhere.

(ii)

If
f,g
are continuous functions and
g
is differentiable almost everywhere such that

\int_{a}^{b}\left[f(t)\odot\eta(t)\oplus g(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\right]\,\textnormal{d}t=0

for all
\eta\in\textnormal{C}^{1}\left([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}\right)
with
\eta(a)=\eta(b)=0
, then

g^{\prime}=f
almost everywhere.

Proof.

Define
F(t)=r_{F}(t)+q_{F}(t)A=\int_{a}^{t}f(s)ds
for all
t\in[a,b].

Note that
F^{\prime}(t)=f(t)
almost everywhere. Using the hypotheses of theorem, the product rule, and integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left[f(t)\odot\eta(t)\oplus g(t)\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\right]\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left[F(t)\odot\eta(t)\right]\textnormal{d}t\oplus\int_{a}^{b}\left[g(t)-F(t)\right]\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left[g(t)\ominus F(t)\right]\odot\eta^{\prime}(t)\textnormal{d}t.

(i)

From Lemma
5
, there exists
u=v+wA\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
such that

g(t)\ominus F(t)\ominus u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere, that is,

(r_{g}(t)-r_{F}(t)-v)+(q_{g}(t)-q_{F}(t)-w)a_{m}=0.

Let
\tilde{g}(t)=F(t)\oplus u
for all
t\in[a,b]
. Note that
\tilde{g}
is continuous in
[a,b]
and differentiable with
\tilde{g}^{\prime}=f
and
g(t)\ominus\tilde{g}(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}^{0}
almost everywhere.

(ii)

From Item (ii) of Corollary
4
, there exists
u\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
such that

g(t)\ominus F(t)=u
almost everywhere.
This implies that
g(t)=F(t)\oplus u
and
g^{\prime}(t)=f(t)
almost everywhere.

∎

6
Final Remarks

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first article that uses the notion of total order in the study of fuzzy variational calculus. This concept is very important since the fundamental problems in this area deal with minimization/maximization of a functional. Another key property that is often used in the classical case is that the square a real number is non negative. Using the proposed order relation and the notion of multiplication
\odot
(
\Psi
-cross product), we show that
B\odot B
=
B^{2}\geq 0
for all fuzzy number
B
. This property is very useful to check several important results such as lemmas of Lagrange and du Bois-Reymond (see Section
5
).

Our study focuses on the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
that is composed by all fuzzy numbers that are linearly correlated with an asymmetric fuzzy number
A
(i.e.,
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}=\{r+qA\mid(r,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
. This space has two important mathematical characteristics: 1) It is a Banach space, which is an ideal framework for developing a theory of differential and integral calculus and, at the same time, 2) it allows us to model phenomena such that uncertainties (or noise) cannot be disregarded in the task of obtaining an adequate solution for the problem at hand.

Since each
B\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
can be written as
B=r+qA
, in analogy to the language used in statistics (or even in stochastic calculus), we can understand
B
as the “composition” of the real part “
r
” and the uncertain part “
qA
”. In this context, we can interpret the elements of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
from an epistemic point of view, which suggests a certain hierarchy of importance of the parameters
r
and
q
in the proposed notion of order on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
(see Proposition
3
).
Note that, between two fuzzy numbers, the greatest is that with the greatest center (
r+qa_{m}
), that is, the greatest middle point of the
1
-level. In the case of a tie, the modulus of the parameter
q
, which concerns the width of the noise (
qA
), decides which one is the greatest.
Thus, since the centers of the fuzzy numbers are used to decide the greatest between two elements,
the proposed order extends the usual order on the set of real numbers.

We would like to present some brief comments comparing our proposal with the theory of fuzzy calculus based on the concept of generalized Hukuhara derivative or, for short,
gH
-derivative, which is the well-known and most widely used in the literature. On the one hand, in a first glance, the notion of generalized Hukuhara derivative seems to be more general since it is not restricted to special classes of fuzzy numbers
[
3
]
.
However, its use requires the identification
a priori
of the switching points of the function that, roughly speaking, are the points of the domain that occur changes in the behavior of the widths of the
\alpha
-levels. In addition, it also requires the application of Stacking Theorem
[
25
]
to ensure its well-definition in at certain point,
since, in general, its calculation is done in the endpoints of
\alpha
-levels.
It is worth noting that the verification of the hypotheses of Stacking Theorem
is a very hard task in practice. Thus, although no restrictions are imposed on the codomain, its use could be restrictive due to the comments above. Furthermore, to the best of our knowledge, the use of some notion of order relation in the study of fuzzy variational calculus under
gH
-derivative is not clear, which can make it difficult to understand the issue of minimizing/maximizing a functional.
Beside the
gH
-derivative, there exist other notions of fuzzy derivative (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
) for which one could study variational problems, but each one could lead us to different approaches to fuzzy variational calculus.
However, there are still few approaches to fuzzy variational calculus in the literature
[
34
]
.

Finally, the next steps of our research follow in the direction of the extension of the main results of the calculus of variations to the fuzzy case, such as the formalization of optimization of fuzzy functional taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
. Note that the order relation on the corresponding function space is given as a natural extension of the order relation on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{F}(A)}
. These results and others will be presented in the other parts of this series of articles on Fuzzy Variational Calculus in Linearly Correlated Space.

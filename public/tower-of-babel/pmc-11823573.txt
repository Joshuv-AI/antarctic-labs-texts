Comparative evaluation of two techniques of shade matching – VITA shade guide under color-corrected light and digital application DentHue with VITA shade guide under natural light: A double blind, randomized control trial
Authors: Pushpal Ingle, S. R. Srinidhi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aim:The aim of the study was to evaluate and compare two techniques of shade matching using VITA shade guide under color-corrected light and mobile application DentHue with VITA shade guide under natural light.Materials and Methods:100 subjects were selected for the study. The visual assessment was based on a comparison between a shade guide and the target tooth. The shade of the tooth was assessed using VITA classical shade guide in natural daylight, using VITA shade guide with a color-corrected light (true light) and a mobile application DentHue. The shade matching using color-corrected light and with the application was compared with that done by VITA shade guide.Results:It was found that there were 69 (69%) correct identifications of the shade when matched under color-corrected light while 31 (31%) were incorrectly identified. It was also found that there were 38 (38%) correct identifications performed by DentHue while there were 62 (62%) incorrect identifications of the shade. This difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05).Conclusion:The use of color-corrected light demonstrated superior accuracy in shade matching using the VITA shade guide as compared to DentHue.

INTRODUCTION

In recent years, there has been an increased perception of esthetic dentistry from patients and dental practitioners. Thus, it has become a necessity for dentists to provide a restoration which is aesthetically fulfilling. Owing to variations in the shades of natural teeth, providing a shade to the prosthesis which will be an exact match to its counterpart/adjacent natural tooth has become challenging. Inappropriate shade matching leads to patient dissatisfaction.

Color is a sensation produced in the eye by visible light and interpreted by the brain. While color may not be physiologically important to a dental restoration, it can play a crucial role in the overall acceptance of the restoration by the patient.[123] The three dimensions of color are hue, value, and chroma. In addition, there are several properties of color, including translucency, metamerism, gloss, opalescence, fluorescence, and iridescence. Good esthetics require a balance of these dimensions and a thorough understanding of these properties.[456]

Several terms have been used for the process of obtaining the shade to be used in the restorative procedure – shade selection, shade matching, or shade determination, of which shade selection is taken into consideration when the composite resin is used for the restoration of the teeth.[789] Although there is no gold standard process, different methods such as visual and instrumental techniques exist to assess the color.[101112]

The visual method includes the use of shade guides which is the most followed and convenient. The currently available shade guides are VITA Classical (VITA Zahnfabrik Bad Säckingen, Germany), VITA Toothguide 3DMaster shade guide (VITA Zahnfabrik, Bad Säckingen, Germany), and Chromascop (Ivoclar Vivadent, Buffalo, NY, USA).[13]

All color-measuring devices comprise three parts: a detector, signal conditioner, and software that converts the signal into data that can be used in the dental laboratory or operatory. Examples of color-measuring instruments are RGB devices, colorimeters, spectrophotometers, digital cameras, hybrid devices, and spectroradiometers.

Factors affecting shade matching include the source of light, the object observed, and the observer. Different sources of light are available in a dental clinic such as natural light, the operating light of a dental chair, and fluorescent ceiling lights. Shade matching under different light sources will provide variable results. Access to natural light is not always available for shade selection. To address this issue, artificial lights have been introduced with an optimal intensity for shade selection, similar to that of natural light. In this study, a handheld color-corrected light called true light was used.

With the advancement of technology and artificial intelligence, various digital applications have been developed to complement traditional methods of selecting tooth shades. One such application is DentHue, which has recently been launched on the Google Play Store. The application requires a clear photograph of the tooth in question to analyze its shade.

The aim of the study was to evaluate and compare two techniques of shade matching, i.e., using VITA shade guide under color-corrected light and digital application DentHue with VITA shade guide under natural light. The null hypothesis is that there is no difference in shade matching when done under natural light, color-corrected light, and with a digital application.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee and registered for the Clinical Trials Registry of India (REF/2023/10/075124). For the study, 100 patients aged between 15 and 50 years were included. Sample size calculation was performed using Open Epi software. The participants were selected from the reporting patients at the department of conservative dentistry and endodontics, with no gender preference. Patients with intact, healthy maxillary anterior teeth were included in the study. Patients with discolored teeth, intrinsic stains, and tooth-colored restorations were excluded from the study.

Every participant was provided with a detailed explanation of the procedure that would be followed. Informed consent was obtained from each participant before the study. The shade matching was conducted between 10:00 am and 1:00 pm. Shade matching for each participant was done using three methods – shade guide under natural light, shade guide under color-corrected light, and a digital mobile application.

The materials used for the study were VITA classical shade guide (VITA Zahnfabrik, Germany), color-corrected light – True Light (GDP, Delhi) and digital mobile application – DentHue.

Prophylactic scaling of each patient was done before carrying out the procedure and bright colors such as makeup/colored gloves/tinted eyeglasses were removed from the field. The shade selection for each patient was done with all three methods, methods A, B, and C. The parameters used for shade selection between the two investigators were calibrated. Initially, shade selection was carried out using VITA shade guide in natural light (Method A) which was conducted in a designated area by investigator SR with the patient made to sit in an upright position. The shade tabs were placed close to the tooth in the same plane and the most similar shade in terms of hue, value, and chroma was selected [Figure 1]. Shade was selected within minimal amount of time (5–7 s, not more than 15 s). This result was blinded from the investigator PI who did the shade selection with the other methods. For checking the shade, the patients were checked with Methods B and C randomly in no particular order. In the DentHue digital application (Method B), the photograph of the tooth was taken using a mobile camera (48 MP with 60-mm telephoto lens, SIGNI, Skyvik). The tooth was divided into 9 grids and the shade of each grid was displayed [Figure 2]. For the next group, the color-corrected light was used along with VITA shade guide (Method C) to select the shade of the tooth. The light was held 5–7 cm away from the tooth and focused on the area of interest. A shade tab was placed adjacent to the selected tooth and observed through the lens provided with the light [Figure 3]. The results from methods A, B, and C for each patient were evaluated and subjected to statistical analysis.

RESULTS

The data were entered in Microsoft Excel and analyzed using IBM SPSS Version 21. For categorical variables, the frequency and percentage of the data were obtained. The Chi-square test was used to compare the shades identified by VITA shade guide, DentHue, and color-corrected light, with the confidence interval (CI) (95%) and P value (P < 0.05).

When the distribution of shades identified by different methods was done, it was found that of the 100 (100%) participants, the shade most identified by VITA shade guide under natural light was A1 (n = 25, 25%), A1 Shade by DentHue (n = 33, 33%), and A2 Shade by color-corrected light (n = 30, 30%). The differences in the proportion of different shades identified by VITA shade guide, DentHue, and color-corrected light were all statistically significant (P < 0.05) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

A study conducted in Saudi Arabia examined the correlation between patient attitude and self-perception on shade selection. The study concluded that the tooth shade affected the participant’s looks as well as the perception of other people toward them. The participants in the study also stated that the correct tooth shade made them look younger and feel happier.[14] In another study done in India, it was reported that the gender of an individual also influenced the psychosocial status. It was found that females were more concerned about dental esthetics compared to males due to the latter being less self-conscious about their appearance.[15]

The optimal conditions, in which shade matching should be carried out are during mid-day. This time is considered optimal because the wavelength of sunlight at this time is the same as that of a blend of wavelengths of all the colors with the color temperature between 5000°K and 6500°K. The color temperature of natural daylight ranges from 1000°K to 20000°K. Bright colors in the surrounding vicinity such as bright gloves and lipstick, jewelry, and dark sunglasses must be removed to prevent any difference in color perception. The ideal backdrop for shade matching is gray to reduce operator eye fatigue. The tooth to be matched must be cleaned properly and should be free from any stains and plaque; the tooth should not be dehydrated and should be moist with saliva. Shade matching should be done at the start of the appointment, before the placement of rubber dam or making an impression. The ideal time required for shade selection must be 5–7 s; the operator must stand at least 2 feet away from the patient; the patient’s teeth must be at the level of operator’s eyes; the shade tab must be kept in the same plane as the tooth of interest.[15]

Artificial lights with an intensity similar to natural light are commonly used in dental clinics that lack direct exposure to sunlight. The present study utilized true light, which has an intensity of 5500 K. It is held 5–7 cm away from the teeth being treated and has a lens through which the shade can be evaluated.

Shade guides are important tools used in dentistry to choose the right color for dental material that matches the patient’s natural teeth. They are durable, affordable, and easy to obtain from most dental suppliers. Shade guides are efficient and help dentists quickly and accurately select the right shade. They also make communication between dentists and dental laboratories easy by providing a standard reference for the color of the dental material required. Shade guides are essential for dental practices that strive for accuracy and efficiency.[16]

The visual method used in the current study is VITA classical shade guide. It has four color groups according to hue: A – reddish brown; B – reddish yellow; C – gray, and D – reddish gray. The color groups are further subdivided and indexed with numbers from 1 to 4. As the number increases, the value decreases and the chroma increases.[16]

There are a few limitations to using shade tabs for tooth restoration. For instance, the color of the shade tabs may vary depending on the manufacturer. Furthermore, there might be a difference between the shade guide and the type of porcelain used for restoring teeth. In addition, the shade tab has a single color, whereas natural teeth have variations. Moreover, the synthetic resin used to make the shade tab is denser than the prosthetic crown. Finally, since shade guides provide subjective results, there is a high probability of inter-operator bias.[17] To overcome inter-operator bias, the investigation was carried out by a single primary investigator.

Conventional visual methods tend to create optical illusions and contrast effects. Hence, digital- and technology-based methods of shade selection were introduced. These methods made the process of shade selection precise, rapid, and perfect.[1618] The results produced by technology-based methods are not influenced by surroundings and lights, reproducible and documented easily.[19]

DentHue is a mobile shade-matching app. It is a paid subscription and available on Google Play Store.[20] When using DentHue, it is advised to take a clear picture of the tooth to be examined with the mobile camera and upload it to the app. The algorithm will analyze the photo and divide the tooth into nine sections. The app will display the results by showing the shade of each section. To further improve the accuracy of the DentHue algorithm, an additional lens was attached to the mobile camera in this study (60-mm telephoto lens, SIGNI, Skyvik). This improved the clarity and resolution of the image.

Only intact maxillary incisors were included in the study to ensure there was no difference in color perception due to the presence of stains. The photographs were taken without any filters or editing. As the age increases, the shade of the tooth becomes darker due to enamel wear. Hence, the age criteria ranged between 15 and 50 years.

Shade matching with color-corrected light yielded better results than DentHue by eliminating the influence of surrounding light. True light focuses only on the tooth of interest with intensity similar to that of natural light.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the study, it was concluded that shade matching using color-corrected light was more accurate than when done with a digital application, thereby making it a viable option for clinicians. The digital shade-matching application DentHue is a promising first step toward using technology-based shade-matching process. Further developments and updates would be required before it can be an essential part of the clinician’s armamentarium.

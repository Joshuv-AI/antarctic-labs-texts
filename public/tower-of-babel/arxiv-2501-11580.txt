A polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa inverse theorem for function fields
Thomas F. Bloom

Abstract
Using the recent proof of the polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture over 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p}^{n} by Gowers, Green, Manners, and Tao, we prove a version of the polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture over function fields. In particular, we prove that if A ⊂ 𝔽 p ​ [ t ] A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}[t] satisfies | A + t ​ A | ⩽ K ​ | A | \left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert then A A is efficiently covered by at most K O ⁡ ( 1 ) K^{O(1)} translates of a generalised arithmetic progression of rank O ⁡ ( log ⁡ K ) O(\log K) and size at most K O ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ | A | K^{O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert . As an application we give an optimal lower bound for the size of A + ξ ​ A A+\xi A where A ⊂ 𝔽 p ​ ( ( t − 1 ) ) A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!) is a finite set and ξ ∈ 𝔽 p ​ ( ( t − 1 ) ) \xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!) is transcendental over 𝔽 p ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{p}[t] .

Question 1

If

An obvious candidate for a set

Definition 1

Theorem 1

Let

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\textrm{ and }\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert\leqslant K_{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

then there is a generalised
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
-progression
P\subseteq\langle A\rangle
of rank
O(\log K_{2})
and size

K_{2}^{-O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert P\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}^{O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

such that

1.

A\subseteq P+X
for some finite set
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
of size
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\leqslant K_{2}^{O(1)}
and

2.

P\subseteq A-A+X^{\prime}
for some finite set
X^{\prime}\subseteq P
of size
\left\lvert X^{\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}^{O(1)}
.

In the above we have decoupled the two doubling constants, since this is useful in some applications. Since
\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert^{2}/\left\lvert A\right\rvert

Corollary 1

Let
K\geqslant 2

A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
is a finite set and
\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert\leqslant K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then there is a generalised
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
-progression
P
of rank
O(\log K)
and size
\left\lvert P\right\rvert\leqslant K^{O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
, together with a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
of size
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\leqslant K^{O(1)}

A\subseteq P+X

The strength of this result is perhaps slightly surprising given that the analogous naive formulation of the polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture is actually false over the integers, as shown by Lovett and Regev

4

– for quantitative bounds of polynomial strength one must work not just with generalised arithmetic progressions but with the more general concept of ‘convex progressions’ (linear images of the intersection of a lattice with a symmetric convex body).

The case when

Remark 1
.

Our proof of Theorem
3
is self-contained and elementary, but Will Sawin has observed that it should also follow the classification of representations of Kronecker quivers (which in this context are simply pairs of vector spaces

and

Our representation is formed by taking

The fact that any non-trivial linear combination of the linear maps in question is injective means that most of the possibilities in this classification should be able to be ruled out, leaving the only possibilities corresponding to some
1

We have not found a clean statement of the relevant classification (which is usually only considered over algebraically closed fields, rather than finite fields) suitable to make this sketch precise. Since our focus is on additive combinatorics, and Theorem

We will use the notation and definitions from

is covered by
K_{1}^{O(1)}
many translates of

.

Furthermore, since

Proof of Theorem
3

If
V
is a finite
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space then so is
V+tV
, and hence
\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert/\left\lvert V\right\rvert=q^{r}
for some
r\geqslant 0
. With this in mind, we call
r=\log_{q}(\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert/\left\lvert V\right\rvert)
the
weak arithmetic dimension
of
V
.

An easy example of a
V
with small weak arithmetic dimension is any space of the form

V=[d_{1}]\cdot x_{1}+\cdots+[d_{k}]\cdot x_{k}

(1)

for some
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\geqslant 1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. Such a
V
has weak arithmetic dimension at most
k
. With this in in mind, we define the
structural arithmetic dimension
of
V
to be the smallest
k
such that (
1
) holds. Observe in particular that, if we consider
d_{1}=\cdots=d_{k}=1
, the structural arithmetic dimension of
V
is at most the dimension of
V
considered as a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Summarising this discussion, we have the chain of inequalities

\dim_{\mathrm{weak}}(V)\leqslant\dim_{\mathrm{struc}}(V)\leqslant\dim(V).

It is less obvious that in fact we always have equality
\dim_{\mathrm{weak}}(V)=\dim_{\mathrm{struc}}(V)
. This is the content of Theorem
3
.

For the proof of this we will use the following technical lemma. This is essentially the one variable case of the fact that a minimal Gröbner basis always exists for any finite set in a polynomial ring; in this special case the proof is particularly simple.

Lemma 1
.

If
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is a finite
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space then there exists a decomposition of the form

V=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{d},

where
\deg x_{1}<\deg x_{2}<\cdots<\deg x_{d}
.

Proof.

We use induction on
\dim V
. If
\dim V=0
then the result is trivial. Otherwise, let
x\in V\backslash\{0\}
be a monic polynomial of minimal degree, and let
W
be such that
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x\oplus W
. The result follows from the inductive hypothesis and the fact that if
w\in W\backslash\{0\}
then
\deg w>\deg x
. This latter fact is true because otherwise we must have a monic
w\in W\backslash\{0\}
such that
\deg w=\deg x
, and hence
w-x\in V\backslash\{0\}
has degree strictly less than
\deg x
, which contradicts the minimality of
\deg x
.
∎

We will now prove Theorem
3
. The proof is inductive, and the trick is to choose the right inductive hypothesis, which appears stronger than we strictly need.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

For the purposes of this proof, we introduce yet another notion of dimension:
V
has strong structural arithmetic dimension
k=\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}(V)
if
k
is minimal such that there exist
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\geqslant 1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
such that

V=[d_{1}]\cdot x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus[d_{k}]\cdot x_{k}

and
d_{1}+\deg x_{1}<\cdots<d_{k}+\deg x_{k}
. Note by Lemma
1
we may always take
d_{1}=\cdots=d_{k}=1
and
k=\dim(V)
, and so
\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}(V)
exists and is at most
\dim(V)
. In general, the following chain of inequalities is immediate from the definitions:

\dim_{\mathrm{weak}}(V)\leqslant\dim_{\mathrm{struc}}(V)\leqslant\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}(V)\leqslant\dim(V).

We will show by induction on
\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}
that
\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}(V)\leqslant\dim_{\mathrm{weak}}(V)
, and hence certainly we have proved that always
\dim_{\mathrm{weak}}=\dim_{\mathrm{struc}}=\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}
. In other words, we will show for all
r\geqslant 0
that if
\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert=q^{r}\left\lvert V\right\rvert
then
\dim_{\mathrm{strong}}(V)\leqslant r
.

The case
r=0
is trivial, since if
x\in V
is an element of maximal degree then
V\cup\{tx\}\subseteq V+tV
, and if
tx\in V
then
x=0
. In particular, if
\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert V\right\rvert
then
V=\{0\}
as required. We shall hence assume that
r\geqslant 1
and that the claim has been proved for
r^{\prime}<r
.

Suppose that
\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert=q^{r}\left\lvert V\right\rvert
. Let

V=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{\ell}

with
\deg x_{1}<\cdots<\deg x_{\ell}
, as provided by Lemma
1
, and for
1\leqslant s\leqslant\ell
let

V_{\leqslant s}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{s}.

We observe that if
x\in V_{\leqslant s}\backslash\{0\}
then
\deg x_{1}\leqslant\deg x\leqslant\deg x_{s}
. Furthermore, if
x\in V\backslash V_{\leqslant s}
then
\deg x>\deg y
for all
y\in V_{\leqslant s}
. Let
1\leqslant s\leqslant\ell
be maximal such that
V_{\leqslant s}
has strong arithmetic dimension at most
r
. If
s=\ell
then we are done, having shown that the strong arithmetic dimension of
V
is at most
r
as required. Suppose then, otherwise, that
1\leqslant s<\ell
and that
V_{\leqslant s}
has strong arithmetic dimension
1\leqslant r^{\prime}\leqslant r
. We must have
r^{\prime}=r
, or this contradicts the maximality of
s
by considering the decomposition

V_{\leqslant s+1}=V_{\leqslant s}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{s+1}=[d_{1}]\cdot y_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus[d_{r^{\prime}}]\cdot y_{r^{\prime}}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot x_{s+1}.

Hence
V_{\leqslant s}
has strong arithmetic dimension
r
, whence we have some decomposition

V_{\leqslant s}=[d_{1}]\cdot y_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus[d_{r}]\cdot y_{r},

such that
d_{1}+\deg y_{1}<\cdots<d_{r}+\deg y_{r}
; furthermore, since
\deg x_{s}
is maximal over all
x\in V_{\leqslant s}
, and so is
t^{d_{r}-1}y_{r}
, we have that
d_{r}+\deg y_{r}=\deg x_{s}+1<\deg tx_{\ell}
. If
\left\lvert V_{\leqslant s}+tV_{\leqslant s}\right\rvert<q^{r}\left\lvert V_{\leqslant s}\right\rvert
then by induction
V_{\leqslant s}
has strong arithmetic dimension of less than
r
, which is a contradiction as noted above. It follows that
\left\lvert V_{\leqslant s}+tV_{\leqslant s}\right\rvert=q^{r}\left\lvert V_{\leqslant s}\right\rvert
and hence, since the
\dim V_{\leqslant s}+r
many elements

\{t^{n_{i}}y_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r,0\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant d_{i}\}

span the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space
V_{\leqslant s}+tV_{\leqslant s}
, they are also linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In particular, if

\alpha_{1}t^{d_{1}}y_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}t^{d_{r}}y_{r}\in V_{\leqslant s}

with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
then we must have
\alpha_{i}=0
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
.

We now use the hypothesis
\left\lvert V+tV\right\rvert\leqslant q^{r}\left\lvert V\right\rvert
to observe that the
\dim V+r+1
elements

\{t^{n_{i}}y_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r,0\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant d_{i}\}\cup\{x_{s+1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}\cup\{tx_{\ell}\}\subset V+tV

are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since the degree of
tx_{\ell}
is strictly larger than that of all elements of
V\cup tV_{\leqslant s}
we in fact have that the set

\{t^{n_{i}}y_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r,0\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant d_{i}\}\cup\{x_{s+1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}\subset V+tV

is linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore there must exist
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, not identically zero, such that

z=\alpha_{1}t^{d_{1}}y_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}t^{d_{r}}y_{r}\in V.

If
\alpha_{r}=0
then
\deg z\leqslant d_{r-1}+\deg y_{r-1}<d_{r}+\deg y_{r}=\deg x_{s}+1
, and hence
z\in V_{\leqslant s}
, which contradicts the above. Hence we must have
\deg z=d_{r}+\deg y_{r}=\deg x_{s}+1
, and hence
z\in V_{\leqslant s+1}
. Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
be such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
and
d_{i}
is minimal, and let
z=t^{d_{i}}y
, say. In particular we have that
t^{j}y\in V_{\leqslant s}
for
0\leqslant j<d_{i}
and
t^{d_{i}}y\in V_{\leqslant s+1}
. We claim that
V_{\leqslant s+1}
has strong arithmetic dimension
r
, with a suitable decomposition provided by

[d_{1}]\cdot y_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus[d_{i-1}]\cdot y_{i-1}\oplus[d_{i+1}]\cdot y_{i+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus[d_{r}]\cdot y_{r}\oplus[d_{i}+1]\cdot y.

(2)

This contradicts the maximality of
s
and completes the proof. The vector space (
2
) is contained in
V_{\leqslant s+1}
, and since
\dim V_{\leqslant s+1}=\dim V_{\leqslant s}+1
, comparing dimensions shows that the vector spaces are equal, provided only that this sum is indeed direct. If the sum is not direct then we have
a_{j}\in[d_{j}]
, not identically zero, and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{i-1}y_{i-1}+a_{i+1}y_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{r}y_{r}+a_{i}y+\beta t^{d_{i}}y=0.

If
\beta=0
then this contradicts the orthogonality of the original decomposition of
V_{\leqslant s}
, and so
\beta\neq 0
. Since the degree of the final summand is
d_{i}+\deg y=\deg z=d_{r}+\deg y_{r}
which is strictly larger than the degree of all the other summands, the left hand side cannot be zero, which is a contradiction. Finally, the fact that this decomposition is a witness to
V_{\leqslant s+1}
having strong arithmetic dimension
r
follows from the fact that
d_{i}+1+\deg y=\deg z+1>d_{r}+\deg y_{r}
.
∎

2
Transcendental dilates

We now apply the inverse theorem to a function field analogue of the following problem, first considered by Konyagin and Łaba
[
3
]
. Let
A
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}
and
\xi
be any transcendental element; must
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert
be large? Konyagin and Łaba proved that
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\gg(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1-o(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Sanders
[
5
]
later observed that, using an argument of Bourgain, such lower bounds can be obtained by combining simple modelling arguments with an inverse sumset result. Using such an argument with the sharpest known form of such an inverse result, Sanders
[
6
]
improved this lower bound to

\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\gg\exp(\Omega((\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{c}))\left\lvert A\right\rvert

for some absolute constant
c>0
. An example by Green, given in
[
3
]
, shows that this is almost the best possible result. Namely, if one takes
A=\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\xi^{i}:1\leqslant a_{i}\leqslant n\}
for suitable choices of
n
and
m
then one can show that

\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\ll\exp(O((\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/2}))\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

This problem over
\mathbb{R}
has now been completely resolved by Conlon and Lim
[
1
]
, who prove that

\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\gg\exp(\Omega((\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/2}))\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

The proof of Conlon and Lim does not use an inverse sumset result, instead arguing geometrically and using compressions.

Over function fields the appropriate analogue of
\mathbb{R}
is the field of Laurent series

\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)=\left\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{k}a_{n}t^{n}:a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\textrm{ and }k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

which is the completion of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
. Since transcendence over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
is the obvious analogue of transcendence over
\mathbb{Z}
, one might hope for similar lower bounds to the above to hold for
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert
when
A
is any finite subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
is any element transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
.

A moment’s thought shows that this is too ambitious; indeed, the analogue of the example outlined above already dashes our hopes. In particular, if
A=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\xi^{i}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
then it is easy to show that
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\leqslant p\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Recalling that we take
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to be fixed, this is essentially a constant upper bound, and hence no non-trivial lower bound can be given.

On examination of this example, however, some hope returns – since such a set is contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi]
and
\xi
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
we have
\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert\gg\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{2}
. Thus one might hope that if
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
does not grow when added to its dilation by some transcendental element, then this forces growth when added to its dilation by
t
.

The construction above is easily adapted to such a situation. Consider the set

A=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\xi^{i}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\textrm{ and }\deg a_{i}<m\right\}.

It is easy to show that
\left\lvert A\right\rvert=p^{nm}
and
\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert=p^{n}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Furthermore,
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert=p^{m}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. It follows that if
\left\lvert A+tA\right\rvert=K_{1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert=K_{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
then

(\log K_{1})(\log K_{2})\asymp\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

This should be compared to the case
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, when we study the single parameter
K
given by
\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert=K\left\lvert A\right\rvert
and Green’s construction gives a set
A
with
(\log K)^{2}\approx\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Thus we see that in the analogous situation in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
there is a ‘splitting’ of the parameter
K
into two distinct parameters, and we may now ask for non-trivial lower bounds on the size of such parameters.

Combining Theorem
1
with the argument of Sanders
[
5
]
(which Sanders in turn attributes to Bourgain) we are able to prove an optimal such result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
be a finite set. If
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
K_{1},K_{2}\geqslant 2
are such that

\left\lvert A+\xi_{1}A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\textrm{ and }\left\lvert A+\xi_{2}A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

then

(\log K_{1})(\log K_{2})\gg\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

A similar conclusion may be obtained with
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in place of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, using Theorem
2
instead of Theorem
1
, but then an additional hypothesis such as
\left\lvert A+\lambda\xi_{i}A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{i}^{O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
i=1,2
is required.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
K_{2}\geqslant K_{1}
and
0\in A
, so that
A^{\prime}=A\cup\xi_{1}A\subseteq A+\xi_{1}A
. Since
A^{\prime}
is finite there exists some
d\geqslant 1
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
with

A^{\prime}\subseteq\left\{x_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}v_{d}:x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]\right\}.

For some large integer
N
we consider the map
f:\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]^{d}\cdot v\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
defined by

f(x_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}v_{d})=x_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2}^{N}+\cdots+x_{d}\xi_{2}^{N(d-1)}.

Provided
N
is large enough (depending on
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},A
) this is an injection on
A^{\prime}
and so if
A^{\prime\prime}=f(A^{\prime})
then
\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert=\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert\asymp\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. Clearly

x+y=x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\textrm{ implies }f(x)+f(y)=f(x^{\prime})+f(y^{\prime}),

and hence if
A^{\prime\prime}=f(A^{\prime})
then

\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}+A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A+A+\xi_{1}A+\xi_{1}A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}^{O(1)}\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert.

Similarly

\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}+\xi_{2}A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A^{\prime}+\xi_{2}A^{\prime}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert A+\xi_{1}A+\xi_{2}A+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{2}^{O(1)}\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}\right\rvert.

By Theorem
1
(applied with the transcendental
\xi_{2}
playing the role of the transcendental
t
) there exists some
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
-arithmetic progression
P\subseteq\langle A^{\prime\prime}\rangle
of rank
O(\log K_{2})
and size
\geqslant K_{2}^{-O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
such that
P\subseteq A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime\prime}+X
for some set
X\subseteq P
of size
\left\lvert X\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}^{O(1)}
. By the pigeonhole principle there exists some
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
and
m\geqslant 1
such that

Q=x+y\cdot\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]_{\deg<m}

has size
\gg(K_{2}^{-O(1)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/K^{\prime}}
for some
K^{\prime}\ll\log K_{2}
and
Q\subseteq A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime\prime}+X
. We define the inverse map
g:\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]^{d}\cdot v
by

g(x_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2}^{N}+\cdots+x_{d}\xi_{2}^{N(d-1)}+x_{d+1}\xi_{2}^{Nd})=x_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}v_{d},

where each
x_{i}
(as a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
) has degree
<N
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
. This is a well-defined map on
\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]:\deg x<N(d+1)\}
, which contains
Q
for large enough
N
, and we can choose
N
large enough such that

g(Q)=g(x)+g(y)\cdot\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]_{\deg<m}

and

Q^{\prime}=g(Q)\subseteq g(A^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime\prime}+X)\subseteq A-A+\xi_{1}A-\xi_{1}A+g(X).

In particular, for any
l\geqslant 1
,

\lvert Q^{\prime}+\xi_{1}^{2}\cdot Q^{\prime}+\cdots+\xi_{1}^{2(l-1)}\cdot Q^{\prime}\rvert\leqslant K_{1}^{O(l)}\lvert(A-A)+\xi_{1}(A-A)+\cdots+\xi_{1}^{2l-1}(A-A)\rvert.

Since
\xi_{1}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
and
Q^{\prime}
is a translate and dilate of a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\xi_{2}]
, however, the left hand side is at least
\left\lvert Q^{\prime}\right\rvert^{l}
. Furthermore, by Plünnecke-Ruzsa sumset estimates (as in
[
5
, Lemma 6.1]
) the right hand side is at most
K_{1}^{O(l)}\left\lvert A\right\rvert
. It follows that

K_{2}^{-O(l/K^{\prime})}\left\lvert A\right\rvert^{O(l/K^{\prime})-1}\leqslant K_{1}^{O(l)}.

Choosing
l
to be some large multiple of
K^{\prime}\ll\log K_{2}
we deduce that

\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert\ll(\log K_{1})(\log K_{2})

as required.
∎

We conclude by conjecturing the analogous result for subsets of the real numbers.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a finite set. If
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
is transcendental and
K_{1},K_{2}\geqslant 2
are such that

\left\lvert A+A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{1}\left\lvert A\right\rvert\textrm{ and }\left\lvert A+\xi A\right\rvert\leqslant K_{2}\left\lvert A\right\rvert

then

(\log K_{1})(\log K_{2})\gg\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert.

The Plünnecke-Ruzsa sumset inequalities imply that
\log K_{1}\ll\log K_{2}
, and hence this conjecture is stronger than the bound
\log K_{2}\gg(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{1/2}
of Conlon and Lim
[
1
]
.

The quasi-polynomial Bogolyubov-Ruzsa bounds of Sanders
[
6
]
and a generalisation of the argument in
[
5
]
(along similar lines to the previous proof) would give a lower bound of the shape
(\log K_{1})(\log K_{2})\gg(\log\left\lvert A\right\rvert)^{c}
for some constant
c>0
. It is possible (although this is not immediately clear) that an adaptation of the argument of Conlon and Lim
[
1
]
would suffice to prove Conjecture
1
. Similarly, it is likely that the polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture over the integers would imply Conjecture
1
, although the fact that strong polynomial bounds would require not just generalised arithmetic progressions but more general convex progressions (see
[
4
]
) mean that the argument above does not immediately generalise.

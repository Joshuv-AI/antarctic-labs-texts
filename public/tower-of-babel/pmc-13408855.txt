Radiographic root pulp visibility of the mandibular left second molar as an indicator of legal adulthood (≥ 18 Years): a panoramic study
Authors: Mehmet Akyuz, Guldane Magat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of mandibular left second molar root pulp visibility (RPV) for legal age classification (≥ 18 years) using panoramic radiographs. A total of 983 orthopantomograms (492 males, 491 females) were retrospectively analyzed. Root pulp visibility was classified according to the staging system described by Olze et al. Diagnostic performance measures, logistic regression analysis, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis were performed. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. A statistically significant association was observed between pulp stage and legal age classification (p < 0.001). Logistic regression analysis demonstrated a stage-dependent increase in the odds of being classified as ≥ 18 years, with Stage 2 and Stage 3 showing strong predictive effects (OR = 6.04 and OR = 19.13, respectively). Diagnostic performance analysis indicated that Stage ≥ 2 demonstrated high specificity (0.94). ROC analysis revealed moderate discriminatory performance (AUC = 0.676; 95% CI: 0.647–0.705). Mandibular second molar root pulp visibility may serve as a useful radiographic marker for legal age threshold assessment, particularly at higher pulp stages.

Introduction

Accurate age estimation plays a crucial role in forensic practice and legal proceedings, particularly in situations where reliable documentary evidence is unavailable, incomplete, or disputed. In such contexts, biological age assessment becomes essential for medico-legal decision-making, influencing outcomes related to criminal responsibility, civil rights, and access to legal protections1. One of the most universally adopted legal thresholds is 18 years of age, representing the boundary between minor and adult status across most judicial systems2. The determination of whether an individual has reached this threshold carries substantial legal implications, particularly in cases involving undocumented individuals, asylum seekers, and age-disputed subjects3. Consequently, the identification of reliable biological indicators capable of supporting legal age assessment remains of considerable forensic importance1–5.

Dental structures are widely regarded as valuable tools in age estimation due to their relative resistance to environmental influences and postmortem changes6. Various dental age estimation methods have been proposed, including the assessment of tooth development, morphological alterations, and radiographic evaluation of age-related changes6–9. Among age-related dental changes, secondary dentin deposition represents one of the most biologically consistent and progressively accumulating processes, leading to a gradual reduction in pulp cavity dimensions10. Radiographic evaluation of pulp-related changes has therefore been widely applied in forensic age estimation studies. Panoramic radiographs provide standardized 2D imaging suitable for large retrospective forensic cohorts6,9.

The radiographic evaluation of root pulp visibility (RPV) has gained increasing attention as a practical and reproducible method, particularly in panoramic imaging. The classification system introduced by Olze et al. offers a simplified categorical framework based on the degree of root canal visibility, facilitating its implementation in large retrospective cohorts11. Previous investigations have demonstrated the potential forensic utility of RPV assessment, especially in third molars11–13. The development of third molars continues from the late teenage years through the early 20s, which is why they have frequently been used for age determination14. A study emphasized the need for further research to expand dental age estimation methods for assessing age in individuals15. Suvarna et al. reported that, due to the high prevalence of anomalies in mandibular third molars (such as agenesis, microdontia, impaction and hypoplasia) and the fact that they are the most commonly extracted teeth, second molars could be used for age determination instead of third molars16,17. However, despite these considerations, the application of RPV staging in mandibular second molars within threshold-based legal age classification frameworks remains limited, and its diagnostic performance for identifying the ≥ 18-year threshold has not been sufficiently established. Therefore, the present study aims to address this gap by evaluating the accuracy and forensic applicability of second molar RPV stages for legal adulthood classification.

Despite numerous studies focusing on dental pulp-related age estimation, most investigations have primarily emphasized chronological age prediction rather than legal age threshold assessment6,9,11. From a forensic perspective, however, the primary diagnostic question is frequently binary: whether an individual has reached legal adulthood. We hypothesized that mandibular left second molar RPV stages would demonstrate significant discriminatory performance for legal age classification (≥ 18 years). Therefore, the aim of this study was to evaluate the diagnostic performance of mandibular left second molar RPV for legal age classification (≥ 18 years) using panoramic radiographs.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective observational study was performed using archived digital panoramic radiographs retrieved from the institutional imaging database.

Ethical approval

The study protocol was approved by Necmettin Erbakan University Faculty of Dentistry Ethics Committee (Approval No: 2022/16–111) and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, Necmettin Erbakan University Faculty of Dentistry Ethics Committee waived the need of obtaining informed consent.

Power analysis

An a priori power analysis was performed using G*Power 3.1 (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Germany). The analysis was based on a chi-square test of independence under the exact test family (χ² tests: Contingency tables). The effect size (Cohen’s w) was derived from previously published contingency data on (RPV) staging at the 18-year legal threshold18. The reported chi-square statistic and sample size from that study were used to compute the effect size according to Cohen’s formula (w = √(χ²/N)). The calculated effect size indicated a large effect (w ≈ 0.62). Assuming α = 0.05, df = 1, and a desired statistical power of 80%, the minimum required sample size was estimated as N = 21. However, the present study substantially exceeded this requirement, thereby ensuring robust statistical power and stable effect estimation.

Study sample

Digital panoramic radiographs obtained between 2016 and 2025 years were screened. A total of 983 radiographs from 983 individuals (491 females, 492 males) aged 14–30 years were included.

Chronological age was obtained from institutional records. For forensic relevance, individuals were categorized into:

To avoid clustering effects and potential bias associated with multiple correlated observations from the same individual, only one tooth per participant was included in the analysis. The mandibular left second molar was selected as a standardized reference tooth to ensure methodological consistency across the dataset. Although the contralateral (right) second molar was considered during the study design phase, inclusion of both sides was not preferred to maintain the independence of observations required for statistical analyses. In addition, no systematic side-related differences in panoramic image quality or visibility that could affect RPV staging were observed during image evaluation. Therefore, the use of a single, consistently selected tooth was deemed appropriate for reliable and reproducible assessment.

Eligibility criteria

Radiographs were included if they belonged to individuals aged 14–30 years, were of diagnostic quality, and demonstrated a fully erupted and clearly identifiable mandibular left second molar. Radiographs were excluded in the presence of factors that could interfere with pulp space visualization or alter tooth morphology, including endodontic treatment, extensive restorations or prosthetic crowns, severe caries, periapical pathology, root resorption, or developmental anomalies. Additionally, images presenting radiographic artifacts or positioning errors affecting the region of interest were excluded from the analysis. Radiographs were excluded if the mandibular left second molar was absent or if image quality was insufficient for reliable evaluation.

Radiographic acquisition

All panoramic radiographs were acquired using the same imaging unit (2D Veraviewpocs, J. MORITA, Kyoto, Japan) with standardized exposure parameters (70 kVp, 5 mA, 15 s) according to manufacturer-recommended settings.

Radiographic evaluation protocol

Radiographic assessments were performed under standardized viewing conditions on a 27-inch monitor (2560 × 1440 resolution) in a dimly lit room.

Brightness and contrast adjustments were permitted solely for visualization purposes using the manufacturer’s native imaging software. No additional image processing, filtering, or enhancement procedures were applied.

Root Pulp visibility (RPV) assessment

RPV of the mandibular left second molar was evaluated according to the Olze classification system11, which categorizes root canal visibility into four stages (Stage 0–Stage 3) (Fig. 1).

Scoring was based on the degree of radiographic visibility of the root canals and applied consistently across all images.

Observer blinding and reliability

Radiographic scoring was performed by an oral and maxillofacial radiologist with seven years of clinical experience (M.A.) in dental radiology. The observer was blinded to the chronological age and sex of the individuals during image evaluation. Intra-observer reproducibility was assessed by re-evaluating approximately 10% of the sample (n = 95) after a 4-week interval. Agreement was quantified using Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (κ = 0,904). To determine inter-observer agreement, 10% of the images (n = 99) were randomly selected and re-evaluated by G.M. Agreement was quantified using the Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (κ = 0.863).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (Version 27, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test. Associations between categorical variables were evaluated using the chi-square (χ²) test. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the predictive effect of pulp stage on legal age classification (≥ 18 years), with results presented as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). An interaction term (pulp stage × sex) was tested. Diagnostic performance measures and ROC curve analysis were performed. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Intra-observer agreement was excellent (κ = 0.904), and inter-observer agreement was also high (κ = 0.863), indicating strong reproducibility of the RPV staging.

A total of 983 individuals were included in the study. The sample consisted of 491 females (49.9%) and 492 males (50.1%). The mean chronological age of the overall study population was 19.55 ± 4.76 years (median: 18; range: 14–30). The distribution of participants according to age group and sex showed that among individuals aged < 18 years (n = 480), 43.3% were female and 56.7% were male, whereas in the ≥ 18 years group (n = 503), 56.3% were female and 43.7% were male.

The distribution of pulp stages demonstrated that Stage 0 was the most frequently observed category (n = 547, 55.6%), followed by Stage 1 (n = 282, 28.7%), Stage 2 (n = 118, 12.0%), and Stage 3 (n = 36, 3.7%). A progressive increase in chronological age was observed across pulp stages. The mean ages corresponding to Stage 0, Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3 were 18.10 ± 4.21, 20.46 ± 4.73, 22.39 ± 4.46, and 25.08 ± 3.93 years, respectively (Table 1) (Fig. 2).

The distribution of pulp stages also demonstrated a statistically significant association with sex (χ² = 8.68, p = 0.034). Despite this difference, the general pattern of stage progression remained comparable between females and males (Table 2).

Logistic regression analysis revealed that pulp stage was a significant predictor of legal age classification. Using Stage 0 as the reference category, the odds of being classified as ≥ 18 years increased progressively with advancing pulp stages. Stage 1 increased the odds by 2.68 times (OR = 2.68, 95% CI: 1.99–3.62, p < 0.001), Stage 2 by 6.04 times (OR = 6.04, 95% CI: 3.75–9.73, p < 0.001), and Stage 3 by 19.13 times (OR = 19.13, 95% CI: 5.78–63.36, p < 0.001). Sex was also identified as a statistically significant variable (OR = 0.63, 95% CI: 0.48–0.82, p = 0.001). The model demonstrated a Nagelkerke R² value of 0.10 (Table 3).

Discussions

The present study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of mandibular left second molar RPV for legal age classification (≥ 18 years). Unlike conventional dental age estimation studies primarily focused on chronological age prediction, this study specifically addressed a legally relevant binary decision threshold. From a forensic perspective, the determination of whether an individual has reached legal adulthood constitutes a critical medico-legal question. Therefore, assessing RPV staging within a diagnostic accuracy framework provides clinically meaningful information beyond simple age correlation analyses.

Previous studies have predominantly investigated pulp-related parameters in third molars8,9,11,18–21. Although third molars offer certain advantages due to their prolonged development, they are also characterized by considerable anatomical variability and a relatively high prevalence of agenesis4,5,11,22,23. In contrast, second molars exhibit greater anatomical consistency and are less frequently affected by congenital absence. The selection of second molars in the present study was therefore intended to explore a potentially more stable and reproducible dental marker for legal age threshold assessment4,5.

Panoramic radiography was selected as the imaging modality due to its widespread clinical availability, non-invasive nature, and suitability for large retrospective datasets2,3. In forensic practice, methods requiring advanced imaging modalities such as CBCT or MRI may not always be feasible, particularly in large-scale evaluations or resource-limited settings3,4. Panoramic imaging provides a standardized two-dimensional representation of dental structures, enabling practical application of RPV staging systems. Moreover, panoramic radiographs remain one of the most frequently utilized imaging tools in forensic odontology, supporting their methodological relevance4,5.

Suvarna et al.24 evaluated RPV staging in a cohort of 936 individuals aged 14–22 years and reported that Stage 0 was predominantly observed in individuals younger than 18 years, whereas higher stages were more frequently detected in individuals aged 18 years and older. Notably, the majority of individuals classified as Stage 1 were ≥ 18 years, and Stage 2 was largely restricted to individuals above the legal threshold. These findings are broadly consistent with the stage distribution pattern observed in the present study, where Stage 0 represented the most frequent category (55.6%), followed by Stage 1 (28.7%), Stage 2 (12.0%), and Stage 3 (3.7%). Lower pulp stages were predominantly detected in individuals younger than 18 years, whereas higher stages were more frequently observed among individuals aged 18 years and older, reflecting the expected biological progression of secondary dentin deposition.

Variability in stage frequencies across studies is not unexpected and likely reflects differences in sample composition, age distribution, and methodological factors9,19. The absence or low prevalence of specific pulp stages should therefore not be interpreted as methodological inconsistency. For example, although Sami et al.23, did not observe Stage 3 cases in individuals aged 18–40 years, da Silva et al.25, reported that Stage 3 was detectable in both sexes aged 18 years and older in a Brazilian population. Collectively, these findings suggest that inter-study differences are unlikely to be attributable solely to variations in maximum age limits, but rather to broader population and study design characteristics.

Logistic regression analysis provided further insight into the probabilistic relationship between pulp stages and legal age classification. The observed stepwise increase in odds ratios across stages indicates a biologically coherent progression pattern. Compared with Stage 0, individuals classified as Stage 1 demonstrated a 2.68-fold increase in the odds of being classified as ≥ 18 years, whereas Stage 2 and Stage 3 were associated with markedly higher probabilities (OR = 6.04 and OR = 19.13, respectively). This gradient-like increase strongly supports the interpretation of RPV staging as a maturity-related marker reflecting cumulative secondary dentin deposition rather than a simple categorical association. From a forensic perspective, these findings reinforce the value of RPV staging as a probabilistic discriminator aligned with threshold-based age assessment frameworks.

Although absolute stage frequencies may vary across studies, the overall distributional pattern remains consistent with the cumulative nature of pulp obliteration. The large sample size and the focus on a legally relevant threshold (≥ 18 years) further strengthen the forensic applicability of the findings. Direct comparisons between studies remain challenging due to variations in tooth selection, sample composition, and methodological approaches. In this context, variability in reported stage-related age values likely reflects differences in population characteristics and study design9,19.

Previous studies have reported varying diagnostic performance metrics for RPV assessment. da Silva et al.25 reported an accuracy value of 72%, whereas the present study demonstrated an accuracy of 65.5%. Such differences are not unexpected and likely reflect variations in study design, tooth selection, and sample composition9,19. Similarly, Akkaya et al.21 reported AUC values of 0.933 in males and 0.83 in females, while the present study yielded AUC values of 0.68 and 0.67 for males and females, respectively. Direct comparisons between studies should be interpreted with caution, as Akkaya et al.21 evaluated third molars, whereas the present study focused on second molars. Collectively, these findings suggest that diagnostic performance metrics may vary depending on the tooth type, population characteristics, and methodological factors. Importantly, the present study demonstrates that second molar RPV assessment may provide diagnostically useful information for legal age threshold evaluation. Vangala et al.26 evaluated first, second, and third molars using a 21-year cutoff and reported higher false-negative rates in first and second molars, highlighting the influence of tooth selection on diagnostic outcomes.

In this study, the overall classification accuracy was 65.5%, with an AUC value of 0.676, indicating moderate global discriminatory performance. However, consistent with previous literature, the diagnostic behavior of individual pulp stages revealed a specificity-dominant pattern. Stage ≥ 1 provided the most balanced diagnostic profile (sensitivity = 0.596; specificity = 0.717), whereas higher stages demonstrated progressively increasing specificity. Stage ≥ 2 exhibited high specificity (0.942) and positive predictive value (0.818), while Stage ≥ 3 yielded near-perfect specificity (0.994), albeit with limited sensitivity (0.066).

A similar pattern has been consistently reported across diverse populations. In the South Indian cohort evaluating first, second, and third molars, Vangala et al.26 demonstrated strong discriminatory capacity for the second molar (AUC = 0.828), accompanied by specificity values exceeding 96% at stage-based cut-offs. Notably, increasing pulp stages were associated with progressively higher post-test probabilities, closely mirroring the specificity-driven behavior observed in the present dataset. Likewise, Kumar et al.27, assessing the diagnostic accuracy of the Olze staging system at the 18-, 21-, and 25-year thresholds, documented specificity values approaching 100% at higher pulp stages, with minimal Type II error. These convergent findings collectively indicate that reduced sensitivity at advanced stages represents an expected diagnostic characteristic of the method rather than a methodological limitation.

Comparable findings have also been reported in the Turkish population. Çakan28, comparing Cameriere’s third molar maturity index and Olze’s RPV staging, demonstrated that although both methods were capable of differentiating individuals below and above the legal age threshold, RPV staging showed a more favorable balance between sensitivity and specificity. Importantly, the study reinforced that stage progression was associated with increasing mean age values, while higher stages provided stronger specificity-driven discrimination. These observations are methodologically consistent with the diagnostic behavior identified in the present study.

European population data further reinforce this interpretation. In the Maltese sample, al Qattan et al.29 reported that stages corresponding to pronounced pulp obliteration were observed exclusively in individuals above the 18-year threshold, whereas earlier stages extended across a broad age spectrum. This distributional behavior explains why moderate overall accuracy does not diminish the evidential value of advanced stages. The variability observed at lower stages likely reflects biological heterogeneity in secondary dentin deposition, a process known to exhibit substantial inter-individual and inter-population variation.

From a forensic decision-making perspective, this trade-off is diagnostically advantageous. Legal age assessment prioritizes minimizing false-positive classifications (Type I error), particularly in medico-legal contexts involving criminal responsibility and civil status determinations. Within this framework, the extremely high specificity associated with Stage ≥ 2 (0.942) and Stage ≥ 3 (0.994) becomes diagnostically more meaningful than moderate global discrimination indices such as AUC2,3,27.

Furthermore, the evaluation of second molars offers a notable methodological advantage over third molars. Third molar agenesis, impaction, and morphological variability frequently limit evaluability, whereas second molars display greater anatomical stability and higher applicability rates25,26. This practical robustness strengthens the utility of second molar RPV staging as a complementary maturity marker.

Taken together, the concordance between our results and prior Brazilian, Indian, and European investigations supports the external consistency of RPV staging. Although moderate AUC values indicate limited standalone discrimination, advanced pulp stages remain highly reliable indicators of legal adulthood. Accordingly, RPV staging should be interpreted probabilistically and ideally integrated with other dental or skeletal maturity markers, consistent with contemporary forensic age assessment strategies. Consistent with the observed AUC values, RPV staging of mandibular second molars demonstrates moderate discriminatory performance and should be considered a complementary indicator rather than an independent diagnostic tool.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. The retrospective design inherently limited control over image acquisition parameters and patient-related variables, and although only radiographs of acceptable diagnostic quality were included, variations in exposure conditions and anatomical superimpositions characteristic of panoramic imaging may have influenced pulp visibility assessment. Furthermore, the use of two-dimensional panoramic radiographs represents a methodological constraint, as panoramic imaging is susceptible to geometric distortion, magnification variability, and structural overlap, all of which may affect the accurate visualization of root canal structures; advanced imaging modalities such as cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) could provide superior structural delineation and potentially improve staging reliability. The sample was derived from a single-center cohort, which may restrict the generalizability of the results, particularly given that secondary dentin deposition is known to exhibit substantial inter-individual and inter-population variability. Although both intra- and inter-observer agreement were high, categorical radiographic staging systems may still be influenced by observer-related variability. Additionally, the relatively low frequency of advanced pulp stages, especially Stage ≥ 3, may have contributed to reduced sensitivity estimates, a distributional characteristic that reflects the natural biological progression of pulp obliteration but nonetheless influences diagnostic performance metrics. Importantly, the overall diagnostic performance of RPV staging demonstrated moderate discriminatory ability, indicating that it should not be interpreted as a standalone age estimation method. Finally, RPV staging, like other dental maturity indicators, should be interpreted within a multifactorial framework and integrated with complementary dental or skeletal markers to enhance forensic decision-making accuracy. Despite these limitations, the study benefits from a large sample size, standardized imaging parameters, and observer blinding, which collectively enhance the reliability and forensic relevance of the findings.

Conclusion

RPV staging demonstrated moderate overall discriminatory performance for legal age classification. Nevertheless, regression modeling revealed a clear stage-dependent increase in the odds of legal adulthood, supporting the biological plausibility of RPV staging as a threshold-oriented maturity indicator rather than a precise chronological age estimator. From a forensic perspective, the specificity-dominant diagnostic behavior observed at advanced pulp stages retains substantial evidential value, particularly in decision-making frameworks prioritizing the minimization of false-negative classifications. Accordingly, RPV staging should be interpreted probabilistically and preferably integrated with complementary dental or skeletal maturity markers to enhance age assessment reliability.

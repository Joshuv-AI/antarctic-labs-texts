On some properties of special functions involving k k -gamma and k − k- digamma functions Thanks: Corresponding author: Li Yin
Li Yin, Jumei Zhang

Abstract
. Based on k k -gamma and k − k- digamma functions, we show four series expansions to the Furdui-type integral related to Riemann zeta function and hypergeometric function, and also present some new identities, series expansions and inequalities on the Hadamard k k -gamma function and the Nielsen k k -beta function. Finally, we also pose an open problem.

Introduction

The Euler gamma function is defined all positive real numbers
x

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt.

It is common knowledge that the logarithmic derivative of
\Gamma(x)

\psi^{(m)}(x)

\psi(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\ln\Gamma(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)}=-\gamma-\frac{1}{x}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x}{n(n+x)},

\psi^{(m)}(x)=\frac{{d^{m}}}{{dx^{m}}}\psi(x)=(-1)^{m}m!\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{{(n+x)^{m+1}}}},x>0.

3.
Series expansions of Furdui-type integral

In 2014, Furdui proposed an open problem 103 in Volume 4, Issue 3 of Mathproblems:

Open Problem.
Calculate, if possible, in terms of the well-known constants the integral
\int_{0}^{1}{{x^{m}}{\psi}(x)}dx
where
k\geq 3
is an integer. If
m=2
, he obtained the formula
\int_{0}^{1}{{x^{2}}{\psi}(x)}dx=\ln\left(\frac{A}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right),
where the constant
A
is Glaisher-Kinkelin constant.
Based on Riemann zeta function and hypergeometric function, we calculate more generalized Furdui integral, and present several series expansions formulas for this type of integral.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
k>0,m\in\mathbb{{N}}
, we have

\int_{0}^{k}{{x^{m}}{\psi_{k}}(x)dx=\frac{{{k^{m}}(\ln k-\gamma)}}{{m+1}}}-\frac{{{k^{m}}}}{m}+{k^{m}}\sum\limits_{s=2}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{s}}}}{{m+s}}}\zeta(s).

(3.1)

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
and the identities

\psi(x+1)=\psi(x)+\frac{1}{x}

\psi(x+1)=-\gamma+\sum\limits_{s=2}^{\infty}{{{(-1)}^{s}}}\zeta(s){x^{s-1}},

(3.2)

(The formula (
3.2
) may see formula 8. 363. 1 in reference
[
10
]
)
we may obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{k}{{x^{m}}{\psi_{k}}(x)dx}=\frac{{{k^{m}}\ln k}}{{m+1}}+\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{k}{{x^{m}}\psi\left({\frac{x}{k}}\right)dx}\\
=\frac{{{k^{m}}\ln k}}{{m+1}}+{k^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}{{t^{m}}\psi\left(t\right)dt}\\
=\frac{{{k^{m}}(\ln k-\gamma)}}{{m+1}}-\frac{{{k^{m}}}}{m}+{k^{m}}\sum\limits_{s=2}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{s}}}}{{m+s}}}\zeta(s).\end{array}

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1

(4.2)

(4.3)

It is worth noting that Luschny
[

]

{\Gamma_{k}}\left({\frac{k}{2}+x}\right){\Gamma_{k}}\left({\frac{k}{2}-x}\right)=\frac{\pi}{{\cos\left({\frac{{2x}}{k}}\right)}}.

Taking the logarithm and then differentiating, we get

{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{k}{2}+x}\right)-{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{k}{2}-x}\right)=\frac{\pi}{k}\tan\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{k}}\right).

Similarly, we also get

{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{x}{2}}\right)+{\psi_{k}}\left({k-\frac{x}{2}}\right)=-\frac{\pi}{k}\cot\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{{2k}}}\right).

Furthermore, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}{H_{k}}(x)=\frac{{{\Gamma_{k}}(x)\sin\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{k}}\right)}}{{2\pi}}\left[{{\psi_{k}}\left({k-\frac{x}{2}}\right)-{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{{k-x}}{2}}\right)}\right]\\
=\frac{{{\Gamma_{k}}(x)\sin\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{k}}\right)}}{{2\pi}}\left[{{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{x}{2}}\right)+\frac{\pi}{k}\cot\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{{2k}}}\right)-{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{{k+x}}{2}}\right)+\frac{\pi}{k}\tan\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{{2k}}}\right)}\right]\\
=\frac{{{\Gamma_{k}}(x)}}{k}-\frac{{{\Gamma_{k}}(x)\sin\left({\frac{{\pi x}}{k}}\right)}}{\pi}{\beta_{k}}(x).\end{array}

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The inequality

k^{y/k}H_{k}(x)+k^{x/k}H_{k}(y)\leq H_{k}(x+y)

(4.9)

holds for all real numbers
x,y\geq\alpha
and
k>0
if and only if
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. Here,
\alpha_{0}
is the only solution of
H_{k}(2x)=2k^{x/k}H_{k}(x)
in
[1.5k,\infty)
.

Proof.

Simple computation yields
{H_{k}}(x)={k^{\frac{x}{k}-1}}{H}\left({\frac{x}{k}}\right)
. Noting Alzer inequality
4.4
, we can complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

For all real number
\left|x\right|<1
,

2x\Phi(-1,1,-x)=\Phi\left(1,1,1-\frac{x}{2}\right)-\Phi\left(1,1,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{x}{2}\right),

(4.10)

where
\Phi(z,s,a)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{z^{n}}}}{{{{(n+a)}^{s}}}}}
is Lerch zeta function.

Proof.

On one hand, we have (See Wikipedia Hadamard gamma function)

{H_{k}}(x)={k^{\frac{x}{k}-1}}{H}\left({\frac{x}{k}}\right)={k^{\frac{x}{k}-1}}\frac{{\Phi\left({-1,1,-\frac{x}{k}}\right)}}{{\Gamma\left({-\frac{x}{k}}\right)}}.

On the other hand, by using identities of integral

\Phi\left({z,s,a}\right)=\frac{1}{{\Gamma(s)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{t^{s-1}}{e^{-at}}}}{{1-z{e^{-t}}}}dt}

and formula (1.3), we also obtain

{H_{k}}(x)=\frac{1}{k}\left[{\frac{{\Phi\left({1,1,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{x}{{2k}}}\right)-\Phi\left({1,1,1-\frac{x}{{2k}}}\right)}}{{-2x{\Gamma_{k}}(-x)}}}\right].

Furthermore, we have

\frac{{2x}}{k}\Phi\left({-1,1,-\frac{x}{k}}\right)=\Phi\left({1,1,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{x}{{2k}}}\right)-\Phi\left({1,1,1-\frac{x}{{2k}}}\right).

The proof is complete.

∎

5.
Several identities for the Nielsen
k
-beta function

The Nielsen’s
\beta
-function is defined as (
[
22
,
27
]
)

\displaystyle\beta(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x-1}}{1+t}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-xt}}{1+e^{-t}}dt=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+x}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right\}

(5.1)

where
x\in(0,\infty).

The function can be used to calculate some integrals(
[
23
]
). Recently, K. Nantomah studied the properties and inequalities of a
p-
generalization of the Nielsen’s function in
[
22
]
.
In 2019, Zhang et. al.
[
36
]
discussed a new
k
-generalization of the Nielsen’s
\beta
-function. Later, they studied the completely monotonicity, convexity and inequalities of the new function. The new function has been called Nielsen
k-
beta function. Its definition is as follows:

\displaystyle\beta_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{x-1}}{1+t^{k}}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-xt}}{1+e^{-kt}}dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\frac{1}{2nk+x}-\frac{1}{2nk+k+x}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\psi_{k}\left(\frac{x+k}{2}\right)-\psi_{k}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right\}

(5.3)

where
k>0,x\in(0,\infty).

Next, we will mainly study some series expansions of this function, which are based on the Riemann zeta function.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
x>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}{{\beta_{k}}\left[{{{(2k)}^{m}}x}\right]=}{\psi_{k}}\left({{2^{n}}{k^{n}}x}\right)-{\psi_{k}}\left({kx}\right)-\frac{{n\ln 2}}{k}.

(5.4)

Proof.

Applying the Legendre relation

\Gamma(2x)=\frac{{{2^{2x-1}}}}{{\sqrt{\pi}}}\Gamma(x)\Gamma\left({x+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

and Lemma
2.1
, we get

{\Gamma_{k}}(2kx)=\frac{{{2^{2x-1}}}}{{\sqrt{k\pi}}}{\Gamma_{k}}(kx){\Gamma_{k}}\left({kx+\frac{k}{2}}\right).

(5.5)

Taking the logarithm of both sides of the formula
5.5
and differentiating, we have

{\psi_{k}}\left({kx+\frac{k}{2}}\right)=2{\psi_{k}}\left({2kx}\right)-{\psi_{k}}\left({kx}\right)-\frac{{2\ln 2}}{k}.

(5.6)

Furthermore, we can get

\begin{array}[]{l}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}{{\beta_{k}}\left[{{{(2k)}^{m}}x}\right]=}\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{{2kx+k}}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({kx}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{{4kx+k}}{2}}\right)\\
-\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({2kx}\right)+\;\cdots+\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({\frac{{{{(2k)}^{n}}x+k}}{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{k}}\left({{{(2k)}^{n-1}}x}\right)\\
\end{array}

Substitute Formula
5.6
into the above equation, and then the proof of the Theorem
5.1
can be completed.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

For
x>0
and
k>0
, we have

{\beta_{k}}(x)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{x+nk}}.}

(5.7)

Proof.

The series expansion
\frac{1}{{1+x}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{{{(-1)}^{n}}{x^{n}}}
can result in

{\beta_{k}}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{{{t^{x-1}}}}{{1+{t^{k}}}}dt=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{{{(-1)}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}{{t^{x+kn-1}}dt}}=}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{x+nk}}.}

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

For
x>0
and
k>0
, we have

{\beta_{k}}\left({\frac{{x+k}}{2}}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{e^{-xt}}}}{{\cosh(kt)}}dt=}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{{2{e^{-xt}}}}{{{e^{kt}}+{e^{-kt}}}}dt.}

(5.8)

Proof.

On one hand, we easily obtain

{\beta_{k}}\left({\frac{{x+k}}{2}}\right)=2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{x+2nk+k}}.}

by using Theorem
5.2
. On the other hand, the substitution
u=e^{-kt}
yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{{2{e^{-xt}}}}{{{e^{kt}}+{e^{-kt}}}}dt}=\frac{2}{k}\int_{0}^{1}{\frac{{{u^{x/k}}}}{{1+{u^{2}}}}du}\\
=\frac{2}{k}\int_{0}^{1}{{u^{x/k}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{{{(-1)}^{n}}{u^{2n}}}du}=2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{x+2nk+k}}.}\end{array}

The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

For
|x|<k
and
k>0
, we have

{\beta_{k}}\left({x+k}\right)=\frac{{\ln 2}}{k}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{m}}(1-{2^{-m}})\zeta(m+1)}}{{{k^{m+1}}}}{x^{m}}.}

(5.9)

Proof.

By using the Taylor formula, we have

{\beta_{k}}\left({x+k}\right)={\beta_{k}}\left(k\right)+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\beta_{k}^{(m)}\left(k\right)}}{{m!}}{x^{m}}=\frac{{\ln 2}}{k}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{m+n}}}}{{{{(k+nk)}^{m+1}}}}}{x^{m}}}.}

Considering to the identity

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{{{(1+n)}^{m+1}}}}}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n}}}}{{{n^{m+1}}}}}-\frac{1}{{{2^{m}}}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{{{n^{m}}}}}=(1-{2^{-m}})\zeta(m+1),

we may get the series expansion
5.9
.
By applying the Cauchy criterion, we obtain that the convergence domain of the series is
|x|<k
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

By using the identity
[
36
, Theorem 2.2]

{\beta_{k}}\left({x+k}\right)+{\beta_{k}}\left({x}\right)=\frac{1}{x},

(5.10)

so, we may obtained a power series expansion of
x{\beta_{k}}\left({x}\right)
as follows:

x{\beta_{k}}\left({x}\right)=1-\frac{{x\ln 2}}{k}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{m+1}}(1-{2^{-m}})\zeta(m+1)}}{{{k^{m+1}}}}{x^{m+1}}.}

This is an alternative series. So we can obtain the following inequalities: For
0<x<k,
we have

\frac{1}{x}-\frac{\ln 2}{k}<\beta_{k}(x)<\frac{1}{x}

and

\frac{1}{x}-\frac{\ln 2}{k}<\beta_{k}(x)<\frac{1}{x}-\frac{\ln 2}{k}+\frac{\pi^{2}x}{12k^{2}}.

.

For
x\neq 0
and
k>0

{\beta_{k}}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{{x+k}}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}{\frac{{{{(-1)}^{n+1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}n\\

(5.11)

Theorem 5.6
.

For
x>0
and
k>0
, the function
\beta_{k}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta_{k}(x)\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}{\beta_{k}(x)+\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}\leq\beta_{k}(k)=\frac{\ln 2}{k},

(5.12)

where the equality is right for
x=k
.

Proof.

The case for equality is trivial. Let
\mu(x)=\ln\left(\frac{2\beta_{k}(x)\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}{\beta_{k}(x)+\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}\right).

Then direct computations result in

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{k}(x)}{\beta_{k}(x)}-\frac{k^{2}}{x^{2}}\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{k}(k^{2}/x)}{\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{k}(x)-\frac{k^{2}}{x^{2}}\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}{\beta_{k}(x)+\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}

(5.13)

which indicates that

\displaystyle x\left[\frac{1}{\beta_{k}(x)}+\frac{1}{\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)}\right]\mu^{\prime}(x)=x\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{k}(x)}{[\beta_{k}(x)]^{2}}-\frac{k^{2}}{x}\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{k}(k^{2}/x)}{[\beta_{k}(k^{2}/x)]^{2}}.

(5.14)

In view of Lemma
2.7
, then
\mu^{\prime}(x)<0,~x\in(k,\infty)
and
\mu^{\prime}(x)>0,~x\in(0,k)
. Hence,
\mu(x)
is decreasing on
(k,\infty)
and increasing on
(0,k)
. By using the properties of the logarithmic function, the proof is complete.

∎

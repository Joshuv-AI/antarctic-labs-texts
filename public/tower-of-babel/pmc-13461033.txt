Tgfbr3 is required for the long-term maintenance of self-renewal potential in murine hematopoietic stem cells
Authors: Xialin Li, Qitai Yang, Jinglei Zhai, Xiaobing Zhang, Fang Dong, Hideo Ema
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) signaling plays a crucial role in maintaining the quiescence and self-renewal potential of hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). Despite decades of research, the underlying mechanisms of TGF-β signaling in HSCs remain elusive due to conflicting phenotypes of various knockout (KO) mouse models. Here, we show that HSCs co-express Tgfbr2, Tgfbr3, and Endoglin (Eng) but rarely express Tgfbr1, whereas lymphocytes co-express Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2 but rarely express Tgfbr3 or Eng. We also demonstrate that Tgfbr3 is dispensable for the maintenance of immune homeostasis, in contrast to Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2, either of which is essential for lymphocyte homeostasis. Serial transplantation assays revealed that deletion of Tgfbr3 in HSCs had little effect on short-term reconstitution but impaired long-term self-renewal potential, a similar phenotype observed in Eng conditional KO mice. Therefore, we propose that lymphocytes rely on the Tgfbr1/Tgfbr2 complex as suggested by the classical model, whereas HSCs require the unique Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3/Eng complex to orchestrate TGF-β signaling. Collectively, this study reveals Tgfbr3 as a critical regulator in the maintenance of HSC self-renewal potential and suggests a novel TGF-β receptor complex specific to HSCs.

1. INTRODUCTION

Adult hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs), capable of generating all blood cell lineages, are in the quiescent (G0) state most of the time.1–3 The G0 state is essential to maintain the self-renewal potential in HSCs throughout a lifetime.4–7 Among the regulatory networks governing the G0 state of HSCs, transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) plays a role as one of the negative regulators.8 However, the precise molecular mechanisms by which TGF-β signaling regulates HSC function remain incompletely understood because of inconsistent phenotypes observed in different knockout (KO) mouse models.

Canonically, TGF-β signaling is initiated by ligands binding with TGF-β type II receptor (Tgfbr2), which then recruits and phosphorylates type I receptor (Tgfbr1), leading to activation of downstream effectors like Smad proteins.9–12 In some cases, type III receptors Tgfbr3 (Betaglycan) and Endoglin (Eng), also known as co-receptors, are required to facilitate ligand–receptor binding.13,14 However, genetic studies have yielded inconsistent phenotypes after deletion of specific signaling components, challenging the canonical model of TGF-β signaling activation. For instance, HSCs deficient in Tgfbr1 or Smad2/3 maintained normal reconstitution capacity,15,16 whereas conditional deletion of Tgfbr2 or Smad4 compromised HSC quiescence and reconstitution.17,18 Furthermore, Eng has emerged as another critical molecule, the absence of which impaired long-term self-renewal potential in HSCs after serial transplantation.19 On the other hand, the effects of TGF-β receptors on immune cells further complicated our understanding.20,21 Mice lacking either Tgfbr1 or Tgfbr2 died of a lethal inflammatory disorder (LID) due to overreaction of lymphocytes, whereas Eng-deficient mice showed no signs of LID.16 Despite diverse phenotypes of TGF-β receptor deletion, the effects of Tgfbr3 on hematopoiesis remain largely unexplored.

To reconcile the discrepancies between TGF-β receptor KO phenotypes, we hypothesized that HSCs utilize a specific combination of TGF-β receptors distinct from lymphocytes. In this study, we analyzed the expression profiles of TGF-β receptors in individual HSCs and found that the receptor combination of Tgfbr2, Tgfbr3, and Eng was expressed more abundantly than the canonical complex of Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2. In addition, analysis of scRNA-seq data revealed that lymphocytes rarely expressed Tgfbr3 and Eng, supporting the notion that lymphocytes were independent of type III receptors, as opposed to HSCs. We then deleted Tgfbr3 specifically in HSCs combined with serial transplantation assays and demonstrated that the self-renewal potential of Tgfbr3-deficient HSCs was impaired, a phenotype similar to that of Eng deficiency. Our findings revealed an important role of Tgfbr3 in the maintenance of HSC self-renewal potential. We propose a revised model where the co-receptors, Tgfbr3 and Eng, play a critical yet previously underestimated role in HSC regulation.

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS

2.1. Mice

Mice were bred and maintained in the animal facility of the Institute of Hematology and Blood Diseases Hospital. Rosa26-Cas9 knock-in mice (Cas9-EGFP mice) (#026179) were obtained from the Jackson Laboratory. B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice and B6-CD45.2 mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology. Eight- to 12-week-old male and female Cas9-EGFP mice served as donors, and 10-week-old B6.SJL-CD45.1 female mice served as recipients. All animal experiments were performed individually in compliance with the animal care guidelines approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees of the State Key Laboratory of Experimental Hematology, Institute of Hematology and Blood Diseases Hospital.

2.2. Flow cytometry and cell sorting

BM cells were flushed from the femora, tibiae, and iliac crests of Cas9-EGFP mice with 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Waltham, Massachusetts) and 2 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, Missouri) in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) using a 26-gauge syringe needle (BD Microlance, Franklin Lakes, New Jersey). BM cells were filtered through a 50 µm nylon mesh and washed with ice-cold PBS with 2% FBS and 2 mM EDTA. Before antibody staining, c-Kit+ cells were enriched with anti-c-Kit antibody-conjugated MACS beads (130-091-224, MACS Miltenyi Biotec, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany). Cells were stained with APC-Cy7-anti-TER-119 (TER-119), APC-Cy7-anti-B220 (RA3-6B2), APC-Cy7-anti-Gr-1 (RB6-BC5), BV605-anti-c-Kit (2B8), BV785-anti-Sca-1 (D7), PE-anti-CD150 (TC15-12F12.2), PE-Cy7-anti-CD48 (HM48-1), and Percp-Cy5.5-anti-CD201 (1560) antibodies. Doublets were minimized with the appropriate side scatter pulse width (SSC-W) and side scatter pulse height (SSC-H) settings, and dead cells were excluded by 4’, 6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (Sigma-Aldrich) staining. EGFP+CD201+CD150+CD48-Kit+Sca-1+Lin- (HSC1) cells were sorted on a FACSAria III (BD Biosciences, San Jose, California).

Peripheral blood (PB) was collected via the tail at the indicated weeks after transplantation. For the exclusion of erythrocytes, PB was treated with buffer containing 0.15 M/L NH4Cl, 10 mmol/L KHCO3, and 0.1 mmol/L EDTA in water (pH 7.2). Cells were stained with APC-Cy7-anti-Gr-1 (RB6-8C5), APC-Cy7-anti-CD11b (M1/70), Percp-Cy5.5-anti-B220 (RA3-6B2), and PE-Cy7-anti-CD3 (145-2C11) antibodies. Cells were analyzed on a BD FACSCanto II Cell Analyzer (BD Biosciences). Flow cytometry data were analyzed using FlowJo software. Total reconstitution was defined by the percentage of EGFP+Crimson+ cells in PB. Myeloid cell reconstitution was defined by the percentage of EGFP+Crimson+Gr-1/CD11b+B220-CD3- cells in PB. B cell reconstitution was defined by the percentage of EGFP+Crimson+Gr-1/CD11b-B220+CD3- cells in PB. T cell reconstitution was defined by the percentage of EGFP+Crimson+Gr-1/CD11b-B220-CD3+ cells in PB.

2.3. Single-cell RT-PCR

Single CD150+CD41-CD34-LSK cells were sorted into 48 wells of a 96-well plate, where each well contained 10 μL of a reverse transcription and specific-target amplification mixture (RT buffer) consisting of 2.5 μL of 0.2× primers, 5.0 μL of a 2× reaction mix, 0.5 μL of Superscript III, and 2.0 μL of Tris-EDTA buffer. Reverse transcription was performed at 50°C for 15 minutes. The samples were incubated at 95°C for 2 minutes, followed by 22 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 4 minutes. 5.0 μL, taken from the samples, was mixed with 20 μL of Tris-EDTA and was used for real-time PCR. Then, 2.7 μL, taken from the diluted samples, was mixed with 3.0 μL TaqMan universal PCR master mix (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, Massachusetts) and 0.3 μL of sample loading buffer (a total of 6.0 μL sample loading mix). On the other hand, 3.0 μL of each set of 20× primers was mixed with 3.0 μL of the assay loading reagent (a total of 6.0 μL assay loading mix). Then, 5.0 μL of the sample loading mix and 5.0 μL of the assay loading mix were applied to a 48-chip, and 48 × 48 reactions were prepared by an integrated fluidic circuit controller. The chip was set on a Fluidigm Biomark system and was incubated at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 60 seconds. The data were analyzed by Biomark real-time PCR analysis software (Fluidigm, South San Francisco, California). All PCR primers were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, Massachusetts).

2.4. sgRNA design and plasmid construction

Lentiviral vectors (pRSC-U6-sgRNA-PGK-Crimson) were constructed with a NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly Kit (New England Biolabs, Ipswich, Massachusetts).22 The CHOPCHOP website (https://chopchop.cbu.uib.no/) was used to design sgRNAs targeting TGF-β receptors. sgTgfbr1 (GGCGAAGGGTGAGTGGCGGG), sgTgfbr2 (GTCATGGCCAGCGACAATGG), and sgTgfbr3 (GAGTGTGCACGGAGGCAATG) were used in this study. A scramble sgRNA (GGAGTACGCAGCTGCACAGG) was used as a control. The vector sequences were verified by Sanger sequencing.

2.5. Lentiviral packaging

For lentiviral packaging, the vector plasmids (pRSC-U6-sgRNA-PGK-Crimson) and lentivirus packaging helper plasmids (pMD2.G, psPAX2) were transfected into HEK293T cells cultured with 30% FBS in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific), using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). After 8 hours, the medium was changed. After 48 to 72 hours, the lentiviral supernatant was harvested and filtered with a 0.45-mm filter. Filtered virus supernatant was concentrated 100-fold by centrifugation at 20,000 ×g for 2 hours and re-suspended in F12 medium (Gibco).

2.6. Lentiviral transduction and transplantation

Five hundred HSC1 cells were sorted into 96-well tissue culture plates (Corning, Corning, New York) coated with 50 μL retronectin (0.128 μg/μL; Takara, Shiga, Japan). Cells were pre-stimulated with 50 ng/mL recombinant mouse stem cell factor (mSCF; Peprotech), 50 ng/mL recombinant mouse thrombopoietin (mTPO; Peprotech, Cranbury, New Jersey), and 10 ng/mL recombinant mouse interleukin-3 (mIL-3; Peprotech) in 200 μL of serum-free F12 medium supplemented with 1% ITS-X (Gibco), 10 mM HEPES (Gibco), 100 µM MEM nonessential amino acids (Gibco), 1% penicillin/streptomycin/glutamine (P/S/G, Gibco), 2 mM L-glutamine (Gibco), 55 µM 2-mercaptoethanol (Gibco), and 0.1% good manufacturing practice grade polyvinyl alcohol (PVA; Japan Vam & Poval Co., Osaka, Japan) at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 18 hours. Cells were transfected with 2-5 μL lentivirus in 100 μL volume of culture medium in the presence of 4 μg/μL polybrene (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). After 8 hours, cells were collected and transplanted together with 5 × 105 BM competitor cells from B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice into lethally irradiated (9.0 Gy, two split doses with 4-hour intervals) B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice via the tail vein. For secondary transplantation, donor-derived EGFP+Crimson+ HSC1 cells were sorted and transplanted into lethally irradiated B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice together with 5 × 105 BM competitor cells of B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice. For tertiary transplantation, BM cells were collected from all secondary recipients and pooled. 1 × 107 BM cells were transplanted into lethally irradiated B6.SJL-CD45.1 mice. PB cells were analyzed 1, 2, 3, and 4 months after transplantation.

2.7. Determination of indel mutations

EGFP+Crimson+ BM cells were collected for genomic DNA extraction using a TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (DP304, TIANGEN, Beijing, China). DNA fragments surrounding the sgRNA target sites were amplified with KAPA HiFi DNA polymerase with the following PCR primers: sgTgfbr1 forward primer: AGTTACAAAGGGCCGGAGC; sgTgfbr1 reverse prim-er: GCGCATTTAGGCAGTGGAAT; sgTgfbr2 forward primer: AGACCCCCTTTCTCCAGAGG; sgTgfbr2 reverse primer: TGCTTTACGTCCACCATGGA; and sgTgfbr3 forward primer: CCGCTGGGTAGTGAAACAGT; sgTgfbr3 reverse primer: ACGCAATGTCTCGCAAAACA. The PCR cycling comprised 95°C for 3 minutes; 35 cycles of 98°C for 20 seconds, 64°C for 15 seconds, and 72°C for 30 seconds; and 72°C for 10 minutes. Column-purified PCR products were used for Sanger sequencing. The indel percentage was determined by the web-based tool Inference of CRISPR Edits (ICE).23 All analysis results were based on an R2 value >0.9.

2.8. Western blotting

EGFP+Crimson+ BM cells were washed with PBS and lysed with 200 µL NuPage LDS sample buffer (NP0007, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California) supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail (78438, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Samples were heated at 95°C for 10 minutes and transferred to ice. Twenty microliters of the sample was subjected to 10% gradient sodium dodecyl sulfate‐polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (IPVH00010, Millipore, Billerica, Massachusetts). PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 and 5% non-fat milk (Bio-Rad, Hercules, California) was used as a blocking buffer and antibody incubation buffer. After blocking the membranes for 1 hour, the following primary antibodies were added: rabbit anti-Tgfbr1 antibody (1:600, ab235578, Abcam, Cambridge, Massachusetts), rabbit anti-Tgfbr2 antibody (1:600, ab269279, Abcam), rabbit anti-Tgfbr3 antibody (1:600, #5544, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, Massachusetts), and rabbit anti-beta actin antibody (1:2000, 66009-1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). After incubation with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C, the membranes were washed and further incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary goat anti-rabbit antibody (1:2000, A6154, Sigma-Aldrich) at room temperature for 1 hour. The proteins were detected with enhanced chemiluminescence reagents (WBKLS0500, Millipore) and the images were visualized using a ChemiDoc (Bio-Rad).

2.9. Statistical methods

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9. Two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Sidak multiple comparisons test and unpaired Student t test (2-tailed) were used as indicated in the figure legends. The number of samples is presented as n in the figure legends. All data are shown as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Significance levels were set at *p < 0.050 and **p < 0.010.

3. RESULTS

3.1. TGF-β receptors type II and type III are co-expressed in HSCs

To characterize the expression profile of TGF-β receptors on HSCs, we performed single-cell real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) with freshly isolated CD150+CD41-CD34-Lin-Sca-1+Kit+ cells (Supplementary Fig. 1A, https://links.lww.com/BS/A178). Tgfbr1 was detected in only 18.75% of cells, whereas Tgfbr2 expression was present in 43.75% of cells (Fig. 1A; Supplementary Fig. 1B–C, https://links.lww.com/BS/A178). In contrast, type III receptors were more abundantly expressed; 60.42% and 91.67% of cells were positive for Tgfbr3 and Eng, respectively (Fig. 1A; Supplementary Fig. S1B–C, https://links.lww.com/BS/A178). Interestingly, the simultaneous expression of Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2 was detected in only 8.33% of cells, while the expression of Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3, Tgfbr2/Eng, and Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3/Eng combinations was detected in 22.92%, 39.58%, and 22.92% of the cells, respectively (Fig. 1B). On closer examination, 19.05%, 52.38%, and 90.48% of Tgfbr2+ cells were positive for Tgfbr1, Tgfbr3, and Eng, respectively (Fig. 1C). These data suggested that Tgfbr2, Tgfbr3, and Eng may play more prominent roles than Tgfbr1 in HSCs. We thus hypothesized that the Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3/Eng complex potentially represents a critical yet underestimated component of TGF-β signaling in HSCs.

3.2. Tgfbr3 deficiency does not induce lethal inflammatory disease

To compare the in vivo effects of Tgfbr KO in HSCs, we used lentiviral single-guide RNA (sgRNA)-Crimson vectors and Cas9-EGFP mice,24 as previously reported.22 HSC1 cells were isolated from bone marrow (BM) of Cas9-EGFP mice, transduced with sgRNA-Crimson targeting Tgfbr1, Tgfbr2, and Tgfbr3, and transplanted into lethally irradiated mice with competitive BM cells (Fig. 2A; Supplementary Fig. 2A, https://links.lww.com/BS/A179). A high transduction efficiency of lentiviruses was confirmed by analyzing the EGFP+Crimson+ cells via flow cytometry (Supplementary Fig. 2B, https://links.lww.com/BS/A179). To verify KO of Tgfbr1, Tgfbr2, and Tgfbr3 in HSC1 cells, donor-derived BM cells were harvested 3 weeks after transplantation for Sanger sequencing and Western blotting (Fig. 2A). Sanger sequencing and Inference of CRISPR Edits (ICE) analysis23,25 revealed an insertion-deletion (indel) frequency of 84% for sgTgfbr1, 93% for sgTgfbr2, and 81% for sgTgfbr3 (Fig. 2B). In line with this, Western blotting showed that Tgfbr1, Tgfbr2, and Tgfbr3 proteins were undetectable in donor-derived cells (Fig. 2C), demonstrating successful KO mediated by sgRNAs in HSC1 cells. Consistent with prior studies using KO mouse models, mice transplanted with either Tgfbr1 or Tgfbr2 KO HSC1 cells died within 6 months after transplantation (Fig. 2D–E) due to an LID driven by dysregulated lymphocytes, especially activated T cells.15,26 In striking contrast, all mice transplanted with Tgfbr3 KO HSC1 cells survived and exhibited no signs of inflammation (Fig. 2E). These results indicated that Tgfbr3 is dispensable for maintaining immune homeostasis and does not act as a negative regulator for T cells.

To explore the molecular basis for the divergent phenotype induced by Tgfbr3 KO, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from our previous publication.27 Interestingly, HSC1 cells showed high levels of Eng expression and moderate levels of Tgfbr1, Tgfbr2, and Tgfbr3 expression (Fig. 2F). In contrast, lymphocytes, especially T cells, displayed scarce expression of either Tgfbr3 or Eng (Fig. 2F). This led us to speculate that lymphocytes, unlike HSCs, rely on both Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2 expression, but neither Tgfbr3 nor Eng expression, for TGF-β signaling.

3.3. Tgfbr3 deficiency does not impair short-term reconstitution

To evaluate the impact of Tgfbr3 KO on HSC1 function, we performed serial transplantation of Cas9-expressing HSC1 cells after transduction with either sgCtrl or sgTgfbr3 lentivirus. Primary transplantation was conducted as described in Figure 2A. For secondary transplantation, 1 × 107 whole BM cells (Fig. 3A) or 500 donor-derived HSC1 cells from primary recipients with 5 × 105 competitor cells (Supplementary Fig. 3A, https://links.lww.com/BS/A180) were transplanted into lethally irradiated recipients. For tertiary transplantation, 1 × 107 whole BM cells from secondary recipients were transplanted into lethally irradiated recipients (Fig. 3A; Supplementary Fig. 3A, https://links.lww.com/BS/A180).

In primary transplantation, Tgfbr3 KO HSC1 cells exhibited total and lineage reconstitution levels comparable to those of Ctrl HSC1 cells (Fig. 3B–C; Supplementary Fig. 3B–C, https://links.lww.com/BS/A180). BM analysis of primary recipients showed no difference in the percentages of donor-derived HSC1 cells and downstream multipotent progenitor (MPP) populations (MPP2, MPP3/4, and MPP5) (Fig. 3D; Supplementary Fig. 3D, https://links.lww.com/BS/A180). These data suggested that Tgfbr3 deficiency does not affect short-term HSC reconstitution potential, recapitulating the phenotype observed in the Eng conditional KO HSCs.19

3.4. Tgfbr3 KO impairs the long-term self-renewal potential in HSCs

The long-term self-renewal potential was assessed by serial transplantation (Fig 3A; Supplementary Fig. S3A, https://links.lww.com/BS/A180). In secondary recipients transplanted with whole BM cells, PB analysis showed no significant differences in the overall and lineage reconstitution (Fig. 4A–B). BM analysis revealed a decrease in the percentage of donor-derived HSC1 cells in the sgTgfbr3 group (Fig. 4C), while the MPP populations remained unaffected (Fig. 4C). To confirm the successful deletion of the Tgfbr3 gene by CRISPR/Cas9 technique, genomic DNA of donor-derived cells from BM of secondary recipients was subjected to Sanger sequencing and ICE analysis, which revealed an indel frequency of 86% (Fig. 4D), although deletions of large fragments (>100 bp) were likely underestimated. In an independent experiment where secondary recipients were transplanted with purified donor-HSC1 cells, PB reconstitution and BM analysis showed no significant differences in the sgTgfbr3 group (Supplementary Fig. 4A–C, https://links.lww.com/BS/A181), with an indel percentage of 74%, confirmed by Sanger sequencing (Supplementary Fig. 4D, https://links.lww.com/BS/A181). These data suggested that Tgfbr3 KO HSC1 cells were capable of multilineage reconstitution after secondary transplantation, possibly due to compensatory mechanisms mediated by other factors.

However, after tertiary transplantation, Tgfbr3 KO HSC1 cells exhibited a significantly lower reconstitution level compared with Ctrl HSC1 cells (Fig. 4E; Supplementary Fig. 4E, https://links.lww.com/BS/A181), with decreased myeloid and B cell reconstitution in PB 4 months after transplantation (Fig. 4F; Supplementary Fig. 4F, https://links.lww.com/BS/A181). BM analysis demonstrated a remarkably decreased percentage of donor-derived HSC1 cells 4 months after tertiary transplantation (Fig. 4G; Supplementary Fig. 4G, https://links.lww.com/BS/A181). Taken together, these data suggested that Tgfbr3 deficiency impaired the long-term self-renewal potential of HSC1 cells.

4. DISCUSSION

In this study, we propose a novel model where HSCs rely on the Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3/Eng complex for TGF-β signal transduction, distinct from the classically described model where cells like lymphocytes require Tgfbr1/Tgfbr2 to mediate response to TGF-β (Fig. 5). This cell-type–specific receptor complex might offer a plausible explanation for the previously observed normal function of HSCs with loss of Tgfbr1, and reduced long-term self-renewal potential of HSCs following deletion of Tgfbr2, Eng, or Tgfbr3.

In lymphocytes (left panel), TGF-β signaling is mediated by Tgfbr1 and Tgfbr2. In HSCs (right panel), Tgfbr3 and Eng serve as co-receptors of Tgfbr2, and these 3 receptors form a previously unrecognized complex to mediate TGF-β signaling. HSC = hematopoietic stem cell, TGF-β = transforming growth factor-β.

We used CD150+CD41−CD34−Lin−Sca-1+Kit+ cells for single-cell RT-PCR and CD201+CD150+CD48−Lin−Sca-1+Kit+ cells for transplantation assays. These HSC populations were compared in Supplementary Fig. 2C, https://links.lww.com/BS/A179, which indicated that the single-cell expression data can be used for the interpretation of the functional results of HSC1 transplantation experiments. Although Tgfbr1 has been considered an essential receptor in propagating TGF-β signaling, its expression was infrequently detected on HSCs. Interestingly, independence of Tgfbr1 in TGF-β signaling has been observed in non-hematopoietic cells, where Tgfbr2 directly phosphorylated the polarity protein partitioning defective 6 (Par6).28,29 Moreover, ligand cross-linking experiments revealed negligible Tgfbr1 within the complex formed by Tgfbr2 and Tgfbr3,14,30 further supporting the notion that Tgfbr1 is not required when Tgfbr3 formed a complex with Tgfbr2.

Tgfbr3 binds and potentiates all three isoforms of TGF-β by presenting them to Tgfbr2, thereby enhancing ligand–receptor binding affinity, particularly for TGF-β2.31,32 Despite a lack of cytosolic kinase domain, several lines of evidence have suggested that Tgfbr3 can be phosphorylated and interacts with β-arrestin 2, which leads to subsequent modulation of downstream signaling, including TGF-β and nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) pathways.33,34 Additionally, TGFBR3 has been reported to regulate phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-protein kinase B (Akt) signaling in renal cell carcinoma35 and p38 signaling in disseminated tumor cells.36 As a closely related TGF-β co-receptor, Eng has been reported to regulate HSC quiescence by positively modulating both canonical and noncanonical effectors of TGF-β pathways, including pSmad2/3 and pMapkapk2 (pMk2). It remains an open question whether Tgfbr3 regulates HSC self-renewal potential through mechanisms analogous to Eng. Future studies are needed to clarify the structural basis on which the Tgfbr2/Tgfbr3/Eng complex triggers a cascade of phosphorylation and activation of downstream signaling in HSCs.

Notably, in the presence of Tgfbr3, multilineage reconstitution was maintained even during tertiary transplantation. One contributing factor could be the compensatory mechanisms mediated by other molecules that have yet to be identified. On the other hand, despite successful KO in the majority of HSCs as confirmed by Sanger sequencing, a residual population of unedited HSCs might have persisted and partially contributed to the multilineage reconstitution after serial transplantation. Nevertheless, our study demonstrated a critical role of Tgfbr3 in the maintenance of long-term self-renewal potential of HSCs and provided novel insights into the regulatory mechanisms of TGF-β signaling in HSCs.

CRISPR/Cas9-mediated KO enables rapid and cost-effective investigation of genes of interest without extensive breeding of mice. Importantly, this strategy allows gene deletion specific to HSCs, whereas gene deletion in other cell types may be involved in conditional KO mice. Furthermore, the CRISPR/Cas9 system enables simultaneous deletion of multiple genes, while the generation of double and triple KO mice is costly and time-consuming. However, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated KO in HSCs has certain limitations, such as variable editing efficiency among experiments and potential off-target effects. Moreover, viral transduction of sgRNA requires ex vivo culture steps, which may compromise HSC function due to inevitable differentiation.37 Functional study of HSCs after gene deletion depends on transplantation procedures, and the reconstituted HSC pool represents a mixture of multiple clones. It is challenging to analyze multiple populations simultaneously. In contrast, conditional KO mice circumvent ex vivo manipulation of HSCs and provide a more consistent and genetically defined system for functional evaluation of HSCs in both stress and non-stress conditions. Future studies using conditional KO mouse models such as Tgfbr3fl/fl; Mx1-Cre will be valuable to provide complementary and mechanistic insights to our current HSC KO results.

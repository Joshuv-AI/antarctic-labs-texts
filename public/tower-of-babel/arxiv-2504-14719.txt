1.
Introduction

A field
k
is called
C_{i}
(
i\in\mathbb{N}
) if every non-constant homogeneous polynomial
f(X_{0},...,X_{n})\in k[X_{0},...,X_{n}]
of degree
d
with
d^{i}\leq n
has a non-trivial zero over
k
. Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we also say that
k
is
C_{i}(d)
if it satisfies the above condition restricted to polynomials of degree
\leq d
. Thus, knowing that
k
is
C_{i}
(or
C_{i}(d)
) gives us some information about the polynomial equations which admit non-trivial solutions in
k
.

Ax-Kochen theorem

In 1936, E. Artin conjectured that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a
C_{2}
field. Strong evidence for this conjecture was given by Lang, who showed that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
is
C_{2}
. Ax-Kochen
[
AK65
]
famously proved an asymptotic version of Artin’s conjecture using model theory:

Theorem

(Ax-Kochen)
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
N(d)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
C_{2}(d)
for every
p>N(d)
.

They deduce this from a general transfer principle between
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
, proved independently by Ershov
[
Ers65
]
, which says that for any sentence
\varphi
in the language of rings, there exists
N(\varphi)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\varphi\mbox{ holds in }\mathbb{Q}_{p}\iff\varphi\mbox{ holds in }{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)

for all
p>N(\varphi)
. This, in turn, is a consequence of a more fundamental theorem about henselian valued field of equal characteristic
0
, saying that two such valued fields are elementarily equivalent precisely when their value groups and residue fields are. The asymptotic transfer between
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
can easily be deduced using ultraproducts.

This transfer principle has several other applications. A recent one, due to Duesler-Knecht
[
DK17
]
, is related to the problem of whether
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
is geometrically
C_{1}
. Recall that a
geometrically
C_{1}
field
is one which admits a rational point on every smooth projective separably rationally connected variety (see §
3.1
).
This problem is a prominent open case of the Lang-Manin conjecture which predicts that every
C_{1}
field is geometrically
C_{1}
, see
[
Esn24
]
. We note that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
is known to be
C_{1}
by a theorem of Lang
[
Lan52
]
. Duesler-Knecht prove the following asymptotic result:

Theorem

(Duesler-Knecht)
.

For every
f\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
, there exists
N(f)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every prime
p>N(f)
, every rationally connected variety over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
with Hilbert polynomial
f
has a rational point.

Their proof relies on the Graber-Harris-Starr theorem
[
GHS03
]
, which asserts that function fields of complex curves are geometrically
C_{1}
. More precisely, one needs the local version, due to Colliot-Thélène’s
[
CT10
]
, that
{\mathbb{C}}(\!(t)\!)
is geometrically
C_{1}
.
The result of Duesler-Knecht was later improved by Pieropan
[
Pie22
]
, replacing the dependence on the Hilbert polynomial by dependence only on the dimension of the variety:

Theorem

(Pieropan)
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
N(d)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
is geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d
for every
p>N(d)
.

It is an interesting question whether anything can be said about such problems for fixed
p
. Somewhat surprisingly,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is not
C_{2}
for any
p
, due to examples of Terjanian
[
Ter66
,
Ter80
]
.
As for the Lang-Manin problem for
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
, there is only a partial result due to Esnault-Levine-Wittenberg
[
ELW15
]
, which says that every rationally connected variety over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
has a zero-cycle of
p
-power degree.
The purpose of this paper is to illustrate how recent work of Jahnke and the author
[
JK25
]
can be used to prove analogous results to the ones presented above, for fixed
p
but high ramification.

Main results

We write
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
for the completed perfection of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t)\!)
.
Our first main result is a perfectoid analogue of the Ax-Kochen theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a finite totally ramified extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that every untilt of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
containing
E
is
C_{2}(d)
.

The result remains valid for any algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
containing
E
whose completion tilts to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
. We therefore obtain plenty of algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which are
C_{2}(d)
.
For example, given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as above such that
E(\pi^{1/p^{\infty}})
is
C_{2}(d)
, for any uniformizer
\pi
of
E
. In §
4.2
, we prove a generalization of the above result for rationally simply connected varieties, building on Starr-Xu
[
SX20
]
.

Our second main result was motivated by a question of Esnault from 2014 (cf. §5.3
[
Esn24
]
), who asked whether anything interesting can be said about the Lang-Manin problem for
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
after passing to a perfectoid field extension. This is a natural question, since
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
is geometrically
C_{1}
, as follows from de Jong–Starr
[
dJS03
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a finite extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
such that every untilt of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
containing
E
is geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d
.

We do not know if the finite extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is necessary in the above results. It may well be the case that every untilt of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
is
C_{2}
and every untilt of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
is geometrically
C_{1}
. As for the latter, we show in §
3.3
that it follows from a standard conjecture on degenerations of rationally connected varieties:

Conjecture A
.

Let
R
be a DVR with fraction field
K
and residue field
k
. Let
X
be a flat, projective
R
-scheme such that the generic fiber
X_{K}
is smooth and separably rationally connected. Then, the special fiber
X_{k}
admits a
k
-morphism from a smooth projective separably rationally connected
k
-variety.

If in addition
X
is regular and
X_{k}
is a strict normal crossings divisor of
X
, it is expected that one of the irreducible components of
X_{k}
is separably rationally connected. The conjecture was originally proposed by Kollár with
R
being the local ring at a
k
-point of a smooth curve over a characteristic
0
field (see Question 11
[
Kol07
]
). The extension to arbitrary DVRs is a suggestion of Colliot-Thélène (see Suggestions 7.9
[
CT10
]
). Conjecture
A
is true in equal characteristic
0
due to Hogadi-Xu
[
HX09
]
(see also Théorème 7.15
[
CT10
]
). We refer the reader to §7
[
CT10
]
for partial results in arbitrary characteristic.

Assuming this conjecture, we prove a perfectoid “geometric
C_{1}
” transfer:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume Conjecture
A
. Let
K
be a perfectoid field with tilt
K^{\flat}
which is geometrically
C_{1}
. Then
K
is geometrically
C_{1}
.

For example, assuming Conjecture
A
, every untilt of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
is geometrically
C_{1}
. As before, the result remains valid for algebraic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
which tilt to
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
, like
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})
. This implies that every smooth projective rationally connected variety
X
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{ur}
has a closed point of
p
-power degree. As noted earlier, Esnault-Levine-Wittenberg
[
ELW15
]
unconditionally proved a weaker statement, namely that such an
X
has a zero-cycle of
p
-power degree.

Leitfaden

We sketch the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which illustrates many of the key ideas in this paper. Let
\kappa
be an untilt of
\kappa^{\flat}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
.

Step 1: Model theory of perfectoid fields

The main result of
[
JK25
]
says that, grosso modo,
\kappa^{\flat}
behaves like a residue field of
\kappa
. To make this precise, we need to replace
\kappa
with some non-principal ultrapower, say
K
. Then, there is valuation
v
on
K
, whose residue field
k
is an elementary extension of
\kappa^{\flat}
, as shown below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa}

The valuation
v
is obtained as a suitable coarsening of the non-standard valuation on
K
and has very nice properties; it is henselian defectless with divisible value group. In particular, the valued field
(K,v)
is
tame
, a property we will exploit later on.

Step 2: Construction of a
C_{2}
field

We now cook up another valued field
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
with value group and residue field that are elementarily equivalent to those of
(K,v)
, but which is also
C_{2}
by design. Start with any maximal totally ramified extension
E_{\infty}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Such a field is
C_{1}
by
[
Kar24b
]
. We equip the rational function field
E_{\infty}(t)
with the Gauss valuation
u
, which has value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
.
We now take an algebraic extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
of
(E_{\infty}(t),u)
which is henselian defectless with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
k^{\prime}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)^{h,\text{perf}}
. This finishes our construction of
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
We claim that

k\equiv k^{\prime}\mbox{ and }\Gamma\equiv\Gamma^{\prime}

The statement about the residue fields follows from another result from
[
JK25
]
, which says that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)^{h,\text{perf}}
is an elementary substructure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
. For the value groups, we use the standard fact that any two non-trivial divisible ordered abelian groups have the same theory.
Finally, note that
K^{\prime}
is indeed
C_{2}
by Lang’s transition theorem, since it has transcendence degree
1
over the
C_{1}
field
E_{\infty}
.

Step 3: Model theory of tame fields

We now use the model theory of tame fields, due to F.-V. Kuhlmann
[
Kuh16
]
, to compare
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
. This theory does not quite imply that
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
are elementarily equivalent, even though their residue fields and value groups are elementarily equivalent. However, it does imply that any given sentence
\varphi
which holds in
K^{\prime}
will also hold in
K
, provided that the relative algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in
K
contains a sufficiently large finite subextension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
E_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(depending on
\varphi
). For any given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we apply this to the sentence which expresses the
C_{2}(d)
property, thereby obtaining that
K
, and hence
\kappa
, satisfies
C_{2}(d)
.

2.1.
Model theory of tame fields

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field with value group
\Gamma
and residue field
k
. A finite valued field extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
of
(K,v)
, with value group
\Gamma^{\prime}
and residue field
k^{\prime}
, is said to be
tame
if the following are satisfied:

(i)

If
p=\text{char}(k)>0
, then
p\nmid[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]
.

(ii)

The residue field extension
k^{\prime}/k
is separable.

(iii)

The extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})/(K,v)
is
defectless
, i.e.,

[K^{\prime}:K]=[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]\cdot[k^{\prime}:k]

We say that
(K,v)
is
tame
if every finite valued field extension of
(K,v)
is tame.

Given first-order
L
-structures
M
and
N
with a common substructure
A
, we use the notation
M\equiv_{A}N
to say that the structures
M
and
N
are elementarily equivalent in the language
L
enriched with constant symbols for each element of
A
. We write
L_{\mathrm{rings}}=\{0,1,+,\cdot\}
for the language of rings,
L_{\mathrm{oag}}=\{0,+,<\}
for the language of ordered abelian groups, and

L_{\mathrm{val}}=L_{\mathrm{rings}}\cup\{\mathscr{O}\}
for the language of valued fields, where
\mathscr{O}
is a unary predicate for the valuation ring.

Fact 2.2

(Theorem 7.1
[
Kuh16
]
)
.

Let
(K_{0},v_{0})
be a defectless valued field and let
(K,v),(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be two tame fields extending
(K_{0},v_{0})
. Assume that
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
is torsion-free and
k/k_{0}
is separable. Then:

(K,v)\equiv_{(K_{0},v_{0})}(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})\mbox{ in }L_{\text{val}}\iff k\equiv_{k_{0}}k^{\prime}\mbox{ in }L_{\text{rings}}\mbox{ and }\Gamma\equiv_{\Gamma_{0}}\Gamma^{\prime}\mbox{ in }L_{\text{oag}}

In equal characteristic, one can take
K_{0}
to be the prime field (either
\mathbb{Q}
or
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
) equipped with the trivial valuation. This yields the following:

Fact 2.3

(Theorem 1.4
[
Kuh16
]
)
.

Let
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be two equal characteristic tame fields. Then:

(K,v)\equiv(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})\mbox{ in }L_{\text{val}}\iff k\equiv k^{\prime}\mbox{ in }L_{\text{rings}}\mbox{ and }\Gamma\equiv\Gamma^{\prime}\mbox{ in }L_{\text{oag}}

Beware that the analogous statement fails in mixed characteristic. Indeed, there are numerous examples of mixed characteristic tame fields
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
with
k=k^{\prime}
and
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}
, yet their algebraic parts over
\mathbb{Q}
are non-isomorphic, implying that they are not elementarily equivalent; see
[
AK16
]
.
Upcoming work of Ketelsen provides more nuanced counterexamples where also the algebraic parts of
K
and
K^{\prime}
are isomorphic. Nevertheless, a compactness argument gives the following more refined statement, which plays a central role throughout:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a sentence in the language of valued fields and
E_{\infty}
be a maximal totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then there exists a finite subextension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
E_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that for any two tame fields
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
extending
(E,v_{p})
with
k\equiv k^{\prime}
in
L_{\text{rings}}
and
(\Gamma,vp)\equiv(\Gamma^{\prime},v^{\prime}p)
in
L_{\text{oag}}
, we have that

\varphi\mbox{ holds in }(K,v)\iff\varphi\mbox{ holds in }(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})

Proof.

Suppose otherwise.
Write
E_{\infty}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}E_{i}
, where

E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq...

is an increasing chain of finite totally ramified extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. By assumption, for each
E_{i}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
there exist tame valued extensions
(K_{i},v_{i})
and
(K_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime})
of
E_{i}
with residue fields
k_{i},k_{i}^{\prime}
and value groups
\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
such that

k_{i}\equiv k_{i}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad(\Gamma_{i},v_{i}p)\equiv(\Gamma_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}p),

and
\varphi
holds in
(K_{i},v_{i})
but not in
(K_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime})
. Let
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and consider the ultraproducts

(K,v)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(K_{i},v_{i})/U\mbox{ and }(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(K_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime})/U

Since tame fields form an elementary class (see §7.1
[
Kuh16
]
), both
(K,v)
and
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
are tame. Moreover, they both extend
(E_{\infty},v_{p})
and we have
k\equiv k^{\prime}
and
(\Gamma,vp)\equiv(\Gamma^{\prime},v^{\prime}p)
by Łoś. Note that
(E_{\infty},v_{p})
is defectless, since every finite extension comes entirely from the residue field, and
\Gamma/\mathbb{Q}
is clearly torsion-free. By Fact
2.2
, we get that

(K,v)\equiv_{(E_{\infty},v_{p})}(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})

which is contrary to the fact that
\varphi
holds in
(K,v)
but not in
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Theorem 2.14
[
Kuh04
]
)
.

Let
(K_{0},v_{0})
be a valued field with value group
\Gamma_{0}
and residue field
k_{0}
. Let
\Gamma
be an ordered abelian group extending
\Gamma_{0}
such that
\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
is a torsion group and
k/k_{0}
be an algebraic extension. Then there exists an algebraic extension
(K,v)
of
(K_{0},v_{0})
with value group
\Gamma
and residue field

.

Proof.

We begin by extending the residue field to
k
. Suppose first that
k/k_{0}
is a finite extension with minimal polynomial
f(X)\in k_{0}[X]
. Let
F(X)\in K_{0}[X]
be a monic lift and
a
be a root of

in
\overline{K_{0}}

[
vdD12
]
, the valuation

extends uniquely to

\Gamma_{0}
and residue field

. The case of an arbitrary algebraic extension follows by transfinite recursion, using the previous construction for the successor step and taking unions for the limit step.

We now extend the value group to
\Gamma
. Suppose that
\gamma\in\Gamma\backslash\Gamma_{0}
is such that
p\cdot\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}
for some prime

. Let
b_{0}\in K_{0}

. By Lemma 5.6
[

extends uniquely to
K_{0}(b)

\Gamma_{0}+\mathbb{Z}\gamma
and residue field

. We can write
\Gamma=\bigcup_{i\in\kappa}\Gamma_{i}
, where
\kappa
is an ordinal and

\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\subseteq...\subseteq\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\Gamma_{i+1}\subseteq...

with

and

. We again apply transfinite recursion, using the previous construction for the successor step, to construct an algebraic extension
(K,v)
of
(K_{0},v_{0})
with value group
\Gamma
and residue field
k
.

.

2.2.
Model theory of perfectoid fields

We refer the reader to §3
[
Sch12
]
for basic facts about perfectoid fields and tilting.

Definition 2.7
.

A
perfectoid field
is a complete valued field
(K,v)
of residue characteristic
p>0
such that the value group is a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
and the Frobenius map
\varphi:\mathscr{O}_{K}/(p)\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{K}/(p):x\mapstochar\rightarrow x^{p}
is surjective.

We can transform any perfectoid field
K
to a perfectoid field
K^{\flat}
of characteristic
p
, called the
tilt
of
K
. As a set, we have
K^{\flat}=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\in K^{\omega}:x_{n+1}^{p}=x_{n}\}
. We define multiplication in
K^{\flat}
coordinatewise

(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\cdot(y_{n})_{n\in\omega}=(x_{n}\cdot y_{n})_{n\in\omega}

Addition in
K^{\flat}
is a bit more involved and is described by the following rule

(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}+(y_{n})_{n\in\omega}=(z_{n})_{n\in\omega}\mbox{ where }z_{n}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}(x_{n+m}+y_{n+m})^{p^{m}}

We have the
sharp
map from
K^{\flat}
to
K
, which simply picks out
x_{0}
, namely

\sharp:K^{\flat}\rightarrow K:(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\mapstochar\rightarrow x_{0}

It is clearly a multiplicative morphism. We define a valuation
v^{\flat}
on
K^{\flat}
by

v^{\flat}(x)=v(x^{\sharp})

Fact 2.8

(Theorem 6.2.3
[
JK25
]
)
.

Let
(K,v)
be a perfectoid field and
\varpi\in\mathfrak{m}_{v}\backslash\{0\}
. Let
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(K_{U},v_{U})
be the corresponding ultrapower. Let
S\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{v_{U}}
be the set of elements with infinitesimal valuation, i.e., smaller than any positive rational multiple of
v_{U}\varpi
, and
\mathscr{O}_{w}=S^{-1}\mathscr{O}_{v_{U}}
. Then:

(I)

The valued field
(K_{U},w)
is tame with divisible value group.

(II)

The tilt
(K^{\flat},v^{\flat})
embeds elementarily into
(k_{w},\overline{v})
, where
\overline{v}
is the induced valuation of
v_{U}
on

.

Let
F
be a perfectoid field of characteristic
p
. An
untilt
of

is a pair
(K,\iota)
, where
K
is a perfectoid field and
\iota:K^{\flat}\rightarrow F
is an isomorphism. Fargues-Fontaine
[

construct a one dimensional noetherian regular

-scheme

(but not of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
) such that the closed points of

are in natural bijection with the set of untilts of
F
modulo an action of Frobenius. More generally, for any finite extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
whose residue field is contained in the residue field of
F
, Fargues-Fontaine define

which parametrizes untilts of
F
containing
E
.

We show that the untilts of
F
approach the same theory as
E
become large:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\varphi
be a sentence in the language of rings. Let
F
be a perfectoid field of characteristic
p
and
E_{\infty}
be a maximal totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then there exists a finite subextension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
E_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that for every untilt
K
of
F
containing
E
, we have that

\varphi\mbox{ holds in }K\iff\varphi\mbox{ holds in }K^{\prime}

for any tame field
K^{\prime}
extending
E
with divisible value group and residue field elementarily equivalent to
F
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
, there is a finite totally ramified extension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that any two tame valued fields extending
(E,v_{p})
with divisible value
group and residue field elementarily equivalent to
F
agree on the truth
value of
\varphi
. Now let
K
be an untilt of
F
containing
E
. Let
U
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(K_{U},w)
be as in Fact
2.8
. Then
(K_{U},w)
is tame with divisible value group, its residue field is elementarily equivalent to
F
, and it extends
(E,v_{p})
, so by Proposition 2.5 we have

\varphi\text{ holds in }K_{U}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\text{ holds in }K^{\prime}

for any tame field
K^{\prime}
extending
E
with divisible value group and residue
field elementarily equivalent to
F
. On the other hand,
K_{U}
is an ultrapower of

as a pure field, so

\varphi\text{ holds in }K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\text{ holds in }K_{U}.

Combining the two equivalences gives the desired statement.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

(i)

In particular, for any untilts

and

of

(depending on

(ii)

).

The following result will also be important:

(Corollary 5.2.2
[

]
)
.

Let

is an elementary substructure of

3.
Perfectoid
C_{1}
transfer

After recalling some definitions and facts about rationally connected varieties, we prove our main theorem about untilts of
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
. We then present some more general results which are conditional on Conjecture
A
.

3.1.
Geometrically
C_{1}
fields

Definition 3.1

( IV, §3
[
Kol13
]
)
.

A geometrically irreducible
k
-variety
X
is called
rationally connected
(resp.
separably rationally connected
) if there is a
\overline{k}
-variety
B
and a morphism
F:B\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow X
such that the induced morphism

B\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow X\times X:(b,t,t^{\prime})\mapstochar\rightarrow(F(b,t),F(b,t^{\prime}))

is dominant (resp. dominant and separable).

In other words, there is an algebraic family of proper rational curves such that for almost any
(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X
, there is a curve in the family joining
x
and
x^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.2
.

(i)

A field
k
is called
geometrically
C_{1}
if every smooth projective separably rationally connected
k
-variety has a
k
-rational point.

(ii)

A field
k
is
geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d

if every smooth projective separably rationally connected
k
-variety of dimension
\leq d
has a
k
-rational point.

The term “geometrically
C_{1}
” is due to Kollár, see
[
HX09
]
. Recall from
[
Kar24b
]
that geometrically
C_{1}
fields form an elementary class. We now show that for the class of geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d
, a single sentence suffices, at least in characteristic
0
.
This essentially follows from the work of
Pieropan
[
Pie22
]
, as we explain below.
The main theorem relies crucially on results from the minimal model program
[
BCHM10
]
.

Fact 3.3

(Theorem 1.3
[
Pie22
]
)
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
. For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the
following statements are equivalent:

(i)

k
is geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d
.

(ii)

Every terminal Fano variety of dimension
\leq d

over
k
has a
k
-point.

Remark 3.4
.

The first clause in
[
Pie22
]
is about proper rather than projective varieties; this does not make a difference by Lemma 3.1
[
Pie22
]
. We also note that the second clause further restricts the class of Fano varieties but we will not need it.

Pieropan proves the following result with
N
and
f_{i}
a priori depending on
k
, but a slight modification of the proof shows that this is not necessary.

Fact 3.5

(cf. Proposition 3.4
[
Pie22
]
)
.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and finitely many polynomials
f_{1},...,f_{s}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
such
that for every field
k
of characteristic
0
and every terminal Fano
k
-variety
X
of dimension
d
, there exists an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
such that
X
has Hilbert polynomial
f_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,...,s\}
.

Proof.

It is enough to provide such
N
and
f_{i}
which work for countable fields of characteristic
0
, since every variety is defined over a countable field. For every such field
k
of characteristic
0
, we fix an embedding
\iota_{k}:k\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
. By Birkar’s Theorem 1.1
[
Bir21
]
, projective
d
-dimensional terminal Fano
{\mathbb{C}}
-varieties form a bounded family. Hence there exist a scheme
T
of finite type over
{\mathbb{C}}
and a projective morphism
\pi\colon V\rightarrow T
such that every
d
-dimensional terminal Fano
{\mathbb{C}}
-variety
X
is isomorphic to a fiber
V_{t}
for some
t\in T({\mathbb{C}})
. Using a flattening stratification of
T
(see Tag 0H3Y
[
Sta
]
), we obtain finitely many projective, flat morphisms

\pi_{i}:V^{i}\rightarrow T^{i},\qquad i=1,\dots,s,

such that every
d
-dimensional terminal Fano
{\mathbb{C}}
-variety appears as a fibre of some
\pi_{i}
. In particular, for every countable field
k
of characteristic
0
and every terminal Fano
k
-variety
X
of dimension
d
, there exist
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and
t\in T^{i}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

V^{i}_{t}\cong X_{\mathbb{C}},

where
X_{{\mathbb{C}}}
denotes the base change of

from
k
to
{\mathbb{C}}
via
\iota_{k}
.
The rest of the proof is as in Proposition 3.4
[
Pie22
]
.
∎

The proof of the next result is similar to the proof of Theorem 11
[
DK17
]
. (Note that there is a small issue with their proof: The morphism
g
should be the universal family over the Hilbert scheme rather than what is written there.)

Lemma 3.6
.

For every Hilbert polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
\forall\exists
-sentence
\varphi
in the language of rings such that for every field
k
of characteristic
0
the following are equivalent:

(i)

Every projective rationally connected
k
-variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
with Hilbert polynomial
f
has a
k
-rational point.

(ii)

The sentence
\varphi
holds in
k
.

Proof.

Let
H_{f}
be the component of the Hilbert scheme parametrizing closed subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{N}
with Hilbert polynomial
f
. Let
\pi:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow H_{f}
be the universal family.
By Proposition 2.6(a)
[
dFF13
]
, there is a constructible subset
Z\subseteq H_{f}
such that for each
z\in H_{f}
we have that

\pi^{-1}(z)\mbox{ is a rationally connected }\kappa(z)\mbox{-variety}\iff z\in Z

where
\kappa(z)
denotes the residue field of
Z
at the point
z
. Since
\mathscr{U},H_{f}
and
\pi
are of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
and
Z\subseteq H_{f}
is constructible, there is a
\forall\exists
-sentence
\varphi
in the language of rings expressing (uniformly in
k
) that for every
z\in Z(k)
the fiber
\pi^{-1}(z)
has a
k
-rational point. By the universal property of the Hilbert scheme, for every field
k
of characteristic
0
and every projective
k
-variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
with Hilbert polynomial
f
,
there is a pullback diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Spec}}}(k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}

Moreover, we have that
X
is rationally connected if and only if
x\in Z(k)
. It easily follows that (i) and (ii) are equivalent.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

We note that Proposition 2.6(a)
[
dFF13
]
, which was used in the above proof, is stated over algebraically closed fields. This assumption is not necessary since the proof in

relies on resolution of singularities and IV, Theorem 3.11
[

]
, both of which are valid for arbitrary fields of characteristic

.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
\forall\exists
-sentence
\varphi_{d}
in the language of rings such that for every field
k
of characteristic
0
, we have that

k\mbox{ is geometrically }C_{1}\mbox{ up to dimension }d\iff\varphi_{d}\mbox{ holds in }k

Proof.

By Fact
3.5
, there are
N\in\mathbb{N}
and finitely many polynomials
f_{1},...,f_{s}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
such
that for every field
k
of characteristic
0
and every terminal Fano
k
-variety
X
of dimension
\leq d
, there exists an embedding
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}_{k}
such that
X
has Hilbert polynomial
f_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,...,s\}
. Let
\varphi_{i}
be a sentence associated to
f_{i}
and

as in Lemma
3.6
. We claim that the conjunction
\varphi_{d}
of the
\varphi_{i}
’s has the desired property.

“
\Rightarrow
”: Let
k
be of characteristic
0
and geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension
d
. By Lemma 3.1
[
Pie22
]
, every projective rationally connected
k
-variety (not necessarily smooth) of dimension
\leq d
has a
k
-point. In particular, this is true for those rationally connected varieties

with Hilbert polynomial
f_{i}
, for each

. It follows that
\varphi_{d}
holds in
k
.

“
\Leftarrow
”: Let
k
be of characteristic
0
and suppose that
\varphi_{d}
holds in

. Then, for every

, every rationally connected
k
-variety with Hilbert polynomial

has a

-point. Since terminal Fano varieties are rationally connected

,

]
, all such

-varieties of dimension
\leq d
have a

that

is geometrically
C_{1}
up to dimension

.
∎

3.2.
Perfectoid
C_{1}
transfer

The following theorem is due to Graber-Harris-Starr
[
GHS03
]
in characteristic
0
and de Jong-Starr
[
dJS03
]
in arbitrary characteristic.

Fact 3.9

(de Jong-Graber-Harris-Starr)
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective irreducible curve over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
f:X\rightarrow C
be a proper flat
k
-morphism whose generic fiber is smooth and separably rationally connected. Then
f
has a section.

Since any smooth projective separably rationally connected variety over the function field
k(C)
spreads out to a family
f:X\rightarrow C
as above, and a section of
f
yields a

k(C)
-rational point on the generic fiber, it follows that the function field

k(C)
is geometrically
C_{1}
. It easily follows that any field of transcendence degree 1 over an algebraically closed field is geometrically
C_{1}
. Less trivially, the same holds for “sufficiently nice” valued fields whose residue field has transcendence degree 1 over an algebraically closed field:

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(K,v)
be a tame field with divisible value group and residue field
k
which has transcendence degree
1
over an algebraically closed field.

(i)

If
(K,v)
is of equal characteristic, then
K
is geometrically
C_{1}
.

(ii)

If
(K,v)
is of mixed characteristic and
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq K
, then
K
is geometrically
C_{1}
.

Proof.

Say
k
is algebraic over
k_{0}(t)
, where
k_{0}
is algebraically closed.

(i) By Proposition 3.1.5
[
Kar24b
]
, geometrically
C_{1}
fields form an elementary class. For any
k
as above, it suffices to construct a single geometrically
C_{1}
tame field
(K,v)
with divisible value group and residue field
k
, since Fact
2.3
guarantees that every other such field has the same theory. By de Jong-Graber-Harris-Starr, the rational function field
\overline{k_{0}(z)}(t)
over
\overline{k_{0}(z)}
is geometrically
C_{1}
. The same is true for its perfect hull, being a colimit of geometrically
C_{1}
fields. Let
v_{z}
be a valuation on
\overline{k_{0}(z)}
extending the
z
-adic valuation on
k_{0}(z)
. We equip
\overline{k_{0}(z)}(t)
with the Gauss extension
u
of
v_{z}
on
\overline{k_{0}(z)}
, with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
k_{0}(t)
. By Lemma
2.6
, there is an algebraic extension
(K,v)
of
(\overline{k_{0}(z)}(t),u)
such that
(K,v)
is tame with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
k
. Since tame fields are perfect, we have that
K
is a separable algebraic extension of the perfect hull of
\overline{k_{0}(z)}(t)
. Since the latter is geometrically
C_{1}
, the same is true for
K
by Lemma 3.1.4(ii)
[
Kar24b
]
.

(ii) Applying Fact
2.2
, with
(\overline{\mathbb{Q}},v_{p})
as the base field, we see that any two tame fields over
(\overline{\mathbb{Q}},v_{p})
with divisible value group and residue field
k
have the same theory. It therefore suffices to construct one such tame field which is geometrically
C_{1}
. Start with any algebraically closed valued field
(K_{0},v_{0})
with residue
k_{0}
and proceed as in (i) to construct a tame field
(K,v)
extending
(K_{0},v_{0})
, with divisible value group and residue field
k
, and
K/K_{0}
of transcendence degree
1
.
∎

The above result is closely related to a theorem of Starr on the existence of rational points on degenerations of rationally connected varieties over function fields of curves:

Fact 3.11

(Theorem 3.10
[
Sta17
]
)
.

Let
R
be a prime regular DVR with fraction field
K
and residue field
k
which has transcendence degree
1
over an algebraically closed field. Let
X
be a flat, projective
R
-scheme with
X_{K}
smooth and separably rationally connected. Then
X(k)\neq\emptyset
.

A
prime regular
DVR is one which is weakly unramified over a DVR with finite residue field (see §3
[
Sta17
]
). The prime regular assumption is relevant only in mixed characteristic and is similar in nature to our assumption that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq K
.
Let us explain how Fact
3.11
follows from Proposition
3.10
, at least for perfect
k
. In fact, the argument is, in essence, quite close to Starr’s original proof.

Proof sketch.

By Lemma
2.6
, there is an algebraic extension
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
of
(K,v)
such that
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
is tame with divisible value group and residue field
k
. Moreover, if
(K,v)
is mixed characteristic, we can arrange that
K^{\prime}
contains
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. The point is that the assumption on the DVR implies that
K\cdot\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is a totally ramified algebraic extension of

and therefore has residue field
k
.
By Proposition
3.10
,
K^{\prime}
is geometrically

and therefore

. By the valuative criterion of properness, we get that
X(\mathscr{O}_{K^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
and thus
X(k)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

.

Given

, there is a finite extension

3.3.
Conditional results

Given a valued field
(K,v)
and a proper
K
-variety
X
, an
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-model
of
X
is a flat, proper
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-scheme whose generic fiber is isomorphic to
X
. By the valuative criterion of properness, a
K
-point of
X
induces a
k
-point on each
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-model. The following result gives a converse in case
(K,v)
is sufficiently nice.

Fact 3.15

(Theorem 1.2.2
[
Kar24b
]
)
.

Let
(K,v)
be a tame field with divisible value group of rank
1
and residue field
k
. Let
X
be a proper
K
-variety. Suppose that
\pazocal{X}(k)\neq\emptyset
for each
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-model
\pazocal{X}
of
X
. Then
X(K)\neq\emptyset
.

The (conditional) transfer principle below essentially follows from
[
Kar24b
]
but we spell out the argument for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 3.16
.

Assume Conjecture
A
. Let
(K,v)
be a tame field with divisible value group and
geometrically
C_{1}
residue field
k
. Then
K
is geometrically
C_{1}
.

Proof.

By Proposition 3.1.5
[
Kar24b
]
, geometrically
C_{1}
fields form an elementary class. By Lemma 5.1.3
loc.cit
, we have that
(K,v)
has an elementary valued subfield
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
such that
\mathscr{O}_{K^{\prime}}
is a colimit of DVRs. Without loss of generality, we can therefore assume that
\mathscr{O}_{K}
itself is a colimit of DVRs. Let
X
be a smooth projective separably rationally connected
K
-variety. Assuming Conjecture
A
, for every
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-model
\pazocal{X}
of
X
, there is a smooth projective separably rationally connected
k
-variety

and a
k
-morphism

. Since

is geometrically

, we get that

and hence
\pazocal{X}_{k}(k)\neq\emptyset
. Since this is true for every
\mathscr{O}_{K}
-model of

, Theorem 1.2.2
loc.cit
gives that
X(K)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Assume Conjecture

. Let

be a perfectoid field with tilt

which is geometrically

. Then

is geometrically
C_{1}
.

Proof.

Using Fact

K^{\flat}\mbox{ geom. }C_{1}\iff k_{w}\mbox{ geom. }C_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{geomc1trans}}}{{\Rightarrow}}K_{U}\mbox{ geom. }C_{1}\iff K\mbox{ geom. }C_{1}

where the first and last equivalences use that geometrically

fields form an elementary class and that

and
K_{U}\equiv K
respectively.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

.

4.1.

We recall the following theorem, whose proof relies crucially on earlier work of Esnault

]

Fact 4.1

(Theorem 1.1.2
[

]
)
.

Every tame valued field with divisible value group and finite residue field is

.

We also state Lang’s transition theorem for the

Fact 4.2

(
[
Lan52
]
)
.

Let
k
be a
C_{i}
field and

be a field extension with
\text{tr.deg}(l/k)=s
. Then

is
C_{i+s}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(K,v)
be a tame field with divisible value group and residue field
k
which has transcendence degree
1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

(i)

If
(K,v)
is of equal characteristic, then
K
is
C_{2}
.

(ii)

If
(K,v)
is of mixed characteristic and
K\cap\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is defectless with divisible value group, then

is
C_{2}
.

Proof.

We proceed as in Proposition
3.10
. Suppose
k
is an algebraic extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
.

(i) For any
k
as above, it suffices to construct one such tame field which is

2.3
. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)
be the Hahn series field with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. By Fact
4.1
, we have that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)
is
C_{1}
.
Consider the rational functon field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)(t)
equipped with the Gauss valuation
u
, extending the
z
-adic valuation on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)
, with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
. By Lemma
2.6
, there is an algebraic extension
(K,v)
of
({\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)(t),u)
such that
(K,v)
is tame with value group
\mathbb{Q}
and residue field
k
. Since the extension
K/{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)
is of transcendence degree

and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(z^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)

, we get that

(ii) We equip

\mathbb{Q}
to

We recall the first counterexample to Artin’s conjecture by Terjanian
[

]
and observe that it admits rational points over every untilt of

:

Example 4.7
.

Define

G(\mathbf{X})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}^{4}-\sum_{i<j}X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}-X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}(X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3})

where
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
. Consider the quartic
\pazocal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{17}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
below

G(\mathbf{X})+G(\mathbf{Y})+G(\mathbf{Z})+4G(\mathbf{U})+4G(\mathbf{V})+4G(\mathbf{W})=0

Let
\pazocal{X}_{s}=\pazocal{X}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
be the special fiber.
Terjanian observed that
\pazocal{X}(\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
, which implies that
\pazocal{X}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})=\emptyset
. One obstruction to the existence of a
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
-rational point is the fact that
\pazocal{X}_{s}^{\text{sm}}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})=\emptyset
. On the other hand, one checks that

(1,1,t,1,1,t,0,...,0)\in\pazocal{X}_{s}^{\text{sm}}({\mathbb{F}}_{2}(t))

By Hensel’s Lemma, for any henselian valued field
K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
whose residue field contains
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
, we get that
\pazocal{X}(K)\neq\emptyset
. This applies to non-principal ultrapowers of untilts of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
and hence
\pazocal{X}(K)\neq\emptyset
for any such untilt.

While no finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is

(or even

), due to Alemu
[

]
, the degree of the counterexamples in

4.2.
RSC varieties over global function fields

Informally, a variety
X
is
rationally simply connected
(RSC) if the space of 2-pointed rational curves on
X
is rationally connected. This is analogous to the topological notion of simple connectedness, just as rational connectedness is analogous to path-connectedness. In our case, we need the notion of a
rationally simply connected fibration
, see Hypothesis 1.2
[
SX20
]
for the precise definition.

The main theorem of Starr-Xu says that a projective scheme over a global function field has a rational point if it deforms to a rationally simply connected variety in characteristic
0
with vanishing elementary obstruction.
More precisely:

Fact 4.9

(Theorem 1.4
[
SX20
]
)
.

Let
R
be a henselian DVR with fraction field
K
of characteristic
0
and finite residue field
k
. Let
C_{R}
be a generically smooth
R
-curve. Let
f_{R}:X_{R}\rightarrow C_{R}
be a flat, projective morphism such that
f_{K}:X_{K}\rightarrow C_{K}
is a rationally simply connected fibration whose generic fiber has vanishing elementary obstruction.
Then, for every generic point
\eta
of
C_{k}
contained in the
R
-smooth locus of
C_{R}
, the pullback
X_{\eta}=X_{R}\times_{C_{R}}k(\eta)
has a
k(\eta)
-rational point.

Starr notes that there is nothing special about finite fields in the proof, apart from the fact that they are
RC solving
(due to Esnault
[
Esn07
]
and Esnault-Xu
[
EX09
]
), i.e., they admit rational points in degenerations of rationally connected varieties.

Fact 4.10

(Proposition 4.3
[
Sta17
]
)
.

Let
R
be a henselian prime regular DVR with fraction field
K
of characteristic
0
and RC solving residue field
k
. Let
C_{R}
be a generically smooth
R
-curve. Let
f_{R}:X_{R}\rightarrow C_{R}
be a flat projective morphism such that
f_{K}:X_{K}\rightarrow C_{K}
is a rationally simply connected fibration whose generic fiber has vanishing elementary obstruction. Then, for every generic point
\eta
of
C_{k}
contained in the
R
-smooth locus of
C_{R}
, the pullback
X_{\eta}=X_{R}\times_{C_{R}}k(\eta)
has a
k(\eta)
-rational point.

For our purposes, we need a version of the above result which is expressible in first-order logic.
This was already done by Starr-Xu, although it was only documented in the first arXiv version
[
SX17
]
. Using this, they gave an extension of the Ax-Kochen theorem in the context of rationally simply connected varieties, see Corollary 1.14
[
SX17
]
. We provide a brief overview in the next section.

4.3.
First-order version

The idea is to work with a family
f:X\rightarrow M
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
which satisfies some rational simple connectedness condition.
The definitions below can be made over a more general base but we only state them over
\mathbb{Q}
for simplicity.

Definition 4.11

(Definition 4.1
[
Sta17
]
)
.

A
parameter datum
over
\mathbb{Q}
with a codimension
>1
compactification is a datum

((M,f:X\rightarrow M,\mathscr{L}_{M}),(\overline{M},\mathscr{O}_{\overline{M}}(1),\iota))

where:

(i)

M
is a smooth
\mathbb{Q}
-variety.

(ii)

f:X\rightarrow M
is a proper, flat morphism.

(iii)

\mathscr{L}_{M}
is an
f
-very ample invertible sheaf on
X
.

(iv)

\overline{M}
is a proper
\mathbb{Q}
-variety.

(v)

\iota:M\rightarrow\overline{M}
is a dense open immersion and
\overline{M}\backslash M
has codimension
>1
.

For every
r\geq 1
, the complete linear system
H^{0}(\overline{M},\mathscr{O}_{\overline{M}}(r))
induces a closed immersion into
\mathbb{P}^{N_{r}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
where
N_{r}=h^{0}(\mathscr{O}_{\overline{M}}(r))-1
. Let
\text{Gr}(N_{r},m-1)
be the Grassmannian variety parameterizing linear subspaces of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{r}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of codimension
m-1
and
G_{r}\subseteq\text{Gr}(N_{r},m-1)
be the open subset parameterizing those subspaces whose intersection with
\overline{M}
is a smooth, irreducible curve which is contained in
M
.

Definition 4.12

(Definition 4.2
[
Sta17
]
)
.

A parameter datum as above satisfies the
RSC property
if there is
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(W_{r})_{r\geq r_{0}}
of dense open subschemes
W_{r}\subseteq G_{r}
such that for every algebraically closed field
K
of characteristic
0
and every
K
-point of
W_{r}
parametrizing a curve
C_{K}\subset M_{K}
, the pullback family
X\times_{M}C_{K}\rightarrow C_{K}
is a rationally simply connected fibration whose generic fiber has vanishing elementary obstruction.

The next result is the last assertion of Proposition 4.4
[
Sta17
]
:

Fact 4.13

(Proposition 4.4
[
Sta17
]
)
.

Consider a parameter datum as above satisfying the RSC property. Let
k
be an RC solving field and
C
be a geometrically integral
k
-curve. Then the map

f:X(k(C))\rightarrow M(k(C))

is surjective.

4.4.
RSC varieties over perfectoid fields

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow M
be a finite type morphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-varieties. Let
F
be a perfectoid field of characteristic
p
and
E_{\infty}
be a maximal totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then there exists a finite subextension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
E_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that for every untilt
K
of
F
containing
E
, we have that

f:X(K)\rightarrow M(K)

is surjective if and only if

f:X(K^{\prime})\rightarrow M(K^{\prime})

is surjective, for any tame field
K^{\prime}
extending
E
with divisible value group and residue field elementarily equivalent to
F
.

Proof.

Since
X,M
and
f
are of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
, there is an
\forall\exists
-sentence
\varphi
in the language of rings such that for any field
K
of characteristic
0
, we have that

\varphi\mbox{ holds in }K\iff f:X(K)\rightarrow M(K)\mbox{ is surjective}

Applying Proposition
2.9
to
\varphi
yields the desired conclusion.
∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Consider a parameter datum satisfying the RSC property. Let
E_{\infty}
be a maximal totally ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then there exists a finite subextension
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
E_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that for every untilt
K
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(\!(t^{1/p^{\infty}})\!)
containing
E
, the map

f:X(K)\rightarrow M(K)

is surjective.

Proof.

By Corollary 1.1.3
[
Kar24b
]
, we know that
E_{\infty}
is geometrically
C_{1}
. By Hogadi-Xu
[
HX09
]
(or Corollary 3.2.12
[
Kar24b
]
), we get that
E_{\infty}
is also RC solving. By Fact
4.13
, we know that

f:X(E_{\infty}(C))\rightarrow M(E_{\infty}(C))

is surjective for any geometrically integral
E_{\infty}
-curve
C
.
Now let
(K^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Since
K^{\prime}
is algebraic over
E_{\infty}(t)
and
\overline{E_{\infty}}\cap K=E_{\infty}
, we see that
K^{\prime}
is a colimit of function fields of geometrically integral
E_{\infty}
-curves. It easily follows that

f:X(K^{\prime})\rightarrow M(K^{\prime})

is surjective. We conclude from Theorem
4.14
.
∎

Theorem
4.4
is a special case of the above theorem by taking
f:X\rightarrow M
the universal family of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
d
. If
n\geq d^{2}
, then the RSC condition is satisfied by the last assertion of Theorem 3.16
[
SX20
]
. For any field
K
, the surjectivity of
f:X(K)\rightarrow M(K)
means precisely that every hypersurface of degree
d
with
n\geq d^{2}
has a
K
-rational point, i.e., that
K
is
C_{2}(d)
.

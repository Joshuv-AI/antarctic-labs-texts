Quasi-invariant states for the action of compact groups
Maria Elena Griseta

Abstract
. We analyze a natural C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic definition of G G -quasi-invariant states for the automorphic action of a compact group G G . We prove that, given a G G -quasi-invariant state with central support, when the action of the group G G commutes with the modular group, its GNS representation is equivalent to that of a G G -invariant state. Mathematics Subject Classification : 46L05, 46L30, 37N20. Key words : C ∗ C^{*} -dynamical systems, quasi-invariant states, invariant states, modular group.



1.
Introduction

States invariant under the action of a group
G
through
*
-automorphism on a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
are very often considered in various fields of mathematics and physics
[
2
,
7
]
.
A larger class of states is associated with quasi-invariant measures, which are an important topic in ergodic theory and for example the natural setting for the construction of type III factors
[
10
,
9
]
. More in details, if
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
is a measurable space and
G

denotes a group of automorphisms acting on it by

x\in X\mapsto\alpha_{g}(x)\in X\,,\quad g\in G\,,

a measure
\mu
on
(X,{\mathcal{B}})
is said to be
G
-quasi-invariant if for any
g\in G
the transformed measure
\mu\circ\alpha_{g}
is equivalent to the measure
\mu
, that is, these measures are absolutely continuous with respect to each other.

Recall also that, given
\mu
and
\nu
two regular Borel measures on the compact Hausdorff space
X
, the multiplication representations associated to them are equivalent if and only if
\mu
and
\nu
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other. Therefore, in this paper, given
G
a compact group, by
G
-quasi-invariant state we shall mean a state
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
such that, for every
g\in G
, one has
that
\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
and
\pi_{\omega}
are unitarily equivalent representations.

Our main goal is to prove that, under suitable assumptions, any quasi-invariant state is ”absolutely continuous” with respect to an invariant state.

We should note that there does not seem to be a generally agreed-upon definition of what a quasi-invariant state should be in noncommutative settings. For example, in
[
6
]
quasi-invariance was defined in terms of quasi-equivalence of the representations
\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
and
\pi_{\omega}
.

Recently, a different approach was given in
[
1
]
. Here, given
G
a group of
*
-automorphisms of a
*
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, and assuming that for every
g\in G
there exists the Radon-Nikodym derivative
x_{g}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, the authors define a state
\varphi
on
{\mathcal{A}}
to be
G
-quasi-invariant if

(1.1)

\varphi(g(a))=\varphi(x_{g}a)\,,\quad a\in{\mathcal{A}}\,.

In the paper mentioned above, the authors assume the existence of the Radon-Nikodym derivative. In these notes, however, we deduce the existence of this positive operator after proving some results.

Successively, in
[
4
]
, several properties of
G
-quasi-invariant states defined in (
1.1
) were studied in depth. Finally, Dharhi and Ricard, in
[
5
]
, characterize
G
-quasi-invariant states with uniformly bounded cocycle
x_{g}
, in terms of invariant normal faithful states on the
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
.

In these note, we show that, given a
G
-quasi-invariant state
\omega
with central support, when the action of the group
G
commutes with the modular group, its GNS representation is equivalent to that associated with the composition of
\omega
with the canonical conditional expectation, obtained by averaging the action of the group. To reach this result, we first prove some technical lemmas.

2.
G
-quasi-invariant states

We consider the action of a compact group
G
through automorphisms of a
C^{*}
-algebra

{\mathfrak{A}}
. In other words, we are given a group homomorphism

\alpha:G\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
,
i.e.

\alpha_{gh}=\alpha_{g}\circ\alpha_{h}
for all
g,h\in G
.
We also need to make some assumptions on the continuity of
\alpha
. As usually done in the literature, we will work under the hypothesis
that
\alpha
is pointwise norm continuous, namely that for every
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
the vector-valued function

G\ni g\mapsto\alpha_{g}(a)\in{\mathfrak{A}}
is continuous, where
{\mathfrak{A}}
is thought of as being given with its norm topology.

We recall that a state
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
is said
G
-invariant if for every
g\in G
one has

\omega\circ\alpha_{g}=\omega
.

Throughout this paper by
G
-quasi-invariant state we shall mean a state
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
such that, for every
g\in G
, one has
that
\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
and
\pi_{\omega}
are unitarily equivalent representation.

As is known, the hypotheses we are working under allow us to define a (faithful) condition expectation,
E_{G}
, from

{\mathfrak{A}}
onto
{\mathfrak{A}}^{G}:=\{a\in{\mathfrak{A}}:\alpha_{g}(a)=a\,\,\textrm{for all}\,g\in G\}
. This is obtained by averaging the action of the group, that is

E_{G}(a):=\int_{G}\alpha_{g}(a)\textrm{d}g\,,a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,.

Let us
\omega
be a
G
-quasi-invariant state. We will always assume that
\omega
has central support.
As is well known, this amounts to asking that the GNS vector
\xi_{\omega}
is separating for
the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}:=\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
. Therefore, the von Neumann algebra

\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\omega})
is in standard form, and we can consider the modular flow
associated with the cyclic and separating vector
\xi_{\omega}
, which we will denote by

\{\sigma_{\omega}^{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(G,{\mathfrak{A}},\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system, where
G
is a compact group and

{\mathfrak{A}}
a
C^{*}
-algebra. For any state
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
, consider the following
statements:

(i)

\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\cong\pi_{\omega}

(ii)

For every
g\in G
,
\pi_{\omega}\circ\alpha_{g}\cong\pi_{\omega}

(iii)

\omega
is a G-quasi-invariant state

Then
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

Proof.

For the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
note that from
\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\cong\pi_{\omega}
one finds

\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\circ\alpha_{g}\cong\pi_{\omega}\circ\alpha_{g}
for all
g\in G
. Since

\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\circ\alpha_{g}\cong\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}\circ\alpha_{g}}=\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}
and
\pi_{\omega}\circ\alpha_{g}\cong\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
, we have
\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\cong\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
for all
g\in G
. The conclusion now follows by transitivity.

The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
is obvious.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(G,{\mathfrak{A}},\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system, where
G
is a compact group and

{\mathfrak{A}}
a
C^{*}
-algebra. If
\omega
is a
G
-quasi-invariant state, then the action of
G

can be lifted to
\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
through automorphisms
\{\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}:g\in G\}\subset{\rm Aut}(\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime})
such that

\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(\pi_{\omega}(a))=\pi_{\omega}(\alpha_{g}(a))\,,\,\,\,a\in{\mathfrak{A}}.

Proof.

It is a straightforward consequence of the existence of the interpolating isomorphism between

\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
and
\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}

and the fact that
\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}\cong\pi_{\omega}\circ\alpha_{g}
for
every
g\in G
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the same hypotheses as the previous lemma and assuming that
{\mathfrak{A}}
is separable, for any

T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
and
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
the vector-valued function

G\ni g\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
is Bochner
integrable. For
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, define

\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]x:=\int_{G}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\,{\rm d}g\,,\,\,x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\,,

then
\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]
lies in
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
.

Furthermore, the map

\widetilde{E_{G}}:\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
is
faithful and normal:
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
with
\widetilde{E_{G}}[T^{*}T]=0
implies
T=0
, and

\varphi\circ\widetilde{E_{G}}
is a normal state for every normal state
\varphi
on
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

We start by noting that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
is a separable Hilbert space. In particular, if

T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, there exist sequence
\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}
such that

\pi_{\omega}(a_{n})
strongly converges to
T
, and
\|\pi_{\omega}(a_{n})\|\leq M
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We next define a sequence
\{f_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of vector-valued functions on
G

by
f_{n}(g):=\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(\pi_{\omega}(a_{n}))x
. Note that each
f_{n}
is continuous (when
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
is endowed with the norm topology).
Further, if we set
f(g):=\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
, we have that
f
is the pointwise norm limit of the sequence
f_{n}
, and thus

f
is measurable. At this point, to ascertain that
f
is Bochner-integrable, it is enough to check

\int_{G}\|f(g)\|\textrm{d}g<\infty
, which is obvious as
\|f(g)\|\leq\|T\|\|x\|
for all
g\in G
.

That
\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]
sits in
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
is a
consequence of von Neumann’s bicommutant theorem. In fact, if
S
is in
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}’
, for every

x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle S\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]x

\displaystyle=S\int_{G}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\,{\rm d}g=\int_{G}S\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\,{\rm d}g=\int_{G}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)Sx\,{\rm d}g

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]Sx\,,

which shows that
\widetilde{E_{G}}[T]
belongs to the double commutant of
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, which coincides with

\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
.

Let us now prove that
\widetilde{E_{G}}
is faithful. Let
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
with
\widetilde{E_{G}}[T^{*}T]=0
.
For every
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
, we have
\langle\widetilde{E_{G}}[T^{*}T]x,x\rangle=0
, and thus

\int_{G}\|\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x\|^{2}{\rm d}g=0
. Let
\{x_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a dense sequence in

{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
and let
N_{k}
be a negligible set such that
\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x_{k}=0
if
g\in G\setminus N_{k}
.
Set
N=\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}N_{k}
. Since
N
is negligible,
G\setminus N
is non-empty. Now
if
g\in G\setminus N
, we have
\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x_{k}=0
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, that is
\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)=0
, and so

T=0
.

Finally, we have to show that

\varphi\circ\widetilde{E_{G}}
is a normal functional if
\varphi
is normal.
To this aim, let

\{T_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
be a monotone increasing sequence of positive operators and let
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
be

\sup_{n}T_{n}
.
There is no lack of generality if we assume

\varphi(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle Ax_{i},x_{i}\rangle
, for every
A\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, with
\{x_{i}:i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
being a sequence in

{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|x_{i}\|^{2}<\infty
. But then we have:

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\varphi(\widetilde{E}_{G}[T_{n}])

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{G}\langle\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T_{n})x_{i},x_{i}\rangle\,{\rm d}g

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\int_{G}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T_{n})x_{i},x_{i}\rangle\,{\rm d}g=\int_{G}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)x_{i},x_{i}\rangle\,{\rm d}g

\displaystyle=\varphi(\widetilde{E}_{G}[T])\,,

thanks to the monotone convergence theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(G,{\mathfrak{A}},\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system, where
G
is a compact group and

{\mathfrak{A}}
a separable
C^{*}
-algebra. If
\omega
is a
G
-quasi-invariant state on
{\mathfrak{A}}
with central support
such that
\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}\circ\sigma_{\omega}^{t}=\sigma_{\omega}^{t}\circ\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}
for all
g\in G

and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then one has

\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\cong\pi_{\omega}\ .

In particular, conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) in Lemma
2.1
are equivalent.

Proof.

Denote by
\widetilde{\omega}
the unique extension of
\omega
to
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, that is

\widetilde{\omega}(T)=\langle T\xi_{\omega},\xi_{\omega}\rangle
,
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
.
Let
\varphi_{\omega}
be the state on
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
obtained as the composition

\varphi_{\omega}:=\widetilde{\omega}\circ\widetilde{E_{G}}
, where

\widetilde{E_{G}}
is the map obtained above. Note that
\varphi_{\omega}
is a faithful normal state on

\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
thanks to Lemma
2.3
.

We next show that
\varphi_{\omega}\circ\sigma_{\omega}^{t}=\varphi_{\omega}
for all
t
. Since
\widetilde{\omega}
is clearly
invariant under the action of its modular flow, the equality we want to prove certainly holds if one shows that

\widetilde{E_{G}}\circ\sigma_{\omega}^{t}=\sigma_{\omega}^{t}\circ\widetilde{E_{G}}
for all
t
. This equality is a matter of easy computation.
Indeed, for
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
and
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
, one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{E_{G}}(\sigma_{\omega}^{t}(T))x=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(\sigma_{\omega}^{t}(T))x\,{\rm d}g=\int_{G}\sigma_{\omega}^{t}(\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T))x\,{\rm d}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\Delta_{\omega}^{it}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)\Delta_{\omega}^{-it}x\,{\rm d}g=\Delta_{\omega}^{it}\int_{G}\widetilde{\alpha_{g}}(T)\Delta_{\omega}^{-it}x\,{\rm d}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\omega}^{it}\widetilde{E_{G}}(T)\Delta_{\omega}^{-it}x=\sigma_{\omega}^{t}(\widetilde{E_{G}}(T))x\,,

which proves the sought equality because both
T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
and
x\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}

are arbitrary.

Because
\varphi_{\omega}
is invariant under the action of the modular flow, we are in a position to apply the so-called Pedersen-Takesaki-Radon-Nikodym theorem (see
e.g.
Theorem 4.10 in
[
8
]
or Theorem 2.2.21 in
[
3
]
). Therefore, there must exist a (possibly unbounded) positive operator
H
, defined
on a dense subspace
D(H)\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
, such that
H
is affiliated with the centralizer of
\widetilde{\omega}
and

\varphi_{\omega}(T)=\widetilde{\omega}(HT)\,,\,\,T\in\mathcal{R}_{\omega}\,.

Note that the above equality implies that
\pi_{\omega}(a)\xi_{\omega}
sits in the domain of
H
for every
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
. In particular,
the GNS vector
\xi_{\omega}
belongs to the domain of
H
, and
a fortiori
it belongs to the domain of
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
as well. We shall consistently refer to
H
as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\varphi_{\omega}
with respect to
\omega
.

We can now get to the conclusion.
Define
W_{0}
on the dense subpace
\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}({\mathfrak{A}})\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega\circ E_{G}}
setting

W_{0}\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(a)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}:=\pi_{\omega}(a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}\,,\,\,a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,,

where
H
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative considered above.

We next show that
W_{0}
is well-defined and isometric. To this end, let us denote by

E_{n}
the spectral projection of
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
corresponding to the interval
[0,n]
. Then
for every
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
we have:

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\omega}(a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}\|^{2}=\langle\pi_{\omega}(a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega},\pi_{\omega}(a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}\rangle=\langle\pi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega},H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}\rangle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle E_{n}\pi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega},H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle H^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{n}\pi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega},\xi_{\omega}\rangle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle HE_{n}\pi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)\xi_{\omega},\xi_{\omega}\rangle=\langle H\pi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)\xi_{\omega},\xi_{\omega}\rangle=

\displaystyle\omega\circ E_{G}(a^{*}a)=\|\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(a)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}\|^{2}.

Denote by
W
the extension by continuity of
W_{0}
. The above computations show that
W
is a linear isometry
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega\circ E_{G}}
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
.

We need to show that
W
intertwines
\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}
and

\pi_{\omega}
, that is
W\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(a)=\pi_{\omega}(a)W
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
. It is enough to check the sought equality on the dense subspace
\{\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(b)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}:b\in{\mathfrak{A}}\}
. But for every
b\in{\mathfrak{A}}
one has

\displaystyle W\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(a)\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(b)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}

\displaystyle=W\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(ab)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\omega}(ab)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}(a)\pi_{\omega}(b)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\omega}(a)W\pi_{\omega\circ E_{G}}(b)\xi_{\omega\circ E_{G}},

and we are done.

All that is left to do is prove that
W
is surjective. This is somewhat technical, and the proof is done separately to keep the present
one to a reasonable length.
∎

The strategy to prove that
W
is a unitary is to show that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}
is still a cyclic vector for
\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})
. To do so, we need a couple of preliminary results.
We recall that a closed densely defined operator
T
is
said to be non-singular if
T
is injective, that is, if
\textrm{Ker}(T):=\{x\in D(T):Tx=0\}
is trivial. If we assume
that
T
is a self-adjoint operator, then injectivity of
T
is clearly the same as asking that
T
has a dense range.

The following general lemma should be a well-known result. Nevertheless, we do provide a detailed proof as it is not easily found in the literature.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
T:D(T)\subset{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is a non-singular self-adjoint operator, then its inverse

T^{-1}
, defined on its natural domain
D(T^{-1})={\rm Ran}(T)
is a self-adjoint operator.
Moreover, if
T
is affiliated to a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{R}
, then
T^{-1}
is also
affiliated to
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement is an easy consequence of the spectral theorem. Precisely, up to unitary
equivalence we can suppose that
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}(X,\mu)
for some finite measure space
(X,\mathfrak{S},\mu)
, and
that
T
is the multiplication operator by some real-valued measurable function
h:X\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
which is finite almost everywhere, that is
\mu(\{x\in X:h(x)=\pm\infty\})=0
. The non-singularity of
T
says that the set
\{x\in X:h(x)=0\}
is
\mu
-negligible. Therefore, the function
\frac{1}{h}
is finite almost everywhere w.r.t.
\mu
, which means
T^{-1}
is still a self-adjoint operator, being in turn a multiplication operator
by a function which is finite almost everywhere.

As for the second part of the statement, set
K:=T^{-1}
and let
U
be a unitary in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. We need to show that

UK\subseteq KU
, that is
x\in D(K)
implies
Ux\in D(K)
and
UKx=KUx
. If we define
y:=Kx
, then

y
lies in
D(T)
. Since
T
is affiliated with
\mathcal{R}
by hypothesis, we have
UT\subseteq TU
. In particular,

Uy
is still in
D(T)
and
UTy=TUy
. From this last equality we see that
Ux=TUy
, so
Ux
is in

D(K)={\rm Ran}(T)
, and
KUx=KTUy=Uy=UKx
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

The operator
H
defined as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\varphi_{\omega}
w.r.t
\omega
is non-singular.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the faithfulness of
\varphi_{\omega}
. Indeed, if
H
failed to be injective, then the projection

E
onto
{\rm Ker}(H)
would be a non-zero positive element of
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
, and we would have

\varphi_{\omega}(E)=\langle HE\xi_{\omega},\xi_{\omega}\rangle=0
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the announced result.

Proposition 2.7
.

The isometry
W
is surjective and thus unitary.

Proof.

Since the range of
W
contains the subspace
\{\pi_{\omega}(a)H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}:a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\}
, it is enough
to prove that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}
is cyclic for
\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})
. By von Neumann’s bicommutant theorem, this is the same as
showing that
H^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{\omega}
is cyclic for
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}=\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}
. Set
T=H^{\frac{1}{2}}
and note
that
T
is non-singular by virtue of Lemma
2.6
.

Let now
x
be any vector in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
, by cyclicity of

\xi_{\omega}
there exists

A
in
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
such that
\|A\xi_{\omega}-x\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
Thanks to Lemma
2.5
,
T^{-1}
is a (positive) self-adjoint operator affiliated with
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
as it is in fact affiliated with the centraliser of
\omega
. For every
n>0
, let
E_{n}
be the spectral
projection of
T^{-1}
corresponding to the interval
[0,n]
. Clearly,
E_{n}
still sits in

\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, which means
B_{n}:=AE_{n}T^{-1}
is a bounded operator that belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
.
Because
E_{n}
strongly converges to the identity
I
as
n
goes to infinity, there must exist
n_{0}
such that

\|E_{n_{0}}\xi_{\omega}-\xi_{\omega}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|A\|}
.
But then we have

\displaystyle\|B_{n_{0}}T\xi_{\omega}-x\|\leq

\displaystyle\,\|AE_{n_{0}}T^{-1}T\xi_{\omega}-A\xi_{\omega}\|+\|A\xi_{\omega}-x\|\leq

\displaystyle\,\|A\|\|E_{n_{0}}\xi_{\omega}-\xi_{\omega}\|+\|A\xi_{\omega}-x\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon\,,

which ends the proof as
\varepsilon
is arbitrary.
∎

3.
Examples

The hypothesis of central support in our main result cannot be dispensed with, as shown by the following class of counterexamples.

Example 3.1
.

Let
G
be any (non-abelian) compact group, and let
U:G\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be a continuous unitary representation, which we will
assume irreducible and with
n\geq 2
. For every
g\in G
, let
\alpha_{g}\in{\rm Aut(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))}
be the inner
automorphism implemented by
U(g)
, to wit
\alpha_{g}(T):=U_{g}TU_{g}^{*}
,
T\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Irreducibility implies that the normalized trace
\varphi
is the only
G
-invariant state. Indeed, any state
\omega

on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is of the form
\omega(A)={\rm Tr}(AT)
,
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, for some positive

T
with
{\rm Tr}(T)=1
.
G
-invariance means
{\rm Tr}(AT)={\rm Tr}(U_{g}AU_{g}^{*}T)={\rm Tr}(AU_{g}^{*}TU_{g})
, hence
{\rm Tr}(A(T-U_{g}^{*}TU_{g}))=0
for all
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
g\in G
. By the faithfulness of the trace, we find

T=U_{g}TU_{g}^{*}
for all
g\in G
, which means
T=\lambda I
as the representation is irreducible. Finally, the condition

{\rm Tr}(T)=1
forces
\lambda
to be equal to
\frac{1}{n}
, hence
\omega
is the normalized trace on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

On the other hand, for any state
\omega
on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
one certainly has
\pi_{\omega}\cong\pi_{\omega\circ\alpha_{g}}
for all
g\in G
, merely because the automorphisms
\alpha_{g}
are inner. This means that any state is automatically
quasi-invariant. If now
\omega_{x}
is any vector state, that is
\omega_{x}(A):=\langle Ax,x\rangle
,
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, for some
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then
\pi_{\omega_{x}}
is irreducible. However,
\pi_{\varphi}
is not irreducible, being the direct sum of
n
copies of the identical representation of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. This shows that the conclusion of Theorem
2.4
fails to hold. The reason is that
\omega_{x}
does not have central support.

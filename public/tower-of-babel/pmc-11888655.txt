Effect of MMP Inhibitors on Shear Bond Strength of Adhesive to Dentin after Different Drying Techniques: An In-Vitro Study
Authors: Sheethal K. Narayanan, Krishnan Hari, Joy Mathew, Anila Sukumaran, Basil Joy, Joel Mathew
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and Objective:Instead of excessive drying or wetness, maintaining an optimal moisture level on the dentin surface may enhance the polymerization process of certain monomers. The study was intended to contrast and evaluate the effect of Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) inhibitors (Chlorhexidine (CHX) and Doxycycline (DOX)) on the shear bond strength (SBS) of adhesive dentin interface after different drying techniques (blotting with tissue paper (TP) and damp cotton pellet (CP)).Materials and Methods:Ninety molars collected were split into three groups according to the materials used during the acid etching step: Group A (control): 37% phosphoric acid only; Group B: 37% phosphoric acid followed by 2% CHX; Group C: 37% phosphoric acid followed by 2% DOX. Each group was categorized into 2 subgroups based on the drying methods used after etching (TP and damp CP). A universal bonding agent was applied after this and composite restoration was placed onto the dentin and cured. The samples underwent thermocycling for 500 thermocycles for degradation of the adhesive surface. The SBS of the specimens was appraised with a universal testing machine until fracture.Results:The CHX group blotted with TP (20.98 ± 0.29 MPa) had the highest SBS among the study sample. This was eventually followed by the CHX group blotted with the CP (18.99 ± 0.28 MPa), DOX blotted with TP (19.73 ± 0.51 MPa), and DOX blotted with the CP (17.09 ± 0.55 MPa). The least SBS was observed in the control group blotted with TP (16.9 ± 0.22 MPa) and then by CP (13.78 ± 0.55 MPa).Conclusion:The study showed that CHX had a superior effect on the SBS when compared to DOX. Among the drying agents used TP yielded better results than drying with a damp CP. The overall SBS was high in the CHX group dried with TP and the least strength was shown by the control group where treatment with an MMP inhibitor was done.

INTRODUCTION

When collagen is impregnated with monomer and resin penetrates dentin, a hybrid layer forms. This method is effective for dentin-resin composite adhesion. One-third of a total-etch system’s shear bond strength (SBS) comes from resin penetration or hybridization of dentin tissue.[1] Monomer penetration and diffusion into the subsurface of treated dentin surfaces create the hybrid layer. Adhesives eventually degrade the resin-dentin bond due to hybrid layer disintegration.[2] Dentin matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are endopeptidases that need zinc and calcium. They contribute to tooth growth, dentin cavities, and composite resin restorative hybrid layer degradation. Dentinal MMPs exposed and activated by self-etching break type I collagen fibrils and weaken the link.[3] Inhibiting MMPs can extend adhesive repair lifespan by preventing hybrid layer breakdown.[4] Endogenous and exogenous MMP inhibitors are created by the body and introduced from outside sources. These inhibitors target extracellular matrix-degrading metalloproteinases. Exogenous MMP inhibitors including chlorhexidine (CHX), doxycycline (DOX), and synthetic analogs such as quaternary ammonium compounds, benzalkonium chloride, and gallardin can stop MMP activity in dentin. This stabilizes the adhesive surface and improves bonding.[5]

Dentin moisture impacts hybrid layer and bonding. Air drying of the acid-conditioned dentin interface disintegrates the collagen matrix, creating a dense, resin-resistant coagulate.[6] Hydrophilic bonding may necessitate moisturization to generate the hybrid layer since essential dentin is wet. Water allows monomer transport through collagen pores. Wet bonding strengthens bonds.[7] Thus, eliminating excess water is better than drying dentin. Applying compressed air, moist CP, or TP to dentin eliminates water. This study examines MMP inhibitors (CHX and DOX) on adhesive dentin interface SBS after blotting TP and wet CP drying.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The in-vitro study was undertaken at the Department of Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics. Mar Baselios Dental College, Kothamangalam, with Institutional Ethical Review Committee Approval No: IEC/MBDC/ENDO/PG/5/2021. SBS testing was conducted at the J. J. Murphy Research Centre in Airapuram. The adhesive contact was degraded through thermocycling at Sree Chitra Tirunal Institute for Medical Sciences and Technology in Thiruvananthapuram. The 90 specimens were sorted into three groups of 30 specimens each and two subgroups of 15 specimens each.

Human molar teeth that were extracted completely or mostly intact (caries affecting only enamel) were included. We excluded specimens with significantly decaying, broken, or endodontically treated teeth.

Division of specimens

The ninety molars collected were divided into three groups according to the materials used during the acid etching step:

Each group was split into 2 subgroups based on the drying methods used after etching (TP and a damp CP).

Specimen preparation

We debrided and preserved 90 human molars in distilled water. A low-speed diamond disk under running water ground the occlusal surfaces and smoothed the dentin. Each tooth was acrylic resin-bonded. A 2% doxycycline solution was made by weighing 2 g of 100 mg capsules and adding 100 ml of distilled water.

Restorative procedures

The exposed dentin surfaces were acid-etched three times after the specimens were randomly divided into three groups. In Group A (control), samples were etched for 15 sec with 37% phosphoric acid and rinsed with water. Group B: After 15 sec of 37% phosphoric acid etching, the test specimens were rinsed and dried, then 2% CHX was applied for 60 sec. Group C: After 15 sec of 37% phosphoric acid etching, rinsing, and drying, 2% DOX was applied for 60 sec. MMP inhibitor coating was brushed onto dentin for 60 sec using a micro brush and blot-dried.

The three clusters were separated into two by drying. Subgroup 1 utilized TP to lightly press on the dentin after etching and rinsing, while subgroup 2 used a wet CP to delicately press until the excess moisture was removed. All three groups repeated this. After drying, a coat of universal bonding agent was applied and left for 20 sec before softly light-curing and blow-drying. A 2 mm-tall, 3 mm-diameter metallic ring was utilized on the conditioned dentin surface. Filled the ring with resin composite and polymerized for 40 sec with a light curing apparatus.

Degradation of adhesive interface

The samples were stored at 37°C in distilled water. Each of the 500 heat cycles that were applied to the samples included water baths at two distinct settings (5°C and 55°C) with a 20-sec dwell period in each.

Shear bond strength testing

The samples were placed into an Instron Universal Testing Machine. Employing a knife-edge blade, a crosshead rotation rate of 1 mm/minute was given to each sample till the restoration material’s adhesive bond deteriorated. The point of failure load was determined by logging the measurement on the machine’s monitor screen. The breaking load in KN was translated to bond strength in Megapascals (MPa).

Evaluation of shear bond strength

SBS was determined in MPa using the formula: SBS (MPa) = peak load at failure/adhesive surface area (mm2).

Statistical analysis

To determine whether there were any statistically significant differences between the means of the SBS across the control, CHX, and DOX groups using different drying approaches damp CP and TP-treated samples, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used. Tukey’s post hoc analysis was carried out for multiple comparisons. For intergroup comparisons, an independent sample t-test was utilized. The SPSS 22.0 program revealed a significant difference between the means at P < 0.05.

DISCUSSION

Dentin contains MMPs and cathepsin. They may hydrolyze collagen and hinder hybrid layer composite-to-mineralized dentin bonding.[8] MMP enzymes, which are similar but have different genes, break down extracellular matrix.[49] Human dentin contains collagenase (MMP-8), gelatinases (MMP-2 and MMP-9), and MMP-20.[10] Dentin etching may activate outer dentin MMPs due to acidic circumstances.[5] MMP inhibitors are applied to bonding components or demineralized dentin pretreatments to avoid MMP activation. Much research has revealed that MMP inhibitors reduce collagen fiber degradation in the dentin hybrid layer, but few have examined how CHX and DOX affect adhesive binds to dentin after drying.

Sealing exposed collagen fibrils with resin composites or adhesive resin can prevent the dentinal collagenolytic attack and dental adhesive degradation.[11] Low concentrations of CHX may suppress MMPs by cation-chelating metallic ions like zinc and calcium, preventing their catalytic domains from activating. High amounts of CHX may denature MMPs instead of chelating cations.[12] CHX is substantial because it binds to hardened dentin for 12 weeks.[13] In-vitro and in-vivo investigations revealed 2% CHX aqueous solution reduced bond strength and nano leakage in deciduous and permanent teeth.[121415]

DOX inhibits MMPs best among tetracycline derivatives, according to enzymatic kinetics research. Semi-synthetic tetracycline antibiotic DOX inhibits MMPs non-competitively by interacting with calcium or zinc particles in their catalytic field. This inhibitory mechanism resembles chlorhexidine.[16] Pei Zheng and Chen[17] and Rahul.[18] reported that the CHX etching pretreatment group had the most collagen-fibril interaction, which was consistent with the present results. Surface moisture affects dentin hybridization, with moister circumstances resulting in stronger bonds.[613] Dentin drying reduces surface moisture and eliminates water. Thus, surface moisture is essential for a hydrophilic bonding mechanism to produce the mixed interdiffusion area or hybrid layer.[619]

The TP-dried groups formed stronger connections than the damp CP groups. Water in intertubular dentin’s interfibrillar gaps stretches the collagen network and preserves resin penetration porosity.[20] This verified the research conducted by Krishnan, Jayaprakash, and Kanca.[62122] This study contains MMP inhibitor-free samples dried using both techniques as the control group. Tissue paper-blotted samples had the strongest linkages in the control group. The demineralized layer has a moist surface that is optimally infiltrated by adhesive resin, increasing SBS.

We used universal adhesive in this study. Its acidity comes from 10-MDP, a phosphate monomer. Hydroxyapatite can interact with MDP and collagen by partially demineralising dentin.[23] Calcium ions from partially dissolving hydroxyapatite diffuse through the hybrid layer to form MDP nanolayers. MDP and hydroxyapatite form a strong nanolayer, improving self-etching bonding. Finally, dentin sticks to moist and dry polyalkenoic acid copolymers.[24]

CONCLUSION

Dentin surface treatment with MMP inhibitor CHX showed the highest SBS than DOX. Among the drying agents, blotting with TP yielded better bond strength than damp CP. Of all the groups the highest SBS were shown by samples treated with CHX and dried with TP. The control group that was not treated with MMP inhibitors showed the least SBS among all the groups.

Abstract
The command did_multiplegt_dyn can be used to estimate event-study effects in complex designs with a potentially non-binary and/or non-absorbing treatment. This paper starts by providing an overview of the estimators computed by the command. Then, simulations based on three real datasets are used to demonstrate the estimators’ properties. Finally, the command is used on four real datasets to estimate event-study effects in complex designs. The first example has a binary treatment that can turn on an off. The second example has a continuous absorbing treatment. The third example has a discrete multivalued treatment that can increase or decrease multiple times over time. The fourth example has two, binary and absorbing treatments, where the second treatment always happens after the first.

Introduction

This paper presents the
did_multiplegt_dyn
Stata and R commands, respectively available from the SSC and CRAN repositories, which compute the event-study estimators proposed by
De Chaisemartin and
D’Haultfœuille 2024
, hereafter referred to as the paper. Those estimators can already be computed by the
did_multiplegt
Stata command, when the
robust_dynamic
option is specified. However,
did_multiplegt
relies on the boostrap for variances’ estimation, while
did_multiplegt_dyn
relies on analytic formulas. Thus,
did_multiplegt_dyn
is much faster than
did_multiplegt
. It is also more closely aligned with the final version of the paper, and it has more options. Therefore, it supersedes
did_multiplegt
for the computation of the estimators proposed by
De Chaisemartin and
D’Haultfœuille 2024
.

The main goal of this companion note is to increase the transparency of the
did_multiplegt_dyn
command, by bridging the paper and its ado. For that purpose, it gives explicit formulas for the estimators and the variance estimators produced by the command, with more details than in
De Chaisemartin and
D’Haultfœuille 2024
. Moreover, for many of the key quantities to compute the estimators and their variances, this companion note gives tags one can use to locate the lines computing those quantities in the .ado and .R files.

This companion note is organized as follows. In Section 1, we review the baseline event-study estimators computed by the command without any option other than
effects
. In Section 2, we review other estimators computed by the command when other options are specified. In particular, we outline in details estimators with control variables. Section 3 presents some simulations results. In Appendix A, we explain how the command deals with imbalanced panels. In Appendix B, we review instances where
did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
can output different point estimates, and explain the source of those differences.

2.1
Set-up and identifying assumptions

Data.

We seek to estimate the effect of a treatment on an outcome. For that purpose, we use a panel of
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle(g,t)

Let
\displaystyle N_{g,t}

\displaystyle(g,t)
in the estimation. If the data is at the
\displaystyle(g,t)
level and the
weight

\displaystyle N_{g,t}=1
. If the data is at the
\displaystyle(g,t)
level and the
weight
option is specified,
\displaystyle N_{g,t}
is equal to the argument inputted to that option. If the data is at a more disaggregated level than the
\displaystyle(g,t)
level
and the

\displaystyle N_{g,t}
is the number of observations with non-missing outcome data in cell
\displaystyle(g,t)
. Finally, if the data is at a more disaggregated level than the
\displaystyle(g,t)
level and the

\displaystyle N_{g,t}
is equal to the summation of the argument inputted to that option across all observations with non-missing outcome data in cell
\displaystyle(g,t)

Treatment and outcome definitions.

If the data is at the
\displaystyle(g,t)
level, let
\displaystyle D_{g,t}

\displaystyle Y_{g,t}
denote the treatment and outcome of cell
\displaystyle(g,t)
. If the data is at a more disaggregated level than the
\displaystyle(g,t)

\displaystyle D_{g,t}
and
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}

\displaystyle(g,t)

Code tag: see “Collapse and weight” section in the code.

Potential and observed outcomes.

For all
\displaystyle(d_{1},...,d_{T})\in\mathcal{D}
, let
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}(d_{1},...,d_{T})
denote the potential outcome of group
\displaystyle g
at
\displaystyle t
if
\displaystyle(D_{g,1},...,D_{g,T})=(d_{1},...,d_{T})
, and let
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}=Y_{g,t}(D_{g,1},...,D_{g,T})
denote the observed outcome of
\displaystyle g
at
\displaystyle t
. This dynamic potential outcome framework follows
Robins 1986

\displaystyle t
to depend on their past and future treatments.

Conditioning on the design.

When defining its target parameters, the paper takes the perspective of a social planner, seeking to conduct a cost-benefit analysis comparing groups’ actual treatments
\displaystyle\bm{D}\equiv(D_{g,t})_{g\in\{1,...,G\}\times\{1,...,T\}}
to the counterfactual “status-quo” scenario where every group would have kept all the time
the same treatment as in period 1. Then, the analysis is conditional on
\displaystyle\bm{D}
, the study’s design. Throughout, we also assume that
\displaystyle N_{g,t}
is non-stochastic. Then, only groups’ potential outcomes are random.

Date of first treatment change.

For all
\displaystyle g
, let
\displaystyle F_{g}=\min\{t:t\geq 2,D_{g,t}\neq D_{g,t-1}\}
. We adopt the convention that
\displaystyle F_{g}=T+1
if
\displaystyle g
’s treatment never changes.
Code tag: see “Date of first treatment change” section in the code.

Condition that the design has to satisfy for our baseline estimators to be applicable.

Our baseline estimators are applicable to any design that satisfies the restriction below.

Design Restriction 1

\displaystyle\exists(g,g^{\prime})
such that: (i)
\displaystyle D_{g,1}=D_{g^{\prime},1}
, (ii)
\displaystyle F_{g}\neq F_{g^{\prime}}
.

(i) requires that there exist groups with the same period-one treatment. This is essentially a restriction on the support of the period-one treatment. If groups’ period-one treatments are i.i.d. draws from a continuous distribution,
\displaystyle D_{g,1}\neq D_{g^{\prime},1}
for all
\displaystyle(g,g^{\prime})
, so (i) fails. On the other hand, if
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
can only take a finite number of values
\displaystyle K
, (i) automatically holds as long as
\displaystyle K<G
. When (i) fails, the command can still be used with the
continuous
option (see below), but for now we assume that (i) holds. (ii) requires that there is heterogeneity in the date at which groups change treatment for the first time. (ii) for instance fails if groups’ treatment is extremely non-persistent, so that
\displaystyle D_{g,1}\neq D_{g,2}
and
\displaystyle F_{g}=2
for all
\displaystyle g
. The command assesses if Design Restriction
1
is satisfied, and returns an error message if it is not.
Code tag: see “Error message if Design Restriction 1 is not met” sections in the code.

Identifying assumptions

We maintain throughout the following condition.

Assumption 1

(No Anticipation)
\displaystyle\forall g
,
\displaystyle\forall(d_{1},...,d_{T})\in\mathcal{D}
,
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}(d_{1},...,d_{T})=Y_{g,t}(d_{1},...,d_{t})
.

Assumption
1
requires that a group’s current outcome does not depend on its future treatments, the so-called no-anticipation hypothesis. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\text{r}}_{1}=\{d:\exists(g,g^{\prime})\in\{1,...,G\}^{2}:D_{g,1}=D_{g^{\prime},1}=d,F_{g}\neq F_{g^{\prime}}\}

be the set of period-one-treatment values such that two groups with different values of
\displaystyle F_{g}
have that period-one treatment. For any
\displaystyle d
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\text{r}}_{1}
and any
\displaystyle t
, let
\displaystyle\bm{d}_{t}
denote a
\displaystyle 1\times t
vector of
\displaystyle d
s. For any integer
\displaystyle k
, let
\displaystyle\bm{D}_{g,1,k}
be a
\displaystyle 1\times k
vector whose coordinates are all equal to
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
.
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}(\bm{D}_{g,1,t})
is
\displaystyle g
’s period-
\displaystyle t
outcome in a counterfactual where it keeps its period-one treatment till period
\displaystyle t
. We refer to it as its “status-quo” potential outcome. In this section, we make the following parallel-trends assumption:

Assumption 2

(Parallel trends for the status-quo outcome, for groups with the same period-one treatment)

\displaystyle\forall(g,g\textquoteright)
, if
\displaystyle D_{g,1}=D_{g\textquoteright,1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\text{r}}
, then
\displaystyle\forall t\geq 2
,

E[Y_{g,t}(\bm{D}_{g,1,t})-Y_{g,t-1}(\bm{D}_{g,1,t-1})|\bm{D}]=E[Y_{g\textquoteright,t}(\bm{D}_{g\textquoteright,1,t})-Y_{g\textquoteright,t-1}(\bm{D}_{g\textquoteright,1,t-1})|\bm{D}].

Assumption
2
requires that if two groups have the same period-one treatment, then they have the same expected evolution of their status-quo outcome. In other words, if their treatments do not change, all groups with the same period-one treatment have the same expected outcome evolution.

Estimators’ asymptotic distributions and variances.

Assumption to derive estimators’ asymptotic distributions.

Asymptotic results in the paper rely on the following assumption.

Assumption 3

(Independent groups) Conditional on
\displaystyle(\bm{D}_{g})_{g\geq 1}

\displaystyle(\bm{Y}_{g})_{g\geq 1}

Assumption

3.1
Placebos:
placebo(#)

The command can compute placebo estimators
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
, to test the no-anticipation and parallel-trends assumptions underlying the event-study estimators it computes. Those placebos are defined as symmetrically as possible to the actual event-study estimators. Namely,
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
is defined exactly as
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
, replacing

\displaystyle Y_{g,F_{g}-1+\ell}-Y_{g,F_{g}-1}
by
\displaystyle Y_{g,F_{g}-1-\ell}-Y_{g,F_{g}-1}
.
Intuitively,
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
compares the outcome evolution of switchers included in
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
to that of their control groups, from period
\displaystyle F_{g}-\ell-1
to period
\displaystyle F_{g}-1
, namely before switchers’ treatment changes for the first time. Accordingly,
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
assesses if those switchers and their control groups experience parallel evolutions of their status-quo potential outcomes, for
\displaystyle\ell
periods, the number of periods over which parallel trends has to hold for
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
to be unbiased. There may be switchers included in
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
such that
\displaystyle F_{g}<\ell+2
: those switchers have to be excluded from the placebo, because they are not observed for sufficiently many time periods prior to their first treatment change, and
\displaystyle Y_{g,F_{g}-1-\ell}-Y_{g,F_{g}-1}
cannot be computed for them. With a balanced panel those are the only switchers included in
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
that have to be excluded from
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
. Conversely, there may be switchers for which
\displaystyle Y_{g,F_{g}-1-\ell}-Y_{g,F_{g}-1}
can be computed but
\displaystyle Y_{g,F_{g}-1+\ell}-Y_{g,F_{g}-1}
cannot: those switchers are not included in
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
, so the command does not included them in
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
, so that the placebo compares switchers’ and controls’ outcome evolution only for switchers included in the event-study effect. This is also the reason why the number of placebos requested cannot be larger than the number of effects requested. Note that while
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
goes from the past (period
\displaystyle F_{g}-1
) to the future (period
\displaystyle F_{g}-1+\ell
),
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{\text{pl}}_{\ell}
goes from the future (period
\displaystyle F_{g}-1
) to the past (period
\displaystyle F_{g}-1-\ell
). This follows the standard practice in event-study regressions, where the reference period is the one before the event. See Section 1.1 of the paper’s Web Appendix for further details. If the user requests that at least two placebos be estimated,
the command computes the p-value of a joint test that all placebos are equal.

3.4
Allowing for group-specific linear trends:
trends_lin

When this option is specified, the command starts by computing the event-study estimators described above, with an outcome variable equal to the outcome’s first-difference rather than to the outcome itself. Those estimators rely on a parallel trends assumption on the outcome’s first-difference, thus allowing for group-specific linear trends. However, they are unbiased for event-study effects the outcome’s first-difference. To recover event-study effect
\displaystyle\ell
on the outcome, event-study effects on the outcome’s first-difference are summed from 1 to
\displaystyle\ell
. One needs to ensure that all event-study effects on the outcome’s first-difference apply to the same switchers, as otherwise the sum of those event-study effects would not apply to a well-defined population of switchers. See Section 1.3 of the paper’s Web Appendix for further details.

3.5
Estimation with a continuous period-one treatment:
continuous(#)

Identifying assumption.

The command can be used when groups’ period-one treatment is continuous, meaning that all groups have a different period-one treatment value. With a discrete period-one treatment, the command compares the outcome evolution of switchers and non-switchers with the same period-one treatment.
But with a truly continuous period-one treatment, there will be no two groups with the same period-one
treatment. Then, the command assumes that group’s status-quo outcome evolution is a polynomial in their period-one treatment.
Specifically,

Assumption 6

(Functional parallel trends for the status-quo outcome, conditional on the period-one treatment) For all
\displaystyle t\geq 2
,
\displaystyle E[Y_{g,t}(\bm{D}_{g,1,t})-Y_{g,t-1}(\bm{D}_{g,1,t-1})|\bm{D}]=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\gamma_{k,t}D_{g,1}^{k}
.

The assumed polynomial order
\displaystyle K
is the option’s argument.

Estimators.

To compute the non-normalized event-study estimators with the
continuous(#)
option, the command starts by redefining the treatment as
\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{g,t}=1\{S_{g}=1,t\geq F_{g}\}-1\{S_{g}=0,t\geq F_{g}\}
, a variable equal to zero before groups’ treatment switches, equal to
\displaystyle 1
for switchers-in after they switch, and to
\displaystyle-1
for switchers-out after they switch. Then, the estimation is run with this modified treatment, adding as control variables

\displaystyle(D_{g,1}^{k}1\{t\geq t^{\prime}\})_{k\in\{0,...,K\},t^{\prime}\in\{1,...,T\}}
, a full set of interactions between the polynomial terms in the baseline treatment and indicators for period
\displaystyle t
being after
\displaystyle t^{\prime}
. As
\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{g,1}=0
for all
\displaystyle g
, the command runs only one residualization regression of
\displaystyle Y_{g,t}-Y_{g,t-1}
on
\displaystyle(D_{g,1}^{k}1\{t\geq t^{\prime}\})_{k\in\{0,...,K\},t^{\prime}\in\{1,...,T\}}
(and potentially other controls specified by the user), among all
\displaystyle(g,t)
cells whose treatment has not changed yet at
\displaystyle t
. To compute the average total effect or normalized event-study estimators, the resulting non-normalized event-study estimators are used in the numerator, and the denominator is computed using the formulas given above, but with the original treatment
\displaystyle D_{g,t}
instead of
\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{g,t}
. The estimators produced with the

continuous(#)
option are unbiased under Assumption
6
, if the requested polynomial order is greater than or equal to
\displaystyle K
.

Variance estimators.

Unlike the other variance estimators computed by the command, those computed when the
continuous
option is specified are not backed by a proven asymptotic normality result, or at least by a linearization of the estimators. Preliminary simulation evidence indicates that when the option is used with a correctly-specified polynomial order, those variance estimators are conservative. On the other hand, when the specified polynomial order is strictly larger than that in Assumption
6
, those variance estimators can become liberal. Thus, when this option is specified, we recommend using the bootstrap for inference, by manually bootstrapping the command. At least, one should perform a robustness check where one compares the analytic variance computed by the command to a bootstrapped variance. Soon, the command will have an option to automatically perform this bootstrap when the
continuous
option is specified.

3.6
Clustering standard errors:
cluster(varlist)

The command can compute variance estimators clustered at a coarser level than groups. Then, the variance estimators are not very different from those given in the definition of
\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{\ell}
above, except that the variables
\displaystyle U^{var}_{g,\ell}
are summed across groups in a given cluster, and the resulting variable is squared and summed across clusters (
Code tag: see the “sum U_Gg_var_l within a cluster” section in the code
). Another difference is that with clustering, cohorts’ cardinality (e.g.:
\displaystyle\#\mathcal{U}_{d,t,\ell}
) are now defined as the number of different clusters that the groups in the cohort belong to. Consequently, while
\displaystyle\#\mathcal{U}_{d,t,\ell}>1
without clustering, with clustering we could have
\displaystyle\#\mathcal{U}_{d,t,\ell}=1:
all switchers and not yet switchers with the same baseline treatment may belong to the same cluster. When
\displaystyle\#\mathcal{U}_{d,t,\ell}=1
, we let
\displaystyle\widehat{E}_{g,t}=0
and we let
\displaystyle\text{DOF}_{g,t}=1
.

3.7
Estimating heterogeneous effects:
by(
varname
)
and
predict_het(
varlist,numlist

When the option

varname
)

When the option

varlist

normalized

controls

Shahn 2023

de Chaisemartin and

3.8
Ensuring that all event-study effects are estimated for the same switchers:
same_switchers

The sample of switchers to which
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
or
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{n}_{\ell}
apply changes with
\displaystyle\ell
, so compositional changes can account for the variation in those effects across
\displaystyle\ell

did_multiplegt_dyn

same_switchers_pl
option is also specified, then
placebos are estimated only for switchers for which all the requested effects and placebos can be estimated.
same_switchers_pl
can only be specified if

is also specified. (
Code tag: see the “If the same_switchers option is specified” section in the code.
)

3.9
Estimating event-study effects separately for switchers in/out:
switchers(
in/out
)

One may be interested in estimating separately the treatment effect of switchers-in, whose treatment after they switch is larger than their period-one treatment, and of switchers-out, whose treatment after they switch is lower than their period-one treatment. In that case, one should run the command first with the

Appendix B
Conventions used by the
did_multiplegt_dyn
Stata command in the presence of missing treatments

The estimators computed by the command crucially rely on the full knowledge of a group’s sequence of treatments. Of particular importance are a group’s baseline treatment
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
, and the first period at which a group’s treatment changes
\displaystyle F_{g}
. While the definitions of
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
and
\displaystyle F_{g}
are uncontroversial when groups’ treatments are observed at every time period, those definitions can become more controversial when the treatments of some groups are unobserved at some time periods. Then,
did_multiplegt_dyn
redefines
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
as a group’s treatment at the first time period where that treatment is observed, and
\displaystyle F_{g}
as the first period where the group is observed with a different treatment value.
did_multiplegt_dyn
uses the following rules to deal with missing treatments.

Conservative option to deal with missing treatments.

If the

drop_if_d_miss_before_first_switch
option is specified,
did_multiplegt_dyn
adopts a conservative approach, and drops all
\displaystyle(g,t)
cells such that there is a
\displaystyle t_{0}\leq t
such that
\displaystyle g
’s treatment is missing at
\displaystyle t_{0}
,
\displaystyle g
’s treatment has not changed yet at
\displaystyle t_{0}
, and
\displaystyle g
’s outcome has been observed at least once at or before
\displaystyle t_{0}
. In such situations, we cannot know for sure when
\displaystyle g
’s first treatment change took place.

Liberal option to deal with missing treatments: groups that experience at least one treatment change.

By default, the command adopts a more liberal approach to deal with missing treatments. For groups that experience at least one treatment change, let
\displaystyle FD_{g}
be the first date at which their treatment is observed, let
\displaystyle LDBF_{g}
be the last date before their first treatment change when their treatment is observed, let
\displaystyle F_{g}
denote the first date when they are observed with a different treatment than that they had at
\displaystyle FD_{g}
, and let
\displaystyle LD_{g}
be the last period when their treatment is observed. We have
\displaystyle FD_{g}\leq LDBF_{g}\leq F_{g}\leq LD_{g}
, and the command deals with missing treatments depending on when they occur with respect to those four dates:

1.

Before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
, the command considers that
\displaystyle g
joins the panel at
\displaystyle FD_{g}
: any non-missing outcome before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
is replaced as missing, but all non-missing outcomes after
\displaystyle FD_{g}
are kept. For groups such that their outcome is observed at least one date before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
, this is a liberal convention: those groups exist before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
, so one could argue that their baseline treatment is missing. Users may drop those groups from the analysis, using the
drop_if_d_miss_before_first_switch
option.

2.

If a treatment is missing at
\displaystyle FD_{g}<t<LDBF_{g}
, it is replaced by
\displaystyle g
’s baseline treatment. In a binary and staggered design, this amounts to imputing the missing treatment of a group later observed with a treatment of zero by a zero, an imputation justified by the design. Outside of binary and staggered designs, this may be a liberal imputation, which can be overruled by using the
drop_if_d_miss_before_first_switch
option.

3.

If
\displaystyle LDBF_{g}<F_{g}-1
,
\displaystyle g
’s treatment is missing at
\displaystyle F_{g}-1
, the period just before it is observed with a treatment different from its baseline treatment. Then, we cannot know the exact date when
\displaystyle g
’s treatment has changed for the first time, even in a binary and staggered design. Therefore, we set
\displaystyle g
’s outcome at missing starting at
\displaystyle LDBF_{g}+1
.

4.

If the treatment is missing at
\displaystyle t>F_{g}
, we replace it by
\displaystyle D_{g,F_{g}}
,
\displaystyle g
’s treatment at the date of its first treatment change. In designs where groups’ treatment can change at most once, this imputation is justified by the design. In other designs, this imputation may be liberal, but it is innocuous for the computation of the reduced-form effects
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
, so we do not give users the option to overrule it.

Liberal option to deal with missing treatments: groups whose treatment never changes.

For those groups, we just have
\displaystyle FD_{g}\leq LD_{g}
, and we will deal with missing treatments depending on when they occur with respect to those two dates.

1.

Before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
, the command again considers that
\displaystyle g
joins the panel at
\displaystyle FD_{g}
: any non-missing outcome before
\displaystyle FD_{g}
is replaced as missing, but all non-missing outcomes after
\displaystyle FD_{g}
are kept. This is again a liberal convention that can be overruled using the
drop_if_d_miss_before_first_switch
option.

2.

Treatments missing at
\displaystyle FD_{g}<t<LD_{g}
are replaced by
\displaystyle D_{g,1}
. In a binary and staggered design, this imputation is justified by the design. Outside of binary and staggered designs, this may be a liberal imputation, which can be overruled by using the
drop_if_d_miss_before_first_switch
option.

3.

For all
\displaystyle t>LD_{g}
,
\displaystyle g
’s outcomes are replaced by missing. Even in a binary and staggered design, we cannot infer
\displaystyle g
’s treatment at
\displaystyle t>LD_{g}
:
\displaystyle g
may have gotten treated at those dates and one may just not observe it.

Appendix C
Comparing the
did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
Stata commands

C.1
Main differences between the two commands

Estimators computed.

Like
did_multiplegt_dyn
, the
did_multiplegt
package also computes the event-study estimators proposed by
De Chaisemartin and
D’Haultfœuille 2024
, when the
robust_dynamic
option is specified.
did_multiplegt_dyn
supersedes

did_multiplegt
to compute those estimators. When the
robust_dynamic
option is not specified,
did_multiplegt
computes the
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{M}
estimator proposed by
de Chaisemartin and D’Haultfœuille 2020
. That estimator is not computed by
did_multiplegt_dyn
:
did_multiplegt
remains the primary tool to compute it.

Syntax.

Like
did_multiplegt
, the basic syntax of
did_multiplegt_dyn
is

did_multiplegt_dyn Y G T D
,

where
Y
is the outcome variable,
G
the outcome variable,
T
the time variable, and
D
the treatment variable.

Variance estimation.

The main difference between
did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
is that the former relies on the bootstrap for variance estimation, while the later relies on analytic variance formulas. Accordingly,
did_multiplegt_dyn
is much faster than
did_multiplegt
, and it does not need the
breps(#)
option.

Notational differences.

The notations used by
did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
are slightly different:
effect_0
in
did_multiplegt
is
effect_1
in
did_multiplegt_dyn
,
effect_1
in
did_multiplegt
is
effect_2
in
did_multiplegt_dyn
, etc… To estimate more than one event-study effect, one has to request the
effects
option with
did_multiplegt_dyn
. That option was called
dynamic
in the
did_multiplegt
command.

Clustering by default.

The package
did_multiplegt_dyn
clusters by default standard errors at the level of the group variable. Standard errors can be clustered at a more aggregated level, but they cannot be clustered at a more disaggregated level.
did_multiplegt
clustered by default at the group
\displaystyle\times
time level.

Options.

Some of the options of
did_multiplegt
do not exist for
did_multiplegt_dyn
. This includes the
recat_treatment
and
threshold_stable_treatment
options. On the other hand,
did_multiplegt_dyn
has some new options, to compute the normalized event-study estimators proposed in the latest version of
De Chaisemartin and
D’Haultfœuille 2024
, and to test for heterogeneous treatment effects.

C.2
Instances where
did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
may output different results.

The two commands do not output different event-study and placebo effects when applied to a balanced panel of groups, the
dont_drop_larger_lower
option is not specified in
did_multiplegt_dyn
while the
drop_larger_lower
option is specified in
did_multiplegt
, and the
trends_nonparam
option is not specified.

Imbalanced panel: missing treatments.

The two packages might output different results when applied to a panel of groups that is imbalanced because some groups’ treatments are missing at some dates. The two commands use different conventions to account for missing treatments. The conventions used by
did_multiplegt_dyn
are explained in great details in Appendix A. The conventions used by
did_multiplegt
are not as explicitly thought out as those used by
did_multiplegt_dyn
: the latter command deals more transparently with missing treatments.

Imbalanced panel: missing outcomes.

When the group-level panel is imbalanced because some groups have missing outcomes at some time periods, the event-study effects outputed by
did_multiplegt_dyn
and
did_multiplegt
are similar, but the placebo effects can differ. When estimating a placebo effect
\displaystyle\text{DID}^{pl}_{\ell}
,
did_multiplegt_dyn
takes into account only the switchers which were used in the estimation of the corresponding dynamic effect
\displaystyle\text{DID}_{\ell}
.
did_multiplegt
, on the other hand, includes some switchers for which that effect could not be computed, because their
\displaystyle F_{g}-1
or their
\displaystyle F_{g}-1+\ell
outcome is missing.

Switchers with no first stage.

Switchers whose average treatment after they switch is exactly equal to their baseline treatment, herafter referred to as switchers with no first-stage, are dropped by
did_multiplegt_dyn
, not by
did_multiplegt
. This can lead to differences between the event-study and placebo effects outputed by the two commands. Switchers with no first stage can only arise when the treatment is non-binary. Such switchers are dropped by both commands when the
dont_drop_larger_lower
option is not specified in
did_multiplegt_dyn
while the
drop_larger_lower
option is specified in
did_multiplegt
, so the event-study and placebo effects outputed by the two commands are the same when this option is specified, even in the presence of switchers with no first-stage.

Missing controls.

did_multiplegt
and
did_multiplegt_dyn
can also output different results when the
controls
option is specified, and some covariates are missing for some
\displaystyle(g,t)
-cells. When a switcher’s covariate is missing at
\displaystyle t
,
did_multiplegt
drops that switcher from the estimation at all subsequent time periods.
did_multiplegt_dyn
, on the other hand, does not drop that switcher from the estimation at all subsequent time periods.

trends_nonparam
.

When the
trends_nonparam(
varlist)
option is specified,
did_multiplegt_dyn
drops switchers with no control group with the same value of
varlist
.
did_multiplegt
only drops those switchers if its option
always_trends_nonparam
is specified. Thus, with the
trends_non_param
option, the two commands can output different results if
did_multiplegt
is ran without the
always_trends_nonparam
option.

Number of observations used to compute the average effect.

The number of observations outputed by
did_multiplegt
was sometimes incorrect. The number outputed by
did_multiplegt_dyn
is correct.

Long term neuropsychological evaluation of adult patients with craniopharyngiomas
Authors: Julie Guillotin, Charlotte Planchon, Julien Engelhardt, Edouard Gimbert, Thomas Cuny, Fadi Alkhiami, Kamel Mohammedi, Antoine Tabarin, Blandine Gatta Cherifi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCraniopharyngiomas are benign tumors that develop in the hypothalamic-pituitary region. Their treatment mainly involves surgery, sometimes combined with radiotherapy. The hypothalamus plays an essential role in hormonal balance and energy regulation, but also in cognition through connectivity circuits. The cognitive impact of craniopharyngioma treatment is poorly understood and has mainly been studied in children.Patients and methodsWe report the neuropsychological assessments of 22 adult patients treated for craniopharyngioma with a follow-up of at least 5 years, 16 of whom were diagnosed while being adults. All assessments were performed by a neuropsychologist using validated tests. More than 65% of patients, including those diagnosed in adulthood, showed at least one impairment in executive functioning, especially cognitive inhibition or deficit in organization. 75% of patients reported fatigue. For half of these patients, these impairments needed specific treatment like cognitive rehabilitation.ConclusionNeuropsychological evaluation has to be part of the follow-up of patients treated for a craniopharyngioma including those diagnosed during adulthood.

Introduction

Craniopharyngioma is a rare tumor of the sellar and parasellar region [1–3]. Management remains challenging often requiring sequential and carefully balanced strategies in expert centers [4]. Indeed, critical structures such as the pituitary stalk and hypothalamus may be compromised by the tumor and its treatments such as surgery and/or radiotherapy, leading to major complications. Endocrine deficiencies and hypothalamic syndrome are well-recognized sequelae and severe hypothalamic obesity resulting from intractable weight gain is a frequent consequence [4, 5].

Less visible but equally disabling outcomes include cognitive impairment, emotional dysregulation which are underreported despite their high prevalence. Neurocognitive function has been studied primarily in children and in patients diagnosed during childhood, showing attention deficits, social difficulties, and a tendency toward internalizing behaviors, particularly in school settings [6–10]. In contrast, and despite that recent guidelines recommend systematic neuropsychological assessment during follow-up, studies evaluating neuropsychological consequences in the long-term in adults are scarce [11]. We report, here, long-term neurocognitive outcomes in adult patients treated for craniopharyngioma at least 5 years earlier.

Patients and methods

Patients

Participants were recruited amongst patients treated in the endocrinology and neurosurgery departments of the university hospital of Bordeaux. All patients were adults (i.e., age ≥ 18 yo) with a history of craniopharyngioma treated at least 5 years ago. They have been contacted by phone to offer a neuropsychological evaluation. Exclusion criteria were: comorbidities impairing cognitive function (e.g., stroke sequelae, Parkinson’s disease), language barrier preventing neuropsychological testing, age > 75 years.

For all patients, sociodemographic (educational level, professional status), anthropometric (including self-reported weight), endocrine status, and tumor characteristics (mode of discovery, treatments received) were recorded.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and all procedures were carried out with the adequate understanding and written inform consent of the participants. Ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of University Hospital of Bordeaux CER-BDX 2025 − 300.

Statistics

Continuous variables were expressed as mean (standard deviations) or median (extremes) for those with skewed distribution. Categorical variables were expressed as number (proportions).

The Student’s t-test, Wilcoxon test and chi-square test were used to compare gender distribution, age at diagnosis of craniopharyngioma, and current age between the group of patients who underwent neuropsychological assessment and those who did not. Linear regression model was used to assess neuropsychological evaluation in patients with multiple surgery compared with those with unique surgery, and in patients who were maintained on GH substitution compared with those without. Models were adjusted for age and BMI at evaluation and time since the last treatment. The statistics were calculated using Prism 10 software and JMP Student 19 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). P < 0.05 was considered as significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Fifty-eight living patients who had received their last treatment for a craniopharyngioma more than 5 years ago were identified. Patients older than 75 years (n = 6) and those with comorbidities impairing cognition—stroke sequelae (n = 1), Parkinson’s disease (n = 1), autism (n = 1), behavioral disorders (n = 2)—were excluded, as well as those with a language barrier (n = 2).

The remaining 45 patients were contacted by phone to offer an evaluation with the neuropsychologist. Fourteen could not be reached. It is not known whether this was due to an incorrect phone number or a lack of response from the patient. Six patients declined participation and three initially agreed but canceled or did not attend. Finaly, 22 patients underwent neuropsychological assessment (Fig. 1).

The mean age at time of diagnosis of craniopharyngioma did not differ between patients who underwent the assessment and those who did not (30 years +/- 17 years for patients who underwent assessment and 25 years +/- 18 years for patients who did not undergo assessment, p = 0.16). The rate of females was significantly higher in the evaluated population than in the non-evaluated population (64% versus 35% in the non-evaluated population, p = 0.003).

Total population

The 22 evaluated patients (14 women) had a mean age of 46 ± 12 years (range, 23–68). The mean interval since the last treatment was 15 ± 6 years. The mean age at diagnosis was 30 ± 17 years. Six patients (27%) were diagnosed during childhood (< 18 years) and 16 (73%) in adulthood.

Intracranial hypertension was the initial symptom of craniopharyngioma in 14 patients (64%), and visual disturbances in 10 (45%). 5 cases (23%) were diagnosed following the investigation of endocrine deficiencies. Cognitive disorders, vasopressin deficiency and hypothalamic syndrome were the initial manifestation of the disease in 4 (18%), 2 (9%), and 1 (5%) patient, respectively. The initial symptom was not documented in the medical record in 1 case (5%).

All patients underwent surgery, which was incomplete in 17 cases (77%) (Table 1). Adjuvant radiotherapy was required in 16 patients (73%). Pathological confirmation of craniopharyngioma was obtained in 21 patients (95%). Histological subtype was available in 8 (36%) that revealed all adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma.

Focus on adult-onset craniopharyngiomas

Among the 22 evaluated patients, 16 including 11 women (69%) were diagnosed during adulthood. Their mean age at assessment was 51 ± 10 years (range, 34–68).

Comparison with non-evaluated adult patients revealed no difference in age at diagnosis (38 ± 13 vs. 37 ± 15 years, p = 0.45). However, evaluated patients were younger at assessment (51 ± 10 vs. 58 ± 9 years, p = 0.04) and more often female (69% vs. 19%, p = 0.001). The mean interval since the last treatment was 13 ± 5 years.

The frequency of symptoms that revealed the craniopharyngioma was similar in the adult population and in the total population (Table 1). At the time of diagnosis of the craniopharyngioma, gonadotropic deficiency was present in 6 patients (38%), thyrotropic deficiency in 3 (19%), somatotropic deficiency in 2 (13%), corticotropic deficiency in 2 (13%), hyperprolactinemia in 2 (13%), and vasopressin deficiency in 2 (13%).

All patients underwent surgery, which was incomplete in 13 cases (81%), all of whom subsequently received radiotherapy (Table 1). The prevalence of hormonal deficiency at the time of the evaluation was similar to the one in the global population. The general characteristics of the patients are summarized in Table 1.

Six patients (38%) were partially or totally disabled. Three patients were retired, including two who retired early for disability. At evaluation, 6 patients (38%) were overweight and 5 (31%) obese.

Discussion

We report the long-term neuropsychological impairment of 22 adult patients with a craniopharyngioma, 16 patients of whom were diagnosed for during adulthood.

In our population, there is a marked impairment of executive functions (Fig. 3).

Similar results were observed in the subgroup including only patients in whom craniopharyngioma has been diagnosed during adulthood. Thus, inhibition, assessed by the Stroop Grefex test, is abnormal in more than 68% of patients. In practice, this translates into difficulties in filtering relevant information, meaning that subjects focus their attention on many things around them, with intrusive thoughts that can cause them to forget the task at hand. This reduces efficiency in performing tasks and causes fatigue, reported in 73% of patients, in both populations (the total and adult -diagnosed one). Disorganization, as revealed by the revised commissions test, is also very pronounced, with at least 68% of abnormal results in both groups. Disorganization makes daily life management complex. Indeed, organization is necessary to carry out daily tasks, establishing priorities with the aim of anticipating the steps required to complete an action. Flexibility, assessed by the TMTB, allows individuals to switch easily from one task to another, a skill that is deficient for around 30% in both groups. This function is linked to the lack of inhibition described above, which is essential for disengaging from one cognitive operation and reengaging in another.

These defects in executive functions can be improved by therapies like cognitive rehabilitation [13–15]. In our cohort, more than half of the patients were referred to support care. This means that these evaluations have really to be part of follow up of patients previously treated for craniopharyngioma.

The decline in executive functioning and the compensatory strategies required to maintain satisfactory cognitive functioning are very energy-intensive. They are even, sometimes, inappropriate because of lack of knowledge about cognitive functioning which is consistent with the excessive fatigue reported by 73% of patients. Of note, fatigue may also be linked to other symptoms frequently diagnosed in patients after the treatment of craniopharyngioma, such as altered sleep-wake rhythms and obstructive ventilatory disorders exacerbated by obesity [16] and also growth hormone and gonadotropin deficiencies. In our population, several patients did indeed have obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), but the exact proportion is unfortunately unknown, due to a significant amount of missing data on this topic. Since asthenia can worsen neuropsychological alterations, all the conditions that can worsen asthenia have to be treated as much as possible.

Few patients (28% and 36% respectively in the groups) are treated with GH despite recommendations [17]. The low proportion of patients treated with growth hormone can be explained by the discontinuation of supplementation due to tumor progression and low patient adherence to this restrictive treatment. The literature shows that GH treatment in adults improves quality of life and fatigue [18, 19]. The literature also reports the benefits of GH treatment on cognition in deficient patients [20]. In our study, we did not find any association between neuropsychological evaluation and GH substitution but our study may lack statistical power to detect small associations. Weekly injections, recently launched on the market, could help increase the percentage of patients receiving replacement therapy.

Fatigue is a symptom that is often found in the literature in this population. A case-control study conducted on 29 adult patients showed a decrease in quality of life using validated questionnaires, including the SF-36, with an impact mainly on general fatigue, physical fatigue, and energy. The following were found to be independent predictors of impaired quality of life: the presence of visual disturbances, female gender, repeated surgeries and the use of radiation therapy [21].

Overall, the mnemonic component is less affected than the executive component. However, for all cognitive functions tested, the rate of pathological results is much higher than the one found in the general population. Indeed, in reference populations according to age and socio-educational level, less than 5% of the results of these tests are abnormal, since the threshold determining pathological status corresponds to the 5th percentile on the raw value.

These results have to be faced to the high prevalence of unemployed patients. Therefore, we find a partial or total disability rate of 50% that is much higher than in the general population. Indeed, according to INSEE (institut national de la statistique et des études économiques), the rate of people in the general population who are limited in their activities due to a health problem is less than 15%. A study based on a German national cohort compared 59 patients who had undergone surgery for craniopharyngioma in childhood, with an average age of 25, to patients with type 1 diabetes. Patients with craniopharyngioma were significantly more likely to live with their parents, were less likely to be in a relationship, were less well integrated socially and professionally, and were more likely to suffer from depression [22].

The hypothalamus is not only a regulator of autonomic and endocrine processes, but also a critical relay structure within the limbic system. Through projections via the mammillothalamic tract and reciprocal connections with the anterior thalamic nuclei, it participates in the Papez circuit, which is closely linked to memory, learning, and executive functions. The hypothalamus also connects to the prefrontal cortex—particularly the orbitofrontal and medial prefrontal areas—through thalamic relays and monoaminergic pathways. These circuits integrate motivational states, emotional regulation, and goal-directed behavior, functions that are central to frontal lobe activity [23]. Neuroimaging studies and neuropsychological assessments in long-term survivors of craniopharyngioma confirm this interaction: structural damage to hypothalamic nuclei and surrounding white matter tracts correlates with poorer outcomes in memory and executive domains.

Recognition of this network-based vulnerability underscores the importance of hypothalamic-sparing treatment approaches and long-term neurocognitive follow-up [17]. Our series is an old one in which the surgical approaches used primarily involved the frontal lobe or a transsphenoidal route, which may have contributed to the deficits observed. 80% of patients suffering from craniopharyngiomas in our neurosurgery department now benefit from an extended transtubercular endonasal approach. It will be interesting to conduct a neurocognitive assessment of patients who have undergone this extended endonasal transtubercular resection, which helps limit damage to brain structures involved in cognition. Of note, we observed higher time during TMTA in patients who have multiple surgery compared with those who had only one although this association was attenuated after adjustment for covariates.

Most studies on the neuropsychological impact of craniopharyngioma treatment are pediatric studies or studies of patients whose craniopharyngioma was diagnosed in childhood. They highlight increased distractibility and inattention, decreased processing speed, behavioral disorders, and difficulties managing emotions, leading to poor academic performance [9, 10, 24, 25]. A systematic review by Özyurt et al. confirms impaired memory (episodic and working) as well as impaired executive functions, particularly attention and cognitive speed [26]. In 2013, Fjalldal et al. described a cohort of 42 adults with craniopharyngiomas that appeared in childhood and who had memory deficits, attention disorders, and processing speed disorders, particularly in individuals with hypothalamic involvement, which leads to higher rates of unemployment, disability pensions, and sick leave. However, patients reported the same quality of life as controls [7].

Our study provides long-term data on neurocognitive outcomes in adult patients treated for craniopharyngioma, an area where evidence remains scarce. In addition, we were also able to report data from patients whom craniopharyngioma has been diagnosed during adulthood that is less investigated.

The first studies on the neuropsychological impact of craniopharyngioma treatment in adults only assessed the short-term impact. Honegger et al. studied the cognitive impact in 13 adult patients, three months after craniopharyngioma resection, using a battery of neuropsychological tests that revealed a decrease in processing speed and concentration difficulties that interfered with their professional activity [27]. In 1998, Donnet et al. evaluated 22 adult patients one year after surgery for craniopharyngioma using a set of neuropsychological tests and found frontal and memory neuropsychological impairment, particularly in patients who had undergone frontotemporal surgery, but there was a bias because patients who had undergone rhinoseptal surgery had better-defined and smaller tumors with a better prognosis. A quarter of the patients had not been able to resume their previous activity [28].

In 2020, Giese et al. evaluated 36 adult patients six years after their craniopharyngioma resection. They administered a battery of standardized tests (verbal learning and memory test [VLMT], visuospatial construction ability and visual memory (Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure Test [ROCF]), intrinsic and phasic alertness (“Testbatterie für Aufmerksamkeitsprüfung” [Test Battery for Attention, TAP-Alertness], as well as cognitive speed and flexibility (Trail Making Tests A and B, [TMT-A, TMT-B]) with a neuropsychologist, who identified neuropsychological impairment in 75% of patients, particularly in the areas of attention, cognitive speed, and working memory. Inhibition and disorganization were not assessed.

Quality of life, assessed using the SF-36 questionnaire, was reduced in 75% of patients. Some risk factors were identified, such as tumor volume greater than 9 cm³, tumor extension toward/into the third ventricle or brainstem, and resection via the bifrontal translamina terminalis or left-side approach. However, fatigue, which is highly prevalent in our study, was not investigated, nor were anxiety and depression. In addition, no specific treatment was offered after the results of these assessments [29].

Our study has several strengths. It represents one of the largest cohorts of adult craniopharyngioma patients with a neuropsychological assessment and provides long-term follow-up data, with a mean interval of more than 10 years since treatment. All neuropsychological tests were administered by a neuropsychologist rather than self-administered questionnaires, whose results are less objective and biased by patients’ perceptions, minimization of symptoms, or deliberate response orientation. In addition, the battery of tests chosen allows for a comprehensive and systematic assessment of a wide range of cognitive functions. These tests are commonly used in clinical practice and in the literature and standardized according to gender, age, and socio-educational level, thus eliminating the need for comparison with a control group of healthy subjects. However, some limitations must be acknowledged. The study was monocentric and based on patients followed in an expert center, which may limit generalizability. Selection bias cannot be excluded, as patients who declined or were lost to follow-up may differ systematically from those who were evaluated. The relatively small sample size, inherent to this rare disease, also limits statistical power. Finally, the cross-sectional design precludes assessment of cognitive trajectories over time.

In conclusion, our study suggests that, similarly to previous studies conducted in children with craniopharyngiomas, adult patients treated for craniopharyngioma remain at high risk of long-term neuropsychological impairment, affecting multiple cognitive domains as well as emotional regulation and quality of life. These findings highlight the importance of systematic neuropsychological assessment in long-term follow-up, as recommended by recent guidelines. Early referral for supportive interventions may mitigate the burden of these sequelae and should be integrated into multidisciplinary care [13–15]. Future studies should explore longitudinal cognitive trajectories and the impact of rehabilitative interventions.

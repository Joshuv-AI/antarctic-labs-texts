Abstract
. In 1987, Albert Schwarz suggested a formula which extends the super Mumford form from the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces into the super Sato Grassmannian. His formula is a remarkably simple combination of super tau functions. We compute the Neveu-Schwarz action on super tau functions, and show that Schwarz’s extended Mumford form is invariant under the the super Heisenberg-Neveu-Schwarz action, which strengthens Schwarz’s proposal that a locus within the Grassmannian can serve as a universal moduli space with applications to superstring theory. Along the way, we construct the Neveu-Schwarz, super Witt, and super Heisenberg formal groups.

Introduction

The super Mumford form
μ
is the trivializing section given by the image of
1
under the canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{3/2}\otimes\lambda_{1/2}^{-5},

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\mapsto μ,

known as the super Mumford isomorphism, which identifies the structure sheaf with a product of Berezinian line bundles over the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
of
N=1
super Riemann surfaces (SRSs) of genus
g
. This super Mumford isomorphism is a generalization of the Mumford isomorphism over the moduli space of Riemann surfaces proved by
[
Mum77
]
using the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem. The proof of the super Mumford isomorphism
[
Del88
,
Vor88
]
provided not only an explicit formula for the super Mumford form, but also showed that the super Mumford isomorphism is canonical, a property lacking for the classical isomorphism.

Prompted by the result of
[
BK86
]
that the Polyakov measure in string theory has a simple explicit relation to the classical Mumford form, algebro-geometric or holomorphic methods became of great interest to computations in (super)string theory. Applying algebro-geometric methods to integrals over the moduli space of SRSs, seen as the ultimate goal, puts the super Mumford form in a place of particular importance. The precise details of the relationship between the superstring measure and the super Mumford form can be found in
[
Wit19
]
.

After a relatively dormant period of some 15 years, D’Hoker and Phong made a breakthrough computation of the amplitudes for the supermoduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
of genus-two super Riemann surfaces
[
DP
,
DP08
,
Wit15
]
as well as proposed a computation-friendly expression for the super Mumford form on
\mathfrak{M}_{3}

[
DPa
]
, which resulted in partial computation of the amplitudes
[
GM13
]
. D’Hoker-Phong’s computations were based on ‘‘splitting’’ the supermoduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
into the underlying moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and vector-bundle data on it, and then identifying the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{2}
of Riemann surfaces with the moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{2}
of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension 2. Eyeing possible extension of D’Hoker and Phong’s results to higher genera, Donagi and Witten
[
DW15
]
showed that the supermoduli space cannot be split. Moreover, since for higher
g
the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is described as a subspace of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
via complicated equations (see Shiota’s solution
[
Shi86
]
of the Schottky problem and also Farkas-Grushevsky-Salvati Manni
[
FGS21
]
), a direct generalization of D’Hoker-Phong’s computations to higher genera seems to be out of reach, at least for the time being. There is a modular-form ansatz
[
Gru09
]
for the odd component of the super Mumford form in arbitrary genus, based on a certain splitting assumption for the super Mumford form and subject to verification of physical constraints, such as the vanishing of the cosmological constant.

On the other hand, the moduli space of SRSs admits an embedding into the super Sato Grassmannian. In the seminal paper
[
Man86
]
, Manin conjectured that the moduli space of curves is an orbit of the Virasoro algebra action on the classical Grassmannian, and that a similar statement holds in the super case. The proof of this conjecture in the super case can be found in
[
Max22
]
. The morphism which embeds supermoduli space in the Grassmannian is known as the super Krichever map
[
MR91
]
.

The embedding of moduli space via the Krichever map lead to proposals that the Sato Grassmannian, or some locus within, is a universal moduli space containing curves of every genus, and similarly in the super case. See for example
[
Mor87
,
Sch98
]
. Besides the promise that the Sato Grassmannian contains the moduli space in a
\CJK@punctchar
\CJK@uniPunct
0"80"9Cuniversal
\CJK@punctchar
\CJK@uniPunct
0"80"9D way, the relatively simple, although infinite, coordinates of the Grassmannian may provide an accessible setting to perform computations. This idea is supported by fact that Shiota’s solution (based on Mulase’s work
[
Mul84
]
) to the Schottky problem relies on characterizing the moduli space locus in the Sato Grassmannian via the KP flow. For the super Schottky problem, Mulase described an analogous solution
[
Mul91
]
using the super KP flow.

This paper focuses on the formula suggested by Albert Schwarz
[
Sch87
,
Sch89
]
defined using the super KP flow, which is a formula for an extended Mumford form over a certain locus within the super Sato Grassmannian. This locus, referred to as Schwarz’s locus, may serve as a universal moduli space as it contains the image of the super moduli space of SRSs under the Krichever map. Schwarz’s extended Mumford form is defined as

\displaystyle M(L)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{{\tau}_{L}(g^{3})}{{\tau}^{3}_{L}(g)},

\displaystyle\text{for any $g$ such that }gL=L^{\perp},

where
\tau_{L}(g)
is a super tau function, defined for a point
L
in the Grassmannian and an element
g
of the super Heisenberg group, which generates the super KP flow. Some properties of these super tau functions were studied in
[
DS90
]
.
It is remarkable that the formula is so simple, and also that, as Schwarz noted, a similar formula in the bosonic (classical) case would not make sense, as it would be divergent.

The goal of this paper is to show the invariance of Schwarz’s formula under the Neveu-Schwarz (NS) action on the Grassmannian,
Theorem

14.6
.
By restricting to the supermoduli space orbit, this gives a short verification of Schwarz’s claim that his formula agrees with the super Mumford form on supermoduli space up to a constant factor. We also give a different proof of the agreement, which removes the constant factor from the claim,
Theorem

15.3
. We also present an explicit construction
Theorem

15.2
of the super Mumford isomorphism
[
Vor88
]
which uses a rational section of the relative dualizing sheaf and thereby does not require the existence of a holomorphic section.

In order to consider the NS action in relation to the super KP flows, we must consider the NS group. However, it is known that the complex NS group does not exist for the same reason that the complex Virasoro group does not exist. Instead, we define the NS, super Witt, super Heisenberg
formal
groups, inspired by their bosonic counterparts
[
ÁMP98
,
MP01
]
, and these supergroups suffice to act on the super Sato Grassmannian and describe Schwarz’s super tau function. These formal supergroups are of interest in their own right, perhaps in relation to super diffeomorphism groups or super universal Teichmüller space.

1.
The superspace of formal Laurent series

Recall from

]
(see also

parametrizes discrete supersubspaces of

H_{j/2}\linebreak\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\linebreak\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)[ζ]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j},

the space of

This definition differs from the approach of Schwarz, which is to identify

-differentials with functions by omitting the

The parity change operator

is odd.
These spaces admit natural multiplications

(1)

\displaystyle H_{i/2}\otimes H_{j/2}

\displaystyle\to H_{(i+j)/2},

\displaystyle g(z|ζ)\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes i}\otimes f(z|ζ)\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}

\displaystyle\mapsto\pm g(z|ζ)\cdot f(z|ζ)\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes(i+j)},

where the sign is given by the Koszul rule, which makes

H_{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}H_{j/2}

a
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
-graded supercommutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with

having parity 1 and degree
1/2
. The commutation relation involves only parity.

The space

has a ‘‘semi-infinite polarization,’’ that is to say, decomposes into the direct sum

H_{j/2}=H^{-}_{j/2}\oplus H^{+}_{j/2},

where

\displaystyle H^{-}_{j/2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}z^{-1}\mathbb{C}[z^{-1}|ζ]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j},

\displaystyle H^{+}_{j/2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{C}[\![z]\!][ζ]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}.

The space
H^{+}_{j/2}
will play the role of a
distinguished
compact subspace
of
H_{j/2}
, whereas
H^{-}_{j/2}
will serve as a

distinguished discrete subspace
, making a basepoint of

{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
. However, the super Sato Grassmannian breaks into more
connected components, which will be labeled by virtual dimension,
whence it will be useful to introduce a supply of standard compact and
discrete subspaces for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, as follows.

(2)

\displaystyle\left(H^{m|n}_{j/2}\right)^{-}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}z^{m-1}\mathbb{C}[z^{-1}]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}\oplus z^{n-1}ζ\mathbb{C}[z^{-1}]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}&j\text{ even}\\

\end{cases},

\displaystyle\left(H^{m|n}_{j/2}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}z^{m}\mathbb{C}[\![z]\!]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}\oplus z^{n}ζ\mathbb{C}[\![z]\!]\;[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}&j\text{ even}\\

\end{cases}.

Note that the distinguished subspaces correspond to the
0|0
index as

\displaystyle\left(H^{0|0}_{j/2}\right)^{-}

\displaystyle=H^{-}_{j/2},

\displaystyle\left(H^{0|0}_{j/2}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle=H^{+}_{j/2}.

2.
The super Heisenberg group

We would also like to speak about Lie supergroup actions, for which the language of functors of points will be convenient, if not necessary.

There will be two formal supergroups under consideration. Let us start with the simpler one, the super Heisenberg group
Γ_{\bullet}
, which is roughly a certain supergroup of ‘‘invertible’’ elements of
H_{\bullet}
. A more precise definition via the functor of points may be given by generalizing the definition of the classical formal Heisenberg group from
[
ÁMP98
, Section 4]
.
For motivational purposes, let us recall the following result from that paper:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
ÁMP98
, Corollary 4.7]
)
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and
f(z)=\sum_{n}a_{n}z^{n}\in R(\!(z)\!)
.
Then
f(z)
is (multiplicatively) invertible if and only if there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

a_{n}\in\sqrt{R}
for all
n<N
and
a_{N}
is invertible.

Here
\sqrt{R}
is the nilradical, the set of nilpotent elements of
R
. A similar statement works in the super case, as we make it clear in the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(super Heisenberg group
Γ_{\bullet}
)
.

Define a functor

Γ_{\bullet}\colon\thinspace\operatorname{\mathbf{SSch}}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Group}}

of even invertible elements of
H_{\bullet}
. More explicitly, for each
j,N\in\mathbb{Z}
,
given a
\mathbb{C}
-superscheme
S
with
R=Γ(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
, define the set of
S
-points of
Γ_{j/2}^{N}
as

\displaystyle Γ_{j/2}^{N}(S)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Big\{f(z|ζ)[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}\in H_{j/2}(S),\text{ invertible in }H_{\bullet}(S)\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{multlined}\Big\{\sum_{n\geq-M}(a_{n}+\alpha_{n}ζ)z^{n}[dz|dζ]^{\otimes j}\in(H_{j/2}\mathrel{\widehat{\otimes}}R)_{\overline{j}}\mathrel{\Big|}\\
\text{for some }M\in\mathbb{Z},a_{N}\in R_{0}^{\times},a_{n}\in\sqrt{R_{0}}\text{ for $n<N$}\Big\},\end{multlined}

where

is the set of invertible even elements of

. The condition of total parity

of these formal Laurent series is equivalent to the condition

is obviously isomorphic to the product
Γ_{0/2}^{0}(S)\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Proposition 2.3
.

The functor

\displaystyle Γ_{\bullet}\colon\thinspace\operatorname{\mathbf{SSch}}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Group}},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto Γ_{\bullet}(S),

is representable by a formal group superscheme, also denoted by
Γ_{\bullet}
. This supergroup is abelian, as it represents a functor to the subcategory of abelian groups in
\operatorname{\mathbf{Group}}
.

Proof.

The argument of
[
ÁMP98
, Theorem 4.10]
can be easily generalized to the super case. We will show the representability of the functor
Γ_{0/2}^{0}
and identify
Γ_{\bullet}
as
Γ_{0/2}^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

We start with constructing a formal superscheme and will then prove that it represents the functor.

Consider the algebra
\mathbb{C}[\dots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\dots|\dots,\xi_{-1},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]
of polynomials in infinitely many even (
x_{i}
) and odd (
\xi_{i}
) variables. For each
M,p\geq 1
, let

(5)

I_{M,p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x_{-1},\dots,x_{-M})^{p}+(x_{-M-1},x_{-M-2},\dots\,|\,\xi_{-M-1},\xi_{-M-2},\dots)

be the ideal generated by the variables
x_{-M-1},\linebreak x_{-M-2},\dots
and
\xi_{-M-1},\xi_{-M-2},\dots
and the

. Introduce a linear topology on
\mathbb{C}[\dots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\dots|\dots,\xi_{-1},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]
by taking the ideal

as a base of the open neighborhoods of 0. Consider the completion of the polynomial algebra in this topology. This version of the algebra of formal power series in infinitely many variables may also be given by the limit

(6)

\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots|\;\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]\{\!\{x_{-1},x_{-2},\dots|\;\xi_{-1},\xi_{-2},\dots\}\!\}\\
\begin{aligned} &\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varprojlim_{M,p}\mathbb{C}[\dots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\dots|\dots,\xi_{-1},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]/I_{M,p}\\

The last property implies that
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots|\;\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]\{\!\{x_{-1},x_{-2},\dots|\;\xi_{-1},\xi_{-2},\dots\}\!\}
is an admissible topological ring. The same is true about its localization at

A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{0}^{-1},x_{1},x_{2},\dots|\;\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots]\{\!\{x_{-1},x_{-2},\dots|\;\xi_{-1},\xi_{-2},\dots\}\!\}

. The formal group superscheme structure on

To prove the claim, observe that, for a complex superscheme
S
with the algebra of regular functions

\Hom_{\operatorname{\mathbf{fSSch}}}(S,\Spf A)=\Hom_{\operatorname{\mathbf{top-\mathbb{C}-sAlg}}}(A,R),

where
\operatorname{\mathbf{fSSch}}

\operatorname{\mathbf{top-\mathbb{C}-sAlg}}

A continuous algebra homomorphism

is equivalent to a collection of even elements
\dots,a_{-1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(x_{-1}),a_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(x_{0}),a_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(x_{1}),\dots

\dots,\alpha_{-1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(\xi_{-1}),\alpha_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(\xi_{0}),\alpha_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(\xi_{1}),\dots

. The latter is equivalent to the condition that

3.
The super Witt group

Next, we consider the super Witt group
\SWitt
and its central extension the Neveu-Schwarz supergroup
{\mathup{NS}}
. Compared with the super Heisenberg group, the super Witt group consisting of superconformal automorphisms of the formal deleted superdisk
\Spf\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)[ζ]
is subtler and, in particular, non-abelian. Even its bosonic prototype, the Witt group, whose central extension is called the Virasoro group, seems to contradict the famous observation of Pressley and Segal
[
PS86
]
that the Virasoro group does not exist. A remarkable achievement of
[
ÁMP98
]
is the construction of the Virasoro group as a
formal
group scheme. This does not contradict the statement that the Virasoro group does not exist as a smooth Lie group. Informally, the formal Virasoro group is a formal extension of Segal’s semigroup of annuli
[
Seg04
]
in the ‘‘negative’’ direction.

Definition 3.1

(The super Witt group
\SWitt
)
.

Define a functor

\displaystyle\SWitt\colon\thinspace\operatorname{\mathbf{SSch}}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Group}},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto\Aut^{s}_{R\operatorname{-Alg}}R(\!(z)\!)[ζ],

of superconformal automorphisms of the topological
R
-algebra
R(\!(z)\!)[ζ]=H_{0/2}\widehat{\otimes}R
, with the group law given by composition and
R=Γ(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
. By a
superconformal automorphism
we mean an
R
-algebra automorphism
\varphi
that preserves the odd distribution generated by
D_{ζ}=\frac{\partial}{\partial ζ}+ζ\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
, i.e.,
\varphi^{*}D_{ζ}=FD_{ζ}
for some
F\in R(\!(z)\!)[ζ]
. An automorphism of a topological algebra is assumed to be a homeomorphism, i.e., to be continuous with a continuous inverse. The
continuity
of
\varphi\in\Aut^{s}_{R\operatorname{-Alg}}R(\!(z)\!)[ζ]
in the
z
-adic topology means that for each
M\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists an
N\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\varphi(z^{N}H_{0/2}^{+}\widehat{\otimes}R)\subset z^{M}H_{0/2}^{+}\widehat{\otimes}R
.

More explicitly, an automorphism is determined by the images of
z
and
ζ
:

(7)

\begin{split}\widehat{z}&=u(z)+ζω(z),\\
\widehat{ζ}&=η(z)+ζv(z),\end{split}

and the superconformality condition may be easily shown
[
Wit19
, Section 2.1.1]
to be equivalent to the equation

(8)

D_{ζ}\widehat{z}=\widehat{ζ}D_{ζ}\widehat{ζ},

which may be rewritten in components as the system

(9)

\begin{split}ω&=ηv,\\
u^{\prime}&=v^{2}-ηη^{\prime}.\end{split}

To represent the functor
\SWitt
, we will relate it to the functor

\displaystyle\widetilde{Γ^{1}_{0/2}}\colon\thinspace\operatorname{\mathbf{SSch}}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Set}},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\mapsto\{(u+ζη,v)\in Γ^{1}_{0/2}(S)\times R_{0}(\!(z)\!)\;|\;v^{2}=u^{\prime}+ηη^{\prime}\},

where
u=u(z)\in R_{0}(\!(z)\!)
and
η=η(z)\in R_{1}(\!(z)\!)
and
u^{\prime}
and
η^{\prime}
denote derivatives in
z
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The map

\displaystyle\Aut^{s}_{R\operatorname{-Alg}}R(\!(z)\!)[ζ]

\displaystyle\to R(\!(z)\!)[ζ]_{0}\times R(\!(z)\!)_{0},

\displaystyle(\widehat{z},\widehat{ζ})

\displaystyle\mapsto(u+ζη,v),

induces a natural isomorphism of functors

(10)

\psi_{S}\colon\thinspace\SWitt(S)\to\widetilde{Γ^{1}_{0/2}}(S),

regarded as functors

\operatorname{\mathbf{SSch}}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{\mathbf{Set}}.

Proof.

First off, we need to see that the pair
(u+ζη,v)
is in
\widetilde{Γ^{1}_{0/2}}(S)
. We know Equation (
9
) is satisfied for
u,v
, and

g(s)=\left(\sum a_{K}z^{K}+a_{K+1}z^{K+1}+\dots\right)[dz|dζ]^{i}

with
K\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a_{K}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and
a_{K+1},a_{K+2},\ldots\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore,
g(s)z^{N}
has leading order
N+K
.
By Nakayama’s lemma, there is a neighborhood
V
of
s
over which

\big\{z^{M},\ldots,z^{(N+K-1)}\mathop{\big|}ζz^{M},\ldots,ζz^{(N+K-1)}\big\}

generate the quotient
z^{M}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{V}\big/gz^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{V}
. Since these elements represent a basis of the quotient space at the point
s
, they will also be linearly independent in the neighborhood. Thus, the quotient will be free of finite type over this neighborhood.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

The action (
13
) of the formal super Witt group
\SWitt
on
H_{\bullet}
induces an action on the super Sato Grassmannian
{\mathup{Gr}}_{\bullet}
, preserving the degree of the differentials:

\displaystyle\SWitt\times{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}\to{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}.

Proof.

An
S
-point
\varphi
of the super Witt group is an automorphism of the graded
R=H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
-algebra
H_{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}R
and, in particular, of the
R
-module
H_{j/2}\widehat{\otimes}R
. It induces an automorphism of the trivial vector bundle
\widehat{(H_{j/2})}_{S}
. If
L\subset\widehat{(H_{j/2})}_{S}
represents an
S
-point of
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
, then in a small enough neighborhood
U
on
S
, there is an
N\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
L_{U}\cap z^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U}
is locally free of finite type and
L_{U}+z^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U}=(\widehat{H_{j/2}})_{U}
. Then
\varphi(L_{U})\cap\varphi(z^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U})
will also be locally free of finite type and
\varphi(L_{U})+\varphi(z^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U})
will still be the whole
(\widehat{H_{j/2}})_{U}
. By the continuity of
\varphi
, we can find

such that
\varphi(z^{N}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U})\subset z^{M}\widehat{(H^{+}_{j/2})}_{U}
. The fact that the quotient is locally free of finite rank may be shown as in the proof of the previous proposition. Therefore,
\varphi L
will also represent an
S
-point of
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
.
Varying
S
, we get a natural transformation:

(\SWitt\times{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2})(S)\to{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}(S),

which gives the required action of
\SWitt
on
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
.
∎

The previous two propositions combine into an action of the semidirect product
Γ_{\bullet}\rtimes\SWitt
on the super Sato Grassmannian
{\mathup{Gr}}_{\bullet}
, because the actions on the Grassmannian are induced by the actions on
H_{\bullet}
and the action of
Γ_{\bullet}
on
H_{\bullet}
is
\SWitt
-equivariant by
Lemma

4.2
.

Corollary 6.3
.

The actions of
Proposition

6.1
and

induce an action of the semidirect product
Γ_{\bullet}\rtimes\SWitt
on the super Sato Grassmannian

.

7.
The Berezinian line bundle

We recall here the definition of the Berezinian line bundle on the super Sato Grassmannian, see
[
Max22
]
.
We will later describe actions on the Berezinian line bundles and naturally arrive at the construction of the formal Neveu-Schwarz group, following the ideas of
[
MP01
]
in the classical, Virasoro case.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the
tautological sheaf
over
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
. This is the universal
\mathcal{O}_{\mathup{Gr}}
-module corresponding to the identity
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
-point
\id\colon\thinspace{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}\to{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
of
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}

Definition 7.1
.

The
Berezinian line bundle

\Ber=\Ber_{j/2}
on
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}
is
the Berezinian of the perfect complex defined by the
addition morphism:

\displaystyle\Ber_{j/2}(\mathcal{L})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Ber\Big(0\to\mathcal{L}\oplus\widehat{\left(H^{+}_{j/2}\right)}_{\mathup{Gr}}\to\widehat{(H_{j/2})}_{\mathup{Gr}}\to 0\Big).

Note that for the classical Sato Grassmannian (and finite classical
Grassmannians) the determinant line bundle has no global sections,
while the dual determinant line bundle does have global sections;
however, for the super Sato Grassmannian (and finite super
Grassmannians
[
Man88a
, Chapter 4 Section 3]
), neither the Berezinian nor the dual Berezinian line
bundles have global sections
[
BR99
, Section 3.1]
.

Later, when considering group actions on the Berezinian line bundle, it becomes necessary to consider the Berezinian line bundles defined using the parity reversed perfect complex. Considering
\mathcal{L}
over
{\mathup{Gr}}_{j/2}(m|n)
, then the parity reversed perfect complex is canonically isomorphic to the dual of the usual Berezinian line bundle as:

(16)

\displaystyle\Ber\Big(0\to Π^{j}\mathcal{L}\oplus Π^{j}H^{+}_{j/2}\to Π^{j}H_{j/2}\to 0\Big)=Π^{m-n}\Ber^{*}_{H_{j/2}^{+}}(\mathcal{L}).

This isomorphism is due to the fact that
\Ber ΠV=Π^{m-n}(\Ber V)^{-1}
for a super vector space

of finite dimension
(m|n)
or, more generally, a perfect complex of Euler characteristic
(m|n)
.

9.2.
The associated group cocycle

Consider

\displaystyle C^{m|n}_{j/2}(χ,ξ)

9.4.
Properties of the group cocycle

Theorem 9.1
.

The action
Γ_{\bullet}\times{\mathup{Gr}}_{\bullet}\to{\mathup{Gr}}_{\bullet}
in
Proposition

6.1
lifts to an action on the Berezinian line bundle.

Proof.

We show this result by first showing that the group cocycle
C^{m|n}_{j/2}
restricted to the group
Γ_{\bullet}
is trivial.

As stated in
26
, the Lie algebraic cocycle
c^{m|n}_{j/2}
restricted to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
is trivial:

\displaystyle c^{m|n}_{j/2}(F,G)=0,

\displaystyle F,G\in\mathfrak{h}.

From this fact and the relationship between the group and Lie algebra cocycles stated in
25
, we claim that
C^{m|n}_{j/2}(f,g)=1
for
f,g\in Γ^{0}_{0/2}
. This can be seen by first noticing that in fact the Lie algebra cocycle is proportional to the first order approximation to a single group cocycle not just the difference of cocycles, and then noticing that
\str(X^{-+}Y^{+-})=0
implies all the higher terms vanish in the expansion of the group cocycle beyond order
1
in
εδ
.

We showed in
Lemma

8.2
that the shift operators
Z^{A}_{i/2}
act on the Berezinian line bundle directly. This direct action is reflected in the group cocycle formula
24
in that the block components of the shift operators
\left(Z^{A}_{i/2}\right)^{-+}=\left(Z^{A}_{i/2}\right)^{-+}=0
, which implies that
C^{m|n}_{j/2}\left(χ,Z^{A}_{i/2}\right)=C^{m|n}_{j/2}\left(Z^{A}_{i/2},χ\right)=1
for any
χ\in Γ_{\bullet}\rtimes\SWitt
.

Therefore it is only left to show that
C^{m|n}_{j/2}\left(fZ^{A}_{i/2},gZ^{B}_{k/2}\right)=1
for any
f,g\in Γ^{0}_{0/2}
and
A,B,i,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. This follows from two applications of the group cocycle condition:

\displaystyle C\left(fZ^{A}_{i/2},gZ^{B}_{k/2}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(Z^{A}_{i/2},gZ^{B}_{k/2}\right)\left(C\left(f,Z^{A}_{i/2}\right)\right)^{-1}C\left(f,gZ^{A+B}_{(i+k)/2}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(f,gZ^{A+B}_{(i+k)/2}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(fg,Z^{A+B}_{(i+k)/2}\right)\left(C\left(g,Z^{A+B}_{(i+k)/2}\right)\right)^{-1}C\left(f,g\right)

\displaystyle=1.

∎

Proposition 9.2
.

\displaystyle=C^{δ}(g,φ),

for

for

δ=n-m
for

odd, and where

As stated in
27
, the Lie algebraic cocycle

has the property

\displaystyle c^{m|n}_{j/2}(X,F)

\displaystyle=c^{δ}(X,F),

\displaystyle=c^{δ}(F,X),

12.

In order to combine Schwarz’s super tau functions into an extended super Mumford form, we need to restrict to a certain locus within the super Sato Grassmannian. This locus is characterized by the duality discussed in this section.

Just as in the classical case, for the super vector space

\displaystyle\Big\langle(v_{0}+v_{1}ζ)[dz|dζ]^{j},(w_{0}+w_{1}ζ)[dz|dζ]^{k}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{j+k,1}\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{1|1}}(v_{0}+v_{1}ζ)(w_{0}+w_{1}ζ)[dz|dζ]

\displaystyle=\delta_{j+k,1}\oint_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(v_{0}w_{1}+v_{1}w_{0})dz.

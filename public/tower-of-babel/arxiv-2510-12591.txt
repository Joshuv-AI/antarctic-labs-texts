Lower Bounds for Faithful Linear Representations of Subgroups of the Mapping Class Group
Thiago Brevidelli

Abstract
. Recently, Korkmaz established the lower bound of 3 ​ g − 2 3g-2 for the dimension of a faithful representation of the mapping class group of an orientable surface of genus g ⩾ 3 g\geqslant 3 . We raise this bound to 4 ​ g − 3 4g-3 in the setting of surfaces of genus g ⩾ 7 g\geqslant 7 . A new ingredient is a finer study of the commutation relations in PMod ⁡ ( Σ ) \PMod(\Sigma) . We use the relations arising from a certain pants decomposition of Σ g \Sigma_{g} to show that any representation of dimension ⩽ 4 ​ g − 4 \leqslant 4g-4 is forced to kill a natural subgroup of the Torelli group. We also establish lower bounds for the dimension of faithful representations of related groups: the Johnson group of a closed surface, arbitrarily low terms of the Johnson filtration of a compact surface with one boundary component and pure braid groups. These lower bounds grow linearly on the genus of the surfaces and the number of strands of the braids. Finally, we also provide some evidence that greater lower bounds for the low-genus cases should lead to improved lower bounds for g ≫ 0 g\gg 0 .

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,r}^{b}

with

b

The mapping class group

\Sigma

Given a closed subsurface

2.1.
Curves & Dehn Twists

Given an unoriented simple closed curve
\alpha\subseteq\Sigma
avoiding the
marked points of
\Sigma
, we denote by

write “
a\subseteq\Sigma
”. Here our homotopies are assumed to
avoid the marked points. All curves considered in this paper are simple closed
curves, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Recall that the

between

We denote by

It is also useful consider the twists about curves parallel to the boundary
components of
\Sigma

(1)

The conjugation relation.
Given

2.3.
The Work of Korkmaz

Let
\Sigma
be a surface as above. We now describe some results due to

(Korkmaz, Theorems 1 & 2

]

Let

g\geqslant 3

, Korkmaz also

(Flag triviality criterion, Lemma 7.1
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma

)

are joined by an edge if and only if

The latter is a Gromov-hyperbolic graph on which

acts by

]
. Given the disjointness relations from

,

and

are related by means of the embedding

taking

to its Dehn twist

.

The starting point of the present article was to consider the disjointness

induced by a family curves

\displaystyle|a_{i}\pitchfork a_{j}|

\displaystyle=0\;\;\forall i\neq j

\displaystyle|b_{i}\pitchfork b_{j}|

\displaystyle=0\;\;\forall i\neq j

\displaystyle|a_{i}\pitchfork b_{j}|

\displaystyle=0\;\;\forall i\neq j

\displaystyle|a_{i}\pitchfork b_{i}|

\displaystyle\geqslant 1\;\;\forall i,

where

denotes the geometric intersection number between

and

.

These correspond to copies of the graph

from

inside

. In terms of

\displaystyle=T_{a_{j}}T_{a_{i}}\text{ for all }i,j

\displaystyle T_{b_{i}}T_{b_{j}}

\displaystyle=T_{b_{j}}T_{b_{i}}\text{ for all }i,j

\displaystyle=T_{b_{j}}T_{a_{i}}\iff i\neq j.

Combined with (

The latter was recently estimated by Kionke–Schesler

Proposition 3.1

(Kionke–Schesler, Theorem 3

]
)
.

Suppose

are non-solvable groups and

is a faithful linear
representation. Then

.

Their proof is short and elementary, making clever use of well known facts
about the representation theory of direct products. Here we make use of a
slightly more general version of their statement, although our proof is really
an adaptation of their argument.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
are non-solvable groups and
\pi:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\twoheadrightarrow H
is a surjective group homomorphism
such that
\pi\!\restriction_{H_{i}}:H_{i}\to H
is injective for all
i
.
Let
\rho:H\hookrightarrow\GL_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be a faithful linear
representation. Then
d\geqslant 2n
.

Proof.

Consider
\rho\circ\pi:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to\GL_{d}(\mathbb{C})
and
take a maximal
(H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n})
-invariant flag

0=W_{0}\leqslant W_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant W_{p+1}=\mathbb{C}^{d},

so that the action
\rho_{i}:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}\to\GL(W_{i+1}/W_{i})
of
H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}
on each successive
quotient is irreducible. In a basis adapted to this flag,

(3.3)

\rho(\pi(h))=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{1}(h)&*&\cdots&*\\
0&\rho_{2}(h)&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\rho_{p}(h)\end{pmatrix}

for all
h\in H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{n}
.

Now each
W_{i+1}/W_{i}
may be decomposed as a tensor product

W_{i+1}/W_{i}=W_{i,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_{i,n}
, where

\rho_{ij}:H_{j}\to\GL(W_{ij})
is an irreducible representation and

\rho_{i}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})=\rho_{i,1}(h_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{i,n}(h_{n})
– see, for example,
[
23

.
In particular, if
\dim W_{ij}=1
then
\rho_{i}(1,\ldots,1,h_{j},1,\ldots,1)=1\otimes\cdots 1\otimes\rho_{ij}(h_{j})\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1
is a scalar operator for all
h_{j}\in H_{j}
.

Assume we can find
j\leqslant n
with
\dim W_{ij}=1
for all
i
. Then
the matrix
\rho(\pi(1,\ldots,1,h_{j},1,\ldots,1))
is upper triangular
with respect to the basis from (

) for all

. Since the group of upper triangular matrices is solvable, it
follows

for some

is
non-solvable and so

, contradicting
the assumption that

is faithful.

This means that, for each

we can find

such that

. It follows

\begin{split}d&=\dim W_{1}/W_{0}+\cdots+\dim W_{p}/W_{p-1}\\
&\geqslant 2^{\#\{j:\dim W_{1,j}\geqslant 2\}}+\cdots+2^{\#\{j:\dim W_{p,j}\geqslant 2\}}\\
&\geqslant 2\cdot\#\{j:\dim W_{1,j}\geqslant 2\}+\cdots+2\cdot\#\{j:\dim W_{p,j}\geqslant 2\}\\

as desired.

\dim E_{1}^{a}<2g-2
and consider the

\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
-invariant flag

0\leqslant E_{1}^{a}=E_{1,1}^{a}\leqslant E_{1,2}^{a}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant E_{1,d}^{a}=\mathbb{C}^{d},

where
E_{1,k}^{a}=\ker(L_{a}-1)^{k}
as above. By the Jordan inequalities
(Lemma
4.4
),
\dim E_{1,k+1}^{a}/E_{1,k}^{a}\leqslant\dim E_{1}^{a}<2g-2
for all
k
. It thus follows from the flag triviality
criterion (Lemma
2.8
) that the restriction
of
\rho
to
\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
is trivial. But then
L_{a}\sim L_{c}=1

for any nonseparating
c\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
, contradicting the assumption

\dim E_{1}^{a}<2g-2
.

It remains to show
\dim E_{1}^{a}\neq 2g-2
. Assume by contradiction
\dim E_{1}^{a}=2g-2
and denote by
\rho_{1}:\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})\to\GL(E_{1}^{a})
and

\bar{\rho}:\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})\to\GL(\mathbb{C}^{d}/E_{1}^{a})
the actions of

\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
on
E_{1}^{a}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d}/E_{1}^{a}
, respectively. It
follows from Theorem
2.7
that
\rho_{1}
and
\bar{\rho}
are either trivial or conjugate to the symplectic representation

\Psi:\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})\to\Sp_{2g-2}(\mathbb{Z})\leqslant\GL(H_{\mathbb{C}})
, where

H_{\mathbb{C}}=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g-1};\mathbb{C})
.

We consider three separate cases.

Case 1.
Assume
\rho_{1}
is trivial.
In this case,
L_{c}\!\restriction_{E_{1}^{a}}=\rho_{1}(T_{c})=1
and thus

E_{1}^{a}\leqslant E_{1}^{c}
for all nonseparating
c\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
.
Since
L_{a}
and
L_{c}
are conjugate, this implies
E_{1}^{a}=E_{1}^{c}
.
By the same token,
E_{1}^{b}=E_{1}^{c}=E_{1}^{a}
for
b
as in
Figure
6
. Now
Lemma
4.5
implies
E_{1}^{a}=E_{1}^{b}
is

\PMod(\Sigma)
-invariant.

We abuse the notation and denote by
\rho_{1}:\PMod(\Sigma)\to\GL(E_{1}^{a})
and
\bar{\rho}:\PMod(\Sigma)\to\GL(\mathbb{C}^{d}/E_{a}^{1})

the actions of
\PMod(\Sigma)
on
E_{1}^{a}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d}/E_{a}^{1}
,
respectively. In that case,
\rho_{1}
and
\bar{\rho}
are both trivial
by Theorem
2.7
. The flag triviality criterion
(Lemma
2.8
) applied to the flag
0\leqslant E_{1}^{a}\leqslant\mathbb{C}^{d}
thus implies
\rho
is trivial, a
contradiction.

Case 2.
Assume
\rho_{1}\sim\Psi
and
\bar{\rho}
is
trivial. In this case, we can find a basis for
\mathbb{C}^{d}
under which

\rho(f)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Psi(f)&\begin{matrix}c_{1}(f)&c_{2}(f)&\cdots&c_{d-2g+2}(f)\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

for all
f\in\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
. It is not hard to check the maps
c_{k}:\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})\to E_{1}^{a}\cong H_{\mathbb{C}}
are crossed
homomorphisms.

Now Lemma
2.12
implies that, given
c\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}
nonseparating,
c_{k}(c)=\mu_{k}\cdot c
for some

\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
. By tweaking the above basis, we can find a
second basis for
\mathbb{C}^{d}
under which

L_{c}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Psi(T_{c})&\begin{matrix}\mu\cdot c&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr 0&1\end{array}\right)

for some
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence
\operatorname{codim}E_{1}^{a}=\operatorname{codim}E_{1}^{c}\leqslant 2
by (
2.2
),
a contradiction for
d>2g
.

We may thus assume
d\leqslant 2g
, in which case
Theorem
2.7
says
\rho
is either trivial or
conjugate to the symplectic representation
\Psi:\Mod(\Sigma)\to\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
. The former contradicts the hypothesis
\rho
is
nontrivial, so
\rho\sim\Psi
. But then
\dim E_{1}^{a}=2g-1
by
(
2.2
), contradicting the assumption
\dim E_{1}^{a}=2g-2
.

Case 3.
Finally, assume
\rho_{1}\sim\bar{\rho}\sim\Psi
. This last case is only possible if
d=4g-4
, which is only
relevant to our proof when
g\geqslant 7
. We thus assume
d=4g-4
and

g\geqslant 7
from now on.

We regard
\mathbb{C}^{4g-4}
as a
\mathbb{Z}[\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})]
-module, where

\mathbb{Z}[\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})]
denotes the group ring of
\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
and

f\in\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
by
\rho(f)
, as in
§
2.4
. In this case,
\mathbb{C}^{4g-4}
is an
extension of
H_{\mathbb{C}}=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g-1};\mathbb{C})
by
H_{\mathbb{C}}
. This means

\mathbb{C}^{4g-4}
fits into a short exact sequence of the form

(4.1)

On the one hand,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime})]}(H_{\mathbb{Z}},H_{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{1}(\Mod(\Sigma^{\prime});\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{\mathbb{Z}},H_{\mathbb{Z}}))

,

. This implies the sequence

for all

. Taking

for some nonseparating

, we can see

, a

Given

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem

)
.

Let

. If

then
\SIP_{0}(\Sigma_{g})\leqslant\ker\rho
. Moreover, if
g\geqslant 7
and
d=4g-4
then
\SIP_{0}(\Sigma_{g})\leqslant\ker\rho
.

Proof.

Given
a\subseteq\Sigma_{g}
, denote
L_{a}=\rho(T_{a})
and
E_{1}^{a}=\ker(L_{a}-1)
as above. Suppose by contradiction
\ker\rho
does not contain

\SIP_{0}(\Sigma)
. This means
L_{a}
and
L_{b}
do not commute for some
(and hence all) nonseparating
a,b\subseteq\Sigma
intersecting twice with

\Sigma\setminus(a\cup b)
is connected.

Take
a_{1},\ldots,a_{3g-3},b_{1},\ldots,b_{3g-g}\subseteq\Sigma
as
above. We claim that the matrices

(5.1)

\displaystyle M_{i}

\displaystyle=L_{a_{i}}-1

\displaystyle N_{j}

\displaystyle=L_{b_{j}}-1

satisfy the conditions in (
3.5
). In that case, it follows from
Proposition
3.8
that
2d\geqslant 9g-9
, a contradiction
for
d\leqslant 4g-4
. To establish the claim, notice
\Gamma_{g}\setminus U_{i}
is connected. This implies
\Sigma_{g}\setminus S_{i}
is connected.
Hence so is
\Sigma\setminus(a_{i}\cup b_{i})
. In particular,

\SIP_{0}(\Sigma)
is normally generated by
[T_{a_{i}},T_{b_{i}}]
for any

i
.

It is clear from Corollary
4.3
that
N_{j}M_{i}=M_{j}N_{i}=0
for
i\neq j
and
M_{j}M_{i}=0
for all
i,j
. On the other
hand, by assumption,
L_{a_{i}}
and
L_{b_{i}}
do not commute. Hence

M_{i}
and
N_{i}
don’t commute. In particular,
M_{i}N_{i}\neq 0
or
N_{i}M_{i}\neq 0
. But
M_{i}N_{i}=0\iff N_{i}M_{i}=0
.

Indeed, the pairs
(T_{a_{i}},T_{b_{i}})
and
(T_{b_{i}},T_{a_{i}})
are
conjugate in
\PMod(\Sigma)
: we can find

with

f(a_{i})=b_{i}
and
f(b_{i})=a_{i}
, so that
fT_{a_{i}}f^{-1}=T_{b_{i}}

and

. Hence
M_{i}N_{i}
and

are

\rho(f)\in\GL_{d}(\mathbb{C})
. We are done.

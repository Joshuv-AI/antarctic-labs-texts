Empirical antibiotic therapy for spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage-associated pneumonia: an ICU-based single-centered retrospective cohort study
Authors: Xuemei Tao, Junwei Wang, Jing Rao, Junjie Zhong, Zhou Shu, Peishan Cai, Qi Li, Fang Zeng, Jie Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPneumonia is a common and severe complication following spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (SICH) and is associated with significantly worse clinical outcomes. However, there remains insufficient evidence supporting empirical antibiotic regimens for SICH-associated pneumonia (SICHAP).ObjectiveTo evaluate the clinical efficacy of different empirical antibiotic regimens in patients with SICHAP and to provide evidence for optimizing initial antimicrobial strategies.MethodsThis single-center retrospective cohort study enrolled adult patients with SICH who developed pneumonia within 7 days of onset between July and December 2024. Patients were stratified according to their initial empirical antibiotic regimen: cefazolin (CFZ), ceftriaxone (CRO), cefoperazone-sulbactam (CFP-SUL), or piperacillin-tazobactam (PIP-TAZ). The primary outcome was the clinical efficacy rate at 72 h. The secondary outcomes included the SICHAP treatment failure rate, in-hospital mortality, length of stay, duration of mechanical ventilation, and duration of antibiotic therapy. Treatment appropriateness was assessed on the basis of sputum culture and antimicrobial susceptibility testing.ResultsA total of 85 patients (mean age 54.2 ± 13.5 years, 63.5% male) were enrolled, with 90.6% presenting with early SICHAP. The 72-hour efficacy rate in the CFP-SUL group (73.3%) was significantly greater than that in the CFZ group (25.0%), CRO group (54.5%), and PIP-TAZ group (58.8%) (p = 0.022). The overall SICHAP treatment failure rate was 55.3%, with no significant intergroup differences (p = 0.137). In the sputum culture-positive subgroup (n = 49), appropriate empirical antibiotic therapy (AEAT) significantly reduced treatment failure (58.1% vs. 94.4%, p = 0.008). Pathogens were predominantly Gram-negative (75.3%), with the most common isolates being Klebsiella pneumoniae (42.9%), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (40.8%), and Acinetobacter baumannii (22.4%). High rates of ESBL production and carbapenemase detection were observed.ConclusionAmong SICHAP patients, empirical CFP-SUL therapy demonstrated greater 72-hour clinical efficacy, and AEATs significantly reduced the risk of treatment failure. These findings suggest that CFP-SUL may be favored for early empirical antimicrobial therapy in this population, although prospective randomized controlled trials are needed to confirm these results.Clinical trialClinical trial number: not applicable.

Introduction

Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (SICH) is the most lethal subtype of stroke, with a mortality rate exceeding 40% within one month of onset [1]. Stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP), a common and severe complication of SICH, occurs in 16.8% to 24.0% of cases, significantly prolongs hospitalization, increases healthcare costs, and increases patient mortality [2–5]. Early initiation of effective empirical antibiotic therapy is crucial for improving clinical outcomes in SAP patients [5, 6].

However, existing research has focused primarily on the preventive use of antibiotics to reduce the incidence of SAP, with a lack of high-quality clinical studies addressing the treatment phase of SAP [7, 8]. Consequently, no universally accepted empirical antibiotic regimen recommendations have been established. Vargas et al. [6] reported a wide variation in antibiotics administered within 7 days post-stroke—including ampicillin, levofloxacin, cephalosporins, piperacillin-tazobactam, and vancomycin—but did not compare the efficacy of these different regimens. Harms et al. [9] surveyed 83 stroke units in Germany and similarly reported substantial variation in empirical antibiotic selection for SAP, ranging from second-generation cephalosporins to carbapenems and even combination regimens, suggesting that clinical decision-making remains largely “empirical.” In 2019, the multidisciplinary Pneumonia in Stroke Consensus Group (PISCES) proposed that empirical antibiotics for early SAP should cover typical community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) pathogens; for late SAP, coverage should additionally include Enterobacteriaceae and, when risk factors are present, Pseudomonas aeruginosa [7]. However, the consensus also noted that no randomized trials guide antibiotic treatment for SAP and that insufficient evidence supports the recommendation of any specific antibiotic agent or class. Current recommendations are derived primarily from the extrapolation of SAP microbiological pathogen data and general pneumonia treatment. Importantly, the studies underpinning this consensus primarily involved patients with ischemic or mixed stroke types [10, 11]. Given the substantial differences in mechanisms, symptoms, and prognoses between ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke, it remains unclear whether these differences influence SAP treatment [10]. Therefore, clinical studies evaluating the efficacy of different empirical antibiotic strategies for treating SICH-associated pneumonia (SICHAP) are highly valuable.

Thus, this study aims to systematically compare the clinical efficacy of different empirical antibiotic regimens in patients with SICHAP through a retrospective cohort study, thereby providing evidence-based guidance for optimizing initial antimicrobial treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study from July to December 2024 in the Department of Neurosurgery at the Chegu Campus of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. The study adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and appropriate clinical practice guidelines. The research protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Ethics No. [2025]0781). Informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) diagnosis of SICH confirmed by cranial CT or MRI; and (3) diagnosis of pneumonia on the basis of the modified Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) criteria [10]. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) hospital admission more than 7 days after SICH onset; (2) preexisting pneumonia prior to SICH; (3) concurrent infection at other sites; (4) duration of antibiotic therapy < 72 h; and (5) incomplete clinical data.

Data collection and definitions

Patient demographics, underlying conditions, and severity indicators at admission and at SICHAP onset were collected via the electronic medical record system. Concurrently recorded were antibiotic regimens, sputum culture and susceptibility results, clinical response to initial empirical therapy within 72 h, duration of mechanical ventilation, length of intensive care unit (ICU) stay, length of hospital stay, and SICHAP outcomes.

The burden of comorbidities was assessed via the Charlson comorbidity index (CCI), and disease severity was assessed via the acute physiology and chronic health evaluation (APACHE) II score. SICHAP was classified on the basis of the time from SICH onset to diagnosis as early SICHAP (< 72 h), late SICHAP (≥ 72 h and ≤ 7 days), or hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP, > 7 days) [7]. All patients underwent sputum sample collection for pathogen culture after SICHAP diagnosis, and empirical antibiotic therapy was initiated immediately. Antibiotic regimens were subsequently adjusted on the basis of the clinical response and final antimicrobial susceptibility results [12]. Empirical antibiotic therapy was defined as antibiotic administration prior to the availability of susceptibility results [13]. If the detected pathogen was susceptible to at least one of the administered antibiotics, it was considered appropriate empirical antibiotic therapy (AEAT); otherwise, it was deemed inappropriate empirical antibiotic therapy (IEAT) [12]. If bacteria are insensitive, empirical antibiotic therapy is considered inappropriate (IEAT). An immunocompromised status is defined as patients who are receiving chemotherapy or high-dose steroid therapy or those with primary immunodeficiency disorders [13].

Outcome measures

The primary outcome measure is the efficacy rate at 72 h after the initiation of empirical antibiotic therapy [14]. Clinical stability achieved after treatment is defined as an effective initial therapy. Initial therapy failure is defined as no improvement in symptoms requiring changes in antimicrobial agents or initial improvement followed by worsening symptoms and disease progression. The secondary outcome measures included the in-hospital SICHAP treatment failure rate, in-hospital mortality, duration of mechanical ventilation, duration of antibiotic therapy, and length of stay. In-hospital SICHAP treatment failure was defined as persistent or worsening SICHAP signs and symptoms at discharge requiring additional antibiotics; death from SICHAP within 3 days of antimicrobial therapy initiation; or the development of SICHAP-related complications such as lung abscess or empyema [15].

Microbiological methods

Sputum samples were processed following standard microbiological procedures. Pathogen identification and antimicrobial susceptibility testing were performed using the VITEK® 2 Compact system (bioMérieux, France) or matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry coupled with broth microdilution, in accordance with Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute guidelines. Phenotypic confirmation of extended-spectrum β-lactamase (ESBL) production in Enterobacteriaceae was performed using the combined disk test. Carbapenemase production in Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Acinetobacter baumannii was screened using the modified carbapenem inactivation method and EDTA-modified carbapenem inactivation method as recommended.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed via R software (version 4.3.3; http://www.R-project.org). The normality of continuous variables was assessed via the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed continuous variables are presented as the means ± standard deviations, whereas non-normally distributed distributed variables are described as medians (interquartile ranges, IQRs). Categorical variables are expressed as n (%). Intergroup comparisons were performed via independent samples t tests (two groups), one-way ANOVA (multiple groups), Mann–Whitney U tests (two groups), or Kruskal–Wallis tests (multiple groups), depending on the variable type and distribution characteristics. Comparisons of categorical variables were conducted via chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient baseline characteristics

A total of 193 patients with SICH were screened, and 85 were ultimately included (Fig. 1). The mean age was 54.2 ± 13.5 years, with males accounting for 63.5% of the sample. 91.8% of patients were admitted to the ICU, 51.8% were comatosed at admission, and 43.5% presented with vomiting. Early SICHAP was present in 90.6% of the patients. Patients were divided into four groups on the basis of initial empirical antibiotic regimens: the cefazolin group (CFZ, n = 12), the ceftriaxone group (CRO, n = 11), the cefoperazone–sulbactam group (CFP-SUL, n = 45), and the piperacillin–tazobactam group (PIP-TAZ, n = 17). Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, comorbidities, and disease severity, were generally balanced across groups, with no statistically significant differences (Table 1).

Primary outcome: efficacy of empirical antibiotic therapy at 72 h

The overall 72-hour efficacy rate was 61.2% (52/85). The differences between the antibiotic groups were statistically significant (p = 0.022): the CFP-SUL group had the highest efficacy (73.3%, 33/45), followed by the PIP-TAZ (58.8%, 10/17) and CRO (54.5%, 6/11) groups, whereas the CFZ group had the lowest efficacy (25.0%, 3/12).

Secondary outcomes

The overall SICHAP treatment failure rate was 55.3% (47/85), but the differences among the four groups were not statistically significant (p = 0.137). The PIP-TAZ group had the highest failure rate (76.5%), whereas the CRO group had the lowest failure rate (45.5%). The in-hospital mortality rate was 4.7% (4/85), with no statistically significant differences between the groups (p = 0.238). There were no statistically significant differences among the groups in terms of length of ICU stay, length of hospital stay, or duration of mechanical ventilation (Table 1).

Analysis of the sputum culture-positive subgroup and appropriateness of treatment

Among the 85 patients, 49 (57.6%) had positive sputum cultures. Of these, 31 (63.3%) received an AEAT, whereas the remaining 18 (36.7%) received an IEAT. The SICHAP treatment failure rate was significantly greater in the IEAT group than in the AEAT group (94.4% vs. 58.1%, p = 0.008), and the 72-hour efficacy rate tended to decrease (33.3% vs. 58.1%, p = 0.140). Compared with the AEAT group, the IEAT group had longer ICU stays, hospital stays, and durations of antibiotic therapy; however, these differences were not statistically significant (Table 2).

Microbiological characteristics

A total of 81 pathogenic bacteria were isolated, including 61 Gram-negative bacteria (75.3%). The most common Gram-negative pathogens were Klebsiella pneumoniae (21/49, 42.9%), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (20/49, 40.8%), and Acinetobacter baumannii (11/49, 22.4%). Among Enterobacteriaceae, the ESBL carriage rate was 20.7% (6/29), with carriage rates of 9.5% for Klebsiella pneumoniae, 0% for Klebsiella oxytoca, 75% for Escherichia coli, and 50% for Enterobacter cloacae. The carbapenemase positivity rate was 25% for Pseudomonas aeruginosa and as high as 81.8% for Acinetobacter baumannii. Among the Gram-positive bacteria, Staphylococcus aureus was the most common (38.8%), with 84.2% being methicillin-susceptible strains (Table 3).

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study is the first to systematically evaluate four commonly used empirical antibiotic regimens in patients with SICHAP. The results revealed that CFP-SUL demonstrated significantly greater 72-hour clinical efficacy than did CFZ, CRO, and PIP-TAZ, suggesting a potential advantage for initial SICHAP therapy. Furthermore, the AEAT significantly reduced the risk of SICHAP treatment failure. These findings provide important clinical evidence for optimizing initial antimicrobial strategies for this patient population.

Empirical antibiotic therapy is widely regarded as a cornerstone in the management of severe infections, with previous studies demonstrating that appropriate empirical treatment is associated with improved survival rates [16]. In our cohort, the etiological spectrum of SICHAP was predominantly composed of Gram-negative bacteria, notably Klebsiella pneumoniae, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Acinetobacter baumannii. In particular, the rate of ESBL production among Enterobacteriaceae was 20.7%, whereas carbapenemase positivity was observed in 25% of Pseudomonas aeruginosa and 81.8% of Acinetobacter baumannii isolates. CFP-SUL exhibits broad-spectrum activity against the predominant pathogens identified in this cohort. It has demonstrated potent efficacy against ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae and carbapenem-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and has been associated with high clinical cure rates for Acinetobacter baumannii infections [17–20]. The relatively poor 72-hour efficacy observed with CFZ and CRO can be attributed to their narrower spectra, which lack reliable activity against ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae and non-fermenting Gram-negative rods such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Acinetobacter baumannii—organisms that were prevalent in our study population. Therefore, the superior antibacterial coverage of CFP-SUL, particularly against these multidrug-resistant organisms, likely explains its significantly higher 72-hour efficacy rate compared to that of CFZ and CRO. Although both PIP-TAZ and CFP-SUL are β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combinations with similar spectra and have shown comparable efficacy for pneumonia in previous studies [15, 21], the PIP-TAZ group in our study exhibited a lower 72-hour efficacy rate and the highest incidence of treatment failure. Although baseline characteristics did not differ significantly across groups, patients receiving PIP-TAZ had relatively higher levels of CRP and PCT, suggesting a greater inflammatory burden at baseline, which may partly explain the observed differential outcomes. Further studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to elucidate these findings.

This study reaffirms the critical role of AEAT in severe infections, particularly among high-risk patients with SICHAP [22]. In our cohort, the SICHAP treatment failure rate was significantly lower with AEAT than with IEAT. Despite a lack of statistical significance for the 72-hour efficacy rate, length of stay, and durations of mechanical ventilation and antibiotic therapy, all outcomes showed trends in a clinically concordant direction, suggesting benefit from AEAT. These findings align with prior critical care evidence. For instance, Ohnuma et al. [22] demonstrated in a large cohort that AEAT was associated with significantly lower in-hospital mortality in patients with bloodstream infections. Similarly, other pivotal studies have established that pathogen coverage by initial empirical therapy independently predicts clinical failure, prolonged hospitalization, and mortality risk, as shown in general critically ill populations [23], in septic shock [24], and in the context of stroke-associated infection [6]. The present analysis provides the first direct clinical data supporting this principle specifically for SICHAP patients, who currently lack specific management guidelines. Therefore, although the sample size limited the statistical power for some outcome measures, the significant reduction in treatment failure observed with AEAT, together with consistent external evidence, underscores the imperative of achieving early and precise empirical therapy in the management of SICHAP.

A systematic review by Kishore et al. [11] indicated that SAP pathogens are predominantly aerobic Gram-negative rods (38%) and Gram-positive cocci (16%). Common species include Enterobacteriaceae (21.8%: e.g., Klebsiella pneumoniae 12.8%, Escherichia coli 9%), Staphylococcus aureus (10.1%), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (6%), Acinetobacter baumannii (4.6%), and Streptococcus pneumoniae (3.5%). The PISCES consensus group therefore recommends empirical SAP treatment covering Gram-positive cocci, Enterobacteriaceae, and Pseudomonas aeruginosa when risk factors are present [7]. This study focused primarily on early SICHAP, where the pathogen spectrum should theoretically resemble that of CAP. However, typical CAP pathogens (e.g., Haemophilus influenzae and Moraxella catarrhalis) were not identified; instead, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter baumannii, and Staphylococcus aureus predominated. This distribution is consistent with the systematic review by Kishore et al. [11], although the proportions of specific microbial species differed, and we observed ESBL and carbapenemase production in some strains. This may be because our study included only patients with hemorrhagic stroke, who were predominantly treated in the ICU and had high-risk factors such as aspiration and impaired consciousness. Therefore, even for early SICHAP, initial empirical therapy should adequately cover resistant Gram-negative bacteria rather than be limited to typical CAP pathogens. On the other hand, the Staphylococcus aureus isolates in this study were predominantly methicillin-susceptible (84.2%), which is consistent with reports by Zhao et al. [10]. These findings suggest that initial SICHAP treatment does not routinely require combination antibiotics covering methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). Furthermore, although approximately half of the patients presented high-risk aspiration factors, such as vomiting or coma, no anaerobic bacteria were isolated from sputum cultures. This may be attributed to specimen collection and submission methods not being routinely optimized for anaerobic culture. Both CFP-SUL and PIP-TAZ possess antianaerobic activity, and their relative superiority may partially stem from effective coverage of potential anaerobic mixed infections [25].

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis with a limited sample size—particularly in the CFZ and CRO groups—it may exhibit insufficient statistical power and restricted generalizability. Second, antibiotic assignment was nonrandomized; despite balanced baseline characteristics, unmeasured confounding biases may persist. Additionally, empirical antibiotic therapy at our center tends to favor β-lactams, and the role of other common antimicrobials, such as fluoroquinolones, in SICHAP treatment has not been evaluated. Future multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trials are needed to further validate the efficacy and safety of different antibiotic regimens for treating SICHAP. Furthermore, it should be noted that detailed susceptibility testing results for the specific antibiotics against the actual isolates in our cohort are not presented herein, and the cited literature encompasses heterogeneous infection types and dosing regimens, which may not be directly extrapolated to SICHAP. Thus, our conclusions are drawn primarily from clinical efficacy outcomes within our cohort, supported by known microbiological susceptibility patterns from local and regional data.

Conclusions

In summary, this single-center retrospective cohort study provides the first comparative data on empirical antibiotic regimens for SICHAP. Within this specific ICU setting and patient population, empirical therapy with CFP-SUL was associated with a significantly higher 72-hour clinical efficacy rate compared to CFZ, CRO, and PIP-TAZ. Furthermore, AEAT markedly reduced the risk of SICHAP treatment failure. These findings suggest that, in environments with similar pathogen profiles and resistance patterns, CFP-SUL may be a rational choice for early empirical therapy. They also reinforce the paramount importance of basing empirical choices on local antimicrobial resistance data and promptly adjusting therapy based on susceptibility results. These observations require validation through large-scale, multicenter, prospective randomized controlled trials to establish more generalizable and evidence-based recommendations for SICHAP management. Additionally, clinicians should consider local drug availability, regulatory status, and regional resistance patterns when applying these findings, as guideline-practice gaps may exist, particularly in settings where certain antibiotics like CFP-SUL are not routinely recommended or accessible.

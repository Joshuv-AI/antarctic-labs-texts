CT parameters of psoas muscle predicts 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis: a retrospective study
Authors: Yun Wang, Tun Zhao, Min Liu, Wenli Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOdjectivesTo investigate the predictive value of computed tomography (CT)-based transverse diameter (TD) and longitudinal diameter (LD) of the psoas muscle at the level of third lumbar vertebra for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis.MethodsThis retrospective single-center cohort study included 115 septic patients aged over 65 years old who were admitted to ICU in Beijing-Chaoyang Hospital between July 2017 and July 2022. The TD and LD of psoas muscle were obtained by measurement on abdominal CT images. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify the prognostic factors for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis, and their predictive performances were evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsMultivariable logistic analysis indicated that TD (OR: 0.405, 95% CI: 0.190–0.864) was a protective factor for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis, whereas LD was not. The areas under the curve (AUCs) of TD, APACHE II, and SOFA were 0.666 (95% CI: 0.565–0.767), 0.660 (95% CI: 0.561–0.760), and 0.679 (95% CI: 0.581–0.777), respectively. Furthermore, the AUCs for the combination of TD with APACHE II or SOFA were 0.766 (95% CI: 0.679–0.853, P < 0.001) and 0.765 (95% CI: 0.679–0.852, P < 0.001).ConclusionsTD of psoas muscle was a predictor for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis. The combination of TD with APACHE II or SOFA scores enhances the early identification of patients at high risk for poor prognosis.Clinical trial numbernot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-024-05559-0.Keywords: Sepsis, Sarcopenia, Psoas muscle, Computed tomography, Mortality

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition resulting from a disrupted host responses to infections, leading to organ dysfunction [1]. Despite recent advances in its understanding and treatment, sepsis remains a common issue in intensive care units (ICU), affecting over 30 million people worldwide each year and carrying a mortality rate of approximately 17% [2, 3]. Older people (age ≥ 65 years) have a high prevalence of sepsis, accounting for 64.9% of all sepsis patients, which is associated with comorbidities and immune system aging [4, 5]. Compared to younger patients, older patients with sepsis exhibit a significantly higher risk of death (odd ratio [OR]: 13.1, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 12.6 ~ 13.6) [6, 7]. Thus, correctly assessing the prognosis of older adults with sepsis is crucial for the early development of effective treatment strategies and the optimization of healthcare resource allocation.

Sarcopenia is characterized by a decline in muscle mass, strength, and physical function [8]. Recent evidence increasingly highlights the impact of sarcopenia on clinical outcomes and prognosis in critically ill patients, including those with sepsis [9–11]. A meta-analysis involving 2396 patients demonstrated that septic patients with sarcopenia had a significantly increased early mortality risk (risk ratio: 2.14, 95% CI: 1.60–2.87, P < 0.001) compared to those without sarcopenia [12]. Furthermore, sarcopenia or loss of muscle mass is strongly associated with unfavorable prognosis in patients with sepsis [13]. Therefore, measurement of muscle mass in patients, particularly during the initial stages of the disease, holds the potential to identify patients with poor prognoses and facilitate timely nutritional interventions.

Currently, skeletal muscle mass can be evaluated using various methods, including computed tomography (CT), dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, bedside ultrasound, and bioelectrical impedance analysis [14]. Of these methods, CT is generally regarded the “gold standard” for noninvasive assessment of skeletal muscle mass due to its minimal impact from body water and its objective nature [15]. A common approach to estimate overall skeletal muscle mass by CT is to calculate the cross-sectional area of the psoas muscle at the third lumbar vertebra (L3) or the fourth lumbar vertebra (L4), where the muscle is minimally affected by movement [16]. However, this method involves image extraction and specialized software analysis, which somewhat limits its broad clinical applicability, and there is still a need to establish simpler and more clinical feasible measurement methods. Given the ellipsoidal shape of the psoas muscle, we hypothesized that longitudinal diameter (LD) and transverse diameter (TD) could serve as metrics for muscle mass assessment based on the ellipse area formula: S = π/4 × LD × TD. Both parameters can be measured using software embedded in the CT operating system, ensuring ease of operation and minimal error. Previously, Matsushima and colleagues demonstrated that using TD of the psoas muscle as an index for evaluating muscle mass can predict postoperative complications in patients with acute diverticulitis [17]. Nevertheless, no studies have reported the prognostic value of these parameters in older patients with sepsis.

To address this gap, this study retrospectively analyzed clinical data from older patients with sepsis. We hypothesized that CT parameters of the psoas muscle, including LD and TD, can predict prognosis in this population.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This was a single-center retrospective study conducted from July 2017 to July 2022 in Beijing Chao-Yang Hospital, China.

Participants

The subjects of this study were older patients with sepsis in the ICU. All eligible patients were diagnosed with sepsis according to the Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3) [1] and were aged 65 years or older. Exclusion criteria: (1) absence of abdominal CT images within 72 h of admission; (2) presence of malignant tumors, including hematologic malignancies; (3) patients with intra-abdominal hemorrhage or other conditions that make psoas muscle assessment difficult. Patient enrollment flowchart

Variables

The dataset was obtained from the hospital information system. Baseline and clinical characteristics of patients at admission were collected, including demographics (age and sex), comorbidities, vital signs, and infection focus. Initial laboratory test results, such as white blood cell, albumin, procalcitonin, alanine transaminase, aspartate transaminase, brain natriuretic peptide, lactate, and D-dimer, were also recorded. To assess illness severity, the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scores were calculated. Additionally, data on organ support therapy were gathered. The primary outcome was 28-day mortality, while secondary outcomes included in-hospital mortality and length of hospital stay.

CT parameter analysis

Abdominal CT images of the patients were acquired utilizing a Revolution Frontier series 64-row spiral CT scanner (General Electric Company, Boston, USA) at the time of admission. The LD and TD of the psoas muscle at the lower edge of the L3 level were measured by two researchers. The LD refers to the longest axis of the muscle, while the TD is the longest axis perpendicular to the LD. Specifically, the researchers identified the endpoints of the LD and TD of the right psoas muscle on the abdominal CT images under blind conditions, with the equipment automatically analyzing and recording these measurements (as shown in Fig. 2). The intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) for TD and LD were 0.877 (95% CI: 0.822–0.915, P < 0.001) and 0.880 (95% CI: 0.827–0.917, P < 0.001), respectively, indicating good agreement between the measurements obtained by the two researchers. The average values of the measurements from the two researchers were calculated for the subsequent statistical analyses.

Schematic of measuring the longitudinal diameter (LD) and transverse diameter (TD) of the psoas muscle on abdominal CT images

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables that conformed to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with group comparisons made using the Student’s t test; those that did not conform to normal distribution were expressed as median (quartiles), with group comparisons made by the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts (percentages), and the chi-square test was used for comparisons between groups. Additionally, univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify independent predictors of 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curves were performed to assess the predictive value of these predictors for patient prognosis. The optimal cutoff values for psoas muscle CT parameters were determined using the Youden index. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS 27.0 (IBM, Armonk, USA), and differences were considered statistically significant at a P-value < 0.05.

Ethics consideration

This study was conducted in accordance to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Chao-Yang Hospital, Capital Medical University [approval number: 2023-Ke-719]. Informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

Results

Baseline information of participants

Of the 115 older patients enrolled, 72 (62.6%) individuals were male, while 43 (37.4%) individuals were female, with a median age of 76.0 (69.0, 84.0) years. Based on the 28-day mortality, all patients were divided into a 28-day survival group and a 28-day death group. There were no significant differences in gender, age, comorbidities, and vital signs on admission between the two groups. In terms of focus of infection, the survival group had a higher proportion of patients with genitourinary infections compared to the death group (P < 0.05), while other characteristics showed no statistical differences. Univariable and multivariable analysis of 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis

To analyze the predictors of 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis, we compared the laboratory indicators, relevant disease severity scores, and psoas muscle CT parameters between the 28-day survival group and the 28-day death group. As shown in Table 2, the D-dimer levels, APACHE II scores, and SOFA scores upon admission were significantly higher in the death group compared to the survival group (P < 0.05). Additionally, the TD and LD of the psoas major muscle were longer in the survival group; however, the difference in the LD was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). Univariable logistic regression analyses were performed for the four variables with P-values less than 0.05. The results indicated that high D-dimer levels, high APACHE II scores, and high SOFA scores were risk factors for 28-day mortality, while a longer TD of the psoas major muscle was a protective factor (Table 3, all P-values < 0.05). After including these four statistically significant variables in the multivariable analysis, TD was found to be an independent protective factor for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis (OR = 0.405, 95% CI: 0.190–0.864, P = 0.019). Further sensitivity analysis demonstrated that TD is a robust predictor (Supplementary Materials).

ROC curve analysis

ROC curve analysis was conducted to assess the predictive performance of TD for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis. As depicted in Fig. 3, the area under the curve (AUC) of TD was 0.666 (95% CI: 0.565–0.767), which was close to that of APACHE II (AUC: 0.660, 95% CI: 0.561–0.760) and SOFA (AUC: 0.679, 95% CI: 0.581–0.777). Furthermore, the AUCs for the combination of TD with APACHE II or SOFA were 0.766 (95% CI: 0.679–0.853, P < 0.001) and 0.765 (95% CI: 0.679–0.852, P < 0.001), respectively. Given that there is some overlap in the assessment content of the APACHE II and SOFA, the combination of all three was not considered here.

Subgroup analysis of psoas muscle CT parameter

In this cohort, the optimal cut-off values for LD and TD were 42.5 mm and 22.7 mm for male, and 41.0 mm and 18.8 mm for female, respectively. Based on the cut-off values of TD, older patients with sepsis were classified into low transverse diameter (LTD) group and non-low transverse diameter (non-LTD) group. LD was excluded from subsequent analyses, as it was not identified as a significant predictor of patients’ mortality.

We compared the disease severity, use of organ support therapies, and clinical outcomes between patients in the LTD and non-LTD groups. The results showed that the SOFA score in the LTD group was markedly higher (median: 9.0 vs. 7.0, P < 0.05, Table 4). Furthermore, the incidence of septic shock was higher in the LTD group, while the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). In terms of treatment, the proportion of patients in the LTD group using vasopressors was greater than that in the non-LTD group (P < 0.05). Besides, the LTD group had significantly higher rates of 28-day mortality (61.2% vs. 24.2%, P < 0.05) and in-hospital mortality (67.3% vs. 30.3%, P < 0.05). However, no notable prolongation of the length of hospital stay was observed in the LTD group (Table 4).

Discussion

In this study, we examined the association between CT parameters of psoas muscle and clinical outcomes in older patients with sepsis. TD of psoas muscle was identified as a protective factor for 28-day mortality in these patients. When combined with severity-of-disease scoring system such as APACHE II or SOFA scores, TD demonstrated a good performance in predicting prognosis in older patients with sepsis. Further subgroup analysis revealed that LTD was associated with worse short-term clinical outcomes (28-day mortality and in-hospital).

The results of a meta-analysis revealed that the scanned muscle mass area was 1.33 times greater in sepsis survivors compared to non-survivors [13]. Consistent with this trend, our findings suggested that early survival rates were higher in septic patients with greater TD. The explanation for this finding is that the reduction in skeletal muscle quality reflects sarcopenia. Generally, sarcopenia is recognized as an indicator of physiological aging, immune system decline, and poor host response to infections, all of which play a crucial role in the pathogenesis and progression of sepsis [12, 18, 19]. Furthermore, sarcopenia may also reflect inadequate nutritional status and overall frailty in patients, factors that can negatively impact the prognosis of those with sepsis [12, 20].

Interestingly, in this study, only TD was identified as an independent protective factor for clinical outcomes in older patients with sepsis, while LD did not show statistical significance in the analysis. Similar findings were reported by Huguet et al. [21], who investigated the predictive value of psoas-muscle TD and LD for mortality in patients with cirrhosis. In their study, TD was a significant predictor, while LD was not. Although a precise explanation remains unclear, it is plausible that this discrepancy arises because the anatomical endpoints of LD are more closely associated with adjacent structures, making them less susceptible to the impact of catabolism compared to TD. In addition, the incidence of lumbar spine osteophytes is high in the elderly population, which may impact the morphology of the LD of the psoas major muscle and consequently weaken its correlation with the prognosis of septic patients [22]. Further research is needed to confirm these hypotheses.

Through ROC curve analysis, we compared the predictive value of TD with other indicators and found that TD’s discriminatory ability is comparable to that of APACHE II and SOFA scores. The latter are commonly used tools for assessing and monitoring critically ill patients. Research has demonstrated that APACHE II and SOFA scores were independent prognostic factors for patients with sepsis [23–25]. Within this study, the AUCs for SOFA and APACHE II scores in predicting 28-day mortality risk in older patients with sepsis were 0.679 and 0.660, respectively, which are similar to or slightly lower than previously reported values [26–28]. Furthermore, our results indicated that combining the severity-of-disease scoring system with TD of psoas muscle improves the predictive capability for short-term clinical outcomes. Therefore, clinicians should not only consider CT diagnostic information, but also measure psoas muscle CT parameters and integrate them with severity-of-disease scoring systems to effectively assess the prognosis of older patients with sepsis.

Implications for clinical practice

Due to the higher early mortality risk indicated by LTD in older patients with sepsis, early assessment and intervention are essential in these patients. Firstly, a comprehensive screening and assessment for sarcopenia should be conducted using various methods, including muscle quality evaluation, strength testing, and functional assessments, with stratified management for high-risk patients [29, 30]. Establishing a multidisciplinary team that includes nutritionists and physical therapists is recommended to provide holistic care. Numerous studies have demonstrated that nutrition and exercise are effective in preventing the development of sarcopenia [31–33]. Therefore, it is vital to ensure that these patients receive with a high-protein diet and adequate nutrient supplementation to support muscle synthesis [34]. Moreover, preventive measures such as resistance and endurance exercises [35] or electrical muscle stimulation [36] can be implemented to help maintain muscle function. Finally, regular monitoring of muscle quality and function, along with adjustments to management strategies based on assessment results, will ensure optimal care.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective study, it lacked certain pertinent information, such as body mass index and debilitation index at admission, which could have enhanced the study’s comprehensiveness. Additionally, the statistics on the length of hospital stay in this study did not account for the influence of transferred patients, which may impact the results. Besides, this study focused solely on short-term survival in older patients with sepsis, and did not address the impact of psoas muscle CT parameters on long-term outcomes, such as 1-year morbidity and mortality of patients. Future research should include prospective multicenter studies with long-term follow-up to better understand these aspects. Finally, it should be noted that the findings of this study are not applicable to the following populations: those who cannot undergo CT scans due to their condition, patients with malignancies, those with intra-abdominal bleeding, patients with abdominal masses or organ enlargement compressing the psoas major muscle, and individuals with neuromuscular diseases.

Conclusion

TD of the psoas muscle serves as a prognostic factor for 28-day mortality in older patients with sepsis. Combining psoas muscle CT parameter with APACHE II or SOFA scores can enhance prognosis in this population by facilitating early identification of high-risk patients and optimizing therapeutic regimens.

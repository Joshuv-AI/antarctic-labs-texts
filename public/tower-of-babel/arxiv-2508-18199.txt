Sparse Polynomial Regression under Anomalous Data
Roozbeh Abolpour, Mohammad Reza Hesamzadeh, Maryam Dehghani

Abstract
This paper starts with the general form of the polynomial regression model. We reformulate the Sparse Polynomial Regression Model (SPRM) with anomalous data filtering as Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP). This MILP is then converted to a non-convex Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program (QCQP). Through a proposed mapping, the derived QCQP is reformulated as a Fractional Program (FP). We theoretically show that the reformulated FP has better computational properties than the original QCQP. We then suggest a conic-relaxation-based algorithm to solve the proposed FP. A Two-Step Convex Relaxation and Recovery (TS-CRR) algorithm is proposed for sparse polynomial regression with anomalous data filtering. Through a series of comprehensive computational experiments (using two different datasets), we have compared the results of our proposed TS-CRR algorithm with the results from several regression and artificial intelligent models. The numerical results show the promising performance of our proposed TS-CRR algorithm as compared to those studied benchmark models.

Problem Formulation

Suppose
{\left\{x^{\left(k\right)}\right\}}^{N}_{k=1}
and
{\left\{y^{\left(k\right)}\right\}}^{N}_{k=1}
are the given input and output data, respectively. The objective is to determine the following polynomial regression model

y^{\left(k\right)}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{c_{\alpha}{x^{\left(k\right)}}^{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{\left(k\right)},\quad\forall k\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},

(1)

where
d
is the polynomial degree,
{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}=\left\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\right|\ \sum^{n}_{i=1}{{\alpha}_{i}}\leq d\}
with
m_{d}
distinct vectors (i.e.
m_{d}=|{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}|
),
\epsilon^{k}
is the error term,
{x^{\left(k\right)}}^{\alpha}=\prod^{n}_{i=1}{{x^{\left(k\right)}_{i}}^{{\alpha}_{i}}\ }
, and
{\left\{c_{\alpha}\right\}}_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}
is the set of polynomial coefficients. The following optimization problem determines these polynomial coefficients.

\displaystyle\min_{\Omega_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\gamma

(2a)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle\quad\left|y^{\left(k\right)}-\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{c_{\alpha}{x^{\left(k\right)}}^{\alpha}}\right|\leq\gamma,\quad\forall k\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},

(2b)

where
\Omega_{1}=\{c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m_{d}},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}
and
\gamma
is the regression error.

Conventionally, a subset of the training data
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
is considered anomalous if its members deviate from the underlying mathematical model of the system. Hence, detecting the anomalous data requires a mathematical model, which may be unavailable in some real-world applications. To address this limitation, an alternative model-independent definition is proposed below.

Definition 1

A subset
\bar{J}\subset\{1,...,N\}
is defined to contain the indices of anomalous data if it satisfies the following condition.

\bar{J}=\underset{J\subset\{1,...,N\},|J|=|\bar{J}|}{\text{argmin}}\left(\max_{k\in\{1,...,N\}\setminus J}{\left|y^{\left(k\right)}-f(x^{(k)})\right|}\right),

(3)

where
f(.)
is the regression model.∎

This means, among all subsets
J
of a given size (same as

\bar{J}
), we select the one whose removal from the dataset leads to the lowest possible maximum training error. This definition can be directly integrated into the training procedure, enabling the identification and elimination of anomalous data during training.

To integrate both the anomalous-data definition
1
and the sparsity property into the polynomial regression optimization model (
2
), binary decision variables are introduced. Let set
{\left\{s_{\alpha}\right\}}_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}
include binary variables indicating whether each monomial term
{\left\{{x}^{\alpha}\right\}}_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}
is included in the regression model. In addition, let set
{\left\{b_{k}\right\}}_{k=1}^{N}
be binary variables indicating the inclusion of the
k^{th}
pair
(x^{(k)},y^{(k)})
in training, where
b_{k}=0
means the
k^{th}
pair is considered anomalous and excluded. The sparse polynomial regression model with anomalous data elimination is formulated as the following MILP.

\displaystyle\underset{\Omega_{2}}{\min}

\displaystyle\quad\gamma

(4a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\quad-M(1-b_{k})+\left|y^{\left(k\right)}-\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{c_{\alpha}{x^{\left(k\right)}}^{\alpha}}\right|\leq\gamma,\quad\forall k\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},

(4b)

\displaystyle\quad-Ms_{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha}\leq Ms_{\alpha}~\forall\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d},

(4c)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}=l_{b},

(4d)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}s_{\alpha}=l_{m},

(4e)

where
\Omega_{2}=\left\{c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m_{d}},s\in{\left\{0,1\right\}}^{m_{d}},\\
b\in\{0,1\}^{N},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\right\}
,
M
is a given large positive constant, and
l_{b}
and
l_{m}
are positive integer numbers. In MILP (
4
), the training error
f(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}{s_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}}
is minimized. In (
4b
), binary variable
b_{k}
determines whether the pair
(x^{(k)},y^{(k)})
contributes to the training error
\gamma
. Similarly, binary variable
s_{\alpha}
in (
4c
) determines the inclusion of monomial term
x^{\alpha}
in the training. Based on constraint (
4d
), the number of anomalous data is given by
N-l_{b}
and due to constraint (
4e
), the final polynomial regression model will be sparse, with the number of monomials fixed at
l_{m}
.

3
Fractional Programming (FP) Reformulation

3.1
Reformulation Motivation

The MILP formulation (
4
) effectively models sparsity and anomalous data filtering through introduced binary variables. However, solving MILPs at scale remains computational intensive, especially when the number of monomial terms and data points grow. To address this limitation, we propose a mathematical transformation through which the MILP is reformulated to a continuous-domain Fractional Program (FP) with improved computational properties.

3.2
Mathematical Transformation

We first reformulate the MILP (
4
) into the following nonconvex QCQP:

\displaystyle\underset{\Omega_{3}}{\min}

\displaystyle\quad\gamma

(5a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\quad-M(1-b_{k})+\left|y^{\left(k\right)}-\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{c_{\alpha}{x^{\left(k\right)}}^{\alpha}}\right|\leq\gamma,\quad\forall k\in\left\{1,\dots,N\right\},

(5b)

\displaystyle\quad\ -Ms_{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha}\leq Ms_{\alpha}~\forall\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d},

(5c)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=1}^{N}{b_{k}}=l_{b},

(5d)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}s_{\alpha}=l_{m},

(5e)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{s_{\alpha}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}\leq{\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathrm{\Gamma}}_{d}}{s_{\alpha}^{2}}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}^{2},

(5f)

where
\Omega_{3}=\left\{c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m_{d}},s\in{\left[0,1\right]}^{m_{d}},b\in[0,1]^{N},\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\right\}
.

It can be readily shown that the nonconvex QCQP (
5
) is equivalent to the MILP (
4
), since constraint (
5f
), together with the bounds
s\in{\left[0,1\right]}^{m_{d}}
, and
b\in[0,1]^{N}
enforce vectors
s
and
b
to take binary values; that is,
s\in\{0,1\}^{m_{d}}
and
b\in\{0,1\}^{N}
. Now, consider the following mathematical mapping in which
\rho
is a sufficiently large positive constant.

\displaystyle\left(\hat{c},\hat{s},\hat{b},\hat{\gamma},\hat{v}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(c,s,b,\gamma)=

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}\left(c,\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}}s,\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}}b,\gamma,1\right)}{\sqrt{\left(\rho-1\right)\left(\|s\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}+1\right)+\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}{s_{\alpha}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}}}.

The inverse of fractional mapping
\mathcal{T}
is given by

\displaystyle\left(c,s,b,\gamma\right)={\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\left(\hat{c},\hat{s},\hat{b},\hat{\gamma},\hat{v}\right)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\hat{c}}{\hat{v}},\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}\hat{s}}{\sqrt{\rho}\hat{v}},\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}\hat{b}}{\sqrt{\rho}\hat{v}},\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{\hat{v}}\right).

(7)

Using fractional mapping (
6
), the nonconvex QCQP (
5
) is transformed into the following FP (
8
).

\displaystyle\underset{\Omega_{4}}{\min}

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{\hat{v}}

(8a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\quad-M\hat{v}+\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}}{\sqrt{\rho}}M\hat{b}_{k}+\left|y^{(k)}\hat{v}-\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}\hat{c}_{\alpha}{x^{(k)}}^{\alpha}\right|\leq\hat{\gamma},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\dots,N\},

(8b)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}}{\sqrt{\rho}}M\hat{s}_{\alpha}\leq\hat{c}_{\alpha}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}}{\sqrt{\rho}}M\hat{s}_{\alpha},\quad\forall\alpha\in\Gamma_{d},

(8c)

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq\hat{s}_{\alpha}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}}\hat{v},\quad\forall\alpha\in\Gamma_{d},

(8d)

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq\hat{b}_{k}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}}\hat{v},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\dots,N\},

(8e)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=1}^{N}\hat{b}_{k}=l_{b}\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}},

(8f)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}\hat{s}_{\alpha}=l_{m}\frac{\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho-1}},

(8g)

\displaystyle\quad\hat{v}=\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}}{\sqrt{\rho(m_{d}+N+1)-1}},

(8h)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{(\rho-1)(\|\hat{s}\|^{2}+\|\hat{b}\|^{2})}{\rho}+\frac{\hat{v}}{\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho-1}}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}\hat{s}_{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\hat{b}_{k}\right)+\hat{v}^{2}\leq 1,

(8i)

\displaystyle\quad\|\hat{s}\|^{2}+\|\hat{b}\|^{2}+\hat{v}^{2}\geq 1,

(8j)

where
\Omega=\left\{\hat{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{d}},~\hat{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{d}},~\hat{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},~\hat{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R},~\hat{v}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
.

4
Solution Algorithm

This section presents a tractable approach for solving the FP (
8

8j

We begin by using SDC to exactly reformulate the nonconvex constraint (

\displaystyle\underset{\Omega_{5}}{\text{min}}

\displaystyle\quad~\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{\hat{v}},

\displaystyle\quad-M\hat{v}+\frac{\sqrt{\rho-1}}{\sqrt{\rho}}M\hat{b}_{k}+\left|y^{(k)}\hat{v}-\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{d}}\hat{c}_{\alpha}{x^{(k)}}^{\alpha}\right|\leq\hat{\gamma},\quad\forall k\in\{1,\dots,N\},

(26b)

5.4
Discussion

The proposed TS-CRR algorithm demonstrates promising performance in both electricity price and temperature measurement applications, outperforming several benchmark regression and intelligent models in various metrics.
It avoids overfitting through sparsity enforcement while preserving enough complexity to capture essential nonlinearities. This results in strong extrapolation performance without substantial compromise in interpolation accuracy. Its simplicity, interpretability, and robustness make it suitable for deployment in real-world forecasting systems where both short-term accuracy and long-term generalization are critical.

6
Conclusions

This paper proposes a new SPRM with anomalous data filtering. First, the SPRM is formulated as a nonconvex QCQP. Then through a proposed fractional mapping, the nonconvex QCQP is reformulated as a FP with only one nonconvex spherical constraint. We theoretically show that the reformulated FP has better computational performance than the original nonconvex QCQP. The proposed FP is then solved by a developed linear-based relaxation model. A TS-CRR algorithm is proposed for SPRM with anomalous data filtering. The TS-CRR algorithm, in step 1, finds optimal monomials and then in step 2 optimally estimates the corresponding coefficients. We have tested the proposed TS-CRR algorithm using two datasets: one contains electricity prices and the other consists of temperature measurements. The results of the TS-CRR algorithm are compared with those obtained from four different different benchmark models. The numerical experiments supported by theoretical developments show a promising performance of the proposed TS-CRR algorithm for real-life applications.

Branch iterated Galois groups with positive fixed-point proportion and positive Hausdorff dimension Thanks: The author is supported by Grigorchuk’s Simons Foundation Grant MP-TSM-00002045 and the department of Mathematics of Texas A&M University.
Santiago Radi

Abstract
. In this article we prove that the arithmetic profinite iterated monodromy group of a post-critically infinite unicritical polynomial is regular branch (and so of positive Hausdorff dimension), and has positive fixed-point proportion when the degree is odd. The examples are instances of a bigger family of regular branch groups constructed in this article, whose fixed-point proportion can be computed explicitly and is positive in many cases. This gives the first examples outside the binary rooted tree where a level-transitive group has positive Hausdorff dimension and positive fixed-point proportion, answering in the negative a question of Jones (2008).

1.
Introduction

Given a
d
-regular rooted tree
T
, the group of automorphisms of
T
, denoted
\Aut(T)
, is the set of bijective functions
g:T\rightarrow T
that preserve adjacency between the vertices. Since elements in
\Aut(T)
preserve adjacency, the group
\Aut(T)
acts on
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, the
n
-th level of the tree for each
n\geq 0
. The kernel of each of these actions is denoted
\St(n)
and then one has natural projections
\pi_{n}:\Aut(T)\rightarrow\Aut(T)/\St(n)
, which corresponds to the action of the elements in
\Aut(T)
over the first
n
levels. If
G
is a subgroup of
\Aut(T)
, then
\St_{G}(n):=G\cap\St(n)
is the stabilizer of level
n
of
G
and we have the restrictions of the maps
\pi_{n}:G\rightarrow G/\St_{G}(n)
. The fixed-point proportion of
G
is calculated as

\FPP(G)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(G):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\#\pi_{n}(G)}.

Odoni in
Odoni1985
was the first one to realize that this quantity had applications to problems in arithmetic dynamics. Using the fixed-point proportion, Odoni proved that the Dirichlet density of primes dividing at least one term in the Sylvester sequence is zero, where the Sylvester sequence is given by the initial term
w_{1}=2
and the recursion
w_{n}=1+w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}
.

Odoni’s ideas were later generalized to number fields and rational functions. Given
K
a number field and
f
a rational function with degree at least
2
and coefficients in
K
, the extension
K(\cup_{n\geq 0}f^{-n}(0))/K
is Galois and its Galois group acts faithfully on the
d
-regular rooted tree of preimages of
0
(see
Jones2014
). Consequently, it embeds in
\Aut(T)
as a closed subgroup. This group is denoted
G_{\infty}(K,f,0)
and called the iterated Galois group. Then, if
a_{0}
is an element of
K
, we have that the Dirichlet density of the set

P_{f}(a_{0}):={\left\{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{0}:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f^{n}(a_{0}))>0\text{ for some $n$ such that $f^{n}(a_{0})\neq 0,\infty$}\right\}},

is bounded above by
\FPP(G_{\infty}(K,f,0))
(see
(
JonesManes2012
, Theorem 6.1)
). Here,
M_{K}^{0}
denotes the set of prime ideals of the ring of integers of
K
.

Later, more applications of the fixed-point proportion of iterated Galois groups to problems in arithmetic dynamics were discovered (see for example
BridyJones2022
;
Juul2014
). Replacing
K
by any field and
0
by an element
t
transcendental over
K
, the extension
K(\cup_{n\geq 0}f^{-n}(t))/K(t)
is still Galois and one denotes the Galois group of the extension as
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
. This group also acts faithfully in the tree of preimages of
t
, it embeds as a closed subgroup in
\Aut(T)
and it represents the generic iterated Galois group. In a loose sense,
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
is the Galois group one expects when we specialize
t
to a value in
K
. The group
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
is level-transitive, namely, the action of
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
over
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is transitive for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the case studied by Odoni, the iterated Galois group was
\Aut(T)
and it is known that the fixed-point proportion of
\Aut(T)
is zero (see for example
(
AbertVirag2004
, Corollary 2.6)
). Even more, if the iterated Galois group has finite index with respect to
\Aut(T)
, the fixed-point proportion is also zero (see
2.10
.(1)). However, it is not completely clear yet in which cases the iterated Galois group has finite index and many cases where the index is not finite are known (see
(
Jones2014
, Section 3)
for a discussion about this and
(
Jones2014
, Conjecture 3.11)
for a conjecture in the quadratic case).

Although there are examples with infinite index, there is a tendency for the iterated Galois groups to have positive Hausdorff dimension. Given
G\leq\Aut(T)
, the Hausdorff dimension of
G
is defined as

\mathcal{H}(G):=\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log(\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|)}{\log(\left|{\pi_{n}(\Aut(T))}\right|)}.

The Hausdorff dimension is another way to measure how large a group inside
\Aut(T)
is. In the context of iterated Galois groups, Pink proved in
(
Pink2013
, Theorem 4.4.2)
that if
f
is a quadratic rational function with critical points
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
whose post-critical orbits are infinite, and
r
is the smallest natural number such that
f^{r+1}(c_{1})=f^{r+1}(c_{2})
, then
\mathcal{H}(G_{\infty}(K,f,t))=1-\frac{1}{2^{r}}
, where
K
is any field of characteristic different to
2
and
t
is transcendental over
K
. In case of degree greater than
2
, authors proved in
(
Benedetto2017
, Theorem 1.1)
that the iterated Galois group of the polynomial
3x^{2}-2x^{3}
has Hausdorff dimension
\approx 0.871
. However, both cases are known to have null fixed-point proportion. On the other hand, Jones proved in
Jones2012
that if
T_{d}
corresponds to the Chebyshev polynomial of degree
d
and
t
is transcendental over
\mathbb{C}
, then

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\pm T_{d},t))=\left\{\begin{matrix}1/2&\mbox{if $d$ is odd,}\\
1/4&\mbox{if $d$ is even.}\end{matrix}\right.

However, the Hausdorff dimension of
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\pm T_{d},t)
is zero (the group is isomorphic to the infinite dihedral group and therefore satisfies the group law
[x^{2},y^{2}]=1
. Then the Hausdorff dimension is zero by
(
Fari~na2025
, Corollary 5)
).

This led Jones to pose the following conjecture:

Question 1.1

(
(

If

and fixed-point proportion of at least

In light of Boston’s counterexample, one can modify
1.1
by asking if the result holds when we restrict to iterated Galois groups. Even more, one can restrict further to the case where the iterated Galois groups are branch. A group
G\leq\Aut(T)
is branch if it is level-transitive and for every level

that act non-trivially below only one subtree at level

generate a subgroup of finite index. From the point of view of number theory, branchness is related to a rapid growth of the field extensions of any preimage of

when we take its iterations by

, closed regular branch groups have positive Hausdorff dimension and its dimension is computable in terms of its set of patterns.

The existence of regular branch iterated Galois groups is known. For example, combining the results of Pink (see
(

, Theorem 6.4.2)
) and the authors in
(

(

and
(

, one obtains that the fixed-point proportion of

is zero.

Question 1.2
.

Can we find regular branch (and therefore with positive Hausdorff dimension) iterated Galois groups with positive fixed-point proportion?

In this article, we answer
1.2
in the positive by providing examples of regular branch iterated Galois groups whose fixed-point proportion and Hausdorff dimension are calculated explicitly. The main result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be a field and
K^{sep}
a separable closure of
K
. Let
t
be a transcendental element over
K
,
d\geq 2
,

and
\xi\in K^{sep}
a primitive
d
th root of unity. Suppose that

is a post-critically infinite unicritical polynomial. Then, the group
G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)
is regular branch, its Hausdorff dimension is

\mathcal{H}(G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t))=\frac{\log(d)}{\log(d!)}

and its fixed-point proportion is

\FPP(G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t))=\frac{\#{\left\{a\in I:a-1\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\}}}{\Phi(d)},

where
I
is the image of the map
\Gal(K(\xi)/K)\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
given by
\sigma\mapsto a
with
\sigma(\xi)=\xi^{a}
, and
\Phi
is the Euler’s totient function.

In the particular case where

, we have

\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},x^{d}+c,t))=\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{p-2}{p-1},

where the product runs over the prime divisors of
d
. So, if
d
is odd, the fixed-point proportion is positive.

The iterated Galois group of

has recently being studied by Adams and Hyde in

1

H=\Sym(d)
that corresponds to the whole groups of automorphisms as
W_{\Sym(d)}=\Aut(T)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(W_{H})=\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log(\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{H})}\right|)}{\log(\left|{\pi_{n}(\Aut(T))}\right|)}=\frac{\log(\left|{H}\right|)}{\log(\left|{\Sym(d)}\right|)}=\frac{\log(\left|{H}\right|)}{\log(d!)}.

∎

2.4.
Fixed-point proportion

We define the fixed-point proportion of a subgroup
G
of
\Aut(T)
acting on a spherically homogeneous tree
T
as

(3)

\FPP(G)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(G):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}.

Lemma 2.9
.

The fixed-point proportion of
G
is well-defined, namely, the limit in
Equation

3
always exists.

Proof.

Consider the surjective map
\pi:\pi_{n+1}(G)\rightarrow\pi_{n}(G)
, namely, the projection of the action on level
n+1
to level
n
. Notice that if
h\in\pi_{n}(G)
does not fix any vertex at level
n
, then all the preimages of
h
under
\pi
will not fix any vertex at level
n+1
. Since
\pi
is a group homomorphism, the number of preimages is
\left|{\pi_{n+1}(G)}\right|/\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|
for any point
h\in\pi_{n}(G)
and consequently we have the inequality

\left|{\pi_{n+1}(G)}\right|-f_{n+1}\geq\frac{\left|{\pi_{n+1}(G)}\right|}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}\left(\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|-f_{n}\right),

where

f_{n}=\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(G):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}.

Rewriting the inequality, we obtain

\frac{f_{n}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}\geq\frac{f_{n+1}}{\left|{\pi_{n+1}(G)}\right|},

that is exactly the sequence considered in the limit of the fixed-point proportion. Since the sequence is bounded below by zero and decreasing, the limit must exist.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G\leq\Aut(T)
and
H\leq_{f}G
. Then

(1)

\FPP(G)=\FPP(\overline{G})
,

(2)

\FPP(H)\leq[G:H]\,\FPP(G)
. In particular, if
\FPP(G)=0
then
\FPP(H)=0
.

Proof.

For (1), we just observe that
\pi_{n}(G)=\pi_{n}(\overline{G})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the definition of the fixed-point proportion only depends on
\pi_{n}(G)
.

For (2), we know that if the index is finite, then there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
[\pi_{n}(G):\pi_{n}(H)]=[G:H]
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. On the other side, if
K\leq\Aut(T)
and we let
f_{n}(K)
denote the number of elements in
\pi_{n}(K)
that fix a vertex in
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, we have that
f_{n}(H)\leq f_{n}(G)
since
H\leq G
. Therefore

\displaystyle\FPP(H)=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f_{n}(H)}{\left|{\pi_{n}(H)}\right|}=[G:H]\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f_{n}(H)}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}\leq[G:H]\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{f_{n}(G)}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}=

\displaystyle[G:H]\,\FPP(G).

∎

By
2.3
, we have that if
H\leq_{f}G
and
G
is infinite, then
\mathcal{H}_{G}(H)=1
. Therefore, a natural generalization for
2.10
.(2) is to consider the case of
H\leq G
such that
\mathcal{H}_{G}(H)=1
. However, we have the following counterexample. Let
G\leq\Aut(T)
be an infinite group with null fixed-point proportion and take the subgroup
H:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\st_{G}(0^{n})
, where
0^{n}
is the leftmost vertex at level
n
. Then, we have
\pi_{n}(H)=\pi_{n}(\st_{G}(0^{n}))
and therefore every element in
\pi_{n}(H)
fixes
0^{n}
, so
\FPP(H)=1
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, we have
[\pi_{n}(G):\pi_{n}(H)]\leq\#\mathcal{L}_{n}
and consequently

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{G}(H)\geq 1-\frac{\log(\#\mathcal{L}_{n})}{\log(\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|)}

Then, it is not hard to find a group
G
such that the previous limit converges to one. For instance,
G=\Aut(T)
.

Due to
2.10
.(1), we may assume that
G
is a closed subgroup of
\Aut(T)
. Since
\Aut(T)
is a profinite group and
G
is closed, then
G
is compact and has a unique probability Haar measure
\mu
.

A remarkable property of the Haar measure is that if
S
is a measurable set in
\overline{G}
, then

(4)

\mu(S)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\#\pi_{n}(S)}{\left|{\pi_{n}(\overline{G})}\right|}.

Given
G
, a closed subgroup of
\Aut(T)
, we define the
fixed-point process
of
G
as the sequence of functions
{\left\{X_{n}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\right\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

X_{n}(g)=\#{\left\{v\in\mathcal{L}_{n}:g(v)=v\right\}}.

By the definition of the Haar measure, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\FPP(G)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(G):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G)}\right|}=\\
\mu({\left\{g\in G:\text{ $g$ fixes one vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\right\}})=\\
\mu\left(\bigcap_{n\geq 0}{\left\{g\in G:X_{n}(g)>0\right\}}\right).\end{split}

So the fixed-point process is involved in the calculation of the fixed-point proportion (see
Jones2014
for more details).

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
T
be a spherically homogeneous tree and
{\left\{G_{n}\right\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of subgroups of
\Aut(T)
such that
G_{n}\leq G_{n+1}
and write
G=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}G_{n}
. Then
\FPP(G_{n})\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow+\infty]{}\FPP(G)
.

Proof.

By
2.10
, we may assume that the subgroups
G_{n}
’s and
G
are closed. Let
\mu_{n}
be the Haar measure on
G_{n}
and consider
\hat{\mu}_{n}
the extension of
\mu_{n}
to
G
defined by

\hat{\mu}_{n}(A)=\mu_{n}(A\cap G_{n}).

In particular,

\FPP(G_{n})=\hat{\mu}_{n}\left(\bigcap_{n\geq 0}{\left\{g\in G:X_{n}(g)>0\right\}}\right)

Denote by
\mu
the Haar measure on
G
. By
(
Katznelson1968
, Exercise 4, Chapter VII)
, the sequence
\hat{\mu}_{n}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow+\infty]{}\mu
in the weak-* topology, so

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{n})=\hat{\mu}_{n}\left(\bigcap_{n\geq 0}{\left\{g\in G:X_{n}(g)>0\right\}}\right)\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow+\infty]{}

\displaystyle\mu\left(\bigcap_{n\geq 0}{\left\{g\in G:X_{n}(g)>0\right\}}\right)=\FPP(G).

∎

2.5.
Iterated Galois groups

Let
K
be a field, and let
f
be a rational function of degree at least
2
(namely, the maximum degree of numerator and denominator is at least
2
) and with coefficients in
K
. We denote the
n
th iteration of
f
by
f^{n}
. Let
t
be a transcendental element over
K
and fix
K(t)^{sep}
a separable closure of
K(t)
. Let us denote
f^{-n}(t)
the set of preimages of
t
by
f^{n}
inside
K(t)^{sep}
and
K_{n}(f):=K(f^{-n}(t))
. Then,
K_{n}(f)/K(t)
is a Galois extension, and
K_{n}(f)\subseteq K_{n+1}(f)
for all
n\geq 0
(see
Jones2014
). Define
K_{\infty}(f):=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}K_{n}(f)
. Then,
K_{\infty}(f)/K(t)
is also a Galois extension. For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup{\left\{\infty\right\}}
, define also

G_{n}(K,f,t):=\Gal(K_{n}(f)/K(t)).

By the definition of
K_{\infty}(f)
we have
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)=\varprojlim G_{n}(K,f,t)
. So, in particular
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
is a profinite group that acts naturally on
f^{-n}(t)
by permuting its elements. We call
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
the
iterated Galois group
of
f
at
t
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero,
f
a rational function in
K(x)
of degree
d
and
t
transcendental over
K
. Then
\#f^{-n}(t)=d^{n}
.

Proof.

Write
f^{n}(z)=p_{n}(z)/q_{n}(z)
with
p_{n},q_{n}\in K[x]
such that
\gcd(p_{n},q_{n})=1
. Then
z\in f^{-n}(t)
if and only if we have
p_{n}(z)-tq_{n}(z)=0
. As
\deg(f^{n})=d^{n}
, then
\deg_{x}(p_{n}(x)-tq_{n}(x))=d^{n}
, so in order to prove that
\#f^{-n}(t)=d^{n}
, it is enough to prove that
p_{n}(x)-tq_{n}(x)
is irreducible as a polynomial in
K(t)[x]
since
K
is a field of characteristic zero.

Write
p_{n}(x)-tq_{n}(x)=A(x,t)B(x,t)
. As the left hand side is a polynomial in both variables, we may assume
A,B\in K[x,t]
. The left hand side has degree
1
on
t
, therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume
A\in K[x]
and
B(x,t)=b_{0}(x)+tb_{1}(x)
. Then
Ab_{0}=p_{n}
and
Ab_{1}=q_{n}
. As
\gcd(p_{n},q_{n})=1
, then
A\in K
.
∎

In light of
2.12
, we can create a
d
-regular rooted tree
T
by putting the
n
-th preimages of
t
by
f
on the
n
-th level of the tree and connecting a vertex
v\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
and a vertex
w\in\mathcal{L}_{n+1}
if and only if
f(w)=v
. Then,
G_{\infty}(K,f,t)
acts on
T
faithfully and preserving adjacency (see again
Jones2014
for a proof of this fact). Therefore, the iterated Galois group can be embedded as a closed subgroup of
\Aut(T)
.

3.
The fixed-point proportion of iterated wreath products

We can extend the definition of the fixed-point proportion given in
Equation

3
to any set
X\subseteq\Aut(T)
in the following way:

(6)

\FPP(X):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{x\in\pi_{n}(X):\text{$x$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(X)}\right|}.

Contrary to
2.9
, the existence of the limit above is not guaranteed for every set
X
.

Let
S
be any subset of
\Sym(d)
and define

W_{S}:={\left\{g\in\Aut(T):g|_{v}^{1}\in S,\forall v\in T\right\}}.

Notice that if
S=H
is a subgroup of
\Sym(d)
, the set
W_{H}
corresponds to the definition of iterated wreath product given in
Equation

2
. For this reason, we will also refer to
W_{S}
as iterated wreath products, even though they are not necessarily subgroups of
\Aut(T)
. We will devote this section to proving that
\FPP(W_{S})
always exists and give a way to calculate it for any set
S
.

Let
f_{n}
denote the number of elements in
\pi_{n}(W_{S})
that fix at least one vertex in
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, write
\sigma_{n}=\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{S})}\right|
and
p_{n}
be the proportion of them, namely,
p_{n}=\frac{f_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}
. The idea will be to give a recurrence formula for
p_{n}
that allows us to calculate the fixed-point proportion of
W_{S}
as

\FPP(W_{S})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}p_{n}.

By definition of
W_{S}
, we have
\sigma_{n+1}=\sigma_{n}^{d}\,\,(\#S)
, because at each vertex of level
1
we can put any element of
\pi_{n}(W_{S})
and then at the root we have all the options of
S
. Another way to think of
\sigma_{n+1}
is that we have
\sigma_{n}
options for the first
n
levels and then at each vertex of level
n
we can put as label any element of
S
, so
\sigma_{n+1}=\sigma_{n}\,\,(\#S)^{\#\mathcal{L}_{n}}=\sigma_{n}\,\,(\#S)^{d^{n}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
, then
\FPP(W_{S})
always exists.

Proof.

To prove that
\FPP(W_{S})
exists, we follow the argument given in
2.9
. Notice that if an element in
\pi_{n}(W_{S})
does not fix any vertex in level
n
, then any extension to level
n+1
of that element will not fix any vertex in level
n+1
either. The number of extensions of each element is
(\#S)^{\#\mathcal{L}_{n}}=(\#S)^{d^{n}}
, therefore

\sigma_{n+1}-f_{n+1}\geq(\#S)^{d^{n}}(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})=\frac{\sigma_{n+1}}{\sigma_{n}}(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})=\sigma_{n+1}(1-p_{n}).

Reordering terms, we get
p_{n+1}\leq p_{n}
. Thus,
{\left\{p_{n}\right\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence bounded by zero and therefore
\FPP(W_{S})
exists.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
. Define
D_{S}(k)
as the number of permutations in
S
having exactly
k
fixed points.

This definition generalizes the concept of the number of derangements for any set (see
Hassani2003
). Clearly,

\sum_{k=0}^{d}D_{S}(k)=\#S.

Our next step will be to find a recurrence formula for
f_{n+1}
. Let
s\in S
be a label for the root with
k
fixed points. If
k=0
, the resulting elements will not produce fixed points at level
n+1
, so we assume that
k>0
.

An element
g\in\pi_{n+1}(W_{S})
whose label at the root is
s
will have fixed points at level
n+1
only in the subtrees fixed by
s
. In the remaining
d-k
subtrees, any element of
\pi_{n}(W_{S})
can be placed freely, contributing a factor of
\sigma_{n}^{d-k}
.

In the
k
fixed subtrees, we need to place at least one element of
\pi_{n}(W_{S})
that fixes a vertex, while the rest may be arbitrary. To avoid repetition, we consider the disjoint sets

F_{n}={\left\{h\in\pi_{n}(W_{S}):\text{$h$ has fixed points on $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}\text{ and }F_{n}^{c}.

We then sum in all the possible
k
-fold Cartesian products of
F_{n}
and
F_{n}^{c}
, excluding the product
F_{n}^{c}\times\dots\times F_{n}^{c}
, which does not produce elements with fixed points at level
n+1
.

To express this formally, let
\beta\in{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}
and define

\displaystyle\overline{f_{n}}^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{matrix}f_{n}&\mbox{if $\beta=1$,}\\
\sigma_{n}-f_{n}&\mbox{if $\beta=-1$.}\end{matrix}\right.

We conclude the following formula:

(7)

f_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}D_{S}(k)\sigma_{n}^{d-k}\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}^{k}\setminus{\left\{(-1,\dots,-1)\right\}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{n}}^{\beta_{i}}

The following lemma will help to clarify the intricate formula:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
. Using the notation introduced earlier, we have

(8)

f_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}D_{S}(k)\sigma_{n}^{d-k}(\sigma_{n}^{k}-(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k}).

Proof.

First, consider the sum

\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{n}}^{\beta_{i}}.

Fix
t\in{\left\{0,\dots,k\right\}}
. Notice that the number of terms of the form
f_{n}^{t}(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k-t}
corresponds to the number of vectors
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})
having
t
entries equal to
1
. To count the number of such vectors, we can think of
{\left\{1,\dots,k\right\}}
as the set of indices, and we are selecting
t
of them to assign the value
1
. There are exactly
\binom{k}{t}
ways to do this. Hence,

\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{n}}^{\beta_{i}}=\sum_{t=0}^{k}\binom{k}{t}f_{n}^{t}(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k-t}.

By the binomial theorem,

\sum_{t=0}^{k}\binom{k}{t}f_{n}^{t}(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k-t}=(f_{n}+(\sigma_{n}-f_{n}))^{k}=\sigma_{n}^{k}.

Now, considering the term
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})=(-1,\dots,-1)
separately, we obtaining:

\sum_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in{\left\{\pm 1\right\}}^{k}\setminus{\left\{(-1,\dots,-1)\right\}}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{f_{n}}^{\beta}_{i}=\sigma_{n}^{k}-(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k}.

Substituting this result into
Equation

7
,

f_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}D_{S}(k)\sigma_{n}^{d-k}(\sigma_{n}^{k}-(\sigma_{n}-f_{n})^{k})

and the result follows.
∎

Dividing
Equation

8
by
\sigma_{n+1}
, using its recurrence formula
\sigma_{n+1}=\sigma_{n}^{d}\,\,\#S
and replacing
\frac{f_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}
with
p_{n}
, we obtain

(9)

p_{n+1}=\frac{f_{n+1}}{\sigma_{n+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}\left(1-\left(1-p_{n}\right)^{k}\right).

Definition 3.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
. We define the characteristic polynomial of
S
as

f_{S}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}\left(1-\left(1-x\right)^{k}\right).

Remark 3.5
.

Using
3.4
and
Equation

9
we conclude that

p_{n+1}=f_{S}(p_{n}).

Taking limit when
n\rightarrow+\infty
, we deduce that
\FPP(W_{S})
is a fixed point of the characteristic polynomial of
f_{S}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
. The characteristic polynomial
f_{S}
depends only on the conjugacy class of
S
in
\Sym(d)
.

Proof.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a set in
\Sym(d)
such that
S^{\prime}=gSg^{-1}
for some
g\in\Sym(d)
. For
x\in{\left\{1\dots,d\right\}}
and
s\in S
, observe that
x
is fixed by
gsg^{-1}
if and only if
g^{-1}(x)
is fixed by
s
. Since
g
is a permutation, this implies that
D_{S^{\prime}}(k)=D_{S}(k)
for all
k=0,\dots,d
. Consequently,
f_{S^{\prime}}=f_{S}
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
, and
f_{S}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the characteristic polynomial of
S
. Then
f_{S}
has the following properties:

(1)

f_{S}(x)\in[0,1]
for all
x\in[0,1]
and
f_{S}(0)=0
. Moreover, if
\deg(f_{S})>0
, then
f_{S}(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
.

(2)

f_{S}^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
, and if
\deg(f_{S})>0
, then
f_{S}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x\in[0,1)
.

(3)

f_{S}^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for
x\in[0,1]
, and strictly negative in
[0,1)
if
\deg(f_{S})>1
.

(4)

If
f_{S}
is not the identity function, the equation
f_{S}(x)=x
has at most two solutions in
[0,1]
, with
x=0
being one of them.

Proof.

For (1), since
x\in[0,1]
, we have
0\leq 1-(1-x)^{k}\leq 1
, and it equals zero if and only if
x=0
for all
k
. Furthermore,
D_{S}(k)\geq 0
for all
k
, so

0\leq f_{S}(x)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}=1.

The only exception occurs when
\deg(f_{S})<1
, where
f_{S}(x)=0
.

For (2), the derivative of
f_{S}
is

f_{S}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)k}{\#S}(1-x)^{k-1}\geq 0

for
x\in[0,1]
. Moreover, if
\deg(f_{S})>0
, then
(1-x)^{k-1}>0
for
x\in[0,1)
, making
f_{S}^{\prime}(x)
is strictly positive in
[0,1)
.

For (3), the second derivative of
f_{S}
is:

f_{S}^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\sum_{k=2}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}k(k-1)(1-x)^{k-2}\leq 0

for
x\in[0,1]
. If
\deg(f_{S})>1
, then
D_{S}(k)>0
for some
k\geq 2
, and
f_{S}^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
for
x\in[0,1)
.

For (4), we use the strict concavity of
f_{S}
established in (3). Concaveness means that for all
x,y
in the domain and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have

f_{S}((1-\alpha)x+\alpha y)>(1-\alpha)f_{S}(x)+\alpha f_{S}(y).

Suppose that
f_{S}
has another fixed-point different to
0
in
[0,1]
and call
x_{1}
the smallest possible. If
x_{1}=1
we are done, if not, let
y\in(x_{1},1]
,
x=0
and take
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
(1-\alpha)x+\alpha y=\alpha y=x_{1}
. Then,

f_{S}(x_{1})=x_{1}>\alpha f_{S}(y).

Dividing by
\alpha
, we obtain that
y>f_{S}(y)
and this for all
y>x_{1}
, so we at most have two fixed points.
∎

We are now ready to conclude the main result about the fixed-point proportion of
W_{S}
:

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
. Then:

(1)

\FPP(W_{S})
equals the largest fixed point of
f_{S}
in
[0,1]
.

(2)

\FPP(W_{S})
is the solution of a polynomial of degree at most
d-1
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{\#S}\right]
.

(3)

\FPP(W_{S})=0
if and only if
f_{S}^{\prime}(0)\leq 1
and
f_{S}
is not the identity function.

(4)

\FPP(W_{S})=1
if and only if every element in
S
fixes at least one element in
{\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
.

Proof.

For (1), as it was observed in
3.5
, the fixed-point proportion of
W_{S}
is a fixed point of
f_{S}
in
[0,1]
.

If
\deg(f_{S})<1
, then
f_{S}(x)=0
and the only fixed point is zero.

If
\deg(f_{S})=1
, then
f_{S}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{D_{S}(1)}{\#S}\leq 1
. If
f_{S}^{\prime}(0)<1
, then the only fixed point is zero. If
f_{S}^{\prime}(0)=1
, this implies that
D_{S}(1)=\#S
or equivalently that every element in
S
fixes exactly one point. Therefore
\FPP(W_{S})=1
, coinciding with the largest fixed point of
f_{S}
.

If
\deg(f_{S})>1
, then by
3.7
, the function
f_{S}
has at most two fixed points, with
0
as one of them. If
0
is the only fixed point we are done. Otherwise, let
x_{0}>0
be the other fixed point. Consider
g(x)=f_{S}(x)-x
. Then
g(0)=g(x_{0})=0
and by Rolle’s theorem there exists
c\in(0,x_{0})
such that
g^{\prime}(c)=0
or equivalently that
f_{S}^{\prime}(c)=1
. Since
f_{S}^{\prime\prime}
is strictly negative, then
f_{S}^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing but since
f_{S}^{\prime}
is always positive then
0<f_{S}^{\prime}(x_{0})<f_{S}^{\prime}(c)=1<f_{S}^{\prime}(0)
. This proves that
0
is a repelling fixed point whereas
x_{0}
is attracting, so
\FPP(W_{S})=x_{0}
.

For (2), since
\deg(f_{S})\leq d
and
f_{S}(0)=0
, if
\deg(f_{S})>1
,
\FPP(W_{S})
satisfies the polynomial:

\frac{f_{S}(x)}{x}-1=0,

which has degree at most
d-1
and coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{\#S}\right]
, as
D_{S}(k)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For (3), the direct was already proved in (1). For the converse, if
f_{S}^{\prime}(0)\leq 1
and
\deg(f_{S})>1
, since
f_{S}^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing, then
f_{S}(x)<x
for all
x>0
and the only fixed point is
0
.

For (4),
\FPP(W_{S})=1
if and only if

f_{S}(1)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{D_{S}(k)}{\#S}=1.

Since
\#S=\sum_{k=0}^{d}D_{S}(k)=1
, this implies that
\FPP(W_{S})=1
if and only if
D_{S}(0)=0
, meaning every element in
S
fixes at least one element in
{\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
.
∎

A particular case that we will be of interest in this article is when
S
is a coset in
\Sym(d)
. We first need the following definitions:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a set
Y
via the action
\rho:G\curvearrowright Y
. Let
S
be a set of
G
, an element
s\in S
and
y\in Y
. We denote by
Y^{s}:={\left\{y\in Y:sy=y\right\}}
and by
\Stab_{S}(y):={\left\{s\in S:sy=y\right\}}
.

Remark 3.10
.

If we consider
G=\Sym(d)
with the natural action on
X={\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
and a set
S\subseteq\Sym(d)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#S}\sum_{s\in S}\#X^{s}=\frac{1}{\#S}\sum_{k=0}^{d}\,\,\sum_{s\in S:\#X^{s}=k}k=\frac{1}{\#S}\sum_{k=0}^{d}D_{S}(k)k=\frac{1}{\#S}\sum_{k=1}^{d}D_{S}(k)k=f_{S}^{\prime}(0).

If
S
is a coset, the quantity on the left-hand side can be studied as a generalization of Burnside’s lemma:

Lemma 3.11

(Burnside Lemma for cosets)
.

Let
G\curvearrowright Y
be an action of a finite group
G
over a finite set
Y
. Let
H\leq G
and
A=gH
be a coset of
H
in
G
.

(1)

If
H\curvearrowright Y
acts transitively, then

\frac{1}{\#A}\sum_{a\in A}\#Y^{a}=1.

(2)

Define
Y^{*}:={\left\{y\in Y:\exists h\in H\text{ s.t. }hy=g^{-1}y\right\}}
. If
g\in N_{G}(H)
, the normalizer of
H
in
G
, then

\frac{1}{\#A}\sum_{a\in A}\#Y^{a}=\#(Y^{*}/H).

Proof.

Consider the set
Z={\left\{(a,y)\in A\times Y:ay=y\right\}}
. We can count the elements of
Z
in two different ways. On one hand, we have:

\#Z=\sum_{a\in A}\#Y^{a}.

On the other hand, we have:

\#Z=\sum_{y\in Y}\#\Stab_{A}(y).

Notice that
a\in\Stab_{A}(y)
if and only if
ay=y
. Since
A=gH
, there exists
h\in H
such that
a=gh
, so
ay=y
if and only if
hy=g^{-1}y
. If no such
h
exists, then
\Stab_{A}(y)=\emptyset
, so we can restrict the sum to
Y^{*}
. If
y\in Y^{*}
, and
h_{0}\in H
is an element such that
h_{0}y=g^{-1}y
, then we have a bijection between
\Stab_{H}(y)
and
\Stab_{A}(y)
given by
h\mapsto gh_{0}h
. Therefore

\#Z=\sum_{y\in Y^{*}}\#\Stab_{H}(y).

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, we know that

\#\Stab_{H}(y)=\frac{\left|{H}\right|}{\#\orb_{H}(y)},

so

\#Z=\left|{H}\right|\sum_{y\in Y^{*}}\frac{1}{\#\orb_{H}(y)}.

If
H
is transitive, then
Y^{*}=Y
, and thus

\#Z=\left|{H}\right|\sum_{B\in Y/H}\sum_{y\in B}\frac{1}{\#B}=\left|{H}\right|\#(Y/H)=\left|{H}\right|=\#A.

This gives the formula in the first case.

If
g\in N_{G}(H)
, the action
G\curvearrowright Y
restricts to
H\curvearrowright Y^{*}
. Indeed, if
y\in Y^{*}
, there exists
h_{0}\in H
such that
h_{0}y=g^{-1}y
. Then, for
h\in H
, there exists
h^{\prime}\in H
such that
g^{-1}h=h^{\prime}g^{-1}
. Therefore

g^{-1}(hy)=h^{\prime}g^{-1}y=h^{\prime}h_{0}y=(h^{\prime}h_{0}h^{-1})h(y).

Since
h^{\prime}h_{0}h^{-1}\in H
, we obtain that
hy\in Y^{*}
.

Thus, we can split the sum over the orbits
Y^{*}/H
, obtaining:

\#Z=\left|{H}\right|\sum_{B\in Y^{*}/H}\sum_{y\in B}\frac{1}{\#B}=\left|{H}\right|\#(Y^{*}/H)=\#A\,\,\#(Y^{*}/H).

This gives the formula in the second case.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let

and

. Then the fixed point proportion of

\displaystyle\text{FPP}(W_{\mathcal{P}})=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\mbox{if $\mathcal{P}$ is transitive over $X$,}\\
\alpha\in(0,1)&\mbox{if $\mathcal{P}$ not transitive and $\exists\,\sigma$ with no fixed points}\\

Moreover, the value
\FPP(W_{\mathcal{P}})
is the solution of a polynomial of degree
d-1
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{\left|{\mathcal{P}}\right|}\right]
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}
is a subgroup, the identity is in

so

and therefore

. The case when

follows from the fact that

, so it is a solution of

.

and

is not the identity function. Since

if and only if

is transitive over

.
∎

The main construction

In this section, we will use
Theorem

3.8
to construct a family of regular branch groups whose fixed-point proportion and Hausdorff dimension can be computed explictly.

Given
d\geq 2
and
T
a
d
-regular tree, consider two subgroups
\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
such that
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}
. Define

G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}={\left\{g\in\Aut(T):g|_{v}^{1}\in\mathcal{P},\,(g|_{v}^{1})(g|_{w}^{1})^{-1}\in\mathcal{Q}\text{ for all $v,w\in T$}\right\}}.

In other words, the elements in this group lie in the iterated wreath product
W_{\mathcal{P}}
(see
Section

2
for the definition of
W_{\mathcal{P}}
) and each element has its labels in the same coset of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
. For example, if
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{P}
, then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}=W_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be a
d
-regular tree with
d\geq 2
and
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
. Then, the group
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
contains
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
,

(2)

W_{\mathcal{Q}}\lhd G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
,

(3)

[G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}:W_{\mathcal{Q}}]=[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]
.

Proof.

We start proving that
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is a group. Clearly, the identity element is in
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. Next, suppose
g,h\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. We must verify that
gh^{-1}\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. For
v,w\in T
, by the properties of sections (see
Equation

1
), we have:

\displaystyle(gh^{-1})|_{v}^{1}\,\,((gh^{-1})|_{w}^{1}))^{-1}=(g|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})\,(h|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}\,(h|_{h^{-1}(w)}^{1})\,(g|_{h^{-1}(w)}^{1})^{-1}.

Rewriting, this becomes:

\displaystyle\left[(g|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})\,(h|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}\,(h|_{h^{-1}(w)}^{1})\,(g|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}\right]\left[((g|_{h^{-1}(v)}^{1})(g|_{h^{-1}(w)}^{1})^{-1})\right].

The second term between brackets is in
\mathcal{Q}
since
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
and the first term in brackets is in
\mathcal{Q}
because
h\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}
. This ensures that
gh^{-1}\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. Hence,
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is a group.

We now prove that
W_{\mathcal{Q}}\lhd G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. The proof is similar to the previous calculation. Specifically, we need to show that if
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
and
h\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
then
(ghg^{-1})|_{v}^{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
for all
v\in T
. Indeed,

\displaystyle(ghg^{-1})|_{v}^{1}=g|_{hg^{-1}(v)}^{1}\,h|_{g^{-1}(v)}^{1}\,(g|_{g^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}.

Rewriting, this becomes:

\displaystyle(ghg^{-1})|_{v}^{1}=\left[g|_{hg^{-1}(v)}^{1}\,h|_{g^{-1}(v)}^{1}\,(g|_{hg^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}\right]\,\left[(g|_{hg^{-1}(v)}^{1})\,(g|_{g^{-1}(v)}^{1})^{-1}\right].

The first term in brackets belongs to
\mathcal{Q}
because
\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}
, and the second term in bracket belongs to
\mathcal{Q}
since
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. Thus,
(ghg^{-1})|_{v}^{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
and consequently
W_{\mathcal{Q}}\lhd G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
.

Finally, consider the map

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}/W_{\mathcal{Q}}&\rightarrow\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}\\
[g]&\mapsto[g|_{\emptyset}^{1}].\end{split}

The map is well-defined because if
h\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
and
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
then

[(gh)|_{\emptyset}^{1}]=[g|_{\emptyset}^{1}][h|_{\emptyset}^{1}]=[g|_{\emptyset}^{1}],

and it is in fact a morphism since the root of the tree is fixed by any element.

To show surjectivity, let
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}
. Define
g\in\Aut(T)
such that
g|_{v}^{1}=\sigma
for all
v\in T
. Then,
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
and
[g|_{\emptyset}^{1}]=[\sigma]
.

To prove injectivity, suppose
[g|_{\emptyset}^{1}]=[h|_{\emptyset}^{1}]
. Then:

(g|_{\emptyset}^{1})(h|_{\emptyset}^{1})^{-1}=(gh^{-1})|_{\emptyset}^{1}\in\mathcal{Q}.

Since
gh^{-1}\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, then
(gh^{-1})|_{v}^{1}\in\mathcal{Q}
for all
v\in T
and consequently
gh^{-1}\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
.
∎

Notice that if
d=2
, we can only construct one group, since
\Sym(2)
only has two subgroups. In this case,
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{P}=\Sym(2)
and
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}=\Aut(T)
, that by
Corollary

3.12
has null fixed-point proportion.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T
be a
d
-regular tree with
d\geq 3
and
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
. Then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is a group of finite type of depth
2
and Hausdorff dimension

\mathcal{H}(G)=\frac{\log(\left|{\mathcal{Q}}\right|)}{\log(d!)}.

In particular, its Hausdorff dimension is always positive.

Proof.

By
Section

2.3
, the group
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is regular branch, branching over
\St_{W_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1)
. Furthermore,
\St_{G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}}(1)=\St_{W_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1)
, so

(\St_{W_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1))_{1}\leq_{f}\St_{W_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1)\leq_{f}G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}.

Therefore,
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is regular branch.

To determine the depth of
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, we apply
Theorem

2.7
. First,
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is not an iterated wreath product, so its depth is greater than
1
. Since
\St_{G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}}(1)=\St_{W_{\mathcal{Q}}}(1)
, it follows by
2.5
that the group
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is branching over
\St_{G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}}(1)
. Therefore
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is of finite type with depth
D=2
by
Theorem

2.7
, as
D-1=1
.

Finally, we compute the Hausdorff dimension of
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. By
2.3
, we have
\mathcal{H}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\mathcal{H}(W_{\mathcal{Q}})
. Then, by
2.8
, the result follows.
∎

The next question concerns the level-transitivity of

.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
T
be a
d
-regular tree with
d\geq 3
and
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
. Then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is level-transitive if and only if
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive on
X={\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
.

Proof.

For the direct, let
x,y\in X
. Since
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is level-transitive, there exists
g\in G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
such that
g(xx)=xy
, where
xx
and
xy
represent vertices at level
2
as words in
X
. Denote
a_{0}=g|_{\emptyset}^{1}
and
a_{1}=g|_{x}^{1}
. By the definition of
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, there exists
q\in\mathcal{Q}
such that
a_{1}=a_{0}q
. Since
\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}
, there exists
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}
such that
a_{0}q=q^{\prime}a_{0}
. Thus,
a_{1}=q^{\prime}a_{0}
, or equivalently,
a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}\in\mathcal{Q}
. Furthermore,

a_{1}a_{0}^{-1}(x)=a_{1}(x)=y,

implying that
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive on
X
.

For the converse, if
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive on
X
, then
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is level-transitive and since
W_{\mathcal{Q}}\leq G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is level-transitive as well.
∎

A closed group is said to be
topologically finitely generated
if it contains a finitely generated dense subgroup.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
T
be a
d
-regular tree with
d\geq 2
and
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{Q}\neq\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
or there exists
x\in X={\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
such that
q(x)=x
for all

, then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is not topologically finitely generated.

(2)

If
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive, then
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is topologically finitely generated if and only if
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
has finite index in
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, the group
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
i topologically finitely generated if and only if
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is.

For the first part, consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}:W_{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{Q}^{\prime},\quad g\mapsto\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\prod_{v\in\mathcal{L}_{n}}(g|_{v}^{1})\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\right),

where the outer product is the Cartesian product and the inner product is the product of the sections. The map is well-defined since we are taking the quotient by
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.

The map is clearly surjective. If
\mathcal{Q}\neq\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
, the image is not topologically finitely generated, and thus
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is not topologically finitely generated.

Suppose there exists
x\in X
such that
q(x)=x
for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. Consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}:W_{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{Q},\quad g\mapsto\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(g|_{x^{n}}^{1}\right),

where
x^{n}
represents the vertex at level
n
reached by following the path labeled by
x
at each level.

The map is a morphism since
g(x^{n})=x^{n}
for all
g\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
. The map is surjective, and since
\mathcal{Q}\neq 1
, the image is not topologically finitely generated. Hence,
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is not topologically finitely generated.

If
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive, the result follows directly from
(
RadiFiniteType2025
, Corollary 3)
or
(
Bondarenko2010
, Theorem 1)

∎

Remark 4.5
.

A profinite group is just-infinite if for every closed non-trivial normal subgroup
N\lhd G
, then
[G:N]<\infty
. A profinite group is strongly complete if its profinite completion coincides with the group. By a recent result proved in
(
RadiFiniteType2025
, Theorem 1)
, which applies to all the groups of the form
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
, the following properties are equivalent:

•

G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is topologically finitely generated

•

G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is just-infinite

•

G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is strongly complete

•

\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}

We conclude this section by showing that the fixed-point proportion of the group
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
can be calculated explicitly in terms of its cosets:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
T
be a
d
-regular tree with
d\geq 2
and
1\neq\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}\leq\Sym(d)
. Then

(11)

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{1}{[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\FPP(W_{A}).

In particular,
\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})>0
if and only
\FPP(W_{A})>0
for one coset
A
.

Proof.

Using the map in
Equation

10
, we deduce that cosets of
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
by
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
are of the form

W_{A}:={\left\{g\in\Aut(T):g|_{v}^{1}\in A,\forall v\in T\right\}},

where
A
is a coset of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
. Therefore, if
A,A^{\prime}
are two different cosets of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
, then
\pi_{n}(W_{A})\cap\pi_{n}(W_{A^{\prime}})=\emptyset
for all
n\geq 1
, and

\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\pi_{n}(W_{A})=\pi_{n}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}).

This allows us to split the calculation of
\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})
as:

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})}\right|}=

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{[\pi_{n}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}):\pi_{n}(W_{\mathcal{Q}})]}\,\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(W_{A}):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{\mathcal{Q}})}\right|}.

By
4.1
, we have
[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]=[\pi_{n}(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}):\pi_{n}(W_{\mathcal{Q}})]
and
\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{\mathcal{Q}})}\right|=\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{\mathcal{A}})}\right|
for any coset in
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{1}{[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\#{\left\{g\in\pi_{n}(W_{A}):\text{$g$ fixes a vertex in $\mathcal{L}_{n}$}\right\}}}{\left|{\pi_{n}(W_{A})}\right|}.

The last limit corresponds to our extension of the definition of the fixed-point proportion of sets made in
Equation

6
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{1}{[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\FPP(W_{A}).

∎

5.
Examples

In this subsection, we calculate the fixed-point proportion for explicit cases of level-transitive groups
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
. By
4.6
, we have

\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{1}{[\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{Q}]}\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\FPP(W_{A}),

where each
A
is a coset of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
.

By
4.3
, the subgroup
\mathcal{Q}
must act transitively on
X={\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive, by
3.11
.(1) applied to
\mathcal{P}\curvearrowright X
, we have

f_{A}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\#A}\sum_{a\in A}\#X^{a}=1.

Therefore, by
Theorem

3.8
.(3), we have only two possibilities:

\displaystyle\FPP(W_{A})=\left\{\begin{matrix}1&\mbox{if every element in $A$ fixes exactly one point in $X$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.

In particular
\FPP(W_{\mathcal{Q}})=0
since the identity is in
\mathcal{Q}
.

5.1.
Construction 1

Seeing
X={\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}}
as
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
, the integer numbers modulo
d
, we define

\mathcal{P}={\left\{x\mapsto ax+b:a\in I,b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}},

where
I\leq(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and

\mathcal{Q}={\left\{x\mapsto x+b:b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}}.

Here,
\mathcal{Q}\lhd\mathcal{P}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
is transitive on
X
. By
4.4
, the group
G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
is not topologically finitely generated.

The cosets of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
are the sets

A_{a}={\left\{x\mapsto ax+b:b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}}.

To determine how many fixed points an element of the form
ax+b
has, we use the following lemma:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the equation

\alpha x\equiv\beta\pmod{n}

has a solution if and only if
\gcd(\alpha,n)\mid\beta
and the number of solutions is
\gcd(\alpha,n)
.

Applying
5.1
with
(\alpha,\beta,n)=(a-1,-b,d)
we conclude

\displaystyle\FPP(W_{A_{a}})=\left\{\begin{matrix}1&\mbox{if $\gcd(a-1,d)=1$,}\\
0&\mbox{$\gcd(a-1,d)>1$.}\end{matrix}\right.

Thus,

(12)

\displaystyle\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{\#{\left\{a\in I:a-1\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\}}}{\Phi(d)},

where
\Phi(d)
is the Euler’s totient function.

In the particular case where
I=(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we can write
Equation

12
in a more compact form. This case will be of particular interest in
Section

6
since it corresponds to the Galois group
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},x^{d}+c,t)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Define
\psi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
the function

\psi(d)=\#{\left\{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}:a-1\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\}}.

Then

\psi(d)=d\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right).

In particular,

\frac{\psi(d)}{\Phi(d)}=\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{p-2}{p-1}.

Proof.

We start proving that
\psi
is multiplicative, namely, if
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
are coprime, then
\psi(mn)=\psi(m)\psi(n)
. Indeed, by Chinese remainder theorem, the map

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle x+mn\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\mapsto(x+m\mathbb{Z},x+n\mathbb{Z})

is a ring isomorphism. Thus,
1
is sent to
(1,1)
and units are sent to units. So
a-1
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
\varphi(a)-1=(a_{1}-1,a_{2}-1)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, meaning that both
a_{1}-1
and
a_{2}-1
are units in their respective rings. Therefore,
\psi(mn)=\psi(m)\psi(n)
.

We now compute
\psi(p^{\alpha})
for
p
a prime number. The elements that are not units are of the form
pk
with
k=1,\dots,p^{\alpha-1}
, so
\psi(p^{\alpha})=p^{\alpha-1}(p-2)
.

Combining everything, if
d=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p^{\alpha_{i}}
, then

\psi(d)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p^{\alpha_{i}-1}(p-2)=d\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right).

Finally, since
\Phi(d)=d\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)
, we obtain

\frac{\psi(d)}{\Phi(d)}=\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{p-2}{p-1}.\qed

Thus, in the case where
I=(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we have
\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=0
if and only if
d
is even, since when
d
is odd we have that
2
and
2-1=1
are invertible modulo
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.

5.1.1.
Construction 2

Write
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{Q}\rtimes\Aut(\mathcal{Q})
with the operation

(q,h)(q,h^{\prime})=(q\,h(q^{\prime}),h\,h^{\prime}).

In particular
(q,h)^{-1}=(h^{-1}(q^{-1}),h^{-1})
.

The group
\mathcal{P}
acts on
\mathcal{P}/\Aut(\mathcal{Q})
by left translation. A set of representatives for
\mathcal{P}/\Aut(\mathcal{Q})
is
{\left\{(q,1)\right\}}_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}
.

The action has the following properties:

•

The action is faithful:
Let
(q,h)\in\mathcal{P}
such that

(q,h)(q^{\prime},1)\equiv(q^{\prime},1)\pmod{\Aut(\mathcal{Q})}

for all
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}
. Since
(q,h)(q^{\prime},1)=(q\,h(q^{\prime}),h)
, this means that
q\,h(q^{\prime})=q^{\prime}
for every
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}
. If
q^{\prime}=1
, then this implies that
q=1
and therefore
h(q^{\prime})=q^{\prime}
for all
q^{\prime}
, so
h
is the identity.

Thus, the action is faithful, and
\mathcal{P}
can be seen as a subgroup of
\Sym(\left|{\mathcal{Q}}\right|)
.

•

\mathcal{Q}
acts transitively:
Given
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}
, then
(q_{2}\,q_{1}^{-1},1)(q_{1},1)=(q_{2},1)
.

The cosets of
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}
have the form

A_{h}={\left\{(q,h):q\in\mathcal{Q}\right\}}.

An element
(q,h)
fixes exactly one point in
\mathcal{P}/\Aut(\mathcal{Q})
if and only if there exists a unique
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}
such that

(q,h)(q^{\prime},1)=(q\,h(q^{\prime}),h)\equiv(q^{\prime},1)\pmod{\Aut(\mathcal{Q})},

namely,
q\,h(q^{\prime})=q^{\prime}
has a unique solution.

Given
d\geq 2
, write
d=2^{n}r
with
r
odd and let
\mathcal{Q}=C_{2}^{n}\times C_{r}
, where
C_{m}
is the cyclic group of order
m
. For this choice,
\Aut(\mathcal{Q})\simeq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\times(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is the field with two elements.

Elements
q\in\mathcal{Q}
can be written as
(\vec{x},z)
, where
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
, and elements

as
(A,\alpha)
, where
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. Since the group
\mathcal{Q}
is abelian, we can use additive notation. Then
(q,h)
has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{P}/\Aut(\mathcal{Q})
if and only if there exists a unique

such that

, namely,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\vec{x}+A\vec{x^{\prime}}=\vec{x^{\prime}}\\

or equivalently

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}(A-1)\vec{x^{\prime}}=-\vec{x}\\

This will have a unique solution if and only if

and

Therefore

\FPP(G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}})=\frac{\#{\left\{A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2}):A-1\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\right\}}}{\left|{\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2})}\right|}\,\prod_{p\mid r}\frac{p-2}{p-1}.

Notice that this is a generalization of construction 1 since when

{\left\{x\mapsto x+b:b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}}

In the case of

6.
Application to iterated Galois groups

In this subsection, we will demonstrate that Construction 1 in
Section

5
corresponds to the iterated Galois group
G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)
where
K
is any field,
t
is transcendental over
K
and
c\in K^{\times}
such that
0
is wandering for
f
(namely,
f^{n}(0)\neq f^{m}(0)
for all
n\neq m
).

Proof of
1
.

We start examining the solutions of
x^{d}+c=t
. These solutions are of the form
\xi^{x_{1}}\sqrt[d]{t-c}
where
\xi
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity, and
x_{1}=1,\dots,d
. Denote these solutions by
\alpha_{x_{1}}
. On the second level of the tree, we consider the preimages of each
\alpha_{x_{1}}
. These preimages are given by

\alpha_{x_{1}x_{2}}=\xi^{x_{2}}\sqrt[d]{\alpha_{x_{1}}-c}.

Continuing in this manner, at level
n
, the solutions are of the form
\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}
, where

\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}=\xi^{x_{n}}\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}d}

and

\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}d}=\sqrt[d]{\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}}-c}.

In the
d
-regular tree, we place
\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}
in the
n
-th level of the tree, ordered by lexicographical order. Notice that all preimages in the same level are different by
2.12
.

To calculate the Galois group
G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)
, we rely on the following theorem:

Theorem 6.1

(
(
Juul2014
, Theorem 3.1)
)
.

Let
k
be a field and
f\in k(x)
a rational function with degree
d\geq 2
such that
f^{\prime}\neq 0
. Let
t
be transcendental over
k
, write
L=k(t)
and
L_{\infty}=L\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f^{-n}(t)\right)
. Let
C
denote the set of critical points of
f
, write
F=L(f^{-1}(t))\cap\overline{k}
and
\mathcal{Q}=\Gal(L(f^{-1}(t))/F(t))
. If for any
a,b\in C
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
f^{m}(a)=f^{n}(b)
if and only if
m=n
and
a=b
, then
\Gal(L_{\infty}/F(t))\simeq W_{\mathcal{Q}}
, where
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
is the iterated wreath product of
\mathcal{Q}
.

In our case, the polynomial
f
has only
0
as its critical point, and the orbit of
0
is wandering by the choice of
c
. Thus,
\Gal(K_{\infty}(f)/F(t))=W_{\mathcal{Q}}
, where
F=K_{1}(f)\cap\overline{K}=K(\xi)
, and

\mathcal{Q}=\Gal(K_{1}(f)/F(t))=\Gal(F(\alpha_{d},t)/F(t)).

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
, there exists
b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
such that
\sigma(\alpha_{d})=\alpha_{b}
and then

\sigma(\alpha_{x})=\sigma(\xi^{x}\alpha_{d})=\xi^{x}\alpha_{b}=\alpha_{x+b}.

This implies that
\mathcal{Q}\simeq{\left\{x\mapsto x+b:b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}}
.

Now, consider the monomorphism of groups
\varphi:\Gal(K(\xi)/K)\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
given by
\sigma\mapsto a
such that
\sigma(\xi)=\xi^{a}
and write
I=\IIm(\varphi)
.

If
g\in G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)
, then there exists
a\in I
such that
\sigma(\xi)=\xi^{a}
and given
x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
, there exist
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
such that
\sigma(\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}d})=\alpha_{y_{1}\dots y_{n}}
. Then, if
x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle g(\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}x_{n}})=g(\xi^{x_{n}}\alpha_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}d})=g(\xi)^{x_{n}}\alpha_{y_{1}\dots y_{n}}=

\displaystyle\xi^{ax_{n}}\alpha_{y_{1}\dots y_{n}}=\alpha_{y_{1}\dots y_{n-1}(ax_{n}+y_{n})}.

This shows that the label
g|_{x_{1}\dots x_{n-1}}^{1}=x\mapsto ax+y_{n}
. Notice that the slope of the linear action of the label does not depend on the vertex and it only depends on the element
g
. Therefore, if we call
\mathcal{P}={\left\{x\mapsto ax+b:a\in I,b\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\right\}}
, we obtain that
G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)\leq G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
.

If we take the quotient
G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)/W_{\mathcal{Q}}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)/W_{\mathcal{Q}}=G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t)/\Gal(K_{\infty}(f)/F(t))

\displaystyle\simeq\Gal(K(\xi,t)/K(t))\simeq\Gal(K(\xi)/K(t))\simeq I

and so

[G_{\infty}(K,x^{d}+c,t):W_{\mathcal{Q}}]=\left|{I}\right|=\left|{\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{Q}}\right|=[G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}:W_{\mathcal{Q}}].

Thus,
G_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},f,t)=G_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{P}}
and
\FPP(G_{\infty}(\mathbb{Q},f,t))
is given by
Equation

12
.
∎

7.
Open questions

We conclude this article by posing the following questions, which are considered by the author to be relevant and interesting for a better understanding of the theory of the fixed-point proportion of groups acting on trees:

(1)

2.11
answers the question about the continuity of the function
\FPP
. The next question is about the surjectivity of
\FPP
in
[0,1]
:

Question 7.1
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
T
a
d
-regular tree. Given
\alpha\in[0,1]
, can we find a group
G\leq\Aut(T)
such that
\FPP(G)=\alpha
? Does the same hold if we restrict to topologically finitely generated groups?

If we restrict to level-transitive groups, the answer to
7.1
is no. Indeed, as the fixed-point proportion has to be smaller than the proportion on the first level by the proof of
2.10
, if
F_{1}
denotes the set of elements in
\pi_{1}(G)
fixing at least one vertex in
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, by Burnside’s lemma,

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}\sum_{g\in\pi_{1}(G)}\#(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{g})=\frac{1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}\sum_{g\in F_{1}}\#(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}\left(d+\sum_{g\in F_{1}\setminus{\left\{1\right\}}}\#(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{g})\right)\geq\frac{1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}\left(d+\#F_{1}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d-1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}+\frac{\#F_{1}}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}

Solving for the last term and using that
\pi_{1}(G)\leq\Sym(d)
,

\displaystyle\FPP(G)\leq\frac{\#F_{1}}{\pi_{1}(G)}=1-\frac{d-1}{\left|{\pi_{1}(G)}\right|}\leq 1-\frac{d-1}{d!}.

For example, when
d=2
, we obtain that no level-transitive group can have fixed-point proportion bigger than
1/2
. Moreover, doing the same argument in the second level and inspecting the possible transitive groups
\pi_{2}(G)
, it can be seen that for level-transitive groups in the binary tree, the fixed-point proportion cannot be bigger than
3/8
.

This leads us to rephrase
7.1
in the case of the family of level-transitive groups:

Question 7.2
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
T
a
d
-regular tree. What is the maximum fixed-point proportion for level-transitive groups acting on
T
? Let
\alpha_{d}
be this maximum. Given
\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{d}]
, does there exist
G
a level-transitive group of
\Aut(T)
such that

Elementary symmetric partitions
Cristina Ballantine

Abstract
Let e k ​ ( x 1 , … , x ℓ ) e_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}) be an elementary symmetric polynomial and let λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}) be an integer partition. Define pre k ⁡ ( λ ) \pre_{k}(\lambda) to be the partition whose parts are the summands in the evaluation e k ​ ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) e_{k}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}) . The study of such partitions was initiated by Ballantine, Beck, and Merca who showed (among other things) that pre 2 \pre_{2} is injective as a map on binary partitions of n n . In the present work we derive a host of identities involving the sequences which count the number of parts of a given value in the image of pre 2 \pre_{2} . These include generating functions, explicit expressions, and formulas for forward differences. We generalize some of these to d d -ary partitions and explore connections with color partitions. Our techniques include the use of generating functions and bijections on rooted partitions. We end with a list of conjectures and a direction for future research.



1
Definitions and introduction

The focus of this paper will be integer partitions.
For any set
S
we denote the cardinality of
S
by
|S|
.
Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
be an
integer partition of
n
, that is, a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
. The
\lambda_{i}
are called
parts
. We let

|\lambda|=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}

and

\ell(\lambda)=\ell.

We call
|\lambda|
the
weight
or
size
of
\lambda
, and
\ell(\lambda)
is the
length
. The use of vertical bars for cardinality or weight should be distinguished by context.
Note that if
n<0
then no such partitions exists, while there is a unique partition of
0
, namely the empty partition
\epsilon
.
Also define

\mathcal{P}(n)=\{\lambda\mid\text{$\lambda$ is a partition of $n$}\},

as well as

p(n)=|\mathcal{P}(n)|,

and

\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)\mid\ell(\lambda)\geq k\}.

For example,

\mathcal{P}_{3}(5)=\{(1,1,1,1,1),\ (2,1,1,1),\ (2,2,1),\ (3,1,1)\}.

We will be particularly interested in
binary partitions
, which are those such that all parts are a power of
2
.
Analogous to the notation just defined, let

\mathcal{B}(n)=\{\lambda\mid\text{$\lambda$ is a binary partition of $n$}\},

and

\mathcal{B}_{k}(n)=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(n)\mid\ell(\lambda)\geq k\}.

To illustrate,

\mathcal{B}_{3}(5)=\{(1,1,1,1,1),\ (2,1,1,1),\ (2,2,1)\}.

We will be combining partitions and elementary symmetric polynomials. The
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial
in a set of variables

\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}
is

e_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell})=\text{ the sum of all square-free, degree $k$ monomials in the $x_{i}$}.

Note that if
\ell<k
then there are no such monomials so that
e_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell})
is the empty sum and hence equals
0
. For a less trivial example,

e_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{4}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{4}+x_{3}x_{4}.

Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
with
\ell\geq k
, we define
\pre_{k}(\lambda)
to be the partition whose parts are the summands in the evaluation
e_{k}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
.

To illustrate, if
\lambda=(3,2,1,1)
then

e_{2}(3,2,1,1)=3\cdot 2+3\cdot 1+3\cdot 1+2\cdot 1+2\cdot 1+1\cdot 1

so that

\pre_{2}(3,2,1,1)=(6,3,3,2,2,1).

We call
\pre_{k}(\lambda)
an
elementary symmetric partition
.
Thus we have defined a map

\pre_{k}:\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{P},

where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all integer partitions.

The function
\pre_{k}
was first studied by Ballantine, Beck and Merca
[
BBM24
]
. In particular, they made the following conjecture, among others.

Conjecture 1

(
[
BBM24
]
)
.

For any
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, the map
\pre_{k}:\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
is injective.

Note that
\pre_{1}
is clearly injective since
\pre_{1}(\lambda)=\lambda
for any partition.
The following weaker form of the conjecture was proved in [
[
BBM24
]
], where one restricts
\pre_{2}
to binary partitions. In it,
\mathcal{B}
is the set of all binary partitions.

Theorem 2

(
[
BBM24
]
)
.

For any
n\geq 0
, the map
\pre_{2}:\mathcal{B}_{2}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is injective. ∎

Because of this result, we will focus part of this work on binary partitions.
We will use the notation

\ImP_{k}(n)=\pre_{k}(\mathcal{P}_{k}(n))

and

\ImB_{k}(n)=\pre_{k}(\mathcal{B}_{k}(n))

for the images of the
\pre_{k}
function applied to the full domain of partitions or just the binary partitions, respectively, of length at least
k
.

We will be interested in counting multiplicities in the image sets of
\pre_{2}
. If
\lambda
is a partition and
i
is a positive integer then the

of

Our techniques will include manipulation of generating functions and bijections. For the latter, we modify the notion of a partition as follows.
A

is a partition
\lambda
with one or more parts distinguished and
marked with hats.
These special parts are called the

of
\lambda
.
The position of the roots among the other parts of the same size matters.
For example,

\lambda=(4,3,\hat{3},3,3,\hat{1},1,\hat{1})

has three roots, namely
\hat{3}
and two copies of
\hat{1}
. Furthermore, it is different from the rooted partition

\lambda^{\prime}=(4,3,3,\hat{3},3,\hat{1},1,\hat{1}).

Rooted partitions were introduced by Sagan
[
Sag24
]
in order to give combinatorial proofs of some results of Merca and Schmidt
[
MS18
,
MS19
]
involving the partition, Euler totient, and Möbius functions. We denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{R}_{i}(n)

\displaystyle=\text{ the set of rooted binary partitions of $n$ with a single root $\hat{\imath}$},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{R}_{i,j}(n)

\displaystyle=\text{ the set of rooted binary partitions of $n$ with exactly two roots $\hat{\imath}$ and
$\hat{\jmath}$}.

The reason that rooted partitions will be useful is as follows. Suppose that a part
k
in

\pre_{2}(\lambda)
came from multiplying parts
i
and
j
of
\lambda
. Then one can think of

as being associated with the partition obtained by rooting
\lambda
at
i
and
j
. Thus we can transfer information about the parts of the range partition into properties of doubly rooted partitions in the domain which will be easier to work with.
This will be made precise in Lemma
3
below.

There are two operations which will be useful for us when working with (rooted) partitions.
If
\lambda
and
\mu
are partitions then their

is
\lambda\oplus\nu
which is obtained by, for each

with the string of

, including any

’s which exist. It is important when considering roots to have the parts from
\lambda
before those in

(3,\hat{3},3,2,1,1)\oplus(3,2,2,\hat{1},1)=(3,\hat{3},3,3,2,2,2,1,1,\hat{1},1).

For the second operation, if
m
is a positive integer and
\lambda
is a partition of
n
then we write
m\lambda
for the partition of
mn
whose parts are the parts of
\lambda
multiplied by
m
. If
\mu
is a partition of
n
such that all parts of
\mu
are multiples of
m
, we write
\mu/m
for the partition of
n/m
whose parts are the parts of
\mu

.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. In the next section we will investigate the sequences
m_{i}(\ImB_{2}(n))
for

,
2
, and
4
. We will derive generating functions as well as expressions for both the sequences themselves and their forward differences. Section

Section

A color partition is one where the parts have been assigned certain colors such that the number of possible colors depends on the size of the part. We end with a section of conjectures and a direction for future work.

Binary partitions

It will be useful for our combinatorial proofs to have an interpretation of the number of times

Lemma 3
.

For
k\geq 0

m_{2^{k}}(\ImB_{2}(n))=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\,|\mathcal{B}\mathcal{R}_{2^{i},2^{k-i}}(n)|.

Proof.

Since the map
\pre_{2}
is injective on binary partitions, each part equal to
2^{k}
in
\mu\in\ImB_{2}(n)
comes from multiplying two parts
2^{i}
and
2^{k-i}
in its preimage

\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(n)
. And to prevent double counting we must assume

.
But such a pair can be associated with a rooting of
\lambda
at these two elements, and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 8

Suppose that

Let us end by discussing how our ideas could be extended to other symmetric polynomials.
There are many standard bases for the algebra of symmetric polynomials, for example, the monomial, complete homogeneous, and Schur bases.
For more information about symmetric polynomials, see the texts of Macdonald
[
Mac15
]
,
Sagan
[
Sag01
,
Sag20
]
, or Stanley
[
Sta24
]
.
One could evaluate any of these on a partition and use the summands as parts.
To illustrate, consider the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial

h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell})=\text{ the sum of all degree $k$ monomials in the $x_{i}$.}

As an example,

h_{3}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}^{3}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}.

So for the partition
\lambda=(4,3)
we have

h_{3}(4,3)=4^{3}+4^{2}\cdot 3+4\cdot 3^{2}+3^{3}

and we could define a partition
\prh(3,1)
by

\prh(3,1)=(64,48,36,27).

It may well be that partitions formed from other bases will also have interesting properties.

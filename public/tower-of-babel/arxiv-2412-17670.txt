On the Hopf superalgebra of symmetric functions in superspace
Masamune Hattori, Renta Yagi, Shintarou Yanagida

Abstract
. We introduce a superspace analogue of combinatorial Hopf algebras (Aguiar–Bergeron–Sottile, 2006), and show that the Hopf superalgebra of quasi-symmetric (resp. symmetric) functions in superspace (Fishel–Lapointe–Pinto, 2019) is a terminal object in the category of all (resp. cocommutative) combinatorial Hopf superalgebras. We also introduce a superspace analogue of chromatic symmetric functions of graphs (Stanley, 1995) using the chromatic Hopf superalgebra of two-colored graphs.

Main results

In this paper, we investigate a superspace analogue of Aguiar–Bergeron–Sottile theory,

To conclude this introduction, we would like to discuss the naturalness
of our formulation of combinatorial Hopf superalgebras.
First, as explained above, it naturally extends the combinatorial Hopf algebras of
symmetric functions and chromatic symmetric functions.
Second, our definition of a supercharacter is a natural extension of the notion of
a character for a (non-super) combinatorial Hopf algebra,
viewed from the perspective of supergeometry.
Third, as will be discussed below, our notion of supercharacter has the potential
to contribute to the combinatorial study of symmetric functions on superspaces.
In the non-super case, quasi-symetric functions are useful for studying certain
combinatorial objects related to symmetric functions.
As a chromatic analogue, Shareshian and Wachs

For example, Kaliszewski

In the super case, we believe that
a super analogue of quasi-symmetric functions can be introduced.
Our supercharacter may serve as a key concept for computing combinatorial objects in superspace,
such as hook coefficients of chromatic symmetric functions in superspace.

2.
Hopf superalgebra

Let us fix the terminology and notation of what we will call Hopf superalgebras.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a field of characteristic
0
,
and consider linear spaces and linear maps over
\mathbf{k}
.
A superspace means a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded linear space.
For a superspace
V
,
the even and odd homogeneous parts are denoted by
V_{\overline{0}}
and
V_{\overline{1}}
, respectively.
A linear map
f\colon V\to W
between superspaces
V
and
W
is even (resp. odd)
if it preserves (resp. reverses) the parity of elements.
A linear map
f
decomposes as

f=f_{\overline{0}}+f_{\overline{1}}
with
f_{\overline{0}}
even and
f_{\overline{1}}
odd.
This makes the set
\Hom(V,W)
of linear maps into a superspace.
Then, superspaces and linear maps between them form a supercategory
\mathsf{SVec}

in the sense of
[
BE
, Definition 1.1]
.

\mathsf{SVec}
has the standard structure of a monoidal supercategory
[
BE
, Definition 1.4]

with tensor product
(V\otimes W)_{\overline{0}}\coloneqq V_{\overline{0}}\otimes W_{\overline{0}}+V_{\overline{1}}\otimes W_{\overline{1}}

and
(V\otimes W)_{\overline{1}}\coloneqq V_{\overline{0}}\otimes W_{\overline{1}}+V_{\overline{1}}\otimes W_{\overline{0}}

for superspaces
V,W
,
and
(f\otimes g)(v\otimes w)\coloneqq(-1)^{\left|g\right|\left|v\right|}f(v)\otimes g(w)

for linear maps
f,g
and
v\in V
,
w\in W
.
Here and hereafter we denote by
\left|x\right|\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
the parity of the object
x
.
Note that the composition of tensor products of linear maps has a sign:

(f\otimes g)\circ(h\otimes i)=(-1)^{\left|g\right|\left|h\right|}(f\circ h)\otimes(g\circ i)
.

Given a monoidal supercategory
\mathsf{C}
,
one has the notions of a
superalgebra object
,
of a (
super
-)
coalgebra object
,
and of a (
super
-)
bialgebra object
in
\mathsf{C}
.
Now, let us introduce:

Definition 2.1
.

A
Hopf superalgebra

H
(over
\mathbf{k}
) is a Hopf algebra object
in the monoidal supercategory
\mathsf{SVec}
.

The monoidal supercategory
\mathsf{SVec}
is symmetric
with braiding
u\otimes v\mapsto(-1)^{\left|u\right|\left|v\right|}v\otimes u
.
Hence, we have the notions of a
commutative
Hopf superalgebra and
of a
cocommutative
Hopf superalgebra.

In preparation for
§​

4
, let us also introduce:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H
be a Hopf superalgebra.

(1)

H
is called
graded
if it has an additional
\mathbb{N}
-grading denoted as

(2.1)

\displaystyle H=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{k}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(H^{k}_{\overline{0}}\oplus H^{k}_{\overline{1}}).

(2)

H
is called
graded connected
if it is graded,
H=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{k}
,
and
H^{0}=\mathbf{k}
.

(3)

A

of Hopf superalgebras is a morphism of Hopf superalgebras
which preserves the
\mathbb{N}
-grading.

Remark 2.3
.

In this note, we will always denote the
\mathbb{N}
-grading of a graded Hopf superalgebra

by the upper index

, and the parity (
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading)
by the lower index
H_{\overline{*}}
as in (

).

For a graded Hopf superalgebra

H^{o}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(H^{k})^{*}

(H^{k})^{*}\coloneqq\Hom(H^{k},\mathbf{k})

If

[

For the existence of the antipode, see

Definition 2.4

We call the Hopf superalgebra structure on

Hopf dual
of

with

(3.12)

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&(\text{if $\alpha_{k}$ is dotted})\\
0&(\text{otherwise})\end{cases}.

The total degree of
\alpha
is defined to be
\sum_{k=1}^{l}\alpha_{k}
,
where the dotted entries
\alpha_{k}=\dot{n}
are replaced by
n
.
The fermionic degree of
\alpha
is defined to be the number of dotted entries in
\alpha
.

Let
(x;\theta)=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc;\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dotsc)
be the set of infinite supervariables
as before, and let
R
be the ring of formal power series of finite degree in
(x;\theta)
.
An element
f\in R
is called a
quasi-symmetric function in superspace

if for every dotted composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{l})
, all monomials

\theta^{\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}}\dotsm\theta^{\eta_{l}}_{i_{l}}x^{\alpha_{1}}_{i_{1}}\dotsm x^{\alpha_{l}}_{i_{l}}

in
f
with indices
i_{1}<\dotsb<i_{l}
have the same coefficient,
where
(\eta_{1},\dotsc,\eta_{l})=\eta(\alpha)
is given by (
3.12
).

Quasi-symmetric functions in superspace form a linear space, which is denoted by
\mathrm{sQSym}
.
It has a bigrading by total degree
n
and fermionic degree
m

[
FLP
, (5.2)]
,
which is denoted by

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{sQSym}=\bigoplus_{n,m\geq 0}\mathrm{sQSym}_{n,m}.

Then,
\mathrm{sQSym}
is a commutative superalgebra whose parity is the fermionic degree modulo 2:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{sQSym}=\mathrm{sQSym}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathrm{sQSym}_{\overline{1}},\quad\mathrm{sQSym}_{\overline{p}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0,\,m\equiv p\bmod 2}\mathrm{sQSym}_{n,m}\quad(p=0,1).

It has an additional
\mathbb{N}
-grading:

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{sQSym}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{sQSym}^{k},\quad\mathrm{sQSym}^{k}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n+m=k}\mathrm{sQSym}_{n,m}\quad(k\geq 0).

One can see that the graded superspace
\mathbf{\Lambda}
of symmetric functions in superspace
is a subspace of
\mathrm{sQSym}
.

In order to explain the comultiplication
\Delta
of
\mathrm{sQSym}
,
let us introduce a natural basis
[
FLP
, §5.1]
.
For each dotted composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{l})
,
we define the
monomial quasi-symmetric function

M_{\alpha}
by

(3.16)

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}\coloneqq\sum_{i_{1}<\dotsb<i_{l}}\theta^{\eta_{1}}_{i_{1}}\dotsm\theta^{\eta_{l}}_{i_{l}}x^{\alpha_{1}}_{i_{1}}\dotsm x^{\alpha_{l}}_{i_{l}}.

The family
\{M_{\alpha}\mid\text{$\alpha$: dotted compositions}\}
is a basis of
\mathrm{sQSym}
.

Now, the comultiplication
\Delta
is given in terms of
M_{\alpha}
,

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{l})
, as

(3.17)

\displaystyle\Delta(M_{\alpha})=\sum_{k=0}^{l}M_{(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{k})}\otimes M_{(\alpha_{k+1},\dotsc,\alpha_{l})}.

Note that
\Delta
is not cocommutative (see
[
FLP
, Example 5.8]
for an explicit example).
By
[
FLP
, Proposition 5.9]
,
\Delta
is graded with respect to the
\mathbb{N}
-grading
(
3.6
), and is a superalgebra morphism
\mathrm{sQSym}\to\mathrm{sQSym}\otimes\mathrm{sQSym}
.
Since
\mathrm{sQSym}
is connected, i.e.,
\mathrm{sQSym}^{0}=\mathrm{sQSym}^{0}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathrm{sQSym}^{0}_{\overline{1}}=\mathbf{k}
,
by
[
FLP
, Theorem 2.1]
, the bialgebra
\mathrm{sQSym}
has a unique antipode,
and is a Hopf superalgebra. Let us summarize as follows.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
FLP
, §5]
)
.

The space
\mathrm{sQSym}
of quasi-symmetric functions in superspace has
a connected graded Hopf superalgebra structure which is commutative but not cocommutative.
The parity is (
3.14
) and the
\mathbb{N}
-grading is (
3.15
).

4.
Combinatorial Hopf superalgebra

We consider a superspace analogue of a combinatorial Hopf algebra
[
ABS
]

(see also
[
GR
, §7]
).

As before, let
\mathbf{k}
be a field of characteristic
0
.
We denote by
\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]
be the commutative superalgebra over
\mathbf{k}

generated by an odd element
\varepsilon
.
Thus, we have
\varepsilon^{2}=0
and
\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]=\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}\varepsilon
as linear spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
H
be a Hopf superalgebra over
\mathbf{k}
.

(1)

An even superalgebra morphism
\zeta\colon H\to\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]
is called a
supercharacter
of
H
.

(2)

A
combinatorial Hopf superalgebra
is a pair
(H,\zeta)
consisting of
a connected graded Hopf superalgebra
H
and a supercharacter
\zeta\colon H\to\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]
.

(3)

Let
(H,\zeta)
and
(H^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
be combinatorial Hopf superalgebras.
A
morphism

(H,\zeta)\to(H^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
of combinatorial Hopf superalgebras is
a graded even morphism
\psi\colon H\to H^{\prime}
of Hopf superalgebras such that
\zeta=\zeta^{\prime}\circ\psi
.
This yields the
category of combinatorial Hopf superalgebras
.

Note that, for a supercharacter
\zeta\colon H\to\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]
,
we have
\zeta(1)=1
,
\left|\zeta(a)\right|=\left|a\right|
and
\zeta(ab)=\zeta(a)\zeta(b)
for
a,b\in H
.
In particular, for
a,b\in H_{\overline{1}}
, we have
\zeta(a),\zeta(b)\in\mathbf{k}\varepsilon
and
\zeta(ab)=0
.

For example, the Hopf superalgebra
\mathrm{sQSym}
has a supercharacter

(4.1)

\displaystyle\zeta_{Q}\colon\mathrm{sQSym}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]

given by the specialization
x_{1}=1
,
x_{2}=x_{3}=\dotsb=0
and

\theta_{1}=\varepsilon
,
\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}=\dotsb=0
.
The same specialization defines a supercharacter on the Hopf superalgebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}
:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\zeta_{S}\colon\mathbf{\Lambda}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon].

Then, by
Propositions

3.1
and
3.2
,
the pairs
(\mathrm{sQSym},\zeta_{Q})
and
(\mathbf{\Lambda},\zeta_{S})
are combinatorial Hopf superalgebras.

Another example of a supercharacter on
\mathrm{sQSym}
is given by
the specialization
x_{i}=1
,
\theta_{i}=\varepsilon
and
x_{j}=\theta_{j}=0
(
j\neq i
)
for some fixed
i
. The case
i=1
gives
\zeta_{S}
.
Note that the same specialization gives
\zeta_{S}
on
\mathbf{\Lambda}
for any
i
.

As will be shown in
Theorem

4.2
,
and
below,
the supercharacters
\zeta_{Q}
and
\zeta_{S}
are natural from the perspective of
the structure theory of the category of combinatorial Hopf superalgebras.

Now we come to the main statement of this note,
which is a superspace extension of
[
GR
, Theorem 7.1.3]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The pair
(\mathrm{sQSym},\zeta_{Q})
is a terminal object
of the category of combinatorial Hopf superalgebras.
In other words, we have:

Let
A=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}A^{k}
be a combinatorial Hopf superalgebra
with supercharacter
\zeta\colon A\to\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]
.
Then, there is a unique graded even morphism
\Psi\colon A\to\mathrm{sQSym}
of graded Hopf superalgebras
such that
\zeta=\zeta_{Q}\circ\Psi
, where
\zeta_{Q}
is the supercharacter (
4.1
).

For the proof, we need the Hopf superalgebra
\mathrm{sNSym}
of
noncommutative symmetric functions in superspace
(see
[
FLP
, §6]
and
[
AGM
]
for details).
It is defined to be the Hopf dual of
\mathrm{sQSym}
(c.f.
Definition

2.4
).
Precisely speaking, its underlying
\mathbf{k}
-linear space is the bigraded dual of
\mathrm{sQSym}
, i.e.,

\mathrm{sNSym}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n,m\geq 0}\mathrm{sNSym}_{n,m}
with
\mathrm{sNSym}_{n,m}\coloneqq(\mathrm{sQSym}_{n,m})^{*}
.
Also,
\mathrm{sNSym}
has a
\mathbf{k}
-basis
\{H_{\alpha}\}
labeled by dotted compositions
\alpha

which is dual to the family
\{M_{\alpha}\}
of
monomial quasi-symmetric functions in superspace (
3.16
).
By
[
FLP
, Proposition 6.2]
,
\mathrm{sNSym}
is isomorphic (as an algebra)
to the free associative algebra with noncommuting generators

\{H_{1},H_{2},\dotsc,;\widetilde{H}_{0},\widetilde{H}_{1},\dotsc\}
, where we set

(4.3)

\displaystyle H_{r}\coloneqq H_{(r)}\quad(r\geq 1),\quad\widetilde{H}_{s}\coloneqq H_{(\dot{s})}\quad(s\geq 0).

The Hopf superalgebra
\mathrm{sNSym}
is connected graded for the grading

\mathrm{sNSym}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{sNSym}^{k}
with
\mathrm{sNSym}^{k}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n+m=k}\mathrm{sNSym}_{n,m}
.
Note that we have
H_{r}\in\mathrm{sNSym}_{r,0}\subset\mathrm{sNSym}^{r}_{\overline{0}}
(
r\geq 1
)
and
\widetilde{H}_{s}\in\mathrm{sNSym}_{s,1}\subset\mathrm{sNSym}^{s+1}_{\overline{1}}
(
s\geq 0
).

Let us also remark that giving a supercharacter on
\mathrm{sQSym}
is equivalent to
giving a family of functionals in each homogeneous space

\mathrm{sQSym}^{k}_{\overline{p}}=\bigoplus_{n+m=k,\,m\equiv\overline{p}\bmod 2}\mathrm{sQSym}_{n,m}
.
Under this equivalence, the supercharacter
\zeta_{Q}
corresponds to the functionals
H_{r},\widetilde{H}_{s}
.
That is, for
f\in\mathrm{sQSym}^{k}
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\zeta_{Q}(f)=\begin{cases}(H_{k},f)&(\left|f\right|=\overline{0})\\
(\widetilde{H}_{k-1},f)&(\left|f\right|=\overline{1})\end{cases},

where
\left|p\right|
denotes the parity of
f
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

We follow the non-super case
[
GR
, Theorem 7.1.3]
.
First, note that we can decompose the given
\zeta\colon A\to\mathbf{k}[\varepsilon]

as
\zeta=\zeta_{\overline{0}}\oplus\zeta_{\overline{1}}\varepsilon
,
where
\zeta_{\overline{p}}\colon A_{\overline{p}}\to\mathbf{k}
(
p=0,1
) are linear,

\zeta_{\overline{0}}
is even and
\zeta_{\overline{1}}
is odd.
Let us define an even linear map

(4.5)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}\colon\left({\textstyle\bigoplus}_{r\geq 1}\mathbf{k}H_{r}\right)\oplus\left({\textstyle\bigoplus}_{s\geq 0}\mathbf{k}\widetilde{H}_{s}\right)\longrightarrow A^{o}=A^{o}_{\overline{0}}\oplus A^{o}_{\overline{1}},

where
A^{o}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(A^{k})^{*}
is the graded dual
of the graded superspace
A=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}A^{k}
, by

(4.6)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(H_{r})(a)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\zeta_{\overline{0}}(a)&(a\in A^{r}_{\overline{0}})\\
0&(\text{$a$ is in the other homogeneous spaces})\end{cases},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}(\widetilde{H}_{s})(a)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\zeta_{\overline{1}}(a)&(a\in A^{s+1}_{\overline{1}}),\\
0&(\text{$a$ is in the other homogeneous spaces})\end{cases}.

Since
\mathrm{sQSym}^{o}=\mathrm{sNSym}
is the free associative superalgebra

\mathbf{k}\langle H_{1},H_{2},\dotsc;\widetilde{H}_{0},\widetilde{H}_{1},\dotsc\rangle
,
the map
\zeta^{\prime}
extends uniquely to an even morphism
\Psi^{*}\colon\mathrm{sQSym}^{o}\to A^{o}

of superalgebras.
Moreover, the definition of
\zeta^{\prime}
implies that
\Psi^{*}
preserves the
\mathbb{N}
-grading.
Since each homogeneous subspace
\mathrm{sQSym}^{k}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) is finite dimensional,
there exists a unique graded even coalgebra morphism
\Psi\colon A\to\mathrm{sQSym}

which is a graded dual of
\Psi^{*}
.

Then, for
a\in A^{r}_{\overline{0}}
(
r\geq 1
), we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle(\zeta_{Q}\circ\Psi)(a)=H_{r}(\Psi(a))=\Psi^{*}(H_{r})(a)=\zeta_{\overline{0}}(a)=\zeta(a),

where the first equality follows from (
4.4
).
Similarly, for
a\in A^{s+1}_{\overline{1}}
(
s\geq 0
), we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\zeta_{Q}\circ\Psi)(a)=\widetilde{H}_{s}(\Psi(a))\varepsilon=\Psi^{*}(\widetilde{H}_{s})(a)\varepsilon=\zeta_{\overline{1}}(a)\varepsilon=\zeta(a).

Thus, we have a unique graded even coalgebra morphism
\Psi

such that
\zeta=\zeta_{Q}\circ\Psi
.

It remains to show that
\Psi
is an algebra morphism.
Consider the following two diagrams:

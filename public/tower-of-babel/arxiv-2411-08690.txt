Eisenstein classes and generating series of modular symbols in SL N \mathrm{SL}_{N}
Romain Branchereau

Abstract
. We define a theta lift between the homology in degree N − 1 N-1 of a locally symmetric space associated to SL N ​ ( ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R}) and the space of modular forms of weight N N . We show that the Fourier coefficients of this lift are Poincaré duals to certain modular symbols associated to certain maximal parabolic subgroups. The constant term is a canonical cohomology classes obtained by transgressing the Euler class of a torus bundle. This Eisenstein lift realizes a geometric theta correspondence for the pair SL N × SL 2 \mathrm{SL}_{N}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2} , in the spirit of Kudla-Millson. When N = 2 N=2 , we show that the lift surjects on the space of weight 2 2 modular forms spanned by an Eisenstein series and the eigenforms with non-vanishing L L -function.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The work of Kudla-Millson

In a series of influential papers
[
KM86
,
KM87
,
KM90
]
, Kudla and Millson construct (in greater generality) a geometric theta lift relating the cohomology of orthogonal locally symmetric spaces
\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{D}
of signature
(p,q)
to holomorphic modular forms of weight
\frac{p+q}{2}
. The symmetric space
\mathbb{D}\simeq\mathrm{SO}(p,q)/\mathrm{SO}(p)\times\mathrm{SO}(q)
is the Grassmanian of negative
q
-planes in a real quadratic space of signature
(p,q)
, and
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SO}(p,q)
is a torsion-free subgroup preserving an integral lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
.
The central ingredient of their construction is a suitable differential
q
-form
\varphi_{\mathrm{KM}}\in\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\pazocal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})
valued in the space of Schwartz function in
\pazocal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})
. The theta machinery introduced by Weil
[
Wei64
]
produces a theta kernel

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(z,\tau)\in\Omega^{q}(\mathbb{D})^{\Gamma}\otimes\pazocal{M}_{\frac{p+q}{2}}(\Gamma^{\prime}),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},\ \quad\tau\in\mathbb{H}

where
\pazocal{M}_{\frac{p+q}{2}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is the space of modular forms of weight
(p+q)/2
and a certain level
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
depending on the choice of the lattice. This theta kernel can be used to define two lifts. Let
\pazocal{S}_{\frac{p+q}{2}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
be the subspace of cusps forms. On the one hand, the Petersson inner product of a cusp form
f
with the theta kernel is a closed form, which defines a lift

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathrm{KM}}\colon\pazocal{S}_{\frac{p+q}{2}}(\Gamma^{\prime})\longrightarrow H^{q}(\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma};\mathbb{C}).

On the other hand, we can integrate the theta kernel on a cycle
C
in
Z_{q}(\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
of degree
q
to define

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}\colon H_{q}(\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\pazocal{M}_{\frac{p+q}{2}}(\Gamma^{\prime}).

The main feature of this lift is that it has the Fourier expansion

(1.4)

\displaystyle\int_{C}\Theta_{\mathrm{KM}}(z,\tau)=\int_{C}c_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[C,C_{n}]q^{n}

where the
special cycles

(1.5)

\displaystyle C_{n}\in Z^{\mathrm{BM}}_{pq-q}(\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})

are locally finite cycles of codimension
q
in
\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma}
, and
[C,C_{n}]

is the intersection number in
\mathbb{D}_{\Gamma}
between the two cycles
C
and
C_{n}
.

In fact, Kudla and Millson constructed a more general lift between the cohomology of orthogonal locally symmetric spaces and Siegel modular forms, corresponding to the dual pair
\mathrm{SO}(p,q)\times\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. They also provided an analogous lift for locally symmetric spaces attached to unitary groups. Recently, Shi
[
Shi23
]
has considered generalized special cycles on Hermitian locally symmetric manifolds, thus extending the work of Kudla and Millson to new dual pairs. The Kudla-Millson lift has become an important tool in arithmetic geometry, and there is an extensive literature on generalizations and applications of these lifts by many authors.

The aim of this paper is to construct a theta lift for the pair
\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, in the style of the Kudla-Millson lift. It is also motivated by the construction of a similar lift by Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia
[
BCG20
]
. Although the construction is different, we believe that the two lifts are the same. (As we will show, the two lifts agree on cycles attached to tori, which suggests that they are indeed equal.) In any case, the main novelty of the construction in this paper is that its Fourier coefficients have a geometric interpretation, which is not present in the work of Bergeron-Charollois-Garcia.

1.3.
The Eisenstein class

Using the Mathai-Quillen formalism, we construct a closed and
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-invariant form

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)\coloneqq j(h_{\tau},i)^{N}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}}\omega(h_{\tau})\varphi(z,\mathbf{v})\chi(\mathbf{v})\in\Omega^{N}(X)^{\Gamma}\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),

where

\Gamma

.

(See

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SO}(N,N)(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}).}

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau,s)\coloneqq p_{\ast}\left(\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)u^{-s}\right)\in\Omega^{N-1}(S)^{\Gamma}\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),

.

The pushforward (

2.
Locally symmetric spaces and special cycles

2.1.
Locally symmetric space of
\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})

Let
X
be the space of real, non-degenerate and positive-definite quadratic forms on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We view an element
z\in X
as a positive definite symmetric matrix, on which the group
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
acts transitively by sending
z
to
gzg^{t}
. The stabilizer of
z=\mathbf{1}_{N}
is
K=\mathrm{SO}(N)
so that we can identify

(2.1)

\displaystyle X\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}/\mathrm{SO}(N).

It is a Riemannian manifold of dimension
\frac{N^{2}+N}{2}
.
We also have the space
S\subset X
of symmetric matrices of determinant one. Similarly, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle S\simeq\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(N),

and the dimension of
S
is
\frac{N^{2}+N-2}{2}
. The action of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
on
X
restricts to an action of
\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
on
S
.

Let
\rho
denote the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{Q})
on
\mathbb{Q}^{2N}
defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}=(v,w)\longmapsto\rho_{g}\mathbf{v}\coloneqq(gv,g^{-t}w).

It preserves the pairing

(2.4)

\displaystyle B\colon\mathbb{Q}^{N}\times\mathbb{Q}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\qquad B(\mathbf{v})\coloneqq v^{t}w,

and gives a representation
\rho\colon\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{SO}(N,N)
.

2.2.
Special cycles in
S

For
\mathbf{v}=(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{N}
we define the submanifold

(2.5)

\displaystyle X_{\mathbf{v}}\coloneqq\left\{\left.z\in X\right|\ v=zw\ \right\}\subset X.

The submanifold is of codimension
N
in
X
. Note that
X_{\mathbf{v}}
is empty when
B(\mathbf{v})\leq 0
, since
v=zw
implies
0<w^{t}zw=w^{t}v=B(\mathbf{v})
. The submanifold
X_{\mathbf{v}}
is invariant by scalar multiplication
i.e

X_{\lambda\mathbf{v}}=X_{\mathbf{v}}
. Moreover, for any
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle gX_{\mathbf{v}}=X_{\rho_{g}\mathbf{v}}

since
v=zw
if and only if
gv=(gzg^{t})g^{-t}w
.
We can identify
X\simeq S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by the diffeomorphism

(2.7)

\displaystyle X\longrightarrow S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0},\qquad z\longmapsto\left(p(z),\det(z)^{-\frac{1}{N}}\right),

where the projection
p\colon X\longrightarrow S
onto
S
is defined by
p(z)=\det(z)^{-\frac{1}{N}}z
. The inverse of this map is

(2.8)

\displaystyle S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longrightarrow X,\qquad(z_{1},u)\longmapsto z=z_{1}u.

Hence, if we identify
\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
via the exponential map, then we can view
X
as a real vector bundle of rank one over
S
, which is
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-equivariant in the sense that

(2.9)

\displaystyle p(gz)=gp(z).

The image of
X_{\mathbf{v}}
by the projection gives a submanifold

(2.10)

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{v}}\coloneqq p(X_{\mathbf{v}})\subset S

in
S
. Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.9
) we see that we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle gS_{\mathbf{v}}=S_{\rho_{g}\mathbf{v}}

for
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.1
.

The cycle
S_{\mathbf{v}}
only depends on the lines spanned by
v
and
w
.

We identify
X
with the bundle
S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
over
S
. A pair
(z_{1},u)\in S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
lies in
X_{\mathbf{v}}
exactly when
v=uz_{1}w
. By taking the length on both sides we find that
u
is uniquely determined by
z_{1}
. In other words, if we restrict the bundle
S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
to
S_{\mathbf{v}}
, we can view
X_{\mathbf{v}}\subset S_{\mathbf{v}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
as the image of the section

(2.12)

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{v}}\longrightarrow S_{\mathbf{v}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0},\qquad z_{1}\longmapsto\left(z_{1},\frac{\lVert v\rVert}{\lVert z_{1}w\rVert}\right).

We deduce the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

The restriction of the projection
p
to
X_{\mathbf{v}}
is a diffeomorphism onto
S_{\mathbf{v}}
. Hence, the image
S_{\mathbf{v}}
is a closed (embedded) submanifold of
S
.

We can also view the submanifold
S_{\mathbf{v}}
as an embedded copy of a smaller symmetric space
X_{N-1}\coloneqq\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}(\mathbb{R})^{+}/\mathrm{SO}(N-1)
inside

. The setting is similar to
[

,

]
.

First, when
\mathbf{v}=(e_{1},e_{1})
with

the standard basis vector, the submanifold

consists of matrices of the form

(2.13)

\displaystyle z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

where

is a symmetric positive-definite invertible matrix of size
N-1
. Hence, the submanifold

is the image of the embedding

(2.14)

\displaystyle j\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X,\qquad h\mathrm{SO}(N-1)\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{SO}(N).

For general
\mathbf{v}=(v,w)
, let us write
v=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
\underline{v}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and
w=\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}\\
\underline{w}\end{pmatrix}
. First, we suppose that
w_{1}>0
. Let

(2.15)

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{v}}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}/\sqrt{n}&-\underline{w}^{t}/w_{1}\\
\underline{v}/\sqrt{n}&\mathbf{1}_{N-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}

where

, and define the embedding
j_{\mathbf{v}}\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X
by

(2.16)

\displaystyle j_{\mathbf{v}}(h)\coloneqq m_{\mathbf{v}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the inverse of
m_{\mathbf{v}}
is

(2.17)

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{v}}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}/\sqrt{n}&\underline{w}^{t}/\sqrt{n}\\
-\underline{v}w_{1}/n&\mathbf{1}_{N-1}-\underline{v}\underline{w}^{t}/n\end{pmatrix},

so that
m_{\mathbf{v}}e_{1}=v/\sqrt{n}
and
m_{\mathbf{v}}^{-t}e_{1}=w/\sqrt{n}
. Hence, the matrix
m_{\mathbf{v}}
maps the line spanned by

to the line spanned by
v
, and whose dual maps to the line spanned by
w
.

Proposition 2.2
.

When
w_{1}
is positive, the submanifold
S_{\mathbf{v}}
is the image of

(2.18)

\displaystyle p\circ j_{\mathbf{v}}\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow S,

where

is the projection from
X
to
S
.

Proof.

Recall that for any matrix
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
we have
gX_{(v,w)}=X_{(gv,g^{-t}w)}
. Let

(2.19)

\displaystyle g\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&-\underline{w}^{t}/w_{1}\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}

be the matrix such that
g^{-t}w_{1}e_{1}=w
. If
z\in X_{(v^{\prime},w_{1}e_{1})}
, then
gz\in X_{(gv^{\prime},w)}=X_{(v,w)}
where

(2.20)

\displaystyle v^{\prime}\coloneqq g^{-1}v=\begin{pmatrix}n/w_{1}\\
\underline{v}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.

For
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=(v^{\prime},w_{1}e_{1})
, with
v^{\prime}
as above, the submanifold
X_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}
consists of positive definite invertible matrices of the form

(2.21)

\displaystyle z=\begin{pmatrix}n/w_{1}^{2}&\underline{v}^{t}/w_{1}\\
\underline{v}/w_{1}&z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

for some
(N-1)\times(N-1)
-matrix

. In fact, it is the image of the embedding

(2.22)

\displaystyle j_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X,\qquad h\mathrm{SO}(N-1)\longmapsto j_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}(h)\mathrm{SO}(N)

where
j_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}
is the embedding (
2.16
) and for
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=(v^{\prime},w_{1}e1)
the matrix

is

(2.23)

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{n}/w_{1}&0\\
\underline{v}/\sqrt{n}&\mathbf{1}_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}.

Indeed, we see that

(2.24)

\displaystyle j_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}(h)j_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}(h)^{t}

\displaystyle=m_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&hh^{t}\end{pmatrix}(m_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}})^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}n/w_{1}^{2}&\underline{v}^{t}/w_{1}\\
\underline{v}/w_{1}&\underline{v}\underline{v}^{t}/n+hh^{t}\end{pmatrix},

where
\underline{v}\underline{v}^{t}/n+hh^{t}
is positive-definite and invertible.

So

is obtained by translating by

and is the image of the embedding

(2.25)

\displaystyle j_{\mathbf{v}}\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X,\qquad h\longmapsto gj^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}(h)=m_{\mathbf{v}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}

where the matrix
m_{\mathbf{v}}
is

(2.26)

\displaystyle m_{\mathbf{v}}=gm^{\prime}_{\mathbf{v}}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}/\sqrt{n}&-\underline{w}^{t}/w_{1}\\
\underline{v}/\sqrt{n}&\mathbf{1}_{N-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}.

Hence, the submanifold
S_{\mathbf{v}}
is the image of

(2.27)

\displaystyle p\circ j_{\mathbf{v}}\colon X_{N-1}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow S.

∎

Note that
\det(m_{\mathbf{v}})=\det(m_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}})=\sqrt{n}/|w_{1}|
so that

(2.28)

\displaystyle p\circ j_{\mathbf{v}}(h)=\left(\frac{|w_{1}|}{\sqrt{n}\det(h)}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}m_{\mathbf{v}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 2.3
.

If
w_{1}
is nonpositive, then one can compose the embedding with a permutation matrix that will bring one of the positive

2.3.
Quotients

Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an integral lattice
i.e.

B(L,L)\subset\mathbb{Z}
and let
\pazocal{L}=L\oplus L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. Let
\pazocal{L}^{\vee}
be the dual with respect to the bilinear form

\displaystyle Q(\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2})=B(v_{1},w_{2})+B(v_{1},w_{2})

on

that is preserved by a torsion-free subgroup
\Gamma
. Hence, the quotient

(2.31)

\displaystyle X_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash X

is a manifold. The group
\Gamma
only acts on
S
so that we have

where

(2.32)

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash S.

Example
.

(1)

Let

be an integer and let
\pazocal{L}=p\mathbb{Z}^{2N}
. If

is the characteristic function of vector
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in\pazocal{L}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}^{2N}
, then
\chi
is preserved by the principal congruence
\Gamma=\Gamma(p,N)
of matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})
that are congruent to the identity modulo

.

(2)

If
\chi
is the characteristic function of

where

\displaystyle L=\left\{\biggl.v=(v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{N})^{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\ \biggr|\ (v_{1},p)=1,\ v_{i}\in p\mathbb{Z}\ \textrm{for}\ 2\leq i\leq N\right\}

then

is preserved by the subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(p,N)
of upper triangular matrices modulo

.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any positive integer

the set

(2.33)

\displaystyle\left\{\biggl.\mathbf{v}\in\Gamma\backslash\pazocal{L}^{\vee}\biggr|\ B(\mathbf{v})=n\right\}

be a

-basis of

and let

such that

and

. Since

is an integral lattice, we have

, so that

. We have a bijection

(2.34)

\displaystyle\Gamma\backslash\pazocal{L}^{\vee}\longrightarrow g_{0}^{-1}\Gamma g_{0}\backslash\mathbb{Z}^{N}\oplus L^{\prime},\quad(v,w)\longmapsto(g_{0}^{-1}v,g_{0}^{t}w)

where

\displaystyle\left\{\biggl.\mathbf{v}\in\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{Z}^{2N}\biggr|\ B(\mathbf{v})=n\right\}.

\displaystyle\gamma v=\begin{pmatrix}D\\
\underline{0}_{N-1}\end{pmatrix}\in De_{1},

\underline{0}_{N-1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w^{\prime}_{1}\\

Reducing

\displaystyle\rho_{\gamma}\left(\begin{pmatrix}D\\

\underline{0}_{N-2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}^{\prime}\\

\underline{r}\end{pmatrix}\right)

3.
Thom forms and the Mathai-Quillen formalism

Let

be the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}

of

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{so}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

with the equivalence relation

and the projection map

. We view it as a metric bundle with the metric

. The bundle is
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-equivariant, where
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
acts on

by
g[g^{\prime},v]=[gg^{\prime},v]
. If
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})
is a discrete subgroup as previously, then we have a real vector bundle
E_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\Gamma\backslash E
over

, of rank

.

3.1.

Let

be the space of

with boundary operator

Combining with the long exact sequence in relative cohomology, we get the long exact sequence

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i-1}(E_{\Gamma}\smallsetminus DE_{\Gamma})}

The integration along the fibers of the bundle

in

on

, that has integral

along the fibers. The Mathai-Quillen formalism is a canonical way to produce such a representative, that will depend on the choice of a connection on the bundle that is compatible with the metric.

If

3.1.1.
Rapidly decreasing forms.

A function
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is said to be
rapidly decreasing
if for every tuple
m=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v^{m}\phi(v)|<\infty

where
v^{m}\coloneqq v_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots v_{N}^{a_{N}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a function
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the following are equivalent

(1)

The function
\phi
is rapidly decreasing,

(2)

for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
there is a positive constant
C_{M}>0
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\phi(v)|\leq C_{M}\frac{1}{(1+\lVert v\rVert)^{M}}

for every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(3)

for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
there is a positive constant
C^{\prime}_{M}>0
such that for all
v
with
\lVert v\rVert>1
we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\phi(v)|\leq C^{\prime}_{M}\frac{1}{\lVert v\rVert^{M}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi
is rapidly decreasing. By expanding
(1+\lVert v\rVert)^{M}
into monomials we see that
(1+\lVert v\rVert)^{M}|\phi(v)|
is bounded for every
v
by some constant
C_{M}>0
. This proves that
1.
implies
2.
, and it is immediate that
2.
implies
3.
since
(1+\lVert v\rVert)^{-M}\leq\lVert v\rVert^{-M}
.
Suppose that for all
M
we
\phi(v)=O(\lVert v\rVert^{-M})
and let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
. Let
M\coloneqq N\max\{a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\}
. Then by the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|v^{m}|=|v_{1}|^{a_{1}}\cdots|v_{N}|^{a_{N}}\leq\left(|v_{1}|^{2}\cdots|v_{N}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{M}{2N}}\leq\frac{\lVert v\rVert^{M}}{\sqrt{N}^{M}}.

For this
M
and
v
large (
\lVert v\rVert\geq 1
) we then have

(3.8)

\displaystyle|v^{m}\phi(v)|\leq\frac{\lVert v\rVert^{M}|\phi(v)|}{\sqrt{N}^{M}}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{M}}{\sqrt{N}^{M}}.

It follows that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\lVert v\rVert>1\end{subarray}}|v^{m}\phi(v)|<\infty.

On the other hand we have
\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\lVert v\rVert\leq 1\end{subarray}}|v^{m}\phi(v)|<\infty
since
\phi
is continuous.
∎

A smooth function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is in the space
\pazocal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
Schwartz functions
if for every tuple
m=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
the derivative

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{v}^{m}\phi(v)=\partial_{v_{1}}^{a_{1}}\cdots\partial_{v_{N}}^{a_{N}}\phi(v)

is rapidly decreasing. By composition with the diffeomorphism

(3.11)

\displaystyle h\colon D\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad w\longmapsto\frac{w}{\sqrt{1-\lVert w\rVert^{2}}}

from the unit disk
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we obtain a map from
\pazocal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to the space of smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
supported on
D
.

Let
\Omega^{i}_{\textrm{rd}}(E_{\Gamma})\subset\Omega^{i}(E_{\Gamma})
be the complex of differential forms that are rapidly decreasing in the fiber of
E_{\Gamma}
. The pullback by

induces a map

whose image in

3.2.1.
Some operations on vector bundles.

The tautological bundle

\displaystyle\bigwedge E_{\textrm{taut}}=(\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\times_{K}\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{N}=\oplus_{j=0}^{N}\bigwedge^{j}E_{\textrm{taut}}.

A differential

-form on

with values in

[
BGV03

with values in

Let us define

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Omega^{i,j}\coloneqq\Omega^{i}(E,\bigwedge^{j}E_{\textrm{taut}})\simeq\left[\Omega^{i}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\otimes\sideset{}{{}^{j}}{\bigwedge}\mathbb{R}^{N}\right]_{\mathrm{bas}}

\Omega^{\bullet,j}
and
\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}
similarly. We can equip

with the multiplication

(3.17)

\displaystyle\Omega^{i,j}\times\Omega^{k,l}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{i+k,j+l}

\displaystyle(\omega\otimes\nu)\wedge(\omega^{\prime}\otimes\nu^{\prime})\coloneqq(-1)^{jk}(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})\otimes(\nu\wedge\nu^{\prime}).

This makes

an associative bigraded algebra over the ring of differential forms on

. We define a positive definite bilinear form on

by

\displaystyle B(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{j},v^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{l}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{if}\ j\neq l\\

With this bilinear form the splitting

is orthogonal. Furthermore, since

preserves this bilinear form, it induces a bilinear form on the bundle. The algebra structure allows us to define the exponential map

(3.20)

\displaystyle\exp\colon\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet}\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet,\bullet},\qquad\omega\otimes\nu\longmapsto\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{1}{k!}(\omega\otimes\nu)^{k}.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{N}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

of
\{1,\dots,N\}

be the susbpace spanned by the vectors

(3.21)

\displaystyle e_{I}\coloneqq e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{k}}

form an orthonormal basis of

. The Berezinian integral is the projection onto the top dimensional component

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int^{B}\colon\Omega^{i,\bullet}\longrightarrow\Omega^{N}(E),\qquad\alpha=\omega\otimes\nu\longmapsto\omega\otimes B(\nu,e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{N}).

Similarly, we have the projections

(3.23)

\displaystyle\Omega^{i,\bullet}\longrightarrow\Omega^{i}(E),\qquad\alpha=\omega\otimes\nu\longmapsto\omega\alpha_{I}=\omega B(\nu,e_{I}).

For disjoint subsets

let define

by

(3.24)

\displaystyle e_{I_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{I_{k}}=\epsilon(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})e_{I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{k}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{i,\bullet}
and
\beta\in\Omega^{k,\bullet}
. We have

(3.25)

\displaystyle(\alpha\wedge\beta)_{J}=\sum_{I\subset J}\epsilon(I,J\smallsetminus I)(-1)^{k|I|}\alpha_{I}\wedge\beta_{J-I}.

Proof.

We can write

(3.26)

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{I_{1}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}}\alpha_{I_{1}}\otimes e_{I_{1}},\quad\beta=\sum_{I_{2}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}}\beta_{I_{2}}\otimes e_{I_{2}}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\beta

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{1},I_{2}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}}(-1)^{k|I_{1}|}\alpha_{I_{1}}\wedge\beta_{I_{2}}\otimes e_{I_{1}}\wedge e_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{1},I_{2}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}}\epsilon(I_{1},I_{2})(-1)^{k|I_{1}|}\alpha_{I_{1}}\wedge\beta_{I_{2}}\otimes e_{I_{1}\cup I_{2}}.

Taking the

and

∎

3.2.3.
The Mathai-Quillen form.

Let us denote by

(3.37)

\displaystyle U\coloneqq(-1)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int^{B}\exp\left(-2\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-2\sqrt{\pi}\nabla v+R\right)\in\Omega^{N}_{\textrm{rd}}(E)^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}}.

Proposition 3.3
.

The form

\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
-invariant and of integral one along the fibers.

Proof.

See Lemma 3.4 and Proposition 3.5 of
[
Bra23
]

\displaystyle d\int^{B}\exp\left(-2\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-2\sqrt{\pi}\nabla v+R\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=\int^{B}\nabla\exp\left(-2\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-2\sqrt{\pi}\nabla v+R\right)

(3.38)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=\int^{B}(\nabla+2\sqrt{\pi}\iota(v))\exp\left(-2\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-2\sqrt{\pi}\nabla v+R\right)

, where
\iota(v)\colon\Omega^{i,j}\longrightarrow\Omega^{i,j-1}
is a contraction along the section

.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
E_{0}
be the image of the zero section and let
\omega\in\Omega_{c}^{(N^{2}-N)/2}(E)

(3.39)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\omega\wedge U=\int_{E_{0}}\omega.

Proof.

Let
U_{t}
be the pullback of

by multiplication by

in the fibers, so that

. By
[
MQ86
]
(see also Proposition

such that

(3.40)

\displaystyle d\delta_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}U_{t}.

We can write

(3.41)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\omega\wedge U_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{0}}\omega\wedge U_{t}+\int_{E\smallsetminus E_{0}}\omega\wedge U_{t}.

Since the domain of the second integral is

we can use the transgression form to show that the second integral is independent in

\displaystyle\int_{E\smallsetminus E_{0}}\omega\wedge U_{t_{1}}-\int_{E\smallsetminus E_{0}}\omega\wedge U_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{E\smallsetminus E_{0}}\omega\wedge d\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\delta_{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle=0.

In particular, we can take the limit as

that

(3.43)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{E\smallsetminus E_{0}}\omega\wedge U_{t}=0.

∎

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta_{\phi\chi}(g,h)u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{\prime}(g,h)\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi\chi)(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}

(4.36)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}.

Proof.

Since
0
is removed from the summation and since the theta series is absolutely convergent, we can split the theta series as

(4.37)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\phi\chi}(g,h)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},1}}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})+\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},2}}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})+\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v}).

Since
\pazocal{F}_{2}
is the Fourier transform in the variable
w
, we can apply Poisson summation to the second singular sum:

(4.38)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}\\
v=0\end{subarray}}\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}\\
v=0\end{subarray}}\omega^{\prime}(g,h)\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi\chi)(\mathbf{v}).

For the first part, the convergence of the two singular integral follows from Proposition
4.3
. The change of the order of summation and integral for the regular term is justified by Lemma
4.7
. For the second part of the proposition, the order of the integral and sum in the singular terms can be changed for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
by Lemma
4.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
the integral

(4.39)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\right|\frac{du}{u}

converges uniformly for
(g_{1},h)
in a compact subset of
\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

We want to show the convergence of

(4.40)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})\right|\frac{du}{u},

which is equivalent to show the convergence of

(4.41)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})\right|\frac{du}{u}.

Let
A\subset\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a compact set. For any integer
M
we can uniformly bound for any
(g_{1},h)\in A

(4.42)

\displaystyle\lvert\omega(g_{1},h)\phi(\mathbf{v})\rvert\leq C_{M,A}\frac{1}{(1+\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert)^{M}}

where
C_{M,A}>0
is a positive constant depending on
M
and
A
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\omega(g,h)\phi(\mathbf{v})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\frac{1}{(1+\lVert\rho_{u}^{-1}\mathbf{v}\rVert)^{M}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\frac{1}{(1+\sqrt{u^{-2}\lVert v\rVert+u^{2}\lVert w\rVert\rvert})^{M}}

(4.43)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\frac{1}{(u^{-2}\lVert v\rVert^{2}+u^{2}\lVert w\rVert^{2})^{M/2}}

where
\mathbf{v}=(v,w)
. Let
\nu=\mathrm{Re}(s)
and let
M
be large enough so that
M\geq|\nu|
. We can bound the integral by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\right|\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{-\nu}}{(u^{-2}\lVert v\rVert^{2}+u^{2}\lVert w\rVert^{2})^{M/2}}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{M,A}}{2}\frac{1}{\lVert w\rVert^{(M-\nu)/2}\lVert v\rVert^{(M+\nu)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{-\nu/2}}{(\alpha^{-1}+\alpha)^{M/2}}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}

(4.44)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{M,A}}{4}\frac{1}{\lVert w\rVert^{(M-\nu)/2}\lVert v\rVert^{(M+\nu)/2}}\beta\left(\frac{M-\nu}{4},\frac{M+\nu}{4}\right),

where we used the substitution
u^{2}=\alpha\lVert v\rVert/\lVert w\rVert
, and the right-hand side converges since
M-\nu
and
M+\nu
are both positive.

Recall that the Weierstrass
M
-test
[
Rud76
, Theorem. 7.10]
asserts that if a family of function
F_{d}(g_{1},h)
is uniformly bounded by
|F_{d}(g_{1},h)|\leq M_{d}
by a sequence
M_{d}
such that
\sum_{d}M_{d}
converges, then
\sum_{d}F_{d}(g_{1},h)
converges uniformly (and absolutely). When
(g_{1},h)
is in compact set
A
we have

\displaystyle\big|F_{d}(g_{1},h)\big|

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in N_{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega(g,h)\phi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}\Bigg|

(4.45)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{M,A}}{4}\beta\left(\frac{M-\nu}{4},\frac{M+\nu}{4}\right)\frac{|N_{d}|}{d^{\frac{M-|\nu|}{2}}}

where

(4.46)

\displaystyle N_{d}\coloneqq\left\{\left.\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}\right|\ d\leq\lVert v\rVert\lVert w\rVert\leq d+1\right\}.

The cardinality
|N_{d}|
is finite and grows polynomially in
d
. Hence, the sum

(4.47)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{reg}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})\rvert\frac{du}{u}\leq\max|\chi|\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\big|F_{d}(g_{1},h)\big|

converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

The integrals

(4.48)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},i}}|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})u^{-s}|\frac{du}{u}

converge uniformly for
(g_{1},h)
in a compact sets
A\subset\mathrm{SL}_{N}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
when
i=1
(resp.
\mathrm{Re}(s)<-N
when
i=2
).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}=(v,0)
be a singular vector with
v\neq 0
, and suppose that
\nu=\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
. For every integer
M>\nu
we have a positive constant
C_{M,A}
such that

(4.49)

\displaystyle\lvert\omega(g,h)\phi(\mathbf{v})\rvert\leq C_{M,A}\frac{1}{(1+u^{-1}\lVert v\rVert)^{M}}.

Thus, we can bound

(4.50)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi(\mathbf{v})\right|u^{-\nu}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{-\nu}}{(1+u^{-1}\lVert v\rVert)^{M}}\frac{du}{u}=\frac{C_{M,A}}{\lVert v\rVert^{\nu}}\beta(\nu,M-\nu).

The
\beta
-function
\beta(\nu,M-\nu)
converges, since
M-\nu>0
and
\nu>0
. Hence, the sum

(4.51)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})\right|u^{-\nu}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\beta\left(\nu,M-\nu\right)\max|\chi|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in L^{\ast}\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lVert v\rVert^{\nu}}

converges since
\nu>N
.
Similarly, for a singular vector
\mathbf{v}=(0,w)
with
w\neq 0
we get

(4.52)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\pazocal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sing},2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\omega(g,h)\phi\chi(\mathbf{v})\right|u^{-\nu}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,A}\beta\left(-\nu,M+\nu\right)\max|\chi|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in L^{\ast}\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lVert v\rVert^{-\nu}},

where the right-hand side converges for
\nu<-N
.
∎

4.2.
Computation of the partial Fourier transforms

We will need to compute the Fourier transforms of the functions
\phi_{\sigma}
and
\phi_{\sigma_{l}}
that appear in the explicit expressions of the differential forms
\varphi
and
\alpha
. For a vector
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and a function
\sigma
partitioning
\{1,\dots,N\}
let us write

(4.53)

\displaystyle\mu_{\sigma}\coloneqq\mu_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\mu_{N}^{d_{N}}

where
d_{m}=|\sigma^{-1}(m)|
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The partial Fourier transforms of
\phi_{\sigma}
and
\phi_{\sigma_{l}}
are

(4.54)

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=i^{N}2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}(\overline{vi+w})_{\sigma}\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right)

and

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma_{l}})(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=-i^{N}2^{N-1}\pi^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\left[\frac{2\pi|v_{l}i+w_{l}|^{2}+|\sigma_{l}^{-1}(l)|}{2\pi\overline{(v_{l}i+w_{l})}}\right]

(4.55)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\overline{(vi+w)}_{\sigma_{l}}\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right).

In particular, we have
\pazocal{F}_{1}(\varphi)(0)=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\varphi)(0)=\pazocal{F}_{1}(\alpha)(0)=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\alpha)(0)=0
so that the theta series
\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,h)
and
\Theta_{\alpha\chi}(z,h)
are rapidly decreasing at
0
and
\infty
.

Proof.

The proposition follows from the computation of the partial Fourier transform of

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}(v,w)

\displaystyle=H_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{\pi}(v+w)\right)\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right)

(4.56)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=1}^{N}H_{d_{m}}\left(\sqrt{\pi}(v_{m}+w_{m})\right)\exp\left(-\pi v_{m}^{2}-\pi w_{m}^{2}\right).

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
we have the generating series

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}+2\sqrt{\pi}(x+y)t-t^{2}\right)=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}H_{d}(\sqrt{\pi}(x+y))e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}}\frac{t^{d}}{d!}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}+2\sqrt{\pi}(x+y)t-t^{2}}\right)e^{2i\pi yy^{\prime}}dy

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=e^{-\pi(x^{2}+(y^{\prime})^{2})+2\sqrt{\pi}(x+iy^{\prime})t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\left(\sqrt{\pi}y-t-iy^{\prime}\sqrt{\pi}\right)^{2}}dy

(4.57)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi(y^{\prime})^{2})}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}2^{d}\sqrt{\pi}^{d}(x+iy^{\prime})^{d}\frac{t^{d}}{d!}

where the integral is a Gaussian integral equal to
1
. Hence, the partial Fourier transform of
H_{d}(\sqrt{\pi}(x+y))e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}}
is

(4.58)

\displaystyle 2^{d}\sqrt{\pi}^{d}(x+iy^{\prime})^{d}e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi(y^{\prime})^{2}}=i^{d}2^{d}\sqrt{\pi}^{d}\overline{(ix+y^{\prime})}^{d}e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi(y^{\prime})^{2}},

and the partial Fourier transform of
\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v})
is

(4.59)

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(\mathbf{v})=i^{N}2^{N}\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right)\prod_{m=1}^{N}\overline{(iv_{m}+w_{m})}^{d_{m}},

where
N=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{N}
.

Recall that

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=(v_{l}-w_{l})H_{\sigma_{l}}(\sqrt{\pi}(v+w))\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right)

(4.60)

\displaystyle=(v_{l}-w_{l})\prod_{m=1}^{N}H_{|\sigma_{l}^{-1}(m)|}(\sqrt{\pi}(v_{m}+w_{m}))\exp\left(-\pi v_{m}^{2}-\pi w_{m}^{2}\right)).

The partial Fourier transform is computed separately for each factor of the product. For the factors
m\neq l
, we use the previous computations for
\phi_{\sigma}
. For the factor
m=l
, we need to compute the partial Fourier transform of
(x-y)H_{d}(\sqrt{\pi}(x+y))e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}}
. In general, the transform of function of the form
(x-y)f(y)
is

(4.61)

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{2}((x-y)f)(y^{\prime})=\left(x+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\prime}}\right)\pazocal{F}_{2}(f)(y^{\prime}).

One can check that the partial Fourier transform of
(x-y)H_{d}(\sqrt{\pi}(x+y))e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi y^{2}}
is

(4.62)

\displaystyle-i^{d+1}2^{d}\sqrt{\pi}^{d}\left[\frac{2\pi|ix+y^{\prime}|^{2}+d}{2\pi\overline{(ix+y^{\prime})}}\right]\overline{(ix+y^{\prime})}^{d}e^{-\pi x^{2}-\pi(y^{\prime})^{2}}.

For the last statement, note that

(4.63)

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}(v,w)=\phi_{\sigma}(w,v),\qquad\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(v,w)=-\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(w,v)

We deduce that

(4.64)

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{1}(\phi_{\sigma})(v,w)=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(w,v),\qquad\pazocal{F}_{1}(\phi_{\sigma_{l}})(v,w)=-\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma_{l}})(w,v).

In particular, it follows that
\pazocal{F}_{1}(\varphi)(0)=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\varphi)(0)
and
\pazocal{F}_{1}(\alpha)(0)=-\pazocal{F}_{2}(\alpha)(0)
. The vanishing of
\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(0)
is clear. The vanishing of
\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma_{l}})(0)
is also clear when
N>1
since

(4.65)

\displaystyle\overline{(vi+w)}_{\sigma_{l}}=(v_{l}i+w_{l})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq l\end{subarray}}^{N}(v_{m}i+w_{m})^{|\sigma_{l}^{-1}(m)|}

vanishes at
\mathbf{v}=0
. When
N=1
then
l=1
and
\sigma_{l}
is a function on an empty set. Hence, the form is

(4.66)

\displaystyle\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma_{l}})(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=-2i\pi(vi+w)\exp\left(-\pi\lVert v\rVert^{2}-\pi\lVert w\rVert^{2}\right),

which also vanishes at
\mathbf{v}=0
.
∎

4.3.
Differential forms as theta series

Let us now consider the theta series for the Schwartz function constructed in Section
3
. As previously we let
\chi\in\mathbb{C}[D{L}]
be a test function preserved by a torsion-free congruence subgroup
\Gamma
.

Using the Weil representation, we can write
\phi_{\sigma}(\rho_{g}^{-1}\mathbf{v})=\omega(g,1)\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v})
so that

(4.67)

\displaystyle\varphi(z,\mathbf{v})=2^{-N}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{\sigma}\omega(g,1)\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v})\otimes\lambda(\sigma)\in\Omega^{N}(X)\otimes\pazocal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})

and similarly

(4.68)

\displaystyle\alpha(z,\mathbf{v})=-2^{-N}\pi^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{\sigma_{l}}(-1)^{l-1}\omega(g,1)\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(\mathbf{v})\otimes\lambda(\sigma_{l}).

Lemma 4.10
.

The functions
\phi_{\sigma}
and
\phi_{\sigma_{l}}
satisfy

(4.69)

\displaystyle\omega(1,k)\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v})=j(k,i)^{-N}\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v}),\qquad\omega(1,k)\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(\mathbf{v})=j(k,i)^{-(N-2)}\phi_{\sigma_{l}}(\mathbf{v})

for
k\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
.

Proof.

Recall the partial Fourier transform intertwines the two representations
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
. Thus, it is equivalent to show that

\phi_{\sigma}
satisfies
\omega^{\prime}(1,k)\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(\mathbf{v})=j(k,i)^{-N}\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(\mathbf{v})
. Let

(4.70)

\displaystyle k=\begin{pmatrix}c&-s\\
s&c\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta)&-\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SO}(2).

We have

(4.71)

\displaystyle(cv+sw)i+(-sv+cw)=(c+is)(vi+w)=j(k,i)(vi+w).

Since
(\overline{vi+w})_{\sigma}
is a polynomial of degree
N
in the entries of the vector
vi+w
, we see that

(4.72)

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}(1,k)\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(v,w)

\displaystyle=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(cv+sw,-sv+cw)=\overline{j(k,i)}^{N}\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})(v,w).

The result for
\phi_{\sigma}
follows from the fact that
\overline{j(k,i)}=c-is=j(k,i)^{-1}
.
The computation is analogous for the function
\phi_{\sigma_{l}}
, noting that
\overline{(vi+w)}_{\sigma_{l}}
is of degree
(N-1)
and that
|j(k,i)|^{2}=1
.
∎

Following the construction of theta series seen in
4.1
, we consider for any
h\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the form

(4.73)

\displaystyle\omega(1,h)\varphi(z,\mathbf{v})=2^{-N}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{\sigma}\omega(g,h)\phi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{v})\otimes\lambda(\sigma)

where
z=gg^{t}
and
\sigma
is a function on
\{1,\dots,N\}
. For
\tau=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}
we now put

(4.74)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq j(h_{\tau},i)^{N}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}}\chi(\mathbf{v})\omega(1,h_{\tau})\varphi(z,\mathbf{v})\in\Omega^{N}(X)^{\Gamma}\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})

where
h_{\tau}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is any matrix sending
i
to
\tau
. We can take

(4.75)

\displaystyle h_{\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{y}&x\sqrt{y}^{-1}\\
0&\sqrt{y}^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

for which
j(h_{\tau},i)=\sqrt{y}^{-1}
. Since we have multiplied by
j(h_{\tau},i)^{N}
, Lemma
4.10
implies that the function is independent of the choice of
h_{\tau}
. For
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}
we have
h_{\gamma\tau}=\gamma h_{\tau}k
for some
k\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
. Thus

(4.76)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\gamma\tau)=j(\gamma,\tau)^{N}\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau),

so the form transforms like a modular form of weight
N
for
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note that the form is not holomorphic but holomorphic in cohomology, as we will see in
4.7

Similarly, we define the theta series

(4.77)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\psi\chi}(z,\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{y}^{-N}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}}\chi(\mathbf{v})\omega(1,h_{\tau})\psi(z,\mathbf{v})\in\Omega^{N}(X)^{\Gamma}\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})

which also transforms like a modular form of weight
N
for
\Gamma^{\prime}
. On the other hand, the transgressed theta series

(4.78)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\alpha\chi}(z,\tau)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{y}^{-(N-2)}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}}\chi(\mathbf{v})\omega(1,h_{\tau})\alpha(z,\mathbf{v})

transforms like a modular form of weight
(N-2)
for
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

4.4.
The Eisenstein class

With respect to the splitting
X\simeq S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
we can write

(4.79)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)=\Theta_{\widetilde{\varphi}\chi}(z,\tau)+\Theta_{\psi\chi}(z,\tau)\frac{du}{u}.

By Proposition
4.9
we have
\pazocal{F}_{1}(\varphi)(0)=\pazocal{F}_{2}(\varphi)(0)=0
. It follows from Proposition
4.3
that
\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)
and
\Theta_{\psi\chi}(z,\tau)
are rapidly decreasing as
u
goes to
0
and
\infty
. Thus, the integral

(4.80)

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau)\coloneqq p_{\ast}\left(\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta_{\psi\chi}(z,\tau)\frac{du}{u}

converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets, by Proposition
4.4
.

Proposition 4.11
.

The form
E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau)
is closed.

Proof.

Since
\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)
is closed and is rapidly decreasing at
0
and
\infty
, Proposition
3.9
implies that

(4.81)

\displaystyle d_{1}E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau)=d_{1}p_{\ast}\left(\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)\right)=p_{\ast}\left(d\Theta_{\varphi\chi}(z,\tau)\right)=0.

∎

We can view
E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau)
as the value at
s=0
of the integral

(4.82)

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau,s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq p_{\ast}\left(\Theta^{\prime}_{\pazocal{F}_{2}(\varphi\chi)}(z,\tau)u^{-s}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta^{\prime}_{\pazocal{F}_{2}(\psi\chi)}(z,\tau)u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}.

It converges absolutely for every
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and as in Proposition
4.5
it converges termwise absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
.

Proposition 4.12
.

For
\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
we can write

(4.83)

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau,s)=\sum_{\sigma}E_{\varphi\chi}(g_{1},\tau,s)_{\sigma}\otimes\left(\iota_{u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}}\lambda(\sigma)\right),

where

(4.84)

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(g_{1},\tau,s)_{\sigma}\coloneqq\Lambda(s)y^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}\\
\mathbf{v}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\pazocal{F}_{2}(\chi)(\mathbf{v})\frac{\left(g_{1}^{-1}(\overline{v\tau+w})\right)_{\sigma}}{\lVert g_{1}^{-1}(v\tau+w)\rVert^{2N+s}}

and

(4.85)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s)\coloneqq\Gamma\left(N+\frac{s}{2}\right)\frac{(-1)^{N-1}i^{N}}{2\pi^{N+\frac{s}{2}}}.

Proof.

Since the integral converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>N
, we can use Poisson summation to write

\displaystyle E_{\varphi\chi}(z_{1},\tau,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta^{\prime}_{\pazocal{F}_{2}(\psi\chi)}(z,\tau)u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}

(4.86)

\displaystyle=j(h_{\tau},i)^{N}\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{2N}}\pazocal{F}_{2}(\chi)(\mathbf{v})\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega^{\prime}(1,h_{\tau})\pazocal{F}_{2}(\psi)(z,\mathbf{v})u^{-s}\frac{du}{u}.

Using the explicit formula for
\psi
in Proposition
3.10
and for
\pazocal{F}_{2}(\phi_{\sigma})
in Proposition
4.9

Corollary 5.1.2

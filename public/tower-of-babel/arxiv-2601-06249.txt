String Theory from Infinite Width Neural Networks
Samuel Frank, James Halverson

Abstract
We realize bosonic string theory with ensembles of infinite width neural networks. The string tension is tuned by the variance of the output weights. The construction provides a new computation of the foundational Virasoro-Shapiro and Veneziano amplitudes as neural network correlators.



I
Introduction

Recent advances in machine learning (ML) theory have motivated an approach to field theory that relies crucially on the defining data of neural networks.

In this Letter we extend the correspondence to quantum gravity by realizing the bosonic string with ensembles of neural networks. We do so by specifying network architectures and probability densities on their parameters, chosen to reproduce the statistics of the free boson and
bc
-ghost system. This description enables a new computation of the Veneziano and Virasoro-Shapiro amplitudes in network parameter space, recovering essential results from string theory that exhibit crossing symmetry, Regge behavior, and softness in the ultraviolet.

The neural network field theory (NN-FT) approach to field theory
Halverson 2021
builds on
an essential ML result
Neal 1995
that many deep neural networks (NN) admit large-
N
limits in which they are draws from Gaussian processes, enabling a theoretical treatment in terms of free field theory. This is the neural network Gaussian process (NNGP) correspondence. The functional form of the network is known as the architecture, and
N
is a measure of its size. For instance,
N
is the width of a feedforward network, the number of channels in a convolutional neural network
Novak et al. 2018
;
Garriga-Alonso et al. 2019
, or the number of attention heads
Hron et al. 2020
in the attention mechanism
Vaswani et al. 2017
that has led to breakthroughs in language models. Many other architectures have been studied
Williams 1996
;
Matthews et al. 2018
;
Schoenholz et al. 2017
;
Jacot et al. 2018
; see
Yang 2019
;
Yang 2020
for a unified perspective. Nevertheless, networks encountered in the wild have finite-
N
, and accordingly have interactions captured by non-Gaussianities
Yaida 2019
;
Naveh et al. 2021
;
Halverson et al. 2021
;
Roberts et al. 2022
.

In
Halverson 2021
it was proposed to use these techniques for field theory, rather than for ML. A number of results are known in this approach, including the origin of symmetries
Maiti et al. 2021
and interactions
Halverson et al. 2021
;
Demirtas et al. 2023
; the relation to Euclidean
quantum
field theories and Euclidean-invariant architectures
Halverson 2021
; a realization of the free scalar
Halverson 2021
, free Dirac spinor
Frank et al. 2025
,
\phi^{4}
theory
Demirtas et al. 2023
, and Yukawa interactions
Frank et al. 2025
; and new interacting supersymmetric theories
Frank et al. 2025
. Neural network conformal field theories were introduced in
Halverson et al. 2025
and conformal defects in
Capuozzo et al. 2025
, relying on the embedding formalism and not restricting the dimension.

Very recently, Robinson
Robinson 2025
introduced NN-FTs that realize the full Virasoro symmetry of 2d CFT in the infinite width limits of certain neural networks. Other classic results include NN-FT realizations of the 2d free boson, the free fermion, and an
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
multiplet.
This work and
Robinson 2025
are largely complementary, but do have some overlapping results. We thank the author for sharing his manuscript while ours was being finalized.

We will develop NN-FT descriptions of the 2d free boson CFT and the
bc
-ghost system. Together, these determine the gauge-fixed Polyakov action of bosonic string theory. We then utilize the new description to compute well-known string amplitudes.

II
The Bosonic String as a NN-FT

We realize the bosonic string as a NN-FT by defining architectures and parameter densities that yield the free boson and
bc
-ghost system in the infinite width limit.

Free Boson
.
We choose an architecture to represent the closed bosonic string embedding
X^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})
in
D
-dimensional Euclidean spacetime. The Euclidean worldsheet coordinates are
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, but we work in complex coordinates with
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
and
\bar{z}=x_{1}-ix_{2}
. The architecture is

X^{\mu}(z)=\frac{C}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{a_{i}^{\mu}}{|W_{i}|}\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(W_{i}z+\bar{W}_{i}\bar{z})+c_{i}\Big),

(1)

where
\mu=1,\ldots,D
labels spacetime directions and the factor
C=\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{\prime}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})\right)/\sigma_{a}^{2}}
normalizes the two-point function.
The parameters are distributed i.i.d. as

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{a}^{2})

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Uniform}[-\pi,\pi]

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Uniform}(B^{2}_{\Lambda}\backslash B^{2}_{\epsilon}),\quad W_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

(2)

where
B^{2}_{\Lambda}\backslash B^{2}_{\epsilon}
denotes the 2d annulus in
\mathbb{C}
of inner radius
\epsilon
and outer radius
\Lambda
. The hyperparameters
\epsilon
and
\Lambda
represent the IR and UV momentum cutoffs, respectively, while
\alpha^{\prime}
appears in the normalization to match the standard string theory convention.

Correlators.

We compute the two-point function
\langle X^{\mu}(z)X^{\nu}(w)\rangle
by averaging over the parameter distributions. Taking expectations over
a
and
c
while using independence along the
i
index yields

\langle X^{\mu}(z)X^{\nu}(w)\rangle=\frac{\sigma_{a}^{2}C^{2}}{2}\;\mathbb{E}_{W}\Big[\frac{\cos[(W\Delta z+\bar{W}\Delta\bar{z})/2]}{|W|^{2}}\Big]\delta^{\mu\nu},

(3)

where
\Delta z:=z-w
and
\Delta\bar{z}:=\bar{z}-\bar{w}
. Define
r:=|\Delta z|
. Then the expectation over
W
becomes an integral involving the Bessel function
J_{0}
:

\displaystyle\langle X^{\mu}X^{\nu}\rangle(r)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\int_{\epsilon}^{\Lambda}d|W|\,\frac{J_{0}(r|W|)}{|W|}\delta^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\delta^{\mu\nu}\Big[-\log r+\log\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}+\dots\Big]\;,

(4)

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. This recovers the standard bosonic string propagator in position space for
1/\Lambda\ll r\ll 1/\epsilon
.
In momentum space with Fourier conventions
F(p)=(2\pi)^{2}\int d^{2}x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}F(x)
:

\langle X^{\mu}X^{\nu}\rangle(p)=\frac{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{p^{2}}\delta^{\mu\nu}\bm{1}_{B^{2}_{\Lambda}\backslash B^{2}_{\epsilon}}\;,

(5)

reproducing the characteristic
1/p^{2}
scaling of the string propagator for
\epsilon<|p|<\Lambda
.
This correlator is the same for all
N
, but by the Central Limit Theorem (see, e.g.,
Demirtas et al. 2023
) higher connected correlators vanish for
N\to\infty
, yielding the required free theory.

How should one understand the string length
\ell_{s}\propto\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
? The constant
C
was carefully chosen with the form above to exactly realize the
\alpha^{\prime}
in (
II
). Without this foresight, one might have instead chosen
C=1
, in which case matching the coefficient of the
\langle XX\rangle
correlator to
\alpha^{\prime}
would yield

\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma_{a}^{2}}{\left(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}\right)}\;.

(6)

The string length is determined in this case by the standard deviation
\sigma_{a}
of the output weights and determines the tension as
T\sim 1/\ell_{s}^{2}
. The only (non-regulator) hyperparameter in the parameter densities,
\sigma_{a}
, determines the only parameter in the physical theory,
\alpha^{\prime}
.

Symmetries and Zero Modes.
Following
Halverson 2021
, rotation and translation invariance on the worldsheet are manifest by the choice of distributions for
W_{i}
and
c_{i}
. Since in string theory spacetime is the target space, symmetries in spacetime are associated with the outputs of the neural network. Spacetime rotational invariance is manifest due to the choice of rotationally invariant Gaussians for
a_{i}^{\mu}
. We include a zero mode as

\tilde{X}^{\mu}(z)=X^{\mu}(z)+X_{0}^{\mu}\,,\qquad X_{0}^{\mu}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2})\;,

(7)

which may be interpreted as adding a Gaussian bias to the architecture.
Under spacetime translations
\tilde{X}^{\mu}\mapsto\tilde{X}^{\mu}+C^{\mu}
the translation may be absorbed into a parameter redefinition
{X_{0}^{\mu}}^{\prime}:=X_{0}^{\mu}+C^{\mu}
. In the limit
\sigma_{0}\to\infty
the density on
X_{0}^{\mu}
is invariant under this transformation, which we will see is one way to obtain spacetime momentum conservation in string amplitudes.

Ghosts
.
We choose the
bc
ghost architectures to be

\displaystyle b(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{\sigma}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\beta_{k}}{\sqrt{|\omega_{k}|}}e^{i\big(\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{k}z+\bar{\omega}_{i}\bar{z})+c_{k}\big)}

\displaystyle c(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{i\sigma}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\chi_{k}}{\sqrt{|\omega_{k}|}}e^{-i\big(\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{k}z+\bar{\omega}_{i}\bar{z})+c_{k}-\phi_{k}\big)},

following
Frank et al. 2025
in defining Grassmann-valued neural networks.
The parameters are distributed i.i.d. as

\displaystyle\beta_{k},\chi_{k}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Grassmann Gaussian}:\;P\propto\exp(\beta_{k}\chi_{k}/\sigma^{2})

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Uniform}[-\pi,\pi]

\displaystyle\omega_{k}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Uniform}(B^{2}_{\Lambda}),\quad\omega_{k}\in\mathbb{C},

where
B^{2}_{\Lambda}
denotes the 2d disk of radius
\Lambda
. The complex frequency
\omega_{k}=|\omega_{k}|e^{i\phi_{k}}
is defined to have phase
\phi_{k}
. The Grassmann weights satisfy
\mathbb{E}[\beta_{k}\chi_{l}]=\sigma^{2}\delta_{kl}
,
\mathbb{E}[\beta_{k}\beta_{l}]=0
, and
\mathbb{E}[\chi_{k}\chi_{l}]=0
, using Berezinian integration.

Computing the two-point function by evaluating first the
\beta
,
\chi
, and
c
expectations yields:

\langle b(z)c(w)\rangle=\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{2i}\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\Big[\frac{e^{\frac{i}{2}(\omega\Delta z+\bar{\omega}\Delta\bar{z})}e^{i\phi}}{|\omega|}\Big]\;.

(8)

Defining
\Delta z=re^{i\theta}
allows us to write

\displaystyle\langle b(z)c(w)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-i\theta}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}d|\omega|\,J_{1}(r|\omega|)=\frac{1-J_{0}(\Lambda r)}{re^{i\theta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z-w}+\dots\;

(9)

with
\mathcal{O}((\Lambda r)^{-1/2})
corrections vanishing as
\Lambda r\gg 1
. Computing the Fourier Transform gives:

\langle bc\rangle(p)=-\frac{2\pi i}{\bar{p}}\bm{1}_{B^{2}_{\Lambda}}\;,

(10)

as desired for the
bc
-ghost system. The
\bar{b}\bar{c}
-system is realized by an independent copy
(\bar{\beta}_{k},\bar{\chi}_{k})
of the Grassmann weights (uncorrelated with
(\beta_{k},\chi_{k})
), and the replacement
\phi_{k}\to-\phi_{k}
in the
c
-architecture. This produces
\langle\bar{b}\bar{c}\rangle(\bar{z},\bar{w})=1/(\bar{z}-\bar{w})+\dots
, with mixed barred-unbarred correlators vanishing.

III
String Amplitudes

Equipped with a new description of the bosonic string, we now utilize it to compute famous string amplitudes.

Virasoro-Shapiro Amplitude
.
The tree-level four-point scattering amplitude for closed string tachyons is:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})=\frac{1}{g_{s}^{2}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\Big\langle\prod_{i=1}^{4}V(p_{i})\Big\rangle\;,

(11)

where
g_{s}
is a hyperparameter corresponding to the string coupling, and
V(p)=g_{s}\,\mathcal{Z}(p)\int d^{2}z\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde{X}(z)}
is the vertex operator with
\mathcal{Z}(p)
a renormalization factor whose importance will be seen in the following. Instead of evaluating the expectation with the gauge fixed Polyakov action, we instead utilize our NN description, integrating with respect to the measure (
31
) together with the Gaussian measure of the zero mode; see Appendix for a full calculation.

Evaluating the expectation of the zero mode
X_{0}
and the output weights
a_{i}^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}

\displaystyle(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\,e^{-\sigma_{0}^{2}(\sum_{i}p_{i})^{2}/2}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{Z}(p_{1})\dots\mathcal{Z}(p_{4})\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})\,\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]\Big)\;,

(12)

where we have used the large
N
limit and used the i.i.d. property to remove the
i
index. We have

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})f(z_{r})f(z_{s})

(13)

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(Wz+\bar{W}\bar{z})+c\Big)}{|W|}\;.

(14)

Splitting the sum and computing the
c
expectation,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{W}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\neq s}\,(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,\frac{\cos\big(\frac{1}{2}(W\Delta z_{rs}+\bar{W}\Delta\bar{z}_{rs})\big)}{|W|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r}\,p_{r}^{2}\,\frac{1}{|W|^{2}}\Big]\;

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{r\neq s}\,(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\log|\Delta z_{rs}|+C(\Lambda,\epsilon,p)\;,

(15)

where
\Delta z_{rs}=z_{r}-z_{s}
and

\displaystyle C(\Lambda,\epsilon,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Big(\sum_{r}p_{r}\Big)^{2}\,\log\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}\Big)-\sum_{r}\,p_{r}^{2}\,\log\Big(\frac{2}{e^{\gamma}}\Big)}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\;.

(16)

We emphasize this splitting of
\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]
into propagator pieces and cutoff-dependent pieces. The latter emerged from contractions of
X(z_{r})
’s from a single vertex operator, which would disappear if we had utilized normally ordered vertex operators with
\mathcal{Z}(p)=1
. Instead, we have foregone normal ordering, choosing instead

\mathcal{Z}(p)=\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma}}{2}\Big)^{\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}/2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{D/8}\;,

(17)

where
\sigma_{\text{eff}}^{2}=\sigma_{0}^{2}+\alpha^{\prime}\log(2\Lambda/\epsilon e^{\gamma})
. This choice has two effects:
to cancel
p_{r}^{2}
-dependent terms in the limit, and to yield a Gaussian normalization.
We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\text{eff}}^{2}(\sum_{r}p_{r})^{2}}{2}}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(18)

and taking
\sigma_{\text{eff}}\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\,\delta^{D}(\sum_{r}p_{r})}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(19)

which is the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude. One may cast this in a form with
\Gamma
-functions by using conformal symmetry to fix
z_{1}=\infty
,
z_{2}=0
,
z_{3}=z
,
z_{4}=1
and evaluating the remaining integral. Alternatively, one may compute the amplitude with
c
-ghost insertions and consideration of their zero modes on the sphere.

Veneziano Amplitude.

Open String NN-FT.

The Veneziano amplitude is the analog of the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude for open strings. Unlike for closed strings, that were defined on
\mathbb{C}
, the worldsheet
\Sigma
of open strings is chosen to be the upper half plane (
UHP
) defined by
\Im z\geq 0
. Since the
UHP
has a boundary
\partial\Sigma
(the real axis), we must enforce a boundary condition on the open string NN-FT. We choose Neumann boundary conditions,

(\partial-\bar{\partial})X^{\mu}_{N}(z,\bar{z})\Big|_{z=\bar{z}}=0\;,

(20)

expressed in complex coordinates, where
\partial:=\partial_{z}
,
\bar{\partial}:=\partial_{\bar{z}}
, and
X_{N}(z,\bar{z})
is a modification of the closed string architecture (
1
) that obeys these boundary conditions:

\displaystyle X^{\mu}_{N}(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[X^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})+X^{\mu}(\bar{z},z)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{\sqrt{2N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{a_{i}^{\mu}}{|W_{i}|}\Big[\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(W_{i}z+\bar{W}_{i}\bar{z})+c_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(W_{i}\bar{z}+\bar{W}_{i}z)+c_{i}\Big)\Big]\;,

(21)

with
z\in\text{UHP}
and parameters are distributed as in (
2
).
Neumann boundary conditions follow from observing that
X^{\mu}_{N}(z,\bar{z})=X^{\mu}_{N}(\bar{z},z)
, so
\partial X^{\mu}_{N}(z,\bar{z})=\bar{\partial}X^{\mu}_{N}(\bar{z},z)
, satisfying the condition (
20
) on
\partial\Sigma
.

Let
G_{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)
be the closed string two-point function (
II
), then

\langle X^{\mu}_{N}(z,\bar{z})\,X^{\nu}_{N}(w,\bar{w})\rangle=G_{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)+G_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\bar{w})
.
Restricting to the real axis, the open-string two-point function is

\displaystyle\langle X^{\mu}_{N}(x)\,X_{N}^{\nu}(x^{\prime})\rangle\Big|_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle=2G_{\mathbb{C}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{\prime}\delta^{\mu\nu}\Big[-\log r+\log\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}+\dots\Big]\;,

(22)

where

x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
r=|x-x^{\prime}|
.

The Veneziano Amplitude.

Now we turn to the calculation of the Veneziano amplitude, defined analogously to the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude (
11
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})=\frac{1}{g_{s}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}\Big\langle\prod_{i=1}^{4}V_{o}(p_{i})\Big\rangle\;,

(23)

where
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is the subgroup of
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
that preserves the boundary of the
UHP
, and the vertex operator
V_{o}(p)=\mathcal{Z}_{o}(p)\sqrt{g_{s}}\int dx\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde{X}_{N}}
, with
\tilde{X}^{\mu}_{N}=X^{\mu}_{N}+X^{\mu}_{0}
(the zero mode
X^{\mu}_{0}
is the same as in the closed string case). The string embedding
\tilde{X}^{\mu}_{N}
is restricted to the real axis.

The calculation of the Veneziano Amplitude proceeds similar to that of Virasoro-Shapiro, with the only substantive change coming from the doubled boundary two-point function (
22
) that causes several coefficients to be scaled by a factor of 2. The final result is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{s}\left(\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}}e^{-\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\text{eff}}^{2}(\sum_{r}p_{r})^{2}}{2}}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}dx_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|x_{r}-x_{s}|^{2\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(24)

where
{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\text{eff}}^{2}=\sigma_{0}^{2}+2\alpha^{\prime}\log(2\Lambda/\epsilon e^{\gamma})
, and the renormalization factors are

\mathcal{Z}_{o}(p)=\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma}}{2}\Big)^{\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}}\left(\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{D/8}\;.

(25)

In the
\sigma^{\prime}_{\text{eff}}\to\infty
limit, we recover

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=\frac{g_{s}\,\delta^{D}(\sum_{r}p_{r})}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}dx_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|x_{r}-x_{s}|^{2\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(26)

which is a standard result. As with the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude, this may be cast in the form of
\Gamma
-functions using conformal symmetry or
c
-ghost insertions.

IV
Discussion

In this Letter, we have extended the NN-FT correspondence to string theory for the first time. Specifically,
we have realized the bosonic string as a NN-FT and utilized this description to provide new computations of famous string amplitudes. The worldsheet embedding
X^{\mu}
and the
bc
-ghost system are realized as infinite width limits of neural network architectures with prescribed parameter distributions.
The Veneziano and Virasoro-Shapiro amplitudes are computed as correlators in network parameter space, recovering the standard results from string theory. Spacetime momentum conservation of the amplitudes emerges naturally from translational invariance of the network arising in the infinite variance limit of a Gaussian-distributed zero mode, or by taking the ratio of momentum cutoffs
\Lambda/\epsilon\to\infty
. The string tension scales with the variance of output weights.

What about other neural network architectures? For simplicity we have chosen a fully connected architecture with a single hidden layer, but many other architectures are possible. For instance, for the free boson one simply needs an architecture with 2d input that in the large-
N
limit is a Gaussian Process with power spectrum
G^{(2)}(p)\sim 1/p^{2}
. Let

X^{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i}a^{\mu}_{i}\varphi_{i}(z)

(27)

be any such architecture where
\varphi_{i}
are neurons of any architecture with
1/p^{2}
power spectrum. Take
a^{\mu}_{i}
are i.i.d. across the
i
index and
\mathbb{E}[a]=0,\mathbb{E}[a^{2}]=:\sigma_{a}^{2}
.
Then
\langle X^{\mu}X^{\nu}\rangle(p)\propto\sigma_{a}^{2}\delta^{\mu\nu}/p^{2}
and therefore
\sigma_{a}
tunes
\alpha^{\prime}
. Doing this concretely would be a different realization of the same GP as (
1
) as
N\to\infty
, but
1/N
corrections may induce architecture dependent non-Gaussianities. It would be interesting to study this for different architectures, including corrections to the Veneziano amplitude.

The free boson architecture
Robinson 2025
is closely related to ours, but puts the input-weight dependence that yields the
1/p^{2}
in the density, rather than our
1/|W|
in the architecture (
1
). This is a simultaneous rescaling of the architecture and probability density that leaves the GP invariant, a mechanism to generate families of theories with the same GP. The two architectures have slightly different neurons
\varphi_{i}
that are related to a seminal ML result
Rahimi and Recht 2007
known as random Fourier features, which have translation-invariant two-point functions.

Several extensions of this work merit exploration. Already in the bosonic string, it should be possible to realize T-duality, D-branes, and the critical dimension; the latter could follow from central charge techniques of
Robinson 2025
. The RNS superstring can likely be realized by incorporating free fermions and the
\beta\gamma
-ghost system. Finite-width corrections induce interactions in the NN-FT that limits to the bosonic string; their relevance for string theory is unclear. Finally, acting with subsequent network layers
\Phi
on our first two layers
X^{\mu}
defines a deep network
\Phi(X^{\mu})
that might be interesting for string field theory, providing a clear motivation for studying deeper architectures.

Appendix A
Virasoro-Shapiro Amplitude

The Virasoro-Shapiro Amplitude is the tree-level four-point scattering amplitude for closed string tachyons. We define it by:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})=\frac{1}{g_{s}^{2}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\Big\langle\prod_{i=1}^{4}V(p_{i})\Big\rangle,

(28)

which depends on the vertex operator

V(p)=\mathcal{Z}(p)\,g_{s}\int d^{2}z\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde{X}(z)}\;,

(29)

where
\mathcal{Z}(p)
is a renormalization factor and
g_{s}
is a hyperparameter corresponding to the string coupling.

Using the NNFT correspondence, (
28
) can be rewritten as an integral over neural network parameters:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int[d\mu_{X}]\;\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\mathcal{Z}(p_{1})\dots\mathcal{Z}(p_{4})\;\exp\Big(i\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{\mu=1}^{D}\,p_{j}^{\mu}X^{\mu}(z_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int d^{D}X_{0}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma_{0}^{2})^{D/2}}\;e^{-X_{0}^{2}/2\sigma_{0}^{2}+i\sum_{j=1}^{4}p_{j}\cdot X_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\exp[-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2}(\sum_{i}p_{i})^{2}]}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int[d\mu_{X}]\;\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\mathcal{Z}(p_{1})\dots\mathcal{Z}(p_{4})\;\exp\Big(i\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{\mu=1}^{D}\,p_{j}^{\mu}X^{\mu}(z_{j})\Big)\;,

(30)

where the integration measure
d\mu_{X}
is given by:

d\mu_{X}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{d^{2}W_{i}}{\text{Vol}(B^{2}_{\Lambda}\backslash B^{2}_{\epsilon})}\,\frac{dc_{i}}{2\pi}\,\prod_{\mu=1}^{D}\frac{da_{i}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{a}^{2}}}\,e^{-(a_{i}^{\mu})^{2}/2\sigma_{a}^{2}}\bigg]\;.

(31)

We now compute expectation with respect to
a
.
Defining
A:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{a}}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})}{N}}
and
f_{i}(z):=\frac{\cos[(W_{i}z+\bar{W}_{i}\bar{z})/2+c_{i}]}{|W_{i}|}
such that the integrand of (
30
) (ignoring the renormalization factors for now) is:

\exp\Big(iA\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\mu=1}^{D}\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{i}^{\mu}\,p_{j}^{\mu}\,f_{i}(z_{j})\Big)=\exp\Big(iA\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\mu=1}^{D}a_{i}^{\mu}S_{i}^{\mu}\Big)\;,

(32)

with
S_{i}^{\mu}:=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\,p^{\mu}_{j}\,f_{i}(z_{j})
. Then, the
a
integral becomes:

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i,\mu}\Big[\frac{da_{i}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{a}^{2}}}e^{-(a_{i}^{\mu})^{2}/2\sigma_{a}^{2}}\,e^{iAa_{i}^{\mu}S_{i}^{\mu}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i,\mu}\int\frac{da}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{a}^{2}}}e^{-a^{2}/2\sigma_{a}^{2}}\,e^{iAaS_{i}^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big[-\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\sigma_{a}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\mu=1}^{D}(S_{i}^{\mu})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\exp\Big[-\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\sigma_{a}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,f_{i}(z_{r})f_{i}(z_{s})\Big]

\displaystyle=\exp\Big[-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,f_{i}(z_{r})f_{i}(z_{s})\Big]\;.

(33)

It is now convenient to define an intermediate variable
Q
by:

Q:=\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})f_{i}(z_{r})f_{i}(z_{s})=\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\frac{\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(Wz_{r}+\bar{W}\bar{z}_{r})+c\Big)\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(Wz_{s}+\bar{W}\bar{z}_{s})+c\Big)}{|W|^{2}}\;,

such that the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\,e^{-\sigma_{0}^{2}(\sum_{i}p_{i})^{2}/2}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\;\mathcal{Z}(p_{1})\dots\mathcal{Z}(p_{4})\,\mathbb{E}_{W,c}\Big[\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})}{2N}\,Q\Big)\Big]^{N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\,e^{-\sigma_{0}^{2}(\sum_{i}p_{i})^{2}/2}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}x_{\alpha}\Big)\;\mathcal{Z}(p_{1})\dots\mathcal{Z}(p_{4})\,[\Phi_{N}]^{N}\;,

(34)

where we have used that the
W
and
c
parameters are drawn i.i.d across the
i
index. By taking the Gaussian limit
N\to\infty
, the integrand of (
34
) is, to leading order in
1/N
,

[\Phi_{N}]^{N}=\exp[N\log\Phi_{N}]=\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})\,\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]\Big)\;.

The expectation
\mathbb{E}_{c}[Q]
is simple because
\mathbb{E}_{c}[\cos(y+c)]=0
for all
y
allows a simplification at an intermediate step. Special care must be taken to separate the sum
\sum_{r,s}=\sum_{r\neq s}+\sum_{r}
before taking expectation with respect to
W
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{W}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s=1}^{4}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,\frac{\cos\big[(W\Delta z_{rs}+\bar{W}\Delta\bar{z}_{rs})/2\big]}{|W|^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{W}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\neq s}\,(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,\frac{\cos\big[(W\Delta z_{rs}+\bar{W}\Delta\bar{z}_{rs})/2\big]}{|W|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r}\,p_{r}^{2}\,\frac{1}{|W|^{2}}\Big]\;.

(35)

The first term in (
35
) gives the standard boson propagator; the second is a trivial logarithmic integral:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{r\neq s}\,(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\,\Big[-\log|z_{r}-z_{s}|+\log\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}\Big)\Big]+\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{r}p_{r}^{2}\,\ln\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{\epsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{r\neq s}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\log|z_{r}-z_{s}|+C(\Lambda,\epsilon,p)\;,

(36)

where we have defined the cutoff-dependent constant:

C(\Lambda,\epsilon,p)=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{r}p_{r}\Big)^{2}\log\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}-\sum_{r}p_{r}^{2}\log\frac{2}{e^{\gamma}}\Big]\;.

(37)

Then, the integrand of (
34
) up to renormalization factors is

\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(\Lambda^{2}-\epsilon^{2})}{2}\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]\Big)=\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\sum_{r\neq s}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\log|z_{r}-z_{s}|\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\log\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}\big(\sum_{r}p_{r}\big)^{2}\Big)\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\sum_{r}p_{r}^{2}\log\frac{2}{e^{\gamma}}\Big)\;.

(38)

We will look at each of these exponential factors individually. The first is simplified as

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\sum_{r\neq s}(p_{r}\cdot p_{s})\log|z_{r}-z_{s}|\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big[\log\Big(\prod_{r\neq s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}/2}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\prod_{r<s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;.

(39)

The second factor combines with the Gaussian involving
\sigma_{0}^{2}
outside the integral to define a new Gaussian
\exp[-\sigma_{\text{eff}}^{2}(\sum_{r}p_{r})^{2}/2]
with (inverse) variance given by

\sigma_{\text{eff}}^{2}=\sigma_{0}^{2}+\alpha^{\prime}\log\frac{2\Lambda}{\epsilon e^{\gamma}}\;.

(40)

The third exponential factor simplifies to:

\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\log\frac{2}{e^{\gamma}}\,\sum_{r=1}^{4}p_{r}^{2}\Big)=\prod_{r=1}^{4}\exp\Big(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{r}^{2}\log\frac{2}{e^{\gamma}}\Big)\;.

(41)

These terms come precisely from the
r=s
case and would not arise if we had utilized normally ordered vertex operators. This may be effectively enforced by choosing renormalization factors

\mathcal{Z}(p)
such that they cancel with (
41
).

\mathcal{Z}(p)=\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma}}{2}\Big)^{\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}/2}\,\Big(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\Big)^{D/8}\;.

(42)

This choice also provides the normalization for the Gaussian that becomes the
\delta
-function below.
Putting it all together, the final result is:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{\text{eff}}^{2}(\sum_{r}p_{r})^{2}}{2}}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;.

(43)

Taking the
\sigma_{\text{eff}}\to\infty
limit,

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=\frac{g_{s}^{2}\,\delta^{D}(\sum_{r}p_{r})}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{C}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}d^{2}z_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|z_{r}-z_{s}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(44)

which is the standard result for the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude; see, e.g., (6.9) of
Tong 2009
. The volume factor may be removed by using conformal symmetry to fix three of the
z
’s, leaving the standard result in terms of
\Gamma
-functions.

Appendix B
Veneziano Amplitude

The Veneziano Amplitude is the tree-level four-point scattering amplitude for open string tachyons. We define it by:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{4})=\frac{1}{g_{s}}\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}\Big\langle\prod_{i=1}^{4}V_{o}(p_{i})\Big\rangle\;,

(45)

where the open string vertex operator

V_{o}(p)=\mathcal{Z}_{o}(p)\,\sqrt{g_{s}}\int dx\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde{X}_{N}(x)}

depends on renormalization factors
\mathcal{Z}_{o}(p)
and the open string
\tilde{X}_{N}(x)
with Neumann boundary conditions evaluated at
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This architecture is given by:

\tilde{X}^{\mu}_{N}(x)=C\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{a_{i}^{\mu}}{|W_{i}|}\cos\Big(\frac{1}{2}(W_{i}+\bar{W}_{i})x+c_{i}\Big)+X^{\mu}_{0}\;,

(46)

where
X_{0}^{\mu}
is a Gaussian distributed zero mode. With these modifications, the calculation of the Veneziano Amplitude mirrors that of the Virasoro-Shapiro Amplitude. The integral over
X_{0}
is the same. The extra factor of
\sqrt{2}
in the architecture (
46
) means that the integral over
a
parameters gives the same answer as in (
33
) but without the factor of 2 in the denominator of the exponent. Then, the calculation of
\mathbb{E}_{W,c}[Q]
proceeds identically, so the end result is that relative to the Virasoro-Shapiro case, there are three places in the final answer where a factor of 2 shows up. One is in the exponent of
|x_{r}-x_{s}|
. Another is in the definition of a new
{\sigma^{\prime}}^{2}_{\text{eff}}=\sigma_{0}^{2}+2\alpha^{\prime}\log(2\Lambda/\epsilon e^{\gamma})
. The third is in the renormalization factors:

\mathcal{Z}_{o}(p)=\Big(\frac{e^{\gamma}}{2}\Big)^{\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}}\Big(\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{D/8}\;,

(47)

where the exponents are
\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}
instead of
\alpha^{\prime}p^{2}/2
. The final expression for the Veneziano Amplitude is therefore:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=\frac{g_{s}\left(\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}^{2}_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}}e^{-\frac{{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\text{eff}}^{2}(\sum_{r}p_{r})^{2}}{2}}}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}dx_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|x_{r}-x_{s}|^{2\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(48)

and taking
\sigma^{\prime}_{\text{eff}}\to\infty
:

\mathcal{A}^{(4)}=\frac{g_{s}\,\delta^{D}(\sum_{r}p_{r})}{\text{Vol}(SL(2,\mathbb{R}))}\int\Big(\prod_{\alpha=1}^{4}dx_{\alpha}\Big)\,\prod_{r<s}|x_{r}-x_{s}|^{2\alpha^{\prime}p_{r}\cdot p_{s}}\;,

(49)

recovering the standard result.

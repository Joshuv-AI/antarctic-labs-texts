AT7867 promotes pancreatic progenitor differentiation of human iPSCs
Authors: Nerea Cuesta-Gomez, Kevin Verhoeff, Nidheesh Dadheech, Rena Pawlick, Braulio Marfil-Garza, Haide Razavy, AM James Shapiro
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryGeneration of pure pancreatic progenitor (PP) cells is critical for clinical translation of stem cell-derived islets. Herein, we performed PP differentiation with and without AKT/P70 inhibitor AT7867 and characterized the resulting cells at protein and transcript level in vitro and in vivo upon transplantation into diabetic mice. AT7867 treatment increased the percentage of PDX1+NKX6.1+ (−AT7867: 50.9% [IQR 48.9%–53.8%]; +AT7867: 90.8% [IQR 88.9%–93.7%]; p = 0.0021) and PDX1+GP2+ PP cells (−AT7867: 39.22% [IQR 36.7%–44.1%]; +AT7867: 90.0% [IQR 88.2%–93.6%]; p = 0.0021). Transcriptionally, AT7867 treatment significantly upregulated PDX1 (p = 0.0001), NKX6.1 (p = 0.0005), and GP2 (p = 0.002) expression compared with controls, while off-target markers PODXL (p < 0.0001) and TBX2 (p < 0.0001) were significantly downregulated. Transplantation of AT7867-treated PPs resulted in faster hyperglycemia reversal in diabetic mice compared with controls (time and group: p < 0.0001). Overall, our data show that AT7867 enhances PP cell differentiation leading to accelerated diabetes reversal.Keywords: human induced pluripotent stem cells, diabetes, islet cell transplant, cell therapy, islet differentiation, pancreatic progenitors, β cells, scalability, vertical wheel bioreactor, bioreactor

Introduction

Islet cell transplantation (ITx) has demonstrated proof-of-concept that β cell replacement therapies have the potential to improve glycemic control in patients with diabetes (Shapiro et al., 2000). Limited donor supply remains a major limitation to widespread ITx and pluripotent stem cells offer a renewable source for the generation of stem cell-derived islets (SC islets) (Verhoeff et al., 2022; Balboa et al., 2022). Pluripotent stem cells can recapitulate embryological development and generate pancreatic progenitors (PP), which can further differentiate in vivo and reverse diabetes (Kroon et al., 2008). Alternatively, PPs can be further differentiated down the β cell pathway and generate SC islets in vitro (Rezania et al., 2014). A major challenge hampering clinical translation of SC islets is the heterogeneity of the cells generated, resulting in proliferative non-endocrine cell populations (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2022; Velazco-Cruz et al., 2019; Augsornworawat et al., 2020). Protocols to improve the efficiency of PP or SC islet differentiation are required to eliminate off-target populations and enable clinical translation.

Cell sorting, disaggregation and re-aggregation, and gene editing have been explored to remove off-target cell populations (Aghazadeh et al., 2022; Veres et al., 2019; Sui et al., 2021; Ben-David et al. 2013), but these approaches result in substantial cell loss and are not economically feasible (Verhoeff et al., 2022). Thus, we believe that optimization of the differentiation protocol to ensure activation of pathways resulting in pancreatic and endocrine commitment is essential. Preliminary studies have suggested that AKT inhibitor AT7867 may induce proliferation of PDX1+NKX6.1+ cells, but the impact on PP maturation or in vivo PP maturation was not evaluated (Kimura et al., 2017).

This study aims to characterize the PP cells generated through the addition of AT7867 to a previously published differentiation protocol at transcript and protein level as well as to evaluate the potential of AT7867-treated PP cells to mature in vivo and reverse diabetes in mice.

Results

AT7867 increases the percentage of PDX1+NKX6.1+ and PDX1+GP2+ cells

We used a previously published protocol (Sui et al., 2021) modified with addition of AT7867 and evaluated the impact of AT7867 on cell composition at stage 4 (Figure 1A). At stage 4, addition of AT7867 resulted in the formation of a homogeneous cell layer while the control cells (−AT7867) showed a monolayer of cells with raised “ribbons” of cells (Figure 1B). Totals of 50.9% (IQR 48.9%–53.8%) and 39.2% (IQR 36.7%–44.1%) of the −AT7867 PPs were PDX1+NKX6.1+ and PDX1+GP2+ compared with 90.8% (IQR 88.9%–93.7%; p = 0.0021) and 90.0% (IQR 88.2%–93.6%; p = 0.0021) in AT7867-treated PPs, respectively. These results have been tested in three independent iPSC lines with similar results; assessment for all stages can be found in Figure S1.

Quantification of the number of cells at the end of PP differentiation showed no differences (−AT7867: 8.7 × 106 cells [IQR 8.1 × 106–9.0 × 106 cells]; +AT7867: 8.5 × 106 cells [IQR 8.3 × 106–9.3 × 106 cells]; p = 0.6991) (Figure 1C). Protein quantification showed no differences in the relative density of KI67 between control and AT7867-treated PPs (−AT7867: 1.07 [IQR 1.02–1.1]; +AT7867: 1.07 [IQR 1.05–1.2]; p > 0.9999) (Figures 1D and 1E). However, the relative density of PDX1 was significantly increased in AT7867-treated PPs compared with control (−AT7867: 3,130 [IQR 1,528–3,294]; +AT7867: 5,975 [IQR 4,667–6,511]; p = 0.0375) (Figure 1F). Increased number of PDX1+ (−AT7867: 43.6% [IQR 38.5%–50.0%]; +AT7867: 86.5% [IQR 81.6%–88.8%]; p = 0.0002), and NKX6.1+ (−AT7867: 42.1% [IQR 37.4%–46.7%]; +AT7867: 88.9% [IQR 85.2%–95.4%]; p = 0.0002) cells with a similar number of KI67+ (−AT7867: 80.9% [IQR 66.7%–92.4%]; +AT7867: 91.0% [IQR 70.9%–95.4%]; p = 0.5054) was confirmed with immunohistochemistry (Figure 1G). No differences were observed in the percentage of KI67+ cells in the PDX1+ population between control and AT7867-treated PPs (−AT7867: 99.55% [IQR 99.07%–99.98%]; +AT7867: 99.16% [IQR98.89%–99.60%]; p = 0.3283; Figure 1I).

On the contrary, expression of pluripotency markers PODXL, ALPL, KIT, FGF4, and LIN28A, and TBX2 was downregulated (p < 0.01). Fold changes and p values can be found in Table S2.

AT7867 treatment generates superior PPs than those obtained through addition of nicotinamide and Sant-1

To study the quality of the PPs generated with AT7867, we compared this population to PPs generated with a protocol that efficiently generates PPs (Balboa et al., 2022) (Figure 3A). Both protocols were able to generate over 90% PDX1+NKX6.1+ (+AT7867: 90.8% [IQR 88.9%–93.7%]; optimized PP: 90.9% [IQR 87.8%–96.5%]) and PDX1+GP2+ cells (+AT7867: 90.8% [IQR 88.9%–93.7%]; optimized PP: 93.6% [IQR 92.0%–95.0%]) with similar proliferation potential (+AT7867: 80.9% [IQR 78.7%–84.1%]; optimized PP: 81.5% [IQR 79.0%–82.9%]). However, there was a significant difference in the number of NKX6.1+CHGA+ cells (+AT7867: 2.7% [IQR 2.4%–4.4%]; optimized PP: 92.2% [IQR 89.4%–93.9%]; p = 0.0021) (Figure 3B).

Transcriptome analysis represented as a heatmap showcased key differences in the transcription of key genes involved in PP differentiation and PEP lineage specification between PP cells generated with each protocol (Figure 3C). Specifically, genes associated with PP commitment (FOXA2, GP2, NKX6.1, ONECUT1, and PDX1) and PEP lineage commitment (ARX, CHGA, HNF4A, ISL1, and UCN3) were all expressed at higher levels in AT7867-treated PPs while MAFB, NEUROD1, NEUROG3, NKX2.2, PAX4, and PAX6 were upregulated in the optimized PP protocol. Expression of pluripotency genes (FGF2, KIT, LIN28A, MYC, PODXL, PODXL2, POU5F1, and TERT) and TBX2 was downregulated in AT7867-treated PPs compared with the optimized protocol PPs (Figure 3C).

Comparative analysis of the expression of PDX1, NKX6.1, GP2, and ONECUT1 throughout differentiation showed a sharp upregulation of these transcripts at stage 4 (Figures 3D–3G); this upregulation was significantly increased upon treatment with AT7867 compared with the use of the optimized differentiation protocol (PDX1: time p = 0.0008 and group p = 0.0054; NKX6.1: time p < 0.0001 and group p = 0.0017; GP2: time p = 0.0001 and group p = 0.0160; ONECUT1: time p = 0.0006 and group p = 0.0013). On the contrary, CHGA (time p = 0.0002; Figure 3H), NEUROG3 (time p = 0.0009 and group p = 0.0001; Figure 3I), and NEUROD1 (time p = 0.0449 and group p = 0.0018; Figure 3J) showed a sharp upregulation at stage 3 in the optimized differentiation protocol while these genes did not show a sharp upregulation until stage 4 in the AT7867-treated PPs. PAX4 and NKX2.2 showed a sharp upregulation at stage 4 (Figures 3K–3L), but the expression was increased in the PPs generated with the optimized differentiation protocol compared with the AT7867-treated PPs (PAX4: time p = 0.0002 and group p = 0.0058; NKX2.2: time p = 0.0002 and group p = 0.0004).

AT7867 treatment reduces graft size without hampering diabetes reversal

We expanded and differentiated iPSCs within clusters, with or without AT7867, using 0.1 L vertical wheel bioreactors (VWB) and transplanted the clusters under the kidney capsule of diabetic SCID beige mice (Figure 4A). Quality control of clusters can be found in Figures S4A–S4I.

Glucose levels of the transplanted mice were monitored three times a week to assess the potential of PP clusters to engraft, differentiate, and reverse diabetes in vivo (Figure 4B). The diabetic control mice exhibited elevated glucose levels over 20 mM for the duration of the experiment. Mice transplanted with control PPs displayed gradual reversal of hyperglycemia over 70 days (IQR 66–78 days) while mice transplanted with AT7867-treated PPs reversed significantly faster, within 45 days (IQR 39–49 days; p < 0.0001) of transplant; all animals remained non-diabetic over the remainder of the experiment (Figure S4J). This result was further confirmed by measurement of area under the curve (AUC) (−AT7867: 1,423 [IQR 1,390–1,458]; +AT7867: 1,080 [IQR 1,033–1,118]; p < 0.0001; Figure 4C).

Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT) performed at 8 weeks post-transplantation showed that mice transplanted with AT7867-treated PPs had IPGTT profiles more similar to naive mice than mice transplanted with control PPs (Figure 4D) as shown by their AUCs (naive control: 1,577 [IQR 1,406–1,748]; +AT7867: 2,029 [IQR 1,648–2,411]; p = 0.2897), while mice transplanted with control PPs had a higher AUC (−AT7867: 2,268 [IQR 2,275–3,061]; p = 0.0161; Figure 4E). Mice transplanted with AT7867-treated PPs had increased concentration of C-peptide at time 0 compared with animals transplanted with control PPs (−AT7867 [t = 0]: 21.3 p.m. [IQR 19.7–22.4 p.m.]; +AT7867 [t = 0]: 44.29 p.m. [IQR 37.8–49.2 p.m.]; p = 0.0065) (Figure 4F) and demonstrated glucose responsive C-peptide production 60 min after glucose administration (+AT7867 [t = 0]: 44.2 p.m. [IQR 37.8–49.2 p.m.]; +AT7867 [t = 60]: 76.6 p.m. [IQR 61.6–91.6 p.m.]; p = 0.0001). Mice transplanted with control PPs were not able to produce C-peptide in response to glucose 8 weeks post-transplantation (−AT7867 [t = 0]: 21.3 p.m. [IQR 19.7–22.4 p.m.]; −AT7867 [t = 60]: 19.3 p.m. [IQR 12.8–19.55 p.m.]; p = 0.8682). However, at 12 weeks post-transplantation, both groups had similar IPGTT profiles to naive mice (Figure 4G) and similar AUC measurements (−AT7867: 1,884 p.m. [IQR 1,744–2,024 p.m.]; p = 0.2352; +AT7867: 1,714 p.m. [IQR 1,438–1,990 p.m.]; p = 0.7043; Figure 4H) as well as similar C-peptide levels at time 0 (−AT7867: 120.6 p.m. [IQR 89.3–126.3 p.m.]; +AT7867: 117.2 [IQR 77.5–150.4 p.m.]; p = 0.9988; Figure 4I) and 60 min after glucose administration (−AT7867: 213.2 p.m. [IQR 195.3–233.4 p.m.]; +AT7867: 248.3 p.m. [IQR 228.5–350.2 p.m.]; p = 0.1456; Figure 4I).

Gross pathological assessment of grafted kidneys 90 days post-transplant demonstrated that the grafts collected from the animals transplanted with AT7867-treated PPs were significantly smaller than control (−AT7867: 5.6 cm2 [IQR 4.9–6.3 cm2]; +AT7867: 0.8 cm2 [IQR 0.6–1.0 cm2]; p = 0.0079; Figures 4J and 4K). Kidney enlargement in control grafts was primarily composed of multiloculated cystic masses with CK19+ lined cysts with SOX9+ interductal tissues; grafts harvested from AT7867-treated cells showed small CK19+ lined cysts with SOX9+ interductal tissues (Figure 4L). Both PPs were able to mature into monohormonal insulin or glucagon-secreting cells. MAFA staining was used to confirm the maturity of insulin-positive cells; the animals transplanted with AT7867-treated cells had a higher percentage of MAFA+ β cells than control (−AT7867: 3.9% [IQR 2.6%–5.4%]; +AT7867: 32.07% [IQR 29.5%–33.7%]; p < 0.0001; Figures 4L and S4K).

Discussion

Addition of AT7867 during PP differentiation significantly increases the proportion of PDX1+NKX6.1+GP2+ cells without altering the total cell yield or proliferation, which, combined with upregulation of PP and PEP commitment genes and downregulation of pluripotency genes, suggests that AT7867 induces differentiation of PP cells rather than proliferation. Furthermore, our results demonstrate that transplantation of high-purity PP cells measured as >90% of PDX1+NKX6.1+GP2+ results in accelerated diabetes reversal following transplant and in vivo maturation.

The presence of uncommitted cells remains the major obstacle for clinical translation of SC islets (Aghazadeh et al., 2022; Rezania et al., 2012); hence, strategies to remove off-target cells are a focus for intense investigation (Verhoeff et al., 2022). Key to pancreatic endocrine cells is the generation of pure PPs. GP2 is a highly specific marker for PP cells capable of differentiating into insulin-secreting cells in vivo (Aghazadeh et al., 2022; Ameri et al., 2017); sorting of GP2+ cells followed by transplantation of 76% GP2+ PPs eliminates contaminating off-target cells and the associated risk of teratoma formation post-transplantation. However, sorting of GP2+ cells resulted in significant cell loss with a recovery of only 16% of the cells (Aghazadeh et al., 2022), making this strategy inappropriate for large-scale manufacturing for clinical translation. Optimization of the differentiation protocol to generate pure populations is a more cost-effective and scalable approach. Herein, we propose a scalable chemical-based approach using AT7867 to generate PP populations with >90% PDX1+GP2+ that give rise to functional β cells in vivo.

AT7867 is an AKT and p70 S6 kinase inhibitor used to decrease tumor growth through G2/M phase arrest and apoptosis (Zhang et al., 2017; Li et al., 2023). AT7867 has also been described to induce the proliferation of PDX1+ cells (Kimura et al., 2017); however, our results show that the increased number of PDX1+NKX6.1+ is a result of improved differentiation rather than proliferation (Aghazadeh et al., 2022; Heller et al., 2021). Of note, despite increased levels of endocrine genes, AT7867-treated PPs are not yet committed to the endocrine lineage as shown by the lack of CHGA protein expression; these cells have the potential to generate ductal, acinar and endocrine populations, which is reflected by the high expression of CK19, SOX9, and CHGA and the presence of ducts in vivo. Addition of AT7867 results in a timely induction of NEUROG3 followed by the expression of mature endocrine markers (Ramond et al., 2017; Krentz et al., 2018). Premature endocrine (CHGA) stimulation in combination with PDX1 and NKX6.1 has previously been described to trigger the cells away from the β cell lineage and to generate poly-hormonal cells in vivo (Buckle et al., 2018). Improved differentiation efficiency and, hence, increased PP mass, resulted in accelerated glucose-stimulated insulin response and faster diabetes reversal, showcasing the importance of generating highly pure PPs.

Several studies have reported in vitro differentiation of PP cells into SC islets followed by diabetes reversal upon transplantation in mice but, to date, no studies have reported the presence (or absence) of teratomas upon transplantation of SC islets (Velazco-Cruz et al., 2019; Hogrebe et al., 2020; Rezania et al., 2012). In vitro SC islet differentiation of could be a potentially safer alternative for clinical translation due to the decreased proliferation rate of cells upon endocrine commitment (Balboa et al., 2022) but survival of β cells upon transplantation is poor due to their high oxygen consumption rate, resulting in increased number of endocrine cells required for transplantation compared with PPs. The lower differentiation yield associated with endocrine differentiation is also a critical limiting factor for scalability for clinical translation. In any case, the generation of pure PP populations remains a limiting factor to prevent teratoma formation.

In vitro generation of SC islets or PP cells provides a unique opportunity to deliver adequate islet or cell progenitor masses to ensure achievement of long-term insulin independence (i.e., of >20,000 IEQ/kg). Assuming a 50% cell loss throughout differentiation, we estimate that the generation and differentiation of up to 109 iPSCs per patient will be required. Optimization of the differentiation protocol to increase yield is essential and generation of pure populations, rather than sorting, is more cost effective for clinical translation. While planar culture conditions support scalability through the addition of more plates or flasks, suspension culture using VWB enables scalability into larger culture vessel formats (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2023). Hence, it is encouraging that our protocol was transferable to suspension culture using 0.1 L VWB, showcasing the potential to generate clinically relevant cell masses.

The outcomes of this study should be contextualized within specific limitations. Addition of AT7867 to this protocol has only been replicated with three healthy human donor-derived iPSC lines generated with Sendai virus-mediated transfection of PBMCs. Differentiation efficiency might vary based on the cell source and the reprogramming method, as well as patient-related factors. Furthermore, additional differentiation runs and long-term transplants to evaluate the safety of the cells would be required prior to potential clinical translation, and in-human safety and efficacy data would still be required to confirm these promising results. Due to the size of the control grafts, the β cells were highly dispersed within the graft, and we have not been able to quantify and compare the total β cell mass in both conditions, which is a limitation of this study. Hence, future work will focus on an in-depth characterization of the graft and quantification of the β cell mass upon long-term engraftment to elucidate the efficiency of the differentiation protocol here presented. Scalability of this differentiation protocol using 0.5, 3, or 15 L VWB remains to be tested; but the scalability of VWB to 0.5 L has been demonstrated for iPSC expansion (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2023) and we do not expect any pitfalls in that aspect.

Herein, we present AT7867 as a novel molecule that improves in vitro differentiation efficiency of human iPSCs into PP cells. Following renal subcapsular transplant, AT7867 PPs are capable of in vivo maturation into monohormonal functional cells with accelerated diabetes reversal. The potential to scale up this protocol using VWB represents a step toward the clinical translation of pluripotent stem cell-derived cellular therapeutics for the treatment of diabetes.

Cell culture

Three human iPSC lines were generated, expanded, and characterized as described previously (Table 1) (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2023). The passage number of the cryopreserved master bank was 12; cells underwent quality control assessment post-thaw and prior to differentiation. Differentiation was carried out using a published protocol with modifications (Sui et al., 2021); 1 μM AT7867 (Tocris, cat. no. 7001) was added at stage 4. Alternatively, iPSCs were differentiated into PPs using a previously published protocol (Balboa et al., 2022).

Flow cytometry

Cells were lifted, disaggregated, and stained as reported previously (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2023). Primary antibodies were incubated for 1 h on ice and secondary antibodies for 30 min on ice (dilutions in Table S3). Data were acquired using the CytoFLEX S flow cytometer and analyzed using the CytExpert software (Beckman Coulter).

Western blotting

Cells were lysed in a buffer containing 50 mM Tris (pH 8.0), 150 mM NaCl, and 1% v/v Triton X-100 and supplemented with protease inhibitor tablets (Thermo Fisher Scientific, cat. no. A32955). Total protein was separated on a 10% PAGE gel (Bio-Rad, cat. no. 456-1033). Primary antibodies used were diluted 1:1,000 (PDX1 [R&D Systems, cat. no. AF2419], KI67 [Abcam, cat. no. ab15580], and actin [Invitrogen, cat. no. AM4302]).

Immunohistochemistry

iPSCs and tissue preparation and staining was performed as described previously (Cuesta-Gomez et al., 2023). Antibodies and concentrations used are listed in Table S3. Slides were visualized using the Zeiss Observer Z1 inverted fluorescence microscope and images were processed using Zeiss software and analyzed using QuPath (Bankhead et al., 2017).

qRT-PCR

Custom designed gene TaqMan Low Density Array Cards were used as per the manufacturer’s instructions (Thermo Fisher Scientific, cat. no. 4342253); gene array set ups are described in Tables S4 and S5. Data acquisition was performed in QuantStudio 12K Flex Real-Time PCR system. Samples were analyzed using GAPDH as reference for data normalization. Data were analyzed and represented as a heatmap, 2(−ΔΔCT) or volcano plots using GraphBio (Zhao and Wang 2022), GraphPad Prism version 9.3.1, or VolcanoSer (Goedhart and Luijsterburg 2020).

Diabetic induction and transplantation

Five days before transplantation, diabetes was induced by intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of 75 mg/kg of streptozotocin (Millipore Sigma, cat. no. 572201) in acetate buffer (pH 4.5). Cells (5 × 106) were transplanted under the kidney capsule (Szot et al. 2007) and an insulin pellet (LinShin Canada, cat. no. As-1-L) was implanted subcutaneously to maintain animal health over a 30-day period.

On post-operative day 90, non-recovery nephrectomy was performed. Kidney cross-sections were fixed in 10% formalin, and paraffinized.

Assessment of glycemic control

Glycemic control was assessed using non-fasting blood glucose measurements three times a week. IPGTTs were conducted at 8 and 12 weeks post-transplant; blood samples were collected before i.p. injection (3 mg/g of weight) and 60 min post-injection to measure human C-peptide content (Mercodia, cat. no. 10-1136-01).

Statistical analysis

Between-group comparisons were carried out using the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test. Two-way ANOVA was used to compare time courses. The α value was set at 0.05 but was modified post hoc to 0.01 for volcano plot evaluation of transcriptomic data. Statistical analysis was completed using GraphPad Prism version 9.3.1 for Mac.

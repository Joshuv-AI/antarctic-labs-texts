Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic profiling identifies a fibroblast-like endothelial state and the MDK-NCL signaling axis in prostate cancer microenvironment remodeling
Authors: Ruiang Wang, Yifan Li, Xiaoxiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single-cell analysis identified a fibroblast-like endothelial state in prostate cancer.•The MDK-NCL axis marked active tumor microenvironment communication.•FLE enrichment was associated with stromal remodeling and poor prognosis.•The FLE-related signature stratified patient risk across three independent cohorts.•NCL silencing inhibited prostate cancer cell growth in vitro.Keywords: Prostate cancer, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Spatial transcriptomics, Fibroblast-like endothelial cells, MDK-NCL signaling axis, Tumor microenvironment

Introduction

Prostate cancer is characterized by substantial clinical and molecular heterogeneity, which contributes to variable disease progression and therapeutic responses despite advances in screening and treatment [[1], [2], [3]]. Integrated models combining complementary clinical and biological features are therefore increasingly being investigated to improve metastatic risk assessment and patient stratification [4]. Increasing evidence indicates that this heterogeneity is shaped not only by malignant epithelial cells but also by the tumor microenvironment, particularly its stromal and vascular compartments [[5], [6], [7]]. Dynamic communication among tumor and microenvironmental cells has also been implicated in prostate cancer progression and metastatic niche formation [8].

Single-cell RNA sequencing has revealed extensive transcriptional remodeling across epithelial, immune, stromal, and vascular populations during prostate cancer progression [9]. Although previous studies have characterized epithelial diversity and cancer-associated fibroblast heterogeneity in detail [10,11], the endothelial compartment remains comparatively underexplored. Tumor-associated endothelial cells are not merely structural components of blood vessels. They can adopt distinct angiogenic, inflammatory, immune-regulatory, and matrix-remodeling states that influence tumor growth, metastatic dissemination, and treatment response [[12], [13], [14]]. Resolving this endothelial heterogeneity may therefore uncover previously unrecognized mechanisms of prostate cancer microenvironment remodeling.

Endothelial plasticity represents a particularly relevant dimension of this heterogeneity. Under sustained exposure to tumor-derived cytokines and stromal signals, endothelial cells may partially lose canonical vascular characteristics while acquiring mesenchymal and extracellular matrix-related features, resulting in endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT)-like states [[15], [16], [17]]. Such intermediate states may co-express endothelial markers together with genes associated with cytoskeletal remodeling, fibroblast activation, and extracellular matrix deposition. Recent evidence suggests that EndMT-related endothelial reprogramming can contribute to prostate cancer progression and neuroendocrine differentiation. Single-cell analyses in other tumor contexts have further demonstrated that vascular endothelial cells can acquire EndMT-related transcriptional programs and participate in extensive intercellular crosstalk during microenvironmental remodeling [18]. However, the cellular composition, transcriptional programs, and clinical relevance of these EndMT-like states in prostate cancer remain insufficiently defined.

The biological significance of endothelial plasticity is also likely to depend on its spatial context. Spatial transcriptomic studies have demonstrated that prostate cancer contains regionally organized tumor, stromal, vascular, and immune programs, with distinct tumor-stromal interactions occurring within specific tissue compartments [[19], [20], [21]]. These findings suggest that fibroblast-like or EndMT-like endothelial states should be interpreted not only according to their transcriptional identity but also according to the spatial niches in which they are located.

In this study, we integrated single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomic information, and bulk transcriptomic cohorts to characterize endothelial heterogeneity in prostate cancer. We identified a fibroblast-like endothelial population with mixed vascular and stromal features, examined its spatial distribution and intercellular communication network, and further characterized an EndMT-like endothelial subset enriched in extracellular matrix and mesenchymal programs. We also investigated the MDK-NCL signaling axis and constructed a FLE-related prognostic model to evaluate the clinical relevance of this endothelial plasticity program.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The single-cell dataset GSE141445 was used to determine cell heterogeneity and FLE cell populations. Additionally, bulk transcriptomic and clinical data from TCGA-PRAD, GSE116918, and GSE46602 were utilized for validation, survival analysis, and model construction. Spatial transcriptomic data of human prostate cancer were obtained from the publicly available 10x Genomics Spatial Gene Expression dataset Human Prostate Cancer Adenocarcinoma with Invasive Carcinoma (FFPE) (1.3.0 chemistry version), which was generated using the Visium Spatial Gene Expression platform. The dataset was downloaded from the official 10x Genomics website (https://www.10xgenomics.com/datasets/human-prostate-cancer-adenocarcinoma-with-invasive-carcinoma-ffpe-1-standard-1-3-0). The dataset contains spatially resolved transcriptomic profiles from human prostate cancer tissue sections.

Single-cell analysis and cell annotation

Single-cell data were processed with a standard workflow including normalization, dimensionality reduction, clustering, and UMAP visualization. Cell types were annotated using canonical markers, including EPCAM/KRT18/KRT8 for epithelial cells, CD3D/CD3E for T cells, TPSAB1/CPA3 for mast cells, PECAM1/VWF for endothelial cells, CD68/CD14/FCGR3A for monocytes, COL1A1/COL1A2 for fibroblasts, and CD79A/MS4A1 for B cells. Because of its mixed marker profile and intermediate position between endothelial and fibroblast clusters, the FLE population was identified within the endothelium-related compartment.

Cell communication and endothelial subclustering

Cell-cell communication was inferred using ligand-receptor analysis. In the CellChat database, the midkine signaling network is designated as the “MK” signaling pathway and comprises multiple interactions involving the MDK ligand and its candidate receptors. Pathway-level communication patterns were therefore referred to as the midkine (MK) signaling pathway, whereas MDK-NCL was described as a specific inferred ligand-receptor interaction within this pathway.

Functional analysis

Genes linked to FLE-related groupings and endothelial subclusters were found using differential expression analysis. Functional enrichment analysis was then used to investigate the biological pathways and processes connected to these gene sets.

Estimation of the FLE enrichment score in bulk transcriptomic cohorts

To project the single-cell-defined FLE state onto bulk transcriptomic cohorts, a FLE signature was constructed using genes preferentially expressed in the FLE population. Genes with low specificity, including ribosomal genes, mitochondrial genes, hemoglobin-related genes, pseudogenes, and long noncoding RNAs, were excluded to reduce the influence of technical and nonspecific transcriptional signals. The remaining protein-coding genes that were available in the corresponding bulk-expression datasets were used as the FLE signature. For each patient in the TCGA-PRAD and GEO cohorts, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed using the FLE signature to calculate a sample-level FLE enrichment score. The score reflects the relative enrichment of the FLE-associated transcriptional program within each bulk sample and should not be interpreted as the absolute proportion or cellular abundance of FLE cells. Patients were divided into high- and low-score groups according to the median FLE enrichment score within each cohort.

hdWGCNA and selection of FLE-related genes

High-dimensional weighted gene co-expression network analysis (hdWGCNA) was performed using the single-cell transcriptomic data to identify gene modules associated with the FLE state. Metacells were constructed by aggregating transcriptionally similar cells within each annotated cell population to reduce data sparsity. A scale-free co-expression network was subsequently generated, and genes were grouped into modules using hierarchical clustering and dynamic tree cutting. The module eigengene and eigengene-based connectivity (kME) of each gene were calculated to evaluate module activity and identify intramodular hub genes. Module activity was compared across the annotated cell populations. The purple module, which showed the highest relative enrichment in the FLE population and limited activity in most other cell populations, was selected as the FLE-related module. Candidate genes were further prioritized according to their module membership, expression availability across the three bulk transcriptomic cohorts, and association with the recurrence-related outcome.

Development and external validation of the prognostic model

TCGA-PRAD was used as the model-development cohort, whereas GSE116918 and GSE46602 served as external validation cohorts. Patients lacking complete gene-expression or recurrence-related follow-up data were excluded. Candidate predictors were derived from the FLE-enriched purple module and restricted to genes available in all three cohorts. Within TCGA-PRAD, genes associated with recurrence-related outcomes by univariable Cox regression (P < 0.05) were retained and evaluated using 21 individual or combined machine-learning strategies implemented in Mime R package, including RSF, GBM, LASSO, ridge, elastic net, plsRcox, CoxBoost, SuperPC, survival-SVM, and stepwise Cox regression. Feature selection and model tuning were performed exclusively in TCGA-PRAD using 10-fold cross-validation. Algorithm-specific tuning parameters were selected using cross-validation; for RSF, bootstrap aggregation was performed with a terminal node size of 5, while the remaining parameters were set to their package defaults. The external cohorts were not used for feature selection, parameter tuning, or model fitting. RSF was selected as the final model based on its performance in TCGA-PRAD and was subsequently applied to the external cohorts without refitting. The RSF ensemble mortality estimate was defined as the continuous risk score, with higher values indicating greater recurrence risk. Patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups using the cohort-specific median risk score. Prognostic performance was evaluated using Harrell’s C-index, Kaplan-Meier (KM) analysis with the log-rank test, and time-dependent ROC curves at 1, 3, and 5 years.

Clinical evaluation and model interpretation

Restricted cubic spline analysis, Schoenfeld residual testing, calibration analysis, and decision curve analysis were used to further evaluate the risk score's predictive significance. The risk score and clinicopathological characteristics were combined to create a nomogram. To determine their contribution to model prediction across cohorts, candidate marker genes were further assessed using survival analysis and diagnostic ROC analysis.

SHAP-based model interpretation

In TCGA-PRAD, GSE116918, and GSE46602, SHAP analysis was carried out to enhance the prognostic model's interpretability. Each marker gene's contribution to the model output was measured using SHAP dependence plots, feature importance plots, and SHAP summary plots. The relative effects of SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 across several cohorts were compared using this methodology.

Cell culture and siRNA transfection

PC3, DU145, LNCaP, and C4–2 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. PC3 and DU145 cells were transfected with two independent NCL-targeting siRNAs (siNCL#1 and siNCL#2) or a nonspecific negative-control siRNA according to the manufacturer's instructions. Knockdown efficiency was evaluated by quantitative real-time PCR.

CCK-8 assay

At 24 h after transfection, PC3 and DU145 cells were seeded into 96-well plates. Cell viability was measured at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h using the Cell Counting Kit-8. At each time point, 10 μL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, followed by incubation for 2 h at 37 °C. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Colony-formation assay

Transfected cells were seeded into six-well plates at 1000 cells per well and cultured for 14 days, with the medium replaced every 3 days. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. Colonies containing >50 cells were counted using ImageJ. Three independent biological experiments were performed.

EdU incorporation assay

DNA synthesis was assessed using an EdU incorporation assay kit. Transfected cells were incubated with 10 μM EdU for 2 h, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and processed according to the manufacturer's protocol. Cell nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope under identical exposure settings. The EdU-positive fraction was calculated as the number of EdU-positive nuclei divided by the total number of DAPI-positive nuclei.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R and GraphPad Prism. Quantitative data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation from at least three independent biological experiments. Two-group comparisons were performed using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test, Welch’s t-test, or Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Multiple-group comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test. Spearman’s correlation analysis was used to assess associations. Survival differences were evaluated using KM analysis and the log-rank test. Cox regression was used to estimate hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Model performance was assessed using Harrell’s C-index and time-dependent ROC curves. All tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of a fibroblast-like endothelial population

UMAP clustering identified the major cell populations in the dataset, including epithelial cells, T cells, mast cells, monocytes, fibroblasts, B cells, and endothelial cells (Fig. 1A). Within the endothelial-related compartment, we further identified a distinct FLE population located between the endothelial and fibroblast clusters, which suggests an intermediate transcriptional state. Cell types were annotated through typical markers. Epithelial cells expressed EPCAM, KRT18 and KRT8. T cells expressed CD3D and CD3E. Mast cells were marked by TPSAB1 and CPA3. Monocytes expressed CD68, CD14, and FCGR3A. Fibroblasts showed high COL1A1 and COL1A2 expression, while endothelial cells expressed PECAM1 and VWF (Fig. 1B). The FLE cluster showed features of both endothelial and fibroblast lineages, consistent with its position between these two cell groups on the UMAP plot. The feature plots supported this annotation pattern. The endothelial cell markers showed higher expression within the endothelial cell clusters, while the fibroblast markers are more abundant in the fibroblasts. However, the FLE population showed a mixed expression pattern, rather than typical characteristics of endothelial cells or fibroblasts (Fig. 1C). Spatial transcriptomic analysis of human prostate cancer tissue demonstrated regionally heterogeneous distributions of major transcriptional programs. Projection of the FLE signature onto the spatial transcriptomic dataset revealed localized enrichment patterns within specific tissue regions (Fig. 1D). Taken together, these findings define the major cellular components of GSE141445 and highlight an intermediate FLE population within the endothelial-related lineage. This result provides the basis for further analysis of endothelial heterogeneity and cell-state transition.

FLE-centered cell communication highlights the MK signaling pathway and the MDK-NCL interaction

The interaction heatmap showed that cell-cell communication strength varied across the major cell populations (Fig. 2A). The communication networks based on interaction number and interaction weight further showed that the FLE cluster maintained extensive connections with other cell types (Fig. 2B and C). In the signaling role analysis, FLE cells displayed both strong outgoing and incoming interaction strength, which placed this population in an active position within the communication network (Fig. 2D). Ligand-receptor analysis identified several candidate communication signals involving the FLE population (Fig. 2E). At the pathway level, CellChat identified an active midkine (MK) signaling network, in which FLE cells participated as candidate signal senders and receivers (Fig. 2F-G). Within this broader pathway, the MDK-NCL ligand-receptor pair was selected for further investigation. Thus, the MK signaling pathway refers to the aggregated pathway-level communication network, whereas the MDK-NCL axis represents one specific computationally inferred interaction within that network. The expression distributions of MDK and its candidate interaction partners, including SDC2, LRP1, NCL, ITGA6, and ITGB1, were examined across the major cell populations (Fig. 2H). These findings support the presence of an FLE-associated MK communication network and nominate MDK-NCL as a candidate interaction for subsequent investigation. NCL expression was shown to be inversely correlated with immune, stromal, and ESTIMATE scores (Fig. 2I). NCL expression was greater in tumor tissues than in normal tissues, according to the box plot (Fig. 2J, left). With log-rank P values of 0.008 and 0.016, respectively, the survival curves further demonstrated that elevated NCL expression was linked to worse disease-specific survival and progression-free interval (Fig. 2J, center and right). The MDK-NCL axis may have biological and therapeutic significance, according to these results.

Endothelial subclustering identified a mesenchymal-like endothelial subset with active lipid metabolism and stromal programs

Endothelial reclustering was used to identify ten endothelial subpopulations, named Endo 1 through Endo 10 (Fig. 3A). Significant intralineage variability was seen in one of them, which had a distinct position on the UMAP map and was separated from the core distribution of endothelial cells. ACTA2, MYLK, COL1A1, and COL1A2 were abundant in a particular endothelial subcluster, according to feature plots and violin plots (Fig. 3B). Endo 6 showed the strongest evidence of this pattern, suggesting that this subgroup possessed both endothelial and fibroblast-like characteristics. These findings support the presence of a mesenchymal-like state within the endothelial lineage. Further examination of pathway activity revealed distinct variations among endothelial subclusters (Fig. 3C). The distribution of lipid-related metabolic processes, such as glycerolipid metabolism, ether lipid metabolism, and sphingolipid metabolism, was not uniform among the endothelial subsets (Fig. 3D). Rather than being uniformly distributed across all endothelial cells, these metabolic signatures were selectively enriched in select subclusters, according to the accompanying UMAP and violin plots. The signaling heatmap showed that several stromal- and transition-related pathways were more active in specific endothelial subclusters (Fig. 3E). These included an EMT-related gene signature, together with Hedgehog, Notch, TGF-β, and WNT signaling. In parallel, differential expression analysis of Endo 6 identified representative upregulated genes such as COL1A2, PDGFRB, and NOTCH3 (Fig. 3F). This result supports the view that Endo 6 represents a fibroblast-like endothelial state. Functional enrichment analysis of the differentially expressed genes showed that the Endo 6-associated program was linked to extracellular matrix remodeling, morphogenesis, angiogenesis-related regulation, and cytoskeleton-associated processes (Fig. 3G). Overall, these findings indicate that the endothelial cells in GSE141445 exhibit diversity, while Endo 6 represents an endothelial subset with mesenchymal-like features and enhanced extracellular matrix-remodeling and lipid-metabolic programs.

FLE enrichment was associated with adverse prognosis and activated mesenchymal signaling

The specificity analysis identified a group of genes with enriched expression in the FLE population, including ribosomal protein genes, ribosomal pseudogenes, MALAT1, hemoglobin-related genes, actin-related genes, and chromosome-associated transcripts (Fig. 4A). The correlation analysis between scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq showed that protein-coding genes had a moderate positive correlation in both cohorts. In contrast, the correlation for lncRNAs was weaker, while pseudogenes showed inconsistent performance between datasets. The survival curves showed that patients with high FLE enrichment scores had worse recurrence-related outcome in the TCGA and GSE46602 cohorts (Fig. 4B and C). Differential expression analysis identified both upregulated and downregulated genes in the FLE group (Fig. 4D). The enrichment network further showed that these genes were mainly involved in translation, organophosphate biosynthetic process, negative regulation of cellular component organization, TNF-α/NF-κB signaling complex, metabolism of lipids, autophagy, WNT signaling, VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling, and several immune- and stress-related pathways (Fig. 4E). Gene set enrichment analysis showed that the androgen response pathway was significantly suppressed in the high FLE group, with NES = −2.45 and adjusted P < 0.001 (Fig. 4F). By contrast, pathway activity analysis showed that TGF-β signaling, Hedgehog signaling, EMT-related gene-set activity, and WNT/β-catenin signaling were all significantly higher in the high FLE group (Fig. 4G). Correlation analysis further confirmed positive associations between FLE enrichment score and these pathways, with Spearman R values of 0.586 for TGF-β signaling, 0.519 for Hedgehog signaling, 0.355 for epithelial-mesenchymal signaling, and 0.342 for WNT/β-catenin signaling (Fig. 4H). Together, these findings suggest that FLE enrichment is associated with a stronger mesenchymal transcriptional program that may be consistent with endothelial plasticity.

hdWGCNA identified a FLE-related module with prognostic value

The hdWGCNA dendrogram identified multiple co-expression modules in the single-cell dataset (Fig. 5A). Module membership analysis showed distinct hub-gene structures across these modules, including the green, blue, turquoise, red, pink, brown, yellow, magenta, black, and purple modules (Fig. 5B). Module feature plots showed that these gene programs were distributed unevenly across cell populations (Fig. 5C). The dot plot further showed that several modules were preferentially enriched in specific cell types (Fig. 5D). Among them, the purple module was mainly concentrated in the FLE population and showed limited expression in most other cell compartments. This pattern suggests that the purple module represents a FLE-related transcriptional program. The network analysis of the purple module identified a compact gene interaction structure centered on endothelial and matrix-related genes, including SPARCL1, SPARC, CAV1, IGFBP7, IGFBP4, MGP, A2M, TIMP3, COL4A1, and GNG11 (Fig. 5E). These genes are consistent with vascular and stromal remodeling features, which supports the biological relevance of this module in the FLE state. Model comparison showed that the RSF model achieved the highest performance in the training cohort, with a C-index of 0.954, and also maintained good performance in the validation cohorts (Fig. 5F). Based on this risk model, patients in the high-risk group had significantly worse survival in TCGA-PRAD, GSE116918, and GSE46602. In TCGA-PRAD, the AUC values at 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.816, 0.816, and 0.783. In GSE116918, the corresponding AUC values were 0.792, 0.694, and 0.630. In GSE46602, the AUC values were 0.795, 0.790, and 0.805 (Fig. 5G). Together, these findings suggest that the FLE-related module has prognostic value with attenuated external performance across independent cohorts.

The FLE-related risk score improved prognostic stratification and clinical prediction

The restricted cubic spline curve showed that the association between risk score and survival risk was not linear (Fig. 6A). As the risk score increased, the hazard ratio rose progressively, and both the overall effect and the nonlinear effect were significant. This result indicates that the prognostic effect of the risk score becomes more pronounced when the numerical value is higher. The Schoenfeld residual test was also used to confirm the proportional hazards assumption. The test result was not significant, and there was no discernible time-dependent variation in the plot (Fig. 6B). This outcome indicated no significant violation of the proportional hazards assumption. Predicted and actual survival rates at 1, 3, and 5 years agreed well, according to the calibration plots (Fig. 6C). The nomogram exhibited acceptable calibration over several follow-up time periods, as seen by the prediction curves' continued proximity to the ideal reference line. Over a variety of threshold probabilities, decision curve analysis revealed that the risk score offered a greater net benefit than a number of conventional clinicopathological factors (Fig. 6D). This trend was seen at 1, 3, and 5 years, indicating that the model could be useful for making therapeutic decisions. The model was able to distinguish between patients with and without events at each time point, according to the time-dependent distribution plots (Fig. 6E). Additionally, the improvement plot demonstrated that, at all three time periods, the new model outperformed the standard model in terms of discrimination (Fig. 6F). When considered together, these results show that the FLE risk score provides value beyond standard clinical indicators and has steady predictive performance.

Exploratory comparison of TMB and mutation profiles between FLE-related risk groups

Missense mutations are the main type of variation, while single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are also the most common form of variation. C>T is the most common type of single nucleotide variation (Fig. 7A). The mutation frequency in the high-risk group is also higher than that in the low-risk group: 149/245 (60.82%) and 129/243 (53.09%) samples were detected with mutations respectively (Fig. 7B). The box plot shows that the tumor mutation burden (TMB) in the high-risk group is higher (Fig. 7C). The scatter plot further indicates a positive correlation between the risk score and TMB (Fig. 7D). Patients with high TMB values have a poorer survival prognosis, while those with low TMB values have a better prognosis (Fig. 7E). When both TMB and risk score are considered, patients with a high risk score and high TMB value have the worst prognosis, while those with a low risk score and low TMB value have the best prognosis (Fig. 7F). However, because tumor stage, Gleason grade, tumor purity, and other clinicopathological factors may influence both mutation detection and the risk score, the observed differences do not establish an independent biological relationship between the FLE program and TMB.

FLE marker genes showed spatial enrichment and clinical relevance

SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 were mostly expressed in the FLE-related area on the UMAP map, according to the feature plots. Additionally, the spatial mapping shows that these genes are not uniformly distributed across the entire tissue section, but rather are mostly localized in certain tissue locations (Figure S1A-E). Their affiliation with the FLE program is supported by this trend. The diagnostic ROC curves also supported the discriminatory value of these genes. The AUC value of SPARC was 0.633, IGFBP7 was 0.737, ESAM was 0.793, CRIP2 was 0.709, and COL4A1 was 0.699. Among them, ESAM showed the best diagnostic performance. Overall, these findings indicate that FLE marker genes are spatially enriched and have prognostic and discriminatory associations.

Machine-learning interpretation revealed cohort-dependent contributions of FLE marker genes

To identify the genes that most consistently contributed to the FLE prognostic model, we compared SHAP feature-importance patterns across TCGA-PRAD, GSE116918, and GSE46602. Although the relative ranking of individual genes varied among cohorts, SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 repeatedly showed substantial contributions to model prediction. These five genes were therefore selected as representative FLE markers for subsequent gene-level interpretation. SHAP dependence plots further demonstrated that the direction and magnitude of their effects differed across cohorts, indicating that the prognostic signal was generated by a coordinated multigene program rather than by a single dominant gene. Their spatial expression patterns, diagnostic performance, and individual prognostic associations were subsequently examined as additional supporting evidence. Survival analyses showed cohort-dependent prognostic associations for the five representative FLE marker genes. In TCGA-PRAD, higher expression of SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 was significantly associated with poorer recurrence-related outcomes. In GSE116918, SPARC, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 reached statistical significance, whereas IGFBP7 showed an unfavorable but nonsignificant trend. In GSE46602, SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, and COL4A1 were significantly associated with poorer outcomes, whereas CRIP2 showed a similar unfavorable direction without reaching statistical significance (Fig. 8A–C). Overall, the directions of association were generally consistent, although statistical significance varied across genes and cohorts. These findings support cohort-dependent contributions of the five genes to the FLE-related prognostic signal rather than uniformly significant individual prognostic effects.

NCL knockdown suppressed prostate cancer cell growth in vitro

Fig. 9A-D indicated that the expression level of NCL in tumor tissues and prostate cancer cell lines was higher than that in the normal control group. Fig. 9E and G confirmed that after siRNA transfection, NCL expression was successfully knocked down in PC3 and DU145 cells. The CCK-8 assay showed that silencing of the NCL gene reduced the cell viability of these two cell lines (Fig. 9F and H). The colony formation experiment further revealed that knockdown of the NCL gene reduced the clonogenic ability of PC3 and DU145 cells (Fig. 9I and J). These results indicated that NCL supported the proliferative capacity of prostate cancer cells. EdU staining showed that knockdown of the NCL gene reduced the proportion of proliferating cells in PC3 and DU145 cells (Fig. 10A and B). This phenomenon was observed in both siNCL constructs, which was consistent with the growth inhibition results observed in the CCK-8 experiment and the colony formation experiment. These findings supported a role for NCL in maintaining the proliferative capacity of PC3 and DU145 cells.

Discussion

In this study, we identified an FLE population in prostate cancer by integrating single-cell and bulk transcriptomic data. This cell state was located between endothelial cells and fibroblasts and showed mixed endothelial and stromal features. Our results extend earlier single-cell studies showing that prostate cancer contains marked microenvironmental heterogeneity beyond malignant epithelial cells. In particular, they suggest that endothelial-related plasticity may represent an additional layer of stromal complexity in prostate cancer.

Blood vessel formation is not the exclusive function of tumor endothelial cells [22]. They regulate angiogenesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, immunological trafficking, and metastatic behavior [23]. Previous studies have shown that endothelial cells can undergo endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT), a plasticity process associated with extracellular matrix remodeling, fibrosis, tumor invasion, and treatment resistance [[24], [25], [26]]. In our study, the enrichment of ACTA2, MYLK, COL1A1, and COL1A2, together with increased activity of TGF-β, WNT, and mesenchymal gene signatures, supports the presence of an EndMT-like transcriptional state. Nevertheless, these transcriptomic findings do not establish a complete or irreversible EndMT process, which would require lineage tracing and protein-level validation. The communication pattern around the MDK-NCL axis is another noteworthy discovery. Strong incoming and outgoing signaling activity was shown by FLE cells, indicating that they are active participants in the microenvironment rather than passive observers. This finding aligns with the larger body of research demonstrating that endothelial cells use soluble factors and receptor-mediated communication to actively sculpt tumor microenvironments [27,28]. Midkine (MDK) is increasingly recognized as a regulator of tumor progression, stromal remodeling, angiogenesis, and immune escape [29,30]. Nucleolin (NCL) is also widely linked to cancer progression and has been studied as both a functional molecule and a therapeutic target [31,32]. In our data, MDK-NCL was identified as a candidate ligand-receptor interaction within the broader CellChat-defined MK signaling pathway. Moreover, the high expression of NCL was associated with a poorer clinical prognosis. This does not confirm the existence of causal signaling through the MDK-NCL axis in prostate cancer, but it provides a clear hypothesis for future experimental validation.

Our findings further support the association between endothelial plasticity and poor prognosis. The FLE-related module identified by hdWGCNA was rich in vascular- and extracellular matrix-related genes, such as SPARCL1, SPARC, CAV1, IGFBP7, TIMP3, and COL4A1. These genes have known roles in matrix remodeling, endothelial cell biology, or extracellular matrix organization. The risk model stratified recurrence-related outcomes across the analyzed cohorts, although its reduced C-index in the external datasets indicated limited transportability and a potential risk of overfitting. This pattern is consistent with the previous research results, which indicate that the characteristics of the stroma (especially the processes related to fibroblasts) have prognostic value in prostate cancer [33,34]. Our data add an endothelial-related component to this prognostic landscape. At the single-gene level, SPARC, IGFBP7, ESAM, CRIP2, and COL4A1 showed generally concordant but cohort-dependent prognostic associations, with statistical significance varying among genes and datasets. This is significant because it suggests that non-malignant tissue components may potentially provide clinical information about prostate cancer. The status of the matrix and blood vessels may influence the disease progression and the response to therapy, according to recent studies on prostate cancer and other malignancies [35,36]. Our results support this theory.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study primarily relied on public datasets, and inter-cohort differences in patient characteristics, sequencing platforms, and clinical endpoints may have affected the results. Second, the FLE state was inferred from marker co-expression and UMAP distribution; however, UMAP proximity cannot establish lineage transition. Therefore, the FLE population should be interpreted as an EndMT-like transcriptional state pending validation by lineage tracing and protein-level experiments. Third, ssGSEA-based estimation of FLE enrichment is dependent on the selected gene signature and may be influenced by transcriptional overlap between endothelial and fibroblast populations. Likewise, ligand-receptor analysis is computational and cannot confirm physical interaction or causal signaling through the MDK-NCL axis. Finally, the decline in model performance from the training cohort to the external cohorts indicates a potential risk of overfitting. Larger prospective cohorts and mechanistic experiments are required to validate these findings.

Conclusion

Our study supports the presence of a fibroblast-like endothelial state in prostate cancer. This state is linked to stromal signaling, active cell communication, and poor prognosis. These findings broaden the current view of prostate cancer heterogeneity and suggest that endothelial plasticity may have both biological and clinical significance.

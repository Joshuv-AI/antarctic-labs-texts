Visualization analysis of research hotspots and trends in Type B Aortic Dissection based on bibliometrics
Authors: Yinghong Li, Yierpani Aierken, Zhian Liang, Yuqi Wang, Yuanhao Sun, Zongwei Liu, Jiayin Guo, Jiaxin Wang, Jiaxue Bi, Xiangchen Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aims to systematically map and analyze the knowledge structure, research hotspots, and evolutionary trends in the field of Type B Aortic Dissection (TBAD) using bibliometric methods.Patients and methodsCiteSpace and VOSviewer were employed to conduct quantitative and visual analyses across multiple dimensions, including publication trends, collaboration networks among countries/institutions/authors, keyword co-occurrence and evolution, as well as co-citation analysis and reference burst detection.ResultsThe number of publications in this field has shown an overall upward trend, with a significant increase since 2013, reflecting the growing clinical and scientific attention to this life-threatening vascular emergency. The United States (USA) and China form a dual-core global collaboration structure, with the USA leading in international cooperation depth and citation impact, and China emerging as a major contributor with rapid growth in research output — an evolution that mirrors the global redistribution of cardiovascular research capacity. Keyword analysis reveals a paradigm shift from technical application to precision intervention and individualized management, while current research focusing on Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair (TEVAR), complication prediction, computational fluid dynamics (CFD), and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted diagnosis and treatment. Co-citation analysis confirms TEVAR as the gold-standard minimally invasive treatment for TBAD, with its widespread acceptance driving the standardization of clinical practice. Burst analysis of keywords indicates that “prediction model” and “deep learning” have become emerging research hotspots, marking the entry of TBAD research into an intelligent, data-driven era.ConclusionResearch on TBAD has developed a sophisticated knowledge system over the past two decades, shifting from traditional surgical exploration to an intelligent, data-driven research paradigm. This bibliometric analysis identifies a USA-China dual-core global collaboration pattern in the field and a three-stage evolution of research focus from pathophysiological exploration to evidence-based TEVAR standardization, and further to the integration of CFD and AI. Critical research gaps are also highlighted, including under-investigation of high-risk populations, insufficient long-term evidence for TEVAR, and inadequate cross-disciplinary integration of CFD, AI and genomics. Future TBAD research should prioritize multicenter prospective trials to upgrade clinical evidence, advance interdisciplinary precision medicine models, and build globally standardized big data platforms for the development and validation of AI-based diagnostic and therapeutic tools, thereby achieving more scientific and personalized management of TBAD.

Introduction

Aortic dissection (AD) is an extremely dangerous large vessel disease. Epidemiological data from the United States (USA) in the early 21st century showed an AD incidence of approximately 2.6–3.5 cases per 100,000 people per year [1]. With the aging of the global population, the incidence of AD has now reached 4.2 cases per 100,000 people per year and is still on an upward trend [2]. AD is an acute process characterized by medial degeneration, tearing of the intimal layer, and blood penetrating the arterial wall, leading to the formation of a false lumen within the media. Based on the site of entry tear, aortic dissection is classified as Stanford type A when involving the ascending aorta and/or the aortic arch; when targeting the descending aorta and/or the abdominal aorta, it is termed Stanford Type B Aortic Dissection (TBAD) [3, 4].

Bibliometrics is a method of literature analysis that involves analyzing published information (such as books, journal articles, datasets, blogs) and their associated metadata (such as abstracts, keywords, citations), using statistics to describe or display the relationships between published works [5]. During the literature analysis process, we can obtain detailed information about authors, keywords, journals, countries, institutions, references, etc., in related research fields [6]. Some scholars believe that “bibliometrics to scientific papers is what epidemiology is to patients” [7]. In recent years, an increasing number of people have begun to apply bibliometric methods for literature analysis [8–10].

As the most common acute aortic disease, AD has always attracted widespread attention from scholars. However, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis that delineates the evolution of the TBAD research landscape, identifies critical gaps, and provides an evidence-based roadmap for future investigations is lacking. To address this gap, this study aims to conduct a bibliometric analysis of TBAD-related literature from 2000 to 2024, in order to identify major contributors, map the knowledge structure, and explore emerging trends and future directions.

Data sources and methods

Search strategy and data acquisition

Web of Science (WoS) is a comprehensive academic resource database, an information service platform developed and maintained by Clarivate Analytics. This platform provides strong support for academic literature retrieval in fields such as natural sciences, social sciences, arts, and humanities. WoS is valued by the academic community for its extensive coverage of literature and citation analysis functions. By retrieving and tracking citation relationships of literature, researchers can gain an in-depth understanding of the academic influence of specific fields and explore related research areas. The data for this study is sourced from the Science Citation Index Expanded within the WoS Core Collection. The search strategy is: TS = (“Type B Aortic Dissection”) OR TS= (“Ⅲ Type Aortic Dissection”), Time range selection (2000-1-1 to 2024-10-31). The retrieved publications with full records and cited references were saved in RefWorks and tab-delimited file formats. Before the start of the study, irrelevant and duplicate literature was manually excluded.

Data analysis

This study uses CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and the R package Bibliometrix for visualization and analysis. CiteSpace is a visual knowledge mapping bibliometric tool based on the Java language, invented by Chaomei Chen of Drexel University [11]. VOSviewer is a software for bibliometric analysis and visualization released in 2010 by Nees Jan van Eck and Ludo Waltman of Leiden University, capable of extracting key information from a large number of publications and constructing collaboration, co-citation, and co-occurrence networks [12]. In our study, these two software programs were used for the following analyses: country and institution analysis, journal and co-cited journal analysis, author and co-cited author analysis, reference and reference co-citation analysis, as well as keyword-related analysis. The R package Bibliometrix (https://www.bibliometrix.org) was created by Dr. Massimo Aria and Dr. Corrado Cuccurullo in 2017 and is a comprehensive mapping analysis tool [13]. It was applied in this study for thematic evolution analysis and to construct a global distribution network of publications on TBAD.

Results

Analysis of publication volume

The typological distribution of publications in the domain of TBAD research is presented (Fig. 1a). Specifically, the figure demonstrates that article-type documents constitute as much as 65.05% of the total corpus, which underscores the predominance of original scientific papers in this field and attests to the maturity of its existing research system. Conference-related papers account for about 20%, showing active academic exchange and a certain degree of frontier exploration. The relatively modest proportion of review articles (8.11%) suggests that although primary research output is abundant, systematic evidence synthesis has not kept pace with the expanding literature. The proportions of Letter and Early access types are small, existing mainly as supplementary forms of research.

To further clarify the nature of the evidence base in TBAD research, we analyzed the distribution of article types among the retrieved literature (Fig. 1b). Clinical research accounted for the largest proportion (77.14%), basic medical research constituted a very low percentage (7.60%), and meta-analyses represented only 1.10% of all published articles. In addition, we also summarized the distribution of clinical research design types. The results showed that analytical research (such as case-control study, cohort study) accounted for 72.00% of the clinical literature, descriptive research (such as case series, case reports) comprised 20.78%, and experimental research (such as randomized controlled trials, non-randomized controlled trials) represented a mere 7.22% (Fig. 1c). The predominance of analytical and descriptive research designs, coupled with the scarcity of randomized controlled trials and meta‑analyses, suggests that the current evidence base in this field is largely derived from observational research. This partly explains the phenomenon observed in the recently published European Society for Vascular Surgery (ESVS) Guidelines for Diseases of the Descending Thoracic and Thoracoabdominal Aorta, in which the rapid expansion of publications in this domain was accompanied by relatively low levels of evidence.

The timeframe of publications included in the present analysis ranges from 2000 to 2024 (Fig. 1d). The temporal distribution of publications in the TBAD domain reveals a sustained growth trajectory overall; notably, the number of publications increased substantially post-2013, coinciding with the publication of landmark clinical trials such as the Randomized Investigation of Stent Grafts in Aortic Dissection Trial and updated European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines, which likely stimulated broader research interest. The zenith of publication activity was observed in 2021–2022, potentially reflecting both the maturation of endovascular techniques and the emergence of computational and artificial intelligence (AI) approaches in this field.

Geographic visualization of the international collaboration network identified the USA as the core collaborative node, which established connections with nearly all participating countries and served as a paradigm for international cooperation (Fig. 1e). In this research field, China ranks closely behind the USA and also demonstrates considerable international influence. Furthermore, the cooperation network in the TBAD research field shows a clear trend towards globalization, with frequent cross-continental cooperation, reflecting a diversified, transnational cooperation model.

National-level scientific research cooperation and publication volume evolution

Through statistical analysis of the annual trends and contribution rates of research papers from various countries, we found that the USA maintained a leading position in the number of publications in the field of TBAD research before 2017. In contrast, the number of papers published by China has shown a rapid growth trend since 2011. This phenomenon reflects the continuously increasing research investment and rising international influence of China in this field, enabling it to keep pace with the USA and become a major contributor to research in this area (Fig. 2a and Table 1).

Visual analysis of transnational collaboration patterns reveals that numerous countries in this field maintain close and extensive cooperative relationships (Fig. 2b-d). The USA and China function as core nodes in the international collaborative landscape of this field. The collaboration network is centered on European and American countries, while also incorporating in-depth participation from nations in the Asia-Pacific region. Collectively, the overall structure not only embodies the coexistence and interweaving of globalized and regionalized paradigms within the discipline, but also highlights the continuous deepening of multilateral research partnerships in this field. To a certain extent, it also reveals regional disparities in the allocation of research resources, providing an opportunity to strengthen transcontinental collaborations for improving data representativeness in the future.

Cooperation relationships among research institutions and personnel

To more clearly demonstrate the collaboration intensity and hotspot regions among institutions, this study conducted a collaborative network analysis of global medical institutions (Fig. 3a-b). By contrast, Chinese academic medical centers including Fudan University and Peking University Health Science Center have developed comparatively cohesive cooperative networks. It is noteworthy that the collaborative networks of key Chinese medical institutions (such as Fudan University, Nanjing University, and the Chinese People’s Liberation Army General Hospital ) have undergone continuous expansion, a trend that reflects the growing influence of China in international medical research, as well as the remarkable achievements in the clustering and platform-based development of domestic medical institutions. Journals like “Circulation” and “European Heart Journal” have larger nodes, indicating high academic influence in the field of vascular surgery research. Journals like “Journal of Vascular Surgery” and “The Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery” are located in the central position of the figure and connect to journals in multiple fields, also possessing high academic value. All top 10 journals are Q1/Q3 in the vascular surgery field, with the New England Journal of Medicine (IF = 78.5) having the highest impact factor, indicating that TBAD research is published in high-quality academic journals. Additionally, the overlapping parts of the green and blue areas indicate cooperation between journals in vascular surgery and radiology or interventional radiology fields. This shows that many journals are not limited to a single field like cardiovascular, thoracic surgery, or vascular surgery; cross-disciplinary cooperation between many journals is evident, reflecting the integration of these disciplines in technology and clinical research.

Analysis of references

Reference co-citation

The reference co-citation network diagram generated by VOSviewer shows the knowledge structure and research foundation in the field of aortic dissection (Fig. 5a and Table 5). The map forms two distinct clusters: the left green cluster is mainly composed of literature from 1999 to 2006, representing the foundational research stage. This cluster focused on pathological mechanisms, clinical diagnosis, and the initial development of endovascular therapy. These studies established the basic knowledge framework for TBAD, including the natural history of the disease, risk factors for mortality, and the first clinical evidence for Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair (TEVAR) as a minimally invasive alternative to open surgery.

Top 10 cited references in TBAD

Using CiteSpace software to analyze references, the overall map presents a multi-core cluster structure (Fig. 5b). Early research mainly focused on the period from 2006 to 2010, with research content focusing on the pathological mechanisms of aortic dissection and initial clinical observations of interventional therapy. As research deepened, around 2013 became an important turning point in this field. Studies such as Nienaber CA (2013), Fattori R (2013), and Brunkwall J (2014) were widely cited, marking the gradual establishment of TEVAR as a mainstream treatment method, promoting the standardization process of clinical practice. The high co-citation rate of TEVAR-related literature confirms its academic recognition as the mainstream treatment for TBAD, but the weak link between TEVAR and long-term aortic remodeling indicates insufficient attention to long-term postoperative outcomes in existing research, and further longitudinal follow-up studies are needed. Furthermore, studies represented by Lombardi JV (2020) and Tadros RO (2019) focus on guideline updates, AI-assisted decision-making, and management strategies for high-risk patients, indicating that research in this field has evolved from early diagnosis and treatment exploration towards individualized, intelligent, and multicenter collaborative directions. The co-citation network demonstrates the evolution from pioneering case reports and technical trials in the early 2000s to standardized TEVAR outcome studies and registry-based analyses after 2013. The persistent citation bursts of seminal works by Nienaber and Dake emphasize how foundational clinical innovations continue to shape contemporary evidence hierarchies.

Reference timeline

To further reveal the research foundation and development context in the field of aortic-related diseases, a literature co-citation time evolution map was constructed based on co-citation analysis (Fig. 5c). Between 1995 and 2005, research mainly focused on technical exploration and case reports, such as catheters (#7) and vascular plug (#8). Subsequently, research hotspots gradually shifted towards macro lesion management and micro mechanism simulation. Between 2006 and 2012, research on thoracic aorta (#4) and endovascular surgery (#1) began to emerge in large numbers, marking endovascular surgery becoming a hotspot. After 2013, research hotspots turned to in-depth study of disease management (#2) and hemodynamics (#5).

Reference burst

Reference burst detection identified the 1999 The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM) studies by Nienaber CA and Dake MD as the most impactful transformative research (burst strength 19.36, 2000–2024)-a burst duration of 24 years that underscores their foundational and enduring influence on the field (Fig. 5d). These two studies are the de facto origin of modern TBAD management, as they revolutionized the field from open surgical repair to minimally invasive endovascular therapy. Additionally, burst literature is mostly published in high-impact factor journals such as NEJM, Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA), Circulation, and Journal of Vascular and Interventional Radiology, providing theoretical foundations and methodological references for subsequent research.

Analysis of research status and hotspot trends

Keyword co-occurrence

The study analyzed the research hotspots and thematic architecture in the field of aortic dissection (Fig. 6a). The results reveal that aortic dissection constitutes the core research theme of this domain, which is further subdivided into specialized subfields represented by the terms TBAD, TEVAR, and endovascular repair. Specifically, the red cluster encompasses basic mechanism research focused on thoracic aorta, false lumen, and aortic remodeling; the green cluster centers on treatment technology keywords including endovascular repair and stent graft; and the purple cluster features methodological terms such as computational fluid dynamics and meta-analysis. This clustering pattern fully reflects the multi-dimensional research landscape of the field.

Applying CiteSpace software for keyword co-occurrence analysis, keywords such as “aortic dissection,” “management,” “aneurysm,” and “endovascular repair” occupy core positions in this field and show obvious clustering phenomena, indicating that the field of TBAD has formed multiple research themes such as clinical management strategies, minimally invasive interventional therapy, complication mechanism exploration, and multicenter data sharing (Fig. 6b). Keyword density

Using VOSviewer software to visually analyze the occurrence density and research hotspots of high-frequency keywords in the aortic dissection research field (Fig. 6c and Table 6). “Aortic dissection” is located in the most central red area of the figure. “TBAD”, “TEVAR”, and “endovascular repair” also appear in reddish-orange, indicating they also have high research heat and attention in this field. This shows that in the TBAD field, research hotspots mainly focus on disease classification and the evolution of endovascular repair techniques. Medium-heat areas revolve around stent application, true/false lumen formation, and complication management, reflecting the research trend of optimizing clinical pathways and postoperative outcomes.

Critically, the analysis also identified peripheral low-heat areas (blue) of Marfan syndrome, aortic dissection during pregnancy, spinal cord ischemia—these are unaddressed research gaps that represent critical clinical needs. Marfan syndrome and pregnancy are high-risk populations for TBAD with unique clinical characteristics that are underrepresented in current research; spinal cord ischemia is a devastating TEVAR complication with no standardized prevention or treatment strategy. The identification of these gaps is one of the most clinically relevant findings of this bibliometric analysis, as it guides future research resource allocation to address unmet clinical needs.

Keyword burst

By conducting burst analysis on literature keywords from 2000 to 2024, keyword burst strength analysis clearly revealed the evolutionary trends of research focuses in the field of aortic dissection and related diseases (Fig. 7b). Keywords with the highest burst strength include “stent graft placement” (strength 19.68, starting in 2008), “placement” (17.91, starting in 2000), “aneurysm” (14.32, starting in 2000), and “stent-graft” (12.41, starting in 2003). These keywords mainly revolve around endovascular stent implantation technology, indicating that with the maturation of minimally invasive treatment methods, this technology has become a long-term and continuous research hotspot in this field.

From a temporal dimension, most keyword bursts began between 2000 and 2010 and continued until 2024, showing that these research directions have high continuity and stability rather than being short-term hotspots. In particular, therapeutic research represented by keywords like “stent graft repair”, “operation”, and “therapy”, along with technological updates and iterations, exhibits characteristics of high burst strength and long duration. This means that research hotspots in the aortic dissection field are centered on stent implantation-based endovascular treatment technology, while continuously expanding in areas such as complication management, surgical pathway optimization, and follow-up evaluation, presenting a research pattern of “technology-driven + clinical evidence-based emphasis”. The keyword evolution map reveals a research trajectory from emergency surgical management toward computational modeling and intelligent prediction. The emergence of “deep learning,” “prediction model,” and “risk stratification” emphasizes the digital transformation of TBAD research, while persistent keywords around “stent graft” and “remodeling” indicate continued clinical focus on procedural optimization. The field is thus transitioning from technique validation to data-driven precision medicine.

Discussion

As a life-threatening cardiovascular emergency, improving the diagnosis and treatment of TBAD is of great clinical significance for reducing patient mortality and improving prognosis. This study moves beyond descriptive bibliometric mapping to provide a comprehensive interpretive analysis of TBAD research from 2000 to 2024, identifying key evolutionary patterns, scientific implications, clinical implications, and critical research gaps. The bibliometric findings reveal a field that has matured dramatically over the past two decades-from a focus on open surgical repair and early endovascular proof-of-concept to a data-driven, intelligent precision medicine field-with clear patterns in global collaboration, research hotspots, and knowledge evolution. Below, we interpret the core bibliometric findings and their implications for clinical practice, research strategy, and global TBAD management, and highlight evidence-based future directions to address the identified gaps.

Status of countries, institutions, authors, and references in the TBAD research field

A key spatial finding of this study is the identification of a dual-core global collaboration structure formed by the USA and China, which reflects the redistribution of cardiovascular research capabilities in the 21st century. The USA maintains a leading position in high-impact translational research through multicenter clinical trials, cross-institutional collaborations, and global registry systems. China has emerged as a core node, driven by sustained increases in research investment and a well-developed clinical infrastructure, effectively addressing the underrepresentation of Asian patients in global TBAD clinical trials.

However, a persistent citation gap between the USA, China, and other emerging nations remains a critical challenge. This gap stems not from insufficient publication volume but from limited international collaboration and insufficient translational medicine impact: Chinese studies are predominantly single-center, lacking international trials or cross-border data sharing, and often focus on clinical description rather than innovation. This finding underscores the imperative for China and other emerging economies to augment cross-border collaborations among institutions and researchers, and to accord priority to engagement in global registry-based studies, thereby strengthening their influence in the field of translational medicine.

The results of institutional collaboration highlight the pivotal role of translational hubs (such as University of Michigan, Harvard Medical School), which drive scientific progress by integrating clinical practice, basic science, and technological innovation. This model serves as a reference for emerging institutions (such as Fudan University, Capital Medical University). Chinese research institutions should move beyond the limitations of single-center studies by establishing in-house basic science and AI teams focused on TBAD hemodynamics, stent-graft innovation, and risk prediction, thereby enhancing translational outcomes through the integration of laboratory research and clinical practice.

The core-periphery structure of the author network facilitates knowledge diffusion and innovation. Future collaborations should prioritize cross-team and interdisciplinary partnerships involving surgeons, engineers, AI researchers, and epidemiologists, among others, to advance precision medicine, intelligent TBAD management, and address interindividual heterogeneity among patients.

Discrepancy between publication volume and evidence level in TBAD research

Notably, a striking discrepancy exists between the continuous growth of TBAD-related publications and the low level of evidence in clinical guidelines. The newly released 2026 ESVS Guidelines on the Management of Descending Thoracic and Thoraco-Abdominal Aortic Diseases revealed that 93.8% of recommendations are categorized as Level C evidence, relying primarily on expert consensus or low-quality studies rather than high-grade evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or large-scale prospective cohorts [24]. This discrepancy can be attributed to four key factors. First, the literature is dominated by retrospective studies, case reports, and single-center experiences, while RCTs and multi-center prospective studies are extremely scarce. The low frequency of keywords such as “randomized controlled trial” and the lack of high-impact clusters further confirm this research type bias. Second, the rapid iteration of endovascular technologies has outpaced the accumulation of long-term clinical evidence. Although TEVAR has become the mainstream treatment for TBAD, data on long-term outcomes such as aortic remodeling, complication recurrence, and survival beyond 5 years remain insufficient to support the formulation of Level A or B recommendations [25, 26]. Third, TBAD exhibits high clinical heterogeneity, with significant variations in patient anatomy, comorbidities, and treatment strategies, making it challenging to standardize research designs and homogenize results across studies. Fourth, emerging research hotspots, such as AI-assisted diagnosis and computational fluid dynamics simulations, which have experienced a burst in keywords since 2020, are still in the preliminary exploration stage. Most studies focus on algorithm development or mechanism simulation rather than clinical validation, resulting in a lack of mature evidence chains that can be incorporated into guidelines. This discrepancy highlights the urgent need for TBAD research to shift from “quantity expansion” to “quality improvement”.

Evolution and core orientation of keywords in TBAD research field

From the keyword co-occurrence and clustering in the literature, since the 21st century, “aortic dissection” has been the core term in this field, giving rise to multiple research subfields such as “TBAD”, “TEVAR”, and “endovascular repair”. This clustering structure indicates that research has expanded multi-dimensionally from basic pathological mechanisms and anatomical structures to endovascular repair techniques, complication prediction, and patient management strategies. The time evolution map and burst analysis also depict the migration path of the research focus from a dynamic dimension. Early research mainly focused on stent implantation technology.

In 1999, Dake’s research team first reported successful stent-graft placement in patients with acute TBAD, while Nienaber’s research team reported experimental results in patients with subacute and chronic AD [15, 19]. The publication of these two studies, to some extent, affirmed the advantages of stent implantation technology. This technology not only reduced the risks of open surgery but also provided another solution when dissections occurred in locations unsuitable for surgery. Subsequent studies have further confirmed the high success rates and feasibility of TEVAR for TBAD [27, 28]. However, some studies have also pointed out issues such as unclear efficacy and the potential for severe complications like retrograde type A aortic dissection [17, 29]. Overall, although TEVAR has certain limitations, it is still a superior treatment method compared to surgical therapy [14, 30, 31].

The occurrence of AD originates from tears in the aortic intima, which is inevitably related to blood flow impact. With the development of computer simulation technology, since 2015, keywords such as “computational fluid dynamics” and “biomechanical model” have frequently appeared. Incorporating hemodynamic factors into research has opened an important direction for exploring the pathogenesis of AD. Hemodynamic simulation and biomechanical modeling have also been gradually applied to postoperative evaluation and risk prediction. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) uses computer-based simulations to analyze fluid flow and has important potential in identifying potential hemodynamic risk factors for TBAD complications [32]. Highly disturbed flow, prolonged residence time, and abnormally low Wall Shear Stress are all markers of pathological hemodynamic changes in aortic dissection [33–35]. For TBAD, developing different computational models to predict thrombus formation, growth, and its impact on blood flow under physiological conditions, and applying them to patient-specific cases, will help clinicians develop personalized treatment plans for patients in the future [36, 37].

After 2020, keywords such as “deep learning,” “prediction model,” and “risk stratification” have strongly burst, marking that AI technology is becoming an important driving force in this field. Deep learning and model prediction have injected new vitality into the diagnosis, treatment, and postoperative recovery of TBAD. Using deep learning, it is possible to diagnose whether a patient with acute chest pain has AD with the assistance of electrocardiograms and chest X-rays [38]. It also provides valuable tools for automatically detecting and differentiating Stanford type A and B in Computed Tomography Angiography scans and for accurate segmentation of the entire aorta, true lumen, and false lumen [39, 40]. Regarding the postoperative period, deep learning-based models can help clinicians automatically predict postoperative adverse events in patients with acute uncomplicated Stanford TBAD who undergo initial TEVAR, as well as distal aortic remodeling after TEVAR for acute or subacute TBAD [41, 42]. In summary, the development of AI technology has opened up new prospects for the prevention and treatment of TBAD.

Key research gaps in the TBAD research field

The key strength of this bibliometric analysis lies in its ability to identify critical, evidence-based research gaps derived from keyword density analysis and thematic evolution analysis. These unresolved research issues represent unmet clinical needs and should be prioritized as key directions for future investigation.

Low-frequency keywords for Marfan syndrome, aortic dissection during pregnancy, and pediatric TBAD highlight the underrepresentation of high-risk and rare populations in current research. These populations have unique clinical characteristics, such as connective tissue disease in Marfan syndrome and hormonal changes during pregnancy, which complicate TBAD management, and the lack of research on these high-risk populations may result in clinical guidelines failing to adequately address their specific needs. While TEVAR maintains high centrality, its weak connection with “long-term aortic remodeling” highlights a gap in long-term evidence. Research on critical TEVAR complications such as spinal cord ischemia, retrograde type A dissection, and incomplete false lumen thrombosis remains insufficient, with a lack of standardized prevention or treatment strategies. Future research should prioritize long-term follow-up studies of TEVAR patients to evaluate long-term aortic remodeling and mortality. Keyword analysis revealed insufficient cross-disciplinary integration among CFD, AI, and genomics, with CFD-AI clusters remaining distant from genomics clusters in the co-occurrence network. This gap impedes the development of fully personalized precision medicine for TBAD by preventing the integration of hemodynamic, genetic, and clinical data to establish individualized risk stratification and treatment models. A further related limitation is the lack of global cross-disciplinary data standards, which hinders international data sharing and the construction of large-scale AI models for TBAD.

Development trends and prospects in the TBAD field

As an important interdisciplinary research field at the intersection of vascular surgery and cardiology, TBAD has formed a relatively clear and continuously evolving knowledge structure and research context over the past two decades. Currently, TBAD research is at a critical turning point from “structural accumulation” to “intelligent leap.” Future research will continue to deepen around the trinity development direction of “mechanism exploration-precision intervention-intelligent diagnosis and treatment,” relying on big data and AI technologies to achieve the transformation and upgrade from empirical medicine to precision medicine.

First, the importance of clinical experimental research should be emphasized. Future efforts should prioritize the design and execution of multicenter randomized controlled trials and prospective registries, standardized data collection (such as expanding the International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection), to bridge the gap between the expanding body of literature and the clinical evidence needed for guideline development, ultimately promoting the transformation of TBAD management from experience-based to evidence-based practice. Second, the advancement of precision medicine through interdisciplinary integration. Priority should be given to conducting interdisciplinary research that integrates CFD, biomechanical modeling, genomics, and clinical data to construct fully personalized management models for TBAD [43]. For instance, hemodynamic modeling based on individual patient characteristics can guide procedures such as stent-graft placement. Third, the acceleration of intelligent healthcare development through AI and big data. A global TBAD big data platform employing unified data standards should be established to support the development of large-scale, generalizable AI models [39, 44]. Examples include rapid and accurate AI-assisted Computed Tomography angiography diagnosis and AI-based risk stratification for acute TBAD.

In summary, TBAD research is evolving towards precision, intelligence, and global coordination. Further strengthening multidisciplinary integration and international cooperation in the future is expected to drive the continuous advancement of aortic disease management from standardization to individualization and from empiricism to evidence-based practice, providing stronger theoretical support and practical guidance for enhancing clinical efficacy and patient prognosis.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis reveals that TBAD research has matured significantly over the past two decades, evolving from early technical exploration of endovascular interventions to an intelligent, data-driven precision medicine field. The key findings reveal a USA-China dual-core collaboration structure that underscores the redistribution of global cardiovascular research capacity, alongside a three-phase knowledge evolution encompassing pathophysiology, followed by the evidence-based standardization of TEVAR through landmark trials and guidelines, and more recently the integration of computational fluid dynamics and artificial intelligence. This trajectory confirms the establishment of a mature “mechanism-intervention-methodology” knowledge framework. Four critical research gaps that represent pressing unmet clinical needs are identified: understudied high-risk populations (such as Marfan syndrome, pregnancy-associated dissection, pediatric cases); insufficient long-term TEVAR outcome data; and limited cross-disciplinary integration among CFD, AI, and genomics. Future research should prioritize multicenter prospective studies and randomized controlled trials to elevate the evidence base; advance interdisciplinary precision medicine models that integrate hemodynamics, genomics, and clinical data; and establish globally standardized big data platforms to enable robust AI model development and validation.

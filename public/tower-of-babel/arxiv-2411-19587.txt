Bounds in radial Moore graphs of diameter 3
Jesús M. Ceresuela, Nacho López

Abstract
Radial Moore graphs are approximations of Moore graphs that preserve the distance-preserving spanning tree for its central vertices. One way to classify their resemblance with a Moore graph is the status measure. The status of a graph is defined as the sum of the distances of all pairs of ordered vertices and equals twice the Wiener index. In this paper we study upper bounds for both the maximum number of central vertices and the status of radial Moore graphs. Finally, we present a family of radial Moore graphs of diameter 3 3 that is conjectured to have maximum status.

Introduction

The number of vertices of a graph that has maximum degree
d
and maximum diameter
k
is upper bounded by

M(d,k)=1+d+d(d-1)+d(d-1)^{2}+\cdots+d(d-1)^{k-1}.

(1)

Hoffman and Singleton gave the name of Moore graphs to graphs holding the mentioned restrictions and attaining the bound
[
1
]
. Also the bound is known nowadays as the Moore bound.

The existence of Moore graphs is a well-studied and almost-closed problem (see
[
2
]
). They must be regular of degree

and the fact that Moore graphs exist for few values of

and

entails the question of how close can you get to this structures with feasible graphs. One way to do it is to preserve the Moore bound as the order of the candidate graphs as well as the regularity of the graph, and then relax the diameter condition a bit, by allowing the existence of some vertices in the graph with eccentricity just
k+1
. These graphs are named radial Moore graphs. More specifically, given two integers
d
and

, a radial Moore graph is a
d
-regular graph of order

with radius
k
and diameter
k+1
.

The existence of such graphs has been thoroughly studied. In
[
3
]
the authors give a construction of a radial Moore graph of diameter
3
, for all values of
d
. Radial Moore graphs of diameter
4
also exist for every value of

(see
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
). The existence of radial Moore graphs for any value of

[
7
]
. This problem has also been addressed in the directed
[
8
]
and mixed
[
9
]
version.

Radial Moore graphs are conceived to approach Moore graphs. There are many radial Moore graphs in some cases (
1062
for
(d,k)=(3,3)
, see
[
3
]
). Thus, a natural question would be, given the parameters
d
and
k
, which is (
are
) the closest radial Moore graph (
graphs
) to being a Moore graph. To quantify the difference between two graphs, a closeness measure must be defined. A very suitable option is the status norm used in
[
3
]
and in
[
9
]
that sorts the radial Moore graphs according to the status vector. This measure is very convenient since every vertex in a Moore graph has the same status

(2)

(see
[

Bound on the maximum number of central vertices in a radial Moore graph

There are two kinds of vertices in a radial Moore graph of radius
k

central vertices
are those vertices with eccentricity

Since every vertex is a central vertex in a Moore graph, we would like to know how many central vertices may exist at most in a radial Moore graph. We will denote this value as

by construction of a family of graphs of degree

having

[
11
]
by exploiting the relationship between central and non-central vertices in a cubic radial Moore graph. Here we extend these ideas to provide a general upper bound for any degree

Every non-central vertex in a radial Moore graph must have at least two non-central neighbours.

Lemma 3.1

Let

On one side, since the order of the graph is

|\Gamma_{2}(v)|+|\Gamma_{3}(v)|=d^{2}+1-(|\Gamma_{0}(v)|+|\Gamma_{1}(v)|)=d(d-1).

(d-1)^{2}-\alpha
descendants of
\Gamma_{1}(c)\setminus\{v\}
regarding every vertex
i\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}
:

Sketch of the distance tree that must have a neighbor of a central vertex with maximum status in a radial Moore graph.

1.

A maximum of
d-g_{i}-1
can see vertex
i
in one step through a direct edge.

2.

A maximum of
d-2
vertices can see vertex
i
in two steps via its ascendant.

3.

A maximum of
(d-g_{i}-1)(d-2)
can see vertex
i
in 2 steps through an adjacency with one of the

direct neighbors in the same level.

4.

The number of vertices that see
i
in 3 steps are forced to see some
i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}
,
i\neq i^{\prime}
in one step in order to reach the ascendants of
i
in 3 steps (remember that for every vertex in
\Gamma_{1}(v)\setminus\{c\}
only one edge goes to some vertex
\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}
), so there is a total of

\sum_{j=1,j\neq i^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(d-g_{j}-1).

These imply the following inequalities for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}
:

(d-2)+(d-g_{i}-1)(d-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(d-g_{j}-1)\geq(d-1)^{2}-\alpha

(5)

where the terms coming from statements 1 and 4 have been gathered in the same summation. Developing this inequality, and taking into account (
4
) we get

g_{i}\leq\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{d-2}.

Since it was also stated that
g_{i}\leq d-\alpha
, the following holds in general

g_{i}\leq\min\left(d-\alpha,\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{d-2}\right).

This can be also written

g_{i}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{d-2}&\text{if }\alpha<\frac{d}{2}\\
d-\alpha&\text{if }\alpha\geq\frac{d}{2}\end{array}\right.

This means that depending on the value of
d
and
\alpha
, there
g_{i}
reaches a maximum value that cannot be exceeded in order that the diameter of the graph is 3. Let

g_{max}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\left\lfloor\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{d-2}\right\rfloor&\text{if }\alpha<\frac{d}{2}\\
d-\alpha&\text{if }\alpha\geq\frac{d}{2}\end{array}\right.

(6)

be the maximum possible, according to (
7
), number of edges incident from vertices
\Gamma_{1}(v)\setminus\{c\}
to a vertex
i=1,\ldots,\alpha
. In order that all
\Gamma_{1}(v)\setminus\{c\}
vertices have one adjacency to vertices
i=1,\ldots,\alpha
it must hold

\alpha\cdot g_{max}\geq d-1.

(7)

If
\alpha<d/2
this condition is written

\alpha\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{d(\alpha-1)}{d-2}\right\rfloor\geq d-1.

(8)

If
\alpha\geq d/2
it can be easily checked that the inequality (
7
) always holds, so this does not provide an extra constraint. Since in this case (
8
) also holds, it can be stated as a general condition for
\alpha
. Now, since
\lceil\frac{d}{d-2}\rceil=1
for
d>4
, the minimum value for
\alpha

8
) is the ceiling of the largest root of the polynomial
x^{2}-x-(d-1)
, which is
\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{4d-3}}{2}\right\rceil
(and in fact this value also coincides for
d=3
and differs just by one unit for
d=4
).
∎

The previous lemma can be used to obtain an upper bound on the status of any vertex of a radial Moore graph of diameter
3
.

Corollary 3.2
.

The status of a vertex
v
in a radial Moore graph of degree
d
and diameter
3
satisfies
s(v)\leq 3d(d-1)
. Moreover, if the graph contains one central vertex and
v
is a neighbor of it then

s(v)\leq 3d^{2}-3d-\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{4d-3}}{2}\right\rceil+1.

(9)

for
d\neq 4
, and
s(v)\leq 35
for
d=4
.

Proof.

The maximum status of a vertex in a radial Moore graph of diameter
3
will be attained when the number of neighbors at distance two is minimum. According to the previous lemma, we know that
|\Gamma_{2}(v)|\geq d
. A vertex with maximal status would be one such that
|\Gamma_{1}(v)|=d
,
|\Gamma_{2}(v)|=d
and
|\Gamma_{3}(v)|=d(d-2)
and thus, the status would be
d+2d+3d(d-2)=3d(d-1)
. The same counting argument with the corresponding improved bound for neighbors of the central vertex produces the other result.
∎

We recall that the status

of a graph

is the sum of the status of all of its vertices. For instance, the status of a Moore graph of degree
d
and diameter
3

2d^{2}-d
. This is precisely a lower bound for the status of a radial Moore graph of the same parameters. An upper bound for the value of

is provided next.

Corollary 3.3
.

The status of a radial Moore graph
G
of degree
d

3
, containing one central vertex, satisfies

s(G)\leq 3d^{4}-3d^{3}+2d^{2}-d\cdot\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{4d-3}}{2}\right\rceil

(10)

Proof.

We have that
s(G)=\sum_{v_{i}\in V(G)}s(v_{i})
. Also,
G
has order
d^{2}+1
and it must contain at least one central vertex (with status
d(2d-1)
). The upper bound (

) for the neighbors of this central vertex and
s(v)\leq 3d(d-1)

s(G)\leq 174
and
s(H)\leq 596
for degrees

3d^{2}-3d

3d^{2}-4d+2
instead. One way to construct them is the following:

1.

Let
d\geq 3
, and let us take
d
disjoint copies of the complete graph
K_{d-1}
that we will denote as

.

2.

Attach a

Finally take the bipartite graph

Roughly speaking, if we hang this graph from vertex

Proposition 3.4
.

Any graph
G_{d}
constructed as above is a radial Moore graph of degree
d
, diameter

and it has vector status

{\bf s}(G_{d}):(d^{2}-d)^{1},(3d^{2}-4d+2)^{d^{2}}.

Proof.

G_{d}
is a clearly an

by construction (it begins with an (

There are many symmetries in the graph

, as one may expect looking at the examples

contains

(the subgraph induced by vertex

is precisely the permutation group of

), and this is precisely what retains the full graph

from the symmetries of its induced complete subgraphs, as next proposition shows.

Proposition 3.5
.

The automorphism group of
G_{d}
is the symmetric group of order
d
, that is,
\textrm{Aut}(G_{d})\cong S_{d}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the labeling of the vertices of
G_{d}
as given above (see Fig.
6
). Let
\varphi_{i,j}\in S_{d}
be the transposition
(i\ j)
, that is, the permutation of the set
\{1,2,\dots,d\}
which exchanges elements
i
and
j
and keeps all others fixed, for all
i\neq j
. For each transposition
(i\ j)
we define the mapping
\Phi_{ij}:V(G_{d})\rightarrow V(G_{d})
such that:

\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi_{ij}(0)=0;\\
\Phi_{ij}(a)=\varphi_{ij}(a),\ \forall a\in\{1,\dots,d\};\\
\Phi_{ij}((a,b))=(\varphi_{ij}(a),\varphi_{ij}(b)),\ \forall a,b\in\{1,\dots,d\},\ a\neq b.\end{array}

Clearly
\Phi_{ij}
is a graph automorphism of
G_{d}
since all the adjacencies are preserved. As a consequence, every permutation of
S_{d}
induces an automorphism of
G_{d}
.

On the other hand, given an automorphism
\phi
of
G_{d}
we have that
\phi(0)=0
since it is the unique vertex of eccentricity
2
. Besides, every vertex
v\in\Gamma_{l}(0)
must be mapped into the same set
\Gamma_{l}(0)
, for
l=1,2
, since the distance preserving spanning tree of any vertex in
\Gamma_{1}(0)
is different from the one of a vertex in
\Gamma_{2}(0)
. Hence, the automorphism
\phi
restricted for the set of vertices in
\Gamma_{1}(0)
defines a permutation
\sigma\in S_{d}
where
\sigma(i)=\phi(i)
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}(=\Gamma_{1}(0))
. Now we will see that
\sigma
determines also the mapping
\phi
for the vertices in
\Gamma_{2}(0)
. Indeed, since
i\sim(i,j)
, it turns out that for every
j\neq i
, it must exist
k\neq\sigma(i)
such that
\phi((i,j))=(\sigma(i),k)
. In addition
(i,j)\sim(j,i)
and hence
\phi((i,j))\sim\phi((j,i))
, that is,
(\sigma(i),k)\sim(\sigma(j),k^{\prime})
, that is,
k=\sigma(j)
. Hence,
\phi((i,j))=(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))
proving that any automorphism of
G_{d}
induces a permutation of
S_{d}
.
∎

4
Final remarks and open problems

Radial Moore graphs appear as an approximation to Moore graphs when the diameter condition is relaxed. The status of a vertex can be useful to test how good is this approach. In this paper we present an upper bound on the status of a radial Moore graph. We start by applying two already-known results recursively to obtain a lower bound on the number of non-central vertices. Then, we focus on the case of diameter
3
where we determine an upper bound in the status of every single vertex that is tight for some parameters of the degree
d
. An extra upper bound is presented for specific vertices. Finally, an infinite family of radial Moore graphs of diameter
3
with high status is presented and we strongly believe that is maximal in terms of the status.

Conjecture 4.1

The radial Moore graph
G_{d}

d\geq 3
.

The upper bound for the maximum number of central vertices in a radial Moore graph presented in Prop.
2.3

, since it provides the value
{\cal C}(d,2)\leq M(d,2)-6
which seems to be far from the exact number of
{\cal C}(d,2)
for large

. Even for
d=7
, where a Moore graphs exists,
{\cal C}(7,2)
seems to be

Improve the bounds for
{\cal C}(d,k)
, specially for the case

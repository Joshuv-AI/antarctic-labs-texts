Empowerment and innovation: Advancing oncology nursing across the Asia–Pacific
Authors: Jianfei Xie, Winnie KW So
Published: 2026

As we approach International Nurses Day 2026, the global nursing community embraces a theme that resonates profoundly with the mission of the Asia–Pacific Journal of Oncology Nursing (APJON): “Our Nurses. Our Future. Empowered Nurses Save Lives”.1 For oncology nurses across the Asia–Pacific region—a vast and diverse landscape bearing more than half the world's cancer burden2—this theme is not merely aspirational; it is an urgent call to action. The Asia–Pacific region presents unique challenges and opportunities for advancement in cancer care. Across settings ranging from highly resourced centers with advanced technologies to resource-constrained communities, oncology nurses confront diverse challenges in daily pratice. However, within this diversity lies tremendous potential for innovation, collaboration, and transformation.

What does empowerment mean for oncology nurses in our region?

José Luis Cobos Serrano, President of the International Council of Nurses, emphasized that empowerment requires “eliminating barriers to enable nurses to fully utilize their professional knowledge and skills, and to establish institutional pathways for nurses to advocate and participate in shaping the future of nursing”.1 In the Asia–Pacific context, this call translates into three urgent priorities for oncology nursing.

Firstly, the foundation of empowerment lies in creating a safe and supportive practice environment. Growing evidence3,4 indicates that nurse staffing, workload, and job satisfaction are closely associated with the quality and safety of cancer care and patient outcomes. When workloads are excessive, core elements of care such as symptom management, psychosocial support, and patient education are more likely to be compromised. It is essential to ensure that every oncology nurse—whether in urban teaching hospitals or rural community clinics—has access to adequate resources and professional support to provide safe and compassionate care for patients affected by cancer.

Secondly, the core of empowerment lies in sustained investment in specialized education and capacity building. Over the past decade, significant progress has been made in oncology nursing education across the Asia–Pacific region,5 although important gaps remain. APJON has consistently served as a vehicle for disseminating innovative educational approaches and a platform for sharing cross-border best practices. It is imperative to continue promoting these initiatives through distant mentoring programs, virtual learning platforms, and regional collaborations, ensuring that high-quality educational resources reach nurses in remote and underserved areas.

Thirdly, the key to empowerment lies in broadening the pathways for the cultivation and research innovation of nursing leadership. As highlighted in the recent call for submissions to APJON, oncology nurses in the region are increasingly leading research, from clinical trials and nurse-led innovations to patient-reported outcomes.6 The special issue “From Clinical Trials to Nurse-Led Innovation: Nurses Driving Advances in Oncology Research” underscores the deepening industry consensus: nurses are not only executors of research but also catalysts for discovery. Meanwhile, the exploration of artificial intelligence applications in cancer care presents new challenges for oncology nurses, prompting them to consider how technology can better serve patient care.7

APJON's role in advancing empowerment

As the official journal of the Asian Oncology Nursing Society, APJON holds unique platform value in advancing regional oncology nursing empowerment. Our scope encompasses the entire spectrum of oncology nursing—from prevention and early detection to survivorship care and palliative care—with a focus on nursing innovations and clinical research findings. As a journal indexed in major databases including the Science Citation Index Expanded and PubMed Central, we provide a professional platform for oncology nurses in the Asia–Pacific region to share knowledge, showcase excellence, and amplify their voices globally.

The empowerment theme aligns closely with our editorial vision. Under the framework of empowerment, oncology nurses conduct rigorous research, implement evidence-based innovations, and lead practice transformations to genuinely improve patient care outcomes. When their work is published and disseminated through journals such as APJON, its influence transcends institutional boundaries, reshaping the development of oncology nursing practices in the Asia–Pacific region and contributing to global learning.

A call to our Asia–Pacific colleagues

As we commemorate International Nurses Day 2026, we should reflect on the practical meaning and implications of empowerment across the region's diverse contexts: Empowerment begins at the individual level, where a nurse is equipped with specialized oncology knowledge to ensure safe and precise care for every patient at the bedside. It expands to the community level, where nurses lead train-the-trainer programs, sharing expertise to make quality care more effective and reachable for larger populations. Ultimately, at the regional level, empowerment means nurses driving policy and setting national standards, ensuring that high-quality cancer care is accessible to all, regardless of geography. The theme “Empowering Nurses, Protecting Lives” reflects our long-held belief: Investment in nursing will genuinely enhance patient safety, care quality, and the resilience of health systems. Let us, as Asia–Pacific oncology nurses, jointly commit to breaking down barriers, creating opportunities, and fostering an environment that enables nurses to shape the future of cancer care.

Through APJON, we will continue to provide a professional platform for your voices, your innovations, and your research. Together, across the Asia–Pacific region, let us turn empowerment into action, evidence and lasting change.

Happy International Nurses Day 2026!

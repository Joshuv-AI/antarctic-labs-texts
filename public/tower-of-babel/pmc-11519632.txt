Accessibility evaluation and multi-scenario optimization of medical services in underdeveloped city driven by multi-source data and latest policies for China
Authors: Jinhua Hu, Chenchen Peng, Yazhuo Hu, Yingying Wang, Hui Yan, Jingzhong Li, Shuna Xu, Shengyuan Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEquitable and high-quality medical services are more urgent in underdeveloped cities for the higher population ageing and demanding social justice. However, there is little attention paid to the multi-level medical services, particularly regarding the time indicators under the latest policies for underdeveloped cities. The improved efforts were hampered partly by single scenario of location optimization, ignoring the integrated optimization for both road infrastructure and institution location. Toward the healthy China 2030 and rural revitalization policy, this study systematically investigated medical services for underdeveloped cities by constructing a multi-level evaluation and multi-scenario optimization framework with Geographical Information System technology in a case study of Xuchang City in China. Following the time goals from the latest policies, the services of high-quality hospitals and primary health centers were multi-level evaluated by network analysis method and further optimized through multi-scenario involving different new multi-level medical facilities and roads. Driven by urban-rural inequalities, candidate facilities were first selected based on multi-source data and then determined by location‑allocation analysis method, while new roads were assumed by space syntax method. The improvement rose rapidly and finally slowly with the increasing number of candidates. Few new roads and facilities could be more suitable, and the priority was explored under the local economy and planning. The findings could provide valuable support for urban healthy development under the latest policies.Keywords: Medical services, Accessibility, Evaluation, Multi-scenario optimization, GISSubject terms: Health policy, Health services, Public health, Quality of life, Sustainability

Introduction

Public service facilities are important material carriers that provide public services to the residents (i.e. healthcare, education, and banking)1. Compared to other public service facilities, equitable and efficient access to medical service facilities is more urgent and essential for residents2, as health is a fundamental guarantee of people’s lives3,4. Moreover, medical facilities services are typically characterized by scarcity in quantity, inequitable distribution, and higher costs5,6. The equity of medical facilities services is a critical matter of social justice and harmony, which could directly relate to the healthy life of residents and affect the society’s development7. However, due to many political, economic, social and cultural factors, the current inequities in medical services between urban and rural areas have been expanding rapidly, which has been a significant concern for worldwide countries8.

As the largest developing country in the world, China has continuously strived to improve the equity and quality of medical care and alleviate the disparities in medical access for over 1.44 billion citizens9,10, such as setting the goal of “equalization of basic public services” in 2005, establishing the Hierarchical Diagnosis and Treatment System (including primary medical institution, secondary hospital, and tertiary hospital) in 2015, implementing rural revitalization strategies in 2017. In recent years, the Chinese president emphasized that the public with basic illnesses could get basic medical treatments in local community and those with minor illnesses could obtain service in local city or county; only those with serious illnesses need to seek care at high-level hospitals in the province during the 14th Five-Year Plan period (http://www.rmlt.com.cn/). In addition, China put forward a strategic goal that by 2030, a basic medical system with 15-min would cover the whole city in the “Healthy China 2030” blueprint11. For rural residents, the National Health Commission also promoted the 30-min health resource allocation goal by local common transportation or walking (http://www.nhc.gov.cn/). However, there are still many challenges for the healthcare service in the underdeveloped cities, such as, aging population, immature hierarchical diagnosis and treatment system, integration of urban and rural development, scarcity of medical resources, and the increasing demand of higher level healthcare services12,13. As the critical nodes of connecting urban and rural areas, underdeveloped cities can better promote rural development and the implementation of rural revitalization strategies. Therefore, more equal and high-quality service by multi-level medical facilities is one of the key points for the underdeveloped cities within golden medical time goals from the latest policies (i.e., the 30-min high-quality hospitals and 15-min primary health centers), which could not only improve the life quality of residents but also promote the construction of healthier and more sustainable cities14.

Consequently, specific emphasis has been placed on examining and improving the spatial efficiency and equity of medical service facilities15–17. Spatial accessibility was first proposed by Hansen since 195918 and has been widely acknowledged as a pivotal factor in the comprehensive assessment of healthcare accessibility19,20. With the rapid development of the powerful GIS software, there has been an increased interest in the spatial accessibility of medical service by GIS-based analysis in several countries, such as New Zealand21, Niger22, Australia23, United Kingdom24, Finland25, Canada26, USA27 and China6. Relevant studies on the evaluation of the spatial accessibility of medical service facilities mainly focus on the following aspects: (1) the evaluation of the accessibility to the single-level medical service facility at the whole Country (Tertiary hospitals in Finland25), Province (Primary healthcares in the state of Victoria, Australia28; Hospitals in Sichuan Province6), or metropolitan cities (Australia capital city29; Primary Healthcare Facilities in Fukuoka City, Japan30; Wuhan31, Shanghai32, Shenzhen33 in China); (2) the spatial distribution analysis of hospitals among a particular population to treat specific diseases34–36; (3) the comparison of accessibility to hospitals by different travel modes27,37 or over multiple periods38; (4) the evaluation of accessibility to healthcare services under the constraint of administrative boundary25,39. However, there are relatively few evaluated cases for underdeveloped regions, such as, the Wa West District in Ghana40, the Bungoma in Western Kenya41, the Deqing County in China42. These limited cases mainly focused on the spatial differences of single-level medical service facility (hospitals or health centers) by accessibility values42, without linking with time indicators from the latest policies for underdeveloped cities.

Improving the spatial accessibility of medical service facilities is also a complex spatial problem, posing a significant challenge for policy makers43. Most previous studies were mainly focused on location optimization and could be broadly classified as43,44: (1) the reallocation of currently existing healthcare resources45,46; (2) the allocation of newly added healthcare resources47. For example, Pan et al.43 applied a two-step optimization approach to investigate the spatial allocation of newly added tertiary general healthcare resources in Chengdu, a metropolitan city of China. To increase Shenzhen’s accessibility within a 15-min impedance, Chen et al.47 selected up to 567 candidate locations for new community healthcare centers by the maximal covering location problem model. Burkey et al.48 evaluated the locations of existing hospitals in four U.S. states, considering two objectives (minimizing hospital-patient distance and maximizing coverage). Fritze et al.49 explored the optimization layout of emergency medical service facilities in Austria to maximize coverage of residential areas. Luo et al.50 provided alternative options for Emergency Medical Service stations in the metropolitan area of Wuhan by a multi-objective model. Yu et al.51 optimized the spatial equality of medical accessibility by adding new medical facility sites and adjusting the capacity of medical facilities by the number of beds in Guangzhou City. Although existing research has laid a foundation for improving medical service equality, there are still gaps in the following areas: (1) Few accessibility-based medical equality optimization models considered variations in medical facility level51; (2) Most studies mainly focused on the single-scenario optimization of facility location in the metropolitan cities, ignoring integration with the optimal traffic road network; (3) There is a lack of exploration on the optimization effects under different time goals from the latest policies50.

In summary, the existing researches have established a foundation for understanding the spatial distribution and accessibility of medical service facilities, but there are still several points that require further attention under the rural revitalization and the healthy China 2030 strategy. Main attentions have been concentrated on the entire country, province or metropolitan city42. However, there has been a long-term dearth of interest for medical issues in underdeveloped cities, which include high-demanding elderly populations and an increasing gap between urban and rural areas50. Limited previous studies were mainly focused on single-level medical service facility, whereas it could not adequately reflect the comprehensive healthcare resources and meet the complex demand of higher-level healthcare services and better medical resource allocation for hierarchical diagnosis and treatment system in underdeveloped cities. Both evaluation and optimization studies emphasized the spatial differences by accessibility values, ignoring the linking of time indicators with the latest policies for underdeveloped cities52. For the complicated and noteworthy optimization problem, single-scenario optimization of institution location have been proposed and simulated, ignoring the integrated optimization for road infrastructure and institution location on the medical accessibility, especially considering the trade-off between investment cost and accessibility in the underdeveloped city53. Furthermore, the issue of deciding the optimal locations and road networks in a sequential process still requires further exploration to enhance the integration of urban and rural development43. Hence, a systematic and comprehensive framework for medical resources is urgently needed to address these previously discussed practical issues in underdeveloped cities. Moreover, the optimized model and results need to be assessed in a typical city with the unequal state of medical services.

In the current study, we aim to (1) propose a multi-level evaluation and multi-scenario optimization framework and implement in a case study of underdeveloped city; (2) comprehensively take the multi-level medical system of “high-quality hospital-basic health center” into consideration and deeply analyze the equal medical services in underdeveloped city for basic primary health center within the 15-min and high-quality hospital within the 30-min goal from the latest policies; (3) construct and simulate the improving multi-scenario for location optimization and road improvement by the location‑allocation analysis method and space syntax method; and (4) provide the scientific supports for the decisions on the location selection and road construction in a sequential process for the future city planning in the post-pandemic era. The remainder of the manuscript is structured as follows. The site description, data collection, and post-processing are introduced in Sect. 2. The evaluated and optimization methods are explained in Sect. 3. The assessed results of multi-level medical service facilities and the improving results of multi-scenarios are exhibited in Sect. 4, followed by the discussion in Sect. 5 and the conclusions in Sect. 6.

Study area and data

Site description

Xuchang City, known as “Lotus City”, is located in the central part of Henan Province in China (Fig. 1) and serves as a transportation and logistics center in the Central Plain Economic Zone (http://www.xuchang.gov.cn/). It is located at 113° 03′ to 114° 19′ E and 33°42′ to 34° 24′ N, covering an area of 4988.80 km2. Xuchang City is bordered by Zhengzhou City (provincial capital) to the north, Zhoukou City to the east, Luohe City to the south, Pingdingshan City to the west, and Kaifeng City to the northeast. It is a vital base for high-tech industries and is home to the ancient city of Cao Wei, a renowned historical and cultural city of the Han and Wei dynasties. The transportation is convenient with a fast and integrated system of highways, high-speed railways, intercity railways, and aviation. Overall, Xuchang City is one of the core cities in the Central Plain Urban Agglomeration and the Central Plain Economic Zone, playing a significant role in the economic and social development of Henan Province. At the end of 2023, Xuchang City had a permanent population of approximately 4.38 million and an urbanization rate of 55.2%. Moreover, a comparative analysis of economic and medical resource indicators between Xuchang City and the national average level reveals that the city’s development is slightly below the nationwide average level (Table 1). The comparison also highlighted that Xuchang City could serve as a representative underdeveloped city for exploring the accessibility of multi-level medical service facilities in underdeveloped cities under the rural revitalization and the Healthy China 2030 strategy.

In addition, Xuchang City is administratively divided into two municipal districts (Weidu and Jian’an), two county-level cities (Yuzhou and Changge) and two counties (Yanling and Xiangcheng), as well as 99 towns. Among them, Weidu District is the central area of Xuchang City and hosts the city government, serving as the political, economic, and cultural center. Data collection and processing

Taking Xuchang City in Henan Province as the primary research area, the dataset mainly included road dataset, statistical information of medical service facilities, administrative divisions, villages, population, DEM and land use data (Table 2). The road dataset was initially collected from the OpenStreetMap platform (https://www.openstreetmap.org/) and corrected with Google Earth satellite images. Then, the topological relationships of road were carefully checked, according to the rules of no pseudo-nodes, no overlapping lines, and no hanging points. The OpenStreetMap road data was categorized into 14 types, including motorway data, primary road data, secondary road data, and tertiary road data, among others (Fig. 2a). After the topological check, the roads were all segmented at the intersection points except for highways, so that all road line elements could become independent line segments. Xuchang City has a well-developed road network (Fig. 2a) and the total number of private car owners reached 0.9 million (https://gaj.xuchang.gov.cn/), which is equivalent to one car for every five people. With the rapid growth in urban vehicle ownership, self-driving has become one of the irreplaceable modes of transportation for residents to seek medical treatment54. In addition, the ageing population level is higher in underdeveloped cities. Although walking constitutes a large portion of daily travel, longer travel distances would hinder older individuals from walking to healthcare services due to declining physical mobility35. Considering the factors of ageing, low literacy, and safety, elderly people often rely on assistance from younger family members for medical treatment, with many preferring to drive their elders to seek medical care. Therefore, this study assesses spatial accessibility through the self-driving mode, aligning with similar studies42.

Under the referral reform of China’s hierarchical health system, the multi-level medical service facilities could be categorized into hospitals and health centers, according to the severity of a patient’s condition and the difficulty of treatment47. Specifically, multi-level medical institutions are responsible for treating different diseases. Hospitals offer comprehensive health services to a region, taking in referrals from primary hospitals, and undertaking teaching and research activities55. Health centers, including township health centers and community health service centers, provide general healthcare and preventive care services to the community.

The medical facility locations were extracted through the Web Services API (https://lbs.amap.com/), including 74 hospitals and 107 health centers (Fig. 2b). For the different district of Xuchang City, Weidu district has 36 hospitals (including 4 3 A hospitals), while Jian’an District, Xiangcheng County, Yanling County, Yuzhou County and Changge County have 3, 5, 6, 18 and 6 hospitals, respectively. There are respectively 13, 19, 17, 12, 26 and 20 health centers in Weidu District, Jian’an District, Xiangcheng County, Yanling County, Yuzhou County and Changge County.

The population data were sourced from the up-to-date population dataset at a 100-m resolution based on China’s seventh census, which was produced by a novel population downscaling approach that leverages stacking ensemble learning and geospatial big data56 (Fig. 2c). The DEM data with a resolution of 12.5 m and land use data (Fig. 2d-e) were acquired from the NASA Earth data (https://nasadaacs.eos.nasa.gov/) and the EULUC-China dataset57, respectively. Finally, the spatial administrative boundary vector data and village data were both derived from the Resource and Environment Science and Data Center of the Chinese Academy of Science (https://www.resdc.cn/) (Fig. 2f).

Methods

To address the issue, a multi-level evaluation and multi-scenario optimization framework for medical service facilities accessibility was constructed mainly based on the service area analysis method for accessibility evaluation, the location‑allocation analysis method for location optimization and the space syntax method for road improvement (Fig. 3). The accessibility of multi-level medical service facilities was initially evaluated for each administrative region within different time goals by the service area analysis method in the Network Analysis toolbox of ArcGIS software (version 10.2, https://www.esri.com). Considering the development planning and practical economic support in underdeveloped cities, multi-scenario optimizations were put forward after identifying low-accessibility areas, which mainly included spatial optimization scenarios of multi-level medical service facilities locations, improvement scenarios of road infrastructure and facilities locations. The first one could be further classified as multi-scenario optimizations involving different numbers of newly added hospitals and health centers, while the second one could be further classified as multi-scenario optimizations involving newly roads, added hospitals and health centers. In specific, preliminary candidate facilities were first selected according to various factors and further imported into the complementary coverage (MCLP-CC) model of the location-allocation analysis method to optimize the final spatial locations within different time goals. The location-allocation analysis method was also carried out by Network Analysis toolbox of ArcGIS software (version 10.2, https://www.esri.com). Meanwhile, the construction of new roads was considered based on the road evaluations by the space syntax method in the Axwoman software (version 6.3, http://livablecitylab.hkust-gz.edu.cn/binjiang/axwoman). Finally, a comparative analysis before and after the multi-scenario optimization was conducted, which is aimed to provide more equal and high-quality medical services in underdeveloped cities, China.

Service area analysis method for accessibility evaluation

Geographic Information System (GIS) is a powerful tool for collecting and analyzing data from various sources, such as remote sensing and ground-based mapping58. With the strong theoretical background, solid scientific foundation, and wide variety of methodological approaches59, network analysis method is a significant tool in the GIS software. It could be used for finding the service coverage and shortest pathways, locating the nearest facilities, selecting the best site, and determining the optimum routes for a fleet of vehicles60. The vector data-based service area analysis method could calculate travel time more accurately in urban and town areas, as complicated road network (e.g. overpass and underpass) and detailed traffic rules (e.g. one-way and turn restrictions) can be considered to represent a multiple-mode transport network42. GIS software and the service area analysis method are widely used in urban geographical studies, particularly in urban and rural planning, to examine the service area or functional radius of specific facilities15,23.

As the network analysis method was performed based on a network dataset in the Geodatabase, the process of creating the road network dataset was introduced in detail, including the establishment of the Geodatabase and the attribute settings for the network dataset (i.e., connectivity, turn restrictions, and impedance type). First, the Geodatabase was established with road dataset, medical service facilities and administrative divisions. The attributes of roads were assigned before constructing the network dataset, including the classification, length, velocity and travel time. Based on the speed limits stipulated in the urban comprehensive transportation system planning standard (GB/T51328), the velocity for each road class was set (Table 3) and then the length and travel time were calculated accordingly. Considering the traffic delays, turn penalties, and traffic signal delays, it was simplified assumption that all roads were allowed bidirectional travel and average 30 s were setting as waiting time for the road intersection61. Additionally, excepting highways only connect with other roads at the entrance and exit points, all other roads connect at any node. Given the different standards for driving speeds on various urban roads, time impedance and distance impedance were added in the attribute settings of the network dataset. The impedance type was set as “cost”, using the corresponding fields in the attribute table as the cost values, measured in minutes (time impedance) and meters (distance impedance). Finally, all previous information was listed for confirming and the road network dataset was completely created after setting a series of traveling rules.

Spatial accessibility can be used to measure the difficulty, of which the residents in a given area can reach a particular service or facility62–64. Service ratio is one of the most popular indices for measuring the spatial accessibility51,65, as it is a convenient and efficient way to evaluate the distribution differences of public service resources across different provinces, cities and counties66. A higher accessibility area ratio indicates better accessibility to medical service facilities, suggesting that these facilities can serve a larger portion of this area67. In this paper, the spatial accessibility to medical service facilities is defined as the ratio of service area and population in different regions under different travel times. In particular, service area analysis method is a key method in network analysis tool, based on the actual traffic road network. Under different driving mode, the service area analysis method could calculate all the streets that reach medical facilities within the given impedance (i.e. time, distance). Location‑allocation analysis method for location optimization

Location optimization is a decision-making process regarding facility redistribution, which determines the optimal locations amongst a limited set of candidate sites to achieve the maximum service coverage. Location-allocation analysis method could be helpful to identify the best locations for facilities53. It has been widely applied to solve various location optimization problems (i.e. schools69, surveillance cameras70, bank facilities71, police patrol areas72, Wi-Fi equipment73, and medical drone launch sites74.

The location-allocation analysis is comprised of 4 steps: Creating a layer; Importing required, candidate, and competitor facilities; Adding demand points and selecting the problem type; Entering parameters and solving the problem58. Space syntax method for road accessibility evaluation

Based on graph theory and urban morphology, space syntax theory splits and slices space into line segments or points to analyze its topological and geometric relationships, exploring the relationship between human activities and space morphology77,78. In urban design, utilizing space syntax theory can evaluate the future feasibility of plans and facilitate adjustments to meet the urban development goals79. Based on the traffic network data, this study adopted the space syntax software Axwoman80 to quantitatively analyze the road accessibility in Xuchang City. This analysis mainly utilized indices of global integration and connectivity.

Results

With the different time goals of increasing equity of medical services from latest policies, the accessibility of multi-level medical service facilities was initially evaluated for each administrative region by the service area analysis method. After identifying low-accessibility areas, multi-scenario optimizations were proposed and simulated by location‑allocation analysis method and space syntax method.

Multi-level evaluation of the current accessibility of medical service facilities

Accessibility of the current hospitals with 30-min

With the 30-min time goal of increasing equity of hospitals from latest policies, service area analysis in the network analysis method was adopted to evaluate the accessibility of hospitals by the time impedances of 10-min, 20-min and 30-min. The service areas of hospitals were initially displayed for the entire Xuchang City (Fig. 4a) and subsequently refined for each district (Fig. 4b-g). In the hospital accessibility map, light pink, light green and light blue represented the regions that can reach the hospital within 10 min, 20 min and 30 min, respectively.

At a driving time of 20 min, the overall hospital service area is 2739.10 km2 and the accessibility ratio is 54.91%. Specifically, Yanling County has the lowest accessibility ratio, with 67.40% for the area and 78.28% for the population; the accessibility area ratio in Yuzhou County is only 73.72%, while the accessibility ratio of the population is 87.58%. It could be explained that the population is more concentrated in the central area in each district.

Multi-scenario for optimizating the accessiblity of multi-level medical service facilities

Based on the accessibility evaluation of the multi-level medical service facilities, the accessibility of hospitals is generally good in these central areas, while the surrounding areas of the city still lack adequate access to medical resources within the 30-min health resource allocation goal, especially the border of Xiangcheng County and the northern part of Yanling County. Compared to hospitals, the spatial distribution of health centers is more even in Xuchang City, but the current service status of existing health centers cannot meet the medical goal of 15-min. According to urban development planning, multi-scenario optimizations were further proposed by location‑allocation analysis and space syntax method. These optimizations primarily included the addition of different numbers of new multi-level medical service facilities, and the construction of new roads.

Spatial optimization scenario of multi-level medical service facilities locations

From a spatial perspective, location optimization is a decision-making process regarding facility redistribution to determine the optimal locations among a limited set of candidate sites to maximize service coverage. The preliminary candidate medical service facilities were first selected by multiple factors and then the location‑allocation analysis method was adopted to address the issues of insufficient number and unreasonable layout of multi-level medical service facilities.

The MCLP-CC optimization model in the location-allocation analysis method was implemented under the constraints of these additional and existing medical service facilities numbers to maximize the service areas. However, the selection of new medical service facility locations is affected by multiple factors. Therefore, it is crucial to consider various conditions for the preliminary candidate medical service facilities before applying the MCLP-CC optimization model. Based on the previous limited cases44,82 and construction standards for medical facilities83, selection conditions and criteria are derived and summarized as follows. The same site selection conditions for the multi-level medical service facilities included: (a) convenient traffic area: within 250 m on both sides of the main road, 150 m both sides of the secondary road or 800 m around the main road intersection; (b) unused open space for land use data; and (c) flat area: the slope gradient is less than 5°. In addition, hospitals were usually established in the main urban areas and had the service radius, thus two restricted conditions were added: (e1) the main urban areas and high surrounding population density areas; (f1) 3 km from existing hospitals (Fig. 10a). Health centers provide general healthcare and preventive services to community, thus two restricted conditions were also added: (e2) low accessibility area; (f2) 2 km from existing health centers (Fig. 10b).

The preliminary selection results of the candidate hospitals and health centers under restricted conditions (The maps in this figure were generated using the ArcGIS software (version 10.2, https://www.esri.com)).

After the preliminary selection of candidate medical service facilities, two new location-allocation layers were established separately for hospitals and health centers based on the network analysis module. In the corresponding new location-allocation layer, the existing medical service facilities and preliminarily selected candidate medical service facilities were imported as mandatory, and candidate facility points, respectively. To implement the MCLP-CC model, the service area for each medical service facility was first generated. To generate the medical service facilities set that covers the i (village residential polygon), the intersection was respectively performed on the demand i and the medical service facility j facility service area (polygon); then the demand amount (the village residential point), covered by the medical service facility service area, was calculated according to the intersection area and summarized by all medical service facilities in . The potential additional medical service facilities were finally confirmed by constraining the service areas to 15-min health centers and 30-min hospitals, respectively. Taking the economic cost and demand into consideration, various scenarios for different candidate medical service facilities were provided to improve the service coverage and accessibility. Specifically, fewer numbers of candidate facilities were focused on low accessibility areas to quickly enhance service coverage and prevent resource waste. Medium numbers were suited for areas with moderate population density and high healthcare needs to further promote the overall service coverage and accessibility. Larger numbers of candidate facilities were directed towards high-density and high-need areas to optimize service coverage, comprehensively improving the service quality and resource accessibility. Consequently, different numbers of expected adding hospitals (4, 6, 10 and 20) were calculated by the MCLP-CC model within the time constriction of 30-min. Similarly, different numbers of expected adding health centers (6, 10, 20 and 50) were obtained by the MCLP-CC model within the time constriction of 15-min.

With the adding and existing medical service facilities, the accessibility ratio of service area and population were calculated and exhibited within the 30-minute time impedance for hospitals (Fig. 11) and the 15-minute time impedance for health centers (Fig. 12). The original accessibility ratio of hospitals service area and population could nearly reach 100% within the time impedance of 30-min for the core urban areas (Weidu District and Jian’an District), 87.87% and 90.29% (Xiangcheng County), 73.72% and 87.58% (Yuzhou County), 91.89% and 93.77% (Changge County), and 67.40% and 78.28% (Yanling County). With the increasing number of new hospitals, the accessibility ratio of service area and population continued to increase from the original value of 83.07% and 91.32%. However, the growth rate of coverage slowed and became relatively stable after adding 10 hospitals for the whole city, reaching 92.97% and 96.94%. Considering the costs, 4 hospitals generated by the optimization model could be established in the first stage in the future urban development and planning (Fig. 13a).

Improvement scenario of road infrastructure and facilities locations

Transport accessibility is vital for realizing fair public resources and promoting the sustainable development of society84. Based on the traffic network data, this study adopted the space syntax software Axwoman to quantitatively analyze the road accessibility in Xuchang City, which was measured through the indexes of global integration and connectivity (Fig. 14). The line colors in the figure vary based on the integration values, with warmer colors representing higher integration and cooler colors indicating lower integration. Global Integration could be used to measure the connectivity between spaces, thus indicating that spaces with higher integration exhibit better accessibility. Specifically, the region surrounding the core urban areas (Weidu District and Jian’an District) demonstrates high topological accessibility, gradually decreasing as one moves from the center urban areas to the outskirts. Typically, the area with highest integration value is referred to as the integration core, often serving as a city’s central hub (Fig. 14a). Connectivity is closely related to traffic potential, with higher choice implying higher passing volumes and greater potential to attract traffic flows, which is reflected in the warmer colors in the figure. Xuchang City exhibited a pattern of high connectivity in the center of turban area and each district and low integration in the surrounding areas (Fig. 14b). Considering that the global integration, connectivity and low accessibility area, new roads could be planed to improve road accessibility in the future urban planning, especially for the Changge County, Yanling County and Xiangcheng County (Fig. 14c).

Discussion

Analysis of the current accessibility of multi-level medical service facilities

For the Multi-level medical service facilities, hospitals provide comprehensive health services to a region, taking in referrals from primary hospitals, and undertaking teaching and research55. Health centers offer general healthcare and preventive care services to the community, typically locating near residential areas and offering relatively low-cost services. As a result, people would initially seek outpatient care at these primary health centers and transfer to the high-level hospitals for more specialized treatment. Comprehensively considering the usage rates of these facilities and the time goals from the latest policies, 30-min high-quality hospitals and 15-min primary health centers are considered optimal for underdeveloped cities, especially in an aging society. Taking the Xuchang City as a case study, the current spatial accessibility of multi-level medical service facilities was deeply interpreted under different time goals from the latest policies.

The distribution of existing hospitals is uneven in Xuchang City, exhibiting a skewed pattern. The accessibility of hospitals is generally good in central areas for each district within the time impedance of 30-min. With the increasing distance or time, the hospital’s service coverage initially starts from the central areas and gradually expands towards the surrounding areas, which was similar with other city’s spatial distribution pattern (i.e. Deqing County in Zhejiang Province)42. In addition, Weidu district is able to reach the hospitals within 10 min. Conversely, the northwestern and southern parts of Xuchang City have the longest service distances and times, resulting in the poorest hospital accessibility in those areas. This spatial inequity is dominantly caused by the imbalanced social development strategy between the city and rural areas85. First, the hospitals are concentrated in the central parts of different administrative divisions in Xuchang City. There are 74 hospitals for Xuchang City, with Weidu District accounting for 48.65% of the medical resources. Secondly, large-scale, technologically advanced, and well-equipped hospitals (3 A hospitals) are primarily concentrated in the core urban areas, for example, Xuchang Central Hospital (Luminghu Hospital), Xuchang Maternal and Child Health Hospital, and Xuchang Municipal Hospital. Both the number of doctors with professional skills and the quantity of costly equipment and instruments in health centers and county hospitals lag far behind those in high-level (3 A) hospitals, letting alone city hospitals. This disparity exacerbates the spatial inequity, as rural patients must travel further to receive treatment for critical illnesses that cannot be addressed in health centers or county hospitals. The hierarchical pattern is mainly affected by the economic factors (GDP per capita, road networks) and naturalfactors (topographic)27,47,86. Overall, the spatial distribution of current hospitals is uneven, leading to inadequate and wasted medical resources.

As a whole, the spatial distribution of health centers is more even than hospitals, which is more convenient to provide general healthcare and preventive care services, especially for the high-demanding elderly people in underdeveloped cities in the aging society. For the health centers, the accessibility ratio of service area and population could reach nearly 100% within the time impedance of 15-min for the core urban areas (Weidu District and Jian’an District). In addition, Changge County and Yuzhou County exhibit relatively higher service coverage areas (better accessibility), while Yanling County has the lowest accessibility (only 67.40% for area and 78.28% for population). The accessibility of Xiangcheng County is slightly higher than Yanling County for 84.90% area and 87.41% for population. Among the prefectural-level cities and counties in Xuchang City, Yuzhou County and Changge County have relatively better economic conditions, resulting in more evenly distributed health centers resources and wider coverage of service areas. There are spatial inequities in access to healthcare services in the study area, which could be illustrated by the close relationship between medical services and urban economic development86. Evidently, the current service status of the existing health centers in Xuchang cannot meet the goal of 15-min.

This imbalance has been prevailing at underdeveloped city level across China. City capitals have accumulated the best medical resources, making it less accessible to large groups of elderly people in rural areas42. In addition, elderly people’s frequency of visiting medical facilities is much higher than that of young people, leading to an increased demand for better access. As a result, it is very imperative to equitable and high-quality medical service under the time goals from latest policies in the future local government planning.

Implications for improving medical equity in underdeveloped city

As the Healthy China 2030 becomes the key national strategy for improving population health87–90, access to medical service facilities is a pivotal determinant of health disparities, which is also globally acknowledged as the foremost objective in addressing individuals’ fundamental health requirements. Despite the challenges associated with achieving equitable access15,16, this paper could provide insights for improving the medical equity for residents and promoting the healthy and sustainable development of underdeveloped city.

In practical application, improving medical equity is rather complicated, which requires a comprehensive consideration of social and economic aspects44. For metropolitan cities, the governments have relatively strong economic foundation to achieve the abundant medical resources and high-quality medical services. There are various previous studies to promote equitable access by adding more medical service facilities for these cities6,47, which is also consistent with the healthcare development plans. For example, according to Shenzhen’s healthcare development plan, the city plans to increase the number of community healthcare centers by up to 1,000 by 202591. However, the large investment with respect to healthcare services poses a huge challenge for underdeveloped cities, especially in the post-pandemic era30. Thus, both equity and efficiency should be carefully considered and multi-scenarios were proposed to improve the accessibility of high-quality hospitals within 30-min and basic healthcare institutions within 15-min by local common transportation.

The first scenario was only adding new medical service facilities in low-access areas without updating the road accessibility. For this scenario, the accessibility of hospitals and health centers was simulated by different potential hospitals (4, 6, 10 and 20) and health centers (6, 10, 20 and 50) for the entire city. With the increasing number of new hospitals and health centers, the accessibility ratio of service areas and populations continued to increase from the original value; however, the growth rate of coverage decreased gradually and the accessibility ratio became relatively stable after adding 4 hospitals or 20 health centers for the whole city. The second was the combination: not only reallocating of medical service facilities but also updating the road accessibility by adding new roads. In the second scenario, the accessibility in response to 4 new hospitals, 10 health centers and new roads was provided and intercompared with that of 20 new hospitals and 50 new health centers, respectively. Based on multi-scenario optimization results, the improved effect of few appropriate road construction and 4 hospitals (or 10 new health centers) was more obvious than that of 20 new hospitals (or 50 new health centers), respectively. In addition, the medical service facilities are usually established in the central area and high surrounding population density area, requiring substantial workforce, material resources, and funding. Therefore, limited number of medical institutions combined with road construction may be a more cost-effective way to improve medical service equity in underdeveloped cities, particularly rural areas. Within the study area, 4 hospitals, 10 health centers and few new roads are necessary to meet the high-quality and equitable accessibility of hospitals within 30-min and basic healthcare institutions within 15-min by local common transportation. However, the location selection and priority sequence of construction are not subjective or arbitrary, considering the economic cost and accessibility43. For the future urban development and planning, if there is a need for establishing new medical service facility, the location of new medical service facilities for these low-access regions should be prioritized in the future development to facilitate the equity of medical service facilities coverage7,42,92. Specifically, in the first stage, 2 hospitals, 4 health centers and few new roads in Yuzhou County and Yanling County could be first considered to address the low accessibility and medical needs; in the second stage, 2 hospitals, 6 health centers and additional roads in Xiangcheng County and Changge County could also be considered to increase the accessibility for rural and urban communities. For years, the government of Xuchang City has made strides to promote medical service development in selected regions and the Municipal Highway Development Center in Xuchang City is making every effort to build a national and provincial trunk road network, which is wide coverage, strong connection and high efficiency (http://www.xuchang.gov.cn). The proposed optimization scheme is consistent with government planning requirements and could provide scientific evidence for the transportation planning.

For underdeveloped cities, the financial expenditure of the government is often minimal especially in the post-pandemic era. More medical service facilities could obviously improve the spatial accessibility of medical service facilities; however, it is not practical to solve the problem of low efficiency and the disparity between urban and rural areas for underdeveloped cities by building a large number of new medical service facilities. Different from urban areas in metropolitan cities, the location optimization of medical service facilities must consider the accessibility of roads in underdeveloped cities. Therefore, comprehensively considering for the best accessibility and the most reasonable cost, proper road transportation and medical institution could be the best cost-effective solution to improve the equity of medical treatment services for underdeveloped cities in the future planning and construction. In addition, the medical institution could be established for different stage to preferably provide convenience for residents in underdeveloped cities, particularly for high-demanding elderly people. Nevertheless, the constructed multi-level evaluation and multi-scenario optimization framework, along with the case study in Xuchang City, lays the foundation for achieving medical equity and promoting sustainable development in underdeveloped cities. This framework could also be adapted for other public service facilities with modifications.

Future studies

The research exhibited geographical accessibility laws and multi-scenario optimizations of multi-level medical service facilities under driving mode in one classical underdeveloped city in Henan province, China. With the rapid increase in urban vehicle ownership, self-driving has become one of the irreplaceable travel modes for urban residents to seek medical treatment. The personal preference for medical resources and traffic congestion in different traveling periods34 are not considered44,93,94, which may bias the assessment of spatial accessibility. The spatial accessibility with different transportation modes at different periods need to be explored in future studies, based on more accurate real-time dataset. There is an assumption that service utilization behavior may be constrained within the whole city administrative boundaries, which the medical insurance is tied to39. The proposed scenarios could provide possible solution and valuable information to support health promotion and urban planning practices, but improving medical equity is a rather complex issue in practical applications44. More diverse solutions could be further developed to maximize equal accessibility between the urban and rural areas in the future43,95, according to the practical economic support and urban development plans from local governments.

Conclusions

Equal and high-quality accessibility of medical services is a crucial issue and challenge for urban planners and managers under the rural revitalization and the healthy China 2030 strategy, which could directly impact the healthy life of residents and the society sustainable development. This paper proposed a multi-level evaluation and multi-scenario optimization framework for accessing and improving the spatial accessibility of Medical Service Facilities in underdeveloped city, taking the Xuchang City as a case study. The key findings are as follows:

For the multi-level medical service facilities, the current spatial distribution is uneven, leading to a coexistence of inadequate and wasted medical resources. The central area for each district had the best accessibility within the 30-min due to the concentrated hospitals, while the northwestern and northern-southern parts of the city experienced the longest service distances and times. For the health centers, the accessibility could reach nearly 100% within the time impedance of 15-min for the core urban areas (Weidu District and Jian’an District), while other rural districts exhibited poor accessibility. The inequity in access to primary health center services within 15-min and high-quality hospitals services within 30-min for underdeveloped city’s residents is an important issue to be addressed in the future medical development plans.

Within the different time goals for multi-level medical service facilities from the latest policies, each optimization scenario could improve the spatial accessibility and service quality. For underdeveloped cities in the post-pandemic era, the integrated optimization for both road infrastructure and institution location could be more cost-effective to improve medical service equity and quality. Furthermore, the optimal locations for multi-level medical service facilities and roads should be constructed in a sequential process, with priority given to low-access regions in future city planning, considering the trade-off between investment cost and accessibility.

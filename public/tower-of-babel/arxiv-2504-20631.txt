Abstract
. For g ∈ Hol ⁡ ( 𝔻 ) g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D}) , we study the class of generalized integration operators T g , a T_{g,a} , acting on Hardy and Bergman spaces of the unit disc in the complex plane. This class of integral operators were introduced to study factorization theorems in the Hardy spaces of the unit disc. We completely characterize the space of symbols g g , for which T g , a T_{g,a} belongs to the Schatten-von Neumann ideals of the Hardy and Bergman spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disc in the complex plane. By
\Hol(\mathbb{D})
, we denote the space of all analytic functions defined in
\mathbb{D}
. Let

(1)

Vf(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta\qquad f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})

be the classical Volterra operator, acting on
\Hol(\mathbb{D})
. For a fixed symbol
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
, we can define the operator

(2)

T_{g}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\,,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}\,,f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D}).

The study of integral operators such as
T_{g}
, originated from the fact that, as
g
varies in
\Hol(\mathbb{D})
,
T_{g}
represents some significant classical operators.
For instance, if
g(z)=z
,
T_{g}
is the Volterra operator
V
, while, when
g(z)=-\log(1-z)
, it coincides with the (shifted) Cesáro summation operator
[
22
]
. It is the above fact, which justify that
T_{g}
operators are also known as generalized Volterra operators.

Originally, the class of generalized Volterra operators was introduced and studied in the context of the Hardy and Bergman spaces of the unit disc. The Hardy space
H^{p}
is the space of all
f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
such that

\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{p}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}<\infty\,,

while for
\alpha>-1
, the standard weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
is the space of all
f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}dA_{\alpha}(z)<\infty\,,

where
dA(z)
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}
and
dA_{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)
.

In particular, Ch. Pommerenke
[
19
]
, characterized the space of symbols
g
for which
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{2}
. The complete characterization of symbols for which
T_{g}
acts boundedly between different Hardy spaces was given in a series of papers
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
. Subsequently, the study of such operators in various spaces of analytic functions has gained since considerable attention (see for example
[
24
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
).

In the initial study of generalized Volterra operators, A. Aleman and A. Siskakis
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
established sufficient and necessary conditions on the symbol
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
for
T_{g}
to belong to the Schatten-von Neumann ideals in the case of the Hilbert space
H^{2}
and
A^{2}_{\alpha}
, when
\alpha>-1
. For the sake of notation, we write heuristically
H^{2}
by
A^{2}_{-1}
.

In particular, for
\alpha\geq-1
,
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
and
1<p<\infty
, we have that
T_{g}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
if and only if
g
belongs to the Besov space
\mathcal{B}_{p}
, while if
0<p\leq 1
and
T_{g}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
, then
T_{g}
is the zero operator.

For
0<p<\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
mp>1
, the Besov spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p}
contain all
f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(m)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{mp}\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}<\infty\,.

The Besov spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p}
do not depend on
m
, as shown in
[
26
, Lemma 5.16 and Theorem 4.28]
and the above quantity defines a seminorm on
\mathcal{B}_{p}
.

In this article, we study a further generalization of the integral operator
T_{g}
.
An inspection of (
2
) shows that
T_{g}
is a primitive of the first term of the derivative of the product
fg
.
Applying the Leibniz rule of differentiation we get

(3)

(T_{g}f)^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}f^{(k)}g^{(n-k)}.

Now, if we consider an arbitrary
n
-tuple
a=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
a\neq\mathbf{0}
and a symbol
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
, we can define the following operator

T_{g,a}f=V^{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}f^{(k)}g^{(n-k)}\right)\quad f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})\,,

where
V^{n}
is the
n
-th iterate of the Volterra operator (
1
).
It is then clear by (
3
) that the generalized Volterra operator is
a particular instance of the operator
T_{g,a}.

The integral operator
T_{g,a}
was introduced by N. Chalmoukis, and studied in the context of Hardy spaces of the unit disc. In his work
[
7
]
, he characterized the space of symbols

g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
for which
T_{g,a}\colon H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded, for
0<p\leq q<\infty
. The case of
0<q<p<\infty
was later resolved by the author and Chalmoukis in
[
8
]
. Around the same time, H. Arroussi et al.
[
6
]
characterized Sobolev-Carleson measures for Bergman spaces and, as a consequence, characterized the space of symbols
g
for which
T_{g,a}
is bounded and compact between Bergman spaces. Since then, significant research has been devoted to identifying the space of symbols
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
, for which
T_{g,a}
is bounded or compact on other spaces of analytic functions. Readers interested in further developments may refer to
[
23
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
25
]
.

The motivation for studying the operator
T_{g,a}
, extends beyond its generalization of the classical
T_{g}
. It also arrises from its connection to factorization theorem of holomorphic functions by W.Cohn
[
9
]
and a theorem of J. Rättyä
[
21
]
about higher order linear differential equations with holomorphic coefficients. For further details, we refer the interested reader to
[
7
, Theorems 1.5 and 1.6]
. Notably, the latter result has been extended to other spaces of analytic functions as well (see
[
23
]
,
[
5
]
,
[

).

Our study focuses on providing sufficient and necessary conditions on the symbol
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
for
T_{g,a}
to belong to the Schatten-von Neumann ideal on Hardy and Bergman spaces. To achieve this, we first examine the case where
a
is a standard unit vector of
\mathbb{C}^{n},
a situation previously addressed from J. Du, S. Li and D. Qu
[
10
]
. In this setting, the operators
T_{g,a}
have the following form

T_{g}^{n,k}(f)(z)=V^{n}(f^{(k)}g^{(n-k)})(z)\qquad 0\leq k<n.

Their result is the following.

Theorem A
.

L
et
\alpha\geq-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq n-1
,
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
and
0<p<\infty
.

(i)

If
\frac{1}{n-k}<p<\infty
, then
T_{g}^{n,k}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
.

(ii)

If
0<p\leq\frac{1}{n-k}
and
T_{g}^{n,k}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
, then
T_{g}^{n,k}
is the zero operator.

We present now the main result of this article.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a=(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
,
\lambda=\max\{k\colon a_{k}\neq 0\}
,
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
, and
0<p<\infty
.

(i)

If
\frac{1}{n-\lambda}<p<\infty
, then
T_{g,a}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
.

(ii)

If
0<p\leq\frac{1}{n-\lambda}
and
T_{g,a}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
, then
T_{g,a}
is the zero operator.

When
T_{g,a}=T_{g}
and then
\lambda=n-1
, we see that Theorem
1.1
reduces to the already known result of A. Aleman and A. Siskakis. We highlight also that our work focuses on proving that
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
is necessary for
T_{g,a}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
. Indeed, since Schatten-von Neumann ideals are vector spaces, Theorem
A
implies that if
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
, then
T_{g,a}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
. Actually, another interpretation of our result is that if
T_{g,a}\in S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
, then every term comprising the operator is forced to be in
S^{p}(A^{2}_{\alpha})
as well. In other words, there is no cancellation between the terms of
T_{g,a}
.

The result concerning the Schatten-von Neumann ideal membership for the operator
T_{g}
relies primarily on the result of Luecking
[
16
]
, which provides sufficient and necessary conditions for which Toeplitz operators belong to the Schatten-von Neumann ideals. Due to this connection, we follow his approach while also incorporating known techniques to handle possible cancellations between the terms of
T_{g,a}
, which ultimately leads to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The article is constructed as follows. In the following section, we provide the necessary background material we require for the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{D}
and Schatten-von Neumman ideals, while also introducing properly the spaces of analytic functions which are involved. In Section 3, we fix
\alpha\geq-1
and we prove Theorem
1.1
for
2\leq p<\infty
. The final section addresses the case of
0<p<2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperbolic geometry

We recall some elementary facts from the hyperbolic geometry of the unit disc. We use the notation

\rho(z,w)=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|,\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{D}

for the
pseudohyperbolic
metric of
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover,

\beta(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,w)}{1-\rho(z,w)},\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{D}

is the
hyperbolic
metric of
\mathbb{D}
.
The set
\displaystyle{D(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{D}\colon\beta(z,w)<r\}}
is the hyperbolic disc, centered at
z
with radius
r>0
. It is known that the modulus of analytic function satisfy sub-mean inequalities for hyperbolic discs. In fact, for every
0<p<\infty
and
r>0
, there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
r
, such that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
,

(4)

|f(z)|^{p}\leq\frac{C}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}\,dA(w).

This inequality is proved in
[
12
, Lemma 13, p. 66]
. It is also well known, see
[
26
, Proposition 4.5]
, that given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
r>0
, there exists constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
, depending on
r
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{1}}|1-\overline{w}a|

\displaystyle\leq|1-\overline{w}z|\leq C_{1}|1-\overline{w}a|\,,\qquad w\in\mathbb{D},a\in D(z,r),

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{2}}(1-|w|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 1-|z|^{2}\leq C_{2}(1-|w|^{2})\,,\qquad w\in D(z,r),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{3}}|D(z,r)|

\displaystyle\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq C_{3}|D(z,r)|.

where
|D(z,r)|
denotes the Lebesgue volume of the set
D(z,r)
. We refer to the estimates of the form (
5
) as hyperbolic estimates and shall be used repeatedly in the proofs of Theorem
1.1
.

A sequence
\{z_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{D}
is called
r
-
hyperbolically separated
if there exists a constant
r>0
such that
\beta(z_{k},z_{\lambda})\geq r
for
k\neq\lambda
, while is said to be an
r
-
lattice
in the hyperbolic distance, if it is
r/2
- separated and

\mathbb{D}=\bigcup_{k}D(z_{k},r).

Hyperbolically separated sequences have the following useful “finite overlapping” property.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r>0
and
Z=\{z_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be a
r
-hyperbolically separated sequence. There exists a positive constant
N
, depending only on
r
, such that for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
there exist at most
N
hyperbolic discs
D(z_{\lambda},r)
such that
z\in D(z_{\lambda},r).

A proof of the above result can be found in
[
26
, Lemma 4.7]
.

2.2.
Hilbert Spaces of analytic functions

In this article, the main Hilbert spaces under consideration are the standard weighted Bergman spaces and the Hardy space. For convenience, we adopt the heuristic notation
A^{2}_{-1}
to denote the Hardy space
H^{2}
. For fixed
\alpha\geq-1
, the inner product on these spaces is given by

\displaystyle\langle f,h\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!\Gamma(\alpha+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+n+2)}a_{n}\overline{b_{n}}\,.

where
\Gamma
is the classical gamma function and
a_{n},b_{n}
are the Taylor coefficients of
f,h
respectively. A consequence of Parseval’s identity for analytic functions, allow us to compute the inner product using an integral involving the derivatives of
f,h
. This technique is the well known
Littlewood-Paley formula
. In fact, given
n>0
and
f\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
such that
f(0)=\dots=f^{(n-1)}(0)=0
, there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
n
, such that

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n+\alpha}dA(z)\leq C\|f\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}}^{2}\,.

For
n=1
and
\alpha=-1
, we refer to the proof of the above result in
[
13
, Lemma 3.2, Ch. VI §3]
. For general
\alpha>-1
, we refer to
[
26
, Theorem 4.28]
. For an arbitrary
n>1
, one works by induction on (
6
) to obtain the result.

As our operators are induced by analytic functions, we require suitable test functions to transfer the information of Schatten
p
-norm into conditions involving only the function
g\in\Hol(\mathbb{D})
. We introduce these functions below. Let
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and
\gamma>1+\frac{\alpha+2}{2}
be sufficiently large. We consider the functions

K_{j,w}(z)=\frac{z^{j}}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{\gamma+j}}\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}\,.

A key estimate, used repeatedly in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, states that for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we can find a constant
C
, depending only on
n,\gamma
such that

(7)

|K_{j,w}^{(n)}(z)|\leq\frac{C}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{\gamma+j+n}}\,.

This class of functions was introduced by Arroussi et al.
[
6
]
to solve a system of linear equations related to the boundedness of the operator
T_{g,a}
in Bergman spaces, a technique that we also employ in this article.

To proceed, we state a consequence of the atomic decomposition of functions
A^{2}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>-1
, written in the language of our test functions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\geq-1
,

be sufficiently large,

and

be a

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\frac{(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{\gamma-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}}{(1-|z_{n}|^{j}\overline{z_{n}}z)^{\gamma}}\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle B_{j}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})^{{\gamma+j}-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}K_{j,z_{n}}(z)\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}\,,

belong to

and there exists a positive constant

, depending only on

, such that

This result follows from a slight modification of a special case of
[

. In the modern literature, the aforementioned result is also proved in

2.3.
Schatten-von Neumann ideals

We formally introduce the spaces of operators considered in this article. Throughout, we fix a separable Hilbert space, equipped with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and denote its induced norm by
\|\cdot\|
. We use the same notation for the operator norm of a bounded operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
.

For a compact operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
, there exists a decreasing sequence of positive numbers
\{\lambda_{n}(T)\}_{n}
and orthonormal sets
\{e_{n}\}_{n},\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that

T(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(T)\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\sigma_{n}\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}\,.

The sequence
\{\lambda_{n}(T)\}_{n}
consists of the eigenvalues of
|T|=(T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, repeated according to their multiplicity, and
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
are the corresponding normalized eigenvectors. The sequence of numbers
\{\lambda_{n}(T)\}_{n}
is called the
singular value sequence of

T
. An equivalent characterization of the singular values is given by the
Rayleigh’s equation
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lambda_{n}(T)
be the
n
-th singular value of the positive operator
T
. Then,

(8)

\lambda_{n+1}=\min_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}\max\{\langle Tx,x\rangle\colon\|x\|=1,x\perp x_{i},1\leq i\leq n\}\,.

A proof of this result can be found in
[
11
, Ch. X.4.3]
.

For
0<p<\infty
, the Schatten-von Neumann ideal of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
, is the space of all compact operators
T
acting on
\mathcal{H}
, such that the singular value sequence of
T
belongs to
\ell^{p}
. Let

\|T\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(T)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,
(S^{p}(\mathcal{H}),\|\cdot\|_{p})
becomes a Banach space, while for
0<p<1
,
\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
defines a metric and
S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
is a complete topological space. Standard textbooks covering the topic of Schatten ideals are
[
14
]
,
[
20
]
.

For
0<p<\infty
, we use the fact that
S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
is a two-sided ideal in the ring of bounded linear operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
. In particular, if
T\in S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
and
A,B
are bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
, then
ATB\in S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
with

(9)

\|ATB\|_{p}\leq\|A\|\|T\|_{p}\|B\|\,.

A proof of the above result can be found in
[
11
, Ch. XI Lemma 9]
. Moreover, the Spectral Mapping Theorem imply that the condition
T\in S^{p}(\mathcal{H})
is equivalent to
T^{*}T\in S^{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathcal{H})
and

(10)

\|T\|_{p}^{p}=\|T^{*}T\|_{p/2}^{p/2}\,.

In the following sections, we estimate the Schatten norm of the
T_{g,a}
operator with a quantity containing its norm on orthonormal bases. We present the result below.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T
be a compact operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
. The following conditions hold,

(i)

If
2\leq p<\infty
, then

\|T\|_{p}^{p}=\max\biggl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|T(e_{n})\|^{p}\colon\{e_{n}\}_{n}\text{ orthonormal base}\biggr\}.

(ii)

If
0<p\leq 2
, then

\|T\|_{p}^{p}=\min\biggl\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|T(e_{n})\|^{p}\colon\{e_{n}\}_{n}\text{ orthonormal base}\biggr\}.

Both max and min are attained when
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
is chosen to be the sequence of the eigenvectors corresponding to the singular value sequence
\{\lambda_{n}(T^{*}T)\}_{n}
.

One can prove this result by applying the results of
[
26
, Theorem 1.27]
and
[
26
, Proposition 1.31, Corollary 1.32]
to
T^{*}T
. This leads to two significant consequences. First, it makes necessary the distinction between two separate proofs for Theorem
1.1
, one for the case
p\geq 2
and one for
0<p<2
. Second, it establishes that the membership of an operator in a Schatten-von Neumann ideal can be determined using an equivalent norm on the Hilbert space, rather than the norm originally equipped on it. This observation plays a fundamental role in allowing the computation of these norms equivalently via the Littlewood-Paley formula (
6
).

Finally, for
0<p<2
, we shall require an estimate from above of the Schatten norm, proved in
[
26
, Proposition 1.29]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
T
be a compact operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
and
0<p\leq 2
. Then, for any orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{H}
, we have

\|T\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle T(e_{n}),e_{k}\rangle|^{p}\,.

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\neq\nu}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{D(\zeta_{m},r)}\frac{[(1-|\zeta_{\kappa}|^{2})(1-|\zeta_{\nu}|^{2})]^{\gamma+J-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}}{|1-\overline{\zeta_{\kappa}}z|^{2\gamma+2J+2k}}|a_{k}g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}dA_{2n+\alpha}(z)\right)^{p/2}\leq

\displaystyle\leq C|A_{R}|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{D(\zeta_{m},r)}|a_{k}g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}dA_{2(n-k)-2}(z)\right)^{p/2}

Consequently, we can find a constant
C_{2}
, depending on
n,r,\gamma,\alpha,p
, such that

(21)

I_{2}\leq C_{2}|A_{R}|\sum_{k=0}^{\lambda}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{D(\zeta_{m},r)}|a_{k}g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}dA_{2(n-k)-2}(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,.

So, we combine (
21
) with (

) to conclude that there is a constant
C_{2}
, depending on
n,r,\gamma,\alpha,p
, such that

(22)

\|E_{J}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}\leq C_{2}|A_{R}|\sum_{k=0}^{\lambda}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{D(\zeta_{m},r)}|a_{k}g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}dA_{2(n-k)-2}(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,.

Taking into account all aforementioned estimates, we finally find a constant

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{D(\zeta_{m},r)}|a_{\lambda}g^{(n-\lambda)}(z)|^{2}dA_{2(n-\lambda)-2}(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C\|T_{g,a}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}\,.

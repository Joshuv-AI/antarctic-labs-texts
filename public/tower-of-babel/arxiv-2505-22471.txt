Phase transitions for contact processes on sparse random graphs via metastability and local limits
Benedikt Jahnel, Lukas Lüchtrath, Christian Mönch

Abstract
We propose a new perspective on the asymptotic regimes of fast and slow extinction in the contact process on locally converging sequences of sparse finite graphs. We characterise the phase boundary by the existence of a metastable density, which makes the study of the phase transition particularly amenable to local-convergence techniques. We use this approach to derive general conditions for the coincidence of the critical threshold with the survival/extinction threshold in the local limit. We further argue that the correct time scale to separate fast extinction from slow extinction in sparse graphs is, in general, the exponential scale, by showing that fast extinction may occur on stretched exponential time scales in sparse scale-free spatial networks. Together with recent results by Nam, Nguyen and Sly (Trans. Am. Math. Soc. 375, 2022), our methods can be applied to deduce that the fast/slow threshold in sparse configuration models coincides with the survival/extinction threshold on the limiting Galton-Watson tree. AMS-MSC 2020 : 60K35 (primary); 05C82, 91D30 (secondary) Key Words : Local weak convergence, random network, SIS model, sparse random graph, locality of phase transition

Introduction and main results

The contact process.

Let
G=(V,E)
denote a locally finite connected graph with a dedicated root
o\in V
and we write
(G,o)
for the rooted graph. The
contact process
with infection rate
\lambda>0
on
G
is the family of set-valued continuous-time Markov processes
\big\{\xi^{A}=(\xi^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}\colon A\subset V\text{ finite }\big\}
. The law of
\xi^{A}
is determined by setting
\xi^{A}_{0}=A
and the transition dynamics, for every
v\in V
,

\displaystyle\xi^{A}_{t}\to\xi^{A}_{t}\setminus\{v\}\text{ at rate }1,

(1)

\displaystyle\xi^{A}_{t}\to\xi^{A}_{t}\cup\{v\}\text{ at rate }\lambda\sum_{w\colon v\sim w}\mathbbm{1}\big\{w\in\xi^{A}_{t}\big\}.

Here and throughout we write
v\sim w
for
\{v,w\}\in E
. For ease of notation, we set
\xi^{v}_{t}=\xi^{\{v\}}_{t}
for
v\in V
and analogously drop the set notation for singletons in similar instances. We write
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}
for the law of the contact process based on the graph
G
with infection rate
\lambda
. Three fundamental and well-known properties of the contact process are

monotonicity:

\xi^{A}_{t}
is stochastically increasing in
\lambda
and
G
,

attractivity:

if
A\subset B
, then
\xi^{B}
stochastically dominates
\xi^{A}
, and

(self-)duality:

\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}(\xi_{t}^{A}\cap B=\emptyset)=\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}(\xi_{t}^{B}\cap A=\emptyset)
for
A,B\subset V
finite.

Duality immediately guarantees that
\xi^{A}
is well-defined for any
A\subset V.

The
extinction time

\tau_{\emptyset}(A)
of
\xi^{A}
denotes the time at which
(\xi^{A}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
first gets absorbed into the state
\emptyset
. In this article, one central quantity of interest for the contact process on infinite graphs is the
survival/extinction threshold
of
(G,o)
, given by

\lambda_{\textsf{1}}(G)=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\colon\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)=\infty)=0\big\},

which is guaranteed to be well-defined by monotonicity. Note that the definition of the critical rate is independent of the choice of the root, since we assume
G
to be connected. For finite graphs, the critical rate is
0
and contains no information about the dynamics of
\xi
, and we discuss more appropriate thresholds for this setting below. Invoking monotonicity again, one sees that, for any infinite connected graph
G
, the value
\lambda_{\textsf{1}}(G)
is at most the extinction/survival threshold for the contact process on
\mathbb{N}
with nearest neighbour edges. The latter is well-known to be finite
[
17
]
and thus
\lambda_{\textsf{1}}(G)<\infty
. The survival/extinction threshold is also known as the
lower critical value
for the contact process on
G
, the
upper critical value
being the threshold at which the infection returns infinitely often to the root.

The contact process and related interacting particle systems were first systematically studied in this form in the 1970s, see e.g.
[
17
,
14
]
. Classical choices for
G
are homogeneous lattices (usually the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) and their finite subgraphs
[
15
,
16
,
10
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
. Using the fact that the contact process is a form of oriented percolation, Bezuidenhout and Grimmett famously proved that the critical contact process on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
dies out
[
5
]
and established exponential decay of the volume of the infected set in
[
6
]
. A comprehensive overview of the classical theory is provided in the monographs
[
25
,
24
]
.

The fast/slow threshold of the contact process on random graphs.

With the advent of network science in the early 2000’s, interest in infection models on finite sparse
random
graphs arose, as they serve as models for the spread of diseases or information in inhomogeneous populations. Since the contact process dies out on any finite graph, the question of extinction vs. survival becomes a question of fast extinction vs. slow extinction. Here, slow extinction means that the infection survives for a time that scales exponentially in the size of the graph. Let
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of finite graphs with
G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n})
. Assume that
|V_{n}|\to\infty
, then the
fast/slow threshold
for
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\lambda_{+}\big((G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{G_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{\emptyset}(V_{n})>{\rm e}^{c|V_{n}|}\big)=0\text{ for all }c>0\big\}.

The main purpose of this article is to investigate the question when
\lambda_{+}\big((G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)

\lambda_{\textsf{1}}(G)
coincide, provided that the finite graphs
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(G,o)
in a suitable sense. To formalise this, we use the framework of
[

Remark 1.1
.

In general, if a sequence
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of (not necessarily connected) random graphs converges locally in probability to a limit graph
(\mathscr{G},o)
and
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

, see

Examples where the limiting distribution is concentrated on trees include sparse Erdös–Renyi graphs, inhomogeneous random graphs, random regular graphs, preferential attachment graphs, and the configuration model. In the context of spatial random graphs, such as e.g. lattice bond and site percolation models, spatial scale-free networks, continuum percolation models and spatial random intersection graphs, the limits are usually not trees.

The classical example for coincidence of the critical values
\lambda_{+}
and

is the case in which

are boxes in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

. The situation is quite different if

Let

\lambda_{-}\big((\mathscr{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\lambda_{+}\big((\mathscr{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{T}),

where for each
n

\mathscr{C}_{n}
is the maximal component of
\mathscr{G}_{n}
, and
\mathscr{T}
is the local limit of
(\mathscr{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\mu

For the moment, we remark that

if and only if

7
]
and that the partial result
\lambda_{+}\big((\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)\leq\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{T})
was already established in
[

]

Overview of main results.

Let
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of finite connected random graphs converging locally in probability to
(\mathscr{G},o)
. Let
(s(m))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be some diverging sequence. We say that the contact process on
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
metastable at time scale
s(m)
if, for all sequences
(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(t(n)\leq s(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))=1
, there exists some
\eta>0
, which may depend on
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|>\eta\Big)>0.

Our goal is to quantify the infection density
|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}|/|\mathscr{V}_{n}|
, and thereby the occurrence of a metastable phase, through the
survival probability
of the contact process on the limit graph
(\mathscr{G},o)
,

\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q})=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)=\infty)\big]=\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)=\infty).

Observe that
\eta_{\lambda}
depends only on the
distribution
of the limit graph, while metastability as well as
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
are, in principle, dependent on the realisation of
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. However, if
(\mathscr{G},o)
is the infinite cluster of some percolation process, then its distribution
\mathsf{Q}
is usually extremal, such as in the setting of Conjecture
1.2
, and then the limiting objects do not depend on the realisation of the graph sequence, cf Remark
1.1
.

Our first main result is that the metastable density of the infection cannot exceed the survival probability in the limit.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of connected locally finite graphs that converges locally in probability to some rooted locally finite random graph
(\mathscr{G},o)
with extremal distribution
\mathsf{Q}
. Then, for any diverging sequence
(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of times and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|\geq\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q})+\varepsilon\Big)=0.

In particular, if
\lambda<\lambda_{1}(\mathscr{G})\equiv\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{Q})
, then

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}0.

Note that the density of the infection is a global quantity, therefore the extinction probability in the limit has to coincide with the ‘annealed’ survival probability
\eta_{\lambda}
.

The absence of metastability for some time scale does not imply that the contact process dies out on that time scale, as is illustrated by our next result. To formulate it, we define a sequence of random graphs
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be
sparse
if the family
\big\{\mathsf{deg}_{G_{n}}(o_{n})\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
is uniformly integrable under
\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}
. Sparsity is a natural assumption in the context of locality of the fast/slow transition in the contact process as it guarantees that
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight in
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{\ast}
, see
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a sequence of sparse graphs
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges locally in probability to some
(\mathscr{G},o)
with extremal distribution
\mathsf{Q}
satisfying
\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{G})>0
and such that, for all
\lambda>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{V}_{n})>\exp\big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|/\log^{1+\varepsilon}(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|)\big)\Big)=1.

(3)

In particular, for these graph sequences we have that
0=\lambda_{-}\big((\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)<\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{G})
.

This result suggests that, if
((\mathscr{G}_{n},o_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is sparse, then the correct time scale to distinguish the fast extinction regime from the slow extinction regime is in general the exponential one, i.e.,
s(m)=\textup{e}^{cm},m\in\mathbb{N}
, for some
c>0.
It is not difficult to see, cf. Lemma
2.5
below, that in this case, absence of metastability on the exponential time scale also implies extinction. On the other hand, it is straightforward that, in sparse graphs, there cannot be survival at super-exponential scales. We use the connection between metastability and survival on the exponential time scale to prove a general inequality for the critical values.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of connected sparse graphs converging locally in probability to a graph
(\mathscr{G},o)
with extremal distrbution
\mathsf{Q}
. Then,

\lambda_{+}\big((\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)\geq\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{G})\equiv\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathsf{Q}).

In particular, our theorem implies the second equality in Conjecture
1.2
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\mu
denote a probability distribution on the non-negative integers and let
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote configuration graphs on
n
vertices derived from
\mu
. Then,

\lambda_{+}\big((\mathscr{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{T}),

where, for each
n
,
\mathscr{C}_{n}
denotes the largest component in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
and
\mathscr{T}
is the local limit of
(\mathscr{C}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

It is well known that the configuration model converges locally in probability to the unimodular Bienaymé–Galton–Watson tree
\mathscr{T}
and that the corresponding giant components converge to the limit tree conditioned on non-extinction, see
[
18
]
. Hence, Theorem
1.5
applies. The converse inequality is provided in
[
28
, Theorem 5]
.
∎

Our final result concerns lower bounds in probability for the metastable density.
For this, consider the condition

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\xi^{o_{n}}_{t(n)}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(o_{n})<t(n)\Big)=0,

(4)

where
\tau^{(n)}_{R}(o_{n})
denotes the first times that a vertex at distance
R
from the root is infected in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
. The next statement asserts that (
4
) is necessary and sufficient for lower bounding the metastable density via the limit’s survival probability.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of connected random graphs with
\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)
, where the limit is distributed according to some extremal distribution
\mathsf{Q}
. Let
(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of diverging times. Then, (
4
) is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|\leq\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q})-\varepsilon\Big)=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0.

This, together with our Theorem
1.3
now implies that (
4
) is equivalent to convergence in probability of the metastable density to the limit’s survival probability.

Corollary 1.8
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
1.7
, the condition (
4
) is equivalent to

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q}).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.7
.
∎

Naturally, establishing (
4
) for a given time-scale is in general hard and the main challenge in proving metastability for a concrete graph sequence.

Further discussion and related work.

Another useful observation pertaining to the locality of metastability on
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is that
\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathscr{G},o)
can be characterised by tightness of the extinction times.

Lemma 1.9
.

Suppose that
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
(\mathscr{G},o)
locally weakly. For any
\lambda>0
,

\{\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight if and only if

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)<\infty)=1.

Lemma
1.9
provides yet another perspective on the fast/slow transition. The law of large numbers in Corollary
1.8
suggests the following definition for the critical value

\lambda_{\rho}\big((\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\rho(\textup{e}^{c|\mathscr{V}_{n}|})>\varepsilon\big)=0\text{ for all }\varepsilon,c>0\big\},

where
\rho(t)=\rho_{n}(t)=|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t}|/|\mathscr{V}_{n}|
denotes the density process associated with
\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}
on
\mathscr{G}_{n}
. Denoting

\lambda_{\rho}^{-}\big((\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\sup\big\{\lambda>0\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\rho(t(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))>\varepsilon\big)=0\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0\text{ and }(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ with }t(n)\to\infty\big\},

we have that
\lambda^{-}_{\rho}\leq\lambda_{\rho}
. However, it is also elementary to see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\rho(t(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))>\varepsilon\big)=0\qquad\text{ for all }\varepsilon>0,t(n)\to\infty

if and only if
\{\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight. Consequently,
\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}=\lambda^{-}_{\rho}\leq\lambda_{\rho}
, in the case where the underlying graphs converge locally in probability. Note that
\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}\leq\lambda_{\rho}
also follows from Theorem
1.3
.
For the configuration model, the bound
\lambda_{\rho}\geq\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}
is essentially
[
28
, Theorem 6]
. Our results show that this inequality always holds if
\mathscr{G}_{n}\to(\mathscr{G},o)
locally in probability. Moreover, our proof of Theorem
1.5
below implies that

\text{if }\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)\text{ and }(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is sparse, then }\lambda_{\rho}=\lambda_{+}.

Further evidence that the polynomial time scale is the natural one for
fast
extinction in sparse locally tree-like graphs is, for instance, given in
[
13
]
and
[
29
]
. Note that, unlike in our Theorem
1.4
, the time scale of extinction in the graphs discussed there is
not
determined solely by the presence of stars. Furthermore, Theorem
1.4
should be contrasted with
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
, where it is shown that the
supercritical
extinction time is at least as large as the time scale given in Theorem
1.4
on

finite graph provided that
\lambda>\lambda_{\mathsf{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Our proof also shows that the time scale
\exp(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|/\log(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|)^{1+o(1)})
is not optimal, cf. Remark
2.2
, but that
\exp(\Theta(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))
cannot be achieved. It is an interesting question to determine whether there are sparse graphs on which fast extinction occurs on time scale
\exp(c^{-}(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))
and slow extinction occurs on time scale
\exp(c^{+}(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|))
with
c^{-}<c^{+}
, or even with
c^{-}=c^{+}
. Another recent work proving exponential extinction times with logarithmic correction for all infection rates is
[
2
]
, see in particular Theorem 1.1.(ii) therein. However, the random graphs considered in that paper are
small worlds
and it is highly likely that their local limits do not display an extinction phase in the parameter regime of
[
2
, Theorem 1.1.(ii)]
. This contrasts with our example in Theorem
1.4
, which is not a small world graph but displays distances comparable to the lattice
[
27
]
and does have a phase transition in the local limit.

Let us further mention that, if sparsity is violated, survival can occur on super-exponential time scales as demonstrated in
[
8
]
.

Finally, we would like to point out, that our approach to metastability in the contact process on random graph was inspired by

Overview of the proof section.

The remainder of the paper is devoted to the derivation of our results. We first recall some facts about local convergence and prove Lemma

We next explain, how the contact procoess can be incorporated into the local convergence setup. The notion of
random networks
describing processes on random graphs that are invariant under graph isomorphisms was coined in
[
1
]
. A

is a rooted graph
(G,o)
together with two maps
\Xi_{V}\colon V\to\mathcal{S},\,\Xi_{E}\colon E\to\mathcal{S}
on edges and vertices. Here,
\mathcal{S}
is some metric mark space. We choose
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{R}\times\{1,-1\})
, the space of all locally finite point measures on
\mathbb{R}
marked by
-1
or
1
. We turn the network into a random network by allowing the underlying graph
\mathscr{G}
to be random, and conditionally on
\mathscr{G}=G=(V,E)
, letting
(\Xi_{E}(e))_{e\in E}
be an independent collection of
\operatorname{Poisson}(2\lambda)
-processes where each point carries mark
1
or
-1
with probability
1/2
and by letting
(\Xi_{V}(v))_{v\in V}
be an independent collection of
\operatorname{Poisson}(1)
-processes where each point carries mark
1
. A realisation of the contact process on
G
with initial infections at
A\subset V
is now obtained by considering infection paths induced by interpreting
(\Xi_{E}(e))_{e\in E}
as infection events with orientations along edges and
(\Xi_{v}(v))_{v\in V}
as recovery events on the vertices. This is the well-known
graphical representation
of the contact process. It is straightforward to see that the law
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}
of the induced oriented percolation model is invariant under rooted isomorphisms of
(G,o)
. If
(G,o)
is selected by some random mechanism, we are therefore justified in interpreting the annealed law
\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}
as a distribution on random networks in the sense of
[
1
]
, in particular we may write

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}=\int\mathbf{P}_{G}^{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\mathsf{Q}(G,o)

to designate the law of the contact process on a random graph with distribution
\mathsf{Q}\in\mathcal{P}({{\mathbb{G}}}_{\ast})
. A similar definition applies to sequences of random graphs and henceforth we will assume, without loss of generality, that graph sequence, limit graph, and all associated contact processes live on the same probability space and have distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}
. This is only for notational convenience, since the framework of local convergence in probability is flexible enough to deal with graphs sequences that do not converge on the same probability space, see
[
19
, Remark 2.12]
for a discussion. We occasionally omit the parameter
\lambda
from the notation if we refer to distributional properties of the underlying graphs under

only.

Proof of Lemma
1.9
.

Let
\lambda
be such that
\xi
dies out almost surely. Fix

and let
\tau^{(n)}_{R}
and
\tau_{R}
denote the first times that a vertex at distance
R
from the root is infected in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
and
\mathscr{G}
, respectively. We have, for fixed

, that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n})>s)-\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)>s)|

(6)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n})>s,\tau_{R}^{(n)}=\infty)-\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{\emptyset}(o)>s,\tau_{R}=\infty)|+|\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{R}<\infty)-\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau_{R}^{(n)}<\infty)|.

By local weak convergence, the second term on the right hand side of (

, since the event in question depends solely on the
R

\mathscr{G}_{n}

Now assume that
\lambda
is such that
\xi

. Under the graph coupling, the infection dynamics can be perfectly coupled as well up to the first time a vertex outside the

from the root in

is positive. This contradicts tightness, since

almost surely as

∎

In this section, we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The proof is based on a comparison of the survival time on the graph
\mathscr{G}_{n}
with the time the process survives on the star graph
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{*}
, induced by the vertex of maximal degree. To this end, let us first recapitulate some known results on the survival time on star graphs. Let
\mathscr{S}^{(k)}
be the star graph with
k

. The first result about the survival time on
\mathscr{S}^{(k)}
is
[
4

, stating that the infection survives, with high probability, exponentially long in the number of leaves. We use a refined version of this statement. First, by
[
30
, Lemma 2.5]
,we have

\mathbf{E}_{\mathscr{S}^{(k)}}^{\lambda}\big[\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{S}^{(k)})\big]\geq{\rm e}^{2c_{\lambda}k},

where
c_{\lambda}>0
is a
\lambda
-dependent constant. Secondly, we have, due to
[
31

\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{S}^{(k)}}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{S}^{(k)})\leq t\big)\leq\frac{t}{\mathbf{E}^{\lambda}_{\mathscr{S}^{(k)}}\big[\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{S}^{(k)})\big]}.

Combining both results and choosing

, we thus infer

\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{S}^{(k)}}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{S}^{(k)})>{\rm e}^{c_{\lambda}k}\big)\geq 1-{\rm e}^{-c_{\lambda}k}.

(7)

Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
, choose
1<p<1+\varepsilon
, and consider the augmented Gilbert graph
\mathscr{G}_{n}
, constructed above, with radius distribution

\mathbb{P}(R>x)=\big(x\log^{p}(x)\big)^{-1}.

(8)

Note that
R
has finite first moment as

\mathbb{P}\big(R^{*}_{n}\leq\tfrac{Kn}{\log^{1+\varepsilon}(n)}\big)=\big(1-\tfrac{\log^{1+\varepsilon}(n)}{Kn\log^{p}(Kn/\log^{1+\varepsilon}(n))}\big)^{n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\log^{1+\varepsilon-p}(n)/K},

which tends to zero, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\mathsf{deg}(X^{*}_{n})>\tfrac{Kn}{\log^{1+\varepsilon}(n)}\big)

Combining this with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{V}_{n})>{\rm e}^{n/\log^{1+\varepsilon}(n)}\big)

2.3
Upper bounds on the metastable density from local limit

Before we provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we introduce some notation and auxiliary results. Throughout this section, let
\big((\mathscr{G}_{n},o_{n})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and its local limit
(\mathscr{G},o)
with distribution
\mathsf{Q}
be given. As usually,
o_{n}
denotes a uniformly chosen vertex here. Fix
\lambda>0
, set
\eta=\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q})
and let
\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)
denote the first time that the infection started in

reaches a vertex of graph distance

from

in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
. Let further

denote the corresponding quantities for the rooted limit graph

\eta_{\geq R}=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}}^{\lambda}(\tau_{R}(o)<\infty)],\quad R\in\mathbb{N},

The proof of Theorem

relies on the following two auxiliary statements.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)
. Then,

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi^{v}_{t(n)}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\big\}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}0,

for any

and any diverging sequence
(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)
where
(\mathscr{G},o)
is distributed according to some extremal measure

. Then we have, for any

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}Z_{\geq R}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}\eta_{\geq R}.

Before we prove Propositions

and

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By additivity, we have that

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(\big|\xi_{t(n)}^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}\big|\in\cdot\big)=\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}\,\in\cdot\Big)

and hence the assertion of the theorem is equivalent to showing

\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}-\eta\Big)\vee 0\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}0.

(9)

Observe that, on one hand,

\eta=\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\bigcap_{R\in\mathbb{N}}\{\tau_{R}(o)<\infty\}\Big)=\lim_{R\to\infty}\eta_{\geq R},

and hence, for any given
\varepsilon
, we may chose
R_{\varepsilon}
so large that, for any
R>R_{\varepsilon}
,

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}\leq\eta_{\geq R}+\varepsilon/2

implies

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}\leq\eta+\varepsilon.

On the other hand, the bound

\displaystyle|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}

\displaystyle=|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}+|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)<\infty\big\}+|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\big\},

\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}-|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}Z_{\geq R}\Big)\vee 0\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}0.

for any

. Choosing

and applying Proposition

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\{\xi^{v}_{t(n)}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\}>\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\Big[|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\{\xi^{v}_{t(n)}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\Big[|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}f_{t(n)}\big(B_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(v,R)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{(H,o_{H})\in\mathcal{G}_{*}}f_{t(n)}(H,o_{H})\mathbb{P}\big(B_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(o_{n},R)=(H,o_{H})\big),

where

By the assumption of local convergence in probability, the distributions of

converge to the distribution of

. Since the limiting graph is locally finite,
\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathscr{G}}(o,R)\in\;\cdot\;)
is a probability measure on

and hence, by Prokhorov’s theorem, the distributions of

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\{\xi^{v}_{t(n)}\neq\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)>t(n)\}>\varepsilon\Big)

We have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\big[|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}Z_{\geq R}-\eta_{R}\big]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o_{n})<\infty\big)\big|\mathscr{G}_{n}\big]-\eta_{R}=0,

(b), hence the first moments asymptotically agree under
\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}
. On the other hand,

\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\big[|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-2}Z_{\geq R}^{2}\big|\mathscr{G}_{n}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o_{n})<\infty,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o^{\prime}_{n})<\infty\big)\big|\mathscr{G}_{n}\big],

where
(o_{n},o^{\prime}_{n})
is uniformly chosen (with replacement) from
\mathscr{V}_{n}\times\mathscr{V}_{n}
. Since the events
\{\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o_{n})<\infty\}
and
\{\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o^{\prime}_{n})<\infty\}
are measurable with respect to the marks
\Xi_{V},\Xi_{E}
of the random network and the graph inside a radius of
R+1
around the respective root, the right-hand side converges to
\eta_{\geq R}^{2}
as
n\to\infty
by Lemma
2.1
(c). Combining the first and second moment limits, yields that the conditional variance of
{Z_{\geq R}}/{|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}
, given
\mathscr{G}_{n}
, vanishes, implying that
{Z_{\geq R}}/{|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}
converges to its expectation
\eta_{\geq R}
in probability and in
L_{1}
.
∎

2.4
Lower bounds on the metastable density from local limits

In this section, we prove Proposition
1.7
.

Proof of Proposition
1.7
.

Assume that
\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)
and let
(t(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote a sequence of diverging times. We begin by showing that

|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}}\eta_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Q})

implies

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(\xi^{o_{n}}_{t(n)}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(o_{n})<t(n)\big)=0.

To this end, observe that, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\Big[|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}>|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\varepsilon\Big).

(11)

The random variable of the second term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\tau_{R}^{(n)}<\infty\big\}-\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\tau_{R}^{(n)}<\infty\big\}-\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}+\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)>t(n)\big\}.

After dividing both sides by
\mathscr{V}_{n}
, the right-hand side converges in probability to
\eta_{R}-\eta(\mathsf{Q})
, as
n\to\infty
by assumption and Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
. As
\eta_{R}\downarrow\eta
, as
R\to\infty
, the expectation in (
11
) can be made arbitrarily small by choosing
n
and
R
large enough, proving the claimed implication.

It remains to prove the other implication, i.e. that (
4
) implies, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t(n)}|<|\mathscr{V}_{n}|(\eta-\varepsilon)\big)\to 0\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(12)

Using additivity, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}<|\mathscr{V}_{n}|(\eta-\varepsilon)\Big)\to 0\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

To obtain this statement, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset\big\}<|\mathscr{V}_{n}|(\eta-\varepsilon)\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}\neq\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)<\infty\big\}<|\mathscr{V}_{n}|(\eta-\varepsilon)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)<\infty\big\}<|\mathscr{V}_{n}|(\eta-\varepsilon/2)\Big)+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)<\infty\big\}>|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\varepsilon/2\Big),

where the first summand tends to zero by Proposition
2.4
. For the second summand, we note that
\xi^{v}_{t(n)}=\emptyset
and
\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)<\infty
together imply
\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)<t(n)
. A application of Markov’s inequality then yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\lambda}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\xi_{t(n)}^{v}=\emptyset,\tau^{(n)}_{R}(v)<t(n)\big\}>\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}\mathbf{E}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\Big[\#\big\{v\in\mathscr{V}_{n}\colon\tau_{R}^{(n)}(v)\leq t(n)\big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbf{P}^{\lambda}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}\big(\xi_{t(n)}^{o_{n}}=\emptyset,\tau_{R}^{(n)}(o_{n})<t(n)\big).

Taking expectations on both sides, we hence see that (
4
) is a sufficient criterion for (
12
).
∎

2.5
Fast extinction by absence of metastability

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
. We begin with an auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(\mathscr{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be sparse with
\mathscr{G}_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\rightharpoonup}}(\mathscr{G},o)
. Then, for every
\delta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{I\subset\mathscr{V}_{n}\colon|I|<\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}\sum_{v\in I}\mathsf{deg}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(v)>\delta|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\Big)=0.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
and denote
|\mathscr{V}_{n}|=N
. Let
D_{1}^{(n)}\leq D_{2}^{(n)}\leq\dots\leq D_{N}^{(n)}
denote the vertex degrees in
\mathscr{G}_{n}
ordered by magnitude. By sparsity and the convergence assumption, it follows that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,

\infty>\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{deg}_{\mathscr{G}}(o)]-\lim_{n\to\infty}N^{-1}\sum_{i=\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor+1}^{N}D_{i}^{(n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}D_{i}^{(n)}=:\sigma_{\varepsilon},

where the convergence is to be understood in probability. In particular, the deterministic term
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
on the right-hand side vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Writing
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(n):=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}D_{i}^{(n)}
, we obtain for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
fixed

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}(n)>2\sigma_{\varepsilon})=0.

The desired result now follows upon choosing
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta)
such that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}<\delta/2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

As
\lambda_{\rho}\geq\lambda_{1}
by Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to show
\lambda_{\rho}=\lambda_{+}
. Clearly,
\lambda_{\rho}\geq\lambda_{+}
by definition, and it hence remains to show
\lambda_{\rho}\leq\lambda_{+}
. To this end, pick
\lambda<\lambda_{\rho}
and show that
\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}
does not survive on the exponential scale. Let
c>0
be arbitrary, denote by
T=T_{n,c}=\textup{e}^{c|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}
the relevant time scale and let

r(\varepsilon,T)=T^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\big\{\big|\xi_{t}^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}\big|\leq\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big\}\;\mathrm{d}t

represent the proportion of time that the infected set spends in low density states. Let

\tau_{1}=\inf\big\{t>0\colon\big|\xi_{t}^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}\big|\leq\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big\}

and denote

\tau_{k}=\inf\big\{t>\tau_{k-1}+1\colon\big|\xi^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}_{t}\big|\leq\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big\},\quad k\geq 2.

Define further
K=\max\{k\colon\tau_{k}\leq T-1\}
. Fix an arbitrary
\delta>0
and let
\varepsilon
be as in Lemma
2.5
. Then, at each time
\tau_{k}
,
k\leq K
, the total size of the infected set is at most
\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|
and, consequently, has total degree no larger than
\delta|\mathscr{V}_{n}|
with probability
1-o(1)
. Hence, conditionally on the evolution of the process up to the stopping time
\tau_{k}
, the probability of immediate extinction just after
\tau_{k}
, meaning that a recovery occurs at every vertex in the time interval
[\tau_{k},\tau_{k}+1)
but no infection, is at least

(1-\textup{e}^{-1})^{|\xi_{\tau_{k}}|}\textup{e}^{-2\lambda\sum_{v\in\xi_{\tau_{k}}}\mathsf{deg}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}(v)}\geq(1-\textup{e}^{-1})^{\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}\textup{e}^{-2\lambda\delta|\mathscr{V}_{n}|},

where the inequality holds with probability exceeding
1-o(1)
uniformly for all
k\leq K
. More precisely, if
E_{n}
denotes the exceptional event in Lemma
2.5
, then we obtain from the strong Markov property and a coupling of the contact process to a geometric experiment,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(\{\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{V}_{n})>T\}\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(E_{n}\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big)+\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\big[\mathbbm{1}\{E_{n}^{\mathsf{c}}\}\mathbbm{1}\{\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{V}_{n})>T\}\mathbbm{1}\{K>T/3\}\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big]+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(K\leq T/3\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(E_{n}\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big)+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}\big(K\leq T/3\,\big|\,|\mathscr{V}_{n}|\big)+3T^{-1}\textup{e}^{-\log(1-\textup{e}^{-1})\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|+2\lambda\delta|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}.

Here, the bound on the last term follows from the fact that
\mathbb{P}(G>t)\leq(pt)^{-1}
for a
\operatorname{Geometric}(p)
random variable
G
. Taking expectations yields

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(\mathscr{V}_{n})>T)\leq\mathbb{P}(E_{n})+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(K\leq T/3)+\mathbb{E}\big[3T^{-1}\textup{e}^{-\log(1-\textup{e}^{-1})\varepsilon|\mathscr{V}_{n}|+2\lambda\delta|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}\big].

(13)

Decreasing the values of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
if needed, the last term of the right-hand side vanishes since
T=\textup{e}^{c|\mathscr{V}_{n}|}
with fixed
c
, as
n\to\infty
, while the first term vanishes by Lemma
2.5
. Hence it remains to show that
\gamma_{n}=\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(K\leq T/3)
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Note that, on the event
\{K\leq T/3\}
, the term

1-r(\varepsilon,T)
is bounded from below by
2/3
. Let
W
be uniform on
[0,T]
under
\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}
and independent of the graph sequence and contact process. Denote by

\rho(t)=|\mathscr{V}_{n}|^{-1}|\xi_{t}^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}|,t\geq 0,

the density process and by
\mathcal{I}_{n}
the
\sigma
-field generated by
\mathscr{G}_{n}
and the corresponding edge marks in the network construction of
\xi
. We have

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\rho(W)>\varepsilon)\geq\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\big[\mathbbm{1}\{K\leq T/3\}\mathbb{P}(\rho(W)>\varepsilon\mid\mathcal{I}_{n})\big]\geq\tfrac{2}{3}\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(K\leq T/3)=\tfrac{2}{3}\gamma_{n}.

On the other hand,

\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\rho(W)>\varepsilon)\leq\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}[\rho(W)],

and by additivity of
\xi
we have

\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}[\rho(W)]\leq\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n})>W)\leq\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(W\leq\sqrt{T})+\mathbb{P}^{\lambda}(\tau^{(n)}_{\emptyset}(o_{n})>\sqrt{T}).

The first term vanishes by choice of
W
. Note that duality implies that the second term equals

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{P}_{\mathscr{G}_{n}}^{\lambda}\big(\xi_{\sqrt{T}}^{\mathscr{V}_{n}}\cap\{o_{n}\}\neq\emptyset\big)\big]=\mathbb{E}^{\lambda}\big[\rho(\sqrt{T})\big],

as
o_{n}
is chosen uniformly. Since
\lambda<\lambda_{\rho}
, we have
\rho(\sqrt{T})\to 0
in probability, and the expectation on the right-hand side thus vanishes by dominated convergence, as
\rho\leq 1
. Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}=0
and the result follows from (
13
).
∎

Unveiling Berry curvature contributions to Hall current in C 4 ​ K C_{4}K materials
T. Farajollahpour, R. Ganesh, K. V. Samokhin

Abstract
We identify a new contribution to the conventional Hall effect that emerges in materials with C 4 ​ K C_{4}K symmetry. This contribution originates from the modification of phase space density due to the Berry curvature, as we demonstrate using semiclassical equations of motion for band electrons. As an illustration, we build a minimal two-band tight-binding model with altermagnetic order that breaks C 4 C_{4} and K K symmetries while preserving C 4 ​ K C_{4}K . The resulting Hall conductivity shows a square-root feature at the altermagnetic phase transition, which is due to the novel Berry curvature-driven contribution emerging below the critical temperature. This effect may offer a simple transport-based signature of an altermagnetic phase transition.



Introduction.
—
The classical Hall effect describes a voltage induced in a metal that is transverse to both an applied electric field and an external magnetic field
Hall 1879
;
Kittel and McEuen 2018
.
In the last several decades, multiple variants of the Hall effect have been discussed as signatures of symmetry breaking and/or topology. A prominent example is the anomalous Hall effect in magnetic materials
Karplus and Luttinger 1954
;
Nagaosa et al. 2010
, which dispenses with the need for an external magnetic field. Another example is the planar Hall effect, where all fields are in the same plane
Nandy et al. 2017
;
He et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2020
. Apart from these phenomena, great strides have been made in the quantized Hall effects that manifest in time-reversal (TR) symmetry breaking insulators
Klitzing et al. 1980
;
Laughlin 1981
;
Li et al. 2021
;
Park et al. 2023
.
Here, we describe a new contribution to the
conventional
Hall effect in a metal, where the applied electric field, external magnetic field, and the induced voltage are in mutually perpendicular directions. We interpret this new contribution as a signature of breaking TR and rotational symmetry, while preserving their combination.

Hall effect measurements have emerged as a powerful probe of band topology in condensed matter systems
Berry 1984
;
Xiao et al. 2006
;
Moore and Orenstein 2010
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
;
Shapere and Wilczek 1989
;
Bohm et al. 2013
;
Haldane 2004
;
Xiao et al. 2010
. This is due to the strong effect of the Berry curvature on charge carrier dynamics, analogous to external electromagnetic fields
Sundaram and Niu 1999
;
Xiao et al. 2010
.
In the semiclassical transport regime, Berry curvature effects arise from two factors: (i) an anomalous contribution to wavepacket velocity and (ii) a correction to the density of states in phase space
Bliokh 2006a
;
Bliokh 2006b
;
Duval et al. 2006
;
Xiao et al. 2007
.
The former has received enormous attention, e.g., as providing an intrinsic contribution to various transport signatures
Bohm et al. 2013
;
Chang et al. 2023
. The latter is relatively less studied, especially in the context of transport signatures.
Here, we demonstrate that this latter effect leads to a distinct contribution to the Hall current.
This contribution appears in addition to the conventional Hall current originating solely from the Lorentz force acting on charge carriers
Stone 1992
;
Du et al. 2021
;
Chang et al. 2023
.

It is well known that the Berry curvature is highly constrained by symmetries. A Berry curvature monopole can only appear in systems that break TR symmetry. A Berry dipole requires breaking of inversion symmetry
Sodemann and Fu 2015
. Recent studies have shown that a Berry curvature quadrupole can appear in systems with
C_{4}K
symmetry
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Farajollahpour et al. 2025
. Here, both TR symmetry (
K
) and a fourfold rotational symmetry (
C_{4}
) are broken, while their combination
C_{4}K
remains a symmetry. In such systems, there is no Berry curvature-induced conductivity in linear or quadratic orders in the applied electric field. It appears only at the third order in the applied electric field.

In this paper, we focus on a different signature that requires both external electric and magnetic fields. The resulting current is linear in both fields.
The requisite
C_{4}K
symmetry is realized in altermagnets
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022b
;
Fedchenko et al. 2024
as well as in magnetically ordered materials belonging to certain magnetic point groups
Zhang et al. 2023
.
Intriguingly, the very same symmetry requirements are also invoked in chiral
higher-order topological crystalline insulators
Schindler et al. 2018
. Here, we restrict our attention to metallic systems where the Hall response appears as a Fermi-surface property
Haldane 2004
;
Wang et al. 2007
.

—
We consider the semiclassical description of transport in the presence of external electric and magnetic fields,
\bm{E}

\displaystyle j_{i}=-e\int\frac{d^{d}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,D(\bm{k})\,{\dot{r}}_{i}\,f(\bm{k}),

—
We have used the long-wavelength model given by Eq. (
7

11
) and (
12

The results of a numerical calculation of

While the conventional Hall conductivity is the only contribution at
T>T_{c}

\sigma^{c}_{H}
and
\sigma^{B}_{H}

\sigma_{H}^{c}
shows a weaker singularity, namely a kink with a discontinuous but non-divergent derivative. This behaviour arises due to changes in the band structure on account of the altermagnetic order. These are robust features, regardless of details such as the strength of altermagnetic order, chemical potential, etc.

Discussion.
—
We have demonstrated that the Berry curvature induces a novel Hall current contribution in
C_{4}K
materials. This effect originates from the modification to phase-space density due to Berry curvature. We have presented a toy tight-binding model in 2D, with analytic results from a Dirac cone approximation confirmed by a full numerical calculation. Our symmetry-based arguments are expected to hold for three-dimensional materials as well.

The Berry-curvature contribution to the Hall conductivity is particularly noticeable at a phase transition where
C_{4}K
order sets in. Its square-root singularity can serve as a simple transport signature for an altermagnetic phase transition in such materials as
\rm{KVe_{2}Se_{2}O}

Jiang et al. 2025
,
\rm{RuO_{2}}
,
\rm{MnO_{2}}
, and
\rm{MnF_{2}}

Šmejkal et al. 2022b
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
. An exciting future direction is to examine the effect of perturbations such as strain or an external magnetic field on the altermagnetic phase transition
Chakraborty et al. 2024
;
Steward et al. 2023
;
Belashchenko 2025
;
Zhou et al. 2025
and its associated Hall current features.

, we solve the Boltzmann equation and calculate
\delta f(\bm{k})
in an order-by-order fashion,

\displaystyle\delta f(\bm{k})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-\tau\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\partial}_{k}})^{n}f_{0}(\tilde{\varepsilon}),

(S17)

where the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
order term is the
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{n})
correction. Retaining terms up to second order, we have

\displaystyle f(\bm{k})=f_{0}(\tilde{\varepsilon})+\delta f_{1}+\delta f_{2},

(S18)

where
f_{0}(\tilde{\varepsilon})
is the equilibrium Fermi distribution function in the presence of a magnetic field, given by

f_{0}(\tilde{\varepsilon})=f_{0}(\varepsilon-{\bm{m}\cdot\bm{B}})\simeq f_{0}(\varepsilon)-f^{\prime}_{0}(\varepsilon){\bm{m}\cdot\bm{B}}
in linear order in
\bm{B}
. For the corrections, we obtain the following expressions:

\displaystyle\delta f_{1}=-\tau\dot{k}_{i}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial k_{i}},

\displaystyle\delta f_{2}=-\tau\dot{k}_{i}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial k_{i}}=\tau^{2}\left(\dot{k}_{i}\frac{\partial\dot{k}_{j}}{\partial k_{i}}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial k_{j}}+\dot{k}_{i}\dot{k}_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{0}}{\partial k_{i}\partial k_{j}}\right),

(S19)

where
\dot{\bm{k}}
is given by

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}=D^{-1}\left[-\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}-\frac{e}{\hbar}({\tilde{\bm{v}}}\times\bm{B})-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}(\bm{E}\cdot\bm{B})\,\bm{\Omega}\right],

(S20)

with

\tilde{\bm{v}}={\frac{1}{\hbar}}\frac{\partial\tilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{k})}{\partial\bm{k}}={\bm{v}}-{\frac{1}{\hbar}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{k}}(\bm{m}\cdot\bm{B})

being the group velocity derived from
\tilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{k})
.

To obtain Eq. (
S20
), we substitute Eq. (
S13
) into Eq. (
S14
) which yields

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}=-\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\tilde{\bm{v}}\times\bm{B}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\dot{\bm{k}}\times\bm{\Omega})\times\bm{B}=-\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\tilde{\bm{v}}\times\bm{B}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\dot{\bm{k}}(\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{\Omega}(\dot{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{B}).

(S21)

Substituting Eq. (
S14
) in the last term and recognizing that
(\dot{\bm{r}}\times\bm{B})\cdot\bm{B}=0
, we arrive at Eq. (
S20
).

To evaluate Eq. (
S15
), we follow an analogous procedure to find
\dot{\bm{r}}
. We substitute
\dot{\bm{k}}
into
\dot{\bm{r}}
, and obtain:

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}=\tilde{\bm{v}}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{\dot{r}}\times\bm{B})\times\bm{\Omega}=\tilde{\bm{v}}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega}-\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{\dot{r}}({\bm{B}}\cdot\bm{\Omega})+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{B}(\bm{\dot{r}}\cdot\bm{\Omega}).

(S22)

Substituting Eq. (
S13
) in the last term and using
(\dot{\bm{k}}\times\bm{\Omega})\cdot\bm{\Omega}=0
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\bm{\dot{r}}=D^{-1}[\tilde{\bm{v}}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{\Omega}\cdot\tilde{\bm{v}})\bm{B}].

(S23)

The current density from Eq. (
1
) comes out to be

\displaystyle j_{i}=-e\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\big[\tilde{v}_{i}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega})_{i}+\frac{e}{\hbar}(\bm{\Omega}\cdot\tilde{\bm{v}})B_{i}\big]\big[f_{0}(\tilde{\varepsilon})+\delta f_{1}+\delta f_{2}\big].

(S24)

This expression for the current is valid to
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})
.

Assuming weak external fields, we focus on the current responses that are (i) linear in the electric field alone and (ii) linear in both electric and magnetic fields, i.e., proportional to
EB
. The current that is linear just in the electric field turns out to be

\displaystyle j_{i}(\propto E)=-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(\bm{E}\times\bm{\Omega})_{i}f_{0}-\tau\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}v_{i}v_{\ell}E_{\ell}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}.

(S25)

The first term here represents the anomalous Hall current arising from the Berry curvature. Notably, this contribution is independent of the relaxation time – it appears even in the absence of scattering processes. The second term is the conventional Drude response, parallel to the applied electric field and linear in
\tau
.

The current proportional to
EB
is given by

\displaystyle j_{i}(\propto EB)=

\displaystyle\tau\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left(\frac{\partial\bm{m}}{\partial k_{i}}\cdot\bm{B}v_{\ell}E_{\ell}-v_{i}E_{\ell}\frac{\partial{\bm{m}}}{\partial k_{\ell}}\cdot\bm{B}\right)\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}+\tau e^{2}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}v_{i}E_{\ell}v_{\ell}({\bm{m}}\cdot\bm{B})\frac{\partial^{2}f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle+\tau\frac{e^{3}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}v_{i}E_{\ell}v_{\ell}(\bm{B}\cdot\bm{\Omega})\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}-\tau\frac{e^{3}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}(\bm{\Omega}\cdot\bm{v})B_{i}E_{\ell}v_{\ell}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\frac{e^{3}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{d}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}v_{i}E_{n}(\bm{v}\times\bm{B})_{\ell}\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial k_{\ell}}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}.

(S26)

The first and second integrals involve
\bm{m}(\bm{k})
, the magnetic moment of the electronic wavepacket.
The third and fourth integrals involve the Berry curvature, encoding the effect of band topology on transport. They depend on the relative orientation of
\bm{B}
with respect to
\bm{\Omega}
and
\bm{v}
.
The last term is the conventional Hall conductivity, describing a transverse current arising due to the Lorentz force.

II
Hall currents in a
C_{4}K
material

We consider the setup described in Fig. 1 of main text, with
an external dc electric field along
y
and a magnetic field along
z
. We adapt the current expressions of Eqs. (
S25
) and (
S26
) to a two-dimensional system with
C_{4}K
symmetry. The net current measured along
x
is given by

\displaystyle j_{x}=\left(\sigma_{AHE}+\sigma_{T}\right)E_{y}+\left(\sigma^{c}_{H}+\sigma^{m}_{H}+\sigma^{B}_{H}\right)E_{y}B_{z}.

(S27)

The first term represents the anomalous Hall response, with

\displaystyle\sigma_{AHE}=-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\Omega_{z}~f_{0}.

(S28)

The second term corresponds to the transverse conductivity, with

\displaystyle\sigma_{T}=-\tau\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{x}v_{y}~\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}.

(S29)

Both
\sigma_{AHE}
and
\sigma_{T}
vanish due to the
C_{4}K
symmetry. This can be seen from the symmetry properties of
v_{x}
,
v_{y}
and
\Omega_{z}
tabulated in Table
2
.

The third term in Eq. (
S27
) describes the conventional Hall effect, which originates from the Lorentz force acting on charge carriers. It is expressed as

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{c}=-\tau^{2}\frac{e^{3}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{x}\bigg(v_{y}\frac{\partial v_{y}}{\partial k_{x}}-v_{x}\frac{\partial v_{y}}{\partial k_{y}}\bigg)\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}.

(S30)

The fourth term which is associated with the magnetic moment of the wavepacket, is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{m}=\tau\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\bm{v}\times{\bm{\nabla}}_{\bm{k}}m_{z}\right)_{z}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}+\tau e^{2}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{x}v_{y}~m_{z}~\frac{\partial^{2}f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon^{2}}.

(S31)

The last term describes the Berry curvature-induced Hall response:

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{B}=\tau\frac{e^{3}}{\hbar}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{x}v_{y}~\Omega_{z}~\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\varepsilon}.

(S32)

This is the sole Berry curvature-dependent contribution to the Hall conductivity. Note that the term proportional to
\bm{\Omega}\cdot\bm{v}
in Eq. (
S26
) vanishes for a two-dimensional sample. Unlike the usual Lorentz force-driven Hall effect,
\sigma_{H}^{B}
is intrinsically linked to the phase-space factor
D(\bm{k})
and is strongly influenced by the band geometry of the material. The effects of the phase-space factor
D(\bm{k})
on the longitudinal conductivity have been studied in Ref.
Yang et al. 2023
.

C_{4}K

We consider a two-dimensional material that breaks
C_{4}
and
K
symmetries, but preserves
C_{4}K
. This also imbues the material with
C_{2}
symmetry, as applying
C_{4}K
twice results in
C_{2}
. The symmetry properties of the velocity
\bm{v}
,
\Omega_{z}
and
m_{z}
are summarized in Table

.

For instance, let us examine the action of
C_{4}K
on the Berry curvature. We begin with the semiclassical equations of motion, Eq. (
S13
), in the absence of magnetic moments,

\displaystyle\dot{x}=v_{x}+\Omega_{z}\dot{k}_{y},\qquad\dot{y}=v_{y}-\Omega_{z}\dot{k}_{x}.

(S33)

Under
C_{4}K
, we have

\dot{x}\rightarrow-\dot{y},~v_{x}\rightarrow-v_{y},~\dot{k}_{y}\rightarrow-\dot{k}_{x}
.
Therefore, in order to preserve the equations of motion, we must have
\Omega_{z}\rightarrow-\Omega_{z}
.

Similarly, we can find how
C_{4}K
acts on the magnetic moment. The semiclassical equations of motion in the presence of nonzero magnetic moments take the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}=v_{x}-\frac{\partial m_{z}}{\partial k_{x}}B_{z}+\Omega_{z}\dot{k}_{y},\qquad\dot{y}=v_{y}-\frac{\partial m_{z}}{\partial k_{y}}B_{z}-\Omega_{z}\dot{k}_{x}.

(S34)

Under
C_{4}K
, we have
\Omega_{z}\rightarrow-\Omega_{z}
. In order to preserve the equations of motion, we must have
m_{z}\rightarrow-m_{z}
.

IV
Tight-binding model

To better understand the origin of various terms in the Hamiltonian, we construct a tight-binding model that is consistent with
C_{4}K
symmetry. We start with a 2D square lattice, as shown in Fig. 2 in the main text. Apart from the usual hopping processes, we have spin-dependent hopping between next-nearest neighbours. These could arise from the Rashba spin-orbit coupling. We introduce “altermagnetic order parameters”,
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
. They encode preferential hopping of each spin along nearest and next-nearest neighbour bonds. Crucially, the
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
processes break
C_{4}
and
K
, but preserve
C_{4}K
. The Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}\sum_{i,\alpha}\big(c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{i+x,\alpha}+c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}c_{i+y,\alpha}+H.c.\big)

\displaystyle-i\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{i,\alpha\beta}(c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}^{y}c_{i-x+y,\beta}-c^{\dagger}_{i-x+y,\beta}{\sigma}^{y}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i,\alpha})

\displaystyle-i\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{i,\alpha\beta}(c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}^{x}c_{i+x+y,\beta}-c^{\dagger}_{i+x+y,\beta}{\sigma}^{x}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i,\alpha})

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{1}}{2}\sum_{i,\alpha}\big(c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}^{z}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i+x,\beta}-c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}^{z}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i+y,\beta}+H.c.\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{J_{2}}{2}\sum_{i,\alpha}\big(c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}^{z}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i-x+y,\beta}-c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}{\sigma}^{z}_{\alpha\beta}c_{i+x+y,\beta}+H.c.\big),

(S35)

where
c^{\dagger}_{i,\alpha}
creates a particle with spin
\alpha
at lattice site
i
,
t
denotes the hopping parameter, and
\lambda
corresponds to the spin-orbit coupling. In momentum space, this Hamiltonian takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle-t\big(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y}\big)\hat{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left[\sin\left(k_{x}+k_{y}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{x}+\sin\left(k_{y}-k_{x}\right)\hat{\sigma}_{y}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}\right]\hat{\sigma}_{z}.

(S36)

Upon diagonalizing this Hamiltonian, the energy eigenvalues are obtained as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\pm}({\bm{k}})=\delta\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\xi+\beta+\gamma},

(S37)

where

\displaystyle\delta=-t(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y}),

\displaystyle\xi=4J_{1}^{2}+J_{2}^{2}+\lambda^{2}-8J_{1}^{2}\cos k_{x}\cos k_{y},

\displaystyle\beta=(2J_{1}^{2}-J_{2}^{2})(\cos 2k_{x}+\cos 2k_{y})+(J_{2}^{2}-\lambda^{2})\cos 2k_{x}\cos 2k_{y},

\displaystyle\gamma=4J_{1}J_{2}(\sin 2k_{x}\sin k_{y}-\sin k_{x}\sin 2k_{y}).

\epsilon_{\pm}({\bm{k}})
denote the energy of upper and lower bands. Fig.

M-Y-\Gamma-X-M

),
\Gamma=(0,0)

\epsilon_{\Gamma}=-2t
and
\epsilon_{M}=2t
. When
t

\Gamma

{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle H_{\Gamma}({\bm{k}})=

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}(4-k^{2})\hat{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(k_{x}+k_{y})\hat{\sigma}_{x}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(k_{y}-k_{x})\hat{\sigma}_{y}-\left[\frac{J_{1}}{2}(k^{2}_{x}-k^{2}_{y})-J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}\right]\hat{\sigma}_{z},

(S38)

whereas near the
M

\displaystyle H_{M}(\bm{k})=\frac{t}{2}(4-k^{2})\hat{\sigma}_{0}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(k_{x}+k_{y})\hat{\sigma}_{x}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(k_{y}-k_{x})\hat{\sigma}_{y}+\left[\frac{J_{1}}{2}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})+J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}\right]\hat{\sigma}_{z}.

(S39)

(S41)

V
Berry curvature and the magnetic moment of two-level system

In a two-level system, the Hamiltonian can be represented as
\hat{H}=d_{0}({\bm{k}})\hat{\sigma}_{0}+{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})\cdot\hat{\bm{\sigma}}
. Here, we take the Pauli matrices to act on the physical spin of the electron. The associated Berry curvature, orbital and spin magnetic moments are given by
Xiao et al. 2010

\displaystyle\Omega_{z,\pm}=\pm\epsilon_{ijz}\frac{1}{2|{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})|^{3}}{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})\cdot\left[\frac{\partial{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{i}}\times\frac{\partial{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{j}}\right],

(S42)

\displaystyle m_{z,\pm}^{\rm orb}=\mp\epsilon_{ijz}\frac{e}{4\hbar}\frac{1}{|{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})|}{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})\cdot\left[\frac{\partial{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{i}}\times\frac{\partial{\bm{d}}(\bm{k})}{\partial k_{j}}\right],

(S43)

and

\displaystyle{\bm{m}}^{\rm s}_{\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\rm B}g\frac{\bm{d}(\bm{k})}{|\bm{d}(\bm{k})|},

(S44)

where
\mu_{\rm B}
is the Bohr magneton,
g
is the Landé
g
-factor, and
\pm
indicates the upper and lower bands.

In the tight-binding model described above, the Berry curvature at an arbitrary point in the Brillouin zone comes out to be

\displaystyle\Omega_{z,\pm}=\mp\frac{\lambda^{2}(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y})[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}]}{8\left(\lambda^{2}\big(1-\cos k_{x}\cos k_{y}\big)+[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}]^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

(S45)

The orbital and spin magnetic moments are given by

\displaystyle m_{z,\pm}^{\rm orb}=\pm\frac{e\lambda^{2}(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y})[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}]}{16\left(\lambda^{2}\big(1-\cos k_{x}\cos k_{y}\big)+[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}]^{2}\right)^{1/2}}

(S46)

and

\displaystyle m^{\rm s}_{z,\pm}=\pm\frac{\mu_{\rm B}g}{2}\frac{J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}}{\left(\lambda^{2}\big(1-\cos k_{x}\cos k_{y}\big)+[J_{1}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})+J_{2}\sin k_{x}\sin k_{y}]^{2}\right)^{1/2}}

(S47)

We immediately see that under
C_{4}K
,
\Omega_{z,\pm}\to-\Omega_{z,\pm}
and
m_{z,\pm}\to-m_{z,\pm}
, as previously argued on symmetry grounds, see Sec.
III
. Additionally we find that
\Omega_{z,+}=-\Omega_{z,-}
and
m_{z,+}=-m_{z,-}
.

S7
and
S8

\Omega_{z,+}
and
m_{z,\pm}^{\rm orb}
over the Brillouin zone for three representative values of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
. The plots exhibit clear quadrupole-like distributions, indicative of
C_{4}K
symmetry.

In the immediate vicinities of
\Gamma
and
M
points, the Berry curvature comes out to be

\displaystyle\Omega^{\Gamma}_{z,\pm}=\pm\frac{[J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]\lambda^{2}}{\left([J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]^{2}+2\lambda^{2}k^{2}\right)^{3/2}}

(S48)

and

\displaystyle\Omega^{M}_{z,\pm}=\mp\frac{[J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})+2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]\lambda^{2}}{\left([J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})+2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]^{2}+2\lambda^{2}k^{2}\right)^{3/2}}.

(S49)

For the orbital magnetic moment around
\Gamma
and
M
we obtain:

\displaystyle m^{{\rm orb},~\Gamma}_{z,\pm}=\mp\frac{e\lambda^{2}[J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]}{2\left([J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]^{2}+2\lambda^{2}k^{2}\right)^{1/2}},

(S50)

and

\displaystyle m^{{\rm orb},~M}_{z,\pm}=\pm\frac{e\lambda^{2}[J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})+2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]}{2\left([J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})+2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]^{2}+2\lambda^{2}k^{2}\right)^{1/2}}.

(S51)

Finally, the spin magnetic moment around
\Gamma
and
M
is given by

\displaystyle m^{{\rm s},~\Gamma}_{z,\pm}=\mp\frac{\mu_{\rm B}g}{2}\frac{J_{1}(k^{2}_{x}-k^{2}_{y})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}}{\left([J_{1}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})-2J_{2}k_{x}k_{y}]^{2}+2\lambda^{2}k^{2}\right)^{1/2}},

J_{2}=0
, (b)
J_{1}=0
and
J_{2}\neq 0
and (c)
J_{1}=J_{2}\neq 0
.

VI
Hall conductivity integrals

We have the Dirac points at
\Gamma
and
M
, reflecting a Kramers-like degeneracy enforced by the
C_{4}K
symmetry. When the Fermi energy is close to these Dirac points, two small Fermi pockets emerge. In the vicinity of the Dirac points, we express the momentum in polar coordinates as
(k_{x},k_{y})=k(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)
.

The band dispersion of the lower band near the
\Gamma
point, as detailed in Eqs. (
S40
) and (
S41
), is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon(\bm{k})=-2t+\frac{tk^{2}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\lambda k\bigg[1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)k^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}+\dots\bigg],

(S54)

where
\tilde{J}(\varphi)=J_{1}\cos(2\varphi)-J_{2}\sin(2\varphi)
. The leading term in this expression describes an isotropic (circular) Fermi surface. We have anisotropies that are proportional to
J_{1,2}^{2}/\lambda^{2}
. Below, we calculate current contributions assuming that
J_{1},J_{2}\ll\lambda
, which corresponds to nearly-isotropic Fermi surfaces. In order to evaluate currents to the leading order in
J_{1,2}/\lambda
, we need

\displaystyle\Omega_{z}=-\frac{\tilde{J}(\varphi)}{2\sqrt{2}\lambda k}\bigg[1-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)k^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}+\dots\bigg],

(S55)

and

\displaystyle m^{\rm orb}_{z}=-\frac{e}{\hbar}\frac{\tilde{J}(\varphi)\lambda k}{2\sqrt{2}}\bigg[1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)k^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}+\dots\bigg].

(S56)

and

\displaystyle m^{\rm s}_{z}=-\mu_{\rm B}g\frac{\tilde{J}(\varphi)k}{2\sqrt{2}\lambda}\bigg[1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)k^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}+\dots\bigg].

(S57)

The Hall conductivities involve several integrals over the Brillouin zone, see Eqs. (
S30
), (
S31
), and (
S32
). We evaluate these integrals assuming that temperature is much smaller than all other energy scales, such as the Fermi energy, bandwidth, etc. In this limit, the equilibrium distribution function
f_{0}
can be viewed as a step-function in energy. After integration by parts, we may write
\partial_{\epsilon}f_{0}=-\delta[\epsilon(\bm{k})-\mu]
, where
\mu
is the chemical potential. Along these lines, each integral in the expressions for the Hall conductivities can be written in the following form:

I=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}k\,dk\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\,F(k,\varphi)\left(\frac{1}{|\partial_{k}\epsilon(k)|}\right)_{k=k_{F}}\delta(k-k_{F}),

(S58)

where
F(k,\varphi)
is some function in 2D polar coordinates,

\displaystyle|\partial_{k}\epsilon(k)

\displaystyle|_{k=k_{F}(\varphi)}=tk_{F}(\varphi)-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\lambda-\frac{3\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)k_{F}^{2}(\varphi)}{4\sqrt{2}\lambda}

(S59)

and the Fermi wave vector is given by

\displaystyle k_{F}(\varphi)\simeq(\mu+2t)\biggl[-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{v_{0}}\frac{\tilde{J}^{2}(\varphi)}{2\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu+2t}{\lambda}\right)^{2}\biggr].

(S60)

Here
v_{0}={d\epsilon_{0}}/dk
is the isotropic Fermi velocity and
\epsilon_{0}=-2t+{tk^{2}}/{2}-\sqrt{2}/{2}~\lambda k
.
Evaluating the integral over
k
, Eq. (
S58
) takes the form

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi~k_{F}~F(k_{F},\varphi)\left(\frac{1}{|\partial_{k}\epsilon(k)|}\right)_{k=k_{F}}.

(S61)

We now evaluate the conventional Hall conductivity, Berry curvature-induced Hall conductivity, and the magnetic moment contribution. For brevity, we provide explicit expressions only for the
\Gamma
pocket, to leading order in
J=J_{1,2}/\lambda
.
To study dependence on temperature, we assume that altermagnetic order sets in via a second-order phase transition at a critical temperature
T_{c}
. Following the standard Landau theory, we suppose that the altermagnetic order parameter is given by

\displaystyle J(T)=J_{0}\sqrt{1-\frac{T}{T_{c}}}\quad\text{for }T<T_{c},

(S62)

where
J_{0}
is a constant that depends on material parameters.
As
T
approaches
T_{c}
from below, the system transitions to a disordered state (with
C_{4}
and
K
symmetries) where the altermagnetic order parameter vanishes. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{c,\Gamma}=-\frac{\tau^{2}e^{3}t^{2}\epsilon_{\Gamma}^{2}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}\lambda^{2}}\bigg[1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{J^{2}(T)}{\lambda^{2}}\bigg],

(S63)

\sigma_{H}^{B,\Gamma}=\frac{\tau e^{3}}{32\pi\hbar\lambda^{2}}\frac{(\lambda^{2}-2t\epsilon_{\Gamma})^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+2t\epsilon_{\Gamma}}J(T),

(S64)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{H}^{m,\Gamma}=\frac{3\tau e^{3}\epsilon_{\Gamma}}{32\pi\hbar}\left[\frac{\lambda^{2}+4t\epsilon_{\Gamma}}{\lambda^{2}-2t\epsilon_{\Gamma}}+\frac{g\mu_{\rm B}\hbar}{e\lambda^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}+4t\epsilon_{\Gamma}}{\lambda^{2}-2t\epsilon_{\Gamma}}\right]J(T),

(S65)

where
\epsilon_{\Gamma}=|2t+\mu|
is the energy of the degenerate band at the
\Gamma
point. Contributions from the vicinity of the M point can be calculated on similar lines.
We find that the magnitude of
\sigma_{H}^{m}
is much weaker than
\sigma_{H}^{c}
and
\sigma_{H}^{B}
for any choice of plausible parameters. We thus focus on the latter two contributions and neglect
\sigma_{H}^{m}
.

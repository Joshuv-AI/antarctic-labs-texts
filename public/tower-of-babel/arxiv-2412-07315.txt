Equality and comparison of generalized quasiarithmetic means Thanks: The first author was supported by the K-134191 NKFIH Grant.
Zsolt Páles, Paweł Pasteczka

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to extend the definition of quasiarithmetic means by taking a strictly monotone generating function instead of a strictly monotone and continuous one. We establish the properties of such means and compare them to the analogous properties of standard quasiarithmetic means. The comparability and equality problems of generalized quasiarithmetic are also solved. We also provide an example of a mean which, depending on the underlying interval or on the number of variables, could be or could not be represented as a generalized quasiarithmetic mean.

Introduction

Let
I
denote a nonempty open subinterval of
\mathbb{R}
in this paper.
In what follows, for an
n

is defined by the following formula:

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(x):=f^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}\bigg)\qquad(x\in I^{n}).

To introduce the notion of the weighted quasiarithmetic mean, denote

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}:=\big\{\lambda\in[0,\infty)^{n}\,\big|\,\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}>0\big\}.

Then,

n

\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}:I^{n}\times\Lambda_{n}\to I
is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda):=f^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{1}f(x_{1})+\dots+\lambda_{n}f(x_{n})}{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}}\bigg)\qquad(x\in I^{n},\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}).

It is well-known that these means possess the mean value property, that is, the following inequalities hold:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\min(x)\leq A^{[n]}_{f}

\displaystyle(x)\leq\max(x)\qquad(x\in I^{n})\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\min\{x_{i}:\lambda_{i}>0\}\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(

\displaystyle x;\lambda)\leq\max\{x_{i}:\lambda_{i}>0\}\qquad(x\in I^{n},\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}).

2.
Basic properties of generalized quasiarithmetic means

For a locally bounded function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, define
f_{-},f_{+}:I\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle f_{-}(x):=\lim_{r\to 0+}\inf_{u\in(x-r,x+r)\cap I}f(u)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{+}(x):=\lim_{r\to 0+}\sup_{u\in(x-r,x+r)\cap I}f(u)\qquad(x\in I).

Then, one can easily see that
f_{-}
is the largest lower semicontinuous function below
f
and
f_{+}
is the smallest upper semicontinuous function above
f
, which we call the lower and upper semicontinuous envelopes of
f
, respectively.

If
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a strictly increasing continuous function, then its range
f(I)
is also an open interval and
f
is a homeomorphism between
I
and
f(I)
, i.e., the inverse of
f
is continuous, strictly increasing and maps
f(I)
onto
I
. Furthermore, we have that
f_{-}=f_{+}=f
.

In the more general case, when
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is a strictly increasing function, then
f:I\to f(I)
is a bijection and
f
is also locally bounded. Due to the opennes of the interval
I
, the function
f
has a left and a right limit at every point of
I
, moreover, we have that

\displaystyle f_{-}(x)=\lim_{u\to x-}f(u)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f_{+}(x):=\lim_{u\to x+}f(u)\qquad(x\in I).

One can also easily see that
f_{-},f_{+}:I\to\mathbb{R}
are strictly increasing functions and, for all elements
u<x<v
in
I
, we have that

\displaystyle f_{+}(u)<f_{-}(x)\leq f(x)\leq f_{+}(x)<f_{-}(v).

In the sequel, the set of points in
I
, where
f
is continuous, will be denoted by
C_{f}
. It is well-known that
C_{f}
is co-countable in
I
, i.e.,
I\setminus C_{f}
is countable. It is also clear that
x
belongs to
C_{f}
if and only if
f_{-}(x)=f(x)=f_{+}(x)
.
For a strictly decreasing function, similar properties can be established.

For a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, the smallest convex set containing
S
, which is identical to the smallest interval containing it, will be denoted by
\conv(S)
. For our definition of generalized quasiarithmetic means, we shall need the following lemma about the existence and properties of the generalized inverse of strictly increasing (but not necessarily continuous) functions.

Lemma 2.1
.

(​​
[
17
, Lemma 1]
)
Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing function. Then there exists a uniquely determined increasing function
f^{(-1)}:\conv(f(I))\to I
which is the left inverse of
f
, i.e., for all
x\in I
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle(f^{(-1)}\circ f)(x)=x.

Furthermore, we have the following assertions:

(i)

f^{(-1)}
is continuous;

(ii)

For all
y\in f(I)
,

\displaystyle(f\circ f^{(-1)})(y)=y;

(iii)

For all
y\in\conv(f(I))
,

\displaystyle(f_{-}\circ f^{(-1)})(y)\leq y\leq(f_{+}\circ f^{(-1)})(y);

(iv)

For all
x\in I
,

\displaystyle(f^{(-1)}\circ f_{-})(x)=(f^{(-1)}\circ f_{+})(x)=x,

equivalently,

\displaystyle(f_{-})^{(-1)}=(f_{+})^{(-1)}=f^{(-1)}.

We should note that assertion (iv) was not explicitly stated in
[
17
, Lemma 1]
, but it easily follows from (
2.1
) by taking left and right limits at
x
and using assertion (i).

The function
f^{(-1)}
described in the above lemma is called the
generalized inverse of the strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
. It is clear from assertions (ii) and (iii) that the restriction of
f^{(-1)}
to
f(I)
equals the inverse of
f
in the standard sense. Therefore,
f^{(-1)}
is the continuous and increasing extension of the inverse of
f
to the smallest interval containing the range of
f
.

As an easy consequence of this lemma, we have the following useful statement which will not require a proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing function and let
x\in I
and
u\in\conv(f(I))
. Then we have the following equivalences:

(i)

f^{(-1)}(u)=x
holds if and only if
u\in[f_{-}(x),f_{+}(x)]
.

(ii)

f^{(-1)}(u)<x
holds if and only if
u<f_{-}(x)
.

(iii)

f^{(-1)}(u)\leq x
holds if and only if
u\leq f_{+}(x)
.

(iv)

x<f^{(-1)}(u)
holds if and only if
f_{+}(x)<u
.

(v)

x\leq f^{(-1)}(u)
holds if and only if
f_{-}(x)\leq u
.

Given a strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic mean

A^{[n]}_{f}:I^{n}\to I
and the
n
-variable weighted generalized quasiarithmetic mean

\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}:I^{n}\times\Lambda_{n}\to I
are defined by the following formulas:

(2.2)

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(x):=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}\bigg)\qquad(x\in I^{n}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda):=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{1}f(x_{1})+\dots+\lambda_{n}f(x_{n})}{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}}\bigg)\qquad(x\in I^{n},\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}).

Clearly, for any
x\in I^{n}
, we have that
A^{[n]}_{f}(x)=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)
if
\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{n}
.
We also mention that, according to Example 4.5(3) of the paper
[
27
]
, the mean
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}
turns out to be a weighted semideviation mean generated by the semideviation function
D_{f}(x,t):=f(x)-f(t)
(
x,t\in I
). On the other hand, these means are the particular cases of the generalized Bajraktarević means which were introduced in the paper
[
17
]
.
The following result was established in this latter paper.

Proposition 2.3
.

(​​
[
17
, Theorem 2]
)
Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the functions
A^{[n]}_{f}:I^{n}\to I
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}:I^{n}\times\Lambda_{n}\to I
given by
(
2.2
)
and
(
2.3
)
are well-defined and they possess the mean value property, that is,
(
1.1
)
holds.

We should note that the second inequality was not explicitly stated in
[
17
]
, but it can be proved similarly as the first one.
Further properties of these means are obtained in the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have the following assertions:

(i)

The means
A^{[n]}_{f}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}
are symmetric functions, i.e., for all permutation
\pi
of the index set
\{1,\dots,n\}
, for all
x\in I^{n}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)};\lambda_{\pi(1)},\dots,\lambda_{\pi(n)}).

(ii)

The means
A^{[n]}_{f}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}
are increasing functions of their variables, i.e., for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, for all fixed
x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}\in I
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
, the maps

\displaystyle x_{i}\mapsto A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad x_{i}\mapsto\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

are increasing on
I
.

(iii)

The mean
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}
is a continuous function of its weights, i.e., for all fixed
x\in I^{n}
, the map

\displaystyle\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)

is continuous over
\Lambda_{n}
.

Proof.

Assertion (i) is direct consequence of the definition of the means
A^{[n]}_{f}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}
. Using that
f
and
f^{(-1)}
are increasing functions, assertion (ii) follows easily.
Applying the continuity of
f^{(-1)}
, we can also see that assertion (iii) is also valid.
∎

The following lemma will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing,
x\in I
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Then we have the following assertions:

(i)

f
is lower semicontinuous at
x
if and only if, for all
y\in I
with
y<x
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{n-1\text{ times}},y)<x.

(ii)

f
is upper semicontinuous at
x
if and only if, for all
y\in I
with
y>x
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{n-1\text{ times}},y)>x.

(iii)

f
is continuous at
x
if and only if, for all
y\in I
with
y\neq x

(2.6)

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{n-1\text{ times}},y)\neq x.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is lower semicontinuous at
x
. Then, for all
y<x
in
I
, we have that
f(y)<f_{-}(x)=f(x)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)f(x)+f(y)}{n}<f(x)=f_{-}(x),

which, in view of assertion (ii) of Lemma
2.2
, implies the inequality (
2.4
).

Conversely, assume that the inequality (
2.4
) holds for all
y<x
in
I
. Then, again by assertion (ii) of Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)f(x)+f(y)}{n}<f_{-}(x).

Upon taking the limit as
y
tends to
x
from the left, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)f(x)+f_{-}(x)}{n}\leq f_{-}(x).

After rearranging this inequality, we conclude that
f(x)\leq f_{-}(x)
, which shows that
f
is lower semicontinuous at the point
x
.

The proof of assertion (ii) is analogous. Assertion (iii) is an easy consequence of assertions (i) and (ii).
∎

Now we show several equivalent conditions equivalent to the continuity of the generating function
f
. Let us emphasize that conditions (iv), (vi), and (viii) of the theorem below correspond to Kolmogorov’s conditions (ii), (iii), and (iv), respectively (see Theorem
C
). We also mention that the proof uses Lemma
2.5
and also Proposition
2.7
, Proposition
3.3
, Corollary
3.4
which will be established in Section 3.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing function. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The function
f
is continuous on
I
.

(ii)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and a continuous, strictly increasing function
g\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
A_{f}^{[n]}=A^{[n]}_{g}
holds on
I^{n}
(i.e.,
A_{f}^{[n]}
is a standard quasiarithmetic mean on
I^{n}
).

(iii)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and a continuous, strictly increasing function
g\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
such that either
A^{[n]}_{f}\leq A^{[n]}_{g}
holds on
I^{n}
or
A^{[n]}_{g}\leq A^{[n]}_{f}
holds on
I^{n}
.

(iv)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that
A^{[n]}_{f}
is continuous.

(v)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that
A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}\leq A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
holds on
I^{n}
.

(vi)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that
A^{[n]}_{f}
is strictly increasing.

(vii)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that
A^{[n]}_{f}
is strict, that is

\displaystyle\min(x)<A^{[n]}_{f}(x)<\max(x)\qquad\text{ for every non-diagonal vector }x\in I^{n}.

(viii)

(A_{f}^{[n]})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is associative, that is, the sequence of means
M^{[n]}:=A_{f}^{[n]}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies property (iv) in Theorem
C
.

Moreover, if any of the above conditions holds, then
(iii)–(vii)
are valid for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

First note that if
f
is continuous, then all the other listed conditions are satisfied by the characterization theorem of Kolmogorov.

Obviously, (ii) implies (iii). On the other hand, if (iii) holds, then, according to statement (iii) of Proposition
3.3
, we get that
C_{f}=C_{g}=I
, and hence
f
has to be continuous, i.e., condition (i) follows. Thus, we have proved the equivalence of conditions (i), (ii), and (iii).

If (iv) holds, then, by Proposition
2.7
, we have that
A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}=A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}=A^{[n]}_{f}
, therefore, condition (v) is satisfied. On the other hand, if (v) is valid, then, according to Corollary
3.4
, it follows that
f
is continuous. This shows the equivalence of conditions (i), (iv), and (v).

It is easy to see that (vi) implies (vii). If (vii) is valid, i.e.,
the mean
A^{[n]}_{f}
is strict, then, according to assertion (iii) of Lemma
2.5
, it follows that
f
is continuous on
I
. Thus, we have verified the equivalence of conditions (i), (vi), and (vii).

Finally, we show that condition (viii) implies condition (vii).
To the contrary, assume that (viii) holds true but (vii) is not valid. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and a non-diagonal vector
x\in I^{n}
such that either

A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=\max(x)
or
A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=\min(x)
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=\max(x)
holds and set
a:=\min(x)
,
b:=\max(x)
. Finally, for all
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the vector

\displaystyle w^{(m,k)}:=(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{m\text{ times}},\underbrace{b,\dots,b}_{k\text{ times}}).

Since
A_{f}^{[n]}
is monotone,
A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=b
yields
A_{f}^{[n]}(w^{(1,n-1)})=b
. Now, we are going to show by induction on
m
that
A_{f}^{[m+n-1]}(w^{(m,n-1)})=b
holds for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We have seen this property for
m=1
. Assume now that it holds for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by using the associativity of
A_{f}
twice, we get

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[n+m]}(w^{(m+1,n-1)})

\displaystyle=A_{f}^{[n+m]}(a,w^{(m,n-1)})

\displaystyle=A_{f}^{[n+m]}\Big(a,A_{f}^{[n+m-1]}\big(w^{(m,n-1)}\big),\dots,A_{f}^{[n+m-1]}\big(w^{(m,n-1)}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=A_{f}^{[n+m]}\big(w^{(1,n+m-1)}\big)=A_{f}^{[n+m]}\big(w^{(1,n-1)},w^{(0,m)}\big)

\displaystyle=A_{f}^{[n+m]}\Big(A_{f}^{[n]}(w^{(1,n-1)}),\dots,A_{f}^{[n]}(w^{(1,n-1)}),w^{(0,m)}\Big)

\displaystyle=A_{f}^{[n+m]}\big(w^{(0,n)},w^{(0,m)}\big)=b,

which completes the induction.
Therefore we have
A_{f}^{[m+n-1]}(w^{(m,n-1)})=b
for all
m\geq 1
. On the other hand, due to the continuity of
f^{(-1)}
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}A_{f}^{[m+n-1]}(w^{(m,n-1)})

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}f^{(-1)}\Big(\frac{mf(a)+(n-1)f(b)}{m+n-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=f^{(-1)}\Big(\lim_{m\to\infty}\big(\tfrac{m}{m+n-1}f(a)+\tfrac{n-1}{m+n-1}f(b)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=f^{(-1)}\big(f(a)\big)=a,

which leads to the contradiction
b=a
.
This completes the proof of the final implication.
∎

The content of the above theorem can be summarized as follows: If a generalized quasiarithmetic mean is not equal (or not comparable) to a standard quasiarithmetic mean, then it is not continuous, not strictly increasing (but increasing), not a strict mean and it is also not associative.

In the next proposition, we establish the properties of the generalized quasiarithmetic mean generated by the lower and upper semicontinuous envelopes of
f
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have the following assertions:

(i)

For
x\in I^{n}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to x-}A^{[n]}_{f}(u)=A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(x)\leq A^{[n]}_{f}(x)\leq A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(x)=\lim_{u\to x+}A^{[n]}_{f}(u)\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to x-}\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(u;\lambda)=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(x;\lambda)\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(x;\lambda)=\lim_{u\to x+}\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(u;\lambda).

(ii)

The means
A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
are lower semicontinuous and the means
A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
are upper semicontinuous on their domains.

(iii)

The means
A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
are subassociative, i.e., for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
x\in I^{n}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
with
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}>0
,

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(y,\dots,y,x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n})\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(z,\dots,z,x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

where
y:=A^{[k]}_{f_{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
z:=\widetilde{A}^{[k]}_{f_{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
.

(iv)

The means
A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
are superassociative, i.e., for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
x\in I^{n}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
with
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}>0
,

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(y,\dots,y,x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n})\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}(z,\dots,z,x_{k+1},\dots,x_{n};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})

where
y:=A^{[k]}_{f_{+}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
and
z:=\widetilde{A}^{[k]}_{f_{+}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
.

Proof.

To prove assertion (i) for the weighted mean, let
x\in I^{n}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
and denote
\lambda_{0}:=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
.
Then, using assertions (i) and (iv) of Lemma
2.1
, we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to x-}\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(u;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\to x-}f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f(u_{i})\bigg)=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\lim_{u_{i}\to x_{i}-}f(u_{i})\bigg)

\displaystyle=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i})\bigg)=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}(x;\lambda),

which proves the first equality in the second chain of inequalities-equalities of assertion (i). The verification of the last equality is analogous. The two inequalities in this chain follow directly from the inequalities
f_{-}\leq f\leq f_{+}
and the increasingness of
f^{(-1)}
.
By taking
\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=1
in the second chain, we obtain the first one.

By the lower semicontinuity of the function
f_{-}
, we have that the maps

\displaystyle x\mapsto\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{-}(x_{i})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(x;\lambda)\mapsto\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i})

are lower semicontinuous on
I^{n}
and on
I^{n}\times\Lambda_{n}
, respectively. Therefore, the composition of these maps with
f^{(-1)}
is also lower semicontinuous on the respective domains. This proves that assertion (ii) is valid.

To prove the subassociativity of the weighted mean
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
, let
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
x\in I^{n}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
with
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}>0
and let
z:=\widetilde{A}^{[k]}_{f_{-}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k};\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
.
Then, using assertion (iii) of Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{-}(z)=f_{-}\circ f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i})\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k})f_{-}(z)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f_{-}(x_{i}).

Substituting the left and right hand sides into
f^{(-1)}
, it follows that the second inequality of assertion (iii) is valid, which proves the subassociativity of
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
. By taking
\lambda_{1}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=1
, the subassociativity of
A^{[n]}_{f_{-}}
follows. Thus the proof of assertion (iii) is complete.

The proof of the superassociativity of
A^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
and
\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f_{+}}
is completely analogous and therefore, it is omitted.
∎

The last result of this section shows that an
n
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic mean completely determines the corresponding
m
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic mean whenever
m<n
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing function and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m<n
. Then, for all
x\in I^{m}
,

\displaystyle A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=\inf\{z\in I\colon A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},z,\dots,z)<z\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{z\in I\colon A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},z,\dots,z)>z\}.

Proof.

We are going to show only the first equality as the second one is analogous. Let
x\in I^{m}
be fixed and define the set
M(x)
by

\displaystyle M(x):=\{z\in I\colon A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},z,\dots,z)<z\}.

We need to show the equality

\displaystyle A^{[m]}_{f}(x)=\inf M(x).

Let
z\in M(x)
be arbitrary. Then, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{m})+(n-m)f(z)}{n}<f_{-}(z).

Therefore, using also the inequality
f_{-}(z)\leq f(z)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{m})}{m}<\frac{nf_{-}(z)-(n-m)f(z)}{m}\leq f_{-}(z),

which, again by Lemma
2.2
, implies that
A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})<z
.
This proves the inequality
A^{[m]}_{f}(x)\leq\inf M(x)
.

To show the reverse inequality, take
z\in C_{f}\cap(A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),\infty\big)
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle f(z)>\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{m})}{m},

which yields

\displaystyle f(z)>\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{m})+(n-m)f(z)}{n}.

Whence, in view of Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
A^{[n]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},z,\dots,z)<z
, and thus
z\in M(x)
. Therefore
M(x)\supseteq C_{f}\cap(A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),\infty\big)
and whence, due to the density of
C_{f}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\inf M(x)\leq\inf C_{f}\cap(A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),\infty\big)=A^{[m]}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),

which completes the proof of the equality
A^{[m]}_{f}(x)=\inf M(x)
.
∎

3.
Comparison of generalized quasiarithmetic means

In the result below we establish that, whenever the comparison inequality holds between two
n
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic means and
m<n
, then it holds between the corresponding
m
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic means, as well.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m<n
. If, for all
x\in I^{n}
, the inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[n]}(x)\leq A_{g}^{[n]}(x)

holds, then, for all
y\in I^{m}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[m]}(y)\leq A_{g}^{[m]}(y).

Proof.

Fix
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in I^{m}
arbitrarily. Then, by (
3.1
), for all
z\in I
, we have

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z,\dots,z)\leq A^{[n]}_{g}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z,\dots,z).

Whence it follows that

\displaystyle\{z\in I\colon A^{[n]}_{f}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z,\dots,z)<z\}\supseteq\{z\in I\colon A^{[n]}_{g}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z,\dots,z)<z\}.

Upon taking infimum side-by-side, in view of Theorem
2.8
, we obtain (
3.2
).
∎

In the subsequent result, we establish a necessary condition for the validity of the inequality (
3.1
). Recall that
\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor
denotes the lower integer part of
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions,
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, and assume that
(
3.1
)
holds for all
x\in I^{n}
. Then, for all
m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
x,y\in I
with
x<y
and
t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\lfloor m\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor m\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
m\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
and
t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}
. Contrary to the statement, suppose that (
3.3
) is not valid, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\lfloor m\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}\right\rfloor<\left\lfloor m\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}\right\rfloor=:k.

Then, obviously,
k\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. This inequality implies that

\displaystyle m\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}<k\leq m\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}.

Rearranging these inequalities, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{kg(x)+(m-k)g(y)}{m}<g(t)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f(t)\leq\frac{kf(x)+(m-k)f(y)}{m}.

Using that
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
and applying Lemma
2.2
to these inequalities, we conclude that

\displaystyle A^{[m]}_{g}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{k\text{ times}},\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{m-k\text{ times}})<t\leq A^{[m]}_{f}(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{k\text{ times}},\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{m-k\text{ times}}),

which shows that the inequality (
3.2
) is not valid for
\big(\underbrace{x,\dots,x}_{k\text{ times}},\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{m-k\text{ times}}\big)\in I^{m}
. Therefore, according to Theorem
3.1
, the inequality (
3.1
) is also not valid for some vector in
I^{n}
. This contradiction proves that (
3.3
) must be valid on the indicated domain.
∎

Observe that, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, the inequality
\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor
is equivalent to
\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor\leq v
, therefore the necessary condition (
3.3
) for the validity of the comparison inequality (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\max_{m\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\frac{1}{m}\left\lfloor m\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}\quad(x,y\in I,\,x<y,\,t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

The next result shows that if two generalized quasiarithmetic means are comparable in the weakest sense (i.e., the corresponding
n
-variable means are comparable), then their generating functions have the same discontinuity points.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions, and let
t\in I
. Assume that
(
3.1
)
holds for all
x\in I^{n}
. Then, we have the following assertions:

(i)

If
g
is lower semicontinuous at
t
, then so is
f
.

(ii)

If
f
is upper semicontinuous at
t
, then so is
g
.

(iii)

The function
f
is continuous at
t
if and only if
g
is continuous at
t
.

Proof.

Assume that
f
is not lower semicontinuous at
t
. Then, according to statement (i) of Lemma
2.5
, there exists a value
y\in I
with
y<t
such that

\displaystyle t\leq A_{f}^{[n]}(t,\dots,t,y)

Using the inequality (
3.1
) for the vector
x=(t,\dots,t,y)\in I^{n}
, we get
that

\displaystyle t\leq A_{g}^{[n]}(t,\dots,t,y).

Therefore, again by statement (i) of Lemma
2.5
, it follows that
g
is not lower semicontinuous at
t
as well. This proves assertion (i).

The similar proof of assertion (ii) is based on statement (ii) of Lemma
2.5
.

Upon taking right limit in (
3.1
), for all
x\in I^{n}
, we get that

(3.4)

\displaystyle A_{f_{+}}^{[n]}(x)=\lim_{u\to x+}A_{f}^{[n]}(u)\leq\lim_{u\to x+}A_{g}^{[n]}(u)=A_{g_{+}}^{[n]}(x).

To prove assertion (iii), we show first that
C_{g}\subseteq C_{f}
. Let
t\in C_{g}
. Then
g_{+}
is lower semicontinuous at
t
. In view of the inequality (
3.4
) and assertion (i) (applied for
(f_{+},g_{+})
instead of
(f,g)
), it follows that
f_{+}
is also lower semicontinuous at
t
and hence,
f
is continuous at
t
. This proves that
C_{g}\subseteq C_{f}
.

The proof of the reverse inclusion
C_{f}\subseteq C_{g}
is based on the inequality

\displaystyle A_{f_{-}}^{[n]}(x)=\lim_{u\to x-}A_{f}^{[n]}(u)\leq\lim_{u\to x-}A_{g}^{[n]}(u)=A_{g_{-}}^{[n]}(x)

and assertion (ii), otherwise it is completely similar to the proof of
C_{g}\subseteq C_{f}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions. Assume that

(3.5)

\displaystyle A_{f_{+}}^{[n]}(x)\leq A_{g_{-}}^{[n]}(x)

holds for all
x\in I^{n}
. Then,
f
and
g
are continuous on
I
and the means in the above inequality are quasiarithmetic in the standard sense.

Proof.

We apply the previous proposition to the pair of functions
(f_{+},g_{-})
. Since
g_{-}
is lower semicontinuous everywhere in
I
, the inequality (
3.5
) and assertion (i) of this proposition imply that
f_{+}
has to be lower semicontinuous as well. Therefore,
f
is continuous everywhere.

On the other hand,
f_{+}
is upper semicontinuous everywhere, thus the inequality (
3.5
) and assertion (ii) of Proposition
3.3
yield that
g_{-}
is upper semicontinuous and hence
g
is continuous.
∎

The following theorem is our main result about the comparison of generalized quasiarithmetic means. It is parallel to the classical comparison theorem stated in Theorem
B
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in I^{n}
,

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(x)\leq A^{[n]}_{g}(x).

(ii)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in I^{n}
, and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{g}(x;\lambda).

(iii)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that, for all
x\in I^{n}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)\leq\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{g}(x;\lambda).

(iv)

For all
x,y\in I
with
x<y
and
t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

\displaystyle\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}\leq\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}.

(v)

There exists a positive function
p:(C_{f}\cap C_{g})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that, for all
x\in I
and
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

\displaystyle f(x)-f(t)\leq p(t)(g(x)-g(t)).

(vi)

There exists an increasing convex homeomorphism
\varphi:\conv(f(I))\to\conv(g(I))
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(f(x))\leq g(x)\qquad(x\in I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\varphi(f(t))=g(t)\qquad(t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

(vii)

There exists an increasing concave homeomorphism
\psi:\conv(g(I))\to\conv(f(I))
such that

\displaystyle f(x)\leq\psi(g(x))\qquad(x\in I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f(t)=\psi(g(t))\qquad(t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

Proof.

The proof of the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is standard. Assuming assertion (i), first one should prove that the inequality in (ii) holds for all weight vector
\lambda
with rational coordinates, i.e., for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{n}
. Then, using the continuity of the weighted generalized quasiarithmetic means in their weights (cf. assertion (iii) of Proposition
2.4
), this inequality can be verified for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is obvious.

To verify the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv), assume that assertion (iii) is valid for some
n\geq 2
. Let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then applying the inequality in (iii) to the vector

(x,y,\dots,y)\in I^{n}
and to the weight vector
(\lambda,1-\lambda,0,\dots,0)\in\Lambda_{n}
, we get that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[2]}_{f}(x,y;\lambda,1-\lambda)\leq\widetilde{A}^{[2]}_{g}(x,y;\lambda,1-\lambda).

Let
t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}
be arbitrary. We prove the inequality asserted in (iv) by contradiction. Both sides of this inequality belong to the interval
[0,1]
. If this inequality is not true, then there exist
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}<\lambda<\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}.

The left and the right hand side inequalities imply that

\displaystyle\lambda g(x)+(1-\lambda)g(y)<g(t)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)>f(t),

respectively. Using assertions (ii) and (iv) of Lemma
2.2
and that
f
and
g
are continuous at
t
, it follows that

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[2]}_{g}(x,y;\lambda,1-\lambda)<t<\widetilde{A}^{[2]}_{f}(x,y;\lambda,1-\lambda).

This inequality contradicts (
3.6
). This contradiction validates the inequality stated in assertion (iv).

Now assume that assertion (iv) holds. Let
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
be fixed and let
x<t<y
be arbitrary elements of
I
. Observe that the inequality in assertion (iv) can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)-f(x)}{g(t)-g(x)}\geq\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{g(y)-g(t)}.

Therefore, for every
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

\displaystyle p(t):=\inf_{x\in I,\,x<t}\frac{f(t)-f(x)}{g(t)-g(x)}\geq\sup_{y\in I,\,y>t}\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{g(y)-g(t)}.

Using the definition of the function
p:C_{f}\cap C_{g}\to(0,\infty)
so defined, for
x,y\in I
with
x<t<y
, we get

\displaystyle f(t)-f(x)\geq p(t)(g(t)-g(x))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f(y)-f(t)\leq p(t)(g(y)-g(t)).

It immediately follows from these inequalities that assertion (v) holds true.

Next, we verify the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(i).
Assume assertion (v), and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in I^{n}
be fixed. Applying the inequality in assertion (v) with
x:=x_{i}
and the adding up the inequalities so obtained, for all
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x_{i})-f(t))\leq p(t)\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g(x_{i})-g(t)).

Choose
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
satisfying the inequality
A^{[n]}_{g}(x)<t
arbitrarily. Then, by assertion (ii) of Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{g(x_{1})+\dots+g(x_{n})}{n}<g(t),

whence it follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g(x_{i})-g(t))<0
. According to the previous inequality, this yields that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x_{i})-f(t))<0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}<f(t).

Using that
f
is continuous at
t
, by assertion (ii) of Lemma
2.2
, we can conclude that
A^{[n]}_{f}(x)<t
. Upon taking the right limit
t\downarrow A_{g}^{[n]}(x)
in the dense set
C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, we can see that the inequality stated in assertion (i) is valid.

So far, we have established the equivalence of assertions (i)–(v).
Finally, we show that assertions (v), (vi) and (vii) are also equivalent.

Assume first that (v) holds. For fixed
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, define the function
\varphi_{t}:\conv(f(I))\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(u)=\frac{u-f(t)}{p(t)}+g(t)\qquad(u\in\conv(f(I))).

Then
\varphi_{t}
is a strictly increasing affine function and, by the inequality of assertion (v), we have that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(f(x))

\displaystyle\leq g(x)\qquad(x\in I,\,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g})

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}(f(t))

\displaystyle=g(t)\qquad(t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

Define the extended real-valued function
\varphi:\conv(f(I))\to(-\infty,\infty]
by

\displaystyle\varphi(u):=\sup_{t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}}\varphi_{t}(u)\qquad(u\in\conv(f(I))).

Upon taking the supremum in the inequality in (
3.7
), we obtain that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\varphi(f(x))\leq g(x)\qquad(x\in I).

The equality in (
3.7
) implies that

\displaystyle g(t)=\varphi_{t}(f(t))\leq\varphi(f(t))\leq g(t)\qquad(t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}),

which implies that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varphi(f(t))=g(t)\qquad(t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

For all
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, we have that
\varphi_{t}
is strictly increasing, whence it follows that
\varphi
is increasing. If
u\in\conv(f(I))
, then there exists
x\in I
such that
u\leq f(x)
.
Then, using the inequality in (
3.8
), we get that
\varphi(u)\leq\varphi(f(x))\leq g(x)
, which shows that
\varphi
is in fact real-valued. Since
\varphi
is the supremum of affine functions, it follows that
\varphi
is convex, which implies that it is also continuous.

We now are going to point out that
\varphi
is not only increasing but it is strictly increasing. If this were not be the case, then, using the convexity of
\varphi
, it follows that there exists
u\in\conv(f(I))
such that
\varphi
is constant on
J:=\conv(f(I))\cap(-\infty,u]
and
\varphi
is strictly increasing on
\conv(f(I))\cap(u,\infty)
. Then, there exists a point
x\in I
such that
f(x)\leq u
. Let
s,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
with
s<t\leq x
. Then,
f(s)<f(t)\leq u
, thus
f(s),f(t)\in J
. Applying that
\varphi
is constant on
J
, it follows that
\varphi(f(s))=\varphi(f(t))
. On the other hand, in view of the equality (
3.9
), the strict increasingness of
g
implies that

\displaystyle\varphi(f(s))=g(s)<g(t)=\varphi(f(t)).

The contradiction so obtained shows that
\varphi
must be strictly increasing.

To complete the proof of the implication (v)
\Rightarrow
(vi), we need to show that
\varphi
is a homeomorphism between the open intervals
\conv(f(I))
and
\conv(g(I))
. First we establish the inclusion
\varphi(\conv(f(I)))\subseteq\conv(g(I))
. Let
u\in\conv(f(I))
. Then there exist
x,y\in I
such that

f(x)\leq u\leq f(y)
, and there exist
s,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
such that
s\leq x
and
y\leq t
. These choices, using (
3.9
), imply that

\displaystyle g(s)=\varphi(f(s))\leq\varphi(f(x))\leq\varphi(u)\leq\varphi(f(y))\leq\varphi(f(t))=g(t).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varphi(u)\in[g(s),g(t)]\subseteq\conv(g(I)).

To show that the reverse inclusion is also valid, let
v\in\conv(g(I))
. Then there exist
x,y\in I
such that

g(x)\leq v\leq g(y)
, and there exist
s,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
such that
s\leq x
and
y\leq t
. Now, using (
3.9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle f(s)=\varphi^{-1}(g(s))\leq\varphi^{-1}(g(x))\leq\varphi^{-1}(v)\leq\varphi^{-1}(g(y))\leq\varphi^{-1}(g(t))=f(t).

Therefore,

\displaystyle v\in[\varphi(f(s)),\varphi(f(t))]=\varphi[f(s),f(t)]\subseteq\varphi(\conv(f(I))).

We now prove that assertion (vi) implies (v). Applying that
\varphi
is convex on the interval
\conv(f(I))
. By well-known properties of convex functions, for every
u\in\conv(f(I))
, we can find a real number
q(u)
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(u)+q(u)(v-u)\leq\varphi(v)\qquad(v\in\conv(f(I)))

In particular, for all
x\in I
and
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, with
u:=f(t)
and
v:=f(x)
, this inequality implies that

\displaystyle\varphi(f(t))+q(f(t))(f(x)-f(t))\leq\varphi(f(x))\qquad(x\in I,\,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

Using the inequality and the equality stated in (vi) (i.e., the inequality (
3.8
) and the equality (
3.9
)), it follows that

\displaystyle g(t)+q(f(t))(f(x)-f(t))\leq g(x)\qquad(x\in I,\,t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}).

This shows that assertion (v) is validated by the function
p:=\frac{1}{q\circ f}
.

To see that assertions (vi) and (vii) are equivalent, observe that if (vi) holds with an increasing homeomorphism
\varphi
, then (vii) is valid with
\psi:=\varphi^{-1}
(which is also an increasing homeomorphism). The reverse implication is analogous.
∎

4.
Equality of generalized quasiarithmetic means

In this section we show two results concerning equalities of generalized quasiarithmetic means. Both of them are obtained by using a corresponding result for the inequalities proved in the previous section and the easy-to-see fact that equality can be seen as two combined inequalities.

First result states that, whenever the equality holds between two
n
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic means and
m<n
, then it holds between the corresponding
m
-variable generalized quasiarithmetic means, as well. It is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions and let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m<n
. If, for all
x\in I^{n}
, the equality

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=A_{g}^{[n]}(x)

holds, then, for all
y\in I^{m}
,

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[m]}(y)=A_{g}^{[m]}(y).

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.5
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly increasing functions. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in I^{n}
,

\displaystyle A^{[n]}_{f}(x)=A^{[n]}_{g}(x).

(ii)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in I^{n}
, and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{g}(x;\lambda).

(iii)

There exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
such that, for all
x\in I^{n}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{f}(x;\lambda)=\widetilde{A}^{[n]}_{g}(x;\lambda).

(iv)

For all
x,y
in
I
with
x<y
and
t\in(x,y)\cap C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

\displaystyle\frac{f(y)-f(t)}{f(y)-f(x)}=\frac{g(y)-g(t)}{g(y)-g(x)}.

(v)

There exist two constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta
such that, for all
x\in I
,

\displaystyle f(x)=\alpha g(x)+\beta.

Proof.

The equivalence of assertions (i)–(iv) directly follows from the equivalence of assertions (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.5
.

Assume now that assertion (i) is valid. Then, applying that assertion (i) implies assertion (v) in Theorem
3.5
twice, we get that there exist two positive functions
p,q:(C_{f}\cap C_{g})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that, for all
x\in I
and
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle f(x)-f(t)\leq p(t)(g(x)-g(t))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad g(x)-g(t)\leq q(t)(f(x)-f(t)).

Therefore, for all
x\in I
and
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(t)}(g(x)-g(t))\leq p(t)(g(x)-g(t)).

Choosing first
x
to be smaller than
t
and then to be bigger than
t
, it follows that
\frac{1}{q(t)}=p(t)
for all
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
. Thus, the two inequalities in (
4.1
) imply, for all
x\in I
and
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
, that

\displaystyle f(x)-f(t)=p(t)(g(x)-g(t))

Let
t\in C_{f}\cap C_{g}
be a fixed element and define
\alpha:=p(t)
and
\beta:=f(t)-p(t)g(t)
. Then the above equality shows that assertion (v) is valid.

Conversely, assume that assertion (v) holds for some real constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta
. Then assertion (iv) follows immediately.
∎

5.
An example and concluding remarks

Define, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }\min(x)\leq 0\leq\max(x),\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}}{n}&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similar constructions of means were introduced in the papers
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
. In what follows, we investigate the representability of the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
as a (generalized) quasiarithmetic mean on the subinterval
I
. It is obvious that if
0\not\in I
, then
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
restricted to
I^{n}
is equal to the
n
-variable arithmetic mean. Therefore, the interesting case is when
0\in I
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
0\in I
. Then, we have the following assertions:

(i)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
restricted to
I^{n}
is not (standard) quasiarithmetic mean on
I^{n}
, i.e., there is no strictly increasing and continuous function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}(x)=A_{f}^{[n]}(x)\qquad(x\in I^{n})

is valid.

(ii)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
restricted to
I^{n}
is a generalized quasiarithmetic mean, that is, there exists a strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(
5.1
)
holds, if and only if
I
is bounded.

(iii)

There is no strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the equality
(
5.1
)
holds.

Proof.

To show the first assertion it is sufficient to observe that all (standard) quasiarithmetic means are strictly increasing (as stated in assertion (iii) of Theorem
C
) while the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
does not possess this property if
n\geq 2
.

Now we proceed to the proof of assertion (ii).
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
be fixed. First assume that
I
is bounded and let
a<0<b
denote the endpoints of
I
. Define
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
by

(5.2)

\displaystyle f(x):=\begin{cases}-\frac{x}{an}-1&\mbox{if }x\in[a,0)\\
0&\mbox{if }x=0,\\
\frac{x}{bn}+1&\mbox{if }x\in(0,b]\\
\end{cases}\qquad(x\in I).

It is easy to see that
f
is strictly increasing.
For the generalized inverse of
f
(restricted to
(a,b)
), we obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle f^{(-1)}(u)=\begin{cases}-an(u+1)&\mbox{if }u\in\big(-\frac{n+1}{n},-1\big),\\
0&\mbox{if }u\in[-1,1],\\
bn(u-1)&\mbox{if }u\in\big(1,\frac{n+1}{n}\big).\end{cases}

We are going to show that (
5.1
) holds for all
x\in I^{n}
. Let
x\in I^{n}
be fixed.

Observe that
f
is affine on the open subintervals
(a,0)
and
(0,b)
and hence, in these subintervals
f
generates the arithmetic mean. Therefore, if either
\max(x)<0
or
0<\min(x)
, then (
5.1
) is valid.

It remains to show that (
5.1
) is satisfied in the case
\min(x)\leq 0\leq\max(x)
, that is,
A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=0
. Equivalently, in view of formula (
5.3
), we have to prove that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}\in[-1,1].

Due to the inequality
\min(x)\leq 0
, for some
i
we have that
x_{i}\leq 0
, and
x_{j}\leq b
for all
j\neq i
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}\leq\frac{f(0)+(n-1)f(b)}{n}=\frac{(n-1)\frac{n+1}{n}}{n}=\frac{n^{2}-1}{n^{2}}<1.

A similar argument shows that

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{1})+\dots+f(x_{n})}{n}\geq\frac{f(0)+(n-1)f(a)}{n}=\frac{-(n-1)\frac{n+1}{n}}{n}=\frac{1-n^{2}}{n^{2}}>-1.

Thus we have proved that (
5.4
) is valid and hence
A_{f}^{[n]}(x)=0
holds, which completes the proof of (
5.1
) in the case
\min(x)\leq 0\leq\max(x)
. Consequently,
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}
restricted to
I^{n}
is a generalized quasiarithmetic mean provided that
I
is bounded.

To complete the proof of assertion (ii) assume now that
I
is unbounded. To the contrary of the statement, assume that there exists a strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that (
5.1
) is valid for some
n\geq 2
. Consider now the subcase when
\sup I=\infty
.
Then,
A_{f}^{[n]}
restricted to the interval
(0,\infty)^{n}
is equal to the arithmetic mean, which, as a quasiarithmetic mean is generated by an affine function. Therefore, according to assertion (v) of Corollary
4.2
below, there exist two real constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta
such that
f(t)=\alpha t+\beta
for all
t>0
. Then
\beta=f_{+}(0)\geq f(0)
, and one can get that
f^{(-1)}(u)=\frac{1}{\alpha}(u-\beta)>0
holds for all
u>\beta
.

On one hand, for arbitrary
t>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}(0,\dots,0,t)=0.

On the other hand, for
t>\frac{(n-1)(\beta-f(0))}{\alpha}\geq 0
, we have that
\frac{(n-1)f(0)+\alpha t+\beta}{n}>\beta
. Therefore

\displaystyle A_{f}^{[n]}(0,\dots,0,t)=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{(n-1)f(0)+f(t)}{n}\bigg)=f^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{(n-1)f(0)+\alpha t+\beta}{n}\bigg)>0,

which shows that (
5.1
) cannot hold at
x=(0,\dots,0,t)
with
t>\frac{(n-1)(\beta-f(0))}{\alpha}
.

To verify the last assertion, assume that (
5.1
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with some strictly increasing function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
. Then, according to assertion (iii) of Proposition
2.4
, for all
x,y\in I
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{f}^{[n]}\big(x,\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{(n-1)\text{ times}}\big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{A}_{f}^{[2]}\big(x,y;\tfrac{1}{n},1-\tfrac{1}{n}\big)=\widetilde{A}_{f}^{[2]}\big(x,y;0,1\big)=y,

while, for all
x<0<y
and
n\geq 2
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}\big(x,\underbrace{y,\dots,y}_{(n-1)\text{ times}}\big)=0.

This equality causes a contradiction in (
5.1
) for large
n
.
∎

Concluding remarks

It this section we would like to emphasize few important remarks concerning generalized quasiarithmetic means (which we will call here briefy g.q.a. means):

(1)

G.q.a. means are the generalizations of quasiarithmetic means, however they also form a subfamily of semideviation means introduced by Páles
[
25
]
and developed in Páles–Pasteczka
[
27
]
. In particular, all results concerning this family are also applicable to g.q.a. means.

(2)

Each Kolmogorov’s axiom expressed in Theorem
C
is either valid for g.q.a. mean (reflexivity and symmetry) or they imply that a g.q.a. mean is a quasiarithmetic mean (such as continuity, strict increasingness, associativity).

(3)

An
n
-variable g.q.a. mean completely determines the corresponding
m
-variable mean whenever
m<n
, however the converse of this statement is not valid.

(4)

Every g.q.a. mean possesses a lower semicontinuous and upper semicontinuous envelop, which are also g.q.a. means.

(5)

Except a few results, weighted g.q.a. means are (intentionally) a bit aside from the main plot of this paper. Since the construction of weighted g.q.a. means follows the general principle introduced in
[
26
]
, this family can be reconstructed using the nonweighted g.q.a. means which imply their properties in a natural manner.

(6)

Due to Theorem
3.5
part (vi), we know that the comparability (for vectors of arbitrary lengths) of two g.q.a. means is localizable (i.e. they are comparable if, and only if, they are comparable for arguments from a neighbourhood of the diagonal). On the other hand, this is not the case when we restrict to vectors with a fixed number of elements.

(7)

The family of g.q.a. means does not follow any extension principles. More precisely:

\circ

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, the mean
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
constructed in this section is not a g.q.a. mean, however its restriction
\mathfrak{M}^{[n]}|_{I^{n}}
is a g.q.a. mean for every compact subinterval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
;

\circ

The mean
\mathfrak{M}\colon\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I^{n}\to I
is not a g.q.a. mean, however its restriction
\mathfrak{M}|_{I^{n}}
is a g.q.a. mean for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(8)

The family of g.q.a. means can be extended to the integral setting in the spirit of Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya
[
18
]
. Namely, for a strictly monotone function
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
and a function
\varphi\colon J\to I
, we can define

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{f}(\varphi):=f^{(-1)}\Big(\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}f\circ\varphi(x)\>dx\Big).

At this stage the mutual relationship between such means and integral quasiarithmetic means is unknown.

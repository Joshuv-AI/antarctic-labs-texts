Phase II Trial of First-Line Durvalumab and Chemotherapy in Patients With Extra-Pulmonary Small Cell Carcinoma
Authors: Peey-Sei Kok, Bryan Chan, Mason Aberoumoud, Alison Zhang, Ken O’Byrne, Subotheni Thavaneswaran, Frank Lin, Maya Kansara, Theresa Corpuz, Patrick Wheeler, Robert John Simes, David Thomas, Chee Khoon Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionProspective randomized trials are lacking in extensive-stage or metastatic extra-pulmonary small cell carcinoma (EPSCC). As a result, treatment for EPSCC is largely extrapolated from SCLC studies.MethodsThis single-arm phase II trial enrolled patients with EPSCC to investigate the activity of first-line durvalumab (1500 mg every 3 wk) and 4 to 6 cycles of chemotherapy (either cisplatin or carboplatin) with etoposide, followed by maintenance durvalumab 1500 mg every 4 weeks until disease progression. All had comprehensive genomic profiling of an archival tumor specimen, using Illumina TruSight Tumour 170, Illumina TSO500, or Foundation Medicine. The primary end point was progression-free survival (PFS) at 12 months. Key secondary end points were objective response rate, overall survival, and prognostic biomarkers, including tumor mutational burden (ACTRN12621001225808).ResultsA total of six participants were enrolled (target: n = 16). The trial closed early due to slow accrual. The primary disease sites were the bladder, colon, gall bladder, esophagus, and pancreas. After a median follow-up of 20.5 months, the rate of PFS at 12 months was 17% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1%–52%), median PFS was 4.9 months (95% CI: 2.0–12.5 mo), and median overall survival was 8.8 months (95% CI: 6.41–not-estimable). The objective response rate was 50% (95% CI: 19%–81%). No new safety signals were observed. Common mutations were microsatellite stability, RB1 and TP53 mutations. Tumor mutational burden ranged from 2.4 to 20.5 mutations per megabase.ConclusionsIn this small prospective study, chemotherapy-durvalumab produces responses comparable to historical chemotherapy outcome, but without clear improvement in durability. Early closure due to slow accrual highlights the challenges of conducting trials in EPSCC. These findings should be considered descriptive and hypothesis generating. This study reveals that durvalumab plus chemotherapy is safe and has some activity in rare small cell cancers outside the lung, but benefits are modest and short lived. Current treatment remains largely chemotherapy based, highlighting the urgent need for larger, collaborative research efforts to find more effective options for these patients.Keywords: Small cell, Durvalumab, Chemo-immunotherapy, Extrapulmonary small cell carcinoma, EPSCC

Introduction

Extrapulmonary small cell carcinoma (EPSCC) is a rare and aggressive malignancy with an incidence of approximately 0.4 per 100,000 people.1 It can arise from nearly any organ, most often the gastrointestinal, genitourinary, gynecologic, or head and neck regions.2 The prognosis is generally poor, particularly for extensive-stage disease, with survival often measured in months.3 Owing to the absence of prospective randomized trials specific to EPSCC, treatment for EPSCC is largely extrapolated from SCLC protocols.

Recent advances in SCLC have established the benefit of combining chemotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Two phase III randomized controlled trials demonstrated that the addition of an ICI to platinum-etoposide chemotherapy significantly improved overall survival (OS) in patients with extensive-stage SCLC, from median OS of 10 months to up to 13 months.4,5 These findings have established chemotherapy-ICI as the new standard of care for extensive-stage SCLC.6 EPSCC typically presents as small nests or sheets of tumor cells with a high mitotic rate and frequent apoptosis and often exhibits areas of necrosis.7 Histologically and immunohistochemically, EPSCC closely resembles SCLC. Both entities are characterized by strong and diffuse positivity for neuroendocrine markers, such as chromogranin, synaptophysin, and CD56, as well as for TTF-1 and Cam5.2, while lacking expression of CK20 and S100.8 The rationale for extending this approach to EPSCC is based on the biological and clinical similarities between SCLC and EPSCC and the mechanistic synergy of chemotherapy and ICIs.

The Australian Cancer Molecular Screening and Therapeutics (MoST) program framework is designed to accelerate the translation of scientific discoveries into clinical care for patients with rare or neglected cancers.9 The MoST program serves as a platform for linking comprehensive molecular screening output with multiple, parallel, signal-seeking clinical trials (or substudies). This investigator-led study aimed to evaluate the activity of durvalumab in combination with chemotherapy as first-line therapy in metastatic EPSCC, using a regimen modelled after the CASPIAN trial in SCLC.

Methods

Study Design and Treatment

This study was designed as a signal-seeking, phase II, open-label, multicenter trial conducted in Australia (ACTRN12621001225808) within the framework of the MoST program. Participants were adults aged above or equal to 18 years with treatment-naive, histologically confirmed, extensive-stage EPSCC, not amenable to curative radiotherapy or surgical resection. Immunohistochemistry staining of the tumor was required to be positive for Cam5.2 and/ or TTF1, chromogranin, synaptophysin, with or without CD56 positivity. Other eligibility criteria were Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 to 1; prior treatment for limited-stage disease was permitted, with allowance of 1 cycle of platinum-based chemotherapy (either cisplatin or carboplatin) within 3 weeks before enrollment; measurable disease according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.110; adequate organ and marrow function; and life expectancy of at least 12 weeks. Key exclusion criteria were prior systemic anticancer treatment for extensive-stage EPSCC; small cell transformation from other histologies; large cell histology, including neuroendocrine origin, small cell carcinoma of the ovary (hypercalcemic type), and contraindications to checkpoint inhibitors. All patients provided written informed consent for participation.

Procedures

Study treatments consisted of the following: (1) etoposide (either 100 mg/m2 intravenously or 200 mg mg/m2 orally) or etopophos (113.6 mg/m2 intravenously) on days 1 to 3 every 3 weeks; (2) investigator's choice of either carboplatin area under the curve 5 or cisplatin 75 mg/m2 (administered on day 1 every 3 wk); and (3) durvalumab 1500 mg every 3 weeks for a total of 4 cycles. In the event of partial or complete response to chemotherapy, up to 6 cycles of cisplatin or carboplatin and etoposide were allowed. This was followed by maintenance durvalumab 1500 mg every 4 weeks until disease progression (as assessed by the investigator), unacceptable toxicity, or other discontinuation criteria were met. Continuation of study treatment after disease progression was permitted if there was evidence of clinical benefit as determined by the treating oncologist. All screened patients had comprehensive genomic profiling of an archival tumor specimen, using Illumina TruSight Tumour 170, Illumina TSO500, or Foundation Medicine.

The primary end point was progression-free survival (PFS) at 12 months. Secondary end points included objective response rate (ORR, defined as complete response [CR] or partial response [PR] as per RECIST 1.110 and immune-related Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (iRECIST),11 the latter accommodating unconfirmed progressive disease [PD] unique to ICI), PFS, OS, safety, and adverse events (AEs). Participants still on treatment at time of analysis were censored at the date of their last reported study treatment. Safety and AEs were defined by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

The planned total sample size of this study was 16. In the two randomized controlled trials of immunotherapy-chemotherapy in extensive-stage SCLC, the observed PFS12 was 12% to 18% in the immunotherapy-chemotherapy arm and 5% in the chemotherapy arm. A 12-month PFS rate of 18% supports the hypothesis for this trial. Using the method of Metha-Cain, boundaries for declaring activity were determined based on a one-sided 95% confidence interval for 12-month PFS rate which would include the hypothesized rate of 18%. Thus, for a sample size of 16 patients, we required one or more patients to be alive and 12-month PFS rate according to conclude that study treatments demonstrate a promising signal of activity. Early closure due to slow accrual prevented achievement of the planned assessable sample size.

As part of the correlative research component of this study, we sought to identify prognostic and predictive immune biomarkers associated with treatment response and resistance. Peripheral blood samples were collected at baseline and at disease progression, and multiparameter flow cytometry was used to profile T cells, B cells, natural killer cells, and monocyte subsets, including activation (HLA-DR) and proliferation (Ki-67) markers. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated, stained using standard protocols, and analyzed on a BD LSRFortessa. Samples with fewer than 50 parent events were excluded, and paired samples were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test.

Results

This study was open between February 2022 and September 2023 and closed early due to slow recruitment. The target recruitment was 16 patients; however, only six patients were recruited. All six patients received the study treatment. Given the small number of patients, the results presented are descriptive.

Baseline Characteristics

The median age of the participants was 64.5 years (Table 1). The primary sites were the bladder (n = 2), colon (n = 1), gall bladder (n = 1), esophagus (n = 1), and pancreas (n = 1). All participants had extensive-stage small cell carcinoma. Two of the six participants received prior chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy for limited-stage disease. Two participants received a single cycle of platinum-based chemotherapy for advanced disease before trial enrollment as permitted by the protocol. All six patients received study treatment with carboplatin, etoposide, and durvalumab. A median of 4 cycles of carboplatin and etoposide and 6.5 cycles of durvalumab were administered per patient.

Antitumor Activity

After a median follow-up of 20.5 months, five of six patients experienced progression or death. The PFS at 12-month rate was 17% (one of six; 95% CI: 1%–52%) with median PFS of 4.9 months (95% CI: 2.0–12.5 mo; Fig. 1). The median time to treatment failure was 4.5 months (range: 1–18 mo).

At the time of study closure, five of six patients had died. The median OS was 8.8 months (95% CI: 6.41–NE; Fig. 3). OS at 12 months was 33% and OS at 18 months was 17%. Quality-of-life data were difficult to interpret due to the small sample size (Supplementary Data).

Safety

All six participants experienced at least one AE of any grade. Only one participant experienced AEs of special interest, specifically hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism. Three of six participants experienced grade 3 or higher AEs, including grade 3 febrile neutropenia and sepsis (n = 1), a grade 3 non-cardiac chest pain (n = 1), and a grade 4 myocardial infarction (n = 1), which was deemed unrelated to the study treatment. All five deaths were attributed to progressive disease.

Quality of Life

Completion of quality-of-life forms was high in the first 6 months (≥80%) but declined at later visits due to disease progression or death, despite 100% attempts (Supplementary Table 1). Based on the limited data, the study treatments improved pain outcomes based on QLQ C30 (Supplementary Table 2) and Brief Pain Inventory scores (Supplementary Table 3).

Correlative Exploratory Research

Tumor sequencing was available for five participants. As expected for tumors of a small cell neuroendocrine origin, the most common variants identified were RB1 (n = 5) and TP53 (n = 5) mutations. Three participants were microsatellite stable, whereas microsatellite status could not be determined in the other two. Tumor mutational burden ranged from 2.4 to 20.5 mutations per megabase. Discussion

This prospective study represents one of the few attempts to evaluate first-line chemo-immunotherapy in patients with extensive-stage EPSCC. Although the study was originally designed as a signal-seeking phase II trial, early closure due to slow accrual limited enrollment to six patients, precluding meaningful evaluation of efficacy end point.

We observed an ORR of 50% by RECIST, but these responses were brief, with most patients progressing within 5 months regardless of primary site. The 12-month PFS was 17%, median PFS was 4.9 months, and the median OS was 8.8 months, reflecting the poor prognosis of EPSCC. Importantly, EPSCC should be regarded as a clinicopathologic descriptor rather than a single biological entity, reflecting substantial heterogeneity across primary tumor sites. In the absence of prospective data specific to EPSCC, treatment strategies are frequently extrapolated from pulmonary SCLC, which represents the current pragmatic clinical approach, but should be interpreted with caution. Outcomes observed in our study compare unfavorably with those reported in the phase III CASPIAN and IMpower133 trials in SCLC, where median OS reached 12.9 and 12.3 months, respectively.4,5

This biological complexity is mirrored in high-grade neuroendocrine tumors (HGNETs), which closely resemble SCLC in both morphology and clinical behavior. Many HGNETs are classified as small cell carcinomas and share key features with EPSCC, leading to a similar treatment approach. However, ICIs have demonstrated limited activity in HGNETs. For example, in KEYNOTE-158, pembrolizumab monotherapy produced an ORR of only 3% to 5%, limited to MSI-high/PD-L1–positive patients.12 Dual ICI trials have reported a wide range of ORR. The DART SWOG 1609 (nivolumab and ipilimumab) demonstrated an ORR of 44% and 6-month PFS rate of 44% among the 18 non-pancreatic HG-NET patients.13 Whereas, the Australian basket trial, CA209-538 (also using nivolumab and ipilimumab), reported an ORR of 24% in the 13 patients with HG-NET.14 These results suggest that combination strategies may overcome some of the resistance found with monotherapy, but the overall benefit remains modest and clearly insufficient for most patients.

Consistent with previous studies, we found near-universal co-mutation of RB1 and TP53 in EPSCC, with few other actionable alterations, mirroring the genetic profile of SCLC. Although loss of TP53/RB1 is consistent, emerging data indicate that molecular subtypes defined by lineage-specific transcription factors similar to those in pulmonary SCLC15 may exist in EPSCC. DLL3 expression was not assessed in this study, which represents a limitation given the growing interest in DLL3-targeted therapies in small cell neuroendocrine malignancies. DLL3 expression, found in up to 65% of EPSCC,16,17 supports exploring DLL3-targeted therapies such as tarlatamab, which has demonstrated OS benefit in SCLC,18 and other agents such as ZL-1310 with promising ORR of 68%,19 both in the second-line setting. Beyond DLL3-targeted strategies, novel agents including lurbinectedin–atezolizumab maintenance20 and berzosertib (an ATR inhibitor) are also in late-stage development for SCLC.21 These advances, paired with molecular characterization, suggest select patients with EPSCC, particularly those with DLL3 expression, may benefit from emerging bispecific T-cell–engager therapies, supporting further preclinical and clinical investigation.

Exploratory analysis revealed consistent reductions in Ki-67 and HLA-DR expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells and CD38high expression on T cells at progression compared with baseline. Owing to sample size limitations, delineating immune subpopulations is difficult. Prior reports have demonstrated high Ki-67, HLA-DR expression, and CD38 expression on T cells associated with better response to ICI22; however, these analyses were performed at early on-treatment time points rather than at disease progression as in our study. As such, the observed reduction in these markers may reflect disease progression, treatment-induced immune suppression, or reduced tumor antigen stimulation. Given the small number of paired samples, these findings are hypothesis generating and support further investigation of dynamic immune biomarkers in EPSCC.

Although this study provides prospective data on first-line chemoimmunotherapy in metastatic EPSCC, several limitations must be acknowledged. The small sample size, driven by the rarity of EPSCC and slow accrual, limits the statistical power and generalizability of our findings. Tumor heterogeneity and the absence of a control arm further complicate interpretation and preclude definitive conclusions about the additive benefit of immunotherapy. Nevertheless, the study’s prospective design of an ultra-rare cancer, molecular profiling confirming RB1/TP53-driven biology consistent with neuroendocrine lineage, and systematic data collection are key strengths. These findings expand the limited evidence base and underscore the need for collaborative, multi-institutional research. This is a real-world demonstration of accrual challenges of such trials. Future studies should consider basket trials including EPSCC and other high-grade neuroendocrine tumors and explore the potential of DLL3-targeted and other novel therapies already demonstrating promise in SCLC.

Conclusion

In this small prospective cohort, chemotherapy-durvalumab demonstrated response similar to historical chemotherapy outcomes, but no clear evidence of improved durability was observed. The early closure of this study precludes any definitive conclusion of the efficacy of chemotherapy-durvalumab, highlighting the challenges conducting prospective trials in EPSCC. Future progress will likely require collaborative multi-institutional basket trials and rare cancer research networks.

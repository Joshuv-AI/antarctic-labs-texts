𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} –connectedness of moduli stack of semi-stable and parabolic semi-stable vector bundles over a curve
Sujoy Chakraborty, Sourav Holme Choudhury

Abstract
. Let C C be an irreducible smooth projective curve of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 over an algebraically closed field. We prove that the moduli stack of semi-stable vector bundles on C C of fixed rank and determinant is 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} –connected. We also show that the moduli stack of quasi-parabolic vector bundles with a fixed determinant and a given quasi-parabolic data along a set of points in C C , as well as its open substacks consisting of 𝜶 \boldsymbol{\alpha} -semistable vector bundles for any system of weights 𝜶 \boldsymbol{\alpha} , are also 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -connected.

14D20, 14D23, 14F42, 14H60

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be an irreducible smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,L)

denote the moduli stack of vector bundles on
C
of rank
n
and determinant
L\in\Pic(C)
. By definition, an object in
\mnl
over a
k
-scheme
S
is given by a rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C\times S
,  together with an isomorphism
\det(\mathcal{E})\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}p^{*}L
, where
p:C\times S\longrightarrow C
is the first projection. We have the following inclusions of open substacks:

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,L)\subset\mnlss\subset\mnl

where
\mnlss
(respectively,
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,L)
) denotes the stack of semistable (respectively, stable) vector bundles of rank
n
and determinant
L
, whose objects over a
k
-scheme
S
is given by a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C\times S
whose restriction for each geometric point
\textnormal{Spec}\,K\rightarrow S
is a
semistable
(respectively, stable) vector bundle over the curve
C_{K}
(see Definition
2.7
for details). It is known that
\mnl
is a smooth algebraic stack over
k
.
Via the nerve construction, any algebraic stack defines an object in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–homotopy category by regarding it as a simplicial sheaf. This naturally raises the question of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of the stacks
\mnl
, as well as for the open substacks
\mnlss
and
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(n,L)
. In
[
HY
]
, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of
\mnl
was proved. It was shown that, two vector bundles of same rank are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant (see Definition
2.5
) if and only if their determinants are isomorphic. This was achieved through looking at extensions of vector bundles rather than individual vector bundles, and showing that, in a certain sense, any extension of vector bundles can be ”joined” to the trivial extension via a line contained in the moduli space of extensions between two fixed vector bundles over a projective scheme. The key observation here was the fact that the moduli space of such extensions of vector bundles is an affine space, which allowed for such a result. Unfortunately, this approach does not carry through for the open substack
\mnlss
or
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(n,L)
, as the presence of semistability causes additional complications. It is also worth mentioning, as pointed out in
[
HY
, Example 2.9]
, that when
n
and
\deg(L)
are coprime, the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of the stable moduli stack
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,L)
cannot be concluded directly from the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of its coarse moduli space alone.

There is also the notion of (quasi-)parabolic vector bundles over a curve introduced by Mehta and Seshadri
[
MS
]
; these are vector bundles together with the data of weighted flags on the fibers over a collection of finitely many points on the curve. From the perspective of algebraic geometry, the study of the moduli stacks of (quasi-)parabolic vector bundles is an important and active area of research.

In this article, we aim to address the question of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of the following moduli stacks:

\bullet

The moduli stack
\mnlss
of semistable vector bundles over
C
of fixed rank
n
and determinant
L
,

\bullet

The moduli stack
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
of quasi-parabolic vector bundles on
C
with a fixed determinant and a quasi-parabolic data
(\boldsymbol{e,m})
along a set of parabolic points on
C
(see Section
3
for details), and

\bullet

The stack
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-ss,\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-semistable parabolic vector bundles for a system of weights
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
; these are open substacks of
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
.

The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of
\mnlss
is shown in Section
2
. Our proof is based on a result of Langton regarding the universal closedness of
\mnlss

[
L
]
. In Section
3
, we prove the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
using the concept of flag bundle stacks. In Section
4
, we show
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of the
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-semistable parabolic moduli
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-ss,\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
. This is again achieved using an analogue of Langton’s result regarding universally closedness for the moduli stack of semistable parabolic vector bundles, due to Mehta and Seshadri
[
MS
,
S
]
. Finally, we conclude our study with a brief discussion of the notion of a
generic
system of weights, and show the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connectedness of the moduli stack
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-stable parabolic vector bundles, denoted by
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-s,\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
, for a generic system of weights
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.

2.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connectedness of the moduli stack of semistable vector bundles over a curve

2.1.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components of simplicial sheaves

Definition 2.1

(Sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components)
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian base scheme of finite Krull dimension, and let
\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
denote the category of smooth schemes of finite type over
S
, equipped with the Nisnevich topology. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(S)
denote the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category of spaces over
S
, which is the homotopy category obtained from the category of simplicial presheaves
\mathrm{sPre}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})
by localizing with respect to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-weak equivalences, as defined in
[
MV
]
. For a space
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(S)
(i.e., an object in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category), the
sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected components
, denoted
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})
, is the Nisnevich sheaf on
\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
defined as the sheafification of the presheaf

U\longmapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(S)}(U,\mathcal{X})

where
U\in\mathrm{Sm}_{S}
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(S)}(U,\mathcal{X})
denotes the set of morphisms from
U
to
\mathcal{X}
in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category.

Equivalently, if
\mathrm{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}:\mathrm{sPre}(\mathrm{Sm}_{S})\to\mathcal{H}_{\bullet}(S)
denotes the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization functor, then

\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})=a_{\mathrm{Nis}}\left(U\mapsto\pi_{0}\left(\mathrm{L}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})(U)\right)\right)

where
a_{\mathrm{Nis}}
denotes Nisnevich sheafification and
\pi_{0}
denotes the set of path components of a simplicial set.

A space
\mathcal{X}
is called
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected
if
\pi_{0}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X})\cong*
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
HY
, Definition 2.6]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a simplicial sheaf on
Sm_{k}
, the Grothendieck site of smooth finite-type schemes over a field
k
equipped with the Nisnevich topology.

(1)

Let
U\in Sm_{k}
. Two objects
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{X}(U)
are said to be
naively

\mathbb{A}^{1}
–
homotopic
, if there exists a map
f:\mathbb{A}^{1}\times_{k}U\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
satisfying
f_{0}=x
and
f_{1}=y
, where
f_{i}
is the composition
U\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb{A}^{1}\times_{k}U\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
.

(2)

We shall denote by
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{X})
the presheaf on
Sm_{k}
, whose values on a
k
-scheme
U
is given by sending

U\mapsto\mathfrak{X}(U)/\sim

where
\sim
stands for the equivalence relation generated by naive
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–homotopy in
(1)
.
The Nisnevich sheafification of the presheaf
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{X})
is denoted by
S(\mathfrak{X})
.

Remark 2.3
.

By a result of Morel (see Lemma 6.1.3 in
[
M
]
) one knows that a ”space”
X
(i.e.
X
is an object in the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy

is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-connected if and only

\pi^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{0}(X)(\textrm{Spec }F)=*\,,

for every finitely generated separable field extension

of

S(X)\to\pi^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{0}(X)
, which is an epimorphism. Using this, one can show that if
S(X)(\textrm{Spec }F)=*
for every finitely generated separable field extension
F
of

, then

is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connected. This has been used in the proof of
[
HY
, Theorem 1.1]
. Moreover, since
S(X)
is the Nisnevich sheafification of the presheaf
S^{\prime}(X)

(see Definition
2.2
), it follows from the properties of the Nisnevich topology that if
S^{\prime}(X)(\textrm{Spec }F)=*
for every finitely generated separable field extension
F
of

, the same holds for its sheafification
S(X)

2.3.
Semistability of vector bundles

The notion of semistability and stability of vector bundles over any irreducible smooth projective variety over a field is well-known; see
[
L
, §2]
for the details. Let us define it for the case of curves, which shall be our main interest.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
C
denote an irreducible smooth projective curve over a field
K
. Let
E
be an algebraic vector bundle over
C
. Since
C
is smooth, the determinant line bundle
\det(E)
corresponds to a divisor class
D
on
C
.

(i)

The
degree
of
E
, denoted
\deg(E)
, is defined to be the degree of the divisor class
D
associated to
\det(E)
.

(ii)

The
slope
of
E
, denoted
\mu(E)
, is defined as the rational number
\deg(E)/\text{rank}(E)
.

(iii)

E
is said to be
semistable
(respectively, stable) if for any proper sub-bundle
F
of
E
, we have
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)\ \ {(\text{respectively, }\mu(F)<\mu(E))}.

The notion of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordance between two vector bundles was introduced in
[
AKW
]
, which shall be recalled below. Note that, this notion of (directly)
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordance coincides with the notion of (naive)
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–homotopy defined in Definition
2.2
, in the context of moduli stacks of vector bundles over a curve.

Definition 2.5

(
[
AKW
, Definition 5.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme over a field
K
. Choose two distinct points
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
. Two vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
on
X
are said to be
directly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant
if there exists a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{0}\simeq\iota_{0}^{*}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}\simeq\iota^{*}_{1}\mathcal{E}
, where
\iota_{0}:X\times\{x_{0}\}\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
and
\iota_{1}:X\times\{x_{1}\}\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
are the obvious inclusions. The vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
are said to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant
if they are equivalent under the equivalence relation generated by direct
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordance.

Thus,
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant if there exists a finite sequence of vector bundles

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ \cdots,\mathcal{F}_{r}=\mathcal{E}_{1}

such that each pair
(\mathcal{F}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i+1})
are directly
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant in the sense of Definition
2.5
.

We would like to have a version of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordance through families of
semistable
vector bundles for our purpose. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme over a field
K
. Two semistable vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
on
X
are said to be
directly semistably
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant
if there exists a family of semistable vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
over
X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
(see Definition
2.7
(2)) such that
\mathcal{E}_{0}\simeq\iota_{0}^{*}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}\simeq\iota^{*}_{1}\mathcal{E}
, where
\iota_{0}:X\times\{x_{0}\}\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
and
\iota_{1}:X\times\{x_{1}\}\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
are the obvious inclusions with respect to two distinct points
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}
.
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
are said to be
semistably
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant
if they are equivalent under the equivalence relation generated by direct semistably
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordance.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
C
be an irreducible smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field
k
. Fix a line bundle
L
on
C
. Let
S
be a
k
-scheme.

(1)

A
family of vector bundles over
C
of rank
n
and determinant
L
parametrized by
S
is a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
C\times S
together with an isomorphism
\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq p^{*}L
, where
p:C\times S\longrightarrow C
is the first projection.

(2)

Consider a family of vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
parametrized by
S
as in (
1
).
\mathcal{E}
is said to be a family of
semistable
vector bundles, if for each geometric point
p:\Spec K\rightarrow S
(where
K
is an algebraically closed field), the restriction
\mathcal{E}|_{C\times\Spec K}
is a
semistable
vector bundle over the curve
C_{K}:=C\times\Spec K
.

Remark 2.8
.

For a
k
-scheme
S
, the
S
-valued points in the moduli stack of vector bundles
\mnl
and its open substack of semistable vector bundles
\mnlss
are given by families of usual vector bundles and semistable vector bundles on
C
parametrized by
S
as in Definition
2.7
, respectively.

Proposition 2.9
.

Consider two semistable vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
over the curve
C_{F}=C\times_{k}\Spec F
for a field extension
F
over
k
, both having rank
n
and determinant
p^{*}L
, where
p:C_{F}\rightarrow C
is the projection and
L\in\Pic(C)
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
are semistably
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant in the sense of Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

It is shown in
[
HY
, Theorem 2.5]
that
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{1})
implies
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–concordant as ordinary vector bundles in the sense of Definition
2.5
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}

as well.

Proof.

Given two
F
-points
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{Y}
, we need to show that
x
and
y
can be joined by a finite sequence of morphisms
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Y}
. If
x
and
y
map to the same
F
-point in
\mathfrak{X}
, then they must lie in the same fiber of
f
. In this case, the claim immediately follows from the well-known fact that flag varieties are rational and hence
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–chain connected. If
x
and
y
have different images under
f
, then one can find a finite sequence of maps from
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{1}
to
\mathfrak{X}
joining
f(x)
to
f(y)
. Without loss of generality, assume that
f(x)
and
f(y)
can be directly joined by a morphism
g:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathfrak{X}
. Let us assume that
g(0)=x
and
g(1)=y
. Now, since
f:\mathfrak{Y}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathfrak{X}
is a Zariski-locally trivial flag bundle stack, it is a representable morphism. It follows that the pullback
g^{*}\mathfrak{Y}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
must be the trivial flag bundle
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\times_{k}Z
. Let us denote the points corresponding to
(0,x)
and
(1,y)
in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\times_{k}Z
as
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
respectively. Since both
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}
and
Z
are
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–chain connected, it follows that their product
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\times_{k}Z
is also
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–chain connected. Thus, one can join
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
by a sequence of morphisms
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\ \longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\times_{k}Z\,\simeq\,g^{*}\mathfrak{Y}
. Composing these sequence of maps with the natural morphism
g^{*}\mathfrak{Y}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathfrak{Y}
, it follows that
x
and
y
can be joined by a sequence of morphisms
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathfrak{Y}
, showing
S^{\prime}(\mathfrak{Y})(\text{Spec}F)=0
as claimed.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

The moduli stack of quasi-parabolic vector bundles
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connected.

Proof.

The forgetful map

\displaystyle\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)\ \longrightarrow\ \mnl

(3.2)

is an iterated flag bundle stack which is Zariski-locally trivial. To see this, let us first consider the case when
D=\{p\}
consists of a single point. Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the universal vector bundle over
\mnl\times C
. In this case, the quasi-parabolic data along
\{p\}
consists of a single positive integer
e
and a tuple
\underline{m}:=(m_{{}_{1}},\cdots,m_{{}_{e}})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{e}m_{{}_{i}}=n
. Let
\iota_{p}:\mnl\ \hookrightarrow\mnl\times C
denotes the inclusion via the point
p
. It immediately follows from Definition
3.4
and
[
BY
, Definition 3.1]
that the
S
-points of the quasi-parabolic moduli stack
\mathcal{M}^{e,\underline{m}}_{C}(n,L)
and the flag bundle stack
\textnormal{Flag}_{\underline{m}}({\iota_{p}}^{*}\mathcal{E})
are equivalent for any
k
-scheme
S
, which implies that the two stacks are isomorphic:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{e,\underline{m}}_{C}(n,L)\ \overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\ \textnormal{Flag}_{\underline{m}}({\iota_{p}}^{*}\mathcal{E})\ .

(3.3)

Thus, for a single parabolic point
D=\{p\}
, the forgetful map in (
3.2
) has the structure of a flag bundle stack over
\mnl
. For multiple parabolic points
D=\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{r}\}
, the stack
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
is the fiber product over
\mnl
of the quasi-parabolic moduli stacks for each parabolic point

. This shows that the forgetful map in (
3.2
) is indeed an iterated flag bundle stack.

Consider the presheaf
S^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)\right)
(see Definition
2.2
), where
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
is seen as a simplicial sheaf as before. The proof of
[

shows the following: for any finitely generated field extension

over

\displaystyle S^{\prime}\left(\mnl\right)(\text{Spec}F)=0.

(3.4)

Using Proposition
3.6
, one immediately concludes that

as well. This implies
\mathcal{PM}_{C}^{\boldsymbol{e,m}}(n,L)
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connected (see Remark

∎

We conclude our study with considering the situation when the system of weights are chosen to be
generic
. We shall only briefly describe the notion here, referring to
[
BY
, §2]
for more details. Recall that, in the usual case of vector bundles, the coprimality of rank and degree implies that any semi-stable vector bundle is automatically stable. A similar situation occurs in the case of parabolic vector bundles for a generic system of weights: when
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is generic, the notion of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-semistability and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-stability coincide. More explicitly, let
(\boldsymbol{e,m})
be a quasi-parabolic data of rank
n
along a fixed set of parabolic points as in Definition
3.1
. The collection of all possible systems of weights
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
associated to
(\boldsymbol{e,m})
forms a simplex
\Delta
inside a Euclidean space. The parabolic semistability condition imposes certain relations on these systems of weights via the vanishing of certain linear polynomials, which give rise to certain hyperplane sections of
\Delta
. These hyperplane sections are called
walls
. Such walls can be shown to be finite in number. The complement of these finitely many walls in
\Delta
is divided into connected components called
chambers
. A system of weights
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
lying inside a chamber is said to be a
generic
system of weights. Equivalently,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is generic if for any quasi-parabolic vector bundle
E
associated to the data
(\boldsymbol{e,m})
along
D
(see Definition
3.2
),
E
is
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-semistable iff it is
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-stable. The next corollary is thus a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3
.

When
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is a generic system of weights, the moduli stack of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-stable parabolic vector bundles of rank
n
and determinant
L
on
C
, denoted by
\mathcal{PM}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-s,\boldsymbol{e,m}}_{C}(n,L)
, is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
–connected.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.2
, since for a generic system of weights, the notion of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-semistability coincides with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-stability.
∎

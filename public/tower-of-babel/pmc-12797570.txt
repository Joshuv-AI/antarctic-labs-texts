Study of zygomaticomaxillary orbital floor complex fractures via endoscopy combined with digital real-time navigation technology
Authors: Dong Wang, Zeyu Wang, Junhui Sun, Liang Liu, Kai Zhang, Tingyi Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the effectiveness of endoscopy combined with digital navigation technology in the treatment of zygomaticomaxillary complex fractures.MethodsA retrospective controlled study was conducted. The endoscopy combined with digital navigation technology group was defined as the new technology group, and the conventional treatment group was defined as the conventional group. Eight patients were selected from each group. The chi-square test or t test was used to compare the basic conditions, postoperative recovery, and orbital volume between the two groups. Multivariate linear regression was performed to assess independent predictors of postoperative orbital volume difference, including digital navigation technology use, operation time, trauma-to-operation interval, and preoperative mouth opening. Results are presented as regression coefficients (β), 95% confidence intervals, and p-values.ResultsThere were no significant differences in sex, age, injury mechanism, etc., between the new technology group and the conventional group. The fracture reduction fit of the new technology group was better than that of the control group, the postoperative orbital volume was closer to the preoperative orbital volume, and the difference was statistically significant.digital navigation technology use was independently associated with a significant reduction in postoperative orbital volume difference (β = -3.14 ml; 95% CI: -4.09 to -2.18; p < 0.001). No significant associations were found for operation time, preoperative mouth opening, or time from trauma to operation.ConclusionBased on the findings of this study, we believe that endoscopy combined with digital real-time navigation technology can improve the efficacy of surgical treatment for zygomaticomaxillary orbital floor complex fractures, thereby achieving better functional recovery.

Introduction

Zygomaticomaxillary fractures (ZMCFs) are the second most common type of facial fractures after nasal bone fractures. When associated with orbital floor involvement, these fractures can directly impair critical functions such as speech, mastication, and vision [1]. In clinical practice, this type of fracture involves multiple anatomical parts of the orbital floor, maxilla, and zygomatic bone, with many cavities and small spaces, making anatomical reduction of the fracture difficult and risky [2].

Orbital floor reconstruction is the most technically demanding aspect of managing complex zygomaticomaxillary fractures. Because of the thin and fragile bony architecture of the medial orbital wall and the presence of multiple fracture fragments, achieving precise anatomical reduction in this region is technically challenging [3]. However, if it is not restored, diplopia, visual impairment and enophthalmos can occur [4].

Given the challenges faced by traditional surgery in accurately restoring the anatomical structure and orbital volume of zygomatico-orbital-mandibular complex fractures (especially the orbital floor), this study aims to explore whether endoscopy combined with digital real-time navigation technology can provide a better solution.The advancement of digital technology has led to rapid progress in the field of fracture repair. Many scholars have reported that this method can effectively assist in fracture reduction [5]. However, there are still situations where the field of vision is insufficient and the operation is difficult [6]. The application of endoscopes in surgical operations is a major innovation. Endoscopic techniques can effectively minimize trauma and enhance intraoperative visualization; however, their application in fracture management remains limited.

To overcome these challenges, we combined endoscopic techniques with near-infrared navigation technology to assist in the reduction of orbit zygomatic-maxillary complex fractures.

This study also summarized our surgical experience and evaluated the outcomes of the treatment.The aim of this study was to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of digital navigation combined with endoscopic assistance in the treatment of orbit zygomatic-maxillary complex fracture.

Materials and methods

This retrospective controlled study followed the STROBE guidelines. This study included zygomaticomaxillary complex fractures patients treated in the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University. This study received ethical approval from the the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University (Approval No. BYFYXJS2021034), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to surgery. This study was conducted in strict accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient selection principles

Patients were eligible if they underwent surgical treatment for ZMCFs and their preoperative examination classified the fractures as type B or type C according to the Zingg classification. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age between 18 and 80 years, preoperative diplopia symptoms, and indications for orbital floor reconstruction. The exclusion criteria included patients whose physical condition precluded surgical intervention and those with monocular or binocular vision loss.

There were 16 patients who met the eligibility criteria with the baseline characteristics

The cohort that underwent the novel surgical technique was designated the New Technique Group (Group 1). For comparison, patients who underwent conventional surgery on the basis of the surgeon’s experience were classified into the Control Group (Group 2). All materials used in this study were non-absorbable pure titanium or titanium alloy internal fixation materials produced by DePuy Synthes.

Preoperative plan for group 1

Preoperatively, patients underwent spiral CT scanning (slice thickness 0.625 mm; Bright Speed 16; GE Healthcare, Buckinghamshire, UK). The scan range was from the cranial vertex to the hyoid bone. DICOM data were imported into Mimics software (Materialize, Leuven, Belgium) for 3D reconstruction. The displaced fracture fragments were virtually reduced using the mirrored contralateral anatomy as a reference template to restore the anatomical contours of the zygomatic complex and orbital floor. The virtual reduction model was exported as an STL file and imported into AccuNavi-A2.1 software (Shanghai, China). Crucially, an attachment strategy for the dynamic reference frame (DRF) was developed. The DRF (equipped with a passive reflective sphere for near-infrared tracking) was designed to be firmly fixed on the Mayfield head clamp. A registration scheme was established in the software. Anatomical fiducial landmarks (usually stable, caries-free tooth cusps or prominent bony landmarks not affected by the fracture, such as the zygomatic arch or frontal bone) were selected on both the virtual model and the planned intraoperative patient. A surface matching algorithm was used to complement the point-based registration to improve the overall accuracy. The target registration error (TRE) was virtually evaluated and considered to be within acceptable limits (< 1.0 mm). The final plan, including registration points and virtually simplified anatomy, was converted into a UEG file for intraoperative use [7].

Surgical procedure

The operation was performed under general anesthesia with nasotracheal intubation by the same surgeon. The preoperative CT image (Fig. 1) revealed that the orbital floors on both sides were not at the same level. A Subciliary Approach incision was carried out to gain access to the fracture of the orbital floor (Fig. 2). (DRF) fixed to the head clamp was employed. Preoperative CT DICOM data were merged with real-time tracking of surgical instruments (Fig. 3). This approach enables precise alignment with imaging data and minimizes soft tissue dissection. An additional incision through the intraoral maxillary vestibular approach is necessary to access the medial aspect of the zygomatic arch. This anatomical site is critical for mobilizing bone segments and restoring the overall facial framework. After completing the reduction of the bone fragments, the navigation probe is guided to verify whether the fracture alignment matches the preoperative planning objectives. After verifying that the fracture frame was satisfactory (Fig. 4), the orbital floor was bent to an appropriate curvature on the basis of the preoperative digital design and the position of the optic nerve. A 4 mm-diameter, 30°-angled rigid endoscope (Karl Storz GmbH, Germany) was introduced via the subciliary approach to provide direct visualization of the posterior orbital floor. Under endoscopic guidance, a pre-contoured titanium mesh plate (DePuy Synthes, 0.3 mm thickness) was slowly inserted to avoid impingement on the infraorbital nerve and orbital contents, the eyeball was moved, eyeball mobility was observed, and the wound was closed [8].Fig. 1CT coronal view shows zygomaticomaxillary complex fractures and the orbital floors on both sides are not on the same levelFig. 2During the operation, a navigation stent is installed and probes, endoscopes and other tools are used to determine the specific condition of the fractureFig. 3Matching the preoperative image with the fracture location using a navigation probeFig. 4Digital navigation verifies whether the effect after reduction is consistent with the preoperative design

Conventional treatment (group 2)

In Group 2, a subciliary incision combined with an intraoral vestibular incision was utilized. Reduction forceps were employed to reposition displaced bone fragments and restore the curvature of the zygomatic arch. Guided by the surgeon’s experience, the fracture ends were aligned to reconstruct the inferior orbital rim, and an orbital floor plate was placed to restore orbital volume.

Variables under study

Primary outcome variables: fracture reduction fit (3D deviation), postoperative orbital volume recovery (compared with the healthy side/preoperative design), postoperative diplopia incidence, postoperative enophthalmos improvement. Enophthalmos was diagnosed using axial CT images. A straight line was drawn between the bilateral frontozygomatic sutures, and the positions of both globes were compared. If the affected globe was displaced more than 2 mm posteriorly compared to the contralateral side, enophthalmos was considered present.

Secondary outcome variables: operation time, postoperative mouth opening increase, sensory numbness incidence, complications (infection, visual loss, internal fixation related).

Covariates: age, sex, injury mechanism, time from injury to surgery, injured side, fracture type (ZMC classification), and whether there are other systemic injuries.

All patient data (demographics, clinical characteristics, imaging data, surgical records, follow-up results) were retrospectively extracted from the hospital electronic medical record system. Imaging data (CT DICOM files) were processed and analyzed using Mimics software. Orbital volume calculations were based on postoperative 3D CT data and were measured using semiautomatic segmentation.

Postoperative treatment and follow-up

Anti-inflammatory, analgesic, hemostatic and other drug treatments were given after the operation, and sodium hyaluronate eye drops were used. Three months after surgery, eye movement photos were taken for comparison with those before surgery, and thin-layer three-dimensional CT was taken. The preoperative design and postoperative data were fitted and compared (Fig. 5). Statistical analysis

SPSS software (version 20.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for data analysis. The chi-square test was used for measurement data, and the t test was used for comparisons of means between two groups (α = 0.05).

To comprehensively evaluate the independent influence of multiple clinical variables on postoperative outcomes, multivariate regression analyses were performed. Specifically, multiple linear regression was conducted with postoperative orbital volume difference as the dependent variable. Independent variables included in the regression model were the use of digital navigation technology, operation.timeTime from trauma to operation,Preoperative mouth opening. Adjusted regression coefficients (β), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p-values were calculated.

Discussion

The ZMCFs, particularly those involving the orbital floor, present significant surgical challenges due to complex anatomy, limited visibility, and difficulty in achieving precise anatomical reduction. Inadequate restoration of orbital volume often leads to debilitating complications such as enophthalmos, diplopia, and functional impairment. To address these limitations, we investigated whether combining endoscopic visualization with digital real-time navigation technology could improve surgical precision in ZMCFs repair. Our study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of this integrated approach in enhancing fracture reduction accuracy, optimizing orbital volume restoration, and reducing postoperative complications compared to conventional techniques.

Previous studies have revealed that ZMCFs rank second only to nasal bone fractures in incidence among all facial fractures, with the prominent anatomical position of the zygomaticomaxillary buttress being the primary contributing factor to the occurrence of such fractures [9] In this study, 90% of the injuries were caused by traffic accidents and a lack of head protection. In addition, zygomaticomaxillary complex fractures in this group of patients mainly occurred on the right side. The main reason for this finding is that the right upper limb of Chinese patients is the dominant side, which is more likely to cause injuries on this side [10].

Among the various procedures involved in treating ZMCFs, reconstruction of the orbital floor is the most technically challenging step, as orbital floor defects are a common cause of postoperative orbital volume expansion [11]. Orbital volume damage can cause facial aesthetic and functional damage, such as enophthalmos and diplopia. Preoperative CT scan assessment of orbital volume is helpful for planning orbital reconstruction [12]. This study found that the orbital volume of the two groups of patients after surgery was significantly different, which has important clinical significance. First, an increase in orbital volume often leads to abnormal changes in the position of the eyeball, especially the posterior displacement of the eyeball, that is, enophthalmos. The literature points out that when the volume change exceeds a certain threshold (usually more than 2–3 ml), it may cause obvious eye socket depression, which significantly affects the patient's facial beauty and mental health [13]. In addition, excessive orbital volume may also change the attachment position or tension of the eye muscles, causing patients to have eye movement disorders and even functional abnormalities such as diplopia [14]. Secondly, previous studies have shown that when the orbital volume expands beyond a certain threshold, it may cause visual impairment. This damage may be caused by abnormal eye position or excessive traction on the optic nerve.

At present, the mainstream surgical method involves the use of orbital floor and orbital fat filling to restore orbital volume, but there are poor recovery effects and a risk of damage to the optic nerve [15]. In this case, we used a digital design to locate the optic nerve position and orbital floor implantation position before surgery. Endoscopic assistance to expand the field of view and intraoperative navigation verification results were satisfactory, restoring the orbital volume and avoiding major complications. According to the literature, the emergence of new materials and technologies such as 3D printing and PEEK also provides a simpler and more convenient method for orbital volume restoration, but the clinical application time is still short, and the specific clinical effects need further verification and discussion [16–18].

In recent years, digital technology has been widely used in clinical medicine, especially in orthopedics and oral and maxillofacial surgery, and has made significant progress [19]. In this study, we innovatively combined preoperative virtual planning, intraoperative digital navigation, and endoscope-assisted technology to improve the accuracy of complex zygomatico-maxillary orbital floor fracture surgery. Specifically, a high-resolution CT scan with a slice thickness of 0.625 mm was used before surgery, and the image data was imported into Mimics 23.0 software (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium) for three-dimensional reconstruction and precise virtual surgical planning. The virtual planning model was then integrated into the AccuNavi navigation system (UEG, China) during surgery. Intraoperative real-time navigation technology can accurately guide the positioning and reduction of the fracture fragments, ensuring that the surgery is highly consistent with the virtual planning plan. In addition, an endoscope with a diameter of 4 mm and an angle of 30° (Karl Storz, Germany), provides high-definition images of the surgical field, realizes real-time verification of navigation accuracy, and significantly improves the accuracy of fracture reduction and orbital floor reconstruction. It has obvious advantages over traditional surgical methods, including improved accuracy of orbital volume restoration after surgery, reduced complications such as enophthalmos, shortened operation time, and improved surgical safety.

In this case, digital technology has the following advantages: 1. Preoperative planning: Through digital technology, we can better understand the position of fracture reduction and accurately measure the three-dimensional structure of important facial organs. 2. Intraoperative assistance: Through digital technology, we can assist in the bending and shaping of fixation devices. 3. Postoperative comparison: After surgery, we used digital technology to analyze and fit the postoperative data with the preoperative data to understand the recovery effect. In summary, digital technology is widely used for the treatment of multiple maxillofacial fractures, with significant results. Notably, the use of digital technology and navigation has improved the accuracy of surgery, but the preoperative navigation design requires certain basic computer knowledge, and the installation of navigation brackets is somewhat complicated. This will extend the learning curve of this technology.

Endoscopic technology was previously used in major surgeries, such as abdominal surgery, which magnifies the field of view and reduces trauma. Currently, some people are gradually using this technology to treat facial fractures and have achieved good clinical results. The concept of minimally invasive surgery originated from this approach [20]. In this study, the new technology group used endoscopy to assist in the implantation of the orbital floor. There was no significant difference in optic nerve damage or visual loss between the new technology group and the control group, but the incidence of postoperative diplopia symptoms was significantly lower. Therefore, we believe that in delicate areas such as the orbital floor, where the visual field is poor, endoscope-assisted surgery can effectively avoid damage to important structures such as the optic nerve and improve the quality of life of patients. In addition, in this study, we combined endoscopy and digital navigation technology to assist each other and better complete this type of surgery.

Emerging technologies such as augmented reality are developing rapidly in the field of surgery and have many advantages over traditional surgery. We can foresee that they can be applied to areas such as zygomatico-maxillary fractures and overcome more difficulties [21]. Of course, the number of cases in this study was small. We will further optimize the process, increase the number of cases, and improve the reliability of the conclusions in the future.

Conclusion

Compared with traditional methods, this combined technique can achieve more accurate fracture reduction and orbital volume restoration, significantly reduce the risk of postoperative diplopia, and does not significantly increase surgical time or trauma. This provides a promising new strategy to improve the efficacy and prognosis of such complex fractures. Future studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up times are needed to further verify its long-term effects and promotion value.

Abstract
. We study the process of ℓ \ell -adic completion of motivic sheaves. We observe that, in equal characteristic, when restricted to constructible objets, it is compatible with the six operations. This implies that one can reconstruct ℓ \ell -adic sheaves of geometric origin over a scheme of finite type over a field from ℓ \ell -adic cohomology of smooth schemes. In the case of finite fields, this includes perverse ℓ \ell -adic sheaves of geometric orgin. However, the analogous behaviour fails systematically in mixed characteristic: the reason is that it would imply strong independence of ℓ \ell results that can be proven to be too optimistic.



Introduction

It known that there is a strong analogy between motives and abelian
groups: abelian groups are motives in complex analytic geometry.
It is thus tempting to try to draw a parallel between
derived categories of motives and the derived category of abelian groups.
An important feature of motivic sheaves, at least if we work locally for the
étale topology, is that (derived)
\ell
-adic completion yields

\ell
-adic realization functors (for
\ell
a prime number distinct from
the residue characteristics of the base). Therefore, completions of motivic sheaves
play a central role in the motivic story, and it is natural to understand
the general structural properties that one can observe through completions
in this setting.
In the context of abelian groups, derived
\ell
-adic completion has a nice
behavior on perfect complexes: given a perfect complex of
\mathbf{Z}
-modules
E
,
since
E
is dualizable, the derived tensor product by
E
is a right adjoint
and we see that the
\ell
-adic completion of
E
is isomorphism to the tensor
product of
E
with the
\ell
-adic completion of
\mathbf{Z}
:

\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}E\cong E_{\ell}

Given a scheme
X
and a motivic sheaf
M
, we investigate whether
the canonical comparison map

\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}M\to M_{\ell}

is invertible in
\mathrm{DM}(X)
. In the first section, we prove that for
X

of finite type over a field and
M
constructible, this is indeed the case
(Proposition
1.3
).
The proof uses de Jong’s alteration theorem and consists in proving
another structural property of
\ell
-adic completion: given
any morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of schemes of finite type over a field,
the pullback functor

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{DM}(Y)\to\mathrm{DM}(X)

commutes with derived
\ell
-adic completion of constructible objects.
A formal consequence of this story is that, given
\ell
-adic cohomology
seen as a cohomology theory of smooth schemes over a field
k
, one
can reconstruct the six-functor formalism of
\ell
-adic sheaves of geometric
origin for schemes of finite type over
k
(Theorem
2.2
).
Furthermore, at least if
k
is the algebraic closure of a finite field,
using Deligne’s theory of weights,
this reconstruction theorem is compatible with the perverse
t
-structure
(Corollary
2.9
), and one can develop of Weil-étale
version of this story (Remark
2.11
).

It is thus natural to ask whether these nice structural properties can be
proved in mixed characteristic. And this may be the most
important contribution of this article; the answer is:
not at all, neither of them (Theorem
3.1
). The proof is not constructive:
we prove that it would imply the existence of a comparison isomorphism
between
\ell
-adic cohomology and rigid cohomology (for schemes over a field
of characteristic
p\neq\ell
), with structural properties that would be too good
to be true (the argument is somehow a variation on the non-existence of
a Weil cohomology with
\mathbf{Q}
-coefficients over a big enough
field of positive characteristic, using products of supersingular elliptic curves).

At the end of the day, this means that we are facing the following situation.
Consider a noetherian scheme
S
of dimension
1
and a closed point
s
in
S
in the regular locus of
S
. We assume that
the residue field
k
of
s
is
algebraically closed and of positive
characteristic, and that the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
has
at most the cardinality of the continuum. If
\ell
is any prime number
distinct from the charactersitc of
k
and
i\colon s\to S
the inclusion map,
there is a motivic sheaf
i^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,S})
over
s

equipped with a map to ordinary
\ell
-adic cohomology of smooth
k
-schemes

i^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,S})\to\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,s}

that we can embed in a fiber sequence:

E\to i^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,S})\to\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,s}

We can then define, for any
k
-scheme
of finite type
X
, motivic cohomology of
X
with coefficients in
E

through

\mathrm{H}^{j}(X,E(j))=\mathrm{H}^{j}(\mathrm{R}\operatorname{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{DM}(X)}(\mathbf{Q}_{X},E_{X}(j)))

where
E_{X}
denotes the pullback of
E
from
s=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
to
X
.
Note that, since the map
i^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,S})\to\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,s}

induces an isomorphism after
\ell
-adic completion,
E
has to be

\mathbf{Q}
-linear. If ever
S
is of finite type over
k
, then we get

\mathrm{H}^{j}(X,E(j))\cong 0
for all
X
and any indices
i,j

(this is what Proposition
1.3
means).
But if
S
is generically of characteristic zero, then this cohomology
theory is not trivial, by Theorem
3.1
.
Constructing explicit non-trivial elements in cohomology groups
of the form
\mathrm{H}^{j}(X,E(j))
is a challenging question:
this is purely of arithmetic nature, apparently violating the analogy between
curves and rings of integers.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
k
be a field
and
\ell
a prime number.
The following properties hold.

(i)

For any
k
-scheme of finite type
X
any any
constructible motivic sheaf
M
on
X
, there is a canonical
isomorphism
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}M\cong M_{\ell}
.

(ii)

For any morphism
f:X\to Y
of
k
-schemes of finite type,
the pull-back functor
f^{*}:\mathrm{DM}(Y,\mathbf{Z})\to\mathrm{DM}(X,\mathbf{Z})

induces an isomorphism

f^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,Y})\cong\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\,.

Proof.

To prove (i), we observe that the class of those
M
such that the canonical
map (
1.2.4
) is invertible is closed under finite (co)limits
and direct factors. Since constructible motivic sheaves are generated
by those of the form
f_{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{U})(n)
for a proper map
f:U\to X

and an integer
n
, and since all functors of interest commute with Tate twists
(see e.g.
[
CD19
, Prop. 4.2.13]
),
it suffices to prove the particular case
where
M=f_{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{U})
for a proper map
f:U\to X
.
We have then the projection formula:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}M

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}f_{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{U})

\displaystyle\cong f_{*}f^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X})\,.

Therefore, since
f_{*}
commutes with limits,
it is sufficient to prove property (ii) in the case where
f
is proper
to prove property (i).

We will now prove property (ii).
Let
p:X\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(k)
and
q:Y\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(k)
be the structural maps.
If we know property (ii) for
p
and
q
, namely,

p^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,k})\cong\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\qquad q^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,k})\cong\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,Y}\,,

we deduce

f^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,Y})\cong f^{*}q^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,k})\cong p^{*}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,k})\cong\mathbf{Z}_{\ell,X}\,.

Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the case where
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(k)
,
which follows immediately from
Lemma
1.2
.

Proposition 1.6

For any scheme of finite type

Proposition 2.8

Let

be a

-category

We assume that the following conditions hold:

(b)

for all objects

\mathcal{C}

We fix a family

-structure on

Proof.

We prove the case where

We can form the object

and form the algebra of its endomorphisms

This is a dg

-algebra with the following properties:

(i)

\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)
is semi-simple.

(ii)

\mathrm{H}^{i}(A)=0
for

(iii)

it is perfect as a complex of

-modules. In particular,

\mathrm{H}^{i}(A)

-module.

That means that we can apply Theorem

and get a

-structure on
\mathit{Perf}(A)
.
But, by construction, we have

,
hence a

-structure on
\mathcal{C}_{S}
,

(resp.

)

containing the elements of

-exact.

To prove the general case, we write

Each

has a

-structure such that the inclusion

\mathcal{C}_{T}\to\mathcal{C}
it
t
-exact, and each transition map in the diagram

-exact. There is thus a unique

-structure
on

such that each inclusion

The induced functor

E-bike agents: Large Language Model-Driven E-Bike Accident Analysis and Severity Prediction
Zhichao Yang, Jiashu He Mohammad B. Al-Khasawneh, Darshan Pandit, Cirillo Cinzia, [6pt] { zcyang97, mbmk2020, dmpandit, ccirillo } \{\text{zcyang97, mbmk2020, dmpandit, ccirillo}\} @umd.edu

Abstract
Electric bicycles (e-bikes) are rapidly increasing in use, raising safety concerns due to a rise in accident reports. However, e-bike incident reports often use unstructured narrative formats, which hinders quantitative safety analysis. This study introduces E-bike agents, a framework that uses large language models (LLM) powered agents to classify and extract safety variables from unstructured incident reports. Our framework consists of four LLM agents, handling data classification, information extraction, injury cause determination, and component linkage, to extract the key factors that could lead to E-bike accidents and cause varying severity levels. Furthermore, we used an ordered logit model to examine the relationship between the severity of the incident and the factors retrieved, such as gender, the type of cause, and environmental conditions. Our research shows that equipment issues are slightly more common than human-related ones, but human-related incidents are more often fatal. Specifically, pedals, tires, and brakes are frequent contributors to accidents. The model achieves a high weighted F1 score of 0.87 in classification accuracy, highlighting the potential of using LLMs to extract unstructured data in niche domains, such as transportation. Our method offers a scalable solution to improve e-bike safety analytics and provides actionable information for policy makers, designers, and regulators.



I
Introduction

Electric bicycles, commonly known as E-bikes, have seen a marked increase in popularity due to their convenience, economic advantages, and environmental benefits. The volume of e-bike trips within the United States experienced a dramatic increase, soaring from a total of 7 million in 2019 to an impressive 31 million by the end of 2022. This upward trend persisted despite the obstacles posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, as the number of e-bikes increased by an additional 71% between 2021 and 2022
(
NABSA2022
)
. However, this growth has been paralleled by a concerning increase in safety-related incidents. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) reports that from 2017 to 2021, there were 53 deaths associated with the use of e-bikes
(
Tark2022
)
. In addition, injuries related to e-bikes composed roughly 11 percent of the total recorded micromobility injuries during that period of time. Thus, it becomes imperative to thoroughly examine the mechanical, electrical and human factors that contribute to these incidents. Nine distinct research avenues are discernible regarding various e-bike safety issues. These include investigations into human elements affecting e-bike accidents, the behavior patterns of e-bike users, structural defects leading to e-bike hazards, the impact of infrastructure on e-bike safety, risk comparison with other forms of transportation, the efficacy of protective gear, technological improvements for enhanced e-bike safety, regulations, and their enforcement regarding e-bikes, as well as public education and awareness regarding e-bike safety.

Investigating methods to improve e-bike safety is crucial. Using safety equipment, especially helmets, is imperative as they directly protect riders. Helmet use has reduced fatal head injuries by 10%, reducing the probability from 89% to 79%
(
Ma2023HelmetEbike
)
. Despite this reduction, the risk of fatal head injuries remains significant, indicating the need for ongoing safety improvements. Technological improvements in electric bikes offer a promising approach in tackling diverse safety challenges. Researchers are creating new devices to prevent accidents on the e-bike, such as those related to charging. "SingMonitor"
(
Jian2023SingMonitor
)
has been introduced to leverage mobile device microphones to effectively monitor and evaluate the charging health of electric bikes remotely.

Such incidents are then passed to the e-bike data analysis team for further processing, which includes: information extractor, injury causes determiner, and incident-component link detector. The information extractor identifies incident factors, which are then analyzed using an ordered logit model to explore their relationship with injury severity levels. The methodologies currently established, despite their effectiveness in certain specific contexts in improving e-bike safety, cannot be easily extended to a comprehensive systematic analysis of the causes and impacts of accidents involving e-bikes. This limitation arises due to the presence of unstructured data that contains myriad perspectives, which are challenging for human experts to pre-define. It is imperative to develop universal frameworks that can incorporate these unstructured accident descriptors, reveal concealed patterns, and subsequently offer actionable insights to improve the safety of e-bikes. The latest developments in machine learning and artificial intelligence methodologies present optimal tools for undertaking this endeavor. Despite the remarkable success achieved by Large Language Models (LLMs) in handling and analyzing unstructured data, the potential application of their reasoning capabilities within the domain of traffic accident analysis has not been thoroughly investigated. This paper aims to investigate this area of research by addressing the challenges posed by unstructured data mining in the context of e-bike-related accidents. We propose exploring how agents powered by LLMs can transform unstructured data from e-bike incidents into meaningful insights that can be acted upon. Specifically, our investigation focuses on the effectiveness of using LLMs to extract vital safety-related information from detailed narrative reports. By converting these data into structured formats, we aim to pinpoint critical human, environmental and equipment-related factors that impact both the occurrence and severity of e-bike incidents. Furthermore, this research endeavors to illustrate how AI-driven data processing can enhance our understanding of the safety risks associated with e-bikes. This enhanced understanding is expected to form a foundational basis for informed policy making and the establishment of improved standards in e-bike design.

II
Related work

This section presents a taxonomy of e-bike safety investigations, categorized by various targeted viewpoints such as aspects related to human behavior, technical limitations of e-bikes themselves, and the surrounding infrastructure.

In-depth studies have been conducted to explore the influence of various human factors, such as age, gender, riding habits, and the type of e-bike used, on the incidence of accidents. These investigations underscore the crucial role human factors play in e-bike accidents. For example, a significant proportion of e-bike accidents involve individuals between 40 and 65 years of age, as shown by the study conducted by
(
WEBER201447
)
. With respect to gender, data from a survey involving 685 e-bike users in Denmark reveal that being female is negatively correlated with perceived safety
(
HAUSTEIN2016386
)
. Furthermore, the use of cell phones while walking or riding significantly increases the risk of road injuries
(
Ren2021
)
. Another research direction seeks to classify e-bikers into distinct categories to examine their impact on e-bike safety, noting that various groups may demonstrate different behaviors. In particular, people tend to favor the e-bike in rural regions over urban ones
(
Hu2021
)
. This geographical variation is attributed to disparities in infrastructure, regulatory frameworks, and cultural practices
(
Hu2021
;
CherryFishman2021
)
. Therefore, taking into account these regional contexts is vital for a comprehensive understanding of the safety implications at the global level. Research on methods to incentivize e-bike users to improve their safety by modifying their riding behavior is essential. Public awareness campaigns and bike safety education are key strategies in this regard. A study assessed the effect of a national helmet promotion campaign in China aimed at riders on motorcycles and electric bikes. The results showed a general increase in helmet use
(
Ning2022HelmetCampaign
)
; however, correct helmet wear practices decreased. The study recommends revising the campaign to incorporate education along with legislative measures to ensure proper helmet usage.

In addition to the influence of human factors, the occurrence of accidents can often be traced back to the technical limitations inherent in e-bikes, rather than their operators. One notable issue is the quality of batteries or chargers, which are often cited as primary causes of incidents such as ignition, explosions, or sparking. These technical failures present significant hazards, including fire, explosion, and burn risks to users
(
Lei2014
)
. Another critical factor in e-bike accidents is the condition and integrity of the tires. Approximately 200 million tires have been reported to be ineffectively discarded each year due to failures predominantly caused by punctures
(
JaffersonSharma2021
)
. Moreover, the braking system is crucial in maintaining the safety of electric bikes, and the braking mechanisms on electric bikes require even more attention than those on traditional bicycles
(
Huertas-Leyva2019
)
. The impact of infrastructure on safety cannot also be overstated. The dimensions of the paths and lanes are vital infrastructural elements that affect the safety of the e-bike. Research indicates that as the width of the paths increases and the surfaces become smoother and flatter, traffic conflicts, including those involving e-bikes, diminish
(
Chen2010TrafficConflict
)
. Furthermore, comparing the risk profiles of e-bikes with those of other transportation methods offers a valuable context to understand e-bike safety. E-bikes tend to have injury severity profiles that align more closely with those of bicycles, rather than with motorcycles
(
Verstappen2021
;
QianShi2023
)
.

Data Classifier

The initial agent evaluates if an incident pertains to an e-bike by examining the narrative descriptions for e-bike-related keywords. Upon detection of such a keyword, the incident is labeled as "yes"; if not, it receives a "no" label. This output is then utilized by the following three agents.

1
Ordered Logit Model

We investigate the connection between severity levels and various incident-related factors using the ordered logit model, which is tailored to examine ordinal dependent variables. These ordinal variables, although ranked, have differences between categories that are neither equal nor precisely known. Our study treats severity level as such an ordinal variable, with 8 sequential categories, each denoting increased severity. Thus, the ordered logit model suits this analysis. Table 2 details the severity levels, from 1 (least severe) to 8 (most severe). The severity classification in this research is derived from CPSRMS descriptions and internal coding, deviating from the conventional KABCOU scale in US transport data
(
FHWA2020
)
. Records with undefined severity (-1) were excluded.

Due to the high proportion (76%) of unspecified race data in the e-bike incident dataset, the race variable was omitted from the analysis as it would likely contribute little variance to the regression model. To better understand the impact of weather, road conditions, and time on severity, these variables were divided into adverse and favorable categories. Weather was labeled as adverse (2) for conditions such as rain, snow, or fog, and favorable (1) otherwise or if unspecified. Road conditions were deemed adverse (2) for wet, icy, or dangerous settings, and favorable (1) for dry or unspecified situations. Similarly, time was considered adverse (2) during evening, night, or midnight, and favorable (1) during day or when unspecified. An ordered logit model was used via the “ologit” command in STATA to evaluate these variables’ effects on severity and their statistical significance.

III-B
2
Likelihood Ratio Test

Given the limited availability of data on weather, road conditions, and time, their inclusion in the model requires a thorough assessment. A likelihood ratio test was used to determine the potential model fit enhancement offered by these variables. This test compares the fit quality of two nested models: a simpler version excluding the target variables and a more detailed version incorporating them.

Step1: Determine the likelihoods: L1 refers to the likelihood of the data in the simpler model, while L2 refers to the likelihood of the data in the more detailed model.

Step2: Calculate the Test Statistic: The test statistic is derived from Equation 2 below:

LR=-2\left(\ln L_{1}-\ln L_{2}\right)

(2)

•

LR
is the statistic of the likelihood ratio test.

•

L_{1}
is the likelihood of the data in the simpler (restricted) model.

•

L_{2}
is the likelihood of the data in the more complex (full) model.

Step 3: Test Statistic Distribution: Assuming the null hypothesis (that the simpler model suffices) holds true, the test statistic is approximately chi-square distributed, with degrees of freedom being the difference in parameter count between the models.

Step 4: Decision Making: Compare the test statistic computed with the critical chi-square value. Reject the null hypothesis if the statistic exceeds the critical value, suggesting that the complex model significantly improves the fit. The test results inform the decision on whether to include or exclude the extracted variables in the final model.

IV
Experiments

This section’s experiments aim to address these research questions: (1) the quantity of e-bike incidents; (2) the demographic characteristics associated with e-bike incidents; (3) the distribution of incidents involving humans, equipment, or both; (4) age and gender-related common injury causes; (5) frequent injury causes related to human, equipment, or both; (6) prevalent severity level by age and gender; (7) common severity level tied to human, equipment, or both; (8) correlation between e-bike incident factors and severity level.

IV-A

Experiment Settings

IV-A
1
Datasets

This study examines data from the Consumer Product Safety Risk Management System (CPSRMS), established by the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) in response to the 2008 Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act (data are available at https://www.cpsc.gov/cgibin/CHQuery/home.aspx). The CPSRMS provides a searchable database of consumer product incident reports to enhance safety and transparency. Its architecture includes a core framework and three main applications: the Consumer/Public Portal, the Industry Partner Portal, and the Incident Management Control Center (IMCC).

The CPSRMS dataset employed in this study compiles incidents from 2017 to 2023 within the United States, detailing each incident’s year, state, description, and demographic data of those affected, like age and gender. The incident descriptions, though informative, are in a narrative text format, which restricts direct retrieval of specifics like weather, road conditions, time, and transportation modes. Consequently, to address our research questions, we must further categorize the dataset in line with each question’s specific goals.

In contrast to traditional transportation safety data like FARS (Fatality Analysis Reporting System
(
NHTSA_FARS
)
) or CRSS (Crash Report Sampling System
(
NHTSA_CRSS
)
), characterized by predefined structured variables, CPSRMS predominantly features narrative incident reports, complicating its immediate use for quantitative analysis. However, its public availability and comprehensive coverage of consumer product incidents, such as e-bikes, constitute a unique yet under-exploited resource for safety studies. The main challenge is to convert these unstructured data into actionable insights, where LLMs have been found to be highly beneficial. The data set includes 1,732 incident reports from 2017 to 2023, with a focus on 544 e-bike-related events.

V
Conclusion

E-bike agents are introduced within a multi-agent framework that translates narrative injury reports into actionable factors linked to human, environmental, and equipment variables, providing insights into their influence on E-bike accident severity. Our findings show that although equipment problems that involve pedals, tires, and brakes are common, human-related incidents are notably more deadly. The ordered logit model confirmed significant impacts of gender, cause, and timing on injury severity. The effects of road conditions were less obvious, but a higher severity in favorable conditions could be due to increased speeds. By merging AI-driven extraction with traditional statistical methods, this approach improves incident understanding and allows for broader safety assessments using unstructured data. Combining automation with expert review achieves high classification accuracy without compromising data integrity. These findings can guide safety campaigns, product improvements, and regulatory standards specific to E-bike risks. Future studies should extend this framework across various datasets and transport modes to further validate its generalizability and impact.

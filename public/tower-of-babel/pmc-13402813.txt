Data-driven risk assessment of hydrothermally altered goafs in deep metal mines via unsupervised typing and nonlinear evolution
Authors: Shuai Li, Jingliang Xue, Zhenyu Dan, Tubing Yin, Jun Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As the “Resource Lifeline” strategy of the China National Gold Group (CNGG) extends to greater depths, its subsidiary mines encounter severe challenges, primarily characterized by tectonic fracturing, intense hydrothermal alteration, and data scarcity. Crucially, traditional empirical stability classifications function as mere “static snapshots” that fail to capture the nonlinear accumulation of hidden hazards, rendering them ineffective for early warning in complex deep environments. To bridge this gap, this study proposes a data-driven stability assessment framework that seamlessly integrates unsupervised structural typing with nonlinear phenomenological evolution. Diverging from reliance on subjective experience, this study first extracts the intrinsic geometric features of 40 typical goafs using K-means clustering to establish an objective structural typology. Subsequently, an Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)-Entropy combined weighting model reveals that hydro-environmental factors account for over 50% of the total risk contribution, highlighting the overwhelming statistical significance of water-rock coupling in driving instability. To quantify the continuous progression from a metastable to a high-risk state, a non-linear dynamic evolution model based on the logistic map is introduced. By incorporating the comprehensive static risk index, this model effectively characterizes the nonlinear damage amplification process. The derived critical threshold (Rc = 0.57) accurately delineates high-risk zones, providing a transparent, quantitative paradigm for prioritizing the remediation of concealed hazards in data-scarce deep mining operations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50767-w.

Introduction

With the gradual depletion of high-quality, easily accessible shallow resources, the core production bases of the CNGG (hereinafter referred to as “the Group”)-including the Qinling (Henan), Wushan (Inner Mongolia), and Tibet mines-have comprehensively transitioned into the deep mining stage1–3.

Compared with other metal mines globally, the geological environment and engineering conditions of the Group’s mines exhibit distinct complexities, primarily manifested in three aspects. First, the occurrence of the ore vein is complex. Most ore bodies manifest as steeply dipping thin or fragile veins, resulting in goafs (mined-out areas) with long, narrow, and irregular morphologies, characterized by large exposed roof areas that are difficult to support. Second, wall rock alteration and tectonic structures are well-developed. Ore bodies typically occur within fault fracture zones where wall rocks have universally undergone intense hydrothermal alterations, such as sericitization and chloritization.

These rock masses are highly susceptible to softening and argillization upon contact with water, exhibiting significant rheological characteristics. Third, historical legacy challenges remain acute. During the Group’s promotion of regional resource integration, numerous undocumented goafs-remnants of early artisanal mining-were incorporated into the official mining boundaries. These voids exhibit chaotic spatial configurations and completely lack historical data, culminating in a vast cluster of “hidden hazard bodies.”

Addressing the practical challenges of “adverse geological conditions and widespread hidden hazards,” existing stability assessment methods face severe limitations in their practical application4. Traditional empirical classification methods (e.g., the Mathews Stability Graph), while computationally simple, are essentially “static snapshots” based on the assumption of homogeneous rock masses5. They often fail to quantitatively account for the chemical weakening effects of groundwater on the intensely altered rock masses specific to the Group’s mines, leading to assessment results that deviate significantly from reality. Numerical simulation methods (e.g., FEM/DEM), although capable of revealing local failure mechanisms, are prohibitively expensive and difficult to implement for mine-wide rapid screening when dealing with legacy goafs characterized by highly irregular morphologies and uncertain geological parameters6.

Facing the dual dilemma of “small samples” and “missing physical and mechanical parameters” prevalent in deep engineering, the international academic community has begun to reflect on the limitations of purely data-driven models and high-fidelity numerical simulations in complex, concealed environments, gradually shifting towards a hybrid assessment paradigm based on the intrinsic structure of engineering data and phenomenological laws. Regarding rock mass structural characterization, researchers have started utilizing dimensionality reduction and unsupervised clustering techniques to mine the statistical distribution patterns of goaf geometric parameters, providing objective mathematical descriptors for the structural typing of undocumented goafs. However, relying solely on static geometric typing still cannot reflect the cumulative process of hazards deteriorating over time or environmental changes. The instability of goafs often exhibits nonlinear acceleration characteristics; however, constrained by the complexity of the micro-constitutive relations of deep, intensely altered rock masses, establishing rigorous physical-mechanical evolution equations directly from first principles is often difficult to implement at engineering sites.

To overcome this, this study proposes a novel goaf risk assessment framework that fuses unsupervised structural cognition with non-linear dynamic evolution modeling. Rather than pursuing generic “big data” mining, this study addresses the status quo of data scarcity in the Group’s mines. Utilizing 40 typical goaf samples, the study employs the K-means clustering algorithm to mine their intrinsic geometric structures characteristic of steeply dipping thin veins. Simultaneously, recognizing that goaf risk accumulation possesses significant nonlinear acceleration features, this study abandons the forced fitting of complex microscopic physical constitutive models and introduces a Logistic nonlinear iterative phenomenological model. This model aims to reveal the evolutionary law of goafs from static risk accumulation to accelerated deterioration in intensely altered, water-rich environments through mathematical mapping. This study aims to provide a closed-loop analysis paradigm integrating unsupervised cognition and risk evolution assessment, solving the Group’s challenge of supervising deep concealed hazard bodies that are currently “invisible, unclear, and unpreventable.”

Methods

This study constructs a data-driven stability evaluation framework tailored for hydrothermally altered, water-rich goafs. It primarily comprises four core modules: Data Preprocessing and Indicator System Construction, Unsupervised Structural Typing, Combined Weighting and Static Risk Evaluation, and a Nonlinear Risk Evolution Model based on the Logistic Map. These modules are organically coupled through unified indicators and state variables: First, a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation based on multi-criteria decision-making provides a static risk index R, serving as a comprehensive statistical indicator to measure the relative vulnerability of the system. Subsequently, by introducing a nonlinear iterative phenomenological model, the mathematical amplification behavior of this risk index under the progression of time or environmental degradation is characterized, thereby identifying the empirical risk threshold for the accelerated deterioration of goafs in this mining area. This establishes a closed-loop evaluation system from static hazard grading to long-term risk evolution assessment (Fig. 1).

As a leading enterprise in China’s gold industry, the Group’s mines span diverse typical geological environments, ranging from tectonic fracture zones, altered rock types, and skarn types to porphyry types7. The accumulation of massive goaf detection and monitoring data provides a solid foundation for stability analysis under multiple working conditions (Table 1).

Integrating engineering experience with data availability, an evaluation system comprising eight indicators was established for subsequent structural typing and risk quantification. The definitions and significance of each indicator are as follows:.

Formation time (years)

This indicator reflects the duration of goaf exposure and the cumulative degree of time-dependent damage, seepage, and softening effects. A longer formation time implies a higher probability of creep, argillization, and structural degradation under identical geological conditions.

Goaf scale

This composite indicator integrates parameters such as length, width, height, and volume to characterize spatial scale and exposed volume. The span directly governs the roof stress state and secondary stress redistribution, serving as a key geometric factor controlling roof stability, while the volume reflects the magnitude of the potential hazard space.

Signs of surface deformation (0/1)

A binary variable, where 1 indicates that the goaf has induced or contributed to surface deformation, and 0 indicates no observable deformation. This indicator characterizes the intensity of the surface response to goaf instability and is crucial for assessing external disaster impacts.

Roof caving degree

Quantified into three levels (0-No caving, 1-Partial caving, 2-Full caving), this indicator reflects the integrity and bearing status of the roof strata. The engineering significance of each level is detailed in Table 2.

Water accumulation status

A binary indicator, where 1 denotes significant water accumulation or direct recharge by groundwater, and 0 indicates a basically dry state or minor seepage. This is the most critical environmental indicator for altered rock mines. Water accumulation significantly weakens wall rock strength through mechanisms such as effective stress reduction, induced pore water pressure, and chemical weakening, making it a primary disaster-inducing factor in strongly altered, water-rich mining areas.

Residual pillars

A binary indicator, where 1 indicates the presence of effective residual pillars or artificial support structures, and 0 indicates a lack of significant pillar support. Pillars serve as key structural units bearing the overburden load; their integrity directly influences stress transmission paths and global stability8,9.

Stability level (1–4)

Based on long-term field experience and safety inspection records, goaf stability is qualitatively evaluated. Levels 1–4 correspond to “Stable, Basically Stable, Unstable, and Extremely Unstable,” respectively. Note that this indicator is excluded from the clustering operation and serves solely as a reference for validating and calibrating model results (Figure2).

Treatment measures (0–4)

Quantified into five levels (ranging from 0-No measures to 4-Backfilling), this indicator reflects the mitigation effect of human intervention on risk. Data preprocessing

To enhance the stability and reliability of the subsequent clustering and evaluation models, systematic preprocessing was performed on the raw data before modeling. This process included missing value imputation, outlier identification and correction, standardization, and the encoding of discrete variables.

Missing value handling

A hierarchical strategy was adopted based on the missing data ratio. For ratios < 5%, mean imputation (for continuous variables) or mode imputation (for categorical variables) was applied; for ratios between 5% and 20%, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) imputation (k = 5) was utilized; for ratios > 20%, the variable or sample would be discarded to avoid systematic bias. In this dataset, the missing rate was < 3%, necessitating no deletion operations.

Numerical encoding of discrete indicators

Discrete indicators such as surface deformation signs, roof caving degree, water accumulation, residual pillars, and treatment measures were encoded using ordinal values with engineering physical significance. This ensures that the encoded values maintain a consistent trend with risk levels or structural states. In the K-means clustering process, only continuous variables highlighting the intrinsic geometric-structural characteristics of the goafs were selected for computation to prevent human interventions (e.g., treatment measures) from directly biasing the structural typing results; discrete variables were subsequently incorporated into the risk evaluation and mechanism analysis10.

Unsupervised structural typing

In deep mining engineering, “data sparsity” is an unavoidable challenge. For the high-quality, small-sample dataset in this study, high-complexity deep models face extreme overfitting risks. Therefore, this study adopts the K-means algorithm, known for its low bias and strong geometric interpretability, to identify structural heterogeneity11.

(1) Feature construction: Based on goaf geometric attributes and spatial morphological features, a subset of continuous indicators covering core parameters like span and volume was selected to construct feature vectors\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:{x}_{i}.$$\end{document} These standardized features form an n-dimensional feature space used to characterize the intrinsic structural differences among goafs. To avoid the “curse of dimensionality,” the number of features was controlled within a moderate range relative to the sample size.

It is important to clarify the rationale behind the selection of clustering variables. Engineering interventions (e.g., treatment measures and backfilling) were deliberately excluded from the geometric feature space. This decision was made to isolate the inherent spatial vulnerability of the goafs prior to human modification, ensuring that the structural typing reflects the natural hazard potential rather than post-mining support states. Furthermore, while fine morphological indicators such as roof curvature are theoretically valuable for stability analysis, they were not included in this study. This is due to the extreme technical limitations of conducting high-resolution 3D laser scanning in deep, water-filled, and completely concealed legacy goafs. Therefore, macroscopic indicators like span and volume were selected as the most reliable and accessible geometric descriptors under current deep-mining conditions. From a pragmatic engineering perspective, these fundamental scale parameters are entirely sufficient to support the initial risk triage of massive hidden hazard bodies, effectively fulfilling the primary objective of macroscopic structural typing.

(2) Algorithm parameter setting and initialization basis: Addressing the typical “small sample” characteristics of deep engineering data, to avoid clustering results falling into local optima and to enhance global stability, strict settings were applied to the algorithm parameter selection: First, the Euclidean distance was chosen as the similarity metric within the standardized feature space to ensure the isotropy of continuous geometric variables (span, volume) weights in multi-dimensional space. Second, the traditional random initialization method was discarded in favor of the K-means + + algorithm for selecting initial cluster centers. This mechanism ensures, through probability distribution, that the initial centroids are as far apart as possible in the feature space, significantly enhancing the convergence speed and the robustness of the clustering boundaries under small-sample datasets.

Non-linear dynamic evolution model

The static risk index R(0 ≤ R ≤ 1) derived from the AHP-Entropy combined model and fuzzy comprehensive evaluation is essentially a dimensionless composite indicator that integrates heterogeneous variables (e.g., discrete caving grade encodings, binary hydrological status, and standardized geometric dimensions). It reflects the relative vulnerability of the goaf under multi-factor coupling, rather than a true physical damage variable derived from continuum thermodynamics13. To address any potential conceptual ambiguities, the static risk index R derived from the AHP-Entropy combined model is strictly defined as a macroscopic statistical indicator for engineering triage, rather than a physical state variable or thermodynamic order parameter. The weighting scheme does not aim to replace rigorous mechanical constitutive equations14 but serves as a practical, data-driven tool to fuse site-specific engineering heuristics with objective data variance under small-sample conditions.

Considering that the instability of deep goafs often exhibits a nonlinear characteristic of slow initial accumulation followed by accelerated deterioration4,15, and because obtaining microscopic constitutive parameters for hydrothermally altered goafs is exceedingly difficult, deriving a strict physical-mechanical evolution equation from first principles is practically unfeasible in engineering. Although the aforementioned typing results possess clear physical significance in terms of geometric features, the sample size (N = 40) is relatively limited due to the difficulty of data acquisition in deep concealed engineering. To eliminate random interference and ensure that the structural typing is not an accidental product dominated by individual samples, this study introduced Bootstrap resampling and Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) for rigorous statistical robustness testing.

First, the Bootstrap method was employed to perform 1,000 iterations of resampling with replacement and K-means clustering on the original dataset. Figure 3(c) displays the distribution of the three structural centroids in the feature space (Span-Volume). The results show that the three centroids form three distinct, internally compact clusters. The 95% confidence ellipses do not overlap, and the separation is particularly high between Class I (Stable, Green) and Class III (High-Risk, Red). This indicates that even with significant data perturbation, the three identified structural patterns remain highly stable.

Second, to quantify the influence of individual samples on the overall typing structure, the ARI was calculated using the Leave-One-Out method. By sequentially removing one sample and comparing the clustering result of the remaining (N-1) samples with the baseline result, an average ARI of 0.86 (standard deviation 0.25) was obtained. This value is far above the statistically recognized threshold for “excellent stability” (ARI > 0.80), with the majority of iterations yielding ARI values close to 1.0. This further confirms that the identified structural types are global robust features of the dataset rather than being determined by outliers or specific subsets.

Dynamic evolution and critical thresholds

Empirical Threshold Determination and Parameter Calibration: To reveal the evolutionary path of goafs from “static characteristics” to “accelerated deterioration,” the aforementioned static risk value R was taken as the initial state R0 and substituted into the iterative equation. This study conducted a rigorous quantitative iterative calibration for the control parameter λ. The specific calculation process was: the initial search interval for λ was set to [1.5,3.0] with an iteration step of 0.01. The objective function was to minimize the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) between the final convergent/divergent state of the model’s evolution curve and the actual instability performance of the samples in the mine’s hazard ledger. The fitting results showed that when the evolution coefficient is λ = 2.30, the model achieved the optimal goodness-of-fit, with a coefficient of determination reaching R2 = 0.88 and a RMSE = 0.062. Therefore, the representative evolution coefficient for the goaf system in this mining area was determined to be λ = 2.30. According to the bifurcation characteristics analysis of the logistic map, the median empirical critical risk threshold for this mining area system was derived as 0.57 (Figure 6).

Parameter Sensitivity and Confidence Interval Analysis: To further verify the statistical robustness of this empirical threshold, a parameter sensitivity test was conducted on the evolution coefficient λ. λ was set to fluctuate by ± 10% around the optimal fitting baseline value (i.e., λ ∈ [2.07,2.53]) to simulate the potential random uncertainties during deep geological environmental changes and the multi-criteria evaluation process. The calculation results showed that as λ fluctuated, the critical threshold Rc for the system’s nonlinear divergence varied accordingly, with its 95% Confidence Interval falling within the range of [0.52,0.61], presenting a narrow uncertainty band. This demonstrates that despite certain data fluctuations, the evolutionary boundary point distinguishing high-risk from low-risk states remains stable around 0.57. Therefore, selecting c = 0.57 as the representative threshold criterion for determining whether the system enters a nonlinear amplification state possesses a strong statistical basis (Figure 7).

Physical interpretability and mechanisms

(1) Intrinsic coupling between Although the structural typing (derived from unsupervised K-means clustering) and the risk classification (derived from multi-indicator comprehensive evaluation) are based on distinct mathematical logics, they exhibit significant physical consistency (Figure 8).

Fig. 8Coupling mechanism between unsupervised structural types and risk levels.

Coupling mechanism between unsupervised structural types and risk levels.

Class I (Small-Scale) overlaps with the low-risk level (Level I) by over 90%. This confirms that within limited exposure spaces, the “arching effect” of the wall rock can function effectively, and the geometric stiffness of the structure itself dominates the system’s self-stabilizing capacity18. Class III (Large-Scale) encompasses all extreme risk (Level IV) samples. This indicates that when the goaf span breaks through a specific boundary, the intensity of wall rock stress redistribution undergoes a qualitative change, causing the system to enter a highly unstable, high-risk state19. This strong “geometry-risk” coupling proves that the K-means typing accurately captures the macroscopic engineering attributes of the goafs.

(2) Statistical dominance of hydro-physical action under multi-factor coupling and empirical evidence.

The combined weighting model identified “Empty Area Water Accumulation” as the highest individual weight factor (0.293), which, together with signs of surface deformation, accounts for 52.6% of the weight. In the mathematical framework of multi-criteria decision-making, this indicates that this variable plays a decisive role statistically in distinguishing between stable and unstable goafs.

To further provide empirical support for the microscopic mechanical mechanism and respond to the high weight allocation given by the model, this study integrated and referenced laboratory mechanical immersion test results of typical hydrothermally altered wall rocks (rich in secondary minerals such as sericite and chlorite) from the study area20. To explicitly quantify this micro-mechanical degradation, experimental testing was conducted on typical hydrothermally altered rock specimens (rich in sericite and chlorite) from the study area. As illustrated in Fig. 9, the transition of rock samples from a dry to a fully saturated state triggers a significant nonlinear exponential decay in macroscopic bearing capacity21,22. Figure 9(a) reveals that the average Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS) of the dry specimens reaches 103.72 MPa, whereas under saturated conditions, the strength plummets to approximately 33.90 MPa - a drastic reduction exceeding 67%. Concurrently, the shear resistance is severely compromised; Fig. 9(b) demonstrates that the internal cohesion drops sharply from 17.51 MPa to a mere 5.74 MPa. This pronounced non-linear attenuation directly visualizes the severe mechanical deterioration induced by hydro-softening23.

Experimental curves illustrating the hydro-chemical weakening effect on altered rock specimens: (a) Nonlinear degradation of UCS from dry to saturated state; (b) Exponential attenuation of shear cohesion under varying moisture degrees.

This microscopic experimental data confirms that the hydration swelling and dissolution of cementing materials, triggered by water molecules wedging into the crystal lattices of hydrophilic phyllosilicate minerals, indeed cause a dual physical-chemical weakening of the rock strength23,24. This empirical regularity achieves a logical closed-loop with the macro-conclusion derived via the AHP-Entropy method that “the water accumulation factor has the highest statistical weight.” However, since the remaining ~ 50% of the weight is distributed among factors like geometric scale and pillar support, it indicates that the ultimate instability of the goaf remains the comprehensive result of complex interactions among multiple factors, including the hydrological environment, geometric exposure, and engineering interventions. By introducing the nonlinear iterative model, this study further quantified the evolutionary trend of such hydro-physically dominated risk indices as the environment deteriorates, and its calibrated goodness-of-fit reached R2 = 0.88, providing strong support for the above analysis.

(3) Internal consistency and statistical limitations of the empirical threshold.

This study derived an empirical critical threshold of Rc ≈ 0.57 both when determining the static risk grading boundary and when inverting the phenomenological evolution model parameters. It must be explicitly pointed out here that these two analysis processes are not mutually independent. Because the exact same hidden hazard ledger records and goaf classification standards were used when calibrating the evolution coefficient λ as were used to determine the static threshold, the numerical convergence of these two thresholds is essentially the inevitable result of fitting the static classification rules and the nonlinear mathematical model to the same dataset. This phenomenon demonstrates that the constructed ‘static grading-risk evolution’ evaluation framework exhibits rigorous internal consistency and mathematical self-consistency. However, since the exact same hidden hazard ledger records were utilized for both the static threshold determination and the parameter calibration, the numerical convergence of the thresholds primarily reflects the framework’s optimal fit to the specific geological conditions of the current dataset. Therefore, treating this convergence as an independent validation of universal predictive capacity requires caution. To firmly establish the generalized predictive validity of the critical threshold Rc = 0.57, future research must employ more rigorous methodologies, specifically temporal sequence forecasting and spatiotemporal cross-validation across distinct mining regions.

Furthermore, constrained by the extreme difficulty of acquiring deep concealed engineering data, the sample size in this study is relatively small (N = 40), among which high-risk samples (Levels III and IV combined) account for only 3. Although the previous Bootstrap analysis confirmed the statistical robustness of the cluster centers, the current statistical testing power remains limited for identifying a significant threshold with universal predictive value.

Addressing these limitations, in future research, more rigorous validation methods must be employed to establish the actual predictive value of this threshold: one is to conduct temporal sequence forecasting validation, i.e., using data from one period to predict goaf instability in a subsequent period; the second is to introduce spatial cross-validation, training the model on data from certain mining areas and conducting independent blind tests on other mining areas with differing geographical and geological conditions25.

(4) Spatial correlation characterization of deep and shallow disasters.

The high weight of “Signs of Surface Deformation” (0.231) reveals the spatial correlation between deep and shallow disasters. In the mining of steeply dipping thin veins, roof caving is not confined to the deep underground but propagates upward along high-angle joints or weak tectonic zones. Tensile cracks appearing on the surface are not isolated phenomena but are macroscopic responses to the accumulation of damage and the breakthrough of wall rock fractures from underground deep goaf groups to the surface26. This finding confirms that, in the absence of precise underground monitoring data, surface response serves as the most reliable external indicator for identifying the transition of a deep system from a metastable state to an accelerated deterioration state.

Conclusion

Targeting the critical engineering challenge of governing hidden goaf hazards in deep metal mining, this study proposes a novel static classification and long-term risk assessment paradigm that integrates “unsupervised structural typing,” “combined weighting fuzzy evaluation,” and “nonlinear phenomenological evolution.” The main innovations and conclusions are as follows:

Supplementary laboratory water content-mechanical strength degradation tests further confirmed the microscopic softening mechanism, wherein the UCS of typical hydrothermally altered rocks drops sharply by 40%−60% upon water contact. Through high-precision parameter iterative optimization (R²=0.88 and RMSE = 0.062), the empirical critical risk threshold for the accelerated deterioration of the system in this mining area was identified as approximately 0.57.

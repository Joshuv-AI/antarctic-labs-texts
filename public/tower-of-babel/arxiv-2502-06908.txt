Volterra map and related recurrences
Andrei K. Svinin

Abstract
. In this paper we analyze recent work [ 10 ] by Hone, Roberts and Vanhaecke, where the so-called Volterra map was introduced via the Lax equation that looks similar to the Lax representation for the Mumford’s system [ 21 ] . This map turns out to be birational and a corresponding dynamical system on an affine space M g M_{g} of dimension 3 ​ g + 1 3g+1 was associated with it. This mapping is related to some discrete equation of the order 2 ​ g + 1 2g+1 associated with the Stieltjes continued fraction expansion of a certain function on a hyperelliptic (elliptic) curve of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 . The authors of the paper provides examples of this equation for the simplest cases g = 1 g=1 and g = 2 g=2 , but for higher values of g g , corresponding equation turns out to be too cumbersome to write them out. We present an approach in which the mentioned ( 2 ​ g + 1 ) (2g+1) -order equation can be written out for all values of g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 in a compact form. This equation is not new and can be found, for example, in [ 18 ] . An essential point in our framework is the use of special class of discrete polynomials which as shown to be closely related to the Stieltjes continued fraction. On the one hand, this allows us to generalize some of the results of the work [ 10 ] . On the other hand, many things in this approach can be presented in a more compact and unified form. Ultimately, we believe that this allows us to give a new perspective on this topic.

Introduction

This work was inspired by a recent work
[

The authors of the paper showed that the Volterra map is birational and therefore can be iterated in both directions. As a result of an infinite number of iterations, it arises a discrete dynamical system on the affine space
M_{g}
of dimension
3g+1
. It should be noted that definition of the Volterra map using the traceless matrix

is very reminiscent of Mumford’s design
[

]
,
[

2.
Volterra map

2.1.
Definition

Let us consider a triple of polynomials

P(x)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{g}p_{j}x^{j},\;\;Q(x)=2+\sum_{j=1}^{g}q_{j}x^{j},\;\;R(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{g+1}r_{j}x^{j}

(2.1)

with coefficients belonging to some field, say
\mathbb{C}
, and corresponding traceless Lax matrix

L(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P(x)&R(x)\\
Q(x)&-P(x)\end{array}\right).

Let
M_{g}\cong\mathbb{C}^{3g+1}
to be an affine space of dimension
3g+1
whose points are determined by the coordinates
\xi=\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{g},q_{1},\ldots,q_{g},r_{1},\ldots,r_{g+1}\right)
. The first thing we would like to do is to define, as in
[
10
]
, some mapping
\mathcal{V}_{g}:M_{g}\mapsto M_{g}
, which turns out to be birational. As in the paper
[
10
]
, we call it the Volterra map. Perhaps the most convenient way to do this is the Lax equation representation. Let us define a linear combination

u=p_{1}-\frac{q_{1}}{2}+\frac{r_{1}}{2}

(2.2)

and write down the matrix relation

L(x)M(x)=M(x)\tilde{L}(x)

(2.3)

with

M(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&ux\\
1&0\end{array}\right).

As one can see, (
2.3
) implies three relations

\tilde{P}(x)=Q(x)-P(x),\;\;\tilde{Q}(x)=\frac{2P(x)-Q(x)+R(x)}{ux},\;\;\tilde{R}(x)=uxQ(x).

(2.4)

As in
[
10
]
, we call the mapping
(P,Q,R)\mapsto(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q},\tilde{R})
the Volterra map and denote it by
\mathcal{V}_{g}
. Of course, we immediately pay attention to the fact that if it turns out that
u
given by (
2.2
) is zero, then the Volterra map is not defined. Let us assume that for given triple
(P,Q,R)
one has
u\neq 0
. Then it is easy to verify that in this case three relations in (
2.4
) are correctly defined and therefore indeed define some mapping.

Evidently, an inverse mapping to (
2.4
) is represented by the formulas

P(x)=\frac{\tilde{R}(x)}{ux}-\tilde{P}(x),\;\;Q(x)=\frac{\tilde{R}(x)}{ux},\;\;R(x)=ux\tilde{Q}(x)-\frac{\tilde{R}(x)}{ux}+2\tilde{P}(x).

(2.5)

Since
u=\tilde{r}_{1}/2
, we see that (
2.5
) is well defined and we therefore conclude that the Volterra mapping is indeed birational.
Let us emphasize once again that the Volterra map is defined only if
u\neq 0
. In this case, the triple
(P,Q,R)
is said to be regular. Well, let this triple be regular, but we cannot be sure that triple
(\tilde{P},\tilde{Q},\tilde{R})
is also regular. To our knowledge, there is no known condition for given triple
(P,Q,R)
that would guarantee the regularity of all triples obtained by repeated iterations of the Volterra map.

2.2.
Invariants

Given a triple of polynomials
(P,Q,R)
, let us define a polynomial

f(x)=-\det L(x)=P^{2}(x)+Q(x)R(x)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{2g+1}c_{j}x^{j}.

(2.6)

We use the same notation
c_{k}
for the invariant and its numerical value in the hope that this will not lead to misunderstanding.

From the Lax equation (
2.3
), it immediately follows that the finite set of coefficients
\left(c_{1},\ldots,c_{2g+1}\right)
represents invariants for the Volterra map. Taking into account (
2.6
), we may write

c_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}p_{j}p_{k-j}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{j}r_{k-j}

(2.7)

with understanding that
p_{0}=1,\;q_{0}=2
and
p_{j}=q_{j}=r_{j+1}=0
for all
j\geq g+1
.

2.3.
Stieltjes continued fraction

Let us consider algebraic function
y=y(x)
defined by the relation

y^{2}=f(x),

(2.8)

where the polynomial
f(x)
is given by (
2.6
). We choose the sign of the solution of (
2.8
) so that the series expansion for the function
y(x)
has the form

y=1+\sum_{j\geq 1}H_{j}x^{j}
, where the coefficients
H_{j}
are defined to be some polynomials in coordinates of the point of the phase-space
M_{g}
. It should be noted that, due to (
2.8
), the
H_{k}
’s are also can be considered as polynomials in the coefficients
c_{k}
.
For example,

H_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{2},\;\;H_{2}=\frac{c_{2}}{2}-\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{8},\;\;H_{3}=\frac{c_{3}}{2}-\frac{c_{1}c_{2}}{4}+\frac{c_{1}^{3}}{16},\ldots

(2.9)

The proof of the following lemma can be found in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Volterra map
\mathcal{V}_{g}
given by the Lax equation (
2.3
) implies the relation

\left(y+P\right)\left(y+\tilde{P}\right)=Q\left(y+\tilde{P}\right)+uxQ\tilde{Q},

(2.10)

where
u
is given by (
2.2
).

Let us rewrite (
2.10
), although in a more cumbersome, but on the other hand more meaningful form

\displaystyle\frac{y+P}{Q}=1+\frac{ux}{\displaystyle\frac{y+\tilde{P}}{\tilde{Q}}}.

(2.11)

It is clear that we can infinitely continue the fraction on the right side of the relation (
2.11
) to get

\frac{y+P}{Q}=1+\frac{u_{0}x}{1+\displaystyle{\frac{u_{1}x}{1+\displaystyle{\frac{u_{2}x}{1+\cdots}}}}},

(2.12)

where
u_{0}=u,\;u_{1}=\tilde{u},u_{2}=\tilde{\tilde{u}},\ldots
As a result we get the Stieltjes continued fraction representation
[
15
]
for the series expansion of the rational function
\left(y+P\right)/Q
defined on the curve
y^{2}=f(x)
that follows from the characteristic equation
\det\left(L(x)-y\right)=0
. For example, we have

u_{1}=\frac{r_{1}+q_{1}}{2}-\frac{2p_{2}+r_{2}-q_{2}}{2p_{1}+r_{1}-q_{1}}.

If
g=1
, we must put
p_{2}=q_{2}=0
. Of course expressions for
u_{2},u_{3}
and so on are very cumbersome and there is no point in writing them out. It is only important to note that, for all
n\geq 0
, there exists unambiguous expression
u_{n}=R_{n}(\xi)
, with
R_{n}
being some rational function of coordinates of a point in phase space
M_{g}
.

2.4.
Dynamical system

Since the Volterra map is birational we are able to iterate it in both directions. By doing so, we get a bi-infinite sequence of points of the phase space
M_{g}
with coordinates
\xi_{n}=\left(p_{n,1},\ldots,p_{n,g},q_{n,1},\ldots,q_{n,g},r_{n,1},\ldots,r_{n,g+1}\right)
uniquely determined by the Lax equation

L_{n}(x)M_{n}(x)=M_{n}(x)L_{n+1}(x)

(2.13)

with

L_{n}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P_{n}(x)&R_{n}(x)\\
Q_{n}(x)&-P_{n}(x)\end{array}\right)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;M_{n}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&u_{n}x\\
1&0\end{array}\right),

where

u_{n}=p_{n,1}-\frac{q_{n,1}}{2}+\frac{r_{n,1}}{2},

(2.14)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Describing (
2.13
) in more detail, we get the relations

P_{n+1}(x)=Q_{n}(x)-P_{n}(x),\;\;Q_{n+1}(x)=\frac{2P_{n}(x)-Q_{n}(x)+R_{n}(x)}{u_{n}x},\;\;

R_{n+1}(x)=u_{n}xQ_{n}(x).

(2.15)

Let us also rewrite, for convenience, the relations (
2.15
) in terms of coordinates. We have

p_{n+1,k}=q_{n,k}-p_{n,k},\;\;q_{n+1,k}=\frac{2p_{n,k+1}-q_{n,k+1}+r_{n,k+1}}{u_{n}},\;\;r_{n+1,k}=u_{n}q_{n,k-1}.

(2.16)

In conclusion of this section let us present the Theorem 3.8 from the work
[
10
]
. Let

\frac{y+P(x)}{Q(x)}=1-\mathcal{H}(x),\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\mathcal{H}(x)=\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j}h_{j}x^{j}.

Substituting
y=-P+\left(1-\mathcal{H}\right)Q
into the relation
y^{2}=P^{2}+QR
, we derive the generating relation

2P-Q+R+2\left(Q-P\right)\mathcal{H}-Q\mathcal{H}^{2}=0.

An analysis of this relationship leads to the theorem that can be found in
[
10
]
. However, we formulate it a bit in our own way

Theorem 2.2
.

The generating relation (
5.7
) implies an infinite number of equations

h_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{g}(-1)^{j}\left(p_{j}-q_{j}\right)h_{n-j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{g}(-1)^{k}q_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k-1}h_{j}h_{n-j-k},\;\;n\geq g+2,

where the set of the initial values
\left(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{g+1}\right)
is related by a birational mapping
\left(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{g+1}\right)\leftrightarrow\left(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{g+1}\right)
depending on the coefficients
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
.

3.
Preliminary calculations

Theoretically, the following is embedded in the formulas (
2.10
) and (
2.12
). Let us define an initial point
\xi
of the phase space
M_{g}
by specifying a triple
(P,Q,R)
. The formula (
2.12
) provides us all iterates
u_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. We would like to discuss in detail the nontrivial fact that the system of equations (
2.16
) is birationally related to some polynomial recurrence

P(u_{n},\ldots,u_{n+2g+1};c_{1},\ldots,c_{g})=0,

(3.1)

where
u_{n}
is defined by (
2.14
).

Before we tackle the general case, let us first look at the simplest examples. In the case
g=1
the system of equations (
2.16
) become

p_{n+1,1}=q_{n,1}-p_{n,1},\;\;q_{n+1,1}=\frac{r_{n,2}}{u_{n}},\;\;r_{n+1,1}=2u_{n},\;\;r_{n+1,2}=u_{n}q_{n,1}.

(3.2)

From the second and fourth equations in (
3.2
), it follows the relation
u_{n+1}q_{n+1,1}=u_{n+2}q_{n+3,1}
which, in turn, using the first relation, can be rewritten as

u_{n+1}\left(p_{n+1,1}+p_{n+2,1}\right)=u_{n+2}\left(p_{n+3,1}+p_{n+4,1}\right).

(3.3)

Since
c_{1}=2p_{n,1}+2r_{n,1}
and
r_{n,1}=2u_{n-1}
then
p_{n,1}=-2u_{n-1}+c_{1}/2
. Substituting the latter into (
3.3
), as a result, we get the recursion

u_{n+1}\left(u_{n}+u_{n+1}-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\right)=u_{n+2}\left(u_{n+2}+u_{n+3}-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\right),

(3.4)

which is a special case of the relation (
3.1
).

Also in the work
[
10
]
, for the case
g=2
, a fifth-order equation was written out, which looks more cumbersome than (
3.4
). It was also shown that for any
g\geq 1
there exists an equation of order
2g+1
which is satisfied by the sequence
(u_{n})
. As
g
grows, this equation looks even more cumbersome if we try to write it explicitly. One of the results of our work which we formulate further in the form of a theorem is that this equation can be briefly written in terms of some discrete polynomials the definition of which we will give in the next section.

We will consider relations (
2.16
) together with constraints

c_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}p_{n,j}p_{n,k-j}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{n,j}r_{n,k-j}

assuming that
g
is large enough so as not to worry about having to zero something out. To illustrate the general approach, we are going to do some preliminary calculations, the results of which lead us to think that some special discrete polynomials constantly appear.
More precisely we would like to show how
(p_{n,k},q_{n,k},r_{n,k})
is expressed through the variables
u_{n}
and
c_{k}
.

Since
c_{1}=2p_{n,1}+2r_{n,1}
and
r_{n,1}=2u_{n-1}
then

p_{n,1}=-2u_{n-1}+\frac{c_{1}}{2}=-2S^{1}_{1}(n-1)+H_{1}

and

q_{n,1}=p_{n,1}+p_{n+1,1}=-2\left(u_{n-1}+u_{n}\right)+c_{1}=-2\left(S^{1}_{2}(n-1)-H_{1}\right),

where
H_{1}=c_{1}/2
. We use the notations like
S^{1}_{1}(n)=u_{n}
and
S^{1}_{2}(n)=u_{n}+u_{n+1}
which seems redundant for now, however, later we will show what meaning these designations have.
Let us now consider the relation

c_{2}=2p_{n,2}+p_{n,1}^{2}+2r_{n,2}+q_{n,1}r_{n,1},

(3.5)

where

r_{n,2}=u_{n-1}q_{n-1,1}=-2u_{n-1}\left(S^{1}_{2}(n-2)-H_{1}\right).

Solving (
3.5
) in favor of
p_{n,2}
, we get

p_{n,2}=2S_{2}^{2}(n-2)-2H_{1}S_{1}^{1}(n-1)+H_{2},

(3.6)

where we have denoted

\displaystyle S^{2}_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n+1}\left(u_{n}+u_{n+1}\right)+u_{n+2}u_{n+1}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n+1}S^{1}_{2}(n)+u_{n+2}S^{1}_{1}(n+1).

In addition we calculate

\displaystyle q_{n,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{n,2}+p_{n+1,2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(S^{2}_{2}(n-2)+S^{2}_{2}(n-1)\right)-2H_{1}\left(S^{1}_{1}(n-1)+S^{1}_{1}(n)\right)+2H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(S^{2}_{3}(n-2)-H_{1}S^{1}_{2}(n-1)+H_{2}\right),

where
H_{2}=c_{2}/2-c_{1}^{2}/8
and

S^{2}_{3}(n)=u_{n+1}S^{1}_{3}(n)+u_{n+2}S^{1}_{2}(n+1)+u_{n+3}S^{1}_{1}(n+2).

(3.9)

Note also that easily verifiable identities

S^{1}_{1}(n)+S^{1}_{1}(n+1)=S^{1}_{2}(n)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;S^{2}_{2}(n)+S^{2}_{2}(n+1)=S^{2}_{3}(n)

are used to get (

4.3.
Stiltjes continued fraction

Given an arbitrary sequence
(u_{n})
, let us consider the corresponding Stiltjes continued fraction

F_{n}(x)=1+\frac{u_{n}x}{1+\displaystyle{\frac{u_{n+1}x}{1+\displaystyle{\frac{u_{n+2}x}{1+\cdots}}}}}

(4.17)

and then define discrete polynomials
(h_{n,j})
by the generating relation

F_{n}(x)=1-\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j}h_{n,j}x^{j}.

The first few coefficients
h_{n,j}
look as

h_{n,1}=u_{n},\;\;h_{n,2}=u_{n}u_{n+1},\;\;h_{n,3}=u_{n}u_{n+1}\left(u_{n+1}+u_{n+2}\right),\;\;

h_{n,4}=u_{n}u_{n+1}\left((u_{n+1}+u_{n+2})^{2}+u_{n+2}u_{n+3}\right),\ldots

The coefficient
h_{n,k}
, for any
k\geq 1
, is a polynomial in variables
\left(u_{n},\ldots,u_{n+k-1}\right)
, but it is important to note that it depends linearly on the variable
u_{n+k-1}
.
This means that

, for any

, can be expressed unambiguously as a rational function of the variables
\left(h_{n,1},\ldots,h_{n,k}\right)
.

By definition, the sequence
(F_{n})
satisfies the recurrence relation

F_{n}F_{n+1}=F_{n+1}+u_{n}x.

(4.18)

The following proposition relates the polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
to the coefficients of the Stiltes continued fraction (
4.17

Lemma 4.6
.

The relation

\mathcal{S}_{s,n}=F_{n+s+1}\mathcal{S}_{s+1,n},\;\;\forall s\geq 0

where the formal power series
\mathcal{S}_{s,n}
is defined by (
4.9

Proof.

Let us define a sequence the formal power series
(F_{s,n})_{s\geq 0}
, by

F_{s,n}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{s,n}}{\mathcal{S}_{s+1,n}},\;\;\forall s\geq 0

By virtue the second relation in (

) we get the relation

F_{s,n}F_{s+1,n}=F_{s+1,n}+u_{n+s+1}x.

Let us define

Substituting the latter into (

depends only on

The goal of this section is to write explicit formulas that relate the system of equations (

5.1.
Main theorem

Let us consider a triple of infinite formal power series
(P_{n},Q_{n},R_{n})
defined by (

). Their coefficients are determined by (
4.33
), (
4.34
) and (
4.35
).
Let us suppose that

p_{n,k}=0,\;\;\forall k\geq g+1

(5.1)

for some

. We observe that, by virtue of (
4.34
) and (
4.35
), truncation only the series

in fact entails truncation the series

and

in such a way that they become polynomials in

of the form (

). As a result, the restriction (
5.1
) forces the sequence
(u_{n})
to be a solution to some equation, which we will write out below. Indeed, from the second and third relation in (
2.16
), it follows that

q_{n+1,g}=\frac{r_{n,g+1}}{u_{n}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;r_{n+1,g+1}=u_{n}q_{n,g}

and as a consequence we get the relation

u_{n+g}q_{n+g,g}=u_{n+g+1}q_{n+g+2,g}.

(5.2)

Finally, taking into account (
4.34
), we can rewrite (
5.2
) as the equation

u_{n+g}\hat{S}^{g}_{g+1}(n)=u_{n+g+1}\hat{S}^{g}_{g+1}(n+2),

(5.3)

where, let us remind ourselves, we use the notation (
4.36
). This is actually the polynomial recursion (
3.1
) we wanted to derive. Next we will show that (

) represents a known equation and of course some facts are known for it.

If we look at the explicit expression (
4.4
) for the polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
, we see that the relation (
5.3
) is a discrete equation of order
2g+1
. Fortunately, the variable
u_{n+2g+1}
enters the relation (
5.3
) linearly and thus we can, if necessary, resolve this relation with respect to this variable to rewrite it as

u_{n+2g+1}=R(u_{n},\ldots,u_{n+2g};H_{1},\ldots,H_{g}),

where

is some rational function of its arguments although it is clear that in this form this equation does not look so nice as (
5.3
) does. Any sequence satisfying (
5.3
) is uniquely determined by a finite set
(u_{0},\ldots,u_{2g},H_{1},\ldots,H_{g})\in\mathbb{C}^{3g+1}
, that is, the number of parameters required to uniquely determine the solution in this case is equal to
3g+1
, that is, this number is equal to the dimension of the phase space
M_{g}
on which the Volterra map is defined.
The difference between desired recursion (
3.1
) and the relation (
5.3
) is only that there are parameters
\left(c_{1},\ldots,c_{g}\right)
in (
3.1
), however in (
5.3
) one sees the parameters
\left(H_{1},\ldots,H_{g}\right)
. But this is not a problem, because, as we already said, these parameters are related to each other by a birational transformation (
2.9
).

Let us formulate the main result of this paper in the form of the following theorem:

Theorem 5.1
.

Given any solution of the system of equations (
2.16
) generated by the Volterra map
\mathcal{V}_{g}
, the sequence
(u_{n})
, where
u_{n}=p_{n,1}-q_{n,1}/2+r_{n,1}/2
satisfies equation (

) with parameters
\left(H_{1},\ldots,H_{g}\right)
related birationally to the invariants
\left(c_{1},\ldots,c_{g}\right)
by the generating relation

\left(1+\sum_{j\geq 1}H_{j}x^{j}\right)^{2}=1+\sum_{j\geq 1}^{2g+1}c_{j}x^{j}.

(5.4)

5.2.

In addition to Theorem

, we note the following. Formulas (

7.1.
On discrete polynomials

In this work we mainly use discrete polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
, but their companions
T^{k}_{s}(n)
can also be used in some situations.
It is known that polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
and
T^{k}_{s}(n)
are connected to each other via the identities
[
16
]

S^{k}_{s}(n)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}S^{k-j}_{s}(n+j)T^{j}_{s}(n)+(-1)^{k}T^{k}_{s}(n)=0

and

T^{k}_{s}(n)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}T^{k-j}_{s}(n+j)S^{j}_{s}(n)+(-1)^{k}S^{k}_{s}(n)=0,\;\;k,s\geq 1.

Perhaps discrete polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
and
T^{k}_{s}(n)
should be understood as a kind of analogue of the complete and elementary symmetric polynomials
h_{k}
and
e_{k}
, respectively, that satisfy an identity

e_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}e_{k-j}h_{j}+(-1)^{k}h_{k}=0,\;\;\forall k\geq 1.

It is known that symmetric polynomials satisfy many identities and it might be interesting to search for analogues of these identities for discrete polynomials
S^{k}_{s}(n)
and
T^{k}_{s}(n)
. Well, as an example, in
[
18
]
the identities

S^{k}_{s}(n)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{K}(-1)^{p}T^{k_{1}}_{s}(n)T^{k_{2}}_{s}(n+k_{1})\cdots T^{k_{p}}_{s}(n+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p-1})

and

T^{k}_{s}(n)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{K}(-1)^{p}S^{k_{1}}_{s}(n)S^{k_{2}}_{s}(n+k_{1})\cdots S^{k_{p}}_{s}(n+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{p-1}),

for all
s\geq 1,
has been presented. The summation in these two formulas is performed over all compositions
K=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{p})
of number
k
with
k_{j}\geq 1
. For instance, in the case
k=2
, we have the identities

S^{2}_{s}(n)=-T^{2}_{s}(n)+T^{1}_{s}(n)T^{1}_{s}(n+1)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;T^{2}_{s}(n)=-S^{2}_{s}(n)+S^{1}_{s}(n)S^{1}_{s}(n+1).

We also note that in
[
16
]
broader classes of discrete polynomials were actually defined and used. For instance, given any natural number
q
, let

S^{k}_{s}(n)=\sum_{0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq s-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}u_{n+\lambda_{j}+(k-j)q}.

These polynomials can be defined again using a pseudo-difference formal operator a kind of (
4.5
). Namely, let us write

\mathcal{T}=\Lambda^{-1}+u_{n-1}x\Lambda^{-1-q}.

The formula for determining
S^{k}_{s}(n)
, in this more general case, has changed slightly compared to (
4.6
) and looks like

\mathcal{T}^{-s}=\Lambda^{s}+\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j}x^{j}S^{j}_{s}(n-(j-1)q)\Lambda^{s-jq}.

One of the results of the paper
[
16
]
is that it has been shown that the integrable hierarchy of evolution differential-difference equation

\frac{du_{n}}{dt}=u_{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q}u_{n+j}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}u_{n-j}\right)

(7.1)

can be written in explicit form as

\frac{du_{n}}{dt_{s}}=u_{n}\left(S^{s}_{sq}(n-(s-1)q+1)-S^{s}_{sq}(n-sq)\right).

(7.2)

Generally speaking, the goal of the work
[
16
]
was that equation (
7.1
) and its hierarchy of generalized symmetries as a whole can be obtained as a result of a suitable reduction to a bi-infinite sequence of Kadomtsev-Petviashvili hierarchies presented in a convenient form. Also within the framework of this approach, it turned out to be possible to obtain an infinite classes of constraints that are compatible with the hierarchy of symmetries (
7.2
). Later in
[
18
]
these ideas has been extended to many systems of evolution equations sharing the property of having a representation in the form of a Lax pair.

As a result, explicit forms of their integrable hierarchies were obtained in terms of suitable discrete polynomials. One way or another, for each specific case, it is necessary to find a suitable pseudo-difference operator like (
4.5
). We will not describe everything in detail further and refer the reader to
[
18
]
for details.

7.2.
On homogeneous equations

Let us also say a few words about the homogeneous equations that arise in our work. A known proof of the Laurent property for the Gale-Robinson recurrence (
6.14
), including the Somos-5 equation (
6.8
), is closely related to its application in the theory of cluster algebras
[
4
]
. The recurrences (
6.15
) and (
6.19
) are apparently not related to the theory of cluster algebras. It would be interesting to find applications for them and prove the Laurent property in a more meaningful way.

Inspired by the examples given above, it can be assumed that for any
g\geq 2
the homogeneous recurrence on the sequence
\left(t_{n}\right)
might look like

\prod_{j=3}^{2g}t_{n+j}\cdot\left(t_{n}t_{n+2g+3}-\alpha t_{n+1}t_{n+2g+2}-\beta t_{n+2}t_{n+2g+1}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{g-1}\gamma_{j}B_{n+1,j},

(7.3)

where
\left(B_{n,1},\ldots,B_{n,g-1}\right)
is supposed to be a finite set of homogeneous polynomials in variables
\left(t_{n},\ldots,t_{n+2g+1}\right)
of degree
2g
. These polynomials must be symmetric in such a way the hypothetical recurrence (
7.3
) to be invariant under the reversing
\left(t_{n},t_{n+1},\ldots,t_{n+2g+3}\right)\rightarrow\left(t_{n+2g+3},t_{n+2g+2},\ldots,t_{n}\right)
. Thus we assume that these recurrences must represent a kind of generalization of the Gale-Robinson equation (
6.14
) of type
\left(2g+3,1,2\right)
.

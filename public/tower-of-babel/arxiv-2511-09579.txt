1.
Introduction

A
Fermat-type functional equation
is a functional equation that generalizes the classical Fermat equation from number theory—namely,

x^{n}+y^{n}=z^{n},

where
x,y,z
are typically integers or complex numbers and
n
is a positive integer.

When this idea is extended to functions instead of numbers, we obtain
functional equations of Fermat type
, which relate the
n
-th powers (or other nonlinear combinations) of functions.

An equation of the form

\displaystyle f^{n}(z)+g^{n}(z)=1

(1.1)

is a classical example of a
Fermat-type functional equation
. For
n\geq 2
, the entire and meromorphic solutions of the equation (
1.1
) have been thoroughly investigated by Montel
[
18
]
, Baker
[
4
]
and Gross
[
8
]
-
[
10
]
. To aid the reader, we briefly summarize the established results below.

Theorem A
.

The solutions
f
and
g
of the functional equation (
1.1
) are characterized as follows:

(i)

If
n=2
,n then the entire solutions are given by
f=\cos(h)
and
g=\sin(h)
, where
h
is an entire function and
the meromorphic solutions are
f=\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}
,
g=\frac{2\beta}{1+\beta^{2}}
,
where
\beta
is a non-constant meromorphic function.

(ii)

If
n>2
, then there are no non-constant entire solutions.

(iii)

If
n=3
, then the meromorphic solutions are

f=\frac{1+\frac{\wp(h(z))}{\sqrt{3}}}{2\wp(h(z))}\;\;\text{and}\;\;g=\frac{1-\frac{\wp(h(z))}{\sqrt{3}}}{2\wp(h(z))}\,\eta,

where
h
is a non-constant entire function,
\eta^{3}=1
, and
\wp
denotes the Weierstrass
\wp
-function satisfying

(\wp^{\prime})^{2}=4\wp^{3}-1

under appropriate periods.

(iv)

If
n>3
, then there are no non-constant meromorphic solutions.

In 1970, Yang
[
25
]
investigated the following Fermat-type equation

\displaystyle f^{n}(z)+g^{m}(z)=1

(1.2)

and obtained that equation (
1.2
) has no non-constant entire solutions when
\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{m}<1
(see the proof of Theorem 1). Therefore it is clear that the equation (
1.2
) has no non-constant entire solutions when
n>2
and
m>2
.

In 2004, Yang and Li
[
26
, Theorem 1]
considered the Fermat-type differential equation

\displaystyle f^{2}(z)+(f^{(1)}(z))^{2}=1

(1.3)

and proved that if

is a transcendental meromorphic solution of (
1.3

f(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(Pe^{-\iota z}+\frac{1}{P}e^{\iota z}\right)
, where
P
is a nonzero constant. If we take
e^{A}=P
, then solutions of (
1.3
) also can be written as
f(z)=\sin(z+A\iota+\frac{\pi}{2})
.

2.
Main results

A natural question is how to find the entire solutions of the partial differential equation of the form

a_{1}\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{n}+a_{2}f^{n}(z_{1},z_{2})=p_{1}e^{r(z_{1},z_{2})}+p_{2}e^{s(z_{1},z_{2})},

(2.1)

where
a_{1},a_{2},p_{1},p_{2}
are non-zero constants,
n
is a positive integer and
r(z_{1},z_{2})
,
s(z_{1},z_{2})
are arbitrary polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Regarding this question, for
n\geq 5
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(z_{1},z_{2})
be an entire solution of the partial differential equation (
2.1
). If
n\geq 5
, then
r(z_{1},z_{2})-s(z_{1},z_{2})
reduces to a constant and
f(z_{1},z_{2})
is of the form
f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}e^{\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}
, where
s(z_{1},z_{2})=az_{1}+g(z_{2})
and
g(z_{2})
is a polynomial in
z_{2}
.

Therefore a natural question arises: can we find the entire solutions to the equation (
2.1
) for
n<5
? However, when
3\leq n\leq 4
and
r(z_{1},z_{2})
,
s(z_{1},z_{2})
are arbitrary polynomials, obtaining a solution to the equation (
2.1
) becomes quite challenging. Therefore, we seek a partial solution by considering

r(z_{1},z_{2})=\lambda_{1}z_{1}+\gamma_{1}z_{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;s(z_{1},z_{2})=\lambda_{2}z_{1}+\gamma_{2}z_{2},

and obtain the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f
be an entire solution of Fermat type partial differential equation

a_{1}\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{n}+a_{2}f^{n}(z_{1},z_{2})=p_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}z_{1}+\gamma_{1}z_{2}}+p_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}z_{1}+\gamma_{2}z_{2}},

(2.2)

where
n
is a positive integer such that
3\leq n\leq 4
and
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. Then
\lambda_{2}=-3\lambda_{1}
or
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{3}\iota)\lambda_{1}
or
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{3}\iota)\lambda_{1}
or
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
. Moreover, we have

(1)

for
n=3

(i)

if
\lambda_{2}=-3\lambda_{1}
, then

f(z)=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{\lambda_{1}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{-\lambda_{1}z_{1}},

where
c_{1}(z_{2})
and
c_{2}(z_{2})
are entire functions such that
a_{2}c_{1}^{2}(z_{2})c_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{p_{1}}{6}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
,
a_{2}c_{2}^{3}(z)=\frac{p_{2}}{2}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
and
a_{1}=-\frac{a_{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{3}}
;

(ii)

if
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{3}\iota)\lambda_{1}
, then

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{\frac{i\sqrt{3}\lambda_{1}}{3}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{\frac{(3-i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}}{6}z_{1}},

where
c_{1}(z_{2})
and
c_{2}(z_{2})
are entire functions such that
a_{2}c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{2}(z_{2})=\frac{p_{1}(3+\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
,
a_{2}c_{1}^{2}(z_{2})c_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{p_{2}(3-\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
and
a_{1}=-\frac{3\sqrt{3}a_{2}\,i}{\lambda_{1}^{3}}
;

(iii)

if
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{3}\iota)\lambda_{1}
, then

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{-\frac{i\sqrt{3}\lambda_{1}}{3}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{\frac{(3+i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}}{6}z_{1}}

where
c_{1}(z_{2})
and
c_{2}(z_{2})
are entire functions such that
a_{2}c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{2}(z_{2})=\frac{p_{1}(3-\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
,
a_{2}c_{1}^{2}(z_{2})c_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{p_{2}(3+\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
and
a_{1}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}a_{2}\,i}{\lambda_{1}^{3}}
,;

(2)

for
n=4
, we have
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
and

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}z_{1}},

where
c_{1}(z_{2})
and
c_{2}(z_{2})
are entire functions such that
8a_{2}c_{1}^{3}(z_{2})c_{2}(z)=p_{1}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
and
8a_{2}c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{3}(z)=p_{2}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
.

3.
Notations and Lemmas

We define
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}[0,+\infty)=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq n<+\infty\}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\mathbb{Z}(0,+\infty)=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:0<n<+\infty\}
.
On
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we define

\partial_{z_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},\ldots,\partial_{z_{i}}^{l_{i}}=\frac{\partial^{l_{i}}}{\partial z_{i}^{l_{i}}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\partial^{I}=\frac{\partial^{|I|}}{\partial z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial z_{m}^{i_{m}}}

where
l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\;(i=1,2,\ldots,m)
and
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+}
is a multi-index such that
|I|=\sum_{j=1}^{m}i_{j}
.

We firstly recall some basic notions in several complex variables (see
[
14
,
20
]
).
On
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, the exterior derivative
d
splits
d=\partial+\bar{\partial}
and twists to
d^{c}=\frac{\iota}{4\pi}\left(\bar{\partial}-\partial\right)
. Clearly
dd^{c}=\frac{\iota}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}
. A non-negative function
\tau:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}[0,b)\;(0<b\leq\infty)
of class
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
is said to be an exhaustion of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
if
\tau^{-1}(K)
is compact whenever
K
is.
An exhaustion
\tau_{m}
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is defined by
\tau_{m}(z)=||z||^{2}
. The standard Kaehler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is given by
\upsilon_{m}=dd^{c}\tau_{m}>0
. On
\mathbb{C}^{m}\backslash\{0\}
, we define
\omega_{m}=dd^{c}\log\tau_{m}\geq 0
and
\sigma_{m}=d^{c}\log\tau_{m}\wedge\omega_{m}^{m-1}
. For any
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m}
, let
S[r]
,
S(r)
and
S\langle r\rangle
be the intersection of
S
with respectively the closed ball, the open ball, the sphere of radius
r>0
centered at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
G(\not=\varnothing)
, where
G
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Then we can write
f(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}P_{i}(z-a)
, where the term
P_{i}(z-a)
is either identically zero or a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
. Certainly the zero multiplicity
\mu^{0}_{f}(a)
of
f
at a point
a\in G
is defined by
\mu^{0}_{f}(a)=\min\{i:P_{i}(z-a)\not\equiv 0\}
.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function on
G
. Then there exist holomorphic functions
g
and
h
such that
hf=g
on
G
and
\dim_{z}h^{-1}(\{0\})\cap g^{-1}(\{0\})\leq m-2
. Therefore the
c
-multiplicity of
f
is just
\mu^{c}_{f}=\mu^{0}_{g-ch}
if
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mu^{c}_{f}=\mu^{0}_{h}
if
c=\infty
. The function
\mu^{c}_{f}:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}
is nonnegative and is called the
c
-divisor of
f
. If
f\not\equiv 0
on each component of
G
, then
\nu=\mu_{f}=\mu^{0}_{f}-\mu^{\infty}_{f}
is called the divisor of
f
. We define

\text{supp}\;\nu=\text{supp}\;\mu_{f}=\overline{\{z\in G:\nu(z)\neq 0\}}
.

For
t>0
, the counting function
n_{\nu}
is defined by

\displaystyle n_{\nu}(t)=t^{-2(m-1)}\int_{A[t]}\nu\upsilon_{m}^{m-1},

where
A=\text{supp}\;\nu
.
The valence function of
\nu
is defined by

N_{\nu}(r)=N_{\nu}(r,r_{0})=\int_{r_{0}}^{r}n_{\nu}(t)\frac{dt}{t}\;\;(r\geq r_{0}).

Also we write
N_{\mu_{f}^{a}}(r)=N(r,a;f)
if
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
N_{\mu_{f}^{a}}(r)=N(r,f)
if
a=\infty
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define the truncated multiplicity function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by
\mu_{f,k}^{a}(z)=\min\{\mu_{f}^{a}(z),k\}
,
and the truncated valence functions

\displaystyle N_{\nu}(t)=\begin{cases}N_{k}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,k}^{a}$}\\
\overline{N}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,1}^{a}$}.\end{cases}

An algebraic subset
X
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is defined as a subset

X=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:P_{j}(z)=0,\;1\leq j\leq l\right\}

with finitely many polynomials
P_{1}(z),\ldots,P_{l}(z)
.
A divisor
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is said to be algebraic if
\nu
is the zero divisor of a polynomial. In this case the counting
function
n_{\nu}
is bounded (see
[
7
,
19
]
). In this case
N_{\nu}(r)=O(\log r)
.

With the help of the positive logarithm function, we define the proximity function of
f
by

m(r,f)=\mathbb{C}^{m}\langle r;\log^{+}|f|\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{m}\langle r\rangle}\log^{+}|f|\;\sigma_{m}.

The characteristic function of
f
is defined by
T(r,f)=m(r,f)+N(r,f)
. We define
m(r,a;f)=m(r,f)
if
a=\infty
and
m(r,a;f)=m(r,1/(f-a))
if
a
is finite complex number. Now if
a\in\mathbb{C}
, then the first main theorem of Nevanlinna theory states that
m(r,a;f)+N(r,a;f)=T(r,f)+O(1)
, where
O(1)
denotes a bounded function when
r
is sufficiently large. Clearly
f
is non-constant, then
T(r,f)\rightarrow\infty
as
r\rightarrow

\infty
. Further
f
is rational if and only if
T(r,f)=O(\log r)
(see
[
7
]
).
On the other hand,
f
to be transcendental then

\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{T(r,f)}{\log r}=+\infty.

We define the order of
f
by

\rho(f):=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log T(r,f)}{\log r}.

We define the linear measure
m(E):=\int_{E}dt
and the logarithmic measure
l(E):=\int_{E\cap[1,\infty)}\frac{dt}{t}
.

In this paper, for
a\in\mathbb{C}
, we write
f=a\Rightarrow g=a
, if
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:f(z)-a=0\}\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:g(z)-a=0\}
.

4.
Proof of Theorems

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We prove the theorem by considering the following two cases.

Case 1.
Let
r(z_{1},z_{2})-s(z_{1},z_{2})
is a non-constant polynomials. Set

G_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{a_{1}}{p_{2}}\left(f(z_{1},z_{2})e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right)^{n},

G_{2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{a_{2}}{p_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right)^{n}

and

G_{3}(z_{1},z_{2})=-\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}e^{(r(z_{1},z_{2})-s(z_{1},z_{2})}.

Then, from (
2.1
), we have

G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}=1.

(4.1)

Note that

\begin{cases}T(r,G_{1})=T\left(r,\frac{a_{1}}{p_{2}}\left(f(z_{1},z_{2})e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right)^{n}\right),\\
T(r,G_{2})=T\left(r,\frac{a_{2}}{p_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right)^{n}\right),\\
T(r,G_{3})=T(r,e^{(r(z_{1},z_{2})-s(z_{1},z_{2})}).\end{cases}

(4.2)

Also, we have

\begin{cases}N_{2}(r,0;G_{1})+\overline{N}(r,G_{1})\leq 2\overline{N}\left(r,0;f(z_{1},z_{2})e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right),\\
N_{2}(r,0;G_{2})+\overline{N}(r,G_{2})\leq 2\overline{N}\left(r,0;\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}e^{-\frac{s(z_{1},z_{2})}{n}}\right),\\
N_{2}(r,0;G_{3})+\overline{N}(r,G_{3})\leq 2\overline{N}\left(r,0;e^{(r(z_{1},z_{2})-s(z_{1},z_{2})}\right)=0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

Now, from (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{3}N_{2}(r,0;G_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\overline{N}(r,G_{j})

\displaystyle\leq

Now proceeding in the same way as done in the proof of Case 1, we get a contradiction.

Next we suppose that

\phi-\left(3(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-\frac{2\hat{a}}{3}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{2}\right)=0,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad\phi=\frac{1}{3}\left(3(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})-2\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{2}\right).

Again from (
4.30
), we get

\displaystyle f\left(-\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-6+\frac{4\hat{a}}{3}\phi\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\right)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\left(\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\phi-\frac{2\hat{a}}{3}\phi^{2}-\hat{a}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\right)

(4.31)

\displaystyle-3\hat{a}\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}=0.

Differentiating partially (
4.29
) with respect to
z_{1}
and then eliminating
\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}
from (
4.31
), we obtain

f\left(-\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-6+\frac{5\hat{a}}{3}\phi\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\right)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\left(\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\phi-\hat{a}\phi^{2}\right)=0,

which yields

-\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-6+\frac{5\hat{a}}{3}\phi\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\phi-\hat{a}\phi^{2}=0.

(4.32)

Now from (
4.32
), we get

\phi=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-9=0.

Again from (
4.32
), we obtain

3(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})=\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{2}.

Therefore, we can easily deduce

\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{3}\,i)\lambda_{1}\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{3}\,i)\lambda_{1}.

If
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{3}\,i)\lambda_{1}
, then from (
4.25
) we get

6\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}-(3+i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}+(1+i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}^{2}f=0,

and so
f
is of the form

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{\frac{i\sqrt{3}\lambda_{1}}{3}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{\frac{(3-i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}}{6}z_{1}}.

Now putting the value of
f
in (
4.12
), we get
\hat{a}=-\frac{3\sqrt{3}\,i}{\lambda_{1}^{3}}
,
c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{2}(z_{2})=\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(3+\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
and
c_{1}^{2}(z_{2})c_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{\hat{p}_{2}(3-\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
.

If
\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\sqrt{3}\,i)\lambda_{1}
, then from (
4.25
) we get

6\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}-(3-i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}+(1-i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}^{2}f=0,

and so
f
is of the form

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{-\frac{i\sqrt{3}\lambda_{1}}{3}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{\frac{(3+i\sqrt{3})\lambda_{1}}{6}z_{1}}.

Now putting the value of
f
in (
4.12
), we get
\hat{a}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}\,i}{\lambda_{1}^{3}}
,
c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{2}(z_{2})=\frac{\hat{p}_{1}(3-\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
and
c_{1}^{2}(z_{2})c_{2}(z_{2})=\frac{\hat{p}_{2}(3+\sqrt{3}i)}{18}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
.

Case 2.2.
Let
n=4
. Then from (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(4\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f\right)f^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\bigg[4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\left(f^{3}+\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-4\left(3f^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}+3\hat{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}\right)-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{3}\bigg].

(4.33)

Define

\psi=\frac{4\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f}{\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}}.

(4.34)

Now we consider following two sub-cases:

Sub-case 2.2.1.
Suppose that
\psi\equiv 0
. Then from (
4.34
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{4}f=0.

(4.35)

First we suppose that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\neq 0
. Then from (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle 4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})f^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\bigg[-4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\hat{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle-4\left(3f^{2}+3\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}\right)-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}\bigg],

which contradicts the fact that
f
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
have no common zeros.

Next we suppose that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=0
. Then from (
4.35
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{4}f=0.

(4.36)

The solution of the equation (
4.36
) is of the form

f(z_{1},z_{2})=c_{1}(z_{2})e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}z_{1}}+c_{2}(z_{2})e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}z_{1}}.

Now putting the value of
f
in (
4.12
), we get
8c_{1}^{3}(z_{2})c_{2}(z)=\hat{p}_{1}e^{\gamma_{1}z_{2}}
and
8c_{1}(z_{2})c_{2}^{3}(z)=\hat{p}_{2}e^{\gamma_{2}z_{2}}
.

Sub-case 2.2.2.
Suppose that
\psi\not\equiv 0
. Clearly, from (
4.2
),
f
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
cannot have common zeros.
Therefore, from (
4.2
), we conclude that if
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a zero of
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
of multiplicity
l
, then
z_{0}
must be a zero of
4\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f
of multiplicity at least
l
.
Consequently
\psi
is a non-zero entire function.

Thus, from (
4.2
), we observe that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}=0\Rightarrow 4\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f=0.

Therefore,
\psi
is a non-zero entire function. Now proceeding in the same way as in the proof of Sub-case 2.1.2, we can easily deduce that
\psi
is a non-zero polynomial in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Now from (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\psi-4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\right)f^{3}

(4.37)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\bigg[4\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}-4\left(3f^{2}+3\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}\right)-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}\bigg].

Since
f
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
have no common zeros, it follows from (
4.37
) that

\psi=4(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}),

(4.38)

and so

4\hat{a}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}-4\left(3f^{2}+3\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{a}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\frac{\partial^{3}f}{\partial z_{1}^{3}}\right)-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\hat{a}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}=0.

(4.39)

Now using (
4.34
), (
4.38
) and (
4.39
), we obtain

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}\left(\psi^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}+\frac{3\hat{a}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{2}f\right)=f^{2}\left(1+\frac{\hat{a}\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}}{16}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\psi^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}+\frac{3\hat{a}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{2}f=0.

(4.40)

Note that
\psi
is a non-zero polynomial and
f
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
have no common zeros. Then from (
4.40
), we can easily conclude that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
has no zeros, which contradicts the fact that
0
is not Picard exceptional value of
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}
.

Hence the proof is complete.

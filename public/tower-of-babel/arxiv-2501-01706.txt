Revisiting the matrix elements of the position operator in the crystal momentum representation
M. S. Si, G. P. Zhang ∗

Abstract
Fewer operators are more fundamental than the position operator in a crystal. But since it is not translationally invariant in crystal momentum representation (CMR), how to properly represent it is nontrivial. Over half a century, various methods have been proposed, but they often lead to either highly singular derivatives or extremely arcane expressions. Here we propose a resolution to this problem by directly computing their matrix elements between two Bloch states. We show that the position operator is a full matrix in CMR, where the off-diagonal elements in crystal momentum 𝐤 {\bf k} only appear along the direction of the position vector. Our formalism, free of singular derivative and degeneracy difficulties, can describe an array of physical properties, from intraband transitions, polarization with or without spin-orbit coupling, orbital angular momentum, to susceptibilities.

The complete integral of the matrix elements of the position operator
\hat{\bf r}

IV

Finally, we come back to Eq.

{\bf k}

translational operator
\hat{T}
to
{\bf r}
to get

\hat{T}{\bf r}={\bf r}+{\bf R}_{l}
, where
{\bf R}_{l}
is the lattice vector of a
crystal. This proves that the position operator is not translationally
invariant, but if we apply it to the commutation
[
29

[\hat{\bf r},\hat{H}]

\hat{H}
is the crystal Hamiltonian operator and has
the periodicity of the crystal, we have

\hat{T}[\hat{\bf r},\hat{H}]=[\hat{\bf r},\hat{H}]

is diagonal in

{\bf k}

\hat{\bf r}
is translationally invariant
and diagonal in
{\bf k}
. Instead, Eq.
1
only means that only
diagonal matrix elements of
\hat{\bf p}
and
\hat{\bf r}
are related to each other.
For the rest, they are not, because
\hat{T}[\hat{\bf r}^{n},\hat{H}]\neq[\hat{\bf r}^{n},\hat{H}]
, where order
n
is larger than 1.

\displaystyle=ia_{1}\frac{-N_{1}e^{iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}(1-e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})+(1-e^{iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}}{(1-e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})^{2}}

(5)

\displaystyle=ia_{1}\frac{-N_{1}e^{iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}+e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}+(N_{1}-1)e^{i(N_{1}+1)(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})}{(1-e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})^{2}}.

(6)

We first examine the denominator by expanding the exponent in terms of

(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}

\displaystyle(1-e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}})^{2}=\left[1-1-i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}-\frac{1}{2!}(i\left(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}\right)^{2}+\cdots\right]^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle=[i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}]^{2}[1+\frac{1}{2}i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+\cdots]^{2},

(8)

which shows the leading order is
[i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}]^{2}
. This means that the
numerator in Eq.
6
must be expanded at least to
[i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}]^{2}
.

The numerator in Eq.
6

is

\displaystyle-N_{1}e^{iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}+e^{i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}+(N_{1}-1)e^{i(N_{1}+1)(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}}

\displaystyle=-N_{1}(1+iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+\frac{(iN_{1}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1})^{2}}{2}+\cdots)

\displaystyle+1+i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+\frac{1}{2}i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle+(N_{1}-1)(1+i(N_{1}+1)(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+\frac{(i(N_{1}+1)(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1})^{2}}{2}+\cdots,

(9)

whose zeroth order in
i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}
is
-N_{1}+1+N_{1}-1=0
, and whose first order
is
-iN_{1}^{2}(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+i(N_{1}^{2}-1)(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}=0
.
The second order is

\frac{(i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1})^{2}}{2}(N_{1}^{2}-N_{1})=-\frac{(q_{1}-k_{1})^{2}a_{1}^{2}}{2}(N_{1}^{2}-N_{1}),

(10)

which is the lowest nonzero term. Equation
18
in the limit

q_{1}\rightarrow k_{1}
is given
by

ia_{1}\frac{-\frac{(q_{1}-k_{1})^{2}a_{1}^{2}}{2}(N_{1}^{2}-N_{1})}{[i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}]^{2}[1+\frac{1}{2}i(q_{1}-k_{1})a_{1}+\cdots]^{2}}=\frac{ia_{1}(N_{1}^{2}-N_{1})}{2},

(11)

If we divide Eq.
11
by
i
, we find

\frac{a_{1}(N_{1}^{2}-N_{1})}{2}
, which matches our above result obtained by
the direct calculation. Here we do not use the BvK boundary condition.

IV
Nearly free-electron model

We first show some details how to get the first-order correction to
the wavefunction. We denote the first-order correction to the
wavefunction as
\psi^{(1)}_{k}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\psi^{(1)}_{k}=\sum_{q\neq k}\frac{\langle\phi_{q}|\sum_{n}U_{n}e^{inbx}|\phi_{k}\rangle}{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{q}^{(0)}}|\phi_{q}\rangle=\sum_{q\neq k}\frac{\sum_{n}U_{n}\delta_{k+nb,q}}{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{q}^{(0)}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{iqx}

(12)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{U_{n}}{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{k+nb}^{(0)}}e^{i(nb+k)x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{U_{n}e^{inbx}}{E_{k}^{(0)}-E_{k+nb}^{(0)}}e^{ikx}.

(13)

Including the zeroth-order wavefunction,
we have our wavefunction up
to the first order correction as

\psi(k,x)=\frac{{\cal C}}{\sqrt{L}}\left(1+\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{U_{n}}{\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m_{e}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}(k+nb)^{2}}{2m_{e}}}e^{inbx}\right)e^{ikx}\equiv\frac{{\cal C}}{\sqrt{L}}\left(1+\sum_{n\neq 0}W_{n}(k)e^{inbx}\right)e^{ikx}

(14)

which is our Eq.
26

in the main text. Here
{\cal C}
is the
normalization constant,
b=\frac{2\pi}{a}
,
U_{n}
is the Fourier
coefficient of
U(x)
, and the dimensionless
W_{n}
is defined through Eq.
14
.

The matrix element of the
position operator for
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
consists of four integrals,

\displaystyle\langle\psi(k_{1},x)|\hat{x}|\psi(k_{2},x)\rangle=\frac{|{\cal C}|^{2}}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\left(e^{-ik_{1}x+ik_{2}x}x+\sum_{m\neq 0}W_{m}(k_{2})e^{imbx+ik_{2}x-k_{1}x}x\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{n\neq 0}W^{*}_{n}(k_{1})e^{i(k_{2}-nb-k_{1})x}x+\sum_{n\neq 0}\sum_{m\neq 0}W^{*}_{n}(k_{1})W_{m}(k_{2})e^{i(mb+k_{2}-nb-k_{1})}x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{|{\cal C}|^{2}}{i(k_{2}-k_{1})}+\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{|{\cal C}|^{2}W_{m}(k_{2})}{i(mb+k_{2}-k_{1})}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{|{\cal C}|^{2}W_{n}^{*}(k_{1})}{i(k_{2}-nb-k_{1})}+\sum_{n,m\neq 0}\frac{|{\cal C}|^{2}W_{n}^{*}(k_{1})W_{m}(k_{2})}{i(mb+k_{2}-nb-k_{1}),}

which is Eq. 11 of the main paper.

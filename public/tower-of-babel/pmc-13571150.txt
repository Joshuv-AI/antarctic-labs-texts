Dietary α-Mangostin alleviates lipopolysaccharide-induced stress and enhances growth performance in broilers via improved intestinal immunity and integrity
Authors: Kaibo Fu, Yuan Yue, Jian Zhao, Xianlei Li, Bingkun Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
α-Mangostin (α-MG), a bioactive xanthone from mangosteen, possesses anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, but its efficacy and optimal dosage in broilers remain unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of α-MG on lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-challenged and normally reared broilers to determine the optimal supplementation level. Two experiments were conducted. In Exp. 1, 396 one-d-old Cobb broilers (initial body weight: 42.45 ± 0.46 g) were divided into six groups, each with six replicates of 11 birds. The negative control group received a basal diet and was injected with saline, and the other groups were challenged with LPS and fed diets containing 0 (positive control), 100, 150, 200, and 250 mg/kg α-MG for 21 d. In Exp. 2, 792 one-d-old Arbor Acres broilers (initial body weight: 42.54 ± 0.44 g) were divided into six groups, each with six replicates of 22 birds, and fed diets containing 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 mg/kg α-MG for 42 d. The results showed that in Exp. 1, supplementation with 150 mg/kg α-MG improved ileal morphology (villus height/crypt depth), immune cell proportions (CD4+CD8-/CD3+), and secretory immunoglobulin A (IgA) concentration compared with the positive control (P < 0.05). In Exp. 2, supplementation with 150 mg/kg α-MG enhanced body weight and average daily gain on d 21, increased muscle glutathione (GSH) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) content, reduced serum interleulin-1 beta (IL-1β) and diamine oxidase (DAO) activities, improved ileal morphology (villus height), and upregulated ERK1 expression level (P < 0.05). In conclusion, dietary supplementation with α-MG at 150 mg/kg effectively mitigates LPS-induced intestinal damage and immune stress, and promotes growth performance in broilers under normal conditions. The benefits are mediated through the enhancement of antioxidant capacity, downregulation of nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) pathway-related gene expression, and reinforcement of intestinal mucosal immunity and integrity.

Introduction

The high rearing densities used in the modern intensive poultry industry are confronted with substantial challenges from environmental and pathogenic stressors, such as microbial infections, dietary toxins, and management issues (Jin et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2023a). These stressors can disrupt intestinal homeostasis, resulting in impaired growth performance, reduced meat quality, and substantial economic losses (Eratalar et al., 2022; Shaukat et al., 2025). The intestinal tract is a critical immune organ that not only facilitates nutrient digestion and absorption but also serves as a first line of defense against external harmful factors, thereby preserving intestinal homeostasis and physical health (Bian et al., 2020; Bogatyrev et al., 2020; Parrish et al., 2023). Under pathological stress conditions, this barrier is highly susceptible to disruption (Balakin et al., 2025; Mohammadzadeh et al., 2025), as it is composed of epithelial cells that are bound by tight junction proteins, a chemical mucus layer, and a sophisticated local immune system (Zhang et al., 2024).

The poultry industry's long-term reliance on antibiotics for growth promotion and disease control (Looft et al., 2014; Tang et al., 2025) may result in intestinal dysbiosis, antibiotic-resistant bacteria, and drug accumulation (Beyari et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024). Finding efficient natural substitutes that can improve resilience and preserve gut health is urgently needed to reduce dependence on antibiotics (Pan et al., 2022). Phytochemicals—bioactive compounds derived from plants—have emerged as promising candidates due to their multifaceted properties, including antioxidant (Kong et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025), anti-inflammatory (Liu et al., 2023a; Wang et al., 2025), and microbiota improvement (Martin et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). The isoprenylated xanthone α-mangostin (α-MG) was isolated from the pericarps of the mangosteen (Garcinia mangostana, Clusiaceae) and has been used for clinical application in traditional Chinese medicinal practices for centuries (Jo et al., 2022). Previous in vitro and rodent studies have demonstrated its potential anti-inflammatory capacity, largely attributed to the inhibition of the nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) signaling pathway (Majdalawieh et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022), as well as its remarkable antioxidant activity (John et al., 2022). However, there is a current lack of clarity regarding the optimal supplementation dosage and impact of α-MG in broiler diets.

The current study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and determine the optimal supplementation level of α-MG as a broiler feed additive in both normal and stressed conditions. Additionally, changes in the integrity of the intestinal barrier, systemic and mucosal immune responses, and critical inflammatory signaling pathways were observed. Reference data and a theoretical foundation for the application of dietary α-MG as a novel nutritional intervention in poultry production will be provided by the results of this research.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The experimental use of animals and related procedures was approved by the Chinese Agricultural University Laboratory Animal Welfare and Experimental Ethical Committee at the China Agricultural University, Hebei Zhuozhou Experimental Base (approval number AW50206202-1-05, approved on February 5, 2026). The management of chicks was consistent with the “Arbor Acres Broiler Pocket Guide” and “Cobb Broiler Management Guide”, respectively (Arbor Acres, 2026; Cobb, 2026).

Reagents

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) (Escherichia coli O55: B5) was purchased from Sigma–Aldrich Corporation (St. Louis, MO, USA) and prepared as a 1 mg/mL solution with 0.9% saline solution. The α-MG (purity 98.5%) was purchased from Guangzhou Leader Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China) and added by replacing the wheat bran in the basal diet.

Animals, diets, and experimental design

All the broilers were fed in a fully enclosed chicken house with 3-tier battery cages (200 cm × 80 cm × 60 cm). Feed in pellet form and water were provided ad libitum. The temperature was set at 34 °C at the beginning of bird rearing, then decreased by 2 to 3 °C every week to adjust to a final temperature of 23 °C. A standard lighting program was followed: 23 h of light and 1 h of darkness for the first 5 d, followed by 20 h of light and 4 h of darkness from d 6 until the end of the trial. A total of 396 one-d-old Cobb Broiler chicks (initial body weight: 42.45 ± 0.46 g) and 792 Arbor Acres Broiler chicks (initial body weight: 42.54 ± 0.44 g) healthy chicks were selected in Exp. 1 and Exp. 2, respectively. In Exp. 1, chicks were allocated into six groups, each with six replicates and 11 birds. The entire experimental period was 21 d. The negative control group chicks were fed the corn-soybean meal without α-MG supplementation, and the experimental chicks (positive control, α-MG100, α-MG150, α-MG200, and α-MG250) were fed the corn-soybean meal diets supplemented with α-MG at 0, 100, 150, 200, and 250 mg/kg, respectively. On d 11 and 13, all chicks in the experimental groups received an injection of LPS at 1 mg/kg body weight (BW), and the chicks in the negative control group received an injection of the same volume of regular saline. In Exp. 2, chicks were allocated into six groups, each with six replicates and 22 birds. The entire experimental period lasted 42 d, divided into two feeding phases: phase 1 (d 1-21) and phase 2 (d 22-42). The experimental chicks (control, α-MG50, α-MG100, α-MG150, α-MG200, and α-MG250) were fed the corn-soybean meal diets supplemented with α-MG at 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 mg/kg, respectively.

The crude protein and amino acid contents were analyzed according to GB/T 6432–2018 (China National Standard, 2018b) and GB 5009.124–2016 (China National Standard, 2016), respectively. The calcium and total phosphorus contents were determined according to GB/T 6436–2018 (China National Standard, 2018a) and GB/T 6437–2018 (China National Standard, 2018c), respectively. The apparent metabolizable energy was calculated based on values from the feed composition tables published by Institute of Animal Sciences et al. (2024).

Growth performance

In Exp. 1, chicks were starved for 12 h before and after LPS challenge and at d 21. The BW and feed intake (FI) of each replicate was recorded. The BW, average daily gain (ADG), average daily feed intake (ADFI), feed conversion ratio (FCR), and phase FI were calculated according to the Chinese agricultural standard (NY/T 823–2020) (Ministry of Agriculture of the People's Republic of China, 2020).

In Exp. 2, BW and FI of each replicate were recorded at d 0, 21, and 42 after starving for 12 h. The calculation methods were the same as the Exp. 1.

Carcass performance

In Exp. 2, at 42 d of age, one chick was selected and euthanized from each replicate. The carcass cutting operation was carried out according to GB/T 19478–2018 (China National Standard, 2018d) and GB/T 24864–2010 (China National Standard, 2010), while dressed weight, half-eviscerated weight (including giblet), eviscerated weight, leg muscle weight, pectoral muscle weight, and abdominal fat weight were measured and their yield calculated following the definitions of NY/T 823–2020 (Ministry of Agriculture of the People's Republic of China, 2020).

Organ indices

The liver, spleen, bursa of Fabricius, and thymus were weighed, and the relative organ weights (expressed as percentages of BW) were calculated according to the methods described by Gorenz et al. (2024) and Islam et al. (2023).

Meat quality

In Exp. 2, the pectoral muscle was used in the same way as the carcass performance. White striping grade was measured using the methods of Kuttappan et al. (2016) (Fig. 1). A section (5 cm × 2 cm × 1 cm) from the same position on each pectoral muscle was removed, weighed, and stored at a 4 °C for 24 h to measure drip loss. The activities of malondialdehyde (MDA; A003-1-2), glutathione (GSH; A006-2-1), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; A015-1-2), superoxide dismutase (SOD; A001-1-2), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px; A005-1-2) in the pectoral muscle were analyzed by using assay kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) and measured with a microplate reader (SpectraMax i3x, Molecular Devices, LLC., San Jose, CA, USA).Fig. 1Scoring standard for white striping (cited from Kuttappan et al., 2016). Serum antioxidant enzyme, pro-inflammatory factors, and intestinal barrier-related parameters

Blood was collected from the wing vein and serum was obtained by centrifuged (1500 × g, 15 min, 4 °C) and stored at −20 °C. In Exp. 2. the activities of T-AOC, SOD, and GSH-Px were analyzed using the assay kits described above. For Exp. 1, the concentrations of tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α; ml002790), interleukin-1β (IL-1β; ml059835), interferon-γ (INF-γ; ml042758), interleukin-6 (IL-6; ml059839), and diamine oxidase (DAO; ml036981) were quantified. For Exp. 2, the concentrations of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS; ml036925), lysozyme (LYS; ml036945), and immunoglobulin G (IgG; SEKCN-0126) were quantified. All enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits were purchased from Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), and the optical density was measured using a microplate reader (SpectraMax i3x, Molecular Devices, LLC., San Jose, CA, USA).

Ileum immune parameters

In Exp. 1, according to the method of Liu et al. (2023b), immune cells from the ileal lamina propria were isolated by mechanical dissociation and then enzymatically digested to obtain a suspension. The ileal content suspension was filtered, centrifuged, washed, and finally resuspended in Roswell Park Memorial Institute-1640 medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). Single-cell suspensions were stained with fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibodies against chicken cell surface markers: cluster of differentiation 45 (CD45; pan-leukocyte; 8270–02, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), CD3 (T cells; 8200–27, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), CD4 (8210–26, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), CD8α (8220–09, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), CD25 (for Treg identification; HCA173F, Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA, USA), bursal disease virus receptor 1 (Bu-1; B cells; 8395–26, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), major histocompatibility complex class II (MHC class II; 8350–02, Southernbiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA), CD11c (for dendritic cells; 12–0116–42, eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA), and monocyte (Mon; 8420–31, SouthernBiotech, Birmingham, AL, USA). Data were acquired on a BD LSR II flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) and analyzed using FlowJo software (Tree Star Inc., Ashland, OR, USA). The ileal mucosa was scraped, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, homogenized, and centrifuged for secretory immunoglobulin A (sIgA) analysis using ELISA kit (ml002778, Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China).

Histological analysis

A 1-cm segment from the midpoint of the ileum (Exp. 1) or jejunum (Exp. 2) was collected and flushed with 0.9% saline, and then fixed in 4% polyformaldehyde solution. Fixed intestinal segments were embedded in paraffin blocks, cut into sections, and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (H&E). After dehydration, the sections were observed using a Pannoramic MIDI (3DHISTECH Ltd., Budapest, Hungary); the villus height (VH) and crypt depth (CD) were measured using CaseViewer 2.4 (3DHISTECH Ltd., Budapest, Hungary), and the ratio of VH to CD (VH/CD) was calculated.

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the ileum and muscle tissues using TRIzol reagent (P118–05, GenStar Biosolutions Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions at the end of the trial, and RNA purity and concentration were measured using a spectrophotometer (NanoDrop, 2000; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). Reverse transcription was performed using StarScript II First-strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (A212–02, GenStar Biosolutions Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Real-time quantitative PCR was carried out with an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-time PCR system (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Foster City, CA, USA) and 2 × RealStar SYBR Mixture (A301–10, GenStar Biosolutions Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The results were normalized to that of β-actin expression, and the relative gene expression levels were calculated via the 2−ΔΔCt method by Livak and Schmittgen (2001). Statistical analysis

All the data were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and the means were compared using Duncan's multiple range test in SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Spearman's correlation analysis was conducted to evaluate the relationships between muscle antioxidant indices and meat drip loss. The linear and quadratic effects of the supplemental α-MG concentration were assessed using regression analysis, as below. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. The data are presented as the mean and pooled standard error of mean (SEM). The regression model was as follows:Yij=β0+β1Xi+eij(linearregression);Yij=β0+β1Xi+β2Xi2+eij(quadraticregression),where Yij is the response variable; β0 is the intercept; β1 and β2 are regression coefficients; Xi is the fixed effect that included α-MG (i = 0, 50, 100, 150, 200, and 250 mg/kg); and eij is the observational error for the ij-th observation.

Discussion

The intensification of poultry production has increased the prevalence of various stressors, which have a detrimental impact on intestinal health and growth, resulting in significant economic losses (Sindiyo et al., 2018; Tompkins et al., 2023). Identifying safe, effective, and natural alternatives to reduce reliance on antibiotics is essential for the poultry industry (Chen et al., 2025; Pham et al., 2023). This study demonstrated that dietary supplementation with α-MG effectively alleviated LPS-induced immune stress and improved growth performance, intestinal health, and antioxidant status in broilers under normal rearing conditions. Under the conditions of this study, 150 mg/kg was considered the optimal supplemental dose.

The significant impairment of growth performance following LPS challenge has been widely reported, where nutrients are diverted from chicken growth to support immune system activation (Fu et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2023). The findings showed that dietary α-MG supplementation mitigated this growth suppression, aligning with the reported effects of other flavonoids (Shi et al., 2022; Song et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2021). Moreover, under normal rearing conditions, α-MG improved ADG and BW in a dose-dependent manner. This indicated that α-MG not only alleviated LPS-induced damage, but also improved growth performance. Both meat yield and the economic benefits of the broiler industry were both significantly influenced by slaughter performance (Huang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). The quality of fresh poultry meat was largely determined by its water-holding capacity, which was linked to consumer acceptance and processed meat yield (Kaić et al., 2021; Pu et al., 2025). The improvement in processing yields and the notable reduction of breast muscle drip loss may provide further commercial relevance. Notably, previous studies have shown that drip loss is closely linked to muscle membrane integrity, which was vulnerable to oxidative damage (Ibrahim et al., 2019; Malila et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024). In this study, dietary α-MG supplementation significantly increased breast muscle GSH activity, a key indicator of antioxidant capacity. Serum T-AOC was markedly elevated in α-MG-supplemented groups at d 21, with the α-MG200 and α-MG250 groups showing a significant increase compared with the control group. Concurrently, serum GSH-Px activity was also enhanced, particularly in the α-MG100 and α-MG200 groups. These results indicate that α-MG supplementation effectively boosts systemic and muscle antioxidant defense, which may play a crucial role in mitigating oxidative stress induced by metabolic challenge. However, no significant correlations were found between drip loss and the measured antioxidant indices, suggesting that the enhanced antioxidant capacity may not directly translate into improved meat quality under the current experimental conditions. Further studies with more sensitive meat quality indicators are warranted to explore this relationship. In summary, dietary α-MG supplementation improved growth performance in both quantitative (growth and yield) and qualitative (antioxidant capacity) aspects.

The results of this study showed that dietary α-MG supplementation could enhance immunity and regulate inflammation. Changes in relative organ weights are sensitive indicators of toxicity disturbance in toxicity studies (Tavares et al., 2023). In the LPS-challenged model, a transient but significant rise was observed the relative of the liver, spleen, and thymus at 14 d post-inoculation. It's a classic marker of systemic immune activation; the liver engages in acute-phase protein synthesis and detoxification, while the spleen and thymus undergo lymphocyte proliferation and activation to mount a defense (Choi et al., 2023; Gong et al., 2025). The subsequent return of these indices to basal levels indicates that the acute inflammation had been alleviated by d 21. Notably, α-MG suppressed the relative weight of the thymus compared with LPS challenge on d 14, suggesting that it alleviates the adverse immune reactions without excessively impairing host defense. Under normal rearing conditions, dietary α-MG supplementation showed a tendency to increase the bursa of Fabricius index. The bursa is a humoral immune organ that plays a role in B lymphocyte maturation and diversification (Dong et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2024). An enlarged bursa index is often associated with enhanced B-cell development and antibody-producing capacity within the physiological range (Sastré-Calderón et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2021). This finding was in accordance with the increment in serum IgG concentration in the α-MG200 group and supports the concept that α-MG positively modulated humoral immunity. Additionally, the lack of significant effects on liver and spleen indices under normal rearing conditions further indicates that the recommended dose of α-MG (150 mg/kg) does not induce pathological organ hypertrophy, affirming its safety in broiler diets. Furthermore, under normal rearing conditions, serum IL-1β concentration was significantly and dose-dependently reduced, and ileal IL-1β, IFN-γ, and the master regulator NF-κB mRNA expression levels were downregulated significantly. Excessive production of pro-inflammatory factors consumes a large amount of nutrients for the immune response rather than for growth (Yang et al., 2021). It also could lead to tissue and organ dysfunction and progressive development of pathology, which may explain the observed changes in organ index (Bajgar and Krejčová, 2023). The concurrent reduction in serum IL-1β concentration and increase in ileal IL-1β and ERK1 mRNA expression levels were observed in the α-MG150 group. This may imply the existence of a regulatory or compensatory feedback mechanism at the transcriptional level. The upregulation of ERK1 mRNA expression level suggests the potential engagement of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, which could be involved in tissue repair and immune modulation. The ERK1 is a key component of the MAPK cascade that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival (Noorbakhsh et al., 2024). In the intestinal epithelium, ERK1 activation has been shown to promote epithelial cell migration and wound healing, thereby contributing to the restoration of mucosal integrity following injury (Roth et al., 2021). Additionally, ERK signaling can modulate immune responses by influencing cytokine production and immune cell function (Lucas et al., 2022). Thus, the upregulation of ERK1 expression level in the ileum of α-MG supplemented broilers may represent a protective mechanism that facilitates tissue repair and immune homeostasis. However, further investigation at the protein (e.g., phosphorylated ERK) and functional levels is necessary to elucidate this mechanism. These alterations contributed to dietary 150 mg/kg α-MG supplementation, which may inhibit the mRNA expression of NF-κB pathway to alleviate intestinal inflammation and prevent pathological organ hypertrophy.

The status of gut health can be determined by intestinal morphology (Recharla et al., 2021), and increased VH and a higher VH/CD ratio are indicative of healthy intestinal morphology (Wang et al., 2024). The LPS challenge induced deepened crypts and a reduced VH/CD ratio, which indicates rapid enterocyte turnover and increased mucosal tissue maintenance requirements (Ayoola et al., 2015; Yu et al., 2024). Similar to other flavonoid additives (Shen et al., 2025; Zhi et al., 2024), the results showed that the supplementation with α-MG, particularly at 150 mg/kg, effectively restored ileal morphology. Under normal rearing conditions, α-MG supplementation significantly increased jejunal VH, indicating that α-MG supplementation could improve the intestinal absorptive capacity rather than repairing intestinal damage, providing a direct mechanistic explanation for the improved nutrient utilization and growth performance. Moreover, the significantly changed in serum DAO activity, an enzyme localized in mature intestinal villus that leaks upon mucosal damage, provide direct biochemical support for the morphological evidence (Tang et al., 2022). The mRNA expression levels of key tight junction proteins (CLDN, OCLN, and ZO-1) and mucin (MUC2) in the ileum was not pronounced; this suggests that α-MG enhances the intestinal barrier primarily through post-translational mechanisms, likely by mitigating the inflammatory and oxidative stress that disrupts junctional complexes, consistent with the observed anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities. Overall, dietary 150 mg/kg α-MG improved the absorption capacity and integrity of the intestines.

The cecal tonsil is the largest intestinal lymphoid tissue in poultry, and its indispensable immune function indirectly contributes to intestinal integrity (Guo et al., 2023). Mucosal sIgA plays a critical role in the immune system (Min et al., 2023). The present study showed that LPS challenge may disturb the cellular immune landscape in the ileal lamina propria, markedly reducing the proportion of CD4+ T helper cells and suppressing the secretion of sIgA for 14 d. Dietary 150 mg/kg α-MG supplementation restored these parameters and increase the proportion of MHC-II+CD11c+ dendritic cells, and also improved sIgA secretion, suggesting a role in re-establishing immune homeostasis and potentiating antigen presentation. These changes were consistent with the intestinal morphology, indicating that the improved intestinal integrity may be linked to improved function of the cecal tonsil and sIgA secretion.

Conclusion

In conclusion, as illustrated in Fig. 5, dietary supplementation with 150 mg/kg α-MG improved growth performance and intestinal health under both LPS-challenge and normal conditions. Additionally, an improved water-holding capacity and increase in antioxidant capacity were observed, suggesting potential benefits in meat quality improvement.

Affine Hulls and Simplices: a Constructive Analysis
Douglas S. Bridges

Abstract
This paper deals with certain fundamental results about affine hulls and simplices in a real normed linear space. The framework of the paper is Bishop’s constructive mathematics, which, with its characteristic interpretation of existence as constructibility , often involves more subtle estimation than its classical-logic-based counterpart. As well as technically more involved proofs (for example, that of Theorem 29 on the perturbation of vertices), we have included a number of elementary ones for completeness of exposition.

Proposition 1

An affine combination of affine combinations in a real linear
space
X
is an affine combination.

Proof.
Consider first an affine combination of two affine combinations.. Let

x=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}b_{i}
and
y=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta_{j}c_{j}
, where each

b_{i}
and each
c_{j}
belongs to
X
, each
\xi_{i}
and each
\eta_{j}

belongs to
\mathbf{R}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}=1=

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta_{j}
. For each
\alpha\in\mathbf{R},

\alpha x+\left(1-\alpha\right)y=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha\xi_{i}b_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-\alpha\right)\eta_{j}c_{j}

where

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha\xi_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-\alpha\right)\eta_{j}=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}+\left(1-\alpha\right)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta_{j}=1.

Thus

points
b_{i}
and

(1\leq i\leq m

in
X
.
Iterating this argument, a finite number of times completes the proof.

\square

Lemma 3

Let

be elements of a normed space
X
,
and let
\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{\mu\in}\mathbf{R}^{n+1}

{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\lambda_{k}=1={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\mu_{k}

{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\mu_{k}a_{k}={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right)(a_{k}-a_{n+1}).

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\mu_{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}+\left(1-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}\right)a_{n+1}

\displaystyle-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\mu_{k}a_{k}-\left(1-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\mu_{k}\right)a_{n+1}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right)(a_{k}-a_{n+1}).

\square

Proposition 4

{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\neq{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}}\mu_{k}a_{k}.

Proof.

{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|\geq\left|{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right)\right|=\left|(1-\lambda_{n+1})-(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|=\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\mu_{n+1}\right|>0

\mathbf{R}^{n}
onto
V
with bounded linear
inverse. Moreover, since both
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
V
are finite dimensional,
the operator norm

\left\|F\right\|\equiv\sup\left\{\left\|F(\mathbf{\lambda})\right\|:\mathbf{\lambda}\in\mathbf{R}^{n},\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}\leq 1\right\}

and the counterpart for
F^{-1}
exist. Now, it is clear that
f_{n}
maps

\Delta_{n}
onto
\Sigma
. For each
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\mathsf{aff}(\Delta_{n})
,

\displaystyle f_{n}(\mathbf{\lambda})

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}+\lambda_{n+1}a_{n+1}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}+\left(1-{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}\right)a_{n+1}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\lambda_{k}(a_{k}-a_{n+1})+a_{n+1}

\displaystyle=F(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})+a_{n+1}.

Thus if
\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{\mu}\in\mathsf{aff}(\Delta_{n})
, then

f_{n}(\mathbf{\lambda})-f_{n}(\mathbf{\mu})=F(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})-F(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})=F(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n}).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}(\mathbf{\lambda})-f_{n}(\mathbf{\mu})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|F(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|F\right\|\left\|(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|F\right\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|\leq\left\|F\right\|\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|=\left\|F\right\|\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}-\mathbf{\mu}\right\|_{1},

from which we see that
f_{n}
is uniformly continuous on
\Delta_{n}
. On the
other hand, by Proposition
4
,
f_{n}
is injective and so has
inverse mapping
f_{n}^{-1}
from
\mathsf{aff}\left\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\right\}
onto
\mathsf{aff}(\Delta_{n})
. If
\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{\mu}\in\mathsf{aff}(\Delta_{n})
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}-\mathbf{\mu}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|+\left|\lambda_{n+1}-\mu_{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|+\left|\left(1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\right)-\left(1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{k}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|+\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=2\left\|(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=2\left\|F^{-1}\circ F(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|F^{-1}\right\|\left\|F(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}-\mu_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle=2\left\|F^{-1}\right\|\left\|f_{n}(\mathbf{\lambda})-f_{n}(\mathbf{\mu})\right\|,

from which we see that
f_{n}^{-1}
is uniformly continuous on
\mathsf{aff}\left\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\right\}
.
\square

Lemma 17

Let
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}
and
\mathbf{\mu}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|.

Proof.
For
1\leq k\leq n
we have

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{n+1}(\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k})+\lambda_{k}(\mu_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda_{n+1})\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|+\lambda_{k}\left|\mu_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|+\left|\mu_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1}\right|\lambda_{k}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}2\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|+\left|\mu_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1}\right|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}2\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|+\left|\mu_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|.

\square

Lemma 18

Let
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}
be such that
\lambda_{n+1}<1
, let
0<\theta<1
, and let
\mathbf{\mu}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
be such
that
\left|1-\mu_{n+1}\right|\geq\theta\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)
. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\mu_{k}}{1-\mu_{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{\theta\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|.

Proof.
By Lemma
17
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\mu_{k}}{1-\mu_{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})}{(1-\mu_{n+1})(1-\lambda_{n+1})}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|}{\left|1-\mu_{n+1}\right|\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{k}(1-\lambda_{n+1})-\lambda_{k}(1-\mu_{n+1})\right|}{\left|1-\mu_{n+1}\right|\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\theta\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\mu_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|.

\square

We define

\Delta_{n}^{+}\equiv\left\{\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}:\lambda_{k}>0\text{
}(0\leq k\leq n+1)\right\}.

Proposition 19

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}
be affinely independent elements of
a real normed linear space
X
, and let
F=\mathsf{co}\left\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right\}.
Let
c=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
, where

\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}
and
0<\lambda_{n+1}<1
. Then

b\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}a_{k}

is the unique point in which the line
L:a_{n+1}+\mathbf{R}(c-a_{n+1})
meets

F
. For each
r>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\mathbf{\xi}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}=1
,
x=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}a_{k}
, and
\left\|x-c\right\|<\delta
, then
0<\xi_{n+1}<1
and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}a_{k}\in B_{X}(b,r)\cap\mathsf{aff}(F).

(4)

Proof.
Since
\lambda_{n+1}=1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}<1,

\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\geq 0
for each
k\leq n
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}=1
,
b
is well defined and belongs to
F
. On
the other hand, if the line
L
meets
F
at the point

\displaystyle\left(1-t\right)a_{n+1}+tc

\displaystyle=\left(1-t\right)a_{n+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}t\lambda_{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(1-t+t\lambda_{n+1}\right)a_{n+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}t\lambda_{k}a_{k},

then
1-t+t\lambda_{n+1}=0
,
t=1/(1-\lambda_{n+1})
, and therefore the point
in question equals
b
.

Now let
r>0
,

M=\max_{1\leq k\leq n+1}\left\|a_{k}\right\|

and

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\lambda_{n+1},1-\lambda_{n+1},\frac{\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)^{2}r}{2M}\right\}.

By Lemma
7
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\ \mathbf{\xi}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}=1
,
x=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}a_{k}
, and
\left\|x-c\right\|<\delta
, then
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\xi_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|<\varepsilon
. For such

\mathbf{\xi}
and
x
we have
\left|\xi_{n+1}-\lambda_{n+1}\right|<\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\lambda_{n+1},1-\lambda_{n+1}\right\}
,
so
\ \xi_{n+1}>\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{n+1}>0
and

\xi_{n+1}<\lambda_{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\lambda_{n+1})<1.

Hence

\left|1-\xi_{n+1}\right|=1-\xi_{n+1}>1-\frac{1}{2}(1+\lambda_{n+1})=\frac{1}{2}(1-\lambda_{n+1})\text{.}

Since also
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}=1
, we see that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}a_{k}\in\mathsf{aff}(F)
. On the
other hand, by Lemma
18
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}a_{k}-b\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\right)a_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\right|\left\|a_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M}{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left|\xi_{k}-\lambda_{k}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{4M\varepsilon}{\left(1-\lambda_{n+1}\right)^{2}}<r,

so (
4
) holds.
\square

Relative interior points

If
X
is a real normed linear space, then the
relative interior
,

\mathsf{relint}(S)
, of
S\subset X
is the interior of
S
relative to

\mathsf{aff}(S)
: thus

\mathsf{relint}(S)=\left\{x\in S:\exists r>0\left(B_{X}(x,r)\cap\mathsf{aff}(S)\subset S\right)\right\}.

Points of
\mathsf{relint}(S)
are called
relative interior points of

S

in
X
.

Corollary 20

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-simplex with vertices
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\
in a real normed linear space
X
, let
F=\mathsf{co}\left\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right\}
, and let
c=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
, where
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}
and
0<\lambda_{n+1}<1
. If

b\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{1-\lambda_{n+1}}a_{k}

is a relative interior point of
F
, then
c
is a relative interior point of

\Sigma
.

Proof.
If
r>0
and
B_{X}(b,r)\cap\mathsf{aff}(F)\subset F
, construct
\delta
as
in Proposition
19
. Let
\mathbf{\xi}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
and

x\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}a_{k}\in B_{X}(c,\delta)\cap\mathsf{aff}(\Sigma).

By our choice of
\delta
,
0<\xi_{n+1}<1
and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}a_{k}\in B_{X}(b,r)\cap\mathsf{aff}(F)\subset F\subset\Sigma.

Thus

x=\left(1-\xi_{n+1}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{k}}{1-\xi_{n+1}}a_{k}+\xi_{n+1}a_{n+1},

which is a convex combination of two elements of
\Sigma
and therefore

\Sigma
. It follows that

and hence that

is a relative interior point of

\Sigma
.
\square

. If
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbf{R}

(1\leq k\leq n)
and
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|>0
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}y_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}x_{k}\right\|-\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}(y_{k}-x_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|\left\|(y_{k}-x_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left(2n+1\right)\delta\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|\delta

\displaystyle=2n\delta\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|>0\text{. }

Thus the vectors
y_{k}

(1\leq k\leq n)
are linearly independent.
\square

Corollary 27

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}
be affinely independent elements of
a real normed linear space
X
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that if

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\
belong to
X
and
\left\|a_{k}-x_{k}\right\|<\delta
for each
k\leq n+1
, then the vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}
are
affinely independent.

Proof.
By Lemma
26
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
y_{k}\in X
and

\left\|\left(a_{k}-a_{n+1}\right)-y_{k}\right\|<2\delta
for each

k\leq n
, then
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
are linearly independent. If
x_{k}\in X

and
\ \left\|a_{k}-x_{k}\right\|<\delta
for each
k\leq n+1
, then

\left\|\left(a_{k}-a_{n+1}\right)-\left(x_{k}-x_{n+1}\right)\right\|\leq\left\|a_{k}-x_{k}\right\|+\left\|a_{n+1}-x_{n+1}\right\|<2\delta\ \ \ (1\leq k\leq n),

from which the result follows.
\square

We need some elementary facts about square matrices. We define two norms on
the linear space
M_{n\text{ }}
of all
n
-by-
n
matrices over
\mathbf{R}

as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left|a_{ij}\right|,

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\sup\left\{\left\|A\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}:\mathbf{\lambda\in R}^{n},\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}\leq 1\right\},

where
a_{ij}
is the
\left(i,j\right)^{\mathsf{th}}
entry of the matrix

A
, and
\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|
. Since
M_{n}
is finite-dimensional, there exists

\kappa>0
such that
\left\|A\right\|_{0}\leq\kappa\left\|A\right\|_{1}
for all
A\in M_{n}
.

Lemma 28

For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if

A\equiv\left[\xi_{kj}\right]
is an
n
-by-
n
matrix over
\mathbf{R}

and
\left\|A-I\right\|_{1}<\delta
, then
\det A>1/2\,\
and

\left\|A^{-1}-I\right\|_{1}<\varepsilon
.

Proof.
The mapping
\det
is continuous at
I
, and the mapping carrying
A
to its
adjoint is continuous everywhere. Thus the mapping

A\rightsquigarrow A^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det A}\mathsf{adj}A

is continuous at
I
. Since the set of all
n
-by-
n
matrices over

\mathbf{R}
can be regarded as a linear subspace of
\mathbf{R}^{n^{2}}
, on
which all norms are equivalent, the result follows.
\square

Theorem 29

Let
X
be a real normed linear space,
\Sigma
an
n
-simplex
with vertices
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}
in
X
, and
c\
a relative interior
point of
\Sigma
. There exists
\delta>0
such that if
x_{k}\in\mathsf{aff}(\Sigma)
and
\left\|x_{k}-a_{k}\right\|<\delta
for each

k\leq n+1
, then the points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}
are affinely independent,
and
\ c
is a relative interior point of the
n
-simplex
\mathsf{co}\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\right\}
.

Proof.
By Corollary
27
, there exists
\delta_{1}
with
0<\delta_{1}<1/2

such that if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\
belong to
X
and
\left\|a_{k}-x_{k}\right\|<\delta_{1}
for each
k\leq n+1
, then
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}
are affinely independent. By Proposition
22
,

c=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}
, where
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\Delta_{n}^{+}
. Let

m=\min_{1\leq k\leq n+1}\lambda_{k}.

With
\kappa
as in the sentence immediately before Lemma
28
,
choose
\delta_{2}
with
0<\delta_{2}<\delta_{1}
such that if
A
is an

n
-by-
n
matrix over
\mathbf{R}
and
\left\|A-I\right\|_{1}<\delta_{2}
, then
\det A>1/2
,
\left\|A^{-1}-I\right\|_{1}<m/2\kappa
, and therefore
\left\|A^{-1}-I\right\|_{0}<m/2
.
Using Lemma
7
, construct
\delta
with
0<\delta<\delta_{2}
such
that if

\ \mathbf{\xi},\mathbf{\eta}\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1},\ \sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}=1=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\eta_{k},\ x=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k}a_{k},\ y=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\eta_{k}a_{k},

and
\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\left(\xi_{k}-\eta_{k}\right)a_{k}\right\|
then
\left|\xi_{k}-\eta_{k}\right|<\delta_{2}

for each
k\leq n+1
. Consider points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}
of
\mathsf{aff}(\Sigma)
such that
\left\|x_{k}-a_{k}\right\|<\delta
for each

k\leq n+1
. Since
\delta<\delta_{1}
, those points
x_{k}
are affinely
independent, so
\Sigma^{\prime}\equiv\mathsf{co}\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\right\}
is an
n
-simplex. For each
k
there exists a unique

\mathbf{\xi}_{k}\equiv\left(\xi_{k,1},\xi_{k,2},\ldots,\xi_{k,n+1}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k,j}=1
and
x_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k,j}a_{j}
. Then

\left|\xi_{k,k}-1\right|<\delta_{2}\text{ and }\left|\xi_{k,j}\right|<\delta_{2}\text{ for }j\neq k.

(5)

I claim that the equations

\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\xi_{k,j}=\lambda_{j}\ \ \ \ (1\leq j\leq n+1).

(6)

have a unique solution
\mathbf{\gamma}
, that
\mathbf{\gamma}\in\Delta_{n}^{+}
, and that
c=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{j}x_{j}
. To justify
this claim, letting
\Xi
be the
n
-by-
n
matrix with
\left(j,k\right)^{\mathsf{th}}
component
\xi_{j,k}
, we see that the equations can be
written as
\Xi^{T}\mathbf{\gamma}=\mathbf{\lambda}
, where
T
denotes
transpose. From (
5
) we see that
\left\|\Xi^{T}-I\right\|_{1}<\delta_{2}
, so

\det\Xi^{T}>\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\left\|\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}-I\right\|_{0}<\frac{m}{2}.

(7)

The equations (
6
) therefore have a unique solution
\mathbf{\gamma}=\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{\lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{j}a_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\xi_{k,j}a_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\xi_{k,j}a_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k,j}a_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}x_{k}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}1=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\xi_{k,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\xi_{k,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{k}\xi_{k,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\lambda_{j}=1\text{,}

from which we see that
c\in\mathsf{aff}\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\right\}
. On the other hand, by (
7
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\left|\gamma_{j}-\lambda_{j}\right|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{\lambda-\lambda}\right\|_{1}=\left\|\left(\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}-I\right)\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}-I\right\|_{0}\left\|\mathbf{\lambda}\right\|_{1}=\left\|\left(\Xi^{T}\right)^{-1}-I\right\|_{0}<\frac{m}{2},

so for each
j\leq n+1
,

\gamma_{j}>\lambda_{j}-\frac{m}{2}\geq m-\frac{m}{2}>0\text{.}

Since also
\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\gamma_{j}=1
,
\mathbf{\gamma}\in\Delta_{n}^{+}

and therefore, by Proposition
22
(iii),
c
is a relative interior
point
\Sigma^{\prime}
.
\square

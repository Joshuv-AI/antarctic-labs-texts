Intention to adherence to social distancing for prevention of Covid-19 in the urban areas of southern Iran: a structural equation modeling (SEM) analysis of theory of planned behavior
Authors: Tayebeh Rakhshani, Victoria Momenabadi, Abdolrahim Asadollahi, Ali Khani Jeihooni
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSocial distancing is a key behavior to minimize and controlling COVID-19 infections. The current study applied an integrated Theory of Planned Behavior to identify the determinants of social distancing behavior, and the processes involved, in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.Methodsa cross-sectional study was conducted in Southern Iran among 1000 people from Shiraz by employing a convenience sampling technique. Data were collected using a pre-tested and structured questionnaire based on the main constructs of the Theory of Planned Behavior. Statistical analysis was done using IBM SPSS software version 22 and Amos version 24. Level of statistical significance was declared at a P-value of less than 0.05.Resultsaccording to the results, the subjective norms (F = 2.696, effect size = 0.139) and attitude (F = 3.582, effect size = 0.155) play an important role in the samples’ PBC (P ≤ 0.05). Final adjusted structural equation model of the effects of independent variables (Gender, subjective norms, attitude) on samples’ intention to adherence social distancing for prevention of Covid-19 with the mediating role of one main variable of PBC. The PBC alone can be an important factor in creating intensive behavior in the samples (F = 3.560, effect size = 0.18).Conclusioncurrent findings highlight the importance of “attitude, subjective norms and PBC” as determinants of social distancing intention. Future research on long-range predictors of social distancing behavior and reciprocal effects in the integrated model is warranted. This evidence is relevant to public and private organizations in the process of developing strategies to promote and engage the population in adopting the behavior of “Adherence to Social Distancing” in Iran.

Background

On December 31, 2019, an outbreak of unusual cases of pneumonia caused by the corona virus was reported in Wuhan, China [1]. On January 30, 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) issued a statement declaring the outbreak of Covid-19 to be the sixth leading cause of public health emergency worldwide, and the disease is now pandemic throughout the world [2, 3].

Thus, it is important for experts in all fields to be fully aware of the characteristics of this virus and the measures needed to protect the health of workers and the general public. The rapid spread of this new virus in different parts of the world and in most countries indicates the importance of rapid transmission and spread of the disease. Coronaviruses are large RNA viruses that were first identified in 1960. Coronavirus is a common disease between humans and animals, but the disease caused by a new coronavirus, called Covid-19, is associated with the symptoms different from other coronaviruses [4].

According to the current knowledge, Covid-19 can be mild to severe with symptoms such as fever, coughs, and shortness of breath, and in more severe cases may lead to pneumonia, kidney failure, and death. The death rate from this virus is reported to be about 2%. In a study by Guan et al., a mortality rate of 1.4% was reported among 1,099 laboratory-approved COVID-19 patients [5, 6]. Preliminary studies have also shown that people with underlying diseases such as heart disease, diabetes, chronic respiratory disease, hypertension, and cancer are at a higher risk of the complications and mortality from the new corona disease [7].

Identifying the ways of transmission of any pathogen plays a crucial role in controlling that disease. So, as a general principle, it can be said that respiratory viruses are transmitted mainly through the droplets produced by coughing or sneezing or by touching infected objects [5].

The airborne transmission of corona virus has played a very important role in the epidemiology of SARS-CoV-1 and MERS-Cov during the last two decades [8].

In addition, one of the most important ways of transmission of SARS-COV-2 virus is the patients’ droplets and their contact with different surfaces infected with the virus [9], because these viruses are active on different surfaces for various periods of time and can cause disease transmission [10].

According to the latest information, the most important transmission route of the new corona virus is from human to human through coughing, sneezing, discharge of nasal and oral secretions, and touching common objects infected with the virus, because it can travel 1 to 2 m through coughs or sneezes to be transmitted to others [5, 6]. Therefore, in order to effectively prevent the new coronavirus, namely Covid-19, through aerosol transmission of large droplets of exhalation while speaking, a safe social distance of 1.6 to 3.0 m is recommended. Given that observing social distancing by people can greatly reduce the chance of contracting the virus, one of the key strategies in the fight against Covid-19 is to apply social distancing to all age groups [10].

One of the main measures taken in social distancing is the reduction of traffic between cities, which reduces the population density in urban areas. Besides, to observe social distancing, some jobs are forcibly closed and some others continue in the form of teleworking [10].

Considering the problems that exist in creating, maintaining, and continuing social distancing behavior, it is necessary to use behavioral change theories and models, because they specify the main factors that affect the intended behavior. The theories also help us identify the factors that should be emphasized in interventions. In other words, theories and models are useful in identifying the elements that should be considered as the main focus of interventions [11].

One of the theories that helps to identify the factors affecting behavior is the theory of planned behavior (TPB), according to which attitudes toward behavior, subjective norms about behavior, and perceived behavioral control are the three main components that shape an individual’s behavioral intentions. The theory of planned behavior is a powerful model to explain the factors affecting behavioral intention. The theory of planned behavior is one of the most effective theories in social and health psychology. This model expresses the complexity of the relationships between human behavior and its determining factors, and more importantly, it points out that human behavior is the result of her intentions [12]. According to the TPB, all conscious, deliberate behavior is determined by the formation of an intention [13]. During the formation of an intention, the subject elaborates a synthesis of possible consequences and the effort needed to behave in a given way, based on underlying beliefs established during his or her lifetime. At the end of this psychological process, the intention is formed and it becomes the last antecedent to the behavior itself. The process of forming the intention, based on a group of beliefs and their relation to behavior, constitutes the central object of the TPB [14, 15].

The constructs of the planned behavior model include behavior, behavioral intention, attitude toward behavior, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. In this theory, it is stated that behavior or behavioral intention is affected by some factors beyond the individual’s control (environmental, social, and cultural factors, etc.) [16, 17].

“Attitude towards behavior” means to what extent the intended behavior (observing social distancing) is desirable, pleasant, useful, or enjoyable for the person; it depends on the individual’s judgment about the effects and consequences of the behavior. “Subjective norms” refer to the amount of social pressure perceived by the individual to engage in the behavior (observance of social distancing); in other words, it is the reflection of social influence on the individual. “Perceived behavioral control” is the degree to which a person feels performing or not performing behavior (observing social distancing) is under his/her voluntary control. Perceived behavioral control may affect the behavior (observance of social distancing) both directly and indirectly through intention [18].

Hagger et al. studied the predictors of social distancing intention and behavior during the corona pandemic and showed that subjective norms and perceived behavioral control were predictors of social distancing intention and behavior [11].

The results of the study conducted by Iwaya et al. based on the theory of planned behavior indicated that attitude, perceived behavioral control, and subjective norms predicted the intention to observe social distancing to prevent the coronavirus [18].

In their study, Andarge et al. found that perceived behavioral control and subjective norms predicted coronavirus-preventing intentions and behaviors [19].

Broomell et al. examined three predictors of preventive behavior, including social distancing, hand washing, and wearing face masks, and suggested that subjective norms were strong predictors of these three behaviors [20].

The results of the study conducted by Tajerimoghadam et al. that attitude, intention and subjective norms predicted coronavirus-preventing behaviors [21].

Since the covid-19 pandemic in 2019, the corona disease has had various strains and sub-strains, so that On 22 February 2023, cases of COVID-19 infection were reported in Qom, Tehran, Gilan, and Markazi provinces. The prevalence of COVID-19 in Iran began to increase on 2 March. Studies have shown that the spatial distribution of COVID-19 rapidly spread from Tehran, Qom, Gilan, Markazi, Mazandaran, and Isfahan provinces to their surrounding regions [22].

Therefore, according to the various mutations of this virus, people should receive the necessary health education in the field of observing social distancing, so that during the epidemic of any of the strains of Covid-19, by cutting the chain of transmission of the disease, the peak of Corona can be contained. Thus, considering that Covid-19 is an emerging disease, the importance of the issue and examining the factors related to social distancing to prevent the disease as well as the need to identify the factors affecting social distancing behaviors based on the TPB and in line with planning to prevent Covid-19 were identified as the most effective variables of the TPB in creating social distancing behaviors. Accordingly, effective and coherent educational interventions are needed to be designed and implemented. Hence, the present study aimed to investigate the predictors of social distancing intention to prevent coronavirus (Covid-19) based on the theory of planned behavior.

Methods

Type of study and participants

This is a cross-sectional and analytical research based on online studies. It was conducted in 2021 on the information of 1000 people from Shiraz who were included in the study through convenience sampling. Shiraz is the capital of Fars province and is located in the southwest of Iran. In this study, the link of an online questionnaire was sent to 1150 individuals, of whom 1000 completed and sent it (Response rate = 86/95).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: Minimum literacy, 10 < years of age, having a mobile phone with WhatsApp software.

Exclusion criteria: unwillingness to participate in the study and failure to complete the questionnaire completely.

Sample size

According to Hagger et al. [11] and Based on the sample size formula in the correlation studies [23], with a confidence interval of 95%, the test power of 80% and Considering the probability of dropout rate, the sample size was 1000 clients in this study concerning its importance.

Data collection instruments

Due to the possibility of the coronavirus transmission through a paper questionnaire, the authors decided to send an electronic questionnaire through the WhatsApp social media to the individuals with the inclusion criteria. They sent the questionnaire link after obtaining the code of ethics from the Vice Chancellor for Research of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences.

The data were collected using self-report questionnaire, as described below:

The data collection tool was a researcher-made questionnaire based on the review of related texts and sources [18] and on the basis of the TPB structures.

To determine the face validity of the tool, a list of the compiled items was provided to 40 individuals with demographic, economic, and social characteristics similar to the target population.

The content validity of the tool was also confirmed by twelve experts (outside the research team) in the fields of health education and promotion (n = 10) and infectious disease (n = 2).

The overall reliability of the questionnaire was reported through a pilot study with the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.84, CR:0.84, AVE: 0.66 and r = 0.98.

The first part of the questionnaire included the questions on demographic information such as age, sex, occupation, education, marital status, insurance coverage, and recent travels.

The second part included4sections and 24 questions that were designed based on the constructs of the theory of planned behavior on a 5-point Likert scale (strongly agree - agree –no idea - disagree - strongly disagree).

Six questions of the second part were related to attitude (sample question: I think maintaining social distancing for the next few days is a logical choice), 6 questions were related to subjective norms (sample question: Most people who are important to me like me to maintain social distancing for the next few days.), 6 questions were associated with perceived behavioral control (sample question: I believe it will be easy to maintain social distancing for the next few days), and 6 were related to intention to observe social distancing (sample question: I am going to maintain social distancing for the next few days).

Ethical considerations

After selecting the individuals, the online questionnaire was completed by them. The informed consent form and study objectives were placed at the beginning of the online questionnaire, and participants were assured that their information would remain strictly confidential with the researchers. after accepting that “I participated in the research with my consent”, people started completing the online questionnaire. The Research Council approved this study of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences with the code of ethics number IR.SUMS.REC.1400.312.

Data analysis

Prior to the analysis, the normality of main research variables was assessed using the Shapiro -Wilk Test. Moreover, data analysis was executed employing SPSS software version 22 and Amos version 24 (IBM Co., Ann Arbore). The average and standard deviation values were used to describe the data. Analysing the data was carried out by utilizing the one-way ANOVA, Chi-Square and structural equation analysis (SEM). During data analysis, various model indicators such as the goodness of fit, including CFI and GFI (above 0.9), RMSEA (below 0.08), and Chi-square index/degrees of freedom (below 3) were evaluated, all of which indicating the suitability of the model. The significance level in all tests was considered 0.05.

Discussion

Regarding to the importance of Covid-19 and the significant role of social distancing in reducing the incidence of new corona, the present study aimed to investigate the predictors of the intention to observe social distancing to prevent Covid-19 based on the theory of planned behavior. This study showed a good relationship between the proposed model and the data. According to the results, attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control were the main predictors of behavioral intention. The authors then analyzed the results of the study.

In the present study, attitude had a significant positive relationship with the Intention to Adherence to Social Distancing, which was also verified by SEM results. Also, the final results of path analysis indicated that the intention to observe social distancing behavior may be direct was predicted by the attitude variable. The standardized coefficients of the direct path, one-unit increase in the attitude improved the average intention to observe social distancing behavior score by 0.21. Consistent with the results of the present research, the studies by Yanqiu et al. [25], Jacob et al. [26] and Iwaya et al. [18] showed that attitude predicted social distancing observance behavior. Attitude is an individual factor influencing behavior. It is the person’s belief in the outcomes of health-related behaviors that is weighted based on the value of the results. In fact, the more people believe that doing preventive behaviors can be useful and wise and protects them from getting sick, the more they will have a positive desire and attitude to follow such recommendations [27]. In this regard, various media are suggested to provide more frequent and interesting explanations of the effects of preventive health measures in pandemics such as Covid-19 in order to improve people’s attitudes. For example, social media and networks can be used to present accurate statistics about the disease and provide practical explanations to show the effect of preventive behaviors such as observing social distancing, disinfecting hands, and wearing masks on reducing the incidence of the disease.

The results of this study showed a significant positive relationship between subjective norms and the intention to observe social distancing behavior, which was also verified by SEM results. Also, the final results of path analysis indicated that the intention to observe social distancing behavior may be directly was predicted by subjective norms variable. The standardized coefficients of the direct path, one-unit increase in the subjective norms improved the average intention to observe social distancing behavior score by 0.32 According to the social-ecological model, interpersonal factors are very influential on people’s health just like personal factors [28]. Subjective norms are considered an interpersonal factor affecting behavior. It refers to the social pressures perceived by the individual and the expectations of the people who are important in the individual’s life, such as relatives, family, and friends. In fact, the more the people who are important to a person expect him/her to engage in preventative behaviors, or the more they approve of him/her when engaging in such behaviors, the more likely the person will be to follow recommendations. This is consistent with the results of the studies by Iwaya et al., Yanqiu et al. [18, 25], Gabriel et al. [29] and Hagger et al. [11].Given that learning from friends and relatives, mass media, social media, and health officials and experts about Covid-19 and the ways to prevent it positively predicts preventative behaviors, the policies and programs of the Ministry of Health to encourage and mobilize the public to take preventive behaviors can include education through the mass media, social networks, posters, and advertisements. On the other hand, if such programs are continuously implemented at regular intervals, people will be constantly exposed to the announcements or reminders of preventive behaviors, and consequently, their emotions will be aroused and they will increasingly engage in the behavior.

The results of this study showed a significant positive relationship between perceived behavioral control and the intention to observe social distancing behavior, which was also verified by SEM results. Also, the final results of path analysis indicated that the intention to observe social distancing behavior may be directly was predicted by perceived behavioral control variable. The standardized coefficients of the direct path, one-unit increase in the perceived behavioral control improved the average intention to observe social distancing behavior score by 0.11. This is consistent with the results of the studies by Iwaya et al. [18] and Hagger et al. [11]. Perceived behavioral control is an individual factor influencing behavior. It actually predicts the degree to which a person has voluntary control over engaging or not engaging in a behavior. The control factors include internal and external ones. Internal factors are personal and include skills, abilities, information, and emotions. However, external factors relate to environmental and occupational factors as well [18]. As stated by Ajzen, perceived behavioral control is an important and effective factor in performing any behavior. When individuals are unsure of their ability to perform certain behaviors, assessing perceived behavioral control could help predict such behaviors [30]. The results can indicate potential intervention strategies that reinforce the relationships between intention and behavior associated with perceived behavioral control.

According to the results, among the demographic factors, gender had significant positive relationship with samples’ intention to adherence social distancing for prevention of Covid-19 with the mediating role of one main variable of PBC. Furthermore, unlike the men in the present study, the women stated that they had not travelled during the corona epidemic. In line with the results of the studies by Pouyan Fard et al. [31], Clark et al. [32], and Lunn et al. [33], our findings indicated that demographic characteristics such as gender (female) and doing preventive behaviors such as social distancing and hand disinfection strongly predicted the prevention of Covid-19. Therefore, it might be stated that women and individuals with personality traits such as conscientiousness were more committed to engaging in corona-related self-care behaviors. Hence, providing appropriate psychological strategies and techniques to increase awareness, responsibility, and perceived sense of danger in all members of society, especially men, as well as providing public education through mass media and social networks seems essential.

This study specifically investigates the effect that the original predictors of TPB (attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control) exercise on the formation of intention to observe social distancing behavior. Various studies use the TPB as a reference model, proposing extensions to the model with the addition of new constructs, a practice recommended by [14], with the goal of observing whether the addition of new constructs will increase its predictive capacity. However, it is most recommended to test the TPB model with its original variables, verifying whether they explain or do not explain a significant portion of the variance in intention.

This study also supplies relevant information about which beliefs have greater effects as predictors of the intention to maintain social distancing. We have found that subjective norms related to perceived social pressure have a strong influence on the intention to maintain social distancing. The attitudes related to the perception of advantages (well-being, safety) also exercise a moderate influence on the intention to maintain social distancing. This information can be considered by public and private organizations in the elaboration of persuasive communication strategies regarding the dissemination of the practice of social distancing.

Limitations of the study

Due to restrictions of quarantine, accessing the target population was problematic. In order to solve this problem, questionnaires were distributed and completed using the internet. Other concerns included self-reporting, ambiguity in questions, lack of attention in answering, and falsified questionnaires due to the physical absence of the questioner. However, these concerns were minimized through ensuring the desirable validity and clarity of the questions and the use of the Likert scale.

Another limitation was that many people who did not have access to the Internet and virtual networks such as WhatsApp did not participate in the study.

Also, this study was conducted during the covid-19 pandemic and during the quarantine period, and it was difficult to measure the final behavior. Therefore, the intention, which is a strong predictor of the behavior, was measured. So, it is suggested to measure the real behavior in the next studies.

Conclusions

Due to climate changes as well as changes in human lifestyle, various re-emerging and emerging diseases threaten human health, and one of these diseases is the corona disease, which has had various strains and sub-strains since the pandemic. It is still considered as a threat to human health, which requires the prevention of human infection. So, Social distance is becoming increasingly important in the world, and all public and private organizations are developing strategies to promote and involve people in adopting “maintain social distance” behavior. Current findings highlight the importance of “attitude and subjective norm, as determinants of social distance. Future research on long-term predictors of behaviors and social distance interactions is essential in an integrated model. It is also recommended to perform educational interventions based on the new model to improve and adopt preventive behaviors from Covid 19 disease.

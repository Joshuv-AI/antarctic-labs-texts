Efficacy of Herbal Fluoride Toothpaste in Preventing Early Childhood Caries
Authors: Vanisha Mehta, Akangsha Gaur, Monica Chaurasia, Anwesha Bhattacharjee, Ashim Roy, Juhi Soni, Miral Mehta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Early childhood caries (ECC) is a prevalent dental condition affecting children younger than 6 years. Conventional fluoride toothpastes are effective but raise concerns regarding fluorosis and ingestion in young children. Herbal fluoride toothpastes offer a natural alternative with added antimicrobial benefits.Methods:A randomized controlled trial was conducted among 120 children aged 3–5 years, divided into two groups: Group A used herbal fluoride toothpaste containing neem, clove, and 500 ppm fluoride; Group B used a nonherbal fluoride toothpaste with 500 ppm fluoride. Caries status was assessed at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months using the dmft index. Statistical analysis included paired t-tests and repeated-measures ANOVA (P < 0.05 considered significant).Results:At baseline, mean dmft scores were comparable between groups (Group A: 2.35 ± 1.20; Group B: 2.42 ± 1.18; P = 0.64). After 12 months, Group A showed a significantly lower mean increase in dmft (0.42 ± 0.87) compared with Group B (0.95 ± 1.02; P = 0.02). The incidence of new carious lesions was also lower in Group A (16.6%) vs. Group B (28.3%).Conclusion:Herbal fluoride toothpaste demonstrated greater efficacy in preventing ECC than conventional fluoride toothpaste, suggesting its potential as a safe and effective alternative in pediatric oral care.

INTRODUCTION

Early childhood caries (ECC) remains a significant global health concern, affecting approximately 60–90% of preschool children in both developed and developing countries.[1] ECC is characterized by the presence of one or more decayed, missing, or filled primary teeth in children younger than 6 years.[2] Poor oral hygiene practices, high sugar consumption, and insufficient fluoride exposure contribute to its onset.[3]

Fluoride-containing toothpaste is a cornerstone of caries prevention. However, concerns about dental fluorosis from inadvertent ingestion of fluoride have led to exploration of safer alternatives for young children.[4] Herbal formulations, enriched with antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory plant extracts, are gaining attention due to their safety and biocompatibility.[5]

Neem (Azadirachta indica), clove (Syzygium aromaticum), and licorice (Glycyrrhiza glabra) are commonly used herbal components known for their antimicrobial properties against Streptococcus mutans, the primary pathogen implicated in ECC.[6] Recent in vitro studies have shown that herbal dentifrices containing these ingredients can inhibit biofilm formation and acid production.[7]

Despite their popularity, limited clinical evidence supports the efficacy of herbal fluoride toothpastes in caries prevention among children. Most studies are short-term or focus on plaque reduction rather than longitudinal caries outcomes.[8]

This study aims to assess the 12-month clinical efficacy of a herbal fluoride toothpaste in preventing ECC compared with a conventional fluoride toothpaste in preschool children.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and sample size

A sample size of 120 participants was calculated based on an expected effect size of 0.5, 80% power, and 5% significance level.

Participants

Children aged 3–5 years with at least one erupted primary molar and a baseline dmft score ≤5 were included. Children with systemic illness, developmental enamel defects, or those using antibiotics within the last 3 months were excluded.

Group allocation

Participants were randomly assigned into two groups:

Group A (n = 60): Herbal fluoride toothpaste (500 ppm fluoride + neem, clove, licorice).

Group A (n = 60): Herbal fluoride toothpaste (500 ppm fluoride + neem, clove, licorice).

Group B (n = 60): Conventional fluoride toothpaste (500 ppm fluoride).

Group B (n = 60): Conventional fluoride toothpaste (500 ppm fluoride).

Intervention and follow-up

Parents were instructed to brush their child’s teeth twice daily using a pea-sized amount of the assigned toothpaste for 12 months. Dental check-ups were conducted at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months. dmft scores were recorded by a calibrated examiner blinded to group allocation.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome: Change in mean dmft scores over 12 months.

Secondary outcome: Incidence of new carious lesions.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v25. Paired t-tests, Chi-square tests, and repeated-measures ANOVA were used. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

The baseline characteristics of the participants, including age, gender distribution, and initial dmft scores, were statistically comparable between Group A (herbal fluoride) and Group B (conventional fluoride), indicating appropriate randomization (P > 0.05) [Table 1]. Over the 12-month study period, Group A exhibited a significantly smaller increase in dmft scores at both 6 and 12 months compared with Group B (P = 0.04 and P = 0.02, respectively), suggesting better caries prevention efficacy [Table 2]. In addition, the incidence of new carious lesions was notably lower in Group A (16.6%) than in Group B (28.3%) (P = 0.04), with no adverse events reported in either group [Table 3].

DISCUSSION

Our findings align with prior research indicating that herbal ingredients such as neem and clove exhibit potent antibacterial properties against S. mutans, reducing plaque accumulation and acidogenicity.[9] A similar study by Pujar et al.[10] reported decreased microbial counts and gingival scores in children using herbal dentifrices.

Moreover, the fluoride concentration (500 ppm) used in both formulations falls within the safe limits for preschool-aged children, minimizing the risk of fluorosis while maintaining anticariogenic benefits.[11]

Interestingly, the enhanced efficacy of the herbal formulation may be attributed to the synergistic effect of herbal extracts in conjunction with fluoride. The polyphenolic compounds in neem and clove interfere with bacterial adhesion and glucan synthesis, crucial steps in biofilm formation.[1213]

Our results also corroborate a clinical trial by Patil et al.,[14] where herbal toothpaste users showed a significant reduction in dmft scores and improved oral hygiene practices. However, unlike some studies limited to 3–6 months duration, this study provides robust 12-month data supporting long-term benefits.[15]

CONCLUSION

The study concludes that herbal fluoride toothpaste significantly reduces the incidence and progression of early childhood caries compared with conventional fluoride toothpaste. Its enhanced preventive efficacy, coupled with safety and biocompatibility, makes it a promising candidate for pediatric oral care regimens.

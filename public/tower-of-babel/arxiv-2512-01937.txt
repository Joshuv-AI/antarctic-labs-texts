On the C 2 C^{2} -local systolic optimality of Zoll odd-symplectic forms
Samanyu Sanjay

Abstract
An odd-symplectic form is a closed and maximally non-degenerate 2 2 -form on a compact odd-dimensional manifold. It describes the dynamics of an autonomous Hamiltonian system on a regular energy level. It is called Zoll if the induced dynamics is a free circle action, up to a global time reparameterization. This article establishes a normal form theorem for odd-symplectic forms close to a Zoll one and cohomologous to it, which is then used to prove that Zoll odd-symplectic forms are the local maximizers of the associated systolic ratio. This generalizes the known systolic optimality of Zoll contact forms in the C 3 C^{3} -topology. As an application, local systolic inequalities are established in symplectic manifolds for hypersurfaces close to Zoll ones. In particular, this applies to certain non-exact twisted cotangent bundles of manifolds of dimension greater than two.

1.2

1.3.2

Consider a Riemannian manifold
(W,g)
of dimension at least two and an arbitrary closed
2
-form
\sigma\in\Omega^{2}(W)

determined by the pair
(g,\sigma)
. The flow

when they are parametrized by arc length. Observe that if

coincides with the geodesic flow of the Riemannian manifold

and the magnetic geodesics on

[

]
and S.P Novikov

and has since grown into a vibrant area of study in symplectic dynamics; see
[

From the point of view of theoretical physics, magnetic dynamical systems can be thought of as modeling the motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field represented by

If the strength at which a particular magnetic system is considered is obvious, we will suppress it in the notation. \displaystyle H_{0}:

\displaystyle T(Q)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(q,

\displaystyle p)\mapsto\pi||p||_{g_{0}}^{2}\;\;\;\;\text{where $\pi\in\mathbb{R}$ is the real constant}

It is worth observing here that in the three-dimensional setting, as observed in

In Theorem

9
Magnetic dynamics on Kähler manifolds

To make this work self-contained, we begin with some relevant preliminaries about Kähler manifolds.

A Kähler manifold is defined to be a triple
(M,\sigma_{0},J)
where
M
is a smooth manifold,
\sigma_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(M)
a symplectic form and
J
an integrable almost complex structure that is compatible with
\sigma_{0}
. Recall that an almost complex structure
J
is said to be integrable if it induces complex charts on
M
and it is compatible with
\sigma_{0}
if the pair
(\sigma_{0},J)
determines a Riemannian metric on
M
in the following way:

\displaystyle g(u,v):=\sigma_{0}(u,Jv)\;\;\;\forall\;u,v\in TM.

(186)

The Riemannian metric determined by the symplectic form and complex structure corresponding to a Kähler manifold will be called the associated Kähler metric. The holomorphic sectional curvature of a Kähler metric
g_{0}
associated with a given Kähler manifold
(M,\sigma_{0},J)
is defined to be:

\displaystyle\text{Hol}:TM\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(187)

\displaystyle(q,v)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{g_{0}(v,R(v,Jv)Jv)}{||v||_{g_{0}}^{4}}

where
R
is the Riemannian curvature tensor of
g_{0}
. We observe here that if
u,v\in TM
span the same complex line, then
\text{Hol}(u)=\text{Hol}(v)
. Therefore, the holomorphic sectional curvature can be considered a function from the complex projectivization
\mathbb{P}(TM)
to the real line.

A recent result of J. Bimmermann asserts the existence of Zoll magnetic systems on Kähler manifolds of constant holomorphic sectional curvature (c.f Theorem
9.1
). We fix some notational conventions before we state the relevant version of Bimmermann’s result here. Firstly, for
\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we will write:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\rho}M:=\{p\in T^{*}M\;\;|\;\;||p||^{2}_{g_{0}}\leq\rho\}

(188)

Next, given a Kähler manifold
(M,\sigma_{0},J)
the following vector field generates the fiber-wise rotation on
T^{*}M
:

\displaystyle V_{(q,p)}:=(Jp)^{\mathcal{V}}

(189)

where the
\mathcal{V}
in the superscript above denotes the lift of the relevant vector to the vertical distribution in
TT^{*}M
. We will denote by
\tau
the dual to the above vector field with respect to the Sasaki metric. In addition to this, to stress the strength of the magnetic system being considered, given some
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we will denote by

the magnetic geodesic vector field of the magnetic system determined by the pair
(g_{0},\sigma_{0})
on the Riemannain manifold
(M,\sigma_{0})
of strength
s
; where
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(T^{*}W)
was the Liouville
1
-form and the metric Hamiltonian is defined using the associated Kähler metric

. The relevant version of the result of Bimmermann referenced above is the following:

.

16

\kappa

and the following symplectomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi:\bigg(\mathbb{D}_{\rho}M,d\lambda-s\pi^{*}\sigma_{0}\bigg)\rightarrow\bigg(\mathbb{D}_{a(\rho)}M,\frac{d\tau}{2}-s\pi^{*}\sigma_{0}\bigg)\;\;\;\text{for all $\rho$ such that $s^{2}+\kappa\rho^{2}>0$}

whose composition with the inclusion

1
-form

\displaystyle a(r)=\begin{cases}\bigg(\frac{2}{|\kappa|}\big(\sqrt{s^{2}+\kappa r^{2}}-s\big)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\;\text{if $\kappa\neq 0$}\\

\frac{r}{\sqrt{s}}\;\;\;\text{if $\kappa=0$}\end{cases}

In the theorem above, if the Kähler manifold

10.1
The size of a
k
-form in terms of its differential

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
and let
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Omega^{k}(M)
for
k<n
. Then, we can find an
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and a sequence
L_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that:

1.

d\alpha=d\tilde{\alpha}

2.

||\alpha||_{C^{k}}\leq L_{k}||d\tilde{\alpha}||_{C^{k}}

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is simple, we use the ellipticity of the Hodge Laplacian to obtain the required bounds after using the Hodge decomposition of
\Omega^{k}(M)
to find the required
\alpha
.

Towards that end, recall that the Hodge Laplacian is a formally self-adjoint elliptic operator defined in the following way(c.f
[
31
, 10.1.22 & 10.1.29]
:

\displaystyle H_{\Delta,k}:=\delta_{k+1}\circ d_{k}+d_{k-1}\circ\delta_{k}:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k}(M)

(262)

where
d_{k}:\Omega^{k}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(M)
is the deRahm exterior derivative and
\delta_{k+1}:\Omega^{k+1}(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k}M)
it’s formal adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product. By the Hodge decomposition theorem
[
31
]
[Theorem 10.4.30 (3)] we know that
\Omega^{k}(M)
has the following orthogonal decomposition:

\displaystyle\Omega^{k}(\Sigma)=\ker(H_{\Delta,k})\bigoplus\Ima(d_{k-1})\bigoplus\Ima(\delta_{k+1})

We now define
\alpha
to be the projection of
\tilde{\alpha}
onto the third factor in the above direct sum decomposition. This ensures that
d\alpha=d\tilde{\alpha}
because, clearly
\ker(H_{\Delta,k})=\ker(d_{k})\cap\ker(\delta_{k})
. So, to finish the proof, we need only to establish the bounds in the statement of Proposition
10.1
.

Since
\alpha
is orthogonal to the kernel of
H_{\Delta,k}
, the Poincaré inequality (
[
31
, Lemma 10.4.9]
) and Morrey’s inequality
[
31
, 10.2.36 (d)]
imply the following bounds:

\displaystyle||\alpha||_{C^{q}}\leq K_{1}||\alpha||_{W^{q+1,2}}\leq K_{2}||H_{\Delta,k}(\alpha)||_{W^{q+1,2}}

(263)

where
W^{q,2}
is the corresponding Sobolev space and
q,K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
some constants. Using the Sobolev embedding theorem and the definition of the Hodge Laplacian in (
262
) we get the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle||H_{\Delta,k}(\alpha)||_{W^{q,2}}\leq C_{1}||\delta_{k-1}(d_{k}(\alpha))||_{C^{q}}\leq C_{1}||\Sigma^{n}_{i=1}\nabla_{e_{i}}\big(\iota_{e_{i}}d\alpha\big)||_{C^{q}}\leq C_{2}||d\alpha||_{C^{k}}

(264)

The last inequality in (
264
) follows from
[
29
, Chapter 4, Lemma 4.6]
. Combining (
263
) and (
264
) we obtained the required bounds on
\alpha
.
∎

10.2
The size of Reeb vector fields of odd-symplectic forms

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be an odd-symplectic form on a closed
2n+1
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
. If
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is another odd-symplectic form on
\Sigma
satisfying:

\displaystyle||\Omega-\Omega_{0}||_{C^{k}}<\epsilon\;\;for\;\;some\;\;k\geq 1

Then, for each
k^{\prime}\geq k
there exists a strictly increasing positive function
\omega_{k^{\prime}}
such that:

\displaystyle||R_{\Omega}-R_{0}||_{C^{k^{\prime}}}\leq\omega_{k^{\prime}}(||\Omega-\Omega_{0}||_{C^{k^{\prime}}})

where
R_{\Omega}
and
R_{0}
are the Reeb vector fields of
\Omega
and
\Omega_{0}
respectively defined with respect to some fixed
1
-form
\alpha_{0}
.

Proof.

For
\Omega_{0}
and
\alpha_{0}
as in the statement of the lemma, we define the following map:

\displaystyle K_{\Omega_{0}}:TM\rightarrow\Omega^{2n}(M)

(265)

given by:

\displaystyle K_{\Omega_{0}}(X)\mapsto\iota_{X}\big[(\alpha_{0})\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n}\big]

This map is smooth and invertible. We now use this inverse to define a vector field in the following way:

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\Omega}:=K^{-1}_{\Omega_{0}}(\Omega^{n})

We also have the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle\iota_{\tilde{R}_{\Omega}}\Omega^{n}=\iota_{\tilde{R}_{\Omega}}K_{\Omega_{0}}(\tilde{R}_{\Omega})=\iota_{\tilde{R}_{\Omega}}\iota_{\tilde{R}_{\Omega}}(\alpha_{0}\wedge\Omega_{0}^{n})=0

therefore we can write the Reeb vector field associated with the pair
(\alpha_{0},\Omega)
in the following form:

\displaystyle R_{\Omega}=\frac{\tilde{R}_{\Omega}}{\alpha_{0}(\tilde{R}_{\Omega_{0}})}=\frac{K^{-1}(\Omega^{n})}{\alpha_{0}(\tilde{R}_{\Omega_{0}})}

So, we now have:

\displaystyle||R_{\Omega}-R_{0}||_{C^{k}}=\Big|\Big|\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\Big|\Big|_{C^{k}}||K^{-1}_{\Omega_{0}}(\Omega^{n})-K^{-1}_{\Omega_{0}}(\Omega_{0}^{n})||_{C^{k}}\leq\omega_{k}(||\Omega-\Omega_{0}||_{C^{k}})

where the last inequality follows from the smoothness of
K_{\Omega_{0}}
defined in (
265
).
∎

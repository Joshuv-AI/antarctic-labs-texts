Order- v 4 v^{4} corrections to heavy quark fragmentation to S-wave heavy quarkonium


Abstract
Within the framework of nonrelativistic quantum chromodynamics (NRQCD) factorization, we compute the 𝒪 ⁡ ( v 4 ) \mathcal{O}(v^{4}) relativistic corrections to the fragmentation of a heavy quark into the color-singlet S [ 1 ] 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0}^{[1]} and S [ 1 ] 1 3 {}^{3}S_{1}^{[1]} quarkonium states. Using the Collins–Soper definition of the fragmentation function, we reproduce the known 𝒪 ⁡ ( v 2 ) \mathcal{O}(v^{2}) results. We find that the 𝒪 ⁡ ( v 4 ) \mathcal{O}(v^{4}) correction gives a positive contribution relative to the leading order result over a wide range of the light-cone momentum fraction z z , while its magnitude remains much smaller than that of the 𝒪 ⁡ ( v 2 ) \mathcal{O}(v^{2}) correction. This behavior indicates a good convergence of the NRQCD relativistic expansion in this process. We further extend the calculation to the fragmentation functions in the unequal‑mass case at 𝒪 ⁡ ( v 4 ) \mathcal{O}(v^{4}) and obtain the corresponding analytical expressions. Keywords: Quarkonium; Fragmentation function; NRQCD; Relativistic corrections

Heavy quarkonium has long served as a crucial probe for testing strong interactions in high-energy collision experiments. Existing experimental data reveal persistent discrepancies between measured cross sections and polarization observables in the high transverse-momentum region and current theoretical predictions. This situation underscores the necessity of higher-precision theoretical studies of quarkonium production mechanisms, motivating extensive investigations of higher-order QCD and relativistic effects across different production channels

II.  Collins-Soper definition of fragmentation

This section introduces the gauge-invariant definition of the fragmentation function given by Collins and Soper
[
15
]
. Based on this definition, Sang et al. have calculated the SDCs for heavy quark fragmentation into equal-mass and unequal-mass
S
-wave quarkonia up to
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
corrections
[
35
]
.

We introduce light-cone coordinates for calculational convenience.
In light-cone coordinates, a four-vector
p^{\mu}
is expressed as

\displaystyle p=(p^{+},p^{-},\mathbf{p_{T}}),

(1)

\displaystyle p^{+}=(p^{0}+p^{3})/\sqrt{2},

(2)

\displaystyle p^{-}=(p^{0}-p^{3})/\sqrt{2}.

(3)

The scalar product of two four-vectors
p
and
q
is then

p\cdot q=p^{+}q^{-}+p^{-}q^{+}-\mathbf{p_{T}}\cdot\mathbf{q_{T}}.

(4)

To describe the light-cone propagation in the fragmentation function, we introduce an auxiliary vector in the “minus” light-cone direction

\hat{n}^{\mu}=(0,\,1,\,\mathbf{0_{T}})

(5)

with
\hat{n}^{2}=0
.

Adopting the gauge-invariant definition by Collins–Soper, the fragmentation function for a heavy quark into a hadron
H
is written as
[
15
]

\displaystyle D_{H/Q}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{\,d-3}}{4\pi}\int dx^{-}\,e^{-iP^{+}x^{-}/z}\,\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{Tr}_{\text{color}}\,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{\text{Dirac}}

(6)

\displaystyle\times\Biggl\{\hat{n}\!\cdot\!\gamma\;\langle 0|Q(0)\,\bar{\text{P}}\exp\!\left[-ig_{s}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\lambda\;\hat{n}\!\cdot\!A^{T}(\lambda\hat{n}^{\mu})\right]

\displaystyle\times a_{H}^{\dagger}(P^{+},\mathbf{0_{T}})\,a_{H}(P^{+},\mathbf{0_{T}})\,\text{P}\exp\!\left[ig_{s}\!\int_{x^{-}}^{\infty}\!d\lambda\;\hat{n}\!\cdot\!A^{T}(\lambda\hat{n}^{\mu})\right]

\displaystyle\times\bar{Q}(0,x^{-},\mathbf{0_{T}})|0\rangle\Biggr\}.\qquad

Here,
z=P^{+}/k^{+}
is the fraction of the heavy quark’s
+
component of momentum that is carried by the hadron
H
with momentum
P
. The parameter
d=4-2\epsilon
denotes the space-time dimension in dimensional regularization. Since our calculation does not involve divergent terms, we set
d=4
in subsequent computations. The term
\text{P}\exp\{\cdots\}
ensures the gauge invariance of the operator.
a_{H}^{\dagger}
represents the creation operator for the hadron
H
and
A_{\mu}
is the gluon field.

This definition serves as the foundation for subsequent NRQCD matching calculations and provides a directly applicable gauge-invariant framework for Sang et al.’s relativistic expansion of the short-distance coefficients for
S
-wave quarkonia
[
35
]
.

III.  NRQCD factorization

Within the framework of NRQCD factorization, the fragmentation function for a heavy quark
Q
fragmenting into a hadron
H
can be expressed as
[
15
,
16
]

\displaystyle D_{H/Q}(z)=\sum_{n}\Bigl[

\displaystyle F^{n}(z)\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{H}(n)|0\rangle+G^{n}(z)\langle 0|\mathcal{P}^{H}(n)|0\rangle

(7)

\displaystyle+H_{1}^{n}(z)\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(n)|0\rangle+H_{2}^{n}(z)\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}(n)|0\rangle\Bigr]+O(v^{6}).

where
\langle 0|\mathcal{O}^{H}(n)|0\rangle
,
\langle 0|\mathcal{P}^{H}(n)|0\rangle
, and
\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{H}(n)|0\rangle(i=1,2,3)
are the NRQCD LDMEs,
F^{n}(z)
,
G^{n}(z)
, and
H_{i}^{n}(z)(i=1,2,3)
are the corresponding SDCs, and
n
labels the quantum numbers of the intermediate
Q\bar{Q}
pair. It is the quantity
H_{1}^{n}(z)+H_{2}^{n}(z)
that we compute in this paper.

For a hadron
H
with quantum numbers
{}^{1}S_{0}
, the relevant operators are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{H}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\chi^{\dagger}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\chi,

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{H}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\bigg[\chi^{\dagger}\bigg(\frac{i\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}}{2}\bigg)^{2}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\chi+\mathrm{H.c.}\bigg],

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\chi^{\dagger}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{2}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{2}\chi,

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\left[\chi^{\dagger}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{4}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\chi+\mathrm{H.c.}\right],

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}(^{1}S_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\left[\chi^{\dagger}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}(\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\cdot g_{s}\mathbf{E}+g_{s}\mathbf{E}\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}})\chi\right.

(12)

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\chi^{\dagger}(\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\cdot g_{s}\mathbf{E}+g_{s}\mathbf{E}\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}})\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\chi\right].

(13)

For a hadron
H
with quantum numbers
{}^{3}S_{1}
, the relevant operators are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{H}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\chi,

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{H}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\left[\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\left(\frac{i\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}}{2}\right)^{2}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\chi+\mathrm{H.c.}\right],

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{2}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{2}\chi,

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\left[\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\right)^{4}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\chi+\mathrm{H.c.}\right],

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}(^{3}S_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\left[\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}(\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\cdot g_{s}\mathbf{E}+g_{s}\mathbf{E}\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}})\chi\right.

(18)

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\chi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}(\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\cdot g_{s}\mathbf{E}+g_{s}\mathbf{E}\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}})\psi\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\psi^{\dagger}\sigma^{i}\chi\right].

(19)

H
denotes the heavy-quarkonium hadronic state,

X
represents the accompanying produced particles, and the summation

\sum_{X}|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|
corresponds to the projection operator onto the final-state subspace containing
H
.

N_{c}=3
is the number of colors.

\psi
and
\chi
are the two-component Pauli spinor fields for the heavy quark and the antiquark, respectively.

\sigma^{i}
are the Pauli matrices, and
\mathbf{D}
denotes the spatial part of the covariant derivative.
The combination
\overleftrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\equiv\overrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}-\overleftarrow{\mathbf{D}}

serves as the basic building block for the expansion in relativistic corrections.

g_{s}
is the strong coupling constant, and
\mathbf{E}
is the chromoelectric field operator.
The operators defined above correspond to the leading contribution
\mathcal{O}^{H}
,
the relativistic correction at order
v^{2}
denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{H}
, and the
order
v^{4}
contributions represented by
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}
,
and
\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}
.
The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}
encodes the interaction between the heavy-quark pair
and the chromoelectric field.

A detailed analysis in Ref.
[
38
]
demonstrates that the matrix element of

\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}
is not independent at relative order
v^{4}
.
Its contribution can be rewritten in terms of the matrix elements associated with

\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}
, meaning that the removal of

\langle\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}\rangle
does not affect the completeness of the NRQCD operator basis at this accuracy.
In potential-model estimates and within the vacuum-saturation approximation, the two
remaining order
v^{4}
operators satisfy the approximate relation

\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(n)|0\rangle\approx\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}(n)|0\rangle,

(20)

so that their SDCs enter only through a single effective
combination

H_{1}^{n}(z)\,\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(n)|0\rangle+H_{2}^{n}(z)\,\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{H}(n)|0\rangle\approx\bigl[H_{1}^{n}(z)+H_{2}^{n}(z)\bigr]\,\langle 0|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{H}(n)|0\rangle.

(21)

Therefore, at order
v^{4}
, the analysis requires only the combined short-distance
coefficient
H_{1}(z)+H_{2}(z)
, and the independent computation of the coefficient
associated with
\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{H}
is unnecessary.

IV.   Calculating the fragmentation functions in the axial gauge

In this section, we compute the fragmentation function for a heavy quark fragmenting into
S
-wave hadronic states up to
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
relativistic corrections in the axial gauge. The momenta of the quark
Q
and antiquark
\bar{Q}
can be expressed as

p_{1}=\frac{P}{2}+q,\qquad p_{2}=\frac{P}{2}-q,

(22)

where
P
is the hadron momentum and
q
the relative momentum. In the rest frame of the
Q\bar{Q}
pair, the components are chosen as

P=(2E,\mathbf{0}),\qquad q=(0,\mathbf{q}),

(23)

so that the three‑momentum of the heavy quark is
\mathbf{q}
and the invariant mass of the
Q\bar{Q}
state satisfies

P^{2}=M^{2}=4E^{2},\qquad E=\sqrt{m^{2}-q^{2}}=\sqrt{m^{2}+\mathbf{q}^{2}},

(24)

with
m
being the heavy‑quark mass. In the axial gauge
\hat{n}\!\cdot\!A=0
, the Wilson line in the Collins–Soper definition becomes trivial.
Consequently, all eikonal-gluon contributions are absent, and only diagrams with a single gluon exchanged
between the fragmenting parton and the heavy-quark pair contribute to the fragmentation function.
According to Ref.
[
38
]
, we introduce the projection operators for spin and color.

\displaystyle\Pi_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\langle\frac{1}{2}s_{1};\frac{1}{2}s_{2}|00\rangle v(p_{2},s_{2})\bar{u}(p_{1};s_{1})=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2N_{c}}E(E+m)}(\not{p}_{2}-m)\gamma_{5}\frac{\not{P}+2E}{2E}(\not{p}_{1}+m),

(25)

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\langle\frac{1}{2}s_{1};\frac{1}{2}s_{2}|1S_{z}\rangle v(p_{2},s_{2})\bar{u}(p_{1};s_{1})=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2N_{c}}E(E+m)}(\not{p}_{2}-m)\gamma_{\mu}\frac{\not{P}+2E}{2E}(\not{p}_{1}+m),

(26)

where
\Pi_{0}
and
\Pi_{1}
denote the projection operators for the spin‑singlet and spin‑triplet states, respectively, and Dirac spinors are normalized as
u^{\dagger}u=v^{\dagger}v=1
.
N_{c}=3
is the number of colors.
We define
v^{2}\equiv\frac{\mathbf{q}^{2}}{m^{2}}
and expand the
S
-wave part of the amplitude
\mathcal{M}
in powers of
v^{2}

[
37
]

\mathcal{M}_{S\text{-wave}}=\mathcal{M}_{0}+\frac{\mathbf{q}^{2}}{m^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{2}+\frac{\mathbf{q}^{4}}{m^{4}}\mathcal{M}_{4}+O\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}^{6}}{m^{6}}\right),

(27)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}=(\mathcal{M})_{\mathbf{q}\to 0},

(28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}=\frac{m^{2}}{2!(d-1)}I^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{M}}{\partial q^{\alpha}\partial q^{\beta}}\right)_{\mathbf{q}\to 0},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{4}=\frac{m^{4}}{4!(d-1)(d+1)}I^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\left(\frac{\partial^{4}\mathcal{M}}{\partial q^{\alpha}\partial q^{\beta}\partial q^{\gamma}\partial q^{\delta}}\right)_{\mathbf{q}\to 0}.

The tensor is defined as

I^{\alpha\beta}=-g^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{P^{\alpha}P^{\beta}}{4E^{2}},

(29)

I^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=I^{\alpha\beta}I^{\gamma\delta}+I^{\alpha\gamma}I^{\beta\delta}+I^{\alpha\delta}I^{\beta\gamma}.

(30)

In the axial gauge, the gluon propagator is defined as

D^{\mu\nu}(l)=\frac{i}{l^{2}}\left(-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{l^{\mu}\hat{n}^{\nu}+l^{\nu}\hat{n}^{\mu}}{l\!\cdot\!\hat{n}}\right),

(31)

where the gluon momentum
l=p_{2}+q_{1}
.

The fragmentation function for a heavy quark
Q
fragmenting into a quark-antiquark pair state
(Q\bar{Q})
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{{{}^{1}S_{0}}({{}^{3}S_{1}})/Q}(z)

\displaystyle=2E\int\frac{dq_{1}^{+}\,d^{2}\mathbf{q_{1T}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2q_{1}^{+}}\,2\pi\delta(k^{+}-P^{+}-q_{1}^{+})F_{c}\times\mathcal{A}_{Q\to(Q\bar{Q})[{}^{2S+1}L_{J}]}.

(32)

Here, the factor
2E
accounts for the relativistic normalization of the final-state hadron. Let
k
,
P
, and
q_{1}
denote the four-momentum of the fragmenting heavy quark, the four-momentum of the quark-antiquark pair
(Q\bar{Q})
, and the four-momentum of the final-state free quark, respectively. The color factor is given by
F_{c}=\mathrm{Tr}(T^{a}T^{a}T^{b}T^{b})=\frac{16}{3}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{Q\to(Q\bar{Q})[{}^{2S+1}L_{J}]}
denotes the squared amplitude from the cut diagram for the fragmentation of a heavy quark
Q
into the state
(Q\bar{Q})[{}^{2S+1}L_{J}]
.

\mathcal{A}_{Q\to(Q\bar{Q})[{}^{2S+1}L_{J}]}=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\not{\hat{n}}\,\mathcal{M}_{S-wave}(\not{q}_{1}+m)\mathcal{M}_{S-wave}^{*}\right].

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{S-wave}

\displaystyle=D_{\mu\nu}(l)\frac{\not{P}+\not{q}_{1}+m}{(P+q_{1})^{2}-m^{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\Pi_{0(1)}\gamma^{\nu},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{S-wave}^{*}

\displaystyle=D^{*}_{\alpha\beta}(l)\gamma^{\alpha}\Pi_{0(1)}^{\dagger}\gamma^{\beta}\frac{\not{P}+\not{q}_{1}+m}{(P+q_{1})^{2}-m^{2}}.

(35)

We obtain the LO result by setting
q=0
and
E=m
, and derive the relativistic corrections at
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
by expanding in
\mathbf{q}
. We perform the expansion at the squared amplitude level, with details provided in Appendix A. Our
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
results agree with Ref.
[
35
]
, and the
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
corrections are new results presented in this work.

V.   Matching the short-distance coefficients

By matching the results of perturbative NRQCD for the
Q\bar{Q}
production process with those of perturbative QCD obtained in the previous section, we derive the SDCs.
Under the standard normalization scheme, the NRQCD matrix elements are given as

\langle\mathcal{O}^{Q\bar{Q}}(^{1}S_{0})\rangle=2N_{c},

(36)

\langle\mathcal{O}^{Q\bar{Q}}(^{3}S_{1})\rangle=2(d-1)N_{c},

(37)

\langle\mathcal{P}^{Q\bar{Q}}(n)\rangle=\bm{\mathbf{q}}^{2}\langle\mathcal{O}^{Q\bar{Q}}(n)\rangle,

(38)

\langle\mathcal{Q}^{Q\bar{Q}}(n)\rangle=\bm{\mathbf{q}}^{4}\langle\mathcal{O}^{Q\bar{Q}}(n)\rangle,

(39)

where

. The fragmentation function in the NRQCD factorization formula can be expressed as

D_{{Q\bar{Q}}(^{1}S_{0})/Q}(z)=2N_{c}\left(F^{{}^{1}S_{0}}(z)+\mathbf{q}^{2}G^{{}^{1}S_{0}}(z)+\mathbf{q}^{4}(H_{1}^{{}^{1}S_{0}}(z)+H_{2}^{{}^{1}S_{0}}(z))\right),

D_{{Q\bar{Q}}(^{3}S_{1})/Q}(z)=2N_{c}(d-1)\left(F^{{}^{3}S_{1}}(z)+\mathbf{q}^{2}G^{{}^{3}S_{1}}(z)+\mathbf{q}^{4}(H_{1}^{{}^{3}S_{1}}(z)+H_{2}^{{}^{3}S_{1}}(z))\right).

(41)

VII.  Summary

Based on the NRQCD factorization framework and the gauge-invariant definition of the fragmentation function introduced by Collins–Soper, we have systematically computed the relativistic expansion of the quark fragmentation process into
S
-wave heavy quarkonium states. Building upon existing calculations of the
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
relativistic corrections, we have further completed the computation of the
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
SDCs for the
S
-wave quarkonium fragmentation functions.
In Appendix B, we have further extended the calculation to the unequal-mass case and obtained the analytical expressions for the
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
SDCs.
Our results demonstrate that the relativistic expansion of heavy quark fragmentation into
{}^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}
states exhibits good convergence. The
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})
term is essential for improving predictive accuracy, while the
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
term, though small, remains meaningful for high-precision descriptions. The
\mathcal{O}(v^{4})
SDCs provided in this work fill a gap in the literature and can be utilized for future high-precision predictions of
\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi
production rates in the large transverse momentum region.

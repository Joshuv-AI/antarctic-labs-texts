Bounds on Linear Turán Number for Trees
Rajat Adak, Pragya Verma

Abstract
A hypergraph H H is said to be linear if every pair of vertices lies in at most one hyperedge. Given a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of r r -uniform hypergraphs, an r r -uniform hypergraph H H is ℱ \mathcal{F} -free if it contains no member of ℱ \mathcal{F} as a subhypergraph. The linear Turán number e ​ x r lin ​ ( n , ℱ ) ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,\mathcal{F}) denotes the maximum number of hyperedges in an ℱ \mathcal{F} -free linear r r -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices. Gyárfás, Ruszinkó, and Sárközy [ Linear Turán numbers of acyclic triple systems , European J. Combin. (2022)] initiated the study of bounds on the linear Turán number for acyclic 3 3 -uniform linear hypergraphs. In this paper, we extend the study of linear Turán number for acyclic systems to higher uniformity. We first give a construction for any linear r r -uniform tree with k k edges that yields the lower bound e ​ x r lin ​ ( n , T k r ) ≥ n ⁡ ( k − 1 ) / r , ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,T_{k}^{r})\geq{n(k-1)}/{r}, under mild divisibility and existence assumptions. Next, we study hypertrees with four edges. We prove the exact bound e ​ x r lin ​ ( n , B 4 r ) ≤ ( r + 1 ) ​ n / r ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,B_{4}^{r})\leq{(r+1)n}/{r} and characterize the extremal hypergraph class, where B 4 r B_{4}^{r} is formed from S 3 r S_{3}^{r} by appending a hyperedge incident to a degree-one vertex. We also prove the bound e ​ x r lin ​ ( n , E 4 r ) ≤ ( 2 ​ r − 1 ) ​ n / r ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,E_{4}^{r})\leq{(2r-1)n}/{r} for the crown E 4 r E_{4}^{r} . Finally, we give a construction showing e ​ x r lin ​ ( n , P 4 r ) ≥ ( r + 1 ) ​ n / r ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{4}^{r})\geq{(r+1)n}/{r} under suitable assumptions and conclude with a conjecture on sharp upper bound for P 4 r P_{4}^{r} .

Introduction

Extremal graph theory is largely concerned with Turán-type problems, which ask for the maximum number of edges in a graph or hypergraph that avoids a prescribed forbidden substructure. In this work, we focus on a more recent line of research, namely the study of linear Turán numbers. The Turán problem for hypergraphs has been investigated extensively; see, for instance, the surveys
[
9
,
8
,
13
]
and the book
[
10
]
.

The study of Turán numbers has a long history, beginning with the classical result of Turán (1941) for cliques.

Theorem 1.1

(Turán
[
18
]
)

For
n\geq r\geq 1
,
ex(n,K_{r+1})\leq|E(T(n,r)|
, where
T(n,r)
denotes the Turán graph on
n
vertices and
r
parts. Equality holds if and only if
G\cong T(n,r)
.

In the hypergraph setting, Turán type problems become significantly more complex due to the added structural conditions and various notions of the missing substructure (e.g., Berge, Loose, Linear). The extension of the Turán problem to hypergraphs has been studied under several of these notions. Turán-type results for loose paths were obtained by Mubayi and Verstraëte
[
15
]
, for linear paths by Füredi, Jiang, and Seiver
[
7
]
, and for Berge paths by Győri, Katona, and Lemons
[
12
]
. In this paper, we focus exclusively on the linear framework.

1.1
Preliminaries

In a hypergraph
H=(V(H),E(H))
,
V(H)
denotes the set of vertices, and
E(H)
is the set of hyperedges in
H
. For
v\in V(H)
,

denotes the degree of the vertex in
H
. that is, the number of hyperedges incident on
v
. For
v\in V(H)
,
E_{v}(H)
denotes the set of hyperedges in

containing the vertex
v
.

H
is called
r

contains
r

1.2
Linear Turán Number

For a family
\mathcal{F}
of linear
r
-uniform hypergraphs, the
linear Turán number

ex_{r}^{lin}(n,\mathcal{F})
captures the largest number of hyperedges an
n
-vertex linear
r
-uniform hypergraph may have while avoiding all members of

A key construction in this framework is the
expansion
of a graph. Given a graph
G
, its expansion
G^{r}
is obtained by inflating each graph edge into an
r
-element hyperedge using
r-2
fresh vertices (kept disjoint across edges). The result is automatically a linear
r
-uniform hypergraph.

The phrase
linear Turán number
was introduced by Collier-Cartaino, Graber, and Jiang
[
3
]
, who established foundational bounds for linear cycles. Yet the phenomenon predates the terminology: the celebrated work of Ruzsa and Szemerédi
[
16
]
can be stated as

n^{2-\frac{c}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}}\leq ex^{\mathrm{lin}}_{3}(n,C^{3}_{3})=o(n^{2}),

where
c>0
and
C_{3}^{3}
denotes the
3
-uniform expansion of the
C_{3}
(
3
-edge cycle).

The systematic study of acyclic structures in this context was initiated by Gyárfás, Ruszinkó, and Sárközy
[
11
]
, who investigated acyclic triple systems and obtained several bounds for
3
-uniform linear expansions. Zhang and Wang
[
21
]
extended this direction to the
4
-uniform setting and proved corresponding bounds for a variety of forbidden configurations.

The current picture continues to rapidly develop. Zhou and Yuan
[
22
]
derived general
r
-uniform bounds, including for
P_{k}^{r}
, showing
ex^{\mathrm{lin}}_{r}(n,P_{k}^{r})\leq\frac{(2r-3)kn}{2}
for
r\geq 3,k\geq 4
. Complementing this line, Khormali and Palmer
[
14
]
and Zhang et al.
[
20
]
investigated star-forests, establishing bounds for sufficiently large
n
.

For linear hypertrees on four hyperedges, the setting already becomes rich. Expansions of trees yield several distinct configurations. For
k=2
, the only possibility is
P_{2}^{r}
(where
P_{k}
denotes a path on
k
edges). It is easy to check that
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{2}^{r})=\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor
. For
k=3
, we obtain precisely

and

(where

denotes a star on
k

[
21
]
, it was shown that
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{3}^{r})\leq n
. When
k=4
, there are three expansions:
P_{4}^{r}
,
S_{4}^{r}
, and
B_{4}^{r}
, where

is the tree obtained by attaching an extra edge to a leaf of
S_{3}
.

Configurations of

and

Alongside these expansions, there is a notable non-expansion configuration known as the

, consisting of three pairwise disjoint hyperedges together with a fourth hyperedge, called the

, that intersects each of the other three.
Crowns have been studied in a variety of settings in
[

,
[

]

, and
[

Finding upper bounds on the Turán number of trees is already very difficult even in the graph setting (see the Erdős–Sós conjecture
[

, 1963). Therefore, we focus on small trees.

1.3
Steiner Systems

A Steiner system
S(t,r,n)
comprises an
n
-vertex set together with blocks of size
r
such that every
t
-subset lies in exactly one block. In particular,
S(2,r,n)
repeatedly emerges in linear Turán extremal problems: its block count realizes the extremal edge density of a linear
r
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices.

Lemma 1

Let
H
be a Steiner system
S(2,r,n)

(
assuming it exists
)
.
Then

|E(H)|=\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{\binom{r}{2}}=\frac{n(n-1)}{r(r-1)},\qquad\text{and}\qquad d(v)=\frac{n-1}{r-1}\ \ \text{for every }v\in V(H).

Proof

Each edge covers
\binom{r}{2}
distinct vertex-pairs, and by the definition, we get that every pair
among the
\binom{n}{2}
pairs is covered exactly once. Hence

|E(H)|=\binom{n}{2}/\binom{r}{2}
.

For the degree: fix
v\in V(H)
. The
(n-1)
pairs
\{v,u\}
(
u\neq v
) must each be covered
exactly once. Each edge containing
v
covers exactly
(r-1)
such pairs, so

d(v)=\frac{(n-1)}{(r-1)}
.
∎

2
Our Results

We begin by determining the exact extremal value for linear stars. Proposition
1
shows that forbidding the
r
-uniform star
S_{k}^{r}
forces a
(k-1)
-regular structure, and this immediately yields a sharp upper bound.

Proposition 1

For
r\geq 3
,
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,S_{k}^{r})\leq\dfrac{n(k-1)}{r}
. Equality holds if and only if the linear
r
-uniform hypergraph is
(k-1)
-regular, given it exists.

To complement this, we obtain a matching lower bound for general linear hypertrees under mild divisibility and existence assumptions, via block constructions arising from Steiner systems.

Theorem 2.1

Let
r\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
. Let
t=(r-1)(k-1)+1
.
Assume that
t\mid n
and that the Steiner system
S(2,r,t)
exists.
Then,

ex^{\mathrm{lin}}_{r}(n,T^{r}_{k})\ \geq\ \frac{n(k-1)}{r}
.
This bound is sharp when
T_{k}^{r}
is
S_{k}^{r}
.

Next, we turn to hypertrees on four hyperedges. For the configuration
B_{4}^{r}
, we obtain a sharp extremal characterization.

Theorem 2.2

For
r\geq 3
,
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,{B_{4}^{r}})\leq\dfrac{(r+1)n}{r}
. Equality holds if and only if the linear
r
-uniform hypergraph is the union of disjoint Steiner systems
S(2,r,r^{2})
, given that the Steiner system
S(2,r,r^{2})
exists.

For the crown hypergraph
E_{4}^{r}
, we obtain the following upper bound.

Theorem 2.3

For
r\geq 3
,
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,E_{4}^{r})\leq\dfrac{(2r-1)n}{r}
.

The linear Turán number for
S_{4}^{r}
follows directly from Proposition
1
.
The upper bound for
P_{4}^{r}
follows from the path result in
[
22
]
, and we obtain a lower bound for

under certain divisibility and existence assumptions.

Theorem 2.4

Assume
r^{2}\mid n
and that the Steiner system
S(2,r,r^{2})
exists. Then,
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{4}^{r})\geq\dfrac{(r+1)n}{r}
.

For each edge

Lemma 5

We know that

Lemma 6

There exists a sequence of functions

such that:

For all

3.

For

,

4.

if

f_{0}(v)=2r-1

Label the vertices in
V(H)
as

through

is in non-decreasing order. We follow the following algorithm to define

1.

First set

for all
v\in V(H)
.

2.

Set

and

3.

While

,

•

Update

. (Note that update to

Clearly we get

Now we iteratively transform

into the degree function

for

Let

be the set of vertices that are ever decreased and

have been among the initially increased vertices (while defining

Hence

∎

Conclusion and Future Directions

In this paper, we studied linear Turán numbers for
r
-uniform linear hypertrees.
We provided a general construction giving the lower bound
ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,T_{k}^{r})\geq\frac{n(k-1)}{r}
under mild assumptions, and showed that this bound is sharp for stars.
For hypertrees on four edges, we determined the exact extremal value for
B_{4}^{r}
, proved an upper bound for the crown
E_{4}^{r}
, and established a lower bound for
P_{4}^{r}
.

We conclude by proposing a conjectural sharp upper bound for
P_{4}^{r}
, matching the lower bound we provided in
Theorem

2.4
.

Conjecture 1

ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{4}^{r})\leq\dfrac{(r+1)n}{r}
. Equality holds if and only if the linear
r
-uniform hypergraph is the union of disjoint Steiner systems
S(2,r,r^{2})
, given that the Steiner system
S(2,r,r^{2})
exists.

A natural direction for future work is to determine a sharp upper bound for
E_{4}^{r}
.

Another direction that can be of interest is to obtain sharp bounds for
P_{k}^{r}
, as the known bound,

ex_{r}^{\mathrm{lin}}(n,P_{k}^{r})\leq\frac{(2r-3)kn}{2}
from
[
22
]
, can be far from optimal, and we already have sharp bound for paths in the graph setting (check
[
5
]
).

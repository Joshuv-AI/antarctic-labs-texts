Improved Bohr-type inequalities for the Cesáro operator
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas and Rajib Mandal

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive the sharp improved versions of Bohr-type inequalities for the Cesáro operator acting on the class of bounded analytic functions defined on the unit disk 𝔻 = { z ∈ ℂ : | z | < 1 } \mathbb{D}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|<1\right\} . In order to achieve these results, we utilize the principle of substituting the initial coefficients of the majorant series with the absolute values of the Cesáro operator associated with a bounded analytic function defined on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and its derivative, as well as for the Schwarz function.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{B}
denote the class of analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
. In 1914, H. Bohr
[
9
]
proved that the majorant series
M_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1
for
r\leq 1/6
. Later, Weiner, Riesz, and Schur

[
13
]
independently improved the number
1/6
to the best possible constant
1/3
. The radius
1/3
is popularly known as the Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{B}
. The
result have also been proved by Sidon
[
39
]
and Tomić
[
41
]
. Note that the inequality
M_{f}(r)\leq 1
fails to hold for
r>1/3
for
f\in\mathcal{B}
.
By considering
f_{a}(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
, it is easy to see that
M_{f_{a}}(r)>1
if, and only if,
r>1/(1+2a)
, which shows that
1/3
is the best possible for
a\to 1^{-}
.

Over the past two decades, researchers have demonstrated a notable interest in Bohr type inequalities. For a
comprehensive review of this topic, we refer to
[
32
,
33
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
2
,
11
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
17
,
20
,
34
,
36
]
and the references cited therein. Boas and Khavinson
[
10
]

have generalized the Bohr radius for the
case of several complex variables by finding the multidimensional
Bohr radius. Many researchers have followed this paper to extend and generalize the phenomenon in different contexts (see
[
1
,
28
,
29
,
35
]
).

Besides the Bohr radius, there is a notion of Rogosinski radius
[
27
,
37
]
which is described as follows:
Let
f\in\mathcal{B}
defined by
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
S_{N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
be the partial sum of
f
. Then,
|S_{N}(z)|<1
for all
N\geq 1
in
the disk
|z|<1/2
. Here
1/2
is sharp, known as Rogosinski radius. Motivated by the Rogosinski radius, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
16
]
have introduced the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
for
f\in\mathcal{B}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle R_{N}^{f}(z):=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}.

It is easy to see that
|S_{N}(z)|=\left|f(z)-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq R_{N}^{f}(z)
. Moreover, the Bohr-Rogosinski sum
R_{N}^{f}(z)
is related to the classical Bohr sum
(Majorant series) in which
N=1
and
f(0)
is replaced by
f(z)
. Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
16
]
have defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius for
f\in\mathcal{B}
as the largest
number
r\in(0,1)
such that
R_{N}^{f}(z)\leq 1
for
|z|<r
. For a comprehensive study of the Bohr-Rogosinski radius, we refer to
[
15
,
18
,
23
]
and the references cited therein.
The following question arises naturally.

Question 1.1
.

For certain complex integral operators defined on different function spaces, is it possible to extend the Bohr type inequality?

The Bohr radius has been studied for the classical Cesáro operator in
[
22
,
23
]
and for the Bernardi integral operator in
[
26
]
, within the context of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
A number of studies (see
[
8
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
26
,
31
]
) have been carried out on inequalities for the Bernardi integral operator, Cesáro operator and its various generalizations on the
family
\mathcal{B}
with the aim of establishing the Bohr type and Bohr-Rogosinski type inequalities.
Furthermore, the boundedness and compactness of the Cesáro operator in various function spaces have been thoroughly investigated.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}=\left\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=\omega^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=\omega^{(m-1)}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\omega^{(m)}(0)\not=0\right\}.

In the context of the classical setting, for an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Cesáro operator
[
11
,
14
,
40
]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f(z):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt.

(1.1)

If
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then the corresponding Bohr’s sum
[
22
, P. 616]
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(r):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\phi_{k}(r)

(1.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|a_{0}|}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\phi_{k}(r),

where

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(r)=\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\frac{r^{i}}{i+1}=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}x^{i}dx\right)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x^{k}}{1-x}dx.

(1.3)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(r)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x^{k}}{1-x}dx\right)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{1-x}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}x^{k}\right)dx=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x^{2}}{(1-x)^{2}}dx.

It is evident that for
f\in\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r<1.

In 2020, Kayumov
et al.

[
22
]
established the following Bohr type inequality for Cesáro operator.

Theorem A
.

[
22
]
If
f\in\mathcal{B}
such that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r<R,

where
R=0.5335...
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 2x-3(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}=0.

The number
R
is the best possible.

In 2021, Kayumov
et al.

[
23
]
established the following Bohr-Rogosinski radius for Cesáro operator on the space of bounded analytic function.

Theorem B
.

[
23
]
If
f\in\mathcal{B}
such that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\phi_{k}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r<R_{N},

where
\phi_{k}(r)
is defined in (
1.3
) and
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 2r^{N+1}-(1-r)\log(1-r)-2Nr(1-r)\phi_{N}(r)=0.

The number
R_{N}
is the best possible.

The following are key lemmas of this paper and will be used to prove the main results.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
25
, Pick’s invariant form of Schwarz’s lemma]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{|f(0)|+|z|}{1+|f(0)||z|}\quad\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
12
,
38
]
Suppose
f
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|f(z)|\leq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|f^{(n)}(z)\right|}{n!}\leq\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{(1-|z|)^{n-1}(1-|z|^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|z|<1.

2.
Main results and their proofs

The following result is the sharp improved version of the Bohr inequality for Cesáro operator on the space of bounded analytic function.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}
such that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z)\right|\phi_{1}(r)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|\phi_{k}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}

(2.1)

for
|z|=r\leq r_{1}\leq R(\approx 0.493411)
, where
r_{1}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\log(1+r)+\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{2r}\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+r\right)+\frac{2}{1+r}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right)=0.

The number
r_{1}
is the best possible.

Proof.

Let
g(z)=f^{\prime}(z)
with
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then, we have
b_{n}=(n+1)a_{n+1}
for
n\geq 0
.
From (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}g(z):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k}b_{n}\right)z^{k}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(tz)}{1-tz}dt,

\displaystyle{\it i.e.,}\quad\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{k}(n+1)a_{n+1}\right)z^{k}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f^{\prime}(tz)}{1-tz}dt.

From (
1.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x}{1-x}dx=\frac{-r-\log(1-r)}{r}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\phi_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x^{2}}{1-x}dx=-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)-1-\frac{r}{2}.

Since
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2}
for
n\geq 1
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|\phi_{k}(r)\leq\left(1-a^{2}\right)\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi_{k}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1-a^{2}\right)}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{x^{2}}{(1-x)^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}-2\phi_{2}(r)\right).

In view of Lemmas
1.1
and
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z)\right|\phi_{1}(r)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}dt\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f^{\prime}(tz)}{1-tz}dt\right|\phi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{|f(tz)|}{1-tr}+\frac{\left(1-|f(tz)|^{2}\right)\phi_{1}(r)}{\left(1-tr\right)\left(1-t^{2}r^{2}\right)}\right]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[|f(tz)|+\frac{\phi_{1}(r)}{\left(1-t^{2}r^{2}\right)}\left(1-|f(tz)|^{2}\right)\right]\frac{dt}{\left(1-tr\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{a+tr}{1+atr}+\frac{\phi_{1}(r)}{\left(1-t^{2}r^{2}\right)}\left(1-\left(\frac{a+tr}{1+atr}\right)^{2}\right)\right]\frac{dt}{\left(1-tr\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{a+tr}{1+atr}+\left(1-\left(\frac{a+tr}{1+atr}\right)^{2}\right)\frac{-r-\log(1-r)}{r\left(1-t^{2}r^{2}\right)}\right]\frac{dt}{\left(1-tr\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1-a}{ar}\log(1+ar)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-tr)\left(1+atr\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1-a}{ar}\log(1+ar)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(1-a^{2}\right)\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r\left(a+1\right)^{2}}\left[-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+ar\right)+\frac{a(a+1)}{1+ar}\right],

where the inequality (
2.2
) holds for

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\phi_{1}(r)}{\left(1-t^{2}r^{2}\right)}=\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{\left(r(1-t^{2}r^{2})\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,1].

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r(1-t^{2}r^{2})}=\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r}\frac{2r^{2}t}{(1-t^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\geq 0,

which shows that
\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)/\left(r(1-t^{2}r^{2})\right)
is a monotonically increasing function of
t\in[0,1]
and thus, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r(1-r^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{holds}\quad r\leq R(\approx 0.493411),

where
R
is the positive root of the equation
2\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)=r\left(1-r^{2}\right)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z)\right|\phi_{1}(r)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|\phi_{k}(r)\leq\frac{1}{r}\log\frac{1}{1-r}+\psi(a,r),

where
\psi(a,r)=(1-a)\phi(a,r)
with

\displaystyle\phi(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r\left(a+1\right)}\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+ar\right)+\frac{a(a+1)}{1+ar}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(1+a\right)\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}-2\phi_{2}(r)\right)-\frac{1}{ar}\log(1+ar).

It is not difficult to show that

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}-2\phi_{2}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in(0,1).

(2.3)

Since
\log(1+r)-(r-r^{2}/2)
and
\log(1-r)-(r+r^{2}/2)
are increasing functions of
r\in[0,1]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\log(1+r)\geq 1-\frac{r}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad-\log(1-r)\geq r+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,1]

(2.4)

Using (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and by differentiating partially
\phi(a,r)
with respect to
a
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\phi(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r(a+1)^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+ar\right)+\frac{a(a+1)}{1+ar}\right)

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r\left(a+1\right)}\left(\frac{1}{1+ar}+\frac{1+2a+a^{2}r}{(1+ar)^{2}}\right)+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{a^{2}r}\log(1+ar)-\frac{1}{a(1+ar)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r(a+1)^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+ar\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))(2+a+ar)}{r(a+1)(1+ar)^{2}}+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{a^{2}r}\log(1+ar)-\frac{1}{a(1+ar)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}(a,r)+A_{2}(a,r)+A_{3}(a,r)+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r),

where

\displaystyle A_{1}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{2}r}\log(1+ar)-\frac{1}{a(1+ar)}\geq\frac{1}{a}-\frac{r}{2}-\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{r}{1+ar}\right)=\frac{r(1-ar)}{2(1+ar)},

\displaystyle A_{2}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))(2+a+ar)}{r(a+1)(1+ar)^{2}}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle A_{3}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-r-\log(1-r)\right)}{r(a+1)^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{r}\log(1-r)+\frac{1}{r}\log\left(1+ar\right)\right).

The presence of two-variable terms in the expression
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\phi(a,r)
makes it challenging to demonstrate that it is greater than or equal to zero. Consequently, our
objective is to express
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\phi(a,r)
in terms of a single variable.

Let
B_{1}(a,r):=r(1-ar)/(2(1+ar))
. Differentiating partially
B_{1}(a,r)
with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}B_{1}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}(1-ar)}{2(1+ar)^{2}}-\frac{r^{2}}{2(1+ar)}\leq 0,

which shows that
B_{1}(a,r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
a\in[0,1]
, and hence, we have

\displaystyle A_{1}(a,r)\geq B_{1}(a,r)\geq B_{1}(1,r)=\frac{r(1-r)}{2(1+r)}.

(2.6)

Differentiating partially
A_{2}(a,r)
with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}A_{2}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+r)(-r-\log(1-r))}{(1+a)r(1+ar)^{2}}-\frac{2(2+a+ar)(-r-\log(1-r))}{(1+a)(1+ar)^{3}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(2+a+ar)(-r-\log(1-r))}{(1+a)^{2}r(1+ar)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(2a^{2}r^{2}+2a^{2}r+ar^{2}+7ar+3r+1)(-r-\log(1-r))}{(1+a)^{2}r(1+ar)^{3}}\leq 0.

Therefore,
A_{2}(a,r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
a\in[0,1]
, and it follows that

\displaystyle A_{2}(a,r)\geq A_{2}(1,r)=\frac{(3+r)(-r-\log(1-r))}{2r(1+r)^{2}}.

(2.7)

Differentiating partially
A_{3}(a,r)
with respect to
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}A_{3}(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))}{(1+a)^{2}r(1+ar)}+\frac{2(-r-\log(1-r))(-\log(1-r)+\log(1+ar))}{(1+a)^{3}r^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))\left(-r-ar+(2+2ar)\left(\log(1+ar)-\log(1-r)\right)\right)}{(1+a)^{3}r^{2}(1+ar)}.

From (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle(2+2ar)\left(\log(1+ar)-\log(1-r)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(2+2ar)\left(ar+r+\frac{r^{2}}{2}-\frac{a^{2}r^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r+2ar+r^{2}+2ar^{2}+a^{2}r^{2}+ar^{3}-a^{3}r^{3}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}A_{3}(a,r)\geq\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))\left(r+ar+r^{2}+2ar^{2}+a^{2}r^{2}+ar^{3}-a^{3}r^{3}\right)}{(1+a)^{3}r^{2}(1+ar)}\geq 0,

which shows that
A_{3}(a,r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
a\in[0,1]
, and it follows that

\displaystyle A_{3}(a,r)\geq A_{3}(0,r)=\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))\log(1-r)}{r^{2}}.

(2.8)

From (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\phi(a,r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{r(1-r)}{2(1+r)}+\frac{(3+r)(-r-\log(1-r))}{2r(1+r)^{2}}+\frac{(-r-\log(1-r))\log(1-r)}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r^{2}(1-r)(1+r)^{2}}B_{2}(r),

where

\displaystyle B_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}+9r^{3}-3r^{5}+r^{6}\right)-\left(r-4r^{2}+r^{3}+2r^{4}\right)\log(1-r)

\displaystyle-\left(2+2r-2r^{2}-2r^{3}\right)(\log(1-r))^{2}.

B_{2}(r)
in

Let
B_{3}(r)=r^{2}+9r^{3}-3r^{5}+r^{6}
and
B_{4}(r)=\left(r-4r^{2}+r^{3}+2r^{4}\right)\log(1-r)+\left(2+2r-2r^{2}-2r^{3}\right)(\log(1-r))^{2}

, we have illustrated the graphical representation of

for

.
Differentiating
B_{2}(r)
with respect to

, we have

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right)-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1+r^{m}).

We now proceed to rearrange the function
\Phi_{m}(r)
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1-r^{m}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)+\frac{2}{1+r^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1-r^{m})\left(\frac{1}{2r^{m}}\log\left(1-r^{m}\right)-\frac{1}{2r^{m}}\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)-\frac{1}{1+r^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right)-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+r^{m}\right).

Differentiating
\Phi_{m}(r)
with respect to
r
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{m}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-4-m+6r+2mr-mr^{2}-8r^{m}-4r^{2m}+12r^{1+m}+6r^{1+2m}}{r(1-r)^{2}\left(1+r^{m}\right)^{2}}

(2.19)

\displaystyle+\frac{\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)}{r^{m+1}}\left(\frac{3m}{2}+\frac{m}{2(1-r^{m})}+\frac{m}{r^{m}}\log(1-r^{m})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\log\left(1-r^{m}\right)}{r^{m+1}}\left(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{m}{1-r^{m}}-\frac{m}{2\left(1+r^{m}\right)}-\frac{mr^{m}}{\left(1+r^{m}\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{m\log\left(1-r^{m}\right)}{r^{m}}\right)-\frac{4}{r^{2}}\log(1-r).

Let

\displaystyle g_{1,m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3mr^{m}}{2}+\frac{mr^{m}}{2(1-r^{m})}+m\log(1-r^{m})\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle g_{2,m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{mr^{m}}{2}+\frac{mr^{m}}{1-r^{m}}-\frac{mr^{m}}{2\left(1+r^{m}\right)}-\frac{mr^{2m}}{\left(1+r^{m}\right)^{2}}+m\log\left(1-r^{m}\right).

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle g_{1,m}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{m^{2}r^{m-1}(2-4r^{m}+3r^{2m})}{2(1-r^{m})^{2}}\geq 0

and

\displaystyle g_{2,m}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{m^{2}r^{3m-1}(13-r^{m}+3r^{2m}+r^{3m})}{2(1-r^{m})^{2}(1+r^{m})^{3}}\geq 0

for
r\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
g_{1,m}(r)
and
g_{2,m}(r)
are both monotonically increasing functions of
r\in[0,1]
and it follows that

\displaystyle g_{1,m}(r)\geq g_{1,m}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2,m}(r)\geq g_{2,m}(0)=0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,1].

Using (
2.4
) in (
2.19
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{m}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{m+1}}\left(\frac{g_{3,m}(r)}{2(1-r)^{2}(1-r^{m})(1+r^{m})^{2}}+2m\log(1-r^{m})\right)=\frac{1}{r^{m+1}}g_{4,r}(r),

where

\displaystyle g_{3,m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{5m}(m-2mr+mr^{2})+r^{4m}(-m+2mr-mr^{2}-4r^{3})

\displaystyle+r^{3m}(m-2mr+mr^{2}-4r^{3})+r^{2m}(9m-18mr+9mr^{2}+4r^{3})

\displaystyle+r^{m}(4m-8mr+4mr^{2}+4r^{3}).

Let
g_{4,m}(r)=(g_{3,m}(r)/(2(1-r)^{2}(1-r^{m})(1+r^{m})^{2}))+2m\log(1-r^{m})
.
Differentiating
g_{4,m}(r)
with respect to
r
, we have

\displaystyle g_{4,m}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{g_{5,m}(r)}{r(1-r)^{3}(1-r^{m})^{2}(1+r^{m})^{3}}\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in(0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

where

\displaystyle g_{5,m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{7m}(-m^{2}+3m^{2}r-3m^{2}r^{2}+m^{2}r^{3})+r^{6m}(6r^{3}+2mr^{3}-2r^{4}-2mr^{4})

\displaystyle+r^{5m}(5m^{2}-15m^{2}r+15m^{2}r^{2}+6r^{3}+2mr^{3}-5m^{2}r^{3}-2r^{4}-2mr^{4})

\displaystyle+r^{4m}(6m^{2}-18m^{2}r+18m^{2}r^{2}-12r^{3}-4mr^{3}-6m^{2}r^{3}+4r^{4}+4mr^{4})

\displaystyle+r^{3m}(m^{2}-3m^{2}r+3m^{2}r^{2}-12r^{3}-4mr^{3}-m^{2}r^{3}+4r^{4}+4mr^{4})

\displaystyle+r^{2m}(3m^{2}-9m^{2}r+9m^{2}r^{2}+6r^{3}+2mr^{3}-3m^{2}r^{3}-2r^{4}-2mr^{4})

\displaystyle+r^{m}(6r^{3}+2mr^{3}-2r^{4}-2mr^{4}).

Therefore,
g_{4,m}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r
and it follows that
g_{4,m}(r)\geq g_{4,m}(0)=0
for
r\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence, we have
\Phi_{m}^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
for
r\in[0,1]
and it follows that
\Phi_{m}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r
. Also,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\Phi_{m}(r)=-1\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\Phi_{m}(r)=\infty.

Therefore, the equation
\Phi_{m}(r)=0
has exactly one root
R_{m,1}\in(0,1)
. Hence,
\Psi_{m}(a,r)\leq 0
for
0\leq r\leq R_{m,1}
, where
R_{m,1}
is the positive root of the equation
\Phi_{m}(r)=0
defined in (
2.18
).

We claim that
R_{m,1}\leq R_{m}
. For
r>R_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-r^{m}-\log(1-r^{m})\right)}{r^{m}(1-r^{2m})}>\frac{1}{2},\quad{i.e.,}\quad-1+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\frac{1}{1-r^{m}}>\frac{1-r^{2m}}{2}.

(2.20)

From (
2.4
), (
2.18
) and (
2.20
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{m}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\frac{1}{1-r^{m}}\right)\left(-\frac{\log\left(1-r^{m}\right)}{r^{m}}+\frac{\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)}{r^{m}}+\frac{2}{1+r^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right)-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1+r^{m})

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1+r^{m})+\frac{1-r^{2m}}{4}\left(\frac{3-r^{2m}}{2}+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)+\frac{2}{1+r^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3+r^{2m}}{4r^{m}}g_{6,m}(r)+2\left(\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+r+2+\frac{2}{r}\log(1-r)\right),

where

\displaystyle g_{6,m}(r)=\frac{(1-r^{2m})r^{m}}{3+r^{2m}}\left\{\frac{3-r^{2m}}{2}+\frac{2}{1+r^{m}}\right\}-\log(1-r^{m}).

Differentiating
g_{6,m}(r)
with respect to
r
, we see that

\displaystyle g_{6,m}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{mr^{m-1}\left(39-45r^{m}-7r^{2m}+43r^{3m}+13r^{4m}-11r^{5m}+3r^{6m}-3r^{7m}\right)}{2(1-r^{m})(3+r^{2m})^{2}}\geq 0

for
r\in[0,1]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
g_{6,m}(r)
is a monotonically increasing function of
r\in[0,1]
and its follows that
g_{6,m}(r)\geq g_{6,m}(0)=0
,
i.e.,

\Phi_{m}(r)>0
for
r>R_{m}
.
Thus, we have
R_{m,1}\leq R_{m}
and hence, we have
\Psi_{m}(a,r)\leq 0
for
0\leq r\leq R_{m,1}\leq R_{m}
, where
R_{m,1}
is the positive root of the equation
\Phi_{m}(r)=0
defined in (
2.18
).
This completes the desired inequality (
2.12
).

To prove that the radius
R_{N,1}
is optimal, let
a\in(0,1]
and consider the functions

\displaystyle\omega(z)=z^{m}\quad\text{and}\quad f(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+az}=a+\left(1-a^{2}\right)\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-a)^{n-1}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore,
f^{\prime}(z)=\left(1-a^{2}\right)/(1+az)^{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
For the above function with
z=r
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z^{m})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a+tr^{m}}{(1-tr^{m})(1+atr^{m})}dt=\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\frac{1}{1-r^{m}}-\frac{(1-a)\log(1+ar^{m})}{ar^{m}},

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z^{m})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1-tr^{m})(1+atr^{m})^{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1-a^{2}\right)}{\left(a+1\right)^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1-r^{m})+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+ar^{m}\right)+\frac{a(a+1)}{1+ar^{m}}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}f(z^{m})\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}f^{\prime}(z^{m})\right|\phi_{1}(r^{m})+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left|a_{k}\right|\phi_{k}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\frac{1}{1-r^{m}}+(1-a)Q(a,r),

where

\displaystyle Q(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-r^{m}-\log(1-r^{m})\right)}{r^{m}\left(a+1\right)}\left(-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1-r^{m})+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+ar^{m}\right)+\frac{a(a+1)}{1+ar^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle-2\left(1+a\right)\phi_{2}(r)+(1+a)\frac{r^{2}}{1-r}+(1+a)O\left((1-a)r^{2}\right)-\frac{\log(1+ar^{m})}{ar^{m}}.

Letting
a\to 1^{-}
in
Q(a,r)
, then we find that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to 1^{-}}Q(a,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-r^{m}-\log(1-r^{m})\right)}{2r^{m}}\left(-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1-r^{m})+\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log\left(1+r^{m}\right)+\frac{2}{1+r^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle-4\phi_{2}(r)+\frac{2r^{2}}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r^{m}}\log(1+r^{m})=\Phi_{m}(r)>0\quad\text{for}\quad r>R_{m,1}.

This proves the sharpness part of the inequality (
2.12
).

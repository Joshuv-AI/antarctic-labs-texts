Study on the influence of inducer and its guide vanes on cryogenic cavitation and pressure fluctuation characteristics of an LNG pump
Authors: Xiaobao Zhang, Xiaomei Guo, Fengqin Li, Xiaoxiao Wei, Ping Li, Mingyu Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Liquefied natural gas (LNG) pumps are prone to cavitation under cryogenic operation. This study establishes a numerical model to investigate the influence of an inducer and its guide vanes on the cavitation and pressure fluctuation characteristics of a two-stage LNG pump. Simulations with and without the inducer were conducted using a thermodynamically modified Zwart-Gerber-Belamri cavitation model and the SST k-ω turbulence model. Results show that the inducer elevates the impeller inlet pressure, increases the head coefficient from 1.74 to 1.88, and reduces the critical cavitation number from 0.0628 to 0.041, thereby delaying cavitation onset. The guide vanes organize the inflow and intensify pressure fluctuation periodicity within the impeller passages, dominated by the guide vane passing frequency, while exerting negligible effect on pressure fluctuation at the impeller outlet and diffuser inlet. This work provides a basis for optimizing inducer and guide vane designs to enhance cavitation resistance and operational stability.

Introduction

It is universally acknowledged that liquefied natural gas (LNG) is an efficient and clean energy source, and it plays a crucial role in the global energy transition. With the ongoing expansion and advancement of the LNG sector, the core equipment of LNG transportation systems—the LNG multistage centrifugal pump1–3—is facing increasingly stringent performance demands. These pumps are required to operate stably for extended periods under cryogenic conditions (typically below − 160℃) and in high-pressure environments, with their operational status directly impacting the safety and economics of LNG storage and transportation systems4–6. However, under deep cryogenic conditions, due to the low saturated vapour pressure of the liquid, high flow velocities and intricate flow patterns, LNG pumps exhibit a marked tendency towards cavitation7–9. This phenomenon leads to head degradation, efficiency loss, and severe vibration, posing a risk to equipment safety.Consequently, enhancing the cavitation resistance and operational stability of LNG pumps has emerged as a vital challenge in cryogenic fluid machinery research.

Cavitation in centrifugal pumps is often induced by insufficient static pressure at the impeller inlet. The inducer10–12, an axial-flow pre-pressurisation device installed upstream of the impeller, effectively delays the onset of cavitation by increasing the impeller inlet static pressure and improving the flow direction. However, a complex matching relationship exists among the inducer, its associated inlet guide vanes13–15 and the impeller. An inappropriate integration may introduce additional flow disturbances or pressure fluctuations16. Thus, elucidating how the inducer and its inlet guide vanes affect the cavitation and vibration performance of LNG pumps is essential to structural optimisation and improved operational reliability.

Several studies have explored the inducer-impeller matching problem. Wang et al.17 found that axial spacing and circumferential offset significantly affect the velocity field symmetry at the impeller inlet, thereby influencing cavitation performance. Dehnavi et al.18 proposed a novel configuration with independent rotational speeds for the inducer and impeller, demonstrating superior performance in counter-rotation mode. Liu et al.19 reported that inlet non-uniform flow exacerbates tip-cavitation asymmetry in condensate pumps equipped with inducers.

Other investigations have consistently shown that inducers improve cavitation resistance. Hou et al.20 and Paul et al.21 examined miniature high-speed and single-stage centrifugal pumps, respectively, and both observed marked enhancements in cavitation performance due to the inducer. Ma et al.22 and Karakas et al.23 examined the performance of constant-pitch and variable-pitch inducers on the cavitation performance of centrifugal pumps. Studies revealed that compared to constant-pitch inducers, the cavitation performance of variable-pitch inducers exhibited greater sensitivity to variations in tip clearance, In contrast, the tip clearance of variable-pitch inducers exerts a negligible effect on the inducer’s non-cavitating performance. Lin et al.24 investigated the impact of different inlet guide vane offset angles on the flow characteristics of a centrifugal pump. The research indicates that increasing the offset angle decreases the vortex intensity in the volute and impeller, but an extensive offset angle causes significant energy loss in the inlet pipe. Cao et al.25 examined the effects of guide vane wrap angle and blade number on the performance of centrifugal pumps. Their findings revealed that the wrap angle significantly influences the performance of the guide vanes and volute, whereas its impact on impeller efficiency is marginal. Increasing the wrap angle improves the flow control within the guide vanes. This enhancement subsequently mitigates both flow separation and the generation of local turbulent entropy.

The studies mentioned above primarily investigated cavitation in room temperature water. They did not account for the latent heat of vapourisation generated by the cavitation of cryogenic thermosensitive media, nor the thermodynamic effects26,27 that must be considered in cryogenic cavitation28,29. Currently, some scholars, both domestically and internationally, have probed cryogenic cavitation. Franc et al.30 experimentally investigated the thermal effects upon cavitation development at the inducer’s leading edge using refrigerant R114 as the medium. Their research indicated that the B-factor is primarily determined by the extent of cavitation development and is independent of other parameters, such as the inducer’s rotational speed or fluid temperature. Li et al.31 conducted research centred on the thermodynamic effects during cavitation in a liquid oxygen turbopump. The findings revealed that, at lower cavitation numbers, the thermodynamic effect leads to a substantial decrease in the vapour volume fraction in the cavitation zone while also diminishing dissipation losses due to velocity gradients. Guo et al.32 refined an isothermal cavitation model, which was grounded in the law of heat conduction, to establish a cryogenic cavitation model and turbulence model for the inducer. In their investigation of liquid nitrogen cavitation flow around a NACA0015 hydrofoil under cryogenic conditions, Fu et al.33 demonstrated that thermal effects strongly restrain bubble evolution. This suppression subsequently alters the adverse pressure gradient, weakens re-entrant jets and destabilises the primary cavitation zone, thereby inducing three evolution modes, namely cloud cavitation, transitional cavitation and stable attached cavitation.

In summary, both the inducer and its associated inlet guide vanes considerably influence pump cavitation and pressure fluctuation, whereas the thermodynamic effects of thermosensitive media under cryogenic conditions also substantially impact cavitation. Therefore, this paper focusses on the two-stage structure of the LNG multistage centrifugal pump. Hence, this study employs a cryogenic cavitation model that incorporates thermodynamic effects to investigate the influence of the inducer and its inlet guide vanes on both cavitation characteristics and vibration performance. By analysing pressure contours, streamlines, static pressure distributions, cavitation performance curves, and vapour volume fractions for configurations with and without the inducer and its guide vanes, it was found that these components play a crucial role in improving the pump’s cavitation resistance.In addition to cavitation performance, pressure fluctuation inside the pump is closely related to its dynamic response. Pressure fluctuation is widely recognized as one of the dominant excitation sources of pump vibration. Therefore, analyzing pressure fluctuation characteristics can provide insight into vibration-related behavior from a fluid dynamic perspective.Subsequently, by comparing and analysing the pressure fluctuation plots in both the time and frequency domains under conditions with and without the inducer and its guide vanes, the study explored their impact upon the pressure fluctuation characteristics of the LNG centrifugal pump. The findings indicate that the periodic guiding effect of the inlet guide vanes enhances the periodicity and amplitude of pressure fluctuation within the impeller passages. However, it has a negligible influence on the pressure fluctuation at the impeller outlet and the diffuser inlet.

Mesh generation and mesh independence test

To ensure computational accuracy, components such as the inducer, inlet guide vanes, impellers, and diffusers were discretised using tetrahedral meshes. In contrast, other structures were discretised using hexahedral meshes. The pump inlet and outlet sections were extended to prevent backflow, and mesh refinement was applied at the walls and clearance gaps. Figure 5 shows a schematic diagram of the mesh.

Fig. 5Mesh structure schematics of the two-stage LNG pump.

Mesh structure schematics of the two-stage LNG pump.

A mesh independence test was conducted using the single-phase head of the pump as the target parameter, as presented in Table 2. When the mesh count increased from 6.45 million to 8.86 million, the head of the LNG pump decreased by 2.47%. Further raising the mesh count beyond 8.86 million resulted in head variations of less than 0.3%. Considering both computational accuracy and efficiency, the mesh configuration with 8.86 million elements was selected for the subsequent calculations.However, numerical uncertainties may still arise from several sources. The selection of the turbulence model is particularly important, as it may significantly influence the prediction of flow separation and vortex structures, especially in regions with complex flow patterns. In addition, the empirical parameters used in the cavitation model may introduce uncertainties in the prediction of phase change processes. Furthermore, the temporal resolution in unsteady simulations may affect the accuracy of pressure fluctuation results, particularly in capturing high-frequency components. Although these factors may influence the quantitative results, the overall trends and comparative conclusions of this study remain reliable.

Boundary conditions

The numerical simulations were conducted in ANSYS CFX, utilising the SST k–ω turbulence model coupled with the thermodynamically corrected ZGB cavitation model. Walls were set as smooth and stationary with a no-slip condition. The inlet and outlet boundaries were specified as a pressure inlet and a mass flow outlet (66.35 kg/s), respectively. Rotating components were assigned a rotational speed of 3000 rpm. The cavitation intensity inside the pump was controlled by adjusting the inlet pressure. The fluid domain was initialised as all liquid form (liquid volume fraction = 1, vapour = 0), and the residual convergence threshold was set at 1 × 10⁻⁵. The working fluid selected was liquid nitrogen, with a density of 779.51 kg/m³ and a saturated vapour pressure of 188,940 Pa.

Conclusions

This study utilised a thermodynamically corrected Zwart cryogenic cavitation model to simulate both cavitation performance and pressure fluctuation in a two-stage LNG pump. The pump’s design specifications include a flow rate of 308 m³/h, a head of 215.91 m and a rotational speed of 3000 rpm. This research examined how the inducer and its inlet guide vanes affect the cavitation performance and pressure fluctuation characteristics of the two-stage LNG pump, yielding the following conclusions:

The numerical results indicate that the inducer markedly lowers the critical cavitation number of the LNG pump from 0.064 to 0.042.

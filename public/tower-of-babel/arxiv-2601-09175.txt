Compactness of multilinear commutators generated by VMO functions and fractional integral operator on Morrey spaces
Daiki Takesako

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to improve the compactness of the multilinear commutators in Morrey spaces generated by VMO functions and fractional integral operators. In this paper, we will use the decomposition of the tilde closed subspaces of Morrey spaces. This gives us more understanding about commutators.



keywords
Morrey space, compact operator, fractional integral operator, multilinear commutator, tilde closed subspace.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to refine the known results of the multilinear commutators in Morrey spaces generated by VMO functions and the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
. First, we recall the definition of Morrey space
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We denote the open ball whose center is
x
with radius
r>0
by
B(x,r)
. Let
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
. For an
L^{q}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-function
f
, its Morrey norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}}
is defined by

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\sup\limits_{(x,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{q}{\rm d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

The Morrey space
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the set of
L^{q}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-functions with this norm finite.

Note that, for
p=q
, we have
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Hölder’s inequality, we get for
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq p<\infty
,
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q_{1}}}\leq\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q_{2}}}\leq\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{p}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

For a measurable function
f
and
0<\alpha<n
, we write
I_{\alpha}
as the fractional integral operator of order
\alpha
defined by

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,

as long as the right-hand side makes sense.

We define the commutator generated by a multiplication operator by a function
a_{1}
and the fractional integral operator as
[a_{\{1\}},I_{\alpha}]f:=a_{1}I_{\alpha}(f)-I_{\alpha}(a_{1}f)
. We will consider the multilinear commutators formed as
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
that are nests of commutators:
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]:=[a_{1},[a_{\{2,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]]
. We assume the
a_{j}
’s are BMO-functions defined as follows:

Definition 1.3
.

We say an
L_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-function
b
is in
{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
when

\|b\|_{*}:=\sup\limits_{(x,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|b(y)-m_{B(x,r)}(b)|{\rm d}y<\infty.

where
m_{B(x,r)}(b)
denotes the average of
b
over
B(x,r)
.

As a consequence of the definitions, we can write
[{a_{\{1,...,l\}}},I_{\alpha}]
as follows:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{j}\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
j=1,...,l
,
0<\alpha<n
,
1<q\leq p<\infty
and
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Define the multilinear commutator
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
by

[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(a_{j}(x)-a_{j}(y))\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy.

Additional conditions of the main theorem are
a_{1}\in{\rm VMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and the target space is the
tilde closed subspace

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
defined below.

Definition 1.5
.

We set
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as the set of
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-functions with compact support. The space
{\rm VMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
under the norm of
{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Also, The subspace
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
under the norm of
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We will establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
1<q\leq p<\infty
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, and
a_{1}\in{\rm VMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Take
s,t
so that

\frac{p}{q}=\frac{s}{t}\quad{\rm and}\quad\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}>0.

Then the operator
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
is compact from
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

This theorem gives us a possibility of approximation of multilinear commutators. Thus we learn that these commutators differ from other compact
{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-
{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
operators.

The study of compactness for commutators on Morrey and Morrey-type spaces has become an important theme in modern harmonic analysis. A significant early contribution was made by Sawano and Shirai
[
11
]
, who demonstrated that compact commutator estimates remain valid even in settings with non-doubling measures. Their work highlighted the robustness of Morrey-space techniques beyond classical Euclidean frameworks and provided a strong foundation for subsequent developments.

Motivated by these ideas, Chen, Ding and Wang
[
3
]
established compactness criteria for commutators of Riesz potentials on classical Morrey spaces. They showed that boundedness of such commutators is governed by BMO-type regularity of the symbol, whereas compactness requires a finer VMO-type condition. This distinction has since been recognized as a central principle underlying compactness phenomena in Morrey-space analysis.

Generalized Morrey spaces, which allow spatially dependent growth functions, were studied in
[
4
]
, where compactness of commutators of Littlewood–Paley operators were obtained. This work extended the classical theory to a broader and more flexible functional setting. Parallel progress occurred for analytic singular integrals: Tao, Yang and Yang
[
12
]
established compactness results for commutators of the Cauchy integral, while Dao, Duong and Ha
[
5
]
investigated Cauchy–Fantappiè type integrals on complex ellipsoids, demonstrating that Morrey-type compactness naturally extends to complex-analytic and geometric contexts.

Weighted Morrey spaces form another important direction of research. Liu and Li
[
8
]
studied commutators of Calderón–Zygmund operators in the weighted setting and obtained corresponding compactness characterizations. Likewise, Gong, Vempati, Wu and Xie
[
7
]
obtained boundedness and compactness results for Cauchy-type integrals on weighted Morrey spaces. These works clarified the role of weighted VMO-type conditions in controlling compactness under non-uniform density conditions.

A unifying and structural perspective was later provided by Bokayev, Burenkov, Matin and Adilkhanov
[
2
]
, who developed general compactness principles for global Morrey-type spaces. Their approach not only yielded new results for Riesz potentials but also provided a framework integrating several earlier contributions into a single conceptual viewpoint.

3
Lemmas for the proof

In this paper, we use assumptions of Morrey norm about BMO-functions twice. Thus, we will check it in this subsection.

Lemma 3.1
.

If

\displaystyle 1\leq q\leq p<\infty,\quad g\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\quad\alpha\geq\beta>0,\quad and\quad y\in B(0,\beta),

then

\displaystyle\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-g(y)|\|_{{\mathcal{M}}{}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{k}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}\left\{\|g\|_{*}\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k+1}\alpha}{\beta}\right)+|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\right\}.

Proof.

Let
J\in\mathbb{N}
is the smallest number which satisfies

B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)\subset 2^{J}B(0,\beta).

Therefore,
J
satisfies

\displaystyle 2^{J}\beta\sim 2^{k}\alpha,\quad and\quad J\sim\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k+1}\alpha}{\beta}\right).

(3.1)

By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-g(y)|\|_{{\mathcal{M}}{}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-g(y)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-m_{2^{J}B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{J}\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|m_{2^{m}B(0,\beta)}(g)-m_{2^{m-1}B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

By the inequality
\|a_{j}\|_{*}\sim\sup\limits_{(x,r)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}|B(x,r)|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\chi_{B(x,r)}|a_{j}-m_{B(x,r)}(a_{j})|\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-g(y)|\|_{{\mathcal{M}}{}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{k+1}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|+(2^{J}\beta)^{\frac{n}{p}}\|g\|_{*}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{J}\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|m_{2^{m}B(0,\beta)}(g)-m_{2^{m-1}B(0,\beta)}(g)|\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Thus from (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\chi_{B(0,2^{k+1}\alpha)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\alpha)}|g-g(y)|\|_{{\mathcal{M}}{}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{k+1}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|+(2^{J}\beta)^{\frac{n}{p}}\|g\|_{*}+\sum_{m=1}^{J}(2^{k}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}\|g\|_{*}

\displaystyle\sim(2^{k}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}\left\{\|g\|_{*}\cdot J+|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\right\}

\displaystyle\sim(2^{k}\alpha)^{\frac{n}{p}}\left\{\|g\|_{*}\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k+1}\alpha}{\beta}\right)+|g(y)-m_{B(0,\beta)}(g)|\right\},

as required.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
j=1,...,l
,
a_{j}\in{\rm BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
1<q\leq p<\infty
,
0<\alpha<n
,
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
and
\frac{p}{q}=\frac{s}{t}
. If

\displaystyle\forall a_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\quad[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]:{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):bounded

then,

\displaystyle\forall a_{1}\in{\rm VMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\quad[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]:{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):compact.

Proof.

Let
a_{1}\in{\rm VMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then we can find a sequence
\{a_{1_{m}}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that
\|a_{1}-a_{1_{m}}\|_{*}\leq m^{-1}
holds. The compactness of
[a_{\{1_{l},2,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
immediately follows from the natural embedding
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. By Proposition
2.6
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]-[a_{\{1_{m},2,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|[a_{1}-a_{1_{m}},[a_{\{2,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]]\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a_{1}-a_{1_{m}}\|_{*}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\|a_{j}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq Cm^{-1}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\|a_{j}\|_{*},

where
C
is independent of
a_{1}
.

Thus, once we establish the boundedness of
[a_{\{1_{m},2,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
, then the compactness of
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
immediately follows; the norm-limit of compact operators is compact.

∎

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{k}R)^{\frac{n}{c}}}{(2^{k}R)^{n-\alpha}}\int_{K}\left\{\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k}R}{\beta}\right)\right\}^{l-1}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\Big\{\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|a_{j}(y)-m_{K}(a_{j})|\Big\}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{k}R)^{\frac{n}{c}}}{(2^{k}R)^{n-\alpha}}\left\{\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k}R}{\beta}\right)\right\}^{l-1}\|f\|_{L^{q}(K)}\left\|\prod_{j=2}^{l}\{\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|a_{j}(y)-m_{K}(a_{j})|\}\right\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\{a_{j}\},f}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}R)^{\frac{n}{c}-n+\alpha}\left\{\log_{2}\left(\frac{2^{k}R}{\beta}\right)\right\}^{l-1},

where
q^{\prime}
satisfies
1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}
.
If we take
\rho>0
which satisfies

-\frac{n}{c}+n-\alpha>\rho>0,

then we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{{B(0,R)}^{c}}[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{c}_{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}R)^{\frac{n}{c}-n+\alpha}\left\{\left(\frac{2^{k}R}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\rho}{l-1}}\right\}^{l-1}

\displaystyle\sim R^{\frac{n}{c}-n+\alpha+\rho}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0\quad(R\rightarrow\infty).

By Proposition
2.2
, for any
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we get

\displaystyle[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in\overset{*}{\mathcal{M}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Also, we get for any
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

\displaystyle\exists L>\beta\quad s.t.\quad\chi_{B(0,L)^{c}}[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in{\mathcal{M}}{}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

4.2
Proof of
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

Secondly, we will show
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Decompose the operator
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]
with
A
,
A_{+}
and
A_{-}
as follows:

\displaystyle[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f=Af+A_{+}f+A_{-}f,

where

Af(x):=\chi_{B(0,L)^{c}}(x)[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f(x),

A_{+}f(x):=\chi_{2K}(x)[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f(x),

A_{-}f(x):=\chi_{(2K)^{c}}(x)\chi_{B(0,L)}(x)[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f(x).

From (
4.1
), we immediately obtain
Af\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

To use Proposition
2.4
, we will show
A_{-}f\in{\mathcal{M}}{}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
A_{+}f\in{\mathcal{M}}{}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
are suitable parameters.

Set the parameters
d^{\prime},\delta,\theta,\gamma
as
\frac{1}{a}=\frac{l-1}{d^{\prime}}
,
\delta={\rm max}(\frac{q}{1+q}n-\alpha,0)
,
\theta=\frac{q+1}{2}>1
and
\gamma=n-\theta(n-\alpha-\delta)
. Then we have
|f|^{\theta}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\frac{p}{\theta}}_{\frac{q}{\theta}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Find
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|\xi|\ll 1
and
I_{\gamma}(|f|^{\theta})(\xi)<\infty
.
Recalling Lemma
3.1
and Minkowski’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|A_{-}f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{\{2\beta\leq|\cdot|\leq L\}}\int_{K}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{l}(a_{j}-a_{j}(y))}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}f(y)dy\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a_{1}}\int_{K}\left\|\chi_{\{2\beta\leq|\cdot|\leq L\}}\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{l}|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1}\int_{K}\left\|\chi_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{l}|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|f(y)|dy.

Since
|\cdot-y|^{\delta}\geq{\beta}^{\delta}
and
|\cdot-y|\sim|\xi-y|
for
1\leq k\leq[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|A_{-}f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1}\int_{K}\left\|\chi_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{l}|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha-\delta}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1}\int_{K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\left\|\chi_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)|\right\|_{L^{d^{\prime}}{(\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\frac{|f(y)|}{|\xi-y|^{n-\alpha-\delta}}dy

Due to Lemma
3.1
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|A_{-}f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1}\int_{K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\Big\{2^{\frac{(k+1)n}{d^{\prime}}}\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|a_{j}(y)-m_{K}(a_{j})|\Big\}\frac{|f(y)|}{|\xi-y|^{n-\alpha-\delta}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\{a_{j}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{[\log_{2}{\frac{L}{\beta}}]+1}\sum_{B\subset\{2,...,l\}}\int_{K}\prod_{j\in B}|a_{j}(y)-m_{K}(a_{j})|\frac{|f(y)|}{|\xi-y|^{n-\alpha-\delta}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{B\subset\{2,...,l\}}\left\|\prod_{j\in B}|a_{j}-m_{K}(a_{j})|\right\|_{L^{\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}}(K)}\times\left(\int_{K}\frac{|f(y)|^{\theta}}{|\xi-y|^{\theta(n-\alpha-\delta)}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\{a_{j}\}}\left(I_{\gamma}(|f|^{\theta})(\xi)\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle<\infty.

By Proposition
2.4
, we get

\displaystyle A_{-}f\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

To prove
A_{+}f\in{\mathcal{M}}{}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we set

\displaystyle A_{+}f(x)

\displaystyle={\rm I}+{\rm II},

where

\displaystyle{\rm I}:

\displaystyle=\chi_{2K}(x)\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(a_{j}(x)-a_{j}(y))\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,

\displaystyle{\rm II}:

\displaystyle=\chi_{2K}(x)\int_{y\notin 2K}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(a_{j}(x)-a_{j}(y))\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy.

Since
a_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, by the mean value theorem and the fact that
a\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we get for any
0<\lambda<1
,

|a_{1}(x)-a_{1}(y)|\lesssim|x-y|^{\lambda}.

Then,

\displaystyle|{\rm I}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\chi_{2K}(x)\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}|a_{j}(x)-a_{j}(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{2K}(x)\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\left\{|a_{j}(x)|+|a_{j}(y)|\right\}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle=\chi_{2K}(x)\sum_{B\subset\{2,...,l\}}\prod_{j\in B}|a_{j}(x)|\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j\notin B}|a_{j}(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy.

As a model case, we will consider

\chi_{2K}(x)\prod_{j=2}^{m}|a_{j}(x)|\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}|a_{j}(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy

for any
1\leq m\leq l
. Here, it will be understood that
\prod_{j=2}^{1}|a_{j}(x)|=\prod_{j=l+1}^{l}|a_{j}(x)|=1
.
Define
a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{3}}{n},\quad\frac{1}{a^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}{n},\quad{\rm and}\quad\frac{p}{q}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}.

Then,
a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime},a
satisfy

a^{\prime}<a^{\prime\prime}<a.

Now, take
\tilde{a}\in(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
with
0<\frac{1}{\tilde{a}}+\frac{\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}{n}<1
and define
\tilde{p}
,
\tilde{q}
,
\tilde{b}
, and
r,w
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{a}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}-\frac{\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}{n},\quad

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}=\frac{\tilde{a}}{\tilde{b}}=\frac{\tilde{p}}{\tilde{q}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{m-1}{r}+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}},\quad{\rm and}

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}=\frac{l-m}{w}+\frac{1}{p}.

Then, we have
\tilde{p}<p
. Thus we get

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{2K}(\cdot)\prod_{j=2}^{m}|a_{j}(\cdot)|\int_{y\in 2K}\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}|a_{j}(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=2}^{m}\left\|\chi_{2K}(\cdot)a_{j}\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\left\|\chi_{2K}(\cdot)\int_{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\chi_{2K}(y)\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}|a_{j}(y)|\frac{|f(y)|}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}}dy\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\tilde{a}}_{\tilde{b}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\{a_{j}\}}\left\|I_{\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\left(\chi_{2K}(\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}|a_{j}|)|f|\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\tilde{a}}_{\tilde{b}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\chi_{2K}\left(\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}|a_{j}|\right)|f|\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\tilde{p}}_{\tilde{q}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=m+1}^{l}\|a_{j}\|_{L^{w}(2K)}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Hence, we get

{\rm I}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

On the other hand, using Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|{\rm II}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{2K}(\cdot)a_{1}(\cdot)\int_{y\notin 2K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}(a_{j}(\cdot)-a_{j}(y))\frac{f(y)}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\chi_{K}(\cdot)a_{1}(\cdot)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}(a_{j}(\cdot)-a_{j}(y))\frac{f(y)}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{a_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}\left\|\chi_{K}(\cdot)\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{l}(a_{j}(\cdot)-a_{j}(y))}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|f(y)|dy.

Thus, we concentrate on estimating the following;

{\rm II}_{k}:=\left\|\chi_{K}(\cdot)\frac{\prod_{j=2}^{l}(a_{j}(\cdot)-a_{j}(y))}{|\cdot-y|^{n-\alpha}}\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},y\in 2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K.

Since we have
|\cdot-y|\sim 2^{k}\beta
, for
\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}=\frac{l-1}{c^{\prime}}
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{k}

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\chi_{K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}|a_{j}(\cdot)-a_{j}(y)|\right\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\times\frac{1}{(2^{k}\beta)^{n-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=2}^{l}\left\|\chi_{K}|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)|\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\times\frac{1}{(2^{k}\beta)^{n-\alpha}}.

Using the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|a_{j}-a_{j}(y)\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}(K)}

\displaystyle=\left\|a_{j}-m_{K}(a_{j})+\sum_{m=0}^{k}(m_{2^{m}K}(a_{j})-m_{2^{m+1}K}(a_{j}))+m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}(y)\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|a_{j}-m_{K}(a_{j})\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}(K)}+\left\|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}(y)\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}(K)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left\|m_{2^{m}K}(a_{j})-m_{2^{m+1}K}(a_{j})\right\|_{L^{c^{\prime}}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq|K|^{\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}}\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|K|^{\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}}|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}(y)|+\sum_{m=0}^{k}2^{n+1}|K|^{\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}}\|a_{j}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}(y)|.

Combining these inequalities and using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|{\rm II}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}{\rm II}_{k}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\Big\{k\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}(y)|\Big\}\frac{|f(y)|}{(2^{k}\beta)^{n-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-\alpha)}}\|f\|_{L^{\eta}(2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K)}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\Big\|k\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}|\Big\|_{L^{\mu}(2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K)},

where

1<\eta<q,\quad{\rm and}\quad 1=\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{l-1}{\mu}.

Also, by triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\Big\|k\|a_{j}\|_{*}+|m_{2^{k+1}K}(a_{j})-a_{j}|\Big\|_{L^{\mu}(2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\|a_{j}\|_{*}(2^{k}\beta)^{\frac{n}{\mu}}+(2^{k}\beta)^{\frac{n}{\mu}}\|a_{j}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a_{j}\|_{*}2^{\frac{kn}{\mu}}k.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|{\rm II}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-\alpha)}}\|f\|_{L^{\eta}(2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K)}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\{\|a_{j}\|_{*}k2^{\frac{kn}{\mu}}\}

\displaystyle\sim_{\{a_{j}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-\alpha)}}\|f\|_{L^{\eta}(2^{k+1}K\backslash 2^{k}K)}k^{l-1}2^{\frac{(l-1)kn}{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|2^{k+1}K|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\eta}}\|f\|_{L^{\eta}(2^{k+1}K)}\frac{1}{2^{k(n-\alpha)}}k^{l-1}2^{\frac{(l-1)kn}{\mu}}(2^{kn})^{\frac{1}{\eta}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}k^{l-1}2^{k(\alpha-\frac{n}{p})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{\eta}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p}_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Therefore, we get
{\rm II}\in{\mathcal{M}}{}^{a^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Hence, by Proposition
2.4
, we now have
A_{+}f\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Since
A_{+}f,A_{-}f,Af\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we get
[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}{}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Hence, we finally obtain

[a_{\{1,...,l\}},I_{\alpha}]f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

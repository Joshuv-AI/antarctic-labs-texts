An analogue of the Erdős Matching Conjecture for permutations with fixed number of cycles
Cheng Yeaw Ku, Kok Bin Wong

Abstract
Let S n S_{n} denotes the set of permutations of [ n ] = { 1 , 2 , … , n } [n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\} . For each integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , let S n , k S_{n,k} be the set of all permutations of [ n ] [n] with exactly k k disjoint cycles. A subset H ⊆ S n , k H\subseteq S_{n,k} is to be a matching if π 1 \pi_{1} and π 2 \pi_{2} do not have any common cycles for all distinct π 1 , π 2 ∈ H \pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in H . The matching number of a family 𝒜 ⊆ S n , k \mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k} is denoted by ν p ​ ( 𝒜 ) \nu_{p}(\mathcal{A}) and is defined to be the size of the largest matching in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . In this paper, we determine the maximum size of a family 𝒜 ⊆ S n , k \mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k} subject to the condition ν p ​ ( 𝒜 ) ≤ s \nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq s .



keywords:
Matchings, Independent sets, Erdős Matching Conjecture, permutations.

1
Introduction

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let
\binom{[n]}{k}
denotes the
family of all
k
-subsets of
[n]
. A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
can be considered as a
k
-graph (
k
-uniform hypergraph) on the vertex set
[n]
.
A subset
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{A}
is called a
matching
or an
independent set
if
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\varnothing
for all
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
with
S_{1}\neq S_{2}
.
The matching number
\nu(\mathcal{A})
is defined as

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{A})=\max\left\{|\mathcal{S}|\ :\ \mathcal{S}\ \ \textnormal{is a matching in}\ \ \mathcal{A}\right\}.

One of the problems in extremal combinatorics is to determine the maximum size of
\mathcal{A}
subject to the condition
\nu(\mathcal{A})\leq s
. In other words, it is to determine

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}(n,k,s)=\max\left\{|\mathcal{A}|\ :\ \mathcal{A}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ \nu(\mathcal{A})\leq s\right\}.

Erdős
[
6
]
made the following conjecture and showed that it is true for sufficiently large
n
, i.e.,
n\geq n_{0}(k,s)
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[Erdős Matching Conjecture]

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}(n,k,s)=\max\left\{\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s}{k},\binom{k(s+1)-1}{k}\right\}.

The Erdős Matching Conjecture is deeply related to certain problems in probability theory dealing with generalizations of Markov’s inequality, as well as some computer science questions. This has been pointed out in
[
1
,
2
]
. The Erdős Matching Conjecture is trivial for
k=1
and the case
k=2
was proved by Erdős and Gallai in
[
7
]
. The case
k=3
was also settled due to the works of Frankl
[
11
]
, Frankl, Rödl and Ruciński
[
13
]
, and Łuczak and Mieczkowska
[
23
]
. For the general case, Bollobás, Daykin, and Erdős
[
3
]
showed that the conjecture holds for
n\geq 2k^{3}s
, which is an improvement to Erdős’s original bound in
[
6
]
. Later, Huang, Loh, and Sudakov
[
14
]
improved the bound further, where they showed that the conjecture holds for
n\geq 3k^{2}s
. Recent work of Frankl and Kupavskii in
[
12
]
has reduced the term in the bound from quadratic in
k
to just linear in
k
. Formally, Frankl and Kupavskii showed that there exists a
s_{0}
, such that for all
s\geq s_{0}
and
n\geq\frac{5}{3}sk-\frac{2}{3}s
, the conjecture is true (see also
[
9
,
10
]
). It is worth mentioning that when
s=1
, the Erdős Matching Conjecture is the well-known Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem in
[
8
]
. Recently, Ku and Wong
[
22
]
showed an analogue of the Erdős Matching Conjecture for weak compositions. As in the case for sets, their result is a generalization of the analogue of Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem for weak compositions
[
16
]
(see also
[
18
,
19
,
21
]
for other related results).

Let
S_{n}
denotes the set of permutations of
[n]
. For a positive integer
k
, define
S_{n,k}
to be the set of all permutations of
[n]
with exactly
k
disjoint cycles, i.e.,

S_{n,k}=\{\pi\in S_{n}:\pi=c_{1}c_{2}\cdots c_{k}\},

where
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k}
are disjoint cycles in
S_{n}
. It is well known that the size of
S_{n,k}
is given by
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=(-1)^{n-k}s(n,k)
, where
s(n,k)
is the
Stirling number of the first kind
.

We shall use the following notations:

(a)

N(c)=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l}\}
for a cycle
c=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{l})
;

(b)

M(\pi)=\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k}\}
for a
\pi=c_{1}c_{2}\dots c_{k}\in S_{n,k}
.

A subset
H\subseteq S_{n,k}
is said to be a matching if
M(\pi_{1})\cap M(\pi_{2})=\varnothing
for all
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in H
with
\pi_{1}\neq\pi_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}
. The size of the largest matching in
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=\max\left\{|H|\ :\ H\ \ \textnormal{ is a matching in $\mathcal{A}$}\right\}.

In this paper, we will show that (see Theorem
2.8
) if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}
satisfying
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq s
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

Now, if we set

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}_{p}(n,k,s)=\max\left\{|\mathcal{A}|\ :\ \mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ \nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq s\right\},

then (see Corollary
2.9
)

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}_{p}(n,k,s)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

(1)

In particular,

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}_{p}(n,k,1)=\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

(2)

A family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}
is said to be
cycle-intersecting
if any two elements of
\mathcal{A}
have at least a common cycle, i.e.,
M(\pi_{1})\cap M(\pi_{2})\neq\varnothing
for all
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
. Note that if
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=0
, then
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing
and if
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=1
, then
\mathcal{A}
is cycle-intersecting. Therefore, if
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq 1
and
\mathcal{A}\neq\varnothing
, then
\mathcal{A}
must be cycle-intersecting.
Note that equation (
2
) follows from the following theorem which is a special case of
[
17
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
. There exists a function
n_{0}(k)=O(k^{3})
such that if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}
is cycle-intersecting and
n\geq n_{0}(k)
, then

|\mathcal{A}|\leq\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

Theorem
1.2
is also well-known as the analogue of Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem for permutations with fixed number of cycles (see also
[
4
,
5
,
15
,
20
]
for other related results).

2
Main result

The following recurrence relation of the unsigned Stirling number
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]
is well-known:

\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+(n-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right],

(3)

with initial conditions
\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
0\end{matrix}\right]=1
and
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
0\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=0
,
n>0
. Note that
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
n\end{matrix}\right]=1
and

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]\geq\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]\geq\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right].

(4)

We will use the two inequalities (
4
) without mentioning them. We shall use the conventions that
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=0
if
k<0
or
n<k
. By using equation (
3
), it is not hard to obtain

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
1\end{matrix}\right]=(n-1)!\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\left[\begin{matrix}k+1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=\frac{(k+1)k}{2}.

(5)

Lemma 2.1
.

If
n\geq k+1
, then

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k+1\end{matrix}\right]\geq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right].

Proof.

When
n=k+1
,
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k+1\end{matrix}\right]=1
and by equation (
5
),

\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=\frac{(k+1)k}{2k^{2}}\leq 1=\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k+1\end{matrix}\right].

So, the lemma holds for
n=k+1
. Assume the lemma holds for all
n^{\prime}
with
k+1\leq n^{\prime}<n
. By equation (
3
),

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k+1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]+(n-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k+1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(k-1)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\frac{n-1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\frac{n-1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+(n-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right].

So, it is true for
n
and by induction, the lemma holds.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
n\geq k+1
and
k\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]>\frac{n}{k^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

Proof.

By equation (
3
) and Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+(n-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\left(\frac{n}{(k-1)^{2}}+1-\frac{1}{(k-1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{(k-1)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

The lemma follows by noting that
(k-1)^{2}<k^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
l\geq 1
and
n\geq l^{2}+1
. Let
b
be a cycle in
S_{n}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Let
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{l}
be disjoint cycles in
S_{n}
and
c_{i}\neq b
for all
i
.
If
|\mathcal{A}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
, then
there exists a
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
c_{i}\notin M(\pi_{0})
for all
i
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq l
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(c_{i})=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{A}\ :\ c_{i}\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(c_{i})\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{i},b\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(c_{i})|\leq\left[\begin{matrix}n-|N(b)|-|N(c_{i})|\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]\leq\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

So,

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq l}\mathcal{A}(c_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}|\mathcal{A}(c_{i})|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq l}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]=l\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

Since
n\geq l^{2}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq l}\mathcal{A}(c_{i})\right|\leq l\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]<\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]\leq|\mathcal{A}|.

Thus, there is a
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq l}\mathcal{A}(c_{i})\right)
. If
c_{j_{0}}\in M(\pi_{0})
for some
j_{0}
, then
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}(c_{j_{0}})
, a contradiction. Hence,
c_{i}\notin M(\pi_{0})
for all
i
.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
r\geq 1
and
n\geq(rk)^{2}+1
. Let
H\subseteq S_{n,k}
be a matching of size
r
. Let
b
be a cycle in
S_{n}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Suppose that
b\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H
.
If
|\mathcal{A}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
, then
there exists a
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\{\pi_{0}\}\cup H
is a matching.

Proof.

Let’s consider the set

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\pi\in H}M(\pi).

Note that
|M(\pi)|=k
for all
\pi\in H
. Since
H
is a matching,
M(\pi)\cap M(\pi^{\prime})=\varnothing
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in H
with
\pi\neq\pi^{\prime}
. Thus,

\left|\bigcup_{\pi\in H}M(\pi)\right|=rk
. Let
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{rk}
be all the elements in
\bigcup_{\pi\in H}M(\pi)
. Note that each
c_{i}
is a cycle in
S_{n}
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a
\pi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
c_{i}\notin M(\pi_{0})
for all
i
.

Suppose
\{\pi_{0}\}\cup H
is not a matching. Then
M(\pi_{0})\cap M(\pi_{1})\neq\varnothing
for some
\pi_{1}\in H
. This implies that
c_{j_{1}}\in M(\pi_{0})\cap M(\pi_{1})
for some
1\leq j_{1}\leq rk
. But this is impossible as
c_{i}\notin M(\pi_{0})
for all
i
. Hence,
\{\pi_{0}\}\cup H
is a matching.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
l\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
and
n\geq((r+l-1)k)^{2}+1
. Let
H\subseteq S_{n,k}
be a matching of size
r
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b_{1}\in M(\pi)\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b_{2}\in M(\pi)\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{l}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b_{l}\in M(\pi)\right\},

where each
b_{i}
is a cycle in
S_{n}
,
b_{i}\neq b_{i^{\prime}}
for all
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
b_{i}\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H
and all
i
.
If
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
for all
1\leq i\leq l
, then
there exists a
\pi_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
for each
i
, such that
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{l}\}\cup H
is a matching.

Proof.

We shall prove by induction on
l
. The case
l=1
follows from Lemma
2.4
. Suppose
l\geq 2
. Assume that the lemma holds for all
1\leq l^{\prime}<l
.

Let
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{rk}
be all the cycles in
\bigcup_{\pi\in H}M(\pi)
. Note that
c_{j}\neq b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l
and
1\leq j\leq rk
. Now, let
c_{rk+i}=b_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l-1
. Then
b_{l}\neq c_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq rk+l-1
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a
\pi_{l}\in\mathcal{A}_{l}
such that
c_{j}\notin M(\pi_{l})
for all
1\leq j\leq rk+l-1
, provided that
n\geq(rk+l-1)^{2}+1
, which is true as
n\geq((r+l-1)k)^{2}+1
.

Now,
H^{\prime}=\{\pi_{l}\}\cup H
is a matching of size
r+1
. Clearly,
b_{i}\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H^{\prime}
and all
1\leq i\leq l-1
. By induction, there exists a
\pi_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq l-1
such that
\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{l-1}\}\cup H^{\prime}
is a matching, provided that
n\geq(((r+1)+l-2)k)^{2}+1=((r+l-1)k)^{2}+1
.
Hence,
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{l}\}\cup H
is a matching.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k,s
be positive integers with
k\geq 3
. If
n\geq sk^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]\geq s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

Proof.

Clearly, the lemma holds if
s=1
. So, we may assume that
s\geq 2
. Let
a_{i}=\displaystyle\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]
.
If
a_{i+1}=0
and
a_{i}\neq 0
, then
a_{i+1}=0<1\leq a_{i}
.
If
a_{i+1}=0
and
a_{i}=0
, then
a_{i+1}=0=a_{i}
. Note that if
a_{i+1}=0
, then
k\leq i+1
. Suppose
k>i+1
. Then
a_{i+1}\neq 0
and
a_{i}\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]>\frac{n-i}{(k-i)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i-1\\
k-i-1\end{matrix}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{a_{i+1}}{a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\binom{s}{i+1}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i-1\\
k-i-1\end{matrix}\right]}{\displaystyle\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]}\leq\left(\frac{s-i}{i+1}\right)\left(\frac{(k-i)^{2}}{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{s-1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{n-s+1}\right)<1,

provided that
n\geq sk^{2}>\frac{(s-1)(k-1)^{2}}{2}+s-1
.

Now,
\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}
is an alternating series with
a_{i+1}\leq a_{i}
. Hence,
\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}\geq a_{1}-a_{2}
, and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{s}
be distinct cycles in
S_{n}
. For each
1\leq t\leq s
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}=\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b_{t}\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Suppose
k\geq 3
and
n\geq(sk)^{2}
. If

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{1\leq t\leq s}\mathcal{A}_{t},

then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
|N(b_{t})|=1
for all
t
, i.e., each
b_{t}
is a cycle of length 1.

Proof.

Suppose each
b_{t}
is a cycle of length 1. Then all the
b_{i}
’s are disjoint cycles. By the Principle of Inclusion and Exclusion,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{1\leq t\leq s}\mathcal{A}_{t}\right|=\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subseteq[s],\\
|V|=i\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in V}\mathcal{A}_{v}\right|.

Now,

\displaystyle\bigcap_{v\in V}\mathcal{A}_{v}=\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ b_{v}\in M(\pi)\ \ \textnormal{for all $v\in V$}\right\}.

Therefore,

\left|\bigcap_{v\in V}\mathcal{A}_{v}\right|=\left[\begin{matrix}n-\sum_{v\in V}|N(b_{v})|\\
k-|V|\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}n-|V|\\
k-|V|\end{matrix}\right],

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|=\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

Suppose
|N(b_{t})|>1
for some
t
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
|N(b_{1})|>1
, i.e.,
b_{1}
is a cycle of length at least 2. Now,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{1\leq t\leq s}\mathcal{A}_{t}\right|\leq\sum_{1\leq t\leq s}\left|\mathcal{A}_{t}\right|=\sum_{1\leq t\leq s}\left[\begin{matrix}n-|N(b_{t})|\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}n-|N(b_{1})|\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\sum_{2\leq t\leq s}\left[\begin{matrix}n-|N(b_{t})|\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\sum_{2\leq t\leq s}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

By Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]>\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\geq s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left(\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle>(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left(\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\right)\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle>(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\geq|\mathcal{A}|,

provided that
\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}>1
, which is true when
n\geq(sk)^{2}
.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
k\geq 4
,
s\geq 1
and
n\geq 4s^{2}k^{6}
. If
\mathcal{A}\subseteq S_{n,k}
with
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq s
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=s
and there exists a
s
-set
T\subseteq[n]
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{t\in T}\mathcal{A}_{t},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}=\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ (t)\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{r}\}
be a matching in
\mathcal{A}
with
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=r
. This means for each
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
,
M(\pi)\cap M(\pi_{j})\neq\varnothing
for some
j\in[r]
. Since
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})\leq s
, we have
r\leq s
. Let

\displaystyle\pi_{1}

\displaystyle=c_{11}c_{12}\cdots c_{1k};

\displaystyle\pi_{2}

\displaystyle=c_{21}c_{22}\cdots c_{2k};

\displaystyle\pi_{r}

\displaystyle=c_{r1}c_{r2}\cdots c_{rk},

be their cycle decompositions in
S_{n,k}
. Now, for each
1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq j\leq k
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{A}\ :\ c_{ij}\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Then

\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}.

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|<\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
for all
i,j
.

Note that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|<\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]=rk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]\leq sk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\geq s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle>s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-s\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right],

provided that
n\geq s^{2}
. Therefore,

|\mathcal{A}|<\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right],

if and only if

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\geq(k+1)\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

(6)

By Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]>\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

Thus, equation (
6
) follows by noticing that

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\geq(k+1)\sqrt{n},

which is equivalent to
\sqrt{n}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\geq(k^{2}-1)(k-1)
and this is true provided that
n\geq k^{6}
.

Case 2.
Suppose
|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
for some
i,j
.

We claim that for each
i
, there is at most one
j
with
|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
. Suppose

|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
and
|\mathcal{A}_{ij^{\prime}}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
for some
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

Let
H=\{\pi_{x}\ :\ 1\leq x\leq r,x\neq i\}
. Then
H
is a matching of size
r-1
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{ij}\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{ij}\in M(\pi)\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{ij^{\prime}}\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{ij^{\prime}}\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Since
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{r}\}
is a matching, we have
c_{ij},c_{ij^{\prime}}\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H
.

Noting that
n\geq(sk)^{2}+1\geq(rk)^{2}+1
, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that there exist
\pi_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{ij}
and
\pi_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{ij^{\prime}}

such that
\{\pi_{1}^{\prime},\pi_{2}^{\prime}\}\cup H
is a matching. This means
\mathcal{A}
has a matching of size
r+1
, which is not possible as
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=r
.
Hence, we have shown that for each
i
, there is at most one
j
for which
|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
.

By relabelling if necessary, we may assume that for each
1\leq i\leq l
, there is a unique
j_{i}
such that
|\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}|\geq\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
and for each
l+1\leq i\leq r
,
|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|<\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]
for all
j
.

Next, we show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}.

(7)

Clearly,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}\subseteq\mathcal{A}.

It is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Suppose
\alpha\notin\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}
. Now,
\alpha\notin\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}
implies that

\displaystyle H_{1}=\{\alpha,\pi_{l+1},\pi_{l+2},\dots,\pi_{r}\},

is a matching of size
r-l+1
.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1j_{1}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{1j_{1}}\in M(\pi)\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2j_{2}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{1j_{2}}\in M(\pi)\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{lj_{l}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\pi\in S_{n,k}\ :\ c_{1j_{l}}\in M(\pi)\right\}.

Since
\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{r}\}
is a matching,
c_{ij_{i}}\neq c_{i^{\prime}j_{i^{\prime}}}
for all
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
c_{ij_{i}}\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H_{1}\setminus\{\alpha\}
and all
i
.
It follows from
\alpha\notin\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}
that
c_{ij_{i}}\notin M(\alpha)
. Thus, for each
i
,
c_{ij_{i}}\notin M(\pi)
for all
\pi\in H_{1}
.
By Lemma
2.5
and
n\geq(sk)^{2}+1\geq(rk)^{2}+1=(((r-l+1)+l-1)k)^{2}+1
, there exist
\mathbf{\beta}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq l
, such that

\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{l}\}\cup H_{1}=\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{l},\alpha,\pi_{l+1},\pi_{l+2},\dots,\pi_{r}\},

is a matching of size
r+1
, contradicting
\nu_{p}(\mathcal{A})=r
. Hence, (
7
) must be true.

We consider two cases.

Case 2.1.
Suppose
1\leq l\leq r-1
. Then
2\leq r\leq s
.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\right|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-|N(c_{ij_{i}})|\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=l\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\leq(r-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\leq(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right|<(r-l)k\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]<sk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right].

Therefore, by (
7
) and Lemmas
2.2
and
2.6
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}\right|+\left|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\leq i\leq r,\\
1\leq j\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right|<(s-1)\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+sk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left(sk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]\right)<s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left(sk\sqrt{n}-\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\right)\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\leq s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-sk\sqrt{n}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]<s\left[\begin{matrix}n-1\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right]-\frac{s(s-1)}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}n-2\\
k-2\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right],

provided that
sk\sqrt{n}-\frac{n-1}{(k-1)^{2}}\leq-sk\sqrt{n}
, which holds for
n\geq 4s^{2}k^{6}
.

Case 2.2.
Suppose
l=r
.

Then (
7
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{ij_{i}}.

By Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}|\leq\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right],

and equality holds if and only if
|N(b_{t})|=1
for all
t
, i.e., each
b_{t}
is a cycle of length 1.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.8
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
s\geq 1
. If
n\geq 4s^{2}k^{6}
, then

\displaystyle\textnormal{EMC}_{p}(n,k,s)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq s}(-1)^{i-1}\binom{s}{i}\left[\begin{matrix}n-i\\
k-i\end{matrix}\right].

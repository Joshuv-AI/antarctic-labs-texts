On exact sequences of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups
Simon Shuofeng Xu

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to first define a Hodge theoretic fundamental group for smooth connected complex algebraic varieties and then prove and study a right exact sequence of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups associated to a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties f : X → B f\colon X\to B . In particular, we study when this right exact sequence is exact, relate this question to some prior results in non-abelian Hodge theory, and give an obstruction to splitting in terms of étale fundamental groups. The main examples we consider in this note is the universal curve f : 𝒞 g → ℳ g f\colon\mathcal{C}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g} and the moduli space of degree 1 1 line bundles on the universal curves p : Pic 𝒞 g / ℳ g 1 → ℳ g p\colon\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}\to\mathcal{M}_{g} .



1.
Introduction

In this note, we define and study a notion of a Hodge theoretic fundamental group. Let
X
be a smooth connected complex algebraic variety. Consider the category of
\VMHS(X)
whose objects are admissible, graded-polarizable
\mathbb{Q}
-variations of mixed Hodge structure on
X
whose underlying local systems are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. Any choice of a base point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
defines a fiber functor
F_{x}
from this category to the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces, and the pair
(\VMHS(X),F_{x})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear neutral Tannakian category.

Definition 1.1
.

We define the
Hodge theoretic fundamental group
of
X
with respect to some base point
x\in X(\mathbb{C})
to be the Tannakian fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X),F_{x})
.

Remark 1.2
.

The term “Hodge theoretic fundamental group” has appeared in the literature before (see e.g.
[
Ara10
]
and
[
Jac22
]
) and it was used to refer to a
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{hdg}}(X))
, an object which we are about to introduce, except the local systems considered by Arapura does not necessarily have integral structures. As we will see, our Hodge theoretic fundamental is bigger and contains
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{hdg}}(X))
as a normal subgroup.

In section
2
, we first generalize this short exact sequence to the relative setting. Throughout this paper, by
a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties
, we mean a smooth projective morpshim
f\colon X\to B
between smooth, connected complex algebraic varieties with connected fibers. Fix some base point
b\in B
and
x\in X_{b}:=f^{-1}(b)
. We have the following proposition:

Theorem 1.4
.

The following natural sequence of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups induced by
f\colon X\to B
is an exact sequence of affine group schemes:

(1.1)

\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))\to 1

For the rest of the paper, we further study this sequence
(\ref{Hodge SES})
. In particular, we focus on the questions of when the first map
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))
is a closed embedding, and when the sequence has no section. The main examples we will be working with is the universal curve
f\colon\mathcal{C}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
of genus
g
and the moduli space of degree
1
line bundles on the universal curve
p\colon\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Here we view these Deligne-Mumford stacks as orbifolds so we may talk about their (orbifold) fundamental group, and local systems as well as variations of (mixed) Hodge structure on them (for an exposition of the orbifold approach to
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, see
[
ACG11
, Chapter XII, section 4]
). In particular, we prove the following theorem

Theorem 1.5
.

(1)

For any
g\geq 2
, there exists some curve
C
of genus
g
such that the sequence

1\to\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(C))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{C}_{g}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{M}_{g}))\to 1

is not left exact (see Theorem
3.2
);

(2)

When
g\geq 3
, the map
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{C}_{g}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{M}_{g}))
induced by
f\colon\mathcal{C}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
and the map
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{M}_{g}))
induced by
p\colon\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
do not splits (see Corollary
6.6
).

Now part
(1)
of Theorem
1.5
says that in general the sequence (
1.1
) is not short exact. However, one can also show that at least after passing to the pro-reductive quotient, under some assumptions, this sequence is exact for an analytically very general choice of base point
b\in B
. More precisely, we prove the following

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
f\colon X\to B
is a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties such that
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X_{b})
injects into
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\text{red}}
be the pro-reductive quotient of an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
. Define

NG(X/B)^{\text{red}}:=\left\{b\in B:(\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b})))^{\text{red}}\to(\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X)))^{\text{red}}\text{ is not injective}\right\}.

Then
NG(X/B)^{\text{red}}
is a countable union of closed analytic subsets of
B
.

Assuming some standard conjectures in non-abelian Hodge theory, this set
NG(X/B)^{\text{red}}
is in fact a countable union of closed algebraic subsets of
B
(see section
3
). Therefore, it’s natural to ask if this is the case without having to pass to pro-reductive quotients: we can define

NG(X/B):=\left\{b\in B:\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\text{ is not injective}\right\}.

Question 1.7
.

Is the locus
NG(X/B)
is always a countable union of closed algebraic subsets of
B
?

In fact, we show that this locus can be empty even in non-isotrivial families:

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
g\geq 2
and any curve
C
of genus
g
,
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(\Pic^{1}(C))
may be identified with
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2g}\times\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
and the sequence

1\to\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2g}\times\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\Pic^{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{M}_{g}}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{M}_{g}))\to 1

is always short exact.

Part
(2)
of Theorem
1.5
is interesting as it provides some examples for which a “Hodge theoretic question” has an affirmative answer (see also
[
Xu24
]
for a slightly different formulation of the Hodge theoretic section question): Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties. Any algebraic section
s\colon B\to X
induces a section of
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
. Then, roughly speaking, the Hodge theoretic section question asks if group theoretic sections of
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
all come from and are determined by algebraic sections. In particular, if
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
has no section, then the Hodge theoretic section question always has an affirmative answer (in a trivial way). Now part
(2)
of Theorem
1.5
is proven by relating sections of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups to sections of étale fundamental group.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties. If the map
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
admits a group theoretic section, then so does the corresponding map of étale fundamental groups
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(B)
.

This is a simple consequence of an observation of André. We will give a different (but morally related) argument. It also allows us to revisit the formulation of the Hodge theoretic section question.

2.
Exact sequence of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups

In this section, our goal is to generalize the work of
[
And21
, Theorem C. 11]
to the relative setting:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties. The following natural sequence of Tannakian fundamental groups

\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))\to 1

is exact.

To prove this theorem, we need to be able to check properties of maps between affine group schemes via the pullback functors between the corresponding category of representations. To this end, we need these facts from the theory of Tannakian categories, which are known to experts and can all be found in
[
DM18
]
and
[
DE22
, Appendix A]
. We include them to make this paper more self-contained.

Consider a sequence of affine group schemes over
\mathbb{Q}
:

\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}.

We would like to understand when the map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
is a closed immersion, when the map
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is faithfully flat, and when the sequence is exact in the middle. To this end, we recall the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
be a homomorphism of affine group schemes over
k
, and
\phi^{*}
the induced pullback functor
\phi^{*}\colon\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{G})\to\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
. Then

(1)

\phi
is faithfully flat if and only if
\phi^{*}
is observable (i.e. maps semi-simple objects to semi-simple objects) and fully faithful (see
[
DE22
, Proposition A. 6]
);

(2)

\phi
is a closed immersion if and only if every object of
\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
is isomorphic to a subquotient of an object of the form
\phi^{*}(V)
for some
V\in\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{G})
(see
[
DM18
, Proposition 2.21 b]
).

The following proposition gives a criterion for exactness in the middle:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathcal{H}\to 1

be a sequence of affine group schemes over
\mathbb{Q}
, where
\psi
is faithfully flat and
\psi\circ\phi
is trivial. Then the sequence is exact in the middle if

(1)

\phi^{*}\colon\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{G})\to\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{F})
is observable (i.e. it maps semi-simple objects to semi-simple objects).

(2)

for every
V\in\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{G})
, there exists
W\in\textbf{Rep}_{k}(\mathcal{H})
such that the maximal trivial subobject of
\phi^{*}V
comes from
\psi^{*}W\subset V
.

Proof.

This is essentially a restatement of
[
DE22
, Prop. A. 13]
. Since that proposition as stated in
[
DE22
]
additionally assume that
\phi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
is a closed immersion, we explain how to remove that assumption. By
[
DE22
, Prop. A. 12]
, we know that
\phi(\mathcal{F})
is a closed normal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
. Now applying
[
DE22
, Prop. A. 13]
to the sequence

1\to\phi(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}\to 1

gives the desired result.
∎

This following Hodge theoretic lemma should also be well-known but since we could not find a reference, we include the proof for completeness:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties (i.e. it’s a smooth projective morphism between smooth connected complex algebraic varieties with connected fibers). Then for any
\mathcal{V}\in\VMHS(B)
, the natural map
f_{*}\circ f^{*}\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be the underlying local system of
\mathcal{V}
and suppose
\rk\mathbb{V}=n
. Then locally
\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus n}
. Since
f
has connected fibre, we know that
f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{B}=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}
, so the natural map
f_{*}f^{*}\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}\to\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}
is an isomorphism of flat bundles and by Riemann-Hilbert, the corresponding map is an isomorphism of local systems.

Then a mixed version of Schmid’s rigidity theorem
[
SZ85
, Proposition 4.20]
says that it’s enough to show that for any
b\in B
, the map
f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}
induce isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures between
(f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{V})_{b}\cong\mathcal{V}_{b}
. This is so since
(f_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{V})_{b}=H^{0}(X_{b},f^{*}\mathcal{V})
and the desired result follows from the theorem of the fixed parts
[
SZ85
, Proposition 4.19]
and the fact that
f^{*}\mathcal{V}
has trivial monodromy over
X_{b}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

We did not
explicitly
use the fact that
f
is smooth and projective. Nevertheless, this assumption is needed to ensure that given a
\mathcal{W}\in\VMHS(X)
,
f_{*}\mathcal{W}\in\VMHS(B)
.

(2)

The results in
[
SZ85
]
are stated in the case where
B
is of dimension
1

[
Kat72
, 4.3.2.0]
and
[
HZ87

Now we are ready to prove the Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We first show that the map
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
is faithfully flat. By Proposition
2.2
, we see that it’s enough to show that
f^{*}
is observable (i.e. sends semi-simple objects to semi-simple objects) and fully faithful. The fact that
f^{*}
is observable follows from the fact that the semi-simple objects in
\VMHS_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
are exactly the direct sums of pure
\mathbb{Q}
-variations of Hodge structure, and the pullback of a pure VHS is still pure. Furthermore, by Lemma
2.4
, we know that
\id
is isomorphic to
f_{*}\circ f^{*}
and so
f^{*}
is full. Finally, given any non-zero map between variations of mixed Hodge structure on
B
, the pull-back of this map will still remain non-zero and so
f^{*}
is faithful. This shows that
\pi(f)
is fully faithful.

Now we would like to apply Proposition
2.3
. First,
\iota^{*}\circ f^{*}
is certainly trivial, as the objects in the image are local systems underlying a variation of mixed Hodge structure on
X_{b}
that are pulled back from a point. Furthermore, by Deligne’s semisimplicity theorem
[
Del71
, Theorem 4.2.6]
, the local system underlying any pure
\mathbb{Q}
-variation of Hodge structure is also semisimple, and hence
\iota^{*}
is observable. Finally, we claim that the maximal trivial subobject
\mathcal{U}
of
\iota^{*}\mathcal{V}
is isomorphic to
\iota^{*}f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{V}
. Note that
\iota^{*}f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{V}
is certainly a trivial subobject of
\iota^{*}\mathcal{V}
so by maximality, we have a map
\iota^{*}f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}
. It’s an isomorphism because the induced maps on stalks are the isomorphism
(\iota^{*}f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{V})_{b}=(f_{*}\mathcal{V})_{b}\cong H^{0}(X_{b},\iota^{*}\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{U}_{b}
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.3
, the sequence is exact in the middle.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Given an affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, recall that its maximal pro-reductive quotient may be viewed as the Tannakian fundamental group associated to the sub-Tannakian category of semi-simple objects in
\text{Rep}(\mathcal{G})
. In the case of
\VMHS(X)
(resp. of
\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X)
, this sub-category is the one generated by polarizable pure
\mathbb{Q}
-variations of Hodge structure over
X
(resp. local systems underlying such variations). If we write

\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg,ss}}(X)):=\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X))^{\text{red}},\quad\quad\pi_{1}(\VHS(X)):=\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))^{\text{red}},

we see that by Deligne’s semi-simplicity theorem, we get a natural sequence of maps

\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg,ss}}(X_{b}))\xrightarrow{\iota_{b}^{s.s}}\pi_{1}(\VHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VHS(B))\to 1.

The same argument as above shows that given a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties
f:X\to B
, this sequence is exact. Note here that the usage of Deligne’s semi-simplicity theorem is necessary, as maps between Tannakian fundamental groups do not in general induce maps between their maximal pro-reductive quotient.

is irreducible, it’s a sub-local system of

-variation of Hodge structure. Call this variation

. By

, which is a sub-variation of

viewed as a polarizable

To prove the second statement, we consider the

We will use this Lemma to show that

Theorem 3.2
.

Fix some integer
g\geq 2
. Then there exists a curve
C
of genus

such that the map

\iota_{[C]}\colon\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(C))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{C}_{g}))

is not injective.

To prove this, we will find a curve

of genus

equipped with a
\SL_{2}
-local system

satisfying some extra conditions which ensure that there are only finitely many curves of a given genus carrying such a local system. First recall that by the uniformization theorem, any curve
C
of genus at least
2
is uniformized by the upper-half plane
\mathcal{H}
and choosing a uniformization gives a representation

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(C,x)\to\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is viewed as the group of orientation preserving isometries of
\mathcal{H}
. Such a representation will be called a uniformization representation.

Definition 3.3

(Arithmetic uniformizing local system)
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus at least
2
and
\mathbb{V}
an
\SL_{2}
-local system on
C
. We say that
\mathbb{V}
is an
arithmetic uniformizing
local system if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathbb{V}
is uniformizing in the sense that the associated monodromy representation

\rho_{\PGL_{2}}\colon\pi_{1}(C,x)\to\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

is conjugate to a uniformization representation
\pi_{1}(C,x)\to\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
obtained by choosing uniformization of

.

(2)

the image of

is an arithmetic Fuchsian group (for a precise definition of arithmetic Fuchsian group, see
[
Tak83
, Definition 1]
).

We will show that for any integer

, there exists a curve

carrying an irreducible arithmetic uniformizing local system

such that

is not in
\LS^{\text{ext}}(C)
and so the map
\iota_{[C]}\colon\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(C))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(\mathcal{C}_{g}))
is not injective. To that end, we need the following finiteness lemma which is essentially due to Takeuchi
[

]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix some
g\geq 2
. There are only finitely many of isomorphism classes of curves of genus

carrying an arithmetic uniformizing local system.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{V}
is uniformizing, we see that the image determines the isomorphism class of the curve as
C\cong\mathcal{H}/\rho_{\PGL_{2}}(\pi_{1}(C,x))
. Therefore, it’s enough to show that the set of conjugacy classes of possible images of arithmetic uniformizing local systems is finite. This follows from
[
Tak83
, Theorem 2.1]
(note that since

is a smooth compact Riemann surface, the image of

has no elliptic or parabolic element).
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Lam)
.

Suppose
C
is a curve of genus
g=2
carrying an irreducible arithmetic uniformizing local system
\mathbb{V}
. Then
\mathbb{V}
is not contained in
\LS^{\text{ext}}(C)
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\mathbb{V}\in\LS^{\text{ext}}(C)
. Since it’s irreducible, we know by Lemma

that for any other curve
C^{\prime}
and any path
\gamma
connecting
C
and
C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}_{g}
,
\eta_{\gamma}^{*}\mathbb{V}
underlies a polarizable complex variation of Hodge structure on

. Let

be the flat bundle associated to
\eta_{\gamma}^{*}\mathbb{V}
. It has a Hodge filtration

, and let
\mathcal{L}:=F^{1}\mathcal{E}
. Consider the short exact sequence

Taking the determinant, we see that
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L}
has to be
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
. As

underlies a polarizable

-variation of Hodge structure, by

Consider the Higgs map associated to this flat bundle

\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{L}\otimes\Omega_{C}^{1}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\otimes\Omega_{C}^{1}.

This map is non-zero as the local system is irreducible, and hence we have the inequality

On the other hand, the image of the monodromy representation

associated to

is an arithmetic uniformizaing local system. As

is connected, we see that every curve then must carry an arithmetic uniformizing local system, contradicting Lemma

Definition 4.1

Let

\displaystyle NG(X/B)

\displaystyle:=\{b\in B:\iota_{b}\colon\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X_{b}))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\text{ is not injective}\}

6.
Hodge theoretic section question and relation to étale fundamental groups

In this section, we continue the discussion of Hodge theoretic section question initiated in
[
Xu24
]
(see also the thesis of Ferrario
[
Fer20
]
). The main goals are twofold: we would like to modify the formulation of the Hodge theoretic section conjecture in
[
Xu24
]
, and then we would like to explain in particular how étale fundamental groups can obstruct existence of sections to exact sequences of Hodge theoretic fundamental groups.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective family of algebraic varieties. Any algebraic section
s\colon B\to X
induces a splitting of the sequence
(\ref{Hodge SES})
. This induces a map

\text{sec}\colon\{\text{algebraic sections to }f\colon X\to B\}\to\{\text{splittings of (\ref{Hodge SES})}\}/\backsim,

where the equivalence relation on the set of splittings is induced by isomorphism of functors via Tannakian duality. Inspired by Grothendieck’s section conjecture, we can formulate the Hodge theoretic section question, which asks when the map
sec
is a bijection.

Now in the anabelian philosophy (see
[
Fal98
]
for an overview), group theoretic maps are not simply maps of étale fundamental groups, but rather maps of short exact sequences. More precisely, given a smooth projective variety
X
over some number field
k
, we have the associated sequence of étale fundamental groups

1\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X_{\overline{k}})\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\to\Gal(\overline{k}/k)\to 1

where
X_{\overline{k}}
is the base change of
X
to a fixed separable closure
\overline{k}
over
k
, and the group theoretic maps in anabelian geometry really are maps between this fundamental short exact sequences up to conjugation by the geometric fundamental group.

Going back to the Hodge theoretic world, we may view the short exact sequence proved by André
[
And21
, Theorem C. 11]
as a Hodge theoretic analogue of the fundamental exact sequence of étale fundamental groups (see also
[
Ara10
, Introduction]
). This motivates the following definition:

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are two smooth connected complex algebraic varieties. Then a map
f\colon\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(Y))
is called
Hodge theoretic
if it induces a map of short exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\MHS)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(Y))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\VMHS(Y))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{1}(\MHS)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

Note that any algebraic morphism between
X
and
Y
induces Hodge theoretic maps between their Hodge theoretic fundamental groups. Then we may modify the definition of the section map: instead of going into the set of group theoretic splittings of (
1.1
), we asks that it lands in the set of Hodge theoretic splittings:

\sec_{\text{Hdg}}\colon\{\text{algebraic sections to }f\colon X\to B\}\to\{\text{Hodge theoretic splittings of }(\ref{Hodge SES})\}/\backsim,

where the equivalence relation is given by conjugation by
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(Y))
.

Question 6.2

(Hodge theoretic section question)
.

When is
\text{sec}_{\text{Hdg}}
a bijection?

Perhaps the simplest way of verifying that the map
\text{sec}_{\text{Hdg}}
is a bijection is to show that the sequence (
1.1
) does not admit any Hodge theoretic splitting. We show that sometimes it’s enough to check at the level of étale fundamental groups:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to B
be a smooth projective map between smooth connected complex quasi-projective varieties. If
\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))
admits a Hodge theoretic splitting, then the induced map of étale fundamental groups
\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\to\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(B)
also splits.

Proof.

Suppose that we have a Hodge theoretic section
s\colon\pi_{1}(\VMHS(B))\to\pi_{1}(\VMHS(X))
. By definition,
s
restricts to a splitting
s|_{\text{LS}}\colon\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(B))\to\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X))
of the map
\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X))\to\pi_{1}(\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(B))
. Applying Tannakian duality, we see that at the categorical side, we get a functor
s^{*}|_{\text{LS}}\colon\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X)\to\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(B)
such that
s^{*}|_{\text{LS}}\circ f^{*}
is isomorphic to the identity functor on
\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(B)
.

We claim that any additive tensor functor between
\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(X)
and
\LS^{\text{Hdg}}(B)
has to preserve local systems of finite monodromy. Assuming this claim is true, then
s^{*}|_{\text{LS}}
further restricts to a functor from the category
\LS_{\text{fin}}(X)
of local systems with finite monodromy on
X
to
\LS_{\text{fin}}(B)
, such that precomposing with
f^{*}
is isomorphic to the identity functor on
\LS_{\text{fin}}(B)
. This concludes the proof as the étale fundamental group of a smooth connected complex algebraic variety
X
can be identified with the Tannakian fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\LS_{\text{fin}}(X))
(see
[
DM18
, Example 2.34]
).
∎

Therefore, it remains to verify the following lemma:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two smooth connected complex quasi-projective varieties. Then any additive tensor functors between (sub)categories of local systems have to send local systems of finite monodromy to local systems of finite monodromy.

Proof.

This follows from Nori’s criterion for a local system to be of finite monodromy: a local system
\mathbb{V}
on a smooth connected quasi-projective variety
X
is of finite monodromy if and only if there are two polynomials
P(x)\neq Q(x)\in\mathbb{N}[x]
such that
P(\mathbb{V})=Q(\mathbb{V})
, where multiplication of local system is interpreted as tensor products and addition is interpreted as direct sum. This is first proven by Nori in the case where
X
is a curve
[
Nor76
, Proposition 3.10]
, and is later generalized to the case where
X
is a smooth connected quasi-projective variety by the work of Biswas-Holla-Schumacher
[
BHS00
, Theorem 3.3]
.
∎

Note that
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the local system corresponding to the action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},[C,x])
on
H:=H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z}(1))
and
\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the local system corresponding to the action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1},[C,x])
on
L:=[\wedge^{3}H](-1)
. There’s a natural projection map
c\colon L\to H
defined by

c(x\wedge y\wedge z)=\omega(x,y)z+\omega(y,z)x+\omega(z,x)y

where
\omega
is the intersection form.

The precise definition of the local system
\mathcal{E}_{\text{cer}}
is not relevant to our story. The key is that it’s an extension of the form

0\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{E}_{\text{cer}}\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}(0)}\to 0.

More importantly, by
[
Pul88
, Corollary 6.7]
, we know that the map
c
is equivariant with respect to the action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
and hence induces a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}_{\text{cer}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{\mathbb{Z}(0)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{Z}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{j^{*}\mathcal{E}_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{\mathbb{Z}(0)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

In particular, the monodromy representation associated to
(j^{*}\mathcal{E}_{0})|_{C}
is determined by sending a loop
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(C,x)
to a map of the form

\mathbb{Z}\to L\xrightarrow{c}H.

Fix such a loop
\gamma
. Notice that
\pi_{1}(C,x)
must be contained in the Torelli subgroup
T_{g,1}
of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1})
as it’s already trivial under the projection onto
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
. The key result of Hain and Matsumoto is the following

Proposition 7.1

(Proposition 6.4 in
[
HM05
]
)
.

The monodromy of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{cer}}
around
\gamma
is given by the map
\gamma\mapsto\tau([\gamma])
, where
\tau
is the Johnson homomorphism
\tau\colon T_{g,1}\to L
, and
[\gamma]
is the homology class of
\gamma
in
H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition
5.5
now follows from a direct computation:

Proof of Proposition
5.5
.

It’s enough to show that the composition

\pi_{1}(C,x)\to T_{g,1}\xrightarrow{\tau}L\xrightarrow{c}H

is given by sending
\gamma
to
(g-1)\cdot[\gamma]
. Now we know that the map
\pi_{1}(C,x)\to T_{g,1}\xrightarrow{\tau}L
has the following explicit description (see e.g.
[
FM12
]
): let
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
be a symplectic basis for
H
with respect to the intersection form
\omega
and then this map is given by

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(C,x)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow L

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\sum x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\right)\wedge[\gamma]

Suppose
[\gamma]=\sum\alpha_{i}x_{i}+\beta_{j}y_{i}
. We may now compute that

\displaystyle c\left(\left(\sum x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\right)\wedge[\gamma]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{g}c(x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\wedge[\gamma])

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{g}[\gamma]+\omega(y_{i},[\gamma])x_{i}+\omega([\gamma],x_{i})y_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{g}[\gamma]-\alpha_{i}x_{i}-\beta_{i}y_{i}

\displaystyle=(g-1)[\gamma]

This concludes the proof of Proposition
5.5
.
∎

. Remark 1.1

In the above, Erdős stated the problem with the condition (

Remark 1.2

It remains an open problem whether there are infinitely many prime gaps that do not contain rough numbers, or to establish the slightly stronger claim that

of the cluster are prime, and in particular does not seem to preclude the possibility that any two consecutive primes in the cluster surround one of the almost primes of that cluster, thus failing to provide an example of a prime gap without a rough number. We therefore suspect that unconditionally resolving the question of whether there are infinitely many prime gaps without rough numbers will require further advances in the theory of small prime gaps.

One can explain

would be expected to have length comparable to

should contain a rough number. Pick a

[
3
]
and de Bruijn

is small compared with

The main technical challenge in making this argument rigorous is to establish a suitable concentration of measure result for rough numbers in short intervals. The simplest way to proceed is via the second moment method; this partially works, but yields a weaker version of

give a sufficiently strong concentration of measure result

First attempt: using the second moment method

In this section we establish the weak version (

The key input will be the following moment estimates for counting rough numbers in short intervals.

Lemma 2.1

(First and second moment estimates)
.

Let
0<\beta<\alpha<1
be fixed constants; we permit all implied constants to depend on these parameters. Let
X\geq 2
, and define the quantities

H\coloneqq\lfloor\log^{\alpha}X\rfloor;\quad z\coloneqq\exp(\log^{\beta}X).

Let
\mathcal{R}(z)\coloneqq\{n:p(n)\geq z\}
denote the set of numbers
n
whose smallest prime factor
p(n)
is at least
z
, and set

(2.1)

R\coloneqq\frac{e^{-\gamma}H}{\log z}\asymp\log^{\alpha-\beta}X,

where
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Then one has the first moment estimate

(2.2)

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\sum_{x<n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\,dx=R+O\left(\frac{1}{\log^{2}X}\right)

and the second moment estimate

(2.3)

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\sum_{x<n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\right|^{2}\,dx=R^{2}+O(R).

In particular, we have

(2.4)

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left|\sum_{x<n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)-R\right|^{2}\,dx\ll R.

Informally, (
2.4

Proof.

We begin with the first moment estimate (
2.2

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\sum_{x<n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}\sum_{X\leq n\leq 2X+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\int_{[X,2X]\cap[n-H,n)}1\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{X}\sum_{X\leq n\leq 2X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)+O\left(\frac{H^{2}}{X}\right).

From Buchstab’s theorem
[
19
, Theorem 7.11]
we have

\sum_{n<X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)=\frac{\omega(u)X}{\log z}-\frac{z}{\log z}+O\left(\frac{X}{\log^{2}X}\right)

where
u\coloneqq\log X/\log z=\log^{1-\beta}X
and
\omega
is the Buchstab function. Using the de Bruijn asymptotics
[

]

\omega(u)=e^{-\gamma}+O\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(u+1)}\right),

where

denotes the Gamma function, one then easily concludes that

\sum_{n<X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)=\frac{e^{-\gamma}X}{\log z}+O\left(\frac{X}{\log^{2}X}\right)

and a similar computation (with a very slightly different value of

) gives

\sum_{n\leq 2X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)=\frac{2e^{-\gamma}X}{\log z}+O\left(\frac{X}{\log^{2}X}\right)

and hence

\sum_{X\leq n\leq 2X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)=\frac{e^{-\gamma}X}{\log z}+O\left(\frac{X}{\log^{2}X}\right).

Inserting this back into the previous estimates, we obtain (
2.2
) after a brief calculation.

Now we turn to the second moment estimate (
2.3
). We expand

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\left(\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\right)^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+H}\sum_{x\leq m\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(m)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+H}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\,dx+\frac{2}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}\sum_{1\leq h\leq H}\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+H-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n+h)

\displaystyle=O(R)+\frac{2}{X}\sum_{1\leq h\leq H}\sum_{X\leq n\leq 2X+H-h}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n+h)\int_{[X,2X]\cap[n-H+h,n]}1\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{X}\sum_{1\leq h\leq H}(H-h)\sum_{X\leq n\leq 2X}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{R}(z)}(n+h)+O(R)

does not contain a rough number, it must be disjoint from

of exceptional

\ll\frac{X}{z}

O\left(\frac{X}{\log^{2}X}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>2\\

We conclude that for any fixed

Taking

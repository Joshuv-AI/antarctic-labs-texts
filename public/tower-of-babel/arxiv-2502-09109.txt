On a Question of Hamkins’
Albert Visser

Abstract
. Joel Hamkins asks whether there is a Π 1 0 \Pi^{0}_{1} -formula ρ ⁡ ( x ) \rho(x) such that ρ ⁡ ( ⌜ ​ φ ​ ⌝ ) \rho({\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner}) is independent over 𝖯𝖠 + φ {\sf PA}+\varphi , if this theory is consistent, where this construction is extensional in φ \varphi with respect to PA -provable equivalence. We show that there can be no such extensional Rosser formula of any complexity. We give a positive answer to Hamkins’ question for the case where we replace Extensionality by a weaker demand that we call Conditional Extensionality . We leave one important question open: what happens when we weaken Extensionality to Consistent Extensionality, i.e., Extensionality for consistent extensions? Consistent Extensionality is between full Extensionality and Conditional Extensionality.

2.
There are no Extensional Rosser Formulas

In this section, we prove that there is no formula that is both independent and
extensional, thus answering
Hamkins’ original question in the negative.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
U
be any consistent theory that extends the Tarski-Mostowski-Robinson theory
R
.
Then, there are no extensional Rosser formulas over base theory
U
.

Note that we do not need any constraint on the complexity of the axiom set of
U
.

Proof.

Suppose
\rho(x)
is an extensional Rosser formula over
U
. We form the fixed points

\vdash\varphi_{0}\leftrightarrow\rho({\ulcorner\varphi_{0}\urcorner})
and
\vdash\varphi_{1}\leftrightarrow\neg\,\rho({\ulcorner\varphi_{1}\urcorner})
.
By Independence, we find (a)
\vdash\neg\,\varphi_{0}
and
\vdash\neg\,\varphi_{1}
.
So,
\vdash\varphi_{0}\leftrightarrow\bot
and
\vdash\varphi_{1}\leftrightarrow\bot
. Thus, by Extensionality, (b)

\vdash\rho({\ulcorner\varphi_{0}\urcorner})\leftrightarrow\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
and
\vdash\rho({\ulcorner\varphi_{1}\urcorner})\leftrightarrow\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
.
By the Fixed Point Equations in combination with (b), we find (c)
\vdash\varphi_{0}\leftrightarrow\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
and

\vdash\varphi_{1}\leftrightarrow\neg\,\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
. By (a) and (c), we find

\vdash\neg\,\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
and
\vdash\rho({\ulcorner\bot\urcorner})
, contradicting the consistency of
U
.
❑

By minor adaptations of the formulation and the proof, we find a similar result for theories that
interpret

R
.

Remark 2.2
.

The above argument only uses the special case of Extensionality for inconsistent formulas.
So, one may wonder what happens if we simply ban this case.
This is our Question
1.2
.

We note that Consistent Extensionality implies Conditional Extensionality, for which we have our positive
result. Thus, Consistent Extensionality is in the gap between our negative answer and our positive result.

4.
There is a Conditionally Extensional
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-Flexible Formula

We develop our positive result that there is a conditionally extensional
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-flexible formula for
c.e. base theories extending
PA
and, more generally, for essentially reflexive, sequential c.e. theories.
We take a consistent arithmetical extension
U
of
PA
as a base. Let
\alpha
be an elementary formula that
gives an axiom set
\mathcal{A}
of
U
. Specifically, we demand that if
\psi\in\mathcal{A}
, then
U\vdash\alpha({\ulcorner\psi\urcorner})
and
if
\psi\not\in\mathcal{A}
, then
U\vdash\neg\,\alpha({\ulcorner\psi\urcorner})
.
It easy to see how the results extend to essentially reflexive sequential c.e. theories.
This only requires slight adaptations of the formulations.

The sentences we provide will be consistency statements of theories of slow provability.

Appendix A
Sufficient Conditions for Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorems

We discuss the conditions given in Subsection
3.1
, to wit:

I.

U+\varphi
is a true arithmetical theory.

II.

U+\varphi
is
\omega
-consistent.

III.

U+\varphi
is 1-consistent, in other words,
U+\varphi
satisfies
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-reflection.

IV.

U+\varphi\nvdash\neg\,\gamma({\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner})
.

As is well known, Gödel avoided Condition I, since, around the time of his discovery, the notion of
truth was considered somewhat suspect. He choose
\omega
-consistency. Somehow, this condition had a long life and occurs
even in recent textbooks. It seems that 1-consistency is a better condition because it allows greater generality.
Since
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
-truth is arithmetically definable it can hardly be suspect.

But why not go on and
opt for Condition IV? That gives us a necessary and sufficient condition and, thus, is the only truly
motivated condition. There are two objections. Firstly, it is trifling, stipulating half of what we wanted to
prove. Secondly, we need coding and the like to define
\gamma
at all and so the condition is not sufficiently natural.
In spite of these objections, I am a fan of Condition IV. It may seem trifling, but it is less so when we
consider more general statements like the
\Pi^{0}_{1}
-flexibility of the Gödel sentence and of the consistency statement.
Moreover, it is well-known that we can, modulo
EA
-provable equivalence, eliminate the arbitrary choices
in the definition of the consistency statement. See e.g.
[
Vis11
]
. Finally, when one considers the
Incompleteness Theorems for slow provability, Conditions I–III play no role at all.
The unprovability of inconsistency follows from the choice of the representation of the axiom set.

Appendix D
On the Proof of Absorption

The fixed point
\nu
with
{\sf EA}\vdash\nu\leftrightarrow(\exists x\,\necessary_{\varphi,x}\psi)<\necessary_{\varphi}\nu

is an instance of the fixed point used in the FGH Theorem, so called after Harvey Friedman, Warren Goldfarb and
Leo Harrington who each, independently, discovered the argument associated with the fixed point.
However, in fact, John Shepherdson discovered the argument first. See
[
She61
]
.

The second half of the proof of Theorem
4.11
is simply a proof of a specific version of the
FGH theorem. We cannot quite follow the usual argument, since the opposite of
\nu
, to wit

\necessary_{\varphi}\nu\leqslant(\exists x\,\necessary_{\varphi,x}\psi)
is not
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
.

The first half of the proof of Theorem
4.11
brings us from
\necessary_{\varphi}{\vartriangle}_{\varphi}\psi
to
\necessary_{\varphi}\nu
.
The crucial point in the paper is the step where
\varphi
-smallness is used is in moving from
\necessary_{\varphi,(x)}\nu
to
\nu
.

What about using the fixed point
{\vartriangle}^{\ast}_{\varphi}\psi
with

{\sf EA}\vdash{\vartriangle}^{\ast}_{\varphi}\psi\leftrightarrow(\exists x\,\necessary_{\varphi,x}\psi)<\necessary_{\varphi}{\vartriangle}^{\ast}_{\varphi}\bot\;?

That would deliver a Fefermanian provability predicate with absorption for the case that
\psi:=\bot
.
We can manipulate this by modifying the definition using
\gnecessary\,
-trickery in order to get closer to extensionality. However, I do not
see how to get full extensionality.

Letter to the editor: Inquiry regarding lumbosacral transitional vertebrae and sagittal alignment
Authors: Diril Sabri Kerem, Akcali Ayhan, Ozyigit Elif, Ekinci Safak
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the recent publication entitled “Characteristics of the positional and morphological parameters of sagittal spine alignment in a cohort of 623 healthy individuals aged >50 years in China” [1]. The authors’ comprehensive investigation into spinopelvic positional and morphological parameters in a healthy community-dwelling population is a valuable contribution to the understanding of normal sagittal spinal alignment, particularly in the aging spine. The emphasis on regional alignment, including thoracic kyphosis (TK) and lumbar lordosis (LL) assessed by Cobb angles, and the importance of pelvic parameters such as sacral slope (SS), pelvic tilt (PT), and pelvic incidence (PI) in elucidating degeneration and compensatory mechanisms, is well-justified.

Specific queries regarding lumbosacral transitional vertebrae

As acknowledged, lumbosacral transitional vertebrae (LSTV) are a common congenital anomaly in the general population, with reported prevalence rates ranging between 4% and 21% [2,3]. Our own work, based on the examination of 500 lumbar plain films, yielded a similar observation, identifying transitional lumbosacral junctions in 17.6% of cases [4,5].

While the objective of the study is highly relevant, we have two fundamental questions for the authors regarding the methodology and findings:

LSTV detection and impact on sagittal alignment

Did the authors screen for and detect the presence of LSTV in their cohort of 623 healthy individuals? If so, did the presence of an LSTV significantly influence the measured spinopelvic and sagittal spinal alignment parameters (TK, LL, SS, PT, PI, etc.)? Given that LSTV can alter the total number of mobile lumbar segments and affect sacral morphology, its influence on sagittal balance is an important consideration, particularly when establishing normal reference values.

Prevalence consistency

Was the observed prevalence of LSTV in the authors’ Chinese population cohort consistent with the rates reported in the broader literature, including the 17.6% rate observed in our own study?

We believe a clarification on the handling of LSTV in the study cohort is crucial, as this anatomical variant may represent a confounding variable in establishing normative sagittal alignment data, especially concerning the definition of the S1 endpoint for parameters like LL and PI.

We eagerly await the authors’ response and comments on this concern.

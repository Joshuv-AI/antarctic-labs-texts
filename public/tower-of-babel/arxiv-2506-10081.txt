II
An effective theory of geometry

At curvature lengths
\ell
much larger than the Planck length
\ell_{P}=\sqrt{G\hbar}\approx 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}
, a quantum theory of spacetime geometry can be described in terms of an effective action organized as an expansion in the number spacetime derivatives
Donoghue 1994
;
Burgess 2004
;
Weinberg 2008
. The background structure is a
4
-dimensional manifold, symmetries of the action are the spacetime diffeomorphisms encoding the general covariance of the theory, and the dynamical variable is assumed to be a Lorentzian metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
. The most general diffeomorphism-invariant action, up to four spacetime derivatives, is

\displaystyle S[g_{\mu\nu}]=\int\differential[4]{x}\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Big(c_{0}+c_{2}\,R

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{4}\,R^{2}+\tilde{c}_{4}\,R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{\tilde{c}}_{4}\,R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\ldots\Big),

(2)

where
g\equiv\det(g_{\mu\nu})
,
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the Riemann curvature tensor,
R_{\mu\nu}=R^{\alpha}_{~\mu\alpha\nu}
is the Ricci tensor,
R=R^{\mu}_{~\mu}
is the curvature scalar, and
c_{n}
are the coupling constants of terms that contains
n
derivatives of the metric. The first two terms reproduce General Relativity: The constants
c_{0}
and
c_{2}
have already been measured and they are related to the Newton’s constant
G
and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
by the identification
c_{0}=\frac{-2\Lambda}{16\pi G}
, and
c_{2}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}
. Using the identity
W^{2}=2R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}-(2/3)R^{2}+\mathcal{E}
, and dropping the Euler density
\mathcal{E}\equiv 4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}-R^{2}-R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
because, by Gauss-Bonnet theorem, it is a total derivative and therefore it does not contribute to the local dynamics, we can reduce the number of independent coupling constants. Therefore, only the
R^{2}
and the
W^{2}
terms remain at the four-derivative order, beyond General Relativity. After a convenient renaming of the coupling constants, the effective action for spacetime geometry reads

S[g_{\mu\nu}]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\!\int\!\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,\big(-2\Lambda+R+\alpha R^{2}-\beta W^{2}\big).

(3)

With these conventions, the coupling constants have dimensions
[G\hbar]=\textrm{length}^{2}
,
[\Lambda]=\textrm{length}^{-2}
,
[\alpha]=\textrm{length}^{2}
and
[\beta]=\textrm{length}^{2}
. The effective action does not predict the value of these coupling constants, which have to be measured. Here we work under the assumption that there is a hierarchy of scales:

1\ll\frac{\beta}{G\hbar}\ll\frac{\alpha}{G\hbar}\ll\frac{1}{G\hbar\Lambda}\,.

(4)

Cosmological observations are already constraining the physics of the primordial universe in the regime where
\alpha
becomes relevant. If Starobinsky inflation is responsible for the inflationary expansion, then the length scale associated to
\alpha
is expected to be about five orders of magnitude larger than the Planck scale,
\ell_{\mathrm{infl}}\sim\sqrt{\alpha}\sim 10^{5}\,\ell_{P}
. In this regime, the effective description (
3
) applies, and including both the
\alpha
and the
\beta
terms in the effective action is unavoidable.

The
\alpha R^{2}
term introduces an additional scalar degree of freedom, commonly referred to as the
scalaron
, together with a modification of the dynamics of the standard transverse tensor modes of General Relativity. This scalar mode is typically stable and can be interpreted as the
inflaton

\tilde{\varphi}
, once one introduces a field redefinition
g_{\mu\nu}\to(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\tilde{\varphi})
to obtain the Einstein frame formulation of Starobinsky inflation
Maeda 1989
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Ketov 2025
. Therefore the
\alpha R^{2}
term provides a natural mechanism that drives the inflationary phase, without the addition of an ad-hoc inflaton scalar field. Here we treat it geometrically, without the introduction of an ad-hoc redefinition.

The
\beta W^{2}
term includes higher-order time derivatives that can lead to a pathological behavior if treated as fundamental, instead of effective. Specifically, the quadratic Lagrangian for perturbations around a cosmological background includes terms of the form
\mathcal{L}\sim\beta\,f(t)\,|\ddot{\Psi}(\mathbf{k},t)|^{2}
, where
f(t)
is a background-dependent function, and
\Psi(\mathbf{k},t)
denotes the scalar or the tensor perturbations, resulting in additional ghost degrees of freedom which pose a challenge to the consistency of the theory at the perturbative level

.

We adopt the assumption that
\beta\ll\alpha
and that the physics of the
\beta W^{2}
term introduces only corrections to Starobinsky inflation that are self-consistent in the limit
\beta\to 0
. This minimal assumption reflects the observational evidence that only three gravitational degrees of freedom are relevant at the inflationary scale: the two transverse-traceless tensor polarizations predicted by General Relativity, and confirmed by the direct detection of gravitational waves
Abbott et al. 2016
;
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2021
, and a single scalar degree of freedom, the scalaron, that naturally provides a dynamical mechanism for the generation of the observed spectrum of primordial curvature perturbations
Starobinsky 1980
;
Mukhanov and Chibisov 1981
;
Vilenkin 1985
. Consequently, contributions induced by the
\beta W^{2}
are treated as effective corrections, in a similar fashion to the effective field theory approach advocated by Weinberg in
Weinberg 2008
, with any higher time derivative reduced in order to obtain a self-consistent expansion that is analytic in the coupling constant
\beta
. This procedure ensures that the effective theory remains predictive and free of unphysical degrees of freedom at larger length scales.

In the next section, we present a pedagogical introduction to the method of
reduction of order
, that guarantees a self-consistent implementation of this prescription.

III
Reduction of Order

Theories described by actions involving higher-than-first-order time derivatives generically lead to pathologies in the dynamics such as runaway solutions and the Ostrogradsky instability; see, e.g.,
Delhom et al. 2022
for a pedagogical introduction. This peculiar behavior is not merely of mathematical interest, but also relevant for a variety of physics problem. A notable example is in electrodynamics: the Lorentz–Abraham–Dirac equation
Dirac 1938
describes the self-interaction of an accelerating charged particle and includes a third-order time derivative in its equation of motion, which leads to unphysical effects such as pre-acceleration and runaway solutions. These issues can be mitigated under reasonable physical assumptions, e.g., by treating the radiation reaction as a small perturbative correction to the Lorentz force. Such an approach leads to the Landau–Lifshitz equation
Landau and Lifschits 1975
, a second-order differential equation that provides a consistent physically-meaningful description. Remarkably, recent experimental efforts are starting to provide supporting evidence for the Landau–Lifshitz formulation
Cole et al. 2018
;
Nielsen et al. 2021
.

Using the Lorentz–Abraham–Dirac equation as an example, Bhabha
Bhabha 1946
identified a key insight: imposing analyticity in the coupling constant associated with the higher-order time derivative corrections is necessary to distinguish physical solutions from unphysical ones. The application of these ideas in the context of gravity with higher-order curvature terms was also discussed by Simon in
Simon 1990
. Here we follow a similar logic.

There are three relevant conditions that an effective theory has to satisfy for the self-consistent implementation of the reduction of order of higher derivative terms with coupling constant
\beta
:

C_{1}
:

Coupling constants and stability
.
The solutions of the effective theory must be stable under small perturbations of the initial conditions. This condition restricts the range of the coupling constant
\beta
to values that avoid unphysical runaway solutions.

C_{2}
:

Initial conditions and analyticity
. The initial conditions are restricted to the class that determines solutions which are analytic in an expansion around
\beta=0
, so that we can consistently consider the uncoupled limit.

C_{3}
:

Regime of validity
. The effective dynamics is defined by an asymptotic expansion in a dimensionless coupling constant
\lambda\propto\beta
which is linear in
\beta
and constructed from the characteristic scales of the problem, with the requirement
\absolutevalue{\lambda}\ll 1
for the leading order corrections to provide accurate predictions.

To illustrate this procedure, we consider a simple toy model: a classical oscillator with mass
m_{0}>0
, frequency
\omega_{0}>0
, and with an extra acceleration term with coupling constant
\beta
of unspecified sign and dimension
[\beta]=\textrm{time}^{2}
, which is described by the action

S[x]=\int\differential{t}\differential{x}m_{0}\quantity[\frac{ \dot{x}(t)^2}{2} - \frac{\omega_0^2 x(t)^2 }{2} - \frac{\beta\ddot{x}(t)^2}{2}].

(5)

The variational principle
\delta S=0
yields the equation of motion,

\ddot{x}(t)+\omega_{0}^{2}\,x(t)+\beta\,\ddddot{x}(t)=0,

(6)

which admits exact solutions of the general form:

x(t)=A_{+}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Lambda_{+}t}+B_{+}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Lambda_{+}t}+A_{-}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Lambda_{-}t}+B_{-}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\Lambda_{-}t},

(7)

where

\dddot{x}(t_{0})
. The characteristic frequencies are

\Lambda_{\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4\beta\omega_{0}^{2}}}{2\beta\omega_{0}^{2}}}\;\omega_{0}\,.

(8)

From this expression we see that the relevant dimensionless coupling constant of the problem is
\lambda=\beta\,\omega_{0}^{2}

7

IV
The inflationary background in the geometric framework

We consider a background spacetime geometry described by a spatially flat Friedman-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\differential{x}^{\mu}\differential{x}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-\differential{t}^{2}+a(t)^{2}\delta_{ij}\differential{x}^{i}\differential{x}^{j}

\displaystyle=a(\eta)^{2}\,(-\differential{\eta}^{2}+\delta_{ij}\differential{x}^{i}\differential{x}^{j})\,,

(16)

with conformal time
\eta
defined in terms of the cosmic time
t
by
\differential{t}=a(\eta)\differential{\eta}
as usual, and
a(t)
the scale factor. An inflationary phase of quasi-de Sitter expansion is defined as an epoch where the Hubble rate
H(t)\equiv\dot{a}(t)/a(t)
varies slowly. To describe this phase, it is useful to introduce dimensionless Hubble flow parameters:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1H}(t)\,

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{H(t)}{H(t)^{2}}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{nH}(t)\,

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\dot{\epsilon}_{n-1\,H}(t)}{H(t)\,\epsilon_{n-1\,H}(t)}\,.

(18)

Moreover, because of the separation of scales, the cosmological constant

) reduces to

S[g_{\mu\nu}]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\differential[4]{x}\,\sqrt{-g}\,\big(R+\alpha R^{2}-\beta W^{2}\big).

(19)

We neglect also the Standard Model matter fields as their contribution becomes important only after the end of inflation, during the reheating phase. Therefore, the variational principle for the action (

) results in field equations with higher curvature terms,

G_{\mu\nu}\,+\,\alpha\,\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}\,-4\,\beta\,\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}=\,0\,,

(20)

) and with the covariantly conserved tensors (

,
\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{H}^{\mu\nu}=0
, and
\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{B}^{\mu\nu}=0
) defined by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,R\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,R^{2}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\frac{1}{4\,\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,W^{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}W_{\alpha\beta\rho\sigma}\,.

(23)

Their components are given by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=R_{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}R\,g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=2\big(R\,G_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{(\mu}\nabla_{\nu)}R+(\square R+\tfrac{1}{4}R^{2})g_{\mu\nu}\big)\,,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\;=\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}W^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}+\tfrac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta}W^{\mu\alpha\nu\beta},

(26)

where
\square=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}
. The variation of the
\beta\,W^{2}
term results in the Bach tensor
\mathcal{B}_{\mu\nu}
, which vanishes on conformally flat spacetimes, such as the FLRW background (
IV
). Therefore the term
\beta\,W^{2}
in the effective action does not affect the background dynamics which is the same as the one described by the Einstein-Starobinsky equations
G_{\mu\nu}+\alpha\,\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}=0
. When evaluated on the FLRW metric (
IV

H(t)^{2}-36\,\alpha\,H(t)^{4}\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)\,\big(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1H}(t)-\tfrac{1}{3}\epsilon_{2H}(t)\big)=0\,.

(27)

A direct consequence of including the higher curvature terms in (
19

Ruzmaǐkina and Ruzmaǐkin 1969

;
Starobinsky 1983
;

, one of the oldest inflationary models, and initially motivated by quantum-gravity arguments on the renormalization of the energy-momentum tensor. In this geometric framework (also known as the Jordan frame), spacetime is described by the physical metric

minimally-coupled to an auxiliary metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

Faraoni et al. 1999

V
Cosmological Perturbations

As shown in Sec.
IV
, the background dynamics is governed by the
R+\alpha\,R^{2}
sector of the full action (
19
). On the other hand, perturbations are sensitive to the
\beta W^{2}
term. We treat the Weyl-squared term
\beta\,W^{2}
self-consistently as a higher-order effective correction, without adding new degrees of freedom to the two transverse-traceless tensor polarizations and the scalaron of the
R+\alpha\,R^{2}
theory. In this sense, this approach follows the same logic as Weinberg’s effective field theory of inflation
Weinberg 2008
, with the further requirement that the only relevant dynamical degrees of freedom are gravitational, without any additional matter field contribution. Accordingly, the action naturally splits into a core sector and its effective correction,

S[g_{\mu\nu}]=S_{0}[g_{\mu\nu}]+S_{\mathrm{eff}}[g_{\mu\nu}]\,,

(32)

where
S_{0}[g_{\mu\nu}]
is the usual Starobinsky action,

S_{0}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,(R+\alpha R^{2})\,,

(33)

and the most general combination of effective corrections at the quadratic order in curvature is encoded in the Weyl-squared action,

S_{\mathrm{eff}}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,(-\beta\,W^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,W_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma})\,,

(34)

treated via reduction of order.
The dynamics of the Scalar-Vector-Tensor (SVT) perturbations is determined by the tensor
F^{\mu\nu}[g_{\mu\nu}]
obtained from the variation of the action (
33
),

F^{\mu\nu}[g_{\mu\nu}]\equiv\functionalderivative{S_0}{g_{\mu\nu}}\,=\,-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\sqrt{-g}\,\big(G^{\mu\nu}+\alpha\,\mathcal{H^{\mu\nu}}\big)\,,

(35)

where we used (
24
)–(
25
) and
\delta g_{\mu\nu}=-g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}
. We restrict to small perturbations
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
of the FLRW metric (
IV
), so that the spacetime geometry is given (exactly) by
g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu}
, and

F^{\mu\nu}[\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu}]=\bar{F}_{0}^{\mu\nu}(t)\,+\,\bar{F}_{1}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(t)\,\delta g_{\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(\delta g^{2})\,,

where
\bar{F}_{1}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(t)=\evaluated{\delta^2 S_0/(\delta g_{\mu\nu} \, \delta g_{\rho\sigma})}_{g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}}
. Note that, since the background metric satisfies the Friedman equation (
27
), we have
\bar{F}_{0}^{\mu\nu}=0
. Therefore, the action (
33
), at quadratic order in the perturbation, can be written as

S_{0}[\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g_{\mu\nu}]=\bar{S}_{0}^{(0)}(t)+S_{0}^{(2)}[\delta g_{\mu\nu}],

(36)

with the quadratic action given by,

S_{0}^{(2)}[\delta g_{\mu\nu}]=\int\!\differential[4]{x}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}_{1}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(t)\,\delta g_{\rho\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(\delta g^{3})\,.

(37)

The homogeneity and isotropy of the background allows us to organize the perturbations into SVT representations of the
3d
Euclidean group,
\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\delta g^{(\mathrm{s})}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g^{(\mathrm{v})}_{\mu\nu}+\delta g^{(\mathrm{t})}_{\mu\nu}
which in the quadratic action decouple, as shown by working in the Fourier domain, where we use
\psi(\mathbf{k},t)
to denote the Fourier transform of any SVT mode
\Psi(\mathbf{x},t)
, with

\Psi(\mathbf{x},t)=\int\frac{\differential[3]{\vb{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\psi(\mathbf{k},t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}.

(38)

The goal is to cast the quadratic contributions to the action, both from (
34
) and (
37
), for each SVT mode, in the form

S^{(2)}[\psi]=\!\int\!\!\differential{t}\!\!\int\!\!\frac{\differential[3]{\vb{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,a(t)^{3}Z_{\psi}(t)\Bigg[\frac{|\dot{\psi}(\mathbf{k},t)|^{2}}{2}-\frac{c_{\psi}(t)^{2}\,k^{2}}{a(t)^{2}}\frac{\absolutevalue{\psi(\vb{k},t)}^{2}}{2}\Bigg],

(39)

where the kinetic amplitude,
Z_{\psi}(t)
, and the speed of sound,
c_{\psi}(t)
, are two independent functions that do not depend on the mode
k
. We require also that they satisfy the no-ghost condition
Z_{\psi}(t)>0
, and the Laplacian stability condition
c_{\psi}(t)^{2}>0
. This is exactly the general form of an effective theory of inflationary perturbations studied in
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
, where we developed the formalism that allows us to provide explicit expressions for the fully expanded primordial power spectrum and other inflationary observables up to the next-to-next-to-next-to leading order (N3LO).

Scalar perturbations

First, note that the Starobinsky action can be written in a form that allows us to recognize the gravitational scalar degree of freedom. If we define
\chi\equiv 1+2\,\alpha R
, then

S_{0}[g_{\mu\nu}]=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\differential[4]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,\quantity(\chi\, R - \frac{1}{4\,\alpha} (\chi-1)^2).

(40)

When
\chi
is treated independently, and assuming small perturbations such that
\chi(\mathbf{x},t)=\bar{\chi}(t)+\delta\chi(\mathbf{x},t)
, the first variation of the action (
40
) gives,

\square\chi(\mathbf{x},t)=\frac{1}{6\,\alpha}\,\chi(\mathbf{x},t)\,

(41)

where the emergence of a propagating scalar degree of freedom, the
scalaron
, becomes evident, as typically found in
f(R)
theories of gravity
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
. For metric perturbations, we use the Arnowitt–Deser–Misner (ADM) variables

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\differential x^{\mu}\differential{x}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-N^{2}\differential{t}^{2}+h_{ij}(N^{i}\differential{t}+\differential{y}^{i})(N^{j}\differential{t}+\differential{y}^{j})\,,

(42)

with
i,j=1,2,3
. Thus, the small metric perturbation
\delta g^{(\mathrm{s})}_{\mu\nu}
can be expressed in terms of the lapse
N=1+\delta N
and of the shift
N^{i}=0+\delta N^{i}
, with scalar perturbations
\delta N(\mathbf{k},t),
and
C
, with
\delta N^{i}(\mathbf{k},t)=\mathrm{i}\,k^{i}C(\mathbf{k},t)
, together with the
3d
metric
h_{ab}=a(t)^{2}\,(\delta_{ab}+\delta h_{ab}^{(\mathrm{s})})
, with scalar perturbations
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{S}
, as

\delta h_{ab}^{(\mathrm{s})}(\mathbf{k},t)=-2\,\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k},t)\,\delta_{ab}+k_{a}k_{b}\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{k},t)\,.

(43)

In summary, we recognize 5 scalar perturbative quantities:

\delta\chi(\mathbf{k},t),\;\delta N(\mathbf{k},t),\;C(\mathbf{k},t),\;\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{k},t),\;\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k},t)\,.

(44)

We work in the comoving gauge, setting
C(\mathbf{k},t)=0
and
\mathcal{H}^{0}{}_{i}=0
for the Starobinsky tensor (
22
), which generalizes the comoving gauge
T^{0}{}_{i}=0
for the energy-momentum in General Relativity with matter. These conditions implicitly imply that
\delta\chi(\mathbf{k},t)=0
. The diffeomorphism constraint
F^{0}{}_{i}\approx 0
imposes

\delta N(\mathbf{k},t)=-\frac{2\,\bar{\chi}(t)\,\dot{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{k},t)}{2H(t)\,\bar{\chi}(t)+\dot{\bar{\chi}}(t)}\,,

(45)

while in combination with the Hamitonian constraint
F^{0}{}_{0}\approx 0
we find,

(46)

where we have introduced the background value for
\bar{\chi}(t)
,

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv 1+2\,\alpha\,\bar{R}(t)

\displaystyle=1+24\,\alpha\,H(t)^{2}\,(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1H}(t))\,,

(47)

together with its Hubble flow parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1\chi}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\dot{\bar{\chi}}(t)}{H(t)\,\bar{\chi}(t)}\,,\,\epsilon_{2\chi}(t)=-\frac{\dot{\epsilon}_{1\chi}(t)}{H(t)\,\epsilon_{1\chi}(t)}\;,\;\mathrm{etc}.

(48)

Therefore, we can express all the five scalar perturbations (
44
) uniquely in terms of the curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k},t)
. Note that, at first order, constant-
t
spatial sections have a scalar curvature given by the
3d
Ricci scalar
{}^{(3)\!}R=4a(t)^{-2}\,\delta^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathcal{R}

S_{0}
in (
37
) for the curvature perturbation

39
), with kinetic amplitude and speed of sound:

\displaystyle Z^{(\alpha)}_{\textrm{s}}(t)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{3\bar{\chi}(t)}{16\pi G}\quantity(\frac{\epsilon_{1\chi}(t)}{1-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{1\chi}(t)})^{2}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle c^{(\alpha)}_{\textrm{s}}(t)\,

\displaystyle=\,1\,.

(50)

The stability of perturbations, also known as no-ghost condition, requires

, or

Vector perturbations

We introduce the transverse vector fields

For the transverse-traceless tensor perturbations, the 3d metric reads

\ddot{\gamma}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k},t)+H(t)\,\quantity(3-\epsilon_{1Zt}^{(\alpha)}(t))\dot{\gamma}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k},t)+\frac{k^{2}}{a(t)^{2}}\,\gamma_{\sigma}(\mathbf{k},t)=0\,,

Since the equation of motion (

VI
Inflationary observables and comparison with observations

We summarize the formalism developed in
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
;
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024b
for the computation of the primordial power spectrum associated to any theory of inflation with quadratic action of the form (
39
). The primordial tensor and curvature perturbations that induce the seeds of the large-scale structure and the CMB temperature anisotropies are assumed to be quantum fields
\hat{\Psi}(\mathbf{x},t)
initially in a Fock vacuum
\ket{0}
, defined by
\hat{a}(\mathbf{k})\ket{0}=0\,
,
\forall\,\mathbf{k}
, with bosonic creation and annihilation operators,
[\hat{a}(\mathbf{k}),\hat{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}})]=(2\pi)^{3}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
. In Fourier space, the field is represented as a mode expansion

\hat{\psi}(\mathbf{k},t)=u(k,t)\,\hat{a}(\mathbf{k})+u^{\ast}(k,t)\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(-\mathbf{k})\,.

(71)

We consider a generalization of the Mukhanov-Sasaki variables, and perform a time reparametrization

t\;\to\;y\equiv-k\,\tau=k\,\hat{c}_{\psi}(t)\,\eta\,,

(72)

where
\tau=-\hat{c}_{\psi}(t)\,\eta
generalizes the conformal time
\eta
including the speed of sound of (
39
), and
\hat{c}_{\psi}(t)
is defined in Appendix
A
. Additionally, the mode functions are rescaled via,

u(k,t)\to\frac{y\,w(y)}{\sqrt{2\,k^{3}\,\mu(y)}}\,,

(73)

with
\mu(y)=(\hbar\,H(t)^{2})^{-1}\,Z_{\psi}(t)\,c_{\psi}(t)\,\tilde{c}_{\psi}^{2}
, and
\tilde{c}_{\psi}=\hat{c}_{\psi}\,(a(t)\,H(t)\,\eta)
. The canonical commutation relations become canonical Wronskian conditions for the mode functions, i.e.,
w(y)\,w^{\prime}{}^{\ast}(y)-w^{\prime}(y)\,w^{\ast}(y)=-2\,\mathrm{i}
, while their equation of motion is given by

w^{\prime\prime}(y)+\quantity(1-\frac{2}{y^2} )w(y)=\frac{g(y)}{y^{2}}\,w(y)\,,

(74)

where
g(y)=g_{1k}+g_{2k}\,\ln(y)\,+\,g_{3k}\,\ln(y)^{2}+\order{\epsilon^4}
, and the coefficients that depend on the background quantities
H(t)
,
Z_{\psi}(t)
, and
c_{\psi}(t)
, are given in (
B
). A derivation of (
74
) is discussed in Appendix
B
. The mode function
w_{\mathrm{qBD}}(y)
, associated to the quasi-Bunch-Davies vacuum
\ket{0}
, is the unique solution of the Wronskian condition and the mode equation (
74
), determined iteratively via the Green function method
Stewart and Gong 2001
;
Auclair and Ringeval 2022
;
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
;
Ballardini et al. 2025
;
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024b
. For this state, the late-time power spectrum at N3LO is given by,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{qBD}}^{(\psi)}(k)\equiv\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}|u_{\mathrm{qBD}}(k,t)|^{2}=\lim_{y\to 0^{+}}\frac{\absolutevalue{y\,w_{\mathrm{qBD}}(y)}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\,\mu(y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar\,H_{\ast}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}Z^{(\psi)}_{\ast}c^{(\psi)3}_{\ast}}\Bigg[p_{0\ast}+p_{1\ast}\ln(\frac{k}{k_\ast} )+p_{2\ast}\ln(\frac{k}{k_\ast})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+p_{3\ast}\ln(\frac{k}{k_\ast})^{3}\Bigg]\,,

(75)

where each of the quantities is evaluated at a pivot scale defined by
k_{\ast}\tau_{\ast}=-1
, following the logarithmic expansion described in Appendix
B
, and the coefficients
p_{0\ast}
,
p_{1\ast}
and
p_{2\ast}
, which depend on the Hubble flow parameters, are explicitly given in Tabs. III, IV, V, and VI of
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
. It is important to note that the generalized conformal time
\tau
depends implicitly on the speed of sound
\hat{c}_{\psi}(t)
. As it is clear from (
V
) and (
V
), scalar and tensor modes have different speeds of sound from each other. As a consequence, the same pivot scale is associated to two slightly different times for the freezing of scalar and of tensor modes. The precision prediction of the tensor-to-scalar ratio requires that we determine the relevant scale for scalars and for tensors consistently. As scalar modes have already been measured, we choose to work with the generalized time associated to scalar modes. To be more concrete, for tensor modes we denote the pivot scale defined by
k_{\triangle}\tau_{\triangle}=k_{\triangle}\,\hat{c}_{\mathrm{t}}(\eta_{\triangle})\,\eta_{\triangle}=-1
, while for scalar modes we have
k_{\odot}\tau_{\odot}=k_{\odot}\,\hat{c}_{\mathrm{s}}(\eta_{\odot})\,\eta_{\odot}=-1

(76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{qBD}}^{(\mathrm{t})}(k)

VII
Discussion

We studied the effective theory of
R+\alpha\,R^{2}-\beta\,W^{2}
inflation, providing precision N3LO predictions obtained via the Green’s function method, together with the reduction of order method. These results extend the previous precision results obtained in
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
for the Starobinsky
R+\alpha\,R^{2}
inflationary model, which provides a de facto one-parameter reference for evaluating the evidence of other inflationary models
Martin et al. 2014
;
Kallosh and Linde 2025
;
Martin et al. 2024
;
Martin et al. 2011
;
Ballardini 2025
;
Sørensen et al. 2025
.

The reduction-of-order method applied to the Weyl-squared term
\beta\,W^{2}
allows us to provide a self-consistent analysis of the most general diffeomorphism-invariant effective theory of geometry, up to quadratic curvature terms, with an effective Lagrangian of the form
-2\Lambda+R+\alpha\,R^{2}-\beta\,W^{2}
. As in any effective theory, the coupling constants have to be measured before predictions can be extracted from the theory. Cosmological observations of the amplitude of scalar perturbations
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}
and of the scalar tilt
n_{\mathrm{s}}
provide a measurement of the coupling constant
\alpha
,

\frac{\alpha}{G\,\hbar}\approx\frac{2}{9\,\pi}\frac{1}{(n_{\mathrm{s}}-1)^{2}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{s}}}\approx 3.7\,\times\,10^{10}\,,

(87)

which is compatible with the assumed hierarchy of scales
G\hbar\ll\alpha\ll\Lambda^{-1}
. Furthermore, a detection of primordial gravitational waves and, specifically, a sufficiently precise measurement of the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
, would provide a measurement of the ratio
\beta/\alpha
, as

\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=6\left(1-\frac{r}{3(n_{\mathrm{s}}-1)^{2}}\right)+\order{\mathrm{N3LO}}\,,

(88)

which is treated at linear order
\order{\beta/\alpha}
in this paper, assuming
G\hbar\ll\beta\ll\alpha
and adopting a self-consistent reduction of order of higher-derivative terms. We note that the value (
88
) of the
\beta/\alpha
correction to the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
reproduces the leading-order result computed in
Anselmi et al. 2020
using other methods, confirming its robustness. Here, using the Green’s function method for effective theories of inflation developed in
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
;
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024b
, we computed the inflationary observables that characterize the primordial power spectrum of scalar and tensor modes up to N3LO precision, as reported in Tab.
1
. These results provide precision predictions, as illustrated in Tab.
2
, which can be tested with next-generation CMB and gravitational wave observatories.

It would be interesting to extend the analysis of
R+\alpha\,R^{2}-\beta\,W^{2}
inflation presented in this paper, to take into account observational constraints from the post-inflationary reheating epoch. In fact, given the recent results from ACT DR6
Calabrese et al. 2025
and other planned cosmological observatories
Ballardini et al. 2024
;
LiteBIRD Collaboration et al. 2023
, a detailed characterization of the reheating epoch becomes increasingly relevant. We note that the description of the reheating phase is sensitive to the coupling of the metric to Standard Model matter fields, and therefore a careful distinction between a Jordan-frame minimal coupling from an Einstein-frame one is needed. In fact, most of the currently available analysis of constraints on Starobinsky inflation are done using an inflaton field potential
V(\tilde{\varphi})
and a number of efoldings
\tilde{N}_{*}
defined with respect to the Einstein-frame auxiliary metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
obtained via the field redefinition
g_{\mu\nu}\to(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\tilde{\varphi})
. In this paper we worked consistently in a geometric framework (Jordan frame) where there is no additional inflaton field. Specifically, the two quantities
N_{\ast}
and
\Delta_{\ast}
are defined directly with respect to the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, assuming minimal coupling of Standard Model matter fields. While each of the results on inflationary observables reported in Tab.
1
are frame independent as they are expressed in a relational form in terms of
n_{\mathrm{s}}
, the values of
N_{\ast}
and
\Delta_{\ast}
explicitly depend on the choice of frame. Specifically, we note that the rescaled reheating parameter
R_{\mathrm{reh}}
which is used to characterize the reheating epoch
Martin and Ringeval 2006
;
Martin and Ringeval 2010
;
Martin et al. 2011
;
Martin et al. 2015
;
Martin et al. 2024
, is related to the inflationary observables via the matching condition
N_{\ast}=N_{0}+\ln(R_{\mathrm{reh}})+\ln(H_\ast/H_{\mathrm{end}})

Zharov et al. 2025
, where
N_{0}\approx 61.1
describes the number of e-foldings from the end of reheating to today, assuming typical values of the Standard Model thermal history. This matching condition can be written in terms of the parameter
\Delta_{\ast}
computed in (
VI
). We find that the rescaled reheating parameter
R_{\mathrm{reh}}
is therefore constrained by current observations thanks to the relation

\displaystyle\ln(R_{\mathrm{reh}})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\ast}-N_{0}\,.

(89)

This relation is a distinctive prediction of
R+\alpha\,R^{2}-\beta\,W^{2}
inflation in the geometric frame, as
\Delta_{\ast}
is completely fixed by the gravitational dynamics and can be expressed in terms of
n_{\mathrm{s}}-1
, as shown in (
VI
). For a fiducial value
n_{\mathrm{s}}=0.97
for the scalar tilt, we have
\Delta_{\ast}\approx 64.4
and therefore the prediction
\ln(R_{\mathrm{reh}})\approx 3.3
. This estimate is consistent with a recent analysis of reheating in Starobinsky inflation using the Einstein frame
Zharov et al. 2025
. Other extensions of Starobinsky inflation have been proposed recently in light of the current ACT DR6 results
Drees and Xu 2025
;
Yin 2025
;
Yogesh et al. 2025
;
Addazi et al. 2025
, and it would be interesting to compare the constraints that arise in these different approaches.

Another important direction for future work is the extension of the framework presented in this paper to include a preinflationary epoch. In fact, the transition from a quantum geometry phase to the effective-field-theory phase can leave observable imprints on the largest scales of the CMB, departing from the quasi–Bunch-Davies vacuum and resulting in features in the primordial power spectrum
Chluba et al. 2015
;
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024b
;
Ballardini et al. 2024
. Encoding the effects of the preinflationary epoch into squeezed vacua for the effective geometric theory of
R+\alpha\,R^{2}-\beta\,W^{2}
inflation opens up a promising window into the phenomenology of quantum gravity. In fact, in the context of non-perturbative approaches to quantum geometry
Ashtekar and Bianchi 2021
;
Ashtekar and Singh 2011
;
Bianchi et al. 2010
;
Gozzini and Vidotto 2021
;
Gielen et al. 2013
;
Dittrich et al. 2022
;
Agullo et al. 2023
, the effects of the Weyl-squared term
Borissova and Dittrich 2023
;
Dittrich and Padua-Argüelles 2024
, and of other possible quantum geometry effects in cosmology
Gielen and Mickel 2025
;
Oriti and Pang 2025
;
Mena Marugán et al. 2024
;
Mena Marugan et al. 2024
, are currently being considered. The precision analysis of the effective theory of quantum geometry, presented here for the inflationary phase, can lead to the falsification of predictions from quantum gravity and quantum geometry, allowing next-generation CMB and gravitational-wave experiments provide stringent tests of quantum gravitational physics.

1/\sqrt{36\,\alpha}
, which is consistent with numerical solutions of (
27
), as shown in Fig.
2
. Additionally, replacing
H_{\mathrm{end}}
into (
99
) gives us
\epsilon_{2H}(t_{\mathrm{end}})\approx-1.883
. One can follow a similar procedure to find a modified Padé approximant for
\epsilon_{2H}(t)
as a function of
\epsilon_{1H}(t)
. Let us assume a rational function of the form

\epsilon_{2H}(t)=-\frac{a\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)+b\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{2}}{1+c\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)+d\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{2}+e\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{3}}\,,

(102)

and we impose four matching conditions with the expansion (
IV
), and one last restriction to
\epsilon_{2H}(t_{\mathrm{end}})=-354/188\approx-1.883
. These five conditions gives a modified [2/3] Padé approximant of the form,

[2/3]_{\epsilon_{2H}(t)}=-\frac{2\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)+\tfrac{5993}{927}\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{2}}{1+\tfrac{3151}{927}\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)+\tfrac{803}{2781}\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{2}-\tfrac{535}{2781}\,\epsilon_{1H}(t)^{3}}\,.

(103)

The amount of cosmological expansion from the time
t_{\odot}
, associated to the pivot scale
k_{\odot}\tau_{\odot}=-1
until the end of inflation, is given by the e-folding number
N_{\odot}
, defined as

a(t_{\mathrm{end}})=\mathrm{e}^{N_{\odot}}\,a(t_{\odot})\,.

(104)

To find
N_{\odot}
, an analytical expression as the integral over the Hubble flow parameters is available,

N_{\odot}\equiv\int_{t_{\odot}}^{t_{\textrm{end}}}H(t)\differential{t}=-\int_{\epsilon_{1H\odot}}^{1}\frac{\differential{\epsilon_{1H}}}{\epsilon_{1H}\;\,\epsilon_{2H}\big(\epsilon_{1H}\big)}\,,

(105)

and after replacing (
103
), we find

N_{\odot}=\frac{1}{2\,\epsilon_{1H\odot}}+\frac{1}{12}\ln(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1H\odot}})-\frac{19049}{35958}+\frac{535}{17979}\epsilon_{1H\odot}+\frac{4232991\,\ln(\tfrac{1854+5993\, \epsilon_{1H\odot}}{7847})}{143664196}\,.

(106)

The expansion for small
\epsilon_{1H\odot}
gives a final expression

N_{\odot}=\frac{1}{2\,\epsilon_{1H\odot}}+\frac{1}{12}\ln(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1H\odot}})-D_{0}+\frac{\epsilon_{1H\odot}}{8}+\order{\epsilon_{1H\odot}^2}

(107)

where
D_{0}=19049/35958+4232991\,\ln(\tfrac{7847}{1854})/143664196\approx 0.57
. This result estimates the number of e-foldings with percent-level accuracy, as confirmed by direct numerical integration. Moreover, the calculation of the constant
D_{0}
represents a refinement of the results in

Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
, where it was previously estimated to be
D_{0}\approx 0.60
using a different approximation scheme.

It is useful to have a relation expressed uniquely in terms of observables, in particular
n_{\mathrm{s}}
. To find a relation of the form
N_{\odot}(n_{\mathrm{s}})
, we can write an ansatz of the form,

N_{\odot}=\frac{a}{1-n_{\mathrm{s}}}+b\,\ln(\frac{1}{1-n_\mathrm{s}})+c\,(1-n_{\mathrm{s}})+d+\order{(1-n_\mathrm{s})^2}\,.

(108)

Then, we replace the expansion
\epsilon_{1H\odot}
to
n_{\mathrm{s}}
, and by matching the coefficients of (
107
), we find

N_{\odot}=\frac{2}{1-n_{\mathrm{s}}}+\frac{1}{12}\ln(\frac{1}{1-n_{\mathrm{s}}})-(c_{0}+\tfrac{\beta}{48\,\alpha})(1-n_{\mathrm{s}})-d_{0}+\order{(1-n_\mathrm{s})^2}\,,

(109)

where
c_{0}=7\pi^{2}/24-C/4-707/288\approx 0.606
, and
d_{0}=(7+\ln(2)+6\,C-6D_{0})/6\approx 0.02
. One can also invert (
109
) and find,

n_{\mathrm{s}}=1-\frac{2}{N_{\odot}}+\frac{\theta_{1}}{N_{\odot}^{2}}-\frac{1}{6}\,\frac{\ln(N_\odot)}{N_{\odot}^{2}}+\frac{\theta_{2}\,\ln(N_\odot)}{N_{\odot}^{3}}-\frac{1}{72}\frac{\ln(N_\odot)^{2}}{N_{\odot}^{3}}+\frac{\theta_{3}+\tfrac{\beta}{12\,\alpha}}{N_{\odot}^{3}}+\order{\frac{1}{N_\odot^4}}\,,

(110)

where
\theta_{1}=\ln(2)/6+2\,d_{0}\approx 0.155
,
\theta_{2}=(1+24\,d_{0}+2\ln(2))/72\approx 0.040
, and

\theta_{3}=(-12\,d_{0}-144\,d_{0}^{2}+288\,c_{0}-\ln(2)-24\,d_{0}\ln(2)-\ln(2)^{2})/72\approx 2.399
. This expression refines a previous calculation found in
Bianchi and Gamonal 2024a
.

It is important to remark that a dependence on
\beta/\alpha
enters only as a sub-leading correction at order
\order{N_\odot^{-3}}
. Consequently, we drop the
\odot
label and use
N_{\ast}
, with the associated pivot
k_{\ast}=a_{\ast}H_{\ast}
, to facilitate the comparison with observations and other works in the literature.

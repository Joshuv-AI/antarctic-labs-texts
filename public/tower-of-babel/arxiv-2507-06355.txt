Density matrix analysis of systems influenced by periodic Hamiltonians
Soham Sen, Manjari Dutta, Sunandan Gangopadhyay

Abstract
In this work, we consider simple systems that are influenced by Hamiltonians with time periodicity. Our analysis is mainly focussed on the density matrix approach and aims to solve the Liouville equation of motion from which one can extract the state of the system when the system is in a pure state. We start our analysis with the standard Rabi-oscillation problem. We consider a density matrix corresponding to the entire model system and solve the Liouville equation of motion. We have then made use of the Lewis-Reisenfeld invariant approach and arrive at the exact same result which implies that the density matrix of the system can indeed be identified with the Lewis invariant. Finally, we consider a two-level system with a constant magnetic field in the z z -direction and a time dependent magnetic field in the x x -direction. Finally, we solve the Liouville equation of motion for this system and calculate the various coherence measures and plot them to investigate the time dependence and reliability of different coherence measures.

Introduction

From the advent of quantum mechanics in the beginning of the twentieth century, the investigation of systems influenced by time-dependent Hamiltonians are of great interest as it very closely portrays real physical systems. In quantum mechanics, a system is represented by a state which eveolves through time via the influence of a Hamiltonian. The most general way to obtain a state of the system is by solving the time-dependent Schrödinger’s equation of motion. While dealing with state vectors for a system one thing that needs to be remembered for multi-level system is that the system can also be in a mixed state such that one cannot truly write down a state vector of the system. Hence, the way out is to write down a density matrix and solve the Liouville equations of motion. Even for systems with pure-state the density matrix of the system weave out a better perspective from a dynamical as well as information theoretical point of view. As it helps one to write down several coherence measures which portray different quantum mechanical effects while considering an open quantum system. \displaystyle=\gamma_{1}^{\lambda}(E_{g}-E_{e})+(\delta_{2}^{\lambda}-\delta_{1}^{\lambda})\alpha_{0}^{*}\Lambda_{ge}e^{i\omega_{0}t}

(45)

\displaystyle i\dot{\gamma}_{2}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{2}^{\lambda}(E_{g}-E_{e})-(\delta_{2}^{\lambda}-\delta_{1}^{\lambda})\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}e^{-i\omega_{0}t}~.

(46)

Taking
\delta_{1}^{\lambda}(t)=\xi^{2}(t)
, and adding eq.(s)(
43
,
44
), we obtain
\dot{\delta}_{2}^{\lambda}(t)=-2\xi\dot{\xi}
. This implies
\delta_{2}^{\lambda}(t)=C-\xi^{2}(t)
with
C
being an integration constant. At
t=0
,
|\psi_{LR}(0)\rangle=|g\rangle
as the system is in the ground state and as a result
\theta(0)=0
with
|\phi_{LR}(0)\rangle=|g\rangle
as well. Now, we know that
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(0)|\phi_{LR}(0)\rangle=|\phi_{LR}(0)\rangle
at
t=0
. The standard action of
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(0)
on
|\phi_{LR}(0)\rangle
gives

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{I}}(0)|g\rangle&=\delta_{1}^{\lambda}(0)|g\rangle+\gamma_{2}^{\lambda}(0)|e\rangle~.\end{split}

(47)

The right hand side must be equal to
|g\rangle
following the eigenvalue equation of
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(t)
. This gives
\gamma_{2}^{\lambda}(0)=0
and
\delta_{1}^{\lambda}(0)=1
. This condition gives
\xi^{2}(0)=1
. As
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(t)
is Hermitian, we can easily conclude
\gamma_{1}^{\lambda}(0)=0
. Correctly multiplying constants and combining the differential equations from eq.(s)(
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
), we arrive at a differential equation in
\xi
as

\begin{split}\frac{\ddot{\xi}}{\xi}+\frac{\dot{\xi}^{2}}{\xi^{2}}+4\Omega^{2}=\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\left(\frac{\Theta^{2}}{2}+C|\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}|^{2}\right)~.\end{split}

(48)

This is the well-known Ermakov-Pinney equation for the current-model system. One can obtain a standard solution after a little-bit of rearrangement of the above equation as

\xi^{2}(t)=\mathcal{B}_{1}\cos 2\Omega t+\mathcal{B}_{2}\sin 2\Omega t+\frac{\Theta^{2}+2C|\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}|^{2}}{4\Omega^{2}}

(49)

which has striking similarities with eq.(
23
). Now
\delta_{1}^{\lambda}=\xi^{2}(t)
and from eq.(
43
) with the initial conditions for
\gamma_{1}^{\lambda}
and
\gamma_{2}^{\lambda}
, we find out that
\dot{\delta}_{1}^{\lambda}(0)=0
. Finally, we can write down the analytical expression for
\xi^{2}(t)
as

\xi^{2}(t)=\frac{(2-C)|\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}|^{2}}{2\Omega^{2}}\cos 2\Omega t+\frac{\Theta^{2}+2C|\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}|^{2}}{4\Omega^{2}}~.

(50)

Similarly the other coefficient
\gamma_{1}^{\lambda}
reads

\begin{split}\gamma_{1}^{\lambda}(t)&=\frac{(E_{e}-E_{g}-\omega_{0})}{2\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}}e^{i\omega_{0}t}(\xi^{2}(t)-1)-\frac{i\xi(t)\dot{\xi}(t)e^{i\omega_{0}t}}{\alpha_{0}\Lambda_{eg}}\end{split}

(51)

and
\gamma_{2}^{\lambda}(t)=\gamma_{1}^{\lambda^{*}}(t)
as
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(t)
is a Hermitian operator. The only discrepancy lies in the determination of the constant
C
. If
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(t)
can be expressed as an outer product of a pure state vector
|\phi_{I}(t)\rangle
then from the eigen-equation of the scaled invariant operator, we find out that
|\phi_{I}(t)\rangle=|\phi_{LR}\rangle
. Hence, the initial condition of the state vector
|\phi_{LR}\rangle
gives

to be unity. For this choice of the constant,
\mathcal{I}(t)
is identical to the density matrix obtained in the earlier subsection. In the current analysis, the time-derivative of the Lewis phase then reads

\dot{\theta}(t)=\langle\phi_{LR}(t)|i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\hat{H}_{R}(t)|\phi_{LR}(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{0}-E_{e}-E_{g})~.

(52)

Hence, the Lewis, phase is obtained as

\theta(t)=\frac{t}{2}(\omega_{0}-E_{e}-E_{g})

(53)

where the constant of integration is set to zero using the initial condition
\theta(0)=0
. This is a very important observation in our work. We find out that the rescaled invariant operator
\hat{\mathcal{I}}(t)
is identical to the density matrix of the system and solving the differential equations corresponding to the coefficients is identical to solving the Liouville equation involving the density matrix of the system. Finally, one important observation is that for Hamiltonian with periodicity, the Lewis phase is linearly dependent on time where the time derivative gives the overall phase for the state vector. Hence, Liouville equations are a special case of equation of motion involving the different class of invariant operators. With this interesting identification, we move towards solving a two-level system influenced by a time-dependent magnetic field.

III
Two-level system influenced by a time-dependent magnetic field

We consider a two-level system which is influenced by a constant magnetic field in the
z
-direction and a time-dependent magnetic field in the
x
-direction. The Hamiltonian for such a model is given as

\begin{split}H(t)&=-\mu B_{z}(\sigma_{z}+f(t)\sigma_{x})\\
&=-E_{0}(\sigma_{z}+f(t)\sigma_{x})\end{split}

(54)

where
\mu B_{z}=E_{0}
and

f(t)=\left\{{\begin{matrix}f_{0}~~t<\frac{T}{2}\\
-f_{0}~~t\geq\frac{T}{2}\end{matrix}}\right.

(55)

with
f_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The periodic square pulse
f(t)
can be expressed as

f(t)=f_{0}\Theta\left(\frac{T}{2}-t\right)-f_{0}\Theta\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)-f_{0}\delta_{t,\frac{T}{2}}

(56)

with
\delta_{t,\frac{T}{2}}
denoting Kronecker-delta. We consider the density matrix of the system as

\rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}(t)&&\rho_{01}(t)\\
\rho_{10}(t)&&\rho_{11}(t)\end{pmatrix}~.

(57)

As before, one can obtain four Liouville equations of motion as

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{00}(t)

\displaystyle=-if(t)E_{0}(\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t))

(58)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{11}(t)

\displaystyle=if(t)E_{0}(\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t))

(59)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)

\displaystyle=-if(t)E_{0}(\rho_{00}(t)-\rho_{11}(t))+2iE_{0}\rho_{01}(t)

(60)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)

\displaystyle=if(t)E_{0}(\rho_{00}(t)-\rho_{11}(t))-2iE_{0}\rho_{10}(t)~.

(61)

As before, from the first two equations, we obtain
\rho_{00}=1-\rho_{11}
. We again consider the state to be purely in the ground state such that
\rho_{00}(0)=1
and
\rho_{01}(0)=\rho_{10}(0)=\rho_{11}(0)=0
. Multiplying eq.(s)(
60
,
61
) with
f(t)
and summing them, we obtain a new equation of the form

\begin{split}f(t)\left(\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)+\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)\right)=&2if(t)E_{0}(\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t))\\
=&-2\dot{\rho}_{00}(t)\\
\implies\dot{\rho}_{00}(t)=&-\frac{f(t)}{2}\left(\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)+\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)\right)\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(f(t)(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t))\right)\\
&+\frac{\dot{f}(t)}{2}(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t))\end{split}

(62)

where in the second line of the above equation, we have made use of eq.(
58
). From eq.(
56
), we obtain that
\dot{f}(t)=-2f_{0}\delta\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right)
. Substituting the expression of
\dot{f}(t)
in the last line of eq.(
62
) and executing the time integral from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\begin{split}\rho_{00}(t)=&\rho_{00}(0)-\frac{f(t)}{2}\left(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t)\right)\\
&-f_{0}\Theta(t-\frac{T}{2})\left(\rho_{01}(T/2)+\rho_{10}(T/2)\right)~.\end{split}

(63)

At
t=T/2
, we can recast the above equation using the analytical form of
f(t)
in eq.(
56
) as

\begin{split}\rho_{00}(T/2)=&1+\frac{f_{0}}{2}\left(\rho_{01}(T/2)+\rho_{10}(T/2)\right)\\
&-f_{0}\Theta(0)\left(\rho_{01}(T/2)+\rho_{10}(T/2)\right)\end{split}

(64)

where we have made use of the initial condition
\rho_{00}(0)=1
. Now using the value of the Heaviside-theta function at
t=T/2
,
\Theta(0)=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\rho_{00}(T/2)=1~.

(65)

As the trace of the density matrix is always unity, we obtain
\rho_{11}(T/2)=0
. We now make a claim that at
t=T/2
the state remains pure and as a result
\rho^{2}=\rho
. A careful calculation reveals that in such a scenario
|\rho_{01}(T/2)|=0
. We can therefore write down the following condition at
t=T/2
for the density matrix as
\rho_{00}(T/2)=1
and
\rho_{11}(T/2)=\rho_{01}(T/2)=\rho_{10}(T/2)=0
. Using these values back in eq.(
63
), we obtain

\begin{split}\rho_{00}(t)&=1-\frac{f(t)}{2}\left(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t)\right)\\
\implies 2(1-\rho_{00}(t))&=f(t)\left(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t)\right).\end{split}

(66)

Subtracting eq.(
61
) from eq.(
60
) and multiplying both sides by
-if(t)E_{0}
, we obtain a new equation of the form

\begin{split}-if(t)E_{0}\left(\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)-\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)\right)=&-2f^{2}(t)E_{0}^{2}(\rho_{00}(t)-\rho_{11}(t))\\
&+2f(t)E_{0}^{2}(\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t))~.\end{split}

(67)

The left hand side of the above equation can be expressed as

\begin{split}-ifE_{0}\left(\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)-\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)\right)&=\frac{d}{dt}\left[-ifE_{0}\left(\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t)\right)\right]\\
&+i\dot{f}E_{0}\left(\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t)\right)\\
&=\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)-\frac{i\dot{f}(t)}{f(t)}\dot{\rho}_{00}(t)\\
&=\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)+\frac{2if_{0}\delta\left[t-\frac{T}{2}\right]}{f\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)}\dot{\rho}_{00}\left[\frac{T}{2}\right]\\
&=\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)-2i~\delta\left[t-\frac{T}{2}\right]\dot{\rho}_{00}\left[\frac{T}{2}\right]\end{split}

(68)

where we have made use of eq.(s)(
58
) in the second line of the above equation and eq.(
56
) in the final two lines of the above equation. Using the values of the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix at
t=\frac{T}{2}
, we obtain from eq.(
56

\dot{\rho}_{00}\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)=0
. Substituting this value back in eq.(
68

-if(t)E_{0}\left(\dot{\rho}_{01}(t)-\dot{\rho}_{10}(t)\right)=\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)~.

(69)

Making use of the above expression along with eq.(
66
) in eq.(
67

\rho_{00}(t)
as

\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)+4E_{0}^{2}(1+f^{2}(t))\rho_{00}(t)=2E_{0}^{2}(2+f^{2}(t))~.

(70)

The above equation is very elegant in a sense that for
t<\frac{T}{2}
and
t>\frac{T}{2}
, eq.(

) reduces to the same structure given as

\ddot{\rho}_{00}(t)+4E_{0}^{2}(1+f_{0}^{2})\rho_{00}(t)=2E_{0}^{2}(2+f_{0}^{2})~.

(71)

For
t<\frac{T}{2}
, we obtain a solution for the above equation as

\rho_{00}(t)=\mathcal{A}_{f_{0}}\cos\left(2\mathcal{E}_{0}t\right)+\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{f_{0}}\sin\left(2\mathcal{E}_{0}t\right)+\frac{2+f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}

(72)

where
\mathcal{E}_{0}\equiv E_{0}\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}
. At
t=0
,
\rho_{00}(0)=1
and
\dot{\rho}_{00}=0
. These initial conditions, help us to completely determine the anlytical form of
\rho_{00}(t)
for
t<\frac{T}{2}
as

\rho_{00}(t)=\frac{f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\cos(2\mathcal{E}_{0}t)+\frac{2+f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}~.

(73)

Similarly for
t>\frac{T}{2}
, and making use of the initial conditions
\rho_{00}(T/2)=1
and
\dot{\rho}_{00}(T/2)=0
, one can obtain the solution of the equation in eq.(
71
) as

\rho_{00}(t)=\frac{f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\cos(2\mathcal{E}_{0}t)+\frac{2+f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}~.

(74)

Now, substituting
t=\frac{T}{2}
in the above equation and also using
\rho_{00}\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)
, we obtain the condition for periodicity to be

T=\frac{2N\pi}{E_{0}\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}

(75)

where
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. As
\rho_{11}(t)
is related to
\rho_{00}(t)
via the relation
\rho_{11}(t)=1-\rho_{00}(t)
, we can obtain the analytical form of the density matrix at all times as

\displaystyle\rho_{00}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\cos(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}})+\frac{2+f_{0}^{2}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}

(76)

\displaystyle\rho_{11}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}^{2}}{1+f_{0}^{2}}\sin^{2}(E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}})~.

(77)

We now need to obtain the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix for which we need to make use of eq.(s)(
58
,
66
). For
t<\frac{T}{2}
and using the solution of
\rho_{00}(t)
from eq.(
76
), we can recast eq.(s)(

\displaystyle\rho_{01}(t)-\rho_{10}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{if_{0}}{\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}\sin\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)

(78)

\displaystyle\rho_{01}(t)+\rho_{10}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}}{1+f_{0}^{2}}\left(1-\cos\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)~.

(79)

For
t<T/2
, we can then obtain the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix as

\displaystyle\rho_{01}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\left(1-\cos\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)-\frac{if_{0}}{2\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}\sin\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle\rho_{10}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\left(1-\cos\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{if_{0}}{2\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}\sin\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)~.

(81)

Similarly for
t>\frac{T}{2}

\displaystyle\rho_{01}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{f_{0}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\left(1-\cos\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)+\frac{if_{0}}{2\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}\sin\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle\rho_{10}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{f_{0}}{2(1+f_{0}^{2})}\left(1-\cos\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)\right)-\frac{if_{0}}{2\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}}\sin\left(2E_{0}t\sqrt{1+f_{0}^{2}}\right)~.

(83)

Again, using the condition
\rho_{01}(T/2)=\rho_{10}(T/2)=0
we get back the same periodicity condition obtained earlier in eq.(
75
). One important thing to observe from eq.(s)(

) that the off-diagonal elements change there respective sign when the time crosses

. This is solely due to the form of the square pulse used in eq.(

) where
f(t)
goes from

for

to
-f_{0}
for

. The periodicity condition implies that at

the density matrix represents a pure state
\rho=\begin{pmatrix}1&&0\\

. In order to extract out the state of the system, we take a similar ansatz as before

|\phi\rangle=\kappa_{0}(t)|0\rangle+e^{i\zeta^{\prime}t}\kappa_{1}(t)|1\rangle~.

(84)

Executing
|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
and comparing it with the form of the density matrix, we obtain the state of the system in the two time regimes as

\displaystyle|\phi_{t<T/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\cos\left[E_{0}t\sqrt{f_{0}^{2}+1}\right]+\frac{i}{\sqrt{f_{0}^{2}+1}}\sin\left[E_{0}t\sqrt{f_{0}^{2}+1}\right]\right)|0\rangle+\frac{if_{0}}{\sqrt{f_{0}^{2}+1}}\sin\left[E_{0}t\sqrt{f_{0}^{2}+1}\right]|1\rangle

(85)

\displaystyle|\phi_{t>T/2}\rangle

(86)

), we have already identified the Lewis Invariant operator with the density matrix of the system and as a result, we can calculate the time derivative of the Lewis phase just by calculating

III.1.2
Frobenius-norm measure of coherence

We shall now proceed towards calculating the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence for the density matrix obtained in section (
III
). The Frobenius-norm measure of coherence is given by
FrobeniusMeasure

\begin{split}C_{F}(\rho)=\sqrt{\frac{d}{d-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\left(\lambda_{i}-\frac{1}{d}\right)^{2}}\end{split}

(90)

where
\lambda_{i}
denotes the eigenvalues of the density matrix. In our current analysis
d=2
, and the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence reduces to the standard form

C_{F}(\rho)=\sqrt{2\left(\left(\lambda_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right)}~.

(91)

For a density matrix, the eigenvalues read

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+|\rho_{01}|^{2}-\rho_{00}\rho_{11}}

(92)

where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{-}
. Substituting the eigenvalues in eq.(
91
), we obtain the analytical form of Frobenius-norm measure of coherence as

C_{F}(\rho)=\sqrt{1+4|\rho_{01}|^{2}-4\rho_{00}\rho_{11}}~.

(93)

For both
t<T/2
and
t>T/2
,
|\rho_{01}|^{2}
has the same analytical structure as can be noted by observing the analytical forms of the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix in these two time sectors from eq.(s)(
80
,
81
,
82
,
83
). It is easy to check that
|\rho_{01}|^{2}-\rho_{00}\rho_{11}=0
from the analytical form of the density matrix. Hence, the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence is

C_{F}(\rho)=1~.

(94)

The above result indeed is very interesting. The unity value of the Frobenius norm measure indicates that the system has geometrically the maximum distance from the maximally mixed state. Hence, the state is always coherent from a geometrical point of view. The primary observation lies in the fact that the
l_{1}
-norm measure of coherence is time-dependent whereas the Frobenius-norm measure of coherence is time-independent. This implies that the states are fully coherenet from a geometric point of view which also coincides with the outcome that the density matrix is separable for all values of
t
. Here,
l_{1}
-norm coherence measure indeed does not capture the full picture as the state is completely separable at
t=0,T/2,\frac{3T}{2}\cdots
, indicating that it is very away from the maximally mixed state which is the two dimensional identity matrix with appropriate numerical pre-factor. Hence, for such a scenario Frobenius-norm measure of coherence is more reliable than the
l_{1}
-norm measure of coherence.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we have considered interaction of two-level systems with time-dependent Hamiltonians which are periodic in time. We resort to a density matrix approach, where we aim to solve the Liouville equations of motion to obtain the analytical form of the density matrix. At first, we solve exactly the standard Rabi-oscilation model by solving the four independent differential equations corresponding to the four components of the density matrix. Making use of appropriate initial conditions and the Floquet theorem, we then obtain the state of the system. Next, we have made use of the Lewis-Riesenfeld invariant approach to solve the similar model. The interesting observation lies in the fact the Lewis invariant operator has the same analytical structure of the density matrix solved in the initial analysis. This gives us a very robust connection between the density marix as well as the Lewis invariant approach. We therefore conclude that the density matrix of a system is the same as the Lewis invariant operator for a system upto some constant factor and we also find out that the Lewis phase of the system for a Hamiltonian with periodicity is some constant multiplied by time. Finally, using this density matrix approach, we solve a two-level system influenced by a time-independent constant magnetic magnetic field in the
z
-direction and a time-dependent magnetic field in the
x
-direction. For a periodic square pulse, we observe a very interesting solution of the density matrix where the diagonal elements remain same over time and the off-diagonal elements pick up a negative sign when
t

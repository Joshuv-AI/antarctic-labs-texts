Novel supplementation of Fe3O4-doped green carbonized nanoparticles on hydrogenases genes and microbial biodiversity for enhancing biohydrogen yield in dark fermentation microbial electrohydrogenesis cells
Authors: Hikmatullah Ahmadi, Anam Jalil, Sohail Khan, Irfan Ali Phulpoto, Zhang Chengyu, Zhisheng Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAchieving high-purity biohydrogen (Bio-H₂) production necessitates the suppression of hydrogenotrophic methanogens, as their activity can impede hydrogen yield. Various inoculum pretreatments have been employed to suppress methane-producing microorganisms; however, these methods can negatively impact the enzymatic activity of hydrogen-producing microorganisms, thereby reducing hydrogen production. To address this challenge, this research investigates a novel approach to enhance Bio-H₂ production by activating microbial enzymes using magnetite Fe₃O4-doped carbonized nanoparticles (NPs) derived from vegetable leaves (VLCFe₃O4-NPs) within a coupled dark fermentation-microbial Electrohydrogenesis system. Characterization results revealed that VLCFe₃O4-NPs exhibited cubic and spherical morphologies, with a small diameter of 1 ± 100 nm and a mean crystallite size of 38.1 nm, indicating high purity. Fermentation tests investigated the impact of different nanoparticle dosages on Bio-H₂ generation, hydrogenase gene expression (Fe-Fe and Ni-Fe), and microbial biodiversity. Bio-H₂ production significantly improved with 500 mg/L VLCFe₃O4-NPs, yielding 1.2-fold more than the control group, while even a low dose of 25 mg/L resulted in a 0.22-fold increase. Relative gene expression analysis using qPCR and the 2-ΔΔCT method demonstrated a 30-fold increase in Cbei 1773 (Fe-Fe hydrogenase) and a 23-fold increase in hucL (Ni-Fe hydrogenase) gene expression, along with an increase in 16S rDNA. Additionally, the abundance of biohydrogen-producing bacteria, Clostridium_sensu_stricto_1 and Clostridium_sensu_stricto_11, increased by 14.3% and 11.1%, respectively, compared to 4.9% and 3.9% in the control group. This research indicates that VLCFe₃O4-NPs offer an eco-friendly solution for boosting biohydrogen production within microbial electrohydrogenesis cells with dark fermentation systems, thereby supporting sustainable bioenergy generation. One-sentence summary: Green carbonized nanoparticles Fe3O4-doped have been shown to turn on the genes of bacteria (Fe-Fe and Ne-Fe) and increase the biodiversity of microbes, both of which are important for biohydrogen production.Keywords: Biohydrogen, DF-MECs, Hydrogenase genes, Microbial biodiversity, VLCFe3O4 nanoparticles

Introduction

The rising demand for sustainable energy sources has led to interest in hydrogen (H2) as a renewable fuel with significant energy potential and minimum environmental impact. Hydrogen is distinguished by its elevated energy density, approximately 120–142 MJ/kg, which is substantially higher than that of conventional fossil fuels (Yang et al., 2022). Furthermore, H2 combustion produces only water vapor, making it an ideal alternative for reducing greenhouse gas emissions and addressing global energy needs. However, conventional hydrogen production methods, including steam reforming, coal gasification, and biomass pyrolysis, are energy-intensive and rely heavily on nonrenewable resources, thus limiting their environmental sustainability. Consequently, there is an upward need to develop alternative, eco-friendly hydrogen production methods that can yield high-purity hydrogen while reducing carbon footprints (Tariq et al., 2024).

Among the biological hydrogen production methods, using an anaerobic dark fermentation system is a viable approach for sustainable biohydrogen generation (Srivastava et al., 2023). Dark fermentation (DF) influences the metabolic activities of anaerobic microbes to convert organic substrates, such as biomass and organic waste, into hydrogen under anaerobic conditions (Zhang et al., 2022). DF not only facilitates clean energy production but also contributes to waste management by repurposing organic waste materials. When combined with microbial electrohydrogenesis, cells with dark fermentation (DF-MECs) shows the potential for enhancing hydrogen yields (YH2) (Lee et al., 2022).

The DF-MECs system operates by integrating microbial electrochemical processes that promote the conversion of organic matter to hydrogen while mitigating competition from hydrogen-consuming methanogens (Liu et al., 2016). The production of Bio-H2 involves facilitating electrochemical reactions by a series of enzymatic genes that play a significant role, including hydrogenase and nitrogenase. H2 gas is utilized and produced (H2 ± 2H+ + 2e) by various microorganisms, including bacteria, archaea, and eukaryotes, through the use of metalloenzymes known as hydrogenases and nitrogenase (Mackie et al., 2024). Nitrogenase and hydrogenase both possess three active sites, with nitrogenase comprising iron-sulfur, p-cluster, and FeMo cluster, and the iron-sulfur clusters playing the most significant role within the nitrogenase enzyme. Conversely, hydrogenase exhibits three active sites: [Ni-Fe], [Fe-Fe], and [Fe] hydrogenase (Mishra et al., 2019a). Nonetheless, dark fermentations with microbial electrohydrogenesis cells technique offers a scalable, reasonably priced method for producing Bio-H2, which is an industry objective for sustainable and clean energy production.

The efficiency of Bio-H₂ production in DF-MECs largely depends on the activity of hydrogenase enzymes, particularly Fe-Fe and Ni-Fe hydrogenases, which play essential roles in the electrochemical reactions required for hydrogen synthesis (Mishra et al., 2019a). Fe-Fe hydrogenases, predominantly found in anaerobic bacteria such as Clostridium species, are known for their high catalytic rates, often surpassing those of Ni-Fe hydrogenases (Abdul Aziz et al., 2022). These enzymes use a unique “H-cluster” cofactor at their active sites, facilitating rapid electron transfer that enhances the hydrogen production rate (HPR) (Søndergaard et al., 2016). Generally, Ni-Fe hydrogenases are widely distributed across different microbial species and are versatile in facilitating electron transfer across various environments (Tian et al., 2023). Fe-Fe and Ni-Fe hydrogenases contribute significantly to Bio-H₂ production by activating essential metabolic pathways (Wang et al., 2022). The pyruvate ferredoxin oxidoreductase pathway primarily converts glucose into pyruvate and then acetyl-CoA, which can further convert into ethanol, acetate, or butyryl-CoA, leading to butyrate formation (Chen et al., 2024). During the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA, hydrogenase plays a crucial role in this route, helping to produce hydrogen. To increase the H2 output and purity of hydrogen while suppressing hydrogenotrophic methanogens, different pretreatment methods have been used, such as physical, chemical, and biological. Due to pretreatment's metabolic rates and H2-producing microorganism growth being slow, various innovative methods have been explored to activate the Hydrogenase enzymes responsible for Bio-H2 production, enhance H2-producing microorganism growth, and facilitate intracellular electron transfer, thereby increasing H2 yield (Thu Ha Tran & Khanh Thinh Nguyen, 2022). These methods include alternative metabolic pathways, optimizing operational parameters, genetic engineering techniques, co-culture systems design, nutrient supplementation, trace metals, and substrate modification (Montserrat-Mesquida et al., 2024). Thus, their optimal functioning is complex for maximizing hydrogen output in DF-MEC systems​.

Recent research highlights the potential of trace metals and nanoparticles, such as metallic (Ni, Fe, Pb, Cu, MgCl2, SiO2, FeSO4, ZnFe2O4, and Ni/Fe2O3), and leaves, root, agricultures waste nanoparticles to boost biohydrogen production by enhancing microbial electroactivity and hydrogenase activity (Mishra et al., 2019b; Rambabu et al., 2021b). Nanomaterials constitute a distinctive category of valuable substances, notable for their small size, large specific surface area, and unique chemical properties. These nanomaterials function as carriers that enrich microbes, facilitate biofilm formation, enhance intracellular secretion, and promote organic nutrient absorption, thereby augmenting microbe growth (Singla et al., 2021). Iron oxide nanoparticles have garnered significant attention due to their distinctive ability to enhance hydrogen production through the oxidative reduction of ferredoxin. Iron oxide (Fe3O4) promotes the development of electronic conductor chains, improving electronic transport capacity in mixed bacterial cultures and increasing co-enzyme activity. Moreover, iron oxide corrosion produces Fe2+ as a byproduct, elevating the concentration of soluble microbial products and facilitating bacterial proliferation (Sait Izgi et al., 2020). Fe3O4 provides iron for the active site of the hydrogenase cluster and increases ferredoxin oxidoreductase activity. They demonstrate surface and quantum size effects, significant electron affinity, and enhanced electron transport from NADPH to hydrogenase. The addition of green synthesized nanoparticles (NPs) to the dark fermentative hydrogen production system has been proven to improve microbial canalization, which has increased hydrogen production potential (Zhang et al., 2021). Nevertheless, there are limited literature findings regarding the impacts of green-synthesized nanoparticles (Nasr et al., 2015; Patel et al., 2018). According to the findings of many investigations, nanoparticles can boost biohydrogen generation in anaerobic digestion by (i) increasing the amount of electron transport inside the cell, (ii) promoting the synthesis of hydrogen enzymes, and (iii) encouraging preferential hydrogen producers within the microbial consortium (Martinez-Burgos et al., 2021). By providing essential trace metals as cofactors for hydrogenase enzymes, these nanoparticles support electron transfer efficiency and stabilize the microbial consortia needed for robust hydrogen production​.

Based on this information, the current study explores a novel green synthesis of Fe3O4-doped carbonized nanoparticles derived from vegetable leaves (spinach and cabbage) (VLCFe3O4-NPs) for application in DF-MEC systems. Using plant-based waste for nanoparticle synthesis aligns with green chemistry principles, offering an environmentally friendly and cost-effective approach that reduces production expenses and ecological impact. This study aims to evaluate the effects of VLCFe3O4-NPs on hydrogenase gene expression, particularly the Fe-Fe and Ni-Fe types, and on microbial community dynamics, targeting enzymatic and microbial pathways to enhance Bio-H₂ yield. Through these green-synthesized nanoparticles, this research seeks to provide a scalable, sustainable solution for biohydrogen production with significant industrial implications, advancing the integration of nanotechnology into renewable energy systems for a low-carbon future.

Materials and Methods

Seed Sludge Composition

A mixed bacterial culture was acquired from the sludge of an anaerobic digester at the Huairou City sewage treatment facility in Beijing in April 2024. The collected sludge was stored at −4°C in a 5-L plastic barrel. The sludge was subjected to preheating at 90°C for 20 min using a hot plate to eradicate H2-consuming and CH4-producing bacteria (Jung et al., 2011). Table 1 shows the detailed properties of seed sludge. The experimental setup was designed using 250 mL Duran reagent bottles equipped with butyl rubber screw caps, each containing a working volume of 200 mL. The bottles were inoculated with 10 mL of treated activated sludge and supplemented with a 2% glucose and rich modified mineral salts medium was also included, with the following composition: 2 g·L−1 NH4Cl, 0.6 g·L−1 KH₂PO4, 0.6 g·L−1 K₂HPO4, 0.50 g·L−1 MgCl₂, 0.2 g·L−1 CaCl₂, 6 g·L−1 MgSO4, 0.02 g·L−1 CuSO4, 0.2 g·L−1 FeSO4, 0.02 g·L−1 Na₂MoO4, and 6.90 g·L−1 NaHCO₃ (Phulpoto et al., 2024). After that, the medium was purged with N2 gas that was 99.9% pure to create an anaerobic environment. it was then covered with Aluminium foil to protect it from light for dark fermentation. This was done before the bottle was placed in a shaker at 120 rpm and then incubated at 37°C in the dark for 48 hr.

Nanoparticle Synthesis

The innovative Magnetite iron oxide-doped carbon nanoparticle synthesis utilizes vegetable leaf waste (spinach and cabbage) for eco-friendly and sustainable bio-H2 production. Initially, the vegetable leaf waste was sourced from a local vegetable market in Beijing's Huairou City. The vegetable leaf was thoroughly cleaned and dried in an oven at 100°C for 6 hr. It was subsequently crushed to a 2–3 mm mesh size before being carbonized at 400°C for 2 hr in SNOL High-Temperature Ceramic Chamber Furnaces and cooled to room temperature. The carbonized nanoparticle was then activated using 1 M NaOH at a ratio of 5:1 (NaOH/NPs) and subjected to magnetic rotation at room temperature for 12 hr. Following this, the activated nanoparticle was washed with deionized water until it reached a neutral pH (7.0 ± 0.2) and dried at 100°C for 6 hr. In the final stage of production of Magnetite iron oxide-doped nanoparticles, Fe3O4 (0.1 Mol/g of NPs) was dissolved in deionized water (100 mL) and heated to 100°C in a magnetic stirrer for metal oxide doping nanoparticles until the water completely evaporated. This resulted in the synthesis of VLCFe3O4-NPs were stored at room temperature for future use.

Characteristics of Nanoparticles

Numerous studies indicate that multiple physical and chemical characteristics are closely connected with the properties of materials. To determine the chemical composition and morphology of VLCFe3O4-NPs, a scanning electron microscope (Germany ZEISS Gemini SEM 300) equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy EDS (Japan Shimadzu IRSpirit) was utilized. To verify the crystalline structure of VLCFe3O4-NPs, X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were conducted using a Bruker X-ray diffractometer (Japan Rigaku SmartLab SE) with Cu-Ka radiation in the nickel-filtered mode. The XRD curves were recorded at a 2-theta scale ranging from 5° to 90° with an accelerating voltage of 40 kV and a current of 200 mA. The surface area was determined using the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET—USA Micromeritics ASAP 2460) method, while the pore size distribution was estimated through the Barrett-Joyner-Halenda adsorption branch method. The functional groups of NPs were identified using a Fourier infrared spectrometer (FTIR—Japan Shimadzu IRSpirit) in the range of 4000–400 cm−1. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopic (XPS) analysis was performed using the KRATOS Axis Ultra instrument (XRD, Bruker D8 Advance, Germany) equipped with an AlKa radiation source, which was operated at a power of 200 W and featured a hemispherical electron energy analyzer. The morphology of the surface and particle size was investigated using a microscope and transmission electron microscopy (TEM—Japan JEOL JEM F200), which was conducted on a Tecnai G2 F30 S-TWIN electron microscope with an accelerating voltage of 200 kV. The particle size distribution of VLCFe3O4-NPs was determined using TEM micrographs analyzed with the image processing software (ImageJ 1.52v), while the particle size was calculated using Origin Pro 2021.

Experimental Design

Batch experiments were conducted in DF-MEC reactors with 250 mL volume. The reactor was designed and constructed in a cylindrical shape using acrylic material, resulting in a single-chamber reactor with a working volume of 200 mL. The anode and cathode were composed of carbon Fiber, with a 2.2 cm diameter and 8 cm height, and during the experiment, an external voltage of 0.8 V was applied using a DC Power Supply (GPE-4323 C, Beijing Good Will Instrument Co., Ltd, China). For gas collection, a 250 mL gas bag was employed to measure the H2 content in biogas through Gas Chromatography (GC, GOW-MAC series 580), utilizing a thermal conductivity detector and a stainless-steel column (1.8 m × 3.2 mm). To ensure dark fermentation conditions, the reactor was covered with aluminum foil, and the substrate components were maintained at a constant level of 10 g/L glucose. Each sample was filled with a mixture consisting of 90% deionized water and 10% heat-treated seed sludge. The primary objective of this research was to evaluate the influence of varying doses of nanoparticles on Bio-H2 yield, microbial community structure, and hydrogenase genes (Ni-Fe and Fe-Fe) in DF-MECs. To achieve this, six different concentrations of VLCFe3O4-NPs (25, 50, 100, 250 500, and 750 mg/L) and a control group without VLCFe3O4-NPs were utilized. The reactors were agitated in an incubator at 120 rpm under mesophilic conditions (37 ± 2°C) for 48 hr, with the pH maintained at 7.0 ± 0.2 using 1 M HCl and 1 M KOH solution. Anaerobic conditions were ensured by purging the coupled dark fermentation microbial Electrohydrogenesis cells reactor with N2 gas (99.9% purity) for approximately 3 min. Results and Discussion

Characterization of Nanoparticles

The morphology, mineral composition crystal structure, specific surface area, elemental valence, and function group of VLCFe3O4-NPs were characterized by SEM, EDX, XRD, BET, XPS, FTIR, and TEM for revealing the mechanism on the DF-MEC system mediated by VLCFe3O4-NPs which are shown in Fig. 1. EDS analysis demonstrated in Fig. 1a that the nanoparticles mostly included carbon, oxygen, and iron, constituting 42.57%, 38.97%, and 18.46% by weight, respectively. The analysis indicated the presence of iron (Fe) in the NPs, which attract bacterial cells (Farghali et al., 2019). Carbon serves as a nutrient source for bacterial growth and likely contributes to hydrogen fermentation by providing ferrous elements and enhancing electron transfer. Additionally, these nanoparticles can penetrate the cell membranes (Emerson, 2018). The SEM image in Fig. 1b indicates the synthesized co-precipitation of VLCFe3O4-nanoparticles predominantly formed cubic and regular smooth shapes with distinct crystal boundaries and increased the specific surface area of AC by Fe3O4, which facilitated microbial colonization (El Aidy et al., 2013). The crystalline phase of the VLCFe3O4-NPs samples, XRD analysis in Fig. 1c. Although the diffraction peaks that were seen for the myco-synthesized NPs displayed increase, this could be because the crystallite size was quite tiny. The occurrence of this phenomenon suggested that the product possessed a higher level of phase purity. The cubic structure of VLCFe3O4 was used as an index for all of the diffraction peaks that were associated with Fe3O4 (JCPDS no. 19–629). The diffractograms obtained for the VLCFe3O4-NPs samples exhibited a series of peaks at 2θ angles of 18.2°, 20.74°, 26.52°, 28.24°, 30.06°, 35.42°, 39.38°, 40.4°, 43.08°, 53.48°, 57.02°, and 62.62°, corresponding to the (011), (101), (111), (210), (240), (220), (222), (440), (400), (422), (511), and (404) planes, respectively (Astuti et al., 2023; Peighambardoust et al., 2021). The structure of pure Fe3O4 (magnetite) was discovered to be the predominant phase in the mesoporous nano-adsorbent that was synthesized with an inverted spinel structure (Abay et al., 2022; Verma et al., 2019). The peaks that were produced at 18.2° and 35.42° revealed this structure. In a prior study on the environmentally friendly synthesis of magnetite (NP), these peaks correlate to those that were found at 18.2° and 35.42° (Yew et al., 2016). The Debye-Scherrer equation was utilized to determine the average crystallographic size (D) of the magnetite nanoparticles. According to the results of the calculation, the mean crystallite size was 38.96 nm, which indicates that the nanoparticle had a crystalline nature that fell somewhere between 28 and 53 nm. It has been observed by other researchers that nanoparticles have a D value that ranges from 13 to 69 nm, which lends credence to the interaction that occurs between the magnetite nanoparticle and the granular activated carbon (Oroujizad et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 1d, the diagrams of the BET approach depict the specific surface area, pore size distribution, and velum distribution of the VLCFe3O4-NPs. The VLCFe3O4-NPs exhibited a specific surface area of 2.81 m²/g, with Total Volume in Pores 0.018 cm³/g and a pore size range of 0.0012 cm³/g nm and an average pore size of 26.31 nm. The N2 adsorption-desorption isotherms classify VLCFe3O4-NPs as a type IV microporous material, as evidenced by the BET results. The overlapping adsorption and desorption curves demonstrated f relative pressure (P/P0) of 0.58–1.0, which were attributed to capillary coalescence and condensation at varying pressures, thereby facilitating electron absorption and microbial interactions (Dong et al., 2020). The nanoscale dimensions and absence of impurities in NPs enhance their accessibility to HPB, consequently promoting biohydrogen production (Bosu & Rajamohan, 2022). Fourier-transform infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) characteristics of the VLC NPs samples before and after Fe3O4 doping are shown in Fig. 1g. The absorption peaks of the VLC NPs samples near 3205 cm−1 are attributed to the O-H telescopic vibration modes, which are broader in shape and are mainly related to the O-H structures of hydrogen-bonded water molecules, alcohols, and phenols. The absorption peak at 2918 cm−1 is attributed to the symmetric/asymmetric stretching vibration modes of methyl and methylene groups, which mainly originate from the aliphatic and cycloalkane structures in the material. The broad peak at 1617 cm−1 is associated with the benzene ring C=C stretching vibration mode and also contains carbonyl C=O stretching vibration mode. The absorption peak at 1416 cm−1 of the sample has a more complex composition and is mainly associated with methyl and methylene bending vibrations, carboxylate C=O symmetric stretching vibrations and other structures. The absorption peaks near 1040 cm−1 are mainly associated with C-O stretching vibrations of phenols and alcohols, and also contain alkane-CH3 wobble vibrations, alkane straight-chain C-C stretching vibrations, and C-H in-plane bending vibrations of aromatic compounds. The rest of the fingerprint regions below 1000 cm−1 mainly correspond to low-frequency vibrational characteristic peaks in the samples, resulting from a combination of multiple motions such as the twisting and wiggling of multiple chemical bonds (Reddy & Kim, 2020). After Fe3O4 loading, it can be observed that the peak shape of the absorption peak of the sample at 3205 cm−1 is shifted, and the splitting and intensity change of the absorption peak at 1416 cm−1 may be because oxygen-containing groups such as hydroxyl and carbon-oxygen double bonds in the material can generate a large number of lone pairs of electrons, which can be complexed and coordinated to Fe or undergo ion-exchange, and thus the characteristic peaks are changed. In addition, the sample shows the characteristic absorption peak of the Fe-O structure at 584 cm−1. The above results indicate that Fe3O4 has been loaded on the surface of the material, affecting the polar group characteristics (Al-Karagoly et al., 2022).

Cumulative Bio–hydrogen Production

The study investigates the impact of different concentrations of VLCFe3O4 nanoparticles on glucose consumption for Bio-H2 production under batch processing conditions. The results, illustrated in Fig. 2a, b, demonstrate that the addition of VLCFe3O4-NPs significantly enhanced both Bio-H2 yield and the HPR in DF-coupled microbial electrohydrogenesis cells. The findings revealed the addition of 500 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs resulted in the highest bio-H2 yield, at 375.33 mL/g glucose, and a maximum H2 production rate of 19.83 mL/g glucose/h which is 118.023% higher than control group (172.7 mL H2/g glucose). The trend indicated that increasing the VLCFe3O4-NPs concentration from 25 to 500 mg/L increases HPR and yields. The addition of a small amount of 25 mg/L of VLCFe3O4-NPs increased the bio-H2 yield by 22.093% compared to the control. The current study demonstrates that the bio-H2 yield enhancement resulting from NPs is comparable to the reported enhancement ranges in previous literature using various types of nanoparticles (see Table 4), Ye and his colleagues have pointed out increasing bio-H2 yield by 21.8% with NiFe2O4-NPs (Ye et al., 2024), 61% with Ag-NPs (Subramani et al., 2024), Fe3O4 nanoparticles with 62.2% (Rambabu et al., 2021a), nickel doped lanthanum-iron oxide nanoparticles 49.9% (Ji et al., 2024). Consequently, nanoparticles are chiefly responsible for the elevation of the NADH/NAD + ratio and hydrogenase activity, which directly enhances the NADH-dependent bio-H2 production pathway. This improvement is also due to effective extracellular electron transfer, facilitating the export of electrons from within the cell to extracellular ions (Cheng et al., 2020). However, increasing the concentration of VLCFe3O4-NPs to 500 and 750 mg/L resulted in a decline in bio-H2 yield from 375.33 to 193.12 mL/g glucose, respectively. The decline in bio-H2 production may be ascribed to the potential toxicity and diminished hydrogenase activity caused by the substantial presence of nanosized metals (Fe) in nanoparticles. Elevated concentrations of metal derivatives, including metals and their oxides, have been reported to induce toxicity and generate free radicals, thereby inhibiting microbial growth and diminishing bio-H2 production (Alian et al., 2021). These findings were further confirmed by the results of the microbial structure analysis in Section 3.3. The bio-H2 yield from added nanoparticles was higher than the control, the magnetic iron nanoparticles which can activate the genes responsible for H2 production, and bacteria using carbon as source food for their metabolic pathway and growth for H2 production during the 48-hr fermentation process, the cumulative hydrogen production of all reactors with NPs increased rapidly from the 6th hour to the 30th hour for the control group from 6 to 24 h. In microbial electrohydrogenesis cells, dark glucose fermentation produces bio-H₂ along with associated volatile fatty acids (VFAs) such as acetate, propionate, butyrate, and ethanol-soluble microbial products (SMPs), as shown in Fig. 2c. In previous studies, volatile fatty acids affect biohydrogen generation. However, in this study the pH slightly drops after 48 hr of fermentation, with glucose breakdown evident in all groups, indicating little impact on pH by volatile fatty acids. During fermentation, the concentration of VLCFe3O4-nanoparticles significantly increases VFAs production in the first 12 hr, subsequently leading to a slow decline as the VFAs are converted into biohydrogen and other by-products. The acetate concentration in the control group was 1096.2 mg/L, increasing to 1312.1 mg/L at a VLCFe3O4-NPs concentration of 25 mg/L, representing a 19.7% increase relative to the control group (p < .01). Additionally, the concentration of NPs at 500 mg/L peaked at 1612 mg/L. Butyrate, propionate, and ethanol concentrations increased from 191.3 225.1, and 615.6 in the control group to 341.3 525.1, and 1115.2 mg/L with an applied dosage of 500 mg/L NPs. It is typical for acetic acid to be associated with the fermentation of other acids, as the trade-off process of acetic acid creates an equivalent quantity of NADH+ and H+. On the other hand, the evolution of acetic acid development in the absence of reducing capacity would lead to a significant surplus of NADH+ H+. It has been established that the conversion of 1 mol of acetyl-CoA to 1 mol of butyrate acid imposes the utilization of 2 mol of NADH. Role VLCFe3O4-NPs on Microorganisms

The influence of nanoparticles (NPs) on microbial community composition during the batch experiment, employing high-throughput sequencing of the 16S rDNA in reactors with 500 and 750 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs and control group is illustrated in Fig. 3a at (genus level) and Fig. 3b at (phylum level). The sequencing produced between 65 770 and 139 567 correct sequence tags per sample, with coverage of 0.9999, signifying the high authenticity and reliability of the data (Li et al., 2018). At the phylum level, Firmicutes was the dominant phylum in all groups, surpassing 98% in both the control and nanoparticle fermentation samples. Firmicutes contained the primary hydrogen-producing bacteria that could utilize various substrates, such as glucose, cellulose, and sucrose, to generate H2. Firmicutes are capable of producing endospores under unfavorable conditions; nevertheless, other bacterial phyla have shown resilience to changes after heat preheat treatment (Venkata Mohan et al., 2011). Firmicutes are the primary component of the crucial hydrolytic, acidogenic, and acetic acid-generating bacteria in wastewater systems. Firmicutes during dark fermentation, they promote the reduction of Fe3þ to Fe2þ, hence augmenting biohydrogen generation (Zhong et al., 2020). The bacterial community composition at the genus level, encompassing Bacillus, Sporolactobacillus, Caloramator, Clostridium_sensu_stricto_1, Clostridium_sensu_stricto11, Clostridium_sensu_stricto_12, Clostridium_sensu_stricto_3, Caproiciproducens, Ruminococcaceae, Escherichia-Shigella and Acinetobacter, was identified in all bioreactor groups. In the control reactor, the dominant genus was: Bacillus (44.7%), Sporolactobacillus (27.1%), Clostridium_sensu_stricto_1 (4.9%), Clostridium_sensu_stricto11 (3.9%), Clostridium_sensu_stricto_12 (5.2%), and Clostridium_sensu_stricto_3 (4.01%). The genus was dominant in reactors with 500 mg/L NPs; however, the abundance of these with high bio-H2 production genera was higher than in the control reactor, 26.1%, 14.3%, 14. 6%, 11.1%, 9.1%, and 7.7%, respectively. These genera are recognized for their capacity to generate biohydrogen in anaerobic environments, with Clostridium notably linked to elevated biohydrogen production potential (Sim et al., 2022). This suggests that the incorporation of nanomaterials enhances the proliferation of bacteria often associated with biohydrogen production. These findings align with prior work indicating that nanoparticles enhanced the microbial population of bacteria involved in biohydrogen generation within the sludge (Shanmugam et al., 2020). At a concentration of 750 mg/L, the predominant groups were Bacillus (52.7%) and Sporolactobacillus (13.8%). High concentrations exert a beneficial influence on the biodiversity of Bacillus and Sporolactobacillus. The nanoparticles additionally enhance other bacterial genera, including Ruminococcaceae, Escherichia-Shigella, and Acinetobacter, which are recognized for their roles in electron and proton transfer, which is essential in microbial electrochemical systems (MEC). Microbial hydrogen production is enabled by hydrogenases, which catalyze the reversible reaction of 2H++2e−↔H2. Hydrogenases are categorized according to the metal site participating in their redox reactions (Khan et al., 2017). VLCFe3O4-NPs possess the capability to enhance microbial biodiversity and bacterial performance, particularly Clostridium maximizing biohydrogen production and ensuring system stability during anaerobic fermentation.

Effect of VLCFe3O4-NPs on Hydrogenase Gens

The expression of the hydrogenase genes may be an acceptable indicator of fermentative bio-H2 generation. This is because hydrogenase is the essential enzyme that is accountable for the production of bio-H2 during the fermentation process (Kareyeva et al., 2011; Roychowdhury & Ghosh, 2024). Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was used to measure the expression level of the hydrogenase gene Cbei 1773 (Fe-Fe) and hucL Group (Ni-Fe) during the fermentation of DF-MECs. This was done to determine the influence that VLCFe3O4-NPs had on hydrogenase activity. Both the amplification curve and the melt curve of the Cbei 1773 and hlydA2 are depicted in (Fig. 4a and b), respectively. An S-shaped pattern can be seen on the magnification curve, and the multipart aperture Ct values are almost equal. On the other hand, the melt curve displays a single peak that is devoid of any heterogeneous peaks. The magnification and melt curves of the Cbei 1773 and hucL genes demonstrated great efficiency of primer amplification and specificity of primer obligatory to the template. As shown in Fig. 4c, the expression of the hucL and Cbei 1773 genes was considerably higher in the presence of 500 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs than it was in the presence of 750 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs and the control group. During the fermentation of DF-MECs, the expression of the hucL gene was approximately 19-fold higher than that of 750 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs and 23-fold higher than that of the group that served as the control. This research reveals that the presence of 500 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs during anaerobic hydrogen fermentation can boost enzymatic activity with a 750 mg/L concentration of a small impact hucL. It was shown that the expression of the Cbei 1773 gene was much higher in the presence of 500 VLCFe3O4-NPs compared to the group that served as the control. More specifically, the expression of the Cbei 1773 gene was approximately 29-fold higher than that of 750 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs and 30-fold higher than that of the group that served as the control throughout the fermentation process of DF-MEC. The expression decreased as a result of the detrimental effect that Fe had on the membrane of the microbial cell when the dosage was increased to 750 mg/L. This was owing to the high concentration of a negative impact Cbei 1773, which has the potential to directly influence the amount of bio-H2 that is produced. Based on this discovery, it is clear that the presence of nanoparticles during anaerobic hydrogen fermentation has the potential to boost the activity of hucL-Group2a and Cbei 1773 hydrogenase reactions. In addition, this discovery lends credence to the direct connection that has been established between the expression of genes and the creation of biological hydrogen in earlier research (Verde-Yáñez et al., 2023). Based on these findings, it appears that elevating the concentration of nanoparticles that through fermentation can boost hydrogenase activity.

Mechanism of VLCFe3O4-NPs on Metabolic Pathway

The enhancement of biohydrogen production in the dark fermentation-microbial electrolysis cell (DF-MEC) system by VLCFe₃O4-nanoparticles is attributed to their crystal defects and the degradation induced by anisotropic iron-reducing bacteria, which collectively upregulate hydrogenase gene expression. As depicted in Fig. 5, the metabolic pathways involved in glucose (C6H₁₂O6) conversion into various fermentation products are illustrated, underscoring the roles of nanoparticles and metal ions (M⁺). Glucose undergoes glycolysis to produce pyruvate, generating 2 molecules of NADH and hydrogen ions (H⁺). The presence of nanoparticles enhances the electron transport chain and activates hydrogenase enzymes, facilitating the conversion of pyruvate into end products such as lactic acid, butyric acid, acetic acid, and ethanol. This process also yields hydrogen gas (H₂) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) as byproducts. The Fe³⁺ ions may gradually leach from Fe₃O4 into the fermentation medium, where they can be absorbed by transporter proteins or phospholipids on the microbial cell membrane. This absorption enhances both active and passive electro-transport processes, promoting the uptake and utilization of substrates like Fe³⁺ and carbon sources (e.g. glucose) derived from nanoparticles. The intracellular uptake of Fe³⁺ facilitates the synthesis of [Fe-Fe] and [Ni-Fe] hydrogenases in hydrogen-producing bacteria (HPB). The nanoparticles, immobilized on hydrogenase, enhance electron transfer due to their quantum size effect. Additionally, the carbonized residue from nanoparticles serves as a nutrient source for microorganisms, while the high surface area of VLCFe₃O4-NPs creates a favorable environment for bacterial growth. The increasing abundance of VLCFe₃O4-NPs contributes to the expansion of the dominant Firmicutes phylum in the microbial community. Functional gene prediction analysis indicates that Fe₃O4 enhances glycolytic activity and facilitates iron acquisition through hydrogenase production.

Conclusions

This study investigates the impact of Fe3O4-doped carbonized nanoparticles from vegetable leaves at different concentrations of 0, 25, 50 100, 200, 500, and 750 mg/L on biohydrogen production in the system dark fermentation-assisted microbial Electrohydrogenesis cell. The highest H2 yield achieved was 375.33 mL/g glucose at 500 mg/L VLCFe3O4-NPs, indicating a 118.023% increase relative to the control (172.7 mL/g glucose). At higher dosages of 750 mg/L, the HPR declines due to the adverse impact of high Fe on the microbial cell membrane. The deliverance of Fe3+ from VLCFe3O4-NPs augmented the genes critical for biohydrogen production and diversified microbial communities. The dominant phylum identified was Firmicutes, accounting for over 98% of the total microbial population across all reactors. Firmicutes played a critical role as the primary bacterial group responsible for reducing Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, thereby enhancing the activity of Fe-Fe and Ni-Fe hydrogenases. On the other hand, fermentation produces bio-H2-associated acetate, propionate, butyrate, and ethanol-soluble microbial products (SMPs). VLCFe3O4-NPs markedly enhance the production of SMPs in the first 12 hr, followed by a gradual decrease that converts SMPs into bio-H2. This study advances existing literature by demonstrating the feasibility of synthesizing unique nanoparticles from vegetable leaves, a technique previously explored with various plant sources in green synthesis research. This approach not only supports sustainable practices but also holds potential for industrial applications in biohydrogen production, where cost-effective and eco-friendly nanoparticle synthesis can enhance process efficiency.

Undecidability of infinite towers of Kummer extensions of 𝔽 p ​ ( t ) \mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
Carlos Martínez-Ranero

Abstract
We prove, assuming resolution of singularities in positive characteristic, an analogue of Siegel’s theorem on sum of squares in positive characteristic. The method of proof combines techniques from central simple algebras with model theory and builds on work of Anscombe, Dittmann and Fehm. As an application, we show that, for each finite field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} of odd characteristic and any positive integer n n coprime with the characteristic of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , the first-order theory of the field given by the compositum of the fields generated by adjoining the n n –th roots of all monic irreducible polynomials in 𝔽 ⁡ [ t ] \mathbb{F}[t] , of degree divisible by n n is undecidable in the language of rings with the variable t t as a constant. 1 1 1 The first named author was supported by Proyecto VRID-Investigación 220.015.024-INV The second named author was supported by Proyecto VRID 2022000419-INI

In

Nevertheless, a well-developed set of tools and techniques for proving the undecidability of the first-order theory of infinite algebraic extensions of

In this article, we investigate the analogous question in positive characteristic, with two primary goals: first, to construct new families of infinite algebraic extensions of

The Kochen ring

\mathfrak{p}
a prime of
K
and

an uniformizer. Let

\mathbb{Z}
. Let

\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
For a field extension
F/K

\mathfrak{P}
a prime of
F
above
\mathfrak{p}

e(\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}):=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}})
and the

We denote by

Remark 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=\emptyset
, then we set
R_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=F.

Let

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F):=\left\{\frac{a}{1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}b}:a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}{(\gamma)})],1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}b\neq 0\right\}

We shall show that the holomorphy ring above defined is equal to the integral closure of the Kochen ring. To do this, we need a few Lemmas. It is worth pointing out that most of the results of this section are well known in the case of formally
p

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F/K
be a field extension and let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}(F)
,
a\in F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)
be given. Let
\beta(x):=\frac{x^{q}-x}{(x^{q}-x)^{2}-1}
. Then

(i)

If
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)>0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\beta(a))=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)>0.

(ii)

If
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)<0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\beta(a))=-qv_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)>0.

(iii)

If
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)>0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\beta(a))=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)>0.

(iv)

If
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)=0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\beta(a))\leq 0.

Proof.

These are just straightforward computations.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F/K
be a field extension and let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
.
Then
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
if and only if
\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)=\emptyset
and
\gamma(a)\geq 0
for all
a\in F
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
be given. Let
a\in F
. Using
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
, we see that
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)\geq 0
for any
a
satisfying clauses (i)-(iii) of the previous Lemma. Now we show that
a
can not satisfy clause (iv) of Lemma
2.2
. Indeed, we deduce from
f(\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p})=1
that
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence,
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)>0
for any
a\in F
with
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)=0
. Thus
\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)=\emptyset
and
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)\geq 0,
for any
a\in F
.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
be given such that
\gamma(a)\geq 0
for all
a\in F.
First we show that
e(\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p})=1.
To see this, let
a\in F
be given with
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)>0
. We see, from clause (i) of Lemma
2.2
, that

0\leq v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma(a))=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}\beta(a))=-v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}})+v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)
. Hence,
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)\geq v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}})
for any
a\in F
. Since
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
has minimal positive value, we have
e(\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p})=1
. Next we show that
f(\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p})=1
. To show this, let
a\in F
with
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a)=0
. If
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)=0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma(a))=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}\beta(a))=-1+v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\beta(a))<0
(by clause (iv) of lemma
2.2
), which is a contradiction.

Thus,
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a^{q}-a)>0
. Since
a
was arbitrary we conclude that
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F/K
be a field extension. If
\mathfrak{P}
is a prime of
F
such that
t_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma))]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
, then
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of
F
as in the statement of the Lemma. First we show that
\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}},
we have that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap K
. On the other hand, let
a\in K\cap\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Using that
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a uniformizer of
\mathfrak{p}
we can find
u\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a=ut^{n}
. Notice that

since
t_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{P}
and thus in particular
t_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}\notin\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. We deduce that
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and hence
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap K=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}.
We next show that
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F).
Using lemma
2.3
, it is sufficient to show that
\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)=\emptyset
. Indeed, since
\gamma
is continuous (with respect to the topology induced by

) and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a closed subset of
F
, we have
\gamma^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}})=F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)

∎

We are now ready to prove the main Theorem of the section.

Theorem 2.5
.

The integral closure of the Kochen ring
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
is equal to
R_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
.

Proof.

Notice that if
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F)\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)]
, then
1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F)\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)]=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F)\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)]
. Thus,
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
is the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)].
Applying Merkel’s Lemma (see appendix in
[
PR84
]
), we obtain that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=F
.

Next suppose
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
is not unit in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F)\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma)]
. Define

\mathbb{V}:=\{\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}(F):\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}},\ \Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\cap\mathfrak{P}\ \rm{is\ a\ maximal\ ideal\ of}\ \Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\}.

Recall that the integral closure of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
is equal to
\bigcap\limits_{\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbb{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
(see
[
EP05
, Theorem 3.1.3]
). Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathbb{V}=\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{(1,1)}_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
be given. We deduce, from Lemma
2.3
, that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F)]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
and thus also
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
(since
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(t_{\mathfrak{p}})=1).
We claim that
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{P}\cap\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
is a maximal ideal. To verify this, notice that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)/\mathfrak{m}
is a field since it is a subring of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}.
Therefore
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal and thus
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbb{V}.

On the other hand, let
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbb{V}
be given. In view of the previous Lemma, it is sufficient to show that
t_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma))]\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. Observe that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma))]\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. We claim that all maximal ideals of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
contain
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Aiming towards a contradiction, suppose that there is a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
which does not contain
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We deduce that
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a unit modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. Thus, there is an
a\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
such that
at_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv 1(\bmod\,\mathfrak{m}).
Write
a:=\frac{b}{1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}c}
where
b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma))].
Then
1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}(c-b)\equiv 0(\bmod\,\mathfrak{m}).
Notice that
1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}(c-b)\neq 0
as
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
is not a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}(\gamma))]
by assumption. Therefore
1\equiv 0(\bmod\,\mathfrak{m})
which is a contradiction. Thus
t_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{m}=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)\cap\mathfrak{P}
. We conclude the proof of the Theorem.
∎

The
\mathfrak{p}
-Pythagoras number

We recall the definition of
\mathfrak{p}
-Pythagoras number introduced by S. Anscombe, P. Dittman and A. Fehm (see
[
ADF20
]
).

Let
F/K
be a field extension. For any
g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, we write

R_{\mathfrak{p},g,t_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F):=\left\{\frac{a}{1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}b}:a,b\in g(\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}{(\gamma)}),\dots,\gamma(F\setminus\operatorname{Poles}{(\gamma}))),1+t_{\mathfrak{p}}b\neq 0\right\},

and for
n\geq 1,
we set
R_{\mathfrak{p},g,t_{\mathfrak{p}},n}(F)

\left\{x\in F:x^{m}+a_{m-1}x^{m-1}+\dots+a_{0}=0,1\leq m\leq n,a_{m-1},\dots,a_{0}\in R_{\mathfrak{p},g,t_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F)\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{p},n}
denote the set of polynomials
g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of degree and height at most
n
. Let

R_{\mathfrak{p},n}(F):=\bigcup\limits_{t_{\mathfrak{p}}}\bigcup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{p},n}}R_{\mathfrak{p},g,t_{\mathfrak{p}},n}(F)

where
t_{\mathfrak{p}}
varies over the (finite) set of uniformizers of
\mathfrak{p}
of minimal height. Note that
\left(R_{\mathfrak{p},n}(F)\right)_{n\geq 1}
is an increasing sequence and
R_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=\bigcup\limits_{n}R_{\mathfrak{p},n}(F)
.

The
\mathfrak{p}
-
Pythagoras number

\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)
is the minimum
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that

R_{\mathfrak{p}}(F)=\bigcup\limits_{k\leq n}R_{\mathfrak{p},k}(F).

4
Diophantine families

In this section we introduce the model theoretic tools that we need to prove the analogue of Siegel’s Theorem.

We first recall the notion of diophantine family introduced by S. Anscombe, P. Dittman and A. Fehm (see
[
ADF20
]
). An
n
-dimensional diophantine family over
K
is a map
D
from the class of field extensions
F/K
to sets which is given by finitely many polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in K[X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}],
for some
m
, in the sense that

D(F)=\{x\in F^{n}:\exists y\in F^{m}[f_{1}(x,y)=0,\dots,f_{r}(x,y=0]\}

for every extension
F/K
. From the point of view of model theory,
D
is given by an existential formula
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in the language of rings with free variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and parameters from
K
, and
D(F)
is the realization of
\varphi
in
F
, i.e.,
D(F)=\{x\in F^{n}:F\models\varphi(a)\}
.

Diophantine families are closed under unions, intersections, cartesian products and images of rational functions, in the sense that there is a diophantine family
D
, such that for any field extension
F/K,

D(F)
realizes the corresponding operation (see
[
ADF20
, Section 3]
for details).

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\rm rings}=\{+,-,\cdot,0,1\}
the language of rings, by
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm rings}\cup\{\mathcal{O}\}
the language of valued fields, where
\mathcal{O}
is a unary predicate symbol, and by
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}(\varpi)=\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}\cup\{\varphi)
, where
\varpi
is a constant symbol. We view a valued field
(K,v)
as an
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}
-structure by interpreting
\mathcal{O}
as the valued ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
. A uniformizer
\pi
of
K
is an element of minimal positive value. We view
(K,v,\pi)
as an
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}(\varpi)
-structure where
\varpi
is interpreted as
\pi
.

Finally, given a field extension
K/C
we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}(\varpi,C)
the expansion of
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}(\varpi)
where we add a constant symbol for each element of
C
, and
(K,v,\pi,C)
denotes the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm val}(\varpi,C)
-structure expanding
(K,v,\pi)
in the usual way, i.e., the constant symbol
c_{x}
is interpreted by
x
.

ADF23
, Proposition 2.3]
for details).

is existentially closed in every extension

with residue field

The following is essentially

Proposition 5.4

For every prime number

of degree

,

Let

[A_{\mathfrak{p}}]:=\operatorname{inv}^{-1}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\frac{1}{\ell}),[A_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}]:=\operatorname{inv}^{-1}_{K_{\mathfrak{q}_{1}}}(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell})

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Br}(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum-\otimes K_{\mathfrak{q}}}

For the first clause, we have that

We claim that

∎

Proposition 6.1
.

For any

,
K\left(\sqrt[n]{p(t})\right)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}((\frac{1}{t}))
.

Proof.

Write

\alpha(X)
has an

\mathbb{F}_{q}[[\frac{1}{t}]]

∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
p(t)\in P_{n}^{+}

For

Proposition 6.4
.

For any positive integer
N
. The set
\{x\in K_{\rm inf}:\|x\|_{\rm max}\leq N\}

Proof.

Let
x\in K_{m+1}
be given. Set
u_{m}:=\sqrt[n]{p_{m}(t)}
. Then
x=a_{0}+a_{1}u_{m}+\dots+a_{n-1}u_{m}^{n-1}
where
a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{F_{m}}.
The conjugates of

are given by
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma(a_{i})\zeta_{n}^{ji}u_{m}^{i}
where
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_{m}/K)
and

.
Using the relation

we obtain that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma(a_{i})\zeta_{n}^{ji}u_{m}^{i}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(\sigma(a_{i})u_{m}^{i}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{ji}\right)=n\sigma(a_{0})

It follows, from the strong triangle inequality, that
\|\sigma(a_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\|x\|_{\rm max}
for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_{m}/K)
. Hence,
\|a_{0}\|_{\rm max}\leq\|x\|_{\rm max}.

Using again the strong triangle inequality we have that

Then we have

\|a_{1}\|_{\rm max}\leq\|x\|_{\rm max}q^{-\deg(p_{m})/n}

n

for

. If

then it follows that

for

Since there are only finitely many polynomials satisfying the inequality

, then there is an

). This gives us a
\mathcal{L}_{\rm ring,t}
–first-order definition of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\rm inf}}
without parameters. A standard reduction argument then shows that the undecidability of
K_{\rm inf}
follows from the undecidability of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\rm inf}}
.
Using
[
MSU23
, Theorem A and Lemma 2.8]
, we further reduce the problem to construct a parametrized family of definable sets of
\mathcal{O}_{K_{\rm inf}}
, that contains sets of arbitrarily large finite cardinalities. To see this, consider the sets
\{x\in K_{\rm inf}:\|t^{-N}x\|_{\rm max}\leq 1\}.
This last set corresponds to
\{x\in K_{\rm inf}:\forall\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}_{\infty}}(K_{\rm inf})(\|t^{-N}x\|_{\mathfrak{P}}\leq 1)\ \}
. By Proposition 6.1 the prime at infinity
\mathfrak{p}_{\infty}
splits completely in
K_{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}.
Therefore
\{x\in K_{\rm inf}:\|t^{-N}x\|_{\rm max}\leq 1\}
is equal to the integral closure of the Kochen ring in
K_{\rm inf}
. Finally, using Theorem 5.8 we obtain that this family is existentially
\mathcal{L}_{\rm ring,t}
–definable. This concludes the proof of the Theorem.

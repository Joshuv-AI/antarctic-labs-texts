Stationary Boltzmann Equation for Polyatomic Gases in a slab
Ki Nam Hong, Marwa Shahine, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
We consider the existence of steady rarefied flows of polyatomic gas between two parallel condensed phases, where evaporation and condensation processes occur. To this end, we study the existence problem of stationary solutions in a one-dimensional slab for the polyatomic Boltzmann equation, which takes into account the effect of internal energy in the collision process of the gas molecules. We show that, under suitable norm bound assumptions on the boundary condition functions, there exists a unique mild solution to the stationary polyatomic Boltzmann equation when the slab is sufficiently small. This is based on various norm estimates - singular estimates, hyperplane estimates - of the collision operator, for which genuinely polyatomic techniques must be employed. For example, in the weighted and singular estimates of the collision operator, we carry out integration with respect to the parameter describing the internal-translational energy distribution, which provides a regularizing effect in the estimate.

Introduction

1.1  Stationary Boltzmann equation for polyatomic molecules

The Boltzmann equation is the fundamental model to describe gaseous system at mesoscopic level by
describing the time evolution of the velocity distribution of underlying particles. The original
Boltzmann equation was derived under the assumption that the molecules are monatomic. This enabled
one to obtain the exact formulation of the pre-collision and post-collision velocities of the molecules
and express the collision operator in an explicit form. However, except for a few exceptions such as helium and
neon, most of the gases in nature are polyatomic. In this regard, Bourgat et al
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},r,R,\sigma)

(8)

A non-negative function

\varphi f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{I};L^{\infty}_{x}(0,1))

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Assume that the inflow boundary data
f_{LR}\geq 0
satisfies

|||f_{LR}|||<\infty,\quad\||v_{1}|^{-1}(1+(|v|^{2}+I)^{\gamma/2})f_{LR}\|_{0}<\infty.

(9)

In
[
48
,
49
]
, Maslova proved the existence of a unique mild stationary solution to the Boltzmann equation in a slab for monatomic molecules with the hard sphere collision kernel. Our goal in this paper is to extend this result in two directions: polyatomic molecules and hard potential collision kernels. An interesting observation in the present work is that these two objectives are interconnected.

To apply the Banach fixed point theorem, we need to estimate the collision operator in various norms.
Maslova proved that the weighted
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm estimate of
Q^{+}
for the monatomic Boltzmann equation can be controlled by
\|f\|_{-1}\|f\|_{0}
in the hard sphere case. Rather unexpectedly, we obtain a stronger estimate of
Q^{+}
for the polyatomic Boltzmann equation over the range of
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Namely, the weighted
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm of the polyatomic
Q^{+}
can be controlled using only the
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm of
f
over the range
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
.

The key observation is that we can enjoy an interesting polyatomic regularizing effect if we carry out the integration in the parameter
r
first in the calculation, which describes the distribution of the internal energy in the pre-post collision process. More precisely, in the estimate of
Q^{+}
in
\|\cdot\|_{0}
, we arrive at the following integral (See Lemma
3.1
):

\displaystyle\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{RE}+r(1-R)E\right)\!\right)E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(1-R)\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI.

By carrying out integration in the internal energy distribution parameter
r
first, we arrive at the integration involving only the pre-collision velocities, which enable one to bound
\|Q^{+}\|_{0}
by
\|f\|_{0}
only:

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi}{a}\int\|ff_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+{\frac{|v_{*}|^{2}}{2}+I+I_{*}}\right)\right)\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI.

Such regularization by the polyatomic parameter, of course, does not arise in the corresponding computation for the monatomic collision operator. This observation is also crucially used in the singular norm estimate of
Q^{+}
(see Lemma
3.3
). Unfortunately, this singular norm estimate is available only for the range
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
due to some integrability issue, which is why our results cover only half of the hard potential range. This will be left for future research.

In
[
48
,
49
]
, the contraction estimate of the head term in the mild form was mistakenly overlooked. In current work, we observe that a velocity growth arise in the missing contraction estimate of the head term, which cannot be controlled by the constant lower bound of the loss term as in
[
48
,
49
]
.
To cancel out this velocity growth and keep the invariance of the solution map, we show that the following stronger lower bound estimate is preserved by the solution map within the solution space (see Lemma
4.4
):

L(f)\geq a_{2}\left(1+\left(|v|^{2}+I\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right).

We note that this estimate does not hold as an a priori estimate but holds only for elements in the solution space, in contrast to the time-dependent problem for the homogeneous Boltzmann equation or the multiplication operator in the linearized collision operator for the Boltzmann equation near equilibrium.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, various preliminary lemmas are presented, which will be fruitfully used throughout the paper. Section 3 is devoted to the derivation of various necessary norm estimates for the gain term of the collision operator. Lemma 2.1
.

For
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma\leq\frac{8\pi}{(1+\gamma)(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}},

is the post-collisional velocity defined in (

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\sigma+w|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|\sigma+\bar{c}|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma,

where
\bar{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{RE}}\left(\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+w\right).

Without loss of generality, we set
\bar{c}=(0,0,|\bar{c}|)
, to find

\displaystyle|\sigma+\bar{c}|^{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle=|(\sin\phi\cos\theta,\sin\phi\sin\theta,\cos\phi+|\bar{c}|)|^{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left((\sin\phi\cos\theta)^{2}+(\sin\phi\sin\theta)^{2}+(\cos\phi+|\bar{c}|)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle=(1+2|\bar{c}|\cos\phi+|\bar{c}|^{2})^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|\sigma+\bar{c}|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{{(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}}\int_{0}^{\pi}(1+2|\bar{c}|\cos\phi+|\bar{c}|^{2})^{-\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\sin\phi\,d\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\int_{-1}^{1}(1+2|\bar{c}|t+|\bar{c}|^{2})^{-\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\,dt\quad(t=\cos\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{|\bar{c}|(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\int_{(|\bar{c}|-1)^{2}}^{(|\bar{c}|+1)^{2}}p^{-\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}\,dp\qquad(p=1+2|\bar{c}|t+|\bar{c}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(1+\gamma)|\bar{c}|(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}(|\bar{c}|+1)^{\gamma+1}-||\bar{c}|-1|^{\gamma+1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{(1+\gamma)(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}.

In the last line, we used

\frac{(x+1)^{1+\gamma}-|x-1|^{1+\gamma}}{x}\leq 4\qquad(x\geq 0)

for any

such that

.

We now give a symmetry property of the Boltzmann gain operator, which will be used in the norm estimates of the gain operator in the following section.

Lemma 2.2

\displaystyle\int\varphi(v,I)

\displaystyle Q^{+}(f,f)\,dvdI

, we obtain

\displaystyle L(f)

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\left(\frac{e^{a}}{a}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+I\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})f_{*}\,dv_{*}dI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\left(\frac{e^{a}}{a}\right)\left(1+\left(|v|^{2}+I\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right)\|f\|_{0}.

In the last line, we used
(x+y)^{t}\leq x^{t}+y^{t}
for
x,y\geq 0
and
0\leq t\leq 1
.
∎

3
Estimates of
Q^{+}

In this section, we provide various estimates of the gain part
Q^{+}
, which will be crucially employed in the following existence proof.

3.1  The estimate of
Q^{+}
in
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm:

We start with
\|\cdot\|_{0}
estimate of the gain term. We note that, taking integration in
r
first plays a key role, which is unobserved in the monatomic case. We also remark that our estimate
improves the corresponding gain term estimate in the monatomic case
[
49
]
, in that we only need
\|\cdot\|_{0}
norm of
f
to control
Q^{+}
while the monatomic estimate needed the
\|\cdot\|_{0}

and
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
norm of
f
. This improvement came from considering the case
E<1
and
E\geq 1
separately, and
using the averaging in the internal energy distribution parameter
r
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
, the following inequality holds

\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}\leq 4\pi\max\left\{\frac{1}{a},1\right\}\|f\|_{0}^{2}.

Proof.

We consider two cases
E\leq 1
and
E>1
separately.

1.

for
E\leq 1
:
We have from
\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})\leq\varphi(v,I)\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})
,

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle=\int\varphi(v,I)\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle=\int\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})\mathcal{B}(r(1-r))^{\alpha}(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v,I)\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v,I)dvdI\right)\left(\int\|f_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})\,dI_{*}dv_{*}\right)\left(\int dRdrd\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi\|f\|_{0}^{2}.

2.

for
E>1
:

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\varphi(v,I)\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle=\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})\mathcal{B}(r(1-r))^{\alpha}(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\sigma\right)^{2}+r(1-R)E\right)\right)\mathcal{B}(1-R)\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+r(1-R)E\right)\!\right)E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(1-R)\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{RE}+r(1-R)E\right)\!\right)E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(1-R)\,dRdrd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI.

We then integrate in
r
first, to find

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a}\int\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{RE}+(1-R)E\right)\right)\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dRd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\int\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{E}\right)\right)\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dRd\sigma dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{a}\int\|{ff_{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{\frac{|v-v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+I+I_{*}}\right)\right)\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi}{a}\int\|f\|_{L^{\infty_{x}}}\|f_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+{\frac{|v_{*}|^{2}}{2}+I+I_{*}}\right)\right)\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{a}\int\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|f_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\varphi(v,I)\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\,dI_{*}dv_{*}dvdI.

Since
E>1
and
\gamma\leq 1
, we have
\frac{1}{E^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq 1,
and therefore we get the desired estimate.

\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{0}\leq\frac{4\pi}{a}\|f\|_{0}^{2}.

∎

3.2  The estimate of
Q^{+}
in
\|\cdot\|_{P}
norm:

Now we move on to the estimate of
Q
restricted on a hyperplane. This estimate naturally arise in the invariance estimate later in the existence proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
, the following inequality holds

\|Q^{+}(f,g)\|_{P}\leq\max\{\pi,2^{2-\gamma}\}(\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{0}+\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{1-\gamma}).

Proof.

Let
P
be given by
av_{1}+bv_{2}+cv_{3}+d=0
. For an arbitrary point
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define
d(v)
to be the distance from
v
to the plane
P
:

d(v)=\frac{av_{1}+bv_{2}+cv_{3}+d}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}

To derive an estimate
Q^{+}
restricted on the hyperplane
P
,
In order to bound
\|Q^{+}\|_{P}
, we introduce a sequence
\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\alpha d^{2}(v)}
that converges to the Dirac delta function concentrating on
P
as
\alpha
goes to infinity.

\displaystyle\int\varphi(v,I)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\alpha d^{2}(v)}Q^{+}(f,g)\,dvdI

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}\exp\left(-\alpha\left(\frac{av_{1}^{\prime}+bv_{2}^{\prime}+cv_{3}^{\prime}+d}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}\right)^{2}\right){(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\mathcal{B}\sqrt{R}(r(1-r))^{\alpha}(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdRd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}\exp\left(-\alpha\left(\frac{(a,b,c)\cdot\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sqrt{RE}(a,b,c)\cdot\sigma+d}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}\right)^{2}\right){(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\mathcal{B}\sqrt{R}(r(1-r))^{\alpha}(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdRd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\pi\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\pi}}\int\exp\left(-\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}\left[a\frac{v_{1}+v_{*}{}_{1}}{2}+b\frac{v_{2}+v_{*}{}_{2}}{2}+c\frac{v_{3}+v_{*}{}_{3}}{2}+d\right]+\sqrt{RE}\cos\phi\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sqrt{R}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sin\phi\,d\phi drdRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

Here,
\phi
denotes the angle between the fixed vector
(a,b,c)
and
\sigma
. For simplicity, we denote

V=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}\left[a\frac{v_{1}+v_{*}{}_{1}}{2}+b\frac{v_{2}+v_{*}{}_{2}}{2}+c\frac{v_{3}+v_{*}{}_{3}}{2}+d\right]

and carry out integration over
\phi
first to get

\displaystyle\int\varphi(v,I)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\alpha d^{2}(v)}Q^{+}(f,g)\,dvdI

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}\left\{\int_{0}^{\pi}\exp\left(-\alpha\left(V+\sqrt{RE}\cos\phi\right)^{2}\right)\sin\phi\,d\phi\right\}\sqrt{R}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}drdRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{RE}}\int_{V-\sqrt{RE}}^{V+\sqrt{RE}}e^{-\alpha t^{2}}\,dt\right\}\sqrt{R}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}drdRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{RE}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\alpha t^{2}}\,dt\right\}\sqrt{R}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}drdRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{RE}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\right\}\sqrt{R}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}drdRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle=\pi\int\varphi\varphi_{*}{(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)}\frac{1}{E^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}.

If
E\geq 1
, this yields

\displaystyle\int\varphi(v,I)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\alpha d^{2}(v)}Q^{+}(f,g)\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\pi\int\varphi\varphi_{*}(fg_{*}+f_{*}g)\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\pi\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{0}.

On the other hand, in the case
E\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int\varphi_{\alpha}(v,I)Q^{+}(f,g)\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-\gamma}\left(\sup_{\omega}\int\varphi_{*}g_{*}\frac{1}{|v_{*}+\omega|^{{}_{1-\gamma}}}\,dv_{*}dI_{*}\right)\int\varphi f\,dvdI

(11)

\displaystyle+2^{1-\gamma}\left(\sup_{\omega}\int\varphi g\frac{1}{|v+\omega|^{1-\gamma}}\,dvdI\right)\int\varphi_{*}f_{*}\,dv_{*}dI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-\gamma}\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{1-\gamma}

Finally, we combine (
10
) and (
11
),
take the limit
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
, and take the supremum over
P
we get the desired estimate:

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{P}

\displaystyle=\sup_{P}\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\int\varphi(v,I)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\alpha d^{2}(v)}Q^{+}(f,f)\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq{\max\{\pi,2^{2-\gamma}\}(\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{0}+\|f\|_{0}\cdot\|g\|_{1-\gamma}).}

∎

3.3  The estimate of
Q^{+}
in
\|\cdot\|_{1-\gamma}
norm:

We note in the previous lemma that
\|\cdot\|_{1-\gamma}
estimate shows up in the estimate of
\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{P}
. Therefore, we need to estimate the collision operator in
\|\cdot\|_{1-\gamma}
norm for the sake of invariance properties in the existence proof.
We also remark that, unlike the monatomic Boltzmann case, using the averaging over the internal energy distribution parameter

plays a crucial role in the derivation of the estimate.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
,
Q^{+}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}\leq\frac{16\pi}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{a}\right\}\|f\|_{0}^{2}

Proof.

When
E\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})ff_{*}\mathcal{B}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdRd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\varphi\varphi_{*}ff_{*}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdRd\sigma.

Taking integration in
\sigma
first and applying Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{\gamma+1}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}ff_{*}E^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}}\frac{1}{{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}dR.

Since
2\gamma-1\geq 0
, we have
E^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}}\leq 1
, therefore

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{\gamma+1}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}ff_{*}\frac{1}{{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}dR

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{\gamma+1}\int\varphi\varphi_{*}ff_{*}\left\{\int\frac{1}{{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}}dR\right\}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\pi}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}\|f\|_{0}^{2}.

In the case
E\geq 1
, we recall (
3
) and employ Lemma
2.1
to compute

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\varphi(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})ff_{*}\mathcal{B}\sqrt{R}(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdRd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int\left\{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{1}{|v^{\prime}+w|^{1-\gamma}}\,d\sigma\right\}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+r(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sqrt{R}(1-R)

\displaystyle dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdR

\displaystyle=\int\left\{\frac{8\pi}{\gamma+1}\frac{1}{(RE)^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\right\}\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+r(1-R)E\right)\right)ff_{*}E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sqrt{R}(1-R)

\displaystyle dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdR

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{\gamma+1}\int\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+r(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}\frac{E^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}{E^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\frac{(1-R)}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}drdR.

Since
E\geq 1
, we have
\frac{1}{E^{\frac{3}{2}-\gamma}}\leq 1
. Therefore, carrying out the integration in
r
first, we obtain

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{1-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{a(1+\gamma)}\int\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}\frac{1}{(1-R)E}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{E^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}}}\frac{(1-R)}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}dR

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{a(1+\gamma)}\int\exp\left(a\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|}{2}+\sqrt{RE}\right)^{2}+(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{E^{\frac{3}{2}-\gamma}}}\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}dR

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{a(1+\gamma)}\int\exp\left(a\left(\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+RE\right)+(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{E^{{\frac{3}{2}-\gamma}}}}\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\,dRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi}{a(1+\gamma)}\int\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{RE}+(1-R)E\right)\right)\;ff_{*}\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}\,dRdvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{a(\gamma+1)}\int\exp\left(a\left(\frac{|v+v_{*}|^{2}}{4}+{E}\right)\right)\;ff_{*}\left\{\int^{1}_{0}\frac{1}{R^{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}}}dR\right\}dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{16\pi}{a(\gamma+1)^{2}}\int\varphi(v,I)\varphi(v_{*},I_{*})\;ff_{*}\,dvdv_{*}dIdI_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\pi}{a(\gamma+1)^{2}}\|f\|_{0}^{2}.

∎

4
Invariance of the solution map
\Psi

In this section, we will provide the necessary proofs for the existence. We will first prove invariance, and then establish contraction, both of which are required to apply the Banach fixed point theorem.

For this, we define our solution space
\mathcal{A}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{I};L^{\infty}_{x}(0,1))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}f\geq 0,\|f\|_{0}\leq a_{1},L(f)\geq a_{2}\left(1+\left(|v|^{2}+I\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right),\|f\|_{1-\gamma}\leq a_{3},\|f\|_{P}\leq a_{4}\right\}.

The value of
a_{i}

(i=1,2,3,4)
will be clarified in the proof.
We now define the solution map
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
as follows:

\Psi(f)=\Psi^{+}(f)\mathbbm{1}_{v_{1}>0}+\Psi^{-}(f)\mathbbm{1}_{v_{1}<0},

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Psi^{+}(f)(x,v,I)&=\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}L(f)(y,v,I)\,dy\right)f_{L}(v,I)\\
&+\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{y}^{x}L(f)(z,v,I)\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy\qquad(v_{1}>0)\\
\Psi^{-}(f)(x,v,I)&=\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{x}^{1}L(f)(y,v,I)\,dy\right)f_{R}(v,I)\\
&+\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{x}^{1}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{x}^{y}L(f)(z,v,I)\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy\qquad(v_{1}<0).\end{split}

(12)

In this section, we prove that the solution map
\Psi
is invariant in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1/2\leq\gamma\leq 1
. Assume
f_{LR}
satisfies (
9
). Then
\Psi
is invariant in
\mathcal{A}
. That is,
\Psi(f)\in\mathcal{A}
for
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

To prove this proposition, we need to show that

\Psi(f)\geq 0,\;\|\Psi(f)\|_{0}\leq a_{1},\;L(\Psi(f))\geq a_{2}\left(1+\left(|v|^{2}+I\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right),\;\|\Psi(f)\|_{1-\gamma}\leq a_{3},\;\text{and \;}\|\Psi(f)\|_{P}\leq a_{4}

for some positive constants
a_{j}

(j=1,2,3,4)
,
which will be given in the following series of lemmas.
Throughout the proof, we will only consider
\Psi^{+}(f)
, since the proof for
\Psi^{-}(f)
is almost identical.

Lemma 4.2
.

\Psi
preserves non-negativity:

\Psi(f)\geq 0

for
f\in\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

This follows directly from the definition of the solution map:

\displaystyle\Psi^{+}(f)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}L(f)\,dy\right)f(0,v,I)+\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{y}^{x}L(f)\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}L(f)\,dy\right)f_{L}\geq 0.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Define

a_{1}\equiv 2|||f_{LR}|||
.
Then, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, we have

\|\Psi(f)\|_{0}\leq a_{1}

whenever
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\Psi^{+}(f)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Psi^{+}(f)\|_{0}

\displaystyle=\int\varphi(v,I)\|\Psi^{+}(f)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}L(f)\,dy\right)f_{L}(v,I)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle+\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{y}^{x}L(f)\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\equiv I+II.

To estimate
I
, we recall
L(f)\geq a_{2}
to get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{0}^{x}L(f)\,dy\right)f_{L}(v,I)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}xa_{2}\right)f_{L}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{1}}{2}.

Then, the first part is bounded by
\frac{a_{1}}{2}
.
For the second part, we have

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{y}^{x}L(f)\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int\varphi(v,I)\left\|\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\int_{y}^{x}\!\!a_{2}\,dz\right)Q^{+}(f,f)\,dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\leq\int\varphi(v,I)\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\tau\right)\,d\tau\right)\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI,

where
\tau=x-y
.
We then divide this integral into small
v_{1}
region and large
v_{1}
region:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{|v_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\varphi(v,I)\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\tau\right)\,d\tau\right)\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{|v_{1}|>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\varphi(v,I)\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\tau\right)\,d\tau\right)\|Q^{+}(f,f)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\,dvdI

\displaystyle\equiv II_{1}+II_{2}.

For the estimate of
II_{1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\left|v_{1}\right|<\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\left(1-\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{|v_{1}|}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}

(13)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\left(1-\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{v_{1}}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}+2\int_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\left(1-\exp\left(-a_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{v_{1}}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+2a_{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\varepsilon}{v_{1}}\,dv_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon+2a_{2}\varepsilon\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-2a_{2}{\varepsilon}\ln\varepsilon

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon+4a_{2}\varepsilon\ln{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}.

to obtain

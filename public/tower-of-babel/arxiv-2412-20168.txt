Nonlinear Conjugate Gradient Methods for Optimization of Set-Valued Mappings of Finite Cardinality
Debdas Ghosh, Ravi Raushan, Zai-Yun Peng, Jen-Chih Yao

Abstract
This article presents nonlinear conjugate gradient methods for finding local weakly minimal points of set-valued optimization problems under a lower set less ordering relation. The set-valued objective function of the optimization problem under consideration is defined by finitely many continuously differentiable vector-valued functions. For such optimization problems, at first, we propose a general scheme for nonlinear conjugate gradient methods and then introduce Dai-Yuan, Polak-Ribière-Polyak, and Hestenes-Stiefel conjugate gradient parameters for set-valued functions. Toward deriving the general scheme, we introduce a condition of sufficient decrease and Wolfe line searches for set-valued functions. For a given sequence of descent directions of a set-valued function, it is found that if the proposed standard Wolfe line search technique is employed, then the generated sequence of iterates for set optimization follows a Zoutendijk-like condition. With the help of the derived Zoutendijk-like condition, we report that all the proposed nonlinear conjugate gradient schemes are globally convergent under usual assumptions. It is important to note that the ordering cone used in the entire study is not restricted to be finitely generated, and no regularity assumption on the solution set of the problem is required for any of the reported convergence analyses. Finally, we demonstrate the performance of the proposed methods through numerical experiments. In the numerical experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods not only on the commonly used test instances for set optimization but also on a few newly introduced problems under general ordering cones that are neither nonnegative hyper-octant nor finitely generated. Keywords Set optimization, Vector optimization, Conjugate gradient method, Wolfe conditions. Mathematics Subject Classifications 49J53, 90C29, 90C47.

Introduction

Set optimization is a class of mathematical problems concerned with minimizing set-valued mappings between two vector spaces, where the image space is partially ordered by a specified closed, convex, and pointed cone. Two primary approaches exist for defining solutions to these problems: the vector approach and the set approach. This paper centers on the latter.
In the set approach, a preorder is defined on the power set of the image space, and minimal solutions of the set-valued problem are determined accordingly. The foundational research in this field began with the works of Young
[
35
]
, Nishnianidze
[
32
]
, and Kuroiwa
[
25
,
27
]
, who first introduced set relations to establish a preorder. Kuroiwa
[
28
]
was the pioneer in applying the set approach to set optimization problems. Since then, this area of research has expanded significantly due to its applications in finance, optimization under uncertainty, game theory, and socioeconomics. For a comprehensive overview of the field, we refer to the monograph
[
21
]
.

The focus of this work is on the development of efficient numerical techniques for solving set optimization problems. The existing approaches to solve set optimization problems can be broadly categorized into the following five groups.

•

Derivative-Free Methods

[
16
,
17
,
22
]
:
The methods in this group are descent methods that employ a derivative-free strategy. These algorithms are designed for unconstrained problems and do not assume any specific structure for the set-valued objective mapping.

•

Sorting Type Algorithms

[
11
,
10
,
26
,
23
]
: This class of methods is designed to tackle set optimization problems with a finite feasible set. As a result, they rely on direct comparisons of the images produced by the set-valued objective mapping.

•

Algorithms Based on Scalarization

[
4
,
5
,
14
,
15
,
19
,
33

: This category of methods adopt a scalarization approach and are intended for problems where the set-valued objective mapping has a structure derived from the robust counterpart of a vector optimization problem under uncertainty. Subsequently, various scalarization techniques are utilized to solve the set optimization problem.

•

Branch and Bound Approach

[
5
]
: In the study by Eichfelder et al.
[
5
]
, an algorithm is introduced to tackle uncertain vector optimization problems, with the assumption that uncertainty arises solely from the decision variable. They proposed a branch-and-bound technique designed to identify a box covering for the solution set.

•

First-Order Solution Methods
: Bouza et al.
[
2
]
and Kumar et al.
[

]
reported algorithms to tackle unconstrained set optimization problems where the set-valued objective mapping is defined by a finite number of continuously differentiable functions. While derivative-free methods can be used to solve such problems, they face similar limitations to those encountered in scalar optimization. Notably, derivative-free methods are expected to perform slower than first-order methods as they do not use the available first-order information of the objective function.

In this paper, in the category of first-order methods, we introduce a general conjugate gradient algorithm for the set optimization problems taken in
[
2
,
24
]
. The motivations for this work are as follows:

•

The considered type of set optimization problems have significant applications in optimization under uncertainty. Indeed, set optimization problems of this nature emerge when determining robust solutions for vector optimization problems under uncertainty, especially when the uncertainty set is finite (see
[
15

•

In

Thus, in this paper, we aim to devise a first-order technique in this study whose convergence does not depend on any regularity condition on the solution set of the problem.

•

In
[
30

, Prudente et al. extended the conventional nonlinear conjugate gradient methods for vector optimization problems. Because of

Thus, there is a need to derive a general nonlinear conjugate gradient method for the set optimization problems under consideration whose convergence is not dependent on the existence of a finite generator of the ordering cone.

With these motivations, we propose a general nonlinear conjugate gradient method for set optimization problems without assuming that the ordering cone is finitely generated. We also establish the convergence of the proposed method without taking any regularity assumption on the solution set of the problem under consideration.

Step 1:

\displaystyle\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{\hat{j}}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}-\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{d})e}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{\hat{j}}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}}\right)-\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{d})

\displaystyle=\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{\hat{j}}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}}\right)-\rho~\underset{j\in[\bar{\omega}]}{\max}\left\{\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{j}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{\hat{j}}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}}\right)-\rho\psi_{e}\left({\nabla{f^{\bar{a}_{\hat{j}}}{(\bar{x})}}^{\top}\bar{d}}\right)

\displaystyle~\leq\psi_{e}\left(-\nabla f^{\bar{a}_{\bar{j}}}\left(\bar{x}+\hat{\alpha}\bar{d}\right)^{\top}\bar{d}+\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)e\right)\leq 0

\displaystyle\implies~

\displaystyle~\psi_{e}\left(\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)e\right)\leq\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{\bar{a}_{\bar{j}}}\left(\bar{x}+\hat{\alpha}\bar{d}\right)^{\top}\bar{d}\right)

\displaystyle\implies~

\displaystyle~\underset{j\in[\bar{\omega}]}{\max}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{\bar{a}_{\bar{j}}}\left(\bar{x}+\hat{\alpha}\bar{d}\right)^{\top}\bar{d}\right)\right\}\geq\psi_{e}\left(\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)e\right)=\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)

\displaystyle\implies~

\displaystyle~\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x}+\hat{\alpha}\bar{d},\bar{d}\right)\geq\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right).

From Lemma
2.3
(iii),
\psi_{e}
is a continuous function. So,
\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x}+\alpha\bar{d},\bar{d})
is also a continuous function of
\alpha
. Hence, by the intermediate value property, there exists
\alpha^{*}\in(0,\bar{\alpha})
such that

\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x}+{\alpha}^{*}\bar{d},\bar{d}\right)=\rho\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right).

Since
\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)<0
and
\sigma\in(\rho,1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)<\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x}+{\alpha}^{*}\bar{d},\bar{d}\right)<0.

(22)

Since
\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x}+\alpha\bar{d},\bar{d})
is a continuous function, there exists a neighbourhood
I\subset I_{0}
of
\alpha^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x},\bar{d}\right)<\bar{\mathcal{F}}\left(\bar{x}+{\alpha}\bar{d},\bar{d}\right)<0\text{ for all }\alpha\in I,

(23)

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Under the hypothesis of
Theorem
3.1
, we get a guarantee for the existence of an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
in which the standard Wolfe conditions (
14
) hold because for a given
K
-descent direction
\bar{d}
of
F
at
\bar{x}
, we have
\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{d})<0
. Thus, (
15b
) implies that for all
\alpha\in I
,

\displaystyle~\left\lvert\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x}+\alpha\bar{d},\bar{d})\right\rvert\leq-\sigma\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{d})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle~\sigma\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{d})\leq\bar{\mathcal{F}}(\bar{x}+\alpha\bar{d},\bar{d}),

which is the condition (
14b
).

For the rest of the analysis on the convergence of any sequence generated by Algorithm
1
, we take the following often-used assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

The level set
\mathcal{L}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:F(x)\preceq^{\ell}F(x_{0})\right\}
is bounded.

Assumption 3.2
.

The functions
\nabla f^{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,p
, are Lipschitz continuous on a nonempty open set
\Gamma
containing
\mathcal{L}
with a common Lipschitz constant
L>0
, i.e.,

\forall~i\in[p]\text{ and }y,z\in\Gamma:~\|\nabla f^{i}(y)-\nabla f^{i}(z)\|\leq L\|y-z\|.

Assumption 3.3
.

For any sequence
\{S_{k}\}\subset F(\mathcal{L})
with

S_{k+1}\preceq^{\ell}S_{k}\text{ for all }k=0,1,2,\ldots,

there exists a bounded set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
S\preceq^{\ell}S_{k}
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is worth highlighting that these three assumptions are natural extensions of those applied in conventional or vector optimization problems. Under Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
, we show below that the general method (
10
) fulfills a condition akin to the conventional Zoutendijk’s criterion if the standard Wolfe conditions (
14
) are employed for choosing the step-length
\alpha_{k}
at the iterate
x_{k}
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
. This result on Zoutendijk-like condition is used later to show the global convergence of the proposed conjugate gradient methods.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
hold. Furthermore, assume that for the iterative scheme (
10
),
d_{k}
is a
K
-descent direction of
F
at
x_{k}
, and
\alpha_{k}
satisfies the standard Wolfe conditions (
14
) for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}{\frac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}}<+\infty.

(24)

Proof.

From the standard Wolfe condition (
14b
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
\psi_{e}
, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma-1)\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})~

\displaystyle\leq~\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k+1},d_{k})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})

\displaystyle=~\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\max}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k+1})^{\top}d_{k}\right)\right\}-\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\max}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq~\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k+1})^{\top}d_{k}\right)-\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k}\right),

\displaystyle~~~~~\text{ where }j_{k}\in\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\argmax}\left\{\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k+1})^{\top}d_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq~\bar{L}\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k+1})-\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})\|\|d_{k}\|,

\displaystyle~~~~~\text{ where }\bar{L}\text{ is a Lipschitz constant of }\psi_{e}

\displaystyle\leq~\bar{L}L\alpha_{k}\|d_{k}\|^{2}\text{ by Assumption \ref{lip_ass}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\leq\hat{L}\alpha_{k}\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\sigma-1},\text{ where }\hat{L}=\bar{L}L.

(25)

Consider a function
\mathscr{G}:\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
defined by

\mathscr{G}(A)=\underset{z\in A}{\inf}~\psi_{e}(z),\text{ where }A\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Notice that
\psi_{e}
has a monotonicity property. Therefore, corresponding to the preorder
\preceq^{\ell}
, the function
\mathscr{G}
is monotone, i.e.,

A_{1}\preceq^{\ell}A_{2}\implies\mathscr{G}(A_{1})\leq\mathscr{G}(A_{2}),\text{ where }A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

By (
14a
), we have

\displaystyle\forall~\alpha_{k}\in(0,\bar{\alpha}_{k}]\text{ and }k\geq 0:F(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k})\preceq^{{\ell}}\left\{f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})e\right\}_{j\in[\omega_{k}]}.

As the function
\mathscr{G}
is monotonic,

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{G}\circ F\right)(x_{k+1})~

\displaystyle\leq~\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\min}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})e\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq~\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\min}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})\right)-\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\psi_{e}(-e)\right\}

\displaystyle=~\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\min}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})\right)+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\right\}

\displaystyle=~\left(\mathscr{G}\circ F\right)(x_{k})+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k}).

Applying this relation repeatedly, we get

\displaystyle~\left(\mathscr{G}\circ F\right)(x_{k+1})-\left(\mathscr{G}\circ F\right)(x_{0})\leq\rho\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}~\mathcal{F}^{i}(x_{i},d_{i})

\displaystyle\implies~

\displaystyle~\mathscr{C}\leq\rho\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}~\mathcal{F}^{i}(x_{i},d_{i})\leq 0,\text{ where }\mathscr{C}=\underset{s\in S}{\inf}\psi_{e}(s)-\left(\mathscr{G}\circ F\right)(x_{0})

\displaystyle\implies~

\displaystyle~\tfrac{\mathscr{C}}{\sigma-1}\geq\tfrac{\rho}{\sigma-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}~\mathcal{F}^{i}(x_{i},d_{i})\geq 0.

Therefore, it follows that
\sum_{k\geq 0}\alpha_{k}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\sigma-1}<+\infty,

and by the relation (
25
), we get

\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}<+\infty.

∎

4
A general nonlinear conjugate gradient method

In this section, we present a general nonlinear conjugate gradient scheme (Algorithm
1
) for the set optimization problem (

⁢
S
O

P
ℓ

). Depending on different ways of choosing
\beta_{k}
in Step 4 of Algorithm
1
, we get different special schemes of the nonlinear conjugate gradient method for (

⁢
S
O

P
ℓ

). We explore later, in Section
5
, three tactful choices of
\beta_{k}
.

Algorithm 1

A general nonlinear conjugate gradient method for the set optimization problem (

⁢
S
O

P
ℓ

)

Step 0
. Choose an arbitrary initial point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the parameters
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\sigma\in(\rho,1)
. Provide a precision scalar
\varepsilon>0
.
Set the iteration counter
k:=0.

Step 1
. Find

M_{k}:=\mathrm{Min}(F(x_{k}),K),~\omega_{k}:=\lvert\mathrm{Min}(F(x_{k}),K)\rvert,\text{ and }P_{k}:=P_{x_{k}}.

Step 2
. Compute

(a_{k},u_{k})\in\underset{(a,u)\in P_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\left(\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\max}{\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u\right)\right\}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}\right).

Step 3
. If
\|u_{k}\|<\varepsilon
, then stop. Otherwise, go to Step 4.

Step 4
. Find

d_{k}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{k}&&\text{ if }k=0\text{ or }\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\right\rvert<\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1}),\\
&u_{k}+\beta_{k}d_{k-1}&&\text{ otherwise},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(26)

where
\beta_{k}
is a given parameter.

Step 5
. Find a step length
\alpha_{k}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{a_{j}}(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k})\preceq_{K}f^{a_{j}}(x_{k})+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})e~\text{ for all }j\in[\omega_{k}]\\
&\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},d_{k})\geq\sigma\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k}),\\
\end{aligned}\right.

or

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{a_{j}}(x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k})\preceq_{K}f^{a_{j}}(x_{k})+\rho\alpha_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})e~\text{ for all }~j\in[\omega_{k}]\\
&\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k})+\alpha_{k}d_{k},d_{k})\right\rvert\leq\sigma|\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})|.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Set
x_{k+1}:=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k},~k\leftarrow k+1,
and go to Step 1.

4.1
Well-definedness of Algorithm
1

The well-definedness of Algorithm
1
depends only on Steps 2 and 5.

For any iterate
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Remark
2.1
(i) ensures the existence of an
(a_{k},u_{k})
for Step 2. So, Step 2 is well-defined. However, notice that the choice of
(a_{k},u_{k})
, in
Step 2
, may not be unique. Even if there are multiple
(a_{k},u_{k})
’s, we are free to choose any
(a_{k},u_{k})
because irrespective of which
(a_{k},u_{k})
is chosen in Step 2, the corresponding
u_{k}
is a
K
-descent direction of
F
at
x_{k}
. During the proof of the global convergence of Algorithm
1
(Theorem ), we will see that we just require that
d_{k}
is a
K
-descent direction.

For Step 5 of Algorithm
1
, in Theorem
3.1
, we have proved a guarantee of the existence of a step length
\alpha_{k}
that satisfies the standard or the strong Wolfe conditions along a
K
-descent direction
d_{k}
. So, Algorithm
1
is well-defined provided at
x_{k}
the direction
d_{k}
in Step 4 is a
K
-descent direction of the set-valued objective function
F
. In the next lemma, we provide some general choices for the parameter
\beta_{k}
in Step 4 of Algorithm
1
so that
d_{k}
, chosen by the formula (
26
), is a
K
-descent direction of the objective function of the problem (

⁢
S
O

P
ℓ

).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
\{x_{k}\}
is a sequence of nonstationary points generated by
Algorithm
1
and for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots,
the value of
\beta_{k}
in
Step 4
of
Algorithm
1
is chosen by the rule

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\in\left\{\begin{aligned} &[0,\infty)&\text{ if }~\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\leq 0,\\
&\left[0,-\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})}\right]&\text{ if }~\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})>0,\end{aligned}\right.

(27)

or

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\in\left\{\begin{aligned} &[0,\infty)&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\leq 0,\\
&\left[0,-\mu\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})}\right]&\text{ if }\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})>0,\end{aligned}\right.

(28)

where
\mu\in(0,1)
. If
\beta_{k}
is chosen by (

), then
d_{k}
is a
K
-descent direction of
F
at
x_{k}
. Moreover, if
\beta_{k}
is chosen by the rule (
28
), then the direction
d_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
) with
c=1-\mu
.

Proof.

We prove the result for the case of (
28
). The case of (
27
) can be similarly proved.

Let
\beta_{k}
be chosen by the rule (
28
). Then, clearly
for
k=0
, as
d_{0}=u_{0}
,

satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
) since

\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})=\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\leq(1-\mu)\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k}).

Next, assume that
k\geq 1
. From the definition of
d_{k}
and Lemma
2.3
(ii), we obtain

\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k}\right)\leq\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)+\beta_{k}\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k-1}\right)\text{ for all }j\in[\omega_{k}]

(29)

because
\beta_{k}\geq 0
. Thus, for the element
a_{k}\in P_{k}
at
x_{k}
, by using the relation (
29
), we get

\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\beta_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1}).

(30)

Since
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})<0
, if
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\leq 0
, then from (

) we obtain

\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\leq(1-\mu)\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k}).

Now, suppose that
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})>0

\beta_{k}\in\left[0,-\mu\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})}\right]

\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})-\mu\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})=(1-\mu)\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k}).

Thus,

satisfies the sufficient descent condition (

) with

).

Remark 4.1
.

In

of

, note that we use a

)
, we could not find any guarantee of

. So, we have used the known descent direction

as

when

.

4.2
Convergence analysis

In this section, we examine the global convergence property of Algorithm

\beta_{k}\geq 0
for all

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
hold and
\{x_{k}\}

Proof.

On the contrary, suppose that (i) is not true. Then, we can find a constant

Notice that

ℓ

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}<0,

From relations (

), and (

For the proof of the statement (ii), similar to (i), if it is false, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\beta_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{the1.3e1}}{\implies}c_{1}\|u_{k}\|^{4}\leq~

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})+\tfrac{\beta^{2}_{k}}{2}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}),

where

.

Using the definition of

(iii), we have

\displaystyle-\beta_{k}d_{k-1}=-d_{k}+u_{k}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\beta^{2}_{k}\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}\leq\left[\|d_{k}\|+\|u_{k}\|\right]^{2}\leq 2\|d_{k}\|^{2}+2\|u_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\|d_{k}\|^{2}\geq-\|u_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\beta^{2}_{k}}{2}\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(37)

Note that

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}+\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\left[{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})+\tfrac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{the1.3e5}}{\geq}\tfrac{1}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\left[{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})+\left(\tfrac{\beta^{2}_{k}}{2}-\tfrac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}\right){\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{the1.3e4}}{\geq}\tfrac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\left[c_{1}\|u_{k}\|^{2}-\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}\right].

From Theorem
3.2
,
\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|}\rightarrow 0.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}+\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{c_{1}}{2}\tfrac{\|u_{k}\|^{4}}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\geq\tfrac{c_{1}\gamma^{4}}{2}\tfrac{1}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}

for sufficiently large
k
. This inequality, together with Theorem
3.2
, yields
\sum_{k\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}<+\infty
, which is contradictory to (
31
). Thus, the result follows.

∎

5
Three special conjugate gradient methods

In this section, we explore three special choices of the conjugate gradient parameter
\beta_{k}
in Step 4 of the general nonlinear conjugate gradient Algorithm
1
.

•

Dai-Yuan (DY):

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}:=\frac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}.

(38)

•

Polak-Ribière-Polyak (PRP):

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{PRP}_{k}}:=\frac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})}{-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}.

(39)

•

Hastenes-Stiefel (HS):

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathrm{HS}_{k}}:=\frac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}.

(40)

Next, we discuss the global convergence of Algorithm
1
for these three particular choices of
\beta_{k}
in Step 4 of Algorithm
1
. Before analyzing the convergence with the parameter
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
, we demonstrate that the direction
d_{k}
generated by Algorithm
1
with the parameter
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
). This result is crucial in the subsequent proof of global convergence.

Lemma 5.1
.

Consider
Algorithm
1
with
0\leq\beta_{k}\leq\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
. Assume that
\{x_{k}\}
is a sequence of nonstationary points generated by this algorithm when
\{\alpha_{k}\}
is chosen by the strong Wolfe conditions (
15
). Then,
d_{k}
satisfies sufficient descent condition (
12
) with
c=\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by the method of induction.

Due to the definition (
26
) of
d_{k}
, for
k=0
,
d_{0}=u_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{a_{0}}(x_{0},u_{0})<0
and
0<\sigma<1
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{a_{0}}(x_{0},d_{0})\leq\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{a_{0}}(x_{0},u_{0}),

i.e., (
12
) holds with
c=\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}
.

Assume that for some
k\geq 1
, the following condition holds:

\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\leq\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1}).

Observe that if
\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\right\rvert<\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})
, then

d_{k}=u_{k}\implies\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})=\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k}).

Accordingly, the direction
d_{k}
holds the condition (
12
) with
c=\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}
.
Therefore, we aim to show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\right\rvert>\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})
,
d_{k}
satisfies the condition (
12
). Since
\alpha_{k}
satisfies the strong Wolfe condition (
15b
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\geq\sigma\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)>\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)

because
0<\sigma<1
and
\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)<0
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)>0.

Thus, the denominator of
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
as given in (
38
) is positive. Consequently,
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
is well-defined. Since
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})<0
, we have
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}>0
.

From the definition of
d_{k}
, for
k\geq 1
, we have

d_{k}=u_{k}+\beta_{k}d_{k-1}.
Therefore,

\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k}=\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}+\beta_{k}\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k-1},j\in[\omega_{k}].

From Lemma
2.3
(i), for all
j\in[\omega_{k}]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k}\right)\leq\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)+\beta_{k}\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}d_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\beta_{k}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1}).

Notice that if
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\leq 0
, then
d_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
). Therefore, assume that
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})>0
and
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}>0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},d_{k-1})\text{ since }0<\beta_{k}\leq\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{1-\mathcal{V}_{k}},\text{ where }\mathcal{V}_{k}:=\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\left(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{1+\sigma}\text{ because from }\eqref{strong. wol.},~\mathcal{V}_{k}\in[-\sigma,\sigma],

which completes the proof.
∎

In the next theorem, under a suitable hypothesis, we demonstrate that global convergence can be achieved if
\beta_{k}
is an appropriate fraction of the DY parameter
\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider
Algorithm
1
with
\beta_{k}=\eta\beta_{\mathrm{DY}_{k}}
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, where
0\leq\eta<\tfrac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}
. Suppose that
Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3

are satisfied. If
\{x_{k}\}
is a sequence of nonstationary points generated by this algorithm where
\alpha_{k}
is chosen by the strong Wolfe conditions (
15
) for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, then
\underset{k\to\infty}{\liminf}~\|u_{k}\|=0
.

Proof.

By the strong Wolfe condition (
15b
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\geq(\sigma-1)\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1}).

(41)

According to Lemma
5.1
,
d_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent direction (
12
) with
c=\tfrac{1}{1+\sigma}
for all
k\geq 0
. Therefore, from (
41
), we get

\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})\geq\tfrac{\sigma-1}{1+\sigma}\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})>0

because
0<\sigma<1
and
\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})<0
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}\leq\tfrac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}.

Define
\delta:=\eta\tfrac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}<1
. Thus, using the definition of
\beta_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=\delta\tfrac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\left[\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta\tfrac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\left[\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}\right]\left[\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta\left[\tfrac{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}\right].

Assume on contrary that there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|\geq\gamma\text{ for all }k\geq 0.

(42)

Note that

\displaystyle\|d_{k}\|^{2}\leq\bigl[\|u_{k}\|+\lvert\beta_{k}\rvert\|d_{k-1}\|\bigr]^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\delta^{2}}\|u_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}\beta^{2}_{k}\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}

because of Lemma
2.2
(iv) with
z_{1}=\|u_{k}\|,~z_{2}=\lvert\beta_{k}\rvert\|d_{k-1}\|,
and
t=\tfrac{\delta}{\sqrt{(1-\delta^{2})}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\tfrac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\delta^{2}}\tfrac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}\tfrac{\beta^{2}_{k}\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{conv_2}}{\leq}\tfrac{1}{1-\delta^{2}}\tfrac{\|u_{k}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}+\tfrac{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{{k-1}},u_{k-1})}.

Since
0\leq\gamma^{2}\leq\|u_{k}\|^{2}<-2\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})
, therefore

\displaystyle\tfrac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}\leq\tfrac{4}{(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}+\tfrac{\|d_{k-1}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}}^{2}(x_{{k-1}},u_{k-1})}.

Applying this relation repeatedly, we obtain

\displaystyle\tfrac{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{4}{(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}k+\tfrac{\|d_{0}\|^{2}}{{\mathcal{F}^{0}}^{2}(x_{0},u_{0})}\leq\tfrac{4}{(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}k+\tfrac{4}{\gamma^{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\geq\tfrac{(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}{4(k+1-\delta^{2})}\geq\tfrac{(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}{4(k+1)}.

(43)

Now, by using the sufficient descent condition (
12
) and the relation (
43
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},d_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\geq\sum_{k\geq 0}c^{2}\tfrac{{\mathcal{F}^{k}}^{2}(x_{k},u_{k})}{\|d_{k}\|^{2}}\geq\tfrac{c^{2}(1-\delta^{2})\gamma^{2}}{4}\sum_{k\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{k+1}=+\infty,

which is contradictory to (
24
). Thus, the result follows.

∎

Next, we consider the convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
for PRP and HS parameters. Our results are based on the work of Prudente et al.
[
30
]
for vector optimization and the work of Gilbert and Nocedal
[
7
]
for scalar optimization by introducing the Property
(\ast)
. The vector extension of this property is given in
[

]
as follows.

Property

(\ast)
Consider a nonlinear conjugate gradient scheme that satisfies

0<\gamma\leq\|u_{k}\|\leq\bar{\gamma}\text{ for all }k=0,1,2,\ldots.

(44)

Under this setup, we say that an iterative method possesses Property
(\ast)
if there exist constants
b>1
and
\lambda>0
such that for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, we have
\beta_{k}\leq b
, and

\|s_{k-1}\|\leq\lambda\implies\beta_{k}\leq\tfrac{1}{2b},

where
s_{k-1}=x_{k}-x_{k-1}
.

Now, we analyze whether the proposed method, possessing Property
(\ast)
, can lead the iterative points to converge to a stationary point of the set-valued objective function
F
. Following this, we demonstrate that PRP and HS methods exhibit Property
(\ast)
under certain mild assumptions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
be a sequence of nonstationary points generated by
Algorithm
1
with
\beta_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
. Suppose that
Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold, and for all
k\geq 0
,
d_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
) and
\alpha_{k}
satisfies the standard Wolfe conditions (
14
) at
x_{k}
. Moreover, we assume that the method exhibits
Property

(\ast)
. Then,
\underset{k\to\infty}{\liminf}\|u_{k}\|=0
.

Proof.

The lines of the proof are similar to
(
30
, Theorem 5.10)
.
∎

We can now establish the convergence of the PRP and HS parameters.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
be a sequence of nonstationary points generated by Algorithm
1
with
\beta_{k}=\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{PRP}_{k}},0\}
or
\beta_{k}=\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{HS}_{k}},0\}
. Assume that
Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
hold and for all
k\geq 0
,
d_{k}
satisfies the sufficient descent condition (
12
) and
\alpha_{k}
satisfies the standard Wolfe conditions (
14
) at
x_{k}
. Then,
\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\liminf}\|u_{k}\|=0
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
5.2
, Algorithm
1
holds the result with with
\beta_{k}=\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{PRP}_{k}},0\}
or
\beta_{k}=\max\{\beta_{\mathrm{HS}_{k}},0\}
if
\mathrm{PRP}
and
\mathrm{HS}
methods have Property
(\ast)
. To prove that these two choices of
\beta_{k}
satisfy Property
(\ast)
, we begin with the assumption of (
44
). Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})<-\tfrac{1}{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}.

(45)

Note that, from Lemma
2.3
(iii),
\psi_{e}
is a Lipschitz continuous function. Therefore, there exists a Lipschitz constant, say,
\bar{L}
such that

\displaystyle\forall~{j}\in[\omega_{k}]:~\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\rvert\leq\bar{L}\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right\|\leq\bar{L}\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})\right\|\|u_{k}\|.

(46)

From the definition of
\mathcal{F}^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})

\displaystyle=\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right),\text{ where }j_{k}=\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\text{argmax}}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=-\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\rvert\overset{\eqref{the1.51}}{\geq}-\bar{L}\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})\|\|u_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\implies-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\bar{L}\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j_{k}}}(x_{k})\right\|\|u_{k}\|.

(47)

From the continuity arguments, there exists constant
\tilde{c}>0
such that
\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})\right\|\leq\tilde{c}
for all
k\geq 0
and
j\in[\omega_{k}]
since
\{x_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{L}
. Furthermore, assume that
\|u_{k}\|\leq\tilde{\gamma}\text{ for all }k\geq 0
. Therefore, using (
45
) and (
47
), we get

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\leq-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\leq\bar{\gamma}_{1}:=\bar{L}\tilde{c}\tilde{\gamma}.

Similarly, we can find
\bar{\gamma}_{2}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\right\rvert\leq\bar{\gamma}_{2}.

Assume that
\bar{\gamma}=\max\{\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2}\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\leq-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\leq\bar{\gamma}

(48)

and

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\right\rvert\leq\bar{\gamma}.

(49)

On the other hand, suppose
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\bar{j}_{k}}}(x_{k-1})^{\top}u_{k}\right)-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\right\rvert,

\displaystyle~~~\text{ where }\bar{j}_{k}=\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\text{argmax}}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k-1})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\bar{j}_{k}}}(x_{k-1})^{\top}u_{k}\right)-\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\bar{j}_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bar{L}\left\|\nabla f^{a_{k,\bar{j}_{k}}}(x_{k-1})-\nabla f^{a_{k,\bar{j}_{k}}}(x_{k})\right\|\|u_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|\leq\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}\lambda,

(50)

where
L
is a Lipschitz constant of
\nabla f^{i}
for all
i\in[p]
and
\|s_{k-1}\|\leq\lambda
.
In a similar manner, if
\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})
, then

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\tilde{j}_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\right\rvert,

\displaystyle~~~\text{ where }\tilde{j}_{k}=\underset{j\in[\omega_{k}]}{\text{argmax}}\left\{\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,j}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\tilde{j}_{k}}}(x_{k-1})^{\top}u_{k}\right)-\psi_{e}\left(\nabla f^{a_{k,\tilde{j}_{k}}}(x_{k})^{\top}u_{k}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}\lambda.

(51)

Because of the relations (
50
) and (
51
), we have

\left\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})\right\rvert\leq\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}\lambda.

(52)

Define
b:=\tfrac{4\bar{\gamma}}{\gamma^{2}}
and
\lambda:=\tfrac{\gamma^{2}}{2\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}b}
for the
\mathrm{PRP}
method. By (
48
) and (
49
), we get

\lvert\beta_{\mathrm{PRP}_{k}}\rvert\leq\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\rvert}{-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})}\leq\tfrac{4\bar{\gamma}}{\gamma^{2}}=b.

Now, if
\|s_{k-1}\|\leq\lambda
, then from (
48
) and (
52
), it follows

\lvert\beta_{\mathrm{PRP}_{k}}\rvert\leq\tfrac{L\bar{L}\tilde{\gamma}\lambda}{\gamma^{2}}=\tfrac{1}{2b}.

Thus,
\mathrm{PRP}
method has Property
(\ast)
.

Let us now examine the
\mathrm{HS}
method. By the standard Wolfe condition (
14b
) and the sufficient descent condition (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})

\displaystyle=(\sigma-1)\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq c(\sigma-1)\mathcal{F}_{k-1}(x_{k-1},u_{k-1})

(53)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{c(1-\sigma)\gamma^{2}}{2}>0.

For the HS method, define
b:=\tfrac{4\bar{\gamma}}{c(1-\sigma)\gamma^{2}}
and
\lambda:=\tfrac{c(1-\sigma)\gamma^{2}}{4\bar{L}L\tilde{\gamma}b}
.

From relations (
48
), (
49
), and (
53
), we have

\lvert\beta_{\mathrm{HS}_{k}}\rvert\leq\tfrac{-\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k},u_{k})+\lvert\mathcal{F}^{k}(x_{k-1},u_{k})\rvert}{\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k},d_{k-1})-\mathcal{F}^{k-1}(x_{k-1},d_{k-1})}\leq\tfrac{4\bar{\gamma}}{c(1-\sigma)\gamma^{2}}=b,

and when
\|s_{k-1}\|\leq\lambda
, it follows from (
52
) and (
53
) that

\lvert\beta_{\mathrm{HS}_{k}}\rvert\leq\tfrac{L\bar{L}\tilde{\gamma}\lambda}{\gamma^{2}}=\tfrac{1}{2b},

which concludes that the
\mathrm{HS}
method has Property
(\ast)
.
∎

Conclusion

In this work, nonlinear conjugate gradient methods have been proposed to identify local weakly minimal points of the set optimization problem (

⁢
S
O

P
ℓ

) under the lower set less preordering relation. We have introduced the concept of
K
-descent direction and a sufficient descent condition for set-valued functions with the help of an auxiliary real-valued function. With the help of this auxiliary function, we have introduced the standard and strong Wolfe line searches for set-valued functions. Following this, we have established a result (Theorem
3.1
and Remark
3.1
) on the existence of a step size for which the standard Wolfe or strong Wolfe conditions are satisfied. Then, we reported (in Theorem
3.2
) that for a given sequence
\{d_{k}\}
of
K
-descent directions of a set-valued function, any sequence of iterates
\{x_{k}\}
defined by
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}
satisfies a Zoutendijk-like condition if the step size sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}
satisfies the proposed standard Wolfe condition. Importantly, this step size existence result is derived without assuming that the ordering cone
K
is finitely generated. Thereby, all the derived results in this study are applicable even for nonfinitely generated ordering cone
K
.

Thereafter, we have proposed a general scheme (Algorithm
1
) for nonlinear conjugate gradient methods for the set optimization problem. Then, we have reported (Proposition
4.1
) some choices of
\beta_{k}
-value so that the direction
d_{k}
given in the general conjugate gradient Algorithm
1
is
K
-descent and sufficient descent. Subsequently, without explicitly restricting the parameter
\beta_{k}
, we have proved the global convergence (Theorem
4.1
) of the proposed algorithm.

Next, we have proposed the DY, PRP, and HS rules to choose the
\beta_{k}
value for set optimization problems. Then, in Theorem
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
, we have established the convergence of the proposed Algorithm
1
with DY, PRP, and HS rules. Finally, we have conducted numerical experiments to demonstrate the performance of the proposed methods. A comparison of the proposed DY, PRP and HS methods with the existing FR and CD methods for set optimization is also provided.

In the future, one may try to address the following issues.

•

As mentioned in Remark
4.1
, a regular restart condition is used in Step 4 to make
d_{k}
as a
K
-descent direction of
F

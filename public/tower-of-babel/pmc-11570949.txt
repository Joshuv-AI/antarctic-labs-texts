Effects of genotype and sex on carcass composition, meat quality, digestive tract morphometries and leg bone dimensions of spent parent Pekin ducks☆
Authors: Marcin Wegner, Dariusz Kokoszyński, Joanna Żochowska-Kujawska, Marek Kotowicz
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of the present study was to analyze carcass composition, physicochemical properties, muscle texture, and structure, as well as morphometry of the digestive tract and leg bone dimensions of Cherry Valley ducks (SM3 Heavy hybrid) and Orvia ducks (ST5 Heavy hybrid) after laying. A total of 32 ducks were dissected, 8 females and 8 males from each breeding set. Analysis of meat quality (breast muscle, leg muscle) in terms of color (L*, a*, b*), acidity (pH24), electrical conductivity (EC24), and basic chemical composition (protein, water, fat, collagen) was performed. Analysis of the texture and structure of the pectoral muscle was also performed. The dimensions of the various lengths of the digestive tract and the dimensions of the tibia and femur were analyzed. Cherry Valley ducks after laying have better slaughter performance and a higher proportion of breast muscle in the carcass (P < 0.001). Also more favorable chemical composition (protein, fat, water) and physical properties (acidity, electrical conductivity, thermal loss) of muscle. In terms of texture characteristics, the pectoral muscle of Cherry Valley ducks was more elastic and cohesive, but also more flexible with higher viscosity, while the pectoral muscles of Orvia ducks in terms of structure were characterized by smaller values of the analyzed characteristics (cross-sectional area of fibers and smaller vertical and horizontal diameter). Analyzing the morphometry of the digestive system, Orvia ducks were characterized by longer sections of the digestive tract, which certainly has a beneficial effect on the absorption of nutrients. In terms of tibia and femur measurements taken, there were no differences between genotypes, while males were characterized by higher values of the analyzed traits compared to females.Key words: Carcass, Bones, Digestive tract, Meat quality, Parent duck

Introduction

Ducks were domesticated in China and belong to the two genera Anas and Cairina. Most of the breeds that are bred worldwide are derived from the mallard duck (Anas platyrhynchos). However the ducks that are found in Africa and Asia are derived from the Muscovy duck (Cairina moschata), which was domesticated in Latin America (Kardaya et al., 2022). According to data (FAO, 2022), world duck meat production in 2021 reached 6.2 million tons, a 32.4 % increase in production over the past 10 years. China is the largest producer, accounting for 77.8 % of global duck meat production. In Europe, France produced the most duck meat in 2021, with 177,000 tons, Hungary 90,000 tons, and Poland 57,000 tons (Vorotnikov, 2022). According to data from 2000, China was not only the largest producer, but also the largest consumer, as it consumed 3.5 billion ducks, accounting for about 85 % of global production (Cao et al., 2021). Poland produced 3187,000 tons of poultry meat in 2020, with most meat coming from broiler chickens 82.6 %, while meat from ducks and geese accounted for only 2.4 % (KRD or KOWR). In Poland, hybrids from parent flocks imported from France and England are most often used for fattening. In France, there are two companies that supply parent flocks Grimaud Frères Sélection (hybrid Star 53 HY) and Orvia (hybrid ST5 Heavy), while in England, Cherry Valley Farms Ltd. (SM3 Heavy hybrid) (Kokoszyński et al., 2022). Ducks are kept for two purposes, egg production and meat production. Parental flocks are kept for egg production usually up to 72 weeks, while the laying period is often extended to 2 years (Kokoszyński et al., 2018; Kardaya et al., 2022). However, in China, meat from old ducks is considered very valuable because it is characterized by high quality and therapeutic effectiveness. Old ducks are mainly processed into meat and soup, but the price, unlike young ducks, is even several times higher (Duan et al., 2023). Meat from old ducks is also used to produce sausages as a source of high-quality protein (Naveen et al., 2016). Meat from ducks after the laying period is characterized by a higher fat content, is tougher and drier, and is mainly used for the production of canned meat, cold cuts and soups (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a; Kardaya et al., 2022). Compared to meat from broiler chickens, duck meat is characterized by twice the fat content and is very valuable because it contains polyunsaturated fatty acids, amino acids, vitamins, minerals, and easily digestible protein (Liu et al., 2013; Sohaib et al., 2015; Augustyńska-Prejsnar et al., 2018; Bai et al., 2020, Wang et al., 2020). The consumption of duck meat is steadily increasing year after year, as it is chosen by consumers for its delicate taste and nutritional value (Cao et al., 2021). The pectoral muscles of ducks are characterized by lower protein and ash content, while they contain more water and fat compared to the pectoral muscles of broiler chickens (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a). In terms of microstructure, breast muscles have a fine-fibered structure whose diameter of white fibers is between 49.7–55.6 mm, while red fibers are 26.0 - 32.5 mm (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a), which consequently affects the tenderness of the meat.

The diet, fed to the birds and the volume of food also has a major impact on the development of the digestive tract, which is influenced by many other factors (species, breed, age, sex) (Wasilewski et al., 2015; Kokoszyński et al., 2018). The length, as well as the structure of the digestive tract, has a major impact on the speed of digestion, for example, the length of the intestines in ducks is 4-5 times the body length, and in pigs, this ratio is 20 : 1 (Kokoszyński et al., 2018). A study by Kokoszyński et al. (2019a) observed that the genotype and sex of Pekin-type ducks influenced the dimensions of the caecum. The sex of the birds not only affects the dimensions of the digestive tract but also the dimensions of the leg bones. A study by Krunt et al. (2022) showed that sex affected the dimensions of the femur and tibia in Muscovy ducks. Also, another study showed that the sex and age of Pekin-type ducks influenced tibia and femur dimensions (Charuta and Reymond, 2007; Włodarczyk et al., 2023). The above studies suggest that genotype, sex, age, and nutrition have a major impact on carcass composition, meat quality, digestive tract dimensions, and leg bone dimensions. There is little work in the available literature on the texture and structure of duck meat, especially after the reproductive period. Therefore, this prompted us to perform the following research.

The study aimed to compare two genotypes of reproductive ducks after laying in terms of carcass composition, physicochemical properties, and meat texture and structure. A comparison of the dimensions of the different intestinal segments and the dimensions of the femur and tibia was also performed. The evaluation of meat quality traits in terms of chemical composition, physicochemical properties, texture, and structure is intended to provide information for consumers and the processing industry as to which genotype has better characteristics and qualities depending on the use of the meat.

Materials and methods

The research was carried out following applicable regulations. The study obtained permission No. 17/2010 of the 23ed June 2010 Committee for the Care of Animals (Ethics Committee) of the Bydgoszcz University of Science and Technology, Poland.

Research materials

At the end of the reproductive period on the farm, 8 males and 8 females from each strain were randomly selected, weighed, and marked, then transported to the poultry slaughterhouse where they were slaughtered. The study was conducted on a total of 32 carcasses of reproductive ducks (8 females and 8 males from each strain) purchased from a poultry slaughterhouse. The carcasses were from parental flocks of two genotypes: Orvia (ST5 Heavy hybrid) and Cherry Valley Farms Ltd. (SM3 Heavy hybrid) after laying (72 weeks). Before slaughtering, the birds were kept in windowless buildings with 24-hour access to water and were fed a complete mix of reproductive ducks containing 18.5 % CP (crude protein) and 11.1 MJ of metabolizable energy per kilogram.

Carcass analysis

After slaughtering, the eviscerated carcasses with neck and guts were transported to the University, where they were cooled at 2 °C in a refrigerated cabinet (Hendi, Gadki, Poland) for 18 h. Then, after cooling, the carcasses were weighed individually on an electronic weighing scale WLC 6/12/F1/R (Radwag, Radom, Poland) with an accuracy of 0.1 g. and dissection was performed using the simplified method described by Ziołecki and Doruchowski (1989). The following was extracted from each carcass during dissection: neck without skin, both wings with skin, skin from the whole carcass, pectoral muscles, leg muscles, and carcass remains. The extracted carcass parts were weighed on the above-mentioned scales, and then the percentage of their weight concerning the weight of the cold eviscerated carcass determined before dissection was calculated.

Analysis of physical and chemical properties

After 24 h postmortem, acidity reading (pH24) and electrical conductivity measurement (EC24) of the superficial pectoral muscle and thigh muscle were taken. A CX-701 pH meter with a knife electrode (Elmetron, Zabrze, Poland) was used to measure the acidity reaction, which was calibrated before measurements in pH 7.0 and 4.0 buffers. The measurement result was read from a liquid crystal display with an accuracy of 0.01. An LF-Star CPU conductivity probe (Ingenieurbüro R. Matthäus, Nobitz, Germany) was used to measure the electrical conductivity of the flesh (EC24). The electrode was placed at a 90-degree angle along the muscle fibers of the pectoralis major muscle and shin muscle. The measurement was performed with an accuracy of 0.1 mS/cm. The method described by Walczak (1959) was used to determine the thermal loss of pectoral and leg muscles. Muscle samples, each weighing 20 ± 2 g. were wrapped in gauze and placed in a water bath for 10 min with a water temperature of 85 °C and then cooled for 30 min in a refrigerated cabinet at 2 °C after removal. After cooling, the samples were weighed using an electronic balance WLC6/12/F1/R (Radwag, Radom, Poland). The percentage weight loss of the sample was calculated based on the difference in the weight of the sample before and after heat treatment.

Color analysis

The pectoral and leg muscles were then evaluated for color. MINOLTA's CR 400 colorimeter (Konica Minolta, Chiyoda-ku, Japan, 2013) was used for the analysis. The pectoral and leg muscles were evaluated in the color system L*-variable brightness, a*-variable red and b*- variable yellow.

Analysis of basic chemical composition

Using FoodScan (Foss, Hilleroed, Denmark), the basic chemical composition (protein, water, fat, and collagen content) was determined by near-infrared transmission spectroscopy (NIT), using the method described by Wegner et al. (2023).

Texture analysis

Texture (springiness, chewiness, cohesiveness, gumminess, hardness, WB shear force, and cutting work). 32 samples of pectoralis major muscle from 16 Orvia ducks (8 females and 8 males) and 16 Cherry Valley ducks (8 females and 8 males) each were heat treated to 70.2 °C and then cooled to approximately 12 °C. The texture was evaluated according to the procedures for texture profile analysis on muscle described by (Bourne, 1982) using the Stable Micro Systems TA.XT plus (Stable Micro Systems, UK), using the TPA test (texture profile analysis test) and the Warner-Bratzler test (WB test). Rheological properties of the meat were evaluated with an Intron 1140 (Intron, USA), using a relaxation test during which a stylus with 0.96 cm in diameter was inserted 2 mm into the sample (deformation of 10 %), registering strain changes for 90 s. Elasticity and viscosity moduli were calculated using a generalized Maxwell model consisting of a Hooke body connected in parallel with two Maxwell bodies (Lachowicz, 1992).

Structure analysis

Samples of the pectoralis major muscle from the middle section were taken from 32 ducks for histological analysis. The prepared meat samples were fixed using Sannomiya solution and then were dehydrated in alcohol and benzene. The prepared samples were then embedded in paraffin blocks. Using a microtome, the blocks were cut into 10 μm-thick slices and then placed on slides. The slides were contrast stained using hematoxylin and eosin (Burck, 1975) and then embedded in Canada balsam. Using the MultiScanBase v. 13 image analysis system (Computer Scanning System Ltd.), fiber cross-sectional area, fiber diameter, horizontal fiber diameter (H), vertical fiber diameter (V) and thickness of the perimysium and endomysium were determined.

Anatomical analysis

Using an electronic balance PS 1000.R2 (Radwag, Radom, Poland), the weight of proventriculus, gizzard, liver, heart, and spleen were determined with an accuracy of 0.01 g. Then the percentage of body weight was calculated and based on the formula: organ weight to body weight ratio multiplied by 100 %. A tape measure was then used to measure the length of each section of the small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, and ileum), as well as the length of the caeca and length of the colon to the nearest 1 mm. On the other hand, the diameter of each segment of the intestine was measured to the nearest 0.01 mm using electronic calipers. Measurements were taken at three locations: the beginning, middle, and end of each intestinal segment.

Bones analysis

According to the method developed and reported by den Driesch, (1976), measurements were taken on leg bones obtained after dissection. Using electronic calipers with an accuracy of 0.01 mm, the lengths of the following were determined on the femur: GL, greatest length; ML, medial length; GB, greatest breadth of proximal end; GD, greatest depth of proximal end; SM, smallest breadth of the corpus; GC, greatest breadth of the distal end; GE, greatest depth of distal end. Measurements of the tibia were then made: GL, greatest length; AL, axial length; GD, greatest diagonal of the proximal end; SB, smallest breadth of the corpus; SD, greatest breadth of the distal end; DD, greatest depth of the distal end.

Statistical analysis

The numerical data collected in this study were characterized by generally accepted methods of statistical analysis. For each trait studied, the arithmetic means of both factors studied (genotype and gender) were calculated for both genotypes combined. Using the Shapiro-Wilk test, the empirical correspondence of the trait distribution to a normal distribution was assessed. Therefore, a parametric test in the form of a two-factor analysis of variance was used to determine the effect of genotype and gender on the trait under study. The following linear model was used: Yijk = µ + ai + bj + (a × b)ij + eijk where: Yijk is the trait value, µ is the overall trait mean, ai is the effect of the i-th genotype, bj is the effect of the j-th sex, (a × b)ij is the genotype of the birds by sex interaction, eijk is the error, and k is the kth observation for the target trait in the group.

SAS software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) version 9.4 Significances of differences at P < 0.05 were verified between genotypes and between males and females using the Tukey post hoc test.

Results

Carcass characteristics

The duck genotype had a significant effect (P < 0.05) on slaughterhouse yield, the share of breast muscles, and carcass remainders, while the sex of ducks had a significant effect on body weight, carcass weight, slaughterhouse yield, the share of breast muscles, wings with skin and carcass remainders (Table 1). The body weight and carcass weight of male ducks were significantly (P < 0.001) higher compared to the analyzed characteristics in females. SM3 Heavy ducks and drakes were characterized by higher slaughter performance compared to ST5 Heavy ducks and females (P = 0.001; P < 0.001, respectively). SM3 Heavy ducks had a significantly higher percentage of pectoral muscle compared to the trait analyzed in ST5 Heavy ducks (P < 0.001). Also, males had a higher percentage of pectoral muscle in the carcass compared to females (P < 0.001). On the other hand, females had a higher percentage of remains and wings compared to the analyzed trait in males (P = 0.002; P = 0.033, respectively). Also, ST5 Heavy ducks were characterized by a higher percentage of carcass residue compared to the analyzed trait in SM3 Heavy ducks (P = 0.006). Interactions between genotype and sex were also demonstrated within the analyzed traits: body weight, carcass weight, percentage of abdominal fat, neck, and carcass residue (P < 0.001 − 0.041).

Physicochemical properties

The duck genotype had a significant (P < 0.05) impact on the acidity (pH24), electroconductivity (EC24), and thermal loss of the breast muscles and leg muscles, while gender had an impact on the pH of the leg muscles and the EC and thermal loss of the breast muscles (Table 2). The pectoral and leg muscles of SM3 Heavy ducks were characterized by significantly (P < 0.05) higher pH24 and electrical conductivity, and lower thermal leakage in the leg muscle compared to the analyzed traits in ST5 Heavy ducks (P < 0.001 - 0.002). The pectoral muscles of males were characterized by higher EC24 and lower thermal leakage compared to females (P = 0.015; P = 0.030, respectively). In contrast, the leg muscles of females had a higher pH24 compared to males (P = 0.001). Genotype-gender interactions were also found in the analyzed traits of pH24 of leg muscles, EC24 of pectoral muscles, and thermal leakage of pectoral and leg muscles (P < 0.001–0.043).

Analyzing the color was a significant effect of genotype and gender on the level of color saturation L* of leg muscles and genotype on the level of color saturation b* of breast muscles (Table 3). The leg muscles of ST5 Heavy ducks and females had a higher level of L* color saturation compared to the leg muscle trait analyzed in SM3 Heavy ducks and males (P = 0.029; 0.031, respectively). In contrast, the pectoral muscles of SM3 Heavy ducks were characterized by a higher level of yellow color compared to those of ST5 Heavy ducks (P = 0.017). There was no interaction between genotype and sex in the analyzed traits of pectoral muscle and leg muscle color (P = 0.061 – 0.924).

Analyzing the basic chemical composition, a significant (P < 0.05) effect of the duck genotype on the content of fat and water in the breast muscle (PM) and leg muscle (LM), as well as protein in the leg muscle was demonstrated (Table 4). The influence of gender on the content of protein, fat, and water in the PM and LM as well as collagen in the pectoral muscle was also demonstrated. The LM of SM3 ducks were characterized by higher protein percentage and pectoral muscle fat content compared to the analyzed traits of ST5 Heavy ducks (P < 0.001). The percentage of water in the PM and LM was also lower in SM3 ducks (P < 0.001). Males, on the other hand, had a higher percentage of protein in PM and LM (P < 0.001; 0.007), and a higher percentage of collagen in PM (P = 0.009) and water in LM (P = 0.017). Texture

Analyzing the PM texture properties (Table 5), a significant (P < 0.05) influence of the genotype on cohesiveness, springiness, the sum of elastic moduli, and the sum of viscous moduli was demonstrated. The influence of gender on the analyzed trait sum of viscous moduli was also demonstrated. The PM of ST5 Heavy ducks was characterized by higher cohesiveness and lower springiness compared to the analyzed PM features in SM3 Heavy ducks (P = 0.004, 0.031; respectively). Analyzing the traits of total modulus of elasticity and total modulus of viscous showed higher pectoral muscle values in SM3 ducks (P = 0.001). The analyzed traits of texture and rheological properties were not affected by the sex of the birds (P = 0.146 - 0.460), except for the sum of viscous moduli. Males were characterized by a higher value of the analyzed trait compared to females (P = 0.001). There was no interaction between genotype and sex in the analyzed traits of texture and rheological properties (P = 0.294 - 0.973).

Structure

Discussion

Among the factors that have a major impact on duck body weight, slaughter performance, carcass weight, and composition, we can include bird genotype, housing system, nutrition, age, and sex (Murawska, 2012; Kokoszyński et al., 2019a; Kokoszyński et al., 2019b; Ding et al., 2021; Bai et al., 2022a, Bai et al., 2022b; Li et al., 2024). In the present study, the effect of genotype on carcass weight, proportion of pectoral muscle, and residue was observed, while duck body weight, slaughter yield, proportion of pectoral muscle, and carcass residue were affected by the sex of the birds. These observations are consistent with the results obtained by other authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2019a; Ding et al., 2021; Li et al., 2024). Pekin ducks (Danish P8) from the genetic reserve were characterized by higher body and carcass weight and the percentage of breast muscles, neck and carcass residues compared to Polish P33 and French P9 ducks at the end of the reproductive period (Kokoszyński et al., 2019a). In contrast, Bai et al. (2022a) observed higher body weight and body fat content in ducks kept in cages compared to those kept on the floor. Similar conclusions were reached by other authors (Zhang et al., 2018), who confirmed the above observations in Chaohu ducks, in which the housing system affected carcass weight, breast muscle content, leg muscle content, and abdominal fat. In other studies, it was also observed that the gender of ducks influenced the carcass weight (Kokoszyński et al., 2022). Males, regardless of the tested genotype, Dworka (Strain D11) and Pekin (Strain P9) were characterized by a higher carcass weight compared to females within the same genotype.

One of the basic factors that influences meat quality is pH, which is strongly correlated with color and thermal leakage (Wegner et al., 2023). According to the authors Rayan et al. (2022), meat with pH < 5.7 has a pale color and a hard consistency after cooking, while meat characterized by a higher pH > 6.20 has a darker color and is more tender, but despite this, it is also worse in technological processing. In the presented studies, the acidity value was influenced by the genotype of ducks, but the results obtained were similar to the results obtained in other works (Qiao et al., 2017; Kokoszyński et al., 2022). Other authors also showed that the duck genotype influenced the acidity of breast muscles (Onk et al., 2019; Li et al., 2024) and leg muscles (Qiao et al., 2017; Li et al., 2024). The technological quality of meat depends largely on pH, which influences, among other things, thermal leakage. The optimal pH value causes the meat structure to close and water to be retained between muscle fibers (Abubakar et al., 2021), and consequently inhibits the glycolysis process. The glycolytic process in meat causes the production of lactic acid, and consequently a decrease in acidity. The higher acidity level in our study was most likely influenced by the genotype of the birds and age (ducks after the reproductive period), which also affected the thermal leakage of the breast and leg muscles. The results of thermal leakage of the breast and leg muscles in ST5 ducks obtained in our study were similar to the results from another study (Zhang et al., 2018). The influence of duck genotype on the analyzed thermal leakage trait was also observed in other works (Quai et al., 2017; Kokoszyński et al., 2022). The results of the analyzed trait in the breast muscle of Dworka ducks (strain D11) obtained by the authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2022) were higher than those obtained in our study in SM3 ducks. On the other hand, the authors obtained significantly lower results (Li et al., 2024) in ducks depending on the growth rate. Meat that is characterized by high water loss during heat treatment is of lower quality because losses during cooking lead to the loss of nutrients (Abubakar et al., 2021). Also, lower losses during cooking are important for the food industry, because they show better quality of the final product (Qiao et al., 2017). Other authors also observed that the genotype of ducks influenced the value of electrical conductivity (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a; Kokoszyński et al., 2020b; Kokoszyński et al., 2022). In our study, we also observed such a relationship. SM3 ducks, regardless of sex, were characterized by a higher value of electrical conductivity (EC24) in the breast muscle and leg muscle compared to ST5 Heavy ducks. The higher EC24 values in the breast muscle of ST5 Heavy ducks compared to the leg muscles were most likely influenced by glycolysis (more intensive) occurring in the muscles after slaughter. During the previously mentioned glycolysis, lactic acid is produced, causing protein denaturation and damage to cell membranes, which consequently leads to an increase in the concentration of ions in the intercellular space, resulting in higher electrical conductivity. The EC24 results obtained by the authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a) were lower by 6.2–7.5 mS/cm in the leg muscle of ducks with a different genotype (P33 - Pekin of Polish origin, P8 - Pekin of Danish origin, LsA - Pekin of English origin) compared to the results obtained in our study in SM3 Heavy ducks, but comparable to the analyzed trait in ST5 Heavy ducks.

When purchasing meat, consumers pay particular attention to the color, which varies depending on the type of meat (breast, leg). Meat color is strongly correlated with acidity, but factors that can affect meat color also include genotype, housing system, nutrition, and utility type (Qiao et al., 2017; Banaszak et al., 2020; Kokoszyński et al., 2020b; Kokoszyński et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022a; Li et al., 2024).). In our studies, we observed that the duck genotype affected the L* color saturation of the leg muscles and the b* color saturation level of the breast muscles. The yellow color saturation of the breast muscles was higher in SM3 ducks compared to the analyzed trait in ST5 ducks, while the leg muscles of females were characterized by higher lightness (L*) compared to the analyzed trait of the leg muscles in females. In other studies, the authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2020b; Kokoszyński et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024) showed the effect of the duck genotype on the b* color saturation of the breast muscles. On the other hand, ducks with a slow growth rate were characterized by a higher saturation of the b* color of the breast muscle compared to the analyzed trait in ducks with a fast growth rate (Li et al., 2024). Other authors also observed that the genotype of ducks from the genetic reserve also influenced the saturation of the breast muscle with L* and a* colors (Kokoszyński et al., 2020b). Higher breast muscle L* values were observed in ducks fed yellow lupin-based feed (Banaszak et al., 2020), and in birds that were kept in cages compared to ducks reared in a floor system (Bai et al., 2022a).

An important factor from the consumer's perspective and the culinary value of meat is its composition. The main factors that have a significant impact on the chemical composition of meat include genotype, age, type of use, nutrition, sex and housing system (Qiao et al., 2017; Biesiada-Drzazga et al., 2019; Kokoszyński et al., 2020a; Kokoszyński et al., 2020b; Cao et al., 2021; Kardaya et al., 2022; Kokoszyński et al. 2022; Wegner et al. 2024). In our study, the genotype of birds influenced the content of protein, fat, and water in the leg muscle and fat and water in the breast muscle. The same relationship was also observed in other studies (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a; Kokoszyński et al., 2020b; Kokoszyński et al., 2022). Comparing different genotypes of Pekin ducks from the genetic reserve after 2 years of laying, the authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a) showed that P33 (Pekin of Polish origin) ducks were characterized by the lowest amount of water and protein and the highest amount of fat and collagen in the breast and leg muscle compared to the analyzed traits in LsA (Pekin of English origin) and P8 (Pekin of Danish origin) ducks. The presented studies also showed that the content of protein, fat, and water in the breast and leg muscle and collagen in the breast muscle was influenced by the gender of the birds. Other authors (Kokoszyński et al., 2022) also observed interactions between sexes comparing Pekin and Dworka ducks after 2 laying periods. Other studies, however, showed that with the age of STAR 53 H.Y. ducks, water content decreased, while fat and protein increased in the breast and leg muscles (Biesiada-Drzazga et al., 2019). However, the authors Cao et al. (2021) obtained different results in Cherry Valley broiler ducks. Protein content increased between 28 and 45 days, while intramuscular fat in the breast muscle decreased. On the other hand, fat and water content in the breast and leg muscles was influenced by the housing system of small-sized meat ducks at 63 days of age (Bai et al., 2022a). Ducks kept in cages were characterized by higher fat content in the breast and leg muscles and lower water compared to ducks kept on the floor (Bai et al., 2022a).

A significantly important feature of meat from the consumer's point of view is its texture. In our study, the genotype of birds and gender did not affect the value of shear force and cutting work. In another study, Pekin ducks were characterized by a higher value of the analyzed feature, which proves that the meat is less tender compared to Dworka ducks (Kokoszynski et al., 2022). Other authors observed that the pectoral muscles of Cherry Valley ducks were characterized by a significantly lower shear force WB value compared to other types of utilitarian ducks (Qiao et al., 2017). Our study showed the influence of genotype on the cohesiveness and springiness of the breast muscle. ST5 ducks were characterized by higher cohesiveness of the breast muscle and lower elasticity. This was confirmed by analyzing the rheological properties. Breast muscles of SM3 ducks were characterized by higher properties (sum of elastic moduli, sum of viscous moduli) compared to the analyzed features in ST5 ducks. Authors Kokoszyński et al. (2020b) also observed the effect of duck genotype after 2 laying periods on texture features (hardness, cohesiveness, elasticity, gumminess, and chewiness). In our study, no effect of gender on texture features was observed. However, the authors of Zhang et al. (2018) observed that female Chaohu ducks were characterized by a lower value of shear force compared to males, which indicates that the meat from females is more delicate. In the study by Kokoszyński et al. (2022a), the effect of gender on the rheological properties of the breast muscle in ducks at 112 weeks of age was demonstrated. We confirmed such a relationship in our study, in which we also showed the effect of gender on the value of rheological properties (sum of viscous moduli).

Chen et al. (2007) observed that there is a positive correlation between shear force and muscle fiber diameter. Our study did not show such a relationship. In contrast, in the study by Kokoszyński et al. (2020a), the authors observed that the genotype of Pekin ducks from genetic reserve resources influenced the cross-sectional area of the fibers, fiber circumference, and vertical diameter (V) of the muscle fiber. P33 ducks were characterized by higher measurements of the above traits compared to LsA ducks at 112 weeks of age. The results obtained in our study were significantly higher (323.42 µm2 – SM3) compared to ducks from genetic reserve resources (58.1 µm2 – P33). In turn, our previous studies did not show an effect of genotype on the above traits, while the authors Kokoszyński et al. (2022) observed differences in the thickness of the perimysium and endomysium in ducks after the reproductive period (113 weeks). Dworka ducks (D11) were characterized by thicker connective tissue (perimysium, endomysium) in relation to the analyzed features in Pekin ducks (P9). In our study, we confirmed this relationship, however, the obtained results of external connective tissue thickness (11.39 µm – ST5 Heavy) were smaller (17.7 µm – Dworka D11), while the internal connective tissue thickness (1.68 µm – SM3) was larger (0.9 µm – P9). The influence of the genotype (P9, K2, KhO1) of ducks from the genetic reserve on connective tissue thickness was also demonstrated in another study (Kokoszyński et al., 2018). In the presented results, we also observed that males, regardless of genotype, were characterized by a larger cross-sectional area, fiber circumference, and vertical (V) and horizontal (H) diameter compared to females. Also in other studies, it was observed that the above traits were higher in males compared to females (Kokoszyński et al., 2022). However, such a relationship of sex influence on the analyzed features of the breast muscle structure was not demonstrated in ducks from the genetic reserve (Kokoszyński et al., 2020a).

In the study by Kokoszyński et al. (2019a), the authors observed that the weight and percentage of the liver in the body weight were influenced by the genotype of the ducks. Pekin P9 ducks were characterized by the heaviest liver weight and percentage in the body weight compared to P33 and P8 ducks. In the same study, the authors also showed that the weight and percentage of the liver were heavier in males compared to females. In our study, we also observed such a relationship in the percentage of the liver in the body weight of ST5 and SM3 ducks. We also showed that the percentage of the proventriculus and gizzard was influenced by the gender of the birds. Males were characterized by a lower share of the analyzed traits compared to females. The share of internal organs may also be influenced by the housing system and feeding of ducks (Bai et al., 2022a; Shazly et al., 2021). In the study by Bai et al. (2022a) the authors showed that the housing system used (cage, floor) influenced the percentage of the gizzard in body weight. Small ducks reared up to 63 days on the floor were characterized by a higher percentage of the gizzard in body weight compared to the analyzed feature in ducks kept in a cage. In other studies, the percentage of spleen and heart in 91-day-old Sudanese ducks was influenced by the applied nutrition (Shazly et al., 2021). Ducks that received the highest amount of protein and phosphorus in their diet (1.15 % Ca: 0.50 % P) were characterized by the lowest percentage of the above-mentioned internal organs compared to ducks fed a diet based on a lower amount of Ca and P (0.70 % Ca: 0.35 % P).

From the point of view of digestion and absorption of nutrients in the digestive tract, its length is of crucial importance. In our studies, we observed that ST5 ducks were characterized by a longer entire digestive tract, in particular the jejunum and caeca. Kokoszyński et al., 2018 also showed the influence of the duck genotype from the genetic reserve on the length of individual intestinal sections (duodenum, jejunum, ileum). However, in other studies, no effect of genotype on the length of individual intestinal sections was observed in ducks after laying (Włodarczyk et al., 2023). Wasilewski et al. (2015) showed that 7-week-old heavy SM3 broilers were characterized by a shorter entire digestive tract and a smaller ratio of the length of the entire intestine to the body length compared to AF51 (two-strain crosses, A55 male x F11 female) ducks. In the presented results, only the length of the entire digestive tract was longer in females, while sex did not affect the remaining lengths. In other studies, the same relationship was observed in SM3 Heavy ducks, while in AF51 Heavy ducks, males were characterized by a longer entire digestive tract compared to females (Wasilewski et al., 2015). In our study, we did not observe significant differences between genotypes in the analyzed features of the diameters of individual intestinal sections, and other authors also reached the same observations (Wasilewski et al., 2015). On the other hand, Włodarczyk et al. (2023) showed that Dworka ducks were characterized by a larger caeca diameter regardless of sex compared to Pekin ducks after the laying period (113 weeks). In our study, we only observed that females were characterized by a larger duodenum and caeca diameter compared to males. The same relationship (duodenum diameter) was also observed by other authors (Włodarczyk et al., 2023).

In our study, we observed that the genotype of ducks did not affect the dimensions of the tibia and femur. However, other authors Włodarczyk et al. (2023) comparing Pekin ducks and Dworka ducks showed the effect of genotype on the dimensions of GL, AL, and DD of the tibia and GL and ML of the femur. The above dimensions of the tibia and femur were significantly (P < 0.05) longer in the Dworka duck at 113 weeks of age. Kokoszyński et al. (2019) observed that Pekin ducks fed with the addition of whole wheat grain were characterized by smaller dimensions of GL, ML, GC, and GE of the femur and DD of the tibia with the analyzed bone traits in ducks from the control group. In our study, we showed a significant effect of sex, regardless of genotype, on the dimensions of GL, ML, GB, GD, SM, and GC of the femur and GL, AL, GD, SB, and SD of the tibia. Males were characterized by larger dimensions of the analyzed features. Most likely, the obtained results were influenced by the final body weight, which was significantly higher, especially in ST5 Heavy drakes compared to ducks. In general, it can be assumed that the obtained results were influenced by weight dimorphism. Other authors also drew very similar conclusions about Muscovy ducks in the 14th week of life (Krunt et al., 2022). The length and width of the tibia and femur were longer in Muscovy males compared to females, while the housing system did not affect the analyzed bone features (Krunt et al., 2022).

In conclusion, the ducks after the reproductive period are characterized by high slaughter efficiency, especially Cherry Valley ducks (SM3 Heavy), by better musculature (breast muscles), but also by more favorable chemical composition (protein, fat, water) and physical properties (acidity, electrical conductivity, thermal leakage) of the muscles. The breast muscles of Cherry Valley ducks are more elastic, cohesive, and viscous, and are also characterized by a larger cross-sectional area of the fibers and the vertical and horizontal diameter of the fibers. Orvia ducks were characterized by longer some sections of the digestive tract (jejunum), which certainly has a positive effect on the absorption of nutrients.

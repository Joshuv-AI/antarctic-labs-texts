Holographic Extended Thermodynamics of deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black hole
Kamal L. Panigrahi, Balbeer Singh

Abstract
We investigate the thermodynamics and phase structure of deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black hole, generated via the gravitational decoupling (GD) method as a minimal geometric deformation. In the bulk canonical ensemble, our results exhibit a van der Waals-type first-order phase transition in addition to the Hawking-Page transition, in the suitable parameter regime. Further, we compute the critical exponents characterizing the bulk transition, confirming their consistency with mean-field theory predictions. Exploiting the exact holographic dictionary between extended black hole thermodynamics and the dual conformal field theory (CFT), we extend this analysis to the boundary and uncover a rich array of phase transitions and critical phenomena across three distinct thermodynamic ensembles. In particular, for an ensemble of fixed volume and central charge, the dual CFT displays a van der Waals-like phase structure. Throughout, we emphasize the pivotal influence of the GD deformation parameter on the thermodynamic behavior, and we elucidate its role in the confinement-deconfinement transitions characteristic of the deformed AdS-Schwarzschild geometry.

Extended bulk thermodynamics

2.1
Thermodynamic quantities

We begin by reviewing the thermodynamic properties of the deformed AdS–Schwarzschild black hole. Imposing the horizon condition
F(r_{h})=0

\displaystyle M=\frac{1}{6}\left[3r_{h}+\frac{3r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\alpha\frac{\beta^{2}+3r_{h}^{2}+3\beta r_{h}}{(\beta+r_{h})^{3}}\right].

(11)

The entropy

\displaystyle S=\frac{A}{4l_{\text{P}}^{2}}=\pi r_{h}^{2},

(12)

The corresponding expression for the Hawking temperature is given by

\displaystyle T_{H}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{F^{\prime}(r)}{4\pi}\right|_{r=r_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12\pi r_{h}^{2}}\Big[\frac{6r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}-\frac{\alpha\left(\beta^{3}+4\beta^{2}r_{h}+6\beta r_{h}^{2}+6r_{h}^{3}\right)}{\left(\beta+r_{h}\right){}^{4}}+6M\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1+r_{h}^{2}\left(\frac{3}{l^{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+r_{h})^{4}}\right)}{4\pi r_{h}}.

(13)

The pressure associated with the AdS background, defined in terms of the (negative) cosmological constant, takes the form

\displaystyle P=\frac{3}{8\pi l^{2}},

(14)

therefore,

\displaystyle P=\frac{4\pi r_{h}T_{H}-1+\frac{\alpha r_{h}^{2}}{(\beta+r_{h})^{4}}}{8\pi r_{h}^{2}}.

Therefore, the physical pressure and physical temperature are given by

\displaystyle[\text{Press}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{l_{P}^{2}}P

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar c}{l_{P}^{2}}\frac{1}{2r_{h}}T_{H}+..

\displaystyle=k\frac{1}{l_{P}^{2}2r_{h}}[\text{Temp}]+...

(19)

In this section, we compute the critical exponents

To compute the universal exponent
\alpha
, we utilize the entropy relation given in Eq. (
12
). Since the entropy is independent of temperature near the critical point, it follows that
\alpha=0
.
The computation of the universal critical exponents for the RN-AdS black hole, corresponding to the case with control parameter
\beta=0
, has already been carried out in ref.
Kubiznak:2012wp
.
However, the case with both parameters
\alpha
and
\beta

We know for vdW phase, the equation of state is given by

\displaystyle P=\frac{T}{v-b}-\frac{a}{v^{2}},

(29)

where
v=V/N
is the specific volume and
N
is the number of constituent particles. The critical point is located by imposing the conditions
\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial v}\right)_{T}=0
and
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial v^{2}}\right)_{T}=0
. Solving these yields

P_{c}=\frac{a}{27b^{2}},\quad v_{c}=3b,\quad T_{c}=\frac{8a}{27b}.

(30)

Introducing reduced variables,

\hat{P}=\frac{P}{P_{c}},\quad\hat{v}=\frac{v}{v_{c}},\quad\hat{T}=\frac{T}{T_{c}},

the equation of state becomes

\hat{P}=\frac{8\hat{T}}{3\hat{v}-1}-\frac{3}{\hat{v}^{2}}.

(31)

Defining reduced number density
\hat{n}=1/\hat{v}
, Eq. (
31

\hat{P}=\frac{8\hat{T}\hat{n}}{3-\hat{n}}-3\hat{n}^{2}.

(32)

To investigate critical behaviour, we introduce small deviations from the critical point:

t=\hat{T}-1,\quad\omega=\hat{v}-1,\quad\pi=\hat{P}-1.

(33)

Near the critical point, the difference in number density between the coexisting gas and liquid phases behaves as

\Delta n=n_{g}-n_{l}\propto(-t)^{\beta},\quad t<0,

(34)

In Fig.

\ln(1-\frac{T}{T_{c}})

\Delta n\sim(1-\hat{T})

3
Extended boundary thermodynamics

In the framework of the AdS/CFT correspondence, a bulk geometry in the AdS spacetime is associated with a conformal boundary that resides in one lower spacetime dimension. The metric of the CFT is identified with the boundary metric of the corresponding asymptotically AdS spacetime, up to a Weyl rescaling factor, as established in
Witten:1998qj
;
Gubser:1998bc
. By considering the asymptotic limit
r\xrightarrow{}\infty
, the bulk metric given in Eq (
1
) simplifies and takes the following form:

\displaystyle ds^{2}\approx-\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{r^{2}}dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right).

(39)

To extract the boundary metric, we use a coordinate transformation
z=\frac{l^{2}}{r}
, so
r=\frac{l^{2}}{z}
, so that as
r\to\infty
,
z\to 0
. Thus, the metric (
39
) becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{l^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(-dt^{2}+dz^{2}+l^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})\right).

(40)

Following the AdS/CFT prescription, the boundary metric is obtained by rescaling by
\left(\frac{z}{l}\right)^{2}
:

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{boundary}}=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z^{2}}{l^{2}}ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+l^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(41)

Applying the Weyl scale factor
\lambda
=
\frac{\omega z}{l}
,
the CFT metric is the Einstein static universe (
\mathbf{R}\times S^{2}
), so up to the constant (Weyl factor),
\omega=\frac{R}{l}
(where
R
is the curvature radius of the manifold where the CFT lives), we obtain

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\omega^{2}\left(-dt^{2}+l^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right)\right).

(42)

which is the standard AdS-Schwarzschild CFT metric, indicating that the deformation does not alter the boundary geometry. In the subsequent sections, we begin by determining the thermodynamic quantities associated with the dual CFT states corresponding to the bulk geometry. This will be followed by an analysis of the phase transition behaviour exhibited within the dual CFT.

3.1
Thermodynamic quantities for dual CFT

The bulk AdS expressions (
11
)–(
13
), in conjunction with the holographic dictionary (
5
) and (
9
), yield insights into the (extended) thermodynamical properties of the dual large-
N
, strongly coupled CFT.
We introduce the following dimensionless parameters:

x=\frac{r_{h}}{l},\quad\tilde{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha}{l^{2}},\quad b=\frac{\beta}{l}.

With these redefinitions, the thermodynamical quantities in the dual CFT can be expressed as:

•

Entropy:

S=4\pi C\,x^{2},

(43)

•

Energy:

E=\frac{2Cx}{R}\left(1+x^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}(b^{2}+3x^{2}+3bx)}{3x(b+x)^{3}}\right),

(44)

•

Temperature:

T=\frac{1+x^{2}\left(3-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{(b+x)^{4}}\right)}{4\pi xR},

(45)

•

Chemical potential:

\mu=\frac{x}{R}\left(1-x^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}x^{2}}{(b+x)^{4}}+\frac{2\tilde{\alpha}(b^{2}+3bx+3x^{2})}{3x(b+x)^{3}}\right).

(46)

The presence of the
1/R
dependence in the thermodynamic expressions is a direct consequence of the scale invariance inherent in conformal field theories. Moreover, the proportionality with the central charge
C
in Eqs. (
43
)–(
44
) reflects the analysis being carried out in the large-
C
(or large-
N
) limit of the dual conformal field theory. In the subsequent section, we examine the phase structure corresponding to various thermodynamic ensembles in the boundary theory.

3.2
Thermodynamic ensembles in the dual CFT

In this section, we investigate the phase structure of various thermodynamic ensembles in the dual CFT, corresponding to thermal states holographically dual to deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black holes. The deformation parameter
\alpha
plays a subtle role and requires a careful interpretation as also discussed in Refs.
Khosravipoor:2023jsl
;
Gogoi:2025rcn
;
Avalos:2023ywb
.
In principle, there exist four distinct thermodynamic ensembles in the boundary CFT, as one may independently choose between two pairs of conjugate thermodynamic variables, namely:

\displaystyle\text{Canonical ensemble, fixed }(\mathcal{V},C):\quad

\displaystyle F\equiv E-TS,

\displaystyle\text{Fixed }(p,C):\quad

\displaystyle G\equiv E-TS+p\mathcal{V},

\displaystyle\text{Fixed }(p,\mu):\quad

\displaystyle\Phi\equiv E-TS+p\mathcal{V}-\mu C,

\displaystyle\text{Fixed }(\mathcal{V},\mu):\quad

\displaystyle\Omega\equiv E-TS-\mu C.

The final ensemble represents a trivial case, as it follows directly from the Euler relation given in Eq. (
8
). In this work, our focus will be on the remaining three ensembles, each of which exhibits nontrivial and rich phase structure. For each ensemble, we also examine the corresponding heat capacity, which serves as a diagnostic tool for assessing the thermodynamic stability of the system.

Conclusions and Discussions

In this work, we have investigated the thermodynamic phase transition of the deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black hole which is obtained from the gravitational decoupling (GD) method
Khosravipoor:2023jsl
, and followed by the study of phase transition of the thermal CFT states dual to the deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black hole in an extended phase space- treating the cosmological constant and its conjugate quantity as thermodynamic variables associated with the pressure and volume, respectively.

On the bulk side, the thermodynamic behaviour of the deformed AdS-Schwarzschild black hole is critically regulated by the deformation parameter
\alpha
in a canonical ensemble, with distinct phase transitions emerging under specific conditions. When the control parameter
\beta\neq 0
, numerical analysis reveals two critical values,
\alpha_{c_{1}}
and
\alpha_{c_{2}}
, bounding an interval
\alpha\in(\alpha_{c_{1}},\alpha_{c_{2}})
where the system exhibits a first-order van der Waals (vdW) phase transition. This is characterized by a non-monotonic temperature profile (Fig.
1
, left panel) and the canonical swallowtail structure in the
P
–
v
diagram (Fig.
2
). Outside this interval (
\alpha<\alpha_{c_{1}}
or
\alpha>\alpha_{c_{2}}
), no vdW transition is observed.
Moreover, when the deformation is turned off, we observe no vdW phase structure (Fig.
3
, right panel). In the limit
\beta=0
, the solution reduces to a charged AdS black hole, where
\alpha
acts as the square of the electric charge (
\alpha\propto Q^{2}
). Here, a single critical value
\alpha_{c}=6.25
triggers a standard vdW transition (Fig.
3
, left panel), consistent with the Reissner-Nordström-AdS universality class. Conversely, when
\alpha<\alpha_{c_{1}}
(with
\beta\neq 0
), the system undergoes a Hawking-Page-type transition between thermal radiation and black hole phases (Fig.
6
). Near criticality, the critical exponents universally satisfy
\alpha=0
,
\beta=1/2
,
\gamma=1
,
\delta=3
, matching mean-field theory predictions for vdW system.

In the dual boundary side, we first obtain the various thermodynamical quantities on the dual CFT states by assuming the variation of the cosmological constant. At the same time we allow the central charge
C
to vary.
The boundary thermodynamics of the dual conformal field theory exhibits distinctive phase behaviour governed by the dimensionless deformation parameter
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha/l^{2}
. In the fixed-volume and central-charge ensemble
(\mathcal{V},C)
, a Hawking-Page transition occurs when
\tilde{\alpha}<\tilde{\alpha}_{c}
for fixed
b=\beta/l
, manifesting as characteristic feature in free energy
F
(Fig.
12
). Increasing
\tilde{\alpha}
elevates both
F
and the Hawking temperature
T_{\rm HP}
(Fig.
12
, right panel), while increasing
b
suppresses these quantities (Fig.
12
, left panel). In the undeformed limit (
\tilde{\alpha}\to 0
), we recover the standard AdS-Schwarzschild result
T_{\rm HP}=(\pi R)^{-1}
. Notably, other ensembles show no conventional phase transitions: The ensemble
(p,C)
exhibits no van der Waals-like behavior in Gibbs free energy
G
(Figs.
16
–
17
), with stable phases confined exclusively to intermediate-entropy branches, while the ensemble
(p,\mu)
does not show any interesting thermodynamic behavior in thermodynamic potential
\Phi=p\mathcal{V}
(Fig.
18
).

From the bulk perspective, the deformation parameter
\alpha
directly modulates gravitational geometry, creating a rich phase structure.
This bulk behaviour holographically maps to confinement/deconfinement transitions in the boundary
(\mathcal{V},C)
ensemble, where
\tilde{\alpha}<\tilde{\alpha}_{c}
triggers deconfinement (Fig.
12
or
13
).
Here the GD deformation introduces new scales (
\alpha,\beta
) that independently control confinement regimes.
Crucially, the deformation induces a mass gap
in the dual theory spectrum, breaking conformal invariance and generating confinement dynamically through the
\beta
-regulated singularity avoidance at
r=0
.
Such GD couplings represent controlled departures from Einstein gravity that preserve (weak) energy conditions while enriching phase diagrams. Future work should explore higher-dimensional generalizations, particularly since critical exponents exhibit
d
-dependent scaling in analogous deformations
Gheorghiu:2013jha
;
Estrada:2018vrl
, suggesting dimensionally tunable phase structures. An interesting direction for future work involves examining other versions of gravitational decoupling approaches
Maurya:2022wwa
;
Brown:2017wpl
and their potential impact on extended holographic thermodynamics.

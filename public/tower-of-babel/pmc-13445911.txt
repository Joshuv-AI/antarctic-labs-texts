Prognostic value of 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters in patients with non–small cell lung cancer who achieved a pathological complete response after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy
Authors: Ye Tao, Rui Guo, Xiang Li, Xinrun Cui, Yaqi Wang, Haoxuan Du, Zengjin Wen, Shi Yan, Nan Li, Nan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough pathological complete response (pCR) is generally associated with favorable survival outcomes, 10–15% of patients who achieve pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy still relapse within 3 years. This study aimed to explore the prognostic value of clinical features and metabolic parameters derived from 18-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT, hereafter referred to as PET/CT).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 121 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with confirmed pCR of both primary tumors and lymph nodes after receiving neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy at Peking University Cancer Hospital. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression were used to identify prognostic factors, including clinical features, baseline (pre-neoadjuvant) and second (post-neoadjuvant) metabolic parameters on PET/CT—maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), peak standardized uptake value (SUVpeak), metabolic tumor volume (MTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG)—as well as their percentage changes prior to surgery.ResultsDuring a median follow-up period of 26 months, 11 patients experienced recurrence or metastasis. The 1-, 2-, and 3-year disease-free survival (DFS) rates were 98%, 92%, and 88%, respectively. PET-defined N2 (HR = 10.32; 95%CI: 1.90–55.99; p = 0.007), squamous cell carcinoma histology (HR = 0.27; 95%CI: 0.07–0.97; p = 0.05), and baseline MTV (HR = 1.02; 95%CI: 1.01–1.03; p = 0.01) were all correlated with DFS among NSCLC patients attaining pCR. When metabolic parameters were dichotomized into high and low groups, those pCR patients with higher metabolic activity had significantly shorter DFS than those with lower activity did. The corresponding hazard ratios for baseline PET/CT parameters were as follows: SUVpeak, 10.17 (95% CI: 1.3–79.51); MTV, 4.26 (95% CI: 1.13–16.1); and TLG, 8.43 (95% CI: 1.08–65.9).ConclusionAmong NSCLC patients achieving pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, multivariable Cox regression analyses revealed that elevated baseline MTV and PET/CT-defined N2 status were independently associated with poor DFS, while squamous histology was associated with more favorable DFS. These findings highlight that even among pCR patients, baseline metabolic, histologic, and nodal staging characteristics retain important prognostic value.Clinical registration numberThis real-world retrospective trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov on April 12th, 2025 (NCT06926179).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-026-01057-1.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer, FDG, PET/CT, Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, Pathological complete response

Background

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Neoadjuvant and perioperative chemoimmunotherapy have transformed the treatment landscape of resectable NSCLC, resulting in a 28% reduction in all-cause mortality [2, 3]. Pathological complete response (pCR) has emerged as a surrogate endpoint for long-term outcomes and is significantly correlated with event-free survival (EFS) [4]. Recent phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have reported pCR rates of 18.1–36.8% following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, accompanied by a significant improvement in EFS (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.40–0.68). However, both RCTs and retrospective studies have shown that 10–15% of patients who achieve a pCR still present with disease recurrence within 3 years after surgery [5, 6], which underscores the importance of identifying high-risk individuals within the heterogeneous group of patients who achieved a pCR.

PET-CT is widely utilized as a reliable staging modality in both the pretreatment and preoperative settings. In the neoadjuvant setting, changes in PET metrics and PET-based radiomic features have been investigated for their associations with pathological response [7, 8] and overall survival (OS) [9, 10]. Its metabolic parameters—including the SUVmax, SUVpeak, MTV, and TLG—reveal the tumor burden and the biological activity of the malignancy. These metrics are also used to identify patients at a high risk for recurrence from those with early-stage NSCLC following resection [11] to locally advanced disease treated with chemoradiotherapy [12]. Typically, elevated metabolic parameters predict poor oncologic outcomes. Nonetheless, the role of metabolic parameters determined by PET/CT in predicting the prognosis of patients who achieve a pCR has not been well established.

Given the recurrence risk among pCR patients, identifying high-risk subgroups remains clinically relevant. In this study, we retrospectively analyzed the data from 121 pCR patients with NSCLC after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, aiming to explore the prognostic value of clinical and metabolic features.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

We retrospectively reviewed patients with NSCLC who underwent neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and R0 tumor resection from December 1, 2019 to December 31, 2023, at Peking University Cancer Hospital (PKUCH). The inclusion criteria were as following: (1) histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC (stage IB–IIIB, AJCC eighth edition), with negative driver mutations (EGFR mutation and ALK rearrangement); (2) PET/CT at PKUCH at baseline; (3) surgical pathology revealing ypT0N0 after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy; (4) received standardized neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy regimen consisting of platinum-based doublet chemotherapy (nab-paclitaxel for squamous cell carcinoma and pemetrexed for adenocarcinoma) in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. The exclusion criteria included the following: (1) stage IV NSCLC; (2) patients with radiologically suspected N3 lymph node metastases and pathological confirmed N3 disease (stage IIIC); (3) unavailable baseline PET/CT; and (4) concurrent or previous primary cancers other than NSCLC.

Assessment of pCR

A pCR was defined as 0% residual viable tumor cells in the primary tumor and the sampled lymph nodes. Two experienced pathologists performed pathological examinations.

Follow-up and outcomes

Patients underwent postoperative follow-up in accordance with a standardized schedule: chest CT, cervical and abdominal ultrasonography, and serum tumor markers were assessed every 3 months during the first 3 years after surgery, every 6 months between years 3 and 5, and annually thereafter. Brain MRI was performed annually.

DFS was defined as the time from surgery to the first occurrence of disease recurrence or death from any cause. Since no patient died during follow-up, overall survival was not analyzed.

PET/CT scanning and image preprocessing

All patients underwent whole-body PET/CT scans 60 ± 10 min after receiving an 18F-FDG dose of 3.7 MBq/kg. Prior to injection, patients fasted for at least 6 h, and their serum glucose levels were ≤ 120 mg/dL. PET/CT scans were conducted using a Siemens Biograph mCT Flow 64 scanner (Siemens, Erlangen, Germany) or a Gemini TF scanner (Philips Medical Systems, The Netherlands) which covered the length from the top of the skull to the mid-thigh. A low-dose CT scan (120 kV, 35 mA, slice 3 mm) was first performed, and the PET acquisition speed was 1.5 mm/s (slice thickness 3 mm, filter: Gaussian, FWHM: 5 mm) or scanning at a total of 9–10 bed positions, with a 90-s acquisition time for each bed position. A Siemens Biograph mCT Flow 64 scanner was used to reconstruct the PET images using three-dimensional iterative reconstruction with the time-of-flight algorithm, and the low-dose CT scans were acquired in CARE Dose 4D mode. The Gemini TF scanner PET reconstruction parameters included the use of a three-dimensional model and the use of the ordered-subcohorts expectation maximization (OSEM) method (two iterations, four subcohorts, 128 × 128 pixels of 5.15 mm). Attenuation corrections of the PET images were performed using data from CT imaging.

A Siemens workstation (Syngo.via VB20, MM Oncology) was used to analyze all the images. Two experienced nuclear medicine practitioners independently reviewed and analyzed the PET/CT, resolving any discrepancies by consensus. Volumes of interest (VOIs) were manually generated for each lesion, and the metabolic parameters (SUVmax, SUVpeak, MTV, and TLG) were automatically calculated. The axial, coronal, and sagittal PET/CT images were qualitatively analyzed by the nuclear medicine physicians. The formula used for the calculation of ΔSUVpeak% was as follows: ΔSUVpeak% = (SUVpeak of scan-1 - SUVpeak of scan-2) / SUVpeak of scan-1 × 100%. In accordance with the positron emission tomography response criteria for solid tumors (PERCIST), the response to neoadjuvant therapy was defined as a complete metabolic response (CMR), partial metabolic response (PMR), progressive metabolic disease (PMD), or stable metabolic disease (SMD).

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables are summarized as counts and percentages, and continuous variables are expressed as the means and interquartile ranges. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to verify the normality of the clinical features and metabolic parameters. On the basis of their distribution characteristics, continuous variables were compared using either a t-test or a Mann–Whitney U test, and categorical variables were evaluated using chi-square (χ²) tests. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify the factors associated with DFS.

The optimal cutoff value of metabolic parameters for DFS stratification was identified on the basis of the Youden index using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, which maximizes the sum of sensitivity and specificity. The C-index was calculated to evaluate the discriminatory ability of metabolic parameters for predicting DFS events.

Log-rank tests, and proportional hazard regressions were conducted to compare the DFS between the groups and estimate the hazard ratio. A two-tailed p-value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. All the statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Results

Patient selection and follow-Up

From a retrospective cohort of 415 patients with NSCLC who underwent neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy followed by R0 resection, 164 (pCR rate: 39.5%) achieved pCR. Among them, we analyzed data from 121 pCR patients (113 males, 8 females; mean age, 61.6 years; range, 45–76 years) for whom baseline PET/CT metrics with available (Fig. 1A). The baseline characteristics was shown in Table 1. Given the limited availability of invasive lymph-node biopsy results (16/30 positive and 14/30 negative), baseline nodal staging was determined according to baseline PET/CT. All 121 patients had stage IB–IIIB NSCLC (AJCC eighth edition), with 75.2% having squamous-cell carcinoma. The baseline clinical stage was IB in 14 (11.6%) patients and IIA-IIB in 36 (29.8%) patients, and the majority (n = 71, 58.6%) presented with clinical stage IIIA–IIIB disease. Baseline N2 disease was present in 47.1% of the patients. All patients completed at least two cycles of platinum-doublet chemotherapy plus an ICI and underwent definitive surgical resection. Most patients underwent lobectomy (n = 112, 92.6%) after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Most patients received Toripalimab (38.8%). Detailed information on the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors is presented in Supplementary Table 1. After neoadjuvant therapy, 63 patients underwent a second PET examination, and representative images from two patients are shown in Fig. 1B. After surgery, 30 pCR patients (30/121, 24.8%) received adjuvant immunotherapy.

As of October 31, 2025, the median follow-up duration was 26.4 months (range, 2.4–68.4 months). The 1-,2- and 3-year DFS rate of pCR patients were 97.5% (94.8%-100%), 92.4% (87.4%-97.7%) and 87.8% (80.2%-96.1%). During follow-up, 11 patients (11/121, 9.1%) experienced recurrence or metastases, of whom six experienced distant bone and brain metastases and five experienced regional recurrences involving the bronchus or mediastinal lymph nodes. Patients who experienced recurrence presented with a more advanced baseline N stage and elevated baseline metabolic parameters including SUVmax, SUVpeak, MTV and TLG.

Univariate and multivariate cox regression analyses

As the baseline PET/CT-defined N2 stage, non-squamous histology and advanced clinical stage were associated with shorter DFS as shown in Fig. 2, we firstly analyzed the differences in baseline metabolic parameters between these subgroups. No significant differences in metabolic parameters were found among the different pathological types (squamous cell carcinoma versus non-squamous cell carcinoma) and baseline N stages (N0/N1 versus N2) as shown in Supplemental Table 2. However, patients with stage IIIA–IIIB disease generally exhibit higher metabolic parameters, including metabolic tumor volume (p < 0.001) and total lesion glycolysis (p < 0.001), compared with patients with stage IB–IIB disease as shown in Supplementary Table 2. Baseline TLG also increased progressively from stage IB to IIA–IIB and IIIA–IIIB (Supplementary Figure 1).

Given the substantial collinearity among metabolic parameters identified by correlation analyses (Supplementary Figure 2), MTV was found to be highly correlated with TLG (r = 0.94), and SUVmax with SUVpeak (r = 0.96). To address multicollinearity, baseline SUVmax and MTV were selected for inclusion in the multivariable Cox regression analysis.

After multivariable Cox regression analysis, PET-defined N stage (N2 vs. N0/N1 HR = 10.32; 95%CI: 1.90–55.99; p = 0.007), histology (squamous cell carcinoma vs. non-squamous histology; HR = 0.27; 95%CI: 0.07–0.97; p = 0.05), and higher baseline MTV (HR = 1.02, 95%CI: 1.01–1.03; p = 0.01) were all independently associated with DFS among pCR patients (Table 2). Sensitivity analyses using univariate and multivariate Cox regression with Firth’s penalty also showed that PET-defined N stage (N2 vs. N0/N1: HR = 8.62; 95%CI: 2.16–52.21; p = 0.001), histology (squamous cell carcinoma vs. non-squamous histology; HR = 0.27; 95%CI: 0.08–0.95; p = 0.04), and higher baseline MTV (HR = 1.02; 95%CI: 1.01–1.03; p = 0.01) were also independently associated with DFS among pCR patients (Supplementary Table 3).

Prognostic value of PET/CT metabolic parameters in patients who achieved a pCR

The optimal cutoff values for the baseline SUVmax, SUVpeak, MTV and TLG were 11.65, 9.46, 21.44, and 85.04, respectively, according to the ROC curves and Youden index shown in Table 3. The ROC curves are shown in Fig. 3. The Kaplan-Meier analyses revealed that compared with pCR patients whose baseline metabolic parameters were lower, pCR patients whose baseline metabolic parameters were higher had significant poorer DFS (Fig. 4). The landmark DFS rates at 1, 2, and 3 years across subgroups stratified by baseline metabolic parameters were summarized in Supplementary Table 4. Patients with lower metabolic parameters demonstrated more favorable outcomes, with 3-year DFS rates of 93.2%–100%, compared to 78.0%–81.4% in those with higher metabolic parameters as shown in Supplementary Table 4. The HR values for the baseline SUVmax, SUVpeak, MTV and TLG were Inf (95%CI, 0–Inf), 10.17 (95%CI, 1.3–79.51), 4.26 (95% CI, 1.13–16.1), 8.43 (95%CI, 1.08–65.9), respectively. We also found that baseline TLG had the best prognostic discrimination ability (C-index = 0.7) among baseline metabolic parameters in predicting DFS events among pCR patients.

Subgroup analysis of DFS in pCR patients receiving perioperative versus neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy

Perioperative chemoimmunotherapy didn’t confer DFS benefit among pCR patients in our cohort (Perioperative vs. Neoadjuvant: HR = 0.33; 95%CI: 0.04–2.56; p = 0.29) as shown in Supplementary Figure 5. We subsequently conducted subgroup analyses stratified by DFS-associated clinical features and baseline metabolic parameters. Among patients with higher baseline TLG, SUVpeak, SUVmax, or PET/CT-defined N2 stage, perioperative chemoimmunotherapy showed a consistent trend toward improved DFS, with hazard ratios of 0.30 (95% CI: 0.04–2.39), 0.58 (95% CI, 0.07–4.64), 0.37 (95% CI: 0.05–2.87), and 0.66 (95% CI: 0.08–5.31), respectively (Fig. 5). Nonetheless, perioperative modality did not confer a statistically significant DFS benefit over neoadjuvant modality in any subgroup.

Discussion

Although pCR after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy is generally associated with a favorable prognosis [4, 13, 14], with the CheckMate 816 trial reporting a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of 96% among pCR patients [2], a subset of patients with pCR experience disease recurrence within 3 years [5, 13], suggesting substantial prognostic heterogeneity. A retrospective study of 130 pCR patients with a median follow-up of 23.3 months reported that no clinical factors significantly associated with relapse risk [15], implying that other distinct intrinsic tumor characteristics underlie the prognostic heterogeneity within the pCR population. Our study suggested that baseline PET-defined N2 stage, non-squamous histology, and elevated MTV were independently associated with worse DFS among NSCLC patients achieving pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.

Metabolic parameters from PET/CT have been extensively investigated as prognostic markers in NSCLC across disease stages. In early-stage NSCLC, metabolic parameters have been shown to stratify prognosis [11, 16], and identify high-risk patients who may benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy [17], whereas in locally advanced disease, they can refine TNM staging [10]. In the context of immunotherapy, Castelllo et al. demonstrated that the MTV is an independent predictor of OS in patients with advanced NSCLC [18], and Wang et al. reported that baseline FDG Ki stratified outcomes in patients receiving induction chemoimmunotherapy and concurrent chemoradiotherapy [19]. Similarly, Grambozov et al. found baseline PET-CT parameters to be prognostic in recurrent NSCLC treated with reirradiation-chemoimmunotherapy [12], and Karolien et al. linked early metabolic responses to improved PFS in stage IV patients receiving immunotherapy [20]. Meta-analyses also confirmed that higher metabolic activity predicts poorer prognosis of advanced NSCLC following immunotherapy [21]. Moreover, the integration of PET-radiomics with genomic profiling has been shown to further improve prognostic stratification [22]. Among the PET-CT metrics, the SUVmax is the most frequently reported parameter; however, limitations have been observed owing to its noise sensitivity and variability across scanners and protocols. SUVpeak, MTV, and TLG also remain limited by challenges in standardization and the definition of the VOI [23]. A higher baseline SUV may reflect more aggressive tumor biology; in a study of 98 patients with small-cell lung cancer, biopsy-based analysis of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes revealed that a high SUVmax was associated with a low cluster of CD8 (+) and CD4 (+) tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, suggesting that elevated metabolic activity primarily reflects tumor cell activity rather than immune infiltration [24]. Further studies are warranted to elucidate the potential mechanisms by which elevated metabolic parameters contribute to poorer DFS.

In patients with resectable NSCLC treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, most studies using metabolic parameters have concentrated on predicting pathological responses (MPR/pCR), whereas few have investigated the utility of these parameters in terms of prognostic heterogeneity. Guo et al. and Chen et al. reported that ΔSULpeak and ΔSULmax are reliable predictors of MPR [7, 25], and Tao et al. reported a significant association between pathological and PERCIST-defined PET responses [26]. Moreover, PET-radiomics outperformed CT-radiomics and conventional metabolic parameters in predicting pCR [27], with further studies showing that incorporating nodal metabolic features with primary tumor metrics improved the predictive accuracy [8]. Previous studies have focused on the prognostic significance of metabolic parameters in the overall population of patients with NSCLC treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, independent of pathological response. A 5-year follow-up study demonstrated that low post-treatment primary tumor and nodal metabolism predict favorable outcomes [9], whereas another study revealed ΔSUVmax as a significant prognostic factor [28]. However, no study to date has specifically addressed the risk stratification of patients who achieve a pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and surgery.

Beyond primary tumor characteristics, lymph node status is also a critical determinant of prognosis in NSCLC. Rahim et al. demonstrated that metastatic involvement of regional lymph nodes impairs CD8⁺ T-cell responses to cancer immunotherapy, highlighting the importance of nodal status in shaping treatment outcomes [29]. Consistently, several studies have shown that the pathological response of lymph nodes provides meaningful prognostic stratification following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy [30, 31]. In addition, PET/CT-defined nodal involvement has been associated with inferior survival in this setting [32]. In addition, Castello et al. demonstrated that lymph node TLG following induction chemotherapy was associated with survival [33]. In line with these findings, our study revealed that PET/CT-defined N2 stage was significantly associated with worse DFS even among patients who achieved a pCR.

Although identifying high-risk pCR patients is clinically meaningful, the optimal adjuvant strategy for this subgroup remains uncertain. Previous studies have indicated that adjuvant immunotherapy may not confer additional benefit for the overall pCR population [5, 34, 35]. In our cohort, the limited number of outcome events and the small proportion of patients receiving adjuvant immunotherapy restricted the statistical power of adjuvant immunotherapy, precluding the development of a prognostic prediction model. These issues merit further investigation in high-risk pCR patients.

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted in a single center and the retrospective design may introduce a selection bias. Second, although PET/CT-derived metabolic parameters reflect tumor burden and glycolytic activity, they cannot fully account for the underlying biological heterogeneity, including genomic alterations, immune contexture, and spatial variations. Furthermore, metabolic parameters were derived from two different PET/CT scanners. Although all images were analyzed using a standardized post-processing workflow on the same workstation, and both scanners underwent routine calibration and quality control procedures, additionally, the relatively large tumor volumes in our cohort may have mitigated the influence of partial volume effects; however, the potential impact of inter-scanner variability cannot be entirely excluded. Future studies are warranted to elucidate the potential mechanisms by which elevated metabolic parameters contribute to poor DFS.

Conclusion

Among NSCLC patients achieving pCR after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, multivariable Cox regression analyses revealed that elevated baseline MTV and PET/CT-defined N2 status were independently associated with poor DFS, while squamous histology was associated with more favorable DFS. These findings highlight that even among pCR patients, baseline metabolic, histologic, and nodal staging characteristics retain important prognostic value.

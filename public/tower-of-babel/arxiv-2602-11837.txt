A family of matrix flows converging to normal matrices
Masaki Izumi

Abstract
The celebrated Antezana-Pujals-Stojanoff Theorem states that the iterated Aluthge transforms of an arbitrary matrix converge to a normal matrix. We introduce a family of matrix flows that share this convergence property by defining them through ordinary differential equations. The family includes a continuous analogue of the Aluthge transform, as well as a differential equation discussed by Haagerup in the context of II 1 factors. We also examine the same type of flows in the setting of Hilbert space operators equipped with unitarily invariant norms.



1
Introduction

This work stems from the study of two examples of operator transformations.
One of them is the Aluthge transform introduced in
[
2
]
.
Throughout the paper, we denote the algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space
H
by
\mathbb{B}(H)
.
For
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
, let
T=U|T|
be its polar decomposition.
Then the Aluthge transform of
T
, denoted by
\Delta(T)
, is defined by
|T|^{1/2}U|T|^{1/2}
.
Thanks to Antezana-Pujals-Stojanoff’s work
[
5
, Theorem 4.12]
,
the iterated Aluthge transforms of any matrix converge to a normal matrix.
For a parameter
0<\lambda<1
, one can similarly define the
\lambda
-Aluthge transform

\Delta_{\lambda}(T)=|T|^{\lambda}U|T|^{1-\lambda},

and the convergence result holds for
\Delta_{\lambda}
as well (see
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
).
For a left invertible
T
, we have

\Delta_{\lambda}(T)=e^{\lambda\log|T|}Te^{-\lambda\log|T|}=T+\lambda[\log|T|,T]+O(\lambda^{2}),

as
\lambda
tends to 0.
Thus it is natural to consider the following ODE as a continuous analogue of the Aluthge transform:

\frac{dX(t)}{dt}=[\log|X(t)|,X(t)],\quad X(0)=T.

(1.1)

In fact, we can show that
\{\Delta_{t/n}^{n}(T)\}_{n}
converges to
X(t)
in the operator norm
(see Proposition
A.1
).

The second example comes from an ODE introduced by Haagerup in his series of lectures
at MSRI in 2001 (see
[
12
, Section 2]
for a published account).
For an element
T
in a II
1
factor
M
, he considered the following ODE, which we call
the
Haagerup equation
:

\frac{dX(t)}{dt}=[[X(t)^{*},X(t)],X(t)],\quad X(0)=T.

(1.2)

He showed that a unique global solution exists on
[0,\infty)
, and that it remains in the similarity orbit

\mathcal{O}_{M}(T)
of
T
in
M
.
Moreover, the flow
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
converges to a normal element in the
*
distribution sense.
The above ODE itself makes sense in
\mathbb{B}(H)
, and Haagerup’s argument works for any finite von Neumann algebras
—in particular, for matrix algebras.
It turns out that
X(t)
in the matrix case is precisely the gradient flow for
the energy function
\Tr([X^{*},X]^{2})/4
(see Proposition
A.2
).
A similarity between the Aluthge transform and the Haagerup equation was
already observed in
[
10
]
.

These two examples can be unified in the following framework.
Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of continuous increasing functions on a finite closed
interval
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
such that
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
is strictly increasing.
For
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
with
\sigma(|T|),\sigma(|T^{*}|)\subset[a,b]
, we set

\varphi(T)=\varphi_{1}(|T|)-\varphi_{2}(|T^{*}|),

where
\sigma(S)
denotes the spectrum of an operator
S
.
The main subject of this paper is the solution of the following ODE:

\frac{dX(t)}{dt}=[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)],\quad X(0)=T.

(1.3)

Note that
(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(x))=(\log x,0)
in the first example, and

(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(x))=(x^{2},x^{2})
in the second.

In the matrix case, it turns out that the usual Lipschitz condition on
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}

assures not only a unique local solution, but a unique global solution on
[0,\infty)

(see Theorem
2.7
), and the flow
F^{\varphi}_{t}(T):=X(t)
is well-defined.
The solution curve
\{F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)\}_{t\geq 0}
stays in the similarity orbit
\mathcal{O}(T)
of
T

because
[\varphi(X),X]
defines a vector field on a subset of the manifold
\mathcal{O}(T)
, and

\{F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)\}_{t\geq 0}
is its integral curve.
One of the main purposes of this paper is to show that the flow
\{F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)\}_{t\geq 0}
converges to a normal matrix for
any matrix
T
under certain regularity assumptions of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
—
assumptions satisfied in both of the above examples (see Theorem
3.7
, Theorem
3.10
, Theorem
3.11
, and
Theorem
3.13
for the precise assumptions).

To show the convergence results, we have an advantage of our continuous systems over
the iterated Aluthge transforms: we can separate the dynamics into that of
upper triangular matrices and that of unitary matrices.
(Thanks to the unitary invariance of Eq.(
1.3
), we may always assume that the initial value

T
is upper triangular).
In fact, unlike in
[
4
]
, we do not need the sophisticated theory of dynamical systems;
instead, ordinary Lyapunov analysis together with matrix analysis suffices.

This paper is organized as follows.
We discuss only matrices up to Section 3, while general bounded operators on Hilbert spaces are treated
only in Section 4.
This arrangement is intended to allow readers interested only in the matrix case to follow the paper easily.

In Section 2, we set up the basic assumptions.
Following Haagerup’s argument, we show that the operator norm of
X(t)
is decreasing as long as
the solution exists.
This, together with the Lipschitz condition, guarantees the existence of a unique global solution.

In Section 3, we first show exponential convergence under the
C^{1}
-condition on
\varphi
for a diagonalizable initial value
by a simple Lyapunov function argument (Theorem
3.7
).
Note that in the case of iterated Aluthge transforms, the corresponding result was already established in
[
4
]
.
An intuitive explanation of these two phenomena is straightforward:
the convergence occurs within the similarity orbit of the initial value if and only if the initial value is diagonalizable.
To obtain more general convergence results, we require a more refined estimate of the Lyapunov function.
For this purpose, we use interpolation polynomials in Theorem
3.10
, holomorphic functional calculus
in Theorem
3.11
, and a combination of both in Theorem
3.13
.
Each case requires a different regularity assumption.

In Section 4, we discuss general Hilbert space operators equipped with a unitarily invariant norm.
More precisely, we consider solutions of Eq.(
1.3
) with
X(t)-T
in a symmetric normed ideal
under the condition that
[\varphi(T),T]
belongs to the same ideal.
We have no convergence theorem in the infinite dimensional case, and the main goal of this section is to prove
the existence of a unique global solution under an appropriate Lipschitz condition.
This again follows from the fact that the operator norm of the solution is decreasing, but the Haagerup argument
no longer applies.
Instead, we take an alternative approach using the Dini derivative.
We show both convergent examples and non-convergent ones, and discuss several questions.

Throughout the paper, for a matrix or a bounded operator
T
, the symbol
\|T\|
denotes its operator norm.
The Schatten
p
-norm is denoted by
\|T\|_{p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
For matrices, we mainly use the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and the operator norm.
We denote by
\sigma(T)
the spectrum of
T
, and by
r(T)
the spectral radius of
T
.
The similarity orbit of
T
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}(T)
in the matrix case.
For Hilbert space operators, there is an issue with the choice of
a subgroup of the group of bounded invertible operators
\mathbb{B}(H)^{-1}
to define the similarity orbit of
T
,
and we therefore use a more descriptive notation in Section 4.

2
The basic properties of the solutions

For the basics of matrix analysis, the reader is referred to
[
6
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
18
]
.

We denote by
\mathbb{M}_{n}
the set of
n
-by-
n
complex matrices, and by
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
the group of its invertible elements.
For
T
, we denote its singular numbers by
\{s_{i}(T)\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a pair of continuous functions
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
defined
on a finite closed interval
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
, we set up the following conditions:

(C0)

\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are increasing, and
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}

is strictly increasing.

(CL)

In addition to (C0), we assume
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\Lip[a,b]
, where

\Lip[a,b]
is the set of Lipschitz functions on
[a,b]
.

(C1)

In addition to (CL), we assume
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C^{1}[a,b]
and

\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(x)+\varphi_{2}^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in[a,b]\setminus\{0\}
.

We set

\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]=\{X\in\mathbb{M}_{n};\;\sigma(|X|)\subset[a,b]\},

and
\varphi(X)=\varphi_{1}(|X|)-\varphi_{2}(|X^{*}|)
for
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Note that
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
implies
\sigma(|X^{*}|)\subset[a,b]
for matrices.

Remark 2.2
.

The map
\varphi:\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]\to\mathbb{M}_{n}
is continuous under the condition (C0), which can be
shown by polynomial approximations.
Furthermore, it is Lipschitz continuous under the condition (CL), which follows from

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}|T|&0\\
0&|T^{*}|\end{array}\right)=\left|\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&T^{*}\\
T&0\end{array}\right)\right|

and
[
1
, Theorem 3.5.1]
.

The next lemma follows from the rearrangement inequality.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a self-adjoint matrix, and let
f
be an increasing function on
\sigma(A)
.
Let
U\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a unitary matrix.
Then

\Tr(f(A)(A-UAU^{*}))\geq 0,

and equality holds if and only if
U
commutes with
f(A)
.

Proof.

We choose an orthonormal basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
consisting of eigenvectors of
A

with
Av_{i}=\lambda_{i}v_{i}
, and assume
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle\Tr(f(A)(A-UAU^{*}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{i}-\langle{UAU^{*}v_{i},v_{i}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}|\langle{Uv_{j},v_{i}}\rangle|^{2}).

Since
(|\langle{Uv_{j},v_{i}}\rangle|^{2})_{ij}
is a doubly stochastic matrix, it is a convex combination
of permutation matrices, and there exists
c:S_{n}\to[0,1]
with

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c(\sigma)=1,

|\langle{Uv_{j},v_{i}}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c(\sigma)\delta_{i,\sigma(j)},

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group (see
[
6
, Theorem II.2.3]
).
Thus

\Tr(f(A)(A-UAU^{*}))=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c(\sigma)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}(f(\lambda_{j})-f(\lambda_{\sigma(j)})).

Letting

\mu_{j}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1},&1\leq j\leq n-1,\\
\lambda_{n},&j=n,\end{cases}

we get

\Tr(f(A)(A-UAU^{*}))=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}c(\sigma)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(f(\lambda_{j})-f(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}))\geq 0.

Assume that equality holds.
We claim that if there exists
i
with
f(\lambda_{\sigma(i)})<f(\lambda_{i})
, we get
c(\sigma)=0
.
Indeed, for such
i
we choose the largest number
i^{\prime}
with
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i^{\prime}}
.
Then
\mu_{i^{\prime}}=\lambda_{i^{\prime}}-\lambda_{i^{\prime}+1}>0
, and

\mu_{i^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{i^{\prime}}(f(\lambda_{j})-f(\lambda_{\sigma(j)}))>0,

which implies
c(\sigma)=0
.
The claim shows that
c(\sigma)>0
necessitates
f(\lambda_{\sigma(j)})=f(\lambda_{j})
for all
j
,
implying that
U
commutes with
f(A)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (C0),
and assume
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Then

\Tr(\varphi(T)[T^{*},T])\geq 0,

and equality holds if and only if
T
is normal.
In particular
[\varphi(T),T]=0
holds if and only if
T
is normal.

Proof.

Let
T=U|T|
be the polar decomposition.
We may and do assume that
U
is a unitary by adding a partial isometry to
U
if necessary.
Then,

\displaystyle\Tr(\varphi(T)[T^{*},T])

\displaystyle=\Tr(\varphi_{1}(|T|)(|T|^{2}-|T^{*}|^{2})+\varphi_{2}(|T^{*}|)(|T^{*}|^{2}-|T|^{2}))

\displaystyle=\Tr(\varphi_{1}(|T|)(|T|^{2}-U|T|^{2}U^{*})+\varphi_{2}(|T|)(|T|^{2}-U^{*}|T|^{2}U)).

The previous lemma shows
\Tr(\varphi(T)[T^{*},T])\geq 0
, and equality holds if and only if

U
commutes with both
\varphi_{1}(|T|)
and
\varphi_{2}(|T|)
.
Since
\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
is strictly increasing, it is the case if and only if
U
commutes
with
|T|
, which is equivalent to
T
being normal.

The second statement follows from

\Tr(T^{*}[\varphi(T),T])=-\Tr(\varphi(T)[T^{*},T])).

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (C0),
and assume
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Let
X(t)
be a solution of Eq.(
1.3
) defined on
[0,\tau]
.
Then
\|X(t)\|_{2n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|X(t)\|
are decreasing with respect to
t
on
[0,\tau]
.

Proof.

We follow the argument in
[
12
, Section 2]
.
A direct computation with Lemma
2.3
shows

\displaystyle\frac{d\|X(t)\|_{2n}^{2n}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\Tr((X(t)^{*}X(t))^{n})}{dt}

\displaystyle=n\Tr((X(t)^{*}X(t))^{n-1}(X^{\prime}(t)^{*}X(t)+X(t)^{*}X^{\prime}(t)))

\displaystyle=2n\operatorname{Re}\Tr((X(t)^{*}X(t))^{n-1}X(t)^{*}[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)])

\displaystyle=-2n\Tr(\varphi(X(t))(|X(t)|^{2n}-|X(t)^{*}|^{2n}))

\displaystyle=-2n\Tr(\varphi_{1}(|X(t)|)(|X(t)|^{2n}-|X(t)^{*}|^{2n}))

\displaystyle-2n\Tr(\varphi_{2}(|X(t)^{*}|)(|X(t)^{*}|^{2n}-|X(t)|^{2n}))

\displaystyle\leq 0,

and
\|X(t)\|_{2n}
is decreasing on
[0,\tau]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
As

\|X(t)\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|X(t)\|_{2n},

\|X(t)\|
is decreasing too.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (C0),
and assume
a>0
and
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Let
X(t)
be a solution of Eq.(
1.3
) defined on
[0,\tau]
.
Then the last singular number
s_{n}(X(t))
is increasing on
[0,\tau]
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi^{\sim}=(-\varphi_{2}(x^{-1}),-\varphi_{1}(x^{-1}))
.
Then
X(t)^{-1}
is a solution of Eq.(
1.3
) with
\varphi^{\sim}
in place of
\varphi

and
T^{-1}
in place of
T
.
Thus
s_{n}(X(t))=\|X(t)^{-1}\|^{-1}
is increasing on
[0,\tau]
.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (CL),
and assume
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Then a unique global solution of Eq.(
1.3
) on
[0,\infty)
exists.

Proof.

First, assume
a>0
.
We choose and fix
0<\varepsilon<a
, and extend
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
to
[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]

such that they satisfy the condition (CL) for
[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]
.
Let
L
be the Lipschitz constant of
[\varphi(X),X]
on
\mathbb{M}_{n}[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]
,
and let

M=\sup\{\|[\varphi(X),X]\|;\;X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]\}.

Then
T_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
with
\|T-T_{1}\|<\varepsilon
assures
\sigma(|T_{1}|)\subset(a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon)

since

|s_{k}(T_{1})-s_{k}(T)|\leq\|T_{1}-T\|,

holds for all
1\leq k\leq n
.
For such
T_{1}
, we have the estimate

\|[\varphi(T_{1}),T_{1}]\|\leq L\|T_{1}-T\|+\|[\varphi(T),T]\|\leq L\varepsilon+M.

Let
\tau=\varepsilon/(L\varepsilon+M)
.
Then there exists a unique solution of Eq.(
1.3
) on
[0,\tau]

(see
[
25
, Theorem 2.2]
for example).
Thanks to Lemma
2.5
and Corollary
2.6
, we have

X(t)\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.
Thus the solution uniquely extends to
t\in[0,2\tau]
.
Repeating the same argument, we get the desired global solution.

When
a=0
, we can apply the same argument to
[0,b+\varepsilon]
in place of

[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]
by using Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (CL),
and assume
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Let
X(t)
be the unique global solution of Eq.(
1.3
) on
[0,\infty)
.
We define a flow
F^{\varphi}_{t}
in
\mathbb{M}_{n}
by
F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)=X(t)
.

We denote
F^{A}_{t}=F^{(\log x,0)}_{t}
and
F^{H}_{t}=F^{(x^{2},x^{2})}_{t}
, and call them

the Aluthge flow
and
the Haagerup flow
respectively.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (CL),
and assume
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
Then the flow
\{F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)\}_{t\geq 0}
stays in the similarity orbit
\mathcal{O}(T)
of
T
.

Proof.

Note that we have
[\varphi(X),X]\in T_{X}\mathcal{O}(T)
for
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]\cap\mathcal{O}(T)
as

[\varphi(X),X]=\frac{d}{ds}e^{s\varphi(X)}Xe^{-s\varphi(X)}|_{s=0},

where
T_{X}\mathcal{O}(T)
denotes the tangent space of
\mathcal{O}(T)
at
X
.
Thus the uniqueness of the solution yields that the flow is the integral curve of
the vector field
[\varphi(X),X]
in
\mathcal{O}(T)
.

Or alternatively, we can follow the argument in
[
12
, Section 2]
.
We solve the two linear equations in
\mathbb{M}_{n}
:

\frac{dV(t)}{dt}=-V(t)\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)),\quad V(0)=I,

\frac{dW(t)}{dt}=\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{t}(T))W(t),\quad W(0)=I,

which have unique global solutions.
A direct computation shows

\frac{d(V(t)W(t))}{dt}=0,

\frac{d(V(t)F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)W(t))}{dt}=0,

which imply
V(t)W(t)=I
and
V(t)F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)W(t)=T
.
Thus
F^{\varphi}_{t}(t)=V(t)^{-1}TV(t)
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

The Aluthge flow has a special property with respect to tensor product.
For
T_{i}\in GL(n_{i},\mathbb{C})
,
i=1,2\cdots,m
, we have

\log\left|T_{1}\otimes T_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{m}\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{m}I^{\otimes^{i-1}}\otimes\log|T_{i}|\otimes I^{\otimes^{n-i}}.

Consequently, we have

F^{A}_{t}(T_{1}\otimes T_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{m})=F^{A}_{t}(T_{1})\otimes F^{A}_{t}(T_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes F^{A}_{t}(T_{m}).

For
T\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
, we denote by
T^{\wedge^{k}}
the restriction of
T^{\otimes^{k}}

to the
k
-th antisymmetric tensor product
\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then we get
F_{t}^{A}(T)^{\wedge^{k}}=F_{t}^{A}(T^{\wedge^{k}})
.
Thus Lemma
2.5
shows that
\|F_{t}^{A}(T)^{\wedge^{k}}\|
is decreasing with respect to
t
,
which means that

\prod_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}(F^{A}_{t}(T))

is decreasing for every
1\leq k\leq n
.
When
k=n
, the above product equals
|\det F^{A}_{t}(T)|=|\det T|
since
F^{A}_{t}(T)\in\mathcal{O}(T)
, and it is a constant.
Thus
F^{A}_{t}(T)
is decreasing in the sense of logarithmic majorization (see
[
3
]
).
In particular,
|||F^{A}_{t}(T)|||
is decreasing for every unitarily invariant norm
|||\cdot|||
.

Question 2.11
.

Is
|||F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)|||
decreasing with respect to
t

for any unitarily invariant norm
|||\cdot|||
?
We have already seen in Lemma
2.5
that it is the case for the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{2n}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

3
Convergence results

Since the flow
F^{\varphi}_{t}
satisfies
F^{\varphi}_{t}(UTU^{-1})=UF^{\varphi}_{t}(T)U^{-1}
for any unitary
U\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
,
by the Schur unitary triangulation theorem, we may assume
that the initial value
T
is upper triangular in order to obtain convergence results.
Throughout this section, we fix
\varphi
satisfying the condition (CL)
and fix an upper triangular matrix
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
with
T\in\mathbb{M}_{n}[a,b]
.
We denote by
\Lambda=\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
the diagonal part of
T
.

Our basic idea is to separate the dynamics of
F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)
into that of
upper triangular matrices and that of unitary matrices.
For this purpose, we need to find a unitary flow
U(t)
satisfying
U(t)F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)U(t)^{-1}=\Lambda+Y(t)

with strictly upper triangular
Y(t)
.
Assuming such
U(t)
exists, we would get

\displaystyle\frac{d(U(t)F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)U(t)^{-1})}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{dU(t)}{dt}F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)U(t)^{-1}+U(t)[\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)),F^{\varphi}(T)]U(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle-U(t)F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)U(t)^{-1}\frac{dU(t)}{dt}U(t)^{-1}

\displaystyle=[\frac{dU(t)}{dt}U(t)^{-1}+\varphi(\Lambda+Y(t)),\Lambda+Y(t)].

Since
U(t)
is required to be unitary, the term
\frac{dU(t)}{dt}U(t)^{-1}
should be skew-adjoint.
On the other hand, to keep the commutator at the end strictly upper triangular, the term

\frac{dU(t)}{dt}U(t)^{-1}+\varphi(\Lambda+Y(t))
should be upper triangular.
We can fulfill this requirement by using the additive decomposition of
\varphi(\Lambda+Y)
into
its strictly upper triangular, diagonal, and strictly lower triangular parts.

In the following arguments, we treat
Y=(y_{ij})
as a variable whose value is in the set of
n
-by-
n

strictly upper triangular matrices
\mathbf{N}_{n}
, while we treat
\Lambda
as a fixed diagonal matrix.
We decompose

Lemma 3.6

Let the notation be as above, and assume that
\varphi

Then

\displaystyle\Tr(\varphi(\Lambda+Y)[(\Lambda+Y)^{*},\Lambda+Y])

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j,\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}}\frac{\varphi_{1}^{[1]}(|\lambda_{i}|,|\lambda_{j}|)+\varphi_{2}^{[1]}(|\lambda_{i}|,|\lambda_{j}|)}{|\lambda_{i}|+|\lambda_{j}|}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|^{2}|y_{ij}|^{2}+o(\|Y\|_{2}^{2})

Proof.

A direct computation shows

\displaystyle\Tr(\varphi(\Lambda+Y)[(\Lambda+Y)^{*},\Lambda+Y])

\displaystyle=\Tr(\varphi(\Lambda)([\Lambda^{*},Y]+[Y^{*},\Lambda]+[Y^{*},Y]))

\displaystyle+\Tr((\varphi(\Lambda+Y)-\varphi(\Lambda))([\Lambda^{*},Y]+[Y^{*},\Lambda]+[Y^{*},Y])).

The first term is equal to

\Tr([\varphi(\Lambda),Y^{*}]Y)=\sum_{i<j}\left(\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{i}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{j}|)+\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{i}|)\right)|y_{ij}|^{2}.

\displaystyle\Tr(\varphi(\Lambda+Y)[(\Lambda+Y)^{*},\Lambda+Y])

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}[(\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{i}|))|y_{ij}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left((\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda+Y|)-\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda|))_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\overline{y_{ij}}\right)]

\displaystyle-\sum_{i<j}[(\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{i}|))|y_{ij}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left((\varphi_{2}(|\Lambda^{*}+Y^{*}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\Lambda|))_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\overline{y_{ij}}\right)].

If

(see, for example,
[

[

are

Assume

We further assume

Note that

from our convention.
Let

be the spectral decomposition.
Then we have

\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda+Y|)-\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda|)=\sum_{\nu_{k}\neq|\lambda_{l}|}\frac{\varphi_{1}(\nu_{k})-\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{l}|)}{\nu_{k}^{2}-|\lambda_{l}|^{2}}F_{k}(|\Lambda+Y|^{2}-|\Lambda|^{2})E_{ll}.

(\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda+Y|-\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda)|))_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\varphi^{[1]}_{1}(\mu_{k},|\lambda_{j}|)}{\mu_{k}+|\lambda_{j}|}\langle{F_{k}(|\Lambda+Y|^{2}-|\Lambda|^{2})e_{j},e_{i}}\rangle.

which is continuous on

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda+Y|)-\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda|))_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle{f_{j}(|\Lambda+Y|)(|\Lambda+Y|^{2}-|\Lambda|^{2})e_{j},e_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{f_{j}(|\Lambda+Y|)(\Lambda^{*}Y+Y^{*}\Lambda)e_{j},e_{i}}\rangle+o(\|Y\|_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle{f_{j}(|\Lambda|)(\Lambda^{*}Y+Y^{*}\Lambda)e_{j},e_{i}}\rangle+o(\|Y\|_{2})

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{i}|))|y_{ij}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left((\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda+Y|)-\varphi_{1}(|\Lambda|))_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\overline{y_{ij}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{1}(|\lambda_{i}|)+\frac{\varphi^{[1]}_{1}(|\lambda_{i}|,|\lambda_{j}|)}{|\lambda_{i}|+|\lambda_{j}|}(2|\lambda_{i}|^{2}-\lambda_{i}\overline{\lambda_{j}}-\overline{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{j})\right)|y_{ij}|^{2}+o(\|Y\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle(\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{i}|))|y_{ij}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\left((\varphi_{2}(|\Lambda^{*}+Y^{*}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\Lambda|))_{ij}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\overline{y_{ij}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{j}|)-\varphi_{2}(|\lambda_{i}|)+\frac{\varphi^{[1]}_{2}(|\lambda_{i}|,|\lambda_{j}|)}{|\lambda_{i}|+|\lambda_{j}|}(\lambda_{i}\overline{\lambda_{j}}+\overline{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{j}-2|\lambda_{j}|^{2})\right)|y_{ij}|^{2}+o(\|Y\|_{2}^{2})

Therefore the statement holds.

\displaystyle[\Lambda,\psi_{2}(B(\Lambda))]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}^{[1]}(t_{1}(Y),t_{2}(Y))[\Lambda,B(Y)]_{ij}+o(\|[\Lambda,Y]\|_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi_{2}^{[1]}(|\lambda_{3}|^{2},0)}{|\lambda_{3}|}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\overline{\lambda_{j}}y_{ij}+o(\|[\Lambda,Y]\|_{2}).

∎

We still do not know if the convergence holds in general for
\varphi(X)=|X|
.
The first test case is

,
m^{a}_{1}=3
, and
m^{g}_{1}=1
.
In this case, we get the following formula from Proposition

\displaystyle[\Lambda,|\Lambda+Y|]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(s_{1}+s_{2})(s_{2}+s_{3})(s_{3}+s_{1})}[\Lambda,A(Y)^{2}-\Tr(A(Y))A(Y)]

\displaystyle+\frac{s_{1}s_{2}+s_{2}s_{3}+s_{3}s_{1}}{(s_{1}+s_{2})(s_{2}+s_{3})(s_{3}+s_{1})}[\Lambda,A(Y)],

where

and

with

.
Since

we encounter difficulties in estimating the first term.
This example suggests that for further analysis, we need a lower estimate of the singular numbers

that approach 0 as

tends to 0.

4
The case of Hilbert space operators

Throughout this section, we assume that

We denote by
\mathbb{B}(H)_{\mathrm{sa}}
the set of self-adjoint operators in
\mathbb{B}(H)
.
For

We first generalize Lemma
2.5
by using the Dini derivatives.
For

, we set

Note that

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
be a pair of functions satisfying the condition (C0), and
let
T\in L\mathbb{B}(H)[a,b]\cap R\mathbb{B}(H)[a,b]
.
Let
X(t)
be a solution of Eq.(
1.3
) in
\mathbb{B}(H)
defined on
t\in[0,\tau]
, where the derivative
is taken with respect to the operator norm.
Then
\|X(t)\|
is decreasing with respect to
t
on
[0,\tau]
.
If moreover
a>0
, then
m(X(t))
is increasing.

Proof.

Note that we implicitly assume that
X(t)\in L\mathbb{B}(H)[a,b]\cap R\mathbb{B}(H)[a,b]

for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.
Let
0\leq s<t\leq\tau
, and let
x\in H
be a unit vector.
Then since

\displaystyle\|X(s)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|X(t)x-(X(t)x-X(s)x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|X(t)x\|^{2}-2\operatorname{Re}\langle{(X(t)-X(s))x,X(t)x}\rangle+\|(X(t)-X(s))x\|^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\|X(t)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|X(s)\|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{s}^{t}\langle{[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]x,X(t)x}\rangle dr

\displaystyle\leq\|X(s)\|^{2}+2(t-s)\operatorname{Re}\langle{[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]x,X(t)x}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\|X(t)\|\int_{s}^{t}\|[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]-[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]\|dr.

Since
\|X(t)\|\in\sigma(|X(t)|)
, there exists a sequence of unit vectors
x_{n}\in H
such that

\{|X(t)|x_{n}-\|X(t)\|x_{n}\}_{n}
converges to 0.
Then
\{\|X(t)x_{n}\|\}_{n}
converges to
\|X(t)\|
.
Let
X(t)=V(t)|X(t)|
be the polar decomposition.
Since

\displaystyle\langle{[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]x_{n},X(t)x_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\varphi_{1}(|X(t)|)V(t)|X(t)|x_{n},V(t)|X(t)|x_{n}}\rangle-\langle{|X(t)|^{2}\varphi_{1}(|X(t)|)x_{n},x_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle{|X(t)|^{2}\varphi_{2}(|X(t)^{*}|)x_{n},x_{n}}\rangle-\langle{|X(t)|\varphi_{2}(|X(t)|)|X(t)|x_{n},x_{n}}\rangle,

we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Re}\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\langle{[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]x_{n},X(t)x_{n}}\rangle$\hss}

\displaystyle\leq\|X(t)\|^{2}\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\langle{\varphi_{1}(|X(t)|)V(t)x_{n},V(t)x_{n}}\rangle-\varphi_{1}(\|X(t)\|))

\displaystyle+\|X(t)\|^{2}\limsup_{n\to\infty}(\langle{\varphi_{2}(|X(t)^{*}|)x_{n},x_{n}}\rangle-\varphi_{2}(\|X(t)\|))

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Thus

\|X(t)\|^{2}\leq\|X(s)\|^{2}+2\|X(t)\|\int_{s}^{t}\|[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]-[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]\|dr

and

\frac{\|X(t)\|^{2}-\|X(s)\|^{2}}{t-s}\leq\frac{2\|X(t)\|}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\|[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]-[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]\|dr.

This shows

\limsup_{s\uparrow t}\frac{\|X(t)\|^{2}-\|X(s)\|^{2}}{t-s}\leq 0,

\limsup_{t\downarrow s}\frac{\|X(t)\|^{2}-\|X(s)\|^{2}}{t-s}\leq 0,

and
[
7
, Chapter 4, Theorem 1.2]
implies that
\|X(t)\|^{2}
is a decreasing function on
[0,\tau]
.

Now assume
a>0
.
Then
X(t)
is invertible for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.
For
s,t
and
x
as above, we have

\displaystyle\|X(t)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|X(s)x\|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{s}^{t}\langle{[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]x,X(t)x}\rangle dr-\|(X(t)-X(s))x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq m(X(s))^{2}+2(t-s)\operatorname{Re}\langle{[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]x,X(t)x}\rangle

\displaystyle-2\|X(t)\|\int_{s}^{t}\|[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]-[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]\|dr-\|X(t)-X(s)\|^{2}.

We take a sequence
\{y_{n}\}_{n}
of unit vectors in
H
such that
\{|X(t)|y_{n}-m(X(t))y_{n}\}_{n}
converges to 0.
Then since
m(X(t))=\min\sigma(|X(t)|)=\min\sigma(|X(t)^{*}|)
, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Re}\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\langle{[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]y_{n},X(t)y_{n}}\rangle$\hss}

\displaystyle\geq m(X(t))^{2}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\langle{\varphi_{1}(|X(t)|)V(t)y_{n},V(t)y_{n}}\rangle-\varphi_{1}(m(X(t))))

\displaystyle+m(X(t))^{2}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(\langle{\varphi_{2}(|X(t)^{*}|)y_{n},y_{n}}\rangle-\varphi_{2}(m(X(t))))

\displaystyle\geq 0.

and

\displaystyle m(X(t))

\displaystyle\geq m(X(s))^{2}-2\|X(t)\|\int_{s}^{t}\|[\varphi(X(r)),X(r)]-[\varphi(X(t)),X(t)]\|dr

\displaystyle-\|X(t)-X(s)\|^{2}.

Thus

\liminf_{s\uparrow t}\frac{m(X(t))^{2}-m(X(s))^{2}}{t-s}\geq 0,

\liminf_{t\downarrow s}\frac{m(X(t))^{2}-m(X(s))^{2}}{t-s}\geq 0,

and
m(X(t))^{2}
is increasing on
[0,\tau]
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The above argument for

breaks down if

and

Since

Note that

As we are interested in the Aluthge flow of a left invertible

We can prove the following lemma in the same way as in the proof of the previous lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<a<b
and let
\varphi_{1}\in C[a,b]
be an increasing function.
Let

in
\mathbb{B}(H)
defined on
[0,\tau]

Then
\|X(t)\|
is decreasing and

is increasing with respect to

on
[0,\tau]
.

We denote the set of compact operators by
\mathbb{K}(H)
, and the set of finite rank operators by
\mathbb{F}(H)
.
Following
[
11
, Eq.(2.7)]
, we define the singular values of
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
by

s_{n}(T)=\inf\{\|T-S\|;\;S\in\mathbb{F}(H),\;\operatorname{rank}S\leq n-1\}.

For the theory of normed ideals, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
and
[
23
]
.
A two-sided ideal
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathbb{B}(H)
is said to be a normed ideal if
\mathcal{J}
is equipped with a norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{J}}
such that
(\mathcal{J},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{J}})
is a Banach space and the following hold:

(1)

\|X\|_{\mathcal{J}}=\|X\|
for every rank one operator
X\in\mathbb{F}(H)
.

(2)

\|YXZ\|_{\mathcal{J}}\leq\|Y\|\|X\|_{\mathcal{J}}\|Z\|
for all
X\in\mathcal{J}
and
Y,Z\in\mathbb{B}(H)
.

It is known that the second condition is equivalent to the following unitary invariance property:

(2)’

\|UXV\|_{\mathcal{J}}=\|X\|_{\mathcal{J}}
for all
X\in\mathcal{J}
and unitaries
U,V\in\mathbb{B}(H)
.

Although it is customary to exclude the case
\mathcal{J}=\mathbb{B}(H)
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{J}}=\|\cdot\|
, we include it in the following discussion.
For a given normed ideal
\mathcal{J}
, we denote by
\mathcal{J}^{(0)}
the closure of
\mathbb{F}(H)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{J}}
.
For example, if
\mathcal{J}=\mathbb{B}(H)
, we have
\mathcal{J}^{(0)}=\mathbb{K}(H)
.

We denote by
B(H)^{-1}
the group of bounded invertible operators.
For a normed ideal
\mathcal{J}
, we set

G_{\mathcal{J}}=\{X\in\mathbb{B}(H)^{-1};\;X-I\in\mathcal{J}\},

which is a Banach Lie group (see
[
14
]
).
For
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
, its similarity orbit with respect to
G_{\mathcal{J}}
is defined by

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{J}}(T)=\{gTg^{-1};\;g\in G_{\mathcal{J}}\}.

When
\mathcal{J}=\mathbb{B}(H)
, we denote it simply by
\mathcal{O}(T)
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we let
\|T\|_{p}=(\sum_{n=1}s_{n}(T)^{p})^{1/p}
, and we denote by
\mathcal{J}_{p}
the Schatten ideal

\mathcal{J}_{p}(H)=\{T\in\mathbb{K}(H);\;\|T\|_{p}<\infty\}.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a normed ideal.
We would like to discuss the solution of Eq.(
1.3
) satisfying
X(t)-T\in\mathcal{J}
under
the condition
[T,\varphi(T)]\in\mathcal{J}
.
For this purpose, we need to set up a relevant Lipschitz condition.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a normed ideal, let
\mathcal{X}
be a subset of
\mathbb{B}(H)
, and let
\psi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{B}(H)
be a map.
We say that
\psi
is
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschitz
if there exists
C>0
such that whenever
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
and
X_{1}-X_{2}\in\mathcal{J}
,
we have
\psi(X_{1})-\psi(X_{2})\in\mathcal{J}
and
\|\psi(X_{1})-\psi(X_{2})\|_{\mathcal{J}}\leq C\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathcal{J}}
.

We say that
f\in C[a,b]
is a
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschitz function
on
[a,b]
if the map
A\mapsto f(A)
is
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschtz on
\mathbb{B}(H)_{\mathrm{sa}}[a,b]
.
In moreover
\mathcal{J}=\mathbb{B}(H)
, we say that

is an
operator Lipschitz function
on
[a,b]
.

Remark 4.5
.

A few remarks are in order.

(1)

Let
1<p<\infty
.
The Potapov–Sukochev theorem
[
22

says that

is
\mathcal{J}_{p}
-Lipschitz on
[a,b]
if and only if

.

(2)

Kato
[
19
]
showed that the absolute value function
|x|
is not operator Lipschitz on
[-b,b]

(see
[
20
]
for related topics).

(3)

Let
f\in C^{1}[a,b]
.
If
f^{\prime}
extends to a function that is the Fourier transform of a complex measure,
then
f
is
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschitz on
[a,b]
for all
\mathcal{J}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1.1]

For example, if
f^{\prime}
extends to a periodic function with absolutely convergent Fourier series, then

satisfies this criterion.
For example, if
\alpha>1
, the function

is
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschitz on
[-b,b]
for all
\mathcal{J}
.

If

and

is
\mathcal{J}
-Lipschitz on

, the map

on

is

-Lipschitz.

and

is smooth on

.
This is no longer the case if

(think of

Thus the

-Lipschitz property of

-Lipschitz on the ball

For example, if

x,y\in H
satisfying
\langle{[\varphi(X_{\infty}),X_{\infty}]x,y}\rangle\neq 0
, and

\langle{F^{\varphi}_{t}(T)x,y}\rangle=\langle{Tx,y}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle{[\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)),F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)]x,y}\rangle dr,

holds.
This implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle{[\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)),F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)]x,y}\rangle dr

converges, which is a contradiction as
\langle{[\varphi(F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)),F^{\varphi}_{r}(T)]x,y}\rangle
converges to a
non-zero number as
r\to\infty
.
Thus
[\varphi(X_{\infty}),X_{\infty}]=0
holds.
∎

This criterion immediately shows that there exists a non-convergent flow.

Example 4.9
.

The Haagerup flow
\{F^{H}_{t}(S)\}_{t\geq 0}
starting from the unilateral shift
S
never converges
in the operator norm (and hence in any unitarily invariant norm).
Indeed, since
[S^{*},S]
is a rank one projection, we can take
\mathbb{K}
equipped with the operator norm as
the operator ideal
\mathcal{J}
.
Assume that there exists

satisfying

[[(S+Z)^{*},S+Z],S+Z]=0.

Then the Kleinecke–Shirokov theorem implies that
S+Z
is normal (see
[
13
, Problem 232]
).
However, this is a contradiction because the Fredholm index of
S+Z
is -1.
Therefore there is no such

.

Our favorite playground for the Aluthge flow is the class of weighted shifts,
as was already observed for the Aluthge transform (see

).

Example 4.10
.

Let
H=\ell^{2}
, and let
S
be the unilateral shift on

.
We regard
\ell^{\infty}
as the diagonal subalgebra of
\mathbb{B}(H)
by identifying
f\in\ell^{\infty}
with the

f

Then every unilateral weighted shift is of the form
Sf
for some

.
Moreover, up to unitary equivalence, we may and do assume

.
To define the Aluthge flow, we need to assume that there exists a constant
a>0
satisfying
a\leq|T|
.
Thus we assume
T=Sf
with
a\leq f
in what follows.

We can explicitly describe the Aluthge flow
F^{A}_{t}(Sf)
as follows.
Let
B:\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{\infty}
be the backward shift.
By the uniqueness of the solution to Eq.(
1.3
), we can find the solution of the form
Sh(t)
,
h(t)\in\ell^{\infty}
, with

\frac{dh}{dt}=(B-I_{\ell^{\infty}})(\log h)h,\quad h(0)=f,

or equivalently with

\frac{d\log h}{dt}=(B-I_{\ell^{\infty}})(\log h),\quad h(0)=f.

\{\mathcal{P}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be

F^{A}_{t}(Sf)=Se^{\mathcal{P}_{t}(\log f)}.

(4.2)

Assume that there exists a scalar
\lambda>0
satisfying
f-\lambda\in c_{0}
.
Then we can show that
\{F^{A}_{t}(Sf)\}_{t\geq 0}
converges to
\lambda S
in the operator norm.
Indeed, let
g=\log(f/\lambda)
.

F^{A}_{t}(Sf)=\lambda Se^{\mathcal{P}_{t}(g)}

Note that

converges to 0.

which is the average of

Let
\mathcal{J}
be an operator ideal satisfying

.
In a similar way, we can show the convergence of

to

in

.

Proposition 4.12

(cf.
[
8
, Corollary 3.3]
)
.

Let
g\in\ell^{\infty}
be given by

g_{n}=\begin{cases}2^{-2N}(n-2^{2N}),&2^{2N}\leq n<2^{2N+1},\\
2^{-2N-1}(2^{2N+2}-n),&2^{2N+1}\leq n<2^{2N+2},\end{cases}

where
N\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
, and let
T=Se^{g}
.
Then

[\log|T|,T]=S(Bg-g)e^{g}\in\mathcal{J}_{p},

for all
p>1
, and
\{F^{A}_{t}(T)\}_{t\geq 0}
does not converge in the weak operator topology as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

By definition,
0\leq g\leq 1
and

(Bg)_{n}-g_{n}=\begin{cases}2^{-2N},&2^{2N}\leq n<2^{2N+1},\\
-2^{-2N-1},&2^{2N+1}\leq n<2^{2N+2}.\end{cases}

Thus
Bg-g\in\ell^{p}
for all
p>1
, and
[\log|T|,T]\in\mathcal{J}_{p}
for all
p>1
.
Since

\langle{F^{A}_{t}(T)\delta_{n},\delta_{n+1}}\rangle=\langle{e^{\mathcal{P}_{t}(g)}\delta_{n},\delta_{n}}\rangle,

to prove non-convergence, it suffices to show that

\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}e^{-t}}{k!}g_{n+k}\right\}_{t\geq 0}

does not converge for some (in fact all)
n\in\mathbb{N}
as
t\to\infty
.

We fix
0<\varepsilon<1/4
.
Let
X_{t}
be a random variable whose law is the Poisson distribution with mean
t
.
Since
\{(X_{t}-t)/\sqrt{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution
as
t\to\infty
, we can choose
a>0
and
t_{0}>0
such that

\operatorname{Pr}(|X_{t}-t|\geq a\sqrt{t})<\varepsilon
holds for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

In the following argument, we fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
t=2^{2N}-n>t_{0}
and
a/2^{N}\leq 1/8
, we have

2^{2N}(1-\frac{1}{8})\leq n+k\leq 2^{2N}(1+\frac{1}{8}),

for
|k-t|<a\sqrt{t}
, and
g_{n+k}\leq 1/4
holds.
Thus

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}e^{-t}}{k!}g_{n+k}\leq\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4}.

Similarly, we can show that if
t=2^{2N+1}-n>t_{0}
with
a/2^{N+1/2}<1/8
,
we have
g_{n+k}\geq 3/4
for
|k-t|<a\sqrt{t}
, and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}e^{-t}}{k!}g_{n+k}\geq\frac{3}{4}(1-\varepsilon).

Since

\varepsilon+\frac{1}{4}<\frac{3}{4}(1-\varepsilon),

this proves the statement.
∎

Definition 4.13
.

Thanks to Lemma
4.1

We denote it by

Question 4.14
.

Does
r(T)=r^{\varphi}(T)
hold when
\varphi
is operator Lipschitz?
An affirmative answer to this question would provide a counterpart of Yamazaki’s theorem
[
26
, Theorem 1]
.

We give an example, other than matrices, in favor of an affirmative answer.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
T=Sf
be a left invertible weighted shift, where
S
is the unilateral shift and
f\in\ell^{\infty}

is positive and invertible.
Then
r(T)=r^{A}(T)
holds.

Proof.

Since
r(sT)=sr(T)
and
r^{A}(sT)=sr^{A}(T)
for a scalar
s>0
, we may and do assume that
f\geq 1
to show
the statement.
Let
g=\log f\geq 0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

M_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B^{k}.

Then

\displaystyle\log r(T)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T^{n}\|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\|S^{n}e^{nM_{n}g}\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|e^{nM_{n}g}\|_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|M_{n}g\|_{\infty}.

On the other hand, Eq.(
4.2
) shows

\log r^{A}(T)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathcal{P}_{t}g\|_{\infty}.

Our goal is to show

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathcal{P}_{t}g\|_{\infty}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\|M_{n}g\|_{\infty}.

Let
p_{t}
be the Poisson distribution with mean
t
, which we regard as an element in
\ell^{1}(\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N})
.
Then the Stirling formula shows
\|\delta_{1}*p_{t}-p_{t}\|_{1}=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}})
as

, and so

\|M_{n}\mathcal{P}_{t}-\mathcal{P}_{t}\|\leq\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k}*p_{t}-p_{t}\|_{1}\to 0,\quad(t\to\infty).

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathcal{P}_{t}g\|_{\infty}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|M_{n}\mathcal{P}_{t}g\|_{\infty}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\mathcal{P}_{t}M_{n}g\|_{\infty}\ \leq\|M_{n}g\|_{\infty},

which shows the desired inequality.
∎

A.1
Aluthge flow

Proposition A.1
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{B}(H)
be a left invertible operator on a Hilbert space
H
.
Then
\{\Delta_{t/n}^{n}(T)\}_{n}
converges to the Aluthge flow
F^{A}_{t}(T)
in the operator norm
uniformly in
t
on compact subsets of
[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
M=\|T\|=\max\sigma(|T|)
and let
m=m(T)=\min\sigma(|T|)>0
.
Then it is well known that
\sigma(|\Delta_{\lambda}(T)|)\subset[m,M]
holds.
Indeed, let
T=U|T|
be the polar decomposition and let
x\in H
be a unit vector.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\lambda}(T)x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle{|T|^{2\lambda}U|T|^{1-\lambda}x,U|T|^{1-\lambda}x}\rangle\leq M^{2\lambda}\langle{U|T|^{1-\lambda}x,U|T|^{1-\lambda}x}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq M^{2\lambda}\langle{|T|^{1-\lambda}x,|T|^{1-\lambda}x}\rangle\leq M^{2}.

Similarly, we can show
\|\Delta_{\lambda}(T)x\|\geq m
, and so
\sigma(|\Delta_{\lambda}(T)|)\subset[m,M]
.

We fix
\tau>0
and let
0\leq t\leq\tau
.
Let
L
be the Lipschitz constant of
[\log|X|,X]
on
L\mathbb{B}(H)[m,M]
.
We take a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and set
Y_{k}=\Delta_{t/n}^{k}(T)
,
Z_{k}=F^{A}_{kt/n}(T)
for
k=0,1,2,\cdots,n
.
Then

Y_{k+1}=Y_{k}+\frac{t}{n}[\log|Y_{k}|,Y_{k}]+\varepsilon_{k}

with

\|\varepsilon_{k}\|=\left\|e^{\frac{t}{n}\log|Y_{k}|}Y_{k}e^{-\frac{t}{n}\log|Y_{k}|}-Y_{k}-\frac{t}{n}[\log|Y_{k}|,Y_{k}]\right\|\leq\frac{C_{1}t^{2}}{n^{2}},

where
C_{1}>0
depends only on
m
,
M
, and
\tau
.
We have

Z_{k+1}=Z_{k}+\frac{t}{n}[\log|Z_{k}|,Z_{k}]+\delta_{k}

with

\|\delta_{k}\|=\left\|\int_{kt/n}^{(k+1)t/n}([\log|F^{A}_{s}(T)|,F^{A}_{s}(T)]-[\log|Z_{k}|,Z_{k}])ds\right\|\leq\frac{C_{2}t^{2}}{n^{2}},

where
C_{2}>0
depends only on
m
,
M
,
\tau
, and
L
.
Thus

\|Y_{k+1}-Z_{k+1}\|\leq\left(1+\frac{Lt}{n}\right)\|Y_{k}-Z_{k}\|+(C_{1}+C_{2})\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}},

and

\|\Delta_{t/n}^{n}(T)-F^{A}_{t}(T)\|=\|Y_{n}-Z_{n}\|\leq\frac{(C_{1}+C_{2})t(e^{Lt}-1)}{Ln}\leq\frac{(C_{1}+C_{2})\tau(e^{L\tau}-1)}{Ln}.

∎

A.2
Haagerup flow

Proposition A.2
.

The Haagerup flow on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the gradient flow with respect to
the energy function
E(X)=\|[X^{*},X]\|_{2}^{2}/4=\Tr([X^{*},X]^{2})/4
.

Proof.

Recall that the standard real inner product of
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is given by

\langle{X,Y}\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\Tr(Y^{*}X)
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{dE(X+tY)}{dt}{\bigg|}_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Tr([X^{*},X]([Y^{*},X]+[X^{*},Y]))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\Tr([X,[X^{*},X]]Y^{*})

\displaystyle=-\langle{[[X^{*},X],X],Y}\rangle,

which shows that Eq.(
1.2
) is the gradient equation for
E(X)
.
∎

A.3
Divided differences

Let
f\in C^{n}[a,b]
.
For
k=0,1,\cdots,n
, the
k
-th divided difference
f^{[k]}\in C[a,b]^{k+1}
is inductively defined as follows.
We set
f^{[0]}(x)=f(x)
.
Assume that
f^{[k-1]}
is defined for
k\leq n
.
If there exist
1\leq i<j\leq k+1
with
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
, we set
f^{[k]}
to be

\displaystyle f^{[k]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{[k-1]}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\cdots,x_{k+1})-f^{[k-1]}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{k+1})}{x_{j}-x_{i}},

and if
x_{1}=x_{2}=\cdots=x_{k+1}
, we set

f^{[k]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{k+1})=\frac{f^{(k)}(x_{1})}{k!}.

It turns out that
f^{[k]}
is well-defined, symmetric, and continuous on
[a,b]^{k+1}
.
When
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
are distinct, we have

f^{[n]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}f(x_{i})\prod_{1\leq j\leq n+1,\;j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}.

The reader is referred to
[
15
, Lemma 2.2.4]
and
[
18
, Chapter 6.1.14]
for the proof of these properties.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
f\in C[0,r]
with
f(0)=0
and assume that the restriction of
f
to
(0,r]
is in
C^{n}((0,r])
.
Then
f^{[n]}\in C((0,r]^{n+1})
extends to a continuous function
F\in C((0,r]^{n}\times[0,r])
, and

F(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n},0)=(x^{-1}f)^{[n-1]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
holds.

Proof.

The existence of the continuous extension
F
follows from induction on
n
by using the recursion

f^{[n]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1})=\frac{f^{[n-1]}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})-f^{[n-1]}(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1})}{x_{1}-x_{n+1}}

because we may assume that
x_{n+1}
is sufficiently close to 0 and
x_{n+1}\neq x_{1}
to demonstrate the desired continuous extension.

Once we know the existence of a continuous extension
F
, we may assume that
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are distinct to evaluate

F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},0)
.
Thus

\displaystyle F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},0)=\lim_{x_{n+1}\to 0}f^{[n]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\lim_{x_{n+1}\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}f(x_{i})\prod_{1\leq j\leq n+1,\;j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{-1}f(x_{i})\prod_{1\leq j\leq n,\;j\neq i}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}

\displaystyle=(x^{-1}f)^{[n-1]}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}).

∎

A.4
Interpolation polynomials

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a self-adjoint matrix with eigenvalues
b\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq a
.
Then the Newton form of the Hermite interpolation polynomial for
f\in C^{n-1}[a,b]
at the eigenvalues of
A
is defined by

p(t)=f(\lambda_{1})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}f^{[k]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k+1})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t-\lambda_{j}).

It is known that
f(\lambda_{i})=p(\lambda_{i})
holds for every
1\leq i\leq n
, and
f(A)=p(A)
holds.
Moreover, if
\lambda_{i}
has a multiplicity
m
, we have
p^{(j)}(\lambda_{i})=f^{(j)}(\lambda_{i})
for
0\leq j\leq m-1

(see
[
17
, Remark 1.6]
,
[
18
, Chapter 6.1.14]
).
Since the eigenvalues are continuous functions of
A
, so are the coefficients of
p(x)
.
The existence of a polynomial with such properties is used in a crucial way in the proof of Theorem
3.10
.
For concrete computation, we show that the coefficients can be expressed as manifestly symmetric functions of the eigenvalues, as follows:

Proposition A.4
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a self-adjoint matrix with eigenvalues
b\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq a
,
and let
f\in C^{n-1}[a,b]
.
We set

p_{f}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}(-1)^{n-1-j-k}e_{n-1-j-k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})(x^{j}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})t^{k},

where
e_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial with
n
variables.
Then
p_{f}
coincides with the Hermite interpolation polynomial for
f\in C^{n-1}[a,b]
at the eigenvalues of
A
.

Proof.

We first show that
f(A)=p_{f}(A)
holds for every polynomial
f
.
Indeed, for
f=1
, the term
(x^{j}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
survives only if
j=n-1
, which forces
k=0
.
Thus we get
p_{1}=1
, and
f(A)=p_{1}(A)
holds.
Assume that the equality holds for a polynomial
f
.
Then thanks to the Cayley-Hamilton theorem,

\displaystyle Af(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}(-1)^{n-1-j-k}e_{n-1-j-k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})(x^{j}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})A^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{n-j-k}e_{n-j-k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})(x^{j}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})A^{k}

\displaystyle+f^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})A^{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}e_{n-j}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})(x^{j}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}(-1)^{n-1-j-k}e_{n-1-j-k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})(x^{j+1}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})A^{k}

\displaystyle+f^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}e_{n-k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})A^{k}-(\chi_{A}f)^{[n-1]}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})

\displaystyle=p_{xf}(A),

where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic polynomial of
A
.
Thus the equality holds for any monomial by induction, and for any polynomial by linearity.

For general
f\in C^{n-1}[a,b]
, we can choose a sequence of polynomials
\{q_{l}\}_{l}
such that
q_{l}^{(k)}
converges to
f^{(k)}

uniformly on
[a,b]
for all
0\leq k\leq n-1
.
Then we get

f(A)=\lim_{l\to\infty}q_{l}(A)=\lim_{l\to\infty}p_{q_{l}}(A)=p_{f}(A).

When
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are distinct, a polynomial
q
with
f(A)=q(A)
whose degree is at most
n-1
is unique,
and
p_{f}
coincides with the Hermite (in fact, Newton) interpolation polynomial
p
.
Since the coefficients of both polynomials are continuous functions of
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
the two polynomials coincide in the general case.
∎

Abstract
Let { Z n , n = 0 , 1 , 2 , … } \left\{Z_{n},n=0,1,2,...\right\} be a critical branching process in i.i.d. random environment, Z r , n Z_{r,n} be the number of particles in the process at moment 0 ≤ r ≤ n − 1 0\leq r\leq n-1 that have a positive number of descendants in generation n n , and { S n , n = 0 , 1 , 2 , … } \left\{S_{n},n=0,1,2,...\right\} be the associated random walk of { Z n , n = 0 , 1 , 2 , … } \left\{Z_{n},n=0,1,2,...\right\} . It is known that if the increments of the associated random walk have zero mean and finite variance σ 2 \sigma^{2} then, for any t ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] t\in[0,1] lim n → ∞ 𝐏 ⁡ ( log ⁡ Z [ n ​ t ] , n σ ​ n ≤ x | Z n > 0 ) = 𝐏 ⁡ ( min t ≤ s ≤ 1 ⁡ B s + ≤ x ) , x ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) , \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\log Z_{\left[nt\right],n}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leq x\Big|Z_{n}>0\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\min_{t\leq s\leq 1}B_{s}^{+}\leq x\right),\;x\in[0,\infty), where { B t + , 0 ≤ t ≤ 1 } \left\{B_{t}^{+},0\leq t\leq 1\right\} is the Brownian meander. We supplement this result by description of the distribution of the properly scaled random variable log ⁡ Z r , n \log Z_{r,n} under the condition { S n ≤ t ​ k , Z n > 0 } , \left\{S_{n}\leq t\sqrt{k},Z_{n}>0\right\}, where t > 0 t>0 and r , k → ∞ r,k\rightarrow\infty in such a way that k = o ⁡ ( n ) k=o(n) as n → ∞ n\to\infty . The case when the distribution of the increments of the associated random walk belongs to the domain of attraction of a stable law is also considered. key words : reduced branching processes, non-favorable random environment, conditional limit theorem MSC: Primary 60G50, Secondary 60J80, 60K37

Introduction

We consider a critical branching process evolving in nonfavorable random
environments. Let
\mathfrak{F}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}
be the
space of all probability measures on
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,...\}
. For
notational reason, we identify a measure
\mathfrak{f}=\left\{\mathfrak{f}(\left\{0\right\}),\mathfrak{f}(\left\{1\right\}),...\right\}\in

\mathfrak{F}
with the respective probability generating function

f(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{f}(\left\{k\right\})s^{k},\quad s\in[0,1],

and make no difference between
\mathfrak{f}
and
f
. Equipped with the
metric of total variation,
\mathfrak{F}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}=\left\{f\right\}
becomes a Polish space. Let

F(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F\left(\left\{k\right\}\right)s^{k},\quad s\in[0,1],

be a random variable taking values in
\mathfrak{F}
, and let

F_{n}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{n}\left(\left\{k\right\}\right)s^{k},\quad s\in[0,1],\quad n\in\mathbb{N=}\left\{1,2,...\right\},

be a sequence of independent probabilistic copies of
F
. The infinite
sequence
\mathcal{E}=\left\{F_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is called a
random environment.

A sequence of nonnegative random variables
\mathcal{Z}=\left\{Z_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
specified on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
is called a branching process in random environment
(BPRE), if
Z_{0}
is independent of
\mathcal{E}
and, given
\mathcal{E}

the process
\mathcal{Z}
is a Markov chain with

\mathcal{L}\left(Z_{n}|Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1},\mathcal{E}=(f_{1},f_{2},...)\right)=\mathcal{L}(\xi_{n1}+\ldots+\xi_{ny_{n-1}})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f_{1},f_{2},...\in\mathfrak{F}
, where
\xi_{n1},\xi_{n2},\ldots
is a
sequence of i.i.d. random variables with distribution
f_{n}.

The sequence

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{n}=X_{1}+...+X_{n},\ n\geq 1,

where
X_{i}=\log F_{i}^{\prime}(1),i=1,2,...,
is called the associated
random walk for the process
\mathcal{Z}
. The growth rate of the population
size of a BPRE essentially depends on the properties of the associated random walk
\mathcal{S}=\left\{S_{n},n\geq 0\right\}
.

Let
X\overset{d}{=}X_{n},n=1,2,....
To describe the restrictions we impose
on the properties of the BPRE we introduce the set

\mathcal{A}:=\{\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\},\,|\beta|<1\}\cup\{\alpha=1,\beta=0\}\cup\{\alpha=2,\beta=0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For a pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
and a random variable
X
we
write
X\in\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
if the distribution of

X
belongs to the domain of attraction of a stable law with density
g_{\alpha,\beta}(x),x\in(-\infty,+\infty),
and the characteristic
function

G_{\alpha,\beta}(w)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{iwx}g_{\alpha.\beta}(x)\,dx=\exp\left\{-c|w|^{\,\alpha}\left(1-i\beta\frac{w}{|w|}\tan\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right\},\ c>0,

and, in addition,
\mathbf{E}X=0
if this moment exists. Under these
assumptions there is an increasing sequence of positive numbers

a_{n}\ =\ n^{1/\alpha}\ell(n)

with a slowly varying at infinity sequence
\ell(1),\ell(2),\ldots,
such
that, as

\left\{\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}},t\geq 0\right\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}=\left\{Y_{t},t\geq 0\right\},

is a strictly stable Levy process with the initial value

functions

and the symbol

stands for the weak convergence in the
spaces of càdlàg functions endowed with Skorokhod topology. Observe
that if

then there exists
the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}>0\right)=\rho=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}>0\right)\in(0,1).

to prove the theorem it is sufficient to show that, for any
y\in[0,\theta^{1/\alpha}t]

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log Z_{r,n}\leq ya_{m};\mathcal{\bar{K}}_{l,n}(N)|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)=A(\theta^{1/\alpha}t,y).

(5.17)

Since
Z_{r}\left(1-F_{r,n}(0)\right)\rightarrow\infty
with probability
1 as
n\gg m=n-r\rightarrow\infty
by Lemma
14
, we
conclude that (
5.17
) will be proved if we show that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log\left[Z_{r}\left(1-F_{r,n}(0)\right)\right]\leq ya_{m};\mathcal{\bar{K}}_{l,n}(N)|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)=A(\theta^{1/\alpha}t,y).

However this fact is an obvious conclusion of the just mentioned Lemmas
5
and
14
.

Theorem
2
is proved.

5.3
Proof of Theorem
3

Similarly to the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
, the first step in the proof of this theorem is to
demonstrate that given conditions of the theorem

\lim_{k\wedge r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\left|\Delta_{r,n}\right|>\varepsilon\Big|\mathcal{R}_{n}(ta_{k})\right)=0

(5.18)

for any
\varepsilon>0.
It follows from Lemma
4
that

\lim_{k\wedge r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\left|\log\frac{Z_{r}}{e^{S_{r}}}\right|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big|\mathcal{R}_{n}(ta_{k})\right)=0.

Thus, to prove (
5.18
) we need to check that

\lim_{k\wedge r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\left|\log\frac{1-F_{r,n}(0)}{e^{S_{\tau_{r,n}}-S_{r}}}\right|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big|\mathcal{R}_{n}(ta_{k})\right)=0\;

under the conditions of Theorem
3
. This is, indeed, the
case, since it follows from the conditions of Theorem
3

that

\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\left|\log\frac{1-F_{r,n}(0)}{e^{S_{\tau_{r,n}}-S_{r}}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\log O_{r,n}\leq\frac{1}{a_{k\wedge r}}\log C(n-r)\rightarrow 0\text{ a.s.}

as
k\wedge r\rightarrow\infty
. Since
a_{k\wedge r}\leq a_{k\bot r},
we
deduce that (
5.18
) and Lemma
3
imply the
estimate

\displaystyle\lim\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\bot r}}\log\left[Z_{r}(1-F_{r,n}(0))\right]\leq y\Big|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\bot r}}\left(\Delta_{r,n}+S_{\tau_{r,n}}\right)\leq y\Big|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{k\bot r}}S_{\tau_{r,n}}\leq y\Big|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)=\hat{Q}(y),

where the limits are calculated when the of parameters
r,k
and
n
vary in
accordance with points 1)–3) of Theorem
3
. Therefore, if
the event
\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)
has occurred and the
parameters
r,k
and
n
vary in accordance to the assumptions of Theorem
3
, then

Z_{r}(1-F_{r,n}(0))\rightarrow\infty\text{ a.s.}

This fact and Lemma
5
allow to claim that if Condition (
1.4
) is valid, then, for any
y\geq 0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\gg k\gg r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log Z_{r,n}\leq ya_{r}|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\gg k\gg r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log Z_{r,n}\leq ya_{r};\mathcal{\bar{K}}_{r,n}(N)|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{n\gg k\gg r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{1}{a_{r}}\log\left[Z_{r}(1-F_{r,n}(0))\right]\leq y\Big|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=C^{\ast\ast}\mathcal{H}(y),

and by the same reasonings, if
n\gg k\sim\theta r
for some
\theta>0,

then, for any
y\geq 0

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log Z_{r,n}\leq ya_{r}|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)=W(\theta^{1/\alpha}t,\theta^{1/\alpha}t\wedge y),

and if
\min\left(r,n-r\right)\gg k
, then, for any
y\geq 0

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\log Z_{r,n}\leq ya_{k}|\mathcal{R}\left(ta_{k},n\right)\right)=1-\left(1-\frac{t\wedge y}{t}\right)^{\alpha\rho+1}.

Theorem
3
is proved.

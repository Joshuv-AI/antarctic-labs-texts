State of neglect and stagnation in prison healthcare
Authors: Board of Directors of the Spanish Society of Prison Health
Published: 2026

28 May 2003. Law 16/2003 on the Cohesion and Quality of the National Health System. Sixth additional provision: Transfer of healthcare services and institutions reporting to Prison Institutions to the autonomous communities. The health services that report to the Prison Institutions shall be transferred to the autonomous communities for full integration into the corresponding regional health services. To this end, prison health services shall be integrated into the National Health System in 18 months, dating from the entry into force of said law and the corresponding royal decree, in accordance with the system of transfers established in regional statutes of autonom1.

18 months after coming into effect! In other words, the complete integration of prison healthcare into the corresponding regional health services should have been made fully effective in October 2004. Twenty-five years and six months have gone by since then, and in the meantime only three autonomous communities have complied with the Law: Catalonia2, the Basque Country3 and Navarre4. This means that in the three regional health services, about 18% of the prison population in Spain has a health provider that operates solely for this purpose, while the provider for the other 82% is an institution whose main purpose is the imprisonment, custody, re-education and rehabilitation of inmates, with little or no concern for healthcare.

In these 21 years, the inaction of the Central Government and Prison Institution has been outrageous. They have hidden behind the excuse of “the enormous amount of work we do every day” and blame heaped on autonomous communities (and often rightly so), and by doing so have attempted to conceal their utter incompetence. For many years, they have ignored the repeated warnings made by professionals working in prison healthcare, in particular the Spanish Society of Prison Health (SESP)5 and have done so while heaping scorn on the knowledge, experience and hard work of those who ensured that prison health in this country continued to function in very difficult periods such as the AIDS epidemic. The political decision makers of the Institution, including the General Sub-Directorate of Prison Healthcare, opted to entrench themselves behind a vantage point of supposed greater wisdom and moral and intellectual superiority, and from there ignored any advice, assistance, request and complaint from prison staff, the SESP or any other healthcare forum; they have remained there ever since.

The questions are; can anyone believe that they are so blind that they cannot see? Can anyone believe that they do not know that without medical personnel no kind of health system can really take shape? Can anyone believe that they are unaware of the harm done to the population we care for and to the general public?

The answers are much the same. No one believes it. They know all too well what they are doing and the consequences of their actions.

The last question would be, why? And we do not have the answer to that. Or rather we don’t dare to find one.

In any case, the reality of the situation is as difficult as it is sad: with every day that passes, the point of no return is that much closer, if it hasn’t already arrived. Nowadays they prefer to offload the entire weight of the system onto nursing staff, forcing them to take on roles and responsibilities that are not theirs. Now their attitude towards prison pharmacies, contracts for medical professionals and the so-called protocols for nursing units are verging on illegality, and the outcome of all this is that medical professionals are now desperate, demoralised and losing hope.

And no one is taking the bull by the horns and making the only possible decision: comply with the law.

We know that this will not solve all the problems in prison health, at least in the short term, given that the issues are deeply seated and long lasting, but we do know, as they do, that if the law is not respected, no solution is possible.

Some years ago, you could feel proud to belong to the prison healthcare community. We only ask that we can feel that pride once again and that the coming generations of prison health professionals can also feel it as we once did.

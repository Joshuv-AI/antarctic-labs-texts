Categories by Kan extension
David I. Spivak

Abstract
Categories can be identified—up to isomorphism—with polynomial comonads on 𝐒𝐞𝐭 \mathbf{Set} . The left Kan extension of a functor along itself is always a comonad—called the density comonad—so it defines a category when its carrier is polynomial. We provide a number of generalizations of this to produce new categories from old, as well as from distributive laws of monads over comonads. For example, all Lawvere theories, all product completions of small categories, and the simplicial indexing category 𝚫 op \mathbf{\Delta}^{\textnormal{op}} arise in this way. Another, seemingly much less well-known, example constructs a so-called selection category from a polynomial comonad in a way that’s somehow dual to the construction of a Lawvere theory category from a monad; we’ll discuss this in more detail. Along the way, we will see various constructions of non-polynomial comonads as well.

Chapter 3
Pra-functors between copresheaf categories

Calculations of left Kan extensions are particularly easy when the categories are copresheaf categories and the functors are parametric right adjoint (pra-) functors. We begin by reminding the reader what a pra-functor between copresheaf categories is.

Lemma 0.9
.

For any functor
p\colon\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}\to\mathcal{D}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

Naturally in
X:\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}
and
d:\mathcal{D}
, the functor
p
has the form

p(X)(d)\cong\sum_{i:I_{d}}\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}\big(A(d,i),X\big),

where

and
A\colon(\el_{\mathcal{D}}I)^{\textnormal{op}}\to\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}
are functors.

(b)

the functor

, induced by slicing
p
over the terminal object
1:\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}
, is a right adjoint.
6
6

6

This is the usual definition of pra-functor, and it generalizes beyond copresheaf categories. If

has a terminal object

is a

iff the induced functor

is a right adjoint.

Proof 0.10
.

We prove each implication in turn.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
: Assuming

, note that
\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}(-,1_{\mathcal{C}})\cong 1
, so

, and we need to prove that

, and for any

, we have

. It follows that

{\vphantom{f}A}\end{bmatrix}
is right adjoint to the Yoneda extension, shown diagonally in the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\el_{\mathcal{D}}I)^{\textnormal{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\el_{\mathcal{D}}I)\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{y}}^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{bmatrix}{\vphantom{f}{\mathcal{y}}^{-}}\\
{\vphantom{f}A}\end{bmatrix}}

Lemma 0.13
.

Copresheaf categories are accessible, and pra-functors between them are accessible.

Proof 0.14
.

Since every set is a filtered colimit of finite sets, and every map from a finite set to a directed colimit of sets factors through a finite stage,
\mathbf{Set}
is accessible. By
[
nLa25
]
, the category of functors from a small category to an accessible category is accessible, so copresheaf categories are accessible. Again by
[
nLa25
]
, any left or right adjoint functor between accessible categories is accessible, and by

between copresheaf categories can be written as a right adjoint

followed by a left adjoint
\mathcal{D}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}/p(1)\to\mathcal{D}\textnormal{-}\mathbf{Set}
.

In the case
\mathcal{C}=1=\mathcal{D}
, one may identify a pra-functor

with a polynomial functor, i.e. a coproduct of representables. Indeed, with

as a coproduct of representables; the converse is easy too.

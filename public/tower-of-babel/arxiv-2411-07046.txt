The Ground State Energy of Heavy Atoms: Leading and Subleading Asymptotics
Long Meng and Heinz Siedentop

Abstract
. We study atomic ground state energies for neutral atoms as the nuclear charge Z Z is large in the no-pair formalism. We show that for a large class of projections defining the underlying Dirac sea – covering not only the physical reasonable cases but also “weird” ones – the corresponding no-pair ground state energy does not exceed the one of the Furry energy up to subleading order. An essential tool is the use and extension of Séré’s results on atomic Dirac-Hartree-Fock theory.



1.
Introduction and outline of paper

In non-relativistic quantum mechanics there are canonical Hamiltonians
to describe atoms, molecules, and other many particle systems
involving electrons. These emerge as canonical quantization of a
classical Hamiltonian. These are successful when low momenta dominate
the physical effects. However, they are clearly inappropriate when
atoms of high atomic number
Z
are present, since their inner nuclei
move with a substantial fraction of the velocity of light
c
. In fact
there are striking effects like the golden color of gold or the
functioning of lead-acid batteries which make a relativistic description
mandatory (see more details in Pyykkö’s review
[
28
]
).

From a fundamental physical point of view such systems should be
described by quantum electrodynamics. However, from a mathematical
point of view a consistent mathematical model is absent. Moreover,
although a perturbative evaluation is possible, in fact very
successful for few electrons, it is out of reach for many electron
systems. Moreover, an alternative ansatz that amounts to replacing the
non-relativistic kinetic energy by the free Dirac operator yields
Hamiltonians whose spectra are the entire real line. This phenomena,
known in the literature as “continuum dissolution” or
“Brown-Ravenhall disease” (Sucher
[
33
]
, Datta and
Jagannathan
[
7
]
) is obviously unphysical. The
dilemma has been recognized shortly after the one-particle Dirac
equation was written down. Early attempts to resolve it go back – at
least – to Chandrasekhar
[
6
]
who used the
semiclassical quantization of the the classical energy momentum
relation
\sqrt{c^{2}{\mathbf{p}}^{2}+c^{4}}
(in the ultra-relativistic limit) to
determine the stability and instability of white dwarfs. It also has
been popular in the mathematical literature study relativistic effects
of Coulomb systems. However, the quantitative results become
inaccurate for larger atoms and fail completely for Radium (
Z=88
)
and elements with higher nuclear charge.

Starting with Brown and Ravenhall
[
4
]
(see also
Bethe and Salpeter
[
3
]
) Hamiltonians implementing
Dirac’s idea of a sea that excludes negative energy states were
derived. These so called no-pair Hamiltonians were systematically
investigated by Sucher
[
32
]
taking into account the
polarization of the vacuum and its polarization by an external field
called the “Furry picture” (see Furry
[
14
]
). Eventually
Mittleman
[
24
]
derived a slightly different
Hamiltonian picture from quantum electro-dynamics by physical
arguments. Although, by definition the Mittleman energies
could be higher than the ground state energies they practically agree
in numerical calculations. Thus, it can conjectured, that the energies
of large neutral atoms agree up to small errors. We will – in fact –
confirm this conjecture.

In Section
2
we will define the no-pair Hamiltonians
and Mittleman’s variational principle in detail and formulate our main
result, i.e., the Mittleman energy and the Furry energy agree
asymptotically up to second order in the atomic number. As a byproduct
we obtain a justification of a Séré’s variant of relativistic
Hartree-Fock theory of neutral atoms from a microscopic physical point
of view: Although a mathematically sound theory it was originally only
an ad hoc transcriptions from the non-relativistic setting. Here it
emerges as a limiting theory of a more fundamental theory.

Section
3
introduces a version of Dirac-Hartree
theory that is patterned in the spirit of Séré. In addition we
formulate an existence result for its minimizers. The functional
considered will be – in some sense – simpler than Séré’s
original one, namely the exchange is dropped and replaced by a
Fermi-Amaldi correction (Fermi and Amaldi
[
10
]
and
Gombas
[
15
, §7]
). Furthermore a generalized

SU(2)
-invariance of the test density matrices is imposed which
results in spherical symmetry of the associated densities.

This functional will be the essential tool in the proof of the main
theorem. Moreover it allows us to describe admissible projections in
Mittleman’s variational characterization of ground state energy. The
definition of admissibility and sufficient criteria for admissibility
will be given in Subsection
3.2
. Subsection

3.3
will give examples of admissible potentials.

Theorem
1
will be proven in Section
4.2
. As
mentioned above the proof uses the Séré type functional and some
new properties of its minimizer
\gamma_{*}
. Although we will follow
the general strategy of Séré
[
31
]
, we need to extend
his existence result in a way suitable for our application and derive
some new properties of the resulting minimizers.

Appendix
A
indicates the existence proof of
minimizers of the functional with particular emphasis on the new
aspects not contained in previous work. Appendix
B
gives
some properties of these minimizers that are essential for our proof.

According to Mittleman

The aim of this paper is twofold:

•

Address the lack of a suitable class of potentials in Mittleman’s
argument, i.e., to specify a reasonable class of admissible

•

Show that Mittleman’s energy agrees up to subleading order with the Furry energy.

3.
Relativistic Hartree Theory à la Séré and admissible potentials

We begin with a study of the Dirac-Hartree functional in

admissible potentials.

3.2.
Admissible potentials

We will now describe the pictures considered, i.e., to specify the

Definition 1
.

We call
A
admissible and write
A\in\mathfrak{A}
, if and only if
A
is a
family of symmetric operators
A(Z)
with
Z\in[Z_{0},\infty)
for
some

such that for all

we have

\mathfrak{Q}(A(Z))\supset\mathfrak{Q}
, that
D_{c,Z}+A(Z)
has a
distinguished self-adjoint extension
D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}
in the sense
of Nenciu, and the the following inequalities hold

(45)

\displaystyle-1\lesssim B_{A(Z)}:=|D_{c,0}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}A(Z)|D_{c,0}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1,

(46)

\displaystyle|D_{c,0}|\lesssim|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|\lesssim|D_{c,0}|,

(47)

\displaystyle P_{Z,A(Z)}^{\perp}D_{c,Z}P_{Z,A(Z)}^{\perp}\leq c^{2},

(48)

\displaystyle{\rm tr\,}\left(A(Z)\gamma_{*}A(Z)|D_{c,0}|^{-1}\right)=o(Z^{\frac{12}{5}}),\ Z\to\infty.

If

fulfills all the above conditions except (

)
we write

.

From now we will – in abuse notation – also use
D_{c,Z}+B
for its
selfadjoint extension in the sense of Nenciu instead of

.

We note that (
47
) is (
10
) and
(

) implies
\mathfrak{Q}_{A}\subset\mathfrak{Q}
which is (
9
).

The following Lemma gives a sufficient condition for an operator to be
in
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
A(Z)
is a family of
symmetric operators in
\mathfrak{H}
with
Z\geq Z_{0}
and for any such
Z

there exist constants
\epsilon_{Z},M_{Z}\in[0,\infty)
such that for
all
\psi\in\mathfrak{Q}

(49)

\|A(Z)\psi\|\leq\epsilon_{Z}\|D_{c,Z}\psi\|+M_{Z}\|\psi\|

and there exists a
\mu\in(0,1)
– independent of

– such that

(50)

\epsilon_{Z}+\frac{M_{Z}}{\sqrt{1-\kappa^{2}}c^{2}}\leq\mu.

Then
A\in\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Proof.

Note that necessarily
\epsilon_{Z}<1
, i.e.,
D_{c,Z}+A(Z)

defined as the operator sum on
\mathfrak{D}(D_{c,Z})=\mathfrak{D}(D_{c,Z}+A(Z))
is
self-adjoint.

Since
|D_{c,Z}|\geq c^{2}(1-\kappa^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, Inequality
(
49
) implies

(51)

|A(Z)|\leq\left(\varepsilon_{Z}+\frac{M_{Z}}{\sqrt{1-\kappa^{2}}c^{2}}\right)|D_{c,Z}|\lesssim|D_{c,0}|

which yields (
45
).

To show (
46
) we pick
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}
, and estimate
by Hardy’s inequality

(52)

\|D_{c,Z}^{A}\psi\|\leq\|D_{c,Z}\psi\|+\|A(Z)u\|\leq\left(1+\epsilon_{Z,A(Z)}+{M_{Z}\over\sqrt{c^{4}-c^{2}Z^{2}}}\right)\|D_{c,Z}\psi\|_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Thus, the upper bound in (
46
) is true with

C:=(1+2\kappa)(1+\mu)\geq(1+2\kappa)\left(1+\epsilon_{Z}+M_{Z}/\sqrt{c^{4}-c^{2}Z^{2}}\right).

Lower bound:

(53)

C^{-1}|D_{c,0}|\leq|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|.

We set

(54)

\mathrm{sgn}(t):=\begin{cases}1,\qquad&t\geq 0;\\
-1,\qquad&t<0.\end{cases}

Proceeding as in Fournais et al.
[
11
, Below
(31)]
, we can get

(55)

\begin{split}&|D_{c,0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}=|D_{c,0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}}}|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{Z}\\
=&\Big(|D_{c,0}|^{{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{D_{c,Z}}}|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\phantom{\Big(}-|D_{c,0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{D_{c,Z}}}\sqrt{|A(Z)|}{\frac{1}{\mathrm{sgn}(A(Z))+B_{Z}}}\sqrt{|A(Z)|}{\frac{1}{D_{c,Z}}}|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)U_{Z}\end{split}

with

and a Birman-Schwinger kernel

.

From (

(56)

|A(Z)|\leq\left(\epsilon_{Z}+\frac{M_{Z}}{\sqrt{1-\kappa^{2}}c^{2}}\right)|D_{c,Z}|.

Therefore

(57)

\|B_{Z}\|\leq\||A(Z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}|D_{c,Z}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\leq\epsilon_{Z}+\frac{M_{Z}}{\sqrt{1-\kappa^{2}}c^{2}}\leq\mu<1

\left\|\frac{1}{\mathrm{sgn}(A(Z))+B_{Z}}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\mu}<\infty.

By Lemma

(59)

\begin{split}&\left\||D_{c,0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\right\|\\
\leq&\left\||D_{c,0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{|D_{c,Z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\right\|\left\|{\frac{1}{|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}|D_{c,Z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|\left(1+\frac{1}{1-\mu}\left\|\sqrt{|A(Z)|}{\frac{1}{|D_{c,Z}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\right\|^{2}\right)\\
\leq&C_{\kappa}^{1/2}\sqrt{1+\mu}\left(1-\mu\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

where the last step uses (
50
). Note that this bound is
uniform in
Z
. Thus we get

(60)

C^{-1}|D_{c,0}|\leq|D_{c,Z}^{A(Z)}|

which is the lower bound.

Eventually we turn to (
47
). This, however, follows from
(
56
) and (
50
).
∎

An alternative criterion is

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
A(Z)
is a family of symmetric operators in
\mathfrak{H}
with

By Lemma

(with

) and (

), we know that

\begin{split}&|A(Z)|\leq\sqrt{2}c\epsilon_{Z}|{\mathbf{p}}|+\sqrt{2}M_{Z}\leq\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{Z}|D_{c,0}|+\sqrt{2}M_{Z}\\
\leq&\sqrt{2}C_{\kappa}^{-1}\epsilon_{Z}|D_{c,Z}|+\sqrt{2}M_{Z}\leq\mu^{\prime}|D_{c,Z}|+\sqrt{2}M_{Z}.\end{split}

Then the selfadjoint extension in the sense Nenciu
[

is just give as the operator sum. Analogously to the proof Lemma

Concerning the upper bound of (

Note that from Lemma

If

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{H}}_{Z}(\gamma_{A})={\mathcal{E}}_{Z}^{\mathrm{H}}(\gamma_{*})+{\rm tr\,}[(D_{*}-c^{2})(\gamma_{A}-\gamma_{*})]+\mathcal{D}[\rho_{\gamma_{A}}-\rho_{*}]\\

Complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic analysis of Inula minipetala Y.N. Lee ex H.B. Kim & D.C. Son (Asteraceae), an endemic Korean species
Authors: Hye Been Kim, Eun Su Kang, Ju Eun Jang, Hee-Young Gil, Dong Chan Son
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe sequenced the chloroplast genome of Inula minipetala and analyzed its phylogenetic relationships. The total length of the chloroplast genome was 150,781 bp, including a large single-copy region of 82,448 bp, a small single-copy region of 18,435 bp, and two inverted repeat regions of 24,949 bp each. It contained 129 genes, including 84 protein-coding sequences (CDSs), 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis using 78 CDSs from 33 taxa showed that I. minipetala is sister to I. linariifolia. These results provide fundamental genomic data for evolutionary and conservation studies of endemic Inula species..Keywords: Plastome, coding sequences, inula, endemic plant, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

Inula minipetala Y.N. Lee ex H.B. Kim & D.C. Son (2026) is an endemic species restricted to Chungcheongnam-do, Korea (Kim et al. 2026). This species was recently validly published under the ICN (Turland et al. 2025) following a previous invalid publication, in line with recent nomenclatural studies of Korean plant taxa (Jang et al. 2025). I. minipetala is characterized by its conspicuously short ligulate ray florets (3–4 mm) and is distinguished from the morphologically similar I. linariifolia by its smaller heads, shorter ray floret laminae, outer phyllaries lacking glandular dots on the adaxial surface, and absence of cypselae pubescence (Kim et al. 2026).

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies have facilitated chloroplast genome sequencing and analysis (Nock et al. 2011; Li et al. 2015; Daniell et al. 2016). Chloroplast genome data are used in plant taxonomy, phylogeny, and evolution studies and provide valuable insights into the evolutionary history of taxa with complex lineages (Yang et al. 2013; Luo et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2023). These applications highlight the importance of chloroplast genome analysis in species identification and conservation.

Endemic plant species restricted to specific countries or regions are important targets for biodiversity conservation (Burlakova et al. 2011; Foggi et al. 2015) and possess value as useful genetic resources (Coelho et al. 2020). Likewise, I. minipetala is of importance for conservation and evolutionary studies; however, its chloroplast genome structure, complete sequence, and phylogenetic relationships have not been reported. Therefore, we report the complete chloroplast genome of I. minipetala as an important foundational data for future phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of the genus Inula, as well as for biodiversity conservation.

Materials and methods

I. minipetala was identified and collected in Seongdang-ri, Chubu-myeon, Geumsan-gun, Chungcheongnam-do, the Republic of Korea (36°14’01.4ʺ N, 127°31’29.6ʺ E) (Figure 1) in August 2025 by Hye Been Kim et al. researchers at the Korea National Arboretum. A voucher specimen was deposited at the herbarium of the Korea National Arboretum (KH) under voucher number KHB1663377 (http://www.nature.go.kr, Contact: Dong Chan Son, sdclym@korea.kr). Fresh leaves were dried and preserved in silica gel for DNA extraction.

Total DNA was extracted using a DNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), following the manufacturer’s protocol. The extracted DNA was detected on a 2% agarose gel, and high-purity genomic DNA (gDNA) was sequenced using an Illumina MiSeq platform with 2 × 301 bp paired-end reads, generating 9,851,954 reads with a Q30 value of 97.8%. De novo plastome assembly was performed using NOVOplasty v.4.3.5 with default parameters, except that the expected genome size was set to 150,000–200,000 bp. The final assembly of the complete cp genome was conducted using Geneious Prime v.2025.2.2 (Kearse et al. 2012). To verify the genome assembly, read coverage depth was assessed using the Draw_SequencingDepth.py script, as described by Ni et al. (2023). Finally, GeSeq (Tillich et al. 2017) was used for genome sequence annotation. Gene arrangement was confirmed by comparing the chloroplast genome data of related species in the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). The chloroplast genome map, including an intron structure map of cis- and trans-splicing genes, was visualized using CPGview (Liu et al. 2023).

To investigate phylogenetic relationships, we analyzed 31 Inuleae species with publicly available plastome data, representing all genera, including Inula, together with one outgroup species from the tribe Athroismeae (Asteraceae). The chloroplast genome sequences of 32 species, excluding I. minipetala, were downloaded from the NCBI GenBank database. In total, 78 coding sequences (CDSs) were concatenated and aligned using MAFFT in the Geneious Prime program v.2025.2.2 (Katoh and Standley 2013). The analysis model was tested using PhyloSuite v.2.0.2 (Zhang et al. 2020; Xiang et al. 2023). The best model for maximum-likelihood (ML) analysis was constructed using ModelFinder v.3.0.1 (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017). ML analysis was performed using IQ-tree v.3.0.1 (Nguyen et al. 2015) by setting the mix {GTR+FO, K3Pu + FO, TPM2u + FO}+R3 model for 5,000 ultrafast bootstraps (Minh et al. 2013). The resulting phylogenetic tree was visualized using FigTree v.1.4.4 (http://tree.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/figtree).

Results

The complete chloroplast genome of I. minipetala has a total sequence length of 150,781 bp and a GC content of 37.7% (Figure 2), with an average read coverage depth of 151.08 X (Figure S1). The genome displayed a typical quadripartite structure comprising a pair of inverted repeats (IR, 24,949 bp each) separating a large single-copy region (LSC, 82,448 bp) from a small single-copy region (SSC, 18,435 bp). The complete genome sequence was deposited in GenBank under accession number PX984400.

The chloroplast genome comprised 129 genes, including 84 CDSs, 37 tRNA genes, 8 rRNA genes. The IR contained 17 genes, including 6 CDS genes, 7 tRNA genes, and 4 rRNA genes (CDS: ndhB, rpl2, rpl23, rps7, rps12, ycf2; tRNA: trnA-UGC, trnl-CAU, trnl-GAU, trnL-CAA, trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnV-GAC; rRNA: rrn4.5, rrn5, rrn16, rrn23). A total of 11 cis-splicing genes were observed; among these, nine genes contained a single intron (atpF, ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, rpl2, rpl16, rpoC1, and rps16) while two genes contained two introns (clpP1 and pafI) (Figure S2). The trans-spliced gene rps12 had a single intron spanning the LSC and IR regions, with the 3′ end in the LSC region and the 5′ end in the IR region (Figure S3).

ML phylogenetic analysis supported the monophyly of the subtribe Inulinae and Plucheinae (bootstrap value = 100) (Figure 3). Within the subtribe Inulinae, except for I. grandis Schrenk ex Fisch. & C.A.Mey., Inula and Pentanema formed a clade (bootstrap value = 100), and within this clade, I. minipetala was resolved as sister to I. linariifolia.

Discussion and conclusion

The chloroplast genome of I. minipetala exhibited a typical quadripartite structure similar to that of most angiosperms (Kim et al. 2024).

The ML analysis results were consistent with those of previous phylogenetic studies of tribe Inuleae (Anderberg et al. 2005; Pornpongrungrueng et al. 2007; Englund et al. 2009; Nylinder and Anderberg 2015; Gutiérrez-Larruscain et al. 2018). Inula taxa formed a clade together with Pentanema taxa (bootstrap value = 100); however, the two genera were recovered as paraphyletic and were not clearly separated at the generic level. These results support previous studies suggesting that the morphological boundary between Inula and Pentanema remains unclear (Anderberg 1991; Duman and Anderberg 1999; Englund et al. 2009), and may also support the view that some lineages should be retained within a broader circumscription of Inula due to the unresolved phylogenetic relationships within the Inula–Pentanema complex (Yuan et al. 2025). Meanwhile, I. minipetala was recovered as sister to I. linariifolia, consistent with previous nrITS-based phylogenetic analyses (Kim et al. 2026). Although bootstrap support was relatively low (bootstrap value = 60), our results provide additional molecular evidence supporting their close phylogenetic relationship. Given that only six Inula taxa and two Pentanema taxa were included in this study, further analyses incorporating additional species will be necessary to more accurately clarify their phylogenetic relationships, including the unresolved paraphyly between Inula and Pentanema and the low bootstrap support of several internal clades.

This study provides comprehensive chloroplast genome data for I. minipetala and serves as a foundational resource for future studies on the evolutionary diversification and taxonomic complexity of the Inula-Pentanema complex. Furthermore, the genome-based data generated in this study will contribute to future phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of Inula and conservation research on endemic plant diversity in Korea.

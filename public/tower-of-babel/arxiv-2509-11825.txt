Rough stochastic filtering
Fabio Bugini, Peter K. Friz, Khoa Lê, Huilin Zhang

Abstract
The theory of rough stochastic differential equations [ FHL24 ] is applied to revisit classical problems in stochastic filtering. We provide rough counterparts to the Kallianpur–Striebel formula and the Zakai and Kushner–Stratonovich equations, seen to coincide with classical objects upon randomization. We follow Crisan–Pardoux [ CP24 ] in doing so in a correlated diffusion setting. Well-posedness of the (rough) filtering equation is seen to hold under dimension-independent regularity assumption, in contrast to the stochastic case.

Introduction

The stochastic filtering problem, concerned with the optimal estimation of an evolving hidden state
X_{t}
from noisy observations
Y_{t}
, has a rich history bridging mathematics, engineering, and applied sciences. Its origins can be traced to the discrete-time works of Kolmogorov and Krein in the 1930s, and to Norbert Wiener’s seminal 1940s studies on continuous-time estimation, culminating in the Wiener filter for stationary signals observed through additive noise. The advent of the Kalman–Bucy filter in the early 1960s marked a breakthrough for linear Gaussian models, enabling exact closed-form solutions and finding wide application in aerospace and control engineering.

The nonlinear filtering theory began to develop soon thereafter. Stratonovich introduced early forms of the filtering equation using his eponymous stochastic calculus, while Kushner provided a rigorous derivation via Itô calculus, followed by the general treatment of correlated signal-observation systems by Shiryaev. The influential Kallianpur–Striebel formula and the Zakai equation (Duncan, Mortensen, Zakai) further advanced the field by offering tractable representations of the conditional law. Rigorous analysis of the resulting stochastic partial differential equations was later achieved by Bensoussan, Pardoux, Krylov, Rozovsky and many others.

Over the last 40 years, the scope of filtering theory has expanded considerable (including pathwise robustness (Clark, Davis), finite-dimensional filters, Lie-algebraic methods) and stochastic filtering remains a vibrant area at the intersection of SPDE theory, particle methods, numerical analysis control, and information theory, with many ongoing works. Organization of this article

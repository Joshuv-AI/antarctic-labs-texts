Strichartz estimates for orthonormal systems on compact manifolds
Xing Wang, An Zhang and Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. We establish new Strichartz estimates for orthonormal systems on compact Riemannian manifolds in the case of wave, Klein-Gordon and fractional Schrödinger equations. Our results generalize the classical (single-function) Strichartz estimates on compact manifolds by Kapitanski [ 22 ] , Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov [ 5 ] , Dinh [ 12 ] , and extend the Euclidean orthonormal version by Frank-Lewin-Lieb-Seiringer [ 14 ] , Frank-Sabin [ 15 ] , Bez-Lee-Nakamura [ 2 ] . On the flat torus, our new results for the Schrödinger equation cover prior work of Nakamura [ 30 ] , which exploits the dispersive estimate of Kenig-Ponce-Vega [ 24 ] . We achieve sharp results on compact manifolds by combining the frequency localized dispersive estimates for small time intervals with the duality principle due to Frank-Sabin. We construct examples to show these results can be saturated on the sphere, and we can improve them on the flat torus by using Bourgain-Demeter’s decoupling theorem to obtain new decoupling inequalities for certain non-smooth hypersurfaces. As an application, we obtain the well-posedness of infinite systems of dispersive equations with Hartree-type nonlinearity.



1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 1
. Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional smooth compact Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplacian-Beltrami operator on
M
. Let
\Delta=-\Delta_{g}
. Let
e^{itP}f
denote the solution to the initial value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+Pu=0,\quad(x,t)\in M\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

A wide class of dispersive equations have this form, such as the Schrödinger equation
P=\Delta
, the fractional Schrödinger equation
P=\Delta^{\alpha/2}
(
\alpha\neq 0,1
), the wave equation
P=\sqrt{\Delta}
, and the Klein-Gordon equation
P=\sqrt{1+\Delta}
.

In quantum mechanics, a system of
N
independent fermions is described by a collection of
N
orthonormal functions
f_{1},...,f_{N}
in
L^{2}
. So functional inequalities that incorporate a significant number of orthonormal functions are highly valuable for the mathematical analysis of large-scale quantum systems. The inequalities have applications to the Hartree equation modeling infinitely many fermions in a quantum system, see Chen–Hong–Pavlovic
[
7
,
8
]
, Frank–Sabin
[
15
]
, Lewin–Sabin
[
26
,
25
]
and Sabin
[
32
]
. The idea in this line of investigation is to generalize the classical inequalities for a single-function input to an orthonormal system. In the pioneering work of Lieb-Thirring
[
29
]
, they first established such an extension of the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality. In the recent work of Frank-Lewin-Lieb-Seiringer
[
14
]
for the Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta}
, they proved a generalization of the well known Strichartz estimates for systems of orthonormal functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Later, Frank-Sabin
[
15
]
, Bez-Hong-Lee-Nakamura-Sawano
[
1
]
, Bez-Lee-Nakamura
[
2
]
, Feng-Mondal-Song-Wu
[
13
]
investigated a wide class of dispersive equations and established the Strichartz estimates for systems of orthonormal functions on the Euclidean space. To our best knowledge, there are only a few results concerning such generalizations on compact Riemannian manfiolds. Frank-Sabin
[
16
]
established the spectral cluster bounds for orthonormal systems on compact manifolds, and recently Ren-Zhang
[
31
]
obtained some improvements on non-positively curved manifolds. Nakamura
[
30
]
studied the Strichartz estimates on the flat torus with orthonormal system input and obtained sharp estimates in certain sense. In this paper, we provide substantial progress in this direction, extending the orthonormal Strichartz estimates to general compact Riemannian manifolds.

The classical (single-function) Strichartz estimates in the Euclidean space date back to the seminal paper of Strichartz
[
39
]
. See also Ginibre-Velo
[
18
]
, Keel-Tao
[
23
]
and references therein. In the case of compact manifolds, Kapitanski
[
22
]
, Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
5
]
, Dinh
[
12
]
obtained Strichartz estimates for the wave, the Schrödinger and the fractional Schrödinger equations respectively. See also Cacciafesta-Danesi-Meng
[
6
]
for the Dirac equations. In the case of the torus, see e.g. the celebrated work of Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
]
. In this paper, we shall investigate their generalizations to orthonormal systems.

In the following, let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a fixed bounded interval of length
|I|\approx 1
. We fix
(e_{k})_{k}
to be an orthonormal eigenbasis in
L^{2}(M)
associated with the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
of
\sqrt{\Delta}
. Here
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...
are arranged in increasing order and we account for multiplicity. We define the Fourier coefficient
\hat{f}(k)=\langle f,e_{k}\rangle
for
f\in L^{2}(M)
. We call
(f_{j})_{j}
an orthonormal system in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
if the functions
f_{j}
are orthonormal in
\mathcal{H}
. We investigate the Strichartz estimates of the form

(1)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim N^{\sigma}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(M)
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
. The estimates are independent of the choice of the eigenbasis
(e_{k})_{k}
. The main goal in the line of investigation is to determine the optimal range of
\beta
for a fixed exponent
\sigma
. A natural choice of
\sigma
is just the one in the classical (single-function) case. In this case, (
1
) trivially holds with
\beta=1
by Minkowski inequality, while the question is to determine the largest
\beta
by exploiting the orthogonality between the functions. On the other hand, it is also interesting to determine the optimal range of
\beta
for any fixed
\sigma
. We shall establish sharp estimates in the form of (
1
) by combining the frequency localized dispersive estimates for small time intervals with the duality principle due to Frank-Sabin. In comparison, Nakamura
[
30
]
and Bez-Lee-Nakamura
[
2
]
exploit stronger frequency global dispersive estimates in the flat case, as in the prior work of Kenig-Ponce-Vega
[
24
]
.

Notations.
Throughout this paper,
X\lesssim Y
means
X\leq CY
for some positive constants

. If the constant depends on
\varepsilon>0
, we denote
X\lesssim_{\varepsilon}Y
. If

1.1.

in

with

, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
, and the following

in the four groups:

(i)
Subcritical regime:
\beta\leq\frac{d}{d-2/p}=\frac{2q}{q+2}

(ii)
Critical point:

(iii)
Supercritical regime:

(iv)
Keel-Tao endpoint:

Next, we obtain new (single-function) Strichartz estimates on the flat torus for fractional Schrödinger equations. We achieve this by establishing a new decoupling inequality for the hypersurface
(\xi,|\xi|^{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Theorem 2
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha\geq 2,\ N\geq 10
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
supp\hat{f}\subset[-N,N]^{d}
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(4)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\sigma_{1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

where

(5)

\sigma_{1}(\alpha)=\max\Big\{0,\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}\Big\}+\varepsilon.

The case
\alpha=2
is due to Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
, and the case
\alpha>2
is new. In particular, at the Keel-Tao endpoint
(p,q)=(2,\frac{2d}{d-2})
we have

(6)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{2}{d}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\ \ \alpha\geq 2.

For
d>4
, this gives the best estimate up to now, even in the case
\alpha=2
. See e.g.
[
9
]
for recent related work on the mixed norm

decoupling inequality. It seems reasonable to expect that

(7)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\ \ \alpha\geq 2.

See
[
4
, Conjecture 2.6]
for the related Discrete Restriction Conjecture on the flat torus.

8
to improve Theorem
1
on the flat torus. By Theorem

Corollary 1
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha\geq 2,\ N\geq 10
. Suppose
2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L_{x}^{q/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim N^{\sigma}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

(f_{j})_{j}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset[-N,N]^{d}
, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
, and all
\sigma\in(0,d]

\beta<\frac{d}{d-\sigma}

The range of

We may improve the range of

Corollary 2
.

Let

,
\alpha\geq 2

1.2.
Wave and Klein-Gordon equations

It is well-known that the Klein-Gordon propagator
e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}
behaves like the wave propagator
e^{it\sqrt{\Delta}}
in the high-frequency regime, while it behaves like the Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta}
in the low-frequency regime. For the Strichartz estimates on compact manifolds, the high-frequency regime is more significant. As in the forthcoming Lemma
3
, the wave and the Klein-Gordon equations share the same dispersive property on compact manifolds. Indeed, they can be handled in a unified way as pseudodifferential operators, see Sogge
[
38
, Chapter 4]
. So we shall only consider the sharp wave admissible pairs
(p,q)
. By the finite propagation speed property, the Strichartz estimates on compact manifolds are essentially the same as in the Euclidean space.

Let
d\geq 2
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. We divide these sharp wave admissible pairs
(p,q)
in the following four groups. See Figure
3
.

(i) Subcritical regime:
d\geq 2
,
2\leq q<\frac{2d}{d-2}

(ii) Critical point:
d\geq 3
,
q=\frac{2d}{d-2}

(iii) Supercritical regime:
d=3
,
\frac{2d}{d-2}<q<\infty
or
d\geq 4
,
\frac{2d}{d-2}<q<\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3}

(iv) Keel-Tao endpoint:
d\geq 4
,
q=\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3}
.

Figure 3.
Sharp wave admissible pairs

Theorem 3
.

Let
d\geq 2,\ m\geq 0,\ N\geq 10
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
.
Let
\sigma_{0}=\frac{2}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}
. Then

(10)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim N^{\sigma_{0}}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

holds for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(M)
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
, and the following
\beta
with respect to the pairs
(p,q)
in the four groups:

(i)
Subcritical regime:
\beta\leq\frac{d-1}{d-1-2/p}=\frac{2q}{q+2}

(ii)
Critical point:
\beta<p/2

(iii)
Supercritical regime:
\beta<p/2

(iv)
Keel-Tao endpoint:

.

The ranges of
\beta
in (ii)(iii)(iv) are sharp on any compact manifold by the necessary condition (

). It is open to show the sharpness for the subcritical regime. Our necessary conditions (

) and (

) show the sharpness in the extreme cases

and

, so we expect that the intermediate cases are also sharp in some sense. The difficulty to show the sharpness for the subcritical regime also appears in the Euclidean version, see Bez-Lee-Nakamura
[

, Section 6]
. As before, the optimality of
\beta
only makes sense when the exponent of

is fixed, so we fix
\sigma_{0}/2
to be the Sobolev exponent in the classical (single-function) Strichartz estimates for the wave equation on compact manifolds by Kapitanski
[

]
(see also
[

]
).

1.3.
Frequency global estimates

By the vector-valued version of the Littlewood-Paley inequality (see
[
33
, Lemma 1]
), one can upgrade the frequency localized estimate (
1
) to the frequency global one: for all
s>\sigma/2
,

(11)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

holds for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
H^{s}(M)
and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
. Unlike the single-function case, it seems difficult to take
s=\sigma/2
in the case of orthonormal data by Littlewood-Paley theory. Similar difficulties also appear in the Euclidean case. Indeed, Bez-Hong-Lee-Nakamura-Sawano
[
1
]
observed a crucial fact that on a certain critical line the desired estimates without the frequency localization are not true, see
[
1
, Prop. 5.2]
. Bez-Lee-Nakamura
[
2
]
achieved frequency global estimates by establishing delicate weighted oscillatory integral estimates and the global dispersive estimates in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see
[
2
, Prop. 6]
. Nevertheless, it seems difficult to proceed in this way on compact manifolds.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

In Section 2, we prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
by combining the frequency localized dispersive estimates for small time intervals with the duality principle due to Frank-Sabin. In Section 3, we prove Strichartz estimates with variable exponents of

and investigate how the optimal range of

2.
Proof of Strichartz estimates on general manifolds

To prove Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
, we shall use the duality principle in Frank-Sabin
[
15
]
that can transfer the orthonormal inequalities to Schatten norm estimates, and the frequency localized dispersive estimates in Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
, Dinh
[
12
, (3.8)]
, Cacciafesta-Danesi-Meng
[
6
, Prop. 3]
, Sogge
[
38
, Chapter 4]
.

We recall the definition of the Schatten norm. For
\beta\in[1,\infty)
,
\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}=\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}(L^{2}(M))
denotes the Schatten space based on
L^{2}(M)
that is the space of all compact operators
T
on
L^{2}(M)
such that
\text{Tr}|T|^{\beta}<\infty
with
|T|=\sqrt{TT^{*}}
, and its norm is defined by
\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}}=(\text{Tr}|T|^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
. If
\beta=\infty
, we define

\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}}=\|T\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}.

Also,
\mathfrak{S}^{2}
is the Hilbert-Schimdt class and the
\mathfrak{S}^{2}
norm is given by

\|T\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}=\|K\|_{L^{2}(M\times M)}

if
K
is the integral kernel of
T
. See Simon
[
36
]
for more details on the Schatten classes.

Next, we recall the duality principle in Frank-Sabin
[
15
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 1

(Duality principle)
.

Let
p,q,\beta\geq 1
. Suppose that
T
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}
to
L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}
. Then

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|Tf_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\leq C\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

holds for all orthonormal system
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}
and all
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}
in
l^{\beta}
if and only if

\|WTT^{*}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta^{\prime}}}\leq C\|W\|_{L_{t}^{2(p/2)^{\prime}}L_{x}^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}}^{2}

holds for all
W\in L_{t}^{2(p/2)^{\prime}}L_{x}^{2(q/2)^{\prime}}
.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-1,1]^{d}}\leq\psi\leq{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-2,2]^{d}}.

Let
(e_{k})_{k}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis in
L^{2}(M)
associated with the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
of
\sqrt{\Delta}
. For
f\in L^{2}(M)
, let

\mathcal{E}_{N}f(t,x)=\sum_{k}\psi(\lambda_{k}/N)\hat{f}(k)e^{it\lambda_{k}^{\alpha}}e_{k}(x).

To prove (
3
), it suffices to show

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|\mathcal{E}_{N}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}(I\times M)}\lesssim N^{\sigma_{0}}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}.

At the endpoint
(p,q)=(\infty,2)
we have
\beta=1
, and by Minkowski inequality and Plancherel theorem

(12)

We extend Theorem

3.1.
Fractional Schrödinger equations

Theorem 4
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\},\ N\geq 10
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. Let
\sigma_{0}
be defined in (
2
).
Then

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim N^{\sigma}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

holds for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(M)
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
, and the following
\sigma
and
\beta
with respect to the pairs
(p,q)
in the four groups:

(i) Subcritical regime:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},d]
,
\beta\leq\frac{d-\sigma_{0}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}

(ii) Critical point:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},d)
,
\beta<\frac{d-\sigma_{0}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}
, or
\sigma=d
,
\beta\leq\infty

(iii) Supercritical regime:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*})
,
\beta<(\frac{2}{p}+\sigma_{0}-\sigma)^{-1}
, or
\sigma\in[\sigma_{*},d]
,
\beta\leq\frac{d-\sigma_{*}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}

(iv) Keel-Tao endpoint:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*})
,
\beta\leq(\frac{2}{p}+\sigma_{0}-\sigma)^{-1}
, or
\sigma\in[\sigma_{*},d]
,
\beta\leq\frac{d-\sigma_{*}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}
.

Here

(23)

\displaystyle\beta_{*}=\frac{d}{d-2/p}=\frac{2q}{q+2},\ \ \sigma_{*}=\sigma_{0}+\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{\beta_{*}}.

When
\alpha>1
, the ranges of
\beta
in (i)(ii) are essentially sharp by the necessary conditions (
54
), and the ranges of
\beta
for
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*}]
in (iii)(iv) are also essentially sharp by the necessary condition (
57
). It is open to show the ranges of
\beta
for
\sigma\in[\sigma_{*},d]
in (iii)(iv) are sharp. Since they are sharp in the extreme cases
\sigma=\sigma_{*}
and
\sigma=d
by the necessary condition (
57
), we expect that they are also sharp for the intermediate values.

Theorem
4
can be deduced from interpolation between Theorem
1
, the universal bound (
52
), and the “kink point” estimate

(24)

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta_{*}}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\sigma_{*}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ q>\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}\\
N^{\sigma_{*}}(\log N),\ q=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}.\end{cases}

By the necessary condition (
57
), the estimate (
24
) is sharp when
\alpha>1
, in the sense that
\beta_{*}
cannot be replaced by any
\beta>\beta_{*}
. Moreover, Nakamura
[
30

obtained similar estimates for the Schrödinger propagator

on the flat torus with an

In Theorem

, the subcritical regime directly follows from interpolation between Theorem
1
and the universal bound (

in the supercritical regime. Unlike the proof of Theorem

, we shall further decompose the kernel of
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{*}
to establish the kink point estimate (

is given by (

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\|WT_{\ell,m}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|W(t,x)K_{\ell,m}(t,x,s,y)\overline{W}(s,y)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,s,y}}

\displaystyle\beta_{*}=\frac{d}{d-2/p},\ \ \sigma_{*}=\sigma_{0}+\frac{2(d+1)}{pd}-1,

given in (
2
), then we have

(28)

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta_{*}}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\sigma_{*}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ r\in[d,d+1)\\
N^{\sigma_{*}}(\log N),\ r=d+1.\end{cases}

Note that
\sigma_{*}=\sigma_{0}
is equivalent to
p=\frac{2(d+1)}{d}
. By interpolation between (
28
), (
52
) and Theorem

, we obtain Theorem
4
.

In particular, when
d\geq 1
, at the critical point
(p,q)=(\frac{2(d+1)}{d},\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1})
, we have
r=d+1
,
\beta=\frac{d+1}{d}
and

(29)

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\frac{2}{p}}(\log N),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \alpha>1\\
N^{\frac{2(2-\alpha)}{p}}(\log N),\ \ \alpha\in(0,1).\end{cases}

When

,

(30)

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{d}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \alpha>1\\
N^{\frac{2(2-\alpha)}{p}+\frac{1}{d}},\ \ \alpha\in(0,1).\end{cases}

Note that when

).

Remark 1.
To compare this method with the proof of Theorem
1
, we remark that one can slightly modify this method to handle the subcritical regime (

) up to a log loss:

(31)

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\frac{2}{p}}(\log N),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \alpha>1\\
N^{\frac{2(2-\alpha)}{p}}(\log N),\ \ \ \alpha\in(0,1).\end{cases}

Indeed, for

by Young’s inequality, we can replace (

\displaystyle\|WT_{\ell,m}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|W(t,x)K_{\ell,m}(t,x,s,y)\overline{W}(s,y)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,s,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{r-d}}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}2^{\frac{2+d-r}{2}m}\cdot\min\{2^{-dm/2}2^{(2-\alpha)d\ell/2},\ 2^{d\ell}\}.

Then repeating the interpolation argument above we can obtain (
31
). So we expect that this method gives essentially sharp bounds for all admissible
(p,q)
.

Remark 2.
It is natural to ask whether one can remove log factor in (

) at the critical point
(p,q)=(\frac{2(d+1)}{d},\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1})

[

.

3.3.
Wave and Klein-Gordon equations

We may also extend Theorem
3
for variable exponents of
N
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
d\geq 2,\ N\geq 10
. Let
m\geq 0
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. Let
\sigma_{0}=\frac{2}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}

Then

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim N^{\sigma}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

(f_{j})_{j}
in

with

, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
, and the following
\sigma
and
\beta
with respect to the pairs
(p,q)
in the four groups:

(i) Subcritical regime:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},d]
,
\beta\leq\frac{d-\sigma_{0}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}

(ii) Critical point:
\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},d)
,
\beta<\frac{d-\sigma_{0}}{d-\sigma}\beta_{*}
, or
\sigma=d
,

\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*})

\sigma\in[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{*})

\beta\leq(\frac{2}{p}+\sigma_{0}-\sigma)^{-1}
, or
\sigma\in[\sigma_{*},d]

Here

(32)

\displaystyle\beta_{*}=\frac{d-1}{d-1-2/p}=\frac{2q}{q+2},\ \ \sigma_{*}=\sigma_{0}+\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{\beta_{*}}.

The ranges of

by the necessary conditions (

) and (

), the range of

in (i) is sharp in the extreme cases

and

. By the necessary condition (

), the ranges of
\beta
for

in (iii)(iv) are sharp in the extreme cases

and

. So we expect that they are also sharp for the intermediate values.

Theorem
5
can be deduced from interpolation between Theorem
3
, the universal bound (

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta_{*}}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\sigma_{*}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ q>\frac{2d}{d-2}\\

By the necessary condition (

), the estimate (

\beta_{*}
cannot be replaced by any

.

The subcritical regime in Theorem

directly follows from interpolation between Theorem

and the universal bound (

). To handle the supercritical regime, we shall modify the proof of Theorem

to establish the kink point estimate (

with respect to

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
5

Let
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(s)=1
be the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, where each
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
is supported in
\{|s|\approx 2^{n}\}
. We define the operator
T_{\ell,n}
with the kernel

K_{\ell,n}(t,x,s,y)=\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(|t-s|)\sum_{k}\psi(\lambda_{k}/N)^{2}\varphi_{\ell}(\lambda_{k})^{2}e^{i(t-s)\sqrt{m^{2}+\lambda_{k}^{2}}}e_{k}(x)\overline{e_{k}(y)}.

This comes from the dyadic decomposition of the operator
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}^{*}
with
\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}
given by (
21
).
Fix
r\in[d-1,d]
. Let
\beta^{\prime}=2(q/2)^{\prime}=(d-1)p/2=r
. As before, we shall estimate
\|WT_{\ell,n}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r}}
by interpolation between the
\mathfrak{S}^{2}
norm and the
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}

We first estimate the
\mathfrak{S}^{2}
norm. By Lemma

we obtain for

Then we have

\displaystyle\|WT_{\ell,n}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|W(t,x)K_{\ell,n}(t,x,s,y)\overline{W}(s,y)\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,s,y}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{4}{r-d+1}}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}2^{n/2}\cdot\min\{2^{-(d-1)n/2}2^{(d+1)\ell/2},\ 2^{d\ell}\}.

Here we require

, namely

, by using the inequality (

).

Next, we estimate the
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\|WT_{\ell,n}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|WT_{\ell,n}\overline{W}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}\to L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|W\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}\|T_{\ell,n}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}\to L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{n}\|W\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}^{2}.

Then interpolation gives

\displaystyle\|WT_{\ell,n}\overline{W}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{r}}\lesssim\|W\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2r}{r-d+1}}L_{x}^{r}}^{2}2^{n(1-\frac{1}{r})}\min\{2^{-(d-1)n/r}2^{(d+1)\ell/r},\ 2^{2d\ell/r}\}.

Summing over

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}\cdot\begin{cases}2^{(\frac{2d+1}{r}-1)\ell},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ r\in[d-1,d)\\

Recall that

\displaystyle\beta_{*}=\frac{d-1}{d-1-2/p},\ \ \sigma_{*}=\frac{2(d+1)}{p(d-1)}+\frac{2d}{p(d-1)}-1,

then summing over

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|\mathcal{E}_{N,\ell}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta_{*}}}\cdot\begin{cases}N^{\sigma_{*}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ r\in[d-1,d)\\
N^{\sigma_{*}}(\log N),\ r=d.\end{cases}

This proves (
33
). Note that
\sigma_{*}=\frac{2}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}
is equivalent to

. By interpolation between (
33
), (
52
) and Theorem

, we obtain Theorem

.

In particular, when
d\geq 2
, at the critical point
(p,q)=(\frac{2d}{d-1},\frac{2d}{d-2})
, we have

,

and

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}}(\log N)\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}.

When
d\geq 3
, at the Keel-Tao endpoint
(p,q)=(2,\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3})
, we have

,
\beta=\frac{d-1}{d-2}
and

\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{it\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}}f_{j}|^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{2}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}+\frac{1}{d-1}}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}.

Note that this estimate is sharp by the necessary condition (
60
).

4.
Decoupling inequalities and improvements on the flat torus

Let
d\geq 1
. For
c_{1},...,c_{d+1}>0
, let
R=[-c_{1},c_{1}]\times...\times[-c_{d+1},c_{d+1}]
be a rectangular box. We shall use two weight functions associated with
R

\omega_{R}(x)=(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}|x_{j}|/c_{j})^{-10d}

\tilde{\omega}_{R}(x)=(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}|x_{j}|/c_{j})^{-8d}.

Let
\alpha>1
and
S=\{(y,|y|^{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:y\in[-1,1]^{d}\}
. We define the extension operator

E_{Q}g(x)=\int_{Q}g(y)e(x_{1}y_{1}+...+x_{d}y_{d}+x_{d+1}|y|^{\alpha})dy

where
Q
is some subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
. Let
\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}([-1,1]^{d})
denote a partition of
[-1,1]^{d}
into cubes of side length
\delta^{1/2}
. We first prove the following decoupling inequality for the hypersurface
S
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. Then we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(34)

\|E_{[-1,1]^{d}}g\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}([-1,1]^{d})}\|E_{\Delta}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{B_{R}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
B_{R}
is a ball of radius
R\geq\delta^{-{\max\{1,\alpha/2\}}}
.

The proof extends the strategy of the one dimensional case in
[
3
]
, which used a piece of parabola to locally approximate the curve and then apply Bourgain-Demeter’s decoupling theorem. For recent works on decoupling inequalities for smooth hypersurfaces with vanishing Gaussian curvature, see e.g. Demeter
[
10
, Section 12.6]
, Yang
[
42
]
, Li-Yang
[
27
,
28
]
and Guth-Maldague-Oh
[
20
]
. Now we use this inequality to obtain the Strichartz estimates, and postpone its proof to the end of this section.

Theorem
6
immediately implies a discrete restriction estimate.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. Let
\Lambda
be a
N^{-1}
-separated set in
[-1,1]^{d}
. Then we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(35)

\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda}a_{\xi}e(x_{1}\xi_{1}+...+x_{d}\xi_{d}+x_{d+1}|\xi|^{\alpha})\Big|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda}|a_{\xi}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
B_{R}
is a ball of radius
R\geq N^{\max\{2,\alpha\}}
.

This discrete restriction estimate together with the trivial endpoint
p=\infty
estimate directly implies the Strichartz estimate on the flat torus in Theorem
7
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha>1,\ N\geq 10
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
supp\hat{f}\subset[-N,N]^{d}
. Then we have for all
\varepsilon>0
and
2\leq q\leq\infty

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\sigma_{1}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

where

(36)

\sigma_{1}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\max\{(2-\alpha)\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}),\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+\alpha}{q}\}+\varepsilon,\ \ 1<\alpha<2\\
\max\{0,\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{q}\}+\varepsilon,\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\alpha\geq 2.\end{cases}

To our best knowledge, the case
\alpha=2
is due to Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
. After completing this paper, we learned that results essentially equivalent to our Theorem
7
were recently obtained by Schippa
[
34
, Prop. 1.1]
, and the special case
d=1
and
p=q=4
was obtained earlier by Demibras-Erdoğan-Tzirakis
[
11
, Theorem 4]
). The proof of Theorem
7
is standard. For completeness, we include a short proof here.

Proof of Theorem
7
.
Let
f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|\leq N}a_{k}e(k\cdot x)
. Let
\Lambda=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\xi=k/N
. Let
I
be a bounded interval of length
|I|>0
, By scaling and periodicity in the space variable, we can use Corollary
3
with
p=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{I\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(N|I|)^{d}N^{\alpha}}\int_{|t|\lesssim N^{\alpha}|I|,\ |y|\lesssim N|I|}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda}a_{N\xi}e(y\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{\alpha})\Big|^{p}dtdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(N|I|)^{d}N^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{N^{d(\alpha-1)}}\int_{|t|\lesssim N^{\alpha}|I|,\ |y|\lesssim N^{\alpha}|I|}\Big|\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda}a_{N\xi}e(y\cdot\xi+t|\xi|^{\alpha})\Big|^{p}dtdy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(N|I|)^{d}N^{\alpha}}\frac{1}{N^{d(\alpha-1)}}\cdot(N^{\alpha}|I|)^{d+1}N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx|I|N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{p}.

Here we require
N^{\alpha}|I|\gtrsim N^{\max\{2,\alpha\}}
in the third inequality. So we fix
|I|=N^{2-\alpha}
when
\alpha<2
and
|I|=1
when
\alpha\geq 2
. Then Theorem
7
follows from interpolation between
p=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
and the endpoints
p=2,\infty
.

As a consequence, we obtain estimates for the sharp Schrödinger admissible pairs.

Theorem 8
.

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\},\ N\geq 10
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
supp\hat{f}\subset[-N,N]^{d}
. Suppose
p\geq 2,\ q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(37)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\sigma_{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

where

(38)

\sigma_{2}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{0}(\alpha)/2,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \quad\quad\quad 0<\alpha<1\\
\min\{\sigma_{0}(\alpha)/2,\ \sigma_{1}(\alpha)\},\ \ \ \ \ \alpha>1,\end{cases}

with
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
given by (
2
) and (
36
).

Proof of Theorem
8
.
The exponent
\sigma_{0}(\alpha)/2
follows from Dinh
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
. We just need to handle
\alpha>1
. We use Hölder inequality to control
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}
norm by
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{q}
norm in Theorem
7
if
p\leq q
. The remaining case
p>q
follows from interpolation between
p=q=\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
and the trivial endpoint
(p,q)=(\infty,2)
. These give the exponent
\sigma_{1}(\alpha)
.

Examples, sharpness and improvements.
Consider the function
f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|\leq N}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}
. We have
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\approx N^{d/2}
and

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\geq\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}(\{|t|<N^{-\alpha}\})L^{q}_{x}(\{|x|<N^{-1}\})}\approx N^{d-\frac{d}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{p}}.

This implies a lower bound

(39)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\gtrsim N^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

On the other hand, we consider the eigenfunction
f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|=N}e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}
. For
R\in\mathbb{N}
, let

r_{d}(R)=\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|^{2}=R\}.

It is well-known that
r_{d}(R)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{d-2}{2}+\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. The bound is essentially sharp, since

(40)

\max_{n\leq R}r_{d}(n)\geq\frac{1}{R}\sum_{n\leq R}r_{d}(n)=\frac{1}{R}\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|^{2}\leq R\}\approx R^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

We have
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\approx\sqrt{r_{d}(N^{2})}
and

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\geq\|f\|_{L^{q}_{x}(\{|x|<N^{-1}\})}\approx r_{d}(N^{2})N^{-\frac{d}{q}}.

This gives a lower bound

(41)

\|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{d+1})}\gtrsim\sqrt{r_{d}(N^{2})}N^{-\frac{d}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Together with (
40
), this implies that the lower bound
N^{\frac{d-2}{2}-\frac{d}{q}}
can be achieved by infinitely many
N
.

For the diagonal cases
p=q
, we restrict to
\alpha>1
. The exponent
\sigma_{1}(\alpha)
in Theorem
7
is essentially sharp for the following cases:

•

1<\alpha<2
for
q\geq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
,

•

\alpha=2
for all
q\geq 2
,

•

\alpha>2
for
2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
.

For the case
q<\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
when
1<\alpha<2
, the potential for further improvements is tied to weighted
l^{2}
decoupling inequalities. Related weighted decoupling inequalities in the weaker
l^{p}
setting were recently obtained by Gan-Wu
[
17
]
.

For the sharp Schrödinger admissible pair
(p,q)
, we have

\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{p}=(2-\alpha)\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})=\frac{2-\alpha}{p},

so the exponent
\sigma_{2}(\alpha)
in Theorem
8
is essentially sharp when

•

0<\alpha<1
for all
q\geq 2
,

•

\alpha>1
for
2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
.

Moreover, note that
\sigma_{2}(\alpha)<\sigma_{0}(\alpha)/2
when
\alpha\geq 2
with
d>4
or
1<\alpha<2
with

, so it improves the exponent in Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
5
]
and Dinh
[
12
]
on general manifolds in these cases.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem

Theorem

and Minkowski inequality.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. Then we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(44)

\|E_{[-1,1]^{d}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{R_{\delta}})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}([-1,1]^{d})}\|E_{\Delta}g\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\omega}_{R_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
R_{\delta}
is a rectangular box of size
\delta^{-1}\times...\times\delta^{-1}\times\delta^{-\max\{1,\alpha/2\}}
.

Proof.
We dyadically decompose the cube
[-1,1]^{d}
into

[-1,1]^{d}=\{y\in[-1,1]^{d}:|y|\leq\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\{y\in[-1,1]^{d}:2^{k-1}\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq|y|\leq 2^{k}\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\}.

Here
K\approx\log(\delta^{-1})
. The first part can be easily controlled by Minkowski and Hölder inequalities. It suffices to prove that for any
\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq a\leq 1/2
and the annulus

A_{a}=\{y\in[-1,1]^{d}:a\leq|y|\leq 2a\}

we have

(45)

\|E_{A_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{R_{\delta}})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}(A_{a})}\|E_{\Delta}g\|_{L^{p}(\tilde{\omega}_{R_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We claim that for any
\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq a\leq 1/2
,

(46)

\|E_{A_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{R_{a,\delta}})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}(A_{a})}\|E_{\Delta}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{R_{a,\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

where
R_{a,\delta}
is a rectangular box of size
\delta^{-1}\times...\times\delta^{-1}\times a^{2-\alpha}\delta^{-1}
. Since
\delta^{-\max\{1,\alpha/2\}}\gtrsim a^{2-\alpha}\delta^{-1}
, we can split
R_{\delta}
into copies of
R_{a,\delta}
. Note that

(47)

\omega_{R_{\delta}}(x)\lesssim\sum_{j}\omega_{R_{a,\delta}(j)}(x)\omega_{R_{\delta}}(j)\lesssim\tilde{\omega}_{R_{\delta}}(x)

where each
R_{a,\delta}(j)
is a copy of
R_{a,\delta}
centered at
j\in\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times a^{2-\alpha}\delta^{-1}\mathbb{Z}
, and the implicit constants only depend on
d
. Then (
46
) implies (
45
) by
(
47
) and Minkowski inequality. It is worth to mention that
\sum_{j}\omega_{R_{a,\delta}(j)}(x)\omega_{R_{\delta}}(j)\approx\omega_{R_{\delta}}(x)
cannot hold, so we should use two slightly different rectangular weight functions in (
47
), which is different from the forthcoming (
51
) for the cube weight functions.

Now we prove (
46
). Let
\phi(y)=|y|^{\alpha}
. For
y_{0}\in A_{a}
, the Taylor expansion

a^{2-\alpha}\phi(y_{0}+z)=a^{2-\alpha}(\phi(y_{0})+\phi^{\prime}(y_{0})z+\frac{1}{2}z^{T}\phi^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})z)+O(a^{-1}|z|^{3})

where

\phi^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})=\alpha|y_{0}|^{\alpha-2}(I_{d}+(\alpha-2)\frac{y_{0}y_{0}^{T}}{|y_{0}|^{2}}).

Thus,
a^{2-\alpha}|z^{T}\phi^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})z|\approx|z|^{2}
whenever
\alpha>1
.

When
|z|\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}
with
\sigma=\varepsilon/3
, the error term
O(a^{-1}|z|^{3})=O(\delta)
since
a\geq\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}
. So when
|y-y_{0}|\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}
, the surface
S_{a}(y)=(y,a^{2-\alpha}|y|^{\alpha})
is in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the paraboloid

(48)

\tilde{S}_{a}(y)=(y,a^{2-\alpha}(\phi(y_{0})+\phi^{\prime}(y_{0})(y-y_{0})+\frac{1}{2}(y-y_{0})^{T}\phi^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})(y-y_{0}))).

Now we rescale the last variable. Let

E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g(x)=\int_{\Delta}g(y)e(x_{1}y_{1}+...+x_{d}y_{d}+a^{2-\alpha}x_{d+1}|y|^{\alpha})dy

Then

E_{\Delta}g(x)=E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g(x_{1},...,x_{d},a^{2-\alpha}x_{d+1})

and

\|E_{\Delta}g\|_{L^{p}(R_{a.\delta})}=a^{\frac{2-\alpha}{p}}\|E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(Q_{\delta})}

where
Q_{\delta}
is a cube of side length
\delta^{-1}
. By the scaling, to prove (
46
), it suffices to show

(49)

\|E_{A_{a},S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}(A_{a})}\|E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It suffices to show that the smallest constant
K_{p}(\delta)
that makes the following inequality holds satisfies
K_{p}(\delta)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}

(50)

\|E_{A_{a},S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}\leq K_{p}(\delta)\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}(A_{a})}\|E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Note that

(51)

\sum_{j}\omega_{Q_{\delta^{1-2\sigma}}(j)}(x)\omega_{Q_{\delta}}(j)\approx\omega_{Q_{\delta}}(x)

where each
Q_{\delta^{1-2\sigma}}(j)
is a copy of
Q_{\delta^{1-2\sigma}}
centered at
j\in\delta^{-1+2\sigma}\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
, and the implicit constants only depend on
d
.
By (
50
) and Minkowski inequality, we get

\|E_{A_{a},S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}\leq CK_{p}(\delta^{1-2\sigma})\Big(\sum_{\tau\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}}(A_{a})}\|E_{\tau,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Applying Bourgain-Demeter’s decoupling inequality
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
in the weighted version
[
10
, Prop. 9.15]
to the paraboloid (
48
), we get

\|E_{\tau,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}\leq D_{p}(\delta)\Big(\sum_{\Delta\in\text{Part}_{\delta^{1/2}}(A_{a}),\Delta\subset\tau}\|E_{\Delta,S_{a}}g\|_{L^{p}(\omega_{Q_{\delta}})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

where the decoupling constant
D_{p}(\delta)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Thus,

K_{p}(\delta)\leq CD_{p}(\delta)K_{p}(\delta^{1-2\sigma}).

Recall
\sigma=\varepsilon/3
. We iterate to get

\displaystyle K_{p}(\delta)

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}D_{p}(\delta)D_{p}(\delta^{1-2\sigma})...D_{p}(\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k-1}})K_{p}(\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k}})

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}C_{\varepsilon}^{k}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}(1+(1-2\sigma)^{2}+...+(1-2\sigma)^{k-1})}K_{p}(\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k}})

\displaystyle=C^{k}C_{\varepsilon}^{k}\delta^{-\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon(1-(1-2\sigma)^{k})}K_{p}(\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k}}).

Recall that
\delta^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq a\leq 1/2
. We choose
k
such that
\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k}}\approx a^{2}\leq 1/4
, then
K_{p}(\delta^{(1-2\sigma)^{k}})\approx 1
and
k\lesssim\log\log(\delta^{-1})
. Thus

K_{p}(\delta)\lesssim C^{k}C_{\varepsilon}^{k}\delta^{-\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon(1-(1-2\sigma)^{k})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}.

5.
Necessary conditions

Let
d\geq 1,\,\alpha\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\},\ N\geq 10
. Let
P=\Delta^{\alpha/2}
or
\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}
with
m\geq 0
. We make several crucial observations that are used to show the optimality of the range of
\beta
in our main theorems. We shall use the Weyl law and the zonal spherical harmonics to construct examples. As before, we fix
(e_{k})_{k}
to be an orthonormal eigenbasis in
L^{2}(M)
associated with the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
of
\sqrt{\Delta}
. Here
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...
are arranged in increasing order and we account for multiplicity. In this section, we also discuss the dependence on the interval
I
so we do not assume
|I|\approx 1
.

Observation 1.
For all
p,q\geq 2
, we have the universal bound

(52)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}(I\times M)}\lesssim|I|^{\frac{2}{p}}N^{d}\|\nu\|_{l^{\infty}}

for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(M)
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
. Indeed, for each
t
the system
\{e^{itP}f_{j}\}_{j}
is also orthonormal in
L^{2}(M)
with Fourier coefficients supported in
\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}(x)|^{2}\leq\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N}|e_{k}(x)|^{2}\lesssim N^{d},\ \ \ \forall t\in I,\ \forall x\in M

by the pointwise Weyl law.
Moreover, the exponent of
N
in (
52
) cannot be replaced by any number less than
d
, since if we fix
\nu_{j}=1
for
j\in\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
then by the Weyl law

(53)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{1}(M)}\approx\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N}1\approx N^{d}.

Observation 2.
The condition

(54)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq 1-\frac{\sigma}{d}

is necessary for the estimate

(55)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}(I\times M)}\lesssim|I|^{\frac{2}{p}}N^{\sigma}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

to hold for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(M)
with
supp\,\hat{f}_{j}\subset\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
, and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
. Indeed,
if we fix
\nu_{j}=1
with
j\in\{k:\lambda_{k}\leq N\}
then by (
53
) we see that (
55
) implies (
54
).

Observation 3.

Let
M=S^{d}
be the standard sphere, and we fix a point
x_{0}\in M
. Recall that each eigenvalue of
\sqrt{\Delta}
on the sphere has the form
\mu_{j}=\sqrt{j(j+d-1)}
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and for each
\mu_{j}\approx N
we can find an
L^{2}
-normalized zonal function
Z_{j}
with
Z_{j}(x)\approx N^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
when the distance
d_{g}(x,x_{0})\ll N^{-1}
. See e.g. Sogge
[
37
]
. These
Z_{j}
form an orthonormal system of size
\approx N
since they are associated with distinct eigenvalues. Since
|e^{itP}Z_{j}|=|Z_{j}|
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j}|e^{itP}Z_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}(I\times M)}\gtrsim N^{d}(N^{-d})^{\frac{2}{q}}|I|^{\frac{2}{p}}.

This gives another necessary condition for (
55
) on the sphere

(56)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq d-\frac{2d}{q}-\sigma.

If the sharp Schrödinger admissible condition
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
holds, then (
56
) is equivalent to

(57)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq\frac{4}{p}-\sigma.

Observation 4.
Let
M
be a compact manifold of dimension
d
. Fix any point
x_{0}\in M
. By the pointwise Weyl law, we have for all large
j

\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\leq j}|e_{k}(x_{0})|^{2}=C_{d}j^{d}+O(j^{d-1}).

Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all large
j

\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\in(j-c,j]}|e_{k}(x_{0})|^{2}\approx j^{d-1}.

So we can construct an orthonormal system
(f_{j})_{j}
of size
\approx N
, where
j\approx N
and

f_{j}(x)=c_{j}\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\in(j-c,j]}e_{k}(x_{0})\overline{e_{k}(x)}.

Here the normalized constant
c_{j}=(\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\in(j-c,j]}|e_{k}(x_{0})|^{2})^{1/2}\approx j^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

So
f_{j}(x)\approx N^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
when
d_{g}(x,x_{0})\ll N^{-1}
. Indeed, this follows from the mean value theorem and Bernstein inequality

\|\nabla f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\lesssim N\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

See e.g. Imekraz- Ouhabaz
[
21
]
. Then

e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}=c_{j}e^{itj^{\alpha}}\sum_{k:\lambda_{k}\in(j-c,j]}e^{it(\lambda_{k}^{\alpha}-j^{\alpha})}e_{k}(x_{0})\overline{e_{k}(x)}.

Note that

|(j-c)^{\alpha}-j^{\alpha}|\lesssim j^{\alpha-1}\approx N^{\alpha-1}.

So we still have
|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}(x)|\approx N^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
when
d_{g}(x,x_{0})\ll N^{-1}
and
|t|\ll N^{1-\alpha}
. Thus, for
|I|\gtrsim N^{1-\alpha}
we have

\Big\|\sum_{j}|e^{it\Delta^{\alpha/2}}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}(I\times M)}\gtrsim N^{d}(N^{-d})^{\frac{2}{q}}N^{\frac{2}{p}(1-\alpha)}.

When
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
|I|\approx N^{1-\alpha}
, this gives a necessary condition for (
55
) on any compact manifold

(58)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq d-\frac{2d}{q}-\sigma.

If the sharp Schrödinger admissible condition
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
holds, then (
58
) is equivalent to

(59)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq\frac{4}{p}-\sigma.

If the sharp wave admissible condition
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{d-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
holds, then (
58
) is equivalent to

(60)

\frac{1}{\beta}\geq\frac{d}{d-1}\frac{4}{p}-\sigma.

6.
Applications

As in the works by Frank–Sabin
[
15
]
, Lewin–Sabin
[
26
,
25
]
, Nakamura
[
30
]
, Bez-Lee-Nakamura
[
2
]
, we can exploit the Strichartz estimates to prove the well-posedness of the infinite systems of dispersive equations with Hartree-type nonlinearity on compact manifolds

(61)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u_{j}=Pu_{j}+(W\rho)u_{j},\ \ j\in\mathbb{N}\\
u_{j}(0,\cdot)=f_{j}\end{cases}

where
\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|u_{j}|^{2}
and
W\rho
is a real-valued function on
M
. We focus on
P=\Delta^{\alpha/2}
or
\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}
with
m\geq 0
. In the flat case, it is standard to take the convolution operator
W\rho=w*\rho
, where
w
is the interaction potential function on
M
.

6.1.
Conditions on the systems

Let
s\geq 0
. First, we need the Strichartz estimates

(62)

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}([0,1]\times M)}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

for all orthonormal systems
(f_{j})_{j}
in
H^{s}(M)
and all sequences
\nu=(\nu_{j})_{j}\in l^{\beta}
. As we have seen in the introduction, the estimates (
62
) can be deduced from the frequency localized estimates
(
1
) as in Theorem
1
,
4
,
3
,
5
and Corollary
1
,
2
. Moreover, (
62
) implies for any
T>0
,

\Big\|\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|e^{itP}f_{j}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{p/2}L_{x}^{q/2}([0,T]\times M)}\lesssim T^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\sqrt{1+\Delta}
. We also need the control of the Hartree-type nonlinearity

(63)

\|\mathcal{D}^{\pm s}(W\rho)\mathcal{D}^{\mp s}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}}\leq C_{s,q,W}\|\rho\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}

or equivalently

\|(W\rho)f\|_{H^{r}(M)}\leq C_{s,q,W}\|\rho\|_{L^{q/2}(M)}\|f\|_{H^{r}(M)},\ \forall f\in H^{r}(M),\ \ r=\pm s.

In the flat case with
W\rho=w*\rho
, the condition (
63
) holds true with
C_{s,q,W}=C_{s,\delta}\|w\|_{B_{(q/2)^{\prime},\infty}^{s+\delta}}
for all
\delta>0
(see
[
30
,
2
]
). Indeed, it follows from the inequality for the Besov norm

\|gf\|_{H^{r}}\leq C_{r,\delta}\|g\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{|r|+\delta}}\|f\|_{H^{r}},\ \ \forall r\in\mathbb{R},\ \forall\delta>0,

and by Hölder inequality

\|w*\rho\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{s+\delta}}\leq\|w\|_{B_{(q/2)^{\prime},\infty}^{s+\delta}}\|\rho\|_{L^{q/2}}\ \ .

See Triebel
[
40
, p. 29 & p. 205]
and Seeger-Sogge
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
for characterizations of Besov space on compact manifolds. A typical example in the flat case is
w(x)=|x|^{-a}
with
a<d
. See Nakamura
[
30
]
. A natural generalization of this convolution operator on compact manifolds is the spectral multiplier
\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}
. We calculate the norm

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}\rho\|_{B_{\infty,\infty}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{js}\|\varphi_{j}(\mathcal{D})\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{j(s-d+a)}\|\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(\mathcal{D})\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{j(s-d+a+2d/q)}\|\rho\|_{L^{q/2}},

where
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
shares essentially the same property as the Littlewood-Paley bump function
\varphi_{j}
.
So we require that
s-d+a+2d/q\leq 0
, which is equivalent to
a\leq d-\frac{2d}{q}-s
. For the sharp Schrödinger admissible pairs
(p,q)
, it is equivalent to
a\leq\frac{4}{p}-s
. For the sharp wave admissible pairs
(p,q)
, it is equivalent to
a\leq\frac{4d}{p(d-1)}-s
.

6.2.
Well-posedness of the systems

For applications, it is useful to state the condition (
62
) in an operator-theoretic version. Given a compact self-adjoint operator
\gamma
on
L^{2}(M)
, by the spectral theorem we can write

\gamma h=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}\langle h,f_{j}\rangle f_{j},\ \ \forall h\in L^{2}(M).

We formally denote the diagonal of the integral kernel of
\gamma
by

\rho_{\gamma}(x)=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}|f_{j}(x)|^{2}.

By the assumption (
62
),
\rho_{\mathcal{D}^{-s}\gamma(t)\mathcal{D}^{-s}}
is well-defined in
L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}
, where
\gamma(t)=e^{itP}\gamma_{0}e^{-itP}
, and satisfies

\|\rho_{\mathcal{D}^{-s}\gamma(t)\mathcal{D}^{-s}}\|_{L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}}\leq C_{*}\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}}

whenever
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}
. We define the Sobolev-type Schatten norm by

\|\gamma\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s}}=\|\mathcal{D}^{s}\gamma\mathcal{D}^{s}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta}}.

It is standard to transform the infinite system (
61
) into the operator formalism

(64)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\gamma=[P+W\rho_{\gamma},\gamma],\ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M,\\
\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}.\end{cases}

The following local well-posedness was proved in the abstract form in
[
2
, Prop. 10]
, and it still holds on compact manifolds. The proof is based on the Duhamel principle and the contraction mapping theorem.

Proposition 1

(Local well-posedness)
.

Suppose (
62
) and (
63
) hold. Then for any
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s}
, there exist
T=T(\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s}},C_{s,q,W})>0
and a unique solution
\gamma\in C_{t}^{0}([0,T];\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s})
to (
64
) on
[0,T]\times M
with
\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}
.

We also have the following global well-posedness for small data. The argument is similar, see
[
30
, Prop. 4.1]
.

Proposition 2

(Almost global well-posedness)
.

Suppose (
62
) and (
63
) hold. Then each
T>0
, there exists
\delta_{T}=\delta_{T}(C_{s,q,W})
such that for any for any
\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s}}<\delta_{T}
, there exists a unique solution
\gamma\in C_{t}^{0}([0,T];\mathfrak{S}^{\beta,s})
to (
64
) on
[0,T]\times M
with
\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{p/2}_{t}L^{q/2}_{x}
.

As corollaries, we obtain the well-posedness of (
64
) for the Hartree-type nonlinearity
W\rho=\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}\rho
by Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
\alpha>1
. Suppose
(p,q)
and
\beta
are as in Theorem
1
. Let
s>\frac{1}{p}
and
a\leq\frac{4}{p}-s
. Let
P=\Delta^{\alpha/2}
and
W\rho=\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}\rho
. Then the system (
64
) has local well-posedness and almost global well-posedness as in Propositions
1
and
2
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Suppose
(p,q)
and
\beta
are as in Theorem
3
. Let
s>\frac{1}{p}\frac{d+1}{d-1}
and
a\leq\frac{4d}{p(d-1)}-s
. Let
P=\sqrt{m^{2}+\Delta}
with
m\geq 0
and
W\rho=\mathcal{D}^{-d+a}\rho
. Then the system (
64
) has local well-posedness and almost global well-posedness as in Propositions
1
and
2
.

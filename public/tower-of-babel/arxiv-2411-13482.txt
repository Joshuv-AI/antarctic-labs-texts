Reflecting compact T 1 T_{1} -spaces into bounded distributive lattices
Mai Gehrke, Elena Pozzan, Matteo Viale

Abstract
We present a contravariant reflection of the compact T 1 T_{1} -spaces with arrows given by closed continuous functions into the category of bounded distributive lattices with arrows given by closed subfit morphisms. This reflection extends both Stone duality and Isbell’s duality between frames and sober spaces for those compact T 1 T_{1} -spaces that fall within each of these dualities, that is, respectively, zero-dimensional compact Hausdorff spaces, and compact sober T 1 T_{1} -spaces. On the topological side, we allow all compact T 1 T_{1} -spaces rather than just sober ones and we identify points in these with minimal prime filters on some base . On the lattice side, the shift goes from the notion of frame homomorphism to that of closed subfit morphism between bounded distributive lattices (closed subfit morphisms are defined by a natural and first order expressible constraint). The reflection becomes a duality when one restricts on the algebraic side to the complete and compact subfit lattices (i.e. compact subfit frames). Furthermore, restricting our duality on the topological side to the subcategory of compact T 2 T_{2} -spaces with all continuous maps, we obtain a duality for these with the category of complete, compact and normal lattices, thus recovering a classical result of Cornish. We also relate our adjunction to the duality introduced by Maruyama between T 1 T_{1} -spaces with continuous maps and a category having as objects a particular type of subfit frames and as arrows a certain type of morphism, of which we give an alternative and explicit algebraic characterization.

Filters on bounded distributive lattices

In this section our focus is the investigation of algebraic conditions on a bounded distributive lattice
P
that characterize topological properties of the spaces
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
induced by families of filters on
P
. We aim to characterize algebraically which bounded distributive lattices can be represented as a base of open sets of some
T_{1}
-space (e.g. subfits), and which bounded distributive lattices give rise to bases for compact Hausdorff spaces.
A key notion for this analysis is given by that of minimal prime filter.

Recall that:

•

An

(P,\leq,\vee)

(a,b)\mapsto a\vee b

(Q,\leq,\wedge)
is a preorder with a binary infima operation
(a,b)\mapsto a\wedge b

•

A

(P,\leq,\vee,\wedge)
is both an upper and lower semi-lattice.

•

A lattice
(P,\leq,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is

if it has a maximum
1

.

A filter

Families of prime filters (ideals) on an upper (lower) semi-lattice

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{p\in L:p\notin J\right\}

Proof.

Obvious.

3.1
Subfit lattices

We revisit the definition of subfit frame as given in
[
12
]
, and extend it to the context of bounded distributive lattices.

Definition 3.1.1
.

A bounded distributive lattice
P
is
subfit
if for every distinct elements
a,b\in P
there exists
c\in P
such that
a\vee c=1_{P}
and
b\vee c\neq 1_{P}
or vice versa.

Remark 3.1.2
.

Subfitness entails the following (seemingly stronger) property:

For every
a,b\in P
such that
a\not\leq b
there exists
c\in P
such that
a\vee c=1_{P}
and
b\vee c\neq 1_{P}
.

(1)

To see this assume
P
is subfit according to
3.1.1
, and
let
a\nleq b
in
P
. Then
a^{\prime}=b\wedge a<a
; in particular
a^{\prime}\neq a
, thus there is a
c\in P
with

(a^{\prime}\vee c=1\text{ and }a\vee c<1)\text{ or }(a^{\prime}\vee c<1\text{ and }a\vee c=1).

Since
a^{\prime}<a
, the leftside disjunct cannot be the case, so

a^{\prime}\vee c<1\text{ and }a\vee c=1

holds.
Now it follows that

b\vee c=(b\vee c)\wedge 1=(b\vee c)\wedge(a\vee c)=(b\wedge a)\vee c=a’\vee c<1

And
1
holds.

Here is an informative characterization of subfitness:

Fact 3.1.3
.

A bounded distributive lattice
L
is subfit if and only if for all
a\in L
:

\uparrow a=\bigcap\left\{F:F\text{ is a minimal prime filter and }a\in F\right\}.

(2)

Proof.

First assume
L
is subfit: clearly
\uparrow a\subseteq\bigcap\left\{F:F\text{ is a minimal prime filter and }a\in F\right\}
holds.
Now let
b\not\in\uparrow a
, i.e.
a\nleq b
. Then there is
c
with

a\vee c=1
and
b\vee c\neq 1
. Let F be a minimal prime filter with
b\vee c\not\in F
(it exists by a basic application of Zorn’s Lemma). Then
a\in F
and
b\not\in F
, thus the converse inclusion holds as well.

Conversely assume
2
holds. Given
a\nleq b
, find (by
2
)
F
minimal prime with
a\in F
and
b\notin F
.
Then there must be
c\in F^{c}
such that
a\vee c=1
(since
F^{c}
is a maximal ideal). Since
b,c\in F^{c}
we also have that
b\vee c<1
(since
F^{c}
is a prime ideal). Since
a\nleq b
are chosen arbitrarily,
L
is subfit.
∎

Definition 3.1.4
.

Let
P
be a preorder and
\mathcal{A}
be an antichain of prime filters on
P
.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
large
for
P
if for every two distinct elements
p,q\in P
there exists
F\in\mathcal{A}
such that
p\in F
and
q\notin F
or vice versa.

Observe that if
P
has a unique minimum
0_{P}
, any large family of filters is dense on the partial order
P^{+}=P\setminus\left\{0_{P}\right\}
.

Fact 3.1.5
.

Assume
(X,\tau)
is a
T_{0}
-topological space and
P
is a base for
\tau
.
Let
\mathcal{A}_{X}
be the family of filters
F_{x}=\left\{p\in P:x\in p\right\}
for all
x\in X
.

Then
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is large for
P
.

Proof.

Given
p\neq q
in
P
, we may assume without loss of generality that there exists
x\in X
such that
x\in p\setminus q
.
Then
p\in F_{x}
and
q\not\in F_{x}
.
∎

Proposition 3.1.6
.

Let
P
be a bounded distributive lattice.
Then,
P
admits a large family
\mathcal{A}
of minimal prime filters on
P
if and only if
P
is subfit.

Proof.

The right-to-left implication follows straightforwardly from Fact
3.1.3
.
For the converse implication, suppose that
P
admits a large family of minimal prime filters. If
a\neq b
, without loss of generality, we may assume that there exists a minimal prime filter
F
such that
a\in F
and
b\notin F
. Hence, by Fact
3.0.5
, there exists an element
c\notin F
such that
a\vee c=1_{P}
and
b\vee c\neq 1_{P}
.
∎

Corollary 3.1.7
.

Let
P
be a bounded distributive lattice. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

P
is subfit;

2.

For some family
\mathcal{A}
of minimal prime filters on
P
, the map
p\mapsto\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
is an injective homomorphism of bounded lattices.

Proof.

Obvious.
∎

Subfit lattices provide a sufficient framework for representing
T_{1}
-topological spaces. Specifically, if we consider a base for such a space that includes the complements of singletons and forms a bounded lattice, it will be subfit. Moreover, the associated neighborhood filters form a large family of minimal prime filters. Actually we will see that we can characterize subfits as those bounded distributive lattices that are isomorphic to a base of a compact
T_{1}
-space.

3.2
Compactness

Theorem 3.2.1
.

(Cornish
[
4
]
)

Let
P
be a bounded distributive lattice and

\mathcal{A}=\left\{F\subseteq P:F\mathrm{\ is\ a\ minimal\ prime\ filter\ on\ }P\right\}

Then
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
is a compact
T_{1}
-space.

We give our own proof of the theorem since it highlights ideas we will use later on to prove original results (our focus is on minimal prime filters of open sets, in
[
4
]
Cornish works with the dual notion of maximal filter of closed sets).

Proof.

(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
is
T_{1}

by Proposition
2.2.1
, since
\mathcal{A}
is an antichain under inclusion.

Let
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}:p\in Q\right\}
be a family of basic open sets of
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
so that, for all finite
Q^{\prime}\subseteq Q
, the subfamily
\left\{\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}:p\in Q^{\prime}\right\}
does not cover
\mathcal{A}
. We need to show that
Q
is not a cover of
\mathcal{A}
. That is, there is a minimal prime filter
G
of
P
so that
G\not\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
, or equivalently
p\not\in G
, for all
p\in Q
. By Corollary
3.0.4
, this is equivalent to showing the existence of a maximal ideal
G^{C}
that contains
Q
. To produce the latter we use Zorn’s Lemma.
Let

\mathcal{Z}=\left\{Z\subseteq L:Q\subseteq Z\ \mathrm{and}\ \bigvee Z^{\prime}\neq 1_{P}\mathrm{\ for\ every\ finite\ }Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z\right\}

ordered by the inclusion.

\mathcal{Z}\neq\emptyset
.

If
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
is finite, then
Q^{\prime}
is not a cover of
\mathcal{A}
, that is,
\bigvee Q^{\prime}\neq 1_{P}
. Therefore, every finite join of elements from
Q
is different from
1_{P}
. Hence,
Q\in\mathcal{Z}
.

Every chain of
\mathcal{Z}
admits an upper bound.

Assume that
\left\{Z_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
is a chain.
V=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}Z_{\alpha}
is in
\mathcal{Z}
: if
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in V
then there exists
\beta\in A
such that
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in Z_{\beta}
, thus
\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\neq 1_{P}
.

By Zorn’s Lemma, there exists a maximal element
J
of
\mathcal{Z}
. An argument left to the reader shows that
J
is a maximal ideal of
P
which clearly contains
Q
.
∎

3.3
Normal lattices versus compact Hausdorff spaces

Recalling the notion of normal frame given in
[
12
]
, we generalize it to bounded distributive lattices.

Definition 3.3.1
.

A preorder
P
is a
normal lattice
if the following conditions are met:

1.

P
is a bounded distributive lattice;

2.

for every distinct elements
p,q\in P
such that
p\vee q=1_{P}
, there exist
r,s\in P
such that
r\wedge s=0_{P}
and
r\vee p=s\vee q=1_{P}
.

Theorem 3.3.2
.

Let
P
be a normal lattice and

\mathcal{A}=\left\{F\subseteq P:F\mathrm{\ is\ a\ minimal\ prime\ filter}\right\}

Then
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
is a compact Hausdorff space.

Proof.

We only need to prove that
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
is Hausdorff. By Proposition
2.2.1
, this is equivalent to proving that the union of two minimal prime filters
F,G
is not a prefilter.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose there exists an element
p\in F
such that
p\notin G
. Since
F
is a minimal prime filter,
F^{C}
is a maximal ideal of
P
, by Corollary
3.0.4
. Hence, there exists some
q
such that
p\vee q=1_{P}
and
q\in F^{C}
, i.e.
q\notin F
. Since
P
is a normal lattice, there exist some
r,s\in P
such that

\displaystyle p\vee r=1_{P}

\displaystyle q\vee s=1_{P}

\displaystyle r\wedge s=0_{P}

Clearly,
1_{P}\in F
and
q\notin F
imply that
s\in F
. Similarly,
1_{p}\in G
and
p\notin G
imply that
r\in G
. Hence,
r,s\in F\cup G
and, consequently,
F\cup G
is not a prefilter.
∎

A similar result can be found in a different form in
[
4
, Thm. 7.3]
. However, our result is more general, as it does not require the assumption of a semi-complemented lattice.

3.4
The Wallman compactification operator

We provide an overview of Frink’s article
[

, focusing on his introduction of a characterization for Tychonoff spaces and relating it to the results of the previous sections.

Definition 3.4.1

•

•

is a

Definition 3.4.2
.

(Frink
[
6
]
)

Let

be a topological space. Let

be a family of closed sets for the topology
\tau

is:

1.

weak normal
when it is a bounded distributive lattice w.r.t. finite union and intersection, and any two disjoint members

of members of

2.

when for every

such that

and

.

•

A

is a weak normal family of closed sets which is a base for the closed sets.

•

•

Frink showed the following:

Theorem 3.4.3
.

(Frink
[
6
]
)

A

-space

.

(Frink

A

We prove it giving a small improvement which outlines that (weak) normal bases produce (weak) Hausdorff compactifications. Again this has the added benefit of introducing ideas and lemmas that we will need in the final part containing our original results.

Definition 3.4.5
.

(Frink
[
6
]
)

Let

. A

such that:

1.

it contains every superset in

2.

3.

Z
.

We can define the weak Hausdorff compactification of any topological space
(X,\tau)

on

.

Definition 3.4.6
.

(Frink
[
6
]
)

Let
Z
be a weak normal base for a

-topological space
(X,\tau)

is

\mathsf{W}(Z,X)=\left\{\mathcal{F}\subseteq Z:\mathcal{F}\mathrm{\ is\ a\ }Z\mathrm{-ultrafilter\ }\right\}

\mathsf{W}(Z,U)=\left\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{W}(Z,X):\mathrm{\ there\ is\ some\ }A\in\mathcal{F}\mathrm{\ with\ }A\subseteq U\right\}

Theorem 3.4.7
.

(Frink
[
6
]
)

Let
Z
be a (weak) normal base for a space

. Then,
(\mathsf{W}(Z,X),\mathsf{W}(Z,\tau))
is a (weak) Hausdorff compactification of
(X,\tau)
via the map:

\displaystyle i:X\to\mathsf{W}(Z,X)

\displaystyle x\mapsto i(x)=\left\{C\in Z:x\in C\right\}

Note that a base for the closed sets of

is
\left\{\mathsf{W}(Z,A):A\in Z\right\}
where

\mathsf{W}(Z,A)=\left\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{W}(Z,X):A\in\mathcal{F}\right\}

It can be proved that the
0
-sets of a Tychonoff space form a normal basis whose relative Wallman compactification is clearly the Stone–Čech compactification.

In general, in any topological space the
0
-sets form a weak normal family, however if the family is not a base for the closed sets, the compact Hausdorff space it produces retains very little information from the starting one.
For example, if the reals are endowed with the Zariski topology, the

-sets are given by polynomial maps
with compact range, any such map must be constant. In particular, the unique non-empty

-set is the set of all reals itself. So its Wallman compactification relative to the family of

Now, we aim to determine which conditions on a preorder

and on a large family

of filters on

\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\mathcal{A})
.

Proposition 3.4.8
.

Let
P
be a bounded distributive lattice and
\mathcal{A}
be a large family of prime filters on
P
. Then
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}=\left\{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}:p\in P\right\}
is a normal basis for
(\mathcal{A},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P})
if and only if
P
is a normal lattice and the elements of
\mathcal{A}
are minimal prime filters of
P
.

Proof.

\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
is a bounded distributive lattice.

It follows from Fact
3.0.2
.

\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
is a weak normal basis if and only if
P
is a normal lattice.

Suppose
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
is weak normal and let
p
and
q
be elements of
P
such that

, i.e.
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}=\mathcal{A}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}=\emptyset
, and, thus, they are respectively subsets of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{r}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
such that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{r}\cap\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{s}=\emptyset
. Therefore,

. Moreover, since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}=\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{r}
then
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{r}=\mathcal{A}
, i.e.

. Analogously,

.

The converse implication is similar.

\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
is disjunctive if and only if the filters of
\mathcal{A}
are minimal prime.

Suppose

and

, i.e.

. Observe that requiring the existence of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}\in\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}

and

is equivalent to requiring the existence of

).

∎

The following is the key connection between the results of the previous sections and the Wallman construction:

Theorem 3.4.9
.

Let
P
be a subfit lattice and
\mathcal{A}
be a large family of minimal prime filters on

. Then,
(\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\mathcal{A}),\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}))
is homeomorphic to
(\mathcal{A}_{P},\tau^{\mathcal{A}_{P}}_{P})
where
\mathcal{A}_{P}
is the family of

the minimal prime filters on

.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\phi:(\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\mathcal{A}),\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\tau^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}))

\displaystyle\to(\mathcal{A}_{P},\tau^{\mathcal{A}_{P}}_{P})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\left\{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\right\}_{p\in_{I_{\mathcal{F}}}}

\displaystyle\mapsto I_{\mathcal{F}}^{C}

\phi
is well defined and bijective.

We claim that
\mathcal{F}=\left\{\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\right\}_{p\in I_{\mathcal{F}}}
is a
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
-ultrafilter if and only if
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is a maximal ideal on

.

It is evident that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}
, thus
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed downwards with respect to

if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is closed upwards with respect to inclusion. Note that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}\cap\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p\vee q}

holds (since the filters in

are prime): therefore
\mathcal{F}
is closed under finite intersections if and only if
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under finite joins.

Additionally,
\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
1_{P}\in I_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Clearly,
\mathcal{F}
is maximal in
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}
if and only if
I_{\mathcal{F}}
is maximal in

.

Thus, by Corollary
3.0.4
, we can conclude that
\phi
is well defined and bijective.

\phi
is open and continuous.

We show that

\phi[\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p})]=\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}_{P}}_{p}.

Recall that for
\mathcal{F}
in
\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P}

is a maximal ideal and

is a minimal prime filter.

Now
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{W}(\sigma^{\mathcal{A}}_{P},\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p})
if and only if there is

such that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}}_{p}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{A}}_{q}\in\mathcal{F}
, i.e. if and only if there is

and

, i.e. if and only if

\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathcal{A}_{P}}_{p}
.

∎

.

3.5
Compact lattices

Following the same pattern for the notion of subfitness, we generalize the notion of compact frame of
[

]
to bounded distributive lattices.

Definition 3.5.1
.

Let
P
be a bounded distributive lattice.

is

if,
whenever

is such that
\sup(A)=1_{P}
, there exists a finite subset

of

Fact 3.5.2
.

Assume
(X,\tau)
is a compact topological space.
Then

(X,\tau)
is

,

Note that a base for a compact space which is a bounded distributive lattice need not be a compact lattice; the key point is that suprema in the base, seen as a lattice, may be computed differently than in the topology. For example:

Fact 3.5.3
.

Let
\mathsf{B}
be an infinite complete boolean algebra. Then
\mathsf{B}
is complete, subfit and not compact.

Proof.

Note that
\mathsf{B}
is isomorphic to the the clopen set of its Stone space
\St(\mathsf{B})
, therefore it is a subfit lattice (and can be seen as a base for a compact Hausdorff space).
Pick a maximal antichain
\left\{b_{i}:i\in I\right\}
of
\mathsf{B}
(such an antichain always exists if
\mathsf{B}
is infinite, by a basic application of Zorn’s Lemma).
Then

1_{\mathsf{B}}=\bigvee\left\{b_{i}:i\in I\right\}

(since
\left\{b_{i}:i\in I\right\}
is a maximal antichain),
while for all finite subset
J
of

1_{\mathsf{B}}>\bigvee\left\{b_{i}:i\in J\right\}.

∎

Fact 3.5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the base for the interval
[0,1]
obtained by closing under finite intersections and unions the set

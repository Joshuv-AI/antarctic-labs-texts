Connected Hopf algebras that are not Hopf Ore Extensions of enveloping algebras Thanks: This research has been supported by the NSFC (Grant No. 12471032).
Mengying Hu, Quanshui Wu

Abstract
. We construct a family of connected Hopf algebras with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, none of which is an iterated Hopf Ore extension of the universal enveloping algebra of its primitive part. This provides a negative answer to a question posed by Li and Zhou. It is also demonstrated that these connected Hopf algebras can be formulated as an iterated crossed product of enveloping algebras.

16T05, 16W70

1.
Introduction

How the coalgebra structure affects the algebra properties of a Hopf algebra is an essential question in the study of Hopf algebras. Suppose
H
is a Hopf algebra over a field
\mathbb{k}
with trivial coradical, that is,
H
is a connected Hopf algebra. If
H
is cocommutative, then
H
is isomorphic to a universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
by Milnor-Moore-Cartier-Kostant Theorem
[
13
, Theorem
5.6.5]
. In fact, later Brown and Gilmartin
[
6
, Question L]
asked if any connected Hopf algebra is a universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra. It is true when the Hopf algebra is of GK-dimension no larger than
4
following the classifications
[
16
]
. Furthermore, all these Hopf algebras are iterated Hopf Ore extensions (IHOE for short) of
\mathbb{k}
. Such kind of results is quite useful for the understanding of the structure of connected Hopf algebras, as the algebraic and homological properties of both enveloping algebras and Ore extensions are rather clear
[
9
,
12
,
11
,
8
]
. The question was later answered negatively by a counter-example
[
5
, Theorem 0.5(2)]
which is an IHOE but no longer an enveloping algebra of any Lie algebra.
On the other hand, obviously, there are examples that are not IHOEs but enveloping algebras. As a matter of fact, the enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra without a complete flag
[
8
]
is not an IHOE. These enveloping algebras are the only known examples of connected Hopf algebras that are not IHOEs up to now.
All known connected Hopf algebras of finite GK-dimension up to now are IHOEs of the enveloping algebras of their primitive parts, including

•

the graded ones
[
10
]
,

•

the cocommutative ones, which are all enveloping algebras,

•

the commutative ones, which are polynomial algebras,

•

all connected Hopf algebras of GK-dimension no more than
4

[
16
]
,

•

the specific example of GK-dimension
5
in
[
5
]
.

So, Li and Zhou
[
10
, Question C]
naturally asked:

Question 1.1
.

Is any connected Hopf algebra
H
of finite GK-dimension an IHOE of the universal enveloping algebra of its primitive part?

In this paper, we answer this question negatively by constructing a counter-example
\UM(r,2s)
in Section
3
, where
r,s
are non-negative integers such that
r\geq 2s
.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
4.4
)
.

The connected Hopf algebra
\UM(r,2s)
is not an IHOE of the universal enveloping algebra of its primitive part. Furthermore, if
r=2s
and
s\geq 2
, then
\UM(r,2s)
is not an Ore extension of any Hopf subalgebra of codimension
1
.

As a consequence, there exists a class of connected Hopf algebras that are counter-examples to Question
1.1
. The smallest one among these examples is of GK-dimension 8. Moreover, part of these examples, which are of GK-dimension at least 19, are not IHOEs of any proper Hopf subalgebras.

In Section
5
, we study the structural and homological property of
\UM(r,2s)
. In Proposition
5.8
, we show that

\UM(r,2s)
is an iterated crossed product of enveloping algebras as Hopf algebras. In Proposition
5.10
, we calculate the Nakayama automorphism of the skew Calabi-Yau algebra
\UM(r,2s)
by using the filtered deformation technique introduced by Wu and Zhu
[
17
]
. In particular, we show that
\UM(2s,2s)
is a Calabi-Yau algebra.

2.
preliminary

Throughout this paper, all algebras considered are
\mathbb{k}
-algebras, where
\mathbb{k}
is a field of characteristic
0
.
A Hopf
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
H
is called connected if its coradical
H_{0}=\mathbb{k}
. Such a Hopf algebra can be canonically viewed as a filtered Hopf algebra by its coradical filtration
\{F_{m}H\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
. The corresponding associated graded Hopf algebra is denoted by
\gr H=\oplus_{m=0}^{\infty}(F_{m}H/{F_{m-1}}H)
.
For any
0\neq u\in H
,
u
is said to be of order
m
if
u\in F_{m}H\setminus F_{m-1}H
. For an element
u
of order
m
,
\overline{u}=u+F_{m-1}\in(\gr H)_{m}=F_{m}H/{F_{m-1}}H
is called the principle symbol of
u
in literature.

Let
\varepsilon
be the counit of
H
,
H^{+}
be the kernel of
\varepsilon
. For any subspace
K
of
H
, let
K^{+}:=K\cap H^{+}
.
Recall that in a connected Hopf algebra
H
, elements of order
1
in
H^{+}
are exactly the primitive elements
P(H)
and
F_{1}H=\mathbb{k}\oplus P(H)

[
13
, Lemma 5.3.2]
. The set
P(H)
canonically has a Lie algebra structure.
We refer to
[
13
]
as the basic reference for Hopf algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A Hopf algebra
H
is said to be a Hopf Ore extension (HOE for short) of a Hopf subalgebra
K
if
H
is an Ore extension (see
[
12
, 1.9.2]
) of
K
as an algebra.

A Hopf algebra
H
is said to be an iterated Hopf Ore extension (IHOE for short) of a Hopf subalgebra
K
if it contains a chain of Hopf subalgebras:

K=H_{(0)}\subseteq H_{(1)}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq H_{(n)}=H,

such that each
H_{(i)}
is a Hopf Ore extension of
H_{(i-1)}
.
If
H
is an IHOE of
K=\mathbb{k}
then we simply say
H
is an IHOE.

If
K
is a Hopf algebra and
K\subseteq H
is an Ore extension as an algebra,
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
characterized when
H
is a Hopf algebra containing
K
as a Hopf subalgebra.
For the case that
K
is connected, see
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Zhuang
[
19
, Proposition 6.4]
proved that the associated Hopf algebra
\gr H
of a connected Hopf algebra is commutative (see also
[
3
, Lemma 5.5]
). If, in addition,
H
has finite GK-dimension, then more algebra properties are known.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
19
, Theorem 6.9]

Let
H
be a connected Hopf algebra. The following are equivalent.

(1)

\GKdim H<\infty
;

(2)

\GKdim\gr H<\infty
;

(3)

\gr H
is finitely generated;

(4)

\gr H
is graded isomorphic to a weighted polynomial algebra.

If one of (1)-(4) holds then
\GKdim H=\GKdim\gr H
. Moreover,
H
is a noetherian domain of finite global dimension equal to
\GKdim H
.

Remark 2.3
.

This result is proved in
[
19
]
under the assumption that
\mathbb{k}
is an algebraically closed field, which is unnecessary as remarked in
[
18
, Remark 3.8, 3.9]
.

As a consequence, to construct a connected Hopf algebra of finite GK-dimension, from the algebra point of view, a priori, we need to construct a set of weighted generators with lower weighted commutators such that the principle symbols of the generating set is the set of polynomial generators of
\gr H
.
To specify such kind of generating sets, we focus on the properties of the
\mathbb{k}
-basis lifted from
\gr H
.

Definition 2.4
.

A totally ordered set
\mathcal{G}=\{z_{1}<z_{2}<\dots<z_{n}\}
in a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
is called a PBW generating set of
A
if
\{z_{1}^{d_{1}}z_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\mid d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a
\mathbb{k}
-basis of
A
.

The PBW generating set is named from the associated Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt-type
\mathbb{k}
-basis, which consists of the lexicographically ordered monomials of
\{z_{1}<z_{2}<\dots<z_{n}\}
.
For convenience, an indexed set
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is called a PBW generating set if it becomes one with the order
z_{1}<z_{2}<\dots<z_{n}
.

In fact, there exists a PBW generating set for any connected Hopf algebra of finite GK-dimension following Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
H
be a connected Hopf algebra of finite GK-dimension. Then
H
has a PBW generating set.

Proof.

Suppose
\GKdim H=n
. By Theorem
2.2
, there exists a set

(2.1)

\mathcal{G}:=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq H\text{ such that }\gr H=\mathbb{k}[\overline{z_{1}},\cdots,\overline{z_{n}}].

That is to say, the set of the principle symbols of elements in
\mathcal{G}
is a polynomial generating set of the graded polynomial algebra
\gr H
.

Let
w_{i}
be the order of
z_{i}
with respect to the coradical filtration. Then the principle symbol
\overline{z_{i}}\in(\gr H)_{w_{i}}
.
For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the space
F_{m}H
is spanned by the subset

S_{m}:=\{z_{1}^{d_{1}}z_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\mid\sum_{i}d_{i}w_{i}\leqslant m,d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\},

following an induction on the filtration. Thus
H
is spanned by

S:=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{m}=\{z_{1}^{d_{1}}z_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\mid d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Then
H
is generated by
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
as an algebra.
Note that

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}F_{m}H=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\oplus_{i=0}^{m}(\gr H)_{i}=|\{(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{n})\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}w_{i}\leqslant m\}|\geqslant|S_{m}|.

It follows that
S_{m}
is a
\mathbb{k}
-linear basis of
F_{m}H
. Since
m
is arbitrary and the coradical filtration is exhaustive,
S
is linearly independent and thus a
\mathbb{k}
-basis of
H
.
∎

Whenever we consider a PBW generating set
\mathcal{G}
in a connected Hopf algebra
H
, we assume that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq H^{+}
. If some element
x\in\mathcal{G}\notin H^{+}
, just replace it with

\mathcal{G}
satisfies (
2.1

Let
(\mathcal{G},\leq)
be a totally ordered set. The following orders are considered on the words
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The lexicographic ordering
\leqslant_{\lex}
is defined as follows. For any two words
u=z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}\cdots z_{i_{t}}
and
v=z_{j_{1}}z_{j_{2}}\cdots z_{j_{t^{\prime}}}

u\leqslant_{\lex}v
if and only if either

•

u
is a prefix of
v
(that is,
t\leqslant t^{\prime}
and
i_{s}=j_{s}
for all
s\leqslant t

•

there exists some
s\leqslant\textrm{min}\{t,t^{\prime}\}
such that
i_{\ell}=j_{\ell}
for all
\ell<s

i_{s}<j_{s}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a weight map
w:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, each word
u=z_{i_{1}}z_{i_{2}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
is equipped with a weight defined by
\omega(u)=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\omega(z_{i_{s}})
. The corresponding weighted lexicographic ordering
\leqslant_{\wlex}
is defined as:

u\leqslant_{\wlex}v
if an only if either

•

\omega(u)<\omega(v)
, or

•

\omega(u)=\omega(v)
and
u\leqslant_{\lex}v
.

With an order given, one can define the leading word of a polynomial
f\in k\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
, which is denoted by
\LW(f)
in this paper.

Let
F=\mathbb{k}\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
be the free algebra over the ordered set
\mathcal{G}=\{z_{1}<z_{2}<\dots<z_{n}\}
and
I
be the ideal generated by
\{z_{j}z_{i}-z_{i}z_{j}+f_{ij}\mid 1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n\}

\{f_{ij}\}
are polynomials in
F

be the quotient algebra. We sort out a special case of the Diamond Lemma to give an example of
\overline{\mathcal{G}}

.
For a quick and nice introduction of the Diamond Lemma and the reduction system we refer to
[
7

For the ordered set
\mathcal{G}
and the ideal

\Re=\{(z_{j}z_{i},z_{i}z_{j}-f_{ij})\mid 1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n\}

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A=\mathbb{k}\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle/I

(1)

(2)

\omega(\LW(f_{ij}))<\omega(z_{i})+\omega(z_{j})

As a consequence,

\{\overline{z}_{1}^{d_{1}}\overline{z}_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots\overline{z}_{n}^{d_{n}}\mid d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\}.

3.
Umbrella Hopf Algebras

In this section, we construct a new class of examples of connected Hopf algebras of finite GK-dimension. The idea comes from the efforts to classify connected Hopf algebras generated as an algebra by the second layer of the coradical filtration. We say a connected Hopf algebra
H
is
m
th-layer generated if it is generated by
F_{m}H
as an algebra. Fix a PBW generating set
\mathcal{G}
as in Lemma
2.8
. Note that
\mathcal{G}
satisfies (
2.1
), which means its set of principle symbols is a polynomial generating set of
\gr H
. As shown in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 2.6]
, we may assume that any element
y
of order
2
in
\mathcal{G}
satisfies that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta y\in y\otimes 1+1\otimes y+P(H)\wedge P(H),

where the wedge product means the term is an anti-symmetric form.
Following the notations in
[
16
]
, we denote by
\delta f:=\Delta f-f\otimes 1-1\otimes f
for any element
f\in H
. We always assume that

\mathcal{G}\subseteq H^{+}.

For convenience, let
\mathcal{G}_{m}
be the subset
\mathcal{G}\cap(F_{m}H\setminus F_{m-1}H)
.
Then,
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, which is denoted by
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r}\}
sometimes, spans the space of all primitive elements
P(H)
. In fact, if
\delta y=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}x_{i}\otimes x_{j},
then
it suffices to replace
y
by
y-\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}
to meet (
3.1
). After the replacement,
\mathcal{G}
still satisfies (
2.1
), thus
\mathcal{G}
is still a PBW generating set of
H
by Lemma
2.5
.

Such an anti-symmetric form is very helpful in the calculations in a
2
nd-layer generated Hopf algebra. For example, one can easily distinguish the elements in the subspace
\mathbb{k}\mathcal{G}_{2}
from other elements in
F_{2}H
by its image under
\delta
. Being more precisely,
\delta(P(H)^{2})
and
\delta(\mathcal{G}_{2})
are symmetric and anti-symmetric tensor forms respectively,
and
\delta(P(H)^{2})\cap\delta(\mathbb{k}\mathcal{G}_{2})=0
.
It is worth mentioning that an element
u\in H
could be determined by
\delta(u)
up to a difference within
\ker\delta=P(H)
.

We need more effort to distinguish one element
y
in the generating subset of order
2
from another. Consider the lexicographic order on
\{x_{i}x_{j}\mid 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r\}
as words of length
2
over
P(H)
. Then
\delta y=\sum_{i>j}\alpha^{y}_{ij}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j}-x_{j}\otimes x_{i})
has a leading term
x_{i_{y}}\otimes x_{j_{y}}-x_{j_{y}}\otimes x_{i_{y}}
, where
x_{i_{y}}x_{j_{y}}=\max\{x_{i}x_{j}\mid\alpha^{y}_{ij}\neq 0\}
.
In fact, through a certain chosen method of
\mathcal{G}
, the leading term of
\delta(y)
of
y\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
will not occur in the coproduct of any other
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
. Let us explain this next. Suppose
\mathcal{G}_{2}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{t}\}
and denote
x_{i_{y_{p}}}x_{j_{y_{p}}}
by
x_{i_{p}}x_{j_{p}}
and
\alpha^{y_{p}}_{ij}
by
\alpha^{p}_{ij}
. We may assume that

x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{1}}\leqslant x_{i_{2}}x_{j_{2}}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant x_{i_{t}}x_{j_{t}}.

If
x_{i_{\ell}}x_{j_{\ell}}<\cdots<x_{i_{t}}x_{j_{t}}
for some
\ell
,
and
x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}=x_{i_{\ell}}x_{j_{\ell}}
for some
k<l
, then replace
y_{k}
by
y_{k}-\frac{\alpha^{k}_{i_{k}j_{k}}}{\alpha^{\ell}_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}}y_{\ell}
.
In finitely many steps, we may assume that the leading terms satisfy

(3.2)

\displaystyle x_{i_{1}}x_{j_{1}}<x_{i_{2}}x_{j_{2}}<\cdots<x_{i_{t}}x_{j_{t}}.

To insure that the leading term
x_{i_{k}}x_{j_{k}}
will not appear in the coproduct of any other
y_{l}
with
l\neq k
, next we do similar operation as above.

Obviously,
\alpha^{l}_{i_{1}j_{1}}=\alpha^{l}_{i_{2}j_{2}}=\cdots=\alpha^{l}_{i_{l-1}j_{l-1}}=0
for any
l
.
If
\alpha^{k}_{i_{l}j_{l}}\neq 0
for some
k>l
, then by replacing
y_{k}
by
y_{k}-\frac{\alpha^{k}_{i_{l}j_{l}}}{\alpha^{l}_{i_{l}j_{l}}}y_{l}
, we may have
\alpha^{k}_{i_{l}j_{l}}=0
. By induction, we may assume that
\alpha^{k}_{i_{l}j_{l}}=0
for any
k\neq l
.
In other words, the pair
(x_{i_{y}},x_{j_{y}})
would be the unique feature of
y
. By the feature, it is relatively easy to decide the coefficients of
y
as an element of the PBW basis for any element in
F_{2}H
. Moreover, the coproducts of generators in higher layers of the coradical filtration have close relation with the commutativity of the elements in
F_{1}H
in turn, see
[
16
, Proposition 4.17]

as a detailed example, where the coproduct of generators in
\mathcal{G}_{3}
forces
P(H)
to be a trivial Lie algebra. In the
2
nd-layer generated cases of our concern, if we collect the set of these feature pairs together as

(3.3)

\Lambda=\{(x_{i_{y}},x_{j_{y}})\mid y\in\mathcal{G}_{2}\},

the larger the set is, the more commutative
P(H)
as a Lie algebra is. The following example, with
|\mathcal{G}_{1}|=3
, provides an intuition for such property.

Example 3.1
.

Suppose
H
is
2
-layer generated and
P(H)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathbb{k}x_{i}
. Since
P(H)\wedge P(H)
is of linear dimension
3
,
|\mathcal{G}_{2}|\leqslant 3
.

∙Case
\mathcal{G}_{2}=\emptyset
.
P(H)
can be any Lie algebra of dimension
3
.

∙Case
\mathcal{G}_{2}=\{y\}
. Then
|\Lambda|=1
, and
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
listed all possible structures of
H
. Among which, all but case (b) have non-trivial
P(H)
. Actually, the possible Lie algebra structures of these primitive parts include all Lie algebras of dimension
3
but
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

∙Case
|\mathcal{G}_{2}|=2
. Then
|\Lambda|=2
. By some tedious but basic calculations,
P(H)
is either a trivial Lie algebra or isomorphic to the one with:

[x_{1},x_{2}]=2x_{2},[x_{1},x_{3}]=x_{3},[x_{2},x_{3}]=0.

∙Case
|\mathcal{G}_{2}|=3
. Then
|\Lambda|=3
, and
P(H)
must be a trivial Lie algebra.

Obviously, if
P(H)
is commutative, then, by
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
,
H
is an enveloping algebra.

The following lemma shows that such commutativity affects to what extent of the commutativity of the higher layers in the coradical filtration.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
H
be a connected Hopf algebra. For a fixed positive integer
k
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

[F_{m}H,F_{l}H]=0
for any
m+l\leqslant k
with
m,l\geqslant 0
.

(2)

[F_{m}H,F_{l}H]\subseteq F_{m+l-k}H
for any integer
m,l\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Since
H
is connected,
F_{0}H\cap H^{+}=0
. It follows from
[H,H]=[H^{+},H^{+}]\subseteq H^{+}
that
F_{0}H\cap[H,H]=0
. So, obviously, (2) implies (1).

Suppose (1) holds, that is,
[F_{m}H,F_{l}H]=0\subseteq F_{m+l-k}H
when
m+l\leqslant k
. We prove (2) by induction. Suppose, for integer
N\geqslant k
,
[F_{m}H,F_{l}H]\subseteq F_{m+l-k}H
whenever
m+l\leqslant N
. We prove that
[u,v]\in F_{N+1-k}H
for any element
u\in F_{m}H
and
v\in F_{l}H
, where
m+l=N+1
. By the basic property of the coradical filtration
[
13
, 5.2.2]
,
\delta(u)
, denoted by
u_{1}{}^{\prime}\otimes u_{2}{}^{\prime}
below, lies in
\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}F_{i}H\otimes F_{m-i}H
. Similarly,
\delta(v)=v_{1}{}^{\prime}\otimes v_{2}{}^{\prime}\in\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}F_{j}H\otimes F_{l-j}H
. Thus, by a direct computation,

\displaystyle\Delta[u,v]=

\displaystyle[u,v]\otimes 1+1\otimes[u,v]\,+

\displaystyle[u,v_{1}{}^{\prime}]\otimes v_{2}{}^{\prime}+v_{1}{}^{\prime}\otimes[u,v_{2}{}^{\prime}]+[u_{1}{}^{\prime},v]\otimes u_{2}{}^{\prime}\,+u_{1}{}^{\prime}\otimes[u_{2}{}^{\prime},v]\,+

\displaystyle[u_{1}{}^{\prime},v_{1}{}^{\prime}]\otimes u_{2}{}^{\prime}v_{2}{}^{\prime}+v_{1}{}^{\prime}u_{1}{}^{\prime}\otimes[u_{2}{}^{\prime},v_{2}{}^{\prime}].

Hence

\displaystyle\delta[u,v]=

\displaystyle\Delta[u,v]-[u,v]\otimes 1-1\otimes[u,v]\,\in

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}([F_{m}H,F_{j}H]\otimes F_{l-j}H+F_{j}H\otimes[F_{m}H,F_{l-j}H])\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}([F_{i}H,F_{l}H]\otimes F_{m-i}H+F_{i}H\otimes[F_{m-i}H,F_{l}H])\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant m-1\\
1\leqslant j\leqslant l-1\end{subarray}}([F_{i}H,F_{j}H]\otimes F_{m+l-i-j}H+F_{i+j}H\otimes[F_{m-i}H,F_{l-j}H]).

It follows from the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\delta[u,v]

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k+1-m}^{l-1}F_{m+j-k}H\otimes F_{l-j}H+\sum_{j=1}^{N-k}F_{j}H\otimes F_{N+1-j-k}H\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k+1-l}^{m-1}F_{i+l-k}H\otimes F_{m-i}H+\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}F_{i}H\otimes F_{N+1-i-k}H\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{(i+j)=k+1}^{N-1}F_{i+j-k}H\otimes F_{N+1-i-j}H+\sum_{(i+j)=2}^{N-k}F_{i+j}H\otimes F_{N+1-i-j-k}H.

Therefore,
\delta[u,v]\in\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}F_{i}H\otimes F_{N+1-i-k}H
. It follows from the definition of the coradical filtration that
[u,v]\in F_{N+1-k}H
. Hence (2) holds.

Some basic observations of the Lie algebra

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be the matrix (

5.
Properties of Umbrella Hopf algebras

In this section, we describe the structure and give some homological properties of

.
As given in the construction, the generating set
\mathcal{G}
of

is divided into three parts. In Proposition

we will show that

is an iterated crossed product of the enveloping algebras of these three parts as a Hopf algebra.

5.1.
Crossed product of Hopf algebras

It is well known that the algebra structure of crossed products can be characterized by cleft extensions
[
13
, Theorem 7.2.2]
. The corresponding coalgebra and Hopf versions are studied in
[
2
]
. We first recall the definition of the crossed product as an Hopf algebra following
[
2
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
K
and
J
be two Hopf algebras. Suppose there is a
J
-weak action
\gamma
on
K

[
2
, Definition 2.0]
(specifically,
K
is a left
J
-module algebra). A linear map
\sigma:J\otimes J\to K
is called a cocycle, if

\displaystyle\sigma(1,a)=\sigma(a,1)=\varepsilon(a)1_{K},

\displaystyle\forall a\in J,

\displaystyle\gamma(a_{1},\sigma(b_{1},c_{1}))\sigma(a_{2},b_{2}c_{2})=\sigma(a_{1},b_{1})\sigma(a_{2}b_{2},c),

\displaystyle\forall a,b,c\in J.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
K
and
J
be two Hopf algebras.
Suppose there is a
K
-weak coaction
\rho
on
J

[
2
, Definition 2.10]

\rho:J\to J\otimes K,a\mapsto\rho(a)^{(1)}\otimes\rho(a)^{(2)}
(specifically,
J
is a right
K
-comodule coalgebra). A linear map
\tau:J\to K\otimes K,a\mapsto\tau(a)^{(1)}\otimes\tau(a)^{(2)}
is called a co-cocycle, if

\displaystyle\quad\varepsilon_{K}(\tau(a)^{(1)})\otimes\tau(a)^{(2)}=\tau(a)^{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}(\tau(a)^{(2)})=\varepsilon_{J}(a)1_{K},

\displaystyle\forall a\in J,

\displaystyle\quad(\tau(a_{1})^{(1)})_{1}\tau(\rho(a_{2})^{(1)})^{(1)}\otimes(\tau(a_{1})^{(1)})_{2}\tau(\rho(a_{2})^{(1)})^{(2)}\otimes\tau(a_{1})^{(2)}\rho(a_{2})^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\tau(a_{1})^{(1)}\otimes(\tau(a_{1})^{(2)})_{1}\tau(a_{2})^{(1)}\otimes(\tau(a_{1})^{(2)})_{2}\tau(a_{2})^{(2)},

\displaystyle\forall a\in J.

Let
K
and
J
be two Hopf algebras with the data
(\gamma,\sigma,\rho,\tau)
as above. Under certain conditions,
(\gamma,\sigma)
and
(\rho,\tau)
give an algebra structure and a coalgebra structure on
K\otimes J
respectively. Furthermore,
K\otimes J
has a Hopf structure under some compatible conditions as below
[
2
, Theorem 2.20]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
K
and
J
be two Hopf algebras. Suppose that there is a
J
-weak action
\gamma
on
K
and an
K
-weak coaction
\rho
on
J
. Let
\sigma:J\otimes J\to K
be a cocycle and
\tau:J\to K\otimes K
be a co-cocycle. If the vector space
K\otimes J
, with the following structures, is a Hopf algebra, then it is called the crossed product of
K
and
J
with the data
(\gamma,\sigma,\rho,\tau)
, denoted by
K^{\tau}\#_{\sigma}J
.

(1)

(unit)
1_{K^{\tau}\#_{\sigma}J}=1_{K}\#1_{J}
.

(2)

(product)
(h\#a)(g\#b):=h\gamma(a_{1},g)\sigma(a_{2},b_{1})\#a_{3}b_{2}
.

(3)

(counit)
\varepsilon_{K^{\tau}\#_{\sigma}J}=\varepsilon_{K}\#\varepsilon_{J}
.

(4)

(coproduct)
\Delta(h\#a)=(h_{1}\tau(a_{1})^{(1)}\#\rho(a_{2})^{(1)})\otimes(h_{2}\tau(a_{1})^{(2)}\rho(a_{2})^{(2)}\#a_{3})
.

(5)

(antipode)
S(h\#a)=(\gamma(S(\rho(a)^{(1)})_{2},S(\rho(a)^{(2)}))\#S(\rho(a)^{(1)})_{1})(Sh\#1)
.

The cocycle
\sigma
is often omitted in the notation if
\sigma(a,b)=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b)1_{K}
for any
a,b\in J
. Similarly, the co-cocycle
\tau
is omitted if
\tau(a)=\varepsilon(a)1_{K}\otimes 1_{K}
for any
a\in J
.

Instead of checking the compatibility conditions, which are tedious, we check the equivalent characterization of cleft extension as a Hopf algebra
[
2
, Definition 3.2.13]
. As is summarized in
[
4
, Proposition 3.1.12]
, any cleft extension
H
of
K
by
J
as a Hopf algebra is isomorphic to a crossed product
K^{\tau}\#_{\sigma}J
.

Definition 5.4
.

A Hopf algebra
H
is a cleft extension of the Hopf algebra
K
by the Hopf algebra
J
if the following conditions hold.

(1)

There is an exact sequence of Hopf algebras
[
2
, Definition 1.2.0]

0\to K\xrightarrow{i}H\xrightarrow{\pi}J\to 0.

(2)

There is a convolution invertible right
J
-comodule map
\chi_{H}:J\to H
such that
\chi_{H}(1_{J})=1_{H}
.

(3)

There is a convolution invertible left
K
-module map
\xi_{H}:H\to K
such that
\varepsilon_{K}\xi_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}
.

(4)

\xi_{H}\chi_{H}=\varepsilon_{J}1_{K}.

Lemma 5.5
.

The conditions (1) and (2) in
Definition
5.4
with
\varepsilon_{H}\chi_{H}=\chi_{J}
are sufficient to give a cleft extension.

Proof.

See
[
4
, Lemma 3.1.14]
.
∎

The Hopf Ore extension of a connected Hopf algebra of invariant type defined in

is a special case of cleft extension as a Hopf algebra. Note that a Hopf Ore extension

. In the following lemma, we use

to denote

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose

is a connected Hopf algebra and

is a Hopf Ore extension of invariant type. Then
H
is also connected and
H\cong K^{\tau}\#\mathbb{k}[\overline{z}]
is a crossed product as a Hopf algebra.

Proof.

First of all, we may assume that

, otherwise replace it with

. Since

\overline{H}:=H/K^{+}H=\oplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{k}\overline{z}^{i}
as a space. As

is an ideal,

is a Hopf algebra and

as an algebra. The freeness also indicates that

is faithfully flat. In other words,

is a quantum homogeneous space of

[

and

of Hopf algebras
follows directly
[
2

. By
[

\delta z\in K\otimes K
. Being more precisely,

as

. Thus
\Delta\overline{z}=\overline{z}\otimes 1+1\otimes\overline{z}
, and the following maps from

to

are
\overline{H}
-comodule homomorphisms.

\chi_{H}(\overline{z}^{i})=z^{i},\ \chi^{\prime}_{H}(\overline{z}^{i})=(-z)^{i}.

It is direct to check that
\chi_{H}(1_{\overline{H}})=1_{H}
and
\varepsilon_{H}\chi_{H}=\varepsilon_{\overline{H}}
. And the convolution of
\chi_{H}
and
\chi^{\prime}_{H}
is the unit in

\displaystyle\chi_{H}\star\chi^{\prime}_{H}(\overline{z}^{i})=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\C^{j}_{i}\chi_{H}(\overline{z}^{j})\chi^{\prime}_{H}(\overline{z}^{i-j})=(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\C^{j}_{i}(-1)^{i-j})z^{i}=0,\forall i\geqslant 1.

By Lemma

is a cleft extension of

by

, so there exist cocycle

Then by the map

Note that

in the above lemma is the enveloping algebra of

as a Hopf algebra. So if

5.3.
Calabi-Yau property of the umbrella algebras

By Theorem
2.2
any connected Hopf algebra
H
of finite GK-dimension is skew Calabi-Yau, that is,
H
is homological smooth (of dimension
d
), and there exists
\sigma\in\Aut(H)
such that as
H
-
H
-bimodules,

\displaystyle\Ext_{H^{e}}^{i}(H,H^{e})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&i<d,\\
{}^{1}H^{\sigma},&i=d.\end{array}\right.

The automorphism
\sigma
is called a Nakayama automorphism of
H
, and
H
is called Calabi-Yau if
\sigma
is an inner automorphism. The following proposition gives the Nakayama automorphism of
\UM(r,2s)
.

Proposition 5.10
.

The Hopf algebra
H=\UM(r,2s)
is a skew Calabi-Yau algebra with Nakayama automorphism

\sigma(x_{i})=x_{i},\sigma(y_{i})=y_{i},

\sigma(M)=M+(2-2s)\tr(M),\forall M\in\mathfrak{so}(B).

If
r=2s
then
H
is a Calabi-Yau algebra.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, the associated graded algebra
\gr H
of
H(=\UM(B)=\UM(r,2s))
with respect to the coradical filtration is a commutative polynomial ring. As the elements
\overline{y_{i}}
lie in
(\gr H)_{2}
and the principle symbols of other elements in
\mathcal{G}
lie in
(\gr H)_{1}
, relations (
3.5
)-(
3.11
) ensure that
\gr H=\mathbb{k}[\overline{\mathcal{G}}]
. Then
H
fits in the framework of
[
17
, Theorem 0.1]
, that is,
H
is a filtered deformation of the Poisson polynomial algebra
(\gr H,\{-,-\})
, where

\{a+F_{m-1}H,b+F_{n-1}H\}=[a,b]+F_{m+n-2}H,\forall a\in F_{m}H,b\in F_{n}H.

It follows that the Nakayama automorphism
\sigma
of
H
satisfies that:

\sigma(a)-a=\phi_{\eta}(a),\forall a\in P(H),

\sigma(y_{i})-y_{i}-\phi_{\eta}(y_{i})=\alpha_{i}\in F_{0}H,\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant r

where
\phi_{\eta}:H\to\mathbb{k},a\mapsto\textrm{Hdet}\{\overline{a},-\}
, and
\textrm{Hdet}\{\overline{a},-\}
is the homological determinant of the Hamilton derivation
\{\overline{a},-\}
on the Poisson polynomial algebra.
In our cases, for any
a\in\mathcal{G}
,
\phi_{\eta}(a)
is exactly the trace of
\{\overline{a},-\}
acting on
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
, see
[
17
, Corollary 0.5, Proposition 0.7]
for details. By direct calculation,

\displaystyle\phi_{\eta}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall 0\leqslant i\leqslant r

\displaystyle\phi_{\eta}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant r

\displaystyle\phi_{\eta}(M)

\displaystyle=2\tr(M)+\tr(\ad_{\mathfrak{so}(B)}M),

\displaystyle\forall M\in\mathfrak{so}(B).

Let
B^{\prime}
be the submatrix of
B
of full rank
2s
, then
\mathfrak{so}(B^{\prime})
is a simple Lie algebra. Recall from Lemma
4.1
that
\mathfrak{so}(B)
consists of the block matrices
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}M^{11}&M^{12}\\
0&M^{22}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
M^{11}\in\mathfrak{so}(B^{\prime})
and
M^{12},M^{22}
are arbitrary matrices. Pick a linear basis

b:=b(\mathfrak{so}(B^{\prime}))\cup\{e_{ij}\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant r,\\
2s+1\leqslant j\leqslant r\end{subarray}}.

For a matrix
M=\sum_{X\in b}\alpha_{X}X
, we denote
X^{*}(M)=\alpha_{X}
. Then by direct calculation,

\displaystyle\tr(\ad_{\mathfrak{so}(B)}M)=

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in b}X^{*}([M,X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr(\ad_{\mathfrak{so}(B^{\prime})}M^{11})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant r,\\
2s+1\leqslant j\leqslant r\end{subarray}}(e_{ij})^{*}([M,e_{ij}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant r,\\
2s+1\leqslant j\leqslant r\end{subarray}}(M_{ii}-M_{jj})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-2s)\tr(M)-r\tr(M^{22}).

However by definition,
M^{11}B^{\prime}
is a symmetric matrix. Then
M^{11}=(M^{11}B^{\prime})(B^{\prime})^{-1}
is a product of a symmetric matrix and an anti-symmetric one, whose trace must be 0. As a consequence,
\tr(M)=\tr(M^{22})
, and
\phi_{\eta}(M)=(2-2s)\tr(M)
.

On the other hand,
\sigma
is an algebra automorphism, so it has to satisfy relations (
3.5
)-(
3.11
). Especially, for any element
M\in\mathfrak{so}(B)
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
,

\sum_{j}M_{ij}\sigma(y_{j})=\sigma([M,y_{i}])=[\sigma(M),\sigma(y_{i})]=[M+2\tr(M),y_{i}+\alpha_{i}]=\sum_{j}M_{ij}y_{j}.

Let
\hat{B}=B+\sum_{i=2s+1}^{r}e_{ii}
, which lies in
\mathfrak{so}(B)
. Since
\hat{B}
is of full rank,
\sigma(y_{i})-y_{i}
is
0
for any
i
.

If
r=2s
, then
\tr(M)=\tr(M^{11})=0.
The Nakayama automorphism
\sigma
acts on the generators trivially, which makes
H
a Calabi-Yau algebra.
∎

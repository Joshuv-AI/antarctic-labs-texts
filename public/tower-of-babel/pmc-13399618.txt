Arthroscopic Segmental Labral Reconstruction Using a Presutured Iliotibial Band Allograft Soaked in Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate
Authors: Jacob D. Kodra, Arthur Saroyan, Kevin Lord, Andrew Schab, Jeremy K. Ansah‐Twum, Jon Salandra, Renato Locks, Andrew E. Jimenez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLabral reconstruction is a surgical procedure in which a graft is used to replace irreparable acetabular labral tissue. Biologic augmentation with bone marrow aspirate concentrate harvested from the ipsilateral iliac crest is increasingly used to enhance graft incorporation and potentially improve functional outcomes, especially in patients with concomitant chondral injury. Recent prospective data indicate that bone marrow aspirate concentrate augmentation during labral repair or reconstruction is associated with greater improvements in patient‐reported outcome measures at intermediate‐term follow‐up. The described technique integrates these advances into segmental labral reconstruction using a presutured iliotibial band allograft, highlighting a reproducible and biologically enhanced treatment for challenging labral pathology.

Irreparable damage to the labrum or lack of viable tissue has led to the evolution of labral reconstruction. 1 , 2 When indicated, acetabular labral reconstruction has been shown to improve patient outcomes and rates of conversion to total hip arthroplasty. 3 , 4 , 5 In femoroacetabular impingement, irreparable labral injuries treated via reconstruction are associated with superior clinical outcomes compared to segmental resection and debridement. 1 , 6 Furthermore, several studies report comparable outcomes between labral repair and reconstruction. 4 , 7 , 8

Bone marrow aspirate concentrate (BMAC) has emerged as a promising biologic adjuvant in hip arthroscopy, particularly in patients with concomitant cartilage damage. BMAC contains mesenchymal stem cells and growth factors that may enhance cartilage regeneration and modulate inflammation. 9 , 10 , 11 Recent evidence shows that patients with moderate cartilage injury undergoing labral repair with BMAC augmentation report significantly greater functional improvements at 12 and 24 months than those without BMAC. 10

The “pull‐through” technique accommodates variable defects without graft mismatch and may decrease operating time. 12 A presutured, pretubularized allograft offers several advantages, including reproducibility, efficiency, improved visualization, and enhanced graft passage. 9 , 13 Although there is a lack of consensus regarding optimal graft selection, iliotibial band (ITB) grafts have shown excellent patient‐reported outcomes and satisfaction. 6 , 9 For fixation, the knotless Q‐Fix anchor (Smith & Nephew, Memphis, TN) uses a “drill‐and‐twist” mechanism, reducing the risk of anchor tip breakage. 14 This anchor also allows for placement near the acetabular rim, with a reduced risk of cartilage penetration and maximized bone preservation. 15

This technique describes a comprehensive approach to acetabular labral reconstruction using an AceConnex presutured, pretubularized ITB allograft (AlloSource, Centennial, Colorado) that was soaked in bone BMAC and secured using either knotless 1.8 mm Q‐Fix all‐suture anchors or 1.8 mm knotless Fibertak (Arthrex, Naples, Florida).

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

Patient Preparation and Positioning

The patient is positioned supine on a Berchtold surgical table (Stryker, Kalamazoo, MI) with a hip distractor (Smith & Nephew, Memphis, TN) (Figure 1A). After intubation, both legs of the table are lowered and positioned laterally. The patient is brought distally to center the operative hip over the support bar for sufficient lateral fluoroscopic clearance. The contralateral leg is then abducted to facilitate fluoroscopic access. Next, the bed is raised to bring the greater trochanter to umbilical height, and the patient is placed in the Trendelenburg position with legs parallel to the floor. Arthroscopy towers and monitors are positioned across from the surgeon, with fluoroscopy and dual monitors stationed distally, and an additional monitor proximally for alpha angle assessment (Video 1).

(B) The 3 portals used are identified as: anterolateral portal, anterior portal, and distal anterolateral accessory portal. Anterior superior iliac spine (*) and greater trochanter (*) are also identified. This setup establishes safe portal placement and anatomic orientation for subsequent labral reconstruction.

Portal Placement

Anterolateral Portal

Manual traction is applied to both legs before routine portal placement (Figure 1B). An 18‐gauge spinal needle is placed anterior and proximal to the tip of the greater trochanter. Proximal‐distal trajectory is confirmed before advancing the needle into the joint using fluoroscopy. After capsule penetration, an air arthrogram confirms intra‐articular entry. The joint is then distracted, and 0.25% bupivacaine with epinephrine is injected upon needle withdrawal.

A 2.5 cm longitudinal skin incision is made with an 11 blade. An 18‐gauge, 3.5‐inch standard spinal needle is introduced at 10° cephalad, targeting the fovea. After guidewire placement, a 4.5 mm Smith & Nephew cannula is advanced into the joint. Dry arthroscopy is maintained initially to avoid hemarthrosis.

Modified Anterolateral/Anterior Portal

A spinal needle is advanced into the triangle between the femoral head and anterior labrum at approximately 30° from horizontal. After local anesthetic injection, a guidewire and 4.5 mm cannula are placed. A Smith & Nephew minitape curved beaver blade initiates the anterior interportal capsulotomy, preserving an acetabular cuff of capsule for closure. A #2 nonabsorbable traction suture is passed through the capsule near the acetabular rim with a slingshot device, elevating the capsule for visualization.

Cannula and camera exchanges are performed via switching sticks to complete the capsulotomy through the anterolateral portal. A second capsular stitch can be placed adjacent to the anterolateral portal for visualization.

Posterolateral Portal

A posterolateral portal is created 1‐2 cm posterior to the anterolateral portal. Next, a switching stick is passed through the interportal capsulotomy, and a cannula is advanced for posterior graft shuttling.

The ligamentum teres, labrum, and chondrolabral junction are systematically evaluated (Figure 2). The labrum is probed and classified according to the Seldes system:1.Detachment of the labrum from the chondral surface.2.Cleavage planes within the labral tissue.

Distal Anterolateral Accessory Portal

Following diagnostic arthroscopy and assessment, the distal anterolateral accessory portal is established. A spinal needle is directed toward the capsulotomy, and is again confirmed with injection. A switching stick and sled facilitate triangular orientation of the cannula with the anterior and anterolateral portals.

Chondrolabral and Graft Preparation

The damaged labrum is debrided to preserve healthy labral tissue at both ends of the reconstruction. A rim of periacetabular tissue is cleared for anchor placement.

An AceConnex ITB allograft is used, typically the largest available size (5.5‐6.0 mm), to facilitate efficient handling and passage (Table 1). The Vicryl ends are excised. On one end, a FiberLoop (Arthrex, Naples, FL) is sequentially passed and locked 5 times, preserving a terminal loop. The opposite end is trimmed and stored in saline‐soaked gauze (Figure 3). The graft is soaked in BMAC obtained from the ipsilateral iliac crest (Arthrex Angel Kit) prior to introduction (Table 2).

Anchor Placement

A slotted drill guide is placed anteriorly on the acetabular rim through the distal anterolateral accessory portal. A 1.8 mm knotless Smith & Nephew Q‐Fix knotless anchor is drilled and seated. Sutures are retrieved out of the distal anterolateral accessory portal and clamped to the drape with a Kelly clamp in sequence for organization. Anchors are sequentially placed posteriorly with even spacing of 7 mm apart (Figure 4).

Intraoperative image of right hip during anchor placement is visualized with an arthroscope from the anterolateral portal. This step illustrates anchor positioning and suture management prior to graft passage. (A, acetabulum; C, capsule.)

Pull‐Through Technique

A loop grasper retrieves blue repair suture from the most anterior anchor. A Keith needle is used to pass the blue suture through the graft in a horizontal mattress configuration. The looped end of the graft is then shuttled from the anterior to the posterior portal and retrieved, pulling it into the joint along the acetabular rim (Figure 5).

The left hip is shown with the patient's head to the right, and feet to the left. Portals are identified by white arrows. In this image, sutures from the Knotless Q‐Fix anchors are attached to the external drape, reflecting their placement from anterior to posterior, with clamps aligned correspondingly from proximal to distal. This configuration confirms sequential anchor placement before graft delivery. (A, anterior; AL, anterolateral; DALA, distal anterolateral accessory; PL, posterolateral.)

Graft Fixation

Fixation proceeds from anterior to posterior in the order the anchors were placed. The white passing suture is retrieved through the anterior portal. The repair stitch is placed through its loop. Pulling the contralateral limb seats the graft against the rim.

For subsequent anchors, the blue repair suture is passed under, then over the graft, and retrieved via the cannula. The white shuttle suture is then used to cinch the blue repair stitch to the rim. Before tightening, the loop position is adjusted on the graft to ensure even spacing.

At the last 2 anchors, the penultimate suture is left slightly loose to allow final stitch passage under the graft with subsequent tightening. The graft excess is then amputated if needed. Traction is released, and the suction seal is confirmed. Cam resection and capsular closure conclude the case (Figure 6). Adjunct Procedures and Closure

Cam resection is performed as indicated using fluoroscopic guidance. Capsular closure is achieved with interrupted Smith & Nephew minitape sutures, approximating the preserved acetabular cuff to restore stability.

Postoperative Protocol

Patients are prescribed aspirin for thromboembolism prophylaxis, and Losartan to reduce the risk of fibrosis and adhesions. Celebrex 200 mg twice daily is also given for heterotopic ossification prophylaxis.

A hip brace is prescribed to restrict excessive motion and protect the repair and worn for 6 weeks. Patients are maintained on partial weight‐bearing restrictions with crutches for the following 6 weeks.

Physical therapy is initiated within 48 hours, emphasizing passive hip motion and stationary bicycle training, with progression to a rehabilitation program focusing on gradual mobility and strength restoration at 6 weeks. By 6 months, patients undergo biomechanical assessment and a home exercise program. Return to sport or high‐level activity is permitted once patients demonstrate restoration of strength and stability.

DISCUSSION

This article describes an efficient and reproducible technique for segmented labral reconstruction performed using a presutured, pretubularized ITB allograft soaked in BMAC.

Labral reconstruction is a proven treatment for irreparable labral pathology of the hip. 1 , 8 , 16 , 17 This procedure is effective in both primary and revision settings, with favorable outcomes from short‐ to long‐term follow‐up. 5 , 18 , 19 , 20 Comparing reconstruction techniques, use of allografts versus autografts may not significantly impact clinical outcomes or satisfaction. 16 However, autografts are linked to lower revision and complication rates, whereas allografts offer technical advantages and eliminate donor‐site morbidity. 21 , 22 , 23 Nonetheless, allograft usage is supported by robust mid‐ to long‐term data with durable improvements in function and patient satisfaction. 16 , 24

The presutured, pretubularized ITB allograft reduces intraoperative variability in graft preparation and may limit traction‐related complications. Outcomes using these grafts remain underreported, yet their use continues to emerge in segmental or circumferential labral reconstruction. 25 Prefixed grafts enable precise sizing, efficient intraoperative handling, and reliable restoration of hip biomechanics. Based on the senior author's (A.J.) experience, employing a presutured and pretubularized allograft reduces surgical variability and traction time, resulting in more consistent and reproducible reconstruction outcomes.

Soaking the graft in BMAC serves as a minimally invasive adjunct that may reduce pain and improve function. 11 , 26 , 27 BMAC is typically harvested from the iliac crest and injected intra‐articularly at the time of reconstruction. 28 Recent prospective cohort data report that BMAC augmentation is associated with improved mid‐term functional outcomes, especially in patients with early degenerative changes. 11 Evidence suggests that BMAC may enhance graft integration and chondral healing, with protocol optimization requiring further investigation. 11 , 27 , 28

Important limitations involve the logistics and intrinsic properties of the presutured and tubularized ITB allograft. This technique requires the surgeon to be highly organized in managing suture placement. Additionally, the AceConnex graft is more expensive than standard grafts. Furthermore, as with any allograft, the concern of immune‐related graft reaction should be acknowledged. 29 Use of BMAC may also require additional time and staffing.

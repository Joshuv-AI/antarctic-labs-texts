Nomogram for predicting postoperative recurrence in elderly patients with hepatocellular carcinoma based on multimodal ultrasound
Authors: Yinling Jiang, Lina Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo develop and validate a nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) based on multimodal ultrasound parameters.MethodsClinical data of 299 elderly HCC patients who underwent laparoscopic hepatectomy in our hospital from January 2021 to June 2024 were retrospectively collected. Patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 209) and a validation cohort (n = 90) at a ratio of 7:3. According to tumor recurrence within 1 year after surgery, patients were classified into recurrence and non-recurrence groups. Preoperative multimodal ultrasound parameters and other clinical characteristics were recorded. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent risk factors for postoperative recurrence. A nomogram prediction model was constructed based on multimodal ultrasound parameters using R software. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsNo significant differences were observed in baseline characteristics between the training and validation cohorts (P > 0.05). In the training cohort, the recurrence group had a lower proportion of intact tumor capsules, lower hepatic artery pulsatility index (HA-PI) and resistive index (HA-RI), higher tumor stiffness and strain ratio (SR), shorter washout time (WT), and a higher prevalence of chronic viral hepatitis compared with the non-recurrence group (all P < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression revealed that non-intact tumor capsule, lower HA-RI, higher SR, shorter WT, and concomitant chronic viral hepatitis were independent risk factors for postoperative recurrence (P < 0.05). Based on these predictors, a nomogram prediction model was developed. ROC analysis showed areas under the curve (AUC) of 0.906 (95% CI: 0.866–0.947) in the training cohort and 0.926 (95% CI: 0.868–0.983) in the validation cohort, indicating excellent discrimination. Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes in both cohorts (P > 0.05). DCA demonstrated substantial clinical net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities (0.01–0.92 in the training cohort; 0.01–0.96 in the validation cohort).ConclusionThe nomogram prediction model based on multimodal ultrasound parameters demonstrates favorable predictive performance for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients.

Introduction

Currently, treatment strategies for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) include surgical resection, liver transplantation, and local ablation, among which laparoscopic hepatectomy is an important approach to prolong patient survival [1]. However, elderly HCC patients, due to age-related decline in physical function and the presence of multiple comorbidities, are particularly prone to early postoperative tumor recurrence, which has become a major challenge affecting their long-term survival [2, 3]. Identifying factors associated with postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients and accurately recognizing high-risk individuals at an early stage are of great significance for guiding individualized treatment, optimizing follow-up strategies, and improving prognosis.

Previous studies have analyzed various factors influencing postoperative recurrence in HCC patients, but most have relied on general clinical data and laboratory indicators such as tumor markers [4, 5]. These approaches fail to fully reflect the biological behavior of tumors, resulting in limited predictive efficiency for individualized recurrence risk [6, 7]. In contrast, multimodal ultrasound, with advantages of being noninvasive, radiation-free, real-time, and reproducible, has demonstrated significant value in the diagnosis and preoperative evaluation of HCC. It allows assessment of tumor biological behavior from multiple perspectives, including morphology, hemodynamics, and tissue stiffness, thus providing multidimensional functional imaging evidence for recurrence prediction [8]. Nevertheless, effectively utilizing these multimodal ultrasound parameters to predict postoperative recurrence risk remains a challenge.

In recent years, nomogram prediction models have been increasingly applied in disease diagnosis, prediction, and prognosis. By integrating multiple parameters, they can visually display results in a graphical format and quantitatively estimate the probability of outcome events, thereby providing concrete data to support clinical decision-making [9]. Based on this background, the present study developed a nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients using multimodal ultrasound parameters, aiming to provide a quantitative reference for individualized clinical decision-making.

Objects and methods

Research subjects

The sample size was calculated using the formula N = Zα2×P(1-P)/δ2. Here, P represents the risk of recurrence within one year after surgery in elderly patients with hepatocellular carcinoma. Referring to the study by LIU et al. [10], P was set at 32.45%, and δ was set at 5.60%. The calculated sample size was 269 cases. Considering that approximately 10% of samples may be invalid during the research process, in order to ensure sufficient sample size, at least 299 cases are required.

A total of 299 elderly patients with HCC who underwent laparoscopic hepatectomy at our hospital between January 2020 and June 2023 and completed one-year postoperative follow-up were retrospectively included. All clinical data were obtained from the hospital electronic medical record system and cross-checked by two independent researchers to ensure accuracy and reliability. A computer-generated list of 299 unique random numbers was created, each corresponding to one patient. Subsequently, an independent investigator who was not involved in the study randomly assigned patients into a training cohort (n = 209) and a validation cohort (n = 90) in a 7:3 ratio. Within one year after surgery, tumor recurrence occurred in 67 patients in the training cohort and 27 patients in the validation cohort.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Meeting the diagnostic criteria for HCC [11]; (2) Age ≥ 65 years; (3) Underwent preoperative multimodal ultrasound examination with clear imaging quality; (4) Tumor stage Ⅰa–Ⅱa according to the China Liver Cancer Staging (CNLC) system [11]; (5) First-time laparoscopic hepatectomy performed successfully by the same experienced surgical team; (6) Postoperative follow-up duration ≥ 1 year; (7) Complete clinical and follow-up data available. Exclusion criteria: (1) Concomitant malignancies; (2) History of other tumor-related non-surgical treatments, such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE); (3) Interval between preoperative multimodal ultrasound and laparoscopic hepatectomy >2 weeks. All patients underwent first-time laparoscopic hepatectomy performed by the same experienced surgical team, ensuring consistency in surgical technique across the study cohort. Methods

Patient clinical data were retrieved via the hospital electronic medical record system, including preoperative multimodal ultrasonography results, general information, and postoperative follow-up.

Multimodal ultrasonography

Examinations were performed on an ACUSON Oxana 2 ultrasound diagnostic system (Siemens Healthcare, USA) with a 3–5 MHz transducer. Patients fasted for 6–8 h prior to scanning and were examined in the supine position. First, conventional two-dimensional ultrasound of the liver was performed to assess lesion anatomical location, number, morphology, size, and the integrity of the capsule. Next, color Doppler flow imaging was applied to evaluate intra-lesional blood-flow distribution. Thereafter, shear-wave elastography was activated; the region of interest was adjusted to 2–3 times the lesion size, and an elasticity imaging score was assigned together with the strain ratio (SR). Finally, contrast-enhanced ultrasound was performed: through a pre-placed peripheral venous catheter in the antecubital fossa, 1.5 mL of SonoVue suspension (Bracco International B.V.; Chinese approval No. HJ20171213; 59 mg) was injected over 3–5 s, followed by a 5 mL saline flush. Injection of the contrast agent was synchronized with the initiation of a timing device, and cine loops were stored. Quantitative analysis of the CEUS time-intensity curve (TIC) was performed using the SonoLiver software, with continuous observation of the enhancement between the lesion and the surrounding liver tissue. A schematic diagram of the CEUS technique and enhancement curve analysis is shown in Fig. 2.

Ultrasound images were independently analyzed in a blinded manner by two experienced sonographers (with ≥ 5 years of work experience in the Department of Medical Imaging). Multimodal ultrasound parameters were recorded, including tumor number (single, multiple), maximum diameter, anatomical location (left lobe, right lobe, bilobar), morphology (regular, irregular), margin [well-defined (a clear histological interface between the tumor and normal liver parenchyma without a blurred infiltrative band on contrast-enhanced imaging) vs. ill-defined (no clear histological interface between the tumor and surrounding parenchyma with a blurred infiltrative band visible on contrast-enhanced imaging)], capsule integrity (intact, non-intact), internal echogenicity (homogeneous, heterogeneous), mean portal venous flow velocity, hepatic artery pulsatility index (HA-PI), hepatic artery resistive index (HA-RI), stiffness, SR, contrast arrival time (AT), time to peak (TTP), and washout time (WT). All continuous parameters were measured three times, and the mean values were taken as the final results. Interobserver agreement between the two sonographers was assessed using the kappa statistic for categorical variables and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for continuous variables. For parameters with poor consistency, the images were re-evaluated by a third senior physician (associate chief physician or higher), and the final results were determined accordingly.

General information

The following patient data were reviewed: age, sex, concomitant chronic viral hepatitis, presence of cirrhosis, smoking status, heavy alcohol use, CNLC tumor stage, operative time, intraoperative blood transfusion, and duration of hepatic inflow occlusion.

Postoperative follow-up

All patients underwent at least 1 year of follow-up. The first visit was scheduled 1–2 months after surgery, followed by visits every 3 months. Ultrasound was performed at each visit; when necessary, dynamic contrast-enhanced CT, dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, and tumor marker tests were obtained. Postoperative recurrence was defined as the appearance of a new intrahepatic lesion confirmed either by pathology or, in the majority of cases, by dynamic contrast-enhanced CT or MRI showing typical imaging characteristics of HCC (arterial phase hyperenhancement with portal or delayed washout). Among the 94 patients who experienced recurrence, 27 (28.7%) were confirmed by pathology, and 67 (71.3%) were diagnosed by imaging criteria alone. Based on recurrence status within 1 year after surgery, patients were classified into a recurrence group and a non-recurrence group.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages [n (%)] and compared using the Chi-square test. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (‾x ± s) and compared using the independent-samples t-test. Factors associated with postoperative recurrence were examined using multivariable logistic regression. Based on the multivariable logistic regression results, a nomogram prediction model was constructed in R version 4.3.3. Model performance was assessed with receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calibration curves, and clinical utility was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA). A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Construction of a nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients

The multimodal ultrasound (US) parameters and the other major variable (concomitant chronic viral hepatitis) identified by multivariate logistic regression analysis were incorporated into the nomogram. Using R software, a nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients was developed (see Fig. 3). Application of the nomogram: for each patient, locate the score corresponding to each variable and sum them to obtain the total point. Then, draw a vertical line downward from this point to the “Predicted probability” axis to determine the risk of postoperative tumor recurrence.

Assessment and validation of nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients

The ROC curve results of the training cohort and validation cohort showed that the areas under the curve (AUC) were 0.906 (95% CI: 0.866–0.947) for the training cohort (Fig. 4A) and 0.926 (95% CI: 0.868–0.983) for the validation cohort (Fig. 4B), indicating excellent discriminative ability of the nomogram prediction model. Calibration curves demonstrated that the deviations between observed outcomes and predicted risks were not statistically significant in either the training cohort (χ² = 5.809, P = 0.669; Fig. 5A) or the validation cohort (χ² = 10.199, P = 0.251; Fig. 5B), suggesting good calibration performance of the model.

Discussion

Previous studies have shown that HCC is highly aggressive, prone to recurrence and metastasis, and that elderly patients have a higher risk of early postoperative recurrence after laparoscopic hepatectomy compared with younger patients, reaching over 30% [12]. In this study, a retrospective analysis of clinical data from 209 elderly HCC patients in the training cohort revealed a 1-year postoperative recurrence rate of 32.06% (67/209), which is consistent with previous reports. These findings indicate that elderly HCC patients are at high risk of postoperative recurrence. Clinically, high-risk patients should be promptly identified, and treatment and follow-up strategies should be adjusted accordingly to reduce tumor recurrence and improve patient prognosis.

Ultrasound is the most commonly used imaging modality for liver evaluation in clinical practice. Multimodal ultrasound integrates conventional two-dimensional ultrasound, color Doppler flow imaging, shear-wave elastography, and contrast-enhanced ultrasound, providing detailed information on HCC morphology, vascular characteristics, and tissue properties from multiple dimensions, thereby demonstrating significant advantages in tumor characterization [13, 14]. In the present study, we analyzed the impact of Multimodal ultrasound parameters on postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients. The results showed that, compared with the non-recurrence group, the recurrence group exhibited a lower proportion of intact tumor capsules, lower HA-RI, higher SR, and shorter WT. Multivariate logistic regression analysis further confirmed that these factors were independent predictors of postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients (P < 0.05), suggesting a potential association between Multimodal ultrasound parameters and recurrence risk. The HCC capsule is anatomically a fibrous layer that can substantially hinder tumor cells from penetrating into surrounding hepatic parenchyma, thus limiting invasiveness and dissemination [15]. When the tumor capsule is incomplete, cancer cells are prone to intraoperative dissemination beyond the primary lesion, leading to incomplete resection, residual disease, and increased postoperative recurrence risk [16]. Additionally, Fernández Moro et al. [17]reported that tumors with incomplete capsules often exhibit higher proliferative activity and invasiveness, allowing residual tumor cells to rapidly proliferate and invade postoperatively, ultimately contributing to recurrence. HA-RI reflects hepatic arterial hemodynamics, with lower values indicating reduced vascular resistance and increased perfusion. A relatively hyperperfused liver can increase shear stress on endothelial cells, enhance vascular permeability, and facilitate micro-metastasis of tumor cells, reducing the effectiveness of surgical clearance and promoting residual disease, thereby increasing early postoperative recurrence risk [18]. Moreover, after laparoscopic hepatectomy, transient ischemia-reperfusion may occur; preoperatively lower HA-RI and local hyperperfusion may exacerbate postoperative oxidative stress, impair hepatic tissue, induce localized immunosuppression, and promote immune evasion of residual tumor cells, further increasing early recurrence risk [19]. The SR, calculated as the stiffness of the lesion relative to surrounding normal tissue, is an important parameter for objectively assessing the mechanical properties of tumors. Higher SR values indicate greater fibrosis and stiffness of the tumor tissue. Tumors with increased fibrosis and stiffness are generally larger and more invasive, making complete surgical resection more challenging and increasing the likelihood of residual microscopic disease, thereby elevating the risk of early postoperative recurrence [20]. Moreover, the fibrotic and stiff tumor microenvironment can activate mechanotransduction pathways, such as integrin–focal adhesion kinase signaling, which in turn stimulate the mitogen-activated protein kinase pathway, enhancing the proliferation and invasiveness of residual tumor cells and ultimately promoting recurrence. WT refers to the interval from the beginning of contrast agent clearance within a lesion to its complete disappearance during contrast-enhanced ultrasound, and it serves as an important parameter reflecting tumor perfusion dynamics [21]. In HCC, due to incomplete endothelial linings and the presence of arteriovenous shunts, contrast agents typically exhibit a “rapid in, rapid out” pattern compared with normal liver tissue, resulting in shorter WT. A shorter WT indicates higher vascular permeability, facilitating tumor invasion into surrounding tissues [22]. As tumor invasiveness increases, the likelihood of residual lesions during surgery rises, thereby elevating postoperative recurrence risk [23]. Additionally, shortened WT suggests the presence of abundant abnormal neovasculature and disrupted intratumoral blood flow, which can promote tumor cell dissemination into the circulation, increasing the risk of micro-metastasis and intraoperative tumor cell retention. The excessive neovascularization also provides nutrients and pathways that support the proliferation and invasion of residual tumor cells, ultimately contributing to early postoperative recurrence.

Furthermore, multivariate logistic regression analysis in this study indicated that, in addition to Multimodal ultrasound parameters, concomitant chronic viral hepatitis is also an independent risk factor for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients. The underlying mechanism may be that chronic viral hepatitis is a major etiological factor for HCC, and its long-term presence causes persistent liver tissue damage and induces chronic inflammatory responses, which can stimulate the proliferation of residual tumor cells and increase the risk of early postoperative recurrence [24]. Moreover, chronic viral hepatitis can impair immune surveillance, reducing the ability of the immune system to eliminate residual tumor cells and creating a favorable environment for tumor recurrence [25].

Based on the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis, this study constructed a nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients, incorporating multimodal ultrasound parameters and concomitant chronic viral hepatitis. The model’s predictive performance was evaluated using ROC curves, calibration curves, and DCA in both the training and validation cohorts. The results demonstrated that the nomogram exhibited high discrimination, good calibration, and strong clinical applicability. These findings suggest that clinicians can utilize this risk prediction model to identify patients at high risk of postoperative recurrence at an early stage and implement individualized preventive interventions based on patient-specific conditions, which may help reduce recurrence risk and potentially prolong survival. Previous studies on HCC postoperative recurrence prediction mainly include two types of models. The first category integrates postoperative pathological parameters such as microvascular invasion, tumor differentiation, and margin status, which limit preoperative clinical use [26, 27]. The second category relies on preoperative CT or MRI-based radiomics features combined with clinical variables [28, 29]. Although these radiomics models achieved good predictive accuracy (AUCs 0.80–0.90), they require specialized imaging protocols, high-cost equipment, and time-consuming feature extraction. Our study differs in that it utilizes multimodal ultrasound data, which provide real-time, non-invasive, and cost-effective assessment of tumor morphology, hemodynamics, stiffness, and perfusion. The nomogram showed excellent predictive performance (AUC 0.906 training; 0.926 validation) and can be conveniently applied in daily preoperative evaluation, offering a practical alternative to CT/MRI-based radiomics approaches. This study has several limitations. First, the follow-up duration was relatively short, which may mainly capture early rather than late recurrence events. Therefore, our nomogram primarily reflects early recurrence risk. Future investigations with longer follow-up and multicenter external validation are needed to assess the model’s long-term predictive accuracy, stability, and generalizability across diverse populations. Second, it was a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, only internal validation was performed; external validation with larger, multicenter cohorts is still needed to confirm the robustness and long-term predictive performance of the nomogram. Future studies with extended follow-up and broader populations are warranted to enhance its clinical applicability and reliability.

In summary, the nomogram prediction model for postoperative recurrence in elderly HCC patients, constructed based on multimodal ultrasound parameters, demonstrates favorable predictive performance and may aid in the early identification of high-risk patients and the development of individualized intervention strategies. However, the clinical applicability of the model in other hospitals requires further confirmation through multicenter studies in the future.

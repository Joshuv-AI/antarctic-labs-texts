Convergence Rates for Stochastic Proximal and Projection Estimators
Diego Morales, Pedro Pérez-Aros, Emilio Vilches

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish explicit convergence rates for the stochastic smooth approximations of infimal convolutions introduced and developed in [ 10 , 11 ] . In particular, we quantify the convergence of the associated barycentric estimators toward proximal mappings and metric projections. We prove a dimension-explicit δ \sqrt{\delta} bound, with explicit constants for the proximal mapping, in the ρ \rho -weakly convex (possibly nonsmooth) setting, and we also obtain a dimension-explicit δ \sqrt{\delta} rate for the metric projection onto an arbitrary convex set with nonempty interior. Under additional regularity, namely C 2 C^{2} smoothness with globally Lipschitz Hessian, we derive an improved linear O ⁡ ( δ ) O(\delta) rate with explicit constants, and we obtain refined projection estimates for convex sets with local C 2 , 1 C^{2,1} boundary. Examples demonstrate that these rates are optimal.

1.
Introduction

Proximal mappings and metric projections are fundamental concepts in variational analysis and optimization. Introduced by J.-J. Moreau in
[
9
]
, proximal mappings have since become central tools in modern optimization, notably through proximal algorithms and operator-splitting methods and their many variants (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
). Given a proper function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\lambda>0
, the proximal point
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)
is the unique minimizer
of
y\mapsto f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}
whenever
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex and
\lambda\in(0,1/\rho)
.
When
f=\iota_{C}
is the indicator of a closed convex set
C
, the proximal mapping reduces to the metric projection

\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)
. In many applications, however, evaluating
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)
or
\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)
exactly is computationally
expensive, and in black-box settings only function values (possibly noisy) are available.

This paper studies a family of
stochastic
(zeroth-order) estimators for proximal points and projections based on
Gaussian perturbations and exponential reweighting. For
\delta>0
and
\lambda>0
, we define the barycenter

(1)

m_{\delta}(x):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta\lambda I)}\!\big[Y\,\exp(-f(Y)/\delta)\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta\lambda I)}\!\big[\exp(-f(Y)/\delta)\big]}.

When
f=\iota_{C}
, the estimator becomes the conditional mean

p_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{E}\big[Y\mid Y\in C\big],\qquad Y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I),

which can be interpreted as a “smoothed projection” onto
C
.

The estimators (
1
) arise naturally from Laplace-type asymptotics for integrals with kernel

\exp(-g/\delta)
, where
g(y):=f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}
. Two recent strands of work developed this idea. First, motivated by Hamilton-Jacobi equations,
[
10
]
proposed the HJ-Prox method and
proved that, for
\rho
-weakly convex objectives under mild regularity, the barycenter converges to the proximal point as
\delta\to 0^{+}
.
Second,
[
11
]
places the relationship between infimal convolutions and self-normalized Laplace approximation
front and center, extending the construction beyond the quadratic kernel and establishing asymptotic validity under
weak local assumptions; in particular, their framework includes smoothed set projections

p_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Y\in C]
and proves
p_{\delta(x)}\to\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
under mild local geometric
conditions near the projection point.

While these references provide a compelling unifying viewpoint and broad asymptotic consistency results, they leave open
a basic quantitative question that is decisive for algorithmic use:
how fast
do these stochastic estimators converge,
and how do the constants depend on the ambient dimension and on the geometry/smoothness of the problem?

Our contributions: explicit and sharp convergence rates

The main goal of this work is to provide
non-asymptotic
,
dimension-explicit
convergence rates for
(
1
) and its projection specialization, and to identify regimes where improved rates hold.

(i) Nonsmooth weakly convex case: a sharp
\sqrt{\delta}
rate.

Assume
f
is proper, lower semicontinuous, and
\rho
-weakly convex, and fix
\lambda\in(0,1/\rho)
with

\mu:=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\rho>0
. Under the mild geometric condition that
\operatorname{dom}f
has nonempty interior,
we prove the global bound (valid for every
\delta>0
)

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n\delta}{\mu}}.

This estimate is complemented by explicit examples showing that the
\sqrt{\delta}
order cannot be improved in general.

(ii) Smooth case: an
O(\delta)
rate with explicit constants.

If
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\rho
-weakly convex and
\nabla^{2}f
is globally Lipschitz with constant
L
, we establish the
linear-rate refinement

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\frac{nL}{\mu^{2}}\,\delta,\qquad\mu=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\rho,

again for every
\delta>0
. This result quantifies a phenomenon suggested by the smoothing interpretations in

[
10
,
11
]
: additional curvature regularity improves the bias of the barycentric estimator.

(iii) Projection corollaries and boundary-sensitive refinements.

Taking
f=\iota_{C}
and
\lambda=1
yields a quantitative convergence estimate for convex projections:

\|p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{n\,\delta}.

Beyond the convex Lipschitz regime, we analyze the case of convex sets with
C^{2,1}
boundary charts at the projection point and obtain a refined
O(\delta)
expansion.
This provides a bridge between the purely asymptotic projection consistency statement in
[
11
]
and the boundary-geometry-dependent expansions needed in fine asymptotic analysis.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
collects the mathematical preliminaries used throughout.
Section
3
establishes the
\sqrt{\delta}
bound for nonsmooth weakly convex functions,
provides sharpness examples, and derives the corollary for convex projections.
Section
4
proves the
O(\delta)
rate under
C^{2}
regularity with Lipschitz continuous Hessian
and develops a refined analysis for convex sets with
C^{2,1}
boundaries. The paper ends with some concluding remarks and possible lines of future research.

2.
Mathematical Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work in the finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. The closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathbb{B}
. The open ball of radius
R
centered at
x
is denoted by
B_{R}(x)
. For simplicity, the open ball of radius
R
centered at
0
is denoted by
B_{R}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We use the convention
\exp(+\infty)=+\infty
and
\exp(-\infty)=0
.

For a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
indicator function
of
A
is defined by

\iota_{A}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{ if }x\in A,\\
+\infty&\text{ if }x\notin A.\end{array}\right.

The
distance function
from
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
A
is given by

d_{A}(x)=\inf\left\{\|x-y\|:y\in A\right\}.

Moreover, if
A
is nonempty, closed and convex, the
projection
of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
A
is denoted by
\operatorname{proj}_{A}(x)
. Given a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the
domain
of
g
is

\operatorname{dom}(g):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g(x)<+\infty\}.

We say that
g
is
proper
if
\operatorname{dom}g\neq\emptyset
. Given
\rho\geq 0
, a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called
\rho
-weakly convex if
g+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex. For
\mu>0
, we say that
g
is
\mu
-strongly convex if
g-\frac{\mu}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex.

The Moreau envelope of index
\lambda>0
of a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is denoted by
\operatorname{e}_{\lambda}g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and is defined by

\operatorname{e}_{\lambda}g(x):=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(g(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right)\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

If
g
is proper, lower semicontinuous, and
\rho
-weakly convex, and if
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{\rho}
(with the convention
1/0=+\infty
), then the above infimum is attained at a unique point
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda g}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}_{\lambda}g(x)

\displaystyle=g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda g}(x))+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda g}(x)\|^{2}.

In this case, the operator
x\mapsto\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda g}(x)
is everywhere defined and is called the
proximal operator
of
g
of index
\lambda
. Moreover,
\operatorname{e}_{\lambda}g
is continuously differentiable and, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\nabla\operatorname{e}_{\lambda}g(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda g}(x)).

We refer to
[
1
,
2
]
for more details.

Let
\delta>0
, and let
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper function such that
e^{-g/\delta}
is integrable and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy>0.

Under these assumptions,
e^{-g/\delta}
induces a probability measure. We denote by

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[w(y)]=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(y)e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}

the expectation of a given function
w
with respect to the probability measure
\sigma_{\delta}
, which is formally defined for every Lebesgue-measurable set
A
as

\sigma_{\delta}(A):=\frac{\int_{A}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}.

3.
Rates of Convergence for Nonsmooth Functions

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a
\rho
-weakly convex function with
\rho\geq 0
.
The goal of this section is to establish an explicit convergence rate for the map

m_{\delta}(x):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta\lambda I)}\!\bigl[y\,\exp(-f(y)/\delta)\bigr]}{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta\lambda I)}\!\bigl[\exp(-f(y)/\delta)\bigr]},

and to quantify its approximation of the proximal point:

m_{\delta}(x)\;\longrightarrow\;\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\qquad\text{as }\delta\downarrow 0.

It is straightforward to verify that if
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex for some
\rho\geq 0
and
\operatorname{dom}f
has nonempty interior, then
m_{\delta}
is well-defined for every
\delta>0
and
0<\lambda<1/\rho
.

The following result provides a convergence rate for this barycenter toward the proximal point.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper, lower semicontinuous, and
\rho
-weakly convex for some
\rho\geq 0
. Assume that
\operatorname{dom}f
has nonempty interior. Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<\lambda<1/\rho
, and set
\mu:=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\rho>0
.
Then, for every
\delta>0
,

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n\delta}{\mu}}.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
0<\lambda<1/\rho
. Consider

g(y):=f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Since
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex, it follows that
g
is
\mu
-strongly convex and

(2)

g(y)\geq g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\textrm{ for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Consider the vector field

F(y):=(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))e^{-g(y)/\delta}.

Now, let us show that
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\langle y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x),\nabla g(y)\rangle\right]\leq n\delta
.

Indeed, since
y\mapsto g(y)
is proper, lsc, and convex, its domain is convex; in particular,
D:=\operatorname{dom}f=\operatorname{dom}g
is convex with nonempty interior (by assumption). On
\operatorname{int}(D)
, every proper lsc convex function is locally Lipschitz, hence differentiable a.e. and belongs to
W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\operatorname{int}(D))
. Consequently,
\nabla g\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\operatorname{int}(D);\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and for a.e.
y\in\operatorname{int}(D)
,

\operatorname{div}F(y)=ne^{-g(y)/\delta}-\frac{1}{\delta}\langle y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x),\nabla g(y)\rangle e^{-g(y)/\delta}.

Fix
R>0
and set
\Omega_{R}:=\operatorname{int}(D)\cap B_{R}
. Since

\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq R+\|\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|=:C_{R}\textrm{ on }B_{R}

and
e^{-g(y)/\delta}\leq e^{-\inf_{B_{R}}g/\delta}=:M_{R,\delta}<\infty
, it follows that for a.e.
y\in\Omega_{R}
,

|\operatorname{div}F(y)|\leq nM_{R,\delta}+\frac{M_{R,\delta}C_{R}}{\delta}\|\nabla g(y)\|,

Since
\nabla g\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\operatorname{int}(D);\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
\nabla g\in L^{1}(\Omega_{R};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and then
\operatorname{div}F\in L^{1}(\Omega_{R})
. For
R>0
, apply the divergence theorem to
\Omega_{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}}\operatorname{div}F(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega_{R}}F\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Moreover, by Lemma
A.1
, we can select
R_{k}\to\infty
such that

\int_{\partial\Omega_{R_{k}}}F\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\underbrace{\int_{\partial D\cap{B}_{R_{k}}}F\cdot\nu_{D}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}_{=:I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\partial{B}_{R_{k}}\cap D}F\cdot\nu_{{B}_{R_{k}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}_{=:I_{2}}.

Now, on the one hand, since
D
is convex and
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\in D
, for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
y\in\partial D
one has

\langle y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x),\nu_{D}(y)\rangle\geq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad F(y)\cdot\nu_{D}(y)\geq 0.

Hence the
\partial D
-term
I_{1}
is nonnegative. On the other hand, using (
2
), the term
I_{2}
is bounded in absolute value by

CR_{k}^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{\mu}{2\delta}(R_{k}-\|\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|)^{2}\right)\to 0\textrm{ as }k\to\infty,

hence the
\partial{B}_{R_{k}}
-term
I_{2}
vanishes as
k\to\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{R_{k}}\cap\partial D}F\cdot\nu_{D}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Big(\int_{\partial D\cap{B}_{R_{k}}}F\cdot\nu_{D}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial{B}_{R_{k}}\cap D}F\cdot\nu_{{B}_{R_{k}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega_{R_{k}}}F\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{R_{k}}}\operatorname{div}F(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\operatorname{div}F(y)dy

\displaystyle=n\int_{D}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{D}\langle y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x),\nabla g(y)\rangle e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy.

Finally, dividing by
\int_{D}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy
and rearranging, we obtain the estimate.
Next, by strong convexity and almost-everywhere differentiability, we obtain

\langle\nabla g(y),y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\rangle\geq\mu\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}.

Taking expectation yields

\mu\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\langle y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x),\nabla g(y)\rangle\right]=n\delta,

hence
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\right]\leq n\delta/\mu
. Finally, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n\delta}{\mu}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
Y\sim\sigma_{\delta}
, where
\sigma_{\delta}
has density proportional to
\exp(-g/\delta)
.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
yields the
mean-square localization
estimate

\mathbb{E}\big[\|Y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\big]\leq\frac{n\delta}{\mu}.

This bound also yields a simple concentration inequality: for every
r>0
,

\sigma_{\delta}\!\big(\|Y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\geq r\big)\leq\frac{n\delta}{\mu\,r^{2}},

and therefore, for any
\eta\in(0,1)
,

\sigma_{\delta}\!\left(B_{\sqrt{n\delta/(\mu\eta)}}(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))\right)\geq 1-\eta.

The following two examples show that our convergence rates are sharp.

Example 3.1
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and consider

f(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max\{y_{i},0\}.

Then
f
is proper, lower semicontinuous, convex and
\operatorname{dom}f=\mathbb{R}^{n}
has nonempty interior. Fix
x=0
and any
\lambda>0
, and set
\mu=1/\lambda
. Then,
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(0)=0
and, by symmetry of
f
, for any
\delta>0

m_{\delta}(0)=(a_{\delta},\ldots,a_{\delta}),

where

a_{\delta}:=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}t\,e^{-\phi(t)/\delta}\,dt}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\phi(t)/\delta}\,dt}\qquad\phi(t):=\max\{t,0\}+\frac{1}{2\lambda}t^{2}.

Moreover, one can show that

a_{\delta}=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\lambda\delta}+O(\delta)\textrm{ as }\delta\to 0^{+}.

Consequently,

\|m_{\delta}(0)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(0)\|=\sqrt{n}|a_{\delta}|=\sqrt{\frac{2n}{\pi\mu}\delta}+O(\delta).

This shows that the order of convergence in Theorem
3.1
is sharp.

The following example shows that the bound obtained in Theorem
3.1
is optimal.

Example 3.2
.

Fix
n\geq 2
, take
\lambda>0
, set
\mu:=1/\lambda
, and let
x=0
. For
\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)
, define the circular cone

K_{\alpha}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\langle y,e_{1}\rangle\geq\|y\|\,\cos\alpha\},

where
e_{1}
denotes the first canonical basis vector.
Let
f=\iota_{K_{\alpha}}
be the indicator function of
K_{\alpha}
. Then
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(0)=0
. Moreover,

\|m_{\delta}(0)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(0)\|=\|m_{\delta}(0)\|=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\mu}}\mathbb{E}[\chi_{n}]\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,n},

where
\chi_{n}
denotes the
\chi
-distribution with
n
degrees of freedom and

\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,n}:=\mathbb{E}\left[U_{1}\mid U_{1}\geq\cos\alpha\right],

with
U
uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
U_{1}=\langle U,e_{1}\rangle
. In particular, since
U_{1}\in[\cos\alpha,1]
on the conditioning event, we have
\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,n}\in[\cos\alpha,1]
and hence
\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,n}\to 1
as
\alpha\searrow 0
. Furthermore,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\chi_{n}]}{\sqrt{n}}\to 1\textrm{ as }n\to\infty.

Consequently, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
n
and
\alpha
such that, for all
\delta>0
,

\|m_{\delta}(0)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(0)\|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\frac{n\delta}{\mu}}.

This shows that the rate of convergence in Theorem
3.1
is sharp.

We end this section by showing that
m_{\delta}(x)
coincides with
\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)
whenever
f
is quadratic.

Example 3.3
.

Let
A
be a symmetric and satisfy
A\succeq-\rho I
for some
\rho\geq 0
, and define

f(y)=\frac{1}{2}\langle Ay,y\rangle+\langle b,y\rangle+c.

Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, every
\delta>0
, and every
0<\lambda<1/\rho
(with the convention
1/0=+\infty
), one has

m_{\delta}(x)=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)=\left(A+\frac{1}{\lambda}I\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}x-b\right).

3.1.
Stochastic Approximation of Projections onto Convex Sets

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set with nonempty interior. We define

p_{\delta}(x):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[y\cdot\mathbf{1}_{C}(y)]}{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[\mathbf{1}_{C}(y)]}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[y\mid y\in C].

As a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain a quantified convergence result:

p_{\delta}(x)\to\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed convex set with nonempty interior.
Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for every
\delta>0
,

\|p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{n\,\delta}.

4.
Smooth results

In this section, we show that additional smoothness of the problem data yields improved convergence rates compared with those in Section
3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be
C^{2}
and
\rho
-weakly convex with
\rho\geq 0
. Assume that
\nabla^{2}f
is globally Lipschitz: there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\|\nabla^{2}f(u)-\nabla^{2}f(v)\|\leq L\|u-v\|\quad\textrm{ for all }u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<\lambda<1/\rho
, and set
\mu:=\frac{1}{\lambda}-\rho>0
.
Then, for every
\delta>0
,

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\frac{nL}{\mu^{2}}\,\delta.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
0<\lambda<1/\rho
. Let us consider

g(y):=f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Since
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex and
0<\lambda<1/\rho
, it follows that
g
is
\mu
-strongly convex and that (
2
) holds. In particular, (
2
) implies that
e^{-g/\delta}
is integrable for every
\delta>0
, so
m_{\delta}(x)
is well-defined.

Given a function
w(\cdot)
, recall that

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[w(y)]=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(y)e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy}.

Claim 1:

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\nabla g(y)]=0
.

Proof of Claim 1:
First, note that the Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian of
f
implies that
\nabla g
grows at most quadratically. More precisely, there exist constants
a,b>0
such that

\|\nabla g(y)\|\leq a\|y\|^{2}+b\qquad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

In particular,
\nabla g
is integrable with respect to the probability measure
\sigma_{\delta}
.

Second, we observe that

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\left(e^{-g(y)/\delta}\right)=-\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y_{i}}(y)e^{-g(y)/\delta}.

Moreover, from (
2
), it follows that

0\leq e^{-g(y)/\delta}\leq e^{-g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))/\delta}\cdot e^{-\frac{\mu}{2\delta}\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}}\textrm{ for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which implies that

0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\left(e^{-g(y)/\delta}\right)dy=-\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y_{i}}(y)e^{-g(y)/\delta}dy,

which implies the claim.

Claim 2:
Let
H=\nabla^{2}g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))
. Then, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\nabla g(y)=H(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))+r(y),

where
\|r(y)\|\leq\frac{L}{2}\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}
.

Proof of Claim 2:
Since
\nabla g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))=0
, the formula follows from Taylor’s theorem with

r(y)=\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla^{2}g(\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)+t(y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)))-H)dt.

Because
\nabla^{2}g=\nabla^{2}f+\frac{1}{\lambda}I
and
\nabla^{2}f
is
L
-Lipschitz, we have

\|\nabla^{2}g(u)-\nabla^{2}g(v)\|\leq L\|u-v\|.

Hence

\|r(y)\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}Lt\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}=\frac{L}{2}\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2},

which proves the claim.

Claim 3:

m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)=-H^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[r(y)]
.

Proof of Claim 3:
From Claim 1 and 2 we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\nabla g(y)]=H(m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))+\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[r(y)],

which implies the result.

Claim 4:
For every
\delta>0
, we have

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\frac{nL}{\mu^{2}}\delta.

Proof of Claim 4:
On the one hand, from Claims 2 and 3, we have

(3)

\displaystyle\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|

\displaystyle=\|H^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[r(y)]\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}\left[\|r(y)\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2\mu}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}],

where we used that
H\succcurlyeq\mu I
, hence
\|H^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1/\mu
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the induced
\ell_{2}
-operator norm. Moreover, using that
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[y]=m_{\delta}(x)
, we get that

(4)

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|y-m_{\delta}(x)\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}].

On the other hand, we observe that
g/\delta
is
\mu/\delta
strongly convex. Hence,
h_{\delta}=e^{-g/\delta}
is strongly log-concave. From Brascamp–Lieb inequality (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
), we get that

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma_{\delta}}(y)\preceq\frac{\delta}{\mu}I\hskip 9.24994pt\Rightarrow\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|y-m_{\delta}(x)\|^{2}]=\operatorname{tr}\left(\operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma_{\delta}}(y)\right)\leq\operatorname{tr}\left(\frac{\delta}{\mu}I\right)=\frac{n\delta}{\mu},

where
\operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma_{\delta}}
denotes the covariance matrix of a random vector under the probability measure
\sigma_{\delta}
. Moreover, Theorem
3.1
gives us the estimation

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}\leq\frac{n\delta}{\mu}.

Plugging (
5
) and the above inequality into (
4
) yields

\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{\delta}}[\|y-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}]\leq\frac{n\delta}{\mu}+\frac{n\delta}{\mu}=\frac{2n\delta}{\mu}.

Finally, from the above inequality and (
3
), we get

\|m_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\|\leq\frac{L}{2\mu}\cdot\frac{2n\delta}{\mu}=\frac{nL}{\mu^{2}}\delta,

which proves Claim 4 and the theorem.
∎

4.1.
Stochastic Approximation of Projections onto Smooth Sets

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty closed set with nonempty interior. Recall that

p_{\delta}(x):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[y\cdot\mathbf{1}_{C}(y)]}{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[\mathbf{1}_{C}(y)]}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}[y\mid y\in C].

Definition 4.2
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set and let
p\in\partial C
. We say that
\partial C

admits a
local
C^{2,1}
chart
at
p
with constants
(\rho,L,M)
(see Figure
1
) if, after a rigid motion
sending
p
to
0
and the outward unit normal at
p
to
e_{n}
, there exists
\rho>0
and a concave function

h\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho})
such that
h(0)=0
,
\nabla h(0)=0
,
\|\nabla^{2}h(z)\|\leq L
,

C\cap\big(\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}\times(-\rho,\rho)\big)=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:\ t\leq h(z)\},

and
\nabla^{2}h
is Lipschitz on
\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}
with constant
M\geq 0
, i.e.,

\|\nabla^{2}h(z)-\nabla^{2}h(z^{\prime})\|\leq M\|z-z^{\prime}\|\qquad\text{for all }z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}.

Figure 1
.
Local
C^{2,1}
chart of
\partial C
.

In the case of a smooth convex set, we obtain the following refinement of Corollary
3.3
, which yields a better convergence rate of order
\delta
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set with nonempty interior, and fix

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus C
. Assume that
\partial C
admits a local
C^{2,1}
chart at

\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)
with constants
(\rho,L,M)
. Then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)\|=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\delta).

Proof.

The proof can be found in Section
B
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

A careful review of the proof of Theorem
4.3
shows that, for this result, it suffices that the set
C
be locally a convex body in a neighborhood of the (necessarily unique) projection point.

The next example shows that the order of the bound obtained in Theorem
4.3
is sharp.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the half-space
C=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,\nu\rangle\leq 0\}
, where
\|\nu\|=1
. Then
\partial C=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,\nu\rangle=0\}
is a hyperplane. Pick
x\notin C
, so that
\langle x,\nu\rangle>0
. Then

\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)=x-\langle x,\nu\rangle\nu,\quad d_{C}(x)=\langle x,\nu\rangle.

Moreover, since
p_{\delta}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}(x,\delta I)}\!\left[\,y\mid y\in C\,\right]
, symmetry implies that the conditional mean in tangential directions is unchanged and the drift is purely normal. Hence,

p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)=\alpha_{\delta}\,\nu

for some scalar
\alpha_{\delta}<0
. In fact, one can show that

\alpha_{\delta}:=d_{C}(x)-\sqrt{\delta}\frac{\phi(d_{C}(x)/\sqrt{\delta})}{\Phi(-d_{C}(x)/\sqrt{\delta})},

where
\phi
and
\Phi
denote the standard normal density and distribution function, respectively.
Therefore

p_{\delta}(x)=\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)+\left(d_{C}(x)-\sqrt{\delta}\frac{\phi(d_{C}(x)/\sqrt{\delta})}{\Phi(-d_{C}(x)/\sqrt{\delta})}\right)\nu,

which implies that
\|p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)\|=O(\delta)
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. Hence, the order of the bound obtained in Theorem
4.3
is sharp.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed convex set with nonempty interior, and for

\Omega_{R}

R>0

(6)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big(\partial D\cap\partial B_{R}\big)=0.

R>0
such that (
6

where
\nu_{D}

D
, defined
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\partial D
,

\nu_{B_{R}}
is the outer unit normal to
B_{R}
. Here,
F
on
\partial\Omega_{R}
denotes the

\partial\Omega_{R}

\operatorname{int}(D)\cap\partial B_{R}

Proof.

The first assertion follows from

7

is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable. Hence, there exist countably many Lipschitz maps

see, e.g.,
[
5

. Fix

[
8

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

and
N_{\operatorname{tail}}=(N_{\operatorname{tail}}^{\tau},N_{\operatorname{tail}}^{\nu})
. Hence,

\displaystyle N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}ze^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\left(\int_{-\rho}^{h(z)}e^{-\frac{(t-d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt\right)\,dz

\displaystyle N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\underbrace{\left(\int_{-\rho}^{h(z)}t\,e^{-\frac{(t-d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt\right)}_{=:I_{\nu}(z)}\,dz

Moreover, we write
p_{\delta}(x)=(p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x),p_{\delta}^{\nu}(x))
, where

(11)

p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x)=\frac{N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}+N_{\operatorname{tail}}^{\tau}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}+D_{\operatorname{tail}}}\quad p_{\delta}^{\nu}(x)=\frac{N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}+N_{\operatorname{tail}}^{\nu}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}+D_{\operatorname{tail}}}.

Step 3: Bounding
N_{\operatorname{tail}}
.

Claim 5:
For all
0<\delta\leq\frac{d^{2}+\rho^{2}}{2n}
, one has

\|N_{\operatorname{tail}}\|\leq\frac{4\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\sqrt{\rho^{2}+d^{2}}(\rho^{2}+d^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\delta e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}+d^{2}}{2\delta}}.

Proof of Claim 5:
From (
10
), one has

\displaystyle\|N_{\operatorname{tail}}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{C_{\operatorname{tail}}}ye^{-\frac{\|y-x\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dy\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C_{\operatorname{tail}}}\|y\|e^{-\frac{\|y-x\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|u\|\geq\sqrt{\rho^{2}+d^{2}}\}}(\|u\|+d)e^{-\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{2\delta}}du,

which, by virtue of Lemma
A.2
, implies the claim.

Step 4: Bounding the normal component
N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}
of
N_{\operatorname{loc}}
.

Claim 6:
The following formula holds:

(12)

N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}=d\,D_{\operatorname{loc}}-\delta I_{\operatorname{num}}+\delta\,e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz,

where
I_{\operatorname{num}}:=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}+A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz
.

Proof of Claim 6:
Recall that
N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}I_{\nu}(z)\,dz
with

I_{\nu}(z)=\int_{-\rho}^{h(z)}t\,e^{-\frac{(t-d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt=\underbrace{\int_{-(\rho+d)}^{-A(z)}ue^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2\delta}}\,du}_{\operatorname{Term}_{1}(z)}+d\underbrace{\int_{-(\rho+d)}^{-A(z)}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2\delta}}\,du}_{\operatorname{Term}_{2}(z)}.

Moreover,
\operatorname{Term}_{1}(z)=-\delta\big(e^{-\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}-e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\big)
and
\operatorname{Term}_{2}(z)=J(z)
. Then,

I_{\nu}(z)=-\delta e^{-\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}+\delta e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}+dJ(z),

which implies that

\displaystyle N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\left[d\,J(z)-\delta e^{-\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}+\delta e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\right]\,dz

\displaystyle=d\,D_{\operatorname{loc}}-\delta I_{\operatorname{num}}+\delta\,e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz,

which proves the claim. ∎

We observe that the third term in the right-hand side of equality (
12
) is
\mathcal{O}(e^{-c/\delta})
,
D_{\operatorname{tail}}=\mathcal{O}(\delta e^{-c/\delta})
(see Claim 1), and
\|N_{\operatorname{tail}}\|=\mathcal{O}(\delta e^{-c/\delta})
(see Claim 5). Hence, informally speaking, by Claim 6, we get

p_{\delta}^{\nu}(x)=\frac{N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}+N_{\operatorname{tail}}^{\nu}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}+D_{\operatorname{tail}}}\approx\frac{N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\nu}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}\approx d-\delta\frac{I_{\operatorname{num}}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}.

Therefore, we consider the estimator

\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}:=d-\delta\frac{I_{\operatorname{num}}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}.

Let us consider the measure
\eta_{\delta}
defined on
\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}
whose density is proportional to

e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}+(d-h(z))^{2}}{2\delta}}.

Claim 7:
Assume that
0<\delta\leq\min\left\{\rho^{2}(1+dL),\frac{2\rho(1+dL)}{L},\frac{d\rho}{1+\frac{dL}{2(1+dL)}}\right\}
. Then there exists a function
r(A(z),\delta)
such that
0<r(A(z),\delta)<\frac{\delta}{A(z)^{2}}
and

\frac{I_{\operatorname{num}}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}=\frac{1}{\delta\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}\left[\frac{1-r(A(z),\delta)}{A(z)}\right]-\Delta(\delta)}\quad\textrm{ for all }0<\delta\leq\delta_{1},

where

0\leq\Delta(\delta)\leq\frac{(1+dL)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{err}}\cdot(\rho+d)}\rho^{n-1}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1})}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{2}}dv}\cdot\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}}e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}+2d\rho}{2\delta}}=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}}e^{-c/\delta}).

Proof of Claim 7:
Let us observe that

\displaystyle J(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{A(z)}^{d+\rho}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{A(z)}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt-\int_{d+\rho}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{A(z)}e^{-\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\big(1-\underbrace{A(z)e^{\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{A(z)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt}_{=:r(A(z),\delta)}\big)-\int_{d+\rho}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt.

Moreover, since
t\geq A(z)
on
[A(z),\infty)
, we have
1/t^{2}\leq 1/A(z)^{2}
. Using this bound and the
standard Gaussian tail estimate (obtained by integration by parts), for all
w>0

(13)

\int_{w}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt\leq\frac{\delta}{w}e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{2\delta}},

we obtain

\displaystyle 0<r(A(z),\delta)

\displaystyle=A(z)e^{\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{A(z)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq A(z)e^{\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\cdot\frac{1}{A(z)^{2}}\int_{A(z)}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq A(z)e^{\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\cdot\frac{1}{A(z)^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta}{A(z)}e^{-\frac{A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{A(z)^{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\operatorname{num}}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}+A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}J(z)\,dz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}\left[\frac{1-r(A(z),\delta)}{A(z)}\right]-\Delta(\delta)},

where

\Delta(\delta):=\frac{\int_{\rho+d}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2\delta}}\,du\cdot\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz}{\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}+A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz}.

On the one hand, by inequality (
13
), we observe that the numerator of
\Delta(\delta)
is bounded by

\frac{\delta}{\rho+d}e^{-\frac{(\rho+d)^{2}}{2\delta}}\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}).

On the other hand, the analysis for the denominator is similar to the analysis of
D_{\operatorname{loc}}
(see Claim 2). Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}_{\rho}}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}+A(z)^{2}}{2\delta}}\,dz\geq\mathcal{K}_{\operatorname{err}}\cdot e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}\left(\frac{\delta}{1+dL}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n-1}}e^{-\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{2}}dv.

Combining the above inequalities, we get the claim. ∎

Claim 8:
We have

\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}=\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[h(z)]-d\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}\!\left[r(A(z),\delta)\right]+O(\delta^{2})\textrm{ as }\delta\to 0^{+}.

Moreover,

|\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}|\leq\left(\frac{L(n-1)}{2}+\frac{1}{d}\right)\delta+O(\delta^{3/2})\textrm{ as }\delta\to 0^{+}.

Proof of Claim 8:
Let
g_{\delta}(z)=\frac{1-r(A(z),\delta)}{A(z)}
. Then, by virtue of Claim 7, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=d-\delta\frac{I_{\operatorname{num}}}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}=d-\frac{\delta}{\delta\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[g_{\delta}(z)]}+O(\delta^{\frac{1-n}{2}}e^{-c/\delta}).

Moreover, by Taylor’s theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[g_{\delta}(z)]}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{d}+\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}\left[g_{\delta}(z)-\frac{1}{d}\right]}=d-d^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}\left[g_{\delta}(z)-\frac{1}{d}\right]+O(\delta^{2}).

Therefore,

\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}=\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[h(z)]-d\,\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[r(A(z),\delta)]+O(\delta^{2}).

Finally, using that
|h(z)|\leq\frac{L}{2}\|z\|^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{\delta}}[\|z\|^{2}]\leq(n-1)\delta+O(\delta^{3/2})
, we obtain that

|\tilde{p}_{\delta}^{\nu}|\leq\left(\frac{L(n-1)}{2}+\frac{1}{d}\right)\delta+O(\delta^{3/2}),

which proves the claim.

Step 4: Bounding the tangential component
N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}
of
N_{\operatorname{loc}}
.

Claim 9:
For all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{1}

\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|\leq\frac{M(n^{2}-1)}{6}(2\pi\delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{2}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}.

Proof of Claim 9:
Recall that

N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}ze^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}J(z)dz.

Since
\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}
is symmetric and
e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}
is even,

N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}ze^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}(J(z)-J(-z))dz.

Hence

\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}\|z\|e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}|J(z)-J(-z)|dz.

Set
F(u):=\int_{-\rho}^{u}e^{-\frac{(t-d)^{2}}{2\delta}}dt
. Hence,
J(z)=F(h(z))
and
F^{\prime}(u)=e^{-\frac{(u-d)^{2}}{2\delta}}
.
By the mean value theorem, for each
z
there exists
\theta_{z}
between
h(z)
and
h(-z)
such that

J(z)-J(-z)=F^{\prime}(\theta_{z})(h(z)-h(-z)).

Moreover, since
h(z)\leq 0
and
h(-z)\leq 0
, we have
\theta_{z}\leq 0
, hence
(\theta_{z}-d)^{2}\geq d^{2}
and thus

|F^{\prime}(\theta_{z})|\leq e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}.

Therefore,

|J(z)-J(-z)|\leq e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}|h(z)-h(-z)|.

From (
8
), we get that

h(z)=\frac{1}{2}\langle Hz,z\rangle+r(z),\quad|r(z)|\leq\frac{M}{6}\|z\|^{3}.

Since the quadratic term is even,

h(z)-h(-z)=r(z)-r(-z),

and hence
|J(z)-J(-z)|\leq\frac{M}{3}\|z\|^{3}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}
. Using the above estimates, we get

\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|\leq\frac{M}{6}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{n-1}}\|z\|^{4}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}dz\leq\frac{M}{6}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|z\|^{4}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}dz

If
W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\delta I_{n-1})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\|z\|^{4}e^{-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2\delta}}dz=(2\pi\delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\|W\|^{4},\quad\mathbb{E}\|W\|^{4}=\delta^{2}(n^{2}-1),

which yields the claim. ∎

Claim 10:
Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
, we have

\|p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x)\|\leq\frac{M(n^{2}-1)d}{6}\delta+O(\delta^{2})\textrm{ as }\delta\to 0^{+}.

Proof of Claim 10:
By (
11
),

\|p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x)\|\leq\frac{\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}+D_{\operatorname{tail}}}+\frac{\|N_{\operatorname{tail}}\|}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}+D_{\operatorname{tail}}}\leq\frac{\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}+\frac{\|N_{\operatorname{tail}}\|}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}.

Using Claim 9 and Claim 2-4, for
0<\delta\leq\delta_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|N_{\operatorname{loc}}^{\tau}\|}{D_{\operatorname{loc}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{M(n^{2}-1)}{6}(2\pi\delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\delta^{2}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}}{\psi(\delta)\mathcal{K}_{\rm err}(\delta)\frac{C_{\rm vol}(n)}{(1+dL)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\delta^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{d^{2}}{2\delta}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M(n^{2}-1)}{6}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+dL)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\mathcal{K}_{\rm err}(\delta)C_{\rm vol}(n)}\cdot\frac{\delta^{2}}{\psi(\delta)}.

By Claim 4,
\psi(\delta)\geq\delta/(2d)
, hence

\frac{\|N_{\rm loc}^{\tau}\|}{D_{\rm loc}}\leq\frac{M(n^{2}-1)d}{3}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+dL)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\mathcal{K}_{\rm err}(\delta)C_{\rm vol}(n)}\cdot\delta.

Finally, Claim 5 and Claim 2–4 imply
\|N_{\rm tail}\|/D_{\rm loc}=O(e^{-c/\delta})
, which can be absorbed into

O(\delta^{2})
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. This completes the proof. ∎

Step 5: Conclusion.

Since
\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)=0
in the chosen coordinates, we have

\|p_{\delta}(x)-\operatorname{proj}_{C}(x)\|=\|p_{\delta}(x)\|\leq|p_{\delta}^{\nu}(x)|+\|p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x)\|.

By Claim 8 (together with Claims 1,5,6 to control the tail and the exponentially small terms), we obtain

|p_{\delta}^{\nu}(x)|\leq\left(\frac{L(n-1)}{2}+\frac{1}{d}\right)\delta+O(\delta^{3/2}).

By Claim 10,

\|p_{\delta}^{\tau}(x)\|\leq C_{\tau}\,\delta+O(\delta^{2}),\qquad C_{\tau}=\frac{M(n^{2}-1)d}{3}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(1+dL)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{C_{\rm vol}(n)}.

Combining the two estimates yields the theorem. ∎

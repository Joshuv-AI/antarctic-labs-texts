Exponential actions defined by vector configurations, Gale duality, and moment-angle manifolds Thanks: The work was funded within the framework of the HSE University Basic Research Program
Taras Panov

Abstract
. Exponential actions defined by vector configurations provide a universal framework for several constructions of holomorphic dynamics, non-Kähler complex geometry, toric geometry and topology. These include leaf spaces of holomorphic foliations, intersections of real and Hermitian quadrics, the quotient construction of simplicial toric varieties, LVM and LVMB manifolds, complex-analytic structures on moment-angle manifolds and their partial quotients, reviewed in this survey. In all cases, the geometry and topology of the appropriate quotient object can be described by combinatorial data including a pair of Gale dual vector configurations.

Introduction and notation

Let
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
k
-dimensional real vector space, and let

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a sequence (a
configuration
) of
m
vectors in the dual space
V^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(V,\mathbb{R})
. We allow repetitions among
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}

and assume that they span the whole of
V^{*}
.

The
exponential action
of
V
on the standard real space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle V\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle({\textbf{{v}}},{\textbf{{x}}})

\displaystyle\mapsto{\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}=\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{m}\bigr).

This is a very classical dynamical system taking its origin in the works of Poincaré.
At the same time, the action (
1.1
) and its holomorphic and algebraic versions arise in important modern constructions of algebraic geometry and topology, including

–

leaf spaces of holomorphic foliations, intersections of real and Hermitian quadrics (
topology and holomorphic dynamics
);

–

the quotient construction of toric varieties (
toric geometry
);

–

torus-invariant non-Kähler complex-analytic structures: LVM and LVMB manifolds (
complex geometry
);

–

smooth and complex-analytic structures on moment-angle manifolds and their partial quotients (
toric topology
).

There is a remarkable connection between the linear properties of the configuration
\Gamma
and the topology of the foliation in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by the orbits of the action (
1.1
), which is the subject of this survey. The connection is most effectively described by the
Gale dual
configuration of vectors
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in the space
W^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m-k}
.

From the geometric point of view, the spaces of
nondegenerate leaves
of the foliation, i. e., subsets of
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
to which the restriction of the action (
1.1
) is free, are of interest. The nonfree orbits form a set of coordinate planes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(a
coordinate subspace arrangement
), and its complement is an open subset
U(\mathcal{K})
defined by a simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}
on the set
[m]
. If the action of
V
on
U(\mathcal{K})
is not only free but also
proper
(which implies the closedness of the orbits), then the leaf space, or the quotient,
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is a smooth manifold. In the case of holomorphic or algebraic actions it is a complex-analytic manifold or algebraic variety, respectively.

The key topological fact is that the action of
V
on
U(\mathcal{K})
is free and proper if and only if the data
\{\mathcal{K};{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
define a
simplicial fan

\Sigma
in the space
W^{*}
. Using Gale duality, the fan criterion can be formulated directly in terms of the original configuration
\Gamma
defining the action. Moreover, the orbit space
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is compact if and only if the fan
\Sigma
is complete. In this case
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is identified with the
real moment-angle manifold

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}
, and for the holomorphic action on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
with the
moment-angle manifold

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{\mathcal{K}}
. Finally, if the configuration
\Gamma
generates a lattice (a discrete subgroup) in
V^{*}
, then the fan
\Sigma
is rational, and the action (
1.1
) extends to an algebraic torus action on
U(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
whose quotient space is the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
.

Also of interest is the class of exponential actions for which the corresponding fan
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a convex polytope. There is a criterion for a fan to be normal in terms of the Gale dual cones. (In the rational case, this gives a criterion for the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
to be projective.) When
\Sigma
is a normal fan, the orbit space
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is identified with the nondegenerate intersection of real quadrics and, in the case of a holomorphic action, with the intersection of Hermitian quadrics.

In Section
2
we describe free orbits of the action (
1.1
) or, equivalently, nondegenerate leaves of the foliation of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by
V
-orbits. The stabiliser of a point
{\textbf{{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is trivial if and only if the subconfiguration of
\Gamma
corresponding to the set of nonzero coordinates of
x
spans
V^{*}
. The set of nonfree orbits is therefore an arrangement of coordinate planes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
corresponding to the universal simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\{I\subset[m]\colon\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}\text{ spans }V^{*}\}

associated with
\Gamma
. Any subcomplex
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
defines an open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, namely,

U(\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\notin\mathcal{K}}\{{\textbf{{x}}}\colon x_{i_{1}}=\cdots=x_{i_{p}}=0\},

such that the restriction of the exponential action (
1.1
) to
U(\mathcal{K})
is free.

The topology of the space of free
V
-orbits can be quite bad unless we consider subsets
U(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that the restriction of the
V
-action (
1.1
) to
U(\mathcal{K})
is
proper
. In this case, the space of orbits
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is Hausdorff and has a smooth atlas of charts transverse to
V
-orbits. It is this quotient manifold
U(\mathcal{K})/V
that features in the constructions of algebraic geometry and topology mentioned above. The necessary and sufficient condition for the
V
-action on
U(\mathcal{K})
to be proper is the
fan condition
, which is most readily formulated in terms of the Gale dual configuration.

We set up the notation for linear Gale duality in Section

From now on, we work with a pair of Gale dual configurations
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
in
V^{*}
and
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in
W^{*}
. The ‘fan condition’ (Section

) is that the data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
define a (simplicial) fan
\Sigma
. A pair
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
satisfying the fan condition is known as a
triangulated vector configuration

[
18
]
, and this concept was brought into the context of holomorphic foliations and LVMB manifolds by Battaglia and Zaffran in
[
7
]
.

The fan condition means that the cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}=\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\colon i\in I\rangle
corresponding to
I\in\mathcal{K}
do not overlap pairwise, that is, the relative interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
does not intersect the relative interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
for different
I,J
in
\mathcal{K}
. In terms of the Gale dual configuration
\Gamma
, this is equivalent to the following: the Gale dual cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{J}}

do overlap
for
I,J
in
\mathcal{K}
(Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
4.4
). This criterion appeared in several works on combinatorial convexity, often implicitly, and it is in the heart of the duality between fans and
bunches
developed in
[
1
, §2.2]
.

In Section
5
we prove that the exponential action of
V
on
U(\mathcal{K})
is proper if and only if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a fan data (Theorem
5.1
). This property is of purely topological nature. On the other hand, it is the algebraic and holomorphic versions of this result that have become well known. The ‘fan condition’ features in the quotient construction of toric varieties, it guarantees that the quotient variety is separated, i. e. Hausdorff in the usual topology, see
[
16
, Theorem 5.1.11]
. The Gale dual to the fan condition appeared as the ‘imbrication condition’ in the work of Bosio
[
10
]
as part of the definition of a class of non-Kähler complex manifolds arising as the leaf spaces of holomorphic foliations, now known as LVMB manifolds. The fact that the ‘fan condition’ and the ‘imbrication condition’ are equivalent via Gale duality was used in several subsequent works relating complex geometry of LVMB manifolds to algebraic geometry of toric varieties
[
35
,
43
,
9
,
7
,
8
]
. In the topological setting, the relationship between the fan condition and the separatedness of the quotient featured in the works
[
27
]
and
[
26
]
.

In Section
6
we prove that the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
by a proper exponential action is compact if and only if the corresponding fan
\Sigma
is complete (Theorem
6.1
). This property is well studied in toric geometry, but again it is of purely topological nature. When formulated in terms of the Gale dual configuration, it is known as
substitute existence
in holomorphic dynamics and complex geometry of LVMB manifolds
[
10
,
9
]
.

In Section
7
the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
corresponding to a complete simplicial fan
\Sigma
is identified with the real moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
or the polyhedral product
(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}
. Thus, the extensive information available on the topology of moment-angle manifolds, including the known descriptions of their cohomology rings and homotopy type, can be brought to bear on the study of the topology of foliations by orbits of exponential actions.
Topology of moment-angle manifolds and polyhedral products is currently a very active area of research, there is an extensive literature on this subject, see
[
14
,
3
]
.

Normal fans of polytopes and their associated presentations of the quotients
U(\mathcal{K})/V
by intersections of quadrics are the subject of Section
8
. The criterion for a fan
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
to be the normal fan of a polytope in terms of the Gale dual configuration
\Gamma
is stated in Theorem
8.3
: instead of intersecting pairwise, the relative interiors of all Gale dual cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
,
I\in\mathcal{C}
, must have a common intersection. This criterion plays an important role in algebraic geometry of toric varieties
[
36
]
and holomorphic dynamics and complex geometry of LVM manifolds
[
33
,
34
]
.
There are two ways to relate (the normal fans of) polytopes to intersections of quadrics, which are Gale dual to each other.

The ‘polyhedra to quadrics’ way starts with an
n
-dimensional polytope (more generally, a polyhedron)
P
given by an intersection of halfspaces,

P=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\}

and produces an intersection of quadrics defined by the Gale dual data:

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\gamma_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}x^{2}_{m}=\delta\}.

The intersection of quadrics is nondegenerate (and therefore smooth) if and only if the polytope is
generic
(in particular, simple)
[
14
, Theorem 6.1.3]
. The nondegenerate intersection of quadrics is identified with the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
by the proper exponential action defined by the normal fan of
P
(Theorem
8.4
). The proof mimics the reconstruction of a Hamiltonian toric manifold from its moment polytope via symplectic reduction
[
19
,
2
,
23
]
; the intersection of quadrics (the moment-angle manifold), appears here as the nondegenerate level set for the moment map.

The ‘quadrics to polyhedra’ way starts with a configuration
\Gamma
and a choice of generic vector
\delta\in V^{*}
, satisfying what is known in holomorphic dynamics the ‘weak hypebolicity’ condition. It produces a nondegenerate intersection of quadrics, which is identified with the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
of the exponential action defined by the normal fan of the associated polyhedron
P
(Theorem
8.7
and Proposition
8.8
).

There is an important particular case when the vectors of the
\mathrm{A}
-configuration satisfy a linear relation with all positive coefficients. In this case,
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}
is the whole of
W^{*}
, while
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma
is a strongly convex (pointed) cone.
The Gale dual vector configuration
\Gamma
can be replaced by an affine point configuration
\Gamma^{\prime}
in an affine hyperplane of
V^{*}
(the
affine Gale transform
of
\mathrm{A}
). The ‘fan condition’ guaranteeing the properness of the exponential action can be restated in terms of the convex hulls of points
\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{\widehat{I}}
instead of cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
. This is precisely the setup used in the definition of LVM and LVMB manifolds, see Section
9
.

Holomorphic exponential actions are considered in Section
10
. Given a complex vector space
\widetilde{V}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
and a vector configuration
\Gamma
in
\widetilde{V}^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(\widetilde{V},\mathbb{C})
, the holomorphic action is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle({\textbf{{v}}},{\textbf{{z}}})

\displaystyle\mapsto{\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}}z_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}}z_{m}\bigr).

The criterions for the properness of the holomorphic action and the compactness of the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\widetilde{V}
are the same as in the real case (Theorems
10.3
and
10.5
). The quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\widetilde{V}
of the proper holomorphic exponential action defined by a complete simplicial fan data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is identified with the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{\mathcal{K}}
(Theorem
10.6
). The latter therefore acquires a complex-analytic structure. Complex geometry of moment-angle manifolds is remarkably rich and peculiar
[
38
,
39
,
28
]
and is far from being understood completely.

When the configuration
\Gamma
is
rational
, that is, its
\mathbb{Z}
-span
L^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\langle\Gamma\rangle
is a discrete subgroup (a lattice) in
V^{*}
, both the real and holomorphic exponential actions extend to an action of the algebraic torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong V\times T_{L}
on
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
, where
T_{L}=L\times_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
is the compact torus defined by
L
. If the algebraic action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
on
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
is proper, then the data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
define a fan
\Sigma
which is rational with respect to the Gale dual lattice
N=\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathrm{A}\rangle
in
W^{*}
. The quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
is the toric variety
V_{\Sigma}
defined by the rational fan
\Sigma
. We obtain a holomorphic principal bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to V_{\Sigma}
with fibre a holomorphic compact torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}/\widetilde{V}\cong T_{L}
, known as the
generalised Calabi–Eckmann fibration

[
32
,
33
,
35
]
. Perturbing the vector configuration
\Gamma
destroys its rationality, the closed holomorphic tori in the fibres of the bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to X_{\Sigma}
‘open up’, and the fibre bundle turns into a
holomorphic foliation

\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
with noncompact leaves. Holomorphic foliated manifolds
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}},\mathcal{F})
model
irrational deformations
of toric varieties
[
5
,
7
,
41
,
29
,
28
,
6
]
.

Thinking of
V
as the space of linear relations between the vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
, we observe that
\Gamma
is a rational configuration if and only if
V
is generated by relations with integer coefficients. More generally, a
subspace
Q\subset V
is
rational
(with respect to
\Gamma
) if it is generated by relations with integer coefficients (Construction
11.2
). A rational subspace
Q\subset V
contains a full-rank lattice

L=\{{\textbf{{q}}}\in Q\colon\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\;\text{ for }k=1,\ldots,m\}.

The exponential action of
V
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
extends to an action of the group
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V\cong T_{L}\times V
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, called the
torus-exponential action
(Section
12
). Once again, the torus-exponential action
(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V)\times U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\to U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
is proper if and only if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a fan data (Theorem
12.2
).
The quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/(T_{L}\times V)
by a proper torus-exponential action is identified with the quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
and is called a
partial quotient
of a moment-angle manifold. There is also a holomorphic version of torus-exponential action
(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{\widetilde{Q}}{\widetilde{V}})\times U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\to U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
, defined by a rational complex subspace
\widetilde{Q}\subset\widetilde{V}
. If
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a fan data, this defines a complex-analytic structure on the partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
as the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{\widetilde{Q}}{\widetilde{V}})
, with a holomorphic torus principal bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
.

Complex moment-angle manifolds
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
are universal examples of
complex manifolds with maximal torus action
. According to a classification result of Ishida
[
26
]
, any such manifold is biholomorphic to a holomorphic partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
. Two cases of holomorphic partial quotients
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
, corresponding to a one-dimensional rational subspace
Q\subset V
and the whole of
V
being rational, respectively, are of particular importance.

LVM manifolds are a class of non-Kähler complex-analytic manifolds introduced by Lopez de Medrano and Verjovsky
[
33
]
and Meersseman
[
34
]
as the leaf spaces of exponential holomorphic foliations on the projectivisations of open subsets
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
. This construction was further generalised by Bosio
[
10
]
and became known as LVMB manifolds. In the original construction of
[
10
]
the conditions ensuring that the leaf space is Hausdorff (or the action is proper) were formulated in terms of overlapping convex regions (see Section
13
). These conditions by Bosio are equivalent to the fan condition on the Gale dual data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
(Proposition
13.2
).

LVMB manifolds are therefore identified with projectivised moment-angle manifolds, that is, holomorphic partial quotients of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
by the diagonal circle (corresponding to a one-dimensional rational subspace
Q\subset V
). In these terms, LVM manifolds can be described as LVMB manifolds corresponding to the normal fans of polytopes, and are diffeomorphic to nondegenerate intersections of special Hermitian quadrics.

Toric manifolds or smooth complete toric varieties
X_{\Sigma}
are partial quotients of moment-angle manifolds
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
by a maximal freely acting subtorus. The corresponding holomorphic torus principal bundles
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to X_{\Sigma}
are described in Section
14
. Theorem
8.3
gives a criterion for the rational fan
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
of
X_{\Sigma}
to
be the normal fan of a polytope in terms of the Gale dual cones, which is equivalent to projectivity of
X_{\Sigma}
. This criterion can be extended to a description of the nef and ample cones of
X_{\Sigma}
(
[
1
, Proposition 2.4.2.6]
and Proposition
14.2
). We expect a similar criterion to hold in the irrational setting, thereby providing a description of nef and ample divisors on irrational deformations of toric varieties. We finish by describing the construction of Hamiltonian toric manifolds via symplectic reduction and their connection with projective nonsingular toric varieties (Theorem
14.5
). Likewise, this connection extends to the irrational setting.

2.
Free orbits (nondegenerate leaves)

We start with a description of free orbits of the exponential action (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.1
.

The stabiliser subgroup
V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}=\{{\textbf{{v}}}\in V\colon{\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}={\textbf{{x}}}\}

of a point
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
under the action (
1.1
) is given by

V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}=\{{\textbf{{v}}}\in V\colon\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=0\;\text{ whenever }x_{i}\neq 0\}.

In particular,
V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}=\bf 0
if and only if
the subset
\{\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\}\subset\Gamma
spans
V^{*}
.

Proof.

We have
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}
if and only if

(x_{1}e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle},\ldots,x_{m}e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),

which holds precisely when
\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=0
for
x_{i}\neq 0
.

For the second statement,
V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\neq 0
if and only if there is a nonzero
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}
such that
\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=0
for
x_{i}\neq 0
, which holds precisely when
\{\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\}\subset\Gamma
does not span
V^{*}
.
∎

Since
\Gamma
itself spans
V^{*}
, the stabiliser of a generic point (with all coordinates nonzero) is zero, so the action is free on a dense open subset
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

For a subset
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\subset[m]
, denote

\Gamma_{I}=\{\gamma_{i}\colon i\in I\}
.
Let
\widehat{I}=[m]\setminus I
denote the complementary subset. We set

\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)=\{I\subset[m]\colon\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}\text{ spans }V^{*}\}.

A
simplicial complex
on
[m]
is a collection
\mathcal{K}
of subsets of
[m]
such that
for any
I\in\mathcal{K}
all subsets of
I
also belong to
\mathcal{K}
. We refer to
I\in\mathcal{K}
as a
simplex
(or a
face
) of
\mathcal{K}
. We
always assume that the empty set
\varnothing
belongs to
\mathcal{K}
. We do not assume that
\mathcal{K}
contains all one-element subsets
\{i\}
. We refer to
\{i\}\in\mathcal{K}
as
vertices
, and refer to
\{i\}\notin\mathcal{K}
as
ghost vertices
. A simplicial complex is
pure
if all its maximal simplices have the same dimension. (The dimension of a simplex is its cardinality minus one.)

Proposition 2.2
.

\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
is a pure simplicial complex of dimension
m-k-1
.

Proof.

If
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
spans
V^{*}
, then so does
\Gamma_{\widehat{J}}\supset\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
for any
J\subset I
. Hence,
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
is a simplicial complex. Also, if
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
spans
V^{*}
, then it contains a basis of
V^{*}
. Such a basis has the form
\Gamma_{\widehat{L}}
for some
L
with
I\subset L
and
|L|=m-|\Gamma_{\widehat{L}}|=m-k
. It follows that
\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
is pure
(m-k-1)
-dimensional.
∎

Given a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
, define the following open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(the complement of an arrangement of coordinate subspaces):

(2.1)

U(\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\notin\mathcal{K}}\{{\textbf{{x}}}\colon x_{i_{1}}=\cdots=x_{i_{p}}=0\}.

It is easy to see that the complement to any set of coordinate planes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
has the form
U(\mathcal{K})
for some
\mathcal{K}
.
For example, if
\mathcal{K}=\{\varnothing\}
, then
U(\mathcal{K})=(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, and if
\mathcal{K}
consists of all proper subsets of
[m]
, then
U(\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{\bf 0\}
.

Let

(2.2)

U_{I}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon x_{j}\neq 0\text{ for }j\notin I\}.

Then
U(\mathcal{K})=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}U_{I}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
is a simplicial subcomplex, then the restriction of the action (
1.1
) to
U(\mathcal{K})
is free.

Proof.

Take
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in U(\mathcal{K})
. Then
I=\{i\colon x_{i}=0\}\in\mathcal{K}
. As
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
, we obtain that
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\{\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\}
spans
V^{*}
. Then
V_{{\textbf{{x}}}}=\bf 0
by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

We restate this by saying that
U(\mathcal{K})
consists of
nondegenerate leaves
of (
1.1
) for any
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
.

3.
Linear Gale duality

The configuration
\Gamma
defines a linear map
\varGamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to V^{*}
that takes the
i
th standard basis vector
{\textbf{{e}}}_{i}
to
\gamma_{i}
. Let
W=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\varGamma
, so we have an exact sequence

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varGamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}V^{*}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\Gamma
spans
V^{*}
, the space
W^{*}
has dimension
n:=m-k
. We identify the dual space of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\mathbb{R}^{m}
via the
isomorphism that takes the standard basis to its dual basis, and write vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by their coordinates in the standard basis. Consider the dual exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varGamma^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R}^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\longrightarrow}}W^{*}\longrightarrow 0,

where the map
\varGamma^{*}
takes
v
to
(\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle)
.
Define
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}=A({\textbf{{e}}}_{i})
. The
linear Gale transform
takes the vector configuration
\Gamma
in
V^{*}
to the vector configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in
W^{*}
. It is an involutive procedure, so
\mathrm{A}
is called the
Gale dual

of
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma
is the Gale dual of
\mathrm{A}
. Then
\mathrm{A}
spans
W^{*}
by duality. The linear function
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
takes a vector in
W
(viewed as a subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
) to its
i
th coefficient.

Choosing bases in
V
and
W
, we view
\varGamma
as a
k\times m
-matrix with column vectors
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
and view
A
as a
n\times m
-matrix with column vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
. The identity
A\varGamma^{*}=0
implies that the rows of
A
form a basis in the space of linear relations between the vectors
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
. The latter space is just
W
, and we may think of
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\in W^{*}
as the linear function that takes a linear relation to its
i
th coefficient.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
I\subset[m]
, the vectors in
\mathrm{A}_{I}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\colon i\in I\}
are linearly independent in
W^{*}
if and only if
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\{\gamma_{j}\colon j\notin I\}
spans
V^{*}
.

Proof.

By duality, we think of
\gamma_{j}
as the
j
th coefficient function on the space
V
of linear relations between
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
. If the vectors
\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
,
i\in I\}
are linearly independent, then any linear relation between
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
has the
j
th coefficient nonzero for some
j\in\widehat{I}
. It follows that, for any vector in
V
, there is a linear function among
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\{\gamma_{j}
,
j\in\widehat{I}\}
that does not vanish on this vector. This implies that
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
spans
V^{*}
. Conversely, if
there is a linear relation between
\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
,
i\in I\}
then for each
j\in\widehat{I}
the
j
th coefficient function
\gamma_{j}
vanishes on this relation. Hence, the vectors
\{\gamma_{j}
,
j\in\widehat{I}\}
do not span
V^{*}
.
∎

4.
Fans and triangulated configurations

A subset
\sigma
of a real vector space
W^{*}
is called a
polyhedral cone
, or simply a
cone
, if it consists of all linear combinations with nonnegative coefficients of a finite set of vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p}
in
W^{*}
. The notation is
\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}({\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p})
. The set of vectors may be empty, in which case the cone consists of the zero vector only, that is,

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}(\varnothing)=\mathbf{0}
. The dimension of
\sigma
is the dimension of its linear span.

A
generator set
of a cone
\sigma
is a set of vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p}
such that
\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}({\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p})
. A cone is
strongly convex
or
pointed
if it does not contain a line. An inclusion-minimal generator set of a strongly convex cone is unique up to multiplication by positive numbers. A cone is
simplicial
if its minimal set of generators is linearly independent.

A
supporting hyperplane
of a cone
\sigma\subset W^{*}
is a hyperplane
H
such that
\sigma
is contained in one of the two closed halfspaces defined by
H
. A
face
of a cone
\sigma
is the intersection of
\sigma
with a supporting hyperplane. We also regard
\sigma
as a face of itself; faces which are different from
\sigma
are called
proper
. The complement of the union of all proper faces in
\sigma
is called the
relative interior
of
\sigma
and is denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\sigma
; it coincides with the interior of
\sigma
in its linear span.
Note that
\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbf{0}
.

A nonzero vector
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
in the dual space defines a hyperplane

H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}\in W^{*}\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle=0\}.

We denote by
H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}^{+}
and
H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}^{-}
its corresponding nonnegative and nonpositive closed halfspaces, respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}({\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p})
is a cone in
W^{*}
, then
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\sigma
if and only if
{\textbf{{a}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
for some positive
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The case
{\textbf{{a}}}=\mathbf{0}
is trivial, so we may assume
{\textbf{{a}}}\neq\mathbf{0}
.

Let
{\textbf{{a}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
with
\lambda_{i}>0
. Suppose that
{\textbf{{a}}}\notin\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\sigma
. Then
a
is contained in a proper face
\tau
of
\sigma
, i. e. there is a supporting hyperplane
H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}
with
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
such that

\sigma\subset H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}^{+}
and
{\textbf{{v}}}\in H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}\cap\sigma=\tau
. Hence,
\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle=0
. On the other hand,
\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle>0
, because each
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle
is nonnegative and
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle>0
for at least one
i
, as
\tau
is a proper face. A contradiction.

Now let
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\sigma
. It is easy to see that for any
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime}\in\sigma
there exists
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime\prime}\in\sigma
such that
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime}+{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime\prime}=\mu{\textbf{{a}}}
for some
\mu>0
. Taking
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime}={\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
we obtain

{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}+{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\mu_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}
for
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\sigma
and
\mu_{i}>0
. Writing each
{\textbf{{a}}}^{\prime\prime}_{i}
as a nonnegative linear combination of
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{p}
and summing up over
i
we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{p}\nu_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mu_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}
with positive
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{p}
. The result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Separation Lemma)
.

The following conditions are equivalent for two different strongly convex cones
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
in
V
:

(a)

the intersection of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
is a proper face of each cone;

(b)

the cones
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are separated by a hyperplane, i. e. there exists a hyperplane
H
such that

\sigma_{1}\subset H^{+},\quad\sigma_{2}\subset H^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad H\cap\sigma_{1}=H\cap\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}.

For the proof, see
[
21
, §1.2]
or
[
16
, Lemma 1.2.13]
.

A
fan
is a finite set
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s}\}
of strongly convex cones in
W^{*}
such that every face of a cone in
\Sigma
belongs to
\Sigma
and the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of each. The
support

|\Sigma|
of a fan is the union of its cones:
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma\subset W^{*}
. A fan is
complete
if
|\Sigma|=W^{*}
and has
convex support
if
|\Sigma|
is convex.

A fan
\Sigma
in
W^{*}
can be defined by specifying generators of its one-dimensional cones and the subsets of generators that span cones. This justifies the following formalism. Let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
be a spanning vector configuration in
W^{*}
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a collection of subsets
of
[m]
containing
\varnothing
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}
-closed
if

•

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
is strongly convex for any
I\in\mathcal{C}
,

•

if
I\in\mathcal{C}
,
J\subset I
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
is a face of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
, then
J\in\mathcal{C}
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{A}
-closed, then all faces of a cone in the set

\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
also belong to this set. The set
\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a fan whenever any two cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
intersect in a common face (which has to be
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}
). In this case we say that
\{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{A}\}
is a
fan data
.

The “fan condition” can be stated in terms of the relative interiors of cones in either of the two Gale dual configurations
\mathrm{A}
and
\Gamma
; the next result also follows from the general correspondence between fans and
bunches
in
[
1
, Theorem 2.2.1.14]
:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
and
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a pair of Gale dual vector configurations, and let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{A}
-closed collection of subsets of
[m]
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a fan;

(b)

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J})=\varnothing
for any
I,J\in\mathcal{C}
,
I\neq J
;

(c)

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{J}})\neq\varnothing
for any
I,J\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is clear.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a). Take any
I,J\in\mathcal{C}
. Set
X=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
. Consider the smallest face of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
that contains
X
; this face is
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I^{\prime}}
for some
I^{\prime}\subset I
,
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. Similarly, let
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J^{\prime}}
be the smallest face of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
containing
X
. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}X\subset(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I^{\prime}})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J^{\prime}})
. By (b), the latter intersection is empty unless
I^{\prime}=J^{\prime}
. Therefore,
I^{\prime}=J^{\prime}
. Then
I^{\prime}\subset I\cap J
, so
X\subset\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I^{\prime}}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}
. On the other hand, we obviously have
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}\subset X
. Hence, the intersection
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}A_{I^{\prime}}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}
is a face of both
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a).
Suppose
(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{J}})\neq\varnothing
. By Proposition
4.1
,

(4.1)

\sum_{k\in\widehat{I}}r_{k}\gamma_{k}-\sum_{l\in\widehat{J}}s_{l}\gamma_{l}=\mathbf{0}

for some positive
r_{k}
and
s_{l}
. This is a linear relation between
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
, so it defines a vector
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
. Recall that the value of
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
on a linear relation is its
i
th coefficient, so we have

(4.2)

\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle=\begin{cases}r_{i}-s_{i},&i\in\widehat{I}\cap\widehat{J}=\widehat{I\cup J},\\
r_{i},&i\in\widehat{I}\setminus\widehat{J}=J\setminus(I\cap J),\\
-s_{i},&i\in\widehat{J}\setminus\widehat{I}=I\setminus(I\cap J),\\
0,&i\in\widehat{\widehat{I}\cup\widehat{J}}=I\cap J.\end{cases}

It follows that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}\subset H^{+}_{{\textbf{{w}}}},\quad\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\subset H^{-}_{{\textbf{{w}}}},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}=H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}=H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}.

Hence,
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
are separated by a hyperplane for each
I,J\in\mathcal{C}
, so (a) holds by Lemma
4.2
.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(c).
Suppose that the intersection of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}
is a face of each cone. By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a hyperplane
H_{{\textbf{{w}}}}
satisfying (
4.3
). The vector
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
satisfies (
4.2
) for some positive
r_{i},s_{i}
. This
w
gives a linear relation (
4.1
) between
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
, which implies

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{J}})\neq\varnothing
.
∎

A fan is
simplicial
if each of its cones is generated by a linearly independent set of vectors. The fan
\Sigma
defined by a fan data
\{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{A}\}
is simplicial if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
. That is, a simplicial fan can be written as
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{K}\}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial complex on
[m]
and
\mathrm{A}_{I}
is linearly independent for any
I\in\mathcal{K}
. The simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is called the
underlying complex
of a simplicial fan
\Sigma
.

A pair
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
consisting of a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
and a spanning vector configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in
W^{*}
is called a
simplicial fan data
or a

triangulated configuration

[
7
]
if
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{K}\}
is a fan in

. This fan is automatically simplicial, since

is linear independent for any

A simplicial complex

5.
Proper actions

A continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
of topological spaces is
proper
if
f^{-1}(C)
is compact for any compact
C\subset Y
. If
X
is Hausdorff and
Y
is locally compact Hausdorff, then
f\colon X\to Y
is proper if and only if it is
universally closed
, where the latter means that for any map
Z\to Y
the pullback
X\times_{Y}Z\to Z
is a closed map.

A continuous action
G\times X\to X
,
(g,x)\mapsto g\,x
, of a topological group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is called
proper
if the
shear map

h\colon G\times X\to X\times X,\quad(g,x)\mapsto(g\,x,x)

is proper.
Equivalently, a
G
-action on a locally compact Hausdorff
X
is proper if the subset

\{g\in G\colon gC\cap C\neq\varnothing\}
is compact for any compact
C\subset X
.

Properness is a key property for noncompact group actions ensuring that the quotient is a well-behaved space. Namely, we have

•

for a proper action of
G
on
X
, the orbits are closed, the isotropy subgroups are compact, and the quotient
X/G
is Hausdorff;

•

for a smooth, free and proper action of a Lie group
G
on a smooth manifold
M
, the quotient
M/G
has a unique smooth structure with the property that the quotient map
M\to M/G
is a smooth submersion.

We refer to
[
31
, Chapter 21]
for the details.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
and
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
be a pair of Gale dual vector configurations in
V^{*}
and
W^{*}
, respectively, and let
\mathcal{K}
be a simplicial complex on
[m]
. Then

(1)

the action
V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})
obtained by restricting (
1.1
) to
U(\mathcal{K})
is free if and only
\mathrm{A}_{I}
is linearly independent for any
I\in\mathcal{K}
;

(2)

the action
V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})
is proper if and only if

\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration, in which case the action is also free.

Proof.

Statement (1) is Proposition
2.3
.

To prove (2), first assume that
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration. We need to check that the preimage
h^{-1}(C)
of a compact subset
C\subset U(\mathcal{K})\times U(\mathcal{K})
under the shear map
h\colon V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})\times U(\mathcal{K})
is compact. Since
V
and
U(\mathcal{K})
are metric spaces, it suffices to check that any infinite sequence
\{({\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)},{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)})\colon k=1,2,\ldots\}
of points in
h^{-1}(C)
contains a convergent subsequence. Since
C
is compact, by passing to a subsequence we may assume that the sequence

\{h({\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)},{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)})\}=\{({\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)},{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)})\}

has a limit in
U(\mathcal{K})\times U(\mathcal{K})
. We set
{\textbf{{y}}}^{(k)}={\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}

and let

{\textbf{{x}}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)},\quad{\textbf{{y}}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\textbf{{y}}}^{(k)},

where
{\textbf{{x}}},{\textbf{{y}}}\in U(\mathcal{K})
. We need to show that a subsequence of
\{{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\}
has a limit in
V
.

By passing to a subsequence we may assume
that each numerical sequence
\{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle\}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, has a
limit in
\mathbb{R}\cup{\pm\infty}
. Let

I_{+}=\bigl\{i\colon\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle=+\infty\bigr\},\quad I_{-}=\bigl\{i\colon\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle=-\infty\bigr\}.

As both sequences
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}\}
,
\{{\textbf{{y}}}^{(k)}={\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}\}
converge, formula (
1.1
) implies
x_{i}=0
for
i\in I_{+}
and
y_{i}=0
for
i\in I_{-}
. Then it follows from the definition of

(
2.1
) that
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
are disjoint simplices of
\mathcal{K}
. Now condition (c) of Theorem
4.4
gives

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{+}})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{-}})\neq\varnothing.

Hence,

0=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{-}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}-\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{+}}s_{i}\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i\in I_{+}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}-\sum_{i\in I_{-}}s_{i}\gamma_{i}+\sum_{i\notin I_{+}\sqcup I_{-}}(r_{i}-s_{i})\gamma_{i}

for some positive
r_{i}
and
s_{i}
. This implies that both
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
are empty, as otherwise

0=\lim_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in I_{+}}r_{i}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle-\sum_{i\in I_{-}}s_{i}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle+\sum_{i\notin I_{+}\sqcup I_{-}}(r_{i}-s_{i})\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle\Bigr)=+\infty,

giving a contradiction. Therefore, each sequence
\{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\rangle\}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, has a finite limit. This implies that
\{{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\}
converges in
V
, because
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
span
V^{*}
. Thus, the action
V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})
is proper.

Now assume that the data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
do not define a fan.
Then condition (b) of Theorem
4.4
fails, and we have

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J})\neq\varnothing

for some
I,J\in\mathcal{K}
,
I\neq J
.
It follows that

0=\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}-\sum_{i\in J}s_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}=\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}r_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}-\sum_{i\in J\setminus I}s_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}+\sum_{i\in I\cap J}(r_{i}-s_{i}){\textbf{{a}}}_{i}

for some positive

and

. By Gale duality, this linear relation between
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
gives a vector
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V
satisfying

\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=\begin{cases}r_{i}-s_{i},&i\in I\cap J,\\
r_{i},&i\in I\setminus J,\\
-s_{i},&i\in J\setminus I,\\
0,&i\notin I\cup J.\end{cases}

Now consider the sequence of points
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with coordinates

x^{(k)}_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in I\cap J,\\
e^{-k\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle},&i\in I\setminus J,\\
1,&i\notin I\end{cases}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in I\cap J,\\
e^{-kr_{i}},&i\in I\setminus J,\\
1,&i\notin I.\end{cases}

Observe that
\lim_{k\to\infty}{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}={\textbf{{x}}}
, where

x_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in I,\\
1,&i\notin I,\end{cases}

and both
{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}
and

lie in
U(\mathcal{K})
. Now define
{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}=k{\textbf{{v}}}
and
{\textbf{{y}}}^{(k)}={\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}^{(k)}
, so that

y^{(k)}_{i}=e^{k\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x^{(k)}_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in I\cap J,\\
1,&i\in I\setminus J,\\
e^{k\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle},&i\notin I\end{cases}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in I\cap J,\\
e^{-ks_{i}},&i\in J\setminus I,\\
1,&i\notin J.\end{cases}

We have

, where

y_{i}=\begin{cases}0,&i\in J,\\
1,&i\notin J,\end{cases}

so both
{\textbf{{y}}}^{(k)}
and

lie in

. On the other hand, no subsequence of
{\textbf{{v}}}^{(k)}
converges in

. Thus, the action

is not proper.

Finally, if

is a triangulated configuration, then

is linearly independent for any

∎

6.
Completeness and compactness

Assume given data
\{\mathcal{K},{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
defining a simplicial fan

\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s}\}
in
W^{*}
, as in Section
4
. A fan
\Sigma
in
W^{*}
is complete if the union of its cones
\sigma_{i}
is the whole of
W^{*}
. The underlying simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
of a complete simplicial fan defines a simplicial subdivision (a
triangulation
) of the unit sphere in
W^{*}
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be the Gale dual configuration in
V^{*}
. The restriction of the exponential action (
1.1
) to
U(\mathcal{K})
is free and proper by Theorem
5.1
.
We have the following criterion of compactness for the quotient of the action
V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration defining a simplicial fan
\Sigma
.
The quotient smooth manifold
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is compact if and only if the fan
\Sigma
is complete.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sigma
is a complete fan.
Because
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is metrisable, it is enough to prove that any sequence of points in
U(\mathcal{K})/V
contains a convergent subsequence.

Take a sequence of points in
U(\mathcal{K})/V
and let
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)},n=1,2,\ldots\}
be its lift to the total space,
{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)}=(x_{1}^{(n)},\dots,x_{m}^{(n)})\in U(\mathcal{K})
. We shall change the sequence
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)}\}
to
\{\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}\}
so that
{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)}
and
\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}
lie in the same
V
-orbit for each
n=1,2,\ldots
and
\{\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}\}
has a convergent subsequence in
U(\mathcal{K})
. Its projection will be the required subsequence in
U(\mathcal{K})/V
.

By passing to a subsequence we may assume that all elements of the sequence
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)}\}
have the same sets of zero coordinates. That is, there is
J\in\mathcal{K}
such that
x^{(n)}_{j}=0
for
j\in J
and
x^{(n)}_{j}\neq 0
for
j\notin J
, for all
n
.

We argue by induction on
\dim W^{*}=m-k
. Let
\dim W^{*}=1
. There is only one complete one-dimensional fan (corresponding to
\mathcal{K}=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\}\}
), so the theorem holds in this case (see Example
5.2
).

If
J\neq\varnothing
, then
({\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)})_{j}=0
for any
j\in J
,
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V
and
n=1,2,\ldots
Consider the link subcomplex
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{link}_{\mathcal{K}}J=\{I\in\mathcal{K}\colon I\cup J\in\mathcal{K},\,I\cap J=\varnothing\}
. Then the data

\{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{link}_{\mathcal{K}}J,{\textbf{{a}}}_{j}\colon j\notin J\}
define a complete fan in the quotient space
W^{*}/\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{j}\colon j\in J\rangle
, which has dimension less than
W^{*}
. Therefore, we can apply the induction assumption in this case.

We therefore may assume
J=\varnothing
, that is,
x_{j}^{(n)}\neq 0
for any
n
and
j\in[m]
.

We denote
\mathbb{R}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Given
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}
, define

\ell({\textbf{{x}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|x_{i}|\in W^{*}.

For any
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V
we have

(6.1)

\ell({\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}|x_{i}|\bigr)\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|x_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|x_{i}|=\ell({\textbf{{x}}}),

where the second-to-last equality follows by Gale duality.

Now we get back to the sequence
\{{\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)}\}
and consider
-\ell({\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)})\in W^{*}
. As
\Sigma
is a complete fan in
W^{*}
, we can find
I\in\mathcal{K}
with
|I|=\dim W^{*}
such that infinitely many terms of the sequence
\{-\ell({\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)})\}
lie in
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
. Hence, by passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\{-\ell({\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)})\}\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
.

By Proposition
3.1
, the vector configuration
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, so we can find
{\textbf{{v}}}^{(n)}\in V
satisfying

\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(n)}\rangle=-\log|x_{i}^{(n)}|,\quad i\notin I.

and set

\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}={\textbf{{v}}}^{(n)}\cdot{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}.

We have

|\widetilde{x}_{i}^{(n)}|=|e^{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}^{(n)}\rangle}x_{i}^{(n)}|=1,\quad i\notin I.

This together with (
6.1
) implies

-\sum\limits_{i\in I}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|\widetilde{x}_{i}^{(n)}|=-\ell(\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)})=-\ell({\textbf{{x}}}^{(n)})\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}.

It follows that
\log|\widetilde{x}_{i}^{(n)}|\leqslant 0
for any
i\in[m]
and
n=1,2,\ldots
. Therefore,

\{\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}^{(n)}\}
is a bounded sequence, so its subsequence has a limit it
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. As
|\widetilde{x}_{i}^{(n)}|=1
for
i\notin I
, the limit may have zero coordinates

only for

, so the limit belongs to
U(\mathcal{K})
, as needed.

Now assume that
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is compact. Take
{\textbf{{a}}}\in W^{*}
. To prove that
\Sigma
is complete, we need to show that

belongs to a cone of
\Sigma
. Write

(6.2)

{\textbf{{a}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},\quad\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

For any
x>0
consider

(x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}})\in(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}\subset U(\mathcal{K})

and let
[x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}}]
denote the image in
U(\mathcal{K})/V
. Setting
x=x^{(n)}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, where
x^{(n)}>0
converges to zero as

and using the fact that
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is compact, we observe that there exists a limit

\lim_{x\to 0}[x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}}]=[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]\in U(\mathcal{K})/V.

Let

I=\{i\in[m]\colon y_{i}=0\}\in\mathcal{K}.

This is well-defined as the set of zero coordinates of a point in
U(\mathcal{K})
is constant along its
V
-orbit. Furthermore,
{\textbf{{y}}}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in U_{I}
, where
U_{I}\in U(\mathcal{K})
is given by (
2.2
).

For any
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
and
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}
, define

f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|x_{i}|^{\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle}.

Then
f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}
defines a function
(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}/V\to\mathbb{R}
, because
f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}({\textbf{{x}}})=e^{\langle\ell({\textbf{{x}}}),{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle}
for
{\textbf{{x}}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}
and
\ell({\textbf{{x}}})
is constant on
V
-orbits (see above). Consider the dual cone

(\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})^{\mathsf{v}}=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle\geqslant 0\text{ for }i\in I\}.

If
{\textbf{{w}}}\in(\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})^{\mathsf{v}}
, then
f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}
extends to a continuous function on
U_{I}/V
.

Now
\lim_{x\to 0}[x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}}]=[{\textbf{{y}}}]
in

, implying that

\lim_{x\to 0}f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}(x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}})=f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}({\textbf{{y}}})

for
{\textbf{{w}}}\in(\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})^{\mathsf{v}}
. On the other hand,

\lim_{x\to 0}f_{{\textbf{{w}}}}(x^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,x^{\alpha_{m}})=\lim_{x\to 0}x^{\langle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle}=\lim_{x\to 0}x^{\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle}.

The latter limit exists if and only if
\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle\geqslant 0
. Hence,
\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle\geqslant 0
for any
{\textbf{{w}}}\in(\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})^{\mathsf{v}}
. This implies that
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
, and therefore the fan
\Sigma
is complete.
∎

The condition of completeness (or compactness of the quotient) can be formulated in terms of the triangulated configuration
(\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A})
as follows.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume that
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration defining a simplicial fan
\Sigma
in an
n
-dimensional space
W^{*}
. Then, the
\Sigma
is complete if and only if one of the following two equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(a)

\mathcal{K}
contains an
n
-element subset (simplex), and each
(n-1)
-element simplex of
\mathcal{K}
is contained in two
n
-element simplices;

(b)

the collection of subsets
\mathcal{E}=\{J\subset[m]\colon|J|=m-n,\widehat{J}\in\mathcal{K}\}
is nonempty, and for any
J\in\mathcal{E}
and
i\in[m]
there exists
j\in J
such that
(J\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (a) and (b) is clear.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete simplicial fan. Then
\Sigma
contains an
n
-dimensional cone, so there is

with
|I|=n
. Suppose there is an
(n-1)
-element subset of
\mathcal{K}
that is contained in at most one
n
-element subset. That is, there is an
(n-1)
-dimensional cone
\tau
of
\Sigma
that is a face of at most one
n
-dimensional cone of
\Sigma
. Then, there is a hyperplane

containing
\tau
such that one of the halfspaces defined by

contains points that do not lie in any cone of
\Sigma
. A contradiction.

Now let condition (a) be satisfied. Suppose

is not complete. Then

is a closed proper subset of

. Its topological boundary

contains an

(n-1)
-dimensional cone of
\Sigma
, say

. Then
\tau
is a face of at most one
n
-dimensional cone of
\Sigma
. A contradiction.
∎

Condition (b) in Proposition
6.2
is the
substitute existence (SE)
condition of Bosio
[
10
]
. It features in the construction of LVMB manifolds, see Section
13
.

7.
Real moment-angle manifolds

When the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is compact, its topology can be described via the polyhedral product decomposition.

Construction 7.1

(polyhedral product)
.

Assume given a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
and a sequence

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})=\{(X_{1},A_{1}),\ldots,(X_{m},A_{m})\}

of
m
pairs of pointed topological spaces. For each
subset
I\subset[m]
we set

(7.1)

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{I}=\bigl\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\prod_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}\colon\;x_{k}\in A_{k}\quad\text{for }k\notin I\bigl\}

and define the
polyhedral product
of
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})

corresponding to
\mathcal{K}
as

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{I}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}\Bigl(\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}\times\prod_{i\notin I}A_{i}\Bigl)\subset\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}.

In the case when all pairs
(X_{i},A_{i})
are the same, i. e.

X_{i}=X
and
A_{i}=A
for
i=1,\ldots,m
, we use the notation

(X,A)^{\mathcal{K}}
for
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}
.

We have
({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
when

\mathcal{K}=\{\varnothing\}
, and

({\textbf{{X}}},{\textbf{{A}}})^{\mathcal{K}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
when
\mathcal{K}
is the simplex

\varDelta[m]
on
m
vertices. Here are more examples.

Example 7.2
.

1.
It is easy to see that
U(\mathcal{K})=(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{\mathcal{K}}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, see (
2.1
).

2.
Let
(X,A)=(D^{1},S^{0})
, where
D^{1}
is a closed
interval
[-1,1]
and
S^{0}
is
its boundary, consisting of two points. The polyhedral product

(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}
is known as the
real moment-angle
complex

[
13
, §3.5]
,
[
14
]
:

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}(D^{1},S^{0})^{I}.

It is a cubic subcomplex in the
m
-cube
(D^{1})^{m}=[-1,1]^{m}
. When

\mathcal{K}
consists of
m
disjoint vertices,
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the 1-dimensional
skeleton of
[-1,1]^{m}
. When

\mathcal{K}=\partial\varDelta[m]
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=\partial[-1,1]^{m}
. In general, if
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
is a face of

\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
has
2^{m-k}
cubic faces of dimension
k

which lie in the
k
-dimensional planes parallel to the

\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
th coordinate plane.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
V\times U(\mathcal{K})\to U(\mathcal{K})
be the exponential action defined by a complete simplicial fan
\Sigma=\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
. Then there is a homeomorphism

U(\mathcal{K})/V\cong\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

We have an inclusion of pairs
(D^{1},S^{0})\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{\times})
, which induces an inclusion

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{\mathcal{K}}=U(\mathcal{K})
. So the required homeomorphism will follow from the fact that the
V
-orbit of any point
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in U(\mathcal{K})
intersects
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
at a single point.

We may assume by induction that
x_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in[m]
, as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Consider
\ell({\textbf{{x}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|x_{i}|\in W^{*}
. As
\Sigma
is complete, there is
I\in\mathcal{K}
with
|I|=\dim W^{*}
such that
-\ell({\textbf{{x}}})\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
. Then
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\{\gamma_{i}\colon i\notin I\}
is a basis of
V^{*}
. By solving the equations
\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=-\log|x_{i}|
,
i\notin I
, we find
\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}={\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}
in the same
V
-orbit satisfying
|\widetilde{x}_{i}|=1
for
i\notin I
. Since
-\ell(\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}})=-\ell({\textbf{{x}}})\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
, we have
\log|\widetilde{x}_{i}|\leqslant 0
for
i\in I
. Hence,
|\widetilde{x}_{i}|\leqslant 1
for
i\in I
. These conditions imply that
\widetilde{{\textbf{{x}}}}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Therefore, every
V
-orbit intersects
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Now suppose that
{\textbf{{x}}},{\textbf{{x}}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
are in the same
V
-orbit. By definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
, there is
I\in\mathcal{K}
such that
|x_{i}|=1
for
i\notin I
and
|x_{i}|\leqslant 1
for
i\in I
, and
J\in\mathcal{K}
such that

|x^{\prime}_{j}|=1
for
j\notin J
and
|x^{\prime}_{j}|\leqslant 1
for
j\in J
. Since
\ell
is constant on
V
-orbits by (
6.1
), we obtain

\sum_{i\in I}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|x_{i}|=\ell({\textbf{{x}}})=\ell({\textbf{{x}}}^{\prime})=\sum_{j\in J}{\textbf{{a}}}_{j}\log|x^{\prime}_{j}|.

It follows that the expression above lies in
-\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}=-\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}
. As the latter is a simplicial cone, we obtain
|x_{i}|=|x^{\prime}_{i}|
for
i\in[m]
. Since the
V
-action preserves the sign of coordinates, we actually have
{\textbf{{x}}}={\textbf{{x}}}^{\prime}
, as needed.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the underlying complex of a complete simplicial fan. Then

(1)

the real moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
has a structure of a smooth compact manifold;

(2)

U(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
have the same homotopy type.

In
[
14
, Theorem 4.7.5]
, it is proved that there is a homotopy equivalence
U(\mathcal{K})\simeq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
for arbitrary
\mathcal{K}
. By virtue of Theorem
7.3
, this homotopy equivalence can be realised as the quotient by a free action of
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
when
\mathcal{K}
is the underlying complex of a complete simplicial fan.

8.
Polyhedra, normal fans, and intersections of quadrics

As we have seen in the previous sections, the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is a smooth compact manifold if and only if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a complete simplicial fan data, see Theorems
4.4
,
5.1
and
6.1
. There is an important class of complete fans arising from convex polytopes, as described next.

8.1.
Normal fans

A
convex polyhedron
in
W
is defined as the nonempty intersection of finitely many halfspaces:

(8.1)

P=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\},

where
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\in W^{*}
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

By referring to a polyhedron
P
we imply a particular system of linear inequalities, not only the set of its common solutions. An inequality
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0
in (
8.1
) is

redundant
if removing it from the system does not change the set of common solutions; otherwise the inequality is
irredundant
.

A
face
of
P
is the intersection of
P
with a supporting hyperplane. Every face is specified by turning some of the defining inequalities into equalities. A face is therefore determined by a subset
I\subset[m]
and is given by

F_{I}=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in P\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}=0\quad\text{for }i\in I\}.

Note that
F_{\varnothing}=P
. A
vertex
is a
0
-dimensional face, and a
facet
is a face of dimension one less the dimension of
P
. We denote
F_{\{i\}}
by
F_{i}
.

A polyhedron
P
is
full-dimensional
if the affine hull of
P
is the whole of
W
.
If
P
is full-dimensional and without redundant inequalities, then each
F_{i}
is a facet with normal
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\in W^{*}
, so there are
m
facets in total.

A bounded polyhedron is called a
polytope
.

Given a face
Q
of
P
, the
polyhedral angle at
Q
is the cone generated by all vectors
{{\textbf{{w}}}^{\prime}-{\textbf{{w}}}}
pointing from
{\textbf{{w}}}\in Q
to
{\textbf{{w}}}^{\prime}\in P
. The cone dual to the polyhedral angle at
Q
is given by

\sigma_{Q}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}\in W^{*}\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle\leqslant\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}^{\prime}\rangle\text{ for all ${\textbf{{w}}}\in Q$ and ${\textbf{{w}}}^{\prime}\in P$}\}.

and is called the
normal cone
of
Q
.
If we write
Q=F_{I}
, where
I\subset[m]
is maximal among all subsets defining
Q
, then

(8.2)

\sigma_{Q}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}({\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}=0\text{ for }{\textbf{{w}}}\in Q).

If
P
is full-dimensional, then
\sigma_{Q}
is a strongly convex cone; it is generated by the normals to the facets containing
Q
. Then

\Sigma_{P}=\{\sigma_{Q}\colon Q\text{ is a face of }P\}

is a fan in
W^{*}
, referred to as the
normal fan
of the polyhedron
P
. If
P
is not full-dimensional, then the cones
\sigma_{Q}
are not strictly convex, and
\Sigma_{P}
is a
generalised fan

[
16
, Definition 6.2.2]

The support
|\Sigma_{P}|
of a normal fan is convex (it coincides with the dual of the
recession cone
of
P
, see
[
16
, Theorem 7.1.6]
). In particular,
\Sigma_{P}
is complete if and only if
P
is a polytope.

A polyhedron (
8.1
) is
generic
if the hyperplanes defined by the equations
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}=0
are
in general position at every point of
P
(that is, for any
{\textbf{{w}}}\in P
the normals
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
of the hyperplanes containing

w
are linearly independent). A generic polyhedron is full-dimensional. A generic polyhedron may have redundant inequalities, but,
for any such inequality, the intersection of the corresponding
hyperplane with
P
is empty. For a generic
P
, each
F_{i}
is either a facet or empty. A generic polytope
P
is
simple
, that is, exactly
n=\dim W
facets meet at each vertex of
P
.

The normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
is simplicial if and only if
P
is a generic polyhedron. In this case, the cones of
\Sigma_{P}
are generated by those sets
\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{i_{1}},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{i_{k}}\}
for which the intersection
F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k}}
is nonempty. Define the
dual simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}_{P}=\bigl\{I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\colon F_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap F_{i_{k}}\neq\varnothing\bigr\}.

Then the simplicial fan
\Sigma_{P}
is defined by the data
\{\mathcal{K}_{P};{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
. The redundant inequalities in a generic polytope (
8.1
) correspond to the ghost vertices of
\mathcal{K}_{P}
.

Our next goal is to describe the normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
in terms of the Gale dual configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
. In what follows, whenever we refer to the normal fan and Gale duality, we assume that the polyhedron (
8.1
) is full-dimensional (so that
\Sigma_{P}
is a fan) and
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
spans
W
(so that the Gale dual configuration is defined).

Lemma 8.1
.

The normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
of a polyhedron (
8.1
) consists of the cones

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
.

Proof.

Take
\sigma_{Q}\in\Sigma_{P}
and write
Q=F_{I}
, where
I\subset[m]
is maximal among all subsets defining the face
Q
. Then
\sigma_{Q}=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
, see (
8.2
). Take
{\textbf{{w}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}Q
. We have
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}=0
for
i\in I
and
r_{i}:=\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}>0
for
i\in\widehat{I}
. Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i})\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}.

It follows that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
, by Proposition
4.1
.

Now consider
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}
for some positive
r_{i}
,
i\in\widehat{I}
. Denote
{\textbf{{b}}}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
{\textbf{{r}}}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
r_{i}=0
for
i\in I
. Then
\varGamma({\textbf{{r}}}-{\textbf{{b}}})=0
. Hence,
{\textbf{{r}}}-{\textbf{{b}}}
is in the image of
A^{*}
, see (
3.1
). In other words,
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle=r_{i}-b_{i}
for some
{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
. This
w
satisfies
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}=0
for
i\in I
and
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}>0
for
i\in\widehat{I}
. Hence,
Q=F_{I}
is a face of
P
, and
{\textbf{{w}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}Q
. The normal cone
\sigma_{Q}
is precisely
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
and
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a pair of Gale dual vector configurations. Take

\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma
, let

\mathcal{C}=\{I\subset[m]\colon\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}\}

and write
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
. Then
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
is the normal fan of the polyhedron
P
given by (
8.1
).

Proof.

Since
\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma
, we may assume that all
b_{i}>0
in the expression

\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
. Then (
8.1
) implies that
\mathbf{0}
is in the interior of
P
, so
P
is full-dimensional. The rest follows from Lemma
8.1
.
∎

Now we can state a criterion for a fan to be the normal fan of a polyhedron. It is originally due to Shephard
[
42
]
.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
and
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a pair of Gale dual vector configurations. Let
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
be a fan with convex support (respectively, a complete fan). Then
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polyhedron (respectively, the normal fan of a polytope) if and only if

\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}\neq\varnothing.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
is the normal fan of a polyhedron
P
given by (
8.1
). Set
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
. Then
\delta\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
by Lemma
8.1
.

Conversely, let
\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}\neq\varnothing
.
Take
\delta\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
. Write

\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
for some
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, and define a polyhedron
P
by (
8.1
). Then each
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
with
I\in\mathcal{C}
belongs to the normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
by Lemma
8.1
, so that
\Sigma
is a subfan of
\Sigma_{P}
. If
\Sigma
is a complete fan, then this implies that
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P}
and we are done. If
\Sigma
has convex support, then it is easy to see that a subfan of
\Sigma_{P}
with convex support is the normal fan of a polyhedron
P^{\prime}
. Indeed, define

P^{\prime}=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0\quad\text{for }\{i\}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

Then
P\subset P^{\prime}
and
\Sigma_{P^{\prime}}
is a subfan of
\Sigma_{P}
. For
\{j\}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have that
F_{j}=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in P^{\prime}\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{j},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{j}=0\}
is either a facet of
P^{\prime}
, or the corresponding vector
{\textbf{{a}}}_{j}
is in the cone generated by
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
,
i\in\mathcal{C}
,
i\neq j
. It follows that

|\Sigma_{P^{\prime}}|=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}({\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\colon\{i\}\in\mathcal{C})=|\Sigma|,

where the second identity holds because
\Sigma
has convex support. Since both

\Sigma_{P^{\prime}}
and
\Sigma
are subfans of
\Sigma_{P}
, we conclude that

\Sigma_{P^{\prime}}=\Sigma
.
∎

We see how the condition for a normal fan is stronger than the condition for a fan: in the former case, the relative interiors of the cones
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
,
I\in\mathcal{C}
, must have a common point, while in the latter case only pairwise intersections must be nontrivial.

A choice of
\delta\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
in Theorem
8.3
defines a polyhedron
P
(whose normal fan is
\Sigma
) up to a translation in
W
: one needs to express
\delta
as a linear combination
\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
to get a polyhedron (
8.1
), and different expressions of the same
\delta
produce polytopes which are different by a translation. Choosing a different
\delta^{\prime}\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
results in a polyhedron
P^{\prime}
with the same normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
; such polyhedra
P
and
P^{\prime}
are called
analogous
.

8.2.
From polyhedra to quadrics

Now we turn to the quotient (leaf space)
U(\mathcal{K})/V
. As we have seen in Theorem
7.3
, the quotient
U(\mathcal{K})/V
is identified with the real moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{K}}
when the data
\{\mathcal{K},{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
define a complete simplicial fan
\Sigma
. When
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polytope, there is another realisation of
U(\mathcal{K})/V
, which has an intrinsic smooth structure as a nondegenerate intersection of real quadratic surfaces.

Consider the smooth map

\mu_{\Gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to V^{*},\quad(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\mapsto x^{2}_{1}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+x^{2}_{m}\gamma_{m}.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let

P=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\}

be a generic polyhedron with normal simplicial fan
\Sigma_{P}=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{K}_{P}\}
. Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be the Gale dual configuration of
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
. Put
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
. Then

(1)

\delta\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{K}_{P}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
;

(2)

\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)\subset U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
;

(3)

\delta
is a regular value of
\mu_{\Gamma}
;

(4)

the action of
V
on
U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
is free and proper, and the quotient
U(\mathcal{K}_{P})/V
is diffeomorphic to the level set
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
, where the latter is given as a nondegenerate intersection of
k
quadratic surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

U(\mathcal{K}_{P})/V\cong\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\colon\gamma_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}x^{2}_{m}=\delta\}.

This result has well-known interpretations in the theory of Hamiltonian torus actions in symplectic geometry and in the theory of toric varieties (see
[
2
, Proposition 3.1.1]
and
[
23
, Appendix 1, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4]
, and also §
14.3
below). However, it is purely topological in nature.

Proof of Theorem
8.4
.

Statement (1) follows from Lemma
8.1
.

To prove (2), take
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
, so that
\gamma_{1}x^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}x^{2}_{m}=\delta
. Let
I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}=\{i\colon x_{i}=0\}\subset[m]
. Then

\delta=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}x_{i}^{2}\gamma_{i}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}.

This implies, by Lemma
8.1
, that
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}\in\Sigma_{P}
. Hence,
I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\in\mathcal{K}_{P}
and
{\textbf{{x}}}\in U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
by definition of
U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
, see (
2.1
).

To prove (3), observe that the differential of
\mu_{\Gamma}
at a point
x
is given by

(d\mu_{\Gamma})_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to V^{*},\quad(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\mapsto 2x_{1}\gamma_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+2x_{m}\gamma_{m}y_{m}.

Now for any
{\textbf{{x}}}\in\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
we have
I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\in\mathcal{K}_{P}
. The differential at such
x
is given by

(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\mapsto\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}2x_{i}\gamma_{i}y_{i}.

Since
P
is generic,
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}
is a simplicial cone. Hence, the set
\mathrm{A}_{I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}
is linearly independent. By Proposition
3.1
, the set
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}
spans
V^{*}
. Therefore, the set

\{x_{i}\gamma_{i}\colon i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\}
also spans
V^{*}
, which implies that the differential
(d\mu_{\Gamma})_{{\textbf{{x}}}}
is surjective at any
{\textbf{{x}}}\in\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
. Thus,
\delta
is a regular value of
\mu_{\Gamma}
.

Since
\delta
is a regular value, the intersection of quadrics in (4) is nondegenerate,
i. e. the
k
gradients of the quadratic surfaces are linearly independent at each point of
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
. In particular,
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is a smooth submanifold of dimension
n
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

To prove the rest of (4), we need to verify that each
V
-orbit of
U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
intersects
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
in one and only one point. Since the
V
-action on
U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
is free, restricting the map
\mu_{\Gamma}
to an orbit
V\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}
gives a map

(8.3)

L\colon V\to V^{*},\quad{\textbf{{v}}}\mapsto e^{2\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{1}^{2}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+e^{2\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{m}^{2}\gamma_{m}.

By Lemma
8.5
below,
L
maps the orbit diffeomorphically onto
\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}
. We have
\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}
, since
I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}\in\mathcal{K}_{P}
. Hence,
\delta
is in the image of
L
, so that the orbit
V\cdot{\textbf{{x}}}
intersects
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
. Finally, since
L
is injective, there is only one point of intersection.
∎

It remains to prove the key fact about the image of
L
. The argument below is based on the properties of the Legendre transform and is taken from
[
23
, Appendix 1]
.

Lemma 8.5
.

The restriction (
8.3
) of the map
\mu_{\Gamma}
to the
V
-orbit of a point
{\textbf{{x}}}\in U(\mathcal{K}_{P})
maps this orbit diffeomorphically onto
\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}(\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0)
.

Proof.

Consider the function
\frac{1}{2}\|{\textbf{{x}}}\|^{2}
, where

\|{\textbf{{x}}}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2}
, and restrict it to the
V
-orbit of a given
{\textbf{{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
. We obtain the function

(8.4)

f\colon V\to\mathbb{R},\quad f({\textbf{{v}}})={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(e^{2\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+e^{2\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{m}^{2}\bigr).

Then
(df)_{{\textbf{{v}}}}=L({\textbf{{v}}})
, i. e.
L
is the (multidimensional)
Legendre transform
of
f
. The second differential

(d^{2}f)_{{\textbf{{v}}}}=2\bigl(e^{2\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{1}^{2}\gamma_{1}\otimes\gamma_{1}+\cdots+e^{2\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{m}^{2}\gamma_{m}\otimes\gamma_{m}\bigr)

is positive definite, since
\{\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\}
generate
V^{*}
. This implies that
f
is strictly convex, and also that
L
is a local diffeomorphism (its differential is nondegenerate).

Given
\delta\in V^{*}
, consider the function
f_{\delta}({\textbf{{v}}})=f({\textbf{{v}}})-\langle\delta,{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle
. This function is also strictly convex, because
d^{2}f_{\delta}=d^{2}f
. For a strictly convex function
f
the following three conditions are equivalent:

(a)

(df)_{{\textbf{{v}}}}=0
at some point
{\textbf{{v}}}\in V
;

(b)

f
has a unique local minimum;

(c)

f({\textbf{{v}}})
tends to
+\infty
as
v
tends to infinity in
V
.

Now
(df_{\delta})_{{\textbf{{v}}}}=(df)_{{\textbf{{v}}}}-\delta=L({\textbf{{v}}})-\delta
, so
f_{\delta}
has a critical point if and only if
\delta
is in the image of
L
. Since a critical point of
f_{\delta}
us unique, the map
L
is injective, so it is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

It remains to identify the image of
L
. Clearly,
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L\subset\mathbb{R}_{>}\langle\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}(\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0)
. The opposite inclusion can be proved directly in the case when
I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}
is largest possible, that is
|I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}|=n
. Then
\{\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\}
is a basis of
V^{*}
. Take
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{>}\langle\gamma_{i}\colon x_{i}\neq 0\rangle
and write

\delta=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}
,
r_{i}>0
. The equation
L({\textbf{{v}}})=\delta
takes the form

\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}e^{2\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}x_{i}^{2}\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}}r_{i}\gamma_{i}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle=\log\frac{\sqrt{r}_{i}}{|x_{i}|},\quad i\in\widehat{I}_{{\textbf{{x}}}}.

The latter system of equations has a (unique) solution
v
, as needed.

In the general case, the function (
8.4
) can be written as the sum of functions corresponding to
x
with
|I_{{\textbf{{x}}}}|=n
. So the result follows from the following fact:

Let
L_{i}
be the Legendre transform of a strictly convex function
f_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(L_{1}+L_{2})=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{1}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{2}
, where the latter is the Minkowski sum of two convex sets in
V^{*}
.

Indeed, the inclusion
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(L_{1}+L_{2})\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{1}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{2}
is clear. For the opposite inclusion, take
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{1}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}L_{2}
. Then
\delta_{i}=L({\textbf{{v}}}_{i})
, so that
{\textbf{{v}}}_{i}
is the unique critical point of
(f_{i})_{\delta_{i}}
. Hence, both

(f_{1})_{\delta_{1}}
and
(f_{2})_{\delta_{2}}
satisfy property (c) above. Then their sum

(f_{1})_{\delta_{1}}+(f_{2})_{\delta_{2}}=(f_{1}+f_{2})_{\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}
is strictly convex and also satisfies (c). Therefore,
(f_{1}+f_{2})_{\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}}
has a (unique) critical point, so
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}
is in the image of
L_{1}+L_{2}
, as claimed.
∎

Corollary 8.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is the underlying simplicial complex of the normal fan of a generic polytope (

and
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}

Proof.

This follows by combining Theorem

and the intersection of quadrics are homeomorphic to the quotient

(see also

∎

8.3.
From quadrics to polyhedra

The input of Theorem

is a generic polyhedron

in

and a choice of generic

Theorem 8.7
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a spanning vector configuration in

(a)

(b)

|J|\geqslant k
,

and let

Then the action (
1.1
) of

Furthermore, the data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
define the normal fan of a generic polyhedron
P
given by (
8.1
), where
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
is the Gale dual configuration in

and
the numbers

are defined by the expression
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}

Proof.

By definition,

is a pure simplicial complex of dimension

Condition (b) implies that if

Now

We refer to

Proposition 8.8
.

Let

Construction 8.9

(GKZ decomposition)

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}

(c)

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma
is a strictly convex (pointed) cone.

\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}

.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) This follows from the fact that

{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
, and
\gamma_{i}
is the linear function on

that takes a linear relation to its

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) Indeed,
\mathbf{0}
is a face of
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma
, defined by the supporting hyperplane
{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(d)

.

\Rightarrow
(a) If

To define the holomorphic version of the exponential action, assume that
\dim V=k

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
is nonzero, the quotient
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}/\widetilde{V}
is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

The criterion for compactness of the quotient is also the same as in the real case, with the same proof:

Theorem 10.5
.

Let
\widetilde{V}\times U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\to U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
be a proper holomorphic exponential action.
The quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\widetilde{V}
is compact if and only if the corresponding fan
\Sigma
is complete.

The
moment-angle complex

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the polyhedral product

(D^{1},S^{0})^{\mathcal{K}}
, where
D^{2}
is a closed unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
and
S^{1}
is
its boundary circle:

(10.3)

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}(D^{2},S^{1})^{I}.

It is a CW-subcomplex in the product of
m
discs
(D^{2})^{m}
with the induced action of
m
-torus
T^{m}=(S^{1})^{m}
. When
\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial subdivision of an
(n-1)
-sphere, i. e.
|\mathcal{K}|\cong S^{n-1}
, the moment-angle complex
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is a closed topological
(m+n)
-dimensional manifold, called the
moment-angle manifold

[
14
, Theorem 4.1.4]
. The topology of moment-angle manifolds can be remarkably complicated, see
[
14
, Chapter 4]
.

If
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration, then the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
can be identified with the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/V
by the
real
exponential action

(10.4)

\displaystyle V\times\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle({\textbf{{v}}},{\textbf{{z}}})

\displaystyle\mapsto{\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}z_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}z_{m}\bigr).

This is proved in exactly the same way as Theorem
7.3
. (Note also that the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is homeomorphic to the real moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{R}_{D(\mathcal{K})}
, where
D(\mathcal{K})
is the
double
of
\mathcal{K}
, see
[
4
]
or
[
14
, Corollary 4.8.14]
.)

Provided that
\dim V=k
is even and a complex structure is chosen on
V
, the quotient of the
holomorphic
exponential action (
10.1
) can also be identified with the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
, thereby endowing the latter with a complex structure:

Theorem 10.6
.

Assume that
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration defining a complete simplicial fan
\Sigma
, and let
\widetilde{V}\times U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\to U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
be the corresponding holomorphic exponential action. Then there is a homeomorphism

U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\widetilde{V}\cong\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

We have an inclusion of pairs
(D^{2},S^{1})\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, which induces an inclusion

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=(D^{2},S^{1})^{\mathcal{K}}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\mathcal{K}}=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
. So the required homeomorphism will follow from the fact that the
\widetilde{V}
-orbit of any point
{\textbf{{z}}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
intersects
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
at a single point.

We may assume that
z_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in[m]
, as in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Given
{\textbf{{z}}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{m}
, define

\ell({\textbf{{z}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|z_{i}|\in W^{*}.

The map
\ell
is constant on
\widetilde{V}
-orbits, since for any
{\textbf{{v}}}\in\widetilde{V}
we have

\ell({\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}|z_{i}|\bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|z_{i}|=\ell({\textbf{{z}}}),

where the last equality follows by Gale duality.
As
\Sigma
is complete, there is
I\in\mathcal{K}
with
|I|=\dim W^{*}
such
that
-\ell({\textbf{{z}}})\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
. Then
\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\{\gamma_{i}\colon i\notin I\}
is a (real) basis of
V^{*}
. By solving the equations

\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=-\log|z_{i}|
,
i\notin I
, we find

\widetilde{{\textbf{{z}}}}={\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}
in the same
\widetilde{V}
-orbit satisfying

|\widetilde{z}_{i}|=1
for
i\notin I
. Since
-\ell(\widetilde{{\textbf{{z}}}})=-\ell({\textbf{{z}}})\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
, we have
\log|\widetilde{z}_{i}|\leqslant 0
for
i\in I
. Hence,

|\widetilde{z}_{i}|\leqslant 1
for
i\in I
. These conditions imply that
\widetilde{{\textbf{{z}}}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Therefore, every
\widetilde{V}
-orbit intersects
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
.

Now suppose that
{\textbf{{z}}},{\textbf{{z}}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
are in the same
\widetilde{V}
-orbit, i. e.

{\textbf{{z}}}^{\prime}={\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}
. By definition of

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
, there is
I\in\mathcal{K}
such that
|z_{i}|=1
for
i\notin I
and
|z_{i}|\leqslant 1
for
i\in I
, and
J\in\mathcal{K}
such that

|z^{\prime}_{j}|=1
for
j\notin J
and
|z^{\prime}_{j}|\leqslant 1
for
j\in J
. We have

\sum_{i\in I}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}\log|z_{i}|=\ell({\textbf{{z}}})=\ell({\textbf{{z}}}^{\prime})=\sum_{j\in J}{\textbf{{a}}}_{j}\log|z^{\prime}_{j}|.

It follows that the expression above lies in
-\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J}=-\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I\cap J}
. As the latter is a simplicial cone, we obtain
|z_{i}|=|z^{\prime}_{i}|=e^{\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}}|z_{i}|
for
i\in[m]
. This implies
\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=0
for
i\in[m]
. Hence,
{\textbf{{v}}}=0
and

{\textbf{{z}}}={\textbf{{z}}}^{\prime}
, as needed.
∎

Corollary 10.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{K}
is the underlying complex of a complete
n
-dimensional simplicial fan
\Sigma
and
m-n=2\ell
is even. Then

(1)

the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
has a structure of a compact complex manifold;

(2)

U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
have the same homotopy type.

Construction 10.8

(adding a ghost vertex)
.

Given a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
, let
\mathcal{K}_{+}
be the same collection of subsets, but viewed as a simplicial complex on
[m+1]
. That is,
\mathcal{K}_{+}
is obtained from
\mathcal{K}
by adding the ghost vertex
\{m+1\}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\times S^{1}
and
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{+})=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

Given a spanning vector configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in
W^{*}
, consider a configuration
\mathrm{A}_{+}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m},{\textbf{{a}}}_{m+1}\}
obtained by appending an arbitrary vector
{\textbf{{a}}}_{m+1}\in W^{*}
. The space of linear relations between the vectors of
\mathrm{A}_{+}
is
V_{+}=V\oplus\mathbb{R}\langle{\textbf{{v}}}_{+}\rangle
, where
V
is the space of relations between
\mathrm{A}
and
{\textbf{{v}}}_{+}
corresponds to a new relation
r_{1}{\textbf{{a}}}_{1}+\cdots+r_{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}+{\textbf{{a}}}_{m+1}=\bf 0
. (For instance, if
{\textbf{{a}}}_{m+1}=\bf 0
, then we may take
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{m}=0
.) The Gale dual vector configuration
\Gamma_{+}
in
V_{+}^{*}
is

\Gamma_{+}=\{\gamma^{+}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{+}_{m},\gamma^{+}_{m+1}\}=\Bigl\{\Bigl(\begin{matrix}\gamma_{1}\\
r_{1}\end{matrix}\Bigr),\ldots,\Bigl(\begin{matrix}\gamma_{m}\\
r_{m}\end{matrix}\Bigr),\Bigl(\begin{matrix}\bf 0\\
1\end{matrix}\Bigr)\Bigr\}.

The exponential action (
10.4
) of
V_{+}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
is given by

\displaystyle V_{+}\times\mathbb{C}^{m+1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m+1}

\displaystyle({\textbf{{v}}},t,{\textbf{{z}}})

\displaystyle\mapsto({\textbf{{v}}},t)\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+r_{1}t}z_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+r_{m}t}z_{m},e^{t}z_{m+1}\bigr).

Clearly, if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration defining an
n
-dimensional fan
\Sigma
, then
\{\mathcal{K}_{+},\mathrm{A}_{+}\}
defines the same fan. In this case, the exponential action of
V_{+}
above restricted to
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{+})=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is free and proper. When the fan is complete, the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{+})/V_{+}
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\times S^{1}
.

Now if
m-n
is odd, then
m+1-n
is even, and therefore
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\times S^{1}
has a structure of a compact complex manifold by Corollary
10.7
.

Elaborating on this further, we get the following generalisation of Example
10.4
.

Example 10.9

(holomorphic tori)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}=\{\varnothing\}
be the simplicial complex on
[m]
consisting of

\varnothing
only, that is, with
m
ghost vertices. Let
\mathrm{A}
be a configuration of
m
zero vectors in
W^{*}=\{\bf 0\}
. The Gale dual configuration

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
is a basis in
V^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The quotient of the real exponential action (
10.4
) of
V
on
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
is
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=T^{m}
, the compact
m
-torus.

Now consider holomorphic exponential actions. We need to specify a complex structure
\mathcal{J}
on
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}
, so that
m
must be even,
m=2\ell
. Then
\widetilde{V}=(V,\mathcal{J})
has complex dimension
\ell
. In order to describe the quotient
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}/\widetilde{V}
of the holomorphic exponential action, consider the exact sequence of
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps

0\longrightarrow\widetilde{V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}^{m}\cong\widetilde{V}\oplus\overline{\widetilde{V}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}^{m}/\widetilde{V}\cong\overline{\widetilde{V}}\longrightarrow 0

Here
\iota
denotes the map
{\textbf{{v}}}\mapsto(\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}},\ldots,\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}})
, and
p
denotes the quotient projection. We have
\widetilde{V}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\widetilde{V}\oplus\overline{\widetilde{V}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{m}
, where

\overline{\widetilde{V}}=(V,-\mathcal{J})
, the map

\iota
is identified through this isomorphism with the inclusion of the first summand, and
p
with the second projection. For

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
, let
{\textbf{{g}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{g}}}_{m}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-dual basis of
V
, that is,

\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{g}}}_{l}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}=\delta_{kl}
. Then
{\textbf{{v}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}{\textbf{{g}}}_{k}
, and the map
p
is given on the standard basis vectors by
{\textbf{{e}}}_{k}\mapsto{\textbf{{g}}}_{k}
,
i{\textbf{{e}}}_{k}\mapsto-\mathcal{J}{\textbf{{g}}}_{k}
. Now we have

(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}/\widetilde{V}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{m}/2\pi i\mathbb{Z}^{m})/\widetilde{V}\cong\overline{\widetilde{V}}/p(2\pi i\mathbb{Z}^{m})\cong\overline{\widetilde{V}}/\mathbb{Z}\langle\mathcal{J}{\textbf{{g}}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}{\textbf{{g}}}_{m}\rangle.

This is a holomorphic torus, the quotient of an
\ell
-dimensional complex space by the lattice generated by the
\mathbb{R}
-basis
\mathcal{J}{\textbf{{g}}}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}{\textbf{{g}}}_{2\ell}
. Every holomorphic torus is obtained from some
\widetilde{V}=(V,\mathcal{J})
in this way.

Proposition 10.10
.

A complex moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is non-Kähler, unless it is a holomorphic torus.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is Kähler of complex dimension
d
. Then the cohomology class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{K})
of the Kähler form satisfies
[\omega]^{d}\neq 0
, as
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is compact.

If
\mathcal{K}
does not have ghost vertices, then it is easy to see using (
10.3
) and the appropriate cell decomposition that
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is 2-connected (see
[
14
, Proposition 4.3.5]
). In general,
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is diffeomorphic to a product of a 2-connected manifold and a torus (see Construction
10.8
). Hence, it can have a 2-dimensional cohomology class satisfying
[\omega]^{d}\neq 0
only if the 2-connected factor is a point, so
\mathcal{K}
consists entirely of ghost vertices and
\mathcal{Z}_{K}
is a
d
-dimensional holomorphic torus.
∎

When
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polytope, the quotient

can be realised as a nondegenerate intersection of

quadrics:

Theorem 10.11
.

Let

P=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\}

be a generic polyhedron with normal simplicial fan
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{K}_{P}\}
. Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be the Gale dual configuration of

. Put

.

The holomorphic exponential action
\widetilde{V}\times U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})\to U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})
is free and proper, and the quotient

is diffeomorphic to the nondegenerate intersection of

Hermitian quadrics in

:

Proof.

The proof is similar to the real case (Theorem

). We consider the map

\mu_{\Gamma}\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to V^{*},\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\mapsto|z_{1}|^{2}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+|z_{m}|^{2}\gamma_{m},

show that
\delta
is a regular value of
\mu_{\Gamma}
and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is a nondegenerate intersection of Hermitian quadrics that meets every

\widetilde{V}
-orbit of
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})
at a single point.
∎

. A rational cone in

if it is generated by part of a basis of

A configuration

.
A triangulated configuration

if

is part of a basis of

for any

. A nonsingular triangulated configuration

defines a nonsingular fan

Proposition 11.3
.

Let

\mathrm{A}

\widehat{\Sigma}

(2)

The surjections

Since

(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I})\cap(\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{J})=\varnothing

For a subset

, define a subgroup of

Proposition 11.4
.

A triangulated configuration
\{\mathcal{K},\widehat{\mathrm{A}}\}
is nonsingular if and only if

for any
I\in\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

We may write
L_{I}=\{{\textbf{{q}}}\in Q\colon\langle\widehat{\gamma}_{k},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\;\text{ for }k\notin I\}
.

Assume that

for any

. Then
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\widehat{I}}=\{\widehat{\gamma}_{k}\colon k\notin I\}
spans

. Indeed, otherwise there is

such that

contains the subspace

Now assume that

12.
Partial quotients and torus-exponential actions

A
partial quotient
of a moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/H
by a freely acting subtorus
H\subset T^{m}
. When
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is triangulated configuration and the subtorus
H
is defined by a rational subspace
Q\subset V
of even codimension, the partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/H
admits a complex structure in which the quotient torus
T^{m}/H
acts by holomorphic transformations. Such a holomorphic partial quotient is the quotient of the open subset
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
by a
torus-exponential action
of the product of an algebraic torus and a complex space. In addition to complex moment-angle manifolds, this construction includes LVMB manifolds and complete nonsingular toric varieties as special cases.

12.1.
Torus-exponential actions and their quotients

The lattice
L
(
11.1
) in a rational subspace
Q\subset V
defines an algebraic torus

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong Q_{\mathbb{C}}/(2\pi iL),

where
Q_{\mathbb{C}}=Q\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}=Q\oplus iQ
denotes the complexification of
Q
, and a compact torus

T_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}\cong Q/(2\pi L).

Both
Q
and
T_{L}
are Lie subgroups of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
(given by
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
, respectively) and we have a product decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=Q\times T_{L}
as Lie groups.

Given a vector configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
in
V
,
the map
\varGamma^{*}|_{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
embeds
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
as a closed subgroup in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
:

(12.1)

(\varGamma^{*}|_{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times})(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L})=\exp\varGamma^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{C}})\\
=\bigl\{(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+i\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{u}}}\rangle},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+i\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{u}}}\rangle})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}\colon{\textbf{{v}}}+i{\textbf{{u}}}\in Q_{\mathbb{C}}\bigr\},

where
\varGamma^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}\colon V_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the complexification of
\varGamma^{*}
and
\exp\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
is the component-wise exponential map. Similarly,
\varGamma^{*}|_{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
embeds
T_{L}
as a closed subgroup in
T^{m}=(S^{1})^{m}
:

(\varGamma^{*}|_{L}\otimes S^{1})(T_{L})=\bigl\{(e^{i\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle},\ldots,e^{i\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle})\in T^{m}\colon{\textbf{{q}}}\in Q\bigr\}.

The subgroup of
T^{m}
on the right hand side above is closed precisely because
Q
is a rational subspace.

We define the Lie group
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V
as the quotient of

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times V
by the subgroup consisting of pairs
(j(-{\textbf{{q}}}),{\textbf{{q}}})
,
where
{\textbf{{q}}}\in Q
and
j\colon Q\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
is the inclusion. There are two product decompositions:

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V\cong T_{L}\times V\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times(V/Q).

Here the first isomorphism is canonical, while the isomorphism

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times(V/Q)\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V
is defined by a splitting
V/Q\to V
.

Both
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
and
V
embed as closed subgroups in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
, as

\exp\varGamma^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\exp\varGamma^{*}(V)
, respectively, see (
12.1
). The intersection of these two subgroups is

\exp\varGamma^{*}(Q)
, so
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V
also embeds as a closed subgroup in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
. We therefore obtain an action of

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by restricting the coordinate-wise action
of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
. Using the canonical decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V\cong T_{L}\times V
, this action can be written explicitly as

(12.2)

\displaystyle(T_{L}\times V)\times\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}

\displaystyle({\textbf{{q}}},{\textbf{{v}}},{\textbf{{z}}})

\displaystyle\mapsto({\textbf{{q}}},{\textbf{{v}}})\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=\bigl(e^{\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+i\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle}z_{1},\ldots,e^{\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle+i\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle}z_{m}\bigr).

We refer to it as the
torus-exponential action
.

Proposition 12.1
.

For a point
{\textbf{{z}}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, let
I=\{i\in[m]\colon z_{i}=0\}
.
The stabiliser of
z
under the action of
T_{L}
given by (

) is

(T_{L})_{{\textbf{{z}}}}=2\pi(L_{I}/L)
.

Proof.

For
{\textbf{{q}}}\in T_{L}=Q/(2\pi L)
, we have

{\textbf{{q}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=(e^{i\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle}z_{1},\ldots,e^{i\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle}z_{m})
.
Hence,
{\textbf{{q}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}={\textbf{{z}}}
if and only if
\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{q}}}\rangle\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
for

, that is,
{\textbf{{q}}}\in 2\pi(L_{I}/L)
.
∎

An action of a Lie group is
almost free
if all stabilisers are discrete subgroups.

Theorem 12.2
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{A}
be a pair of Gale dual vector configurations,

a rational subspace, and
\mathcal{K}
a simplicial complex on

is proper if and only if

is a triangulated configuration, in which case the action is almost free with finite stabilisers.

Proof.

Since

is a compact group, the action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}\times_{Q}V\cong T_{Q}\times V
is proper if and only if the action of
V
is proper. Hence, the assertion about the properness follows from Theorem

. It also gives that the action of

is free. For the action of

spans

, so

is a full-rank lattice in

. It follows that

is a finite group.
∎

12.2.
Holomorphic torus-exponential actions

The holomorphic version of the torus-exponential action is defined when the dimension of

\mathcal{J}\colon V/Q\to V/Q

(V/Q,\mathcal{J})

12.3.
Maximal torus actions

The quotient torus
T^{m}/T_{L}
acts on a holomorphic partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
by holomorphic transformations.

An action of a torus
T
on a connected smooth manifold
M
is
maximal
if there is a point
x\in M
such that
\dim T+\dim T_{x}=\dim M
, where
T_{x}
is the stabiliser subgroup of
x
. Examples of maximal torus actions are the action of a half-dimensional compact torus on a smooth complete toric variety, the free action of
T^{m}
on itself and the action of
T^{m}
on a moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
. These are particular cases of the action of
T^{m}/T_{L}
on a partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
:

Proposition 12.5
.

The action of the torus
T^{m}/T_{L}
on a partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
is maximal.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{K}}(D^{2},S^{1})^{I}
as in (
10.3
), where

(D^{2},S^{1})^{I}=\prod_{i\in I}D^{2}\times\prod_{i\notin I}S^{1}
. Choose a maximal simplex
J\in\mathcal{K}
,
|J|=n
, and a point
x\in\prod_{j\in J}0\times\prod_{j\notin J}S^{1}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Writing
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\cong U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/V
, we obtain that
x
corresponds to the
V
-orbit of
{\textbf{{z}}}\in U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
with
z_{j}=0
for
j\in J
. The stabiliser of
x
for the
T^{m}
-action on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the coordinate subtorus
T^{J}=\prod_{j\in J}S^{1}\subset\prod_{i=1}^{m}S^{1}=T^{m}
.

Let
G=T^{m}/T_{L}
and let
\overline{x}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
be the image of
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
in the quotient. We have
T^{J}\cap T_{L}=\{1\}
, since
T_{L}
acts freely
on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Hence, the stabiliser
G_{\overline{x}}
is isomorphic to
T^{J}
and is
n
-dimensional. We obtain
\dim G+\dim G_{\overline{x}}=m-\dim Q+n=\dim\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}-\dim Q=\dim\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
, so the
G
-action on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
is maximal.
∎

Compact complex manifolds
M
with maximal torus actions by holomorphic transformations are classified by Ishida in
[
26
]
. The main result
[
26
, Theorem 1.5]
implies that every such
M
is equivariantly biholomorphic to a holomorphic partial quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
. (For the latter formulation, see
[
44
, Theorem 4.8]
.) It also implies that any complex manifold
M
with a maximal torus action is the base of a holomorphic torus principal bundle with total space a complex moment-angle manifold.

Two cases of holomorphic partial quotients
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{L}
, corresponding to a one-dimensional rational subspace
Q\subset V
and the whole of
V
being rational, respectively, are of particular importance. They are considered in the next two sections.

and Bosio
[
10

. These manifolds are defined as the leaf spaces of exponential holomorphic foliations on the
projectivisations
of open subsets
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
defined by polytopal fans (in the case of LVM manifolds) or complete simplicial fans (in the case of LVMB manifolds). Therefore, an LVMB manifold can be described as the projectivisation of a complex moment-angle manifold, or the partial quotient of a moment-angle manifold by the diagonal circle action. We describe this link here and draw some consequences.

We start with a spanning vector configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
in
V^{*}
for which there exists
{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}\in V
such that
\langle\gamma_{i},{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}\rangle=1
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Equivalently, the Gale dual configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
satisfies the relation

\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}=\bf 0
. In particular, the conditions of Proposition
9.1
are met. Let
R=\mathbb{R}\langle{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}\rangle
.
Thinking in terms of relations between the vectors of
\mathrm{A}
, the subspace
R
is generated by a single relation with all coefficients
1
, so it is rational. The lattice (
11.1
) is the

\mathbb{Z}
-span of
{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}
. Let
V^{\prime}=V/R
and choose a splitting
s\colon V^{\prime}\to V
as in Section
9
. Such a splitting is defined by a choice of
\delta\in V^{*}
satisfying
\langle\delta,{\textbf{{v}}}_{1}\rangle=1
, then
s(V^{\prime})=\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}\delta
. Let

\Gamma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m}\}
be the corresponding point configuration in
(V^{\prime})^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ann}}R
, where
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}=s^{*}(\gamma_{i})=\gamma_{i}-\delta
.

Assume further that
\dim V^{\prime}=k-1
is even, and let
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}
be a complex structure. Denote the resulting dimensional complex space
(V^{\prime},\mathcal{J}^{\prime})
by
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
. An LVMB-manifold is the quotient of
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
by a proper holomorphic torus-exponential action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{d}\times\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
, where

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{d}
is the one-dimensional diagonal algebraic subtorus
of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
. The original construction
[
10
]
is as follows.

Construction 13.1

(LVMB manifold)
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a point configuration that affinely spans
(V^{\prime})^{*}
, and
let
\mathcal{E}
be a nonempty collection of subsets of
[m]
such that for each
J\in\mathcal{E}
the subconfiguration
\Gamma^{\prime}_{J}
is a minimal affine generating set of
(V^{\prime})^{*}
(in particular, each
J
has cardinality
k
).

A pair
(\mathcal{E},\Gamma^{\prime})
is called an
LVMB datum
if the two conditions are satisfied:

[
imbrication
]

for any
I,J\in\mathcal{E}
, the polytopes

\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{I}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{J}
have a common interior point;

[
substitute existence
]

for any
J\in\mathcal{E}
and
i\in[m]
there exists
j\in J
such that
(J\setminus\{j\})\cup\{i\}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Define the following open subset in the projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{m-1}
:

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\bigl\{[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{m}]\in\mathbb{C}P^{m-1}\colon\text{there is $J\in\mathcal{E}$ such that }\{j\colon z_{j}\neq 0\}\supset J\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal{E}}\bigl\{[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{m}]\in\mathbb{C}P^{m-1}\colon z_{j}\neq 0\text{ for }j\in J\bigr\}.

and the holomorphic exponential action

(13.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{\prime}\times P(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow P(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle({\textbf{{v}}}^{\prime},{\textbf{{z}}})

\displaystyle\longmapsto{\textbf{{v}}}\cdot{\textbf{{z}}}=\bigl[e^{\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}}z_{1}:\cdots:e^{\langle\gamma^{\prime}_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}}z_{m}\bigr].

Bosio proves in
[
10

that this action is proper if and only if

(\mathcal{E},\Gamma^{\prime})
satisfies the imbrication condition, such a proper action is free,
and the quotient
P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
of a proper action is compact if and only if the substitute existence condition is satisfied.

The quotient
\mathcal{N}=P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
defined by an LVMB datum

(\mathcal{E},\Gamma^{\prime})
is called an
LVMB manifold
. It is a compact complex manifold.

To link the definition of LVMB manifold with the holomorphic exponential actions considered in Section
10
, we relate LVMB data
\{\mathcal{E},\Gamma^{\prime}\}
to fan data (triangulated vector configurations). Let
\mathcal{K}
be the pure
(m-k-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex whose maximal simplices are complements to
J\in\mathcal{E}
, that is,

(13.2)

\mathcal{K}=\{I\subset[m]\colon I\subset\widehat{J}\text{ for some }J\in\mathcal{E}\}.

An element
i\in[m]
is called
indispensable
for
\mathcal{E}
if it belongs to every
J\in\mathcal{E}
. Clearly,
i
is indispensable if and only
\{i\}
is a ghost vertex of
\mathcal{K}
.

Proposition 13.2
.

(1)

P(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathbb{C}P^{m-1}
is the projectivisation of
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
;

(2)

\{\mathcal{E},\Gamma^{\prime}\}
is an LVMB datum if and only if

\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration defining a complete simplicial fan;

(3)

the LVMB manifold
\mathcal{N}=P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
is the quotient of the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
by the diagonal free action of the circle
S^{1}
, and the complex structure is the one of the quotient of
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
by the holomorphic torus-exponential action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Statement (1) follows by comparing the definitions of
P(\mathcal{E})
and
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
, see (
10.2
) and (
2.2
).

To prove (2), first observe that the imbrication condition is equivalent to the condition that
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{J}}
have a common interior point for any
I,J\in\mathcal{K}
(compare Theorem
9.2
). The latter condition is equivalent to

\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
being a triangulated configuration by Proposition
4.4
. Now the fan is complete if and only if the substitute existence condition is satisfied, see Proposition
6.2
.

For (3), observe that
P(\mathcal{E})=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\mathcal{N}=P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
. Hence,
\mathcal{N}=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\widetilde{V}^{\prime})
. The latter is the quotient
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/S^{1}
by Proposition
12.4
.
∎

By Proposition
13.2
, every LVMB manifold is the base of a principal
S^{1}
-bundle with total space a moment-angle manifold. There is also an entirely holomorphic version of this construction:

Proposition 13.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
be an LVMB manifold and
\mathcal{K}
the corresponding simplicial complex (
13.2
) on
[m]
. Then there is a holomorphic principal bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}\to\mathcal{N}
with fibre a one-dimensional complex torus and total space a complex moment-angle manifold corresponding to

with one added ghost vertex.

Proof.

Let
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
be the triangulated configuration corresponding to
\mathcal{N}
. We add a ghost vertex to
\mathcal{K}
. The triangulated configuration
\{\mathcal{K}_{+},\mathrm{A}_{+}\}
, where
\mathrm{A}_{+}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m},\bf 0\}
, defines the same fan, see Construction

. We have

defines a complex structure on
V_{+}
. Denote the resulting complex spaces by

. Then we have a diagram of holomorphic quotients (

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\widehat{\Sigma}}=P(\mathcal{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{+}}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}}

An
LVM manifold
is an LVMB manifold whose corresponding data

\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
define a normal fan. The original construction
[
34
]
is as follows.

Construction 13.4

(LVM manifold)
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m}\}
be a point configuration in a complex vector space
(\widetilde{V}^{\prime})^{*}
of real dimension
k-1
satisfying the two conditions:

[
Siegel condition
]

\mathbf{0}\in\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}
;

[
weak hyperbolicity
]

if
\mathbf{0}\in\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{J}
, then
|J|\geqslant k
.

Let

\mathcal{E}=\{J\subset[m]\colon\mathbf{0}\in\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{J},\;|J|=k\}.

It is shown in
[
34
]
that the holomorphic exponential action
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}\times P(\mathcal{E})\to P(\mathcal{E})
given by (
13.1
) is free and the quotient
P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
is identified with the nondegenerate intersection of projective quadrics

(13.3)

\mathcal{N}=\bigl\{[z_{1}\colon\cdots\colon z_{m}]\in\mathbb{C}P^{m-1}\colon\gamma^{\prime}_{1}|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+\gamma^{\prime}_{m}|z_{m}|^{2}=0\bigr\}.

More precisely
\mathcal{N}\in P(\mathcal{E})
, and each orbit of the
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
-action on
P(\mathcal{E})
intersects
\mathcal{N}
transversely at a single point. Therefore, the compact manifold
\mathcal{N}
acquires a complex structure as the holomorphic quotient
P(\mathcal{E})/\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
. It is called the
LVM manifold
corresponding to the point configuration
\Gamma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{\prime}_{m}\}
satisfying the Siegel and weak hyperbolicity conditions.

Now let
V=V^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\\
1\end{pmatrix}
, let
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
be the Gale dual configuration to
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
, and let
\mathcal{K}
be the simplicial complex given by (
13.2
). By the holomorphic versions of Theorems
8.7
and
9.3
, the data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
define the normal fan of a simple polytope

given by (
8.1
), and and the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/(\mathbb{R}_{>}\times\widetilde{V}^{\prime})
is diffeomorphic to the nondegenerate intersection of Hermitian quadrics

(13.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrcl}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\colon&\gamma^{\prime}_{1}|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+\gamma^{\prime}_{m}|z_{m}|^{2}&=&\mathbf{0},\\[2.84526pt]
&|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{m}|^{2}&=&1.\end{array}\right\}

The latter is known as the
link of a system of special Hermitian quadrics
. The manifold (
13.3
) is obtained by taking further quotient by the diagonal circle. It follows that an LVM manifold is an LVMB manifold whose defining fan data
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is that of a normal fan.

LVM manifolds can also be characterised within LVMB manifolds in terms of the canonical foliation
\mathcal{F}
on an LVMB manifold, introduced by Cupit-Foutou and Zaffran in

is transversely Kähler if and only if the LVMB manifold is LVM.

Analytic subsets and invariants of the complex structure of these manifolds, including the Hodge numbers and Dolbeault cohomology, holomorphic automorphism groups and moduli spaces of complex structures are currently the subject of active research. We refer to

for the aspects of complex geometry of these manifolds.

14.
Toric varieties and irrational deformations

Complete nonsingular toric varieties are defined by nonsingular rational fans and can be obtained as partial quotients
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}/T_{V}
; this corresponds to the case when the entire space
V
is rational with respect to the vector configuration
\Gamma
. By deforming the configuration
\Gamma
we obtain holomorphic foliated manifolds
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}},\mathcal{F})
which model irrational deformations of simplicial toric varieties.

14.1.
Toric varieties as partial quotients

Assume that the whole space
V
is rational with respect to a spanning vector configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
, see Construction
11.2
. Then

(14.1)

L=\{{\textbf{{v}}}\in V\colon\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\;\text{ for }k=1,\ldots,m\}.

is a full rank lattice in
V
, and
L^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\rangle
is the dual lattice. The lattice
L
defines the algebraic torus

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and the compact torus
T_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
of dimension
k=\dim V
with a product decomposition
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=V\times T_{L}
as Lie groups.

The Gale dual rational vector configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
in
W^{*}
spans a full rank lattice
M^{*}
, which we denote by
N
.

The torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
embeds in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
as the closed holomorphic subgroup

(14.2)

G=(\varGamma^{*}|_{L}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times})(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L})=\exp\varGamma^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\mathbb{C}})\\
=\exp(\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}A_{\mathbb{C}}\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to W^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}).

This subgroup is in fact algebraic, as it can be written as

(14.3)

G=\Bigl\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}\colon\prod_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}^{\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle}=1,\quad{\textbf{{w}}}\in M\Bigr\}.

The torus-exponential action (
12.2
) becomes the algebraic action of

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Given a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
[m]
, we obtain an algebraic action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
on
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})
by restriction. By Theorem
12.2
, this action is proper if and only if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a triangulated configuration, in which case the action is almost free with finite stabilisers.

When the vectors of the configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
are primitive in the lattice
N
spanned by
\mathrm{A}
, the (geometric) quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
by a proper algebraic action of

\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
is the
toric variety

X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to the rational simplicial fan
\Sigma
defined by

\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}

[
16
, §5.1]
. The algebraic torus
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
acts on
X_{\Sigma}=U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
with a Zariski open orbit. The toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is nonsingular if
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is a nonsingular triangulated configuration (this implies that the action of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
is free and the vectors

{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
are primitive in the lattice
N
).

More generally, when the vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
are not necessarily primitive in the lattice
N
, the quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
is still the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to the fan
\Sigma
defined by
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
, yet with the non-standard orbifold structure. This phenomenon is treated in
[
35
, §4]
, see also
[
8
]
. We illustrate it on a simple example.

Example 14.1
.

Consider the two-vector configuration
\mathrm{A}=\{2,-1\}
in one-dimensional space
W^{*}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{K}=\{\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\}\}
. Then the triangulated configuration
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
defines a complete nonsingular fan in
\mathbb{R}
, but the vector
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1}=2
is non-primitive in the lattice
N=\mathbb{Z}
spanned by
\mathrm{A}
. The Gale dual configuration is
\Gamma=\{1,2\}
and the group (
14.2
) is given by

G=\{(e^{v},e^{2v})=(t,t^{2})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}\}.

The
G
-action on
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
has a nontrivial stabiliser of order
2
at the point
(0,1)
.
The quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}/G
is the projective line
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
as a variety, but
but comes equipped with an orbifold structure with one singular point of index
2
.

When
V
is entirely rational with respect to
\Gamma
, the commutative diagram of holomorphic quotients (
12.4
) takes the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\Sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/\widetilde{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/\widetilde{T}_{L}}

Here
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is the moment-angle manifold with the complex structure defined by the holomorphic quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\widetilde{V}
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to X_{\Sigma}
is a holomorphic (Seifert) principal bundle with fibre a holomorphic compact torus
\widetilde{T}_{L}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}/\widetilde{V}
.

A complex structure on
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
is defined by a triangulated configuration
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
and a choice of complex structure on
V
, see §
10
. When the fan
\Sigma
defined by
\{\mathcal{K},\mathrm{A}\}
is rational, there is a holomorphic torus fibre bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to X_{\Sigma}
over the corresponding toric variety. A perturbation of the vector configuration
\mathrm{A}
destroys the rationality, the subgroup (
14.2
) ceases to be closed in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
, the closed holomorphic tori in the fibres of the bundle
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}\to X_{\Sigma}
“open up”, and the fibre bundle turns into a
holomorphic foliation

\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
with noncompact leaves
G/\widetilde{V}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is an example of the
canonical foliation
on a complex manifold, considered in
[
25
]
(see also
[
28
]
). The holomorphic foliated manifolds
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}},\mathcal{F})
therefore model
irrational deformations
of toric varieties, which have recently been studied from several different perspectives in
the works
[
5
,
7
,
41
,
29
,
28
,
6
]
.

On the other hand, the construction of toric varieties as quotients takes as input a lattice
N
and a rational fan
\Sigma
in the space
N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, which is
W^{*}
in our notation. The vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
are the primitive lattice vectors generating the one-dimensional cones of
\Sigma
. If the fan
\Sigma
is simplicial, the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
can be defined as the geometric quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/G
, where
G
is the algebraic subgroup of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{m}
given by (
14.3
). It may turn out that the vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
do not span the lattice
N
; in particular,
\mathbb{Z}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\rangle
may be a sublattice of finite index in
N
. In the latter case the group (
14.3
) is a product of an algebraic torus and a finite group
N/\mathbb{Z}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\rangle
. When we take as input a pair of Gale dual rational vector configurations
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{A}
, the only natural choice for a lattice
N
is
\mathbb{Z}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\rangle
, so the group (
14.3
) does not have finite factors and is isomorphic to the algebraic torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{L}
. To take account of all simplicial toric varieties, one can follow the approach of Prato
[
40
]
to
toric quasifolds
and include a finitely generated abelian subgroup
N
of
W^{*}
(a
quasilattice
) containing the vectors
{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}
as part of the input data. In this way, all complete simplicial toric varieties (orbifolds), as well as toric quasifolds, can be obtained as quotients of LVMB manifolds
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
.

14.2.
The nef and ample cone of a toric variety

A toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is defined by a lattice
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and a fan
\Sigma
in
N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=W^{*}
which is rational with respect to
N
. Let
\{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{A}\}
be the fan data of
\Sigma
, where

\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
is the set of primitive generators of one-dimensional cones of
\Sigma
and
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathrm{A}
-closed collection of subsets of
[m]
, see Section
4
. We continue assuming that
\mathrm{A}
spans
W^{*}
. The quotient construction
[
16
, §5.1]
identifies
X_{\Sigma}
with the good categorical quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\!\!/G
, where
\mathcal{K}
is the smallest simplicial complex containing
\mathcal{C}
and
G
is given by (
14.3
). The categorical quotient
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K})/\!\!/G
is geometric if and only if the fan
\Sigma
is simplicial (so that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{K}
).

Let
M=N^{*}
be the dual lattice in
W
. The map
A^{*}\colon W\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
takes

{\textbf{{w}}}\in W
to
(\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{m},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle)
. It can be interpreted in terms of torus-invariant Weil divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
. Namely, we identify the
i
th standard basis vector
{\textbf{{e}}}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with the irreducible torus-invariant divisor
D_{i}
corresponding the the
i
th ray of
\Sigma
(with primitive generator
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
). Then
A^{*}
takes
{\textbf{{w}}}\in M
to the principal divisor
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle D_{i}
of the character
\chi^{{\textbf{{w}}}}
of the torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{N}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
corresponding to
{\textbf{{w}}}\in M=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{N},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. The quotient
\mathbb{Z}^{m}/A^{*}(M)
is the
class group

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})
, and we have an exact sequence of abelian groups

0\longrightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{Z}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})\longrightarrow 0.

If
N=\mathbb{Z}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\rangle
, then the map
A^{*}\colon M\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}
is split injective and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})
is identified with the lattice
L^{*}
spanned by the Gale dual rational configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
in
V^{*}
. In general,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})
is isomorphic to the sum of
L^{*}
and the finite group
N/\mathbb{Z}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\rangle
. In any case, we can identify
\gamma_{i}
with the divisor class
[D_{i}]
in
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=V^{*}
. Tensoring the exact sequence above with
\mathbb{R}
we obtain

(14.4)

0\longrightarrow W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R}^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varGamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}V^{*}\longrightarrow 0.

In what follows we assume that the support
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma\subset W^{*}
of the fan
\Sigma
is convex of full dimension. Torus-invariant Cartier divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
correspond to piecewise linear functions
\varphi
on
|\Sigma|
which are linear on the cones of
\Sigma
and take integer values on the lattice
N
. The restriction of such a function
\varphi
to a cone

\sigma\in\Sigma
is given by
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\rangle
for
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\sigma
and some
{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\in M
. A piecewise linear function
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
corresponds to the Cartier divisor
D=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})D_{i}
. Conversely, a torus-invariant Cartier divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}D_{i}
which is given by a collection of characters
\chi^{{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}}
on the affine pieces
X_{\sigma}\subset X_{\Sigma}
(the
Cartier data
) corresponds to the piecewise linear function
\varphi_{D}\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\varphi_{D}({\textbf{{a}}})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\rangle
for
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\sigma
.

The
Picard group

\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}(X_{\Sigma})\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}(X_{\Sigma})
consists of classes of Cartier divisors. It has a finite index in
\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}(X_{\Sigma})
if and only if the fan
\Sigma
is simplicial
[
16
, Proposition 4.2.7]
. When
\Sigma
simplicial, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=V^{*}
, while in general
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is a proper subspace in
V^{*}
.

A piecewise linear function
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\rangle
for
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\sigma
is convex if and only if
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})\leqslant\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\rangle
for all
{\textbf{{a}}}\in|\Sigma|
, and
\varphi
is called
strictly convex
if
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})<\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{\sigma}\rangle
for all
{\textbf{{a}}}\in|\Sigma|\setminus\sigma
. A fan
\Sigma
with convex support admits a strictly convex piecewise linear function
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P}
is the normal fan of a convex polyhedron
P
. Namely, for a strictly convex
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
one has that
\Sigma
is the normal fan of the polyhedron

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle\geqslant\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})\text{ for }{\textbf{{a}}}\in|\Sigma|\}

\displaystyle=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\}.

Conversely, if
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P}
, then the
support function
of
P
given by
\varphi_{P}({\textbf{{a}}})=\min_{{\textbf{{w}}}\in P}\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle
is defined on

|\Sigma_{P}|
, linear on the cones of
\Sigma_{P}
and strictly convex.

A torus-invariant Cartier divisor
D
on
X_{\Sigma}
is basepoint free if and only if the corresponding function
\varphi_{D}\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
is convex
[
16
, Theorem 6.1.7]
. When the fan
\Sigma
is complete, a torus-invariant Cartier divisor
D
on
X_{\Sigma}
is ample if and only if the corresponding function
\varphi_{D}\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly convex
[
16
, Theorem 6.1.14]
. In particular, a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is projective if and only if
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a convex polytope
P
.

The classes of basepoint free divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
span a cone in
V^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Cl}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, called the
nef cone
and denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
(a Cartier divisor on a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is numerically effective if and only if it is basepoint free). The set of positive multiples of ample divisor classes on a complete toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is called the
ample cone
and denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})
. The variety
X_{\Sigma}
is projective if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})\neq\varnothing
. (Note that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})
is not precisely a cone; it is a subset in
V^{*}
whose closure is a polyhedral cone or empty.)

The nef cone and the ample cone can be described effectively in terms of the Gale dual cones of
\Sigma
. The proposition below is related to the criterion for a fan to be a normal fan in Theorem
8.3
(see also
[
1
, Proposition 2.4.2.6]
):

Proposition 14.2
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
be a rational fan in
W^{*}
with full-dimensional convex support, and let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be the Gale dual rational configuration in
V^{*}
.

(1)

The nef cone of the toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is given by

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})=\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}.

(2)

If
\Sigma
is complete, then the ample cone of
X_{\Sigma}
is given by

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})=\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}

and is nonempty if and only if
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polytope.

(3)

If
\Sigma
is complete and simplicial, then
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})
is the interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
, and
X_{\Sigma}
is projective if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
has full dimension in
V^{*}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\gamma_{i}\in V^{*}
is identified with the divisor class
[D_{i}]
.

(1) Let
[D]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}[D_{i}]\in V^{*}
be a class of base free divisor, so that
b_{i}=-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})
for a convex piecewise linear function
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
. Since
\varphi
is linear on any
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
for
I\in\mathcal{C}
, we get
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle
for some
{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\in W
and
i\in I
. Then

[D]=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})[D_{i}]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle[D_{i}]=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}(\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i}))\gamma_{i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}

for any
I\in\mathcal{C}
. Here the first identity above holds since
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle D_{i}
is a principal divisor (and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle\gamma_{i}=0
by Gale duality), and the inclusion holds because
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})\geqslant 0
for a convex
\varphi
. It follows that
[D]\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
.

To prove the opposite inclusion, it is enough to show that any rational vector

\gamma\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
is a positive multiple of a basepoint free divisor class. We write
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}[D_{i}]
with rational
b_{i}
. Take
I\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
, we can also write

\gamma=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}b^{\prime}_{i}[D_{i}]
with rational
b^{\prime}_{i}\geqslant 0
. Now

\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}b^{\prime}_{i}D_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}D_{i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\varGamma
(see (
14.4
)), so that there is a rational
{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\in W
such that

(14.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle D_{i}=\sum_{i\in\widehat{I}}b^{\prime}_{i}D_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}D_{i}.

The data
\{{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\colon I\in\mathcal{C}\}
define a piecewise linear function
\varphi\colon|\Sigma|\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle
for
{\textbf{{a}}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\mathrm{A}_{I}
. From (
14.5
) we obtain
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})=-b_{i}
for
i\in I
. Since each
{\textbf{{a}}}_{i}
belongs to a cone, we obtain
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})=-b_{i}
for any
i
. Identity (
14.5
) also implies that

\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})=\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle+b_{i}=b^{\prime}_{i}\geqslant 0\quad\text{for }i\in\widehat{I},

so that
\varphi
is convex. Since
\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})
is rational for any
i
, multiplying by a common denominator we achieve that
\varphi
is integer on
N
. It follows that
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}[D_{i}]=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})[D_{i}]
is a basepoint free divisor class up to a positive multiple.

The proof of (2) is similar: non-strict inequalities like
\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}_{I}\rangle-\varphi({\textbf{{a}}}_{i})\geqslant 0
and
b^{\prime}_{i}\geqslant 0
are replaced by strict ones and the function
\varphi
is now strictly convex. The fact that
\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{relint}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
is nonempty if and only if
\Sigma
is the normal fan of a polytope is proved in Theorem
8.3
.

(3) When
\Sigma
is simplicial, each
\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}
is a full-dimensional cone in
V^{*}
for
I\in\mathcal{C}
, and its relative interior is the interior. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})=\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{int}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{int}\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{C}}\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}\Gamma_{\widehat{I}}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{int}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma}),

and this is nonempty if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
has full dimension.
∎

When
X_{\Sigma}
is not simplicial,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
cannot have full dimension in
V^{*}
, as
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})\subset\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is a proper subspace in
V^{*}
. However,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
can have full dimension in
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. When
X_{\Sigma}
is projective,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Ample}(X_{\Sigma})
is the relative interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
(or the interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
in
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
). On the other hand,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
may have full dimension in
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
with

still not being projective. To illustrate this, we elaborate on an example due to Fujino

14.3.
The Kähler cone and symplectic reduction

A smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is a Kähler manifold, so in particular it has a symplectic structure, obtained by restricting the Fubini–Study form via an embedding into a projective space. The algebraic torus action on
X_{\Sigma}
restricts to the action of the compact torus
T_{N}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}\cong T^{n}
which is Hamiltonian with respect to the symplectic structure on
X_{\Sigma}
. On the other hand, by the Delzant theorem
[
19
]
, any
2n
-dimensional compact connected symplectic manifold
M
with an effective Hamiltonian action of
T^{n}
(a
Hamiltonian toric manifold
) is determined up to an equivariant symplectomorphism by the image of its moment map
\mu\colon M\to\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lie}(T^{n})^{*}=W
. This image is a convex polytope
P
with nonsingular rational normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
. Therefore, any Hamiltonian toric manifold is the underlying symplectic manifold of a smooth projective toric variety. It follows that a smooth complete toric variety is projective if and only if it has a symplectic structure.

When
X_{\Sigma}
is complete and smooth, the interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
consists of Kähler classes in
V^{*}=H^{2}(X_{\Sigma};\mathbb{R})
and is known as the
Kähler cone
. It is empty when
X_{\Sigma}
is nonprojective. The space
V^{*}
contains the lattice
L^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Pic}(X_{\Sigma})=H^{2}(X_{\Sigma};\mathbb{Z})
, and lattice points in the interior of
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Nef}(X_{\Sigma})
correspond to Kähler classes coming from ample divisors on
X_{\Sigma}
.

A Hamiltonian toric manifold
M
can be reconstructed from its moment polytope
P
using
symplectic reduction
. We outline the construction below; the details can be found in
[
2
, Chapter 6]
and
[
23
, Chapter 1]
.

Construction 14.4

(Hamiltonian toric manifolds as symplectic quotients)
.

The symplectic structure on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
with coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
,
z_{k}=x_{k}+iy_{k}
, is given by the
2
-form

\omega=-i\sum_{i=1}^{m}dz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}=-2\sum_{i=1}^{m}dx_{k}\wedge dy_{k}.

The coordinatewise action of the torus
T^{m}
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is Hamiltonian with the moment map given by

\mu\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lie}(T^{m})^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\mapsto(|z_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|z_{m}|^{2}).

A rational vector configuration
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
in
V^{*}\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
L^{*}=\mathbb{Z}\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and the dual lattice (
14.1
) defines a torus
T_{L}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}
and an inclusion of tori
\varGamma^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1}\colon T_{L}\to T^{m}
, so that

(14.6)

K=(\varGamma^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}S^{1})(T_{L})=\bigl\{\bigl(e^{2\pi i\langle\gamma_{1},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\langle\gamma_{m},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle}\bigr)\colon{\textbf{{v}}}\in L\bigr\}

is a
k
-dimensional subtorus in
T^{m}
.

The moment map for the Hamiltonian action of
K
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is obtained by restriction and is given by the composite
\mathbb{C}^{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lie}(T^{m})^{*}\to\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lie}(T_{L})^{*}
. The second map here is identified with
\varGamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to V^{*}
, so we obtain

\mu_{\Gamma}\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to V^{*},\quad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\mapsto|z_{1}|^{2}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+|z_{m}|^{2}\gamma_{m}.

The real version of this map was considered in Subsection
8.2
and its regular values are described in terms of
\Gamma
in Theorems
8.4
and
8.7
.

If
\delta\in V^{*}
is a regular value of the moment map
\mu_{\Gamma}
, then
the intersection of Hermitian quadrics

\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\colon\gamma_{1}|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}|z_{m}|^{2}=\delta\}

is nondegenerate, so it is a smooth submanifold of dimension
2m-k=m+n
. The action of
K\cong T_{L}
on
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is almost free. The stabiliser of a point
{\textbf{{z}}}\in\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is a finite group
L_{I}/L
(see Proposition
12.1
), where
I=\{i\in[m]\colon z_{i}=0\}
and

L_{I}=\{{\textbf{{v}}}\in V\colon\langle\gamma_{k},{\textbf{{v}}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\;\text{ for }k\notin I\}.

The restriction of the symplectic form
\omega
to
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
degenerates precisely along the orbits of the
K
-action.
If the action of
K
on
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is free, then the quotient
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K
is a manifold, and there is a unique symplectic form
\omega^{\prime}
on
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K
satisfying
p^{*}\omega^{\prime}=i^{*}\omega
, where
i\colon\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the inclusion and
p\colon\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)\to\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K
is the projection. The symplectic manifold
M_{\Gamma,\delta}=\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K
is referred to as the
symplectic quotient
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by
K
.

The
2n
-dimensional manifold
M_{\Gamma,\delta}=\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K
has a residual Hamiltonian action of the quotient
n
-torus
T_{N}=T^{m}/K
. The image of the corresponding moment map
\mu_{N}\colon M_{\Gamma,\delta}\to\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lie}(T_{N})^{*}=W
is the convex polytope given by

(14.7)

P=\{{\textbf{{w}}}\in W\colon\langle{\textbf{{a}}}_{i},{\textbf{{w}}}\rangle+b_{i}\geqslant 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m\},

where
\mathrm{A}=\{{\textbf{{a}}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{{a}}}_{m}\}
is the Gale dual configuration of
\Gamma
and
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
, see Theorem
8.4
. (Note that
P
is defined by
(\Gamma,\delta)
up to a translation in
W
.) The normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
of
P
is defined by the triangulated configuration
(\mathcal{K}_{P},\mathrm{A})
, and it is nonsingular if and only if the action of
K
on
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is free, see Proposition
11.4
. A polytope
P
with a nonsingular normal fan is called
Delzant
. (Note that the vertices of a Delzant polytope do not necessarily lie in the lattice
M=N^{*}\subset W
.)

Conversely, given a Delzant polytope
P
, the Gale dual configuration
\Gamma
defines a
k
-dimensional subtorus (
14.6
) in
T^{m}
, and the regular value
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\gamma_{i}
of the moment map
\mu_{\Gamma}
, as in Theorem
8.4
. The level set
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
is identified with the moment-angle manifold
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}_{P}}
(Theorem
10.11
).

By a theorem of Delzant
[
19
]
, every Hamiltonian toric manifold
M
can be obtained as a symplectic quotient
M_{\Gamma,\delta}
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by a torus, so
M
is determined up to an equivariant symplectomorphism by its moment image
P
.

The symplectic and algebraic quotient constructions are related as follows.

Theorem 14.5
.

Let
P
be a polytope (
14.7
) with nonsingular normal fan
\Sigma_{P}
. The inclusion
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)\subset U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})
induces a
T_{N}
-equivariant diffeomorphism

M_{\Gamma,\delta}=\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)/K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})/G=X_{\Sigma_{P}}

between the corresponding Hamiltonian toric manifold and smooth projective toric variety.

Furthermore, if the polytope
P
has vertices in the lattice
M\subset W
, then the symplectic structure on
X_{\Sigma_{P}}
defined by the ample divisor corresponding to the support function
\varphi_{P}
coincides with that of
M_{\Gamma,\delta}
.

Proof.

To prove the first statement we need to check that each
G
-orbit of
U_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{K}_{P})
intersects
\mu_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\delta)
at a single
K

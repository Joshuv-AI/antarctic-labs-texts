Classifying weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank 3 3
Zhengning Hu, Rohan Joshi

Abstract
. We provide a systematic method to classify all smooth weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank 3 3 in any dimension using Macaulay2, and describe the classification explicitly in dimensions 3 3 and 4 4 . There are 28 28 and 114 114 isomorphism classes of rank 3 3 weak Fano toric threefolds and fourfolds, respectively.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14M25, 14-04

1.
Introduction

Toric varieties are algebraic varieties that contain an algebraic torus as an open dense subset such that the action of the torus on itself extends to the whole variety.
Normal toric varieties can be described by combinatorial data, specifically by a fan sitting in a real vector space generated by a lattice.
Normal toric varieties admit a computational theory, where geometric properties, topological invariants, sheaf cohomology, etc. can be computed from this combinatorial data.
Many of these methods are implemented in the
NormalToricVarieties
package
[
Smi
]
in computer algebra system Macaulay2
[
GS
]
.

A Fano (
resp.
weak Fano) variety is a smooth projective algebraic variety
X
whose anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample (
resp.
nef and big).
There are finitely many isomorphism classes of weak Fano toric varieties in any dimension.
In this paper we consider the problem of classifying weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank
3
up to isomorphism.
By work of Batyrev
[
Bat91
]
, any smooth projective toric variety of Picard rank
3
over an algebraically closed field either has three primitive collections, in which case it is a toric projective bundle, or has five primitive collections, in which case it can be generated by a combinatorial recipe described by Batyrev.
At the 2024 Macaulay2 workshop in Salt Lake City, the authors and other members of the Toric Varieties working group wrote functions for the
NormalToricVarieties
package that construct toric projective bundles as well as implement Batyrev’s recipe.
We build on this work and prove bounds on the inputs to these functions such that all weak Fano outputs can be generated by bounded inputs.
We write Macaulay2 functions for exhaustively constructing all weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank
3
in any dimension and computing their numerical invariants. Using this, we describe explicitly all isomorphism classes of weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank
3
in dimensions
3
and
4
: see Appendices
C
and
D
.

In addition we explore the flops and (regular) blowdowns of these varieties. We write Macaulay2 functions which implement possible equivariant blowups and blowdowns based on work of Sato
[
Sat00
, Section 4]
.
Using these functions, we explicitly describe possible flops and blowdowns of toric varieties in our lists: see the column “Blowdown and flop” in Appendices
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
.
We also prove a converse to Bott-Steenbrink-Danilov vanishing
[
CLS11
, Theorem 9.3.1]
, characterizing Fano varieties among weak Fano toric varieties by the vanishing of a cohomology group.

In this paper we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of arbitrary characteristic.

The classification of Fano threefolds was completed by

Isk77
,
Isk78
,

]
: a description of all families of Fano threefolds is available on the website Fanography

]
(throughout this paper, Fano and weak Fano varieties are understood to be smooth and projective).
Fano toric varieties are classified up to dimension six, by Batyrev

]
, Sato
[

]
, and Øbro
[
Øbr07a
]
.
This classification is available online in the Graded Rings Database and is also incorporated into

.
Øbro claims in his thesis to have completed the classification up to dimension eight
[

]
.
Weak del Pezzo toric surfaces are classified in
[
Sat02a
]
.
Moreover,
[
Sat02a
]
also classifies
weakened
toric Fano threefolds: a weakened Fano variety is a weak Fano variety that is not Fano but can be deformed to a Fano variety under a small deformation.
Finally, weak Fano (not necessarily toric) threefolds of Picard rank

are classified except for a few remaining open cases in
[

]
and
[

2.
Primitive collections and primitive relations

Let
M\cong\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the character lattice of a
d
-dimensional torus
T
with dual lattice
N=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
We associate to a rational polyhedral fan
\Sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
the toric variety
X=X(\Sigma)
.
For every integer
1\leq l\leq d
, we denote by
\Sigma(l)
the set of cones in
\Sigma
of dimension
l
, and
\rays(\Sigma)
or
\rays(X)
the set of primitive generators of rays of
\Sigma
.

In this paper we shall consider smooth and proper (or projective) toric varieties
X=X(\Sigma)
over
k
where
\Sigma
is a regular and complete (or projective) fan in the vector space
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
We first review in this section some useful definitions and results we are going to use regarding primitive collections and primitive relations.
More details can be found in
[
CLS11
, Chapter 6]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X=X(\Sigma)
be a toric variety with
\Sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
its associated regular and complete fan.
We call
P=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\subseteq\rays(\Sigma)
a
primitive collection
of
\Sigma
if
P
does not generate a cone of
\Sigma
but every proper subset of
P
does.
We denote the set of primitive collections of
X
by
\PC(X)
.
Since
\Sigma
is complete, the vector
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}
is in the relative interior of a unique cone
\sigma(P)
.
Let
y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}
be the primitive generators of
\sigma(P)
.
Then
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}
can be uniquely written as a positive
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of
y_{1},\cdots,y_{m}
, that is

x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}y_{m}.

This linear relation among elements of
\rays(\Sigma)
is called the
primitive relation
associated to the primitive collection
P
.
We can consider
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}-(a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}y_{m})
as an element
r(P)\in N^{1}(X)^{\vee}=N_{1}(X)
, the group of
1
-cycles on
X
up to numerical equivalence.
We define the
degree
of
P
by

\deg P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-K_{X})\cdot r(P)=n-(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}).

Since a projective toric variety
X
is log Fano (
i.e.,
there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
X
such that the pair
(X,D)
is klt and
-(K_{X}+D)
is ample), the Cone Theorem implies the Mori cone (the closure of cone of curves in
N_{1}(X)
) of
X
, denoted by
\overline{\NE}(X)
, is rational polyhedral.
This can also be shown by more elementary means.
As stated in
[
Rei83
, Proposition 1.6]
, if
X
is a proper toric variety, every irreducible curve on
X
contained in a smallest stratum can be deformed in the stratum to a union of curves, each of which is contained in a lower dimensional substratum, by a
1
-parameter subgroup of the big torus with points of indeterminacy resolved.
Thus every effective
1
-cycle on
X
is rationally (and numerically) equivalent to a positive linear combination of some torus-invariant strata.
Since the cone of curves of
X
is spanned by torus-invariant curves which is a finite set, its Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(X)
is rational polyhedral.

The following proposition gives an explicit description of the Mori cone in terms of primitive relations.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.4.11]

Let
X=X(\Sigma)
where
\Sigma
is a projective regular fan. Then the Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(X)
is generated by the elements
r(P)\in N_{1}(X)
for all primitive collections
P
of
\Sigma
.

It is worth noting that not every primitive relation is contained in an extremal ray of the Mori cone, for example, in Section
4
, the Batyrev varieties have five primitive collections, but only three of them are contained in extremal rays.
We illustrate this fact by an example below.

Consider

Such

By Theorem
2.2
, we have the following criterion for a smooth projective toric variety to be Fano or weak Fano.

Corollary 2.4
.

A smooth projective toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is Fano (
resp.
weak Fano) if and only if for all primitive collections
P\in\PC(X)
,
\deg P>0
(
resp.

\geq 0
).

Let
Y
be a smooth toric variety and consider a collection of line bundles
\mathcal{O}(D_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{O}(D_{k})
on
Y
.
Since the divisor class group of a toric variety is generated by torus-invariant divisors, we may assume that the
D_{i}
’s are torus-invariant.
Then we can take the projectivization of the decomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{O}(D_{0})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{k})
which is a smooth toric variety
X=\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}(D_{0})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{k}))
. Since tensoring a vector bundle by a line bundle doesn’t change the total space of its projectivization, we may assume
D_{0}=0
.
We will call varieties constructed in this way
toric projective bundles
. We can use primitive relations to state a criterion for a toric variety to be a toric projective bundle.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
Bat91
, Proposition 4.1]

The toric variety
X=X(\Sigma)
is a toric
\mathbb{P}^{k}
-bundle if and only if there exists a primitive collection
P=\{x_{0},\cdots,x_{k}\}\in\PC(X)
such that
x_{0}+\cdots+x_{k}=0
,
and
P
is disjoint from any other primitive collection.

Definition 2.6
.

A fan is a
splitting fan
if any two distinct primitive collections are disjoint.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
Bat91
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
\Sigma
be a splitting fan. Then the corresponding toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is a projectivization of a decomposable bundle over a toric variety associated to a splitting fan of a smaller dimension.

Varieties of the form above which are iterated toric projective bundles are sometimes called “generalized Bott towers” in the literature.

Kleinschmidt and Sturmfels showed that a smooth proper toric variety of Picard rank
3
is always projective
[
KS91
]
. Batyrev proved a strong theorem classifying smooth projective varieties of Picard rank
3
into two types, based on the number of primitive collections.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
Bat91
, Theorem 5.7]

A smooth projective toric variety
X
of Picard rank
3
has either three or five primitive collections.
If
X
has three primitive collections, then
\Sigma
is a splitting fan, so
X=\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{k}))
for some toric divisors
D_{1},\cdots,D_{k}\in\Pic(Y)
, where
Y
is a smooth projective toric variety of Picard rank
2
.

When
X
has five primitive collections, it can be constructed using a combinatorial recipe described in
[
Bat91
, Theorem 6.6]
.
In order to pursue our classification of weak Fano toric varieties of Picard rank
3
, we will consider the cases of three and five primitive collections separately in the next two sections.

3.
Projective bundles

Let
X
be a weak Fano toric variety of Picard rank 3 with three primitive collections.
According to Theorem
2.8
, we know there exists a smooth projective toric variety
Y
and toric divisors
D_{1},\cdots,D_{k}
on
Y
such that
X\cong\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{k}))
.
In fact, the toric variety
Y
must also be weak Fano by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a decomposable vector bundle on a projective toric variety
X
so that the projectivization
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is a toric projective bundle. If
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is Fano (
resp.
weak Fano), then
X
is also Fano (
resp.
weak Fano).

Proof.

The Fano case is proved in
[
SW90
, Theorem 1.6]
. Now let’s assume
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is weak Fano.
We denote by
p\colon\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})\to X
the projection.
Since
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is a projective toric variety, the discussion after Definition
2.1
shows that the Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E}))
is generated by the classes of finitely many rational curves
C_{1},\cdots,C_{n}
on
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
.
By the proof of
[
SW90
, Lemma 1.5]
, we have
p^{*}(-K_{X})\cdot C_{i}\geq 0
for each
i
since
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is weak Fano.
Now for any integral curve
D
in
X
, there exists an integral curve
C
in
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
such that
p(C)=D
.
Together with projection formula, we have
0\leq p^{*}(-K_{X})\cdot C=(-K_{X})\cdot p_{*}C=m(-K_{X}\cdot D)
for some positive integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and thus it implies that
-K_{X}\cdot D\geq 0
.
Therefore
X
is also weak Fano.
∎

3.1.
Toric varieties of Picard rank
2

Kleinschmidt in
[
Kle88
]
studied smooth proper toric varieties of Picard rank
2
, and proved that they are all projective bundles of decomposable vector bundles over projective spaces.
That is, if
X
is such a variety of dimension
d^{\prime}
, then there exists a positive integer
r<d^{\prime}
and a nondecreasing sequence of nonnegative integers
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r}
such that

X\cong X_{r}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(a_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(a_{d^{\prime}-r})).

We call varieties constructed in this way
Kleinschmidt varieties
.

Furthermore, these
r
,
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r}
are uniquely determined, except for the isomorphism

X_{r}(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{\text{$(d^{\prime}-r)$ copies}})\cong X_{d^{\prime}-r}(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{\text{$r$ copies}})

which corresponds to the isomorphism switching the factors in a direct product of projective spaces:
\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{d^{\prime}-r}\cong\mathbb{P}^{d^{\prime}-r}\times\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

The projective bundle
X
has
d^{\prime}+2
rays which can be decomposed into a disjoint union of two primitive collections
\rays(X)=\mathcal{X}\sqcup\mathcal{Y}
where

\mathcal{X}=\{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1},x_{1},\cdots,x_{d^{\prime}-r}\},\quad\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{r+1}\},

and the primitive generators of the rays in
\rays(X)
are explicitly given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}&=(-1,\cdots,-1,&0,\cdots,0)\\
x_{1}&=(1,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0)\\
&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{d^{\prime}-r}&=(0,\cdots,1,&0,\cdots,0)\\
y_{1}&=(0,\cdots,0,&1,\cdots,0)\\
&\vdots&\vdots\\
y_{r}&=(0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,1)\\
y_{r+1}&=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r},&-1,\cdots,-1).\end{array}

The primitive relations corresponding to primitive collections
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are given respectively by

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r+1}x_{i}=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r}a_{i}x_{i}.

Thus
X
is Fano (
resp.
weak Fano) if
r+1-\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r}a_{i}>0
(
resp.

\geq 0
). We give complete lists of weak Fano toric threefolds and fourfolds of Picard rank
2
in Appendices
A
and
B
respectively.

The projective bundle
X
has maximal cones given by
\Cone(\rays(X)\smallsetminus\{x_{i},y_{j}\})
where
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
and
y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
.
Thus any pair
\{D_{x_{i}},D_{y_{j}}\}
forms a basis for
\Pic(X)
. To be consistent with the construction in Section
3.2
, we use the basis
\{D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}},D_{y_{r+1}}\}
to describe elements of
\Pic(X)
going forward.

Proposition 3.2
.

The Cartier divisor
D=bD_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+cD_{y_{r+1}}
on
X
is ample (
resp.
nef) if
b>0
(
resp.

b\geq 0
) and
c>0
(
resp.

c\geq 0
).

Proof.

Let
D=bD_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+cD_{y_{r+1}}
.
By
[
CLS11
, Theorem 6.4.9]
,
D
is ample (
resp.
nef) if and only if
b>0
(
resp.

b\geq 0
) and
c>0
(
resp.

c\geq 0
) according to the primitive relations (
1
) and (
2
).
∎

3.2.
Projective bundles of Picard rank
3

By Proposition
3.1
, any weak Fano toric variety of Picard rank
3
with three primitive collections is the projectivization of a decomposable vector bundle over a weak Fano toric variety of Picard rank
2
.
We can describe all such varieties of dimension
d
as follows.

Let
r
,
d^{\prime}
be positive integers with
r<d^{\prime}<d
and let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r}
be a nondecreasing sequence of nonegative integers.
Let
X
be the projective bundle over
Y=\mathbb{P}^{r}
defined as in Section
3.1
by

X=\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(a_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(a_{d^{\prime}-r})).

Then given integers
b_{i}
and
c_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d-d^{\prime}
, let
Z
be the
\mathbb{P}^{d-d^{\prime}}
-bundle over
X
defined by

Z=\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{1},c_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{d-d^{\prime}},c_{d-d^{\prime}}))

where we assume all
b_{i}\geq 0
and the pairs
\{(b_{i},c_{i})\}
are ordered lexicographically.
Here by
\mathcal{O}(b,c)
we mean the line bundle associated to the divisor
bD_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+cD_{y_{r+1}}
, using the basis for
\Pic(X)
described in Section
3.1
.
By
[
Bat91
]
, the toric variety
Z
has three disjoint primitive collections

\rays(Z)=\mathcal{X}\sqcup\mathcal{Y}\sqcup\mathcal{Z}=\{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1},x_{1},\cdots,x_{d^{\prime}-r}\}\sqcup\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{r+1}\}\sqcup\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{d-d^{\prime}+1}\},

with the primitive relation
\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}+1}z_{i}=0
endowing
Z
with the
\mathbb{P}^{d-d^{\prime}}
-bundle structure.
The maximal cones of
Z
are also easy to describe.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\rays(Z)=\mathcal{X}\sqcup\mathcal{Y}\sqcup\mathcal{Z}
be a disjoint union of three primitive collections each of which consists of at least two rays.
Then the maximal cones of the toric variety
Z
are all sets of type
\Cone(\rays(Z)\smallsetminus\{x_{i},y_{j},z_{k}\})
where
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
,
y_{j}\in\mathcal{Y}
and
z_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}
.

We give the other two primitive relations in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
b_{i}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq d-d^{\prime}
. Besides
\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}+1}z_{i}=0
, two other primitive relations of
Z
are expressed as follows.

(a)

If
c_{i}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq d-d^{\prime}
, then we have

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r+1}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}b_{i}z_{i},

and

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r}a_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}c_{i}z_{i}.

(b)

If there exists an index
1\leq j\leq d-d^{\prime}
such that
c_{j}<0
, then instead of the relation (
4
), we have the following relation: letting
l
be the index such that
c_{l}=\min\{c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq d-d^{\prime}\}<0
,

(5)

\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r}a_{i}x_{i}+(-c_{l})z_{d-d^{\prime}+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}(c_{i}-c_{l})z_{i}.

Proof.

Since the rays of
Z
are given by

\begin{array}[]{llll}x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}&=(-1,\cdots,-1,&0,\cdots,0,&b_{1},\cdots,b_{d-d^{\prime}})\\
x_{1}&=(1,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0)\\
&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{d^{\prime}-r}&=(0,\cdots,1,&0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0)\\
y_{1}&=(0,\cdots,0,&1,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0)\\
&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
y_{r}&=(0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,1,&0,\cdots,0)\\
y_{r+1}&=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{d^{\prime}-r},&-1,\cdots,-1,&c_{1},\cdots,c_{d-d^{\prime}})\\
z_{1}&=(0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0,&1,\cdots,0)\\
&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
z_{d-d^{\prime}}&=(0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,1)\\
z_{d-d^{\prime}+1}&=(0,\cdots,0,&0,\cdots,0,&-1,\cdots,-1),\\
\end{array}

the result easily follows based on the description of the maximal cones (see Proposition
3.3
).
∎

A basis for the Picard group
\Pic(Z)
can be easily derived by the description of the rays listed in the proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

The torus-invariant divisors
D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}
,
D_{y_{r+1}}
and
D_{z_{d-d^{\prime}+1}}
generate the Picard group
\Pic(Z)
.

Using Proposition
3.4
and taking degrees of primitive relations give upper bounds on the values of the
b_{i}
’s and
c_{i}
’s for which the projective bundle
Z
is Fano or weak Fano.
We can also prove bounds on the
b_{i}
’s and
c_{i}
’s in another way, as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{1},c_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{d-d^{\prime}},c_{d-d^{\prime}})
be defined as above with
b_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
.
If the projective bundle
Z=\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
is Fano (
resp.
weak Fano), then

(a)

If all
c_{i}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq d-d^{\prime}
, then we have inequalities

(6)

\displaystyle(d^{\prime}-r+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}b_{i}>0(\text{\emph{resp.} }\geq 0),

(7)

\displaystyle(r+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r}a_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}c_{i}>0(\text{\emph{resp.} }\geq 0);

(b)

If there exists

, replace the inequality (

Proof.

Let

be the projection.
Taking determinant of the relative Euler sequence,

0\to\Omega^{1}_{Z/X}\to p^{*}\mathcal{E}(-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to 0

we have

\det\Omega_{Z/X}^{1}=p^{*}\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-d+d^{\prime}-1).

Thus the cotangent bundle sequence gives

K_{Z}=p^{*}\det\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\det\Omega^{1}_{Z/X}=p^{*}(K_{X}\otimes\det\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-d+d^{\prime}-1).

Note that every torus-invariant curve on a proper toric variety is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let

be a torus-invariant curve on

-K_{Z}\cdot C=-K_{X}\cdot p_{*}C+\det\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\cdot p_{*}C+\mathcal{O}_{Z}(d-d^{\prime}+1)\cdot C.

We have the following cases to consider

(i)

If
p_{*}C
is a point in

then
-K_{Y}\cdot C=d-d^{\prime}+1>0
since

is contained in a fiber of
p
.

(ii)

If

is a line in

, note that

\displaystyle-K_{X}=D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+D_{x_{1}}+\cdots+D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r}}+D_{y_{1}}+\cdots+D_{y_{r+1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{1}D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+c_{1}D_{y_{r+1}})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(b_{d-d^{\prime}}D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}+c_{d-d^{\prime}}D_{y_{r+1}}),

then if
p_{*}C
is the torus-invariant curve defined by the primitive relation (
1

-K_{X}\cdot p_{*}C+\det\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\cdot p_{*}C=(d^{\prime}-r+1)-\sum_{i=1}^{d-d^{\prime}}b_{i};

if

is defined by the primitive relation (

Moreover, the intersection

can be negative, for instance when

is contained in the Cartier divisor determined by the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(d-d^{\prime}+1)
. Now assume there is a

such that

on

and denote by

Since

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{L})}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(1)\otimes p^{*}\mathcal{L}

.
Moreover, by projective formula we have

This proves the proposition.

4.
Batyrev’s construction

Let’s briefly recall Batyrev’s construction of smooth proper toric varieties of Picard rank
3
with five primitive collections
[
Bat91
, Theorem 6.6]
.

Fix the dimension
d
. Let
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})
be a positive partition of
d+3
, which is the number of rays.
Furthermore, choose nonnegative integers
b_{1},\cdots,b_{p_{3}}
and
c_{2},\cdots,c_{p_{2}}
(note that the indices for
c_{i}
’s start from
2
).
Batyrev in
[
Bat91
]
proves that we can associate to this data a toric variety as follows.
For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}
we can find a collection of vectors
X_{i}
with
|X_{i}|=p_{i}
, such that these vectors are primitive generators of the rays of a regular and complete fan.
The five sets
P_{i}=X_{i}\cup X_{i+1}
are the primitive collections of the fan, and we have the primitive relations

(8)

\begin{split}&(v_{1}+\cdots+v_{p_{0}})+(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{p_{1}})=(c_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+c_{p_{2}}z_{p_{2}})+((b_{1}+1)t_{1}+\cdots+(b_{p_{3}}+1)t_{p_{3}}),\\
&(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{p_{1}})+(z_{1}+\cdots+z_{p_{2}})=(u_{1}+\cdots+u_{p_{4}}),\\
&(z_{1}+\cdots+z_{p_{2}})+(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{p_{3}})=0,\\
&(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{p_{3}})+(u_{1}+\cdots+u_{p_{4}})=(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{p_{1}}),\\
&(u_{1}+\cdots+u_{p_{4}})+(v_{1}+\cdots+v_{p_{0}})=(c_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+c_{p_{2}}z_{p_{2}})+(b_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+b_{p_{3}}t_{p_{3}}),\end{split}

where

X_{0}=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{0}},\quad X_{1}=\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{1}},\quad X_{2}=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{2}},\quad X_{3}=\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{3}},\quad X_{4}=\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p_{4}}.

Then the toric variety associated to this fan is smooth and proper of Picard rank
3
with five primitive collections, and is uniquely determined by the
p_{i}
,
b_{i}
and
c_{i}
up to isomorphism
[
Bat91
, Theorem 6.6]
.
Let’s denote this variety, which is well-defined up to isomorphism, as

\Bat(\{p_{i}\},\{b_{i}\},\{c_{i}\})\coloneqq\Bat(\{p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\},\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{p_{3}}\},\{c_{2},\cdots,c_{p_{2}}\}).

We will call varieties that can be constructed in this way
Batyrev varieties
. More explicitly, one can construct
\Bat(\{p_{i}\},\{b_{i}\},\{c_{i}\})
by labelling the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by

\{v_{1},\dots v_{p_{0}},y_{2},\dots y_{p_{1}},z_{2},\dots z_{p_{2}},t_{1},\dots t_{p_{3}},u_{2},\dots u_{p_{4}}\}

and then setting

\displaystyle z_{1}=-z_{2}-\cdots-z_{p_{2}}-t_{1}-\cdots-t_{p_{3}},

\displaystyle y_{1}=-y_{2}-\dots-y_{p_{1}}-z_{1}-\cdots-z_{p_{2}}+u_{1}+\cdots+u_{p_{4}},

\displaystyle u_{1}=-u_{2}-\cdots-u_{p_{4}}-v_{1}-\cdots-v_{p_{0}}+c_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+c_{p_{2}}z_{p_{2}}+b_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+b_{p_{3}}t_{p_{3}}.

Then these vectors satisfy all the primitive relations and thus generate the rays that define the fan of the Batyrev variety.

Evidently, any rearrangement of the
b_{i}
’s and
c_{i}

b_{1},\cdots,b_{p_{3}}
and
c_{2},\cdots,c_{p_{2}}
are
nondecreasing
sequences.

It is stated in
[

that the data

is unique for a given isomorphism type of a Batyrev variety, up to a “symmetry of the pentagon”.
To explain this remark, consider a pentagon whose vertices correspond to the five sets
X_{0},\cdots,X_{4}

P_{0},\cdots,P_{4}

be the dihedral group of order
10

the group of symmetries of the pentagon, so it acts on both the sets
\{X_{0},\cdots,X_{4}\}
and
\{P_{0},\cdots,P_{4}\}
.

Furthermore, let

be two Batyrev varieties and suppose
\tau\in D_{5}

for all

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\{p_{i}\},\{b_{i}\},\{c_{i}\})

be two distinct triples of lists of nonnegative integers as above, so that they define Batyrev varieties.

\begin{cases}&p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{4}>0,\\
&p_{3}+p_{4}-p_{1}>0,\\
&p_{0}+p_{1}-p_{3}>\sum_{i=2}^{p_{2}}c_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{p_{3}}b_{i},\\
&p_{0}+p_{4}>\sum_{i=2}^{p_{2}}c_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{p_{3}}b_{i}.\end{cases}

5.
Star subdivisions and blowdowns

In this section, we discuss star subdivisions, blowdowns and flops of smooth projective toric varieties of Picard rank
3
. Star subdivisions and blowdowns of toric varieties with complete simplicial fans are studied in
[
Sat00
, Section 4]
in terms of primitive collections and primitive relations.
We write Macaulay2 functions for implementing these functions: specifically,
[
Sat00
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
Sat00
, Corollary 4.9]
are implemented by Macaulay2 functions
primitiveBlowup
and
primitiveBlowdown
respectively in
[
HJ25
]
.

By studying blowdowns we can understand how the rank
3
weak Fano toric varieties we have constructed can contract to simpler varieties of smaller Picard rank such as Kleinschmidt varieties. Also, we can computationally investigate when various rank
3
weak Fano toric varieties flop to each other, including the interesting phenomenon of a variety with three primitive collections flopping to a variety with five primitive collections.

Let’s first recall the definition of star subdivision of a complete simplicial fan.

Definition 5.1

(Star subdivision)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete simplicial fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma=\Cone(u_{1},\cdots,u_{l})
a cone in
\Sigma
.
Let
u\in N
be a primitive vector contained in the relative interior of
\sigma
.
The star subdivision of
\Sigma
relative to
(\sigma,u)
is the fan (again complete and simplicial) defined by

\Sigma^{*}(\sigma,u)=\{\tau\in\Sigma\mid\sigma\not\subseteq\tau\}\cup\bigcup_{\sigma\subseteq\tau\in\Sigma}\{\Cone(R)\mid R\subseteq\{u\}\cup\tau(1),\sigma(1)\not\subseteq R\}.

In particular, if
\sigma
is a smooth cone with
u=\sum_{i=1}^{l}u_{i}
, then the star subdivision is

\Sigma^{*}(\sigma,u)=(\Sigma\smallsetminus\{\sigma\})\cup\Sigma^{\prime}(\sigma)

where
\Sigma^{\prime}(\sigma)
is the set of all cones generated by subsets of
\{u,u_{1},\cdots,u_{l}\}
except
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{l}\}
.
In this case we write
\Sigma^{*}(\sigma)
instead of
\Sigma^{*}(\sigma,u)
.

If all cones of
\Sigma
which contain
\sigma
are smooth and
u=\sum_{i=1}^{l}u_{i}
, then the refinement
\Sigma^{*}(\sigma)
of
\Sigma
induces a toric morphism
X(\Sigma^{*}(\sigma))\to X(\Sigma)
which is a (equivariant) blowup of
X(\Sigma)
along the orbit closure with respect to
\sigma
.

The inverse transformation of a star subdivision is called a (equivariant) blowdown along
(P,u)
where
P=\rays(\sigma)
is a primitive collection of
\Sigma^{*}(\sigma,u)
.
We call a star subdivision (
resp.
blowdown)
regular
if the fans before and after the star subdivision (
resp.
blowdown) are regular.
Hence a necessary condition for the existence of a regular blowdown is the existence of a primitive collection
P=\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{l}\}
with primitive relation
\sum_{i=1}^{l}u_{i}=u
.
In this case, we can simply refer to the blowdown along
P
instead of
(P,u)
.

Kleinschmidt proves that any projective bundle
X=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus d^{\prime}-r-1}\oplus\mathcal{O}(1))
can be further blown down to
\mathbb{P}^{d^{\prime}}

[
Kle88
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus d^{\prime}-r}\oplus\mathcal{O}(a))
,
a>0
. Then
X
can be further blown down to a weighted projective space of dimension
d^{\prime}
:

\mathbb{P}(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{\text{$(r+1)$ copies}},\underbrace{a,\cdots,a}_{\text{$(d^{\prime}-r)$ copies}}).

Proof.

The Kleinschmidt variety
X
has disjoint primitive collections
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{d^{\prime}-r},x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}\}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{r+1}\}
which satisfy the primitive relations

\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r+1}x_{i}=0,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}=ax_{d^{\prime}-r}.

By
[
Sat00
, Corollary 4.9]
, after the blowdown along
(\mathcal{Y},x_{d^{\prime}-r})
, the primitive collection is a single set of rays
(\mathcal{X}\cup\mathcal{Y})\smallsetminus\{x_{d^{\prime}-r}\}
satisfying the relation

ax_{d^{\prime}-r+1}+a\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}-r-1}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}=0,

and thus the result follows.
∎

By
[
CLS11
, Example 8.3.3]
, a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(q_{0},\cdots,q_{n})
with reduced weight vector
(q_{0},\cdots,q_{n})
is Gorenstein Fano if and only if each
q_{j}
divides
\sum q_{i}
.
Thus the weighted projective space above is Gorenstein Fano if and only if
a
divides
r+1
.

Starting from now on,
we will only discuss regular star subdivisions and blowdowns for toric varieties of Picard rank
3
.
In
[
Sat00
, Theorem 4.10]
, Sato provides a criterion for the existence of a regular blowdown.
For toric varieties of Picard rank

6.
A converse to Bott vanishing

Assume
X
is a (smooth, proper) weak Fano toric variety, so
-K_{X}
is nef and big.
By
[
CLS11
, Theorem 9.3.3]
, we have
H^{p}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-K_{X}))=0
for all
p>1
. If
X
is Fano, then
H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-K_{X}))=0
by the Bott-Steenbrink-Danilov vanishing theorem (see
[
CLS11
, Theorem 9.3.1]
). In fact, the converse holds: this cohomology group vanishes precisely when
X
is Fano. We discovered this interesting fact empirically through numerical experimentation with Macaulay2.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a weak Fano toric variety. If
H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-K_{X}))=0
, then
X
is Fano.

Proof.

Assume
X
is a smooth projective toric variety of dimension
d
which is weak Fano, and let
\Sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
be its associated complete fan.
The anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}
is nef (and it is always big for toric varieties).
By the first Ishida complex, the cotangent sheaf satisfies the exact sequence

0\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\to M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\bigoplus_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}\to 0.

Twisting the sequence by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})
and taking long exact sequence on cohomology we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(-K_{X}))\to H^{0}(X,M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}\bigoplus_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(-K_{X}))\to M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))=0

since
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))=0
by Demazure vanishing.
Let’s first consider
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))
for any fixed ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.
By the exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{\rho})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}\to 0
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))\to 0.

By
[
CLS11
, Exercise 9.1.13]
, the graded piece for
m\in M
satisfies the property that
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\neq 0
implies that
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}=0
.
Recall that in
[
CLS11
, Section 9.1]
, for any Weil divisor
D=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
, we denote by

V_{D,m}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\Conv\left(u_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma(1),\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle<-a_{\rho}\right),

where
u_{\rho}
is the primitive generator of the ray
\rho
.
In particular, using
[
CLS11
, Theorem 9.1.3]
of computing sheaf cohomology via reduced cohomology, we derive the following cases for
m\in M
and the corresponding
m
-graded pieces:

(i)

If
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\neq 0
.
This is equivalent to
V_{-K_{X}-D_{\rho},m}=\varnothing
which implies
V_{-K_{X},m}=\varnothing
.
Thus
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}
.
Together with
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}=0
, we have
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))_{m}=0
.

(ii)

If
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\neq 0
.
It implies that
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}=0
, which is equivalent to
V_{-K_{X}-D_{\rho},m}\neq\varnothing
.
Thus for such
m\in M
, the exact sequence

0\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))_{m}\to H^{0}(X,O_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))_{m}\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\to 0

forces
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))_{m}\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}
.

(iii)

If
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))_{m}=0
.
Since
V_{-K_{X}-D_{\rho},m}\neq\varnothing
, either there exists a ray
\rho^{\prime}\neq\rho
such that
\langle m,u_{\rho^{\prime}}\rangle<-1
or
\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle<0
. Therefore

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho}}(-K_{X}))_{m}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\quad\text{if $\langle m,u_{\rho}\rangle=-1$ and $\langle m,u_{\rho^{\prime}}\rangle\geq-1$ for all $\rho^{\prime}\neq\rho$,}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now let’s turn our attention to the case when
X
is weak Fano but not Fano.
By
[
CLS11
, Lemma 6.1.13]
, it implies that there are two maximal cones
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(d)
with intersection
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(d-1)
a wall, such that
m_{\sigma}=m_{\sigma^{\prime}}\in M
.
Let
m=m_{\sigma}=m_{\sigma^{\prime}}
be the common Cartier data in
M
.
Firstly
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}
since
m\in P_{-K_{X}}
.
Let
(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})(1)=\{\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{d-1}\}
, and consider
\sigma(1)=(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})(1)\cup\{\rho_{d}\}
and
\sigma^{\prime}(1)=(\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime})(1)\cup\{\rho_{d+1}\}
.
Then for any
i=1,\cdots,d+1
, we have
\langle m,u_{\rho_{i}}\rangle=-1
and thus
V_{-K_{X}-D_{\rho_{i}},m}\neq\varnothing
.
It means that
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho_{i}}))_{m}=0
.
That
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}
forces
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho_{i}}))_{m}=0
, and hence
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{D_{\rho_{i}}}(-K_{X}))_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}
for all
i=1,\cdots,d+1
.
So that
\phi
fails to be surjective on
m
-graded pieces for
m=m_{\sigma}=m_{\sigma^{\prime}}
which implies that
H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(-K_{X}))_{m}\neq 0
.
∎

In the case that
X
is of Picard rank
3
, the proof can be simplified by the following fact.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
X=X(\Sigma)
be a weak Fano toric variety of Picard rank
3
. Then

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))=0

for any
i\geq 1
and ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.

Proof.

Let’s consider separately two cases:
X
is a Batyrev variety and
X
is a projective bundle.
We denote by
D=-K_{X}-D_{\rho}
.
First assume that
X
is a Batyrev variety with five primitive collections (
8
) denoted by
P_{0},\cdots,P_{4}
.
It is easy to see that
\deg P_{1}
and
\deg P_{3}
cannot be zero simultaneously: if so, then
p_{2}+p_{3}=0
which is a contradiction.
Thus either
\deg P_{1}>0
or
\deg P_{3}>0
.
To prove the proposition, by
[
Mus02
, Theorem 2.4(ii)]
, it suffices to find a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
E=\sum_{j}a_{j}D_{j}
on
X
where
a_{j}\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
such that
D+E
is
\mathbb{Q}
-ample.
If the primitive generator
u_{\rho}\in X_{2}
or
X_{3}
, we can take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}D_{y_{1}}
if
\deg P_{3}>0
, and
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}D_{u_{1}}
if
\deg P_{1}>0
.
If
u_{\rho}\in X_{0}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}D_{y_{1}}+\frac{1}{4}D_{u_{1}}
if
\deg P_{3}>0
and
E=D_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}D_{y_{1}}+\frac{3}{4}D_{u_{1}}
if
\deg P_{1}>0
.
If
u_{\rho}\in X_{1}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+D_{z_{1}}
if
\deg P_{3}>0
and
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}D_{u_{1}}
if
\deg P_{1}>0
.
If
u_{\rho}\in X_{4}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+D_{z_{1}}
if
\deg P_{3}>0
and
E=D_{\rho}+D_{v_{1}}+\frac{1}{2(1+b_{i})}D_{t_{i}}
if
\deg P_{1}>0
for any
t_{i}\in X_{3}
.
Now assume that
X
is a projective bundle with three primitive collections stated in Proposition
3.4
.
If
u_{\rho}\in\mathcal{Z}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{y_{1}}+D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}
.
Likewise if
u_{\rho}\in\mathcal{Y}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{y}+D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}
for any
y\in\mathcal{Y}\smallsetminus\{u_{\rho}\}
.
Finally if
u_{\rho}\in\mathcal{X}
, we take
E=D_{\rho}+D_{y_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{d^{\prime}-r}+1}D_{x_{1}}
if
u_{\rho}=x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}
and
E=D_{\rho}+D_{y_{1}}+D_{x_{d^{\prime}-r+1}}
otherwise.
It can be easily checked that by choosing
E
as above,
D+E
can be made
\mathbb{Q}
-ample. Therefore we obtain the vanishing of
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}-D_{\rho}))
for any
i>0
and
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

We can also derive Theorem
6.1
from a combinatorial formula of Mavlyutov.
Notice that the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
of
X
is
d
-semiample in the sense of
[
Mav08
]
.
According to
[
Mav08
, Corollary 2.7]
, we have

\displaystyle\dim H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Gamma\preccurlyeq P_{-K_{X}}}l^{*}(\Gamma)\sum_{j=0}^{1}{d-\dim\Gamma-j\choose 1-j}(-1)^{1-j}\left|\Sigma_{\sigma_{\Gamma}(j)}(1)\right|

(9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Gamma\preccurlyeq P_{-K_{X}}}l^{*}(\Gamma)(\left|\Sigma_{\sigma_{\Gamma}}(1)\right|-d+\dim\Gamma)

where the summation is over all faces
\Gamma
of the polytope
P_{-K_{X}}
, and

•

l^{*}(\Gamma)
is the number of interior lattice points inside the face
\Gamma
. Note that the lattice
N
is equipped with the subspace topology from the standard topology on the Euclidean space
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus if
\Gamma\preccurlyeq P_{-K_{X}}
is a vertex,
l^{*}(\Gamma)=1
;

•

\sigma_{\Gamma}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
is the cone in the fan
\Sigma_{-K_{X}}
which corresponds to the face
\Gamma
. Explicitly by duality, the dual cone
\sigma_{\Gamma}
is defined as

\sigma_{\Gamma}=\Cone(\{u_{F}\mid\text{ $F$ is any facet of $P_{-K_{X}}$ which contains $\Gamma$}\}),

and
\Sigma_{\sigma_{\Gamma}}=\{\tau\in\Sigma\mid\widetilde{\pi}(\tau)\subseteq\sigma_{\Gamma}\}
is a subfan of
\Sigma
;

•

\widetilde{\pi}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to N_{-K_{X}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is the map defined in
[
Mav08
, Theorem 2.2]
which maps the fan
\Sigma
to the induced fan
\Sigma_{-K_{X}}
. Since the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
on a toric variety is always big, the surjective homomorphism
\widetilde{\pi}
is also injective.

If
X
is not Fano,
i.e.,

-K_{X}
is nef but not ample, then the support function
\varphi_{-K_{X}}
is only convex but not strictly convex.
As in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, this is equivalent to the existence of two maximal cones
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(d)
such that their Cartier data satisfy
m_{\sigma}=m_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
Since
-K_{X}
is basepoint-free there is a vertex
v\in M
of the polytope
P_{-K_{X}}
with

\left|\Sigma_{\sigma_{v}}(1)\right|-d+\dim v=\left|\Sigma_{\sigma_{v}}(1)\right|-d>0,

thus
\dim H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}(-K_{X}))>0
.

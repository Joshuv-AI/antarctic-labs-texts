Star-Black Hole Interactions in Young Star Clusters
Sara Rastello 1,2, Giuliano Iorio 1,2, Mark Gieles 1,3, Long Wang 4,5

Abstract
Close encounters between stars and black holes can trigger micro-tidal disruption events (micro-TDEs) in dense young star clusters (YSCs). Using direct N N -body simulations with PETAR , we found that most micro-TDEs arise from few-body multiple encounters. The inferred rate is ∼ 350 \sim 350 - 450 450 Gpc -3 yr -1 . Micro-TDEs could be detected both by upcoming surveys such as LSST, expected to observe ∼ 10 \sim 10 - 100 100 events per year, and by their gravitational-wave (GW) signals peaking in the deci-Hertz band, detectable with future instruments such as LGWA and DECIGO.

1
Introduction

Dynamical encounters between stars and black holes (BHs) in dense stellar clusters (SCs) such as YSCs, can lead to a wide variety of outcomes. These range from the formation of detached binaries (e.g., the so-called
Gaia BHs
;
El-Badry et al., 2023
;
Gaia Collaboration et al., 2024
;
Rastello et al., 2023
;
Marín Pina et al., 2024
;
Iorio et al., 2024
) to disruptive events such as
micro-TDEs
. The latter occur when a star (of mass
m_{\rm*}
and radius
r_{\rm*}
) passes within the tidal radius (
r_{\rm\rm t}
) of a BH (
r_{\rm\rm t}\simeq r_{\rm*}\left({m_{\rm\rm BH}}/{m_{\rm*}}\right)^{1/3}
) and is partially or fully disrupted
(
Rees, 1988
;
Perets et al., 2016
)
. Micro-TDEs can be viewed as the scaled-down counterparts of tidal disruption events (TDEs) involving super-massive BHs, and they may give rise to luminous, multi-wavelength electro magnetic (EM) flares as well as bursts of GW
(
Komossa, 2015
;
Toscani et al., 2020
;
Toscani et al., 2022
;
Toscani et al., 2025
)
. Previous analytical and numerical work suggests that micro-TDEs may occur at non-negligible rates, often triggered or enhanced by binary or higher-order dynamical interactions
(
Perets et al., 2016
;
Rastello et al., 2020
;
Rastello et al., 2019
;
Kremer et al., 2021
;
Ryu et al., 2023
)
. These events could represent an important class of energetic transients, potentially linked to ultra-long gamma-ray bursts and fast blue optical transients
(
Perets et al., 2016
;
Kremer et al., 2021
;
Kremer et al., 2022
;
Kremer et al., 2023
)
.

2
Methods

We performed direct
N
-body simulation of a sample of YSCs modeled with masses
10^{3}
-
10^{5}\,M_{\rm\odot}
, half mass radius and density based on observed Galactic clusters
(
Krumholz et al., 2019
)
. Stellar and binary populations were sampled from
Kroupa, 2001
and
Moe and Di Stefano, 2017
, and initialized using
limepy

(
Gieles and Zocchi, 2015
)
King models (
W_{\rm 0}=7
). We simulated 3600 realizations at both low (
Z=0.0002
) and Solar (
Z=0.02
) metallicity, including primordial and dynamically formed binaries (See
Rastello et al., 2025
for more details.)
. Cluster evolution was followed with the code
PETAR

(
Wang et al., 2020
)
, to which we implemented a micro-TDE prescription (removing stars passing within the tidal radius of a compact object, with 10% of their mass accreted onto the BH, see appendix A in
Rastello et al., 2025
), while stellar evolution was treated with the updated
BSE
framework
(
Hurley et al., 2002
;
Banerjee et al., 2020
)
. All clusters were evolved in a Galactic potential
(
Bovy, 2015
)
on circular orbits and integrated for
\sim 1.5
Gyr.

3
Micro-TDEs pathways

Micro-TDEs in YSCs are produced through three main pathways: i) single encounters, ii) binary-mediated interactions, and iii) few-body multiple encounters.
Single events are rare (
\sim 3
%), occurring when a star and a BH approach on a parabolic orbit (Fig.
1
, left panel), typically occurring in dense region (40% events in within cluster cores). At
Z=0.0002
, BHs are more massive (
17
-
52\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
), producing deeper encounters (
\beta\sim 1
-
10
), whereas at Solar metallicity they are lighter (
13
-
31\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
) and less penetrating events occur (
\beta\sim 2
-
3
).
Binary-mediated micro-TDEs (Fig.
1
, central panel) contribute only
\sim 7
% of the total, mostly (
\sim 82
%) in metal-poor clusters. They occur either through binary hardening during dynamical encounters or stellar expansion during binary evolution. At
Z=0.0002
, they involve a wide range of BH (
3
-
170\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
) and stellar (MS and giant) masses (
0.1
-
134\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
). At Z=0.02, the systems are lighter (BHs:
4
-
35\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
, stars:
0.2
-
27\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
). Most binary-mediated micro-TDEs have
\beta\approx 1
and originate from primordial binaries undergoing common envelope or mass transfer episodes.
The vast majority of micro-TDEs in YSCs (
\sim 90
%) occur during few-body interactions involving three or more objects (85% at low-
Z
, 90% at Z=0.02; (Fig.
1
, right panel). At
Z=0.0002
, disrupted stars are mainly MS (
83
%), with smaller fractions of giants (8%), naked He stars (7%), and WDs (0.5%); BHs range from
3
to
138\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
. At Z=0.02, MS stars dominate even more strongly (98%), while giants (0.9%) and WDs (1.1%) are rare; stellar masses span
0.09
-
18\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
and BHs
11
-
75\,{\rm M}_{\rm\odot}
. Nearly all encounters (
>99
%) occur on highly eccentric (
e\sim 0.99
) orbits.

4
Micro-TDEs efficiency & Rates

We find a global production efficiency
\eta
(i.e. the number of micro-TDEs per unit stellar mass) in YSCs of
\eta\approx 5\times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{M}_{\rm\odot}^{-1}
. The distribution (Fig.
2
, left panel) shows a strong dependence on cluster initial density but is independent of metallicity.
From the derived efficiency, we estimated the comoving event rate density (see Eq. 4-6 in
Rastello et al., 2025
) as
R(z)=\eta f_{\rm\rm SF,SC}\,\rho_{\rm\rm SFRD}(z)

where
f_{\rm\rm SF,SC}
is the fraction of star formation occurring in clusters
(
Lada and Lada, 2003
;
Kremer et al., 2021
)
, and
\rho_{\rm\rm SFRD}(z)
is the cosmic star formation rate density
(
Madau and Fragos, 2017
)
. The total volumetric rate of micro-TDEs is therefore estimated to rise from 350-450 Gpc
-3
yr
-1
at
z=0
up to 2000-3000 Gpc
-3
yr
-1
at
z=2
, with the majority of events originating from multiple dynamical encounters.
Accordingly, the number of expected events per year rises from just a few within the local Universe (
z<0.05
, approximately 200 Mpc) to as many as
10^{5}
out to redshift
z=1
.

5
EM and GW Detectability

By combining the predicted rates we assessed the detectability of micro-TDEs with current and future surveys as Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) at Palomar Observatory
(
Graham et al., 2019
)
,
Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) at the Vera C. Rubin Observatory
(
Ivezić et al., 2019
)
,
and Ultraviolet Transient Astronomy Satellite (ULTRASAT)
(
Shvartzvald et al., 2024
)
with the wind-reprocessed emission models of
Kremer et al., 2023
. These models yield peak luminosities in the range
10^{40}
-
10^{43}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}
.
We find that LSST is the most promising instrument to unveil the micro-TDE population in YSCs, expecting to detect from tens up to several tens of thousands of micro-TDEs per year.

Micro-TDEs are multi-messenger sources, and in addition to EM emission, they are expected to produce GW bursts. Following the formalism of
(
Toscani et al., 2020
)
, we find that WD disruptions by stellar-mass BHs produce GW signals in the deci-Hz band, detectable up to
z\sim 1
by future detectors such as LGWA and DECIGO (Fig.
3
). MS disruptions yield weaker, lower-frequency bursts, observable only within
\sim 10
Mpc.

6
Conclusion

Micro-TDEs in YSCs are mostly produced through interactions involving multiple systems, with rates peaking around

\sim 10^{3}\,\mathrm{Gpc^{-3}\,yr^{-1}}
at cosmic noon, largely independent of metallicity but dependent on cluster density.
Micro-TDEs are promising multi-messenger transients, potentially detectable by upcoming facilities such as LSST that is expected to detect from tens to several tens of thousands of micro-TDEs
per year, and future deci-Hz GW observatories as LGWA and DECIGO.

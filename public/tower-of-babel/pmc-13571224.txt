Nifedipine-associated maternal oxygen desaturation in an IVF twin pregnancy complicated by preterm premature rupture of membranes: a case report
Authors: Yu-Ting Cheng, Chih-Jung Liu, Te-Fu Chan, Wei-Yun Hsu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOBJECTIVETo report a case of nifedipine-associated maternal oxygen desaturation in an IVF-conceived twin pregnancy complicated by PPROM with complete clinical documentation, and to provide denominator context from a retrospective review of institutional experience prompted by this case.STUDY DESIGNCase report at a single tertiary center. Following the Index Case, we conducted a retrospective review of all 103 patients who received nifedipine tocolysis at our institution over the preceding 2 years (January 2024–January 2026). Among these, 10 (9.7%) had IVF-conceived multiple gestations complicated by PPROM—the clinical profile shared with the Index Case; 3 of these 10 (30%) had developed oxygen desaturation. Only the Index Case had complete clinical workup and is reported in detail.RESULTSAmong 103 nifedipine tocolysis patients, 10 (9.7%) had IVF-conceived multiple gestations complicated by PPROM; 3 of these 10 (30%) developed oxygen desaturation. Two additional cases lacked complete workup and could not be attributed to nifedipine. The Index Case: a 36-year-old with IVF-conceived DCDA twins at 23+3 weeks developed SpO₂ 88% to 90% within hours of nifedipine. Echocardiography: preserved LVEF; BNP 626 pg/mL; troponin I<0.01 ng/mL; chest radiography: no alveolar pulmonary edema. Magnesium sulfate was never concurrent with nifedipine; it was given once for fetal neuroprotection after Episode 1, before either rechallenge. SpO₂ normalized without diuretics after each nifedipine discontinuation. Two deliberate rechallenges (Episodes 2 and 3, both without MgSO₄) both reproduced desaturation; Episode 2 required intensive care unit admission. Atosiban was initiated without recurrence.CONCLUSIONThis case, strengthened by two positive rechallenges occurring without concurrent magnesium sulfate, provides compelling temporal evidence for nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise in an IVF twin pregnancy with PPROM. The pattern of desaturation without radiographic alveolar pulmonary edema, elevated BNP, and resolution without diuresis suggests subclinical hemodynamic perturbation as the underlying mechanism. Clinicians should maintain heightened vigilance for unexplained oxygen desaturation during nifedipine tocolysis in this patient population.Key words: brain natriuretic peptide, calcium channel blockers, echocardiography, magnesium sulfate, maternal respiratory compromise, multiple gestation, pharmacovigilance, pulmonary edema, tocolysis, vasodilation

Introduction

Preterm labor remains a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide.1 Nifedipine—a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker—is widely used for tocolysis owing to its oral administration, favorable side-effect profile, and evidence supporting superior neonatal outcomes compared with beta-agonists.2

Despite its widespread use, nifedipine carries a risk of cardiovascular and respiratory adverse effects, including pulmonary edema, particularly in high-risk obstetric patients.3, 4, 5 Pregnancy is characterized by physiological adaptations that narrow the safety margin against pulmonary fluid accumulation: a 40% to 50% increase in plasma volume, reduction in colloid oncotic pressure from approximately 25 to 18 mmHg, and enhanced capillary permeability.6, 7, 8 These changes are amplified in multiple gestations, where plasma volume expansion is further increased and maternal anemia is substantially more prevalent.

The concurrent use of magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection may further complicate this picture through its vasodilatory and calcium-antagonist properties.9, 10, 11 Twin pregnancies conceived via IVF may represent a particularly at-risk subgroup, given associated alterations in vascular reactivity, placental abnormalities, and heightened inflammatory states.12, 13, 14 The Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) has specifically warned about pulmonary edema risk with calcium channel blockers in multiple pregnancies.15

We report a case of nifedipine-associated maternal oxygen desaturation in an IVF twin pregnancy with PPROM characterized by three independent desaturation episodes, two deliberate positive rechallenges occurring without concurrent magnesium sulfate, and complete clinical investigation including echocardiography and serial cardiac biomarkers. This case prompted a retrospective review of all 103 patients who received nifedipine tocolysis at our institution over the preceding 2 years, identifying a subset of 10 with the specific clinical profile of IVF-conceived multiple gestation and PPROM, and providing important denominator context.

Materials and methods

We report a case of nifedipine-associated maternal oxygen desaturation (the Index Case) that occurred at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital. This case prompted a retrospective review of all 103 patients who received nifedipine tocolysis at our institution over the preceding 2 years (January 2024–January 2026). This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB No. KMUHIRB-E(II)-20260041, approval date: January 27, 2026). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this case report.

Among the 103 nifedipine tocolysis patients, we identified the subset sharing the clinical profile of the Index Case: IVF-conceived multiple gestation complicated by PPROM. This comprised 10 patients (9.7%), representing the denominator population most relevant for estimating the incidence of this complication. For each of the three patients in this subset who developed oxygen desaturation, we assessed whether the clinical workup was sufficiently complete to reasonably exclude major alternative diagnoses (including primary cardiac dysfunction, nephrotic-range proteinuria, pulmonary embolism, and community-acquired pneumonia). Cases with incomplete investigations—leaving multiple alternative etiologies unresolvable—were not attributed to nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise. Only the Index Case met the threshold for complete investigation and is reported herein.

Results

Denominator context: Following the Index Case, a retrospective review of all 103 patients who received nifedipine tocolysis at our institution over the preceding 2 years identified 10 (9.7%) with IVF-conceived multiple gestations complicated by PPROM—the clinical profile shared with the Index Case (9 DCDA twin pregnancies and 1 triplet pregnancy). Among these 10, 3 (30%) had developed clinically significant oxygen desaturation temporally associated with nifedipine administration—the Index Case being one of these three. Upon review of the two additional cases, both were found to have incomplete clinical workup: neither had echocardiography performed, alternative diagnoses including pulmonary embolism and cardiac dysfunction could not be adequately excluded, and concurrent clinical comorbidities further complicated attribution. These two cases therefore cannot be definitively attributed to nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise and are not included in this report. The seven IVF-twin-PPROM patients who received nifedipine without developing oxygen desaturation tolerated the drug without clinically significant respiratory events. Case report

A 36-year-old gravida 2, para 0 woman (BMI 23.0 kg/m²) with an IVF-conceived DCDA twin pregnancy was admitted at 23+3 weeks’ gestation for management of cervical incompetence complicated by PPROM. Her medical history was notable for NK cell elevation and a prior cerclage; she had no pre-existing cardiac or pulmonary disease. Baseline SpO₂ was 98% to 99% on room air; blood pressure was 118/74 mmHg.

Episode 1 (nifedipine Course 1, 70 mg): Oral nifedipine was initiated for tocolysis. Magnesium sulfate was not administered at this time. Concurrent medications included antenatal corticosteroids, prophylactic antibiotics for PPROM, and enoxaparin. Within 2 to 3 hours of 7th dose of nifedipine, SpO₂ decreased to 88% to 90% on room air, requiring nasal cannula at 2 L/min; blood pressure after nifedipine was 108/65 mmHg. Given concern for imminent preterm delivery, nifedipine was immediately discontinued, and magnesium sulfate was subsequently initiated for fetal neuroprotection—this was the first time magnesium sulfate was used in this admission, and it was started only after nifedipine had already been stopped. After approximately 2 days, maternal SpO₂ and vital signs remained stable; magnesium sulfate was discontinued owing to less necessity of emergent delivery in 24 hours.

Episode 2—Rechallenge 1 (nifedipine Course 2, 40 mg; no concurrent MgSO₄): Following magnesium sulfate discontinuation, persistent preterm uterine activity prompted reintroduction of nifedipine for tocolysis. No magnesium sulfate was coadministered. Within hours, the patient developed markedly more severe respiratory distress than Episode 1, with oxygen requirements escalating from nasal cannula to high-flow nasal cannula. All tocolytic agents were discontinued. A portable chest radiograph obtained during this episode showed no definitive alveolar pulmonary edema (Figure, A). Echocardiography demonstrated preserved left ventricular ejection fraction without diastolic dysfunction. BNP was 626 pg/mL; troponin I was <0.01 ng/mL; albumin was 2.8 g/dL; hemoglobin was 9.9 g/dL; CRP was 66.44 mg/L; d-dimer was 4.71 mg/L; urinalysis revealed no proteinuria (Table). The patient was transferred to the intensive care unit (ICU) for monitoring. SpO₂ normalized progressively in the ICU without diuretic therapy.

Episode 3—Rechallenge 2 (nifedipine Course 3, 10 mg; no concurrent MgSO₄): During the ICU stay, regular uterine contractions recurred. Nifedipine was administered again in an attempt to suppress labor. No magnesium sulfate was coadministered. Oxygen desaturation recurred promptly. Portable chest radiography again showed no definitive alveolar pulmonary edema (Figure, B). After three independent episodes of oxygen desaturation—each temporally linked to nifedipine and none involving concurrent magnesium sulfate—nifedipine was permanently abandoned. SpO₂ normalized without diuresis. Atosiban was subsequently initiated as the alternative tocolytic agent; no further episodes of oxygen desaturation occurred, and the patient remained clinically stable throughout the remainder of hospitalization. Total cumulative nifedipine dose across three courses was 120 mg (70+40+10 mg).

Comment

We report a case of nifedipine-associated maternal oxygen desaturation in an IVF DCDA twin pregnancy with PPROM, distinguished by three features that provide exceptionally strong evidence: (1) complete clinical investigation excluding primary cardiac and pulmonary alternative diagnoses; (2) two deliberate positive rechallenges with nifedipine (Episodes 2 and 3), the second of which required ICU admission, both reproducing desaturation; (3) the complete absence of concurrent magnesium sulfate in all three episodes; and (4) serial chest radiography showing no alveolar pulmonary edema in either episode (Figure, A and B), definitively excluding MgSO₄ as a necessary coprecipitant. To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise in this clinical context with a double-positive rechallenge.

Pathophysiology of respiratory vulnerability

Multiple converging physiological factors created heightened vulnerability to pulmonary fluid imbalance in this patient. Plasma volume expansion in DCDA twin pregnancy amplifies the already-widened Starling forces of normal gestation.6, 7, 8 The patient’s hemoglobin of 9.9 g/dL (below the WHO threshold of 11.0 g/dL for pregnancy anemia) and borderline hypoalbuminemia (2.8 g/dL) further reduced plasma colloid oncotic pressure. PPROM-associated subclinical chorioamnionitis (CRP 66.44 mg/L) may have contributed to endothelial vulnerability.16 In this context of background physiological compromise, nifedipine’s vasodilatory and negative inotropic effects likely represented the decisive precipitant.

Mechanisms and the double positive rechallenge

Nifedipine produces systemic vasodilation through L-type calcium channel blockade and may exert differential precapillary vasodilation that transiently raises pulmonary capillary hydrostatic pressure.4,5,17 The mechanistic evidence in this case is exceptionally strong, resting on four converging observations.

First, SpO₂ normalized after nifedipine discontinuation in all three episodes without diuretic therapy—a resolution time course consistent with nifedipine’s pharmacokinetic half-life, and inconsistent with the natural history of established alveolar pulmonary edema, which typically requires active diuresis. Second, echocardiography demonstrated preserved ejection fraction and no diastolic dysfunction, with normal troponin I, excluding primary cardiac etiologies. Third, and most critically, the clinical course included two deliberate positive rechallenges: nifedipine was reintroduced on two separate occasions, and each time oxygen desaturation recurred promptly. Episode 2 was notably more severe than Episode 1, requiring escalation to high-flow nasal cannula and ICU admission; Episode 3 confirmed the pattern in the ICU. A double positive rechallenge—each reproducing the same adverse event—fulfils, at the highest level, the pharmacovigilance criteria of the Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale and provides near-experimental confirmation of nifedipine’s causal role.

Fourth, and decisively, magnesium sulfate was never administered concurrently with nifedipine in any of the three episodes. MgSO₄ was initiated only once during the hospitalization—for fetal neuroprotection after Episode 1’s nifedipine had already been stopped—and was discontinued before nifedipine was reintroduced for either rechallenge. This sequence definitively and completely excludes magnesium sulfate potentiation as a contributing mechanism in this case. These four observations together—resolution without diuresis, normal cardiac function, double positive rechallenge, and complete absence of concurrent MgSO₄—constitute an exceptionally robust pharmacovigilance case for nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise. Our findings extend those of Khattab et al,18 who described overt nifedipine-induced pulmonary edema requiring intubation and diuresis, suggesting our case represents an earlier or milder point on the same pathophysiological spectrum.

Alternative diagnoses addressed

The complete workup allows systematic exclusion of alternative etiologies. Cardiac dysfunction was excluded by echocardiography (preserved ejection fraction, no diastolic dysfunction) and normal troponin I. Nephrotic syndrome was excluded by urinalysis (protein-negative). Pulmonary embolism was not formally excluded by CT pulmonary angiography (d-dimer 4.71 mg/L, nonspecific in pregnancy); however, the consistent temporal association with nifedipine across three independent episodes, the absence of tachycardia or pleuritic symptoms, and the resolution-rechallenge-resolution-rechallenge pattern argue decisively against PE as the primary etiology. Community-acquired pneumonia was excluded by the normal chest radiographs on both dates (Figure, A and B) and absence of fever. Fluid overload cannot be completely excluded as a cocontributor to Episode 1, but cannot explain Episodes 2 and 3 (rechallenges), which occurred after the acute fluid loading period. Magnesium sulfate potentiation is definitively excluded, as no MgSO₄ was present during any of the three episodes.

Clinical implications

This case illustrates that nifedipine-associated respiratory compromise in IVF twin pregnancies with PPROM may present without radiographic alveolar pulmonary edema, potentially be misattributed to other causes, and may resolve spontaneously after drug discontinuation without requiring diuretics. Clinicians should consider prompt nifedipine discontinuation when unexplained oxygen desaturation occurs in this population. Lung ultrasound, more sensitive than chest radiography for early interstitial fluid via B-line detection, should be considered for prospective monitoring.19 Atosiban (an oxytocin receptor antagonist without vasodilatory properties) represents a safe alternative tocolytic in this context; its use in the present case without respiratory recurrence provides direct clinical support for this recommendation.

Limitations

This is a single case report, which inherently limits generalizability. Pulmonary embolism was not formally excluded by CT pulmonary angiography. Fluid balance was not precisely quantified. Serum magnesium was not measured. Two additional cases with oxygen desaturation temporally associated with nifedipine were identified in the retrospective review; both had incomplete clinical workup (no echocardiography; pulmonary embolism and cardiac dysfunction could not be excluded), and are therefore not attributed to nifedipine in this report. We acknowledge this as an inherent limitation: a possible nifedipine contribution in those cases can neither be confirmed nor excluded.

In conclusion, this case report provides the first well-documented account of nifedipine-associated oxygen desaturation in an IVF twin pregnancy with PPROM featuring a double positive rechallenge—two deliberate reintroductions of nifedipine, each reproducing desaturation, neither involving concurrent magnesium sulfate—confirming the temporal drug–event association at the highest pharmacovigilance evidentiary level. The absence of radiographic alveolar pulmonary edema, preserved cardiac function on echocardiography, and resolution without diuresis in all three episodes support subclinical hemodynamic perturbation as the underlying mechanism. Clinicians should be aware of this clinical scenario, avoid nifedipine in IVF twin pregnancies with PPROM whenever possible, and substitute atosiban as the preferred tocolytic agent in this population.

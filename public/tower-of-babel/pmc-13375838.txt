Itch Relief and Quality-of-Life Improvement with Abrocitinib and Dupilumab in Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Post Hoc Analysis of JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE
Authors: Gil Yosipovitch, Melinda J. Gooderham, Sarina B. Elmariah, Gianluca Bonfanti, Roger A. Edwards, Stamatios Gregoriou, Pinaki Biswas, Daniela E. Myers, Justine Alderfer, Erman Güler, Christopher Koulias
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAtopic dermatitis is a chronic immune-inflammatory skin disease that significantly impairs quality of life, with itch representing a key, but not exclusive, contributor to this burden.ObjectiveWe aimed to evaluate itch relief and quality-of-life improvements with abrocitinib or dupilumab for 16 weeks alongside topical therapy in patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.MethodsThis post hoc analysis included pooled data from patients who received abrocitinib (200 mg/day) or dupilumab (300 mg/every 2 weeks) in phase III JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE. Assessments included proportions of patients achieving a ≥ 4-point improvement from baseline in Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale (PP-NRS4), itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) by baseline itch severity, and proportions of patients with residual itch achieving Dermatology Life Quality Index score of 0/1 (DLQI 0/1), total Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM) score ≤ 2, SCORing Atopic Dermatitis (SCORAD) sleep loss visual analog scale score of 0/1 (SCORAD sleep loss visual analog scale 0/1), and POEM sleep item score of 0 (POEM sleep 0).ResultsAmong 1196 patients (abrocitinib, n = 588; dupilumab, n = 608), those with greater baseline itch severity experienced greater atopic dermatitis severity and worse quality of life than patients with less severe itch. At week 16, numerically more patients achieved PP-NRS4 (67% vs 63%) and PP-NRS 0/1 (36% vs 27%) with abrocitinib versus dupilumab, respectively, regardless of baseline itch severity, although 95% confidence intervals overlapped for both measures. Median time to achieve PP-NRS4 (11 vs 29 days) and PP-NRS 0/1 (57 vs 139 days) was shorter with abrocitinib versus dupilumab, respectively. Numerically greater proportions of patients achieving PP-NRS 0/1 also achieved DLQI 0/1, POEM ≤ 2, SCORAD sleep loss visual analog scale 0/1, or POEM sleep 0 than patients with residual itch (PP-NRS ≥ 2).ConclusionsIn this post hoc pooled analysis of the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials, the proportion of patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis achieving clinically meaningful itch responses, including an itch-free state, was generally higher with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab. Median time to achievement of both PP-NRS4 and an itch-free state was shorter with abrocitinib than with dupilumab. Patients who achieved an itch-free state experienced greater improvements in quality of life and sleep compared with patients with residual itch.Clinical Trial RegistrationJADE COMPARE (NCT03720470; registration date: 2018/10/24); JADE DARE (NCT04345367; registration date: 2020/04/10).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40257-026-01030-5.

Key Points

In a pooled post hoc analysis of the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials, numerically greater proportions of patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis achieved itch responses with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab, and median time to itch response was shorter with abrocitinib.Greater proportions of patients achieving an itch-free state also achieved better quality of life and sleep scores compared with those who did not meet these itch efficacy responses.These findings suggest that prioritizing complete itch resolution as a treatment goal may be clinically meaningful for improving overall quality of life and symptom management in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a common chronic immune-inflammatory skin disease that is associated with the highest burden (in disability-adjusted life-years) among all dermatologic disorders globally [1, 2]. Intense itch, the hallmark symptom of AD, is frequently reported as the most distressing symptom of the disease [2, 3]. In a study of 380 adults with moderate-to-severe AD, 60.8% reported severe or unbearable itch in the past 2 weeks, 85.8% experienced daily itch according to the Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM), and 63.1% reported itch lasting at least 12 h per day based on the 5-D Pruritus Scale [4].

Patients with AD describe intense itch as difficult to control and attempts to alleviate itch through scratching often cause feelings of embarrassment and social difficulties [5, 6]. Patients with AD also frequently have comorbid psychiatric conditions, including anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideation [7]. Stress and anxiety are known to exacerbate itch, aggravating the itch-scratch cycle and worsening quality of life (QOL) [8]. Nocturnal scratching in AD causes sleep disturbances that exacerbate psychological comorbidities and lead to fatigue, poor concentration, and daily functioning and work productivity impairments [4, 5, 9–11]. Diminished QOL among patients with AD is directly related to itch severity [12–14]. Moreover, the itch-scratch cycle in AD is self-perpetuating; intense itch leads to scratching, resulting in inflammation, skin barrier damage and dysfunction, infection, and overall disease and symptom exacerbation [2].

Previous studies have indicated that achievement of an itch-free state as well as rapid onset of itch relief are highly important to patients [15–18]. Moreover, in a pooled analysis of abrocitinib monotherapy studies, patients who achieved a greater reduction in itch reported a lesser impact of AD on their QOL [19]. However, the impact of varying baseline itch severity levels on the clinical significance of improvements in itch and QOL is not fully understood. A recent consensus statement developed using a Delphi methodology identified a 3-point improvement from baseline in the Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale (PP-NRS) as a clinically relevant outcome for patients with moderate-to-severe AD [20]. Although a 3-point improvement may be substantial for patients with low baseline PP-NRS scores, it may not adequately address the needs of patients experiencing severe itch at baseline. Understanding the variations of treatment response according to baseline PP-NRS could refine AD treatment approaches and optimize patient outcomes.

Dupilumab, an injectable interleukin (IL)-4 receptor-α antagonist that inhibits IL-4 and IL-13 signaling, is approved for patients with moderate-to-severe AD aged ≥ 6 months [21, 22]. Abrocitinib is an oral, once-daily, Janus kinase 1 (JAK1)-selective inhibitor approved for the treatment of adults [23–26] and adolescents [23–25] with moderate-to-severe AD. In the phase III trials JADE COMPARE (NCT03720470) and JADE DARE (NCT04345367), abrocitinib 200 mg provided more rapid itch improvement than dupilumab in patients with moderate-to-severe AD, with improvement observed as soon as 1 day after the first dose [27, 28]. In both trials, abrocitinib 200 mg also provided numerically greater improvements compared with dupilumab in patient-reported disease severity (assessed using POEM) and QOL (assessed using the Dermatology Life Quality Index [DLQI]) [27, 29]. Using data from the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials, this post hoc analysis evaluated the itch relief dynamics and QOL improvements following treatment with abrocitinib or dupilumab in patients with moderate-to-severe AD, providing further granularity and insights on the impact of differing baseline itch severity levels on the clinical relevance of improvements in itch and QOL as well as the attainment of an itch-free state.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

This post hoc analysis included pooled data from adult patients with moderate-to-severe AD treated with abrocitinib 200 mg (once daily) or dupilumab 300 mg (once every 2 weeks) for 16 weeks, in combination with topical therapy, in the phase III JADE DARE (NCT04345367) and JADE COMPARE (NCT03720470) clinical trials. Full study details, including the inclusion and exclusion criteria, for JADE DARE and JADE COMPARE have been published previously [27, 28]. Briefly, patients were aged ≥ 18 years with moderate-to-severe AD and defined as having an Investigator’s Global Assessment score of ≥ 3, an Eczema Area and Severity Index score of ≥ 16, ≥ 10% body surface area affected, a PP-NRS (used with permission from Regeneron Pharmaceuticals Inc., and Sanofi) score of ≥ 4 (PP-NRS4), and an inadequate response to topical medication or requirement for systemic therapy to control their AD. Pooled data were categorized by itch severity at baseline, assessed using the PP-NRS. Itch severity categories at baseline were PP-NRS scores of 4 (least severe itch given inclusion criteria), 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 (most severe itch). Inclusion criteria for JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE included a baseline PP-NRS score ≥ 4; therefore, patients with lower itch scores were not enrolled and were not available for analysis.

Outcomes

Efficacy assessments included the proportions of patients in each itch severity subgroup achieving a clinically meaningful improvement in itch (≥ 4-point improvement from baseline in PP-NRS) and an itch-free state (PP-NRS score of 0 or 1 [PP-NRS 0/1]) at weeks 2 and 16. Median time to achievement of PP-NRS4 or PP-NRS 0/1 and the proportion of patients achieving daily a PP-NRS score of < 2 from day 1 through week 2 were also determined. Quality-of-life assessments included the proportions of patients attaining a DLQI score of 0 or 1 (DLQI 0/1; indicating no effect of AD on QOL) at weeks 2, 12, and 16, and a total POEM score ≤ 2 (indicating clear/almost clear AD) at weeks 12 and 16; patients were stratified based on itch severity by PP-NRS at the respective study visits. Sleep quality assessments included the proportion of patients achieving a SCORing Atopic Dermatitis sleep loss visual analog scale score of 0 or 1 (SCORAD sleep loss visual analog scale [VAS] 0/1; indicating little/no sleeplessness) at weeks 2, 12, and 16, and a POEM sleep item (“Over the last week, on how many nights has your sleep been disturbed because of the eczema?”) score of 0 (POEM sleep 0; indicating little/no impact) at weeks 12 and 16. POEM data were not collected at week 2.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy was assessed in the full analysis set, which was defined as all randomly assigned patients who received at least one dose of study medication. The estimate and confidence intervals (CIs) for differences in proportions were calculated based on the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel weighted (by study) average difference for each study using the normal approximation of binomial proportions [30]. For binary outcomes, if a patient withdrew from the study, they were considered a nonresponder at all visits after discontinuation. Continuous POEM and DLQI outcomes were assessed using descriptive statistics, with no imputation for missing data. Estimates of the median time to achievement of PP-NRS4 or PP-NRS 0/1 responses were derived using the Kaplan–Meier method [31], and P-values were based on the log-rank test.

Ethics Considerations

These trials were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. All local regulatory requirements were followed. The trials were approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at each trial site. All patients provided written informed consent.

Results

Baseline Disease Characteristics

This analysis included pooled data from 1196 patients, including 588 who received abrocitinib 200 mg and 608 who received dupilumab 300 mg. Median age and duration of AD were generally balanced across baseline itch severity subgroups and across treatment arms (Table S1 of the Electronic Supplementary Material [ESM]). At baseline, patients with greater itch severity generally experienced greater disease severity, as indicated by higher Investigator’s Global Assessment, Eczema Area and Severity Index, and POEM scores, a higher percentage of body surface area affected, and worse QOL, as measured by the DLQI, compared with patients with less severe itch.

Itch Relief

The proportions of patients in the total population achieving PP-NRS4 were numerically greater with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab at weeks 2 and 16 (50% vs 26% and 67% vs 63%, respectively), but 95% CIs overlapped at week 16 (Fig. 1). At week 2, for baseline itch scores of 6–9, a greater proportion of patients who received abrocitinib versus dupilumab achieved symptom relief, while for baseline itch scores of 10, a numerically greater proportion of patients who received abrocitinib versus dupilumab achieved symptom relief. At week 16, the differences between the two treatments persisted, although with overlapping 95% CIs. The proportion of patients who achieved daily PP-NRS scores < 2 was generally numerically greater with abrocitinib than with dupilumab through day 15 in the total population (day 5: 5% vs 1%; day 10: 11% vs 3%; day 15: 17% vs 5%) and irrespective of baseline itch severity in almost all cases (Fig. S1 of the ESM). The proportion of patients who achieved an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) was numerically greater with abrocitinib (16.7–37.2%) compared with dupilumab (4.8–24.5%) at weeks 2, 12, and 16 (Table S2 of the ESM). Consistent with these findings, median time to achievement of an itch-free state was shorter with abrocitinib than with dupilumab, with differences of approximately 80–140 days observed in patients with higher baseline itch severity (Table 1).

Itch Relief Impact on QOL Patient-Reported Outcomes: POEM, DLQI, and Sleep

A higher proportion of patients who received abrocitinib achieved a POEM score of ≤ 2 in the total population compared with those who received dupilumab at weeks 12 and 16 (29% vs 14% and 27% vs 15%, respectively; Fig. 2). At weeks 12 and 16, substantial proportions of patients who achieved an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) with abrocitinib and dupilumab achieved a POEM score of ≤ 2. The mean DLQI scores in the total population at weeks 2, 12, and 16 were numerically lower with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab (6.2 vs 8.1, 4.0 vs 4.9, and 3.7 v 4.4, respectively; Table 2). At weeks 2, 12, and 16 of treatment with abrocitinib or dupilumab, patients with residual itch (PP-NRS score of ≥ 2) had a QOL impairment indicated by mean DLQI scores of > 3. A higher proportion of patients who received abrocitinib achieved DLQI 0/1 at weeks 2, 12, and 16 in the total population compared with those who received dupilumab (Fig. 2). Higher proportions of patients who achieved an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) with abrocitinib or dupilumab also achieved DLQI 0/1 compared with patients who had residual itch (PP-NRS score of ≥ 2) at each timepoint. Overall, a greater proportion of patients achieved combined scores of PP-NRS 0/1 and DLQI 0/1 at week 2 and PP-NRS 0/1, DLQI 0/1, and POEM < 3 at weeks 2, 12, and 16 with abrocitinib compared with those who received dupilumab (Fig. 3). Notably, at week 12, 38% of patients receiving abrocitinib achieved PP-NRS 0/1, 34% achieved DLQI 0/1, and 29% achieved POEM ≤ 2, compared with 25%, 28%, and 14%, respectively, in the dupilumab group. In the total population, a numerically greater proportion of patients treated with abrocitinib achieved a minimal impact of AD on their sleep as shown by SCORAD sleep loss VAS 0/1 scores compared with dupilumab at weeks 2, 12, and 16, and POEM sleep item 0 at weeks 12 and 16 (Fig. 4). Discussion

In this post-hoc pooled analysis of the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials, numerically greater proportions of patients achieved PP-NRS4, PP-NRS 0/1, POEM ≤ 2, POEM sleep item 0, DLQI 0/1, and SCORAD sleep loss VAS 0/1 with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab, irrespective of baseline itch severity. While early improvements in itch favored abrocitinib, differences between treatments were attenuated for some endpoints at week 16, with numerically greater responses for abrocitinib and overlapping CIs. Greater proportions of patients achieving an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) also achieved DLQI 0/1, POEM score of ≤ 2, SCORAD sleep loss VAS 0/1, and POEM sleep item 0 than patients with residual itch (PP-NRS ≥ 2). Itch improvement, as measured by PP-NRS4 and an itch-free state, occurred earlier with abrocitinib than with dupilumab, with median differences of up to approximately 4 months observed among patients with higher baseline itch severity. Rapid itch relief was particularly notable in patients with severe baseline itch, where the largest difference in median time to achieve an itch-free state was observed. Results are consistent with previous reports that abrocitinib treatment leads to rapid and substantial itch improvements (PP-NRS4 and PP-NRS 0/1), even in patients with severe baseline itch (PP-NRS ≥ 7) [14, 32, 33]. Across various thresholds, itch relief with abrocitinib and dupilumab was associated with QOL improvements. Patients highly value rapid itch relief and the achievement of an itch-free state [15–18], and abrocitinib may be an appropriate treatment option for patients who have severe itch and are seeking to achieve an itch-free state.

Although PP-NRS4 is well established as a prespecified outcome for clinical development programs for dermatologic therapies [34, 35], its clinical relevance may vary depending on the residual AD symptoms and impact on QOL experienced by patients. A previous study showed that in patients with more severe baseline itch, greater reductions in itch severity as assessed by the 24-hour VAS and NRS are required to achieve a minimal clinically important difference [36]. Greater proportions of patients who achieved PP-NRS 0/1 with either abrocitinib or dupilumab also achieved POEM scores of ≤ 2, DLQI 0/1, SCORAD sleep loss VAS 0/1, and POEM sleep 0. Many patients who did not reach an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1) did not achieve these outcomes. In a previous report, the greatest improvement in QOL by DLQI with abrocitinib 200 mg was observed among patients (baseline PP-NRS score of 7) who achieved PP-NRS 0/1, compared with those who only achieved PP-NRS4 [19]. Another study showed that more severe itch, as assessed by Patient-Oriented SCORAD, was positively associated with the increasing priority patients placed on achieving almost complete and complete skin clearance [17]. Taken together, these findings suggest that achievement of an itch-free state (PP-NRS 0/1), rather than a 4-point improvement, may be a more meaningful treatment target for patients with moderate-to-severe AD and across the spectrum of baseline itch severity.

Findings from this post hoc analysis of the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials highlight the focal role of JAK1 signaling in the pathogenesis of pruritus in AD and underscore the potential benefit of JAK1 inhibitors for patients requiring rapid itch relief. Current hypotheses suggest that selective JAK1 inhibition may influence a broader spectrum of immune and epidermal cytokines implicated in disease biology (e.g., thymic stromal lymphopoietin, IL-4, IL-13, IL-22, IL-31) [37]. However, further research is needed to delineate differences in mechanisms of action both among JAK inhibitors and in comparison with other systemic therapies. This post hoc analysis is subject to several limitations, including the relatively small sample sizes of the subgroups that may impact the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, relief in symptoms of AD other than itch may have influenced improvements in patients’ QOL or self-rated disease severity, potentially confounding the interpretation of results. For example, a previous report found that 66% of the patients who achieved PP-NRS 0/1 also achieved DLQI 0/1, suggesting that other factors influenced the QOL scores of the 34% of patients who reported DLQI scores > 1 [19]. Furthermore, post hoc analyses are not designed to directly compare the treatment responses; as such, any observed differences between the abrocitinib and dupilumab treatment groups are merely descriptive and should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusions

In this post hoc analysis of the JADE COMPARE and JADE DARE trials, the proportion of patients in the total population with moderate-to-severe AD achieving an itch-free state was higher with abrocitinib compared with dupilumab at early timepoints, with similar though numerically greater sustained itch control at week 16 and beyond. Median time to itch response was shorter with abrocitinib, particularly among patients with more severe baseline itch. Across treatments, patients who achieved an itch-free state experienced greater improvements in QOL and sleep outcomes than those with residual itch. Taken together, findings from this analysis suggest that prioritizing complete itch resolution (PP-NRS 0/1) as a treatment goal, especially in patients experiencing severe itch, may be more clinically meaningful for improving overall QOL and symptom burden in moderate-to-severe AD.

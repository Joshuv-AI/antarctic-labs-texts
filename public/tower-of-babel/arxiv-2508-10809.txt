Entangled Polariton States in the Visible and Mid-Infrared Spectral Ranges Preprint: APS/123-QED
Vladislav Yu. Shishkov 1, Oleg Kotov 2, Darius Urbonas 3, Lee A. Rozema 4, Francisco J. Garcia-Vidal 2,5, Johannes Feist 2,5, Anton V. Zasedatelev 1

Abstract
Entanglement generation in polariton systems is fundamentally constrained by high losses and decoherence, which typically outweigh polariton nonlinearities. Here, we propose a conceptually different approach that uses optomechanical interactions, rather than polariton–polariton interactions, to generate entangled polaritons. Our double-resonant scheme relies on strong exciton–phonon coupling, found in both inorganic and molecular semiconductors, enabling room-temperature generation of spectrally disparate photon pairs. The quantum coherent and delocalized nature of polariton states inside optical cavity ensures efficient single-mode outcoupling and allows for unconditional quantum state preparation - not relying on any post-selection or projective measurements. When conditioned on exciton-polariton emission, single phonon–polariton states can be prepared that subsequently yield bright, heralded single-photon emission in the mid-IR. We introduce a double-resonant optomechanical platform that enables scalable, room-temperature quantum polaritonics without relying on conventional excitonic nonlinearities.



Introduction.

Quantum technologies are currently transforming communication, sensing, and computation. Among the various platforms, photonic approaches are especially promising.
With respect to quantum communication, photons remain the leading candidate for interfacing different quantum systems and transmitting quantum information between remote parties
Wang et al. 2020a
.
Photonic quantum computing is also advancing
Madsen et al. 2022
;
PsiQuantum_team 2025
, but it is hindered by the absence of strong, controllable photon–photon interactions required for deterministic two‑qubit gates and for generating entanglement.
Matter-based systems, by contrast, readily provide interactions, but are hampered by decoherence. Hybrid quasiparticles known as polaritons, coherent superpositions of photonic and electronic degrees of freedom, inherit the best of both worlds: a light effective mass and high group velocity from their photonic component, together with strong nonlinearities from their matter component
Carusotto and Ciuti 2013
.
This seems to make polaritons uniquely poised serve both as a nonlinear medium and as an interface between quantum communication channels and quantum computing devices.

Early theoretical work predicted that polariton–polariton interactions in solid‑state microcavities could naturally produce entangled states
Ciuti et al. 2001
;
Ciuti 2004
;
Oka and Ishihara 2008
.
In principle, parametric scattering of polaritons is sufficiently strong because of their matter component
Savvidis et al. 2000
;
Messin et al. 2001
;
Saba et al. 2001
;
Sun et al. 2017
.
In practice, however, the situation proved more complex: the desired quantum correlations are entirely washed out by competing processes such as thermalization via acoustic phonons, incoherent scattering by disordered states, and still-large cavity losses that exceed the pair‑particle scattering rate
Karr et al. 2004
;
Suárez-Forero et al. 2016
. Consequently, to-date entanglement has not been demonstrated in solid‑state polariton systems.

Meanwhile, the quantum-coherent nature of polaritons at the single-particle level has been explored in various settings
Cuevas et al. 2018
;
Muñoz-Matutano et al. 2019
;
Delteil et al. 2019
.
An important experimental tests involved injecting externally entangled photons into a polariton system
Cuevas et al. 2018
, demonstrating that polaritons can inherit and transmit quantum information. The measured concurrence remained high (0.806), indicating strong preservation of quantum correlations within exciton-polariton systems.

Therefore, the ability to generate entanglement intrinsically within a polariton system remains a central challenge in solid-state quantum optics, that would provide a novel route towards scalable quantum technologies.
Polariton blockade could potentially isolate two-particle states, leading to quantum-correlated photon pairs
Muñoz-Matutano et al. 2019
;
Delteil et al. 2019
.
The interaction strength required to achieve this, quantified by the interaction over loss ratio
g/\gamma

With current estimates of
g/\gamma\sim 0.1
, the blockade effect was evidenced by antibunching at the level
g^{(2)}(0)=0.9

Muñoz-Matutano et al. 2019
;
Delteil et al. 2019
, which so far keeps these proposals at bay.
Enhancement via Feshbach resonances involving biexciton or triexciton bound states
Takemura et al. 2014
could push the system toward the strong single-particle interaction regime
g/\gamma>1

Carusotto et al. 2010
, to be achieved.
In such a regime, cascaded emission from anharmonic ladders of interacting polaritons could serve as a source of entangled photon pairs
Scarpelli et al. 2024
, similar to the biexciton cascades in quantum dots
Akopian et al. 2006
.
Strong light–matter coupling with Rydberg excitons in
\rm Cu_{2}O

Kazimierczuk et al. 2014
;
Heckötter et al. 2018
;
Heckötter et al. 2021
;
Orfanakis et al. 2022
.
Similarly, dipolar polaritons
Rosenberg et al. 2018

;
Unuchek et al. 2018

Beyond the single-particle level and discrete variables, the macroscopic quantum coherence of polaritons has been demonstrated through collective phenomena such as Bose–Einstein condensation
Kasprzak et al. 2006
, superfluidity
Amo et al. 2009
;
Lerario et al. 2017
, quantized vortices
Lagoudakis et al. 2008
among others
Carusotto and Ciuti 2013
. These observations confirm that polaritons can form collective quantum states, motivating proposals to harness such states for entanglement generation. In particular, a recent theoretical scheme shows that spin squeezing can be realized in a resonantly pumped spinor polariton condensate
Feng et al. 2021
leveraging the system’s intrinsic nonlinearities to drive one-axis twisting dynamics analogous to those used in atomic BECs
Kitzinger et al. 2021
. Two-mode squeezing below the standard quantum limit can be used as experimental witness for bi-partite entanglement in continuous variables
Feng et al. 2021
;
Feng et al. 2023
. Achieving strong squeezing in polariton BEC represents a standalone challenge, though first evidences for quadrature squeezing in polariton system has already been demonstrated
Boulier et al. 2014
;
Adiyatullin et al. 2017
.

In this work, we propose a conceptually new approach to generate entangled polaritons, fundamentally distinct from conventional schemes relying on polariton-polariton nonlinearities. Our platform leverages a double-resonant polariton system with intrinsic optomechanical interaction, where strong exciton–phonon coupling - naturally present in both inorganic and molecular semiconductors - enables robust entanglement generation at room temperature.
In conventional optomechanics, photons interact with mechanical motion via radiation pressure or dispersive coupling
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
. In our system, both the optical and mechanical modes are no longer pure - they are hybrid light–matter excitations. The optical mode is an exciton-polariton, a coherent superposition of cavity photons and electronic excitations in matter. The mechanical mode arises from collective vibrational type excitations within the matter, delocalized
phonons
. The optomechanical interaction between them allows for the bright emission of spectrally disparate entangled photon pairs (e.g., mid-IR and visible), with spectral flexibility enabled by non-resonant excitation. Crucially, the delocalized nature of polariton states ensures efficient outcoupling into a single optical mode, inherently erasing which-path information from individual emitters coupled to the cavity. This enables quantum state preparation at room temperature without the need for mode post-selection.

We present a new paradigm for highly efficient generation of entangled light under ambient conditions.

The proposed system is a cavity coupled to matter, hosting excitons and phonons.
The hosted phonons are Raman and IR active. For the exciton-polariton states considered throughout this work, we use parameters from existing polariton platforms that have successfully demonstrated strong sideband-resolved optomechanical interactions at room temperature

Unlike conventional optomechanics, where photons couple to mechanical motion via radiation pressure or dispersive interactions
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014

Besides the aforementioned optomechanical physics of exciton-polaritons and phonons, our system expresses strong interaction between phonons and the IR cavity, leading to the formation of phonon-polaritons.
Thus, we enter a new regime in optomechanics where both optical and mechanical degrees of freedom are hybrid light-matter states, allowing the manipulation with IR light and optical readout of mechanical states.

Probing of quantum entanglement between exciton-polaritons and phonon-polaritons via their emitted light requires the ability to detect single photons in the mid-IR spectral range, which is limited at the current level of technology

Additionally, we propose converting the two-mode squeezing associated with the entanglement into single-mode quadrature squeezing of the visible-range emission from exciton-polariton states, followed by interferometric detection of the quadrature squeezing below the standard quantum limit (see Supplementary Information for details).

Conclusion.
In summary, we have proposed and analyzed a double-resonant polariton system that enables entanglement generation between exciton-polaritons and phonon-polaritons under coherent optical pumping. The scheme exploits intrinsic optomechanical interactions arising from strong exciton–phonon coupling, enabling robust entanglement generation at room temperature.
Indeed, the system proves to be resilient against thermal fluctuations due to the high phonon energies, and, owing to the strong light-matter interaction, quantum correlations remain robust across a wide range of parameters.
Our theoretical model demonstrates that this system emits spectrally disparate entangled photon pairs, spanning the visible and mid-IR regions, with distinct correlations arising from quantum interference between upper and lower phonon-polariton pathways. We demonstrate entanglement by crossing the separability boundary in logarithmic negativity and through strong violation of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in second-order photon correlation function.

Importantly, the double-resonant optomechanical system enables highly-efficient generation of entangled photon pairs spanning the visible and mid-IR spectral regions. This is particularly relevant for quantum sensing, imaging, and spectroscopy in the infrared, where direct detection is often challenging
Defienne et al. 2024
;
Defienne et al. 2019
;
Lemos et al. 2014
;
Kviatkovsky et al. 2020
;
Kviatkovsky et al. 2022
;
Gilaberte Basset et al. 2023
. A key limitation for mid-IR quantum technologies is the lack of materials with suitable bandgaps to efficiently produce high-brightness single photons in this range
Lu et al. 2019
;
Iles-Smith et al. 2025
. In this context, heralding visible photons from strongly correlated visible–mid-IR pairs
Goldschmidt et al. 2008
;
Kaneda et al. 2016
;
Clark et al. 2013
;
Mosley et al. 2008
;
Meany et al. 2014
offers a route to create bright and chip-scale single-photon sources in the mid-IR.

, which is consistent with the experiments on strong vibrational coupling in Fabry–Perot cavities
Simpkins et al. 2015
;
Simpkins et al. 2023
.
We also denoted
X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k},{\rm Vis}|{\bf k}}=X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k},{\rm VisR}|{\bf k}}+X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k},{\rm VisL}|{\bf k}}
and
X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm Vis}|{\bf k}}=X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm VisR}|{\bf k}}+X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm VisL}|{\bf k}}
and set
\omega_{\Omega}=\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}
, such that the frequency of the external field matches the frequency of the lower polariton state with the wave vector
{\bf k}_{i}
.

Preserving only the resonance terms in Eq. (
22
), corresponding to the phonon-assisted transitions from upper exciton-polaritons to lower exciton-polaritons, we obtain

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\rm Dark}+\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}+\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+\hbar\Omega_{{\bf k}_{i}}\left(\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}+\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}\right)\\
+\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}={\bf q}}\left(\hbar g_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+h.c.\right)+\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}={\bf q}}\left(\hbar g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+h.c.\right)\\
+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar{\omega_{\rm Vib}}\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar{\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}}\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}{{\hat{a}}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}}+\sum\limits_{{\bf q}}\hbar\Omega_{{\rm IR}}\left(\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}+\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}\right)

(22)

where the optomechanical interaction constants of exciton-polaritons with bright vibrations and mid-IR cavity photons,
g_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}
and
g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}
, are

g_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}=\frac{\Lambda\Omega_{\rm Vis}}{\sqrt{N_{\rm exc}}}\left(X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}},{\rm Vis}|{\bf k^{\prime}}}^{*}X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm Exc}}-X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}},{\rm Exc}}^{*}X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm Vis}|{\bf k}}\right)

(23)

g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}=-\frac{\Lambda\Omega_{\rm IR}}{\sqrt{N_{\rm exc}}}X_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}},{\rm Exc}}^{*}X_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k},{\rm Exc}}

(24)

The term proportional to
g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}
is not trivial, as there is no direct coupling between the mid-IR cavity photons and either the visible cavity photons or the excitons in the initial Hamiltonian (
12
).
Nevertheless, this term describes the coupling of mid-IR cavity photons with exciton-polariton states originating from the optomechanical interaction.
This interaction is a direct consequence of the excitonic dressing of the vibrational states.
The term
\hat{H}_{\rm Dark}
represents the dark states that act as a reservoir for both the exciton-polaritons and phonon-polaritons
Shishkov et al. 2024c
.

To address the strong coupling between vibrational states and the mid-IR cavity photons, we introduce upper phonon-polaritons,
\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}
, and lower phonon-polaritons,
\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}
, as follows

\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}=\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}\sin\phi_{{\bf q}}+\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}\cos\phi_{{\bf q}},

(25)

\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}=\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}\cos\phi_{{\bf q}}-\hat{c}_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf q}}\sin\phi_{{\bf q}},

(26)

where

\phi_{{\bf q}}={\rm arctg}\left[\sqrt{\frac{(\omega_{\rm Vib}-\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}})^{2}+4\Omega_{\rm IR}^{2}}{4\Omega_{\rm IR}^{2}}}-\frac{\omega_{\rm Vib}-\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}}{2\Omega_{\rm IR}}\right].

(27)

The eigen frequencies of the upper and lower phonon-polaritons are

\omega_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf q}}=\frac{\omega_{\rm Vib}+\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\left(\omega_{\rm Vib}-\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}\right)^{2}}{4}+\Omega_{\rm IR}^{2}},

(28)

\omega_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf q}}=\frac{\omega_{\rm Vib}+\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{\left(\omega_{\rm Vib}-\omega_{{\rm IR}|{\bf q}}\right)^{2}}{4}+\Omega_{\rm IR}^{2}}.

(29)

After transformation to upper and lower phonon-polaritons, the Hamiltonian (
22
) can be reformulated as follows

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\rm Dark}+\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}+\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf q}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf q}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}\\
+\hbar\Omega_{{\bf k}_{i}}\left(\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}+\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}\right)+\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}={\bf q}}\left[\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}\left(\hbar g_{{\rm U}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}+\hbar g_{{\rm L}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}\right)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+h.c.\right],

(30)

where the optomechanical interaction constants for phonon-polaritons are

g_{{\rm U}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}=g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\sin\phi_{{\bf k^{\prime}}-{\bf k}}+g_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\cos\phi_{{\bf k^{\prime}}-{\bf k}},

(31)

g_{{\rm L}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}=g_{{\rm IR}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\cos\phi_{{\bf k^{\prime}}-{\bf k}}-g_{{\rm Vib}|{\bf kk^{\prime}}}\sin\phi_{{\bf k^{\prime}}-{\bf k}}.

(32)

Thus, strong interaction between excitons and vibrations with cavity modes in the visible range and mid-IR range leads to the formation of new eigenstates, exciton- and phonon-polaritons
Kirton and Keeling 2013
;
Wu et al. 2016
;
Tereshchenkov et al. 2024
.
The part of the Hamiltonian (

Shishkov et al. 2024c
,

\hat{H}_{\rm OM}=\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}}+\sum\limits_{\bf k}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}}+\hbar\Omega_{{\bf k}_{i}}(t)\left(\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}+h.c.\right)+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf q}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf q}}\\
+\sum\limits_{\bf q}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf q}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf q}}+\sum_{{\bf k}_{i},{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}+h.c.\right]+\sum_{{\bf k}_{i},{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}+h.c.\right],

(33)

.

As we discussed in the main text, when the main channel of energy flow from directly driven upper exciton-polaritons is the dissipation, their dynamics become completely independent of the evolution of the lower exciton-polaritons and phonon-polaritons.
In this case, we can exclude upper exciton-polaritons adiabatically, replacing
\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)
with
\sqrt{\langle\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)\rangle}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}=\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}
and disregarding the redundant constant phase of
\hat{s}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)
.
Thus, we can exclude upper exciton-polaritons adiabatically and obtain the linearized optomechanical Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\rm OM}\approx\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}+\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}+\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\\
+\sum_{{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}+h.c.\right]+\sum_{{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}+h.c.\right].

(39)

We consider slow dynamics of lower exciton-polaritons, replacing
\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}=\hat{S}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}
.
As a result we obtain

\hat{H}_{\rm OM}\approx\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\left(\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}-\omega_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}}\right)\hat{S}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{S}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}+\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}+\sum\limits_{{\bf k}_{f}}\hbar\omega_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\\
+\sum_{{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)}\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{S}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}+h.c.\right]+\sum_{{\bf k}_{f}}\left[\hbar g_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)}\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}\hat{S}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}+h.c.\right].

(40)

For the sake of briefness in the

instead of

in Hamiltonian (

Appendix C
Covariance matrix of exciton-polaritons and phonon-polarions

In this Appendix, we use the Hamiltonian (
40
) (or equally Hamiltonian (
39
)) to find the covariance matrix of exciton-polaritons and phonon-polarions, i.e. matrix
\langle\hat{A}(t)\hat{B}(t+\tau)\rangle
, where
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
each means one of the following operators
\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}
,
\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}
,
\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
,
\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}
,
\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
, or
\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}^{\dagger}
.
We consider two limiting cases: CW excitations, when
n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)=n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}=const
, and pulsed excitation with short pulse such that
n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t)=n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(0)e^{-\gamma_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t}
, where
n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(0)
is the number of initially excited upper polaritons.
Finally, we will discuss the applicability of the linearized approach.

C.1
CW excitation

To describe the quantum dynamics of the system in the CW excitation regime, we use Hamiltonian (
39
) and obtain the following Heisenberg–Langevin equation for each wave vector
{\bf k}_{f}
separately

\frac{d\hat{\bf A}(t)}{dt}=\mathcal{M}\hat{\bf A}(t)+\hat{\bf F}(t)

(41)

where
\hat{\bf A}(t)
is the vector of exciton-polariton and phonon-polariton operators

\hat{\bf A}(t)=\left(\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t},\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t},\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{v}^{\dagger}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{v}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right)^{\rm T}

(42)

and
\hat{\bf F}(t)
is the vector of noise operators

\hat{\bf F}(t)=\left(\hat{f}_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t},\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)e^{-i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t},\hat{f}_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{f}_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t),\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right)^{\rm T}.

(43)

The noise operators in
\hat{\bf F}(t)
have zero mean
\langle\hat{\bf F}(t)\rangle=0
and are delta-correlated,
\langle\hat{\bf F}(t_{1})\hat{\bf F}^{\rm T}(t_{2})\rangle=\mathcal{D}_{F}\delta(t_{1}-t_{2})

Gardiner and Zoller 2004
;
Coffey and Kalmykov 2012

\mathcal{D}_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{D}_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{D}_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}&0\\
0&0&\mathcal{D}_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\end{pmatrix},

(44)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha|{\bf q}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\gamma_{\alpha|{\bf q}}(1+n^{\rm th}_{\alpha|{\bf q}})\\
\gamma_{\alpha|{\bf q}}n^{\rm th}_{\alpha|{\bf q}}&0\end{pmatrix},

(45)

n^{\rm th}_{\alpha|{\bf q}}=(e^{\hbar\omega_{\alpha|{\bf q}}/k_{B}T}-1)^{-1}
,
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant, and
T
is the ambient temperature.

The matrix of the differential equation (
41
) is

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{s_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}+\mathcal{J}\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}&\mathcal{G}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}&\mathcal{G}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\\
\mathcal{G}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}&\mathcal{L}_{v_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}&0\\
\mathcal{G}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}&0&\mathcal{L}_{v_{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}\end{pmatrix},

(46)

where

\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix},

(47)

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha|{\bf q}}=\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_{\alpha|{\bf q}}-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha|{\bf q}}}{2}&0\\
0&i\omega_{\alpha|{\bf q}}-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha|{\bf q}}}{2}\end{pmatrix},

(48)

\mathcal{G}_{{\rm U/L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-ig_{{\rm U/L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}}\\
ig_{{\rm U/L}|{\bf k}_{f}{\bf k}_{i}}^{*}\sqrt{n_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(49)

The solution of Eq. (
41
) is
\hat{\bf A}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\mathcal{M}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{\bf F}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
.
We use this solution to obtain all the paired correlations of the operators listed in Eq. (
42
).

\langle\hat{\bf A}(t_{1})\hat{\bf A}^{\rm T}(t_{2})\rangle=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\min(t_{1},t_{2})}e^{\mathcal{M}(t_{1}-t^{\prime})}\mathcal{D}_{F}e^{\mathcal{M}^{\rm T}(t_{2}-t^{\prime})}dt^{\prime}.

(50)

It is evident that
\langle\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{1})\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{2})\rangle e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t_{1}}
is the first row, third column of the matrix
\langle\hat{\bf A}(t_{1})\hat{\bf A}^{\rm T}(t_{2})\rangle
;
\langle\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{1})\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{2})\rangle e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}t_{1}}
is the first row, fifth column;
\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{1})\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{2})\rangle e^{i\omega_{s_{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}}(t_{2}-t_{1})}
is the second row, first column;
\langle\hat{v}^{\dagger}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{1})\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{2})\rangle
is the forth row, third column; and
\langle\hat{v}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{1})\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t_{2})\rangle
is the sixth row, fifth column of this matrix.

(52)

where
n^{\rm th}_{\alpha|{\bf q}}=(e^{\hbar\omega_{\alpha|{\bf q}}/k_{B}T}-1)^{-1}

E.1
Second-order cross-correlation function

The second-order cross-correlation function between visible light with the wave vector
{\bf k}_{f}
and the mid-IR light with the wave vector
{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}
is

g^{(2)}_{\text{Vis-IR}}(\tau)=\frac{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle}{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\right\rangle}.

(77)

Below the optomechanical instability threshold, we can use the linearized version of Hamiltonian (
30
) over the coherent drive
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
.
In this case, the operators
\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}
,
\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
, and
\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
are proportional to the noise operators.
In the Born–Markov approximation, these noise operators obey Isserlis’s theorem
Coffey and Kalmykov 2012
.
Therefore, the Isserlis’s theorem also applies to the operators
\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}
,
\hat{v}_{{\rm U}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
, and
\hat{v}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}
.
We apply Isserlis’s theorem to the numerator of Eq. (
77
) and obtain

{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle}=\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\right\rangle\\
+\left|\left\langle\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\right|^{2}+\left|\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\right|^{2}

(78)

The solution of the Heisenberg–Langevin equation provided in Appendix
C
implies
\left|\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\right|^{2}=0
.
Thus, we obtain

g^{(2)}_{\text{Vis-IR}}(\tau)=1+\frac{|\left\langle\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle|^{2}}{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t+\tau)\right\rangle}

(79)

E.2
Second-order autocorrelation function of heralded mid-IR light

The second-order cross-correlation function of a heralded single-photon source of mid-IR light is
Panyukov et al. 2022

g^{(2)}_{\text{IR(her)}}(0)=\frac{g^{(3)}_{\rm Vis-IR}(0)}{\left[g^{(2)}_{\rm Vis-IR}(0)\right]^{2}}

(80)

where

g^{(3)}_{\text{Vis-IR}}(0)=\frac{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle}{\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle^{2}}

(81)

We apply Isserlis’s theorem to the numerator of Eq. (
81
) and obtain

\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle=\\
2\left\langle\hat{s}^{\dagger}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle^{2}+4\left|\left\langle\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{s}_{{\rm L}|{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle\right|^{2}\left\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\hat{a}_{{\rm IR}|{\bf k}_{i}-{\bf k}_{f}}(t)\right\rangle

(82)

As a result, we obtain

g^{(2)}_{\text{IR(her)}}(0)=\frac{4}{g^{(2)}_{\text{Vis-IR}}(0)}-\frac{2}{[g^{(2)}_{\text{Vis-IR}}(0)]^{2}}.

(83)

Integrating carbon reduction and ecological resilience strategies in the silk road economic corridor
Authors: Wei He, Jun Zhang, Wei Zheng, Weichun Gao, Penghui Zhang, Nan Qu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The Silk Road Economic Belt has become a pivotal region for China's sustainable development, focusing on the balance between carbon emissions and ecological resilience. Understanding the coordination between these factors is crucial for advancing China's westward economic shift and optimizing urban and demographic structures. This study investigates the coupling and coordination dynamics between carbon emissions and ecological resilience across 15 key cities in the Silk Road Economic Belt, using data from 2008 to 2018. By applying a "pressure-state-response" framework, the ecological resilience of the region is quantitatively assessed. Spatial disparities and the dynamic evolution of coupling coordination are analyzed through the Gini coefficient and kernel density estimation. The findings reveal significant regional variations in ecological resilience and a decline in coupling coordination over time. Shifts in the patterns of coordination indicate opportunities for enhancing resource utilization and environmental management. This research provides theoretical insights for policymakers to improve green development strategies, optimize industrial structures, and plan urban green spaces more effectively within the Silk Road Economic Belt.

Introduction

Climate change, primarily driven by the sharp rise in carbon emissions, stands as one of the most urgent global challenges (IPCC, 2021). Its far-reaching impacts disrupt ecosystems, economies, and societies, prompting countries worldwide to find a delicate balance between economic development and environmental sustainability [1,2]. In this context, regions like the Silk Road Economic Belt (SREB), a key component of China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), have gained attention for their dual role in economic growth and environmental conservation [3]. The SREB, extending across multiple provinces, not only facilitates China's westward economic shift but also serves as a critical battleground for achieving the country's carbon reduction and sustainability goals [4].

Despite significant economic progress, the SREB faces considerable environmental challenges, with cities in this region acting as major contributors to carbon emissions due to their industrial activities [5]. As China pledges to peak carbon emissions by 2030 and achieve carbon neutrality by 2060, understanding the interplay between carbon emissions and ecological resilience in these urban centers has become increasingly vital [6]. Urban areas, responsible for more than 70 % of China's total emissions, are central to national carbon reduction efforts and sustainable development [7].

While previous research has explored carbon emissions at national and provincial levels, limited attention has been given to the relationship between carbon emissions and ecological resilience at the city level, particularly in the context of sustainable urban development [8]. Urban resilience, typically defined as a city's ability to recover from economic and environmental shocks, now requires a broader approach that considers the challenges of integrating environmental sustainability with economic growth [9].

This study aims to address this gap by examining the coupling and coordination dynamics between carbon emissions and ecological resilience across 15 key cities in the SREB between 2008 and 2018. By applying a pressure-state-response framework [10] and utilizing tools such as the Gini coefficient and kernel density estimation, this research investigates the spatial disparities in these dynamics across the Eastern, Southwest, and Northwest regions of the SREB. The primary objectives of this study are: (1) to quantify the relationship between carbon emissions and ecological resilience in key cities; (2) to examine the regional disparities in these dynamics; and (3) to provide insights for policymakers to develop green development strategies and optimize urban planning.

By offering a city-level perspective, this study contributes to a deeper understanding of how the SREB can integrate carbon reduction initiatives with ecological sustainability. The findings are intended to inform targeted policy interventions that balance economic growth and environmental protection, providing a more sustainable development framework for the region [11].

Materials and methods

In this section, a succinct overview of the distribution of the sampled cities along the Silk Road Economic Belt is provided. Subsequently, the development of the index system and the sourcing of data are briefly delineated. Within the ambit of this study, three primary analytical steps are undertaken. Firstly, carbon emission data are meticulously computed adhering to the formula prescribed by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). Secondly, the coupling coordination degree model is deployed to meticulously assess the degree of interdependence between ecological resilience and carbon emissions in the central cities situated within the Silk Road Economic Belt. Thirdly, the Dagum Gini coefficient serves as a robust tool to exhaustively examine the magnitude and sources of disparities. Furthermore, kernel density estimation is employed to elucidate the absolute differences, distribution patterns, and spatial locations of the coupling coordination degree within the Silk Road Economic Belt, offering a comprehensive understanding of the dynamic interplay between ecological resilience and carbon emissions in this strategic corridor.

Site description

According to the Belt and Road Global Action report, the Silk Road Economic Belt encompasses 16 provinces, namely Heilongjiang, Jilin, Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, Henan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia, Qinghai, Chongqing, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Guangxi, Xinjiang, and Tibet. This conception underscores the Silk Road Economic Belt's aim to optimize urban and demographic dispersion to its fullest extent [12]. The geomorphic characteristics of the provinces within this economic belt exhibit profound variations, particularly those located in the Northwest region—Ningxia, Gansu, Qinghai, and Xinjiang—which are situated within the desert zone, characterized by predominantly desert and Gobi landscapes, meager rainfall, sparse vegetation cover, and arduous ecological conditions. While initial environmental protection and restoration efforts have yielded positive outcomes, marked by notable improvements in ecological environment quality, the concomitant expansion of industrial scale and rapid urbanization have led to the encroachment of ecological space. Furthermore, industrial development has become increasingly resource-intensive, with issues of high resource consumption and low utilization efficiency remaining salient [13]. Cities, as pivotal nodes of economic growth and ecological governance, play a crucial role in facilitating coordinated and environmentally sustainable development. Since zhang et al.(2023) [15] seminally conceptualized the resilience of the "society-ecological" system through his "adaptive cycle" model, the notion of resilience has evolved and broadened, encompassing constructs such as "single balance," "multiple balance," and ultimately, "complex adaptive systems." Resilience, with its dynamic essence, coevolutionary attributes, and associated qualities, has garnered widespread application in research exploring coping strategies aimed at fortifying the resilience of urban systems. Urban resilience is fundamentally defined as a city's innate ability to mitigate harm and self-recover in the face of unpredictable internal and external perturbations [16]. Consequently, urban resilience serves as a metric to assess the adaptability and responsiveness of complex urban systems confronted with emerging threats. However, this study specifically focuses on the dimension of ecological resilience, as it offers a more nuanced indicator of a city's capability to preserve ecological systems and manage pollution. Ecological resilience, owing to the intricate interplay of its measurement components, stands as a potent catalyst for urbanization's sustainable progression. The model of carbon emission strength

In instances where official statistics on carbon emissions are not readily available, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) methodology is routinely employed as a reliable means to quantify greenhouse gas emissions. Recognizing the significance of accounting for greenhouse gas emissions stemming from energy consumption at the production stage, this study adopts the methodology developed by Mo (2024) [17]. Specifically, the terminal consumption of eight distinct energy types—encompassing unprocessed coal, coke, crude oil, gasoline, kerosene, diesel, fuel oil, and natural gas—is meticulously selected and analyzed. Carbon emissions (CE) are subsequently computed based on these consumption patterns, offering a precise quantification of emissions. Furthermore, to gain a deeper understanding of the regional carbon footprint, the regional carbon emission intensity (CI) is calculated. This metric is derived by dividing the total emissions of CO2 by the corresponding regional GDP, thereby providing an indication of CO2 emissions per unit of economic output. Discussion

This study presents an in-depth analysis of the coupling and coordination dynamics between carbon emissions and ecological resilience across key cities in the Silk Road Economic Belt (SREB). The findings provide valuable insights into the spatial and temporal disparities within this critical region, which is central to China's efforts to balance economic development with environmental sustainability.

Regional disparities in carbon emissions and ecological resilience

One of the most significant findings is the pronounced regional disparities in carbon emission intensity, with the Northwest region consistently exhibiting the highest levels throughout the study period. This can be attributed to the region's economic reliance on energy-intensive industries, such as heavy manufacturing, mining, and fossil fuel-based energy production, which significantly contribute to its carbon footprint [22]. Despite policies aimed at reducing emissions, the region's industrial composition makes it difficult to achieve significant improvements in carbon intensity [23]. In contrast, the Eastern region demonstrates relatively lower carbon emissions intensity, reflecting its more advanced economic structure and the implementation of cleaner energy technologies. This finding aligns with previous research, which suggests that economically developed regions in China tend to adopt more stringent environmental regulations and advanced technologies, thus reducing their carbon intensity [24]. The Southwest region, although lagging behind the Eastern region, shows improvement in carbon emission efficiency due to recent industrial upgrades and the adoption of more sustainable practices [25].

Temporal decline in coupling coordination

The observed decline in the coupling coordination degree between carbon emissions and ecological resilience over the study period highlights the complexity of achieving a balance between economic growth and environmental sustainability. The initial sharp decrease from 2008 to 2013 corresponds to a period of rapid economic growth and industrial expansion in the SREB, particularly in regions where environmental governance lagged behind economic development. This is consistent with the findings of Wu et al. (2022), who identified a similar trend in the early phases of China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) [11]. From 2014 to 2018, the slower rate of decline in coupling coordination can be partially explained by the introduction of more targeted environmental policies and efforts to curb emissions, especially in the Eastern region. However, the fact that ecological resilience did not improve at the same rate as emissions declined suggests that the current environmental policies may not be sufficient to restore ecological systems that have been degraded by years of industrial activity [13]. The slower progress in restoring ecological resilience also points to the need for more comprehensive strategies that address not only emission reductions but also the broader ecological health of urban and regional systems.

Underlying mechanisms

Several key mechanisms contribute to the observed regional and temporal variations in the coupling coordination between carbon emissions and ecological resilience:

Economic and Industrial Drivers: The disparity in carbon emissions intensity across regions can be largely explained by differences in industrial structures. Regions such as the Northwest, which are dominated by energy-intensive industries, face significant challenges in reducing emissions despite economic growth [8]. The Eastern region, by contrast, benefits from a more diversified economy and advanced technological infrastructure, enabling it to pursue greener development paths [26].

Policy and Governance: The differences in regional environmental policies also play a crucial role. The Eastern region's relatively advanced environmental policies, including stricter emissions controls and promotion of renewable energy, have contributed to its better performance in coupling coordination [27]. However, regions like the Northwest lag behind due to weaker environmental governance and a greater reliance on traditional, carbon-intensive industries [22].

Broader implications

The findings have important implications for policymakers, especially as China aims to peak carbon emissions by 2030 and achieve carbon neutrality by 2060. The significant regional disparities observed in the SREB suggest that one-size-fits-all policies may not be effective in addressing the complex dynamics of carbon emissions and ecological resilience. Instead, region-specific strategies should be developed to reflect the unique economic and environmental contexts of each area. For example, in the Northwest region, policies should focus on transforming the industrial base toward more energy-efficient and less carbon-intensive industries, while also investing in technologies that enhance ecological resilience [3]. In the Eastern region, further development of green technologies and renewable energy sources should be encouraged to maintain and enhance the existing progress in reducing emissions [20].

Global significance

The broader significance of this study lies in its contribution to understanding how large economic corridors like the SREB can simultaneously pursue economic growth and environmental sustainability. Given that the SREB covers a significant portion of China's population and economy, its success or failure in achieving low-carbon development will have a substantial impact on global efforts to combat climate change. The findings also underscore the need for continued innovation in both policy and technology to ensure that ecological resilience can keep pace with economic growth. In line with global commitments under the Paris Agreement, the SREB must continue to explore sustainable development models that reduce emissions while enhancing the resilience of ecosystems to climate change [[28], [29], [30], [31]]. This study provides valuable insights into how regional disparities can hinder or accelerate these efforts and offers a basis for developing more targeted strategies to achieve balanced development.

Conclusions and policy recommendations

Conclusions

First, significant regional imbalances exist across the Silk Road Economic Belt, with the Southwest, Northwest, and Eastern regions exhibiting distinct patterns in the coupling and coordination between carbon emissions and ecological resilience. The southern regions tend to have higher coordination levels, while northern regions show lower coordination, suggesting the need for targeted regional interventions.

Second, the study reveals an increasing trend of spatial disequilibrium in the coupling coordination between carbon emissions and ecological resilience in the Southwest and Northwest regions, while the Eastern region shows gradual improvement. These findings underscore the importance of region-specific strategies to promote balanced and sustainable development.

Third, the kernel density analysis demonstrates a leftward shift in the primary peak, indicating a decline in the overall coupling coordination between carbon emissions and ecological resilience over the observation period. This calls for continuous monitoring and strategic policy adjustments to align development with long-term sustainability goals.

Policy recommendations

In light of the government's strategy for sustainable and comprehensive development in tandem with the Silk Road Economic Belt, and drawing upon the research findings, we propose the following policy amendments to enhance the coordination between ecological resilience and carbon emission reduction within the region:

Promoting circular and ecologically sound development models. It is essential to establish a green, circular, and resilient economic growth system that balances environmental conservation with economic development. This requires accelerating the shift towards low-carbon lifestyles and production methods. The focus should move from controlling fossil fuel consumption to controlling overall emission levels. Support for renewable energy sources and sustainable industries must be strengthened, using the achievements in economic development to improve ecological quality. This will create a positive feedback loop, ensuring the sustainable growth of the region.

Mitigating ecological pressures and enhancing ecological resilience. Due to the specific resource constraints faced by central cities in the Silk Road Economic Belt, it is crucial to promote green industrial development. Traditional industries need to undergo green transformation, phasing out resource-intensive and highly polluting production capacities. Implementing an early warning system, coupled with dynamic monitoring and information-sharing platforms for critical resources such as land and water, will be instrumental in safeguarding the ecological environment. These initiatives will also enable more efficient allocation of resources and encourage cross-regional cooperation on river basin management.

Facilitating coupling coordination through localized and region-specific measures. Policy measures must recognize regional differences and implement tailored strategies for each area. For example: Eastern Region (e.g., Changchun, Harbin, Hohhot): Focus on improving resource efficiency, optimizing urban land use, and enhancing green space planning. Key objectives include setting ecological red lines and alleviating ecological pressure through smart urban planning. Northwest Region (e.g., Lanzhou, Yinchuan): Urgently optimize industrial structures, promote clean energy, and support low-carbon industries. This region should prioritize improving its ecological response mechanisms to better manage environmental challenges. Southwest Region (e.g., Chengdu, Chongqing): Continue prioritizing ecological resilience through scientific urbanization and the creation of green space systems. These cities should capitalize on their experience and focus on bolstering ecosystem services, particularly those related to self-purification and the support of ecological industries. Urumqi: Strengthen investments in transportation and public infrastructure to boost urbanization. Establish mechanisms for talent recruitment and retention to support long-term urban and economic growth.

Research limitations and future research

This study is subject to several limitations. First, the lack of more granular, city-level data limits the ability to explore intra-city variations in carbon emissions and ecological resilience. Future research should leverage more detailed data sources, such as satellite imagery and real-time environmental monitoring, to gain deeper insights into local dynamics. Second, while this study focuses on carbon emissions and ecological resilience, other factors such as social and economic resilience should also be considered in future studies to provide a more holistic understanding of sustainability in the SREB.

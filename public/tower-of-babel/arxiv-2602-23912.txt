Block-weighted random graphs: planar and beyond
Mihyun Kang, Zéphyr Salvy, Ronen Wdowinski

Abstract
We investigate random connected graphs from a block-stable class whose distribution is weighted based on the number of 2 2 -connected components, or blocks. This includes the class of planar graphs. For this, we develop a notion of a decorated block tree. Following similar ideas to Fleurat and the second author on block-weighted planar maps , we find a phase transition in the singular behaviour of the appropriate generating function and in the typical structure of the block tree. Moreover, for certain block-stable classes (including planar graphs), we obtain precise enumeration results and determine also the typical sizes of the largest blocks in subcritical, critical, and supercritical regimes. It strengthens previously known results on block sizes in uniform random planar graphs.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and background

Planar maps and planar graphs have received much attention from enumerative and probabilistic points of view. The study of planar map enumeration started with the groundbreaking combinatorial work of Tutte, who gave a close formula for the number of rooted planar maps of size
n
in the 1960s
[
32
]
. Tutte’s work prompted many perspectives and extensions since
[
9
,
10
,
22
,
23
,
29
]
. Despite extensive studies, the asymptotic number of planar graphs were successfully determined only much more recently by Giménez and Noy
[
18
]
in 2009, building on work of Bender, Gao, and Wormald
[
5
]
.

Many results on planar maps and planar graphs rely on applying decompositions that work in tree-like fashion, allowing combinatorial properties to be deduced. A prominent example of this theme is the
block decomposition
. For both planar maps and connected planar graphs, there is a unique way to decompose them into maximal
2
-connected submaps or subgraphs, called
blocks
(see, e.g.,
[
11
,
19
,
32
]
). In both cases, one can construct tree-like structures by viewing the blocks as vertices of a graph whose edges join overlapping blocks.

In the case of planar maps, such a tree was described by Addario-Berry
[
2
]
, following Tutte’s description of the block decomposition
[
32
]
. Moreover, Addario-Berry
[
2
]
showed that this tree could be used to obtain information about
random
planar maps: Since the block tree of a uniform planar map of size
n
follows the law of a
Bienaymé tree
1
1

1

Also called
Galton–Watson tree
or
Bienaymé–Galton–Watson tree
, we adopt the change of notation proposed by Addario-Berry, Brandenberger, Hamdan, and Kerriou
[
1
]
.
, whose properties are well-known, properties on the sizes of the
2
-connected components of a random map follow. It was subsequently shown that this approach fruitfully allows one to consider
block-weighted models
: instead of sampling according to a uniform law, one considers a parameter
u>0
, and samples a map of size
n
with probability proportional to
u
to the power of its number of blocks. (Note that
u=1
corresponds to the usual uniform law.) Fleurat and the second author
[
15
]
showed that the block tree of such a random planar map still follows a Bienaymé law, and used this to obtain results on the sizes of blocks of such maps as well as scaling limits. More precisely, they discovered a phase transition between the universality classes of maps (converging to the Brownian sphere) and plane trees (converging to the Brownian tree), depending on the value of
u
, obtaining precise results on block sizes in each phase.

Using an extensive list of decomposition of maps into subfamilies
[
4
]
, the second author
[
27
,
28
]
showed that this approach can be successfully adapted to deal with different decompositions of maps, giving a very similar phase transition between a “map phase” and a “tree phase”. Albenque, Fusy, and the second author
[
3
]
used this approach for a family of
decorated
maps, which are planar maps endowed with one of their spanning tree. They found a phase transition between a “tree-rooted map phase” and a “tree phase”.

Since this technique of using block decompositions to obtain interesting results for planar
maps
is robust, it is intriguing to extend this approach to connected planar
graphs
. The planar graph case is slightly more intricate than the planar map case because one has to take into account the labelling and cannot rely on an embedding as in the map case.

1.2
Main results

The main contributions of this paper are two-fold. First, we show that a random connected rooted labelled graph from a block-stable class (e.g., a random connected planar graph), which is sampled according to a block-weighted law, exhibits a phase transition (see
\cref
thm:phase-transition). To this end, we introduce a
decorated block tree
that encodes a block decomposition of such a connected graph (see
\cref
sec:decorated). This extends the lines of previous study based on a block tree decomposition from planar
maps
to connected planar
graphs
. Because block-stable classes are very general, our results are also very general, but not necessarily precise.

Second, we show how the block tree methodology can be used to obtain more precise results. First, we use it to obtain enumeration results for the block-weighted generating function. Then we determine the distribution of block sizes of random block-weighted planar graphs and block-stable graphs of the same singular type (see
\cref
thm:block-sizes). As a result, we obtain more precise information on block size of a uniform random connected planar graph than what was previously known in the literature
[
17
,
25
]
.

1.2.1
Phase transition in block-weighted block-stable graphs

A class
\mathcal{C}
of connected graphs is said to be
block-stable
if for each graph
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}
each of its
2
-connected components (called
blocks
) belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. Write
\mathcal{B}
for the class of
2
-connected elements of
\mathcal{C}
. Write
B(y)
and
C(x)
for the corresponding exponential generating functions (EGFs), and set
C^{\bullet}(x)=xC(x)
. It was shown, e.g., by Robinson
[
26
, Theorem 4]
, that

C^{\bullet}(x)=x\exp(B^{\prime}(C^{\bullet}(x))).

(1)

As we will see in
\cref
subsec:anal-comb-block-tree, this equation can be translated into a “decorated” block tree.

More precisely, let
C^{\bullet}(x,u)
be the exponential generating function for
connected rooted
graphs of
\mathcal{C}
, where
x
marks vertices and
u
marks blocks. Write
\rho(u)
for the radius of convergence of
C^{\bullet}(\cdot,u)
. For
u>0
, we consider the following
block-weighted
probability laws on
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
: for a graph
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}
of size
|\mathfrak{g}|
with
b(\mathfrak{g})
many
2
-connected components, we set

\mathbb{P}_{u}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right):=\frac{\rho(u)^{|\mathfrak{g}|}u^{b(\mathfrak{g})}}{|\mathfrak{g}|!C^{\bullet}(\rho(u),u)},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{P}_{n,u}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right):=\frac{u^{b(\mathfrak{g})}}{[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)}\text{1}_{|\mathfrak{g}|=n}.

We show that, when considering a block-weighted law on a connected rooted graphs from a block-stable classes (including connected rooted planar graphs), this block tree follows a Bienaymé tree distribution (see
\cref
prop:law-TG). Our first main theorem is about phase transitions in the criticality of this Bienaymé tree.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a block-stable class of connected graphs, let
\mathcal{B}
be its class of
2
-connected components whose EGF we denote by
B(y)
, and let
\rho_{B}
be the radius of convergence of
B
. Let

u_{C}:=\frac{1}{\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})}.

Then the following hold.

•

The singular behaviour of
C^{\bullet}(x,u)
depends on the relative positions of
u
and
u_{C}
;

•

The block tree corresponding to a graph drawn according to
\mathbb{P}_{n,u}
follows a subcritical Bienaymé tree law if
u<u_{C}
, and a critical Bienaymé tree law if
u\geq u_{C}
.

For a more precise version of this theorem, see
\cref
th:phase-trans-singular,thm:phasetransition_block_weighted. For the special case of planar graphs (and similar classes), we also show that the subcritical case corresponds to a “rooted graph phase” while the supercritical case corresponds to a “tree phase”. Our key proof techniques build upon the methods previously developed by Fleurat and the second author for block-weighted planar maps
[
15
]
, where analogous behaviour was exhibited.

It is well-known that the relative positions of
\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})
and
1
allow us to distinguish (sub)criticality (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
). More precisely, when
\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})>1
, it is known that the graphs behave “tree-like”, which is consistent with
u_{C}<1
and the uniform case being supercritical. On the contrary, when
\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})<1
, the graphs behave as general planar graphs do, which is consistent with
u_{C}>1
.

We note that block-weighted block-stable graph classes were already considered by Stufler
[
30
, §5]
in a general setting using the framework of Gibbs partitions, but our current work has a different focus.

1.2.2
Enumeration and block sizes in block-weighted planar graphs

Our next results focus on the case where
B^{\prime}
has a singularity of type
3/2
(which encompasses planar graphs). Under this assumption, we also show how the phase transition described in
\cref
thm:phase-transition affects the behaviour of
[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)
, as well as the typical sizes of blocks. The following is our main result on the former.

Theorem 2
.

For every
u>0
, there exist
c>0
,
c_{1}(u)>0
, and
c_{2}(u)>0
such that:

•

in the subcritical phase
u<u_{C}
,

[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}c_{1}(u)n^{-5/2}\rho(u)^{-n};

•

in the critical phase
u=u_{C}
,

[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}cn^{-5/3}\rho(u_{C})^{-n};

•

in the supercritical phase
u>u_{C}
,

[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}c_{2}(u)n^{-3/2}\rho(u)^{-n}.

For a more precise version of this theorem, see
\cref
th:dvp-sing-Cbxu. Thus, in the subcritical phase, the polynomial correction is that of rooted graphs:
n^{-5/2}
. Conversely, in the supercritical phase, the correction is that of plane trees:
n^{-3/2}
. This justifies why one can view the subcritical phase as a “general rooted graph phase” and the supercritical phase as a “tree phase”. It is worth noting that in the critical phase, i.e., when
u=u_{C}
, a novel asymptotic behaviour emerges, characterised by a polynomial correction of
n^{-5/3}
.

The next theorem summarises our main results on the typical block sizes in the three different phases.

Theorem 3
.

There is a phase transition for block sizes depending on the value of
u
:

•

In the subcritical phase
u<u_{C}
, there is a largest block of size
\Theta_{\mathbb{P}}(n)
and a second largest block of size
\Theta_{\mathbb{P}}(n^{2/3})
;

•

In the critical phase
u=u_{C}
, there is a largest block of size
\Theta_{\mathbb{P}}(n^{2/3})
and likewise for the next largest block sizes;

•

In the supercritical phase
u>u_{C}
, there is a largest block of size
\Theta_{\mathbb{P}}(\ln(n))
and likewise for the next largest block sizes.

As we will see in
\cref
thm:phasetransition_block_sizes, we also determine precise limit laws for the block sizes when appropriately scaled, in the subcritical and critical cases, and provide a more precise description of what happens in the supercritical case. Our results extend some previous work in the literature
[
17
,
25
]
from uniform to block-weighted random planar graphs. In fact, the above two results hold for any block-stable class where
B^{\prime}
is
singular with exponent
3/2
(for a definition, see e.g.,
[
4
, Equation (46)]
). This includes the class of planar graphs as shown by Bender, Gao, and Wormald
[
5
]
(see
\cref
eq:asymp-exp-B’ for the singular expansion of
B^{\prime}
).

1.3
Key proof techniques and related work

The primary proof methods used in this paper build on those used for block-weighted planar maps in
[
15
]
, with some ideas originating from
[
2
]
. The Lagrangean form of
\cref
eq:Cbullet-block implicitly gives a tree-like structure and we make it combinatorially explicit in the form of a
decorated block tree
, detailed in
\cref
subsec:anal-comb-block-tree. Our decorated block tree looks similar to the usual notion of a block decomposition of a connected graph, but is different in that the vertices of our tree represent cut-vertices instead of blocks, and these cut-vertices are decorated by sets of blocks. We use tools from singularity analysis and results about Bienaymé trees to pinpoint and describe a phase transition, as in
\cref
thm:phase-transition,thm:enum.

To prove our results about typical block sizes in random block-weighted planar graphs, as in
\cref
thm:block-sizes, we also incorporate some ideas on planar maps from
[
15
]
, but extra steps are needed due to the fact that our block tree decorations are not blocks, but
sets of blocks
, or more precisely, elements of the class
\mathrm{Set}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. First we apply known results on degree distributions of Bienaymé trees (by, e.g., Janson
[
21
]
) to our block tree. Then we combine this with results on Gibbs partitions by Stufler
[
31
]
to deduce typical block sizes. Similar ideas were used to study block-weighted simple triangulations in
[
28
, §4.2]
, where the decorations were sequences of blocks.

Finally, we mention some other related works. In the uniform case, Panagiotou and Steger
[
25
]
proved a dichotomy theorem on the behaviour of the size of a largest block in connected graph classes: For classes similar to planar graphs there is a unique largest block of linear size almost surely, whereas for classes similar to series-parallel graphs all components are at most logarithmic in size almost surely. Giménez, Noy, and Rué
[
17
, Theorem 5.4]
subsequently refined the dichotomy theorem of Panagiotou and Steger
[
25
]
and also showed a limiting distribution on the sizes of the largest blocks, involving the Airy distribution. As mentioned earlier, our work considers in particular planar graphs with block weights and thus generalises some of the results in the works above.

1.4
Plan of the paper

In
\cref
sec:preliminaries, we give notation and recall usual notions of block decompositions of graphs. In
\cref
sec:decorated, we detail the construction of the decorated block tree of a rooted graph. In
\cref
sec:block-weighted, we introduce the block-weighted model for block-stable graph classes and obtain results about it. In
\cref
sec:block-sizes, we focus on classes where
B^{\prime}
is singular with exponent
3/2
(which includes planar graphs), and obtain results on enumeration and block sizes. In
\cref
sec:conclusion, we discuss possible extensions and future work.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

The set of natural numbers is written as
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
k_{1},\dots,k_{p}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{p}k_{i}=n
, a
multinomial coefficient
is defined as

\binom{n}{k_{1},\dots,k_{p}}\coloneqq\frac{n!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{p}!}.

By convention,
0!=1
. For a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of random variables and a positive sequence
(a_{n})
, one writes:

•

A_{n}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(a_{n})
to mean that, for all sequences
(u_{n})
of positive numbers that tend to
+\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|A_{n}|\leq a_{n}u_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1;

•

A_{n}=\Theta_{\mathbb{P}}(a_{n})
to mean that, for all sequences
(u_{n})
of positive numbers that tend to
+\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{a_{n}}{u_{n}}\leq|A_{n}|\leq a_{n}u_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}1.

We also use standard Landau notation (e.g.,
O(\cdot),\Theta(\cdot)
) in the non-probabilistic setting.

2.2
Labelled classes

An object of size
n
is said to be
well-labelled
if its collection of labels is exactly the elements of the integer interval
[1,n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. When relabelling one or several objects with labels, the relabelling is said to be
consistent
if it preserves the order relations among labels (for more information, see, e.g.,
[
14
, II.2]
). The elements of a labelled combinatorial class are always well-labelled. The
size
of such an element is its number of labelled atoms.

2.3
Graphs and their generating functions

All graphs considered in this paper are simple. For a graph
\mathfrak{g}
, let
V(\mathfrak{g})
(resp.
E(\mathfrak{g})
) denote its set of vertices (resp. edges). Unless stated otherwise, the graphs are vertex-labelled with labels ranging from
1
to
|V(\mathfrak{g})|
.

A
rooted
graph is a graph with a distinguished vertex. A
derived
graph
\mathfrak{g}
is a graph with one unlabelled vertex, where the other vertices are labelled with a number between
1
and
|V(\mathfrak{g})|-1
. The
size
of a graph
\mathfrak{g}
is the number of labelled vertices in
\mathfrak{g}
and is denoted by
|\mathfrak{g}|
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a block-stable class of connected graphs, and let
\mathcal{B}
be the subclass of
2
-connected elements. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
be the class of rooted elements of
\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
the class of derived elements of
\mathcal{B}
. We write
C(x)
,
B(y)
,
C^{\bullet}(x)
,
B^{\prime}(y)
for the associated exponential generating functions. Let
\rho_{C}
(resp.
\rho_{B}
) be the radius of convergence of
C(x)
(resp.
B(y)
). It holds that
C^{\bullet}(x)=xC^{\prime}(x)
.

For the case of the class of
planar
graphs, Bender, Gao, and Wormald
[
5
]
showed that
B^{\prime}
has a unique dominant singularity
\rho_{B}
and has the following singular expansion in a
\Delta
-domain around
\rho_{B}
:

B^{\prime}(y)=B^{\prime}(\rho_{B})-B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})(\rho_{B}-y)+c_{B}(\rho_{B}-y)^{3/2}+O\left((\rho_{B}-y)^{2}\right)

(2)

for some constant
c_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. By a transfer theorem (see, e.g.,
[
14
, Theorem VI.1]
), this implies that

[y^{n}]B^{\prime}(y)\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{c_{B}\rho_{B}^{3/2}}{\Gamma(-3/2)}\rho_{B}^{-n}n^{-5/2}.

(3)

2.4
Block decomposition of a connected graph

It is well-known that every connected graph can be uniquely decomposed into maximal
2
-connected subgraphs, called
blocks
, which can overlap in at most one vertex (see e.g.,
[
11
,
19
]
). We consider a single edge to be
2
-connected, but not a single vertex. A vertex where two or more blocks overlap is called a
cut-vertex
. The resulting
block decomposition
of a graph
\mathfrak{g}
is usually described in one of the following two ways:

1.

Block graph

B(\mathfrak{g})
(see e.g.,
[
19
]
): The vertices of
B(\mathfrak{g})
are the blocks of
\mathfrak{g}
, and two blocks are joined by an edge in
B(\mathfrak{g})
if they overlap at a vertex. The resulting graph
B(\mathfrak{g})
is characterised by the property of being connected and that all of its blocks are cliques.

2.

Block-cut graph

T(\mathfrak{g})
(see e.g.,
[
11
]
): The vertices of
T(\mathfrak{g})
are the blocks and the cut-vertices of
\mathfrak{g}
, and a cut-vertex is joined to a block by an edge in
T(\mathfrak{g})
if that vertex is contained in that block. The resulting graph
T(\mathfrak{g})
is a tree.

In the present work, we use an alternative block decomposition that naturally arises from the Lagrangean form of (
1
) of the generating series
C^{\bullet}(x)
. It originates from work of Addario-Berry
[
2
]
on planar maps (see also
[
28
]
), and we give a precise algorithmic description in
\cref
subsec:anal-comb-block-tree. We note that this construction works specifically for
rooted
labelled connected graphs, and we call its output a
decorated block tree
. In a decorated block tree, the vertices represent all the vertices of the original graph
\mathfrak{g}
but each vertex is decorated by a set of blocks that contain that vertex. In
[
2
]
, the decorations were simply blocks, but in our construction the decorations are more general
sets of blocks
.

3
Decorated block trees of connected block-stable graphs

In this section, we describe
decorated block trees
, which are a central tool in our analysis.

3.1
Construction of decorated block tree

Recalling the Lagrangean form of
\cref
eq:Cbullet-block, we define

\Phi(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi_{k}}{k!}y^{k}\coloneqq\exp(B^{\prime}(y))\in\mathbb{C}[[y]],

(4)

which is precisely the exponential generating function for the class
\mathrm{Set}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. For convenience we also use
\Phi
to denote the class
\mathrm{Set}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. It follows from
\cref
eq:Cbullet-block that

C^{\bullet}(x)=x\Phi(C^{\bullet}(x)).

As explained in
[
28
, §2.2.1]
, there is a systematic way to define a (plane) block tree from a Lagrangean decomposition scheme. Namely, each vertex of the tree is decorated with an object
\varphi
from the class
\Phi
, and the subtrees attached to a vertex of the tree (corresponding to some
\varphi
) are the trees of the rooted graphs substituted into
\varphi
. The situation in the case of graphs is a little more involved than in the case of maps because of the labelling possibilities. Thus, we use the following notion.

Remember that elements of the class
\Phi
are well-labelled. Therefore, for each
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}
, when we consider sets of derived blocks of
\mathfrak{g}
, it is always implied that the set has been relabelled with a well-labelling which is consistent with the order of the labels in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Figure 1:
The image on the left is a connected labelled rooted planar graph
\mathfrak{g}
, with root highlighted in grey. The image on the right is the associated decorated block tree
{T}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Now, we give an algorithmic definition of decorated block trees. An example is provided in
\cref
fig:block-tree-2.

Definition 4
.

For
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
(which for ease of presentation
\mathfrak{g}
is also allowed to be a rooted derived graph, with a root and one unlabelled non-root vertex), we define the
decorated block tree
{T}_{\mathfrak{g}}
as the result of the following construction:

1.

Let
r
be the root of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\varphi=\{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{\ell}\}
be the set of derived blocks containing
r

and no unlabelled vertex
in
\mathfrak{g}
, where the label of the vertex corresponding to
r
is removed. Start with a root vertex
r_{{T}}
in
{T}_{\mathfrak{g}}
and decorate it with
\varphi
.

2.

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, we construct a derived graph
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}
by deleting
r
from
\mathfrak{g}
, keeping the connected component of
\mathfrak{g}
that intersects
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{i}
, and then adding
r
back to this subgraph. We consistently relabel the vertices of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}
except
r
which remains unlabelled. For every labelled vertex
v
of
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}_{i}
, we construct the rooted derived graph
\mathfrak{g}_{(v)}
by rooting
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}^{\prime}
at
v
(and keeping the unlabelled vertex).

3.

For
v
a vertex in
\varphi
, in increasing order of label in
\varphi
, let
\mathfrak{t}_{v}:={T}_{\mathfrak{g}_{(v)}}
be the decorated block tree on this rooted subgraph with an unlabelled vertex. Add
\mathfrak{t}_{v}
as the rightmost child of
r_{{T}}
.

This finishes the construction the decorated block tree. In addition, we define the
block tree of
\mathfrak{g}
to be the result of forgetting the decorations of
{T}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, giving a plane tree.

Notice that, contrary to the more traditional block-cut tree described in
\cref
subsec:block-decomp-tradi, one cannot use the undecorated block tree to directly read off the number of blocks in the original graph. On the other hand, the number of vertices of the block tree is the same as the number of vertices of the original graph. In addition, the outdegree of a vertex in the block tree is equal to the size of the set that decorates it. In particular, leaves are decorated by the empty set.

3.2
Reverse construction

In
\cref
subsec:anal-comb-block-tree we described how to construct both a decorated and undecorated rooted plane tree from a given graph in
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
, with the decorations given by sets of derived blocks. One can reverse this construction to obtain a graph of
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
from a decorated block tree. This comes from gluing together the blocks that decorate a given vertex of the block tree, where the gluing happens along the unlabelled vertex at each of those blocks. Then we identify vertices across different gluings based on their ordering in the (plane) block tree.

Now, there may be multiple labelled graphs with the same decorated block tree because our vertex decorations are
well-labellings
, and there are multiple labellings of the original graph that result in the same well-labelling in a decoration. However, once we fix both a decorated block tree and an allocation of the appropriate number of labels to the root of the graph and to the decorated vertices of the block tree, one can associate a unique graph. This leads to the following proposition, quantifying the number labelled connected rooted graphs with the same decorated block tree. Recall that
\Phi
is the class
\mathrm{Set}\circ\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, with EGF defined in
\cref
eq:def-Phi.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a rooted plane tree with
n
vertices. Let
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}
be the outdegrees of its vertices. There are

{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{t})\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{d_{i}}

ways to decorate the vertices of
\mathfrak{t}
with an element of the class
\Phi
of size equal to the outdegree of the vertex. Moreover, it holds that

\left|\left\{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}\mid{T}_{g}=\mathfrak{t}\right\}\right|={\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{t})\cdot n\binom{n-1}{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}.

4.2
Phase transition of block-weighted graphs

Bienaymé trees exhibit a phase transition depending on the mean of their reproduction law (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
). It is easy to compute that

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mu}^{u}\right]=\frac{y(u)\Phi^{\prime}(y(u),u)}{\Phi(y(u),u)}=uy(u)B^{\prime\prime}(y(u)),

(7)

where the derivation of the function
\Phi
is done with respect to the first variable. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\left[{\mu}^{u}\right]=1\iff y(u)=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y(u))}.

To understand the behaviour of
y(u)
and, more generally, to exhibit a phase transition for block-weighted graphs from block-stable classes, one looks into the singularity
\rho(u)
of
C^{\bullet}(\cdot,u)
. From
\cref
eq:Cbullet-u, there are two possible sources of singularity:

1.

The first case when
y(u)=\rho_{B}
. In this case, we have
\rho_{B}=\rho(u)\exp(uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B}))
, i.e.,

\rho(u)=\rho_{B}\exp(-uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B})).

2.

The second case when
\frac{\partial F}{\partial c}(\rho(u),y(u))=0
where
F:(x,c)\mapsto c-x\exp(uB^{\prime}(c))
. In this case, we have

\rho(u)=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y(u))\exp(uB^{\prime}(y(u)))}.

Since by definition we have
\rho(u)=y(u)\exp\left(-uB^{\prime}(y(u))\right)
, it holds that

y(u)=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y(u))}.

Consider the equation
y=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y)}
, or equivalently
u=\frac{1}{yB^{\prime\prime}(y)}
. The function
y\mapsto yB^{\prime\prime}(y)
is increasing for
y\in[0,\rho_{B}]
, with range in
[0,\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})]
, so this equation has a solution (which is unique) if and only if
u\geq 1/(\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B}))
.

These considerations imply the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a block-stable class of connected graphs, let
\mathcal{B}
be its class of
2
-connected components with EGF
B(y)
, and let
\rho_{B}
denote the radius of convergence of
B
. Let

u_{C}:=\frac{1}{\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})}.

Then the radius of convergence
\rho(u)
of the series
C^{\bullet}(\cdot,u)
(on block-weighted rooted elements of
\mathcal{C}
) and
y(u)=C^{\bullet}(\rho(u),u)
satisfy the following:

•

If
u\leq u_{C}
, then

\rho(u)=\frac{\rho_{B}}{\exp(uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B}))}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y(u)=\rho_{B};

•

If
u\geq u_{C}
, then

\rho(u)=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y(u))\exp(uB^{\prime}(y(u)))}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y(u)=\frac{1}{uB^{\prime\prime}(y(u))}.

Remark 10
.

When considering the block-stable class of connected
planar graphs
, it holds that
\rho_{B}B^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{B})<1

[
18
, Claim 1]
, so in fact
u_{C}>1
and
uniform
planar graphs fall into the subcritical case. It holds that

u_{C}\approx 24.837\dots

Together with
\cref
eq:Exp-muT-u,prop:law-TG,
\cref
th:phase-trans-singular implies the following.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\mathbf{G}_{n,u}
be a graph of size
n
from a block stable class distributed according to

. Then its block tree

\cref
eq:equiv-bpn, it holds that

\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(N=n\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(X=n\right)}=\frac{B^{\prime}(\rho_{B})}{\exp(uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B}))}\left(\frac{uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B})}{\rho_{B}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{b^{\prime}_{n}}\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{cB^{\prime}(\rho_{B})}{\exp(uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B}))}\frac{(uB^{\prime}(\rho_{B}))^{n}}{n!}n^{5/2}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

which verifies that condition (iv)d holds.
∎

Remark 17
.

Stufler’s result
[
31
, Theorem 3.11]
also implies that the expected number of elements in a set decorating a vertex of
\mathbf{T}_{n,u}
is
u/u_{C}
.

Now we state our main theorem on block sizes in random connected rooted planar graphs, which is a more precise version of
\cref
thm:block-sizes. For notation, we write
\mathrm{LB}^{(j)}\left(\mathfrak{g}\right)
to denote the size of the
j
-th largest block of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 18
.

Let
u>0
. The block-weighted graph
\mathbf{G}_{n,u}
exhibits the following behaviours when
n\to\infty
.

Subcritical case.

If
u<u_{C}
, then
\cref
th-taille-blocs-sous-crit holds for block sizes:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(c(u)n)^{2/3}}

\displaystyle\left(\left(1-\frac{u}{u_{C}}\right)n-\mathrm{LB}^{(1)}\left(\mathbf{G}_{n,u}\right),\left(\mathrm{LB}^{(j)}\left(\mathbf{G}_{n,u}\right):j\geq 2\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.0pt\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{(d)}\left(L_{1},\left(\Delta L_{(j-1)}:j\geq 2\right)\right)

where
(L_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is a stable process of parameter
3/2
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-sL_{1}}\right]=e^{\Gamma(-3/2)s^{3/2}}
and
\Delta L_{(1)}\geq\Delta L_{(2)}\geq\dots
is the ranked sequence of its jumps.

Critical case.

If
u=u_{C}
, then
\cref
eq:size-bloc-critical holds for the block sizes:

\left(\frac{\mathrm{LB}^{(j)}\left(\mathbf{G}_{n,u_{C}}\right)}{n^{2/3}}:j\geq 1\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{(d)}\left(E_{(j)}:j\geq 1\right),

where the
E_{(j)}
are as defined in
\cref
prop:degrees-Tnu.

Supercritical case.

If
u>u_{C}
, then
\cref
eq:grandbloc holds for the block sizes (fixing
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
):

\mathrm{LB}^{(j)}\left(\mathbf{G}_{n,u}\right)=\frac{\ln(n)}{\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{B}}{y(u)}\right)}-\frac{5}{2}\frac{\ln(\ln(n))}{\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{B}}{y(u)}\right)}+O_{\mathbb{P}}(1).

Proof.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. The quantity
\mathbf{m}_{n,u}^{(i)}
from
\cref
prop:gibbs-partition represents the size of a block.
By
\cref
prop:gibbs-partition,prop:degrees-Tnu,
we already know the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathbf{m}_{n,u}^{(i)}
(and it corresponds to the statement). It therefore remains to show that this asymptotic behaviour coincides with that of the size of the
i
-th largest block.

In the supercritical case, this follows immediately because all block sizes that we identified have the same asymptotic behaviour. In the remaining cases, observe that the limits of the rescaled quantities
\mathbf{m}_{n,u}^{(i)}
are strictly decreasing in
i
. Consequently, for every fixed
j
and for all sufficiently large
n
, the ordering of the first
j
blocks coincides with that of the sequence
(\mathbf{m}_{n,u}^{(i)})
with high probability.
∎

6
Conclusion

This paper studied the typical structure of block-weighted random graphs from block-stable classes using tools from analytic combinatorics. We developed a notion of a decorated block tree, and depending on the block weight
u>0
, we found phase transitions in the law of the block tree (
\cref
thm:phasetransition_block_weighted) and, when the series
B^{\prime}
is singular with exponent
3/2
, in the behaviour of
[x^{n}]C^{\bullet}(x,u)
(
\cref
th:dvp-sing-Cbxu) and in the typical sizes of the blocks themselves (
\cref
thm:phasetransition_block_sizes).

For future work, we aim to more closely investigate block-stable graph classes beyond those that behave like planar graphs, including perhaps higher genus graph classes (see
[
6
,
8
]
) and minor-closed classes (see
[
7
]
). In particular, we aim to find dichotomy behaviours similar to those observed by Panagiotou, Steger
[
25
]
and Giménez, Noy, Rué
[
17
]
. In addition, we look to find scaling limits of our block-weighted graph classes. Finally, a more challenging future direction would be to investigate block-weighted graph classes in unlabelled graph settings (see
[
12
]
).

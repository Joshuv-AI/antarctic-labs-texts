Implementation and evaluation of the impact of virtual workshops of voluntary educational assistance among occupational health and safety students: Case study, Ardabil, Iran
Authors: Negin Fathi, Mozhdeh Taheri Afshar, Farahnaz Khajehnasiri, Ali Askari, Robab Hosseinpour, Parvin Sepehr, Ali Salehi Sahlabadi, Maliheh Eshaghzadeh, Yosef Hamidzadeh Arbabi, Mohsen Poursadeghiyan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The advancements and growth in information and communication technology have created new paths in the process of teaching and learning, leading to a shift in focus towards the use of electronic resources and digital teaching platforms. This study aimed to examine the impact of virtual workshops as a tool for improving learning among industrial health and safety students.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This study was a descriptive-analytical study and was conducted on 104 students of occupational health and safety at the University of Medical Sciences in Ardebil during the course of a year. In this study, five specialized courses were chosen, including the following: “OSCO”, “ESCC”, and “Erg”. “SSM” and “EMS”. In this study, the participants were divided into two groups: participating and non-participating students. The results of their grades in the workshop were compared. The study consisted of two parts, the preparation stage and the evaluation stage, which included the preparation of the workshop, data collection and analysis, and the final stage of evaluation. Then, a list of workshops related to the courses was prepared, and the obtained results were analyzed by SPSS software (Ver. 22).RESULTS:The results showed that the correlation between the student’s scores in the cognitive, emotional and psychomotor areas before and after attending the virtual workshops was significant. Variance analysis test showed a significant difference between the scores of the cognitive, emotional, and psychomotor areas, both before and after (P > 0.001) participation in workshops.CONCLUSION:This study suggests that additional workshops, in parallel with classroom training, are an effective tool in raising student academic performance, as the students who participated gained better grades than those who did not participate in the courses. Therefore, it is possible to enhance student academic levels by increasing the number of workshops in addition to course unit.

Introduction

Distance education refers to the use of distance learning technologies to provide education facilities to individuals who are unable to attend traditional in-person classes.[1] The COVID-19 pandemic has caused challenges in distance education, particularly for teachers.[2] Meanwhile, the pandemic has resulted in a significant growth in the use of distance learning technologies.[1] Distance educational rehabilitation is a new field that utilizes technology to provide rehabilitation services to individuals who are unable to attend traditional in-person sessions. This approach has become increasingly popular due to its convenience for individuals living in remote areas, with mobility issues, or busy schedules.[3] Numerous studies have examined dimensions of this growing trend, highlighting various factors impacting individuals’ experiences when learning remotely.[14] Some research works during the last three years suggest that psychosocial factors,[15] ergonomic conditions,[16] and mental health[78] are key factors that can impact individuals’ experiences with distance learning. It is important to consider these factors when designing and implementing distance-learning programs to ensure that individuals can learn effectively without experiencing harmful effects. Lidor et al.[9] reported that distance learning can be a meaningful learning opportunity during the pandemic, and can be structured in a way that promotes success. Savkın et al.[10] study shows that distance technology can be effective in promoting physical activity in cardiovascular disease rehabilitation. Also, Hakala et al.[11] study indicates that attitudes toward distance learning in physical therapy and rehabilitation undergraduate students during the pandemic were positive. Soldner et al.[12] mention the fact that instructors who utilize innovative and evidence-based practices are more likely to be successful in this field. To summarize, changes in global education and the increased use of distance learning technologies[1] have made it vital to enhance the quality of medical education. As universities transition toward online learning and distance education, it is crucial to introduce innovative learning methods to ensure learner success and knowledge in clinical practice and the workplace.[13] With the pandemic shifting in-person teaching to online and distance learning, the necessity of this mode of education is now more evident.[14] Information and communication technology is revolutionizing education, helping learners guide their studies to meet their own and their community’s needs.[15] Furthermore, the research results of Ibrahimzadeh and colleagues[16] using the Friedman test for the purpose of classifying different forms of interaction also show that interaction between learners and teachers is of the greatest importance. The increasing number of forms of interaction in universities and virtual campuses increases student motivation and leads to deeper, more meaningful, and higher-quality learning. However, e-learning alone faces problems such as a lack of human and emotional interaction, face-to-face connection in the classroom, and the ineffectiveness of virtual educational spaces in such a way that learners work independently and without any interaction with the teacher or other learners.[17] While several studies have been conducted on the impact of online education, the usefulness, efficacy, and stakeholder satisfaction of virtual workshops have received less attention.[18] However, given the characteristics of virtual education, it can be used as an additional tool to traditional in-person education, particularly in skills-based education, like clinical education.[19] According to the results of the study conducted by Pakdaman and Karimi,[20] both online and workshop-based instruction led to a significant improvement in knowledge and perception. A majority of the students considered the combined instruction method to be useful in assessing the risk of tooth decay. It is recommended that this method be used as a supplement to teaching for undergraduate dental students. In the field of occupational health and safety, only a few studies can be found. For example, new technologies like virtual reality (VR) have the potential to develop engaging learning tools for occupational health and safety. Although the use of VR in occupational health and safety education is still expanding, the learning outcomes of VR training have only been evaluated in a few studies. Nykänen et al.[21] results show that technologies like VR have the potential to develop engaging learning tools for safety training. In addition, previous studies have shown that learning to identify human factors at work has positive results for safety skills.[17] However, the effectiveness of the workshop method has been validated in various studies when compared to other methods of education,[2223] and the use of it has been recommended by researchers.[17] A study by Amiri et al.[23] shows that the participants are satisfied with the use of knowledge and expertise from professors from other universities through online communication, and it is advised that the online teaching method be used to leverage the knowledge and expertise of qualified professors from other universities to enhance the quality of education. Considering that the study style for medical and health students has traditionally been to study intensively before exams, and they do not give much importance to the lessons presented during the semester, it is no longer necessary for them to attend workshops, and they can participate in related workshops, if they wish, only for the purpose of expanding their knowledge. As a result, this study aims to create a program that allows students to participate in workshops related to their field of study in a smart and free way to get the most out of these workshops. The unique and innovative aspect of this study is that this method is implemented for the first time in Iran, and a similar process of education could not be found in an online search on various databases.

Materials and Methods

This study is a descriptive and analytical study, conducted among 104 students of occupational health and safety management at the Medical Sciences University of Ardabil-Iran over the course of one academic year, 2020–2021. In this study, samples were taken using a census method. The criteria for inclusion into the study were full satisfaction of students for their participation in the study and attendance to the held workshops. In this study, five specialized lessons were selected, including “Occupational Safety in Construction Operations (OSCO),” “Emergency Situations and Crisis Conditions (ESCC),” “Ergonomics (Erg.),” “Safety System Management (SSM),” and “Electrical and Machine Safety (EMS).” The list of workshops related to these lessons was created, and the titles of the workshops were communicated to the students. Discussion

According to one-way ANOVA, there was a statistically significant difference between the CEP scores before and after attending the workshops (P < 0.05). In conclusion, it can be said that the presence of students in virtual workshops due to the low-cost feature, along with their high interest and motivation in attending the workshops, increased the accuracy of learning and improved the students’ learning in all investigated areas (CEP). Amiri et al.[23] compared the impact of electronic and workshop learning on the level of learning among students of nursing, and found that both electronic and workshop learning were effective in improving students’ learning. They concluded that the two methods have a meaningful effect on their learning, and do not differ significantly in their impact. In fields like safety and occupational health that are related to the health of human resources, the use of virtual learning alone is not sufficient and may lead to challenges in the quality of education. Therefore, using virtual learning alongside other forms of learning such as workshops and in a combined format is more effective and impactful.[24] Oh et al.[25] study results were in line with the results of the present study, and showed that training in work skills can increase income by 69.6% and that training in work skills also had a positive impact on improving performance in work tasks. The study by Mokhtari et al.,[26] aimed to investigate the impact of evidence-based nursing training on the implementation of nursing processes and on the skills of nursing students, found that evidence-based nursing training was effective in improving the skills of nursing students in nursing process implementation. The results of the study by Arefi et al.[27] showed that the students believed that the information provided during the workshops was useful to them and that, overall, the content provided in the workshops was practical. The results of the present study showed that students have acquired a significant amount of information through attending workshops regarding acquiring work skills and educational skills. Taking into account the easy availability of training sessions, both virtually and in person, suitable to the capabilities of students, in addition to the qualifications of workshop instructors with knowledge and skills, more than 50% of students have gained the skills of occupational safety and workplace safety from the content provided in the workshops, which led to improved capabilities of the participants. Furthermore, the content presented has improved the participants’ capabilities in the workshops. Therefore, the results of the present study are in line with the previously mentioned study by Arefi et al. The study results by Karimian[28] on evaluating the impact of the first Electronic Learning Summer School on improving the knowledge, attitude, and skills of the students showed that these educational courses had a positive impact on sharpening their skills and knowledge. It can be concluded that the present study is in line with the study done by Karimian, due to the confirmation of the positive effect of education on skills and knowledge. Hannani et al.[29] investigated the opinions of 56 graduates of this field with the aim of examining the conformity of the content of the industrial health education program at the undergraduate level with the expected performance aspects of the graduates working in Kashan. The results showed that the application of the content of the basic and specialized courses of this field in the work environment was moderate and very high, respectively. In the field of scientific knowledge and practical skills, 71.1% of basic courses and 30% of specialized courses, 15.7% of basic courses and 54.5% of specialized courses were presented, respectively. Management was the least used in the field of scientific knowledge, and 90% of people wanted to increase the practical aspects of the courses and leave the theoretical aspect. Winge et al.[30] conducted a study about training on how to establish and implement safety to prevent and manage crises and disasters. The results showed that the phases of recognition and planning for crisis management are joint and implemented separately only in the field of operations. To combine these two phases, such as identification and planning, assessing risk is the best way. The result obtained from the present study in the same line with the mentioned study is that according to the student’s satisfaction with the courses held on different topics, including risk assessment as a significant part of crisis, disaster, and safety management, they have acquired the necessary skills. In the study by Ghafari and Shakari[31] which is titled, “The level of correspondence between the syllabus of undergraduate environmental engineering and their professional needs,” it has been found that changing the educational programs and providing the necessary facilities for achieving goals, professional needs, and training specialized human resources is essential. In this context, scientific and research societies play a significant role. Consistent with the results of the present study, there is no significant difference in the average scores of the “EMS” course between the two groups under study. It seems that the pass-or-fail nature of the subject and the emphasis on protective measures in the regulations did not exert an impact on the final grades of the students. Even in comparing the pre-test and the test taken after the workshops, taking the entire impact of the workshops into consideration, they are effective on the students. The average scores of the two groups in the course of “SSM” were reported to be significant, and there was no significant difference in the average scores of the course of “Erg.” between the two groups. It seems that the practical nature of the majority of the related lessons and the emphasis on practical, project-based learning have not had an impact on the final scores of the students, although overall, the impact of the workshops on the students has been found to be effective. Additionally, the results have shown that in the course of OSCO, there is no significant difference in the average scores of the two groups of students. The interpretation of this finding suggests that all the students attending the workshops were seeking academic progress, but the basic academic performance of the students has been influential. Therefore, either each one of the students, those with strong educational foundations or weak ones, have attended the workshops with different intents and purposes. For example, a strong student will want to be at the top of the class while a weak student will also want to improve, not only to avoid falling behind their classmates but also to obtain better grades than them. Therefore, even if there is no significant difference in terms of grades, the educational foundation level of the students can have an important impact on the topic. In fact, the one-sided analysis test of factors showed that there was a significant difference between the grades of the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral-motor domains before and after the workshops. Therefore, it can be concluded that the presence of free virtual workshops, together with the enthusiasm and motivation of students, increased their attention, and improved their learning in all dimensions of analysis. The interpretation of this result could be that because the workshops were offered free, along with the students’ higher motivation to attend the workshops due to the low cost, the accuracy of their learning increased, which led to improved learning in all three areas and domains.

Limitations

One of the limitations of this study was that an adequate infrastructure for online learning was not available. This includes slow internet speed, lack of free educational internet for students, and limited familiarity with this method of learning.

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate the effectiveness of workshops along with classroom learning, and the participating students achieved higher scores than those who did not participate in the workshops. This is an indication of the meaningful impact of virtual workshop sessions on the learning acquired by the students. Incorporating workshops, in addition to the classroom, is an effective method to improve the level of scientific knowledge for students.

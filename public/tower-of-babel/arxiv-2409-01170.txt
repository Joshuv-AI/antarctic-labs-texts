Abstract
. We show that the volume of the boundary of a bounded Sobolev ( p , q ) (p,q) -extension domain is zero when 1 ≤ q < p < q ​ n ( n − q ) . 1\leq q<p<\frac{qn}{(n-q)}.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-Sobolev extension domain with
1\leq q<p.
This simply means that every function
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
has an extension that belongs to
W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with norm controlled by the original norm.
In the case
q=p,
the domain
\Omega
is then necessarily locally Ahlfors
regular in the sense that

(1.1)

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta{r}^{n}

holds for every
x\in\partial\Omega
and each
0<r<1.

Especially, the volume of
\partial\Omega
must be zero. For this see

[
HKT08
]
and also
[
GM85
]
,
[
Kos90
]
,
[
Vod87
]
for earlier
results. However, (
1.1
) does not need to hold in the case
1\leq q<p
as shown in the work of Maz’ya and Poborchi
[
MPi86
]
. Indeed, this
condition fails at tips of monomial exterior cusps, which are extension
domains for suitable values of
q
and
p
. The best version of (
1.1
) known up to now is

(1.2)

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta r^{s}

under the assumption that
q>n-1
(
q\geq 1
when
n=2
),
where
s>n
depends on
q,p
and
n,
and tends to infinity for a fixed
q
when
p
tends to infinity
[
KUZ22
]
. When
1\leq q\leq n-1
with
n\geq 3,
one can not bound the decay order in general. On the other hand,
the validity of (
1.2
) for all
x\in\partial\Omega
of a given
domain
\Omega
does not rule out the possibility of
\partial\Omega

having positive volume. Nevertheless, it was shown in
[
KUZ22
]
that
\partial\Omega
is necessarily of volume zero when
q>n-1.
The bound
n-1
is sharp in the sense that examples of extension domains with positive
boundary volume for
n\geq 3
were constructed in
[
KUZ22
]
for any value
of
1\leq q<n-1,
for a suitable
p.
The natural question of the
dependence of
p
in terms of
q
in such examples was also raised in

[
KUZ22
, Question 8.3]
.
Our main result shows that the volume of
\partial\Omega
is necessarily
zero when
1\leq q<p<q^{*},
where
q^{*}=\frac{qn}{(n-q)}
when
1\leq q<n

and we define
q^{*}=\infty
when
q\geq n.
This is the first result without
additional density assumptions on the domain in question when
1\leq q\leq n-1.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq q<p<q^{*}
be given. If
\Omega
is a bounded
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain, then
|\partial\Omega|=0.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is new only when
q\leq n-1
since the case of

q>n-1
is already covered by
[
KUZ22
]
.
We do not recover the full scope of the case
q>n-1
because our technique
does not apply capacity estimates that indeed give more refined
information when
q>n-1.

Notice that we have not required the extension operator to be linear nor
it to be in any sense local. Even in the case of linearity, locality may
fail in a strong sense. Indeed,
[
KUZ22
, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 5.1]
exhibit a bounded
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain with a bounded linear extension operator that
extends a function
u,
which is identically one on
B(x,r)\cap\Omega,

to
Eu
which vanishes on a set of positive volume contained in
B(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega.
Actually, ruling out this behavior is equivalent to the boundary being of volume zero
[
KUZ22
, Theorem 5.1]
. Theorem

1.1
thus shows that this kind of a pathological situation is only possible when
p
is large in terms of
q.

Let us close this introduction by explaining the strategy of our proof of
Theorem
1.1
. We begin by establishing a version of (
1.2
)
under our assumption
p<q^{*}.
This seems to be the first result of this
type for small values of
q.
We succeed in this by adapting a technique
from
[
HKT08
]
to our setting. This estimate by itself is not enough. We
continue by showing that the decay order of the volume has to exceed this
bound at almost every point of density of the boundary. This is
established via a delicate iteration argument with the help of a set
function from
[
KUZ22
]
.
This set function was used in
[
KUZ22
]
for lower bounds of the volume,
but we succeed here to apply it towards upper bounds. Our estimates here
again strongly rely on the assumption
p<q^{*}.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty.
For
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we say that
u
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega),
if
u
is weakly differentiable and its weak (distributional) gradient
\nabla u
belongs to
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u(x)\right|^{p}+\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
and
n\geq 2
be given. A bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be a
(W^{1,p},W^{1,q})
-extension domain (Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain in short) if there exists a bounded extension operator

(2.1)

E:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

that is,
Eu_{|\Omega}=u
and there exist
C>0
such that

\|Eu\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Our next lemma shows that we may assume in what follows that
1\leq q<n.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
and
\Omega
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain. Then
\Omega
is also a bounded Sobolev
(p,s)
-extension domain for all
1\leq s<q.

Proof.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball with
\Omega\subset\subset B.
Since
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain, for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),
there exists
Eu\in W^{1,q}(B)
such that
Eu{|}_{\Omega}\equiv u
and

(2.2)

\|Eu\|_{W^{1,q}(B)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

with a constant
C
independent of
u.

By using Hölder’s inequality, we get that for
1\leq s<q

(2.3)

\|Eu\|_{W^{1,s}(B)}\leq|B|^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}\|Eu\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}.

By combining (
2.3
) and (
2.2
), we conclude that

(2.4)

\|Eu\|_{W^{1,s}(B)}\leq C|B|^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Since
B
is a bounded Sobolev
(s,s)
-extension domain, by (
2.4
) we deduce that
\Omega
is a bounded Sobolev
(p,s)
-extension domain.
∎

We recall two important estimates.

Global Sobolev embedding theorem
:- Let
1\leq p<n.
Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n,p)
such that

(2.5)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{pn}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for each
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Sobolev-Poincaré inequality
:-Let
1\leq p<n.
Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0.
There exists a constant
C=C(n,p)
such that

(2.6)

\left(\int_{B(y,r)}|u(x)-u_{B(y,r)}|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}dx\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C\left(\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for each ball
B(y,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
u\in W^{1,p}(B(y,r)).
Here,

u_{B(y,r)}=\frac{1}{|B(y,r)|}\int_{B(y,r)}u(x)dx.

We also need a set function associated to our extension operator. We borrow it from
[
KUZ22
]
, also see [
[
Ukh20
]
,
[
Ukh99
]
]. For convenience of the reader we review its construction and its crucial properties.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a collection of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
A function
\Phi,

\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)

is said to be a quasiadditive set function if, for all
U_{1}\subset U_{2},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69054ptU_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{M},

\Phi(U_{1})\leq\Phi(U_{2})

and there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every collection of pairwise disjoint open sets
\{U_{i}\in\mathcal{M}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
we have

(2.7)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi(U_{i})\leq C\Phi\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}\right).

The upper and lower derivatives of a quasiadditive set function, defined on
\mathcal{M}
containing all open balls, are

\overline{D\Phi}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}{\frac{\Phi(B(x,r))}{|B(x,r)|}}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054pt\underline{D\Phi}(x)=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}{\frac{\Phi(B(x,r))}{|B(x,r)|}}.

We formulate a result from
[
VU03
]
and
[
RR55
]
, in a convenient form.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Phi
be a quasiadditive set function defined on
\mathcal{M}
containing all open balls. Suppose that there exists
M>0
such that
\Phi(U)\leq M
for all
U\in\mathcal{M}.
Then for almost all points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
the upper derivative is finite and

(2.8)

\overline{D\Phi}(x)\leq C\underline{D\Phi}(x)<\infty.

The constant
C
above is the same as the one in (
2.7
).

From (
2.8
), we conclude that

(2.9)

\Phi(B(x,r))\leq C_{x}|B(x,r)|,

for all
0<r<r_{x},
where both
C_{x}
and
r_{x}
may depend on
x.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain.
Suppose that
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set with
U\cap\Omega\neq\phi
. We define

W_{0}^{p}(U,\Omega):=\{u\in C(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega):u\equiv 0\hskip 5.69054pt\text{on}\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega\setminus U\}.

For every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
U\cap\Omega\neq 0
and each
u\in W_{0}^{p}(U,\Omega),
we deﬁne the
q-
Dirichlet energy
\Gamma^{q}_{U}
on
U
with respect to the boundary value
u
by setting

\Gamma_{U}^{q}(u):=\inf\left\{\left(\int_{U}|\nabla v(z)|^{q}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}:v\in W^{1,q}(U),v|_{U\cap\Omega}\equiv u\right\}.

Then the set function
\Phi
is defined by setting

(2.10)

\Phi(U):=\sup\left\{\left(\frac{\Gamma_{U}^{q}(u)}{\|u\|_{W^{p}_{0}(U\cap\Omega)}}\right)^{k}:u\in W_{0}^{p}(U,\Omega)\right\}

with
\frac{1}{k}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p},
and by setting
\Phi(U)=0
for those open sets that do not intersect
\Omega.

The following theorems are taken from
[
KUZ22
]
. According to the first theorem,
\Phi
is a quasiadditive set function. The second theorem gives an important gradient estimate.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain. Then the set function
\Phi
deﬁned in
eq.

2.10
is a bounded quasiadditive set function deﬁned on open subsets
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain and
\Phi
be the set function from
eq.

2.10
. Then, for each ball
B:=B(x,r)
with
x\in\partial\Omega
and every function
u\in W^{p}_{0}(B,\Omega)
there exists a function
v\in W^{1,q}(B)
with
v|_{B\cap\Omega}\equiv u
and

(2.11)

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q}(B)}\leq 2\Phi^{\frac{1}{k}}(B)\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(B\cap\Omega)}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{1}{k}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}.

By combining Theorem
2.7
and (
2.9
) we obtain a key estimate.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
1\leq q<p<\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain and
\Phi
be the set function from
eq.

2.10
. Then for every
x\in\partial\Omega
for which
\overline{D\Phi}(x)<\infty,
there exist
r_{x}
and
C_{x}
so that for each ball
B(x,r)
with
0<r<r_{x}
and every
u\in W^{p}_{0}(B(x,r),\Omega)
there exists an extension
Eu\in W^{1,q}(B(x,r))
of
u
with

(2.12)

\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{q}(B(x,r))}\leq C_{x}|B(x,r)|^{\frac{1}{k}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{where}\hskip 5.69054pt\frac{1}{k}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}.

Especially, the conclusion holds for almost every
x\in\partial\Omega.

3.
Auxiliary Lemmas

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain with
1\leq q<p<q^{*}.
Then there exists
\delta>0
and
s=s(p,q)\geq n
such that

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta r^{s},

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
and all
0<r\leq\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)}{6}.
When
q<n,
we may choose
s=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
we may without loss of generality assume that
1\leq q<n.

Fix
0<r\leq\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)}{6}
and
x\in\partial\Omega.
Then
|\Omega\setminus B(x,r)|\geq\delta_{0}>0,
where
\delta_{0}
neither depends on
x
nor on
r.

Write
r_{0}=r.
For each
j\geq 1,
choose
r_{j}
so that
|B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega|=\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|}{2^{j}}.

Fix
j\geq 1
and define

u(y)=\max\{0,1-\frac{1}{(r_{j-1}-r_{j})}d(y,B(x,r_{j}))\}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptx\in\Omega.

Then,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(B(x,r_{j-1})\setminus B(x,r_{j}))\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{1}{(r_{j-1}-r_{j})}+|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}(r_{j-1}-r_{j})^{-1}+|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+(r_{j-1}-r_{j})^{-1})|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(r_{j-1}-r_{j})^{-1}|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

So
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

and hence
Eu\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

(3.2)

\|Eu\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

By the Global Sobolev embedding inequality (
2.5
)

(3.3)

\|Eu\|_{L^{q^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{q}\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{q}\|Eu\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we conclude that

(3.4)

\|Eu\|_{L^{q^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{{}^{\prime}}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.

Since
u\equiv 1
on
B(x,r_{j}),
we conclude that

(3.5)

|B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\leq\|Eu\|_{L^{q^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By using (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle|B(x,r_{j})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\leq C_{n,q}(r_{j-1}-r_{j})^{-1}|B(x,r_{j-1})\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This implies that

\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}}{2^{\frac{j}{q^{*}}}}(r_{j-1}-r_{j})\leq C_{n,q}\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2^{\frac{(j-1)}{p}}},

that is,

(3.6)

(r_{j-1}-r_{j})\leq C_{n,q}\left(\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}}}}{2^{j(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}})}}\right).

Since
p<q^{*}
, by summing (
3.6
) over
j,
we arrive at

r\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(r_{j-1}-r_{j})\leq C_{n,q}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}})}}\right).

Hence, for
\delta=\left(\frac{1}{C_{n,q}M}\right)^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}}}}
, where
M:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}})}}\right),
we have

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta r^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q^{*}}}}.

∎

For a domain
\Omega
and given
x\in\partial\Omega
, we define
A_{r_{1},r_{2},\Omega}
as

A_{r_{1},r_{2},\Omega}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<|y-x|<r_{2}\}\cap\Omega,

where
r_{2}>r_{1}>0.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<c<1
and
1\leq q<p<q^{*}.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain. If, for a given
x\in\partial\Omega,
both

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|}{r^{n}}=0

and

\overline{D\Phi}(x)<\infty

hold, then there exists
r_{x,c}>0
such that

\min\{|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|,|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|,

when
0<r<r_{x,c}.

Proof.

Given
\delta>0
, our assumptions give the existence of
0<r_{x,\delta}<1
such that

(3.7)

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\leq\delta r^{n},

whenever
0<r<r_{x,\delta}.

Consider the function
u
defined by

\displaystyle u(y):=\begin{cases}1,\hskip 8.53581pt\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pty\in B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega,\\
-2\frac{|y-x|}{r}+2,\hskip 11.38109pt\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pty\in[B(x,r)\cap\Omega]\setminus B(x,r/2),\\
0,\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if}\hskip 2.84526pty\in\Omega\setminus B(x,r).\\
\end{cases}

By Corollary
2.8
there exist
r_{x}
and
C_{x}
such that, for
0<r<r_{x},

(3.8)

\|\nabla Eu\|_{L^{q}(B(x,2r))}\leq C_{x}r^{n/q-n/p}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}

for an extension
Eu
of
u
, where
C_{x}>1.

Next, by using the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality (
2.6
), (
3.8
) and the definition of
u
we deduce that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\min\{|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},|B_{r/2,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,q)\left(r^{(n/q-n/p)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}+r^{(n/q-n/p)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)}\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,q)\left(r^{(n/q-n/p)}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+r^{(n/q-n/p-1)}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\right),

when
0<r<r_{x}.

Case 1:- If
\min\{|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\}=|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}
, then we conclude that

(3.10)

|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|\leq C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}}+C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}}.

In other words

\displaystyle|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle+C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|.

We use (
3.7
) and the assumption that
p<q^{*}
to conclude that, for
0<r<\min\{r_{x},r_{x,\delta}\},

\displaystyle|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}r^{n(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle+\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}r^{n(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)(r^{q*}+1)|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Now, choosing
\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}<\frac{c}{2C(n,q)},
we get

(3.11)

|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Case 2:-Suppose finally that
\min\{|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}},|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}\}=|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{1}{q^{*}}}
.
Then (
3.9
) gives

(3.12)

|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\leq C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}}+C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}}.

In other words

\displaystyle|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle+C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|^{\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1}|A_{r/2,r,\Omega}|.

We use (
3.7
) and the assumption that
p<q^{*}
to conclude that, for
0<r<\min\{r_{x},r_{x,\delta}\},

\displaystyle|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p)q^{*}}r^{n(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle+\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)r^{(n/q-n/p-1)q^{*}}r^{n(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)(r^{q*}+1)|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}\cdot C(n,q)|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Now, choosing
\delta^{(\frac{q^{*}}{p}-1)}<\frac{c}{2C(q,n)},
we get

(3.13)

|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\leq c|B(x,r)|.

∎

Towards the upper bound for the volume, we employ the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<c<1/2
and
1\leq q<p<q^{*}
be given and let
\Omega
be a bounded Sobolev
(p,q)
-extension domain. If, for a given
x\in\partial\Omega,
both

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|}{r^{n}}=0,

and

\overline{D\Phi}(x)<\infty

hold, then there exists
r_{x,c}>0
such that

|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|,

when
0<r<r_{x,c}.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.2
, we have

(3.14)

\min\{|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|,|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

when
0<r\leq r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}.

Fix
0<r\leq\frac{r^{{}^{\prime}}_{x,c}}{2}
and suppose first that

(3.15)

|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|<|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|.

Then, by using (
3.14
), we get

(3.16)

|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Choose
R=2r
. Again by using Lemma
3.2
, we have

(3.17)

\min\{|A_{R,2R,\Omega}|,|B(x,R/2)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,R)\cap\Omega|,

since
0<R\leq r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}.

That is,

(3.18)

\min\{|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|,|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|.

Suppose that

(3.19)

\min\{|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|,|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\}=|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

Then by using (
3.18
), we conclude that,

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\leq c|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|.

That is,

(3.20)

|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\leq c(|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|+|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|).

By using (
3.20
) and (
3.16
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|,

which cannot hold since
\frac{c^{2}}{1-c}<1.
We conclude that

(3.21)

\min\{|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|,|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\}=|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|.

Hence (
3.18
) and (
3.16
) give

(3.22)

\displaystyle|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|+|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+c^{2})\left(|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+1)^{2}\left(|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\right).

That is,

(3.23)

|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|\leq(c+1)^{2}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

We repeat the argument for
R=4r,

(3.24)

\min\{|A_{4r,8r,\Omega}|,|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,4r)\cap\Omega|,

assuming that
0<r<\frac{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}{8}.

Suppose that

\min\{|A_{4r,8r,\Omega}|,|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|\}=|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|.

Then, by (
3.23
), we conclude that

(3.25)

\displaystyle|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c|B(x,4r)\cap\Omega|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|+|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|)

By using (
3.25
) and the first line of (
3.22
) we deduce that

\displaystyle|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|,

which cannot hold. Hence

(3.26)

\min\{|A_{4r,8r,\Omega}|,|B(x,2r)\cap\Omega|\}=|A_{4r,8r,\Omega}|.

By combining (
3.16
), (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and (
3.26
), we obtain

(3.27)

\displaystyle|A_{4r,8r,\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(|B(x,4r)\cap\Omega|\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|+|A_{r,2r,\Omega}|+|A_{2r,4r,\Omega}|\right),

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+c^{2}+c(c+c^{2}))|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+1)^{3}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

We claim that, for
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,\lceil\log_{2}(\frac{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}{r})\rceil\}
,

(3.28)

\min\{|A_{2^{j-1}r,2^{j}r,\Omega}|,|B(x,2^{j-2}r)\cap\Omega|\}=|A_{2^{j-1}r,2^{j}r,\Omega}|

when
0<2^{j}r\leq r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}.

We will prove (
3.28
) by induction.

The case
j=1
holds by our assumption (
3.15
) and the case
j=2
is true by (
3.21
).

Assume that (
3.28
) holds for
j=k.
We will show that it also holds for
j=k+1.

Suppose that for
j=k+1
, (
3.28
) does not hold. By Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\min\{|A_{2^{k}r,2^{k+1}r,\Omega}|,|B(x,2^{k-1}r)\cap\Omega|\}\leq c|B(x,2^{k}r)\cap\Omega|,

which gives

|B(x,2^{k-1}r)\cap\Omega|\leq c\left(\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|B(x,2^{k-1}r)\cap\Omega|,

which cannot hold. Hence (
3.28
) is true for the case
j=k+1.

By using (
3.28
) we deduce that, for
j\in\{1,2,\cdots,\lceil\log_{2}(\frac{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}{r})\rceil\}
,

\displaystyle|A_{2^{j-1}r,2^{j}r,\Omega}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+1)^{j}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|

when
0<2^{j}r\leq r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}.

For
j=\lceil\log_{2}(\frac{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}{r})\rceil
,

\frac{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}{2}\leq 2^{j-1}r\leq r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}},

and since
\sigma:=\underset{r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}\leq s\leq 2r^{{}^{\prime}}_{x,c}}{\inf}\{|A_{s/2,s,\Omega}|\}>0,
we get that

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(c+1)^{j}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}2^{j}r^{n},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}r^{n-1},

when
0<r<r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}.

This cannot be the case when
r\leq\left(\frac{\sigma}{c_{n}r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

We define

r_{x,c}:=\left(\frac{\sigma}{c_{n}r_{x,c}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

In conclusion, (
3.15
) cannot hold when
0<r<r_{x,c}
and we conclude that

(3.29)

|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\leq c|A_{r/2,2r,\Omega}|

whenever
0<r<r_{x,c}.

∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

Suppose that
|\partial\Omega|>0.
Then there exists
E\subset\partial\Omega,
such that
|E|>0
and

(4.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|\partial\Omega\cap B(x,r)|}{|B(x,r)|}=1

for all
x\in E.

Let
x\in E.
Then (
4.1
) yields

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|}{|B(x,r)|}=0.

Suppose that
x\in E
is such that

(4.2)

\overline{D\Phi}(x)<\infty.

Let
0<c<1/2.
Then by Lemma
3.3
, we have

(4.3)

|B(x,r/2)\cap\Omega|\leq c|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

for all
0<r<\min\{\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)}{6},r_{x}\}.

By iterating (
4.3
), we conclude that for
j\in\mathbb{N}

(4.4)

|B(x,\frac{r}{2^{j}})\cap\Omega|\leq c^{j}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|.

By Lemma
3.1
, we also have

(4.5)

|B(x,R)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta R^{s},

for all small
R.
That is, for all large
j\in\mathbb{N}

(4.6)

|B(x,\frac{r}{2^{j}})\cap\Omega|\geq\delta\left(\frac{r}{2^{j}}\right)^{s}.

From (
4.6
) and (
4.4
), we conclude that

(4.7)

c^{j}|B(x,r)\cap\Omega|\geq\delta\cdot\left(\frac{r}{2^{j}}\right)^{s}.

From (
4.7
), we get that

c^{j}C(n)r^{n}\geq\delta\cdot\left(\frac{r}{2^{j}}\right)^{s}.

Hence, for all large
j\in\mathbb{N}

j(s\ln{2})\geq\ln{\left(\frac{\delta r^{s-n}}{C(n)c^{j}}\right)}=j\ln\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)+\ln(b),

where
b=\frac{\delta r^{s-n}}{C(n)}.

Since
0<c<1/2,

j\ln{\frac{1}{c}}\geq 2|\ln b|

when
j
is sufficiently large. In conclusion,

(4.8)

j\geq\frac{j}{2s\ln 2}\ln\left({\frac{1}{c}}\right)

for all sufficiently large
j.

Now, choosing
c
so small that
\ln\left({\frac{1}{c}}\right)\geq 4s\ln{2}
, we get that

j\geq 2j

which cannot hold. Hence there is no
x\in E
for which (
4.2
) holds.
In conclusion,
\overline{D\Phi}(x)=\infty
for all
x\in E,
and (
2.9
) yields that the set
E
has measure zero, and hence
|\partial\Omega|=0.

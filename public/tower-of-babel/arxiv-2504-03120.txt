Distributed Resilience-Aware Control in Multi-Robot Networks
Haejoon Lee, Dimitra Panagou

Abstract
Ensuring resilient consensus in multi-robot systems with misbehaving agents remains a challenge, as many existing network resilience properties are inherently combinatorial and globally defined. While previous works have proposed control laws to enhance or preserve resilience in multi-robot networks, they often assume a fixed topology with known resilience properties, or require global state knowledge. These assumptions may be impractical in physically-constrained environments, where safety and resilience requirements are conflicting, or when misbehaving agents corrupt the shared information. In this work, we propose a distributed control law that enables each robot to guarantee resilient consensus and safety during its navigation without fixed topologies using only locally available information. To this end, we establish a new sufficient condition for resilient consensus in time-varying networks based on the degree of non-misbehaving or normal agents. Using this condition, we design a Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based controller that guarantees resilient consensus and collision avoidance without requiring estimates of global state and/or control actions of all other robots. Finally, we validate our method through simulations.

\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)=(\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{2}(t))

\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{G}(t))\geq F^{\prime}

\forall t\geq t_{0}
and
\exists T\geq t_{0}
such that
\delta_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_{2}(T))<F^{\prime}
. Since
\delta_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_{1}(t))\geq F^{\prime}

\forall t\geq t_{0}
, all agents (both normal and malicious agents) in
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
have at least
F^{\prime}
neighbors
\forall t\geq t_{0}
. On the contrary, all normal agents in
\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
have at least
F^{\prime}
neighbors, whereas at least one malicious agent

in
\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
has less than
F^{\prime}
neighbors at

. By

,
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
is
(F+1,F+1)
-robust for all

, which is a sufficient condition for the achievement of resilient consensus under

-total malicious attacks
[
3
, Corollary 1]
. Thus in
\mathcal{G}_{1}(t)
, normal agents have enough redundancy in information from normal agents by having
F^{\prime}
neighbors
\forall t\geq t_{0}
to achieve resilient consensus through W-MSR. Now, assume to the contrary that resilient consensus is not achieved among normal agents in
\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
. Note that resilient consensus is achieved when all normal agents achieve consensus, regardless of the consensus states of malicious agents. Furthermore, in
\mathcal{G}_{2}(t)
, normal agents have
F^{\prime}
or more neighbors
\forall t\geq t_{0}
. This implies that they also have enough redundancy in information from normal agents
\forall t\geq t_{0}
to achieve resilient consensus through W-MSR, which is a contradiction.
∎

A similar condition is presented in
[
6
, Corollary 1]
, but it considers continuous-time consensus dynamics and focuses on the entire network. Our result focuses on ensuring discrete-time resilient consensus through the connectivity (degree) of
normal agents
rather than the entire network.
Based on
Theorem

1
, to guarantee resilient consensus under
F
-total malicious attacks, it suffices that robots navigate the environment such that
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{G}(t))\geq F^{\prime}=F+\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}}\rfloor

\forall t\geq t_{0}
. Since the numbers of total robots

and malicious robots

are fixed, each robot

can compute

locally. Now we design a control law for the physical states of robots so that normal robots always maintain
F^{\prime}
neighbors to achieve resilient consensus without collisions.

IV
Distributed Resilience-Aware Controller

In this section, we design a distributed controller for robots’ physical states that enables robots to navigate the environment with guarantees of resilient consensus and safety. Consider a multi-robot network
\mathcal{G}(t)
formed by the physical states
\mathbf{x}(t)
of
n
robots at time
t\geq t_{0}
. By
Theorem

1
,
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{G}(t))\geq F^{\prime}=F+\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}}\rfloor

\forall t\geq t_{0}
guarantees resilient consensus through W-MSR. Thus, we design a controller that ensures that all normal robots maintain
F^{\prime}
or more neighbors without collisions
\forall t\geq t_{0}
, thereby addressing
2
.

There exist many works on decentralized control for connectivity maintenance
[
17
,
16
,
22
]
. However, these methods either (a) require robots to obtain global knowledge through inter-agent communication, which becomes unreliable in the presence of malicious agents, or (b) enforce fixed topologies, which may not always be feasible in certain settings or environments. Thus, we design a controller that (a) uses only local information while being robust to malicious sharing and (b) preserves a flexible network formation.

How we construct the controller (given in (
19
)) is as follows: first, we define constraint functions to enforce both degree maintenance and collision avoidance. These functions are then composed into one centralized candidate CBF (
11
) whose corresponding CBF constraint (
12
) involves global state knowledge and control actions of all robots. Next, we decompose the constraint to create
n
separate
distributed
constraints (
14
) - one constraint for each robot - that only requires local information. Finally, we construct the controller with these distributed constraints, proving that it guarantees degree maintenance and safety under some assumptions.

Constraint with Global State Knowledge

VI
CONCLUSIONS

In this work, we propose a distributed CBF-based controller that guarantees resilient consensus among robots during navigation without relying on fixed network topologies. We first establish a sufficient condition for resilient consensus based solely on the minimum degree of normal agents in a network. Leveraging this condition, we design a controller that enables robots to make movement decisions using only local information, ensuring resilient consensus in a time-varying network. In future work, we aim to extend this framework to accommodate more complex robot dynamics with obstacles. Furthermore, our current analysis assumes continuous connectivity information and a specific form of malicious attack, while real systems operate with discrete updates and potentially varied threats. We aim to address these issues in future extensions.

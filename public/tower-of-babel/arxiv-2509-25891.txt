On a fractional Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman-type monotonicity formula
Fausto Ferrari, Davide Giovagnoli and Enzo Maria Merlino

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35R35, 35R11, 34K37.

Abstract.
In this note, by exploiting mean value properties of
s
-harmonic functions, we introduce some monotonicity formulas in the nonlocal setting. We take into account intrinsically nonlocal functionals mimicking those introduced by Alt, Caffarelli and Friedman in the seminal work
[
6
]
. Our approach is purely nonlocal and does not rely on the extension technique.
As a byproduct we also established interior nonlocal gradient estimates and a nonlocal analogue of the Bochner identity.

1.
Introduction

The Alt-Caffarelli-Friedman (ACF) monotonicity formula plays an important role in free boundary problems. Originally introduced by Alt, Caffarelli, and Friedman in their seminal paper
[
6
]
the formula is applied to prove the regularity result for solutions to two-phase Bernoulli-type elliptic free boundary problems. However, it can also be applied in regularity theory of PDEs to produce gradient estimates,
[
33
, Section 2.2]
.

More precisely, the classical ACF monotonicity formula says that for every two nonnegative continuous subharmonic functions
u_{1},u_{2}\in H^{1}(B_{1})
, such that
u_{1}\cdot u_{2}=0
and
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)=0
, the functional

(1.1)

I_{ACF}(u_{1},u_{2},R):=\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u_{1}|^{2}}{|x|^{n-2}}dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u_{2}|^{2}}{|x|^{n-2}}dx\right),

is bounded for
0<r<1
and is an increasing function of
r\in(0,1)
, where, as usual,
B_{R}
denotes the Euclidean ball of size
R
centered at
0
.
Several generalizations of this formula can be found in the literature; see, for instance
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
20
,
25
,
32
,
41
,
42
,
47
]
.

In this paper, we begin to investigate some possible counterparts of the classical result for the fractional Laplacian.

In literature, we may find several results on nonlocal free boundary problems where, in principle, a nonlocal ACF formula might be useful. In light of some weak connections with the present paper, let us recall
[
21
]
, where the authors study a local one-phase Bernoulli problem associated to the Caffarelli–Silvestre extension of the fractional Laplacian and, in the same line of research, the results on the extended two-phase version available in
[
3
,
4
]
. In addition, it is worth pointing out the following papers
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
, where a purely nonlocal one-phase Bernoulli problem is addressed using new techniques that bypass the use of monotonicity formulas. For the fractional obstacle problem, in its extended formulation, we also refer to
[
5
]
, where a sharp analysis concerning the non-existence of two phases in that extended framework is carefully discussed.

As a first step, we recall that, in the local case, the functional

(1.2)

J_{ACF}(u,R):=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{n-2}}dx

enjoys monotonicity properties, see for instance
[
33
]
. This functional, together with its generalizations associated with different classes of operators, has been extensively studied, see for instance
[
26
,
28
,
29
,
33
]
.

Hence, motivated by these researches, we introduce a formula that can be regarded as the natural nonlocal counterpart of (
1.2
). In the following, we outline our main idea.

In the local case, the Poisson kernel for Laplace operator in the ball
B_{r}
is given by

K_{r}(x,y):=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}r}\frac{r^{2}-|x|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n}},

for
x\in B_{r}
and
y\in\partial B_{r}
, where
\omega_{n}
is the
n
-Lebesgue measure of the unit ball in
\R^{n}
, see for instance
[
30
]
. Thus, given a sufficiently regular function
u
in the ball
B_{1}
, applying the coarea formula yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{n-2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{r^{n-2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}r\fint_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dr=\int_{0}^{R}r\int_{\partial B_{r}}K_{r}(0,y)|\nabla u(y)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dr,

for
0<R<1
.

Hence, the functional in (
1.2
) turns in

(1.3)

J_{ACF}(u,R)=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2}}\,dy=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{R}r\int_{\partial B_{r}}K_{r}(0,y)|\nabla u(y)|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,dr.

This remark motivates the introduction of the following functional

(1.4)

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R):=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{r}}K_{r}^{s}(0,y)g_{u}(y)dy\,dr,

as the nonlocal counterpart of the monotonicity formula,
where
K_{r}^{s}
is the Poisson kernel for fractional Laplacian, defined as

(1.5)

K^{s}_{r}(0,y):=a_{n,s}\Bigg(\frac{r^{2}}{|y|^{2}-r^{2}}\Bigg)^{s}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},

see
[
31
, Chapter I, Section 6, n. 23]
, or Subsection
2.2
in this paper, for the definition of
K^{s}_{r}
. Moreover,
g_{u}:\R^{n}\to\R
is a function that would like to represent the non-local effect played by the squared norm of the gradient in the local functional (
1.3
). We will discuss this point later on, in the Introduction.

Our approach is inspired by an idea developed in
[
27
]
, in the local setting, where the authors relate the monotone increasing behavior of
J_{ACF}
with the (sub)mean value property of (sub)harmonic functions. More precisely, in the local case, if
u
is harmonic, then
|\nabla u|^{2}
is subharmonic and the monotonicity of
J_{ACF}(u,R)
can be deduced from the monotonicity of
\fint_{\partial B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}
, for sufficiently small
R
.

Similarly, in the nonlocal setting, one could prove the monotonicity of (
1.4
) if
g_{u}
were a
s
-subharmonic function.
Although this condition on
g_{u}
arises naturally, in the nonlocal framework, it is not always straightforward to verify. In particular, it remains unclear which quantity best plays the role of
|\nabla u|^{2}
in the nonlocal context.

As a consequence, the function
g_{u}
plays a central role in this approach. In the nonlocal framework, however, its definition has not yet been firmly established, since in the current state of the play, there are several possible alternatives to choose from. In this paper, we focus on two examples that appear to be reasonable candidates, although both of them might seem partially unsatisfactory from different perspectives.

The first candidate is closely related to the variational formulation of the fractional Laplacian. Specifically, we assume that
g_{u}
is the function
G_{u}
, defined as

G_{u}(y):=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(y)-u(\eta))^{2}}{\left\lvert y-\eta\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\eta\quad\text{for }y\in\R^{n},

where
C_{n,s}
is a suitable normalization constant controlling the behavior of
G_{u}
as
s\to 1^{-}
. The function
G_{u}
would play the role of a surrogate for the squared norm of the gradient in the variational formulation of the fractional Laplacian, since

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{u}(y)dy=[u]^{2}_{W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
[u]^{2}_{W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is the Gagliardo seminorm, defined in (
2.1
).
Moreover,
G_{u}
naturally arises when computing
(-\Delta)^{s}u^{2}

and therefore appears particularly effective in obtaining interior gradient estimates via integration by parts, see Theorem
1.3
.

An alternative choice for
g_{u}
is motivated by the contributions in fractional calculus proved in
[
48
]
and in
[
8
,
18
,
19
]
. In fact, several authors focused on a nonlocal notion of gradient known as the
distributional Riesz fractional gradient
. Originally introduced in the work of Riesz
[
35
]
, see also the description of partial derivative of fractional order contained in
[
39
]
, distributional Riesz fractional gradient provides a natural counterpart of the local gradient:

\nabla^{s}u(y):=\mu_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(y-\eta)(u(y)-u(\eta))}{|y-\eta|^{n+s+1}}\,d\eta,\quad\text{for }y\in\R^{n},

where,
\mu_{n,s}
is a suitable normalization constant controlling the behavior of
\nabla^{s}
as
s\to 1^{-}
. For further details, see
[
48
]
as well as
[
18
, Section 1]
. Following this idea, we consider the choice
g_{u}(y)=|\nabla^{s}u(y)|^{2}
, where

(1.7)

|\nabla^{s}u(y)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(y_{i}-\eta_{i})(u(y)-u(\eta))}{|y-\eta|^{n+s+1}}dy\right)^{2}\quad y\in\R^{n}.

Furthermore, analogously to (
1.6
), whenever
\nabla^{s}u\in L^{2}(\R^{n},\R^{n})
, there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
n
and
s
, such that

\|\nabla^{s}u(y)\|_{L^{2}(\R^{n},\R^{n})}^{2}=C[u]_{W^{s,2}(\R^{n})},

see, for instance,
[
2
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Of course, we point out that many regularity results in the framework of fractional operator have been established through monotonicity formulas involving lifted operators, via the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension, see for instance
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
.

Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, a nonlocal counterpart of the one-phase functional
J_{ACF}
, as defined in (
1.2
), is not known yet.

In fact, this paper aims to prove the existence of an intrinsic nonlocal counterpart of (
1.2
) that exhibits a monotone behavior in the radius. Moreover, we study its properties, under suitable assumptions on the function
u
and
g_{u}
as well. Nevertheless, we don’t know if the existence of such tool, in the fractional framework, may find a useful application to free boundary problems as well as in the local case happens. In any case, as a consequence of this research, we obtained some interesting byproducts.

More precisely, the main results we obtain are the following ones.

Theorem 1.1

(Monotonicity formula for
G_{u}
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon,\,\delta>0
be fixed. Assume that
u\in C_{loc}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})
and

G_{u}\in C_{loc}^{2s+\delta}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})\;\text{ and }\;(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}\leq 0\text{ in a neighborhood of the origin}.

Then the map

R\mapsto J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R):=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{r}(0)}K^{s}_{r}(0,y)G_{u}(y)\,dy\,dr

is monotone increasing for
R
sufficiently small.

The next result proves that our definition of
J_{ACF}^{s}(u,\cdot)
is coherent with
J_{ACF}(u,\cdot)
as
s\to 1^{-}
. Namely fixed
u
and
R>0
, it holds

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)\to J_{ACF}(u,R),\quad\text{as }s\rightarrow 1^{-}.

Theorem 1.2

(Stability of
J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)
as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon,\,\delta>0
be fixed. Assume that
u\in C_{loc}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})
and
G_{u}\in C_{loc}^{2s+\delta}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})
, for
R>0
it holds

(1.8)

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)\longrightarrow\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2}}\,dy\quad\text{as }s\rightarrow 1^{-}.

As already mentioned, the definition of
G_{u}
also allows obtaining interior
nonlocal gradient
estimates

Theorem 1.3

(Interior
nonlocal gradient
estimates)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
with
2s\neq n
. Let
u\in C^{2}_{loc}(\R^{n})\cap L^{2}_{s}(\R^{n})
, be such that

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=f\quad&\textit{in }B_{1}\\
u=0\quad&\textit{on }\R^{n}\setminus B_{1},\end{cases}

with
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
. Let us assume that for
G_{u}\in C^{2s+\delta}_{loc}(\R^{n})\cap L^{1}_{s}(\R^{n})
some
\delta>0
small, and
(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}\leq 0
in
B_{1}
. Then

(1.9)

\|G_{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}^{1/2}\leq C_{0}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\right),

for some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(n,s)>0.

We report hereafter the results obtained for
the case of
g_{u}=|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}
. The first is the companion result of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4

(Monotonicity formula for
|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}
)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. Assume that
u\in C^{s+\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\R^{n})\cap L^{1}_{s/2}(\R^{n})
and that

|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}\in C^{2s+\delta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\R^{n})\cap L^{1}_{s}(\R^{n}),\ \text{with }(-\Delta)^{s}|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}\leq 0\text{ in a neighborhood of the origin}.

Then the map

(1.10)

R\mapsto\mathfrak{J}_{ACF}^{s}(u,R):=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{r}(0)}K^{s}_{r}(0,y)|\nabla^{s}u(y)|^{2}\,dy\,dr

is monotone increasing for
R>0
sufficiently small.

Furthermore, for sufficiently regular functions, the functional
\mathfrak{J}_{ACF}^{s}
recovers the classical one-phase formula as
s\to 1^{-}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Stability of
\mathfrak{J}_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)
as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}
)
.

Let
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\R^{n})
and
|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}\in C_{\textrm{loc}}^{2s+\delta}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})
. Then, for
R>0
,

\mathfrak{J}_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)\longrightarrow\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u(y)|^{2}}{|y|^{n-2}}\,dy\quad\text{as }s\to 1^{-}.

Concerning the assumption on the
s
-subharmonicity of
g_{u}
, we point out that in the local case, as discussed in
[
29
]
, the Bochner identity represents a natural link between the harmonicity of a function and the subharmonicity of the squared norm of its gradient:

(1.11)

(-\Delta)(|\nabla u|^{2})=2\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla((-\Delta)u)\right\rangle-2\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert^{2}.

Motivated by this observation, in Section
5
, we investigate a nonlocal Bochner-type identity that involves our choice of
g_{u}
is discussed.
In particular, under suitable hypothesis on
u
we obtain the following formula

(1.12)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}

\displaystyle=2C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-z))(-\Delta)_{x}^{s}(u(x)-u(x-z))}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle-C_{n,s}^{2}\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{\left(u(x)-u(x-z)-u(y)+u(y-z)\right)^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy\,dz

this can be understood as a nonlocal analogue of (
1.11
), since in the limit
s\to 1^{-}
the former converges to the latter, and (
1.12
) is naturally connected with the
s
-subharmonicity of
G_{u}
.
Along the way, among the particular cases in which the
s
-subharmonicity of
G_{u}
is satisfied, we are able to provide an alternative proof of a classical nonlocal Liouville-type theorem, see Theorem
5.4
.
Nonetheless, the
s
-subharmonicity condition for
G_{u}
is generally difficult to verify explicitly.

Analogous results, concerning a Bochner-type identity, hold also for
|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}
. In particular, we remark that the required assumptions in this case are easier to be handle.
In fact, in this case, the commutativity property of
\nabla^{s}
with the fractional Laplacian allows the construction of explicit examples where the monotonicity behavior can be established, leading to the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\R^{n})
be such that
(-\Delta)^{s}u
is constant in a neighborhood of the origin.
Then the map

(1.13)

R\mapsto\mathfrak{J}_{ACF}^{s}(u,R):=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{r}(0)}K^{s}_{r}(0,y)\,|\nabla^{s}u(y)|^{2}\,dy\,dr

is monotone increasing for
R>0
sufficiently small.

The work is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the main notation and we recall the basic results we need to apply in the sequel.
In Section
3
we prove the main results when
g_{u}=G_{u}
, pointing out that the scheme of the proofs applies when
g_{u}=|\nabla^{s}u|^{2}
. Indeed, the companion proofs are collected in Section
4
, where some properties of
\nabla^{s}
are recalled as well.
Moreover, we dedicate Section
5
to studying a nonlocal Bochner-type formula associated to
G_{u}
.
Finally, we added Appendix
6
, where we give some details about a few technical steps contained in the proofs.

2.
Notations and preliminary results

2.1.
Some nonlocal quantities

Let
\Omega\subseteq\R^{n}
be an open set and
s\in(0,1)
. Then we define the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,2}(\Omega)
as

W^{s,2}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\Big|[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\},

where

(2.1)

[u]_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is the Gagliardo seminorm of
u
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
, the
s
-fractional Laplacian of
u
is the nonlocal operator defined as

(2.2)

\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\;:=\;&C_{n,s}\,P.V.\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\\
\;=\;&C_{n,s}\,\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{{\R^{n}}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy,\end{split}

where
u:\R^{n}\to\R
and the constant
C(n,s)
is a dimensional constant that depends on
n
and
s
, precisely given by

(2.3)

C_{n,s}=\left(\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{1-\cos(\zeta_{1})}{|\zeta|^{n+2s}}\,d\zeta\right)^{\!-1}

where
\zeta_{1}
is, up to rotations, the first coordinate of
\zeta\in\R^{n}
, see
[
22
]
.
In general, we will omit
P.V.
in the notation. For
s\in(0,1)
, the definition (
2.2
) is well posed for sufficiently smooth functions belonging to the weighted space

L^{1}_{s}(\R^{n}):=\Big\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\R^{n})\;\mbox{ s.t. }\;\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}\,dx<\infty\Big\}.

Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
, sufficiently small and
u\in L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})\cap C_{loc}^{0,2s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})
(or
C_{loc}^{1,2s+\varepsilon-1}(\R^{n})
for
s\geq 1/2
), the fractional Laplacian is well defined as in (
2.2
), see
[
40
, Propostion 2.1.4]
. In the following, we always write
C^{2s+\varepsilon}
to denote both
C^{0,2s+\varepsilon}
for
s<1/2
and
C^{1,2s+\varepsilon-1}
for
s\geq 1/2
. We usually use this regularity assumption to guarantee its pointwise meaning. We recall that the fractional Laplacian can be viewed as a pseudo-differential operator of symbol
|\xi|^{2s}
, namely if
u\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
it holds

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,=\,\mathscr{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2s}(\mathscr{F}u(x)))

for any
x,\xi\in\R^{n}
.
We recall that a function
u\in C^{2s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L^{1}_{s}(\R^{n})
, is
s
-harmonic (respectively
s
-subharmonic or
s
-superharmonic) in
\Omega
if

(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\,(\text{resp.}\leq 0\,\text{ or }\geq 0),\qquad\text{ in }\Omega.

Let
s\in(0,1)
we define

(2.4)

G_{u}(y):=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(y)-u(\eta))^{2}}{\left\lvert y-\eta\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\eta,

where
u:\R^{n}\to\R
and
C_{n,s}
is the constant defined in (
2.3
). The term
G_{u}
in the nonlocal setting plays the role of the gradient norm squared. We motivate this choice observing that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{u}(y)dy=[u]^{2}_{W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

that is the energy associated to the variation formulation of the fractional Laplacian.

Moreover, the term
G_{u}
naturally arises in the expansion of the fractional Laplacian of the square of the function
u
. Namely, it plays the role of the gradient norm squared in the classical identity for
\Delta(u^{2})
, as clarified in the next result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. Then for
u\in L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})\cap C_{loc}^{2}(\R^{n})
we have

(-\Delta)^{s}u^{2}(x)=2u(x)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-G_{u}(x),\quad\text{for }x\in\R^{n}.

Proof.

By expanding the square and rearranging terms, we obtain

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{u^{2}(x)-u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)+u(y))(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=C_{n,s}\,u(x)\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy+C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{u(y)(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=2u(x)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy=2u(x)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-G_{u}(x).

\Box

Remark 2.2
.

Differently from the case of the fractional Laplacian, the integral in (
2.4
) does not need to consider the principal value sense. Indeed, let us suppose that
u\in\mathcal{S}(\R^{n})
, since
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}<\infty
, by Mean value Theorem, it exists a suitable constant
C>0
such that for any
R>0
it holds

\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\left|x-y\right|\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}\left|x-y\right|<C\left|x-y\right|.

So, passing to polar coordinates, for any
R>0
, for some constant
c>0
(possibly changing line by line), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{\,|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy+4\,\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{1}{\,|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\left(\int_{B_{R}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+2s-2}}\,dy+\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{1}{\,|x-y|^{n+2s}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{R}\!\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{\rho^{n+2s-2}}\,d\rho\,+\int_{R}^{+\infty}\!\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{\rho^{n+2s}}\,d\rho\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{R}\!\frac{1}{\rho^{2s-1}}\,d\rho\,+\int_{R}^{+\infty}\!\frac{1}{\rho^{2s+1}}\,d\rho\right)\,<\,+\infty,

since
2s-1<1
and
2s+1>1
, for any
s\in(0,1)
. Nevertheless, as for the fractional Laplacian, for
s\in(0,1)
in order to have the integral of the right-hand side of (
2.4
) well defined, we will assume that
u\in L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})\cap C_{loc}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})
, where for any
1\leq p<\infty
, the space
L^{p}_{s}
is the weighted
L^{p}
space defined by

L^{p}_{s}(\R^{n}):=\Big\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\R^{n})\;\mbox{ s.t. }\;\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{1+|x|^{n+2s}}\,dx<\infty\Big\}.

2.2.
Fractional mean value properties.

In this subsection, we recall some well-known results concerning potential theory in the framework of the fractional Laplacian, see
[
31
]
.

The fundamental solution for the fractional Laplacian with pole at the origin, denoted by
\Phi_{s}
, is given by

(2.5)

\Phi_{s}(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\kappa_{n,s}{|x|^{-n+2s}}\quad&\text{if }n\neq 2s,\\
-\frac{1}{\pi}\log|x|\quad&\text{if }n=2s=1,\end{cases}

where where
\kappa_{n,s}
is a constant depending only on
n
and
s
, defined by

(2.6)

\kappa_{n,s}:=2^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-s)}{\Gamma(s)}\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\qquad\text{ for }n\neq 2s.

In the distributional sense, we have

(-\Delta)^{s}\Phi_{s}=\delta_{0}\qquad\text{in }\R^{n},

where
\delta_{0}
is a Dirac delta at the origin. More precisely, for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\R^{n})
it results

(-\Delta)^{s}(\Phi_{s}*f)=f\qquad\text{ in }\R^{n}.

Fixed
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\R^{n}
, for any
x\in B_{r}(x_{0})
and any
y\in\R^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})
, the Poisson kernel for
(-\Delta)^{s}
in the ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
, denoted by
K_{r,x_{0}}^{s}
, is the following (see
[
31
, Ch. I, Def (1.6.11’)]
)

(2.7)

K^{s}_{r,x_{0}}(x,y):=a_{n,s}\Bigg(\frac{r^{2}-|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{|y-x_{0}|^{2}-r^{2}}\Bigg)^{s}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}.

where the constant
a_{n,s}
is the constant depending only on
n
and
s
given by

(2.8)

a_{n,s}:=\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\pi^{-n/2-1}\sin(\pi s)

which is used for normalization purposes, which we clarify in the following. When the center is the origin, we omit it in the notation, writing just
B_{r}
and
K_{r}^{s}
.
As in the local case, Poisson kernel
K_{r}^{s}
gives the explicit solution of the homogeneous Dirichlet problem for the fractional Laplacian in the ball
B_{r}
.
Specifically, given
r>0
and

the function defined by

(2.9)

u(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{{\R^{n}}\setminus B_{r}}K^{s}_{r}(x,y)g(y)\,dy&\quad\,\text{if }x\in B_{r},\\
g(x)&\quad\,\text{if }x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}}\end{cases}

belongs to

and it solves

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=0\qquad&\mbox{ in }{B_{r}},\\
u=g\qquad&\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}},\end{cases}

see one more time
[
31
, Ch. I,(1.6.19) p.125]
.
Moreover, from the Poisson kernel in a ball, it is possible to derive the companion mean value property for
(-\Delta)^{s}
. Fixed
r>0
, let us set the function

A^{s}_{r}(y):=\begin{cases}K^{s}_{r}(0,y)=a_{n,s}\frac{r^{2s}}{(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}|y|^{n}}\quad&y\in\R^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r},\\
0\quad&y\in\overline{B}_{r}.\end{cases}

By choice of the constant
a_{n,s}
in (
2.8
), for any
r>0

\int_{\R^{n}}A^{s}_{r}(y)\,dy=1,

see
[
9

defined in (

s

plays the role of the

-mean kernel. Namely, the following propositions hold, see respectively
[

Remark 3.1
.

Given

\displaystyle J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}(0)}\frac{r^{2s}}{\left(|y|^{2}-r^{2}\right)^{s}|y|^{n}}G_{u}(y)\,dy\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{r^{2s}}{\left(r^{4}|x|^{-2}-r^{2}\right)^{s}|x|^{\,n-2s}}\left(\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{\bigl(u\bigl(\frac{r^{2}}{|x|^{2}}x\bigr)-u(\eta)\bigr)^{2}}{|\frac{r^{2}}{|x|^{2}}x-\eta|^{\,n+2s}}\,d\eta\right)dx\,dr

With the representation formula given by (

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
g\in C_{\textrm{loc}}^{2s+\delta}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})
it holds

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}4a_{n,s}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{1}{(r^{2}-\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2})^{s}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2s}}g(y)\,dy=\fint_{\partial B_{r}(0)}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

Proof.
By the Coarea formula, we reach

\displaystyle 2a_{n,s}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{1}{(r^{2}-\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2})^{s}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2s}}g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=2a_{n,s}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\int_{\partial B_{t}(0)}\frac{1}{(r^{2}-t^{2})^{s}}\frac{1}{t^{n-2s}}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=2a_{n,s}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\int_{\partial B_{t}(0)}\frac{1}{r^{2s}(1-\frac{t^{2}}{r^{2}})^{s}}\frac{1}{t^{n-2s}}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\right)\,dt.

Letting
\tau=\frac{t}{r}
and defining

h^{s}(r,\tau):=r^{1-n}(1+\tau)^{-s}\int_{\partial B_{r\tau}(0)}g(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y),

we get

\displaystyle 2a_{n,s}\int_{B_{r}(0)}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r^{2}-\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2})^{s}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2s}}g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=2a_{n,s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{r\tau}(0)}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\right)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=2a_{n,s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-\tau)^{s}}h^{s}(r,\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{2a_{n,s}}{1-s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\{\left[-(1-\tau)^{1-s}h^{s}(r,\tau)\right]_{\varepsilon}^{1}+\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}(1-\tau)^{1-s}\partial_{\tau}h^{s}(r,\tau)\,d\tau\right\}

Since
\frac{2a_{n,s}}{1-s}\to\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}
as
s\to 1^{-}
, taking the limit, we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left[h^{1}(r,\varepsilon)+\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\partial_{\tau}h^{1}(r,\tau)\,d\tau\right]=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n}}h^{1}(r,1)=\frac{1}{2}\fint_{\partial_{B_{r}(0)}}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

\Box

The first remarkable property of
J^{s}_{ACF}
that we provide is the following scaling invariance, which mimics the invariance of the local ACF functional under linear dilatation. This motivates also the choice of the normalizing power that appears in the functional.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u\in C_{\textrm{loc}}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L^{2}_{s}(\R^{n})
and
R>0
be fixed, the functional
J_{ACF}^{s}
is invariant with respect to the scaling given by

u_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{s}}u(\lambda x).

Namely

J_{ACF}^{s}\left(u_{\lambda},\frac{R}{\lambda}\right)=J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R).

Proof.

At first, we notice the following scaling property of
G_{u}

\displaystyle G_{u_{\lambda}}(y)

\displaystyle=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u_{\lambda}(y)-u_{\lambda}(\eta))^{2}}{\left\lvert y-\eta\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\eta=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\lambda^{-2s}\frac{(u(\lambda y)-u(\lambda\eta))^{2}}{\left\lvert y-\eta\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\lambda^{-n-2s}\frac{(u(\lambda y)-u(\xi))^{2}}{\left\lvert y-\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\xi=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(\lambda y)-u(\xi))^{2}}{\left\lvert\lambda y-\xi\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,d\xi=G_{u}(\lambda y),

where the third equality we used the change of variable
\lambda\eta=\xi
. As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle J_{ACF}^{s}\left(u_{\lambda},\frac{R}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,a_{n,s}\lambda^{1+s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R/\lambda}r^{s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}(0)}\frac{r^{2s}}{(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}-r^{2})^{{}^{s}}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n}}\,G_{u}(\lambda y)\,dy\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\,a_{n,s}\lambda^{-2s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{s}\int_{B_{\rho/\lambda}^{c}(0)}\frac{\rho^{2s}}{(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}-\lambda^{-2}\rho^{2})^{{}^{s}}\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n}}\,G_{u}(\lambda y)\,dy\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\,a_{n,s}\lambda^{-2s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{s}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(0)}\frac{\rho^{2s}}{\lambda^{-2s}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}-\rho^{2})^{{}^{s}}\lambda^{-n}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{n}}\,G_{u}(x)\lambda^{-n}\,dx\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\,a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{s}\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(0)}\frac{\rho^{2s}}{(\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{2}-\rho^{2})\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{n}}\,G_{u}(x)\,dx\,d\rho=J_{ACF}^{s}\left(u,R\right),

where in the second equality we used the change of variable
\rho=\lambda r
and in the third equality the change
x=\lambda y
. This concludes the proof.

\Box

The main result of this section concerns the following nonlocal ACF-type monotonicity formula, under
s
-subharmonicity assumption of
G_{u}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let us briefly explain the motivation behind the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
(although they might be further relaxed). At first, the condition
u\in C_{\textrm{loc}}^{s+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
ensures that the quantity
G_{u}
is well defined, controlling both its behaviour near the singularity (via local Hölder continuity) and at infinity (via integrability). Global Hölder regularity is in fact necessary for the pointwise convergence
G_{u}\to 2|\nabla u|^{2}
as
s\to 1^{-}
.

Moreover, the requirement
G_{u}\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2s+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
guarantees the well‐posedness of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}
.

We are ready to introduce the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.

For every
R>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, using the notation of (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}(0)}K_{r}^{s}(0,y)G_{u}(y)\,dy\,dr

\displaystyle\underbrace{=}_{\lambda r=\rho}\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{\lambda R}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\right)^{s}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{\lambda}}^{c}(0)}\frac{\left(\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\right)^{2s}}{\left(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)^{s}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n}}G_{u}(y)\,dy\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n,s}}{(\lambda R)^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{\lambda R}\rho^{s}\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{\lambda}}^{c}(0)}\frac{\rho^{2s}}{\left(\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{2}-\rho^{2}\right)^{s}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n}}G_{u}(y)\,dy\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\lambda R)^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{\lambda R}\rho^{s}M_{s}\left(G_{u},\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\right)(0)\,d\rho

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(\lambda R)^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{\lambda R}\rho^{s}M_{s}\left(G_{u},\rho\right)(0)\,d\rho=J_{ACF}^{s}(u,\lambda R)

where, in the last inequality, we use Proposition
2.4
having
\frac{\rho}{\lambda}>\rho
.
That is for every
R_{1}\leq R_{2}

(3.4)

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R_{1})\leq J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R_{2}).

∎

Regarding the limit as
s\nearrow 1^{-}
, we write
J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)
as

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)=\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}M_{s}(G_{u},r)(0)\,dr.

For every
g\in C_{\textrm{loc}}^{2s+\delta}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})
it is known that (see
[
1
, Appendix C]
)

(3.5)

M_{s}(g,r)(0)=\int_{B_{r}^{c}(0)}K_{r}^{s}(0,y)g(y)\,dy\longrightarrow\fint_{\partial B_{r}(0)}g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\quad\textit{as }s\nearrow 1^{-}.

By Lemma
6.1
it follows the pointwise convergence of

to

as

.
Hence

r^{s}M_{s}(G_{u},r)\longrightarrow 2r\fint_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{r^{n-2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\quad\textit{as }s\nearrow 1^{-}.

Now, integrating from

\frac{1}{R^{1+s}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}M_{s}(G_{u},r)\,dr\longrightarrow\frac{2}{n\omega_{n}}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\left\lvert y\right\rvert^{n-2}}\,dy\quad\textit{as }s\nearrow 1^{-}.

∎

In the next result, we provide an estimate for
J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)
in terms of another functional that mirrors the structure of the local Alt–Caffarelli–Friedman monotonicity formula (
1.2
). The appearance of the fundamental solution of the fractional Laplacian in this estimate plays a decisive role: it is the key ingredient that allows us to derive interior nonlocal gradient bounds.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon>0
be a small fixed constant. Assume that
u\in C_{loc}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})
. Then for
R>0
, it holds

(3.6)

J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)\leq\frac{C}{R^{2s}}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-2s}}\,dx,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
and
s
.

Proof.

For
R>0
, by Tonelli’s Theorem, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)&=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(\int_{0}^{R}r^{s}\int_{B_{r}^{c}}\frac{r^{2s}}{(|x|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\,dx\,dr\right)\\
&=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{r^{3s}}{(|x|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\,dr\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n}}\int_{0}^{|x|}\frac{r^{3s}}{(|x|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}\,dr\,dx\right)\\
&=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{r^{3s}}{\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)^{s}}\,dr\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}\int_{0}^{|x|}\frac{r^{3s}}{\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)^{s}}\,dr\,dx\right)\\
&=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{|x|}}\frac{|x|^{3s+1}\tau^{3s}}{\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{s}}\,d\tau\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n+2s}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|x|^{3s+1}\tau^{3s}}{\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{s}}\,d\tau\,dx\right)\\
&=\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-s-1}}\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{|x|}}\frac{\tau^{3s}}{\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{s}}\,d\tau\,dx+\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-s-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tau^{3s}}{\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{s}}\,d\tau\,dx\right)\end{split}

For any
s\in(0,1)
, it holds that
2s-n<s+1-n
, thus
|x|^{s+1-n}\leq|x|^{2s-n}
for
|x|\leq 1
.
Consequently, the second term of (
3.7
) can be further estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-s-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle=R^{1+s}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{G_{u}(Ry)}{|y|^{n-s-1}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq R^{1+s}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{G_{u}(Ry)}{|y|^{n-2s}}\,dy=R^{1-s}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-2s}}\,dy.

This leads to the following bound for
J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)
.

(3.8)

\displaystyle J_{ACF}^{s}(u,R)\leq\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(R^{1+s}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(Ry)}{|y|^{n-s-1}}\int_{0}^{|y|^{-1}}\hskip-10.00002pt\frac{\tau^{3s}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\,d\tau\,dy\right.

\displaystyle+\left.R^{1-s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tau^{3s}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\,d\tau\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-2s}}\,dx\right).

Defining

\rho\mapsto Q(\rho):=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\tau^{3s}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\,d\tau,

we get

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{Q(\rho)}{\rho^{1-s}}=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\rho^{3s}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{s}}\frac{(1-s)^{-1}}{\rho^{-s}}=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\rho^{4s}}{(1-\rho^{2})^{s}}\frac{1}{1-s}=0.

\displaystyle R^{1+s}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}

\displaystyle\frac{G_{u}(Ry)}{|y|^{n-s-1}}\int_{0}^{|y|^{-1}}\hskip-10.00002pt\frac{\tau^{3s}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\,d\tau\,dy=R^{1+s}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(Ry)}{|y|^{n-s-1}}\left(Q(|y|^{-1})|y|^{1-s}\right)|y|^{s-1}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{n,s}}{R^{1+s}}\left(CR^{1-s}\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-2s}}\,dx+R^{1-s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tau^{3s}}{(1-\tau^{2})^{s}}\,d\tau\int_{B_{R}}\frac{G_{u}(x)}{|x|^{n-2s}}\,dx\right)

\Box

4.
The distributional Riesz fractional gradient

In this section, we propose an alternative definition of the nonlocal quantity
g_{u}
in (

[
12
,
18
,
8
,
19
,
48
]
).
We begin by recalling some basic notions and results concerning the distributional Riesz fractional gradient. Subsequently, we show that most of the analysis carried out in Sections
3
also extends to this alternative choice of
g_{u}
.

In particular, we will highlight that, under suitable regularity assumptions, the commutativity property between the fractional Laplacian and the distributional fractional gradient naturally yields situations in which the compatibility conditions required for the validity of the monotonicity formula (

4.1.
The operators
\nabla^{s}
and
\div^{s}

Now we briefly recall some fundamental definitions and the essential features of the non-local operators
\nabla^{s}
and
\div^{s}
, see
[
48
,
18
,
8
,
19
]
and
[
34
, Section 15.2]
.

Letting
s\in(0,1)
, let us set

(4.1)

\mu_{n,s}:=2^{s}\,\pi^{-\frac{n}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)}.

We let

\nabla^{s}f(x):=\mu_{n,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{|y|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{y\,f(x+y)}{|y|^{n+s+1}}\,dy,\quad x\in\R^{n},

be the
fractional
s
-gradient
of
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n})
and, similarly, we let

\div^{s}\varphi(x):=\mu_{n,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{|y|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{y\cdot\varphi(x+y)}{|y|^{n+s+1}}\,dy,\quad x\in\R^{n},

be the
fractional
s
-divergence
of
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n};\R^{n})
.
The non-local operators
\nabla^{s}
and
\div^{s}
are well defined in the sense that the involved integrals converge and the limits exist. Moreover, since

\int_{\{|z|>\varepsilon\}}\frac{z}{|z|^{n+s+1}}\,dz=0,\quad\forall\varepsilon>0,

it is immediate to check that
\nabla^{s}c=0
for all
c\in\R
and

\displaystyle\nabla^{s}f(x)

\displaystyle=\mu_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(y-x)(f(y)-f(x))}{|y-x|^{n+s+1}}\,dy,\quad x\in\R^{n},

for all
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n})
. Analogously, we have

\displaystyle\div^{s}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\mu_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(y-x)\cdot(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))}{|y-x|^{n+s+1}}\,dy,\quad x\in\R^{n},

for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n})
.
From the above expressions, it is not difficult to recognize that, given
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n})
and
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n};\R^{n})
, it holds that

\nabla^{s}f\in L^{p}(\R^{n};\R^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\div^{s}\phi\in L^{p}(\R^{n})

for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, see
[
18
, Corollary 2.3]
.
Finally, the fractional operators
\nabla^{s}
and
\div^{s}
are
dual
, in the sense that

\int_{\R^{n}}f\,\div^{s}\phi\,dx=-\int_{\R^{n}}\phi\cdot\nabla^{s}f\,dx

for all
f\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n})
and
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(\R^{n};\R^{n})
, see
[
48
, Section 6]
and
[
18
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Finally, we point out that, in general, the assumptions on
f
can be relaxed. Indeed, an argument similar to that in Remark
2.2
shows that, for
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional gradient
\nabla^{s}f
is pointwise well-defined for any function
f\in C^{s+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{1}_{s/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, this notation is coherent with the asymptotic behavior of the fractional operators
\nabla^{s}
and
\div^{s}
when
s\to 1^{-}
for sufficiently regular functions, see the analysis made in
[
19
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By
[
19
, Proposition 2.1]
, we know that

\nabla^{s}u=I_{1-s}\nabla u=\nabla I_{1-s}u,

where, for any
\alpha\in(0,n)
, the Riesz potential of order
\alpha
is defined by

I_{\alpha}u(x):=\frac{\mu_{n,1-\alpha}}{n-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

We recall that, if
\alpha,\beta\in(0,n)
satisfy
\alpha+\beta<n
, then the semigroup property holds:

I_{\alpha}(I_{\beta}\,\varphi)=I_{\alpha+\beta}\,\varphi.

Moreover, for
s\in(0,1)
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

I_{s}u(x)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{-s}\mathscr{F}u(\xi)\right)(x),\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2s}^{-1}u(x)=(-\Delta)^{s}u(x).

Then, for
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any
i=1,\ldots,n
, we get

(4.2)

\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}(\partial^{s}_{i}u)(x)&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2s}\mathscr{F}(\partial^{s}_{i}u)(\xi)\right)(x)\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2s}\mathscr{F}(I_{1-s}\partial_{i}u)(\xi)\right)(x)\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{2s}|\xi|^{s-1}(i\xi_{i}\mathscr{F}u(\xi))\right)(x)\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(i\xi_{i}|\xi|^{3s-1}\mathscr{F}u(\xi)\right)(x)\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(i\xi_{i}|\xi|^{s-1}\mathscr{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi)\right)(x)\\
&=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{s-1}\mathscr{F}(\partial_{i}((-\Delta)^{s}u))(\xi)\right)(x)\\
&=I_{1-s}\partial_{i}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(x)=\partial^{s}_{i}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(x).\end{split}

That is, the operators
(-\Delta)^{s}
and
\nabla^{s}
commute.

As done in Section

5.
A nonlocal Bochner-type formula and some applications of the monotonicity formula

The Bochner formula is one of the fundamental tools in geometric analysis. It provides a deep identity connecting the analytic behavior of smooth functions and differential forms with the underlying geometry of the manifold, through the Ricci tensor. Specifically, for a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the formula reads

(5.1)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}=\|D^{2}f\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla(\Delta f)\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f).

Thus, the Bochner identity relates the Laplacian of the squared gradient norm to both the Hessian of
f
and the Ricci curvature. In the special case of the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{\mathrm{eucl}})
, the Ricci tensor vanishes identically, and the identity (
5.1
) reduces to

(5.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}=\|D^{2}f\|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla(\Delta f)\rangle

which is a key tool to study conditions ensuring the subharmonicity of
|\nabla f|^{2}
, see for example
[
29
]
in the framework of Carnot groups. Thus, in order to analyze the condition
(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}\leq 0
, which is required for the validity of the monotonicity formula in Theorem
1.1
, we aim in this section to investigate a possible nonlocal analogue of (
5.2
), involving the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}
. This is the content of the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
u\in C^{3}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})
be such that
G_{u}\in L_{s}^{1}(\R^{n})
. Then the following identity holds

(5.3)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}(x)

\displaystyle=2C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-z))(-\Delta)_{x}^{s}(u(x)-u(x-z))}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle-C_{n,s}^{2}\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{\left(u(x)-u(x-z)-u(y)+u(y-z)\right)^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy\,dz\quad\text{for }x\in\R^{n}

where the constant
C_{n,s}
is defined in (
2.3
).

Proof.

The delicate step in the proof of this result is to justify the interchange between the fractional Laplacian and the integral sign. Indeed, once this has been established, Lemma
2.1
immediately yields

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}G_{u}

\displaystyle=C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}(u(x)-u(x-z))^{2}}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle=2C_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x-z))(-\Delta)_{x}^{s}(u(x)-u(x-z))}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad-C_{n,s}^{2}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{1}{\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{\left(u(x)-u(x-z)-u(y)+u(y-z)\right)^{2}}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy\,dz\quad\text{for }x\in\R^{n}.

Therefore, the crucial step in the proof is to rigorously justify the first passage. In particular, by employing the representation of the fractional Laplacian as a weighted second-order difference quotient, one needs to ensure the summability of the integral

\int_{\R^{n}}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x+k)-u(x+k-h))^{2}+(u(x-k)-u(x-k-h))^{2}-2(u(x)-u(x-h))^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh\,dk.

In particular, the nontrivial singularity comes up around the origin. Expanding
u
twice near
x
up to the third order terms, we focus on the least order terms that do not vanish. Notice that

\displaystyle u(x+k)-u(x+k-h)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle+\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,k\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,h\right\rangle+g_{1}(h,k)

\displaystyle u(x-k)-u(x-k-h)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle-\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,k\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,h\right\rangle+g_{2}(h,k)

\displaystyle u(x)-u(x-h)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,h\right\rangle+g_{3}(h,k)

The remaining term of lowest order can be estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\frac{2\left(\left\langle D^{2}u(x)h,k\right\rangle\right)^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh\,dk

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert D^{2}u(x)\right\rVert^{2}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}\left\lvert k\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh\,dk

Remark 5.7

We now provide an explicit example in which Theorem

(-\Delta)^{s}\bigl(1-|x|^{2}\bigr)_{+}^{\,s}\;=\;c_{n,s}>0,\qquad\text{in }\ B_{1}.

Let

\rho\geq 0

. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)

has compact support in

and

\rho_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon})

(-\Delta)^{s}(u_{0}*\rho_{\varepsilon})=\bigl((-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}\bigr)*\rho_{\varepsilon},

x\in\R^{n}

Finally, if

and

, then

. Therefore, for

, we obtain

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\R^{n}}c_{n,s}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=c_{n,s}\int_{\R^{n}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=c_{n,s}.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any

(6.1)

\frac{C_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy\longrightarrow\left\langle\nabla u(x),\nabla v(x)\right\rangle\quad\text{as }s\to 1^{-}

Proof.

Take
u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\R^{n})
, we begin by splitting the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\R^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{(u(x)-u(x+h))(v(x)-v(x+h))}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\R^{n}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{(u(x)-u(x+h))(v(x)-v(x+h))}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}

we focus only on
\mathcal{I}_{1}

C_{n,s}\,\mathcal{I}_{2}\to 0
as

. For some
\xi,\eta\in B_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x+h))(v(x)-v(x+h))}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}u(\xi)h,h\right\rangle)(\left\langle\nabla v(x),h\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D^{2}v(\eta)h,h\right\rangle)}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\left(\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle\left\langle\nabla v(x),h\right\rangle\right)}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\left(\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle\left\langle D^{2}v(\eta)h,h\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla u(x),h\right\rangle\left\langle D^{2}u(\xi)h,h\right\rangle\right)}{2\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\left\langle D^{2}u(\xi)h,h\right\rangle\left\langle D^{2}v(\eta)h,h\right\rangle}{4\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh=:\mathcal{I}_{1}^{1}+\mathcal{I}_{1}^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{1}^{3}.

Exploiting the symmetry of
B_{1}
we can compute
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{1}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}^{1}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)\partial_{x_{i}}v(x)h_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)\partial_{x_{j}}v(x)h_{i}h_{j}\right)\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{-n-2s}\,dh

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)\partial_{x_{i}}v(x)\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i}^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh=\frac{1}{n}\int_{B_{1}}\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{2-n-2s}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\rho^{2-n-2s}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\theta)\,d\rho=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2(1-s)}

We can manage the term
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{2}
just observing that for any

We proceed similarly for
\mathcal{I}_{1}^{3}
having

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(D^{2}u(\xi))_{ij}h_{i}h_{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}(D^{2}v(\eta))_{kl}h_{k}h_{l}\Big)}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(D^{2}u(\xi))_{ij}(D^{2}v(\eta))_{ji}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i}^{2}h_{j}^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

for which the last part can be computed as follows

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i}^{2}h_{j}^{2}}{\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dh

\mathcal{I}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2(1-s)}\left\langle\nabla u(x),\nabla v(x)\right\rangle+\frac{\omega_{n}}{4(n-1)(2-s)}\tr(D^{2}u(\xi)D^{2}v(\eta))

exploiting the asymptotics of
C_{n,s}
as
s\to 1^{-}

[
22
, Corollary 4.2]
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\frac{C_{n,s}}{2}\int_{\R^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{C_{n,s}}{2}\left(\frac{\omega_{n}}{2(1-s)}\left\langle\nabla u(x),\nabla v(x)\right\rangle+\frac{\omega_{n}}{4(n-1)(2-s)}\tr(D^{2}u(\xi)D^{2}v(\eta))\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{C_{n,s}}{2}\frac{\omega_{n}}{2(1-s)}\left\langle\nabla u(x),\nabla v(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla u(x),\nabla v(x)\right\rangle.

Now, by a density argument, if
u,v\in C_{loc}^{s+\varepsilon}(\R^{n})\cap L_{s}^{2}(\R^{n})
, let be
(\varphi_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}},(\psi_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\R^{n})
such that
\varphi_{h}\rightarrow u
and
\psi_{h}\rightarrow v
uniformly on compact sets of
\R^{n}
. The first part of the proof it is enough to obtain the pointwise convergence desired.

\Box

The following simple result is employed in the asymptotic analysis for higher order terms.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
a multi-index of height
|\alpha|=k
. For
h\in\R^{n}
, if we denote by
h_{\alpha}=h_{\alpha_{1}}\cdot h_{\alpha_{2}}\dots\cdot h_{\alpha_{n}}
,
then

\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(h_{\alpha})^{2}}{|h|^{n+2s}}\,dh=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{2\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}(k-s)}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{(h_{\alpha})^{2}}{|h|^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{h_{i_{1}}^{2}\cdot\dots\cdot h_{i_{k}}^{2}}{|h|^{n+2s}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}}\int_{B_{1}}|h|^{2k-n-2s}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{2k-2s-1}\,d\rho=\frac{n\omega_{n}}{2\displaystyle\binom{n}{k}(k-s)}.

\Box

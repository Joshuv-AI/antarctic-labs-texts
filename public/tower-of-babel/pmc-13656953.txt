Long-term dietary pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) supplementation on growth, immune response, and antioxidant status in juvenile stellate sturgeon (Acipenser stellatus)
Authors: Seyed Hossein Hoseinifar, Hamed Vardastzadeh, Mostafa Beygi, Majid Khanzadeh, Hien Van Doan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the effects of dietary pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) on growth performance, immune responses, and antioxidant status in juvenile stellate sturgeon (Acipenser stellatus). A total of 120 juvenile sturgeons (121.02 ± 5.91 g) were randomly distributed into four groups, each with three replicates, including a control diet without PQQ and three experimental diets supplemented with 1.5, 3, and 4.5 mg/kg PQQ for 86 days. Following the feeding trial, growth performance was evaluated, and immune markers in both serum and mucus were analyzed, along with serum antioxidant enzyme activities. The results showed that the diet containing 3 and 4.5 mg/kg PQQ significantly improved growth parameters compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Immune indicators, particularly serum and mucus lysozyme activity (in serum of all groups and mucus of group 3 mg/kg) and total immunoglobulin (3 and 4.5 mg/kg) levels, were significantly enhanced in PQQ-supplemented groups (P < 0.05). ACH50 activity was significantly increased in all groups compared to the control group (P < 0.05). A significant increase in soluble mucus protein was observed at 3 and 4.5 mg/kg compared to the control group. Furthermore, PQQ supplementation markedly improved SOD activity across all doses, increased CAT activity at 3 and 4.5 mg/kg, and modulated GPx activity at 1.5 and 3 mg/kg. These results demonstrate that dietary PQQ effectively enhances immune function and growth, leading to improved health and production in juvenile stellate sturgeon.Keywords: Acipenser stellatus, Antioxidant, Growth parameters, Innate immunity, Pyrroloquinoline quinone

Introduction

Sturgeons are among the most economically important fish in the aquaculture industry, which this economic value is mainly due to the high price of their meat and caviar (Dudu & Georgescu, 2024). However, for various reasons including overfishing, destruction of natural habitats, and pollution of water resources, most species of this family face the risk of extinction (Ghasemi et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2022). The stellate sturgeon, one of the five anadromous sturgeon species living in the Caspian Sea, is highly dependent on the rivers of the Caspian basin for reproduction. Due to the construction of dams, changes in riverbed structure (due to excessive sand extraction), and pollution of rivers with urban wastewater, its stocks have severely decreased, and it has been classified as an endangered species (Vahdatiraad et al., 2023). Considerable efforts have been made to restore the stocks of this species and release artificially propagated juveniles into the rivers of the Caspian basin (Abdolmalaki et al., 2021). Despite these efforts, significant mortality in juveniles has been reported due to stress factors, causing economic problems for the breeding farms of this species (Vahdatiraad et al., 2023). For this reason, and in line with the FAO sustainable development goals, there is an increasing interest in the use of natural and environmentally friendly compounds to increase resistance against diseases, stress factors, and environmental challenges.

In this regard, pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a non-covalent redox cofactor containing quinone that has been recently identified and mainly found in bacteria. PQQ can occur in three distinct redox states – oxidized (quinone), semiquinone (semiquinone), and reduced (hydroquinone) – in biological systems, which are converted to each other depending on the availability of protons and electrons (Jonscher et al., 2021). Due to this exact reversible redox property, PQQ has diverse biochemical reactivity and has multiple physiological roles in animals. In fact, due to its high oxidation–reduction (redox) potential, PQQ has significant antioxidant capacity and can protect cells and regulate redox homeostasis. Studies have shown that PQQ can increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes by modulating the SIRT1/Nrf2/Keap1 signaling cascade. Also, this substance can cause the reduction of inflammatory cytokines release in different tissues by simultaneous suppression of the NF-κB pathway.

So far, limited studies in aquaculture have addressed the effects of PQQ on aquatic animals. For example, a study conducted on zebrafish (Danio rerio) has shown that dietary PQQ significantly reduces liver oxidative damage caused by toxin exposure and increases the activity of antioxidant enzymes (Li et al., 2023). In this regard, a study conducted on yellow catfish (Pelteobagrus fulvidraco) showed significant positive effects of PQQ on growth indices, serum indices, and total antioxidant capacity (Shi et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024). Also, PQQ in common carp (Cyprinus carpio) could increase feed consumption and growth efficiency in cold environments and upregulate the expression of genes related to glutathione production and antioxidant defense (Gopika et al., 2022).

Despite these promising findings, most previous studies have been limited to short-term studies and teleost species, and their results cannot be directly generalized to primitive fish such as sturgeons, which have distinct physiological and metabolic characteristics. Also, there is no information about the effects of long-term PQQ application on growth performance, immune responses (especially mucosal immunity), and antioxidant defense in sturgeon species.

Materials and methods

Experimental design and fish rearing

An 86-day feeding trial was conducted at the Ghareh Borun Sturgeon Farming Complex (Sari, Iran) to evaluate the effects of PQQ. At the beginning of the experiment, 120 juvenile stellate sturgeons (A. stellatus) with an initial mean body weight of 121.02 ± 5.91 g and a total length of 38.22 ± 1.52 cm were randomly distributed into 12 fiberglass tanks (10 fish per tank). The tanks were equipped with a recirculating water system. After a two-week acclimation period, fish were randomly assigned to 4 dietary treatments, each with 3 replicates. The treatments included a control group receiving the basal diet without supplementation, and 3 experimental groups fed diets supplemented with 1.5, 3.0, and 4.5 mg PQQ per kg of feed. The selected doses were based on recent papers, such as Shi et al. (2022) on yellow catfish, which showed peak physiological responses between 1.0 and 5.0 mg/kg.

Water quality parameters, including temperature (19 ± 1 °C) and dissolved oxygen (7.65 ± 0.38 mg/L), were monitored daily using an oximeter (HANNA Instruments, model HI9146), whereas pH (7.78 ± 0.06) was measured with a pH meter (EZDO, model 6011). The experimental tanks were maintained under a controlled 12 h light:12 h dark photoperiod. A recirculating seawater system was employed to ensure continuous water exchange and maintain optimal water quality by facilitating the removal of feces and the detoxification of ammonia. All parameters were maintained within the optimal ranges for sturgeon culture.

2–2. Diet preparation and feeding

Fish were hand-fed four times daily (03:00, 09:00, 15:00, and 21:00) at a feeding rate of 2% of body weight using the experimental diets. The basal diet consisted of a commercial feed specifically formulated for stellate sturgeon (produced by Yaghoot Talaei Caspian Co., Iran), and its proximate composition is shown in Table 1. The main ingredients of the diet included Kilka fish meal, corn gluten, wheat gluten, wheat flour, fish oil, soybean oil, lecithin, mineral and vitamin premixes, organic acids, antioxidants, and antifungal compounds. PQQ (product code sc-210,178, CAS No. 72,909-34–3; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, USA) of 99.5% purity, as verified by HPLC data provided in the Certificate of Analysis for the specific lot used, was incorporated into the basal diet, with its inclusion level adjusted based on the requirements of the experimental design. The PQQ was first dissolved in water and subsequently applied to the feed by spraying to ensure uniform distribution (Shi et al., 2022). Following PQQ application, the feed was air-dried at room temperature for 24 h. Subsequently, a 3% gelatin solution was applied as a coating, and the feed was again dried under the same conditions for an additional 24 h to minimize the leaching and potential degradation of PQQ’s bioactive compounds in water.

Growth performance assessment

To evaluate growth performance parameters, biometric parameters of fish, including body weight (measured using a digital balance with 0.1 g accuracy) and total length (measured on a biometric board with millimeter precision), were recorded every two weeks and at the end of the feeding trial. For this purpose, fish were fasted for 24 h prior to each measurement, and anesthesia was induced using clove powder at a concentration of 200 mg/L (Hoseinifar et al., 2020). Growth indices, including weight gain (WG), specific growth rate (SGR), feed conversion ratio (FCR), survival rate (SR), and condition factor (CF), were calculated using standard formulas (Homska et al., 2022).

Sampling

At the end of the feeding trial (day 86), following 24 h of feed deprivation, anesthesia was induced in the fish using clove powder at a concentration of 200 mg/L. Then, mucus and blood samples were randomly collected from each replicate (n = 3 per replicate (tank); 9 fish per treatment). Skin mucus was obtained using the modified method of Ross et al. (2000). Briefly, fish were individually placed in polyethylene zip-lock bags containing 10 mL of a 50 mM NaCl solution. After approximately one minute of gentle exposure, they were removed, and the mucus released into the solution was carefully collected into sterile vials. The obtained mucus samples were centrifuged at 4 °C for 10 min at 1600 ×g to remove debris, and the resulting supernatant was transferred into sterile 2 mL microtubes for subsequent analyses and stored at −80 °C. Blood was drawn from the caudal vein, and the serum was separated by centrifugation (3000 rpm, 15 min, 4 °C) and stored at –80 °C until subsequent antioxidant and immunological analyses (Khanzadeh et al., 2025).

Immune parameters (Serum and mucus)

Lysozyme activity in serum and mucus samples was evaluated spectrophotometrically based on the turbidity reduction method described by Subramanian et al. (2007). The assay employed Micrococcus luteus (ATCC 4698) as the substrate. To prepare the bacterial suspension, lyophilized M. luteus previously cultured on nutrient agar was dissolved in 0.1 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0), and the absorbance was adjusted to 0.6–0.7 at 450 nm using the buffer as a blank. A 2.9 mL aliquot of this bacterial suspension was transferred into a cuvette and allowed to equilibrate for 4–5 min at room temperature. Subsequently, 0.1 mL of potassium phosphate buffer was added to the control cuvette, while 0.1 mL of serum or mucus sample was added to the test cuvettes. The decline in absorbance of M. luteus cells was monitored at 450 nm for 10 min. One unit of lysozyme activity was defined as the quantity of enzyme producing a decrease of 0.001 in absorbance per minute at 25 °C (Subramanian et al., 2007). Soluble protein concentration was determined using a commercial kit (Parstous, Mashhad, Iran) based on the Bradford (1976) method. Serum and mucus total immunoglobulin contents were determined following the procedure described by Siwicki and Anderson (1993). Briefly, 12% polyethylene glycol was introduced into each sample to precipitate immunoglobulins, after which the total protein concentration was quantified. The mixtures were incubated for 2 h at room temperature and subsequently centrifuged. The protein concentration of the supernatant was then remeasured using the Bradford method. The immunoglobulin level was calculated as the difference between the initial and post-precipitation protein concentrations. Complement activity was determined following the procedure described by Yano (1992), which relies on the hemolytic reaction of rabbit erythrocytes. In summary, 0.1–0.25 mL of the test sample was transferred into a tube, and the volume was brought to 0.25 mL using barbital buffer containing Mg²⁺ ions. Subsequently, 0.1 mL of rabbit erythrocyte suspension was added. The mixture was incubated for 2 h at 22 °C, after which 3.15 mL of 0.9% saline solution was introduced. The samples were then centrifuged at 4 °C for 5 min at 386 ×g to eliminate any unlysed red blood cells. The optical density of the resulting supernatant was recorded at 414 nm (Yano, 1992).

Antioxidant parameters

Serum antioxidant enzyme activities were also assessed, encompassing superoxide dismutase (SOD), which was measured using commercial kits supplied by Navand Salamat, Urmia, following the method described by Marklund and Marklund (1974). Catalase (CAT) activity was analyzed by quantifying the decomposition of H₂O₂ and recording absorbance according to the method of Goth (1991), using commercial kits provided by Navand Salamat, Urmia.

Glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activity was determined by assessing NADPH oxidation and recording spectrophotometric changes following the procedure described by Wendel (1980), using commercial kits supplied by Navand Salamat, Urmia.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 16). Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, and homogeneity of variances was evaluated using Levene’s test. Significant differences among groups were determined by one-way analysis of variance (One-way ANOVA), and mean comparisons were performed using Tukey’s multiple range test at a significance level of α = 0.05. To identify the optimal dietary PQQ level, a regression analysis was conducted using a quadratic model relating PQQ dose to the measured responses. The fitted model was used to estimate the dose producing the most favorable performance, and model fit was evaluated using R2. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationships among the measured growth parameters. Correlation coefficients were used to determine the direction and strength of associations, with positive values indicating direct relationships and negative values indicating inverse relationships. The experimental unit was the tank to prevent pseudoreplication. When more than one fish was sampled from a tank, the mean value of the sampled fish was calculated before statistical analysis was performed (true n = 3 per treatment). Data are expressed as mean ± SEM.

Growth performance

The groups receiving 3 and 4.5 mg/kg showed a significant increase in growth parameters, including final weight, weight gain, percentage weight gain, and specific growth rate, compared to the control group (CTL) (P < 0.05). In addition, the feed conversion ratio (FCR) in the 3 and 4.5 mg/kg groups was significantly lower than that in the 1.5 mg/kg and control (CTL) groups (P < 0.05). In contrast, the condition factor and survival rate at different PQQ levels showed no significant differences compared to the CTL (P > 0.05). Fig. 1A-D shows the regression analysis used to determine the optimal dietary PQQ dose in stellate sturgeon. The quadratic regression indicated that 3 mg/kg feed was the most effective supplementation level, producing the best overall growth performance and feed utilization, as reflected by higher FW, WG, and SGR, together with a lower FCR.

Discussion

In the present study, the effects of different dietary PQQ supplementation levels on growth performance, innate immunity (serum and mucus), and antioxidant status of juvenile stellate sturgeon were evaluated over an 86-day period. Our findings demonstrated that dietary PQQ supplementation, particularly at 3 and 4.5 mg/kg, significantly improved growth parameters, including FW, SGR, and FCR. These results clearly highlight the beneficial role of PQQ in enhancing growth performance in this valuable aquaculture species. This study also showed that 3 mg PQQ/kg feed was the optimal dose for growth performance.

This improvement in growth performance aligns with previous reports in other aquatic species, such as zebrafish (Danio rerio) (Li et al., 2023), yellow catfish (Pelteobagrus fulvidraco) (Shi et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024), and common carp (Cyprinus carpio) (Gopika et al., 2022). Studies on non-aquatic species, including broilers, mice, and piglets, have also shown similar results on growth efficiency and feed intake (Samuel et al., 2015; Steinberg et al., 2003; Yin et al., 2019). These positive effects on growth efficiency may be due to regulation of mitochondrial function (Bauerly et al., 2006). Previous studies have shown that PQQ increases mitochondrial biogenesis by increasing cAMP response element-binding protein phosphorylation and increasing the expression of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma activator 1-alpha (PGC-1α), which subsequently improves energy metabolism and, consequently, growth (Chowanadisai et al., 2010). In this study, no fish mortality was observed during the feeding period, which may indirectly indicate improved health status in this sensitive species. This is supported by previous studies that show the positive effects of antioxidants on fish survival. (Zhang et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2019).

The non-specific immune system in fish, as the first line of defense, plays a vital role in fish survival (Mokhtar et al., 2023). The study of immune indices of fish fed PQQ-containing diets in the present study showed significant effects on serum and mucus immune indices. The increase in total immunoglobulin levels in serum and mucus indicates stimulation of the immune system by PQQ. The increase in immune indices was more significant at medium and high levels, indicating the higher efficacy of high levels as an immunostimulant. Mucosal immunity is the first line of defense of the fish body against environmental pathogens and plays a key role in protecting epithelial surfaces such as the skin, gills, and intestine (Salinas et al., 2022). Although the mechanism of PQQ's effect on mucosal immune indices was not investigated in the present study, the improvement in mucosal immune indices is likely due to PQQ's function as a vitamin-like cofactor that can modulate cell signaling pathways involved in immune function. Also, Chowanadisai et al. (2010) suggested that PQQ may stimulate immunity by activating pathways related to the proliferation and differentiation of B lymphocytes (responsible for antibody production) and by increasing immunoglobulin synthesis in lymphoid organs. In addition, PQQ indirectly supports the expression of immune-related genes by maintaining the functional integrity of antibody-producing cells through its high antioxidant properties and reduction of oxidative stress (Steinberg et al., 2003; Harris et al., 2020). Consistent with the results of the present study on immune stimulation, researchers reported an increase in the expression of cytokines that stimulate immunoglobulin production following the administration of PQQ (Kumazawa et al., 1995; Tian et al., 2020).

Investigation of lysozyme activity in serum and mucus also showed a significant increase in fish fed with PQQ. Lysozyme has antibacterial effects by destroying the cell walls of Gram-positive bacteria and also plays a role in modulating inflammatory responses (Ellis, 2001; Song et al., 2021). The significant increase in mucus lysozyme activity in fish fed with 3 mg/kg PQQ as well as the increase in serum lysozyme activity in all groups (regardless of the level of PQQ application) indicates the immunostimulatory effects of PQQ (Misra et al., 2006). The present study did not investigate the mechanism of action of PQQ, but based on previous findings, it can be attributed to improvements in metabolic status and mitochondrial function in immune cells, including phagocytic cells, as well as protection of epithelial cells. Mucosal protection against oxidative damage (Chowanadisai et al., 2010). (Bauerly et al., 2006; Steinberg et al., 2003). Due to the very important role of mucus in the immunity of chondrichthyans, the use of supplements such as PQQ that mobilize mucosal immunity can help maintain health and survival. (Shohreh et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025).

The study of mucus soluble protein in the present study showed a significant increase following feeding with PQQ, and this increase was more significant at medium and high application levels. In line with this, Ma et al. (2024) observed an increase in soluble protein in mucus in a study conducted on yellow catfish, which could be due to protective and mucosal immune activities (Rucker et al., 2009). In other words, PQQ, through its growth factor-like activity and antioxidant properties, can activate cell signaling pathways such as MAPK and NF-κB that are involved in the induction of immune protein synthesis and play a role in epidermal and mucosal cells (Steinberg et al., 2003). Similar to the results obtained for other immune indices, serum ACH50 activity also increased significantly after PQQ administration, confirming stronger activation of the lytic complement pathway. Activation of the complement pathway plays an important role in increasing the primary defense capacity of fish against pathogens (Wei et al., 2022). In line with the results obtained from the study of immune indices, PQQ feeding significantly increased the activity of antioxidant enzymes. Studies conducted on yellow catfish (Ma et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2022), zebrafish (Li et al., 2023), as well as in laying hens (Wang et al., 2016), weaned piglets (Ming et al., 2021), and broiler chickens (Samuel et al., 2015) also reported such an increase.

Antioxidant enzymes play a key role in neutralizing reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Shi et al., 2022) and preventing oxidative stress (Kennedy et al., 2005). These effects may potentially involve PGC-1α and Nrf2/ARE signaling pathways, although this hypothesis requires direct experimental verification (Chowanadisai et al., 2010). In particular, the significant increase in SOD activity at a dose of 4.5 mg/kg (the highest PQQ level) indicates a potentiating effect of PQQ on antioxidant defense lines (Fridovich, 1995). This finding is consistent with the study by Ma et al. (2024), which showed that PQQ increases the expression of defense enzymes such as SOD by reducing free radical production and activating the Nrf2 signaling pathway (Akagawa et al., 2016). The highest GPx activity was observed at a dose of 1.5 mg/kg, indicating an optimal balance at moderate PQQ doses for stimulating this enzyme. GPx, as a second line of defense, neutralizes lipid peroxides and hydroperoxides (Rotruck et al., 1973). The increase in CAT activity at concentrations of 3 and 4.5 mg/kg also indicates an improvement in the physiological capacity of the fish to cope with hydrogen peroxide. PQQ, as a potent non-enzymatic antioxidant, can directly scavenge reactive oxygen species (Ouchi et al., 2009) and can further reduce systemic oxidative stress by enhancing gut health and reducing inflammation (Yin et al., 2019). The activation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway and expression of defense enzymes in sturgeon in response to PQQ is consistent with previous studies on other dietary supplements with antioxidant effects in sturgeon. Studies on antioxidants derived from medicinal plants have shown that under intensive culture conditions, these substances neutralize ROS and effectively reduce serum lipid peroxidation, confirming the importance of these redox cofactors in maintaining physiological homeostasis (Xu et al., 2023; Shohreh et al., 2025).

Conclusions

The findings of the present study showed that the application of PQQ has positive effects on growth performance, innate immunity, and antioxidant capacity in stellate sturgeon. Investigations of different doses indicated that higher doses (levels of 3 and 4.5 mg/kg) were more effective. However, the mechanisms underlying the effects of PQQ were not directly investigated in the present study, and further molecular-level analyses are needed. As the experiment was conducted under non-challenge conditions, changes in immune-related biomarkers cannot be considered direct evidence of enhanced disease resistance; pathogen-challenge trials are therefore warranted. Future studies should also evaluate immune-related gene expression, digestive enzyme activities, and intestinal microbiota to clarify the immunological, digestive, and gut-health effects of PQQ. In addition, long-term feeding trials in other commercially important aquaculture species are required to confirm its efficacy, safety, and species-specific responses.

Observance of ethical codes of midwifery profession in students and graduates of department of midwifery and reproductive health, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences
Authors: Zahra Sohrabi, Mitra Savabi-Esfahani, Mohsen Shahriari, Kobra Salehi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Ethical performance is one of the main components of maternal and fetal care. Nurturing and training students who meet professional ethics in midwifery is of utmost importance. It is essential to assess the level of adherence of students and graduates to the codes of ethics in order to determine the extent of education needs in this regard. Therefore, the present research aims to draw a comparison between the level of adherence to codes of ethics between midwifery students and graduates in the fields of midwifery and reproductive health department at Isfahan University of Medical Sciences.MATERIALS AND METHOD:This descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted in 2021–2022 on 93 midwifery students and 107 graduate midwives of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences. It is crucial to note that the study included all-year midwifery students. The data collection tools included a demographic questionnaire and one more questionnaire to assess adherence to the codes of ethics, which were made available to participants through Google Forms. Data were analysed using SPSS software statistics version (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). A significance level of 0.05 was considered significant for all tests.RESULTS:The mean adherence to codes of ethics was 79.01 ± 14.80 for the students and 79.07 ± 14.55 for the graduate midwives. There was a significant difference in the overall mean score of adherence to the codes of ethics, professional commitments, and self-communication between the two groups of students and graduates (P < 0.05).CONCLUSION:Although ethics is addressed in midwifery education programs, it is required to put considerable emphasis on teaching codes of professional ethics to both the students and graduates. Additionally, an educational and clinical setting is required to identify errors and their causes so that it can help take steps towards adhering to codes of ethics.

Introduction

Humanity is finally moving towards a rational and ethical approach to meeting material and spiritual needs. Therefore, ethics has become the center of future developments. It mostly influences professions that are intertwined with human services, such as medical sciences-related.[12] Midwifery is a sub-branch of medical sciences that concerns the physical, psychological, and social care of women during pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum, as well as newborns.[34] Considering ethics as one of the main components of maternal and fetal care, midwives must follow a set of principles. Adhering to these principles can improve the quality of care, increase public trust, and use them to achieve standards and desirable performance.[35] In most societies, in addition to a set of rules established by legislative bodies, midwives have set and accepted a behavioral and ethical system with no legislative power. Professional ethics in midwifery is a behavioral system that uses self-regulation to monitor behaviors.[6]

Midwifery practice is framed by codes of professional ethics and training students who follow professional ethics is a cornerstone in midwifery care.[7] A system of virtue leads to professional ethics, resulting in development of professional competence or what society expects from us.[6]

In 2013, the Ministry of Health and Medical Education of the Islamic Republic of Iran developed and published 85 midwifery codes of professional ethics in six domains based on working conditions and Iranian culture with the aim of enhancing the quality of midwifery care services in this country.[8] Despite compiling codes of professional ethics, the lack of implementation by midwifery students and graduates could negatively impact the scientific results of the most effective healthcare.[8] Shaali et al. (2018) reported a satisfactory adherence to the codes of ethics among midwives working in maternity and gynecology wards.[9] The study by Rahimparvar et al. (2014) showed a moderate compliance rate of codes of professional ethics among midwives employed in Maternal Child Health Centers.[10] However, in the study of Baghany et al. (2012), the level of compliance to the codes of professional ethics among midwifery students regarding professional commitment and midwifery relationship were 50% and 86%, respectively.[11] Considering the patient’s bill of rights, it is necessary to improve patient-healthcare providers’ communication to ensure quicker and improved treatment; patient satisfaction is an important measure of healthcare quality, which leads to favourable social relationships in the organization.[12]

Medical error is a serious public health problem that can pose a threat to patient safety. Patient safety plays a crucial role in healthcare. The increasing number of medical errors has raised concerns regarding the safety of healthcare services, which has added to its importance.[13]

Adherence to professional ethics is one of the underlying components of maternal and fetal healthcare; as a result, midwives need to follow the codes of professional ethics.[1415] Accordingly, implementing and adhering to these codes result in patient safety, reduced anxiety, increased self-confidence, and mutual satisfaction.[16]

It is of paramount importance to assess professional performance in terms of adherence to the codes of ethics among midwifery students and graduates; therefore, these codes are institutionalized among midwifery students and graduates.[417] Moreover, this helps draw the attention of policymakers to address such prioritized matters based on the evidence acquired.[918]

It is essential to evaluate students’ adherence to the codes of ethics to determine ethical-related educational needs and the evaluation of the mentioned educations. Identifying weaknesses in adherence to these codes can contribute to address the issues. Since healthcare providers’ commitment and responsibility is intertwined with adherence to professional ethics, the present study aimed to make a comparison between adherence to the codes of professional ethics between midwifery students and graduates of the midwifery and reproductive health at the Isfahan University of Medical Sciences.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted in 2022–2023 with the aim of determining adherence level to the codes of professional ethics among midwifery students and graduates of midwifery and reproductive health at the Isfahan University of Medical Sciences.

Study participants and sampling

The study population included all-year undergraduate midwifery students, midwifery master’s students (all areas of study), and Ph.D. students majoring in reproductive health in 2022–2023. The study also included all the midwifery graduates, both undergraduate and master’s (all areas of study), as well as Ph.D. graduates who majored in reproductive health from 2018 to 2021 and who met the inclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria were as follows: willingness to participate in the study and respond to the questionnaire, undergraduate students in their seventh or eighth year of study, master’s students in their second year of the research or even higher (due to completion of specialized courses), Ph.D. students, graduates with undergraduate’s degree and master’s degree in midwifery, as well as Ph.D. graduates majored in reproductive health from 2018 to 2021 at the Isfahan University of Medical Sciences. The sample size was determined to be 200 students and graduates selected through the census method using the following formula.

The education department of the faculty provided a list of all the students’ names. Students and graduates who met the study’s inclusion criteria were added to a WhatsApp group after obtaining verbal consent. Then, an informed consent form was obtained from all the participants after explaining the research objectives.

Data collection tool and technique

Demographic questionnaires and the questionnaire on adherence to codes of professional ethics were provided to the students and graduates through Google Forms. The demographic questionnaire included age, academic degree, work experience, marital status, and hospitalization experience in a maternity ward. It also included some professional questions: number of labor cares, average number of deliveries, job satisfaction (completely satisfied, relatively satisfied, dissatisfied), knowledge of midwifery codes of ethics (fully aware, somewhat aware, not at all aware), and information source regarding midwifery codes of professional ethics (practical courses, internships, books, articles, online sources, workshops, in-service trainings, theoretical courses, some other sources).

Besides a demographic questionnaire, another one named adherence to codes of professional ethics was used in this study; however, it was obtained from the study of Shaali et al. with the permission from the authors. The questionnaire consisted of 61 items, 4 domains including professional commitments, providing midwifery services to the patients and their companions, communication with colleagues, and finally self-communication. In the mentioned study, face validity and content validity were applied, and the reliability of the questionnaire was as 0.91 using the test-retest with a 10-day interval.[9]

Ethical consideration

The present study was approved under the ethics code of IR.MUI.NUREMA.REC.1400.156 by the Research Deputy and Ethics Committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences. The participants were assured that their information would be confidential. An informed consent form was obtained from all individuals included in the study.

Data analysis

The data were analysed using a descriptive and analytical statistics and SPSS version 22.0. The significance level of 0.05 was considered significant for all tests.

Discussion

The present study aimed to determine the adherence level to codes of professional ethics among midwifery students and midwifery, health, and reproductive graduates at the Isfahan University of Medical Sciences. The adherence to codes of professional ethics among students (53.8%) and graduates (54.2%) was ranked in the category of “very good.” Similar results were reported in the study of Shaali et al.[9] In the research of Yousefzadeh et al., most midwives showed a moderate performance in adhering to the codes of ethics.[19] In the research of Yousefzadeh et al., researchers completed a checklist regarding the performance of midwives during various shifts. In contrast, a self-report checklist was used to collect data in the study conducted by Shaali et al. and the present study. The main disadvantages of a self-report scale might be the possibility of providing invalid answers because respondents can answer in such a way that they look better.

In the study of Safaee et al., mothers’ bill of rights concerning labor and delivery by midwives responsible for delivery was at an optimum level, which the mothers assessed. In addition, respecting maternal rights by the responsible midwives was at a higher level, which was scored through a self-assessment scale by the midwives. Most midwives responsible for delivery claimed that the rights are perfectly respected.[20]

In the present study, the mean of overall scores of adherence to codes of ethics and professional commitments and self-communication was significantly higher in graduates compared to the students. The study of Shaali et al. showed no significant difference in adherence to codes of ethics regarding all the four mentioned domains among midwives working in labor and delivery units and maternity wards, implying the same adherence levels to codes of ethics in both groups due to the different work environment, responsibilities, and stress.[9] According to the results of the present study, it can be inferred that students are under direct surveillance of clinical experts during their education. At the same time, they experience increased independency after graduation and increased communication with colleagues. Therefore, it is recommended to provide them with ethical-related education and familiarity with the codes of professional ethics during education and clinical training. It should continue after graduation to ensure that all healthcare providers consider and implement ethical values. Ultimately, this leads to improved adherence to codes of ethics in terms of performance among midwifery students and graduates.

Moreover, the study results indicated that most individuals were unaware of the number of medical errors they had committed. Medication error was the most common among students (44.1%) and graduates (57%). The fear of supervisors was reported as the major barrier to reporting medical errors. Additionally, the majority of errors occurred during patient hospitalization in both groups. In the study by Bagaei et al., out of 84 reported errors, 36 were related to medication errors, and 48 were related to non-medication errors. Among medication errors, the most common error was giving patients the wrong medicine, while the least common error was related to incorrect treatment.[21] Similar results were found in other studies.[2223] Hung et al. showed that the main barriers to reporting medical errors are as follows: fear of being blamed, fear of being labelled as incompetent, fear of the future, legal consequences, and feeling judged by colleagues and managers.[24] Considering the importance of patient safety in providing services, it is essential to focus on prevention strategies to reduce errors and their consequences.

A proactive approach and creating an environment for discussion and exchanging ideas among colleagues and students could increase the intention to report errors. Furthermore, providing education regarding adherence to codes of ethics emphasizing midwifery services to patients and their companions can contribute to reducing errors and reporting in case of occurrence.

Conclusion

Despite the fact that in the present study, the level of adherence to codes of ethics was ranked “very good” for most students and graduates, it is a necessity to continue improving and promoting codes of professional ethics. Although codes of ethics are somewhat mentioned in the midwifery education program, it is still inadequate and incomplete. Thus, it is necessary to include codes of professional ethics in the education program of midwifery students and graduates, and the importance of teaching these codes of ethics needs to be emphasized in the midwifery community.

On the other hand, patient safety has been recognized as one of the important components of providing healthcare services to patients. Educational and clinical settings need to provide an opportunity for discussion and exchange of ideas concerning the errors and their causes. It is required to provide a safe environment where individuals can speak their minds without fear of punishment in order to identify the root causes of errors. This is an important step towards enhancing midwives’ adherence to the codes of professional ethics, leading to healthcare quality improvement and patient satisfaction.

Limitations and recommendations

The use of a self-report form may have overestimated the results of the present study. A larger sample size of students and graduates in various medical fields would also increase generalizability. Furthermore, in order to examine the influence of environment and educational programs, it is recommended to conduct studies on a larger scale to compare the adherence levels to the codes of ethics among students and graduates of different medical universities throughout the country.

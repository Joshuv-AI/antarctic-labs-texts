Abstract




Abstract.

Since near maximum distance separable (NMDS) codes have good algebraic properties and excellent error-correcting capabilities, they have been widely used in various fields such as communication systems, data storage, quantum codes, and so on. In this paper, basing on the generator matrix of Roth-Lempel codes, we present
two classes of NMDS codes which generalize Han’s and Zheng’s constructions in 2023 and 2025, respectively. And we also completely determine their weight
distributions.

Keywords.

NMDS codes, weight
distributions, Roth-Lempel codes

1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q=p^{m}
elements and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
, where
p
is prime. An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
and length
n
. The dual code of
\mathcal{C}

is defined as

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\left\{\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\bm{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=0,\forall\bm{y}=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\right)\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

Let
A_{i}(i=1,\ldots,n)
be the number of codewords with Hamming weight
i
in
\mathcal{C}
, the weight enumerator of
\mathcal{C}
is defined by the polynomial
1+A_{1}z+\cdots+A_{n}z^{n}
and the sequence
(1,A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
is called its
weight distribution. The weight distribution is an important parameter for a linear code, which can
be applied to determine the capability for both error-detection and error-correction
[
1
]
.

For an
[n,k,d]_{q}
linear code
\mathcal{C}
, the Singleton bound of
\mathcal{C}
is
defined by the non-negative integer
S(\mathcal{C})=n−k+1−d
. If
S(\mathcal{C})=0
, then
\mathcal{C}
is maximum distance separable (in short, MDS). If
S(\mathcal{C})=1
, then
\mathcal{C}
is almost MDS (in short, AMDS). If
S(\mathcal{C})=S(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=1
, then
\mathcal{C}
is near MDS (in short, NMDS).

The Reed-Solomon code, as a good class of MDS linear codes, is defined as

\mathrm{RS}_{k}(\bm{\alpha}):=\left\{\left(f\left(\alpha_{1}\right),\ldots,f\left(\alpha_{n}\right)\right)|f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}[x]\right\},

where
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}[x]=\left\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f_{i}x^{i}\mid f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},0\leq i\leq k-1\right\},

and
\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}(i\neq j)
.
It’s easy to prove that

\bm{G}_{RS}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1&\cdots&1&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{n-1}&\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\quad&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{k-2}&\alpha_{2}^{k-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{n-1}^{k-2}&\alpha_{n}^{k-2}\\
\alpha_{1}^{k-1}&\alpha_{2}^{k-1}&\cdots&\alpha_{n-1}^{k-1}&\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\\
\end{matrix}\right)_{k\times n}

(1.1)

is a generator matrix of
\mathrm{RS}_{k}(\bm{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{RS}_{k}(\bm{\alpha})
has the parameters
\left[n,k,n-k+1\right]_{q}
. In addition, if a linear code
\mathcal{C}
is not equivalent to any RS code, then
\mathcal{C}
is the non-Reed-
Solomon (non-RS) type and
\mathcal{C}
is called to be a non-RS code.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the definition of the generalized Roth-Lempel code and recall some necessary lemmas.

Definition 2.1

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of
q
elements, where
q
is a prime power. Let
l+1\leq k+1\leq n\leq q
,
\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}(i\neq j)
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
\bm{v}=

\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
.
The generalized Roth-Lempel (in short, GRL) code
\mathrm{GRL}_{k}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},\bm{M}_{l\times l})
is defined as

\mathrm{GRL}_{k}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},\bm{M}_{l\times l})\triangleq\left\{\left(v_{1}f\left(\alpha_{1}\right),\ldots,v_{n}f\left(\alpha_{n}\right),\bm{\beta}\right)|f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}[x]\right\},

where
\bm{M}_{l\times l}=(a_{ij})_{l\times l}\in\mathrm{GL}_{l}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)
and

\displaystyle\bm{\beta}=

\displaystyle\left(f_{k-l},\ldots,f_{k-1}\right)\bm{M}_{l\times l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a_{11}f_{k-l}+a_{21}f_{k-(l-1)}+\cdots+a_{l1}f_{k-1},\ldots,a_{1l}f_{k-l}+a_{2l}f_{k-(l-1)}+\cdots+a_{ll}f_{k-1}\right).

Remark 2.1

By taking
\bm{v}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
\bm{M}_{l\times l}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\delta\end{pmatrix}
or
\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&1&\tau\\
1&\delta&\pi\\
\end{pmatrix}
in Definition
2.1
, the corresponding code is just the classical Roth-Lempel code or
\overline{\mathrm{RL}_{k}\left(\bm{\alpha},\delta,k,n+2\right)}(\bm{u})
in
[
19
]
or
[
23
]
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 3.7)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k]
code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
k\geq 1
.
\bm{G}
and
\bm{H}
are the

(1)\ \mathcal{C}

(2)

(3)

(4)\ \mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is MDS.

The following lemma provides the crucial information on the weight distributions of NMDS codes and their duals.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
16
]
, Lemma 2)
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,n-k]
NMDS code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
have the same number of
minimum weight codewords, i.e.,
A_{n-k}=A_{k}^{\perp}.
Moreover, the
weight distributions of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
are given by

\displaystyle A_{n-k+\ell}=\binom{n}{k-\ell}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{n-k+\ell}{j}(q^{\ell-j}-1)\!+\!(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}A_{n-k}(1\leq\ell\leq k),

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle A_{k+\ell}^{\perp}=\binom{n}{k+\ell}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{k+\ell}{j}(q^{\ell-j}-1)\!+\!(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n-k}{\ell}A_{k}^{\perp}(1\leq\ell\leq n-k),

(2.2)

respectively.

It’s well-known that the subset sum problem is an
\mathbf{NP}
-complete problem. Given
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
D\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
|D|\geq k
, to determine the value of

\displaystyle\#N(k,b,D)=\#\left\{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\subseteq D\big|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=b\right\}

is the subset sum problem.

For
D=\mathbb{F}_{q}
or
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
\#N(t,b,D)
is given explicitly by the following

Lemma 2.3

(
[
21
]
, Theorem 1.2)
For any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define
v(b)=\begin{cases}-1,&\text{if}\ b\neq 0;\\
q-1,&\text{if}\ b=0,\\
\end{cases}
then

\displaystyle\#N(k,b,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=\frac{1}{q}\binom{q-1}{k}+(-1)^{k+\lfloor\frac{k}{p}\rfloor}\frac{v(b)}{q}\binom{\frac{q}{p}-1}{\lfloor\frac{k}{p}\rfloor},

and

\#N(k,b,\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q}\binom{q}{k},&\text{if}\ p\nmid k;\\
\frac{1}{q}\binom{q}{k}+(-1)^{k+\frac{k}{p}}\frac{v(b)}{q}\binom{\frac{q}{p}}{\frac{k}{p}},&\text{if}\ p\mid k.\\
\end{cases}

3
The NMDS property of
\text{RL}_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},M_{2\times 2}\right)

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\left\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{q-1}\right\}
, in this section, we prove that
\mathrm{RL}_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\bm{M}_{2\times 2}\right)

and determine its weight distribution for

3\leq k\leq q-2

Lemma 3.1

For any positive integer
k\geq 3
, we have
d\left(\mathrm{RL}_{k}^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\bm{M}_{2\times 2}\right)\right)\geq k
.

Proof
. By Definition
2.1
, it’s easy to show that
\mathrm{RL}_{k}^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\bm{M}_{2\times 2}\right)
has the parity-check matrix

\bm{G}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0&0\\
\beta_{1}&\cdots&\beta_{q-1}&0&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\beta_{1}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{q-1}^{k-4}&0&0\\
\beta_{1}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{q-1}^{k-3}&0&0\\
\beta_{1}^{k-2}&\ldots&\beta_{q-1}^{k-2}&a_{11}&a_{12}\\
\beta_{1}^{k-1}&\cdots&\beta_{q-1}^{k-1}&a_{21}&a_{22}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{k\times(q+1)},

(3.1)

where
\bm{M}_{2\times 2}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

For the convenience, we set

\bm{u}_{1}=\left(0,\ldots,0,a_{11},a_{21}\right)^{T},\bm{u}_{2}=\left(0,\ldots,0,a_{12},a_{22}\right)^{T}.

By Corollary 1.4.14 of
[
3
]
, it’s enough to prove that any
k-1
columns of
\bm{G}_{1}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly
independent, i.e., the submatrix consisted of any
k-1
columns in
\bm{G}_{1}
is nonsingular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus we have the following three cases.

Case 1
. Assume that the submatrix
\bm{B}_{1}
consisted of
k-1
columns in
\bm{G}_{1}
contains neither
\bm{u}_{1}
nor
\bm{u}_{2}
, i.e.,

\bm{B}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
\beta_{i_{1}}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-4}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-3}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-2}&\ldots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-2}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-1}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}_{k\times(k-1)},

we consider the matrix
\bm{D}_{1}
given by deleting the last row of
\bm{B}_{1}
, i.e.,

\bm{D}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1\\
\beta_{i_{1}}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-4}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-3}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-2}&\ldots&\beta_{i_{k-1}}^{k-2}\end{pmatrix}_{(k-1)\times(k-1)},

then

\det(\bm{D}_{1})=\prod\limits_{1\leq j<l\leq k-1}\left(\beta_{i_{l}}-\beta_{i_{j}}\right).

Note that
\prod\limits_{1\leq j<l\leq k-1}\left(\beta_{i_{l}}-\beta_{i_{j}}\right)\neq 0,

hence, the
k-1
columns of
\bm{B}_{1}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent.

Case 2
. Assume that the submatrix
\bm{B}_{2}
consisted of
k-1
columns in
\bm{G}_{1}
contains either
\bm{u}_{1}
or
\bm{u}_{2}
, i.e.,

\bm{B}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-4}&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-3}&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-2}&\ldots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-2}&a_{1s}\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-1}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-1}&a_{2s}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{k\times(k-1)}(s=1,2),

Note that
\bm{M}_{2\times 2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)
, it means that
a_{1s}
and
a_{2s}
can not be zero, simultaneously. Without loss of generality, we set
a_{1s}\neq 0
. Now, we consider the matrix
\bm{D}_{2}
given by deleting the last row of
\bm{B}_{2}
, i.e.,

\bm{D}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-4}&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-3}&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-2}&\ldots&\beta_{i_{k-2}}^{k-2}&a_{1s}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{(k-1)\times(k-1)}(s=1,2),

then

\det(\bm{D}_{2})=a_{1s}\prod\limits_{1\leq j<l\leq k-2}\left(\beta_{i_{l}}-\beta_{i_{j}}\right).

Note that
\prod\limits_{1\leq j<l\leq k-2}\left(\beta_{i_{l}}-\beta_{i_{j}}\right)\neq 0,

hence, the
k-1
columns of
\bm{B}_{2}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent.

Case 3
. Assume that the submatrix
\bm{B}_{3}

\bm{u}_{2}

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-4}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-3}}^{k-4}&0&0\\
\beta_{i_{1}}^{k-3}&\cdots&\beta_{i_{k-3}}^{k-3}&0&0\\

and

Lemma 3.2

For any
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q+1})\in\mathrm{RL}_{k}^{\perp}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\bm{M}_{2\times 2}\right)

k

(1) there does not exist any
k
-elements subset
\left\{c_{i_{1}},\ldots,c_{i_{k}}\right\}
of
\left\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{q-1}\right\}
such that
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}c_{i_{j}}\neq 0
and
c_{q}=c_{q+1}=0
;

(2) there does not exist any
(k-2)
-elements subset
\left\{c_{i_{1}},\ldots,c_{i_{k-2}}\right\}
of
\left\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{q-1}\right\}
such that
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-2}c_{i_{j}}\neq 0
and
c_{q}c_{q+1}\neq 0

Proof
. For Lemma
3.2
(1), it’s enough to prove that there does not exist any codeword with Hamming weight

has one of the following forms,

\bm{G}_{41}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0&0\\
\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-3}&0&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-4}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-3}^{q-4}&0&0\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-3}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-3}^{q-3}&0&1\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-3}^{q-2}&1&0\end{pmatrix},\bm{G}_{42}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0\\
\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-4}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-4}&0\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-3}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-3}&0\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-2}&1\end{pmatrix},\bm{G}_{43}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&1&0\\
\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-4}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-4}&0\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-3}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-3}&1\\
\alpha_{1}^{q-2}&\cdots&\alpha_{q-2}^{q-2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Note that

\det(\bm{G}_{41})=-\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq q-3}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\neq 0,

\det(\bm{G}_{42})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq q-2}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})\neq 0,

and

\det(\bm{G}_{43})=(-1)^{2q-3}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{q-2}\alpha_{t}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq q-2}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}).

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\#N\left(q-2,0,\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)=0
, it means
\sum\limits_{t=1}^{q-2}\alpha_{t}\neq 0
, and then
\det(\bm{G}_{43})\neq 0.
Furthermore, any
(q-1)\times(q-1)
submatrices of
\bm{G}_{4}
is nonsingular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and so
\mathrm{RL}_{k}\left(\mathbb{F}_{q},\bm{M}_{0}\right)
is MDS with the parameters
[q+2,q-1,4]_{q}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

\hfill\Box

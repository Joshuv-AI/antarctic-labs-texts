1.
Introduction

All the work presented in this note is performed over an algebraicaly closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic zero.

1.1.
G
-rational Fano threefolds

Let
X
be a rational variety of dimension
n
. Given a subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, a classical problem is to ask if
X
is
G
-rational
, that is, if there exists a
G
-birational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}
. This is equivalent to the condition that (for any birational identification of
X
and
\mathbb{P}^{n}
) the group
G
is conjugate in
\mathrm{Cr}_{n}(\mathbf{k})
to a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
. In this case, we will also say that the group
G
(or more precisely its action on
X
) is
linearisable
. We are interested in the
G
-rationality of Fano threefolds. When
G
stands for the Galois group of a non-closed field, this question has been fully answered by Kuznetsov-Prokhorov
[
11
]
. They use a generalization to non-closed fields by Benoist-Wittenberg
[
2
]
of the intermediate Jacobian obstruction provided by Clemens and Griffiths in
[
6
]
. In the present note, we utilise the Clemens-Griffiths criterion in a
G
-equivariant setting for geometric group actions over an algebraically closed field. We apply this technique for Fano threefolds in the deformation Family
\textnumero 2.12
. See
[
8
]
and
[
5
]
for other applications of this method.

1.2.
Fano threefolds in the family №2.12

A Fano threefold
X
in family №2.12 is the smooth complete intersection of three divisors of bidegree
(1,1)
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
. We may write the three equations in the form

(1)

\mathbf{x}^{T}M_{i}\mathbf{y}=0,

where
M_{i}
is a four-by-four matrix,
i
varies from
1
to 3, and
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0}:\dots:x_{3}]
and
\mathbf{y}=[y_{0}:\dots:y_{3}]
are the coordinates of a respective copy of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Now consider
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
, the first projection map. This map is the blowup along a non-hyperelliptic curve
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
of genus 3 and degree 6. Similarly, the second projection
\pi^{\prime}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
is the blowup of a second curve
C^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}
. The curves are isomorphic because they are both isomorphic to the plane quartic

\det(xM_{1}+yM_{2}+zM_{3})=0

where the
M_{i}
are as in (
1
).

Now let
G
be a group acting faithfully on
X
. Recall that
X
is
G
-Fano
if
\Pic^{G}(X)=\mathbb{Z}[-K_{X}]
. Our main result, answering a question of Cheltsov-Li-Ma’u-Pinardin
[
3
, Remark 6]
, is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group acting faithfully on
X
. Then the
G
-action on
X
is linearisable if and only if
X
is not
G
-Fano.

If
X
is not
G
-Fano, then the result is almost trivial, so the content of the result is the converse direction. Our approach is to isolate an element
\sigma\in G
which leaves only
K_{X}
invariant, and compute the
\sigma
-action on the Lie algebra of the intermediate Jacobian
\operatorname{IJ}_{X}
of
X
. We then show that this differs by a sign from the action on the Lie algebra of
\J_{C}
, the Jacobian of
C
, where the action on
C
is induced in a natural way. Since
C
is a plane quartic, this would imply the result.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Denote
E
denote the class of the exceptional divisor of the blowup
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
and let
H
be the pullback of the hyperplane class on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Then
\Pic(X)=\mathbb{Z}H\oplus\mathbb{Z}E
. Similarly, we have a second exceptional surface
E^{\prime}
and a second hyperplane class
H^{\prime}
coming from
\pi^{\prime}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Now

H^{\prime}\sim 3H-E,E^{\prime}\sim 8H-3E,

and moreover
H
and
H^{\prime}
span the nef cone. Note that

K_{X}\sim-4H+E\sim-H-H^{\prime},

in particular the embedding in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
(or more precisely its composition with the Segre embedding in
\mathbb{P}^{15}
) is anticanonical.
We begin with an equivalent condition for
G
-Fanoness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group acting faithfully on
X
. Then
X
is
G
-Fano if and only if
G
contains an element
\sigma
that swaps
H
and
H^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
H
and
H^{\prime}
span the nef cone, this
G
must preserve the set
\{H,H^{\prime}\}
. If
G
does not contain such a
\sigma
, then
G
must preserve
H
and
H^{\prime}
individually, whence
H,H^{\prime}\in\Pic^{G}(X)
so
\Pic^{G}(X)=\mathbb{Z}[H]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[H^{\prime}]
. Conversely, if
G
contains such a
\sigma
, then
\Pic^{G}(X)=\mathbb{Z}[-K_{X}]=\mathbb{Z}[H+H^{\prime}]
.
∎

Let us assume
X
is
G
-Fano, and let
\sigma\in G
be an element which swaps
H
and
H^{\prime}
. It clearly suffices to prove (the nontrivial direction of) Theorem
1.1
with
G=\langle\sigma\rangle
, so we assume this going forward.

Now by assumption, we may choose an isomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}(\sigma^{*}H)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{X}(H^{\prime})
. This in turn induces an isomorphism

\beta\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}(\sigma^{*}H^{\prime})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(\sigma^{*}(-K_{X}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(\sigma^{*}(-H))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-H^{\prime})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(H).

Then
\beta\circ\alpha
(or more precisely
\beta\circ\sigma^{*}\alpha
) is a linearisation of the
\sigma^{2}
-action on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)
, and this induces a representation of
\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle
on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))
, which splits as a direct sum of characters (i.e. is diagonalisable). Picking coordinates
x_{0},\dots,x_{3}
on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))
such that
\sigma^{2}
acts as

\sigma^{2}\cdot(x_{0},\dots,x_{3})=(\varpi^{r_{0}}x_{0},\dots,\varpi^{r_{3}}x_{3}),

where
\varpi
is a primitive
|\sigma|
-root of unity, set
y_{i}:=\alpha(x_{i})\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H^{\prime}))
. Then
G
acts on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))\oplus H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(H^{\prime}))
as

(3)

\sigma\cdot((x_{0},\dots,x_{3}),(y_{0},\dots,y_{3}))=((y_{0},\dots,y_{3}),(\varpi^{r_{0}}x_{0},\dots,\varpi^{r_{3}}x_{3}))

And this induces an action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
for which the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
is equivariant.

This also induces a
G
-action on the two-dimensional linear system of
(1,1)
-divisors on
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}
which contain
X
. Explicitly, it is given by

(4)

\sigma\cdot\mathbf{x}^{T}M_{i}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}^{T}\diag(\varpi^{r_{0}},\dots,\varpi^{r_{3}})M_{i}^{T}\mathbf{y},

where the
M_{i}
are as in (
1
).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
X
is
G
-Fano, then we may choose our three
(1,1)
-divisors to be
G
-invariant.

Proof.

The three equations (
1
) cutting out
X
is exactly the kernel of the restriction map
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}(1,1))\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,1))
which is clearly equivariant. Since

is cyclic, its action on
\ker(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}(1,1))\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,1)))
is diagonalisable, and choosing an invariant basis, we obtain the result.

The

Let us now turn our attention to the

3.2.
The action on the intermediate Jacobian

The key technical result is the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\operatorname{IJ}_{X}
be the intermediate Jacobian of
X
. Then

\Lie(\operatorname{IJ}_{X})=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{3}\chi_{s_{j}+(\sum_{i=1}^{3}s_{i})-(\sum_{i=0}^{3}r_{i})}\otimes\mathrm{sgn},

where
\mathrm{sgn}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the sign representation which sends
\sigma
to
-1
. In particular, the three-dimensional
G
-representations
\Lie(\operatorname{IJ}_{X})
and
\Lie(\J_{C})
differ by a sign, and therefore cannot be isomorphic.

We proceed with a few lemmas:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
0\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-n}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{1}\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence of sheaves on
X
. Let
0\leq s\leq n
. If for every
r\in\{0,...,n\}\setminus\{s\}
we have
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}^{-r})=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
then we have
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}^{1})=H^{i+s}(X,\mathcal{F}^{-s})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let us induct on
n
. If
n=0
the result is trivial. Now supposing true for
n-1
, we consider the exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-n}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{1}\rightarrow 0
. Write
d^{i}
for the map
d^{i}\colon\mathcal{F}^{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{i+1}
. We may truncate the sequence to obtain the following exact sequences:

(5)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-n}\rightarrow...\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-s-1}\rightarrow\im d^{-s-1}\rightarrow 0,

(6)

0\rightarrow\im d^{-s-1}=\ker d^{-s}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-s}\rightarrow\im d^{-s}\rightarrow 0,

and

(7)

0\rightarrow\im d^{-s}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{-s+1}\rightarrow...\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{1}\rightarrow 0.

By induction applied to (
5
), it follows that
\im d^{-s-1}
has no cohomology in any degree. It thus follows from (

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}^{-s})=H^{i}(X,\im d^{-s})
for all

. Finally, it follows from the inductive hypothesis that
H^{i+s}(X,\im d^{-s})=H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}^{1})
for all

, as desired.
∎

(9)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-3,-3)\xrightarrow{d^{-3}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-2,-2)^{3}\xrightarrow{d^{-2}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-1,-1)^{3}\xrightarrow{d^{-1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow 0.

More generally, by twisting (
9
) we obtain exact sequences:

(10)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-3+a,-3+b)\xrightarrow{d^{-3}_{a,b}}\dots\xrightarrow{d^{-1}_{a,b}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(a,b)\xrightarrow{d^{0}_{a,b}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(a,b)\rightarrow 0

for each
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Now applying Lemma
3.5
to (
9
) with
s=0
and
n=3
(noting that by the Künneth formula we have
H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-r,-r)=0
for all
1\leq r\leq 3
) we find that

(11)

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\text{if }i=0,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now applying Lemma
3.5
to (
10
) with
a=-1,b=0
, we have

(12)

H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1,0))=0=H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(0,-1))

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
(the second equality holds by symmetry).

The result now follows from (
11
) and (
12
) applied to the long exact sequence of (
8
).

∎

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Recall that
\Lie(\operatorname{IJ}_{X})=H^{2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
. This follows directly from the Hodge diamond.
Let us consider the conormal sheaf sequence.

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}|_{X}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X}\rightarrow 0.

Since the map
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}|_{X}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X}
is clearly equivariant, the conormal sheaf
\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}
naturally inherits a
G
-linearisation. In particular, each of its cohomologies inherit the structure of a
G
-representation. Thus by Lemma
3.7
, there is an isomorphism of
G
-representations
H^{3}(X,\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2})\cong H^{2}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X})
.

By Lemma
3.2
, we may choose our three (1,1) divisors to be
G
-invariant. Since
X
is the complete intersection of these divisors, this means the conormal sheaf
\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}
decomposes into three
G
-invariant line bundles, say
\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{L}_{i}
each abstractly isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1,-1)
and each inheriting a linearisation determined by the
G
-action on
F_{i}=\mathbf{x}^{T}M_{i}\mathbf{y}
. In particular, on
\mathcal{L}_{i}
which corresponds to
F_{i}
, the element
\sigma
acts on a local generator with eigenvalue
\varpi^{s_{i}}
.

Let us consider the twisted Koszul resolution (
10
) with
a=b=-1
again. Since our divisors are invariant, it is clear that for all linearisations of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1,-1)
, we can linearise
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,r)
for
-4\leq r\leq-1
, so that the maps in (
10
) are equivariant. In particular, applying Lemma
3.5
, we find isomorphisms of
G
-representations

H^{3}(\mathcal{L}_{i})\cong H^{6}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-4,-4))

for each
i
. Let us compute the
G
-action on
H^{6}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-4,-4))
for each
i
.

Set
\eta_{x}:=(x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})^{-1}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\mathcal{O}(-4))
, and similarly with
\eta_{y}
. By the Künneth formula
[
13
,
Tag OBED
]
, a basis element of
H^{6}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-4,-4))
is given by
\pi^{*}(\eta_{x})\cup\pi^{\prime*}(\eta_{y})
. Our
\sigma
sends this element to

\varpi^{-\sum r_{i}}\pi^{*}(\eta_{y})\cup\pi^{\prime*}(\eta_{x})=(-1)^{3\times 3}\varpi^{-\sum r_{i}}\pi^{*}(\eta_{x})\cup\pi^{\prime*}(\eta_{y})=-\varpi^{-\sum r_{i}}\pi^{*}(\eta_{x})\cup\pi^{\prime*}(\eta_{y}).

However, since our
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-4,-4)
is obtained from a Koszul resolution, it has local generator
F_{1}\wedge F_{2}\wedge F_{3}
. Our
\sigma
acts on this local generator with eigenvalue
\varpi^{\sum s_{i}}
. However, the
\sigma
also acts on a local generator of
\mathcal{L}_{i}
with eigenvalue
\varpi^{s_{j}}
as explained above. Thus in conclusion
\sigma
acts on the one-dimensional
H^{6}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-4,-4))\cong H^{3}(\mathcal{L}_{i})
with eigenvalue
-\varpi^{s_{j}+(\sum_{i=1}^{3}s_{i})-(\sum_{i=0}^{3}r_{j})},
as asserted.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In fact, the same calculation shows that if
\sigma
does not swap

and

, then

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Firstly recall that
\Aut(X)
sits in an exact sequence

1\rightarrow\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{3},C)\rightarrow\Aut(X)\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2},

where the final map is surjective if and only if
\Aut(X)
contains an element which swaps
E
and
E^{\prime}
- see
[
3
, §1]
. Now suppose
X
is not
G
-Fano. Then
G
is contained in
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{3},C)\subseteq\Aut(X)
, and thus the blow-down
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
of
E
is
G
-equivariant.

We now suppose that
X
is
G
-Fano. Then by Lemma
3.1

G
contains an element
\sigma
which swaps
H
and
H^{\prime}
. It clearly suffices to assume
G=\langle\sigma\rangle
. Now suppose that
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a
G
-equivariant birational map, where
G
acts linearly on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. By
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.3]
(see also
[
10
, Prop. 3.6]
),
\varphi
factors equivariantly as:

\varphi=\varphi_{l}\circ\varphi_{l-1}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{1},

where for each
i
, either
\varphi_{i}
or
\varphi_{i}^{-1}
is a blow-up of a smooth
G
-invariant centre.

Since
X
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
along the curve
C
, then by
[
12
, Lemma 8.1.2.]
its intermediate Jacobian
\operatorname{IJ}_{X}
may be identified with the Jacobian
\mathrm{J}_{C}
of
C
. Thus since
X
has Picard rank two, then by the Torelli theorem and the uniqueness the decomposition of principally polarised abelian varieties into simple components, we have that one of the
\varphi_{i}
is a blowup along a
G
-invariant curve which is isomorphic to
C
. In particular, we have a
G
-action
\alpha_{1}\colon G\times C\rightarrow C
such that the isomorphism
\operatorname{IJ}_{X}\cong\J_{C}
is
G
-equivariant. Let us show this cannot be the case.

As explained in §
3.1
, the
G
-action on
X
induces a
G
-action
\alpha_{2}\colon G\times C\rightarrow C
on
C
. By Proposition
3.4
, the
G
-action on
\Lie(\operatorname{IJ}_{X})=\Lie(\J_{C})
induced by
\alpha_{1}
and the
G
-action induced by
\alpha_{2}
differ by a sign and are not isomorphic. But since
C
is a plane quartic, the action on
C
is determined by the action on
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(C,K_{C}))=\mathbb{P}(\Lie(\J_{C}))
. In particular,
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
must be the same group action, which is absurd, since their induced actions on
H^{0}(C,K_{C})
do not agree.
∎

Research on target recognition method for machine-plucked fresh tea leaves based on improved YOLOv11
Authors: Xinyong Shi, Wenguang Zheng, Rongyang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the process of multi-stage singulation, machine-plucked fresh tea leaves are arranged in a low-occlusion manner; however, challenges such as partial occlusion, difficulty in small-object detection, and the need for accurate recognition and classification remain in sorting. To address these issues, this study proposes an improved YOLOv11-based detection model, termed YOLOv11-MSC. The model incorporates three key enhancements—multi-scale edge information enhancement, convolution–attention fusion, and spatial attention optimization—to strengthen feature extraction and localization capabilities. To comprehensively evaluate its effectiveness, a series of experiments were conducted, including ablation studies, comparative analyses with different attention mechanisms, comparisons with mainstream detection models, and visual examinations using feature maps and heatmaps. Experimental results demonstrate that YOLOv11-MSC achieves a mean average precision of 90.1% (mAP@0.5) while maintaining a lightweight architecture with only 2.89 M parameters and 6.4 GFLOPs. These results demonstrate excellent performance in both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, indicating strong suitability for deployment in real-time tea-leaf grading applications.

Introduction

Tea is one of the most popular beverages worldwide. Beyond its refreshing taste, it has attracted sustained attention because bioactive components such as polyphenols and flavonoids provide antioxidant and cardiovascular protective effects1. With growing health awareness and improving living standards, the demand for high-end functional teas has risen rapidly2. Although premium tea accounts for less than 5% of total output, it contributes over 20% of the industry’s value, underscoring its importance in market competition and economic returns3.

Within the tea production chain, sorting fresh leaves is a crucial step for improving the quality and efficiency of machine-plucked tea and is fundamental to achieving full mechanization. Conventional mechanical sorting methods—such as chain-driven conveyor systems4, vibrating screens optimized via kinematics and response-surface analysis5, and screening structures designed with discrete-element modeling6—offer mature structures and high throughput. However, when handling pliable and irregular leaves, these devices often suffer from pore blockage, leaf entanglement, and limited grading precision, which cannot meet the requirements of high-accuracy sorting.

In recent years, advances in machine vision have opened new possibilities for tea processing. Early studies typically combined spectral imaging with traditional algorithms. For example, Li et al. classified multispectral images of famous teas using wavelet features and multi-class least-squares SVM, while Xie et al. employed hyperspectral imaging to track color and category changes during drying, enabling quantitative assessment of quality evolution7,8. Although such approaches are interpretable, they rely heavily on illumination conditions, acquisition settings, and preprocessing, making them difficult to deploy robustly in post-harvest production lines with continuous, multi-batch operation. With the development of deep learning, research has shifted toward end-to-end visual analysis. Chen et al. used convolutional neural networks to locate optimal plucking points for tea buds, improving accuracy by about 15%9. Qian et al. proposed an improved convolutional encoder–decoder network that achieved over 93% segmentation accuracy for tea buds10. Tang et al. applied LBP-based methods for tea classification with accuracy exceeding 90%11. Wang et al. developed TeaPoseNet to recognize tea-leaf poses accurately and extend capability to classification, grading, and maturity assessment, reaching an average precision above 91%12. Deep learning has also been adopted for quality and grade evaluation: a ResNet-50 model built on hyperspectral data achieved 92.31% accuracy with transfer learning and optimization strategies13, and an improved Inception network outperformed conventional CNNs in black-tea appearance grading14. Overall, these studies provide important algorithmic and system foundations, but most are aimed at tea-garden harvesting or single-object segmentation/classification, and remain insufficiently tailored to multi-target, fine-grained grading on post-harvest conveyor lines.

Meanwhile, YOLO-series object detectors, known for their balance of accuracy and speed, have been widely used in automated tea-related vision tasks, including disease detection15,16 and mountainous-area recognition and plucking17. YOLO-based detection has become a major focus in fresh-leaf classification and grading. Tang proposed an improved T-YOLOv8n integrating CBAM attention, BiFPN multi-scale fusion, and CIOU with Focal Loss, achieving 74.4% mAP in complex tea-garden environments18. Xia et al. enhanced YOLOv8n by embedding SPD-Conv, incorporating a Super-Token Vision Transformer, and adopting MPDIoU, producing recognition rates close to 90% across categories19. Wang et al. developed an improved TBC-YOLOv7 that fuses a Transformer, bidirectional feature pyramids, coordinate attention, and SIOU loss, increasing mAP by 3.4% to 87.5% while accelerating convergence20. Building on these advances, research has begun to extend toward post-harvest fresh-leaf grading. Zhao introduced an improved YOLOv8x (SPPCSPC+CBAM) for fresh-tea grade classification, improving accuracy by roughly 3–5% points over mainstream networks under both stacked and scattered-leaf conditions3. Yang and Zhang proposed automated tea plucking and sorting systems that combine machine vision and intelligent algorithms; using SVM, YOLOv3, and deep learning for bud recognition and localization, their systems drove Delta robotic arms for path planning and sorting, demonstrating high recognition accuracy and operational reliability21,22. Cao developed a YOLOv5-based multi-channel sorting system for fresh tea leaves, achieving 4.7 ms/frame recognition speed and an overall sorting accuracy of 85.4% with full-moisture training and SGD optimization23. These YOLO-series models and their variants have shown clear advantages in improving the accuracy, speed, and robustness of tea-leaf grading, and have become a core technical route for intelligent tea processing.

Although YOLO-based grading studies have increased in recent years and achieved progress in tea-bud detection and recognition under complex backgrounds, most validations are still conducted in tea-garden harvesting scenarios or experimental settings with stacked or scattered leaves. Consequently, the corresponding data acquisition and training conditions remain different from those of the online recognition stage in practical sorting equipment. To better align with the intelligent sorting workflow targeted in this study, we configured a multi-stage singulation conveyor on our experimental platform to preliminarily spread machine-plucked fresh leaves, so that the leaves enter the vision inspection station in a relatively low-occlusion conveying state. Image data were collected under stable illumination, thereby establishing a data basis that better matches the online recognition conditions of the equipment. Therefore, this work focuses on the practical application scenario of intelligent post-harvest sorting equipment for machine-plucked fresh tea leaves. Before entering the vision-based recognition stage, the collected fresh leaves have undergone mechanical screening, after which the proportion of non-tea impurities is very low. The screened material is then conveyed through multi-stage singulation and spreading, and enters the vision-based recognition and grading stage in a low-occlusion state. To address the small-object detection and fine-grained grading challenges of four picking-form fresh-leaf categories in this process, an improved detection framework is proposed based on the YOLOv11n baseline, with three main contributions. First, we introduce a Multi-Scale Edge Information Enhancement (MSEIE) module to strengthen the representation of bud–leaf edges and texture details, improving discriminability between visually similar grades. Second, we embed a Spatial Enhancement Attention Module (SEAM) into the detection head to refine spatial recalibration at the decision stage, thereby enhancing localization stability and classification robustness. Third, we incorporate a Channel Attention Fusion Module (CAFM24 to enable more effective channel-wise information selection and fusion, further improving feature representation quality. Experimental results demonstrate that, without substantially increasing model complexity, the proposed method markedly improves detection accuracy and grading reliability for fresh-leaf targets under post-harvest low-occlusion conditions, providing effective algorithmic support for high-precision online grading in intelligent sorting equipment.

Methods

Overall workflow of the proposed method

As shown in Fig. 1, our method follows an end-to-end workflow for post-harvest fresh tea leaf detection and grading. After the fresh leaves are arranged in a relatively low-occlusion manner for image capture, the collected images are used to construct the dataset, which is first split into training, validation, and test sets. Data augmentation is then applied only to the training set to improve generalization. An improved YOLOv11-based model (YOLOv11-MSC) is developed by integrating the proposed enhancement modules, followed by systematic experimental evaluation (including ablation and comparative studies) and final testing on the held-out test set to verify effectiveness.

Overall workflow of the proposed method.

YOLOv11 network model

YOLOv11 achieves a strong balance between detection accuracy and speed through efficient attention mechanisms, lightweight design, and dynamic feature fusion. Its network architecture is illustrated in the accompanying Fig. 2. The model includes several variants (YOLOv11n, YOLOv11s, YOLOv11m, YOLOv11x) optimized for different computational constraints. Guided by the No Free Lunch theorem, this study adopts YOLOv11n as the baseline for fresh tea-leaf detection to ensure real-time performance and mobile deployment feasibility.

The architecture of YOLOv11 consists of four components: the input layer, backbone, neck, and detection head25. The backbone integrates multi-stage convolutional downsampling with the lightweight C3k2 module, strengthening feature modeling while maintaining low complexity. A Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast (SPPF) module broadens the receptive field and aggregates multi-scale features, followed by a C2PSA module with channel attention to enhance sensitivity to key regions. Overall, the design achieves a compact yet effective structure that facilitates efficient multi-scale feature fusion, offering robust semantic support for detection in complex environments. These advances improve inference speed and deployment flexibility, making YOLOv11 particularly suitable for real-time edge-computing scenarios.

Architecture of the YOLOv11 network.

YOLOv11 network improvements

In this study, YOLOv11 was adopted as the baseline for detecting mechanically harvested fresh tea leaves and optimized to enhance feature representation, localization, and efficiency. Three modules were introduced: the Multi-Scale Edge Information Enhancement (MSEIE), embedded in the backbone to strengthen boundary features and high-frequency details; the Spatial Enhancement Attention Mechanism (SEAM), placed in the detection head to decouple classification and regression while applying Distribution Focal Loss (DFL) for precise localization; and the Channel Attention Fusion Module (CAFM), inserted between the backbone and detection head to fuse local convolutional and global semantic information. The resulting model, termed YOLOv11-MSC (MSEIE–SEAM–CAFM), achieves a lightweight yet accurate architecture suitable for intelligent tea-leaf detection systems (Fig. 3).

Multi-scale edge information enhancement (MSEIE) module

The Multi-Scale Edge Information Enhancement (MSEIE) module integrates multi-scale feature extraction, edge enhancement, and convolutional operations into a unified design. Its primary objective is to capture features at different receptive fields, emphasize edge information, and fuse them into a single enhanced representation for subsequent processing. This structure significantly improves the feature expression ability of the network. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the module consists of two main pathways: a primary convolutional stream and a set of parallel multi-scale branches.

Fig. 4Architecture of the MSEIE module.

Architecture of the MSEIE module.

The primary stream applies a standard 3 × 3 convolution to extract local structural information. In parallel, four adaptive average pooling branches with kernel sizes of 3 × 3, 6 × 6, 9 × 9 and 12 × 12 are employed to capture contextual information at different receptive fields. Each branch is processed by a 1 × 1 convolution for dimensionality adjustment, followed by a 3 × 3 convolution to refine local details. An edge-enhancement operation is then applied, which emphasizes high-frequency components by subtracting pooled background responses and modulating them with a sigmoid-based weighting scheme.

The outputs of all branches are upsampled to the original resolution and concatenated together with the main-path feature map. A final 1 × 1 convolution is applied for channel compression, yielding the fused output that integrates local detail, multi-scale context, and edge-sensitive information. Through this design, the MSEIE module significantly improves the quality of feature maps by enhancing structural details and boundary representation.

Convolution and attention fusion module (CAFM)

The Convolution and Attention Fusion Module (CAFM) used in this study follows the design proposed by Hu et al. (2024) in their work on hyperspectral image denoising24. We incorporate this module into our framework because it effectively integrates the complementary advantages of convolutional operations and attention mechanisms: convolution excels at modeling local details and textures, whereas attention is capable of capturing global dependencies and semantic relationships. For detecting fresh tea leaves in complex backgrounds, this joint modeling is especially advantageous, since the task requires both precise boundary information and context-aware semantic consistency.

As illustrated in Fig. 5, the CAFM consists of two parallel branches. The local branch is designed to capture fine structural details. It begins with a 1 × 1 convolution for channel adjustment, followed by a channel shuffle operation that mixes information across groups to promote cross-channel interactions. Subsequently, a 3 × 3 convolution further extracts spatial textures, ensuring that subtle morphological features of tea leaves are preserved. The global branch, in contrast, adopts a lightweight self-attention mechanism. Queries, keys, and values are generated by convolutional transformations, and their interactions produce an attention map that models long-range dependencies across channels. This branch complements the local pathway by embedding structural and semantic information that extends beyond the receptive field of convolution alone.

The outputs of the two branches are then merged in a residual manner, producing fused features that are both structurally complete and semantically coherent. In this way, CAFM enriches the intermediate feature maps with detail-level precision and global contextual awareness, ultimately improving the robustness of the detection network. Its inclusion in the improved YOLOv11 backbone enhances the model’s capacity to handle cluttered scenes and to accurately recognize small and ambiguous tea-leaf targets.

Network architecture of the convolution and attention fusion module.

Improved detection head design: Spatial enhancement attention module (SEAM)

The Spatial Enhancement Attention Module (SEAM) is incorporated into the detection head to strengthen spatially discriminative cues for both localization and classification, which is particularly important for post-harvest tea leaves that are small and morphologically similar. As shown in Fig. 6, the enhanced detection head is redesigned on top of the original dual-branch structure: a SEAM block is inserted into both the regression branch and the classification branch immediately before their final prediction layers (i.e., the pointwise convolution layers used to output box coordinates and class scores). Consequently, the improved head no longer relies solely on standard convolutions for feature processing; instead, it injects spatial attention into both pathways in a synchronized manner, ensuring that the features used for bounding-box regression and category classification are spatially enhanced, thereby improving the discriminative capacity of the head.

Fig. 6Comparison of the detection head before and after improvement.

Comparison of the detection head before and after improvement.

The internal workflow of SEAM is illustrated in Fig. 7. Given an input feature map, SEAM first performs a channel-wise spatial convolution to capture local spatial patterns, followed by a pointwise convolution to aggregate channel information and form spatial descriptors. These descriptors are then passed through a sigmoid activation to produce a single-channel spatial attention (SA) map that highlights informative locations. The SA map is multiplied element-wise with the input feature map to emphasize critical regions, and a residual addition is performed to preserve the original information flow, yielding an enhanced feature map. the enhanced map is fed into the regression and classification sub-branches, whose outputs are concatenated along the channel dimension to form the final head output. Therefore, by introducing spatial attention re-calibration prior to prediction in both branches, the SEAM-augmented head in Fig. 6 refines feature utilization at the decision stage and improves bounding-box regression accuracy and classification robustness under the post-harvest low-occlusion grading setting.

Fig. 7Schematic diagram of the SEAM module.

Schematic diagram of the SEAM module.

In essence, SEAM operates as a spatial attention refinement inserted at the head level, reinforcing fine-grained location cues that matter for grading. This targeted enhancement improves localization precision and mitigates inter-class confusion among small, visually similar tea leaves under post-harvest low-occlusion conditions.

Experiments and analysis results

Image acquisition

This study used Anji White Tea as the research subject, constructing a dataset from images of fresh tea leaves arranged in low-occlusion order after multi-stage singulation on a laboratory conveyor system. A Canon 6D II camera was used to record videos, from which frames were serialised and extracted to obtain 1,412 usable images of fresh tea leaves of different grades for the classification dataset. Figure 8 shows representative examples of the four post-harvest fresh-leaf morphology categories: one bud with one leaf, two leaves, three leaves, and multiple leaves.

Fig. 8Schematic illustration of four post-harvest fresh tea-leaf morphology classes.

Schematic illustration of four post-harvest fresh tea-leaf morphology classes.

Data selection and image annotation

Fresh tea leaf images were manually annotated with axis-aligned bounding boxes using LabelImg. Each instance was categorized into one of four picking forms—one bud with one leaf, one bud with two leaves, one bud with three leaves, and one bud with multiple leaves—corresponding to classes 0–3 (Table 1). The annotations were stored in PASCAL VOC (XML) format, with each file containing all labeled objects in an image, allowing for multiple instances per image. For training, the VOC annotations were converted into YOLO format through a Python script, generating one text file per image. Each line in the file specifies the object category along with the normalized bounding box parameters, represented by the center coordinates and the box dimensions (width and height), all scaled to the range [0,1]. Subsequently, the dataset was split into training, validation, and test sets at a ratio of 7:2:1.

Data augmentation

To further enhance the generalization ability of the dataset and mitigate overfitting, we performed data augmentation only on the training set after dataset partitioning. Specifically, the training set was expanded fourfold using rotation, mirroring, scaling, and adjustments of brightness, hue, contrast, and sharpness (Fig. 9), while the validation and test sets were kept unchanged to ensure an unbiased evaluation.

Experimental environment

The experimental platform in this study was based on a 64-bit Windows 10 operating system. The computational setup included an RTX 3090 GPU with 24 GB of VRAM, an Intel Xeon(R) Platinum 8362 CPU, driver version 550.67, and CUDA version 12.4.

Ablation experiments

YOLOv11 was adopted as the baseline, and MSEIE, CAFM, and SEAM were introduced in a progressive manner. Ablation experiments were performed to assess the contribution of each module as well as their combinations (Table 2).

The results indicate that all three modules bring performance gains. Incorporating MSEIE alone increases mAP@0.5 from 87.0% to 87.8%, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing edge and texture information. Adding CAFM yields 88.2% mAP@0.5, with precision and recall reaching 83.9% and 82.4%, respectively, confirming its benefit for fine-grained discrimination. SEAM mainly enhances spatial representation within the detection head, achieving 87.9% mAP@0.5 without increasing parameters. When SEAM is combined with CAFM, mAP@0.5 further improves to 88.0%, showing that spatial enhancement and channel fusion are complementary. In contrast, the MSEIE+SEAM pair attains 87.7% mAP@0.5, indicating a modest gain over the baseline but less improvement than CAFM-related configurations, suggesting that edge enhancement alone is not sufficient to fully resolve fine-grained inter-class ambiguity. Combining MSEIE and CAFM produces 88.8% mAP@0.5, and integrating all three modules delivers the best overall performance, reaching 90.1% mAP@0.5 with 86.4% precision and 82.3% recall. Importantly, the final model maintains a lightweight profile, with only a slight increase in complexity (2.89 M parameters and 6.4 G FLOPs), remaining suitable for practical deployment.

Overall, MSEIE, CAFM, and SEAM each contribute to improved accuracy and stability, and their joint integration exhibits strong synergy, validating the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed strategy.

The experimental results demonstrate that YOLOv11-MSC achieves the highest detection accuracy among all compared models, reaching an mAP@0.5 of 90.1%. This represents improvements of 3.6%, 3.8%, and 3.2% over YOLOv5, YOLOv7-tiny, and YOLOv9t, respectively. It also significantly outperforms classical detectors such as Faster R-CNN (85.7%) and SSD (84.3%). Beyond accuracy, YOLOv11-MSC exhibits strong computational efficiency with only 6.4 GFLOPs and 2.89 million parameters—substantially lower than Faster R-CNN (370.3 GFLOPs, 13.7 M) and SSD (62.7 GFLOPs, 26.4 M). In summary, YOLOv11-MSC achieves an optimal trade-off across accuracy, efficiency, and model size, validating the effectiveness of the proposed architectural improvements. It is particularly well-suited for deployment in edge computing scenarios requiring high precision and low latency.

Feature visualization and heatmap comparison

To further evaluate the model’s ability to capture structural and semantic features of fresh tea leaf targets, feature map visualizations were conducted for both the original YOLOv11 and the improved YOLOv11-MSC models. As shown in Fig. 11, the upper panels correspond to YOLOv11-MSC and the lower panels to YOLOv11.

With increasing network depth, the extracted features transition from local textures to higher-level semantic structures. Shallow layers focus on edge details and core areas, while deeper layers capture global shapes and semantic boundaries. The results indicate that YOLOv11-MSC produces more focused activations around critical bud–leaf structures and offers broader semantic coverage. In contrast, YOLOv11 exhibits scattered responses in some cases, resulting in weaker boundary delineation and reduced discriminative performance.

Conclusion

This study proposes an improved YOLOv11-based method for the recognition of mechanically harvested fresh tea leaves. The method enables fast and accurate classification of tea leaf targets arranged in a low-occlusion layout after multi-stage single-line transmission. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) The proposed Multi-Scale Edge Information Enhancement (MSEIE) module effectively strengthens the representation of boundary and detail features, providing more reliable support for small-object detection.

(2) The Convolution–Attention Fusion Module (CAFM) enhances the perception of texture and semantic information while maintaining a lightweight architecture, significantly improving the recognition of small targets.

(3) The Spatial Enhancement Attention Module (SEAM) increases the detection head’s sensitivity to critical spatial regions, thereby improving bounding box localization accuracy and classification stability.

(4) Experimental results demonstrate that the improved YOLOv11-MSC model achieved an mAP@0.5 of 90.1% on the low-occlusion fresh tea-leaf dataset, with precision and recall reaching 86.4% and 80.2%, respectively, while requiring only 2.78 M parameters and 6.1 GFLOPs. These results highlight the model’s favorable balance between detection performance and computational efficiency.

In future work, this study will be further extended in the following two directions:

(1) The current research focuses on a representative type of machine-plucked tea leaves. Future studies will expand to different tea varieties with diverse morphological characteristics, and corresponding adaptations in dataset construction and model design will be explored to improve the generalization and applicability of the proposed sorting equipment across multiple tea types.

(2) Building upon the validated algorithm and model improvements, the next stage of this research will involve practical deployment of the proposed method, completing a necessary step toward the realization of intelligent tea-leaf sorting equipment in real-world applications.

A Cautionary Note on Quantum Oracles
Avantika Agarwal, Srijita Kundu

Abstract
In recent years, the quantum oracle model introduced by Aaronson and Kuperberg (2007) has found a lot of use in showing oracle separations between complexity classes and cryptographic primitives. It is generally assumed that proof techniques that do not relativize with respect to quantum oracles will also not relativize with respect to classical oracles. In this note, we show that this is not the case: specifically, we show that there is a quantum oracle problem that is contained in the class 𝖰𝖬𝖠 \mathsf{QMA} , but not in a class we call 𝗉𝗈𝗅𝗒𝖰𝖢𝖯𝖧 \mathsf{polyQCPH} . The class 𝗉𝗈𝗅𝗒𝖰𝖢𝖯𝖧 \mathsf{polyQCPH} is equal to 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} with respect to classical oracles, and it is a well-known result that 𝖰𝖬𝖠 \mathsf{QMA} is contained in 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} (also with respect to classical oracles). We also show that the same separation holds relative to a distributional oracle, which is a model introduced by Natarajan and Nirkhe (2024). We believe our findings show the need for some caution when using these non-standard oracle models, particularly when showing separations between quantum and classical resources.



1
Introduction

In this note, we discuss the problem of relativization with respect to quantum and distributional oracles. In recent years, there have been multiple results in quantum complexity theory and quantum cryptography which show oracle separations with respect to quantum or distributional oracles. With the goal of constructing an oracle separation between the complexity classes
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
,
[
AK07
]
introduce quantum oracles and show a quantum oracle separation between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
. Separations between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
with slightly different quantum oracles are also shown in
[
FK18
,
BFM23
]
. More recently,
[
AHHN24
]
show a quantum oracle separation between the complexity classes
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{uniqueQMA}
.
[
NZ24
,
CKP24
]
study the cloneability and uncloneability of quantum proof states relative to quantum oracles.

In quantum cryptography,
[
Kre21
]
constructs a quantum oracle relative to which
\mathsf{BQP}=\mathsf{QMA}
but cryptographic pseudorandom quantum states exists. More recently,
[
GZ25
]
show conditions under which cryptographic separations in the Common Haar Random State Model can be lifted to quantum unitary oracle separations. See
[
CM24
]
and
[
GMMY24
]
for some other quantum oracle separations in quantum cryptography. While the quantum oracle considered in
[
AK07
]
is a unitary oracle, some works such as
[
GMMY24
]
have considered the even more general setting of CPTP maps as oracles.

On the other hand, in order to make progress towards a classical oracle separation between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
,
[
NN24
]
introduced the distributional oracle model and showed a separation in this model. Later,
[
LLPY24
]
construct a different distributional oracle separating
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
.

One of the motivations for showing oracle separations between complexity classes is the following: if two classes can be separated in the relativized setting, then a relativizing proof technique certainly cannot prove they are equal. Indeed, in
[
AK07
]
, the authors state the following: “Currently, we do not know of any quantumly non-relativizing techniques that are not also classically non-relativizing.” In this note, we show that there is such a relativizing technique: there is a complexity class containment that does not relativize with a quantum oracle, but does relativize with a classical oracle. We also show similar results hold for the distributional oracle setting.

1.1
Our observations

Quantum oracles.

We show that there is a quantum oracle relative to which
\mathsf{QMA}
is not contained in a class we call
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
. The class
\mathsf{QCPH}
(introduced in
[
GSSSY22
]
) informally consists of decision problems that can be solved by an efficient quantum verifier with constantly many alternating classical proofs, see Section
2.1
for a formal definition.
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
is defined similar to
\mathsf{QCPH}
, except the number of alternating classical proofs is polynomial (see Section
2.1
for formal definitions of these classes). In the unrelativized world, and with respect to classical oracles,
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
is equal to
\mathsf{PSPACE}
(this is due to the
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-completeness of the
\mathsf{TQBF}
problem), and it is a well-known result that
\mathsf{QMA}
is contained in
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. Therefore, the containment
\mathsf{QMA}\subseteq\mathsf{polyQCPH}
does not relativize quantumly, but does relativize classically.

It does not make sense to talk about
\mathsf{PSPACE}
relative to quantum oracles, because
\mathsf{PSPACE}
machines cannot access arbitrary quantum oracles. Instead we can talk about the class
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
, which captures polynomial-space quantum computation with bounded error. In the unrelativized world,
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
is equal to
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. With respect to quantum oracles, the containment
\mathsf{polyQCPH}

\subseteq\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
still holds, so we must have
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}\not\subseteq\mathsf{polyQCPH}
in this setting (although note that
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
may not be contained in
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
with respect to classical oracles either;
\mathsf{PSPACE}\subseteq\mathsf{polyQCPH}
holds for classical oracles, but
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}=\mathsf{PSPACE}
seems not to).

The quantum oracle with which we show
\mathsf{QMA}\not\subseteq\mathsf{polyQCPH}
is the same one used in
[
AK07
]
to separate
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
. We think this demonstrates that this oracle is powerful when separating quantum and classical resources in particular, and quantum oracles in general must be used with caution when attempting such separations.

Distributional oracles.

We show that there is a distributional oracle relative to which
\mathsf{QMA}
is not contained in
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
. In particular, for classical oracles,
\mathsf{PSPACE}=\mathsf{polyQCPH}
but this is not the case for distributional oracles. The distributional oracle with which we show
\mathsf{QMA}\not\subseteq\mathsf{polyQCPH}
is the same one used in
[
LLPY24
]
to separate
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
. We think therefore that distributional oracles must also be used with caution when attempting separations.

2.1
Complexity Classes

2.1
True Quantified Boolean Formula (
\mathsf{TQBF}
).

The language
\mathsf{TQBF}
is defined as the set of quantified Boolean formula which are true, where a quantified Boolean formula
\psi
is the following:

\displaystyle\psi=Q_{1}x_{1}Q_{2}x_{2}\ldots Q_{n}x_{n}\phi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n})

where
Q_{i}
are the quantifiers
\exists
or
\forall
, and
\phi
is a Boolean formula of size
m
. The size of
\psi
is then defined to be
n+m
.

2.2
Quantum Turing Machine
[
Wat09
]
.

A quantum Turing machine (QTM) consists of three tapes: a read-only input tape, a classical work tape and a quantum work tape with qubits initialized to the
\lvert 0\rangle
state. It has five tape heads, one each for the input and classical work tape, and three for the quantum tape. In every step of the computation, the Turing machine can read an input bit or perform one step on the classical tape, or apply one quantum gate (from Toffoli, Hadamard or phase-shift gates) on the quantum tape or perform a single-qubit measurement on the quantum tape.

The runtime of the QTM is the number of steps taken by the machine, and the space used by the QTM is the number of squares used on the classical work tape plus the number of qubits used on the quantum work tape.

2.3
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}

[
Wat09
]
.

A promise problem
L=(L_{yes},L_{no})
is in
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
if there is a quantum Turing machine

) and a polynomial

such that

decides

using

\displaystyle x\in L_{yes}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Pr[T(x)=1]\geq\frac{2}{3}

\displaystyle x\in L_{no}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Pr[T(x)=1]\leq\frac{1}{3}

The classical analogue of

where the Turing machines described above do not have a quantum tape. It follows from definition that

for every classical and distributional oracle. It is known that
\mathsf{PSPACE}=\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
(shown in
[
Wat03
]
). However, the proof of this result might not relativize for classical oracles according to our understanding.

2.4
\mathsf{PQP}
.

A promise problem

is in

\displaystyle x\in L_{yes}

It is known (shown in

2.8

\displaystyle\mathsf{QCPH}=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\;\mathsf{QC\Sigma}_{i}=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\;\mathsf{QC\Pi}_{i}.

[

\mathsf{QCPH}

If we do allow the number of proofs to grow, say, polynomially with the length of the input, then we get a different complexity class, which we will call

3.1
Theorem 4.11 of
[
AB09
]
.

The problem
\mathsf{TQBF}
is complete for
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. This holds relative to all classical oracles
O
.

Proof.

Consider
L\in\mathsf{PSPACE}
and let
M
be the corresponding Turing Machine. Let
G_{M,x}
be the configuration graph of
M
on input
x
. Then
M
accepts
x
iff there is a path from
C_{init}
to
C_{acc}
in the configuration graph
G_{M,x}
. Suppose
M
uses space
m=p(|x|)
on input
x
, where
p:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a polynomial. We will convert this problem of finding a path between
C_{init}
and
C_{acc}
to a quantified Boolean formula

which is true iff such a path exists. This construction is done inductively, and we define
\psi_{i}(C_{1},C_{2})
to be the quantified Boolean formula which is true iff there is a path of length at most

between vertices

and
C_{2}
of

. Then we want to set
\psi=\psi_{m}(C_{init},C_{acc})
. We know that

can be written as a Boolean formula of size

, using a construction similar to Cook-Levin theorem. Then we can define

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(C_{1},C_{2}):=\exists C\forall D_{1}\forall D_{2}[(D_{1}=C_{1}\wedge D_{2}=C)\vee(D_{1}=C\wedge D_{2}=C_{2})]\Rightarrow\psi_{i-1}(D_{1},D_{2})

The formula above encodes the fact that there is a path of length at most
2^{i}
between
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
iff there is a vertex
C
such that there is a path of length at most

between
C_{1}
and
C
and between

and
C_{2}
. Then the formula

has size

. We can also check that each of these steps will work in the presence of an arbitrary classical oracle, where note it is important that the quantified variables

and the Boolean formulas

are allowed to depend on the oracle

.
∎

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Pr[W^{U}(x)=1]\geq\frac{1}{2^{p(n)}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{r(n)}}\right)

\displaystyle x\in L_{no}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\Pr[W^{U}(x)=1]\leq\frac{1}{2^{r(n)}}

Thus
W^{U}
is a
\mathsf{PQP}^{U}

L
for the given choice of

. Therefore,
L\in\mathsf{PQP}^{U}
.
∎

4.2
.

For every quantum oracle
U
,
\mathsf{PQP}^{U}\subseteq\mathsf{BQPSPACE}^{U}
.

Proof.

Let
L\in\mathsf{PQP}^{U}
and
V^{U}
be the corresponding verifier. Suppose the verifier
V^{U}
accepts with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{p(n)}}
on an input

n

and with probability at most
\frac{1}{2}
otherwise. Then define a verifier

which on input

independently for

and accepts iff the fraction of accepting iterations is at least
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{p(n)}}-\frac{1}{2^{2p(n)}}
. Using Chernoff bound (Proposition

W^{U}
makes an error with probability at most

is a sufficiently large constant. Further,

can be run in polynomial space, since it can reuse the same qubits for each iteration of running

(which takes polynomially many qubits) and store a counter for the number of accepting iterations on the classical tape, which take

∎

\mathsf{TQBF}
completeness fails for quantum oracles.

In the classical setting, it is known that if we allow the number of alternating proofs in
\mathsf{PH}
to grow polynomially with the length of the input, then we get the same class as
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. This follows from Lemma
3.1
. However, in the case of quantum oracles,
\mathsf{PSPACE}
and
\mathsf{TQBF}
do not make sense anymore (note that
\mathsf{TQBF}
may not be complete for
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
even for classical oracles). If we try to do a similar proof as the classical one, but now for
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
and
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
, it also does not seem to work. In particular, suppose the classical alternatively quantified proofs are supposed to capture a path in the configuration graph of the classical tape of a
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
machine
Q
. Since
Q
is a
\mathsf{BQPSPACE}
machine, it can potentially run for an exponential amount of time, provided it does not use more than polynomial space. Therefore, we can’t defer measurements in

by adding ancilla qubits. In particular, the future steps of the classical tape can depend on these intermediate measurement outcomes. Therefore, a
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
predicate may not capture this possibility, since all the quantum measurements in
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
happen after all the classical proofs have been specified. Therefore, while
\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{U}\subseteq\mathsf{BQPSPACE}^{U}
, the reverse containment does not seem to hold.

4.3
Discussion

It’s important to understand why a strategy like in the proof of Theorem
4.4
would not work for classical oracles, and we now provide some discussion on this. In the
[
AK07
]
quantum oracle problem, there is a single no oracle
U=I
. Therefore for a given algorithm
A
, and any yes instance
U_{\psi}
, we can consider a witness
w
which maximizes the distinguishing advantage of
A
between
U_{\psi}
and
I
. This can then be used to define the set
S(w)
for every witness
w
and it allows us to pick
w^{\ast}
, such that
S(w^{\ast})
has large number of yes instances. We can now pick a yes instance from
S(w^{\ast})
, and no other witness can do better than
w^{\ast}
to distinguish from the single no instance. If there were multiple no instances, then for any yes instance, there will be different witnesses
w
maximizing the distinguishing advantage for different no instances. In particular, it is possible that any given choice of proofs
y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)}
maximizes the distinguishing advantage for
some
no instance. It’s not clear how to define the set
S(w)
and consider
S(w^{\ast})
under these circumstances.

We believe it’s simply not possible to get a classical oracle problem with a single no instance for which a statement such as Theorem
4.3
holds. Indeed, this can be verified to not be true for the candidate classical oracle separations between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
in
[
LLPY24
,
BK24
,
Zha25
,
LMY24
]
. All of these have multiple no instances. For these oracles, one could try to pick a
w
which maximizes the distinguishing advantage between the yes instance and some fixed no instance. We now discuss why this way of picking
S(w)
cannot be combined with the proof techniques of
[
LLPY24
,
BK24
,
Zha25
,
LMY24
]
to give a separation between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{polyQCPH}
(or even
\mathsf{QCPH}
— indeed we believe all of these problems are contained in
\mathsf{PH}
).
1
1

1

Note that none of these works prove a full unconditional separation between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
with a classical oracle. However, unlike the
\mathsf{QMA}
vs
\mathsf{QCMA}
candidate of
[

In all of these works, after fixing a witness

One could instead consider a different way of defining

5
Distributional Oracles

The distributional oracle model was introduced by
[
NN24
]
, with the aim of making progress towards showing a classical oracle separation between
\mathsf{QMA}
and
\mathsf{QCMA}
. In this model, there is a family of yes distributions
\{D_{Y}\}_{Y}
and a family of no distributions
\{D_{N}\}
. Each distribution
D_{Y}
(or
D_{N}
) has support on, say,
M
elements. The oracle
O
is sampled from one of these families of distributions and the aim of an algorithm is to tell which family of distributions the oracle is sampled from, potentially with the help of a prover who can send a classical witness. The crucial difference from classical oracle setup is that the prover only knows the distribution
D_{Y}
(or
D_{N}
) from which
O
is sampled, and not the explicit sample
O
. After the sampling of
O
, the
i\in[M]
which tells which element in the support is picked is provided to the verifier. In particular, the prover does not know
i
, so the proof it sends cannot depend on
i
. Note that the number
[M]
has to be small, otherwise the verifier cannot read
i
.

5.1
\mathsf{QMA}
is contained in
\mathsf{PSPACE}

In this section, we show that for every distributional oracle
D
,
\mathsf{QMA}^{D}
is contained in
\mathsf{PSPACE}^{D}
. We begin by noting that
\mathsf{QMA}^{D}\subseteq\mathsf{PQP}^{D}
for every distributional oracle
D
, and this proof is the same as Lemma
4.1
, with the quantum oracle replaced by a distributional oracle. We now show that
\mathsf{PQP}^{D}\subseteq\mathsf{PSPACE}^{D}
for every distributional oracle
D
. This is essentially the same argument as
[
NC16
]
.

5.1
.

For every distributional oracle
D
,
\mathsf{PQP}^{D}\subseteq\mathsf{PSPACE}^{D}
. The same holds true for all classical oracles
O
.

Proof.

Let
L\in\mathsf{PQP}^{D}
and
V^{D}
be the corresponding verifier. We assume that
V^{D}
makes all it measurements at the end of the computation, and on an input
x
of length
n
, it stops in time
p(n)
, where
p:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a polynomial. Then we can think of the action of
V^{D}
on the input
x
as follows:

\displaystyle V^{D}(x)=V_{p(n)}O_{D}V_{p(n)-1}\ldots O_{D}V_{1}\lvert x0^{q(n)}\rangle

where
q(n)
is the number of ancilla qubits used by
V^{D}
. Then we define a classical Turing machine
M^{D}
which computes the probability that the output of measuring
V^{D}(x)
is
y
. This is done by inserting
I=\sum_{z}\lvert z\rangle\langle z\rvert
between every
V_{i}
and
O_{D}
, and then computing the following sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots z_{2p(n)-2}}\langle y\rvert V_{p(n)}\lvert z_{2p(n)-2}\rangle\langle z_{2p(n)-2}\rvert O_{D}\lvert z_{2p(n)-3}\rangle\ldots\langle z_{2}\rvert O_{D}\lvert z_{1}\rangle\langle z_{1}\rvert V_{1}\lvert x0^{q(n)}\rangle

This sum can be computed in polynomial space, since
M
only needs to store a partial sum and
2p(n)-1
values for computing the products in each sum. Note that to compute these products,
M
only needs to make classical queries to
O_{D}
because
z_{i}
are classical strings. Hence
L\in\mathsf{PSPACE}^{D}
. Note that the proof also works if
D
is a classical oracle.
∎

\mathsf{TQBF}
completeness fails for distributional oracles.

In the classical oracle model, it is known that if we allow the number of alternating proofs in
\mathsf{PH}
to grow polynomially with the length of the input, then we get the same class as
\mathsf{PSPACE}
. This follows from Lemma
3.1
. However, in the case of distributional oracles, the proof from Lemma
3.1
does not work. In particular, consider
\psi_{i}
from Lemma
3.1
which we defined as follows:

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(C_{1},C_{2}):=\exists C\forall D_{1}\forall D_{2}[(D_{1}=C_{1}\wedge D_{2}=C)\vee(D_{1}=C\wedge D_{2}=C_{2})]\Rightarrow\psi_{i-1}(D_{1},D_{2})

Recall, that the existentially quantified proof
C
is the vertex in the configuration graph which has a path of length at most
2^{i-1}
to both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Therefore,
C
will be dependent on the oracle, because the oracle responses determine whether there is an edge between two vertices of the configuration graph. If the proof
C
is only allowed to depend on the distribution that the oracle is sampled from, that may not be sufficient to specify the required vertex in the configuration graph.

\mathsf{QMA}

\mathsf{polyQCPH}

\mathsf{polyQCPH}

\mathsf{polyQCPH}
. We first describe the oracle problem.

[
LLPY24
]
Distributional Oracle Problem.

Fix a code

\Sigma=\{0,1\}^{\Theta(n)}
. This is the code
C
defined in
[
YZ24
]
, the properties of which are used in the proof of Lemma
5.2
below, but we do not state these properties. Given a function
H:[n]\times\Sigma\rightarrow\{0,1\}
, define a function
f_{C}^{H}:C\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle f_{C}^{H}(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})=H(1,v_{1})||H(2,v_{2})||\ldots||H(n,v_{n})

We can use this function
f^{H}_{C}
to define a relation
R_{H}\subseteq\Sigma^{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}
where
(v,r)\in R_{H}\Leftrightarrow f_{C}^{H}(v)=r
(this is the same
R_{H}
as in the
[
LLPY24
,
BK24
]

). Then the oracle
O_{n}^{b}[H_{n},r_{n}]
is defined as follows:

•

(b = 1):
O_{n}^{1}[H_{n},r_{n}]
takes
v\in\Sigma^{n}
as input and outputs
1
if
(v_{n},r_{n})\in R_{H}
and
0
otherwise.

•

(b = 0):
O_{n}^{0}[H_{n},r_{n}]
takes
v\in\Sigma^{n}
as input and always outputs

.

The final distribution on the oracles is
\{(O_{n}^{b_{n}}[H_{n},r_{n}],r_{n})\}_{n}
. Here the functions

parametrize the yes and no distributions, so the prover will know

. The support of the distribution is parametrized by the string

, since this determines the particular values the oracle takes. The strings

then correspond to
i\in[M]
and are sampled uniformly at random. The verifier knows

, but the prover does not.

Lemma 6.1 of
[
LLPY24
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
be the set of functions
H:[n]\times\Sigma\rightarrow\{0,1\}
, where
\Sigma=\{0,1\}^{\Theta(n)}
. Then there exists a family of oracles
\{O_{n}^{b}[H,r]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},b\in\{0,1\},H\in\mathcal{H}_{n},r\in\{0,1\}^{n}}
such that for the uniform distribution
\mathcal{D}_{n}
over
\mathcal{H}_{n}\times\{0,1\}^{n}
:

1.

There exist a quantum polynomial-time algorithm
\mathcal{A}
and quantum witness
\{\lvert z_{H}\rangle\}_{H}
of polynomial size such that

\displaystyle\Pr_{(H,r)\sim\mathcal{D}_{n},b\sim\{0,1\}}[\mathcal{A}^{O_{n}^{b}[H,r]}(\lvert z_{H}\rangle,r)=b]\geq 1-\mathsf{negl}(n)

2.

For any quantum polynomial-time algorithm
\mathcal{B}
, polynomial
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and for any
s(n)
-bit classical witness
\{z_{H}\}_{H}

\displaystyle\left|\Pr_{(H,r)\sim\mathcal{D}_{n}}[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{1}[H,r]}(z_{H},r)=1]-\Pr_{(H,r)\sim\mathcal{D}_{n}}[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{0}[H,r]}(z_{H},r)=1]\right|\leq\mathsf{negl}(n)

5.3
.

There is a distributional oracle
O
relative to which
\mathsf{QMA}^{O}\not\subset\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{O}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a unary language chosen uniformly at random. For every
n
, set
b_{n}=1
iff
1^{n}\in L
. Then the oracle
O=\{O_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is sampled as
O_{n}:=(O_{n}^{b_{n}}[H_{n},r_{n}],r_{n})
where
(H_{n},r_{n})\sim\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Then
[
LLPY24
]
show that
L\in\mathsf{QMA}^{O}
with probability
1
over the choice of
\{(b_{n},H_{n},r_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Eventually the oracle distribution fixes
H_{n}
for every
n
, and so the quantum and classical witnesses for
\mathsf{QMA}^{O}
and
\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{O}
verifiers are allowed to depend on
H_{n}
; however, the distribution over
r_{n}
remains, so the verifiers are now allowed to depend on
r_{n}
.

We now show that
L\notin\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{O}
with probability
1
over the choice of
\{(b_{n},H_{n},r_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Fix a verifier
V^{O}
which takes
q(n)
alternatively quantified proofs of length
q(n)
on inputs of length
n
, where
q:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a polynomial. Let
S_{V}(n)
be the event that
V^{O}
succeeds on
1^{n}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{V}(n)=[\exists y_{1}\forall y_{2}\ldots Q_{q(n)}y_{q(n)}\Pr[V^{O}(1^{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)})=1|b_{n}=1]\geq 2/3]

\displaystyle\vee[\forall y_{1}\exists y_{2}\ldots\overline{Q}_{q(n)}y_{q(n)}\Pr[V^{O}(1^{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)})=1|b_{n}=0]\leq 1/3]

Then consider an algorithm
\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{b_{n}}[H_{n},r_{n}]}
which has
H_{i},b_{i}
hardcoded in it for
i\neq n
. It randomly samples
r_{i}
for
i\neq n
, gets
r_{n}
as input and simulates the behaviour of
V^{O}
. In particular, for every query that
V^{O}
makes
O_{i}
such that
i\neq n
,
\mathcal{B}
uses its hardcoded value of
H_{i},b_{i}
and the sampled value of
r_{i}
to generate the query response. If
V^{O}
queries a bit of
O_{n}
, then
\mathcal{B}
queries its own oracle, and if
V^{O}
queries a bit of
r_{n}
, then
\mathcal{B}
uses its input value of
r_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\Pr_{H_{n},r_{n}}[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{b_{n}}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)},r_{n})=1]=\Pr_{\{r_{i}\}_{i},H_{n}}[V^{O}(1^{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)})=1|\{H_{i},b_{i}\}_{i\neq n},b_{n}]

Hence, we have that
\Pr_{\{(b_{i},H_{i},r_{i})\}_{i}}[S_{V}(n)]=\frac{1}{2}(\Pr[E_{1}]+\Pr[E_{2}])
where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are the following events (note that the witnesses
y_{i}
can depend on
H_{n}
, but not on
r_{n}
):

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\exists y_{1}\forall y_{2}\ldots Q_{q(n)}y_{q(n)}\Pr[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{1}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)},r_{n})=1]\geq 2/3

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\forall y_{1}\exists y_{2}\ldots\overline{Q}_{q(n)}y_{q(n)}\Pr[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{0}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{q(n)},r_{n})=1]\leq 1/3

We know from Lemma
5.2
that for all polynomial sized witnesses
w_{H_{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\Pr_{H_{n},r_{n}}[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{1}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]-\Pr_{H_{n},r_{n}}[\mathcal{A}^{O_{n}^{0}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]\right|\leq\mathsf{negl}(n)

On applying Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\Pr_{H_{n},r_{n}}\left[\left|\Pr[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{1}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]-\Pr[\mathcal{A}^{O_{n}^{0}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]\right|+1\geq\frac{5}{4}\right]\leq\frac{4}{5}(1+\mathsf{negl}(n))

Therefore, for at least
1-\frac{4}{5}(1+\mathsf{negl}(n))
fraction of
(H_{n},r_{n})
:

\displaystyle\left|\Pr[\mathcal{B}^{O_{n}^{1}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]-\Pr[\mathcal{A}^{O_{n}^{0}[H_{n},r_{n}]}(w_{H_{n}},r_{n})=1]\right|<\frac{1}{4}

Therefore, for any such choice of
(H_{n},r_{n})
, if
E_{1}
occurs then
E_{2}
does not occur. So
\Pr[E_{1}\wedge E_{2}]\leq\frac{4}{5}(1+\mathsf{negl}(n))
. Thus

\displaystyle\Pr[S_{V}(n)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\Pr[E_{1}]+\Pr[E_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\Pr[E_{1}\wedge\neg E_{2}]+\Pr[E_{1}\wedge E_{2}]+\Pr[E_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\Pr[\neg E_{2}]+\frac{4}{5}(1+\mathsf{negl}(n))+\Pr[E_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{11}{12}

Then standard arguments can be used (as also done in
[
LLPY24
]
) to show that

\displaystyle\Pr_{\{(b_{i},H_{i},r_{i})\}_{i}}[\exists V\bigwedge_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{V}(n)]=0

So
L\notin\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{O}
with probability
1
over the choice of
\{b_{n},H_{n},r_{n}\}_{n}
. By fixing
H_{n},b_{n}
for every
n
, we get a language
L
such that
L\in\mathsf{QMA}^{O}
and
L\notin\mathsf{polyQCPH}^{O}
, where
O
is now the uniformly random distribution over
\{r_{n}\}_{n}
.
∎

Discussion.

The above proof works due to item 2 of Lemma
5.2
. We suspect a statement such as item 2 can only hold for distribution oracles. Indeed, it can be verified for this specific oracle that when the classical proofs
z
are allowed to depend on
r
as well as
H
, this statement no longer holds. Since in a standard classical oracle, the proofs would depend on
z
as well, this proof technique would thus not work for classical oracles.

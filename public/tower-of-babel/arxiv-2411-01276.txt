. 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we introduce a new higher-order operator in the framework of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces that generalizes the well-known biharmonic Laplacian:

\Delta^{2}u=\Delta(\Delta u).

Given
\Omega
a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we formally define the biharmonic
g
-Laplacian as

\Delta_{g}^{2}u=\Delta\left(\dfrac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right),

where
g=G^{\prime}
, with
G
an N-function, that is,
G:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is even and is given by

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds,

where
g
is odd, continuous, non-decreasing,
g(0)=0
,
g(t)>0
for
t>0
, and
g(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.

In this article, we will discuss basic properties of
\Delta_{g}^{2}
related to the solvability and uniqueness of elliptic problems driven by this operator. Among them, we shall prove existence and continuity of the derivative of the energy functional
L
associated to
\Delta_{g}^{2}
. Monotonicity properties, such as the
(S_{+})
-condition, together with the continuity of the inverse of the derivative
L^{\prime}
will also be established. As it is well-known in the literature, this list of properties may be applied to study several problems related to
\Delta_{g}^{2}
.

Moreover, we shall also consider the existence of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions and their relation to variational problems. As it is known in this non-standard framework (see for instance
[
10
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
23
]
), eigenvalues are not variational, that is, they may not be characterized by taking infimun of Rayleigh quotients. This is due in general to the lack of homogeneity of
N
-functions and, as a particular feature of eigenvalue problems in Orlicz spaces, the solvability of the eigenvalue problems depends on normalization conditions. To illustrate this observation, in the case of the
g
-Laplacian operator, it has been proved in
[
18
]
that the constrained minimization problem

\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}G(|\nabla u|)\,dx,\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx=r\right\}

has a solution
u_{r}\neq 0
which is a weak solution of

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u=\lambda_{r}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}u,\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
g=G^{\prime}
, for some
\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
. We point out that the eigenvalues depend on the normalization condition

\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx=r.

Observe that when
G(t)=|t|^{p}
,
p>1
, problem (
1.1
) becomes the more familiar eigenvalue problem for the
p
-Laplacian:

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=0\qquad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Delta_{p}u:=div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
. See for instance
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, and the references therein.

In
[
21
]
, the author proved the existence of an infinite sequence of eigenvalues for (
1.1
) tending to infinity without any extra condition on
G
(no
\Delta_{2}
-condition is assumed). There, the classical Ljusternik-Schnirelmann theory is not available due to nonreflexivity of the underlying Orlicz-Sobolev space. Applications of Mathematical Programming to eigenvalue problems in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces can be seen in
[
11
]
. More recently, the isolation of the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
for the
g
-Laplacian has been considered in
[
17
]
(see also
[
23
]
for further properties of the eigenfunctions).

In this setting, our contributions are the following: given the nonlinear eigenvalue problem in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}^{2}u=\lambda\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u,\qquad\text{in }\Omega\\
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where
g=G^{\prime},b=B^{\prime}
, with
B
and
G
two
N
-functions, we prove:

(i)

When
G=B
, and for each normalization constraint

\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx=r,\,\,r>0,

there is
\lambda_{r}>0
and
u_{r}\neq 0
solving (
1.2
). Moreover, any eigenvalue
\lambda_{r}
of (
1.2
) is necessary greater or equal than the following infimum of
Rayleigh quotients

\lambda_{0}=\inf_{u\neq 0}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u|)|\Delta u|\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)|u|\,dx}.

In our framework, it is an open question if
\lambda_{0}
is indeed an eigenvalue of (
1.2
), even for domains with small diameters (compare to Theorem 3.3 in
[
10
]
where thanks to a Poincaré inequality the corresponding
\lambda_{0}
is positive).

(ii)

The spectrum is the whole interval
(0,\infty)
in two cases. The first one, is when
B
grows much faster than
G
near
\infty
, much slower than
G
near
0
, but it is still subcritical (in the sense of the embedding between Orlicz-Sobolev and Orlicz spaces). This is done by showing that the energy funcional associated with (
1.2
) has a non-trivial global minimum. See Theorem
6.13
.
The second case, is when
B
grows much slowly than
G
near
\infty
and much faster near
0
. Here, the coerciveness of the energy functional is essential to find a non-trivial critical point. See Theorem
6.9
.

(iii)

When
G
grows much slowly than
B
around
0
, the spectrum is concentrated around
0
, that is, there is
\lambda^{*}>0
such that any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is an eigenvalue of (
1.2
). This is done by showing that the associated energy functional has a mountain pass geometry, but since no Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition is satisfied, the conclusion is obtained by the Ekeland’s variational principle and functional properties of the main operator
\Delta_{g}^{2}
. We refer to Theorem
6.1
.

(iv)

Finally, when
B
grows much slowly than
G
near
0
, then the spectrum is concentrated around
\infty
, that is, there is
\lambda^{**}>0
such that any
\lambda>\lambda^{**}
is an eigenvalue of (
1.2
). We refer to Theorem
6.11
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give definitions and useful results in Orlicz and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. In section
3
, we motivate and discuss the definition of weak solutions to Dirichlet problem involving the biharmonic
g
-Laplacian. Next, in Section
4
, we present some functional properties for
\Delta_{g}^{2}
. In Section
5
, we studied the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) and, afterwards, in Section
6
, we analize the behaviour of the spectrum of problem (
1.2
) under different regimes.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce basic definitions and preliminary results related to Orlicz spaces. We start recalling the definition of an N-function.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
G\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an N-function if it admits the representation

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau,

where the function
g
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and satisfies the conditions

g(0)=0,\quad g(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

By
[
12
, Chapter 1]
, an N-function has also the following properties:

(1)

G
is continuous, convex, increasing, even and
G(0)=0
.

(2)

G
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=0

and

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=\infty.

Indeed, the above conditions serve as an equivalent definition of N-functions.

An important property for N-functions is the following:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the N-function
G
satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

G(2x)\leq CG(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Examples of functions satisfying the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition are:

•

G(t)=t^{p}
,
t\geq 0
,
p>1
;

•

G(t)=(1+|t|)\log(1+|t|)-|t|
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}\chi_{(0,1]}(t)+t^{q}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)
,
t\geq 0
,
p,q>1
.

By
[
12
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
, an N-function satisfies
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if and only if there is
p^{+}>1
such that

(2.1)

\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

Associated to
G
is the N-function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\widetilde{G}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-G(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

Moreover, the following representation holds for
\widetilde{G}
:

\widetilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g^{-1}(s)\,ds,

where
g^{-1}
is the right-continuous inverse of
g
. We recall that the role played by
\widetilde{G}
is the same as the conjugate exponent functions when
G(t)=t^{p}
,
p>1
.

The definition of the complementary function assures that the following Young-type inequality holds

(2.3)

at\leq G(t)+\widetilde{G}(a)\text{ for every }a,t\geq 0.

We also quote the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
5
, Lemma 2.9]

Let
G
be an N-function. If
G
satisfies (
2.1
) then

\tilde{G}(g(t))\leq(p^{+}-1)G(t),

where
g=G^{\prime}
and
\tilde{G}
is the complementary function of
G.

By
[
12
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the N-function
\widetilde{G}
complementary to
G
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{-}>1
such that

(2.4)

p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

From now on, we will assume that the N-function
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau
satisfies the following growth behaviour:

(2.5)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty,~~~~~\forall t>0.

For the last section of this work, we need to assume the following condition

(2.6)

p^{-}-1\leq\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq p^{+}-1,\quad\text{for }t>0.

It is well known the condition (
2.6
) implies (
2.5
).
Given two
N
-functions
A
and
B
, we say that
A
increases essentially more slowly than
B
, denoted by

A\ll B,
if for any
c>0
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{A(ct)}{B(t)}=0.

Given an N-function
G
, with
g=G^{\prime}
, we define the Orlicz-Lebesgue class
L^{G}(\Omega)
as follows

L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx<\infty\right\}.

If
G
and its complementary function
\tilde{G}
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition, then
L^{G}
becomes a vector spaces. It is a Banach space with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{G}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Moreover, we denote the convex modular by

\rho_{G}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx.

From now on we shall assume that any
N
-function and its complementary satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Moreover, for a positive integer
m
we will also consider the next Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

W^{m,G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega),\,|D^{\alpha}u|\in L^{G}(\Omega),\,\text{for all multi-index }\,|\alpha|\leq m\right\},

where for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
,

D^{\alpha}u=(\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}u,...,\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}u)

denotes the distributional derivative of
u
of order
\alpha
. The space
W^{m,G}(\Omega)
equipped with the norm

\|u\|:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|D^{\alpha}u\|_{G}

is a Banach space.
We will always assume that
G
satisfies:

•

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{(n+1)/n}}\,ds<\infty

•

\int_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{(n+1)/n}}\,ds=\infty

For a given
N
-function
G
, define the first order Sobolev conjugate function
G_{1}^{*}
of
G
by means of

(G_{1}^{*})^{-1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{1+1/n}}\,ds.

Then
G_{1}^{*}
is an
N
-function (see
[
6
]
). Next, we define the
m
-th order conjugate Sobolev function of
G
as recursively as follows

G^{*}_{0}:=G

G^{*}_{j}:=(G^{*}_{j-1})^{*},\quad j=1,...,m.

At each stage, we assume that

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{(G_{j}^{*})^{-1}(s)}{s^{(n+1)/n}}\,ds<\infty.

We obtain in this way a finite sequence of
N
-functions
G^{*}_{j}
,
j=0,...,m_{0}
, where
m_{0}
is such that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(G_{m_{0}-1}^{*})^{-1}(s)}{s^{(n+1)/n}}\,ds=\infty

but

\int_{1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(G_{m_{0}}^{*})^{-1}(s)}{s^{(n+1)/n}}\,ds<\infty.

Indeed,
m_{0}\leq n
, since by induction it can be proved that (see
[
3
]
)

(G_{m}^{*})^{-1}(t)\leq K_{m}t^{(n-j)/n}.

Then, we have the following embedding theorem for higher-order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces stated in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with the cone property. Let
G
be an
N
-function and let
m_{0}
be defined as before. Then

(1)

if
1\leq m\leq m_{0}
, then
W^{m,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{G_{m}^{*}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if
B
is an
N
-function increasing essentially more slowly than
G^{*}_{m}
near infinity, then the embedding
W^{m,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\Omega)
is compact;

(2)

if
m>m_{0}
, then
W^{m,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

In this paper we will consider the second order case
m=2
. Indeed, in order to take into account boundary conditions, we denote
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{2,G}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.5
.

By
[
22
]
, the norm
\|u\|
in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
is equivalent to

\|u\|_{2,G}:=\|\Delta u\|_{G}.

From now, we will consider the norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,G}
.
The relevant modular defined on
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
is given by

\rho_{2,G}(u):=\rho_{G}(\Delta u)=\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx.

By standard properties of modulars in Orlicz spaces, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then

•

\|u\|_{2,G}<1\Leftrightarrow\rho_{2,G}(u)<1.

•

\|u\|_{2,G}=1\Leftrightarrow\rho_{2,G}(u)=1.

•

\|u\|_{2,G}>1\Leftrightarrow\rho_{2,G}(u)>1.

•

\|u\|_{2,G}\to 0\,(\to\infty)\Leftrightarrow\rho_{2,G}(u)\to 0\,(\to\infty)

To close the section, we quote the following further useful relation between modulars and norms.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be an N-function satisfying (
2.5
), and let

\xi^{\pm}\colon[0,\infty)

\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

\xi^{-}(t):=\min\big\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\big\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\xi^{+}(t):=\max\big\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\big\}.

Then

\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{2,G})\leq\rho_{2,G}(u)\leq\xi^{+}(\|u\|_{2,G}).

3.
On the definition of weak solutions for the biharmonic
g
-Laplacian

In this section, we discuss the notion of weak solutions to problems of the form:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}^{2}u=f(x,u)\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where the nonlinearity
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying a growth condition so that

f(\cdot,u)v\in L^{1}(\Omega),

for any
u,v\in W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega)
. For instance, it is enough to assume that

|f(x,u)|\leq Cg(u)+\phi(x),\quad g=G^{\prime},\phi\in L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega).

We consider the energy function
\Phi:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated to the problem (
3.1
):

\Phi(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\,dx,\quad u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),

where

F(x,u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(x,t)\,dt.

Observe that
\Phi\in C^{1}(W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
and that

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(\Delta u)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\Delta v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx,\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega).

In the next results, we will stablish some useful properties of
L^{\prime}
.

To motivate the definition of weak solutions and the boundary conditions in (
3.1
), suppose that
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
is a classical solution to Problem (
3.1
). Then, for any
v\in C^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have by applying integration by parts twice:

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx&=\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right)\,d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right)\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Delta v\,\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\,dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\,d\sigma\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Delta v\,\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\,dx.\end{split}

Based on the previous comments, we define next the notion of weak solutions to Problem (
3.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of Problem (
3.1
) if and only if

\int_{\Omega}\Delta v\,\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,dx,\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega).

4.
Basic properties of
\Delta_{g}^{2}

Let
L:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

L(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have that
L\in C^{1}(W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\mathbb{R})
and its derivative
L^{\prime}
satisfies

(i)

L^{\prime}:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to[W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
is a bounded, uniformly monotone homeomorphism. In particular, the inverse operator
(L^{\prime})^{-1}:[W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}\to W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
exists and it is continuous.

(ii)

L^{\prime}
is an operator of class
S_{+}
, that is, for any sequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\langle L^{\prime}(u)-L^{\prime}(u_{n}),u-u_{n}\right\rangle\leq 0,

there holds
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
L^{\prime}:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to[W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
given by

\left\langle L^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\Delta v\,dx

is continuous in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We next prove that
L^{\prime}
is bounded, that is, it takes bounded subset of
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
into bounded subsets of
[W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
. Suppose for simplicity that
\|u\|_{2,G}\leq 1
, then for all
v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|v\|_{2,G}\leq 1
we have

\begin{split}|\left\langle L^{\prime}(u),v\right\rangle|&=\big|\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\Delta v\,dx\big|\\
&\leq C\left\|\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right\|_{\tilde{G}}\|\Delta v\|_{G}\\
&\leq\max\left\{\left[\rho_{\tilde{G}}\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right)\right]^{1/\tilde{p}^{+}},\left[\rho_{\tilde{G}}\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u\right)\right]^{1/\tilde{p}^{-}}\right\}\\
&\leq C\max\left\{[\rho_{G}(\Delta u)]^{1/\tilde{p}^{+}},[\rho_{G}(\Delta u)]^{1/\tilde{p}^{-}}\right\}\leq C.\end{split}

Hence,
\|L^{\prime}(u)\|_{[W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)]^{\prime}}\leq C
for all
\|u\|_{2,G}\leq 1
.

To prove that
L^{\prime}
is uniformly monotone, we proceed as follows

\begin{split}\left\langle L^{\prime}(u-v),u-v\right\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta(u-v)|)|\Delta(u-v)|\,dx\\
&\geq p^{-}\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta(u-v))\,dx\\
&\geq p^{-}\min\left\{\|u-v\|^{p^{-}}_{2,G},\|u-v\|^{p^{+}}_{2,G}\right\}\\
&\geq p^{-}\|u-v\|_{2,G}a(\|u-v\|_{2,G}),\end{split}

where

a(t):=\min\left\{t^{p^{+}-1},t^{p^{-}-1}\right\}.

Hence,
L^{\prime}
is uniformly monotone.

In order to prove that
L^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism of class
S_{+}
, we will show that it is hemicontinuous, coercitive and apply
[
24
, Theorem 26.A]
. To prove that
L^{\prime}
is hemicontinuous, that is, the function

t\to\left\langle L^{\prime}(u+tv),w\right\rangle,\quad t\in[0,1],\,u,v,w\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)

is continuous, observe that for any
t\in[0,1]
and any sequence
t_{n}\in[0,1]
converging to
t_{n}
, it follows that

\frac{g(|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|)}{|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|}(\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v)\to\frac{g(|\Delta u+t\Delta v|)}{|\Delta u+t\Delta v|}(\Delta u+t\Delta v),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{g(|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|)}{|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|}(\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v)\Delta w\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{G}\left(\frac{g(|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|)}{|\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v|}(\Delta u+t_{n}\Delta v)\right)+G(\Delta w)

\displaystyle\leq C(G(\Delta u)+G(\Delta v)+G(\Delta w))\in L^{1}(\Omega).

By dominated convergence theorem, we get that

\left\langle L^{\prime}(u+t_{n}v),w\right\rangle\to\left\langle L^{\prime}(u+tv),w\right\rangle.

We finally prove that
L^{\prime}
is coercitive, that is,

\dfrac{\left\langle L^{\prime}(u),u\right\rangle}{\|u\|_{2,G}}\to\infty,\quad\text{as }\|u\|_{2,G}\to\infty.

This is a consequence of the following inequalities for
\|u\|_{2,G}
large

(4.1)

\left\langle L^{\prime}(u),u\right\rangle\geq p^{-}\rho_{G}(\Delta u)\geq p^{-}\|u\|^{p^{-}}_{2,G}.

Therefore, by
[
24
, Theorem 26.A]
, the proposition follows.

5.
Eigenvalue problem for the biharmonic
g
-Laplacian

In this section, we consider the following eigenvalue problem: given
r>0
, find a function
u_{r}
and
\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}^{2}u_{r}=\lambda_{r}\dfrac{g(|u_{r}|)}{|u_{r}|}u_{r},\qquad\text{in }\Omega\\
u_{r}\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=r.\end{cases}

As the biharmonic
g
-Laplacian is not homogeneous, the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions depend on the normallization condition

\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=r.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying (
2.5
). Given
r>0
, let

(5.2)

c_{r}:=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx:u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx=r\right\}.

Then, there exists
u_{r}\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that:

\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx=c_{r}.

Proof.

Take a minimizing sequence
u_{n}\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
for
c_{r}
, that is:

\int_{\Omega}G(u_{n})\,dx=r,\quad\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u_{n})\,dx\to c_{r},

as
n\to\infty
. Hence

\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u_{n})\,dx\leq C,\quad\text{for all n}

and so by Lemma
2.7
,
u_{n}
is bounded in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence, there is
u_{r}\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and a subsequence of
u_{n}
, still denoted by
u_{n}
, such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{r}\qquad\mbox{ in }W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)

In particular,
\Delta u_{n}\rightharpoonup\Delta u_{r}
in
L^{G}(\Omega)
. Since the modular
\rho_{G}
is sequentially lower semi-continuous, we get

\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u_{r})\,dx\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u_{n})\,dx=c_{r}.

Hence, to conclude the proof, we just need to show that
u_{r}
satisfies the constrain

\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=r.

By the compact Theorem
2.4
,
u_{n}\to u_{r}
strongly in
L^{G}(\Omega)
. Hence,

(5.3)

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{\Omega}(G(u_{r})-G(u_{n}))\,dx\bigg|&=\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}g(tu_{r}+(1-t)u_{n})(u_{r}-u_{n})\,dt\,dx\bigg|\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{r}|+|u_{n}|)|u_{r}-u_{n}|\,dx\bigg|\\
&\leq C\|g(|u_{r}|+|u_{n}|)\|_{\tilde{G}}\|u_{r}-u_{n}\|_{G}\\
&\leq C\|u_{r}-u_{n}\|_{G}\to 0,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

Then,
u_{r}
satisfies the normalization condition and so it solves (
5.2
).

Remark 5.2
.

Observe that
c_{r}>0
, since if
c_{r}=0
, then

\Delta u_{r}=0\text{ in }\Omega,

and so

\|u_{r}\|_{2,G}=\|\Delta u_{r}\|_{G}=0

which yields
u_{r}=0
. This clearly contradicts the constraint

\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=r>0.

We next show that
u_{r}
is indeed a solution of the eigenvalue problem (
5.1
). Instead of applying Lagrange Multipliers, we employ a method from
[
18
]
which can be applied even if the
N
-functions do not satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

We start by quoting the following technical lemma from
[
18
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
u,v\in L^{G}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}uv\,dx\neq 0.

Then, the condition

\int_{\Omega}G((1-\varepsilon)u+\delta v)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx,

defines a continuously differentiable function
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
in some interval
(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
, with
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Moreover,
\delta(0)=0
and

\delta^{\prime}(0)=\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}uu\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u|)}{|u|}uv\,dx}.

Finally, we state that
u_{r}
solves (
5.1
).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
u_{r}
be a solution of (
5.2
). Then, there is
\lambda_{r}>0
such that

(5.4)

\Delta_{g}^{2}u_{r}=\lambda_{r}\dfrac{g(|u_{r}|)}{|u_{r}|}u_{r}\quad\text{weakly in }\Omega.

Proof.

Define linear functionals
F:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and
G:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by:

F(v)=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u_{r}|)}{|u_{r}|}u_{r}v\,dx,

H(v)=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta u_{r}|}\Delta u_{r}\Delta v\,dx.

We shall prove that Ker H
\subset
Ker F, which will show by
[
24
, Proposition 43.1]
that there is
\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
such that (
5.4
) holds. Let

P_{F}:=\left\{v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega):F(v)>0\right\}

and

P_{H}:=\left\{v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega):H(v)>0\right\}.

We will prove that
P_{F}\subset P_{H}
. Let
v\in P_{F}
. Then,

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u_{r}|)}{|u_{r}|}u_{r}v\,dx\neq 0.

From Lemma
5.3
, there are
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a
C^{1}((-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}))
-function
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
such that

\int_{\Omega}G((1-\varepsilon)u_{r}+\delta v)\,dx=r,\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}).

By Lemma
5.3
, we get
\delta^{\prime}(0)>0
, so

\dfrac{1}{2}\delta^{\prime}(0)<\delta^{\prime}(\varepsilon)<2\delta^{\prime}(0),

for all
\varepsilon
small. Hence, for
\varepsilon>0
small enough,

(5.5)

\dfrac{1}{2}\delta^{\prime}(0)<\dfrac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}<2\delta^{\prime}(0).

Next, denote
v_{\varepsilon}=(1-\varepsilon)u_{r}+\delta(\varepsilon)v
. Since
u_{r}
solves (
5.2
), there holds

(5.6)

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,dx\geq 0.

Also,

\begin{split}I_{\varepsilon}:=\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{\delta(\varepsilon)}&=\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|-|\Delta u_{r}|}\dfrac{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-|\Delta u_{r}|^{2}}{(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|+|\Delta u_{r}|)\delta(\varepsilon)}\\
&=\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|-|\Delta u_{r}|}\dfrac{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|-|\Delta u_{r}|}{\varepsilon}\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\end{split}

When
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, since
\Delta v_{\varepsilon}\to\Delta u_{r}
a.e. in
\Omega
, it follows

\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|-|\Delta u_{r}|}\to g(|\Delta u_{r}|).

Moreover,

\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\to\dfrac{1}{\delta^{\prime}(0)},\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Finally, letting

\Phi(\varepsilon)=|(1-\varepsilon)\Delta u_{r}+\delta(\varepsilon)v|,

we get

\dfrac{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|-|\Delta u_{r}|}{\varepsilon}=\dfrac{\Phi(\varepsilon)-\Phi(0)}{\varepsilon}\to\Phi^{\prime}(0)=\dfrac{\Delta u_{r}}{|\Delta u_{r}|}\left(-\Delta u_{r}+\delta^{\prime}(0)\Delta v\right).

Therefore,

(5.7)

I_{\varepsilon}\to g(|\Delta u_{r}|)\left(-\dfrac{1}{\delta^{\prime}(0)}|\Delta u_{r}|+\dfrac{\Delta u_{r}\Delta v}{|\Delta u_{r}|}\right),

a.e. in
\Omega
when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. In addition,

\begin{split}\bigg|\dfrac{G(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)-G(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\bigg|&\leq\left(g(|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}|)+g(|\Delta u_{r}|)\right)\dfrac{|\Delta v_{\varepsilon}-\Delta u_{r}|}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\\
&\leq\left(g(|\Delta u_{r}|+\|\delta(\varepsilon)\|_{L^{\infty}([-\varepsilon_{0}/2,\varepsilon_{0}/2])}|\Delta v|\right)\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta(\varepsilon)}|\Delta u_{r}|+|\Delta v|\right)\\
&\leq\left(g(|\Delta u_{r}|+\|\delta(\varepsilon)\|_{L^{\infty}([-\varepsilon_{0}/2,\varepsilon_{0}/2])}|\Delta v|\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{\delta^{\prime}(0)}|\Delta u_{r}|+|\Delta v|\right)\in L^{1}(\Omega),\end{split}

where we have used (
5.5
) and Hölder’s inequality. Therefore, by dominated convergence theorem, we conclude taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
5.6
) and recalling (
5.7
), that

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta u_{r}|}\Delta u_{r}\Delta v\,dx\geq\dfrac{1}{\delta^{\prime}(0)}\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u_{r}|)|\Delta u_{r}|\,dx>0.

Observe that the last inequality follows from Remark
5.2
. Hence,
v\in P_{H}
. This concludes the proof.

The following result gives a straightforward lower bound of
\lambda_{r}
in terms of
c_{r}
, using the condition (
2.5
).

Proposition 5.5
.

For any
r>0
, the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{r}
satisfies the lower bound

\lambda_{r}\geq\dfrac{p^{-}c_{r}}{rp^{+}}.

Proof.

To prove the proposition, take
v=u_{r}
in the definition of weak solution to (
5.1
). Then,

\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u_{r}|)|\Delta u_{r}|\,dx=\lambda_{r}\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{r}|)|u_{r}|\,dx.

By (
2.5
), we get

p^{-}\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u_{r})\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u_{r}|)|\Delta u_{r}|\,dx=\lambda_{r}\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{r}|)|u_{r}|\,dx\leq\lambda_{r}p^{+}\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx.

The proof follows by recalling that
\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=r
and the definition of
c_{r}
(
5.2
).

The objective of the next result is to bound from below the eigenvalues of
\Delta^{2}_{g}
in terms of the variational quantity:

\lambda_{0}:=\inf_{u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),u\neq 0}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u|)|\Delta u|\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)|u|\,dx}.

Proposition 5.6
.

Any eigenvalue
\lambda_{r}
of (
5.1
) satisfies

\lambda_{r}\in[\lambda_{0},\infty).

Proof.

Suppose that

(5.8)

\lambda_{r}<\lambda_{0}

for some
r>0.
Then, we know that there is
u_{r}\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\int_{\Omega}G(u_{r})\,dx=1
, such that

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{r}|)}{|\Delta u_{r}|}\Delta u_{r}\Delta v\,dx=\lambda_{r}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|u_{r}|)}{|u_{r}|}u_{r}v\,dx,

for all
v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. In particular, taking
v=u_{r}
, we get

\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u_{r}|)|\Delta u_{r}|\,dx=\lambda_{r}\int_{\Omega}g(|u_{r}|)|u_{r}|\,dx

which, in view of (
5.8
), contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{0}
.

6.
Nonlinear eigenvalue problem with two Orlicz functions

Here, we are concerned with an eigenvalue problem driven by two
N
-functions
G
and
B
. We will see that depending on the relative growth of
G
and
B
, there is a continuous spectrum concentrated around
0
, around
\infty
or that coincides with the whole interval
(0,\infty)
.

In any case, we let
G
and
B
be two
N
-functions satisfying (
2.5
), and we consider the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}^{2}u=\lambda\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u,\qquad\text{in }\Omega\\
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega),\end{cases}

where
b=B^{\prime}
. Given the
\Delta_{2}
-exponents:

p^{-}:=\inf_{t>0}\dfrac{tg(t)}{G(t)},\,\,p^{+}:=\sup_{t>0}\dfrac{tg(t)}{G(t)},

and the corresponding
p^{-}_{B}
and
p_{B}^{+}
for
B
, we will consider all relative growth behaviours between
G
and
B
in terms of the above exponents, that is:

•

Theorem
6.1
:
1<p_{B}^{-}<p^{-}<p_{B}^{+}<p^{+}
and
1<p_{B}^{-}<p^{-}<p^{+}<p_{B}^{+}
;

•

Theorem
6.9
:
1<p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}<p^{-}<p^{+}
;

•

Theorem
6.11
:
p^{-}<p_{B}^{-}<p^{+}<p_{B}^{+}
and
p^{-}<p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}<p^{+}
;

•

Theorem
6.13
:
p^{-}<p^{+}<p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}
.

Theorem 6.1

(Spectrum concentrated around
0
)
.

Let
G
and
B
be two
N
-functions satisfying (
2.5
). Moreover assume that
B\ll G^{*}
, that

(6.2)

1<p_{B}^{-}<p^{-}<p_{B}^{+},

and that there is
t_{0}>0
such that

(6.3)

p_{B}^{-}-1\leq\dfrac{tb^{\prime}(t)}{b(t)},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,t_{0}),\,b=B^{\prime}.

Then, there is
\lambda^{*}>0
such that any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is an eigenvalue of the problem (
6.1
).

Remark 6.2
.

Observe that due to the hypothesis (
6.2
) on the exponents of
B
, we cannot guarantee that the right hand side term

\lambda\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u

satisfies the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition (that is, there is
\mu>2
such that
\mu B(u)\leq ub(u)
). Then, although the associated energy functional has a mountain pass geometry, the Palais Smale condition may not hold. So, the mountain pass Theorem may not be possible to use in this context.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we will prove some preliminary results. For any
\lambda>0
, let define
\Phi_{\lambda}:W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi_{\lambda}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}B(u)\,dx.

Lemma 6.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, there is
\lambda^{*}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, there are
\rho,\alpha>0
such that

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\alpha,\quad\text{for any }u\in\partial B_{\rho}(0).

Proof.

By the compact embedding Theorem
2.4
, there is
C>1
such that

||u||_{B}\leq C||u||_{2,G}.

Choose
0<\rho<1
so that

(6.4)

C\rho<1.

Let
\|u\|_{2,G}=\rho
, then

(6.5)

\begin{split}\Phi_{\lambda}(u)&=\int_{\Omega}G(\Delta u)\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}B(u)\\
&\geq\min\left\{\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}-\lambda\max\left\{\|u\|_{B}^{p_{B}^{+}},\|u\|_{B}^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}\\
&\geq\min\left\{\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}-\lambda\max\left\{(C\|u\|_{2,G})^{p_{B}^{+}},(C\|u\|_{2,G})^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}\\
&=\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}}-\lambda C\|u\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{cond rho})\\
&=\rho^{p^{+}}-\lambda C\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}=\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}(\rho^{p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}}-\lambda C).\end{split}

Hence, choosing

\lambda^{*}=\dfrac{\rho^{p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}}}{2C}

we conclude the proof from (
6.5
) and the assumption (
6.3
).

Lemma 6.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.1
, there is
v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
,
v\geq 0
,
v\neq 0
such that

\Phi_{\lambda}(tv)<0\qquad\text{for all $t>0$ small}.

Proof.

First, observe that assumption (
6.3
) implies that the function

h(t):=\dfrac{tb(t)}{B(t)},\quad t>0,

is nondecreasing. Indeed,

h^{\prime}(t)=\dfrac{(b(t)+tb^{\prime}(t))B(t)-tb(t)b(t)}{B(t)^{2}}\geq\dfrac{(b(t)+tb^{\prime}(t))B(t)-p_{B}^{-}B(t)b(t)}{B(t)^{2}}=\dfrac{tb^{\prime}(t)+(1-p_{B}^{-})b(t)}{B(t)}>0.

Hence, for
0<\varepsilon<p^{-}-p_{B}^{-}
, there is
0<t_{0}<1
such that

\dfrac{tb(t)}{B(t)}<p_{B}^{-}+\varepsilon_{0}<p^{-},

for any
0<t<t_{0}
. Integrating both side and assuming that
B(1)=1
for simplicity, we get

(6.6)

B(t)\geq t^{p_{B}^{-}+\varepsilon},\quad t\in(0,t_{0}).

Let now
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, nonnegative and such that
v(x),|\Delta v(x)|\leq 1/t_{0}
for all
x\in\Omega
. Then, for any
t\in(0,t_{0})
, it follows

\begin{split}\Phi_{\lambda}(tv)&\leq t^{p^{-}}\max\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}-\lambda t^{p^{-}_{B}+\varepsilon}\|v\|_{p_{B}^{-}+\varepsilon}^{p^{-}_{B}+\varepsilon}\\
&=c_{1}(v)t^{p^{-}}-\lambda t^{p^{-}_{B}+\varepsilon}c_{2}(v)\\
&=t^{p^{-}}(c_{1}(v)-\lambda c_{2}(v)t^{p^{-}_{B}+\varepsilon-p^{-}}).\end{split}

Hence, taking
t
small enough (and depending on the norms of
v
), we conclude the proof of the lemma.

Remark 6.5
.

We point out that assumption (
6.2
) means that
G
grows much slower than
B
near
0
. Indeed, as it will be shown in the proof of Lemma
6.4
, hypothesis (
6.3
) implies that the quotient

\dfrac{tb(t)}{B(t)}

is nodecreasing. Hence, for
\varepsilon\in(0,p^{-}-p_{B}^{-})
, there is
0<t_{0}<1
(see again the proof of Lemma
6.4
) such that

B(t)\geq t^{p_{B}^{-}+\varepsilon},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,t_{0}).

Therefore, by the choice of
\varepsilon
,

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{G(t)}{B(t)}\leq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{t^{p^{-}}}{t^{p_{B}^{-}+\varepsilon}}=0.

This proves the assertion. Also, observe that we are not assuming any behaviour between
B
and
G
around
\infty.

Remark 6.6
.

Observe that the lower bound
B(t)\geq\min\left\{t^{p_{B}^{-}},t^{p_{B}^{+}}\right\}
does not help to prove Lemma
6.4
. We need to use a sharper lower bound, see (
6.6
) .

Next, we finish the proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

First, by Lemma
6.3
, there is
\rho>0
such that

\inf_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\Phi_{\lambda}>0.

Moreover, for any
u\in B_{\rho}(0)
,

(6.7)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}}-C\lambda\|u\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\geq-C\lambda\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}>-\infty.

Thus,

(6.8)

-\infty<\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda}<0.

Let

0<\varepsilon<\inf_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\Phi_{\lambda}-\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda},

then by the Ekeland’s Variational Principle, there is
u_{\varepsilon}\in\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}
such that

(6.9)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon})<\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda}+\varepsilon,

and

(6.10)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon})<\Phi_{\lambda}(u)+\varepsilon\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{2,G},\quad u\neq u_{\varepsilon}.

Since (
6.8
) and (
6.9
)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon})<\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda}+\varepsilon<\inf_{\partial B_{\rho}(0)}\Phi_{\lambda},

we deduce that
u_{\varepsilon}
belongs to
B_{\rho}(0)
.

Let us define now the functional
\Psi_{\lambda}:\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Psi_{\lambda}(u):=\Phi_{\lambda}(u)+\varepsilon\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{2,G}.

Then, by (
6.10
),
u_{\varepsilon}
is a minimum point of
\Psi_{\lambda}
and so

(6.11)

\dfrac{\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon}+tv)-\Psi_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon})}{t}\geq 0,\quad t>0,v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega).

Letting
t\to 0^{+}
in (
6.11
), we obtain

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{\varepsilon}),v\right\rangle+\varepsilon\|v\|_{2,G}>0.

This implies that
\|\Phi^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\|\leq\varepsilon
. In this way, taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{n}
, we build a sequence
u_{n}\in B_{\rho}(0)
such that

(6.12)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{n})\to\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda},\quad\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})\to 0.

Since
u_{n}
is bounded, there is
u\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence, by Theorem
2.4
,

u_{n}\to u\text{ in }L^{B}(\Omega).

Next, observe that

(6.13)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u-\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{n}|)}{|\Delta u_{n}|}\Delta u_{n}\right)(\Delta u-\Delta u_{n})\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u-\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{n}|)}{|\Delta u_{n}|}\Delta u_{n}\right)(\Delta u-\Delta u_{n})\,dx\\
&\qquad-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\right)(u-u_{n})\,dx+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\right)(u-u_{n})\,dx\\
&\qquad=\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)-\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n}),u-u_{n}\right\rangle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\right)(u-u_{n})\,dx.\end{split}

By (
6.12
) and the weak convergence of
u_{n}
to
u
, we obtain:

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)-\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n}),u-u_{n}\right\rangle\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Moreover,

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\right)(u-u_{n})\,dx\bigg|\leq C\bigg\|\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\bigg\|_{\tilde{B}}\|u_{n}-u\|_{B}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
, since

\bigg\|\dfrac{b(|u|)}{|u|}u-\dfrac{b(|u_{n}|)}{|u_{n}|}u_{n}\bigg\|_{\tilde{B}}\leq C

for all
n
by the weak convergence in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\|u_{n}-u\|_{B}\to 0
by the strong convergence in
L^{B}(\Omega).
Therefore, by (
6.13
), we obtain that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{g(|\Delta u|)}{|\Delta u|}\Delta u-\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{n}|)}{|\Delta u_{n}|}\Delta u_{n}\right)(\Delta u-\Delta u_{n})\,dx\leq 0,

hence by Proposition
4.1
, we conclude that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Therefore, (
6.12
) implies that

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),v\right\rangle=0,

for any
v\in W^{2,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. So,
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is an eigenvalue of (
6.1
).

Remark 6.7
.

Observe that the critical point
u
is not zero, since by (
6.12
),

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)=d=\inf_{\overline{B_{\rho}(0)}}\Phi_{\lambda}<0.

On the other hand, we get for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, there exists
u_{\lambda}
that

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{g(|\Delta u_{\lambda}|)}{|\Delta u_{\lambda}|}\Delta u_{\lambda}\Delta v\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{b(|u_{\lambda}|)}{|u_{\lambda}|}u_{\lambda}v\,dx.

Choosing
v=u_{\lambda}
, we have

\frac{\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u_{\lambda}|)|\Delta u_{\lambda}|\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}b(|u_{\lambda}|)|u_{\lambda}|\,dx}=\lambda.

Taking infimum

\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega),u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u|)|\Delta u|\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}b(|u|)|u|\,dx}\leq\lambda.

Hence, as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, we obtain

\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega),u\neq 0}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u|)|\Delta u|\,dx}{\int_{\Omega}b(|u|)|u|\,dx}=0,

and so the following anti-Sobolev type inequality holds:

Corollary 6.8
.

For any constant
C>0
, there is
u\in W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}g(|\Delta u|)|\Delta u|\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}b(|u|)|u|\,dx.

Observe that in the previous case, we do not use any relation between the exponents
p^{-}
and
p_{B}^{+}
. Indeed, the proof also works in the case

p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}<p^{-}.

However, we will see that in this case, the spectrum is larger.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
G
and
B
be two
N
-functions satisfying (
2.5
). Moreover assume that
B\ll G^{*}
and that

(6.14)

1<p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}<p^{-},

Then, any
\lambda>0
is an eigenvalue of the problem (
6.1
).

Proof.

In this case, we will show that for any
\lambda>0
, the functional
\Phi_{\lambda}
has a nontrivial minimum point.

We start by showing that the functional
\Phi_{\lambda}
is coercive:

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\to\infty,\text{ as }\|u\|_{2,G}\to\infty.

Observe, by a similar reasoning as in (
6.5
), that for
\|u\|_{2,G}>1
and thanks to (
6.14
),

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}-C\lambda\|u\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{+}}\geq\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}(1-C\lambda\|u\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{+}-p^{-}})\to\infty

as
\|u\|_{2,G}\to\infty
. This shows that
\Phi_{\lambda}
is coercive. Moreover,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is sequentially weakly lower-semicontinuous, since the modular

\rho_{G}(\cdot)

is seq. lower semi-continuous and

\int_{\Omega}B(u_{n})\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}B(u)\,dx

for any sequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega)
(see for instance the calculation (
5.3
)). Then, there is
u\in W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega)
such that

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)=\inf_{W_{0}^{2,G}(\Omega)}\Phi_{\lambda}.

Hence
u
is a weak solution of (
6.1
). In order to show that
u\neq 0
, take any
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
so that
0<\|v\|_{2,G}
. Then,

\Phi_{\lambda}(v)\leq\max\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}-\lambda C\min\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}.

Hence, choose

(6.15)

\lambda^{**}:=\dfrac{\max\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}}{C\min\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}}>0.

In this way, for any
\lambda>\lambda^{**}
, there is
v
so that
\Phi_{\lambda}(v)<0
. Next, we will analize the possible values of
\lambda^{**}
. Introducing the function

F(t):=\dfrac{\max\left\{t^{p^{+}},t^{p^{-}}\right\}}{C\min\left\{t^{p_{B}^{+}},t^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}},

there holds

F(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{C}t^{p^{-}-p_{B}^{+}},\quad 0<t<1\\
\dfrac{1}{C}t^{p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}},\quad t>1.\end{cases}

By assumption (
6.15
),
p^{-}-p_{B}^{+},p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}>0
, so the range of
F
is
(0,\infty)
. Hence, by (
6.15
), any
\lambda>0
may be chosen to get
\Phi_{\lambda}(v)<0
for some
v
.
We conclude that
u\neq 0
. This ends the proof of the Theorem.

Remark 6.10
.

Observe that under the assumption (
6.14
), we have

\dfrac{G(t)}{B(t)}\geq\dfrac{t^{p^{-}}}{t^{p_{B}^{+}}}\to\infty\quad\text{as }t\to\infty

and

\dfrac{G(t)}{B(t)}\leq\dfrac{t^{p^{+}}}{t^{p_{B}^{-}}}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to 0.

Thus, in Theorem
6.9
, we have
B\ll G
near
\infty
and
G\ll B
near
0
.

In the next result, we state the case where
B
grows much slower than
G
near
0
.

Theorem 6.11

(Spectrum concentrated at
\infty
)
.

Let
G
and
B
be two
N
-functions satisfying (
2.5
). Moreover assume that
B\ll G^{*}
and that

(6.16)

1<p^{-}<p_{B}^{-}<p^{+}

and that there is
t_{0}>0

(6.17)

p^{-}-1\leq\dfrac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,t_{0}).

Then, there is
\lambda^{**}>0
such that any
\lambda\in(\lambda^{**},\infty)
is an eigenvalue of the problem (
6.1
).

Proof.

We combine the strategies of the proofs of Theorem
6.1
and Theorem
6.9
. Indeed, reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
, there is
t_{0}^{\prime}<t_{0}
such that

G(t)\geq t^{p^{-}+\varepsilon},\quad\text{for all }t\in(0,t_{0}^{\prime}),

and with
\varepsilon\in(0,p_{B}^{-}-p^{-})
. Hence, for
\rho\in(0,t_{0}^{\prime})
and
\|u\|_{2,G}=\rho
,

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\|u\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}+\varepsilon}-C\lambda\|u\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}=\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}(\rho^{p^{-}+\varepsilon-p_{B}^{-}}-C\lambda).

Thus, for any
\lambda>0
, and taking
\rho
small enough, we have that for any
u\in\partial B_{\rho}(0)
. there holds
\Phi_{\lambda}(u)>0
.

Also, taking any
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
so that
0<\|v\|_{2,G}<1
, we get

\Phi_{\lambda}(v)\leq\max\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{-}}\right\}-\lambda C\min\left\{\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{+}},\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}.

As in the proof of Theorem
6.9
, let

F(t):=\dfrac{\max\left\{t^{p^{+}},t^{p^{-}}\right\}}{C\min\left\{t^{p_{B}^{+}},t^{p_{B}^{-}}\right\}},

then there holds

F(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{C}t^{p^{-}-p_{B}^{+}},\quad 0<t<1\\
\dfrac{1}{C}t^{p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}},\quad t>1.\end{cases}

Observe that by (
6.16
),
p^{-}-p_{B}^{+}<0
and
p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}>0
, the range of
F
is
(1/C,\infty)
. Hence, choosing

(6.18)

\lambda^{**}:=\dfrac{1}{C}>0,

we get that for any
\lambda\in(\lambda^{**},\infty)
, there is
v
so that
\Phi_{\lambda}(v)<0
. The rest of the proof follows as for Theorem
6.1
applying the Ekeland’s Variational Principle.

Remark 6.12
.

Under the assumption (
6.16
), we have

\dfrac{B(t)}{G(t)}\leq\dfrac{t^{p_{B}^{-}}}{t^{p^{-}+\varepsilon}}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to 0.

Thus, in Theorem
6.11
, we have
B\ll G
near
0
.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
G
and
B
be two
N
-functions satisfying (
2.5
). Moreover assume that
B\ll G^{*}
and that

(6.19)

1<p^{-}<p^{+}<p_{B}^{-}<p_{B}^{+}.

Then, any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
is an eigenvalue of the problem (
6.1
).

Proof.

Again, we will check that Lemma
6.3
and Lemma
6.4
hold. Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, take
\rho\in(0,1)
, and let
\|u\|_{2,G}=\rho
. Following the calculations from (
6.5
), we get

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\rho^{p^{+}}-\lambda C\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}=\rho^{p_{B}^{-}}(\rho^{p^{+}-p_{B}^{-}}-\lambda C).

So, by (
6.19
), for
\rho
small enough, there is
\alpha>0
such that
\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\geq\alpha
for any
\|u\|_{2,G}=\rho
.

Next, for
t>1
, take
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
1<\|v\|_{2,G}
. Then,

(6.20)

\Phi_{\lambda}(tv)\leq t^{p^{+}}\|v\|_{2,G}^{p^{+}}-C\lambda t^{p_{B}^{-}}\|v\|_{2,G}^{p_{B}^{-}}\to-\infty,\quad\text{as }t\to\infty.

Hence, we conclude following the lines of the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Remark 6.14
.

Under the assumption (
6.19
), we have

\dfrac{B(t)}{G(t)}\leq\dfrac{t^{p_{B}^{-}}}{t^{p^{+}}}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to 0,

and

\dfrac{G(t)}{B(t)}\leq\dfrac{t^{p^{+}}}{t^{p_{B}^{-}}}\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to\infty.

Thus, in Theorem
6.13
, we have
B\ll G
near
0
and
G\ll B
near
\infty
.

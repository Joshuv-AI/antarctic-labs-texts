Symbolic powers of polymatroidal ideals
Antonino Ficarra, Somayeh Moradi

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the componentwise linearity and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of symbolic powers of polymatroidal ideals. For a polymatroidal ideal I I , we conjecture that every symbolic power I ( k ) I^{(k)} is componentwise linear and reg ​ I ( k ) = reg ​ I k \text{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\text{reg}\,I^{k} for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We prove that reg ​ I ( k ) ≥ reg ​ I k \text{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\text{reg}\,I^{k} for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 when I I has no embedded associated primes, for instance if I I is a matroidal ideal. Moreover, we establish a criterion on the symbolic Rees algebra ℛ s ​ ( I ) \mathcal{R}_{s}(I) of a monomial ideal of minimal intersection type which guarantees that every symbolic power I ( k ) I^{(k)} has linear quotients and, hence, is componentwise linear for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . By applying our criterion to squarefree Veronese ideals and certain matching-matroidal ideals, we verify both conjectures for these families. We establish the Conforti-Cornuéjols conjecture for any matroidal ideal, and we show that a matroidal ideal is packed if and only if it is the product of monomial prime ideals with pairwise disjoint supports. Furthermore, we identify several classes of non-squarefree polymatroidal ideals for which the ordinary and symbolic powers coincide. Hence, we confirm our conjectures for transversal polymatroidal ideals and principal Borel ideals. Finally, we verify our conjectures for all polymatroidal ideals either generated in small degrees or in a small number of variables.

Introduction

The study of symbolic powers of ideals has long been a central theme in Commutative Algebra. For an ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring
R
, the
k
th symbolic power of
I
is defined as

I^{(k)}\ =\ \bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(I^{k}R_{P}\cap R),

where
\textup{Ass}(I)
is the set of associated prime ideals of
I
.

Symbolic powers first appeared in the literature in Krull’s proof of his principal ideal theorem
[
42
]
. Subsequent work by Zariski
[
56
]
and Nagata
[
48
, page 143]
highlighted their geometric significance, where they showed that if
P
is a prime ideal of a polynomial ring
S
over a field
K
, then one has
P^{(k)}=\bigcap\mathfrak{m}^{k}
, where the intersection runs through all maximal ideals of
S
containing
P
. This result was later refined by Eisenbud and Hochster
[
13
]
. More recently, De Stefani, Grifo and Jeffries extended these ideas to smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras using
p
-derivations
[
10
]
. Moreover, the question of whether the symbolic Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{(k)}t^{k}
is Noetherian was answered in the negative by Roberts
[
49
]
, following counterexamples to Hilbert’s 14th problem by Nagata
[
47
]
. The containment problem between ordinary and symbolic powers has since been a major line of inquiry (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
and the references therein).

Our primary motivation to study symbolic powers comes from combinatorics and the theory of monomial ideals. Over the past two decades, there has been an extensive investigation of symbolic powers of monomial ideals, beginning with the seminal work of Herzog, Hibi and Trung
[
32
]
(see also
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
19
,
26
,
43
,
44
,
45
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
and the references therein).

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring over a field
K
. In
[
32
]
, it is shown that for any monomial ideal
I\subset S
, the symbolic Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
is Noetherian and consequently, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}
is a quasi-linear function for
k\gg 0
. By contrast, the regularity of the ordinary powers
\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
is eventually linear (see
[
8
,
41
]
). In the context of edge ideals, a remarkable conjecture of Minh
[
43
]
posits that
\textup{reg}\,I(G)^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I(G)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, where
I(G)
is the edge ideal of a finite simple graph
G
. If true, this would imply that
\textup{reg}\,I(G)^{(k)}
is eventually linear, rather than merely quasi-linear, a surprising and impactful result.

Among monomial ideals, polymatroidal ideals hold a distinguished place. These ideals are the algebraic counterpart of discrete polymatroids, a multiset analogue of matroids
[
55
]
introduced by Herzog and Hibi
[
28
]
. A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
polymatroidal
if the exponent vectors of its minimal generators form the set of bases of a discrete polymatroid. These ideals enjoy many desirable properties: for example, the product of polymatroidal ideals is again polymatroidal, they have linear resolutions, and consequently, all their powers have linear resolutions. Moreover, Bandari and Rahmati-Asghar
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
characterized polymatroidal ideals as those equigenerated monomial ideals that have linear quotients with respect to the lexicographic order induced by any ordering of the variables.

However, not much is known about the symbolic powers of polymatroidal ideals. In this paper, our main objective is to address the following Conjectures A and B.

Conjecture A
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

(1)

If true, this would imply that
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}
is eventually linear. Based on extensive computational evidence, we expect that every graded component of
I^{(k)}
has a linear resolution, i.e.,
I^{(k)}
is
componentwise linear

[
27
]
. We thus further raise the following

Conjecture B
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
I^{(k)}
is componentwise linear for all
k\geq 1
.

If, in addition, the final degrees of
I^{(k)}
and
I^{k}
coincide (i.e.,
\omega(I^{(k)})=\omega(I^{k})
), then Conjecture
A
would follow from Conjecture
B
.

In Section

1.
General bounds for the regularity of symbolic powers

In this section, we bound the regularity of symbolic powers of monomial ideals, and prove the inequality
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\textup{reg}\,I^{k}

\deg(w_{F})\leq\deg(v_{F})k+k-1\leq(k_{j}-1)k+k-1=kk_{j}-1.

This shows that
w=u^{k}/x_{i}\notin P_{j}^{kk_{j}}
. Equation (
6
) implies that
u^{k}/x_{i}\notin I^{(k)}
, for every
i\in\textup{supp}(u)
. Therefore
u^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
.

(b) Let
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
be a monomial with
\deg(u)=\omega(I)
. By (a) we have
u^{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
. Hence,
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\omega(I^{(k)})\geq\deg(u^{k})=\omega(I)k
.

(c) We have
\textup{reg}\,I^{k}=\omega(I^{k})=\omega(I)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence, the assertion follows from (b) because
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\omega(I^{(k)})\geq\omega(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

(d) Since

has linear powers, we have
\textup{reg}\,I^{k}=\alpha(I)k=\omega(I)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Using part (b), Theorem
1.1
(a) and the assumptions
\textup{reg}_{x}\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)=0
and

for all

, we have
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\omega(I)k=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, as desired.

∎

As a consequence, we establish the inequality
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
of the equation proposed in Conjecture
A
for any polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
without embedded associated primes. Such an ideal is of minimal intersection type and has linear powers. Hence, Theorem
1.2
(c) implies

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal without embedded associated primes. Then for all
k\geq 1
,

\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}\geq\textup{reg}\,I^{k}.

One may wonder whether the condition
d(I,k)=\omega(I)k
given in Theorem
1.2
(d) is met by interesting families of monomial ideals. In this direction, we have

Proposition 1.4
.

Let

be a monomial ideal such that for any minimal monomial generator

of
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
with

Proof.

The assumption implies that

, where the

. Hence,

\omega(I)k\leq d(I,k)\leq\max_{\bf c}\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}(i+\omega(I)-1)c_{ij}\},

(7)

where the maximum is taken over all vectors

Hence

and so

(\omega(I)-1)c_{1}+\dots+(\omega(I)-1)c_{r}\leq(\omega(I)-1)k

(9)

Summing (
8
) and (
9
) and comparing with (

∎

We close this section by providing an interesting family of monomial ideals

satisfying the condition

for all

.

For a finite simple graph
G
with the vertex set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set

, the

of

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\ \{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G))

By

.

Let

. Then

for all

.

2.
The
x
-condition for symbolic Rees algebras

In this section we study the symbolic powers of monomial ideals of minimal intersection type by investigating the defining ideal of their symbolic Rees algebra. We provide a criterion on monomial ideals of minimal intersection type which implies componentwise linearity for all symbolic powers of such ideals.

A homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
is called
componentwise linear
, if the ideal

I_{\langle d\rangle}=(f\in I:\,f\textrm{ is homogeneous of degree }d)

has linear resolution for any positive integer
d
. If
I\subset S
is componentwise linear, then
\textup{reg}\,I=\omega(I)
. An useful approach to show that an ideal is componentwise linear is to show that it has linear quotients. Indeed, monomial ideals with linear quotients are componentwise linear
[
30
, Theorem 8.2.15]
.

Recall that
I
has
linear quotients
if the set
\mathcal{G}(I)
can be ordered as
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
such that for each
i=2,\ldots,m
, the ideal
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})
is generated by variables.
In the following, for two monomials
u
and
v
, we set
u:v=u/\gcd(u,v)
. Notice that

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})=(u_{1}:u_{i},\dots,u_{i-1}:u_{i}).

Now, let
I=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}^{k_{i}}
be a monomial ideal of minimal intersection type. Let
\Delta(I)
be the simplicial complex whose facets are
F_{i}=\textup{supp}(P_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then the symbolic Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
is the vertex cover algebra
A(\Delta(I),w)
of the weighted simplicial complex
(\Delta(I),w)
, where
w:\mathcal{F}(\Delta(I))\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is the (weight) function
w(F_{i})=k_{i}
for all
i
, see
[
32
, Lemma 4.1]
. Here
\mathcal{F}(\Delta(I))
denotes the set of maximal faces of
\Delta(I)
. The
vertex cover algebra

A(\Delta,w)
of a weighted simplicial complex
\Delta
with the weight function
w:\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
was introduced in
[
32
]
, as the
S
-algebra generated by all monomials

{\bf x^{a}}t^{k}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}t^{k}
, such that

{\bf a}=(a_{1}\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
satisfies

\sum_{i\in F}a_{i}\geq kw(F)\ \ \ \textup{for all }F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta).

(10)

A vector
{\bf a}
satisfying (
10
) is called a
vertex cover
of
(\Delta,w)
of order
k
or simply a
k
-cover of
(\Delta,w)
.
A
k
-cover
{\bf a}
is called
indecomposable
, if one can not write
{\bf a}
as
{\bf a}={\bf a}_{1}+{\bf a}_{2}
, where
{\bf a}_{1}
is a
k_{1}
-cover and
{\bf a}_{2}
is a
k_{2}
-cover of
(\Delta,w)
with
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
.
Clearly, the set of the indecomposable covers of
(\Delta,w)
corresponds to the (unique) minimal monomial generating set of
A(\Delta,w)
as a
S
-algebra.

For a vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1}\ldots,a_{n})
we set
|{\bf a}|=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}
. We say that
I
has a
linear cover function
, if there are integers
c,d\geq 0
such that
|{\bf a}|=ck+d
for any indecomposable
k
-cover
{\bf a}
of
(\Delta(I),w)
and any
k
. If this is the case, then we say that
\nu_{I}(k)=ck+d
is the (linear)
cover function
of
I
.

By
[
32
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
is a finitely generated
S
-algebra. The minimal monomial generators
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}
of the
S
-algebra
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
are the monomials
{\bf x^{a}}t^{k}
for which
{\bf a}
is an indecomposable
k
-cover of
(\Delta(I),w)
. Let
T=S[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be a polynomial ring and let
\varphi:T\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
be the
S
-algebra map defined by
\varphi(y_{i})=w_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

Let
J=\textup{Ker}\,\varphi
. We say that
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to a monomial order
>
on
T
if any minimal monomial generator of
\textup{in}_{<}(J)
is of the form
vv^{\prime}
with
v\in S
of degree at most one and
v^{\prime}\in K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
.

We fix an order on the generators
w_{1}>\dots>w_{m}
of
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
as follows:

\textup{$ut^{p}>vt^{q}$ $\Longleftrightarrow$ $p>q$ or $p=q$ and $u>_{\textup{lex}}v$},

(11)

where
>_{\textup{lex}}
is the lex order on
S
induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Let
>_{\textup{rlex}}
be the reverse lex order induced by
y_{1}>\dots>y_{m}
. We fix a monomial order
>
on
T
as follows:

(\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}})(\prod_{j}x_{j}^{b_{j}})>(\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}^{\prime}})(\prod_{j}x_{j}^{b_{j}^{\prime}})

(12)

if and only if

(i)

either
\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}^{\prime}}
,

(ii)

or else
\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}}=\prod_{i}y_{i}^{a_{i}^{\prime}}
and
\prod_{j}x_{j}^{b_{j}}>_{\textup{lex}}\prod_{j}x_{j}^{b_{j}^{\prime}}
.

The following lemma is required for the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal of minimal intersection type which has a linear cover function, say
\nu_{I}(k)=ck+d
. Let
g\in R=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
be a monomial, and
k>0
be the integer with
\varphi(g)\in I^{(k)}t^{k}
. Then
\deg(\varphi(g))=(c+1)k+\deg(g)d
.

Proof.

Let
g=y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{s}}
, and
\varphi(y_{i_{j}})=\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}_{j}}t^{k_{j}}
for indecomposable
k_{j}
-covers
\textbf{a}_{j}
of
(\Delta(I),w)
, and integers
k_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,s
. Then
\varphi(g)=\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}_{1}+\cdots+\textbf{a}_{s}}t^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}}
, and from
\varphi(g)\in I^{(k)}t^{k}
we obtain
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}=k
. Moreover,
|\textbf{a}_{j}|=\nu_{I}(k_{j})=ck_{j}+d
for all
j
. Hence,

\deg(\varphi(g))=k+\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\textbf{a}_{j}|=k+\sum_{j=1}^{s}(ck_{j}+d)=k+ck+sd=(c+1)k+\deg(g)d,

as desired.

∎

The next result gives a criteria for the componentwise linearity of symbolic powers of monomial ideals of minimal intersection type.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal of minimal intersection type which has a linear cover function. If
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to the order given in
(
12
)
, then
I^{(k)}
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
I^{(k)}
is componentwise linear for all
k
.

Proof.

We adopt the notation introduced above. For a minimal monomial generator
u
of
I^{(k)}
, the element
ut^{k}\in A(\Delta(I),w)
has a presentation of the form
ut^{k}=(\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}_{1}}t^{q_{1}})\cdots(\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}_{s}}t^{q_{s}})
such that each
\textbf{a}_{j}
is an indecomposable
q_{j}
-cover of
(\Delta(I),w)
,
q_{j}>0
, and
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{s}=k
. Such presentation is the image of a monomial
y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{s}}
in
R=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
under the map
\varphi
with
\varphi(y_{i_{\ell}})=\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}_{\ell}}t^{q_{\ell}}
for all
\ell
. Then, there exists a presentation among all presentations of
ut^{k}
in
A(\Delta(I),w)
, whose preimage in
R
is the smallest term with respect to
>_{\textup{rlex}}
among the preimages of all presentations of
ut^{k}
. Indeed, the relations
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{s}=k
and
q_{i}>0
for all
i
show that any monomial in
R
which is mapped to
ut^{k}
is of degree at most
k
. So the number of monomials in
R
which are mapped to
ut^{k}
is finite and hence there is a minimal one among them with respect to
>_{\textup{rlex}}
. We denote this monomial by
f_{u}
.

First we show that
f_{u}\notin\textup{in}_{<}(J)
for any
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
.
Assume on the contrary that there exists a relation
h=f_{u}-vg\in J
, with
\textup{in}_{<}(h)=f_{u}
, where
v\in S
and
g\in R
are monomials. Then
ut^{k}=\varphi(f_{u})=v\varphi(g)
and
\varphi(g)\in I^{(k)}t^{k}
. Since
u
is a minimal generator of
I^{(k)}
, we conclude that
v=1
. Then
h=f_{u}-g\in J
which means that
\varphi(g)=\varphi(f_{u})=ut^{k}
. Hence
g
is also the preimage of
ut^{k}
under
\varphi
. Then by our choice of
f_{u}
we get
g>_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u}
. This contradicts to
\textup{in}_{<}(h)=f_{u}
. Consequently
f_{u}\notin\textup{in}_{<}(J)
, as desired.

We put an order on the minimal set of monomial generators of
I^{(k)}
as follows:
for
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
we set
u<u^{\prime}
if and only if
f_{u}<_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u^{\prime}}
. We show that

I^{(k)}
has linear quotients with respect to this order on its minimal generating set. Let
u_{1}<\cdots<u_{r}
be such an order on
\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
. Then
f_{u_{1}}<_{\textup{rlex}}\cdots<_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{r}}
. Fix an integer
2\leq i\leq r
and let
v\in(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})
be a monomial. Then
vu_{i}=v^{\prime}u_{j}
for some
j<i
and a monomial
v^{\prime}\in S
. Hence,
p=vf_{u_{i}}-v^{\prime}f_{u_{j}}\in J
with
\textup{in}_{<}(p)=vf_{u_{i}}
. By our assumption that
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition, there exists a monomial
v_{1}g_{1}\in\textup{in}_{<}(J)
with
v_{1}\in S
of degree at most one and
g_{1}\in R
such that
v_{1}g_{1}\mid vf_{u_{i}}
. First we show that
v_{1}\neq 1
. Indeed, if
v_{1}=1
, then
g_{1}\in\textup{in}_{<}(J)
and since
g_{1}\mid f_{u_{i}}
, we get
f_{u_{i}}\in\textup{in}_{<}(J)
, which is not possible, as shown above.
Hence,
v_{1}\neq 1
. Since
v_{1}
is of degree at most one, we obtain
v_{1}=x_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. So
x_{\ell}g_{1}\in\textup{in}_{<}(J)
. Let
p^{\prime}=x_{\ell}g_{1}-v_{2}g_{2}\in J
be a binomial with
\textup{in}_{<}(p^{\prime})=x_{\ell}g_{1}
, where
v_{2}\in S
and
g_{2}\in R
are monomials. From
g_{1}\mid f_{u_{i}}
we have
f_{u_{i}}=g_{1}g_{3}
with
g_{3}\in R
. Therefore,
g_{3}p^{\prime}=x_{\ell}f_{u_{i}}-v_{2}g_{2}g_{3}\in J
. Since
g_{1}>_{\textup{rlex}}g_{2}
we have
f_{u_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}g_{2}g_{3}
.
We consider the following two cases.

Case 1.

Suppose that
g_{2}g_{3}
is mapped to a minimal monomial generator, say
u_{s}t^{k}
of
I^{(k)}t^{k}
. Then
g_{2}g_{3}\geq_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{s}}
. From this and
f_{u_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}g_{2}g_{3}
we get
f_{u_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{s}}
. The definition of our order on
\mathcal{G}(I^{(k)})
implies that
u_{s}<u_{i}
. Moreover,

x_{\ell}u_{i}t^{k}=\varphi(x_{\ell}f_{u_{i}})=\varphi(v_{2}g_{2}g_{3})=v_{2}\varphi(g_{2}g_{3})=v_{2}u_{s}t^{k}.

Hence
x_{\ell}\in(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})
with
x_{\ell}
dividing
v
, as desired.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\varphi(g_{2}g_{3})
is not a minimal generator of
I^{(k)}t^{k}
. Then
\varphi(g_{2}g_{3})=v^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}t^{k})
for some monomial
v^{\prime\prime}\in S
with
v^{\prime\prime}\neq 1
and some

. Then
g_{2}g_{3}-v^{\prime\prime}f_{u_{s}}\in J
. We show that
g_{2}g_{3}>_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{s}}
. To this aim it is enough to show that
\deg(f_{u_{s}})<\deg(g_{2}g_{3})
. Suppose that this is not the case and
\deg(f_{u_{s}})\geq\deg(g_{2}g_{3})
. Since
I
has a linear cover function, by Lemma
2.1
, there exist integers
c,d\geq 0
such that
\deg(u_{s}t^{k})=(c+1)k+\deg(f_{u_{s}})d
and
\deg(\varphi(g_{2}g_{3}))=(c+1)k+\deg(g_{2}g_{3})d
. Hence,
\deg(u_{s}t^{k})\geq\deg(\varphi(g_{2}g_{3}))
.
This contradicts to
v^{\prime\prime}\neq 1
. Therefore,
g_{2}g_{3}>_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{s}}
. From
f_{u_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}g_{2}g_{3}
we obtain
f_{u_{i}}>_{\textup{rlex}}f_{u_{s}}
and hence

. Moreover,

x_{\ell}f_{u_{i}}-v_{2}v^{\prime\prime}f_{u_{s}}=(x_{\ell}f_{u_{i}}-v_{2}g_{2}g_{3})+v_{2}(g_{2}g_{3}-v^{\prime\prime}f_{u_{s}})\in J.

So we get

x_{\ell}u_{i}t^{k}=\varphi(x_{\ell}f_{u_{i}})=\varphi(v_{2}v^{\prime\prime}f_{u_{s}})=v_{2}v^{\prime\prime}u_{s}t^{k}.

Hence,
x_{\ell}u_{i}=v_{2}v^{\prime\prime}u_{s}
with
s<i
. Therefore,
x_{\ell}\in(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})
with
x_{\ell}
dividing
v
.

∎

3.
Symbolic powers of squarefree Veronese ideals

The goal of this section is to prove Conjectures
A
and
B
for any squarefree Veronese ideal.
Let
n
and
d
be positive integers. The
squarefree Veronese ideal
of degree
d
of
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the monomial ideal
I_{n,d}
generated by all squarefree monomials of degree

in

,

I_{n,d}\ =\ (x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}\ :\ 1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{d}\leq n).

Notice that
I_{n,d}=(0)
, whenever

. We remark that

is a polymatroidal ideal of minimal intersection type.

Let
I=I_{n,d}
. By
[
32
, Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.6]
it follows that

\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)=S[x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q+d-1}}t^{q}:\ 1\leq q\leq n-d+1,\ i_{1}<\cdots<i_{d+q-1}].

(13)

It is easy to see from (
13
) that a vector
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
is an indecomposable
q
-cover of
(\Delta(I),w)
if and only if
\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}}
is squarefree of degree

. Hence, Proposition
1.4
implies immediately

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
I=I_{n,d}
be the squarefree Veronese ideal of degree

. Then

for all

.

The following theorem plays a crucial role for our aim.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
I=I_{n,d}
be the squarefree Veronese ideal of degree
d
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition.

Proof.

We show that
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I)
satisfies the
x
-condition with respect to the order
>
introduced in (
12
). To this aim, let
fy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}\in\textup{in}_{<}(J)
be a minimal monomial generator with
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{r}
and
f\in S
a monomial. We need to show that
\deg(f)\leq 1
. We have a binomial relation of the form

b=fy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}-gy_{j_{1}}\cdots y_{j_{s}}\in J

with
g\in S
a monomial,
j_{1}\leq\dots\leq j_{s}
and
\textup{in}_{<}(b)=fy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}
. By the minimality of
\textup{in}_{<}(b)
we have
\gcd(f,g)=1
and
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}\cap\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}=\emptyset
. If
f=1
there is nothing to prove. Suppose that
\deg(f)\geq 1
. We prove that
f
is a variable and
r\leq 2
. Let
\varphi(y_{i_{\ell}})=u_{\ell}t^{q_{\ell}}
for all
\ell=1,\dots,r
and
\varphi(y_{j_{\ell}})=v_{\ell}t^{q_{\ell}^{\prime}}
for all
\ell=1,\dots,s
. By the definition of the order
>
given in (
12
) we have
r\geq s
. Now, we distinguish the two possible cases.

Case 1.
Let
r>s
. Since
\deg(f)\geq 1
, there exists a variable
x_{p}\mid f
. We claim that there exist distinct integers
\ell,h
such that
x_{p}\nmid u_{\ell}
and
x_{p}\nmid u_{h}
. Indeed, if this is not the case then
\deg_{x_{p}}(fu_{1}\cdots u_{r})\geq r
. Since

is a relation we have
fu_{1}\cdots u_{r}=gv_{1}\cdots v_{s}
. From

we conclude that
\deg_{x_{p}}(gv_{1}\cdots v_{s})\leq s<r
which is a contradiction. Hence there exist integers

such that

and
x_{p}\nmid u_{h}
. Then

and hence by (

The next result presents the generating degrees of each symbolic power of a squarefree Veronese ideal.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I=I_{n,d}
. Then, for all

(a)

We have

I^{(k)}\ =\ \sum_{m=1}^{k}(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1}+\dots+s_{m}=k-m\\
0\leq s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\leq n-d\end{subarray}}I_{n,d+s_{1}}\cdots I_{n,d+s_{m}}).

(b)

\beta_{0,\ell}(I^{(k)})\neq 0
if and only if
\ell=m(d-1)+k
, where
\lceil k/(n-d+1)\rceil\leq m\leq k
.

(c)

\omega(I^{(k)})=dk

Proof.

(a) From equation (
13
) it can be seen that

I^{(k)}t^{k}\ =\ \sum I_{n,d+s_{1}}\cdots I_{n,d+s_{m}}t^{(s_{1}+1)+\cdots+(s_{m}+1)},

where the sum is taken over all integers
1\leq m\leq k
and all integers
s_{i}\geq 0
such that
I_{n,d+s_{i}}\neq(0)
and
(s_{1}+1)+\cdots+(s_{m}+1)=k
. Thus

for all

and
s_{1}+\dots+s_{m}=k-m
.

(b) Suppose that
\beta_{0,\ell}(I^{(k)})\neq 0
. From (a) we know that
\ell
is the degree of a monomial in
I_{n,d+s_{1}}\cdots I_{n,d+s_{m}}
, for some integers

and

with
s_{1}+\dots+s_{m}=k-m
. Therefore,
\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(d+s_{i})=md+(k-m)=m(d-1)+k
. Since

for all

, we obtain

, which implies that

.

Conversely, let
\lceil k/(n-d+1)\rceil\leq m\leq k
be an integer and let
\ell=m(d-1)+k
.
The inequality

implies that
k-m\leq m(n-d)
. Therefore, there exist integers

such that
0\leq s_{i}\leq n-d
for all
i
and
s_{1}+\dots+s_{m}=k-m
. For such integers
s_{i}
we have

and
(0)\neq I_{n,d+s_{1}}\cdots I_{n,d+s_{m}}\subset I^{(k)}
. We may assume that
s_{1}\leq\cdots\leq s_{m}
. Set
u_{\ell}=\prod_{i=1}^{d+s_{\ell}}x_{i}
for

. Then we have

.
Hence, in order to show that
\beta_{0,\ell}(I^{(k)})\neq 0
, it is enough to show that
u
is a minimal generator of
I^{(k)}
.
Suppose by contradiction that this is not the case, and a monomial

strictly divides
u
. From (a) we know that

for integers

and
s^{\prime}_{i}\geq 0
with

. Moreover,

. Therefore,

.
Let

and

. We have

and
b_{i}\leq p
for all

, since

is the product of
p
squarefree monomials. So

\displaystyle p(d-1)+k\ =\ \deg(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d+s_{m-p+1}}b_{i}+\sum_{i=d+s_{m-p+1}+1}^{n}b_{i}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq\ p(d+s_{m-p+1})+\sum_{i=d+s_{m-p+1}+1}^{n}b_{i}.

Notice that

u=(\prod_{i=1}^{d+s_{1}}x_{i}^{m})(\prod_{i=d+s_{1}+1}^{d+s_{2}}x_{i}^{m-1})\cdots(\prod_{i=d+s_{m-2}+1}^{d+s_{m-1}}x_{i}^{2})(\prod_{i=d+s_{m-1}+1}^{d+s_{m}}x_{i}).

a_{i}=q

\displaystyle\sum_{i=d+s_{m-p+1}+1}^{n}b_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{i=d+s_{m-p+1}+1}^{n}a_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\ (p-1)(s_{m-p+2}-s_{m-p+1})+\cdots+3(s_{m-2}-s_{m-3})

From equations (

(c) follows from (b).

for all
1\leq\ell\leq s
. Moreover,
u_{1}\cdots u_{r}t^{q_{1}+\cdots+q_{r}}=gv_{1}\cdots v_{s}t^{q^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+q^{\prime}_{s}}
. Hence,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(q_{\ell}+d-1)=\deg(u_{1}\cdots u_{r})=\deg(g)+\deg(v_{1}\cdots v_{s})=\deg(g)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}(q^{\prime}_{\ell}+d-1).

From this and the equality
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}q_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}q^{\prime}_{\ell}
, we obtain
r(d-1)=\deg(g)+s(d-1)
. Notice that since
\textup{in}_{<}(b)=y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}
, we have
r\geq s
. Then
\deg(g)=(r-s)(d-1)
.
Since
u_{r}\mid u_{i}
for all
i
, we have
u_{r}^{r}\mid u_{1}\cdots u_{r}=gv_{1}\cdots v_{s}
. From the fact that all
v_{i}
’s are squarefree, we obtain
u_{r}^{r-s}\mid g
. Thus

(r-s)(q_{r}+d-1)=\deg(u_{r}^{r-s})\leq\deg(g)=(r-s)(d-1).

This would imply that
(r-s)q_{r}\leq 0
. Since
q_{r}>0
and
r\geq s
, we obtain
r=s
. Thus
\deg(g)=(r-s)(d-1)=0
, which means
g=1
and
b=y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}-y_{j_{1}}\cdots y_{j_{r}}
. Moreover,
u_{r}^{r}\mid v_{1}\cdots v_{r}
, which implies that
u_{r}\mid v_{i}
for all
i
. In particular,
u_{r}\mid v_{r}
. So we conclude that
v_{r}>_{\textup{lex}}u_{r}
and
q^{\prime}_{r}+(d-1)=\deg(v_{r})\geq\deg(u_{r})=q_{r}+(d-1)
. Thus
q^{\prime}_{r}\geq q_{r}
and by (
11
) we have
y_{j_{r}}>y_{i_{r}}
. Hence,

y_{j_{1}}\cdots y_{j_{r}}>_{\textup{rlex}}y_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{r}}
. This means that
\textup{in}_{<}(b)=y_{j_{1}}\cdots y_{j_{r}}
, a contradiction.

∎

Question 3.8
.

When does the defining ideal of
\mathcal{R}_{s}(I_{n,d})
have a quadratic Gröbner basis for some monomial order
<
?

4.
Matching-matroidal ideals

In this section, we consider matroidal ideals attached to matching matroids and we prove Conjectures
A
and
B
for some special families of such ideals.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{t}\}
be a finite collection of non-empty subsets of
[n]
. A
transversal
of
\mathcal{A}
is the image of an injective map
\psi:[t]\rightarrow[n]
with the property that
\psi(j)\in A_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,t
. It is well-known that the collection of all transversals of
\mathcal{A}
is the set of bases of a matroid, called a
transversal matroid
.
Next, we provide a short algebraic proof of this fact.

Given
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
we put
{\bf x^{b}}=x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{b_{n}}
. For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
and a vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we define the ideal
I^{\leq{\bf a}}
as the monomial ideal generated by all monomials
{\bf x^{b}}\in I
such that
{\bf b}\leq{\bf a}
, that is
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
for all
i
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
I^{\leq\bf a}
is polymatroidal too.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{\leq{\bf a}})
with
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
. Since
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
I
is polymatroidal, there exists
j
with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. We claim that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I^{\leq{\bf a}})
. Indeed,
\deg_{x_{s}}(x_{j}(u/x_{i}))\leq\deg_{x_{s}}(u)\leq a_{s}
for all integers
s\neq j
and
\deg_{x_{j}}(x_{j}(u/x_{i}))=\deg_{x_{j}}(u)+1\leq\deg_{x_{j}}(v)\leq a_{j}
. This shows that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I^{\leq{\bf a}})
. Hence
I^{\leq{\bf a}}
is polymatroidal.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma
4.1
we have

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{t}\}
be a collection of non-empty subsets of
[n]
. The collection of transversals of
\mathcal{A}
is the set of bases of a matroid.

Proof.

Let
J=P_{A_{1}}\cdots P_{A_{t}}
. Since each
P_{A_{i}}
is polymatroidal,
J
is polymatroidal too. Let
I=J^{\leq{\bf 1}}
where
{\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
. By Lemma
4.1
it follows that
I
is a matroidal ideal. Let
B
be the set of the transversals of
\mathcal{A}
. We claim that the set consisting of the supports of the minimal monomial generators of
I
coincides with
B
. This will imply the assertion.

A minimal monomial generator of
J
is of the form
u=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}
with
i_{j}\in A_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,t
. This monomial
u
belongs to
\mathcal{G}(I)
if and only if
u
is squarefree. That is, if and only if
i_{j}\neq i_{\ell}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq t
. So the map
\psi_{u}:[t]\rightarrow[n]
defined by
\psi_{u}(j)=i_{j}
is injective and
\psi_{u}(j)\in A_{j}
for all
j
. Hence
\textup{Im}\,\psi=\textup{supp}(u)\in B
. Conversely, let
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\dots,i_{t}^{\prime}\}\in B
be a transversal. Then
x_{i_{j}^{\prime}}\in P_{A_{j}}
for all
j
and the monomial
u^{\prime}=x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{i_{t}^{\prime}}
is squarefree. This shows that
u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
, as desired.

∎

Next, we provide a different description, due to Edmonds and Fulkerson, of the matroids considered in this section.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
with the edge set
E(G)
. A
k
-matching
of
G
is a collection of
k
edges of
G
which are pairwise disjoint. The
matching number
of
G
, denoted by
\nu(G)
, is the maximum size of a matching of
G
.

We say that a subset
B\subseteq[n]

meets
a matching
M
, if
B\subseteq\bigcup_{e\in M}e
. Now, let
A\subseteq[n]
be non-empty. Then

\{B:\ B\subseteq A,\ B\textup{ meets}\ M\textup{ for some}\ \textup{matching}\ M\ \textup{of}\ G\}

is the collection of independent sets of a matroid. We denote this matroid by
\textup{Match}(G;A)
and call it a
matching-matroid
. This fact was proved by Edmonds and Fulkerson (see
[
55
, Theorem 1 on page 246]
).

When
A=[n]
, we denote
\textup{Match}(G;[n])
simply by
\textup{Match}(G)
. An algebraic proof that
\textup{Match}(G)
is a matroid can be found for instance in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
. See also
[
14
, Theorem 1.7]
,
[
18
, Theorem 1.8]
and
[
39
, Theorem 4.3]
for some related algebraic results.

We say that a squarefree monomial ideal
I
is
matching-matroidal
if the supports of the minimal monomial generators of
I
are the bases of a matching-matroid.

A deep theorem of Edmonds and Fulkerson shows that a matroid is transversal if and only if it is a matching-matroid (see
[
55
, Theorem 2 on page 248]
). Let
{\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
. For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, we call
I^{\leq{\bf 1}}
the
squarefree part
of
I
.

Using the theorem of Edmonds and Fulkerson combined with Corollary
4.2
we obtain the following algebraic characterization.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then
I
is matching-matroidal if and only if
I=(P_{1}\cdots P_{t})^{\leq{\bf 1}}
for some monomial prime ideals
P_{1},\dots,P_{t}
.

At the moment we are not able to prove Conjectures
A
and
B
for all matching-matroidal ideals. Therefore, in what follows we restrict our attention to a special but wide family of matching-matroidal ideals.

Given a non-empty subset
A
of
[n]
we denote by
I_{A,d}
the squarefree Veronese ideal of degree
d
in the polynomial ring
S_{A}=K[x_{i}:\ i\in A]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{t}\}
be a finite collection of non-empty subsets of
[n]
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is of
Veronese type
, if whenever we have
A_{i}\cap A_{j}\neq\emptyset
then
A_{i}=A_{j}
. Let
I
be the matching-matroidal ideal attached to a set
\mathcal{A}
of Veronese type, that is
I=(P_{A_{1}}\cdots P_{A_{t}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}
. We say that
I
is a
matching-matroidal ideal of Veronese type
. Notice that for such an ideal
I
we have
I=(P_{B_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots P_{B_{m}}^{d_{m}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}
, where
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
. Since the ideals
P_{B_{i}}^{d_{i}}
have pairwise disjoint supports and
(P_{B_{i}}^{d_{i}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}=I_{B_{i},d_{i}}
we obtain that

I=(P_{B_{1}}^{d_{1}}\cdots P_{B_{m}}^{d_{m}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}=(P_{B_{1}}^{d_{1}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}\cdots(P_{B_{m}}^{d_{m}})^{\leq{\bf 1}}=I_{B_{1},d_{1}}\cdots I_{B_{m},d_{m}}.

Hence, we have shown

Proposition 4.4
.

A monomial ideal
I
is a matching-matroidal ideal of Veronese type if and only if
I
is the product of squarefree Veronese ideals with pairwise disjoint supports.

The following remark follows easily from the definition of symbolic powers.

Remark 4.5
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset S
be monomial ideals with pairwise disjoint supports. Then
(I_{1}\cdots I_{t})^{(k)}=I_{1}^{(k)}\cdots I_{t}^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Now, we verify Conjectures
A
,
B
for matching-matroidal ideals of Veronese type.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a matching-matroidal ideal of Veronese type. Then,
I^{(k)}
has linear quotients and
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

For the proof of this theorem, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\subset S
be monomial ideals with pairwise disjoint supports. Then the following statements hold.

(a)

Suppose that
I_{j}
is componentwise linear for all
j=1,\dots,t
. Then
I_{1}\cdots I_{t}
is componentwise linear.

(b)

Suppose that
I_{j}
have linear quotients for all
j=1,\dots,t
. Then
I_{1}\cdots I_{t}
has linear quotients.

(c)

Suppose that
\textup{reg}\,I_{j}^{(k)}=a_{j}k
for all
j=1,\dots,t
and all
k\geq 1
. Then
\textup{reg}\,(I_{1}\cdots I_{t})^{(k)}=(a_{1}+\dots+a_{t})k
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

To prove the statements (a), (b) and (c), proceeding by induction on
t
, it is enough to prove the case
t=2
, with the base case
t=1
being trivial. Therefore, it is enough to consider two monomial ideals
I,J
of
S
with
\textup{supp}(I)\cap\textup{supp}(J)=\emptyset
.

(a) It is clear that for all
d
we have

(IJ)_{\langle d\rangle}\ =\ \sum_{i+j=d}I_{\langle i\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}\ =\ \sum_{i=0}^{d}I_{\langle d-i\rangle}J_{\langle i\rangle}.

We claim that for all
j=0,\dots,d
the ideal
H_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}I_{\langle d-i\rangle}J_{\langle i\rangle}
has a
d
-linear resolution. Since
H_{d}=(IJ)_{\langle d\rangle}
this will yield the assertion. To prove our claim we proceed by finite induction. For
j=0
,
H_{0}=I_{\langle d\rangle}
has
d
-linear resolution by assumption. Now let
j>0
. Notice that
H_{j}=H_{j-1}+I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}
. By induction
H_{j-1}
has
d
-linear resolution. Furthermore
I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}
has
d
-linear resolution for it is the product of monomial ideals with disjoint supports having
(d-j)
-linear and
j
-linear resolution, respectively. It follows from
[
21
, Corollary 2.4]
that
H_{j}=H_{j-1}+I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}
is a Betti splitting. Now, using
[
7
, Proposition 1.8]
we have that
H_{j}
has
d
-linear resolution if and only if
H_{j-1}\cap I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}
has a
(d+1)
-linear resolution. Notice that
I_{\langle d\rangle}\subset I_{\langle d-1\rangle}\subset\cdots\subset I_{\langle d-j\rangle}
and
J_{\langle 0\rangle}\supset J_{\langle 1\rangle}\supset\cdots\supset J_{\langle j\rangle}
. Taking into account that the sum of monomial ideals distributes over intersections, we obtain that

\displaystyle H_{j-1}\cap I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}\

\displaystyle=\ (\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}I_{\langle d-i\rangle}J_{\langle i\rangle})\cap I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}\ =\ \sum_{i=0}^{j-1}[(I_{\langle d-i\rangle}J_{\langle i\rangle})\cap(I_{\langle d-j\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle})]

\displaystyle=\ \phantom{,}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}[(I_{\langle d-i\rangle}\cap I_{\langle d-j\rangle})(J_{\langle i\rangle}\cap J_{\langle j\rangle})]\ =\ \sum_{i=0}^{j-1}I_{\langle d-i\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}

\displaystyle=\ (\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}I_{\langle d-i\rangle})J_{\langle j\rangle}\ =\ I_{\langle d-j+1\rangle}J_{\langle j\rangle}

has
(d+1)
-linear resolution. Hence
(IJ)_{\langle d\rangle}
has
d
-linear resolution for all
d
, as desired.

(b) Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
be linear quotients orders of
I
and
J
, respectively. Since
\textup{supp}(I)\cap\textup{supp}(J)=\emptyset
, we have
\mathcal{G}(IJ)=\{u_{i}v_{j}:\ i=1,\dots,m,j=1,\dots,\ell\}
. We claim that

u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{1},\dots,u_{m}v_{1},\,u_{1}v_{2},\dots,u_{m}v_{2},\,\dots,\,u_{1}v_{\ell},\dots,u_{m}v_{\ell}

is a linear quotients order of
IJ
. To this end, consider two generators
u_{i}v_{j}>u_{p}v_{q}
. Then, either
j<q
or
j=q
and
i<p
.

Suppose that
j<q
. Since
J
has linear quotients, there exists
r<q
such that
v_{r}:v_{q}=x_{s}
divides
v_{j}:v_{q}
. Since
u_{i}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{q}=(u_{i}:u_{p})(v_{j}:v_{q})
it follows that
x_{s}
divides
u_{i}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{q}
too. Consider the monomial
u_{p}v_{r}
. Notice that
u_{p}v_{r}>u_{p}v_{q}
because
r<q
. Moreover
u_{p}v_{r}:u_{p}v_{q}=v_{r}:v_{q}=x_{s}
divides
u_{i}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{q}
, as desired.

Suppose now
j=q
, then
u_{i}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{q}=u_{i}:u_{p}
. Since
I
has linear quotients, there exists
r<p
such that
u_{r}:u_{p}=x_{s}
divides
u_{i}:u_{p}
. Then
u_{r}v_{j}>u_{p}v_{j}
and
u_{r}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{j}=x_{s}
divides
u_{i}v_{j}:u_{p}v_{q}=u_{i}:u_{p}
, as desired.

(c) By Remark
4.5
we have
(IJ)^{(k)}=I^{(k)}J^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
. By the assumption,
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=ak
and
\textup{reg}\,J^{(k)}=bk
for all
k\geq 1
. Since
\textup{supp}(I^{(k)})\cap\textup{supp}(J^{(k)})=\emptyset
, we have
\textup{reg}\,(IJ)^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}J^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}+\textup{reg}\,J^{(k)}=(a+b)k
for all
k\geq 1
.

∎

Now, we are in the position to prove Theorem
4.6
.

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

By Proposition
4.4
,
I=I_{B_{1},d_{1}}\cdots I_{B_{m},d_{m}}
with
B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
. Using Remark
4.5
we have
I^{(k)}=I_{B_{1},d_{1}}^{(k)}\cdots I_{B_{m},d_{m}}^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
with
\textup{supp}(I_{B_{i},d_{i}}^{(k)})\cap\textup{supp}(I_{B_{j},d_{j}}^{(k)})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq m
. The assertion now follows by combining Lemma
4.7
(b)-(c) with Corollary
3.3
.

∎

5.
Comparison between ordinary and symbolic powers

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Notice that
I^{k}\subseteq I^{(k)}
. However, in general
I^{(k)}
is a much larger ideal compared to
I^{k}
. In this section, we aim to classify the polymatroidal ideals
I
for which
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Firstly, we consider the squarefree case, i.e.,
I
is matroidal.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal. We say that
I
is
König
if
I
contains a monomial regular sequence of length
\textup{height}(I)
. We say that
I
is
packed
if every ideal obtained from
I
by setting a (possibly empty) subset of the variables equal to
0
and a disjoint (possibly empty) subset of the variables equal to
1
is König.

A famous conjecture posed by Conforti and Cornuéjols
[
5
]
has been restated equivalently in algebraic terms by Gitler, Villarreal and others
[
23
,
24
]
as follows.

Conjecture 5.1
.

A squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
satisfies
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
if and only if
I
is packed.

This conjecture is sometimes referred as the
Packing problem
. It is known that Conjecture
5.1
holds for any squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree two, that is for any edge ideal, see
[
23
, Proposition 4.27]
. Moreover, the following implication is well-known.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal. Suppose that
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Then
I
is packed.

Following
[
46
]
we say that a (squarefree) monomial ideal
I\subset S
is
vertex splittable
if
I
can be obtained by the following recursive procedure.

(i)

If
u
is a monomial and
I=(u)
,
I=(0)
or
I=S
, then
I
is vertex splittable.

(ii)

If there is a variable
x_{i}
and vertex splittable ideals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
such that
I=x_{i}I_{1}+I_{2}
,
i\notin\textup{supp}(I_{2})
,
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}(I)
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{G}(x_{i}I_{1})
and
\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
, then
I
is vertex splittable.

The following result was shown in
[
6
, Proposition 2]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

(
Componentwise
)
polymatroidal ideals are vertex splittable.

By
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
any polymatroidal ideal satisfies the so-called
dual exchange property
, namely: for all
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)<\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
there exists
j
with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)>\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
.

For the proof of Theorem
5.5
we need also the following splitting decomposition which was proved in
[
17
, Lemma 5.6]
, (see also the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
). We regard the zero ideal
(0)
as a polymatroidal ideal.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal generated in degree
\alpha(I)=d\geq 2
. For any
i\in\textup{supp}(I)
there exist polymatroidal ideals
I_{1},I_{2}\subset S
with
\alpha(I_{1})=d-1
such that
I=x_{i}I_{1}+I_{2}
,
i\notin\textup{supp}(I_{2})
and
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
.

For a graded ideal
I\subset S
, we denote by
\mu(I)=\dim_{K}(I/\mathfrak{m}I)
the minimal number of generators of
I
. Notice that if
I
is a monomial ideal, then
\mu(I)=|\mathcal{G}(I)|
.

Now, we establish Conjecture
5.1
for matroidal ideals.

Theorem 5.5
.

For a matroidal ideal
I\subset S
the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

I=P_{F_{1}}\cdots P_{F_{t}}
with
F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

(b)

I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

(c)

I
is packed.

Proof.

The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) follows from Remark
4.5
together with the fact that
P^{(k)}=P^{k}
for all
k
, if
P
is a monomial prime ideal. The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c) follows from Proposition
5.2
.

Let us prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(a). We proceed by induction on
\alpha(I)=d\geq 1
. If
d=1
there is nothing to prove. Now, let
d>1
. Next, we proceed by induction on
\mu(I)\geq 1
. If
\mu(I)=1
, then
I
is principal and (a) holds. Let
\mu(I)>1
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
I
is fully-supported. Applying Lemma
5.4
we can write
I=x_{1}I_{1}+I_{2}
with
I_{1},I_{2}
matroidal ideals such that
\alpha(I_{1})=d-1
,
1\notin\textup{supp}(I_{1})\cup\textup{supp}(I_{2})
and
I_{2}\subseteq I_{1}
. Since by assumption
I
is packed, it follows that putting
x_{1}=0
and
x_{1}=1
in
I
, the resulting squarefree monomial ideals are also packed. Putting
x_{1}=0
in
I
we obtain that
I_{2}
is packed. Whereas, putting
x_{1}=1
we obtain that
I_{1}+I_{2}=I_{1}
is packed. Here we used that
I_{2}\subseteq I_{1}
.

If
I_{2}=(0)
, since
\alpha(I_{1})<\alpha(I)
and
I_{1}
is packed, by induction we have that
I_{1}=P_{G_{2}}\cdots P_{G_{d}}
and so
I=x_{1}I_{1}=P_{\{1\}}P_{G_{2}}\cdots P_{G_{d}}
is of the required form.

Now, suppose that
I_{2}\neq(0)
. Then
\alpha(I_{2})=d
and
\mu(I_{2})<\mu(I)
. Recall that
\alpha(I_{1})<\alpha(I)
. By induction on
\alpha(I)
and
\mu(I)
we have that
I_{1}=P_{G_{1}}\cdots P_{G_{d-1}}
and
I_{2}=P_{F_{1}}\cdots P_{F_{d}}
with
G_{i}\cap G_{j}=\emptyset
and
F_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

We claim that
\textup{supp}(I_{2})=[n]\setminus\{1\}
. Otherwise assume that
i\notin\textup{supp}(I_{2})
for some
2\leq i\leq n
. Then,
i\in\textup{supp}(I_{1})
because

is fully-supported. Hence, there is a monomial
v\in\mathcal{G}(x_{1}I_{1})\subset\mathcal{G}(I)
divided by
x_{1}x_{i}
. Let

. We have
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)<\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
. By the dual exchange property we can find an integer
j

\deg_{x_{j}}(u)>\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
w=x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Since
i\in\textup{supp}(w)
and

we have
w\notin I_{2}
. Hence
w\in x_{1}I_{1}
. However this is impossible because
x_{1}\nmid w
as
u\in\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
. This contradiction proves our claim.

From
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
we have
P_{F_{1}}\cdots P_{F_{d}}\subset P_{G_{1}}\cdots P_{G_{d-1}}\subset P_{G_{i}}
for all
i
. Hence,
F_{\ell_{i}}\subseteq G_{i}
for some
\ell_{i}
. If
i\neq j
, then
\ell_{i}\neq\ell_{j}
, otherwise,
F_{\ell_{i}}\subseteq G_{i}\cap G_{j}
, which contradicts to
G_{i}\cap G_{j}=\emptyset
. Thus, after a relabeling we have
F_{1}\subseteq G_{1},\ldots,F_{d-1}\subseteq G_{d-1}
. Since
\textup{supp}(I_{2})=[n]\setminus\{1\}
, we have
F_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup F_{d}=[n]\setminus\{1\}
. Hence,
G_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}(G_{i}\cap F_{j})
. Notice that for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, we have
G_{i}\cap F_{j}\subseteq G_{i}\cap G_{j}
. So
G_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset
for
j\neq i
with
1\leq j\leq d-1
. Therefore,

G_{i}\subseteq(G_{i}\cap F_{i})\cup(G_{i}\cap F_{d})=F_{i}\cup(G_{i}\cap F_{d}),

(18)

for all
i
. We claim that
F_{d}
intersects at most one of the sets
G_{1},\ldots,G_{d-1}
. Suppose that this is not the case and let
r\in F_{d}\cap G_{i_{1}}
and
s\in F_{d}\cap G_{i_{2}}
with
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
. Then we can find
u\in\mathcal{G}(I_{1})
with
x_{r}x_{s}\mid u
. Hence
x_{1}u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Choose
v\in\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
. Since
\deg_{x_{1}}(x_{1}u)>\deg_{x_{1}}(v)
, there exists an integer
t
such that
\deg_{x_{t}}(x_{1}u)<\deg_{x_{t}}(v)
and
x_{t}(x_{1}u)/x_{1}=x_{t}u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Thus
x_{t}u\in\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
. Since
x_{r}x_{s}\mid u
, this means that at least one between
r
and
s
belongs to some
F_{\ell}
with
\ell\neq d
. Then we get
F_{\ell}\cap F_{d}\neq\emptyset
, a contradiction. So the claim is proved and then we may assume that
G_{i}\cap F_{d}=\emptyset
for
2\leq i\leq d-1
. From this and (
18
) we obtain
G_{i}\subseteq F_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq d-1
. Since we had the other inclusions as well, we get
G_{i}=F_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq d-1
. Moreover,
F_{1}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq F_{1}\cup F_{d}
. Hence

I=x_{1}I_{1}+I_{2}=(x_{1}P_{G_{1}}+P_{F_{1}}P_{F_{d}})P_{F_{2}}\cdots P_{F_{d-1}}.

Putting
x_{i}=1
in
I
for all
i\in F_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup F_{d-1}
we obtain the ideal
J=x_{1}P_{G_{1}}+P_{F_{1}}P_{F_{d}}
which is packed. Note that
J=I(G)
is an edge ideal. By
[
23
, Proposition 4.27]
it follows that
G
is bipartite. Since
J=I(P)
, where
P=(x_{i}:i\in F_{1}\cup F_{d}\cup\{1\})
, by
[
36
, Corollary 3.2]
the ideal
J
is again matroidal. Now, using
[
40
, Theorem 2.3]
the only matroidal edge ideals are edge ideals of complete multipartite graphs. Since
G
is bipartite, it follows that
G
is a complete bipartite graph. Hence
J=P_{A}P_{B}
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Finally
I=P_{A}P_{B}P_{F_{2}}\cdots P_{F_{d-1}}
and the sets
A,B,F_{2},\dots,F_{d-1}
are pairwise disjoint because

is squarefree.

∎

Now we turn to the non-squarefree case. Non-squarefree polymatroidal ideals
I
satisfying
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
, abound, as we show next. We begin our discussion by stating an elementary lemma which gives a condition ensuring that
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be an ideal with
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
. Then
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
and
I^{k}S_{\mathfrak{m}}\cap S=I^{k}
we have
I^{(k)}\subseteq I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. The assertion follows because the opposite inclusion always holds.

∎

An immediate consequence of this result is given in the following corollary which allows to construct, starting with any polymatroidal ideal, several polymatroidal ideals whose ordinary and symbolic powers coincide.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
I,J\subset S
be polymatroidal ideals such that
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
. Then
(IJ)^{(k)}=(IJ)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.6
it is enough to show that
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(IJ)
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(I)
we have
\textup{depth\,}(S/I)=0
. By the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula
\textup{proj\,dim}(S/I)=n
. We have the following isomorphisms

\textup{Tor}^{S}_{n-1}(K,I)\cong\textup{Tor}^{S}_{n}(K,S/I)\cong H_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};S/I)\cong((I:\mathfrak{m})/I)\,e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n},

where
H_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n};S/I)
denotes the
n
th Koszul homology of
S/I
with respect to the sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and each
e_{i}
has degree one. Let
\alpha(I)=d
,
\alpha(J)=d^{\prime}
. Since
I
has a
d
-linear resolution, it follows that
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{n-1}(K,I)_{j}=0
if and only if
j\neq n+d-1
. Since
\textup{proj\,dim}(S/I)=n
it follows that
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{n-1}(K,I)_{n+d-1}\neq 0
. Due to the above isomorphisms and since
e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{n}
has degree
n
, we deduce that
((I:\mathfrak{m})/I)_{d-1}\neq 0
. Hence, we can find a monomial
u\in(I:\mathfrak{m})\setminus I
of degree
d-1
. Clearly
I:(u)=\mathfrak{m}
. Now let
v\in\mathcal{G}(J)
. Then
vu\notin IJ
because
\deg(uv)=d+d^{\prime}-1
but
\alpha(IJ)=d+d^{\prime}
. Hence the ideal
IJ:(uv)
is proper and contains
\mathfrak{m}
. This shows that
IJ:(uv)=\mathfrak{m}
and so
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}(IJ)
, as desired.

∎

In view of Proposition
5.7
, it is hopeless to give a full classification of non-squarefree polymatroidal ideals whose ordinary and symbolic powers coincide.

Next, we provide more nice classes of non-squarefree polymatroidal ideals whose ordinary and symbolic powers coincide.

Recall that a
transversal
polymatroidal ideal is defined as the product of any arbitrary number of monomial prime ideals.

Let
u=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}\in S
be a monomial of degree
d
, with
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{d}\leq n
. The
principal Borel ideal
generated by
u
, is defined as the monomial ideal
B(u)\subset S
such that

\mathcal{G}(B(u))=\{x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}:\ j_{1}\leq i_{1},\,\dots,\,j_{d}\leq i_{d}\}.

It is well-known that
B(u)
is a polymatroidal ideal. Such an ideal is
strongly stable
. That is, it has the following property: for all monomials
w\in B(u)
and all
i<j\leq n
with
x_{j}\mid w
we have
x_{i}(w/x_{j})\in B(u)
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Consider the following families of polymatroidal ideals.

(a)

Polymatroidal ideals generated in degree
2
which are not squarefree.

(b)

Transversal polymatroidal ideals.

(c)

Principal Borel ideals.

Then
I^{(k)}=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
for any ideal
I
belonging to one of the families
(a)-(c)
. In particular,
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
and
I^{(k)}
has linear quotients, and so it is componentwise linear, for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
I
be an ideal belonging to one of the families (a)-(c). Without loss of generality, we may assume that
I
is fully-supported.

(a) Since
I
is not squarefree, up to a relabeling we can assume that
x_{1}^{2}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. We claim that
I:(x_{1})=\mathfrak{m}
. Then the assertion follows Lemma
5.6
. Clearly
x_{1}\in I:(x_{1})
. Let

. Since

. If
q=1
, then
x_{p}\in I:(x_{1})
. Suppose

\deg_{x_{q}}(x_{p}x_{q})>\deg_{x_{q}}(x_{1}^{2})
. Thus, by the exchange property

, as desired.

(b) Let
I=P_{1}P_{2}\cdots P_{t}
be a transversal polymatroidal ideal, with each

[
36
, Section 3]
, we define the

of

and such that

with the graded maximal ideal

. Since the ideals

(c) Let

∎

6.
Componentwise linearity of symbolic powers

In this final section, we determine several families of polymatroidal ideals which satisfy Conjectures
A
and
B
.

A graph
G
is called a
complete multipartite graph
if there exists a partition of the vertex set
V(G)=A_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup A_{m}
with
m\geq 2
and
A_{i}\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,\dots,m
such that
E(G)=\{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}:\ x_{i}\in A_{i},\,x_{j}\in A_{j},\,i\neq j\}
. If
m=2
, then
G
is called a
complete bipartite graph
.

A matroidal ideal generated in degree two is the edge ideal
I=I(G)
of some graph
G
. It follows from
[
40
, Theorem 2.3]
that
G
is a complete multipartite graph. Using this fact, in the following proposition we show that Conjectures
A
and
B
hold for such polymatroidal ideals.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a matroidal ideal generated in degree two. Then
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}=2k
and
I^{(k)}
is componentwise polymatroidal for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

We have
I=I(G)
, where
G
is a complete multipartite graph. Now, let
V(G)=A_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup A_{m}
be the vertex partition of
G
as described before. It follows that
G^{c}
is the disjoint union of the complete graphs on the vertex sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
. Thus
G^{c}
is a block graph. It is shown in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 2.3(a)]
that
(I^{(k)})_{\langle d\rangle}
has linear quotients with respect to the lex order induced by any perfect elimination order of
G^{c}
in the sense of Dirac
[
11
]
. Notice furthermore that any order of the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a perfect elimination order of
G^{c}
. Hence, combining this with
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
we obtain that
I^{(k)}
is componentwise polymatroidal for all
k
. Finally, by
[
19
, Corollary 2.4]
we have
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}=2k
for all
k
.

∎

As a consequence of this result and Theorem
5.8
(a) we have

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal generated in degree two. Then
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
and
I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
.

We define the
greatest common divisor
of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
as the monomial
\gcd(I)=\gcd(u:\ u\in\mathcal{G}(I))
. It is clear that any monomial ideal
I\subset S
can be written as
\gcd(I)J
for a unique monomial ideal
J\subset S
with
\gcd(J)=1
. Notice furthermore that
\gcd(I)=1
if and only if
\textup{height}(I)>1
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and let
u\in S
be a monomial. Then,
(uI)^{(k)}=(u)^{k}I^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\textup{reg}(uI)^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}+\deg(u)k
.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that
\textup{Ass}(uI)=\textup{Ass}(I)\cup\{(x_{i}):\ x_{i}\mid u\}
. To this end, consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/((uI):(u))\rightarrow S/(uI)\rightarrow S/(uI,u)\rightarrow 0.

(19)

Notice that
(uI):(u)=I
using
[
30
, Proposition 1.2.2]
, and
(uI,u)=(u)
. Then, the exact sequence (
19
) implies that
\textup{Ass}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}(uI)\subseteq\textup{Ass}(I)\cup\textup{Ass}((u))
. We have
\textup{Ass}((u))=\{(x_{i}):\ x_{i}\mid u\}
. Since
uI\subseteq P_{\{i\}}
for each
i
with
x_{i}\mid u
, and
\textup{height}(P_{\{i\}})=1
, it follows that
\textup{Ass}((u))\subseteq\textup{Ass}(uI)
. The claim is proved.

Now, assume for the moment that
\gcd(I)=1
. Later we discuss the general case. Given a monomial prime ideal
P
we denote by
u_{P}
the monomial of
S
obtained from
u
by applying the substitutions
x_{i}\mapsto 1
for
x_{i}\notin P
. Let
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. Using formula (
5
) and our claim we have

\displaystyle(uI)^{(k)}\

\displaystyle=\ \bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(uI)}((uI)(P))^{k}

\displaystyle=\ (\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}((u))}((uI)(P))^{k})\cap(\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}((uI)(P))^{k}).

Let
P\in\textup{Ass}((u))
. Then
P=(x_{i})
for some
x_{i}\mid u
. Since
\gcd(I)=1
, we get that
((uI)(P))^{k}=(x_{i}^{ka_{i}})
. Whereas, if
P\in\textup{Ass}(I)
, then
((uI)(P))^{k}=(u_{P}^{k})(I(P))^{k}

\displaystyle(uI)^{(k)}\

\displaystyle=\ (\bigcap_{x_{i}\mid u}(x_{i}^{ka_{i}}))\cap(\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(u_{P}^{k})(I(P))^{k})\ =\ (u^{k})\cap(\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(u_{P}^{k})(I(P))^{k})

\displaystyle=\ \bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(u^{k})\cap((u_{P}^{k})(I(P))^{k}).

(20)

Notice that for any

with

. Therefore, since
(I(P))^{k}
is a monomial ideal whose support is contained in

\displaystyle(u^{k})\cap((u_{P}^{k})(I(P))^{k})

\displaystyle=\ (u_{P}^{k}v)\cap((u_{P})^{k}(I(P))^{k})

(21)

\displaystyle=\ (v)[(u_{P})^{k}\cap((u_{P})^{k}(I(P))^{k})]

\displaystyle=\ (v)(u_{P})^{k}(I(P))^{k}\ =\ (u)^{k}(I(P))^{k}.

Combining (

(uI)^{(k)}=\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(u^{k})(I(P))^{k}=(u^{k})(\bigcap_{P\in\textup{Ass}(I)}(I(P))^{k})=(u)^{k}I^{(k)}.

Suppose now
\gcd(I)\neq 1
. Then
I=vJ
for some monomial

and a monomial ideal
J\subset S
with
\gcd(J)=1
. By what we proved before, we have
I^{(k)}=(v)^{k}J^{(k)}
and
(uI)^{(k)}=((uv)J)^{(k)}=(uv)^{k}J^{(k)}
. Hence
(uI)^{(k)}=(u)^{k}(v)^{k}J^{(k)}=(u)^{k}I^{(k)}
.

∎

The following elementary result is an immediate consequence of Proposition
6.3
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and let

be a monomial. Suppose that
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
and that

has linear quotients for all

\textup{reg}\,(uI)^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,(uI)^{k}
and
(uI)^{(k)}
has linear quotients for all

.

A polymatroidal ideal
I
satisfies the
strong exchange property
if for all

, all

with
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
and
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)

.

Given an integer

, the ideal of
Veronese type

\mathcal{G}(I_{n,d,{\bf a}})\ =\ \big\{x_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{b_{n}}\ :\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}=d\ \textup{and}\ b_{i}\leq a_{i}\ \textup{for}\ i=1,\dots,n\big\}.

.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let

If

a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=d+1
. Then,

I=(x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}x_{3}^{a_{3}-1},x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}-1}x_{3}^{a_{3}},x_{1}^{a_{1}-1}x_{2}^{a_{2}}x_{3}^{a_{3}})=x_{1}^{a_{1}-1}x_{2}^{a_{2}-1}x_{3}^{a_{3}-1}(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3}).

Notice that
(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})
is the edge ideal of a complete graph on three vertices. Hence, the assertion follows by combining Proposition
6.1
with Corollary
6.4
.

∎

Next, we consider matroidal ideals generated in small degrees. We are able to establish Conjectures
A
and
B
for all matroidal ideals in at most four variables.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
I\subset S
be a matroidal ideal in at most four variables. Then,
\textup{reg}\,I^{(k)}=\textup{reg}\,I^{k}
and
I^{(k)}
has linear quotients, for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
I\subset S
be a matroidal ideal in a most four variables. Then
\alpha(I)\leq 4
. Notice that
\alpha(I)=1
if and only if
I
is a monomial prime ideal and
\alpha(I)=|\textup{supp}(I)|
if and only if
I=(\prod_{i\in\textup{supp}(I)}x_{i})
. In these cases the assertion holds. If
\alpha(I)=2
, then
I
is a matroidal edge ideal and the assertion follows from Proposition
6.1
. Therefore if
|\textup{supp}(I)|\leq 3
there is nothing to prove.

Suppose now
\textup{supp}(I)=[4]
. We only have to consider the case
\alpha(I)=3
. Using Lemma
5.4
we can write
I=x_{4}I_{1}+I_{2}
, with
4\notin\textup{supp}(I_{2})
,
I_{2}\subset I_{1}
and
I_{1},I_{2}
are polymatroidal. If
I_{2}=(0)
, then
I
satisfies the assertion by using Corollary
6.4
because
I_{1}
satisfies the assertion by our discussion. Suppose
I_{2}\neq(0)
. Since
\textup{supp}(I_{2})\subseteq[3]
and
\alpha(I_{2})=3
we have
I_{2}=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})
. Notice that
\alpha(I_{1})=2
. Therefore, by using
[
40
, Theorem 2.3]
,
I_{1}
is the edge ideal of a complete multipartite graph on two or on three vertices. Hence, up to a relabeling, we have the next three possible cases: (i)
I_{1}=(x_{1}x_{2})
, (ii)
I_{1}=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3})
or (iii)
I_{1}=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})
. Therefore, we have the following cases:

(i)

I=x_{4}(x_{1}x_{2})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})=(x_{1})(x_{2})(x_{3},x_{4})
is a transversal polymatroidal ideal and it satisfies the assertion by Theorem
5.8
(c).

(ii)

I=x_{4}(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})=I_{\{1\},1}I_{\{2,3,4\},2}
is a matching-matroidal ideal of Veronese type and it satisfies the assertion by Theorem
4.6
.

(iii)

I=x_{4}(x_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})=I_{4,3}
is a squarefree Veronese ideal and it satisfies the assertion by Corollary
3.3
.

The proof is complete.

∎

As the final conclusion of the paper, in the next result we summarize the families of polymatroidal ideals for which we know that Conjectures
A
and
B
hold.

Theorem 6.7
.

Conjectures
A
and
B
hold for the following families of polymatroidal ideals.

(a)

Polymatroidal ideals generated in degree two.

(b)

Polymatroidal ideals in at most three variables.

(c)

Matroidal ideals in at most four variables.

(d)

Transversal polymatroidal ideals.

(e)

Squarefree Veronese ideals.

(f)

Matching-matroidal ideals of Veronese type.

(g)

Principal Borel ideals.

Furthermore, Conjectures
A
and
B
hold for any product of polymatroidal ideals, having pairwise disjoint supports, belonging to the families
(a)-(g)
.

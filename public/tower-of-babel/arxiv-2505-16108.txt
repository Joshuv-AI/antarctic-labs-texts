Shape-invariant Potentials and Singular Spaces
Peng Yu, Yuan Zhong, Ziqi Wang, Hui Wang, Mengyang Zhang

Abstract
We report two exact classical solutions in two-dimensional singular spaces. These solutions are lower-dimensional versions of some five-dimensional brane world solutions. Unlike the higher-dimensional model, our solutions have exactly solvable linear perturbation equations, namely, Schrödinger-like equations with Pöschl-Teller I potential and Eckart potential. Both potentials are shape-invariant and can be solved exactly using supersymmetric quantum mechanics methods. In our work, the Pöschl-Teller I potential has infinitely many bound states, while the Eckart potential captures a finite number of bound states. Both potentials are positive-definite which indicates that the background solutions are stable against linear perturbations.



I
Introduction

Two-dimensional (2D) gravitational models have demonstrated significant potential in solving complex problems
Brown 1988
;
Grumiller et al. 2002
. The simplicity and manageability make these models effective tools for exploring diverse phenomena across topics such as quantizing gravity
Rovelli 2008
;
Carlip 2001
, black holes
Brown et al. 1986
;
Russo et al. 1992
;
Frassino et al. 2015
, cosmological models
Cooper et al. 1991
;
Devecchi and Froehlich 2005
;
Anninos et al. 2024
and so on.

In two-dimensional space-time, the Einstein tensor always vanishes for any metric, and one usually introduces a dilaton field to generate non-trivial gravitational dynamics. A well-known 2D gravity model that resembles Einstein gravity in many aspects, is the so-called Mann-Morsink-Sikkema-Steele (MMSS) model
Mann et al. 1991
, which has the following action:

S=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int d^{2}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}+\varphi R+\kappa\mathcal{L}\right].

(1)

Here
\varphi
is the dilaton field and
\mathcal{L}
is the Lagrangian density of matter fields, which is assumed to be a real scalar field in this work. A virtue of this model is that for a metric of the following form

ds^{2}=-e^{2A(x)}dt^{2}+dx^{2},

(2)

the dynamical equations have a simple first-order formalism, from which exact gravitating soliton solutions can be easily constructed
Stötzel 1995
;
Zhong 2021
;
Zhong et al. 2021
;
Zhong 2022
;
Wang et al. 2024a
;
Wang et al. 2024b
;
Zhong et al. 2024
;
Feng and Zhong 2022
. Many of these solutions have asymptotic
\rm AdS_{2}
metric, and can be regarded as 2D versions of some five-dimensional thick brane solutions
DeWolfe et al. 2000
;
Gremm 2000a
;
Csaki et al. 2000
;
Dzhunushaliev et al. 2010
;
Bazeia et al. 2009
.

In particular, it was found that in some models with noncanonical scalar matter fields, there exists static sine-Gordon type solitons, whose linear perturbation equations are Schrödinger-like equations with exactly solvable Pöschl-Teller II potetential
Zhong 2022
and Rosen-Morse potential
Wang et al. 2024a
. The solvability of the linear perturbation equation may be useful in studying the quantum properties of gravitating solitons.

Notably, in the models of Refs.
Stötzel 1995
;
Zhong 2022
;
Wang et al. 2024a
, the background space-time geometry are smooth without singularities. In the study of 5D brane world models, however, singular spaces might be physically meaningful
Afonso et al. 2006
;
Freedman et al. 2004
;
Gremm 2000b
. It is then natural to ask if there are also connections between singular gravitational soliton solutions and shape invariant potentials.

With this question in mind, we will work on two solutions and show that the corresponding stability equations can be related to the shape invariant Pöschl-Teller I
Pöschl and Teller 1933
;
Cooper et al. 1995
;
Khare 2004
and Eckart potential
Gangopadhyaya et al. 2017a
. The Pöschl-Teller I potential is a well-known exactly solvable potential in quantum mechanics, which has infinitely many bound states. The Eckart potential is another exactly solvable potential first studied by C. Eckart in 1930
Eckart 1930
. Both potentials are shape invariant, so that their eigenvalues can be determined using supersymmetric quantum mechanical (SUSY QM) methods
Dutt et al. 1988
.

The organization of this paper is as follows: in Sec.
II
, we review the model and the main results of Ref.
Zhong 2022
. In Sec.
III
, we derive two exact solutions by selecting two hyperbolic superpotentials and demonstrate that the effective potentials in the stability equations are the Pöschl-Teller I and Eckart potentials. We give a brief summary in Sec.
IV
.

II
The Model

As in Ref.
Zhong 2022
, we use the action in Eq. (
1
) with a noncanonical scalar matter field
\mathcal{L}(\phi,X)
, where
X=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\mu}\phi
is the kinetic term of the scalar matter field
\phi
.
Variation of the action yields the Einstein equation, the scalar equation and the dilaton equation. For the metric in Eq. (
2
), the dilaton field becomes:

\varphi(x)=2A(x),

(3)

and the independent equations need to be solved are the Einstein equations:

\displaystyle-4\partial_{x}^{2}A=\kappa\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(\partial_{x}\phi\right)^{2}

,

(4)

\displaystyle 4\partial_{x}^{2}A+2\left(\partial_{x}A\right)^{2}=\kappa\mathcal{L}

,

(5)

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}\equiv\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial X}
. Note that without specifying the form of
\mathcal{L}(\phi,X)
, there will be more unknown variables than independent equations. So we need to impose some constraints.

We first introduce an arbitrary function “
W(\phi)
”, termed superpotential, by demanding:

X=-\frac{1}{2}W_{\phi}^{2},

(6)

or equivalently:

\partial_{x}\phi=W_{\phi},

(7)

where
W_{\phi}\equiv\frac{dW}{d\phi}
.

If
\mathcal{L}(\phi,X)=X-V(\phi)
, then the dynamical equations (
4
) and (
5
) can be rewritten as a very simple first-order formalism:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}A=-\frac{1}{4}\kappa W,

(8)

\displaystyle V=\frac{1}{2}W_{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\kappa W^{2}.

(9)

The advantage of this formalism is that, if we carefully choose the superpotential
W(\phi)
, exact soliton solutions can be constructed easily as shown in
DeWolfe et al. 2000
;
Gremm 2000a
;
Csaki et al. 2000
and
Zhong 2021
.

For noncanonical models where
\mathcal{L}\neq X-V(\phi)
, the Einstein equations usually take more complicate form than Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
). However, for the following Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}=U(\phi)\left(X+\frac{1}{2}W_{\phi}^{2}\right)^{2}+X-V(\phi),

(10)

\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{X}
will be the same form as those of the canonical case, if Eq. (
6
) is satisfied
Zhong 2022
;
Wang et al. 2024a
. Thus the dynamical equations for a model with the above Lagrangian will retain the same form as the canonical model. However, difference appears as soon as linear perturbations are considered
Zhong 2022
. The model described by Eq. (
10
) is called a twinlike model of the canonical model, which was first discussed in cosmology and brane world models
Andrews et al. 2010
;
Bazeia et al. 2011
;
Adam and Queiruga 2011
.

To perform linear stability analysis, we first introduce a coordinate transformation:

r\equiv\int e^{-A(x)}dx,

(11)

with which the metric in Eq. (
2
) becomes conformally flat:

ds^{2}=e^{2A(r)}\left(-dt^{2}+dr^{2}\right).

(12)

Suppose we have a static background solution {
\varphi(r)
,
\phi(r)
,
A(r)
} and let {
\delta\varphi(r,t)
,
\delta\phi(r,t)
,
\delta g_{\mu\nu}(r,t)
} be small perturbations. For convenience, we define
Zhong et al. 2021
;
Zhong 2022
:

\displaystyle\delta g_{\mu\nu}(r,t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle e^{2A(r)}h_{\mu\nu}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2A(r)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}h_{00}(r,t)&\Phi(r,t)\\
\Phi(r,t)&h_{rr}(r,t)\end{array}\right),

and
\Xi\equiv 2\dot{\Phi}-h_{00}^{\prime}
(where overdots and prime denote derivatives with respect to time and space, respectively) and work with the gauge condition
\delta\varphi(r)=0
. After linearization we obtain three independent perturbation equations. Two of them can be used to eliminate
\Xi
and
h_{rr}
in terms of
\delta\phi
, and finally one can obtain an equation of the following form
Zhong et al. 2021
:

-\partial_{t}^{2}G+\partial_{y}^{2}G-V_{\rm{eff}}(y)G=0,

(16)

after performing another coordinate transformation
r\rightarrow y=\int dr\gamma^{-1/2}
. Here,
G
and
\gamma
is defined as:

\displaystyle G(y,t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}^{1/2}\gamma^{1/4}\delta\phi(y,t),

(17)

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 1+2\frac{\mathcal{L}_{XX}X}{\mathcal{L}_{X}},

(18)

and the effective potential
V_{\rm{eff}}(y)
is:

V_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)\equiv\frac{\partial_{y}^{2}f}{f},

(19)

where

f(y)\equiv\mathcal{L}_{X}^{1/2}\gamma^{1/4}\frac{\partial_{y}\phi}{\partial_{y}A}.

(20)

Obviously, to make the rescaling of
\delta\phi
as well as the coordinate transformation
r\to y
well defined, the following conditions must be satisfied
Zhong et al. 2021
:

\mathcal{L}_{X}>0,\quad\gamma>0.

(21)

By performing mode expansions:

G(t,y)=\sum_{n}e^{i\omega_{n}t}\psi_{n}(y),

(22)

we obtain a Schrödinger-like equation with factorizable Hamiltonian:

H\psi_{n}\equiv\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}\psi_{n}=-\frac{d^{2}\psi_{n}}{dy^{2}}+V_{\rm eff}(y)\psi_{n}=\omega_{n}^{2}\psi_{n},

(23)

where,

\mathcal{A}=\frac{d}{dy}+\mathcal{W},\quad\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}=-\frac{d}{dy}+\mathcal{W},

(24)

and,

\mathcal{W}\equiv\frac{\partial_{y}f}{f},

(25)

which is also called the superpotential in the context of supersymmetric quantum mechanics (SUSY QM), but is different from the one defined in Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
). In SUSY QM, one can construct two partner Hamiltonian operators:

H_{\pm}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+V_{\pm}(y),

(26)

where
V_{\pm}(y)=\mathcal{W}^{2}\pm\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{dy}
are called partner potentials. If the partner potentials are non-singular, the eigenvalues of the corresponding partner Hamiltonians are non-negative, and have equal spectra except the ground state
Dutt et al. 1988
. Moreover, the partner potentials are called shape invariant if they satisfy the following equation:

V_{+}\left(y;a_{0}\right)=V_{-}\left(y;a_{1}\right)+F\left(a_{1}\right),

(27)

where
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are two constants and
F(a_{1})
is an arbitrary function of the parameter in the argument
Dutt et al. 1988
. In this case, the corresponding Schrödinger equation is exactly solvable
Khare and Bhaduri 1994
;
Chuan 1991
.

Note that to derive the Schrödinger-like equation, one needs to introduce two coordinate transformations
x\to r\to y
. The above Schrödinger-like equation is obtained in the
y
-coordinates while background solutions are usually derived in the
x
-coordinates. Usually, it is hard to get the analytical expression of the effective potential
V_{\rm eff}(y)
, needless to say to solve the stability equation exactly. However, it was found that for some models with noncanonical scalar Lagrangian, such as the one in Eq. (
10
), the second coordinate transformation inverses the first one, provided that
Zhong 2022
:

U(\phi)=\frac{1}{2W_{\phi}^{2}}\left(1-e^{\frac{\kappa}{2}\int\frac{W}{W_{\phi}}d\phi}\right).

(28)

This makes the
y
-coordinate system and the
x
-coordinate equivalent and in this case, it is possible to obtain both the background solutions and the Schrödinger-like equations in the
x
-coordinate.
In general, given the superpotential
W(\phi)
, we can use Eqs. (
7
), (
8
) and (
19
) to find
\phi(x)
,
A(x)
and
V_{\rm eff}(y)
, respectively.

2D Brane World Solutions

III.1
Solution 1

For our first solution, we choose the following superpotential:

W(\phi)=b\sinh(a\phi),

(29)

where
a
,
b
are parammeters to be specified. The solution takes the following form:

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}{\rm arcsinh}(\tan(a^{2}bx)),

(30)

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{4a^{2}}\ln(\cos(a^{2}bx)),

(31)

\displaystyle V(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}b^{2}\left(4a^{2}\cosh^{2}(a\phi)-k\sinh^{2}(a\phi)\right),

(32)

\displaystyle U(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\text{sech}^{2}(a\phi)\left(\cosh^{\frac{k}{2a^{2}}}(a\phi)-1\right)}{2a^{2}b^{2}}.

(33)

It can be seen that the warp factor diverges at

, so the background space-time is only well-defined within those boundaries, and its singular nature is revealed by the scalar curvature:

R(x)=\frac{1}{16}b^{2}k\sec^{2}\left(a^{2}bx\right)\left(k\cos\left(2a^{2}bx\right)+8a^{2}-k\right).

(34)

The presence of singularities at the two boundaries can be observed in Fig.

We also note that the solutions (

Gremm 2000b
, which suggests that our solutions can be interpreted as 2D counterparts of some 5D brane world models. Furthermore, as shown in

IV
Conclusion

In this paper we find two novel 2D brane world solutions in the MMSS gravity model with noncanonical scalar matter fields. These solutions are derived from the first-order formalism of the field equations by choosing two simple hyperbolic superpotential functions. Both solutions have singular metric, and is different from the solutions of Refs.
Zhong 2022
;
Wang et al. 2024a
where the background space-time is smooth.

More interestingly, the effective potentials related to the linear stability analysis are governed by the shape invariant Pöschl-Teller I and Eckart potentials. For certain parameter values, the former captures infinitely many bound states, while the latter has finite number of bound states. Moreover, the energy levels in the effective potentials are non-negative which confirms the stability of our solutions.

The study of 2D brane world models with Schrödinger-type perturbation equations and shape invariant potentials provides an interesting framework for exploring the interplay between gravity and quantum mechanics. The presence of singularities in the background space-time raises intriguing questions about the nature of these solutions and their implications for the underlying physics. In this regard, we hope our work could provide some insights towards better understanding connections between quantum theory and gravitational models.

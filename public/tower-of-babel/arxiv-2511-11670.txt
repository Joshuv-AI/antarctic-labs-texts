Generalized Evolution Semigroups and h − h- Dichotomies for Evolution Families on Banach Spaces Thanks: The first and third authors were funded by FONDECYT Regular Grant 1240361.
Álvaro Castañeda, Verónica Poblete, Gonzalo Robledo

Abstract
. This paper develops a comprehensive theory generalizing exponential decay patterns for evolution processes in Banach spaces. We replace classical exponential bounds with more flexible decay rates governed by an increasing homeomorphism h h . The core of our approach lies in constructing particular group structures induced by h h , which allow us to define generalized semigroups on function spaces. We prove that these h h -semigroups are equivalent to classical evolution semigroups through a natural transformation. Our main result establishes that three fundamental concepts are equivalent: hyperbolicity of the generalized semigroup, dichotomy of the underlying evolution process, and a spectral condition on the generator. This work extends classical dichotomy theory to encompass a wider class of decay patterns, providing new tools for analyzing asymptotic behavior in dynamical systems.

Introduction

1.1.
Preliminaries

The property of exponential dichotomy
for evolution families
\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}
on a Banach space
(X,||\cdot||_{X})
has been widely studied in the literature
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
18
,
20
,
21
]
. A noteworthy consequence is the existence of an associated
C_{0}
–semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
together with its corresponding infinitesimal generator
G
. A classical result
[
3
, Th. 3.17 and Th. 4.25]
and
[
7
, Th. VI.9.18]
establishes an equivalence
between three distinguished properties:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bullet&\textnormal{The exponential dichotomy of the family $\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}$},\\
\bullet&\textnormal{the hyperbolicity of $\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}$},\\
\bullet&\textnormal{a geometrical property of the resolvent $G$, namely},\\
&\textnormal{its intersection with the imaginary axis is empty}.\end{array}\right.

An essential assumption of this equivalence is that the evolution family is
exponentially bounded
, that is there exist
K\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
such that

(1.2)

||U(t,s)||\leq Ke^{\alpha(t-s)}\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ with $t\geq s$}.

The main goal of this article is to study the above equivalences –in a generalized framework– under the existence of bounds encompassing (
1.2
), namely,

(1.3)

||U(t,s)||\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{\alpha}\quad\textnormal{for any $t,s\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}$ with $t\geq s$},

where
h\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a strictly increasing homeomorphism generalizing the exponential function. The idea behind to consider these maps
h(\cdot)
is to allow the rates of contraction and expansion to be determined by general growth functions describing the behavior of solutions of differential equations and has been developed in a systematic way in the works of Martin
[
14
]
, Muldowney
[
16
]
and Naulin–Pinto
[
17
]
.

In order to relate the bounds (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) we will recall
the tough–provoking article of Peña and Rivera–Villagrán
[
19
]
, where the authors noticed – in a slighlty different context– that the
change of time variables

(1.4)

t=h^{-1}(e^{\tilde{t}})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\tilde{t}=\ln(h(t)),

transforms (
1.3
) as follows:

(1.5)

||U(h^{-1}(e^{\tilde{t}}),h^{-1}(e^{\tilde{s}}))||\leq Ke^{\alpha(\tilde{t}-\tilde{s})}.

Furthermore, a noteworthy remark of
[
19
]
was to explain that the above transformation (
1.4
) does not trivially transforms the general bound (
1.3
) into an exponential one. In fact the change of variables reveals something much more subtle, namely, a commutative diagrams of isomorphisms of topological groups
such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R},+)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R}^{+},\cdot)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{-1}}

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R}^{+},\cdot)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{R},+),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ln}

where the topological abelian group
(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}})
– a formal definition will be given later – is such that the isomorphism
h\colon(\mathbb{R},\ast_{h})\to(\mathbb{R}^{+},\cdot)
verifies
h(t\ast s)=h(t)h(s)
,
which mimics the exponential identity
e^{t+s}=e^{t}e^{s}
. Moreover, as an abelian group is a
\mathbb{Z}
–module,
we note that its external composition law can be written in terms of
*_{h}
and will be denoted by
\odot
.

Motivated by (
1.5
), it is natural to ask what happens when the asymptotic behaviour of
an evolution family is not governed by a fixed exponential rate, but rather by a more
general growth rate. In particular, we would like to replace the classical weight

e^{(t-s)}
by ratios of the form
h(t)/h(s)
, and to study the resulting
notions of stability and dichotomy in a semigroup framework adapted to this new time
scale.

To this end, we fix a map
h(\cdot)
and endow
\mathbb{R}
with the induced
operations
*_{{}_{h}}
and
\odot
, obtaining a one-dimensional normed vector space

(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)
with neutral element
e_{*}
.
Families

U=\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}
are then indexed by
(\mathbb{R}_{*},*_{{}_{h}},e_{*})
. The family
U
is called
h-
evolution family, and its growth and decay
are measured with respect to the
h
–time rather than the classical
linear time. This leads naturally to the concept of
h
-dichotomy
, where the
role of the exponential weights is played by the ratio
h(t)/h(s)
.

In this context, we can observe a growing interest in dichotomies and decays beyond the exponential case, namely, the work
of Megan, Sasu and Sasu
[
15
]
devoted to nonuniform exponential dichotomy and the works of Lupa and Popescu
[
12
,
13
]
focused in a generalized exponential dichotomy. With respect to nonexponential decays we refer to the seminal and tough provoking work of Borichev and Tomilov
[
1
]
which provided conditions ensuring a polynomial decay.

1.2.
Structure and Novelties of the article

The present paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2 we revisit the abelian group
(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}})
which is totally ordered allowing
to define an absolute value
|\cdot|_{*}
. Moreover, as a novelty, we prove that the vector space
(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)

denoting it as
\mathbb{R}_{*}=(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot,|\cdot|_{*})

In Section 3 we regard

-semigroup acting on

-evolution family

on

we associate to it an operator family

, indexed by

we then show (cf. Lemma
9
) that, if

satisfies (

), the family

forms a so called

-semigroup on

.

A first step to generalize the equivalences (
1.1
) is provided by Theorem
1
, which gives a precise
characterization of the generator of the
h
-semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
. As a
consequence, we obtain detailed spectral information for this
h-
semigroup. The proof
relies on the construction of a classical
C_{0}
-semigroup associated with the original

h
-evolution family
U
, and on a conjugacy map that transfers spectral properties
between the classical and the
h
-reparametrized settings.

In Section 4, as a second step to address the generalization of the equivalences (
1.1
), we develop the semigroup approach further by relating the notion of

h
-dichotomy to the spectral theory of the associated evolution
h
-semigroup on

C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
. In particular, we show that
h
-dichotomy of
U
is equivalent
to the invertibility of the generator
B_{h}
of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
together with the
spectral gap condition

\sigma(B_{h})\cap i\mathbb{R}=\varnothing.

This yields an operator-theoretic characterization of
h
-dichotomy which extends, to
the
h
-framework, the classical results for evolution families on the half-line; see Theorem
2

which states the equivalence between the following properties

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bullet&\textnormal{The $h$--dichotomy of the family $\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}$},\\
\bullet&\textnormal{the hyperbolicity of the $h$--semigroup $\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}$},\\
\bullet&\textnormal{the geometrical property $\sigma(B_{h})\cap i\mathbb{R}=\varnothing$}.\\
\end{array}\right.

2.
Algebraic Preliminaries and contextualization

We begin by recalling the notion of a growth rate, and then introduce the algebraic
and topological structures it induces on the real line. These constructions provide
the basic framework and tools that will support the rest of the paper.

Definition 1
.

We will say that the function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a growth rate if
h
is a strictly increasing homeomorphism.

Some examples of growth rates are given by
h(t)=e^{t},\ h(t)=e^{(t-t_{0})^{n}}
for any odd
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and

h(t)=t+\sqrt{t^{2}+1}
.

The growth rates allows to define the following laws of composition on
\mathbb{R}
:

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathbb{R}\\
(t,s)&\mapsto&t\ast_{{}_{h}}s:=h^{-1}\left(h(t)h(s)\right),\end{array}

and

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathbb{R}\\
(\alpha,t)&\mapsto&\alpha\odot t:=h^{-1}\left(h(t)^{\alpha}\right),\end{array}

which are well defined since
h(t)^{\alpha}>0
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 1
.

Notice that, when considering
h(t)=e^{t}
, the above composition laws becomes

(t,s)\mapsto t+s
and
(\alpha,t)\to\alpha t
, namely, the classical addition and
the scalar multiplication in
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
.

2.1.
Abelian ordered group induced by the growth rate
h

The composition law (
2.1
) allows to state the following result, which has been stated without proof in
[
6
, Proposition 1]
and
[
19
, Sect.3]
:

Proposition 1
.

The pair
(\mathbb{R},\ast_{{}_{h}})
is an abelian group where the unit element and the inverse for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
are respectively defined by:

(2.3)

e_{*}:=h^{-1}(1)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad t^{*-1}:=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(t)}\right).

Proof.

Notice that the operation (
2.1
) is commutative

\begin{array}[]{rcl}t\ast_{{}_{h}}s=h^{-1}(h(t)h(s))=h^{-1}(h(s)h(t))=s\ast_{{}_{h}}t,\end{array}

then we only need to verify the group axioms.

Firstly, we can see that (
2.1
) allows an easy verification of the associative property

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt(t\ast_{{}_{h}}s)\ast_{{}_{h}}r&=&h^{-1}(h(t)h(s))\ast_{{}_{h}}r=h^{-1}\left(h(h^{-1}(h(t)h(s)))h(r)\right)\\
&=&h^{-1}(h(t)h(s)h(r))\\
&=&h^{-1}(h(t)h\{h^{-1}(h(s)h(r))\})\quad\textnormal{since $h(s)h(r)=h(h^{-1}(h(s)h(r)))$}\\
&=&t\ast_{{}_{h}}h^{-1}(h(s)h(r))=t\ast_{{}_{h}}(s\ast_{h}r).\end{array}

By using (
2.1
) and
e_{*}
from (
2.3
) we have that

t\ast_{{}_{h}}e_{*}=h^{-1}(h(t)h(e_{*}))=h(h^{-1}(e_{*}))=t\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in\mathbb{R}$}

and it follows that
e_{*}
is the unit element.

By using again (
2.1
) and
t^{*-1}
from (
2.3
) we have that

t\ast_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1}=h^{-1}(h(t)h(t^{*-1}))=h^{-1}\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(t)}\right)=h^{-1}(1)=e_{*},

and the result follows.
∎

.

A vector space induced by the growth rate
h

Notice that the results devoted to the ordered group
(\mathbb{R},*)
were deduced
by using the first composition law (

) and its consequences. Moreover, it is well known that

,
[

(k,t)&\mapsto&t^{*k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\underbrace{t\ast\cdots\ast t}_{k-\textnormal{times}}&\textnormal{if}&k>0\\

By using recursively the identities (

we can see that

t^{*k}=h^{-1}(h(t^{*k}))=h^{-1}(h(t)^{k})\quad\textnormal{for any $k\in\mathbb{Z}$},

and the above external composition law can be revisited as

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}&\to&\mathbb{R}\\
(k,t)&\mapsto&t^{*k}=k\odot t:=h^{-1}\left(h(t)^{k}\right),\end{array}

A big novelty of this article is to notice that (
2.9
) can be extended to (
2.2
), that is,
the
\mathbb{Z}
–module
(\mathbb{R},*_{h})
is an

–vector space:

Lemma 4
.

(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)
is a vector space over the field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, we know that
(\mathbb{R},\ast_{{}_{h}})
is an abelian group.
In consequence, we only need to verify the scalar multiplication axioms. In order to do
that, let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
s,t\in(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)
.

i)
Distributivity over scalar addition
.
It is,
(\alpha+\beta)\odot t=(\alpha\odot t)*_{{}_{h}}(\beta\odot t).

In fact, by using (
2.2
) we can easily verify that

h\big((\alpha+\beta)\odot t\big)=h(t)^{\alpha+\beta}=h(t)^{\alpha}h(t)^{\beta}=h\big(\alpha\odot t\big)\,h\big(\beta\odot t\big).

Now, by using (
2.4
) we can see that

h\big((\alpha+\beta)\odot t\big)=h\big((\alpha\odot t)*_{{}_{h}}(\beta\odot t)\big),

and the property follows since
h
is invertible.

ii)
Distributivity over vector operation
. We will verify that

\alpha\odot(t*_{{}_{h}}s)=(\alpha\odot t)*_{{}_{h}}(\alpha\odot s).

In fact, by using (
2.4
) and (
2.2
) we have

h\big(\alpha\odot(t*_{{}_{h}}s)\big)=h(t*_{{}_{h}}s)^{\alpha}=h(t)^{\alpha}h(s)^{\alpha}=h\big(\alpha\odot t\big)\,h\big(\alpha\odot s\big)=h\big((\alpha\odot t)*_{{}_{h}}(\alpha\odot s)\big),

and the property follows.

iii)
Compatibility with field multiplication
. The identity
(\alpha\beta)\odot t=\alpha\odot(\beta\odot t)

is a direct consequence of (
2.2
) since

h\big((\alpha\beta)\odot t\big)=h(t)^{\alpha\beta}=(h(t)^{\beta})^{\alpha}=h\big(\beta\odot t\big)^{\alpha}=h\big(\alpha\odot(\beta\odot t)\big).

∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
a,b\in(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)
and
\alpha\in(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot).
The following properties are verified

(1)

The additive inverse of
a
is
(-1)\odot a=a^{*-1}.

(2)

(\alpha\odot a)^{*-1}=\alpha\odot a^{*-1}=(-\alpha)\odot a.

(3)

If
a\leq b
and
\alpha>0
then
\alpha\odot a\leq\alpha\odot b.
If
\alpha<0
then
\alpha\odot a\geq\alpha\odot b.

Proof.

(1) Since
h((-1)\odot a)=h(a)^{-1}
and hence
a*_{{}_{h}}((-1)\odot a)=e_{*}
.

(2) Note that

(\alpha\odot a)^{*-1}=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(\alpha\odot a)}\right)=h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{h(a)^{\alpha}}\right)=\alpha\odot a^{*-1}=(-\alpha)\odot a.

(3) Assume that
a\leq b,
it is,
e_{*}\leq b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt(\alpha\odot b)*_{{}_{h}}(\alpha\odot a)^{*-1}&=(\alpha\odot b)*_{{}_{h}}(\alpha\odot a^{*-1})=\alpha\odot(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1})\\
&=h^{-1}(h(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1})^{\alpha}).\end{array}

Since
e_{*}\leq b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1}
we have that
1\leq h(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1}).

If
\alpha>0
then
1\leq h(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1})^{\alpha}
and so

concluding that

equivalently
\alpha\odot a\leq\alpha\odot b.

Now, if
\alpha<0
then
1\geq h(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1})^{\alpha}
and so
e_{*}\geq h^{-1}(h(b*_{{}_{h}}a^{*-1})^{\alpha})

hence
(\alpha\odot b)*_{{}_{h}}(\alpha\odot a)^{*-1}\leq e_{*}

∎

3.
A Generalized Evolution Semigroup

In this section, we will consider the normed vector space
\mathbb{R}_{*}:=(\mathbb{R},*_{{}_{h}},\odot,|\cdot|_{*})
and
the Banach space
X=(X,||\cdot||_{X})
such that
\mathcal{B}(X)
denotes the set of bounded linear operators in
X.
Furthermore,
C(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
is the space of all continuous functions
X-
valued whereas
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
denotes the Banach space of all functions in

C(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
vanishing at
\pm\infty,
endowed with the norm

||u||_{*\infty}:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{*}}||u(s)||_{X}.

Remark 1
.

The space
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X):=\{u\in C(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)\,:\,\mathrm{supp}(u)\ \mbox{is compact}\}
is dense in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X).
In fact, when considering
(\mathbb{R}_{*},*_{{}_{h}},\odot)
as a
one–dimensional normed real vector space
with norm
|\cdot|_{*}
together with a compact set

B_{r}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{*}:\ |t|_{*}\leq r\}
we can choose continuous cut-offs
\psi_{n}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
with
\psi_{n}\equiv 1
in
[0,n]
and
\psi_{n}\equiv 0
on
[n+1,\infty)
, and set
\eta_{n}(t):=\psi_{n}(|t|_{*})\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*},[0,1])
(indeed
\mathrm{supp}\,\eta_{n}\subset B_{n+1}
).
For
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
define
f_{n}:=\eta_{n}f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
. If
t\in B_{n}
then
f_{n}(t)=f(t)
; if
t\notin B_{n}
then
\|f(t)\|_{X}
is small by the definition of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
, hence
\|f-f_{n}\|_{*\infty}\to 0
.
Thus,
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
is dense in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
.

We will modify the classical concept of an evolution semigroup associated with an evolution family on the half-line to fit the case
t\geq e_{*}
and the linear flows may not agree with the restricted hypothesis of uniform exponential bounded growth (
1.2
). For this purpose we establish the following definitions.

Definition 5
.

Let
(Y,||\cdot||_{Y})
be a Banach space. A family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
of bounded linear operators from
Y
into
Y
is an
h-
semigroup if

(i)

T_{e_{*}}=\mathrm{Id}

(ii)

T_{t*_{{}_{h}}s}=T(t)T(s)
for every
t,s\geq e_{*}.

In addition, we will say that
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
is a
strongly continuous
h-
semigroup
if

\lim_{t\to e_{*}^{+}}T_{t}y=y\quad\mbox{for every}\ \ y\in Y,

or equivalently

\forall\,\varepsilon>0\,\,\exists\,\delta>e_{*}\ \textnormal{such that for any}\ y\in Y,\ e_{*}<t<\delta\Rightarrow\left\|T(t)y-y\right\|_{Y}<\varepsilon.

Definition 6
.

An
h-
evolution family on
X
is a collection
\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}
of bounded linear operators acting on
X
such that the following properties hold:

•

U(t,t)=\mathrm{Id},\ t\in\mathbb{R}_{*};

•

U(t,\tau)U(\tau,s)=U(t,s),\ t\geq\tau\geq s;

•

for each
x\in X
, the mapping
(t,s)\to U(t,s)x
is continuous on

\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}_{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{*}\,:\,t\geq s\}.

We will say that the above
h-
evolution family
\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}
is
h-
bounded if there exist constants
\alpha>0
and
K\geq 1
such that

(3.1)

{}||U(t,s)||\leq K[h(t*_{{}_{h}}s^{*-1})]^{\alpha},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ t\geq s.

Remark 4
.

Notice that, by using the identities (
2.4
), we can easily deduce that (
3.1
)
is equivalent to

||U(t,s)||\leq K\left(\frac{h(t)}{h(s)}\right)^{\alpha},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ t\geq s.

For any

u\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
and
t\geq e_{*}
we will define

(3.2)

T_{t}u(s):=U(s,s*_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1})u(s*_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1})\quad\textnormal{for any $t\in[e_{*},+\infty)$}.

The next result states that
s\mapsto T_{t}u(s)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
for any

u\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
and
t\geq e_{*}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\{U(t,s)\}_{t\geq s}
be an
h-
bounded evolution operator with constants
K
and
\alpha
. Then
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
given by (

)
is a strongly continuous

Proof.

Let
t\geq e_{*}
and

fixed. Note that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt||T_{t}u(s)||_{X}&\leq||U(s,s*_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1})||\,||u(s*_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1})||_{X}\\
&\leq K[h(s*_{{}_{h}}(t*_{{}_{h}}s^{*-1})^{*-1})]^{\alpha}\,||u||_{*\infty}\\
&=K\displaystyle\,\left[\frac{h(s)}{h(s*_{{}_{h}}t^{*-1})}\right]^{\alpha}\,||u||_{*\infty}=K\displaystyle\,\left[h(t)\right]^{\alpha}\,||u||_{*\infty},\end{array}

where the last identities are a consequence of (
2.4
).

The function

belongs to

since, as stated by equation (
3.2
),
is a composition of functions which are continuous with respect to

. In addition, the first above inequality combined with

implies that

when

and consequently the map

is well defined.

Now, we will verify the
h-
semigroup properties stated in Definition
5
. The property (i) follows
directly from

. Indeed, notice that:

T_{e_{*}}u(s)=U(s,s*_{{}_{h}}e_{*})u(s*_{{}_{h}}e_{*})=U(s,s)u(s)=u(s).

Now, given any pair

and noticing that

we have:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptT_{(t*_{{}_{h}}\tau)}u(s)&=U(s,s*_{{}_{h}}(t*_{{}_{h}}\tau)^{*-1})u(s*_{{}_{h}}(t*_{{}_{h}}\tau)^{*-1})\\

hence

and

We need to prove that
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq e_{*}}
is strongly continuous. Since

is dense in

it is enough to prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{t_{n}}u(s_{n})-u(s_{n})=0

for

for any

and

fixed.

Case 1. If

is unbounded, that is,

then

Since

is of support compact, we obtain that

for
n
large enough.

Case 2. Suppose that
\{s_{n}\}
is bounded and let
\varepsilon>0.
Since

and taking a subsequence

of

if necessary, we can assume that

and then

for

From this,

(3.3)

||u(s_{n})-u(s_{0})||_{X}<\eta,\quad\mbox{where}\quad\eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2(K+1)}

for
n
large enough and
K
from (
3.1
). Using again that

is for compact support, it follows that
u
is uniformly continuous, there exists
\delta_{1}(\varepsilon)>e_{*}
such that

(3.4)

||u(s^{\prime})-u(s^{\prime\prime})||_{X}<\eta\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ d(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})<\delta_{1}.

On the other hand, for
s\geq\tau,
we know that

is continuous for each

Choosing

if

then

for

thus, we can assume that

Now, there exists

Note that

Finally, from (

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt||T_{t_{n}}u(s_{n})-u(s_{n})||_{X}=&||U(s_{n},s_{n}*t_{n}^{*-1})u(s_{n}*_{{}_{h}}t_{n}^{*-1})-u(s_{n})||_{X}\\

3.3.
Measure on the
\mathbb{R}_{*}

Following the ideas developed in
[
19
]
, we define a measure
\mu_{*}
on
\mathbb{R}_{*}
, which is invariant under the action of elements of
\mathbb{R}_{*}.
For this purpose we will assume that
h
is a derivable function. The measure is an absolutely continuous and its Radon-Nikodym derivative is the logarithmic derivative of
h
, that is, for Borel measurable set
R\subset\mathbb{R}_{*}
we define

\mu_{*}(R)=\int_{R}\frac{h^{\prime}(\tau)}{h(\tau)}dm(\tau),

where
m(\cdot)
is the Lebesgue measure. To see that this an invariant measure, it is enough to prove its is invariant for compact intervals, as these sets generate the Borel
\sigma-
algebra.

Lemma 12
.

Given a compact interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{*}
the measure
\mu_{*}
is invariant under operation
*
by elements of
\mathbb{R}_{*},
that is, for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\mu_{*}([a,b])=\mu_{*}([\gamma*a,\gamma*b]).

Moreover, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\mu_{*}(\alpha\odot[a,b])=|\alpha|\,\mu_{*}([a,b]).

Proof.

The measure of the interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}_{*}
is

\mu_{*}([a,b])=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{h^{\prime}(\tau)}{h(\tau)}dm(\tau)=\ln(h(b))-\ln(h(a))=\mu(b)-\mu(a).

Given any constant
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
we obtain

\mu_{*}([\gamma*_{{}_{h}}a,\gamma*_{{}_{h}}b])=\ln\left(\frac{h(\gamma*_{{}_{h}}a)}{h(\gamma*_{{}_{h}}b)}\right)=\ln\left(\frac{h(a)}{h(b)}\right)=\mu_{*}([a,b]).

Note that
\mu_{*}([a,b])=-\mu_{*}([b,a]).
If
a\leq b
and
\alpha<0
from Proposition
2
we have that
\alpha\odot b<\alpha\odot a

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\mu_{*}(\alpha\odot[a,b])&=\mu_{*}([\alpha\odot b,\alpha\odot a])\\
&=\ln(h(\alpha\odot a))-\ln(h(\alpha\odot b))\\
&=\ln(h(a)^{\alpha})-\ln(h(b)^{\alpha})\\
&=\alpha(\ln(h(a))-\ln(h(b)))\\
&=-\alpha\mu_{*}([a,b])=|\alpha|\,\mu_{*}([a,b]).\end{array}

For
a\leq b
and
\alpha>0
from Proposition
2
we have that
\alpha\odot a<\alpha\odot b

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\mu_{*}(\alpha\odot[a,b])&=\mu_{*}([\alpha\odot a,\alpha\odot b])\\
&=\ln(h(b)^{\alpha})-\ln(h(a)^{\alpha})\\
&=\alpha(\ln(h(b))-\ln(h(a)))\\
&=|\alpha|\,\mu_{*}([a,b]).\end{array}

∎

Definition 9

||T_{t_{0}}\mathcal{P}_{h}y||_{Y}\leq N\,h(t_{0})^{-\nu}||y||_{Y}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ ||(T_{t_{0}})^{-1}_{\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\,\mathcal{Q}_{h}y||_{Y}\leq N\,h(t_{0})^{-\nu}||y||_{Y}

In the case where

Q_{h}(t_{0})=I-P_{h}(t_{0}),
and the restriction
U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0}):Q_{h}(s_{0})X\to Q_{h}(t_{0})X
is invertible, for all
t_{0}\geq s_{0}
;

(b)

there exist constants
\nu>0,\,N\geq 1
such that

||U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0})||\leq N\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(s_{0})}\right)^{-\nu}\mbox{and}\ \ ||U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(t_{0})||\leq N\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(s_{0})}\right)^{-\nu},

for
t_{0}\geq s_{0}.

We also have to
||P_{h}(t_{0})||\leq N
for every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}.
Following similar ideas to
[
22
, Lemma 4.2]
, one may prove that the mapping
t_{0}\to P_{h}(t_{0})
is strongly continuous and thus
P(\cdot)\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{*},\mathcal{B}_{s}(X)),
the space of all bounded and continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}_{*}
with values in
\mathcal{B}(X)
endowed with the topology of strong convergence:

||P_{h}(t_{0})||:=\sup\{||P_{h}(t_{0})x||_{X}\,:\,||x||_{X}\leq 1\}.

The main result of this article extends the equivalences from Proposition
4
to the framework
of
h
–evolution families.

Theorem 2
.

Let
U
be an
h-
evolution family which is
h-
bounded on a Banach space
X
, let
\{T_{t_{0}}\}_{t_{0}\geq e_{*}}
be the associated evolution
h-
semigroup on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
and
B_{h}
its generator. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\{T_{t_{0}}\}_{t_{0}\geq e_{*}}
is
h-
hyperbolic on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
;

(2)

U
admits an
h-
dichotomy on
X
;

(3)

\sigma(B_{h})\cap i\mathbb{R}=\varnothing.
In this case,
B_{h}
is invertible and, if
h
is continuously differentiable then its inverse is given by

(4.2)

(B_{h}^{-1}g)(t_{0})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{h}(t_{0},s_{0})g(s_{0})d\mu_{*},\quad\forall g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X),\ \ t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*},

where

\Gamma_{h}(t_{0},s_{0})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptU(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0}),&t_{0}>s_{0},\\
-U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(s_{0}),&t_{0}<s_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Since
U
is an
h
–bounded
h-
evolution family on
X
, from Lemma
10
we have that
V
given by (
3.8
) is an evolution family exponentially bounded.

(1)
\Rightarrow(2).
Due to
\{T_{t_{0}}\}_{t_{0}\geq e_{*}}
is
h-
hyperbolic on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
from Lemma
14
we obtain that
\{S_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup hyperbolic on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R},X)
. Furthermore, by Proposition
4
we deduce that
V
admits an exponential dichotomy on
X
.

Let
P(t),\,t\in\mathbb{R},
the associated projection to the dichotomy of
V.
We define

P_{h}(t_{0})=(P\circ\mu)(t_{0})=P(\mu(t_{0})),

and we claim that
P_{h}(t_{0}),\,t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
is a projection associated to
U
. In fact, is clear that
P_{h}(t_{0}):X\to X
is well defined, and

P_{h}(t_{0})U(t_{0},s_{0})=P(t)U(\mu^{-1}(t),\mu^{-1}(s))=P(t)V(t,s)=V(t,s)P(s)=U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0})

since
\mu(t_{0})=t.

Note that
Q_{h}(t_{0})=I-P_{h}(t_{0})=I-P(\mu(t_{0}))=Q(\mu(t_{0})),
for
t_{0}\geq s_{0},
we have

U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(t_{0})=U_{Q_{h}}(\mu^{-1}(t),\mu^{-1}(s))^{-1}Q(t)=V_{Q}(t,s)^{-1}Q(t),

due to
\mu(t_{0})=t.

Hence, considering that
\mu(t_{0})=\ln(h(t_{0}))=t
combined with the property of dichotomy exponential of
V,
there exist constants
\nu>0,\,N\geq 1
such that

||U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0})||=||V(t,s)P(s)||\leq Ne^{-\nu(t-s)}=N\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(s_{0})}\right)^{-\nu}

and

\ \ ||U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(t_{0})||=||V_{Q}(t,s)^{-1}Q(t)||\leq Ne^{-\nu(t-s)}=N\left(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(s_{0})}\right)^{-\nu},

for
t_{0}\geq s_{0}.
So, we conclude that
U
admits an
h-
dichotomy.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). Let
P_{h}(t_{0})
be the projection associated with
U.
We write

P(t)=P_{h}(\mu^{-1}(t)).

Similarly to the previous demonstration, we see that
P(t)
is a projection that verify (a) and (b) in Definition

, hence
V
admits an exponential dichotomy. From Proposition

\sigma(A)\cap i\mathbb{R}=\varnothing.
Using Lemma

It remains to prove the formula (

). For this pick
g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
and set

It is clear that
g\circ\mu^{-1}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R},X).
By Proposition
4
and, considering
\mu(t_{0})=t\in\mathbb{R},

\,t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptA^{-1}(g\circ\mu^{-1})(\mu(t_{0}))=A^{-1}(g\circ\mu^{-1})(t)&=\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t,s)(g\circ\mu^{-1})(s)ds\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rl}&=\displaystyle-\left[\int_{-\infty}^{t}\Gamma(t,s)(g\circ\mu^{-1})(s)ds+\int_{t}^{+\infty}\Gamma(t,s)(g\circ\mu^{-1})(s)ds\right]\\
&=\displaystyle-\left[\int_{-\infty}^{t}V(t,s)P(s)(g\circ\mu^{-1})(s)ds-\int_{t}^{+\infty}V_{Q}(t,s)^{-1}Q(s)(g\circ\mu^{-1})(s)ds\right]\\
&=\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{t}U(\mu^{-1}(t),\mu^{-1}(s))P(\mu(\mu^{-1}(s))g(\mu^{-1}(s))\,ds\\

&=\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{*}}\Gamma_{h}(t_{0},s_{0})g(s_{0})d\mu_{*}=w(t_{0}).\end{array}

Hence

. Thus

Example 2
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be given by
h(t)=e^{(t-2)^{3}}.

Then

h^{-1}(\tau)=2+\sqrt[3]{\ln\tau},\qquad e_{*}:=h^{-1}(1)=2,

and the inverse of
t
in
(\mathbb{R},*,\odot)
is

t^{*-1}=h^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{1}{h(t)}\Big)=2+\sqrt[3]{-(t-2)^{3}}=4-t.

Let
\{U(t_{0},s_{0})\}_{t_{0}\geq s_{0}}
be an
h
–evolution family on
X
satisfying the
h
–bound

\|U(t_{0},s_{0})\|\;\leq\;K\Big(\frac{h(t_{0})}{h(s_{0})}\Big)^{\alpha}=K\exp\!\big(\alpha\big[(t_{0}-2)^{3}-(s_{0}-2)^{3}\big]\big),\qquad t_{0}\geq s_{0}.

By Definition
12
, we say that
U
admits an
h
–dichotomy, that is, there exist projections
P_{h}(t_{0}):X\to X
and constants
N\geq 1
,
\nu>0
such that

\displaystyle P_{h}(t_{0})U(t_{0},s_{0})

\displaystyle=U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0}),\qquad t_{0}\geq s_{0},

\displaystyle\|U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq N\exp\!\big(-\nu\big[(t_{0}-2)^{3}-(s_{0}-2)^{3}\big]\big),

\displaystyle\|U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(t_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq N\exp\!\big(-\nu\big[(t_{0}-2)^{3}-(s_{0}-2)^{3}\big]\big),

where
Q_{h}(t_{0})=I-P_{h}(t_{0})
and
U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})
is the restriction

U(t_{0},s_{0})\big|_{Q_{h}(s_{0})X}:Q_{h}(s_{0})X\to Q_{h}(t_{0})X
.

From Proposition
9
, the evolution
h
–semigroup
\{T_{t_{0}}\}_{t_{0}\geq e_{*}}
on

C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)
is

(T_{t_{0}}u)(s_{0})=U\Big(s_{0},\;2+\sqrt[3]{(s_{0}-2)^{3}-(t_{0}-2)^{3}}\Big)\,u\Big(2+\sqrt[3]{(s_{0}-2)^{3}-(t_{0}-2)^{3}}\Big),

and its generator
B_{h}
(Definition
8
) becomes

B_{h}w=\lim_{t_{0}\to 2^{+}}\frac{T_{t_{0}}w-w}{\ln(h(t_{0}))}=\lim_{t_{0}\to 2^{+}}\frac{T_{t_{0}}w-w}{(t_{0}-2)^{3}},

with domain

D(B_{h})=\Bigl\{w\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X):\lim_{t_{0}\to 2^{+}}\frac{T_{t_{0}}w-w}{(t_{0}-2)^{3}}\ \text{exists in }C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)\Bigr\}.

Now, if we define the additive time variable
t:=\mu(t_{0})=(t_{0}-2)^{3}
and

V(t,s):=U\big(\mu^{-1}(t),\mu^{-1}(s)\big)=U\big(2+\sqrt[3]{t},\,2+\sqrt[3]{s}\big),\qquad t\geq s.

Then
V
is an exponentially bounded evolution family on
X
and its associated
(classical) evolution semigroup
\{S_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R},X)
is

(S_{t}v)(s)=V(s,s-t)\,v(s-t).

As shown in Theorem
1
, we have the conjugacy

T_{t_{0}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}S_{\mu(t_{0})}\mathcal{F},\qquad B_{h}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}A\mathcal{F},

where
A
is the generator of
\{S_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and

\mathcal{F}:C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{*},X)\to C_{0}(\mathbb{R},X)
is given by
(\mathcal{F}w)(s)=w(\mu^{-1}(s))
.

For
h
, one has

\frac{h^{\prime}(t)}{h(t)}=\frac{3(t-2)^{2}e^{(t-2)^{3}}}{e^{(t-2)^{3}}}=3(t-2)^{2},

so the invariant measure on
\mathbb{R}_{*}
is

d\mu_{*}(\tau)=3(\tau-2)^{2}\,d\tau.

Thus, the representation of
B_{h}^{-1}
in Theorem
2
becomes

(B_{h}^{-1}g)(t_{0})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{h}(t_{0},s_{0})\,g(s_{0})\,3(s_{0}-2)^{2}\,ds_{0},\qquad t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{*},

where

\Gamma_{h}(t_{0},s_{0})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}U(t_{0},s_{0})P_{h}(s_{0}),&&t_{0}>s_{0},\\
-\,U_{Q_{h}}(t_{0},s_{0})^{-1}Q_{h}(s_{0}),&&t_{0}<s_{0}.\end{array}\right.

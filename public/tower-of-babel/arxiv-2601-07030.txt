Special L L -values of certain CM weight three Hecke eigenforms
Paresh Arora, Koustav Mondal, Akio Nakagawa, Fang-Ting Tu

Abstract
. Ramanujan’s theories of elliptic functions to alternative bases for modular forms connect hypergeometric series with modular forms and have led to applications such as the modularity of certain hypergeometric Galois representations. In this paper, we relate special values of L L -function of certain CM cusp forms to Ramanujan’s alternative bases via the modularity of hypergeometric Galois representations arising from CM elliptic curves over real quadratic fields. We also give a complete classification of these representations.

Introduction

Special values of
L
-functions play a central role in modern number theory. They encode arithmetic information and serve as a bridge between analysis, algebra, and geometry, as illustrated by the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture and its generalizations, such as the Bloch–Kato and Beĭlinson–Bloch conjectures
[
17
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
78
,
79
, et al.]
. These conjectures relate the analytic behavior of
L
-functions to arithmetic invariants, including ranks of elliptic curves and Selmer groups. Meanwhile, Deligne’s conjecture on critical values predicts precise algebraic properties of special
L
-values attached to modular forms
[
34
]
. Such values are also of interest due to their connections with Bernoulli numbers, binary quadratic forms, and zeta functions of real quadratic fields, originating in the work of Eichler and Shimura, and further developed in the theory of Hilbert modular forms
[
27
,
39
,
51
,
52
,
73
, et al.]
.

Let
f
be a cusp form of weight
k
with Fourier expansion
f=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
. The associated
L
-series is defined by

L(f,s)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}.

The series converges in a right half-plane and extends analytically to the complex plane via the Mellin transform. Of particular interest are its critical values, which occur at integers
s=n+1
with
0\leq n\leq k-2
. The special values admit explicit integral expressions,

(1.1)

L(f,n+1)=\frac{(2\pi)^{n+1}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(it)t^{n}dt,

and are often referred to as periods of the form
f
.

As a classical example, the modularity theorem associates to every elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
a weight two Hecke eigenform
f_{E}
such that

L(E/\mathbb{Q},s)=L(f_{E},s).

The value
L(f_{E},1)=L(E,1)
is a rational multiple of the real period of
E
. In particular, when
E
has complex multiplication (CM) by an imaginary quadratic field
K
, its
L
-function can be described more explicitly in terms of a Hecke character
\psi_{E}
of
K
, which defines a one-dimensional Galois representation of
{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. The Hecke eigenform
f_{E}
arises via the induction of
\psi_{E}
to
\mathbf{GL}_{2}/\mathbb{Q}

[
35
]
. This perspective naturally leads to higher-weight Hecke eigenforms obtained from powers of Hecke characters. More generally, one expects that
L
-functions arising from the étale cohomology of higher-dimensional varieties defined over
\mathbb{Q}
are modular.
In particular, certain
K3
surfaces defined over
\mathbb{Q}
are known to be modular and correspond to weight
3
modular forms, see
[
2
,
30
,
40
]
for example. Understanding the special values of the associated
L
-functions is therefore an important problem, both from the perspective of arithmetic geometry and from that of automorphic forms.

In this paper, we compute special
L
-values attached to a class of weight
3
Hecke eigenforms using Ramanujan’s theories of elliptic functions to alternative bases. These theories relate modular forms to hypergeometric series, have led to many applications including Ramanujan
1/\pi
-series, and have more recently played a role in the study of certain
hypergeometric Galois representations

[
14
,
19
,
20
,
36
,
42
,
46
,
47
,
57
, et al.]
, which are expected to be modular or automorphic
according to Langlands philosophy
[
53
,
55
,
64
]
.

Our focus is on the Galois representations of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq{\rm Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
arising from hypergeometric type
K3
-surfaces
[
2
,
48
]
, and from the following families of elliptic curves

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x(1-x)(x-t),\qquad\widetilde{E}_{3}(t)\colon y^{2}+xy+\frac{t}{27}y=x^{3},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{4}(t)

\displaystyle\colon y^{2}=x\left(x^{2}+x+\frac{t}{4}\right),\qquad\widetilde{E}_{6}(t)\colon y^{2}+xy=x^{3}-\frac{t}{432}.

L

2.
Hypergeometric functions

This section provides a summary of classical hypergeometric functions, covering their definition, key properties, and certain transformation identities.
Throughout, all classical hypergeometric identities are assumed to hold whenever both sides of the equality are well defined and convergent. For detailed discussions, the reader is referred to
[
5
,
15
,
76
,
88
,
89
]
.

For multi-subsets
\alpha=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
,
\beta=\{b_{1}\coloneqq 1,\cdots,b_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{Q}
with
b_{i}\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
, and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, define the classical hypergeometric function
{}_{n}F_{n-1}
as

(2.1)

\,{}_{n}F_{n-1}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{n}\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{k}\cdots(a_{n})_{k}}{(b_{1})_{k}\cdots(b_{n})_{k}}{z^{n}},

where
(a)_{0}=1
,
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)=\Gamma(a+n)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol and
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function. The function
{}_{n}F_{n-1}
converges when
|z|<1
.

Each generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{n}F_{n-1}
satisfies a linear
Fuchsian differential equation of order
n
with three regular singular
points at
0
,
1
, and
\infty
. In the case
n=2
, the Gauss hypergeometric
function
\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}a&b\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
&c\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right]
, regarded as a function of
z
, satisfies the
hypergeometric differential equation

(2.2)

HDE(a,b,c;z):\qquad z(1-z)\frac{d^{2}F}{dz^{2}}+\bigl[c-(a+b+1)z\bigr]\frac{dF}{dz}-abF=0.

We record some of the key algebraic transformation properties of hypergeometric functions that will be essential for the rest of the paper. We emphasize that the arguments on the both hand-sides of the identities have absolute value less than

3.1.

We begin with the following general definition of Eisenstein series to take care of convergence in certain cases.

Definition 3.1

Proposition 3.2

-expansion

4.1.
Hecke Characters and Hecke L-functions

Hecke characters, also identified as Grössencharaktere or idéle class characters for number fields, generalize the notion of Dirichlet characters and have associated
L
-functions that also satisfy functional equations, following the treatment in
[
43
]
and
[
74
]
; other references include
[
63
,
68
]
. In what follows, we provide a brief overview and present an example to demonstrate more explicit connections to the theory of complex multiplication.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers

\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
A modulus
\mathfrak{m}
is a nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
I_{K}(\mathfrak{m})
denote the group of fractional ideals of
K
that are
coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
, and let
P_{K,1}(\mathfrak{m})
be the subgroup of
principal ideals
(\alpha)
satisfying the congruence

\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}
.

A Hecke character modulo
\mathfrak{m}
is a group homomorphism

\psi:I_{K}(\mathfrak{m})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

that factors through the ray class group
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak{m}}\coloneqq I_{K}(\mathfrak{m})\big/P_{K,1}(\mathfrak{m})
, and is determined by
a pair
(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
associated with the modulus

\mathfrak{m}
and infinity type
\{(n_{v})\}_{v\mid\infty}
, where
n_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The finite component is a finite-order character

\psi_{f}:(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},

and for any
\alpha\in P_{K,1}(\mathfrak{m})
, the archimedean component is defined by

\psi_{\infty}(\alpha)\coloneqq\prod_{v\mid\infty}\sigma_{v}(\alpha)^{\,n_{v}}.

For a principal ideal
(\alpha)
with
(\alpha,\mathfrak{m})=1
, the value of

\psi
is given by

\psi\big((\alpha)\big)\;=\;\psi_{f}(\alpha)\,\psi_{\infty}(\alpha).

The weight of the Hecke character
\psi
is
m=\sum_{v\mid\infty}n_{v}
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke character of conductor
\mathfrak{f}
, which is the minimum modulus such that
\psi
factors through it. The associated Hecke
L
-function is defined by

L(s,\psi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{f}}\left(1-\frac{\psi(\mathfrak{p})}{N\mathfrak{p}^{\,s}}\right)^{-1},\quad\mathrm{Re}\,(s)>1,

where
N\mathfrak{p}
is the absolute norm of
\mathfrak{p}
. It admits meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies a functional equation relating
L(s,\psi)
and
L(m+1-s,\psi)

[
63
,
74
]
.
When the field
K
is an imaginary quadratic field and
\psi
is non-trivial, the converse theorem for modular forms
[
56
,
85
]
implies that, there exists a cuspidal Hecke eigenform
f_{\psi}(\tau)
of weight
m+1
such that its associated
L
-function
L(f_{\psi},s)
satisfies

(4.1)

L(f_{\psi},s)=L\left(s,\psi\right).

We now recall the connection between CM elliptic curves and Hecke characters. For simplicity, we give the statement for curves have CM by maximal imaginary quadratic orders

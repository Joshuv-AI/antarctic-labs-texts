Boundary Curvature Scalars on Conformally Compact Manifolds
A. R. Gover ♭ , J. Kopiński ♮ & A Waldron ♯

Abstract
. We introduce a sequence of conformally invariant scalar curvature quantities, defined along the conformal infinity of a conformally compact (CC) manifold, that measure the failure of a CC metric to have constant negative scalar curvature in the interior, i.e. its failure to solve the singular Yamabe problem. Indeed, these “CC boundary curvature scalars” compute canonical expansion coefficients for singular Yamabe metrics. Residues of their poles yield obstructions to smooth solutions to the singular Yamabe problem and thus, in particular, give an alternate derivation of generalized Willmore invariants. Moreover, in a given dimension, the critical CC boundary scalar characterizes the image of a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the singular Yamabe problem. We give explicit formulæ for the first five CC boundary curvature scalars required for a global study of four dimensional singular Yamabe metrics, as well as asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes. Keywords: Singular Yamabe problem, conformal geometry, conformally compact, Dirichlet-to-Neumann, boundary and hypersurface invariants, Willmore type energies, asymptotically de Sitter, AdS/CFT, general relativity.

Remembering Stanley Deser

By Andrew Waldron

Stanley Deser was a truly wonderful human being. He had a highly developed sense of humor—everybody walked away smiling after spending any amount of time with him. He was also highly knowledgeable and incredibly intelligent. Chances are you also walked away wiser. I first met Stanley via a series of terse emails leading to a, for me formative, post-doctoral position at Brandeis in 1999. After leaving Brandeis, Stanley and I continued our highly fruitful collaboration over Skype.
Those meetings followed Stanley’s standard—50% jocular persiflage plus 50% question bombardment—format.
Back at Brandeis, Stanley usually arrived at work shortly after lunch, oftentimes muttering about seemingly agrarian peculiarities such as Bach and Cotton tensors. Later I realized this was because he was deeply interested in conformal anomalies. Indeed his work with Adam Schimmer on such was not only fundamental for the modern day understanding of how physical theories behave at different scales, but also laid the foundation for many mathematical studies of conformal manifolds.
Curiously enough, my work with Stanley on partially massless higher spin theories, led me first to Tom Branson and in turn Rod Gover, another long term collaborator and expert in all things conformal. In that sense there is direct line from those early mutterings to the current paper; indeed the operator in Equation (
20
) is the conformal antecedent of the spin 2 partially massless theory.
I am sure Stanley would have enjoyed the results presented here and sorely miss being able to discuss them with him. Maybe a discussion would have gone something like this (from one of our last email exchanges):

A:

{\tt What\ about\ a\ prequel\ to\ the\ Bible?\ The\ last\ line\ reads}

{\tt\hskip 20.99979pt\hskip 20.99979pt\hskip 10.4999pt``in\ the\ end\ there\ was\ light".}

S:

{\tt that^{\prime}s\ a\ typo:\ in\ the\ end\ there\ was\ gravity!}

. More recently, also in four dimensions, fundamental forms were used to characterize the allowed stress energy of asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes as well as initial, isotropic, cosmological singularities
[
14
,
15
]
.

3.
Conformally Compact Boundary Curvatures

To construct CC boundary curvatures we first need to solve Problem
1.1
. For that we recall the Laplace–Robin operator
(see
[
19
]
) specialized to
act on conformal densities.
Given any defining density
\bm{\eta}
, the conformally invariant Laplace–Robin operator mapping

{\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}}:\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[w])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[w-1])

is defined for a choice of
g\in\bm{g}
and
\bm{\varphi}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[w])
by

(6)

\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}{\varphi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}(d+2w-2)\big(\nabla_{\nu}+w\rho_{\eta})\varphi-\eta(\Delta^{g}+wJ^{g})\varphi\,.

In the above, the conormal
\nu:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\eta
and
\rho_{\eta}:=-\tfrac{1}{d}(\Delta^{g}\eta+J^{g}\eta)
.
Specializing to the case where
\bm{\varphi}
is the weight
w=1
defining density
\bm{\eta}
itself, we can recover the scalar curvature of the CC metric

g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
, via

(7)

{\mathscr{L}}_{\bm{\eta}}\bm{\eta}=-{\hskip 0.85358pt}\frac{Sc^{g_{+}}}{d-1}\,.

The above can be easily verified by examining the conformal transformation rule for scalar curvatures. Moreover

(8)

\frac{1}{d}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\mathscr{L}}_{\bm{\eta}}\bm{\eta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}|\nu|^{2}_{g}+2\rho_{\eta}\eta\,,

measures the squared-length of the conormal
\nu
along
\Sigma
, where
\eta=0
. Also, along
\Sigma
, the Laplace–Robin operator becomes a conformally invariant Robin-type normal operator with leading term
\nabla_{\nu}
. Indeed, it can be checked (see
[
16
]
) that, along
\Sigma
,

\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}^{k}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,\big|_{\Sigma}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(d+2w-k-i)\nabla^{k}_{\nu}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,\big|_{\Sigma}+{\rm ltots}\,,

where once again “ltots” denotes terms of lower transverse derivative order, meaning lower order in derivatives normal to
\Sigma
.
Evidently from Equation (
7
), specializing to a singular Yamabe metric
g_{+}^{\rm SY}=\bm{\sigma}^{-2}\bm{g}
, then
\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\sigma}}\bm{\sigma}=1
and for any
k\geq 2
one has

\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\sigma}}^{k}\bm{\sigma}=0\,.

Hence, Problem
1.1
is solved by setting

\delta_{k}=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}^{k}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt{\hskip 0.85358pt}\big|_{\Sigma}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(d+2w-k-i)}\,,

so long as the denominator is non-zero.
Indeed, the precise structure of dimension-dependent poles in such operators is important.
Because we are here acting on weight one densities the denominator is

(d-k+1)(d-k)\cdots(d-2k+2)\,.

Observe that the Robin-type operator mapping, for some choice of
g\in\bm{g}
,

(9)

\bm{\varphi}\to(\nabla_{\nu}+w\rho_{\nu})\varphi\big|_{\Sigma}\,,

is conformally invariant, so from Equation (
6
) we see that
(d-2k+2)^{-1}\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}^{k}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt{\hskip 0.85358pt}\big|
is both well-defined and conformally invariant when
d=2(k-1)
. In fact, there are other removable singularities when the operator
\mathscr{L}_{\bm{\eta}}^{k}
is suitably improved by adding conformally invariant subleading transverse order terms, see Gover and Peterson
[
16
]
. The pole at
d=k-1
, cannot be removed this way. While such modified operators can be used to solve Problem
1.1
for further values of
k
, they cannot be used when
k=d+1
. Indeed, from Equation (
2
), we see that this is precisely the order where, in general, the singular Yamabe solution
\bm{\sigma}
fails to be smooth along
\Sigma
so that
\nabla_{\nu}^{d+1}\sigma
is not well-defined along
\Sigma
.
In
d
dimensions, for a choice of
g\in\bm{g}
we will explicitly construct operators (for cases
k\leq 5
and motivated by the methods of
[
16
]
), such that

(10)

\delta_{k}\propto\frac{\bar{B}_{k-1}}{d-k+1}+\delta_{k}^{\rm ren}\,,

where the operator
\delta_{k}^{\rm ren}
is not singular when evaluated in
d=k-1
dimensions and
\bar{B}_{k-1}
is some tensor defined in any dimension that specializes to the conformally invariant obstruction density for the
singular Yamabe problem in

d=k-1
dimensions. Upon suitably specializing the embedding geometry, the operator
\delta_{d+1}^{\rm ren}
also yields an invariant that characterizes a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for the singular Yamabe problem.

In fact, there are further poles that cannot be removed by applying the Gover–Peterson improvement. There are two cases to consider.
The first is when the residue of the pole is given by the restriction to the critical dimension
d_{\rm crit}
of a tensor that is conformally invariant in all dimensions. Then one can construct a conformally invariant operator by simply removing the invariant pole term. One then must check whether the onshell Property (
iii
) still holds.
In second case the residue is some tensor that has no invariant continuation to general dimensions. In that case the variation of the residue takes the form
(d-d_{\rm crit})X
for some tensor
X
. This determines the failure of the remainder to be invariant after the pole is removed. Thus one must
impose the condition
X=0

in order that the pole can be removed and still yield an invariant.
For example, when
d=3
requiring
\pi_{1}=1
, or equivalently that the geometry is asymptotically hyperbolic, suffices to remove such a residue this way.
The fact that we can find CC boundary scalars whose invariance in certain critical dimensions is conditioned on restrictions upon lower order CC boundary scalars
mimics a similar feature observed in
[
4
]
for conformal fundamental forms.

3.1.
Expansion Coefficients

To construct an asymptotic solution to the singular Yamabe problem,
typically one considers a distinguished compactified metric
g_{\rm GL}(\bar{g})\in\bm{g}
, where
\bar{g}\in\bm{g}
denotes a given choice of boundary metric in the conformal class of metrics induced by
\bm{g}
. For that first observe that
given any CC metric
g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
,
using Equations (
7
) and (
8
) we can canonically construct, at least in a collar neighborhood of
\Sigma
, an asymptotically hyperbolic metric

g_{+}^{\rm AH}=\hat{\bm{\eta}}^{-2}\bm{g}\,,

by setting

\hat{\bm{\eta}}=\sqrt{-\frac{d(d-1)}{Sc^{g_{+}}}}\>\bm{\eta}\,.

Then one relies on a key result of Graham and Lee
[
25
]
that given any asymptotically hyperbolic metric
g_{+}^{\rm AH}=\hat{\bm{\eta}}^{-2}\bm{g}
as well as a boundary metric
\bar{g}\in\bar{\bm{g}}
, there exists a “Graham–Lee” scale
\bm{\tau}_{\rm GL}
such that, also at least in a sufficiently small collar neighborhood of
\Sigma
, the function

r:=\frac{\hat{\bm{\eta}}}{\bm{\tau}_{\rm GL}}

is the geodesic distance to the boundary as measured by the compactified metric
g_{\rm GL}=\bm{\tau}_{\rm GL}^{-2}\bm{g}
. Away from the collar there may be no distinguished choice for
\bm{\tau}_{\rm GL}
, but this ambiguity is irrelevant for our considerations. Now, given the Graham-Lee compactified metric
g_{\rm GL}(\bar{g})
for some (reference) CC metric
g_{+}
and corresponding distance function
r
, one expands the singular Yamabe scale
\bm{\sigma}
expressed in the Graham–Lee choice of scale as

\sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{\rm GL}\!(\bar{g})}}{{=}}r(s_{0}+s_{1}r+s_{2}r^{2}+\cdots)\,,

to as high order as regularity of the singular Yamabe scale
\bm{\sigma}
allows.
Here, one can require that the functions
s_{i}
obey the Lie derivative condition
{\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}s_{i}=0
, since the Graham–Lee metric
g_{\rm GL}
admits a normal form

g_{\rm GL}(\bar{g})=dr^{2}+h\,,

where the symmetric tensor
h(r)
obeys

h({\frac{\partial}{\partial r}},{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt{\hskip 0.85358pt})=0
, so that
g^{-1}_{\rm GL}(dr,{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt{\hskip 0.85358pt})={\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}
. Moreover, since the unit conormal
\hat{n}
with respect to
g_{\rm GL}
equals
{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}r|_{\Sigma}
, it follows that acting on scalars, the operator

\hat{n}^{a_{1}}\cdots\hat{n}^{a_{k}}\nabla_{a_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{a_{k}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}={\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}^{k}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}\,.

Hence from Equation (
5
) we have that
-1/k!
times the terms labeled “ltots” in Point (
i
) of Problem
1.1
yields the expansion coefficient
s_{k-1}

in the above expansion. Explicit results for
s_{0},\ldots s_{4}
are given in Section (
5.5.5
).

4.
The Dirichlet-to-Neumann Map

We wish to characterize the Neumann data for the singular Yamabe problem in a canonical way.
Given a second
order elliptic PDE one may prescribe only its Dirichlet data to study its solution space. Thus, an apparent freedom to prescribe further Neumann data is only a property of formal asymptotic solutions, for example the density
\bm{C}
in Equation (
2
) depends on the original choice of defining density
\bm{\sigma}_{0}
so is not an absolute invariant of the underlying geometric structure. However, there are situations, such as Poincaré–Einstein metrics in even dimensions, where
one can assign a conformally invariant Dirichlet-to-Neumann map to the geometric structure
[
23
,
5
]
. Rather than relying on dimension parity-dependent properties of Poincaré–Einstein metrics, here we will construct
a conformally invariant Dirichlet-to-Neumann map in the case when the extrinsic geometry of the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{g})
is suitably restricted. While the procedure is general in nature, we focus on cases where
d\leq 4
.

Our plan is to “renormalize” the operator
\delta_{k}
in Equation (
10
), by replacing it with
\delta_{k}^{\rm ren}
in the case where
d=k-1
. For this we need to know whether
\delta_{k}^{\rm ren}
is invariant. For that we compute the conformal variation of
\bar{B}_{k-1}
in
d
dimensions.
In the current setting involving natural tensors, it can be shown (see for example
[
16
]
) that this must take the form

\bar{B}^{\Omega^{2}g}_{k-1}-\bar{\Omega}^{1-k}\bar{B}^{g}_{k-1}=(d-k+1)\beta\,,

for some tensor
\beta
that is non-singular in
d=k-1
dimensions.
We then analyze sufficient conditions on the extrinsic geometry of the conformal embedding
\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{g})
such that
\beta
vanishes. When such conditions exist it follows, upon imposing them,
that the renormalized operator
\delta_{d+1}^{\rm ren}
is conformally invariant. Dimensional continuation arguments of this type
can be rather subtle, so we also
check this invariance explicitly.

The operator
\delta_{d+1}^{\rm ren}
can now be used to invariantly extract singular Yamabe Neumann data. However, since its leading transverse order is
d+1
, it is ill-defined acting on solutions with only
C^{d}
regularity. Hence—see Equation (
2
)—we must further restrict the extrinsic geometry such that the obstruction
\bar{\bm{B}}_{d}=0
.
For our current purposes let us call a conformal embedding such that both
\beta
and
\bar{\bm{B}}_{d}
vanish
superumbilic
, because in dimension
d=3
this amounts to the embedding being umbilic. For superumbilic geometries, we can define a
Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
which takes as input a closed, oriented, superumbilic, hypersurface embedding in a suitable conformal manifold,
and outputs a conformal density according to

\big(\Sigma\hookrightarrow(M,\bm{g})\big)\longmapsto\delta^{\rm ren}_{d+1}\bm{\sigma}_{\rm SY}=:\pi_{d+1}^{\rm ren}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[-d])\,.

In the above
\bm{\sigma}_{\rm SY}
is the uniquely determined solution to the singular Yamabe problem defined on the interior region
M_{+}
determined by the closed hypersurface
\Sigma
. We also require that the interior region
M_{+}
defined by
\Sigma
has itself boundary
\Sigma
.

At this point it might be unclear how the above map is related to an undetermined term in an asymptotic solution such as the density
\bm{C}
in Equation (
2
). One may even think, based on Equation (
5
) and the discussion of Section
3.1
, that the image
\pi_{d+1}^{\rm ren}
of the Dirichlet-to-Neuman map vanishes.
Indeed, when the geometry is superumbilic,
the singular Yamabe scale is smooth and the quantity

{\mathscr{L}}_{\bm{\sigma}}^{d+1}\bm{\sigma}

is well-defined and vanishing. However, since
\pi_{d+1}^{\rm ren}
is constructed via the dimensional continuation coming from Equation (
10
), it does not
a priori
follow that
\pi_{d+1}^{\rm ren}=0
. In fact, given some
\bar{g}\in\bar{\bm{g}}
,
in the Graham–Lee scale

\bm{\tau}_{\rm GL}^{\rm SY}

of the singular Yamabe metric itself, for the case
d=3
,
we will find

\pi^{\rm ren}_{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}}}{{=}}-3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}(\nabla_{c}P_{ab})\big|_{\Sigma}\,.

When one is equipped with a reference CC metric
g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
with geodesic distance function
\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}}}{{=}}r
, and the embedding is superumbilic, one has the smooth expansion

\sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}}}{{=}}r(s_{0}+s_{1}r+\cdots+s_{d-1}r^{d-1}+Cr^{d})+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{d+2})\,.

Thus

\pi_{d+1}^{\rm ren}=(d+1)!\,C+{\rm ltots}\,,

and so can be used to compute the formally undetermined coefficient
C
in the asymptotic solution. Explicit formulæ are given in Equations (
17
,
25
) below.

5.
Explicit Results

We are now ready to present the first five CC boundary curvatures. The fifth is novel. As discussed in Section
3
these can be computed by studying powers of the Laplace–Robin operator. In principle it would also be possible to employ the tractor calculus construction of
[
16
]
, but at fifth order this involves a rank five tractor, a difficulty avoided by the Laplace–Robin method. Another method is simply to write down all possible extrinsically coupled Riemannian operators of the correct homogeneity and then determine which linear combination of terms solves Problem
1.1
.
For this it is sufficient to study linearized conformal variations; see
[
6
]
and the discussion in Section
1.1
. For the results that follow, we employed both the Laplace–Robin operator construction and linearized variation methods using the symbolic manipulation software FORM
[
34
]
.
Note that, as mentioned earlier, one can also act with the normal operators of
[
16
]
on the scalar curvature of the CC metric
g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
. That method produces curvatures quadratic in
\bm{\eta}
and returns zero when
\bm{\eta}
is the singular Yamabe scale.

5.1.
First conformally compact boundary scalar

The operator

\delta_{1}:\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[0])\,,

defined for some
g\in\bm{g}

by

\delta_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\nabla_{a}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}\,.

Observe that acting on the space
\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])
,
the Robin operator of Equation (
9
) becomes the above conformally invariant Neumann-type operator.
Hence
\delta_{1}
obeys Properties (
i
) and (
ii
). Now let
g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
be CC. Then the first CC scalar curvature is simply

\pi_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\delta_{1}\eta=\hat{n}.\nu|_{\Sigma}\,.

Here
\nu:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\eta
.
By virtue of Equation (
8
), when
\bm{\eta}
equals the singular Yamabe defining density
\bm{\sigma}
,
it follows that
|n|_{g}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma}}{{=}}1
so that
\hat{n}=n|_{\Sigma}
, where
n:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\sigma
. Hence, in that case
\delta_{1}\sigma=1
and the on-shell Property (
iii
) holds.

Notice that the condition
\pi_{1}=1
, is equivalent to requiring that
g_{+}
be asymptotically hyperbolic. Moreover, if
\pi_{1}^{\rm ext}
is any smooth extension of
\pi_{1}
to
M
, then the metric

g_{+}^{\rm AH}=\frac{g_{+}}{(\pi_{1}^{\rm ext})^{2}}

is necessarily asymptotically hyperbolic.

5.2.
Second conformally compact boundary scalar

The operator

\delta_{2}:\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[-1])\,,

is defined for some
g\in\bm{g}

by

(11)

\delta_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}-H\delta_{1}\,.

Clearly property (
i
) holds,
and using the conformal transformation rule for mean curvatures

\bar{\Omega}H^{\Omega^{2}g}=H^{g}+\hat{n}.\Upsilon|_{\Sigma}\,,

where
\Upsilon:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\log\Omega
,
it is not difficult to verify property (
ii
). For the onshell property (
iii
), we need a short computation. Firstly,
it can easily be proved (see for example
[
13
]
) that

\rho_{\sigma}|_{\Sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{=}}-H^{g}\,,

when
g_{+}^{\rm SY}=\bm{\sigma}^{-2}\bm{g}
is a singular Yamabe metric.
But, in that case (calling
\rho:=\rho_{\sigma}
)

\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\sigma|_{\Sigma}=\hat{n}^{a}n^{b}\nabla_{a}n_{b}|_{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{n}^{a}\nabla_{a}n^{2}|_{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{n}^{a}\nabla_{a}(1-2\rho\sigma)|_{\Sigma}=-\rho|_{\Sigma}\,.

In the above we repeatedly used Equation (
8
). The onshell property (
iii
) now follows. Hence, relaxing to the case when
g_{+}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}\bm{g}
is CC,

\pi_{2}:=\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}\nu_{b}|_{\Sigma}-H\pi_{1}

is the second CC boundary scalar.

Remark 5.1
.

The second order normal operator
\delta_{2}
becomes critical in dimension
d=1
. To see that, call
\kappa:=\bar{g}^{ab}{\rm{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}_{ab}
. Then the mean curvature
H=\tfrac{1}{d-1}\kappa
, and we can rewrite Equation (
11
) as

\delta_{2}=\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}-\tfrac{1}{d-1}\kappa\,\delta_{1}\,.

Although
\kappa=0
in dimension
d=1
, it does not follow that
\hat{\delta}_{2}:=\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}
is an invariant operator when
d=1
. Rather

\bar{\Omega}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hat{\delta}_{2}^{\Omega^{2}g}\circ\Omega=\hat{\delta}_{2}+\hat{n}^{a}\Upsilon_{a}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}\,.

This is an example of the general phenomenon discussed in Section
4
.

5.3.
Third conformally compact boundary scalar

At normal orders three and higher, another subtle phenomenon arises.
Namely, conformally invariant operators and curvatures intrinsic to the boundary appear as residues of poles when the dimension
d
is viewed as a parameter.
We will use the notation
\delta_{k}^{[I]}
where
I
is some set of positive integers for which the operator is not defined for reasons of poles
1/(d-x)
for
x\in I
.
Recall that from Equation (
2
), the order
k
CC boundary scalar is sensitive to Neumann data in dimension
d=k-1
.
This will correspond to a pole
1/(d-k+1)

in the operator
\delta_{k}^{[I]}
. We are less interested in poles
1/(d-x)
for integers
x<k-1
, because these probe data beyond the order of the Neumann term. Thus we primarily focus on integers
k-1\leq x\in I
.

The operator

\delta_{3}^{[2,3]}:\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[-2])\,,

is defined for
d\neq 3
and some
g\in\bm{g}

by

\delta_{3}^{[2,3]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{\delta}_{3}-\Big(\tfrac{3}{d-3}\bar{\Delta}-\tfrac{2}{d-2}{\hskip 0.85358pt}K\Big)\delta_{1}\,\,,

where

\hat{\delta}_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}+2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\delta_{1}\,\,.

For later use, in dimensions
d>2
and for any
x\neq 2,3
, we also define the operator

\delta_{3}^{(x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}+\Big(2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}+\tfrac{2}{x-2}{\hskip 0.85358pt}K\Big)\delta_{1}-\tfrac{3}{x-3}\bar{\Delta}\delta_{1}\,.

Observe that the singularity of
(d-3)\delta^{[3]}_{3}
at
d=3
is removable and yields the operator
-3\bar{\Delta}\delta_{1}
which is conformally invariant precisely in dimension
d=3
because the Laplacian
\bar{\Delta}
intrinsic to
\Sigma
is conformally invariant acting on functions, or equivalently conformal densities of weight
0
. (There is also a removable singularity in
(d-2)\delta^{[3]}_{3}
when
d=2
, but the corresponding conformal invariant
K:=\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab}
vanishes because the second fundamental form is pure trace for curves embedded in two-manifolds.)
That the operator
\delta^{[3]}_{3}
obeys Properties (
i
-
iii
) in general dimensions
d
can be checked relatively easily by direct computation.

In dimensions
d\geq 4
, we now obtain a third
CC boundary scalar

(12)

\pi_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nu_{c}|_{\Sigma}+\big(2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}-\tfrac{3}{d-3}\bar{\Delta}+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tfrac{2}{d-2}K\big)\pi_{1}\,.

Now let us consider the case
d=3
.
The linearized conformal variation of
\bar{\Delta}\pi_{1}
is

\bar{\updelta}^{L}\bar{\Delta}\pi=(d-3)\nabla_{\bar{\Upsilon}}\pi_{1}\,,

where
\bar{\Upsilon}:={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\bar{\varpi}
.
Thus, as explained in Section
4
, we remove the term
-\tfrac{3}{d-3}\bar{\Delta}
from
\pi_{3}
and then continue to
d=3
dimensions and require that

\pi_{1}
is constant, in which case also

\bar{\Delta}\pi_{1}=0
.
Then, as can be easily verified,

\pi_{3}|_{\pi_{1}=1,d=3}:=\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nu_{c}|_{\Sigma}+2(P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}+K)\,,

is conformally invariant meaning
\pi_{3}|_{\pi_{1}=1,d=3}(\Omega^{2}g,\Omega\sigma)=\bar{\Omega}^{-2}\pi_{3}|_{\pi_{1}=1,d=3}(g,\sigma)
.
Recall that for asymptotically hyperbolic metrics
\pi_{1}=1
.
So
\pi_{3}|_{\pi_{1}=1,d=3}
is invariant when
g_{+}^{\rm AH}=\bm{\eta}^{-2}g
is asymptotically hyperbolic.
This is the conditional invariance phenomenon first discussed in Section
3
.

There is a rather interesting way to relax the
asymptotically hyperbolic condition for embeddings that are nowhere umbilic. For that we define an operator mapping
\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}[-4])
for
d>2
and any
g\in\bm{g}
by

(13)

K\bowtie\hat{\delta}_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}K\big(\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}+2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}\hat{n}^{a}\nabla_{a}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,|_{\Sigma}\big)+\tfrac{3}{2}(\bar{\nabla}^{a}K)\bar{\nabla}_{a}\,\delta_{1}\,.

Since the rigidity density vanishes iff the embedding is umbilic, the above operator
can be used to define a third CC boundary scalar for
d=3
, nowhere-umbilic embeddings,

\pi_{3}^{\scalebox{.7}{$\mbox{\sout{ umbilic }}$}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nu_{c}|_{\Sigma}+\big(2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}+\tfrac{3}{2}(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\log K){\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\big)\pi_{1}\,.

The situation in
d=2
dimensions is particularly subtle.
The pole term
\frac{2}{d-2}K
in Equation (
12
) poses no difficulty
because it is invariant in general dimensions and moreover
\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}
and hence
K
vanish identically in two dimensions. The issue is instead the Schouten tensor term, since in
d
dimensions

P^{g}=\frac{1}{d-2}\big(Ric-\frac{1}{d-1}{\hskip 0.85358pt}g{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}Sc\big)
, and there is no condition on the geometry alone for which the linearized conformal variation

-(d-2)\nabla\Upsilon
of the tensor
Ric-\frac{1}{d-1}g{\hskip 0.85358pt}Sc
vanishes.
However an operator of transverse order three ought access the undetermined Neumann data for singular Yamabe solutions when
d=2
. Therefore we now no longer search for an invariant third order CC boundary scalar that vanishes when evaluated on singular Yamabe metrics, but instead search for a third normal order operator with a distinguished conformal transformation.
For that we observe that in
d
dimensions one has the Ricci-type identity

\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}P_{ab}|_{\Sigma}=J|_{\Sigma}-\bar{J}+\tfrac{d-1}{2}H^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2(d-2)}K\,.

Hence, in
d
dimensions, we see from Equation (
12
) that the scalar

(\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nu_{c}+2J)|_{\Sigma}-2\bar{J}+(d-1)H^{2}

is conformally invariant. However, because there is no intrinsic scalar curvature quantity in one dimension,
\bar{J}
is not defined when
d=2
. Hence let us consider

\pi_{3}^{\rm rel}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}(\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nu_{c}+2J)|_{\Sigma}+H^{2}\,.

We note that this defines a
relative conformal invariant
, meaning that if
{\mathcal{J}}=[\bar{g},j]
is any equivalence class of boundary tensors such that

(\bar{g},j)\sim\Big(\bar{\Omega}^{2}\bar{g},\bar{\Omega}^{-2}\big(j-\bar{\Delta}\log\bar{\Omega}+\tfrac{1}{2}|{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\log\bar{\Omega}|^{2}_{\bar{g}}\big)\Big)\,,

then the combination

\pi_{3}^{\rm rel}-2{\mathcal{J}}

is conformally invariant. The additional structure
{\mathcal{J}}
required along
\Sigma
amounts to a tensor that transforms in the same way as
\bar{J}
would have had it existed. This can be viewed as a one dimensional analog of a Möbius structure.

5.4.
Fourth conformally compact boundary scalar

The fourth CC boundary scalar measures local deviations of CC metrics from the singular Yamabe metric in dimensions
d>3
. In three dimensions, we can use it to extract the Willmore invariant as well as study Neumann data for the singular Yamabe metric. (As promised, from hereon we focus on operators in dimensions
d>2
so will need no special treatment of
d=2
poles.) Before writing down the requisite normal operator
\delta_{4}^{[3,5]}
, we need some additional technology.

Let
\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}
be a symmetric trace-free tensor on
\Sigma
. Then we can define an operator
{\sf L}_{ab}
, mapping
\Gamma(\odot_{\circ}^{2}T^{*}\Sigma[4-d])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[-d])
,
such that

\bar{\Omega}^{d}{\sf L}_{ab}^{\Omega^{2}g}(\bar{\Omega}^{4-d}\mathring{\bar{X}}^{ab})={{\sf L}}^{g}_{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}^{ab}\,,

given for any
g\in\bm{g}
by

(14)

{\sf L}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}+\bar{P}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}\,,&d>3\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}+P^{\top}_{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}^{ab}+H\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}\,,&d=3\,.\end{array}\right.

Also, in any dimension
d
, let us call
{\sf L}^{(3)}_{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}^{ab}:=\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}+P^{\top}_{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}^{ab}+H\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}
.
Of course, this quantity only enjoys a conformal invariance property when
d=3
.
In particular, let us define in any dimension the scalar

\bar{B}_{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}-\tfrac{1}{3}{\sf L}^{(3)}_{ab}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\,,

which equals the Willmore invariant when
d=3
.
Its (
d
-dimensional) linearized conformal variation is given by

(15)

\bar{\updelta}^{L}_{-3}\bar{B}_{3}=(d-3)\big(\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\Upsilon}_{b}+2(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab})\bar{\Upsilon}^{b}\big)=(d-3){\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{\square}^{\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{(3)}\bar{\varpi}\,,

where the operator

\bar{\square}^{\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{(d)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\big((d-2){\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}+2(\bar{\nabla}_{a}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab})\bar{\nabla}_{b}\big)

is conformally invariant
acting on functions in
d
dimensions.

In general dimensions we employ the notation
\bar{\sf L}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}+\bar{P}^{ab}\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}
even in the case when the tensor
\mathring{\bar{X}}_{ab}
may not have the correct transformation property/weight required for this combination to be invariant.

We need one further operator intrinsic to the conformal manifold
(\Sigma,\bar{\bm{g}})
:
Let
\bar{f}
be a smooth function on
\Sigma
. Then when
\bar{d}={\rm dim}\Sigma\geq 2
, we define the
Yamabe operator

\bar{\square}
, mapping
\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[1-\frac{\bar{d}}{2}])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[-1-\frac{\bar{d}}{2}])
,
for any
\bar{g}\in\bar{\bm{g}}
by

\bar{\square}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{g}}}{{:=}}\bar{\Delta}+\big(1-\tfrac{\bar{d}}{2}\big)\bar{J}\,.

This obeys the conformal invariance property

\bar{\Omega}^{1+\tfrac{\bar{d}}{2}}\,\bar{\square}^{\bar{\Omega}^{2}\bar{g}}\big(\bar{\Omega}^{1-\tfrac{\bar{d}}{2}}\bar{f}\big)=\bar{\square}^{\bar{g}}\bar{f}\,.

Given an integer
x
, let us denote in any dimension the operator
\bar{\square}^{(x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{g}}}{{:=}}\bar{\Delta}+\big(1-\tfrac{x}{2}\big)\bar{J}
. Note that, acting on a section
\bar{f}
of
{\mathcal{E}}\Sigma[w]
, its linearized conformal variation is

(16)

\updelta^{\rm L}_{w}(\bar{\square}^{(x)}f)=(\bar{d}+2w-2)\bar{\nabla}_{\bar{\Upsilon}}f+(w-1+\tfrac{x}{2})(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\Upsilon}_{a})f\,.

We are now ready to build the fourth CC boundary curvature.
The operator

\delta_{4}^{[3,5]}:\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{0}M[1])\to\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}M[-3])\,,

is defined for
d\neq 3,5
and some
g\in\bm{g}

by

\delta_{4}^{[3,5]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{\delta}_{4}-\tfrac{6}{d-5}\bar{\square}^{(4)}\delta_{2}-\tfrac{3}{d-3}\big(2\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\hskip 1.99168pt{\rm\mathring{\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{ab}-3\bar{B}_{3}\big)\delta_{1}\,,

where

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{4}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\hat{n}^{d}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\nabla_{d}\,{\textstyle\bm{\cdot}}\hskip 0.14226pt\,\big|_{\Sigma}+2H\hat{\delta}_{3}+2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}\delta_{2}

\displaystyle\>-\big(8{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}-\tfrac{3}{d-2}(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab})+4(\bar{\nabla}^{a}H)\bar{\nabla}_{a}-2HP_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}-3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}(\nabla_{a}P_{bc})\big)\delta_{1}\,.

Observe that the residues of the
d=3,5
poles in
\delta_{3}^{[3,5]}
are conformally invariant when
d=3,5
, respectively.

In dimensions
5\neq d\geq 4
we now obtain a fourth CC boundary curvature

\displaystyle\pi_{4}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\hat{n}^{d}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\nu_{d}|_{\Sigma}\!-\tfrac{6}{d-5}\bar{\square}^{(4)}\pi_{2}\!-\tfrac{3}{d-3}\big(2\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\hskip 1.99168pt{\rm\mathring{\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{ab}\!-3\bar{B}_{3}\big)\pi_{1}-\!\tfrac{4}{d-2}\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}HK\pi_{1}

\displaystyle\>\>+\!2H\big(\pi_{3}\!+\!\tfrac{3}{d-3}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{\Delta}\pi_{1}\big)\!+\!2P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}\pi_{2}

\displaystyle\>\>-\Big(8{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\!\bar{\nabla}_{b}-\tfrac{3}{d-2}(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\bar{\nabla}^{b}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}_{ab})\!\!+\!4(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\!H)\bar{\nabla}_{a}\!\!-\!2H\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}\!\!-\!3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}(\nabla_{\hat{n}}\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{ab})\Big)\pi_{1}.

Let us now analyze this result. First note from Equation (
12
), that the combination

\pi_{3}\!+\!\tfrac{3}{d-3}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\bar{\Delta}\pi_{1}

has no pole at
d=3
. The remaining singularity in
(d-3)\pi_{4}
at
d=3
is removable and, as promised, yields the multiple

9\bar{B}_{3}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\pi_{1}

of the higher Willmore invariant
\bar{B}_{3}
.

Next we consider the case
d=5
. Importantly, while
\bar{\square}^{(4)}\pi_{2}
is invariant when
d=5
, its failure to be invariant in general dimensions is proportional to
d-5
; see Equation (
16
). Hence, along exactly the same lines as discussed in Section
5.3
for
\pi_{3}
in
d=3
dimensions, to obtain a fourth CC boundary curvature in
d=5
dimensions, we must condition on vanishing of the second CC boundary curvature
\pi_{2}
, yielding the conformal invariant

\displaystyle\pi_{4}\big|_{\pi_{2}=0,d=5}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{:=}}\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\hat{n}^{d}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\nabla_{c}\nu_{d}|_{\Sigma}+2H\pi_{3}-\tfrac{15}{2}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\hskip 1.99168pt{\rm\mathring{\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{ab}\pi_{1}

\displaystyle\>-\Big(8{\hskip 0.85358pt}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\bar{\nabla}_{a}\!\bar{\nabla}_{b}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\bar{\nabla}_{a}\bar{\nabla}_{b}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab})+\tfrac{3}{2}\bar{P}_{ab}\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}+3H\bar{\Delta}+\!4(\bar{\nabla}^{a}\!H)\bar{\nabla}_{a}+\tfrac{4}{3}HK

\displaystyle\qquad-\,2H\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}-3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}(\nabla_{\hat{n}}\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{ab})\Big)\pi_{1}.

It now remains to study the case
d=3
. We already know that
(d-3)\pi_{4}
recovers the higher Willmore invariant
\bar{B}_{3}
, which obstructs smoothness of singular Yamabe solutions. We now wish to extract their Neumann data. We must consider the residue of the
d=3
pole in
\pi_{4}(g_{+}^{\rm SY})
which is given by
9\bar{B}_{3}
. Notice here that not only does
\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}^{ab}\hskip 1.99168pt{\rm\mathring{\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{I\hskip-0.56905ptI\hskip-0.56905ptI}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}}_{ab}
vanish when
d=3
but more importantly, this tensor is invariant in general dimensions
d

so can be discarded without affecting conformal invariance of the normal operator we are trying to construct.
Vanishing of the variation of the Willmore invariant as given in Equation (
15
)
for general exact one-forms
\bar{\Upsilon}
imposes the condition
\hskip 0.28453pt{\rm\mathring{{I\hskip-0.56905ptI}}}{\hskip 0.56905pt}=0
, or in other words umbilicity of the embedding.
This in turn implies that the
Willmore invariant
\bar{B}_{3}=0
.
Hence, because
\pi_{1}-1=0=\pi_{2}=\pi_{3}
on singular Yamabe solutions, for umbilic embeddings
\Sigma^{2}\hookrightarrow(M^{3},\bm{g})

we obtain

(17)

\displaystyle\pi_{4}^{\rm ren}

\displaystyle:=\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{\hat{n}}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}n_{c}|_{\Sigma}+2H\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}+3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\nabla_{\hat{n}}\!{\hskip 0.85358pt}P_{ab}\,.

This defines a conformal invariant of three dimensional singular Yamabe metrics for conformal manifolds whose boundary is
umbilically embedded. Moreover, since it is normal order three, it extracts the Neumann data.

5.4.1.
Expansion coefficients

We can also compute the expansion coefficients for the singular Yamabe density
\bm{\sigma}
relative to a given CC metric
g_{+}
. Let us here focus on
d=3
dimensions. Let
g_{\rm GL}(\bar{g})
be the Graham–Lee compactified metric for a choice of
\bar{g}\in\bar{\bm{g}}
and

When in addition the embedding is umbilic we have

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{\rm GL}}}{{=}}r\big(1+\tfrac{1}{2}Hr-\tfrac{1}{3}P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}r^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4!}\left[3\sqrt{\tfrac{\det g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}}{\det g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}}}\,\big(\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}P_{bc}\big)^{g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}}-3\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{a}P_{bc}-2HP_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}\right]{\hskip 0.85358pt}r^{4}

(18)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{5!}\left[4\Big(\tfrac{\det g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}}{\det g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big(\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\hat{n}^{d}(\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}P_{cd})-(P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}})^{2}\Big)^{g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\>\>\>-5H\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\nabla_{\hat{n}}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}n_{c}|_{\Sigma}-10H^{2}P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}}-4\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}\hat{n}^{d}(\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}P_{cd})

\displaystyle\qquad\>\>\>-20H\hat{n}^{a}\hat{n}^{b}\hat{n}^{c}(\nabla_{a}P_{bc})+4(P_{\hat{n}\hat{n}})^{2}+(\bar{\nabla}_{a}H)(\bar{\nabla}^{a}H)\Bigg]\>r^{5}+{\mathcal{O}}(r^{6})\,,

where
g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}(\bar{g})
is the Graham–Lee compactified metric for the singular Yamabe metric
g_{+}^{\rm SY}
.
The above was obtained by comparing
\pi^{\rm ren}_{4}
when computed with respect to
g_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm GL}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm SY}(\bar{g})
and
g_{+}^{\rm SY}
.
We also used that
the mean curvature of a
Graham–Lee compactified metric for the singular Yamabe metric vanishes.
In addition we used the fifth order
tensor of Section
5.5.4
to compute one term beyond the order of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map.

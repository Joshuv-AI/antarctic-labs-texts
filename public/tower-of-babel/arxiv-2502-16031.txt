The applications of Bieri-Neumann-Strebel invariant on Kähler groups
Yuan Liu

Abstract
Résumé We give several applications of the Bieri-Neumann-Strebel invariant on Kähler groups. Specifically, we provide simpler proof of the Napier-Ramachandran theorem on the HNN extension about Kähler groups and show that amenable Kähler groups have an empty complement of the BNS invariant.

1
Introduction

We say a finitely presented group
G
is a Kähler group if it can be realized as the fundamental group of a compact Kähler manifold. Not every finitely presented group is a Kähler group, and it is a question of Serre to characterize Kähler groups. The readers may refer to the monographs
[
ABC+96
]
and
[
Py24
]
for this topic. The overall picture for the Kähler groups is still very unclear.

For
G
a finitely generated group, Bieri, Neumann, and Strebel introduced a celebrated invariant
\Sigma(G)
in
[
BNS87
]
(hereafter, we call it the BNS invariant). Unfortunately, the computation of the BNS invariant is extremely difficult and is only known for restricted types of groups up to now. Surprisingly, the combination of these two mysterious objects, i.e.
(the complement of) the BNS invariant of a Kähler group, is described completely by Delzant in
[
Del10
]
, using Simpson’s Lefschetz theorem
[
Sim93
]
.

It is thus quite natural to use this BNS invariant to put restrictions on Kähler groups. For the HNN extension, some results were known for a long time thanks to the work of Napier and Ramachandran
[
NR08
, Theorem 0.2 and Theorem 0.3]
. Now, with the help of the BNS invariant, we give a much easier explanation (
theorems

3.4
and
3.5
). Moreover, we show that amenable Kähler groups have the empty complement of the BNS invariant (
proposition

3.3
).

This note is organized as follows. In
section

2
, we provide essential background on the BNS invariant. In
section

3
, we prove the results on HNN extensions about Kähler groups and on amenable Kähler groups.

2
The Bieri-Neumann-Strebel invariant

2.1
General theory

In 1987, a famous group theoretic invariant was introduced by Bieri, Neumann, and Strebel
[
BNS87
]
, which is now called the Bieri-Neumann-Strebel (BNS for short) invariant. For a finitely generated group
G
, this invariant gives the full information when the kernel of an abelian quotient of
G
is finitely generated (for its precise meaning, see
proposition

2.3
). The readers may refer to
[
BNS87
]
or the unpublished monograph
[
BS
]
for more details.
From now on, we always assume that the first Betti number
b_{1}(G)>0
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
be a nontrivial group homomorphism. Denote
G_{\chi}=\{g\in G\colon\chi(g)\geqslant 0\}
. For any given set of generators
S
, denote the associated Cayley graph of
G
as
C\coloneqq\mathrm{Cay}(G;S)
. We define its subgraph
C_{\chi}
as follows : (i) a vertex in
C
belongs to
C_{\chi}
if its
\chi
-value is nonnegative, (ii) an edge in
C
belongs to
C_{\chi}
if
both
vertices of this edge have nonnegative
\chi
-values. We can consider if
C_{\chi}
is connected and record the
\chi
when this happens. Notice that this property does not depend on the generating set
S
(see
[
BS
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[
Py24
, Proposition 11.1]
) and is invariant if we multiply
\chi
by a positive real constant. Denote
[\chi]
to be this equivalent relation of multiplication by a positive real constant, and write

\mathrm{S}(G)=(\mathrm{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R})-\{0\})/\penalty\mathbb{R}^{+}
for the collection of all these equivalent classes.

Definition 2.1
.

With the same notation as above, for
G
being a finitely generated group, we define its BNS invariant as

\Sigma(G)=\{[\chi]\in\mathrm{S}(G)\colon C_{\chi}\text{\ is connected}\}

We need the following properties of this invariant for later use.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
BNS87
, Theorem C]
)
Let
G
be a finitely presented group with no nonabelian free subgroups, then

\Sigma(G)\cup-\Sigma(G)=\mathrm{S}(G)

where
-\Sigma(G)
is the image of
\Sigma(G)
under the antipodal map.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
BNS87
, Theorem B1]
)
If
G
is a finitely generated group and
A
is its nontrivial abelian quotient with kernel
N
, then
A
is finitely generated if and only if

\mathrm{S}(G,N)\subseteq\Sigma(G)

where
\mathrm{S}(G,N)=\{[\chi]\in\mathrm{S}(G)\colon\chi(N)=0\}
. In particular,
G^{\prime}
is finitely generated if and only if
\Sigma(G)=\mathrm{S}(G)
, where
G^{\prime}=[G,G]
is the commutator subgroup of
G
.

2.2
The BNS invariant for Kähler groups

When this group
G
is the fundamental group of a finite CW-complex
X
, we can give a topological explanation of this invariant
[
BNS87
, section 5]
. If we denote
\hat{X}
as the maximal free abelian covering of
X
with Galois group
H
. Notice that
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R})
naturally factor through the maximal free abelian quotient
H
of
G
, for which we still denote as
\chi:H\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
f:\hat{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be the primitive function in the sense that
f(h.x)=f(x)+\chi(h)
for any
h\in H
and
x\in\hat{X}
. Now consider
\{x\in X\mid f(x)\geqslant 0\}
, it has a unique component on which
f
is unbounded, and we denote it as
\hat{X}_{f}
(
[
BNS87
, Lemma 5.2]
). Then we have
[\chi]\in\Sigma(G)
if and only the morphism on fundamental groups induced by the inclusion
\hat{X}_{f}\hookrightarrow\hat{X}
is onto (
[
BNS87
, Theroem 5.1]
). Using this topological explanation along with Simpson’s Lefschetz theorem, Delzant in
[
Del10
]
gives a complete description of (the complement of) the BNS invariant for Kähler groups.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
Del10
, Theorem 1.1]
)
Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with the fundamental group
G
. For a nontrivial
\chi\in H^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})
, we have
\chi
is exceptional, i.e.
[\chi]\notin\Sigma(G)
, if and only if there exists a holomorphic fibration (i.e. a holomorphic map with connected fibers) onto a hyperbolic Riemann orbifold
S
of genus greater than or equal to
1
, say
f:X\to S
, such that
\chi\in f^{*}(H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(S);\mathbb{R}))
.

The following conclusion is immediate, which is also stated in
[
Py24
, section 11.3]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

(Symmetric property of Kähler groups) Any Kähler group
G
has symmetric BNS invariant, i.e.

\Sigma(G)=-\Sigma(G)

Démonstration.

For any
[\chi]\notin\Sigma(G)
, by
theorem

2.4
, we have holomorphic fibration
f:X\to S
with
\chi=f^{*}(v)
for some
v\in H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(S);\mathbb{R})
. Then
-\chi=f^{*}(-v)
and
-v\in H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(S);\mathbb{R})
. Thus
[-\chi]\notin\Sigma(G)
, and the conclusion is proved.
∎

3
The applications

In this section, we will use the HNN-valuation introduced in
[
Bro87
]
to reprove some classical results on Kähler groups in
[
NR08
]
. Notice a conflict in the ascending (resp. descending) HNN extension definition in the above two papers, and we will use the same notation as in
[
NR08
]
here. These results are also achieved by Fridel and Vidussi in
[
FV16
]
using the rank gradient.

Definition 3.1

(HNN-extension)
.

Take any base group
B
and a stable letter
t
. Let
\varphi:B_{1}\to B_{2}
be an isomorphism between two subgroups
B_{1},B_{2}
of
B
. We say a group
G
admits an HNN extension if it can be written as

G=\langle B,t\ |\ B_{1}^{t}\coloneqq tB_{1}t^{-1}=\varphi(B_{1})=B_{2}\rangle

If
B_{1}=B
(and
B_{2}\neq B
), we say that
G
is (properly) descending ; if
B_{2}=B
(and
B_{1}\neq B
), we say that
G
is (properly) ascending.

For a group
G
as an HNN extension, we have a naturally associated group homomorphism
\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
defined as
\chi(t)=1
and
\chi(B)=0
. Set
N=\text{Ker}(\chi)
, and obviously we have
N=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{k}Bt^{-k}
. The main criterion we will apply is :

Proposition 3.2
.

(
[
Bro87
, Proposition 3.1]
)
Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
a surjective homomorphism. The following three conditions are equivalent :

1.

G
admits a decomposition as a descending HNN extension with finitely generated base group
B
and with
\chi
as the associated homomorphism.

2.

G
does not admit a decomposition as a properly ascending HNN extension with
\chi
as the associated homomorphism.

3.

There is no non-trivial HNN valuation
v
on
G
with
\chi=\chi_{v}
.

Moreover, if these conditions hold then every HNN decomposition of
G
with
\chi
associated is descending.

With this notation at hand, Brown in
[
Bro87
]
defined
\Sigma(G)
to be the set of classes
[\chi]\in(\text{Hom}(G;\mathbb{R})-\{0\})/\penalty\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\chi
satisfies condition 3 above with
\mathbb{Z}
replaced by
\mathbb{R}
, and lately showed that this invariant coincide with the BNS invariant.

The first result we prove is the combination of
[
FV16
, Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2]
. Their original proof uses the rank gradient and we here prove the same result using solely the property of BNS invariant.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a Kähler group which does not contain any free nonabelian subgroup, then
\Sigma(G)=\mathrm{S}(G)
and the commutator subgroup
G^{\prime}
is finitely generated. In particular, if
G
is an amenable Kähler group, we have the above conclusion holds.

Démonstration.

By
proposition

2.2
, we have
\Sigma(G)\cup-\Sigma(G)=\mathrm{S}(G)
. Also,
\Sigma(G)=-\Sigma(G)
by
corollary

2.5
, then we have the desired equality. The property of
G^{\prime}
follows directly from
proposition

2.3
.
∎

We can reprove the theorems in
[
NR08
]
on HNN extensions with these results.

Theorem 3.4
.

(
[
NR08
, Theorem 0.2(b)]
)
Let
G
be the fundamental group of a compact Kähler manifold
X
. If
G
admits a properly ascending HNN extension, then
X
admits a holomorphic fibration onto a hyperbolic Riemann orbifold.

Démonstration.

Let
\chi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
be the naturally associated homomorphism. By
proposition

3.2
,
[\chi]\notin\Sigma(G)
and
X
fibers over
S
according to
theorem

2.4
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

(
[
NR08
, Theorem 0.3 (b)]
)
Any properly ascending (descending) HNN extension with a finitely generated base group is not Kähler.

Démonstration.

We only consider that
G
is a properly ascending HNN extension with a finitely generated base group and the descending case is similar. By condition 2 of
proposition

3.2
, we have
[\chi]\notin\Sigma(G)
while condition 1 there shows that
[-\chi]\in\Sigma(G)
. Thus
G
can not be a Kähle group, since it contradicts the symmetric property (
corollary

2.5
).
∎

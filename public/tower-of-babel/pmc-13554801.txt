DENND3-p.R534S disrupts dyadic microdomain architecture to drive potentially pro-arrhythmic calcium and electrophysiologic instability
Authors: Shan Gao, Changsung John Kim, Dan Ye, David J Tester, John R Giudicessi, Michael J Ackerman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsInherited ventricular arrhythmias (VAs) frequently occur in the absence of pathogenic variants in canonical ion channel genes, suggesting alternative mechanisms of electrical instability. DENND3 is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor that regulates Rab GTPase-mediated trafficking, but its role in cardiac excitation-contraction coupling and membrane microdomain organization remains undefined.Methods and resultsWe studied induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes generated from a CRISPR/CAS9-engineered ultra-rare DENND3-p.R534S variant-inserted line (previously identified in an idiopathic ventricular fibrillation pedigree) and matched isogenic controls. Multielectrode array recordings, live-cell calcium imaging, super-resolution imaging using expansion microscopy, and biochemical analyses were used to assess electrical activity, calcium handling, membrane architecture, and calcium release unit organization. Potentially therapeutic studies were performed using genetic and pharmacologic inhibition of Rab11b. DENND3-p.R534S induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes exhibited multicellular electrical instability characterized by increased beat-to-beat variability, arrhythmic activity, conduction slowing, and prolonged excitation-contraction delay. These abnormalities were accompanied by heterogeneous and dyssynchronous calcium cycling despite preserved expression of major calcium-handling proteins. Super-resolution imaging revealed disruption of BIN1-dependent membrane architecture and nanoscale uncoupling of Cav1.2 and RyR2. Inhibition of Rab11b restored BIN1 organization, re-established dyadic coupling, normalized calcium cycling, and improved electrical stability.ConclusionThese findings support a model in which altered trafficking balance contributes to disruption of membrane microdomain organization, leading to dyadic uncoupling, calcium instability, and electrical dysfunction. Modulation of the Rab11b-mediated trafficking pathway restored structural and functional abnormalities, supporting the trafficking-associated pathway as a potential therapeutic target in DENND3-associated ventricular arrhythmia.Keywords: Arrhythmia, Calcium handling, DENND3, BIN1, Rab GTPases

Introduction

Sudden cardiac death due to ventricular arrhythmias (VAs) remains a major cause of mortality worldwide. Although inherited arrhythmia syndromes such as long QT syndrome, catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, and Brugada syndrome have provided key mechanistic insights, a substantial proportion of patients with malignant VAs lack pathogenic variants in the canonical cardiac ion channel genes.1–6 These observations suggest that mechanisms beyond classical channelopathies contribute to life-threatening VA susceptibility.

Abnormal intracellular calcium handling is a central driver and one of the final common intracellular pathways of ventricular arrhythmogenesis.7–9 Heterogeneous and dyssynchronous calcium cycling can promote triggered activity, electrical alternans, and rhythm instability, even in the absence of primary defects in ion channel biophysics.8,10–13 Increasing evidence indicates that calcium instability may arise from disruption of subcellular organization rather than changes in protein abundance. However, the molecular pathways linking intracellular organization to calcium-driven electrical instability remain incompletely defined.7,14,15

Efficient excitation-contraction coupling (ECC) depends on the precise spatial organization of calcium release units (CRUs), where CACNA1C-encoded L-type calcium channels (Cav1.2) in the sarcolemma are closely apposed to ryanodine receptors (RyR2) in the sarcoplasmic reticulum.16–18 This nanoscale alignment ensures synchronized calcium-induced calcium release and uniform calcium transients.19,20 Disruption of this architecture, even without changes in channel expression, can impair calcium release efficiency and promote spatiotemporal heterogeneity of calcium signalling.21,22

Membrane organization and ion channel localization are regulated tightly by endosomal trafficking pathways governed by Rab family GTPases.23 The balance between these pathways is critical for maintaining membrane composition and the integrity of calcium signalling microdomains.24,25 Disruption of Rab-mediated trafficking has emerged as an important contributor to cardiac disease, yet its role in potentially life-threatening VAs remains poorly defined.26

Among key determinants of membrane microdomain organization, BIN1 (Bridging Integrator 1) functions as a membrane scaffolding protein that localizes to T-tubules and T-tubule-like membrane invaginations in cardiomyocytes.27,28 BIN1 regulates membrane curvature, supports formation of tubule-like structures, and facilitates Cav1.2 clustering within functional calcium microdomains required for efficient ECC.28,29 Reduced BIN1 expression or mislocalization has been implicated in cardiomyopathy and heart failure, but whether BIN1-dependent membrane organization contributes to primary inherited arrhythmogenesis in the absence of structural heart disease remains unclear.30

DENND3 encodes a guanine nucleotide exchange factor that regulates Rab GTPase activity.31 We previously identified an ultra-rare missense variant, DENND3-p.R534S, in a multigenerational family with idiopathic ventricular fibrillation and demonstrated that this pathogenic variant alters Rab-mediated trafficking and increases membrane retention of multiple cardiac ion channels.32 However, altered channel abundance alone does not fully explain the complexity of the arrhythmic phenotype, suggesting that disruption of membrane organization and calcium microdomain integrity may play a central role.

Here, we hypothesized that DENND3-p.R534S promotes VAs by disrupting Rab-mediated membrane organization, leading to BIN1 mislocalization, nanoscale uncoupling of CRUs, and heterogeneous calcium cycling. Using induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes (iPSC-CMs), we integrated multicellular electrophysiology, live-cell calcium imaging, and super-resolution imaging using expansion microscopy with mechanistic rescue approaches to define the structural and functional consequences of DENND3 dysfunction.

Results

DENND3-p.R534S induces multicellular electrical instability

To determine whether the DENND3-p.R534S variant disrupts electrical stability at the multicellular level, we performed multielectrode array (MEA) recordings in iPSC-CM monolayers generated from the DENND3-p.R534S variant-inserted line and matched isogenic controls (ICs).

Representative MEA local extracellular action potential recordings demonstrated stable, rhythmic electrical activity in IC-CM monolayers, whereas DENND3-p.R534S monolayers exhibited unstable morphology characterized by beat-to-beat variability, waveform heterogeneity, and intermittent abnormal deflections suggestive of triggered electrical activity (Figure 1A). Corresponding Poincaré plots revealed tight clustering of successive beat intervals in IC iPSC-CMs, in contrast to dispersion in DENND3-p.R534S CMs, indicating impaired beat-to-beat temporal stability (Figure 1A).

DENND3-p.R534S drives multicellular electrical instability and electromechanical uncoupling in iPSC-CMs. (A) Representative MEA recordings demonstrating stable, rhythmic electrical activity in IC monolayers and irregular, heterogeneous waveforms with intermittent abnormal deflections in DENND3-p.R534S iPSC-CMs. Corresponding Poincaré plots show tight clustering of beat intervals in ICs vs. marked dispersion in DENND3-p.R534S CMs, indicating impaired beat-to-beat temporal stability. (B) Quantification of beat period coefficient of variation (CoV), percentage of electrodes exhibiting arrhythmic activity, conduction velocity, and excitation-contraction delay. DENND3-p.R534S monolayers exhibit increased rhythm variability, elevated arrhythmogenic burden, slowed conduction, and prolonged electromechanical delay. Data were obtained from three independent cardiomyocyte differentiation batches (N = 3). Each data point represents one independent iPSC-CM monolayer. Data are presented as mean ± SEM, *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ****P < 0.0001.

Consistent with these observations, quantitative analysis demonstrated a significant increase in beat period variability in DENND3-p.R534S monolayers compared with ICs (IC: 0.233 ± 0.025; DENND3-p.R534S: 0.339 ± 0.035; n = 15; P < 0.05; Figure 1B). This instability was accompanied by a marked increase in the percentage of electrodes exhibiting arrhythmic activity (IC: 1.333 ± 1.333; DENND3-p.R534S: 66.200 ± 10.370; n = 15; P < 0.0001; Figure 1B), indicating that electrical abnormalities occur across the multicellular network rather than as isolated events.

In addition to temporal instability, DENND3-p.R534S CMs exhibited impaired conduction properties, as reflected by reduced conduction velocity compared with IC (IC: 0.331 ± 0.085 mm/ms; DENND3-p.R534S: 0.146 ± 0.008 mm/ms; n = 13; P < 0.05; Figure 1B). Furthermore, excitation-contraction delay was significantly prolonged, indicating disruption of coupling between electrical activation and downstream contractile responses (IC: 227.500 ± 7.771 ms; DENND3-p.R534S: 271.200 ± 9.105 ms; n = 13; P < 0.05; Figure 1B).

Together, these data demonstrate that DENND3-p.R534S is associated with a multifaceted phenotype of multicellular electrical instability characterized by impaired rhythm regularity, increased arrhythmic activity, conduction slowing, and electromechanical uncoupling.

Electrical instability is associated with heterogeneous and dyssynchronous calcium cycling independent of calcium-handling protein expression

To determine whether multicellular electrical instability in DENND3-p.R534S iPSC-CMs was associated with abnormal intracellular calcium behaviour, we performed live-cell calcium imaging using Fluo-4 AM in paced iPSC-CMs.

Representative calcium line-scan recordings demonstrated uniform and spatially synchronized calcium transients in IC-CMs. In contrast, DENND3-p.R534S CMs exhibited abnormal spatiotemporal calcium behaviour, including irregular calcium release patterns and beat-to-beat variability (Figure 2A).

Quantitative assessment of calcium behaviour demonstrated a uniformly penetrant abnormal calcium transient phenotype in DENND3-p.R534S CMs compared with IC (IC: 0%, DENND3-p.R534S: 100%; n = 30 cells per group; Figure 2B. While all IC-CMs exhibited synchronized calcium transients, DENND3-p.R534S CMs displayed heterogeneous abnormal spatiotemporal calcium behaviours, including distinct patterns of irregular and dyssynchronous calcium release (Figure 2B). Representative examples of these abnormal calcium transient patterns are shown in Figure 2B.

In CMs with analyzable calcium transients, waveform analysis demonstrated altered calcium transient kinetics in DENND3-p.R534S CMs, including prolonged calcium transient duration at 90% decay (CTD90, s; IC: 1.387 ± 0.025 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.719 ± 0.026 s; n = 30; P < 0.0001) and prolonged peak-to-90% decay time (s; IC: 1.100 ± 0.028 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.383 ± 0.038 s; n = 30; P < 0.0001), accompanied by reduced calcium transient amplitude (ΔF/F0; IC: 0.358 ± 0.021, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.088 ± 0.013; n = 30; P < 0.0001) and reduced maximal upstroke velocity (Vmax, ΔF/F0·s−1; IC: 1.933 ± 0.132 s−1, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.450 ± 0.052 s−1; n = 30; P < 0.0001; Figure 2C).

Despite these functional abnormalities, immunoblot analyses demonstrated no significant differences in total protein expression of major calcium-handling proteins, including RyR2 (IC: 1.000 ± 0.045, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.970 ± 0.048; n = 7; P = 0.652), SERCA2a (IC: 1.000 ± 0.109, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.896 ± 0.065; n = 7; P = 0.428), PLN (IC: 1.000 ± 0.110, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.022 ± 0.075; n = 7; P = 0.870), and NCX1 (IC: 1.000 ± 0.100, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.984 ± 0.060; n = 7; P = 0.892), between DENND3-p.R534S and IC-CMs (Figure 2D).

Together, these data demonstrate that DENND3-p.R534S is associated with abnormal calcium cycling and altered calcium transient kinetics that occur independently of changes in total calcium-handling protein expression.

DENND3-p.R534S disrupts BIN1-dependent membrane architecture and pre-T-tubule organization

BIN1 is a membrane scaffolding protein that supports the formation of tubule-like membrane invaginations and organizes calcium channel microdomains required for efficient ECC. Given that DENND3-p.R534S CMs exhibited abnormal calcium cycling independent of changes in calcium-handling protein expression (Figure 2), we next asked whether these functional abnormalities were associated with alterations in BIN1-dependent membrane architecture.

Super-resolution imaging using 4× expansion microscopy combined with Airyscan detection revealed well-organized, striated BIN1-positive membrane structures in IC-CMs, exhibiting a coherent and directional membrane pattern consistent with preserved pre-T-tubule organization and coordinated Cav1.2 localization (Figure 3A). In contrast, DENND3-p.R534S CMs displayed marked disruption of BIN1 organization, characterized by loss of striated membrane patterning and increased spatial dispersion. This disorganization was accompanied by a more diffuse and heterogeneous distribution of Cav1.2 (Figure 3A).

(A) Representative Airyscan super-resolution images (4× expansion microscopy) showing organized, striated BIN1-positive membrane structures and coordinated Cav1.2 localization in IC-CMs, compared with dispersed BIN1 organization and heterogeneous Cav1.2 distribution in DENND3-p.R534S CMs. (B) Quantification of membrane organization using directionality analysis, demonstrating increased dispersion of BIN1-positive structures in DENND3-p.R534S CMs. (C) Representative immunoblot and densitometric quantification of total BIN1 expression. Imaging data were obtained from N = 3 independent cardiomyocyte differentiation batches. Each data point in (B) represents one individual cardiomyocyte. Immunoblot analyses were performed using protein collected from N = 7 independent cardiomyocyte differentiation batches, and each data point in (C) represents one independent differentiation batch. Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Scale bars as indicated. Scale bars = 10 μm, ****P < 0.0001.

Quantitative analysis of pre-T-tubule organization using directionality measurements demonstrated significantly increased dispersion of BIN1-positive structures in DENND3-p.R534S CMs compared with IC (IC: 9.682 ± 0.897°, DENND3-p.R534S: 34.390 ± 2.084°; n = 18; P < 0.0001; Figure 3B). Higher dispersion values indicate loss of coherent membrane alignment and increased structural disorganization.

To determine whether altered pre-T-tubule organization was accompanied by changes in protein abundance, we assessed total BIN1 expression by immunoblot analysis. Densitometric quantification demonstrated reduced BIN1 protein levels in DENND3-p.R534S CMs compared with IC (IC: 1.000 ± 0.070, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.542 ± 0.027; n = 7; P < 0.0001; Figure 3C).

Consistent disruption of BIN1 organization was also observed using standard Airyscan super-resolution imaging, supporting the robustness of the structural phenotype (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1).

Together, these data indicate that abnormal calcium cycling observed in DENND3-p.R534S CMs is associated with disruption of BIN1-dependent pre-T-tubule membrane organization and reduced BIN1 expression.

Disruption of BIN1 is associated with nanoscale uncoupling of CRUs

Because BIN1-dependent membrane architecture supports spatial organization of CRUs (Figure 3A), we next assessed whether BIN1 disruption in DENND3-p.R534S CMs was associated with altered nanoscale coupling between Cav1.2 and RyR2.

Super-resolution imaging using 4× expansion microscopy revealed close spatial apposition of Cav1.2 and RyR2 clusters along BIN1-positive pre-T-tubule structures in IC-CMs. In contrast, DENND3-p.R534S CMs exhibited marked disruption of pre-T-tubule architecture, accompanied by disorganization of Cav1.2 and RyR2 spatial arrangement. (Figure 4A).

IC-CMs exhibit close spatial apposition of Cav1.2 and RyR2 along BIN1-positive pre-T-tubule structures, whereas DENND3-p.R534S CMs display disrupted architecture and loss of coordinated spatial alignment. Insets show higher magnification. (B) Three-dimensional rendering of the regions shown in (A) and quantitative nearest-neighbour analysis demonstrating increased Cav1.2-RyR2 separation distance in DENND3-p.R534S CMs. Imaging data were obtained from N = 3 independent cardiomyocyte differentiation batches. Each data point represents one individual cardiomyocyte. Data are presented as mean ± SEM. Scale bars as indicated. Scale bars = 10 μm, ****P < 0.0001.

Three-dimensional rendering of regions corresponding to the dashed boxes in Figure 4A demonstrated that, in IC-CMs, Cav1.2 channels are organized along pre-T-tubule membranes with RyR2 clusters closely aligned and surrounding these structures. In contrast, DENND3-p.R534S CMs displayed loss of organized pre-T-tubule scaffolding and disrupted spatial alignment between Cav1.2 and RyR2.

Higher magnification three-dimensional rendering of these structures (Figure 4B) further highlights this phenotype, showing that, in IC-CMs, Cav1.2 is anchored along pre-T-tubule membranes with closely apposed RyR2 clusters, whereas in DENND3-p.R534S CMs, this organized architecture is disrupted, with loss of alignment and increased spatial separation.

To quantitatively assess nanoscale organization, surface-to-surface nearest-neighbour distance analysis was performed using Imaris. DENND3-p.R534S CMs demonstrated a significant increase in Cav1.2-RyR2 separation distance compared with IC (IC: 0.284 ± 0.020 µm, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.472 ± 0.026 µm; n = 18; P < 0.0001; Figure 4B), indicating impaired spatial coupling of CRUs.

Consistent spatial uncoupling of Cav1.2 and RyR2 was observed using standard Airyscan super-resolution imaging; however, due to its lower effective spatial resolution and reliance on spot-based nearest-neighbour analysis, this may underestimate true membrane surface separation (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2).

These findings indicate that disruption of BIN1-dependent membrane architecture in DENND3-p.R534S CMs is associated with nanoscale uncoupling of CRUs.

Rab11b inhibition restores membrane architecture, dyadic organization, calcium cycling, and electrical stability

Given the observed dysregulation of Rab11-mediated trafficking in DENND3-p.R534S CMs,32 we next tested whether inhibition of Rab11b could restore the structural and functional abnormalities identified above.

Inhibition of Rab11b, using either a dominant-negative (DN) Rab11b construct or the small-molecule Rab11b inhibitor CDKI-73, restored organized BIN1 localization and significantly improved BIN1 striation regularity in DENND3-p.R534S CMs, with values approaching those observed in IC (IC: 9.727 ± 0.747°, DENND3-p.R534S: 34.590 ± 1.923°, +Rab11b DN: 9.605 ± 0.686°; n = 18; IC: 9.844 ± 0.816°, DENND3-p.R534S: 34.750 ± 1.815°, +CDKI-73: 9.693 ± 0.738°; n = 18; Figure 5A). These findings indicate that Rab11b inhibition was associated with improved BIN1 organization in DENND3-p.R534S CMs.

Consistent with these structural improvements, surface-to-surface nearest-neighbour analysis demonstrated normalization of Cav1.2-RyR2 spatial coupling following Rab11b inhibition, with distances restored to levels comparable to IC-CMs (IC: 0.275 ± 0.017 µm, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.436 ± 0.033 µm; +Rab11b DN: 0.267 ± 0.228 µm; n = 18; IC: 0.266 ± 0.020 µm, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.461 ± 0.033 µm; +CDKI-73: 0.259 ± 0.022 µm; n = 18; Figure 5B), consistent with improved dyadic organization.

Restoration of dyadic structure was accompanied by normalization of calcium cycling behaviour. Rab11b inhibition reduced calcium transient heterogeneity and improved calcium transient kinetics, including normalization of calcium transient duration (IC: 1.437 ± 0.036 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.739 ± 0.052 s, +Rab11b DN: 1.490 ± 0.028 s; n = 30; IC: 1.423 ± 0.042 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.649 ± 0.023 s, +CDKI-73: 1.480 ± 0.037 s; n = 30), decay time (IC: 1.150 ± 0.037 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.359 ± 0.058 s, +Rab11b DN: 1.136 ± 0.028 s; n = 30; IC: 1.188 ± 0.041 s, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.312 ± 0.014 s, +CDKI-73: 1.163 ± 0.025 s; n = 30), amplitude (ΔF/F0; IC: 0.371 ± 0.021, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.122 ± 0.014, +Rab11b DN: 0.430 ± 0.029; n = 30; IC: 0.331 ± 0.032, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.229 ± 0.018, +CDKI-73: 0.411 ± 0.035; n = 30), and maximal upstroke velocity (ΔF/F0·s−1; IC: 1.997 ± 0.157 s−1, DENND3-p.R534S: 0.555 ± 0.059 s−1, +Rab11b DN: 2.161 ± 0.155 s−1; n = 30; IC: 1.910 ± 0.177 s−1, DENND3-p.R534S: 1.112 ± 0.083 s−1, +CDKI-73: 2.148 ± 0.158 s−1; n = 30) towards control levels (Figure 5C). In addition, spontaneous calcium release events were no longer observed following Rab11b inhibition, indicating improved calcium stability.

These structural and calcium-handling improvements were accompanied by improved multicellular electrical behaviour. MEA recordings demonstrated reduced beat-to-beat variability (IC: 0.229 ± 0.017, DENND3-p.R534S: 45.030 ± 6.606; +Rab11b DN: 0.240 ± 0.030; n = 10; IC: 0.150 ± 0.019, DENND3-p.R534S: 10.150 ± 4.739; +CDKI-73: 0.206 ± 0.042; n = 10) and normalization of excitation-contraction delay (IC: 222.800 ± 6.257 ms, DENND3-p.R534S: 518.900 ± 96.800 ms; +Rab11b DN: 231.600 ± 13.800 ms; n = 10; IC: 231.900 ± 3.496 ms, DENND3-p.R534S: 285.400 ± 10.610 ms; +CDKI-73: 225.100 ± 2.914 ms; n = 10) following Rab11b inhibition, consistent with restoration of rhythm regularity and electromechanical coupling (Figure 5D).

Consistent structural rescue was also observed in standard Airyscan datasets (see Supplementary material online, Figure S3), further supporting restoration of membrane organization and dyadic coupling.

Together, these data demonstrate that inhibition of Rab11b is sufficient to restore membrane organization, re-establish dyadic coupling, normalize calcium cycling, and improve electrical stability in DENND3-p.R534S CMs.

Discussion

We demonstrate that the ultra-rare DENND3-p.R534S variant is associated with multicellular electrical instability accompanied by calcium mishandling and disruption of dyadic microdomain organization. Using variant-specific iPSC-CMs, we observed heterogeneous and dyssynchronous calcium cycling despite preserved expression of major calcium-handling proteins. Together, these findings support a model in which altered membrane organization contributes to electrical instability and suggest that disruption of dyadic structure represents an additional mechanism contributing to arrhythmogenesis.

Multicellular electrophysiological analyses revealed pronounced rhythm irregularity, increased arrhythmogenic activity, conduction slowing, and electromechanical uncoupling in DENND3-p.R534S CMs. These electrical abnormalities were accompanied by heterogeneous and dyssynchronous calcium cycling, placing calcium instability at the centre of the observed phenotype. Importantly, calcium-handling abnormalities occurred in the absence of detectable changes in total calcium-handling protein expression, indicating that altered subcellular organization rather than protein abundance underlies the observed dysfunction. In addition, resting membrane potential was comparable between IC and DENND3-p.R534S iPSC-CMs (IC: −52.650 ± 2.780 mV; DENND3-p.R534S: −54.730 ± 2.153 mV; n = 8; P = 0.565; Supplementary material online, Figure S4A). Similarly, the injected depolarizing current was comparable between IC and DENND3-p.R534S iPSC-CMs (IC: 312.500 ± 44.070 pA; DENND3-p.R534S: 337.500 ± 37.500 pA; n = 8; P = 0.672; Supplementary material online, Figure S4B), indicating that the observed calcium-handling and electrophysiological abnormalities are unlikely to be explained by differences in baseline membrane polarization or stimulation conditions.

Although ICa,L was not directly measured in the present study, we previously demonstrated in the same DENND3-p.R534S iPSC-CM model that this variant increases Cav1.2 membrane localization and ICa,L current density without significant changes in channel gating properties. Therefore, the calcium-handling abnormalities observed in the present study are unlikely to be explained by reduced L-type calcium channel expression or function. These findings further support a structural mechanism involving disrupted dyadic microdomain organization.

A central finding of this study is the identification of BIN1-dependent membrane architecture as a candidate structural link between DENND3-mediated trafficking imbalance and calcium instability. BIN1 is known to regulate membrane curvature and Cav1.2 clustering required for efficient dyadic coupling.28,29 In DENND3-p.R534S CMs, BIN1 mislocalization and reduced abundance were associated with disruption of pre-T-tubule organization and nanoscale uncoupling of Cav1.2 and RyR2. Even modest increases in dyadic separation can impair the fidelity of calcium-induced calcium release, providing a structural framework for the observed heterogeneity and slowing of calcium transients.22,33 Together, these findings support a model in which BIN1 disruption contributes to calcium cycling instability rather than representing a downstream epiphenomenon.

Notably, the structural phenotype was observed consistently across both standard Airyscan imaging and expansion microscopy-enhanced Airyscan imaging, supporting the robustness of DENND3-mediated dyadic disruption.

Although iPSC-CMs lack fully developed transverse tubules, accumulating evidence demonstrates that BIN1-dependent membrane invaginations form functional calcium microdomains capable of supporting ECC.29,34,35 Accordingly, nanoscale dyadic organization remains essential for synchronized calcium release. Therefore, disruption of these structures is sufficient to impair dyadic coupling and calcium synchronization. Previous studies have shown that dyadic assembly and transverse-tubule development occur progressively during cardiomyocyte maturation, and developing postnatal cardiomyocytes likewise exhibit immature transverse-tubule architecture while maintaining functional excitation-contraction coupling. Thus, the abnormalities observed in this study likely reflect biologically relevant mechanisms within pre-T-tubule membrane microdomains rather than artefacts of cellular immaturity. Nevertheless, future studies using more mature cardiomyocyte models will be important to determine whether these mechanisms are similarly conserved in the adult heart.

DENND3 regulates Rab GTPase activity, and our prior work demonstrated that the DENND3-p.R534S perturbs Rab-mediated trafficking through suppression of Rab12 activity accompanied by increased Rab5 activity and abnormal membrane retention of multiple cardiac ion channels.32 We previously proposed that disruption of Rab12-dependent degradative trafficking may induce compensatory trafficking responses involving Rab5-dependent endocytosis and Rab11-mediated recycling to preserve intracellular transport homeostasis. The present study extends this framework by demonstrating that altered trafficking balance disrupts BIN1 organization and dyadic microdomain integrity. Although Rab11b activity itself was not altered in our previous study, both genetic and pharmacologic inhibition of Rab11b restored BIN1 organization, calcium cycling, and electrical stability, supporting Rab11b-mediated trafficking as an important contributor to the observed phenotype. This interpretation is further supported by studies demonstrating that perturbation of Rab family proteins, including Rab11, disrupts T-tubule formation and supports a role for Rab-mediated membrane trafficking in membrane domain organization.36

The present study supports the concept that defects in intracellular trafficking and membrane microdomain organization may contribute to the arrhythmogenic phenotype associated with the DENND3 variant through mechanisms beyond altered ion channel abundance.37–40 More broadly, BIN1 dysregulation and trafficking imbalance have been implicated in cardiomyopathy and heart failure, suggesting that the mechanisms described here may extend beyond rare genetic variants to more common forms of cardiac disease.30,41 Taken together, these observations raise the possibility that similar trafficking-dependent mechanisms may contribute to other inherited VAs, although this remains to be determined.

Collectively, our results provide a mechanistic framework linking intracellular trafficking to dyadic microdomain instability. Although iPSC-CMs provide a useful platform for mechanistic investigation, they do not fully reproduce mature ventricular architecture and T-tubule organization. Therefore, further validation in adult CMs and in vivo systems will be important to define the physiological and therapeutic relevance of targeting trafficking-associated pathways.

In summary, these data support a model in which altered trafficking balance disrupts membrane microdomain organization, leading to dyadic uncoupling, calcium instability, and electrical dysfunction. Restoration of trafficking balance through either the genetic or pharmacologic inhibition of Rab11b rescues structural and functional abnormalities, identifying the DENND3-Rab11b-BIN1 axis as a mechanistically grounded and potentially targetable pathway associated with the DENND3 variant.

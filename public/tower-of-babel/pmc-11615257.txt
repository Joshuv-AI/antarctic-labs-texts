Energy-efficient data routing using neuro-fuzzy based data routing mechanism for IoT-enabled WSNs
Authors: Sakthi Shunmuga Sundaram Paulraj, T Deepa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a novel Neuro-fuzzy-based Data Routing (NFDR) mechanism for efficient data routing and dynamic cluster formation in Internet of Things (IoT) enabled Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs). The NFDR mechanism incorporates optimal scalability factors computed from past and present network parameter values, acting as an additional buffer factor to sustain nodes within clusters, even with partial satisfaction of network parameter values. The neural network determines cluster formation requirements, while the objective function adjusts according to the updated fuzzy logic of identified cluster members. Super heads are initially selected, and cluster member size is adjusted to sustain maximum data transmission without affecting network parameter thresholds. Simulation results demonstrate that the NFDR mechanism enhances clustering range and cluster members while sustaining evaluation metrics such as 75% energy retention, 20% end-to-end delay reduction, and a 15% reduction in dead nodes, ultimately contributing to the development of efficient and robust IoT-enabled WSNs.Keywords: Dynamic environment adaptability, Scalability feature, IoT-enabled WSNs, Neuro-fuzzy based data routing, Maximum data transmission rate, And network efficiencySubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Seamless communication and data exchange between various devices because of the Internet of Things has revolutionized the way we interact with our surroundings. However, when combined with WSNs for facilitating the data collection and data transmission from dynamic environments1,2. Such a network maintains a unique way of node deployment and distribution and responding to its central base station continuously. Efficient data routing mechanisms must ensure reliable and timely delivery of the data and, at the same time, optimize the network utilization. Data routing in IoT-enabled WSNs is a complex problem. With the help of the dynamic nature of the network, limiting energy limitations of sensor nodes with finite energy at their disposal, the tendency to maximize the data transmission rate causes difficulties in designing the data routing mechanisms. Traditional routing algorithms do not always adapt to change network conditions and ensure that the most effective performance is optimized in terms of energy efficiency, latency, and throughput. Various efforts have been made in this field using cluster-based routing protocols and fuzzy logic-based techniques. Many existing works made significant contributions. Cluster-based Energy-aware Data Aggregation Routing (CEDAR) protocol is introduced for achieving reliable data routing with minimized traffic congestion, energy overhead, and network lifetime. It combines both a fuzzy system and an influencing Capuchin Search Algorithm (CapSA). This approach is mainly focused on two important aspects such as (i) effective cluster formation, and (ii) energy-driven routing mechanisms that exist between inter and intra clusters. However, this algorithm lags behind the operational efficiency of the dynamic environment of IoT-enabled WSNs3. Similar to this approach, the Efficient Relay Node Selection Scheme for Mission Critical Communication (ERNSS-MCC) method is presented which involves hard and soft cluster formation induced by involving a modified K-mean algorithm which is properly trained by temporary database unit for learning emergency overhead from the roadside unit (RSU) that can be more effective for adjusting the internal network parameters includes packet delivery ratio (PDR) and throughput4. However, this approach has existing computational complexity as well as latency problems associated with IoT-enabled WSNs. Introducing a fuzzy Super Cluster Head (SCH) selection method to sort out the latency issues by properly encountering the following factors such as battery limitation concerning each node, always computing distance between the source node to the BS, capacity of dense node distribution, and communication quality utilizing range-free localization. this operation is executed by another routing protocol named Fuzzy-based Energy Efficient Clustering (FEEC) protocol. Still, it has exhibited latency and energy constraints. The optimization towards fuzzy logic generation is highly required to reduce the latency issues and enhance the data transmission rate5. Based on this, a stable routing mechanism is introduced such as Cross-layer-based energy-efficient cluster head selection using a hybrid particle swarm wild horse optimizer (CL-HPWSR) that minimizes energy consumption, communication delay, latency, etc. It allows bi-concentric hexagonal cluster formation by cross-layer evaluation over the physical, medium access control, and network6. In this way, CL-HPWSR achieves an optimal data routing path that identifies the node that formed bi-concentric clusters. The simulation results convey that reasonable improvement is obtained over QoS, energy, and computational complexity. Similar to this approach, the Multi-Objective Taylor Crow Optimization (MOTCO) algorithm is introduced for supporting reliable data routing by providing dynamic cluster formation that follows optimal pathfinder which is computed by combining both the mathematical model of the Taylor series and search engine for optimal number of nodes in the cluster by the Crow Search Algorithm (CSA). According to this, the feasibility of this outcome by introducing the objective function which is computed by evaluating certain network parameters such as distance between the nodes in the cluster, energy of the nodes, traffic density of the cluster, and the delay in transmitting the data packets7. However, existing works have their limitations, as they fail to develop more efficient and adaptable pathways to the exponential growth in networking patterns and volumes. Indeed, future WSNs are expected to accommodate the increase in the number of devices, which is bound to create more demand for data transmissions and data processing capabilities. It is also worth noting that the existing WSN algorithm fails to account for the dynamic nature of IoT-enabled WSNs and is ultimately limiting the data transmission rates. Related works

In this section, we review recent articles on data routing algorithms, including those utilizing neural networks and fuzzy logic, to achieve optimal operations for maximizing data transmission rates while maintaining network parameters within standard norms.

Energy-efficient routing with deep learning networks

IoT-enabled WSNs require an efficient routing protocol for various innovative approaches. Based on that, many routing protocols have been proposed. A deep learning-based routing protocol for efficient data transmission is introduced8, which involves optimal cluster head selection using the generalized intuitionistic fuzzy soft-set method. It gives more weight to nodes that exhibit optimal network parameters through a hybrid optimization approach. However, it fails to sustain the maximum data transmission rate under dynamic environmental conditions. Further optimization and modification are required in the execution process. Similarly, on the other hand, there must be a focus on enhancing security through various optimization techniques. According to that, a cross-layer security MAC-aware routing protocol is presented9 for IoT-enabled WSNs. It evaluates user credentials before allowing data to be sent to the required destination node because it can adapt the best data routing path in which cross-layer security features are established. Therefore, well-secured and fast data routing is exhibited among the secured routing path nodes. Again, concerning maximum data transmission rate concerns, it lags in performance whenever the requesting data transmission rate automatically increases. Security aspects are sustained, but the weight towards data transmission is lost. A priority-based energy-efficient routing method is developed to improve energy efficiency in routing IoT-enabled WSNs10–12. This method is influenced by the Ad hoc On-Demand Distance Vector (AODV) protocol for attaining prioritized energy-aware routing among inter and intra-clustering groups. According to this study, the performance of clustered groups towards data traffic congestion and transmission delay for IoT-enabled WSNs is evaluated. The results conveyed that better performance is achievable, provided there is dynamic adjustment over the priority assignation among the valid cluster nodes. Sustaining network credit requires additional follow-up network parameters that induce additional energy constraints. To sort that out, an energy-aware clustering and multi-hop routing protocol for IoT-assisted wireless sensor networks has been proposed13,14. It emphasizes the importance of energy efficiency in routing protocols for IoT applications. For every iteration, the required network parameters are evaluated in such a way that the execution of data transmission among the cluster nodes would be satisfied as per the user-requested data transmission rate. However, the suitability loss of dynamic clustering is affected by huge variations in data transmission established in the instantaneously forming cluster groups. Subsequently, an improved energy-efficient solution for routing in IoT was introduced by enhancing existing protocols with a Cluster Formation Protocol using Neuro-Fuzzy Rules15. This enhancement aimed to address the performance limitations of traditional routing techniques in low-power networks. Results showed that reasonable improvement was achieved, but the continuity of performance was leveraged by utilizing a deep learning approach for optimal route selection based on various parameters like energy, bandwidth, trust, delay, and congestion levels16–18. Thus, it has focused on energy-efficient and bandwidth-aware optimal routing for IoT-enabled WSNs. With the growing cyber-attack threats on cyber-physical systems, especially smart grids, detecting false data injection attacks (FDIAs) that degrade the accuracy of data and reliable operation of these systems have become a crucial research problem. Learning-based methods such as deep learning and federated learning have been proposed to detect and mitigate these attacks with protection of user privacy19,20.

Fuzzy logic routing with machine learning algorithms

Fuzzy logic has emerged as a promising approach to address data routing challenges in IoT-enabled WSNs. According to that, several studies have explored the incorporation of fuzzy logic during data routing with formed cluster groups for enhancing the efficiency and performance of IoT-enabled WSNs. In this study21,22, fuzzy logic is involved in enhancing the potential of cooperative multi-robotic wireless sensor networks. This method reduces the computational complexity of executing several cluster nodes instantaneously within this specific network paradigm. Results inferred that fuzzy logic is suitable for tackling the complexities of a dynamic environment and switches the network parameters according to the required data transmission norms. However, sustaining the features of fuzzy logic in this study still needs to address challenges related to IoT-enabled WSNs. A fuzzy logic-based energy-aware routing protocol for IoT, known as FLEA-RPL, is presented23. This protocol considers several evaluation metrics, including load balancing, residual energy, latency threshold, and expected transmission count for optimal route selection, emphasizing energy efficiency in IoT environments. However, the outcome is not convincing regarding the latency aspect because it affects data traffic congestion once the data transmission rate increases linearly. This protocol sustains for a short while once the data reaches the expected level of data transmission. It affects the entire network performance in terms of energy and network lifetime. In24–26, an optimized deep neuro-fuzzy network for cyber forensic investigation in big data-based IoT infrastructures is introduced. This model acts to automatically detect misbehavior in IoT devices, thereby enhancing security by identifying vulnerabilities and potential attacks. However, towards data transmission capability, it loses efficiency in terms of energy as well as latency. Subsequently, another study discusses fuzzy-based techniques for clustering in wireless sensor networks, focusing on recent advancements, challenges, and future directions27. The study provides insights into the application of fuzzy logic in clustering within WSNs, shedding light on the status and potential advancements in this area. Identifying the location of injection of dummy data (an FDIA) is a top priority to maintain the security and stability of power systems. There are different state-of-the-art methods to detect and identify an FDIA attack using different machine learning approaches supported by graph models28–30. Additionally, it uses additional techniques, like short-time Fourier transform (STFT) and complex-valued graph convolutional neural networks (GCNs), to improve the interpretability and performance of the localisers31.

Neuro-fuzzy based data routing mechanism

In this section, we describe the proposed neuro-fuzzy-based data routing (NFDR) mechanism, which aims to sustain maximum data transmission capability even under dynamic environment conditions. This is achievable by introducing the scalability features of cluster nodes that can carry additional data based on their potential ability, which is observed from the existing network parameters. This implies that for every iteration of the data transmission, the instantaneous value of each cluster node is evaluated. If it satisfies the required data transmission capability, it is automatically either included or excluded in the upcoming cluster formation. After identifying the suitable nodes, the selection of super nodes is initialized, where their responsibility is purely focused on maintaining a steady data transmission process without causing data traffic congestion. Moreover, it can support the energy utilization factor by saving a certain percentage of energy as a backup for further data transmission as well as assigning additional data-carrying capability to the cluster nodes. The NFDR mechanism combines both neural networks and fuzzy logic to ensure robust and energy-efficient cluster formations in IoT-enabled wireless sensor networks. It includes an energy-driven model that ensures cluster nodes consistently maintain predefined energy levels. Nodes that do not meet these thresholds are excluded from cluster formation until they do. The mechanism aims for energy efficiency and adaptability in the dynamic environment condition of efficient data routing between inter and intra-cluster formations. The Neural Network updates fuzzy rules based on parameters from the IoT-enabled WSNs and a decision manager. Comparing node energy levels pre- and post-evaluation determines cluster formation. Fuzzy rules refine the energy-driven model to ensure nodes within clusters meet essential metrics such as the degree of super nodes, expected energy per cluster, energy variance, and residual energy. According to the proposed NFDR mechanism, cluster nodes are classified into two types: normal nodes and super nodes. Supernodes have repetitive appearances in the same cluster group. The energy allocation for a super node is always greater than that of a normal node. The Objective Function aids the neural network in updating fuzzy rules by crafting a function based on potential energy drops due to various environmental conditions. The performance of the NFDR mechanism is evaluated based on metrics like energy variance, residual energy, and end-to-end delay across transmission instances. The NFDR mechanism promotes flexible and scalable cluster formation, ensuring efficient data transmission and optimal energy usage. It prioritizes energy-driven models and scalability features inherent in IoT-enabled WSNs. Results and discussion

In this section, we present a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed NFDR mechanism, emphasizing its energy efficiency and cluster scalability features facilitated by the integrated neural network. Our experimental analysis utilizes the MATLAB neuro-fuzzy simulation tool, chosen for its adaptability to dynamic changes in the objective function, thereby ensuring efficient cluster node selection and enabling scalable cluster formation. For our simulation environment, we assume a network area of 300 × 300 m^2. Within this space, 200 cluster nodes are randomly distributed, while the sink (central node) is strategically positioned at the center of the network field. Each cluster node is assigned an initial energy of 2 joules. We have set the energy consumption () and path loss () values at 10 (nJ/bit) and 20 (pJ/bit/m^2) respectively. Data packets, each of size 100 bits, circulate among the cluster nodes at intervals of 50 microseconds. These packets are transmitted at a rate of 200 packets/second, with a transmission range varying between 10 and 20 m. For accuracy and reliability, our results are derived from the average of 10 runs per simulation iteration. This approach ensures optimal node selection for scalable cluster formations. Our NFDR mechanism performance metrics include variations in the objective function relative to data transmission, individual node energy fluctuations, energy differences before and after transmission, and the average end-to-end delay for each transmission instance. Conclusion

This study demonstrated the performance evaluation of the proposed NFDR mechanism in terms of energy-efficient data routing with dynamic cluster formations in IoT-enabled WSNs. The proposed NFDR model depicts scaling and energy-efficient data routing by integrating neural systems and fuzzy logic. It has developed pattern-strong and adaptive group forms for adjusting dynamic conditions. The pattern’s capability to measure energy-related parameters, fine-tune the objective function, and execute periodic fuzzy rule updates enhances the feasible choice process of cluster head and data transmission route while maintaining the networks’ energy effectively. This model has enhanced the performance by allocating energy to cluster nodes based on their functions and ensuring that all nodes acquire an adequate energy threshold to guarantee energy efficiency. Our simulations show that the proposed NFDR mechanism demonstrates improved network efficiency compared to existing strategies such as CEDAR3, ERNSS-MCC4, FEEC5, CL-HPWSR6, and MOTCO7. According to that, the percentage of the number of dead nodes by 15%, which shows guaranteed the estimated network’s lifetime is significantly longer. There is a considerable increase in data transmission for individual cluster nodes by 20% additional and with an average of 75%, there is an energy budget remaining per load. Such improvements are critical for an application that demands a real-time response. Furthermore, the continuation of our research work, more focus on integrating natural-inspired optimization algorithms to promote scaling, adaptiveness, and energy efficiency.

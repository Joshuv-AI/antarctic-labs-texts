Weak A2 spaces, the Kastanas game and strategically Ramsey sets
Clement Yung

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a weak A2 space (or wA2 -space), which generalises spaces satisfying Todorčević’s axioms A1 - A4 and countable vector spaces. We show that in any Polish weak A2 space, analytic sets are Kastanas Ramsey, and discuss the relationship between Kastanas Ramsey sets and sets in the projective hierarchy. We also show that in all spaces satisfying A1 - A4 , every subset of ℛ \cal{R} is Kastanas Ramsey iff Ramsey, generalising the recent result by [ 2 ] . Finally, we show that in the setting of Gowers wA2 -spaces, Kastanas Ramsey sets and strategically Ramsey sets coincide, providing a connection between the recent studies on topological Ramsey spaces and countable vector spaces.

The four axioms are as follows:

(A1)

(1)

(2)

(3)

For each

denote the unique

(A2)

There is a quasi-ordering

(1)

(2)

(3)

\forall a,b\in\cal{AR}[a\sqsubseteq b\wedge b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}c\to\exists d\sqsubseteq c[a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}d]]

(A3)

We may define the

\displaystyle[a,A]

\displaystyle:=\{B\in\cal{R}:B\leq A\wedge\exists n[r_{n}(B)=a]\},

\displaystyle[n,A]

\displaystyle:=[r_{n}(A),A].

Then the depth function defined by, for

\displaystyle 0pt_{B}(a):=\begin{cases}\min\{n<\omega:a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{n}(B)\},&\text{if such $n$ exists},\\

satisfies the following:

\displaystyle r_{n}[a,A]

\displaystyle:=\{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]:\operatorname{lh}(b)=n\}.

3.
The Kastanas game in
wA2
-spaces

We shall introduce the abstract Kastanas game in
wA2
-spaces, and study the set-theoretic properties of Kastanas Ramsey sets.

3.1.
The abstract Kastanas game

Definition 3.1

(Definition 5.1,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. Let

and

. The
Kastanas game
played below
[a,A]
, denoted as

, is defined as a game played by Player
I
and
II
in the following form:

Turn

I

II

1

A_{0}\in[a,A]

a_{1}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A_{0}]

B_{0}\in[a_{1},A_{0}]

2

A_{1}\in[a_{1},B_{0}]

a_{2}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a_{1})+1}[a_{1},A_{1}]

B_{1}\in[a_{2},A_{1}]

3

A_{2}\in[a_{2},B_{1}]

a_{3}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a_{2})+1}[a_{2},A_{2}]

B_{2}\in[a_{3},A_{2}]

\vdots

\vdots

\vdots

The outcome of this game is
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\in\cal{R}
(i.e. the unique element

such that
r_{n}(B)=a_{n}
for all
n
). We say that

(resp.

) has a strategy in

to
reach

\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
if it has a strategy in

to ensure that the outcome is in
\cal{X}
.

Note that we do not require
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
to satisfy either
A2
or
A4
for the game to make sense. In particular, we may consider the abstract Kastanas game in countable vector spaces.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. A set
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is

if for all

and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, there exists some
B\in[a,A]
such that one of the following holds:

(1)

I
has a strategy in
K[a,B]
to reach
\cal{X}^{c}
.

(2)

II
has a strategy in
K[a,B]
to reach
\cal{X}
.

The seemingly unintuitive decision to define Kastanas Ramsey sets such that

plays into the set
\cal{X}^{c}
, instead of
\cal{X}
, allows us to describe the relationship between Kastanas Ramsey sets and strategically Ramsey sets, projections and projective sets more easily.

We conclude the section with some definitions and notations which are useful in studying the Kastanas game.

Definition 3.3

, so by the same lemma,
I
has a strategy in
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}^{c}
. Indeed, otherwise let
\tau
be a strategy in
K[a,B]
witnessing that (2) holds for
(a,B)
. Player
II
shall follow
\tau
until they reach some turn
n
, ending with
II
playing
(a_{n},B_{n}^{\prime})
, such that
I
has no strategy in
K[a_{n},B_{n}^{\prime}]
to reach
\cal{X}_{n}^{c}
. Since
\cal{X}_{n}
is Kastanas Ramsey,
II
may instead play
(a_{n},B_{n})
in the last turn, where
B_{n}\in[a_{n},B_{n}^{\prime}]
, such that
II
has a strategy in
K[a_{n},B_{n}]
to reach
\cal{X}_{n}
. Afterwards,
II
follows this strategy to reach
\cal{X}_{n}
. Since
\cal{X}_{n}\subseteq\cal{X}
, this constitutes a strategy for
II
in
K[a,B]
to reach
\cal{X}
, contradicting our assumption.
∎

We now turn our attention to some negative results.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space, and let
\cal{O}\subseteq\cal{AR}
.

(1)

\cal{O}
is
(a,A)
-biasymptotic
, where
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, if for all
B\in[0pt_{A}(a),A]
,
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\cap\cal{O}\neq\emptyset
and
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]\cap\cal{O}^{c}\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

\cal{O}
is
biasymptotic
if
\cal{O}
is
(a,A)
-biasymptotic for all
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
.

Thus, the assertion that
\cal{R}
does not satisfy
A4
is equivalent to the assertion that there exists an
(a,A)
-biasymptotic set for some
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}
. We illustrate some examples here.

Example 3.8

(Infinite block sequences
\mathbf{FIN}_{\pm k}^{[\infty]}
)
.

Recall that for each
x\in\mathbf{FIN}_{\pm k}
, we defined:

\displaystyle n_{x}:=\min\{n<\omega:x(n)=\pm k\},

and:

\displaystyle Y:=\{x\in\mathbf{FIN}_{\pm k}:x(n_{x})=k\}.

Then, for all
A\in\mathbf{FIN}_{\pm k}^{[\infty]}
,
\left\langle A\right\rangle\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
\left\langle A\right\rangle\cap Y^{c}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, the set:

\displaystyle\cal{O}:=\{a\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[<\infty]}:a=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\wedge x_{n}\in Y\}.

is a biasymptotic set.

Example 3.9

(Countable vector space
E^{[\infty]}
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field such that
|F|>2
, and let
E
be an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space of dimension
\aleph_{0}
. We defined the set:

\displaystyle Y:=\{x\in E:\text{$x=e_{n}+y$ for some $n$ and $e_{n}<y$}\}.

We have that
\left\langle A\right\rangle\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
\left\langle A\right\rangle\cap Y^{c}\neq\emptyset
for all

. Thus, the set:

\displaystyle\{a\in E^{[<\infty]}:a=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\wedge x_{n}\in Y\}

is biasymptotic.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. If
A4
fails, then there exists some
\cal{X}\in\bm{\mathcal{KR}}\cap\overline{\bm{\mathcal{KR}}}
which is not Ramsey.

Proof.

Let
\cal{O}
be an
(a,A)
-biasymptotic set for some
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. Define:

\displaystyle\cal{X}:=\{C\in\cal{R}:a\sqsubseteq C\wedge r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}(C)\in\cal{O}\}.

Since
\cal{O}
is
(a,A)
-biasymptotic, for any
B\in[a,A]
, there exists some
C\in[a,B]
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}(C)\in\cal{O}
, and some
C^{\prime}\in[a,B]
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}(C)\notin\cal{O}
. Consequently,
[a,B]\cap\cal{X}\neq\emptyset
and
[a,B]\cap\cal{X}^{c}\neq\emptyset
, so
\cal{X}
is not Ramsey as
B
is arbitrary. However,
\cal{X}
is a countable union of clopen sets, so it is Borel (under the metrisable topology). By the Borel determinacy for
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}
, the game
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}
or
\cal{X}^{c}
is always determined, so
\cal{X}\in\bm{\mathcal{KR}}\cap\overline{\bm{\mathcal{KR}}}
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space, and assume that it has a biasymptotic set. If
\bm{\mathcal{KR}}\neq\cal{P}(\cal{R})
, then
\bm{\mathcal{KR}}
is not closed under complements.

Proof.

Fix a biasymptotic set
\cal{O}
, and let
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
be not Kastanas Ramsey. Define two sets as follows:

\displaystyle\cal{X}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{C\in\cal{X}:\forall m\,\exists n\geq m[r_{n}(C)\in\cal{O}]\},

\displaystyle\cal{X}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{C\in\cal{X}:\exists m\,\forall n\geq m[r_{n}(C)\notin\cal{O}]\}.

Observe that both
\cal{X}_{0}^{c}
and
\cal{X}_{1}^{c}
are Kastanas Ramsey: Indeed, for any
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
,
II
has a winning strategy in
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}_{0}^{c}
by playing
a_{n}\notin\cal{O}
for all
n
, and
II
also has a winning strategy in
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}_{1}^{c}
by playing
a_{n}\in\cal{O}
for all
n
. On the other hand, we have that
\cal{X}_{0}\cup\cal{X}_{1}=\cal{X}
, so if both
\cal{X}_{0}
and
\cal{X}_{1}
are Kastanas Ramsey, then so is
\cal{X}
by Proposition
3.6
, a contradiction. Thus, at least one of
\cal{X}_{0}
or
\cal{X}_{1}
witnesses that
\bm{\mathcal{KR}}
is not closed under complements.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space, and assume that it has a biasymptotic set. If
\bm{\mathcal{KR}}\neq\cal{P}(\cal{R})
, then
\bm{\mathcal{KR}}
is not closed under finite intersections.

Proof.

Fix a biasymptotic set
\cal{O}
, and let
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
be a Kastanas Ramsey set in which
\cal{X}^{c}
is not Kastanas Ramsey. Define two sets as follows:

\displaystyle\cal{X}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{C\in\cal{X}:\forall m\,\exists n\geq m[r_{n}(C)\in\cal{O}]\},

\displaystyle\cal{X}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{C\in\cal{X}:\exists m\,\forall n\geq m[r_{n}(C)\notin\cal{O}]\}.

By the same argument as in Proposition
3.11
,
\cal{X}_{0}^{c}
and
\cal{X}_{1}^{c}
are Kastanas Ramsey. However,
\cal{X}^{c}=\cal{X}_{0}^{c}\cap\cal{X}_{1}^{c}
is not.
∎

3.3.
Kastanas Ramsey sets in topological Ramsey spaces

We shall now give a proof of Theorem

Proposition 3.13

(Proposition 4.2,
[

]

Let

\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}

The second proposition is the following:

Proposition 3.14
.

Suppose that
(\cal{R},\leq,r)

-
A4

and

A proof of Proposition

for selective topological Ramsey spaces (Definition 5.4,
[

Definition 3.15
.

Let
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
.

(1)

A family of subsets
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
is
dense open below
[a,A]
if for all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]
,
\cal{D}_{b}
is a
\leq
-downward closed subset of
[b,A]
, and for all
B\in[b,A]
, there exists some
C\in[b,B]
such that
C\in\cal{D}_{b}
.

(2)

Let
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
be dense open below
[a,A]
. We say that
B\in[a,A]

diagonalises

\vec{\cal{D}}
if for all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,B]
, there exists some
A_{b}\in\cal{D}_{b}
such that
[b,B]\subseteq[b,A_{b}]
.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
is an
A2
-space, then every family of subsets
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
which is dense open below
[a,A]
has a diagonalisation.

In other words, Lemma
3.16
asserts that
\cal{R}
is a “semiselective coideal”.

Proof.

Fix some
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. Suppose that
\vec{\cal{D}}=\{\cal{D}_{b}\}_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
is dense open below
[a,A]
. We shall define a fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
in
[a,A]
, with
a_{n+1}:=r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}(A_{n})
, such that
A_{n+1}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}]
: Let
A_{0}:=A
, and suppose that
A_{n}
has been defined. Let
\{b_{i}:i<N\}
enumerate the set of all
b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A_{n}
such that
a\sqsubseteq b
and
b\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}a_{n+1}
. Let
A_{n+1}^{0}:=A_{n}
. If
A_{n+1}^{i}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}]
has been defined, let
B_{n+1}\in\cal{D}_{b_{i}}
be such that
B_{n+1}\in[b_{i},A_{n+1}^{i}]
, which exists as
\cal{D}_{b_{i}}
is dense open in
[b_{i},A]
. By
A3
, we then let
A_{n+1}^{i+1}\in[a_{n+1},A_{n}^{i}]
such that
[b_{i},A_{n+1}^{i+1}]\subseteq[b_{i},B_{n+1}]
. We complete the induction by letting
A_{n+1}:=A_{n+1}^{N}
. Let
B
be the limit of the fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n<\omega}
, and we have that
B
diagonalises
\vec{\cal{D}}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
3.14
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
. Suppose that
f:[a,A]\to r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]
and
g:[a,A]\to[a,A]
are two functions such that for all
B\leq A
:

(1)

f(B)\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,B]
.

(2)

g(B)\in[f(B),B]
.

We also say that these two functions
f,g
are
suitable
in
[a,A]
. Then there exists some
E_{f,g}\in[a,A]
such that for all
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,E_{f,g}]
, there exists some
B\in[a,A]
such that
f(B)=b
and
[b,E_{f,g}]\subseteq[b,g(B)]
.

Proof.

For each
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]
, we define:

\displaystyle\cal{D}_{b,0}:=\{D\in[b,A]:\exists B\in[a,A]\text{ s.t. }f(B)=b\wedge D\in[b,g(B)]\},

\displaystyle\cal{D}_{b,1}:=\{D\in[b,A]:\forall C\in[a,A],\,g(C)\in[b,D]\to g(C)\notin\cal{D}_{b,0}\}.

Let
\cal{D}_{b}:=\cal{D}_{b,0}\cup\cal{D}_{b,1}
. Observe that
\cal{D}_{b}
is dense open in
[b,A]
: Clearly both
\cal{D}_{b,0}
and
\cal{D}_{b,1}
are open. If
D\in[b,A]
and
D\notin\cal{D}_{b,1}
, then there exists some
C\in[a,A]
such that
g(C)\in[b,D]
. Then
g(C)\leq D
and
g(C)\in\cal{D}_{b,0}
, so
\cal{D}_{b}
is dense.

By Lemma
3.16
, there exists some
D\in[a,A]
diagonalising
(\cal{D}_{b})_{b\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}[a,A]}
. Now let:

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{0}:=\{b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,D]:\exists B\in\cal{D}_{b,0}\,[b,D]\subseteq[b,B]\},

\displaystyle\cal{O}_{1}:=\{b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,D]:\exists B\in\cal{D}_{b,1}\,[b,D]\subseteq[b,B]\}.

By
A4
, there exists some
E_{f,g}\in[a,D]
such that
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,E_{f,g}]\subseteq\cal{O}_{0}
or
r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,E_{f,g}]\subseteq\cal{O}_{1}
. However, we see that the latter case is not possible: In this case, we let
b:=f(E_{f,g})
. Then
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,E_{f,g}]\subseteq\cal{O}_{1}
, so let
B\in\cal{D}_{b,1}
such that
[b,D]\subseteq[b,B]
. Then
g(E_{f,g})\in[b,E_{f,g}]\subseteq[b,D]\subseteq[b,B]
, so
g(E_{f,g})\notin\cal{D}_{b,0}
. But
g(E_{f,g})\in[b,B]
, so this implies that
f(E_{f,g})\neq b
, a contradiction.

We shall show that
E_{f,g}
works. Let
b\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,E_{f,g}]
. Then
b\in\cal{O}_{0}
, so there exists some
B\in\cal{D}_{b,0}
such that
f(B)=b
and
[b,E_{f,g}]\subseteq[b,D]\subseteq[b,g(B)]
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.14
.

Let
\sigma
be a strategy for
II
in
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}
. We shall construct a strategy
\tau
for
I
in
K[a,A]
to reach
\cal{X}
as follows: We shall assign a state
s
(for
II
) in
K[a,A]
following
\tau
, to a state
t_{s}
(for
II) in
K[a,A]
, following
\sigma
, such that:

(1)

a(s)=a(t_{s})
.

(2)

\operatorname{last}(s)\leq\operatorname{last}(t_{s})
.

(3)

If
s^{\prime}\sqsubseteq s
, then
t_{s^{\prime}}\sqsubseteq t_{s}
.

We begin by defining
t_{\emptyset}:=\emptyset
. Now suppose that
s
is a state (for
II
) in
K[a,A]
following
\tau
so far. We define the functions
f_{s},g_{s}
by stipulating that for all
B\in[a(t_{s}),\operatorname{last}(t_{s})]
,
(f_{s}(B),g_{s}(B)):=\sigma({t_{s}}^{\frown}B)
. Observe that
f_{s},g_{s}
are suitable in
[a(s),\operatorname{last}(s)]
(when restricted to
[a(s),\operatorname{last}(s)]
), so by Lemma
3.17
there exists some
E_{s}\in[a(s),\operatorname{last}(s)]
such that for all

, there exists some

such that

and

. Thus, we define
\tau(s):=E_{s}\in[a(s),\operatorname{last}(s)]
, and for all

and

, define:

\displaystyle t_{s^{\frown}(b,C)}:={t_{s}}^{\frown}(b,g(B_{s,b})).

Clearly, (1) and (3) of the induction hypothesis are satisfied. (2) is also satisfied, as:

\displaystyle\operatorname{last}(s)=C\in[b,E_{s}]\subseteq[b,g(B_{s,b})].

This completes the induction. Since every total state following
\tau
corresponds to a total state following
\sigma
with the same outcome,
\tau
is a strategy for
I
to reach
\cal{X}
.
∎

, we get:

.

4.
Kastanas Ramsey Sets and the Projective Hierarchy

Given a
wA2
-space
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
, if
\cal{AR}
is countable then the metrisable topology is Polish, allowing us to discuss the projective hierarchy on
\cal{R}
. We discuss some relationships between Kastanas Ramsey sets and sets in the projective hierarchy.

4.1.
Projective Hierarchy

In this section, we introduce prove a general relationship between Kastanas Ramsey sets and sets in the projective hierarchy, which, in particular, gives a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Given a
wA2
-space
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
, we shall construct another
wA2
-space
(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega},\preceq,r)
as follows:

(1)

\cal{A}(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}):=\bigcup_{n<\omega}\cal{AR}_{n}\times 2^{n}
.

(2)

Given
(A,u)\in\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}
, let
r_{n}(A,x):=(r_{n}(A),u\mathord{\upharpoonright}n)
. Thus, if
(a,p)\in\cal{A}(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega})
, then
\operatorname{lh}(a,p)=\operatorname{lh}(a)=|p|
.

(3)

We define a
\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}
on
\cal{A}(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega})
by stipulating that
(a,p)\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}(b,q)
iff
a\leq_{\mathrm{fin}}b
.

(4)

Given
(A,u),(B,v)\in\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}
, we write:

\displaystyle(A,u)\preceq(B,v)\iff\forall n\,\exists m[r_{n}(A,u)\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}r_{m}(B,v)].

We remark that
\preceq
is
never
a partial order. For instance, if
a\in\cal{AR}_{2}
, then
(a,(0,1))\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}(a,(1,0))
and
(a,(1,0))\preceq_{\mathrm{fin}}(a,(0,1))
, but
(a,(0,1))\neq(a,(1,0))
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space (resp.
A2
-space). Then the closed triple
(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega},\preceq,r)
defined above is a
wA2
-space (resp.
A2
-space) which has a biasymptotic set.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega},\preceq,r)
satisfies
A1
,
wA2
(resp.
A2
) and
A3
. A biasymptotic set would be:

\displaystyle\cal{O}=\{(a,p)\in\cal{A}(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}):|p|>0\wedge p(|p|-1)=1\}.

∎

Let
\pi_{0}:\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}\to\cal{R}
denote the projection to the first coordinate, which is a surjective map which respects
\leq
(i.e. if
(A,p)\preceq(B,q)
then
\pi_{0}(A,p)\leq\pi_{0}(B,q)
). We also use
\vec{0}
to denote the infinite tuple of zeroes
(0,0,0,\dots)\in 2^{\omega}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. Let
\cal{C}\subseteq\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}
be a subset. Let
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. If
II
has a strategy in
K[(a,p),(A,\vec{0})]
to reach
\cal{C}
for some
p\in 2^{\operatorname{lh}(a)}
, then
II
has a strategy in
K[a,A]
to reach
\pi_{0}[\cal{C}]
.

Proof.

If
\sigma
is a strategy for
II
in
K[(a,p),(A,\vec{0})]
to reach
\cal{C}
, then the strategy
\tau
for
II
in
K[a,A]
defined by
\tau(A_{0},\dots,A_{n-1}):=(b,B)
, where
\sigma((A_{0},\vec{0}),\dots,(A_{n-1},\vec{0}))=((b,p),(B,u))
for some
p,u
, is a strategy to reach
\pi_{0}[\cal{C}]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. Let
\cal{C}\subseteq\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}
be a subset. Let
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
. If for all
p\in 2^{\operatorname{lh}(a)}
and
B\in[a,A]
, there exists some
C\in[a,B]
such that
I
has a strategy in
K[(a,p),(C,\vec{0})]
to reach
\cal{C}^{c}
, then

to reach

to reach

This also allows us to extend Corollary

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose that

4.2.
\Sigma_{2}^{1}

We dedicate this section to showing that Theorem

is consistently optimal for a family of sufficiently well-behaved

-space.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. We say that
\cal{R}
is
deep
if for all
A\in\cal{R}
,

and

We shall see later in the proof of Corollary

that all examples of

-spaces introduced in §2.2, except for the singleton space (Example

is infinite. We do not know if there are any “natural” examples of

-spaces which are not deep.

The main theorem of this section is as follows.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a deep
wA2
-space, and assume that
\cal{AR}
is countable. Suppose that there exists a
\Sigma_{2}^{1}
-good well-ordering of the reals. Then there exists a
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}^{1}
subset of
\cal{R}
which is not Kastanas Ramsey.

See also Theorem IV.7.4 of
[
1
]
. Observing that if
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
is deep, then so is
(\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega},\preceq,r)
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a deep
wA2
-space, and assume that
\cal{AR}
is countable. Then there exists a coanalytic subset of
\cal{R}\times 2^{\omega}
which is not Kastanas Ramsey.

We now furnish a proof of Theorem
4.8
. We shall now introduce two related games, which serve as a “reduction” of the Kastanas game for each player.

Definition 4.10
.

Let

Definition 4.12

(2)

.

has a strategy in

to
reach

\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
if

.

It is also easy to see that this gives us another reduction for

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

.

We shall now introduce the reduction of

Let

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a deep
wA2
-space. For any
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}
, if

has a strategy in

to reach
\cal{X}
, then

has a strategy in

to reach
\cal{X}
.

Proof.

We first note that since
\cal{R}
is deep, it is always possible for

to respond with a legal move in

. The deepness of
\cal{R}
also allows us to view

as a “special case” of

, if

responded with

, and

wants to restrict the next response by

for some

can respond to

by playing any

such that for all

,

. Therefore, a strategy for

in
K[a,A]
to reach

may be passed to a strategy for

in

to reach
\cal{X}
.

Definition 4.16
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space. A set
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{R}
is
pre-Kastanas Ramsey
if for all
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
, there exists some
B\in[a,A]
such that one of the following holds:

(1)

I
has a strategy in
Z^{*}[a,B]
to reach
\cal{X}^{c}
.

(2)

II
has a strategy in
Y[a,B]
to reach
\cal{X}
.

It is clear from the definition that every Kastanas Ramsey set is pre-Kastanas Ramsey.

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

It suffices to construct a
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}^{1}
subset of
\cal{R}
which is not pre-Kastanas Ramsey. Define the following two sets:

\displaystyle S_{\textbf{I}}

\displaystyle:=\{(a,A,\sigma):a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A\wedge\text{$\sigma$ is a strategy for {I} in $Z^{*}[a,A]$}\},

\displaystyle S_{\textbf{II}}

\displaystyle:=\{(a,A,\sigma):a\in\cal{AR}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A\wedge\text{$\sigma$ is a strategy for {II} in $Y[a,A]$}\}.

Note that a strategy in a game is a function from the set of partial states of the game to a play of the game. Since
\cal{AR}
is countable, the sets of partial states of
Z^{*}[a,A]
and of
Y[a,A]
are also countable, and in both games all players play from a countable set. Therefore, we may naturally embed both sets
S_{\textbf{I}}
and
S_{\textbf{II}}
to the reals, giving us a
\Sigma_{2}^{1}
-well ordering
\prec_{s}
of
S_{\textbf{I}}
and
S_{\textbf{II}}
. Note that
\prec_{s}
is of order-type
\omega_{1}
, so every triple in
S_{\textbf{I}}\cup S_{\textbf{II}}
has countably many
\prec_{s}
-predecessors.

Given a triple
(a,A,\sigma)\in S_{\textbf{I}}\cup S_{\textbf{II}}
, we build
B_{a,A,\sigma}\in\cal{R}
by an increasing sequence
a=b_{0}\sqsubseteq b_{1}\sqsubseteq\cdots
with
\operatorname{lh}(b_{n})=\operatorname{lh}(a)+n
, and let
B_{a,A,\sigma}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}
. We consider two cases.

(1)

If
(a,A,\sigma)\in S_{\textbf{I}}
, then we shall construct some
B_{a,A,\sigma}\in\cal{R}
such that
B_{a,A,\sigma}
is the outcome of some full play in
Z^{*}[a,A]
, with
I
following
\sigma
, and that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\neq B_{a^{\prime},A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}
for all
(a^{\prime},A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\prec_{s}(a,A,\sigma)
. We do this as follows: We first enumerate the
\prec_{s}
-predecessors by
\{(a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}):n<\omega\}
. Following a play in
Z^{*}[a,A]
where
I
follows
\sigma
, suppose that
I
started the
n^{\text{th}}
turn with
B_{n}\in[b_{n},A]
. If
b_{n}\neq r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n}(B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}})
or
B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}\not\leq A
, then pick any
b_{n+1}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}[b_{n},B_{n}]
. Otherwise, since
\cal{R}
is deep we may pick some
b_{n+1}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}[b_{n},B_{n}]
such that
0pt_{A}(b_{n+1})>0pt_{A}(r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}(B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}))
. This construction ensures that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\neq B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}
for all
n<\omega
.

(2)

If
(a,A,\sigma)\in S_{\textbf{II}}
, then we shall construct some
B_{a,A,\sigma}\in\cal{R}
such that
B_{a,A,\sigma}
is the outcome of some full play in
Y[a,A]
, with
II
following
\sigma
, and that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\neq B_{a^{\prime},A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}
for all
(a^{\prime},A^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})\prec_{s}(a,A,\sigma)
. We do this as follows: Again, we enumerate the
\prec_{s}
-predecessors by
\{(a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}):n<\omega\}
. Following a play in
Y[a,A]
where
II
follows
\sigma
, suppose that the sequence
b_{n}
has been played so far. If
B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}\leq A
, we then ask that
I
respond with
0pt_{A}(r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}(B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}))+1
, so that for any
b_{n+1}
that
II
respond with next,
b_{n+1}\neq r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+n+1}(B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}))
. Otherwise,
I
may respond with any
k<\omega
. This construction ensures that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\neq B_{a_{n},A_{n},\sigma_{n}}
for all
n<\omega
.

Now let:

\displaystyle\cal{X}:=\{B_{a,A,\sigma}:(a,A,\sigma)\in S_{\textbf{I}}\}.

\cal{X}
is
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}^{1}
, as the well-ordering
\prec_{s}
is
\Sigma_{2}^{1}
and the construction of
\cal{X}
is natural from
\prec_{s}
. We then see that
\cal{X}
is not pre-Kastanas Ramsey: Let
A\in\cal{R}
and
a\in\cal{AR}
.

(1)

If
\sigma
is a strategy for
I
in
Z^{*}[a,A]
, then
B_{a,A,\sigma}
is the outcome of a run following
\sigma
such that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\notin\cal{X}^{c}
, so
\sigma
is not a winning strategy for
I
.

(2)

If
\sigma
is a strategy for
II
in
Y[a,A]
, then
B_{a,A,\sigma}
is the outcome of a run following
\sigma
such that
B_{a,A,\sigma}\notin\cal{X}
, so
\sigma
is not a winning strategy for
II
.

This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that the set

would similarly produce a

)

The following

(2)

Proof.

By Corollary

, it suffices to show that every

-space above is deep.

(1)

Let

, and let

be finite. For all

such that

, we have that

. Therefore,

and
0pt_{A}(a\cup\{n_{N-1}\})\geq N
.

(2)

Let
A=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)\in\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]}
, and let

. For all
N
such that
x_{N}>a
, we have that
0pt_{A}(a^{\frown}x_{N-1})=N
. Therefore,
a^{\frown}x_{N-1}\in r_{\operatorname{lh}(a)+1}[a,A]
and
0pt_{A}(a^{\frown}x_{N-1})\geq N
.

(3)

The proof is identical to that of
(\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]},\leq,r)
.

(4)

Let
A=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)\in W_{Lv}^{[\infty]}
, and let

. Since

is rapidly increasing, for

. Therefore,

.

(5)

Let
A\subseteq T
be a strong subtree. Given any

, we let

be the set of terminal nodes in

. Since

for some

. We observe that:

\displaystyle 0pt_{A}(a)=N\iff S_{a}\subseteq\operatorname{split}_{N}(A).

Fix any
a\in\mathcal{S}_{<\infty}\mathord{\upharpoonright}A
and

be large enough. For each

, let

be such that

,

and

. We let:

\displaystyle b:=\{u\in A:u\sqsubseteq t_{s,i}\text{ for some $s\in S_{a}$ and $i\in\{0,1\}$}\}.

Observe that

is an initial segment of

. Thus,

Let

\displaystyle(i,j)\in b\iff\begin{cases}(i,j)\in A\text{ and }(i\in\operatorname{dom}(a)\text{ or }j\in\operatorname{dom}(a)),\text{ or};\\
(i\notin\operatorname{dom}(a)\text{ and }j\notin\operatorname{dom}(a)).\end{cases}

Given

such that

(7)

The proof is identical to that of
(\mathbf{FIN}_{k}^{[\infty]},\leq,r)

5.
Strategically Ramsey Sets and Gowers Spaces

5.1.
Gowers Spaces

de Rancourt first introduced Gowers spaces in
[
4
]
as a common abstraction to the topological Ramsey space
[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
(i.e. the Ellentuck space, or Mathias-Silver space) and countable vector spaces (i.e. Rosendal space). We recall the definition.

Definition 5.1

(Definition 2.1,
[
4
]
)
.

A
Gowers space
is a quintuple
(P,X,\leq,\leq^{*},\lhd)
, where
P\neq\emptyset
is the set of
subspaces
,
X\neq\emptyset
is at most countable (the set of
points
),
\leq,\leq^{*}
are two quasi-orders on
P
, and
\lhd\subseteq X^{<\omega}\times P
is a binary relation, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For all
p,q\in P
, if
p\leq q
, then
p\leq^{*}q
.

(2)

For all
p,q\in P
, if
p\leq^{*}q
, then there exists some
r\in P
such that
r\leq p
,
r\leq q
and
p\leq^{*}r
.

(3)

For every
\leq
-decreasing sequence
(p_{n})_{n<\omega}
of
P
, there exists some
p^{*}\in P
such that
p^{*}\leq^{*}p_{n}
for all
n<\omega
.

(4)

For all
p\in P
and
s\in X^{<\omega}
, there exists some
x\in X
such that
s^{\frown}x\lhd p
.

(5)

For all
s\in X^{<\omega}
and
p,q\in P
, if
s\lhd p
and
p\leq q
, then
s\lhd q
.

Given
p,q\in P
, we also write
p\lessapprox q
iff
p\leq q
and
q\leq^{*}p
.

de Rancourt proceeded to introduce various games in this abstract setting. We hereby provide a summary of the games we’re interested in. Note that we have employed some changes in the names/notations of the game.

Definition 5.2

(Definition 2.2,
[
4
]
)
.

For each
p\in P
, the
adversarial Gowers game

AG(p)
is defined as a game played by Player
I
and
II
in the following form:

I

x_{0},q_{0}

x_{1},q_{1}

\cdots

II

p_{0}

y_{0},p_{1}

y_{1},p_{2}

\cdots

such that
x_{n},y_{n}\in X
and
p_{n},q_{n}\in P
for all
n
, and that the following additional condition must be fulfilled for all
n<\omega
:

(1)

(x_{0},y_{0},\dots,x_{n-1},y_{n-1},x_{n})\lhd p_{n}
.

(2)

(x_{0},y_{0},\dots,x_{n},y_{n})\lhd q_{n}
.

(3)

p_{n}\leq p
and
q_{n}\leq p
.

The outcome of this game is
(x_{0},y_{0},x_{1},y_{1},\dots)
. We say that
I
(resp.
II
) has a strategy in
K(p)
to
reach

\cal{X}\subseteq X^{\omega}
if it has a strategy in
K(p)
to ensure that the outcome is in
\cal{X}
.

Definition 5.3

(Definition 2.2,
[
4
]
)
.

For each

, the
adversarial Gowers game for

AG_{\textbf{I}}(p)
(resp.
for

with the following additional restrictions:

(1)

For

.

(2)

For
AG_{\textbf{I}}(p)

.

Definition 5.4

(Definition 2.5,
[

]
)

(1)

Definition 5.5

(Definition 3.1,
[
4
]

.

For each

, the

p_{1}

\cdots

(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\lhd p_{n}

p_{n}\leq p

The outcome of this game is

(resp.

) has a strategy in

to
reach

\cal{X}\subseteq X^{\omega}

(Definition 3.1,

.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let

and
\cal{X}\subseteq X^{\omega}
.

(1)

has a strategy in

to reach
\cal{X}
for some

iff there exists some
q\leq p
such that
I
has a strategy in

to reach
\cal{X}
.

(2)

II
has a strategy in
R(q)
to reach
\cal{X}
for some

iff there exists some
q\leq p
such that
II
has a strategy in
AG_{\mathbf{II}}(q)
to reach
\cal{X}
.

Proof.

The forward direction for both statements has been proven in Proposition 2.6 of
[
4
]
, so we only prove the converse for (1) (the proof for the converse for (2) is almost verbatim). Suppose
\sigma
is a strategy for
I
in
AG_{\textbf{I}}(p)
to reach
\cal{X}
, and we define a strategy
\tau
for
I
in
R(p)
. For each state
s
for
II
of
R(p)
following
\tau
, we shall correspond it to a state
t_{s}
for
II
of
AG_{\textbf{I}}(p)
realising
a(s)
. Start by letting
t_{(p_{0})}:=(p_{0})
for any
p_{0}\leq p
. Now let
s
be a state of
R(p)
for
II
following
\tau
so far, with
s={s^{\prime}}^{\frown}(y_{n},p_{n+1})
. Suppose by the induction hypothesis that there exists a corresponding state
t_{s}
of the game

such that:

(1)

a(s^{\prime})=a(t_{s^{\prime}})
;

(2)

\sigma(t_{s^{\prime}})=(x_{n},q_{n}^{\prime})
for some
q_{n}^{\prime}\lessapprox p
.

Now let

, and suppose
\sigma(t_{s})=(x_{n+1},q_{n+1}^{\prime})
for some

. Since

, by Property (2) of Definition

there exists some

such that

. Then

is a legal continuation. This completes the inductive definition of

, which is a winning strategy as every complete play following
\tau
corresponds to a complete play following
\sigma
realising the same sequence.

5.2.
The Kastanas Game

We now introduce (our version of) the Kastanas game for Gowers spaces.

Definition 5.11

(Definition 2.5,
[
4
]
)
.

For each
p\in P
, the
Kastanas game

K(p)
is defined as a game played by Player
I
and
II
in the following form:

I

p_{0}

p_{1}

p_{2}

\cdots

II

x_{0},q_{0}

x_{1},q_{1}

\cdots

such that
x_{n}\in X
and
p_{n},q_{n}\in P
for all
n
, and that the following additional condition must be fulfilled for all
n<\omega
:

(1)

(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\lhd p_{n}
.

(2)

q_{n}\leq p_{n}
and
p_{n+1}\leq q_{n}
.

The outcome of this game is
(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)
. We say that
I
(resp.
II
) has a strategy in
K(p)
to
reach

\cal{X}\subseteq X^{\omega}
if it has a strategy in
K(p)
to ensure that the outcome is in
\cal{X}
.

Definition 5.12
.

A set
\cal{X}\subseteq\cal{X}^{\omega}
is
Kastanas Ramsey
if for all
p\in P
, there exists some
q\leq p
such that one of the following holds:

(1)

I
has a strategy in
K(q)
to reach
\cal{X}^{c}
.

(2)

II
has a strategy in
K(q)
to reach
\cal{X}
.

Proposition 5.13
.

A subset
\cal{X}\subseteq X^{\omega}
is Kastanas Ramsey iff
\cal{X}
is strategically Ramsey (Definition 3.2,
[
4
]
).

We shall prove this proposition as a corollary of Proposition
5.10
.

Proof.

We let
(P,X,\leq,\leq^{*},\lhd)
be a Gowers space, and assume WLOG that
0\notin X
. We then define a relation
\blacktriangleleft\;\subseteq(X\cup\{0\})^{<\omega}\times P
such that for all
s\in(X\cup\{0\})^{<\omega}
:

(1)

If
\operatorname{lh}(s)
is odd, then for all
x\in X\cup\{0\}
and
p\in P
,
s^{\frown}x\blacktriangleleft P
iff
x=0
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{lh}(s)
is even, then for all
x\in X\cup\{0\}
and
p\in P
,
s^{\frown}x\blacktriangleleft P
iff
x\neq 0
and
s^{\frown}x\lhd P
.

It is easy to verify that
(P,X\cup\{0\},\leq,\leq^{*},\blacktriangleleft)
is a Gowers space. We now define an injective function
f:X^{<\omega}\to(X\cup\{0\})^{<\omega}
by:

\displaystyle f((x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})):=(x_{0},0,x_{1},0\dots,x_{n-1},0)

and naturally extend
f
to
X^{\omega}\to(X\cup\{0\})^{\omega}
. Note that
f
is injective. For each
p\in P
, we also define the functions
g,h
by:

\displaystyle g(p_{0},0,p_{1},0,p_{2},\dots)

\displaystyle:=(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},\dots),

\displaystyle h(x_{0},q_{0},x_{1},q_{1},\dots)

\displaystyle:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots).

We may now observe that:

(1)

\sigma
is a strategy for
I
in
K(p)
to reach
\cal{X}
iff:

\displaystyle s\mapsto\begin{cases}\sigma(s),&\text{if $s=\emptyset$},\\
(0,\sigma(s)),&\text{if $s\neq\emptyset$}.\\
\end{cases}

is a strategy for
II
in
R(p)
to reach
f[\cal{X}]
.

(2)

\sigma
is a strategy for
II
in
K(p)
to reach
\cal{X}
iff
\sigma\circ g
is a strategy for
I
in
R(p)
to reach
f[\cal{X}]
.

(3)

\sigma
is a strategy for
I
in
F(p)
to reach
\cal{X}
iff:

\displaystyle s\mapsto\begin{cases}(\sigma\circ h)(s),&\text{if $s=\emptyset$},\\
(0,(\sigma\circ h)(s)),&\text{if $s\neq\emptyset$}.\\
\end{cases}

is a strategy for
II
in
AG_{\textbf{II}}(p)
to reach
f[\cal{X}]
.

(4)

\sigma
is a strategy for
II
in
G(p)
to reach
\cal{X}
iff
s\mapsto((\sigma\circ g)(s),p)
is a strategy for
I
in
AG_{\textbf{I}}(p)
to reach
f[\cal{X}]
.

Therefore, the proposition follows from Proposition
5.10
.

5.3.

-Spaces

We shall now reformulate the above result in the context of

-spaces. In
[

]
, Mijares introduced the notion of an

for spaces satisfying

-

, which may be applied to

Notation 5.14
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a

and

, we write

Note that

need not be a transitive relation - counterexample would be the topological Ramsey space of strong subtrees (which satisfies

-

, we may identify each element

with the sequence

.

Let

be a

is

if there exists a relation

such that the following properties hold:

-

)

For all

and

,

is a Gowers space (when identifying elements of
\cal{AR}
with

(
G6
)

Let

. Let

(1)

[a,A]\subseteq[a,B]
iff

for all

.

(2)

If there exists some

.

(

For all

Example 5.16

(Natural numbers/Ellentuck space

)
.

We show that
([\mathbb{N}]^{\infty},\subseteq,r)

wA2
-space.

(
G1
-
5
)

Let
A\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\infty}
and

C\setminus N\subseteq B
for some
N\geq\max(a)
. Given

and

.

(1)

(2)

If

.

(3)

Let

be a
\subseteq

(4)

Given

for any

If

(

)

Let

6.
Further remarks and open questions

Todorčević proved in
[
15
]
that if
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
is a closed triple satisfying

-
A4
, then every Suslin-measurable subset of
\cal{R}
is Ramsey. This is strictly stronger than Corollary
4.6
, which is our best conclusion from our general results regarding Kastanas Ramsey sets. However, by Proposition
3.12
, Kastanas Ramsey sets need not be closed under the Suslin operation in general.

Question 1
.

Can we prove a general result about Kastanas Ramsey subsets of a
wA2
-space which, when restricted to the setting of topological Ramsey space, directly implies that Ramsey subsets are closed under the Suslin operation?

There are various set-theoretic properties shared by Ramsey subsets of topological Ramsey spaces and strategically Ramsey subsets of countable vector spaces, but it is not apparent to us if one can provide general results (in the context of
wA2
-spaces) which encompass them. One such property concerns the statement “Every set is Kastanas Ramsey”. It is a classic result that in Solovay’s model, every subset of a Polish space has the property of Baire. Since the Ellentuck topology refines the Polish topology, we have the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a closed triple satisfying
A1
-
A4
such that
\cal{AR}
is countable. Let
\kappa
be an inaccessible cardinal, and let
\cal{G}
be
\operatorname{Coll}(\omega,<\kappa)
-generic. Then in the model
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathbf{V}[\cal{G}]}
, every subset of
\cal{R}
is Ramsey.

On the other hand, we have the following property of strategically Ramsey sets:

Theorem 6.2

(Lopez-Abad,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal, and let
\cal{G}
be
\operatorname{Coll}(\omega,<\kappa)
-generic. Then in the model
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathbf{V}[\cal{G}]}
, every subset of
E^{[\infty]}
is strategically Ramsey.

This leads to the following conjecture.

Conjecture
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a
wA2
-space, and assume that
\cal{AR}
is countable. It is consistent with sufficiently large cardinal assumptions that every subset of
\cal{R}
is Kastanas Ramsey.

We conclude with a question that naturally extends Corollary
4.9
:

Question 2
.

Let
(\cal{R},\leq,r)
be a sufficiently well-behaved
wA2
-space. Assume that it has a biasymptotic set, and that
\cal{AR}
is countable. If there exists a
\Sigma_{2}^{1}

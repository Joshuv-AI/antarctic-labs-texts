No Language Data Left Behind: A Comparative Study of CJK Language Datasets in the Hugging Face Ecosystem
Dasol Choi, Woomyoung Park, Youngsook Song, Yonsei University, MODULABS, SAIONIC AI, Lablup Inc.dasolchoi@yonsei.ac.krmax@sionic.aiyssong@lablup.com

Abstract
Recent advances in Natural Language Processing (NLP) have underscored the crucial role of high-quality datasets in building large language models (LLMs). However, while extensive resources and analyses exist for English, the landscape for East Asian languages—particularly Chinese, Japanese, and Korean (CJK)—remains fragmented and underexplored, despite these languages together serving over 1.6 billion speakers. To address this gap, we investigate the HuggingFace ecosystem from a cross-linguistic perspective, focusing on how cultural norms, research environments, and institutional practices shape dataset availability and quality. Drawing on more than 3,300 datasets, we employ quantitative and qualitative methods to examine how these factors drive distinct creation and curation patterns across Chinese, Japanese, and Korean NLP communities. Our findings highlight the large-scale and often institution-driven nature of Chinese datasets, grassroots community-led development in Korean NLP, and an entertainment- and subculture-focused emphasis on Japanese collections. By uncovering these patterns, we reveal practical strategies for enhancing dataset documentation, licensing clarity, and cross-lingual resource sharing—ultimately guiding more effective and culturally attuned LLM development in East Asia. We conclude by discussing best practices for future dataset curation and collaboration, aiming to strengthen resource development across all three languages.



1
Introduction

With the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) transforming the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP)
Kenton and Toutanova 2019
;
Brown et al. 2020
;
Achiam et al. 2023
, the importance of high-quality datasets in model development has become increasingly critical. For datasets to be valuable in this context, they must meet both quantitative requirements (sufficient size and coverage) and qualitative standards (reliability and representativeness). While English-language resources have been extensively studied, the landscape of datasets for East Asian languages—particularly Chinese, Japanese, and Korean (CJK)—remains comparatively underexplored
Joshi et al. 2020
;
Bender 2019
. This gap is especially noteworthy given that these languages collectively serve over 1.6 billion speakers and originate from major hubs of technological innovation.

In recent years, platforms such as HuggingFace have emerged as central repositories for distributing and accessing NLP datasets, making these resources widely accessible while introducing new challenges in dataset discovery, quality assessment, and cross-lingual collaboration
Hugging Face 2023
;
Lhoest et al. 2021
. These challenges are particularly pronounced for CJK languages due to their unique linguistic features, distinct cultural contexts, and varying approaches to data sharing and documentation.

While CJK languages play an increasingly important role in global NLP research, several critical issues need to be addressed. First, there is limited understanding of how dataset creation patterns differ across these language communities—and how those differences reflect their respective NLP ecosystems. Second, although cultural and institutional factors evidently influence dataset generation, their specific impacts on dataset characteristics and quality have yet to be systematically investigated. Third, the potential for cross-lingual synergies among CJK languages remains largely untapped, despite their many shared cultural and linguistic foundations. For instance, while benchmarks like MMLU
Hendrycks et al. 2020
are being generated in multiple languages, insufficient comparative analysis exists regarding how these variants differ from traditional parallel corpora or how download patterns across regions reflect distinct cultural preferences.
To address these challenges, this study investigates the HuggingFace ecosystem for Chinese, Japanese, and Korean NLP resources, focusing on dataset development and usage patterns. The specific objectives of this work are to:

•

Examine the current landscape of CJK datasets on HuggingFace, including key metadata such as domain, dataset size, documentation practices, and usage statistics.

•

Analyze the cultural characteristics and development patterns underlying dataset creation in each language community, highlighting commonalities and differences in curation and documentation.

•

Identify potential synergies and cross-lingual opportunities among the three languages, and propose strategies for more effective, collaborative dataset development.

Related Work

Documentation efforts for CJK language resources have expanded progressively over time. For Chinese,
Tao et al. 2009
introduced systematic approaches for constructing and evaluating linguistic data resources. More recently,
Li et al. 2023a
compiled a comprehensive repository of Chinese datasets, emphasizing detailed accessibility and classification frameworks. In the Korean context,
Cho et al. 2020
provided a thorough survey of existing NLP resources, stressing the need for enhanced documentation and broader data accessibility. Building on this,
Cho et al. 2023
examined the historical interplay between dataset creation and NLP model development, underscoring how local research cultures influence resource development. However, these earlier studies largely predate the rise of large generative models (e.g., GPT) and relied on GitHub as the primary hub for open-source NLP efforts. Since then, new platforms such as HuggingFace have substantially reshaped data-sharing practices, yet systematic, cross-lingual analyses of CJK datasets within these newer ecosystems remain rare.

In parallel, major multilingual projects— including BigScience

, CC100

While these initiatives contribute valuable resources, they do not investigate the deeper cultural or linguistic factors affecting dataset usage within specific communities such as CJK. Moreover, the broader movement toward improved dataset documentation (e.g.,
Datasheets for Datasets
;
Gebru et al. 2021
) suggests increasing awareness of ethical and practical considerations, yet how such guidelines are adopted varies widely across languages and platforms.

Over the past few years, HuggingFace has emerged as a central repository for sharing models, datasets, and evaluation metrics in NLP. Studies focusing on HuggingFace have flagged challenges such as inconsistent documentation
(
Yang et al. 2024
)
, limited transparency about training data
(

)

(

)
.
Additionally, recent work by
Darg̀is et al. 2024
offers valuable insights into building systematic evaluation frameworks for languages with particular linguistic and cultural traits—an approach that could inform the future assessment of CJK datasets. Nonetheless, no existing study has comprehensively analyzed how the unique ecosystem of Chinese, Japanese, and Korean language datasets on HuggingFace is shaped by local research cultures, licensing preferences, and community-driven development.

Building on these perspectives, our research provides the first large-scale, comparative analysis of CJK datasets within the HuggingFace ecosystem. We investigate how cultural contexts, platform-specific practices, and documentation standards influence dataset usage patterns and resource quality. Additionally, we highlight potential synergies for cross-lingual collaboration and offer insights that may guide more effective and ethically grounded dataset curation for East Asian NLP.

4.3.1
Chinese Dataset Ecosystem

Comprehensive Evaluation Frameworks

Chinese NLP resources on Hugging Face frequently feature large-scale, well-structured evaluation suites. For example,

Specialized Medical Domain Resources

Chinese datasets also demonstrate significant depth in specialized fields. The

Dialectal and Cultural Diversity

In addition to specialized domains, Chinese datasets often emphasize linguistic diversity and cultural preservation. The

raptorkwok/cantonese-traditional-chinese-parallel-corpus

Future efforts could enrich such dialectal resources by detailing speaker demographics, annotation workflows, and language-specific quirks—thereby promoting more equitable research coverage across China’s diverse linguistic communities.

Korean Dataset Ecosystem

Community-Driven Development and Its Impact

The broader ecosystem is dominated by individual and community contributors who account for 79.2% of Korean datasets. For instance, Korean has the highest proportion of “Unknown” licenses among CJK languages (35.3%), indicating gaps in legal clarity and potential challenges for commercial or cross-institutional usage. Researchers must therefore verify these constraints to avoid unintended usage restrictions or downstream complications.

5
Discussion

Our analysis of Chinese, Japanese, and Korean (CJK) datasets on Hugging Face reveals key trends for LLM development in East Asia. Each language community exhibits distinct characteristics and challenges: Chinese datasets show strong institutional backing but inconsistent documentation; Korean datasets demonstrate active community participation but face licensing and documentation issues; and Japanese datasets feature unique subcultural content while dealing with copyright constraints and synthetic data quality concerns.

From a practical standpoint, three points warrant attention. First, the diversity of licensing schemes—particularly the prevalence of “Unknown” licenses in Korean datasets and copyleft or restricted licenses in Japanese resources—complicates commercial or collaborative projects. Although Hugging Face offers an official dataset card template, many CJK datasets only partially adopt its recommended sections. More consistent adherence to existing templates or integration of additional guidelines (e.g., “Datasheets for Datasets”
Gebru et al. 2021
) could enhance clarity and reusability. Second, domain clustering (e.g., medical for Chinese, subcultural for Japanese, moderation tasks for Korean) may leave other areas underserved. Broadening coverage could yield more general-purpose LLM development. Third, culturally specific content—dialects, humor traditions, and local censorship norms—requires transparent documentation, given that simple translations often miss nuanced cultural meanings.

Despite these differences, there is strong potential for synergy. Joint benchmarks spanning all three languages could facilitate fairer cross-lingual performance comparisons, while unified documentation frameworks could standardize metadata, licensing, and annotation. Collaborative consortia could streamline data-cleaning pipelines and repository best practices. Finally, future work may integrate linguistic experts for dataset reviews, conduct controlled experiments to gauge the effects of domain specialization, and broaden the platform scope beyond Hugging Face. Overall, our findings underscore both the richness and fragmentation of CJK resources, suggesting that clearer licensing practices, documentation standards, and cross-lingual collaboration can foster a robust and inclusive ecosystem for LLM across East Asia.

6
Limitations

Our analysis primarily focused on datasets with relatively high download counts, which may have led us to overlook smaller or emerging resources that could shed light on niche trends or specialized applications. Furthermore, we limited our scope to the Hugging Face platform; investigating additional repositories (e.g., GitHub, Kaggle, or Papers with Code) could reveal a broader range of dataset characteristics and host factors. Although we manually requested permission to access certain private or restricted datasets, some ultimately remained inaccessible, thereby constraining the representativeness of our findings.

In addition, while Korean and Japanese datasets were examined with input from language experts, our review of Chinese data relied solely on documentation, potentially affecting the depth of our analysis. Finally, we chose to focus on three major East Asian languages, excluding many low-resource languages and dialects, whose inclusion could further expand and enrich our findings.

7
Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of Chinese, Japanese, and Korean datasets on the HuggingFace platform. Our analysis of over 3,300 datasets reveals distinct ecosystem characteristics: Chinese datasets exhibit strong institutional involvement, Korean datasets grow mainly through community-driven efforts, and Japanese datasets emphasize subcultural content while facing challenges in native resource development.
The varying levels of documentation quality, licensing preferences, and ownership structures suggest that effective dataset development strategies must consider linguistic features but also cultural and institutional contexts. These insights can guide more effective dataset curation strategies and foster collaboration across East Asian NLP communities, ultimately supporting the development of more inclusive language technologies.

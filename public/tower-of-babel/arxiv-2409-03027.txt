Non-harmonic analysis of the wave equation for Schrödinger operators with complex potential
Aparajita Dasgupta, Lalit Mohan, Shyam Swarup Mondal

Abstract
. This article investigates the wave equation for the Schrödinger operator on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , denoted as ℋ 0 := − Δ + V \mathcal{H}_{0}:=-\Delta+V , where Δ \Delta is the standard Laplacian and V V is a complex valued multiplication operator. We prove that the operator ℋ 0 \mathcal{H}_{0} , with Re ⁡ ( V ) ≥ 0 \operatorname{Re}(V)\geq 0 and Re ⁡ ( V ) ​ ( x ) → ∞ \operatorname{Re}(V)(x)\to\infty as | x | → ∞ |x|\to\infty , has a purely discrete spectrum under certain conditions. In the spirit of Colombini, De Giorgi, and Spagnolo, we also prove that the Cauchy problem with regular coefficients is well-posed in the associated Sobolev spaces, and when the propagation speed is Hölder continuous (or more regular), it is well-posed in Gevrey spaces. Furthermore, we prove that it is very weakly well-posed when the coefficients possess a distributional singularity.

The family of linear operators applies to various potentials

By

\mathbb{C}
with a property that it grows at most polynomially at infinity, i.e., there exist constants
\ell<\infty
and
C_{u,\ell}
such that

|u(\xi)|\leq C_{u,\ell}\langle\xi\rangle^{\ell}

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
. The collection of such
u:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
form a distribution space which is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I})
.

Definition 3.5

(Fourier transform)
.

The
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier transform

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}f\right)(\xi)=(f\mapsto\widehat{f}):C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})

is given by

\hat{f}(\xi):=\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}f\right)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{v_{\xi}(x)}~dx.

Similarly, the
\mathcal{H}^{*}
-Fourier transform

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}\cdot^{*}}f\right)(\xi)=\left(f\rightarrow\widehat{f}_{*}\right):C_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow S(\mathcal{I})

is defined by

\hat{f}_{*}(\xi):=\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}f\right)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{u_{\xi}(x)}~dx.

Proposition 3.6
.

The
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}
is a bijective homeomorphism from
C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})
. The inverse of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}
,

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}:\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is given by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}h\right)(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}h(\xi)u_{\xi}(x),~h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})

in order that the Fourier inversion formula is given by

f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\hat{f}(\xi)u_{\xi}(x),~~f\in C_{\mathcal{H}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Similarly,
\mathcal{H}^{*}
-Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}:C_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})
is a bijective homeomorphism and its inverse
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{-1}:\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I})\rightarrow C_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
given by

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{-1}h\right)(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}h(\xi)v_{\xi}(x),h\in S(\mathcal{I})

such that the conjugate Fourier inversion formula is given by

f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\widehat{f}_{*}(\xi)v_{\xi}(x),f\in C_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

3.2.
Plancherel formula and Sobolev spaces

We revisit the Sobolev spaces generated by the operator
\mathcal{H}
in this subsection. Additionally, we introduce appropriate sequence spaces
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H})
and
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{*})
to obtain the Plancherel identity.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H})
be the linear space of complex-valued functions
g
on
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
i.e., if there exists
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}f=g.
Then the space of sequences
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle a,b\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}a(\xi)\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}b\right)(\xi)}

for arbitrary
a,b\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}}).

Here the norm on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})
is given by

\|a\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}a(\xi)\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}^{-1}a\right)(\xi)},~~\quad a\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}}).

In the similar manner, the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{*})
be the linear space of complex-valued functions
g
on
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{-1}g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
with the inner product

\langle a,b\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})}:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}a(\xi)\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{-1}b\right)(\xi)}

for arbitrary
a,b\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{*})
.
The sequence spaces
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H})

and
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{H}^{*})
are thus generated by the biorthogonal
systems
\{u_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
and
\{v_{\xi}\}_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}
respectively. The following Plancherel’s identity provides a clear indication of the reasoning behind this specific definition selection.

Proposition 3.8

(Plancherel’s identity)
.

If
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
\widehat{f_{1}},\widehat{f_{2}}\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}}),\widehat{f_{1}}_{*},\widehat{f_{2}}_{*}\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})

and the inner products take the form

\langle\widehat{f_{1}},\widehat{f_{2}}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}_{*}(\xi)}

and

\langle\widehat{f_{1}}_{*},\widehat{f_{2}}_{*}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\widehat{f_{1}}_{*}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi)}.

Particularly, we have

\overline{\langle\widehat{f_{1}},\widehat{f_{2}}\rangle}_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}=\langle\widehat{f_{2}}_{*},\widehat{f_{1}}_{*}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})}

and the Parseval identity takes of the form

\langle{f_{1}},{f_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\langle\widehat{f_{1}},\widehat{f_{2}}\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}_{*}(\xi)}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\widehat{f_{1}}_{*}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\xi)}.

Also, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
we have
\widehat{f}\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}}),\widehat{f}_{*}\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})
and

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})}=\|\widehat{f}_{*}\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})}.

Let us now recall the definition of Sobolev spaces generated by the operator
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 3.9

(Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
)
.

For
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s\in\mathbb{R},
we
say that
f\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}^{*}})
. The norm on
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{*}(\xi)}\right)^{1/2}.

The Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is then the space of
\mathcal{H}
-distributions
f
for which
\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of
\mathcal{H}^{*}
-distributions
f
for which

\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\widehat{f}_{*}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi)}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Observe that
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For every
s\in\mathbb{R},
the Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}_{*}(\xi)}

Similarly, with respect to the following inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\widehat{f}_{*}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi)},

the Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also a Hilbert space for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. It can be observed that the Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}^{*}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are isometrically isomorphic and in particular
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also, for real
s\geq 1
, we define the (Roumieu) Gevrey space
\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
by the formula

f\in\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\Longleftrightarrow\exists A>0:\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{I}}\mathrm{e}^{2A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}<\infty.

(3.1)

4.
Proofs of the main results

In this section, our first aim is to prove the well-posedness to the
Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with regular coefficients. Here we will consider only the
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier transform. However, one can similarly obtain all the results by considering
\mathcal{H}^{*}
-Fourier transform.

Taking the
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier transform of (
1.3
) with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)+|\lambda_{\xi}|a(t)\widehat{v}(t,\xi)+q(t)\widehat{v}(t,\xi)=\widehat{f}(t,\xi),\quad(t,\xi)\in(0,T]\times\mathcal{I},\\
\widehat{v}(0,\xi)=\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{I},\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(0,\xi)=\widehat{v}_{1}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{I}.\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

The main concept behind our further analysis is that we can analyze each equation in (
4.1
) individually and then using the Plancherel formula one can obtain required estimates. We can reformulate the Cauchy problem (
4.1
) in the
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier space as the first order system using the following matrix transformation:

V(t,\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i\langle\xi\rangle\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\end{array}\right),\quad V_{0}(\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i\langle\xi\rangle\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi)\\
\widehat{v}_{1}(\xi)\end{array}\right).

(4.2)

Using above transformation, we see that (
4.1
) is equivalent to the following first order system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}V(t,\xi)=i\langle\xi\rangle A(t)V(t,\xi)+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}Q(t)V(t,\xi)+F(t,\xi),\quad(t,\xi)\in(0,T]\times\mathcal{I},\\
V(0,\xi)=V_{0}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{I},\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

where

A(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
a(t)&0\end{array}\right),~~\quad Q(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
q(t)-a(t)&0\end{array}\right),~~\text{ and }F(t,\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\end{array}\right).

(4.4)

Note that the matrix
A(t)
has eigenvalues
\pm\sqrt{a(t)}
and its symmetriser
S
is given by

S(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(t)&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

i.e., we have

SA-A^{*}S=0.

One can observe that
S(t)
is positive for each
t\in[0,T]
, and

\|S(t)\|\leq(1+|a(t)|),\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

(4.5)

Also from definition of
S
and
Q
, we have

\partial_{t}S(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{t}a(t)&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&a(t)-q(t)\\
q(t)-a(t)&0\end{array}\right).

Hence

\left\|\partial_{t}S(t)\right\|\leq\left|\partial_{t}a(t)\right|~~\text{and}~~\left\|\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t)\right\|\leq|q(t)|+|a(t)|,\quad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T].

(4.6)

Now we are ready to prove the well-posedness results for Case 1-Case 4. We start with our first key result in the scenario when the propagation speed
a
is regular.

Proof of Theorem
2.1

(Case 1)
.

First, we define the energy

E(t,\xi):=(S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)).

Then

\displaystyle E(t,\xi)=(S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle=\langle\xi\rangle^{2}a(t)|\widehat{v}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\{a(t),1\}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{0}|V(t,\xi)|^{2},\quad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T]~~\text{and}~~\xi\in\mathcal{I},

where
c_{0}=\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\{a(t),1\}
. Similarly, we can deduce that

E(t,\xi)\geq c_{1}|V(t,\xi)|^{2},\quad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T]~~\text{and}~~\xi\in\mathcal{I},

(4.8)

where
c_{1}=\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\inf}\{a(t),1\}
. Using (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E(t,\xi)=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(S(t)\partial_{t}V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(S(t)V(t,\xi),\partial_{t}V(t,\xi)\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+i\langle\xi\rangle(S(t)A(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))+(S(t)F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}(S(t)Q(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))-i\langle\xi\rangle(S(t)V(t,\xi),A(t)V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle-i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}(S(t)V(t,\xi),Q(t)V(t,\xi))+(S(t),F(t,\xi)V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+i\langle\xi\rangle\left(\left(SA-A^{*}S\right)(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+

\displaystyle i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+2\operatorname{Re}(S(t)F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}S(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+2\operatorname{Re}(S(t)F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}S(t)\right\||V(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left\|\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t)\right\||V(t,\xi)|^{2}+2\|S(t)\||F(t,\xi)\|V(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\partial_{t}S(t)\right\|+\left\|\left(SQ-Q^{*}S\right)(t)\right\|+\|S(t)\|\right)|V(t,\xi)|^{2}+\|S(t)\||F(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\|\partial_{t}a\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)|V(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)|F(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{\prime}|V(t,\xi)|^{2}+c^{\prime\prime}|F(t,\xi)|^{2},

where
c^{\prime}=1+\|\partial_{t}a\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
c^{\prime\prime}=1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Applying the Gronwall’s lemma to inequality (
4.9
), we can conclude that

E(t,\xi)\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}c^{\prime}\mathrm{~d}\tau}\left(E(0,\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}c^{\prime\prime}|F(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right),

(4.10)

or all
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
. Now, combining (
4.7
), (
4.8
), and (
4.10
), we deduce that

\displaystyle c_{0}|V(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq E(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}c^{\prime}\mathrm{~d}\tau}\left(E(0,\xi)+\int_{0}^{t}c^{\prime\prime}|F(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{c^{\prime}T}\left(c_{1}|V(0,\xi)|^{2}+c^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}|F(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

Therefore, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
, there exists a constant
C_{00}>0
such that

|V(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq C_{00}\left(|V(0,\xi)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|F(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

(4.11)

Hence

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{00}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(0,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|F(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq C_{00}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(0,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}|\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

Multiplying (
4.12
) with
\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}
and applying Plancherel’s formula, we can conclude that

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|v_{t}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}\right),

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where the constant
C>0
independent of
t\in[0,T]
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Now we prove our second key result in the scenario when the propagation speed
a
is a Hölder continuous function of order
0<\alpha<1.

Proof of Theorem
2.2

(Case 2)
.

We assume that
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
with
0<\alpha<1
such that
\underset{t\in[o,T]}{\inf}a(t)=a_{0}>0.
In this case, we apply the technique established by Colombini and Kinoshita in
[
7
]
. Our aim is to find the solution of the system (
4.3
) in the following form:

V(t,\xi)=e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}H(t)W(t,\xi),

(4.13)

where
\rho\in C^{1}([0,T])
is a real-valued function with the property that
\rho(0)=0
,
W=W(t,\xi)
will be determined later and

H(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
-\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\end{array}\right).

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, here we define
\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)
as

\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)=\left(\sqrt{a}*\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)(t)

with
\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\psi(\frac{t}{\varepsilon})
for each
t\in[0,T]
, and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-negative function such that

supp(\psi)\subseteq[0,T]\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{T}\psi(x)\mathrm{~d}x=1.

By above construction, we can conclude that
\lambda^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,T])
and

\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\geq\sqrt{a_{0}}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1].
Therefore,

\operatorname{det}H(t)=2\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\geq 2\sqrt{a_{0}}.

Furthermore, for every
t\in[0,T]
, using the Hölder regularity property of
a(t)
with an order of
\alpha
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)-\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\sqrt{a}*\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)(t)-\sqrt{a(t)}\right|

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{a(t-y)}\psi_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathrm{~d}y-\sqrt{a(t)}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(y)\mathrm{~d}y\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{a(t-\varepsilon x)}\psi(x)\mathrm{~d}x-\sqrt{a(t)}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(x)\mathrm{~d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mid a(t-\varepsilon x)-a(t))\mid}{\sqrt{a(t-\varepsilon x)}-\sqrt{a(t)}}\psi(x)\mathrm{~d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

Now, substituting (
4.13
) in (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}H(t)\partial_{t}W(t,\xi)+e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}(\xi)}\left(-\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}\right)(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}H(t)W(t,\xi)

\displaystyle-e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H(t)}{(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{2}}H(t)W(t,\xi)+e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}\left(\partial_{t}H(t)\right)W(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\langle\xi\rangle e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}A(t)H(t)W(t,\xi)+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}e^{-\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))^{-1}Q(t)H(t)W(t,\xi)+F(t,\xi).

Multiplying the above equation by
e^{\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))H(t)^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W(t,\xi)-\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}W(t,\xi)-\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H(t)}{\operatorname{det}H(t)}W(t,\xi)+H(t)^{-1}\partial_{t}H(t)W(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\langle\xi\rangle(H(t))^{-1}A(t)H(t)W(t,\xi)+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}H(t)^{-1}Q(t)H(t)W(t,\xi)+e^{\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}(\operatorname{det}H(t))H(t)^{-1}F(t,\xi).

Therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}=2\operatorname{Re}\left(\partial_{t}W(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right)

(4.15)

\displaystyle=2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}+2\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H(t)}{\operatorname{det}H(t)}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}-2\operatorname{Re}\left(H(t)^{-1}\partial_{t}H(t)W(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle-2\langle\xi\rangle\operatorname{Im}\left(H(t)^{-1}A(t)H(t)W(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right)-2\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\left(H(t)^{-1}Q(t)H(t)W(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+2e^{\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\operatorname{Re}\left((\operatorname{det}H(t))H(t)^{-1}F(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right).

For any matrix
C=C(t)\in M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we know that

2\operatorname{Im}\left(H(t)^{-1}C(t)H(t)W(t,\xi),W(t,\xi)\right)\leq\left\|H^{-1}CH-\left(H^{-1}CH\right)^{*}\right\||W(t,\xi)|^{2}

for each
t\in[0,T]
. This implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}+2\left|\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H(t)}{\operatorname{det}H(t)}\right||W(t,\xi)|^{2}+2\left\|H^{-1}\partial_{t}H\right\|\left|W(t,\xi)^{2}\right|

(4.16)

\displaystyle+\langle\xi\rangle\left\|H^{-1}AH-\left(H^{-1}AH\right)^{*}\right\||W(t,\xi)|^{2}+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left\|H^{-1}QH-\left(H^{-1}QH\right)^{*}\right\||W(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle+2e^{\rho(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\left\|(\operatorname{det}H)H^{-1}\right\||F(t,\xi)||W(t,\xi)|.

Now, using the methods described in
[
21
,
34
]
, we prove the following estimations to handle the aforementioned term:

(1)

\|\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H}{\operatorname{det}H}\|\leq c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}
,

(2)

\left\|H^{-1}\partial_{t}H\right\|\leq c_{2}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}
,

(3)

\left\|H^{-1}AH-\left(H^{-1}AH\right)^{*}\right\|\leq c_{3}\varepsilon^{\alpha}
,

(4)

\left\|H^{-1}QH-\left(H^{-1}QH\right)^{*}\right\|\leq c_{4}\varepsilon^{\alpha}
,

(5)

\left\|(\operatorname{det}H)H^{-1}\right\|\leq c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha},

where
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},c_{5}
are positive constants. Proof of the above five estimates are as follows:

(1)

It is easy to check that

\left|\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H(t)}{\operatorname{det}H(t)}\right|=\left|\frac{2\partial_{t}\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)}{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)}\right|\leq\frac{|\partial_{t}\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)|}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}

and

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sqrt{a}*\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{a(t-s\varepsilon)}\psi^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{~d}s\right|

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}|\sqrt{a(t-s\varepsilon)}-\sqrt{a(t)}|\psi^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\frac{|a(t-s\varepsilon)-a(t)|}{\sqrt{a(t-s\varepsilon)}+\sqrt{a(t)}}\psi^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1},\quad\text{for each $t\in[0,T]$},

where
c^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{~d}s
. Thus

\left\|\frac{\partial_{t}\operatorname{det}H}{\operatorname{det}H}\right\|\leq\frac{c^{\prime}_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}=c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\quad\text{with $c_{1}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{1}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}.$}

(2)

Note that
H^{-1}(t)=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&-1\\
\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&1\end{array}\right)
and
\partial_{t}H(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\end{array}\right)
. Thus

H^{-1}(t)\partial_{t}H(t)=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&-\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\\
\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)&\partial_{t}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\end{array}\right).

Therefore

\left\|H^{-1}\partial_{t}H\right\|\leq\frac{c^{\prime}_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}=c_{2}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\quad\text{with $c_{2}=\frac{c^{\prime}_{1}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}.$}

(3)

It is easy to verify that

H^{-1}(t)A(t)H(t)-\left(H^{-1}(t)A(t)H(t)\right)^{*}=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-2a(t)+2\left(\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right)^{2}\\
2a(t)-2\left(\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right)^{2}&0\end{array}\right).

(4.18)

Also, from (
4.14
), one can deduce that

\displaystyle\left|a(t)-\left(\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\sqrt{a(t)}-\left(\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right)\right)\left(\sqrt{a(t)}+\left(\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)\right)\right)\right|

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\left|\sqrt{a(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{a(t-s)}\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)\mathrm{~d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}\varepsilon^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=c_{3}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{\alpha},\quad\text{where $c_{3}^{\prime}=\frac{\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])}}{\sqrt{a_{0}}}\|\sqrt{a}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}.$}

Thus

\left\|H^{-1}AH-\left(H^{-1}AH\right)^{*}\right\|\leq\frac{c_{3}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}=c_{3}\varepsilon^{\alpha},\quad\text{
where}\quad c_{3}=\frac{c_{3}^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

(4)

A direct calculations yields

H^{-1}(t)Q(t)H(t)-\left(H^{-1}(t)Q(t)H(t)\right)^{*}=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-(q(t)-a(t))&-(q(t)-a(t))\\
q(t)-a(t)&q(t)-a(t)\end{array}\right).

(4.20)

Therefore

\left\|H^{-1}QH-\left(H^{-1}QH\right)^{*}\right\|\leq\frac{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}=c_{4}\varepsilon^{\alpha},

where
c_{4}=\frac{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+\|q\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}}{2\sqrt{a_{0}}}.

(5)

For each
t\in[0,T],
a simple computation gives us

|\lambda^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}

for some positive constant
c_{5}
. Thus

\left\|(\operatorname{det}H)H^{-1}\right\|=\left\|\lambda^{\varepsilon}\right\|\leq c_{5}\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

Now, using all the five estimates described above and the fact that
|F(t,\xi)|\leq c_{0}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}
for some constants
c_{0},A>0
, the estimate (
4.16
) further reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\rho^{\prime}(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+2c_{1}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+2c_{2}\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\langle\xi\rangle c_{3}\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}c_{4}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\right)|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

(4.21)

\displaystyle+2c_{0}c_{5}e^{\left(\rho(t)-A\right)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}|W(t,\xi)|.

Let us choose
\varepsilon=\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}
and define
\rho(t)=-kt,
where
k>0
will be chosen later. Then (
4.21
) becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(-2k\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+2c_{1}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+2c_{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+\langle\xi\rangle c_{3}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha}+\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}c_{4}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha}\right)|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

(4.22)

\displaystyle+2c_{0}c_{5}e^{\left(-kt-A\right)\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha}|W(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(-2k\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}+2c_{1}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+2c_{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+c_{3}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+c_{4}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}\right)|W(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle+2c_{0}c_{5}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-\alpha}|W(t,\xi)|.

Without any loss of generality, we assume that
|W(t,\xi)|\geq 1.
We also assume that
\frac{1}{s}>1-\alpha.
Then

\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq\left(-2k\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}+k_{0}\langle\xi\rangle^{1-\alpha}\right)|W(t,\xi)|^{2},

where
k_{0}=2c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}+c_{4}+2c_{0}c_{5}.
Further, if we choose
k>\frac{k_{0}}{2}
, then for each
\xi\in\mathcal{I},
we obtained that

\partial_{t}|W(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq 0.

This implies that
|W(t,\xi)|
is a monotone function for each
\xi\in\mathcal{I},
and hence

\displaystyle|V(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\|H(t)\||\operatorname{det}(H(t))|^{-1}e^{kt\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|W(t,\xi)|

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq\|H(t)\||\operatorname{det}H(t)|^{-1}e^{kt\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\|H(0)\|^{-1}|\operatorname{det}H(0)||V(0,\xi)|.

Since

\|H(t)\||\operatorname{det}H(t)|^{-1}\|H(0)\|^{-1}|\operatorname{det}H(0)|\leq b_{0}

for some
b_{0}>0,
we have

|V(t,\xi)|\leq b_{0}e^{kT\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V(0,\xi)|.

Now, using (
4.2
), we get

\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq b_{0}^{2}e^{2kT\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(0,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}\right).

Since the initial Cauchy data
v_{0},v_{1}\in\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, we can conclude that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) has a unique solution
v\in C^{2}\left([0,T];\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
provided

1\leq s<1+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}.

∎

Now we prove our third key result in the scenario when the propagation speed
a\in\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])
with
l\geq 2.

Proof of Theorem
2.3

(Case 3)
.

In this case, we assume that
a\in\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])
with
l\geq 2,
such that
a(t)\geq 0.
Using the notations from the preceding cases, we want to investigate the well-posedness of the system (
4.3
). Let
V(t,\xi)
be a column vector with column entry
V_{1}(t,\xi)
and
V_{2}(t,\xi).
Consider the quasi-symmetriser

P_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(t)&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

for the matrix
A(t)
given in (
4.4
).
Then for each
t\in[0,T],
we have the following estimate

\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)=\left(a(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}.

(4.24)

Keeping in mind

P_{\varepsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{*}(t)P_{\varepsilon}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\varepsilon^{2}\\
-\varepsilon^{2}&0\end{array}\right)

(4.25)

for each
t\in[0,T],
we can write

\displaystyle i\left(\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{*}(t)P_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

(4.26)

\displaystyle=i\left(\varepsilon^{2}\overline{V_{1}(t,\xi)}V_{2}(t,\xi)-\varepsilon^{2}V_{1}(t,\xi)\overline{V_{2}(t,\xi)}\right)

\displaystyle=-2i^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\operatorname{Im}\left(V_{1}(t,\xi)\overline{V_{2}(t,\xi)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}|V_{1}(t,\xi)||V_{2}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\varepsilon^{2}\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(\left(a(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right).

Following the techniques given in
[
20
, Theorem 6]
, we can obtain the following estimate:

\left|i\left(\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)Q(t)-Q^{*}(t)P_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)\right|\leq c_{1}(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi))

(4.27)

for some positive constant
c_{1}.
Now, we define the energy as

E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)).

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)\partial_{t}V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),\partial_{t}V(t,\xi)\right)

(4.28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+i\langle\xi\rangle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)A(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)Q(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle-i\langle\xi\rangle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),A(t)V(t,\xi)\right)-i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),Q(t)V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),F(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+i\langle\xi\rangle\left(\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)A(t)-A^{*}(t)P_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)Q(t)-Q^{*}(t)P_{\varepsilon}(t)\right)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(P_{\varepsilon}F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(P_{\varepsilon}V(t,\xi),F(t,\xi)\right).

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1],
using the equlity (
4.24
), we have

\displaystyle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|a\|_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}+1\right)\left|V(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(a(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon^{2}\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1}\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{2}^{-1}\epsilon^{2}|V(t,\xi)|^{2},

where
c_{2}=\|a\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T])}+1.
Combining (
4.29
) and (
4.30
), we have

c_{2}^{-1}\epsilon^{2}|V(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)\leq c_{2}\left|V(t,\xi)\right|^{2}.

(4.31)

Also, one can observe that

\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)=\left(a(t)+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\left|V_{1}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\geq\left|V_{2}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}.

(4.32)

Since
f\in C\left([0,T];\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
, there exist positive constants
c_{3}
and
A
such that
|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\leq c_{3}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)F(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)V(t,\xi),F(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=V_{2}(t,\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\xi)}+\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\overline{V_{2}(t,\xi)}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Re}\left(V_{2}(t,\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(t,\xi)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)||V_{2}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{3}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V_{2}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle=c_{4}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V_{2}(t,\xi)|,\quad\text{where $c_{4}=2c_{3}.$ }

Without any loss of generality, we also assume that
|V_{2}(t,\xi)|\geq 1.
Then (
4.28
) also can be express as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

(4.33)

\displaystyle+c_{1}\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+c_{4}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}|V_{2}(t,\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+c_{1}\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)+c_{4}e^{-A\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{s}}}\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}{\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}+\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle+c_{1}+c_{4}\right)E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}{\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}+\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle+c_{5}\right)E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),

where
c_{5}=c_{1}+c_{4}.
Again, from
[
21
]
, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have the following estimate

\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\left(\partial_{t}P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}{\left(P_{\varepsilon}(t)V(t,\xi),V(t,\xi)\right)}\mathrm{~d}t\leq C_{0}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}

(4.34)

for some constant
C_{0}>0.
Now, applying Gronwall’s lemma, we get

E_{\epsilon}(t,\xi)\leq e^{(K_{0}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}+\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle+c_{5})T}E_{\epsilon}(0,\xi),

(4.35)

where
K_{0}=C_{0}\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}^{l}([0,T])}^{\frac{1}{l}}.
Let
\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}=\varepsilon\langle\xi\rangle
and
\sigma=1+\frac{l}{2},
then
\varepsilon=\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{-l}{2\sigma}}
and
\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{l}}=\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}.
Thus (
4.35
) become

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq e^{c_{5}T}e^{\frac{K_{00}}{2}(\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}+\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}})}E_{\epsilon}(0,\xi)\leq c_{6}e^{K_{00}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}E_{\epsilon}(0,\xi),

where
K_{00}=2\max\{K_{0},1\}T
and
c_{6}=e^{c_{5}T}.
Consequently, from (
4.31
), we obtain

c_{2}^{-1}\epsilon^{2}|V(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq E_{\epsilon}(t,\xi)\leq c_{6}e^{K_{00}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}E_{\epsilon}(0,\xi)\leq c_{2}c_{6}\left|V(0,\xi)\right|^{2}e^{K_{00}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}},

which also gives us

|V(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq c_{2}^{2}c_{6}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{l}{\sigma}}e^{K_{00}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}\left|V(0,\xi)\right|^{2}

for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in\mathcal{I}.
This implies that

\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(t,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq c_{2}^{2}c_{6}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{l}{\sigma}}e^{K_{00}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}|\widehat{v}(0,\xi)|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}\right).

Let us assume
s<\sigma
. Since the initial Cauchy data
v_{0},v_{1}\in\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, we can conclude that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) has a unique solution
v\in C^{2}\left([0,T];\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
provided

1\leq s<1+\frac{l}{2}.

∎

Now we prove our fourth key result in the scenario when the propagation speed
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
with
0<\alpha<2.

Proof of Theorem
2.4

(Case 4)
.

In this case we assume that
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T])
with
0<\alpha<2,
be such that
a(t)\geq 0.
Here the roots
\pm\sqrt{a(t)}
may coincide and are Hölder continuous of order

. Using the same techniques demonstrated in the proof of Theorem

for

(instead for

∎

5.
Very weak solutions

In this section, we investigate the existence of very weak solutions to the
Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with distributional coefficients. We allow coefficients
a
and
q
to be irregular. As previously mentioned in Section
1
, we possess a concept of very weak solutions for solving problems that may lack a significant solution in the usual distributional sense and discuss the corresponding results for
a,q\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T])
. In this case, using the Friedrichs-mollifier, i.e.,
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\psi\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi=1
, we regularize the distributional coefficient
a
to derive the families of smooth functions
\left(a_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
, namely

a_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left(a*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right)(t),\quad\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}(t)=\frac{1}{\omega(\varepsilon)}\psi\left(\frac{t}{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right),\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1],

where
\omega(\varepsilon)\geq 0
and
\omega(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
K\Subset\mathbb{R}
denote
K
as a compact set in
\mathbb{R}
.

(i)

A net
\left(a_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}\in L_{m}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{(0,1]}
is said to be
L_{m}^{\infty}
-moderate if for all
K\Subset\mathbb{R}
, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
C_{K}>0
such that

\left\|\partial^{k}a_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C_{K}\varepsilon^{-N-k},\quad\text{for }k=0,1,\ldots,m,

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

(ii)

A net
\left(a_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}\in L_{m}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})^{(0,1]}
is said to be
L_{m}^{\infty}
-negligible if for all
K\Subset\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists
C_{K}>0
such that

\left\|\partial^{k}a_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C_{K}\varepsilon^{q},\quad\text{ for }k=0,1,\ldots,m,

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

(iii)

A net
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)^{(0,1]}
is said to be
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-moderate if there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
C>0
such that

\left\|v_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];H_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}\leq C\varepsilon^{-N}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

(iv)

A net
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)^{(0,1]}
is said to be
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-negligible if for all
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists
C>0
such that

\left\|v_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];H_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}\leq C\varepsilon^{q}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

It is important to emphasize that the conditions of moderateness are natural in the sense that regularizations of distributions are moderate. Furthermore, by the structure theorems for distributions, we have

\text{ compactly supported distributions }\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\subset\left\{L^{2}\text{-moderate families}\right\}.

Therefore, it may be possible that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) does not have a solution in compactly supported distributions
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. To overcome the difficulty described above, we introduce the concept of a very weak solution for the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) in the following manner:

Definition 5.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},
and
\left(v_{0},v_{1}\right)\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
. The net
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)^{(0,1]}
is a very weak solution of order
s
to the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) if there exist

(1)

L_{1}^{\infty}
-moderate regularisation
a_{\varepsilon}
of the coefficient
a
,

(2)

L^{\infty}
-moderate regularisation
q_{\varepsilon}
of the coefficient
q
,

(3)

L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-moderate regularisation
f_{\varepsilon}
of the source term
f
,

such that
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
solves the regularised problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)
-moderate.

We say that a distribution
a
is positive if
\langle a,\psi\rangle\geq 0
whenever
\psi\in

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\psi\geq 0
. Also, a distribution
a
is strictly positive if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that
a-\alpha
is a positive distribution. Now, the existence theorem for the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) with distributional coefficients can now be formulated as follows:

Theorem 5.3
.

(Existence)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let the coefficients
a
and
q
be positive distributions with supports contained in
[0,T]
such that
a\geq a_{0}>0
for some constant
a_{0}
and the initial Cauchy data
\left(v_{0},v_{1}\right)\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
. Let
f(\cdot,x)
be a distribution whose support is in
[0,T]
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) has a very weak solution of order
s
.

Moreover, one can understood the uniqueness of the very weak solution in the sense that if there is negligible modification in the approximations of the coefficients
a,q
, then it has negligible effect on the family of very weak solutions.

Definition 5.4
.

We say that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) has a
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)
-unique very weak solution, if

(1)

for all
L_{1}^{\infty}
-moderate nets
a_{\varepsilon},\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\left(a_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is
L_{1}^{\infty}
-negligible,

(2)

for all
L^{\infty}
-moderate nets
q_{\varepsilon},\tilde{q}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\left(q_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{q}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{\infty}
-negligible; and

(3)

or all
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-moderate nets
f_{\varepsilon},\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\left(f_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-negligible,

the net
\left(v_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)
-negligible, where
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
and
\left(\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
are the families of solutions corresponding to the
\varepsilon
-parametrised problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+\tilde{q}_{\varepsilon}(t)\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

respectively.

The next theorem establishes the uniqueness of the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (

) in the sense of Definition

.

Theorem 5.5
.

(Uniqueness)

Assume that
a,q
be positive distributions with supports contained in

such that

for some constant

and the initial Cauchy data

for some

. Let

be a distribution whose support is in

for all

) is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)
-unique.

We now provide the consistency result, which indicates that the weak solutions in Theorem

are able to capture the classical solutions provided by Theorem

Theorem 5.6
.

(Consistency)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

. Assume that

be such that

, and
q\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
be a positive distribution, and the initial Cauchy data
\left(v_{0},v_{1}\right)\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}

v
be a very weak solution of (

). Then for any regularising families

in Definition

, the very weak solution

We start by proving the existence of the very weak solution in the sense of Definition
5.2
.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

For a fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, consider the following regularised Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}v_{\varepsilon}(t,k)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}(0,k)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

Considering
\mathcal{H}
-Fourier transform with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and using the similar transformation as in Theorem
2.1
, we see that
(
5.4
) is equivalent to the following first order system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)=i\langle\xi\rangle A_{\varepsilon}(t)V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+i\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}Q_{\varepsilon}(t)V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+F_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),\quad(t,\xi)\in(0,T]\times\mathcal{I},\\
V_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)=V_{0}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathcal{I},\end{array}\right.

(5.5)

where

V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i\langle\xi\rangle\widehat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\\
\partial_{t}\widehat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\end{array}\right),\quad V_{0}(\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}i\langle\xi\rangle\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi)\\
\widehat{v}_{1}(\xi)\end{array}\right),

(5.6)

and

A_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
a_{\varepsilon}(t)&0\end{array}\right),~~Q_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
q_{\varepsilon}(t)-a_{\varepsilon}(t)&0\end{array}\right),F_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\widehat{f}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\end{array}\right).

(5.7)

Note that the matrix
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
has eigenvalues
\pm\sqrt{a_{\varepsilon}(t)}
and its symmetriser
S_{\varepsilon}
is given by

S_{\varepsilon}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(t)&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

(5.8)

i.e.,

S_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}-A_{\varepsilon}^{*}S_{\varepsilon}=0.

Also, for each
t\in[0,T]
, the matrix
S_{\varepsilon}(t)
is positive and

\|S_{\varepsilon}(t)\|\leq(1+|a_{\varepsilon}(t)|).

(5.9)

Again, from the definition of
S_{\varepsilon}
and
Q_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\partial_{t}S_{\varepsilon}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{t}a_{\varepsilon}(t)&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\left(S_{\varepsilon}Q_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\varepsilon}^{*}S\right)(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&a_{\varepsilon}(t)-q_{\varepsilon}(t)\\
q_{\varepsilon}(t)-a_{\varepsilon}(t)&0\end{array}\right).

Therefore

\left\|\partial_{t}S_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\|\leq\left|\partial_{t}a_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|~~\text{and}~~\left\|\left(S_{\varepsilon}Q_{\varepsilon}-Q_{\varepsilon}^{*}S_{\varepsilon}\right)(t)\right\|\leq|q_{\varepsilon}(t)|+|a_{\varepsilon}(t)|,\quad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T].

(5.10)

Considering the energy equation

E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi):=(S_{\varepsilon}(t)V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi))

and following the techniques used in (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we obtain

\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{a_{\varepsilon}(t),1\right\}\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{a_{\varepsilon}(t),1\right\}\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}.

(5.11)

Since
\psi\in

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\psi\geq 0,\operatorname{supp}(\psi)\subseteq K
and
a,q
are positive distributions with compact supports contained in
[0,T]
, throughout the section, we can assume that
K=[0,T]
. By the structure theorem for compactly supported distributions, there exist
L_{1},L_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\left|\partial_{t}^{k}a_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\leq c_{1}\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}-k}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left|\partial_{t}^{k}q_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\leq c_{2}\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{2}-k}

(5.12)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover, we can write

\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(a*\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}\right)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t-\tau)\psi_{\omega(\varepsilon)}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(t-\omega(\varepsilon)\tau)\psi(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{K}}a(t-\omega(\varepsilon)\tau)\psi(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\geq a_{0}\int_{\mathrm{K}}\psi(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau:=\tilde{a}_{0}>0,\quad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T],

where the positive constant
\tilde{a}_{0}
is independent of
\varepsilon
. Now, combining (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we get

c_{00}\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\leq\left(1+c_{1}\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}\right)\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2},

(5.14)

where
c_{00}
is a positive constant. Further, using the estimates (
5.9
), (
5.10
), and (
5.12
), we obtain

\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\leq c_{0}\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}-1}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{2}}\right)E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+c^{\prime}\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}\right)|F_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}

(5.15)

for some constants
c_{0},c^{\prime}>0.
Applying the Gronwall’s lemma to (
5.15
), we can conclude that

E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\leq e^{c_{0}T\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{2}}\right)}\left(E_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)+c^{\prime}\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

(5.16)

Thus, combining (
5.14
) and (
5.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle c_{00}\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq e^{c_{0}T\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}-1}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{2}}\right)}\left(\left(1+c_{1}\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}\right)\left|V_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+c^{\prime}\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime\prime}e^{c_{0}T\left(1+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}-1}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}+\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{2}}\right)}\times e^{\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L_{1}}}\left(\left|V_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime\prime}e^{c_{0}T}e^{C\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L}}\left(\left|V_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right),

where
c^{\prime\prime}=\max\{1,c_{1},c^{\prime}\}
,
C=4\max\{c_{0}T,1\}
and
L=\max\{L_{1}+1,L_{2}\}
. This gives us

\left|V_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\leq c^{\prime\prime}e^{c_{0}T}c_{00}^{-1}e^{C\omega(\varepsilon)^{-L}}\left(\left|V_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left|F_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

Letting
\omega(\varepsilon)\sim(\log(\varepsilon^{\frac{-L}{C}}))^{\frac{-1}{L}}
and using the transformation considered in (
5.6
), we get

\langle\xi\rangle^{2}\left|\widehat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widehat{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\right|^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L}\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{2}\left|\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi)\right|^{2}+\left|\widehat{v}_{1}(\xi)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\left|\widehat{f}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)\right|^{2}\mathrm{~d}\tau\right).

(5.17)

Now multiply (
5.17
) by
\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}
, and then by applying Plancherel’s formula, we conclude that

\|v_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}{v_{\varepsilon}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L}\left(\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}+\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}\right),

(5.18)

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since
\left(f_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is a
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
moderate regularisation of
f
, thus we can find a constant
L_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L_{0}}.

(5.19)

Now, integrating the estimate (
5.18
) with respect to
t\in[0,T]
variable and combining it with (
5.19
), we deduce that

\left\|v_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L-L_{0}}\text{ and }\left\|\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L-L_{0}-1}.

This implies that
v_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)
-moderate and this completes the proof of the theorem.

\|w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L}\left\|h_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}

L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
-negligible, we get

\left\|w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-L}\varepsilon^{L+q}=\varepsilon^{q},

for all
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in[0,T]
. Taking integration with respect to the
t\in[0,T]
variable, we obtain

\left\|w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];H_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];H_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{q},\quad\text{ for all }q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

with the constant independent of
t
. This implies that
w_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
is
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}_{\hbar,V}}^{1+s}\right)
-negligible and this completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

In the following result we show that very weak solutions are consistent.

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

Let
\tilde{v}
represents the classical solution in Theorem
2.1
. By definition of the classic solution and very weak solution, we know that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{v}(t,x)+a(t)\mathcal{H}\tilde{v}(t,x)+q(t)\tilde{v}(t,x)=f(t,x),(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\tilde{v}(0,k)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(5.31)

and there exists a net
\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)v_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x),(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v_{\varepsilon}(0,k)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(5.32)

We also can rewrite (
5.31
) as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{v}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}\tilde{v}(t,x)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)\tilde{v}(t,x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+h_{\varepsilon}(t,x),(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\tilde{v}(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(0,x)=v_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(5.33)

where

h_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\left(a_{\varepsilon}-a\right)(t)\mathcal{H}\tilde{v}(t,x)+\left(q_{\varepsilon}-q\right)(t)\tilde{v}(t,x)+\left(f-f_{\varepsilon}\right)(t,x).

For
a\in L_{1}^{\infty}([0,T])
,
b\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
, and
f\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
,
since the nets
\left(a_{\varepsilon}-a\right)_{\varepsilon}
,
\left(q_{\varepsilon}-q\right)_{\varepsilon}
, and
\left(f_{\varepsilon}-f\right)_{\varepsilon}
are converging to 0 as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, then
h_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
and
h_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. From (
5.32
) and (
5.33
), we get that
w_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\left(\tilde{v}-v_{\varepsilon}\right)(t,x)
solves the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+a_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathcal{H}w_{\varepsilon}(t,x)+q_{\varepsilon}(t)w_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=h_{\varepsilon}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
w_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(5.34)

Now, one can obtain the following energy estimate using the similar techniques as in the proof of the Theorem
2.1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left|\partial_{t}a_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|+|q_{\varepsilon}(t)|+|a_{\varepsilon}(t)|+1+\left|a_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\right)|W_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}+(1+\left|a_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|)|H_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}

(5.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+|H_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|^{2}),

5.35
) and using the

), we can conclude that

Now, applying Plancherel’s formula and using the fact that
W_{\varepsilon}(0,\xi)\equiv 0
for all

\left\|w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1+s}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|h_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\right)}^{2}

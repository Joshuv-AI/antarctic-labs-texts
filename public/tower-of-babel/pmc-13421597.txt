WTCFNet for industrial defect detection using wavelet transform and cross layer feature fusion
Authors: Hao Chen, Yu-Bo Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAt present, the detection of industrial surface defects often faces challenges such as difficult identification of complex background defects, weak semantic information contained in tiny defects and significant changes of defect objects, which are related to product quality and people’s life safety. To solve the above problems, this paper proposes a novel industrial defect detection method (WTCF-Net). Firstly, to prevent the loss of semantic information in the process of layer-by-layer transmission of feature information, we propose a Wavelet Feature Convolution block (WFC), which decomposes the image into low-frequency and high-frequency components of different scales through wavelet transform to enhance the representation of defect features and suppress redundant information. Secondly, to improve defect feature recognition under complex background, we design an Interactive Residual Module (IRM) to aggregate feature information of different dimensions. At the same time, the Interactive Residual Feature extractor (IRF) is used to capture cross-dimensional information interaction and establish the dependency relationship between different dimensions, so that the network can capture more subtle complex features. Then, to improve the detection of multi-scale defects in industrial products, this paper proposes a Cross-level Feature Aggregation Network (CFA-Net), and designs a three-layer gradually decreasing feature fusion path to enhance the interaction between different scales through the fusion of adjacent layers and cross-layers. In addition, the Feature Enhancement Module (FEM) is designed. By aggregating the features of adjacent levels, cross-level semantic association is established, thus improving the feature extraction ability of the model for multi-scale defects. At the same time, Collaborative Filtering Module (CFM) is used to filter redundant information after fusion, which enriches feature representation. Finally, we design the loss function of defect regression intersection ratio (CDIou), and improves the accuracy of defect detection by calculating the loss of width and height product. The validity and generalization of the model is tested on the data sets of NEU-DET, PCB and DeepPCB. Compared with the baseline model, the mAP value of the model is increased by 5.9%, 1.3% and 1.4%. The detection speed is 53 FPS, which has a wide application potential.Keywords: Defect detection, Wavelet convolution, Interactive residual module, Cross-layer feature fusion, Defect regression lossSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The advancement of industrial automation in China has led to substantial improvements in manufacturing technologies, necessitating stricter quality standards for industrial outputs and accelerating the transition from conventional production to intelligent quality assurance systems. During manufacturing processes, various surface defects—such as cracks, embedded particles, and abrasions—often occur due to factors including material inconsistencies, equipment tolerances, and environmental conditions. These flaws not only undermine the structural integrity and service life of manufactured components but may also introduce significant operational risks. Therefore, the implementation of accurate defect detection systems1 has become an essential element of modern manufacturing workflows to ensure product reliability and mitigate potential safety issues.

Traditional industrial surface defect detection relies heavily on manual inspection, which involves a substantial amount of repetitive tasks. This approach is prone to visual fatigue and human misjudgment, often leading to missed detections or false diagnoses. Moreover, it entails significant labor costs over time and is susceptible to variability due to multiple influencing factors.

Contemporary research in computer vision has shown that deep learning approaches2 can effectively perform feature extraction and defect identification in an integrated pipeline for surface quality assessment, which can play an active role in solving many practical problems in industrial scenarios and substantially improve the detection performance of industrial products. For example, Xu et al.3 constructed a defect detection network for detecting deformation defects in a given image. Wang et al.4 proposed a network for surface defect detection on steel with few samples, which employs both two-domain generalisation strategies and a noise regularisation strategy to make its network performance comparable to that of a model trained with a large amount of data. Luo et al.5 focused on printed circuit board surface defect detection, proposing a two-stage target detection framework. Wang et al.6 proposed a defect detection method that suppresses the effects of complex backgrounds and noisy pixels on the defect detection accuracy and demonstrates the effectiveness of their method on five defect datasets. Wang et al.7 proposed a high-precision steel surface defect detection method, building a jump-layer connection module for steel surface defects. Tian et al.8 proposed a steel surface defect detection method that uses an anchorless frame design, locates the centre point using key point estimation. Li et al.9 proposed to improve the photovoltaic defect detection of YOLOv8, construct a fast down-sampling convolution block, and add channel attention to the detection head to enhance its performance in complex background. Liu et al.10 proposed a defect detection network for power generation blades, constructed a cross-layer connection fusion mechanism based on attention, and strengthened the ability to pay attention to defective areas. Guder et al.11 used EfficientNetv2 and mixed attention to detect fabric defects, and comprehensively evaluated the detection performance of different models on industrial fabrics. In the defect detection process of industrial products, researchers have used deep learning technology to innovate in multiple aspects such as feature fusion as well as feature extraction.

However, at present, industrial surface defect detection still faces multiple challenges. For example, (1) it is difficult to detect defects in complex background. Usually, the background texture of defect images is complex and noise interference is obvious, which leads to low contrast between defects and background and poor signal-to-noise ratio. (2) The difficulty of small target detection lies in the small proportion of target pixels and weak appearance characteristics, which makes it difficult to effectively separate them from complex background, and it is easy to lose details and spatial information in the process of deep network sampling. Although the existing methods try to alleviate the above problems by enhancing the feature pyramid or introducing attention mechanism, there are still some disadvantages such as over-reliance on a large number of data enhancement and complex structure, high calculation cost and limited generalization ability. (3) Because of the different scales and shapes of industrial defects, it is necessary to effectively integrate the characteristics of various defects to adapt to various objectives. However, the current methods rely on the aggregation of adjacent features, and pay less attention to the information exchange between non-adjacent layers.

This research proposes an original methodology specifically designed to address the limitations of current industrial flaw detection systems. First, to mitigate the problem of semantic information loss and feature redundancy caused by sequential feature propagation, a wavelet feature convolution block is introduced. This component employs adaptive feature filtering to reduce redundant information while enhancing the model’s ability to identify and retain semantically meaningful features through dynamic channel-wise recalibration. Furthermore, an Interactive Residual Feature Extractor (IRF) combined with an Interactive Residual Module (IRM) is proposed to improve the model’s feature representation capacity. The IRF enhances feature extraction by learning multi-scale features and aggregating information across different dimensions. The IRM enables the network to capture more subtle and complex features, thereby reducing fine-grained feature loss and improving recognition of defects in complex backgrounds. Additionally, a Cross-Layer Feature Aggregation Network (CFA-Net) is introduced to strengthen multi-scale interactions through cross-layer aggregation. Finally, to improve the detection of small industrial defects, a novel defect regression loss function named CDIoU is designed. By incorporating width-height product loss calculation, CDIoU enhances detection accuracy for tiny defects.

The primary contributions of the proposed approach are summarized as:

(1) To prevent the loss of semantic information in the process of layer-by-layer transmission of feature information, we propose a WFC module, which decomposes the image into low-frequency and high-frequency components of different scales through wavelet transform, extracts refined defect features by deep convolution and point-by-point convolution, enhances the representation of defect features and suppresses redundant information, and then exchanges local feature information on different channels by channel shuffling operation to enhance the nonlinear expression ability of convolution.

(2) To improve the recognition of defect features in complex background and the conflict between characteristics, this paper designs Interactive Residual Module (IRM), which uses multi-branch structure to realize the aggregation of feature information in different dimensions and enhance the feature extraction ability of images. In addition, Interactive Residual VFeature extractor (IRF) is used to capture cross-dimensional information interaction, and the dependency relationship between different dimensions is established, so that the network can capture more subtle and complex features and improve the recognition of complex background defects.

(3) To improve the detection of multi-scale defects in industrial products, this paper proposes a cross-layer perceptual fusion network (CPF-Net), which firstly, designs a three-layer gradually decreasing feature fusion path, and adopts partial convolution (PConv) instead of the standard convolution kernel to reduce the number of model parameters; and secondly, compensates for the loss of fine-grained and semantic information during the gradual refinement of the features by fusing the features from the neighbouring layers and cross-layers, and improves the recognition of different scale targets. Enhances multi-scale feature representation while progressively refining target recognition accuracy.

(4) Aiming at the problem of semantic differences between multi-scale features, this paper designs FEM module, which can effectively establish cross-level semantic association by aggregating feature representations of adjacent levels, thus improving the feature extraction ability of the model for multi-scale defects.At the same time, Collaborative Filtering Module (CFM) is used to filter redundant information and enrich feature representation.

(5) To enhance the detection accuracy of small industrial targets, we propose a novel defect regression loss function called CDIoU, which incorporates a cross-parallel ratio mechanism. By integrating a width-height product loss term into the optimization process, the proposed method significantly improves the localization precision of small defects.

Related work

Industrial defect detection methods

Industrial defect detection methods12 are primarily categorized into traditional approaches and deep learning-based techniques. Conventional methods rely on handcrafted feature extraction, such as edge detection13 and texture analysis14. However, these approaches tend to be highly sensitive to imaging conditions, lack robustness, and are susceptible to subjective bias. The advancement of deep learning15 has revolutionized object detection by enabling automatic learning of hierarchical feature representations, which significantly improves both accuracy and robustness. Current deep learning-based detection methods can be broadly classified into single-stage and two-stage frameworks.

Single-stage detectors, such as YOLO16 and SSD17, directly predict target categories and locations within the network, offering faster processing speeds but relatively lower accuracy, particularly for small target identification. The recent advancements in deep learning have spurred significant research into defect detection methodologies18. Notable contributions include Li et al.19 enhanced approach for steel defect identification, demonstrating the field’s progress, designing heterogeneous attention into the backbone network and expanding the model sensing field to improve the deep network sensing ability. Li et al.20 designed a new fusion network using different scale features to connect. Song et al.21 proposed a defect detection algorithm to improve YOLOv8. They used a two-way pyramid network to achieve redistribution of weighted features and introduced channel attention to focus on defective feature identification. To enhance global feature learning, Chen et al.22 implemented a Transformer-based feature extraction network in their PCB defect detection framework.

Two-stage detection methods employ a region proposal mechanism followed by target classification and regression, achieving higher accuracy at the cost of increased computational complexity and slower training speeds, as demonstrated by models like Fast R-CNN23 and Mask R-CNN24. In recent years, researchers have optimized and improved the existing two-stage detection methods in many aspects. Wu et al.25 proposed an improved RCNN method for cotter pin defect detection, designed an efficient feature extraction module by using extended convolution, and enhanced multi-class defect feature extraction. Zou et al.26 proposed an improved two-stage defect detection network, which used multi-scale convolution to improve the ResNet backbone network and added channel attention to pay attention to the location of defect channels, which improved the detection accuracy but brought a huge increase in parameters. Leng et al.27 proposed the steel defect detection network, and added a feature fusion module and a lightweight channel attention mechanism between the feature pyramid network and the regional suggestion network, which significantly improved the model’s ability to extract and perceive subtle features.

The end-to-end trainable nature of deep learning enables seamless integration of feature extraction and defect detection, delivering robust performance in multi-scale, multi-class defect identification for industrial applications. Unlike existing approaches, this study proposes a novel industrial defect detection framework that incorporates wavelet convolutional bootstrapping. Specifically, wavelet feature convolution blocks are designed to enhance defect feature representation while suppressing redundant information. Secondly, to prevent limitations in feature extraction, an Interactive Residual Feature Extractor (IRF) and an Interactive Residual Module (IRM) are introduced to reduce the loss of fine-grained features and improve the recognition of complex background defects. Furthermore, a Cross-Layer Feature Aggregation network (CFA-Net) is proposed, which employs cross-layer fusion to strengthen interactions across different feature scales. This method facilitates the simultaneous incorporation of local image details and global contextual information, significantly enhancing detection performance across targets of varying sizes.

Multi-scale feature fusion

Multi-scale feature fusion28 is a method to enhance model representation by fusing features of different levels or resolutions.By strategically combining high-resolution spatial details from early layers with rich semantic abstractions from deeper layers, the method achieves comprehensive feature representation. Methods such as Feature Pyramid Networks (FPNs) or cross-layer connectivity are commonly employed to fuse high-resolution, low-semantic features with low-resolution, high-semantic features in spatial or channel dimensions29. The advantage of multiscale feature fusion techniques in target detection is that they do not rely on a single level of feature maps, but the approach integrates multi-level feature maps through fusion operations, subsequently performing object detection on the combined feature representation.Examples of such models are FPN30, PANet31 and BiFPN32, although the above networks have achieved significant results in multi-scale feature fusion.But current methods inadequately combine hierarchical features from the backbone network, leading to significant information loss during feature propagation.

To address these challenges, the proposed cross-level perceptual fusion framework addresses these limitations by combining adjacent layer features to restore fine details and strengthen scale-spanning feature relationships, thereby elevating multi-scale detection accuracy.

Methodology

Overall network architecture

As illustrated in Fig. 1, WTCF-Net incorporates two key components in its backbone: a Wavelet Feature Convolution (WFC) block and an Interactive Residual Module (IRM). This design simultaneously strengthens defect feature extraction while mitigating semantic information loss in deeper network layers. Secondly, the Cross-layer Perceptual Fusion Network(CPF-Net) improves the recognition of targets at different scales by fusing neighbouring layers to compensate for the fine-grained information lost during the gradual feature refinement. The features fused by CPF-Net network pass through FEM module, and the feature representation of adjacent levels is aggregated to improve the feature extraction ability of the model for multi-scale defects. Finally, the defect detection results are output by three layers with different sizes of detection heads, while the defect regression intersection* and integration ratio loss function DRIoU are designed to optimise the performance of the model in predicting the defect boundaries for small target detection.

Wavelet feature convolution block

With the increasing depth of the network model, although it can enhance the model’s ability to represent features, it leads to the problem of losing semantic information. This is due to the fact that in deep networks, defective features are gradually attenuated or distorted during the layer-by-layer transfer process, resulting in the network’s difficulty in capturing and retaining important semantic features in the input data, which not only affects the final performance of the model but also may lead to problems such as overfitting.

Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes a Wavelet Feature Convolution block (WFC), as shown in Fig. 2, and integrates it into the feature extraction network to enhance the defective feature representation and optimise the problem of missing semantic information. Firstly, the number of channels is reduced by 1×1 convolution to preserve potential connections between channels as much as possible; secondly, two branches, wavelet transform convolution33 and partial convolution34, are carried out respectively. Through wavelet transforms, this branch decomposes input images into frequency components at varying scales, improving model discriminability by simultaneously encoding wide-range context and fine-grained background characteristics. Some of the channels are convolved using PConv to reduce the amount of model computation, and refined defect features are extracted using deep convolution. Finally, using the channel blending operation, the feature information on different channels of wavelet transform convolution and partial convolution is uniformly blended by mapping the features generated by partial convolution to each part of the features generated by wavelet transform, and exchanging the local feature information uniformly on different channels, so as to make the texture information of the feature maps it outputs more prominent. Interactive residual module

To improve the recognition of defect features in complex background, an Interactive Residual Module (IRM) is designed in this paper. As shown in Fig. 3, the multi-branch structure is used to realize the aggregation of feature information in different dimensions and enhance the feature extraction ability of images. The IRM module uses Interactive Residual Feature extractor (IRF) to capture finer and more complex features, and then learns more complex feature representation through interactive feature feed-forward network (IFFN), thus enhancing the model’s understanding of defect features. Among them, the left branch of IFFN extracts local features through 1×1 convolution compression channel and 3×3 depth separable convolution, which enhances the recognition of complex background defects, and the right branch adjusts the number of channels through convolution block. Finally, the outputs of the two branches are fused, and the residual connection is established to strengthen the interaction of features.

IRF module structure, which consists of a double-branch structure.

While feature fusion networks effectively capture multi-scale representations and enhance model expressiveness, their exclusive focus on channel-wise operations neglects spatial dimension information, constraining feature extraction capacity. To address this limitation, we introduce the IRF module incorporating cross-dimensional attention mechanisms. This innovative design simultaneously models channel-spatial interactions and establishes inter-dimensional dependencies, thereby facilitating richer feature propagation and aggregation while significantly strengthening feature representation power.

Specifically, the IRF module achieves efficient feature enhancement through a multi-branch feature reweighting mechanism. The module first processes the input features through CBS (Conv layer, BN batch normalisation layer, Silu activation function) and WFC, which improves texture detail while mitigating semantic information degradation. Subsequently, a dual-path spatial attention mechanism is employed to perform adaptive mean pooling in the height and width dimensions, respectively, and then feature reweighting is achieved by combining the Sigmoid activation function and group normalisation. Subsequently, a channel attention branch is introduced to generate channel weights through adaptive mean pooling and Softmax, which are co-optimised with spatial attention and residual joins are used to preserve the original information. Cross-layer perceptual fusion networks

Figure 4 shows several common feature fusion networks, but each of them has some problems, for example, FPN is easy to lose detailed information when fusing features; PANet increases the bottom-up feature transfer path, but the number of parameters is large, which leads to lower efficiency; While PANet enhances feature propagation through additional bottom-up pathways, its expanded parameter count results in reduced computational efficiency. Although BiFPN utilizes skip connections and weighted feature fusion to combine multi-scale features, it still falls short in effectively incorporating spatial information from adjacent backbone layers and underutilizes the closely related features extracted from neighboring levels. In its cross-layer integration process, BiFPN primarily focuses on intermediate layers, neglecting the complementary potential of combining the shallowest and deepest features. Moreover, the high computational complexity of the structure often leads to challenges during training. In contrast, the network proposed in this paper emphasizes the fusion of adjacent-layer features within the backbone. Since adjacent layers exhibit small semantic gaps, this approach reduces semantic conflict by integrating each pair of neighboring features. Furthermore, considering that shallow features preserve more fine-grained details while deeper layers carry richer semantic information, a cross-layer connection is introduced between the shallowest and deepest levels to enhance the complementarity between detail and semantics, thereby providing more informative feature representations for detection. Additionally, a progressively reduced three-layer architecture is designed to significantly lower computational cost without compromising performance.

Comparison of different converged networks.

To address the above problems, this paper proposes a cross-layer perceptual fusion network (CPF-Net), designing a three-layer layer-by-layer decreasing fusion network, adopting partial convolution (PConv) instead of standard convolution kernel, and reducing computational redundancy through the layer-by-layer decreasing structure; for the problem of weakly coupled cross-layer feature interactions, using the adjacent layer-by-layer decreasing structure, we use the neighbouring layer-by-layer decreasing structure to reduce computational redundancy. For the problem of weak coupling of cross-layer feature interaction, the fine-grained information compensation characteristic of neighbouring layer-level feature mapping is used to achieve hierarchical orthogonal fusion of shallow high-resolution detail features and deep semantic features. Meanwhile, for the neglect of cross-layer feature interaction in the fusion process, CPF-Net introduces cross-layer fusion to enhance the interaction between features of different scales. By jointly considering fine-grained local features and comprehensive global context, the method significantly improves detection accuracy across varying target scales.

Feature enhancement module

To address the semantic gap and compatibility issues that arise from fusing multi-scale features directly, which can result in noise and diminished model performance, this paper introduces a Feature Enhancement Module (FEM), as shown in Figs. 5 and 6. The Multi-branch Fusion Module (MFM) of Fig. 5 facilitates effective cross-level semantic interaction among multi-scale features. It begins by using 1x1 convolutions to reduce channel dimensions and unify the sizes of features Q1, Q2, and Q3. Subsequently, these features undergo 3x3 point-by-point convolutions followed by 1x1 convolutions to refine them. Weight maps are then generated through a Softmax function and are element-wise multiplied with the original feature maps to emphasize significant features. The weighted feature maps are concatenated, and the final enhanced feature map M is produced via a convolutional layer. This approach efficiently merges features across scales and bolsters the model’s capacity to detect critical features. Can be expressed as:

To coordinate convolution operations of different scales, effectively filter redundant features and suppress conflicting information, the feature map M obtained by MFM module is filtered by Collaborative Filtering Module (CFM) to improve the ability of capturing defect features of the model. As shown in Fig. 6, CFM first captures defect features from different angles by multi-branch convolution, and fuses features in parallel convolution operations to fuse feature information in different directions and dimensions. Then, it uses deep convolution to further mine the deep information of features and extract more abundant features, and finally fuses the extracted multi-branch features.

CDIoU loss function

While fundamental in general object detection35, regression loss functions prove equally vital when applied to industrial product defect identification scenarios. Currently, the CIoU36 loss is widely used in target detection models, but the aspect ratio in the CIoU loss is a relative value, which does not reflect the real value or the real relationship between the real frame and the predicted frame.Uncertainties in aspect ratio alignment between predicted and actual frames adversely affect the model’s regression accuracy, especially for minute defects.

In response to the above problems, this paper proposes a defect regression cross-index ratio loss function (CDIoU loss), CDIoU loss improves the detection of small target defects by calculating the product loss of width and height.

Ablation studies of different modules

To comprehensively assess WTCF-Net’s performance, we performed systematic ablation studies on its constituent modules and network components, with quantitative results presented in Table 1. The data demonstrate, adding the wavelet feature convolution block (WFC) to the backbone network improves the R-value by 1.1%, mAP value improves by 1.0%. This is due to the fact that the WFC enhances the defective feature representation suppresses the redundant information, and is able to filter the background noise efficiently, which improves the model’s ability to detect the defects. Secondly, by adding the interactive residual module (IRM) designed in this paper, the R-value improves by 1.7%, mAP value improves by 2.5% and reduces the number of model parameters, which is due to the design of the IRM module that enables the network to capture more subtle and complex features, reduces the loss of fine-grained features, and improves the identification of defects in complex backgrounds. Using the cross-layer perceptual fusion network (CPF-Net) in this paper, the R-value is improved by 3.9%, mAP value improves by 3.7%. This is due to the fact that the CPF-Net compensates for the fine-grained and semantic information lost during the gradual refinement of the features by the fusion of neighbouring and cross-layer features to improve the recognition of targets at different scales. Adding FEM module to establish different levels of semantic information interaction and enhance feature representation, the mAP value is increased by 1.4%. The CDIoU metric is specifically engineered to enhance defect area localization and measurement precision, consequently boosting detection accuracy for microscopic defects.

Comparative tests

Table 5 shows the comparison between this paper’s model WTCF-Net and the SOTA model, WTCF-Net obtains the highest R and F1 values, the mAP value is slightly lower than that of the Faster RCNN39 with high computational complexity, and it is much faster than the Faster RCNN model in terms of inference speed. The high performance of Faster R-CNN benefits from the efficient generation of candidate frames by Regional Proposal Network (RPN), which ensures that no defects are left out. Subsequently, the RoI pool is used to align features, and the classification and regression network are used for fine identification and location. This “rough selection+fine repair” mechanism is particularly accurate for defect detection of small targets and complex backgrounds. Compared with the models YOLOv5s, YOLOv7-tiny40, PP-YOLOE41 and YOLOv10s42, which have not much difference in the number of parameters, the R-value is improved by 5.6%, 7.4%, 1.4%, 4.6%, and the mAP value is improved by 6.2%, 8.2%, 1.6%, and 5.6%, respectively. In the comparison of YOLO series models, the industrial detector PP-YOLOE has achieved excellent detection performance, with a mAP value of 93.1%. This is because it uses a more powerful CSPRepResNet backbone network to enhance the feature extraction ability, provide richer feature representation for subsequent target detection, and help to improve the detection accuracy of different types and scales of targets. However, the mAP values of SSD and Retinanet models are only 85.6% and 86.2%, and the predefined anchoring mechanism limits their ability to fully cover small objects and large aspect ratio objects, resulting in poor performance in detecting multi-scale defects. The latest YOLOv12s model introduces the dynamic weight allocation strategy, which enhances the feature extraction ability of complex scenes and small targets, and its performance is higher than the previous version. This model WGCF-Net has the highest detection accuracy on the steel defect dataset, using wavelet convolution block to enhance the defect feature representation, and can effectively filter the background noise, so as to improve the model’s ability to detect defects. Discussion

This paper introduces a novel defect detection model named WTCF-Net, tailored to address the challenges of identifying defects against complex backgrounds, the limited semantic information in small defects, and the variability of defect objects in industrial settings. To validate the model’s effectiveness, extensive ablation and comparative experiments were conducted on the NEU-DET dataset, demonstrating WTCF-Net’s superiority over existing detectors. Further experiments on the PCB and DeepPCB datasets confirm the model’s generalization capabilities. Additional ablation studies substantiate the efficacy of the various modules introduced in this paper and highlight the model’s robustness. Overall, the proposed model offers a significant advantage in terms of both detection accuracy and speed, which is crucial for industrial product defect detection.

Conclusion

This research introduces WTCF-Net, an advanced neural network architecture specifically designed to address critical challenges in industrial defect detection.Firstly, this paper effectively solves the problem of semantic loss and redundancy in the feature transfer process by designing a wavelet feature convolution block, which significantly enhances the defect feature representation and suppresses the background noise interference. Secondly, the IRF and the IRM module are used to improve the distribution of spatial semantic features through multi-scale feature learning and dimensionality aggregation, which enhances the capability of capturing defective features under complex backgrounds, thus enhancing the feature extraction capability of industrial defects. In addition, to address the critical challenge of multi-scale defect identification in industrial manufacturing, we develop CFA-Net , which strengthens the interaction between different scale features through a three-layer progressive fusion path, and significantly improves the recognition of multi-scale defects. At the same time, the FEM module is used to aggregate the features of adjacent levels and establish cross-level semantic association, which improves the ability of the model to extract multi-scale defects. Finally, this paper proposes the defect regression cross-combination ratio loss function (CDIou), which improves the detection accuracy of small defects significantly by optimising the calculation of the wide-height product loss.

In the future, we will explore more lightweight models and design real-time lightweight models using lightweight techniques and tools.

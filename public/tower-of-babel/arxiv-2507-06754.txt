1.
Introduction

The families of abelian varieties defined over a global field are fundamental. In this paper, we study families of elliptic curves over function fields. By a family, we mean specifically an
elliptic fibration
, that is an algebraic surface
X
that admits a proper flat morphism
f:X\to C
to a smooth projective curve
C/k
over a field
k
such that a general fiber is a smooth curve of genus one. Such an
X
is sometimes called an
elliptic surface
in other literature. It is natural to work with the case when there exists a distinguished section
s:C\hookrightarrow X
coming from the identity points on each of the elliptic fibers. An elliptic fibration is called
relatively-minimal
if none of the fibers contain any
(-1)
-curves.

It is natural to ask how many elliptic fibrations
f:X\to C
exist. This question is equivalent to determining the total number of rational points on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}
over a function field
K=k(C)
. For proper stacks, unlike schemes, there is a distinction between rational and integral points. Moreover, rational points have extra automorphism groups. In the case where
K=\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
with
\text{char}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})>3
, the exact number of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
K
is established in
[
BPS22
, Theorem 9.7]
. The proof relies on the height moduli framework developed in
[
BPS22
, Theorems 1.2 & 5.1]
by Bejleri, Satriano, and the author. Specifically, the method involves extracting coefficients of rational motivic height zeta functions
Z_{\vec{\lambda}}(t)
associated to the height moduli spaces and their variants on the corresponding inertia stacks
\mathcal{I}Z_{\vec{\lambda}}(t)
as described in
[
BPS22
, Theorem 8.9]
. In the present work, we extend the enumerations to the remaining cases
\text{char}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})=2,3
inspired by the classical work of
[
dJ02
]
.

Specifically, we establish the following sharp enumeration of elliptic curves over a global function field
K=\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
with precise lower order main terms. Recall that the height of the discriminant of an elliptic curve

Motives & Point counts over finite fields

In this section, we briefly review the arithmetic of algebraic stacks over a perfect field
k
, with a focus on the case when
k=\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite field. Afterward, we introduce motivic invariants of moduli stacks via the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
of
k
-stacks. We compute the motives of
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
and its inertia stack
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
. We also recall the height moduli spaces on
0
-dimensional weighted projective stack
\mathcal{P}(a)
for later computation of the motives of height moduli spaces on the classifying stacks
\mathcal{B}Q_{12}
,
\mathcal{B}Q_{24}
and
\mathcal{B}Z
, where
Q_{12}
and
Q_{24}
denote the dicyclic groups of orders 12 and 24, respectively, and
Z
denotes the non-reduced group scheme of order 2.

Due to the presence of automorphisms, point counts of an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
over finite fields are weighted.

Definition 2.1
.

The weighted point count of an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
with finite inertia over
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
is defined as a sum

\#_{q}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(x)|},

where
\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim
is the set of
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
–isomorphism classes of
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
–points of
\mathcal{X}
.

The main advantage of the weighted point count is that it is
algebro-topological
as it depends only on the cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
and is equal to the usual point count of the coarse moduli space via the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula for algebraic stacks proven by classical works of
[
Beh93
,
Sun12
]
.

It is important to note that the above sum runs over the set
\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim
of isomorphism classes over
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
thus the weighted point count
\#_{q}(\mathcal{X})
is
not equal
to the number
|\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim|
of
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
–isomorphism classes when there is a non-trivial automorphism
|\mathrm{Aut}(x)|\neq 1
for some stacky point
x\in\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim
. Because of this, for enumeration purposes, it is important to consider the unweighted count of isomorphism classes. The following result of
[
HP23
]
shows that the unweighted point count is also natural and depends on the arithmetic of the inertia stack of
\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 1.1. of
[
HP23
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack over
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
of finite type with quasi-separated finite type diagonal and let
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X})
be the inertia stack of
\mathcal{X}
. Then,

|\mathcal{X}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})/\sim|=\#_{q}(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{X}))

In this paper, we study proportions via motivic classes in the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
of
k
-stacks. We review some properties of the Grothendieck ring of stacks introduced in
[
Eke25
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
Eke25
, §1]

The
Grothendieck ring of stacks

K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
is the abelian group generated by classes
\{\mathcal{X}\}_{k}
for each algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
of finite type over
k
with affine inertia modulo the relations

•

\{\mathcal{X}\}_{k}=\{\mathcal{Z}\}_{k}+\{\mathcal{X}\smallsetminus\mathcal{Z}\}_{k}
for
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
a closed substack,

•

\{\mathcal{E}\}_{k}=\{\mathcal{X}\times_{k}\mdmathbb{A}^{n}\}_{k}
for
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle of rank
n
on
\mathcal{X}
.

Multiplication on
K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
is induced by
\{\mathcal{X}\}_{k}\{\mathcal{Y}\}_{k}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{X}\times_{k}\mathcal{Y}\}
.
There is a distinguished element
\mdmathbb{L}\coloneqq\{\mdmathbb{A}^{1}\}_{k}\in K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
, called the
Lefschetz motive
. We drop the subscript if
k
is clear.

We denote by
K_{0}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
the ring obtained by imposing only the cut-and-paste relation but not the vector bundle relation and denote the class of a stack in this ring by
\{\mathcal{X}\}^{\prime}
. The Grothendieck ring is universal among all additive and multiplicative invariants. For instance, when
k=\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
, the point counting measure
\{\mathcal{X}\}\mapsto\#_{q}(\mathcal{X})
is a well-defined ring homomorphism
\#_{q}:K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{\mdmathbb{F}_{q}})\rightarrow\mdmathbb{Q}
giving the weighted point count
\#_{q}(\mathcal{X})
of
\mathcal{X}
over
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
. When
\{\mathcal{X}\}
is mixed Tate i.e. a polynomial in the Lefschetz motive
\mdmathbb{L}\coloneqq\{\mdmathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\}
which serves as a natural “measure” of the affine line, the weighted point count is a polynomial in
q
.

Recall that an algebraic group
G
is
special
in the sense of
[
Ser58
]
and
[
Gro58
]
, if every
G
-torsor is Zariski-locally trivial; for example
\mdmathbb{G}_{a},~\GL_{d},~\SL_{d}
are special and
\PGL_{2},~\PGL_{3}
are non-special. If
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is a
G
-torsor and
G
is special, then we have
\{\mathcal{X}\}=\{G\}\cdot\{\mathcal{Y}\}
(
[
Eke25
, Prop. 1.1 iii)]
).

Finally, we can use the following result to access unweighted point counts.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
dFLNU07
, Prop. 5.3]
The association
\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathcal{I}\mathcal{X}
extends to a unique ring homomorphism

\mathcal{I}:K_{0}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})\to K_{0}^{\prime}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})

which we call the inertia operator.

Note that
\mathcal{I}
does not descend to a well defined operator on
K_{0}(\mathrm{Stck}_{k})
as in
[
Eke25
, Prop. 1.1 iii)]
. In order to keep track of the primitive roots of unity contained in
\mdmathbb{F}_{q}
, we define the following auxiliary function.

\delta(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\text{ divides }q-1,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
k
be a perfect field with
\mathrm{char}(k)\nmid a,b
. The motivic classes of the weighted projective stack
\mathcal{P}(a,b)
and its inertia stack
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(a,b)
in

are equal to

\left\{\mathcal{P}(a,b)\right\}=\mdmathbb{L}+1

\left\{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(a,b)\right\}=\gcd(a,b)\cdot(\mdmathbb{L}+1)+\delta(a)\cdot(a-\gcd(a,b))+\delta(b)\cdot(b-\gcd(a,b))

Proof.

As
\mathcal{P}(a,b)\coloneqq\left[(\mdmathbb{A}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\})/\mdmathbb{G}_{m}\right]
we have
\left\{\mathcal{P}(a,b)\right\}=\frac{\mdmathbb{L}^{2}-1}{\mdmathbb{L}-1}=\mdmathbb{L}+1
which holds as
\mdmathbb{G}_{m}=\GL_{1}
is a special group. For the inertia stack
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(a,b)
, note that

\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(a,b)=\sqcup_{\gcd(a,b)}\mathcal{P}(a,b)\sqcup_{a-\gcd(a,b)}\mathcal{P}(a)\sqcup_{b-\gcd(a,b)}\mathcal{P}(b)

by
[
HP23
, Proposition 3.5]
which translates to

\left\{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{P}(a,b)\right\}=\gcd(a,b)\cdot\left\{\mathcal{P}(a,b)\right\}+\delta(a)\cdot(a-\gcd(a,b))\cdot\left\{\mathcal{P}(a)\right\}+\delta(b)\cdot(b-\gcd(a,b))\cdot\left\{\mathcal{P}(b)\right\}

As
\mathcal{P}(r)\coloneqq\left[(\mdmathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\})/\mdmathbb{G}_{m}\right]
hence
\left\{\mathcal{P}(r)\right\}=\frac{\mdmathbb{L}-1}{\mdmathbb{L}-1}=1
we get the desired formula.
∎

3.
Counting isomorphism classes of
E/\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)

In counting the exact unweighted number of isomorphism classes of
E/\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, it is vital to understand the families of elliptic curves that are non-constant (i.e. height
n\geq 1
) and isotrivial (i.e. a fixed
j
-invariant) with extra automorphisms (i.e. more than the generic hyperelliptic involution automorphism of order 2) having strictly additive bad reductions (i.e. potentially good reduction) which leads to various lower order main terms that are hidden from the exact weighted number of isomorphism classes of
E/\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
(c.f.
[
BPS22
, Rmk 9.8]
).

In this regard, for
\text{char}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})=3,2
, the automorphism group of a geometric point of
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
is of order 2 away from
j=0
(c.f.
[
Sil09
, Ap. A, Prop. 1.2]
). In
\text{char}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})=2
, the generic geometric points with
j_{g}\neq 0
have the stabilizer groups of
Z=\Spec k[x]/(x^{2}-1)
which is non-reduced as
x^{2}-1=(x-1)^{2}
. The automorphism group of a geometric point at
j=0
is
Q_{12}
(resp.
Q_{24}
) the dicyclic group of order 12 (resp. 24). The dicyclic groups are non-abelian with order
4n
for
n\geq 3
and each contains a unique element of order 2.

A given generalized Weierstrass form
F=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}-y^{2}-a_{1}xy-a_{3}y
, where
a_{i}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(in))
defines an
E/\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
and if
F
defines an
E/\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t)
with a fixed
j
-invariant then as explained in
[
Sil09
, Ap. A, Prop. 1.1]
there exists a coordinate change of the form
x\mapsto x+b_{1}
and
y\mapsto y+b_{1}x+b_{3}
with
b_{i}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(in))
such that for
\text{char}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q})=3
and
j=0
has the following normal Weierstrass form

y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6}

As explained in
[
dJ02
, §4.13 (b2)]
, there exists a unique choice of the pair
(b_{1},b_{3})
so that we get

y^{2}=x^{3}+a^{\prime}_{4}x+a^{\prime}_{6}

where
a^{\prime}_{4}
is a nonzero section of
\mathcal{O}(4n)
and
a^{\prime}_{6}
is any section of
\mathcal{O}(6n)
. As in the paragraph before
[
dJ02
, Prop. 4.14]
, we have
(q^{4n+1}-1)q^{6n+1}q^{4n+2}
as the total number of Weierstrass polynomials which are non-constant and isotrivial with
j=0
where
(q^{4n+1}-1)
corresponds to counting a nonzero section of
\mathcal{O}(4n)
and
q^{6n+1}
corresponds to counting any section of
\mathcal{O}(6n)
and
q^{4n+2}
corresponds to counting any sections of
\mathcal{O}(n)
and
\mathcal{O}(3n)
due to
(b_{1},b_{3})
worth of freedom.

After dividing out the coordinate change factor of
q^{6n+3}(q-1)
consisting of
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
worth of freedom and a nonzero scalar
\lambda\in k^{*}

(q^{4n}+\ldots+1)\cdot q^{4n}=(q^{8n}+\ldots+q^{4n})=(q^{8n}+\ldots+1)-(q^{4n-1}+\ldots+1)

This expression is the total number of minimal Weierstrass forms that are isotrivial with
j=0
upto height
n

[
dJ02

n\geq 1

at

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We prove for
q=3^{r}
case first. We recall that the weighted count
\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)
in
[
BPS22
, Theorem 9.7]
is the same for all positive characteristic as shown in
[
dJ02
, Proposition 4.12]
. As for the unweighted count, we multiply
2
to the weighted count
\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)
since any stacky point away from
j=0
has the hyperelliptic involution automorphism group of order 2 (c.f.
[
Sil09
, Ap. A, Prop. 1.2]
).

For
j=0
, we take the weighted count in Proposition
3.1
and multiply appropriate factors which are
(4-2)=2
for r odd case and
(6-2)=4
for r even case depending on the parity of prime power of 3 as there are 4 isomorphism classes (resp. 6 isomorphism classes) of supersingular elliptic curves for r odd case (resp. r even case) (c.f.
[
CJ04
, Thm. 3.5]
) and -2 comes from taking into account the hyperelliptic involution.

Thus for the unweighted count, we would like to compute the following.

When r is odd:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),B)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)+2\cdot(\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(8)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{4}))

When r is even:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),B)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)+4\cdot(\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(8)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{4}))

We first compute the following by summing over
n\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\left\lfloor\frac{log_{q}B}{12}\right\rfloor}\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{7}-1}{q^{7}-q^{6}}\cdot(B^{2/3}-q^{8})

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\left\lfloor\frac{log_{q}B}{12}\right\rfloor}\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{4})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{q^{3}-1}{q\cdot(q^{3}-q^{2})}\right)\cdot(B^{1/3}-q^{4})

which leads to the following as we sum over all
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(8)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{4})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q^{7}-1}{q^{7}-q^{6}}\right)\cdot(B^{2/3}-q^{8})-\left(\frac{q^{3}-1}{q^{4}-q^{3}}\right)\cdot(B^{1/3}-q^{4})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{ }+(q^{8}+\ldots+q^{2})-(q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1)+1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q^{7}-1}{q^{7}-q^{6}}\right)B^{2/3}-\left(\frac{q^{3}-1}{q^{4}-q^{3}}\right)B^{1/3}

from which the result follows.

We now prove for
q=2^{r}
case. We recall that the weighted count
\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)
in
[
BPS22
, Theorem 9.7]
is the same for all positive characteristic as shown in
[
dJ02
, Proposition 4.12]
. As for the unweighted count, we multiply
2
to the weighted count
\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)
since any stacky point away from
j=0
has the hyperelliptic involution automorphism group of order 2 (c.f.
[
Sil09
, Ap. A, Prop. 1.2]
). There needs to be an adjustment, however, as the weighted number of rational points on
\mathcal{P}(2)
and
\mathcal{P}(2)-\mathcal{P}(\check{1})
differ at height
n=1
. The difference of
\left\{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\mathrm{min}}(\mathcal{P}(2))\right\}=\mdmathbb{L}^{2}
and
\left\{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\mathrm{min}}(\mathcal{P}(2)-\mathcal{P}(\check{1}))\right\}=\mdmathbb{L}^{2}-1
is
-1
.

Thus taking account of all points for every
j_{g}\neq 0
, we need to subtract
(q-1)
from the total number of weighted count corresponding to
\mathcal{M}_{1,1}-\{j=0\}
for a certain fixed
j_{g}\neq 0
. And then we need to add
1
from the total number of weighted count as we subtract the rational points landing on
j=\infty
since we do not want to count the generically singular
j=\infty
isotrivial elliptic curves.

Lastly for
j=0
, we take the weighted count in Proposition
3.3
and multiply appropriate factors which are
(3-2)=1
for r odd case and
(7-2)=5
for r even case depending on the parity of prime power of 2 as there are 3 isomorphism classes (resp. 7 isomorphism classes) of supersingular elliptic curves for r odd case (resp. r even case) (c.f.
[
Men93
, Thm. 3.6 & 3.7]
) and -2 comes from taking into account the hyperelliptic involution.

Thus for the unweighted count, we would like to compute the following.

When r is odd:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),B)

\displaystyle=2\cdot(\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)-(q-1)+1)+(\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(9)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{6}))

When r is even:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),B)

\displaystyle=2\cdot(\mathcal{N}^{w}(\mdmathbb{F}_{q}(t),~B)-(q-1)+1)+5\cdot(\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(9)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{6}))

We first compute the following by summing over
n\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\left\lfloor\frac{log_{q}B}{12}\right\rfloor}\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{8}-1}{q^{8}-q^{7}}\cdot(B^{3/4}-q^{9})

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\left\lfloor\frac{log_{q}B}{12}\right\rfloor}\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{6})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{q^{5}-1}{q\cdot(q^{5}-q^{4})}\right)\cdot(B^{1/2}-q^{6})

which leads to the following as we sum over all
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(9)-\#_{q}\mathcal{W}_{n,\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}(\check{6})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{8}-1}{q^{8}-q^{7}}\cdot(B^{3/4}-q^{9})-\left(\frac{q^{5}-1}{q^{6}-q^{5}}\right)\cdot(B^{1/2}-q^{6})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\text{ }+(q^{9}+\ldots+q^{2})-(q^{5}+q^{4}+q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1)+1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q^{8}-1}{q^{8}-q^{7}}\right)B^{3/4}-\left(\frac{q^{5}-1}{q^{6}-q^{5}}\right)B^{1/2}

from which the result follows.

Lie algebras whose derivation algebras are simple
Jörg Feldvoss, Salvatore Siciliano

Abstract
. It is well known that a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is simple exactly when its derivation algebra is simple. In this paper we characterize those Lie algebras of arbitrary dimension over any field that have a simple derivation algebra. As an application we classify the Lie algebras that have a complete simple derivation algebra and are either finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic p > 3 p>3 or ℤ \mathbb{Z} -graded of finite growth over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Introduction

The extent to which the structure of a Lie algebra is determined by its derivation algebra
has already been studied extensively in the past.

The main result of this paper is a characterization of Lie algebras whose derivation algebras
are simple. For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
L
over a field of characteristic zero, Hochschild
showed in
[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
that its derivation algebra
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is semi-simple if, and
only if,
L
is semi-simple. As a consequence, we have in this case that
\mathrm{Der}(L)\cong L
,
and
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple exactly when
L
is simple (see Theorem 3 (1) and the observation
after the Remark on page 303 in
[
9
]
). These results fail to hold for Lie algebras defined
over fields of non-zero characteristic or for Lie algebras of infinite dimension. Some partial
results for Lie algebras whose derivation algebras are simple were obtained by de Ruiter in

[
9
, Theorem 3]
. We settle this problem here in full generality by proving that, for a
Lie algebra
L
of arbitrary dimension defined over any field, the derivation algebra
\mathrm{Der}(L)

is simple if, and only if,
L
is a suitable homomorphic image of the
universal
central
extension of a simple Lie algebra (see Theorem
3.1
). Moreover, we show that

\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple and complete if, and only if,
L
is a covering algebra of a complete
simple Lie algebra (see Corollary
3.2
). Recall that a Lie algebra is called

complete
if it has zero center and every derivation is inner, and a
covering
algebra
of a Lie algebra
L
is a central extension of
L
that is perfect. As an application,
we classify those Lie algebras that have a complete simple derivation algebra and are
either finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p>3
(see
Corollary
3.3
) or
\mathbb{Z}
-graded of finite growth over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero (see Corollary
3.5
). Since every finite-dimensional
simple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is always complete, Corollaries

3.3
and
3.5
generalize the above mentioned consequence of
Hochschild’s classical result to these classes of modular or infinite-dimensional Lie algebras.

In this paper the identity function on a set
X
will be denoted by
\mathrm{id}_{X}
, the additive
group of integers will be denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
, the additive monoid of non-negative integers
will be denoted by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and the additive semigroup of positive integers will be denoted
by
\mathbb{N}
. All other notation is either standard or the same as in
[
28
]
.

1.
Derivations and central extensions of Cartan algebras

Let
r
be a positive integer, and let
A:=\mathbb{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{r}]
denote the algebra of polynomials
in
r
commuting variables
x_{1}
, …,
x_{r}
with coefficients in a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
zero. In the following we briefly describe the four families of infinite-dimensional Cartan algebras
(see
[
21
, Section 0.1.3]
). Let
W_{r}:=\mathrm{Der}(A)
be the Lie algebra of derivations of
A
,
the so-called
Witt algebra
. Recall that the module of Kähler differentials of
A
is the
free
A
-module
\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}:=A\mathrm{d}x_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus A\mathrm{d}x_{r}
generated by
the differentials
\mathrm{d}x_{1}
, …,
\mathrm{d}x_{r}
of the variables of
A
. Note that any derivation

D\in W_{r}
acts in a natural way as a derivation on the exterior algebra
\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}:=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\Lambda^{n}\Omega^{1}_{A/\mathbb{F}}\right)
of Kähler differential forms
on
A
. It is straightforward to see that for any differential form
\omega\in\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}

the sets
\{D\in W_{r}\mid D(\omega)=0\}
and
\{D\in W_{r}\mid D(\omega)\in A\omega\}

are subalgebras of
W_{r}
.

In this way one obtains the following three families of simple subalgebras of the Witt algebras
(see
[
21
, Section 0.1.3]
):

•

The
special Lie algebras

S_{r}:=\{D\in W_{r}\mid D(\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{r})=0\}
,

•

the
Hamiltonian algebras

H_{2r}:=\{D\in W_{2r}\mid D\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\mathrm{d}x_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{j+r}\right)=0\}
,

•

the
contact Lie algebras

K_{2r+1}:=\{D\in W_{2r+1}\mid D(\omega)\in A\omega\}
,
where
\omega:=\mathrm{d}x_{2r+1}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(x_{j}\mathrm{d}x_{j+r}-x_{j+r}\mathrm{d}x_{j})
.

These four families of infinite-dimensional simple Lie algebras are called
Cartan algebras
.
It is well known that every Cartan algebra
L
is simple (see
[
21
, Lemma 1.14]
) and graded
over the integers, i.e.,
L=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}
such that
[L_{m},L_{n}]\subseteq L_{m+n}
.
In this section we will determine which of the Cartan algebras are complete and then compute
the central extensions of the complete Cartan algebras.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
L
be a Cartan algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic zero. Then
L
is complete if,
and only if,
L
is a Witt algebra
W_{r}
or a contact Lie algebra
K_{2r+1}
.

Proof.

As every Cartan algebra
L
is simple, we have that
C(L)=0
. Hence, it suffices to compute
the first adjoint cohomology space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L,L)
.

We begin by considering the Witt algebras
W_{r}
. In order to show that the first adjoint
cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{1}(W_{r},W_{r})
vanishes, we will use that
W_{r}
can be considered as
a
G
-graded subalgebra of the two-sided Witt algebra

W(G,I):=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{a}\in G}\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i)\,,

where
G:=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
,
I:=\{1,\dots,r\}
, and the Lie bracket on the basis elements
w(\mathbf{a},i)

is defined by

[w(\mathbf{a},i),w(\mathbf{b},j)]=a_{j}w(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b},i)-b_{i}w(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b},j)

for all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in G
(see
[
19
, Section 1]
).
Namely, set
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}:=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{a_{r}}
for every
r
-tuple
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
of integers,
\mathbf{e}_{j}:=(\delta_{ij})_{i\in I}
, where
\delta_{ij}

denotes the usual Kronecker symbol, and
\partial_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

for every
i\in I
. Then the linear transformation
\theta:W_{r}\to W(G,I)
defined
by
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\partial_{i}\mapsto-w(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{i},i)
is a monomorphism of Lie algebras.
Moreover, the Witt algebra
L:=\theta(W_{r})
inherits a
G
-grading
L=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{a}\in G}L_{\mathbf{a}}
from
W(G,I)
via
L_{\mathbf{a}}:=W_{\mathbf{a}}\cap L
,
where
W_{\mathbf{a}}:=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i)
, and one observes that

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}W_{\mathbf{a}}&\mbox{ if }a_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ for every }i\in I\\
\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},j)&\mbox{ if }a_{j}=-1\mbox{ for exactly one }j\in I\mbox{ and }a_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ for every }i\in I\setminus\{j\}\,.\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

In particular, we have that
L_{\mathbf{0}}=W_{\mathbf{0}}
, where
\mathbf{0}:=(0,\dots,0)\in G
,
and
L_{-\mathbf{e}_{i}}=\mathbb{F}w(-\mathbf{e}_{i},i)
for every
i\in I
.

Next, we would like to apply
[
15
, Proposition 1.2]
. Note that it follows from

[
21
, Lemma 3.11]
in conjunction with
[
21
, Lemma 3.4]
that
L
is finitely
generated unless
r=1
. But in the latter case it is clear that
\{\theta(\partial_{1}),\theta(x_{1}\partial_{1}),\theta(x_{1}^{2}\partial_{1}),\theta(x_{1}^{3}\partial_{1})\}
generates
L
.

It remains to verify that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{a}})=0
for every
r
-tuple
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{0}
of integers and
\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(L_{\mathbf{a}},L_{\mathbf{b}})=0
for all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in G
with
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{b}
. Note that
L_{\mathbf{a}}
is either 0
or
\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},j)
for some integer
j\in I
or the direct sum of the one-dimensional

L_{\mathbf{0}}
-modules
\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i)
(
i\in I
). If
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then we have that

[\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i)]^{L_{\mathbf{0}}}=0
for every integer
i\in I
, and since
L_{\mathbf{0}}
is abelian,
it follows from
[
1
, Lemma 3]
that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(L_{\mathbf{0}},L_{\mathbf{a}})=0
. Moreover,
we obtain that

\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(L_{\mathbf{a}},L_{\mathbf{b}})=\bigoplus_{i\in I_{\mathbf{a}},j\in I_{\mathbf{b}}}\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i),\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{b},j))

for some subsets
I_{\mathbf{a}}\subseteq I
and
I_{\mathbf{b}}\subseteq I
, and thus it suffices
to show that every summand vanishes when
\mathbf{a}\neq\mathbf{b}
. So by contraposition,
we suppose that there exists a non-zero element
\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{L_{\mathbf{0}}}(\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{a},i),\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{b},j))
for fixed but arbitrary integers
i\in I_{\mathbf{a}}
and
j\in I_{\mathbf{b}}
.
Then
\psi(w(\mathbf{a},i))=\lambda w(\mathbf{b},j)
for some non-zero scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
,
and therefore we obtain for every integer
k\in I
that

-a_{k}\lambda w(\mathbf{b},j))=\psi([w(\mathbf{0},k),w(\mathbf{a},i)])=[w(\mathbf{0},k),\psi(w(\mathbf{a},i))]=-b_{k}\lambda w(\mathbf{b},j)\,

i.e.,
(a_{k}-b_{k})\lambda w(\mathbf{b},j)=0
. Hence, we conclude that
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}
.

Consequently, we deduce from
[
15
, Proposition 1.2]
that

\mathrm{Der}(L)=\mathrm{ad}(L)+\mathrm{Der}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}\,,

where
\mathrm{Der}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
denotes the subspace of the degree-preserving derivations
of
L
, and it remains to show that every derivation
D\in\mathrm{Der}(L)_{\mathbf{0}}
is inner.
For this purpose, we will now use the arguments of Ikeda and Kawamoto in their
proof of Theorem 2 for a finite index set (see
[
19
, Section 3]
). Note that
the elements
w(-\mathbf{e}_{i},i)
and
w(\mathbf{a}+e_{i},i)
in (3.10) and (3.11) are contained
in
L
. Of course, for the latter this might only be the case when the corresponding
element
w(\mathbf{a},i)
belongs to
L
. The same is true for the elements
w(\mathbf{e}_{h},h)
,

w(\mathbf{e}_{h},i)
(
h\neq i
), and
w(2\mathbf{e}_{h},i)
in (3.20)–(3.23). Finally, this also holds
for the element
x
in (3.31) as
L_{\mathbf{0}}=W_{\mathbf{0}}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{F}w(\mathbf{0},i)
. In conclusion, the arguments in Section 3 of
[
19
]
prove that every
degree-preserving derivation of
L
is inner, and therefore
\mathrm{Der}(L)=\mathrm{ad}(L)
, as
desired.

We now consider the contact Lie algebras
K_{2r+1}
. In the notation of the paper

[
25
]
, one observes that
K_{2r+1}
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
\mathscr{K}(\vec{\ell},\sigma,\Gamma,\mathscr{I})
in the particular case when
\vec{\ell}:=(0,0,0,0,0,r)
,
\sigma:=0
,
\Gamma:=\{0\}
, and
\mathscr{I}:=\mathbb{N}_{0}^{1+2r}
.
As a consequence, it follows from
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
that every derivation of

K_{2r+1}
is inner.

In order to show that the special Lie algebras and the Hamiltonian algebras have outer
derivations, we use their usual
\mathbb{Z}
-gradings and consider the “degree” derivation

D_{0}:=(\mathrm{ad}\,h)_{|L}\in\mathrm{Der}(L)
for
L:=S_{r}
or
L:=H_{2r}
, where
h:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\partial_{i}\in W_{r}
. Suppose that
D_{0}=\mathrm{ad}_{L}\,D
for some

D\in L
. Then we obtain that

\mathrm{deg}(D)D=[h,D]=D_{0}(D)=[D,D]=0

(cf. Theorem 2.5 (1) in Chapter 4 of
[
29
]
), and therefore we deduce that

\mathrm{deg}(D)=0
or
D=0
, i.e.,
D\in L_{0}
. In the first case
[D,X]=[h,X]=\mathrm{deg}(X)X=0

for any
X\in L_{0}
also shows that
D\in C(L_{0})=0
(as
L_{0}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{r}(\mathbb{F})
or

L_{0}\cong\mathfrak{sp}_{2r}(\mathbb{F})
are both simple Lie algebras), i.e., in both cases we
conclude that
D_{0}=0
, which contradicts
D_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{L_{1}}
and
L_{1}\neq 0
.
∎

Proposition 1.2
.

Let

\mathrm{H}^{2}(W_{r},\mathbb{F})=0
and

\mathrm{H}^{2}(K_{2r+1},\mathbb{F})=0
.

In the notation of the paper
[

]
,
W_{r}
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra

. Hence, it follows from
[

that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(W_{r},\mathbb{F})=0
. Moreover, as already has been observed in the proof of Proposition

1.1
, in the notation of the paper
[

,
K_{2r+1}
is isomorphic
to the Lie algebra
\mathscr{K}(\vec{\ell},\sigma,\Gamma,\mathscr{I})

\vec{\ell}:=(0,0,0,0,0,r)
,

[

∎

2.
Covering algebras

Let
0\to\bar{C}\to\bar{L}\to L\to 0
be a central extension of a Lie algebra
L
. Then

\bar{L}
is called a
covering algebra
of
L
if
\bar{L}
is perfect, i.e.,
\bar{L}=[\bar{L},\bar{L}]
(see
[
16
, Definition 1.4]
or
[
2
, Section 2]
). Since homomorphic
images of perfect Lie algebras are perfect, only perfect Lie algebras admit a covering algebra.
Moreover, if
C(L)=0
, then
\bar{C}=C(\bar{L})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
with
\mathrm{H}^{2}(L,\mathbb{F})=0
. Then
L
has at most
one covering algebra up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\bar{L}
be any covering algebra of
L
, i.e.,
\bar{L}
is perfect and there exists
a central extension
0\to\bar{C}\to\bar{L}\to L\to 0
. Since we have that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(L,\mathbb{F})=0
, the central extension splits, i.e.,
\bar{L}\cong L\times\bar{C}
, and thus
we obtain that

\bar{L}=[\bar{L},\bar{L}]\cong[L\times\bar{C},L\times\bar{C}]=[L,L]=L\,,

which completes the proof.
∎

A
universal central extension
of a Lie algebra
L
is a central extension

0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}L\to 0

of
L
such that for every central extension
0\to Z\to E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}L\to 0

of
L
there exists a unique homomorphism
\varphi:\hat{L}\to E
of Lie algebras
satisfying
\pi\circ\varphi=\hat{\pi}
. Note that then necessarily
\varphi(\hat{C})\subseteq Z
. It is clear from this definition that a universal central extension of

L
is unique up to isomorphism, in case it exists. Since universal central extensions
must be perfect, they are covering algebras. It is well known that a Lie algebra
L

over a field
\mathbb{F}
has a universal central extension if, and only if,
L
is perfect
(see
[
31
, Theorem 7.9.2]
). Moreover, we conclude from
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]

and
[
31
, Lemma 7.9.3]
that a central extension
0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}L\to 0
is universal exactly when
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\hat{L},\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{H}^{2}(\hat{L},\mathbb{F})=0
. In particular, the property of being universal is independent
of
L
. This will be used in the proof of the next result which shows that every
covering algebra of a Lie algebra
L
is a homomorphic image of some universal
central extension of
L
. For this reason sometimes universal central extensions
are called
universal covering algebras
(e.g. cf.
[
16
, Definition 1.6]

or
[
2
, Section 2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
L
be a perfect Lie algebra. Then a universal central extension of
L
is
also a universal central extension of every covering algebra of
L
. In particular,
if
0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}L\to 0
is a universal central
extension of
L
, and
\bar{L}
is a covering algebra of
L
, then there
exists a central ideal
C
of
\hat{L}
such that
C\subseteq\hat{C}
and

\bar{L}\cong\hat{L}/C
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{L}
be a covering algebra of
L
, i.e.,
\bar{L}
is perfect and
there exists a central extension
0\to\bar{C}\to\bar{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\pi}}}{{\to}}L\to 0
. Let
\varphi:\hat{L}\to L
be the unique homomorphism of Lie algebras
such that the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hat{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hat{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\pi}}
\textstyle{{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bar{C}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bar{L}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\pi}}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

It follows from the commutativity of the diagram that
\bar{\pi}(\mathrm{Im}(\varphi))=L=\bar{\pi}(\bar{L})
, and therefore we obtain that
\bar{L}=\mathrm{Im}(\varphi)+\bar{C}
. Since
\bar{L}
is perfect and
\bar{C}
is central in
\bar{L}
,
we conclude then that
\varphi
is surjective. Moreover, we obtain from the
commutativity of the diagram that
C:=\mathrm{Ker}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\hat{\pi})=\hat{C}\subseteq C(\hat{L})
, and therefore it follows from the cohomological
characterization of universal central extensions observed above that
0\to C\to\hat{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}\bar{L}\to 0
is a universal central extension of

\bar{L}
. Moreover, the second part of the assertion is an immediate consequence.
∎

Note that Lemma
2.2
is the key result in the proof of Theorem
3.1

below and in the proof of the next result which will be needed to deduce Corollaries

2.5
and
2.7
below.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. If
L
admits a universal central extension

0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}L\to 0
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\hat{C}=1
,
then
L
and
\hat{L}
are the only covering algebras of
L
up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\bar{L}
be a covering algebra of
L
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
that there
exists a central ideal
C
of
\hat{L}
such that
C\subseteq\hat{C}
and
\bar{L}\cong\hat{L}/C
. If
C=0
, then
\bar{L}\cong\hat{L}
. If
C\neq 0
, then for dimension reasons

C=\hat{C}
, and so we have that
\bar{L}\cong\hat{L}/C=\hat{L}/\hat{C}\cong L
.
∎

\mathbb{F}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
V
denote the restricted Virasoro algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of prime characteristic
p>3
.
Then
\mathrm{H}^{1}(V,\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})=0
. In particular, the restricted Virasoro algebra is a universal
central extension of the Witt algebra.

Proof.

In order to prove that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(V,\mathbb{F})=0
, we need to show that
V
is perfect. We have
that
[e_{2},e_{p-2}]=\frac{1}{6}(p-3)(p-2)(p-1)z
, and as
\frac{1}{6}(p-3)(p-2)(p-1)\neq 0
, we obtain that

. In addition,

for every

shows that
e_{n}\in[V,V]
for every
n\neq 0
. Finally, we obtain from
[e_{-1},e_{1}]=2e_{0}

that
e_{0}\in[V,V]
. Hence, we conclude that
V=[V,V]
.

Now we will prove that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})=0
. We obtain from
[
18
, Theorem 7]
the following
exact sequence:

\mathrm{H}^{1}(V,\mathbb{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(W,\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{2}^{\prime}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(W,\mathbb{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l_{2}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(W,\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F}))\,.

Since we have

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{F}
, we deduce that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(W,\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F}))\cong\mathbb{F}
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(W,\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}z,\mathbb{F}))\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(W,\mathbb{F})=0
. This in conjunction with

\mathrm{H}^{1}(V,\mathbb{F})=0
yields the short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{2}^{\prime}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(W,\mathbb{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l_{2}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})\to 0\,.

As
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrm{H}^{2}(W,\mathbb{F})=1
, the injectivity of
d_{2}^{\prime}
implies that
d_{2}^{\prime}

is surjective. Hence, we obtain from the exactness of the sequence that
\mathrm{Ker}(l_{2})=\mathrm{Im}(d_{2}^{\prime})=\mathrm{H}^{2}(W,\mathbb{F})
, and thus
l_{2}=0
. So we conclude that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{Im}(l_{2})=0
.

Finally, it follows from
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
31
, Lemma 7.9.3]
in conjunction with

\mathrm{H}^{1}(V,\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{H}^{2}(V,\mathbb{F})=0
that
V
is a universal central extension of
W
.
∎

2.3
and Lemma
2.4
we
obtain:

Corollary 2.5
.

\mathbb{F}
be a field of prime characteristic

3.
Lie algebras whose derivation algebras are simple

In this section we characterize Lie algebras whose derivation algebras are simple
or simple and complete. For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
L
over a field of
characteristic zero it follows from
[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
that
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple
exactly when
L
is simple. Some partial results for not necessarily finite-dimensional
Lie algebras over arbitrary fields were obtained by de Ruiter (see
[
9
, Theorem
3]
). The next result settles this problem completely.

We begin by introducing some notation. In analogy to the
idealizer
of a
subspace of
L
we consider the
stabilizer
of a subspace
V
of a (left)

L
-module
M
, i.e.,

\mathrm{Stab}_{L}(V):=\{x\in L|\,x\cdot V\subseteq V\}\,.

Clearly,
\mathrm{Stab}_{L}(V)
is a subalgebra of
L
, and
V
is an
L
-submodule of
M

exactly when
\mathrm{Stab}_{L}(V)=L
. Now, let

0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\to 0

be a universal central extension of a centerless Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then clearly
\hat{C}=C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
and, in view of
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
, every derivation
D
of
\mathfrak{g}
can
be uniquely lifted to a derivation
\hat{D}
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
, so that one has
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathrm{Der}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
. Thus, we can view
C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
as a
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
-module by
setting
D\cdot x:=\hat{D}(x)
, for every
D\in\mathrm{Der}(L)
and every
x\in C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
.
With respect to this action,
C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
is annihilated by
\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
, so we can regard

C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
as an
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
-module as well, where
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g}):=\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the Lie algebra of outer derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Then
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple if, and only if,
L\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C
, where
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is a universal central extension of a simple Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{F}
and
C
is a central ideal of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})}(C)=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple. As
\mathrm{ad}(L)
is an ideal of
\mathrm{Der}(L)
, we have
\mathrm{Der}(L)=\mathrm{ad}(L)\cong L/C(L)
. Consequently,
L
is a central extension of the simple Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Der}(L)
. Moreover, it follows from
[
9
, Theorem 3 (3)]
that
L

is perfect, and therefore
L
is a covering algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

If
0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\to 0
is a universal central extension
of
\mathfrak{g}
, then we have that
\hat{C}=C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
because
\mathfrak{g}
is centerless. Thus, by
Lemma
2.2
, there exists a central ideal
C
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
L\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C
. Consequently, we deduce from
[
22
, Theorem 2.6 (c)]
that
\mathrm{Der}(L)

is isomorphic to the subalgebra of
\mathrm{Der}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of all derivations
D
such
that
D(C)\subseteq C
. Since this subalgebra contains
\mathrm{ad}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
as an ideal, the
simplicity of
\mathrm{Der}(L)
implies that the derivations of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
leaving
C
invariant are
precisely the inner derivations. This shows that
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})}(C)=0
, which
finishes the proof that the given conditions are necessary.

Conversely, suppose that
0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\to 0
is a
universal central extension of a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
L\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C

and
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})}(C)=0
for some central ideal
C
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since

\mathfrak{g}
is centerless and
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is perfect, we have that
\hat{C}=C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
and

L
is perfect. From this we conclude that
L
is a covering algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Hence, it
follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is also a universal central extension
of
L
. As a consequence, it follows from
[
22
, Theorem 2.6 (c)]
that
\mathrm{Der}(L)

is isomorphic to the subalgebra of
\mathrm{Der}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of all derivations
D

such that
D(C)\subseteq C
. But
\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})}(C)=0
, so we necessarily
have
\mathrm{Der}(L)\cong\mathrm{ad}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cong\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,
as
\mathfrak{g}
is simple,
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Then
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple and complete if,
and only if,
L
is a covering algebra of a complete simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose that
L
is a covering algebra of a complete simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, it follows from
[
31
, Theorem 7.9.2]
that
\mathfrak{g}
has a universal
central extension
0\to\hat{C}\to\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\pi}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{g}\to 0
. By virtue
of Lemma
2.2
,
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is also a universal central extension of
L
. As
a consequence, we conclude from
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
in conjunction with the
completeness of
\mathfrak{g}
that
\mathrm{Der}(L)\cong\mathrm{Der}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cong\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{g}
.
In particular, as
\mathfrak{g}
is simple and complete,
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is also simple and complete,
which shows the sufficiency of the condition.

Conversely, suppose now that
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple and complete. By Theorem

3.1
there exists a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that
L\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C
,
where
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the universal central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
and
C
is a central
ideal of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then we have that

L/C(L)\cong[\hat{g}/C]/[C(\hat{g})/C]\cong\hat{\mathfrak{g}}/C(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\cong\mathfrak{g}\,,

and because
\hat{g}
is perfect, the same is true for
L
. Hence,
L
is a covering
algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Finally, as in second part of the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we
obtain that
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathrm{Der}(L)
, which implies that
\mathfrak{g}
is simple and complete.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We would like to point out to the interested reader the following observations:

(1) Note that a complete Lie algebra is necessarily restricted with a unique

[p]
-map (see the remark after the definition on p. 72 and Corollary 2.2 (1) in
Chapter 2 of
[
29
]
). So it should not be surprising that all the Lie algebras
appearing in (i)–(v) of Corollary
3.3
are restricted. Moreover, in principle,
in the proof of Corollary
3.3
we would have needed only the classification
of the
restricted
simple Lie algebras (see
[
6
]
). But since the Block-Wilson
classification of the finite-dimensional restricted simple Lie algebras is only valid for
algebraically closed fields of prime characteristic
p>7
, this would have given a
weaker result.

(2) One might be tempted to use Theorem
3.1
and the Block-Wilson-Strade-Premet
classification in order to find also an explicit description of those finite-dimensional Lie
algebras over algebraically closed fields of prime characteristic
p>3
that have a (not
necessarily complete) simple derivation algebra. However, for Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
of
Cartan type, it turns out to be hard to determine those central ideals
C
that satisfy
the conditions in Theorem
3.1
unless
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})=0
or
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\hat{C}\leq 1
. In this regard, it is worth recalling that
\hat{C}
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F})
, the second homology space of
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the
trivial module
\mathbb{F}
(see e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 7.9.2]
). In turn, it follows from
Proposition 5.1 in Chapter VI of
[
7
]
that
\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F})^{*}\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F})
, where
(-)^{*}
denotes the linear dual. Consequently, the finite-dimensional
(not necessarily restricted) graded simple Lie algebras for which
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\hat{C}>1

holds are the special Lie algebras
S(r,\underline{n})^{(1)}
, the Hamiltonian algebras

H(2r,\underline{n})^{(2)}
, and those contact Lie algebras
K(2r+1,\underline{n})^{(1)}

that satisfy
r+2\equiv 0\,\mbox{\rm(mod $p$)}
or
r+3\equiv 0\,\mbox{\rm(mod
$p$)}
(see
[
8
, Corollary 4.1]
,
[
13
, Theorem 2.4 (2) and (3)]
, and

[
14
, Theorem 4.5]
). But in addition, there are also the non-graded simple
Lie algebras of Cartan type whose central extensions do not seem to be known
except in rank one (see
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
for the Albert-Frank algebras and

[
27
, Theorem 6.3]
for the Block algebras).

(3) As mentioned earlier, for a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
L
over a field
of characteristic zero, Hochschild proved in
[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
that
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is
semi-simple if, and only if,
L
is semi-simple. In view of (v) in Corollary
3.3
,
this is not true in non-zero characteristic. But it would be very interesting to know
which (finite-dimensional) modular Lie algebras have a semi-simple derivation algebra.
Unfortunately, at the moment it seems that this problem is out of reach. Even in the
finite-dimensional case, the main difficulty is that modular semi-simple Lie algebras need
not be a direct product of simple Lie algebras, and the description provided by Richard
Block in
[
5
, Theorem 9.3]
seems not to be helpful for our purposes.

(4) The restricted Virasoro algebra
V
is an example of a finite-dimensional
Lie algebra over a field of non-zero characteristic whose derivation algebra is simple, but
whose center is non-zero (see also the Remark after Theorem 3 in
[
9
]
, where an
example of an infinite-dimensional Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero having
these properties is mentioned). Of course, by virtue of Hochschild’s classical result

[
17
, Theorem 4.4]
, every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic
zero whose derivation algebra is simple is itself semi-simple, and thus its center is zero.

As another application of Corollary
3.2
we determine those Lie algebras
that have a complete simple derivation algebra and are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded of finite growth over
an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic zero.

Let

According to Corollary
3.2
,
\mathrm{Der}(L)
is simple and complete if, and only if,

is either a finite-dimensional simple Lie

According to Whitehead’s first lemma, every finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra is complete.
Moreover, it follows from Proposition
1.1
that

and

to conclude that

or

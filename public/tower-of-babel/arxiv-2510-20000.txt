Classification for dynamics of Markov chains on non-negative integers with arbitrary transition rates and its application
Minjun Kim, Seokhwan Moon, Jinsu Kim

Abstract
Continuous-time Markov chains on non-negative integers can be used for modeling biological systems, population dynamics and queueing models. Qualitative behaviors of birth-and-death models, typical examples of such a one-dimensional continuous-time Markov chains, have been substantially studied. For one-dimensional Markov chains with polynomial transition rates, recent studies provided criteria for their long-term behavior. In this paper, we provide a classification of Markov chains on non-negative integers when the transition rates are arbitrary functions. The criteria are written with asymptotics of the transition rates. This classification implies their dynamical properties, including explosivity, recurrence, positive recurrence, and exponential ergodicity. As an application, we derive a complete classification (if and only if conditions) for those dynamical features when the transition rates have certain expansion forms, which include all rational functions, so that our classifications cover mass-action kinetics, Michaelis–Menten kinetics, and Haldane equations. Our classification solely relies on easily computable quantities: the maximal degree of the expansion of the transition rates, the mean and the variance of the transition rates. We demonstrate the utility of this classification framework using the approximation of high-dimensional mass-action systems by a one-dimensional reaction system with general rational kinetics.

Introduction

One-dimensional continuous-time Markov chains on non-neg- ative integers have been widely used to model population growth in various biological and ecological systems, particularly describing the time evolution of copy numbers of proteins, bacteria, cells, and chemical species
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
Thanks to their simplicity, these models are particularly useful and commonly applied. Typical examples include birth-and-death processes. They are sophisticated enough to serve as powerful tools for studying the copy numbers of biochemical species, yet simple enough to allow detailed analyses of their qualitative and quantitative behaviors.

However, when the Markov chain allows multiple jumps with arbitrary jump sizes, their qualitative properties such as positive recurrence and exponential ergodicity have not been fully characterized for general transition rates. In this paper, we provide simple criteria for determining various dynamical features of continuous-time Markov chains on non-negative integers. When the transition rates of these stochastic processes are rational functions, our criteria yield a complete classification based on their dynamical properties.

A critical motivation of our work is that one-dimensional Markov chains with non-polynomial transition rates can often be derived as reductions of higher-dimensional Markov chains governed by mass-action kinetics, which are fundamental polynomial laws for biochemical reaction systems.
A typical example is the reduction of an enzyme–substrate model to the Michaelis–Menten kinetics model via a quasi–steady-state approximation
[
4
]
.
Such time-scale separation–based model reductions can lead to more general one-dimensional Markov chains with non-polynomial transition rates. For instance, consider two biochemical species that catalyze each other under two distinct timescales described by the following biochemical reaction network:

\displaystyle\emptyset\to 2A,\quad B+2A\to B+A,\quad B+A\to B

\displaystyle\text{(slow reactions)},

\displaystyle\emptyset\to B,\quad B+2A\to 2A,\quad B\to\emptyset

\displaystyle\text{(fast reactions)}.

Here, the scaled copy number of
B

A
.
Consequently, the dynamics of

can be approximated by a one-dimensional process involving the reactions
A\to\emptyset\to 2A

6.4

Due to such an utility of one-dimensional Markov models, previous studies focused qualitative behaviors of one-dimensional Markov models with non-polynomial transition rates

,

Several previous studies have investigated one-dimensional Markov chains obtained through such model reductions.
In

In
[

Methods for reducing various biochemical systems to one-dimensional models were proposed in
[

, with examples including bursty gene expression, receptor–ligand interactions, and the flagellar motor of

Furthermore, it was shown that a high-dimensional Markov chain can be bounded by a coupled one-dimensional Markov chain, and this coupling approach was applied to verify ergodicity of the original system
[

]

Notably, most of the resulting one-dimensional models in these studies admit non-polynomial transition rates.

There also exist systematic approaches to model reduction for biochemical reaction systems.
In

Model reduction techniques employing intermediate species were further developed in

Given the broad applicability of one-dimensional Markov models, understanding their long-term qualitative behavior is essential.
Various properties, such as explosivity, recurrence, positive recurrence, and exponential ergodicity, have been analyzed for certain classes of one-dimensional population models, providing valuable insights into their stability and dynamics
[

However, the applicability of these previous studies is limited.
The criteria for ergodicity and exponential ergodicity proposed in

Moreover, the criteria for exponential ergodicity given in

In other words, those results are confined to Markov chains where upward transitions are limited to births of size one.

For models with multiple birth sizes, criteria for various dynamical features were provided in
[
7
]

These criteria can be easily checked using the degrees of the transition rates and the corresponding transition vectors.
Notably, in that work, the transition rates were restricted to polynomial functions.
Therefore, the applicability of such results remains limited, since, as discussed earlier, many realistic biological models can be represented by one-dimensional Markov chains whose transition rates are rational or more general non-polynomial functions.

In this paper, we provide general criteria for determining the dynamical features of one-dimensional Markov chains on non-negative integers.
Notably, our conditions are valid for arbitrary types of transition rates and for any finite set of possible jump sizes.
The dynamical properties of interest include explosivity, transience, null recurrence, positive recurrence, and exponential ergodicity.

We first establish general criteria on the arbitrary transition rates that can be used to verify these dynamical features.
The key idea underlying our approach is to generalize the classical analysis of simple birth-and-death models, whose dynamical properties can be determined by elementary calculations involving the ratio of the birth and death rates.
To achieve this generalization, we primarily employ Lyapunov function techniques.

We then apply these general conditions to derive a complete classification of one-dimensional Markov chains whose transition rates

(1)

The exponent

Motivation

Our main theoretical tool for obtaining easily verifiable sufficient conditions for various dynamical features is the construction of Lyapunov functions.
For a continuous-time Markov chain
\bar{X}
on a countable state space
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
, its infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)=\sum_{y\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}}q_{x,y}f(y)\quad\text{for each $x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}$},

(2)

for a suitable function
f
, where
q_{x,y}
denotes the transition rate from
x
to
y
.
With a Lyapunov function
f
, the generator
\mathcal{A}
can characterize long-term behaviors of
\bar{X}
, including recurrence, transience, and exponential ergodicity
[
13
,
14
]
.
In particular, if there exists a positive function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\mathbb{S}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that i)
\mathcal{A}f(x)\leq 0
and ii)
f
increases to infinity, then
\bar{X}
is recurrent.

{sec : motivation}

The construction of Lyapunov functions in this work is inspired by birth-and-death models, which are continuous-time Markov chains
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For such processes, the transition rates are given by

\displaystyle P(X(t+\Delta t)=x+1\mid X(t)=x)=b_{x}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),

\displaystyle P(X(t+\Delta t)=x-1\mid X(t)=x)=d_{x}\Delta t+o(\Delta t),

for sufficiently small
\Delta t
, where
b_{x}
and
d_{x}
are the birth and death rates, respectively, with
d_{0}=0
to ensure that
X
remains on the non-negative integers.

The following classical theorem provides a necessary and sufficient condition for recurrence of
X

[
15
]
:

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 1,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
X
be a birth-and-death process with birth rates
b_{x}
and death rates
d_{x}
.
Then
X
is recurrent if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}\prod_{y=1}^{x}\frac{d_{y}}{b_{y}}=\infty.

(3)

From (
3
), the following asymptotic criteria are derived
[
16
, Theorem 1]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{if }\frac{b_{x}}{d_{x}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x\log x}\text{ for all but finitely many }x,\\
&\text{ then $X$ is recurrent},\\
&\text{if }\frac{b_{x}}{d_{x}}\geq 1+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{q}{x\log x}\text{ for all but finitely many }x\text{ with some }q>1,\\
&\text{ then $X$ is transient}.\end{split}

(4)

Alternatively, (
4
) can be derived using the Foster-Lyapunov criteria.
More precisely, for a birth-and-death process
X
, the generator in (
2
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)=\frac{1}{d_{x}}\left(\frac{b_{x}}{d_{x}}\big(f(x+1)-f(x)\big)+\big(f(x-1)-f(x)\big)\right).

(5)

By the Foster-Lyapunov criteria for recurrence and transience (see Proposition
4.6
), it suffices to construct a suitable function
f(x)
such that (
5
) is nonpositive for sufficiently large
x
. A natural choice is
f(x)\sim x^{p}(\log x)^{q}
for some constants
p
and
q
, which immediately yields (
4
) by the inequality
\mathcal{A}f(x)<0
.
Moreover, the same Lyapunov function of the form
f(x)\sim x^{p}(\log x)^{q}
can be applied directly to one-dimensional Markov chains with multiple jump sizes to derive sharp criteria for explosivity, transience, null recurrence, and positive recurrence.
We further use the Lyapunov function
f(x)=e^{px}
for some constant
p
to derive sufficient conditions only for exponential ergodicity.
All the sufficient conditions are expressed in terms of the asymptotic behaviors of the transition rates, making them easily verifiable without additional analysis (see Remark
2.1
).
We then show that these conditions are sharp enough to fully classify the dynamical behaviors of one-dimensional Markov chains whose transition rates satisfy (
1
), based on readily checkable indices associated with the transition rates.

Remark 2.1
.

{rem : motivation}

In Section
4
, the sufficient conditions derived via Lyapunov functions for the dynamical properties of general one-dimensional Markov chains are expressed in terms of

\displaystyle H_{p}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{(\log x)(m(x)x-pv(x))}{v(x)},\quad\text{and}\quad J(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{m(x)x}{v(x)},

(6)

where
m(x)
and
v(x)
denote the mean and variance of the drift of
X
, respectively (see Definition
3.1
).

For birth-and-death processes,
H_{1}(x)
emerges naturally when the classical recurrence and transience conditions are rewritten in terms of
m(x)
and
v(x)
.
In this case,
m(x)=b_{x}-d_{x}
and
v(x)=\frac{b_{x}+d_{x}}{2}
, so that the upper part of (
4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{b_{x}}{d_{x}}=\frac{m(x)+2v(x)}{-m(x)+2v(x)}\leq 1+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x\log x}.

Hence, for large
x
, we have

\displaystyle m(x)\leq v(x)\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x\log x}\right),

(7)

which implies that
X
is recurrent.
Similarly, for transience, if there exists a constant
q>1
such that

\displaystyle m(x)\geq v(x)\left(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{q}{x\log x}\right)

(8)

for large
x
, then
X
is transient.

Each of the following inequalities implies the corresponding condition in (
7
) and (
8
), respectively:

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)<1,\quad\text{and}\quad\liminf_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)>1.

For other dynamical properties of more general one-dimensional Markov chains (i.e, multiple jump sizes), the Foster-Lyapunov approach allows the sufficient conditions to be expressed in terms of
m(x)
and
v(x)
, and thus can also be represented using
H_{p}(x)
and
J(x)
.
This approach provides a unified framework to handle both single-jump (birth-and-death) and multiple-jump cases.

3.1
Main model

{sec:main model}

Throughout this paper, we consider an irreducible conti- nuous-time Markov chain
X
on a countable state space
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle P(X(t+\Delta t)=x+\eta\mid X(t)=x)=\lambda_{\eta}(x)\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\quad\text{for small }\Delta t.

Remark 3.1

Any countable state space

Throughout this manuscript,

denotes an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on

3.3
Dynamical features of CTMC

The main goal of this paper is to provide sufficient conditions for various dynamical properties of a continuous-time Markov chain defined in Section
3.1
.
The dynamical properties we consider include explosivity, non-explosivity, transience, recurrence, null-recurrence, positive recurrence, non-exponential ergodicity, and exponential ergodicity.
While these properties are elementary, we provide precise definitions below.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\bar{X}
be an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
. Then

1.

\bar{X}
is explosive if
P(T<\infty)>0

\bar{X}
is non-explosive.

2.

\bar{X}
is recurrent if
P_{x}(\tau_{x}<\infty)=1

. Otherwise,
\bar{X}
is transient.

3.

\bar{X}
is positive recurrent if
\mathbb{E}_{x}(\tau_{x})<\infty
for some
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}
. If
\bar{X}
is recurrent but not positive recurrent, it is null-recurrent.

4.

\bar{X}
is exponentially ergodic if it is ergodic and there exist constants

and

such that

\displaystyle\|P_{x}(\bar{X}(t)\in\cdot)-\pi(\cdot)\|_{TV}\leq C(x)e^{-\eta t}\quad\text{for all $\bar{X}(0)=x$.}

If
\bar{X}
is ergodic but not exponentially ergodic, it is non-exponentially ergodic.

These dynamical features imply important statistical properties of
\bar{X}
.
For example, for an irreducible
\bar{X}
on a countable state space,

is positive recurrent if and only if there exists a unique

Definition 3.4
.

For a continuous-time Markov chain

P_{\pi}(\bar{X}(t)=x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{y}P_{y}(\bar{X}(t)=x)\pi(y)=\pi(x)

If

The one-step analysis shows that a stationary measure

\displaystyle\sum_{y}q_{y,x}\pi(y)-\sum_{y}q_{x,y}\pi(x)=0\quad\text{for each $x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}$}

Proposition 4.2

Proposition 4.13

(
[

]

{prop: null}

Let

, a finite subset
A\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{S}}
, and
f\in\text{Dom}_{+}(\mathcal{A})
that increases to infinity such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)\leq-Cf(x)\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}\setminus A.

Using this proposition, we derive criteria for exponential and non-exponential ergodicity.

Theorem 4.17

(Exponential ergodicity)
.

{thm: expo}

Assume that
X
is positive recurrent. Then
X
is exponentially ergodic if either

(a)

there exists a constant
c>2
such that
v(x)\gtrsim x^{c}
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)<1
,

(b)

v(x)\sim x^{2}
and
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}J(x)<1
, or

(c)

1\lesssim v(x)
and
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}J(x)/x<0
.

Proof.

In this proof, under each of the assumptions
(a)
,
(b)
, and
(c)
, we construct a positive function
f
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
4.16
, respectively.

For
(a)
, let
f(x)=(\log x)^{\epsilon/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{x>1\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq 1\}}
, where
\epsilon>0
is chosen such that
\limsup_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)\leq 1-\epsilon
. Applying (
36
) and using
v(x)\gtrsim x^{c}
with
c>2
, we obtain
\mathcal{A}f(x)\lesssim-f(x)
, which ensures exponential ergodicity by Proposition
4.16
.

For
(b)
, let
f(x)=x^{\epsilon/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{x>1\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq 1\}}
with
\epsilon>0
such that
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}J(x)\leq 1-\epsilon
. By (
20
) with
p_{i}
replaced by
\epsilon/2
and
v(x)\sim x^{2}
, we have
\mathcal{A}f(x)\lesssim-f(x)
, which again implies exponential ergodicity.

For
(c)
, define
f(x)=e^{(\epsilon/2)x}
with sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. By 2 of Proposition
3.1
, as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)

\displaystyle=(\epsilon/2)e^{(\epsilon/2)x}\{m(x)+(\epsilon/2)v(x)+O(\epsilon^{2})O(v(x))\}

\displaystyle=(\epsilon/2)e^{(\epsilon/2)x}v(x)\left\{\frac{J(x)}{x}+\epsilon-(\epsilon/2)+O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)\right\}.

Since
1\lesssim v(x)
and
\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}J(x)/x<0
, we can choose
\epsilon
so that
\mathcal{A}f(x)\lesssim-f(x)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we provide sufficient conditions for non-exponential ergodicity. Recall that
[
24
, Proposition 12]
and
[
25
, Theorem 2]
give a necessary and sufficient condition:
\bar{X}
is exponentially ergodic if and only if there exists
\rho>0
such that
\rho<q_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{y\neq x}q_{x,y}
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}
and
E_{x}[e^{\rho\tau_{x}}]<\infty
for all
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}
, where
\tau_{x}
is the first return time to
x
. Therefore, showing that
E_{x}[\tau_{x}^{\ell}]=\infty
for some
\ell>0
and
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}
implies non-exponential ergodicity.

An existing study provided criteria for an irreducible Markov chain
\bar{X}
on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
to have an infinite
\ell
th moment for some
\ell>0
.

Proposition 4.18

(
[
21
]
, Theorem 1.5, Corollary 2.13)
.

{prop:non-exponential}

Let
\bar{X}
be an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
.
Let
f\in\text{Dom}_{+}(\mathcal{A})
such that
|\{x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)\leq N\}|<\infty
for every
N>0
. Let
S_{a}(f)=\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(x)\leq a\}
. Suppose that there exist positive constants
a,c_{1},c_{2},k
and
r>1
such that

(i)

\mathcal{A}f(x)\geq-c_{1}
for
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}\setminus S_{a}(f)
,

(ii)

f^{r}\in\text{Dom}_{+}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{A}f^{r}(x)\leq c_{2}f^{r-1}(x)
for
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}\setminus S_{a}(f)
, and

(iii)

f^{k}\in\text{Dom}_{+}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{A}^{k}f(x)\geq 0
for

.

Then we have that
E_{x}[\tau^{\ell}_{\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{a}(f)}]=\infty
for all
x\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}\setminus S_{a}(f)
if
\ell\geq k
, where
\tau_{S_{a}(f)}
is the first return time to
S_{a}(f)
.

Note that
E_{x}[\tau^{\ell}_{\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{a}(f)}]=\infty
implies
E_{x}[\tau_{x}^{\ell}]=\infty
for each
\ell>0
and for some
x\in S_{a}(f)
. This is because there exists
x\in S_{a}(f)
such that
P_{x}(\bar{X}_{T_{1}}=y)>0
for some

. Then we have
E_{x}[\tau^{\ell}_{x}]\geq E_{y}[\tau^{\ell}_{x}]P_{x}[\bar{X}_{J_{1}}=y]=\infty
. Using this fact together with Proposition
4.18
, we now obtain criteria for non-exponential ergodicity.

Theorem 4.19

(Non-exponential ergodicity)
.

Assume that
X
is positive recurrent.

X
is non-exponentially ergodic if either

(a)

v(x)\lesssim x^{c}
for some
c<2
and
-\infty<\liminf_{x\to\infty}J(x)\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}J(x)<\infty
, or

(b)

v(x)\lesssim x^{c}
for some
c<0
.

Proof.

In this proof, under each of the assumptions
(a)
and
(b)
, we construct a positive function
f
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
4.18
.

Assume first that
(a)
holds.
By Proposition
4.18
, it suffices to find
f
and constants
r>1
and
k>0
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
,
\mathcal{A}f(x)\gtrsim-1
,
\mathcal{A}f^{r}(x)\lesssim f^{r-1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{A}f^{k}(x)\geq 0
for large
x
, since
\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{a}(f)
is finite for any constant
a
.
Define
f(x)=x^{2-c}\mathbbm{1}_{\{x>1\}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq 1\}}
on
\mathbb{S}
. Then
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
.
Substituting
p_{i}=2-c
in (
20
) yields
\mathcal{A}f(x)\gtrsim-1
.
Fix an arbitrary constant

.
Using the assumption

and replacing

with

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f^{r}(x)\lesssim\frac{x^{(2-c)r}v(x)}{x^{2}}\lesssim x^{(2-c)(r-1)}\sim f(x)^{r-1}.

Finally, take

so that

Then, by substituting

Next, assume that

Since

Then there exists no

such that

for all

.

∎

5
Criteria for transition rates with Laurent-type asymptotics

{sec : main-ctmc}

Under Assumption
3.1
, we further restrict our attention to continuous-time Markov chains
X
whose transition rates admit a
Laurent-type asymptotic expansion
, which allows us to simplify the criteria for their dynamical properties.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that the collection of transition rates
\{\lambda_{\eta}(x)\}_{\eta\in\Gamma}
of
X
is defined with a
Laurent-type asymptotic
if there exists an integer
R\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{\eta}(x)=a_{\eta}x^{R}+b_{\eta}x^{R-1}+O(x^{R-2})\quad\text{for each $\eta\in\Gamma$},\end{split}

(23)

where
a_{\eta},b_{\eta}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{\eta}>0
for at least one
\eta\in\Gamma
.
Here,
R
denotes the maximal degree among all transition rates. Note that
a_{\eta}
or
b_{\eta}
may be zero for some
\eta\in\Gamma
.

Under this assumption, both
m(x)
and
v(x)
admit Laurent-type asymptotic forms whose degrees and coefficients are directly determined as

\displaystyle m(x)=\alpha x^{R}+\gamma x^{R-1}+O(x^{R-2}),\qquad v(x)=\vartheta x^{R}+O(x^{R-1}),

(24)

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{\eta\in\Gamma}\eta a_{\eta},\quad\gamma=\sum_{\eta\in\Gamma}\eta b_{\eta},\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\eta\in\Gamma}\eta^{2}a_{\eta}.

(25)

Remark 5.1
.

If all transition rates are rational functions of
x
, then they satisfy the Laurent-type asymptotic condition.

In the previous section, we derived sufficient conditions for various dynamical properties in terms of
m(x)
and
v(x)
. These conditions, however, are not if-and-only-if, and hence do not yield a complete classification. In particular, there exist critical regimes where neither recurrence nor transience can be determined directly. For instance, if

\limsup_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)\geq 1\geq\liminf_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x),

then neither Theorem
4.8
nor Theorem
4.9
applies.
Under the Laurent-type asymptotic assumption (
23
), however, such ambiguity does not occur. Indeed, we have as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle H_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)\frac{\log x\,(\alpha x^{R+1}+(\gamma-\vartheta)x^{R})}{\vartheta x^{R}}+O\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}H_{1}(x)=\frac{\log x(\alpha x+\gamma-\vartheta)}{\vartheta}=\begin{cases}-\infty,&\text{if }\alpha\leq 0\text{ and }\min(\alpha,\gamma-\vartheta)<0,\\[3.0pt]
0,&\text{if }\alpha=\gamma-\vartheta=0,\\[3.0pt]
-\infty,&\text{if }\alpha\geq 0\text{ and }\max(\alpha,\gamma-\vartheta)>0,\end{cases}

(26)

which shows that recurrence and transience can be completely characterized in terms of the parameters
\alpha
,
\gamma
, and
\vartheta
.

In fact, under (
23
), all dynamical properties of
X
can be classified solely by the parameters
(\alpha,\gamma,\vartheta,R)
. More precisely, for each
(\alpha,\gamma,\vartheta,R)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
, one can determine whether
X
is explosive or non-explosive, whether it is transient, null recurrent, or positive recurrent, and whether it is exponentially ergodic.

In this section, we focus on the cases
R\leq 0
. The case
R\geq 1
was largely analyzed in
[
7
]
. While some parameter regimes—particularly regarding exponential ergodicity—were not fully classified there, we address those in Section
6.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

{thm: main for laurante}

Let
R\leq 0
. Then
X
is non-explosive and

(i)

transient if
\alpha>0
, or if
\alpha=0
and
\gamma>\vartheta
,

(ii)

null recurrent if
\alpha=0
and
(R-1)\vartheta\leq\gamma\leq\vartheta
,

(iii)

non-exponentially ergodic if
\alpha=0
and
\gamma<(R-1)\vartheta
, or if
\alpha<0
and
R<0
, and

(iv)

exponentially ergodic if
\alpha<0
and
R=0
.

Proof.

Note that

m(x)=x^{R}(\alpha+\gamma x^{-1}+O(x^{-2})),\qquad v(x)=x^{R}(\vartheta+O(x^{-1})),

and hence

m(x)x-v(x)=x^{R}(\alpha x+\gamma-\vartheta+O(x^{-1})).

Since
R\leq 0
, non-explosivity of
X
follows from part
2(a)
of Theorem
4.1
.
Throughout this proof, we set
c=R
.

From (
26
), case
(i)
and the recurrence in
(ii)
–
(iv)
follow directly from Theorem
4.1
.

Now assume
(ii)
.
If
(R-1)\vartheta<\gamma
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}J(x)=\gamma/\vartheta>c-1
.
If
(R-1)\vartheta=\gamma
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}H_{c-1}(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log x(\gamma-(R-1)\vartheta)}{\vartheta}=0.

In both cases, null recurrence follows from parts
6(a)
and
6(b)
of Theorem
4.1
, respectively.

Next, observe that
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}J(x)=(\alpha x+\gamma)/\vartheta<c-1
under the assumptions of either
(iii)
or
(iv)
, implying positive recurrence by part
5(a)
of Theorem
4.1
.
In case
(iii)
, the first condition yields
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}J(x)=\gamma/\vartheta
, which ensures non-exponential ergodicity by part
8(a)
of Theorem
4.1
.
The second condition similarly implies non-exponential ergodicity by part
8(b)
of Theorem
4.1
.

Finally, for case
(iv)
, since
R=0
implies
1\lesssim v(x)
and
\alpha<0
gives
\lim_{x\to\infty}J(x)/x

=\alpha<0
, exponential ergodicity follows from part
7(c)
of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

6
Applications

{sec : applications}

The main application of Theorem
5.1
lies in
stochastic reaction networks
. Reaction networks provide a graphical representation of biochemical interaction systems, such as

\displaystyle A+B\xrightarrow{\kappa_{1}}2A\xrightarrow{\kappa_{2}}\emptyset,\qquad B\xrightarrow{\kappa_{3}}A.

In general, a reaction network consists of reactions of the form

\displaystyle\nu_{i1}S_{1}+\nu_{i2}S_{2}+\dots+\nu_{id}S_{d}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{i}}\nu^{\prime}_{i1}S_{1}+\nu^{\prime}_{i2}S_{2}+\dots+\nu^{\prime}_{id}S_{d},\qquad i=1,\dots,k.

We denote such a network by
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R})
, where

\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{d}\}
is the set of
species
,

\mathcal{C}=\{\sum_{j}\nu_{ij}S_{j},\sum_{j}\nu^{\prime}_{ij}S_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i=1,\dots,k\}
is the set of
complexes
, and

\mathcal{R}=\{\sum_{j}\nu_{ij}S_{j}\to\sum_{j}\nu^{\prime}_{ij}S_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i=1,\dots,k\}
is the set of
reactions
.
The associated collection of
rate constants
is
\mathcal{K}=\{\kappa_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i=1,\dots,k\}
.
Then
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{K})
specifies a
reaction system
, which determines the corresponding stochastic dynamics.

The generator of the associated continuous-time Markov chain
\bar{X}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{R}\lambda_{i}(x)\big(f(x+\eta_{i})-f(x)\big),\qquad\text{for all }f\in\text{Dom}_{+}(\mathcal{A})\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d},

where
\eta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the
reaction vector
with components
\eta_{ij}=\nu^{\prime}_{ij}-\nu_{ij}
, representing the net change in the
i
th reaction.
As indicated by the generator, the transition of
\bar{X}
is given by
\eta_{i}
, and its corresponding rate is
\lambda_{i}
.

Under the
mass-action kinetics
, the transition rate
\lambda_{i}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(x)=\kappa_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{x_{j}!}{(x_{j}-\nu_{ij})!}\mathbbm{1}_{\{x_{j}\geq\nu_{ij}\}}.

(27)

For example, for the reaction
2S_{1}+S_{2}\xrightarrow{\kappa}S_{3}
, the corresponding transition is
\eta=(-2,-1,1)
, and the mass-action transition rate is
\lambda(x)=\kappa x_{1}(x_{1}-1)x_{2}
.
However, as previously noted, when a multi-species reaction network under mass-action is reduced to an effective one-dimensional model, the resulting transition rates can often be approximated by more general forms such as rational functions (see Section
5
).

6.1
Every ergodic single-species reaction network under mass-action is exponentially ergodic

{sec: every single}

In
[
26
]
, a special class of continuous-time Markov chains associated with reaction networks was introduced, which admits non-exponential ergodicity.
In particular, when the chain reaches a certain part of the boundary—correspon- ding to the extinction of some species—it may become trapped in a long-lasting loop, leading to slow mixing.
For example, consider the continuous-time Markov chain
X
associated with the following mass-action reaction network:

\displaystyle A+B\rightleftharpoons\emptyset,\qquad B\rightleftharpoons 2B.

When the associated Markov chain
\bar{X}
reaches a state of the form
(n,0)
for large
n
, it is likely to oscillate between
(n,0)
and
(n+1,1)
for an extended period with high probability (see Example 2.1 in
[
26
]
).
Since such boundary behavior arises from the extinction of one species, one can expect that this slow-mixing phenomenon does not occur in single-species reaction networks.

For a single-species reaction network under mass-action, one might still wonder whether non-exponential ergodicity could appear without such boundary effects, namely under either condition
8(a)
or
8(b)
of Theorem
4.1
.
We will show that under mass-action kinetics, these conditions can never be satisfied.

Exponential ergodicity of ergodic single-species reaction networks under mass-action was proved in
[
7
]
except for the case of
R=2
and
\gamma<\vartheta
. For this remaining case, we prove exponential ergodicity.

Proposition 6.1
.

{prop:R2}

Let
X
be a one-dimensional continuous-time Markov chain on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
If
R=2
,
\alpha=0
, and
\gamma<\vartheta
, then
X
is exponentially ergodic.

Proof.

We will show that when
R=2
, the conditions of part
7(b)
in Theorem
4.1
hold under mass-action kinetics.
Since
R=2
, we have
v(x)\sim x^{2}
.
Furthermore, by (
24
),
\alpha=0
and
\gamma<\vartheta
imply

\lim_{x\to\infty}J(x)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\gamma x^{2}}{\vartheta x^{2}}=\frac{\gamma}{\vartheta}<1,

which establishes the claim.
∎

Based on Proposition
6.1
and the preceding discussion, we obtain the following complete classification for single-species reaction systems under mass-action.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
X
be the continuous-time Markov chain associated with a reaction system
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{K})
under mass-action kinetics (
27
).
If
X
is ergodic, then it is exponentially ergodic.

6.2
One-dimensional Michaelis-Menten kinetics

In real biochemical systems, reaction rates do not necessarily follow mass-action kinetics. A well-known and biologically relevant example is the Michaelis-Menten kinetics, which describes the rate of enzymatic reactions where a substrate is converted into a product with the help of an enzyme
[
4
]
. In the one-dimensional case, the Michaelis-Menten mechanism can be written as

\displaystyle E+nA\rightleftharpoons C\rightarrow E+P,

(28)

where
E
is an enzyme,
n,m
are positive integers, and
C
is the enzyme–substrate complex.
If
E+nA\rightleftharpoons C
represents the formation of the complex
C
that actually occurs through a sequence of reversible binding reactions, then the rate of the reaction follows a rational function.
Specifically, when we denote by
EA_{i}
the intermediate complex formed by one enzyme
E
and
i
molecules of
A
, the elementary reversible steps are
EA_{i}+A\rightleftharpoons EA_{i+1}
, where
EA_{n}=C

[
4
,
27

Assuming that the dissociation constants of these intermediate reactions are equal to

, the quasi steady-state approximation reduces (
28
) to
nA\to P
whose the reaction intensity takes the form

\displaystyle\lambda_{c}(x;n,V,K)=\frac{Vx^{n}}{1+(x/K)+\cdots+(x/K)^{n}}=VK^{n}-VK^{n+1}x^{-1}+O(x^{-2}),

(29)

where
x
is the copy number of
A
, and
V
is the maximal rate
[
4
,
28
,
29
]
.

As a simple application of such special types of Michaelis-Menten kinetics, we can consider a birth–and-death process where each reaction rate follows (
29

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{1}A\xrightarrow{\lambda_{-c_{1}}(x;n_{1},V_{1},K_{1})}(n_{1}-c_{1})A,\quad n_{2}A\xrightarrow{\lambda_{c_{2}}(x;n_{2},V_{2},K_{2})}(n_{2}+c_{2})A.\end{split}

(30)

Then, by (
25
), we have

R=0,~\alpha=-c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}+c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}},~\gamma=c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}+1}-c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}+1},~\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}(c_{1}^{2}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}+c_{2}^{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}})

, the stochastic reaction network (

(a)

exponentially ergodic if
c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}>c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}}

(b)

c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}<c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}}

c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}=c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}}

(c)

and

,

(d)

null recurrent if
c_{1}V_{1}K_{1}^{n_{1}}=c_{2}V_{2}K_{2}^{n_{2}}
and
-\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{2}\leq K_{1}-K_{2}\leq\tfrac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{2}
.

Note that if

In this case, the same classification can also be derived using the classical results of

6.4
General rational kinetics

{sec:reduction}

As described in the Introduction, a reaction network with multiple species under mass-action can be approximated by a single-species reaction network under rational kinetics.

For example, consider a mass-action reaction system
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{K})
given by

\displaystyle\emptyset\xrightarrow{1}2A,\quad B+2A\xrightarrow{2/V}B+A,\quad B+A\xrightarrow{6/V}B,

(33)

\displaystyle\emptyset\xrightarrow{VU}B,\quad B+2A\xrightarrow{U}2A,\quad B\xrightarrow{U}\emptyset,

(34)

where the scaling parameters
U
and
V
represent the acceleration rate and the system volume, respectively.
Let
X_{A}(0)=1
and
X_{B}(0)=V
. Under this initial condition, the reaction intensities of the three reactions in (
33
) and those in (
34
) are of order
1
and
V
, respectively. Thus the system admits a natural time-scale separation.

Remarkably, the scaled process
X_{B}(t)/V
converges in probability to
x_{B}(t)
, where
x_{B}(t)
solves

\displaystyle x_{B}^{\prime}(t)=U(1-(X_{A}(X_{A}-1)+1)x_{B}),\quad x_{B}(0)=1.

This convergence follows from the relationship between deterministic and stochastic models of reaction systems
[
34
,
35
]
, which is based on the law of large numbers applied to the random time change representation:

\displaystyle\frac{X_{B}(t)}{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{X_{B}(0)}{V}+\frac{N_{1}}{V}(VUt)-\frac{N_{2}}{V}\!\left(VU\int_{0}^{t}X_{A}(X_{A}-1)\frac{X_{B}(s)}{V}ds\right)-\frac{N_{3}}{V}\!\left(U\int_{0}^{t}\frac{X_{B}(s)}{V}ds\right),

where
N_{i}(t)
’s are independent unit-rate Poisson processes.

When
U
is sufficiently large,
x_{B}(t)
rapidly converges to the steady state
\frac{1}{X_{A}(X_{A}-1)+1}
(Figure
2
c).
During this fast convergence, the copy number
X_{A}(t)
remains nearly constant, as the reactions in (
33
), which only affect
X_{A}
, occur on a much slower time scale.
Once
x_{B}
has reached its quasi-steady value
\frac{1}{x_{A}(x_{A}-1)+1}
for
X_{A}(t)=x_{A}
, a subsequent jump of
X_{A}
to
x^{\prime}_{A}
induces a quick relaxation of
x_{B}
to another quasi-steady value
\frac{1}{x^{\prime}_{A}(x^{\prime}_{A}-1)+1}
(Figure
2
c).
Although this behavior can be rigorously analyzed via singular perturbation techniques, we describe it here only heuristically.

Because
X_{B}/V
converges almost instantaneously, the stochastic process associated with (
33
)–(
34
) can be reduced to a one-dimensional model

\displaystyle A\to\emptyset\to 2A,

(35)

whose transition rates are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(x)=\frac{2x(x-1)+6x}{x(x-1)+1}=2+6x^{-1}+O(x^{2}),\qquad\lambda_{2}(x)=1,

(see Figure
2
a,b).
Hence, in the reduced model (
35
), we have
R=0
,
\alpha=0
,
\gamma=-6
, and
\vartheta=4
.
By Theorem
5.1
,
X
for (
35
) is therefore non-exponentially ergodic.

Figure 2
:
a
,
b
: The mean (a) and variance (b) of
X
in the original model (

)–(

) and in the approximation (

and

trajectories.

In general, for a two-species reaction network, suppose that the reactions are separated into
fast
and
slow
reactions such that

(a)

Y
evolves only through fast reactions catalyzed by
X
, and

(b)

X
evolves only through slow reactions catalyzed by
Y
.

Here we say that a reaction is
catalyzed by
X
if the reactant involves
X
but the reaction does not change the copy number of
X
.
For example,
X+2Y\to X+Y
is catalyzed by
X
.
Then
X
can be approximately modeled by a reduced system whose transition rates are arbitrary Laurent-type functions, including rational functions.

To formalize this reduction, it is convenient to use the notation

x^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{x!}{(x-n)!}\mathbbm{1}_{x\geq n},

for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, to express the mass-action form compactly.
Since general rational transition rates arise from such reductions, we represent rational functions in terms of

rather than

Proposition 6.3
.

{prop:general rational function}

Let
L,K\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and define
f_{1}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{K}s_{i}x^{(i)}
and
f_{2}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{L}r_{j}x^{(j)}
, where
f_{1}
is nonnegative and
f_{2}
is strictly positive for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Let
g(x)=f_{1}(x)/f_{2}(x)
.
Then there exists a reaction system
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{K})
satisfying:

1.

\mathcal{S}=\{X,Y\}
;

2.

every reaction in
\mathcal{R}
is catalyzed by
X
;

3.

each rate constant
\kappa_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
depends on parameters
V
and
U
;

4.

for the associated Markov chain
(X,Y)
with
Y(0)=O(V)
and arbitrary
X(0)=x
, if
U
is sufficiently large, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{U\to\infty}\lim_{V\to\infty}P\!\left(\sup_{\delta\leq s\leq t}\left|\frac{Y(s)}{V}-g(x)\right|>\epsilon\right)=0,

for any
t>0
,
\delta\in(0,t)
, and
\epsilon>0
, under mass-action kinetics.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\{iX+Y\xrightarrow{-s_{i}U}iX\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}s_{i}<0\}\cup\{iX+Y\xrightarrow{s_{i}U}iX+2Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}s_{i}>0\}

\displaystyle\quad\cup\{jX+2Y\xrightarrow{-r_{j}U/V}jX+Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}r_{j}<0\}\cup\{jX+2Y\xrightarrow{r_{j}U/V}jX+3Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}r_{j}>0\}.

These reactions leave
X(t)\equiv X(0)=x
invariant for all
t
, since none alters the copy number of
X
.
Hence we only track
Y(t)
, which evolves as a one-dimensional Markov chain with reactions

\displaystyle\{Y\xrightarrow{-s_{i}Ux^{(i)}}\emptyset\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}s_{i}<0\}\cup\{Y\xrightarrow{s_{i}Ux^{(i)}}2Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}s_{i}>0\}

\displaystyle\cup\{2Y\xrightarrow{-r_{j}Ux^{(j)}/V}Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}r_{j}<0\}\cup\{2Y\xrightarrow{r_{j}Ux^{(j)}/V}3Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}r_{j}>0\},

under mass-action kinetics.
The mean transition rate of
Y
thus has degree
R=2
with leading coefficient
\alpha=-Uf_{2}(x)/V<0
, ensuring non-explosivity by
[
7
]
.

By
[

, for each fixed

\lim_{V\to\infty}P\!\left(\sup_{s\leq t}\left|\frac{Y(s)}{V}-y(s)\right|>\epsilon\right)=0,

Hence, for all

\displaystyle P\!\left(\sup_{\delta\leq s\leq t}\left|\frac{Y(s)}{V}-g(x)\right|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\!\left(\sup_{\delta\leq s\leq t}\left|\frac{Y(s)}{V}-y(s)\right|>\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}\right).

Finally, suppose that some slow reactions catalyzed by

7
Discussion

In this paper, we established criteria for various dynamical properties of continuous-time Markov chains on
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
These criteria were formulated in terms of the asymptotic behaviors of the mean and variance of the transition rates (Theorem
4.1
).
Based on these results, when the transition rates admit a Laurent-type expansion (
23
), we provided a complete classification of the dynamical features of continuous-time Markov chains on
\mathbb{S}
in terms of the degrees and coefficients of their transition rates (Theorem
5.1
).

As applications of this classification, we showed that every single-species stochastic reaction system under mass-action kinetics is exponentially ergodic whenever it is positive recurrent.
Using the same framework, we further analyzed the dynamical properties of birth-and-death processes with Michaelis–Menten and Haldane kinetics.

Finally, we demonstrated that reaction networks with a single species and rational transition rates admitting Laurent-type expansions can approximate multi-species mass-action reaction systems.
Beyond reproducing the probability distributions of the original reaction systems, such one-dimensional systems can also provide insights into their dynamical behaviors.
For instance, for a multi-dimensional Markov chain
\bar{X}(t)=(\bar{X}_{1}(t),\dots,\bar{X}_{d}(t))
, let
\tau_{x_{1}}=\inf\{t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{X}_{1}(t)=x_{1}\}
.
If
P(\tau_{x_{1}}=\infty)>0
, then the entire system
\bar{X}
is transient.
That is, the projection of
\bar{X}
onto a single coordinate can determine the transience of the original system.
Similarly, if there exists
x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
|P(\bar{X}_{1}(t)=x_{1})-\pi(x_{1})|
does not decay exponentially in time
t
for the stationary distribution
\pi
, then
\bar{X}
is non-exponentially ergodic. That is, a behavior of a single coordinate of
\bar{X}
can be used to determine dynamical features of
\bar{X}
.

A similar viewpoint was already proposed in
[
8
]
, where the positive recurrence of high-dimensional reaction networks is studied via their projected Markov chains on one-dimensional state spaces.
In this sense, a one-dimensional projection can serve as a lens through which the behavior of the entire system can be examined.
Since such projections can be approximated by one-dimensional Markov chains, as shown in Section
6.4
, our classification provides a simple and effective methodology for analyzing the dynamical properties not only of one-dimensional Markov chains but also of higher-dimensional ones.

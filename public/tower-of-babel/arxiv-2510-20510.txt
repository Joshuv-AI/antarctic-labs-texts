Abstract
. Let G G be a p p -adic reductive group with p p “very large.” For any irreducible admissible representation π \pi of G G over an algebraically closed field C C of characteristic ≠ p \not=p , we define a “local character expansion” of π \pi with coefficients c 𝒪 ​ ( π ) ∈ ℚ c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\in\mathbb{Q} , that does not use the character of π \pi directly but instead use the multiplicities of degenerate Moy-Prasad types. The existence of such local character expansion for mod- ℓ \ell representations is shown in another paper of the author using a different and quicker method.

Let

,
G
,

For any

for all possible

\psi

\pi

(3)

v_{\mathcal{O}}^{>r}:=\left(\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\phi+\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x>-s}])\right)_{{s>r},(x,\phi)\in\operatorname{dMP}(s)}\in\underline{\operatorname{DM}}^{>r}

\operatorname{DI}^{>r}:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\langle v_{\mathcal{O}}^{>r}\;|\;\mathcal{O}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathrm{nil}}/\!\!\sim\rangle\subset\underline{\operatorname{DM}}^{>r}.

Equip

(4)

\begin{array}[]{llll}\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\phi+\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x>-s}])&\not=&0,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}\geq\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\phi},\\
\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\phi+\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{x>-s}])&=&0,&\text{if }\mathcal{O}\not\geq\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\phi}.\\
\end{array}

Moreover, for any
s
and any
\mathcal{O}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathrm{nil}}/\!\!\sim
there exists

such that

. From this one easily sees that

\begin{array}[]{l}G_{x_{u}\geq s_{u}}=G_{x_{v}\geq s_{v}}\\
G_{x_{u}>s_{u}}=G_{x_{v}>s_{v}}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x_{u}\geq s_{u}}=\mathfrak{g}_{x_{v}\geq s_{v}}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x_{u}>s_{u}}=\mathfrak{g}_{x_{v}>s_{v}}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x_{u}\geq-s_{u}}=\mathfrak{g}_{x_{v}\geq-s_{v}}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x_{u}>-s_{u}}=\mathfrak{g}_{x_{v}>-s_{v}}\end{array}

In particular
(s_{u},(x_{u},\phi_{u}))
and
(s_{v},(x_{v},\phi_{v}))
are connected for trivial reason.

(ii)

Consider any
i\in\{0,1,...,m\}
and arbitrary
u\in(t_{i-1},t_{i+1})
(for which we take
t_{-1}=t_{0}=0
and
t_{m+1}=t_{m}=1
).
Write
s=s_{t_{i}}
,
x=x_{t_{i}}
,
\phi=\phi_{t_{i}}
,
\tau=s_{u}
,
y=x_{u}
and
\varphi=\phi_{u}
. Then

(7)

\begin{array}[]{l}G_{x>0}\subset G_{y>0}\subset G_{y\geq 0}\subset G_{x\geq 0}\\
G_{x>s}\subset G_{y>\tau}\subset G_{y\geq\tau}\subset G_{x\geq s}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x>s}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{y>\tau}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{y\geq\tau}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{x\geq s}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{y\geq-\tau}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{x\geq-s}\\
\end{array}

It remains to show that
(s,(x,\phi))
and
(\tau,(y,\varphi))
as in
(ii)
are connected, i.e. to prove Proposition
3
in this case. Note that
\varphi
can be viewed as a coset
\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}
which is a union of
\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}
cosets:

(8)

\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}=\bigsqcup_{\chi\in\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}}\chi+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}.

The definition of DeBacker lifts implies that
\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}\geq\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}
for any degenerate
\chi
in (
8
). Suppose
\chi
is such that
\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}=\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}
. In this case
[
DeB02b
, Corollary 5.2.3]
asserts that

(9)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left(\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}\right)\cap\operatorname{Ad}(G)\Phi\subset\operatorname{Ad}(G_{y>0})\Phi\\
\implies&\left(\chi+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\right)\cap\operatorname{Ad}(G)\Phi\subset\operatorname{Ad}(G_{y>0})\Phi\subset\operatorname{Ad}(G_{x\geq 0})\Phi\subset\operatorname{Ad}(G_{x=0})\phi+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\\
\implies&\chi\in\operatorname{Ad}(G_{x=0})\phi.\end{array}

Here
\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}=\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}
is used to guarantee that
\left(\chi+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\right)\cap\operatorname{Ad}(G)\Phi
is non-empty so that the last implication in (
9
) holds. We have shown that in (
8
) we have either

(A)

\chi
is non-degenerate,

(B)

\chi\in\operatorname{Ad}(G_{x=0})\phi
, or

(C)

\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}>\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}
.

Let
N>0
be the number of
\chi
in
(B)
; this is a power of
p
because such
\chi
lie in a single orbit under
\operatorname{im}(G_{y>0}\rightarrow G_{x=0})
, which is a finite
p
-group since
G_{y>0}
is a pro-
p
group. Let
\chi_{1},...,\chi_{m}\in\mathfrak{g}_{x=-s}
be a list of
\chi
in case
(C)
. For any
\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{nil}}/\!\!\sim
we have

\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}])=N\cdot\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\phi+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}])+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{\mathcal{O}}([\chi_{j}+\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}]).

This proves the part of Proposition
3
for
v\in\operatorname{DI}^{>r}
. Next, take any
C
and
\psi
as in the introduction and a finite-length admissible
C
-representation
\pi
with
0pt(\pi)\leq r
.
We have to prove

\dim_{C}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{y\geq\tau}}(\psi_{\varphi},\pi)=N\cdot\dim_{C}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x\geq s}}(\psi_{\phi},\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\dim_{C}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x\geq s}}(\psi_{\chi_{j}},\pi).

Note that
\varphi
is a
k
-linear functional on
\mathfrak{g}_{y=\tau}=\mathfrak{g}_{y\geq\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{y>\tau}
which can be pulled back to
\mathfrak{g}_{y\geq\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{x>s}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{x=s}
. The set of its possible extensions to
\mathfrak{g}_{x=s}
is exactly the indexing set in the RHS of (
8
). Thus we have

(10)

\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{y\geq\tau}}(\psi_{\varphi},\pi)=\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{y\geq\tau}/G_{x>s}}(\psi_{\varphi},\pi^{G_{x>s}})=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x=s}}(\psi_{\chi},\pi^{G_{x>s}}).

=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\\
\chi\text{ is degenerate}\\
\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}=\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x=s}}(\psi_{\chi},\pi^{G_{x>s}})+\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\varphi+\mathfrak{g}_{y>-\tau}/\mathfrak{g}_{x>-s}\\
\chi\text{ is degenerate}\\
\mathcal{O}_{x,-s,\chi}>\mathcal{O}_{y,-\tau,\varphi}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x=s}}(\psi_{\chi},\pi^{G_{x>s}}).

=N\cdot\dim_{C}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x\geq s}}(\psi_{\phi},\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\dim_{C}\operatorname{Hom}_{G_{x\geq s}}(\psi_{\chi_{j}},\pi)

as promised. We have thus proved Proposition
3
and therefore completed the proof of Theorem
1
.

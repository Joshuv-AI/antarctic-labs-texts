Early cardiac doppler findings and venous hemodynamic burden after combined superficial venous treatment for primary lower extremity varicose veins: an exploratory retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yunkai Luo, Chenke Pan, Bin Ying, Jingjin Wu, Yanbo Lou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study was designed to explore early postoperative cardiac Doppler findings, venous structural and hemodynamic burden in patients with primary lower extremity varicose veins (LEVVs) undergoing a combined superficial venous treatment pathway. A retrospective descriptive statistical analysis was performed on preoperative and postoperative one-week postoperative transthoracic echocardiographic Doppler data from 69 patients with primary LEVVs. All patients underwent endovenous laser ablation (EVLA) of the incompetent great saphenous vein with adjunctive treatment of tributaries when indicated, including ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy, followed by standardized postoperative compression. Preoperative venous reflux volume, great saphenous vein (GSV) diameter and the extent of tributaries were quantified. Potential associations between venous hemodynamic measures and ventricular Doppler parameters were explored. Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) was applied to evaluate multiple outcomes and subgroup comparisons. In the overall paired analysis, postoperative decreases in tricuspid E-wave velocity, tricuspid E/e′ and an increase in tricuspid systolic annular velocity were nominally significant (raw P = 0.048, 0.027 and 0.031), but these findings did not remain significant after within-table FDR correction (adjusted P = 0.288, 0.279 and 0.279, respectively). Age-stratified and CEAP-stratified cardiac findings were likewise not significant after adjustment (all adjusted P ≥ 0.246). In contrast, venous severity metrics remained robust after FDR correction, including GSV diameter and shrinkage measures (adjusted P = 0.005–0.007), number of involved tributaries (adjusted P = 0.007), preoperative TAMEAN, CSA, reflux volume across CEAP classes (adjusted P = 0.027, 0.008 and 0.024, respectively). No statistically significant linear correlation was observed between preoperative reflux volume and resting cardiac Doppler parameters. Venous structural and reflux burdens correlate significantly with CEAP severity. Early cardiac Doppler changes after the combined superficial venous treatment pathway were small, nominal, and not significant after FDR correction. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than evidence that EVLA alone improves in right ventricular diastolic function.

Introduction

Lower extremity varicose veins (LEVVs), a manifestation of chronic venous insufficiency, affect millions worldwide, which impacting physical health, psychological well-being and overall quality of life [1–4]. Presently, varicose vein pathogenesis remains controversial in the international community. Dynamic venous hypertension caused by venous valve insufficiency weakened venous walls, with venous blood stasis widely recognized as one of the primary contributing factors [5]. In addition, venous return is also intricately linked to the central pump (heart) and the pressure differentials between the systemic capillaries and the right ventricle (RV) [6].

Non-invasive assessment of diastolic function is critical in this context. Among the most extensively validated and widely utilized techniques, Doppler measurements of mitral and tricuspid inflow velocities, along with annular velocities, serve as the primary methods for assessing left and right ventricular (LV and RV) diastolic function [7, 8]. An earlier study has identified an association between venous hypertension and RV diastolic dysfunction [9]. Emerging evidence indicated that invasive therapeutic modalities for varicose veins, including surgical ligation/stripping and radiofrequency ablation (RFA), primarily lower venous reflux, thereby optimizing RV diastolic compliance [10]. Notably, compared to RFA, previous studies had concluded that EVLA demonstrated superior outcomes in Venous Clinical Severity Score (VCSS) and Chronic Venous Disease Quality of Life Questionnaire (CIVIQ-20) scores at both 6 and 12 months postoperatively, with lower recurrence rates [11]. In addition, during long-term follow-up, EVLA is related to lower complication rates and higher quality of life scores in patients [12]. However, as a first-line treatment recommended worldwide, there remains a gap in studies examining the the effects of endovenous laser ablation (EVLA) for varicose veins on cardiac diastolic function and its potential mechanisms.

In the present study, we explored early changes in mitral and tricuspid Doppler parameters in patients with primary LEVVs who underwent EVLA. The main purpose was to describe venous structural and hemodynamic burden across CEAP clinical classes and to examine whether early cardiac Doppler signals were present after treatment.

Materials and methods

Study population

This retrospective study utilized data consecutively collected from patients diagnosed with saphenofemoral junction incompetence via duplex ultrasound at the Department of Ultrasound in Medicine from February 2024 to January 2025. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) was performed both before and after EVLA. The inclusion criteria included individuals aged 18 years or older, classified as C2-C6 according to the Clinical Etiological Anatomical Pathophysiological (CEAP) system [13], who underwent EVLA. The 2022 ESVS Guidelines recommend simultaneous treatment of tributaries during main trunk ablation. Moreover, ultrasound-guided foam sclerotherapy used for the management of tributaries [14]. Compared with phlebectomy, it offers the advantages of less postoperative pain and a quicker return to normal activity. Therefore, sclerotherapy was performed on its tributaries.The diagnostic criteria for saphenofemoral junction incompetence were as follows: evaluations were conducted in a physiological upright position using color and pulsed wave (PW) Doppler ultrasound to determine blood flow direction, reflux origin and reflux duration. A compression/release maneuver was applied to identify venous incompetence [15]. PW Doppler reflux time exceeding 0.5s was established as the diagnostic threshold for incompetence in the superficial venous system [16]. Formal exclusion criteria consisted of previously diagnosed and newly detected cardiac conditions that may affect cardiac Doppler parameters, including coronary artery disease, hypertension, pericardial constriction, valvular stenosis or calcification, deep vein thrombosis, and any deep venous reflux [17]. Deep venous competence was assessed by duplex ultrasound before inclusion using color and PW Doppler evaluation of the femoral and popliteal deep venous segments during compression/release and, where appropriate, Valsalva maneuvers. Patients with any detectable pathological deep venous reflux were excluded because deep venous reflux could independently alter cardiac hemodynamics. Patients with insufficient echocardiographic image quality (n = 3), which would make accurate measurement of Doppler parameters impossible, were also excluded. Finally, 69 patients were included. Age represents a major factor influencing Doppler parameters in healthy adult populations [18]. Patients were allocated into two age subgroups, with 50 years as the threshold, given that only six patients were aged between 30 and 40 years and nine patients between 40 and 50 years. Moreover, C5 and C6 CEAP clinical classes were combined due to the limited number of patients, because each category included only two patients. Echocardiographic Doppler and clinical data were collected within one week preoperatively and again within one week postoperatively. This early time point reflected the routine follow-up pathway in our institution and should be interpreted as early postoperative physiology rather than durable cardiac remodeling.

Ultrasound examination

Lower limb venous and transthoracic echocardiography were conducted by the same sonographer following standardized protocols [19, 20] utilizing a Siemens ACUSON Sequoia ultrasonic scanner. The examination of lower extremity veins utilized high-frequency ultrasound transducers (4–9 MHz), whereas transthoracic echocardiography employed low-frequency ultrasound transducers (2–5 MHz). Prior to echocardiographic assessment, heart rate and blood pressure were recorded, and compression stockings were removed during the postoperative evaluation. The cardiac examination was carried out in the left lateral position. In the apical four-chamber view using PW Doppler, mitral and tricuspid inflow velocities (E- and A-waves) were assessed to calculate their ratios (E/A ratios). Moreover, mitral and tricuspid annular velocities (e′-, a′- and s′-wave) were measured to calculate E/e′. All parameters were assessed at the end of spontaneous expiration. The diameter of the main saphenous vein was measured before and after surgery. The diameter and cross-sectional area (CSA) of GSV were measured using a cross-sectional B-mode image. After a provocation maneuver, time-averaged mean velocity (TAMEAN) and reflux time were measured using PW Doppler in a longitudinal plane with the sample volume adjusted to insonate the lumen of the vessel. The Doppler insonation angle was set at 60°. All ultrasound examinations were performed by a trained ultrasonographer experienced in venous disease.

Endovenous laser ablation

A tumescent solution comprising 1,000 mL of 0.9% saline, 50 mL of 2% lidocaine and 1 mL of epinephrine (1:1,000) was administered to safeguard the tissues and nerves surrounding the veins. A laser fiber was introduced via the Seldinger technique from the GSV at the medial malleolus to a position 1 cm distal to the saphenofemoral junction. During ablation, a bare-tipped 980 nm laser fiber (Diomed, AngioDynamics Inc., Latham, NY, USA) was withdrawn at 0.2–0.3 cm/s, with 1 s pulse duration, 1 s interval and 12 W power setting along the entire GSV trunk. Varicose tributaries were managed as part of the same superficial venous treatment pathway, including EVLA of selected varices and adjunctive foam sclerotherapy when indicated. The mean endovenous energy density applied was approximately 80 J/cm. Following the procedure, treated limbs were wrapped in sterile bandages and secured with a self-adhering compression bandage for 48 h to ensure therapeutic compression. Patients were advised to ambulate as soon as possible postoperatively while avoiding heavy lifting and strenuous activities. Subsequently, they were instructed to wear medical compression stockings (25 mm Hg at the ankle, daytime use only) for a minimum of four weeks [21]. Because all patients received EVLA, tributary treatment and postoperative compression, early postoperative cardiac Doppler findings should be attributed to the combined treatment pathway rather than to isolated truncal EVLA.

Foam sclerotherapy

The sclerosant foam was prepared by mixing the sclerosing solution (Fibrovein solution) with air at a ratio of 1: 5. Subsequently, the foam was injected into the varicose vein under ultrasound guidance [22].

Statistical analysis

SPSS 29.0 statistical software (IBM) and GraphPad Prism 10. 0 were utilized for statistical analysis. Normally distributed continuous variables were summarized as mean (standard deviation) non-normally distributed data were presented as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (percentages). According to the distribution of variables, continuous variables were compared using either the paired data t-test or ANOVA, the Mann-Whitney test or the Kruskal-Wallis test. Spearman correlation was used to analyze associations between reflux volume and cardiac Doppler parameters. Because multiple outcomes and subgroup comparisons were examined, the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure was applied within each table-specific family of related analyses. Raw and FDR-adjusted P values are reported together in the tables. Findings were considered statistically significant when the corresponding adjusted P value was < 0.05. Subgroup and secondary analyses were interpreted as exploratory. Heart rate and blood pressure were compared descriptively between the preoperative and postoperative assessments to contextualize potential preload and afterload variation; no causal adjusted model was prespecified because of the retrospective single-arm design and modest sample size.

Preoperative and postoperative overall changes in cardiac diastolic function

In the single-arm pre/post comparison, several tricuspid Doppler indices showed nominal changes after EVLA. Patients had lower early diastolic inflow velocities (tricuspid E-WV, −3.4 cm/s) and E/e′ (−0.4) postoperatively, and higher tricuspid systolic annular velocities (s′-WV, + 0.9 cm/s; raw P = 0.048, 0.031 and 0.027, respectively). However, after Benjamini-Hochberg correction within Table 1, the corresponding adjusted P values were 0.288, 0.279 and 0.279, respectively. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as small exploratory cardiac Doppler signals rather than statistically robust evidence of improved right ventricular diastolic function (Table 1; Fig. 1).

Comparison of LV and RV diastolic function between the age-stratified subgroups

Age-, CEAP-, and sex-stratified cardiac analyses were performed as exploratory secondary analyses. In patients aged 50 years and older, mitral A-WV and tricuspid E/e′ showed nominal pre/post changes, but neither remained significant after FDR correction. CEAP-stratified analyses similarly showed only nominal signals in selected strata, including tricuspid E-WV in C2 and tricuspid E/e′ in C4, all of which were non-significant after adjustment. We subsequently assessed whether the magnitude of preoperative reflux volume was directly related to cardiac Doppler parameters. In the entire cohort, Spearman correlation analysis revealed no statistically significant linear correlation between reflux volume and mitral or tricuspid Doppler parameters. Subgroup analyses stratified by CEAP class also revealed no consistent significant correlations between reflux volume and cardiac indices; the few unadjusted borderline associations did not persist after FDR correction. Detailed correlation matrices are provided in Supplementary Tables S6 and S7. These findings indicate that reflux volume reflects peripheral disease severity, but its relationship with resting cardiac Doppler parameters was not demonstrated in this dataset.

Discussion

Our findings revealed that early cardiac Doppler changes after EVLA were modest and only nominally significant before multiplicity correction. The associations between CEAP severity and venous structural/hemodynamic burden were robust to multiple-testing correction. After within-table FDR adjustment, the overall paired changes in tricuspid E-WV, tricuspid s′-WV and tricuspid E/e′ no longer met the significance threshold, and the age-stratified and CEAP-stratified cardiac analyses should likewise be viewed as exploratory. Accordingly, the central interpretation of this study is not that isolated EVLA definitively improves RV diastolic function, but that venous structural burden is strongly related to CEAP severity and that small early cardiac Doppler signals may warrant prospective evaluation.

Tricuspid Doppler parameters could be sensitive to shifts in venous capacitance and preload because the RV receives systemic venous return [23, 24]. Nevertheless, Doppler indices are affected by various physiological factors, so the minor postoperative changes seen here should be interpreted with caution. Volume overload is a predominant escalation in preload and is influenced by volume, ventricular structural remodeling and ventricular capacity [25, 26]. The nominal reduction in tricuspid E-WV and E/e′ after treatment, together with the nominal increase in tricuspid systolic annular velocity, might still be physiologically consistent with lower RV filling pressure or improved RV compliance. However, this explanation remains speculative because the present study did not directly measure central venous pressure, right atrial pressure, RV volume, RV strain or invasive hemodynamics. Postoperative compression may also decrease venous capacitance and peripheral pooling. Therefore, the observed early Doppler signals should be attributed to the combined treatment pathway rather than to EVLA alone. The nominal absolute changes in cardiac parameters were relatively small and fall within the physiological variability range of Doppler measurements. These alterations may reflect early physiological adaptation in venous return rather than immediate structural cardiac remodeling. The assessment at one week postoperatively primarily captured early postoperative physiology and may have been influenced by transient factors such as perioperative hydration, ambulation, postoperative inflammatory responses, compression bandaging and altered preload conditions. Therefore, these findings should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of sustained cardiac remodeling. Further longitudinal studies are needed to determine whether these early hemodynamic changes translate into long-term clinical benefits.

Our experimental data demonstrated no robust postoperative differences in mitral valve parameters and the apparently favorable tricuspid changes also lost statistical significance after multiplicity correction. This pattern still suggested that the RV may be more hemodynamically responsive to venous flow dynamics than the LV, which was consistent with its anatomical role as the primary reservoir for systemic venous return [25], but larger studies with prespecified cardiac endpoints are required before firm inferences can be drawn. Advanced age has been established as the predominant independent risk factor for the pathogenesis of varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency [27], even among healthy individuals, advanced age was associated with impaired RV diastolic filling [8]. In our age-stratified analysis, patients aged 50 years and older showed nominal postoperative changes in mitral A-wave velocity and tricuspid E/e′. However, these findings did not constitute robust evidence after considering the multiple-testing burden and should therefore be regarded as hypothesis-generating. Larger studies are needed to determine whether age truly modifies the cardiac response to EVLA.CEAP clinical class also appeared to influence several Doppler parameters, with nominal postoperative improvement in tricuspid E-WV in C2 and in mitral septal s′-WV and tricuspid E/e′ in C4. However, these subgroup signals arose from multiple comparisons across relatively small strata, especially the C5 + C6 group and should be interpreted cautiously. Accordingly, we have reframed these observations as exploratory rather than confirmatory evidence of CEAP specific treatment effects.

Recent clinical data further emphasize that varicose vein cohorts often include middle-aged and older patients with variable symptom duration, limb edema and skin changes, underscoring the need to interpret Doppler findings together with clinical class rather than as isolated vascular measurements [28]. Clinically, the increase in GSV diameter, tributary participation, and reflux volume across CEAP categories reflects the cumulative progression of chronic venous disease. Our findings reaffirmed that the anatomical burden of venous disease was intrinsically linked to clinical severity. A significant progressive increase was observed in GSV diameter across advancing CEAP classes (raw P = 0.003, adjusted P = 0.0053), with the C5 + C6 group exhibiting a mean diameter of 10.96 mm versus 7.60 mm in the C2 class. This finding aligned with the findings of Munteanu K et al., who discovered that the diameter of the GSV was not only an indicator of venous reflux, but may also be related to the severity of venous insufficiency [29]. At one week after the combined superficial venous treatment pathway, a 100% occlusion rate was detected using duplex ultrasound, with treated veins presenting as non-compressible fibrotic cords. The reduction in diameter also remained statistically significant after FDR correction (postoperative diameter raw P = 0.004, adjusted P = 0.0053; diameter reduction raw P = 0.002, adjusted P = 0.0053; shrinkage rate raw P = 0.007, adjusted P = 0.0070). Variations in the initial shrinkage rate across CEAP classes might indicate differences in venous wall remodeling, edema, and tissue fibrosis [14, 30, 31], though these interpretations are speculative due to the brief follow-up period and the small size(n = 4) of the C5-C6 group. Future studies with larger cohorts are required to validate the hemodynamic changes in the active and healed ulceration stages.

Our research also indicates that the transition from C2 to C3 was a critical pathophysiological tipping point. While C2 patients primarily exhibited truncal reflux with minimal tributary involvement, the increase in tributary numbers observed in C3, C4 and C5 + C6 patients remained statistically significant after FDR correction (raw P = 0.007; adjusted P = 0.0070), indicating that the development of edema and skin changes strongly associated with a widespread failure of the secondary venous network. This aligned with the 2020 update of the CEAP classification, which emphasized that advanced clinical classes reflect cumulative hemodynamic deterioration [13]. The uniform success in achieving postoperative occlusion validated the use of concomitant sclerotherapy and truncal ablation. This approach was consistent with the ESVS 2022 Guidelines, which recommended treating symptomatic tributaries to improve quality of life and reduce recurrence risk [14]. Wang et al. discovered that the combined use of EVLA and sclerosing foam can improve treatment efficacy and reduce postoperative symptoms, including venous edema and pain [32]. Early intervention is recommended. There was no significant difference in the number of C3 and C4 tributaries, implying that waiting for skin changes before intervention did not provide an anatomical advantage but causes irreversible tissue damage. Therefore, it was recommended to initiate treatment in the early stages of C2 or C3 to avoid collateral vessel recruitment and the progression of severe complications.

The quantification of reflux volume was an important addition to our study, driven by the need to mechanistically link circulatory disorders to cardiac dynamics. Our findings indicated that reflux volume was a sensitive hemodynamic marker that parallels the clinical severity of venous insufficiency. Relevant studies indicated that among patients with varicose veins, there was no correlation between the duration of reflux and the severity of the condition [33]. This was consistent with our findings. A significant increase was observed in reflux volume from the C2 to the C5 + C6 classes, driven primarily by increases in venous diameter (CSA) and flow velocity. These relationships remained statistically significant after FDR correction (TAMEAN raw P = 0.020, adjusted P = 0.0267; CSA raw P = 0.002, adjusted P = 0.0080; reflux volume raw P = 0.012, adjusted P = 0.0240). Previous studies have demonstrated that the role of vein diameter and flow velocity in the increase of reflux volume [34, 35]. Therefore, we speculate that “overall volume load” is a more accurate pathological determinant for veins than reflux time.However, an intriguing finding from our correlation analysis was the absence of a direct, linear relationship between preoperative reflux volume and resting cardiac diastolic parameters (E/e′, E-WV) in both the overall cohort and within CEAP subgroups. This seemingly contradictory phenomenon—where venous severity indices show significant correlation with CEAP classification while cardiac Doppler changes exhibit only nominal association—aligns with physiological concepts of cardiac reserve function and compensatory adaptation. The RV possesses a remarkable ability to adapt to preload changes through the Frank-Starling mechanism and unique diastolic compliance properties [36–38]. In patients with chronic venous insufficiency, the accumulation of reflux volume occurs gradually. The highly compliant right ventricle and venous capacitance vessels may effectively buffer the volume load during rest, masking linear associations [39]. The improvement observed after the combined superficial venous treatment pathway likely represents the ‘unloading’ of this chronic burden, allowing the RV to operate at a more optimal point on its compliance curve rather than correcting an overt failure. However, the distinct improvements observed in advanced CEAP classes (e.g., C4-C6) suggested that once the cumulative hemodynamic burden exceeded a certain threshold, the right ventricle became more sensitive to preload reduction following EVLA [36]. On the other hand, several methodological factors may lead to this lack of correlation. First, using two-dimensional ultrasound to calculate the inverse flow depends on the following formula:

Second, reflux parameters derived from manual compression maneuvers are subject to inter-operator variability because the generated reflux volume depends on the force and duration of the distal compression, which are difficult to standardize completely [40]. Consequently, while reflux volume theoretically represents the hemodynamic burden, the calculated values in clinical settings may have a signal-to-noise ratio that masks potential subtle correlations with cardiac dynamics. This aligns with current guidelines, which prioritize reflux time as the most reproducible standard for defining valvular incompetence [14].

Fatih Koca et al. suggested cardiac changes after superficial venous intervention [10]. Rusinovich et al. [9] and Zhang et al. [6]identified a correlation between chronic venous insufficiency and the Doppler parameters of the right heart. As previously described, a degree of discrepancy exists among these three research findings, this is probably because of variations in population traits, CEAP distribution, treatment methods, timing of evaluation, and statistical techniques. In contrast to some earlier studies, our research utilized FDR correction.Interestingly, while several preoperative differences across CEAP classes were nominally significant, they did not remain significant after FDR correction, postoperative evaluations likewise also showed no significant intergroup differences in tricuspid parameters. The apparent postoperative homogenization of cardiac parameters may still indicate attenuation of preoperative heterogeneity, but this interpretation should be considered provisional and requires confirmation in larger studies.No gender-based disparities were identified in preoperative or postoperative mitral or tricuspid parameters, signaling that biological gender did not modulate cardiac responsiveness to EVLA within this cohort. This observation contrasts with epidemiological data demonstrating disproportionate prevalence rates of varicose veins (57% in males vs. 73% in females) [41]. This finding may reflect the relatively balanced gender distribution in our cohort (51% female) or the homogenizing effect of EVLA on venous hemodynamics, independent of gender.

The therapeutic strategy also deserves careful interpretation. High ligation and stripping, RFA, EVLA, foam sclerotherapy and combined endovenous approaches can all reduce pathological superficial reflux, and high ligation with stripping may theoretically produce similar hemodynamic benefits by eliminating the incompetent reflux pathway [42]. However, these modalities differ in invasiveness, perioperative pain, recovery time, nerve injury risk, thermal profile, bruising, recurrence patterns and the need for adjunctive tributary management. The reported quality-of-life differences between RFA and EVLA are therefore unlikely to reflect a single mechanism; they may arise from differences in energy delivery, vein closure durability, postoperative pain, inflammatory response, adjunctive procedures, compression protocols and the timing of outcome measurement [11, 12, 43]. Because patients in our study received truncal EVLA, adjunctive tributary treatment and standardized compression, the present findings evaluate a real-world combined superficial venous treatment pathway rather than isolated EVLA. This point is clinically important because postoperative bandaging and compression can independently alter venous capacitance, peripheral pooling and preload, while foam sclerotherapy and treatment of tributaries can reduce the peripheral reflux reservoir beyond the effect of truncal ablation alone.

Beyond conventional procedural comparisons, emerging technologies may help refine both venous management and future research design. The Internet of Surgical Things and related Internet of Things frameworks may support perioperative data capture, remote monitoring, compression adherence tracking, ambulation assessment and integration of wearable physiologic signals with imaging-derived venous parameters [44]. For varicose vein management, these tools could help distinguish procedure-related effects from postoperative behavioral and compression-related effects, which is directly relevant to the causal uncertainty observed in the present study. Similarly, 3D printing has been increasingly discussed as a bridge between imaging, education and practice; in vascular surgery, it may facilitate patient-specific anatomical visualization, procedural simulation, trainee education and communication of complex venous anatomy [45]. Although these technologies were not used in the present cohort, they provide a practical direction for future prospective studies that combine standardized duplex ultrasound, echocardiography, longitudinal symptom assessment and objective postoperative monitoring.

Limitations

Nonetheless, this study was limited by several factors. First, the retrospective, single-center design may introduce selection bias and potential confounding factors, and the absence of an untreated, conservative-treatment, compression-only or sham comparator prevents causal attribution. Second, all patients received postoperative bandaging and compression therapy, and many underwent adjunctive tributary treatment including foam sclerotherapy; therefore, the early Doppler changes cannot be attributed to isolated truncal EVLA. This study was not designed to isolate the effect of EVLA from compression therapy or adjunctive sclerotherapy, nor to determine durable cardiac remodeling.Third, the follow-up interval was limited to one week and may capture transient postoperative physiology rather than durable cardiac remodeling. Longer follow-up at 3 and 6 months after completion of compression therapy would be more appropriate for evaluating sustained cardiac effects. Fourth, the study did not directly measure central venous pressure, right atrial pressure, RV volumes, strain or invasive hemodynamics. Fifth, the sample size was modest, and some subgroups, particularly C5 + C6, were very small. Sixth, because multiple outcomes and subgroup comparisons were analyzed, there is an increased risk of type I error; after FDR correction, several near-threshold cardiac findings no longer met statistical significance. Finally, heart rate and blood pressure were reported descriptively but were not incorporated into a multivariable causal model. Larger prospective studies with prespecified cardiac endpoints, comparator groups, standardized compression protocols and longer follow-up are warranted.

Conclusion

This exploratory study showed that venous structural and reflux burden increased with CEAP clinical severity in patients with primary LEVVs. Early postoperative cardiac Doppler changes after EVLA were small, nominal and not significant after FDR correction. Therefore, the present data should be considered hypothesis-generating and do not establish that isolated EVLA improves right ventricular diastolic function. Prospective controlled studies with prespecified cardiac endpoints and longer follow-up are needed to determine whether superficial venous treatment has sustained cardiac hemodynamic effects.

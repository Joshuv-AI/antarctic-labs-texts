Near-Linear Time Computation of Welzl Orders on Graphs with Linear Neighborhood Complexity
Jan Dreier, Clemens Kuske

Abstract
Orders with low crossing number, introduced by Welzl, are a fundamental tool in range searching and computational geometry. Recently, they have found important applications in structural graph theory: set systems with linear shatter functions correspond to graph classes with linear neighborhood complexity. For such systems, Welzl’s theorem guarantees the existence of orders with only 𝒪 ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(\log^{2}n) crossings. A series of works has progressively improved the runtime for computing such orders, from Chazelle and Welzl’s original 𝒪 ⁡ ( | U | 3 ​ | ℱ | ) \mathcal{O}(|U|^{3}|\mathcal{F}|) bound, through Har-Peled’s 𝒪 ⁡ ( | U | 2 ​ | ℱ | ) \mathcal{O}(|U|^{2}|\mathcal{F}|) , to the recent sampling-based methods of Csikós and Mustafa. We present a randomized algorithm that computes Welzl orders for set systems with linear primal and dual shatter functions in time 𝒪 ⁡ ( ‖ S ‖ ​ log ⁡ ‖ S ‖ ) \mathcal{O}(\|S\|\log\|S\|) , where ‖ S ‖ = | U | + ∑ X ∈ ℱ | X | \|S\|=|U|+\sum_{X\in\mathcal{F}}|X| is the size of the canonical input representation. As an application, we compute compact neighborhood covers in graph classes with (near-)linear neighborhood complexity in time 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \mathcal{O}(n\log n) and improve the runtime of first-order model checking on monadically stable graph classes from 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 5 + ε ) \mathcal{O}(n^{5+\varepsilon}) to 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 3 + ε ) \mathcal{O}(n^{3+\varepsilon}) .

Twin partitions

Consider a bipartite graph
G=(A,B,E)
.
Two vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in A
are

if
N(v)=N(v^{\prime})
.
Note that the twin relation is an equivalence relation. We call the (unique) partition of

into twin equivalence classes the

of
A

The definition of twin classes provides a natural way to express the notion of neighborhood complexity.

\pi_{G}(n):=\max_{A^{\prime}\subseteq A,|A^{\prime}|\leq n}|\mathcal{B}_{A^{\prime}}|\textnormal{\quad\quad where $\mathcal{B}_{A^{\prime}}$ is the twin partition of $B$ in $G[A^{\prime},B]$}.

\pi^{*}_{G}(n):=\max_{B^{\prime}\subseteq B,|B^{\prime}|\leq n}|\mathcal{A}_{B^{\prime}}|\textnormal{\quad\quad where $\mathcal{A}_{B^{\prime}}$ is the twin partition of $A$ in $G[A,B^{\prime}]$}.

Use
X\symdiff Y
to denote the symmetric difference between two sets
X
and
Y
.
We say
v,v^{\prime}\in A
are
k
-near twins
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, if
|N(v)\symdiff N(v^{\prime})|\leq k
.
For a bipartite graph
G=(A,B,E)
, a partition
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell}\}
of the vertex set
A
,
and a set
P^{\prime}\subseteq A
of representatives with
P^{\prime}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}\}
,
we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a
k
-near twin partition with representatives
P^{\prime}
if for all
i
we have
v_{i}\in P_{i}
and every

is a
k
-near twin of
v_{i}
.
Note that
0
-near twins are twins, and that a
0
-near twin partition is the twin partition.

Relation between twin partitions and Welzl orders

Before introducing the algorithm, we examine the relation between twin partitions and crossing numbers, as this connection is central to our approach.

The first observation, already made by Welzl
[
15
]
, can be restated as follows: when computing an order on
A
with low crossing number, we can temporarily ignore the twins in
A
without increasing the crossing number.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G=(A,B,E)
be a set system.
For any order on
A
, duplicating a vertex (that is, inserting a new vertex
a^{\prime}
with
N(a)=N(a^{\prime})
immediately after an existing vertex
a
in the order) does not change its crossing number.

Proof.

Consider an order
\prec
on
A
and a vertex
a
with successor
a^{\prime\prime}
.
We now insert a new vertex
a^{\prime}
with
N(a^{\prime})=N(a)
immediately after
a
, but before
a^{\prime\prime}
.
Fix any
b\in B
.
The new pair
(a,a^{\prime})
contributes no crossing since
a\in N(b)\iff a^{\prime}\in N(b)
.
Moreover, if a new crossing
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
appears, then previously a crossing
(a,a^{\prime\prime})
existed, which now vanishes.
Hence, the crossing number is unchanged.
∎

A subroutine of our algorithm contracts the twin partition of
A
into a set of representatives (while remembering for every removed vertex its corresponding representative).

Lemma

2.1
ensures that all removed vertices can later be reinserted next to their representative in the final order without increasing the crossing number.

The second observation deals with
near twins
in
B
. When computing an order on
A
, we can replace a
k
-near twin partition of
B
by its representatives, increasing the crossing number by at most
2k
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=(A,B,E)
be a set system and let
\mathcal{B}
be a
k
-near twin partition of
B
with representatives
B^{\prime}
.
Any order on
A
with crossing number at most
m
on
G[A,B^{\prime}]
has crossing number at most
m+2k
on
G
.

Proof.

Let
\prec
be an order on
A
with crossing number at most
m
on
G[A,B^{\prime}]
.
For any
b\in B
, let
b_{\mathrm{rep}}\in B^{\prime}
be its representative in
\mathcal{B}
.
By definition,
|N(b)\symdiff N(b_{\mathrm{rep}})|\leq k
.

The crossing number of
\prec
with respect to
b
counts adjacent pairs
(a,a^{\prime})
where exactly one of
a,a^{\prime}
is in
N(b)
.
An adjacent pair
(a,a^{\prime})
contributes differently to the crossing numbers for
b
and
b_{\mathrm{rep}}

only if at least one of
a,a^{\prime}
lies in
N(b)\symdiff N(b_{\mathrm{rep}})
.
Since each vertex participates in at most two adjacent pairs (with its predecessor and successor),
the number of pairs that contribute differently is at most
2k
.
Thus the crossing number for
b
differs from that for
b_{\mathrm{rep}}
by at most
2k
.

Since
b_{\mathrm{rep}}\in B^{\prime}
and the order has crossing number at most
m
on
G[A,B^{\prime}]
,
the crossing number for
b
is at most
m+2k
.
This holds for all
b\in B
, proving the claim.
∎

3.1
Bounding the number of iterations

Theorem 3.1
.

Given a set system
G=(A,B,E)
of linearity
c\geq 1
,
the main loop of
Figure

1
terminates after at most
\log|A|-1
iterations.

Proof.

Let
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
be the values of
A_{\mathrm{cur}}
and
B_{\mathrm{cur}}
at the beginning of the
i
th iteration of the main loop.
Thus,
A_{1}=A
and
B_{1}=B
.
We argue that in the
i
th iteration,
|A_{i+1}|\leq 1/2\cdot|A_{i}|+c^{2}
.

Using the size bound
|W_{i}|=\left\lceil|A_{i}|/(2c^{2})\right\rceil
for the set
W_{i}
sampled in the
i
th iteration,
and the fact that
G
has linearity
c
, we obtain

|A_{i+1}|\leq c\cdot|B_{i+1}|\leq c^{2}\cdot|W_{i}|=c^{2}\cdot\left\lceil\frac{|A_{i}|}{2c^{2}}\right\rceil<c^{2}\left(\frac{|A_{i}|}{2c^{2}}+1\right)=\frac{1}{2}|A_{i}|+c^{2}.

We now unfold the recurrence. By induction,

|A_{i+1}|\leq\frac{|A|}{2^{i}}+c^{2}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{2}+\cdots+\tfrac{1}{2^{i-1}}\Bigr).

The geometric sum is bounded by
2
, and, hence,

|A_{i+1}|\leq\frac{|A|}{2^{i}}+2c^{2}.

The main loop terminates after iteration

|A_{i+1}|\leq 12c^{2}\cdot\log|A|
. Therefore, it is sufficient
to find
i
such that

\frac{|A|}{2^{i}}+2c^{2}\leq 12c^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{|A|}{2^{i}}\leq 10c^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2^{i}\geq\frac{|A|}{10c^{2}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i\geq\log(|A|)-\log(10c^{2}).

Thus, we stop after at most
\log(|A|)-\log(10c^{2})

\log|A|-1
.
∎

3.2
Bounding the crossing number

Having established the bound on the number of iterations, it remains to analyze the resulting crossing number.

Theorem 3.2
.

Given a set system
G=(A,B,E)
of linearity

1

Proof.

We analyze the crossing number of the output order assuming the algorithm does not return

Let

Lemma 3.5

Let

3.4
Bounding the failure probability

Intuitively, a sample is bad if there exist
b,b^{\prime}\in B
with a large symmetric difference
|N(b)\symdiff N(b^{\prime})|\geq 6c^{2}\log N
such that the sample
W
contains no vertex from this symmetric difference, and therefore fails to distinguish
b
and
b^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G=(A,B,E)
be a bipartite graph with linearity
c\geq 1
,
and let
X\subseteq A
with
|X|\geq 6c^{2}\log N
for some
N\geq|A|
.
For
W\subseteq A
sampled uniformly at random of size
|W|=\left\lceil|A|/(2c^{2})\right\rceil
, we have

\Pr\!\left[\,X\cap W=\emptyset\,\right]\ \leq\ N^{-3}.

Proof.

Since
W
is chosen uniformly among all subsets of this size, we have

\Pr[X\cap W=\emptyset]\;=\;\frac{\binom{|A|-|X|}{|W|}}{\binom{|A|}{|W|}}\;\leq\;\prod_{i=0}^{|W|-1}\frac{|A|-|X|-i}{\,|A|-i\,}\;\leq\;\left(1-\frac{|X|}{|A|}\right)^{\!|W|}\;\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{|W|}{|A|}|X|\right).

With
|W|=\left\lceil|A|/(2c^{2})\right\rceil
and
|X|\geq 6c^{2}\log N
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pr[X\cap W=\emptyset]\;\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{|W|}{|A|}|X|\right)\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\cdot 6c^{2}\log N\right)\;

\displaystyle=\;\exp\!\left(-3\log N\right)\;

\displaystyle=\;N^{\tfrac{-3}{\ln 2}},\;

\displaystyle\leq\;N^{-3},

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G=(A,B,E)
be a bipartite graph of linearity
c\geq 1
and let
N\geq|A|
.
For a uniformly random sample
W\subseteq A
of
size
|W|=\lceil|A|/2c^{2}\rceil
,
the probability that there are
b,b^{\prime}\in B
with
N(b)\cap W=N(b^{\prime})\cap W
and

|N(b)\symdiff N(b^{\prime})|\geq 6c^{2}\log N
is at most
c^{2}/N
.

Proof.

As the graph has linearity
c
, the vertices in
B
have at most
c\cdot|A|
distinct neighborhoods in
A
.
To prove this lemma, it is sufficient to restrict the choices of
b,b^{\prime}
to representatives of the
\leq c\cdot|A|
twin classes of
B
.
We can hence assume without loss of generality that
|B|\leq c\cdot|A|
.
Fix
b,b^{\prime}\in B
.
By
Lemma

3.7
with
X=N_{G}(b)\symdiff N_{G}(b^{\prime})
we have

\Pr\bigl[|X|\geq 6c^{2}\log N\textnormal{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }X\cap W=\emptyset\,\bigr]\leq N^{-3}.

There are
\binom{|B|}{2}<|B|^{2}\leq c^{2}|A|^{2}
unordered pairs
b,b^{\prime}\in B
that could satisfy this condition.
By the union bound, the probability that
any
such pair satisfies it is at most

c^{2}|A|^{2}/N^{3}\leq c^{2}/N
, proving the lemma.
∎

We now bound the probability that the algorithm returns
false
.
Recall that the algorithm returns
false
only when the guarantee check fails,
which happens when the sampled partition
\mathcal{B}
is not a
(6c^{2}\log|A|)
-near twin partition.

Theorem 3.9
.

Figure

1
returns
false
with probability at most
1/3
.

Proof.

Let
G=(A,B,E)
be the input graph.
In a given iteration of the main loop, let
G[A_{\mathrm{cur}},B_{\mathrm{cur}}]
be the current graph and
W\subseteq A_{\mathrm{cur}}
the sample.
We call
W

bad
if for the resulting partition
\mathcal{B}
there exist
b,b^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{cur}}
in the same part of
\mathcal{B}
with

|(N(b)\cap A_{\mathrm{cur}})\symdiff(N(b^{\prime})\cap A_{\mathrm{cur}})|>6c^{2}\log|A|.

The algorithm only fails the guarantee check and returns
false
if
W
is bad.
By
Lemma

3.8
applied to the current graph with
N=|A|
, the probability that
W
is bad is at most
c^{2}/|A|
.

By
Theorem

3.1
, there are at most
\log|A|
iterations.
By the union bound, the probability that any iteration has a bad sample is at most
\log|A|\cdot c^{2}/|A|
.
The main loop only executes when
|A_{\mathrm{cur}}|>12c^{2}\log|A|
,
so in particular

.
Therefore,

\log|A|\cdot\frac{c^{2}}{|A|}\leq\frac{c^{2}\log|A|}{12c^{2}\log|A|}=\frac{1}{12}<\frac{1}{3}.\qed

4
Extension for higher VC-dimensions

By changing some parameters in our algorithm, we can extend it to compute Welzl orders for set systems with higher VC-dimension,
as claimed in
Theorem

1.3
. We sketch the main changes and omit routine details.
For set systems whose primal and dual shatter functions are bounded by
c\cdot k^{d}
, this extension returns an order with crossing number
\mathcal{O}(|A|^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\cdot\log^{2}|A|)
while preserving the near-linear runtime.

We again shift to our representation of a set system as a bipartite graph
G=(A,B,E)
.
We denote the color classes at the beginning of an iteration by
A_{\mathrm{cur}}
and
B_{\mathrm{cur}}
.
We capture bounded primal and dual VC-dimension by assuming the primal and dual shatter functions satisfy
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}(k),\pi^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}(k)\leq c\cdot k^{d}
for some
c\geq 1
and
d\geq 2
.
There are three adjustments in the extended algorithm:

•

reduce the sample size to
\big\lceil\left(\tfrac{|A_{\mathrm{cur}}|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\big\rceil
,

•

change the size bound in the loop invariant to
4\cdot c^{d+1}\cdot(2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\cdot\log|A|
, and

•

raise the near-twin parameter to
12c\cdot d\cdot|A|^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\cdot\log|A|
.

The adjusted algorithm can be found in

.

(b)

4.1
Number of iterations for higher VC-dimensions

Theorem 4.1

(Extension of
Theorem

3.1
for higher VC-dimensions)
.

Given a set system
G=(A,B,E)
with
\max\{\pi_{G}(k),\pi^{*}_{G}(k)\}\leq c\cdot k^{d}
for
c\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
the modified algorithm terminates after at most
\log|A|-1
iterations.

Proof.

Let
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
be the values of
A_{\mathrm{cur}}
and
B_{\mathrm{cur}}
at the beginning of the
i
th iteration of the main loop. Thus,
A_{1}=A
and
B_{1}=B
.

With this new sample size, together with our approach of finding near-twins on each color class once and polynomial neighborhood complexity, we get the following reduction of the size of the graph in step
i
.

|A_{i+1}|\leq c\cdot(c\cdot|W_{i}|^{d})^{d}=c^{d+1}\big\lceil\left(\frac{|A_{i}|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\big\rceil^{d^{2}},

where
W_{i}
denotes the sample in iteration
i
.

Now let
x_{i}=\left(\frac{|A_{i}|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}
, so
|W_{i}|\leq x_{i}+1
. Then

|A_{i+1}|\leq c^{d+1}\cdot(x_{i}+1)^{d^{2}}=c^{d+1}\cdot x_{i}^{d^{2}}\cdot(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{i}})^{d^{2}}=c^{d+1}\cdot\frac{|A_{i}|}{4c^{d+1}}\cdot(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{i}})^{d^{2}}=\frac{|A_{i}|}{4}\cdot(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{i}})^{d^{2}}.

Thus, to ensure
|A_{i+1}|\leq\tfrac{|A_{i}|}{2}
, it is sufficient to require
(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{i}})^{d^{2}}\leq 2
, which is true for
x_{i}\geq 2d^{2}
, as

(1+\tfrac{1}{2d^{2}})^{d^{2}}=\left((1+\tfrac{1}{2d^{2}})^{2d^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\leq e^{1/2}\leq 2.

We enforce
x_{i}\geq 2d^{2}
by increasing the loop invariant to
4c^{d+1}(2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\cdot\log|A|
. We know that
|A_{i}|
is greater than that value in each iteration. Therefore, we have

x_{i}>\left(\frac{4c^{d+1}(2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\cdot\log|A|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}=\left((2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\cdot\log|A|\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\geq\left((2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}=2d^{2}.

Putting it all together, we get

|A_{i+1}|\leq\frac{|A_{i}|}{4}\cdot(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{i}})^{d^{2}}\leq\frac{|A_{i}|}{4}\cdot(1+\tfrac{1}{2d^{2}})^{d^{2}}\leq\frac{|A_{i}|}{4}\cdot e^{1/2}\leq\frac{|A_{i}|}{2}.

With this reduction in each iteration, together with the loop invariant, we bound the number of iterations to
\log|A|-\log\left(4\cdot c^{d+1}\cdot(2d^{2})^{d^{2}}\cdot\log|A|\right)\leq\log|A|-1
, which proves the claim.
∎

4.2
Failure probability for higher VC-dimensions

Lemma 4.2

(Extension of
Lemma

3.7
for higher VC-dimensions)
.

Given a set system
G=(A,B,E)
with
\max\{\pi_{G}(k),\pi^{*}_{G}(k)\}\leq c\cdot k^{d}
for
c\geq 1
,
d\geq 2
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
X\subseteq A
with
|X|\geq 12c\cdot d\cdot N^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\cdot\log N
for some
N\geq|A|
.
For
W\subseteq A
sampled uniformly at random of size
|W|=\lceil\left(\frac{|A|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\rceil
, we have

\Pr\!\left[\,X\cap W=\emptyset\,\right]\ \leq\ N^{-3d}.

Proof.

As before, since
W
is chosen uniformly among all subsets of this size, we have

\Pr[X\cap W=\emptyset]\;\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{|W|}{|A|}|X|\right).

We first compute a bound for
\frac{|W|}{|A|}
for
|W|=\big\lceil\left(\frac{|A|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\big\rceil
.

\frac{|W|}{|A|}\geq\frac{1}{|A|}\cdot\left(\frac{|A|}{4c^{d+1}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}=\frac{1}{|A|}\cdot\frac{|A|^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}{(4c^{d+1})^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}\geq\frac{1}{|A|}\cdot\frac{|A|^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}{4c}=\frac{|A|^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}-1}}{4c}=\frac{1}{4c\cdot|A|^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}.

Since
1/d^{2}\leq 1
and
(d+1)/d^{2}\leq 1
for
d\geq 2
, and both
4\geq 1
and
c\geq 1
, we have
4^{1/d^{2}}\leq 4
and
c^{(d+1)/d^{2}}\leq c
, giving
(4c^{d+1})^{\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\leq 4c
.

With
|X|\geq 12c\cdot d\cdot N^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\cdot\log N
and
N\geq|A|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pr[X\cap W=\emptyset]\;\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{|W|}{|A|}|X|\right)\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{4c\cdot|A|^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}\cdot|X|\right)\;

\displaystyle=\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{4c\cdot|A|^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}}\cdot 12c\cdot d\cdot N^{1-\tfrac{1}{d^{2}}}\log N\right)\;

\displaystyle\leq\;\exp\!\left(-3\cdot d\cdot\log N\right)\;

\displaystyle=\;N^{-\tfrac{3d}{\ln 2}},\;

\displaystyle\leq\;N^{-3d}.

This proves the claim.
∎

The proof of
Lemma

3.8
carries over almost verbatim: as we have polynomial neighborhood complexity, we may restrict to representatives of the twin classes of
B
and assume
|B|\leq c\cdot|A|^{d}
for
c\geq 1
and
d\geq 2
. Hence,
|B|^{2}\leq c^{2}\cdot|A|^{2d}
,
and the union bound gives a per-iteration failure probability of at most
c^{2}\cdot|A|^{2d}\cdot N^{-3d}\leq c^{2}\cdot N^{-d}\leq c^{2}/N
(using
N\geq|A|
and
d\geq 2
).

With the modified analogue of
Lemma

3.8

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background and motivation

In this paper, we investigate pointwise estimates of fundamental solutions for higher order Schrödinger equations under finite rank perturbations. The equations are given by

{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=H_{N}u(x,t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

where

H_{N}=(-\Delta)^{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,P_{j}=\langle\cdot\,,\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j},

(1.2)

and
\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}
,
1\leq j\leq N
. Here and in the rest of the paper,
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The fundamental solution is a basic tool in partial differential equations and it plays a central role in developing potential and regularity theory
for solutions of related PDEs.
We mention in particular the recent paper
[
30
]
where fundamental solution is used in the regularity problem of poly-harmonic boundary value problems and
[
1
]
for applications to the theory of layer potentials.

In the second order case, it is well known that the fundamental solution of the free Schrödinger equation is given by

e^{{\rm i}t\Delta}(x,y):=(4\pi{\rm i}t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{{\rm i}|x-y|^{2}}{4t}},\qquad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.3)

It implies immediately the following dispersive estimate

\|e^{{\rm i}t\Delta}\|_{L^{1}-L^{\infty}}\leq C|t|^{-\frac{n}{2}},\qquad\,\,t\neq 0.

(1.4)

Motivated by nonlinear problems, the extension of (
1.4
) to the Schrödinger operators
-\Delta+V(x)
has evoked considerable interest in the past three decades, and we refer to
[
23
,
42
,
34
]
for pioneering works on this theme as well as the surveys
[
36
,
37
]
.

Higher order Schrödinger equations are important in modeling spinless, weakly relativistic particles and quantum mechanical systems. Notably, the bi-Laplacian case was introduced by Karpman-Shagalov
[
24
]
to investigate the stabilizing role of higher-order dispersive effects on soliton instabilities in light propagation, For further related research, we refer to
[
2
,
3
]
.

From the perspective of fundamental solutions, the higher-order case does not admit a simple closed-form expression as (
1.3
).
A further distinction between higher-order and second-order
Schrödinger equation is the presence of additional spatial decay.
Indeed, it is known that (see e.g. in
[
31
]
):

|e^{-{\rm i}t(-\Delta)^{m}}(x,y)|\leq C|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\left(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\right)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\qquad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.5)

We refer to
[
20
,
26
,
31
]
for fundamental solution estimates of
e^{{\rm i}tP(D)}
with general elliptic operator
P(D)
.

On the other hand, the theory of finite rank perturbations originates from Weyl’s seminal work in
1910
[
41
]
, where these perturbations were first utilized to determine the spectrum of Sturm-Liouville operators.
They also arise in various mathematical physics problems,
for example, Simon and Wolff
[
38
]
found a relationship between rank one perturbation and discrete random Schrödinger operators, and applied it to the Anderson localization problem.
It deserves to mention that despite the formal simplicity, the study of this restrictive perturbation is very rich and interesting, we refer to
[
39
]
for the spectral analysis and the survey paper
[
29
]
for other problems.

Nier and Soffer
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
first investigated

L^{p}
decay estimates in the second order case. Specifically, they showed that when
m=1
in (
1.2
) and
n\geq 3
,

\|e^{-itH}\|_{L^{p}-L^{p^{\prime}}}\leq C(N,n,\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N})\cdot t^{-n(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})},\quad\,\,\,\,t>0,\quad\,1<p\leq 2

(1.6)

holds under suitable conditions on each
\varphi_{j}
. This was later extended
in
[
4
]
to include the endpoint case
p=1
and lower spatial dimensions
n=1,2
.

Aim and motivation.
In light of the existing results for the second order case mentioned above, the following question arises naturally: Is the pointwise estimate (
1.5
) still valid in the presence of finite rank perturbations?
Our goal is to establish the pointwise estimates of the fundamental solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for all spatial dimensions
n\geq 1
and for every pair of positive integers
m>1
and
N\geq 1
.

1.2.
Rank one perturbations

We begin by considering rank one perturbations (
N=1
in (
1.2
)). More precisely, let

{H}={H}_{0}+\alpha\left\langle\cdotp,\varphi\right\rangle\varphi,\ \ {H}_{0}={(-\Delta)}^{m},\ \ \alpha>0,

(1.7)

where
\varphi\in L^{2}
.
Before stating our assumptions on
\varphi
, we highlight the crucial role played by the following function

F(z):=\langle((-\Delta)^{m}-z)^{-1}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\int{\frac{d\mu_{H_{0}}^{\varphi}(\lambda)}{\lambda-z}},

(1.8)

where
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
. This can be viewed as the Borel transform of the spectral measure
d\mu_{H_{0}}^{\varphi}
for
\varphi
(here
\mu_{H_{0}}^{\varphi}((a,b))=\langle E_{(a,b)}(H_{0})\varphi,\varphi\rangle
). Moreover, if
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
with
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, where
L^{2}_{\sigma}:=\{(1+|x|)^{-\sigma}f:\,f\in L^{2}\}
, then by the well-know limiting absorption principle (see e.g. in
[
27
]
), the boundary values

F^{\pm}(\lambda):=\langle((-\Delta)^{m}-\lambda\mp{\rm i}0)^{-1}\varphi,\varphi\rangle,

(1.9)

are defined when
\lambda\neq 0
and coincide on
(-\infty,0)
.

Assumption 1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
\varphi
be a real-valued,
L^{2}
-normalized function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the following conditions hold:

(i)
(Decay and smoothness)   There exists a constant
M>0
such that

|\varphi(x)|\leq M\langle x\rangle^{-\delta},

(1.10)

where

\delta>\left\{\begin{aligned} &\ n+3/2,&n\geq 2m,\\
&\ 2m+1,&1\leq n<2m.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

Moreover, when
n>4m-1
, we assume that
\varphi(x)\in C^{\beta_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\beta_{0}=[\frac{n+5}{4}]-m
, and

|\partial^{\beta}_{x}\varphi(x)|\leq M\langle x\rangle^{-\delta},\,\,\,\,\,|\beta|\leq\beta_{0},\,\,\,\,\,\delta>n+\frac{3}{2}.

(1.12)

(ii)
(Spectral assumption)    There is an absolute constant
c_{0}>0
such that

|1+\alpha F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})|\geq c_{0},\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\lambda>0,

(1.13)

where
F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
is given by (
1.9
).

Nier and Soffer
[
33
, Lemma 2.6]
showed that the assumption (
1.13
), together with some decay condition on
\varphi
, yields that the spectrum of
H
is absolutely continuous for all
n\geq 3
. Furthermore, it is proved in
[
4
, Lemma B.1]
that this is true for all
n\geq 1
.

We state our first result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
H
be given by (
1.7
). Suppose that Assumption
1
is satisfied, then there exists an absolute constant
C=C(\alpha,\varphi)>0
such that
{e}^{-{\rm i}tH}
has integral kernel satisfying

\left|K(t,x,y)\right|\leq C|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\ \ t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.14)

We make the following remarks related to Theorem
1.1
.

As far as we are aware, Theorem
1.1
seems to provide the first pointwise estimates for the fundamental solution of
{e}^{-{\rm i}tH}
(
H
is given by (
1.7
)) in any dimension
n\geq 1
. Moreover, the upper bound in (
1.14
) is also sharp compared to the free case (
1.5
).

As an immediate application, Theorem
1.1
implies the following dispersive estimate

\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}\|_{L^{1}-L^{\infty}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}},\qquad\,\,t\neq 0,

(1.15)

as well as the Strichartz-type estimate (see e.g. in
[
25
]
): For
n>2m
, and let
\frac{2m}{q}+\frac{n}{r}=\frac{n}{2}
and
2\leq q\leq\infty
, then

\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(1.16)

In recent years, there has been a surge of interest in the dispersive estimates for higher order Schrödinger operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
.
Local dispersive estimates have been established by Feng, Soffer, and Yao
[
12
]
, as well as Feng, Soffer, Wu, and Yao
[
13
]
.
For fourth-order Schrödinger equations, Erdoǧan, Green and Toprak
[
9
]
derived global dispersive estimates for three-dimensional cases, with Green and Toprak
[
18
]
later extending these results to four-dimensional scenarios. Subsequent work by Soffer, Wu, and Yao
[
40
]
, along with Li, Soffer, and Yao
[
28
]
, demonstrated dispersive bounds in 1-D and 2-D settings, respectively. Moreover, Erdoǧan, Goldberg, and Green
[
8
]
established dispersive estimates for scaling-critical potentials under the condition
2m<n<4m
and
m>1
. For
n>2m
, Erdoǧan and Green
[
10
,
11
]
obtained dispersive estimates by examining the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators. Most recently, pointwise estimates for the fundamental solution of
e^{-{\rm i}tH}
in odd dimensions were proved in
[
5
]
.

We briefly outline the strategy employed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Inspired by the work on Schrödinger operators, our starting point is to represent the propagator via the Stone’s formula. The peculiarity of the rank one perturbation is the so-called Aronszajn-Krein formula (see (
2.2
)). As usual, we treat the high and low energy parts respectively. One of the key ingredients throughout the proof is to take suitable space-time decompositions when estimating various oscillatory integrals appeared in both the high and low energy regimes. This strategy ultimately leads to a simplification of the problem to the analysis of certain 1-dim oscillatory integrals, as detailed in Lemmas
2.7
and
2.6
.

In the high energy part, we write the kernel of
e^{-{\rm i}tH}-e^{{\rm i}tH_{0}}
in the form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{I_{n}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)G_{1}(x,x_{1})G_{2}(x_{2},y)\,{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}},

(1.17)

where
I_{n}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)
stands for the following type of oscillatory integrals

\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda\left(|x-{x}_{1}|+|{x}_{2}-y|\right)}\frac{\psi(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)}{1+\alpha F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})}{\rm d}\lambda,

(1.18)

where
r_{0}>0
is some fixed constant.
The explicit expression of
G_{1},G_{2},
and
\psi
depend on the dimension.
Since the behavior of the free resolvent differs in different dimensions, we shall divide the analysis into two different cases:
n<4m
and
n\geq 4m
. When
n\geq 4m
, the magnitude of
\lambda
in (
1.18
) may become excessively large. To overcome this difficulty, we borrow ideas from
[
6
,
4
]
and employ integration by parts arguments with respect to the oscillating term
e^{{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}
.

In the low-energy regime, the symbol
|\xi|^{2m}
(
m>1
) is degenerate at
\xi=0
when compared to the the second order case, and the asymptotic behavior of the resolvent
R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
at zero energy is significantly more intricate. Further, the function
\frac{1}{1+\alpha F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})}
exhibits distinct asymptotic behaviors near zero,
depending on whether
n>2m
and
n\leq 2m
. It deserves to mention that when
n\leq 2m
, the behavior is also determined by the vanishing moment conditions of
\varphi
, particularly whether
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{x^{\gamma}\varphi}=0
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
To establish the asymptotic properties in this case, we employ appropriate expansions of the free resolvent (see Lemma
4.3
-
4.5
). Moreover, in the process of estimating the fundamental solution, we shall rewrite
R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi
in the following form

(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi)(x)=e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W^{\pm}(\lambda,x),

where we isolate the oscillatory component
e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}
. This separation allows us to derive pointwise estimates for
W^{\pm}
and its derivatives (see Proposition
4.6
,
4.9
and
4.10
). Additionally, it reduces to estimate certain one dimensional oscillatory integrals, which can be treated in a unified manner.

1.3.
Finite rank perturbations and
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates

Now we address the same problem for finite rank perturbations (
1.2
). Denote by

P=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,P_{j}=\langle\cdot\,,\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}.

We assume that

\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N}
are
L^{2}
-normalized and mutually orthogonal, i.e.,

\langle\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij},\,\,\,\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq N,

(1.19)

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta function. In addition to the decay and smoothness condition on each
\varphi_{j}
, we also require a spectral assumption analogous to that of (
1.13
). Instead of the scalar function defined in (
1.9
), we define the following
N\times N
matrix

F_{N\times N}(z)=(f_{ij}(z))_{N\times N},\qquad f_{ij}(z)=\langle((-\Delta)^{m}-z)^{-1}\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle,\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty).

(1.20)

Note that if
\varphi_{i}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
(
1\leq i\leq N
) for some
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, then it is meaningful to define the boundary value

F_{N\times N}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=F_{N\times N}(\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}0),\qquad\lambda>0.

(1.21)

In view of (
1.13
), it is natural to make the following assumption:
For
n\geq 1
, there exists some absolute constant
{c}_{0}>0
such that

\left|\det(I+{F}_{N\times N}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{m}))\right|\geq{c}_{0},\ \ \ \text{for any}\,\,\lambda>0.

(1.22)

Based on the above assumptions, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
H_{N}
be given by (
1.2
). Assume that (
1.19
) holds and each
\varphi_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq N
) satisfies the smoothness and decay condition
(i)
in Assumption
1
, as well as the spectral assumption (
1.22
).
Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(N,\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N})
such that
{e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{N}}
has integral kernel satisfying

\displaystyle\left|K(t,x,y)\right|\leq C|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-y\right|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\ \ t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.23)

The basic strategy in our proof of Theorem
1.2
is to simplify the problem to the rank one case by examining the resolvent
R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=(H-(\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}0))^{-1}

\displaystyle PL^{2}={\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2},\,\,\,\,\ J=\begin{cases}\ \{0,\,1,...,m-\frac{n+1}{2},m-\frac{n}{2}\},&\mbox{if $n$ is odd},\\

It turns out that the problem is ultimately transformed into considering the Gram matrix associated with the coefficients of

We mention that analogous difficulties are encountered in the context of higher order Schrödinger operators

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Spectral representation

Inspired by the dispersive estimates for Schrödinger operators
-\Delta+V
(see e.g.
[
34
,
35
]
), we begin with the spectral representation of the propagator as given by Stone’s formula:

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}f,g\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda}\left\langle[R^{+}_{\alpha}(\lambda)-R^{-}_{\alpha}(\lambda)]f,\,g\right\rangle\,{\rm d}\lambda},

(2.1)

Here,
f,g
are Schwartz functions,
P_{ac}
denotes the projection onto the absolutely continuous spectrum of
H
, and
{R}^{\pm}_{\alpha}({\lambda})={(H-\lambda\mp{\rm i}0)}^{-1}
.

Compared with the case of Schrödinger operators
(-\Delta)^{m}+V
, the main difference here is the following Aronszajn-Krein formula (see
[
39
]
): For

z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash[0,\infty)
, one has

R_{\alpha}(z)=R_{0}(z)-\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha F(z)}R_{0}(z)\varphi\big\langle R_{0}(z)\cdot,\varphi\big\rangle,

(2.2)

where
R_{0}(z)=((-\Delta)^{m}-z)^{-1}
is the free resolvent and
F(z)
is given by (
1.8
).
Plugging the Aronszajn-Krein formula (
2.2
) into (
2.1
) and noting that

{e}^{-{\rm i}t{H}_{0}}=\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}({R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})-{R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m})){\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda,

then the change of variables
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{2m}
in (
2.1
) leads to

\displaystyle{e}^{-{\rm i}tH}-{e}^{-{\rm i}t{H}_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha{F}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})}{R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})\varphi\left\langle{R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})\cdotp,\varphi\right\rangle{\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda

(2.3)

\displaystyle+\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha{F}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m})}{R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m})\varphi\left\langle{R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m})\cdotp,\varphi\right\rangle{\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda

\displaystyle:={K}_{n}^{+}-{K}_{n}^{-}.

By (

{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
, by (
3.11
) and the inequality (
2.35
) in Lemma
2.8
, we have

\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

(
ii
) If
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|>1
, we deduce the following

\displaystyle|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-{x}_{1}|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{1})|}{{|x-x_{1}|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{|\varphi({x}_{2})|}{{|{x}_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\langle x\rangle)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-y\right|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

where in the first inequality above, we used (
3.11
) and the fact that
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-{x}_{1}|\leq 1
in
\Omega_{1}
; in the second inequality, we employed the following estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}-\frac{n-1}{2}}|\varphi(x_{1})|{\rm d}x_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}-\frac{n-1}{2}}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

which, in turn, is derived from the decay assumption (
1.10
)-(
1.11
), Lemma
2.8
, and the fact that

\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}+\frac{n-1}{2}<n
;
while in the last inequality, we utilized the
relationship that
|x-y|\lesssim\langle x\rangle
, which is a consequence of the assumption
|x|\geq|y|
.

Thus, by (
i
) and (
ii
), it follows that

{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
satisfies the desired estimate (
3.1
).

Step 2: Estimates for
{K}_{n,2}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
.

Recall that in the region
\Omega_{2}
, we have
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x-{x}_{1}|+|{x}_{2}-y|)>1
. Thanks to (
3.9
), we apply (
2.33
) in Lemma
2.7
with
b=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then it follows that

\begin{split}\left|{I}_{n}^{\pm,h}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n+1}{4m}}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-{x}_{1}\right|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}\end{split}

(
i
) If
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
, inserting the above inequality into (
3.8
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|{K}_{n,2}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{t}^{-\frac{n+1}{4m}}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-{x}_{1}\right|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}\frac{|\varphi({x}_{1})|}{{|x_{1}-x|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{|\varphi({x}_{2})|}{{|x_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-{x}_{1}|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}|\varphi({x}_{1})|\frac{|\varphi({x}_{2})|}{{|x_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-y\right|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

(3.12)

where in the equality above, we used the identity:

\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}+\frac{n-1}{2}=\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1};

(3.13)

the second inequality follows from Lemma
2.8
and the fact that
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-{x}_{1}|\geq 1
in
\Omega_{2}
; while in the last inequality, we used the assumption that
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
.

(
ii
) If
{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|>1
, we derive that

\displaystyle|{K}_{n,2}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-{x}_{1}|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}|\varphi({x}_{1})|\frac{|\varphi({x}_{2})|}{{|x_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\langle x\rangle)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left|x-y\right|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

(3.14)

where the above first inequality follows from the same as the second line in (
3.12
);
in the second inequality, we utilized the following estimate

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-{x}_{1}|^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}|\varphi({x}_{1})|{\rm d}{x}_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

which follows from Lemma
2.8
and the decay assumption (
1.10
).

By (
3.12
) and (
3.14
), it follows that
{K}_{n,2}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
also satisfies the desired estimate (
3.1
).
Therefore, combining results from
Step 1
and
Step 2
, we prove the pointwise estimate (
3.1
) in dimensions
n\leq 4m-1
.

3.2.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
for
n\geq 4m

In view of (
2.5
) and the form of the resolvent kernel given in (
2.26
), it suffices to consider the following four terms:

\displaystyle{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-{x}_{1}|+|{x}_{2}-y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{2m-1}

\displaystyle\times W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)\frac{\varphi(x_{1})}{{\left|x-{x}_{1}\right|}^{n-2m}}\frac{\varphi(x_{2})}{{\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}^{n-2m}}{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2},

\displaystyle{K}_{n,2}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-{x}_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)\frac{\varphi(x_{1})}{{|x-{x}_{1}|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\varphi(x_{2})}{{|{x}_{2}-y|}^{n-2m}}{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2},

\displaystyle{K}_{n,3}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|{x}_{2}-y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)\frac{\varphi(x_{1})}{{|x-{x}_{1}|}^{n-2m}}\frac{\varphi(x_{2})}{{|{x}_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2},

and

\displaystyle{K}_{n,4}^{\pm,h}\left(t,x,y\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|{x}_{2}-y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{n-2m}

\displaystyle\times W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)\frac{\varphi(x_{1})}{{|x-{x}_{1}|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\frac{\varphi(x_{2})}{{|{x}_{2}-y|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}.

In the following we also assume that (
3.10
) holds and divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: Estimates for
{K}_{n,j}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
,
j=1,2,3
.

We shall reduce the analysis of
{K}_{n,j}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\ (j=1,2,3)
to the estimate of the following oscillatory integrals :

I_{j}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m})\psi_{j}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|){\rm d}\lambda,\quad t>0,

(3.15)

where

\psi_{1}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)={\lambda}^{2m-1}W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|),

\psi_{2}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)={\lambda}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|),

\psi_{3}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)={\lambda}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|).

By (
2.27
), it follows that
W_{0}^{\pm}(\cdot)
and
W_{1}^{\pm}(\cdot)
have the same estimate. Meanwhile, we have
\lambda^{2m-1}\leq\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
for
\lambda>\lambda_{0}/2
. Thus we have

\sup_{x,x_{1},x_{2},y}\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\psi_{j}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-l},\quad 0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1.

(3.16)

We proceed to prove the estimate specifically for
{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}
, noting that the proofs for
{K}_{n,j}^{\pm,h}
(
j=2,3
) are the same. We shall discuss the following three cases separately.

Case 1:

t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
and
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)\leq 1
.
We perform integration by parts
n_{0}=\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]
times in the integral (
3.15
) by using the following identity:

\frac{1}{2{\rm i}tm\lambda^{2m-1}}\cdot\frac{d}{d\lambda}e^{{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}=e^{{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}.

Then, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)=&\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=n_{0}}C_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}}t^{-n_{0}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)^{s_{1}}\int_{\Omega}e^{{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}\\
&\qquad\cdot\frac{d^{s_{2}}}{d\lambda^{s_{2}}}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m})\frac{d^{s_{3}}}{d\lambda^{s_{3}}}\psi_{1}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\lambda^{-(2m-1)n_{0}}{\rm d}\lambda,\end{split}

and by (
3.3
) and (
3.16
), it follows that for
0\leq r\leq 1
,

\left|\frac{d^{r}}{d\lambda^{r}}\left(\frac{d^{s_{2}}}{d\lambda^{s_{2}}}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m})\frac{d^{s_{3}}}{d\lambda^{s_{3}}}\psi_{1}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\lambda^{-(2m-1)n_{0}}\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{0}-s_{3}-r}.

Note that if
n\notin 2m\mathbb{N}
, in order to utilize Lemma
2.7
, we observe that

\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{0}-s_{3}\leq n-1-2mn_{0}<2m-1.

This allows us to apply (
2.34
) with
b=n-1-2mn_{0}
and
k=1
and derive that

\begin{split}\left|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim&\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}t^{-n_{0}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}t^{-\frac{1+n-1-2mn_{0}}{2m}}=\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}\\
&\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),\end{split}

(3.17)

where in the last inequality, we used the fact

\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}\lesssim 1+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

This is valid since
n_{0}=\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
.

If
n\in 2m\mathbb{N}
, then
n_{0}=\frac{n}{2m}\in\mathbb{N}
. To apply Lemma
2.7
, we note that

\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{0}-s_{3}\leq 0,

thus, we apply (
2.34
) with
b=0,\ k=1
to obtain that

\begin{split}|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)|\lesssim&\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-1}\\
\lesssim&t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|^{-1}+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),\end{split}

(3.18)

where in the second inequality, we used the assumption
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)\leq 1
and
|x-x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}-y|
in this case; while in the last inequality, we employed the inequality

\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-1}\lesssim|x-x_{1}|^{-1}+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

which, in turn, follows from the fact
-1\leq s_{1}-1\leq\frac{n}{2m}-1\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
in this case.

Therefore, combining (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we derive that

\displaystyle\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|^{-1}+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}})\frac{|\varphi(x_{1})|}{{|x-{x}_{1}|}^{n-2m}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{2})|}{{\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}^{n-2m}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

(3.19)

where in the second inequality, we used the estimate

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{-(1+n-2m)}|\varphi(x_{1})|{\rm d}x_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(1+n-2m)}\lesssim C,

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{-(n-2m-\frac{n-1}{2})}|\varphi(x_{1})|{\rm d}x_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m-\frac{n-1}{2})}\lesssim C,

which follow from Lemma
2.8
as well as the facts that
0\leq 1+n-2m<n
and
0\leq n-2m-\frac{n-1}{2}<n
; in the last inequality, we used the assumption that
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
.

Case 2:
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
and
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)>1
.
In this case, we apply (
2.33
) with
b=(2m-1)(\frac{n}{2m}-n_{0})-\frac{1}{2}
and
k=1
to deduce that

\begin{split}\left|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim&\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{0}}t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}+\frac{n}{2m}-n_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),\end{split}

where the second inequality is justified by the fact that
0\leq s_{1}+\frac{n}{2m}-n_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. By employing the same arguments to that used in (
3.19
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

(3.20)

Case 3:
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|>1
and
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)\leq 1
.
We perform integration by parts
n_{1}=\left[\frac{n-1}{2(2m-1)}\right]
times in the integral (
3.15
) to obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)=&\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=n_{1}}C_{s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}}t^{-n_{1}}(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)^{s_{1}}\int_{\Omega}e^{{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}\\
&\qquad\cdot\frac{d^{s_{2}}}{d\lambda^{s_{2}}}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m})\frac{d^{s_{3}}}{d\lambda^{s_{3}}}\psi_{1}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\lambda^{-(2m-1)n_{1}}{\rm d}\lambda,\end{split}

and for
r=0,1
, it follows that

\left|\frac{d^{r}}{d\lambda^{r}}\left(\frac{d^{s_{2}}}{d\lambda^{s_{2}}}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}({\lambda}^{2m})\frac{d^{s_{3}}}{d\lambda^{s_{3}}}\psi_{1}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\lambda^{-(2m-1)n_{1}}\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1}-s_{3}-r}.

Moreover,
note that
\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1}-s_{3}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1}<2m-1
, this allows us to apply (
2.34
) with
b=\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1},\ k=1
and derive the following:

\begin{split}\left|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-n_{1}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}t^{-\frac{1+\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1}}{2m}}=\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}t^{-\frac{n_{1}}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}.\end{split}

(3.21)

(i)
If
n_{1}\geq-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-n_{1}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|)^{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-n_{1}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}},

where we used
n_{1}\geq-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}
and
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|\leq 1
in this case. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-n_{1}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{1})|}{{\left|x-{x}_{1}\right|}^{n-2m}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{2})|}{{\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}^{n-2m}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\langle x\rangle)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

(3.22)

where in the second inequality, we observe that by the definition of
n_{1}
, we have
n_{1}\leq\frac{n-1}{2(2m-1)}
. This implies that
-1\leq s_{1}-n_{1}-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}\leq n-2m
. Thus the following inequality holds

0\leq n_{1}-s_{1}+\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}+n-2m<n.

This, together with Lemma
2.8
and
n-2m\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{-(n_{1}-s_{1}+\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}+n-2m)}|\varphi(x_{1})|{\rm d}x_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(n_{1}-s_{1})}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}-\frac{n-1}{2}}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}-\frac{n-1}{2}};

in the last inequality, we used the assumption
|x-y|\lesssim\langle x\rangle
and the identity (
3.13
).

(ii)
If
n_{1}<-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}
, thanks to (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}t^{-\frac{n_{1}}{2m}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{1})|}{{|x-{x}_{1}|}^{n-2m}}\frac{|\varphi(x_{2})|}{{|x_{2}-y|}^{n-2m}}{\rm d}{x}_{1}{\rm d}{x}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}t^{-\frac{n_{1}}{2m}}\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m-s_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\langle x\rangle)^{-(\frac{n-1}{2}-n_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

(3.23)

in the second inequality, we used

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|x-{x}_{1}|^{-(n-2m-s_{1})}|\varphi(x_{1})|{\rm d}x_{1}\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m-s_{1})},

which is deduced by Lemma
2.8
, with the condition
0\leq n-2m-s_{1}<n
; in the third inequality, we used
n-2m-s_{1}\geq\frac{n-1}{2}-n_{1}
, which holds when
n\geq 4m
; in the last inequality, we made use of
|x-y|\lesssim\langle x\rangle
and

-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}-n_{1}\right)\leq-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1},

which follows from the assumption
n_{1}<-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}
and the identity (

).

It follows from (

) and (

\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

(3.24)

Case 4:
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|>1
and

.
Following the same approach used in

and obtain

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}(t,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-n_{1}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}}t^{-\frac{1+\frac{n-1}{2}-(2m-1)n_{1}}{2m}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}+(2m-1)n_{1}}{2m-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}t^{-\frac{\frac{n+1}{2}}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|^{s_{1}-n_{1}}(t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-x_{1}|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}.

Thus, by employing the same arguments as in (

\left|{K}_{n,1}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

.

Step 2: Estimate for
{K}_{n,4}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
.

For
{K}_{n,4}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
, in contrast to
{K}_{n,j}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
for
j=1,2,3
, the main difference is that when
n\geq 4m
,
n-2m
can exceed
\frac{n-1}{2}
, thus differing from the structure in (
3.15
). In order to overcome this difficulty, we borrow ideas from
[
6
,
4
]
and employ integration by parts arguments with respect to the oscillating term
e^{{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}
. To be more precise, let us define

{L}_{{x}_{1}}:=\frac{1}{{\rm i}\lambda}\frac{{x}_{1}-x}{\left|{x}_{1}-x\right|}\cdotp{\nabla}_{{x}_{1}},\quad\quad{L}_{{x}_{2}}:=\frac{1}{{\rm i}\lambda}\frac{{x}_{2}-y}{\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}\cdotp{\nabla}_{{x}_{2}},

and their dual operators

{L}_{{x}_{1}}^{*}=\frac{{\rm i}}{\lambda}{\nabla}_{{x}_{1}}\left(\frac{{x}_{1}-x}{\left|{x}_{1}-x\right|}\right),\quad\quad{L}_{{x}_{2}}^{*}=\frac{{\rm i}}{\lambda}{\nabla}_{{x}_{2}}\left(\frac{{x}_{2}-y}{\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}\right),

Note that

{L}_{{x}_{1}}{e}^{{\rm i}\lambda|x-x_{1}|}={e}^{{\rm i}\lambda|x-x_{1}|},\qquad{L}_{{x}_{2}}{e}^{{\rm i}\lambda|x_{2}-y|}={e}^{{\rm i}\lambda|x_{2}-y|}.

Set

v=\left[\frac{n+5}{4}\right]-m.

We apply the operators
{L}_{{x}_{1}}
and
{L}_{{x}_{2}}
to the terms
{e}^{{\rm i}\lambda\left|x-{x}_{1}\right|}
and
{e}^{{\rm i}\lambda\left|{x}_{2}-y\right|}

v
times respectively. Then we can express
{K}_{n,4}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
as

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|)}F_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}(\lambda^{2m})\lambda^{n-2m}\\
&\cdotp{({L}_{{x}_{1}}^{*})}^{v}(\frac{W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)\varphi(x_{1})}{|x-x_{1}|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}){({L}_{{x}_{2}}^{*})}^{v}(\frac{W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)\varphi(x_{2})}{|x_{2}-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}){\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2},\end{split}

which can be further written as a combination of

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}I_{n,4,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{\pm,h}\left(t,\left|x-x_{1}\right|,\left|x_{2}-y\right|\right){G}_{1}\left(x,x_{1}\right){G}_{2}\left(x_{2},y\right){\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2},

where
0\leq\left|{\beta}_{1}\right|,\left|{\beta}_{2}\right|\leq n
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&I_{n,1,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{\pm,h}\left(t,\left|x-x_{1}\right|,\left|x_{2}-y\right|\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda\left(|x-x_{1}|+|x_{2}-y|\right)}F_{\pm}^{\alpha,h}(\lambda^{2m})\psi_{4}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\rm d\lambda,\end{split}

with

\begin{split}\psi_{4}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)=&\lambda^{n-2m-2v}\cdot(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)^{|\beta_{1}|}(W_{1}^{\pm})^{(|\beta_{1}|)}(\lambda|x-x_{1}|)\\
&\times(\lambda|x_{2}-y|)^{|\beta_{2}|}(W_{1}^{\pm})^{(|\beta_{2}|)}(\lambda|x_{2}-y|).\end{split}

A direct computation yields that

\begin{split}&\left|G_{1}\left(x,x_{1}\right)\right|\leq C\cdot M(\left|x-x_{1}\right|^{-n+2m}+\left|x-x_{1}\right|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}})\cdot{\left\langle{x}_{1}\right\rangle}^{-\delta},\delta>n+\frac{3}{2},\\
&\left|G_{2}\left(x_{2},y\right)\right|\leq C\cdot M(\left|x_{2}-y\right|^{-n+2m}+\left|x_{2}-y\right|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}})\cdot{\left\langle{x}_{2}\right\rangle}^{-\delta},\delta>n+\frac{3}{2}.\end{split}

Thanks to (
2.27
), (
3.3
) and the fact that
n-2m-2v\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, we deduce that

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\psi_{4}(\lambda,|x-x_{1}|,|x_{2}-y|)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-l},\ 0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1.

Now the condition in (
3.15
) is satisfied, then the result for
{K}_{n,4}^{\pm,h}(t,x,y)
can be derived using the same arguments as those presented in
Step 1
.

Therefore, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

2m<n<4m
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\varphi(y){\rm d}y=e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x),

(4.25)

and

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\min\{\lambda^{-l},\ \lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\}.

(4.26)

(ii) When
n\geq 4m
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\varphi(y){\rm d}y=e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)+e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W_{0,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x),

(4.27)

and

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}W_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-l}\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m)},

(4.28)

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}W_{0,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}.

(4.29)

(iii) Further, for
n>2m
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\right]\varphi(y){\rm d}y=e^{{\rm i}\lambda|x|}U_{0,0}^{+}(\lambda,x)-e^{-{\rm i}\lambda|x|}U_{0,0}^{-}(\lambda,x),

(4.30)

and

\sup_{x}\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\widetilde{U}_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{n-2m-l}.

(4.31)

Proof.

We first prove statement (i).
It follows from (
2.26
) that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\varphi(y){\rm d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x-y|}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2m}}W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)+\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)\right)\varphi(y){\rm d}y\\
=&e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-y|-|x|)}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2m}}W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)+\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)\right)\varphi(y){\rm d}y\\
:=&e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x).\end{split}

Note that
\text{{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}}W_{0}^{\pm}(\cdot)\subset[0,1]
,
\text{{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}}W_{1}^{\pm}(\cdot)\subset[\frac{1}{2},\infty)
and by (
2.27
), we have

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2m}}W_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)+\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}W_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|))\right)\right|\lesssim_{l}\lambda^{-l}\min\left\{|x-y|^{2m-n},\,\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\right\},\end{split}

where
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
2m<n<4m
.
Meanwhile, a direct computation shows that

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}\left(e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x-y|-|x|)}\right)\right|\lesssim_{l}|y|^{l}.

Thus, by (
2.35
), we deduce that (
4.26
) holds when
0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1\leq 2
and
2m<n<4m
. By Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
, the results in (ii) and (iii) are derived through analogous arguments. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

We derive the pointwise decay (
4.1
) by examining
two distinct regions:
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x|+|y|)>1
and
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x|+|y|)\leq 1
. Moreover, as highlighted in Remark
2.1
, we may, without loss of generality, assume that
|x|\geq|y|
during the proof.

4.2.1.
In the region where
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x|+|y|)>1
.

In light of (
2.24
) and (
2.26
), we further divide the proof into the following subcases.

\bullet

Subcase 1 :
2m<n\leq 4m-1
.

Using (
2.6
) and (
4.25
),
K_{n}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)
can be written as

\begin{split}K_{n}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)=&-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x|+|y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,l}(\lambda^{2m})W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}{\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

By (
4.6
) and (
4.26
), we deduce that for
0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left(\lambda^{2m-1}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,l}(\lambda^{2m})W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)W_{1,1}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}},\ \ \ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

(4.32)

We apply (
2.31
) with
b=\frac{n-1}{2}
to obtain

\begin{split}|K_{n}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)|&\lesssim t^{-\frac{n+1}{4m}}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left||x|+|y|\right|)^{-\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}\\
&\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\end{split}

where in the second inequality above, we used the identity

\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}=\frac{n-1}{2}+\frac{m-1-\frac{n-1}{2}}{2m-1}

and the assumption
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
; in the last inequality, we used the fact
|x-y|\lesssim 2|x|
since we have assumed that
|x|\geq|y|
.

\bullet

Subcase 2 :
n\geq 4m
.

By (
2.6
) and (
4.27
), we have

\left|K_{n}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim\sum_{s,j=0}^{1}\left|K_{n,s,j}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)\right|,

where

\begin{split}K_{n,s,j}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)=-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\lambda(|x|+|y|)}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,l}(\lambda^{2m})W_{0,s}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)W_{0,j}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}{\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

Observe that
\frac{n+1}{2}-2m\geq 0
when
n\geq 4m
. Thanks to (
4.6
), (
4.28
) and (
4.29
), when
s=1
,
{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,l}(\lambda^{2m})W_{0,s}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)W_{0,j}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}
also satisfies the estimate in (
4.32
). Thus

K_{n,s,j}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)
with
s=1
satisfies (
4.1
) by the same arguments used in Subcase 1. We omit the details here.

Next, it remains to consider
K_{n,0,j}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)
(
j=0,1
). Similarly, using (
4.6
), (
4.28
) and (
4.29
), we have for
0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left(\lambda^{2m-1}{F}_{\pm}^{\alpha,l}(\lambda^{2m})W_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)W_{0,j}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{2m-1-l}\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m)},\ \ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1.

It follows from (
2.31
) with
b=2m-1
that

\displaystyle\left|K_{n,0,j}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-1}({t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}\left||x|+|y|\right|)^{\frac{m}{2m-1}}\langle x\rangle^{-(n-2m)}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},

where we have used the fact that
n-2m-\frac{m}{2m-1}\geq{\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}
.

4.2.2.
In the region where
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x|+|y|)\leq 1
.

In this case, we shall take advantage of the difference
R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})
, which provides a gain of
\lambda^{n-2m}
. Specifically, by the following algebraic identity

\prod_{k=1}^{3}{A_{k}^{+}}-\prod_{k=1}^{3}{A_{k}^{-}}=(A_{1}^{+}-A_{1}^{-})A_{2}^{+}A_{3}^{+}+A_{1}^{-}A_{2}^{-}(A_{3}^{+}-A_{3}^{-})+A_{1}^{-}(A_{2}^{+}-A_{2}^{-})A_{3}^{+},

we break
K_{n}^{+,l}-K_{n}^{-,l}
into

\displaystyle K_{n}^{+,l}-K_{n}^{-,l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})){R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})\varphi\left\langle{R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})\cdotp,\ \varphi\right\rangle{\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda

\displaystyle-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}){R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m})\varphi\left\langle({R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})-{R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m}))\cdotp,\ \varphi\right\rangle{\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda

\displaystyle-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})({R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})-{R}_{0}^{-}({\lambda}^{2m}))\varphi\left\langle{R}_{0}^{+}({\lambda}^{2m})\cdotp,\ \varphi\right\rangle{\lambda}^{2m-1}\rm d\lambda

\displaystyle:=-\frac{m\alpha}{\pi{\rm i}}\left(K_{n,1}^{l}+K_{n,2}^{l}+K_{n,3}^{l}\right).

(4.33)

We remark that
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
and
K_{n,3}^{l}(t,x,y)
can be estimated by the same way, thus we only consider
K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)
and
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
here.

Step 1: Estimate for
K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)
.

\bullet

Subcase 1 :
2m<n\leq 4m-1
.

Using (
4.25
),
K_{n,1}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)
can be written as (up to constants)

\begin{split}K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)=&\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}+{\rm i}\lambda(|x|+|y|)}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}))W_{1,1}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{1,1}^{+}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}{\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

It follows from the estimate (
4.3
) and (
4.26
) that for
0\leq l\leq\left[\frac{n}{2m}\right]+1
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left(\lambda^{2m-1}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}))W_{1,1}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{1,1}^{+}(\lambda,y)\right)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{n-1-l}.

(4.34)

Note that
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\leq 1
also holds, then it follows from (
2.32
) with
b=n-1
that when
2m<n\leq 4m-1
,

\displaystyle\left|K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

\bullet

Subcase 2 :
n\geq 4m
.

\left|K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim\sum_{s,j=0}^{1}\left|K_{n,1,s,j}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|,

where

\begin{split}K_{n,s,j}^{l}(t,x,y)=&\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}+{\rm i}\lambda(|x|+|y|)}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}))W_{0,s}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{0,j}^{+}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}{\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

(
4.28
) and (
4.29
) indicate that
\lambda^{2m-1}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}))W_{0,s}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{0,j}^{+}(\lambda,y)
still satisfies (
4.34
) for all
s,j\in\{0,1\}
. Thus, we have that in the dimensions
n\geq 4m
,

\left|K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

Step 2: Estimate for
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
.

\bullet

Subcase 1 :
2m<n\leq 4m-1
.

Using (
4.25
) and (
4.30
),
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
can be written as a linear combination of

K_{n,2}^{\pm,l}(t,x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}-{\rm i}\lambda(|x|\mp|y|)}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})W_{1,1}^{-}(\lambda,x)U_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)\lambda^{2m-1}{\rm d}\lambda.

(4.35)

By (
4.26
) and (
4.31
), we have that
\lambda^{2m-1}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})W_{1,1}^{-}(\lambda,x)U_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)
satisfies (
4.34
). Note that
t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}||x|\mp|y||\leq 1
, then using (
2.32
) in Lemma
2.6
, it follows that

\left|K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

\bullet

Subcase 2 :
n\geq 4m
.

In this subcase, it suffices to replace
W_{1,1}^{-}(\lambda,x)
by
W_{0,s}^{-}(\lambda,x)
for
s=0,1
in (
4.35
). By (
4.28
) and (
4.29
), it follows that
\lambda^{2m-1}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})W_{0,s}^{-}(\lambda.x)U_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,y)
still satisfies the estimate (
4.34
), thus we have the desired bound.

Therefore the proof for
n\geq 2m
is finished.
∎

4.3.
The proof of Theorem
4.1
for
1\leq n<2m

Compared to the case
n>2m
, the situation for
n<2m
is much more complicated due to the expansion of the free resolvent (see Lemma
2.1
).
To proceed, we need the following lemma which deals with functions exhibiting specific vanishing moments. The proof can be found in
[
5
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have included a proof in Appendix
C
for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that
\varphi(x)\in{L}_{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\sigma>\frac{n}{2}+\mathbf{k}_{0}
,
\mathbf{k}_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and

\langle x^{\beta},\varphi(x)\rangle=0,\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,0\leq|\beta|\leq\mathbf{k}_{0}-1.

Then, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{r}\varphi(y){\rm d}y\right|\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{{L}_{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-\mathbf{k}_{0}},\ \ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(4.36)

provided
r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
-\frac{n-1}{2}\leq r\leq\mathbf{k}_{0}-1
.

By invoking Lemma
4.8
, we establish the following proposition concerning the property of
R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi
, which depends on the order of the vanishing moment for
\varphi
and plays a key role in the proof.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
n<2m
and let
\varphi
satisfy Assumption
1
. If there exists
\mathbf{k}_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0\leq\mathbf{k}_{0}\leq m+1-[\frac{n+1}{2}]
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)dx=0,\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,\ 0\leq|\beta|<\mathbf{k}_{0}.

Then we have the following expression

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\varphi(y){\rm d}y=e^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x|}W_{1,2}^{\pm}(\lambda,x),\,\,\,0<\lambda<1.

(4.37)

Moreover, for
l=0,\,1
,
W_{1,2}^{\pm}
satisfies

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}W_{1,2}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\min\left\{\lambda^{n-2m+\mathbf{k}_{0}-l},\,\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+\mathbf{k}_{0}-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\},\,\,\,0<\lambda<1,

(4.38)

except for
n=2m-2
and
\mathbf{k}_{0}=2
. While in this exceptional case,
W_{1,2}^{\pm}
satisfies

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}W_{1,2}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\min\left\{\lambda^{-l}|\log{\lambda}|,\,\lambda^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\},\,\,\,0<\lambda<1.

(4.39)

Proof.

First, (
4.37
) follows if we set

W_{1,2}^{\pm}(\lambda,x)=\langle e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})(x-\cdotp),\varphi(\cdotp)\rangle.

Given that the proof of (
4.38
) is different depending on whether
\lambda\langle x\rangle\leq 1
or
\lambda\langle x\rangle>1
, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1: We prove (
4.38
) and (
4.39
) when
\lambda\langle x\rangle>1
.

By (
2.24
), we have

\displaystyle\langle e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})(x-\cdotp),\varphi(\cdotp)\rangle=\left\langle\frac{{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{{|x-\cdot|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}{e}^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x-\cdot|}U_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-\cdot|),\,\varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle.

(4.40)

Notice that (
2.25
) implies

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left(e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}{e}^{\pm{\rm i}\lambda|x-\cdot|}U_{1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-\cdotp|)\right)\right|\lesssim_{l}\langle\cdot\rangle^{l}\lambda^{-l},\quad 0<\lambda<1.

(4.41)

Thus, by(
4.40
) and (
2.35
), we obtain the following estimates for
|x|\leq 1
and
l=0,\,1
:

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\langle e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})(x-\cdotp),\varphi(\cdotp)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\left|\langle{|x-\cdot|}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}},\,\langle\cdot\rangle\varphi(\cdot)\rangle\right|

(4.42)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\left\{\lambda^{n-2m+{\textbf{k}}_{0}-l},\,\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\},

where in the second inequality, we applied Lemma
4.8
; and in the last inequality we used the fact that
\lambda\langle x\rangle>1
.

Now we consider the case
|x|>1
. In this case, note that
1<\lambda\langle x\rangle\lesssim\lambda|x|
.
Then, applying Taylor expansion to
{e}^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z)
at
z_{0}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}{e}^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}\frac{{(e^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z))}^{(j)}(z_{0})}{j!}{(z-z_{0})}^{j}+r_{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}^{\pm}(z,z_{0}),\end{split}

where

{r}_{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}^{\pm}(z,z_{0})=\frac{(z-z_{0})^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}}{({\textbf{k}}_{0}-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}{(e^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z))}^{({\textbf{k}}_{0})}(z_{0}+u(z-z_{0})){\rm d}u.

Thus, choosing
z=\lambda|x-\cdot|
and
z_{0}=\lambda|x|
in (
4.40
), we see that

\mbox{RHS of \eqref{eq-lambda x>1}}=T_{1}(\lambda,x)+T_{2}(\lambda,x)

where

T_{1}(\lambda,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}\left\langle\frac{{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{{|x-\cdot|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}\displaystyle\frac{{(e^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z)}^{(j)}(\lambda|x|)}{j!}{(\lambda|x-\cdot|-\lambda|x|)}^{j},\varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle,

and

\begin{split}T_{2}(\lambda,x)=\left\langle\frac{{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}}{{|x-\cdot|}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}r_{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-\cdot|,\,\lambda|x|),\varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle.\end{split}

It follows from the Leibniz rule that

{(e^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z)}^{(l)}(z_{0})=\sum_{s=0}^{l}(\pm{\rm i})^{s}\frac{l!}{s!(l-s)!}e^{\pm{\rm i}z_{0}}(U_{1}^{\pm})^{(l-s)}(z_{0}),\quad l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then, by (
2.25
), we derive that for
0\leq j\leq{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1
and
0\leq l\leq 1

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\left[e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}\frac{{(e^{\pm{\rm i}z}U_{1}^{\pm}(z))}^{(j)}(\lambda|x|)}{j!}\right]\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-l},\,\,\,0<\lambda<1.

(4.43)

For the term
T_{1}
, by invoking Lemma
4.8
, the binomial theorem, (
4.43
) and the assumption
\lambda\left\langle x\right\rangle>1
, we obtain that for
0\leq l\leq 1

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}T_{1}(\lambda,x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{l}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}{\lambda}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j-l}{\left\langle x\right\rangle}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}+j-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{l}\min\left\{\lambda^{n-2m+{\textbf{k}}_{0}-l},\,\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m-l}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\},\quad\,0<\lambda<1.

(4.44)

Regarding the term
T_{2}
,
we observe that

e^{\mp{\rm i}z_{0}}r_{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}^{\pm}(z,\,z_{0})=\sum_{s=0}^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}(\pm{\rm i})^{s}\frac{{\textbf{k}}_{0}\,(z-z_{0})^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}}{s!({\textbf{k}}_{0}-s)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}{e^{\pm{\rm i}u(z-z_{0})}(U_{1}^{\pm}})^{(s)}(z_{0}+u(z-z_{0})){\rm d}u.

It follows from (
2.25
) that when
l=0,1
,

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}(e^{\mp{\rm i}\lambda|x|}r_{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}^{\pm}\big(\lambda|x-\cdot|,\,\lambda|x|)\big)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}-l}|x-\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle\cdot\rangle^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}+1},\qquad 0<\lambda<1,\,\,|x|>1.

This implies that for
|x|>1
and
l=0,1
,

When
t^{-\frac{1}{2m}}(|x|+|y|)\leq 1
,
as per the expression (
4.33
), it is sufficient to consider the kernels
K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)
and
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
. Using (
4.30
), they can be expressed as follows (up to constants):

\begin{split}K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}+{\rm i}\lambda(|x|+|y|)}({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})){\lambda}^{2m-1}W_{1,3}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{1,3}^{+}(\lambda,y){\rm d}\lambda,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)=\sum_{\pm}\mp\int_{0}^{+\infty}{e}^{-{\rm i}t{\lambda}^{2m}-{\rm i}\lambda(|x|\mp|y|)}{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{2m-1}W_{1,3}^{-}(\lambda,x)U_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,y){\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

We first estimate
K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)
.
In light of Proposition
4.10
, we shall examine two distinct situations: the case where
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x){\rm d}x\neq 0
and the case where it is zero. We define

\psi_{1}(\lambda,x)=({F}_{+}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})-{F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m})){\lambda}^{2m-1}W_{1,3}^{+}(\lambda,x)W_{1,3}^{+}(\lambda,y).

If
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x){\rm d}x\neq 0
, then by (
4.23
), (
4.62
), we deduce that

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}\psi_{1}(\lambda,x)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{n-1-l}.

(4.70)

While if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x){\rm d}x=0
, the estimate (
4.70
) remains valid due to (
4.21
) and (
4.63
).
Therefore, applying (
2.32
) with
b=n-1
, we obtain

\left|K_{n,1}^{l}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim{t}^{-\frac{n}{2m}}(1+{t}^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}}.

Next, we estimate
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
.
We define

\psi^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda,x)={F}_{-}^{\alpha,l}({\lambda}^{2m}){\lambda}^{2m-1}W_{1,3}^{-}(\lambda,x)U_{0,0}^{\pm}(\lambda,y).

It follows from (
4.30
) and Proposition
4.10
that
\psi^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda)
satisfies (
4.70
), regardless of whether the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x){\rm d}x
vanishes or not. Consequently, we conclude that
K_{n,2}^{l}(t,x,y)
satisfies (
4.1
) as desired.

Therefore the proof when
n=2m
is finished.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

We mention that during the proof of the low energy estimate (
4.1
), all properties that we need for
F^{\alpha,l}_{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
are contained in Lemma
4.2
-

. These properties will be used in the subsequent analysis of finite rank perturbations.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we consider the finite rank case (
1.2
) and prove Theorem
1.2
.
By limiting absorption principle and the decay assumption (
1.10
) of
\varphi_{j}\ (j=1,\cdots,N)
, we are allowed to define

F_{N\times N}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=\left(f_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\right)_{N\times N},\,\,\,\text{where}\,\,f_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=\langle R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i}\rangle,\,\,\,\,\,1\leq i,j\leq N.

(5.1)

Moreover, each
f_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
. Denote the boundary value of the matrix by

A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=A(\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}0)=I_{N\times N}+F_{N\times N}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}),\qquad\,\,\,\lambda>0.

(5.2)

By assumption (
1.22
), it follows that
H
only has the absolutely continuous spectrum (see
[
4
, Lemma B.1]
) .
Let
P=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}
, where
P_{j}=\langle\cdot,\,\varphi_{j}\rangle\varphi_{j}
.
By resolvent identity, we have the following Aronszajn-Krein type formula

\displaystyle R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})

\displaystyle=R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})-R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P\Big(I+PR^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P\Big)^{-1}PR^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})

(5.3)

\displaystyle=R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})-\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}{g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})}\cdot R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i}\langle R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\cdot,\varphi_{j}\rangle,

where

R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=(H_{N}-(\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}0))^{-1},\quad g_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=\Big\langle\Big(I+PR^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P\Big)^{-1}\varphi_{j},\,\varphi_{i}\Big\rangle.

(5.4)

Set
G^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=\left(g_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\right)_{N\times N}
, then by (
5.2
), we have

G^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}.

(5.5)

Now we are ready to give a spectral representation formula for the evolution
e^{-{\rm i}tH_{N}}
. Based on the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
, we have

\displaystyle e^{-{\rm i}tH_{N}}-e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P(I+PR^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P)^{-1}PR^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\lambda^{2m-1}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle+\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}R^{-}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P(I+PR^{-}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})P)^{-1}PR^{-}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\lambda^{2m-1}\,d\lambda

(5.6)

\displaystyle:=\left(K^{+}_{n,N}-K^{-}_{n,N}\right).

By Stone’s formula and (
5.3
), we also have

\displaystyle e^{-{\rm i}tH_{N}}-e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}g^{+}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i}\langle R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\,\cdot,\varphi_{j}\rangle\lambda^{2m-1}\,{\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle+\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}g^{-}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})R^{-}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i}\langle R^{-}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\,\cdot,\varphi_{j}\rangle\lambda^{2m-1}\,{\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}{\left(K^{+}_{n,ij}-K^{-}_{n,ij}\right)}.

(5.7)

We remark that (
5.7
) plays the same role as (
2.3
) in the rank one case (with
\alpha=1
).

As in the rank one case, we first choose a smooth cut-off function
\chi(\lambda)
satisfying (
2.4
) where
\lambda_{0}
is a small constant to be chosen later. Then we split (
5.7
) into the high and low energy part respectively:

\displaystyle K^{\pm,h}_{n,ij}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}(1-\chi(\lambda))g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i}\langle R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\,\cdot,\varphi_{j}\rangle\lambda^{2m-1}\,{\rm d}\lambda,

K^{\pm,l}_{n,ij}:=-\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}}\chi(\lambda)g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i}\langle R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\,\cdot,\varphi_{j}\rangle\lambda^{2m-1}\,{\rm d}\lambda.

We mention that by comparing (
5.7
) with (
2.3
), the main difference is that
\frac{1}{1+F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})}
is replaced by
g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})
. Should we establish that
g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})\ (1\leq i,j\leq N)
shares the same properties with
\frac{1}{1+F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})}
, the analysis could be reduced to the rank one case. However, Lemma
4.3
-
4.5
indicate that the vanishing moment conditions of
\varphi_{j}
will affect the analysis of
g^{\pm}_{ij}(\lambda^{2m})
in the low energy part when
n\leq 2m
, so we will treat them separately.

To this end, we divide the proof into the following four parts.

5.1.
High energy estimate.

By Remark
3.4
, it’s enough to prove that for any
\lambda_{0}>0
, there exists an absolute constant
C=C(N,\lambda_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N})>0
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
, and
1\leq i,j\leq N
, one has

\begin{split}\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}g_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\right|\leq\begin{cases}C,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\mathrm{if}\,\,\,l=0,\\
C\lambda^{1-2m},\,\,\,\,\,\quad\mathrm{if}\,\,\,~l=1,\cdots,[\frac{n}{2m}]+1.\end{cases}\end{split}

(5.8)

Indeed, we observe that similar to the proof of (
3.2
) in Lemma
3.2
, it follows from the decay assumptions on
\varphi_{j}
that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
,

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}f^{\pm}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})\right|\leq C\lambda^{1-2m},\,\,\,\quad\mathrm{if}\,\,\,~l=0.1,\cdots,[\frac{n}{2m}]+1.

(5.9)

Recall that by (
5.4
),

\left(g_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\right)_{N\times N}=A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det{(A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))}}\text{adj}(A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})),

(5.10)

where
\text{adj}(A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))
is the adjugate matrix of
A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
(given in (
5.2
)).
Note that by assumption (
1.22
) one has
|\det{A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})}|\geq c_{0}>0
, thus by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), there are some positive constants depending on
N,\lambda_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N}
such that

|g_{ij}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})|\leq C(N,\lambda_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N}),

which proves (
5.8
) for
l=0
. Further, we notice that

\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}=A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{l}\sum_{l_{m,1}+\cdots+l_{m,s}=l}C_{l_{m,s}}\prod_{m=1}^{s}\left[\frac{d^{l_{m,s}}}{d\lambda^{l_{m,s}}}(A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))\cdot A^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}\right],

(5.11)

where
C_{l_{m,s}}=C(l_{m,1},\cdots,l_{m,s})
depends on
l_{m,1},\cdots,l_{m,s}
.
This, together with (
5.9
), yields (
5.8
) for
l=1,\cdots,[\frac{n}{2m}]+1
.

5.2.
Low energy estimate for
n>2m
.

Now we turn to the low energy estimates. Before proceeding, we highlight a key fact that will be applicable for all
n\geq 1:

Observation.
All the results about
F^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=\langle R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi,\varphi\rangle
in Lemma
4.2
-
4.5
are still valid for
f^{\pm}_{i,j}=\langle R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle
.

In fact, this follows immediately since each
\varphi_{j}
(
j=1,\cdots,N
) has the same decay assumption as
\varphi
in the rank one case.

Let
\lambda_{0}
be given in Lemma
4.2
here and in the rest of this section. By Remark
4.11
, it suffices to prove that for
0\leq l\leq[\frac{n}{2m}]+1
,

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}g^{\pm}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0},

(5.12)

and

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}\left[g^{+}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})-g^{-}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})\right]\right|\lesssim\lambda^{n-2m-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

(5.13)

In order to prove these, we note that by (
1.22
), (
4.4
) and the above
Observation
, we have

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}f^{\pm}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0},

(5.14)

and

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}\left[f^{+}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})-f^{-}_{i,j}(\lambda^{2m})\right]\right|\lesssim\lambda^{n-2m-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

(5.15)

Therefore the property (
5.14
), together with (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and the assumption (
1.22
), yields (
5.12
).
And
(
5.13
) follows from (
5.15
) and the following identity

A^{+}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}-A^{-}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}=A^{+}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}\left(A^{-}(\lambda^{2m})-A^{+}(\lambda^{2m})\right)A^{-}(\lambda^{2m})^{-1}.

This proves the low energy part for
n>2m
.

5.3.
Low energy estimate for

n<2m
and odd
n
.

By (
2.12
), the Hilbert-Smith property and the decay assumption of
\varphi
(see (
1.10
)),

I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P
has the following expansion on
PL^{2}
,

\left(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P\right)f=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}{a}_{j}^{\pm}{\lambda}^{n-2m+2j}P{G}_{2j}Pf+T_{0}f+{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)f,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(5.16)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{G}_{2j}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|x-y|}^{2j}f(y)dy,\ {G}_{2m-n}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}{|x-y|}^{2m-n}f(y)dy,\\
&{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)f=\lambda^{n-2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}P{r}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)Pf(y)dy,\end{split}

and

T_{0}=I+b_{0}PG_{2m-n}P.

In contrast to case
n>2m
, the estimates (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) are not valid in general, and it is intricately linked to the vanishing moment condition of
\varphi_{j}
for
j=1,\cdots,N
. To be more precise, the validity of these estimates depends on the fulfillment of the following vanishing condition:

P(x^{\beta})=0,\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,0\leq|\beta|\leq m-\frac{n+1}{2}.

(5.17)

Thus we further divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1: The vanishing condition (
5.17
) holds.

In this case, we have

I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P=T_{0}+{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)

(5.18)

holds on
PL^{2}
.
Further, by (
2.13
) and the decay assumption (
1.11
), we have

\left\|\frac{d^{l}}{dz^{l}}{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)\right\|_{L^{2}-L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{1-l},\quad\text{for}\,\ l=0,\,1,\,\cdots\frac{n+1}{2}.

(5.19)

Note that
T_{0}
coincides with
I+P(-\Delta)^{-m}P
on
PL^{2}
. Since
I+P(-\Delta)^{-m}P
is strictly positive on
PL^{2}
,
T_{0}
is invertible, moreover,
it follows from (
5.19
) and the Neumann series that there exists a small
\lambda_{0}
such that
I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P
is also invertible on
PL^{2}
and

\left\|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}\left(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P\right)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

(5.20)

Therefore, by (
5.5
), the function
g_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})
satisfies

\left|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}g_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})\right|_{L^{2}-L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-l},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0},

(5.21)

which is consistent with the estimate (
4.7
) in rank one perturbation case. This shows that the proof of the estimate for low energy part
K^{\pm,l}_{n,ij}(t,x,y)
can be reduced to the rank one case (see the arguments in Section
4.3
).

Case 2: The vanishing condition (
5.17
) does not hold.

In this case,
I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P
does not have a simple form as (
5.18
).
The main difficulty lies in the asymptotic expansion of
(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P)^{-1}
on
PL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
near the zero energy threshold. Our approach to obtain the inverse is based on a suitable decomposition of space
PL^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
To this end, we introduce some notations that will be used throughout this section. First, denote

J=\{0,\,1,...,m-\frac{n+1}{2},m-\frac{n}{2}\}.

Now we define some subspaces of
P{L}^{2}
. More precisely, let
Q_{j}:L^{2}\to Q_{j}L^{2}
be the orthogonal projections with

{Q}_{0}{L}^{2}:=\mbox{span}\left\{\varphi_{i},1\leq i\leq N:\int{\varphi_{i}{\rm d}x}\neq 0\right\},

(5.22)

and for
1\leq j\leq m-\frac{n+1}{2}
,

\displaystyle{Q}_{j}{L}^{2}:=\mbox{span}\Big\{\varphi_{i},1\leq i\leq N:\mbox{there exists $\kappa$ with $|\kappa|=j$}

\displaystyle\mbox{such that $\int{x^{\kappa}\varphi_{i}{\rm d}x}\neq 0$ and $\int{x^{\beta}\varphi_{i}{\rm d}x}=0$ for $|\beta|<j$}\Big\},

(5.23)

finally,

{Q}_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}:=\mbox{span}\left\{\varphi_{i},1\leq i\leq N:\int{x^{\beta}\varphi_{i}{\rm d}x}=0\,\,\mbox{for all\,\, $|\beta|\leq m-\frac{n+1}{2}$}\right\}.

(5.24)

From the above definitions, we have the following decomposition

PL^{2}={\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2},

(5.25)

as well as the relation

Q_{j}P=Q_{j}.

(5.26)

We point out that the vanishing moment condition of
\varphi_{j}
may vary from one to one.
The following orthogonal property associated with
Q_{j}
is needed.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
i,j\in J
and
l\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we have

Q_{i}{G}_{2l}Q_{j}=0,

(5.27)

provided
2l\leq[i+\frac{1}{2}]+[j+\frac{1}{2}]-1
.

Proof.

By the definitions of
Q_{j}
above, we deduce that

Q_{j}(x^{\alpha})=0,\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,|\alpha|\leq[j+\frac{1}{2}]-1.

(5.28)

Since

|x-y|^{2l}=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=2l}C_{\alpha,\beta}(-1)^{|\beta|}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\quad l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

we have

Q_{i}{G}_{2l}Q_{j}f=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=2l}C_{\alpha,\beta}(-1)^{|\beta|}Q_{i}(x^{\alpha})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(Q_{j}(x^{\beta})\big)(y)f(y){\rm d}y.

Given that
|\alpha|+|\beta|=2l
, if
2l\leq[i+\frac{1}{2}]+[j+\frac{1}{2}]-1
, then either
|\alpha|\leq[j+\frac{1}{2}]-1
or
|\beta|\leq[j+\frac{1}{2}]-1
. Therefore (
5.27
) follows from (
5.28
).
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists a sufficiently small
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, the operator
I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P
is invertible on
PL^{2}
and

\displaystyle(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P)^{-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in J}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}\Big(M_{i,j}^{\pm}+{\Gamma}_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\Big)Q_{j},

(5.29)

where
Q_{j}
(
j\in J
) is given by (
5.22
)-(
5.24
), and
M_{i,j}^{\pm}
,
{\Gamma}_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
are bounded operators on
L^{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{d^{l}}{d\lambda^{l}}{\Gamma}_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\right\|_{L^{2}-L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-l},\quad l=0,\,1,\,\cdots\frac{n+1}{2}.

(5.30)

Proof.

The proof is based on the decomposition of the space
PL^{2}
in (
5.25
).
More precisely, we define
B=(\lambda^{-j}Q_{j})_{j\in J}\
:
{\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2}\ \rightarrow L^{2}
by

Bf=\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\lambda^{-j}Q_{j}f_{j},

where
f=(f_{j})_{j\in J}\in{\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
Q_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\in J
. Otherwise, let
J^{\prime}
be the set of indices
j
for which
Q_{j}\neq 0
and it suffices to replace
J
by
J^{\prime}
in the following proof.

Let
B^{*}
be the dual operator of
B
and define
\mathbb{A}^{\pm}
on
{\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{\pm}=\lambda^{2m-n}B^{*}(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P)B.

(5.31)

Notice that
B
is a bijection between
{\bigoplus}_{j\in J}Q_{j}L^{2}
and
PL^{2}
. Thus
I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P
is invertible on
PL^{2}
if and only if
(\mathbb{A}^{\pm})^{-1}
exists. Moreover, we have

(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P)^{-1}=\lambda^{2m-n}B(\mathbb{A}^{\pm})^{-1}B^{*},

(5.32)

provided that the inverse exists.

Now we prove the existence of
(\mathbb{A}^{\pm})^{-1}
. By (
5.16
) and the assumption in
Case 2
, we obtain

I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}{a}_{j}^{\pm}{\lambda}^{n-2m+2j}P{G}_{2j}P+T_{0}+P{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)P.

(5.33)

By Lemma
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{-i}Q_{i}(I+P{R}_{0}^{\pm}({\lambda}^{2m})P)\lambda^{-j}Q_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l=O_{i,j}}^{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}{a}_{l}^{\pm}{\lambda}^{n-2m+2l-i-j}Q_{i}{G}_{2j}Q_{j}+\lambda^{-i-j}Q_{i}T_{0}Q_{j}+\lambda^{-i-j}Q_{i}{\mathcal{R}}_{2m-n+1}^{\pm}(\lambda)Q_{j},

where
O_{i,j}=[\frac{[i+\frac{1}{2}]+[j+\frac{1}{2}]}{2}]+1
.
Combining this and (
5.31
), we have

\mathbb{A}^{\pm}=D^{\pm}+{({r}_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda))}_{i,j\in J},

(5.34)

where

D^{\pm}
is given by

d_{i,j}^{\pm}=\begin{cases}a_{\frac{i+j}{2}}^{\pm}Q_{i}G_{i+j}Q_{j},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,i+j\,\,\mbox{is even},\\
Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}}T_{0}Q_{m-\frac{n}{2}},&\mbox{if}\,\,i=j=m-\frac{n}{2},\\
0,&\mbox{else},\end{cases}

(5.35)

and by (
5.19
) and (
5.33
)-(
5.34
), for

\begin{cases}\left|\langle x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\ \varphi(y)\rangle\right|\lesssim\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{|\alpha|}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}},\quad&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|>{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1,\\
\langle x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\ \varphi(y)\rangle=0,&\text{if}\,\,|\beta|\leq{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1.\end{cases}

Thus we obtain (
4.36
) in this case.

Case 2:
r\geq 0
is odd.
We express
|y-x|^{r}
as

|x-y|^{r}=(|x-y|^{r}-|x||x-y|^{r-1})+|x||x-y|^{r-1}.

For the term
|x||x-y|^{r-1}
,
since
r-1
is even, it follows from
Case 1
that

\left|\langle|x||x-\cdot|^{r-1},\ \varphi(\cdot)\rangle\right|\lesssim\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}.

(C.1)

For the first term, we further decompose it as

\displaystyle|x-y|^{r}-|x||x-y|^{r-1}=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=r-1}(-1)^{|\beta|}C_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{2x^{\alpha}x_{i}}{|x|^{|\alpha|+1}}\frac{|x|^{|\alpha|+1}y^{\beta}{y_{i}}}{|x|+|x-y|}-\frac{x^{\alpha}}{|x|^{|\alpha|}}\frac{|x|^{|\alpha|}y^{\beta}{y_{i}}^{2}}{|x|+|x-y|}\right).

(C.2)

Therefore, it suffices to consider the general form
\frac{|x|^{l}y^{\gamma}}{(|x|+|x-y|)}
with fixed
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{n}
satisfying
r=|\gamma|+l-1\geq 0
, we verify by induction that

\displaystyle\frac{|x|^{l}y^{\gamma}}{(|x|+|x-y|)}=\sum_{s=1}^{1+{\textbf{k}}_{0}-|\gamma|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha_{1}|\in\{{\textbf{k}}_{0},{\textbf{k}}_{0}+1\}\\
|\alpha_{1}|+h-s=r\end{subarray}}L_{s,\alpha_{1}}(x)\frac{|x|^{h}y^{\alpha_{1}}}{(|x|+|x-y|)^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha_{2}|\leq{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1\\
i+|\alpha_{2}|=r\end{subarray}}L_{\alpha_{2}}(x)|x|^{i}y^{\alpha_{2}},

(C.3)

where
|L_{s,\alpha_{1}}(x)|,\ |L_{\alpha_{2}}(x)|\lesssim 1
.

For the term
\frac{|x|^{|\alpha|+1}y^{\beta}{y_{i}}}{|x|+|x-y|}
, we apply (
C.3
) with
l=|\alpha|+1
,
\gamma=\beta+e_{i}
and
q=1
;
for the term
\frac{|x|^{|\alpha|}y^{\beta}{y_{i}}^{2}}{|x|+|x-y|}
, we apply (
C.3
) with
l=|\alpha|
,
\gamma=\beta+2e_{i}
and
q=1
. Note that from (
C.3
), we have
h-s=r-|\alpha_{1}|
. Moreover, since
|\alpha_{1}|\in\{{\textbf{k}}_{0},{\textbf{k}}_{0}+1\}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{|x|^{h}y^{\alpha_{1}}}{(|x|+|x-y|)^{s}}\right|\leq|x|^{h-s}\langle y\rangle^{\alpha_{1}}\leq\langle x\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}\langle y\rangle^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}+1}.

(C.4)

By the assumption on
\varphi
, we have

\left\langle\frac{|x|^{h}y^{\alpha_{1}}}{(|x|+|x-y|)^{s}},\varphi(y)\right\rangle\lesssim\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\|\left\langle y\right\rangle^{-\sigma}\left\langle y\right\rangle^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}+1}\|_{L^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}\lesssim\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}},

and

\left\langle|x|^{i}y^{\alpha_{2}},\varphi(y)\right\rangle=0.

The above two imply that

\left|\left\langle|x-\cdot|^{r}-|x||x-\cdot|^{r-1},\ \varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle\right|\lesssim\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}.

This, together with (
C.1
), yields (
4.36
).

Case 3:

\frac{1-n}{2}\leq r<0
. In this case, we denote by
k=-r
, thus
0<k\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. For any fixed
{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have the following identity

|x-y|^{-k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}C_{l}\frac{(|x|-|x-y|)^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}}{|x|^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}+l}|x-y|^{k-l}}+\sum_{l=0}^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}-1}C^{\prime}_{l}\frac{(|x|-|x-y|)^{l}}{|x|^{k+l}},

(C.5)

for some
C_{l},C_{l}^{{}^{\prime}}>0
.
On the one hand, note that by Lemma
2.8
and the assumption on
\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\frac{(|x|-|x-\cdot|)^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}}}{|x|^{{\textbf{k}}_{0}+l}|x-\cdot|^{k-l}},\ \varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{-{\textbf{k}}_{0}-l}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\langle y\rangle^{-2(\sigma-{\textbf{k}}_{0})}|x-y|^{-2(k-l)}{\rm d}y}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}}.

On the other hand, the binomial theorem gives

\frac{(|x|-|x-y|)^{l}}{|x|^{k+l}}=\sum_{s=0}^{l}\frac{(-1)^{s}l!}{s!(l-s)!}\frac{|x|^{s}|x-y|^{l-s}}{|x|^{k+l}}.

Then by
Case 1
and
Case 2
, we have for each
s\in\{0,\cdots,l\}
that

\displaystyle|x|^{s-k-l}\left|\left\langle|x-\cdot|^{l-s},\ \varphi(\cdot)\right\rangle\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{2}}\left\langle x\right\rangle^{r-{\textbf{k}}_{0}},

In view of (
C.5
), (
4.36
) follows by combining the above two estimates.

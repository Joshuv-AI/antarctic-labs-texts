1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the open unit disk,
\mathbb{T}
its boundary,
\mathbb{N}
the set of nonnegative integers, and
{\mathbb{N}^{+}}
the set of the positive integers. Let
H(\mathbb{D})
denote the set of all holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T})
be the set of all finite nonnegative Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T})
,
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
m\in{N^{+}}
, the
weighted Dirichlet-type integral
of order
m
is defined

D_{\mu,m}(f):=\frac{1}{m!(m-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{(m)}(z)\right|^{2}P_{\mu}(z)\,(1-|z|^{2})^{m-1}\,dA(z),

where
dA(z)
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
,
f^{(m)}(z)
is the
m
-th order derivative of
f
, and
P_{\mu}(z)
is the Poisson integral of
\mu
, defined by

P_{\mu}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{2}}\,d\mu(\zeta),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If
\mu
is the Dirac measure
\delta_{\lambda}
at a point
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, we employ the abbreviated notation
D_{\lambda,m}(\cdot)
for
D_{\delta_{\lambda},m}(\cdot)
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and nonzero
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T})
, define the semi-inner product space

\mathcal{H}_{\mu,m}:=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):D_{\mu,m}(f)<\infty\right\},

endowed with the seminorm
\sqrt{D_{\mu,m}(\cdot)}
.

If
\mu=\delta_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, the resulting space is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,m}
and is called the
local Dirichlet space of order
m
at
\lambda
. If
\mu=0
, then
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,m}=H^{2}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
H^{2}
denotes the classical Hardy space of the unit disk.

For the case
\mu=\sigma
, where
\sigma
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
, a direct computation shows that for any
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in H(\mathbb{D})
,

D_{\sigma,m}(f)=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\binom{k}{m}|a_{k}|^{2},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Here, the binomial coefficient is defined for
k\geq m
by
\binom{k}{m}:=\frac{k!}{m!(k-m)!}
.

A linear space
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
, naturally associated with
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,m}
, is defined as follows:

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}:=\left\{f(z)=\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}a_{j}z^{j}:\ a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\ \text{and}\ D_{\mu,m}(f)<\infty\right\}.

For any nonzero measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T})
, the functional
\sqrt{D_{\mu,m}(\cdot)}
defines a seminorm on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu,m}
. Moreover,
D_{\mu,m}(f)=0
if and only if
f
is a polynomial of degree at most
m-1
. In contrast, this same functional, also denoted
D^{1/2}_{\mu,m}(\cdot)
, induces a norm on the subspace
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
, which consequently becomes a Hilbert space. These spaces have been extensively studied from various perspectives over the past two decades. Notably, Ghara, Soumitra, and Gupta extended the local Douglas formula to higher-order weighted Dirichlet-type integrals
[
3
]
. In subsequent work, the same authors investigated Hadamard multipliers for these higher-order spaces
[
4
]
. This work is particularly motivated by
[
2
]
, in which Fricain, Emmanuel and Mashreghi employed the local Douglas formula to furnish an elementary proof of an analogue of Fejér’s Theorem for weighted Dirichlet spaces with superharmonic weights.

For a given
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
, the norm convergence
{S_{m,n}}f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\to f
as
n\to\infty
is not guaranteed. A fundamental question arises: under what conditions does this convergence hold? We demonstrate that convergence can be restored by modifying the coefficients of the partial sums
{S_{m,n}}f(z)
. Specifically, we construct a sequence of polynomials with carefully designed weights applied to the coefficients
a_{k}
; this new sequence is guaranteed to converge to
f
in the
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
-norm.

The reminder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 3, we construct a counterexample of a function
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
whose partial sums
{S_{m,n}}f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}
fails to converge to
f
as
n\to\infty
. Section 4 is devoted to proving an analogue of Fejér’s Theorem in higher-order weighted Dirichlet spaces. In Section 5, we consider the case where
\mu={c_{1}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{1}}}}+{c_{2}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{2}}}}+\cdots+{c_{s}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{s}}}}
is a finite linear combination of Dirac measures. For this special class of measures, the coefficient modifications required for convergence admit an explicit closed-form expression. This work extends classical polynomial approximation theory to higher-order weighted spaces, thereby providing novel perspectives on summability methods in complex analysis.

2.
Bridging Formula and Counterexamples

Weighted Dirichlet spaces challenge classical polynomial approximation paradigms. Although the local Douglas formula rigorously connects function decompositions to weighted norms, and the coefficient correspondence theorem aligns Taylor series with these spaces, their unmodified partial sums often diverge. This paradox, revealed by explicit counterexamples, highlights a critical gap in conventional approximation strategies. By analyzing these contradictory results, this section establishes a foundation for resolving the instability of polynomial expansions in higher-order weighted settings. This work paves the way for a systematic framework to restore convergence.

2.1.
Local Douglas formula for higher-order weighted Dirichlet-type integrals

The analysis in this subsection is based on two foundational elements: the local Douglas formula, which decomposes functions via their derivatives and boundary values, and the coefficient correspondence theorem, which links Taylor coefficients to weighted Dirichlet norms.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, and
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Then
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,m}
if and only if

f(z)=\alpha+(z-\lambda)g(z)\quad\text{for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\text{ and }g\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}.

Moreover, in this case, the following statements hold

(i)

D_{\lambda,m}(f)=D_{\sigma,m-1}(g)
,

(ii)

The limit of
f(z)
equals
\alpha
as
z
tends to
\lambda
in each oricyclic approach region

{\Omega_{\lambda}}=\left\{{z\in\mathbb{D}:\left|{z-\lambda}\right|<\kappa{{\left({1-{{\left|z\right|}^{2}}}\right)}^{1/2}}}\text{for arbitrary}\ \kappa>0\right\},

and can be written as

f^{*}(\lambda)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Omega_{\lambda}\\
|z|\to\lambda\end{subarray}}f(z)=\alpha.

Proof.

A detailed proof of this result is provided in
[
3
]
.
∎

The above tool not only unify disparate strands of existing theory but also expose a fundamental paradox: usual polynomial approximations, despite their simplicity, may fail catastrophically in certain weighted spaces.

2.2.
Failure of Usual Polynomial Approximation

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m}
and
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}
. Then,
{S_{m,n}}f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m}
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we see that there exists some
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\sigma,m-1}
such that

f(z)=a+(z-1)g(z).

We derive the following relationships between the coefficients:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}b_{0}=b_{1}=\dots=b_{m-1}=a,\\
b_{k-1}-b_{k}=a_{k},\quad\quad k\geq m.\end{array}\right.

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{({b_{k-1}}-{b_{k}}){z^{k}}}

\displaystyle=a+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n+m}{{b_{k-1}}{z^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}

\displaystyle=a+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m-1}{{b_{k}}{z^{k+1}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}

\displaystyle=a+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m-1}{{b_{k}}{z^{k+1}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m-1}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}-{b_{n+m}}{z^{n+m}}}

\displaystyle=a-{b_{n+m}}+(z-1)\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m-1}{({b_{k}}-{b_{n+m}}){z^{k}}}.

By Lemma
2.1
, it is obvious that
{S_{m,n}}f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then, there exists a function
f\in\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
such that

D_{\mu,m}(S_{m,n}f-f)\not\to 0.

Proof.

Consider the linear map

\displaystyle S_{m,n}:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m}\longmapsto\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m},

\displaystyle\quad f\longmapsto S_{m,n}(f).

Let
f_{n}(z)=nz^{n+m+1}-(n+1)z^{n+m}+z^{m}
and
(S_{m,n}f_{n})(z)=-(n+1)z^{n+m}+z^{m}.

Note that

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=nz^{n+m+1}-(n+1)z^{n+m}+z^{m}

\displaystyle=(z-1)\left(nz^{n+m}-z^{m}-z^{m+1}-\cdots-z^{m+n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(z-1){g_{n}}(z)\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m}

and

\displaystyle(S_{m,n}f_{n})(z)

\displaystyle=-(n+1)z^{n+m}+z^{m}

\displaystyle=-n-(n+1)(z-1)(1+z+\cdots+z^{m+n-1})

\displaystyle\quad+(z-1)(1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1})

\displaystyle=-n-(z-1){v_{n}}(z)\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{1,m},

where

\displaystyle{g_{n}}(z)

\displaystyle:=nz^{n+m}-z^{m}-z^{m+1}-\cdots-z^{m+n-1}

and

\displaystyle{v_{n}}(z)

\displaystyle:=(n+1)(1+z+\cdots+z^{m+n-1})+(1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1})

\displaystyle=n(1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1})+(n+1)(z^{m}+z^{m+1}+\cdots+z^{m+n-1}).

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle D_{1,m}(S_{m,n}f_{n})

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}({v_{n}}(z))

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}[-nz^{m-1}-(n+1)\left(z^{m}+z^{m+1}+\cdots+z^{m+n-1}\right)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=m}^{n+m-1}(n+1)^{2}\binom{j}{m-1}+n^{2}\binom{m-1}{m-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=m-1}^{n+m-1}(n+1)^{2}\binom{j}{m-1}-2n-1

\displaystyle=(n+1)^{2}\binom{m+n}{m}-2n-1,

and

\displaystyle D_{1,m}(f_{n})

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}({g_{n}}(z))=D_{\sigma,m-1}\left(nz^{n+m}-z^{m}-\cdots-z^{n+m-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=m}^{m+n-1}\binom{j}{m-1}+n^{2}\binom{m+n}{m-1}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\binom{m+n}{m-1}+\binom{m+n}{m}-1.

Then

\displaystyle\left\|{{S_{m,n}}}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{{{\left\|{{S_{m,n}}{f_{n}}}\right\|}_{1,m}}}}{{{{\left\|{{f_{n}}}\right\|}_{1,m}}}}=\frac{{\sqrt{{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({v_{n}})}}}{{\sqrt{{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({g_{n}})}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{{{{(n+1)}^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m+n\\
m\end{array}\right)-2n-1}}{{{n^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m+n\\
m-1\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m+n\\
m\end{array}\right)-1}}}\rightarrow\infty

as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
\sup\limits_{n}\|S_{m,n}\|=\infty
. By uniform boundness principle, there exists
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
such that

\sup_{n}\|S_{m,n}f\|_{\mu,m}=\infty.

In particular,
\|S_{m,n}f-f\|_{\mu,m}\not\to 0
.
∎

The previous counterexample highlights the subtlety of polynomial approximation in weighted Dirichlet spaces. As we see, unmodified partial sums may diverge in these spaces.

3.
Convergence via Weighted Polynomial Modifications

Despite the failure of usual partial sums in approximating functions in
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
, we show that strategic modifications to the Taylor coefficients yield convergent sequences. In this section, we introduce a novel class of polynomials, termed weighted modification polynomials, whose coefficients incorporate oscillatory damping factors. Through the local Douglas formula and a coefficient correspondence theorem, we establish norm convergence in higher-order weighted Dirichlet spaces
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
. Specifically, we demonstrate that for any function
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,m+1}
such that the weighted Dirichlet norm
D_{\lambda,m}(f-f_{n})
converges to zero as
n
tends to infinity.

To facilitate the approximation, we introduce a complex number array
\left(w_{n,k}\right)_{n\geq 0,k\geq m+1}
as below.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,k\geq m+1}
be an array of complex numbers satisfying the following conditions:

(3.1)

\displaystyle(1).w_{n,k}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{for~each~}n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}k>m+n+1;

(3.2)

\displaystyle(2).|w_{n,k}|\leq M

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}m+1\leq k\leq m+n+1;

(3.3)

\displaystyle(3).\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n,k}=1

\displaystyle\quad\text{for~all }k\geq m+1;

(3.4)

\displaystyle(4).|w_{n,k+1}-w_{n,k}|\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{(n+1)\binom{m+n+1}{m}}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}m+1\leq k<m+n+1.

If we list them out, then it is a lower-triangular matrix

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0,m+1}&0&0&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
w_{1,m+1}&w_{1,m+2}&0&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
w_{2,m+1}&w_{2,m+2}&w_{2,m+3}&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\cdots\\
w_{k,m+1}&w_{k,m+2}&w_{k,m+3}&\cdots&w_{k,m+k+1}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\end{pmatrix}

where all elements in the matrix are bounded; each column vector converges to 1, and the distance between two adjacent elements in each row vector is controlled by
\frac{L}{\sqrt{(n+1)\binom{m+n+1}{m}}}
for
k<m+n+1
.

These conditions ensure that the coefficients
w_{n,k}
behave predictably and do not cause excessive oscillations or growth, which is crucial for the convergence of the approximating polynomials.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
with the representation
f(z)=a+(z-\lambda)g(z)
, where
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}
.
Then the coefficients correspondence is as follows.

\begin{cases}a=\lambda b_{0},\\
b_{k-1}=\lambda b_{k},&\text{for }1\leq k\leq m,\\
b_{k-1}-\lambda b_{k}=a_{k},&\text{for }k\geq m+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=a+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k+1}-\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=a+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k-1}z^{k}-\lambda\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}+b_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=(a-\lambda b_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(b_{k-1}-\lambda b_{k})z^{k}.

Comparing both sides of the equation, the result follows easily.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

(Modified Taylor Polynomial).
Given
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
with the representation
f(z)=a+(z-\lambda)g(z),\text{~where~}a\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}
. Define the modified Taylor polynomial
p_{n}(z)
as

p_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}a_{k}z^{k},

where the coefficients
a_{k}
are related to
b_{k}
through the correspondence established in Lemma
3.2
.

In Section 2, we observe that for
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
, the partial sums
S_{m,n}f
may fail to converge to
f
. However, for
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
, if the partial sums are modified to
p_{n}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}a_{k}z^{k}
, we prove the convergence

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}D_{\lambda,m}(p_{n}-f)=0.
This result is then extended to the general space
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
via the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. We first establish a foundational result for the simpler case of the space
\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}
: the modified polynomials
p_{n}
converge to
f
in this setting.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
m\in{N^{+}}
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
.

Proof.

If

f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1},

then

\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}=a+(z-\lambda)\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}.

and

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}.

That is,
\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\binom{k}{m}<\infty
, which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m-1}^{\infty}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}

\displaystyle=\left|b_{m-1}\right|^{2}+\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\left|b_{k}\right|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}

\displaystyle=\left|b_{m-1}\right|^{2}+m\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{{{{\left|{{b_{k}}}\right|}^{2}}}}{{k-m+1}}\binom{k}{m}<\infty.

This means
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}
, and
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}.
The proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Given
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
with the representation
f(z)=a+(z-\lambda)g(z),\text{~where~}a\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}
. Define

(3.5)

{g_{n}}(z):=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{w_{n,k+1}}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}

and set
f_{n}(z)=(z-\lambda)g_{n}(z)
, then

{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

Since Lemma
3.4
gives
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
, we obtain
g\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}
and
{g_{n}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\sigma,m-1}
by Lemma
2.1
, which implies that
{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})={D_{\sigma,m-1}}(g-{g_{n}})
.
For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose a sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.6)

\sum\limits_{k=N}^{\infty}{|{b_{k}}{|^{2}}}\binom{k}{m-1}<{\varepsilon^{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left({g-{g_{n}}}\right)=D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left({\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}}{z^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{w_{n,k+1}}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left({{b_{m-1}}{z^{m-1}}+\sum\limits_{k=m}^{N-1}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}-\sum\limits_{k=m}^{N-1}{{w_{n,k+1}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}+\sum\limits_{k=N}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=N}^{n+m}{{w_{n,k+1}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m}^{N-1}{(1-{w_{n,k+1}}){b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}\right]+D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left({\sum\limits_{k=N}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}\right)+D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left({\sum\limits_{k=N}^{n+m}{{w_{n,k+1}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}},\right)

where by
(3.3)
, the first term tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
and by
(3.6)
, the second term and the third term tend to 0 as
n\to\infty
.

Hence,
{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})={D_{\sigma,m-1}}(g-{g_{n}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
f(z)=a+(z-\lambda)g(z)\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m+1}
with
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in H_{\sigma,m}
. We define
{g_{n}}(z)
as in (3.5) and
{f_{n}}(z):=(z-\lambda){g_{n}}(z)
. Then there exists a positive constant
{C_{1}}
such that

{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})\leq C_{1}^{2}{D_{\lambda,m+1}}(f).

Proof.

\displaystyle{D_{\lambda,m}}\left({f-{f_{n}}}\right)={D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left({g-{g_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2{D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left(g\right)+2{D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left({{g_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{\infty}{{{\left|{{b_{k}}}\right|}^{2}}}\binom{k}{m-1}+2\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{{\left|{{w_{n,k+1}}}\right|}^{2}}{{\left|{{b_{k}}}\right|}^{2}}}\binom{k}{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}{D_{\sigma,m}}(g)=C_{1}^{2}{D_{\lambda,m+1}}(f).

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
q(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}c_{k}z^{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\sigma,m}
. Then

D_{\lambda,m}(q)\leq(n+1)\binom{n+m+1}{m}D_{\sigma,m}(q).

Proof.

Writing
q(z)
in terms of its decomposition around the point
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
as in Lemma
2.1
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}c_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=q(\lambda)+(z-\lambda)\sum_{k=0}^{n+m}d_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=\left[{q(\lambda)-\lambda{d_{0}}}\right]+d_{n+m}z^{n+m+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n+m}(d_{k-1}-\lambda d_{k})z^{k}.

By comparing coefficients, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q(\lambda)=\lambda{d_{0}},\\
{d_{k-1}}=\lambda{d_{k}},&\text{for }1\leq k\leq m,\\
c_{k}=d_{k-1}-\lambda d_{k},&\text{for }m+1\leq k\leq n+m,\\
c_{n+m+1}=d_{n+m}.&\end{array}\right.

Now, we estimate the coefficients
d_{k}
in three cases.

For
m\leq k\leq n+m-1
, we can express each
d_{k}
in terms of the
c_{j}

d_{k}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n+m+1}\lambda^{j-(k+1)}c_{j}.

This implies

|d_{k}|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{n+m+1}|\lambda|^{2(j-k-1)}\right)\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{n+m+1}|c_{j}|^{2}\right)=(n+m+1-k)\sum_{j=k+1}^{n+m+1}|c_{j}|^{2}

by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

For
m\leq k\leq n+m-1
, we have

\displaystyle|d_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)\sum_{j=m+1}^{n+m+1}|c_{j}|^{2}\binom{j}{m}=(n+1)D_{\sigma,m}(q).

For
k=m-1
or
k=n+m
, similar estimation can be obtained that

|d_{m-1}|^{2}=|d_{m}|^{2}\leq(n+1)D_{\sigma,m}(q)

and

|d_{n+m}|^{2}=|c_{n+m+1}|^{2}\leq(n+1)D_{\sigma,m}(q).

By using Lemma
2.1
again, we get

\displaystyle D_{\lambda,m}(q)

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n+m}d_{k}z^{k}\right)=\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m}|d_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m}(n+1)D_{\sigma,m}(q)\binom{k}{m-1}=(n+1)\binom{n+m+1}{m}D_{\sigma,m}(q).

This completes the proof.
∎

Building on the earlier analysis where we leveraged Lemma
2.1
to derive bounds for the coefficients
|d_{n+m}|
and established key inequalities for
D_{\lambda,m}(q)
, we now turn to a more general setting involving finite positive Borel measures on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. This shift is motivated by the need to characterize the behavior of functions in Hardy spaces
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m+1}
, whose series expansions involve coefficients weighted by such measures.

The subsequent lemma is pivotal as it addresses the convergence properties of these weighted coefficient series and their relationship to the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
D_{\lambda,m}
. Specifically, it establishes the integrability of
D_{\lambda,m+1}(f)
with respect to
\mu
, proves pointwise convergence of
D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})
to zero, and quantifies the rate of this convergence via a constant dependent only on the weight array
(w_{n,k})
. These results not only extend the earlier estimates but also provide foundational tools for analyzing the fine structure of functions in Hardy spaces under measure-theoretic perturbations, bridging the gap between coefficient decay and functional convergence in this context.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}
,
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m+1}
and
p_{n}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}a_{k}z^{k}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\lambda,m+1}(f)\,d\mu(\lambda)<\infty,

and in particular,
D_{\lambda,m+1}(f)<\infty
for
\mu
-almost every
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. Furthermore, for each such
\lambda
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})=0\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})\leq C^{2}D_{\lambda,m+1}(f),

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on the array
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,k\geq m+1}
.

Proof.

For
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m+1}
, there exists some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in{\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m}}
such that
f(z)=\alpha+(z-\lambda)g(z)
. Let
{g_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{w_{n,k+1}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}
and
{f_{n}}(z)=(z-\lambda){g_{n}}(z)
, where
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,k\geq m+1}
is an array defined in Definition
3.1
. For arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, choose a sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{k=m+N+1}^{\infty}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m}<\varepsilon^{2}.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}(p_{n}-f)

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left[(p_{n}-f_{n})+(f_{n}-f)\right]

\displaystyle\leq D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}(p_{n}-f_{n})+D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}(f_{n}-f).

A direct computation yields that

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=(z-\lambda)g_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=(z-\lambda)\sum_{k=m}^{n+m}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m}^{n+m}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k+1}-\lambda\sum_{k=m}^{n+m}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}b_{k-1}z^{k}-\lambda\sum_{k=m}^{n+m}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}(b_{k-1}-\lambda b_{k})z^{k}+\lambda\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}-\lambda{w_{n,m+1}}{b_{m}}{z^{m}}.

By the relationship give in Lemma
3.2
,
a_{k}
=
b_{k-1}-\lambda b_{k}
, which implies that

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}a_{k}z^{k}+\lambda\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}-\lambda{w_{n,m+1}}{b_{m}}{z^{m}}.

Therefore,

f_{n}(z)-p_{n}(z)=\lambda\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}-\lambda{w_{n,m+1}}{b_{m}}{z^{m}},

and then

\displaystyle D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}(f_{n}-p_{n})\leq D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left[\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}\right]+D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}({w_{n,m+1}}{b_{m}}{z^{m}})

\displaystyle\leq D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left[\sum_{k=m+1}^{m+N}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}\right]+D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left[\sum_{k=m+N+1}^{n+m}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left[(w_{n,n+m+1}-w_{n,n+m+2})b_{n+m+1}z^{n+m+1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left[N\binom{N+m}{m}\sum_{k=m+1}^{m+N}|w_{n,k+1}-w_{n,k}|^{2}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[(n+1)\binom{n+m+1}{m}\sum_{k=m+N+1}^{n+m}|w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1}|^{2}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left({{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{n+m+1}}}\right).

By
(3.3)
, the first term tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, it follows from
(3.4)
and
(3.7)
that the second term tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. It remains to estimate the third term. To this end, we first note that

{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{n+m+1}}={\lambda^{n+m+1}}{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}+(z-\lambda)\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n+m}{{\lambda^{(n+m)-k}}{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{k}}}.

This together with Lemma
2.1
, yields

\displaystyle D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}\left({{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{n+m+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n+m}\lambda^{(n+m)-k}w_{n,n+m+1}b_{n+m+1}z^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m}|w_{n,n+m+1}|^{2}|b_{n+m+1}|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left[|w_{n,n+m+1}|^{2}|b_{n+m+1}|^{2}\binom{n+m+1}{m}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq M\left[\left|b_{n+m+1}\right|^{2}\binom{n+m+1}{m}\right]^{1/2}.

By
(3.7)
,
M\left[\left|b_{n+m+1}\right|^{2}\binom{n+m+1}{m}\right]^{1/2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This means that
D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}({f_{n}}-{p_{n}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since we have already known that

D_{\lambda,m}^{1/2}(f_{n}-f)\to 0\ \text{as }n\to\infty,

which indicates that

\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})=0.

We still need to show the latter part of the estimate. By using Lemma
3.7
and
(3.4)
, there exists some positive constant
{C_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle D_{\lambda,m}(f_{n}-p_{n})\leq 2{D_{\lambda,m}}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{n+m}{({w_{n,k}}-{w_{n,k+1}})}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\right]+2{D_{\lambda,m}}\left[{{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{n+m+1}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2(n+1)\binom{n+m+1}{m}D_{\sigma,m}\left[\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m}(w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1})b_{k}z^{k}\right]+2{D_{\lambda,m}}\left[{{w_{n,n+m+1}}{b_{n+m+1}}{z^{n+m+1}}}\right]

\displaystyle=2(n+1)\binom{n+m+1}{m}\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m}|w_{n,k}-w_{n,k+1}|^{2}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m}+2\binom{n+m+1}{m}|w_{n,n+m+1}b_{n+m+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{2}D_{\sigma,m}(g)+2\binom{n+m+1}{m}|w_{n,n+m+1}b_{n+m+1}|^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{2}{D_{\sigma,m}}(g)=C_{2}^{2}{D_{\lambda,m+1}}(f).

Combining this with Corollary
3.6
, we see that there exists some positive constant
{C_{1}}
such that

(3.9)

D_{\lambda,m}(f-f_{n})\leq C_{1}^{2}D_{\lambda,m+1}(f).

Hence,

D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})\leq C^{2}D_{\lambda,m+1}(f),

where
C={C_{1}}+{C_{2}}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,\;k\geq m+1}
be an array of complex numbers as in Definition
3.1
.
For
f(z)=\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m+1}
, let

p_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n+m+1}w_{n,k}a_{k}z^{k}.

Then,
\|f-p_{n}\|_{\mu,m}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.8
we know that

\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})=0\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})\leq C^{2}D_{\lambda,m+1}(f).

Applying the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\mu,m}(f-p_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\lambda,m}(f-p_{n})\,d\mu(\lambda)=0.

Thus,

\|f-p_{n}\|_{\mu,m}\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty,

and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Dirac Measure Combinations and Closed-Form Solutions

The general theory developed thus far relies on abstract measure-theoretic arguments. In special cases where
\mu
is a combination of Dirac measures, however, the modification process admits an explicit closed-form expression. This section explores this simplification, leveraging the interplay between polynomial interpolation and weighted Dirichlet norms.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,k\geq m}
be an array of complex numbers satisfying the following conditions:

(4.1)

\displaystyle 1.w_{n,k}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{for~each~}n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}k>m+n;

(4.2)

\displaystyle 2.|w_{n,k}|\leq M

\displaystyle\quad\text{for~each~}n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}m\leq k\leq m+n;

(4.3)

\displaystyle 3.\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n,k}=1

\displaystyle\quad\text{for~all }k\geq m;

(4.4)

\displaystyle 4.|w_{n,k+1}-w_{n,k}|\leq\frac{L}{\sqrt{(n+1)\binom{m+n}{m-1}}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{~and~}m\leq k<m+n.

If we list them out, then it is a lower-triangular matrix

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0,m}&0&0&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
w_{1,m}&w_{1,m+1}&0&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
w_{2,m}&w_{2,m+1}&w_{2,m+2}&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\cdots\\
w_{k,m}&w_{k,m+1}&w_{k,m+2}&\cdots&w_{k,m+k}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(w_{n,k})_{n\geq 0,\,k\geq m}
be an array of complex numbers satisfying conditions
(4.1)
,
(4.2)
and
(4.3)
.
For
g\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}
with
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
, we define

\displaystyle g_{n}(z):=\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}.

Then

(4.5)

\displaystyle D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}(g_{n}-g)\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we choose a sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum\limits_{k=N}^{\infty}{|{b_{k}}{|^{2}}}\binom{k}{m-1}<{\varepsilon^{2}}
. We first verify that
{g_{n}}\in{H_{\sigma,m-1}}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle D_{\sigma,m-1}(g_{n})

\displaystyle=D_{\sigma,m-1}\left(\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}|w_{n,k+1}|^{2}\,|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq{M^{2}}\sum_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}\leq{M^{2}}{D_{\sigma,m-1}}(g)<\infty,

and

\displaystyle{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({g_{n}}-g)

\displaystyle={D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left({\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}}{z^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}{{w_{n,k+1}}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle={D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{N-1}{{b_{k}}(1-{w_{n,k+1}}){z^{k}}+\sum\limits_{k=N}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=N}^{n+m-1}{{w_{n,k+1}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2{D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{N-1}{{b_{k}}}(1-{w_{n,k+1}}){z^{k}}}\right]+4D_{\sigma,m-1}\left(\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+4D_{\sigma,m-1}\left(\sum_{k=N}^{n+m-1}w_{n,k+1}b_{k}z^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2{D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{N-1}{{b_{k}}(1-{w_{n,k+1}}){z^{k}}}}\right]+4(1+M)^{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

By
(4.3)
,
{D_{\sigma,m-1}}\left[{\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{N-1}{{b_{k}}(1-{w_{n,k+1}}){z^{k}}}}\right]
tends to zero as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}D_{\sigma,m-1}^{1/2}(g_{n}-g)\leq 2(1+M){\varepsilon}.

The desired result follows from the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

For
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,m}
such that

D_{\lambda,m}(f-f_{n})\to 0,~\text{as~}n\to\infty.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, for
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
, it can be decomposed as
f(z)=a+(z-\lambda)g(z)\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
, where
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
g(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}{{b_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}
. Define
{g_{n}}(z):=\sum\limits_{k=m-1}^{n+m-1}{{w_{n,k+1}}}{b_{k}}{z^{k}}{\rm{}}
, and set
f_{n}(z)=(z-\lambda)g_{n}(z)
. Lemma
2.1
indicates that
{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})={D_{\sigma,m-1}}(g-{g_{n}})
. Combining this with Lemma
4.2
, we see that
D_{\lambda,m}(f-f_{n})\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty
.
∎

Although we have already obtained
D_{\lambda,m}(f-f_{n})\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty
, it is unfortunate that
f_{n}\notin\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
, and further modification still be required. To illustrate the possibility of such a modification, we consider a special case first.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
f=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
.Then

{q_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+\left({\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{\lambda^{k-n-m}}}}\right){z^{n+m}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}

satisfying

{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{q_{n}})\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
{w_{n,k}}=1
for
m\leq k\leq m+n
and zero elsewhere. It is obvious that

{f_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+\lambda{b_{n+m}}{z^{n+m}}-\lambda{b_{m-1}}{z^{m-1}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,m}

Moreover, it follows from Theorem
4.3
that
{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{f_{n}})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Set

{q_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+\lambda{b_{n+m}}{z^{n+m}}.

Then
{q_{n}}(z)\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
and

\displaystyle q_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}a_{k}z^{k}+\lambda b_{n+m}z^{n+m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-1}a_{k}z^{k}+a_{n+m}z^{n+m}+\lambda b_{n+m}z^{n+m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-1}a_{k}z^{k}+b_{n+m-1}z^{n+m},

where the third equality is from Lemma
3.2
. Notice that for arbitrary
N\geq m+n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+m}^{N}a_{k}\lambda^{k-n-m}

\displaystyle={\lambda^{1-n-m}}\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{N}{{a_{k}}{\lambda^{k-1}}}

\displaystyle={\lambda^{1-n-m}}\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{N}{({b_{k-1}}-\lambda{b_{k}}){\lambda^{k-1}}}

\displaystyle={\lambda^{1-n-m}}\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{N}{\left({{b_{k-1}}{\lambda^{k-1}}-{b_{k}}{\lambda^{k}}}\right)}

\displaystyle={\lambda^{1-n-m}}({b_{n+m-1}}{\lambda^{n+m-1}}-{b_{N}}{\lambda^{N}})

\displaystyle={b_{n+m-1}}-{b_{N}}{\lambda^{N+1-n-m}},

where
{b_{N}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
since the sequence
\{{b_{k}}\}
satisfies
\displaystyle\sum_{k=m-1}^{\infty}|b_{k}|^{2}\binom{k}{m-1}<\infty
.
Hence,
\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{\lambda^{k-n-m}}}={b_{n+m-1}}
, and

{q_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-1}{{a_{k}}}{z^{k}}+\left({\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}}{\lambda^{k-n-m}}}\right){z^{n+m}}{\rm{}}

satisfying

{D_{\lambda,m}}(f-{q_{n}})\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty

immediately.
∎

Now we define the difference quotient operator
{Q_{\lambda}}:{D_{\lambda,m}}\to{D_{\sigma,m-1}}
by

({Q_{\lambda}}f)(z)=\frac{{f(z)-f(\lambda)}}{{z-\lambda}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We equip the space
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\lambda,m}
with the norm

(4.6)

\left\|f\right\|_{\lambda,m}=D_{\lambda,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}(f)=D_{\sigma,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q_{\lambda}f).

Let
{\lambda_{1}},\cdots,{\lambda_{s}}
be
s
distinct points on
\mathbb{T}
and
{c_{j}}\geq 0
. Set

\mu={c_{1}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{1}}}}+{c_{2}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{2}}}}+\cdots+{c_{s}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{s}}}}.

Similar to the treatment of Section 2 in
[
2
]
, each
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
can be uniquely represented as

(4.7)

f(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\cdots+a_{s-1}z^{s-1}+(z-\lambda_{1})(z-\lambda_{2})\cdots(z-\lambda_{s})g(z),

where
g:=Q_{\lambda_{1}}Q_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots Q_{\lambda_{s}}f\in\mathcal{H}_{\sigma,m-1}
. In this case, we define

(4.8)

\left\|f\right\|_{\mu,m}:=[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{s}{{c_{k}}{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}f)}]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
and
\Lambda=\{{\lambda_{j}}:1\leq j\leq s\}
be a subset of distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
.
Define

(4.9)

P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f(z):=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-s}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+b_{n+m-s+1}^{\Lambda}{z^{n+m-s+1}}+\cdots+b_{n+m}^{\Lambda}{z^{n+m}},~\text{for each}\ n\geq s.

Note that
P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f
equals
S_{m,n}f
after modifying the
s
-th coefficient. Moreover, by appropriating values of
b_{i}^{\Lambda}s

(n+m-s+1\leq i\leq n+m)
, we deduce the following equations. That is,

P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f({\lambda_{j}})=f({\lambda_{j}}),\quad 1\leq j\leq s.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
,
\Lambda=\{{\lambda_{j}}:1\leq j\leq s\}
be a subset of distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
and

\mu={c_{1}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{1}}}}+{c_{2}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{2}}}}+\cdots+{c_{s}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{s}}}},

where
c_{j}>0

\left({1\leq j\leq s}\right)
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

For each
1\leq j\leq s
, the series
f({\lambda_{j}})=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}}
converges.

(ii)

There exist unique coefficients
b_{n+m-s+1}^{\Lambda},\cdots,b_{n+m}^{\Lambda}
such that the modified Taylor
polynomials

P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f(z):=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-s}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+b_{n+m-s+1}^{\Lambda}{z^{n+m-s+1}}+\cdots+b_{n+m}^{\Lambda}{z^{n+m}},\quad n\geq s,

satisfy

(4.10)

P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f({\lambda_{j}})=f({\lambda_{j}}),\quad 1\leq j\leq s,n\geq s.

Proof.

(i): By lemma
2.1
, the series
f({\lambda_{j}})
converges obviously. By Corollary
3.5
,

\begin{cases}a={\lambda_{j}}b_{0},\\
b_{k-1}={\lambda_{j}}b_{k},&\text{for }1\leq k\leq m-1,\\
b_{k-1}-{\lambda_{j}}b_{k}=a_{k},&\text{for }k\geq m.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle{S_{m,n}}f({\lambda_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{({b_{k-1}}-{\lambda_{j}}{b_{k}})\lambda_{j}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{b_{k-1}}\lambda_{j}^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m}{{b_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k+1}}

\displaystyle={b_{m-1}}\lambda_{j}^{m}-{b_{n+m}}\lambda_{j}^{n+m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}}-b_{n+m}{\lambda_{j}^{n+m+1}},

since Corollary
4.4
gives us
{b_{m-1}}=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k-m}}
. This implies
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}{S_{m,n}}f({\lambda_{j}})=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}}=f({\lambda_{j}})
.

(ii): According to (i), the condition (5.10) is equivalent to

b_{n+m-s+1}^{\Lambda}+\cdots+b_{n+m}^{\Lambda}\lambda_{j}^{s-1}=\sum\limits_{{k}=n+m-s+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k-(n+m-s+1)}},\quad 1\leq j\leq s.

these equations can be represented as the matrix form
AB=C
, where
A
is the Vandermonde matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\lambda_{1}&\cdots&\lambda_{1}^{s-1}\\
1&\lambda_{2}&\cdots&\lambda_{2}^{s-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\lambda_{s}&\cdots&\lambda_{s}^{s-1}\end{pmatrix},

and

B=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{b_{n+m-s+1}^{\Lambda}}\\
{b_{n+m-s+2}^{\Lambda}}\\
\vdots\\
{b_{n+m}^{\Lambda}}\end{array}}\right),\quad\quad C=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\sum\limits_{k=n+m-s+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}}\lambda_{1}^{k-(n+m-s+1)}}\\
{\sum\limits_{k=n+m-s+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}}\lambda_{2}^{k-(n+m-s+1)}}\\
\vdots\\
{\sum\limits_{k=n+m-s+1}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}}\lambda_{s}^{k-(n+m-s+1)}}\end{array}}\right).

Noting that
\det(A)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq s}{({\lambda_{j}}-{\lambda_{i}})}
, and that

\{{\lambda_{j}}:1\leq j\leq s\}
is a subset of distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
, we conclude that
\det(A)\neq 0
and
A
is invertible.
This proves that the coefficients
b^{\Lambda}_{n+m-s+1},\ldots,b^{\Lambda}_{n+m}
are uniquely determined.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mu,m}
,
\Lambda=\{{\lambda_{j}}:1\leq j\leq s\}
be a subset of distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
and

\mu={c_{1}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{1}}}}+{c_{2}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{2}}}}+\cdots+{c_{s}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{s}}}},

where
c_{j}>0\left({1\leq j\leq s}\right)
. Then

{\left\|{P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f-f}\right\|_{\mu,m}}\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

When
\Lambda={\Lambda_{1}}=\{{\lambda_{1}}\}
is a singleton, the result has been proven directly in Corollary
4.4
. We obtain

P_{m,n}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f(z)=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-1}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+\left({\sum\limits_{k=n+m}^{\infty}{{a_{k}}\lambda_{1}^{k-n-m}}}\right){z^{n+m}},

which also implies that

{\left\|{P_{m,n}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f(z)-f(z)}\right\|_{{\lambda_{1}},m}}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Suppose the result holds for
s-1
distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
, and let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\}
be a set of
s
distinct points in
\mathbb{T}
. To proceed, we consider the following two subsets of
\Lambda
,

\Lambda_{1}=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\},\quad\Lambda_{2}=\{\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{s}\},\text{consisting of}\ s-1\text{ distinct points}.

Let
P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f
and
P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f
denote the corresponding modified Taylor polynomials. By our assumption

\displaystyle P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-s}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+b_{n+m-s+1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}{z^{n+m-s+1}}+\cdots+b_{n+m-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}{z^{n+m-1}},

\displaystyle P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=m}^{n+m-s}{{a_{k}}{z^{k}}}+b_{n+m-s+1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}{z^{n+m-s+1}}+\cdots+b_{n+m-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}{z^{n+m-1}}.

Furthermore,

(4.11)

\displaystyle P^{\Lambda_{1}}_{n-1}f(\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=f(\lambda_{j}),\hskip 20.00003ptj\in\{1,3,4,\ldots,n\},

(4.12)

\displaystyle P^{\Lambda_{2}}_{n-1}f(\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=f(\lambda_{j}),\hskip 20.00003ptj\in\{2,3,4,\ldots,n\}

such that

(4.13)

{\left\|{P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f}\right\|_{{\mu_{1}},m}}\to 0\quad and\quad{\left\|{P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f}\right\|_{{\mu_{2}},m}}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
, where

\mu_{1}=c_{1}\delta_{\lambda_{1}}+c_{3}\delta_{\lambda_{3}}+\cdots+c_{s}\delta_{\lambda_{s}},

and

{\mu_{2}}={c_{2}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{2}}}}+{c_{3}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{3}}}}+\cdots+{c_{s}}{\delta_{{\lambda_{s}}}}.

Set
a=(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{-1}
and
b=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{-1}
, then

(4.14)

a(z-\lambda_{1})+b(z-\lambda_{2})=1,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Define

{h_{m,n}}(z)=a(z-{\lambda_{1}})P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f(z)+b(z-{\lambda_{2}})P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f(z).

According to (4.13), it is obvious that the first
n+s-1
coefficients of
{h_{m,n}}
coincide with the first
n+s-1
coefficients of
f
. Moreover, applying
(4.11)
and
(4.12)
, we obtain

{h_{m,n}}({\lambda_{1}})=P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f({\lambda_{1}})=f({\lambda_{1}}),

{h_{m,n}}({\lambda_{2}})=P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f({\lambda_{2}})=f({\lambda_{2}}).

For each
{\lambda_{j}}\in\left\{{{\lambda_{3}},{\lambda_{4,}}\cdots{\lambda_{s}}}\right\}
,

{h_{m,n}}({\lambda_{j}})=a({\lambda_{j}}-{\lambda_{1}})P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f({\lambda_{j}})+b({\lambda_{j}}-{\lambda_{2}})P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f({\lambda_{j}})=f({\lambda_{j}}).

By Theorem
4.5
(ii), if such
P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f
exists, it must be unique. Thus,
P_{m,n}^{\Lambda}f={h_{m,n}}
.
Now we aim to show that

{\left\|{{h_{m,n}}-f}\right\|_{\mu,m}}\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

According to (4.8) and (4.13), we know that

(4.15)

{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f))\to 0\quad\text{for}\ 1\leq k\leq s\ \text{~with~}\ k\neq 2,

and

(4.16)

{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f))\to 0\quad\text{for}\ 2\leq k\leq s.

By
(4.14)
, we have

{h_{m,n}}(z)-f(z)=a(z-{\lambda_{1}})(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f(z)-f(z))+b(z-{\lambda_{2}})(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f(z)-f(z)).

Noting that

P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f({\lambda_{k}})-f({\lambda_{k}})=P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f({\lambda_{k}})-f({\lambda_{k}})=0\quad\text{for}\ 3\leq k\leq s,

we have

{Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f)=a(z-{\lambda_{1}}){Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f)+b(z-{\lambda_{2}}){Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f).

Combining this with
(4.15)
and
(4.16)

\displaystyle{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f))

\displaystyle\leq 4\left|a\right|{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f))+4\left|b\right|{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f))

\displaystyle\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

We still need to show that it is also valid for
k=1
and
k=2
. Since
{h_{m,n}}({\lambda_{1}})-f({\lambda_{1}})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle{Q_{{\lambda_{1}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f)(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{{a(z-{\lambda_{1}})(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f(z)-f(z))+b(z-{\lambda_{2}})(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f(z)-f(z))}}{{z-{\lambda_{1}}}}

\displaystyle=a(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f)(z)+b(z-{\lambda_{2}}){Q_{{\lambda_{1}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f)(z),

which, together with
(4.13)
and
(4.15)
, indicates that

\displaystyle{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{1}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f))

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|a\right|{D_{\sigma,m-1}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{2}}}f-f)+4\left|b\right|{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{1}}}}(P_{m,n-1}^{{\Lambda_{1}}}f-f))

\displaystyle\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

Similarly discussion also implies that

{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{2}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f))\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

Consequently, we conclude that

\left\|{{h_{m,n}}-f}\right\|_{\mu,m}^{2}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{s}{{c_{k}}{D_{\sigma,m-1}}({Q_{{\lambda_{k}}}}({h_{m,n}}-f))}\to 0\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

Abstract

Related work and positioning

Historical note.

\Gamma
-convergence
was introduced by De Giorgi
(
8
)
;
see also the early note on variational convergence
(
7
)
.

Regge calculus and measure convergence.

Following the original discretization framework of Regge
(
17
)
, the closest rigorous results to our setting are the measure convergence results of Cheeger–Müller–Schrader for piecewise-flat spaces, showing that (Lipschitz–Killing) curvature measures of triangulations approximate their smooth counterparts
in the sense of measures

(
4
)
.
However, this line does not provide a
\Gamma
-limit for the Einstein–Hilbert (EH) action, nor does it address stability of minimizers.

Boundary terms in discrete gravity.

In the Regge–calculus framework
(
17
)
, boundary contributions compatible with the continuum Gibbons–Hawking–York (GHY) term were derived and analyzed
(
12
)
.
These works identify the right discrete form of the boundary term, yet they do not establish
\Gamma
-convergence of EH+GHY.

Graph and metric-measure formulations.

Discrete EH–type energies built from graph curvature (e.g. Ollivier curvature) have been shown to converge to continuum quantities in metric or Gromov–Hausdorff senses
(
13
)
.
Such results concern value/metric convergence and do not yield a
\Gamma
-limit for variational problems over metrics.

Continuum structure and boundary terms.

On the continuum side, the necessity and form of the GHY term for well-posed boundary variations are classical
(
21
;
10
)
.
The structural restriction of local, natural scalar densities of order
\leq 2
to combinations of
1
and
R
(up to divergences), and of boundary order
\leq 1
to
K
, follows from the general classification of natural operations and Lovelock-type arguments
(
14
;
15
)
.

Finite elements and boundary-fitted meshes.

Our mesh assumptions and boundary-fitting are standard in the finite element literature
(
5
;
3
)
, but these references do not address
\Gamma
-limits for EH+GHY.

MDL and model selection.

Our discrete value functional is cast in MDL terms and interpreted through code lengths and regret.
For background on MDL and stochastic complexity see
18
;
19
;

;
for universal coding/regret and the NML construction see

.
Prequential motivation is classical
(

)
.

Positioning and contribution.

To the best of our knowledge
, there is no published
\Gamma
-convergence result for a diffeomorphism-natural discrete gravity functional that converges to the full EH+GHY action with a boundary first-layer analysis.
Our contribution is to provide Carathéodory densities
f_{\mathrm{in}}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}R
and
f_{\mathrm{bdry}}=\beta_{1}K
, prove the
\liminf/\limsup
inequalities with a recovery sequence built from reflected Fermi smoothing, and thereby obtain a
\Gamma

c_{0}\!\int_{M}dV_{g}+c_{1}\!\int_{M}R_{g}\,dV_{g}+c_{2}\!\int_{\partial M}K_{g}\,dS_{g},

together with stability of minimizers

Proof roadmap

Our proof follows the standard De Giorgi
\Gamma
-convergence
paradigm
(
8
)
,
with the liminf/limsup steps adapted to the
G
-invariant setting.

We outline the logical dependencies and scales before entering the technical sections.

Scales and windows.

Interior cells live at volumetric scale
h^{d}
; boundary first-layer cells at surface scale
h^{d-1}
.
Normalized isotropic moments up to order 2 (interior) give an
even expansion
; the anisotropic normal window with
\langle t\rangle=\mu_{1}h
linearizes the boundary contribution.

TP1 (interior blow-up).

Second-jet expansion and parity yield

E_{n}(c)=h^{d}[\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}R(x)]+O(h^{d+2})
.
Leading order:
\Delta\ell(c)=O(h^{2d-2})
,
a_{n}\Delta\ell(c)=O(h^{d})
.

TP2 (boundary blow-up, first layer).

Reflected Fermi charts; Jacobian
1-tK+O(t^{2})
; anisotropic normal window.
Linearization gives
\psi(s,t)=\beta_{\rm loc}K(s)t+O(t^{2})+O(h)
and hence

E_{n}(c)=h^{d-1}[\beta_{1}K(s_{c})+O(h)]+o(h^{d-1})
with
\beta_{1}=\beta_{\rm loc}\mu_{1}
.

TP3 (Carathéodory and quasi-additivity).

Local densities
f_{\rm in}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}R
,
f_{\rm bdry}=\beta_{1}K
.
Lemma M: the additivity defect on
A\cup B
is controlled by a tube of volume
\asymp\delta_{n}\Per_{g}(A\cap B)
.
Counting: the number of tube cells is
\asymp\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}/h^{d}
, with a per-cell bound
\lesssim h^{d}
this yields a total defect
\lesssim\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}
.
We use the Carathéodory construction and the tubular estimate from geometric measure theory
(
9
)
.

TP4/TP5 (liminf/limsup).

Liminf on
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|_{C^{1}})
via a smoothing step
\tilde{g}_{n}=S_{\varepsilon_{n}}[g_{n}]
: apply Lemma U uniformly on a
C^{1,1}
neighbourhood for
\tilde{g}_{n}
, then pass
n\to\infty
and
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
.
Vitali and Besicovitch coverings control the global assembly
(
9
)
.
Limsup via recovery
g_{n}=S_{\eta h}[g]
:

R_{g_{n}}\to R_{g}
in
L^{1}(M)
,
K_{g_{n}}\to K_{g}
in
L^{1}(\partial M)
.
Global interior remainder
O(h^{2})
, boundary
O(h)
.

TP6/TP7 (structure, scaling, calibration).

Naturality in the sense of Peetre–Slovák and scaling
a_{n}\simeq h^{2-d}
force

f_{\rm in}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}R
and
f_{\rm bdry}=\beta_{1}K

(
14
;
15
)
.
Calibration fixes
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
.

Group and naturality.

Let

The discrete functionals

Geometry and regularity.

Dimension
d\geq 2
. The manifold
M
is compact, orientable, of class
C^{2}
with (possibly)
C^{2}
boundary
\partial M
.
We work on
\mathcal{X}\subset C^{1,1}
under

\iota_{0}>0

\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}\|_{\infty}\leq C

Uniform ellipticity

Hence, in a fixed finite-overlap atlas there exist constants

Boundary reach.

\reach_{g}(\partial M)
denotes the tubular radius of the normal exponential map of

\partial M

Boundary regularity convention (no corners).

We assume

with no corners/edges. Corner (codimension-two) terms are excluded and do not appear in
the first-layer boundary analysis (TP2), the liminf/limsup arguments, or the integral representation.

Boundary-fitted meshes.

We are given a sequence of shape-regular, boundary-fitted partitions
\{\mathcal{T}_{n}\}
with meshsize
h_{n}\downarrow 0
;
the discrete boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}_{n}
converges to
\partial M
in Hausdorff distance at rate

.
This is the standard finite-element mesh setting
(

)

Besicovitch constant.

On manifolds with

there exists a finite overlap constant

for Vitali/Besicovitch coverings
(

)
.

Mesoscale and scan.

We use block windows
B_{r_{n}}(c)
with
r_{n}\to\infty
and
R_{n}:=r_{n}h_{n}\to 0
; set
\delta_{n}:=R_{n}
.
Example:

Crucial scale separation:

\Phi_{n}(c)
is computed from data

(interior support

Thus the effective averaging radius is

serves only for scan-indifference (BA3).

Scan indifference (BA3).

For the formal statement of (BA3) see the compact BA list below; a full proof is given in Appendix F.

MDL functional and scaling.

For each cell
c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
a local, normalized statistic
\Phi_{n}(c)
is computed from
B_{r_{n}}(c)
.
The loss
\ell\in C^{2}
is defined on a compact feature set
K_{\mathrm{feat}}
and obeys

\sup_{u\in K_{\mathrm{feat}}}(\|D\ell(u)\|+\|D^{2}\ell(u)\|)<\infty

Define the code-length
L_{n}(g_{n})=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\ell(\Phi_{n}(c))
and the calibrated excess

\Delta L_{n}:=L_{n}(g_{n})-L_{n}^{\text{flat\_ref}}
.
Our use of MDL follows the classical literature
(

;
11
;

;
20
;
6
)
.
We fix the scaling

\boxed{\,h:=h_{n},\qquad a_{n}\simeq h^{\,2-d},\qquad F_{n}(g_{n}):=a_{n}\,\Delta L_{n}\,}.

Interior windows and moments (scale
h
).

Work in dimensionless local variables
\xi:=z/h
(normal coordinates around the cell).
The interior window
w_{\mathrm{in}}(\xi)\geq 0
has compact support
|\xi|\leq\Lambda_{z}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w_{\mathrm{in}}(\xi)\,d\xi=1
.
Isotropy up to order 2:

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}\,\delta^{ij},

\displaystyle\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\xi^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

and uniform moment bounds
\langle|\xi|^{k}\rangle\leq C_{k}
(e.g.
k\leq 4
, optional
k\leq 6
).
In physical variables
z=h\xi
, this is equivalent to
support

|z|\leq\Lambda_{z}h
and

\langle|z|^{k}\rangle\leq C_{k}\,h^{\,k}
.
A uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound
\sup_{n}\|w_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{\infty}<\infty
is
not
required.

Boundary windows (anisotropic).

In Fermi coordinates
(z^{\prime},t)
with
t
the inward normal coordinate, tangential scale is
O(h)
and normal depth is
O(h)
.
Equivalently, we use the 1D normal kernel

w_{n}(t)=h^{-1}\,w_{0}(t/h),\qquad\mathrm{supp}(w_{0})\subset[0,c_{*}],\quad\int_{0}^{c_{*}}\!w_{0}=1.

Then
\langle t\rangle=\mu_{1}h
and
\langle t^{k}\rangle\leq C_{k}h^{\,k}
.
The constant
c_{*}\in(0,1)
is fixed (depends only on the shape-class).
We fix this anisotropic boundary window class throughout; all constants below depend only on the constants bracket and this shape-class.
At the first boundary layer the leading per-cell term is of order
h^{d-1}
(not
o(h^{d-1})
); summation over first-layer cells yields the boundary integral and an
O(h)
global remainder (see TP2).

BA assumptions (visible).

•

(BA1)

\ell\in C^{2}
on the compact feature set; normalized moments up to order 2 (interior) and order 1 (boundary) are smoothly computable.

•

(BA2)

(Assumption; automatically satisfied for the boundary windows of Section

under the reflected Fermi-layer setup)
:
For reflected Fermi layers of thickness

with anisotropic windows (fixed

,
\mu_{1}
, bounded moments), the per-layer contribution for

is
O(h^{\,d})
; summing layers yields
\leq C\,\varepsilon\,\Area(\partial M)
.

•

(BA3) Scan indifference.

There is a universal
C<\infty
such that for any scanning orders
\sigma,\tau

\sup_{\sigma,\tau}\bigl|F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)-F_{n}^{\tau}(M)\bigr|\;\leq\;C\,\delta_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)\;=\;o(1).

A proof is given in Appendix F.

We keep (BA2) as an explicit assumption to cover general window families; for the fixed windows of Section
3
it holds with constants depending only on the constants bracket. Hypothesis (BA2) is used only in the boundary assembly (TP3/TP4/TP5) to bound non-first-layer terms; it is

4
Assumptions, exclusions, and conventions (EC, boundary, perimeter)

Equi-coercivity regime (EC).

We do
not
claim coercivity of the EH(+GHY) action.
Throughout,
equi-coercivity
means that sublevel sets of
\{F_{n}\}
are precompact in
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|_{C^{1}})
.
This matches the standard notion in
\Gamma
-convergence
(
16
)
.
It holds automatically in constrained settings where one imposes, for some
C<\infty
,

1.

uniform ellipticity and a uniform
C^{1,1}
bound on admissible metrics
(g\in\mathcal{X})
,

2.

fixed volume and boundary area bounds to prevent collapse,

3.

an a priori
L^{1}
bound on the negative part
R_{g}^{-}
and on
K_{g}^{-}
along
\partial M
to exclude conformal blow downs.

Any variational problem under these constraints, or any equivalent precompactness mechanism, falls into our EC regime.

Boundary regularity; corner exclusion.

We assume
\partial M\in C^{2}
; there are
no
corners or edges.
Polygonal/polyhedral boundaries generate additional corner terms not covered by the first-layer analysis.
Our boundary-fitted meshes approximate a smooth
C^{2}
boundary with Hausdorff error
O(h)
.

Perimeter convention.

\Per_{g}(\cdot)
denotes the De Giorgi perimeter with respect to
g
.
We use the tubular estimate
\Vol_{g}(N_{\delta}(E))\asymp\delta\,\Per_{g}(E)
for
(d-1)
rectifiable
E
, as standard in geometric measure theory
(
9
)
.
All counting constants are absorbed in the constants bracket.

5
TP1 — Interior blow-up: 2-jet and even expansion

Proposition 5.1

(TP1: interior cell asymptotics)
.

Let
c_{n}\subset M^{\circ}
be interior cells with
\mathrm{dist}(c_{n},\partial M)\geq C\,R_{n}
and shape-regularity uniform in
n
.
Assume the normalized, isotropic moment conditions up to order 2 for the interior window, and
\ell\in C^{2}
on the compact feature set
K_{\mathrm{feat}}
.
Then there exist constants
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, depending only on the data in the constants bracket, such that

E_{n}(c_{n};g):=a_{n}\Bigl[\ell\bigl(\Phi_{n}(c_{n};g)\bigr)-\ell\bigl(\Phi_{n}(c_{n};\mathrm{flat\_ref})\bigr)\Bigr]=h^{d}\,\bigl[\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\,R_{g}(x)\bigr]\;+\;O(h^{d+2}),

uniformly on compact subsets of
M^{\circ}
. In particular,

\Delta\ell(c_{n})=O(h^{2d-2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{n}\,\Delta\ell(c_{n})=O(h^{d})\quad\text{with }a_{n}\simeq h^{2-d}.

Proof.

Constants bracket.
All implicit constants in the
O(\cdot)
,
o(\cdot)
terms below depend only on the data listed in Section
13
.
Fix
x\in c_{n}
and work in normal coordinates
z=(z^{1},\dots,z^{d})
centered at
x
.
By bounded geometry and standard normal-coordinate expansions (uniform on compact sets),

\displaystyle g_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}-\tfrac{1}{3}R_{ikjl}(x)\,z^{k}z^{l}+O(|z|^{3}),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det g(z)}

\displaystyle=1-\tfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{Ric}_{kl}(x)\,z^{k}z^{l}+O(|z|^{3}),

(5.2)

as
|z|\to 0
, and
R_{ijkl}(z)=R_{ijkl}(x)+O(|z|)
.
Shape-regularity and the distance-to-boundary condition imply that the support of the interior window around
c_{n}
lies in a normal chart and satisfies
|z|\lesssim h
.

Step 1: Even expansion from moment conditions.

Work with the dimensionless variable
\xi:=z/h
.
The interior window is normalized, compactly supported and isotropic up to order 2:

\langle\xi^{i}\rangle=0
,
\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\rangle=\mu_{2}\delta^{ij}
,
\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\xi^{k}\rangle=0
,
which in physical variables yields
\langle|z|^{k}\rangle\leq C_{k}h^{k}
for the required
k
.
Hence any integrand whose Taylor polynomial at order
\leq 3
is
odd
in
z
averages to zero.
Consequently, all
O(h)
contributions vanish and the first nonzero terms arise at order
O(h^{2})
in the
local
variables.

Step 2: Feature increments are
O(h^{2})
.

Let
\Phi_{n}(c;g)
be the normalized feature vector computed on the mesoscale block
B_{r_{n}}(c)
(cf. Section
3
).
By (
5.1
)–(
5.2
), each smooth local invariant entering the features has an expansion whose coefficients are (tensorial) polynomials in the 2-jet of
g
at
x
, with remainder
O(|z|^{3})
.
Because
|z|\lesssim h
on the support and odd terms average to zero, every component of

\Delta\Phi_{n}:=\Phi_{n}(c;g)-\Phi_{n}(c;\mathrm{flat\_ref})

satisfies

\Delta\Phi_{n}=O(h^{2})

(5.3)

uniformly on compact subsets, where
\mathrm{flat\_ref}
is chosen as in Appendix C (Lemmas
C.1
–
C.2
) so as to match volume and the normalization moments (hence suppressing any
O(h)
mismatch).

Step 3: Taylor of
\ell
and cell scaling.

Since
\ell\in C^{2}
on the compact feature set

With the scaling

Step 4: Identification of the leading density.

Define the rescaled cell functional

H_{n}(j^{2}g(x))\;:=\;h^{-d}\,E_{n}(c;g)\,.

By Steps 1–3,

depends smoothly on the 2-jet of

for a smooth function

By diffeomorphism naturality, locality, and order
\leq 2
in the sense of Peetre–Slovák,

is a natural scalar density of order at most two
(
14
)
.
Together with isotropy of the averaging window and the even expansion, the only order-
\leq 2
scalar invariants compatible with the interior volumetric scaling are the constant density and the scalar curvature:

Interior total divergences reduce to boundary contributions and therefore do not affect the principal bulk density identified in TP1; they are handled by the boundary calibration via the Euclidean ball (TP7, case D3) and vanish at the leading scales.
Higher Lovelock densities are excluded by the scaling test (cf. TP6)
(
15
)
.

Step 5: Remainder
O(h^{d+2})
and uniformity.

The remainder arises from the

5.2
) and from the quadratic Taylor remainder of

Since

and the fourth (and higher) moments are uniformly bounded, these contributions accumulate to

Uniformity on compact subsets of

follows from bounded geometry and Lemma U (uniform in a small

Collecting the above gives

with
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
depending only on the constants bracket; mesh independence is stated in Lemma S (Section

∎

Proposition 6.1

(TP2: boundary cell asymptotics, first layer)
.

Sign convention.

Throughout, the

Let

be the set of boundary-touching cells

in reflected Fermi charts
(z^{\prime},t)
along
\partial M

Work in reflected Fermi coordinates
(z^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle dV_{g}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-t\,K_{g}(s)+O(t^{2})\bigr)\,dz^{\prime}\,dt,

(6.1)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=dt^{2}+h_{\alpha\beta}(s,t)\,dz^{\alpha}dz^{\beta},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{\alpha\beta}(s,0)

\displaystyle=-2\,II_{\alpha\beta}(s),

(6.3)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta}(s,t)

\displaystyle=h_{\alpha\beta}(s,0)+O(t).

(6.4)

Here

These tube and Jacobian expansions are standard and used with uniform constants from bounded geometry; see also the tubular neighbourhood estimates in geometric measure theory
(

The

Windows and moments.

By assumption, the averaging window separates normal and tangential variables:

Note that

Quasi-additivity: the perimeter control

Lemma 7.2

(Lemma M: interior quasi-additivity via perimeter)
.

There exists
C<\infty
(depending only on the constants bracket) such that for all Borel sets

with

\operatorname{dist}_{g}\big(\partial^{\ast}(A\cap B),\,\partial M\big)\ \geq\ \varepsilon_{0}>0,

is the geodesic
\delta_{n}
-neighbourhood and

.

The defect of additivity is supported in the set of cells that intersect both

and
B
, hence in the
\delta_{n}
-tube around the interface

By standard GMT tubular estimates on manifolds with bounded geometry
(

)

\Vol_{g}\!\bigl(N_{\delta_{n}}\big((A\cap B)\cap M^{\circ}\big)\bigr)\;\asymp\;\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}\big((A\cap B)\cap M^{\circ}\big),

(7.2)

with constants depending only on the geometry (absorbed in the constants bracket).
Each cell has volume
\asymp h^{d}
, hence the number of tube cells satisfies

\displaystyle\#\{\text{tube cells}\}\;

\displaystyle\asymp\;\frac{\Vol_{g}\!\big(N_{\delta_{n}}\big((A\cap B)\cap M^{\circ}\big)\big)}{h^{d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\;\frac{\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}\big((A\cap B)\cap M^{\circ}\big)}{h^{d}}.

\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\partial^{\ast}(A\cap B),\partial M)\geq\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\bigl|F_{n}(A\cup B)-F_{n}(A)-F_{n}(B)\bigr|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\#\times\sup_{c}|E_{n}(c)|

\displaystyle\;\lesssim\;\frac{\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}(A\cap B)}{h^{d}}\cdot h^{d}

\displaystyle\;=\;C\,\delta_{n}\,\Per_{g}\big((A\cap B)\cap M^{\circ}\big).

as claimed. Finite overlap
N(d,\iota_{0})
from Besicovitch coverings is used implicitly to sum local contributions without loss.
∎

Interior cells

Lemma C.1

(Existence and matching to second order)
.

Let

be an interior cell of diameter
\asymp h
and shape-regularity uniformly controlled.
In normal coordinates centered at a point

g_{0}=\delta
on a cell with the

with
\lambda_{h}=O(h^{2})

1.

\Vol_{g_{0}}(c)=\Vol_{g}(c)
(volume matching),

2.

the interior window

(support
\subset B_{\Lambda_{z}h}

3.

the normalized feature vector

Proof.

Work in normal coordinates
z
at
x
. On

\displaystyle g_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+Q_{ij,kl}\,z^{k}z^{l}+O(|z|^{3}),

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det g(z)}

\displaystyle=1-\tfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{Ric}_{kl}(x)\,z^{k}z^{l}+O(|z|^{3}).

Define
g_{0}=\delta
on a cell

Interpreted at the cell scale (with the TP1 aggregation/scaling) this yields
\Delta\ell(c)=O(h^{2d-2})

∎

Lemma C.2

(Quasi-uniqueness up to

)

Appendix F
Scan indifference (proof of BA3)

Model for scan-dependence.

Let
F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)
denote the MDL aggregate computed by scanning cells
c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
in order
\sigma
.
We write the per-cell contribution as a local update

\Delta_{\sigma}(c)\ :=\ a_{n}\Bigl[\ell\bigl(\Phi_{n}(c;\,H_{\sigma}(c))\bigr)-\ell\bigl(\Phi_{n}(c;\mathrm{flat\_ref})\bigr)\Bigr],\qquad F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\Delta_{\sigma}(c),

where
H_{\sigma}(c)
denotes the scan history prior to visiting
c
.
By construction (Section
3
), the features
\Phi_{n}
are computed from a block window

B_{r_{n}}(c)
of mesoscale radius
R_{n}:=r_{n}h\to 0
and are
local
and
natural
.

Lemma F.1

(Localization of scan influence)
.

For any scans
\sigma,\tau
and any cell
c
,

\Delta_{\sigma}(c)=\Delta_{\tau}(c)

whenever
B_{r_{n}}(c)\subset M_{R_{n}}:=\{x:\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial M)>R_{n}\}
.
In particular, the set of cells with potentially scan-dependent increments is contained in

\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\mathrm{bdry}}(R_{n})\ :=\ \{\,c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}:\ B_{r_{n}}(c)\cap N_{R_{n}}(\partial M)\neq\emptyset\,\}.

Proof.

If
B_{r_{n}}(c)\subset M_{R_{n}}
, then any two histories
H_{\sigma}(c),H_{\tau}(c)
differ only outside
B_{r_{n}}(c)
.
Locality of
\Phi_{n}
implies
\Phi_{n}(c;H_{\sigma}(c))=\Phi_{n}(c;H_{\tau}(c))
, hence
\Delta_{\sigma}(c)=\Delta_{\tau}(c)
.
∎

Lemma F.2

(Counting boundary-affected cells)
.

There exists
C<\infty
, depending only on the constants bracket, such that

\displaystyle\#\,\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\mathrm{bdry}}(R_{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{\Vol_{g}\!\big(N_{R_{n}}(\partial M)\big)}{h^{d}}

(F.1)

\displaystyle\asymp C\,\frac{R_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)}{h^{d}}\;=\;C\,\frac{\delta_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)}{h^{d}}.

(F.2)

Recall
\delta_{n}:=R_{n}
.

Proof.

Shape-regular cells have volume
\asymp h^{d}
.
By the tubular estimate on manifolds with bounded geometry,

\Vol_{g}(N_{R_{n}}(\partial M))\asymp R_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)
.
Every boundary-affected cell lies in this tube; divide by
h^{d}
.
∎

Proposition F.3

(BA3: scan indifference)
.

There is
C<\infty
(constants bracket) such that for all scans
\sigma,\tau
,

\bigl|F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)-F_{n}^{\tau}(M)\bigr|\ \leq\ C\,\delta_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
F.1
,

\displaystyle F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)-F_{n}^{\tau}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\mathrm{bdry}}(R_{n})}\bigl(\Delta_{\sigma}(c)-\Delta_{\tau}(c)\bigr).

By TP1/TP2 and Lemma U (uniformity on a
C^{1,1}
-neighbourhood), the per-cell increment satisfies

|\Delta_{\bullet}(c)|\leq C\,h^{d}
(interior bound; first-layer boundary terms scale
h^{d-1}
and pick up the depth

).
Hence

\displaystyle\bigl|F_{n}^{\sigma}(M)-F_{n}^{\tau}(M)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\#\,\mathcal{T}_{n}^{\mathrm{bdry}}(R_{n})\cdot C\,h^{d}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Vol_{g}(N_{R_{n}}(\partial M))}{h^{d}}\cdot C\,h^{d}

\displaystyle\asymp C\,R_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M)\;=\;C\,\delta_{n}\,\Area_{g}(\partial M).

∎

(Scope)

Entropy, entanglement and susceptibility of three qubits near quantum criticality
Bastian Castorene, Francisco J. Peña, Ariel Norambuena, Sergio E. Ulloa, Cristobal Araya, Patricio Vargas

Abstract
In this work, we investigate a system of three entangled qubits within the XXX model, subjected to an external magnetic field in the z z -direction and incorporating an anisotropy term along the y y -axis. We explore the system’s thermodynamics by calculating its magnetic susceptibility and analyzing how this quantity encodes information about entanglement. By deriving rigorous bounds for the susceptibility, we demonstrate that violations of these bounds serve as an entanglement witness. Our results show that anisotropy enhances entanglement, extending the temperature range over which it persists. Furthermore, when the system is traced over the degrees of freedom of two qubits, the reduced entropy of the remaining single qubit corresponds to that of a system in a thermal bath with temperature T > 0 T>0 K.

IV.2
Magnetic Susceptibility

Magnetic susceptibility is a classical thermodynamic quantity that can be experimentally measured and serves as an entanglement witness. It can be derived from the magnetization expression Eq. (

\displaystyle\chi_{z}=T\evaluated{\left(\frac{\partial^2{\ln Z}}{\partial B^2}\right)_{T}}_{B=0}=\evaluated{\left(\pdv{M}{B}\right)_{T}}_{B=0}

In the special case of a zero magnetic field, the magnetic susceptibility takes the following form:

\displaystyle\chi_{z}(J,\alpha,K,T)=\frac{1}{2T}\quantity(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2K}\frac{K+3T-3e^{\frac{2K}{T}}(K+T)}{1+e^{\frac{2K}{T}}\qty(1+e^{\frac{3J}{2T}}+e^{\frac{J(1+2\alpha)}{2T}})})

We analyze the high-temperature inverse susceptibility behavior of the system in this scenario, resulting in the form of the Curie-Weiss law:

\displaystyle 1/\chi_{z}\xrightarrow{T\gg 1}\frac{4T}{3}+\frac{2J}{9}(2+\alpha)

We observe from the last Equation. (

V
Conclusions

We have conducted a comprehensive study of the Heisenberg
XXX
model subjected to an external magnetic field along the
z
-axis, incorporating an anisotropy term in the
y
-direction. Our analysis focused on the behavior of an isolated qubit within this system, revealing the emergence of an effective temperature resulting from its interaction with the surrounding qubits. This temperature manifests in the reduced entropy of the system’s ground state, obtained by tracing over the degrees of freedom of the other two qubits.

In the ferromagnetic regime, our results indicate that the effective temperature is highly sensitive to both the anisotropy constant and the external magnetic field. In contrast, in the antiferromagnetic ring regime—where the ground state does not depend on anisotropy—the system is susceptible to the initial weighting of the prepared states.

These findings underscore the pivotal role of anisotropy, ground-state weighting, and magnetic interactions in shaping the system’s thermodynamic properties.

Additionally, we computed the magnetic susceptibility across various regimes—antiferromagnetic, ferromagnetic, and paramagnetic. By establishing rigorous bounds on susceptibility for non-entangled states as a function of temperature, we identified the presence of entanglement when these bounds were violated.

Our findings show that entanglement occurs at low temperatures, specifically in the antiferromagnetic regime. Moreover, introducing anisotropy enhances the entanglement, extending the temperature range over which it persists. This enhancement underscores the intricate relationship between quantum interactions and thermodynamic properties, demonstrating the profound impact of anisotropy on the system’s entanglement dynamics.

We can construct a density matrix that is a direct tensor product of individual separable
\rho_{i}

\displaystyle\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{n}

(28)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{i}=p_{i}\ket{0}\bra{0}+(1-p_{i})\ket{1}\bra{1}

Here,

is a scalar between 0 and 1 associated with the state probability.

The total spin operators for the
n

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=\\

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{kk^{\prime}}^{n}\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\sigma_{\alpha k}\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}k^{\prime}}

The total magnetic susceptibility can be expressed in terms of magnetic fluctuations as:

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{\expectationvalue{\vu{S}_n^2}-\expectationvalue{\vu{S}_n}^{2}}{k_{B}T}

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\mathcal{O}}=\Tr{\rho\cdot\mathcal{O}}

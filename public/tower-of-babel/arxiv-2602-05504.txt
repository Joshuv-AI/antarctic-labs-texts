Continuized Nesterov Momentum Achieves the 𝒪 ( ε − 7 / 4 ) \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4}) Complexity without Additional Mechanisms
Julien Hermant, Jean-François Aujol, Charles Dossal, Lorick Huang, Aude Rondepierre

Abstract
For first-order optimization of non-convex functions with Lipschitz continuous gradient and Hessian, the best known complexity for reaching an ε \varepsilon -approximation of a stationary point is 𝒪 ( ε − 7 / 4 ) \mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{-7/4}\right) . Existing algorithms achieving this bound are based on momentum, but are always complemented with safeguard mechanisms, such as restarts or negative-curvature exploitation steps. Whether such mechanisms are fundamentally necessary has remained an open question. Leveraging the continuized method, we show that a Nesterov momentum algorithm with stochastic parameters alone achieves the same complexity in expectation. This result holds up to a multiplicative stochastic factor with unit expectation and a restriction to a subset of the realizations, both of which are independent of the objective function. We empirically verify that these constitute mild limitations.

Introduction

Many real-world problems can be formulated as the minimization of a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, that is, as
\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)
. Yet, without structural assumptions—such as convexity of
f
—computing a global minimizer is generally intractable
(
20
, Section 2.2)
. In this work, assuming
f
is lower bounded and differentiable, our goal is thus to find a stationary point of
f
,
i.e.
approximate
x^{\ast}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\nabla f(x^{\ast})=0.

The rising computational cost and expanding data scales in modern applications—ranging from machine learning to signal processing—have made
first-order optimization algorithms
widely used. These algorithms rely on gradient information to compute their iterates; gradient descent being a classic example. Among existing acceleration techniques designed to improve their convergence speed
35
;
39
, one of the most influential is
momentum
, whose idea dates back to the seminal work of
55
.

Nesterov momentum

The
Nesterov momentum
(
NM
) algorithm
50
, sometimes called
Nesterov accelerated gradient
, is known to achieve optimal convergence bounds among first-order algorithms in various settings in convex optimization
48
;
52
, see Appendix
A.1
for a more detailed discussion.
One way to write Nesterov momentum is the following

\left\{\begin{aligned} &y_{n}=x_{n}+\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1}),&\qquad&\longleftarrow\ \textit{momentum step}\\
&x_{n+1}=y_{n}-s\nabla f(y_{n}).&\qquad&\longleftarrow\ \textit{gradient step}\end{aligned}\right.

(NM)

The momentum step can be viewed as a memory term, which disappears in the case of gradient descent (
\alpha_{n}=0
in (
NM
)). Because a wide range of applications are formulated as a non-convex optimization problem
29
;
13
;
21
;
10
;
24
;
23
;
41
, an important question is whether the benefits of momentum extend beyond convex optimization. Empirically, it is often observed that the use of momentum improves the speed of convergence for non-convex minimization tasks such as neural network optimization
61
;
30
.
Theoretically, acceleration has been shown in some mild non-convex settings where finding a global minimizer remains tractable, see
e.g.

34
;
32
;
28
. In this work, we only adopt
smoothness
assumptions, which fall outside regimes where guarantees of global minimization can be obtained.

Lipschitz Gradient

When the objective
f
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient, finding a
\varepsilon
-stationary point,
i.e.

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)\right\rVert\leq\varepsilon
, can be achieved by gradient descent in at most
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
gradient evaluations
(
52
, (1.2.22))
. This bound is worst-case optimal among first-order algorithms
16
. The momentum mechanism thus does not provably accelerate gradient descent, achieving a similar rate
25
. This impossibility result suggests that the class of functions with merely Lipschitz-continuous gradients does not provide an adequate framework for understanding the acceleration mechanisms observed in practice. This motivates the study of higher-order smoothness assumptions.

Lipschitz Hessian

Assuming in addition that the Hessian is Lipschitz continuous, the aforementioned bound can be improved to
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})

14
;
37
;
42
.
This class of functions therefore admits accelerated rates, while still encompassing important non-convex problems, such as matrix factorization
36
. All existing first-order algorithms achieving this complexity rely on (
NM
) as their core update. However, these methods also need to perform a
security check
at each iteration. If it fails, an
alternative mechanism
is triggered. At a conceptual level, these procedures can be summarized as follows:

if

Security check succeed

then

1

Run update rule (
NM
)
else

2

Trigger alternative mechanism

Algorithm 1

Structure of existing algorithms achieving the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
complexity

In
14
;
37
, the security check monitors the level of non-convexity between consecutive iterates; when it exceeds a threshold, a negative-curvature exploitation step is triggered as an alternative mechanism.
42
employs a security check based on the length of the trajectory residual; if it becomes too large, a restart mechanism is activated. Detailed algorithmic descriptions and further discussion are provided in Appendix
A.3
.
This raises the following critical question:

Is (
NM
) itself fundamentally unable to achieve the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
complexity ?

In a recent result,
53
shows that the time needed for the solution of the Heavy Ball ordinary differential equation (ODE) to find a
\varepsilon
-stationary solution is at most
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
. Importantly, the Heavy Ball ODE can be viewed as a continuous-time analogue of momentum algorithms
59
;
58
. This raises the question of whether this result can be transferred to the discrete setting. To our knowledge, no such discrete counterpart currently exists.

Contribution

To bridge the gap between the continuous result of
53
and the discrete setting, we study the
continuized version
of Nesterov momentum

. Its analysis involves a process that mixes continuous momentum and gradient steps at random times, leading to an alternative formulation of (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{k}&=\tilde{x}_{k}+\alpha_{k}(\tilde{z}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k}),\\
\tilde{x}_{k+1}&=\tilde{y}_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k}),\\
\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=\tilde{z}_{k}+\beta_{k}(\tilde{y}_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k})-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k}),\end{array}\right.

where the sequences
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\beta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are stochastic.
Noting
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
the underlying probability space, our main result is the following (see Theorem

(Informal version)

Related works

First order acceleration under Lipschitz gradient and Hessian
The
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
complexity with a first-order algorithm was first achieved by
14
.
The proposed nested-loop algorithm combines Nesterov momentum (
NM
) with negative-curvature exploitation and the minimization of a regularized surrogate function. This approach is subsequently simplified in

Replacing the negative curvature exploitation with a restart mechanism,
42

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
bound, additionally improving by a logarithmic factor. This restart-based line of work has since been extended to a parameter-free algorithm
43
, a forward-backward version

57

. The existing lower bound on the number of gradient evaluations needed to find a
\varepsilon
-stationary point in this setting is
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-12/7})

17
, which does not match the best known upper bound
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4})
. Closing this gap remains an open problem.

Second-order stationary points
In contrast to our setting,
several works have focused on second-order stationary points,
which under suitable assumptions correspond to local minimizers, see a formal definition in Appendix
A.2
. With probability
1-\delta
, such a point is found in at most
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-7/4}\log(\frac{d}{\delta\varepsilon}))
gradient and Hessian-vector product evaluations
1
;
15
, or with gradient evaluation only
37
;
42
. Nevertheless, we note that (i) first-order procedures exist that transform algorithms for finding first-order stationary points into ones that find second-order stationary points

;
2
and (ii) under random initialization, gradient-based methods typically converge almost surely to local minimizers —see
40
for gradient descent,
60
for the heavy ball algorithm.

Continuized Nesterov
The continuized Nesterov equations are introduced in

, motivated by the study of asynchronous algorithms. They show that the continuized Nesterov algorithm recovers, in expectation, the existing rate of the classic Nesterov Momentum for (strongly)-convex functions, further generalized to (strongly)-quasar convex functions

;
31
. This method has known some extensions in the specific field of decentralized asynchronous algorithms

;
46
, but remains largely unexplored in optimization.

2.1
Continuized Nesterov Algorithm

In this section, we describe the process studied in this work, and the resulting algorithm. An intuitive way to present the process is as follows: consider a sequence of random times
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
T_{0}=0
and
T_{k+1}-T_{k}\overset{i.i.d}{\sim}\mathcal{E}(1)
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For some constant parameters
\eta,\eta^{\prime},\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
, we define a continuous-time process
(x_{t},z_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
on each interval
(T_{k},T_{k+1})
as the solution of the following equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d{x}_{t}&=\eta(z_{t}-x_{t})dt,\\
d{z}_{t}&=\eta^{\prime}(x_{t}-z_{t})dt.\end{array}\right.

At
t=T_{k}
, the process jumps by performing gradient steps,

x_{T_{k}}=x_{T_{k}^{-}}-\gamma\nabla f(x_{T_{k}^{-}}),\quad z_{T_{k}}=z_{T_{k}^{-}}-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(z_{T_{k}^{-}}).

This defines the
continuized Nesterov equation
.
It can be written in a more compact form as the following stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dx_{t}&=\eta(z_{t}-x_{t})dt-\gamma\nabla f(x_{t^{-}})dN_{t},\\
dz_{t}&=\eta^{\prime}(x_{t}-z_{t})dt-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(x_{t^{-}})dN_{t},\end{array}\right.

where
dN_{t}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\delta_{T_{k}}(dt)
is a Poisson point measure with intensity
dt
. It mixes the continuous component, the
dt
factor, with the gradient component that acts at discrete random times, the
dN_{t}
factor.
A remarkable property of (
2.1
) is that, upon defining the sequences
\tilde{y}_{k}:=x_{T_{k+1}^{-}}
,
\tilde{x}_{k}:=x_{T_{k}}
and
\tilde{z}_{k}:=z_{T_{k}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, they satisfy a recursive relation that takes the form of a Nesterov momentum algorithm, with stochastic parameters depending on the random times
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Proposition 2.3

(
31
, Proposition 5)
.

Let
(x_{t},z_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
follow (
2.1
) with
\eta+\eta^{\prime}>0
and with underlying jumping times
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Define
\tilde{y}_{k}:=x_{T_{k+1}^{-}}
,
\tilde{x}_{k+1}:=x_{T_{k+1}}
and
\tilde{z}_{k+1}:=z_{T_{k+1}}
as evaluations of this process. Then,
(\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
writes as the following continuized Nesterov algorithm (
2.3
)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{k}&=\frac{\eta}{\eta+\eta^{\prime}}\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right)(\tilde{z}_{k}-\tilde{x}_{k})+\tilde{x}_{k}\\
\tilde{x}_{k+1}&=\tilde{y}_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k})\\
\tilde{z}_{k+1}&=\tilde{z}_{k}+\eta^{\prime}\frac{\left(1-e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}\right)}{\eta^{\prime}+\eta e^{-(\eta+\eta^{\prime})(T_{k+1}-T_{k})}}(\tilde{y}_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k})-\gamma^{\prime}\nabla f(\tilde{y}_{k})\end{array}\right.

In words, (
2.3
) is an exact evaluation of a realization of (
2.1
). Intuitively, exact access to the continuous process (
2.1
) is possible because gradient information is only queried at discrete times. Because the random times
\{T_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
can be readily simulated, (
2.3
) is implementable.
This direct correspondence between (
2.3
) and (
2.1
) is powerful: it enables convergence guarantees for a discrete algorithm to be derived via continuous-time analysis. This comes at the cost that even with deterministic gradients, the resulting algorithm is inherently stochastic, thus leading to non-deterministic convergence guarantees.

On the formulation of the algorithm
(
2.3
) is a three-point formulation of Nesterov’s algorithm, contrasting with the two-point formulation of (
NM
). These two formulations are related, see
(
33
, Appendix B.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\Lambda_{t}\left\lVert\nabla f(\overline{x}_{t})\right\rVert dN_{t}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\Lambda_{t}\left\lVert\frac{w_{t}(t)(z_{t}-x_{t})}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\right\rVert dN_{t}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\alpha^{2}L_{2}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\eta^{2}\left\lVert z_{s}-x_{s}\right\rVert^{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T_{n}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\alpha(\tau-t)}e^{\alpha(\sigma-t)}(\tau-\sigma)dN_{\sigma}dN_{\tau}dN_{t}\right)ds\right]

(52)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}\alpha^{2}L_{2}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\gamma^{2}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{s^{-}})\right\rVert^{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T_{n}}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\alpha(\tau-t)}e^{\alpha(\sigma-t)}(N_{\tau^{-}}-N_{\sigma^{-}})dN_{\sigma}dN_{\tau}dN_{t}\right)dN_{s}\right].

(53)

We bound each term of the above equation.

1. Bounding the right hand side of (

).

Recalling
w_{t}(t)=\frac{\alpha e^{\alpha t}}{\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha s}dN_{s}}
, one has

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{w_{t}(t)(z_{t}-x_{t})}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha e^{\alpha t}}{(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)}\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha s}dN_{s}}\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)}\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-t)}dN_{s}}\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert

So

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\Lambda_{t}\left\lVert\frac{w_{t}(t)(z_{t}-x_{t})}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\right\rVert dN_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-t)}dN_{s}\right)\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert dN_{t}\right]

(54)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\Lambda_{t}dN_{t}\right]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}\right]},

where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t^{-}}-x_{t^{-}}-(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)\nabla f(x_{t^{-}})\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t^{-}}-x_{t^{-}}\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}+2(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{t^{-}})\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}

From (
49
), we have

\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert\nabla f(x_{t^{-}})\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}\leq\frac{4}{\gamma}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}),

(55)

and from (
50

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t^{-}}-x_{t^{-}}\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\eta+\eta^{\prime}}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast})=\frac{1}{\alpha}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}).

(56)

Combining (
55
) and (
56
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left\lVert z_{t}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}dN_{t}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{4(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)^{2}}{\gamma}}

(57)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\alpha^{-1}(f(x_{0})-f^{\ast})}\sqrt{1+\frac{4(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)^{2}}{\gamma}},

the last inequality using

. Note that we assume

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\Lambda_{t}\left\lVert\frac{w_{t}(t)(z_{t^{-}}-x_{t^{-}})}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\right\rVert dN_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma}\sqrt{3\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha\right)(1+2\alpha)n}\sqrt{2\frac{f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha n}\sqrt{\frac{6\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha\right)(1+2\alpha)}{\gamma}}\sqrt{f(x_{0})-f^{\ast}}

So, combining (

C.6
Control of the Denominator in (

)

In (
26
), we use the following control.

Lemma C.3
.

Let
\alpha=n^{-1/7}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\geq 8
. Then, we have

\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}+\frac{(1+2\alpha)(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)}{2}(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

We first note that

\displaystyle\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}+\frac{(1+2\alpha)(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)}{2}(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+2\alpha)}{2}\left(2+\alpha+(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-4(1+\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\right).

Then, because

(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})\geq(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n}),

we have

\displaystyle(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-4(1+\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}

\displaystyle=(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\left((1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)-4(1+\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=-(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\left(3+\frac{5}{2}\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{11}{2}n^{\frac{6}{7}}.

In the last inequality, we use that as
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, we have
-(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})\geq-1
and
-\left(3+\frac{5}{2}\alpha\right)\geq-\frac{11}{2}
. We verify that
-\frac{11}{2}n^{\frac{6}{7}}\geq-1
, as long as
n\geq\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)^{\frac{7}{6}}\approx 7.3
. So, assuming
n\geq 8
, we deduce that

\displaystyle 2+\alpha+(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-4(1+\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\geq 2+\alpha-1\geq 1,

and as
(1+2\alpha)\geq 1
, we conclude

\frac{(1+2\alpha)}{2}\left(2+\alpha+(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}-4(1+\alpha)(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})n^{-\frac{6}{7}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

∎

Other Lemmas used to prove Theorem

In this section, we state and prove lemmas used to prove Theorem

, but not explicitly stated in Section

Lemma D.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
i\geq 1
. We have for
c>0

\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1+c\alpha)^{-(i-j)}=\frac{1+c\alpha-(1+c\alpha)^{-i+1}}{c\alpha},

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1+c\alpha)^{-(i-j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}(1+c\alpha)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-(1+c\alpha)^{-i}}{1-(1+c\alpha)^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+c\alpha-(1+c\alpha)^{-i+1}}{c\alpha}

∎

Lemma D.2
.

Let
\gamma\neq 1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
. We have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\gamma^{k}=\gamma\frac{1-(n+1)\gamma^{n}+n\gamma^{n+1}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}.

(65)

Proof.

Define
\varphi(\gamma):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma^{k}=\frac{\gamma-\gamma^{n+1}}{1-\gamma}
. We have

\varphi^{\prime}(y)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\gamma^{k-1}=\frac{(1-(n+1)\gamma^{n})(1-\gamma)+\gamma-\gamma^{n+1}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}=\frac{1-(n+1)\gamma^{n}+n\gamma^{n+1}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}.

Multiply by
\gamma
allows to get (
65
).

∎

Lemma D.3
.

Let
\gamma\neq 1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
. We have

\sum_{k=l_{0}}^{n}k\gamma^{k}=\frac{\gamma^{l_{0}}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}(l_{0}-(n+1)\gamma^{n-l_{0}+1})(1-\gamma)+\gamma-\gamma^{n-l_{0}+2})

Proof.

Define
\varphi(\gamma):=\sum_{k=l_{0}}^{n}\gamma^{k}=\frac{\gamma^{l_{0}}-\gamma^{n+1}}{1-\gamma}
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)=\sum_{k=l_{0}}^{n}k\gamma^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(l_{0}\gamma^{l_{0}-1}-(n+1)\gamma^{n})(1-\gamma)+\gamma^{l_{0}}-\gamma^{n+1}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{l_{0}-1}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}(l_{0}-(n+1)\gamma^{n-l_{0}+1})(1-\gamma)+\gamma-\gamma^{n-l_{0}+2}).

Multiplying both sides by
\gamma
gives the result.
∎

Lemma D.4
.

If
i\leq j
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\alpha(T_{j}-T_{i})}\right]=(1+\alpha)^{-j+i}.

Proof.

It is trivially true if
i=j
. If
i<j
, we have

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha(T_{j}-T_{i})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-1}e^{-\alpha(T_{k+i+1}-T_{k+i})}

By independence of the
T_{k+1}-T_{k}
for all
k
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-1}e^{-\alpha(T_{k+i+1}-T_{k+i})}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\alpha(T_{i+j+1}-T_{i+j})}\right].

Recall
T_{k+1}-T_{k}
for all
k
follows an exponential law with parameter
1
, such that we have
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\alpha(T_{i+j+1}-T_{i+j})}\right]=(1+\alpha)^{-1}
. So,

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\alpha(T_{i+j+1}-T_{i+j})}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{j-i-1}(1+\alpha)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha)^{-j+i}.

∎

Lemma D.5
.

For
n\geq 1
,
\alpha>0
, we have:

(i)

The following upper bound

(ii)

The following equality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-t)}dN_{s}\right)^{2}dN_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}n+\frac{(1+2\alpha)(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha)}{2\alpha}(1-(1+2\alpha)^{-n})

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{\alpha}(1-(1+\alpha)^{-n})

Proof.

Point (i)

We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-t)}dN_{s}\right)^{2}dN_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\alpha e^{\alpha(T_{j}-T_{i})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}+2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}.

(66)

We focus on the first term in (
66
). We have by using Lemma
D.4
and Lemma

(

)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-j)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1+2\alpha-(1+2\alpha)^{-i+1}}{2\alpha}

Omitting the non positive term, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}\right]\leq\alpha n\frac{1+2\alpha}{2}.

We focus on the second term of (

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}e^{-\alpha(T_{l}-T_{j})}

We take expectation and we use Lemma
D.4

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}\right]

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}e^{-\alpha(T_{l}-T_{j})}\right]

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-2\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\alpha(T_{l}-T_{j})}\right]

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-l)}(1+\alpha)^{-(l-j)}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}(1+2\alpha)^{-(l-j)}(1+\alpha)^{-(i-j)}.

Now, using twice Lemma

with

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{i}\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-l)}(1+\alpha)^{-(i-j)}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{i}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-l)}\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}(1+\alpha)^{-(l-j)}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{i}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-l)}\left(\frac{1+\alpha-(1+\alpha)^{-i+1}}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha(1+\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{i}(1+2\alpha)^{-(i-l)}

\displaystyle=2\alpha(1+\alpha)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1+2\alpha-(1+2\alpha)^{-i+1}}{2\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)n

We then deduce that

\displaystyle 2\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j<l\leq i}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{j})}e^{-\alpha(T_{i}-T_{l})}\right]\leq(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)n.

Finally, we have the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-t)}dN_{s}\right)^{2}dN_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\frac{1+2\alpha}{2}n+(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)n

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\alpha\right)(1+2\alpha)n.

Point (ii)

Let
Y_{k}=\left(\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs\right)^{2}
. We have

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{[0,T_{k+1}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k+1})}dNs\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs+\int_{]T_{k},T_{k+1}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k+1})}dNs\right)^{2}.

\int_{]T_{k},T_{k+1}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k+1})}dNs=\alpha
. So

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=e^{2\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}Y_{k}+2\alpha e^{\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs+\alpha^{2}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{k+1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}Y_{k}+2\alpha e^{\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs+\alpha^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}Y_{k}\right]+2\alpha\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs\right]+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{k}\right]+2\alpha\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\alpha(T_{k}-T_{k+1})}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{[0,T_{k}]}\alpha e^{\alpha(s-T_{k})}dNs\right]+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+2\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{k}\right]+2\alpha(1-(1+\alpha)^{-k})+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+2\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{k}\right]-2\alpha(1+\alpha)^{-k}+\alpha(2+\alpha).

Noting

u_{k}=a^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{b_{i}+c}{a^{i+1}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(b_{i}+c)a^{k-i-1}.

We compute (

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}ca^{k-i-1}

\displaystyle=c\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a^{j}

(68)

\displaystyle=c\frac{1-a^{k}}{1-a}

\displaystyle=\alpha(2+\alpha)\frac{1-(1+2\alpha)^{-k}}{1-(1+2\alpha)^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}-\frac{2+\alpha}{2}(1+2\alpha)^{-k+1}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle a^{k-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{b_{i}}{a^{i}}

\displaystyle=-2\alpha a^{k-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1+2\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)^{i}

\displaystyle=-2\alpha a^{k-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{1+2\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)^{k}\right)\left(1-\left(\frac{1+2\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=2(1+\alpha)\left(a^{k-1}-a^{-1}(1+\alpha)^{-k}\right)

\displaystyle=2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)((1+2\alpha)^{-k}-(1+\alpha)^{-k})

\displaystyle=2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1+2\alpha)^{-k}-2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1+\alpha)^{-k}.

Injecting (
68
) and (
69
) into (

), we get the following expression for

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}-\frac{2+\alpha}{2}(1+2\alpha)^{-k+1}+2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1+2\alpha)^{-k}

\displaystyle-2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1+\alpha)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}+\left(-\frac{(2+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)}{2}+2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)\right)(1+2\alpha)^{-k}

\displaystyle-2(1+\alpha)(1+2\alpha)(1+\alpha)^{-k}

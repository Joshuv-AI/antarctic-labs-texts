Local controllability of free boundary three-dimensional semilinear radial parabolic equations
Juan Límaco, Luis P. Yapu

Abstract
We prove that a free boundary semilinear heat equation with Stefan boundary condition and radially symmetric data is locally null controllable. The strategy involves reducing the problem to the corresponding one-dimensional formulation and adapting a Carleman inequality in that setting. The local null controllability of the free-boundary problem is then established via the Schauder fixed-point theorem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first controllability result for this problem with Stefan boundary condition in more than one spatial dimension.

1
Introduction

The free-boundary problem with the Stefan boundary condition is a classical problem about the evolution of the temperature of a system composed by phases of a fluid. More specifically, in the heat evolution model that we consider, an evolving phase composed of water is surrounded by ice at zero temperature. Since the ice temperature does not evolve, this is called a one-phase Stefan problem.
The equation models the evolution of the temperature
y(x,t)
at some position and time. The water phase is in the time-dependent domain
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with boundary
\partial\Omega(t)
that separates the water and the ice.

1.1
Main contributions

In this paper, we are interested in the case of a three-dimensional one-phase Stefan problem with radial symmetry.
Let
B_{R}
denote the Euclidean three-dimensional ball of radius
R>0
centered at the origin.
Consider the domain
Q_{R}=\{(x,t):x\in B_{R(t)},t\in(0,T)\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, with lateral boundary
\Sigma_{R}=\{(x,t):x\in\partial B_{R(t)},t\in(0,T)\}
, where
R(t)\in C^{1}([0,T])
describes the radius that changes with time and verifies, for some positive constants
R_{*}
and
E
,

0<R_{*}\leq R(t)\leq E.

(1)

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a globally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(0)=0
.
Fix the control region
\omega:=B_{b}
, where
0<b<R_{*}
and consider the controlled free-boundary problem

\begin{cases}y_{t}-\Delta y+f(y)=1_{\omega}u,&\text{in }Q_{R},\\
y(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\Sigma_{R},\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0},&\text{in }B_{R(0)},\end{cases}

(2)

where
u(x,t)
indicates the control and the Stefan condition describing the evolution of the boundary
\partial B_{R(t)}
is given by

R^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla y(x,t)\cdot\hat{n},\quad x\in\partial B_{R(t)},

(3)

where
\hat{n}
denotes the outward-pointing unit normal vector at
(x,t)
.

System (
2
) is said
locally null controllable
at time
T>0
if there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that, if
\|y_{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B_{R(0)})}\leq\varepsilon
, there is a solution
(y,u,R)
of (
2
), (
3
), such that

\displaystyle R\in C^{1}([0,T]),\qquad

\displaystyle 0<R_{*}\leq R(t)\leq E,

\displaystyle u\in L^{2}(\omega\times(0,T)),\qquad

\displaystyle y\in C^{0}([0,T],H_{0}^{1}(B_{R(t)}),

and
y(x,T)=0
, for
x\in B_{R(T)}
.

The main result is the following local null controllability theorem in the three-dimensional radially symmetric case:

Theorem 1
.

Assume that there are constants
0<b<R_{*}<R_{0}<E
.
Let
T>0
and
y_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{R_{0}})
radially symmetric.
Then, putting
R(0)=R_{0}
, the three-dimensional free-boundary problem (
2
)-(
3
) is locally null controllable.

The result is a consequence of the controllability of the associated linearized system and the use of Schauder’s fixed-point theorem to the nonlinear system.
This is the first result of controllability for the free-boundary problem (
2
) in more than one spatial dimension using the Stefan condition (
3
), rather than a modified condition such as the one considered in
Demarque_EFC-18
in the two-dimensional case and star-shaped domain.

Although the Stefan problem has been extensively studied
Aronson-83
;
Friedman-82
;
Meirmanov-92
, results concerning its controllability are recent. Most known controllability results pertain to the one-dimensional case.

1.2
Related work

The first local null controllability result for the heat equation with free boundary in one dimension was given by Fernández-Cara, Límaco and Menezes in
EFC_Lim_Men-16
. Soon after, this was generalized to include a semilinearity by Fernández-Cara and Sousa in
EFC_Ivaldo-17
. Both papers treat the intrinsic nonlinearity of the problem using a fixed-point strategy and are limited to the one-dimensional case because the fixed point map uses an explicit integration of the one-dimensional Stefan condition. Afterwards, Fernández-Cara, Hernández and Límaco in
EFC_Lim_Freddy-18
proved the same results using an inverse function theorem, which could in principle be suitable to treat the higher dimensional case, but no further results where published in that direction. The only related work we are aware of in two dimensions is by Demarque and Fernández-Cara
Demarque_EFC-18
, where the condition describing the evolution of the free boundary was modified introducing a diffusion term.

In
EFC_Lim_Freddy-18
the authors used a compactness-uniqueness method in order to treat de Stefan condition as a constraint. Geshkovski and Zuazua used the same method to treat the Burgers equation with free boundary in
Gesh_Zua-21
. Moreover, Araujo, Fernández-Cara, Límaco and Souza in
Arau_EFC_Sou-22
used also compactness-uniqueness arguments to prove the local null controllability of the two-phase Stefan problem and also to control the boundary curve between the two phases.

More recently, Barcena-Petisco, Fernández-Cara and Souza
Bar_EFC_Sou-23
proved the controllability to trajectories of the one-phase Stefan problem. Also Costa,
Límaco, Lopes and Prouvée
Vitor_JAL-23
included in the model nonlocal nonlinearities. This required a more careful study and Hölder regularity of the initial state. Moreover, Wang, Lan, Lei in
WLL-22
studied the quasilinear case, which also needs Hölder regularity of the initial state. On the other hand, Wang, Lei and Wu
WLW-22
studied the existence of insensitizing controls for the semilinear Heat equation.

In a different geometry, Geshkovski and Maity in
Gesh_Maity-23
studied the Stefan problem in a periodic box. Their methods are different, using the periodicity to find a Fourier decomposition in one dimension and the study of the resultant sequence of one-dimensional systems and proving an observability result which is uniform for the sequence.

1.3
Overview

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present the reduction of the radial problem to the one-dimensional case and show the null controllability of the linearized system using an adapted Carleman inequality. In Section
3
we prove
Theorem
1
using Schauder’s fixed point theorem. Finally, in section
4
we observe that boundary controllability holds directly when both the domain and the control regions are annular (excluding the origin). We also present several problems that are already known to be locally null controllable in the one-dimensional case.

2
Controllability of the associated one-dimensional linearized system

In this section, using the radial symmetry we reduce the equation to one dimension and after a change of variables, we prove the controllability of the system without the Stefan condition, supposing that the boundary follows a path determined by a function
R(t)\in C^{1}([0,T])
such that (
1
) holds.

Using the radial symmetry, the Laplacian term can be written in polar coordinates. We get an equation applied to the radial function
z(r,t)=y(x,t)
, with
r=|x|
. In this case, the 3D Laplacian is given by
\Delta z=\frac{1}{r^{2}}(r^{2}z_{r})_{r}=z_{rr}+\frac{2}{r}z_{r}
, with radial control
v(r,t)=u(x,t)
, and the system (
2
) becomes

\begin{cases}z_{t}-z_{rr}-\frac{2}{r}z_{r}+f(z)=1_{\omega}v,&\text{in }\hat{Q}_{R},\\
z_{r}(0,t)=0,\quad z(R(t),t)=0,&\text{in }(0,T),\\
z(\cdot,0)=z_{0},&\text{in }(0,R(0)),\end{cases}

(4)

where the domain
\hat{Q}_{R}=\{(r,t):r\in[0,R(t)],\ t\in(0,T)\}
is one-dimensional in space. Let us observe that
y(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(B_{R(t)})
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
is equivalent to
z(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}([0,R(t)],r^{2}dr)
, where in the first case the
L^{2}
space is in three dimensions and in the second case it is a weighted
L^{2}
space in one dimension, i.e., for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, we have

\int_{0}^{R(t)}|z(r,t)|^{2}r^{2}dr<\infty
. On the other hand,
the Stefan condition (
3
) becomes

R^{\prime}(t)=-z_{r}(R(t),t),\qquad t\in(0,T).

(5)

The linearized system about zero (stationary solution of (
4
)) is given by

\begin{cases}z_{t}-z_{rr}-\frac{2}{r}z_{r}+a(r,t)z=1_{\omega}v(r,t),&\text{in}\quad\hat{Q}_{R},\\
z_{r}(0,t)=0,\qquad z(R(t),t)=0,&\text{in}\quad(0,T),\\
z(\cdot,0)=z_{0},&\text{in}\quad(0,R(0)),\end{cases}

(6)

where
a(r,t)
is a bounded function.

In order to remove the singular term at
r=0
, we define the functions
\tilde{z}(r,t)=r\,z(r,t)
and
\tilde{v}=r\,v(r,t)
. Then, we have that
\tilde{z}(0,t)=0
and (
6
) becomes

\begin{cases}\tilde{z}_{t}-\tilde{z}_{rr}+a(r,t)\tilde{z}=1_{\omega}\tilde{v}(r,t),&\text{in}\quad\hat{Q}_{R},\\
\tilde{z}(0,t)=0,\quad\tilde{z}(R(t),t)=0,&\text{in}\quad(0,T),\\
\tilde{z}(\cdot,0)=\tilde{z}_{0},&\text{in}\quad(0,R(0)).\end{cases}

(7)

Note that after the change of variable,
z(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}([0,R(t)],r^{2}dr)
is equivalent to
\tilde{z}(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}([0,R(t)],dr)
.

The Stefan condition (
5
) is transformed into

R^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\tilde{z}_{r}(R(t),t)}{R(t)}-\frac{\tilde{z}(R(t),t)}{R(t)^{2}}=\frac{\tilde{z}_{r}(R(t),t)}{R(t)},

(8)

where in the last equality we used the boundary condition. We observe, from the condition
0<R_{*}<R(t)<E
, that the denominator is not zero.

Remark 1

(On the
N
-dimensional case)
.

The expression of the Laplacian used above is valid in tree spatial dimensions. If the system is

The adjoint system of (

\begin{cases}-\phi_{t}-\phi_{rr}+a(r,t)\phi=F(r,t),&\text{in}\quad\hat{Q}_{R},\\
\phi(0,t)=0,\quad\phi(R(t),t)=0,&\text{on}\quad(0,T),\\
\phi(\cdot,T)=\phi_{T}.&\text{in}\quad(0,R(T)),\end{cases}

and

The adjoint system (

For any

2.1
Carleman estimate

Let us set the notation
\hat{Q}_{RR}=\{(r,t):r\in[-R(t),R(t)],\ t\in(0,T)\}

EFC_Lim_Men-16

Lemma 4

For some

\eta_{0}\in C^{1}(\hat{Q}_{RR})

\begin{cases}\alpha_{0}(r,t)=0,\quad&(r,t)\in\{R(t),-R(t)\}\times(0,T)\\
|\alpha_{0,r}|>0,\quad&(r,t)\in((-R(t),-b_{0})\cup(b_{0},R(t)))\times(0,T)\\
\alpha_{0}(r,t)=1-\frac{r-b}{R(t)-b},\quad&(r,t)\in((-R(t),-b)\cup(b,R(t)))\times(0,T).\end{cases}

For the proof we can take, for instance, the even extension on
[-R(t),R(t)]
of the following function, which is an adaptation from
EFC_Lim_Men-16

\alpha_{0}(r,t)=\begin{cases}1+p\left(\frac{2(b-r)}{2b},\frac{2b}{2(R(t)-b)}\right),&\text{if}\quad 0\leq r<b,\\
\frac{R(t)-r}{R(t)-b},&\text{if}\quad b\leq r\leq R(t),\end{cases}

where

. We verify easily that

\alpha_{0,r}(0,t)=-\frac{1}{b}p_{w}(1,\frac{2b}{2(R(t)-b)})=0

For some parameter

, we define

\alpha_{1}(r,t)=\alpha_{0}(r,t)+1
,

\sigma(r,t)=e^{2\lambda\|\alpha_{1}\|_{\infty}}-e^{\lambda\alpha_{1}(r,t)},

We use the notation

\begin{split}I(\phi):=&\iint_{\hat{Q}_{R}}e^{-2s\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{s\xi}(|\phi_{t}|^{2}+|\phi_{rr}|^{2})+\lambda^{2}s\xi|\phi_{r}|^{2}+\lambda^{4}s^{3}\xi^{3}|\phi|^{2}drdt\right)\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2s\alpha(R(t),t)}\lambda s\xi(R(t),t)|\phi_{r}(R(t),t)|^{2}dt.\end{split}

(11)

We obtained the following Carleman inequality:

Proposition 5

There exist positive constants

,

(12)

The proof of this result can be found in Appendix

Local controllability of the nonlinear system

The following compact embeddings from

Hölder regularity of

Given an integer

\langle u\rangle_{r,G}^{(\alpha)}=\sup_{(r,t),(r^{\prime},t)\in\bar{G}}\frac{|u(r,t)-u(r^{\prime},t)|}{|r-r^{\prime}|^{\alpha}},\qquad\langle u\rangle_{r,G}^{(m+\alpha)}=\sum_{2k+l=m}\langle D_{t}^{k}D_{r}^{l}u\rangle_{r,G}^{(\alpha)},

\tilde{z}_{\epsilon}\in K^{0,\kappa}(\hat{Q}_{0})
and

|\tilde{z}_{\epsilon}|_{\hat{Q}_{0}}^{0,\kappa}\leq C,

(20)

for some
C>0
depending only on
R_{*}
,
E
,
T
,
\kappa
and
\|\tilde{z}_{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}([0,R_{0}])}
.
Moreover, from (
13
),
there exists a constant
\tilde{C}>0
, only depending on the previous data, such that

\|R_{\epsilon}\|_{C^{1,\kappa}}\leq\tilde{C}.

(21)

The continuity of the map
(z,R)\mapsto\Lambda_{\epsilon}(z,R)
follows also as in
EFC_Ivaldo-17
.

Applying Schauder’s fixed point theorem to the map

\Lambda_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Z}
,
we get a fixed point
(\tilde{z}_{\epsilon},R_{\epsilon})
, for any
\epsilon>0
. The triple
(\tilde{z}_{\epsilon},\tilde{v}_{\epsilon},R_{\epsilon})
is a solution of (
14
) and (
15
), for any
\epsilon>0
, and by (
20
), (
10
) and (
21
), each component of the triple is uniformly bounded. Thus, as
\epsilon\to 0
we can extract subsequences such that

\begin{cases}R_{\epsilon}\to R\quad&\text{strongly in}\quad C^{1}([0,T]),\\
\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup\tilde{v}\quad&\text{weakly in}\quad L^{2}([0,b)\times(0,T)),\end{cases}

where the strong converge is obtained by the compactness of the inclusion
C^{1+\kappa}\hookrightarrow C^{1}
.
We deduce from (
15
), as
\epsilon\to 0
, that
\tilde{z}
, the state associated to the control
\tilde{v}
and the path
R(t)
, verifies that
\tilde{z}(r,T)=0
, for
r\in[0,R(T)]
, and we conclude that the system (
7
) is locally null controllable. In the original variables this implies that the system (
2
)-(
3
) is locally null controllable.
∎

4
Additional remarks and open questions

4.1
Boundary controllability in two-dimensions

In this section, we consider the case where the system is defined in an annular domain in two dimensions. That is, given a function
R(t)\in C^{1}([0,T])
such that
0<A<R_{*}\leq R(t)\leq E
,
t\in(0,T)
, consider the annular domain
Q_{A,R}=\{(x,t):x\in B_{R(t)}-B_{A},\ t\in(0,T)\}
with exterior boundary

\Sigma_{R}=\{(x,t):x\in\partial B_{R(t)},\ t\in(0,T)\}
.

Consider the following problem in
Q_{A,R}
where the interior boundary
\partial B_{A}
is fixed, acted on by a boundary control
h
, and the exterior boundary
\Sigma_{R}
follows a free-boundary condition as was considered in the previous sections. As above, let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a globally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(0)=0
and consider the system

\begin{cases}y_{t}-\Delta y+f(y)=0,&\text{in }Q_{A,R},\\
y(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\Sigma_{R},\\
y(x,t)=h(t),&\text{on }\partial B_{\bar{A}}\times(0,T),\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0},&\text{in }B_{R(0)}-B_{\bar{A}},\end{cases}

(22)

with Stefan condition

R^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla y(x,t)\cdot\hat{n},\quad x\in\partial B_{R(t)}.

(23)

Using the radial symmetry, we get an equation applied to the radial function
z(r,t)=y(x,t)
, where
r=|x|
. In this case the 2D Laplacian is given by
\Delta z=z_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}z_{r}
. The system (
22
) becomes

\begin{cases}z_{t}-z_{rr}-\frac{1}{r}z_{r}+f(z)=0&\text{in }\hat{Q}_{A,R},\\
z(A,t)=h(t),\quad z(R(t),t)=0&\text{on }(0,T),\\
z(\cdot,0)=z_{0}&\text{in }(A,R(0)),\end{cases}

(24)

where
\hat{Q}_{A,R}=\{(r,t):r\in[A,R(t)],\ t\in(0,T)\}
. Moreover
y(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(B_{R(t)}-B_{A})
is equivalent to
z(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}([A,R(t)],r\ dr)
, as was observed in Section
2
for
N=3
.

Since the interior boundary
\partial B_{A}
is fixed and
A>0
, the factor
\frac{1}{r}
in (
24
) is bounded and we do not need to perform the second change of variables used in Section
2
.

We use a standard method to pass from boundary control to interior control: we work in an extended interval
[A-\delta,R(t)]
and add an interior control
\bar{v}
acting in an open subset
\bar{\omega}\subset(A-\delta,A)
. Then, the boundary control is given by
h(t)=\bar{u}(A,t)
where
\bar{u}
denotes the solution given by the controllability result of
EFC_Ivaldo-17
with interior control
\bar{v}
in the extended interval. As a consequence, we get the boundary controllability:

Proposition 6
.

Let
f
be a globally Lipschitz continuous function such that
f(0)=0
, and
T>0
,
A,E>0
. Assume that
0<A<R_{*}<R_{0}<E
. Then, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, if
\|y_{0}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(B_{R_{0}})}\leq\varepsilon
, there is a solution
(y,R,h)
of (
22
)-(
23
), with
R(0)=R_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle R\in C^{1}([0,T]),\quad R_{*}\leq R(t)\leq E,\quad h\in L^{2}([0,T]),\quad y\in C^{0}([0,T],H_{0}^{1}(B_{R(t)}),

and
y(x,T)=0
, for
x\in B_{R(T)}.

We note that the
N
-dimensional generalization of this result holds, but in applications it is difficult to imagine a scenario where one acts on the interior boundary of an
N
-dimensional annular region for
N>2
.

4.2
Related questions

Next, we present some related open questions. We consider the same notation used throughout the paper.

•

Consider the problem of the null controllability of the free-boundary radial problem with general diffusion coefficient
C(|x|,t)
given by

\begin{cases}y_{t}-div_{x}(C(|x|,t)\ \nabla_{x}y)+f(y)=1_{\omega}u,&\text{in }Q_{R},\\
y(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\Sigma_{R},\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0},&\text{in }B_{R(0)},\end{cases}

(25)

with Stefan condition describing the evolution of the boundary,

R^{\prime}(t)=-C(|x|,t)\ \nabla y(x,t)\cdot\hat{n},\quad x\in\partial B_{R(t)},

where
\hat{n}
denotes the outward unit normal vector at the point
(x,t)
. We observe that we cannot use the same change of variable that we used in this paper, since depending on the form the function
C
, there is no certainty that we can cancel the singular term at the origin as in (
7
).

•

Consider the function
\beta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
with bounded derivatives and such that

0<\beta_{0}<\beta(r)<\beta_{1}<+\infty.

Let us consider the problem of null controllability of the free-boundary radial problem with non-local term given by

\begin{cases}y_{t}-\beta\left(\int_{0}^{R(t)}ydx\right)\Delta y+f(y)=1_{\omega}u,&\text{in }Q_{R},\\
y(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\Sigma_{R},\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0},&\text{in }B_{R(0)},\end{cases}

(26)

with Stefan condition

R^{\prime}(t)=-\beta\left(\int_{0}^{R(t)}ydx\right)\nabla y(x,t)\cdot\hat{n},\quad x\in\partial B_{R(t)}.

The one-dimensional case has been studied in
Vitor_JAL-23
where higher regularity of the initial state was needed, i.e.
y_{0}\in C^{2+\frac{1}{2}}([0,R_{0}])
, and also Hölder regularity of the approximate controls.

•

Let us assume that the real function
a=a(r)
is of class
C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and possesses bounded derivatives up to order two and such that

0<C_{0}<a(r)<C_{1}.

Consider the null controllability of the free-boundary quasi-linear radial problem given by

\begin{cases}y_{t}-div_{x}(a(y)\nabla_{x}y)+f(y)=1_{\omega}u,&\text{in }Q_{R},\\
y(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\Sigma_{R},\\
y(\cdot,0)=y_{0},&\text{in }B_{R(0)},\end{cases}

(27)

with Stefan condition

R^{\prime}(t)=-a(y)\nabla y(x,t)\cdot\hat{n},\quad x\in\partial B_{R(t)}.

The one-dimensional case was studied in
WLL-22
based on the previous work
Liu_Zhang-12
, where Hölder regularity was needed for the initial state and the controls obtained where also Hölder. In fact,
y_{0}\in C^{2+\kappa}([0,R_{0}])
and
u\in K^{\kappa,\frac{\kappa}{2}}
for any
\kappa\in(0,1)
.

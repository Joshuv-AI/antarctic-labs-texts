1.
Introduction and main results

Let
A
be a finite set of integers. It has been shown in many aspects that the size of
A+A=\{a_{1}+a_{2}:a_{1},a_{2}\in A\}
conveys much structural information of
A
. For instance, Freiman’s famous theorem
[
1
]
states that if
|A+A|
is comparable to
|A|
, then
A
must be contained in some generalized arithmetical progression of proper dimension and size. Conversely, without such structure,
|A+A|
is supposed to be considerably larger than
|A|
. An interesting example is the set of primes.

The binary Goldbach conjecture asserts that every sufficiently large even integer is a sum of two odd primes. Though this full conjecture is still out of reach by current methods, it is proved in
[
2
]
that for sufficiently large
x
, there are at most
O(x^{0.879})
exceptions among even integers in
[2,2x]
. Let
A
be the set of primes in
[1,x]
. This means

|A+A|\gtrsim(\log x)|A|.

It is believed that similar results will hold for subsets of the primes, provided the relative density is not too small. To quantify this, we suppose
A\subset[1,x]
is a set of primes with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. Note first that by prime number theorem we have
\alpha\leq 1+o_{x}(1)
.

In
[
9
]
, Ramaré and Ruzsa considered relative dense subsets of the primes. They showed that for any fixed
\alpha>0
, as
x\gtrsim_{\alpha}1
, we have

(1)

|A+A|\gtrsim\frac{\log x}{\log\log 3\alpha^{-1}}|A|,

where the implicit constant was later made explicit and sharp in the work of Matomäki
[
3
]
.
When
\alpha
is not fixed, in particular when
\alpha=o_{x}(1)
which corresponds to the case
A
is relative sparse, Cui-Li-Xue
[
4
]
proved that for any
c_{0}>0
, if
\alpha\geq(\log x)^{-c_{0}}
then as
x\gtrsim 1
we have

(2)

|A+A|\gtrsim_{c_{0}}\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}|A|.

Due to the presence of Siegel-Walfisz theorem in their method, the implicit constant here is ineffective.

Observe that there is an obvious gap between these two results, that is, (
2
) does not recover (
1
) if we assume
A
is relative dense. To remedy this, we couple ideas from
[
9
]
and
[
4
]
to derive the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a set of primes with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for
\alpha\geq c(\log x)^{-1/2}\log\log x
, we have

|A+A|\gtrsim\frac{\log x}{\log\log 3\alpha^{-1}}|A|.

Since our method does not invoke Siegel-Walfisz theorem, the above result is effective. In fact, this bound is also sharp in the sense that if we take
A=\{p\leq x:p\equiv a(\operatorname{mod}q)\}
with
q=\prod_{p<z}p
,
z\leq\log\log x
and
(a,q)=1
, then
\alpha\asymp\phi(q)^{-1}
,
z\asymp\log q
and thus

|A+A|\lesssim\frac{\phi(q)}{q}(\log x)|A|\asymp\frac{\log x}{\log z}|A|\asymp\frac{\log x}{\log\log q}|A|\asymp\frac{\log x}{\log\log 3\alpha^{-1}}|A|.

For sparser
A
, we have instead the following bound, which is effective, slightly weaker than (
1
) but stronger than (
2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of primes with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

|A+A|\gtrsim\frac{\log x}{\log 2\alpha^{-1}}|A|.

Remark 1.3
.

The above theorem is trivial if
\alpha\leq x^{-\delta}
for some constant
\delta>0
. For these extremely sparse sets, we can deduce from
[
10
]
that there is some
c\in(0,1/4)
such that

|A+A|\geq e^{\left(\log\log|A|\right)^{c}}|A|,

provided
|A|\geq e^{(\log\log x)^{2}}
and
x\gtrsim 1
. This bound, however, is too weak to recover (
2
).

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will follow
[
4
]
to replace the primes by Granville’s model for primes to transfer our problem to a local one, and then follow
[
9
]
to solve the local problem. For a detailed discussion of this probabilistic model for primes, see
[
6
]
. The main advantage of our method takes place in the first step, where we use Brun’s sieve coupled with several analytic results instead of Selberg’s sieve in
[
9
]
. We also rely on the restriction theorem for primes, in particular the sharp one proved by Ramaré
[
8
]
, based on which we can derive a restriction theorem for sparse subsets of the primes. And then Theorem
1.2
is a simple consequence.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we fix some notations. In Section 3 we introduce Granville’s model for primes. In Section 4 we introduce the sharp restriction theorem and prove Theorem
1.2
. In section 5 we prove Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Granville’s model

Let
x
be a sufficiently large real number. Let
w\in[e^{2},\frac{1}{2}\log x]
and
W=\prod_{p<w}p
. These parameters will be in force throughout the rest of this paper. For
n\leq x
, Granville’s model for
1_{n\in\mathbb{P}}\log n
is defined by
1_{(n,W)=1}\frac{W}{\phi(W)}
, where
\phi
is Euler’s totient function. We next define their Fourier transforms by

(3)

S(\theta)=\sum_{p\leq x}(\log p)e(p\theta),

and

(4)

G(\theta)=\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}e(n\theta),

where we used the abbreviation
e\left(\theta\right)=e^{2\pi i\theta}
.
Our main task of this section is to verify the idea
that
G(\theta)
is a good approximation to
S(\theta)
, provided
w
is large enough.

Proposition 3.1
.

As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}|S(\theta)-G(\theta)|\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x.

The proof of the above proposition is based on circle method with respect to the parameter
Q=e^{\frac{1}{3}(\log x)^{1/3}}
. We now
identify
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
with
(Qx^{-1},1+Qx^{-1}]
and define the major arcs by

\mathfrak{M}=\bigcup_{1\leq q\leq Q}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\mathfrak{M}(a,q)=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}:|\theta-a/q|\leq Qx^{-1}q^{-1}\}.

Then minor arcs are simply defined by
\mathfrak{m}=(Qx^{-1},1+Qx^{-1}]\setminus\mathfrak{M}
. For convenience, we set

r(n)=1_{n\in\mathbb{P}}\log n-1_{(n,W)=1}\frac{W}{\phi(W)}

and
R(\theta)=S(\theta)-G(\theta)
.

The estimate on minor arcs is quite standard. Let
\theta\in\mathfrak{m}
.
By Dirichlet approximation theorem there exists a co-prime pair
a,q
with
1\leq q\leq xQ^{-1}
such that
|\theta-a/q|\leq Qx^{-1}q^{-1}
. Notice that when
q\leq Q
and
x>2Q^{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Q}{xq}\leq\frac{1}{Q}-\frac{x}{Q}<\frac{x}{Q},

which means
1\leq a\leq q
and then contradicts to the fact
\theta\in\mathfrak{m}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we see that
x>2Q^{2}
holds and thus
q\in(Q,xQ^{-1}]
. Combing
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
,
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
and the fact

\frac{W}{\phi(W)}=\prod_{p<w}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\lesssim\log w,

this leads to

|R(\theta)|\lesssim xQ^{-1/2}(\log x)^{O(1)}\lesssim xe^{-\frac{1}{12}(\log x)^{1/3}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}}|R(\theta)|\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x.

To proceed on major arcs, we need to estimate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}r(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}\log p-\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}

for
1\leq l\leq q\leq Q
and
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
. For the prime part, we use two different types of the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions to show the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq l\leq q\leq Q
and
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}\log p=\frac{1_{(l,q)=1}}{\phi(q)}\left(y-1_{q\in[w,Q]}\chi_{1}(l)\frac{y^{\beta_{1}}}{\beta_{1}}\right)+O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}),

where
\beta_{1}\in[1/2,1]
is the Landau-Siegel zero with respect to the Dirichlet character
\chi_{1}(\operatorname{mod}q)
.

Proof.

When
q\in[w,Q]
, using
[
5
, Theorem 8.29]
we have for
y\geq 2
and some absolute
c>0
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}\log p=\frac{1_{(l,q)=1}}{\phi(q)}\left(y-\chi_{1}(l)\frac{y^{\beta_{1}}}{\beta_{1}}\right)+O(ye^{-c(\log y)^{1/2}}).

As
x\gtrsim 1
, the error term is
O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}})
for
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
. When
q<w
and thus
q\leq\log x
, we instead use
[
5
, Theorem 8.31]
to obtain for
y\geq 3
and some absolute
c^{\prime}>0
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}\log p=\frac{1_{(l,q)=1}}{\phi(q)}y+O(ye^{-c^{\prime}(\log y)^{1/2}(\log\log y)^{-2}}).

As
x\gtrsim 1
, this error term is also
O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}})
for
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
.
∎

As for the part of Granville’s model, we appeal to the simplest form of Brun’s sieve that for
r\geq 1
,

(5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(n,W)\\
\omega(d)\leq 2r-1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\leq 1_{(n,W)=1}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(n,W)\\
\omega(d)\leq 2r\end{subarray}}\mu(d),

where
\omega(d)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
d
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq l\leq q\leq Q
and
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
. Write
q=q_{1}q_{2}
with
q_{1}=(q,W)
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}=\frac{1_{(l,q_{1})=1}}{\phi(q_{1})q_{2}}y+O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}).

Proof.

We first give an estimate on

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}.

Notice that by (
5
), we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid W\\
\omega(d)\leq 2r-1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}md\leq x\\
md\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid W\\
\omega(d)\leq 2r\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}md\leq x\\
md\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1.

It is clear that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}md\leq y\\
md\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1=\rho(d)\frac{y}{q}+O(1),

where
\rho(d)=(q,d)/d
if
(q,d)\mid l
and otherwise
\rho(d)=0
. We thus have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}=\frac{y}{q}\sum_{d\mid W}\mu(d)\rho(d)+O\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid W\\
\omega(d)\geq 2r\end{subarray}}\rho(d)\frac{y}{q}\right)+O(w^{2r}).

Let
2r
be the nearest even number to
\sqrt{\log y}
. It then follows that

\sum_{d\mid W}\mu(d)\rho(d)=\prod_{p<w}(1-\rho(p))=1_{(l,q,W)=1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<w\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right),

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid W\\
\omega(d)\geq 2r\end{subarray}}\rho(d)\frac{y}{q}\leq y\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid W\\
\omega(d)\geq 2r\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}&\leq y\sum_{k\geq 2r}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\sum_{p<w}\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\\
&\lesssim y\sum_{k\geq 2r}\left(C\frac{\log\log w}{k}\right)^{k}\\
&\lesssim y\sum_{k\geq 2r}e^{-k}\\
&\lesssim ye^{-\sqrt{\log y}}.\end{split}

and

w^{2r}=y^{2r\frac{\log w}{\log y}}\leq y^{O\left(\frac{\log w}{\sqrt{\log y}}\right)}\leq\sqrt{y},

as
x\gtrsim 1
.
As a result, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}=1_{(l,q,W)=1}\frac{y}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<w\\
p\nmid q\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O(ye^{-\sqrt{\log y}}).

Multiplying both sides by
W/\phi(W)
and using
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
we arrive at

\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}1_{(n,W)=1}=1_{(l,q,W)=1}\frac{y}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid(q,W)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}+O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}),

which is essentially the desired result.
∎

Now we can give an estimate for
R(\theta)
on major arcs.

Lemma 3.5
.

As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have

\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{M}}|R(\theta)|\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}

Proof.

We now fix a major arc
\mathfrak{M}(a,q)
with
1\leq a\leq q\leq Q
and
(a,q)=1
. Let
z=\theta-a/q
. Observe that

R(\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e\left(la/q\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}r(n)e(zn).

When
q<w
and thus
q\mid W
, as
x\gtrsim 1
we can apply Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
to deduce for
1\leq l\leq q
and
y\in[\sqrt{x},x]
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}r(n)=O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}),

Integrating by parts this gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}r(n)e(zn)\lesssim(1+x|z|)xe^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}+\sqrt{x}\log x.

Hence by
|z|\leq Qx^{-1}
and
w\leq\frac{1}{2}\log x
,

|R(\theta)|\lesssim Q^{2}xe^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}=xe^{-\frac{1}{3}(\log x)^{1/3}}\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x

Otherwise, when
q\in[w,Q]
, we have instead

(6)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq y\\
n\equiv l(\operatorname{mod}q)\end{subarray}}r(n)=&\frac{1_{(l,q)=1}}{\phi(q)}\left(y-\chi_{1}(l)\frac{y^{\beta_{1}}}{\beta_{1}}\right)-\frac{1_{(l,q_{1})=1}}{\phi(q_{1})q_{2}}y\\
&+O(ye^{-(\log x)^{1/3}}),\end{split}

where
\chi_{1},\beta_{1}
are given by Lemma
3.3
and
q_{1},q_{2}
are given by Lemma
3.4
.
As before, integrating by parts, this soon gives
R(\theta)=R_{1}(\theta)-R_{2}(\theta)-R_{3}(\theta)+O(xe^{-\frac{1}{3}(\log x)^{1/3}})
, with

R_{1}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e(la/q)\right)\int_{0}^{x}e(zu)du,

R_{2}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{q}\chi_{1}(l)e(la/q)\right)\int_{0}^{x}u^{\beta_{1}-1}e(zu)du,

and

R_{3}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\phi(q_{1})q_{2}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
(l,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e(la/q)\right)\int_{0}^{x}e(zu)du.

It is well-known that the Ramanujan sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
(l,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e(la/q)=\mu(q)

and the Gauss sum

\left|\sum_{l=1}^{q}\chi_{1}(l)e(la/q)\right|\leq\sqrt{q}.

Writing
l=l_{2}q_{2}+l_{1}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
(l,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}^{q}e(la/q)=\sum_{l_{2}=0}^{q_{2}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}=1\\
(l_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}^{q-1}e(l_{2}a/q_{1}+l_{1}a/q)=\sum_{l_{2}=0}^{q_{2}-1}e(l_{2}a/q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}=1\\
(l_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}^{q-1}e(l_{1}a/q_{1})=1_{q_{2}=1}\mu(q_{1}).

We thus obtain

|R(\theta)|\lesssim\left(\frac{\sqrt{q}}{\phi(q)}+e^{-\frac{1}{3}(\log x)^{1/3}}\right)x\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x.

Since
\mathfrak{M}(a,q)
is chosen arbitrarily, we conclude the proof.

∎

Proof of Proposition 3.1.

Apply Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.2
. The theorem follows readily since
(Qx^{-1},1+Qx^{-1}]=\mathfrak{M}\cup\mathfrak{m}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Assuming that there is no Landau-Siegel zero, the above argument can give a stronger bound

|R(\theta)|\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{w}x,

since there is no Gauss sum.
This bound will further imply Theorem

4.
Sharp Restriction theorem

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of primes with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. From Bourgain’s result
[
7
, Equation (4.39)]
, one can deduce that for any
q>2
,

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{q}d\theta\lesssim_{q}|A|^{q-1}\frac{\alpha^{1-q/2}}{\log x}.

Observe that, compared to Plancherel identity where the bound
|A|^{q-1}
is available, we gain an extra factor
\alpha^{\frac{2-q}{2}}(\log x)^{-1}
. However, this factor makes sense only when
\alpha\geq(\log x)^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}
. To treat sparser subsets of the primes, we invoke Ramaré’s work
[
8
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of primes with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we have for
q>2
that

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{q}d\theta\lesssim_{q}|A|^{q-1}\frac{\log 2\alpha^{-1}}{\log x}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can restrict
q\in(2,3]
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we can deduce from
[
8
, Corollary 1.3]
that

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{q}d\theta\lesssim|A|^{q-1}\frac{\alpha^{1-q/2}}{(q-2)\log x}.

Let
q^{\prime}-2=(q-2)(10\log 2\alpha^{-1})^{-1}<q-2
and apply the above to
q^{\prime}
. We have

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{q}d\theta\leq|A|^{q-q^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{q^{\prime}}d\theta\lesssim_{q}|A|^{q-1}\frac{\log 2\alpha^{-1}}{\log x}.

∎

Inserting the weight
\log p
into
[
8
, Corollary 1.3]
, we also obtain a weighted version of the above.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of primes with
\sum_{p\in A}\log p=\alpha x
. Then as
x\gtrsim 1
, we have for
q>2
that

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}(\log p)e(p\theta)|^{q}d\theta\lesssim_{q}(\alpha x)^{q-1}\log 2\alpha^{-1}.

Since the argument of
[
8
]
is sieve-theoretic, Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
are both effective. To see the sharpness we can simply take
A=\{2\}
. Finally, to conclude this section, we give the short proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem 1.2.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of integers with
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\begin{split}|A|^{4}=\left(\sum_{n\in A+A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=p_{1}+p_{2}\\
p_{1},p_{2}\in A\end{subarray}}1\right)^{2}&\leq|A+A|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+p_{2}=p_{3}+p_{4}\\
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\in A\end{subarray}}1\\
&=|A+A|\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{4}d\theta\end{split}

Meanwhile, as
x\gtrsim 1
, by Proposition
4.1
we have

\int_{0}^{1}|\sum_{p\in A}e(p\theta)|^{4}d\theta\lesssim|A|^{3}\frac{\log 2\alpha^{-1}}{\log x}.

∎

5.
Proof of Theorem 1

Before proving Theorem
1.1
, we show a local result which provides the crucial saving in our argument.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
L_{1},L_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{W}
with
|L_{1}|,|L_{2}|\leq\beta W
and
\beta\in(0,1]
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i\in L_{1},j\in L_{2}\end{subarray}}1\lesssim|L_{1}||L_{2}|\cdot\frac{\log\log 3\beta^{-1}}{\log w}

Proof.

The case
\log 3\beta^{-1}\geq\ w
is trivial so we assume
\log 3\beta^{-1}<w
. Using
[
9
, Theorem 3]
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i\in L_{1},j\in L_{2}\end{subarray}}1\lesssim|L_{1}||L_{2}|\exp\left(-\sum_{\log 3\beta^{-1}\leq p<w}\frac{1}{p}+3(\log 3\beta^{-1})\sum_{\log 3\beta^{-1}\leq p<w}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).

Since

\exp\left(-\sum_{\log 3\beta^{-1}\leq p<w}\frac{1}{p}\right)\lesssim\frac{\log\log 3\beta^{-1}}{\log w}

and

\exp\left((3\log 3\beta^{-1})\sum_{\log 3\beta^{-1}\leq p<w}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\lesssim 1,

we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Let
A\subset[1,x]
be a non-empty set of primes and define

S_{A}(\theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in A\end{subarray}}(\log p)e(p\theta).

Suppose first that
|A|=\alpha x(\log x)^{-1}
with
\alpha\geq(\log x)^{-1}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we see

S_{A}(0)\geq\sum_{p\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}x}\log p\gtrsim\alpha x.

Note that by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

(7)

\begin{split}S_{A}(0)^{4}=\left(\sum_{n\in A+A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=p_{1}+p_{2}\\
p_{1},p_{2}\in A\end{subarray}}\log p\right)^{2}&\leq|A+A|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+p_{2}=p_{3}+p_{4}\\
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\in A\end{subarray}}(\log p_{1})(\log p_{2})(\log p_{3})(\log p_{4})\\
&=|A+A|\int_{0}^{1}|S_{A}(\theta)|^{4}d\theta.\end{split}

We now use Granville’s model to estimate the energy integral

E=\int_{0}^{1}|S_{A}(\theta)|^{4}d\theta.

Let
S(\theta)
be as in (
3
) and
G(\theta)
be as in (
4
). Notice that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}+p_{2}=p_{3}+p_{4}\\
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\in A\end{subarray}}(\log p_{1})(\log p_{2})(\log p_{3})(\log p_{4})\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+p_{1}=p_{2}+p_{3}\\
p\leq x\\
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in A\\
\end{subarray}}(\log p)(\log p_{2})(\log p_{3})(\log p_{4}).

We have

E\leq\int_{0}^{1}S(\theta)S_{A}(-\theta)|S_{A}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta.

By Proposition
3.1
, this gives

E\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x\int_{0}^{1}|S_{A}(\theta)|^{3}d\theta+\int_{0}^{1}G(\theta)S_{A}(-\theta)|S_{A}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta.

Denote the above inequality by
E\lesssim E_{1}+E_{2}
. As
x\gtrsim 1
, we deduce from Proposition
4.2
that

E_{1}\lesssim\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}x^{3}\alpha^{2}\log 2\alpha^{-1}.

To estimate
E_{2}
, we notice that

E_{2}\leq\frac{W}{\phi(W)}(\log x)^{3}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in A}1_{(W,p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1})=1}.

For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}
, denote
A_{j}=\{p\in A:p\equiv j(\operatorname{mod}W)\}
. Then

\sum_{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in A}1_{(W,p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1})=1}=\sum_{p\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}\end{subarray}}|A_{i}||A_{j+p}|\leq\frac{\alpha x}{\log x}\max_{p\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}\end{subarray}}|A_{i}||A_{j+p}|.

Meanwhile, we let
|A_{j}|=\alpha_{j}x(\phi(W)\log x)^{-1}
. Thus

E_{2}\leq\alpha x^{3}\frac{W}{\phi(W)^{3}}\max_{p\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j+p}

Recall that
W\leq\prod_{p<\frac{1}{2}\log x}p\lesssim x^{3/4}
. Then by Brun-Titchmarsh theorem (see
[
5
, Theorem 4.16]
) we have
\alpha_{j}\in[0,10]
, as
x\gtrsim 1
. Now we have the constraint

(8)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}}\alpha_{j}=\alpha\phi(W),\quad\alpha_{j}\in[0,10]\text{ for }j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}.

Suppose that
\{\alpha_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}}
and
\{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}}
maximize the bilinear form

(9)

\Phi(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{j}

with both of them subject to (
8
). We claim that we can assume there is at most one
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}
such that
\ \alpha_{j}^{\prime}\not\in\{0,10\}
and the same for
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed, if there exist
\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime},\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}\in(0,10)
with
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
, then for any
S_{2}\geq S_{1}\geq 0
, we have

\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}S_{1}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}S_{2}\leq\begin{cases}(\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}-10)S_{1}+10S_{2},&\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}\geq 10,\\
0\cdot S_{1}+(\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime})S_{2},&\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}<10.\end{cases}

This means we can replace
\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime},\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}
by
\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}-10,10
or
0,\alpha_{j_{1}}^{\prime}+\alpha_{j_{2}}^{\prime}
to let
\Phi(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})
increase with
\alpha^{\prime}
still subject to
(
8
)
.

Now we have

\begin{split}\Phi(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime})&\leq 20\alpha\phi(W)+100\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{1},L_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{W}\\
|L_{1}|,|L_{2}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{10}\phi(W)\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j\in\mathbb{Z}_{W}^{*}\\
i\in L_{1},j\in L_{2}\end{subarray}}1\\
&\lesssim\alpha\phi(W)+\alpha^{2}\phi(W)^{2}\frac{\log\log(3\alpha^{-1}\log w)}{\log w},\end{split}

where in the last step we used Lemma
5.1
. It then follows

\begin{split}E_{2}&\lesssim\alpha^{2}x^{3}\frac{W}{\phi(W)^{2}}+\alpha^{3}x^{3}\frac{\log\log(3\alpha^{-1}\log w)}{\log w}\frac{W}{\phi(W)}\\
&\lesssim\alpha^{2}x^{3}e^{-\frac{1}{2}w}+\alpha^{3}x^{3}\log\log(3\alpha^{-1}\log w)\end{split}

and thus

E\lesssim E_{1}+E_{2}\lesssim\alpha^{3}x^{3}\left(\alpha^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}w}+\log\log(3\alpha^{-1}\log w)+\frac{\log\log w}{\sqrt{w}}\alpha^{-1}\log 2\alpha^{-1}\right).

Set
w=10e^{2}(\alpha^{-1}\log 2\alpha^{-1})^{2}\geq e^{2}
. Let
c_{1}>0
be such that
\alpha^{-1}\leq c_{1}(\log x)^{1/2}(\log\log x)^{-1}
implies
w\leq\frac{1}{2}\log x
. Then

E\lesssim\alpha^{3}x^{3}(1+\log\log 3\alpha^{-1}+\log\log w)\lesssim\alpha^{3}x^{3}\log\log 3\alpha^{-1}.

Combing this with (
7
) we conclude the proof with
c=c_{1}^{-1}
.
∎

Enhanced detection of network intrusions and anomalies in internet of things applications using a hybrid artificial intelligence model combining CNN and LSTM
Authors: Bharathi Subramaniam, Sudhakar Sengan, Julian L. Webber, Abolfazl Mehbodniya, Meshal Ghalib Alharbi, Majed Alsafyani, Ahmed Emara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid development of the internet of things (IoT) has made an increasing number of large-scale, interconnected networks more vulnerable to advanced attacks. There are increased risks of hidden intrusions and compromised security in IoT environments, resulting from class imbalance in network traffic datasets, high-dimensional feature spaces, and the requirement for spatial and temporal anomaly detection. Traditional intrusion detection systems (IDS) fail to address these problems. This research work presents IaADS, an intrusion and anomaly detection system integrating 3 balancing components: a conditional generative adversarial network (cGAN) for minority-class data balancing, the slime mould algorithm (SMA) for wrapper-based feature selection (FS), and a dual convolutional neural network and long short-term memory (CNN+LSTM) for spatiotemporal feature extraction (FE). The cGAN generates class-conditioned synthetic samples for under-represented attack types, and SMA reduces training overhead by 18.0% using principled feature dimensionality reduction from 41 to an optimal discriminative subset. Evaluated on the NSL-KDD benchmark dataset against 6 baseline models—SVM, RF, KNN, MLP, LSTM, and CNN—IaADS achieves 97.1% accuracy on DoS detection, 81.5% on R2L, and 70.4% on U2R attack types, with a false positive rate of 3.2% on DoS and 17.4% on U2R. Five-fold cross-validation provides a mean accuracy of 96.4 ± 0.4% and a mean F1-score of 96.0 ± 0.4%, with statistical significance confirmed at p < 0.01 against all baselines. Inference latency of 2.3 ms per sample confirms suitability for near-real-time IoT gateway deployment. The attack taxonomy of NSL-KDD aligns directly with attack types documented in IoT network security, and validation on IoT-native datasets is a direction for future work.

Introduction

The internet of things (IoT) has been developing rapidly, enabling the connection of billions of devices to the internet. This has led to the development of large networks that serve multiple real-time applications simultaneously, such as automated manufacturing, renewable energy management, healthcare, and smart cities1,2. Despite the advantages that IoT devices provide, including improved connectivity and data-driven analysis, they also pose notable security risks due to the limited computing resources they use and the range of their components3. Modern cyberattacks, including denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, data exfiltration, and malware such as ransomware, are becoming increasingly frequent due to the complete attack surface resulting from the growing number of IoT networks4,5. IoT networks have specific requirements, such as real-time anomaly detection (AD)6–8, which classic intrusion detection systems (IDS) were not designed for in static, high-power environments. A novel model for securing IoT environments is for Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) to monitor AD in network traffic, which can indicate malicious activity9,10. Data imbalance, high-dimensional data streams, and the need for spatial and temporal data analysis11 are just a few of the specific problems that classic IDS fails to address in the IoT environment. A boosted false positive rate (FPR) and not optimal efficiency on real-world IoT networks can be the result of conventional machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), which, considering their strength, have failed to detect attack types that are present much less frequently and have an inbuilt propensity to focus on types of attacks that are hit more commonly.

The present study recommends an entirely novel intrusion and anomaly detection system (IaADS) for IoT environments to address these problems. This work has integrated technological solutions within the IaADS. These methods include conditional generative adversarial networks (cGAN) for data balancing, the slime mould algorithm (SMA) for feature selection (FS), and a hybrid convolutional neural network [CNN-LSTM] for AD. The cGAN component addresses class imbalance in network intrusion datasets by generating fake trials for less frequent attack classes. This also makes the model more accurate at identifying infrequent attacks. By focusing on the most valuable features, SMA improves FS, reduces load and heterogeneity, and improves AD accuracy. The CNN+LSTM captures spatial and temporal relations across the network data. This enables IaADS to identify anomalies in a standard range of attack types with high accuracy and a minimum of False Positives (FP).

Most of the attack methods identified in network intrusion datasets used as benchmarks for IDS evaluation are also common in IoT systems. As previously reported in the most recent IoT security research3,9,12, the NSL-KDD dataset employed in this research includes DoS, Probing, R2L, and U2R attack types. Several attack classes target IoT gateways, edge nodes, and embedded connectivity layers. Despite having been developed before IoT communication protocols, including message queuing telemetry transport (MQTT) and constrained application protocol (CoAP), became common, authors developing IoT-oriented IDS frequently use its depiction of internet traffic features as a proxy for testing, enabling complete benchmarking against a known standard.

Related work

Theoretical models for anomaly detection in the IoT, hybrid DL-like methods, GAN-based data balancing, and metaheuristic FS have each contributed to the ongoing demand for intrusion detection in IoT and industrial network environments.

Compared with NIDS that rely on a single approach, a hybrid DL that includes convolutional and recurrent layers consistently outperforms the traditional method. Bamber et al.13 evaluated multiple DL on NSL-KDD using recursive feature elimination for preprocessing, establishing that CNN+LSTM achieved 95% accuracy with an F1-score of 0.94, outperforming standalone artificial neural network (ANN), long short-term memory (LSTM), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM), and gated recurrent unit (GRU) configurations.

Altunay and Albayrak14 extended this line of work to industrial IoT networks, proposing a CNN+LSTM-based IDS evaluated on the Network Security Laboratory—Knowledge discovery in databases (NSL-KDD) and the UNSW-NB15, reporting 99.21% accuracy on NSL-KDD using residual block-enhanced feature learning.

Alashjaee15 further advanced hybrid spatiotemporal modelling by incorporating a self-attention mechanism into a CNN+LSTM pipeline, achieving 94.8–97.5% accuracy on NSL-KDD and botnet internet of things (Bot-IoT) with sub-35 ms inference latency, signifying practical suitability for real-time detection. Saheed et al.16 explored a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN)+LSTM in an autoencoder configuration for intrusion detection in industrial control systems, signifying that joint spatial–temporal FE from critical setup traffic provides robust generalisation across hidden attack patterns. Collectively, these works confirm that hybrid CNN+LSTM pipelines consistently outperform single-network models, yet leave the class imbalance and feature redundancy problems insufficiently addressed.

GAN-based data augmentation has emerged as a principled response to the severe class imbalance characteristic of intrusion detection datasets. Yang et al.17 invented community evolutionary generative adversarial network (CE-GAN), a conditional GAN with an encoder-decoder network incorporating Nash equilibrium-based classifier ensemble optimisation, indicating on NSL-KDD and university of new south wales—network behaviour 2015 (UNSW-NB15) datasets that conditional synthetic augmentation of minority attack classes substantially improves multi-class classification metrics compared to standard oversampling methods. The reported gains in rare-class recall directly validate the design rationale of the cGAN component in the proposed IaADS, where minority-class augmentation for user-to-root attack (U2R) and remote-to-local (or remote-to-user) attack (R2L) types is a primary objective.

Metaheuristic FS has been commonly adopted to reduce dimensionality and computational overhead in IDS pipelines. As suggested by Xu et al.18, using PSO-guided feature subset selection in combination with CNN, BiLSTM, and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) classification techniques on NSL-KDD, systematic feature reduction achieves detection accuracy higher than 99% while drastically reducing training complexity.

Babu et al.19 suggested a hybrid whale optimization algorithm—differential evolution (WOA-DE) metaheuristic for FS in imbalanced IDS datasets evaluated on NSL-KDD, achieving 97.6% accuracy with improved precision-recall balance in minority attack classes. Both studies confirm that wrapper-based metaheuristic FS operating under a classification fitness evaluator consistently reduces noise-feature interference and improves per-class detection rates, directly supporting the SMA-based wrapper method employed in the present work.

IoT-specific hybrid IDS address the additional constraints of resource-limited deployment environments. Saheed et al.20 demonstrated that Ensemble Learning (EL), combining bagging, stacking, and AdaBoost, achieves 99% accuracy with low FPR on supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) and industrial sensor network datasets, affirming that hybrid ensemble methods generalise across IoT-adjacent network environments. Saheed and Chukwuere21 further established, through a cyber-physical systems and industrial internet of things (CPS-IIoT) attention-based model combining Pearson correlation-driven privacy preservation with BiLSTM and scaled dot-product attention, that explainability-aware temporal DL models achieve near-perfect detection on University of New South Wales—Network Behaviour 2015 (UNSW-NB15) and X-Connectivity- and Device-agnostic Intrusion Dataset for Industrial Internet of Things (X-IIoTID). By benchmarking a hybrid LSTM-CNN on the Bot-IoT dataset, Sinha et al.22 verified that LSTM-CNN hybrids remain useful for IoT deployment in challenging environments, achieving 99.87% accuracy and a False Positive Rate (FPR) of 0.13%.

Data balancing with conditional GAN (cGAN)

In NIDS datasets, class distributions are frequently highly unbalanced, with a large proportion of data belonging to the “Normal” class and a relatively small number of samples labelled as “Anomalous” or “Intrusive.” The following can be challenging because it might result in unreliable. This condition describes the scenario in which the class distributions are considered “normal”. This imbalance can hinder model performance, as models trained on such data tend to be biased toward the majority class, leading to poor detection of minority class examples (i.e., Anomalies). A cGAN is a data augmentation method that generates false data points based on class labels, effectively balancing the dataset24. This is the method used to address this problem.

Two neural networks, the generator ‘G’ and the discriminator ‘D’, are trained simultaneously in a minimax game, resulting in a GAN. A random vector ‘z’ is collected from a prior distribution ‘p(z)’, which is generally a Gaussian distribution. By training the distribution of the minority (Anomalous) class and basing the generation method on the anomaly label, the conditional GAN generates fake data for this class. The present study may provide a balanced dataset with real and fake anomaly test cases by sampling from the generator based on the anomaly class label. In this method, the predictive model will be trained with a valid number of anomalous data25.

In this work, the cGAN is trained using an iterative method that updates the generator and discriminator simultaneously. The discriminator is fine-tuned during training to maximise its ability to distinguish between real and fake data. On the contrary, the generator is designed to “fool” the discriminator into detecting fake data as accurate, thereby enhancing the quality and accuracy of the generated samples. The generator’s ability to generate realistic anomaly sample data conditioned on the anomaly label improves after training26.

By correcting the inherently unbalanced distribution of classes in network traffic data, this balanced dataset significantly improves the AD predictive ability to detect typical classes. Also, enhancing the AD’s generalizability across various IDS is the predicted GAN’s ability to generate data in a controlled, class-specific manner, enabling it to generate fake samples matched to multiple anomaly types27.

The cGAN implementation employs a generator comprising three Fully Connected (FC) layers with 128, 256, and 512 units, each followed by batch normalisation and a LeakyReLU activation (slope 0.2), with a final tanh output layer that generates normalised synthetic feature vectors. Feature selection using SMA

In NIDS, the FS counts are used because they sample key features while reducing data dimensionality. Reducing noise and redundant features improves the accuracy and efficiency of the AD model. This research uses the SMA in a Wrapper-based FS to improve feature subsets based on a learning algorithm’s results. A case study of a classifier is the k-Nearest Neighbours (k-NN) classifier. SMA is stimulated by the diffusion and foraging behavior of slime moulds, which are adaptively driven by environmental stimuli. This metaheuristic algorithm is divided into three main phases: Approach Food (AF), Wrap Food (WF), and Grabble Food (GF).

Training the proposed model

The model is trained under a supervised learning framework, with the NSL-KDD training set partitioned into 80% training and 20% validation subsets using stratified splitting to preserve class distribution across both partitions. The Adam optimiser is selected over stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and root mean square propagation (RMSProp) based on its adaptive per-parameter learning rate mechanism, which adjusts gradient updates using first and second moment estimates—a property particularly helpful for temporal network traffic data where feature gradients exhibit high variance and non-stationarity across attack types; SGD with fixed learning rate is prone to slow convergence under such conditions, while RMSProp lacks the momentum term that stabilises updates during the early training phases of recurrent layers. The learning rate is set to 0.001, and the simulation is trained for 50 epochs to verify that proper weight adjustment can be achieved without significant overfitting. To stabilise intermediate activation processes, batch normalisation is used at every step of the procedure. Also, dropout layers have been integrated into densely connected layers to significantly reduce feature co-adaptation. In binary anomaly classification, the proper loss function is binary cross-entropy. In Section “Result and discussion”, we report the training and validation loss and accuracy curves across all 50 epochs. The following is used to validate the convergence method and verify that there is no overfitting. The model’s performance is measured using fivefold cross-validation across all training datasets. The mean and standard deviation of accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and FPR are presented across folds to verify the statistical accuracy of the results. Conclusion and future work

This research presents IaADS, an intrusion and anomaly detection system designed for IoT network security that addresses the persistent challenges of class imbalance, high-dimensional traffic features, and the simultaneous detection of multiple attack types in real time. The proposed model integrates cGAN for minority-class data balancing, SMA for discriminative feature selection, and a dual CNN+LSTM for joint spatial and temporal FE. The findings of a test on the NSL-KDD dataset indicate that IaADS consistently outperforms baseline models in terms of accuracy, recall, precision, and F1-score, while maintaining a low FPR across all attack types. For example, cGAN-based balancing significantly improves the detection of low-frequency attack types, including U2R and R2L. On the other hand, SMA-driven FS reduces computational overhead without compromising detection performance, effectively validating the significant role of each network component in the overall security of the proposed model.

For practical IoT implementation prerequisites, the IaADS is directly correlated. By reducing the complexity of the data provided to a minimal discriminative subset, SMA-based FS minimises inference-time overhead at resource-constrained edge nodes and Internet of Things gateways. The CNN+LSTM classification algorithm can be used for near-real-time traffic monitoring in the surrounding areas of IoT networks because the cGAN balancing functions are completely balanced during training. This avoids the requirement for a higher computational cost during network installation. The attack classification used by NSL-KDD, which includes types such as DoS, Probing, R2L, and U2R, corresponds to attack classes commonly documented in IoT network security contexts. This provides support for the stated practical importance of the detection rate. NSL-KDD, on the other hand, does not capture the behaviour of Internet of Things-specific protocols, such as MQTT publish-subscribe patterns or CoAP request-response cycles. Validation on recent IoT-based data sources, such as CICIoT2023 and BoT-IoT, is an important phase toward attaining more comprehensive adaptation.

Future research should focus on extending IaADS to detect emerging and zero-day attack variants while further reducing model complexity for deployment on severely resource-constrained IoT endpoints. The present work establishes a methodological basis for scalable, adaptive IoT network security, particularly as the diversity and frequency of cyber-attacks targeting interconnected device ecosystems continue to increase.

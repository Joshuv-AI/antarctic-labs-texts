Rescuing a Lost Side-Branch After Covered Stenting of Coronary Perforation
Authors: Cheuk Yin Lai, Timothy Ho Him Kam, Guang-Ming Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding AuthorKey words: complication, intravascular ultrasound, percutaneous coronary intervention, stents

Coronary perforation is one of the most feared complications of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Despite advances in equipment design, the increasing number of patients referred for complex PCI means that the risk of coronary perforation remains 0.5%-1%,1,2 especially with the use of advanced techniques such as directional atherectomy. Bailout algorithms suggest treatment by prolonged balloon tamponade or embolization with coils or fat (Figure 1).3 However, these measures rely on inducing perivascular thrombosis to seal off perforation, which may not be sufficient when perforation involves large vessels.2,4 Covered stents are a definitive way of sealing the bleeding vessel, but they have their drawbacks, namely poorer deliverability and limitations in balloon overexpansion, leading to inadequate sealing or long-term restenosis.5 Ideally, the bleeding site should also be free from significant side branches, whose compromise may cause significant downstream ischemia. Emergent V-stenting has been described in such cases involving bifurcations,6 although their long-term patency is unknown. Jailed wire technique with recrossing and kissing balloon inflation can potentially tackle the above shortcomings, but recrossing the covered stent itself presents a significant challenge, because of the polymeric membrane blocking the side-branch access. Alternatively, in situ laser fenestration (ISLF), a technique adapted from side-branch rescue during endovascular aneurysm repair, might also be used.7,8

Coronary Perforation Bailout Methods

In this issue of JACC Case Reports, Watanabe et al9 reported a successful case in which a massive left anterior descending (LAD) perforation in an 87-year old woman following directional atherectomy was managed with intravascular ultrasound (IVUS)-guided covered stenting with a PK Papyrus stent (Biotronik). The resultant jailed left circumflex artery was rescued by recrossing the covered stent using a high penetration wire and a jailed wire as a marker, followed by kissing balloon inflation to restore flow. The article is notable in that the perforation site is in proximity to the left main bifurcation, thereby posing a substantial risk of side-branch occlusion with covered stent deployment. There are scant case reports on side-branch rescue after covered stenting; most elected to forego kissing balloon inflation, rather focusing on using microcatheters and stiff wires to fenestrate the covered stent membrane, with or without subsequent stenting of the side branch.10, 11, 12 Watanabe et al9 had the foresight to place a jailed wire at the left circumflex artery before deploying the covered stent, such that recrossing can be guided by the wire. The choice of a polyurethane-covered stent with a single-layer membrane was also prudent in facilitating side branch recrossing. Finally, the use of IVUS was also instrumental to the success of the rescue, as the investigators were alerted in the initial IVUS assessment to the possibility of imminent perforation, averting the catastrophic event of converting it into complete coronary rupture, and the images also proved critical to the rapid identification of the bleeding site and covered stent sizing during the procedure.

The investigators have demonstrated that, with careful choice of covered stent and guidewire placement, bifurcation optimization can be adequately performed even during emergent rescue of coronary perforation. As they observed, covered stenting limits the use of jailed balloon techniques; hence, kissing balloon inflation after covered stents is not routinely performed; similarly, the potential risk of entrapment of a jailed guidewire may need to be investigated. Finally, although their patient demonstrated good stent patency at 6-month follow-up, whether the extra step of kissing balloon inflation reduces long-term restenosis rates remains to be elucidated. Nevertheless, the investigators showcase that covered stenting may be feasible in complicated bifurcation lesions, potentially widening their applicability in bailout algorithms.

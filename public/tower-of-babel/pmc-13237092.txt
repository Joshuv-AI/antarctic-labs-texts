Enhancing long-term structure in symbolic music generation via a cascaded Skeleton-to-texture framework
Authors: Yang Yalan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the field of symbolic music generation, maintaining macro-structural coherence and preventing logical drift within long sequences remains a critical challenge. Traditional autoregressive models primarily rely on implicit probabilistic statistics to capture contextual dependencies. Consequently, they often struggle to retain memory of initial musical motifs over hundreds of time steps. To address this limitation, this paper proposes CAST, a framework for long-range music generation based on explicit skeleton guidance. This method decouples the complex sequence generation task into two subprocesses: macro-harmonic planning and micro-texture filling. Specifically, MusicBERT is introduced to extract deep semantic skeletons. We then utilize a cross-attention mechanism to establish a dynamic mapping between these skeletons and the MuseFormer generator. This design achieves explicit modeling of long-range dependencies. For validation, we selected MuseFormer, which represents the state-of-the-art in sparse attention mechanisms, as the baseline model. Experiments were conducted to verify the superiority of explicit structural constraints over purely implicit learning. Quantitative evaluations demonstrate significant improvements in generating sequences up to 1000 tokens. The CAST framework reduced the structural error from 0.58 (baseline) to 0.22. Additionally, it increased chord generation accuracy to 96%. These results indicate an effective resolution to the logical collapse problem in long-sequence generation. Furthermore, mechanism analysis reveals significant functional backtracking patterns within the model. This confirms that our method guides the model to acquire deep harmonic grammar logic, thereby generating complex musical works that are structurally rigorous and stylistically unified.

Introduction

With the rapid development of generative artificial intelligence, the field of symbolic music generation has transitioned from simple monophonic melody synthesis to complex polyphonic arrangement. Current deep generative models demonstrate capabilities comparable to human composers in capturing short-term musical textures, harmonic colors, and rhythmic patterns. Consequently, computer-generated music segments now achieve high fidelity and artistic expressiveness at the local level1. However, music is inherently a highly structured temporal art. Its essence lies not merely in instantaneous acoustic effects but in logical construction and thematic development over long time windows2. This structural integrity spans hundreds or even thousands of time steps, ranging from the correspondence between the exposition and recapitulation in sonata form to the alternation of verse and chorus in pop music. Maintaining such integrity is a key criterion for determining whether AI truly masters the logic of musical creation3. It represents a significant gap that must be bridged to advance music generation technology from experimental exploration to industrial application.

Although existing sequence modeling methods have achieved remarkable success with discrete data such as text, they face severe challenges regarding “structural drift” when dealing with long-sequence symbolic music4. Music sequences typically contain high information density and complex polyphonic dependencies. A standard-length musical piece often corresponds to thousands of discrete tokens. Under the mainstream autoregressive generation paradigm, models tend to overemphasize the immediate local context. As a result, they gradually forget early thematic motifs5. This leads to a loss of tonal center and structural direction as the generation progresses. This linear “step-by-step” prediction mechanism ensures smooth transitions between adjacent bars. However, it struggles to maintain the global macro-layout. Consequently, generated pieces often exhibit disorganized sections, fail to return to the theme, or suffer from a collapse in harmonic logic6. At a macro level, the results appear loose and lack an internal skeleton.

Addressing the issue of long-range structural consistency cannot rely solely on blindly scaling model parameters or extending context windows. Such approaches not only incur unbearable computational costs but also fail to fundamentally endow models with global planning capabilities similar to those of human composers. Human creation processes often follow a hierarchical mindset of “skeleton first, flesh second.” Specific note filling occurs only after establishing the harmonic progression and formal structure7. Therefore, there is an urgent need to construct a computational framework that decouples macro-structural planning from micro-texture generation. By introducing explicit structural guidance mechanisms, the generation process shifts from pure probabilistic prediction to purposeful hierarchical construction8. This approach effectively curbs structural degradation in long sequences. Furthermore, it provides new theoretical perspectives and technical pathways for achieving high-quality long-range music generation that combines rigorous logic with rich detail. Establishing such a generation paradigm that maintains long-range structural robustness while preserving micro-level expressiveness has become a central objective in the field, and constitutes the key to advancing machine composition from surface-level probabilistic imitation toward deep logical conception.

Related work

Driven by the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence, the field of symbolic music generation is undergoing a profound paradigm shift from traditional rule-based systems to data-driven deep models. The core objective is to model sequences of discrete musical events using computational methods, thereby creating complex works that possess both artistic quality and logic9. Given the significant isomorphism between music and natural language in terms of syntactic structure and sequence characteristics, cutting-edge methods from Natural Language Processing (NLP) have been widely adapted for music information retrieval and generation tasks. In particular, Transformer-based attention architectures have become the mainstream backbone networks for capturing complex temporal dependencies and multimodal contexts in music1,10. To overcome the inherent hierarchical features of polyphonic music and the memory bottlenecks caused by long sequences, recent research has focused on developing improved sequence models. By introducing meta-learning strategies or hierarchical attention modules, these studies have significantly enhanced model generalization under limited data and the ability to maintain macro-sectional structures11,12. Building on this, to meet the demands of interactive creation, academic focus is expanding from unconditional generation to refined controllable generation. Researchers have achieved flexible manipulation of high-dimensional attributes such as musical texture, local tension, and perceived emotion by designing new encoding formats, probe-assisted controls, or conditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)13–15. Meanwhile, the establishment of specialized standardized toolkits and objective evaluation systems provides solid infrastructure support for quantifying generation quality, reproducing baseline models, and visualizing music data16.

Although existing generative models have laid the foundation for sequence modeling, recent studies have concentrated on multimodal alignment and self-supervised representation learning to further enhance the perception of deep musical logic. However, these methods still face specific limitations in directly enabling long-range structural generation. In the multimodal domain, Yu et al. proposed the MuDaR framework, which learns joint representations of music and dance through explicit and implicit rhythm synchronization mechanisms. While this method effectively captures cross-modal rhythmic alignment, its structural perception relies heavily on external visual cues. Thus, it is difficult to independently resolve long-range logical consistency issues within the pure auditory domain17. Regarding discrete representations, Zhu et al. introduced the MuQ model based on Mel-Residual Vector Quantization (Mel-RVQ). While self-supervised learning significantly improved downstream understanding tasks, this lossy quantization process often discards micro-level dynamic and duration details crucial for high-fidelity texture generation18. In terms of structural analysis, Buisson et al. utilized contrastive learning to extract multi-level audio representations to assist in music segmentation. However, such methods are essentially discriminative analysis tools. They can identify existing section boundaries but cannot guide generative models to actively construct new macro structures19. Similar limitations exist in the work of Hashizume et al., who optimized music similarity retrieval by separating instrument subspaces. Although this solved timbre decoupling at the orchestration level, it did not touch upon the logic of formal development in the time dimension20. Regarding pre-training paradigms, the M3BERT model proposed by Greer et al. utilized multi-task learning to enhance the understanding of musical emotion and style. However, its architecture is designed primarily for classification and regression tasks, lacking an autoregressive decoding mechanism to translate high-level semantics into continuous musical sequences21. Furthermore, traditional signal processing methods, such as the low-rank sparse representation proposed by Cai and Zhang, can improve genre classification accuracy, but their shallow linear models struggle to capture complex non-linear long-range dependencies22. Similarly, while the Multi-channel Convolutional Neural Network (MRNet) proposed by Heo et al. captures multi-scale features through different dilation rates, the inherent local receptive field of CNNs limits their ability to plan the overall architecture of minute-level long movements23. In summary, the current field lacks a unified framework capable of endogenously fusing deep structural understanding with fine-grained texture generation.

However, a horizontal comparison reveals that most methods still tend to treat long-range structure as an implicit statistical feature for end-to-end learning, rather than planning it as an independent explicit variable. Furthermore, research on the decoupled modeling of harmonic skeletons and polyphonic textures remains insufficient. This absence of a “macro-planning/micro-filling” mechanism is the primary reason why existing models produce structurally loose results when generating minute-level long movements. A critical distinction exists between the proposed CAST framework and prior hierarchical generation approaches. Methods based on Variational Autoencoders, such as MusicVAE, learn implicit latent structures through encoder-decoder architectures. The latent codes in these models are continuous vectors without direct musical interpretation, making it difficult for users to understand or manipulate the structural planning process. Compound Word Transformer and similar tokenization-based methods employ fixed-granularity hierarchical representations, where the hierarchy is determined by the encoding scheme rather than musical semantics. In contrast, CAST operates on explicit harmonic skeletons that are both human-readable and musically meaningful. Our contributions

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:Proposal of a new paradigm for long-range music generation based on explicit skeleton guidance: Addressing the pervasive problem of long-range structural drift in symbolic music generation, this paper proposes a planning-filling decoupled framework (CAST). The key distinction from existing hierarchical approaches lies in the nature of the intermediate representation. While Variational Autoencoder-based methods such as MusicVAE rely on implicit continuous latent codes that lack musical interpretability, CAST operates on explicit symbolic skeletons composed of human-readable harmonic and structural tokens. This design enables direct inspection and optional manual editing of the structural plan before texture generation. By decomposing the complex sequence generation task into macro-harmonic skeleton planning and micro-texture filling, we provide a new solution for generating long musical pieces with rigorous formal structures.Design of a dynamic semantic alignment mechanism based on cross-attention: We constructed a cross-modal feature interaction module that utilizes a cross-attention mechanism to establish a dynamic mapping between the deep semantic representations of MusicBERT and the sparse generation space of MuseFormer. This mechanism differs fundamentally from naive concatenation strategies that simply prepend skeleton tokens to the input sequence. The cross-attention design enables time-step-specific queries into the skeleton representation, allowing the texture generator to selectively attend to relevant structural information at each generation step. This dynamic alignment not only achieves precise correspondence between skeleton and texture but also endows the model with functional backtracking capabilities, where the generator actively retrieves and echoes historical segments sharing the same harmonic function. Such behavior ensures stylistic unity over long time spans and has not been observed in prior pipeline-based generation systems.Validation of the effectiveness of explicit structural constraints: Extensive quantitative experiments demonstrate that this method significantly outperforms current baseline models in key metrics such as structural error and chord accuracy. It effectively breaks through performance bottlenecks in long-sequence generation. Mechanism analysis confirms that the model acquires deep musical organizational logic through explicit structural guidance, rather than merely fitting local statistics.

Macro-Skeleton planning based on MusicBERT

Conventional autoregressive models process musical sequences strictly from left to right, which prevents the utilization of future context during the generation of earlier tokens. This inherent look-ahead blindness often leads to error accumulation and structural drift, making it difficult to maintain thematic coherence across long-range time spans.

MusicBERT addresses this limitation by employing a bidirectional attention mechanism pre-trained via Masked Language Modeling (MLM), enabling the simultaneous perception of both past and future contexts. Figure 1 illustrates the architecture of this macro-skeleton planning module. The bidirectional Transformer encoder provides a global receptive field for capturing deep semantic dependencies and macro-structural patterns such as recurrence and modulation. The training objective combines pseudo log-likelihood loss with a global structural consistency constraint that regularizes long-range dependencies between sequence head and tail. Macro-structure planning via bidirectional masked modeling

In the first stage of the cascade generation framework, the core task is to construct a parameterized model \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi$$\end{document} to sample a skeleton sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{\mathscr {S}}$$\end{document} with long-range logical self-consistency from the high-dimensional music structure space \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Omega _s$$\end{document}. Unlike traditional unidirectional autoregressive generation, skeleton planning requires the model to utilize both past and future contextual information simultaneously to establish current structural nodes (such as harmonic functions or sectional cadences). To this end, a bidirectional Masked Language Model (MLM) based on the Transformer architecture is adopted to model the prior distribution of the skeleton.

First, we define the input embedding representation of the skeleton sequence. Initializing with a fully masked sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {S}^{(0)} = \{\texttt {[MASK]}\}^M$$\end{document}, in the k-th iteration, the probability distributions of all positions are updated in parallel in a non-autoregressive manner. It establishes that the bidirectional architecture does not impose any intrinsic range limitation on inter-node communication, thereby providing a necessary structural condition for capturing long-span correspondences such as the exposition-recapitulation relationship in sonata form. However, this connectivity property alone does not guarantee lossless information preservation in practice. The theorem should therefore be interpreted as confirming the architectural accessibility of distant structural information rather than as an unconditional guarantee of distance-independent information propagation.

Micro-texture infilling based on MuseFormer

Standard Transformer architectures rely on full self-attention mechanisms with quadratic computational complexity (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(T^2)$$\end{document}). This imposes a severe bottleneck when processing long symbolic music sequences typically containing thousands of tokens, often necessitating sequence truncation that disrupts the integrity of long-form musical structures.

MuseFormer addresses this efficiency challenge by employing a hybrid attention strategy. Figure 2 illustrates the architecture of this micro-texture infilling module. The hybrid attention mechanism utilizes fine-grained attention to capture immediate local nuances through neighboring windows and coarse-grained attention to summarize distant historical contexts through pooled block representations. The skeleton-texture cross-attention module dynamically injects structural guidance by using texture hidden states as queries and skeleton representations as keys and values. The composite training objective combines maximum likelihood estimation with semantic consistency regularization, optimized through differentiable Gumbel-Softmax sampling. This design reduces computational costs to near-linear complexity while enabling the model to maintain effective memory over extremely long sequences.Fig. 2The architecture of Micro-Texture Infilling based on MuseFormer.

The core challenge at this stage is maintaining micro-note coherence while strictly following macro-skeleton guidance over extremely long sequence lengths T (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T \gg 1000$$\end{document}). Time Complexity The hierarchical decomposition strategy significantly optimizes the computational efficiency of the proposed CAST framework. In Phase 1, macro-structure planning operates on the skeleton sequence length M. This is substantially shorter than the full texture length T (typically \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M \ll T$$\end{document}). Consequently, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(M^2)$$\end{document} complexity of the bidirectional self-attention in MusicBERT incurs negligible computational cost. Phase 2 presents the primary computational burden. However, employing the MuseFormer architecture with hybrid fine-coarse attention circumvents the prohibitive \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(T^2)$$\end{document} complexity associated with standard Transformers. Instead, complexity is reduced to approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(T \sqrt{T})$$\end{document} or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(T \cdot (L_{fine} + T/L_{block}))$$\end{document}, depending on the block size configuration. Furthermore, the cross-attention mechanism for structural guidance introduces a term of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(T \cdot M)$$\end{document}, which scales linearly with the target sequence length.

Space Complexity Regarding space complexity, the memory footprint is primarily determined by the storage of activation maps and attention matrices during the micro-texture in-filling phase. Since the skeleton generator operates in a low-dimensional latent space with short sequences, its memory contribution is minimal. In the texture generator, sparse attention patterns (fine-grained local and coarse-grained global) ensure that memory requirements for attention weights grow sub-quadratically with respect to T. Additionally, the cross-attention module only requires storing the key-value pairs of the skeleton sequence. This adds a space complexity of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {O}(M \cdot d)$$\end{document}, where d is the hidden dimension. This linear dependency on skeleton length—contrasted with the quadratic dependency on texture length in full-context models—enables the CAST framework to process thousands of time steps within the VRAM constraints of standard industrial GPUs.

Experiments

Experimental setup

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed CAST framework in both long-range structural planning and micro-texture generation, we selected the GiantMIDI-Piano (GP) dataset. This dataset serves as the core corpus for training and evaluation. It represents one of the largest symbolic classical piano music libraries available and aims to address the lack of large-scale, high-quality classical piano data in traditional symbolic music research. The dataset covers 10,855 solo piano works composed by 2786 composers. In total, it contains approximately 38.7 million transcribed notes. Unlike traditional quantized MIDI data derived from rule-based generation or manual input, GiantMIDI-Piano utilizes a high-resolution piano transcription system. It converts real audio recordings retrieved from the International Music Score Library Project (IMSLP) into symbolic MIDI files. This construction method ensures the inclusion of not only basic pitch and duration information but also key expressive attributes. These include velocity, pedal usage, and micro-timing offsets for note onsets and offsets. Over 90% of the samples in the dataset originate from live human performances rather than mechanical sequence inputs. This non-quantized real data distribution contains rich human performance rhythms and dynamic changes, which is crucial for training MuseFormer to capture delicate micro-textures. Specifically, we employed a strictly filtered high-quality subset containing 7236 works to ensure metadata accuracy and minimize the impact of transcription noise on structural learning.

Evaluation metrics

Three objective metrics are employed to quantify the generation quality from complementary perspectives.

Structural Error measures the discrepancy between the macro-formal organization of generated sequences and ground truth references. This metric is computed based on Self-Similarity Matrices (SSM), which characterize repetition patterns and sectional boundaries within a musical piece. For a sequence \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {X}$$\end{document}, the SSM is constructed by computing pairwise cosine similarities between bar-level chroma feature vectors. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{c}_i \in \mathbb {R}^{12}$$\end{document} denote the normalized chroma vector for bar i. Chord Accuracy quantifies the harmonic fidelity of the generated texture with respect to the guiding skeleton. For each bar in the generated sequence, the predominant chord is extracted using the Chordino algorithm, which performs simultaneous estimation of chord labels and downbeat positions from symbolic input. Perplexity (PPL) serves as a standard measure of local prediction quality, computed as the exponentiated average negative log-likelihood over all tokens in the test set.

The attention entropy column quantifies the diffuseness of skeleton-texture feature interaction, where lower values indicate more focused attention patterns. Concatenation exhibits the highest attention entropy (2.38), more than double that of cross-attention (1.12), indicating difficulty in establishing precise correspondence between skeleton positions and generation steps when structural information is provided as a static prefix. FiLM and Adaptive Layer Normalization produce intermediate attention entropy values of 1.89 and 1.73 respectively. These global modulation approaches apply skeleton-dependent transformations uniformly across the sequence, limiting their capacity for position-specific structural guidance. The low attention entropy of cross-attention (1.12) reflects its ability to enable explicit, dynamic queries from each generation step to relevant skeleton positions. This position-specific alignment capability underlies the functional backtracking behavior analyzed in Section “Skeleton-texture alignment accuracy: mechanism verification”.

Discussion

This study proposes CAST, a framework for long-range symbolic music generation based on explicit skeleton guidance. It aims to resolve the structural drift problem faced by traditional autoregressive models in long-form generation. However, experimental data reveal that the structural error of pure autoregressive models in long-sequence generation reaches as high as 0.58. This indicates that relying solely on probabilistic fitting fails to maintain memory of initial motifs after hundreds of time steps. In contrast, CAST essentially decouples the complex optimization problem of “long-range dependency” into two sub-problems: “skeleton planning” and “texture filling.” This is achieved by introducing an explicit harmonic skeleton generated by MusicBERT. Quantitative analysis shows that this hierarchical strategy significantly reduces the structural error to 0.22. Furthermore, it maintains low error stability throughout generation processes spanning up to 1000 tokens. These results empirically support the view that introducing low-frequency structural priors (skeleton) to constrain high-frequency sampling (texture) is crucial in symbolic music generation. Conclusion

This study addresses the pervasive challenge of long-range structural drift in the field of symbolic music generation by proposing the CAST framework based on explicit skeleton guidance. Leveraging the deep semantic understanding of MusicBERT and the sparse attention mechanism of MuseFormer, CAST utilizes a cross-attention module to establish a dynamic semantic channel between skeleton and texture. This effectively breaks through the memory bottleneck of traditional autoregressive models in long contexts. Extensive quantitative experiments and qualitative analyses demonstrate that the framework significantly reduces structural error to 0.22 in generation processes up to 1000 tokens. Simultaneously, it achieves a harmonic match rate of 94.8%, proving its superior performance in maintaining logical self-consistency and harmonic grammatical precision. Furthermore, visual analysis of the attention mechanism reveals the “functional backtracking” characteristic within the model. This confirms that explicit structural constraints effectively guide the model to acquire deep musical organizational logic rather than mere probabilistic statistical features. This work not only establishes a new benchmark for interpretable, high-fidelity long-form music creation but also lays a solid theoretical and technical foundation for future extensions into multi-track orchestration and interactive human-machine co-creation.

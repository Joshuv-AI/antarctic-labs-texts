The Ising magnetisation field and the Gaussian free field
Tomás Alcalde López ∗ , Lorca Heeney ∗ , and Marcin Lis

Abstract
We construct a natural coupling between the continuum Gaussian free field (GFF) and the critical Ising magnetisation field (IMF) in a planar domain. In fact, we show that two independent IMFs with + + boundary conditions and two independent IMFs with free boundary conditions are a deterministic function of a single instance of the GFF together with a sequence of independent coin flips. This construction should be seen as an extension of the bosonisation phenomenon, and to the best of our knowledge its existence has not been predicted before. We arrive at our main result in the continuum by studying novel discrete structures. Our starting point is a coupling resembling the Edwards–Sokal coupling between the Ising model and the Fortuin–Kasteleyn random cluster model, though with role of the latter played by a different percolation model obtained from the double random current model. By taking a scaling limit of the coupling at criticality, we obtain a continuum Edwards–Sokal-like representation of the IMFs in terms of certain two-valued sets of the GFF introduced by Aru, Sepúlveda and Werner.

Introduction

In this work we uncover an intrinsic relationship between two planar random fields that play central roles in random conformal geometry – the continuum
Gaussian free field
(GFF) and the
Ising magnetisation field
(IMF).
The former is a universal object appearing as the (in many cases still conjectural) scaling limit of discrete height function models in their delocalized phase
[

,

,
52
,
20
,
38
]
.
In physics it is a model of a free (non-interacting)
bosonic field theory
, and in mathematics it is the foundation on which numerous theories are built, e.g. imaginary geometry
[

,
80
,
81
]

[

,

[

The IMF on the other hand arises as the scaling limit of the discrete magnetisation field of the celebrated Ising model at its critical point
[

,
31
,
25
]
.

To be more precise, the GFF
h
with zero boundary conditions in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
is a random Gaussian field whose covariance kernel is the Green’s function
G_{D}
of the Brownian motion in
D
killed upon hitting the boundary
\partial D
, and as such is a conformally invariant object (see
[
21
]
for an exhaustive account on the GFF).
Since
G_{D}
diverges on the diagonal, the field is too rough to be made sense of as a random function and must be considered only as a random Schwartz distribution acting on test functions:
for smooth functions
f,g
with support in
D
, the values of

tested against

and

(h,f)
and

\mathbb{E}[(h,f)(h,g)]=\int_{D}\int_{D}G_{D}(x,y)f(x)g(y)\,dx\,dy.

In beautiful works of Aru, Sepúlveda and Werner
[
11
,
12
]
, that will be crucial for our considerations, it was shown that the GFF has a rich structure of built-in geometric objects called
two-valued sets
. For

, where
\lambda=\pi/2

is (under the normalisation we use in this article) the

of the GFF defined in
[
94
]
, their boundaries

are countable collections of non-crossing fractal-like loops in
D
, which may be heuristically thought of as the
level lines of the field at values
-a
and
b
, despite the fact that the field itself is not a function.

The main novelty of our work is to show that two-valued sets with particular values of
a
and
b
give rise to the IMF through a geometric representation, decomposing the field into a signed sum of the “area” measures of the two-valued sets, where the signs are given by independent coin tosses. In fact, we are able to couple
four
(two pairs of two independent) IMFs with a single instance of a GFF.
As a direct consequence the IMFs are measurable functions of the GFF and the signs. Moreover, we prove that two-valued sets with different values of
a
and
b
define a one-parameter family of continuum fields associated with the GFF, and conjecture them to be the continuum magnetisation fields of critical
Ashkin–Teller
models.

We achieve our main result by taking the scaling limit of a
new
representation of the Ising model. This representation is analogous to the Edwards–Sokal coupling
[

,
44
]
between the Ising model and the FK-Ising model (also referred to as the Fortuin-Kasteleyn random cluster model with cluster weight
q=2
), but the role of the latter is played by a different percolation model defined in terms of the double random current (DRC) model. This is a classical graphical representation of the (products of) Ising correlation functions introduced by Griffiths, Hurst and Sherman
[
56
]
, and developed by Aizenman, Duminil-Copin and others
with great success in the study of both planar and higher-dimensional Ising (and related) models
[

,
2
,
4
,
42
,
5
,
6
,
38
]
.
It was recently shown by Duminil-Copin, Qian and the third author
[
38
,
37
]
that the scaling limit of a critical DRC is given by a specific iteration of two-valued sets.
Our results build on this and may be seen as an extension, which adds the GFF into the picture, of the link developed by Camia, Garban and Newman
[
26
,
24
]
between the IMF and planar random geometry beyond the conformal loop ensembles with dual parameters
\kappa=3
and
\kappa=16/3
.

We highlight that our representation of the IMF in terms of the GFF is
not
a local one like e.g. the
vertex operators
in the free-boson conformal field theory of the GFF.
Indeed, the
bosonisation
framework in physics describes the Ising magnetisation field as a
twist field
rather than a vertex operator of the GFF
[
104
,
76
,
33
]

These two classes of fields belong to two different sectors in the
orbifold CFT
description of bosonisation – the twisted and untwisted one correspondingly.
Unlike the vertex operators, the twist fields are not “local functions” of the GFF, and their construction proceeds via a change in the topology of the domain on which the GFF lives by considering a double cover branching around the points of insertion of the twist fields
[
33

In discrete terms, twist fields are therefore more like disorder operators in the Kadanoff-Ceva sense

that change the state-space of the discrete model, whereas the

What we achieve in this work is a natural probabilistic coupling of the twist fields in the orbifold CFT, realised as true random distributions, together with the GFF itself and its vertex operators (that include the scaling limit of the critical XOR-Ising model

,
8
]

During the preparation of this article, we learned that the statement of our main Theorem

was also conjectured by Aru and Lupu who provided evidence for it at the level of matching two-point functions.
They build on the twist field description of the magnetisation field (see Section

) and the recent interpretation of such fields through topological events in the GFF
[

We refer to their upcoming work
[

]
for details.

1.1
Main results

Let us start by recalling the definition of the Ising model, whose history goes back more than a century to the foundational work of Lenz
[
70
]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The Ising model with coupling constant
J\geqslant 0
and free boundary conditions, is a probability measure on spin configurations
\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{V}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,J}(\sigma)\propto\prod_{uv\in E}\exp\big(J\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\big).

(1.1)

The model with
+
boundary conditions is defined by distinguishing a particular subset of the vertices on which
\sigma
is required to be
+1
.

To define the IMF in a simply connected domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
, one starts by considering a sequence of discrete domain approximations
D_{\delta}\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Here, we abuse notation and write
D_{\delta}
also for the vertex-set of these graphs.
We consider the Ising model
\sigma_{\delta}
on
D_{\delta}
at the critical
[
85
]
value of the coupling constants

J_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\sqrt{2}),

(1.2)

with either free boundary conditions or
+
boundary conditions imposed on the boundary vertices of
D_{\delta}
.
Each spin configuration
\sigma=\sigma_{\delta}\in\{\pm 1\}^{D_{\delta}}
can be thought of as a field (generalised function) acting on test functions
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
by defining

\displaystyle(\sigma,f):=\delta^{2}\sum_{v\in D_{\delta}}f(v)\sigma_{v},

(1.3)

where the vertices of
D_{\delta}
are identified with the corresponding points in the plane. The breakthrough result of Chelkak, Hongler and Izyruov
[
31
]
showed that the scaling limit
\delta\to 0
of spin correlation functions, under free or
+
boundary conditions, exists and is conformally covariant. In turn, it was shown in work of Camia, Garban and Newman
[
26
]
that this readily implies that the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-1/8}\sigma_{\delta}=:\Phi_{D}

(1.4)

exist in law, when considered in proper spaces of generalised functions (and under very mild regularity assumptions on
\partial D
). The limit above is taken as the definition of the IMF.

We highlight that the methods in this paper do not rely on the existence of the scaling limit of the discrete IMF, but rather provide a new proof, which solely requires the convergence of the one-point function of the Ising model. This was already the case in the geometric construction of
[
26
, Section 2]
, and indeed our approach is similar in spirit, yet with some key differences we discuss in-depth in Section
4.1
.

Statement of the continuum couplings.
We start by stating the existence of a coupling between one Gaussian free field (GFF) and four critical Ising magnetisation fields (IMFs). It is important to note that there is no canonical coupling of all four IMFs at once: there is always an arbitrary choice in how the signs for the
+
and free boundary condition fields depend on each other — see Remark
1.3
.

Theorem 1.1

(Four IMFs from one GFF and independent coin tosses)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a Jordan domain. Let
h
be a GFF in
D
with zero boundary conditions,
and let
\xi=(\xi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
be i.i.d. symmetric
\{\pm 1\}
-valued random variables.
Then, there exist (explicit) deterministic measurable functions
\Phi^{+}
,
\tilde{\Phi}^{+}
,
\Phi^{\textnormal{f}}
,
\tilde{\Phi}^{\textnormal{f}}
such that

•

\Phi^{+}(h,\xi)
and
\tilde{\Phi}^{+}(h,\xi)
have the law of two
independent
IMFs with
+
boundary conditions,

•

\Phi^{\textnormal{f}}(h,\xi)
and
\tilde{\Phi}^{\textnormal{f}}(h,\xi)
have the law of two
independent
IMFs with free boundary conditions.

As already introduced, the explicit functions in Theorem
1.1
are given by sums of signed measures, each of which is a measurable function of the GFF. This is precisely the content of the next statement. For the sake of exposition, we explain how to sample the measures and their supports thereafter.

Theorem 1.2

(Representation of IMFs via two-valued sets of the GFF)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
,
h
and
(\xi^{+})_{k\geqslant 1}
,
(\xi^{\textnormal{f}})_{k\geqslant 1}
be as above. There exist signs
(\tau_{k}^{+})_{k\geqslant 1}
,
(\tau_{k}^{\textnormal{f}})_{k\geqslant 1}
and measures
(\mu_{k}^{+})_{k\geqslant 0}
,
(\mu^{\textnormal{f}}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that

\Phi^{+}=\mu_{0}^{+}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi^{+}_{k}\mu_{k}^{+},\quad\tilde{\Phi}^{+}=\mu_{0}^{+}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi^{+}_{k}\tau_{k}^{+}\mu_{k}^{+},\quad\Phi^{\textnormal{f}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi_{k}^{\textnormal{f}}\mu_{k}^{\textnormal{f}},\quad\tilde{\Phi}^{\textnormal{f}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi_{k}^{\textnormal{f}}\tau_{k}^{\textnormal{f}}\mu_{k}^{\textnormal{f}},

(1.5)

are the IMFs in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover,

•

The convergence of the sums holds a.s. in the Sobolev space
H^{s}_{\textrm{loc}}(D)
for any
s<-1
.

•

The ordering of the sum is by decreasing diameter of the supports
(\mathcal{C}_{k}^{+})_{k\geqslant 0}
,
(\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{f}}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
of the respective measures.

•

The measures are a measurable function of their supports, which in turn are a measurable function of
h
.

•

The signs
(\tau_{k}^{+})_{k\geqslant 1}
,
(\tau_{k}^{\textnormal{f}})_{k\geqslant 1}
are measurable functions of
h
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the statement of Theorem
1.2
, we have chosen to use different coin tosses for different boundary conditions. This is an arbitrary choice, albeit natural, and the statement remains valid also when choosing e.g. the same coin tosses.

Remark 1.4
.

Decompositions of the form (

Statement of the discrete couplings and convergence.
While all the statements above concern only objects in the continuum, their proofs almost exclusively rely on their discrete counterparts. The heart of the coupling is the observation in Theorem
1.9
of a new representation of the Ising model, of which we provide two different proofs in Section
2.1
.

Before stating it precisely, let us introduce some notation. Given a graph
G
, we write
V(G)
and
E(G)
for the sets of its vertices and edges respectively. For a subset
S\subset V(G)
, we denote by
E(S)
the edges with both endpoints in
S
. When
G
is a finite connected planar graph embedded in the plane, we let
G^{*}
be its (strong) dual graph and
G^{\dagger}
its
weak
dual graph, i.e. its dual graph with the vertex corresponding to the outer face removed.
We also denote by
\partial G
the set of vertices of
G
lying on its unbounded face.

Definition 1.7

(Coin tosses)
.

For any percolation configuration
\omega\subset E(G)
, we denote by

\mathscr{S}(\omega)

the assignment of independent symmetric
\{\pm 1\}
-valued spins to each cluster (i.e. connected component) of
\omega
.
This gives rise to a spin configuration on
V(G)
given by the sign of the cluster to which the vertex belongs. We write
\mathscr{S}^{+}(\omega)
for the same procedure, with the exception that each cluster intersecting
{\partial G}
is always assigned the spin
+1
.

Definition 1.8
.

Given
\eta\subset E(G)
, we let
\eta^{*}\subset E(G^{*})
be its
dual complement
defined by

e\in\eta\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad e^{\dagger}\notin\eta^{*},

where
e^{\dagger}
is the dual edge of
e
. Note that
\eta\supset E({\partial G})
if and only if
\eta^{*}\subset E(G^{\dagger})
.

Let

\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\omega=(\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}^{\dagger})^{*}.

Throughout the paper we work with an extension of the so-called

introduced in

Each of the two configurations defines a spin model

\tau=\mathscr{S}^{+}(\mathbf{{n}})\quad\text{and}\quad\tau^{\dagger}=\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{{n}}^{\dagger}),

with the law of a XOR-Ising model (i.e. with the law of the product of two independent Ising models). In turn, this defines a height function

by imposing zero boundary conditions and defining its gradient to be

\displaystyle H(u^{\dagger})-H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\tau(u)\tau^{\dagger}(u^{\dagger}),

(1.9)

for

and

and use Theorem

to define two Ising models

\sigma=\mathscr{S}^{+}(\omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma^{\dagger}=\mathscr{S}(\omega^{\dagger}),

on
G
with

boundary conditions and on
G^{\dagger}
with free boundary conditions respectively.
Finally, one can recover two more Ising fields simply by setting

\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma\tau\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\sigma}^{\dagger}=\sigma^{\dagger}\tau^{\dagger},

which must be independent of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\dagger}
, respectively. This augmentation of the master coupling with Ising spins is the starting point of our considerations.

The following statement extends the convergence result in

Theorem 1.10

(Joint scaling limit at criticality)
Let

be a Jordan domain with discrete domain approximation

\big(h_{\delta},\ \omega_{\delta},\ \omega^{\dagger}_{\delta},\ \delta^{-1/8}\sigma_{\delta},\ \delta^{-1/8}\tilde{\sigma}_{\delta},\ \delta^{-1/8}\sigma_{\delta}^{\dagger},\ \delta^{-1/8}\tilde{\sigma}_{\delta}^{\dagger}\big)\overset{(d)}{\longrightarrow}\big(\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}h,\ \omega,\ \omega^{\dagger},\ \Phi,\ \tilde{\Phi},\ \Phi^{\dagger},\ \tilde{\Phi}^{\dagger}\big),

where the discrete spin configurations
\sigma_{\delta},\tilde{\sigma}_{\delta},\sigma_{\delta}^{\dagger},\tilde{\sigma}_{\delta}^{\dagger}
are thought of as random distributions as in (

1.2
Further discussion and context

The purpose of this section is two-fold. First, we introduce the Ashkin-Teller model and explain how our main results fit into a much broader picture, which motivates all of the conjectures we pose in Section
1.3
. Secondly, we review some of the literature around previous approaches to the IMF
[
26
,
24
]
and rigorous mathematical descriptions of bosonisation.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The Ashkin–Teller (AT) model
[
13
]
with coupling constants
J\geqslant 0,\ U\in\mathbb{R}
, and free/free boundary conditions,
is a probability measure on pairs of spin configurations
(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma})\in\{-1,1\}^{V}\times\{-1,1\}^{V}

given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G,J,U}(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma})\propto\prod_{uv\in E}\exp\big(J(\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}+\tilde{\sigma}_{u}\tilde{\sigma}_{v})+U\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\tilde{\sigma}_{u}\tilde{\sigma}_{v}\big).

(1.10)

The measure under
+/+
boundary conditions is defined as for the Ising model, namely by distinguishing a particular subset of the vertices on which
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are both required to be
+1
. The single-spin configurations
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are referred to as the
magnetisation
spins, while the (coordinate-wise) product-spin configuration

\tau=\sigma\tilde{\sigma}
is referred to as the
polarisation
spin. On the
free fermion line

U=0
the spins
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
become two independent Ising models with coupling constant
J
, and the polarisation spin is traditionally called the
XOR-Ising model
.

The
critical line

[
15
]
of the AT model is defined by

\displaystyle\sinh(2J)=e^{-2U},\qquad J\geqslant U.

(1.11)

It has been predicted in the physics literature
[
33
,
15
,
84
]
that the model possesses explicit critical exponents that vary continuously on the critical line, and
a conformally covariant scaling limit as
\delta\to 0
. In particular, given a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
and its discrete domain approximation
D_{\delta}\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the following limits are predicted to exist and be conformally covariant:

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\ \delta^{-1/8}\ \langle\sigma_{x_{\delta}}\rangle^{+}_{D_{\delta},U}=:\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{+}_{D,U}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\lim_{\delta\to 0}\ \delta^{-1/(2g)}\ \langle\tau_{x_{\delta}}\rangle^{+}_{D_{\delta},U}=:\langle\tau_{x}\rangle^{+}_{D,U},\

(1.12)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle^{+}_{D_{\delta},U}
denotes the expectation with respect to the critical model
\mathbb{P}_{D_{\delta},J_{c}(U),U}^{+}

with
+/+
boundary conditions and
x_{\delta}\in D_{\delta}
is at distance
O(\delta)
from some fixed
x\in D
. The critical exponent of the polarisation is given by

\displaystyle g:=\frac{8}{\pi}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}(1+\exp(4U))^{1/2}\right)\in(4/3,4].

(1.13)

Interestingly, the critical exponent of the magnetisation field
\sigma
is constant and equal
1/8
, whereas the polarisation exponent.
As already discussed, for
U=0
, owing to its strong form of solvability, the convergence above was rigorously proved in recent breakthrough work of Chelkak, Hongler and Izyurov
[
31
]
. Even though the AT model on the critical line is known to be tightly related to the
six-vertex model
which is also exactly solvable
[
15
]
(though in a weaker sense), the statements in (
1.12
) outside the critical free fermion point are still beyond the reach of rigorous proofs.

An important feature of our predictions for the AT model is a conceptual explanation of the value
1/8
on the whole critical line.
Namely, we relate it to the fact that the Hausdorff dimension of the two-valued set
\mathbb{A}_{-2\lambda,2\lambda}
is equal to
2-1/8

[
93
,
83
,
92
]
. This set appears in our conjectural geometric representation of the continuum AT fields
irrespectively of the point on the critical line
. Moreover, the variation of the predicted exponent
g
along the critical line corresponds to the fact that the two-valued sets with height gap
2\sqrt{2}
in
(+)
and
(\text{free})
are replaced by two-valued sets with height gap
2\sqrt{g}
, which have dimension
2-1/(2g)

[
92
]
. We refer to Conjectures
1.2
-
1.3
. for the precise statements.

Based on (
1.12
), and viewing the discrete configurations as distributions as in (
1.3
), one expects that the limits

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-1/8}\sigma_{\delta}=:\Phi_{D},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-1/(2g)}\tau_{\delta}=:\uptau_{D}

(1.14)

exist in law, again when considered in proper spaces of generalised functions. Beyond the case
U=0
, one of our contributions is an explicit conjecture describing the field
\Phi_{D}
in terms of the appropriate two-valued sets. The identification of
\uptau_{D}
with a vertex operator of the GFF (or
imaginary multiplicative chaos
in the mathematics terminology
[
60
]
) follows directly from bosonisation and has been non-rigorously established in the physics literature
[
15
,
84
,
61
]
. We restate this connection in Conjecture
1.1
, which has been already established for the Ising model
[
60
]
.

In
[
26
,
28
,
24
]
the Edwards–Sokal coupling between the Ising model and the FK-Ising model was employed to study the IMF from a geometric point of view. The coupling states that to sample the Ising model on
G
, one can first sample a configuration
\omega^{\textup{FK}}\subset E(G)
of the FK-Ising model and toss coins on each cluster. The critical FK-Ising model on the square lattice has been extensively studied in the last fifteen years. Thanks to its desirable features like exact solvability and positive association, a number of
breakthroughs were achieved including a complete RSW theory
[
36
]
, and proofs of conformally invariant scaling limits
[
97
,
64
]
.
In particular it was shown that the collection of all boundaries of the clusters on
D_{\delta}
converges to the conformal loop ensemble CLE
16/3

[
64
]
in
D
, which in turn allowed for a fully geometric description of the continuum Ising field
[
26
,
24
]
. We note that the dimension of the gasket of CLE
16/3
is also known to be
2-1/8

[
82
]
, thus the critical exponent of the magnetisation provides a nice conceptual explanation as for why this must equal the dimension of the carpet of
\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
.

The path to achieve our main results is indeed similar to
[
26
,
24
]
, but the study of the dual complement
\omega
of the DRC differs in many aspects, as will become clear. It may even come as a surprise that two different laws on a percolation model can satisfy the Edwards–Sokal property. However, an observation to the same effect in a general planar setup can be found already in the work of the third author
[
71
, Theorem 3.2]
and was explicitly stated in the master thesis of
Ivan Hejný
[
58
]
. In hindsight, our approach is closely related to that in
[
55
]
, and as mentioned therein it fits the framework of FK-repersentations
of the Ashkin–Teller model introduced by Pfister and Velenik
[
88
]
(though it falls outside the range of couplings constants considered there). We elaborate further on this point at the beginning of Section
1.3
.

One can think of our coupling in the case of planar graphs as an enhancement of the bosonisation of the Ising model originating in the work of Zuber and Itzykson
[
104
]

with mathematical treatments among others in
[
35
,
23
,
39
]
. In the continuum, this theory establishes exact identities between squares of Ising correlation functions (i.e. XOR-Ising correlation functions) and correlations of vertex operators of the GFF. Building on
[
31
,
32
]
, the full picture for the continuum correlations has been completed in
[
16
]
. In the discrete this is mirrored via a combinatorial
relation between
two independent
copies of the Ising model and a
dimer model
on a
bipartite
decoration of the original graph
[
35
,
23
,
39
]
.
Such dimer models can be thought of as models of random surfaces via their associated
height function
, where the XOR-Ising field is given by the parity of said function.
Since the groundbreaking work of Kenyon
[
65
]
, dimer height functions are known to converge to the GFF in the scaling limit. Intriguingly, the first dimer representation of the Ising model goes back to the work of Fisher
[
45
]
, who mapped a
single
copy of the Ising model to a dimer model on a decorated graph.
However, the decoration is
nonbipartite
and as such does not correspond to a height function and hence does not fit in the framework of bosonisation.
Moreover, none of the aforementioned works makes a prediction about how a single Ising model could fit into the coupling between the XOR-Ising and the height function.
This is amended by our contributions.

In terms of convergence of (dimer) height functions, of particular interest for the purposes of this paper is the convergence of the critical XOR-Ising height function to the GFF
[
38
]
, which justifies the name bosonisation used in reference to the combinatorial map between two Ising models and a dimer model. The work of Duminil-Copin and the third author
[
39
]
already made an implicit step in this direction by jointly coupling the DRC together with the
XOR-Ising model and the associated height function. This was in turn inspired by the
alternating-flow
representation of the DRC model introduced in
[
71
]
.
In
[
38
]
the coupling from
[
39
]
was extended to the master coupling (which was briefly sketched in the previous section) of both primal and dual double random currents, XOR-Ising models and height functions. Moreover, it was realised in
[
38
]
that the DRC model itself (i.e. not its dual complement) is coupled with the XOR-Ising model via the Edwards–Sokal procedure.
This is in contrast to the FK-Ising model whose dual complement is also an FK-Ising model, and hence both are coupled with a single Ising model An extension to the AT model (including the
AT random currents
defined in
[
72
]
, the AT polarisation fields and the associated height functions) was simultaneously given in
[
73
]
.

It was shown by Duminil-Copin, Qian and the third author
[
38
,
37
]
that the critical master coupling has a joint scaling limit in which the height function converges to a GFF, and where the outer and inner boundaries of the
clusters of the DRC converge to certain loops arising from two-valued sets of that GFF – see Theorem
1.14
. We stress again that the couplings introduced in this paper take the setup of
[
38
,
37
]
as a starting point, and proceed by adding the magnetisation fields as explain in Theorem
1.9
. It is precisely the duality relation in this statement that allows us identify the scaling limit of the clusters of our percolation model by invoking the results of
[
38
,
37
]
.

Lastly, we mention another approach to study planar spin systems that goes back to the work of Peierls
[
87
]
, who interpreted a spin configuration by the collection of interfaces that separate the
+1
and
-1
spins. For the critical Ising model with so-called
Dobrushin
boundary conditions, a single interface connecting two boundary points was shown to converge to Schramm–Loewner evolution SLE
3

[
30
]
, whereas for
+
boundary conditions the full collection of loops converges to CLE
3

[
19
]
.
The scaling limits of the analogous interfaces for the XOR-Ising model have been predicted by Wilson
[
102
]
, and should be described by the loops of certain two-valued sets, with different boundary values than those considered until now.
This is supported by the fact that in the discrete bosonisation picture the interfaces of the XOR-Ising model are actually level lines of the discrete height function
[
35
,
23
]
.

We recall that the conformal loop ensembles CLE
κ
for
\kappa\in(8/3,8)
are countable collections of random fractal non-self crossing loops, which are uniquely identified by conformal invariance and a type of spatial Markov property
[
95
]
. Similarly, we already know that the loops of a two-valued set possess also a type of Markov property, now with respect to the underlying GFF. Conformal invariance and Markov property should usually be a feature of scaling limits of curves in critical models, and hence these two families yield natural candidates for the limits of the (XOR-)Ising interfaces.

On the other hand, the interfaces of the
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
configurations in the critical AT model away from the free fermion point do not possess any natural spatial Markov property even in the discrete. Indeed conditioning on the value of one spin along a circuit, still allows for information to flow from the outside of the circuit to the inside
through the interaction with the other spin. For this reason there is no prediction on the scaling limit of these interfaces.
We fill this gap by conjecturing (see Conjecture
1.4
) that the continuum curves corresponding to critical AT magnetisation interfaces are given by a coin tossing procedure on two valued-sets,
and then taking the interfaces between the two spin values. This is an analogue of the CLE percolation procedure described in
[
78
]
and we plan to explore this direction in a future work.

1.3
Conjectures for the Ashkin–Teller model

Based on the recent works of the third author
[
71
,
72
,
73
]
, it turns out that a completely analogous discrete coupling exists for the general Ashkin–Teller model (see Section
2.3
for the proofs).
Namely, the counterpart of the double random current on
G
is the
AT current

\mathbf{{n}}=(\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}},\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}})
defined in
[
72
]
, whose law is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\textnormal{AT}}(\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}})\propto 2^{k(\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}})}x^{|\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}|}y^{|\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}|},

(1.15)

where
k(\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}})
is the number of clusters of
\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}=\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}\cup\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}
,
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
and
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}
are disjoint and
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
is constrained to have even degree around every vertex in
V(G)
(i.e.
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
is an
even
subgraph of
G
).
When compared to (
1.10
), the parameters satisfy

x=e^{2U}\sinh(2J),\quad\textnormal{and}\quad y=e^{2U}\cosh(2J)-1.

As was shown in
[
71
]
for
U=0
, the current
\mathbf{{n}}
is the projection of the sourceless DRC (defined as a function from
E(G)
to
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, see Definition
2.2
) onto the sets of edges where it takes odd and even positive values, resulting in
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
and
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}
respectively.
With each such current one can associate a height function
h
, called the
nesting field
, which can be defined in the same way as the one for the noninteracting case introduced by Duminil-Copin and the third author in
[
39
]
. It is an integer-valued function defined on the faces of the graph that changes values by
\pm 1
between neighbouring faces only when crossing an edge of
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
, and moreover the
increments across two edges are independent for edges in different clusters of
\mathbf{{n}}
. For a precise definition we refer to
[
39
]
, and for an alternative but equivalent one to (
1.9
) we refer to Section
2.2
and Section
2.3
.

A coupling between primal and dual AT currents, the corresponding primal and dual nesting fields and the two polarisation fields was given in
[
73
]
.
The extension to include the magnetisation fields follows from
[
55
, Proposition 8.1]
for the
self-dual model on the square lattice, and from
[
72
, Proposition 13]
in full generality of planar graphs and coupling constants.
As a result, all identities in law (in particular various spatial Markov properties that are crucial for the identification of scaling limits) at the discrete level described in this article for the case
U=0
hold also for the corresponding objects in general AT models. The uncovering of this rich structure leads to new directions in the further study of the AT model described below. The proofs of precise statements can be either found in
[
72
,
73
]

or are straightforward generalisations of formulas presented here.

A particular feature of this coupling that instantiates the phenomenon of bosonisation is that the polarisation field is the complex exponential of the dual nesting field. This will be mirrored in one of the conjectures below.
Moreover, the joint low of the primal and dual height function is known to be the same as that of the height function in the associated
six-vertex model
(see e.g.
[
55
,
73
]
).
Together with the physics prediction that the latter
converges to an explicit constant multiple of the Gaussian free field
[
15
,
84
,
61
]
,
it allows to make the following precise conjectures on the critical behaviour of the AT model.

The first one is a reiteration of the convergence of the height function and the bosonisation relation for the polarisation field.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a Jordan domain with discrete domain approximation
D_{\delta}\subset\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. At each point of the critical line (
1.11
), as
\delta\to 0
,
the nesting field of the AT current on

under the
+/+
or free/free boundary conditions converges to

\frac{1}{2\lambda\sqrt{g}}\ h=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{g}}\ h,

where

is a GFF with zero boundary conditions in
D
, and
g
is defined in (
1.13
).
Moreover, the polarisation fields under
+/+
and free/free boundary conditions, respectively, converge to

\cos\big(h/\sqrt{g}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\sin\big(h/\sqrt{g}\big),

corresponding to the
imaginary Gaussian multiplicative chaos
with parameter
\alpha(g)=1/\sqrt{g}\in[1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)\subset[0,\sqrt{2}].

The second (and the first novel) conjecture is a direct analog of the main result of
[
38
,
37
]
..

Conjecture 1.2
.

The almost verbatim analog of Theorem
1.14
holds for the Ashkin–Teller currents defined by (
1.15
) at each point of the critical line (
1.11
), the only difference being that every explicit occurrence of
\sqrt{2}
has to be replaced with
\sqrt{g}
defined in (
1.13
). In particular, the appearance of
\mathbb{A}_{-2\lambda,2\lambda}\equiv\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
in the scaling limit is universal on this line, and the remaining

\mathbb{A}_{-2\lambda,(2\sqrt{g}-2)\lambda},\ \mathbb{A}_{(2-2\sqrt{g})\lambda,2\lambda}\ \ \text{or}\ \ \mathbb{A}_{-\sqrt{g}\lambda,\sqrt{g}\lambda}.

We reiterate that the convergence of the outermost boundaries of the clusters to a CLE
4
is a geometric analog (that actually carries much more information)
of the classical conjecture that the exponent of the magnetisation fields are constant and equal to
1/8
on the entire critical line (see e.g.
[
15
]
).

Remark 1.12
.

Recall that at the endpoint
J=U
of the critical curve we have
g=4
. In this case, all sets in the iteration are actually CLE
4
.
This is consistent with the fact that the law of the AT becomes that of the 4-state Potts model, and the associated current
\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}
becomes the critical FK random cluster model with cluster weight

. The former follows simply from the inspection of the Hamiltonian. The latter can be seen by noticing that the two weights

and

, which in turn implies that when one forgets which edges of
\mathbf{{n}}
are in
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
and which in
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}
, an additional combinatorial factor
of

appears due to the total number of even subgraphs of
\mathbf{{n}}
(see e.g.
[

). In particular, this yields a total weight proportional to

, which is the critical FK(4) random cluster model weight

[

that the outer and inner boundaries of the clusters of the critical FK(4) model converge in the scaling limit to CLE

’s.

The next conjecture is a generalisation of our main results, namely Theorems

and

, and predicts a geometric decomposition of the continuum Ashkin-Teller magnetisation fields.

Conjecture 1.3
.

The statement of Theorem
1.2
under the almost verbatim analogue of the iterations

and
(\text{free})
holds for the Ashkin–Teller magnetisation field defined by (

) at each point of the critical line (

has to be again replaced with

).

We believe that the methods we apply in this paper are robust, so that Conjecture

Part of the statement above is that the relevant fields are well defined in the continuum, which we in fact prove.

Proposition 1.13
.

The analogous series to those in (
1.5
), but with
\sqrt{2}
replaced with
\sqrt{g}
in the definition of the relevant two-valued sets, converge a.s. and in

for

(or, equivalently,

was independently discovered by Aru and Lupu, and will appear in their upcoming work

As a final conjecture, we describe a continuum percolation procedure, in the spirit of

Conjecture 1.4
.

Consider the clusters of the decomposition described in Conjecture
1.1
under
+/+
boundary conditions. Toss a fair coin for each cluster to assign a value
+1
or
-1
, except for the boundary cluster which is assigned

. If two clusters with the value

(resp.

1.4
About the proof

Let us now discuss all the inputs needed in the proofs of our main results above. We do so in the hope of identifying the minimal set of pre-existing results needed to generalise our arguments to Ashkin-Teller models.

The crucial first input in our proof is the identification of the continuum clusters as found in
[
38
,

2.1
Definition and main properties

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite connected graph.
For any subset of vertices
S\subset V(G)
, we denote by
E(S)
those edges with both endpoints contained in
S
.
We consider a family of ferromagnetic coupling constants
J:E(G)\to[0,\infty)
.

We also fix
V^{+}\subset V(G)
to be a (possibly empty) connected subset of the vertices and set
E^{+}=E(V^{+})
. These will be used to impose
+
boundary conditions on
V^{+}
for spin configurations and wired boundary conditions on
E^{+}
for percolation configurations.

Below we will often assume that
G
is planar and embedded in the plane, and we will write
{\partial G}
for the set of vertices of
G
incident to the outer face.
We then denote by
G^{*}
the dual graph of
G
and by
G^{\dagger}
the weak dual of
G
, that is the (strong) dual graph with the vertex corresponding to the outer face of
G
removed.
For those statements which rely on planarity, we will
additionally assume
that
V^{+}
is either empty or is equal to
{\partial G}
.

Given any coupling constants
J
there is a unique dual family of coupling constants
J^{\dagger}
on
G^{\dagger}
which are defined through the relation

\tanh(J^{\dagger}_{(uv)^{\dagger}})=\exp(-2J_{uv}),

(2.1)

for every
uv\in E(G)
, where
(uv)^{\dagger}
is the dual edge of
uv
.
We note that the mapping
J\mapsto J^{\dagger}
is an involution and the unique fixed point gives the self-dual coupling constants as in (
1.2
), which are critical on the square lattice.

Given
A\subset V(G)
, we define
\Sigma^{A}
to be the set of spin configurations
\sigma:V\mapsto\left\{\pm 1\right\}
which satisfy
\sigma_{v}=+1
for all
v\in A
.

Definition 2.1

(Ising model)
.

The
Ising model
with
+
boundary conditions on
V^{+}
is the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}=\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G,J}
on spin configurations
\sigma:V(G)\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
given by

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}(\sigma)\propto\mathbf{1}(\sigma\in\Sigma^{V^{+}})\exp\Big(\sum_{uv\in E(G)}J_{uv}\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\Big).

When the set
V^{+}
is empty, we instead say that the model has
free
boundary conditions and we write
\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{G}
.

We immediately extend the measure to include a second independent and identically distributed Ising model
\tilde{\sigma}
,

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma})\propto\mathbf{1}(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma}\in\Sigma^{V^{+}})\exp\Big(\sum_{uv\in E}J_{uv}(\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}+\tilde{\sigma}_{u}\tilde{\sigma}_{v})\Big).

(2.2)

This is the non-interacting version of the Ashkin–Teller model with
U=0
, and the product spin will be denoted by

\tau=\sigma\tilde{\sigma}.

The marginal of
\tau
under
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{G}
) is referred to as the
XOR-Ising model
with
+/+
(resp. free/free) boundary conditions.

Definition 2.2

(Double Random Current)
.

A current is a map
\mathbf{{n}}:E(G)\mapsto\left\{0,1,2,\ldots\right\}
. We denote by
\partial\mathbf{{n}}\subseteq V(G)
the set of its

,

\partial\mathbf{{n}}=\left\{u\in V(G)\setminus V^{+}\ \mid\ \textstyle\sum_{v:\ uv\in E(G)}\mathbf{{n}}_{uv}\text{ is odd}\right\}.

(2.3)

For
A\subseteq V(G)
, we write
\Omega_{A}
for the set of all currents with sources
A
.
We introduce a weight
w=w_{J}
on currents by setting

w(\mathbf{{n}})=\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\frac{J_{uv}^{\mathbf{{n}}_{uv}}}{\mathbf{{n}}_{uv}!}.

(2.4)

Whenever
A,B\subseteq V(G)
are of even size, the
double random current (DRC)
probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{A,B}_{G}=\mathbb{P}^{A,B}_{G,J}
with sources
A,B
is defined to be the law of
\mathbf{{n}}_{1}+\mathbf{{n}}_{2}
, where
\mathbf{{n}}_{1}
and
\mathbf{{n}}_{2}
are independently sampled proportional to the weight
w_{J}
from
\Omega_{A}
and
\Omega_{B}
respectively.

The case of main importance for this article is when
A=B=\emptyset
, and if so we say that the currents are
sourceless
.
The model is said to have free boundary conditions when
V^{+}
is empty, otherwise it is said to have wired boundary conditions.
The
trace

\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}
of
\mathbf{{n}}
is a percolation configuration given by

\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}=\left\{uv\in E(G)\mid\mathbf{{n}}_{uv}>0\right\}.

We define
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{odd}}
and
\mathbf{{n}}_{\textrm{even}}
as the subsets of
\widehat{\mathbf{{n}}}
on which
\mathbf{{n}}
takes odd and even values respectively.

This definition for random currents with wired boundary conditions is not the most standard but it is natural. The set of sources (
2.3
) arises from expanding in the usual manner the partition function of the Ising model with
+
boundary conditions on

. The constraint

ensures that

\textstyle\sum_{u\in V^{+}}\textstyle\sum_{v:\ uv\in E(G)}\mathbf{{n}}_{uv}\ \text{ is even,}

so that the current is completely sourceless when considered on the graph with the edges of
E^{+}
contracted and all vertices of
V^{+}
merged into one.

Given a spin configuration
\sigma:V(G)\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
, we write

\xi^{\pm}(\sigma)=\left\{uv\in E(G)\mid\sigma_{u}=\sigma_{v}=\pm 1\right\}

and we define
\xi(\sigma)=\xi^{+}(\sigma)\cup\xi^{-}(\sigma)

When
G
is planar, the dual complement of

is the

\sigma

\partial\sigma=\xi(\sigma)^{*}=\left\{(uv)^{*}\in E(G^{*})\mid\sigma_{u}\neq\sigma_{v}\right\}.

(2.5)

Before proceeding, we briefly recall the definition of the FK-Ising model and refer to
[
57

for a full account. We define

\omega^{\text{FK}}=\xi(\sigma)\cap\eta^{\prime},

where

and
\eta^{\prime}

As above, we will write

2.2

In this section we fix

Properties of the representation

3.1
Correlation inequalities

In this section, we will study the FKG properties of the measure
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}
in Definition
2.19
. A recurring theme of our argument is the issue that the law of
\omega
is not known to satisfy the FKG inequality. In fact, it is generally expected that the double random current (equivalently
\omega
) does not satisfy the FKG inequality, although ruling it out completely is still seemingly open
[
67
]
.

The results of this section may be seen as one approach to overcome this hurdle. The first result recovers a specific type of FKG inequality for the decomposition
(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})
. It originates from the beautiful work of
[
54
]
. After this, we prove a technical extension so that we can apply it to the construction of the IIC in Section
3.3
. We then state and prove a simple correlation inequality which will allow us to decorrelate an increasing event from a decreasing event.

We turn first our attention to the FKG-properties of the measure
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}
.
We briefly recall that a random variable
X\sim\mu
taking values in a (finite) partially-ordered set
(\mathcal{X},\leqslant)
has the
FKG property
if for any two increasing functions
f,g:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
,

\mu\left[f(X)g(X)\right]\geqslant\mu[f(X)]\mathbb{E}[g(X)].

The only partially-ordered sets we will consider are the spaces of spin configurations
\left\{\pm 1\right\}^{V}
and bond configurations
\left\{0,1\right\}^{E}
with their natural order, and various products of these spaces. We use the notation
\tau_{S}
or
\omega^{+}_{H}
to denote the restriction
\tau|_{S}
or
\omega^{+}|_{H}
for any subset
S\subset V
or
H\subset E
.

The most important result of this section concerns the distribution of the triple
(\tau,\omega^{+},\omega^{-})
under the law
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}
. We use the notation
(\tau,\omega^{+},-\omega^{-})
to denote that we consider the triple living in the partially-ordered set

\left\{\pm 1\right\}^{V}\times\left\{0,1\right\}^{E}\times\left\{0,1\right\}^{E},

with the order
(\tau_{1},\omega^{+}_{1},\omega^{-}_{2})\leqslant(\tau_{2},\omega^{+}_{2},\omega^{-}_{2})
if and only if
\tau_{1}\leqslant\tau_{2}
,
\omega^{+}_{1}\leqslant\omega^{+}_{2}
and
\omega^{-}_{1}\geqslant\omega^{-}_{2}
.
That is, we have
reversed the order on the third component
and a function
f
is considered increasing in
(\tau,\omega^{+},-\omega^{-})
if it is increasing in both
\tau
and
\omega^{+}
but decreasing in
\omega^{-}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The coupling
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{G}
of Definition
2.19
enjoys the following stochastic properties:

1.

The triple
(\tau,\omega^{+},-\omega^{-})
has the FKG property.

2.

The boundary cluster
\mathcal{C}_{0}
of
\omega
has the FKG property.

The first property should be understood as saying that individually
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
have the FKG property but that they are
negatively correlated
with each other. This replaces the lack of a traditional FKG inequality for the union
\omega=\omega^{+}\cup\omega^{-}
.
The second property in Proposition
3.1
is an immmediate consequence of the first (since the boundary cluster
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is an increasing function of
\omega^{+}
) but is remarkably useful and worth highlighting by itself.

The result of Proposition
3.1
is contained in
[
54
]
, where it stated in terms of the six-vertex spin representation of the square lattice. There, this intricate FKG structure was key to deriving delocalisation for the six-vertex height function.
We review the proof here for completeness and because it is an essential component in what is to come. It is a consequence of three important facts:

1.

The marginal law on
\tau
has the FKG property, see Lemma
3.7
.

2.

The percolations
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
are conditionally independent given
\tau
.

3.

Given
\tau
, the conditional law of
\omega^{+}
(resp.
\omega^{-}

\tau
.

The first of these facts was proved in
[
29
, Lemma 1]
when
U\geqslant 0
. It was also proved in
[
55
]
and
[
54
]
along the entire self-dual curve of the square lattice. These later proofs only superficially use planar duality and their adaptation to a general setting is the proof we give here.
The second and third facts rely on the structure of the coupling elucidated in Proposition
2.12
. Indeed, they follow from general properties of the FK-Ising model
\phi_{G,J}
on a graph: independence across connected components of the
G
and monotonicity of the law in
J

(\tau,\omega^{+},-\omega^{-})
satisifies the FKG inequality.

We also observe that the first and third facts above imply that, under the measure
\mathbb{P}^{+}
(or trivially
\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}
), there is a stochastic domination

\omega^{+}\succeq\omega^{-}.

(3.1)

Remark 3.2
.

Applications so far have generally been restricted to

on

is monotonic if it satisfies the following condition:

1.

Let
X\sim\mu
. For each subset

, the conditional measure

\mu(X\in\cdot\mid X_{H})

is stochastically increasing in

Whenever the measure

is strictly positive

7

7

The reader should beware that various measures we consider will not always be positive due to hard-core constraints.
this condition is equivalent to another condition:

2.

Let
X\sim\mu
. For each subset

and

We refer to
[

The combination of the two conditions (1) and (2) will play the important role in our argument in Section

They should be understood as stating that it is possible to partially explore

Unforunately, we are unable to prove that the law of

Let

Remark 3.4
.

The constraint that

We now turn to a correlation lemma for
\omega
which, whilst not strictly necessary, is helpful to streamline the proof of Theorem

and may be useful more generally.
We introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.5

(Primitive events)
.

An increasing event

is primitive for
\omega
if for any
\omega\in A
and any colouring of the clusters of
\omega
there is still a monochromatic subset which satisifies

.

The definition should be interpreted that there is a single cluster ‘witnessing’ the event
A
. Most increasing events related to the standard RSW theory of planar percolation (for instance the event
A\leftrightarrow B
for two sets
A
and
B
) are seen to be primitive events. The usefulness of this definition is as follows. For any increasing primitive event
A
, if we set
A^{\zeta}=\left\{\omega^{\zeta}\in A\right\}
for each

, then there is simple inclusion

Note that for events depending only on the cluster of a single vertex

say, then is the exact decomposition

.
Overall, if
A
is any increasing primitive event, then using also (

\mathbb{P}(\omega^{+}\in A)\leqslant\mathbb{P}(\omega\in A)\leqslant 2\mathbb{P}(\omega^{+}\in A).

(3.2)

This turns out to be a very useful comparison tool when combined with the joint FKG property of
\omega^{+}
and
\omega^{-}
.

Lemma 3.6

(Anti-correlation lemma)
.

Let
A
be an increasing primitive event and
B
a decreasing event. Then,

\mathbb{P}(\omega\in A\cap B)\leqslant 2\mathbb{P}(\omega^{-}\in B)\mathbb{P}(\omega\in A).

Proof.

Using a union bound and the inclusions
A\subset A^{+}\cup A^{-}
(since

is primitive) and
B\subset B^{\pm}
(since
B
is decreasing),

\mathbb{P}(\omega\in A\cap B)\leqslant\mathbb{P}(A^{+}\cap B^{+})+\mathbb{P}(A^{-}\cap B^{-}).

We may now apply the FKG property in Proposition
3.1
along with the stochastic domination
\omega^{+}\succeq\omega^{-}
to deduce the result.

The marginal

Proof.

where, for any spin

As can be seen from Lemma

\nu(\tau)\propto\mathbf{1}(\tau\in\Sigma^{V^{+}})\times y(\tau)\times Z_{G}(x^{+}_{\tau})\times Z_{G}(x_{\tau}^{-}).

where

\displaystyle y_{uv}=\exp(-2(J_{uv}+U_{uv}))

x_{\tau}^{\pm}=\begin{cases}x_{uv}=\exp(-4J_{uv})\quad\text{ if }\tau_{u}=\tau_{v}=\pm 1,\\
1\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1) The term

y(\tau\vee\tau^{\prime})y(\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime})=\left(\prod_{uv\in\mathcal{E}_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{y_{uv}^{2}}\right)y(\tau)y(\tau^{\prime}).

(3.8)

(2) The term

We need two properties of the partition function

The first is the FKG-lattice condition

9

3.2
RSW Theory

In this section, we state and prove the minimal RSW theory needed to prove Theorem
4.6
. Let us note that much stronger crossing results for the double random current have been established in
[
37
]
but we do not rely on these. We also mention that RSW results were also developed in
[
54
]
for the percolations
\omega^{+},\omega^{-}
on the section of the self-dual line when
U\leqslant J
, which may be very useful in generalising our work in the direction of Conjecture
1.3
.

We introduce the notation we use that will be specific to the square lattice.
We will restrict ourselves always to
domains

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that are a finite connected union of faces of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
We take
\partial\Lambda
to be the set of vertices
u\in\Lambda
incident to a vertex
v\not\in\Lambda
.
We further impose that the boundary of such a domain
(\partial\Lambda,E(\partial\Lambda)
has the property that it is always connected.
We say that
\Gamma
is a
circuit
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
if it is the possible edge-boundary of such a domain (i.e. a vertex-self-avoiding closed path of edges in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) and we denote by
\overline{\Gamma}
the unique domain with
E(\partial\overline{\Gamma})=\Gamma
.
The most common examples of domains we will use are the boxes
\Lambda_{n}
, defined for each
n\geqslant 1
by

\Lambda_{n}=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid|x|,|y|\leqslant n-1\right\}.

Unless otherwise stated, the measure
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}
will always refer to the coupling on the graph
(\Lambda,E(\Lambda))
with
+
boundary conditions on
\partial\Lambda
and the coupling constants fixed to take their critical value given in (
1.2
).

Before turning to crossing estimates we will need two further properties of the measure
\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}
. The first is perhaps the most important tool in the study of planar percolation.

•

(
Markov property
) Let
\Gamma
be any circuit in
\partial\Lambda
. Under the conditional law

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(\cdot\mid\Gamma\text{ is }\omega\text{-open}),

the triplet
(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma},\omega)
restricted to the vertices and edges of
\overline{\Gamma}
is given by
\mathbb{P}_{\overline{\Gamma}}^{+}
, except that possibly the global sign of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
must be flipped (which does not affect
\omega
) to ensure they are
+/+
on
\Gamma
.

In particular, the the state of
\omega|_{\overline{\Gamma}}
and
\omega|_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{\Gamma}}
are, conditionally on
\Gamma
being
\omega
-open, independent. We will often use this to explore an circuit from the outside so that no information about inside the circuit is revealed. Note that (unlike
\phi_{G}
) there is no Markov property with respect to
\omega
-closed cutsets (e.g. after discovering the entirety of a
\omega
-cluster).
The other property we will need is a spatial mixing estimate. We will use this only in the proof of Lemma
3.13
.

•

(
Spatial mixing
) There are constants
c,C>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
,
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{2n}
and events
A
(resp.
B
) depending only on the state in
\Lambda_{n}
(resp.
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{2n}
) we have

c\times\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(A)\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(B)\leqslant\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(A\cap B)\leqslant C\times\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(A)\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(B).

(3.17)

To prove these two properties we take a common approach and rely on analagous properties of the triple
(\sigma,\tilde{\sigma},\eta)
and then extend them to
\omega
using the simple relation
\omega=\xi(\sigma)\cap\xi(\tilde{\sigma})\cap\eta
.
For the first property, we can use the Markov property of the Ising model together with the observation that if
\Gamma
is
\omega
-open then necessarily both
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are constant on
\Gamma
.
Similarly, the second property immediately reduces to a mixing estimate for the critical Ising model which are well known (see e.g.
[
99
]
or
[
40
,
36
]
for mixing statements for the FK-Ising model).

Remark 3.9
.

We remark that a similar strategy can be used to prove mixing statements for the (sourceless) critical random current
\mathbf{{n}}_{1}
on the square lattice: It suffices to prove mixing for
(\mathbf{{n}}_{1})_{\textrm{odd}}
(since
(\mathbf{{n}}_{1})_{\textrm{even}}
is a sprinkling on top) and this may be derived by the identification
(\mathbf{{n}}_{1})_{\textrm{odd}}=\partial\sigma
and mixing of the Ising model.
Other stronger mixing properties of the planar random current model are proved in
[
37
]
.

Remark 3.10
.

We could improve the result to a polynomial-mixing statement since this is also known for the critical Ising model (see
[
40
,
36
]
for analagous proofs for the FK-Ising model), though we have no use for this improvement in this paper.

We define
A(n,N)
to be the event that there is an open circuit encircling the annulus
\Lambda_{N}\setminus\Lambda_{n}
.
It is known (it can be derived from the results of
[
37
]
) that

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(\omega\not\in A(n,N)\right)=\mathbb{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{(\mathbb{Z}^{2})^{*}}\left(\Lambda^{\dagger}_{n}\overset{\mathbf{{n}}}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N}^{\dagger}\right)\asymp\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{1/4}.

(3.18)

Notice that here
1/4
is the one-arm exponent of the double random current, whilst the one-arm exponent of
\omega
is instead
1/8
owing to its connection with the Ising model. The double random current is then an interesting example of a (critical) planar model which is not self-dual, and moreover, whose one-arm and dual one-arm exponents
differ
. We do not rely on (
3.18
) or any other results from
[
37
]
in this work.

Instead, we state and prove the following result which we will then bootstrap to a polynomial (but non-explicit) bound on the probability of there not existing any circuits in
\omega^{+}
or
\omega^{-}
in a large annulus. This dual one-arm exponent of
\omega^{+}
, which we do not know how to identify explicitly (see Remark
3.14
), will be the main error term in Theorem
4.6
.
In light of Lemma
3.6
and the lack of an FKG inequality for
\omega
, it will also be very useful to derive a bound on the dual one-arm exponent for
\omega^{-}
as well.

Lemma 3.11

(Existence of circuits)
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 1
and any domain
\Lambda
containing
\Lambda_{2n}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega^{+}\in A(n,2n))\geqslant c.

Moreover,
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega^{-}\in A(n,2n))\geqslant c
whenever
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{4n}
.

Proof.

Observe that the event
A(n,2n)
is primitive. Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\omega^{+}\in A(n,2n))\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}(\omega\in A(n,2n)).

Let
\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}
be the free measure on the domain
\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}
.
It follows from known monotonicity properties of the double random current
[
37
, Lemma 3.2]
that

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega\in A(n,2n))\geqslant\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(\omega\in A(n,2n)).

Finally, there is an exact identity relating this probability to the free-boundary FK-Ising measure
\phi^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}
,

\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(\omega\in A(n,2n))=1-(1-\phi^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(A(n,2n)))^{2}\geqslant\phi^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(A(n,2n)).

Indeed, from the duality relation between
\omega
and
\mathbf{{n}}
,

1-\mathbb{P}^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(\omega\in A(n,2n))=\mathbb{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{(\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n})^{*}}\left(\Lambda_{n}\overset{\mathbf{{n}}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Lambda_{2n}^{c}\right)

where
(\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n})^{*}
is the full (i.e. not weak) dual and the event is that
\mathbf{{n}}
connects the inner face to the outer face. Then, by writing the connection event as a spin correlation(see …) and using the self-duality of the FK-Ising model, we have

\mathbb{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{(\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n})^{*}}\left(\Lambda_{n}\overset{\mathbf{{n}}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Lambda_{2n}^{c}\right)=(1-\phi^{\textnormal{f}}_{\Lambda_{2n}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}(A(n,2n)))^{2}.

The result is then immediate from the RSW property of the FK-Ising model
[
36
]
. The statement for
\omega^{-}
follows by applying the mixing result in (
3.17
) which is why we impose the boundary of
\Lambda
not to be too close.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

It is possible to prove a corresponding upper bound whenever
\partial\Lambda
is sufficiently far from
\partial\Lambda_{2n}
. The subtelty here is that the planar double random current does not satisfy RSW results which are uniform in the domain. In particular, the probability

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}(\Lambda_{n}\overset{\mathbf{{n}}}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{2n})

is
not
uniformly positive for all
n\geqslant 1
when
\Lambda=\Lambda_{2n}
, but is when
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{(2+\varepsilon)n}
for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
(i.e. only when the free-boundary is macroscopically far away)
[
37
]
. This is also the expected behaviour of the random cluster model with
q=4
, since the scaling limit of the outer boundaries of large clusters in both models is the
\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
, whose loops are known not to touch the boundary of the domain.

Lemma 3.13

(Dual one-arm exponent)
.

There exists
\alpha,C>0
such that for all
\zeta\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}
,
N\geqslant n\geqslant 1
and any
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{N}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}\left(\omega^{\zeta}\not\in A(n,N)\right)\leqslant C\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

The argument is standard and consists of applying Lemma
3.11
and the spatial mixing property of
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}
to show that the set of scales on which there is a
\omega^{\pm}
-circuit stochastically dominates a point process of uniformly positive density.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

It is not difficult to derive the required quasi-multiplicativity to prove the existence of the dual one-arm exponent
\alpha
for
\omega^{+}
. The exact value of
\alpha
is then an interesting question, not least because it represents the main error term in Theorem
4.6
. Using the relation with
\tau
and the FKG properties (Proposition
3.1
), one can show that it must satisfy
\alpha\leqslant 1/8
. If the value
\alpha=1/8
were to be true, then the model
\omega^{+}
would be ‘approximately self-dual’ in the sense that the one-arm and dual one-arm exponents would coincide. However, it is generally the case that there are polynomial improvements to applications of the FKG inequality for arm-exponents
[
18
,
89
,
51
]
, suggesting that possibly
\alpha<1/8
.

Suppose that for some integer
n\geqslant 1
and two vertices
a,b\in\Lambda
are such that the two boxes
a+\Lambda_{2n}
and
b+\Lambda_{2n}
are both contained in
\Lambda
and are disjoint. In this case, we define

A=a+\Lambda_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad B=b+\Lambda_{n},

two boxes of size
n\geqslant 1
. The next proposition is concerned with the probability of the connection event
\left\{A\leftrightarrow B\right\}
.
Its proof is a standard application of the FKG inequality and the bounds on existence of circuits above. It will allow us to take sharp estimates on site-to-site connection probabilities (derived from (
2.10
)) and translate them into estimates for box-to-box connections. We write
X\lesssim Y
if there is a universal constant
C>0
such that
X\leqslant CY
and
X\asymp Y
if
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.

Proposition 3.15

(Box-to-box connection estimate)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the following is true. Let
\Lambda
be a domain containing two boxes
A
and
B
satisfying the conditions above. Then,

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}\left(A\leftrightarrow B\right)\leqslant Cn^{1/4-4}\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda}\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in A,y\in B\end{subarray}}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\right].

Proof.

We define also

A^{\prime}=a+\Lambda_{2n}\quad\text{ and }\quad B^{\prime}=b+\Lambda_{2n},

Let
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
be the outermost
\omega^{+}
-open circuits in the annuli
A^{\prime}\setminus A
and
B^{\prime}\setminus B
respectively (if they exist). Let
C_{A,B}
be the event that
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
do both exist and that

\Gamma_{A}\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Gamma_{B}.

It follows from (
3.2
), the FKG inequality and Lemma
3.11
that

\mathbb{P}(A\leftrightarrow B)\leqslant 2\mathbb{P}(A\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}B)\lesssim\mathbb{P}(C_{A,B}).

By conditioning on the state of
\omega
outside
\Gamma_{A}
and
\Gamma_{B}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x\in A,y\in B}\mathbf{1}(x\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}y)\right]\geqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{1}(C_{A,B})\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x\in A}\mathbf{1}(x\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Gamma_{A})\mid\Gamma_{A}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{y\in B}\mathbf{1}(y\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Gamma_{B})\mid\Gamma_{B}\right]\right].

Then, by the FKG property of the boundary cluster (or in this case simply by monotonicity of Ising spin correlations in the domain),

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x\in A}\mathbf{1}(x\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}\Gamma_{A})\mid\Gamma_{A}\right]\geqslant\mathbb{E}^{+}_{\Lambda_{2n}}\left[\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{n}}\sigma_{x}\right]\gtrsim n^{2-1/8},

where the last estimate is contained in Lemma
A.3
. Applying the same for the box
B
and rearranging all the terms leaves the desired inequality.
∎

3.3
Construction of the IIC measure

In this section, we construct the
incipient infinite cluster
(or IIC) measure of
\omega
, by which we mean we prove that in an appropriate the sense the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{N}}^{+}(\cdot\mid o\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N})

exists. This a classical problem in percolation theory first solved by Kesten
[
66
]
in the case of Bernoulli percolation. Results of this kind for dependent percolation models are not extensive but there has recent progress in a variety of models
[
86
]
.
The construction of a suitable IIC measure is an integral aspect to the
L^{2}
argument introduced in
[
50
]
. It is used to argue that the behaviour of macroscopic clusters, when viewed at a mesoscopic scale, concentrates sharply around the IIC measure. It is this concentration which will play the key role in the proof of Theorem
4.6
.
In
[
50
]
, the analysis is mostly focused on constructing the pivotal measure of Bernoulli site percolation. For the pivotal measure, the analagous input is to construct an infinite-volume measure conditioned on an alternating
four-arm
event.
There, a scale-by-scale coupling argument was used to prove the existence of such a measure. The analysis is intricate and requires certain seperation of arm estimates, however, a simpler approach for the case of a single arm (which is relevant to the cluster measures) was also suggested
[
50
, Proposition 5.2]
. Whilst it is possible to adapt this to
\omega
using the RSW theory in Section
3.2
, we take another approach.
We utilise an argument recently introduced in
[
59
]
. It uses a simple and explicit coupling to prove a quantitative convergence towards the IIC measure based on the dual-one-arm exponent. The argument there was written for Bernoulli site percolation but it immediately extends to any monotonic measure on bond configurations. After overcoming a slight difficult due to not knowing
\omega^{+}
is monotonic, our argument will follow
[
59
]
almost exactly. The result we will prove is the following.

Proposition 3.16

(Existence of IIC measure)
.

There exists
C,\alpha>0
such that the following holds. For each
N\geqslant k\geqslant n\geqslant 1
and each domain
\Lambda\supset\Lambda_{N}
, the measures

\omega^{(1)}\sim\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda_{N}}(\cdot\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\Lambda_{N})\quad\text{and}\quad\omega^{(2)}\sim\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(\cdot\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\Lambda)

may be coupled so that with probability
1-C(\frac{k}{N})^{\alpha}
they satisfy:

•

The restriction of
\omega^{(1)}
and
\omega^{(2)}
agree inside
\Lambda_{k}
and furthermore, the restrictions of the boundary clusters
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\omega^{(1)})
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\omega^{(2)})
agree inside
\Lambda_{k}
.

As a consequence of Proposition
3.16
, we may speak of the limiting measure
10
10

10

We note that other definitions of the IIC (which we will not use) can be easily shown to exist and coincide with this one by combining this result with the RSW estimates in Section
3.2
.
as
N\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(\cdot\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\infty).

In Section
4
, we will use this measure to define a quantity
\beta
which should be interpreted as the ‘density’ of the infinite cluster
\mathcal{C}_{0}
under this measure.
To be more precise, we expect that since the infinite cluster
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is conditioned to intersect the box
\Lambda_{n}
, then
|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|
will be of the order of
n^{2-1/8}
.
In fact, we will prove that

\mathbb{E}^{+}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|\mid\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\infty\right]=(\beta+o(1))n^{2-1/8},

for some constant
\beta>0
.
The existence of this limit will use the following lemma, which itself is a simple consequence of Proposition
3.16
.

Lemma 3.17

(Cluster-box intersection estimate)
.

There exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that for any
N^{\prime}\geqslant N\geqslant n\geqslant 1
,

\left|\mathbb{E}^{+}_{\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|\mid\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}^{+}_{\Lambda_{N}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|\mid\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{N}]\right|\leqslant Cn^{2-1/8}(\frac{n}{N})^{\alpha/2}.

Proof.

By using the coupling of Proposition
3.16
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce

|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}]-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{N}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N}]|\\
\lesssim(\frac{n}{N})^{\alpha_{1}/2}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|^{2}\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N^{\prime}}]+\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{N}}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|^{2}\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda_{N}]}.

(3.19)

Then, by the FKG property of the boundary cluster (Proposition
3.1
), we can compare to when the boundary conditions are wired on
\partial\Lambda_{n}
,

\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{N}}\left[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|^{2}\mid\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{N}\right]\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda_{n}}\left[|\mathcal{C}_{0}\cap\Lambda_{n}|^{2}\right]\leqslant\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda_{n}}(x\leftrightarrow y),

and so we can use Lemma
A.2
to conclude.
∎

Let us now explain the details of the coupling argument which is based on an exploration algorithm. The basic strategy is simple to understand but formalising the details will be a little fiddly. We recommend the exposition in
[
59
]
and also
[
40
, Proposition 2.6]
for an argument of a similar flavour. The main additional complication to these proofs is that we do not know
\omega^{+}
is monotonic so must instead use Lemma
3.3
for the pair
(\tau,\omega^{+})
.

We fix
N\geqslant k\geqslant n\geqslant 1
as in the statement. A first observation is that for each domain
\Lambda

\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}
, the event
\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda
is both measurable with respect to
\omega^{+}
and an increasing event for
\omega^{+}
. We will let

X=(\tau,\omega^{+})

and focus solely on the law of
X
for the time being. We will actually couple not two but three configurations

with the marginals

\displaystyle X^{(1)}=(\tau^{(1)},(\omega^{+})^{(1)})

\displaystyle X^{(2)}=(\tau^{(2)},(\omega^{+})^{(2)})

\displaystyle X^{(3)}=(\tau^{(3)},(\omega^{+})^{(3)})

Actually, we will sample

according to the equivalent measure

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(\cdot\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega}{\longleftrightarrow}\Lambda_{N},\omega^{+}_{E(\partial\Lambda_{N}\cup\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{N})}=1)

so that it is extended to the domain
\Lambda
and can still be viewed as the law

conditioned on an increasing event.
The coupling will have the property that

X^{(1)}\geqslant X^{(3)}\quad\text{and}\quad X^{(2)}\geqslant X^{(3)}.

(3.20)

The existence of such a coupling is guaranteed by the FKG inequality (Proposition
3.1
). To give an explicit coupling we will explore the simultaneuous state of
X^{(1)},X^{(2)},X^{(3)}
starting from the boundary
\partial\Lambda
and proceeding inwards.
That we have the desired ordering (
3.20
) will follow from inductively applying Lemma
3.3
.
They key step will be to to ensure with high probability
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
agree exactly before the box
\Lambda_{k}
is reached, and so our coupling is as desired.

Let us now say a bit more about this key step. The plan will be to find a circuit encircling the annulus
\Lambda_{N}\setminus\Lambda_{k}
which is entirely
(\omega^{+})^{(3)}
open, which by Lemma
3.13
will happen with high probability. Due to (
3.20
), it must also be open in both
(\omega^{+})^{(1)}
and
(\omega^{+})^{(3)}
.
Then, we can argue that if such a circuit
\Gamma
is discovered from the outside to be open in both
(\omega^{+})^{(1)}
and
(\omega^{+})^{(2)}
, then, the law of
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
inside
\Gamma
is precisely the same.
Indeed, together with the Markov property of
\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}
, there is a
decoupling
of the conditioning upon finding
\Gamma
so that is now equivalent to
\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\Gamma
.
Thus, the coupling can be completed whilst ensuring that
\omega^{(1)}
and
\omega^{(2)}
agree exactly inside
\Gamma
.

We now spell out exactly how Lemma
3.3
can be used to explore the configurations whilst preserving the order. We focus our attention on just showing
X^{(2)}\geqslant X^{(3)}
with the other case treated similarily. Suppose that we have explored the state of both
\tau^{(2)}
and
\tau^{(3)}
on a common set of vertices

(\omega^{+})^{(2)}
and
(\omega^{+})^{(3)}
on a common set of edges
H
with

. As our inductive hypothesis, we may assume that so far the ordering is as desired,

\tau^{(2)}_{S}\geqslant\tau^{(3)}_{S}\quad\text{and}\quad(\omega^{+})^{(2)}_{H}\geqslant(\omega^{+})^{(3)}_{H}.

(3.21)

We will now sample the state of an edge
e\notin H
. For simplicitly, we will assume that already
H\cup\left\{e\right\}\subset E(S)
(see Stage 2 of the algorithm otherwise).
To continue the coupling whilst maintaining
X^{(2)}\geqslant X^{(3)}
, we need to show that the conditional probability (given what we have observed so far) of the edge being open in
(\omega^{+})^{(2)}
is at least that of it being open in
(\omega^{+})^{(3)}
.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega^{+}_{e}=1\mid\Lambda_{n}\overset{\omega^{+}}{\longleftrightarrow}\partial\Lambda,\tau_{S}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(2)}_{S},\omega^{+}_{H}=(\omega^{+})^{(2)}_{H})

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega^{+}_{e}=1\mid\tau_{S}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(2)}_{S},\omega^{+}_{H}=(\omega^{+})^{(2)}_{H})

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}_{\Lambda}^{+}(\omega^{+}_{e}=1\mid\tau_{S}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(3)}_{S},\omega^{+}_{H}=(\omega^{+})^{(3)}_{H}).

(3.22)

Here, we have applied both aspects of Lemma
3.3
(using our assumption on
S
and
H
). In the first inequality, we used that the conditional measure

\mathbb{P}^{+}_{\Lambda}(X\in\cdot\mid\tau_{S}=\tau^{(2)}_{S},\omega^{+}_{H}=(\omega^{+})^{(2)}_{H})

has the FKG property and that the events
\left\{\omega^{+}_{e}=1\right\}

Proposition 4.3

, Lemma 4.5]
. A sequence of measures
(\mu_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
converges to
\mu
with respect to the vague topology if

\mu_{n}[g]\longrightarrow\mu[g]

for every continuous function
g\in C_{c}(D)
with compact support in
D
. Again, this topology is metrizable in a way that turns
\mathsf{M}
into a complete and separable metric space
[
63
, Lemma 4.6]
. It is a standard fact that weak convergence is equivalent to weak convergence and tightness of the sequence. For sequences of (countable) collections of measures, convergence is taken to be coordinate-wise.

(4) Sobolev Spaces:
We closely follow the definitions as found in
[
60
]
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
is defined as the space of tempered distributions
h
such that

\lVert h\rVert^{2}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{C})}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\hat{h}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty,

where
\hat{h}
is the Fourier transform of
h
. The space
H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
is a separable Hilbert space. For every
s>0
, the space
H^{-s}(\mathbb{C})
is the dual of
H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
under the standard
L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
dual pairing. Moreover, the space
H^{-s}(\mathbb{C})
is a subspace of the space of distributions in
\mathbb{C}
, i.e. the space of continuous linear functions acting on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
. We highlight that

(i)

The Dirac distribution
\bm{\updelta}_{x}
is an element of
H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if
s<-1
. The same holds for any compactly supported Borel measure (so, in particular, our discrete area measures).

(ii)

There exists a continuous embedding of
H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
into
C_{c}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if
s>1
.

For these reasons, all of our fields are taken to converge in Sobolev spaces with regularity
s<-1
.

For any domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
, the space
H^{s}(D)
is defined via restriction. That is,
f\in H^{s}(D)
if there exists
h\in H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
such that
f=h|_{D}
. The local Sobolev space
H^{s}_{loc}(D)
is defined as the space of distributions
h
such that
h\psi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{C})
for every
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
. For further background on Sobolev spaces we refer to
[
1
]
, see also
[
21
,
60
,
34
,
49
]
for their usage in similar contexts.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
be a sequence of collections of loops in

converging to
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the metric
d_{\mathsf{L}}
. Suppose that the loops in

are pairwise disjoint. Then,
(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L}_{\delta}))_{\delta>0}
converges to
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L})
with respect to the metric

.

Proof.

This follows from
[
100
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

A set
C
is said to be tangent to
A
if
C\cap A\neq\emptyset
and
C\cap A^{\circ}=\emptyset
, where
A^{\circ}
is the interior of
A
.

Proposition C.2
.

Let
(C_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
be a sequence of closed sets converging to
C
as
\delta\to 0
with respect to the Hausdorff metric. Fix a box
A
and suppose that
C
is not tangent to
A
. Then,

\mathbf{1}(C_{\delta}\cap A\neq\emptyset)\longrightarrow\mathbf{1}(C\cap A\neq\emptyset)\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

Proof.

First, suppose that
C\cap A=\emptyset
. Then,
d=d_{H}(C,A)>0
and for small enough
\delta
we have that
d_{H}(C_{\delta},C)<d/2
. Thus
d_{H}(C_{\delta},A)>d/2
eventually, and the claim follows. Otherwise, we must have that
C\cap A^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
. Choose
z\in C\cap A^{\circ}
and
r>0
such that
B(z,r)\subset A
. For small enough
\delta
, there exists
z_{\delta}\in C_{\delta}
such that
|z_{\delta}-z|<r/2
. Hence
z_{\delta}\in B(z,r)\subset A
and
C_{\delta}\cap A\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma C.3
.

Let
A\subset D
be a fixed (deterministic) box. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the carpet of a
\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
in
D
. Then,
\mathcal{C}
is not tangent to
A
almost surely.

Proof.

We work with the construction of the
\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
in terms of
\textup{SLE}_{4}(-1,-1)
and
\textup{SLE}_{4}(-1)
as introduced in
[
11
, Section 6]
. First, we note that if
A\cap\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
, then necessarily
A\cap(D\setminus\mathcal{C})\neq\emptyset
. In particular
A
must intersect some level line
\eta
used in the construction of the
\textnormal{CLE}_{4}
.

By
[
79
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
21
, Appendix A.3]
), we know that
\eta
is a continuous curve and, by the above, there exists a finite stopping time
\tau
such that
\eta_{\tau}\in\partial A
. Moreover, by
[
79

, the underlying driving system of SDEs gives rise to an almost surely continuous Markov process. By applying the strong Markov property at time

, which concludes the proof.

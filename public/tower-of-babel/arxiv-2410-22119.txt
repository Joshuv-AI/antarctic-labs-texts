Deep Q-Exponential Processes
Zhi Chang, Chukwudi Obite, Shuang Zhou, Shiwei Lan

Abstract
Motivated by deep neural networks, the deep Gaussian process (DGP) generalizes the standard GP by stacking multiple layers of GPs. Despite the enhanced expressiveness, GP, as an L 2 L_{2} regularization prior, tends to be over-smooth and sub-optimal for inhomogeneous subjects, such as images with edges. Recently, Q-exponential process (Q-EP) has been proposed as an L q L_{q} relaxation to GP and demonstrated with more desirable regularization properties through a parameter q > 0 q>0 with q = 2 q=2 corresponding to GP. Sharing the similar tractability of posterior and predictive distributions with GP, Q-EP can also be stacked to improve its modeling flexibility. In this paper, we generalize Q-EP to deep Q-EP to enjoy both proper regularization and improved expressiveness. The generalization is realized by introducing shallow Q-EP as a latent variable model and then building a hierarchy of the shallow Q-EP layers. Sparse approximation by inducing points and scalable variational strategy are applied to facilitate the inference. We demonstrate the numerical advantages of the proposed deep Q-EP model by comparing with multiple state-of-the-art deep probabilistic models.



Keywords:
Deep Models, Inhomogeneous Subjects, Regularization, Latent Representation, Model Expressiveness

2.1
Multivariate

Based on

-exponential distribution

[

\pi_{q}(u)\propto\exp{(-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{q})}

is one of the following exponential power (EP) distributions
\mathrm{EP}(\mu,\sigma,q)
with

p(u|\mu,\sigma,q)=\frac{q}{2^{1+1/q}\sigma\Gamma(1/q)}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{u-\mu}{\sigma}\right|^{q}\right\}.

This family includes normal distribution
{\mathcal{N}}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
for

and Laplace distribution
L(\mu,b)
with
\sigma=2^{-1/q}b
for
q=1
as special cases.

[
24
]
generalizes the univariate
q
-exponential random variable to a multivariate random vector on which a stochastic process can be defined with two requirements by the Kolmogorov’ extension theorem
[
28
]
: i)
exchangeability
of the joint distribution, i.e.
p({\bf u}_{1:N})=p({\bf u}_{\tau(1:N)})
for any finite permutation
\tau
; and ii)
consistency
of marginalization, i.e.
p({\bf u}_{1})=\int p({\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2})d{\bf u}_{2}
.

Suppose a function
u(x)
is observed at
N
locations,
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\in{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

[
24
]
find a consistent generalization, named

q
-exponential distribution
,
for

from the family of elliptic contour distributions
[
17

8
]

Definition 1
.

A multivariate
q
-exponential distribution for a random vector
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, denoted as
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{N}(\bm{\mu},{\bf C})
, has the following density

p({\bf u}|\bm{\mu},{\bf C},q)=\frac{q}{2}(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}|{\bf C}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}r^{(\frac{q}{2}-1)\frac{N}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{r^{\frac{q}{2}}}{2}\right\},\quad r({\bf u})={({\bf u}-\bm{\mu})}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf C}^{-1}({\bf u}-\bm{\mu}).

(1)

The following proposition describes the role of matrix
{\bf C}
in characterizing the covariance between the components
[
24
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
{\bf u}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{N}(\bm{\mu},{\bf C})
, then we have

\mathrm{E}[{\bf u}]=\bm{\mu},\qquad\mathrm{Cov}({\bf u})=\frac{2^{\frac{2}{q}}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+\frac{2}{q})}{N\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\bf C}\overset{\cdot}{\sim}N^{\frac{2}{q}-1}{\bf C},\quad as\quad N\to\infty.

2.2
Q
-exponential Process and Multi-output Q-EP

[
24
]
prove that the multivariate
q
-exponential random vector
{\bf u}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{N}(0,{\bf C})
satisfies the conditions of Kolmogorov’s extension theorem hence it can be generalized to a stochastic process.
For this purpose, we scale it by a factor
N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}
so that its covariance is asymptotically finite (refer to Proposition
2.1
). If
{\bf u}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{N}(0,{\bf C})
, then we denote
{\bf u}^{*}:=N^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}{\bf u}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}^{*}_{N}(0,{\bf C})

scaled

q
-exponential random variable.
With a covariance (symmetric and positive-definite) kernel
{\mathcal{C}}:{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
we define the following
q
-exponential process (Q-EP)
based on the scaled
q
-exponential distribution
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}^{*}_{N}(0,{\bf C})
.

Definition 2
.

A (centered)
q
-exponential process
u(x)
with kernel
{\mathcal{C}}
,
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(0,{\mathcal{C}})
, is a collection of random variables such that any finite set,
{\bf u}:=(u(x_{1}),\cdots u(x_{N}))

q
-exponential distribution
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}^{*}(0,{\bf C})
, where
{\bf C}=[{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i},x_{j})]_{N\times N}

If
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{I}}

is said to be
marginally identical but uncorrelated (m.i.u.)
.

Remark 1
.

When
q=2
,
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{N}(\bm{\mu},{\bf C})
reduces to
{\mathcal{N}}_{N}(\bm{\mu},{\bf C})
and
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(0,{\mathcal{C}})
becomes
\mathcal{GP}(0,{\mathcal{C}})
. When

,
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(0,{\mathcal{C}})
lends flexibility to modeling functional data with more regularization than GP.

One caveat of Q-EP is that uncorrelation (identity covariance) does not imply independence except for the special Gaussian case (
q=2
). For multiple Q-EPs,
(u_{1}(x),\cdots,u_{D}(x))

1
)). Rather, uncorrelation is a preferable assumption. In general, we define multi-output (multivariate) Q-EPs through matrix vectorization.

Definition 3
.

A multi-output (multivariate)
q
-exponential process
,
u(\cdot)=(u_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,u_{D}(\cdot))

\mathrm{vec}([u_{1}({\bf x}),\cdots,u_{D}({\bf x})]_{N\times D})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}_{ND}(\mathrm{vec}(\bm{\mu}),{\bf C}_{t}\otimes{\bf C}_{x})
, where we have
u_{j}({\bf x})={[u_{j}(x_{1}),\cdots,u_{j}(x_{N})]}^{\mathsf{T}}
, for

,
\bm{\mu}=[\mu_{1}({\bf x}),\cdots,\mu_{D}({\bf x})]_{N\times D}
and
{\bf C}_{x}=[{\mathcal{C}}_{x}(x_{n},x_{m})]_{N\times N}
. We denote
u\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(\mu,{\mathcal{C}}_{x},{\bf C}_{t})
.
In particular,
\{u_{j}(\cdot)\}
are m.i.u. if
{\bf C}_{t}={\bf I}_{D}
.

In the following, we will stack m.i.u. multi-output Q-EPs to build a deep Q-EP.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we generalize Q-EP to deep Q-EP, which includes deep GP as a special case. Moreover, deep Q-EP inherits the flexible regularization controlled a parameter
q>0
, which is advantageous in learning latent representations and modeling data inhomogeneity. We first generalize Bayesian GP-LVM to Bayesian QEP-LVM (as shallow Q-EP layer) and develop the variational inference for it. Then we stack multiple shallow Q-EP layer to build the deep Q-EP model. The novel deep model demonstrates substantial numerical benefits in various learning tasks and can be combined with neural network for better characterizing the latent representation of big data applications.

As common in GP and NN models, we do observe multi-modality of the posterior distributions, especially in the hyper-parameter spaces. Sub-optimal solutions can appear in the stochastic training process. These issues can be alleviated by dispersed or diversified initialization, or with adaptive training schedulers.
One potential application of deep Q-EP is the inverse learning, similarly as done by deep GP
[
16
,
1
]
. Theory of the contraction properties
[
9
]
is also an interesting research direction.

PROOFS

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The gradients of log-likelihood (
6
) with respect to
{\bf K}
can be found as

\frac{\partial L}{\partial{\bf K}}=-\frac{D}{2}{\bf K}^{-1}-\left[\frac{ND}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)r^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\right]{\bf K}^{-1}{\bf Y}{{\bf Y}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}^{-1}.

(9)

The MLE for
{\bf X}
can be found by setting
\frac{\partial L}{\partial{\bf X}}=2\alpha^{-1}\frac{\partial L}{\partial{\bf K}}{\bf X}=0
, which leads to

{\bf X}=\left[-N\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)r^{-1}+\frac{1}{D}\frac{q}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\right]{\bf Y}{{\bf Y}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}^{-1}{\bf X}.

(10)

Now suppose we have the formal solution for (
10
) as
{\bf X}={\bf U}{\bf L}{\bf V}
, where
{\bf L}
is an
N\times Q
matrix whose only nonzero entries
\{l_{i}\}
are on the main diagonal to be determined.
Based on the formal solution of
{\bf X}
, we have

{\bf K}={\bf U}(\alpha^{-1}{\bf L}{{\bf L}}^{\mathsf{T}}+\beta^{-1}{\bf I}){{\bf U}}^{\mathsf{T}},\quad r({\bf Y})=\mathrm{tr}({\bf K}^{-1}{\bf Y}{{\bf Y}}^{\mathsf{T}})=\sum_{i=1}^{D\wedge Q}\lambda_{i}(\alpha^{-1}l_{i}^{2}+\beta^{-1})^{-1}.

Denote by
h(r):=-N\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)r^{-1}+\frac{1}{D}\frac{q}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}-1}
. We substitute the above quantities into (
10
) and get

{\bf U}{\bf L}{\bf V}={\bf U}\bm{\Lambda}(\alpha^{-1}{\bf L}{{\bf L}}^{\mathsf{T}}+\beta^{-1}{\bf I})^{-1}{\bf L}{\bf V}h(r),

which reduces to

l_{i}=\lambda_{i}(\alpha^{-1}l_{i}^{2}+\beta^{-1})^{-1}l_{i}h(r),\quad i=1,\cdots,D\wedge Q.

Let
h(r)=c
with
c
to be determined.
Assume
l_{i}\neq 0
. Then we can solve
l_{i}=\sqrt{\alpha(c\lambda_{i}-\beta^{-1})}
.
This yields

h(r)=-N\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)c(D\wedge Q)^{-1}+\frac{1}{D}\frac{q}{2}(D\wedge Q)^{\frac{q}{2}-1}c^{1-\frac{q}{2}}=c.

And it solves
c=\left[\frac{q(D\wedge Q)^{\frac{q}{2}}}{2D(D\wedge Q)+(q-2)ND}\right]^{\frac{2}{q}}
. Hence the proof is completed.

Appendix B
Computation of Variational Lower Bounds

B.1
Shallow Q-EP

The variational lower bound for the log-evidence is

\log p({\bf Y})\geq{\mathcal{L}}(q):=\int q({\bf X})\log\frac{p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})p({\bf X})}{q({\bf X})}d{\bf X}=\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}(q)-\mathrm{KL}(q({\bf X})\|p({\bf X})),

where the first term
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}(q)=\int q({\bf X})\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})d{\bf X}
is intractable and hence difficult to bound.

B.1.1
Lower bound for the marginal likelihood

To address such intractability issue and speed up the computation, sparse variational approximation
[
34
,
23
]
is adopted by introducing a set of inducing points
\tilde{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times Q}
with their function values
{\bf U}=[f_{1}(\tilde{\bf X}),\cdots,f_{D}(\tilde{\bf X})]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times D}
.
Hence the marginal likelihood
p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})
defined in (
7
) can be augmented to the following joint distribution each being a
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}
:

p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})\propto p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})p({\bf F}|{\bf U},{\bf X},\tilde{\bf X})p({\bf U}|\tilde{\bf X}),

where we have
\mathrm{vec}({\bf U})|\tilde{\bf X}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf 0},{\bf I}_{D}\otimes{\bf K}_{MM})
and the conditional distribution

\mathrm{vec}({\bf F})|{\bf U},{\bf X},\tilde{\bf X}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}(\mathrm{vec}({\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf U}),{\bf I}_{D}\otimes({\bf K}_{NN}-{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf K}_{MN})).

(11)

The inducing points
\tilde{\bf X}
are regarded as variational parameters and hence they are dropped from the following probability expressions.
We then approximate
p({\bf F},{\bf U}|{\bf X})\propto p({\bf F}|{\bf U},{\bf X})p({\bf U})
with
q({\bf F},{\bf U})=p({\bf F}|{\bf U},{\bf X})q({\bf U})
in another variational Bayes as follows

\displaystyle\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})

\displaystyle\geq\int q({\bf F},{\bf U})\log\frac{p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})p({\bf F}|{\bf U},{\bf X})p({\bf U})}{q({\bf F},{\bf U})}d{\bf F}d{\bf U}

(12)

\displaystyle=\int p({\bf F}|{\bf U})q({\bf U})d{\bf U}\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})d{\bf F}+\int q({\bf U})\log\frac{p({\bf U})}{q({\bf U})}d{\bf U}.

Different from
[
34
,
35
]
using the variational calculus,
[
12
, SVGP]
computes the marginal likelihood ELBO (
12
) in two stages.
Instead of the variational free form, we follow
[
12
]
to use the variational distribution for
{\bf U}
of the following format conjugate to
p({\bf F}|{\bf U})
:

q({\bf U})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf M},\diag(\{\bm{\varSigma}_{d}\})).

(13)

{\bf F}|{\bf U}

{\bf U}

\displaystyle q({\bf F})=\int q({\bf F},{\bf U})d{\bf U}=\int p({\bf F}|{\bf U})q({\bf U})d{\bf U}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}(\mathrm{vec}({\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M}),

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle I}_{D}\otimes({\bf K}_{NN}-{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf K}_{MN})+\diag(\{{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}\bm{\varSigma}_{d}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf K}_{MN}\})).

Therefore, the variational lower bound of the marginal likelihood (

\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf X})\geq\langle\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}-\mathrm{KL}(q({\bf U})\|p({\bf U})).

Denote by
\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})=\varphi(r({\bf Y},{\bf F}))
, where

, and

is a quadratic form of random variable
{\bf Y}

Therefore, by Jensen’s inequality, we can bound from below as

\langle\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}=\langle\varphi(r({\bf Y},{\bf F}))\rangle_{q({\bf F})}\geq\varphi(\langle r({\bf Y},{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}).

where we can calculate the expectation of the quadratic form

\displaystyle\langle r({\bf Y},{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}=

\displaystyle r({\bf Y},{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M})+\beta D\mathrm{tr}({\bf K}_{NN}-{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf K}_{MN})

\displaystyle+\beta\sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathrm{tr}({\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}\bm{\varSigma}_{d}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf K}_{MN}).

Denote by
h({\bf Y},{\bf X})=\langle\langle\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}\rangle_{q({\bf X})}
. Then by Jensen’s inequality again we have

h({\bf Y},{\bf X})\geq\varphi(\langle\langle r({\bf Y},{\bf F})\rangle_{q({\bf F})}\rangle_{q({\bf X})})=:h^{*}({\bf Y},{\bf X}).

Define

\Psi_{2}=\langle{\bf K}_{MN}{\bf K}_{NM}\rangle_{q({\bf X})}

(14)

\displaystyle+\beta\sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathrm{tr}({\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}\bm{\varSigma}_{d}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}\Psi_{2}),

\displaystyle\langle r({\bf Y},{\bf K}_{NM}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}=

\displaystyle r({\bf Y},\Psi_{1}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M})+\beta\mathrm{tr}({{\bf M}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}(\Psi_{2}-{\Psi}^{\mathsf{T}}_{1}\Psi_{1}){\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M}).

We also need to compute the K-L divergence
\mathrm{KL}_{\bf U}:=\mathrm{KL}(q({\bf U})\|p({\bf U}))

\mathrm{KL}_{\bf U}=\int q({\bf U})\log q({\bf U})d{\bf U}-\int q({\bf U})\log p({\bf U})d{\bf U}=-\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf U})-\langle\log p({\bf U})\rangle_{q({\bf U})}.

Denote by
r={{\mathrm{vec}}^{\mathsf{T}}({\bf U}-{\bf M})}^{\mathsf{T}}\diag(\{\bm{\varSigma}_{d}\})^{-1}{\mathrm{vec}}^{\mathsf{T}}({\bf U}-{\bf M})
. Then
\log q({\bf U})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\log|\bm{\varSigma}_{d}|+\frac{MD}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\log r-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}}

[
24

we know that
r^{\frac{q}{2}}\sim\chi^{2}(MD)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf U})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\log|\bm{\varSigma}_{d}|+\frac{MD}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\frac{2}{q}\mathcal{H}(\chi^{2}(MD))+\frac{MD}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\log|\bm{\varSigma}_{d}|+\frac{MD}{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)\left[\frac{MD}{2}+\log\left(2\Gamma\left(\frac{MD}{2}\right)\right)+\left(1-\frac{MD}{2}\right)\psi\left(\frac{MD}{2}\right)\right]+\frac{MD}{2}.

Denote by
\varphi_{0}(r):=-\frac{D}{2}\log|{\bf K}_{MM}|+\frac{MD}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\log r-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}}
. Then by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\langle\log p({\bf U})\rangle_{q({\bf U})}

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi_{0}(\mathrm{tr}({{\bf U}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf U}))\rangle_{q({\bf U})}\geq\varphi_{0}(\langle\mathrm{tr}({{\bf U}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf U})\rangle_{q({\bf U})}),

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{tr}({{\bf U}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf U})\rangle_{q({\bf U})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}({{\bf M}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}{\bf M})+\sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\varSigma}_{d}{\bf K}_{MM}^{-1}).

The elements of
\psi_{0}
,
\Psi_{1}
and

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int k({\bf x}_{n},{\bf x}_{n})\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf x}_{n}|\bm{\mu}_{n},{\bf S}_{n})d{\bf x}_{n},

\displaystyle(\Psi_{1})_{nm}

\displaystyle=\int k({\bf x}_{n},{\bf z}_{m})\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf x}_{n}|\bm{\mu}_{n},{\bf S}_{n})d{\bf x}_{n},

\displaystyle(\Psi_{2}^{n})_{mm^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int k({\bf x}_{n},{\bf z}_{m})k({\bf z}_{m^{\prime}},{\bf x}_{n})\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf x}_{n}|\bm{\mu}_{n},{\bf S}_{n})d{\bf x}_{n}.

With ARD SE kernel (

\psi_{0}=N\alpha^{-1}
.
While the integration in

\displaystyle\approx\alpha^{-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\langle{({\bf x}_{n}-{\bf z}_{m})}^{\mathsf{T}}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})({\bf x}_{n}-{\bf z}_{m})\rangle_{q({\bf x}_{n})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}[{(\bm{\mu}_{n}-{\bf z}_{m})}^{\mathsf{T}}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})(\bm{\mu}_{n}-{\bf z}_{m})+\mathrm{tr}(\diag({\bm{\gamma}}){\bf S}_{n})]\right\},

\displaystyle(\Psi_{2}^{n})_{mm^{\prime}}

If we use the ARD linear form,
k({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})={{\bf x}}^{\mathsf{T}}\diag({\bm{\gamma}}){\bf x}^{\prime}
, then we have

\displaystyle\psi_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\diag({\bm{\gamma}})(\bm{\mu}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{n}+{\bf S}_{n})),\quad(\Psi_{1})_{nm}={\bm{\mu}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{n}\diag({\bm{\gamma}}){\bf z}_{m},

\displaystyle(\Psi_{2}^{n})_{mm^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={{\bf z}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{m}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})(\bm{\mu}_{n}{\bm{\mu}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{n}+{\bf S}_{n})\diag({\bm{\gamma}}){\bf z}_{m^{\prime}}.

B.1.2
Lower bound for the K-L divergence added terms

Lastly, we need to compute the K-L divergence

\mathrm{KL}(q({\bf X})\|p({\bf X}))=\int q({\bf X})\log q({\bf X})d{\bf X}-\int q({\bf X})\log p({\bf X})d{\bf X}=-\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf X})-\langle\log p({\bf X})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}.

Denote by
r={\mathrm{vec}({\bf X}-\bm{\mu})}^{\mathsf{T}}\diag(\{{\bf S}_{n}\})^{-1}\mathrm{vec}({\bf X}-\bm{\mu})
. Then
\log q({\bf X})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log|{\bf S}_{n}|+\frac{NQ}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\log r-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}}
. From
[
24
, Proposition A.1. of]

we know that
r^{\frac{q}{2}}\sim\chi^{2}(NQ)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf X})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log|{\bf S}_{n}|+\frac{NQ}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\frac{2}{q}\mathcal{H}(\chi^{2}(NQ))+\frac{NQ}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log|{\bf S}_{n}|+\frac{NQ}{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)\left[\frac{NQ}{2}+\log\left(2\Gamma\left(\frac{NQ}{2}\right)\right)+\left(1-\frac{NQ}{2}\right)\psi\left(\frac{NQ}{2}\right)\right]+\frac{NQ}{2}.

Denote by
\varphi_{0}(r):=\frac{NQ}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\log r-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}}
. Then by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\langle\log p({\bf X})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi_{0}(\mathrm{tr}({{\bf X}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf X}))\rangle_{q({\bf X})}\geq\varphi_{0}(\langle\mathrm{tr}({{\bf X}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf X})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}),

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{tr}({{\bf X}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bf X})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}({\bm{\mu}}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\mu})+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}({\bf S}_{n}).

B.2
Deep Q-EP

We only consider the hierarchy of two QEP-LVMs because the general
L
-layers follows by induction:

\displaystyle y_{nd}

\displaystyle=f_{d}^{Y}({\bf x}_{n})+\varepsilon_{nd}^{Y},\quad d=1,\cdots,D,\quad{\bf x}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{Q},

(15)

\displaystyle x_{nq}

\displaystyle=f_{q}^{X}({\bf z}_{n})+\varepsilon_{nq}^{X},\quad q=1,\cdots,Q,\quad{\bf z}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{Q_{Z}},

where
\bm{\varepsilon}^{Y}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf 0},\Gamma^{Y})
,
\bm{\varepsilon}^{X}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf 0},\Gamma^{X})
,
f^{Y}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(0,k^{Y})
and
f^{X}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathcal{EP}(0,k^{X})
.
Consider the prior
{\bf Z}\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf 0},{\bf I}_{NQ_{Z}})
.
The variational inference for
p({\bf Z}|{\bf Y})
requires maximizing the following ELBO

\log p({\bf Y})\geq{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{Q}}):=\int_{{\bf Z},{\bf F}^{X},{\bf X},{\bf F}^{Y}}{\mathcal{Q}}\log\frac{p({\bf Y},{\bf F}^{Y},{\bf X},{\bf F}^{X},{\bf Z})}{{\mathcal{Q}}},

(16)

where the joint probability can be decomposed

p({\bf Y},{\bf F}^{Y},{\bf X},{\bf F}^{X},{\bf Z})=p({\bf Y}|{\bf F}^{Y})p({\bf F}^{Y}|{\bf X})\cdot p({\bf X}|{\bf F}^{X})p({\bf F}^{X}|{\bf Z})p({\bf Z})

Similarly as in Section
3.1
, sparse variational approximation
[
35
]
is adopted to introduce inducing points
\tilde{{\bf X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times Q},\tilde{{\bf Z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times Q_{Z}}
with associated function values
{\bf U}^{Y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times D},{\bf U}^{X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times Q}
respectively. Hence the augmented probability replaces the joint probability:

\displaystyle p({\bf Y},{\bf F}^{Y},{\bf X},{\bf F}^{X},{\bf Z},{\bf U}^{Y},{\bf U}^{X})=

\displaystyle p({\bf Y}|{\bf F}^{Y})p({\bf F}^{Y}|{\bf U}^{Y},{\bf X})p({\bf U}^{Y}|\tilde{{\bf X}})\cdot

\displaystyle p({\bf X}|{\bf F}^{X})p({\bf F}^{X}|{\bf U}^{X},{\bf Z})p({\bf U}^{X}|\tilde{{\bf Z}})p({\bf Z}),

where
{\bf F}^{Y}
and
{\bf U}^{Y}
are drawn from the same Q-EP; and similarly are
{\bf F}^{X}
and
{\bf U}^{X}
.
Now we specify the approximation distribution as

{\mathcal{Q}}=p({\bf F}^{Y}|{\bf U}^{Y},{\bf X})q({\bf U}^{Y})q({\bf X})\cdot p({\bf F}^{X}|{\bf U}^{X},{\bf Z})q({\bf U}^{X})q({\bf Z}).

and choose
q({\bf U}^{Y})
and
q({\bf U}^{X})
, and
q({\bf X})
and
q({\bf Z})
to be uncorrelated
\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}
’s:

\displaystyle q({\bf U}^{Y})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf M}^{Y},\diag(\{\bm{\varSigma}_{d}^{Y}\})),

\displaystyle q({\bf U}^{X})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}({\bf M}^{X},\diag(\{\bm{\varSigma}_{d}^{X}\})),

\displaystyle q({\bf X})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}(\bm{\mu}^{X},\diag(\{{\bf S}^{X}_{n}\})),

\displaystyle q({\bf Z})\sim\mathrm{q}\!-\!\mathrm{ED}(\bm{\mu}^{Z},\diag(\{{\bf S}^{Z}_{n}\})).

Then the ELBO (
16
) becomes

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{Q}})

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\bf Z},{\bf U}^{X},{\bf F}^{X},{\bf X},{\bf U}^{Y},{\bf F}^{Y}}{\mathcal{Q}}\log\frac{p({\bf Y}|{\bf F}^{Y})p({\bf U}^{Y})p({\bf X}|{\bf F}^{X})p({\bf U}^{X})p({\bf Z})}{q({\bf U}^{Y})q({\bf X})q({\bf U}^{X})q({\bf Z})}

\displaystyle=h({\bf Y},{\bf X})-\mathrm{KL}_{{\bf U}^{Y}}+h({\bf X},{\bf Y})-\mathrm{KL}_{{\bf U}^{X}}+\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf X})-\mathrm{KL}_{\bf Z},

where we have

h({\bf Y},{\bf X})=\left\langle\log p({\bf Y}|{\bf F}^{Y})\right\rangle_{q({\bf F}^{Y})q({\bf X})},\quad h({\bf X},{\bf Z})=\left\langle\log p({\bf X}|{\bf F}^{X})\right\rangle_{q({\bf F}^{X})q({\bf X})q({\bf Z})}.

Note,
h({\bf Y},{\bf X})\geq h^{*}({\bf Y},{\bf X})
is the same as in the bound (
8
) for Bayesian LVM. However,
h({\bf X},{\bf Z})
has an extra integration with respect to
q({\bf X})
. Replacing
{\bf X}
with
{\bf Z}
and
{\bf Y}
with
{\bf X}
in (
14
), we compute

\langle r({\bf X},\Psi_{1}({\bf K}^{X}_{MM})^{-1}{\bf U}^{X})\rangle_{q({\bf X})}=r(\bm{\mu}^{X},\Psi_{1}({\bf K}^{X}_{MM})^{-1}{\bf U}^{X})+\mathrm{tr}(({\bf I}_{D}\otimes(\Gamma^{X})^{-1})\diag(\{{\bf S}_{n}^{X}\})).

Therefore we have a updated bound for
h({\bf X},{\bf Z})\geq h^{*}({\bf X},{\bf Z})=\varphi(r_{\bm{\mu}^{X}};\Gamma^{X},Q)
, where

\displaystyle r_{\bm{\mu}^{X}}=

\displaystyle r(\bm{\mu}^{X},\Psi_{1}({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}{\bf M}^{X})+\mathrm{tr}({({\bf M}^{X})}^{\mathsf{T}}({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}(\Psi_{2}^{X}-{\Psi}^{\mathsf{T}}_{1}(\Gamma^{X})^{-1}\Psi_{1})({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}{\bf M}^{X})

\displaystyle+Q[\psi_{0}-\mathrm{tr}(({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}\Psi_{2}^{X})]+\sum_{d=1}^{Q}\mathrm{tr}(({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}\bm{\varSigma}_{d}^{X}({\bf K}_{MM}^{X})^{-1}\Psi_{2}^{X})

\displaystyle+\mathrm{tr}(({\bf I}_{Q}\otimes(\Gamma^{X})^{-1})\diag(\{{\bf S}_{n}^{X}\})).

Lastly, we have

\mathcal{H}_{q}({\bf X})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log|{\bf S}_{n}^{X}|,\;-\mathrm{KL}(q({\bf Z})\|p({\bf Z}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log|{\bf S}_{n}^{Z}|+\varphi_{0}(\mathrm{tr}({(\bm{\mu}^{Z})}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\mu}^{Z})+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}({\bf S}_{n}^{Z})),

where
\varphi_{0}(r):=\frac{NQ_{Z}}{2}\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\log r-\frac{1}{2}r^{\frac{q}{2}}
.

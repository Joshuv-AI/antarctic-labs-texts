The square of a subcubic planar graph without a 5-cycle is 7-choosable
Seog-Jin Kim, Xiaopan Lian, Atsuhiro Nakamoto, Kenta Ozeki

Abstract
The square of a graph G G , denoted G 2 G^{2} , has the same vertex set as G G and has an edge between two vertices if the distance between them in G G is at most 2 2 . Thomassen [ 12 ] showed that χ ⁡ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph. A natural question is whether χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 or not if G G is a subcubic planar graph. Recently Kim and Lian [ 11 ] showed that χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph of girth at least 6. And Jin, Kang, and Kim [ 10 ] showed that χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph without 4-cycles and 5-cycles. In this paper, we show that the square of a subcubic planar graph without 5-cycles is 7-choosable, which improves the results of [ 10 ] and [ 11 ] .

Preliminary

Let

<

The

P_{G}(\bm{x})=\prod_{u\sim v,u<v}(x_{u}-x_{v}),

where

It is easy to see that a mapping

\bm{c}=\big(c(v)\big)_{v\in V(G)}

Therefore, to find a proper coloring of

so that

.
The following theorem, which was proved by Alon and Tarsi, gives sufficient conditions for the existence of such assignments as above.

Theorem 6

(
[
1
]
)
.

(Combinatorial Nullstellensatz) Let
\mathbb{F}
be an arbitrary field and let
f=f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Suppose that the degree
\deg(f)
of
f
is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}
where each
t_{i}
is a nonnegative integer, and suppose that the coefficient of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{t_{i}}
of
f
is nonzero. Then if
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{n}
are subsets of
\mathbb{F}
with
|S_{i}|\geq t_{i}+1
, then there are
s_{1}\in S_{1}
,
s_{2}\in S_{2}
,…,
s_{n}\in S_{n}
so that
f(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n})\neq 0
.

In particular, a graph polynomial

is a homogeneous polynomial and
\deg(P_{G})
is equal to
|E(G)|
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
L:V(G)\to 2^{\mathbb{N}}
be a list.
Then
\bm{c}
is an

if and only if

,
where
\bm{c}=(c(v_{1}),c(v_{2}),\dots,c(v_{n}))

Therefore, if there exists a monomial

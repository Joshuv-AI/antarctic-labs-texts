Values of ternary quadratic forms at integers and the Berry-Tabor conjecture for 3-tori Thanks: JM’s research was supported by EPSRC grant EP/W007010/1. WK was supported by Korea Institute for Advanced Studies (HP101301).
Wooyeon Kim and Jens Marklof and Matthew Welsh

Abstract
. Berry and Tabor conjectured in 1977 that spectra of generic integrable quantum systems have the same local statistics as a Poisson point process. We verify their conjecture in the case of the two-point spectral density for a quantum particle in a three-dimensional box, subject to a Diophantine condition on the domain’s proportions. A permissible choice of width, height and depth is for example 1 , 2 1 / 3 , 2 − 1 / 3 1,2^{1/3},2^{-1/3} . This extends previous work of Eskin, Margulis and Mozes (Annals of Math., 2005) in dimension two, where the problem reduces to the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture for quadratic forms of signature ( 2 , 2 ) (2,2) . The difficulty in three and higher dimensions is that we need to consider the distribution of indefinite forms in shrinking rather than fixed intervals, which we are able to resolve for special diagonal forms of signature ( 3 , 3 ) (3,3) in various scalings, including a rate of convergence. A key step of our approach is to represent the relevant counting problem as an average of a theta function on SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ) 3 \ SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) 3 \operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})^{3}\backslash\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{3} over an expanding family of one-parameter unipotent orbits. The asymptotic behaviour of these unipotent averages follows from Ratner’s measure classification theorem and subtle escape of mass estimates.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Berry-Tabor conjecture

A principle motivation for this work is the Berry-Tabor conjecture
[
BT77
]
, which asserts that the statistical correlations in the quantum spectrum of a generic, classically integrable system are that of a Poisson point process, i.e., are maximally uncorrelated. In contrast, the statistics of quantum systems with “chaotic” classical limit are expected – according to Bohigas, Giannoni and Schmit
[
BGS84
]
– to be governed by one of the Gaussian random matrix ensembles, where the choice of ensemble depends on the time-reversal symmetries of the system. Despite overwhelming numerical evidence and major theoretical advances, there is currently no complete proof of either phenomenon for a single example.

We will here investigate the validity of the Berry-Tabor conjecture for a specific model, a particle confined to a
d
-dimensional box with edge lengths
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d}
. The particle’s classical motion is along straight lines with elastic reflection at the boundary. This motion lifts to the Kronecker flow on a
d
-torus obtained by reflection on all faces of the box, and is therefore completely integrable. The standard quantisation of this dynamical system is given by the Hamiltonian
H=-\Delta_{D}
, where
\Delta_{D}
is the Dirichlet Laplacian on the cuboid
[0,\ell_{1}]\times\cdots\times[0,\ell_{d}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The spectrum

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\to\infty
of the quantum Hamiltonian
H=-\Delta_{D}
is given by the values of the positive definite quadratic form

(1.1)

Q_{0}(m)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}m_{j}^{2},\qquad x_{j}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell_{j}^{2}},

where
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})
runs over the integer vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with positive coefficients.

The spectrum of the Laplacian for the above
d
-torus (comprising
2^{d}
copies of the box) is represented by the same quadratic form, but
m
now runs over all of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Our results therefore apply directly to this case. In fact, the reflection symmetry of the rectangular torus under the dihedral group
D_{2h}
allows a decomposition of its spectrum according to the one-dimensional unitary representations of
D_{2h}
. These are in turn realised by the Laplacian with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions on the various faces of the original box.

Weyl’s law for the spectrum of
H=-\Delta_{D}
tells us that

(1.2)

\#\{j:\lambda_{j}<T\}\sim\frac{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\omega_{d}\,T^{d/2}\qquad(T\to\infty),

where
\omega_{d}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In dimension
d=2
the law is linear in
T
and hence the average spacing between consecutive elements is asymptotically constant. This is no longer the case in dimension three and higher. Thus, in order to measure correlations on the correct local scale, we unfold the spectrum by setting

(1.3)

\xi_{j}=\frac{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\omega_{d}\,\lambda_{j}^{d/2}.

This simple rescaling produces a sequence whose average spacing is now asymptotic to constant one.

For
T>0
, the two-point (or pair correlation) measure
\mathcal{R}_{T}
of the unfolded, truncated sequence
(\xi_{j}\mid\xi_{j}<T)_{j}
is defined by

(1.4)

\mathcal{R}_{T}(A)=\#\{(i,j)\mid i\neq j,\;\xi_{i}<T,\;\xi_{j}\in A+\xi_{i}\},

with
A\subset\mathbb{R}
a bounded Borel set. We are interested in the asymptotic behaviour for large
\xi
, and say that the two-point measure of the full sequence
(\xi_{j})_{j}
is Poissonian if

(1.5)

\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{R}_{T}(A)}{T}=|A|

for any Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
with
|\partial A|=0
. Here
|A|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
A
.
The reason for this terminology is that the above holds almost surely if
(\xi_{j})_{j}
is a realisation of a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
of intensity one.

We say that a real number
x
is
\kappa
-Diophantine for some
\kappa\geq 1
if there exists a finite constant
C>0
such that
|x-\tfrac{r}{q}|\geq Cq^{-\kappa-1}
for all integers
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
. For example, all quadratic surds are
1
-Diophantine, and all irrational algebraic numbers are
\kappa
-Diophantine for every
\kappa>1
(Roth’s theorem). Furthermore, for any given
\kappa>1
, the set of
\kappa
-Diophantine numbers has full Lebesgue measure.

The following theorem proves the Berry-Tabor conjecture for the second-order statistics of the spectrum of the Hamiltionan
H=-\Delta_{D}
. Equivalently, it states that the two-point correlations of the values of the degree
d
homogeneous form
Q_{0}(m)^{d/2}
at integers
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{d}
are Possonian.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\xi_{j})_{j}
be the deterministic sequence given by the unfolded spectrum (
1.3
) of the Dirichlet Laplacian for a box in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with edge lengths
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
.
If the ratios
\ell_{i}^{2}/\ell_{j}^{2}
are
\kappa
-Diophantine for all
i\neq j
with
\kappa
sufficiently close to 1, then the pair correlation measure of
(\xi_{j})_{j}
is Poissonian.

By Roth’s theorem, the conditions of the theorem are satisfied, e.g., for
\ell_{1}=1
and algebraic numbers
\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
so that
\ell_{2}^{2},\ell_{3}^{2},\ell_{2}^{2}/\ell_{3}^{2}\notin\mathbb{Q}
. This includes the example given in the abstract. The analogous statement was proved by Eskin, Margulis and Mozes
[
EMM05
]
for positive definite binary quadratic forms
Q_{0}
, under similar Diophantine conditions, where (as we will discuss further below) this is a special – and particularly difficult – case of the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture.
The necessary unfolding for
d\geq 3
requires a different approach from that taken in
[
EMM05
]
or in the more recent effective version by Lindenstrauss, Mohammadi and Wang
[
LMW23b
]
.
We will here use a similar strategy as in
[
Mar02
,
Mar03b
]
and translate the problem to convergence of unipotent averages over Siegel theta functions associated to
Q_{0}
.
Theta functions were also used in connection with quantitative versions of the Oppenheim conjecture, as well as the Davenport-Lewis conjecture, for quadratic forms with five or more variables in the work of Götze
[
Göt04
]
and Buterus, Götze, Hille and Margulis
[
BGHM22
]
.

It is well known that the Diophantine conditions in Theorem
1.1
are satisfied for
\ell_{1}=1
and almost every (in the sense of Lebesgue measure) choice of
\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
. Our theorem therefore strengthens, in dimension
d=3
, recent results by Blomer and Li
[
BL23
]
proved for almost all rectangular tori.
Previous proofs of the Poisson nature of almost all flat tori are due to
[
Sar97
]
in dimension 2, and VanderKam
[
Van99b
,
Van99a
,
Van00
]
in dimension
d\geq 4
. VanderKam
[
Van00
]
also proves convergence to the Poissonian
k
-point correlation measure, provided
d\geq 2k
.

Triple and higher correlation densities are much less understood in the deterministic setting, i.e., under explicit conditions on the edge lengths
\ell_{i}
. The best current results for binary forms are due to Aistleitner, Blomer and Radziwiłł
[
ABR24
]
. The problem of understanding the distribution of gaps
\xi_{j+1}-\xi_{j}
appears to be equally difficult; cf. Bourgain, Blomer, Rudnick and Radziwiłł
[
BBRR17
]
.

The Diophantine conditions in Theorem
1.1
are likely not sharp. Note, however, the statement evidently fails for all rational length ratios, when the two-point measure diverges. Following Sarnak
[
Sar97
]
, see also
[
Van99b
,
Van99a
,
Van00
,
Mar02
,
Mar03a
,
Mar03b
]
, one can in fact show that the divergence persists for a set of second Baire category of edge lengths, i.e., topologically generic boxes.

1.2.
Uniform variant of the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture

Instead of unfolding the sequence as in (
1.3
), we can also count values in shrinking intervals to compensate for the increasing mean density. This will lead to a uniform variant of the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture of Eskin, Margulis and Mozes
[
EMM98
,
EMM05
]
, which we will prove here for the special quadratic forms of signature
(3,3)
given by

(1.6)

Q(m)=Q_{0}(m_{1})-Q_{0}(m_{2}),\qquad m=(m_{1},m_{2}),

where
Q_{0}
is the positive definite ternary diagonal form in (
1.1
).

Denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Schwartz class of functions
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. For
M,L>0
,
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
we define the counting measure
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}
by

(1.7)

\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}F(M^{-1}m)\psi(LQ(m)),

and extend this to Borel measurable functions as usual. Thus, if
F
and
\psi
are characteristic functions of a unit ball and interval
[a,b]
, respectively, then
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}
describes the distribution of values of
Q(m)
in the interval
[a/L,b/L]
as
m
runs over all lattice points
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in the ball of radius
M
. In the classical setting of the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture
[
EMM98
,
EMM05
]
,
L
is fixed. A key observation is that in the signature
(2,2)
case Diophantine conditions on the coefficients are necessary, whereas for signature
(3,1)
and indefinite forms in more than four variables, only irrationality of the form is required.
However, this changes
if we count in shrinking intervals, i.e.,
L\to\infty
as
M\to\infty
at suitable rates.

In view of (
1.2
), the mean spacing between the values of
Q_{0}(m)
is about
M^{-(d-2)}
, and hence we expect
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)
to be of size
\asymp M^{d}\times M^{d-2}/L=M^{2d-2}/L
. The scaling
L=M^{d-2}
is the critical scaling which captures the local correlations relevant to Theorem
1.1
, and we will see below that the following statement in fact implies the former as a corollary.

Let
\mathrm{e}(z)=\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}z)
, and denote by
D_{2h}
the symmetry group of a generic rectangular cuboid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, centred at the origin, with
\operatorname{ord}D_{2h}=2^{d}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
d=3
.
If the ratios
x_{i}/x_{j}
are
\kappa
-Diophantine for all
i\neq j
with
\kappa
sufficiently close to 1, then for
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
L,M\geq 1
,

(1.8)

\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)=\frac{M^{2d-2}}{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(v)\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi Q(v))\mathrm{d}v\bigg)\mathrm{d}\xi\\
+M^{d}\,\psi(0)\sum_{\sigma\in D_{2h}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F((u,\sigma(u)))\mathrm{d}u+O_{F,\psi}\bigg(\frac{M^{2d-2-\delta}}{L}\bigg)+o_{F,\psi}(M^{d}).

for any
\delta<\tfrac{1}{\kappa+1}
.

We remark that for the special choice
F(u_{1},u_{2})=\varphi_{1}(Q_{0}(u_{1}))\varphi_{2}(Q_{0}(u_{2}))
with
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, (
1.8
) becomes

(1.9)

\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)=\frac{M^{2d-2}}{L}\,\frac{d^{2}}{2}\,\frac{\omega_{d}^{2}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{1}(\lambda)\varphi_{2}(\lambda)\lambda^{d-2}d\lambda\\
+2^{d}M^{d}\,\frac{d}{2}\,\frac{\omega_{d}}{(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})^{1/2}}\psi(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{1}(\lambda)\varphi_{2}(\lambda)\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\mathrm{d}\lambda+O_{F,\psi}\bigg(\frac{M^{2d-2-\delta}}{L}\bigg)+o_{F,\psi}(M^{d}).

If
L=M^{d-2}
, Theorem
1.1
now follows from (
1.9
) by the approximation argument of
[
Mar02
, Section 2]
. (Note that we need to take into account the dihedral symmetry, which explains the additional factor of
2^{d}
.)

As remarked earlier, Eskin, Margulis and Mozes established (
1.8
) (without a rate) for
L
fixed and
d\geq 3
in
[
EMM98
]
only assuming at least one of the ratios
x_{i}/x_{j}
is irrational, and for
d=2

[
EMM05
]
allowing
\kappa
-Diophantine ratios for any
\kappa\geq 1
.
Also for
d=2
and
\kappa
-Diophantine ratios, Lindenstrauss, Mohammadi and Wang have proved a version of (
1.8
) with an effective bound for the remainder, allowing for
L
to grow as a small power of
M
.

The approach taken in
[
EMM05
]
requires
L
fixed and the bound for remainder in
[
LMW23b
]
depends quite sensitively on
L
, so taking
L
as large as
M^{d-2}
for
d\geq 3
requires a different strategy.
Here we use Fourier inversion on
\psi
to transform
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)
into a unipotent average of a theta function.
The advantage is that
L
appears only in the length of the unipotent average and not implicitly in the test functions.
This feature is used in a similar way in
[
Mar02
,
Mar03b
]
and as well as by Buterus, Götze, Hille and Margulis
[
BGHM22
]
.

Buterus, Götze, Hille and Margulis
[
BGHM22
]
obtain convergence rates of the form (
1.8
) for general non-singular quadratic forms (both definite and indefinite) with more than five variables for expanding (
L\to 0
) and shrinking intervals (
L\to\infty
), as
M\to\infty
under Diophantine conditions similar to those in
[
EMM05
]
.
While our work provides a stronger rate of convergence in the special case we consider,
the key breakthrough is that we can deal with the scaling regime
M,L\to\infty
with
L\geq M^{d-2-\delta}
, which is when the second term on the right hand side of (
1.8
) dominates the error term. This includes the critical scaling
L=M^{d-2}
required for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
A further advantage of our approach, particularly relevant to the calculation of the second term, is the deployment of theta sums with general cut-off functions
F
, via the Shale-Weil representation, not only classical theta series with Gaussian weights as in
[
BGHM22
]
.

As mentioned above, a rate of convergence for signature
(2,2)
, in the critical scaling (
L
fixed), was recently obtained by Lindenstrauss, Mohammadi and Wang
[
LMW23b
]
as an application of their ground-breaking work on effective Ratner equidistribution
[
LMW22
,
LMW23a
]
.
Strömbergsson and Vishe
[
SV20
]
obtained rates of convergence for the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture for special inhomogeneous forms studied in
[
Mar02
,
Mar03b
]
. The quantitative Oppenheim conjecture for general inhomogeneous forms was proved by Mohammadi and Margulis
[
MM11
]
without rate of convergence. More is known if one restricts to error rates valid for almost every quadratic form of a given signature; see the papers by Bourgain
[
Bou16
]
, Ghosh and Kelmer
[
GK17
,
GK18
]
, Athreya and Margulis
[
AM18
]
, and Ghosh, Kelmer and Yu
[
GKY22
,
GKY23
]
.

1.3.
Escape of mass and Margulis functions

Our strategy for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to relate
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)
to homogeneous dynamics on
\Gamma^{d}\backslash G^{d}
,
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
,
G=\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, via a theta function
\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}
defined on the semi-direct product of a Heisenberg group with
G^{d}
using the Shale-Weil representation. The connection between the counting function
\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)
and theta function
\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}
is given by

(1.10)

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{M,L}(F,\psi)&=\frac{1}{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\psi}(L^{-1}\xi)\bigg(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}F(M^{-1}m)\mathrm{e}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi Q(m)\big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{M^{d-2}}{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\psi}(L^{-1}\xi)\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}\big(1,g_{0}n(\xi)a(\log M)g_{0}^{-1}\big)\mathrm{d}\xi,\end{split}

where
\hat{\psi}
is the Fourier transform of
\psi
,

(1.11)

\hat{\psi}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(x)\mathrm{e}(-\xi x)\mathrm{d}x.

The theta function is evaluated at the product of the elements

(1.12)

g_{0}=\left(\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&x_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j\leq d}\in G^{d},

and

(1.13)

n(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad a(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{-t}&0\\
0&\mathrm{e}^{t}\end{pmatrix}\in G,

where we identify
G
with its diagonal embedding in
G^{d}
.

Our analysis of the unipotent average over
\xi
in (
1.10
) is significantly complicated by the unboundedness of the theta function.
In fact, the first term on the right of (
1.8
) comes from the contribution of
\xi
of size at most
\tfrac{\epsilon}{M}
for a small
\epsilon>0
, see Section
3.1
.
We view this part of the integral as analogous to a “major arc” in the circle method. This is analogous to the approach taken in
[
Mar02
,
Mar03b
]
and
[
BGHM22
]
.

The remaining
\xi
, the “minor arc,” contributes the second term in (
1.8
), which represents the “diagonal” term in the two-point density.
This is obtained from the equidistribution of the translated unipotent segment.
As results on the equidistribution of unipotent flows, e.g. Ratner’s theorems, typically require bounded functions, we need to control the escape of mass in (
1.10
).
We prove the following new upper bound for the Margulis function on the space of three-dimensional lattices, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
d=3
.
If the ratios
x_{i}/x_{j}
are
\kappa
-Diophantine for all
i\neq j
with
\kappa
sufficiently close to 1,
then for any
0<\delta<\tfrac{1}{\kappa+1}
,
\eta>0
sufficiently small and
1\leq L\leq M^{d-2}
, there is
\beta>1
such that

(1.14)

\frac{1}{L}\int_{M^{-1-\eta}<|\xi|\leq L}\alpha_{3}(g_{0}n(\xi)a(\log M))^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi=O_{\beta,\kappa}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{L}M^{d-2-\delta}\bigg).

Here
\alpha_{d}(g)
is a Margulis function, introduced in the proof of the quantitaive Oppenheim conjecture
[
EMM98
]
. It measures how high
\Gamma^{d}g
is in the cusp of
\Gamma^{d}\backslash G^{d}
. As for Theorem
1.2
, we expect the above bound to be true also for any
d\geq 4
under suitable Diophantine conditions.

We note that for
L>M^{1-\delta}
the average (
1.14
) is bounded.
In comparision,
[
BGHM22
]
obtains bounds for similar (more general) averages of size roughly
M^{3\beta-2}
, which holds without Diophantine conditions.
The stronger estimate (
1.14
) in the special case we consider is main source for the improvements in Theorem
1.2
over the results of
[
BGHM22
]
.
We remark that Diophantine conditions are used in
[
BGHM22
]
to obtain pointwise bound for the Margulis functions, whereas the proof of Theorem
1.3
uses the Diophantine condition in an essential way in controlling the average.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is given in Section
4
.
Our strategy begins simlarly to
[
EMM98
, Section 5]
.
First a global contraction of
\alpha_{3}(g)^{\beta}
under translates of unipotent segments (Proposition
4.6
) is proved by combining purely local calculations with geometric properties of the cusp of
\Gamma^{3}\backslash G^{3}
, and then (
1.14
) is proved by iterating the global contraction.

However, a straightforward adaptation of
[
EMM98
]
only establishes (
1.14
) for
\beta<\tfrac{2}{3}
while
\beta>1
is required for (
1.10
).
The lack of contraction for
\beta>\tfrac{2}{3}
is already evident in the local calculations and is related to possible repeated roots of a certain polynomial.
We regain contraction away from an exceptional set by modifying the Margulis function to account for the distance between roots of this polynomial using a strategy developed in
[
Kim25
]
.
The bound (
1.14
) for
\beta>1
is then obtained by iterating the contraction and by controlling the contribution of the exceptional set by an avoidance estimate (Proposition
5.1
).
Our proof of this avoidance estimate uses the above-mentioned work by Lindenstrauss, Mohammadi, and Wang
[
LMW23a
, Theorem 1]
on the polynomial effective equidistribution of one-parameter unipotent flows on
\Gamma^{2}\backslash G^{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Theta functions and Shale-Weil representation

The Heisenberg group
H_{d}
is defined to be the set
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
with multiplication given by

(2.1)

(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})=(x_{1}+x_{2},y_{1}+y_{2},z_{1}+z_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut y_{2}}-x_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut y}_{1})),

where vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are represented as rows.
As
\operatorname{Sp}(d,\mathbb{R})
preserves the symplectic form
x_{1}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut y}_{2}-x_{2}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut y}_{1}
,
g\in G^{d}
acts on
H_{d}
by automorphisms

(2.2)

h^{g}=\left(\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}g,z\right),\ \mathrm{where\ }h=(x,y,z).

The group
H_{d}\rtimes G^{d}
is then defined to have multiplication

(2.3)

(h_{1},g_{1})(h_{2},g_{2})=(h_{1}h_{2}^{g_{1}^{-1}},g_{1}g_{2}).

The Schrödinger representation
W
of
H_{d}
acts on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the unitary transformations

(2.4)

[W_{d}(x,y,z)f](t)=\mathrm{e}(-z+\tfrac{1}{2}x\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut y})f(t+x).

For
g\in G^{d}
, we obtain another unitary representation
W^{g}
of
H_{d}
by
W^{g}(h)=W(h^{g})
. By the Stone-von Neumann theorem on the unitary equivalence of infinite-dimensional, irreducible representations of
H_{d}
, there is an operator
R_{d}(g)
that intertwines
W
and
W^{g}
, i.e., for which

(2.5)

W_{d}^{g}=R_{d}(g)^{-1}W_{d}R_{d}(g).

These
R_{d}(g)
yield the projective Shale-Weil representation of
\operatorname{Sp}(d,\mathbb{R})
, which is central in the construction of Siegel theta functions associate with a quadratic form in
d
variables. Since we are dealing here with diagonal forms only, it suffices to consider a subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}(d,\mathbb{R})
, which we identify with the
d
-power of
G=\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{Sp}(1,\mathbb{R})
via the embedding

(2.6)

G^{d}\to\operatorname{Sp}(d,\mathbb{R}),\qquad\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}&b_{j}\\
c_{j}&d_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{1\leq j\leq d}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&&&b_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&&&\ddots&\\
&&a_{d}&&&b_{d}\\
c_{1}&&&d_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&&&\ddots&\\
&&c_{d}&&&d_{d}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The Shale-Weil representation
R_{d}
restricted to
G^{d}
is then given as the
d
th tensor product of
R_{1}
. That is, for
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in G^{d}
, we have

(2.7)

R_{d}(g)=R_{1}(g_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes R_{1}(g_{d}).

Proposition
2.1
gives an explicit method for computing unitary operators
R_{1}(g)
on a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
For

(2.8)

p=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

and any
g\in G
, we have

(2.9)

R_{1}(p)f(x)=|a|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}abx^{2})f(ax).

Moreover, for
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G
with
c\neq 0
, we have

(2.10)

R_{1}(g)f(x)=|c|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{a}{2c}x^{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{d}{2c}y^{2}-\tfrac{1}{c}xy)\mathrm{d}y.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the theta function
\Theta_{f}
is defined by

(2.11)

\Theta_{f}(h,g)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}[W(h)R_{d}(g)f](m).

We observe that for
1=(0,0,0)\in H_{d}
and
g=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{j}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&y_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!1\leq j\leq d}
,

(2.12)

\Theta_{f}(1,g)=(y_{1}\cdots y_{d})^{\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\left((m_{j}y_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}})_{j}\right)\mathrm{e}\bigg(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}m_{j}^{2}\bigg).

We now extend the above to a unitary representation
R_{d,d}(g)
of
G^{d}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2g})

as the tensor product

(2.13)

R_{d,d}(g)=R_{d}(g)\otimes[CR_{d}(g)C],

where
C
denotes complex conjugation. We highlight that
R_{d,d}
is (unlike
R_{1}
, say) an actual representation for all
d
. The corresponding theta function is defined, for
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, by

(2.14)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(h,g)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}[W(h)R_{d,d}(g)F](m).

For example, for
F=f\otimes\overline{f}
we have

\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(h,g)=|\Theta_{f}(h,g)|^{2}
.
Furthermore, for
g
as in (
2.12
), we have the explicit formula

(2.15)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)=(y_{1}\cdots y_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}}F\left((m_{j}y_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}})_{j},(n_{j}y_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}})_{j}\right)\mathrm{e}\bigg(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}(m_{j}^{2}-n_{j}^{2})\bigg).

2.2.
Automorphy of theta functions

Define

(2.16)

\bar{\Gamma}=\{((m,n,z)h_{\gamma},\gamma)\subset H_{d}\rtimes G^{d}:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\ z\in\mathbb{R},\gamma\in\Gamma^{d}\},

where for

Theorem 2.2
.

For

and

\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}\big(\bar{\gamma}(h,g)\big)=\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(h,g).

Note that in particular

2.4.
Diophantine property

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
g_{0}=\left(\begin{pmatrix}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&x_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}
with
\tfrac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}

\kappa
-Diophantine for
i\neq j
.
Then for any
\eta>0
, if
\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\eta)t}<|\xi|\leq 10
, then
\alpha_{3}(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))\ll\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{3\kappa}{\kappa+1}+3\eta)t}
.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}n(\xi)a(t)=(g_{j})_{j}
,
\gamma(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))=\left(\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
c_{j}&d_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}
.
We may assume using Lemma
2.4
(ii) that
t
is large enough so that
c_{j}\neq 0
.
We set
\rho(g_{j})=\delta_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
and we note that
|\xi|\ll 1
implies that
|d_{j}|\ll|c_{j}|\ll\delta_{j}\mathrm{e}^{t}
.

Let us suppose that
d_{j}\neq 0
.
From Lemma
2.4
(i) and the Diophantine condition we have

(2.32)

(\delta_{i}\delta_{j}\mathrm{e}^{2t})^{-\kappa}\ll|c_{i}d_{j}\tfrac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}-c_{j}d_{i}|\ll\delta_{i}\delta_{j},

so
(\delta_{i}\delta_{j})^{-1}\ll\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\kappa}{\kappa+1}t}
.
On the other hand, if

, we have

(2.33)

\delta_{j}^{-1}\ll\mathrm{e}^{-t}+|\xi|^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{t}\ll\mathrm{e}^{\eta t}.

The claim follows.
∎

The following lemma connects the classical Diophantine notion with that used in
[
LMW23a
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
L\geq 1
,
g_{0}=\left(\left(\begin{matrix}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&x_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{matrix}\right)\right)_{\!\!j=1,2}\in G^{2}
and assume that
\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}
is
\kappa
-Diophantine.
Then for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and for any closed orbit

, where

embedded diagonally,

\operatorname{dist}_{\Gamma^{2}\backslash G^{2}}(g_{0}n(\xi),gH)\gg(\operatorname{Vol}(gH))^{-D}

for some constant
D=D(\kappa)>1
.

Proof.

Let

\tilde{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi_{j}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2}.

We may write
\Gamma^{2}g_{0}n(\xi)=\Gamma^{2}g_{0}\tilde{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
, for suitable bounded
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

It is therefore sufficent to show that
\operatorname{dist}_{\Gamma^{2}\backslash G^{2}}(g_{0}\tilde{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),gH)\gg(\operatorname{Vol}(gH))^{-D}

for
|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|\ll 1
.

We may assume
g=(\mathrm{id},h)
.
We note that if for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
,
g_{1}Ag_{2}^{-1}=A
for all
(g_{1},g_{2})\in gHg^{-1}
, then
A
is a multiple of
h
.
Since
\Gamma^{2}\cap gHg^{-1}
is Zariski dense, then the same holds if
\gamma_{1}A\gamma_{2}^{-1}=A
for all
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}\cap gHg^{-1}
.
Since
\gamma_{1}h\gamma_{2}^{-1}=h
for
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\Gamma^{2}\cap gHg^{-1}
defines a system of integer linear equations with a one-dimensional set of solutions, it follows that
h=(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}A
with
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2\times 2}
.

We may assume the entries of
A
are coprime.
Since
\Gamma\cap h^{-1}\Gamma h
is conjugate in
\Gamma
to
\Gamma_{0}(\det A)
, where

\Gamma_{0}(N)=\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma:c\equiv 0\bmod N\}

is the Hecke congruence subgroup of level
N
.
The index of
\Gamma_{0}(\det A)
in
\Gamma
is
\geq|\det(A)|
, so
|\det A|\ll\operatorname{Vol}(gH)
.

Suppose
\operatorname{dist}_{\Gamma^{2}\backslash G^{2}}(g_{0}\tilde{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),gH)\leq\epsilon
.
Then there is
\gamma\in\Gamma^{2}
,
h_{1}\in G
with
|h_{1}-\mathrm{id}|\ll\epsilon
, and
h_{2}\in G
such that
g_{0}\tilde{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})(\mathrm{id},h_{1})=\gamma(\mathrm{id},h)(h_{2},h_{2})
.
It follows that

(2.34)

\begin{pmatrix}(\tfrac{x_{1}}{x_{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}&(x_{1}x_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})\\
0&(\tfrac{x_{1}}{x_{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\det A}}A^{\prime}+O(\epsilon).

for

.
Inspecting the
(2,2)
-entry, the Diophantine condition on

implies

∎

=\tfrac{1}{L}\mathrm{e}^{dt}\int_{|\xi|\leq\epsilon\mathrm{e}^{-t}}\hat{\psi}(\tfrac{1}{L}\xi)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F^{0}(u,u^{\prime})\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi\mathrm{e}^{2t}(u\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}-u^{\prime}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\xi+O_{f,\psi,A}(\epsilon^{A}).

The inner integral over
\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}
is
O_{F}((1+|\xi|\mathrm{e}^{2t})^{-d})
, so replacing
\hat{\psi}(\tfrac{1}{L}\xi)
with
\hat{\psi}(0)
creates an error bounded by

(3.11)

\ll_{\psi,F}L^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{dt}\int_{|\xi|\leq\epsilon\mathrm{e}^{-t}}\frac{|\xi|}{(1+|\xi|\mathrm{e}^{2t})^{d}}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll L^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{(d-4)t}.

After changing variables
\xi\leftarrow\mathrm{e}^{-2t}\xi
, we have that (
3.10
) is

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\tfrac{1}{L}\mathrm{e}^{(d-2)t}\hat{\psi}(0)\int_{|\xi|\leq\epsilon\mathrm{e}^{t}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F^{0}(u,u^{\prime})\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(u\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}-u^{\prime}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\qquad+O_{F,\psi,A}(\epsilon^{A}+L^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{(d-4)t})\\
&=\tfrac{1}{L}\mathrm{e}^{(d-2)t}\hat{\psi}(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F^{0}(u,u^{\prime})\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(u\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}-u^{\prime}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&\qquad+O_{F,\psi,A}(\epsilon^{A}+L^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{(d-4)t}+L^{-1}\epsilon^{-d+1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}).\end{split}

Finally, we recall that
F_{0}=R_{d,d}(g_{0}^{-1})F
, which yields after the appropriate variable substitution

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F^{0}(u,u^{\prime})\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(u\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}-u^{\prime}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\\
&=(\det X_{0})^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(uX_{0}^{-1/2},u^{\prime}X_{0}^{-1/2})\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi(u\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}-u^{\prime}\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u}^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(v)\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\xi Q(v))\mathrm{d}v.\end{split}

∎

3.2.
Remaining

To analyze the integral (

We let

We note that from Theorem

\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x=(x_{j})_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

q\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut x}\neq 0
for any nonzero

For

Moreover, by the condition on

\mathbb{R}^{d}/2\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Since
\tilde{n}(\xi)
for

parametrizes the full horospherical subgroup for

, it follows that the set
\{\Gamma^{\prime}\tilde{n}(\xi)a(t):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/2Z^{d}\}
is dense in in

.
It follows that
\{\Gamma^{\prime}g_{0}n(\xi)a(t)g_{0}^{-1}:\xi\in\mathbb{R}\}
is dense in

.
∎

By a theorem of Shah
[
Sha96
]
, Lemma
3.2
implies that for any bounded continuous function
\varphi
on
\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}
,

(3.14)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{L}\int\hat{\psi}(\tfrac{\xi}{L})\varphi(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))\mathrm{d}\xi=\psi(0)\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}}\varphi(g)\mathrm{d}\mu(g),

where

is the

-invariant probability measure on

.
We now use Theorem
1.3
(proved below) to extend (

) to the unbounded theta function.
We specialize to

.

We let
\mathcal{C}_{y}=\{g\in\Gamma^{3}\backslash G^{3}:\alpha_{3}(g)\geq y\}
, and we let

be a continuous function on

satisfying

.
By theorems
2.5
and

, we have

(3.15)

\tfrac{1}{L}\int_{|\xi|\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\eta)t}}|\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))|\,\chi_{y}(g_{0}n(\xi)g(t))\mathrm{d}\xi\\
\leq\tfrac{1}{L}y^{1-\beta}\int_{|\xi|\geq\epsilon\mathrm{e}^{-t}}\alpha_{d}(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll y^{1-\beta}(1+\tfrac{1}{L}M^{(3\beta-2)(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}+\eta)})

for some

.

On the other hand,
|\Theta(h,g)|^{2}(1-\chi_{y}(g))\ll y
, so (
3.14
) implies that

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\tfrac{1}{L}\int_{\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\eta)t}\leq|\xi|\leq L}\psi(\tfrac{1}{L}\xi)\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))(1-\chi_{y}(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t)))\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\tfrac{1}{L}\int_{|\xi|\leq L}\psi(\tfrac{1}{L}\xi)\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))(1-\chi_{y}(g_{0}n(\xi)a(t)))\mathrm{d}\xi+O_{\psi}(\tfrac{1}{L}y\mathrm{e}^{-t})\\
&\underset{t\to\infty}{\to}\psi(0)\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)(1-\chi_{y}(g))\mathrm{d}\mu(g)\\
&=\psi(0)\int_{\Gamma_{d}\backslash G^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)\mathrm{d}\mu(g)+O(y^{-1}),\end{split}

where the last line follows from

Combining this with (

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{L}\int_{\epsilon\mathrm{e}^{-t}\leq|\xi|\leq L}\psi(\tfrac{1}{L}\xi)\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g_{0}n(\xi)a(t))\mathrm{d}\xi\\
=\psi(0)\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)\mathrm{d}\mu(g).

Together with Lemma
3.3
below, this gives the second term in (
1.8
).

Lemma 3.3
.

For
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,

,

(3.18)

\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)\mathrm{d}\mu(g)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{2h}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x,\sigma(x))\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

As
\tilde{n}
parametrizes the full horospherical subgroup associated to

, it follows from the mixing property of

via standard arguments (e.g. Margulis’ thickening trick) that

\int_{\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash G^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,g)\mathrm{d}\mu(g)=\lim_{t\to\infty}2^{-d}\int_{[0,2]^{d}}\Theta_{F}(1,\tilde{n}(\xi)a(t))\mathrm{d}\xi.

From (
2.12
) we have

(3.20)

\begin{split}&2^{-d}\int_{[0,2]^{d}}\widetilde{\Theta}_{F}(1,\tilde{n}(\xi)a(t))\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=2^{d}\mathrm{e}^{-dt}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}F(m_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-t},m_{2}e^{-t})\int_{[0,2]^{d}}\mathrm{e}(\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\xi_{j}(m_{1,j}^{2}-m_{2,j}^{2}))\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\mathrm{e}^{-dt}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\sigma\in D_{2h}}F(m\mathrm{e}^{-t},\sigma(m)e^{-t}).\end{split}

As

∎

4.
Escape of mass

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
assuming the avoindance estimate (Proposition
5.1
) proved in Section

.

4.1.
Local contraction

The main result of this section is Lemma
4.3
, which establishes a local contraction property of the height functions
\rho_{k}
, compare to
[
KM99
, Lemma 5.1]
.
In our setting, we modify these height functions to also measure the distance to an exceptional set on which
\rho_{k}^{-\beta}
does not contract for
\beta>\tfrac{2}{k}
.
In this way, we obtain contraction for the modified functions for
1<\beta<2
.

The distance to the exceptional set is controlled by the following functions.
For
g=\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{j}&b_{j}\\
c_{j}&d_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}\in G^{3}
we define

(4.1)

\Delta_{2}(g)=\min_{j=1,2,3}\max_{i\neq j}\min(1,|\tfrac{d_{i}}{c_{i}}-\tfrac{d_{j}}{c_{j}}|)

and

(4.2)

\Delta_{3}(g)=\min_{\!\!j=1,2,3}\prod_{i\neq j}\min(1,|\tfrac{d_{i}}{c_{i}}-\tfrac{d_{j}}{c_{j}}|),

where here and in what follows, if
c_{i}c_{j}=0
, we take
1
in corresponding minimum.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g=\left(\begin{pmatrix}r_{j}&-s_{j}\\
s_{j}&r_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}\in\operatorname{SO}(2)^{3}
.
For any
s>0
, the measure of the set of
\xi\in[-1,1]
such that
\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq\delta
is
O(\min(\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{e}^{-s},\delta\Delta_{2}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-s},\delta\Delta_{3}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-3s}))
.

Proof.

We note that since
r_{j}^{2}+s_{j}^{2}=1
, if
s_{j}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
, we have
|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|\asymp 1
for
\xi\in[-1,1]
.
Therefore

(4.3)

\begin{split}\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))&=\prod_{j=1}^{3}|(\mathrm{e}^{-s}s_{j},\mathrm{e}^{s}(r_{j}+s_{j}\xi))|\\
&\gg\mathrm{e}^{3s}\prod_{s_{j}^{2}>\tfrac{1}{10}}|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|\gg\mathrm{e}^{3s}\prod_{s_{j}^{2}>\tfrac{1}{10}}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|,\end{split}

and we may assume that product is nonempty since otherwise the lemma is trivial.

Let
j
be so that
|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|
is minimal among
j
so that
s_{j}^{2}>\tfrac{1}{10}
.
If
|\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|>2
, then the lemma is again trivial.
It follows that (
4.3
) is

(4.4)

\gg\mathrm{e}^{3s}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|^{3}.

We also have that (
4.3
) is

(4.5)

\gg\mathrm{e}^{3s}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}^{2}>\tfrac{1}{10}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|\tfrac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}-\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|\gg\mathrm{e}^{3s}\Delta_{3}(g)|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|,

where the last bound follows from
|\tfrac{r_{i}}{s_{i}}-\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|\gg 1
if
s_{i}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
.

The first and third bounds claimed in the lemma follow from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).
For the second claimed bound, we aim to show

(4.6)

\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\gg\mathrm{e}^{s}\Delta_{2}(g)|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|

with
j
be as before.

Without loss of generality we assume
j=3
.
If both
s_{1}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
and
s_{2}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
, then
\Delta_{2}(g)=\Delta_{3}(g)=1
, so (
4.6
) follows from (
4.5
).
If
s_{1}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}<s_{2}^{2}
, then from the left of (
4.3
) we have

(4.7)

\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\geq\mathrm{e}^{s}|s_{2}||r_{1}+s_{1}\xi||r_{3}+s_{3}\xi|\gg\mathrm{e}^{s}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{3}}{s_{3}}|,

and (
4.6
) follows similarly if
s_{2}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}<s_{1}^{2}
.

Finally, if both
s_{1}^{2},s_{2}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
, we assume without loss of generality that
|\tfrac{r_{1}}{s_{1}}-\tfrac{r_{3}}{s_{3}}|\geq|\tfrac{r_{2}}{s_{2}}-\tfrac{r_{3}}{s_{3}}|
.
We have

(4.8)

\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\geq\mathrm{e}^{s}|s_{2}||r_{1}+s_{1}\xi||r_{3}+s_{3}\xi|\gg\mathrm{e}^{s}|\tfrac{r_{1}}{s_{1}}-\tfrac{r_{3}}{s_{3}}||\xi+\tfrac{r_{3}}{s_{3}}|,

so (
4.6
) follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
s>0
,
\beta>1
, and
g\in G
, we have

(4.9)

\int_{-1}^{1}\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\min(\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-2)s},\Delta_{2}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-4)s},\Delta_{3}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-6)s})\rho_{k}(g)^{-\beta}.

Proof.

We note if
g^{\prime}=\left(\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}
, then
\rho_{3}(g^{\prime}g)=\rho_{3}(g^{\prime})\rho_{3}(g)
, so we may assume
g=\left(\begin{pmatrix}r_{j}&-s_{j}\\
s_{j}&r_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!j=1,2,3}\in\operatorname{SO}(2)^{3}
, where
\rho_{3}(g)=1
.
We note that since
r_{j}^{2}+s_{j}^{2}=1
, it follows that if
s_{j}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
, then
|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|\gg 1
for all
\xi\in[-1,1]
.
It follows that
\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\gg\mathrm{e}^{-(3-2k)s}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-3s}
, where
k
is the number of
j
such that
s_{j}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
.
We also note that
\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\ll\mathrm{e}^{3s}
.

We decompose the region of integration according to
\xi
such that
\delta<\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq 2\delta
ranges dyadically from
\mathrm{e}^{-3s}
to
\mathrm{e}^{3s}
(
\delta=c2^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-3s}
,
0\leq j\ll s
, and
c>0
a small constant).
We obtain

(4.10)

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\gg\mathrm{e}^{-3s}\\
\mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}\delta^{-\beta}\mathrm{meas}\{\xi\in[-1,1]:\rho_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq\delta\}\\
&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\gg\mathrm{e}^{-3s}\\
\mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}\delta^{-\beta}\min(\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{e}^{-s},\delta\Delta_{2}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-s},\delta\Delta_{3}^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-3s})\\
&\ll\min(\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-2)s},\Delta_{2}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-4)s},\Delta_{3}(g)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{(3\beta-6)s}),\end{split}

the second bound following from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

We set

(4.11)

\phi(g)=\Delta_{3}(g)^{-1}\rho_{3}(g)^{-\beta}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
g\in G
,
s>0
, and
\beta>1
, we have

(4.12)

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(gn(\xi)a(s))\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\mathrm{e}^{-3(2-\beta)s}\phi(g).

Proof.

We observe that

(4.13)

\Delta_{3}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{-1}\Delta_{3}(g)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{-4s}&\mathrm{if\ }\Delta_{2}(g)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2s}\\
\mathrm{e}^{-2s}\Delta_{2}(g)&\mathrm{if\ }\Delta_{3}(g)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2s}\leq\Delta_{2}(g)\\
\Delta_{3}(g)&\mathrm{if\ }\mathrm{e}^{-2s}\leq\Delta_{3}(g).\end{cases}

Applying the bounds in Lemma
4.2
to each of these cases (in the corresponding order) implies (
4.12
).
∎

We end this section by recording the following lemma establishing a weaker bound than Lemma
4.3
, but for a larger range of
\beta
that we omitted from the proof of Lemma
4.3
for the sake of exposition.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
d\geq 1
,
1\leq k\leq d
,
s>0
,
\beta>\tfrac{1}{k}
, and
g\in G^{d}
, we have

(4.14)

\int_{-1}^{1}\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\mathrm{e}^{-(2-\beta k)s}\rho_{k}(g)^{-\beta}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, for
g^{\prime}=\left(\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
0&*\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!1\leq j\leq d}
, we have
\rho_{k}(g^{\prime}g)=\rho_{k}(g^{\prime})\rho_{k}(g)
, so we may assume that
g=\left(\begin{pmatrix}r_{j}&-s_{j}\\
s_{j}&r_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\!\!1\leq j\leq d}\in\operatorname{SO}(2)^{d}
, where we have
\rho_{k}(g)=1
.

We have

(4.15)

\rho_{k}(g)\asymp\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\dots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\max_{J_{1}\subset J}\mathrm{e}^{-(k-2|J_{1}|)s}\prod_{j\in J\setminus J_{1}}|s_{j}|\prod_{j\in J_{1}}|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|.

Let
J_{0}
be the set of
j
such that
s_{j}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
, so that if
j\in J_{0}
,
|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|\asymp 1
for
\xi\in[-1,1]
.
Taking
J_{1}=J_{0}\cap J
in (
4.15
) shows that
\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\gg\mathrm{e}^{-(k-|J_{0}\cap J|)s}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-ks}
.

On the other hand, we claim that the measure of
\xi\in[-1,1]
such that
\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq\delta
is
\ll\delta^{\frac{1}{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-s}
.
Taking
J_{1}=J
, we have

(4.16)

\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\gg\mathrm{e}^{ks}\prod_{j\in J\setminus J_{0}}|r_{j}+s_{j}\xi|\gg\mathrm{e}^{ks}\prod_{j\in J\setminus J_{0}}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|.

Let
j\in J\setminus J_{0}
be so that
|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|
. If
|\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|\geq 2
then the claim is trivial.
Otherwise we have

\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\gg\mathrm{e}^{ks}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|^{|J\setminus J_{0}|}\gg\mathrm{e}^{ks}|\xi+\tfrac{r_{j}}{s_{j}}|^{k},

and the claim follows in this case as well.

Decomposing
\xi\in[-1,1]
according to
\tfrac{1}{2}\delta<\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq\delta
, it follows that

(4.17)

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\gg\mathrm{e}^{-ks}\\
\mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}\delta^{-\beta}\mathrm{meas}\{\xi\in[-1,1]:\rho_{k}(gn(\xi)a(s))\leq\delta\}\\
&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\gg\mathrm{e}^{-ks}\\
\mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}\delta^{\frac{1}{k}-\beta}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\ll\mathrm{e}^{-(2-\beta k)s},\end{split}

as required.
∎

4.2.
Global contraction

We first prove a crude bound for averages of
\alpha_{d}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}
.
We note that this bound does not exhibit contraction for
\beta\geq\tfrac{2}{d}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
. For any
g\in G^{d}
,
s>0
, and
\beta>\tfrac{1}{d}
, we have

(4.18)

\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha_{d}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\mathrm{e}^{(\beta d-2)s}\alpha_{d}(g)^{\beta}+\mathrm{e}^{\beta s}\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha_{d-1}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}d\xi.

In particular, we have

(4.19)

\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha_{d}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\mathrm{e}^{(\beta d-2)s}\alpha_{d}(g)^{\beta}+\mathrm{e}^{\beta ds}.

Proof.

We let
\gamma(\xi)=\gamma(gn(\xi)a(s))
and we note that (
4.18
) follows from the following

(4.20)

\rho_{d}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\xi\ll\rho_{d}(\gamma(g)g)^{\beta}+\mathrm{e}^{2s}\alpha_{d-1}(gn(\xi)a(s))^{\beta}

together with Lemma
4.4
.

If

In order to obtain a contraction, we define a global modified height function

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

Proposition 4.8

\mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}\delta^{-\beta}\sum_{1\leq c\ll\delta\mathrm{e}^{t}}\sum_{0<|d|\ll|c|}\tfrac{\delta}{|c|}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\ll 1,

For

Performance Studies on machine learning based channel modelling for vehicular visible light communication
Authors: L Ramya, K Umadevi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCar headlights and taillights, to provide cost-effective and high-data-rate interference-resilient communication, have spawned Vehicular Visible Light Communication (V2LC), a possible additional technology to radio-frequency systems. Since they can be formulated in an empirical or deterministic way, traditional optical channel models can be easily adapted to changing vehicle environments. To counter the weakness of the conventional modelling techniques, the paper examines channel modelling techniques grounded in machine learning and suited to V2LC scenarios. A few variables, such as the shape of LEDs, speed, road topology, and atmospheric perturbations, do significantly influence the received signal strength in vehicular optical channels that are highly non-linear in nature and vary over time. To solve these issues, the paper gives Machine Learning-based Vehicular Visible Light Communication Channel Modelling (ML-V2LC-CM), a hybrid learning system as a proposed framework. The regression models and ensemble learning of the framework and deep neural predictors are all integrated, which can be used to estimate the channel gain, path loss, and signal distortion. Compared to baseline empirical models, experimental tests show that ML-V2LC-CM is significantly more effective. The framework performs well and has the highest performance of 18.7% in terms of improvement in prediction, 22.4% in terms of reduction in Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) estimation error of less than 1.6 dB, and low prediction latency of 4-7ms. It is very robust when blocked with a degradation rate of 6–9% and gives over 92% generalization behaviour under a variety of lighting conditions and provides fusion-layer improvement of 11.3% greater than the best standalone model. Introduction

Background and motivation

Vehicular visible light communication has recently become a topic of much interest as a promising alternative to radio-frequency (RF) communications in Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS)1. Using V2LC, high-speed data communication becomes achievable by taking advantage of the lighting infrastructure that is already present in vehicles, such as headlights, taillights, and traffic lights2. The benefits that come with this infrastructure include immunity to electromagnetic interference, enhanced security, and low cost of deployment3. The current and upcoming advancements in smart mobility and autonomous vehicles are increasing the need to have vehicular communication that is reliable, has a high capacity, and is low-latency4. Due to this, V2LC is considered to be a possible solution to the situation when the communication between the vehicles and the infrastructure is involved5.

Need for accurate channel modelling in V2LC

Whether any communication system is successful, accurate channel modelling is essential, especially in the dynamics of vehicles6. Vehicle-to-light communication channels are more prone to environmental conditions such as dust, fog, and lightning strikes as compared to optical communication systems that are stationary or employed indoors7. These factors make the optical wireless channel very nonlinear and time varying8. Although they are still used, the traditional analytical and empirical channel models tend to simplify and are not applicable to real-world situations that are not simple9. This restriction affects the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) and the Bit Error Rate (BER), as well as the general communication reliability10.

Limitations of conventional channel models

The deterministic optical models often involve lengthy physical measurements and complex ray-tracing calculations and are hence difficult to scale11. However, empirical models are unable to adapt when there is a shift in the environment or the dynamics of the vehicle12. The two approaches have a problem in modelling noise sources, LED emission patterns, object reflections, and multi-path effects13. Models that learn data well, adapt, and predict in real time are highly demanded because settings of vehicles are becoming even more automated and altered14.

Machine learning (ML) as a solution

Eventually, recent advances in ML have increased the number of opportunities for data-driven channel modelling15. ML methods can overcome the limitations in conventional models by learning complex patterns based on measurement data, as well as various nonlinear relationships25. Neural networks, hybrid predictors, and regression ensembles techniques could predict channel gain, signal distortions, and path loss with considerable accuracy26. In a wide range of conditions, when they are trained using a wide range of vehicle datasets, this is why V2LC settings are the best in ML-based modelling27,28.

Related works

Having a high bandwidth, security capabilities, and the capacity to work without radio frequency (RF), Visible Light Communication has been a fast-paced wireless system of communication in vehicles, indoors, and at great altitudes. Some of the latest research in VLC has been in smart transportation systems, networks of manlift aerial vehicles, localization, and energy-saving communication.

Sikder et al.16 introduce an ITS-VLC analysis framework to evaluate the current state of the art and current challenges of integrating visible light communication with intelligent transport systems. The paper emphasizes the need to have ML-based channel prediction and outlines VLC-ITS limitations that relate to vehicle motility, optical noise, and real-time flexibility. To install ITS in the future that relies on optical wireless networks with high efficiency, their survey offers a roadmap by unifying the state-of-the-art VLC techniques, future topologies, and hardware limitations.

To maximize the placement of aerial nodes to improve the coverage and communication reliability, Wang et al.17 offer a deep learning-based UAV-VLC deployment approach (DL-UAV-VLC). The method learns optimal flight configurations and illumination patterns through convolutional neural networks that are combined with optimization layers. The findings demonstrate the potential of DL-guided UAV positioning for the next-generation VLC networks and significant improvements in SNR dispersion and coverage uniformity.

Xing et al.18 present an indoor VLC network energy-saving rate-splitting multiple-access strategy (RSMA-VLC) with a focus on spectral efficiency and minimization of power budget. Optical power constraints, user fairness, and beamforming are all considered at the same time in their design. RSMA-VLC is a scalable multiple-access scheme to support high-density VLC indoor systems, which has high throughput and reduced energy consumption, based on iterative optimization algorithms.

Experimental validation was conducted using practice drone-mounted LED transmitters to aid in the development of a UAV-based VLC channel model (UAV-VLC-CM) by Wang et al.19. Their findings indicate that channel fluctuations that are important and that are both height and motion dependent are observed. The channel model improves the accuracy of predictions of mobile aerial VLC applications by using the path loss and the noise features, which are measured.

Developing the topic of channel properties, modulation strategies, deployment concerns, and emerging trends, Mapunda et al.20 provide a full Homeland indoor VLC tutorial (Indoor-VLC-TS). They consider optical and RF channels and indicate where VLC is safer than RF. The paper identifies challenges that affect system design, and they consist of factors such as shadowing, multi-path reflections, and distortions as a result of mobility.

Cheng et al.21 introduce B5G-V2X-CM in their paper, a model of beyond-5G vehicular channels, which relies on detailed information to assess the dynamics of the propagation in vehicles. They investigate Doppler effects, multi-path structures, obstruction patterns, and all of these.

Játiva et al.22 propose an underground VLC channel model (UG-VLC-SM) that considers the scattering and shadowing effects that are characteristic of mining tunnels. Their model considers non-standard geometries, variation in surface reflectance, and dust attenuation.

Intelligent reflecting surface-assisted VLC, or IRS-VLC, is one of the new advances in optical wireless networks that Sun et al.22 discussed. They consider the ability of programmable optical surfaces to enhance signal-to-noise ratio, direct beams, and reduce interference. There is a hint that IRS-VLC can significantly improve link robustness and coverage, particularly in Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) or blocked conditions.

Based on photodiode measurements and camera sensing, Bai et al.23 introduce CV-VLC-Loc, a computer vision localization method in VLC. To be able to determine pinpoint accuracy to within millimetres, they adopted a hybrid solution that incorporated picture characteristics as well as VLC signals. This technology has a lot of potential in indoor navigation, robotics, and autonomous systems that are supported by optical wireless positioning.

Imoize et al.24 conduct a detailed study of standard MIMO propagation models (MIMO-PM), which represent space correlation, fading statistics, and multi-antenna behaviour. Although the methods used in the modelling are applied to RF channels, the modelling may apply to multielement optical systems. Their review gives credence to cross-domain model adaptation by illuminating the way in which classical MIMO theory can inform complex VLC channel models.

The literature that has been reviewed has registered significant progress in the modelling of the VLC channels, optimizing the performance, and ensuring their mobility, flexibility, and smart integration into the systems. The advancements in the fields of efficient multiple-access frameworks, computer-vision-based localization, UAV-based VLC networks, and machine-learning-related deployment are worthy of mention. All of these works refer to the revolutionary edge of VLC in hybrid and vehicle wireless networks; however, they suggest challenges of its implementation in the real world because of the problem of alignment, channel variation, energy efficiency, and so forth and the overall comparison of these papers are given in Table 1.

Research gap

The available literature in the field of VLC, V2X channels, Rate-Splitting Multiple Access (RSMA) systems, and MIMO-based prediction models is mainly concerned with single-channel behaviour, restricted mobility, or within single-model prediction strategies. The majority of approaches have problems with rapid channel variations, multi-obstacle surroundings, and cross-scenario generalization. Furthermore, existing algorithms do not often incorporate multi-model fusion, attention, and hybrid learning to improve the prediction accuracy. Thus, an integrated, resilient, and flexible channel prediction framework is only loosely studied.

The suggested ML-V2LC-CM framework is an improvement of a mere collaboration of regression, ensemble, and deep learning models, as it presents the adaptive fusion mechanism guided by attention to dynamically weight the model outputs in accordance with the real-time conditions of the vehicle and its surrounding environment. It adds a loop of confidence-aware iteration refinement, thus allowing the system to re-process low-confidence predictions in order to gain better reliability in highly dynamic V2LC situations. The framework is also more data-driven, unlike traditional hybrid methods, it incorporates physics-based Lambertian optical modelling with data, to have superior generalization and interpretability. Additionally, multi-domain spatio-temporal feature synchronization pipeline and multi-task prediction strategy enables both channel gain, path loss and distortion to be estimated simultaneously and with high accuracy. All these innovations have distinguished the suggested framework with the available hybrid machine learning models of wireless channel prediction.

Proposed methodology

This section presents the proposed ML-V2LC-CM framework, designed to address the challenges of modelling highly dynamic and nonlinear vehicular visible light communication (V2LC) channels. Unlike conventional approaches, the proposed method adopts a structured multi-stage pipeline that integrates data preprocessing, feature extraction, hybrid machine learning, and adaptive fusion within a unified architecture. The framework combines physics-based optical modelling with data-driven learning to capture both theoretical propagation characteristics and real-world variations. Furthermore, an attention-based fusion mechanism and a confidence-aware refinement strategy are incorporated to enhance prediction accuracy and robustness under varying vehicular and environmental conditions. The following subsections describe each component of the framework in detail, ensuring clarity, logical flow, and reproducibility.

The suggested ML-V2LC-CM approach combines data-driven processing and sophisticated learning models to respond to the non-linear dynamic property of the visible light communication channels of vehicles. Through a balanced combination of structured data learning, machine-learned hybrid and boosted prediction modelling, the framework ensures resilience to mobility, weather conditions, and optical distortions, and the prediction of channels with a level of confidence, thus providing the next-generation vehicular communication systems with reliable channel estimations.

Bayesian optimization is used to optimize the hyperparameters of the machine learning models. In contrast to grid search or random search, Bayesian optimization builds a probabilistic surrogate model of the objective function and uses it to steer the search towards valuable areas of the hyperparameter space. In this, the surrogate model is a Gaussian Process (GP) which is employed to estimate the correlation between hyperparameters and model performance (measured by validation RMSE). To trade off exploration and exploitation, an Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition functionality is applied to estimating the next set of hyperparameters that optimizes the expected improvement. Each iteration requires updating the surrogate model with the past-examined configurations, and the acquisition function identifies the next candidate point. This cycle is repeated several times, within which the optimal hyperparameters are efficiently converged.

The ML-V2LC-CM framework combines regression, ensemble learning, deep neural models, and attention-based fusion to provide accurate and flexible V2LC channel predictions. The system minimizes error and improves the generalization, as well as stability in the changing vehicle environment, through optimized training and cross-model correlation. The approach facilitates effective V2LC implementation because it records the rich channel behaviour and enhances communication characteristics.

Results and discussion

The results section provides an in-depth analysis of the proposed hybrid fusion-driven channel prediction framework in varying conditions of vehicular and environmental factors. To confirm the enhancement in the accuracy, robustness, and generalization, multiple baseline models, statistical measures, and performance indicators are compared. The findings indicate that the fusion architecture improves the stability of prediction, minimizes error, and maximizes flexibility in changing speeds, distance, and lighting conditions.

The suggested ML-V2LC-CM framework is run with a clearly laid out architectural and training settings. Regression models involve Linear Regression, Support Vector regression and random forest. There are ensemble models such as XGBoost and AdaBoost. The deep learning module consists of a DNN and three hidden layers a 1D-CNN, and two-layer temporal sequence modelling LSTM. The received signal strength (RSS), path loss, delay spread, incidence/irradiance angles, vehicle speed, acceleration, LED geometry parameters, and ambient light intensity, are input features that are normalized with min-max scaling and temporally aligned with 100 ms sliding windows separated by 20 ms intervals. The data is divided into 70% training, 15% validation and 15% testing. The optimization is done by AdamW optimizer and learning rate 1 × 103, batch size 256 and early stopping 100 epochs. Bayesian optimization is applied to the hyperparameters after 50 trials. The fusion algorithm applies an attention-based weighting model, in which model outputs are fused with learned normalized weights based on a ridge-based meta-learner. Each and every model is being rolled out in Python with PyTorch and Scikit-learn and the tests are being run on a system equipped with an NVIDIA GPU to guarantee smooth training and inference.

Dataset

This dataset contains the synchronized camera records of events and frames, LED markers, and ground-truth pose and illumination data, which captures dynamic LED behaviour in motion and changing light. Though it is created with a VLC-based localization study, it provides rich temporal, spatial, and light-intensity data that can be reused in training and testing channel estimation models with vehicular Visible Light Communication systems.

The application of the E-VLC dataset, developed on the principle of VLC-based localization, is explained by the fact that this dataset is rich in the representation of temporal, spatial, and optical signal variations that are directly related to channel modelling. In particular, the data offers high-resolution LED light patterns, optical signal variations in events, vehicle movement paths, and ambient light level, all of which play a significant role to channel gain, path loss, and distortion in V2LC systems. These raw values are systematically converted into channel-related features in this work with physics-guided preprocessing where received signal strength, attenuation trends, and noise features are obtained based on known optical propagation principles. Moreover, feature engineering and temporal synchronization allow you to extract dynamic channel behaviour in different conditions of mobility and in different conditions of light. Accordingly, although it was not designed to be used in channel modelling, the dataset can be viewed as a valid proxy, when used in conjunction with the suitable feature transformation and modelling strategies, as it allows realistic and data-driven V2LC channel estimation.

V2LC channel modelling is a regression problem, therefore root mean square error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) and coefficient of determination (R2) are the major evaluation metrics. The term prediction accuracy used in the previous sections is the relative error reduction relative to a base model as a percent change in RMSE. Likewise, the value of combined accuracy (98.6) is equal to the normalized goodness of fit using R2 score (R2 = 0.986) which is much larger, or more agreeable, between the predicted and actual channel values. The generalization behavior is measured by the model performance when applied to unseen test situations such as varying speeds, light and noise conditions and is quantified by the RMSE and the consistency of the R2 at those situations. These explanations make sure that all metrics that are covered by these reports are in line with the norms of the standard practice of evaluating regression and can be interpreted in the context of channel prediction.

Experiments were carried out on five independent runs, in which the initializations and data shuffling are random. The findings are given in mean ± standard deviation of all the measures of evaluation. Also, 95% confidence ranges are calculated to estimate the variability of performances of models in the course of runs. To determine statistical significance between the proposed ML-V2LC-CM framework and baseline models (RF, XGBoost, DNN), a paired t-test is conducted, and p-values below 0.05 denote a statistically significant improvement. The error variance of the repeated experiments is low, which proves the stability and strength of the proposed approach.

Ablation of study

Every component of the ML-V2LC-CM architecture is addressed in the ablation study with the purpose of determining its role in the channel modelling accuracy. Having built a model using regression alone, the modules that followed, including ensemble learning, feature-optimized preprocessing, and deep neural prediction layers, were added. The results indicate that feature optimization enhances stability in prediction by 15% and the noise sensitivity is decreased by 12% when using ensemble learning. The deep neural predictor cuts down the RMSE by 22% in the case of high-mobility conditions, and this benefit is the largest. The combination of ML-V2LC-CM offers the most successful results in regard to the generalizability and robustness, which proves that each of the modules is a necessity to achieve an accurate V2LC channel estimate.

Limitations

The latter still has several limitations, albeit that ML-V2LC-CM significantly enhances the prediction of V2LC channels. With the optical attenuation patterns not equal to the training distribution, e.g., in thick fog or heavy rain, performance could deteriorate. Poor representation of unusual vehicle behaviours may affect generalization because the system requires vast labelled data. Implementing deep models on low-power vehicle hardware in real-time is difficult because it incurs a high computational cost. Moreover, assumptions made by various car manufacturers regarding the largely constant LED emission patterns in the framework may vary slightly. To overcome these limitations in unpredictable conditions of light, lightweight designs, more datasets, and adaptive processes are required.

Conclusion and future works

In an attempt to address the weaknesses of traditional deterministic and empirical V2LC channel models, this study presents ML-V2LC-CM, a model that is machine-learning-driven. The proposed approach is able to capture the nonlinear, time-varying characteristics of vehicle optical channels by combining feature engineering, ensemble regression, and deep neural prediction layers. According to the findings of the experimental tests, there are notable improvements as compared to the baseline models. Such advances as a reduction in prediction error by 35% and an augmented capacity to perform under high-mobility conditions are included. Judging by these results, data-driven strategies can be used to make the vehicular VLC communication systems more versatile and reliable. Despite such benefits, barriers still exist. The Deep learning parts may impose additional resource demands on vehicles that are already weak in managing resources, and the model itself is not as useful in uncommon or harsh conditions due to its reliance on other datasets. The collection was underrepresented with a number of real-world VLC aberrations, such as dirt, occlusions, and aged LEDs.

Future plans include looking into lightweight and hardware-efficient architectures, like transformer-based predictors made for edge devices, pruning, and quantization. Domain adaptation and semi-supervised learning will be employed to increase generalization under unexpected conditions. Other priorities will involve the incorporation of weather-adaptive optical modelling and increasing the dataset by using large-scale field tests. Finally, the real-life applicability of the system will be confirmed by applying and experimenting with real-time V2X to 6G vehicle communication topologies.

Strong convergence of finite element schemes for the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch equation
Agus L. Soenjaya

Abstract
. The dynamics of magnetisation in a bounded ferromagnet in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} ( d = 1 , 2 d=1,2 ) at high temperatures can be described by the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (sLLB) equation, which is a vector-valued quasilinear stochastic partial differential equation. In this paper, assuming adequate regularity of the initial data, we establish strong convergence in L 2 ​ ( Ω ) L^{2}(\Omega) of several semi-implicit and implicit fully discrete finite element schemes for the sLLB equation, together with explicit convergence rates. The analysis relies on localised error estimates and new exponential moment bounds for the exact solution. As a by-product, these moment bounds yield mean-square exponential stability of solutions and uniqueness of the invariant measure in one spatial dimension under a small noise assumption. We also sharpen existing convergence-in-probability results for the numerical schemes. Numerical experiments are presented to illustrate and support the theoretical findings.



1.
Introduction

The study of micromagnetics at elevated temperatures has gained interest in recent years due to its wealth of applications. A modern implementation of this theory leads to the development of HAMR (heat-assisted magnetic recording), a state-of-the-art hard drive with unprecedented capacity and reading/writing speed
[
18
,
23
,
31
]
. At high temperatures, noise-induced transitions between equilibrium states and the effects of thermal fluctuations in a ferromagnet become important, thus it is necessary to consider a stochastic model for micromagnetics to take these into account
[
12
,
10
,
24
]
.

The dynamics of magnetisation vector field
\boldsymbol{u}:[0,T]\times\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
in a ferromagnet (a bounded domain)
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for temperatures above the Curie temperature, assuming the effective field is dominated by the exchange field, is commonly described by the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (sLLB) equation
[
6
,
10
,
11
]
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=\left(\kappa_{1}\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\Delta\boldsymbol{u}-\kappa_{2}(1+\mu\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2})\boldsymbol{u}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\kappa_{1}\boldsymbol{g}+\gamma\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\circ\mathrm{d}W,

with an initial condition
\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D})
and a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition
\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
\partial\mathscr{D}
. The physical constants
\kappa_{1},\gamma,\kappa_{2}
, and
\mu
are all positive, and the vector field
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathscr{D})
is given for some
s\geq 2
to be specified. The coefficient
\boldsymbol{g}
encodes the intensity and spatial structure of the thermal fluctuations driving the magnetisation dynamics, with its magnitude determined by the system temperature through the fluctuation-dissipation relation
[
8
]
. Here,
W
is a real-valued Wiener process on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
with respect to the usual filtration and
\circ
denotes the Stratonovich differential. Countable number of Wiener processes following the framework in
[
6
,
14
]
can also be considered, but we consider here only a single Brownian motion without loss of generality, just for simplicity of presentation. Note that (
1.1
) is a vector-valued quasilinear stochastic PDE.

Existence of a martingale weak solution for (
1.1
) is shown in
[
19
]
, while the existence of a unique pathwise strong solution for
d\leq 2
is shown in
[
6
]
.
A fully-discrete numerical scheme has been proposed in
[
14
]
to solve the sLLB equation for
d\leq 2
. For
d=1
, a
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-conforming finite element scheme is proposed to discretise (
1.1
) directly, while for
d=2
, a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-conforming finite element scheme is proposed to discretise a regularised version of (
1.1
), namely:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}=\left(-\varepsilon\Delta^{2}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}+\kappa_{1}\Delta\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}+\gamma\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}\times\Delta\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}-\kappa_{2}(1+\mu\left|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2})\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\kappa_{1}\boldsymbol{g}+\gamma\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\circ\mathrm{d}W,

with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, equipped with an initial condition
\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(0)=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathscr{D})
and homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions
\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\partial_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\Delta\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
\partial\mathscr{D}
. Such regularisation is necessary since higher order regularity results for (
1.1
) are not known for
d=2
. In all cases, convergence in probability with certain rates are shown for the proposed numerical methods. The strong solution of (
1.2
) is then shown to converge to that of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

It is worth noting that (
1.2
) is a special case of the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Baryakhtar (sLLBar) equation, another stochastic micromagnetic model at elevated temperatures analysed in
[
16
]
. We also mention that when
\boldsymbol{g}=\boldsymbol{0}
, problem (
1.1
) reduces to the deterministic LLB equation
[
20
]
for which several numerical schemes have been developed
[
21
,
27
,
28
]
; see also
[
26
]
for results in the regime below the Curie temperature. When
\boldsymbol{g}=\boldsymbol{0}
, problem (
1.2
) reduces to a special case of the deterministic LLBar equation
[
29
,
27
,
30
]
.

We elaborate available numerical results for (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in more detail. Let
h
and
k
denote the mesh size and time step, respectively. For
d=1
, it has been shown in
[
14
]
that a semi-implicit
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-conforming finite element method applied directly to (
1.1
) converges strongly in the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm to the exact solution, up to a discrete stopping time, at rate
O\big(h^{\frac{1}{2}}+k^{\frac{1}{4}-\delta}\big)
for any
\delta>0
, assuming
\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
h\leq Ck
. This also implies convergence in probability with the same rate. For
d=2
, a semi-implicit
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-conforming method for the regularised system (
1.2
) achieves a rate of
O\big(h^{\frac{1}{2}}+k^{\frac{1}{8}-\delta}\big)
, assuming
\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
.
This similarly leads to a convergence in probability with the same rate. Related work
[
15
]
has also established convergence of a mixed finite element method for the sLLBar equation, which includes (
1.2
) as a special case, using a truncation approach to handle the nonlinearities.

However, under the analytic and stochastic frameworks of
[
14
]
, the convergence rates in probability of the considered finite element schemes do not appear to be optimal, and strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is only established up to a discrete stopping time.
In this paper, we build on these results and extend them in several directions. First, we prove sharper convergence rates for the proposed numerical methods: the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-conforming scheme attains an
\mathbb{L}^{2}
rate of convergence of
O\big(h+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\big)
, while the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-conforming scheme achieves the rate
O\big(h^{2}+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\big)
, both in probability and strongly over a large sample space. Second, for the one-dimensional case (
d=1
), we propose a new implicit finite element scheme and prove an optimal convergence rate in probability with respect to a stronger norm, namely
O\big(h+k^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\big)
measured in the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
norm. Finally, we establish strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with explicit, though suboptimal, rates for all the aforementioned schemes.

We note that problem (
1.1
) is of quasilinear type, while (
1.2
) is of semilinear type, both containing non-globally Lipschitz and non-monotone nonlinearities. Therefore, general results on numerical schemes for monotone stochastic PDEs from
[
17
,
22
]
, for instance, do not apply and any error analysis must proceed by ad-hoc arguments due to the nature of the nonlinearities.
Differently from
[
14
]
, we make use of the localisation technique in
[
7
,
4
]
to derive error estimates over a large sample space. For the semi-implicit schemes in particular, we ensure that the sample space depends only on the exact solution (and not on the finite element solution) to obtain a better global rate of convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, making use of a newly obtained exponential moment bound for the exact solution. Given the form of the nonlinearities, the estimates are nontrivial and have to be done more carefully.
In the case
d=1
, the newly proposed implicit scheme attains an optimal rate of convergence in probability. This is achieved by proving a stronger stability result, which in turn yields a sharper error estimate in a stronger norm. Numerical experiments are presented to corroborate our theoretical results.

In summary, the main results of this paper are:

(i)

exponential moment bounds for
d\leq 2
(Theorem
3.5
), which imply mean-square exponential stability and uniqueness of the invariant measure for
d=1
under sufficiently small noise;

(ii)

sharper convergence rates for the schemes proposed in
[
14
]
, over a large sample space (Corollaries
4.3
and
5.3
) and in probability (Corollaries
4.4
and
5.4
);

(iii)

strong convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for these schemes with rates (Theorems
4.5
and
5.5
), whose proofs rely on exponential moment bounds for the exact solution;

(iv)

a new implicit finite element scheme for
d=1
, where an optimal rate of convergence in probability (Corollaries
6.5
and
6.6
) and a strong convergence is shown (Theorem
6.7
).

(i)

Section

2.
Notations

We begin by defining some notations used in this paper. Let
\mathscr{D}
be an interval (if
d=1
) or a convex polygonal domain (if
d=2
). The function space
\mathbb{L}^{p}:=\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathscr{D};\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the usual space of
p
-th integrable functions defined on
\mathscr{D}
and taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\mathbb{W}^{k,p}:=\mathbb{W}^{k,p}(\mathscr{D};\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the usual Sobolev space of
functions on
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We
write
\mathbb{H}^{k}:=\mathbb{W}^{k,2}
.
The partial derivative

\partial/\partial x_{i}
will be written by
\partial_{i}
for short. The partial derivative of
f
with respect to time
t
will be denoted by
\partial_{t}
. The operator
\Delta
(or
\partial_{xx}
if
d=1
) denotes the Neumann Laplacian acting on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued functions with domain

\text{D}(\Delta):=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}:\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}{\partial\boldsymbol{n}}=0\text{ on }\partial\mathscr{D}\right\}.

If
X
is a Banach space,
L^{p}(0,T;X)
and
W^{k,p}(0,T;X)
denote respectively the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces of strongly measurable functions on
(0,T)
taking values in
X
. Similarly, the space
L^{p}(\Omega;X)
denotes the space of
X
-valued strongly measurable random variables with finite
p
-th moment, where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space. The space
\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];X)
and
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];X)
denote respectively the space of continuous functions and the space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions on
[0,T]
taking values in
X
. For brevity, we will denote the spaces
L^{p}(0,T;X)
,
\mathcal{C}^{0}([0,T];X)
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];X)
by
L^{p}_{T}(X)
,
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{T}(X)
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}(X)
, respectively.

We denote the scalar product in a Hilbert space
H
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and its corresponding norm by
\|\cdot\|_{H}
. The expectation of a random variable
Y
will be denoted by
\mathbb{E}[Y]
. We will not distinguish between the scalar product of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
vector-valued functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the scalar product of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
matrix-valued functions taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, and denote them by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

In various estimates, the constant
C
in the estimate denotes a
generic constant which takes different values at different occurrences. If
the dependence of
C
on some variables, e.g.
R
and
S
, is highlighted, we will write
C(R,S)
.

Remark 2.1
.

In this work, we restrict ourselves to one-dimensional intervals and two-dimensional convex polygonal domains, where triangulations with straight-edged elements suffice, standard elliptic regularities hold, and the relevant Sobolev embeddings are valid. For domains with smooth boundaries, one could approximate the boundary by polygons (introducing a geometric error) or use isoparametric curved elements, at the cost of more involved analysis (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
). While these approaches are relevant and interesting, we do not pursue them here for simplicity of exposition.

3.
Analytical preliminaries: well-posedness, stability, and invariant measures

First, we collect known results on existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solution to (

) and (
1.2

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle

.

, and suppose that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]

Proof.

Recalling that all coefficients have been set equal to 1, we may write equation (
1.1
) in Itô form as

(3.7)

\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=F(\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}t+G(\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}W,

where

\displaystyle F(\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle:=\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{u}\times\Delta\boldsymbol{u}-(1+|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2})\boldsymbol{u}+\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g},

\displaystyle G(\boldsymbol{u})

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}.

First, we will prove (
3.5
). Applying Itô’s lemma
[
25
]
to the map
\boldsymbol{v}\mapsto\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\left(2\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},F(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle+\left\|{G(\boldsymbol{u})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t+2\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},G(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Noting that
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot(\boldsymbol{a}\times\boldsymbol{b})=0
for any
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},F(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|{G(\boldsymbol{u})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s

(3.8)

\displaystyle\quad=\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+t\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Now, let
\alpha>0
and define
R_{t}:=2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}
. Multiplying (
3
) by
\alpha
and rearranging the terms give

\displaystyle\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha t\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+R_{t}-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Note that
R_{t}
is a martingale with quadratic variation
\langle R\rangle_{t}
, where

\displaystyle\langle R\rangle_{t}\leq 4\alpha^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, for any
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle R_{t}-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq R_{t}-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}\langle R\rangle_{t},\quad\text{where }\,\alpha_{0}:=\big(4\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)^{-1}.

Now, let

X_{T}:=\exp\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(R_{t}-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right].

Noting this, by a standard exponential martingale argument, we obtain for any
K>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[X_{T}\geq e^{K}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(R_{t}-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\geq K\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(R_{t}-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}\langle R\rangle_{t}\right)\geq K\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\left(\frac{2\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}R_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{2\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}R\right\rangle_{t}\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{2\alpha_{0}K}{\alpha}\right)\right]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2\alpha_{0}K}{\alpha}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{2\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}R_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{2\alpha_{0}}{\alpha}R\right\rangle_{T}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2\alpha_{0}K}{\alpha}\right),

where in the last step we used the expected value of the stochastic exponential of
R
.
By (
3
), for any
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}]
, we have
\mathbb{P}(X_{T}\geq e^{K})\leq\exp\left(-2\alpha_{0}K/\alpha\right)\leq e^{-2K}
for any
K>0
. Using this inequality for any
\eta:=e^{K}>1
(so
K>0
), we infer that
\mathbb{E}[X_{T}]\leq 2+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{T}\geq\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq 3
. Therefore, taking supremum over
[0,T]
, applying the exponential, and taking the expected value on (
3
), we obtain (
3.5
).

Next, we aim to prove (
3.5
). Using the notation in (
3.7
), applying Itô’s lemma to the map
\boldsymbol{v}\mapsto\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\left(-2\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u},F(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle+\left\|{\nabla G(\boldsymbol{u})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t-2\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u},G(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u},F(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\nabla\big((\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u},(\boldsymbol{u}\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u},(\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle.

Similarly, noting that
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot(\boldsymbol{a}\times\boldsymbol{b})=0
for any
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla G(\boldsymbol{u})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{g},\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u},G(\boldsymbol{u})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle.

Substituting these expressions back into (
3.11
) and applying Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we infer that

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{|\boldsymbol{u}(s)||\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+4\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),(\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g}+(\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\boldsymbol{g})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{g},\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big(-2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big(-2\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+t\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right|\left|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s+2t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}t\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big(2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle-2\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Multiplying (
3
) by
\beta>0
and rearranging some terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{|\boldsymbol{u}(s)||\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+4\beta t\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,4}}^{4}+\beta t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\beta\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+M_{t}-\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{|\boldsymbol{u}(s)||\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s

(3.13)

\displaystyle=:V_{t}+M_{t}-\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

where
\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{v}\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}:=\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{|\boldsymbol{v}||\nabla\boldsymbol{v}|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
and

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle:=\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+4\beta t\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,4}}^{4}+\beta t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\beta\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle M_{t}

\displaystyle:=2\beta\int_{0}^{t}\big(\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{u}(s)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle-\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{g}\right\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

Let

Y_{T}:=\exp\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(M_{t}-\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right].

Taking supremum of (
3
) over
[0,T]
, then applying the exponential and the expected value, we obtain by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\beta\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s+\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|{|\boldsymbol{u}(s)||\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(V_{T}\right)\cdot Y_{T}\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2V_{T})\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{T}^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We will estimate each term on the last line.
To this end, note that
M_{t}
is a martingale with quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{t}
such that

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle_{t}\leq 8\beta^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, we have for any
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle M_{t}-\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leq M_{t}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta}\langle M\rangle_{t},\quad\text{where }\beta_{1}:=\big(8\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\big)^{-1}.

In view of the above, by repeating the argument used in (
3
), we obtain, for any
K>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[Y_{T}\geq e^{K}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(M_{t}-\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\left\lvert\boldsymbol{u}(s)\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert\hskip-1.0pt\right\lvert^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\geq K\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\left(\frac{2\beta_{1}}{\beta}M_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{2\beta_{1}}{\beta}M\right\rangle_{t}\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{2\beta_{1}K}{\beta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2\beta_{1}K}{\beta}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{2\beta_{1}}{\beta}M_{T}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{2\beta_{1}}{\beta}M\right\rangle_{T}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2\beta_{1}K}{\beta}\right),

where in the last step we used the expected value of the stochastic exponential of
M
.
Hence, for any
\beta\in(0,\beta_{1}/2]
, we have

\mathbb{P}(Y_{T}^{2}\geq e^{K})=\mathbb{P}(Y_{T}\geq e^{K/2})\leq\exp\left(\frac{-\beta_{1}K}{\beta}\right)\leq e^{-2K},\quad\forall K>0.

Using this inequality for any
\eta:=e^{K}>1
(so
K>0
), we infer that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{T}^{2}]\leq 2+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(Y_{T}^{2}\geq\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq 3,\quad\forall\beta\in(0,\beta_{1}/2].

For the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2V_{T})\right]
in (
3
), we use (
3.5
) to infer that for any
\beta\leq\min\left\{\alpha_{0}/\big(2\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\big),2\alpha_{0}\right\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2V_{T})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(2\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+8\beta t\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,4}}^{4}+2\beta t\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(4\beta\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{4}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(2\beta\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+8\beta G_{0}T\right)\cdot\exp\left(\alpha_{0}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha_{0}T\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+3\right).

Substitution of (
3.15
) and (
3
) into (
3
) yields (
3.5
), valid for any

for which the bounds (
3.15
) and (

) hold. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Consequently, we obtain the following mean-square exponential stability result in the

norm for

, provided the noise coefficient is sufficiently small.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
d=1
. Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
are two solutions to (
1.1
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, respectively, driven by the same Wiener process. There exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}
such that, if
\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,4}}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

for some constants
C,\lambda>0
independent of
t
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{v}
are pathwise solutions to (
1.1
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
, respectively. For any
t>0
, let
\boldsymbol{w}(t):=\boldsymbol{u}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}(t)
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{0}:=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{0}
. Note that we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{w}=\left[\boldsymbol{w}\times\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{v}\times\Delta\boldsymbol{w}+\Delta\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{w}-\big((\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\big)\boldsymbol{u}-\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{w}+\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{w}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g}\right]\mathrm{d}t+(\boldsymbol{w}\times\boldsymbol{g})\,\mathrm{d}W.

Applying Itô’s lemma to the map
\boldsymbol{w}\mapsto\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\left(2\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{w},\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle-2\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{w}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-2\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-2\left\langle\left|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}-\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Here, the Itô correction term cancels exactly due to the skew-symmetry of the noise operator, while the stochastic integral vanishes since
\boldsymbol{w}\cdot(\boldsymbol{w}\times\boldsymbol{g})=0
pointwise. By Young’s inequality and the monotonicity property of the map
\boldsymbol{v}\mapsto\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{v}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{w}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{w}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

By the Gronwall lemma and the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
in one spatial dimension, we infer that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\exp\left(-2t+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right).

We will apply inequality (
3.5
), noting that in this case
\alpha_{0}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}
.
Taking expectations in (
3.17
), and assuming that the noise coefficient
\boldsymbol{g}
is sufficiently small such that
\beta_{0}\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}e^{-2t}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\exp\left(-2t+4G_{0}t+\frac{1}{8}t\right),

where the constant
C
is independent of
t
.
Consequently, if in addition
G_{0}<15/32
, then there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{w}_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}e^{-\lambda t}.

We note that a smallness condition on
\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,4}}
is sufficient to guarantee both
\beta_{0}\geq 1
and
G_{0}<15/32
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

) for

. Let

\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}

to be the Lagrange finite element space

(4.1)

\mathbb{V}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C(\overline{\mathscr{D}};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the space of linear polynomials on

taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We define the orthogonal projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

The operator
\Pi_{h}
has the following approximation property: there exists
C>0
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

A semi-implicit finite element scheme proposed in
[
14
]
for the sLLB equation when
d=1
is as follows. Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be the approximation in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
of
\boldsymbol{u}(t)
at time
t=t_{n}:=nk\in[0,T]
, where
n=0,1,2,\ldots,N
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
. We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(0)\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, we find
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
-adapted and
\mathbb{V}_{h}
-valued random variable
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-k\left\langle\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{k}{2}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n},

for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Here,
\overline{\Delta}W^{n}:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,k)
.

The scheme (
4
) is linear and well-posed by the Lax–Milgram lemma. Moreover, it satisfies the following stability property.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
, such that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{l\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{l}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

This is obtained by taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
in (
4
) and applying Jensen’s inequality, noting that
(\boldsymbol{a}\times\boldsymbol{b})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}=0
for any
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
; see
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
.
∎

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}

As such by (

Furthermore, define a sequence of subsets of

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa,m}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\right\},

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{v}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-\gamma_{0}\sqrt{\log[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}]}\right)

for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The constant
C
depends on
T
, while the constant
\gamma_{0}
depends on
\beta_{0}
(defined in Theorem
3.5
).

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{\kappa,n}
be defined in (
4.7
). Note that by Hölder’s inequality with exponents
2^{q-1}
and
p=2^{q-1}/(2^{q-1}-1)
, where
q>1
, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left(\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2^{q}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}}

and

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left(\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)^{2^{q-1}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}}.

We need a good control on
\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)
, while at the same time balancing the error committed in (
4.3
), (
4.23
), and (
4.24
). By the exponential Markov inequality, with

\beta_{0}
as defined in Theorem
3.5
, we obtain

(4.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\beta_{0}\kappa}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\beta_{0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]\leq Ce^{-\beta_{0}\kappa},

where the second inequality follows from Theorem
3.5
. The
constant
C
depends on
\beta_{0}
and
T
, but is independent of
\kappa
and
\beta
. We now choose
\kappa>0
such that

(4.26)

\displaystyle\exp(\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2})\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}\kappa}{p}\right),

where
\widetilde{C}
is the constant in (
4.9
), i.e.
\kappa
is the positive solution to the quadratic equation

\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{p}\kappa+\log(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})=0.

For any
h,k>0
sufficiently small such that
h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}<1
, this equation has a positive solution

(4.27)

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{1}{2\widetilde{C}}\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{p}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{p}\right)^{2}+4\widetilde{C}\log[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}]}\right).

Note that
\kappa\to\infty
as
h,k\to 0^{+}
. With this choice of
\kappa
, continuing from (
4.25
) we have for any
p>1
and sufficiently small
h,k>0
,

(4.28)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n}^{\complement}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}\kappa}{p}\right)\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{p}\sqrt{\log[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}]}\right).

With
\kappa
,
A_{n}
, and
\widetilde{A}_{n}
defined in (
4.27
), (
4.19
), and (
4.20
), respectively, we note (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), and apply (
4.26
), (
4.28
) and (
4.3
) to obtain for any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[A_{n}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{A_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{p}\sqrt{\log[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}]}\right).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The order of convergence in probability in (
4.21
) appears to be optimal for the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-norm, though sub-optimal in the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm, given the regularity of the exact solution described in (
3.3
). In contrast, the rate of strong convergence over the full probability space in (
4.22
) is likely to be suboptimal. Nevertheless, the right-hand side of (
4.22
) tends to zero as
h,k\to 0^{+}
. For sufficiently small
h,k>0
, we note that this theoretical rate is slower than
C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{\frac{1}{r}}
for any
r\geq 1
, yet faster than
C_{r}\left|{\log(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})}\right|^{-r}
.

5.
A semi-implicit finite element approximation of the regularised sLLB equation for
d=2

In this section, we consider a finite element approximation of the regularised sLLB equation (
1.2
) for
d=2
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a regular triangulation of
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
into triangles with maximal mesh-size
h
. Let
T>0
be fixed and
k
be the time-step size. We define
\mathbb{W}_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}
to be the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-conforming finite space based on the reduced Hsieh–Clough–Tocher elements or the singular Zienkiewicz elements
[
9
]
satisfying the homogeneous Neumann condition on edges belonging to
\partial\mathscr{D}
.

Define a bilinear form
\mathfrak{a}:\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(5.1)

\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}):=\left\langle\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle+\varepsilon\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{v},\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle.

With respect to this bilinear form, we define the elliptic projection operator
Q_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\mathbb{W}_{h}
by

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(Q_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi})=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{W}_{h}.

We also define the orthogonal projection operator
P_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{W}_{h}
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{W}_{h}.

A straightforward application of the Céa lemma as in
[
9
, Chapter 6.1]
, followed by the Aubin–Nitsche trick
[
5
, Chapter 5.8]
and interpolation
[
5
, Chapter 14.3]
, yields the following approximation property for the elliptic projector
Q_{h}
: there exists
C>0
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h^{2}\left\|{\Delta(\boldsymbol{v}-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq Ch^{3}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}.

Similarly, there exists
C>0
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h^{2}\left\|{\Delta(\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq Ch^{3}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}.

We now consider a semi-implicit finite element scheme to solve the regularised sLLB equation (
1.2
) for
d=2
proposed in
[
14
]
.
Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\varepsilon,n}
be the approximation in
\mathbb{W}_{h}
of
\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t)
at time
t=t_{n}
, where
n=0,1,2,\ldots,N
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
. For simplicity, we will write
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
in place of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\varepsilon,n}
.
We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(0)\in\mathbb{W}_{h}
. Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{W}_{h}
, we find
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
-adapted and
\mathbb{W}_{h}
-valued random variable
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon k\left\langle\Delta\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\Delta\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

(5.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{k}{2}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n},

for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{W}_{h}
. Here,
\overline{\Delta}W^{n}:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,k)
.

The above scheme employs a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-conforming finite element discretisation. Although such methods allow for a direct discretisation of the fourth-order differential operator without introducing auxiliary variables, they are typically more costly to implement than non-conforming or mixed formulations. This scheme is therefore primarily theoretical and the analysis complements our companion paper
[
15
]
, where we study a mixed FEM formulation for a physically relevant regularisation of (
1.1
).
Scheme (
5
) is linear, well-posed by the Lax–Milgram lemma, and enjoys the following stability property.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
, such that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{l\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{l}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

This is obtained by taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
in (
5
), see
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

To facilitate the proof of the error estimate, we decompose the error of the numerical method at time
t_{n}
in two different ways:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\right)+\left(Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right)=:\overline{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{n}+\overline{\boldsymbol{\xi}}^{n},

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-P_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})\right)+\left(P_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right)=:\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n},

As such by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), with
\mathfrak{a}
defined in (
5.1
), we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(\overline{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{W}_{h},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{W}_{h}.

Furthermore, define a sequence of subsets of
\Omega
which depend on
\kappa
and
m
:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa,m}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\right\},

where
\kappa>1
is to be specified. We prove an auxiliary error estimate next. In the following estimate, we do not trace the dependence of the constant on
\varepsilon
explicitly.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
d=2
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}

\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}
is the pathwise solution to (

\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}

5

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=Q_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}

\Omega_{\kappa,m}
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}
be as defined in (
5.11

5.8

n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}}\left(h^{4}+k^{2\alpha}\right),

for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
The constant
\widetilde{C}
depends on
\varepsilon
and
T

n
,
h
,
k

\kappa

Proof.

First, we subtract (

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big)-\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell-1})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\big),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\big)\times\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s),\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\times\big(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big),\nabla\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\left|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)}\right|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}|^{2}\big)\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}|^{2}\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

(5.12)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big((\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\big)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

By using (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), noting (
5.9
) and (

\displaystyle\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big)-\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell-1})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\big),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

Noting this, we put
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}
in (

\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}

Using (

4

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\mathfrak{a}\big(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell})+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell-1}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+J_{1}+J_{2}+\cdots+J_{8}.

We will estimate each term on the last line. We recall that

\displaystyle\left|{J_{1}}\right|\leq Ch^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha}+\delta k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right].

\displaystyle\left|{J_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\right)\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})}^{2}\right]+Ch^{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{3})}^{4}\right]+\delta k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+Ck^{1+\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{1})}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}+Ch^{4}+C\kappa^{2}k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\delta k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]+\delta k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

For the next term, noting Lemma

and applying Young’s inequality, similarly we have

\displaystyle\left|{J_{3}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left(\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(s)-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{\ell})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

Next, for the terms

in (

We now substitute all the above estimates into (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]+\varepsilon k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\Delta\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\ell}}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+Ch^{4}+Ck^{2\alpha}+C\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq\ell}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

By choosing
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}
such that

, say
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=P_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we infer the required result by the discrete Gronwall lemma.
∎

Consequently, we have the following corollaries on error estimate in a large sample space and convergence in probability of the scheme.

.

, for

we have

\displaystyle+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}(t_{m})-\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

(5.14)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}}\left(h^{4}+k^{2\alpha}\right),

for any

and

,

, and

6.
An implicit finite element approximation of the sLLB equation for
d=1

We now consider an implicit finite element approximation of the sLLB equation for
d=1
. In this case, the scheme has a better stability property, which allows us to deduce an error estimate in a stronger norm, namely convergence in
L^{2}\big(\Omega;\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}))\big)
. Assuming a more regular initial data, we show that the order of convergence (in probability or strongly over a large sample space) is optimal in the traditional sense of finite element analysis. Throughout this section, we assume that
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
, thus Theorem
3.3
holds with
s=4
.

Before describing the numerical scheme, we state some relevant preliminary results. We introduce the 1-D discrete Laplacian operator
\partial_{xx}^{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
given by

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

as well as the Ritz projection operator
R_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
defined by

(6.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{v},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\quad\text{such that}\quad\left\langle R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{1}\right\rangle=0,

where
\boldsymbol{1}:=(1,1,1)^{\top}
.
The operator
R_{h}
has the following approximation property: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h\left\|{\partial_{x}(\boldsymbol{v}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

By taking
\boldsymbol{\chi}=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
in (
6.1
), for any
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
we have

(6.4)

\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Our implicit scheme can be described as follows. Let the space
\mathbb{V}_{h}
be as defined in (
4.1
). Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
be the approximation in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
of
\boldsymbol{u}(t)
at time
t=t_{n}:=nk\in[0,T]
, where
n=0,1,2,\ldots,N
and
N=\lfloor T/k\rfloor
.
We start with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(0)\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, we find
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
-adapted and
\mathbb{V}_{h}
-valued random variable
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
satisfying
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=k\left\langle\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\times\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle-k\left\langle\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

(6.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{k}{2}\left\langle\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n},

for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Here,
\overline{\Delta}W^{n}:=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})\sim\mathcal{N}(0,k)
. Note that we treat the nonlinear cross product term implicitly in (
6
).

The following proposition shows that the scheme is well-posed for any
k\in(0,1)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Given
k\in(0,1)
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, there exists a
\mathbb{V}_{h}
-valued random variable
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
that solves the fully discrete scheme (
6
).

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
and suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)
is given.
Define a continuous map
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)-k\big(\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\big)-k\Pi_{h}\big(\boldsymbol{v}\times\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{v}\big)-k\Pi_{h}\big(|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)|^{2}\boldsymbol{v}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{k}{2}\Pi_{h}\big((\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big)-\Pi_{h}(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)\times\boldsymbol{g})\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega).

The scheme (
6
) is equivalent to solving
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\big)=\boldsymbol{0}
. The existence of solution to this equation will be shown using Brouwer’s fixed point theorem. To this end, let
B_{\rho}(\omega):=\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}:\left\|{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\rho\}
, where
\rho
is to be determined later. For any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\partial B_{\rho}(\omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle+k\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\langle(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)\times\boldsymbol{g})\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}-4\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\rho-\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-4\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega)}\right|^{2}

Therefore, for sufficiently large
\rho
, precisely

\rho>2\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+16\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}(\omega)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}(\omega)}\right|^{2}

we have
\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle>0
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\partial B_{\rho}(\omega)
. By a version of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem
[
13
, Corollary VI.1.1]
, we infer the existence of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}(\omega)
such that
\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\omega}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\big)=\boldsymbol{0}
. The
\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
-measurability of the map
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
follows by the same argument as in
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Some stability estimates for the scheme are derived in the following lemmas. While Lemma

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of

,
h
, and
k
, such that for any
p\in[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{l\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{l}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

This is obtained by taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}
in (

Lemma 6.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
, such that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{l\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{l}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]\leq C.

Proof.

Taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
in (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+k\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=k\left\langle\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right|^{2}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n},\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle-\frac{k}{2}\left\langle(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\times\boldsymbol{g})\times\boldsymbol{g},\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle-\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We estimate each term on the last line as follows. Let
\delta>0
. For the term
I_{1}
, by Agmon’s inequality, Young’s inequality, and (
6.4
) we have

\displaystyle\left|{I_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq k\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big(\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big)\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+Ck\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{12}\right)+Ck\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{8}\right)+\delta k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

For the term
I_{2}
, by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left|{I_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

For the last term, note that by (
6.1
), Young’s inequality, and the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-stability of
\Pi_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n}+\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+C\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}+\partial_{x}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{n}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-1}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{n}.

We take
\delta=1/8
. Substituting these estimates into (
6
), rearranging the terms, taking the
p
-th power, summing over
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
, and applying the expected value, we obtain for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k\left(\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+C\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{12p}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{8p}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}+\partial_{x}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\boldsymbol{g})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{j}}\right|^{2}\right)^{p}\right]

(6.7)

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big(\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{j}\right|^{p}\right].

It remains to bound the last two terms of the above inequality. To this end, note that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}+\partial_{x}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\boldsymbol{g})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{j}}\right|^{2}\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\left|{\overline{\Delta}W^{j}}\right|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{p-1}k^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[k\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}+\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2p}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2p}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2p}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq CT^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[k\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(1+\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right)\right],

where we also used the tower property of conditional expectation and the independence of the Wiener increment in the second inequality. Next, by the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\langle\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\times\boldsymbol{g},\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}\right\rangle\overline{\Delta}W^{j}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(k\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j\leq n}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{j\leq n}\left(1+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right],

where in the last step we used Young’s inequality. Therefore, continuing from (
6
), rearranging the terms and noting Lemma
6.2
, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\ell\leq n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}k\left(\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right)^{p}\right]\leq C+C\mathbb{E}\left[k\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{j-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right].

The required inequality then follows by the discrete Gronwall lemma.
∎

As before, we decompose the error of the numerical method at time

:

(6.8)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})\right)+\left(R_{h}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{n})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right)=:\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}.

By (

), we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{n},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

Furthermore, define a sequence of subsets of
\Omega
that depend on
\kappa
and

(and
h
):

(6.10)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa,m}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\max_{t\leq t_{m}\wedge T}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}^{2}+\max_{\ell\leq m}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\right\},

where

is to be specified. We prove an auxiliary error estimates next. In the following proposition, since we now employ (

.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
d=1
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
. Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}
is the pathwise solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
, and let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\}_{n}

6

Let
\Omega_{\kappa,m}
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{n}
be as defined in (
6.10

6.8

n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}

(6.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}}\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right),

for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The constant
\widetilde{C}
depends on
T
, but is independent of
n
,
h
,
k
, and
\kappa
.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we have
for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big)\times\partial_{xx}\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\times\big(\partial_{xx}\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\left|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)}\right|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}|^{2}\big)\boldsymbol{u}(s),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}|^{2}\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}\big),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s

(6.12)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big((\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}\big)\times\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}.

First, we set
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}
in (
6
) and multiply the resulting equations by
\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}
, where the set
\Omega_{\kappa,m}
is defined in (
6.10
). Summing over
\ell\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, taking the maximum over
m\leq n
, and then applying the expectation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\left|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right|\left|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right|}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})+\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell},\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{4}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\mathbb{H}^{4})}^{4}\right]+\delta k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right],

where in the last step we used the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
, Lemma
6.3
, and the regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
. For the term
J_{7}
, we apply similar argument as in (
4
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left|{J_{7}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{4}+k^{2\alpha})+C\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

For the term
J_{8}
, a straightforward application of Young’s inequality, (
6.10
), the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
, and Lemma
6.3
yields

\displaystyle\left|{J_{8}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}+C\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

For the term
J_{9}
, similarly we have

\displaystyle\left|{J_{9}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{8}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{T}(\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{4}\right]+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{2\alpha}+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

The term
J_{10}
is estimated in a similar manner as the term
J_{4}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|{J_{10}}\right|\leq C(h^{4}+k^{2\alpha})+Ck\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+\widetilde{\delta}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

The stochastic integral term
J_{11}
is split further as:

\displaystyle J_{11}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big((\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\langle\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\big((\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g}\big),\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right]

(6.18)

\displaystyle=:J_{11a}+J_{11b}.

For the term
J_{11a}
, by the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-stability of
\Pi_{h}
, the Hölder continuity of
\boldsymbol{u}
, the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy and the Young inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\left|{J_{11a}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})+Ck\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right].

For the term
J_{11b}
, we use Young’s inequality, Itô’s isometry, and the Hölder continuity of
\boldsymbol{u}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|{J_{11b}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\partial_{x}\Pi_{h}\left((\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g}\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\int_{t_{\ell-1}}^{t_{\ell}}\left\|{(\boldsymbol{u}(s)-\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell-1})+\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\ell-1}+\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1})\times\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})+Ck\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]+\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right].

We gather all the above estimates for
J_{1}
up to
J_{11}
, set
\widetilde{\delta}=1/50
, and substitute them back into (
6
), thus obtaining

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]

(6.19)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})+C\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right].

Adding (
6
) with (
6
) and rearranging the terms, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+k\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\partial_{xx}^{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]+C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})+C_{1}\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,\ell-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\ell-1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right].

Therefore, when
k<1/(C_{1}\kappa^{2})
, we deduce that

(6.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]+C(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})+C_{1}\kappa^{2}k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq\ell}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right].

The required result then follows by the discrete Gronwall lemma.
∎

Consequently, we have the following results on strong convergence over a large sample space as well as convergence in probability, with rates which appear to be optimal in the traditional sense of finite element analysis.

Corollary 6.5
.

Under the same hypotheses as Proposition
6.4
, for
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
we have

(6.21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)\right]\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}}\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right),

for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The constant
\widetilde{C}
depends on
T
, but is independent of
n
,
h
,
k
, and
\kappa
.

In particular, when
h=O(k^{\frac{3}{4}})
, for the set

\displaystyle\Omega_{h,k}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\log\big(\log\left[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}\right]\big)}\right\}

which satisfies
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{h,k}\right]\geq 1-\left(\log\big(\log\left[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}\right]\big)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
we have for every
\delta>0
,

(6.22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{h,k}}\left(\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left\|{\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}(t_{\ell})-\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)^{1-\delta}.

Proof.

Inequality (
6.21
) follows directly from (
4.3
), Proposition
6.4
, and the triangle inequality. To prove (
6.22
), we observe that if
\kappa=\sqrt{\log\big(\log\left[(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{-1}\right]\big)}
, then
k=O(\kappa^{-2})
. Consequently, the assumptions underlying the estimate (
6.21
) remain valid, which allows us to deduce (
6.22
).
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Suppose that
h=O(k^{\frac{3}{4}})
and let
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. For every
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and
\gamma>0
, we have

(6.23)

\displaystyle\lim_{h,k\to 0^{+}}\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\geq\gamma(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha})^{1-\delta}\right]=0.

Proof.

This follows by the same argument as in Corollary
4.4
, noting the result in Corollary
6.5
.
∎

Finally, we deduce the following theorem on the strong convergence of the implicit scheme (
6
) over the whole sample space. In this case, since (
6.10
) involves the norm of the numerical solution, the argument based on (
3
) and the exponential Markov inequality is no longer applicable, and the resulting convergence rate is weaker than that in (
4.22
) (see Remark
4.6
).

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
d=1
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
. Suppose that
\boldsymbol{u}
is the pathwise solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{4}
, and let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of random variables solving (
6
) with
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
. Assume that
h=O(k^{\frac{3}{4}})
. Then the implicit scheme (
6
) converges in
L^{2}\big(\Omega;\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})\big)
. More precisely, for sufficiently small
h
and
k
, and for all
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have

(6.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)}\right|^{-r}

for any
r\geq 1
. The constant
C_{r}
depends on
T
and
r
, but is independent of
n
,
h
, and
k
. In particular, the right-hand side of (
6.24
) tends to zero as
h,k\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

This follows by a similar argument as in Theorem
4.5
. By Hölder’s inequality with exponents
2^{q-1}
and
p=2^{q-1}/(2^{q-1}-1)
, for any
q>1
, we obtain

(6.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2^{q-1}}}.

The last terms on the right-hand side of (
6.25
) are bounded due to the assumed regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
and the stability estimate in Lemma
6.3
. It remains to establish a bound for the probability of the ‘bad’ set
\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}
. To this end, by Chebyshev’s inequality and the definition of the set
\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}
in (
6.10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{\kappa,n-1}^{\complement}\right)\leq\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}}^{2^{q}}+\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2^{q}}\right)\right].

This implies

(6.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\kappa,m-1}^{\complement}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]\leq C_{q}\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}.

We now choose

\kappa^{2}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{C}}\left(\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)}\right|-(2^{q-1}-1)\log\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)}\right|\right),

where
\widetilde{C}
is the constant from (
6.21
). For sufficiently small
h
and
k
, the right-hand side is positive. With this choice of
\kappa
, and using (
6.26
), we infer from (
6.21
) and (
6.26
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{m\leq n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}(t_{m})-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}e^{\widetilde{C}\kappa^{2}}\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)+C_{q}\kappa^{-2^{q-1}}\leq C_{r}\left|{\log\left(h^{2}+k^{2\alpha}\right)}\right|^{-r},

for any
r\geq 1
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

7.
Numerical experiments

We present a set of numerical experiments to validate the theoretical convergence properties of the proposed finite element schemes for the stochastic Landau–Lifshitz–Bloch (LLB) equation above the Curie temperature. All computations are carried out in the
FEniCS
environment
[
1
]
. The computational domain is taken to be either the unit square or the unit interval. The magnetisation vector field is discretised in space using continuous piecewise linear finite elements on a family of quasi-uniform meshes. Time integration is performed using either the semi-implicit scheme (
4
) or the implicit scheme (
6
).

We remark that a
C^{1}
-conforming scheme (
5
) is included in our analysis in Section
5
for completeness, since we improve upon the theoretical results of
[
14
]
. However, due to its computational cost, especially in the stochastic setting, numerical experiments for this scheme will not be performed. Instead, we have developed a mixed scheme for a regularised fourth-order formulation of the LLB system for
d=2
in a separate work
[
15
]
, which is more efficient, and numerical validation for that approach will be reported there. In the following, we will focus on verifying the order of convergence for schemes (
4
) and (
6
).

To assess convergence, we compute a reference solution on a very fine mesh and with a small time step. This reference solution serves as an approximation of the exact stochastic solution for each realisation of the Wiener process. For coarser discretisations, we use the same Brownian path so that the difference between the numerical solution and the reference is meaningful pathwise. The error
\mathcal{E}_{s}(h,k)
at the final time
T
is then measured in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}^{s})
-norm for
s=0
or
1
, defined by

\mathcal{E}_{s}(h,k):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{N}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{ref}}(T)}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here,
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{N}
denotes the numerical solution with mesh size
h
at time
T=Nk
, and
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{ref}}(T)
is the reference solution at the same final time along the same realisation of the Brownian motion. In practice, the expectation is approximated by a Monte Carlo average over
M
independent sample paths.

We vary separately the mesh size
h
and the time step
k
to test spatial and temporal convergence. To verify spatial convergence, we fix a sufficiently small time step and compare errors across a sequence of meshes (
h=2^{-j}
, for
j=2,3,4,5
, with
j=6
as the reference solution). For temporal convergence, we fix a fine mesh and vary the time step size (
k=(25\times 2^{j})^{-1}
, for
j=0,1,2,3
, with
j=4
as the reference solution). In both cases, errors are averaged over
M=50
realisations. The experimental rate of convergence is obtained by fitting the errors against mesh size or time step in a log-log plot.

7.3.
Simulation 3

This simulation is designed to verify the exponential stability established in Proposition
3.6
.
Let the spatial domain be
\mathscr{D}=[0,1]
, and let all physical and numerical parameters be chosen as in Simulation 1. We consider two numerical solutions
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{w}
of the stochastic LLB equation, driven by the same Wiener process but with different initial data
\boldsymbol{v}_{0},\boldsymbol{w}_{0}\in{H}^{1}
. Specifically, we take

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(0,\cos(2\pi x),\sin(2\pi x)\big),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\big(\epsilon,\cos(2\pi x),\sin(2\pi x)\big),

where the parameter
\epsilon>0
measures the size of the initial perturbation. Throughout this experiment, we fix the spatial mesh size
h=1/32
and the time step
k=1/100
, and employ the scheme (
4
).

We first set
\epsilon=10^{-2}
and choose the noise coefficient

\boldsymbol{g}(x)=\big(0.2,0.1(1+x),0\big),

corresponding to a small-noise regime.
Figure
7(a)
shows
30
sample paths of
\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}(t)-\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}^{2}
as functions of time
t\in[0,2]
, together with their Monte Carlo average. After a short transient, the averaged difference exhibits a clear exponential decay.
Furthermore, Figure
7(b)
displays the Monte Carlo mean
{E}\left[\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}(t)-\boldsymbol{w}(t)}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]
on a semi-logarithmic scale for
\epsilon=10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3}
. The curves differ only by a vertical shift while sharing the same asymptotic decay rate, thus providing a numerical confirmation of the exponential stability estimate in Proposition
3.6
.

Next, we repeat the same experiment with increased noise amplitudes in order to explore the behaviour outside the small-noise regime. Figure
8(a)
corresponds to the moderately large noise coefficient

\boldsymbol{g}(x)=\big(0.5,0.3(1+x),0\big),

while Figure
8(b)
corresponds to the significantly larger choice

\boldsymbol{g}(x)=\big(2.0,1.5(1+x),0\big).

In these cases, the exponential decay observed in the small-noise regime is no longer present. For moderately large noise, the Monte Carlo mean exhibits a mild growth, while for large noise the curves become highly irregular and do not display any clear contraction. This may indicate a loss of the contractive mechanism underlying Proposition
3.6
.

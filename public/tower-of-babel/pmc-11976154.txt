Colour stability comparison between ormocer nanohybrid composites and udma-based nanocomposites after immersion in coffee – Experimental study
Authors: Julia Elizabeth Guy, Tawfiq Hijazi Alsadi, Susana Muwaquet Rodriguez
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionColour stability of composite materials is an essential property that greatly influences the longevity of dental restorations. Once in the oral cavity, dental materials must provide a stain resistance ability against environmental exposures, such as coffee. This experimental study compares the colour stability of two composite resin materials with differing nanocompositions.ObjectivesAnalyze the effect of coffee on the colour stability of nanohybrid composites and nanocomposites.Material and Methods40 composites discs were utilized in total, with 20 fabricated of an Ormocer-based nanohybrid composite (Admira Fusion) and 20 of a UDMA-based nanocomposite (Normofill nanoceram) in shade A2. 10 discs of each material were then immersed in coffee or water for 12 days. Colour measurements were taken before and after the staining period using a VITA Easyshade spectrophotometer. The colour difference (ΔE) of both composites was calculated using the CIE L*a*b* values.ResultsBoth Ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira Fusion) and UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram) exhibited a colour change after the 12-day period. The UDMA-based nanocomposite (Normo fill nanoceram) exhibited significantly more discolouration compared to the Ormocer-based nanohybrid composite (Admira Fusion).ConclusionsOverall, there was a significant difference in colour generated by immersion in coffee as compared to water. Additionally, the Ormocer-based nanohybrid composite (Admira Fusion) exhibited significantly greater colour stability as compared to the UDMA-based nanocomposite (Normo fill nanoceram).

Introduction

Dentistry is traditionally perceived as a science-based healthcare profession concerned with the treatment of diseases and conditions of the orofacial region. However, the profession has greatly evolved to encompass the progressive demands of patients. Contemporary dentistry now extends beyond patients solely seeking to resolve various dental issues to now include elective aesthetic procedures. This increased interest in aesthetic dentistry is greatly attributed to the rapidly expanding presence of social media in today’s society. In a survey conducted by the American Academy of Cosmetic Dentistry (AACD), dentists noted a 12.5 % surge in demand for aesthetic interventions over the past 5 years. They accredit this heightened demand for aesthetic dental treatment to media coverage (Abbasi et al., 2022).

Aesthetics of the orofacial region is a principal component of beauty. It is important to acknowledge the profound influence physical appearance has on the quality of life for individuals. Particularly, the satisfaction with one’s dentition plays a critical role in the general well-being of patients. Some common characteristics of concern include teeth colour, size, shape as well as position in the dental arch (Campos et al., 2020). Therefore, a fundamental objective of dental care is to assist patients in achieving a high level of contentment with their orofacial features. Additionally, the assessment of sociopsychological aspects of aesthetic treatment is crucial to achieve successful results.

In a study conducted by the Research Committee of the Faculty of Dentistry of Al Jouf University, a questionnaire was developed to evaluate the satisfaction of individuals with their oral appearance. These questions examined various tooth characteristics as well as the treatment patients desired to improve their aesthetics. A significant discovery was made that nearly 66 % of participants were not satisfied with the colour of their teeth. Coincidentally, tooth whitening was establishes as the most desired treatment among individuals. These results serve as evidence to the importance of tooth colour for dental appearance (Al-Zarea, 2013).

Teeth microstructure

Teeth are composed of several colours that form a gradation along the external surfaces. This is due to the hierarchical microstructure of the individual hard tissue components of the tooth. The outermost layer of the crown is formed by enamel. Enamel is considered the hardest tissue in the human body, consisting predominantly of hydroxyapatite crystals. This highly mineralized substance acts as a protective barrier against various external environmental factors, such as mechanical damage, staining and entry of microorganisms. It is to be noted that this structure is relatively colourless. On the other hand, the bulk of the hard tissue structure of the tooth is composed of detin. Dentin consists of 70 % inorganic material, 20 % organic substances (mainly type I collagen) and 10 % water. It is further organized into distinct dentinal tubules containing odontoblastic processes. These serve as an additional shield for the tooth pulp and play a critical role in the metabolism of enamel and cementum (Sarna-Boś et al., 2023).

Dental material discolouration

A high demand for restorative procedures in clinical dentistry is observed globally, with both the placement and replacement of restorations being the most common dental procedures. Overall, the replacement of resin restorations in anterior teeth is often related to aesthetic concerns, such as noticeable discolouration (Demarco et al., 2015).

Discolouration of restorative materials has been classified into 3 main types. These include intrinsic or body discolouration, surface or sub-surface colour alteration, and external discolouration. Intrinsic staining is due to physicochemical reactions in the deeper layers of the restorative material. Surface colour alteration occurs due to the slight penetration of the superficial layer of composite resins. External discolouration is caused by plaque accumulation and/or surface stains (extrinsic stain). These extrinsic stains are predominantly induced by colourants found in beverages, food, and smoking products (Poggio et al., 2017).

Coffee is considered one of the most popular beverages globally. Statistics reveal that around 2.25 billion cups of coffee are consumed worldwide every day (Stanek et al., 2021). Several studies have reported beneficial health effects related to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions (Grosso et al., 2017). Despite having certain health benefits, drinking coffee is associated with discolouration of dental hard tissues and restorative dental materials. It is therefore imperative that aesthetic dental materials are developed with colour stability as a critical characteristic (Yeslam and AlZahrani, 2022).

Dental composite resin composition

Dental composite resins are seen as the dental material of choice for direct restorations in both anterior and posterior teeth. Direct restorations are a conservative treatment approach that remove only infected or affected carious structures. This is advantageous as patients can retain a larger quantity of their own tooth structure.

The basic components of most composite resins include dimethacrylate monomers, filler materials, photoinitiators, coupling agents and various additives. Some examples of dimethacrylate monomers include Bis-GMA (bisphenol A-glycidyl methacrylate), TEGMA (triethylene glycol methacrylate), and UDMA (urethane dimethacrylate). These components form the matrix structure of resin composites (Mandhalkar et al., 2023). Dental restorative materials can be further classified based on the filler particles used. This includes macrofill, microfill, hybrid, nanofill, minifill, and midfill composite materials (Glowacka-Sobotta et al., 2023).

Fillers have been developed with the purpose of enhancing various physicochemical properties of restorative materials. For instance, they have been used to improve flexural strength, tensile strength, wear resistance, and toughness. Newer materials have also been designed to exhibit antibacterial action and remineralization capabilities.

Nanotechnology has revolutionized the medical and dental fields by improving the physical and optical properties of materials. This field of science deals with nanostructures, those that occupy a three-dimensional space in the range of 1–100 nm. Replacing micro-sized particles with nanoparticles allows for enhanced affinity and efficacy of molecular interactions (AlKahtani, 2018). More specifically, the introduction of nanoparticles in dentistry, such as nanofillers, has greatly expanded the capabilities of dental materials and their manner of interacting with oral tissues. Nanocomposites are a newer class of resin-based composites (RBCs). They are becoming increasingly popular in recent years because of their enhanced clinical features. The main types of nanocomposites include nano-filled and nanohybrid resin composites.

Nanofilled RBCs contain both nanomers and nanoclusters. Nanomers are between 5–75 nm in size. On the other hand, nanoclusters are approximately 0.6–1.4 µm. These nanoclusters are formed by zirconia and silica nanoparticles that fuse and agglomerate together. Silane then infiltrates this porous microstructure. Nanohybrid RBCs are composed of nanoparticles (40–50 nm in size) and milled glass fibers (Tanthanuch et al., 2016).

Due to the increasing concern over aesthetics in the general population, it is vital that dental materials are fabricated with two critical features in mind. This includes excellent colour matching and excellent colour stability. Major advancements have been made in relation to colour matching, allowing direct restorations to restore the natural tooth appearance. However, the main determinant that reduces the longevity of direct RBC restorations is colour stability. Once placed within the oral environment, restorative materials become exposed to various internal and external factors that eventually produce discolouration (Meshki and Rashidi, 2022).

In order to maximize the long-term success of aesthetic restorations it is imperative that dentists acquire the necessary skills and training needed for proper shade selection. This requires a fundamental understanding in colorimetry. Additionally, it is important to determine the staining potential of various food and beverage products. By acquiring this information, the consumption of certain goods with high staining power can be avoided to minimize colour alteration. Furthermore, the chemical substances causing staining can be determined and possibly removed from future commercial products (Meshki and Rashidi, 2022, Al-Shami et al., 2023).

Colour analysis

When discussing the concept of colour, it is important to understand that it involves both physical and perceptual dimensions. This becomes apparent when viewing identical objects, for instance teeth, in front of different coloured backgrounds. Despite the teeth having the same physical colour, distinct perceptual interpretations of the objects occur depending on the observer. This describes the discrepancy between perceptual colour, which is shaped by human observation, and physical colour, based on intrinsic properties (Pan and Westland, 2018).

The Munsell colour system outlines 3 dimensions of colour that can be quantified. These include hue, value and chroma. Hue is the term utilized to describe colour tone or groups of colours, such as reds, blues, and yellows. Value describes the relative lightness or darkness of a colour. Finally, chroma represents the colour intensity or degree of saturation (Pustina Krasniqi et al., 2017).

Colour can either be expressed subjectively, based on visual perception, or objectively, using colour measuring instruments. The fastest and least expensive method in teeth colour comparison involves visual analysis. Several dental colour keys have been developed to streamline the process of shade selection for dental practitioners. Two popular shade guides include the Vita Classical key and Vitapan’s 3D-Master shade guide. The main disadvantage of visual colour matching is that it involves subjective error. Several variables such as dentist experience, physical condition, eye diseases, age, and light source can alter results (Draghici et al., 2016).

Colorimetry is a branch of science concerned with the numerical characterization of colour. It has been popularly accepted as the standard instrumental technique in colour evaluation. This is due to the fact that it helps eliminate subjective errors made in various other visual analysis methods. The Commission internationale de l’éclairage (CIE) system of colorimetry highlights 3 essential factors that govern colour perception. This includes the optical properties of the object, the nature of the illumination, and the response of the observer. Comprehensive data is collected on these factors and is then utilized in the calculation of tristimulus values (X, Y and Z). Based on these values, the precise colour of an object can be determined (Broadbent, 2017).

Another commonly utilized colour system is CIELAB. The CIELAB system, similarly to the XYZ system, expressed colour using 3 numbers. L* stands for the perception of lightness, while the a* and b* represent the main colours of human vision. Differently, from the XYZ system, CIELAB presents colours in a non-linear manner, assigning them a specific three-dimensional location in space. Other quantitative colour formulation systems RGB and LCh. Overall, these numerical systems help standardize the field of colour science (Pustina Krasniqi et al., 2017).

The most common colour measurement devices that are commercially available include spectrophotometers, colorimeters, spectroradiometers, and digital cameras. Each of these instruments have different colour measurement capabilities (Pan and Westland, 2018).

Hypothesis

H1: Ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira Fusion) have significantly greater colour stability as compared to UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram).

H0: Ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira Fusion) do not have significantly greater colour stability as compared to UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram).

Objectives

This experimental study aimed to analyze the effect of coffee on the colour stability of nanohybrid composites and nanocomposites. Additionally, it aimed to analyze if Ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira fusion) have significantly greater colour stability as compared to UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram).

Methods

The following experiment was carried out to observe the colour difference between 2 composite materials after placing them in coffee for 12 days. Additionally, a statistical analysis was performed to provide a greater understanding of the results in a numerical format.

Preparation of composite discs

To begin with, 40 stainless steel washers of a diameter of 0.5 mm were separated into four groups (10 in each group).•Group 1: Admira fusion submerged in coffee•Group 2: Admira fusion submerged in water (control)•Group 3: Normo fill nanoceram submerged in coffee•Group 4: Normo fill nanoceram submerged in water (control)

Once the washers were separated into their specific groups, the 1st glass mixing slab was placed on a flat surface. Then one stainless-steel washer was placed on top. Using the composite modelling instruments, composite material was placed into the central hole. Once filled, the 2nd glass slab was placed over the stainless-steel washer. Slight pressure was then applied to ensure the creation of a compact composite disc. The superior glass slab was then removed, and an LED curing lamp was utilized to cure the composite for 20 s per disc. This procedure was repeated with both composite materials (Admira Fusion and Normo fill nanoceram) to create all 40 composite discs. Finally, the discs were all placed into labelled ice cube trays.

Initial colour measurement

Once the discs were fabricated, the VITA Easyshade V digital spectrophotometer was used to measure the colour of the composite discs prior to placement in their respective liquids. It is important to note that each disc was placed against a white background to prevent any colour absorption when evaluating the disc colour. The figures that were recorded included the shade values, as well as the colour values (L*, a*, b*). These measurements were conducted three times in order to attain average values.

Exposure of composite discs in coffee solution

To prepare the coffee solution, 25 g of ground Marcilla coffee was diluted in 1000 mL of distilled boiling water. The coffee was then allowed to cool down to room temperature.

For groups 1 and 3, 60 mL of coffee solution was poured into each ice cube receptacle. For groups 2 and 4, 60 mL of distilled water was poured into each ice cube receptacle. Overall, the composite discs were kept submerged in liquid for 12 days. The ice cube trays containing the liquid solutions and discs were kept at room temperature in a completely dark room to avoid external influences on the end results.

Final colour measurement

After 12 days of submersion, the composite discs were removed from the liquid solutions and blotted dry with tissue paper. Each disc’s shade/ colour values were then measured three times using the VITA Easyshade V digital spectrophotometer. Once again, a white background was utilized to prevent colour absorption.

Colour difference calculation

The L*, a* and b* values were collected before and after liquid submersion using a digital spectrophotometer. The average values of each composite specimen were then used to calculate the colour difference, ΔE* of each tooth. Analysis of ΔE

The immersion of composite discs in water produces a colour change (ΔE) with a mean of 5.15 ± 1.13 in the Normo fill discs and 8.18 ± 1.51 in the Admira discs. Furthermore, immersion in coffee resulted in means of 20.31 ± 5.75 and 13.88 ± 2.73 respectively. Fig. 1 presents the corresponding distributions.Fig. 1Box and Whisker plot of ΔE distributions.

Box and Whisker plot of ΔE distributions.

The horizontal line dividing the box represents the median value, with 50 % of the cases lying above and below. The upper and lower edges of the box correspond to the 1st and 3rd quartiles, below which lies 25 % and above which lies 75 % of the sample, respectively. The “whiskers” extend to values within an acceptable range, above which lie the outliers (circles) and extreme values (asterisks).

The descriptive impression is that water might be causing a more noticeable colour change in the Admira specimens. However, the conclusion is the opposite when comparing those immersed in coffee.

The confidence intervals for the 4 means (4.34–5.96; 7.11–9.26; 16.19–24.42; 11.93–15.83) exclude the value 0 with ample sufficiency, and therefore, it must be admitted that there is always a colour change (for any composite and solution used).

On the other hand, a certain asymmetry is observed in the distribution of Normofill in water, which justifies the following analysis with the logarithmic transformation.

The most important conclusion from ANOVA evaluation is that there are differences in the colour change experienced depending on the composite, and these differences depend on the solution in which the material is immersed (p < 0.001). Conversely, the effect of the type of solution specifically depends on the material being used (p < 0.001).

In relation to the results of multiple Bonferroni comparisons, after immersion in water, the colour change was significantly greater in the Admira discs compared to the Normo fill discs (p < 0.001). After immersion in coffee, the colour change was significantly smaller in the Admira discs compared to the Normo fill discs (p = 0.001). This markedly different pattern demonstrated between solutions perfectly explains the significant interaction estimated by the model (p < 0.001).

The Normo fill discs experienced a significantly greater colour change with coffee compared to water (p < 0.001). Similarly, the Admira discs experienced a significantly greater colour change with coffee compared to water (p < 0.001).

In this case, with seemingly equal p-values, it is difficult to explain the significant interaction of the model. However, the p-value for Normo fill is on the order of 10–15; while for Admira, it is only 10–6. Therefore, with Normo fill, the difference attributable to the solution is much stronger.

To conclude, some authors (Marconyak, Ruyter, Khokhar, etc.) have conducted studies on the levels of visual perception of a colour change. According to their conclusions:•ΔE < 1 → imperceptible•1 < ΔE < 3.3 → perceptible only by professionals•ΔE > 3.3 → perceptible by the majority of the population

Analysis of the change in the VITA Classical scale

All specimens were classified as ‘A1′ at the initial moment. For this reason, the study of the change is reduced to comparing the final classification.

We observe, for example, that 30 % of the Normo fill discs in water changed their value, compared to 60 % of the Admira discs. In coffee, 100 % of the specimens changed shade values.

The final VITA Classical evaluation in both water and coffee resulted in statistically significant differences in the Normo fill discs (p = 0.004).

Analysis of the change in the VITA Master scale

For this specific colour dimension, it is no longer true that all the specimens start with the same baseline classification. Some have a value of ‘0′ and others have a value of ‘1′.

For example, at time T0, there are 4 Normo fill discs classified as ‘0′ that changed to ‘1′ after immersion in water. Another 6 discs were classified as ‘0′ at T0 and changed to ‘1′ at T1. Since the initial situation is variable, the difference in the value dimension (T1-T0) must be defined. This is represented as the number of degrees of increase.

Differences are detected with water. The 10 Admira discs increase by 1 degree (from ‘0′ to ‘1′), while only 4 Normo discs increase by 1 degree. Meanwhile, in coffee, there are no differences noted.

Changes in value

In either material, a greater increase in value gradation is detected with coffee as compared to water. This dimension of colour is stable (reported as ‘M’) in all combinations of group and solution.At time T0, the hue can be ‘0′ or ‘1′.

Changes in hue

Differences in the hue dimension (T1-T0) are detected with water. The 10 Admira discs decrease by 1 degree (from ‘3′ to ‘2′), while only 4 Normo fill discs exhibit a decrease by 1 degree. The remaining 6 Normo fill discs remain stable at ‘2′. Meanwhile, in coffee, there are no differences noted. It is to be noted that there are no differences in ‘hue’ in the specimens immersed in coffee.

In Normo fill, no statistically significant differences in hue were detected between water and coffee. More specifically, in water, 6 discs remained stable, and in coffee, 8 discs remained stable. Therefore, it can be said that the pieces tend to maintain their initial hue in both solutions.

Nevertheless, in Admira, the 10 discs reduce hue with water, while the other 10 discs remain stable with coffee.

Discussion

Colour stability of composite resin materials is an essential property that greatly influences the longevity of dental restorations (Shamszadeh et al., 2016). Shade match is initially required to place the restorations. Once in the oral cavity, dental materials must provide a stain resistance ability against environmental exposures, such as food and drinks.

Nanotechnology applications in dentistry have introduced filler particle sizes within the range of 0.1–100 nm to improve physicochemical properties of materials, such as colour stability. This involves replacing micro-sized particles with a greater proportion of nanofillers (AlKahtani, 2018). In the present study, the effect of coffee staining on the colour stability of an Ormocer-based nanohybrid composite (Admira fusion) was compared to a more traditional UDMA-based nanocomposite (Normo fill nanoceram).

Ormocer is an acronym for organically modified ceramic. These materials are produced via hydrolysis and polycondensation reactions (sol–gel processing) that create a long-chain inorganic silica backbone with organic lateral branches. Composite materials containing ormocer claim to possess superior properties, such as an increased degree of conversion and greater colour stability (Ebaya et al., 2022).

Colour variability can either be determined subjectively through visual analysis or objectively using specialized instruments, namely spectrophotometers. Visual shade matching involves comparing the colour of a tooth or a dental material with a dental shade guide. Despite being rapid and cost-effective, this technique is greatly influenced by clinician skill, light source characteristics, and physical eye condition (Draghici et al., 2016).

In relation to objective colour analysis methods, spectrophotometers utilize the CIE L*a*b* coordinate system to acquire quantitative values (Draghici et al., 2016). These values are then utilized the calculate ΔE. The ΔE value represents the colour difference between a single object at different times or between two distinct objects. According to research, a ΔE value below 1 is undetectable to the naked eye. However, when ΔE is equal to or greater than 3.3, the colour change is clinically unacceptable (Khosravi et al., 2016).

In my opinion, the use of a spectrophotometer provides more accurate values for colour analysis as compared to the use of shade guides. The quantitative results collected are precise, accurate, and reproducible. Additionally, use of electronic colour systems helps remove human errors. Several authors are in agreement that the use of instruments provides more precise values (Bahannan, 2014, Alshiddi and Richards, 2015). For this reason, in our experiment, the VITA Easy shade spectrophotometer was utilized. It is to be noted that this device also provides shade values based on the VITA Classical key and Vitapan’s 3D-Master shade guide. Therefore, to provide the most complete analysis of colour variation, both the CIE L*a*b* values and shade values were collected.

In our experiment, the two composites were immersed in coffee for the same 12-day period as in the study conducted by Mahajan et al (Mahajan et al., 2019). Several other authors conducted experiments over a 28-day period with incremental colour assessment times to obtain more comparative results (Poggio et al., 2017, Shamszadeh et al., 2016, Poggio et al., 2016, Sherif et al., 2020, Huang et al., 2022).

The results of this analysis reveal significant colour changes (ΔE) across different immersion solutions and between the two composite materials. Coffee staining produced higher colour changes overall in comparison to those in water storage. This is in accordance with previous investigations; Shamszadeh et al., Poggio et al., and Manojlovic et al. immersed composite resin materials into various staining solutions and demonstrated that coffee produced greater colour changes compared to water immersion (Shamszadeh et al., 2016, Poggio et al., 2016, Manojlovic et al., 2015).

Based on the findings of the individual components of the L*a*b* coordinate system, there was an overall loss of luminosity in all the specimens after the 12-day period. Additionally, the changes in the red-green axis (a) showed a general shift from green to red. Moreover, the changes in the blue-yellow axis (b) showed a general shift from blue to yellow. This specific colour shift in the blue-yellow axis (b) can be attributed to the ability of yellow pigments in coffee to penetrate the microstructures of composite resin materials (Eldwakhly et al., 2019).

Current study findings present that water storage can also lead to minor discolouration. The staining susceptibility of composite resin materials is mainly affected by their water sorption and solubility. The greater the sorption and or solubility, the more susceptible a dental material is to staining. Research has demonstrated that materials with higher filler content and a lower resin matrix content yield lower water sorption values (Zidan and Mansouri, 2018). The results of this study showed that immersion in water produces a greater colour change (ΔE) in Admira discs than Normo fill discs, with mean values of 8.18 ± 1.51 and 5.15 ± 1.13 respectively. However, this contradictory finding may be attributed to the fact that slight rusting was found on the metal surfaces of the Admira composite disc preparations after immersion in water.

Comparing the colour difference (ΔE) of the two composite materials after coffee immersion, results showed that the ormocer-based nanohybrid composite (Admira fusion) was subjected to less discolouration than the UDMA-based nanocomposite (Normo fill nanoceram). More specifically, the mean ΔE values were 13.88 ± 2.73 and 20.31 ± 5.75 respectively. Additionally, after statistical analysis, the p-value was less than 0.001, indicating that there is a significant difference between the colour variation of both composite materials after being placed in coffee for 12-days. For this reason, the main hypothesis (H1) was accepted as the colour stability was significantly greater in ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira fusion) as compared to UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram). This result was in agreement with Ceci et al., which demonstrated that Ormocer composite reported significantly less (ΔE) values after exposure to coffee compared to other test composites (Ceci et al., 2017). However, these results are contrary to those collected in the study conducted by Sherif et al in 2020 (Sherif et al., 2020).

Evaluation of the shade values was also conducted using the VITA Classical scale and the VITA 3D Master colour system. The VITA Classical A1-D4 system is considered the gold standard shade value system used in dentistry, consisting of 16 natural tooth shades (Alnusayri et al., 2022). Most composite manufacturers name their shades after the VITA Classical nomenclature. The VITA 3D Master colour system consists of 26 physical shade tabs and 49 “intermediate shades” without physical representation. Following this system, shade selection should be conducted in the following order: first a number (value), then a letter (chroma), and lastly a number (hue) (Gómez-Polo et al., 2022).

In this study, the VITA Classical scale results demonstrated that 100 % of the composite specimens changed shade values after the 12-day period in coffee. Nevertheless, only Normo fill discs showed statistically significant differences, with the largest shade change being from shade A1 to A3.5.

In relation to the VITA 3D Master colour system, an analysis was conducted for each of the three dimensions of colour. In either material, a larger increase in the value gradation was detected with coffee as compared to water. Additionally, a greater increase was detected in the Normo fill specimens than the Admira fusion specimens. The only colour dimension that remained stable throughout the immersion period was chroma. In all combinations of composite group and solution the reported value was ‘M’. Finally, in Normo fill specimens, no statistically significant differences in hue were detected between water and coffee. Contrarily, in Admira specimens, the discs exhibited a decrease in hue by 1 degree with water. However, the discs remained stable with coffee.

Limitations

The two main limitations of this study include the in vitro conditions and disc fabrication technique. Firstly, the following study was performed under in vitro conditions. Therefore, several factors that could affect composite colour in the oral cavity, such as changes in mouth temperature, pH and salivary flow could not be accurately replicated. Future research is required to study these changes in an in vivo environment. Finally, the second limitation of the study was the disc fabrication technique. Slight discolouration was produced from the metal composite discs because of rusting. This likely affected the ΔE values in the control groups immersed in water. For this reason, for future studies, the use of silicone molds to create the composite discs would be utilized. This would avoid the issue of discolouration by the metal substance.

Conclusions

The colour stability of two composite materials was analyzed and compared after being submerged in a coffee solution for 12 days. Within the confines of this experimental study, the findings prompted the following conclusions.1.After analyzing the effect of coffee on the colour stability of nanohybrid composites and nanocomposites after being placed in coffee for 12 days, there was a significant variation in the colour in both materials. In addition, coffee produced significantly more discolouration as compared to immersion in water.2.After analyzing the effect of coffee on both materials, Ormocer-based nanohybrid composites (Admira fusion) had significantly greater colour stability as compared to UDMA-based nanocomposites (Normo fill nanoceram).

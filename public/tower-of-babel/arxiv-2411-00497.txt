Topological complexity of enumerative problems and classifying spaces of PU n \mathrm{PU}_{n}
Weiyan Chen, Xing Gu

Abstract
. We study the topological complexity, in the sense of Smale, of three enumerative problems in algebraic geometry: finding the 27 lines on cubic surfaces, the 28 bitangents and the 24 inflection points on quartic curves. In particular, we prove lower bounds for the topological complexity of any algorithm that finds solutions to the three problems and for the Schwarz genera of their associated covers. The key is to understand cohomology classes of the classifying spaces of projective unitary groups PU n \mathrm{PU}_{n} .

55M30, 55R80, 55R40

1.
Introduction

Enumerative geometry is concerned with counting the number of solutions to geometric problems. For example, it is classically known that over
\mathbb{C}

(1)

any smooth cubic surface has 27 lines;

(2)

any smooth quartic plane curve has 28 bitangent lines;

(3)

any generic quartic plane curve has 24 inflection points.

After knowing the number of solutions, a natural question is: How complex are the solutions to these problems? In this paper, we study the
topological complexity
, in the sense of Smale
[
11
]
, of the three enumerative problems listed above.

Following Smale, by an
algorithm
we mean a finite rooted tree consisting of a root for the input, leaves for the output, and internal nodes of the following two types:

computation nodes

and
branching nodes

.

The
topological complexity
of an algorithm is the number of branching nodes in the tree. The topological complexity of a problem
P
is the minimum of the topological complexity of any algorithm solving
P
. Smale
[
11
]
first proved a lower bound for the topological complexity of the problem of finding roots of a polynomial
f(z)=0
. Smale’s lower bounds were later improved by Vassiliev
[
13
]
, De Concini-Procesi-Salvetti
[
3
]
, and Arone
[
1
]
. After results about finding roots of polynomials which are 0-dimensional objects, it is natural to ask similar question about solutions to enumerative problems for high-dimensional objects such as curves and surfaces, which is mostly unknown. A first step in this direction is the work by the first author and Wan
[
2
]
concerning the topological complexity of finding inflection points on cubic plane curves. In this paper, we consider the following problems:

•

\mathbf{Line}(\epsilon)
: Given any cubic surface defined by a homogeneous polynomial
F(x,y,z,w)
of degree 3, find all of its 27 lines
(l_{1},\cdots,l_{27})
within
\epsilon
.

•

\mathbf{{Bitangent}}(\epsilon)
: Given any quartic curve defined by a homogeneous polynomial
F(x,y,z)
of degree 4, find all of its 28 bitangent lines
(l_{1},\cdots,l_{28})
within
\epsilon
.

•

\mathbf{Flex}(\epsilon)
: Given any quartic curve defined by a homogeneous polynomial
F(x,y,z)
of degree 4, find all of its 24 inflection points
(p_{1},\cdots,p_{28})
within
\epsilon
.

An algorithm is said to solve the problem
\mathbf{Line}(\epsilon)
if it inputs an arbitrary homogeneous cubic polynomial
F(x,y,z,w)
and outputs a sequence of lines
(l_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,l_{27}^{\prime})
such that each
l_{i}^{\prime}
is
\epsilon
-close to a lines
l_{i}
where
l_{i}
’s are the 27 distinct lines on the cubic surface defined by
F
. Here, the “within
\epsilon
” condition can be measured by any metric on the Grassmannian consisting of lines in
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
inducing the standard topology. Similar conditions hold for
\mathbf{{Bitangent}}(\epsilon)
and
\mathbf{Flex}(\epsilon)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

When
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, we have

(1)

the topological complexity of the problem
\mathbf{Line}(\epsilon)
is at least 15,

(2)

the topological complexity of the problem
\mathbf{{Bitangent}}(\epsilon)
is at least 8,

(3)

the topological complexity of the problem
\mathbf{Flex}(\epsilon)
is at least 8.

The lower bounds in Theorem
1.1
come from the following theorems about the topology of various parameter spaces associated to the three enumerative problems. First, consider

\displaystyle B_{\text{line}}

\displaystyle:=\{\text{nonsingular homogeneous cubic polynomials $F(x,y,z,w)$}\}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\displaystyle E_{\text{line}}

\displaystyle:=\{(F,l_{1},\cdots,l_{27}):\text{ $l_{i}$'s are the 27 lines on the cubic surface $F\in B_{\text{line}}$}\}.

The space
B_{\text{line}}
is an open submanifold of
\mathbb{CP}^{19}
consisting of all homogeneous cubic polynomials in four variables. The projection
E_{\text{line}}\to B_{\text{line}}
given by
(F,l_{1},\cdots,l_{27})\mapsto F
is a normal
S_{27}
-cover, where
S_{27}
acts on
E_{\text{line}}
by permuting the ordering of the 27 lines.

Definition 1

(
[
10
]
)
.

The
Schwarz genus
of a covering
E\to B
, denoted by
g(E\to B)
or simply
g(E)
, is the minimum size of an open cover of
B
consisting of open sets such that there exists a continuous section of the covering map
E\to B
over each open set.

Theorem 1.2
.

g(E_{\text{line}}\to B_{\text{line}})\geq 16
.

Similarly, consider

\displaystyle B_{\text{btg}}

\displaystyle:=\{\text{nonsingular homogeneous quartic polynomials $F(x,y,z)$}\}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\displaystyle E_{\text{btg}}

\displaystyle:=\{(F,l_{1},\cdots,l_{28}):\text{ $l_{i}$'s are the 28 bitangent lines to the curve $F\in B_{\text{btg}}$}\}

The space
B_{\text{btg}}
is an open submanifold of
\mathbb{CP}^{14}
consisting of all homogeneous quartic polynomials in three variables. The projection
E_{\text{btg}}\to B_{\text{btg}}
gives a normal
S_{28}
-cover.

Theorem 1.3
.

g(E_{\text{btg}}\to B_{\text{btg}})\geq 9
.

A generic quartic curve has 24 inflection points. However, unlike in the previous examples, being smooth is not enough for a quartic curve to have 24 distinct inflection points because two inflection points can coincide. Hence, we consider

\displaystyle B_{\text{flex}}

\displaystyle:=\{F(x,y,z):F\text{ defines a quartic curve with 24 distinct inflection points}\}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\displaystyle E_{\text{flex}}

\displaystyle:=\{(F,p_{1},\cdots,p_{24}):\text{ $p_{i}$'s are the 24 distinct inflection points on $F\in B_{\text{flex}}$}\}.

B_{\text{flex}}
is an open submanifold of
\mathbb{CP}^{14}.
The projection
E_{\text{flex}}\to B_{\text{flex}}
gives a normal
S_{24}
-cover.

Theorem 1.4
.

g(E_{\text{flex}}\to B_{\text{flex}})\geq 9
.

Let us briefly outline the proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. The parameter space of quartic curves admits a natural action of the projective unitary group
\mathrm{PU}_{3}=\mathrm{U}_{3}/Z(\mathrm{U}_{3})
. The lower bound in Theorem
1.4
is obtained by restricting the cover
E_{\text{flex}}\to B_{\text{flex}}
to a subspace of
B_{\text{flex}}
given by the
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-orbit of the the Klein quartic curve defined by
x^{3}y+y^{3}z+z^{3}x=0
. This orbit is homeomorphic to the homogeneous space
\mathrm{PU}_{3}/\mathrm{Aut}(F)
where
\mathrm{Aut}(F)
is the automorphism group of the Klein quartic curve
F
. The most difficult part of this proof is to find nonzero obstruction classes in the cohomology of this homogeneous space using results about the cohomology of the classifying spaces of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
. Similarly, to prove Theorems
1.2
, we consider the Fermat cubic surface
x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}+w^{3}=0
and its
\mathrm{PU}_{4}
-orbit. This proof is rather flexible and can be modified to study the topological complexity of enumerative problems other than the three we considered in this paper.

Can the inequalities in the theorems above be equalities? We don’t expect so. For example,
B_{\text{line}}
is an open submanifold of
\mathbb{CP}^{19}
and hence by the Andreotti–Frankel theorem is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex of dimension 19. For dimension reasons, we have that
g(E_{\text{line}}\to B_{\text{line}})\leq 20
by Corollary
2.3
below. Similarly, we can show that
g(E_{\text{line}}\to B_{\text{line}})\leq 15
and
g(E_{\text{flex}}\to B_{\text{flex}})\leq 15
. In general, it is a difficult problem to determine the exact value of Schwarz genus. Even for the most well-studied example, the Schwarz genus of the
S_{n}
-cover of the configuration space of
n
distinct unordered points in
\mathbb{C}
has various upper and lower bounds thanks to the works of Smale
[
11
]
, Vassiliev
[
13
]
, De Concini-Procesi-Salvetti
[
3
]
, and Arone
[
1
]
, but its exact value remains unknown for infinitely many integers
n
. The main point of this paper is to broaden the domain of study of topological complexity and Schwarz genus, from finding roots of polynomials to finding solutions of enumerative problems about curves and surfaces. We leave the problem of improving the bounds to future works.

The cohomology of the classifying space of
\mathrm{PU}_{n}
, which is the main technical tool in this paper, is not yet fully understood despite a half century of research. Kono-Mimura
[
8
]
, Toda
[
12
]
, Vezzosi
[
14
]
, and Vistoli
[
15
]
have made significant contributions.
More recently, the second author
[
6
]
determines the cohomology groups
H^{k}(B\mathrm{PU}_{n};\mathbb{Z})
for
k\leq 10
, which is a key ingredient of proofs in this paper. In contrast to the cohomology classes of
B\mathrm{U}_{n}
,
i.e.
the universal Chern classes, which have rich geometric interpretations and wide applications to geometric topology and algebraic geometry, the cohomology classes of
B\mathrm{PU}_{n}
do not have as many geometric interpretations and applications. For this reason, we believe that our results would be an interesting addition to the literature about the cohomology of
B\mathrm{PU}_{n}
which has been active in recent years.

Preliminary results

We first recall and prove some general results that will be used later in our proofs.

2.1.
General results about Schwarz genus

We recall some general results about Schwarz genus, mostly due to A. Schwarz
[
10
]
. We assume all spaces to be normal and Hausdorff. We also assume each open cover to have a partition of unity subordinated to it. This assumption holds if the space is paracompact.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
10
]
, p.71)
.

Let
i^{*}E\to B^{\prime}
denote the pullback of a cover
E\to B
along a continuous map
i:B^{\prime}\to B
. Then
g(i^{*}E\to B^{\prime})\leq g(E\to B)
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
10
]
, p.71)
.

Consider a covering

E\to B
with fiber
F
. Let

F^{*k}\to E_{k}\to B

denote the fiberwise join bundle where
F^{*k}
is the topological join of
F
with itself
k
times.
Then
g(E\to B)\leq k
if and only if
E_{k}\to B
has a continuous section.

Corollary 2.3

(
[
10
]
, p.76)
.

If
B
is a CW complex of dimension
d
, then
g(E\to B)\leq d+1.

Proof (Proposition
2.2

\Rightarrow
Corollary
2.3
).

The obstruction to extending a section of
E_{d+1}\to B
to the
i
-skeleton of
B
is an element in
H^{i+1}(B;\pi_{i}(F^{*(d+1)}))
. This cohomology group vanishes for
i\geq d
since
B
has cohomological dimension
d
. If
i\leq d-1
, then
\pi_{i}(F^{*(d+1)})=0
since
F^{*(d+1)}
is always
(d-1)
-connected. Hence there is no obstruction to a section.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(
[
10
]
, p.98)
.

Suppose that
E\to B
is a normal
\Gamma
-cover (or equivalently, a principal
\Gamma
-bundle) with a classifying map
cl:B\to B\Gamma
where
B\Gamma
is the classifying space of
\Gamma
. Then we have

g(E\to B)\geq\min\{k:\mathrm{H}^{i}(B\Gamma;A)\xrightarrow{cl^{*}}\mathrm{H}^{i}(B;A)\text{ is zero for any }i\geq k\}

for any
\Gamma
-module
A
. The integer on the right hand side of the inequality above is called the
homological
A
-genus
of the cover
E\to B.

Proposition 2.5

(Disconnected covers)
.

Consider a cover
E\to B
with path-components
E=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i}
where each
E_{i}\to B
is also a cover. Suppose that there exists an
m\in I
such that for any
i\in I
, there exists morphism of coverings
E_{i}\to E_{m}
. Then
g(E)=g(E_{m}).

Proof.

In general, a morphism of coverings
E\to E^{\prime}
implies that
g(E^{\prime})\leq g(E)
, because any section of
E
composes to a section of
E^{\prime}.
In our situation, we have two morphisms of covers

E_{m}\hookrightarrow E=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i}\longrightarrow E_{m}

where the first map is the natural inclusion and the second map is the disjoint union of morphisms
E_{i}\to E_{m}
for all
i\in I
. Hence we have
g(E_{m})\leq g(E)\leq g(E_{m}).

∎

Cohomology of
\mathrm{PU}_{n}

Consider a subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{PU}_{n}

to calculate the homological genus of the principal

Definition 2
.

E_{2}^{s,t}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{s,t}_{H^{*}(B\mathrm{PU}_{n};\mathbb{F}_{p})}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p},H^{*}(B\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})\big)\Rightarrow H^{s+t}(\mathrm{PU}_{n}/\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p}).

-grading in the Eilenberg-Moore spectral sequence. Here we follow the convention in

9
]

\operatorname{Tor}_{R}^{s,t}(M,N)\cong H_{-s,t}(P^{*}\otimes_{R}N)

\mathbf{Line}(\epsilon)

.

For

denote the subset of

consisting of all those inputs that will arrive at the
i
-th output leaf of the given algorithm. Then
B_{\text{line}}

A_{i}
’s. Each

is locally closed because it is the intersection of finitely many closed subsets consisting of inputs satisfying
h\leq 0
at those branching nodes where the path from the input root to the
i
-th output leaf turns to the right, and finitely many open subsets consisting of inputs satisfying
h>0

\epsilon
-close to a line
l_{i}
where
l_{i}
’s are the 27 distinct lines on the cubic surface
F
. By the Tietze Extension Theorem,
\psi_{i}
can be extended to an open subset

Recall that the
\mathrm{PU}_{4}
-orbit of the Fermat cubic surface
F
in
B_{\text{line}}
gives a well-defined map
\eta:\mathrm{PU}_{4}/K\to B_{\text{line}}
as defined in (
3.2
). Let

\epsilon_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}\text{ minimum distance between two distinct lines on $gF$ among all }g\in\mathrm{PU}_{4}.

\mathrm{PU}_{4}

\epsilon_{0}

Polynomial and combinatorial analogues of Gauss congruence
Fern Gossow

Abstract
The cyclic sieving phenomenon provides a link between a polynomial analogue of Gauss congruence known as q q -Gauss congruence, and a combinatorial analogue of Gauss congruence based on sequences of cyclic group actions. We strengthen this link in two major ways: by characterising q q -Gauss congruence via explicit formulae, and by developing a universal model for the combinatorics based on necklaces which allow beads to vary in both colour and length. This gives many novel examples of cyclic sieving involving necklaces, path walks, tubings and more. We extend the definition of Gauss congruence to sequences indexed by an arbitrary ranked semigroup, and synthesise known results into this theory.

An integer sequence

2
Semigroups

We give the necessary definitions and examples of semigroups which will be used frequently throughout the paper to index sequences.

A
commutative semigroup
is a pair
(S,+)
consisting of a set
S
and an associative, commutative operation
+:S\times S\to S
. We usually write
S
as shorthand for
(S,+)
. A
morphism
of commutative semigroups is a map
\phi:S\to T
such that
\phi(s_{1}+s_{2})=\phi(s_{1})+\phi(s_{2})
for every
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
denote the semigroup of positive integers with the usual addition.

Definition 2.1
.

A
ranked semigroup
is a commutative semigroup
S
with a specified morphism
\mathsf{rk}:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
, called the
rank
function.

Remark 2.2
.

Such semigroups are known as metrical semigroups (in
[
Sau98
]
) or semigroups with length (in
[
How95
]
). They have no additive identity.

A semigroup
S
has
finite decomposition
if there are only finitely many ways to express each
s\in S
in the form
s=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{k}
for
s_{i}\in S
. All finitely-generated ranked semigroups have this property.

Fix a ranked semigroup
S
and
s,t\in S
. Define
s-t:=\{u\mid u+t=s\}
. We define a partial order on
S
by setting
t\leqslant s
if
s=t
or
s-t
is nonempty. We call
S

cancellative
if
|s-t|\leqslant 1
for every
s,t\in S
.

For
d\geqslant 1
, define
d\cdot s:=s+s+\cdots+s
with
d
summands. If
s=d\cdot t
for some
s,t\in S
, we write
t\mid s
and set
s/t:=\mathsf{rk}(s)/\mathsf{rk}(t)=d
. The relation
|
is a partial order on
S
. We also define
s/d:=\{t\mid d\cdot t=s\}
and write
d\mid s
if
s/d
is nonempty. A semigroup is
torsion-free
if
|s/d|\leqslant 1
for every
s\in S
and
d\geqslant 1
.

Our first example is the free ranked semigroup on
A
, where the rank of each element is determined by a function
\psi
(although we allow
\psi
to take arbitrary integer values).

Example 2.3
.

Fix an arbitrary set
A
and a function
\psi:A\to\mathbb{Z}
. For
\alpha:A\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, set

\psi(\alpha):=\sum_{a\in A}\psi(a)\alpha(a)\hskip 8.50012pt\text{and}\hskip 8.50012pt|\alpha|:=\sum_{a\in A}\alpha(a).

Let
A^{\psi}
be the ranked semigroup of functions

\{\alpha:A\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\mid 0<|\alpha|,\psi(\alpha)<\infty\}

with pointwise addition and rank function
\mathsf{rk}(\alpha)=\psi(\alpha)
.

As a special case, define
*:A\to\mathbb{Z}
by
a\mapsto 1
for every
a\in A
. Then
*(\alpha)=|\alpha|
, so
A^{*}
is the ranked semigroup of functions with
0<|\alpha|<\infty
, and
\mathsf{rk}(\alpha)=|\alpha|
.

The ranked semigroup
A^{\psi}
is cancellative, torsion-free and has finite decomposition. For
d\geqslant 1
and
\alpha\in A^{\psi}
, we have
d\mid\alpha
if and only if
d\mid\alpha(a)
for every
a\in A
, in which case
(\alpha/d)(a)=\alpha(a)/d
. If
A=\{1\}
and
\psi(1)=1
, then
A^{\psi}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
S
be a ranked semigroup and let
T
be a commutative semigroup. Define the ranked semigroup

S[T]:=\{(s,t)\mid s\in S,\,t\in T\}

with
(s_{1},t_{1})+(s_{2},t_{2}):=(s_{1}+s_{2},t_{1}+t_{2})
and
\mathsf{rk}(s,t):=\mathsf{rk}(s)
.

We call this operation
chaining
. If
S
and
T
are ranked semigroups and
U
is a commutative semigroup, then
S[T][U]\cong S[T[U]]
under
((s,t),u)\mapsto(s,(t,u))
, so we write this element as
(s,t,u)
. Likewise, we have
S[T][U]\cong S[U][T]
under
(s,t,u)\mapsto(s,u,t)
.

3
Gauss congruence

We extend Gauss congruence to sequences
(-_{s})
indexed by a ranked semigroup
S
and state the analogue of Theorem
1.1
. We generalise a number of known identities relating Gauss congruences to their parameter sequences.

3.1
Definition

Definition 3.1
.

An integer sequence
(a_{s})
satisfies
Gauss congruence
(on
S
) if

\sum_{t\mid s}\mu(s/t)a_{t}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathsf{rk}(s)}

for every
s\in S
.

Remark 3.2
.

Gauss congruence was defined on arbitrary posets in
[
GGL22
]
, and viewing
S
as a poset under division recovers the above definition. For more information, see the proof of Theorem
3.3
in Section
9
.

3.2
Characterisation

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
S
be a ranked semigroup with finite decomposition and
(a_{s})
a sequence of integers indexed by
S
. The following are equivalent:

•

The sequence
(a_{s})
satisfies Gauss congruence.

•

There exists an integer sequence
(b_{s})
such that for every
s\in S
:

a_{s}=\sum_{t\mid s}b_{t}\mathsf{rk}(t)

(3.1)

•

There exists an integer sequence
(c_{s})
such that for every
s\in S
:

a_{s}=\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots=s}\mathsf{rk}(s_{1})c_{s_{1}}c_{s_{2}}\cdots.

(3.2)

Example 3.4
.

Fix a formal power series
D(t)=d_{0}+d_{1}t+d_{2}t^{2}+\cdots
with integer coefficients. The sequence
(a_{n,k})
given by

a_{n,k}:=[t^{k}]D(t)^{n}

satisfies Gauss congruence on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}[\mathbb{Z}]
. The associated sequence
(c_{n,k})
is given by
c_{1,k}:=d_{k}
and
c_{n,k}:=0
for
n>1
. When
D(t)=1+t
,
1/(1-t)
and
t/(1-t)
respectively we obtain the Gauss congruences

a_{n,k}=\binom{n}{k},\hskip 17.00024pta_{n,k}=\binom{n+k-1}{k},\hskip 8.50012pt\text{and}\hskip 8.50012pta_{n,k}=\binom{k-1}{k-n}

where the binomial coefficient is zero outside of its usual range.

By
[
GGL22
, Theorem 44]
(also see the proof of Theorem
3.3
), the Möbius function in the definition of Gauss congruence can be replaced with other number theoretic functions such as Euler’s totient
\phi
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a function such that
\varphi(1)=\pm 1
and

\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(n/d)\equiv 0\pmod{n}

for every
n\geqslant 1
. Then
(a_{s})
satisfies Gauss congruence if and only if

\sum_{t\mid s}\varphi(s/t)a_{t}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathsf{rk}(s)}

for every
s\in S
.

3.3
Identities

We generalise identities between the sequences
(a_{n})
,
(b_{n})
and
(c_{n})
in the case
S=\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
appearing in
[
DHL03
]
and
[
DHL05
]
.

For each summand of (
3.2
), define a function
\alpha:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
by setting
\alpha(t)
as the number of occurences of
t
. Summing over all relevant such functions gives

a_{s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in S^{*}\\
\tau(\alpha)=s\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathsf{rk}(s)}{|\alpha|}\binom{|\alpha|}{\alpha}\prod_{t\in S}c_{t}^{\alpha(t)}

(3.3)

where
\tau:S^{*}\to S
is given by
\tau(\alpha)=\sum_{s\in S}\alpha(s)s
, and

\binom{|\alpha|}{\alpha}:=\frac{(\sum_{a\in\alpha}\alpha(a))!}{\prod_{a\in A}\alpha(a)!}

for
\alpha\in A^{\psi}
(or in this case,
S^{*}
).

Alternatively, we can rewrite (
3.2
) as an almost-linear recurrence by ‘peeling off’ the final term in each summand. Then

a_{s}=\mathsf{rk}(s)c_{s}+\sum_{t+u=s}c_{t}a_{u}=\mathsf{rk}(s)c_{s}+\sum_{t<s}c_{t}a_{s-t}

(3.4)

where
a_{s-t}:=\sum_{u\in s-t}a_{u}
.

For
S=\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
, we can rewrite (
3.2
) as

a_{n}=\mathrm{tr}\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}&c_{3}&\cdots\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots\end{bmatrix}^{n}

(3.5)

which can be used to prove that
a_{n}=\mathrm{tr}(M^{n})
satisfies Gauss congruence for any integer matrix
M
. We also have a direct relationship between
(b_{n})
and
(c_{n})
expressed as an equality of formal power series

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}c_{n}x^{n}=1-\prod_{n\geqslant 1}(1-x^{n})^{b_{n}}.

(3.6)

Versions of these identities can be generalised by working over the ring of infinite
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations of symbols
\{x^{s}\mid s\in S\cup\{0\}\}
with
x^{s}x^{t}=x^{s+t}
for
s,t\in S
and where
1:=x^{0}
is the mutliplicative identity. This perspective in the case
S=\{1,\dots,r\}^{*}
was studied in
[
BHS18
]
, although a different generalisation of Gauss congruence is used.

4
q-Gauss congruence

As with Gauss congruence, we extend the notion of
q
-Gauss congruence from
[
Gor19
]
to ranked semigroups. We build a number of morphism-theoretic tools for constructing these congruences. The main result is Theorem
4.16
, which gives a number of explicit characterisations of
q
-Gauss congruences. This theory is foundational to finding examples of cyclic sieving results in Section
5

4.1
Setup

We work with sequences indexed by a fixed ranked semigroup
S
.

Definition 4.1
.

A sequence of integer polynomials
(f_{s}(q))
satisfies
q
-
Gauss congruence
(on
S
) if

\sum_{t\mid s}\mu(s/t)f_{t}(q^{s/t})\equiv 0\pmod{[\mathsf{rk}(s)]_{q}}

for every
s\in S
.

Gorodetsky in
[
Gor19
]
gave an extremely useful arithmetic characterisation of
q
-Gauss congruences, which holds in our generality. Recall that
\omega_{d}
denotes a primitive
d^{\text{th}}
root of unity.

Proposition 4.2
.

A sequence of integer polynomials
(f_{s}(q))
satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence if and only if

f_{s}(\omega_{d})=\sum_{t\in s/d}f_{t}(1)

for every
s\in S
and
d\mid\mathsf{rk}(s)
.

Before giving examples of
q
-Gauss congruences, we provide a number of tools for translating congruences between ranked semigroups. Proofs are given in Section
9
, and make heavy use of Proposition
4.2
. We refer to these operations by their names throughout the paper.

Definition 4.3
.

A morphism of ranked semigroups
\phi:S\to S^{\prime}
is;

•

rank-multiplying
if
\mathsf{rk}(s)\mid\mathsf{rk}(\phi(s))
and

•

rank-dividing
if
\mathsf{rk}(\phi(s))\mid\mathsf{rk}(s)

for every
s\in S
.

Lemma 4.4

(Pushforward)
.

Suppose
\phi:S\to T
is a rank-dividing morphism such that
\phi^{-1}(t)
is finite for every
t\in T
. If
(f_{s}(q))
satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence on
S
, then

g_{t}(q):=\sum_{s\in\phi^{-1}(t)}f_{s}(q)

satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence on
T
.

Taking
\phi
to be the rank function
\mathsf{rk}:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
, we obtain that

g_{n}(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in S\\
\mathsf{rk}(s)=n\end{subarray}}f_{s}(q)

gives a
q
-Gauss congruence on
\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
whenever
(f_{s}(q))
is a
q
-Gauss congruence on
S
.

Lemma 4.5

(Pullback)
.

Suppose
\phi:S\to T
is a rank-multiplying morphism such that
\phi
restricts to a bijection
\phi:s/d\to\phi(s)/d
for every
s\in S
and
d\mid\mathsf{rk}(s)
. If
(g_{t}(q))
satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence on
T
, then

f_{s}(q):=g_{\phi(s)}(q)

satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence on
S
.

Remark 4.6
.

When
S
is torsion-free, the bijection condition on
\phi
is equivalent to the following: if

and

, then

.

Taking
S^{\prime}
to be a subset of
S
closed under addition and division, using the inclusion morphism
\iota:S^{\prime}\to S
we obtain that
(f_{s}(q))
satisfies
q
-Gauss congruence on
S^{\prime}
whenever it does on
S
.

Finally, we consider multiplying
q
-Gauss congruences on torsion-free semigroups. We extend this to chains of semigroups from Definition

.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
S
is torsion-free and
(f_{s}(q))
and
(g_{s}(q))
are

-Gauss congruences on

, then so is

.

Remark 4.8
.

The proof allows a slight relaxation of the condition on

: we only need that
g_{s}(\omega_{d})=g_{s/d}(1)
for every

, rather than for every

.

Lemma 4.9

(Prefix Chaining)
.

Suppose
S
,

and

are torsion-free semigroups, and

is ranked. If
(f_{s,t}(q))
and
(g_{s,u}(q))
satisfy

-Gauss congruence on

respectively, then

-Gauss congruence on

.

(Suffix Chaining)
.

Suppose

,

and

are torsion-free semigroups, and

and

are ranked. If
(f_{s,t}(q))
and
(g_{t,u}(q))
satisfy

Recall the free ranked semigroup

5
Combinatorial analogue for Gauss congruence

Fix a ranked semigroup
S
and an integer sequence
(a_{s})
satisfying Gauss congruence. This congruence can be described by the parameter sequences
(b_{s})
or

from Theorem

-Gauss congruence

A combinatorial analogue of this congruence consists of a sequence of sets

and

Definition 5.1
.

A

is a sequence of finite sets where each

is equipped with an action of the cyclic group
\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{rk}(s)}
satisfying the following: for

fixed by
\mathsf{C}_{d}\leqslant\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{rk}(s)}
is

\#X_{s}^{\mathsf{C}_{d}}=\sum_{t\in s/d}\#X_{t}.

We call two Lyndon structures
(X_{s})
and
(Y_{s})

isomorphic
if there are bijections
X_{s}\xrightarrow{\sim}Y_{s}
which commute with the actions of the cyclic groups.

Theorem 5.2
.

For a Lyndon structure
(X_{s})
, the sequence
(a_{s})
defined by
a_{s}:=\#X_{s}
satisfies Gauss congruence. Moreover,
(b_{s})
counts orbits of order

in

, and
(X_{s},\mathsf{C}_{\mathsf{rk}(s)},f_{s}(q))
satisfies the cyclic sieving phenomenon for every

.

Proof.

If

counts orbits of order
\mathsf{rk}(s)
in each

, then

a_{s}=\#X_{s}=\sum_{t\mid s}\mathsf{rk}(t)b_{t}

which proves that
(a_{s})
is the Gauss congruence associated to
(b_{s})
. For

to satisfy cyclic sieving with

, we require that

for every

, which is equivalent to
q
-Gauss congruence by Lemma

.
∎

7.1

Tubings are a combinatorial object on graphs which correspond to the faces of the graph associahedron (see

Definition 7.1

Let

Given a tubing

On the interval graph

7.2
Bijections with lattice paths

We count tubings via bijections with lattice path walks. Fix the vectors

{\nearrow}:=(1,1),\hskip 8.50012pt{\searrow}:=(1,-1),\hskip 8.50012pt{\longrightarrow}:=(2,0)

in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

V:=\{{\nearrow},{\searrow},{\longrightarrow}\}
. A

of
length

is a word

in

such that

and the

of this step is the

. A

is a Delannoy path where all steps have nonnegative height, and this path is

if all steps
{\longrightarrow}
have positive height.

We use these lattice path walks to enumerate tubings by finding relevant bijections. In the case of
\Gamma_{n}
, we believe such a bijection is novel. We also give a lemma about strict Schröder paths which will be used in Example

There is a bijection between tubings on

•

•

{\longrightarrow}

•

at height

There is a bijection between improper tubings on

with

Let

7.3
Cyclic sieving results

Let

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We prove the equivalence between the first two statements by translating the more general result of
[
GGL22
, Theorem 16]
to our language. Write
t\wr s
if
t\mid s
but
t\neq s
. By induction on
\mathsf{rk}
, there is a unique function
\mu^{*}:S\times S\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

\mu^{*}(t,s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }t=s,\\
-\sum_{t\mid u\wr s}\mu^{*}(t,u)&\text{if }t\wr s,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This is the
generalised Möbius function
on the poset of
S
under division. By generalised Möbius inversion (see
[
Sta97
, Section 3.7]
) we have

a_{s}=\sum_{t\mid s}\mathsf{rk}(t)b_{t}\hskip 8.50012pt\text{if and only if}\hskip 8.50012pt\mathsf{rk}(s)b_{s}=\sum_{t\mid s}\mu^{*}(t,s)a_{t}.

For
t\mid s
, the interval

[t,s]:=\{u\in S\mid t\mid u\mid s\}

is isomorphic to the poset of integers
\{1,\dots,s/t\}
under division, so
\mu^{*}(t,s)=\mu(s/t)
and

a_{s}=\sum_{t\mid s}\mathsf{rk}(t)b_{t}\hskip 8.50012pt\text{if and only if}\hskip 8.50012ptb_{s}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{rk}(s)}\sum_{t\mid s}\mu(s/t)a_{t}.

There is always a unique rational sequence of values
(b_{s})
satisfying the appropriate relationship with
(a_{s})
, and the above equivalence proves that
(a_{s})
satisfies Gauss congruence if and only if
(b_{s})
is an integer sequence.

We give a novel proof involving
q
-Gauss congruence of the equivalence between the first and third statements. Recall from (
3.3
) that for any integer sequence
(c_{s})
we have the identity

a_{s}:=\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots=s}\mathsf{rk}(s_{1})c_{s_{1}}c_{s_{2}}\cdots=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in S^{*}\\
\tau(\alpha)=s\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathsf{rk}(s)}{|\alpha|}\binom{|\alpha|}{\alpha}\prod_{t\in S}c_{t}^{\alpha(t)}

In proving Theorem
4.16
we (independently) construct a
q
-Gauss congruence

evaluating at

to

, proving that this sequence satisfies Gauss congruence. Conversely, if
(a_{s})
is any integer sequence satisfying Gauss congruence, we can find a rational sequence
(c_{s})
satisfying the equation with
(a_{s})
. Define the sequence
(c_{s}^{\prime})
by
c_{s}^{\prime}:=\lfloor c_{s}\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the sequence
(a_{s}^{\prime})
defined by

a_{s}^{\prime}=\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots=s}\mathsf{rk}(s_{1})c_{s_{1}}^{\prime}c_{s_{2}}^{\prime}\cdots

satisfies Gauss congruence. Assume by contradiction that

is not an integer sequence, and let

c_{s}=c_{s}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\mathsf{rk}(s)}\sum_{t\mid s}\mu(s/t)a_{t}-\frac{1}{\mathsf{rk}(s)}\sum_{t\mid s}\mu(s/t)a_{t}^{\prime}.

But each term on the right-hand side is an integer, which contradicts

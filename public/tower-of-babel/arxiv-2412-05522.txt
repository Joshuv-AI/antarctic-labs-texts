Clique Covers and Decompositions of Cliques of Graphs
József Balogh, Jialin He, Robert A. Krueger, The Nguyen, Michael C. Wigal

Abstract
In 1966, Erdős, Goodman, and Pósa showed that if G G is an n n -vertex graph, then at most ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor cliques of G G are needed to cover the edges of G G , and the bound is best possible as witnessed by the balanced complete bipartite graph. This was generalized independently by Győri–Kostochka, Kahn, and Chung, who showed that every n n -vertex graph admits an edge-decomposition into cliques of total ‘cost’ at most 2 ​ ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ 2\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor , where an i i -vertex clique has cost i i . Erdős suggested the following strengthening: every n n -vertex graph admits an edge-decomposition into cliques of total cost at most ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor , where now an i i -vertex clique has cost i − 1 i-1 . We prove fractional relaxations and asymptotically optimal versions of both this conjecture and a conjecture of Dau, Milenkovic, and Puleo on covering the t t -vertex cliques of a graph instead of the edges. Our proofs introduce a general framework for these problems using Zykov symmetrization, the Frankl–Rödl nibble method, and the Szemerédi Regularity Lemma.

It is easy to see that

We are interested in the following extremal question: what is the maximum value of

In other words, it is the hardest to cover the edges of a graph with cliques when the graph is triangle-free and the number of edges is maximized with respect to this condition; furthermore, such a covering by cliques may be replaced by decomposition into edges and triangles. Bollobás

1.1
Proof framework

We first introduce some more notation. For a function

The

For a graph

such that for every

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\end{subarray}}f(K)\geq 1.

(1)

In this formulation, for a cost vector

\mathbf{c}
-cost of a
t
-clique cover of

-clique cover
is an assignment of

denote the minimum total

A

Our first general main theorem shows that the maximizer, at least for the fractional version, is always a complete multipartite graph.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let

n
-vertex graph,

The proof of Theorem

Zykov’s proof

Theorem 1.8
.

Let

If

Let

t\geq 1
, we have

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})\leq\max\{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{0}\to V_{1}),\mathbf{c}),\ \mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{1}\to V_{0}),\mathbf{c})\}

and

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})\leq\max\{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{0}\to V_{1}),\mathbf{c}),\ \mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{1}\to V_{0}),\mathbf{c})\}.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for the cover number and note the small changes needed to prove the lemma for the decomposition number.

Observe that
V_{0}\cup V_{1}
is an independent set in
G
,
G(V_{0}\to V_{1})
, and
G(V_{1}\to V_{0})
. Also observe that, since
V_{0}\cup V_{1}
is a set of pairwise clones in
G(V_{0}\to V_{1})
and
G(V_{1}\to V_{0})
, any permutation of
V(G)
fixing
V(G)\setminus(V_{0}\cup V_{1})
is an automorphism of
G(V_{0}\to V_{1})
and
G(V_{1}\to V_{0})
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all these permutations.

For
i\in\{0,1\}
, let
f_{i}
be an optimal
\mathbf{c}
-cost fractional
t
-clique cover of
G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i})
. Let
\bar{f}_{i}
be the average of
f_{i}\circ\sigma
over all permutations
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
. Since each
f_{i}\circ\sigma
is an optimal
\mathbf{c}
-cost fractional
t
-clique cover of
G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i})
, it is easy to check that
\bar{f}_{i}
is one as well. Because of this averaging, we have that
\bar{f}_{i}(K\cup\{v\})
does not depend on
v\in V_{0}\cup V_{1}
for cliques
K
in the neighborhood of
V_{i}
.

Define
f:\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as follows:

\displaystyle f(K)=\begin{cases}\frac{|V_{0}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\bar{f}_{0}(K)+\frac{|V_{1}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\bar{f}_{1}(K),&\quad|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0,\\
\bar{f}_{0}(K),&\quad|K\cap V_{0}|=1,\\
\bar{f}_{1}(K),&\quad|K\cap V_{1}|=1,\end{cases}

which is well-defined for cliques
K\in\mathcal{K}(G)
since
V_{0}\cup V_{1}
is an independent set, and hence
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|\leq 1
. We claim that
f
is a fractional
t
-clique cover of
G
. Indeed, for
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
with
|T\cap V_{i}|=1
for some
i\in\{0,1\}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}f(K)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\geq 1,

since the set of cliques
K
in the sum is the same in
\mathcal{K}(G)
and
\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))
. (For decompositions instead of covers, the final inequality above would be equality.) For
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
with
|T\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}f(K)=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0\end{subarray}}\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\bar{f}_{i}(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap V_{i}|=1\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\Big).

(2)

Let
K
be a clique in the neighborhood of
V_{i}
. Recall, for
v\in V_{0}\cup V_{1}
, as
\bar{f}_{i}(K\cup\{v\})
does not depend on
v
, for
i\in\{0,1\}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap V_{i}|=1\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)=\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap V_{i}|=1\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{|V_{1-i}|}{|V_{i}|}\right)\bar{f}_{i}(K)=\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=1\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K).

Continuing (
2
), it follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}f(K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=1\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\Big)\geq\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}=1.

(Again, for decompositions instead of covers, the inequality above would be equality.)
A similar calculation bounds the
\mathbf{c}
-cost of
f
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}c_{|K|}f(K)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0\end{subarray}}c_{|K|}f(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=1\end{subarray}}c_{|K|}f(K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap(V_{0}\cup V_{1})|=0\end{subarray}}c_{|K|}\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\bar{f}_{i}(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
|K\cap V_{i}|=1\end{subarray}}c_{|K|}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{0,1\}}\Big(\frac{|V_{i}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}(G(V_{1-i}\to V_{i}))}c_{|K|}\bar{f}_{i}(K)\Big)=\frac{|V_{0}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\left\lVert\bar{f}_{0}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+\frac{|V_{1}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\left\lVert\bar{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})\ \leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{|V_{0}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{1}\to V_{0}),\mathbf{c})+\frac{|V_{1}|}{|V_{0}|+|V_{1}|}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{0}\to V_{1}),\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \max\{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{0}\to V_{1}),\mathbf{c}),\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G(V_{1}\to V_{0}),\mathbf{c})\}.\qed

By repeatedly symmetrizing, we eventually obtain a complete multipartite graph, yielding Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Observe that the maximal sets of pairwise clones are the equivalence classes of
\sim
on
V(G)
, where
u\sim v
if and only if
N(u)=N(v)
. If there exist disjoint maximal sets of pairwise clones
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
in
G
with no edges between
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
, then by Lemma
2.1
, at least one of the symmetrizations,
V_{0}
to
V_{1}
or
V_{1}
to
V_{0}
does not decrease the fractional
t
-clique cover/decomposition number of the graph. At the same time, this symmetrization decreases the number of maximal sets of pairwise clones, since vertices which were previously clones remain clones. Repeat this symmetrization process until
V(G)
is partitioned into maximal sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
of pairwise clones for some
k
, where there is an edge between every pair of
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
with
i\neq j
. Since the
V_{i}
’s are sets of pairwise clones, this final graph is a complete
k
-partite graph.
∎

3
Solving linear programs: proof of Theorem
1.8

In this section, we aim to maximize
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})
and
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})
over all
n
-vertex complete multipartite graphs
H
.
We first show that, through appropriate normalization, this problem is equivalent to the following collections of linear programs (LPs).

Let
k\geq 2
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
be nonnegative reals such that
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
. These are the parameters of our LPs; here,
k
is the number of parts of the complete multipartite graph
H
, and
x_{i}
is the fraction of vertices in part
i
, so naturally
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
. Since every clique of
H
meets each part in at most one vertex, the type of a clique can be identified with the set
I\subseteq[k]
of parts that it meets. The variables of our LPs are
f(I)
, representing the total weight assigned to cliques of type
I
, for
f:2^{[k]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
I\subseteq[k]
.

For the cover problem, the LP in question, parameterized by
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
, is

\begin{array}[]{rll}\text{minimize}&\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}c_{|I|}f(I)&\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{I\supseteq T}f(I)\geq\prod_{i\in T}x_{i},&\forall T\in\binom{[k]}{t},\\
&f(I)\geq 0,&\forall I\subseteq[k].\end{array}

(CCLP)

For the decomposition problem, the LP in question is

\begin{array}[]{rll}\text{minimize}&\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}c_{|I|}f(I)&\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{I\supseteq T}f(I)=\prod_{i\in T}x_{i},&\forall T\in\binom{[k]}{t},\\
&f(I)\geq 0,&\forall I\subseteq[k].\end{array}

(CDLP)

These LPs are the cover/decomposition problems for weighted complete graphs, where the weights on the
t
-cliques are derived from weights on the vertices. Note that these LPs are feasible by taking
f(T)=\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}
for all
T\in\binom{[k]}{t}
and all other
f(I)=0
, and they are also bounded.
We now show the equivalence of these LPs to maximizing
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})
and
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})
over all
n
-vertex complete multipartite graphs
H
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a nonnegative sequence, and let
t\geq 2
. Let
H
be an
n
-vertex complete multipartite graph with parts
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
, and let
x_{i}=|V_{i}|/n
for
i\in[k]
. If
f
is an optimal solution to (
CCLP
) with parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
, then
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})=n^{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}
. Similarly, if
f
is an optimal solution to (
CDLP
) with parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
, then
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})=n^{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for the cover number and note the small changes needed to prove the lemma for the decomposition number.

Let
f
be a solution to (
CCLP
) with parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
. We create a fractional
t
-clique cover
g
of
H
as follows: for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(H)
, let
g(K)=n^{t}f(J)/\prod_{j\in J}|V_{j}|
, where
J=J(K)=\{j\in[k]:K\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset\}
. Let
\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(H)
and
T=T(\tilde{T})=\{i\in[k]:\tilde{T}\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
. We define an empty product to be
1
, as usual. The total weight of the cliques of
H
containing
\tilde{T}
is

\sum_{I\supseteq T}n^{t}\frac{f(I)}{\prod_{i\in I}|V_{i}|}\prod_{\ell\in I\setminus T}|V_{\ell}|=n^{t}\sum_{I\supseteq T}\frac{f(I)}{\prod_{i\in T}|V_{i}|}\geq n^{t}\prod_{i\in T}\frac{x_{i}}{|V_{i}|}=1,

where the inequality comes from (
CCLP
), and would be replaced by equality of (
CDLP
) for the decomposition number. Thus
g
is a fractional
t
-clique cover of
H
. The total
\mathbf{c}
-cost of
g
is

\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}c_{|I|}n^{t}\frac{f(I)}{\prod_{i\in I}|V_{i}|}\prod_{i\in I}|V_{i}|=n^{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}},

and hence
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})\leq n^{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}
.

For the other direction, let
g
be a fractional
t
-clique cover of
H
. We create a feasible solution to (
CCLP
) as follows: for each
I\subseteq[k]
, let
H(I)=\{K\in\mathcal{K}(H):K\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset\text{ iff }i\in I\}
, and set

f(I)=\frac{1}{n^{t}}\sum_{K\in H(I)}g(K).

Then for every
T\in\binom{[k]}{t}
, we have

\sum_{I\supseteq T}f(I)=\sum_{I\supseteq T}\frac{1}{n^{t}}\sum_{K\in H(I)}g(K)=\sum_{\tilde{T}\in H(T)}\frac{1}{n^{t}}\sum_{I\supseteq T}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in H(I)\\
\tilde{T}\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)\geq\sum_{\tilde{T}\in H(T)}\frac{1}{n^{t}}=\prod_{i\in T}x_{i},

where the inequality comes from the definition (
1
) of a
t
-clique cover and would be replaced by equality for decompositions. The total
\mathbf{c}
-cost of
g
is

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}c_{|I|}f(I)=\frac{1}{n^{t}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}(H)}c_{|K|}g(K)=\frac{1}{n^{t}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}},

and hence the optimal value of (
CCLP
) is at most
\frac{1}{n^{t}}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})
.
The decomposition case is identical, with each covering inequality replaced by equality. Hence the same argument gives the CDLP statement.
∎

Lemma
3.1
tells us that in order to prove the bounds
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H)\leq\eta n^{t}
or
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(H)\leq\eta n^{t}
for all complete multipartite
H
, it suffices to find feasible solutions
f
to (
CCLP
) or (
CDLP
) for all parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
such that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}\leq\eta
. We do this first for the cover number, corresponding to the first part of Theorem
1.8
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k\geq t\geq 2
be integers, and let

x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
be nonnegative real numbers satisfying
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{k}

and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=1.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a sequence of nonnegative reals such that
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\leq c_{t}
for all
i\geq t
. Then there exists a feasible solution
f
to (
CCLP
) such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}\leq c_{t}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right).

Proof.

Define
f:2^{[k]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

f(\{1,2,\dots,j\})=\left(x_{j}-x_{j+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}

for
t\leq j\leq k
, where we let
x_{k+1}=0
, and
f(I)=0
for all other
I
. We first show that
f
is a feasible solution to (
CCLP
). For
T\in\binom{[k]}{t}
with
m=\max(T)
, we have

\sum_{I\supseteq T}f(I)=\sum_{j=m}^{k}f(\{1,2,\dots,j\})=\sum_{j=m}^{k}\left(x_{j}-x_{j+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}=x_{m}\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\geq\prod_{i\in T}x_{i},

where the last inequality follows from
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{k}
, which also shows that
f(I)\geq 0
for all
I
. Thus
f
is feasible. Using
c_{j}-c_{j-1}\leq c_{t}
for all
j>t
and
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=t}^{k}c_{j}\left(x_{j}-x_{j+1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)\left(c_{t}x_{t}+\sum_{j=t+1}^{k}x_{j}(c_{j}-c_{j-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)\sum_{j=t}^{k}c_{t}x_{j}=c_{t}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right).\qed

It is more difficult to find solutions to (
CDLP
) than (
CCLP
) because of the strict equality conditions. With more work, we are able to give a good enough bound on (
CDLP
) to imply Theorem
1.8
(ii).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a sequence of nonnegative reals such that
2c_{i}\geq c_{i-1}+c_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 3
. Then when
t=2
, there exists a feasible solution to (
CDLP
) such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}\leq c_{2}\cdot\max_{J\subseteq[k]}\left(\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j\not\in J}x_{j}\right).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=2
, the optimal solution
f
to (
CDLP
) is trivially
f(\{1,2\})=x_{1}x_{2}
and
0
elsewhere. Since
x_{1}+x_{2}=1
, we have
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=c_{2}x_{1}(1-x_{1})
.

Suppose
k\geq 3
, and let
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{k}
be given. We begin with a
g:2^{[k-1]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that for every
i,j\in[k-1]
with
i<j
, we have

\sum_{I\ni i,j}g(I)=\begin{cases}x_{i}x_{j}&\text{ if }j\neq k-1,\\
x_{i}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})&\text{ if }j=k-1.\end{cases}

That is, we have a feasible solution to (
CDLP
) when
x_{k-1}
and
x_{k}
are merged; in the formulation of complete multipartite graphs,
g
corresponds to a fractional clique-decomposition of the graph where we delete all the edges between the smallest two parts. Using
g
, we write down a feasible solution to (
CDLP
) with
\mathbf{c}
-cost at most that of
g
. Let
\xi:2^{[k-3]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be arbitrary for now. For
I\subseteq[k-2]
, we define

\begin{array}[]{llll}f(I)&=&g(I)&+\mathbf{1}_{k-2\in I}\cdot\xi(I\setminus\{k-2\}),\\
f(I\cup\{k-1\})&=\ \frac{x_{k-1}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}&g(I\cup\{k-1\})&-\mathbf{1}_{k-2\in I}\cdot\xi(I\setminus\{k-2\}),\\
f(I\cup\{k\})&=\ \frac{x_{k}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}&g(I\cup\{k-1\})&-\mathbf{1}_{k-2\in I}\cdot\xi(I\setminus\{k-2\}),\\
f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})&=&0&+\mathbf{1}_{k-2\in I}\cdot\xi(I\setminus\{k-2\}).\end{array}

Observe that for
\xi=0
, this definition of
f
splits up
g
among
x_{k-1}
and
x_{k}
proportionally. We can interpret the role of
\xi
as moving equal weights from
I\cup\{k-1\}
and
I\cup\{k\}
to their union and intersection; the hope is that for a suitable choice of
\xi
, the weight moved to
I\cup\{k-1,k\}
will satisfy the constraint
\{i,j\}=\{k-1,k\}
of (
CDLP
). We claim that, for any choice of
\xi
,
f
satisfies the all constraints of (
CDLP
) except possibly the nonnegativity constraints and the constraint corresponding to
\{i,j\}=\{k-1,k\}
. Observe that for every
I\subseteq[k-2]
, we have

f(I)+f(I\cup\{k-1\})+f(I\cup\{k\})+f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})=g(I)+g(I\cup\{k-1\}).

Let
i,j\in[k]
with
i<j
. If
j\leq k-2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k]}f(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k-2]}f(I)+f(I\cup\{k-1\})+f(I\cup\{k\})+f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k-2]}g(I)+g(I\cup\{k-1\})=\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k-1]}g(I)=x_{i}x_{j}.

Similarly, if
j=k-1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k]}f(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I\subseteq[k-2]}f(I\cup\{k-1\})+f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})=\sum_{i\in I\subseteq[k-2]}\frac{x_{k-1}\cdot g(I\cup\{k-1\})}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k-1}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k-1]}g(I)=\frac{x_{k-1}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}x_{i}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})=x_{i}x_{k-1}.

Finally, if
j=k
and
i\leq k-2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k]}f(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I\subseteq[k-2]}f(I\cup\{k\})+f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})=\sum_{i\in I\subseteq[k-2]}\frac{x_{k}\cdot g(I\cup\{k-1\})}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k-1]}g(I)=\frac{x_{k}}{x_{k-1}+x_{k}}x_{i}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})=x_{i}x_{k}.

The missing constraint,
\{i,j\}=\{k-1,k\}
, is

\sum_{i,j\in I\subseteq[k]}f(I)=\sum_{I\subseteq[k-2]}f(I\cup\{k-1,k\})=\sum_{I\subseteq[k-3]}\xi(I)=x_{k-1}x_{k}.

Choosing
\xi(I)=\frac{x_{k-1}x_{k}}{x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})}g(I\cup\{k-2,k-1\})
satisfies this constraint:

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k-3]}\xi(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k-1}x_{k}}{x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})}\sum_{k-2,k-1\in I\subseteq[k-1]}g(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k-1}x_{k}}{x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})}x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})=x_{k-1}x_{k}.

The nonnegativity of
f
either follows directly from the nonnegativity of
g
, or from the following calculation for this choice of
\xi
: for
I\subseteq[k-2]
with
k-2\in I
, we have

\displaystyle f(I\cup\{k-1\})

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k-1}(x_{k-2}-x_{k})}{x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})}g(I\cup\{k-1\})\geq 0,

\displaystyle f(I\cup\{k\})

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k}(x_{k-2}-x_{k-1})}{x_{k-2}(x_{k-1}+x_{k})}g(I\cup\{k-1\})\geq 0.

Thus, with this choice of
\xi
,
f
is feasible for (
CDLP
). We now compute the
\mathbf{c}
-cost of
f
as

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k]}c_{|I|}f(I)=\sum_{I\subseteq[k-3]}\sum_{J\subseteq\{k-2,k-1,k\}}c_{|I\cup J|}f(I\cup J)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k-3]}\bigg(c_{|I|}g(I)+c_{|I|+1}\left(g(I\cup\{k-2\})+g(I\cup\{k-1\})+\xi(I)\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +c_{|I|+2}\left(g(I\cup\{k-2,k-1\})-2\xi(I)\right)+c_{|I|+3}\xi(I)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[k-1]}c_{|I|}g(I)+\sum_{I\subseteq[k-3]}\left(c_{|I|+1}-2c_{|I|+2}+c_{|I|+3}\right)\xi(I)\leq\sum_{I\subseteq[k-1]}c_{|I|}g(I)=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}},

since
c_{|I|+1}-2c_{|I|+2}+c_{|I|+3}\leq 0
and
\xi(I)\geq 0
.

By induction, we may take
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}
to be at most

c_{2}\max_{J\subseteq[k-1]}\left(\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}^{\prime}\right)\left(\sum_{j\not\in J}x_{j}^{\prime}\right)\leq c_{2}\max_{J\subseteq[k]}\left(\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j\not\in J}x_{j}\right),

where
x_{j}^{\prime}=x_{j}
if
j\in[k-2]
and
x_{k-1}^{\prime}=x_{k-1}+x_{k}
.
∎

We quickly derive Theorem
1.8
from Lemmas

,
3.2
, and
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let

be a complete multipartite graph with parts
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
, and let
x_{i}=|V_{i}|/n
for

, where
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{k}
.

For the first part of Theorem
1.8
, by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we have

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H,\mathbf{c})\leq n^{t}c_{t}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}x_{i}\right)=c_{t}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}|V_{i}|\right)\left(\sum_{i=t}^{k}|V_{i}|\right).

By standard optimization techniques, the right side is maximized when
|V_{1}|
,
|V_{2}|
, …,
|V_{t-1}|
,
|V_{t}|+|V_{t+1}|+\ldots+|V_{k}|
are as equal as possible. This optimum value is simply
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(T_{n,t},\mathbf{c})
.

For the second part of Theorem
1.8
, by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
, we have

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{2}(H,\mathbf{c})\leq n^{2}c_{2}\max_{J\subseteq[k]}\left(\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j\not\in J}x_{j}\right)=c_{2}\max_{J\subseteq[k]}\left(\sum_{j\in J}|V_{j}|\right)\left(\sum_{j\not\in J}|V_{j}|\right).

By standard optimization techniques, the right side is maximized when there is a bipartition of the

’s that is as equal as possible, matching the
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{2}(T_{n,2},\mathbf{c})
.
∎

We take the rest of this section to discuss optimal solutions to (
CCLP
) and (
CDLP
). We encode the variables of the dual LP as a function
g:\binom{[k]}{t}\to\mathbb{R}
. The dual LP to (
CCLP
) is

\begin{array}[]{rll}\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\binom{[k]}{t}}g(T)\displaystyle\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}&\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{T\subseteq I}g(T)\leq c_{|I|},&\forall I\subseteq[k],\\
&g(T)\geq 0,&\forall T\in\binom{[k]}{t}.\end{array}

(CCdual)

The dual LP to (
CDLP
) is the same, just without the nonnegativity constraints:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\text{maximize}&\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\binom{[k]}{t}}g(T)\displaystyle\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}&\\
\text{subject to}&\displaystyle\sum_{T\subseteq I}g(T)\leq c_{|I|},&\forall I\subseteq[k].\end{array}

(CDdual)

We remark on two interesting cases: when
c_{i}=1
for all
i
, and when
c_{i}=i-1
for all
i
. When
c_{i}=1
for all
i
, we claim that the optimal value of (
CCLP
) is
\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}
. That is, we can determine
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(H)
for all complete multipartite graphs. This optimal value is realized by
f([k])=\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}
and
0
otherwise, where
x_{1}\geq\ldots\geq x_{k}
. Optimality is witnessed by
g([t])=1
and
0
otherwise.

Now consider (
CDLP
) when the cost vector is
c_{i}=i-1
and
t=2
, that is, the original setting of Erdős’s problem. These weights may seem arbitrary, but they turn out to be special: the dual (
CCdual
) to the cover problem is a maximum spanning tree LP, that is, interpreting
g
as (fractionally) selecting the edges of a weighted complete graph, the constraints are that every
i
vertices induces at most
i-1
edges. With this interpretation in hand, the optimal solution to (
CCdual
) is just
g(\{1,j\})=1
for all
2\leq j\leq k
, and
0
otherwise, with optimal value
x_{1}(1-x_{1})
. The dual (
CDdual
) to the decomposition problem drops the nonnegativity constraints of (
CCdual
), which make many techniques applicable to (
CCdual
) no longer valid. Nevertheless, we can still use the same

to show that the optimal value of (
CDLP
) is at least
x_{1}(1-x_{1})
. We believe that this should in fact be the optimal value of (
CDLP
), but we were unable to prove it. It appears that the optimal solutions depend on various relations among the
x_{i}
, such as whether
x_{1}-x_{2}\geq x_{2}-x_{3}
.

4
Integer to fractional: proof of Theorem
1.9

The main tools of the proof of Theorem
1.9
are Szemerédi’s regularity lemma and the Frankl–Rödl nibble. The following is a high-level sketch of the proof. Given
\varepsilon>0
and an
n
-vertex graph
G
, we ‘clean’
G
in a standard way using the regularity lemma to obtain a subgraph
\tilde{G}
of
G
such that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(\tilde{G})}+\varepsilon n^{t}
. This
\tilde{G}
has a nice partite structure, and we also need
\tilde{G}
to have bounded clique number for technical reasons (for example, the dependence of
\varepsilon_{0}
on
k
in Lemma
4.4
; see the remark at the end of Section
4.3
). We use the information contained in an optimal fractional
t
-clique cover of
\tilde{G}
to partition
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\tilde{G})
among
K\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{G})
, and then for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(\tilde{G})
, we apply the Frankl–Rödl nibble to obtain an integer
t
-clique cover (of the required total weight) of the associated
t
-cliques. This shows that for these ‘cleaned’ graphs, the fractional and integer
t
-clique cover numbers are close to each other. Since the fractional
t
-clique cover number is bounded by Theorem
1.7
, we get the desired bound on
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(\tilde{G})}
and hence on
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}
as well.

In Section
4.1
we describe Szemerédi’s regularity lemma and the associated cleaning. In Section
4.2
we state the Frankl–Rödl nibble method and give our application of it. In Section
4.3
we describe how to use a fractional
t
-clique cover of a cleaned graph to obtain an integer
t
-clique cover of the same graph, via an application of the Frankl–Rödl nibble. Finally, in Section
4.4
we give an additional technical lemma needed to get bounded clique number, and then we compile all the pieces into a proof of Theorem
1.9
.

One bit of notation is particularly helpful in simplifying the technicality of our statements and proofs. We say
x=a\pm b
(where
b
is always positive) to mean
a-b\leq x\leq a+b
. Furthermore, for a monotone function
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we say
x=f(a\pm b)
to mean
f(a-b)\leq x\leq f(a+b)
. We read this notation going left-to-right, so
a\pm b=c\pm d
means
[a-b,a+b]\subseteq[c-d,c+d]
. Finally, we have
(a\pm b)+(c\pm d)=(a+c)\pm(b+d)
; and if
a
and
c
are positive,
(a\pm b)(c\pm d)=(ac+bd)\pm(ad+bc)
.

For a graph

4.2
The Nibble Method

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, we denote by
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
the
degree
of a vertex
v
, which is the number of edges of
\mathcal{H}
containing
v
. The
co-degree
of a pair of vertices
u,v
, denoted by
d_{\mathcal{H}}(u,v)
, is the number of edges of
\mathcal{H}
containing both
u
and
v
. The following simple statement of the Frankl–Rödl nibble, due to Pippenger (see
[
1
, Theorem 4.7.1]
or
[
10
, Theorem 8.4]
), states that a nearly regular
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph with small co-degrees has a nearly perfect edge cover/packing, that is, a cover/packing with nearly
n/r
edges.

Theorem 4.5
.

For every integer
r\geq 2
and reals
C\geq 1
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
, there exist
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}(r,C,\delta^{\prime})>0
and
D_{0}=D_{0}(r,C,\delta^{\prime})
such that for every
n
and
D\geq D_{0}
the following holds. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

for all but at most
\gamma^{\prime}n
vertices
x\in V({\cal H})
,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(x)=(1\pm\gamma^{\prime})D
,

(ii)

for all
x\in V(\mathcal{H})
,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(x)<CD
, and

(iii)

for any two distinct
x,y\in V(\mathcal{H})
,
d_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)<\gamma^{\prime}D
.

Then
\mathcal{H}
has an edge packing (i.e., matching) of size at least
(1-\delta^{\prime})(n/r)
.

We remark here that Theorem
4.5
is stated in
[
1
, Theorem 4.7.1]
in terms of the existence of nearly perfect coverings. It is well-known that the existence of a nearly perfect covering implies the existence of a nearly perfect packing.

We will apply Theorem
4.5
in the following way. Recall that
R
is the reduced graph of the cleaned graph
G
(see Section
4.1
for the cleaning). Fix
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
, and recall that
G(K)
is the set of cliques in
G
‘corresponding’ to
K
. We want a subset of
G(K)
which covers as efficiently as possible all
t
-cliques corresponding to some
t
-clique
T\subseteq K
. To do this, we set up a
\binom{k}{t}
-uniform
\binom{k}{t}
-partite hypergraph
\mathcal{H}_{K}
whose parts are
G(T)
for
t
-cliques
T\subseteq K
and whose edges are given by
G(K)
, where a hyperedge
\tilde{K}\in G(K)
has vertices
\tilde{T}\in G(T)

with
\tilde{T}\subset\tilde{K}
.

Unfortunately,
\mathcal{H}_{K}
cannot have a nearly optimal edge cover, since the parts of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
do not necessarily have the same size — their sizes depend on the densities of the pairs of parts of
G
, as given by Lemma
4.4
. However, when we randomly sample subsets of these parts so that these subsets all have about the same size, then we can use Lemma
4.4
to check the hypotheses of Theorem
4.5
and thus obtain a nearly perfect edge cover. How exactly we choose these random subsets depends on the fractional
t
-clique cover/decomposition of
G
that we start with. We postpone these details until Section
4.3
. For now, we show in general terms that the nibble method can be applied to this ‘randomly thinned’ subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

For every pair of integers
k
and
t
such that
1\leq t<k
and pair of positive real numbers
d\leq 1
and
\delta
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(k,d,\delta)>0
such that for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and for all
\alpha_{0}>0
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(k,\alpha_{0},d,\delta)
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be a graph with vertex partition
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}
where each
V_{i}
is an independent set of size
n\geq n_{0}
and
(V_{i},V_{j})
is an
\varepsilon
-regular pair of density
d_{i,j}\geq d
for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
, and let
\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha\leq d^{\binom{t}{2}}
.

For each
T\in\binom{[k]}{t}
, let
G(T)=\{T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G):\forall i\in T,|T^{\prime}\cap V_{i}|=1\}
, let
p_{T}=\frac{\alpha n^{t}}{|G(T)|}
, and let
G(T)^{*}
be a random subset of
G(T)
where we include each element independently with probability
p_{T}
, independently of the other
T^{\prime}\in\binom{[k]}{t}
. Then with probability at least
1-1/n
,
there exists a partition of
\bigcup_{T\in\binom{[k]}{t}}G(T)^{*}
into two sets
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}

such that

(i)

\mathcal{S}_{1}
can be partitioned into sets of the form
\{T^{\prime}:T^{\prime}\subseteq K^{\prime}\}
for
K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{k}(G)
;

(ii)

|\mathcal{S}_{1}|=(1\pm\delta)\binom{k}{t}\alpha n^{t}
;

(iii)

|\mathcal{S}_{2}|\leq\delta\alpha n^{t}
.

Note that, crucially,
\alpha_{0}
in Lemma
4.6
may be taken arbitrarily close to
0
, bounded away from zero independently of only
n
. This is important, because later
\alpha
may depend on these regularity lemma parameters in a way we cannot meaningfully control (that is,
\alpha
will be inversely polynomial in
\ell
, the number of parts in the regularity partition, which may be an exponential tower of height inversely polynomial in
\varepsilon
; see the remark at the end of Section
4.3
). Also note that
1-1/n
is not the optimal probability — what we really get is an error probability exponentially small in
n
.

When
t=2
, we recover the main technical lemma of Yuster
[
27
]
in the special case of clique packings. Conceptually, the
t=2
case may be simpler than the
t>2
case, as the randomly thinned
\mathcal{H}_{K}
is just a random spanning subgraph of our
\varepsilon
-regular partition, and so an off-the-shelf counting lemma could be applied. We note that there are dependencies among the indicator random variables whose sum is the degree of a given vertex in the randomly thinned
\mathcal{H}_{K}
. In order to estimate these degrees, Yuster splits each of these random variables into classes of independent variables and applies the Chernoff bound to these classes. We instead apply McDiarmid’s concentration inequality
[
19
]
for Lipschitz functions of independent random variables. We give these inequalities now.

Proposition 4.7

(Chernoff bound)
.

Let
X
be the sum of finitely many independent indicator random variables. Then for every
0\leq\eta<1
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\geq\eta\mathbb{E}[X]\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\eta^{2}}{3}\mathbb{E}[X]\right).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
f:\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{X}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables such that
X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
for every
i\in[n]
. If for all
i\in[n]
and for all
x_{1}\in\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}
, the function
f
satisfies

\sup_{x^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{i}}\left|f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})-f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i}^{\prime},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})\right|\leq b_{i}

for some
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, then for every
\mu>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\mathbb{E}[f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})]\right|\geq\mu\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\dfrac{2\mu^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\ (1-\gamma)^{2{\binom{k}{t}}-2}\alpha^{4\binom{k}{t}-4}2^{-k}t^{-1}{d_{T}^{2}d_{K}^{2}}\hat{d}_{K}^{-4}n\geq\ \alpha^{4{\binom{k}{t}}-4}2^{-2{\binom{k}{t}}-k+2}t^{-1}{d_{T}^{2}d_{K}^{2}}\hat{d}_{K}^{-4}n,

where the last inequality follows as
\gamma\leq 1/2
.

Therefore, by McDiarmid’s inequality and (
13
), with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Omega(n))
,

\displaystyle\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{T})\leq(1+\gamma)\mathbb{E}[\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{T})]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\gamma)^{{\binom{k}{t}}}\deg_{G}(\tilde{T})\alpha^{\binom{k}{t}-1}\frac{d_{T}}{\hat{d}_{K}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\gamma)^{{\binom{k}{t}}}n^{k-t}\alpha^{\binom{k}{t}-1}\frac{d_{T}}{\hat{d}_{K}}\quad\quad\text{[because $\deg_{G}(\tilde{T})\leq n^{k-t}$]}

\displaystyle=(1+\gamma)^{{\binom{k}{t}}}\frac{d_{T}}{d_{K}\hat{d}_{K}}D\leq 2^{\binom{k}{t}}d^{-2\binom{k}{2}\binom{k-2}{t-2}}D=CD,

where the last inequality follows from
\gamma\leq 1
and
1\geq d_{i,j}\geq d
for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
, and the last equality is the definition of
C
from (
4
). Taking a union bound over all
O(n^{t})
choices of
\tilde{T}
shows that the maximum degree of
\mathcal{H}
is bounded by
CD
, that is, condition (ii) of Theorem
4.5
holds, with high probability.

Finally we establish condition (iii) of Theorem
4.5
, that is, for every
T_{1},T_{2}\in\binom{[k]}{t}
, the co-degree of
\tilde{T}_{1}\in G(T_{1})^{*}
and
\tilde{T}_{2}\in G(T_{2})^{*}
is small compared to
D
. Their co-degree is the number of
\tilde{K}\in G(K)^{*}
which contain
\tilde{T}_{1}\cup\tilde{T}_{2}
, which is trivially at most
n^{k-|T_{1}\cup T_{2}|}\leq n^{k-t-1}
. Thus, for
n
sufficiently large in terms of
d
,
k
, and
\alpha_{0}
, and
\delta^{\prime}
, we have

n^{k-t-1}<\gamma^{\prime}n^{k-t}\alpha_{0}^{\binom{k}{t}-1}d^{\binom{k}{2}\left(1-\binom{k-2}{t-2}\right)}\leq\gamma^{\prime}n^{k-t}\alpha_{0}^{\binom{k}{t}-1}\frac{d_{K}}{\hat{d}_{K}}\leq\gamma^{\prime}D,

establishing condition (iii) of Theorem
4.5
.

Thus for
n
sufficiently large with respect to
d
,
k
, and
\alpha_{0}
, by Theorem
4.5
, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}
has an edge packing of size at least
(1-\delta^{\prime})|V(\mathcal{H})|/{\binom{k}{t}}
with high probability. Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the set of
t
-cliques covered in this packing, and let
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\bigcup_{T\in\binom{[k]}{t}}\left(G(T)^{*}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{1}\right)
.

We now show that
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
satisfy properties (i), (ii), and (iii) of Lemma
4.6
, completing the proof. By definition of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, (i) holds.
As
\gamma
is sufficiently small (see (
9
)), we have

|\mathcal{S}_{1}|=\binom{k}{t}(1\pm\delta^{\prime})|V(\mathcal{H})|/\binom{k}{t}=(1\pm\delta){\binom{k}{t}}\alpha n^{t},

implying (ii). Since
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is the set of uncovered vertices of
V(\mathcal{H})
, using that
\gamma
is sufficiently small (see (
9
)), we may conclude

|\mathcal{S}_{2}|\leq(1+\gamma)\binom{k}{t}\alpha n^{t}-|\mathcal{S}_{1}|\leq\delta\alpha n^{t}.\qed

4.3
Fractional to integer covers for cleaned graphs

Recall that an
(n,\ell,\varepsilon,d)
-graph
G
has
n
vertices which can be equi-partitioned into independent sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}
such that for every
1\leq i<j\leq\ell
, either
(V_{i},V_{j})
is an
\varepsilon
-regular pair with density at least
d
or
V_{i}\cup V_{j}
is an independent set. Here we show that such graphs have fractional
t
-clique cover number and decomposition number close to their integer
t
-clique cover number and decomposition number, respectively. Furthermore, this closeness is effectively bounded in terms of the clique number of the graph.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
t\geq{2}
be an integer and let
\mathbf{c}=(c_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real numbers, where each
c_{i}\geq 1
for all
i\geq t
. For every integer
b
and pair of real numbers
0<d,\rho<1
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(b,d,(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b},\rho)>0
, such that for every
\ell
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(b,d,(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b},\rho,\ell)

such that the following holds. Let
G
be an
(n,\ell,\varepsilon,d)
-graph such that
n\geq n_{0}
,
\omega(G)\leq b
, and
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})+\rho n^{t}

and

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})+\rho n^{t}.

Proof.

We prove the lemma for covers and decompositions at the same time, noting the relevant differences when they arise.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{\ell}
be a vertex partition of
G
witnessing that
G
is an
(n,\ell,\varepsilon,d)
-graph. Let
R
be the reduced graph of
G
, that is, the graph on
[\ell]
where
i
is adjacent to
j
if and only if
(V_{i},V_{j})
is a regular pair of density at least
d
, and denote by
d_{i,j}
the density of
(V_{i},V_{j})
. Let
\rho\in(0,1)
be given.

Let
f:\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an optimal fractional
t
-clique cover/decomposition of
G
. Note that as
\mathbf{c}
is a positive sequence,
f
is supported on cliques with at least
t
vertices. Let
\hat{f}:\mathcal{K}(R)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\hat{f}(K)=\sum_{K^{\prime}\in G(K)}f(K^{\prime}).

We will use
\hat{f}
as a guide for producing a nearly optimal integer
t
-clique cover/decomposition of
G
via Lemma
4.6
. Essentially, if
\hat{f}(K)
is not too small, then we will apply Lemma
4.6
to the graph induced on
G
by the parts corresponding to
K
with probabilities
p_{T}=\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}
for some suitably large
C
not depending on
T
or
K
. For each
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
, we couple our choices of random subsets of
G(T)
for different
K
in such a way that they cover/partition each
G(T)
. Care was taken in the set-up of Lemma
4.6
so that it applies as long as
\hat{f}(K)/C
is bounded away from zero independently of
n
, but
\hat{f}(K)
can depend on any of the other parameters, in particular, the regularity parameters
\ell
and
\varepsilon
. When
\hat{f}(K)
is too small, we will forego applying Lemma
4.6
and instead just put each
t
-clique of
G
randomly assigned to
K
into the integer cover/decomposition.

We first show that the
\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}
will be suitable choices for the probabilities
p_{T}
in our applications of Lemma
4.6
. We claim that

\hat{f}(K)\leq C\cdot|G(T)|

(14)

for every
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
and every
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
with
T\subseteq K
, where

C=C(d,b,t):=2\binom{b}{t}d^{-\binom{t}{2}}.

First, observe that by Lemma
4.4
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
with respect to
d
and
b
, we have

\frac{1}{2}d^{\binom{t}{2}}(n/\ell)^{t}\leq|G(T)|\leq(n/\ell)^{t}.

(15)

We say that
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
is
tight
with respect to
f
if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T^{\prime}\subseteq K^{\prime}\end{subarray}}f(K^{\prime})=1.

Note that if
f
is a decomposition, then all
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
are tight with respect to
f
, by definition.
If
f
is an optimal fractional
t
-clique cover, then every
K^{\prime}
in the support of
f
must contain a tight
t
-clique; otherwise, we could reduce
f(K^{\prime})
slightly, keeping
f
the same everywhere else, and obtain another fractional
t
-clique cover of
G
with slightly lower total weight. Thus, for both the cover and decomposition cases, for every
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
,

\sum_{K^{\prime}\in G(K)}f(K^{\prime})\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{*}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)\\
T^{*}\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{\prime}\in G(T^{*})\\
T^{\prime}\text{tight}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T^{\prime}\subseteq K^{\prime}\end{subarray}}f(K^{\prime})\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{*}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)\\
T^{*}\subseteq K\end{subarray}}|G(T^{*})|\leq\binom{b}{t}(n/\ell)^{t}.

By (
15
), we have that

\hat{f}(K)=\sum_{K^{\prime}\in G(K)}f(K^{\prime})\leq\binom{b}{t}\frac{n^{t}}{\ell^{t}}\leq 2\binom{b}{t}d^{-\binom{t}{2}}|G(T)|=C\cdot|G(T)|,

that is, (
14
) holds.

Additionally, note that, for every
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\hat{f}(K)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\sum_{K^{\prime}\in G(K)}f(K^{\prime})=\sum_{T^{\prime}\in G(T)}\sum_{K^{\prime}\supseteq T^{\prime}}f(K^{\prime})\geq\sum_{T^{\prime}\in G(T)}1=|G(T)|,

(16)

where the first equality follows from the definition of
\hat{f}
, the second equality from the fact that
G
is an
(n,\ell,\varepsilon,d)
-graph, and the inequality from
f
being a
t
-clique cover. When
f
is additionally a decomposition, this inequality is in fact an equality (furthermore, in this case, (
16
) implies (
14
) with
C=1
, although we will not use this fact).

Now we use
\hat{f}
to choose random subsets of
G(T)
for
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
on which to apply Lemma
4.6
.
For each
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
, we arbitrarily cover/partition the interval
[0,1]
with intervals
I^{i}_{T,K}
, where
i\in[C]
and
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
with
T\subseteq K
such that
|I_{T,K}^{i}|=\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}
. Such a covering/partition exists because, by (
14
) we have
\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}\leq 1
, and by (
16
), we have

\sum_{K\supseteq T}\sum_{i\in[C]}\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}=\ \sum_{K\supseteq T}\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{|G(T)|}\geq 1,

where the inequality is in fact equality in the case of decompositions.
For each
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
, choose
x_{T^{\prime}}\in[0,1]
independently and uniformly at random. For
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
,
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
with
T\subseteq K
, and
i\in[C]
, let

S_{T,K}^{i}=\left\{T^{\prime}\in G(T):x_{T^{\prime}}\in I_{T,K}^{i}\right\}.

Observe that since the
I_{T,K}^{i}
cover/partition
[0,1]
for every
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
, we have that
\{S_{T,K}^{i}\}_{i,K}
covers/partitions
G(T)
for every
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(R)
. Furthermore, for each fixed
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
and
i\in[C]
,
S_{T,K}^{i}
is a random subset of
G(T)
where we include each element independently with probability
|I^{i}_{T,K}|=\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C|G(T)|}
, independently of the other
S_{T^{\prime},K}^{i}
with
T^{\prime}\subseteq K
.

Let

\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(\rho,b,\ell,(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b},d):=\min\left\{\frac{\rho}{3}\left(\binom{\ell}{\leq b}Cc_{t}2\binom{b}{t}\right)^{-1},d^{\binom{t}{2}}\right\},

and
\delta
be chosen smaller than
\min_{t\leq j\leq b}\{\frac{\rho c_{j}}{3c_{t}^{2}},\frac{\rho}{3c_{t}}\}
. Since
\alpha_{0}
depends only on
\rho
,
(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b}
,
d
,
\ell
,
b
and
t
, Lemma
4.6
holds for
n
sufficiently large: for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
and
i\in[C]
, if
\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}\geq\alpha_{0}n^{t}
, then with probability at least
1-1/n
there exists a partition
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i},\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}
of
\bigcup_{T\subseteq K}S_{T,K}^{i}
, such that

•

\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i}
can be partitioned into sets of the form
\{T^{\prime}:T^{\prime}\subseteq K^{\prime}\}
for
K^{\prime}\in G(K)
,

•

|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i}|\leq(1+\frac{\rho}{3c_{t}})\binom{|K|}{t}\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}
, and

•

|\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}|\leq\frac{\rho c_{|K|}}{3c_{t}^{2}}\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}
.

Since there are at most
\binom{\ell}{\leq b}C
choices of
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
and
i\in[C]
and
n
is sufficiently large, we may take a union bound over all such choices so that such a partition
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i},\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}
exists for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
and
i\in[C]
with high probability. When
\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}<\alpha_{0}n^{t}
, we simply set
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}=\bigcup_{T\subseteq K}S_{T,K}^{i}
. For
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
, the expected size of
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}
is
\binom{|K|}{t}\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}
, so if
\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}<\alpha_{0}n^{t}
, then by the Chernoff bound (Proposition
4.7
),

|\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}|\leq 2\binom{|K|}{t}\alpha_{0}n^{t}

with probability at least
1-1/n
. This means that we are able to take a union bound as before.

By construction, every
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
appears in at least one
\mathcal{S}_{K,i}^{j}
, where
K\in\mathcal{K}(R)
,
i\in[C]
, and
j\in\{1,2\}
, and in exactly one in the case of decompositions. Thus the union of the given clique decompositions of all the
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{K,i}
together with the
t
-cliques of all the
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{K,i}
forms an integer
t
-clique cover/decomposition of
G
. The
\mathbf{c}
-cost of this cover/decomposition is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C\geq\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in[C]}\left(c_{|K|}\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{K,i}^{1}|}{\binom{|K|}{t}}+c_{t}|\mathcal{S}_{K,i}^{2}|\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C<\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in[C]}c_{t}|\mathcal{S}_{K,i}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C\geq\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in[C]}\left(c_{|K|}\left(1+\frac{\rho}{3c_{t}}\right)+c_{t}\frac{\rho c_{|K|}}{3c_{t}^{2}}\right)\frac{\hat{f}(K)}{C}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C<\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i\in[C]}c_{t}\cdot 2\binom{|K|}{t}\alpha_{0}n^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C\geq\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}c_{|K|}\left(1+\frac{2\rho}{3c_{t}}\right)\hat{f}(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}(R)\\
\hat{f}(K)/C<\alpha_{0}n^{t}\end{subarray}}Cc_{t}\cdot 2\binom{|K|}{t}\alpha_{0}n^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\ (1+2\rho/3c_{t})\|\hat{f}\|_{\mathbf{c}}+\binom{\ell}{\leq b}Cc_{t}2\binom{b}{t}\alpha_{0}n^{t}\ \leq\ \left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+\rho n^{t},

where the final inequality follows from
\|\hat{f}\|_{\mathbf{c}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}\leq c_{t}n^{t}
and the definition of
\alpha_{0}
.
∎

Remark.

There are two main technical challenges in the proof of Lemma

that may be difficult to see through the details. First, we need the cliques in the cover/decomposition to have size bounded independently of
\varepsilon
(and hence

) so that Lemma
4.6
may be applied. Second, when we are not able to apply Lemma

, that is, when

, we need that the total cost of the

-cliques covered is small. Since there may be many such

(polynomial in

, which depends on
\varepsilon

.

Proof of Theorem

We first establish some preliminary lemmas which allow us to reduce to ‘cleaned’ graphs with bounded clique number.

.

Let
G

n

, and
\mathbf{c}
be a nonnegative real sequence. If

. Define

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}\leq\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{t}|\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)\setminus\mathcal{K}_{t}(G^{\prime})|\leq\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2},

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, and

such that

. Then there exists an
n
-vertex subgraph

of

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(\tilde{G},\mathbf{c})}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(\tilde{G},\mathbf{c})+\rho\tilde{n}^{t}/6

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})}\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G^{\prime},\mathbf{c})}+c_{t}n^{t}/b\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G^{\prime},\mathbf{c})}+\rho n^{t}/6.

Thus we conclude

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})}\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(\tilde{G},\mathbf{c})}+\rho n^{t}.\qed

5
Integrality gaps and packings

Let
G
be a graph. In our proof of Theorem
1.9
, we prove that for a suitable cleaned graph
\tilde{G}
,
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(\tilde{G},\mathbf{c})
is close to
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})
in an appropriate sense. The ratio/difference between
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})
and
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})}
(
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})
and
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})
) is known as the multiplicative/additive
integrality gap
for the
t
-clique cover (decomposition) number.

5.1
Upper bounds

Theorem
1.10
states that the additive integrality gap for decompositions is
o(n^{t})
. To prove this, we first need an inverse version of Lemma
4.10
for the decomposition case.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, let
t\geq 2
and
\mathbf{c}
be a nonnegative real sequence such that
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\leq c_{t}
for all
i\geq t
. If
G^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
G
, then for all integers
b\geq t+1
,

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G^{\prime},\mathbf{c})\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G,\mathbf{c})+\frac{2c_{t}n^{t}}{(b-t)^{t-1}}+c_{t}\binom{b}{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2}.

Proof.

Let
b\geq t+1
. Let
g:\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be an optimal fractional
t
-clique decomposition of
G
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)=\{K\in\mathcal{K}(G):|K|>b\}
, and recall
\mathcal{K}_{b}(G)
is the set of all cliques of
G
of size
b
. We modify
g
in the following way. Consider the map
\hat{g}:\mathcal{K}(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, such that

\displaystyle\hat{g}(Q)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G),\\
g(Q)&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{K}_{<b}(G),\\
g(Q)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
Q\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{K}_{b}(G).\end{cases}

Essentially, we take each clique on more than
b
vertices and replace it by all of its
b
-cliques, appropriately weighted. Note by construction,
\hat{g}
has support on cliques of size at most
b
, and
\hat{g}
is a
t
-clique decomposition of
G
, since for all
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}\hat{g}(Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}_{b}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}g(Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
T\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}g(Q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}g(Q)=1.

Indeed, for every fixed
K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)
containing
T
,
there are exactly

\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}
cliques

Q\in\mathcal{K}_{b}(G)
satisfying
T\subseteq Q\subseteq K
.
Thus, after reversing the order of the double sum above, this multiplicity
cancels the factor
\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\hat{g}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{K}_{b}(G)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
Q\subseteq K\end{subarray}}c_{b}g(K)\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)}c_{|K|}g(K)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{b}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{K}_{b}(G)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)\\
Q\subseteq K\end{subarray}}g(K)\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{b}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)}g(K)\binom{|K|}{b}\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+(b-t+1)c_{t}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)}g(K)\binom{|K|}{b}\binom{|K|-t}{b-t}^{-1}

(17)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+(b-t+1)c_{t}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{>b}(G)}g(K)\binom{|K|}{t}\binom{b}{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+(b-t+1)c_{t}|\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)|\binom{b}{t}^{-1}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{t}(b-t+1)\frac{n^{t}}{(b-t)^{t}}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+2c_{t}\frac{n^{t}}{(b-t)^{t-1}},

where (
17
) follows from
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\leq c_{t}
for all
i\geq t
, and (
18
) follows from
g
being a
t
-clique decomposition, meaning that each
t
-clique of
G
is covered by cliques of weight totaling exactly one.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the set of cliques of
G
, which contain an edge of
E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})
, i.e.,

\mathcal{Q}=\{K\in\mathcal{K}(G):E(K)\cap(E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime}))\neq\emptyset\}.

We modify
\hat{g}
to a
t
-clique decomposition
g^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
by replacing each clique of
\mathcal{Q}
with its
t
-cliques:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(Q)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{Q},\\
\hat{g}(Q)&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\setminus(\mathcal{Q}\cup\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)),\\
\hat{g}(Q)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{Q}\\
Q\subseteq K\end{subarray}}\hat{g}(K)&\text{ if }Q\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)\setminus\mathcal{Q}.\end{cases}

Indeed,
g^{\prime}
has support on
\mathcal{K}(G^{\prime})
and for any
T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G^{\prime})
, we have,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G^{\prime})\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}g^{\prime}(Q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\setminus\mathcal{Q}\\
T\subsetneq Q\end{subarray}}\hat{g}(Q)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{Q}\\
T\subsetneq Q\end{subarray}}\hat{g}(Q)+\hat{g}(T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{K}(G)\\
T\subseteq Q\end{subarray}}\hat{g}(Q)=1.

Thus, restricting the domain of
g^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{K}(G^{\prime})
yields a fractional
t
-clique decomposition of
G^{\prime}
. Now, as
\hat{g}
was supported on cliques of size at most
b
, we have,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\hat{g}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(c_{t}\binom{|Q|}{t}\hat{g}(Q)-c_{|Q|}\hat{g}(Q)\right)\leq\left\lVert\hat{g}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{t}\binom{b}{t}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\hat{g}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\hat{g}\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+c_{t}\binom{b}{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathbf{c}}+2c_{t}\frac{n^{t}}{(b-t)^{t-1}}+c_{t}\binom{b}{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

We may suppose
\rho<1
. Let
b,b^{\prime}>t
be integers such that

\frac{2c_{t}}{(b^{\prime}-t)^{t-1}}\leq\frac{\rho}{100}\text{ and }\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}\frac{100c_{t}}{\rho}\leq b.

Choose
d\in(0,1)
and a positive integer
m_{0}
such that

1/m_{0}+d\leq\frac{\rho}{100c_{t}}\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}^{-1}.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}(b,d,(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b},\rho/100)
and
n_{0}(b,d,(c_{i})_{i=t}^{b},\rho/100)
be as in Lemma
4.9
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph such that
n\geq\max\{4b,n_{0}(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\}
. Let

\varepsilon<\min\left\{\varepsilon_{0},\frac{\rho}{100c_{t}}\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}^{-1}\right\}.

By Lemma
4.11
, there exists
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
such that
|V(G^{\prime})|=n
,
\omega(G^{\prime})\leq b
, and
|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|\leq n^{2}/b
. By Lemma
5.1
and our choice of
b
,

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G)\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G^{\prime})+c_{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G^{\prime})+\rho n^{t}/100.

By Lemma
4.2
,
G^{\prime}
has a subgraph
\tilde{G}
such that
\tilde{G}
is an
(\tilde{n},\ell,\varepsilon,d)
-graph,
\tilde{n}=|V(\tilde{G})|\geq(1-\varepsilon)n
, and
|E(G^{\prime})\setminus E(\tilde{G})|\leq(2\varepsilon+1/m_{0}+d)n^{2}
where
m_{0}\leq\ell\leq M(\varepsilon)
. By Lemma
5.1
and our choice of
\varepsilon
,
m_{0}
, and
d
,

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(\tilde{G})+c_{t}|V(G^{\prime})\setminus V(\tilde{G})|n^{t-1}+c_{t}|E(G^{\prime})\setminus E(\tilde{G})|n^{t-2}\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(\tilde{G})+4\rho n^{t}/100.

As
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
\tilde{n}=(1-\varepsilon)n\geq n_{0}
, by Lemma
4.9
,

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(\tilde{G})\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(\tilde{G})+\rho\tilde{n}^{t}/100\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(\tilde{G})+\rho n^{t}/100.

By Lemma
5.1
(with
b^{\prime}
serving the role of
b
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(\tilde{G})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G^{\prime})+\frac{4c_{t}n^{t}}{(b^{\prime}-t)^{t-1}}+c_{t}\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}|E(G^{\prime})\setminus E(\tilde{G})|n^{t-2}+c_{t}\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}|V(G^{\prime})\setminus V(\tilde{G})|n^{t-1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G^{\prime})+6\rho n^{t}/100

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G)+6\rho n^{t}/100+\frac{2c_{t}n^{t}}{(b^{\prime}-t)^{t-1}}+c_{t}\binom{b^{\prime}}{t}|E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})|n^{t-2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G)+8\rho n^{t}/100.

Combining this sequence of inequalities, we conclude that

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}_{t}(G)\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{t}(G)+\rho n^{t}.\qed

We conjecture that the additive gap for the
t
-clique cover number is small.

Conjecture 5.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and positive integer
t
, there exists
n_{0}
such that for every graph
G
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices

\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)\leq\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G)+\varepsilon n^{t}.

Certainly Conjecture
5.2
could be made for non-uniform costs, although it is not clear what (mild) conditions on the cost vector are necessary. It is not clear if the cover analogue of Lemma
5.1
is true. We note that removing a single edge could increase the cover number by a significant amount:
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}(K_{1,1,n})=n
, while
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}(K_{2,n})=2n
, despite
K_{1,1,n}
and
K_{2,n}
differing in only one edge.

5.2
Lower bounds

We show that the additive gap could be as big as
n^{2-o(1)}
for some graphs. To do this, we first construct a graph with a large multiplicative gap using a construction of Scheinerman and Trenk
[
23
]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

For every
t\geq 2
and even
n\geq 2t
, there exists a graph
G_{n}
on
n
vertices such that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G_{n})}/\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G_{n})=\Omega_{t}(\log(n))
.

Proof.

Let
t\geq 2
and
n
be given such that
n
is even and
n\geq 2t
. Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph with
n/2
classes of size
2
each. By the discussion on (
CCLP
) and (
CCdual
), when
c_{i}=1
for all
i
, the optimal value of (
CCLP
) is
\prod_{i=1}^{t}x_{i}
(see the penultimate paragraph of Section
3
). Thus we have
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G)=2^{t}
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}>\left\lfloor\log_{2}(n/t)\right\rfloor
.

Let
\{v_{i},u_{i}\}
for
i\in[n/2]
be the parts of
G
, and let
f
be an optimal
t
-clique cover of
G
, i.e.,
\left\lVert f\right\rVert={\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}
, and assume the support of
f
consists only of maximal cliques of
G
. For each maximal clique
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\text{max}}(G)
we associate a binary vector
x
of length
n/2
such that

\displaystyle x_{i}=\begin{cases}0\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }u_{i}\in K,\\
1\quad\quad\quad\text{ if }v_{i}\in K.\end{cases}

By induction, for every
k\geq 1
, for every set
B
of
k
binary vectors of length
n/2
, there exists a set of coordinates
L_{k}
of size at least
\left\lfloor n/2^{k}\right\rfloor
such that for every
x\in B
, every coordinate of
x
is the same on
L_{k}
(but for
x\neq y\in B
they could be different).
Let
k=\left\lfloor\log_{2}(n/t)\right\rfloor
so that
n/2^{k}\geq t
.

Let
B
be the set of binary vectors associated to the support of
f
. If
\left\lVert f\right\rVert=|B|\leq k
, then there exists
t
coordinates such that for every
x\in B
, on those
t
coordinates
x
has the same value. In other words, there is a set of
t
classes of
G
on which the support of
f
covers only two cliques. Since
t\geq 2
, this implies that there is a
t
-clique not covered by
f
, and hence
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert>k=\left\lfloor\log_{2}(n/t)\right\rfloor
.
∎

The gap exhibited in Proposition
5.3
is best possible up to the constant factor. To see this, we can interpret the
t
-clique cover problem as a set cover problem in the following way. We let the ground set be
\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)
and let our sets consist of
\{T\in\mathcal{K}_{t}(G):T\subseteq K\}
for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(G)
. The classical greedy algorithm of Chvátal
[
5
]
for the set cover problem shows
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}/\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G)=O(\log(|\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)|))
. As
|\mathcal{K}_{t}(G)|\leq\binom{n}{t}
for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
, we conclude
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G)}/\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G)=O_{t}(\log(n))
.

To show that the worst additive gap is at least
n^{2-o(1)}
, we make use of Proposition
5.3
and a strengthening of a classical result of Erdős, Frankl, and Rödl
[
7
]
due to Mubayi and Verstraëte
[
21
]
.

Theorem 5.4

(Mubayi and Verstraëte
[
21
]
)
.

For every
R,N\geq 3
with
N\geq R\geq\log N
, there exists an
N
-vertex
R
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with the following properties:

(i)

|E(\mathcal{H})|\geq N^{2}/R^{8\sqrt{\log_{R}N}}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{H}
is linear, i.e., for every pair of distinct edges
e,f\in\mathcal{H}
we have
|e\cap f|\leq 1
.

(iii)

\mathcal{H}
is triangle-free, that is, for every three distinct edges,
e,f,g\in\mathcal{H}
, if
|e\cap f|=|f\cap g|=|g\cap e|=1
, then
|e\cap f\cap g|=1
.

Now we are ready to present our graph with large additive gap.

Theorem 5.5
.

For every
t\geq 2
there is some constant
c>0
such that for every
n\geq e^{4}+t
there exists a graph
G_{n}
such that

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{t}(G_{n})}-\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{t}(G_{n})>\frac{c\log\log n}{\left(\log n\right)^{8\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}}}}n^{2}=n^{2-o(1)}.

Proof.

First, observe that it is sufficient to prove the statement for
t=2
: given such an example on
n-t+2
vertices, we can add a clique on
t-2
vertices, joined with a complete bipartite graph to the rest of the
n-t+2
vertices. Hence, we assume now that
t=2
.
As
\log n\geq 4
, by Proposition
5.3
, there exists a graph
H_{n}
on
2\left\lceil\log(n)/2\right\rceil
vertices and a constant
c^{\prime}>0
, such that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{2}(H_{n})}\geq c^{\prime}\log\log n\cdot\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}(H_{n})
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph as in Theorem
5.4
, with
N=n
and
R=\left\lceil\log n\right\rceil
. Define the graph

G_{n}=\bigcup_{S\in E(\mathcal{H})}H_{S}
, where for each
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
,
H_{S}
is an isomorphic copy of
H_{n}
such that
V(H_{S})=S
.

The linearity of
\mathcal{H}
implies that for every
e\in E(G_{n})
we have
e\in E(H_{S})
for exactly one
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
. As
\mathcal{H}
is triangle-free, for all cliques
Q\subseteq G_{n}
,
Q\subseteq H_{S}
for exactly one
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
. It follows that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{2}(G_{n})}=|E(\mathcal{H})|{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{2}(H_{n})}
and
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}(G_{n})=|E(\mathcal{H})|\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{2}(H_{n})
. As
|E(\mathcal{H})|\geq n^{2}/R^{8\sqrt{\log_{R}n}}
, the claim follows.
∎

5.3
Packings

In general, decomposition problems can be relaxed in two directions: covering, as we have addressed up to now, and packing. For a graph
G
, and a (possibly infinite) family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, let
\mathcal{F}(G)
be the collection of copies of elements of
\mathcal{F}
in
G
. The
\mathcal{F}
-packing number, denoted
\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(G)
is the maximum number of pairwise edge-disjoint elements of
\mathcal{F}(G)
. The fractional packing number
\nu^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}(G)
is the maximum
\left\lVert w\right\rVert
where
w:\mathcal{F}(G)\to[0,1]
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in H\in\mathcal{F}(G)\end{subarray}}w(H)\leq 1

for every
e\in G
. Yuster
[
27
]
strengthened an earlier result of Haxell and Rödl
[
14
]
giving a bound on the additive error of
\nu^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}(G)-\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(G)
.

Theorem 5.6
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a fixed family of graphs and
G
is a graph with
n
vertices, then
\nu^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}(G)-\nu_{\mathcal{F}}(G)=o(n^{2})
.

Yuster also gave a lower bound for the additive error by a probabilistic construction in a similar strategy to our proof of Theorem
5.5
.

Proposition 5.7

(Yuster
[
27
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
t=t(\varepsilon)
and
N=N(\varepsilon)
such that for all
n>N
, there exists a graph
G
with
n
vertices for which
\nu_{K_{t}}^{*}(G)-\nu_{K_{t}}(G)>n^{2-\varepsilon}
.

Yuster remarked that there is an infinite family of graphs such that
\nu_{K_{3}}^{*}(G)-\nu_{K_{3}}(G)=\Theta(n^{1.5})
and asked if
\nu_{K_{3}}^{*}(G)-\nu_{K_{3}}(G)=O(n^{1.5})
for all graphs
G
. We strengthen Proposition
5.7
utilizing Theorem
5.4
, and show that the integrality gap for packing triangles is even larger.

Theorem 5.8
.

For all integers
t\geq 3
and
n\geq e^{t+1}
, there exists a graph
G_{n}
such that

\nu^{*}_{K_{t}}(G_{n})-\nu_{K_{t}}(G_{n})>\frac{cn^{2}}{\left(\log n\right)^{8\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}}}}

for some constant
c=c(t)>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph as in Theorem
5.4
, with
N=n
and
R
be the smallest integer greater than
\log n
such that
t+1
divides
R
. Let
H
be the disjoint union of
R/(t+1)
copies of
K_{t+1}
.
Define the graph
G_{n}=\bigcup_{S\in E(\mathcal{H})}H_{S}
where for all
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
,
H_{S}
is an isomorphic copy of
H
and
V(H_{S})=S
. Note that

\nu_{K_{t}}^{*}(H_{S})=\frac{t+1}{t-1}\cdot\frac{R}{t+1}=\frac{t+1}{t-1}\nu_{K_{t}}(H_{S}).

As
\mathcal{H}
is linear, for all
e\in E(G_{n})
, we have that
e\in E(H_{S})
for exactly one
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
. Furthermore, as
\mathcal{H}
is triangle-free, all cliques of size
t+1
of
G_{n}
are contained in exactly one
S\in E(\mathcal{H})
. As
\nu_{K_{t}}(G_{n})=|E(\mathcal{H})|\nu_{K_{t}}(H)
,
\nu^{*}_{K_{t}}(G_{n})=|E(\mathcal{H})|\nu^{*}_{K_{t}}(H)
, and
|E(\mathcal{H})|\geq n^{2}/R^{8\sqrt{\log_{R}n}}
, the claim follows.
∎

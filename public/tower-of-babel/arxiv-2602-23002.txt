V. Ayyar, R. C. Brower, G. T. Fleming, J. Ingoldby, X. Y. Jin, N. Matsumoto, A. S. Meyer, E. T. Neil, J. C. Osborn, S. Park, C. T. Peterson, D. Schaich, P. Vranas, O. Witzel, Lattice Strong Dynamics Collaboration

Abstract
The thermodynamics of the S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) gauge theory with a single flavor of fundamental quarks is analyzed on the lattice with dynamical fermion simulations, which is the low-energy sector of a realistic, strongly-interacting dark matter model—the Hyper Stealth Dark Matter. The gravitational wave spectrum from the first-order confinement transition in the early universe is further calculated, where the effect of the dark sea quarks, which decrease the interface tension in the effective potential of the Polyakov loop, is shown numerically to lower the gravitational wave amplitude.



Introduction—
The relevance of cosmology and astrophysics to particle physics is becoming increasingly profound, as they provide phenomenological grounds for the beyond-Standard-Model physics such as dark matter.
The purpose of this letter is to investigate the possibility that dark matter is a composite particle and its consequences in the early universe.
The Hyper Stealth Dark Matter (HSDM) model
Fleming et al. 2025
, pushing forward the idea of the Stealth Dark Matter (SDM)
Appelquist et al. 2015a
;
Appelquist et al. 2015b
, embodies this possibility with an economical dark sector of single-flavor fundamental dark quarks and
SU(N_{D})
(
N_{D}\geq 3
) dark gluons, accompanied by the equilibration sector of heavy fermions with the full-strength electroweak interaction.

In the following, we focus on the dark sector and set
N_{D}=4
, which is the smallest
N_{D}
for which the dark-matter-candidate baryon has bosonic statistics.
The generic phase structure of the one-flavor theory
Fukugita et al. 1988
;
Fukugita and Ukawa 1988
;
Alexandrou et al. 1999
;
Shoji and Takaishi 2004
;
Takaishi and Nakamura 2007
can be found in the Columbia plot

:
The theory exhibits a first-order phase transition at a finite temperature
T_{c}
for heavy quark mass starting from the quenched (
i.e.
, pure glue) limit, and as we lower the quark mass
m_{q}
, the first-order transition terminates at a critical mass
m_{q,c}
(see also Ref.
Ayyar et al. 2018
for a study with multiple fermion representations).
The HSDM allows the baryon mass to be as light as about 50 GeV in the heavy-dark-quark limit.
The existence of the first-order phase transition raises further interest as a source of gravitational waves
Caprini et al. 2020
;
Auclair et al. 2023
, whose detection will be supporting evidence for the strongly-interacting dark sector (see Ref.
Escrivà et al. 2025
for discussion on crossovers).

After the seminal work of Ref.
Schwaller 2015
that studied the gravitational waves from a wide class of composite dark sectors, Refs.
Huang et al. 2021
and
Pasechnik et al. 2024
reported quantitative predictions from the confinement transition of the pure glue theory with different numbers of colors and from the chiral transition with the linear sigma model, respectively, importing results from lattice calculations.
To some surprise, the results fell below the threshold of the near-future detectors.
On this ground, it is, of course, important to verify the result with independent calculations, and, at the same time, explore how the spectrum varies for different strongly coupled theories and understand the underlying physics, which help to develop accurate methodology
Morgante et al. 2023
;
Bruno et al. 2024
;
Huber et al. 2025
;
Houtz et al. 2025
.
State-of-the-art lattice calculations will play an indispensable role by providing a first-principles framework.

Concerning the theory space of composite dark matter in general, this amounts to adding a dark-quark-mass parameter to the

Gravitational wave parameters
—Among several possible sources of gravitational waves

Bubbles of the true vacuum nucleate during a supercooling process, which expand and collide to take over the universe eventually.
The temperature

Polyakov loop effective potentials
—As is well known, the insertion of the Polyakov loop
L({\textbf{x}})
can be interpreted as setting a probe quark at the spatial point
x

Polyakov 1980
.
Upon proper renormalization, the expectation value measures the relative free energy of the static quark:

\displaystyle\langle L({\textbf{x}})\rangle\equiv e^{-(\Omega/T)(f_{q}-f)}\quad\big(Z\equiv e^{-(\Omega/T)f}\big),

(9)

where
\Omega\equiv\int d^{3}{\textbf{x}}
is the three-dimensional volume and
Z
the partition function.

Though
\langle L({\textbf{x}})\rangle
ceases to be an exact order parameter in the presence of sea quarks, when the confined and deconfined phases are separated by a first-order phase transition, we can expect a finite jump in
\langle L({\textbf{x}})\rangle
, denoting the discontinuous change in the required free energy to place a probe quark.
This makes the Polyakov loop a suitable observable for our study of thermodynamics.
The dimensionally-reduced effective action for the Polyakov loop may be written as

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\int d^{3}{\textbf{x}}\,\big(T^{2}|\partial_{i}L|^{2}+V(L)\big),

(10)

where the temperature factor is supplied in the kinetic term for dimensionality
Pisarski 2000
.
The potential
V
is expected to have a double-well shape in the first-order regime.

To estimate
V
, we introduce the constrained effective potential
\bar{V}
for the averaged Polyakov loop
Fukuda and Kyriakopoulos 1975
;
O’Raifeartaigh et al. 1986
;
Wetterich 1993
;
Bhattacharya et al. 1992
;
Korthals Altes 1994
, which is related to the normalized histogram
h(\bar{L})
as

\displaystyle h(\bar{L})\equiv e^{-(\Omega/T)\,\bar{V}(\bar{L})}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big\langle\delta^{2}\Big(\bar{L}-\frac{1}{\Omega}\int d^{3}{\textbf{x}}\,L({\textbf{x}})\Big)\Big\rangle,

(11)

where
\delta^{2}(\bar{L})\equiv\delta({\rm Re}\bar{L})\delta({\rm Im}\bar{L})
.
We immediately see that

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\bar{L}\,e^{-(\Omega/T)\,\bar{V}(\bar{L})}\bar{L}=e^{-(\Omega/T)(f_{q}-f)},

(12)

where
d^{2}\bar{L}\equiv d{\rm Re}\bar{L}\,d{\rm Im}\bar{L}
.
In the thermodynamic limit, the saddle point analysis becomes exact, and the location of the minimum
\bar{L}_{0}
describes the expectation value of the averaged Polyakov loop
{\bar{L}}
.

Strictly speaking,
V
and
\bar{V}
must be distinguished as
\bar{V}
becomes the convex hull of
V
in the thermodynamic limit
Heller and Seiberg 1983
;
O’Raifeartaigh et al. 1986
.
The potential barrier exists due to the interface tension
Munster 1989
;
Munster 1990
;
Bhattacharya et al. 1992
;
Iwasaki et al. 1994
;
Beinlich et al. 1997
;
Giovannangeli and Korthals Altes 2005a
;
Giovannangeli and Korthals Altes 2005b
;
Pisarski 2006
in the mixed phase configuration (
i.e.
, between the local minima), which will be dominated in the infinite volume by the bulk free energy.
However, as was reported in Ref.
Heller and Seiberg 1983
, which is also the case in our study, the disappearance of the potential barrier turns out to be slower than the convergence of the tail part of the potential, and we find that
\bar{V}
at a reasonably large but finite volume is a good estimate of
V
.
Though further volume scaling study of
\bar{V}

Binder 1981
;
Brezin and Zinn-Justin 1985
;
Iwasaki et al. 1994
would be beneficial, this is beyond the scope of this paper.

Renormalization
—The continuum dimensionful quantities estimated from the lattice are dimensionally transmuted and measured at a reference mass scale
\mu

\mu\equiv 1/\sqrt{t_{0}}
(see End Matter for more detail).
The value of
1/\sqrt{t_{0}}
in GeV defines the overall scale of the dark sector.
We define the line of constant physics with the mass
M_{B}\sqrt{t_{0}}
of the lightest baryon.

M_{B}\sqrt{t_{0}}
and
1/\sqrt{t_{0}}
in GeV are thus the inputs to the theory.

To renormalize the Polyakov loop

;
Kaczmarek et al. 2002

We measure the renormalized Polyakov loop
L({\textbf{x}})

1/\sqrt{t_{0}}

Discussion
—The amplitude saturates as we reach the pure glue limit,
M_{B}\sqrt{t_{0}}\to\infty
.
Our result for the largest baryon mass
M_{B}\sqrt{t_{0}}=4.0
may be considered as near the pure glue limit, and is in agreement with the former works on the pure glue theory
Huang et al. 2021
;
Morgante et al. 2023
;
Huber et al. 2025

in order of magnitude.
The amplitude may be amplified by varying the number of colors
N_{D}

Schwaller 2015
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Huber et al. 2025
or the Lie group
Bruno et al. 2024
.

Note that the observed quark-mass dependence is mainly from the change in the decay rate:
In our application, we have
\Delta V\ll\partial\Delta V/\partial\log T
in Eq. (
4
), and the strength parameter is consistently
\alpha\sim 1/3
, as in Refs.
Huang et al. 2021
;
Morgante et al. 2023
;
Huber et al. 2025
(see Fig.
6
).
It is further noteworthy that such dependence of
\alpha
relies on the calculation scheme with the effective scalar variable.
To understand the sea-quark effects better, calculating gluonic and fermionic contributions to the latent heat
Boyd et al. 1996
;
Lucini et al. 2005
;
Borsanyi et al. 2010
;
Shirogane et al. 2016
separately may be useful.
In combination with the lattice methods for the decay rate
Shen et al. 2023
;
Jin and Swaim 2025
, one can obtain
\alpha
and
\tilde{\beta}
without introducing the effective potential
V
.

Another simplification in the calculation is the wall velocity
v_{w}=v_{J}
that assumes detonation.
Accurate determination of

requires evaluation of hydrodynamic quantities such as fluid velocity in the two phases

;
Huber et al. 2025
.
It will be interesting to develop a lattice framework in parallel to holographic models

;
Bigazzi et al. 2021
;
Janik et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023
.

Concerning the HSDM as the theory of dark matter, glueballs and mesons, together with baryons, provide essential inputs to its phenomenology

.
Detailed spectroscopy and scattering studies will follow in future work (see also Refs.
Farchioni et al. 2007
;
Jaeger et al. 2023
;
Della Morte et al. 2023
;
Bergner et al. 2024

Our spectroscopy result for the connected diagrams (see End Matter) suggests that, together with the glueball masses of the quenched theory

The simple setup of the one-flavor theory also serves as a theoretically intriguing system for scrutinizing the confining dynamics of quantum chromodynamics.

A.1
(i) Efficiency factor

Following Refs.
Huang et al. 2021
;
Morgante et al. 2023
;
Pasechnik et al. 2024
;
Huber et al. 2025
, we use the expression:

\displaystyle\kappa\equiv\sqrt{\tau_{\rm sw}}\kappa_{v}.

(13)

The factor
\sqrt{\tau_{\rm sw}}
takes into account the lifetime of the sound-wave source
Ellis et al. 2020
;
Guo et al. 2021
:

\displaystyle\tau_{\rm sw}

\displaystyle=1-\left[1+2\frac{(8\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}v_{w}}{\tilde{\beta}U_{f}}\right]^{-1/2},\quad U_{f}^{2}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\kappa_{v},

(14)

which lowers the amplitude in numerics.

\kappa_{v}
is the factor that is derived from hydrodynamical analysis, and is given for the Jouguet detonation as
Espinosa et al. 2010

\displaystyle\kappa_{v}(v_{w}=v_{J})=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{0.135+\sqrt{0.98+\alpha}}.

(15)

A.3
(iii) Bounce action

To obtain the bounce solution
Coleman 1977
, the equation of motion
Huang et al. 2021

\displaystyle-\frac{d^{2}L}{dr^{\prime 2}}-\frac{2}{r^{\prime}}\frac{dL}{dr^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{dV^{\prime}}{dr^{\prime}}=0

(17)

is solved with the boundary conditions
dL(r^{\prime}=0)/dr^{\prime}=0
and
L(r^{\prime}=\infty)=L_{+}
by the shooting method.
Obtained solutions are shown in Fig.

\beta

S^{\prime}_{3}

\beta

Huang et al. 2021

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\prime}=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}r^{\prime 2}\Big[\Big(\frac{dL}{dr^{\prime}}\Big)^{2}+V^{\prime}(L)-V^{\prime}(L_{+})\Big],

(18)

we obtain the values for the classical action

A.5
(v) Mass landscape

In one-flavor quantum chromodynamics, the confinement scale and the quark mass are the primary scales in the theory.
The mass landscape may be observed through the lightest masses in the connected diagrams for the baryonic and mesonic correlators compared against the quenched glueball masses (Fig.
13
).
It should be noted, however, that the mesonic masses may not be regarded as physical due to omitted contributions from the disconnected diagrams.
We nevertheless observe that, roughly around the critical mass
0.1<m_{q,c}<0.2
, the quenched glueball and mesonic masses turn over.
Detailed analysis will be given with full spectroscopy results in future studies.

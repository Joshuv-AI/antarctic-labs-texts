Scattering Networks on Noncommutative Finite Groups
Maria Teresa Arias, Davide Barbieri, Eugenio Hernández

Abstract
Scattering Networks were initially designed to elucidate the behavior of early layers in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) over Euclidean spaces and are grounded in wavelets. In this work, we introduce a scattering transform on an arbitrary finite group (not necessarily abelian) within the context of group-equivariant convolutional neural networks (G-CNNs). We present wavelets on finite groups and analyze their similarity to classical wavelets. We demonstrate that, under certain conditions in the wavelet coefficients, the scattering transform is non-expansive, stable under deformations, preserves energy, equivariant with respect to left and right group translations, and, as depth increases, the scattering coefficients are less sensitive to group translations of the signal, all desirable properties of convolutional neural networks. Furthermore, we provide examples illustrating the application of the scattering transform to classify data with domains involving abelian and nonabelian groups. Keywords: scattering, machine learning, CNNs, equivariance, groups.



1
Introduction

Many interesting problems involve analyzing and manipulating structured data, often real-valued functions defined on domain sets with some structure. This is the case of image recognition, where convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been extremely successful, giving rise to many generalizations and to a great interest in understanding how they work.

The concept of
scattering transform
was originally introduced in
[
21
]
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It can be viewed as a CNN which builds a representation of the data having provable properties. It has a multi-layer structure and uses convolution with wavelets
\psi_{\lambda}
to propagate signals and convolution with a filter
\phi
to generate the features (See Figure
1
).

Figure 1
:
The structure of the scattering transform of a signal
f
.

In this work, we introduce a scattering transform for signals in
L^{2}(G)
, where
G
is a finite group, not necessarily abelian. This generalization is motivated by the need to analyze data exhibiting non-commutative symmetries, which arise naturally in various contexts. For instance, in cryptography, signals defined in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, representing permutations of data, inherently possess a noncommutative structure. Furthermore, the underlying symmetries of certain data transformations, such as those associated with wavelet or time-frequency analysis, can often be more accurately modeled using affine groups over finite fields. Indeed, extending the scattering transform to this setting is a natural progression, given that convolutional structures are not merely sufficient, but also necessary, for equivariance to the action of a compact group
[
17
]
. To achieve this, we introduce a family of wavelets on
L^{2}(G)
, adapted to the group structure, and use them to construct the group scattering transform. Our framework is based on representation theory and harmonic analysis, providing a rigorous foundation for the analysis of these signals. We prove that the resulting
G
-scattering transform exhibits desirable equivariance properties, respecting the underlying group symmetries, and demonstrate its stability, ensuring robustness to small perturbations in the input signal.

1.1
Results

Section
2
introduces and motivates our definition of
G
-wavelets, drawing connections to existing wavelet constructions in the literature. A
G
-wavelet
\psi
is defined as a linear combination of the characters
\{\chi^{\pi}:\pi\in\widehat{G}\}
of the group
G
, taking into account the degree,
d_{\pi}
, of the unitary representations
\pi
that give rise to the character
\chi^{\pi}(x)=Tr(\pi(x)),\ x\in G
. That is (see Definition
2.2
)

\psi(x)=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}d_{\pi}\gamma_{\psi}(\pi))\chi^{\pi}(x)\,\quad x\in G\,.

The coefficients
\{\gamma_{\psi}(\pi):\pi\in\widehat{G}\}
will play an important role in proving the result described below.

We define the
G
-wavelet transform of
f\in L^{2}(G)
as a group convolution with a family of
G
-wavelets
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{J}\subset L^{2}(G)
, by

Wf(j,x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\psi_{j}(h)f(h^{-1}x)=\psi_{j}\ast f(x)\,.

Under the Calderón type condition

\mathscr{C}(\pi)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\gamma_{\psi_{j}}(\pi)|^{2}=1\,,\quad\forall\ \pi\in\widehat{G}\,,

(1)

the family of group translations of the
G
-wavelets form a Parseval frame, i.e.

\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|Wf(j,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\,,\ \forall\ f\in L^{2}(G)\,.

(2)

In fact, conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent (see Theorem
2.6
for a more general result).

The
G
-wavelet transform has the desirable property of being equivariant under both left and right group translations. That is, if
L_{g}f(x)=f(g^{-1}x)
is the left translation operator and
R_{g}f(x)=f(xg)
is the right translation operator in the group
G
, then, for all
g\in G
and all
f\in L^{2}(G)
, it holds that
W(L_{g}f)=L_{g}W(f)
and
W(R_{g}f)=R_{g}W(f)
(see Subsection
4.3
).

Section
3
presents our notion of group scattering transform on finite groups: for a Parseval
G
-wavelet frame
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{J}\subset L^{2}(G)
, that is, one that satisfies (
2
), and denoting by
\phi=\psi_{0}
, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
we define

S[\emptyset]f=\phi\ast f\,,\ S[j_{1},\dots,j_{m}]f=\phi\ast|\psi_{j_{m}}\ast\dots|\psi_{j_{1}}\ast f|\dots|.

Here
m
denotes the
depth
of the convolutional structure. We invite the reader to see Figure
3
that contains a graphical description of this transform.

In Section
4
, we prove the key structural properties of this transform. Namely, with Lemma
4.1
we prove non-expansivity, that is

\|Sf\|^{2}:=\|\phi\ast f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j_{1},\dots j_{m}\in\{1,\dots,J\}}\|S[j_{1},\dots,j_{m}]f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\leq\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\,,\ \forall\ f\in L^{2}(G).

This is a nontrivial property that ensures that, despite the exponential growth of the number of coefficients of the scattering transform, the total energy is controlled by the norm of the original signal. In particular, this implies that for all practical purposes, one can restrict to a finite depth of the scattering coefficients since they will become negligibly small as depth increases. We also prove, with Proposition
4.2
, stability with respect to variations on the signals, namely we prove that

\|Sf-Sf^{\prime}\|^{2}\leq\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\,,\ \forall\ f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G).

Note that, since
S
is not linear, this is not a direct consequence of the non-expansivity.

A finer property that we prove, with Theorem
4.6
, is energy preservation, that is

\|Sf\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\,,\ \forall\ f\in L^{2}(G)\,.

This holds for the scattering transform of a Parseval frame wavelet if, in addition, we assume that the function
\phi=\psi_{0}
satisfies the admissibility condition
\beta:=\min_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}|\gamma_{\phi}(\pi)|^{2}>0
(see Definition
4.3
). This same admissibility condition also ensures injectivity of the scattering transform. We will further discuss in Section
5
a relaxed admissibility condition, which relies on a structure property of the unitary irreducible representations of finite groups.

Due to the underlying equivariance property of the wavelet construction, we show with Proposition
4.7
that the group scattering transform is equivariant with respect to left and right group translation. Finally, under the previously mentioned admissibility condition, we prove in Theorem
4.8
that as depth increases, an approximate invariance with respect to group translations emerges. More precisely, we show that there exists a positive constant
\alpha<1
and a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{j_{1},\dots j_{m}\in\{1,\dots,J\}}\|S[j_{1},\dots,j_{m}]L_{g}f-S[j_{1},\dots,j_{m}]f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\leq C\alpha^{m}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}

for all
f\in L^{2}(G)
. This indicates that the scattering coefficients at increasing depths are less and less sensitive to group translations of the signal, which is a desirable property in convolutional neural networks.

Section
6
provides three illustrative examples of applications. In the first one we refine the results of
[
36
]
in the context of the MNIST dataset (Section
6.1
). After choosing an appropriate wavelet frame, we construct the scattering transform for abelian translations and show accuracy of more than 97% in the classification. In the second example (Section
6.2
), we demonstrate the utility of the scattering transform for the class of groups
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
in audio classification of signals belonging to two different categories (audio of barks and meows from dogs and cats). Here we first represent audio signals on affine groups by means of time-scale wavelet representation. This classical representation provides very poor results for classification with linear separation such as SVM. In this example, we show how to construct a group wavelet transform whose associate group scattering transform produces classification of the sound dataset with more than 87% accuracy. In the third and last example (Section
6.3
) we consider a novel classification problem on groups: that of functions on symmetric groups. We first consider distance functions that appear in problems from cryptography, then consider random functions. In each case, we show how to use the representation theory of the symmetric group to construct a group scattering transform that is adapted to the problem.

1.2
Related work

The original scattering transform is defined for signals in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
The wavelets are constructed from a mother wavelet
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, dilating and rotating it with
\lambda=2^{j}r
, where
j<J
and
r
is an element of a finite rotation group
G
, to obtain

\psi_{\lambda}(x)=2^{dj}\psi(2^{j}r^{-1}x).

(3)

The convolution with the scaling function

\phi_{J}(x)=2^{-dJ}\phi(2^{-J}x)

(4)

localizes a function over spatial domains of size proportional to
2^{J}
. Such a scattering transform is a non-expansive operator and approximately invariant to translation and rotation: under strong admissibility assumptions on the wavelet and the scaling function, as the coarsest scale tends to infinity, the scattering transform is fully invariant to translations and rotations. Moreover, it is Lipschitz continuous with respect to smooth deformations. However, in dimensions greater than
1
, no wavelets has been reported satisfying such strong admissibility conditions and for the case of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it has been proved
[
34
]
that similar results can be achieved with much less restrictive conditions on the wavelets than the ones used in
[
21
]
. The problem of the stability has been further analysed in
[
23
]
. There is a complementary theory to scattering of functions on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
35
]
which encompasses at each layer convolution with general semi-discrete frames in the role of wavelets, general Lipschitz-continuous non-linearities in the role of the modulus, and that adds a general Lipschitz-continuous pooling operator, emulating sub-sampling and averaging.

In applied settings, classification algorithms are commonly employed in conjunction with principal component analysis (PCA) in the scattering transform. State-of-the art classification results were obtained for instance, for handwritten digit recognition and texture classification
[
2
]
, as well as for musical genre and phone classification
[
1
]
. The method demonstrates particular promise in low-data regimes, where the number of training samples is limited, for example, it has been effectively utilized for the classification of underwater objects based on sonar signal analysis
[
27
]
. However it has certain limitations: in
[
8
]
it was shown that traditional scattering networks are not able to represent complex, edge-like patterns, which constrains their representational capacity for certain classes of visual patterns. The scattering transform has been also used to study certain types of random processes
[
3
]
.

Beyond translations and rotations, additional classes of symmetries can, in principle, be incorporated into the scattering framework. Mallat’s original formulation of the scattering transform is theoretically flexible enough to ensure stability under the action of a compact Lie group
G
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, in practice, these generalized constructions are computationally prohibitive. They rely on Littlewood–Paley decompositions on compact manifolds (see
[
31
]
) and unitary wavelet transforms on compact Lie groups
[
13
]
, which are challenging to implement efficiently or at scale. As a result, practical implementations tend to focus on more tractable forms of invariance. For instance,
[
30
]
developed a scattering transform invariant to rotations, scalings, and deformations for texture discrimination, while
[
26
]
proposed a computationally feasible architecture with built-in rotation invariance. More recently,
[
12
]
introduced a learnable extension of the scattering transform based on Morlet wavelets, allowing the scales, orientations, and aspect ratios of the filters to be optimized for specific tasks.

There are a large and increasing number of papers dealing with generalizations to other domains in addition to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There are non-Euclidean extensions of the scattering transform. A geometric scattering is defined on compact, smooth, connected Riemann manifolds without boundary
[
24
]
and on Measure Spaces
[
6
]
, this last having properties of stability and invariance, but not energy preserving. This architecture uses wavelets similar to diffusion wavelets
[
7
]
. Scattering has also been generalized to graphs in
[
36
]
, where they use wavelets built in
[
16
]
using the eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator of the graph. In Section
2.3
we describe the relationship between such wavelets on graphs and the
G
-wavelet introduced in the present paper.

2
G-wavelets

In this section we are going to design wavelets on finite groups. We will start by recalling some notations, and refer to
[
33
, Chapt. 15]
for the basic results of Fourier analysis on finite groups needed in this section.

Let
G
be a finite group with identity
\mathbf{1}
. For
x\in G
, its conjugacy class is
[x]=\{yxy^{-1}|y\in G\}
.

A representation of
G
is an homomorphism
\pi:G\to GL(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
of dimension
d_{\pi}
. The number
d_{\pi}
is called the degree of the representation
\pi
. Note that from
\pi(xy)=\pi(x)\pi(y)
for all
x,y\in G
, one can deduce
\pi(\mathbf{1})=I_{d_{\pi}}
, where
I_{d_{\pi}}
is the
d_{\pi}
-dimensional identity matrix, and
\pi(x^{-1})=\pi(x)^{-1}
for all
x\in G
.
The character
\chi^{\pi}
of a representation
\pi
of a finite group
G
is defined by
\chi^{\pi}(x)=Tr(\pi(x))
for all
x\in G
. The characters are complex-valued functions defined on
G
which are constant on conjugacy classes. Indeed, let
x\in G
, let
z\in[x]
, and let
y\in G
be such that
z=yxy^{-1}
. Then

\chi^{\pi}(z)=Tr(\pi(yxy^{-1}))=Tr(\pi(y)\pi(x)\pi(y^{-1}))=Tr(\pi(x))=\chi^{\pi}(x).

(5)

Complex valued functions with this property are called class functions (see e.g.
[
33
, Definition on p. 258]
). Precisely,
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is a class function if for all
x,y\in G
,

f(yxy^{-1})=f(x).

(6)

A representation
\pi
of
G
is unitary if
\pi(x)
is a unitary matrix for all
x\in G
.

A representation
\pi
of
G
is irreducible if for all
x\in G
, the linear operator
\pi(x):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
does not have invariant subspaces other than the trivial ones. Two representations
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
of
G
are said to be equivalent if there exits an invertible square matrix
T
such that
\pi_{1}(x)=T^{-1}\pi_{2}(x)T
for all
x\in G
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a finite group
G
, the dual
\widehat{G}
is the set of irreducible unitary representations of
G
up to unitary equivalence.

It is known (see e.g.
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 3]
) that the number of elements of
\widehat{G}
coincides with the number of conjugacy classes of
G
. Let us denote by
k
this number, let us choose a system of inequivalent unitary irreducible representation so that we can identify
\widehat{G}=\{\pi^{1},\ldots,\pi^{k}\}
, and for each
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, write
\chi^{i}
for the character of
\pi^{i}.
Denote by
\mathscr{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{C}_{k}
the conjugacy classes of
G
, where
\mathscr{C}_{1}=[\mathbf{1}]=\{\mathbf{1}\}
. By (
5
), each
\chi^{i}
is constant in each class
\mathscr{C}_{r}
,
1\leq r\leq k
, and we denote by
\chi^{i}_{r}
the value of
\chi^{i}
in the conjugacy class
\mathscr{C}_{r}
. The square matrix of size
k\times k
formed with the elements
\chi^{i}_{r}
is called the character table of
G
. Observe that if
d_{i}
is the degree of
\pi^{i}\in\widehat{G}
, then
d_{i}=\chi^{i}_{1}
. There exist algorithms to calculate the character table of finite groups (see
[
15
]
(Chapter 7),
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
28
]
).

A relevant unitary representation of
G
is the left regular representation
L
. It is defined on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(G)=\{f:G\to\mathbb{C}\}
, which is considered to be endowed with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{g(x)},\quad f,g\in L^{2}(G),

(7)

and the induced norm
\|f\|=(\langle f,f\rangle)^{1/2}
, by

L(h)f(x)=f(h^{-1}x)\,,\quad\textnormal{for }f\in L^{2}(G)\textnormal{ and }x,h\in G.

Recall that
L
is equivalent to the direct sum of irreducibles (see e.g.
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Lemma 2]
)

L(x)\approx\bigoplus_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\pi^{r}(x)\quad\forall\,x\in G

(8)

where
d_{r}\pi^{r}
indicates the direct product of
d_{r}
copies of the representation
\pi^{r}
.

The convolution of
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
is defined as

f*g(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}f(y)L(y)g(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{z\in G}f(xz^{-1})g(z),\quad f,g\in L^{2}(G).

(9)

The convolution is in general noncommutative. But if
f\in L^{2}(G)
is a class function (see (
6
)) then, for all
g\in L^{2}(G)
and all
x\in G

f*g(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{z\in G}f(xz^{-1})g(z)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{z\in G}g(z)f(x(z^{-1}x)x^{-1})=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{z\in G}g(z)f(z^{-1}x)=g*f(x).

(10)

The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{2}(G)
is defined as the matrix valued function (see Chapters 3 and 5 of
[
11
]
and Chapter 15 of
[
33
]
)

\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\pi^{r}(x^{-1}),\quad\pi^{r}\in\widehat{G}.

It factorizes convolutions in the following way: for
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
, it is easy to see that

\widehat{f\ast g}(\pi^{r})=\widehat{g}(\pi^{r})\widehat{f}(\pi^{r}).

(11)

Moreover, for finite groups one can write Plancherel’s theorem as (see
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 2]
, but observe that here we are considering the normalized scalar product)

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}\left|\left\langle f,\pi^{r}_{ij}\right\rangle\right|^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}\left|\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})_{ij}\right|^{2}

(12)

for any
f\in L^{2}(G)
, where
\pi^{r}_{ij}:G\to\mathbb{C}
is the
ij
-th element of the representation
\pi^{r}
.

2.1
G-Wavelet transform

The definition of the
G
-wavelets is inspired by the idea of
[
16
]
(see Section
2.3
).

Definition 2.2
.

Given a measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
, we call
kernel
a family of functions
\gamma=\{\gamma_{\omega}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
. The
G
-wavelet associated to the kernel
\gamma_{\omega}
is

\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(x):=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\gamma_{\omega}(r)\chi^{r}(x),\quad x\in G.

(13)

Assume that for
\omega\in\Omega
, we have
\displaystyle B_{\omega}:=\max_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|<\infty
. Then
\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\in L^{\infty}(G)
. Indeed, since the trace of a unitary matrix is less than or equal to the dimension of the matrix,

\|\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in G}|\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(x)|\leq\sup_{x\in G}\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)||\chi^{r}(x)|\leq B_{\omega}\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}^{2}=B_{\omega}|G|<\infty\,,

(14)

where the have used that
\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}^{2}=|G|
(see
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Lemma 2]
).

The
G
-wavelets given in (
13
) are class functions. This can be seen directly, using the property of the trace:

\displaystyle\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(h^{-1}xh)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\gamma_{\omega}(r)\chi^{r}(h^{-1}xh)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\gamma_{\omega}(r)Tr(\pi^{r}(h^{-1})\pi^{r}(x)\pi^{r}(h))

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\gamma_{\omega}(r)Tr(\pi^{r}(x))=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\gamma_{\omega}(r)\chi^{r}(x).

We also have that the Fourier transform of a
G
-wavelet is a multiple, given by the defining kernel, of the identity operator on
L^{2}(G)
:

\widehat{\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}}(\pi^{r})=\gamma_{\omega}(r)I_{d_{r}}.

(15)

This can be obtained by direct computation of the matrix elements starting from (
13
):

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}\sum_{s=1}^{k}d_{s}\gamma_{\omega}(s)\chi^{s}(x)\pi^{r}(x^{-1})_{i,j}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}d_{s}\gamma_{\omega}(s)\sum_{x\in G}Tr(\pi^{s}(x))\pi^{r}(x^{-1})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{s=1}^{k}d_{s}\gamma_{\omega}(s)\sum_{l=1}^{d_{s}}\sum_{x\in G}\pi^{s}(x)_{l,l}\pi^{r}(x^{-1})_{i,j}=\gamma_{\omega}(r)\delta_{i,j}

where the last identity is Schur’s orthogonality theorem (see e.g.
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 1]
).

We define the
G
-wavelet transform in terms of the left translates of a
G
-wavelet, as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a kernel
\gamma
, we define the
G
-wavelet transform of
f\in L^{2}(G)
by

W_{\gamma}f(\omega,h):=f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(h)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(x^{-1}h),\quad\omega\in\Omega,h\in G.

Since the wavelets are class functions, by (
10
) we can write

W_{\gamma}f(\omega,h)=f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(h)=\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\ast f(h).

(16)

Assume that for
\omega\in\Omega
, we have
\displaystyle B_{\omega}:=\max_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|<\infty
. By (
14
) and the fact that
G
is finite,

\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\in L^{\infty}(G)\subset\ L^{1}(G).
Hence, by Young’s inequality and (
16
) we conclude that
W_{\gamma}f(\omega,\cdot)\in L^{2}(G).

Moreover, using (
11
), we have that

\widehat{W_{\gamma}f}(\omega,\cdot)(\pi^{r})=\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})\widehat{\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}}(\pi^{r}).

Using (
15
), we immediately obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given a kernel
\gamma
as in Definition
2.2
, the
G
-wavelet transform of
f\in L^{2}(G)
satisfies

\widehat{W_{\gamma}f}(\omega,\cdot)(\pi^{r})=\gamma_{\omega}(r)\,\widehat{f}(\pi^{r}).

2.2
Properties

Let us first show that, if a Calderón type condition holds, we have a reconstruction formula for the introduced
G
-wavelet transform, analogous to that for the continuous wavelet transform.

In the sequel, for
f\in L^{2}(G)
we write
f^{\dagger}(x)=\overline{f(x^{-1})}
for the natural involution in
L^{2}(G)
. As in (
15
), it can be proved that

\widehat{\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}}(\pi^{r})=\overline{\gamma_{\omega}(r)}I_{d_{r}}.

(17)

Theorem 2.5

(Inverse formula)
.

If the kernel
\gamma=\{\gamma_{\omega}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies the condition

\int_{\Omega}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|^{2}d\mu(\omega)=1\quad\forall\ r\in\{1,\ldots,k\},

(18)

then for all
f\in L^{2}(G)

f(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\int_{\Omega}W_{\gamma}f(\omega,h)\,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(x)\,d\mu(\omega),

(19)

for every
x\in G
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.3
we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\int_{\Omega}W_{\gamma_{\omega}}f(h)\,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(x)\,d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\int_{\Omega}f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}(h)\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(h^{-1}x)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(x)d\mu(\omega).

By (
11
) and (
17
) we have that
\widehat{\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}}(\pi^{r})=|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|^{2}I_{d_{r}}
, so that, by the same argument leading from (
13
) to (
15
), the second convolution reads

\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|^{2}\chi^{r}(x).

Thus, using Calderón’s condition (
18
), and recalling (
8
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}\ast\psi_{\gamma_{\omega}}^{\dagger}(x)d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\int_{\Omega}|\gamma_{\omega}(r)|^{2}d\mu(\omega)f*\chi^{r}(x)=f\ast\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\chi^{r}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=f\ast Tr\left(\sum_{r=1}^{k}d_{r}\pi^{r}\right)(x)=f\ast Tr(L)(x)=f(x)

where the last identity is due to the fact that the trace of the left regular representation is
|G|
when computed in the identity, and zero otherwise.
∎

For any practical computation,
\Omega
must be a finite number of scales
\Omega=\{0,\dots,J\}
. The natural question is how appropriated the
G
-wavelet set
\{\psi_{\gamma_{j}}|0\leq j\leq J\}
will be for representing functions on the group. This question can be addressed by characterizing when the system of translates is a frame of
L^{2}(G)
, which is the content of the next theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
and denote by

\mathscr{C}(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}\,,\ r\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

The following are equivalent

i)

The set
\{L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\,|\,0\leq j\leq J,h\in G\}
is a frame of
L^{2}(G)
with constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
, i.e.

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\quad\forall\ f\in L^{2}(G).

(20)

ii)

There exist two constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

A\leq\mathscr{C}(r)\leq B\quad\forall\ r\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

(21)

Proof.

For all
h\in G
and all
j\in\{0,1,\dots,J\}
we have

\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\rangle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{\psi_{\gamma_{j}}(h^{-1}x)}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}(x^{-1}h)=f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}(h)\,.

By (
11
) and (
17
), for any
\pi^{r}\in\widehat{G}
,

\widehat{f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}}(\pi^{r})=\overline{\gamma_{j}(r)}\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})\,.

By Plancherel’s identity (
12
), for all
j\in\{0,\dots,J\}
we have

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}|\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\rangle|^{2}=\|f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}|\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})_{nm}|^{2}.

Then

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}\right)d_{r}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{r}}|\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})_{nm}|^{2}.

(22)

We can now complete the proof by recalling that Plancherel’s identity (
12
) reads

\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|(\widehat{f}(\pi^{r}))_{nm}|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}.

Indeed, if (
21
) holds, then one immediately obtains the frame condition (
20
). On the other hand, suppose that (
20
) holds and suppose, by contradiction, that (
21
) is not true because
\mathscr{C}(r_{0})<A
for one
r_{0}\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then, if we choose
f_{0}\in L^{2}(G)
to be such that
\widehat{f_{0}}(\pi^{r})=\delta_{r,r_{0}}M
for a nonzero
d_{r_{0}}\times d_{r_{0}}
matrix
M
, we get that (
22
) reads

\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}|\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\rangle|^{2}=d_{r_{0}}\mathscr{C}(r_{0})\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{r}}|\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})_{nm}|^{2}<Ad_{r_{0}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{r}}|\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})_{nm}|^{2}=A\|f\|^{2}

which contradicts (
20
). The other inequality can be treated similarly.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The same result as in Theorem
2.6
holds if we consider the set
\{L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}\,|\,0\leq j\leq J,h\in G\}.
In fact,

\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}\rangle=f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{j}}(h)\,.

and by (
11
) and (
15
), for any
\pi^{r}\in\widehat{G}
,

\widehat{f\ast\psi_{\gamma_{j}}}(\pi^{r})=\gamma_{j}(r)\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})\,

so that the above proof goes unchanged to obtain

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\sum_{j=0}^{J}|\langle f,L(h)\psi_{\gamma_{j}}^{\dagger}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\ast f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\quad\forall\ f\in L^{2}(G).

2.3
Connection with Hammond et al. Wavelets

In
[
16
]
D.K. Hammond, P. Vandergheynst, and R. Givonval define and study wavelets on weighted connected finite graphs. Let
V
be the set of vertices of the graph, denoted by
V=\{1,2,\dots N\}
,
E
the set of edges of the graph and
w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
the weights.

The adjacency matrix of the weighted graph is the
N\times N
matrix
A=(a_{m,n})
where

a_{m,n}=\begin{cases}w(e),\text{ if }e\in E\ \text{connect vertices}\ m\ \text{and}\ n\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

For each vertex
m
in
V
, denote by
d(m)
the sum of the weights of all edges incident to
m
. Let
D
be the matrix with diagonal entries
d(1),d(2),\dots,d(m)
, and 0 otherwise. The Laplacian of the weighted graph
\{V,E,w\}
is the
N\times N
matrix

\mathcal{L}=D-A.

The matrix
\mathcal{L}
is real and symmetric. Therefore, it has a complete set of orthonormal eigenvectors, denoted by
\chi_{\ell},\ell=1,2,\dots.N
, with associated eigenvalues
\lambda_{l}
, that is
\mathcal{L}\chi_{\ell}=\lambda_{l}\chi_{\ell}
. Moreover,
\lambda_{1}=0
is an eigenvalue of multiplicity one and the remainder eigenvalues are all positive. (See
[
16
]
for details.)

For a kenel
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
the
spectral graph wavelets
with parameter
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
n=1,2,\dots,N
are given in
[
16
]
by

\psi_{t,n}(m)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g(t\lambda_{\ell})\chi_{\ell}(n)\chi_{\ell}(m)

(23)

The purpose of this section is to show that if
G
is a finite abelian group, the
G
-wavelets defined in (
13
) coincide with the spectral graph wavelets defined in (
23
) for appropriate kernels
\gamma_{\omega}
and
g
. Observe that our results in Sections (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are valid for finite groups not necessarily abelian.

The connection between our wavelets and the spectral graph wavelets of
[
16
]
is provided by the Cayley graph of a group.

Definition 2.8
.

Given a group
G
and a symmetric set of generators
S
of
G
(meaning
S
generates
G
and
S
is closed under taking inverses) , the Cayley graph
X=Cay(G,S)
of
G
is a graph whose vertices are the elements of
G
, two elements
x
and
y
in
G
,
are connected if there exists an element
s\in S
such that

x=sy
and all the weights of the edges are 1.

For
G
abelian group with
N
elements, all unitary irreducible representations are unidimensional and they are equal to the characters of the group. We write

\widehat{G}=\{\chi^{1},\chi^{2},\dots,\chi^{N}\,.\}

There is a connection between the characters of the abelian group and the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the Laplacian of the Cayley graph of the group, as the following theorem (due to Lovász
[
20
]
), shows.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
G
be an abelian group of order
N
with
characters
\chi^{1},\chi^{2},\ldots,\chi^{N}
. Let
S\subset G
be a symmetric set of generators of
G
. Then the eigenvalues of the Laplacian of the Cayley graph
X=Cay(G,S)
of
G
are given by

\lambda_{i}=|S|-\sum_{x\in S}\chi^{i}(x)

(24)

Moreover, an eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{i}
is
(\chi^{i}(x):x\in G)^{t}
(that is, the column vector whose
x
-entry is
\chi^{i}(x)
).

Given a kernel
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let
\gamma_{t}:\{1,2,\dots,N\}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be given by
\gamma_{t}(\ell)=g(t\lambda_{l})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.
Then, for
x\in G
, equation (
13
) reads:

\psi_{\gamma_{t}}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g(t\lambda_{\ell})\chi^{l}(x),

(25)

which coincides with (
23
) when
n
is the identity of the group. For any other

, the wavelets

\psi_{\gamma_{t},h}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g(t\lambda_{\ell})\chi^{\ell}(h)\chi^{\ell}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}g(t\lambda_{\ell})\chi^{\ell}(hx)=\psi_{\gamma_{t}}(hx)

are all translations of the wavelet given by (
25
).

3
Construction of Scattering Networks on Finite Groups

Our construction will rely on the
G
-wavelets defined in the previous section. Consider as measure space the finite set
\Omega=\{0,\dots,J\}
and, for a finite group
G
whose dual has size
k
, consider a
kernel

\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
. The
k(J+1)
values of this kernel are the only parameters of the network to be defined. Let us also denote by
\{\psi_{j}=\psi_{\gamma_{j}}\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
the associated family of
G
-wavelets defined in (
13
). For more clarity in the presentation of the scattering network construction, we will denote by
\phi=\psi_{0}
, and set the kernel in such a way that

\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}=1-|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\,,\quad r=1,2,\dots,k,

(27)

hence constraining the
G
-wavelet to be a Parseval frame (see Theorem
2.6
).

For a fixed
j\in\{1,..,J\}
, we call
one-step
G
-propagator

U[j]:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
the operator defined by

U[j]f:=|\psi_{j}*f|,\quad f\in L^{2}(G).

(28)

For
m,J>0
, we denote by
\Lambda_{J}^{m}=\{1,\ldots,J\}^{m}
and call it the set of
J
-paths of length
m
. For
m>0
and
p=(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}
, the
m
-steps
G
-scattering propagator

U[p]:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
is the operator defined by

U[p]f:=U[j_{m}]\ldots U[j_{1}]f,\quad f\in L^{2}(G).

(29)

Observe that
U[p]
takes
L^{2}(G)
into
L^{2}(G)
due to the fact that
\psi_{j}\in L^{\infty}(G)\subset L^{1}(G),j=1,2,\dots,J
(see (
14
)).

The collection of all
J
-paths of finite length will be denoted by
P_{J}:=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{J}^{m}
, with the convention that
\Lambda_{J}^{0}=\emptyset
. With a slight abuse of notation, we will also indicate, for the empty path,
U[\emptyset]f=f
.

For a path
p=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}
and a
j_{m+1}\in\{1,\ldots,J\}=\Lambda_{J}^{1}
, following
[
21
]
we define the path
p+j_{m+1}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m},j_{m+1})\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}
. Then

U[p+j_{m+1}]:=U[j_{m+1}]U[p].

(30)

We will use the function
\phi
introduced above to define the
G
-average operator

Q:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
as

Qf=\phi*f\,,\quad f\in L^{2}(G).

(31)

In the nomenclature introduced in
[
21
]
we will then call
G
-windowed scattering operator
for a path
p\in P_{J}
the map
S[p]:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
defined by

S[p]f:=QU[p]f,\quad f\in L^{2}(G).

(32)

Observe that
S[p]
takes
L^{2}(G)
into
L^{2}(G)
due to the fact that
\phi\in L^{\infty}(G)\subset L^{1}(G)
(see (
14
)).

Definition 3.1
.

The
G
-scattering transform with
J
generators is defined as

S[P_{J}]f=\{S[p]f\}_{p\in P_{J}},\quad f\in L^{2}(G).

We will denote by
\|\cdot\|_{2,J}
its
\ell_{2}(P_{J},L^{2}(G))
norm, given by

\displaystyle\|S[P_{J}]f\|_{2,J}=\left(\sum_{p\in P_{J}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|QU[j_{m}]\ldots U[j_{1}]f\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The
G
-scattering transform acts as a group convolutional neural network. At the
m
-th layer, the
propagated signal
is
\{U[p]f:p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}\}
and the
extracted features
are
\{S[p]f:p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}\}
.

:

Remark 3.2
.

The scattering networks constructed in this section follows the original idea of S. Mallat in
[
21
]
(see, also, Figure 1 in the Introduction). For this scattering transform to have good properties, the wavelets used must satisfy the equations that appear in Proposition 2.7 of
[
21
]
.

The same idea is used by D. Zou and G. Lerman in
[
36
]
to construct scattering networks in weighted connected finite graphs. To prove good properties for their scattering transform, they use a scaling function
\phi
and a wavelet
\psi
that satisfy

|\widehat{\phi}(2^{J}\omega)|^{2}+\sum_{j>-J}|\widehat{\psi}(2^{-j}\omega)|^{2}=1\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

that can be achieved with the theory of Multiresolution Analysis (MRA).

While our
G
-scattering transform is also a cascade of modulus of convolutions, our wavelets are different from the ones in
[
21
]
and
[
36
]
. In these works the scattering network depends only on the chosen wavelet and at each level
m\geq 1
there are infinitely many paths selected from
\{(j_{1},\dots,j_{m}):j_{i}\geq-J\}
. In our case, described above, our wavelets in (
13
) depend on
(J+1)k
parameters
\{\gamma_{j}(r):1\leq r\leq k\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
, where
k
is the number of conjugacy classes of
G
, with the restriction (
27
). Therefore, G-wavelets are easy to construct and at each level
m\geq 1
the number of features extracted is finite.

Now we introduce a structural property of the scattering transform that will be needed in Chapter
4
, which states that, in the scattering networks, the propagated norm at the
m
-th layer splits into the propagated norm at the next layer plus the output energy at the current layer.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
be a kernel satisfying (
27
), and let
U[\cdot]
and
S[\cdot]
be the associated
G
-scattering propagator and the
G
-windowed scattering operator. Then for all
m\geq 0

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}.

(33)

Proof.

The case
m=0
is a consequence of condition (
27
) and Remark
2.7
with
A=B=1
. Indeed,

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|\psi_{j}*f\|^{2}=\|\phi\ast f\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\psi_{j}*f\|^{2}=\|Qf\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|U[j]f\|^{2}.

By repeating this argument replacing
f
with
U[p]f
we can obtain, for any
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}

\|U[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|U[p+j]f\|^{2}+\|S[p]f\|^{2}.

Finally, we can conclude by summing the previous equation for
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}
:

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|U[p+j]f\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}

where the last identity is due to

\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}=\bigcup_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}p+j.\qed

4
Properties of the
G
-scattering transform

The number of paths over which scattering can be computed is theoretically infinite; however, under condition (
27
), the energy of the
G
-scattering transform is not expanding, as it is made precise in the following Lemma. This result is a consequence of the structural property of the scattering transform stated in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
Non expanding
)
Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be a kernel satisfying (
27
). Then, for
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

\|S[P_{J}]f\|_{2,J}\leq\|f\|\,.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.3
, for all
M\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}-\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}-\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{M+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}\,.

Taking the limit when
M
goes to infinity we obtain the result.
∎

4.1
Stability

We now prove that, under condition (
27
), if two sets of data
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G)
are closed in the
L^{2}(G)
norm, then the
G
-scattering transforms of
f
and
f^{\prime}
are also close in the
\ell_{2}(P_{J},L^{2}(G))
norm. Observe that if the
G
-scattering transform were a linear operator (which is not), this will easily follow form Lemma
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(
Stability
)
Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be a kernel satisfying (
27
). Then, the scattering transform is stable in the following sense: if
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G)
,

\|S[P_{J}]f-S[P_{J}]f^{\prime}\|_{2,J}\leq\|f-f^{\prime}\|.

(34)

Proof.

Let
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m},m\geq 0.
Apply
condition (
27
) and Remark
2.7
with
A=B=1
to the function
U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G)
to obtain

\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\psi_{j}*(U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime})\|^{2}+\|\phi*(U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime})\|^{2}.

(35)

By the triangle inequality, for
j=1,2,\dots,J,

|\psi_{j}*(U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime})|\geq|\psi_{j}*U[p]f|-|\psi_{j}*U[p]f^{\prime}|\,.

Summing the equality (
35
) over
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\psi_{j}*U[p]f-\psi_{j}*U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\phi*U[p]f-\phi*U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\||\psi_{j}*U[p]f|-|\psi_{j}*U[p]f^{\prime}|\|^{2}+\|\phi*U[p]f-\phi*U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|U[p+j]f-U[p+j]f^{\prime}\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f-S[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}+\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f-S[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Summing the previous inequality over
0\leq m\leq M,M\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}-\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}\geq\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f-S[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}.

Canceling equal terms in the left hand side of this inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f-S[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}-\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{M+1}}\|U[p]f-U[p]f^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}\,.

Taking the limit when
M
goes to infinity we deduce the result.
∎

4.2
Energy preservation

Equality in Lemma
4.1
cannot hold in general for a kernel
\gamma
satisfying
(
27
). For example, if
\gamma_{0}(r)=0
for all
r=1,2,\dots,k
, so that (
27
) becomes
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}=1,r=1,2,\dots,k
, then
\phi(x)=0
for all
x\in G
. Therefore,
\phi*f\equiv 0
for any
f\in L^{2}(G)
and
S[p]f\equiv 0
for all
p\in P_{J}
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

A kernel
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
is called
admissible
if in addition to (
27
) it holds that

\beta_{\gamma}:=\min_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}>0\,.

(36)

Under the admissibility condition (
36
) it can be proved that the
G
-scattering transform,
S[P_{J}]:L^{2}(G)\to\ell_{2}(P_{J},L^{2}(G))
is inyective. In fact, if
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G)
and
S[P_{J}]f=S[P_{J}]f^{\prime}
, in particular
\phi*f=\phi*f^{\prime}
. Taking Fourier transforms in both sides and using (
15
) we obtain
\gamma_{0}(r)\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})=\gamma_{0}(r)\widehat{f^{\prime}}(\pi^{r})
for
r=1,2,\dots,k
. Since
\beta_{\gamma}>0
,
\gamma_{0}(r)\neq 0
for
r=1,2,\dots,k
. Therefore,
\widehat{f}(\pi^{r})=\widehat{f^{\prime}}(\pi^{r}),r=1,2,\dots,k
. By the Fourier inversion formula (see
[
33
]
, Chapter 15, Theorem 2), we conclude
f=f^{\prime}
in
L^{2}(G).

We can quantify this inyectivity. In fact, by the definition of
\|\ \|_{2,J}
for
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(G)
we can write

\|S[P_{J}]f-S[P_{J}]f^{\prime}\|_{2,J}^{2}\geq\|\phi*f-\phi*f^{\prime}\|^{2}=\|\phi*(f-f^{\prime})\|^{2}\,.

We now use Plancherel identity (
12
) twice and condition (
36
) to obtain

\|S[P_{J}]f-S[P_{J}]f^{\prime}\|_{2,J}^{2}\geq\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\left|(\widehat{f}-\widehat{f^{\prime}})(\pi^{r})_{ij}\right|^{2}\geq\beta_{\gamma}\|f-f^{\prime}\|^{2}\,.

More important, under the admissibility condition given in Definition
4.3
, we are able to prove that the
G
-scattering transform preserves energy, that is, equality holds in Lemma

4.1
. Before proving this, we show a result about the energy decay rate of the scattering propagator
U
at each level
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be an admissible kernel as given in Definition
4.3
. Then, for each
m=1,2,\dots
, the scattering propagator
U
has norm decay rate
\alpha=1-\beta_{\gamma}<1
at the
m
-th layer, meaning that for all
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\leq\alpha\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,.

(37)

Proof.

For any path
p\in P_{J}
and
f\in L^{2}(G)
, by definition of
S[p]f
and Plancherel equality (
12
) we can write

\|S[p]f\|^{2}=\|\phi*U[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\left|\widehat{U[p]f}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}.

Since
|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\geq\beta_{\gamma}
for all
r=1,2,\dots,k
we get

\displaystyle\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta_{\gamma}{\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}\left|(\widehat{U[p]f}(\pi^{r}))_{i,j}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\gamma}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,,

where we have used again Plancherel equality (
12
). Summing the above inequality over all
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}
we have

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}\geq\beta_{\gamma}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}.

Using the structural property of the scattering network given in Lemma
3.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}-\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta_{\gamma})\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}=\alpha\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,.\qed

As a corollary we can prove that the energy carried out by the extracted features of the
G
-scattering transform at level
m,m\geq 1,
decreases exponentially in the presence of the admissibility condition.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be an admissible kernel as given in Definition
4.3
. Then, for each
m=1,2,\dots
and each
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}\leq\left(\max_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\right)\alpha^{m}\|f\|^{2}\,,

where
\alpha=1-\beta_{\gamma}<1
and
\beta_{\gamma}
is defined in (
36
).

Proof.

Since
S[p]f=\phi*f
for each
p\in\Lambda_{p}^{m}
, by Plancherel equality (
12
) and (
15
)

\displaystyle\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}\left|\widehat{\phi*Uf}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\left|\widehat{Uf}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\right)\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,.

Summing over all
p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}
and using (
37
)
m
times, the result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

(
Energy preservation
)
Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be an admissible kernel as given in Definition
4.3
. Then, for each
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

\|S[P_{J}]f\|_{2,J}=\|f\|\,.

Proof.

By the structural property of the scattering network given in Lemma
3.3
, for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}-\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|^{2}-\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{M+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,.

(38)

By Proposition
4.4
, since
\alpha=1-\beta_{\gamma}<1
,

0\leq\lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{M+1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\leq\lim_{M\to\infty}\alpha^{M}\|f\|^{2}=0\,.

Taking limits when
M\to\infty
in (
38
) we obtain the result.
∎

4.3
Equivariance and approximate invariance

Consider the left and right regular representations of the finite group
G
on
L^{2}(G)
. These are

L:G\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G)),\quad L_{g}f(x)=f(g^{-1}x)

and

R:G\to\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G)),\quad R_{g}f(x)=f(xg),

for
g,x\in G
and
f\in L^{2}(G).

For
\psi\in L^{1}(G)
consider the convolution operator
C_{\psi}:L^{2}(G)\to L^{2}(G)
given by
C_{\psi}(f)=\psi*f.
This convolution operator is
equivariant
(also termed
covariant
in some literature) with respect to the right regular representation; that is, for
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

C_{\psi}(R_{g}f)=R_{g}C_{\psi}(f),\quad g\in G\,.

(39)

This follows inmediately from the definition of convolution given in (
9
).

If
\psi\in L^{1}(G)
is also a class function (see (
6
)),
C_{\psi}
is equivariant with respect to the left regular representation, that is, for
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

C_{\psi}(L_{g}f)=L_{g}C_{\psi}(f),\quad g\in G\,.

(40)

Indeed, since
\psi
is a class function, for
x\in G
use (
10
) and (
9
) to write

C_{\psi}(L_{g}f)(x)=\psi*L_{g}f(x)=L_{g}f*\psi=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}f(g^{-1}y)\psi(y^{-1}x)\,.

With
y^{-1}x=z
,

C_{\psi}(L_{g}f)(x)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}f(g^{-1}xz^{-1})\psi(z)=f*\psi(g^{-1}x)=\psi*f(g^{-1}x)=L_{g}C_{\psi}(f)\,,

where we have used (
9
) and that
\psi
is a class function.

It is easy to see that the modulus operator commutes with
R_{g}
and
L_{g}
for all
g\in G
. Since the scattering networks described in Section
3
are made with convolution with class functions and modulus, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be a kernel and
S
the
G
-scattering operator. For all
g\in G
,
p\in P_{J}
, and
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

a)\ S[p]L_{g}f=L_{g}S[p]f\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad b)\ S[p]R_{g}f=R_{g}S[p]f\,.

Another property of operators is invariance. For the convolution operators
C_{\psi}
and the right and left regular representations this means

a)\ S[p]L_{g}f=S[p]f\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad b)\ S[p]R_{g}f=S[p]f\,.

(41)

f\in L^{2}(G)
and
g\in G
. If (
39
) and part
b)
of (
41
) hold simultaneously, for all
g\in G
and all
x\in G
,

L_{g}C_{\psi}(f)(x)=C_{\psi}(f)(xg).

Taking
x=e
, the identity of the group
G
, it follows that
C_{\psi}f(g)=C_{\psi}(e)
, and thus,
C_{\psi}(f)
is a constant function in
G
. Similarly if
(
40
) and part
a)
of (
41
) hold simultaneously. Consequently, we cannot have, in general, invariance of the
G
-scattering transform. Nevertheless, we can prove that at the depth of the scattering propagator increases, the
G
-scattering features become closer to be invariant, and, hence, closer to be constant functions.

Theorem 4.8
.

(
Approximate invariance
)
Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,2,\dots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{j=0,1,\dots,J}
be an admissible kernel as given in Definition
4.3
. For each
m\geq 1,f\in L^{2}(G),g\in G
,

\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]L_{g}f-S[p]f\|^{2}\leq\left(\max_{1\leq r\leq k}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\right)4\,\alpha^{m}\|f\|^{2}\,,

where
\alpha=1-\beta_{\gamma}<1
and
\beta_{\gamma}
is defined in (
36
). Moreover, a similar result holds replacing
L_{g}
by
R_{g},g\in G.

Proof.

By part
a)
of Proposition
4.7
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]L_{g}f-S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|L_{g}S[p]f-S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{g}-I\|^{2}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{J}^{m}}\|S[p]f\|^{2}

In view of Corollary
4.5
, we only need to prove the estimate
\|L_{g}-I\|^{2}\leq 4.
This follows from the following computation. For any
f\in L^{2}(G)
,

\displaystyle\|L_{g}f-f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}|f(g^{-1}y)-f(y)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}(|f(g^{-1}y)|+|f(y)|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|G|}\sum_{y\in G}(|f(g^{-1}y)|^{2}+|f(y)|^{2})=4\|f\|^{2}\,.

The proof for the right regular representation is similar. ∎

5
Relaxing the admissibility condition

If the kernel
\gamma
does not satisfy (
36
), it holds that
\gamma_{0}(r)=0
for some
r\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}.
Hence, the proof of Proposition
4.4
does not give a norm decay rate smaller than 1 for the scattering propagator. In this case, we are going to show how to obtain a norm decay rate
\alpha<1
for the scattering propagator. To do this, we need to briefly explain the result contained in Theorem 5 of
[
18
]
.

Let
S\subset\{1,2,\dots,k\}
be a subset of indexes or non-equivalent irreducible unitary representations of a finite group
G
. Consider the representation of
G
given by

\rho^{S}:=\bigoplus_{r\in S}d_{r}\pi^{r}\,,

whose character is
\chi^{\rho^{S}}=\sum_{r\in S}d_{r}\chi^{r}
. For a representation
\rho
of
G
, denote by
\rho^{\dagger}
the contragredient representation given by
\rho^{\dagger}(x)=\overline{\rho(x)}^{t}
for all
x\in G
.

Consider now the representation
\rho^{S}\otimes(\rho^{S})^{\dagger}
. It has a decomposition in terms of irreducible unitary representations of
G
, of the form

\rho^{S}\otimes(\rho^{S})^{\dagger}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{k}n_{S}(r)\pi^{r}.

Let
T(S)=\{r\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}:n_{S}(r)\neq 0\}
. Define
\displaystyle deg(S)=\sum_{r\in S}d_{r}^{2}.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem 5 in
[
18
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(G)
be nonnegative and
S
a subset of indexes of the inequivalent irreducible representations of
G
. Then

\sum_{r\in T(S)}d_{r}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}|(\widehat{f}(\pi^{r}))_{i,j}|^{2}\geq\frac{\text{deg(S)}}{|G|}\|f\|^{2}\,.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\gamma=\{\gamma_{j}:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\mathbb{C}\}_{0\leq j\leq J}
be a kernel satisfying (
27
). Let
S\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}
and suppose that
\gamma
is such that

\beta_{\gamma}(S):=\frac{\text{deg(S)}}{|G|}\min_{r\in T(S)}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}>0\,.

Then, for
f\in L^{2}(G)
and
m=2,3,\dots,

\sum_{p\in\lambda_{J}^{m}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\leq\alpha(S)\sum_{p\in\lambda_{J}^{m-1}}\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,,

where
\alpha(S):=1-\beta_{\gamma}(S)<1.

Proof.

For any path
p\in P_{J},\ p\neq\emptyset
, and
f\in L^{2}(G)
, by definition of
S[p]f
and Plancherel equality (
12
) we can write

\displaystyle\|S[p]f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\phi*U[p]f\|^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\left|\widehat{U[p]f}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{r\in T(S)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2}\left|\widehat{U[p]f}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(\min_{r\in T(S)}|\gamma_{0}(r)|^{2})\sum_{r\in T(S)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{r}}d_{r}\left|\widehat{U[p]f}(\pi^{r})_{i,j}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta_{\gamma}(S)\|U[p]f\|^{2}\,,

where the last inequality is due to Theorem
5.1
since
U[p]f
is nonnegative. The proof now proceeds as in the proof of Proposition

4.4
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Under the same conditions of Theorem
5.2
, the same inequality as in Corollary
4.5
can be proved replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha(S).
Moreover, under these conditions we also have the energy preservation result stated in Theorem
4.6
.

Note that
S
can be different or equal to
T(S)
, as shown in the following examples.

Example 5.4
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. A system of irreducible characters of
G
is
\{e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor(\cdot)},\ldots,e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}\lfloor\frac{N-1}{2}\rfloor(\cdot)}\}
. Let
n\leq\frac{N-1}{2}
, and let
S=\{-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor\}
be the indexes of the characters
\{e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor(\cdot)},\ldots,e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor(\cdot)}\}
. Then

\chi^{\rho^{m_{S}}}(x)=\sum_{l=-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}e^{\frac{2\pi ilx}{N}},

(42)

so

\chi_{\rho^{m_{S}}}(x)\overline{\chi_{\rho^{m_{S}}}(x)}=\sum_{l=-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{m=-\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}e^{\frac{2\pi i(l-m)x}{N}}

(43)

Since
l-m
varies from
-n+1
to
n-1
, thus
T(S)\neq S
, because
T(S)=\{-n+1,\dots,n-1\}
contains all the indexes of
\{e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}(n-1)(\cdot)},\ldots,e^{\frac{2\pi i}{N}(n-1)(\cdot)}\}
and has
2n-1
elements, while
S
has
n
elements.

Example 5.5
.

As another example, if
G=S_{n}
and
S
contains the indexes of the trivial character and the character given by the sign of the permutation (
\chi^{1}=I
and
\chi^{2}=\operatorname{sign}
) then
deg(S)=2
and

\rho_{m_{S}}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&\operatorname{sign}(x)\end{bmatrix},

(44)

so that

\rho_{m_{S}}(x)\otimes(\rho_{m_{S}})^{\dagger}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&\operatorname{sign}(x)&0&0\\
0&0&\operatorname{sign}(x)&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix},

(45)

thus
T(S)=S
.

6.2.1
The group

The group we are going to use is the (non-abelian) Affine group over a finite field. Let
p
be a prime. Consider the group of transformations
x\mapsto ax+b,\,a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
a\neq 0
, denoted by
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, with composition operation. It has
p(p-1)
elements.
The elements of
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
can be identified with matrices of the form

\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p},a\neq 0\,,

(47)

and the operation is multiplication of matrices.
Since

\begin{bmatrix}c&d\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}c&d\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}a&(1-a)d+bc\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

the conjugacy classes are

C^{0}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\right\}

C^{1}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}\right\},

C^{a}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\},a\in\{2,3,\ldots,p-1\}.

Thus
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
has
p
conjugacy classes, and, hence
p
irreducible unitary representations (see e.g.
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 3]
). Let
g
be a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, so that all non-zero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are
g^{j}
for some
j\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\}
. By the properties of the matrix product, for
k\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\}
, the homomorphisms below are one-dimensional representations:

\displaystyle\chi^{k}:

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\quad\quad\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}g^{j}&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\longmapsto\exp{\left(\frac{2\pi ikj}{p-1}\right)},\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\},\ b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

Since
\displaystyle|\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}d_{i}^{2}
(see e.g.
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Lemma 2]
), the dimension of the last irreducible representation is
p-1
. Therefore,
\chi^{p-1}(C^{0})=p-1
. Using the orthogonality relations for characters (
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 3]
), we can complete the character table of
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
:
\chi^{p-1}(C^{1})=-1
and
\chi^{p-1}(C^{g^{k}})=0
for

. See Table

To define complex valued functions on

For

as a dot product of the function

and wavelet
\Psi

Wf(\delta,s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}\overline{\Psi\left(\frac{t-s}{\delta}\right)}\,dt\,.

(48)

The wavelet we use is the Morlet wavelet cmorB-C from the module pyct.cwt from PyWavelets (with

and

), which is

\Psi(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi B}}\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{B}}\,e^{2\pi iCt}\,.

(49)

It is easy to see that
\Psi
has

-norm equal to 1 and

-norm equal to

6.2.3
The scattering

Let us denote by

de set of all sounds in the dataset (50 meows and 46 barks) after the preprocessing. In subsection

(see (

To perform a scattering transform we choose a kernel
\{\gamma_{j}:\{0,\dots p-1\}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{0\leq j\leq 2}
. To learn a kernel we take a training set
\mathcal{M}
of 20 meows and a training set
\mathcal{B}
of 20 barks, leaving the rest
\mathcal{S}\setminus(\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{B})
to test de accuracy of the scattering transform with the kernel designed.

For each character
\chi^{k}\,,\ 0\leq k\leq p-1
of
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, define

C_{\mathcal{B}(k)}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}}|\langle W_{p}f\,,\,\chi^{k}\rangle|\,,

and

C_{\mathcal{M}(k)}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}|\langle W_{p}f\,,\,\chi^{k}\rangle|\,.

That is, for each training group we perform the arithmetic mean fo the absolute value of the inner product of
W_{p}f
and
\chi^{k}.

The values of
C_{\mathcal{B}(k)}
(red dots) and
C_{\mathcal{M}(k)}
(blue dots),
0\leq k\leq p-1
, for
p=31
are given in Figure
17
. Similar results are obtained for
p=19
and
p=61
. Observe that the values of
C_{\mathcal{B}(k)}
and
C_{\mathcal{M}(k)}
given in Figure

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
f\in S
, any
p
prime, and any character
\chi^{k}\,,0\leq k\leq p-1
, of
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,

|\langle W_{p}f\,,\,\chi^{k}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}\,,

where
B
is the decay of the Morlet wavelet (see (
49
)). Consequently, for all
0\leq k\leq p-1
,

C_{\mathcal{B}(k)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{\mathcal{M}(k)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}\,.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for all
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,

\displaystyle|W_{p}f(a,b)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}f(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{a\lfloor T/p\rfloor}}\Psi\left(\frac{t-b\lfloor T/p\rfloor}{a\lfloor T/p\rfloor}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}|f(t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{a\lfloor T/p\rfloor}\left|\Psi\left(\frac{t-b\lfloor T/p\rfloor}{a\lfloor T/p\rfloor}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(52)

Since
|f(t)|\leq 1/2
,

\left(\frac{2}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}|f(t)|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{T}T\right)^{1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

After bounding the sum by an integral and doing a change of variables, the second factor of the right hand side of (
52
) can be bound by
\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}.
Therefore,

|W_{p}f(a,b)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}\,.

(53)

Use, temporarily, the notation
G=\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
53
),

\displaystyle|\langle W_{p}f,\chi^{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{(a,b)\in G}W_{p}f(a,b)\,\overline{\chi^{k}(a,b)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{(a,b)\in G}|W_{p}f(a,b)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{(a,b)\in G}|\chi^{k}(a,b)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi B)^{1/4}}\left(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{(a,b)\in G}|\chi^{k}(a,b)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(54)

The last factor in the right hand side of (
54
) is
1
(see
[
33
, Chapt. 15, Theorem 3]
or use Table
2
). This proves the result.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

For
B=3.5
in the Morlet wavelet (
49
), the upper bound for
C_{\mathcal{B}(k)}
and
C_{\mathcal{M}(k)}

To classify these distance functions, we construct a scattering transform with
J=6
with depths 1 and 2. To learn the kernel, we use all the functions from the training set. For
j\in\{1,\ldots,6\}
, let

\tilde{d_{j}}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\max_{\pi\in S_{3}}d_{j}((1),\pi)}d_{j}((1),\cdot)\,,

be the normalized distance to the identity. We define

\gamma_{j}(r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{J}}|\langle\tilde{d_{j}},\chi^{r}\rangle|\,.

(55)

Note that by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\sum_{j=1}^{6}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\langle\tilde{d_{j}},\chi^{r}\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\tilde{d_{j}}\|^{2}<1\,,

so we can define

\gamma_{0}(r)=\sqrt{1-\sum_{j=1}^{6}|\gamma_{j}(r)|^{2}}.

(56)

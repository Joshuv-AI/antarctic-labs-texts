Samit Ghosh, Arjun Paul

Abstract
. Let X X be an irreducible smooth complex projective variety. Let G G be a linear algebraic group over ℂ \mathbb{C} . We define the notion of Lie algebroid valued connection on holomorphic principal G G –bundles on X X , and study their basic properties under extension and reduction of structure group. Finally we address the question of existence of a Lie algebroid connection on principal G G –bundles on smooth complex projective curves.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J60, 53C07, 32L10.

1.
Introduction

A famous theorem of A. Weil
[
17
]
says that a holomorphic vector bundle
E

on a compact connected Riemann surface
X
admits a holomorphic connection if and only if
each indecomposible holomorphic direct summand of
E
has degree zero.
In
[
5
]
M. Atiyah generalizes the notion of holomorphic connections
in the context of holomorphic principal
G
–bundles on compact Kähler manifolds,
and gives an algebro-geometric proof of Weils’ theorem for holomorphic vector bundles
on compact connected Riemann surfaces.
In
[
3
]
Azad and Biswas generalize Weils theorem for
holomorphic principal
G
–bundles on compact connected Riemann surfaces.
It is clear from these results that not every holomorphic vector bundle
and principal
G
–bundle can admit holomorphic connections.
This naturally leads one to consider the notion of meromorphic connections.
One of the simplest kind of meromorphic connections is the notion of
logarithmic connection, which are treated for holomorphic vector bundles
and holomorphic principal
G
–bundles, for example in

[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
etc.

In the context of complex algebraic and differential geometry, the classical notion
of holomorphic as-well-as singular connections has natural generalization by
replacing tangent bundle with a Lie algebroid leading to the notion of

Lie algebroid connections
, which is more convenient to work with in some setups
like Poisson geometry, foliation theory etc. The notion of Lie algebroid connections
also generalize the notion of holomorphic and logarithmic connections.
It is an interesting problem to study Lie algebroid connections on holomorphic
vector bundles and principal bundles.

Let
X
be a connected compact Riemann surface. Fix a holomorphic Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
with
V
a stable vector bundle. In
[
9
]
the authors shows that every holomorphic vector bundle on
X
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection generalizing a result
[
4
, Corollary 3.17]
. It is natural to generalize the notion of Lie algebroid connection on principal
G
–bundles and study the question of existence of Lie algebroid connection on holomorphic or algebraic principal
G
–bundles.

In this paper we generalize the notion of
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connections in the context of principal
G
–bundles in §
2
. In §
3
we study Lie algebroid connections under extension and reduction of the structure group of the principal bundles. Finally in §
4
we address the question of existence of a Lie algebroid connection on principal
G
–bundles over a smooth complex projective curve
X
. In §
4.1
we briefly recall the notion of semistable principal
G
–bundles, and then in §
4.2
we prove the following result which generalize
[
4
, Corollary 3.17]
in the setup of principal
G
–bundles.

Proposition 1.0.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth complex projective curve. Fix a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
such that
V
is a stable vector bundle on
X
with the slope
\mu(V)\neq 2-2g
, where
g
is the genus of
X
. Let
G
be a reductive affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Then any semistable principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.

The case when
E_{G}
is not semistable is addressed in §
4.3
. Here we consider the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of the adjoint bundle
\ad(E_{G})
of
E_{G}
, and use the Harder-Narasimhan reduction of
E_{G}
to reduce to the case of semistable principal bundle. Then we use Proposition
4.2.1
and Proposition
3.2.7
to prove the following general case.

Theorem 1.0.2
.

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth complex projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Fix a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
such that
V
is a stable vector bundle on
X
with the slope
\mu(V)\neq 2-2g
. Let
G
be a reductive linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Then any holomorphic principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.

This generalize the main result of
[
9
]
to the case of holomorphic principal
G
–bundles on
X
.

2.
Lie Algebroid Connections

2.1.
The case of vector bundles

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}
be the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
X
, and let

TX
be the holomorphic tangent bundle of
X
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

[
4
, § 1.1]

A
Lie algebroid
on
X
is a triple
\mathcal{V}:=(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\varphi)
, where

(i)

V
is a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
,

(ii)

[\cdot,\cdot]:V\times V\to V
is a
\mathbb{C}
–bilinear skew-symmetric
morphism of sheaves such that for all locally defined sections

u,v,w
of
V
, the following Jacobi identity holds:

[u,[v,w]]+[v,[w,u]]+[w,[u,v]]=0;

(iii)

\varphi:V\to TX
is a vector bundle homomorphism
satisfying the following properties: for all locally defined sections
s,t
of
V
and
locally defined section
f
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we have

(a)

Compatibility of Lie algebra structures
:

\varphi([s,t])=[\varphi(s),\varphi(t)]
, and

(b)

Leibniz rule
:
[fs,t]=f[s,t]-\varphi(t)(f)s
.

The homomorphism
\varphi
is called the
anchor map
of the Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}
.
The
degree
and the
rank
of
\mathcal{V}
is defined to be the degree and the rank,
respectively, of the underlying vector bundle
V
of
\mathcal{V}
.

The dual of the anchor map gives a holomorphic vector bundle homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:\Omega_{X}^{1}\longrightarrow V^{*},

where
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is the holomorphic cotangent bundle of
X
.
Fix a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}:=(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

A
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
on

\mathcal{E}
on
X
is a
\mathbb{C}
–linear homomorphism of sheaves

D:\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\otimes V^{*}

satisfying the
\varphi^{*}
–twisted Leibniz rule
:

(2.1.3)

D(f\cdot s)=fD(s)+s\otimes\varphi^{*}(df),

for all locally defined section
s
of
\mathcal{E}
and for all locally defined
section
f
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

2.2.
The case of principal
G
–bundles

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}

with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\Lie(G)
.
Let
p:E_{G}\to X
be a holomorphic (resp., algebraic) principal
G
–bundle on
X
.
The adjoint representation

{\rm ad}:G\longrightarrow\GL(\mathfrak{g})

of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a vector bundle

\ad(E_{G}):=E_{G}\times^{\rm ad}\mathfrak{g}

on
X
, called the
adjoint vector bundle
of
E_{G}
.
If
E
is the frame bundle of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
n
on
X
,
then we have
\ad(E)\cong\End(\mathcal{E})
, the endomorphism bundle of
\mathcal{E}
.
The surjective submersion
p:E_{G}\to X
gives rise to an exact sequence of
vector bundles

(2.2.1)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ad(E_{G})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\At(E_{G})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{\prime}p}
\textstyle{TX\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

called the Atiyah exact sequence of
E_{G}
.
Recall that a
holomorphic
(resp.,
algebraic
) connection on
E_{G}

is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–module homomorphism
\nabla:TX\to\At(E_{G})

such that
d^{\prime}p\circ\nabla=\Id_{TX}
; see
[
5
]
.

As before, fix a holomorphic (resp., algebraic) Lie algebroid

\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
.
To extend the notion of
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection to the
case of holomorphic (resp., algebraic) principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
,
we need to modify the Atiyah bundle
\At(E_{G})
in such a way which essentially
keep track of the sections of
\At(E_{G})
whose image under the map
d^{\prime}p
in
(
2.2.1
) lands inside the image of
the anchor map
\varphi:V\to TX
in an appropriate way.
For this purpose following
[
8
,
10
]
we consider the map

\rho:\At(E_{G})\oplus V\longrightarrow TX

defined by

(2.2.2)

\rho(\xi,v)=d^{\prime}p(\xi)-\varphi(v),

for all locally defined section
\xi
of
\At(E_{G})
and locally defined section

v
of
V
. Note that
\rho
is a surjective homomorphism of vector bundles, and

(2.2.3)

\At_{\varphi}(E_{G}):=\rho^{-1}(0)

is a vector bundle on
X
.
Note that
\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})\cong\At(E_{G})\times_{d^{\prime}p,\,TX,\,\varphi}\,V
.
The restriction of the second projection map gives rise
to a vector bundle homomorphism

(2.2.4)

\widetilde{\rho}:\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})\longrightarrow V

with kernel

\mathrm{Ker}(\widetilde{\rho})=\ad(E_{G}).

Thus we have the following short exact sequence

(2.2.5)

0\longrightarrow\ad(E_{G})\longrightarrow\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\rho}}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\longrightarrow 0

of vector bundles on
X
, which fits into the following
commutative diagram

Definition 2.2.7
.

A
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
on
E_{G}
is a
vector bundle homomorphism

\nabla:V\longrightarrow\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})

such that
\widetilde{\rho}\circ\nabla=\Id_{V}
,
where
\widetilde{\rho}
is defined in (
2.2.4
).

Example 2.2.8
.

(i)

Let
\varphi:V\to TX
be an isomorphism of Lie algebroids
(i.e., isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–modules preserving the Lie bracket).
Then it follows from the above commutative diagram
(
2.2.6
) that giving a
\mathcal{V}
–valued
Lie algebroid connection on
E_{G}
is the same as giving a holomorphic
connection on
E_{G}
.

(ii)

Let
D
be a reduced effective divisor on
X
, let
V=TX(-\log D)
be the
logarithmic tangent bundle of
X
, and let
\varphi:TX(-\log D)\hookrightarrow TX

be the inclusion map. Then it follows from the commutative diagram
(
2.2.6
) that giving a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid
connection on
E_{G}
is the same as giving a logarithmic connection on
E_{G}
singular
along the divisor
D
.

Remark 2.2.9
.

Fix a holomorphic Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
. Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
on
X
, and let
\widetilde{E}
be the holomorphic frame bundle of
E
. Note that
\widetilde{E}
is a holomorphic principal
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
–bundle on
X
.
Then
E
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection if and only if
\widetilde{E}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.
Indeed, given a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
D:E\to E\otimes V^{*}
on
E
, for each local section
v
of
V
contraction with
D
defines a first order differential operator

D_{v}:E\to E,\ \ s\mapsto D_{v}(s)=\langle\,D(s),v\rangle

on
E
with the principal symbol
\varphi(v)\in TX
, which by
[
5
]
corresponds to a section
\zeta_{v}
of
\At(\widetilde{E})
satisfying
d^{\prime}p(\zeta_{v})=\varphi(v)
. Consequently the pair
(\zeta_{v},v)
lies in
\At_{\varphi}(\widetilde{E})=\rho^{-1}(0)
, and hence the assignment
v\mapsto(\zeta_{v},v)
defines a vector bundle homomorphism
\nabla:V\to\At_{\varphi}(\widetilde{E})
satisfying
\widetilde{\rho}\circ\nabla=\Id_{V}
.
Conversely, suppose that
\widetilde{E}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection

\nabla:V\to\At_{\varphi}(\widetilde{E}).

Since
\widetilde{\rho}\circ\nabla=\Id_{V}
, given any local section
v
of
V
its image
\nabla(v)
is given by a pair
(\zeta_{v},v)\in\At(\widetilde{E})\oplus V
satisfying
\rho(\zeta_{v},v)=0
, which gives
d^{\prime}p(\zeta_{v})=\varphi(v)
. Then
\zeta_{v}\in\At(\widetilde{E})
corresponds to a first order differential operator on
E
with the principal symbol
\varphi(v)
. Then for given a local section
f
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and a local section
s
of
E
, we have

\zeta_{v}(f\cdot s)=f\zeta_{v}(s)+\varphi(v)(f)s.

This gives a
\mathbb{C}
–linear map
D:E\to E\otimes V^{*}
satisfying the relation

\langle D(s),v\rangle=\zeta_{v}(s),\ \forall\ v\in V.

Then we have

\displaystyle\langle D(f\cdot s),v\rangle=\zeta_{v}(f\cdot s)=f\zeta_{v}(s)+\varphi(v)(f)s=f\langle D(s),v\rangle+\langle s\otimes\varphi^{*}(df),v\rangle\,.

Since the above identity holds for all local sections
v
of
V
, it follows that

D(f\cdot s)=fD(s)+s\otimes\varphi^{*}(df)
. Thus we get a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection on
E
.

The short exact sequence (
2.2.5
)
defines a cohomology class

(2.2.10)

\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})\in H^{1}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes V^{*}),

such that the exact sequence (
2.2.5
)
splits holomorphically if and only if
\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})=0
.
Thus we have the following result.

Proposition 2.2.11
.

A holomorphic principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued
holomorphic Lie algebroid connection if and only if
\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})=0
.
We call
\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})
the
\mathcal{V}
–valued Atiyah class of
E_{G}
.

Let
\nabla:V\to\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})
be a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
on
E_{G}
over
X
. For all locally defined holomorphic sections
s
and
t
of
V
, let

\kappa_{\nabla}(s,t):=[\nabla(s),\nabla(t)]-\nabla([s,t]).

Since the homomorphism
\widetilde{\rho}:\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})\to V
respects the Lie algebra
structures on the sheaves of sections,
\kappa_{\nabla}(s,t)
defines a holomorphic local
section of
\ad(E_{G})
. Thus we obtain a section

\kappa_{\nabla}\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*}),

called the
curvature
of the
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection

\nabla
on
E_{G}
. The section
\kappa_{\nabla}
can be considered as an obstruction
for
\nabla
to be a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Definition 2.2.12
.

A
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
\nabla
on a
principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
is said to be
flat
if
\kappa_{\nabla}=0
.

Proposition 2.2.13
.

If
{\rm rank}(\mathcal{V})=1
, any
\mathcal{V}
–valued
Lie algebroid connection on
E_{G}
is flat.

Proof.

If
{\rm rank}(\mathcal{V})=1
, then
\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*}=0
and so for any

\mathcal{V}
–valued connection
\nabla
on
E_{G}
, its curvature

\kappa_{\nabla}
, being an element of
H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{2}V^{*})=0
,
vanishes identically. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Reduction of structure group

Now it is interesting to ask the following question:
Suppose that
f:G\to H
be a homomorphism of linear algebraic groups over
\mathbb{C}
.
If
E_{H}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued connection, does
E_{G}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued connection?
We give partial answers to this question.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Let
f:G\to H
be an injective homomorphism of linear algebraic groups over
\mathbb{C}

with
G
reductive. Let
E_{G}
be a principal
G
–bundle on
X
, and let

E_{H}=E_{G}\times^{f}H

be the principal
H
–bundle on
X
obtained by extending the structure group of

E_{G}
along the homomorphism
f
.
If
E_{H}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection, then
E_{G}
admits a

\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:={\rm Lie}(G)
and
\mathfrak{h}:={\rm Lie}(H)
be the Lie algebras of
G

and
H
, respectively. Let
df:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the Lie algebra homomorphism
induced by
f
, and let

\ad(f):\ad(E_{G})\longrightarrow\ad(E_{H})

be the vector bundle homomorphism induced by
df
.
Let
\alpha:G\to\End(\mathfrak{g})
and
\beta:H\to\End(\mathfrak{h})
be the
adjoint actions of
G
and
H
, respectively, on their Lie algebras.
Then the composite map

\beta\circ f:G\to\End(\mathfrak{h})

gives an adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
Since
df
is a
G
–module homomorphism and
G
is reductive,
there is a
G
–submodule
W
of
\mathfrak{h}
such that

(3.2.2)

\mathfrak{h}=df(\mathfrak{g})\bigoplus W

as
G
–modules. Since
df
is injective, from the direct sum decomposition of

G
–modules in (
3.2.2
) projecting to the first factor
we get a
G
–module homomorphism
\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that

\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ df=\Id_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
induces a vector bundle
homomorphism

(3.2.3)

\widetilde{\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}}:\ad(E_{H})\longrightarrow\ad(E_{G})

such that
\widetilde{\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\circ\ad(f)=\Id_{{\rm ad}(E_{G})}
.

Suppose that
E_{H}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.
Then there exists a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–module homomorphism

\eta:\At_{\varphi}(E_{H})\to\ad(E_{H})

such that
\eta\circ\iota_{H}=\Id_{\ad(E_{H})}
,
where
\iota_{H}:\ad(E_{H})\to\At_{\varphi}(E_{H})
is the homomorphism
in (
3.1.1
).
Then it follows from the commutativity of the diagram
(
3.1.1
)
that the composition

\widetilde{\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\circ\eta\circ\At(f):\At_{\varphi}(E_{G})\to\ad(E_{G})

gives an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–linear splitting of the top exact sequence in
(
3.1.1
).
Thus
E_{G}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.
∎

4.
Existence of Lie Algebroid Connections

In this section we prove existence of Lie algebroid connection on a principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on a smooth complex projective curve
X
. We first prove it for the case of semistable principal bundles, and then prove the general case by analyzing the Harder-Narasimhan reduction of
E_{G}
.

4.1.
Semistable principal
G
–bundles

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth complex projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1
. Fix an ample line bundle
H
on
X
. Let
E
be a torsion-free coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–modules
E
on
X
. Note that
E
is locally free outside a closed subset of codimension
\geq 2
. The
degree
of
E
is defined by

\deg(E):=\int_{X}c_{1}(E)\cup[H]^{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z},

where
c_{1}(E)
stands for the first Chern class of the determinant line bundle
\det(E)
of
E
. Note that
\deg(E)
depends on choice of an ample class
[H]
on
X
.
The rational number

\mu(E):=\frac{\deg(E)}{{\rm rank}(E)}

is called the
slope
of
E
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

A torsion-free coherent sheaf
E
on
X
is said to be
stable
(resp.,
semistable
) if for any non-zero proper subsheaf
F
of
E
we have
\mu(F)<\mu(E)
(resp.,
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)
).

The notion of slope semistablity and stability has a natural generalization to the case of principal
G
–bundles due to Ramanathan
[
14
,
15
,
2
]
, which coincides with the corresponding notion for rank
r
vector bundles by taking
G=\GL_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
G
be a reductive linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Let

p:E_{G}\longrightarrow X

be a principal
G
–bundle on
X
.
Recall that a reduction of structure group of
E_{G}
to a closed subgroup
P\subseteq G
over an open subset
U\subseteq X
is given by a section

\sigma\in\Gamma(U,E_{G}/P)

over
U
of the fiber bundle
E_{G}/P\longrightarrow X
. If
\pi:E_{G}\to E_{G}/P
is the projection map, then for given a section
\sigma:U\longrightarrow E_{G}/P
of the fiber bundle
E_{G}/P\to X
, the inverse image
\pi^{-1}(\sigma(U))\subseteq E_{G}
defines a principal
P
–bundle
E_{P}
over
U
such that the principal
G
–bundle
E_{P}\times^{P}G
on
U
obtained by extension of structure group of
E_{P}
along the inclusion homomorphism
P\hookrightarrow G
is isomorphic to
{E_{G}}\big|_{U}
.

Definition 4.1.2
.

A principal
G
–bundle
p:E_{G}\to X
is said to be
stable
(resp.,
semistable
) if for any maximal proper parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and for every reduction
\sigma:U\to E_{G}/P
of its structure group to an open subset
U\subset X
with
{\rm codim}_{X}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
, we have

\deg\left(\sigma^{*}(T_{\rm rel})\right)>0\ (\text{resp., }\,\geq\,0),

where
T_{\rm rel}
is the relative tangent bundle for the natural projection
E_{G}/P\longrightarrow X
.

Semistability of principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
, with
G
a reductive affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, can be characterized in terms of semistability of its adjoint vector bundle.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

[
2
]

A principal
G
–bundle
E_{G}
on
X
is semistable if and only if its adjoint vector bundle
\ad(E_{G}):=E_{G}\times^{G}\mathfrak{g}
on
X
is semistable.

4.2.
Existence for semistable
G
–bundles

Let
X
be an irreducible smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
.
Fix a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X

such that the underlying vector bundle
V
of
\mathcal{V}
is stable.
Let

\mu(\mathcal{V}):=\frac{\deg(V)}{{\rm rank}(V)}

be the
slope
of the underlying vector bundle
V
of the Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}
. Note that
TX
is a line bundle on
X
with the slope
\mu(TX)=2-2g
, where
g
is the genus of
X
.

If
\mu(\mathcal{V})>2-2g=\mu(TX)
, then both
V
and
TX
being stable vector bundles
on
X
we have
H^{0}(X,\Hom(V,TX))=0
(see
[
13
, Proposition 1.2.7]
),
and hence
\varphi=0
in this case. Then a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
on
E_{G}
is just a global section of
\ad(E_{G})\otimes V^{*}
; so we may take the
zero section in
H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes V^{*})
, in particular.

If
\mu(\mathcal{V})=2-2g=\mu(TX)
, then any non-zero
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–module homomorphism

\varphi:V\to TX
is an isomorphism (see
[
13
, Proposition 1.2.7]
).
Then we may replace
V
with
TX
so that a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection
on
E_{G}
is nothing but a holomorphic connection on
E_{G}
. This case is studied in
detail in
[
3
]
.

Now we assume that
\mu(\mathcal{V})<2-2g=\mu(TX)
. Then we have the following.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
G
be a reductive linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
.
With the above assumptions on
\mathcal{V}
, any semistable principal
G
–bundle

E_{G}
on
X
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})\in H^{1}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes V^{*})
be the
\mathcal{V}
–valued Atiyah class
of
E_{G}
. By Serre duality, we have

H^{1}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes V^{*})\cong H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})^{*}\otimes V\otimes K_{X})^{*},

where
K_{X}=\Omega_{X}^{1}
is the canonical line bundle on
X
.
Since
E_{G}
is semistable by assumption, its adjoint bundle
\ad(E_{G})
is semistable
by
[
2
, Proposition 2.10]
.
Then the tensor product bundle
\ad(E_{G})^{*}\otimes V\otimes K_{X}
is semistable
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.1.4]
).
Since
G
is reductive, there is a
G
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric
\mathbb{C}
–bilinear form
\kappa
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, where
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
; c.f.
[
16
]
. This gives an isomorphism of the adjoint vector bundle
\ad(E_{G})
with its dual vector bundle
\ad(E_{G})^{*}
on
X
. From this we have
\deg(\ad(E_{G}))=0
, and hence

\mu(\ad(E_{G})^{*}\otimes V\otimes K_{X})=\mu(K_{X})+\mu(V)=2g-2+\mu(V)<0.

Then by
[
13
, Proposition 1.2.7]

H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})^{*}\otimes V\otimes K_{X})=0
,
and hence
\Phi_{\mathcal{V}}(E_{G})=0
. Hence the result follows.
∎

4.3.
The case of non-semistable
G
–bundles

Now consider the case when
E_{G}
is not semistable.
We study this case by analyzing Harder-Narasimhan filtration of the adjoint vector bundle of
E_{G}
. To make the exposition self-contained, we briefly recall some results from
[
2
]
.

Let
G
be a reductive affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Then there is a
G
–invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, where
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Fix such a form
\kappa
on
\mathfrak{g}
and call it the “Killing form” on
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
E_{G}
be a principal
G
–bundle on
X
. Suppose that
E_{G}
is not semistable, then
E:=\ad(E_{G})
is not semistable by Lemma
4.1.3
.
Let

(4.3.1)

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{k}=\ad(E_{G})

be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
E:=\ad(E_{G})
by its subsheaves
E_{i}
, with
E/E_{i}
torsion free, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then there exists a non-empty open subset
U\subseteq X
with
{\rm codim}_{X}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
such that
E_{i}
and
E/E_{i}
are locally free on
U
, for all
i\geq 0
. Let
E_{j}^{\perp}\subseteq E
be the subsheaf of
E=\ad(E_{G})
whose fiber at each
x\in U
is

E_{j,x}^{\perp}:=\left\{v\in E_{x}:\kappa(v,E_{j,x})=0\right\}.

Since
\kappa
is non-degenerate,
E_{j}^{\perp}\big|_{U}\cong{(E/E_{j})^{*}}\big|_{U}
, for all
i\geq 0
. Moreover,
E^{*}\cong E
. Since the dual
F^{*}
of a semistable torsion-free coherent sheaf
F
on
X
is again semistable with slope
\mu(F^{*})=-\mu(F)
and the the filtration

(4.3.2)

0=E_{k}^{\perp}\subset E_{k-1}^{\perp}\subset\cdots\subset E_{1}^{\perp}\subset E_{0}^{\perp}

satisfies
\mu(E_{k-1}^{\perp})>\mu(E_{k-2}^{\perp}/E_{k-1}^{\perp})>\cdots>\mu(E_{0}^{\perp}/E_{1}^{\perp})
, by uniqueness of Harder-Narasimhan filtration (c.f.
[
13
, Theorem 1.3.4]
) of
E
the filtration (
4.3.2
) coincides with the filtration (
4.3.1
). Therefore,
E_{j}^{\perp}=E_{k-j}
, for all
j=0,1,\ldots,k
.
Therefore, by reindexing, we can rewrite the filtration (
4.3.1
) as

0=F_{-(\ell+1)}\subset F_{-\ell}\subset\cdots\subset F_{-1}\subset F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots F_{\ell-1}\subset F_{\ell}=E,

where
F_{-i}
is orthogonal to
F_{i-1}
with respect to the Killing form
\kappa
on the Lie algebra bundle
E=\ad(E_{G})
.
It follows from the slope inequality and semistability of the successive quotients of terms of the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
E
that the following composite maps

\displaystyle F_{0}\otimes F_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[\cdot\,,\,\cdot]}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\twoheadrightarrow E/F_{0},

and

\displaystyle F_{-i}\otimes F_{-i-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[\cdot\,,\,\cdot]}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\twoheadrightarrow E/F_{-i-1}

vanishes for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore,
F_{0}
is a sheaf of Lie subalgebras of
E=\ad(E_{G})
and

[F_{-i},F_{-1}]\subseteq F_{-i-1},\ \forall\ i\geq 0.

In particular,
F_{-1}
is a sheaf of nilpotent Lie subalgebra of
F_{0}
, which is also an ideal of
F_{0}
. Moreover, by
[
11
, Lemma II.2]
,
F_{0}
is a sheaf of parabolic Lie subalgebra of
E=\ad(E_{G})
which, by
[
2
, Lemma 2.11]
, gives a reduction
E_{P}\subset E_{G}
of structure group of
E_{G}
to a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
outside a closed subset of
X
of codimension
\geq 2
, known as the Harder-Narasimhan reduction or the canonical reduction of
E_{G}
. In particular,
\ad(E_{P})=F_{0}
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}_{u}(P)
be the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
. Fix a maximal torus
T
of
G
. Then the quotient
P/\mathfrak{R}_{u}(P)
is isomorphic to a closed connected reductive subgroup
L
of
P
such that the restriction of the quotient map
q:P\to P/{\mathfrak{R}}_{u}(P)
to
L\subset P
is an isomorphism of algebraic groups. Then the principal
L
–bundle

E_{L}:=E_{P}\times^{q}L

on
X
obtained by extension of the structure group of
E_{P}
by the quotient homomorphism

q:P\to P/\mathfrak{R}_{u}(P)\cong L

is semistable (see
[
1
, Theorem 1 and Lemma 6]
).
Then by Proposition
4.2.1
the principal
L
–bundle
E_{L}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection. Now the action of
P
on the nipotent radical
\mathfrak{n}:={\rm Lie}(\mathfrak{R}_{u}(P))
of the Lie algebra of
P
gives rise to a subbundle
E_{P}(\mathfrak{n})=E_{P}\times^{P}\mathfrak{n}
of the adjoint bundle
\ad(E_{P})
of
E_{P}
. Note that
E_{P}(\mathfrak{n})\cong F_{-1}
by
[
1
, Lemma 6]
,
where
F_{-1}
is the sheaf of nilpotent Lie subalgebra of
F_{0}=\ad(E_{P})
as mentioned in the preceding paragraph. Since for any surjective
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module homomorphism of torsion-free coherent sheaves
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
we have
\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{E})\geq\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
(see e.g.,
[
13
, p. 29]
), it follows from
[
1
, Lemma 7]
that

\mu_{\min}(E_{P}(\mathfrak{n}))>0.

Since
V\otimes K_{X}
is semistable with
\mu(V\otimes K_{X})<0
,
it follows from
[
1
, Proposition 1]

that
\Hom(E_{P}(\mathfrak{n}),V\otimes K_{X})=0
, and hence
by Serre duality we have

H^{1}(X,E_{P}(\mathfrak{n})\otimes V^{*})=0.

Then by Proposition
3.2.7

the principal
P
–bundle
E_{P}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection, and then
by Proposition
3.1.4
the principal

G
–bundle
E_{G}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.
Thus we obtain the following.

Theorem 4.3.3
.

Fix a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{V}=(V,[\cdot\,,\,\cdot],\varphi)
on
X
such that
V
is stable with
\mu(V)<2-2g=\deg(TX)
.
Let
G
be a reductive linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
E_{G}
be a principal
G
–bundle on
X
.
Then
E_{G}
admits a
\mathcal{V}
–valued Lie algebroid connection.

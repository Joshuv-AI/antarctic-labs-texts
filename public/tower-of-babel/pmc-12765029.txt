Glucocorticoid inhibits lung cancer cells proliferation and migration by suppressing smad2/3 activation
Authors: Lei Xu, Qi Li, Chang Xu, Dongmei Tan, Yi Tan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Glucocorticoid (GCs), as an adjuvant drug, is widely used for alleviating chemotherapy or immunotherapy-induced adverse events, such as nausea and immune-related diseases in lung cancer treatment. However, whether GCs directly affects lung cancer progression remains unclear. This study focused on the effects of dexamethasone (DEX) on the proliferation and migration of lung cancer cells as well as the related potential mechanisms using EdU, CCK8, Transwell, Wound healing, TCGA database, HPA database, qPCR and Western blotting. Additionally, subcutaneous tumor and tail vein tumor injection models were established for investigating DEX action in vivo. Further, to confirm the key functional of Smad2/3 for DEX regulating lung cancer cells proliferation and migration. Mechanistic assays explored the upstream regulatory molecules TGFβ1, TGFβR1 and p-TGFβR1 expression after DEX treatment. Moreover, DEX modulates lung cancer cell proliferation and migration on a glucocorticoid receptor (GR)-independent manner and the association with Smad2/3 activation were investigated. Results: The results showed that DEX (1 µM) treatment significantly inhibited the proliferation and the migration of lung cancer cells A549 and LLC cells. TCGA and HPA databases showed Smad2/3 expression was positively correlated with lung cancer progression. Mechanistically, DEX treatment significantly inhibited Smad2/3 activation in vitro and in vivo, manifested as a decrease in the expression of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3. Smad2/3 knockdown significantly inhibit the proliferation, migration and Smad2/3 activity of A549 and LLC cells. Creatine, the agonist of Smad2/3 activation, markedly reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on the proliferation and migration of A549 and LLC cells. Smad2/3 overexpression also reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on A549 cell proliferation and migration. Further studies showed DEX inhibited the expression of TGFβR1 and p-TGFβR1(Ser165) and the levels of TGFβ1. Moreover, GR knockout significantly promoted the proliferation, migration and Smad2/3 activity in A549 cell. DEX can inhibits A549 cell proliferation and migration through suppressing Smad2/3 activation independent of GR. Conclusion: DEX inhibited the proliferation and migration of lung cancer cells by suppressing Smad2/3 activation, providing a theoretical basis and experimental data for clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-29163-3.

Introduction

Lung cancer has the highest global incidence and mortality rates among all cancers, accounting for nearly one-fifth of all cancer-related death1,2. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancers with a 5-year survival rate of less than 3%3. In clinics, traditional chemotherapy and immunotherapy targeting immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), such as PD1, PDL1, and CTLA4, are used to treat NSCLC patients4. However, chemotherapy can cause some side effects, such as nausea, vomiting, and allergies, while immunotherapy can lead to immune-related adverse events (irAEs)5,6.

Glucocorticoid (GCs) are widely used as chemotherapy and immunotherapy adjuvant drugs to combat the related side effects in NSCLC treatment, such as nausea, edema, and autoimmune disease7,8. However, their direct effects on NSCLC progression remain unclear. Clinical studies showed that GCs could reduce the nodules number of lung lesions as well as the chances of partially solid nodules becoming cancerous9,10. Moreover, preclinical results showed that GCs, such as DEX and budesonide, could inhibit the proliferation of A549 by regulating the phosphorylation of retinoblastoma (Rb) and preventing G1 to S phase progress11–13. However, a study also showed DEX could enhance the proliferation of the calu-6 cell in vitro and in vivo11. Based on these observations, GCs, especially DEX, might significantly alter NSCLC progression; however, the molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways involved in these phenotypic changes remain poorly understood.

Smads are key mediators of the transforming growth factor-β (TGFβ) signaling pathway, which plays an important role in cancer progression, such as the proliferation, migration, and apoptosis of cancer cells14. Briefly, TGF-β activates its relevant receptors that induce receptor-regulated phosphorylation of Smads (such as Smad2 and Smad3); the activated Smads bind to Smad4 and translocate to the nucleus, recruiting transcription factors and regulating target genes15. Previous studies showed that the TGF-β pathway is significantly activated in NSCLC, and ALKBH5 inhibit the expression of Smad3, which then suppresses NSCLC cell invasion16. In lung cancer, Smad2/3 activation is closely associated with malignant properties, including EMT and metastasis; suppressing either Smad2 or Smad3 expression can inhibit these effects17. Targeting the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway for therapeutic intervention of cancer has emerged as a crucial clinical challenge. GCs bind cytoplasmic GR, and the GC-GR complex enters the nucleus to control target gene expression18, however, GCs modulates lung cancer cell proliferation and migration on GR-independent manner and the association with Smad2/3 activation remain unclear.

This study aimed to examine the inhibitory role of DEX in the proliferation and migration of A549 and LLC cells as well as investigate the potential mechanistic pathways in NSCLC. The results showed that DEX could suppress lung cancer cell proliferation and migration by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation. This study provides insight into how DEX regulates Smad2/3 signaling in the proliferation and metastasis of NSCLC cells and suggests a valuable clinical therapeutic agent.

Results

DEX inhibited the proliferation of LLC and A549 cells

We analyze the effect of DEX on cell proliferation at different time points using 2-way ANOVA. The ANOVA results showed there exists the interaction effect (F(3,24) = 8.238, P = 0.0006). The multiple comparisons showed treatments with DEX for 48 h (P = 0.0051) and 72 h (P < 0.0001) suppressed the proliferation of LLC (Fig. 1A). Similarly, there exists the interaction effect (F(3,24) = 191.9, P < 0.0001) and DEX treatments for 24 h (P < 0.0001), 48 h (P < 0.0001), and 72 h (P < 0.0001) showed inhibitory effects in A549 cells (Fig. 1B). The EdU assay showed that the DEX treatment inhibited the proliferation of LLC and A549 cells (Fig. 1C, D).

DEX suppressed the proliferation and migration of LLC and A549 cells by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation

Bioinformatics analysis was performed to further explore the underlying inhibitory mechanisms of DEX. According to TCGA and HPA databases, the mRNA expression of Smad2 and the protein expression of Smad2/3 were upregulated in patients with tumors as compared to normal individuals (Fig. 3A-C). Moreover, the overall survival of cancer patients was negatively correlated with the high expression of Smad2/3 (Fig. 3D, E). For the primary tumor, the expression levels of Smad2 and Smad3 were the highest in the T4 and T3 stages, respectively (Fig. 3F). No significant correlation was observed between the Smad2/3 expression and lymph node metastasis status (Fig. 3G). The Smad3 expression analysis showed a positive trend in distant metastatic lesions (M1) as compared to non-metastatic cases (M0); however, the trend was statistically insignificant (P = 0.07) (Fig. 3H). Furthermore, the results showed that DEX inhibited Smad2/3 activation in LLC and A549 cells, manifested as a decrease in the expression of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3 (Fig. 3I-J). Moreover, DEX downregulates Smad2/3 expression at the transcriptional level by qPCR assay analyzed (Fig. 3K-L).

DEX suppressed the proliferation of NSCLC cells by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation in tumor-bearing mice

In order to analyze the effects of DEX on NSCLC cell growth in vivo, a subcutaneous xenograft mouse model was established. The control group mice were treated with 0.9% NaCl, and other groups were treated with DEX (Fig. 4A). Statistical analysis was performed using an independent samples t-test, and the results showed that compared with the control group, both tumor volume and weight in the DEX-treated group were significantly reduced (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B-D). IHC analysis showed that the DEX treatment significantly decreased the expression of Ki67 and PCNA (Fig. 4E). The effects of DEX on inhibiting tumor growth were further investigated based on Smad2/3 activity. IHC analysis showed that DEX treatment significantly inhibited Smad2/3 activation, manifested as a decrease in the expression of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3 expression (Fig. 4F).

DEX suppressed the metastasis of NSCLC cells by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation in tumor-bearing mice

In order to analyze the effects of DEX on NSCLC cell migration in vivo, a lung metastasis xenograft model was established. The model mice were divided into two treatment groups, receiving either 0.9% NaCl or DEX (Fig. 5A). The results showed that DEX treatment significantly reduced the number of metastatic lesions (Fig. 5B). Moreover, hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining also revealed a decrease in the formation of metastatic foci in the DEX-treated mice (Fig. 5C). E-cadherin and Vimentin play an important role in EMT; the IHC analysis showed that after DEX treatment significantly upregulated the expression of E-cadherin and downregulated that of vimentin (Fig. 5D). The effects of DEX on inhibiting tumor metastasis were further investigated based on Smad2/3 activity. IHC analysis showed that DEX treatment significantly inhibited Smad2/3 activation, manifested by decreased expression levels of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3 (Fig. 5E).

Smad2/3 knockdown significantly promoted the proliferation and migration of LLC and A549 cells

Firstly, qPCR was employed to identify the Smad2/3 interference efficient. The results showed that Smad2/3 expression was significantly declined notably in Smad2/3 siRNA group in comparison with the control group in LLC and A549 cells (Fig. 6A, B). To explore the effect of Smad2/3 expression on the proliferation and migration of NSCLC cells. CCK8 assay was employed to identify the cell proliferation, the results showed knockdown Smad2, Smad3 or co-knockdown Smad2/3 could significantly suppress the proliferation of LLC and A549 cells (Fig. 6C, D). Transwell assay was employed to identify the cell migration, the results showed knockdown Smad2, Smad3 or co-knockdown Smad2/3 could significantly suppress the migration of LLC and A549 cells (Fig. 6E, F). Moreover, knockdown Smad2, Smad3 or co-knockdown Smad2/3 could significantly inhibit Smad2/3 activity (Fig. 6G, H).

Smad2/3 activator creatine reversed the DEX-mediated Inhibition of NSCLC cell proliferation and migration

In order to further explore whether DEX could suppress the proliferation and migration of NSCLC cells by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation, the LLC and A549 cells were cultured with DEX (1 µM), creatine (1 µM) or their combination for 48 h. EdU assay showed that treatment with creatine increased the LLC and A549 cell proliferation and reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on LLC and A549 cell proliferation (Fig. 7A, B). Similarly, the wound healing assay also showed that treatment with creatine increased LLC and A549 cell migration and reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on LLC and A549 cell migration (Fig. 7C, D). Moreover, the results revealed that treatment with creatine activated Smad2/3 signaling, reversing the inhibitory effects of DEX on Smad2/3 activation in LLC and A549 cells (Fig. 7E, F). These results indicated that DEX could suppress the proliferation and migration of NSCLC cells by inhibiting the Smad2/3 activation.

Smad2/3 overexpression could reverse the DEX-mediated Inhibition of A549 cell proliferation and migration

To further explore the relationship between DEX and Smad2/3 activation in suppressing cell proliferation and migration, we conducted rescue experiments using gene overexpression techniques, followed by DEX treatment. EdU assay showed that Smad2/3 overexpression could increase the proliferation of A549 cell and reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on its proliferation (Fig. 8A). Moreover, the transwell assay showed that the overexpression of Smad2/3 increased the migration ability of A549 cell and reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on its migration (Fig. 8B). Additionally, total proteins extracted from the cells were used to verify the transfection efficiency of overexpression (Fig. 8C). These results indicated that the overexpression of Smad2/3 reversed the inhibitory effects of DEX on the proliferation and migration of A549 cell.

Discussion

Glucocorticoids (GCs) have been widely incorporated into NSCLC treatment regimens as supportive medications, which can effectively mitigate chemotherapy-associated emesis and ICI-induced autoimmune complications20,21. However, their direct effects, especially those of DEX, on NSCLC progression remain unclear. Previous studies showed that it could activate GR and inhibit the NSCLC cell growth22. Epidemiological studies showed that it could reduce lung cancer incidence23. Moreover, clinical trials also showed that it decreased the number of nodules in patients with lung lesions9. These studies indicated that it was associated with NSCLC progression; however, the direct underlying mechanisms still remain elusive. The current study aimed to evaluate the direct effects of DEX on the growth and migration of NSCLC cells in vitro and in vivo and identify the direct underlying mechanisms. The results showed DEX could inhibit the growth and migration of lung cancer by regulating Smad2/3 activation. Thus, DEX, potentially targeting Smad2/3, might be a valuable therapeutic agent for the inhibition of NSCLC cell growth and metastasis in clinical practice.

In order to investigate the direct effects of DEX on NSCLC progression, first, the effects of DEX on NSCLC cell proliferation were evaluated using A549 and LLC in vitro. Previous studies showed that DEX and budesonide could inhibit the A549 cell proliferation by inhibiting the Rb phosphorylation24. In the current study, results showed that the DEX treatment could significantly inhibit tumor cell proliferation, which was indicated by EdU and CCK8 assay. Previous studies showed that GCs could promote lung metastasis of pancreatic cancer cells and promote breast cancer metastasis by upregulating the expression of kinase ROR125[,26, suggesting the use of GCs for cancer treatment needs attention. The current study further evaluated the role of DEX on NSCLC cell migration; the results showed that DEX treatment significantly inhibited NSCLC cell migration, evaluated using Transwell and wound healing assays. These results suggest that DEX might be a potential drug for lung cancer treatment.

TGF-β signaling pathway plays an important role in tumorigenesis and progression. Smad2 and Smad3 are the members of receptor-regulated SMADs (R-SMADs)-conserved family of proteins that promote TGF-β signaling; this has been shown to be associated with the progression of tumors, including NSCLC27,28. Previous studies showed that Smad3-knockdown could significantly inhibit the growth of LUAD cells by targeting P2126. Smad3 binds to the promoter of RAB26, promoting its expression and thereby promoting NSCLC progression30. Moreover, the knockdown of SMAD2 inhibited TGF-β-induced EMT as well as the migration and invasion in NSCLC cells31. Consistent with previous studies, bioinformatics analysis in the current study indicated that the expression of Smad2/3 was upregulated in the patients with tumors as compared to the healthy individuals in the HPA database, and the overall survival of cancer patients was negatively correlated with high expression of Smad2/3 in TCGA database. According to the seventh edition of the TNM classification standard32, the Smad2/3 expression levels were significantly upregulated in the T4 or T3 stage, respectively, suggesting their possible role in advanced local invasion. Interestingly, while Smad2/3 expression showed no significant correlation with lymph node metastasis, a suggestive positive trend emerged for Smad3 in distant metastasis (M1 vs. M0, p = 0.07). Previous studies showed that the activation of Smad2 and Smad3 could promote tumor progression, such as colorectal cancer33, gastric cancer34, and lung cancer35. Further exploring the effects of DEX on the activation of Smad2 and Smad3, the current study showed that the DEX treatment significantly inhibited Smad2 and Smad3 activation, indicating a decrease in the expression levels of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3. Previous studies showed that low dose of DEX (100 nM) inhibit cell migration by suppress the activity of TGF-β135[,36. Here we found that DEX (1 µM) suppress the activity of TGF-β1 and its downstream regulatory molecules Smad2/3. However, previous studies also found DEX (10 mM) induced apoptosis of A549 cells via activating the TGF-β1/Smad2 signaling pathway37. The discrepancy may be attributed to the concentration of DEX. The biological effects of DEX show a significant dose-dependent relationship. Low dose DEX was believed to exert their effects through regulation transcriptional of target genes38, here we found treatment with DEX significantly inhibit the transcriptional of Smad2/3. High doses of GCs could exert non-genomic actions by mediating MAPK signaling pathways38,39. Previous showed that the MAPK pathway crosstalks with the TGF-β/Smad signaling by regulating Smad phosphorylation at the linker region40, which may be indirectly regulated TGF-β1/Smad2.

The in vivo effects of DEX on NSCLC cell growth were further evaluated by establishing a subcutaneous xenograft mouse model. The results showed that DEX treatment significantly reduced the tumor size in tumor-bearing mice. IHC analysis showed that DEX treatment significantly decreased the expressions of Ki67 and PCNA. TGF-β/Smad2/3 signaling pathway is a central regulator in various tumor growth. Smad2/3 activation, manifested by an increase in the phosphorylation of Smad2/3 expression was positively correlated with various tumor growth, including LUAD41, ovarian cancer42, and renal cell carcinoma43. The current study demonstrated that treatment with DEX significantly inhibited Smad2/3 activation, manifested by the downregulation of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3 expression. These findings indicated that DEX could inhibit NSCLC cell proliferation by targeting Smad2/3 activation. Moreover, the effects of DEX on NSCLC cell metastasis were confirmed by establishing a lung metastasis xenograft model. The results showed that treatment with DEX significantly reduced the number of metastasis nodules, upregulated and downregulated the expression of EMT-regulating proteins E-cadherin and Vimentin, respectively. Previous studies found that MDM2 could activate the Smad2/3 pathway, thus promoting EMT in lung cancer. Salvianolic acid B can inactivate the Smad2/3 pathway, thereby acting against lung cancer cell migration44. The current study showed that treatment with DEX significantly inhibited Smad2/3 activation, manifested by the downregulation of p-Smad2 (Ser465/467), Smad2, p-Smad3 (Ser423/425), and Smad3 expression. These findings indicated that DEX could suppress the metastasis of NSCLC cells by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation. To further confirm whether the effects of DEX on NSCLC cell proliferation and migration depended on Smad2/3 activation, firstly, our results showed that Smad2/3 itself could regulate the proliferation and migration of NSCLC cells. Then, the cells were treated with an activator of Smad2/3 and Smad2/3 overexpression, proliferation and migration assay showed its could reverse DEX-mediated inhibition of NSCLC cells proliferation and migration. These results further confirmed that DEX could suppress the growth and migration of lung cancer by inhibiting the Smad2/3 activation.

TGF-β binds to its receptor on the cell membrane, forming a ligand–receptor complex, which subsequently recruits and phosphorylates Smad proteins45. Here, we found that after DEX treatment, the level of TGFβ1 and the expression of TGFβR1 and p-TGFβR1(ser165) were significantly downregulated. Previous studies showed TGFβ1 could induce EMT and promoted lung adenocarcinoma migration and invasion46. TGFβ1 knockdown could reduce the cell proliferation and migration of human NSCLCs cells47. The results indicated DEX could indirectly inhibits Smad2/3 by suppressing the TGFβ1/TGFβR1 interaction. In the future, we would further investigate other potential molecules in the process of DEX-mediated inhibition of cell proliferation and migration. GCs bind cytoplasmic GR, and the GC-GR complex enters the nucleus to control target gene expression, however, GCs modulates lung cancer cell proliferation, migration and Smad2/3 activation on GR-independent manner remain unclear. Here we found GCs inhibits lung cancer cell proliferation, migration and Smad2/3 activation on GR-independent manner. Future studies should further investigate the molecular mechanisms by which DEX interacts with Smad2/3 activation on a GR independent manner.

Methods

Animals and cell lines

Six-week-old male C57BL/6 mice and male BALB/C nude mice were purchased from Charles River Bioscience (Beijing, China). All animals were kept in specific pathogen-free environments with access to sterilized food and water ad libitum. The mouse and human NSCLC cell lines, LLC and A549, and HEK293 cell were purchased from Suncell Bioscience (Wuhan, China). The cell lines were cultured in DMEM (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, VivaCell, Shanghai, China) and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) in an incubator with a humidified atmosphere and 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

Establishment of stable GR-Knockout cells

The target sgRNAs were cloned and inserted into lentiCRISPR v2 (Plasmid #52961, Addgene, USA). GR-sgRNA sense (5,−3,): CACCGGCAGTGTGCTTGCTCAGGAG, GR-sgRNA antisense (5,−3,): AAACCTCCTGAGCAAGCACACTGCC. Targeted plasmids, PsPAX2 and pMD2.G vectors (Plasmid #12260, #12259, Addgene, USA) were simultaneously transfected in HEK 293 cells. After lentiviral transfection of A549 cells, the cells were selected with puromycin (Solarbio, Beijing, China) according to previous reported48.

SiRNA transfection

The A549 and LLC cells were transfected with 50nmol/L of human Smad2-siRNA (UGGAGAAACAAGUGACCAA/UUGGUCACUUGUUUCUCCA), Smad3-siRNA (GCGGAGUACAGGAGACAGA/UCUGUCUCCUGUACUCCGC), mouse Smad2-siRNA (GCUGAGUGCCUAAGUGAUA/UAUCACUUAGGCACUCAGC), Smad3-siRNA(CCUCAGUGACAGUGCUAUU/AAUAGCACUGUCACUGAGG), negative control (UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGU/ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAA) by using lipofectamine™ 2000, respectively (Invitrogen, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacture,s instructions. After 0 h, 24 h, 48 h and 72 h, the transfected cells were collected for CCK8 assay. After 24 h, the transfected cells were collected and processed for Transwell assay. After 48 h, the transfected cells were collected and processed for qPCR and Western blotting assay.

Construction of Flag-Smad2 and GFP-Smad3-overexpressing plasmid

Primers were designed based on the human Smad2 and Smad3 (GenBank: NM_001003652.4, NM_001145102.2) gene sequences in the NCBI database. The CDS region of Smad2 and Smad3 genes were amplified and inserted into pTriEx-flag and pTriEx-GFP vectors to construct overexpression plasmids. The primer sequences are listed as follows:

Smad2 Sense(5,−3,) ATGTCGTCCATCTTGCCATT,

Smad2 Anti-sense(5,−3,) TTATGACATGCTTGAGCAAC,

Smad3 Sense(5,−3,) ATGGAGCTGTGTGAGTTCGCC,

Smad3 Anti-sense(5,−3,) CTAAGACACACTGGAACAGCGG.

CCK8 assay

The cell viability was investigated using a cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay following the manufacturer’s protocol (MCE, China). Briefly, the cells (LLC and A549) were seeded onto 96-well microplates (5,000 cells/well in 100 µL medium) and cultured under standard conditions. Then, the cells were treated with DEX (1 µM). After treatment for 0 h, 24 h, 48 h and 72 h, 10µL of CCK8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 1 h. All experiments were performed in five replicates. The absorbance at OD450 was analyzed using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher, USA).

EdU assay

The cell proliferation was analyzed using EdU assay kits (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Briefly, LLC or A549 cells were cultured for 48 h under the following conditions: DEX (1 µM), Smad2/3 plasmid transfection, creatine (1 µM), DEX combined with Smad2/3 plasmid transfection or DEX combined with creatine, respectively. The sg-CTL and sg-GR A549 cells were cultured and treatment with DEX (0 and 1 µM) for 48 h. After treatment for 48 h, the cells were cultured in EdU solution-containing media for another 2 h. After washing with PBS, the cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 30 min, followed by washing with 0.3% PBST (PBS + 0.3% Triton-X100) for 15 min. Then, the click reaction mixture (incluidng Azide 488 or Azide 555) was added to each well and incubated for 30 min, followed by staining the nucleus with DAPI for 10 min. The cells were visualized and imaged under a Nikon fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Transwell assay

For the migration assay, the cells (LLC and A549) were seeded into the upper chamber of Transwell plate (1 × 104 cells/well in 100µL serum-free DMEM medium) (Corning life sciences, USA). DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% antibiotic-antimycotic, and DEX (0 and 1 µM) was added to the lower chambers for 24 h. Additionally, A549 cells were plated and transfected with Smad2/3-overexpressing plasmid prior to transwell assay. After 24 h, the cells were seeded into the upper chamber of Transwell plate and the lower chambers were exposed to DEX for an additional 24 h. Then the migrated cells were fixed for 30 min in 4% PFA, followed by staining with 0.05% crystal violet solution at room temperature for 30 min. The cells were visualized and imaged under a microscope (Leica, Germany).

Wound healing assay

The migration ability of cells was analyzed using a wound-healing assay. Briefly, the LLC and A549 cells were seeded and cultured into a 30-mm cell culture dish. When the cells confluency reached about 60%, the plate was scratched using a pipette tip and incubated with fresh 1% FBS medium with or without 1-µM DEX for 24 h in a humidified atmosphere and 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Additionally, the cells were seeded and cultured into a 30-mm dish. When the cell confluency reached 60%, the plate was scratched using a pipette tip and incubated with fresh 1% FBS medium with DEX (1 µM), creatine (1 µM), or their combination for 48 h in a humidified atmosphere and 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Moreover, the sg-CTL and sg-GR A549 cells were cultured and treatment with DEX (0 and 1 µM) for 48 h. The cells were imaged at 0 h, 24 h, or 48 h after scratching using a Leica inverted light microscope (Leica, Germany).

Bioinformatics analyses

The mRNA and protein expression levels of Smad2/3 as well as their correlations with overall survival or tumor, node, and metastasis (TNM) stages (T1-T4, N1-N3, M0-M1) in NSCLC tissues were assessed using the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database. The HPA database (https://www.proteinatlas.org) was used to explore the protein expression levels of Smad2/3 in normal human tissues and lung cancer tissues (Accessed: December 10, 2023). In order to investigate the correlations between Smad2/3 expression levels and clinical outcomes in LUAD patients, survival analyses were performed using the R packages ‘survival’ and ‘survminer’. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to visualize the survival differences with statistical significance determined using log-rank tests. Both the univariate and multivariate Cox regression models were used to evaluate prognostic correlations in the TCGA-LUAD cohort49. In order to explore the correlation between Smad2/3 levels and tumor progression stages, statistical analyses and data visualization were performed using the R statistical software (v4.4.0). The data distribution was visualized using boxplots generated with the ggboxplot() function from the ‘ggpubr’ package. Intergroup comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with statistical significance denoted by asterisks: *P < 0.05 and **P < 0.01.

Western blotting assay

The A549 cells were seeded and cultured into a 60-mm cell-culture dish, transfected with Smad2/3-overexpressing plasmid, and treated with DEX (0, 1µM). Additionally, the LLC and A549 cells were seeded and cultured into a 60-mm cell culture dish and treated with DEX (0 and 1 µM), Creatine (1 µM), or their combination. Moreover, the sg-CTL and sg-GR A549 cells were seeded and cultured into a 60-mm cell culture dish and treated with DEX (0 and 1 µM). After treatment for 48 h, the cells were lysed with 1% SDS lysis buffer (containing phosphatase and protease inhibitors). After heating at 95 °C for 10 min, the samples were centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 15 min at room temperature. Protein concentration was measured using BCA assay (Thermo Scientific, USA). The samples (20 µg) were separated using SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes (0.22 μm, Millipore, USA). Subsequently, the membranes were blocked with 5% fat-free milk in TBST at room temperature for 2 h and then incubated with primary antibodies at 4℃ overnight. The following antibodies were used: anti-Smad2 (catalog number #5339, 1:1500, CST, USA), anti-p-Smad2 (Ser465/467) (catalog number #18338, 1:1000, CST, USA), anti-Smad3 (catalog number #9523, 1:1500, CST, USA), anti-p-Smad3 (Ser423/425) (catalog number #9520, 1:1000, CST, USA), anti-GR (catalog number # 24050-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), anti-TGFβR1 (catalog number #AF5347, 1:800, Affinity, China), anti-p-TGFβR1(Ser165) (catalog number #AF8080, 1:800, Affinity, China), anti-Flag (catalog number #HT201-01, 1:1000, TransGen Biotech, China), anti-GFP (catalog number #50430-2-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), anti-β-actin (catalog number # HC201-01, TransGen Biotech, China) and anti-β-Tubulin (catalog number #10068-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China). After washing with TBST, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies (catalog number CW0103S, 1:2000, CWBIO, China) for 1 h. Finally, the detection was performed using ECL reagents (Smart Lifesciencess, China).

RNA isolation and qPCR assay

Total RNA was extracted form cells using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Then 2 µg of RNA were reverse transcribed into cDNA according to the manufacturer,s protocols (Thermo Scientific, USA). The mRNA level was detected by UltraSYBR Mixture (CWBIO, China) and normalized relative to the GAPDH mRNA levels. Human Primer sequences (5,−3,) used were:

Smad2 F: CGTCCATCTTGCCATTCACG, R: CTCAAGCTCATCTAATCGTCCTG.

Smad3 F: CCATCTCCTACTACGAGCTGAA, R: CACTGCTGCATTCCTGTTGAC.

GAPDH F: ACAACTTTGGTATCGTGGAAGG, R: GCCATCACGCCACAGTTTC

Mouse Primer sequences (5,−3,) used were:

Smad2 F: ATGTCGTCCATCTTGCCATTC, R: AACCGTCCTGTTTTCTTTAGCTT.

Smad3 F: CACGCAGAACGTGAACACC, R: GGCAGTAGATAACGTGAGGGA.

GAPDH F: TGGCCTTCCGTGTTCCTAC, R: GAGTTGCTGTTGAAGTCGCA

ELISA assay

The media were collected from LLC and A549 cells (4 × 105 cells/well) treatment with DEX (1µM) for 48 h. TGFβ1 (Mouse) and TGFβ1 (Human) levels were determined using ELISA kits according to the manufacturer,s protocols (Dumabio, Shanghai, China). The absorbance of each well was measured using a microplate reader at 450 nm (Thermo Fisher, USA).

Animal experiments

All the animal experiments were performed according to the institutional guideline for the use of laboratory animals and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Chongqing Medical University. All methods are reported in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. Furthermore, all methods of this study were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. For the tumor growth assay, a total of 16 six-week-old male nude mice were subcutaneously injected with A549 cells (1 × 10⁶ cells in 100 µL PBS) in their flanks. After 7 days, the mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 8 per group) and treated with 0.9% NaCl and DEX (10 mg/kg) every other day, respectively. The tumor sizes were measured using digital vernier calipers and the tumor volume was calculated using Eq: Tumor volume = (length) x (width) x (width)/2. After 21 days, the mice were anesthetized intraperitoneally with 1.25% tribromoethanol (Dowobio Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) at a dose of 0.2 ml/10 g. Then the tumors were collected post-euthanasia by cervical dislocation under anesthesia. For the lung metastasis experiment, 8 six-week-old male C57BL/6 mice were maintained as an untreated control group. Another 16 age-matching mice were intravenously injected with LLC cells (1 × 10⁶ cells in 100 µL PBS) via their tail veins to establish a pulmonary metastases model. The tumor-bearing mice were then randomly divided into two treatment groups (n = 8 per group), which were treated with 0.9% NaCl and DEX (10 mg/kg) every other day, respectively. After the treatments for 30 days, the tumor metastatic burden was analyzed post-euthanasia by cervical dislocation under anesthesia.

H&E staining and immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis

The freshly isolated lung tissues were immediately fixed in freshly prepared 4% PFA (Sangon Biotech, China). Tissues were embedded in paraffin and cut into 3-µm-thick sections, which were then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) solution (Sangon Biotech, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. For IHC analysis, the paraffine-embedded tumor and normal lung tissue sections were deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated gradually. Antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) in a microwave heating at 100 °C for 20 min. The samples were then incubated with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide in methanol for 30 min at room temperature to inhibit the endogenous peroxidase activity. Subsequently, 10% normal bovine serum was added to avert non-specific reactions, and the tissue sections were incubated at 4℃ for 12 h with primary antibodies against the following antibodies: anti-Ki67 (catalog number ab15580, 1:200, Abcam, UK), anti-PCNA (catalog number #13110, 1:200, CST, USA), anti-E-cadherin (catalog number #3195, 1:200, CST, USA), anti-Vimentin (catalog number 10366-1-AP, 1:200, Proteintech, China), anti-Smad2 (catalog number #5339, 1:200, CST, USA), anti-p-Smad2 (Ser465/467) (catalog number #18338, 1:100, CST, USA), anti-Smad3 (catalog number #9523, 1:200, CST, USA), anti-p-Smad3 (Ser423/425) (catalog number #9520, 1:100, CST, USA). After washing with PBS, the tissue sections were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibody (catalog number ZB-2301, 1:1000, ZSGB-BIO, China) at 37℃ for 2 h. IHC staining was visualized using DAB (catalog No. ZLI-9018, ZSGB-BIO, China), and the tissue sections were imaged using a DM4B microscope (Leica, Germany).

Statistical analysis

All data were statistically analyzed using Prism 7 (GraphPad Software, USA). All the experiments were performed in three independent replicates. The results were presented as mean ± SEM. Differences between the two groups were analyzed using the unpaired Student’s t-test. 2-way ANOVA and one-way ANOVA was performed and followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison tests. Significance is indicated by ns (non-significant, P > 0.05), *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 and ****P < 0.0001. Additionaly, the relative proliferation ability of cells in EdU assay, and the migration/invasion abilities of cells in wound healing and Transwell assays were quantified using Image J software (NIH, USA).

Conclusion

In conclusion, DEX suppresses the growth and migration of lung cancer by inhibiting Smad2/3 activation. DEX might be a valuable therapeutic agent, and the Smad2/3 signaling pathway might be its potential therapeutic target for inhibiting NSCLC cell growth and metastasis in clinical practice.

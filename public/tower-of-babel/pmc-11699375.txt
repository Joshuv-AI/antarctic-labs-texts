Practical treatment strategies and novel therapies in the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway in hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative (HR+/HER2−) advanced breast cancer
Authors: K. Fanucci, A. Giordano, T. Erick, S.M. Tolaney, S. Sammons
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Mutations in the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway occur in 30%-40% of patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative (HR+/HER2−) breast cancer. For most patients, endocrine therapy with a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitor is the first-line treatment. Recent studies indicate that adding inavolisib, a PI3Kα inhibitor, to palbociclib/fulvestrant benefits patients with endocrine-resistant HR+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer with a PIK3CA mutation. Alpelisib and capivasertib are both US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved in combination with fulvestrant in patients with endocrine-resistant HR+/HER2−, PIK3CA-mutant metastatic breast cancer, both with activity in the post-CDK4/6 setting. Capivasertib added to fulvestrant is the first AKT inhibitor to show a significant progression-free survival benefit with a trend for overall survival benefit and the only approved option for patients with phosphate and tensin homolog (PTEN) or AKT alterations. Toxicity profiles of all agents necessitate careful patient selection. Several mutant-selective and pan-mutant-selective novel inhibitors are under investigation with the potential to improve tolerability and efficacy.

Introduction

Hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative (HR+/HER2−) breast cancer (BC) is the most common subtype, accounting for ∼70% of BCs.1 Although most patients with HR+/HER2− BC are diagnosed in the early stages, there is a lifelong risk of metastatic recurrence.2 The current standard of care (SOC) first-line therapy for patients with HR+/HER2− metastatic BC (MBC) is endocrine therapy (ET) in combination with a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitor (CDK4/6i), regardless of mutational status. A standard second-line treatment for patients who have developed resistance to ET with CDK4/6i has not been established. Rather, second- and later-line therapies depend on actionable genomic alterations.3 Currently, many approved and experimental therapies target alterations in the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway.

The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is an important regulator of cell growth, metabolism, and proliferation.4 Genomic alterations that result in hyperactivation of this pathway are common in patients with HR+/HER2− MBC and can contribute to endocrine resistance through a variety of mechanisms and have been shown to be a negative prognostic indicator.5, 6, 7, 8 Blocking PI3K in HR+ BC triggers upregulation of estrogen receptor (ER)-dependent transcription that can be reversed with ER-targeting therapies. Thus simultaneous blockade of PI3K and ER may be needed for optimal treatment of HR+ BCs with aberrant activation of the PI3K pathway.9 Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA) mutations (PIK3CAmts) are the most common pathway alterations, and are found in 30%-40% of patients with HR+/HER2− MBC.10, 11, 12, 13 PIK3CA encodes the p110a subunit of PI3K. More than 80% of somatic mutations in PIK3CA occur in the kinase and helical domains of the gene, with gain-of-function hotspot mutations in E542K and E545K (helical) and H1047R (kinase) resulting in increased tumor survival and growth.14 PIK3CA alterations are generally early mutational events found in the primary tumor, though rarely can be acquired during metastatic progression.15 In a meta-analysis of 25 studies reporting PIK3CA mutational status in primary breast tumors and matched metastases, PIK3CA mutational status was discordant in 9.8% of cases.16

Additional PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway alterations in HR+/HER2− BC include activating mutations in AKT1 and inactivating mutations in phosphate and tensin homolog (PTEN).10,17,18 Approximately 7% of HR+/HER2− BCs have activating mutations in AKT1.19,20 Among AKT1 mutations, E17K mutation is the most common alteration (∼80%) and constitutively activates AKT signaling.21, 22, 23, 24 PTEN is a negative regulator of the PI3K pathway. Its loss can activate downstream kinase (AKT/mTOR) signaling and may contribute to ET resistance.25, 26, 27

In the PALOMA-3 (NCT01942135) trial of palbociclib plus fulvestrant versus placebo plus fulvestrant, the median PFS (mPFS) among patients with PIK3CAmts was 10.9 months in the palbociclib plus fulvestrant arm versus 3.5 months in the placebo plus fulvestrant arm (hazard ratio 0.55, 95% CI 0.31-0.99).13,28 In correlative analyses of the MONARCH 2 trial (NCT02107703), which evaluated the efficacy of abemaciclib plus fulvestrant versus placebo plus fulvestrant, the mPFS in patients with PIK3CAmts was 17.1 months on abemaciclib versus 5.7 months on placebo (hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.33-0.84). In patients randomized to placebo plus fulvestrant, the mPFS was numerically shorter for patients with PIK3CAmt (5.7 months) compared with those without PIK3CAmt (12.3 months), while the mPFS was similar for patients randomized to the abemaciclib plus fulvestrant arm regardless of whether their tumors were PIK3CAmt (17.1 months) or PIK3CA wild-type (16.9 months).29 In the MONALEESA-3 trial (NCT02422615) of ribociclib plus fulvestrant versus placebo plus fulvestrant, the mPFS was numerically improved for patients with PIK3CA-mutant tumors who were randomized to ribociclib plus fulvestrant (16.4 months) versus placebo plus fulvestrant (11.1 months), although this difference was not statistically significant (hazard ratio 0.75, 95% CI 0.52-1.08).30 We prefer ribociclib or abemaciclib added to fulvestrant for endocrine-resistant HR+/HER2− advanced BC (ABC) with or without PIK3CAmt due to overall survival (OS) benefit and longer PFS over palbociclib in pivotal trials. Although HR+/HER2− MBC patients with PIK3CAmts receive benefit from ET plus CDK4/6i, their prognosis appears to be worse than patients with PIK3CA wild-type disease (Table 1).

Currently, SOC first-line treatment for PIK3CA-mutant HR+/HER2− MBC is ET with a CDK4/6i. However, the INAVO120 trial (NCT04191499) recently showed promising efficacy of the addition of first-line PI3K inhibitor (PI3Ki) inavolisib to palbociclib and fulvestrant in endocrine-resistant patients with metastatic progression or within 12 months of adjuvant ET. This strategy was approved in October 2024 by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for patients with endocrine-resistant, PIK3CA-mutated, HR+/HER2− ABC or MBC31 and is awaiting registrational approval by the European Commission (EC).32 Approximately 20%-40% of patients with HR+/HER2− MBC have endocrine-resistant disease at the time of initiation of first-line metastatic treatment; therefore a small proportion of patients with PIK3CA-mutant disease could benefit from this upfront strategy.33

Second-line therapies for HR+/HER2− MBC with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations

After progression on ET plus CDK4/6i, there are several second-line options for patients with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations, including alpelisib, capivasertib, and everolimus.

PI3K inhibitors

Alpelisib

Alpelisib is an allosteric small-molecule phosphatidulinositol-3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitor with specific activity against PI3Kα34 and is first to market. Data informing the approval of alpelisib are derived from the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter phase III SOLAR-1 trial (NCT02437318), which enrolled 572 patients with HR+/HER2− ABC. Patients with PIK3CA-wild-type and PIK3CA-mutant MBC were enrolled into separate cohorts; 341 patients had confirmed PIK3CAmts in tumor tissue (Table 2). PIK3CAmt analysis was carried out with real-time PCR assays, the QIAGEN therascreen PIK3CA RGQ PCR kit, and a Novartis clinical trial assay. Eligible patients were men or postmenopausal women with relapse or progression of their disease with prior exposure to aromatase inhibitor (AI) therapy for treatment of either localized or advanced disease. Patients were ineligible if they had received prior fulvestrant or chemotherapy for ABC. Alpelisib reduced the risk of PFS event by 35% in the cohort with PIK3CA-mutated cancer (hazard ratio 0.65, 95% CI 0.50-0.85; P < 0.001). The mPFS was 11.0 months in the alpelisib plus fulvestrant group versus 5.7 months in the placebo plus fulvestrant group. There was no significant difference in PFS between treatment arms in the cohort without PIK3CAmts (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.58-1.25). Only 6% of patients in the PIK3CA-mutated cohort received prior treatment with a CDK4/6i. In the PIK3CA-mutated cohort, the median OS (mOS) was 39.3 months in the alpelisib plus fulvestrant arm and 31.4 months in the placebo plus fulvestrant arm (hazard ratio 0.86, 95% CI 0.64-1.15; P = 0.15).35 The final OS analysis included a subgroup analysis of patients with lung and/or liver metastases. In this subgroup, the mOS was 37.2 months (95% CI 28.7-43.6) in the alpelisib plus fulvestrant arm versus 22.8 months (95% CI 19.0-26.8) in the placebo plus fulvestrant arm (hazard ratio 0.68, 95% CI 0.46-1.00).36 Based on these results, the FDA granted approval in 2019 and the EC granted approval in 2020 for alpelisib plus fulvestrant for postmenopausal women and men with HR+/HER2−, PIK3CA-mutated ABC.

The BYLieve trial (NCT03056755) evaluated the efficacy of alpelisib plus ET in patients with PIK3CA-mutated HR+/HER2− ABC whose disease had progressed after CDK4/6i. The study evaluated patients whose immediate prior therapy was a CDK4/6i plus AI (cohort A), CDK4/6i plus fulvestrant (cohort B), or chemotherapy or ET (cohort C). In cohort C, 32.5% of patients had prior fulvestrant exposure.37 In cohort A, patients received alpelisib plus fulvestrant. The mPFS was 8.0 months and the mOS was 27.3 months. Patients in cohort B received alpelisib plus letrozole, with an mPFS of 5.6 months and mOS of 29.0 months. Patients in cohort C received alpelisib plus fulvestrant, with an mPFS of 5.6 months and mOS of 20.7 months. Patients in cohorts A and B had numerically longer mOS if they were treated with the alpelisib combination in the second rather than third line.38,39 Biomarker analyses of paired circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) samples at baseline and end of treatment (EOT) showed an increase in loss-of-function mutations in PTEN from 9% at baseline to 14% at EOT in cohort A and 12% at baseline to 22% at EOT in cohort B, indicating PTEN mutation as a potential mechanism of resistance to alpelisib.40 ctDNA from patients treated in a phase I/II trial of alpelisib plus AI revealed loss-of-function PTEN mutations were present in 25% of patients with resistance to the regimen, suggesting that patients with concurrent PTEN alterations do not respond as well to PI3Ki.41

Ongoing trials in HR+ BC are evaluating the role of alpelisib in combination with sacituzumab govitecan (NCT05143229), fulvestrant and dapagliflozin (NCT05025735), and ribociclib and letrozole (NCT01872260). Enrollment in a prior phase Ib study of the combination of alpelisib, ribociclib, and fulvestrant was stopped due to unexpected toxicity.42 The phase Ib portion of the ELEVATE trial (NCT05563220) to evaluate the safety and determine the recommended phase II dose of elacestrant in combination with either alpelisib, everolimus, ribociclib, palbociclib, or abemaciclib is ongoing for the combination of alpelisib and elacestrant.43

The toxicity of alpelisib related to the inhibition of wild-type PI3Kα is one limitation of widespread use. Patients often experience hyperglycemia, rash, diarrhea, and appetite changes. In the SOLAR-1 trial, all-grade toxicities included hyperglycemia (63.7% in the alpelisib group versus 9.8% in the placebo group), diarrhea (57.7% versus 15.7%), nausea (44.7% versus 22.3%), decreased appetite (35.6% versus 10.5%), rash (35.6% versus 5.9%), and maculopapular rash (14.1% versus 1.7%). Grade 3/4 adverse events (AEs) included hyperglycemia (36.6% versus 0.7%), rash (9.9% versus 0.3%), maculopapular rash (8.8% versus 0.3%), and diarrhea (6.7% versus 0.3%). Permanent discontinuation of alpelisib or placebo due to AEs occurred in 25.0% of patients in the alpelisib group and 4.2% in the placebo group.35 Zyrtec and metformin prophylaxis have dramatically reduced the rates of high-grade rash and hyperglycemia.44,45 The phase II, single-arm METALLICA trial (NCT04300790) enrolled patients with normoglycemia [hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) < 5.7%] or prediabetes (HbA1c 5.7%-6.4%) and started prophylactic metformin with ET before the initiation of alpelisib; 1 of 48 (2.1%) patients with normoglycemia before enrollment and 3/20 (15%) of patients with prediabetes at the time of enrollment had grade 3-4 hyperglycemia. None of the patients discontinued alpelisib due to hyperglycemia AE.46

Everolimus

Everolimus is an inhibitor of the mTOR. Data informing the approval of everolimus in HR+/HER2− ABC are based on the BOLERO-2 trial (NCT00863655). In this randomized phase III, double-blind trial, 724 postmenopausal women with recurrence or progression on prior nonsteroidal AI (NSAI) were randomized to receive everolimus plus exemestane versus placebo plus exemestane. Everolimus reduced the risk of PFS event by 55% (hazard ratio 0.45, 95% CI 0.38-0.54; P < 0.0001) with an mPFS of 7.8 months in the everolimus plus exemestane arm versus 3.2 months in the placebo plus exemestane arm (Table 2).47,48 The mOS was 31.0 months in the everolimus plus exemestane arm versus 26.6 months in the placebo plus exemestane arm (hazard ratio 0.89, 95% CI 0.73-1.10; P = 0.14).49 Based on these results, the FDA and EC granted approval for everolimus plus exemestane in postmenopausal patients with HR+/HER2− ABC after progression on NSAI. In the ctDNA analysis of BOLERO-2, everolimus also prolonged the mPFS in patients with PIK3CA H1047R (hazard ratio 0.37) and E545K/E542K mutations (hazard ratio 0.30) with similar magnitude.50

The PrE0102 study was a randomized phase II trial of fulvestrant plus everolimus versus fulvestrant plus placebo that enrolled 131 postmenopausal women with HR+/HER2− MBC who had experienced disease progression on AI. Everolimus plus fulvestrant significantly improved the mPFS compared with placebo plus fulvestrant (10.3 versus 5.1 months; hazard ratio 0.61, 95% CI 0.40-0.92; stratified log-rank P = 0.02).51 The randomized, open-label, phase II MANTA trial (NCT02216786) included 333 patients with HR+ MBC who had progressed on AI. Participants were randomized to receive fulvestrant, fulvestrant plus vistusertib (a dual inhibitor of mTOR complex 1 and 2) daily, fulvestrant plus vistusertib intermittently, or fulvestrant plus everolimus. The mPFS in the fulvestrant plus everolimus arm was the longest at 12.3 months, compared with 5.4 months in the fulvestrant monotherapy arm (hazard ratio 0.63, 95% CI 0.42-0.92; log-rank P = 0.01).52 Based on the results of these trials, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network and the European Society for Medical Oncology guidelines recognized everolimus plus fulvestrant as appropriate second-line treatment in this population.

None of the patients in the BOLERO-2, PrE0102, or MANTA trials had received a CDK4/6i before enrollment, and no randomized trials have been conducted to evaluate response to everolimus plus ET following progression on a CDK4/6i. Thus a prospective observational study was conducted in patients with ER+/HER2− MBC who had received treatment with a CDK4/6i and were receiving everolimus plus fulvestrant as second-line therapy. The investigators enrolled 57 patients with a median of one prior line of therapy in the metastatic setting. The mPFS was 6.9 months and the mOS was 38.3 months. In the subgroup with PIK3CAmts, the mPFS was 3.1 months and the mOS was not reached.53 In the phase Ib/II ELEVATE trial, the phase Ib portion enrolled 23 patients with prior treatment with CDK4/6i to receive everolimus plus elacestrant. In the seven patients enrolled at the recommended phase II dose (everolimus 7.5 mg and elacestrant 345 mg daily), the majority of AEs were grade 1 or 2. Grade 3 stomatitis was reported in one patient. The clinical benefit rate was 85% in 13 assessable patients. A phase II study of everolimus plus elacestrant is ongoing.43

The most frequent all-grade toxicities associated with everolimus plus exemestane are stomatitis (56%), rash (36%), fatigue (33%), hyperglycemia (23%), diarrhea (30%), and decreased appetite (29%). Grade 3/4 AEs include hyperglycemia (4%), rash (8%), anemia (6%), dyspnea (4%), and pneumonitis (3%). In the BOLERO-2 trial, permanent discontinuation of everolimus or placebo due to AEs occurred in 19% of patients receiving combination therapy.47 Prophylactic dexamethasone oral solution reduces the rate of oral stomatitis and should be prescribed prophylactically to patients starting an everolimus-containing regimen.50

Given the limited data for everolimus in PIK3CA-altered patients following CDK4/6i treatment, we prefer PI3K or AKT inhibition over everolimus and reserve everolimus plus fulvestrant for wild-type patients. However, in parts of the world where these inhibitors are not available, everolimus plus fulvestrant remains a viable option.

Capivasertib

Capivasertib is a small-molecule inhibitor of all three isoforms of AKT54 that is FDA and EC approved in combination with fulvestrant for patients with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-altered HR+/HER2− MBC with disease progression on one or more prior line of ET in the metastatic setting (or within 12 months of completing adjuvant ET), and who have at least one alteration as detected by an FDA- or EC-approved companion diagnostic assay.

Data informing this approval are based on the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter phase III CAPItello-291 trial (NCT04305496), which enrolled 708 patients with HR+/HER2− ABC, 289 of whom had tumors with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations (Table 2). Participants were randomized to receive capivasertib plus fulvestrant or placebo plus fulvestrant. AKT pathway alteration was assessed with FoundationOne CDx assay. All patients had progressed on prior AI-based treatment and were eligible if they had received two or fewer prior lines of ET and one or fewer lines of chemotherapy for ABC. Capivasertib reduced the risk of a PFS event by 40% in the overall population and 50% in the AKT pathway-altered population. The absolute benefit in these populations was modest—the mPFS with capivasertib plus fulvestrant versus placebo plus fulvestrant was 7.2 months versus 3.6 months in the overall population (hazard ratio 0.60, 95% CI 0.51-0.71; P < 0.001) and 7.3 months versus 3.1 months in the AKT pathway-altered population (hazard ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.38-0.65; P < 0.001).46 The mPFS in patients with PIK3CA-altered tumors was 5.6 months versus 2.1 months (hazard ratio 0.51, 95% CI 0.37-0.70); in patients with AKT1-altered tumors, the mPFS was 9.1 months versus 3.7 months (hazard ratio 0.51, 95% CI 0.22-1.12); and in patients with PTEN-altered tumors, it was 9.1 months versus 3.6 months (hazard ratio 0.43, 95% CI 0.21-0.88). Patients with liver metastases had numerically shorter PFS regardless of the treatment arm they were randomized to (mPFS 3.8 months versus 1.9 months; hazard ratio 0.61, 95% CI 0.48-0.78), meriting caution when considering capivasertib as treatment in this higher risk population.55 Sixty-nine percent of patients in this trial received a CDK4/6i before enrollment. The mPFS for patients with prior CDK4/6i exposure was shorter regardless of the treatment arm (5.5 months for capivasertib versus 2.6 months for placebo; hazard ratio 0.59, 95% CI 0.48-0.72) when compared with those without prior CKD4/6i exposure (10.9 months for capivasertib versus 7.2 months for placebo; hazard ratio 0.64, 95% CI 0.45-0.90). While OS data are not yet mature, the interim analysis showed an HR for death of 0.74 (95% CI 0.56-0.98) in the overall population and 0.69 (95% CI 0.45-1.05) in the AKT pathway-altered population.56

The efficacy of capivasertib in patients without AKT pathway alterations was limited, with a hazard ratio of 0.79 (95% CI 0.61-1.02). This lack of benefit for patients with nonaltered AKT pathway, along with the finding that the PFS benefit in the overall population was primarily driven by the AKT pathway-altered subgroup, may have led the FDA and EC to limit the approval of capivasertib plus fulvestrant to patients with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-altered ABC.56

These data provide further evidence of the utility of assessing patients’ tumors for PIK3CA, AKT1, and PTEN alterations in standard clinical practice. Along with the approval of capivasertib with fulvestrant, the FDA approved the FoundationOne CDx assay as a companion diagnostic device to identify patients who may benefit from capivasertib. Trials are ongoing to investigate the role of capivasertib as a first-line treatment for HR+/HER2− MBC. CAPItello-292 is a phase Ib/III trial to evaluate the safety and efficacy of capivasertib plus CDK4/6i (palbociclib or ribociclib) and fulvestrant in patients with HR+/HER2− ABC who have not received prior ET for advanced disease (NCT04862663).57 Among patients with measurable disease at baseline, the objective response rate was 62.5% (95% CI 24.5-91.5) and the clinical benefit rate at 24 weeks was 53.8% (95% CI 25.1-80.8).58

Additional trials investigating the safety and efficacy of capivasertib in combination with other antineoplastic agents have been completed or are underway. The phase I SERENA-1 trial (NCT03616587) evaluated the safety of the combination of capivasertib and the oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) camizestrant in patients with HR+ MBC with no limit on prior lines of ET. Forty-eight percent of patients had received prior chemotherapy. The safety and tolerability of the combination was similar to each drug individually, with the most common AEs of any grade being diarrhea (66%), visual effects (62%), bradycardia (34%), nausea (34%), and fatigue (21%). The mPFS was 8.3 months.59 The planned SERENA-8 trial will investigate camizestrant plus capivasertib versus physician’s choice SOC ET/ET plus PI3K/AKT/mTOR-targeting agent. The planned primary endpoints are PFS in the overall, PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN-altered, PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN-nonaltered, and ESR1-mutant populations. A trial randomizing premenopausal patients with HR+ MBC who have received two or fewer prior lines of ET for their metastatic disease into four arms containing goserelin/fulvestrant +/− capivasertib +/− durvalumab is ongoing (NCT05720260) and the phase Ib/II ELEVATE trial evaluating capivasertib plus elacestrant following progression on a CDK4/6i-containing regimen is currently enrolling (NCT05563220).

Common AEs of any grade on capivasertib were diarrhea (72.4%), rash (38.0%), and nausea (34.6%). Hyperglycemia of any grade occurred in 16.3% of the patients who received capivasertib–fulvestrant, which is substantially lower than alpelisib while allowing patients with HgA1C up to 8 to enroll. It is worthwhile to note that earlier trials such as SOLAR-1 were done using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 4.0 to classify AEs, which defined grade 3 hyperglycemia as glucose 251-500 mg/dl or hospitalization required and grade 4 as glucose >500 mg/dl with life-threatening consequences. The CAPItello-291 trial used CTCAE version 5.0, which removed the fixed glucose cut-offs and defined grade 3 hyperglycemia as insulin therapy initiated or hospitalization indicated and grade 4 as a life-threatening consequence or urgent intervention indicated.60,61 Given this update, it is likely that some of the lower rates of hyperglycemia observed in CAPItello-291 may be attributed to the change in definition between CTCAE versions. Inavolisib

Inavolisib is an ATP-competitive PI3Kα inhibitor that also induces selective degradation of mutant p110α. In preclinical models, inavolisib demonstrated 300-fold selectivity for PI3Kα over the other class I PI3K isoforms.62 Efficacy and safety of inavolisib were first explored in the open-label, phase I/Ib dose-escalation study of inavolisib alone or with endocrine or targeted therapies (NCT03006172). Of the 55 patients enrolled in arm D (inavolisib plus fulvestrant in PIK3CA-mutated, HR+/HER2− MBC), most had received two or more prior lines of therapy for MBC, including a CDK4/6i. In addition, 35% had received chemotherapy in the metastatic setting and half had received fulvestrant. The median inavolisib treatment duration was 5.3 months (range 0.2-31.9 months). The pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics were favorable and target inhibition was validated by decreased PIK3CAmt allele frequency in ctDNA.63

INAVO120 was the registrational phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of inavolisib versus placebo plus palbociclib and fulvestrant in patients with PIK3CAmt, HR+/HER2− ABC (Table 2).32 Eligible patients had disease progression during or within 12 months of adjuvant ET and had not received systemic therapy for MBC. Patients were required to have a fasting blood glucose level <126 mg/dl and an HbA1c < 6%. A total of 325 patients were randomized 1 : 1 to the triplet regimen of inavolisib plus palbociclib and fulvestrant or placebo plus palbociclib and fulvestrant until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The addition of inavolisib improved mPFS from 7.3 months with palbociclib plus fulvestrant to 15 months with the triplet (P < 0.0001). At the interim analysis, the mOS was not reached in the inavolisib arm and was 31.1 months in the placebo arm (P = 0.0338 in favor of inavolisib), trending toward early significance but also validating the poor prognosis of the enrolled population. The safety of the triplet was comparable to the combined toxicity of each individual agent. The FDA approved this triplet in October 2024 and EC approval is anticipated for this high-risk, treatment-refractory population.

INAVO121 (NCT05646862) is an ongoing randomized phase III trial enrolling patients with HR+/HER2− PIK3CA-mutant ABC that has progressed after CDK4/6i and who have received two or fewer lines of therapy in the advanced setting. Participants are randomized to inavolisib plus fulvestrant versus alpelisib plus fulvestrant.64

In INAVO120 the primary all-grade toxicities associated with inavolisib were hyperglycemia (59% in the inavolisib arm versus 9% control arm), stomatitis (51% versus 27%), diarrhea (48% versus 16%), and rash (25% versus 17%). Grade 3/4 AEs of interest were hyperglycemia (6% of patients on the inavolisib arm versus 0% in the control arm), stomatitis (6% versus 0%), and diarrhea (4% versus 0%). Low-grade ocular toxicity, a concern from preclinical research, was noted in 22.2% of patients in the inavolisib group versus 13% in the control arm.

The population who may benefit from this first-line approval is limited to those with PIK3CAmts, first-line endocrine-resistant disease, HgA1C < 6, and no prior CDK4/6i. It is unclear if a triplet strategy will have improved OS and quality of life compared with sequential use of CDK4/6i and PIK3CA or AKT inhibition.

Investigational therapies for HR+/HER2− MBC with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations

Gedatolisib

Gedatolisib, an investigational pan-class I isoform PI3K and mTOR inhibitor, exhibits low nanomolar potency against p110α, p110β, p110γ, and p110δ isoforms; mTORC1; and mTORC2. Its unique mechanism of action and pharmacokinetic properties differentiate it from currently approved and investigational therapies. Unique among the other agents discussed in this review which are given by mouth, gedatolisib is administered intravenously.65

In a phase Ib study, gedatolisib was administered with palbociclib and fulvestrant in patients with HR+/HER2− ABC. The mPFS was 12.9 months in patients with prior CDK4/6i treatment. Notably, efficacy was observed irrespective of PIK3CA mutation status. For treatment-naïve patients in escalation arm A (n = 11) and expansion arm A (n = 30), the mPFS was 45.8 and 48.6 months, respectively. When treatment-naïve patients from both arms were combined (n = 41), the mPFS was 48.6 months, and the median duration of response was 46.9 months.66

Gedatolisib had a 4% discontinuation rate due to treatment-related AEs. Stomatitis was the most common (89%). Hyperglycemia occurred in 25% of patients.66 In previous clinical trials in endometrial cancer, the most common AEs associated with gedatolisib were nausea (53%), mucosal inflammation (50%), decreased appetite (40%), diarrhea (38%), fatigue (35%), and dysgeusia and vomiting (each 30%).67 The phase III VIKTORIA-1 trial (NCT05501886) will evaluate gedatolisib with fulvestrant +/− palbociclib in HR+/HER2− ABC previously treated with any CDK4/6i plus NSAI. Patients without PIK3CAmts will be enrolled in study 1 and randomized to treatment arm A (gedatolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant), arm B (gedatolisib and fulvestrant), or arm C (fulvestrant). Patients with PIK3CAmt will be enrolled to study 2 and randomized to treatment arm D (gedatolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant), arm E (alpelisib and fulvestrant), or arm F (gedatolisib and fulvestrant).68

Mutant-selective inhibitors

RELAY 2608 (RLY-2608) represents a first-in-class allosteric mutant-selective inhibitor targeting PI3Kα. This distinguishes RLY-2608 from orthosteric inhibitors that lack mutant selectivity and inadvertently inhibit wild-type PI3Kα.69 Preclinically, mutant-selective inhibitors spare wild-type receptors avoiding hyperglycemia. RLY-2608 is currently being evaluated as monotherapy and in combination in the dose-escalation phase of the first-in-human ReDiscover trial. The trial enrolled 19 patients with unresectable or metastatic solid tumors harboring PI3Kα mutations in the monotherapy arm, and 23 patients with PI3Kα-mutant, HR+/HER2− ABC in the combination arm. Among 27 patients with BC (4 monotherapy + 23 combination), distinct mutation types were observed, including 12 patients with kinase mutations, 10 with helical mutations, and 9 with other mutations.70

Notably, RLY-2608 exhibited limited impact on glucose homeostasis, with no grade 3 hyperglycemia observed. The decline of PI3Kα-mutant ctDNA from patient samples provided initial clinical validation, affirming the ability of RLY-2608 to selectively inhibit a wide range of PI3Kα mutations in a dose-dependent manner. Among the 16 patients with BC with measurable disease, 9 experienced radiographic tumor reductions, 12 exhibited stable disease, and 1 achieved a partial response. The median duration of treatment for patients with BC was ∼4 months. Ongoing dose exploration and combinations with CDK4/6i are underway.70

STX-478, a second-generation, allosteric, mutant-selective PI3Kα inhibitor, binds to a newly characterized allosteric pocket within PI3Kα. STX-478 selectively inhibited PI3K signaling activity in cultured tumor cells harboring PI3Kα kinase-domain mutations and exhibited robust antiproliferative activity against cell lines with helical and kinase domain mutations. Compared with high-dose alpelisib, STX-478 demonstrated similar or greater antitumor efficacy without causing insulin resistance or other signs of metabolic dysfunction in vivo.71 STX-478 is currently being evaluated in humans with PI3Kα mutation in a phase I study (NCT05768139). Trial enrollment is ongoing. Analysis of the first 61 patients, 29 of whom had HR+/HER2− BC has been presented.72 Fifty-two percent of patients had diabetes/prediabetes. Hyperglycemia was noted in 23% of patients, all grades 1-2. Objective response was observed in 5/22 (23%) of assessable patients with BC with a disease control rate of 70% across tumor types.

LOXO-783 is an allosteric, mutant-selective PI3Kα inhibitor with high selectivity for PI3Kα H1047R over wild-type PI3Kα and other PI3K isoforms. In vivo, LOXO-783 exhibited robust antitumor activity against ER+/HER2− PI3Kα H1047R-mutant BC models. Moreover, LOXO-783 is highly brain-penetrant, as demonstrated by dose-dependent inhibition of tumor growth in brain metastasis models. Single-agent LOXO-783 treatment did not cause hyperglycemia and LOXO-783 showed preclinical efficacy in combination with SOC therapies (fulvestrant; abemaciclib; abemaciclib plus fulvestrant) or investigational agents (imlunestrant; abemaciclib plus imlunestrant). LOXO-783 is currently in clinical development in the phase I PIKASSO-01 trial (NCT05307705) as monotherapy and in combination with other anticancer therapies.73

TOS-358 is a highly selective first-in-class covalent inhibitor of PI3Kα. In multiple cell-derived xenografts and patient-derived xenograft models, TOS-358 demonstrated superior efficacy compared with reversible PI3Kα inhibitors (ATP competitive and allosteric). In metabolic studies, TOS-358 did not cause significant hyperglycemia in mice, rats, or dogs. TOS-358 is currently in clinical development in a phase I trial that is enrolling patients with several different types of PI3Kα-mutated solid tumors (NCT05683418).74 This highly specific and potent covalent inhibitor of PI3Kα could lead to superior efficacy with an improved safety profile.

Treatment of patients with co-occurrence of AKT pathway alteration and ESR1 mutation

While a comprehensive discussion of SERDs is beyond the scope of this review, elacestrant, the first FDA- and EC-approved oral SERD, is another ET that is approved for use in a similar population of patients with ER+/HER2− advanced or MBC after progression on at least one line of ET in the metastatic setting. Elacestrant is approved only for patients whose tumors harbor an ESR1 mutation. Co-occurring PIK3CA and ESR1 mutations have been found in 15%-30% of patients with ER+/HER2− MBC.29,75 In patients with both AKT pathway alteration and ESR1 mutation in whom additional endocrine-based therapy is desired, providers have the choice between treatment with either capivasertib plus fulvestrant or elacestrant monotherapy. No trials exist comparing these agents head-to-head but exploratory analysis of the EMERALD trial (NCT03778931), upon which the approval of elacestrant is based, provides further evidence of the higher-risk behavior of tumors with PIK3CA mutation.

The EMERALD phase III randomized trial enrolled patients with ER+/HER2− ABC who had progressed on one or two lines of prior ET including one in combination with CDK4/6i. Patients were allowed up to one line of prior chemotherapy in the metastatic setting. Patients were randomized to receive either elacestrant or SOC endocrine monotherapy (fulvestrant or AI). The primary endpoint of PFS in the ESR1-mutant population found an mPFS of 3.8 months in patients randomized to elacestrant versus 1.9 months in patients randomized to SOC (hazard ratio 0.55, 95% CI 0.39-0.77). The most common all-grade AEs were nausea (35% in the elacestrant arm versus 19% SOC), fatigue (19% in both arms), and vomiting (19% versus 8%). Grade 3-4 AEs were nausea (3% versus 1%), back pain (3% versus 0.4%), and alanine transaminase increase (2% versus 0.4%).76

To identify patients whose disease may remain endocrine sensitive after progression on ET, and therefore derive more benefit from endocrine-based treatment, the EMERALD authors carried out an exploratory analysis of patients who were previously treated with CDK4/6i + ET ≥ 12 months and found in this subpopulation mPFS was 8.6 months versus 1.9 months (hazard ratio 0.41, 95% CI 0.26-0.63). A further subanalysis of this population with CDK4/6i + ET ≥ 12 months showed the fairly consistent benefit of elacestrant over SOC in many subgroups regardless of metastatic location, TP53 mutation, HER2-low expression, and ESR1 mutation subtype, but patients with co-occurring PIK3CA mutation treated with elacestrant had a numerically shorter mPFS of 5.5 months.77 Outcomes for patients with ESR1 mutation in the CAPItello-291 trial have not been published.

In patients with co-occurring AKT pathway alteration and ESR1 mutation whose disease exhibits more indolent behavior as measured by prior CDK4/6i ≥ 12 months, it is reasonable to select elacestrant as second-line therapy given improved AE profile and similar reported efficacy compared with capivasertib plus fulvestrant, but in general for patients with these co-occurring mutations we favor capivasertib plus fulvestrant over elacestrant in the second line given improved efficacy in this higher-risk group.

Toxicity concerns of PIK3CA/mTOR/AKT inhibitors

Inhibitors of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway have different toxicity profiles, which are important to consider when speaking to patients about which therapy to choose. Toxicities of this pathway have proven challenging for both patients and providers. The risk of hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia associated with orthotopic PI3Ki and mTOR/AKT inhibitors is a significant consideration for patient selection and adherence. PI3Ki-induced hyperglycemia is recognized as an on-target effect, stemming from the inhibition of the PI3K pathway, which leads to decreased glucose transport, increased glycogenolysis, and gluconeogenesis.78 This effect triggers a compensatory increase in insulin release, potentially compromising treatment efficacy by reactivating the PI3K pathway, creating a complex interplay in the metabolic response.78 Patients with insulin deficiencies, insulin resistance, or pancreatic dysfunction may experience exacerbated or prolonged hyperglycemia and patients with diabetes or prediabetes were excluded from pivotal clinical trials of these agents.

Diverse HbA1C criteria were used for different trials and should be considered when selecting an agent. Newer mutant selective PI3Kis are expected to have less hyperglycemia. For patients with any risk factor for hyperglycemia (including prediabetes and obesity), capivasertib is the preferred pathway inhibitor.

Stomatitis, characterized by inflammation of the oral mucosa, represents a significant challenge in cancer therapies, particularly AKT and mTOR inhibitors. This condition manifests as painful oral ulcers and lesions, impairing patients’ ability to eat and maintain oral hygiene. The phase II SWISH prevention study assessed the efficacy of alcohol-free dexamethasone oral solution four times daily for 8 weeks in preventing stomatitis associated with everolimus therapy.79 Patients with HR+/HER2-negative MBC received everolimus and exemestane alongside dexamethasone mouthwash for 8 weeks, with the option for continued use for up to 8 additional weeks. The incidence of grade ≥2 stomatitis by 8 weeks was meaningfully lower (2%) compared with historical controls (33%). Although most patients experienced AEs, prophylactic dexamethasone oral solution substantially reduced stomatitis severity. We recommend prophylactic mouthwash with everolimus and upon any grade stomatitis with other pathway inhibitors.

A maculopapular rash is also very common in patients receiving either alpelisib or capivasertib. The rash is associated with increased blood eosinophils with alpelisib and histamine production.80,81 In SOLAR-1, patients who received rash prophylaxis (most often antihistamines) had a lower incidence of all-grade and grade 3 rash compared with the overall population.82 For this reason, prophylactic nonsedating antihistamines such as cetirizine are recommended for all patients starting alpelisib. We suggest consideration of antihistamine prophylaxis in patients initiating capivasertib as well given the low-risk nature of this intervention and rather high incidence of rash in CAPItello-291.

Choosing the right path for PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN-altered HR+/HER2− MBC

Choosing between regimens containing capivasertib, inavolisib, and alpelisib will be driven by inclusion criteria in pivotal trials and side-effect profiles (Table 2). Most patients will receive first-line CDK4/6i with ET. As inavolisib has gained FDA approval, next-generation sequencing in the first-line setting for patients with endocrine-resistant disease is recommended. Currently, many physicians reserve testing until the post-CDK4/6i setting where decision making is dependent on biomarkers. Select patients with PIK3CAmt, first-line endocrine resistance, and an HbA1C < 6 could be considered for first-line triplet therapy with inavolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant. The enhanced toxicity of the triplet should be discussed with patients as well as the option for treatment with sequential pathway inhibitors. A limitation of INAVO120 is the control arm of palbociclib/fulvestrant, which has no OS benefit and a limited PFS benefit in the endocrine-resistant setting. Ribociclib and abemaciclib plus fulvestrant both improved OS and had excellent efficacy in PIK3CA-mutant subgroup analyses. Furthermore, second-line therapy after triplet progression will likely be chemotherapy, as endocrine-based strategies would be limited.

There are several reasons why we prefer capivasertib over alpelisib in PIK3CA-altered HR+/HER2− MBC post-CDK4/6i therapy. CAPItello-291 was the first modern randomized phase III clinical trial with a predominantly post-CDK4/6i population to show PFS benefit and a trend toward OS benefit. SOLAR-1 did not have a statistically significant OS benefit and most patients did not have prior CDK4/6i exposure. Furthermore, capivasertib led to less hyperglycemia, albeit more diarrhea, than alpelisib. The major toxicities of both drugs are compared in Table 3. Alpelisib should only be used in patients with HbA1c ≤ 6.5. In SOLAR-1, patients with HbA1c 6.5%-7.9% were initially eligible, but the study protocol was amended to only include patients with HbA1c ≤ 6.5% and fasting plasma glucose < 140 mg/dl because patients with HbA1c 6.5%-8.0% had an increased risk of developing grade ≥3 hyperglycemia. Capivasertib had low hyperglycemia rates in patients with HgA1c < 8. Both capivasertib and alpelisib cause appreciable rash and prophylactic cetirizine is recommended. Capivasertib, in combination with fulvestrant, is the only pathway inhibitor approved for AKT1 or PTEN alterations.

We suggest prioritizing clinical trial enrollment for patients with pathway alterations, as newer PI3K-targeting agents have the potential of efficacy with the hope of decreased toxicity. Agents currently under investigation are pan-mutant selective allosteric PI3Kα inhibitors (RLY-2608 and STX-478), H1047R/X selective allosteric PI3Kα inhibitors (LOXO-783 and OKI-219), and a covalent PI3Kα inhibitor (TOS-358). Patients whose tumors have mutations not included in the original trials evaluating alpelisib and capivasertib should be encouraged to participate in clinical trials. While there is preclinical evidence of the potential efficacy of alpelisib in patients with C2 domain PI3KCAmts not included in the SOLAR-1 trial, outcomes for these patients remain understudied.83

In conclusion, we currently recommend capivasertib plus fulvestrant as the SOC for patients with PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN-altered HR+/HER2− MBC after progression on first-line ET with CDK4/6i (Figure 1). Everolimus plus fulvestrant remains a viable alternative in settings where capivasertib is not available. The combination of inavolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant is appropriate to consider in the first line for patients with PIK3CA-altered endocrine-resistant tumors. Several mutant-selective PI3Kis are currently in development, and therapeutic options will likely expand in the future. It is imperative to enroll patients in clinical trials.

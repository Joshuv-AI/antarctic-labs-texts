The stability for F F -Yang-Mills functional on ℂ ​ P n \mathbb{C}P^{n}
Yang Wen

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we study the critical points of
F
-Yang-Mills functional on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, which are called
F
-Yang-Mills connections, which is a generalization of
Yang-Mills connections. We prove that if
(2+\frac{4}{n})F^{\prime\prime}(x)x+(n+1)F^{\prime}(x)<0
, then the weakly stable
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
must be flat. Moreover, if
(2+\frac{4}{n})F^{\prime\prime}(x)x+(n+1)F^{\prime}(x)=0
, we obtain the structure of curvatures corresponding to weakly stable connections. We also show a gap theorem for
F
-Yang-Mills connections on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. Our approach is inspired by Lawson-Simons’ study of Yang-Mills stability on spheres.

Keywords:

F
-Yang-Mills connection, stability

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an n-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold and
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
with structure group
G
, where
G
is a compact Lie group. The classical Yang-Mills functional is given by

\displaystyle YM(\nabla)=\int_{M}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}dV,

where
\nabla
is a connection on
E
and
R^{\triangledown}
is its curvature. We denote
d^{\triangledown}
to be the exterior differential operator on
E
induced by
\nabla
and
\delta^{\triangledown}
is its adjoint operator. The Euler-Lagrange equation of
YM
is

\displaystyle\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}=0

and the critical point of
YM
is called Yang-Mills connection.

We are interested in the stability of a Yang-Mills connection. We call
\nabla
is weakly stable if the second variation of
YM
is non-negative at
\nabla
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}YM(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}\geq 0

for any curve of connections
\nabla^{t}
such that
\nabla^{0}=\nabla
.

This stability research stems from the Lawson–Simons conjecture that there is no stable minimal surface on a compact, simply connected,
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched Riemannian manifold. By virtue of the profound similarities between minimal surfaces, harmonic maps, and Yang–Mills fields, computational techniques originally developed for minimal surfaces have been successfully transferred to harmonic maps and Yang–Mills theory. Xin
[
1
]
proved that there is no weakly stable harmonic maps from
S^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. Using similar methods, Bourguignon and Lawson
[
2
]
shows that there is no weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
S^{n}
for
n\geq 5
. Laquer
[
3
]
showed that if the Yang-Mills functional is unstable on a compact irreducible symmetric spaces
M
, then
M
is isometric to
S^{n}
(
n\geq 5
),
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{C}ay)
or
E_{6}/F_{4}
. If
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold isometric immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, Kobayashi-Ohnita-Takeuchi
[
4
]
gives the condition of the second fundamental form such that the Yang-Mills functional is unstable. Moreover, Parker
[
5
]
gives a method to calculate the second variation by using conformal Lagrangian of weight.

The
F
-Yang-Mills functional generalizes the Yang-Mills framework and is studied to gain deeper insights into solutions of Yang-Mills equations. Let
F\in C^{2}([0,+\infty))
be a non-negative, increasing function. We define the
F
-Yang-Mills functional to be

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{F}(\nabla)=\int_{M}F(\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2})dV.

(1)

When
F(x)=(1+2x)^{\alpha}
, Hong-Tian-Yin
[
6
]
introduced this
\alpha
-Yang-Mills functional to investigate the existence of weak solutions for the Yang-Mills flow. Inspired by exponential harmonic maps, by setting
F(x)=e^{2x}
, Fumiaki-Hajime
[
7
]
proposed the exponential Yang-Mills functional.

The Euler-Lagrange equation of
\mathscr{A}_{F}
is

\displaystyle\delta^{\triangledown}(F^{\prime}R^{\triangledown})=0.

(2)

The solution of (
2
) is called an
F
-Yang-Mills connection.

Similar to the Yang-Mills functional, a
F
-Yang-Mills connection
\nabla
is called weakly stable if for any curve of connections
\{\nabla^{t}\}
on
E
such that
\nabla^{0}=\nabla
, there is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathscr{A}_{F}(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}\geq 0.

(3)

If
M
is isometric immersed in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, Kurando and Kazuto
[
8
]
give a condition of the second fundamental form such that the
F
-Yang-Mills functional is unstable.

We are interested in the stability of
F
-Yang-Mills functional on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. Inspired by Cheng’s study
[
10
]
on the stability of Ginzburg-Landau functional on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i) If
(2+\frac{4}{n})F^{\prime\prime}(x)x+(n+1)F^{\prime}(x)<0
, then the
F
-Yang-Mills connection is unstable on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
.

(ii) Assume
(2+\frac{4}{n})F^{\prime\prime}(x)x+(n+1)F^{\prime}(x)=0
and
\nabla
is a weakly stable
F
-Yang-Mills connection. Then there exists
\sigma\in\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
such that

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}(X,Y)=g(X,JY)\sigma,

(4)

where
g
is the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
and
J
is the almost complex structure.

As an example, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

(1) If
F=x^{\alpha}
,
0<\alpha<\frac{2}{3}
, then any
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
is weakly stable.

(2) If
F=x^{\alpha}
,
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, then any
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
is weakly stable.

We also give a gap theorem for the
F
-Yang-Mills functional.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\nabla
is a
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}

(n\geq 2)
and
F^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. If

\|R^{\triangledown}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{(2n-1)\sqrt{2n(2n-1)}}{8(n-1)},

then
R^{\triangledown}\equiv 0
.

Connections and curvatures on vector bundles

Let

n

\displaystyle\langle[\phi,\psi],\rho\rangle=\langle\phi,[\psi,\rho]\rangle.

The inner product on

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},...,i_{p}\leq n}\langle\phi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}),\psi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}})\rangle,

\langle\ ,\ \rangle

\displaystyle\nabla=d+A,

.

induces differential operators

and the adjoint operators
\delta^{\triangledown}:\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{p-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})

\displaystyle d^{\triangledown}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\nabla_{X_{i}}\phi(X_{1},...,\hat{X_{i}},...,X_{p+1})

\displaystyle\delta^{\triangledown}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p-1})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi(e_{i},X_{1},...,X_{p-1})

for any
\phi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and
X_{i}\in\Gamma(TM)
.

Let
R^{\triangledown}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
be the curvature induced by
\nabla
such that

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}=dA+\frac{1}{2}A\wedge A,

where

\displaystyle[\phi,\psi](X,Y)=[\phi(X),\psi(Y)]-[\phi(Y),\psi(X)]

for any

and

.
R^{\triangledown}

\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}(X,Y)\phi=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}\phi-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}\phi-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\phi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E}).

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}(X,Y)\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p})=[R^{\triangledown}(X,Y),\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p})]-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\phi(X_{1},...,R_{M}(X,Y)X_{i},...,X_{p}).

We can define the Laplace-Beltrami operator

\displaystyle\Delta^{\triangledown}=d^{\triangledown}\delta^{\triangledown}+\delta^{\triangledown}d^{\triangledown}

and the rough Laplacian operator by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla=-\sum_{j}(\nabla_{e_{j}}\nabla_{e_{j}}-\nabla_{D_{e_{j}}e_{j}}).

For

and
\phi\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})

as what in

2

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(\psi)(X)=\sum_{j}[R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},X),\psi(e_{j})],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(\phi)(X,Y)=\sum_{j}[R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},X),\phi(e_{j},Y)]-[R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},Y),\phi(e_{j},X)].

Then we have the following Bochner-Weizenböck formula (see
[
2
]

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\psi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and

, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\triangledown}\psi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\psi+\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(\psi)+\psi\circ Ric,

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\triangledown}\phi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\phi+\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(\phi)+\phi\circ(Ric\wedge I+2R),

(6)

where

•

R_{M}
is the curvature of

,

•

is the Ricci transformation defined by

•

Ric\wedge Id
is the extension of the Ricci transformation

\left(Ric\wedge Id\right)(X,Y)=Ric(X)\wedge Y+X\wedge Ric(Y),

•

For any map

Now we assume
M
is the complex projection space
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
with Fubini-Study metric

be the local coordinate system on

. The Fubini-Study metric on

is

\displaystyle g_{i\bar{j}}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{1+|z|^{2}}-\frac{\bar{z}_{i}z_{j}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}},

(7)

where

For simplicity, we denote

and
\bar{Z}_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{i}}

, let

\begin{split}e_{\alpha}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(Z_{\alpha}+\bar{Z}_{\alpha}),\\
e_{n+\alpha}&=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(Z_{\alpha}-\bar{Z}_{\alpha}).\end{split}

(8)

Then
\{e_{1},...,e_{2n}\}
is an orthogonal basis of

and

,

. For

, the curvature tensors at

are

\begin{split}&R(e_{\alpha},e_{\beta})e_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\beta\gamma}e_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}e_{\beta},\\
&R(e_{\alpha},e_{\beta})e_{n+\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\beta\gamma}e_{n+\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}e_{n+\beta},\\
&R(e_{n+\alpha},e_{\beta})e_{\gamma}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}e_{n+\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\beta\gamma}e_{n+\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\alpha\gamma}e_{n+\beta},\\

(9)

Then we have

\begin{split}&(i)\ Ric(X)=(n+1)X,\\

&(iii)\ \sum_{j=1}^{2n}R(JX,e_{j})Je_{j}=-(n+1)X.\end{split}

(10)

, where

is a finite dimensional

-vector space.

(iii) For any

, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\cong\mathfrak{f}_{x}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{x},

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}_{x}=\{V\in\mathcal{K}\mid V(x)=0\}

In fact, Kobayashi and Nomizu

For any

3.1
The second variation of
F
-Yang-Mills functional

Assume
\nabla
is a
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
and
\{\nabla^{t}\}
is a family of connections on
E
compatible with
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
such that
\nabla^{0}=\nabla
and
\frac{d}{dt}\nabla^{t}\mid_{t=0}=B
. The second variation of
\mathscr{A}_{F}
is

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathscr{A}_{F}(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}:=\mathscr{L}_{F}^{\triangledown}(B),

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{F}^{\triangledown}(B)=\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}F^{\prime\prime}\langle R^{\triangledown},d^{\triangledown}B\rangle^{2}+\langle\delta^{\triangledown}(F^{\prime}d^{\triangledown}B),B\rangle+F^{\prime}\langle\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(B),B\rangle dV.

(13)

For simplicity, we define

\displaystyle X_{0}=grad(\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\langle\nabla_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown},R^{\triangledown}\rangle e_{j}.

Then the
F
-Yang-Mills equation is equal to

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}=F^{\prime\prime}i_{X_{0}}R^{\triangledown},

(14)

where

is the contraction about
X
.

For any

, we demonstrate that using

as the variation direction yields zero second variation. Thus, we employ
JV
to construct the variation direction, which is a vector field everywhere orthogonal to
V
. This selection method is inspired by the construction of special vector fields on spheres (see Remark
3.1
), yet here we adopt the perspective where the complex structure acts on Killing vector fields, primarily because Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
significantly facilitates subsequent computations.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
V\in\mathcal{K}
, let
B_{V}=i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{F}^{\triangledown}(B_{V})=\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}J_{V}^{1}(x)+J_{V}^{2}(x)+J_{V}^{3}(x)+J_{V}^{4}(x)dV,

(15)

where

\begin{split}J_{V}^{1}=&F^{\prime\prime}(\langle R^{\triangledown},\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}-\langle i_{X_{0}}\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown},i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle),\\
J_{V}^{2}=&F^{\prime\prime}(2\sum_{i,j}\langle R^{\triangledown},\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})\rangle-\langle i_{D_{X_{0}}JV}R^{\triangledown},i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)\\
&-F^{\prime}\sum_{i,j}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})+\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}JV}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{j})\rangle,\\
J_{V}^{3}=&F^{\prime}\langle\nabla_{JV}\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown},i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle,\\
J_{V}^{4}=&F^{\prime\prime}(\sum_{i,j}\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})\rangle)^{2}+F^{\prime}\langle i_{D^{\ast}DJV}R^{\triangledown}-i_{Ric(JV)}R^{\triangledown},i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\\
&+F^{\prime}\sum_{i,j}\langle R^{\triangledown}(D^{2}_{e_{i},e_{j}}JV,e_{i})+R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},R(e_{i},JV)e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{j})\rangle.\\
\end{split}

(16)

Proof
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, let
\{e_{1},...,e_{2n}\}
be an orthogonal basis of

such that

\begin{split}d^{\triangledown}i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})&=\nabla_{e_{i}}i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}(e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i})\\
&=\nabla_{e_{i}}(R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{j}))-\nabla_{e_{j}}(R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{i}))\\
&=\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{j})+R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}(JV,e_{i})-R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{j}}JV,e_{i})\\
&=\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})+R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})-R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{j}}JV,e_{i}),\end{split}

(17)

where we use
d^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}=0
. Thus

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}\langle R^{\triangledown},d^{\triangledown}i_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(\langle R^{\triangledown},\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})\rangle^{2}+2\langle R^{\triangledown},\nabla_{JV}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}JV,e_{j})\rangle)).

Note that (
17
) is independent to the choice of

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(4|R^{\triangledown}|^{4}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}+\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)).

We still assume
\{V_{1},...,V_{q}\}
be an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{K}
and let
B_{V_{1}},...,B_{V_{q}}
be the variations. Lemma
3.1
, lemma
3.2
and lemma
3.6
show that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mathscr{L}_{F}^{\triangledown}(B_{V_{k}})=\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{q}J_{V_{k}}^{4}(x)dV,

(20)

where
\sum_{k=1}^{q}J_{V_{k}}^{4}(x)
is given by lemma
3.6
.

3.2
The estimation of the second variations under specific variation fields

We will prove the main theorem in this section. Proceeding from (
20
), we need to estimate
\sum_{k=1}^{q}J_{V_{k}}^{4}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, we assume
\{e_{1},...,e_{2n}\}
be an orthogonal basis of
T_{x}\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. Then we have

\displaystyle(i)\

\displaystyle 2|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})\rangle+|\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|^{2}\leq(4+4n)|R^{\triangledown}|^{2},

\displaystyle(ii)\

\displaystyle 4|R^{\triangledown}|^{4}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}+\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)\geq(4+\frac{8}{n})|R^{\triangledown}|^{4}

at
x
, where the two unequal signs both take equal signs if and only if there exists
\sigma\in\mathfrak{so}(E_{x})
such that

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}(X,Y)=g(X,JY)\sigma

(21)

for any
X,Y\in T_{x}\mathbb{C}P^{n}
.

Proof
.

(i) By Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})\rangle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})||R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}+|R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|R^{\triangledown}|^{2},

where the equal sign is taken if and only if

\displaystyle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})=R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})

for any
1\leq i,j\leq 2n
. By Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\sum_{i=1}^{2n}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|^{2}\leq(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|)^{2}\leq 2n\sum_{i=1}^{2n}|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|^{2}\leq 4n|R^{\triangledown}|^{2},

where the necessary and sufficient conditions for taking equal signs of the three inequality are as follows:

\displaystyle(1)\

\displaystyle\textrm{There exists }\sigma\in\mathfrak{so}(E_{x})\textrm{ and }\mu_{i}\geq 0,\ i=1,...,2n,\textrm{ such that }R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})=\mu_{i}\sigma\textrm{ for any }1\leq i\leq 2n,

\displaystyle(2)\

\displaystyle|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|=|R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},Je_{j})|\textrm{ for any }1\leq i,j\leq 2n,

\displaystyle(3)\

\displaystyle\textrm{There exists }\sigma_{ij}\in\mathfrak{so}(E_{x}),\ 1\leq i,j\leq 2n,\textrm{ such that }R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})=g(e_{i},Je_{j})\sigma_{ij}.

Hence these three inequalities are all equal if and only if (
21
) holds.

(ii) Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle^{2}+\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle+\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)^{2}.

By Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle 2n\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}(\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle+\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}|\langle i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{e_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle+\langle i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown},i_{Je_{j}}R^{\triangledown}\rangle|)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}|i_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown}|^{2}+|i_{Je_{i}}R^{\triangledown}|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16|R^{\triangledown}|^{4}.

The above inequality is an equality if (
21
) holds.

By combining lemma
3.6
, lemma
3.7
, and (
20
), we can complete the proof of theorem
1.1
.

Remark 3.1

For any
V\in\mathcal{K}
,
JV
is the gradient of a eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator and such eigenfunction has the form

\displaystyle f_{Q}(z)=\frac{\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq n}q_{ij}z_{i}\bar{z}_{j}}{|z|^{2}},

where
Q=(q_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
is a Hermitian metric and
tr(Q)=0
. Adopting the approach of constructing variations through gradients of eigenfunctions corresponding to the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian on a manifold coincides with the selection method on spheres (e.g. see
[
2
]
). Moreover, Santiago
[
13
]
gives the isometric embedding
\iota:\mathbb{C}P^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
JV
is the projection of a constant vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that is,

\displaystyle\iota_{\ast}JV=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}\mid_{\iota(\mathbb{C}P^{n})}

for some
a_{1},...,a_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus our choice of the variation is consist with that by using the second fundamental form (e.g. see
[
8
]
).

3.3
A gap theorem

In this section, we give a gap theorem of
F
-Yang-Mills functional on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
\nabla
is a
F
-Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{C}P^{n}

(n\geq 2)
and
F^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. If

\|R^{\triangledown}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{(2n-1)\sqrt{2n(2n-1)}}{8(n-1)},

then
R^{\triangledown}\equiv 0
.

Proof
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, let
\{e_{1},...,e_{2n}\}
be an orthogonal basis of
T\mathbb{C}P^{n}
such that
De_{j}(x)=0
. Then we have

\begin{split}\Delta F(\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2})=-\sum_{j}e_{j}e_{j}(F)=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime\prime}grad(|R^{\triangledown}|^{2})^{2}-F^{\prime}|\nabla R^{\triangledown}|^{2}+F^{\prime}\langle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla R^{\triangledown},R^{\triangledown}\rangle,\end{split}

(22)

d^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}=0
and the Bochner-Weizenböck formula (
5
) shows that

\displaystyle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla R^{\triangledown}=d^{\triangledown}\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}-R^{\triangledown}\circ(Ric\wedge I+2R)-\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(R^{\triangledown}).

The
F
-Yang-Mills equation implies
\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}F^{\prime}\langle d^{\triangledown}\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown},R^{\triangledown}\rangle dV=0.
Integral (
22
) and note that
\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}\Delta FdV=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}F^{\prime}\langle R^{\triangledown}\circ(Ric\wedge I+2R)+\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(R^{\triangledown}),R^{\triangledown}\rangle dV\leq 0.

(23)

By direct calculation, we have
\langle R^{\triangledown}\circ(Ric\wedge I),R^{\triangledown}\rangle=(2n+2)|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}
and

\displaystyle\langle R^{\triangledown}\circ 2R,R^{\triangledown}\rangle=

\displaystyle-|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\langle R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\langle R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i}),R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},Je_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}(|R^{\triangledown}(Je_{i},Je_{j})|^{2}+|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2})-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}(|R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},Je_{i})|^{2}+|R^{\triangledown}(e_{j},Je_{j})|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-3|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}.

By proposition 5.6 in
[
2
]
, we have
\langle\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(R^{\triangledown}),R^{\triangledown}\rangle\geq-\frac{8(n-1)}{\sqrt{2n(2n-1)}}|R^{\triangledown}|^{3}
. Put them into (
23
) and we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}P^{n}}(2n-1-\frac{8(n-1)}{\sqrt{2n(2n-1)}}|R^{\triangledown}|)|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}dV\leq 0.

(24)

Then we finish the proof.

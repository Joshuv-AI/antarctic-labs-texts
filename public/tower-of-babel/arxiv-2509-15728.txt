Finiteness of K-moduli compactifications Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 14J45. Keywords : Log Fano pair, K-stability, K-moduli, Wall crossing. Competing interests : The authors have no conflict of interest to declare.
Chuyu Zhou

Abstract
. In this note, we aim to prove the finite semi-algebraic chamber decomposition theorem for K-semi(poly)stability under the assumption of the log boundedness of K-semistable degenerations. This boundedness assumption is naturally arising from the finiteness of K-moduli compactifications.



We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
throughout the article.

1.
Introduction

Let
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})\to T
be a log smooth morphism over a smooth base
T
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a smooth divisor on
\mathcal{X}
. Suppose

(\mathcal{X}_{t},x\mathcal{D}_{t})
is a K-(poly)stable log Fano pair for any
t\in T
and any
x\in[0,1)
. By
[
10
]
complemented by
[
11
]
,
for any given
c\in[0,1)
, there exists an Artin stack of finite type
\mathcal{M}_{c}
generically parametrizing the fibers of
\pi_{c}:(\mathcal{X},c\mathcal{D})\to T
, and
\mathcal{M}_{c}
descends to a proper good moduli space
M_{c}
, which is a K-moduli compactification of
T
. Write
\phi_{c}:\mathcal{M}_{c}\to M_{c}
to be the descending map. We pose the following question:

Question: Is the set of K-moduli compactifications
K(T):=\{M_{c}\ |\ c\in[0,1)\}
finite?

This question is naturally related to the wall crossing property of K-moduli. In the case where
\mathcal{D}
is proportional to
-K_{\mathcal{X}}
over
T
, there is a finite rational interval chamber decomposition

0=c_{0}<c_{1}<c_{2}<...<c_{k}<c_{k+1}=1
such that
\phi_{c}:\mathcal{M}_{c}\to M_{c}
does not depend on
c\in(c_{i},c_{i+1})
for any given index
0\leq i\leq k
(e.g.
[
1
,
16
]
). This clearly implies that the set
K(T)
is finite. However, the question is much more subtle if
\mathcal{D}
is not proportional to
-K_{\mathcal{X}}
over
T
. In the non-proportional case, if
{\rm{vol}}(-K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}-\mathcal{D}_{t})>0
, we still have a finite interval chamber decomposition of
[0,1)
such that
\phi_{c}:\mathcal{M}_{c}\to M_{c}
does not change restricted to each open interval; if
{\rm{vol}}(-K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}-\mathcal{D}_{t})=0
while we only focus on
c\in(0,1-\epsilon]
for some
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, we also have a finite interval chamber decomposition of
[0,1-\epsilon)
such that
\phi_{c}:\mathcal{M}_{c}\to M_{c}
does not change restricted to each open interval (e.g.
[
12
]
). The different point is that the finite interval chamber decompositions in non-proportional case may not be rational (e.g.
[
3
]
). Above all, the difficulty arises when
{\rm{vol}}(-K_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}-\mathcal{D}_{t})=0
and we consider all
c\in[0,1)
. Regarding this, the non-proportional wall crossing theory developed in
[
12
]
is not complete since we do not touch the case when the coefficient is close to the Calabi-Yau boundary.
Towards this difficulty, the expected solution should be as follows:
there exists a finite interval chamber decomposition of
[0,1)
such that
\phi_{c}:\mathcal{M}_{c}\to M_{c}
does not change restricted to each open interval.
This solution will totally address the finiteness question we pose at first.

Suppose the set
K(T)
is finite, then the set
\cup_{c\in[0,1)}\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
is clearly log bounded. This actually indicates the key ingredient in the program of developing a complete non-proportional wall crossing theory, i.e. the set of all K-semistable degenerations (as the coefficient varies) should be log bounded. We divide the program into two steps:

Step 1: Show that the set of K-semistable degenerations, i.e.
\cup_{c\in[0,1)}\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
, is log bounded;

Step 2: Develop a complete non-proportional wall crossing theory for K-moduli under the log boundedness in step 1.

In this note, we will work in a more general setting and complete step 2.
Throughout, we fix two positive integers
d,k
and a rational polytope
P\subset[0,1]^{k}
. We also fix a log bounded set
\mathcal{G}
of couples depending on
(d,k,P)
such that the couple
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
in
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

X
is a normal projective variety of dimension
d
;

(2)

D_{j},1\leq j\leq k,
are effective Weil divisors on
X
;

(3)

there exists a vector
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in P
such that
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{j})
is a
K-semistable weak log Fano pair
.

We emphasize that the set
\mathcal{G}
is given log boundedness (one could for example take it as the set of fibers of
\pi
given at the beginning).

Definition 1.1
.

For any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\mathcal{G}
, we say
(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
is a
dominant small modification
of
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
if
Y
is a normal projective variety and there is a small morphism
(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})\to(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
, where
\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}
is the birational transform of
\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j}
. Denote
\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
to be the set of all dominant small modifications of all objects in
\mathcal{G}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
. If
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
, we say
\mathcal{G}
is
complete under dominant small modifications.

Usually it is not hard to prove the log boundedness of
\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
under the log boundedness of
\mathcal{G}
(e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 1.7]
). For example, if we take
\mathcal{G}
to be the set of fibers of
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D})\to T
given at the beginning, we clearly have
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
. So it is harmless to assume
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{{\rm{dsm}}}
at first, and we will technically benefit from this assumption (e.g. Proposition
5.2
).

From now on, we will always tacitly assume that the given log bounded set
\mathcal{G}
is complete under dominant small modifications
. We also define the K-moduli completion
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
of the set
\mathcal{G}
as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

We say a couple
(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
is contained in
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if there exists a family of couples
(\mathcal{Y},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{j})\to C\ni 0
over a smooth pointed curve such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{j,t})\in\mathcal{G}
for
t\neq 0
;

(2)

(\mathcal{Y}_{0},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{j,0})\cong(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
;

(3)

(\mathcal{Y},\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j})\to C
is an
\mathbb{R}
-Gorenstein family of K-semistable weak log Fano pairs for some
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in P
, i.e.
-K_{\mathcal{Y}}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and every fiber
(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j,t})
is a K-semistable weak log Fano pair.

A priori, we do not know whether the set
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded, though the set
\mathcal{G}
is assumed to be log bounded. However, we expect this is true.

Conjecture 1.3
.

(
[
12
]
)

The set
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded.

This boundedness seems tricky and we will treat it in future works. In this note, we just work through with the assumption that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded. Under this log boundedness, we have the following wall crossing property for K-semi(poly)stability.

Theorem 1.4
.

(= Theorem
6.4
)

Under the assumption that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded, there exists a finite chamber decomposition
P=\cup_{i}A_{i}
such that
A_{i}
’s are all semi-algebraic sets and mutually disjoint; moreover, for any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
and any piece
A_{i}
, the K-semistability and K-polystability of
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})
do not depend on
(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in A_{i}
.

From the above theorem, each chamber
A_{i}
gives one proper K-moduli space, thus there are only finitely many K-moduli spaces appearing as we change the coefficients. This gives a positive answer to the finiteness question put at the beginning, under the assumption of the log boundedness of the set of K-semistable degenerations. The strategy towards Theorem
1.4
is to prove that the number of K-semistable domains is finite and each K-semistable domain is semi-algebraic, under the log boundedness of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
(see Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
6.3
).

Remark 1.5
.

We finally make a comment towards the above theorem. In
[
12
]
, the above result is worked out under the following volume condition: there exists a positive real number
\epsilon_{0}
such that
{\rm{vol}}(-K_{X}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})\geq\epsilon_{0}
for any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
and any
(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in P
. The volume condition automatically implies that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded (e.g.
[
12
, Section 7]
). However, we usually lose the volume condition as the coefficients approach the boundary of
P
. The above theorem can be viewed as a conceptual generalization of the result proved in
[
12
]
, which reduces the non-proportional wall crossing problem to the boundedness problem, i.e. Conjecture
1.3
.

Notation and Conventions

•

A polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
means a compact subset
P
cut out by finitely many inequalities
H_{i}\geq 0
, where each
H_{i}
determines a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
; a face of the polytope
P
is a subset of
P
determined by the intersection of some hyperplanes
H_{i}=0
;

•

We say the polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is rational if all of its vertices admit rational coordinates;

•

For a polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we use
P^{\circ}
to denote the interior part of
P
under the relative topology;

•

For any subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we use
A(\mathbb{Q})
to denote the set of rational points in
A
;

•

Given a polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, a finite polytope chamber decomposition of
P
means a finite union
P=\cup_{s}P_{s}
, where each
P_{s}
is a polytope and
P_{i}^{\circ}\cap P_{j}^{\circ}
is empty for any different
i,j
; if each chamber
P_{s}
is rational, we say it is a finite rational polytope chamber decomposition;

•

Given a polytope
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, a finite semi-algebraic chamber decomposition of
P
means a finite union
P=\cup_{s}A_{s}
, where each chamber
A_{s}
is semi-algebraic and any two chambers are disjoint;

•

When we mention a point on the base of a family of varieties, it always means a closed point; and a fiber of the family always means the fiber over a closed point;

•

A Weil divisor means a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor which is not necessarily irreducible;

•

A constructible stratification of a variety
T
means decomposing
T
into a finite disjoint union where each piece is locally closed.

2.
Preliminaries

We say
(V,B)
is a
couple
if
V
is a normal projective variety and
B
is an effective Weil divisor. We say
(V,B)
is a
log pair
if
V
is a normal projective variety and
B
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor such that
K_{V}+B
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. We say a log pair
(V,B)
is
log Fano
(resp.
weak log Fano
) if
(V,B)
admits klt singularities and
-K_{V}-B
is ample (resp. big and nef).
We say
(X,\Delta)\to T
is a family of couples if
X\to T
is a projective morphism between finite type schemes and
\Delta
is an effective divisor on
X
such that every fiber
(X_{t},\Delta_{t})
is a couple. Given a family of log Fano pairs (resp. weak log Fano pairs)
(X,\Delta)\to T
over a smooth base
T
, i.e. every fiber
(X_{t},\Delta_{t})
is a log Fano pair (weak log Fano pair), we say it is an
\mathbb{R}
-(
\mathbb{Q}
-)Gorenstein family if
-K_{X/T}-\Delta
is
\mathbb{R}
-(
\mathbb{Q}
-)Cartier.

For a comprehensive understanding on the K-stability of log Fano pairs with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients, we refer to
[
14
]

[
15
]
. For some basic understanding on the K-stability of log Fano pairs with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficients, we refer to
[
11
]
. Below we just briefly survey some elementary concepts and results that will be applied in this note.

2.1.
K-stability

The following definition is inspired by
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Fujita–Li)
For a given log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
, we say it is
K-semistable
if

\beta_{X,\Delta}(E):=A_{X,\Delta}(E)-S_{X,\Delta}(E)\geq 0
for any prime divisor
E
over
X
, where

A_{X,\Delta}(E):={\rm{ord}}_{E}\left(K_{Y}-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)\right)+1,

S_{X,\Delta}(E):=\frac{1}{{\rm{vol}}(-K_{X}-\Delta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm{vol}}(-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)-tE){\rm d}t.

Here
f:Y\to(X,\Delta)
is a proper log resolution such that
E
is a prime divisor on
Y
. The notation
\beta_{X,\Delta}(E)
is also called the Fujita-Li invariant of
(X,\Delta)
with respect to
E
. For a given weak log Fano pair, we say it is K-semistable if its anti-canonical model (which is a log Fano pair) is K-semistable.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a K-semistable log Fano pair. We say it is
K-polystable
if any special test configuration of
(X,\Delta;-K_{X}-\Delta)
with a K-semistable central fiber is of product type.

By
[
7
]
, a K-semistable log Fano pair with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients always admits a K-polystable degeneration via a special test configuration. The same result applies to K-semistable log Fano pairs with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficients by
[
11
]
.

Before Definition
2.1
, K-semistability of a log Fano pair is tested by the non-negativity of the generalized Futaki invariants of special test configurations (see
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
). The generalized Futaki invariant can be reformulated by the CM-line bundle.

Let
Q\subset[0,1]^{k}
be a rationa polytope and
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j})\to T
is a family of couples of relative dimension
d
over a smooth base
T
. Suppose
\pi_{\mathbf{x}}:(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j})\to T
is an
\mathbb{R}
-Gorenstein family of log Fano pairs for any
\mathbf{x}:=(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in Q
. By the work of Mumford-Knudsen
[
5
]
, for any
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
, there exist (uniquely determined)
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles
\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{x})\ (i=0,1,...,d+1)
on
T
such that we have the following expansion for all sufficiently divisible
m\in\mathbb{N}
:

\det\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})^{\otimes m})=\lambda_{d+1}(\mathbf{x})^{\binom{m}{d+1}}\otimes\lambda_{d}(\mathbf{x})^{\binom{m}{d}}\otimes...\otimes\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{x})^{\binom{m}{1}}\otimes\lambda_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

For convenience, we write
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j}
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}):=-K_{\mathcal{X}/T}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j}
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
15
, Definition 9.29]
)
For any given rational vector
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
, the CM-line bundle for the family
\pi_{\mathbf{x}}:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x});\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\to T
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
T
defined as
\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathbf{x}):=-\lambda_{d+1}(\mathbf{x})
, which depends on
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
.

The above definition only applies to rational
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
. It is generalized to arbitrary
\mathbf{x}\in Q
as follows.

Proposition-Definition 2.4
.

(
[
12
, Section 8.1]
)
Notation as in Definition
2.3
, there exist finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles
M_{q}
’s on
T
and finitely many
\mathbb{Q}
-polynomials
g_{q}(x_{1},...,x_{k})
’s such that

\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{q}g_{q}(x_{1},...,x_{k})\cdot M_{q}

for any
\mathbf{x}=Q(\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, if
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j})\to T
admits a
G
-equivariant action for some algebraic group
G
, then
M_{q}
’s can be chosen
G
-equivariant. By the above formulation, one can naturally extend the definition of
{\rm{CM}}
-line bundle from
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
to arbitrary
\mathbf{x}\in Q
.

We have the following well-known property of the CM line bundle.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
13
]
)

Notation as above. Suppose for any given
\mathbf{x}\in Q(\mathbb{Q})
,
\pi_{\mathbf{x}}:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x});\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\to T
is a special test configuration
(
T=\mathbb{A}^{1}
in this case
)
of a log Fano pair
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})
. Write
L(\mathbf{x}):=-K_{X}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j}
. Then
\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathbf{x})
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-linearized
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on the base
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and we have

{\rm{Fut}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x});\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))=\frac{1}{(d+1)L(\mathbf{x})^{d}}\cdot{\rm{wt}}(\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathbf{x})).

Under the condition of Proposition
2.5
, we know that

\pi_{\mathbf{x}}:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{x});\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\to T
is a special test configuration for arbitrary
\mathbf{x}\in Q
. Note that

{\rm{wt}}(\lambda_{{\rm{CM}}}(\mathbf{x}))=\sum_{q}g_{q}(\mathbf{x})\cdot{\rm{wt}}(M_{q})

for some
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-linearized line bundles
M_{q}
’s and some polynomials
g_{q}(\mathbf{x})
’s (see Proposition-Definition
2.4
). We thus see that the formulation of the generalized Futaki invariant in Proposition
2.5
also applies to arbitrary
\mathbf{x}\in Q
by the continuity of both sides.

2.2.
Boundedness of algebraic varieties

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of projective varieties of dimension
d
. We say
\mathcal{P}
is bounded if there exists a projective morphism
\mathcal{Y}\to T
between schemes of finite type such that for any
X\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists a closed point
t\in T
such that
X\cong\mathcal{Y}_{t}
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be a set of projective couples of dimension
d
. We say

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is log bounded if there exist a projective morphism
\mathcal{Y}\to T
between schemes of finite type and a reduced Weil divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathcal{Y}
such that for any
(X,\Delta)\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists a closed point
t\in T
such that
(X,{\rm{red}}(\Delta))\cong(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{D}_{t})
. Here
{\rm{red}}(\Delta)
means taking all the coefficients of irreducible components in
\Delta
to be one.

3.
Constructible log Fano domians

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a subset. Given a couple
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, recall the following definition of log Fano domain (resp. weak log Fano domain) restricted to the set
A
:

{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{A}:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in A\ |\ \text{$(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})$ is a log Fano pair}\};

({\rm resp.}\ \ {\rm{WLF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{A}:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in A\ |\ \text{$(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})$ is a weak log Fano pair}\})

For a couple
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, it is not hard to see the closure of
{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}
is a rational polytope. If we write
Q=\overline{{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}}
and let
\{F_{l}\}_{l=1}^{m}
be the set of all proper faces of
Q
, then we have the following formulation:

{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}=Q^{\circ}\ \cup\ \cup_{i=1}^{s}F^{\circ}_{r_{i}},

where
\{r_{1},r_{2},...,r_{s}\}
is a subset of
\{1,2,...,m\}
. The weak log Fano domain satisfies the same property.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose there exists a family of couples over a finite type scheme
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j})\to T
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is contained in the set of fibers of
\pi
. Then there exists a constructible stratification
T=\amalg_{i}T_{i}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

each
T_{i}
is smooth;

(2)

the induced family (obtained via base-change)
\pi_{i}:(\mathcal{X}^{(i)},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j}^{(i)})\to T_{i}
is flat;

(3)

the fibers of
(\mathcal{X}^{(i)},\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{j}^{(i)})\to T_{i}
admit the same log Fano domain (resp. weak log Fano domain) restricted to
P
for any index
i
.

Proof.

By
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
, up to replacing
T
with another finite type scheme, we may assume that for any
t\in T
, the fiber
(\mathcal{X}_{t},\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}\mathcal{D}_{j,t})
is log Fano or weak log Fano for some
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in P
. We are done by applying
[
12
, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a finite rational polytope chamber decomposition
P=\cup_{s}P_{s}
such that for any face
F
of any chamber
P_{s}
, we have the following:
for any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, if
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}D_{j})
is a log Fano pair (resp. weak log Fano pair) for some vector
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in F^{\circ}
, then
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})
is a log Fano pair (resp. weak log Fano pair) for any vector
(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in F^{\circ}
.

Proof.

We only deal with the log Fano case, as the weak log Fano case can be proved similarly. Note that
\overline{{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}}
is a rational polytope for any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})
. Put
Q:=\overline{{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}}
, and let
\{F_{l}\}_{l=1}^{m}
be the set of all proper faces of
Q
, then we see the following formulation

{\rm{LF}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}=Q^{\circ}\ \cup\ \cup_{i=1}^{s}F^{\circ}_{r_{i}},

where
\{r_{1},r_{2},...,r_{s}\}
is a subset of
\{1,2,...,m\}

∎

4.
Finiteness of K-semistable domains

Let

be a subset. Given a couple
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
, recall the following definition of K-semistable domain restricted to the set

{\rm{Kss}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{A}:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in A\ |\ \text{$(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})$ is a K-semistable log Fano pair}\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the assumption that the set
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded, the following set of K-semistable domains restricted to

is finite:

\{{\rm{Kss}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}\ |\ \text{$(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}$}\}.

Given the log boundedness of

and there is a constructible stratification of

∎

In this section, we aim to show that
{\rm{Kss}}(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})_{P}
is semi-algebraic for any
(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}

[

Proposition 6.1
.

(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}D_{j})\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}

. Suppose

is log Fano for any

q
of any chamber

(indexed by

), where each

is of the following form for some prime divisor

over

G_{l}(x_{1},...,x_{k})={\rm{vol}}(-K_{X}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})\cdot\left(A_{X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j}}(E_{l})-S_{X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j}}(E_{l})\right).

Note that in this proposition we do not assume that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
is log bounded.

Proof.

First note that
{\rm{vol}}(-K_{X}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})
and
\delta(X,\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}D_{j})
are both continuous on

(e.g.
[
11
, Proposition 2.10]

\epsilon_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
(which may be assumed
<1
) such that

for any
(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in Q
. The rest of the proof is almost identical to that of
[
12
, Proposition 8.8]
. We sketch it below.

Step 1. We construct a set of couples
\mathcal{A}(\delta_{0},\epsilon_{0})
consisting of
(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})
satisfying:

(1)

Y
is normal projective of dimension
d
and
B_{j}
’s are effective divisors;

(2)

(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}B_{j})
is a weak log Fano pair for some vector
(c_{1},...,c_{k})\in Q
with
\delta(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_{j}B_{j})\geq\delta_{0}

(3)

{\rm{vol}}(-K_{Y}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}B_{j})\geq\epsilon_{0}
for any
(x_{1},...,x_{k})\in Q
.

We know that
\mathcal{A}(\delta_{0},\epsilon_{0})
is log bounded (e.g.
[
12
, Section 7]
), and there exists a finite rational polytope chamber decomposition
Q=\cup_{s}Q_{s}
such that

(1)

for any couple
(Y,\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j})\in\mathcal{A}(\delta_{0},\epsilon_{0})
and any face
q
of any chamber
Q_{s}
, if

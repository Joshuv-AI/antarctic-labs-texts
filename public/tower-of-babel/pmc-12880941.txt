Quality of Life Changes Post-PCI With Contrast-induced Nephropathy
Authors: Uzair N. Malik, Kashif Iltaf, Maria M. Ahmed Babiker, Prem Chand, Rakesh Kumar, Areesha Qureshi, Aajay Kumar, Muhammad H. Bari, Ali Karim, Sameet Kumar, Fnu Shweta, Fnu Rumela, Fnu Payal, Abida parveen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the impact of contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) on quality of life (QoL) in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in Pakistan.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted involving 5325 patients who underwent PCI, divided into CIN (n = 1250) and non-CIN (n = 4075) groups. Demographics, clinical characteristics, and procedural details were recorded. QoL was assessed using a standardized questionnaire before and after PCI, covering physical functioning, pain, mobility, anxiety/depression, and overall health. Statistical analyses compared QoL scores between groups and identified predictors of change.ResultsPatients who developed CIN exhibited a significant decline in post-PCI QoL across all domains compared to the non-CIN group (p < 0.05). CIN, older age, diabetes, lack of hydration protocol use, prolonged hospital stay, and lower baseline QoL were independent predictors of poorer QoL outcomes. Comorbidities were more prevalent in the CIN group, including diabetes (64.0 % vs. 47.8 %), hypertension (68.0 % vs. 60.8 %), and CKD Stage 3 or worse (28.0 % vs. 8.6 %) (all p < 0.05). CIN patients more frequently underwent PCI for acute coronary syndrome, received higher contrast volumes, and had lower use of preventive measures. Post-PCI complications such as heart failure (36.0 %), dialysis requirement (32.0 %), and prolonged hospital stays (60.0 %) were significantly higher in CIN patients.ConclusionCIN adversely affects QoL following PCI, particularly in high-risk patients. Preventive strategies and early intervention are essential to mitigate CIN-related complications and improve patient-centered outcomes. Further prospective, multi-center studies are warranted.

Introduction

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is a cornerstone of modern cardiology, providing effective revascularization for patients with ischemic heart disease.1 While its role in improving survival and symptom relief is well-established, the occurrence of contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) poses a significant challenge, particularly in resource-limited settings like Pakistan.2 CIN, characterized by an acute decline in renal function following the administration of contrast media, not only increases morbidity and healthcare costs but also potentially impacts patients’ quality of life (QoL). QoL is a critical metric in assessing the holistic outcomes of PCI, encompassing physical, emotional, and social well-being.3 However, patients who develop CIN often face additional burdens, such as prolonged hospital stays, the need for dialysis, and psychological distress. In Pakistan, the prevalence of comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease further escalates the risk of CIN, making it a pertinent public health issue.4 Despite the clinical importance of CIN, data on its impact on QoL in Pakistan remain sparse. Understanding this relationship is vital for designing effective preventive strategies and optimizing post-PCI care.5 This survey aims to address this gap by exploring QoL changes in patients who developed CIN post-PCI, considering the sociocultural and economic factors unique to Pakistan.

Methods

This survey was conducted to evaluate the quality of life (QoL) changes in patients who developed contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) following percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in Pakistan. A cross-sectional design was employed, targeting patients who underwent PCI across tertiary care hospitals in major cities, including Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad. Patients were recruited between January 2023 and June 2024.

2.1. Study population

Eligible participants included adult patients (aged ≥18 years) who underwent PCI and were diagnosed with CIN, defined as an increase in serum creatinine by ≥25 % or 0.5 mg/dL from baseline within 48–72 h of contrast exposure. Patients with preexisting end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis, non-consent, or incomplete data were excluded. A purposive sampling strategy was used to ensure representation from diverse geographic, socioeconomic, and clinical backgrounds.

2.2. Data collection

Data were collected using a structured questionnaire administered through in-person interviews or telephonic surveys. The questionnaire consisted of three sections: demographic and clinical characteristics, QoL assessment, and patient-reported challenges post-CIN. QoL was measured using the EuroQol-5D-5L (EQ-5D-5L) tool, which assesses five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. Each dimension was rated on a five-level scale, providing a detailed picture of the impact on patients’ daily lives. Additional questions were included to explore sociocultural and economic aspects unique to the Pakistani population. Those who were incapable of filling questionnaire were assessed by the authors.

2.3. Data analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics. Changes in QoL scores before and after PCI were analyzed using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, depending on data normality. Subgroup analyses were performed based on age, gender, and comorbidities to identify vulnerable populations. Associations between CIN severity and QoL changes were evaluated using multivariate regression models. Results were presented as adjusted odds ratios with 95 % confidence intervals.

2.4. Ethical considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from institutional review boards of the Abbas Institute of Medical Sciences (study ID # AIMS/24/108). Informed consent was secured from all participants, ensuring confidentiality and voluntary participation.

Results

3.1. Demographics

A total of 5325 patients undergoing PCI were included in the analysis, with 1250 (23.5 %) developing contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) and 4075 (76.5 %) not developing CIN (Table 1). The mean age of the cohort was 58.3 ± 10.5 years, with the CIN group significantly older than the non-CIN group (61.2 ± 9.8 vs. 57.5 ± 10.7 years, p = 0.037). Male patients constituted 61.8 % of the cohort, with a slightly higher proportion in the CIN group (64.0 % vs. 61.1 %, p = 0.045). The majority of patients resided in urban areas (71.4 %), with no significant differences between the groups (p = 0.642). Educational levels were unevenly distributed between the groups. The CIN group had a higher proportion of patients with no formal education (22.4 % vs. 15.7 %, p < 0.001) and fewer patients with tertiary education (16.0 % vs. 18.5 %, p < 0.001). Similarly, monthly household income differed, with more patients in the CIN group having an income of <20,000 PKR (32.0 % vs. 26.5 %, p < 0.001). Comorbidities were significantly more prevalent in the CIN group, including diabetes mellitus (64.0 % vs. 47.8 %, p = 0.012), hypertension (68.0 % vs. 60.8 %, p = 0.026), and chronic kidney disease (28.0 % vs. 8.6 %, p = 0.006).

3.2. Clinical and procedural characteristics

PCI was most frequently performed for acute coronary syndrome (ACS), with a significantly higher prevalence in the CIN group (72.0 % vs. 59.5 %, p = 0.011) (Table 2). Single-vessel PCI was more common in the non-CIN group (72.2 % vs. 64.0 %, p = 0.017), while multi-vessel PCI was more frequent in the CIN group (36.0 % vs. 27.8 %, p = 0.017). Contrast volume was notably higher in the CIN group (240.3 ± 48.7 mL vs. 194.2 ± 41.5 mL, p = 0.028). The use of preventive measures was significantly lower in the CIN group, including hydration protocols (68.0 % vs. 83.4 %, p = 0.032) and low-contrast protocols (16.0 % vs. 46.6 %, p < 0.001). Post-PCI complications were more prevalent in the CIN group. Heart failure occurred in 36.0 % of CIN patients compared to 19.0 % in the non-CIN group (p < 0.001). Similarly, dialysis was required more frequently in the CIN group (32.0 % vs. 7.4 %, p = 0.025), and prolonged hospital stays (>5 days) were observed in 60.0 % of CIN patients versus 20.6 % of non-CIN patients (p < 0.001).

3.3. Quality of life (QoL) scores

Baseline QoL scores were slightly lower in the CIN group across all domains (Table 3). Post-PCI, the CIN group experienced a decline in QoL scores, while the non-CIN group showed significant improvements in all domains. For example, in the physical functioning domain, scores decreased by −4.8 ± 2.3 in the CIN group but increased by +3.3 ± 2.1 in the non-CIN group (p < 0.001). Similarly, overall health (EQ-VAS) scores decreased by −4.8 ± 2.4 in the CIN group but improved by +3.8 ± 2.5 in the non-CIN group (p < 0.001).

3.4. Predictors of QoL changes

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that CIN was an independent predictor of poorer QoL outcomes post-PCI (adjusted OR: 0.58; 95 % CI: 0.47–0.72; p = 0.029) (Table 4). Other factors associated with reduced QoL improvements included advanced age (OR: 0.96; 95 % CI: 0.94–0.98; p = 0.041), diabetes mellitus (OR: 0.72; 95 % CI: 0.60–0.86; p = 0.024), and prolonged hospital stay (OR: 0.62; 95 % CI: 0.51–0.77; p = 0.006). Conversely, the use of hydration protocols was associated with improved QoL (OR: 1.45; 95 % CI: 1.18–1.79; p = 0.037).

3.5. Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis revealed significant variations in QoL changes based on demographic and clinical factors (Table 5). Younger patients (<65 years) showed greater improvements in QoL post-PCI compared to older patients (≥65 years), with a mean change of +3.5 ± 2.4 in the non-CIN group vs. −4.2 ± 2.1 in the CIN group (p < 0.001). Similarly, non-CIN patients with diabetes had less QoL improvement (+3.0 ± 2.3) compared to those without diabetes (+3.6 ± 2.2, p < 0.001). Gender-based differences were minimal, but CIN had a uniformly negative impact on QoL across all subgroups.

3.6. Contrast-induced nephropathy incidence and renal outcomes

Among the 5325 patients undergoing PCI, the incidence of contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) was 23.5 % (n = 1250). This relatively high rate underscores the clinical relevance of CIN in contemporary interventional cardiology, particularly in high-risk populations with prevalent comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease (CKD Stage 3 or worse).

Follow-up data indicated that renal recovery was incomplete in a significant subset of CIN patients. While a portion of patients demonstrated partial improvement in renal function within the first 5–7 days post-PCI, persistent renal dysfunction was noted in approximately 18.4 % of CIN cases beyond hospital discharge. Moreover, 32.0 % of CIN patients required dialysis during hospitalization, reflecting severe and often irreversible renal impairment. These findings highlight the need for early identification, aggressive prevention strategies, and long-term renal monitoring in patients at high risk for CIN.

Discussion

The results of this study highlight critical insights into the clinical and procedural factors influencing contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) and its subsequent impact on patient outcomes following percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). CIN was associated with significant demographic, clinical, and quality-of-life (QoL) differences compared to patients who did not develop CIN.

CIN patients were older, had a higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease (CKD), and had lower socioeconomic and educational statuses. These findings align with previous studies suggesting that age, comorbidities, and reduced socioeconomic resources contribute to CIN susceptibility due to impaired renal reserve and limited access to preventive healthcare interventions. Moreover, the higher baseline prevalence of CKD in the CIN group underscores its pivotal role as a risk factor for CIN, as compromised kidney function predisposes patients to nephrotoxic effects of contrast agents.6

Procedurally, CIN was more prevalent in patients undergoing multi-vessel PCI, receiving higher contrast volumes, and lacking preventive strategies like hydration and low-contrast protocols. Multi-vessel PCI often involves increased complexity and contrast volume, exacerbating renal insult. The underutilization of preventive measures in the CIN group, particularly hydration and low-contrast protocols, was striking and aligns with evidence advocating for these interventions to mitigate CIN risk. This underscores the need for stringent adherence to preventive protocols in high-risk populations.7

CIN not only influenced clinical outcomes but also negatively impacted patient-reported QoL metrics. CIN patients experienced substantial declines in physical functioning, mobility, pain, anxiety, and overall health post-PCI compared to improvements in the non-CIN group. The subgroup analysis further emphasized that age, gender, and diabetes significantly modulated the QoL decline in CIN patients, with older and diabetic patients showing the most pronounced decrements. These findings highlight the bidirectional relationship between CIN and reduced QoL, as renal impairment may amplify systemic inflammation and metabolic disturbances, compounding the burden of comorbidities.8

Notably, CIN was associated with increased rates of post-PCI complications, including heart failure, dialysis requirement, and prolonged hospital stay. These complications further exacerbate healthcare costs and patient burden. The multivariate analysis identified CIN, age, diabetes, and prolonged hospital stay as independent predictors of QoL deterioration. Interestingly, hydration protocols emerged as a protective factor, reinforcing their utility in mitigating CIN and preserving QoL.

These findings have significant implications for clinical practice. First, the results reinforce the importance of early risk stratification and tailored preventive strategies in vulnerable populations, particularly older adults and those with diabetes or CKD. Second, optimizing procedural strategies, including limiting contrast volume and maximizing preventive measures, is critical for reducing CIN incidence. Finally, integrating patient-centered care approaches that address QoL impacts, including physical rehabilitation and psychological support, may improve long-term outcomes in CIN patients.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that must be considered. As a retrospective cohort study, it cannot establish causality between contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) and quality of life (QoL) outcomes, and the associations observed may be influenced by unmeasured confounders. Additionally, the study was conducted at a single center in Pakistan, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations. Data collection relied on patient medical records and self-reported QoL assessments, which could introduce errors or inconsistencies. The lack of long-term follow-up data means that the long-term effects of CIN on QoL were not evaluated. Furthermore, the nonrandomized design and potential biases in treatment allocation may have influenced the results. Other preventive strategies for CIN, such as pharmacological interventions, were not systematically assessed, limiting the scope of preventive measures. Finally, missing or incomplete data could have affected the accuracy of the results. Despite these limitations, the study offers valuable insights into the impact of CIN on QoL and underscores the need for improved preventive strategies in high-risk PCI patients.

Conclusion

In conclusion, contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) significantly impacts the quality of life in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI), with notable declines in physical functioning, pain, mobility, and overall health. Patients with CIN experienced more adverse outcomes compared to those without CIN, underscoring the need for effective prevention and management strategies. The study highlights the importance of early identification and intervention to minimize the long-term consequences of CIN. Future research with larger, multi-center, and long-term follow-up studies is necessary to further understand the full impact of CIN on patient outcomes. Improved preventive measures and clinical practices are essential to enhance patient well-being post-PCI.

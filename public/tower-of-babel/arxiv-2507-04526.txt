On the theories classified by an étendue
J. L. Wrigley

Abstract
. We give a model-theoretic characterisation of the geometric theories classified by étendues – the ‘locally localic’ topoi. They are the theories where each model is determined, syntactically and semantically, by any witness of a fixed collection of formulae. Key words and phrases. Étendue, co-ordinatisable theory, uniform rigidity. 1991 Mathematics Subject Classification. 03G30, 18F10, 18B25, 03C50.



Introduction

In this paper, we establish a logical characterisation of a class of Grothdendieck topoi called
étendues
. Let us first motivate this question by discussing a potential future application. Classically, Stone duality establishes a dual equivalence between Boolean algebras and a class of topological spaces
[
38
,
39
]
. Since Boolean algebras give algebraic semantics to classical propositional logic, the logical content of Stone duality can be described as associating a theory of classical propositional logic with a ‘space of models’. Generalising from the description of Boolean algebras as (Boolean) rings (
[
38
, §1]
),
non-commutative
Stone duality establishes a dual equivalence between
Boolean inverse semigroups
and a class of
étale topological groupoids

[
18
,
19
,
33
]
. The duality, and its extensions
[
20
,
4
]
, have found applications to
C^{*}
-algebra theory (see, for instance,
[
18
, §3]
), building on the work of
[
32
,
31
]
. The logical character of the duality, i.e. what logical theories are described by Boolean inverse semigroups, is not yet understood. This paper constitutes the first steps towards such a logical description of non-commutative Stone duality using the language of topos theory.

Every topos classifies a theory of
geometric logic
(
[
13
, §D3.1]
,
[
3
, §2.1]
), where we recall that the classifying topos of a theory
\mathbb{T}
is a topos
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
such that morphisms
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
correspond to models of
\mathbb{T}
internal to
\mathcal{F}
, or more precisely there is an equivalence of pseudofunctors
{\bf Topos}(-,\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}})\simeq\mathbb{T}\text{-}{\bf Mod}(-)
(the morphisms of topoi we consider are
geometric morphisms
,
[
13
, Definition A4.1.1]
). As the name suggests, classifying topoi are analogous to classifying spaces from algebraic topology (cf.
[
1
, §IV.2]
and
[
26
]
). Specific classes of topoi can be carved out in terms of which geometric theories they classify. The topoi of sheaves on a locale, for example, can be characterised logically as the classifying topoi for
propositional
geometric theories (
[
13
, Remark D3.1.14]
), and this can be further restricted: the topoi of sheaves on a Stone space are the classifying topoi for classical propositional theories. In order to classify first-order theories, however, it is necessary to step outside of localic topoi.

Not far beyond the topoi of sheaves on a locale, we encounter
étendues
. As expressed in
[
21
, §3]
, an étendue is a topos that is ‘locally sheaves on a locale’, or explicitly:

Definition

(§IV.9.8.2(e)
[
1
]
)
.

A topos
\mathcal{E}
is an

if there exists an object
A\in\mathcal{E}
whose support
A\to 1
is an epimorphism (we say that
A
is
inhabited
) and whose slice

is equivalent to a topos of sheaves on a locale.

Étendues relate to non-commutative Stone duality since the topos of sheaves on an étale topological groupoid is an étendue. Additionally, an important body of literature has coalesced around étendues concerning their relation to
foliations
and
orbifolds
from differential geometry
[
17
,
28
,
30
,
36
]
. It is not within the scope of the current paper to explore these latter connections, and instead the evocative account in
[
29
]
is recommended.

Given their importance in non-commutative Stone duality and other areas of topos theory, it is natural to wonder if étendues can be logically characterised in terms of which geometric theories they classify

Our characterisation falls into the well-worn logical paradigm of a syntactic characterisation of semantic phenomena. For an example of such a interplay of syntax and semantics, consider the following situation: let
\mathbb{T}
be a theory and
\varphi
a unary formula in the language, i.e. with one free variable. Gaifman’s co-ordinatisation theorem asserts that the following are equivalent:

(1)

given a pair of models
M,N\vDash\mathbb{T}
, each isomorphism of the induced substructures
\varphi(M)\cong\varphi(N)
extends to a unique isomorphism
M\cong N
(we say that
\mathbb{T}
is

over
\varphi

(2)

for any model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
, every element of

is in the

of
\varphi(M)
(see
[

(See

for a proof; as shown in

In this paper we aim to logically characterise étendues by considering similar conditions. Our characterisation theorem is the following:

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a topos and let
\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory classified by
\mathcal{E}
. The following are equivalent:

(A)

\mathbb{T}
is uniformly co-ordinatisable,

(B)

\mathbb{T}
is uniformly rigid,

(C)

\mathcal{E}
is an étendue.

Essentially, the prefix ‘uniformly’ means that, rather than each model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
being determined by the substructure
\varphi(M)

M
is determined by

\varphi(M)

n

B_{n}(\vec{x})
expressing that
\vec{x}
is an
n
-tuple of maximally linearly independent vectors.

Prerequisites and notation

By topos, we mean a Grothendieck topos. We will assume knowledge of geometric logic and classifying topos theory (textbook accounts can be found in

\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})\vdash\psi(\vec{x},\vec{z})
to mean “for all
\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{z}

\varphi(\vec{x},\vec{y})

\psi(\vec{x},\vec{z})
”. We write
A\subseteq B

1.
Uniform co-ordinatisability and rigidity

Example 1.1
.

It is useful to have a basic example to revisit when each new notion is introduced: we will use finite dimensional vector spaces over a finite field
F
. Let
\mathbb{V}_{F}
denote the classical theory of
F
-vector spaces. We can view
\mathbb{V}_{F}
as a positive theory (called a coherent theory in
[
13
]
, i.e. avoiding mention of the negation symbol) via a process known as
Morleyization
– for each positive formula
\varphi(\vec{x})
, we add a relation symbol
(\neg\varphi)(\vec{x})
and axioms expressing that
(\neg\varphi)(\vec{x})
is the negation of
\varphi(\vec{x})
(see
[
13
, Lemma D1.5.13]
). As far as geometric logic is concerned, this means we can negate any formula that does not include an infinitary disjunction.

We write
B_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
as shorthand for the formula

\textstyle\bigwedge_{i<n}(x_{i}\neq{\bf 0})\land\bigwedge_{\vec{\lambda}\in F^{n}\setminus\vec{0}}(\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x_{n}\neq{\bf 0})\\
\textstyle\land\,\neg\left(\exists y.\ (y\neq{\bf 0})\land\bigwedge_{\vec{\lambda}\in F^{n+1}\setminus\vec{0}}(\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x_{n}+\lambda_{n+1}y\neq{\bf 0})\right)

expressing that
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is a maximally linearly independent set of vectors, i.e. a basis of length
n
. The geometric theory of
finite dimensional
F
-vector spaces

\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
is given by adding to
\mathbb{V}_{F}
the geometric axiom
\top\vdash\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\exists\vec{x}.\ B_{n}(\vec{x})
.

Let
V\vDash\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
be a model that satisfies
V\vDash\exists\vec{x}.\ B_{n}(\vec{x})
, i.e.
V
is an
n
-dimensional vector space. It goes without saying that any witness
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in V^{n}
of
B_{n}(\vec{x})
determines
V
. Firstly, any other vector
v^{\prime}\in V
can be expressed syntactically in terms of
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
– in this sense,
V
is
co-ordinatised
over
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
. Secondly, given any other
W\vDash\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
with
W\vDash B_{n}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
, there is a unique isomorphism
V\cong W
that sends
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in V^{n}
pointwise to
(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in W^{n}
.

The notions of
uniform co-ordinatisability
and
uniform rigidity
capture, respectively, these two phenomena. They are strengthenings of the co-ordinatisability and relative categoricity properties discussed in
[
9
]
.

1.1.
Co-ordinatisable theories

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory and let
\Psi
be a set of geometric formulae in the language of
\mathbb{T}
(note that we allow the free variables of each
\psi(\vec{x})\in\Psi
to vary). Given a model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
, we write
\psi(M)
for the interpretation of
\psi(\vec{x})
in
M
, i.e. the set
\left\{{\vec{m}\in M^{n}}\,\middle|\,{M\vDash\psi(\vec{m})}\right\}
, and we write
\Psi(M)
for the coproduct
\coprod_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi(M)
.

Definition 1.2
.

(1)

(
[
9
, §7]
) The theory
\mathbb{T}
is
co-ordinatised
over
\Psi
if, for any model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
, every
m\in M
is in the definable closure of
\Psi(M)
, i.e. for every
m\in M
there exists a geometric formula
\varphi(\vec{x},y)
and
\vec{n}\in\Psi(M)^{n}
for which
m
is the unique element of
M
such that
M\vDash\varphi(\vec{n},m)
.

(2)

We say that
\mathbb{T}
is
uniformly co-ordinatised
over
\Psi
if, for each
\psi(\vec{x})\in\Psi
, there is a set of geometric formulae
\Theta_{\psi}
of the form
\theta(\vec{x},y)
, such that
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequents

\displaystyle\theta(\vec{x},y)

\displaystyle\vdash\psi(\vec{x}),

\displaystyle\theta(\vec{x},y)\land\theta(\vec{x},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\vdash(y=y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)

\displaystyle\vdash\textstyle\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}}\theta(\vec{x},y).

We will say that
\mathbb{T}
is
uniformly co-ordinatised
if it is uniformly co-ordinatised over some
inhabited

\Psi
, i.e. a set of formulae for which
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\top\vdash\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi}\exists\vec{x}.\ \psi(\vec{x})
.

Example 1.3
.

The theory
\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
is uniformly co-ordinatised over
\left\{{B_{n}(\vec{x})}\,\middle|\,{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, the witnessing formulae being given by
\{{B_{n}(\vec{x})\land(\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x_{n}=y)}\,|\,{\vec{\lambda}\in F^{n}}\}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Suppose that, for some
\psi^{\prime}\in\Psi
, the set
\Theta_{\psi^{\prime}}
is empty. Then
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\psi^{\prime}(\vec{x})\land(y=y)\vdash\bigvee\emptyset\equiv\bot
and so
\psi^{\prime}(\vec{x})
is
\mathbb{T}
-provably equivalent to
\bot
, i.e.
\psi^{\prime}(\vec{x})
is inconsistent with
\mathbb{T}
. Therefore,
\mathbb{T}
still proves the sequent
\top\vdash\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi\setminus\{\psi^{\prime}\}}\exists\vec{x}.\ \psi(\vec{x})
and thus we can safely assume that, for all
\psi\in\Psi
, the set
\Theta_{\psi}
is non-empty.

Remark 1.5
.

For each model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
of a uniformly co-ordinatised theory, every
m\in M
is in the definable closure of the singleton
\{\vec{n}\}
, for every
\vec{n}\in\Psi(M)
. Hence, if
\mathbb{T}
is uniformly co-ordinatised over
\Psi
, it is co-ordinatised over
\Psi
. Any theory is co-ordinatised over the formula
(x=x)
, and so clearly there are plenty of examples of theories that are co-ordinatisable but not uniformly co-ordinatisable.

Remark 1.6
.

Suppose
\mathbb{T}
is uniformly co-ordinatised over a set of geometric formulae
\Psi
, then the set of co-ordinatising formulae
\Theta_{\psi}
, for each
\psi(\vec{x})\in\Psi
, can be taken to consist only of
regular
formulae, i.e. formulae built only using the logical connectives
\{\,=,\exists,\land\,\}
(
[
13
, Definition D1.1.3]
). Given a geometric formula
\theta(\vec{x},y)\in\Theta_{\psi}
, recall from
[
13
, Lemma D1.3.8]
that, up to provable equivalence,
\theta(\vec{x},y)
can be rewritten as an infinite disjunction
\bigvee_{i\in I_{\theta}}\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y)
where each
\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y)
is a regular formula. Note that, for each
i\in I_{\theta}
, the theory
\mathbb{T}
still proves the following sequents:

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y)\vdash\theta(\vec{x},y)

\displaystyle\vdash\psi(\vec{x}),

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y)\land\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y^{\prime})\vdash\theta(\vec{x},y)\land\theta(\vec{x},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\vdash(y=y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)

\displaystyle\textstyle\vdash\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}}\theta(\vec{x},y)\equiv\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}}\bigvee_{i\in I_{\theta}}\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y),

and so the set of geometric formulae
\Theta_{\psi}
can be replaced by the set of regular formulae
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}}\left\{{\theta_{i}(\vec{x},y)}\,\middle|\,{i\in I_{\theta}}\right\}
.

Remark 1.7
.

The definition of a theory co-ordinatised over a formula
\Psi
given in
1.2
differs from the notion presented in
[
9
]
in that
1.2
refers to geometric logic, whereas
[
9
]
exists within classical first-order logic. We briefly confirm that, the definable closure of
\Psi(M)
using geometric formulae coincides with the definable closure of
\Psi(M)
using positive formulae. Given a model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
, and suppose that
m\in M
is the unique element such that
M\vDash\varphi(\vec{n},m)
, for some
\vec{n}\in\Psi(M)^{n}
and geometric formula
\varphi(\vec{x},y)
. By
[
13
, Lemma D1.3.8]
,
\varphi(\vec{x},y)
is provably equivalent to an infinite disjunction of positive formulae
\bigvee_{i\in I}\varphi_{i}(\vec{x},y)
, and so
M\vDash\varphi_{i}(\vec{n},m)
for some
i
. Given another element
m^{\prime}\in M
for which
M\vDash\varphi_{i}(\vec{n},m^{\prime})
, then clearly
M\vDash\varphi(\vec{n},m^{\prime})
too. Thus, we conclude that
m=m^{\prime}
.

Remark 1.8
.

Informally, uniform co-ordinitisation can be seen as a variant of co-ordinatisation where the quantification of clauses has been changed, in the sense that a theory
\mathbb{T}
is co-ordinatised over, for simplicity, a single unary formula
\psi(x)
if and only if:

\forall M\vDash\mathbb{T}\text{ a model}.\ \exists\,\Phi_{M}\text{ a set of geometric formulae}.\ \forall m\in M.\\
\exists\vec{n}\in\psi(M)^{n}.\ \exists\varphi(\vec{x})\in\Phi_{M}.\ M\vDash(\varphi(\vec{n},m)\land\forall y.\ (\varphi(\vec{n},y)\rightarrow y=m)),

whereas
\mathbb{T}
is uniformly co-ordinatised over
\psi(x)
only if:

\exists\,\Theta\text{ a set of binary geometric formulae}.\ \forall M\vDash\mathbb{T}\text{ a model}.\ \forall n\in\psi(M).\\
\forall m\in M.\ \exists\theta\in\Theta.\ M\vDash(\theta(n,m)\land\forall y.\ (\theta(n,y)\rightarrow y=m)).

The above ‘only if’ becomes an equivalence if we can equate provability modulo
\mathbb{T}
with satisfiability in set-based models, i.e. we assume that the geometric theory
\mathbb{T}
has
enough set-based models
(
[
3
, Definition 1.4.17]
). (This is normally known as the ‘completeness theorem’, but for infinitary logics such as geometric logic, the completeness theorem fails, see for instance
[
13
, Example C1.2.8]
.)

Remark 1.9

(cf. Proposition 7.2
[
9
]
)
.

We are restricted to
finite
model theory if we only consider positively uniformly co-ordinatised theories, that is a positive (i.e. coherent) theory
\mathbb{T}
that is uniformly co-ordinatised over an inhabited set of positive formulae
\Psi
. Since
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\top\vdash\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi}\exists\vec{x}.\ \psi(\vec{x})
, by the compactness theorem for positive logic we have that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}}\exists\vec{x}.\ \psi(\vec{x})
for some finite subset
\Psi^{\prime}\subseteq\Psi
. Similarly, as
\mathbb{T}
proves
\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)\vdash\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}}\theta(\vec{x},y)
, for each
\psi\in\Psi
, a second application of compactness gives us that
\mathbb{T}
proves
\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)\vdash\bigvee_{\theta\in\Theta^{\prime}_{\psi}}\theta(\vec{x},y)
for some finite subset
\Theta^{\prime}_{\psi}\subseteq\Theta_{\psi}
. Thus, for any set-based model
M
of
\mathbb{T}
, there must be a witness
\vec{m}\in M
for one of the finitely many formulae in
\Psi^{\prime}
, and once such a witness of
\psi\in\Psi^{\prime}
is fixed, every other element
n\in M
is the unique element satisfying
\theta(\vec{m},y)
for one of the finitely many formulae
\theta\in\Theta^{\prime}_{\psi}
. Hence, every set-based model of
\mathbb{T}
is finite (cf. also
2.14
).

1.2.
Uniformly rigid theories

Since a theory
\mathbb{T}
that is uniformly co-ordinatised over a set of formulae
\Psi
is, in particular, co-ordinatised over
\Psi
, if follows by Gaifman’s co-ordinatisation theorem that every (set-based) model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
is rigid over
\Psi(M)
. Given the special nature of a uniform co-ordinatisation, it is not too surprising that
M
is rigid in a stronger sense.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory. We say that a model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
is
uniformly rigid
over a set of geometric formulae
\Psi
if, for any element
\vec{m}\in\Psi(M)
, the only automorphism of the model
M
that fixes
\vec{m}
pointwise is the identity.

Remark 1.11
.

The notion of uniform rigidity given in
1.12
makes sense in any topos
\mathcal{F}
. Given a model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
internal to
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
13
, §1.2]
for how to interpret
\mathbb{T}
inside an arbitrary topos), we can still construct the coproduct
\Psi(M)
, and we take
\vec{m}\in\Psi(M)
to mean a
global element

\vec{m}\colon 1\to\Psi(M)
. Note that, in an arbitrary topos, such a global element need not necessarily factor through any of the coproduct inclusions
\psi(M)\hookrightarrow\Psi(M)
.

Definition 1.12
.

Thus, we say that a geometric theory
\mathbb{T}
is
uniformly rigid
over a set of geometric formulae
\Psi
if every model, in any topos, is uniformly rigid over
\Psi
. We say that
\mathbb{T}
is
uniformly rigid
, without qualification, if there exists such a set of geometric formulae which is inhabited.

Example 1.13
.

Basic linear algebra tells us that
\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
is uniformly rigid over the set of formulae
\{{B_{n}(\vec{x})}\,|\,{n\in\mathbb{N}}\}
.

Proposition 1.14
.

If
\mathbb{T}
is uniformly co-ordinatised over
\Psi
, then
\mathbb{T}
is uniformly rigid over
\Psi
.

The proof is relatively simple in the case of set-based models: given a set-based model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
and an element
\vec{m}\in\psi(M)\subseteq\Psi(M)
, for some
\psi\in\Psi
, then every other element
n\in M
is the unique element for which
M\vDash\theta(\vec{m},n)
, for some
\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}
, and so it is also fixed by any automorphism of
M
that fixes
\vec{m}
. However, we desire a version of
1.14
that is valid for models internal to any topos. Essentially, the argument does not change, but we must be more careful when reasoning element-wise.

1.14
.

Given a model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
internal to an arbitrary topos and a global element
\vec{m}\colon 1\to\Psi(M)
. For each
\psi\in\Psi
, let
U_{\psi}
denote the subterminal object given by the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coprod_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong\Psi(M).}

Since the coproduct inclusions
\psi(M)\hookrightarrow\Psi(M)
form a jointly epimorphic family of morphisms, which are stable under pullback in a topos, the subterminals

form a cover, i.e.
1=\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi}U_{\psi}
. We should think of
U_{\psi}
as the subterminal measuring the extent to which
\vec{m}
satisfies
\psi(\vec{x})
.

Next, for each
\theta\in\Theta_{\psi}
, let
\theta(\vec{m},y)
denote the pullback in the diagram

Remark 1.15
.

Before moving on, we relate uniform co-ordinatisability and uniform rigidity with relative categoricity. As always, let
\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory and
\Psi
a set of formulae. For any (set-based) model
M\vDash\mathbb{T}
, we can endow the set
\Psi(M)
with the datum of a ‘substructure’. The basic definable subsets consist of
\psi(M)\subseteq\coprod_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi(M)=\Psi(M)
, for each
\psi\in\Psi
, and the subsets
\chi(M)\subseteq\psi_{1}(M)\times\dots\times\psi_{n}(M)\subseteq\Psi(M)^{n}
for each formula for which
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent
\chi(\vec{x}_{1},\dots,\vec{x}_{n})\vdash\psi_{1}(\vec{x}_{1})\land\dots\land\psi_{n}(\vec{x}_{n})
. When
\Psi
consists of a single unary formula
\varphi(x)
, this returns the usual definition of the substructure
\varphi(M)\subseteq M
where the definable subsets are the intersections of
\varphi(M)^{n}\subseteq M^{n}
with some definable subset
\chi(M)\subseteq M^{n}
.

Recall from
[
9
, §1]
that
\mathbb{T}
is said to be
relatively categorical
over
\Psi
if, given any pair of models
M,N\vDash\mathbb{T}
, every isomorphism of the structures
\Psi(M)\cong\Psi(N)
extends to an isomorphism
M\cong N
. By
1.5
and Gaifman’s co-ordinatisation theorem, a geometric theory that is uniformly co-ordinated over
\Psi
is relatively categorical over
\Psi
. Indeed, we can easily adapt the proof given in
[
9
, Proposition 7.3]
to our setting, and thus we have that, for any geometric
\mathbb{T}
that is uniformly co-ordinatised over an inhabited set of geometric formulae
\Psi
, and every pair of set-based models
M,N\vDash\mathbb{T}
, each homomorphism between the induced substructures
f\colon\Psi(M)\to\Psi(N)
extends to a unique homomorphism of models
\underline{f}\colon M\to N
.

The homomorphism
\underline{f}
is defined as follows. Given a witness
\vec{m}\in\psi(M)\subseteq\Psi(M)
, which can be chosen since
\mathbb{T}
proves
\top\vdash\bigvee_{\psi\in\Psi}\exists\vec{x}.\ \psi(\vec{x})
, then each other
m^{\prime}\in M
is the unique element satisfying
\theta(\vec{m},y)
for some
\theta(\vec{x},y)\in\Theta_{\psi}
. Since
\Psi(M)\vDash\exists y.\ \theta(\vec{m},y)
, we have that
\Psi(N)\vDash\exists y.\ \theta(f(\vec{m}),y)
, and we define
\underline{f}(m^{\prime})
as the unique element of
N
that satisfies
\theta(f(\vec{m}),y)
. If
f\colon\Psi(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Psi(N)
is an isomorphism of the induced substructures with inverse
f^{-1}
, their unique extensions
\underline{f}\colon M\to N
and
\underline{f}^{-1}\colon N\to M
are necessarily an isomorphism of models of
\mathbb{T}
, thus proving that
\mathbb{T}
is relatively categorical over
\Psi(M)
.

(

2.1.
Étendues are unifromly co-ordinatisable

We now prove that any theory classified by an étendue is uniformly co-ordinatisable. Recall that the classifying topos
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
of a geometric theory
\mathbb{T}
contains the
syntactic category
of
\mathbb{T}
as a full subcategory, and moreover this subcategory generates the topos (see, for instance,
[
23
, §8]
). The objects of the syntactic category are given by geometric formulae in context, which we write as
\varphi(\vec{x})
, while an arrow
[\theta]\colon\varphi(\vec{x})\to\psi(\vec{y})
is labelled by a
provably functional formula
(up to provable equivalence modulo
\mathbb{T}
; see
[
13
, §D1.4]
,
[
3
, Definition 1.4.1]
), i.e. a formula
\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})
such that
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequents

\displaystyle\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})

\displaystyle\vdash\varphi(\vec{x})\land\psi(\vec{y})

\displaystyle\text{(well-definedness)},

\displaystyle\varphi(\vec{x})

\displaystyle\vdash\exists\vec{y}.\ \theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})

\displaystyle\text{(totality)},

\displaystyle\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})\land\theta(\vec{x},\vec{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\vdash\vec{y}=\vec{y}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\text{(functionality)}.

The composite of two such arrows
[\chi]\circ[\theta]
is given by the relational composite, i.e.
\exists\vec{y}.\ \theta(\vec{x},\vec{y})\land\chi(\vec{y},\vec{z})
. Given a set
\Psi
of geometric formulae, each
\psi(\vec{x})\in\Psi
yields an object of the syntactic category and hence of the classifying topos
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
. We will therefore also use
\Psi
to denote their coproduct
\coprod_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi(\vec{x})
in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
(note that this object may land outside of the syntactic category).

Proposition 2.5
.

\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory and let
\Psi
be a set of formulae. Viewing

as an object in the classifying topos

of

is localic then

is uniformly co-ordinatisable over

.

Proof.

If

is localic, then in particular the product projection
\Psi\times(y=y)\to\Psi
, considered as an object of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}/\Psi
, admits a covering by morphisms whose domains are subterminals in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}/\Psi
, i.e. subobjects of
\Psi
in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
. Here
(y=y)
, the true formula with one free variable, is considered as an object of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
, i.e.
(y=y)
is the

of the topos
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
(in the terminology of
[
13
, Definition D3.2.3]
) given by the choice of theory (see
[
13
, Theorem D3.2.5]
, and recall that we are assuming that
\mathbb{T}
is single-sorted).

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the covering of

by subterminals factors through the covering by subobjects

\textstyle\left\{{\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)\subseteq\coprod_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi(\vec{x})\land(y=y)\cong\Psi\times(y=y)}\,\middle|\,{\psi\in\Psi}\right\}.

That is, for each

, we ask for a jointly epimorphic family of morphisms

making the diagram

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a geometric theory. If the classifying topos
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{T}}
is an étendue, then there exists an inhabited set of geometric formulae
\Psi
, i.e.
\mathbb{T}
proves the sequent

, such that

is uniformly co-ordinatised over
\Psi

Proof.

Let

be an étendue over an inhabited object

. Since the objects of the form

generate the topos

.
∎

2.3.
Étendues have a small set of points

The set-based models of
\mathbb{V}_{F}^{f}
are all finite by design, but the essentially unique set-based
G
-torsor is infinite for
G
infinite. This highlights a key difference between geometric logic and (finitary) first-order logic: the upwards Löwenheim-Skolem theorem for the latter implies that a theory with an infinite model has unboundedly many. Consequently uniformly co-ordinatised theories with infinite models are somewhat unnatural from a classical model-theoretic point of view since a uniformly co-ordinatised theory only ever has a small set of set-based models, or equivalently an étendue always has a small set of points. We first give the topos-theorist’s proof of this well-known fact, before mentioning how it can be deduced logically.

Proposition 2.13
.

Any étendue has a small set of points up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an étendue over an object
A\in\mathcal{E}
. Note that, since
\mathcal{E}/A
is localic, it has a small set of points (up to isomorphism). Consequently, there is a small set of points of
\mathcal{E}
that factor through
\mathcal{E}/A\to\mathcal{E}
. We show that any point
p\colon{\bf Set}\to\mathcal{E}
factors through
\mathcal{E}/A\to\mathcal{E}
. Since surjective étale geometric morphisms are stable under pullback, we have that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Set}/Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}/A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf Set}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

is a pullback for some non-empty set
Y
, specifically
Y\cong p^{\ast}A
, and hence the morphism
{\bf Set}/Y\to{\bf Set}
admits a section, induced by any section of the support
Y\twoheadrightarrow 1
. Therefore,
p
factors as
{\bf Set}\to{\bf Set}/Y\to\mathcal{E}/A\to\mathcal{E}
.
∎

2.4.
Reducible expansions

First proved in

Definition 2.15
.

Let

(1)

(2)

for which

Let

(1)

(2)

(

Example 2.21
.

We give an explicit description of a reducible, conservative expansion to a uniformly co-ordinatisable theory in the case for the theory
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
of
infinite decidable objects
, i.e. the theory over the signature with one binary relation
\neq
and, as axioms, the sequents

\displaystyle x=x^{\prime}\land x\neq x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\vdash\bot,

\displaystyle\top

\displaystyle\vdash x=x^{\prime}\lor x\neq x^{\prime},

\displaystyle\top

\displaystyle\vdash\exists y.\ (y\neq x_{1})\land\dots\land(y\neq x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Recall that
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
is classified by the
Schanuel topos
, which can be described as the topos
{\bf B}\Omega(\mathbb{N})
of continuous actions on discrete sets by the topological group
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
of permutations of the naturals
\mathbb{N}
, viewed as an amorphic countable set, equipped with the
pointwise convergence topology
(see, for instance,
[
13
, Example D3.4.10]
). On the other hand, the topos
{\bf Set}^{\Omega(\mathbb{N})}
of all
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-actions on sets, continuous and non-continuous, classifies the geometric theory of
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-torsors (
2.10
). The inclusion of continuous
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-actions into all actions is the inverse image of a geometric morphism
{\bf Set}^{\Omega(\mathbb{N})}\to{\bf B}\Omega(\mathbb{N})
which is moreover hyperconnected, as is easily checked (see also
[
13
, p. 225]
). As it stands, this geometric morphism does not yield the theory of
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-torsors as a reducible, conservative expansion of the theory of decidable objects. We can instead replace the theory of
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-torsors with a
Morita equivalent
theory, i.e. a geometric theory with the same classifying topos.

Consider the following (single-sorted) geometric theory
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}^{+}
.

(1)

We have two unary relation symbols,
T(x)
and
D(x)
, along with the axioms

\top\vdash T(x)\lor D(x),\quad T(x)\land D(x)\vdash\bot,\quad\top\vdash\exists x.\ T(x).

(2)

For each
\sigma\in\Omega(\mathbb{N})
, we add an endomorphism
\overline{\sigma}
of
T(x)
and the axioms

T(x)\vdash\overline{e}x=x,\quad T(x)\vdash\overline{\sigma}\,\overline{\sigma}^{\prime}x=\overline{(\sigma\sigma^{\prime})}x,

T(x)\land T(y)\vdash\bigvee_{\sigma\in\Omega(\mathbb{N})}y=\overline{\sigma}x,\ \text{ and }\ \overline{\sigma}x=x\vdash\bot\ \text{for all $\sigma\in\Omega(\mathbb{N})\setminus e$.}

Thus,
T(x)
is endowed with the structure of an
\Omega(\mathbb{N})
-torsor.

(3)

We add a binary relation
\neq_{D}
and the axioms

\displaystyle x\neq_{D}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\vdash D(x)\land D(x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle D(x)\land D(x^{\prime})\land x=x^{\prime}\land x\neq_{D}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\vdash\bot,

\displaystyle D(x)\land D(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\vdash x=x^{\prime}\lor x\neq_{D}x^{\prime},

\displaystyle D(x_{1})\land\dots\land D(x_{n})

\displaystyle\vdash\exists y.\ D(y)\land(y\neq_{D}x_{1})\land\dots\land(y\neq_{D}x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, expressing that the equality predicate restricted to
D(x)\land D(x^{\prime})
is decidable and
D(x)
is infinite.

(4)

We add a function

from
T(x)
to
D(x)
along with the axioms

\displaystyle D(x)

\displaystyle\vdash\exists y.\ T(y)\land q(y)=x,

\displaystyle q(y)=q(y^{\prime})\dashv

\displaystyle\vdash T(y)\land T(y^{\prime})\land\bigvee_{\sigma\in\mathrm{stab}(0)}y=\overline{\sigma}(y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle q(y)\neq_{D}q(y^{\prime})\dashv

\displaystyle\vdash T(y)\land T(y^{\prime})\land\bigvee_{\sigma\not\in\mathrm{stab}(0)}y=\overline{\sigma}(y^{\prime}),

where
\mathrm{stab}(0)
denotes the stabilizer of the element

(since
\mathbb{N}
is amorphic, we could have chosen any element). In other words,
D(x)
is the quotient of
T(x)
by the definable equivalence relation

, and moreover the interpretation of

is fixed by this quotient.

As the interpretation of
D(x)
and

Pretreatment intratumoral mature TLSs in non-clear cell renal cell carcinoma are associated with response to immunotherapy rechallenge
Authors: Yanfeng Tang, Junru Chen, Jue Hu, Dingbang Liu, Keha Ma, Yuntian Chen, Ling Wang, Xingming Zhang, Xu Hu, Jinge Zhao, Xi Wang, Jingjing Guo, Haoyang Liu, Junjie Zhao, Zilin Wang, Nanwei Xu, Sike He, Xinyuan Wei, Yuan Wang, Zeqi Wang, Jin Yao, Ni Chen, Pengfei Shen, Zhenhua Liu, Guangxi Sun, Hao Zeng, Jiayu Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-based therapy has shown promising efficacy in patients with metastatic non-clear cell renal cell carcinoma (nccRCC) in the first-line setting, the optimal treatment following disease progression remains undefined. We aimed to investigate the efficacy, safety, and predictive markers of ICI rechallenge in this population.Methods39 patients with metastatic nccRCC who received ICI rechallenge were enrolled. Key outcomes included objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival. Whole exome sequencing, bulk RNA sequencing, and multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) were performed to further explore potential biomarkers associated with the efficacy of ICI-rechallenge among nccRCC.ResultsFollowing ICI-rechallenge, the cohort demonstrated a median PFS of 11.8 months, an ORR of 13.9% and a DCR of 69.4%. Among clinicopathological variables, the fumarate hydratase-deficient renal cell carcinoma subtype was the only factor associated with the potential clinical benefit from ICI-rechallenge. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that an enrichment of B cells and central memory T cell signatures was associated with longer PFS (21.3 vs 7.9 months). Notably, patients with a higher mature intratumoral tertiary lymphoid structure (m-iTLS) score assessed by mIF showed higher ORR (50.0% vs 0.0%) and longer PFS (28.2 vs 5.0 months) compared with those with a lower m-iTLS score. In terms of safety, ICI rechallenge was generally well tolerated, with no accumulation or treatment-related deaths observed.ConclusionsICI rechallenge may offer favorable clinical benefit and acceptable safety in patients with metastatic nccRCC. Higher density of mature iTLSs correlated with the efficacy of ICI-rechallenge but warrants further validation.

Introduction

Over the past decades, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)-based combination therapies have demonstrated a superior clinical efficacy among patients with advanced/metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma (mccRCC).15 Consequently, these combinations have been recommended as standard first-line regimens for mccRCC.6 However, a notable proportion (5.4%–18%) of patients exhibit primary resistance. Furthermore, even among those with initial response, disease progression due to acquired ICI-resistance occurs 11.2–23.1 months later. Thus, effective subsequential therapeutic strategies for patients failed from front-line ICI-based therapy need to be explored.

ICI rechallenge refers to reintroduction or switch of ICI therapy in patients who discontinued front-line ICI therapy due to progression or toxicity. Currently, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) are recommended as one of the optional regimens for ccRCC patients failed from front-line ICI therapy7 8 but with limited efficacy. The phase II KEYNOTE-146 trial, which evaluated lenvatinib combined with pembrolizumab in 104 ICI-pretreated ccRCC patients, reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 62.5% and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 12.2 months, suggesting potential utility of ICI rechallenge in this population.9 Unfortunately, two large phase III trials, CONTACT-03 and TiNivo-2, failed to demonstrate a significant survival advantage of ICI rechallenge over TKI monotherapy.8 10 However, approximately 20%–40% of patients still exhibited objective response to ICI rechallenge, highlighting a clinically meaningful subset of potential responders. This underscores a critical unmet need to identify appropriate candidates who are most likely to benefit from ICI rechallenge and potential differences between ccRCC and non-clear cell RCC (nccRCC).

nccRCC comprises a heterogeneous group of subtypes with distinct clinical characteristics and molecular pathogenesis, accounting for about 25% of all RCC cases.11 However, the management of metastatic nccRCC remains challenging due to its low incidence and significant biological diversity.1220 Recently, several prospective clinical trials12 13 21 have demonstrated promising efficacy of ICI-based combination therapy among patients with advanced/metastatic nccRCC. These findings have led to the recommendation of ICI-based combination strategies as the preferred first-line treatments for this population.6 However, evidence on the efficacy of ICI rechallenge following failure from prior ICI-based therapy in nccRCC remains sparse.

Here, we conducted a multicenter retrospective study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ICI rechallenge in patients with metastatic nccRCC and to explore optimal candidates for ICI rechallenge through multiomics profiling and multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF).

Materials and methods

Study population

This was a multicenter retrospective cohort study of patients with pathologically confirmed RCC who received ICI-based therapy between 2019 and 2024. Patients with both ccRCC and nccRCC histologies were identified from the multicenter RCC database. Patients without disease progression after front-line ICI-based therapy, those who continued treatment beyond progression, and those with insufficient follow-up were excluded. The remaining eligible patients were then categorized into the ICI rechallenge and non-rechallenge cohorts. ICI rechallenge was defined as the administration of an alternative ICI regimen following failure from or intolerance with front-line ICI-based treatment, wherein a different ICI agent was employed. The cohort assembly process is shown in online supplemental figure 1.

Definitions of treatment strategies

ICI rechallenge was defined as administration of a subsequent ICI-based regimen after radiologic progression or intolerance on front-line ICI-based therapy, in which a different ICI agent was used than in the front-line regimen. Continuous rechallenge was defined as discontinuation of front-line ICI-based therapy due to radiologic progression or intolerance, followed by immediate initiation of an ICI-based regimen incorporating a different ICI agent, with or without a change in the TKI. In contrast, intermittent rechallenge was defined as re-initiation of an ICI-based regimen after one or more intervening systemic treatment lines, most commonly TKI-based regimens, resulting in delayed re-exposure to a different ICI agent in the third or later line.

Outcomes

Treatment response was assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors V.1.1.22 The study endpoints included ORR, PFS, and overall survival (OS). ORR was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as their best radiological outcome during therapy. Disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients who achieve CR, PR, or stable disease (SD) as their best radiological outcome during therapy. PFS1 was defined as the time from initiation of front-line ICI-based therapy to documented disease progression. PFS2 was defined as the time from initiation of ICI rechallenge to documented disease progression or death. OS1 was defined as the time from initial diagnosis to death from any cause, and OS2 was defined as the time from initiation of ICI rechallenge to death from any cause.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as median and range, while categorical variables were presented as percentages. Median survival was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. HRs and 95% CIs were calculated using the Cox proportional hazards model. Differences in ORR between treatment groups were assessed using the χ² test. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to compare tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) maturity and distribution between responders and non-responders. All statistical analyses were two-sided and conducted using R software (V.4.2.3) and SPSS (V.25.0). A p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Total RNA isolation, library preparation, sequencing, and analysis

Total RNA was extracted from each sample using the RNeasy FFPE Kit (Qiagen, 73504, Hilden, Germany), following the manufacturer’s protocol as previously described.23 Ribosomal RNA was depleted using the KAPA RiboErase HMR Kit, followed by reverse transcription and library preparation with the UMI Adapter System. Whole-transcriptome sequencing was performed on the Illumina next-generation sequencing (NGS) platform. Gene expression profiles were generated for 23 patients. DEGs were identified using the R package DEseq2, with a significance threshold of p<0.05. TME-related analyses were performed using the R package IOBR.24 Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis was performed using the R package GSVA to quantify the relative enrichment of predefined gene sets in each sample.

mIF examination

Tumor specimens used for H&E staining, RNA sequencing, and mIF were archival samples obtained before initiation of first-line systemic therapy. The mIF experiments were performed by AlphaXPainter X30 (Alpha X Bio, Beijing, China) and analyzed according to three panels, in which primary antibodies were used as listed below: CD3 (Alpha X Bio, Cat#AXB1014), CD23 (ZSBIO, 1:200, Cat#ZA0516), CD20 (Abcam, 1:200, Cat#ab78237), and PanCK (ZSBIO, 1:200, Cat#ZM0069). All the primary antibodies were incubated for 1 hour at 37°C. Then slides were incubated with Alpha X Polymer HRP Ms+Rb (Alpha X Bio, Beijing, China) for 10 mins at 37°C. Alpha X 7-Color IHC Kit (AXT37100031, Alpha X Bio, Beijing, China) was used for visualization. After each staining cycle, heat-induced epitope retrieval was performed to remove all the antibodies, including primary and secondary antibodies. The slides were counter-stained with DAPI for 5 mins and enclosed in Antifade Mounting Medium (I0052; NobleRyder, Beijing, China). Images were scanned with ZEISS AXIOSCAN 7 (ZEISS, Oberkochen, Germany). Images were analyzed with HALO software (V.3.6; Indica Labs, USA).

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

From January 11, 2019 to December 1, 2024, a total of 327 patients with mRCC who received at least one ICI-based regimen were identified across participating centers in our multicenter RCC database, including 140 patients with ccRCC and 187 with nccRCC (online supplemental figure 1). Among the nccRCC cohort, 103 patients without disease progression after front-line ICI and 24 patients with treatment beyond progression or insufficient follow-up were excluded. The remaining 60 eligible nccRCC patients were further categorized into an ICI rechallenge cohort (n=39) and a non-rechallenge cohort (n=21). Among the ccRCC cohort, 67 patients had no disease progression after front-line ICI, 30 had treatment beyond progression or insufficient follow-up, 26 underwent ICI rechallenge, and 17 comprised the non-rechallenge cohort. The 39 nccRCC patients who underwent ICI rechallenge were included in the present analyses (figure 1A). The baseline characteristics of these eligible patients are summarized in online supplemental table 1. The male-to-female ratio was 2.3:1, and the median age was 42 years (range: 9–71 years). Histologically, the predominant morphological pattern was papillary (16/39, 41.0%), followed by adenoid (4/39, 10.3%) and solid patterns (4/39, 10.3%). The most common pathological subtype was fumarate hydratase-deficient RCC (FH-dRCC, 21/39, 53.8%), followed by TFE3-rearranged RCC (TFE3-rRCC, 8/39, 30.5%), papillary RCC (pRCC, 5/39, 12.8%), RCC not otherwise specified (RCC-NOS, 4/39, 10.3%), and SMARCB1-deficient medullary RCC (1/39, 2.6%). Twenty (24/39, 61.5%) patients had synchronous metastases. The most common sites of metastasis included retroperitoneal lymph nodes (24/39, 61.5%), followed by bone (15/39, 38.5%), liver (7/39, 17.9%), and lung (7/39, 17.9%).

A total of 76.9% (30/39) of patients received initial ICI-based combination therapy as first-line treatment, while seven patients received it as second-line therapy and one patient as third-line therapy. The most common regimen was axitinib plus sintilimab (24/39, 61.5%), with other regimens detailed in online supplemental table 2). Among 36 patients with evaluable lesions at front-line ICI-based therapy, 7 (7/36, 19.4%) achieved PR, 21 (21/36, 58.3%) had SD, 8 (8/36, 22.2%) experienced progressive disease (PD) as best tumor response. The median PFS 1 (mPFS1) for all 39 patients during front-line ICI therapy was 13.6 months (95% CI 9.1 to 19.2). When stratified by best response to front-line ICI, the mPFS1 was 13.6 months (95% CI 9.0 to NR) in the PR group, 12.2 months (95% CI 9.1 to 19.0) in the SD group, and 3.0 months (95% CI 2.9 to NR) in the PD group.

Of the 39 patients, 36 (92.3%) received ICI-rechallenge due to disease progression, and the remaining three due to grade 3 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) (online supplemental table 2). Decision with ICI rechallenge for all patients was made following multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion. The majority of patients underwent continuous ICI-rechallenge (34/39, 87.2%), while five patients (5/39, 12.8%) received intermittent ICI-rechallenge (figure 1B). On ICI-rechallenge, eighteen patients (18/39, 46.2%) underwent dual-switching of both the TKI and ICI agents, whereas twenty-one patients maintained the original TKI and switched only the ICI component.

Clinical outcomes of ICI rechallenge

We first compared our cohort with the non-rechallenge control group (n=21). The ICI rechallenge group demonstrated superior OS1 and OS2 compared with the control group. Additionally, a numerical improvement in PFS2 was observed, supporting a potential benefit of ICI rechallenge (online supplemental figure 2A–C). For best tumor response to ICI rechallenge, five (5/36, 13.9%) achieved PR, sixteen (20/36, 55.5%) had SD, and nine (11/36, 30.6%) experienced PD (figure 2A). The ORR was 13.9% (95% CI 6.1% to 28.7%), and the DCR was 69.4% (95% CI 53.1% to 82.0%) (figure 2B). Notably, all rechallenge PRs were observed among patients whose best response to front-line ICI-based therapy was SD (3/5) or PD (2/5) (figure 2A). Representative radiological responses in patients undergoing ICI rechallenge are illustrated in figure 2C. The median follow-up duration was 37.4 months (95% CI 21.0 to 53.9). The median PFS2 for ICI-rechallenge reached 11.8 months (95% CI 9.2 to NR; figure 2D), the median OS1 was 59.9 months (95% CI 59.0 to NR; figure 2E), and the median OS2 was not reached (95% CI 26.4 to NR; figure 2F) following ICI rechallenge. Safety for all 39 patients was evaluated. The most common TRAEs during ICI-rechallenge were proteinuria (62%), hypertriglyceridemia (51%), and increased blood creatinine level (51%). Grade ≥3 TRAEs were observed in 9 patients (23%), the most frequent being hypertriglyceridemia (5%), rash (5%), anemia (5%), and carcinomatous hydrothorax (5%). Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 6 patients (15%) during front-line ICI therapy. No statistically significant difference was observed between the two treatment periods (front-line ICI-based therapy and ICI-rechallenge therapy periods) (p=0.57; onlinesupplemental tables 3 4), suggestingno exacerbation or accumulation of toxicity. Notably, patients 10, 15, and 29 tolerated with ICI-rechallenge without TRAE-related discontinuation. No treatment-related deaths occurred.

We further conducted an exploratory comparison with a cohort of patients with ccRCC (n=26) undergoing ICI rechallenge in our center (online supplemental table 5). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed comparable PFS1 between the nccRCC and ccRCC cohorts (mPFS1: 13.6 vs11.8 months, p=0.81; online supplemental figure 2D). In contrast, ICI rechallenge was associated with a significantly longer PFS2 in nccRCC compared with ccRCC (mPFS2: 11.8 vs7.0 months, p<0.01; online supplemental figure 2F). With front-line ICI-based therapy, ORR was 30.8% (95% CI 16.5% to 50.0%) in the ccRCC cohort and 19.4% (95% CI 9.8% to 35.0%) in the nccRCC cohort. With ICI rechallenge, ORR was 7.7% (95% CI 2.1% to 24.1%) in ccRCC and 13.9% (95% CI 6.1% to 28.7%) in nccRCC (online supplemental figure 2E,G).

Subgroup analysis based on pathological type revealed differential responses to ICI (table 1). Patients with FH-dRCC were associated with a significantly longer PFS2 compared with those with other pathological types (mPFS2: 20.7 vs 5.3 months, p<0.05; figure 2G). Although the difference was not statistically significant, patients with adenoid or papillary architectures (n=20) demonstrated numerically superior PFS2 (mPFS2: 15.2 vs 9.7 months, p=0.37) compared with other morphologies (n=19). No survival difference was observed when patients were stratified according to baseline metastatic site, lines of front-line ICI-based therapy, progression pattern of front-line ICI, interval of rechallenge, type of rechallenge approach, and even PD-L1 status (online supplemental figure 3A).

Immuno-transcriptomic features related to survival outcome

Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed in 15 patients, which revealed a low tumor mutation burden (TMB) and a paucity of recurrent hotspot mutations across the cohort. No TMB levels, somatic mutations, or mutational patterns were found to be associated with clinical outcomes or response to immunotherapy. To further evaluate the correlation between molecular characteristics and treatment response, we performed bulk RNA sequencing on pretreatment tumor samples (n=23). CIBERSORT analysis revealed higher abundance of naïve B cells and resting memory CD4+T cells in the PR group (n=4) (online supplemental figure 4A). In contrast, the PD group (n=6) exhibited a tendency toward higher infiltration of M2 macrophages. Additionally, the Immunophenoscore was higher in patients who responded to ICI rechallenge, suggesting higher tumor immunogenicity of responders (online supplemental figure 4B). To validate these findings, we further explored the prognostic value of relevant immune cell signatures. We found that patients with higher B cell and central memory T cell signature scores showed significantly prolonged PFS2 compared with those with lower scores (mPFS2: 21.3 vs 7.9 months, p<0.05; figure 3A). Collectively, these findings indicate that a pre-existing immune-active tumor microenvironment (TME) characterized by T and B cell enrichment may predict potential beneficial clinical outcomes in patients undergoing ICI rechallenge.

Mature TLSs associated with favorable outcomes among patients with ICI rechallenge

B cells and T cells are major cellular components of TLSs, which resemble and function similarly to secondary lymphoid organs (SLOs).25 The presence of TLSs is frequently associated with favorable response to immunotherapy.2629 We first examined the association between TLS characteristics and front-line ICI efficacy using H&E-stained whole-slide images, and the total TLS count showed no association with PFS1. To assess the impact of spatial distribution, TLSs were classified as intratumoral or peritumoral, and patients were categorized into low-density and high-density groups based on median cutoffs (figure 3B). While independent analysis of intratumoral or peritumoral density yielded only numerical trends, a higher intratumoral-to-peritumoral TLS density score was significantly associated with prolonged PFS1 (mPFS1: 16.2 vs 7.4 months; p=0.013; online supplemental figure 5). Consistently, the high-score group achieved a higher DCR than the low-score group (83.3% (95% CI 55.2% to 95.3%) vs 61.5% (95% CI 35.5% to 82.3%)). However, this spatial metric failed to correlate with outcomes in the ICI rechallenge setting.

Recently, an increasing number of studies have indicated that the maturation of TLS plays a critical role in its immunoregulatory functions.2931 We therefore used mIF to assess TLS maturation status in our cohort (online supplemental table 6). Mature TLS and early TLS were identified in both intratumoral and peritumoral regions (figure 3C). Notably, ICI-rechallenge responders exhibited a higher proportion of mature intratumoral TLSs (m-iTLSs; p=0.0049) and a lower proportion of early iTLSs (e-iTLSs; p=0.027) compared with patients in the PD group (figure 3D–E). Patients were subsequently divided into two groups based on their intratumoral maturation scores. Those with high scores (m-iTLShi) achieved better ORR compared with those with low scores (m-iTLSlo, 50.0% (95% CI 21.5% to 78.5%) vs 0.0% (95% CI 0.0% to 27.8%), p<0.001; figure 3F). Furthermore, the m-iTLShi group exhibited significantly longer PFS2 after ICI rechallenge compared with the m-iTLSlo group (mPFS2: 28.2 vs 5.0 months, p<0.001; figure 3G).

To determine whether the predictive value of m-iTLS was confounded by histologic subtype, particularly FH-dRCC, we conducted exploratory multivariable analyses. In a Firth penalized Cox regression model that accounted for the limited sample size, a high iTLS maturation score remained independently associated with longer PFS2 (HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.08 to 0.95; p=0.039). In contrast, FH status was not independently associated with PFS2 (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.21 to 1.22; p=0.128). These results support iTLS maturation score as a candidate biomarker for ICI rechallenge with predictive value beyond histologic classification.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first retrospective cohort investigating ICI rechallenge in patients with metastatic nccRCC. We observed potential clinical benefits, with an ORR of 13.9%, a DCR of 69.4%, and a median PFS2 of 11.8 months. Immune profiling revealed that responders harbored higher levels of naïve B cells, and elevated B cell and central memory T cell scores correlated with prolonged PFS2. Furthermore, higher intratumoral maturation scores evaluated by mIF were associated with better outcomes following ICI rechallenge. These findings suggest that ICI rechallenge can offer favorable efficacy and acceptable safety in nccRCC, and that the localization and maturation of TLSs may serve as potential predictive biomarkers for treatment benefits from ICI rechallenge.

The management of metastatic nccRCC remains particularly challenging due to the rarity and heterogeneity of the disease. While growing evidence has demonstrated encouraging efficacy of first-line ICI-based combination therapies in advanced or metastatic nccRCC,12 13 21 32 33 the optimal treatment strategy following disease progression remains undefined. A retrospective study by Fujita et al reported a median PFS of only 5 months with second-line TKI therapy following ICI-based combination therapy in metastatic nccRCC,34 whereas our study demonstrated a longer PFS of 11.8 months with ICI rechallenge in a similar population. We also conducted a comparative analysis using a non-rechallenge control group. The significantly superior OS observed in the rechallenge group provides supportive evidence for a clinical benefit associated with ICI rechallenge. Furthermore, the safety profile of ICI rechallenge was acceptable. Notably, in our cohort, three patients underwent ICI rechallenge despite having previously discontinued front-line ICI therapy due to grade 3 adverse events. Although these patients experienced clinical benefits without a recurrence of prior toxicities following MDT consultation, this high-risk approach should be undertaken with extreme caution in clinical practice.

In this study, we sought to identify predictive markers of benefits from ICI rechallenge by integrating clinicopathological characteristics and multiomics analysis. Current evidence regarding the impact of front-line therapeutic response on the efficacy of subsequent ICI remains contentious.3537 In our cohort, a notable 17.2% of patients who did not respond to front-line therapy achieved a PR during ICI rechallenge. Two potential mechanisms may explain this observation. In patients who achieved a front-line PR, initial ICI exposure may exert selective pressure, preferentially eliminating immunogenic clones while promoting the emergence of immune-refractory subclones that are less responsive to subsequent PD-1 or PD-L1 blockade. In contrast, in patients with front-line SD, a broader clonal repertoire may persist and remain targetable when treatment is modified.38 Under this framework, adding an additional checkpoint target or switching to another agent may enable clinical benefit on rechallenge. Besides, differences in epitope engagement and binding affinity across PD-1 inhibitors may also contribute differential responses.39 Among clinicopathological factors, only the FH-dRCC subtype was significantly associated with improved outcomes. This result corroborates previous findings that FH-dRCC is characterized by a highly inflamed TME,40 41 which may confer greater clinical benefit from ICI-based therapy regardless of the treatment line. However, not all FH-dRCC patients responded to ICI rechallenge, and some non-FH-dRCC cases also derived benefit, highlighting the limitations of using histology alone to guide treatment decisions. Additionally, we found that tumors with adenoid and papillary morphological patterns were associated with numerically longer PFS2 compared with those with other types. Nevertheless, the predictive role of tumor morphological patterns remains controversial.21 32

To explore this further, we performed RNA-seq and observed that enrichment of B cells and central memory T cell signatures correlated with prolonged PFS2. This prompted us to investigate TLSs, characterized by a core of CD20+B cells surrounded by a mantle zone of CD3+T cells.25 Our study revealed that mature iTLSs were significantly associated with better clinical outcomes following ICI rechallenge. Consistently, Vanhersecke et al previously reported that the presence of mature TLSs predicted favorable outcomes in multiple cancer types, independent of PD-L1 expression status and CD8+ T cell infiltration.29 While recent studies have highlighted the prognostic value of TLS distribution and maturity in ccRCC,4245 literature on TLSs in the heterogeneous TME of nccRCC remains scarce. Our work, therefore, is the first to demonstrate the predictive value of mature iTLSs for therapeutic benefit in this specific context. From a translational perspective, obtaining high-quality secondary biopsy specimens (re-biopsy) on disease progression or acquired resistance is clinically challenging, invasive, and frequently confounded by extensive tumor necrosis or fibrosis. Our findings demonstrate that baseline TLS is not merely a snapshot of the treatment-naive state, but rather a potential surrogate for the underlying ‘immune fitness’ of the tumor.

Our study has several limitations. First, the cohort is significantly enriched for FH-dRCC. While our institution serves as a major regional hub for complex renal malignancies, leading to a high concentration of rare nccRCC cases, these results may not fully represent common nccRCC subtypes. This underscores the need for further validation in histologically diverse populations. Second, rechallenge regimens were heterogeneous because approximately half of the patients switched their TKI agent alongside the ICI. The intrinsic retrospective design precludes our ability to definitively decouple the relative therapeutic contributions of TKI vs anti-PD-1/PD-L1 switching. Though exploratory analyses identified no independent association between the switching strategy and PFS2, the HR favored the dual-switch approach. This suggests a potential further clinical advantage of TKI switch on the basis of ICI rechallenge that may have been underpowered in our current cohort, and these findings should be interpreted with caution until validated in larger datasets. Third, TLS evaluation was performed on archival tumor specimens obtained before initiation of systemic therapy rather than immediately prior to ICI rechallenge. Therefore, these baseline tissue features may not fully reflect the tumor immune microenvironment at rechallenge. Finally, despite being the largest nccRCC ICI-rechallenge cohort to date, the small sample size reflects the clinical rarity of these tumors. These observations should therefore be considered preliminary and require confirmation in larger prospective datasets.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrated that ICI rechallenge showed a manageable safety profile and promising efficacy in selected patients with metastatic nccRCC. The iTLS maturation score emerged as a candidate biomarker associated with clinical benefit from ICI rechallenge in this setting. These findings warrant validation in larger prospective studies with standardized TLS assessment to clarify their utility for refining patient selection for subsequent immunotherapy strategies.

Polynomization of Sun’s Conjecture
Bernhard Heim, Markus Neuhauser

Abstract
. Let p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) denote the number of partitions of a natural number n n . As n → ∞ n\to\infty , the n n th root of p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) tends to 1 1 , which is related to the Cauchy–Hadamard test for power series. Andrews also discovered an elementary proof. Sun conjectured that this happens in a certain way for n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 : p ⁡ ( n ) n > p ⁡ ( n + 1 ) n + 1 . \sqrt[n]{p(n)}>\sqrt[n+1]{p(n+1)}. The conjecture was proved by Wang and Zhu; shortly thereafter, Chen and Zheng independently obtained a second proof. In this paper, we follow an approach by Rota. We consider p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) as special values of the D’Arcais polynomials, known as the Nekrasov–Okounkov polynomials. This identifies Sun’s conjecture as a property of the largest real zero of certain polynomials. This leads to results towards k k -coloured partitions, overpartitions, and plane partitions. Moreover, we also consider Chebyshev and Laguerre polynomials. The main purpose of this paper is to offer a uniform approach.

The conjecture has been proven by Wang and Zhu

“The one contribution of mine that I hope will be remembered

This is now called the critical problem.”

encodes the inequality (

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic properties of sequences

Let
\{\alpha(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive real numbers.
We consider roots and quotients of sequence members. This has already been
a successful approach in analysis finding the radius of convergence in the associated power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha(n)\,t^{n}
:

R_{\alpha}(n):=\sqrt[n]{\alpha(n)}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,Q_{\alpha}(n):=\frac{\alpha(n)}{\alpha(n-1)}.

Then we obtain for
n\geq 1
:

(2.1)

R_{\alpha}\left(n\right)>Q_{\alpha}\left(n\right)\Leftrightarrow R_{\alpha}\left(n-1\right)>R_{\alpha}\left(n\right)\Leftrightarrow R_{\alpha}\left(n-1\right)>Q_{\alpha}\left(n\right).

A sequence is
called log-concave for
n\geq n_{0}
if

\alpha(n)^{2}-\alpha(n-1)\alpha(n+1)\geq 0

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. It is
called strictly log-concave if the inequality is strict.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let the sequence
\{\alpha(n)\}_{n}
of positive real numbers be log-concave
for all
n\geq n_{0}
for
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the initial condition

(2.2)

R_{\alpha}\left(n_{0}-1\right)>R_{\alpha}\left(n_{0}\right)

implies that the sequence
\{\sqrt[n]{\alpha(n)}\}_{n=n_{0}-1}^{\infty}
is strictly decreasing.

Proof.

A sequence
\{\alpha(n)\}_{n}
is log-concave at
n
if
and only if

Q_{\alpha}(n)\geq Q_{\alpha}(n+1)
. From the general property (
2.1
), we observe that the initial condition is equal to
R_{\alpha}(n_{0})>Q_{\alpha}(n_{0})
. And applying again (
2.1
) leads to
R_{\alpha}(n_{0})>R_{\alpha}(n_{0}+1)
. The proof can be finished
applying mathematical induction.
∎

If we consider
1/\alpha(n)
, we obtain similar results for log-convex sequences. Wang and Zhu first observed that the monotonicity
of
quotients implies the monotonicity of the roots stated in (
[

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
g\left(n\right)=\psi_{0}\left(n\right)=1
for all
n

\Delta_{n}^{\psi_{0}}(x)\,=\,\left(P_{n}^{\psi_{0}}\left(x\right)\right)^{n+1}-\left(P_{n+1}^{\psi_{0}}\left(x\right)\right)^{n}

Proof.

We obtain

\Delta_{n}^{\psi_{0}}\left(x\right)=\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x+k}{k+1}\right)^{n+1}-\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n}\frac{x+k}{k+1}\right)^{n}=\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x+k}{k+1}\right)^{n}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x+k}{k+1}-\left(\frac{x+n}{n+1}\right)^{n}\right).

0,-1,\ldots,1-n

each
n
fold and the zeros of

We consider the double sequence
\{N_{\ell}(n)\}

Let
\ell,n\geq 1

C_{\ell,n}:=\left\{\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{\ell})\in S_{n}^{\ell}\,:\,\pi_{j}\pi_{k}=\pi_{k}\pi_{j}\text{ for }1\leq j,k\leq\ell\right\}.

Each

corresponds to a group homomorphism from

\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
to

then define

N_{\ell}(n):=\frac{|C_{\ell,n}|}{n!}=\frac{\left|\mathop{\rm Hom}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\ell},S_{n}\right)\right|}{n!}\in\mathbb{N},\text{ with }N_{\ell}(0):=1.

Dey
[
De65
]
and Wohlfahrt
[
Wo77
]

offer a compelling
recursive approach
determining
the number of homomorphisms
n!\,N_{G}(n):=\left|\mathop{\rm Hom}\left(G,S_{n}\right)\right|

of a finitely generated group
G
into
S_{n}
. Let

G

with index
n
.
Notably, there has been

by Lubotzky and Segal
[

]

studying the growth of these numbers in

(6.1)

N_{G}(n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{G}(k)\,N_{G}(n-k),

.

. Then

and

.

]

[

]

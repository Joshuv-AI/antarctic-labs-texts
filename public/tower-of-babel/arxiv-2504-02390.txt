2.
Flag geometry on the Heisenberg group

We are interested in the (noncommutative) Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which we identify with

\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The group multiplication is given by

(w,s)(z,t)=(w+z,s+t+\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(w\cdot\bar{z}))

for all
(w,s),(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
The Heisenberg group admits dilations
\delta_{r}:(z,t)\mapsto(rz,r^{2}t)
that are group automorphisms, and its homogeneous dimension
Q
is equal to
2n+2
, that is,
|\delta_{r}E|=r^{Q}|E|
, where
|E|
is the measure of a measurable subset
E
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
When the details of the multiplication are not important, we may abbreviate elements of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
to
g
.
Thus, convolution on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by

f\ast_{{(1)}}f^{\prime}(g^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(g)f^{\prime}(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\,dg=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(g^{\prime}g)f^{\prime}(g^{-1})\,dg\qquad\forall g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

If
f
lives on the centre
G_{2}:=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we integrate over
G_{2}
only:

\displaystyle f\ast_{{}^{(2)}}f^{\prime}(g^{\prime})=\int_{G_{2}}f(g_{2})f^{\prime}(g_{2}^{-1}g^{\prime})\,dg_{2}\qquad\forall g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

\displaystyle f^{\prime}\ast_{{}^{(2)}}f(g^{\prime})=\int_{G_{2}}f^{\prime}(g^{\prime}g_{2})f(g_{2}^{-1})\,dg_{2}\qquad\forall g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

The Lie algebra of left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is spanned by the fields

\mathcal{X}_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}-\tfrac{1}{2}y_{j}\partial_{t},\qquad\mathcal{Y}_{j}=\partial_{y_{j}}+\tfrac{1}{2}x_{j}\partial_{t},\qquad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}=\partial_{t};

the nontrivial commutators of these are determined by the relations
[\mathcal{X}_{j},\mathcal{Y}_{j}]=\mathcal{T}
for all
j
.
The fields
\mathcal{X}_{j}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{j}
are called horizontal, while
\mathcal{T}
is called vertical.
The horizontal (or subriemannian) gradient is then

\nabla_{(1)}=(\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{n},\mathcal{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Y}_{n}),

and the associated sublaplacian
\Delta_{(1)}
is defined to be
-\sum_{j}\left(\mathcal{X}_{j}^{2}+\mathcal{Y}_{j}^{2}\right)
(the sign makes it a positive operator).
To make the notation consistent, we also write

\nabla_{(2)}=\mathcal{T}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_{(2)}=-\mathcal{T}^{2}.

The vertical vector field
\mathcal{T}=\partial_{t}
is a commutator of horizontal vector fields, so two horizontal derivatives control one vertical derivative.

Much of what we write may be extended to more general stratified groups, but more complicated notation is needed.

The basic geometrical objects on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are the Korányi balls

B^{(1)}(o,r):=\bigl\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{1/4}<r\bigr\},

and the basic objects in the centre are intervals

B^{(2)}(0,s):=\bigl\{(0,t)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:|t|<s\bigr\}.

The basic objects in flag geometry are “tubes”
T
, or
T(g,r,s)
, which are sets of the form

gB^{(1)}(0,r)B^{(2)}(0,s).

The tube
T
has
width

\mathrm{w}(T)
equal to
r
,
height

\mathrm{h}(T)
equal to
r^{2}+s
, and centre
\mathrm{c}(T)
equal to
g
.
We write
2T
for the tube

gB^{(1)}(0,2r)B^{(2)}(0,4s),

which is a dilated version of
T
.
If
s<r^{2}
, then
T(g,r,0)\subset T(g,r,s)\subset T(g,\sqrt{2}r,0)
, and these three tubes are comparable in size.
We normally assume that
s\geq r^{2}
.

In
[
4
]
, we also use objects that we call shards, which are fractal versions of dyadic rectangles, and have better disjointness and nestedness properties than tubes.
These properties are useful for proving an appropriate version of Journé’s lemma (see
[
7
,
9
]
).
The existence of shards depends on the existence of discrete cocompact subgroups in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which do not exist in general stratified groups, and we do not use them here.

3.
The atomic Hardy space

Let
T
be a tube in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
We say that a function
a_{T}
is a
particle associated to
T
if
a_{T}
is supported in
2T
and certain
cancellation conditions
hold; we define and discuss these below.
Let
U
be an open set of finite measure
|U|
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and
\mathfrak{T}(U)
be the set of tubes
T
contained in
U
.
We say that a function
a
is an
atom associated to
U
if
a=\sum_{T\in\mathfrak{T}(U)}a_{T}
, where, for all choices of sign functions
\sigma:\mathfrak{T}(U)\to\{\pm 1\}
,

\biggl\|\sum_{T\in\mathfrak{T}(U)}\sigma(T)a_{T}\biggr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq|U|^{-1/2}.

This condition implies the usual square condition that is imposed on particles, but in the general context of stratified groups, where analogues of dyadic rectangles do not exist, it is still utilisable, while the square function condition relies on having some kind of disjointness or nestedness.

In particular, a particle
a_{T}
is an atom if and only if

(1)

|T|^{1/2}\left\|a_{T}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq 1.

We say that a function
f
belongs to the
flag Hardy space
H_{F}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if and only if we may write

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
,
where all
a_{j}
are atoms and all
\lambda_{j}
are in
\mathbb{C}
, and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
.
The atomic norm of
f
is the infimum of all sums
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|
over all such representations of
f
.

The main theorem of
[
4
]
shows that the Hardy space
H^{1}_{F}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
may be characterised in various ways, in particular, by

•

boundedness of certain singular integrals (in particular, double Riesz transforms);

•

boundedness of certain maximal functions (in particular, radial and nontangential versions);

•

boundedness of certain square functions (with discrete or continuous parameters);

•

boundedness of certain area functions.

All these characterisations involve showing that the boundedness of some operator
\mathcal{A}
implies the existence of an atomic decomposition of the function (which is arguably the nontrivial part of the result) and showing that the same operator
\mathcal{A}
is bounded from
H_{F}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
; this involves working with particles
a_{T}
and estimating
\mathcal{A}a_{T}
.
The estimates for the particles are put together to obtain estimates for atoms using the flag version of Journé’s lemma.

4.
cancellation

At a conference in honour of Guido Weiss in 1990, Elias M. Stein discussed various problems connected with Hardy spaces, and suggested that the particles for the flag Hardy space might be the convolution product
a^{{(1)}}*_{{(2)}}a^{{(2)}}
of an atom
a^{(1)}
for the Folland–Stein–Christ–Geller Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

[
6
,
3
]
and an atom
a^{(2)}
for the classical Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}

[
5
]
.
There atoms are characterised by size conditions, support conditions and cancellation conditions.

More precisely, take
a^{(1)}
supported in
B^{(1)}(g,r)
of mean
0
,
and
a^{(2)}
supported in
B^{(2)}(0,h)
of mean
0
,
such that

\left\|a^{(1)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq|B^{(1)}(g,r)|^{-1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\|a^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq|B^{(2)}(0,h)|^{-1/2};

then
a:=a^{(1)}*_{(2)}a^{(2)}
should be a normalised particle associated to
T:=T(g,r,h)
.

We investigate and prove a version of this conjecture.
Deciding whether a function
a
supported in a tube
T
admits a factorisation as a convolution product
a^{{(1)}}*_{{(2)}}a^{{(2)}}
seems to be hard, so we need to look for alternative descriptions.

Since
a_{2}
has mean
0
, we may write
a_{2}
as a derivative:
a_{2}=\nabla_{(2)}b_{2}
, where

\left\|b_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq h|B^{(2)}(0,h)|^{-1/2}.

Hence
a_{1}\ast_{(2)}a_{2}
is also a derivative:
a_{1}\ast_{(2)}a_{2}=\nabla_{(2)}(a_{1}\ast_{(2)}b_{2})
.
Further,
a_{1}\ast_{(2)}b_{2}
has mean
0
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
because
a_{1}
has mean
0
.

Hence we consider the following potential cancellation condition:

(2)

\displaystyle a=\nabla_{{(2)}}A\quad\text{where $\operatorname{supp}(A)\subseteq T$ \ and \ $\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}A(g)\,dg=0$};

since
A
is the integral of
a
along vertical lines, it is easy to check that

|T|^{1/2}\left\|A\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim h,

and
A
satisfies an appropriate size condition.

Now we consider a family of such functions
a_{h}
, for
h\geq 1
, given by

a_{h}(z,t)=h^{-1}a(z,t/h),

where
a(z,t)=a_{1}(z)a_{2}(t)
,
a_{1}\geq 0
,
\operatorname{supp}(a_{1})\subseteq B^{(1)}(o,1)
and
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}a_{1}(z)\,dz\neq 0
, while
a_{2}\neq 0
,
\operatorname{supp}(a_{2})\subseteq B^{(2)}(0,1)
and the zeroth and first moments of
a_{2}
vanish.
If (
2
) were the “right” cancellation condition, then the
a_{h}
would be uniformly bounded multiples of atoms.
We consider the convolution
a_{h}\ast_{(1)}k
of
a
with a singular integral kernel
k
, of the form

k(z,t)=\omega(z)/(|z|^{4}+|t|^{2})^{-(2n+3)/4},

where
\omega
is smooth, homogeneous of degree
0
and has mean
0
on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, so that
k
is smooth, homogeneous of degree
-(2n+2)
and has mean
0
on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
We would expect that
\left\|a_{h}\ast_{{(1)}}k\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
is uniformly bounded in
h
.
However, this is not so.

Indeed, if
(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\operatorname{supp}(a_{h})
and
|z|>1
, then
|z-z^{\prime}|
is bounded below, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\,h\,k(z-z^{\prime},ht-ht^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{z}))\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\frac{h\omega(z-z^{\prime})}{(|z-z^{\prime}|^{4}+|h(t-t^{\prime})-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{z})|^{2})^{(2n+2)/4}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\to a(z^{\prime},t)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\omega(z-z^{\prime})}{(|z-z^{\prime}|^{4}+|t^{\prime}|^{2})^{(2n+2)/4}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=a(z^{\prime},t)\frac{d_{n}\omega(z-z^{\prime})}{|z-z^{\prime}|^{2n}}

\displaystyle\qquad=:\tilde{k}(z)\,,

say, where
d_{n}
is an appropriate nonzero constant
d_{n}
and
\tilde{k}
is a classical singular integral kernel on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Hence, as
h\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\iint_{B(0,1)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}}|a_{h}\ast_{{(1)}}\,k(z,t)|\,dt\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad=\iint_{B(0,1)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}}\left|\iint_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}}a(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\,h\,k(z-z^{\prime},ht-ht^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Im}(z^{\prime}\cdot\bar{z}))\,dz\,dt^{\prime}\right|\,dt\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad\to\iint_{B(0,1)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}a(z^{\prime},t)\,\tilde{k}(z-z^{\prime})\,dz\right|\,dt\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad=\iint_{B(0,1)^{c}\times\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}a_{1}(z^{\prime})a_{2}(t)\,\tilde{k}(z-z^{\prime})\,dz\right|\,dt\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad=\infty.

This establishes that
\left\|a_{h}\ast_{{(1)}}k\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
is
not
uniformly bounded in
h
, and so our putative cancellation condition does not work.

However, writing
a
as
a^{(1)}\ast_{(1)}a^{(2)}
, where
a^{(1)}
and
a^{(2)}
have mean
0
and are supported in
B^{(1)}(g,r)
and
B^{(2)}(0,h)
does work,

provided that

a^{(1)}=\nabla_{(1)}\cdot A^{(1)}
and
a^{(2)}=\nabla_{(2)}A^{(2)}
, where

\left\|A^{(1)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim r\left\|a^{(1)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\|A^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim h\left\|a^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})};

if this is so
, then

(3)

a=\nabla_{(1)}\cdot{}\nabla_{(2)}A,

where
\operatorname{supp}(A)\subseteq T
, and precursor estimates hold:

(4)

\left\|(\nabla_{(1)}\cdot{})^{i}\nabla_{(2)}^{j}A\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim r^{1-i}(r^{2}+h)^{1-j}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

when
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.
We consider
a\ast_{{(1)}}k
, where
k
is a flag singular integral kernel, given by

(5)

k=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,,

where
\varphi^{(1)}_{s}
and
\varphi^{(2)}_{t}
are normalised dilates of
\varphi^{(1)}
and
\varphi^{(2)}
respectively, and
\varphi^{(1)}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
\varphi^{(2)}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{(1)})\subseteq B^{(1)}(o,1)
and
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{(2)})\subseteq B^{(2)}(0,1)
, and
\varphi^{(1)}
and
\varphi^{(2)}
have mean
0
.

If
R
and
S
are dyadic rectangles and
R\subseteq S
, then we define

(6)

\displaystyle\rho(R,S):=\frac{\mathrm{w}(R)}{\mathrm{w}(S)}+\frac{\mathrm{h}(R)}{\mathrm{h}(S)}\,.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that singular integral kernel
k
is as in (
5
) above.
Then for each particle
a
associated to a tube
T
, and for each tube
S\supset 2T
,

\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k(g)|\,dg\lesssim_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\rho(R,S)|R|^{1/2}\left\|a_{T}\right\|_{L^{2}(G)}.

Proof.

We suppose that (
3
) and (
4
) hold and that
A
is supported in
T
.
By translation and dilation invariance, we may and shall suppose that
\mathrm{c}(T)=o
,
\mathrm{w}(T)=1
and
\mathrm{h}(T)=h>1
; we may and shall also suppose that
\mathrm{c}(S)=o
,
\mathrm{w}(S)=w^{*}>1
and
\mathrm{h}(S)=h^{*}>h
.

We partition
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
into four regions
\Omega^{j}
, given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}:r\leq 1,s\leq h\}\qquad

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}:r\leq 1,s>h\}

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}:r>1,s\leq h\}

\displaystyle\Omega_{4}

\displaystyle=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}:r>1,s>h\},

and we write
a\ast_{(1)}k=a\ast_{(1)}k^{1}+a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}+a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}+a\ast_{(1)}k^{4}
, where

k^{i}:=\iint_{\Omega^{i}}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

We treat the four summands
a\ast_{(1)}k^{i}
separately.
The key to our estimation is the fact that

(7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\bigl(a\ast_{(1)}\varphi_{r,s}\bigr)\subseteq T(o,1,h)T(o,r,s)=T(o,r+1,s+h).

First, by definition,
\operatorname{supp}(a\ast_{(1)}k^{1})\subseteq 2S
, and there is nothing to consider; in any case,
a\ast_{(1)}k^{1}\in L^{1}(G)
.

Second, to treat
a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}
, we observe that if
a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)\neq 0
, where
g\in S^{c}
and
(s,t)\in\Omega^{2}
, then
h^{*}\leq h+s+1\leq h+2
, and so

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}(g)|\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{c}}\left|\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right|\,dg.

Recall that
a=\nabla_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A)
, and write

\displaystyle a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla_{{(2)}}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A))\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A)\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\,\frac{ds}{s}\right)\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}c^{2}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,,

say, where

c^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A)\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,.

Now
\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A
is supported in
T
, so
c^{2}
is supported in
T^{*}
, say, where
|T^{*}|\eqsim|T|
.
Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality and one-parameter singular integral estimates (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
),

\left\|c^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim|T|^{1/2}\left\|c^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim_{\varphi^{(1)}}|T|^{1/2}\left\|\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot A\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\lesssim h|T|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k^{2}(g)|\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{c}}\left|\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}c^{2}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}(g)\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\right|\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|c^{2}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}(g)|\,dg\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\left\|c^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\eqsim_{\varphi^{(2)}}\frac{1}{h^{*}}\left\|c^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varphi^{(1)}}\frac{h}{h^{*}}|R|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Third, to treat
a\ast_{(1)}k^{3}
, we argue similarly.
If
a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)\neq 0
, where
g\in S^{c}
and
(s,t)\in\Omega^{3}
, then
w^{*}\leq s+1
, whence

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k^{3}(g)|\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{c}}\left|\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{h}^{\infty}a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\right|\,dg.

Now we write

\displaystyle a\ast_{(1)}k^{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{h}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\cdot(\nabla_{{(2)}}A))\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}c^{3}\ast_{(1)}(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}\,,

say, where

c^{3}=\int_{0}^{h}(\nabla_{{(2)}}A)\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k^{3}(g)|\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{c}}\left|\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}c^{3}\ast_{(1)}(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}(g)\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}\right|\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|c^{3}\ast_{(1)}(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}(g)|\,dg\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varphi^{(1)}}\frac{1}{w^{*}}\left\|c^{3}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varphi^{(2)}}\frac{1}{w^{*}}|R|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Last, we treat
a\ast_{(1)}k^{4}
.
If
a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)\neq 0
, where
g\in S^{c}
and
(s,t)\in\Omega^{4}
, then either
s+1\geq w^{*}
, or
s+1<w^{*}
and
t+s^{2}+h\geq h^{*}
, whence

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k^{4}(g)|\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S^{c}}\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{h}^{\infty}|a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,dg

\displaystyle+\int_{S^{c}}\int_{1}^{w^{*}}\int_{h^{*}}^{\infty}|a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,dg.

Next,

\displaystyle a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{st}A\ast_{(1)}(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\,.

By estimating much as before, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{h}^{\infty}|a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{h}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|A\ast_{(1)}(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}(g)|\,dg\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{w^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{h}^{\infty}\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|(\accentset{\leftharpoonup}{\nabla}_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varphi^{(1)},\varphi^{{(2)}}}\frac{1}{hw^{*}}\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{w^{*}}|R|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

while

\displaystyle\int_{S^{c}}\int_{1}^{w^{*}}\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}|a\ast_{(1)}\varphi^{(1)}_{s}\ast_{(2)}\varphi^{(2)}_{t}(g)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\,\frac{ds}{s}\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|A\ast_{(1)}(\nabla_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\ast_{(2)}(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}(g)|\,dg\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{h^{*}/2}^{\infty}\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|(\nabla_{{(1)}}\varphi^{(1)})_{s}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|(\nabla_{{(2)}}\varphi^{(2)})_{t}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\,\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\,\frac{ds}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varphi^{(1)},\varphi^{(2)}}\frac{1}{h^{*}}\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{h}{h^{*}}|R|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

In conclusion,

\int_{(S^{*})^{c}}|a\ast_{(1)}k(g)|\,dg\lesssim_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\rho(R,S)|R|^{1/2}\left\|a\right\|_{L^{2}(G)},

as required.

5.
Conclusion

Hardy spaces have been applied to establish delicate estimates in PDE.
This little paper shows that an understanding of PDE (of a more geometric flavour) is needed to understand Hardy spaces.

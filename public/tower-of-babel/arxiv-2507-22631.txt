Irreducibility of polarized automorphic Galois representations in infinitely many dimensions
Zachary Feng, Dmitri Whitmore

Abstract
. Let π \pi be a polarized, regular algebraic, cuspidal automorphic representation of GL n ⁡ ( 𝔸 F ) \operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) where F F is totally real or imaginary CM, and let ( ρ λ ) λ (\rho_{\lambda})_{\lambda} be its associated compatible system of Galois representations. We prove that if 7 ∤ n 7\nmid n and 4 ∤ n 4\nmid n then there is a Dirichlet density 1 1 set of rational primes ℒ \mathcal{L} such that whenever λ | ℓ \lambda\mid\ell for some ℓ ∈ ℒ \ell\in\mathcal{L} , then ρ λ \rho_{\lambda} is irreducible.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview and main result

It is a prediction of the Langlands program that if we can attach a
\ell
-adic Galois representation
\rho
to a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
for a number field
F
, then
\rho
must be irreducible. For a general number field
F
, we do not know how to construct their associated Galois representations, let alone determine their irreducibility. However, if
F
is totally real or imaginary CM, and
\pi
is polarizable and regular algebraic, then there is a well-known geometric construction due to the work of many authors, including but not limited to: Bellaiche, Caraiani, Chenevier, Clozel, Harris, Kottwitz, Labesse, Shin, and Taylor (see
[
BLGGT14
, Theorem 2.1.1]
for a concise reference). Without the polarized assumption, two constructions are known due to
[
HLTT16
]
and
[
Sch15
]
.
Letting
(\rho_{\lambda})
be the compatible system of Galois representations attached to
\pi
, where
\rho_{\lambda}:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{M_{\pi,\lambda}})
and
M_{\pi}
is a number field, it is then of interest to show that these Galois representations are irreducible.

Let
P=\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}\mid\ell\text{ prime}\}
. Results on irreducibility typically demonstrate the existence of a subset
\mathcal{L}
of rational primes (possibly depending on
\pi
), where
\mathcal{L}
satisfies one of the following properties, such that whenever
\lambda\mid\ell
for some
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
, then
\rho_{\lambda}
is irreducible.

(1)

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}=\{\mathcal{L}\subset P\mid\mathcal{L}\text{ is infinite}\}

(2)

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{>0}=\{\mathcal{L}\subset P\mid\mathcal{L}\text{ has positive Dirichlet density}\}

(3)

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{\mathcal{L}\subset P\mid\mathcal{L}\text{ has Dirichlet density }1\}

(4)

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{aa}=\{\mathcal{L}\subset P\mid P\setminus\mathcal{L}\text{ is finite}\}

(5)

\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{a}=\{P\}

Returning to the polarized setting, although the result of
[
BLGGT14
]
is the first to give an irreducibility criteria for general
n
, it is important to note that the “extremely regular weight” hypothesis of
[
BLGGT14
]
is often not satisfied in practice. Nevertheless, the strategy of
[
BLGGT14
]
– which combines a powerful potential automorphy theorem, results of
[
LP92
]
, and an analytic argument involving
L
-functions – is integral to subsequent approaches to this problem appearing in
[
PT15
,
Xia19
,
Hui23
]
. However, these successive attempts to weaken the extremely regular weight assumption (to just regular) have incurred significant trade-offs in one way or another, by restricting to either
n\leq 6
or
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{>0}
.

The main innovation of this paper is that we replace the extremely regular weight assumption with regular, while maintaining
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
under only a mild divisibility condition on
n
(we allow any
n
such that
7\nmid n
and
4\nmid n
). In particular, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be totally real or imaginary CM. Let
(\pi,\chi)
be a polarized regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
(\rho_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
be its associated compatible system of Galois representations. Suppose that
7\nmid n
and
4\nmid n
.
Then there exists a Dirichlet density
1
set of rational primes
\mathcal{L}
such that if
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
and
\lambda\mid\ell
, then
\rho_{\lambda}
is irreducible.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and a density one result for residual irreducibility of summands of regular, weakly compatible systems proved in
[
BLGGT14
]
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
, there exists a Dirichlet density
1
set of rational primes
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
such that if
\ell\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
and
\lambda\mid\ell
, then the residual representation
\overline{\rho_{\lambda}}|_{G_{F(\zeta_{\ell})}}
is irreducible.

1.2.
Summary of our methods

We now explain our overall strategy. Let
\pi
be a polarized regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
where
F
is either totally real or imaginary CM. Let
(\rho_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
denote its associated compatible system of Galois representations. A reduction step of Xia (cf.
Section

3
) tells us that there exists
m\mid n
and a polarized regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi_{1}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F_{1}})
(where
F_{1}
is imaginary CM) such that if
(\rho_{1,\lambda})_{\lambda}
is the compatible system associated to
\pi_{1}
, then the absolute irreducibility of
\rho_{1,\lambda}
and
\rho_{\lambda}
are equivalent, and moreover at a positive Dirichlet density set of primes
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
(which is a subset of the positive Dirichlet density subset of primes given by
[
PT15
]
) in fact
\rho_{1,\lambda}
is strongly irreducible. There is also a finite Galois extension
E_{1}/F_{1}^{+}
(independent of
\lambda
) such that
G_{\rho_{1,\lambda}}|_{G_{E_{1}}}
is connected (
G_{\rho}
being the Zariski closure of the image of a representation
\rho
) and such that
F_{1}/F_{1}^{+}
is the maximal CM subextension of
E_{1}/F_{1}^{+}
. We can therefore replace
\pi
with
\pi_{1}
and
suppose that
F/F^{+}
is maximal CM subextension of
E/F^{+}
, where
E/F
is a finite extension such that
G_{\rho_{\lambda}}|_{G_{E}}
is connected.

The next step, carried out in Section
4
, is to show that at a place
\lambda_{0}
dividing a prime in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, there exists a tensor product decomposition
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}\cong\otimes_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i}
, where
k
is the number of simple factors of
\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\rho_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
and
\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\rho_{i}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
is simple for each
i=1,\dots,k
. We give an overview of how this is done. First, we show that there is a decomposition
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}|_{G_{E}}\cong\otimes_{i}\rho_{i}^{\prime}
(recalling that
G_{\rho_{\lambda_{0}}|_{G_{E}}}
is connected) which is unique up to reordering and twisting by characters, and such that each
\operatorname{Lie}G_{\rho_{i}^{\prime}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
is simple.
Importantly, we can take each
\rho_{i}^{\prime}
to be geometric, using results of
[
Pat19
]
.
This gives us a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{GL}_{n_{i}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i}\rho_{i}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{\lambda_{0}}|_{G_{E}}}

where
\kappa
is the Kronecker product of matrices. So we have shown that
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}(G_{E})\subset\operatorname{im}(\kappa)
. Our goal now is to show that actually
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}(G_{F})\subset\operatorname{im}(\kappa)
, which more or less gives us the claim.

Fix a complex conjugation
c\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and let
[c]
denote its
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
-conjugacy class. Since
F/F^{+}
is the maximal CM subextension of
E/F^{+}
, the group
\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)
is generated by the set
S=\{(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})|_{E}:\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in[c]\}\subset\operatorname{Gal}(E/F^{+})
. Therefore, it suffices to show that for all
\gamma\in S
, any lift
\widetilde{\gamma}\in G_{F}
of
\gamma
satisfies
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}(\widetilde{\gamma})\in\operatorname{im}(\kappa)
.
By an argument involving Schur’s lemma, it further suffices to show that if
\sigma\in[c]
, then

(1.1)

(\rho_{i}^{\prime})^{\sigma}\cong(\rho_{i}^{\prime})^{\vee}\otimes\chi_{\sigma,i}

(for some character
\chi_{\sigma,i}
), since for any lift
\widetilde{\gamma}\in G_{F}
of
\gamma\in S
, we would have
(\rho_{i}^{\prime})^{\widetilde{\gamma}}\cong\rho_{i}^{\prime}\otimes\varphi_{\gamma,i}
(for some character
\varphi_{\gamma,i}
).

Establishing (
1.1
) is one of the key technical results of this paper, with our argument inspired by the proofs in
[
Xia19
, §7]
.
While we do not know a priori whether each
\rho_{i}
is pure, we can at least show (by considering the de Rham character
\det\rho_{i}
) that for almost all
v
: if
\alpha
is an eigenvalue of
\rho_{i}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})
, then
\alpha^{n}
is a
q_{v}
-Weil number. We apply this observation at a prime
v
such that
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}|_{G_{E}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})
has distinct
n
th powers of eigenvalues and accordingly we adjoin certain
n
th powers to the CM field of definition
M_{\pi}
. This comes at the expense of possibly shrinking
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to a smaller (but still positive density) subset, and replacing
\lambda_{0}
with another prime lying above a prime in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. The condition on the eigenvalues of
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}|_{G_{E}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})
then forces the conjugate of
\rho_{i}^{\prime}
under the action of (an extension of) complex conjugation on coefficients to be a twist of
(\rho_{i}^{\prime})^{\vee}
. The isomorphism (
1.1
) now follows as a consequence of regularity of
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}
and our choice of
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
.

At this point, we have a tensor product decomposition
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}\cong\otimes_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i}
as above. We can apply potential automorphy to the collection
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
to obtain a finite imaginary CM extension
F^{\prime}/F
and polarized regular algebraic cupsidal automorphic representations
\pi_{i}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}})
, together with their associated compatible systems
(\rho_{\pi_{i},\lambda})_{\lambda}
satisfying
\rho_{\pi_{i},\lambda_{0}}\cong\rho_{i}|_{G_{F^{\prime}}}
(we note that the new compatible systems might be defined over an enlarged coefficient field, but we are ignoring this technicality for now). It is not difficult to show (by Goursat’s lemma and the fact that the rank of
G_{\rho_{\lambda}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
is independent of
\lambda
) that if for each
i
, we can show the (strong) irreducibility of
(\rho_{\pi_{i},\lambda})_{\lambda}
at a Dirichlet density
1
set of primes, then the same must be true for
(\rho_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
.

We can therefore replace
\pi
with any of the
\pi_{i}
going forward, and henceforth assume there exists
\lambda_{0}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}:=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\rho_{\lambda_{0}}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
is a simple Lie algebra, and
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}
is strongly irreducible. In Section
5
, we iterate through the possibilities for simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
on a case-by-case basis. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}:=\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\rho_{\lambda}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
for another place
\lambda
. Let
t_{\lambda_{0}}:\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
t_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
be their induced faithful representations. By using a combination of techniques, some of which we describe below, we can show that
\rho_{\lambda}
(and hence
t_{\lambda}
) is irreducible for
\lambda\mid\ell\in\mathcal{L}
in a Dirichlet density
1
set of rational primes
\mathcal{L}
.

The first key tool is the potential automorphy theorem of
[
BLGGT14
]
.
With a suitable choice of
\mathcal{L}
, we can guarantee that every irreducible summand
\sigma
of
\rho_{\lambda}
which is moreover polarizable, is potentially automorphic, and therefore forms a part of a compatible system of Galois representations.
If, in addition, one finds that polarizability (and hence potential automorphy) is satisfied by every irreducible summand of
\rho_{\lambda}
, then an argument of
[
BLGGT14
]
using
L
-functions implies that
\rho_{\lambda}
must be irreducible.
However, it often suffices to find the existence of a single such summand
\sigma
, due to properties of formal characters in compatible systems, which we now recall.

Building on the work of Serre,
[
Hui13
, Theorem 3.19]
states that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
have the same rank, and that the induced representations
t_{\lambda_{0}}
and
t_{\lambda}
have the same formal character. Moreover, since
\rho_{\lambda_{0}}
has regular Hodge-Tate weights, the representation
t_{\lambda_{0}}
must be multiplicity-free.
This multiplicity-freeness plays two important roles, the first being that it greatly simplifies the list of possibilities for
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
and
t_{\lambda_{0}}
(see Theorem
2.21
).
The second consequence is that we obtain useful criteria for the existence of polarizable summands of the Galois representation.
It is proven in
[
Xia19
]
that the set
W=\{w+cw:w\text{ a weight of }t_{\lambda}\}
is, in a suitable sense, independent of
\lambda
(see §
5.2
for a precise statement).
If
0\in W
, then for every
\lambda
we can find a weight
w
for which
cw=-w
.
Multiplicity-freeness implies both that there is a unique summand
\sigma
of
\rho_{\lambda}
admitting
w
as a weight, and that this summand must be polarizable.

Other important tools that we exploit, which we will recall in
Section

5.1
, come from Chun-Yin Hui’s thesis
[
Hui13
]
. Hui proves that every semisimple Lie algebra contains an equal rank subalgebra consisting of only type
A
simple factors and that if two faithful representations
\mathfrak{g}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
have the same formal character, then
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
must have the same number of type
A_{k}
factors for
k\geq 9
and
k=6
. Therefore, if
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda_{0}}\leq\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\leq\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
are choices of type
A
equal rank subalgebras, in which case clearly the induced faithful representations
t_{\lambda_{0}}:\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
t_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
still have the same formal character, then
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
must have the same number of type
A_{k}
factors for
k
sufficiently large. This is especially useful in the cases when
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}=\mathfrak{sl}_{k}
for
k\geq 9
, for example. When
t_{\lambda_{0}}
and
t_{\lambda}
have the same formal character, we can define inner products on
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
(where
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}}
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}}
are the weight lattices of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, respectively) which are moreover isometric. This isometric structure is used to deal with the case where
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
.

1.3.
Limitations

Theorem
1.1
makes two divisibility assumptions on
n
, for separate reasons.
The case that
4\mid n
is excluded because we can no longer verify oddness of the tensorands
\rho_{i}
, a necessary ingredient for applying potential automorphy theorems.
(A similar issue occurs in
[
CEG22
]
, where the case of even
n>2
is consequently excluded from their main theorem.)
It is possible that this hypothesis could be weakened (say to
8\nmid n
), if one is willing to perform a similar case analysis in situations where the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}
is not necessarily simple, but at least has a small number of simple factors - a problem we intend to study in a future version of this article.
The other assumption, that
7\nmid n
, seems to be more serious.
This is because of the case where
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, the exceptional rank
2
Lie algebra, with
t_{\lambda_{0}}
its unique
7
-dimensional irreducible representation.
Indeed,
t_{\lambda_{0}}
has the same formal character as the representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
acting via
\operatorname{Std}\oplus\operatorname{Std}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbf{1}
, which is self-dual but does not have self-dual irreducible constituents (as can be seen from restricting
t_{\lambda_{0}}
to a copy of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{2}
).
Existing potential automorphy theorems seem to be insufficient to handle the possibility of such non-polarizable summands.

One could ask whether the conclusion that
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
could be improved to
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{P}_{aa}
.
This also appears to be challenging at present, because of our reliance on the density one result of
[
BLGGT14
]
showing residual irreducibility of irreducible summands, which seems tricky to avoid in handling certain cases.
This issue was surmountable in
[
Hui23
]
since when
n\leq 6
, it is shown that if
\rho_{\lambda}
is reducible then there must typically exist a summand with type
A
image, whence the images in characteristics zero and
\ell
are closely related for almost all
\ell
(cf.
[
Hui23
, Theorem 3.12]
).
For larger
n
, there are many possibilities for irreducible representations with the same formal character as a reducible representation whose irreducible summands are not of type
A
.

Notation

We fix the following notation throughout the paper.
Given a perfect field
F
with algebraic closure
\overline{F}
, we write
G_{F}
for the absolute Galois group of
F
.
We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and, for each prime number

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
of

together with an abstract isomorphism
\iota_{\ell}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}
.
We fix an inclusion

.
If

, then we can view the decomposition group at

, as a subgroup of
G_{F}
via
\iota_{\ell}^{-1}\circ j
.
We let

denote a lift of the (geometric) Frobenius element.

Let
F
be a characteristic zero field.
By an (
\ell
-adic) Galois representation of
F
, we mean a continuous homomorphism
\rho:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
.
We let
\overline{\rho}:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
denote the semisimplification of the mod-
\ell
reduction of
\rho
.
We let
\epsilon_{\ell}:G_{F}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
denote the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic character.
If
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
, we let
{}^{\sigma}\rho
denote the composition of
\rho
with the automorphism of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
induced by
\sigma
.
If
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{F})

, let
\rho^{\gamma}
denote the composition of the automorphism of

induced by
\gamma
with
\rho
.
More generally, if

is an intermediate extension and
\overline{\gamma}\in\operatorname{Aut}(K)
with

, then let
\rho^{\overline{\gamma}}=\rho^{\gamma}
for some choice of extension

of

Let
F
be a number field and
\rho:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

.
For each embedding

, let
\operatorname{HT}_{\tau}(\rho)

.

If
F_{v}

\ell
), then we write
\operatorname{WD}(\rho)=(r,N)
for the associated Weil-Deligne representation, and
\operatorname{WD}(\rho)^{F-ss}
for its Frobenius semisimplification. If
\pi_{v}
is an irreducible smooth representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, we write
\operatorname{rec}_{v}^{T}(\pi)
for the Weil-Deligne representation associated to
\pi_{v}
under the normalized local Langlands correspondence of
[
HT01
]
.

We call a number field
F
a

if

has an automorphism

such that for all embeddings

one has
c\circ i=i\circ c
. It follows that either
F
is totally real, or
F
is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field. In the second case, we may also say that

.

2.
Representation-theoretic preliminaries

2.1.
Weakly compatible systems

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
and
M
be number fields.
A weakly compatible system of
n
-dimensional representations of
K
, defined over
M
and unramified outside of
S
a finite set of places of
K
, is a collection of Galois representations

(\rho_{\lambda}:G_{K}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{M_{\lambda}}))_{\lambda}

indexed by finite places
\lambda
of
M
satisfying the following properties.

(1)

For each
\lambda|\ell
, the representation
\rho_{\lambda}
is unramified outside of
S\cup\{v|\ell\}
.

(2)

For each
\lambda|\ell
and
v\not\in S\cup\{v|\ell\}
, the characteristic polynomial

Q_{v,\lambda}(X):=\det(1-\rho_{\lambda}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})X)

lies in
M[X]
.
If additionally
\lambda^{\prime}|\ell^{\prime}
and
v\nmid\ell^{\prime}
, then we have equalities of characteristic polynomials

Q_{v,\lambda}(X)=Q_{v,\lambda^{\prime}}(X).

(3)

Each
\rho_{\lambda}
is de Rham at every

Let

Definition 2.7
.

We say that

We call a polarized

\epsilon_{v}=1

of

Suppose that

and

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose that
F
is an imaginary CM field.
Let
\rho:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
and
\sigma:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
be polarizable, irreducible Galois representations.

(1)

If

is odd, then
\rho
is totally odd.

(2)

If

is irreducible and two of

are totally odd, then all three are totally odd.

Proof.

The first part is a special case of
[
CEG22
, Lemma 1.5.3]
.
For the second part, note that the polarization pairings associated to

and

are unique (up to scalars) by Schur’s lemma.
It follows that the multiplier character on

Since total oddness is equivalent to the multiplier character sending each complex conjugation to

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\Omega

(1)

Suppose firstly that
\Omega

(resp.

-conjugacy.

(2)

In general, if

is the formal character of

.

(3)

We say that the formal character (resp. bi-character) of

is
multiplicity-free
if the corresponding representation of
T
(resp.
T\cap G^{\operatorname{der}}
) is isomorphic to a direct sum of pairwise distinct characters.

Let

be a non-empty collection of subgroups with each
\Omega_{i}
a field.
The collection is said to have the same formal character (resp. bi-character) if there exists a diagonal
\mathbb{Z}
-subtorus

(resp. chain of
\mathbb{Z}
-subtori

for every

.

Let

be a number field and let

Let

denote the Zariski closure of the image of

in
\operatorname{GL}_{n,\overline{M_{\lambda}}}
.
We refer to the formal (bi-)character of

as the formal (bi-)character

of

Let

be a polarized, regular algebraic, cuspidal automorphic representation of

In this section we reduce the proof of

to proving

Theorem 3.3

4.
Tensor product factorisation

We fix some notation which will be in place throughout this section.
Let
(\pi,\chi)
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.3

We recall from

3
that we will view the weakly compatible system attached to
(\pi,\chi)
as defined over a sufficiently large CM field

By assumption,

is the maximal CM subfield of a certain finite extension

We fix a finite extension

We recall the positive density set

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E
be a number field.
Let
\rho:G_{E}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
be a strongly irreducible representation such that the following conditions hold.

(1)

\rho
is unramified outside of finitely many places.

(2)

There exists an isomorphism of semisimple Lie algebras
\operatorname{Lie}G_{\rho}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}\cong\prod_{i\in I}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, where

is a finite index set and, for

is a semisimple Lie algebra, under which the representation of
\operatorname{Lie}G_{\rho}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{n}

\boxtimes_{i\in I}V_{i}
where, for

is a faithful irreducible representation of

(3)

\rho(G_{E})\subset\mathrm{im}(\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{GL}_{n_{i}}\xrightarrow{\otimes}\operatorname{GL}_{n})(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

\otimes

\boxtimes_{i\in I}V_{i}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{n}

, unramified outside finitely many places, such that

inducing isomorphisms
\operatorname{Lie}G_{\rho_{i}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

are uniquely defined, up to twisting by characters.

Suppose in addition that the following conditions hold.

(4)

\rho
is de Rham (resp. crystalline) at each place
v|\ell
of
E
.

(5)

\rho\cong r|_{G_{E}}
for some polarizable Galois representation

where
E^{\mathrm{CM}}
, the maximal CM subfield of

Then we can additionally take each
\rho_{i}
to be de Rham (resp. crystalline) at each place

.

Proof.

The first part of the proposition follows from
[
CEG22
, Lemma 3.2.13]
and
[
Con11
, Proposition 5.3]
(with the former reference not making use of their assumption that the number field

is imaginary CM).
The proof of uniqueness is the same as that of
[
CEG22
, Lemma 3.2.14]
.

Suppose now that
\rho
is also de Rham and a restriction of a polarizable representation
r
of

.
We may therefore apply
[

, Theorem 3.2.10]
to the homomorphism
\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{GL}_{n_{i}}\to\mathrm{im}(\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{GL}_{n_{i}}\xrightarrow{\otimes}\operatorname{GL}_{n})

∎

Using Proposition
4.1
, we will write
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}|_{G_{F_{1,\pi}}}
as a tensor product of de Rham Galois representations for suitably chosen
\lambda
.
In order to obtain a similar decomposition of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
, we will show that the action of

on these tensorands is trivial.
If we knew that these Galois representations were both: (1) defined over a number field, and (2) pure, then we could firstly show (using the Chebotarev density theorem) that the dual of each tensorand is a twist of the complex conjugated (on coefficients) tensorand (cf.
[
PT15
, Lemma 1.2(1)]

Using polarizability and regularity of

together with our choice of

indeed acts trivially on the tensorands (by an argument similar to the proof of
[

Unfortunately, we do not know (a priori) that these tensorands are pure.
Instead, we will use the existence of a sufficiently generic element in the image of

together with purity of Hodge–Tate characters to establish that the dual of each tensorand is a twist of the complex conjugated tensorand.
This motivates what follows below.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a number field.
Let
\rho:G_{E}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
be a Galois representation unramified outside of a finite set

with regular Hodge–Tate weights.
Then there exists a positive Dirichlet density set of primes

such that, letting
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
denote the eigenvalues of
\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})^{\mathrm{ss}}
, we have for every
i\neq j

\big(\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha_{j}}\big)^{n}\neq 1.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:G_{\rho}^{\circ}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}
be the natural representation arising from
\rho
.
Consider the
n
-fold tensor product representation,
\varphi^{\otimes n}:G_{\rho}^{\circ}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n^{n}}
.
Suppose

is such that

is
\Gamma
-regular in the sense of
[
LP92
, Definition 4.5]
(with respect to the faithful quotient of

through which

factors).
As the formal character of

is multiplicity-free by Lemma

, we see that

is regular semisimple and that the

Since

is open, the result now follows from combining Chebotarev density with

applied to

∎

Let

be an algebraically closed field.
Given (multi-)sets

In the case that

is a singleton, we let

denote

.
Define an equivalence relation

on such multi-sets by requiring

if and only if there exists

such that

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
C
be a non-empty finite set of elements of
\Omega^{\times}
.
Then there exists only finitely many inequivalent decompositions of the form

C=A_{1}\ldots A_{k},

with each
\#A_{i}>1
.
Here two decompositions
C=A_{1}\ldots A_{k}
and
C=B_{1}\ldots B_{l}
are said to be equivalent if

and, after rearranging, we have
A_{i}\sim B_{i}
for every
i
.

Proof.

By induction on
\#C
, it suffices to show that there are only finitely many inequivalent decompositions of the form
C=AB,
with
\#A=a
and
\#B=b
fixed.
Let
\sigma:\{1,\ldots,a\}\times\{1,\ldots,b\}\to C
be a bijection.
Consider the system of equations
x_{i}y_{j}=\sigma(i,j)\in\Omega^{\times}
for
1\leq i\leq a
and
1\leq j\leq b
.
If

and
y_{j}=\beta_{j}
is a solution, then this solution is uniquely determined by
\alpha_{1}
, because
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{1}\frac{\sigma(i,1)}{\sigma(1,1)}
and
\beta_{j}=\frac{\sigma(1,j)}{\alpha_{1}}
.
Hence if

and

is a second solution, then
\{\alpha_{i}\}=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}\{\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\}
and similarly
\{\beta_{j}\}\sim\{\beta_{j}^{\prime}\}
.
Since the data of a decomposition

and

uniquely determine a bijection

, which in turn determines

and

.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
be a semisimple Lie algebra and let
\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathfrak{h}
be a semisimple subalgebra such that the induced map
\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is surjective for every
1\leq i\leq k
.
If there is an equality of ranks
\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{f}=\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}
, then there is an equality of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
, first proving the lemma in the case where
k=2
(since the case
k=1
is obvious).
A version of Goursat’s lemma for Lie algebras states that there exists ideals
I_{i}\leq\mathfrak{h}_{i}
and an isomorphism
\varphi:\mathfrak{h}_{1}/I_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{h}_{2}/I_{2}
such that

\mathfrak{f}=\{(x,y)\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}\times\mathfrak{h}_{2}:\varphi(x+I_{1})=y+I_{2}\}.

Since each
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is semisimple, each ideal
I_{i}
is a semisimple subalgebra and direct product factor of
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
.
There is a short exact sequence

0\to I_{1}\times I_{2}\to\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{h}_{1}/I_{1}\to 0,

from which we see that
\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}_{1}+\operatorname{rk}I_{2}=\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{f}=\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}_{1}+\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}_{2}
.
We must therefore have
\mathfrak{h}_{2}=I_{2}
and the result in this case now easily follows.

Now suppose that
k>2
.
Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
and let
\pi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
denote the natural projection.
Let
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\pi(\mathfrak{f})
.
Then the map
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is surjective for every
1\leq i\leq k-1
and we have

\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{f}-\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h}_{k}\leq\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{f^{\prime}}\leq\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{h^{\prime}},

so equalities must hold throughout.
By induction, we can therefore apply the lemma to
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
to deduce that
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.
We see that
\mathfrak{f}
surjects onto both
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{k}
, so applying the lemma once again, we see that
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{h}
, which completes the proof.
∎

5.1.
Equalities of formal characters in type A

We begin by recalling some notions regarding formal characters of Lie algebra representations, following
[
Hui13
, §2]
.
Suppose that we have, for
i=1,2
, complex semisimple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
n
-dimensional faithful representations
V_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.
We can choose Cartan subalgebras
\mathfrak{t}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and decompose
V_{i}|_{\mathfrak{t}_{i}}
to obtain the character of
V_{i}
.
We call the corresponding element of
\mathbb{Z}[\Lambda_{i}]
, where
\Lambda_{i}
is the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, the
formal character
of the representation
\mathfrak{g}_{i}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V_{i})
.
The representations
V_{i}
are said to have the same formal character if there is an isomorphism
F:\mathfrak{t}_{1}^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{t}_{2}^{*}
sending the character of
V_{1}
to the character of
V_{2}
. If
\rho:G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is a continuous representation, and
T^{\prime}\subset T\subset\operatorname{GL}_{n}
is the formal bi-character of
G_{\rho}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, then
\operatorname{Lie}(T^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_{n})
is the formal character of the induced representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{\rho}
in this setting.

If
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[\Lambda_{1}]
is the formal character of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we can define an inner product on
(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathbb{R})^{*}
by setting

(x_{1},x_{2})_{1}=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}(x_{1})\alpha_{j}(x_{2}).

Let
\langle-,-\rangle_{1}
denote the dual inner product on
(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathbb{R})
.
Similarly, we obtain an inner product
\langle-,-\rangle_{2}
on
\Lambda_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
Since
\langle-,-\rangle_{i}
is invariant with respect to the action of the Weyl group
W_{\mathfrak{g}_{i}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, this inner product respects the decomposition of the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
into irreducible root systems and restricts to the unique (up to scalar) Weyl group invariant inner product on the weight space of each simple factor of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

Suppose from now on that the formal characters of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are the same.
The isomorphism
F
induces an isometry of the above inner product spaces.
Moreover, if
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda_{1}
are weights appearing in the character of
V_{1}
then

(5.1)

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{1}=\langle F(\alpha),F(\beta)\rangle_{2}.

In order to constrain the possibilities for
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
in terms of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, Hui reduces to the case where each
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is of type
A
, using the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
Hui13
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra then
\mathfrak{g}
contains a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
of the same rank such that every simple factor of
\mathfrak{h}
is of type
A
.
Moreover, for

or

, the number of simple factors of type

of

and

coincide.

We note that the statement of
[

, Lemma 2.5]
contains a small mistake, in that

states that the number of

factors of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
must coincide, but this doesn’t hold for
\mathfrak{sl}_{5}^{2}\subset\mathfrak{e}_{8}
, for example (with only the parity of the number of

factors preserved in general).

Lemma

allows one to assume that each

is of type

, since restricting a representation to a subalgebra of the same rank yields an isomorphism of formal characters.
Via an intricate analysis of the possibilities for the inner products and norms of roots of

, Hui proves the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
Hui13
, Theorems 2.14, 2.17]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
) be a semisimple complex Lie algebra of type
A
and let
V
(resp.
V^{\prime}
) be a faithful representation of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
).
Suppose that
V
and

have the same formal characters.
Let

(resp.
a_{n}^{\prime}
) denote the number of type

factors of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
).
Then

and
a_{n}=a_{n}^{\prime}
for

or
n\geq 9
.

The following result constitutes part of the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
Hui13
, §2.3]
)
.

Suppose that we are in the setup of Theorem
5.2
with
\mathfrak{g}
simple of type
A_{n}
.
If
n\in\{4,5,6\}
or
n\geq 9
, then
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
.
Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n+1}
denote the weights of the standard representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, which span
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
and are subject to the relation
\sum_{i}e_{i}=0
.
Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be a
W_{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant inner product on
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
, normalised so that
\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle=\frac{n}{n+1}
.
We then have formulas (
[
Hui13
, §2.7]
)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}-\frac{1}{n+1}

(5.3)

\displaystyle||\sum_{i}a_{i}e_{i}||^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}na_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i\neq j}a_{i}a_{j}}{n+1}.

In the following two lemmas, let
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
V
be the irreducible representation
\Lambda^{a}\operatorname{Std}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, for some
1\leq a\leq n
.

(1)

The weights of
V
(with multiplicity) are given by

\mathcal{S}_{V}=\{\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}|I\subset[n+1],\#I=a\}.

(2)

Each
v\in\mathcal{S}_{V}
satisfies
||v||^{2}=\frac{a(n+1-a)}{n+1}
.

(3)

If
v\in\mathcal{S}_{V}
then

\displaystyle\max_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{V}\setminus\{v\}}\langle u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=||v||^{2}-1,

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{V}\setminus\{v\}}\langle u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=||v||^{2}-\min(a,n+1-a).

Proof.

The first part is standard.
Let
I,J\subset[n+1]
and set
u=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}
and
v=\sum_{j\in J}e_{j}
.
Let
k=\#(I\cap J)
.
Then
\langle u,v\rangle=\frac{nk-(a^{2}-k)}{n+1}=\frac{(n+1)k-a^{2}}{n+1}
by (
5.3
).
The second part follows and we see that
\langle u,v\rangle=||v||^{2}-(a-k)
.
Fixing
v
and varying
u
, we see that
k
may take any integer value in
[\max(0,2a-n-1),a]
, from which the final part now follows.
∎

The next lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
V
be the irreducible representation
\operatorname{Sym}^{a}\operatorname{Std}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, for some

.

(1)

The weights of
V
(with multiplicity) are given by

\mathcal{S}_{V}=\{\sum_{i\in[a]}e_{f(i)}|f:[a]\to[n+1]\}.

(2)

The set
\mathcal{S}_{V}
has

elements of maximal norm, given by

for

.

The following lemma will allow us to prove a variant of Lemma
5.3
for every

and a large class of

.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{q}_{i}
be a semisimple Lie algebra, with each
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
simple of type
A_{n_{i}}
.
Let
r
denote the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
V
be a faithful representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be any
W_{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant inner product on
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
(giving rise to a norm
\left\lVert\,\cdot\,\right\rVert
).
Let
W_{\max}
denote the set of weights of
V
of maximal norm.
If
W_{\max}
spans
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
, then
\#W_{\max}\geq r+1
, with equality holding possibly only if
k=1
.

Proof.

By our assumptions on
W_{\mathrm{max}}
we can choose a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that for each
I\in\mathcal{P}
there exists an irreducible summand
U_{I}\leq V
such that
U_{I}\cong\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}U_{I,i}
with
U_{I,i}
non-trivial for
i\in I
and
U_{I}
admitting a weight in
W_{\max}
.
If
i\in I
, then
U_{I,i}
admits at least
n_{i}+1
weights of norm maximal with respect to the restriction of
||\cdot||
to
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
(since the size of the orbit of any non-zero weight of
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
under the action of
W_{\mathfrak{q}_{i}}\cong S_{n_{i}+1}
is given by a non-trivial multinomial coefficient, and is hence at least

).
It follows that

\#W_{\max}\geq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{P}}\prod_{i\in I}(1+n_{i})\geq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{P}}(1+\sum_{i\in I}n_{i})\geq r+1,

with equality holding throughout only if

.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that we are in the setup of Theorem

.

with

.

, with the dual case being similar.
The representation

admits exactly

weights of maximal norm with respect to

.
We see from (

) that

must also have exactly

∎

5.2.
Formal characters and complex conjugation

Suppose, throughout the rest of this section, that we are in the setup of Theorem
4.8
.
Let
\rho=\rho_{\pi,\lambda_{0}}
satisfying
\rho^{c}\cong\rho^{\vee}\otimes\rho_{\chi}|_{G_{F}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Lie}G_{\rho_{\lambda}}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda_{0}}

Let

on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{n}

for

by Lemma

Let

be a positive density set of rational primes as in Theorem

We recall a result of
[

]
about the formal characters of weakly compatible systems and the action of complex conjugation, applicable to our setup.
As in
[
Xia19
, §8]
, choose a certain abelian, semisimple, weakly compatible system

of

defined over

Define an auxiliary weakly compatible system

(\beta_{\lambda}:=(\operatorname{Ind}_{F^{+}}^{F}\rho_{\pi,\lambda})\oplus\rho_{\chi,\lambda}\oplus(\operatorname{Ind}_{F^{+}}^{F_{1,\pi}}\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{m},\lambda}))_{\lambda}.

Let

Let

Let

Proposition 5.8

(
[
Xia19
, Proposition 17]
)
.

For each pair of primes

.

We can use Proposition
5.8
to obtain criteria for polarizability of summands of Galois representations.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\lambda
be a prime of
M_{\pi}
and let

be an irreducible summand of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
.
Suppose that there exists a weight

of

, viewed as a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, such that

is a weight of

.
Then

is polarizable.

Proof.

The formal bi-character of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
is multiplicity-free by Lemma
2.20
.
Let
X
(resp.

) be the unique irreducible

-subrepresentation of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}^{c}
(resp.
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}^{\vee}
) which, when viewed as a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, admits

as a weight.
By uniqueness, we must have

and

.
It follows that, under the isomorphism
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}^{c}\cong\rho_{\pi,\lambda}^{\vee}\otimes\rho_{\chi,\lambda}
,

is identified with

as representations of

.
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
be primes of
M_{\pi}
. Suppose that
c_{\lambda}
acts as a trivial outer automorphism of
G_{\lambda}^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
.

(1)

Then

acts trivially on

.

(2)

Suppose, in addition, that

is an irreducible

-subrepresentation of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda^{\prime}}
such that, when viewed as a representation of

, contains a non-trivial irreducible self-dual subrepresentation.

Proof.

Note that

acts trivially on

, as is argued in the discussion following
[
Xia19
, Proposition 21]
.
By Proposition
5.8
, we see that
c_{\lambda^{\prime}}
acts trivially on
X^{*}(T_{\lambda^{\prime}})
.
For the second part, let
w
be a non-zero weight of a non-trivial irreducible self-dual summand of

, on viewing

as a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
.
Then
-w
is also a weight of
U
.
Then since

acts trivially, we see that

is a weight of

.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
K/F
and
M/M_{\pi}
be finite extensions.
Let
(\sigma_{\eta})_{\eta}
be a weakly compatible system of Galois representations of

defined over

.
Suppose that

and that there exists a prime

of

lying above a prime

such that

is a direct summand of

and

is at least the smallest possible dimension of a multiplicity-free faithful irreducible representation of

.

Proof.

We may form a new weakly compatible system

(\tau_{\eta}:=\rho_{\pi,\eta}|_{G_{K}}\oplus\sigma_{\eta})_{\eta}

of
(n+m)
-dimensional Galois representations of
G_{K}
, where

.
Since
\sigma_{\eta}
is a direct summand of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}|_{G_{K}}
, we see that
\mathfrak{g}_{\tau_{\eta}}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\rho_{\pi,\eta}}
has rank equal to that of

.
It follows from Theorem
2.18
that
\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{g}_{\tau_{\eta_{0}}}=\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{g}
, where
\eta_{0}|\lambda_{0}
is a prime of

.
Since

5.3.
Proofs of irreducibility

We are almost ready to begin proving each case of Theorem
4.8
.
The following lemma will be useful in controlling the component group of
G_{\rho}
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\Omega
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
H\subset\operatorname{GL}_{n,\Omega}
be a possibly disconnected, algebraic subgroup with
H^{\circ}
acting irreducibly.
Let
Z=Z(\operatorname{GL}_{n})
and let
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Lie}(H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
.
Then there is a natural inclusion

ZH/(ZH^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Out}(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Since
ZH
normalizes
H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
, there is an inclusion

ZH/(ZH^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})=ZH/(ZH^{\circ})\hookrightarrow N_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}(ZH^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})/(ZH^{\circ}),

with the first equality holding by Schur’s lemma.
If
g\in N_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}(H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})
acts as an inner automorphism of
H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
, then
g=hz
, for some
h\in H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}}
and
z\in Z_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}(H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})=Z
.
We deduce that there is an injection

N_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}(ZH^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})/(ZH^{\circ})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Out}(H^{\circ,\operatorname{der}})\cong\operatorname{Out}(\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}).\qed

Due to the next lemma, it will suffice to only establish

of

in a density one set, in order to prove Theorem

.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\lambda
be a prime of
M_{\pi}
such that

is irreducible.
Then
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
is strongly irreducible.

Proof.

By combining Lemma
2.20
, Theorem

and Lemma

, we see (recalling that

) that
\operatorname{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
is cyclic of order at most

.
After possibly replacing

with its twist by an infinite order character, we may suppose that

Lemma
5.12
then implies that

is a cyclic extension of degree at most

.
Suppose that

is not strongly irreducible. Then

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
be a maximal type
A
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
.
We claim that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
.

If
k\geq 5
, then by Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
we see that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
and the claim follows.
If
k=3
, then Lemma
5.3
shows that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{6}
.
If
k=2
or
k=4
, we firstly show that
V
must be isomorphic to
\operatorname{Sym}^{a}\operatorname{Std}
(or its dual), where
\operatorname{Std}
is the standard representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
and
a\geq 1
.
By Theorem
2.21
, the only other possible faithful irreducible multiplicity-free representations of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
are the alternating powers of the standard representation.
When
k=4
, the possible dimensions of these proper alternating powers are
28
,
56
or
70
, each of which cannot occur by our assumption that
7\nmid n
.
When
k=2
, the only possibility is
\Lambda^{2}\operatorname{Std}
, which we can exclude because
V
is not self-dual.
We therefore see that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
by Proposition
5.7
, establishing the claim.

With the claim in hand note that, by considering maximal type
A
subalgebras of simple Lie algebras obtained via
[
Hui13
, Table 1]
and Lemma
5.1
, in every case we must have
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
itself is of type
A
or
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{e}_{7}
.
The case of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{e}_{7}
can be ruled out because multiplicity-freeness, Theorem
2.21
and the fact that
0
is not a weight of
V
(ruling out any
1
-dimensional summands) would together imply that
n=56
, a multiple of
7
.
We therefore have that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
, with
c_{\lambda}
acting as the unique non-trivial outer automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
by Proposition
5.8
.
We deduce that every irreducible summand
U\leq V_{\lambda}
satisfies
U^{c_{\lambda}}\cong U^{\vee}
.
It follows from Lemma
5.9
that each summand of
\rho_{\pi,\lambda}
is polarizable and the result now follows from Theorem
2.16
.
∎

Proposition 5.17
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
for some
k\geq 3
with
V
self-dual.
Then the conclusion to Theorem
4.8
holds.

Proof.

As a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
, we know from Theorem
2.21
that
V\cong\Lambda^{k}\operatorname{Std}
with
n={{2k}\choose{k}}
.
If
k=3
(resp.
k=4
) then
n=20
(resp.
n=70
), so neither of these cases occur by our assumption on
n
.
Therefore, suppose that
k\geq 5
.
It then follows from Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
that
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
contains a maximal type
A
subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
and that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
.

Recall from §
5.1
that there is a natural inner product
\langle-,-\rangle_{\lambda_{0}}
(resp.
\langle-,-\rangle_{\lambda}
) on the weight space of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
).
As mentioned there, these inner product spaces are isometric under the isomorphism of formal characters given by Theorem
2.18
.
Since every weight
w\in W_{\lambda_{0}}
has the same norm with respect to this inner product (Lemma
5.4
), the same is therefore true of weights in
W_{\lambda}
.

Suppose that the representation
U
of
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{n}
is reducible.
As a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2k}
,
U
is isomorphic to a direct sum of (distinct) alternating or symmetric powers of the standard representation, by Theorem
2.21
.
The symmetric powers
\operatorname{Sym}^{a}\operatorname{Std}
(or their duals) for
a\geq 2
do not appear as a constituent of
U
.
This is because any such symmetric power admits weights with distinct norms by Lemma
5.5
.
Furthermore, we must have
U\cong\Lambda^{a}\operatorname{Std}\oplus\Lambda^{2k-a}\operatorname{Std}
for some
1\leq a\leq k-1
.
Indeed, Lemma
5.4
states that (up to a fixed scalar) the squared norms of the weights of the representation
\Lambda^{a}\operatorname{Std}
are all given by
a(2k-a)
.
The expression
a(2k-a)
is quadratic in
a
, attaining its maximum at
a=k
, and can therefore only take on the repeated values at
a
and
2k-a
.
We see from Lemma
5.4
that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2}{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\max_{v\in W_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus\{w\}}\langle w,v\rangle_{\lambda_{0}}}{||w||_{\lambda_{0}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\max_{v^{\prime}\in W_{\lambda}\setminus\{w^{\prime}\}}\langle w^{\prime},v^{\prime}\rangle_{\lambda}}{||w^{\prime}||_{\lambda}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\max(1-\frac{2k}{a(2k-a)},\frac{2k}{2k-a}-1).

Similarly to the above, we cannot have
\frac{2}{k}=\frac{2k}{a(2k-a)}
, since
a<k
.
If
1-\frac{2}{k}=\frac{2k}{2k-a}-1
then
a=k-1-\frac{1}{k-1}
cannot be an integer, since
k\geq 5
.
In either case we obtain a contradiction, from which we see that
\mathfrak{h}_{\lambda}
, and hence
\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, must act irreducibly.
The result now follows.
∎

Remark 5.18
.

Hui brought to our attention that a significantly shorter proof of
Proposition

5.17
is possible using a modified version of
[
Hui18
, Theorem 3.10]
; while the theorem assumes that each member of our compatible system is defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
(coming from the étale cohomology of a smooth projective variety), these assumptions are not actually necessary, as the proof only relies on the existence of an isomorphism of formal characters after base-change to
\mathbb{C}
.
Proposition
5.17
for
k\geq 5
immediately follows from this, and the remaining cases of
k=2,3
can be excluded in an identical manner to the proof of Proposition
5.17
. Although this observation may also be applied to
Proposition

5.15
and
Proposition

5.16
for large values of
k
, we do not know how to give a proof for small
k
without using the arguments that we have given (which essentially work for all
k
).

We now consider the cases of the other classical simple Lie algebras, where the following lemma will be useful in finding polarizable summands.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a semisimple Lie algebra and
W
a faithful representation of
\mathfrak{h}
.
Suppose that
W
has the same formal character as the standard
m
-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{so}_{m}
,
m\geq 3
.
Then every irreducible summand of
W
is self-dual.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
\mathfrak{h}
is of type
A
.
To see this, firstly note that
\mathfrak{h}
contains a type
A
subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{h}
of equal rank by Lemma
5.1
and that the restriction
W|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
has the same formal character as
\mathfrak{h}
.
Moreover, if every irreducible summand of
W|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
is self-dual then the same conclusion holds for
W
, since each irreducible summand will of
W
be a sum of irreducible summands of
W|_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
and self-duality can be checked on the level of characters.

Write
\mathfrak{h}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{sl}_{m_{i}+1}
for some integers
m_{i}\geq 1
.
Suppose that (after reordering)
W
contains an irreducible, non-self-dual summand of the form
U\cong(\boxtimes_{i=1}^{j}U_{i})\boxtimes(\boxtimes_{i=j+1}^{k}\mathbf{1})
for some
1\leq j\leq k
and faithful irreducible representations
U_{i}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{m_{i}}
.
Let
X\cong U\oplus U^{\vee}
be the minimal self-dual summand of
W
containing
U
.
Then
\dim X\leq\dim W-2\sum_{i=j+1}^{k}m_{i}
.
This is because the complement of
X
inside
W
must restrict to a faithful self-dual representation of
\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}\mathfrak{sl}_{m_{i}+1}
, while the dimension of any faithful self-dual representation of a semisimple Lie algebra is always at least twice the rank.
Since
\dim W\leq 1+2\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
(because
\mathfrak{so}_{m}
has the same rank as
\mathfrak{h}
), we have

2\dim U=\dim X\leq 1+2\sum_{i=1}^{j}m_{i}.

Since each
U_{i}
is a faithful irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{m_{i}}
, we have
\dim U_{i}\geq 1+m_{i}
.
We obtain a contradiction, since we have inequalities

1+\sum_{i=1}^{j}m_{i}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1+m_{i})\leq\dim U\leq\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}m_{i}.\qed

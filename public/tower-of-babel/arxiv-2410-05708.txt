Functorial languages in homological algebra and lower central series
Nikita Golub, Vasily Ionin, Lev Mukoseev

Abstract
There is a general phenomenon in algebra that numerous functors of homological significance admit characterization as derived limits of elementary functors defined over categories of free extensions. We demonstrate that upon restriction to appropriate subcategories of the category of groups, one may express analogously more interesting functors, including homology groups with cyclic coefficients. Moreover, we are laying the foundations of the so-called 𝐟𝐫 ∞ \mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}_{\infty} -language, extending the 𝐟𝐫 \mathbf{f}\mathbf{r} -language of Roman Mikhailov and Sergei O. Ivanov. This language is constructed by augmenting the 𝐟𝐫 \mathbf{f}\mathbf{r} -language through the introduction of an infinite family of letters 𝐫 m \mathbf{r}_{m} corresponding to the lower central series γ m ​ ( R ) \gamma_{m}(R) of the group of relations and leads to some neat computations.

Introduction

In a series of works
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, the authors initiated the development of what can be called
a functorial group theory
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category of some sort of algebraic objects (e.g. groups, associative algebras, Lie algebras…), and let
A\in\mathcal{C}
be some fixed object. The category of free presentations of
A
, denoted
\mathsf{Pres}(A)
, naturally arises in this context and leads to several fundamental questions.

Of particular importance is the investigation of which invariants of
A
can be obtained through the study of functors on the category
\mathsf{Pres}(A)
. It is known for a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathsf{Pres}(A)\to\mathsf{Set}
that
\mathsf{lim}\,\mathcal{F}=\mathsf{inv}\,\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathsf{inv}
denotes a maximal constant subfunctor. For example, for a group
G
and a free extension
R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G
there is a Hopf’s formula

H_{2}(G)\cong\frac{R\cap[F,F]}{[R,F]}.

One may verify
H_{2}(G)
is the maximal constant subfunctor of
R/[R,F]
considered as a functor
\mathsf{Pres}(G)\to\mathsf{Grp}
. Namely, one has

\mathsf{lim}\,R/[R,F]=H_{2}(G).

(1)

The generalization of this formula to higher homology groups presents significant technical challenges. These obstacles can be resolved, however, by replacing the consideration of functorial subgroups of a free group
F
with the study of functorial ideals in the corresponding group ring
\mathbb{Z}[F]
. Namely, for every free extension
R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G
there are two-sided ideals
\mathbf{r}\subset\mathbf{f}\subset\mathbb{Z}[F]
given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}

\displaystyle=(R-1)\mathbb{Z}[F],

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}

\displaystyle=(F-1)\mathbb{Z}[F],

and one may show that

\displaystyle H_{2n}(G)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{r}^{n}\cap\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n-1}\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}}=\mathsf{lim}\,\frac{\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}},

\displaystyle n\geq 2,

\displaystyle H_{2n+1}(G)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{k}\cap\mathbf{r}^{k}\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{r}^{n+1}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}}=\mathsf{lim}\,\frac{\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{r}^{n+1}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}\mathbf{f}},

\displaystyle n\geq 0.

From the basic homological algebra we recall that the functor of limit

\mathsf{lim}\colon\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{Pres}(G),\mathsf{Ab})\to\mathsf{Ab}

is left exact. It is standard that the aforementioned functor category admits sufficient injective objects, thereby guaranteeing the existence of right derived functors for computing limits. Following standard conventions, we denote those functors by

\mathsf{lim}^{i}\colon\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{Pres}(G),\mathsf{Ab})\to\mathsf{Ab},\quad i\geq 0,

reffering to them as
higher limits
.
One may readily verify that for a functorial ideal
\mathbf{a}\subset\mathbb{Z}[F]
one has
\mathsf{lim}\,\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{a}=\mathsf{lim}^{1}\,\mathbf{a}
.
The utilization of higher limits consequently enables us to focus our analysis on subideals of the functorial enveloping ring
\mathbb{Z}[F]
. One may form arbitrary sums and intersections of monomials in letters
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{r}
— such as
\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}\cap\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
or
\mathbf{r}^{n+1}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}^{n}
— and regard them as functors
\mathsf{Pres}(G)\to\mathsf{Ab}
. We call such functors
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
-codes
, and the fundamental problem is to calculate their higher limits.

As initially demonstrated in
[
4
,
5
]
, a plethora of intriguing phenomena emerge already in this case.
Many non-trivial functors can be obtained in this way. For example,

\mathsf{lim}^{1}\,\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}=\mathsf{coker}\{H_{3}(G)\otimes G_{\mathrm{ab}}\to H_{2}(G;\mathbf{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}\mathbf{g})\},

where
\mathbf{g}=\Delta(G)\subset\mathbb{Z}[G]
is a fundamental ideal of a group
G
, and

\mathsf{lim}^{2}\,\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}=(\mathbf{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}\mathbf{g})\otimes\mathbf{g}.

A functorial linguistics
is a study of functors obtained from basic ‘alphabetic’ set of elementary functors (
letters
) via some universal operations (such as sums, products and intersections). We think of higher limits of such functors as of the translation of the corresponding codes. Typically, this is a study of languages with very simple syntax but complex semantics.

It is important to emphasize that certain invariants still elude description within the
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
-language paradigm. Apparently, the translations of
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
-codes are limited to include homology only with integral coefficients. In the present work, we extend the language with an infinite family of new letters

\mathbf{f}\supset\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{1}\supset\mathbf{r}_{2}\supset\mathbf{r}_{3}\supset\ldots,

given by the lower central series of the group of relations
R
:

\mathbf{r}_{m}=(\gamma_{m}(R)-1)\mathbb{Z}[F].

We call this extension
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}_{\infty}
-language.
We are able to give a functorial description of some homology groups with finite coefficients in terms of higher limits of some sentences in
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}_{\infty}
-language.

We wish to highlight an alternative perspective on functorial linguistics. Specifically, numerous computations in combinatorial group theory inherently depend upon the selection of a free group presentation; see
[

,

,

,

Moreover, it helps to streamline the exposition: it turns out that many formulas may be written down more systematically in a uniform way. While certain computations of higher derived limits can be readily obtained from classical results through elementary diagram chasing, others require substantially more sophisticated techniques - an investment we contend is justified given their theoretical significance.

(\mathsf{const}\dashv\mathsf{lim})
lifts to the Quillen adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}^{\Delta})_{\mathrm{inj}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}^{\Delta}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{lim}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{const}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

There is a total right derived functor
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{lim}\colon\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}^{\Delta})\to\mathcal{A}^{\Delta}
, and for a functor
M\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
there is a natural equivalence

\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,M\simeq\pi^{i}(\mathbb{R}\mathsf{lim}\,M)
, where
M
on the right is considered as a copresheaf of constant cosimplicial objects
c\mapsto\mathsf{const}(M)
.
The computational difficulty stems from the fact that to compute
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{lim}\,M
we have to choose some fibrant replacement for
M
in
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}^{\Delta})_{\mathrm{inj}}
, and the fibrations in the injective model structure are not obvious. However, when the index category
\mathcal{C}
shares some nice categorical properties, namely when it is strongly connected and have binary coproducts, we can use the so-called standard complex to bypass this difficulty
2.11.1
.

2.9
Main example

Let
G
be a group and consider a category
\mathcal{C}=\mathsf{Pres}(G)
of free extensions of
G
. The objects of
\mathsf{Pres}(G)
are short exact sequences of groups
R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G
with
F
being free, and the morphisms are the commutative diagrams of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{G.}}

For every commutative ring
\mathsf{k}
there is a functorial ring
\mathcal{O}\colon\mathsf{Pres}(G)\to\mathsf{Ring}
given by

\mathcal{O}(R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G)=\mathsf{k}[F],

the free group
\mathsf{k}
-algebra.
We mainly interested in the study of higher limits of functorial
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
.

It turns out that there is a plethora of interesting phenomena occuring when we specialize to the case
M=\mathbf{a}
for some functorial ideal
\mathbf{a}\subset\mathsf{k}[F]
(explicitely, this means that
\mathbf{a}(R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G)
is some two-sided ideal of
\mathsf{k}[F]
and the structure maps of the functor
\mathbf{a}
are restricted from those of
\mathcal{O}=\mathsf{k}[-]
).

There are functorial ideals
\mathsf{k}[-]\supset\mathbf{f}\supset\mathbf{r}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}(R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{ker}\{\mathsf{k}[F]\to k\}=(F-1)\mathsf{k}[F],

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}(R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{ker}\{\mathsf{k}[F]\to\mathsf{k}[G]\}=(R-1)\mathsf{k}[F].

That is,
\mathbf{f}=\Delta_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
and
\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{g}=\Delta_{\mathsf{k}}(G)
.

2.10
Relation module

Let us remind the basics on the relation modules. One may use
[
7
]
for a general reference.

Let
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
.
Given a free extension
R\hookrightarrow F\twoheadrightarrow G
, a
relation module
associated with it may be identified with the kernel in the short exact sequence

R_{\mathrm{ab}}\hookrightarrow P\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{g},

(3)

where
P={\mathbb{Z}[G]}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[F]}\mathbf{f}
.
The sequence (
3

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathsf{Pres}(G),\mathsf{Mod}({\mathbb{Z}[G]}))
, and is referred to as a

. The embedding of the relation module

into a free
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-module

.

In terms of
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
-language, the sequence (

\mathbf{r}/\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{r}\mathbf{f}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{r}

2.11
Computational toolkit

In this section, we give the basic computational tools for higher limits: standard complex, limits spectral sequence, and Künneth formula.

2.11.1
Standard complex

If
\mathcal{C}
is a strongly connected category with binary coproducts, then for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
there is a cosimplicial object
\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}\colon\Delta\to\mathcal{C}
given by
\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)^{n}=\sqcup^{n+1}c
with the natural structure maps.
In
[
5
, Theorem 2.12]
, the authors prove that for a functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{Ab}
one has a natural isomorphism

\mathsf{lim}^{n}\,\mathcal{F}=\pi^{n}\mathcal{F}\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c).

As always, the cohomotopy groups of a cosimplicial abelian group
A
can be computed with cohomology groups of the Moore (cochain) complex
QA
or the alternating sum complex
CA
. The natural projection
CA\twoheadrightarrow QA
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
f\colon\mathsf{k}\to\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
be a homomorphism of commutative rings. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a strongly connected category with binary coproducts.

1.

If
M
is a functorial
\mathsf{k}^{\prime}
-module on
\mathcal{C}
, then the restriction of scalars induces a natural isomorphism

\mathsf{lim}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}_{\mathsf{k}^{\prime}}\,M\simeq\mathsf{lim}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}_{\mathsf{k}}\,M.

2.

If
f
is flat, and
M
is a functorial
\mathsf{k}
-module on
\mathcal{C}
, then the extension of scalars induces a natural isomorphism

\mathsf{lim}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}_{\mathsf{k}^{\prime}}\,(M\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{k}^{\prime})\simeq(\mathsf{lim}^{\mathord{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}}_{\mathsf{k}}\,M)\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{k}^{\prime}.

Proof.

Recall that the restriction of scalars
f^{*}\colon\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k}^{\prime})\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k})
is an exact functor, and the extension of scalars
(-\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{k}^{\prime})\colon\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k})\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k}^{\prime})
is exact precisely when

The result follows since higher limits could be computed as cohomologies of the standard complex, and exact functors commute with cohomologies.
∎

3
Shifting formulas

In this section, we establish shifting formulas that eventually enable partial computation of the higher limits of the functors
(\Lambda^{n}R_{\mathrm{ab}})_{G}
; see Corollary
3.6
, Proposition
3.7
and Proposition
3.9
. Our method is a direct generalization of the method suggested in
[
3
, Proposition 6.1]
.

We say that a group
G
is
n
-torsionless
, if no nontrivial element
x
of
G
satisfies the equation
x^{n}=1
. This is equivalent to the statement that there is no element in
G
of order
p
for every prime divisor
p
of
n
.
Note that a group
G
is
n!
-torsionless if and only if there is no element in
G
of order
p
for every prime
p
such that
p\leq n
.

By employing a standard dimension-shifting argument, we obtain a canonical isomorphism that elucidates the fundamental relationship between homology groups with exterior power coefficients and their symmetric power counterparts.
This result generalizes
[
9
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
G
be a
n!
-torsionless group. Let
A\hookrightarrow B\twoheadrightarrow C
be a short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-modules, such that
B
is free as a
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-module and
C
is a torsionless abelian group. Then for every
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-module
M
there are isomorphisms

H_{i+n}(G;S^{n}C\otimes M)\simeq H_{i}(G;\Lambda^{n}A\otimes M),\quad i\geq 1.

Proof.

Since
A,B
and
C
are torsionless abelian groups, there is a corresponding Koszul sequence.
Tensoring it over
\mathbb{Z}
with
M
yields a long exact sequence

\Lambda^{n}A\otimes M\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{n-1}A\otimes B\otimes M\to\dots\to S^{n}B\otimes M\twoheadrightarrow S^{n}C\otimes M.

(4)

Since
G
has no torsion up to
n
we conclude that for any free
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-module
B
the exterior powers
S^{k}B,k\leq n
, are free
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-modules; see
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Since
A
is a free abelian group, the exterior powers
\Lambda^{*}A
are free abelian groups, and all modules
\Lambda^{n-k}A\otimes S^{k}B
are free as
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-modules; see
[
15
, III §5, Corollary 5.7]
. Hence, by Shapiro’s lemma, tensoring the resulting sequence with any
M
yields modules with trivial homology, i.e.

H_{i}(G;\Lambda^{n-k}A\otimes S^{k}B\otimes M)=0,\quad i\geq 1.

Finally, by a simple dimension shifting we derive the result.
∎

The following proposition establishes that, within the functorial framework and under the specified additional hypotheses, the higher limits induce inverse shiftings relative to those presented in the preceding lemma.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
n!
-torsionless group and
M
be a
{\mathbb{Z}[G]}
-module. If for some
0\leq k\leq n
we have

\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,(\Lambda^{n-j}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes S^{j}P\otimes M)_{G}=0,\quad 0\leq i\leq k,\;1\leq j\leq k-i+1,

then there are isomorphisms

\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,(\Lambda^{n}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes M)_{G}=H_{n-i}(G;S^{n}(\mathbf{g})\otimes M),\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

If
k=n
, then
\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,(\Lambda^{n}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes M)_{G}=0
for
i>n
.

Proof.

Consider a Koszul sequence for the relation sequence (
3
).
Cut it into short exact sequences by introducing

N_{j}=\mathsf{coker}\{\Lambda^{n-j+1}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes S^{j-1}P\to\Lambda^{n-j}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes S^{j}P\},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

For convenience, we set
N_{0}=\Lambda^{n}R_{\mathrm{ab}}
. Note that
N_{n}=S^{n}(\mathbf{g})
.
Then we can cut the long exact sequence (
4
) into short exact sequences

N_{j-1}\otimes M\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{n-j}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes S^{j}P\otimes M\twoheadrightarrow N_{j}\otimes M,\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

The middle term is
H_{*}(G;-)
-acyclic (see the proof of the previous lemma), hence for every
1\leq j\leq n
there are isomorphisms

H_{l+1}(G;N_{j}\otimes M)\simeq H_{l}(G;N_{j-1}\otimes M),\quad l\geq 1,

(5)

and there is the short exact sequence

H_{1}(G;N_{j}\otimes M)\hookrightarrow(N_{j-1}\otimes M)_{G}\to(\Lambda^{n-j}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes S^{j}B\otimes M)_{G}\twoheadrightarrow(N_{j}\otimes M)_{G}.

(6)

Iterating (

), we obtain for any

that

H_{1}(G;N_{j}\otimes M)\simeq H_{n-j+1}(G;N_{n}\otimes M)=H_{n-j+1}(G;S^{n}(\mathbf{g})\otimes M)

is a constant functor, which implies that it has trivial higher limits.

Since functor
\mathsf{lim}^{0}
is left exact, applying it to the sequence (

) yields identifications

\mathsf{lim}\,(N_{j-1}\otimes M)_{G}=H_{1}(G;N_{j}\otimes M)\overset{\eqref{eq:ni_h1_identification}}{=}H_{n-j+1}(G;S^{n}(\mathbf{g})\otimes M)

(8)

for
1\leq j\leq k
.

Iteratively applying Proposition
2.14
to the 4-term exact sequence (
6
), we obtain the sequence of identifications

\displaystyle H_{n-i}(G;S^{n}C\otimes M)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ni_homology_as_lim}}{=}\mathsf{lim}\,(N_{i}\otimes M)_{G}=\mathsf{lim}^{1}\,(N_{i-1}\otimes M)_{G}=\cdots

\displaystyle=\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,(N_{0}\otimes M)_{G}=\mathsf{lim}^{i}\,(\Lambda^{n}R_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes M)_{G}

for
0\leq i\leq k
.
∎

5
Higher limits of functorial ideals

In this section, we establish key results that facilitate the computation of higher derived limits
\mathsf{lim}^{*}\,\mathbf{a}
, where
\mathbf{a}
is a functorial ideal in a group ring
\mathsf{k}[F]
of a free extension
F\twoheadrightarrow G
. It turns out that Hopf ideals play a special role in this subject matter; see §
5.16
.

Let
\mathsf{FunIdeals}(\mathsf{k}[F])
be a class of all two-sided ideals in
\mathsf{k}[F]
depending functorialy on a free extension
F\twoheadrightarrow G
. We observe that the most general functorial universums

\mathcal{U}\subset\mathsf{FunIdeals}(\mathsf{k}[F]),

amenable to productive study, are constructed via the following scheme. Take any finite set of functorial Hopf ideals
\mathbf{h}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{h}_{n}
. We call these ideals
letters
. Then, take its closure in
\mathsf{FunIdeals}(\mathsf{k}[F])
under finite sums, products and intersections.
Providing a general description of such languages presents an interesting theoretical challenge.

5.1
Higher limits of free modules

We start with a fundamental result that shows how to compute limits of functorial free modules.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a commutative ring, and
\mathsf{k}[-]\colon\mathsf{Set}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k})
be a free module functor. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a strongly connected category with binary coproducts.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
U\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{Set}
be a functorial set. Then

\mathsf{lim}^{n}\,\mathsf{k}[U]=\begin{cases}\mathsf{k}[\mathsf{lim}\,U],&n=0,\\
0,&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\mathsf{k}[U]\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{k})
. Fix some
c\in\mathcal{C}
. There is a standard cosimplicial object
\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)
in
\mathcal{C}
. Consider a cosimplicial abelian group
\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)
. We aim to compute
\mathsf{lim}^{n}\,\mathcal{F}=\pi^{n}\,\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)
. There are the alternate sum complex
C=C\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)
and the Moore complex
Q=Q\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)
of normalized cochains. There is a quasi-isomorphism
C\twoheadrightarrow Q
, and the
n
-th cohomology computes
\mathsf{lim}^{n}\,\mathcal{F}
.

Let
n=0
. Then

\mathsf{lim}^{0}\,\mathcal{F}=H^{0}(C)=\mathcal{Z}^{0}(C)=\mathsf{ker}\{d^{0}-d^{1}\colon\mathsf{k}[U(c)]\to\mathsf{k}[U(c\sqcup c)]\}.

We have

\mathsf{lim}\,U=\mathsf{inv}\,U(c)=\mathsf{eq}\{U(c)\xrightarrow{d^{0},d^{1}}U(c\sqcup c)\},

hence
\mathsf{k}[\mathsf{lim}\,U]
naturally embeds into
\mathsf{lim}^{0}\,\mathcal{F}
. To show the reverse inclusion we proceed as follows. Let
t\in\mathcal{Z}^{0}(C)
be a
0
-cocycle. We can write
t=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
, where
x_{\alpha}
are some pair-wise distinct elements of
U(c)
, and
\lambda_{\alpha}
are non-zero elements of
\mathsf{k}
. It is enough to show that for every
\alpha
one has
d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=d^{1}(x_{\alpha})
. Note that if for some
i,j\in\{0,1\}
and some indices
\alpha,\beta
one has an equality
d^{i}(x_{\alpha})=d^{j}(x_{\beta})
, then

x_{\alpha}=s^{0}d^{i}(x_{\alpha})=s^{0}d^{j}(x_{\beta})=x_{\beta}.

The condition for
t
to be a cocycle deciphers as

\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=d^{0}(t)=d^{1}(t)=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}d^{1}(x_{\alpha}).

Note that all elements
\lambda_{\alpha}d^{0}(x_{\alpha})
of
\mathcal{F}(c\sqcup c)
are pair-wise distinct, since
d^{0}
is an embedding, and
\mathcal{F}(c\sqcup c)
is a free
\mathsf{k}
-module with the basis
U(c\sqcup c)
. Hence, for every
\alpha
there is some index
\beta
such that
d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=d^{1}(x_{\beta})
, which implies (by the observation above) that
x_{\alpha}=x_{\beta}
, and shows the desired result.

Now, let
n\geq 1
. Then

\mathsf{lim}^{n}\,\mathcal{F}=H^{n}(C)=\mathcal{Z}^{n}(C)/\mathcal{B}^{n}(C).

We aim to show that every
n
-cocycle
t\in\mathcal{Z}^{n}(C)
is a coboundary. Since the natural projection
C\twoheadrightarrow Q
is a quasi-isomorphism, it is enough to show that
t
maps to a coboundary of
Q
. This is equivalent to show that
t
lies in the sum of the images of cofaces
d^{i}\colon\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)^{n-1}\to\mathcal{F}\,\text{\sf\reflectbox{B}}(c)^{n}
.
As before, we write
t=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}
.
It is enough to show that each
x_{\alpha}
is in image of some
d^{i}
.

The condition for
t
to be a cocycles deciphers as

0=d(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}d^{i}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\sum_{\alpha}(-1)^{i}\lambda_{\alpha}d^{i}(x_{\alpha}).

Since
\mathcal{F}(c^{\sqcup\,n+1})
is a free
k
-module with the basis
U(c^{\sqcup\,n+1})
, for every
\alpha
there exist
i
and
\beta
such that
(i,\beta)\neq(0,\alpha)
and there is an identity

d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=d^{i}(x_{\beta}).

(11)

There are three cases
i=0
,
i=1
and
i\geq 2
which we consider separately.

First, if
i=0
, then
x_{\alpha}=x_{\beta}
(since
d^{0}
is monomorphism), and
\alpha=\beta
(since
x
’s are distinct), which contradicts
(i,\beta)\neq(0,\alpha)
.

Second, if
i=1
, then

x_{\alpha}=s^{0}d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=s^{0}d^{1}(x_{\beta})=x_{\beta},

(12)

and

x_{\alpha}=s^{1}d^{1}(x_{\alpha})\overset{\eqref{eq:lim_free_group_2}}{=}s^{1}d^{1}(x_{\beta})\overset{\eqref{eq:lim_free_group_1}}{=}s^{1}d^{0}(x_{\alpha})=d^{0}s^{0}(x_{\alpha}),

which shows that
x_{\alpha}
is in the image of
d^{0}
.

Lastly, if
i\geq 2
, then

x_{\alpha}=s^{0}d^{0}(x_{\alpha})\overset{\eqref{eq:lim_free_group_1}}{=}s^{0}d^{i}(x_{\beta})=d^{i-1}s^{0}(x_{\beta}),

which shows that
x_{\alpha}
is in the image of
d^{i-1}
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let

be a functorial set.
Then for every

and
\mathsf{lim}\,U^{n}/S_{n}=(\mathsf{lim}\,U)^{n}/S_{n}

The first part is trivial, since limits commute with products.

We aim to prove the second part. We can identify the quotient

The inclusion

\{d^{0}(x_{1}),\ldots,d^{0}(x_{n})\}=\{d^{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,d^{1}(x_{n})\}.

Then for every
i
there is some
j
such that
d^{0}(x_{i})=d^{1}(x_{j})
. As before,

x_{i}=s^{0}d^{0}(x_{i})=s^{0}d^{1}(x_{j})=x_{j},

and

and

5.16
Hopf ideals

A group homomorphism
G\to K
yields a homomorphism of algebras
\mathsf{k}[G]\to\mathsf{k}[K]
.
This homomorphism preserves the natural structure of comultiplication, that is, this is actually a
homomorphism of Hopf algebras.

Let
G
be a group. If
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we denote with
(H-1)\mathsf{k}[G]
the kernel of
the homomorphism
\mathsf{k}[G]\to\mathsf{k}[G/H]
induced by the projection
G\twoheadrightarrow G/H
.
If
\mathbf{a}
is an ideal of the
group algebra
\mathsf{k}[G]
, we denote by
(\mathbf{a}+1)\cap G
the normal subgroup of
G
given by
\{x\in G\mid x-1\in\mathbf{a}\}
.
Hence, we have two morphisms of posets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left\{\text{normal subgroups}\;H\subset G\right\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\left\{\text{two-sided ideals}\;\mathbf{a}\subset\mathsf{k}[G]\right\}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\mapsto(H-1)\mathsf{k}[G]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{a}+1)\cap G\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\mapsto$}}\mathbf{a}}

One may readily verify that the correspondence
\mathbf{a}\mapsto(\mathbf{a}+1)\cap G
is a left inverse of
H\mapsto(H-1)\mathsf{k}[G]
, hence the set of normal subgroups embeds into the set of ideals of a group ring as a retract, with the image consisting of precisely Hopf ideals.

The following properties render Hopf ideals particularly significant for our investigation: in some sense they connect the
\mathbf{f}\mathbf{r}
-language — studying ideals in a free group ring — with the
FR
-language — studying subgroups in a free group.

Fact 5.17

(
[
4
, Corollary 4.4]
)
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
be normal subgroups of a free group
F
, and let
\mathbf{h}_{i}=(H_{i}-1)\mathsf{k}[F]
be the corresponding Hopf ideals. Then the product
\mathbf{h}_{1}\mathbf{h}_{2}\ldots\mathbf{h}_{n}
is a free right (or left)
\mathsf{k}[F]
-module.

Fact 5.18

(
[
4
, Lemma 4.5, Lemma 4.6]
)
.

Let
H
be a normal subgroup of
F
, and
\mathbf{h}=(H-1)\mathsf{k}[F]
be the corresponiding Hopf ideal.

1.

The long sequence

\cdots\to\frac{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{h}^{2}}{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{h}^{3}}\to\frac{\mathbf{h}^{2}}{\mathbf{h}^{3}}\to\frac{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{h}}{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{h}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathbf{h}}{\mathbf{h}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathbf{f}}{\mathbf{f}\mathbf{h}}\to\frac{\mathsf{k}[F]}{\mathbf{h}}\to\mathsf{k}\to 0

(13)

is a free resolution of the right
\mathsf{k}[F/H]
-module
\mathsf{k}
.

2.

For every

there is an isomorphism

3.

There is an isomorphism

\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{h}\simeq\Delta_{\mathsf{k}}(F/H).

Proposition 5.19
.

Let
\mathbf{h}=(H-1)\mathsf{k}[F]
be a functorial Hopf ideal in
\mathsf{k}[F]
given by some functorial subgroup

5.3

∎

.

A

\cdots\hookrightarrow B_{3}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow A_{2}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow B_{2}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow A_{1}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow B_{1}^{\prime},

and

B_{1}^{\prime}=\mathsf{lim}^{1}\,\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathsf{lim}\,H_{1}(F/\gamma_{2}(R))\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathsf{lim}\,F_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0,

we have
A_{k}^{\prime}=B_{k}^{\prime}=0
for all
k
.
Therefore, there are short exact sequences

\displaystyle S^{2k}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\hookrightarrow\underbrace{\mathsf{lim}^{2k+1}\,\mathbf{s}^{k}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{s}^{k}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}_{A_{k}}\twoheadrightarrow\underbrace{\mathsf{lim}^{2k}\mathbf{s}^{k}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{s}^{2k-1}\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}_{B_{k}},\quad k\geq 1,

\displaystyle S^{2k+1}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\hookrightarrow\underbrace{\mathsf{lim}^{2k+2}\,\mathbf{s}^{k+1}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{s}^{k}\mathbf{f}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}_{B_{k+1}}\twoheadrightarrow\underbrace{\mathsf{lim}^{2k+1}\,\mathbf{s}^{k}\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{f}\mathbf{s}^{k}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}_{A_{k}},\quad k\geq 0.

Since over
\mathbb{Q}
every exact sequence splits, we have

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=S^{2k}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\oplus S^{2k-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G},\quad k\geq 0,

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=S^{2k-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\oplus S^{2k-2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G}\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbf{g})_{G},\quad k\geq 1,

and the result follows.
∎

.

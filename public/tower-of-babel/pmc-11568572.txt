Establishment of callus induction and plantlet regeneration systems of Peucedanum Praeruptorum dunn based on the tissue culture method
Authors: Haoyu Pan, Ranran Liao, Yingyu Zhang, Muhammad Arif, Yuxin Zhang, Shuai Zhang, Yuanyuan Wang, Pengcheng Zhao, Zaigui Wang, Bangxing Han, Cheng Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPeucedanum praeruptorum Dunn has typical stacked umbels and medicinal value; however, the lack of an effective tissue culture system for P. praeruptorum has limited the large-scale propagation of its seedlings.ResultsWe systematically established an in vitro regeneration system for P. praeruptorum using young leaves and stems as explants. Tissue culture plantlets were successfully obtained within 123 and 90 d of somatic embryogenesis and organogenesis, respectively. Combined plant growth regulators (PGRs) were optimized to promote efficient plant regeneration at each stage of the culture process. Specifically, embryogenic callus induction was superior in Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium supplemented with 0.5 mg/L 6-benzyladenine (BA) and 2.0 mg/L 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D). For somatic embryonic development, the highest differentiation rates were achieved using BA, 2,4-D, and 6-furfuryl aminopurine (6-KT). Induction of organogenesis resulted in the highest differentiation rates and proliferation coefficients of buds in MS medium supplemented with BA and α-naphthaleneacetic acid (NAA). Moreover, regeneration of P. praeruptorum seedlings was achieved by adjusting the BA and indole-3-butyric acid (IBA) concentrations in 1/2 MS medium.ConclusionOur results provide a technical system for the rapid propagation of P. praeruptorum, which can facilitate germplasm improvement, resource conservation, and further genetic transformation of Peucedanum species.

Background

Plant regeneration involves the use of specific tissues or organs, such as embryogenic or non-embryogenic calli, and even a single cell or cell mass can differentiate and develop into complete plants or regenerate specific organs and tissues through pluripotent cell differentiation [1]. The ability of plant tissue to regenerate is determined by the pluripotency of the plant. The culture method through which fixed plant tissues differentiate and develop into complete plants at multiple stages is called the plant regeneration system. Since the totipotency of plant cells was successfully verified by Steward and Mapes in 1958, researchers have regenerated new somatic embryos (SEs) from fine phloem cells of carrot roots using the totipotency or pluripotency characteristics of plant tissues [2]. This leads to asexual reproduction in plants through somatic embryogenesis and organogenesis [3]. Various economic crops, medicinal and ornamental plants, and other regeneration systems have been established on a large scale. They can be used in bioreactors, micropropagation, genetic transformation, biological breeding, and other scientific and commercial applications. Organ regeneration and somatic embryogenesis are essential asexual propagation pathways in plant regeneration systems. Both pathways can produce calli, but there are significant differences in the regeneration process and practical applications of these two methods because of the different characteristics of induced potency and pluripotency [4, 5]. Organogenic pathways have various applications, including germplasm preservation, plant detoxification, and homozygous regeneration [6, 7]. Most plants undergo genetic transformation via embryogenic calli induced by somatic pathways [8]. Previous studies have shown that the growth status and induction efficiency of P. praeruptorum calli are affected by the explant type [9]. Embryoid formation is induced by protoplast and fine cell suspension cultures, which then differentiate into complete plants [10]. To date, systematic studies have not been conducted on the indirect regeneration of P. praeruptorum through organogenesis or somatic embryogenesis. The absence of an explicit process for the various types of differentiation throughout different periods and the emergence of different stages of SEs during the regeneration process of P. praeruptorum hinders our evaluation of regeneration efficiency in a diverse manner.

Peucedanum praeruptorum Dunn is a perennial plant belonging to the Peucedanum genus in the Apiaceae family, and its dried roots have medicinal properties. The Chinese traditional medical book ‘Ming Yi Bie Lu’ first documented the medicinal properties of its dried roots [11]. The primary authentic production regions of P. praeruptorum are Ningguo in Anhui, Chun Bostan in Zhejiang, and Shangrao in Jiangxi. More than 180 types of proprietary Chinese medicines use this plant as a raw material, such as Taiji cough oral liquid, Chuanbei loquat cough syrup, and Zhikebao tablets. Thus, the domestic market demand for P. praeruptorum is increasing daily, leading to large-scale cultivation in Sichuan, Guizhou, and other regions [12]. Owing to increasing market demand, artificial planting accounts for over 80% of domestically produced P. praeruptorum [13]. Despite their high demand, P. praeruptorum seeds are commonly used for propagation. Following sowing, the seeds germinated in spring and entered the vegetative growth stage. However, artificially cultivated P. praeruptorum often suffers from early bolting in the same or the following year. Peucedanum praeruptorum Dunn is typically harvested during autumn and winter of the same year or before bolting in the second year. Early bolting decreases the coumarin content in the roots, which falls below the standards set the Chinese Pharmacopoeia for praeruptorin A (≥ 0.9%) and praeruptorin B (≥ 0.24%) [13]. The decline in underground root coumarins and intensification of lignification accompany the transition from vegetative to reproductive growth [14]. Thus, the roots of P. praeruptorum cannot be used as a raw material in traditional Chinese medicine.

The cultivation method for seed propagation of P. praeruptorum has not resolved its poor quality and germplasm degradation, which have severely limited its harvest. Therefore, there is an urgent need for research on the production of P. praeruptorum to develop a new method of reproduction. Establishing an in vitro regeneration system for P. praeruptorum through tissue culture is an effective method of obtaining high-quality germplasm resources with a high accumulation of medicinal components and late bolting. This strengthens the preservation, cultivation, and improvement of germplasm resources [15]. In the present study, a plant regeneration system was systematically established in P. praeruptorum through indirect somatic embryogenesis and organogenesis. The optimization of explant selection and the concentration and type of growth regulators at different stages resulted in histological observations of the SE development process and the construction of an efficient and stable regeneration system for P. praeruptorum. This study provides a research basis for large-scale reproduction, breeding, germplasm preservation, and genetic transformation of P. praeruptorum.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and culture conditions

Peucedanum praeruptorum seeds were collected in November 2022 and sown at the Ta-pieh Mountain Medicinal Botanical Garden of the West Anhui University (Luan, China). In March 2023, 2-month-old P. praeruptorum seedlings with healthy growth were collected and stored in the traditional Chinese medicine herbarium of West Anhui University (no. WXBH-09). After rinsing with tap water for 20 min to ensure that the seedlings were clean, they were soaked in a benzalkonium bromide disinfectant solution for 30 min. Thereafter, the seedlings were transferred to a sterile environment, and a surgical blade was used to divide the seedlings into three explant types: tender leaves, stems, and roots. The explants were treated with 75% ethanol for 30 s, rinsed once with sterile water, and soaked in 0.1% HgCl2 for sterilization for 8 min (leaves),10 min (stems), and 12 min (roots). Finally, the explants were rinsed four to five times with sterile water. The culture medium was supplemented with 30 g/L sucrose and 6 g/L agar. The pH was adjusted to 5.8 using 2 M NaOH and sterilized in an aseptic culture flask at 121 °C for 20 min. Plant regeneration was carried out in an aseptic culture room maintained at a temperature of 25 ± 1 °C, with a photoperiod of 16/8 h light/dark, and a light intensity of 2000 lx.

Callus induction from different explants and PGR combinations

Sterilized tender leaves were trimmed into 1–2 cm2 blocks on a clean worktable. Small segments (1 cm in length) were cut from young stems and roots. Young leaves, stems, and roots were inoculated into MS medium supplemented with different concentrations of 6-benzyladenine (BA), thidiazuron (TDZ), and 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) for adaxial culture. The culture flask was placed in a culture room at 25 ± 1 °C for seven d, followed by 16 h / 8 h of sunshine culture. Each culture bottle was inoculated with five explants, and each treatment group had three replicates with 15 explants per group. After 30 d of in vitro culture, we calculated the induction rate of different explants on the medium and the growth status of the calli.

Histomorphological observation

Histological observations of SEs were conducted at various stages of callus and embryogenic callus differentiation. Samples were fixed in FAA solution (70% ethanol accounted for 90%, glacial acetic acid 5%, and formalin 5%) for 24 h, dehydrated using an ethanol gradient (70%, 85%, 95%, and 100%) for 1 h, soaked in a mixture of xylene and ethanol for 1 h, soaked in a mixture of xylene and paraffin, and embedded in paraffin. The 8 μm slices of the embedded samples were obtained using a slicing machine (RM2016, Leica, Germany) and stained with safranin O-fast green. The tissue morphologies of calli and SEs at different stages were observed under a microscope (ECLIPSE E100, NIKON, Japan).

Differentiation and proliferation of Adventitious buds Induced by different PGR combinations

Calli in a good growth state were selected from MS medium supplemented with 0.5 mg/L BA, 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D, and 0.2 mg/L 6-KT. They were then transferred to MS medium supplemented with varying concentrations of BA, NAA, indole-3-butyric acid (IBA), and 2,4-D to investigate the effects of different PGR concentrations on adventitious bud differentiation. Each culture bottle was inoculated with five calli, and each treatment group had three replicates with 15 calli per group. After 35 d of culture, we observed the differentiation state of the buds and recorded the number of buds.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were analyzed using the SPSS software (version 27.0). Significance was tested using analysis of variance, and significant differences were tested using Duncan’s multiple range test (p < 0.05).

Results

Effects of explant type and PGR combination on embryogenic callus induction

Callus induction and culture were performed using tissue samples from the leaves, stems, and roots of the same plant variety at the same growth stage. Significant differences in callus morphology were observed among the explants. After 7 d of dark culture, the morphology of calli was obtained from different explants of P. praeruptorum as follows. On the 8th day, P. praeruptorum formed a flocculent and fragile milky-white water-based callus at the wound site. However, calli grew slowly and had a high contamination rate during culture (Fig. 1A). On the 14th day, the wound edge extended irregularly and curled, forming loose and irregular light-yellow or milky calli (Fig. 1B). On the 10th day, yellowish-green calli formed at both ends of the stem segment, and the wound gradually formed a regular, granular, and dense callus. After 2 weeks of continuous culture, most calli exhibited rapid growth, resulting in the formation of dense, dumbbell-shaped structures (Fig. 1C).

Callus induction was significantly affected by different concentrations of growth regulators. The rate of callus induction was generally higher in the BA-supplemented medium than in the TDZ medium (Fig. 1D). EC that were more compact and granular were induced in medium supplemented with BA, whereas non-embryogenic calli (NEC) that were loose and watery were induced in medium supplemented with TDZ (Fig. 2A and B). Histomorphological observations showed that EC calli had dense cytoplasm, more starch granules, and larger nuclei (Fig. 2C), whereas NEC calli had finer cytoplasm and smaller nuclei (Fig. 2D). A significant difference in tissue morphology was observed between the two groups. The experiment showed that stem explants had the highest callus induction rate, reaching 77.77% in MS medium supplemented with 0.5 mg/L BA + 2 mg/L 2,4-D. Leaf explants had a callus induction rate of 75.77% in the MS medium supplemented with 0.5 mg/L BA + 5 mg/L 2,4-D (Fig. 1D). These results indicate that the effects of different types and concentrations of growth regulators on callus induction in different explants were significantly different. The optimal method for inducing embryogenic callus included culturing stem segments in MS medium + 0.5 mg/L BA + 2 mg/L 2,4-D for 25 d, resulting in healthy yellow-green embryogenic callus.

Effects of different PGR combinations on the proliferation and development of P. praeruptorum EC

Plant calli regulate growth and promote differentiation and proliferation through the action of different PGRs. The EC induced from P. praeruptorum stem segments was further cultured for proliferation. The effects of different combinations of cytokinins (BA and 6-KT) and auxins (2,4-D and NAA) on callus proliferation were examined. Significant differences were observed in the growth status and proliferation coefficient of calli exposed to different PGR combinations. Specifically, the combination of cytokinin and 2,4-D resulted in a higher proliferation coefficient than the auxin-BA combination (Fig. 3). The highest proliferation coefficient observed was 1.68 after 28 d of culture in MS medium + 0.5 mg/L BA + 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D + 0.2 mg/L 6-KT. The second group comprised calli treated with 0.5 mg/L BA, 1.0 mg/L 2,4-D, and 0.2 mg/L 6-KT, resulting in a proliferation coefficient of 1.48. The calli appeared yellow-green with compact and moderately hard tissues, which was favorable for subsequent bud induction, differentiation, and proliferation (Fig. 4B). Morphologically, higher PGR levels were not conducive to callus proliferation and differentiation, and flocculent and fragile milky calli were easily produced (Fig. 4I and J). However, callus browning occurred in treatments with 0.5 mg/L BA + 1.0 mg/L, 2 mg/L 6-KT + 0.4 mg/L 6-KT, and 1.0 mg/L BA + 1.0 mg/L 2,4-D + 0.2 mg/L NAA (Fig. 4K and L). Callus proliferation and growth stabilized after the 21st day. Treatment with 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D showed that higher levels of cytokinin promoted the differentiation of calli into tender green bud spots and roots. After 28 d, plants with intact organs had differentiated in the two groups: one treated with 0.5 mg/L BA + 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D + 0.4 mg/L 6-KT, and the other with 1.0 mg/L BA + 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D. When treated with 0.5 mg/L BA, the callus differentiated into more white bud spots due to higher auxin levels; however, the extent of differentiation was reduced. For the proliferation, growth, and differentiation processes, the combination of BA and the two types of auxins had a significant impact on weaving growth and proliferation coefficient of the P. praeruptorum callus group. The optimal PGR combination for proliferation and differentiation was MS medium + 0.5 mg/L BA + 0.5 mg/L 2,4-D + 0.2 mg/L 6-KT. The callus volume was the largest after 28 d of culture, which was the most suitable growth state for induction and differentiation.

Histomorphological observations of SEs at different developmental stages

The somatic embryogenesis process in EC was identified through histological microscopy. The histology of SEs at various developmental stages was observed using saffron fast- and green-stained paraffin sections. After further cultivation, the cells at both ends of the globular embryo gradually developed into torpedo embryos with vascular tissue (Fig. 5A and B, and 5C). The embryo undergoes several stages of development, including the transition from a globular to heart-shaped embryo, followed by the accumulation of starch granules in the torpedo embryo to form a bipolar structure. This structure then develops into a cotyledon embryo, which eventually produces leaf primordia after culture. Finally, SEs developed into complete plants (Fig. 5D and E). During the development of SEs in P. praeruptorum, changes were observed from globular embryos to cotyledon embryos, threaded ducts, and vascular bundles (Fig. 5F). Following subculture, the surface or subsurface cells of EC differentiated into globular embryos with a spherical structure through the exogenous origin pathway (Fig. 5G). In contrast, the inner-layer cells in EC formed globular embryos via the endogenous origin pathway (Fig. 5H). This process of somatic embryogenesis can be explained by these dynamic changes.

Effects of different PGR combinations on adventitious bud induction and proliferation of P. praeruptorum

The differentiation and proliferation of adventitious buds in P. praeruptorum calli is crucial for establishing a regeneration system. This step determines the number of plant materials with high production and good growth. Therefore, it is important to combine auxins with cytokinins. In this study, well-developed calli from the proliferation culture were used to optimize the concentration of cytokinin and auxin combinations to promote adventitious bud differentiation and proliferation. The results indicated that the combination of BA and 2,4-D was more effective than BA, NAA, or IBA alone in inducing adventitious buds with varying growth potentials. The lowest bud differentiation rate was 62.22% (Fig. 6A). Treatment with low concentrations of BA did not promote the differentiation or proliferation of adventitious buds. However, an increase in BA concentration led to a significant increase in the number of adventitious buds induced by the medium-concentration treatment. The rate of bud differentiation was highest, reaching 97.78%, when treated with MS medium + 1.0 mg/L BA + 0.2 mg/L 2,4-D, and the shoot proliferation coefficient was significant, with an average of 11.7 adventitious buds per callus (Fig. 6B).

Different combinations of BA and auxins can also affect adventitious bud growth in P. praeruptorum. Bud differentiation and proliferation can be divided into four stages. Within 6 d of transfer culture, tender green or light-white bud spots appeared on the surface of the callus (Fig. 6C). In the medium supplemented with BA and 2,4-D, tender green bud spots rapidly protruded from the callus surface. The number of bud points gradually increased with increasing culture time. However, under the combined treatment with BA, NAA, and IBA, a few light-white bud spots appeared on the callus surface, and the growth time was relatively long. On the 10th day, tender green bud spots gradually differentiated and developed, forming tender bud leaf organs (Fig. 6D). After the 15th day, adventitious buds continued to grow, develop, and elongate (Fig. 6E). The clustered buds produced by BA and 2,4-D grew well; however, the number of metamorphotic seedlings was lower. The treatment groups with BA, NAA, and IBA induced a few light green vitrification seedlings, which grew at a slower rate and had a metamorphosis seedling rate of 13.96–34.23%. After the 25th day, the clustered buds grew vigorously, and the lateral roots differentiated at the base of the buds (Fig. 6F). Gradually, complete plants with thick green leaves were formed in the culture medium (Fig. 6G). The clustered buds were then separated and transferred to 1/2 MS medium without growth regulators, prior to domestication and transplantation. In summary, combinations of different types and concentrations of cytokinins and auxins heavily influence the differentiation, growth, and multiplication of clustered buds. To induce bud differentiation and proliferation, it is best to culture for 35 d in MS medium + 1.0 mg/L BA + 0.2 mg/L 2, 4-D medium. After separation and culture, P. praeruptorum plants can be hardened and transplanted.

Effects of explant types and different PGR combinations on organogenesis of P. praeruptorum

In vitro morphogenesis of adventitious buds can be achieved via organogenesis using different explants. Additionally, the ability to induce organogenesis and the effects on the growth of adventitious buds were evaluated using different combinations of BA, TDZ, and NAA concentrations. Our results indicated that adventitious buds can be formed morphologically in the leaves and stems of P. praeruptorum treated with NAA and various cytokinins. The leaves gradually extended and curled in MS medium supplemented with NAA and cytokinins. In contrast to the BA and 2,4-D treatments, regular calli were not promptly induced. However, a small amount of NEC was induced at the edges of the curled leaves, which began to form adventitious roots and continued to grow until the 21st day (Fig. 7A). As the culture time increased, adventitious buds gradually formed on the edges of the leaves or calli induced by adventitious roots. After 45–60 d of culture, the number of adventitious buds increased, and they elongated to form clustered buds with a stem height of 3–6 cm (Fig. 7B). Stem explants grew and elongated in the culture medium. Subsequently, calli were induced at both ends of the stem. After approximately 18 d, more adventitious roots were formed. Adventitious buds formed at both ends of the stem segments and on calli induced by adventitious roots after 25 d (Fig. 7C). After 45–60 d of culture, strong and stable clustered buds were formed (Fig. 7D).

In the present study, bud explants were used to induce adventitious bud organogenesis using different concentrations of NAA and BA/TDZ. The various explants exhibited similar trends in differentiation and proliferation when subjected to different PGR treatments. However, the differentiation rate and proliferation coefficient of adventitious buds induced by leaves were lower than those of stem explants, and the induced growth rate was slower. With the combination of NAA and TDZ, the medium concentration of TDZ significantly affected morphogenesis and proliferation of adventitious buds. The best results were obtained under the treatment of 0.5 mg/L TDZ + 1.0 mg/L NAA, with a differentiation rate of 66.67% for adventitious buds in stem segments and a bud proliferation coefficient of 73.33%. For leaves, the differentiation rate was 83.33%, and the bud proliferation coefficient was 5.89 (Fig. 7E and F). High TDZ concentrations inhibited adventitious bud induction to a certain extent. The cytokinin-to-auxin ratio was more important than the medium BA concentration. The highest differentiation rate and proliferation coefficient of adventitious buds were observed at a BA-to-NAA ratio of 1:10. The differentiation rate of adventitious buds was 100% under the treatment of 0.5 mg/L BA + 5.0 mg/L NAA. The rate of differentiation of adventitious buds and the coefficient of multiplication of leaves were 83.33% and 14.25%, respectively (Fig. 7E and F). As the concentration of cytokinin increased, the number of abnormal seedlings decreased, and higher concentrations of CK resulted in fewer abnormal seedlings. In conclusion, BA was more effective than TDZ alone in inducing adventitious bud formation and proliferation. The optimal organogenesis-mediated morphogenesis and proliferation of adventitious buds were achieved by inducing stem segments in MS medium + 0.5 mg/L BA + 5.0 mg/L NAA for 60 d.

Effect of BA and auxin combinations on rooting induction of adventitious buds of P. praeruptorum

The clustered buds of P. praeruptorum were separated into individual plants and transferred to a rooting medium. Different auxins were used to induce rooting, and their effects on rooting rate, root growth, and adventitious bud development were observed. Combinations of BA and NAA/IBA were added to 1/2 MS medium. The results showed that after continuous culture for 12–15 d, the base of adventitious buds expanded and began to root. After 4 weeks of culture, the number of roots for each adventitious bud increased. Three to six white roots extended from the base and were accompanied by lateral roots. The aerial parts of the tissue culture seedlings also continued to grow, with the leaves extending and darkening to a deep green color. At the same concentration, tissue culture seedlings treated with IBA exhibited strong rooting, developed roots (Fig. 8A and B), and had a larger extension and coverage area in the medium. Conversely, tissue culture seedlings treated with NAA displayed finer root morphology and weaker roots (Fig. 8C and D). Further analysis revealed that various combinations of BA and NAA/IBA had significantly different effects on the average root number of P. praeruptorum seedlings. However, the average root length and rooting rate did not differ significantly among the treatments. When the concentration of auxin was held constant, a lower concentration of BA had a more positive effect on the rooting state of the tissue culture seedlings. This was evidenced by the higher average root number and length than those in seedlings treated with higher concentrations of BA. The rooting rate was at least 84.72% across all treatment groups. The tissue culture seedlings had the highest rooting rate (97.22%), with an average root number of 6.29 and an average root length of 9.49 cm under the treatment of 0.2 mg/L BA + 1.0 mg/L IBA (Table 1). Lower concentrations of BA and IBA were more beneficial for the rooting of P. praeruptorum tissue culture plantlets, based on the rooting conditions of the seedlings. The optimal state for rooting P. praeruptorum tissue culture seedlings was achieved by culturing them in 1/2 MS medium + 0.2 mg/L BA + 1.0 mg/L IBA for 30 d.

Discussion

Plant regeneration primarily involves the cultivation and propagation of cells, tissues, organs, and intact plants under aseptic conditions [16]. The ability to regenerate depends on different types and concentrations of growth regulators, explants, and basic media. For in vitro plant cultures, the medium provides water and nutrients for plant growth and development. MS medium is the most suitable medium for stem growth of dicotyledons and monocotyledons because of its high nitrate and ammonium nitrogen contents [17–21]. In this study, MS medium was used as the primary medium to establish the regeneration system, which demonstrated the successful regeneration of Codonopsis pilosula [22]. The addition of activated carbon to the medium was found to absorb growth regulators and oxidizing substances, thereby enhancing plant growth and development while reducing browning effects [23]. All plant tissues and cells have the potential to regenerate; however, the degree of totipotency varies among plant varieties and among different tissues and cells within the same plant. Therefore, the regeneration abilities of the different explants varied. Effective explants for differentiation and regeneration include cotyledons, hypocotyls, tender leaves, and young stems of medicinal plants such as Scutellaria bornmuelleri, Cassia angustifolia, and Santalum album L [24–28]. Young leaves and stems were used as explants to induce EC and adventitious buds, respectively. In 2023, Yang et al. used tender leaves and stems of Rhodomyrtus tomentosa seedlings to induce calli and buds [29]. The varying regeneration abilities of explants in different parts may be attributed to differences in endogenous hormones [30]. The addition of exogenous PGRs can alter endogenous hormone levels and regulate key enzymes in the cell cycle, leading to changes in plant morphological regeneration [31]. Auxins and cytokinins primarily regulate the expression of several genes involved in growth and development. The developmental process of somatic embryogenesis or organogenesis is determined by the combination and proportion of auxins and cytokinins [32, 33].

There are two primary methods for in vitro plant morphological regeneration: somatic embryogenesis and organogenesis. Regulation of the time rhythm has a relatively small effect on organogenesis. Indirect somatic embryogenesis was successfully achieved in P. praeruptorum. We observed the morphological characteristics of SEs at different stages, optimized the pathway of indirect organogenesis, simplified the culture steps of organogenesis, and improved the growth efficiency of the morphological regeneration of P. praeruptorum. Our results describe the growth patterns and developmental characteristics of different explants during the process of morphological establishment, while highlighting the advantages of the two methods of plant regeneration. Indirect organogenesis allows for the regeneration of numerous complete plants from explants in only 90 d. This method reduces production costs and is suitable for rapid micropropagation. Indirect somatic embryogenesis can serve as a foundation for subsequent genetic transformations.

Regeneration of tissue culture plantlets of P. praeruptorum through indirect somatic embryogenesis

Plants can undergo dedifferentiation into embryonic stem cells through either individual cells or cell clusters. Alternatively, ECs can be formed indirectly through tissue weaving or organ dedifferentiation. These cells can then differentiate into complete plants [4], forming a complete pathway for somatic embryogenesis. Indirect somatic embryogenesis is commonly used for large-scale plant propagation owing to its high yield. During the process of proliferation, EC induced by explants develop into SEs from single cells or cell masses on the surface or within the callus, which is primarily influenced by auxin growth regulators during cell proliferation [34]. Exogenous auxins promote changes in endogenous hormones and initiate fine SEs formation. The production of ethylene synthesis precursors, such as 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid, is accompanied by the formation of EC [35]. Because of EC’s prolonged proliferation of ECs, the indirect somatic embryo pathway can grow and proliferate in many plants compared with direct embryogenesis [36].

The stems and tender leaves of P. praeruptorum seedlings were used as explants to induce EC under 2,4-D treatment. High concentrations of 2,4-D significantly affect callus induction, which has also been observed in other plants [37, 38]. Treatment with 2 mg/L 2,4-D resulted in the highest callus induction rate for stem segments, leading to the highest number and growth morphology of EC. A hormone concentration of 2 mg/L 2,4-D was most effective in inducing EC in both Pogonatherum paniceum and Zea mays [39, 40]. Cytokinins were also found to have a synergistic effect during EC induction, with appropriate amounts promoting cell division and increasing the number of embryogenic calli [41]. Studies have reported that callus induction is optimal using MS medium with 3 mg/L 2,4-D and 0.5 mg/L BA [42]. Similarly, cytokinins and auxins promote callus proliferation and play important roles in increasing the frequency of somatic embryo formation [43]. Previous studies have shown that the combination of cytokinins with NAA and 2,4-D can increase the callus proliferation coefficient of Paeonia lactiflora and peony, which is beneficial for callus proliferation [44, 45]. An interesting phenomenon was observed. Various PGR combinations have distinct effects on EC growth. A yellow-green callus with compact and moderately hard tissue is more suitable for somatic embryo formation and differentiation. In contrast, cytokinins (BA and 6-KT) plus 2,4-D increased the callus proliferation coefficient more than cytokinins, 2,4-D, or NAA alone. During EC proliferation, we observed tissue morphology at different stages of SEs. These were divided into globular, heart-shaped, torpedo, and cotyledon embryos, similar to observations in Angelica sinensis [46]. Mature and developing vascular bundles composed of xylem and phloem were observed in the torpedo and cotyledon embryos. In addition, an obvious bipolar structure composed of stem and root primordia was observed. It has been demonstrated that SEs develop and differentiate into organs, thereby enhancing plant survival during domestication and in the wild [47].

Cytokinins are commonly used as growth regulators and induce adventitious buds by stimulating competent cells during embryo redifferentiation and promoting their proliferation in coordination with auxins [48]. The concentration and ratio of cytokinins to auxins have a significant effect on adventitious bud differentiation in Lilium [49]. This study found that the bud differentiation rate and multiplication coefficient were low when the ratio of BA to 2,4-D was 1:2 or 1:1. However, the rate and coefficient increased as the ratio increased. The best growth potential for adventitious buds was obtained at a ratio of 5:1. This finding supports the theory that a high cytokinin-to-auxin ratio promotes adventitious bud formation [41]. The ratio of BA to auxin significantly affected the differentiation and proliferation of adventitious buds in P. praeruptorum. However, only the 10:1 ratio showed a decrease compared to the 5:1 ratio. However, the optimal cytokinin-auxin ratio for adventitious bud regeneration and differentiation requires further investigation.

Regeneration of tissue culture plantlets of P. praeruptorum through indirect organogenesis

Plant regeneration can occur through organogenesis or somatic embryogenesis. Despite the division of organogenesis and somatic embryogenesis into direct and indirect methods, organogenesis does not produce SEs. Direct organogenesis involves regeneration of adventitious buds or roots from the meristem or organs of the plant. In contrast, indirect organogenesis causes NEC to differentiate into adventitious buds or roots based on plant tissue pluripotency [50]. The PGR cytokinins BA and TDZ are responsible for initiating organogenesis and somatic embryogenesis, respectively, and their concentrations determine the plant regeneration pathway [51]. In the present study, we investigated the effects of combining BA/TDZ and 2,4-D/NAA on the regeneration of P. praeruptorum explants. Mixing BA/TDZ and 2,4-D enhanced the indirect somatic embryogenesis pathway. In contrast, mixing BA/TDZ and NAA significantly affected the indirect organogenesis pathway. BA is a stable adenine cytokinin that promotes the production of the endogenous component isopentenyladenosine (iPA) by regulating the expression of cytokinin synthesis- and activation-related genes [42]. This effectively stimulated the regeneration and proliferation of adventitious buds. Previous studies have shown that a higher BA/NAA ratio is beneficial for bud organogenesis in Lonicera caerulea, Lilium pumilum, and Hedychium forrestii Diels [52–54]. Our study findings indicate that the combination of BA and NAA promotes bud organ regeneration in P. praeruptorum. However, a higher BA/NAA ratio resulted in a lower bud differentiation rate for tender leaves and stem segments, as well as a low bud proliferation coefficient. The bud differentiation and proliferation coefficients showed an upward trend as the ratio gradually decreased. The most significant effect was observed at a ratio of 1:10. However, organogenesis of Metabriggsia ovalifolia at lower BA concentrations was similar to this process [55]. This may be because of the higher ratio of auxin to cytokinin, which promotes the differentiation of adventitious roots. Numerous adventitious roots first dedifferentiated into NEC and then differentiated into adventitious buds, increasing the rate and number of adventitious buds.

The success of plant morphological regeneration through indirect organogenesis depends on high-quality root culture. Auxins are key regulators of cell division that promote cell differentiation and participate in root formation, growth, and development, and they also play an important role in promoting rooting [56, 57]. Our study findings demonstrated that IBA had a better growth-promoting effect than NAA at the same concentration and was generally superior to NAA in terms of average root length and rooting rate (Table 1). The rooting culture of medicinal plants such as Anaphyllum wightii Schott and Kaempferia angustifolia showed that IBA significantly promoted rooting culture [38, 58]. However, NAA induced the formation of finer roots, although the rooting effect was most diminished at higher concentrations. Low NAA concentrations cause uneven roots, whereas high NAA concentrations negatively affect root induction [59]. Cytokinins and auxins are regulatory factors involved in rooting and culture. BA has proven beneficial for root induction and development during rooting [60]. The quality of the in vitro plant rooting culture often determines the success rate of subsequent domestication. Tissue culture seedlings with weak roots have difficulty resisting excessive stimulation from the external environment. Additionally, insufficient or excessively short roots can negatively affect the nutrient uptake of plants from the soil, which is not conducive to survival. Therefore, tissue culture seedlings with well-developed roots are likely to survive domestication.

Conclusions

Our results suggest that stem segments of P. praeruptorum seedlings are suitable explants for efficient propagation, and different concentrations of PGR combinations can influence the induction pathway of stem segments. The combination of BA and NAA can induce adventitious roots in explants, leading to the development of complete plants through indirect organogenesis. Stem explants could be regenerated into 66–116 tissue culture seedlings within 90 d of indirect organogenesis. Alternatively, BA and 2,4-D could induce healthy embryogenic callus, which developed into complete plants through somatic embryogenesis. Moreover, the pathway produced 71–187 tissue culture seedlings after 123 d of in vitro culture. However, this method, while enhancing production capacity, requires more time and incurs higher costs. An in vitro regeneration system for P. praeruptorum was systematically established through the indirect regeneration of somatic embryogenesis and organogenesis. However, the induction rate of EC was relatively low, indicating that the somatic embryogenesis pathway requires further optimization. Additionally, the use of cotyledons, hypocotyls, and other explants, along with more precise combinations of PGRs, could further optimize the regeneration system for P. praeruptorum. Moreover, as a potential future research direction, the regeneration system for P. praeruptorum can be further optimized to enhance organogenesis and induce numerous adventitious roots, serving as bioreactors for the production of essential secondary metabolites.

Unified Dark Matter and Dark Energy in a model of Non-Canonical Scalar-Tensor Theory
Nihal Jalal Pullisseri, Sanil Unnikrishnan

Abstract
We consider a model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory in which the kinetic term in the Brans-Dicke action is replaced by a non-canonical scalar field Lagrangian ℒ ⁡ ( X , ϕ ) = λ ​ X α ​ ϕ β − V ⁡ ( ϕ ) \mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}-V(\phi) where X = ( 1 / 2 ) ​ ∂ μ ϕ ​ ∂ μ ϕ X=(1/2)\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi and α \alpha , β \beta and λ \lambda are parameters of the model. This can be considered as a simple non-canonical generalization of the Brans-Dicke theory with a potential term which corresponds to a special case of this model with the values of the parameter α = 1 \alpha=1 , β = − 1 \beta=-1 and λ = 2 w B ​ D \lambda=2w_{{}_{BD}} where w B ​ D w_{{}_{BD}} is the Brans-Dicke parameter. Considering a spatially flat Friedmann-Robertson-Walker Universe with scale factor a ⁡ ( t ) a(t) , it is shown that, in the matter free Universe, the kinetic term λ ​ X α ​ ϕ β \lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta} can lead to a power law solution a ⁡ ( t ) ∝ t n a(t)\propto t^{n} but the maximum possible value of n n turns out to be ( 1 + 3 ) / 4 ≈ 0.683 (1+\sqrt{3})/4\approx 0.683 . When α ≥ 18 \alpha\geq 18 , this model can lead to a solution a ⁡ ( t ) ∝ t 2 / 3 a(t)\propto t^{2/3} , thereby mimicking the evolution of scale factor in a cold dark matter dominated epoch with Einstein’s General Relativity (GR). With the addition of a linear potential term V ⁡ ( ϕ ) = V 0 ​ ϕ V(\phi)=V_{0}\phi , it is shown that this model mimics the standard Λ \Lambda CDM model type evolution of the Universe. The larger the value of α \alpha , the closer the evolution of a ⁡ ( t ) a(t) in this model to that in the Λ \Lambda CDM model based on Einstein’s GR. The purpose of this paper is to demonstrate that this model with a linear potential can mimic the GR based Λ \Lambda CDM model. However, with an appropriate choice of the potential V ⁡ ( ϕ ) V(\phi) , this model can provide a unified description of both dark matter and dynamical dark energy, as if it were based on Einstein’s GR.



I
Introduction

Cosmological observations, such as the light curves of Type Ia supernovae and cosmic microwave background radiation, Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data, and the large scale structure formation data strongly indicate that the Universe is currently undergoing accelerated expansion
Riess et al. 1998
;
Perlmutter et al. 1999
;
Adame et al. 2025
;
Adame et al. 2025
;
Di Valentino et al. 2020
;
Ade et al. 2014
;
Aghanim et al. 2020a
;
Aghanim et al. 2020b
;
Eisenstein et al. 2005
.
Within the framework of Einstein’s General Theory of Relativity, this observed acceleration implies that approximately
70\%
of the total content of the Universe at the present epoch is dark energy, which is defined as a form of matter with negative pressure and the ratio of pressure to its energy density is less than
-1/3

Copeland et al. 2006
;
Sahni and Starobinsky 2000
;
Padmanabhan 2003
;
Frieman et al. 2008
;
Peebles and Ratra 2003
;
Li et al. 2011
;
Bamba et al. 2012
.
Furthermore, cosmological observations also suggest that about
26\%
of the Universe’s total content is dark matter, which behaves as pressureless matter
Cirelli et al. 2024
;
Sahni 2004
;
Bertone and Hooper 2018
.
The remaining
4\%
consists of ordinary matter, the types of matter detectable in laboratories, primarily composed of Baryons.

Although numerous models of dark matter and dark energy have been proposed, the fundamental nature of both remains unknown.
Like ordinary matter, dark matter behaves gravitationally as an attractive form of matter, playing a pivotal role in the formation of large-scale structures in the Universe.
In contrast, dark energy acts as a repulsive form of matter, driving the observed accelerated expansion of the Universe at late times. Despite their differing gravitational behaviors, the energy densities of both dark matter and dark energy at the present epoch are of similar order of magnitude
Zlatev et al. 1999
.
This suggests the possibility that dark matter and dark energy could arise from a single matter content, which behaves differently at different epochs and cosmological scales.
Models of a unified dark sector have also been explored in the literature
Scherrer 2004
;
Bertacca et al. 2007
;
Bertacca et al. 2010
;
Bose and Majumdar 2009
;
Sahni and Sen 2017
;
Mishra and Sahni 2021
;
Benisty and Guendelman 2018
;
Benisty et al. 2019
;
Benisty and Guendelman 2017
.

Considering dark matter and dark energy as separate entities, one of the simplest models to explain the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe is to add a cosmological term
\Lambda
, in Einstein’s equation
Weinberg 1989
;
Carroll 2001
.
Together with pressureless dark matter, also known as cold dark matter, this model with the cosmological constant is known as the
\Lambda
CDM model.
Although recent cosmological observations, such as the Hubble tension and Dark Energy Survey Instrument (DESI) Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) observational data
Dainotti et al. 2023
;
Perivolaropoulos and Skara 2022
;
Scolnic et al. 2025
;
Verde et al. 2024
;
Pourojaghi et al. 2025
;
Di Valentino et al. 2021
, may indicate a deviation from the cosmological expansion predicted by the
\Lambda
CDM model, this simple concordance model has still not been ruled out
Blanchard et al. 2024
;
Colgáin et al. 2024
;
Cortês and Liddle 2024
.
For the cosmological constant, the equation of state parameter, which is defined as the ratio of pressure to energy density, turns out to be
-1
.

In addition to the non-zero cosmological constant as an explanation for the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe, models of dark energy based on scalar fields have also been proposed in the literature. These include quintessence, Tachyon, phantom, and k-essence models of dark energy
Tsujikawa 2013
;
Nojiri and Odintsov 2006a
;
Padmanabhan 2002
;
Caldwell 2002
;
Capozziello et al. 2006
;
Armendariz-Picon et al. 2001
;
Chiba et al. 2000
.
While quintessence corresponds to scalar field dark energy whose Lagrangian has the standard structure of a kinetic term minus a potential term, k-essence corresponds to non-canonical scalar field models of dark energy, where the Lagrangian is a general function of the scalar field and the kinetic term
Garriga and Mukhanov 1999
.
The equation of state parameter in all these types of scalar field models of dark energy deviates from
-1
and can also evolve with time.

The existence of dark energy driving the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe is based on the assumption that gravity on cosmological scales operates according to Einstein’s GR.
However, it is also possible that this assumption may be incorrect, and that the gravitational laws on cosmological scales deviate from those described by GR.
Various models of modified gravity that account for the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe have been explored in the literature
Carroll et al. 2004
;
Nojiri and Odintsov 2006b
;
Clifton et al. 2012
;
Koyama 2016
;
Nojiri and Odintsov 2011
;
Nojiri et al. 2017
.
These include Brans-Dicke theory, scalar-tensor theory,
f(R)
gravity models, and others

;
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2010
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2010
;
Faraoni and Capozziello 2011
;
Capozziello and De Laurentis 2011
;
Elizalde et al. 2008
;
Hussain et al. 2024
;
Hussain et al. 2025
;
Arora et al. 2025
.

The Brans-Dicke theory is a class of scalar-tensor theories in which Newton’s gravitational constant,
G_{{}_{N}}
, is not a constant but a dynamic quantity whose evolution is influenced by the matter content of the Universe
Brans and Dicke 1961
.
In this framework,
G_{{}_{N}}\propto 1/\phi
, where
\phi
represents the Brans-Dicke field, and its evolution depends on both the Brans-Dicke parameter,
w_{{}_{BD}}
, and the energy-momentum tensor of the matter content.
The Brans-Dicke theory with a cosmological constant term has been studied to alleviate the Hubble Tension
Solà Peracaula et al. 2020
.
A general canonical scalar-tensor theory can be seen as an extension of the Brans-Dicke theory, where the Brans-Dicke parameter is a function of the field
\phi
, i.e.,
w_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)
, and a potential term,
V(\phi)
, is added to the Brans-Dicke action
Fujii and Maeda 2007
;
Faraoni 2004
;
Elizalde et al. 2004
.
Modified gravity models, such as
f(R)
gravity, can also be described as a canonical scalar-tensor theory with
w_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)=0
, but with a non-zero potential term,
V(\phi)

Chiba 2003
.
The scalar-tensor theories are referred to as such because they extend beyond the traditional framework of Einstein’s gravity, which solely uses the metric tensor to describe gravitational interactions.
In contrast, Scalar-Tensor theories introduce additional scalar fields along with the metric tensor to describe the gravitational field.

A non-canonical scalar-tensor theory generalizes the canonical scalar-tensor theory by replacing its standard kinetic term with a more general Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
, where
X=(1/2)\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi
represents the kinetic term.
This is equivalent to modifying the kinetic term in Brans-Dicke theory by replacing it with
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
.
Models with a tachyonic form of
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
have been shown to drive the accelerated expansion of the Universe
Unnikrishnan and Seshadri 2008
, and other models with different forms of
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
have also been explored
Ferrari et al. 2025
;
Jana and Kar 2017
;
Quiros 2019
;
Kim 2005
.

In this paper, we introduce a model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory in which the usual kinetic term in the Brans-Dicke action is replaced by a term proportional to
X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants.
This can be considered as a generalization of the Brans-Dicke theory, which corresponds to a special case of this non-canonical scalar-tensor theory with parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
set to
1
and
-1
, respectively.
Considering a spatially flat Friedman-Robertson-Walker Universe, we show that this non-canonical scalar-tensor model admits a power law solution for the scale factor
a(t)\propto t^{n}
, along with a similar solution for the scalar field, i.e.,
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
, in a matter free Universe.
However, in this model, it turns out that the maximum value that
n
in the solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
can take is
0.68
.
Therefore, the matter free case in this model cannot lead to accelerated expansion of the Universe, which requires
n>1
.
However,
n=2/3
is a possible solution in this model, which corresponds to the solution for the scale factor
a(t)
in Einstein’s GR when the Universe is dominated by pressureless matter.
Further, by adding a linear potential term
V(\phi)
to the action, we show that this model mimics the evolution of
a(t)
as if it were in the
\Lambda
CDM model in Einstein’s GR. The greater the value of the parameter
\alpha
, the closer the solution
a(t)
in this model approaches the corresponding
a(t)
in the GR
\Lambda
CDM model.
Notably, in our model, the scalar field in the non-canonical scalar-tensor theory behaves like cold dark matter at early times and like
\Lambda
(dark energy) at late times, providing a unified description of the dark sector.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec. (
II
) we review the canonical scalar-tensor theory, which is equivalent to Brans-Dicke theory with a potential
V(\phi)
and with a scalar field dependent Brans-Dicke parameter

.

Further, in Sec. (

) that this model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory mimic the evolution of the scale factor as if it were a
\Lambda
CDM model based on Einstein’s GR.
We summarize the main results of this paper in Sec. (
VII
).

II
Canonical Scalar-tensor Theory

The action in a general canonical scalar-tensor theory is given by

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-f(\psi)R+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial^{\mu}\psi-U(\psi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

(1)

where
U(\psi)
is the scalar field potential and
(1/2)\partial_{a}\psi\partial^{a}\psi
is the kinetic term and
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is the Lagrangian of the matter.
The above action replaces the gravity part of the standard Einstein-Hilbert action namely
(1/16\pi G)\left[-R\right]
with the term
(1/16\pi)\left[-f(\psi)R+(1/2)\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial^{\mu}\psi-U(\psi)\right]
. Thus, in the scalar-tensor theory of gravity, not only the metric tensor but also the value of the scalar field determines the gravitational interactions. Note that, compared with the standard Einstein-Hilbert action, the above action for the scalar-tensor theory also implies that the Newtonian gravitational constant
G=1/f(\psi)
is no longer constant, but instead depends on the scalar field, which may evolve with time.

Without loss of generality, we can introduce a field
\phi
defined as

\phi=f(\psi),

where
f
is a smooth and invertible function.
Using this, the above action (
1
) becomes

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-\phi R+\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)\left(\frac{\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi}{\phi}\right)-V(\phi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

(2)

where

\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)=\frac{f(\psi)}{2f^{\prime}(\psi)}~~~\mathrm{and}~~~~V(\phi)=U(\psi)

where
\psi
can be expressed as a function of
\phi
by inverting the function
\phi=f(\psi)
.
A prototype of the scalar-tensor theory called the Brans-Dicke theory corresponds to the case when in the action (
2
),
\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)=\omega_{{}_{BD}}=\mathrm{constant}
and
V(\phi)=0
.
With this, the action for the Brans-Dicke theory is given by
Brans and Dicke 1961

\mathcal{S}_{{}_{\mathrm{BD}}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-\phi R+\omega_{{}_{BD}}\left(\frac{\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi}{\phi}\right)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x.

(3)

In Brans-Dicke theory, the Newtonian gravitational constant
G=1/\phi
is not a constant.
Note that dividing the kinetic term in the Brans-Dicke action by
\phi
makes the Brans-Dicke parameter
\omega_{{}_{BD}}
a dimensionless constant.

Comparing the standard Brans-Dicke theory represented by the action (
3
) with the action for the general canonical scalar-tensor theory given in Eq. (
2
), it is evident that action (
2
) can be considered as the generalized Brans-Dicke theory where its parameter
\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)
is no longer a constant and the evolution of
\phi
is also governed by a non-zero potential term
V(\phi)
.
Note that since action (
2
) is an equivalent representation of action (
1
), the canonical scalar-tensor theory represented by action (
2
) is indeed a canonical generalization of the Brans-Dicke theory.
Even an
f(R)
model of modified gravity can be equivalently represented by such a generalized Brans-Dicke theory with its parameter
\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)=0
but with a non-zero potential term
V(\phi)
in the action (
2
)
Chiba 2003
;
Olmo 2007
.

III
General Non-Canonical scalar-tensor Theory

The action for a general non-canonical scalar-tensor theory with a non-canonical scalar field can be expressed as

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-f(\psi)R+p(Y,\psi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

(4)

where
p(Y,\psi)
is the Lagrangian of the non-canonical scalar field
\psi
where
Y=(1/2)\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial^{\mu}\psi
is the kinetic term.
Within the framework of Einstein’s GR, a non-canonical scalar field can drive inflation
Unnikrishnan et al. 2012
;
Armendariz-Picon et al. 1999
;
Chen et al. 2007
and it can also drive the late-time accelerated expansion of the Universe as a model of dark energy
Unnikrishnan 2008
;
Armendariz-Picon et al. 2000
;
Malquarti et al. 2003
;
Csillag and Jensko 2025
.
In this paper, we consider a non-canonical scalar field within the framework of scalar-tensor theories in which the non-canonical scalar field is non-minimally coupled to the Ricci scalar in the Einstein-Hilbert action and to investigate the possibility of such a scalar field behaving as both dark matter and dark energy.
A non-canonical scalar field which is non-minimally coupled to gravity can also drive inflation
Oikonomou 2021
.

Note that the action (
4
) is the non-canonical generalization of the action (
1
) with the standard canonical scalar field Lagrangian in the action (
1
)
viz
..
\mathcal{L}=Y-V(\psi)
being replaced by a non-canonical Lagrangian
p(Y,\psi)
.
Similar to the analysis done in the preceding section and without loss of generality, we can introduce a new scalar field defined as
\phi=f(\psi)
.
With this, the action (
4
) becomes

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-\phi R+\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

(5)

where
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
is the Lagrangian of the scalar field
\phi
, which is a general function of
\phi
and the kinetic term
X
is defined as

X=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi.

In the above action (
5
), the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
is such that
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)\,=\,p\left(Y(X,\phi)\,,\,\psi(\phi)\right)
which is obtained by inverting the function
\phi=f(\psi)
and substituting this inverted function in the action (
4
).

Note that the standard Brans-Dicke theory described by the action (
3
) corresponds to a special case of action (
5
) with
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=2\,\omega_{{}_{BD}}X/\phi
.
Further, the generalized Brans-Dicke theory described by the action (
2
) corresponds to the case when, in the action (
5
),
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=2\,\omega_{{}_{BD}}(\phi)X/\phi\,-\,V(\phi)
.
Therefore, the action (
5
) or equivalently the action (
4
) can be viewed as the non-canonical generalization of the standard Brans-Dicke theory.

In this paper, we focus on a particular form of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
in the action (
5
) which can lead to a
\Lambda
CDM like evolution of the scale factor in the absence of matter. In this scenario, the same scalar field can mimic the behavior of both cold dark matter and dark energy. Before proceeding to the specific model, we first describe in this section all the relevant equations for the general non-canonical scalar-tensor theory defined by the action.

On varying the action (
5
) with respect to the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}

(6)

where

is the Einstein-tensor,

is the energy-momentum tensor of the matter content described by the Lagrangian

in the action (

(7)

Note that in Eq. (
6
),
\mathcal{L}_{X}
is the partial derivative of Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)
with respect to the kinetic term
X
.
On varying the action (
5
) with respect to the scalar field
\phi
leads to the following equation of motion for
\phi

\mathcal{L}_{X}\Box\phi\,=\,\mathcal{L}_{\phi}-2X\mathcal{L}_{X\phi}-\mathcal{L}_{XX}\partial_{\mu}X\partial^{\mu}\phi-R,

(8)

By taking the trace of equation (
6

\Box\phi=\left(\frac{8\pi T}{\phi\mathcal{L}_{X}+3}\right)\,+\,\left(\frac{\phi\mathcal{L}_{\phi}+X\mathcal{L}_{X}-2X\phi\mathcal{L}_{X\phi}-2\mathcal{L}-\phi\mathcal{L}_{XX}\partial_{\mu}X\partial^{\mu}\phi}{\phi\mathcal{L}_{X}+3}\right),

(9)

is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor of the matter content described by the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
in the action (
5
).

We consider a spatially flat Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) Universe with the line element given by

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-a^{2}(t)[dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}],

(10)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor.
The evolution of the scale factor is governed by Einstein’s equations (
6
), which leads to the following two Friedmann’s equations

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{8\pi}{3\phi}\right)\rho\,-\,H\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\right)\,+\,\frac{1}{6\phi}\left[2X\mathcal{L}_{X}-\mathcal{L}\right],

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

Substituting this in Eqs. (

IV
A model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory

In this paper, we introduce a model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory with the action given by

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\left[-\phi R\,+\,\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}\,-\,V(\phi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x,

(17)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\lambda
are constants or parameters of this model.
Note that the standard Brans-Dicke theory described by the action (
3
) corresponds to a special case of the above action (

) with
\alpha=1
,
\beta=-1
,
\lambda=2\omega_{{}_{BD}}

.
Therefore, the action (

\alpha

The action (

) as

\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)\,=\,\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}\,-\,V(\phi).

(18)

With this Lagrangian

, the Friedmann equations (
11
) and (
12
) and the field equation for
\phi

viz
. Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{4\pi}{3\phi}\right)\left(\rho+3p\right)\,-\,\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\frac{H\dot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{6}\right)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1}\,+\,\frac{V(\phi)}{6\phi},

(20)

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi(\rho-3p)-3H\dot{\phi}(\alpha\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3)+(\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta-2)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}}{\alpha(2\alpha-1)\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3}

(21)

\displaystyle\;+\;\frac{2V(\phi)-\phi V^{\prime}(\phi)}{\alpha(2\alpha-1)\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3}.

In this paper, we are interested in investigating the possibility of a
\Lambda
CDM-like evolution of the scale factor
a(t)
in this model when the contribution of matter is insignificant i.e. when
\mathcal{L}_{m}=0
in the action (
17
).
This model will then lead to a uniform description of both dark matter and dark energy by the same non-canonical scalar field of the scalar-tensor theory.

We are interested in the solution for
a(t)
in this model such that it mimics the same solution as one gets in the
\Lambda
CDM model when the gravity is described by Einstein’s GR.
Note that in Einstein’s GR, the scale factor satisfies the following Friedmann equations in the
\Lambda
CDM model

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{8\pi G}{3}\right)\rho_{{}_{m0}}a^{-3}\,+\,\frac{\Lambda}{3},

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{4\pi G}{3}\right)\rho_{{}_{m0}}a^{-3}\,+\,\frac{\Lambda}{3},

(23)

where
\rho_{{}_{m0}}
is the energy density of the cold dark matter at the present epoch, at which
a(t)=1
and
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant.
Comparing Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
) with Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
), respectively, it is evident that if we consider a linear potential of the form
V(\phi)=V_{0}\phi
in the action (
17
), then twice the value of the constant
V_{0}
can act as the cosmological constant term in the Friedmann equations (
19
) and (
20
).
However, in the matter free case with
\rho=p=0
in Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
), to have a
\Lambda
CDM model like solution for
a(t)
, it is required that initially
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
to mimic the initial cold dark matter dominated evolution in a similar way as in the case of
\Lambda
CDM model within Einstein’s GR.
This is possible if the kinetic term in the Lagrangian (
18
)
viz
.
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
leads to a solution
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
initially when the contribution of the linear potential term with
V(\phi)=V_{0}\phi
is insignificant.
Later, this linear potential term can lead to the accelerated expansion of the Universe, similar to the one driven by the cosmological constant term in Einstein’s GR.
In the next section, we will discuss the possibility of having a solution of the type
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
when the kinetic term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
significantly dominates the potential term in the Lagrangian (
18
) and with insignificant contribution of matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
in the action (
17
).

V
Power law solution when
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}

Let us first consider the scenario when the contribution of matter described by the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
and the potential term
V(\phi)
in the action (
17
) are insignificant. This means that we are considering the case when only the term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
in the action (
17
) drives the expansion of the Universe.
With
V(\phi)=0
along with
\rho=p=0
, the Friedmann equations (
19
) and (
20
) along with the field equation of
\phi
given by Eq. (
21
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\right)\,+\,\left(\frac{2\alpha-1}{6}\right)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1},

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\frac{H\dot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{6}\right)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1},

(25)

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-3H\dot{\phi}(\alpha\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3)+(\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta-2)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}}{\alpha(2\alpha-1)\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3}.

(26)

We are interested in investigating whether the above equations admit a power law solution for both
a(t)
and
\phi(t)
and for what values of
\alpha
and
\beta
such power law solutions are viable.
We consider the power law solutions of the form

\displaystyle a(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{{}_{i}}\left(\frac{t}{t_{i}}\right)^{n},

(27)

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{{}_{i}}\left(\frac{t}{t_{i}}\right)^{m},

(28)

where
m
and
n
are real numbers with
n>0
as we consider the expanding Universe solution for
a(t)
.
In Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
),
a_{{}_{i}}
and
\phi_{{}_{i}}
are the values of the scale factor and scalar field, respectively, at an initial time
t=t_{i}
.

From the Friedmann equations (
25
) and (
26
), it follows that

\frac{\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1}}{2}=-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{\phi}-\frac{2H\dot{\phi}}{\phi}-\left(2\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}+\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right).

(29)

Substituting the power law solutions given by Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) in the above equation implies that

\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1}\,=\,\frac{C_{1}}{t^{2}},

(30)

where the constant
C_{1}
is given by

C_{1}\,=\,-2\left(3n^{2}+m^{2}+2mn-2n-m\right).

(31)

With
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
, it follows from Eq. (
30
) that

\beta\,=\,\frac{(m-2)\,+\,2\alpha(1-m)}{m}.

(32)

It is evident from the above equation that when
\alpha=1
one gets
\beta=-1
.
Note that
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-1
in the action (
17
) with
V(\phi)=0
corresponds to the Brans-Dicke theory described by the action (
3
).
As we are considering the non-canonical scalar-tensor theory, which corresponds to the case when
\alpha\neq 1
, Eq. (
32
) can be expressed as

m\,=\,\frac{2(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha+\beta-1}.

(33)

This relation (
33
) gives the value of
m
in the power law solution
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
for a given value of the parameter
\alpha
and
\beta
in the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
with the assumption that
\alpha\neq 1
.

To determine the value of
n
in the solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
for given values of
\alpha
and
\beta
, we substitute Eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) in the Friedmann equation (
24
) and this gives the following equation

\alpha\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{m^{2}-m-2n-mn}{3n^{2}-2n+m^{2}-m+2mn}\right].

(34)

Note that while arriving at Eq. (
29
) and the consequent Eq. (
32
), we have combined both the Friedmann equations (
24
)
and (
25
) whereas Eq. (
34
) follows only from the first Friedmann equation (
24
).
It can be verified that on substituting the power law solutions
a(t)\propto t^{n}
and
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
in the second Friedmann equation (
25
) one gets the same relations for
\alpha
as given in Eq. (
34
).

On substituting the power law solutions
viz
..
a(t)\propto t^{n}
and
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
in the field equation for
\phi
described by the Eq (
26
), we get the following relation

m(m-1)\,=\,\frac{-3mn(2\alpha C_{1}+3m^{2})+C_{1}m^{2}(\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta-2)}{2\alpha(2\alpha-1)C_{1}+3m^{2}},

(35)

where
C_{1}
is given by Eq. (
31
).

It is straightforward to verify that Eq. (
35
) is consistent with Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
).
Consequently, Eq. (
35
) is not an independent relation. Therefore, the two Friedmann equations and the field equation for
\phi

viz
.. Eqs. (
24
) to (
26
) implies that for power law solution of the form
a(t)\propto t^{n}
and
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
, the two independent equations given by Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) must be satisfied using which the values of
m
and
n
can be determined for given values of the parameter
\alpha
and
\beta
in the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
.

To determine the value of
n
in the power law solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
, we use Eq. (
34
) together with Eq. (
33
) for
m
.
Consequently, Eq. (
34
) can be expressed as a quadratic equation on
n
which leads to the following roots for
n

n=\frac{2-\beta+\alpha(2\beta-1)\pm\sqrt{S}}{6\alpha(2\alpha+\beta-1)},

(36)

where

S\,=\,(\beta-2)^{2}+24\alpha^{3}(1+\beta)+\alpha(8+22\beta-4\beta^{2})+\alpha^{2}(-35-40\beta+4\beta^{2}).

(37)

For
n
to be a real number, it is required that
S\geq 0
.
Note that, for a given value of
\alpha
, Eq. (
37
) can be expressed as a quadratic equation in
\beta
in the following way

S\,=\,\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}\beta^{2}+\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}\beta+\mathcal{P}_{{}_{0}},

(38)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(2\alpha-1)^{2},

(39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,24\alpha^{3}-40\alpha^{2}+22\alpha-4,

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{{}_{0}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,24\alpha^{3}-35\alpha^{2}+8\alpha+4.

(41)

Note that
\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}>0
for
\alpha>1
.
Therefore, for a given value of
\alpha
, Eq. (
38
) implies that
S
is a quadratic function of
\beta
with its parabola opening upwards.
This means that, for
S\geq 0
, the value of
\beta
must be such that

\beta\,\leq\,\frac{-\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}-\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}^{2}-4\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}\mathcal{P}_{{}_{0}}}}{2\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}}~~~~~~~\mathrm{or}~~~~~~~\beta\,\geq\,\frac{-\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{{}_{1}}^{2}-4\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}\mathcal{P}_{{}_{0}}}}{2\mathcal{P}_{{}_{2}}}.

(42)

Consequently, for
n
to be a real number, the value of
\beta
for each value of
\alpha>1
must be such that

\beta\,\notin\,\left(B\,,\,B^{\prime}\right),

(43)

where

\displaystyle B\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-3\alpha\,-\,6\sqrt{\frac{(\alpha-1)^{3}\alpha}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}}\,+\,2,

(44)

\displaystyle B^{\prime}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-3\alpha\,+\,6\sqrt{\frac{(\alpha-1)^{3}\alpha}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}}\,+\,2.

(45)

This forbidden open interval
\left(B\,,\,B^{\prime}\right)
for the value of the
\beta
is a necessary condition for having a real value of
n
in the power law solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
.
Note that we are considering the non-canonical scalar-tensor theory with
\alpha
being a positive integer greater than one.
It is important to emphasize that, for
\alpha>1
, the value of
(1-2\alpha)
always lies within the open interval
\left(B\,,\,B^{\prime}\right)
.
Therefore, for
\alpha>1
, the allowed values of
\beta
is such that
\beta\neq(1-2\alpha)
and consequently the denominator of Eq. (
33
) is never zero for the power law solution.

Note that it is also possible to express Eq. (
34
) as a quadratic equation in
m
, which leads to the following roots for
m
.

m\,=\,\frac{-1+2\alpha-n-4\alpha n\,\pm\,\sqrt{W}}{2(2\alpha-1)},

(46)

where

W\,=\,(1-2\alpha+n+4\alpha n)^{2}-4(2\alpha-1)(2n-4\alpha n+6\alpha n^{2}).

(47)

Recall that
m
is the parameter in the power law solution
\phi(t)\propto t^{m}
.
For
m
to be a real number, it follows from Eq. (
46
) that this is possible only when
W\geq 0
.
Similar to Eq. (
38
), we can express
W
as

W=\mathcal{Q}_{2}n^{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}n+\mathcal{Q}_{0},

(48)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{{}_{2}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,1+32\alpha-32\alpha^{2},

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{{}_{1}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\left(5-14\alpha+8\alpha^{2}\right),

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{{}_{0}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(1-2\alpha)^{2}.

(51)

Since
\alpha
is a positive integer greater than one, it is evident from Eq. (
49
) that
\mathcal{Q}_{{}_{2}}
is a negative number.
This would mean that the
W
described by Eq. (
48
) is a parabola on
n
that opens up downwards.
Consequently, to have
W\geq 0
, it is required that
n
must lie between the two roots of the quadratic equation
\mathcal{Q}_{2}n^{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{1}n^{2}+\mathcal{Q}_{0}=0
.
Therefore, a real value for
m
in Eq. (
46
) which requires
W\geq 0
restrict the value of
n
to the following

N\leq\;n\;\leq N^{\prime},

(52)

where,

\displaystyle N\,=\,\frac{8\alpha^{2}-14\alpha+5}{32\alpha^{2}-32\alpha-1}-2\sqrt{6}\,\sqrt{\frac{8\alpha^{4}-20\alpha^{3}+18\alpha^{2}-7\alpha+1}{\bigl(32\alpha^{2}-32\alpha-1\bigr)^{2}}},

(53)

\displaystyle N^{\prime}\,=\,\frac{8\alpha^{2}-14\alpha+5}{32\alpha^{2}-32\alpha-1}+2\sqrt{6}\,\sqrt{\frac{8\alpha^{4}-20\alpha^{3}+18\alpha^{2}-7\alpha+1}{\bigl(32\alpha^{2}-32\alpha-1\bigr)^{2}}}.

(54)

Figure 1:
Plot of
N^{\prime}
for different integer values of
\alpha
from

to

. Note that

as determined by Eq. (

in the solution

. It is evident from this figure that only when

.

For each integer value of
\alpha

, Eq. (

) for

gives the maximum allowed value of

in the power law solution

.
Figure (

) shows the values of

for different integer values of
\alpha
with
\alpha\,>\,1
.
Note that, as

, it follows from Eq. (

.
Therefore, the power law expansion of the Universe with

is possible in the case of kinetic term driven expansion in the action (
17
) with
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
but the maximum allowed value of

is

.
This is one of the main result of this paper.

Evidently, with only the kinetic term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
in the action (
17
), it is not possible to have an accelerated expansion of the Universe which requires
n>1
in the solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
.
However,
n=2/3
is possible as it is less than the upper bound of
n=0.683
.
In Einstein’s GR, the expansion of the Universe during the cold dark matter dominated epoch is a power law expansion with
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
.
It is possible to mimic this type of expansion in the model with action (
17
) when the kinetic term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
dominated the dynamics of the Universe.

With

m=\frac{-5-2\alpha\pm\sqrt{73-76\alpha+4\alpha^{2}}}{12\alpha-6}.

Since

(56)

This point is also illustrated in the Figure (

is less than

.

Hence, it is proved that the model described by action (

) with

when
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
dominates the dynamics of the Universe.
This power law expansion is identical to the cold dark matted dominated expansion of the Universe based on Einstein’s GR.
Hence, the kinetic term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
behaves as cold dark matter when the value of the parameter
\alpha\,\geq\,18
.

Before we conclude this section, it is important to note that
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
is not possible in the case when the kinetic term dominates in the Brans-Dicke theory which corresponds to setting
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-1
in the action (
17
).
Following a similar analysis as presented in this section, the maximum allowed value of
n
turns out to be
1/3
assuming that the parameter
\lambda>0
in the action (
17
) along with
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-1
which corresponds to the parameters for the Brans-Dicke theory.

Unlike the case of non-canonical scalar tensor theory, the value of

and

in the power law solution depends on the value of the parameter
\lambda
in the Brans-Dicke case.
To see this, consider the action (
17
) without contributions from matter or the potential, and rescale the scalar field such that

for some dimensional constant
c_{{}_{1}}
.
If we choose

such that
\lambda c_{{}_{1}}^{2\alpha+\beta-1}=1
, then the new action for the field
\tilde{\phi}
does not depend on
\lambda
and the two action differ by a multiplicative constant

.

.
The implication of this is that, in the Brans-Dicke case, we cannot eliminate the dependence on
\lambda
.
With

, one get the maximum value of

.
This then rules out the possibility of

in the matter free expansion of the Universe in the Brans-Dicke theory without a potential term.
We therefore, in the next section, consider
\alpha\,\geq\,18
in the action (

) for the model to mimic the behavior of cold dark matter initially.

VI
\Lambda
CDM like evolution of
a(t)

We consider a matter free (
\mathcal{L}_{m}=0
) spatially flat Universe based on action (
17

In the preceding section, we have shown that the kinetic term

Further, as mentioned in Sec. (

Our aim in this section is to determine whether the evolution of scale factor

in this model is identically the same as one gets in the

With a linear potential of the form

along with
\rho=p=0
, the two Friedmann equations (
19
) and (
20
) and the field equation for
\phi
given by Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{a}^{2}}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\phi}\right)\,+\,\left(\frac{2\alpha-1}{6}\right)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1}\,+\,\frac{V_{{}_{0}}}{6},

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\frac{H\dot{\phi}}{2\phi}\,-\,\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{6}\right)\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta-1}\,+\,\frac{V_{{}_{0}}}{6},

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-3H\dot{\phi}\left[\alpha\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3\right]+\left[\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta-2\right]\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}+V_{{}_{0}}\phi}{\alpha(2\alpha-1)\lambda X^{\alpha-1}\phi^{\beta+1}+3}.

(60)

To numerically determine the solution
a(t)
in this model, we first non-dimensionalise the above set of equations.
We consider the following dimensionless variables

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{{}_{i}}t,

(61)

\displaystyle\bar{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{a_{{}_{i}}},

(62)

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\phi}{\phi_{{}_{i}}},

(63)

where

and

.

z\approx 1000
.
In Eq. (
61

H_{{}_{i}}
is the value of the Hubble parameter

at
t=t_{{}_{i}}
.
The variable
\tau
can be interpreted as the dimensionless time variable.

In terms of the dimensionless variables
\bar{a}
and
\bar{\phi}
, the two Friedmann equations (
58

59
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{a}^{\prime}}{\bar{a}\,}\right)^{2}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\bar{H}\left(\frac{\bar{\phi}^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)\,+\,\left(\frac{(2\alpha-1)A}{6}\right)(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{2\alpha}\bar{\phi}^{\beta-1}\,+\,U,

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{a}^{\prime\prime}}{\bar{a}}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}}{\bar{\phi}\,}\right)\,-\,\left(\frac{\bar{H}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}}{2\phi}\right)\,-\,\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{6}\right)A(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{2\alpha}\bar{\phi}^{\beta-1}\,+\,U,

where the prime notation, like

\displaystyle A\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\lambda H_{{}_{i}}^{2(\alpha-1)}\phi_{{}_{i}}^{2\alpha+\beta-1}}{2^{\alpha}},

(66)

\displaystyle U\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{V_{{}_{0}}}{6H_{{}_{i}}^{2}}.

(67)

Note that in these equations
\bar{H}
is defined as

\bar{H}\,=\,\frac{1}{\bar{a}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{a}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\right).

(68)

Similar to the above Friedmann equations in terms of dimensionless variables, the field equations for
\bar{\phi}
take the form

\frac{\bar{\phi}^{\prime\prime}}{\bar{\phi}\,}\,=\,\frac{\mathcal{N}_{T1}\,+\,\mathcal{N}_{T2}}{\mathcal{D}_{T}},

(69)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{T1}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\left(\frac{3\bar{H}\bar{\phi}^{\prime}}{\phi}\right)\left[2\alpha A(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{2(\alpha-1)}\bar{\phi}^{\beta+1}\,+\,3\right],

(70)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{T2}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left(\alpha+\beta-2\alpha\beta-2\right)A(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{2\alpha}\bar{\phi}^{\beta-1}\,+\,6U,

(71)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\alpha(2\alpha-1)A(\bar{\phi}^{\prime})^{2(\alpha-1)}\bar{\phi}^{\beta+1}\,+\,3.

(72)

As shown in the preceding section, the term

VII
Summary and Conclusions

In this paper we considered a model of non-canonical scalar-tensor theory described by the action (
17
).
This can be considered as a non-canonical generalization of the Brans-Dicke theory which corresponds to setting the parameters
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-1
in the model.

For an integer value of
\alpha>1
, we studied the evolution of scale factor
a(t)
in a spatially flat matter free Universe in this model described by the action (
57
) with a linear potential term
V(\phi)=V_{{}_{0}}\phi
.

It is shown that when
V_{{}_{0}}=0
or when the potential term is subdominant, the kinetic term
\lambda X^{\alpha}\phi^{\beta}
leads to a power law solution for the scale factor with
a(t)\propto t^{n}
.
However, we find the following important results:

(i)

The maximum value of
n
in the power law solution
a(t)\propto t^{n}
turns out to be
n=(1+\sqrt{3})/4\approx 0.683

(ii)

A solution of the form
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
is possible only if the parameter
\alpha\geq 18
.

(iii)

This solution
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
is independent of the choice of the potential
V(\phi)
provided that potential term is subdominant and the kinetic term drives the evolution of
a(t)
.

The solution
a(t)\propto t^{2/3}
is what one gets in a cold dark matter dominated epoch when the gravity is described by Einstein’s GR.
With a choice of linear potential of the form
V(\phi)=V_{{}_{0}}\phi
, it is shown that the evolution of
a(t)
in the model described by the action (
57
)
is the same as one gets in the
\Lambda
CDM model based on Einstein’s GR.
This is the main result of this paper.

Although, the recent cosmological observational data indicate a tentative deviation from the
\Lambda
CDM like evolution of the scale factor
Abbott et al. 2024
;
Gu et al. 2025
;
Lodha et al. 2025
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
, the purpose of this paper is to illustrate that the model with action (
57
) can provide a unified description of dark matter and dark energy as if it is a GR based
\Lambda
CDM model.
With an appropriate choice of potential
V\phi)
other than the linear one, it is possible that the model may provide a unified description of the dark matter and a dynamical dark energy. This needs to be investigated.
Further, the evolution of the cosmological perturbations, in particular, how the perturbations in the dark matter component evolve, needs to be studied, as this may impose restrictions on the value of the parameters.

The Perfect Matching Hamiltonian property in Prism and Crossed Prism graphs
Francesco Colangelo, Federico Romaniello

Abstract
A graph G G has the Perfect Matching Hamiltonian property (or for short, G G is P ​ M ​ H PMH ) if, for each one of its perfect matchings, there is another perfect matching of G G such that the union of the two perfect matchings yields a Hamiltonian cycle of G G . In this note, we show that Prism graphs 𝒫 n \mathcal{P}_{n} are not P ​ M ​ H PMH , except for the C ​ u ​ b ​ e ​ g ​ r ​ a ​ p ​ h Cube\ graph , and indicate for which values of n n the Crossed Prism graphs 𝒞 ​ 𝒫 n \mathcal{CP}_{n} are P ​ M ​ H PMH . Keywords: Cubic graph, perfect matching, Hamiltonian cycle. Math. Subj. Class.: 05C15, 05C45, 05C70.

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple connected graph of even order, with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. A
k
-factor
of
G
is defined as a
k
-regular spanning subgraph of
G
(not necessarily connected). Two prominent topics in graph theory are
perfect matchings
and
Hamiltonian cycles
. A perfect matching corresponds to the edge set of a 1-factor, while a Hamiltonian cycle represents a connected 2-factor of a graph. For
t\geq 3
, a
cycle
of length
t
(or a
t
-cycle), denoted as
C_{t}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})
, consists of a sequence of mutually distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{t}
with an associated edge set
\{v_{1}v_{2},\ldots,v_{t-1}v_{t},v_{t}v_{1}\}
. We refer the reader to
[
4
]
for any definitions not explicitly defined here.

A graph
G
admitting a perfect matching is said to have the
Perfect-Matching-Hamiltonian property
(for short the
PMH-property
) if for every perfect matching
M
of
G
there exists another perfect matching
N
of
G
, with
M\cap N=\emptyset
, such that the edges of
M\cup N
induce a Hamiltonian cycle of
G
. For simplicity, a graph admitting this property is said to be
PMH
. This property was first studied in the 1970s by Las Vergnas
[
10
]
and Häggkvist
[
8
]
, and for more recent results about the
PMH
-property we suggest the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
11
]
. If a cubic graph
G
is
PMH
, then every perfect matching of
G
corresponds to one of the colours of a (proper)
3
-edge-colouring of the graph, and we say that every perfect matching can be extended to a
3
-edge-colouring. This is achieved by alternately colouring the edges of the Hamiltonian cycle containing a predetermined perfect matching using two colours, and then colouring the edges not belonging to the Hamiltonian cycle using a third colour.
However, there are cubic graphs which are not
PMH
but have every one of their perfect matchings that can be extended to a
3
-edge-colouring. The following proposition, stated in
[
2
]
characterises all cubic graphs for which every one of their perfect matchings can be extended to a 3-edge-colouring of the graph.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a cubic graph admitting a perfect matching. Every perfect matching of
G
can be extended to a
3
-edge-colouring of
G
if and only if all
2
-factors of
G
contain only even cycles.

The following corollary follows immediately:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a cubic graph admitting a perfect matching. If there exists a
2
–factor of
G
that contains an odd cycle, then
G
is not PMH.

We will now give informal definitions of both Prism graphs and Crossed Prism graphs. Formal definitions with appropriate labelling on vertices and edges for both families will be given in Section
2
and Section
3
, respectively.

A
prism graph

\mathcal{P}_{n}
is obtained by connecting two identical cycles with edges between corresponding vertices. The resulting graph resembles a three-dimensional prism, hence the name. A
crossed prism graph

\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is a variation of the prism graph. Still, it consists of two cycles of equal length with edges between corresponding edges, and in the
outer cycle
edges connect consecutive vertices. In contrast, the
inner cycle
is twisted, meaning that each vertex of the inner cycle connects not directly to the corresponding vertex on the outer cycle, but instead to another vertex in a pattern that creates a crossing structure within the inner cycle. Figure
1
shows on the left the Prism graph and on the right the Crossed Prism graph, both on 16 vertices.

Figure 1:
The
8
-prism graph
\mathcal{P}_{8}
and the
4
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{2}
, both on 16 vertices.

In Section 1, we show that the only
PMH

n-prism graph

\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the Cube graph, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{4}
. In Section 2, we prove that the
2
n-crossed prism graph

\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is
PMH
only when
n
is even.

2
The PMH-property in Prism graphs

As stated in the introduction, this section shall be devoted to studying the
PMH
-property in Prism graphs. We start by formally defining the
n
-prism graph
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. The
n
-prism graph
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the graph on
2n
vertices such that
V(\mathcal{P}_{n})=\{u_{i},v_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,n\}
, where

(1)

(u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{n})
and
(v_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{n})
are two disjoint cycles of length
n
,

(2)

u_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{i}
, for each
i=1,\dots,n
.

The
n
-cycle induced by the sets of vertices
\{u_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,n\}
is referred to as the
outer-cycle
, whilst the
n
-cycle induced by the vertices
\{v_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,n\}
is referred to as the
inner-cycle
. The edges on these two
n
-cycles are said to be the
outer-edges
and
inner-edges
accordingly, whilst the edges
u_{i}v_{i}
are referred to as
spokes
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then, the only PMH
n
-prism graph
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the Cube graph, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{4}
.

Proof.

We start by noticing that when
n
is odd, the inner and the outer cycle of the graph have odd length
n
, and so there is a 2-factor of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
that contains an odd cycle, hence for Corollary
1.2
it cannot be PMH.

If
n=4
, the Graph
\mathcal{P}_{4}
is the
Cube Graph

Q_{3}
, and it was shown in
[
5
]
that it is
PMH
.

Thus, we may suppose
n
even,
n\geq 6
and consider the following perfect matching, say
M
, of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
built as follows: we take the edges
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}
and then we alternate, as far as possible, a pair of parallel edges and a spoke. We have the following 3 different possibilities, depending on the parity of
n
modulo 6:

1)

if
n\equiv 0\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: there will be 4 consecutive spokes, that are
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2},

u_{n}v_{n}
and
u_{n-1}v_{n-1}
,

2)

if
n\equiv 2\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: there will be 3 consecutive spokes, that are
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}
and
u_{n}v_{n}
,

3)

if
n\equiv 4\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: there will be 2 consecutive spokes, that are
u_{1}v_{1}
and
u_{2}v_{2}
.

We will show below that the perfect matching
M
of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, when
n
even and
n\geq 6
, defined as above cannot be extended to a Hamiltonian cycle.

In fact, suppose on the contrary that
M
can be extended to a Hamiltonian Cycle
H
. The edges
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}\in M
, so they also belong to
H
. Let
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(H)
, then
u_{3}v_{3}\in E(H)
and also
v_{1}v_{2}\in E(H)
, but
(u_{1}v_{1}v_{2}u_{2}u_{1})
is a
4
-cycle in
H
.

Now suppose
u_{1}u_{n}\in E(H)
. We have 3 possible cases:

(1)

n\equiv 0\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: then
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2},u_{n}v_{n},u_{n-1}v_{n-1}\in M
.
If
v_{1}v_{2}\in E(H)
then
v_{3}u_{3}\in E(H)
. But this forces
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(H)
, a contradiction. So
v_{1}v_{n}\in E(H)
, but this forces the
4
-cycle
(v_{n}v_{1}u_{1}u_{n}v_{n})
to be in
H
, a contradiction.

(2)

n\equiv 2\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: then
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2},u_{n}v_{n}\in M
. Since
u_{1}u_{n}\in E(H)
, then
u_{n-1}v_{n-1}\in E(H)
. But this forces the
4
-cycle
(u_{1}v_{1}v_{n}u_{n}u_{1})
to be in
H
, a contradiction.

(3)

n\equiv 4\ ({\rm mod}\ 6)
: then
u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}\in M
. If
v_{1}v_{2}\in E(H)
then
v_{3}u_{3}\in E(H)
. But this forces
u_{1}u_{2}\in E(H)
, a contradiction. So
v_{1}v_{n}\in E(H)
. If
u_{n-1}v_{n-1}\in E(H)
, this forces the
6-
cycle
(u_{1}v_{1}v_{n}v_{n-1}u_{n-1}u_{n}u_{1})
to be in
H
, a contradiction. Thus
v_{n-1}v_{n-2}
and
u_{n-1}u_{n-2}\in E(H)
, but this forces the
8
-cycle
(u_{1}v_{1}v_{n}v_{n-1}v_{n-2}u_{n-2}u_{n-1}u_{n}u_{1})
to be in
H
, a contradiction.

Therefore,
M
belongs to no Hamiltonian Cycles in
\mathcal{P}_{n}
when
n\geq 6
.
∎

The PMH-property in Crossed prism graphs

As mentioned in the introduction, this section will focus on studying the
PMH
-property in Crossed Prism graphs. As above, we first formally define the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. The
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is the graph on
8n
vertices such that
V(\mathcal{CP}_{n})=\{u_{i},v_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,4n\}
, where

(1)

(u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{4n})
is a cycle of length
4n
,

(2)

u_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{i}
, for each
i=1,\dots,4n
,

(3)

the adjacencies between the vertices
v_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,4n
, form a cycle of length
4n
given by the edge set

\{v_{2i-1}v_{2i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,2n\}\cup\{v_{2i-1}v_{2i+2}\ |\ i=1,\dots,2n-1\}\cup\{v_{4n-1}v_{2}\}.

The
4n
-cycle induced by the sets of vertices
\{u_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,4n\}
is referred to as the
outer-cycle
, whilst the
4n
-cycle induced by the vertices
\{v_{i}\ |\ i=1,\dots,4n\}
is referred to as the
inner-cycle
. The edges on these two
4n
-cycles are said to be the
outer-edges
and
inner-edges
accordingly, whilst the edges
u_{i}v_{i}
are referred to as
spokes
. The edges
u_{1}u_{4n}
,
v_{2}v_{4n-1}
,
v_{2n-1}v_{2n+2}
,
u_{2n}u_{2n+1}
are denoted by
a,b,c,d
, respectively, and we shall also denote the set
\{a,b,c,d\}
by
\mathcal{X}
. The set
\mathcal{X}
is referred to as the
principal
4
-edge-cut
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
(see Figure
3
).

-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}

\mathcal{T}_{j}
, for

.

In what follows we will use the same notation used in
[
2
, Section 2]
, as it is easy to see that Crossed Prism graphs almost have the same structure of Balanced Papillon graphs, except for the principal
4
-edge-cut.

A
multipole

\mathcal{Z}
is defined by a set of vertices
V(\mathcal{Z})
and a collection of generalised edges, where each generalised edge is either a standard edge (with two endvertices) or a semiedge. A
semiedge
is a generalised edge with exactly one endvertex. The set of semiedges of
\mathcal{Z}
is denoted by
\delta\mathcal{Z}
, while the set of standard edges (with two endvertices) is represented by
E(\mathcal{Z})
. Two semiedges are said to be
joined
if they are both removed and their respective endvertices are made adjacent. A
k
-pole
refers to a multipole with
k
semiedges. A perfect matching
M
of a
k
-pole
\mathcal{Z}
is a subset of the generalised edges of
\mathcal{Z}
such that every vertex of
\mathcal{Z}
is incident with exactly one generalised edge in
M
.

In what follows, we shall construct Crossed Prism graphs by joining the semiedges of various multipoles. Specifically, given a perfect matching
M
of a graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
and a multipole
\mathcal{Z}
used as a building block for constructing
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
, we say that
M
contains a semiedge
e
of the multipole
\mathcal{Z}
if
M
includes the edge in
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
that results from joining
e
to another semiedge during the construction of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
.

The
4
-pole
\mathcal{Z}
with vertex set
\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}\}
, such that
E(\mathcal{Z})
induces the
4
-cycle
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})
and with exactly one semiedge incident to each of its vertices is referred to as a
C_{4}
-pole
(see Figure
4
). For each
i=1,\dots,4
, let the semiedge incident to
z_{i}
be denoted by
f_{i}
. The semiedges
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are referred to as the
upper left semiedge
and the
upper right semiedge
of
\mathcal{Z}
, respectively. On the other hand, the semiedges
f_{3}
and
f_{4}
are referred to as the
lower left semiedge
and the
lower right semiedge
of
\mathcal{Z}
, respectively (see Figure
4
).

\mathcal{Z}

, let
\mathcal{Z}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{Z}_{m}

e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
are the four semiedges of a chain
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
of

-poles (possibly of length

), we mean that
e_{1}
and
e_{2}

\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
, whilst

e_{1}

(similarly,
e_{3}

) of

Let
M
be a perfect matching of the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. Since
\mathcal{X}=\{a,b,c,d\}
is a
4
-edge-cut of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
,
|M\cap\mathcal{X}|\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, that is,
|M\cap\mathcal{X}|
is
0,2
or
4
. The following is a useful lemma which shall be used frequently in the results that follow.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let M be a perfect matching of the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
and let
\mathcal{X}
be its principal
4
-edge-cut. If
|M\cap\mathcal{X}|=k
, then
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{j}|=k
for each
j=1,\dots,2n
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a perfect matching of the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. We first note that the left semiedges of a
C_{4}-
pole are contained in the same perfect matching. The lemma is proved by considering three cases depending on the possible values of
k
, that is 0,2 or 4.

Case 1:

k=0
.

Since
a
and
b
do not belong to
M
, the left semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
aren’t contained in
M
, and so
M
cannot contain its right semiedges. Therefore
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{1}|=0
. Consequently, the left semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
aren’t contained in M implying again that
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{2}|=0
. By repeating the same argument up till the
n^{th}

C_{4}-
pole, we have
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{j}|=k
, for every
j=1,\dots,n
. By noting that
c
and
d
don’t belong to
M
and repeating a similar argument to the 4
-
poles in the left
n-
chain, we can deduce that
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{j}|=k
, for every
j=1,\dots,2n
.

Case 2:

k=4
.

Since
a
and
b
belong to
M
, the left semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
are contained in
M
, and so
M
contains its right semiedges as well. Therefore,
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{1}|=4
. Consequently, the left semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are contained in
M
implying again that
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{2}|=4
. As in Case 1, by noting that
c
and
d
belong to
M
and repeating a similar argument to the 4
-
poles in the left
n-
chain, we can deduce that
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{j}|=4
, for every
j=1,\dots,2n
.

Case 3:

k=2
.

In this case, we first claim that
M\cap\mathcal{X}
must be equal to
\{a,d\}
or
\{a,c\}
or
\{b,c\}
or
\{b,d\}
. In fact, suppose that
M\cap\mathcal{X}=\{a,b\}
, without loss of generality. This means that the right semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
are also contained in
M
, implying that
|M\cap\mathcal{T}_{1}|=4
. This implies that the left semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are contained in
M
, which forces
|M\cap\mathcal{T}_{j}|
to be equal to 4, for every
j=1,\dots,2n
. In particular,
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{n}|=4
, implying that the edges
c
and
d
belong to
M
, a contradiction since
M\cap\mathcal{X}=\{a,b\}
. This proves our claim.
The natural automorphism
\psi
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
, which exchanges the outer- and inner-cycles, exchanges also
\{a,d\}
with
\{b,c\}
, combined with the mirror automorphism through the horizontal axis allow us to assume, without loss of generality, that
M\cap\mathcal{X}=\{a,d\}
. Since
b\notin M
,
1\leq|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{1}|\leq 3
. But
\delta\mathcal{T}_{1}
corresponds to a 4-edge cut in
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
, and so, by using a parity argument,
|M\cap\delta\mathcal{T}_{1}|
must be equal to 2, implying that exactly one of the right semiedges of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is contained in
M
. This means that exactly one left semiedge of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is contained in
M
, and consequently, by a similar argument now applied to
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we obtain
|M\cap\mathcal{T}_{2}|=2
. By repeating the same argument and noting that
\mathcal{T}_{n+1}
has exactly one left semiedge (corresponding to the edge
d
) contained in
M
, one can deduce that
|M\cap\mathcal{T}_{j}|=2
for every
j=1,\dots,2n
.

∎

.

.

Let

\mathcal{CP}_{n}
and let
\mathcal{X}
be its principal

-edge-cut such that

. For each

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer. Then, the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is not PMH.

Proof.

Consider the following perfect matching of the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
:

M=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\{u_{2i-1}u_{2i},v_{2i-1}v_{2i}\}.

We claim that
M
cannot be extended to a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. For, let
F
be a 2-factor of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
containing
M
. Since
u_{1}u_{2}\in M
and
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is cubic,
F
contains exactly one of the following two edges:
u_{1}u_{4n}
or
u_{1}v_{1}
. In the former case, the choice of
u_{1}u_{4n}
forbids all the spokes of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
to belong to
F
; hence, all the edges of the outer- and inner-cycle will belong to
F
. So we have two disjoint cycles each of length
4n
. In the latter case,
F
must also contain all spokes
u_{i}v_{i}
, for
i=2,\dots,4n
. In fact, the subgraph induced by the set of spokes is exactly the complement of the 2-factor obtained in the former case. So,
F
will consist of
2n
disjoint 4-cycles.
∎

Let

be a perfect matching of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
with

.

(2)

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
n
be a positive even integer. Then, the
2n
-crossed prism graph
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
is PMH.

Proof.

Let
M_{1}
be a perfect matching of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. We need to show that there exists a perfect matching
M_{2}
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
such that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
induces a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. Three cases, depending on the intersection of
M_{1}
with the principal 4-edge-cut
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
, are considered.

Case 1:

|M_{1}\cap\mathcal{X}|=2
.

By Lemma
3.4
, there exists a perfect matching
N
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
such that
|N\cap\mathcal{X}|=2
and
M_{1}\cap N=\emptyset
. Moreover, the complementary 2-factor of
N
is a Hamiltonian cycle. Since
M_{1}
is contained in the mentioned 2-factor, the result follows.

Case 2:

|M_{1}\cap\mathcal{X}|=4
.

In this case, we can define
M_{2}
to be the following perfect matching:

M_{2}=\{u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2}\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=2}^{2n}\{u_{2j-1}u_{2j},v_{2j-1}v_{2j}\}

In fact,
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
induces the following Hamiltonian cycle:

(u_{1},v_{1},v_{4},v_{3},\ldots,v_{2n},v_{2n-1},v_{2n+2},\ldots,v_{4n-3},v_{4n},v_{4n-1},v_{2},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4},\ldots,u_{4n}),

where
v_{3}
and
v_{2n+2}
are respectively followed by
v_{6}
and
v_{2n+1}
.

Case 3:

|M_{1}\cap\mathcal{X}|=0
.

Clearly,
|M_{2}\cap\mathcal{X}|
cannot be zero, because, if so, choosing
M_{2}
to be disjoint from
M_{1}
,
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
induces
2n
disjoint
4
-cycles. Therefore,
|M_{2}\cap\mathcal{X}|
must be equal to 2 or 4.
Let
\mathcal{R}=\{\mathcal{T}_{(1,2)},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{(n-1,n)}\}
and
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{T}_{(n+1,n+2)},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{(2n-1,2n)}\}
be the sets of 2-chains within the left and right
n
-chains of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
namely the right and left
n
-chains each split into
\frac{n}{2}

2
-chains. We consider two cases depending on the parity of the number of
2
-chains in
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
which are asymmetric with respect to
M_{1}
. Let the function
\Phi:\mathcal{\mathcal{R}}\cup\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\{-1,+1\}
be defined on the
2
-chains
\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{L}
such that:

\Phi(\mathcal{T})=\begin{cases}+1&$if $\mathcal{T}$ is symmetric with respect to $M_{1},\\
-1&$otherwise.$\end{cases}

Subcase 3.1:

\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
each have an even number (possibly zero) of asymmetric
2
-chains with respect to
M_{1}
.

We claim that there exists a perfect matching such that its union with
M_{1}
gives a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. Since the number of asymmetric
2
-chains in
\mathcal{R}
is even,
\prod\limits_{{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{R}}\Phi({\mathcal{T}})=+1
, and consequently, by appropriately concatenating paths as in Remark
3.6
, there exists a path
R
with endvertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2n}
whose vertex set is
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
such that it contains all the edges in
M_{1}\cap(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}E({\mathcal{T}}_{i}))
. We remark that this path intersects exactly one edge of
\{xy\in E(G):x\in V({\mathcal{T}}_{j}),y\in V({\mathcal{T}}_{j+1})\}
, for each
j=1,\dots,n-1
. By a similar reasoning, since
\prod\limits_{{T}\in\mathcal{L}}\Phi({\mathcal{T}})=+1
, there exists a path
L
with endvertices
u_{2n+1}
and
u_{4n}
whose vertex set is
\bigcup\limits_{i=2n+1}^{4n}\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
, such that it contains all the edges in
M_{1}\cap(\bigcup\limits_{i=n+1}^{2n}E({\mathcal{T}}_{i}))
. Once again, this path intersects exactly one edge of
\{xy\in E(G):x\in V({\mathcal{T}}_{j}),y\in V({\mathcal{T}}_{j+1})\}
, for each
j=n+1,\ldots,2n-1
. These two paths, together with the edges
a
and
d
form the required Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
containing
M_{1}
, proving our claim. We remark that this shows that there exists a perfect matching
M_{2}
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
such that
M_{2}\cap\mathcal{X}=\{a,d\}
,
M_{1}\cap M_{2}=\emptyset
and with
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
inducing a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. One can similarly show that there exists a perfect matching
M_{2}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
such that
M_{2}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{X}=\{b,c\}
,
M_{1}\cap M_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
and with
M_{1}\cup M_{2}^{\prime}
inducing a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
.

Subcase 3.2:
One of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
has an odd number of asymmetric
2
-chains with respect to
M_{1}
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\mathcal{R}
has an odd number of asymmetric
2
-chains with respect to
M_{1}
, that is,
\prod\limits_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{R}}\Phi(\mathcal{T})=-1
. Let
M_{2}
be the perfect matching of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
such that
|M_{2}\cap\mathcal{X}|=4
. We claim that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
induces a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
. Since
\prod\limits_{{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{R}}\Phi({\mathcal{T}})=-1
, by appropriately concatenating paths as in Remark
3.7
we can deduce that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
contains the edge set of two disjoint paths
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, such that:

1.

|V(R_{1})|=|V(R_{2})|=2n
;

2.

V(R_{1})\cup V(R_{2})=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
;

3.

the endvertices of
R_{1}
are
u_{1}
and
v_{2n-1}
; and

4.

the endvertices of
R_{2}
are
v_{2}
and
u_{2n}
.

We shall be using the fact that

\{u_{1}u_{4n},v_{2}v_{4n-1},v_{2n-1}v_{2n+2},u_{2n}u_{2n+1}\}=\{a,b,c,d\}=\mathcal{X}\subset M_{2}
.

By Remark
3.7
we can deduce that
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
contains the edge set of two disjoint paths
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, such that:

1.

|V(L_{1})|=|V(L_{2})|=2n
;

2.

V(L_{1})\cup V(L_{2})=\bigcup\limits_{i=2n+1}^{4n}\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
;

3.

the endvertices of
L_{1}
are
v_{2n+2}
and
v_{4n-1}
; and

4.

the endvertices of
L_{2}
are
u_{2n+1}
and
u_{4n}
.

The concatenation of the following paths and edges gives a Hamiltonian cycle of
\mathcal{CP}_{n}
containing
M_{1}
:

R_1 v_2n-1v_2n+2 L_1 v_4n-1v_2 R_2 u_2nu_2n+1 L_2 u_4nu_1.

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Conclusions

In this brief note, we have studied the
PMH
-property in two well-known families of cubic graphs, the Prisms and the Crossed Prisms. As far as the authors know, no cubic
PMH
graphs with girth at least 8 have been found, and we believe that the technique of appropriately modifying the edge set of a graph to make it
PMH
could be useful in this direction.

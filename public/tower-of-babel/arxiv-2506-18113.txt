Large grid subsets without many cospherical points
Zichao Dong, Zijian Xu

Abstract
Motivated by intuitions from projective algebraic geometry, we provide a novel construction of subsets of the d d -dimensional grid [ n ] d [n]^{d} of size n − o ⁡ ( n ) n-o(n) with no d + 2 d+2 points on a sphere or a hyperplane. For d = 2 d=2 , this improves the previously best known lower bound of n / 4 n/4 toward the Erdős–Purdy problem due to Thiele in 1995. For d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , this improves the recent Ω ⁡ ( n 3 d + 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) \Omega\bigl(n^{\frac{3}{d+1}-o(1)}\bigr) bound due to Suk and White, confirming their conjectured Ω ⁡ ( n d d + 1 ) \Omega\bigl(n^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\bigr) bound in a strong sense, and asymptotically resolves the generalized Erdős–Purdy problem posed by Brass, Moser, and Pach.

One might try to come up with other curves to achieve better lower bounds. Bezout’s theorem from algebraic geometry shows us that a curve of degree
k
intersects a typical sphere in
2k
points (including projective infinite points). To avoid
d+2
cospherical points, the curve is supposed to be of degree at most
\frac{d+1}{2}
. However, when
d\geq 3
, this is too small for a curve to escape a single plane.

To address this caveat, our strategy is to utilize infinite points. Projectively, every sphere passes through a same set of infinite points. If our selected curve goes through many of these infinite points as well, then the number of spherical intersections decreases drastically. This motivates us to analyze rational curves
\Bigl\{\bigl(\frac{f_{1}(t)}{h(t)},\,\dots,\,\frac{f_{d}(t)}{h(t)}\bigr)\Bigr\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
, where
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},h
are well-behaved polynomials.

We shall find desired polynomials in

, and prove
Theorems

2
and
3
in

.

2
Constructing the polynomials

Throughout this section, we take
p
as a fixed prime number with
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
p>(d+1)!
. Importantly, the square root of
-1
exists in the
p
-element field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Take
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\alpha^{2}+1=0
.

In the univariate polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
, we call a polynomial
f

nice
if the linear coefficient of
f
vanishes. That is, a nice
f
takes the form
a_{0}+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{3}t^{3}+\cdots
, where
a_{0},a_{2},a_{3},\dots\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
d\geq 2
is an integer. Let
p
be a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
p>(d+1)!
. Then there exist
d+2
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
with the following properties.

•

(
identity
)
They satisfy the polynomial identity
f_{1}^{2}+\dots+f_{d}^{2}=gh
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
.

•

(
degree
)
They have
(\deg f_{1},\dots,\deg f_{d},\deg g,\deg h)=(d,\dots,d,d-1,d+1)
.

•

(
independence
)
The
d+2
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g,h
are linearly independent.

•

(
vanish
)
The
d+1
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},h
are nice. (Note that
g
is excluded.)

Remark.
We shall make use of
vanish
only in the proof of
Theorem

3
.

Proof.

We first construct
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g,h
satisfying
identity
,
degree
, and
independence
.

Suppose
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
are distinct and set
g_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(t-\lambda_{1})\cdots\widehat{(t-\lambda_{j})}\cdots(t-\lambda_{d})
for
j=1,\dots,d
. Observe that
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
are linearly independent polynomials of degree
d-1
.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be an
d\times d
matrix. For
i=1,\dots,d
, we define degree-
d
polynomials

f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{a_{i,j}}{g_{j}(\lambda_{j})}\cdot g_{j}+\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t-\lambda_{j}).

Observe that
f_{i}(\lambda_{j})=a_{i,j}
holds for every
i\in[d]
and every
j\in[d]
.

Claim 5
.

There exists
j\in[d]
such that
g_{j}
does not appear in the linear span of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose to the contrary that each of
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
can be written as a linear combination of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
. We known that
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
form a basis of polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
of degree at most
d-1
. So do
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
. However, each of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
is of degree
d
, a contradiction.
∎

Claim 6
.

If the matrix
A
is invertible, then the polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
are linearly independent.

Proof of claim.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there exist coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{d}
that are not all zero with
c_{1}f_{1}+\dots+c_{d}f_{d}=0
. Evaluating at
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
, it follows that
c_{1}a_{1,j}+\dots+c_{d}a_{d,j}
holds for every
j\in[d]
. This implies that the row vectors of
A
are linearly dependent with respect to coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{d}
, contradicting the assumption that
A
is invertible.
∎

Set
\lambda_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}i-1
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for
i=1,\dots,d
. Take
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\alpha^{2}+1=0
and specify the matrix
A
as

a_{i,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}1\qquad&\text{if $i=j$},\\[-5.0pt]
\alpha\qquad&\text{if $i=j+1$},\\[-5.0pt]
\pm\alpha\qquad&\text{if $(i,j)=(1,d)$},\\[-5.0pt]
0\qquad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Here the “
a_{1,d}=\pm\alpha
” entry is chosen so that
A
is invertible. In fact, it is not hard to compute that
A
has determinant
1\pm\alpha^{d}
. Since
1+\alpha^{d}=1-\alpha^{d}=0
cannot happen in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we may always find a legal choice of
a_{1,d}
. As an example, we write out the matrix
A
below in the
d=6
case:

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!\alpha\\[-5.0pt]
\alpha&\!\!1&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0\\[-5.0pt]
0&\!\!\alpha&\!\!1&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0\\[-5.0pt]
0&\!\!0&\!\!\alpha&\!\!1&\!\!0&\!\!0\\[-5.0pt]
0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!\alpha&\!\!1&\!\!0\\[-5.0pt]
0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!0&\!\!\alpha&\!\!1\end{pmatrix}.

It is important that
\lambda_{1}=0
and the sum of squares of entries in each column of
A
equals
0
.

Claim 7
.

The polynomial
(t-\lambda_{1})\cdots(t-\lambda_{d})
divides
f_{1}^{2}+\cdots+f_{d}^{2}
.

Proof of claim.

This is true because
f_{1}^{2}(\lambda_{k})+\dots+f_{d}^{2}(\lambda_{k})=0
holds for all
k\in[d]
.
∎

Pick
g\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}g_{j}
as in
5
. Then
7
implies that
g
divides
f_{1}^{2}+\dots+f_{d}^{2}
, and we set
h
as the quotient, implying
identity
. Since the leading coefficient of
f_{1}^{2}+\dots+f_{d}^{2}
is
d\neq 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have

\deg(f_{1}^{2}+\dots+f_{d}^{2})=2d,\qquad\deg(g)=d-1,

and so
\deg(h)=d+1
, implying
degree
. Since
h
is is of higher degree than
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g
, evidently
h
is linearly independent on
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g
. So,
independence
follows from
6
and
5
.

We then fix
g
and tweak
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
to fulfill
vanish
. We sketch the strategy below:

•

Replace
f_{i}
by
f_{i}+\mu_{i}g
, where
\mu_{i}
is appropriately chosen so that every
f_{i}+\mu_{i}g
is nice.

•

Replace
f_{1}
by
f_{1}+\nu xg
, where
\nu
is appropriately chosen so that the resulted
h
is nice.

To achieve this, the crucial properties of
g
we require are that
g
is not nice and that
g(0)=0
. From the assumption
p>(d+1)!
we deduce that each of
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
is not nice, and so
g
is not nice. To further enforce
g(0)=0
, we need the following stronger version of
5
under our specific
A
.

Claim 8
.

There exists
j\in[d]\setminus\{1\}
such that
g_{j}
does not appear in the linear span of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose to the contrary that each of
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
can be written as a linear combination of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
. Our choice of
A
implies
f_{1}=\frac{1}{g_{1}(\lambda_{1})}\cdot g_{1}+\frac{\alpha}{g_{2}(\lambda_{2})}\cdot g_{2}
, and so
g_{1}
can also be written as a linear combination of
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
. However, this contradicts
5
.
∎

Our choice of
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
implies that
g_{2}(0)=\dots=g_{d}(0)=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Thanks to
5
, we can choose
g
to be one of
g_{2},\dots,g_{d}
so that
g(0)=0
without spoiling
identity
,
degree
, and
independence
.

Since
g
is not nice, the linear coefficient of
g
is nonzero in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It follows that for each
i=1,\dots,d
, there exists a unique
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that the linear coefficient of
f_{i}+\mu_{i}g
vanishes. Set

\widetilde{f}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}f_{1}+(\mu_{1}+\nu t)g,\quad\widetilde{f}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}f_{2}+\mu_{2}g,\quad\dots,\quad\widetilde{f}_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}f_{d}+\mu_{d}g,\quad\widetilde{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{\widetilde{f}_{1}^{2}+\dots+\widetilde{f}_{d}^{2}}{g}.

Then
\widetilde{f}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{f}_{d}
are nice while
identity
,
degree
, and
independence
are left intact. It remains to verify that there is an appropriate choice of
\nu
to make
\widetilde{h}
nice. We compute

\widetilde{h}=h+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i}(2f_{i}+\mu_{i}g)+2(f_{1}+\mu_{1}g)\nu t+\nu^{2}t^{2}g.

(1)

Denote by
[t]f
the linear coefficient of polynomial
f
. Since
g(0)=0
and

g_{2}(0)=0\implies f_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{g_{1}(\lambda_{1})}\cdot g_{1}(0)+\frac{\alpha}{g_{2}(\lambda_{2})}\cdot g_{2}(0)=\frac{1}{g_{1}(0)}\cdot g_{1}(0)+\frac{\alpha}{g_{2}(1)}\cdot g_{2}(0)=1,

from (
1
) we deduce that

[t]\widetilde{h}=[t]\biggl(h+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i}(2f_{i}+\mu_{i}g)\biggr)+2\bigl(f_{1}(0)+\mu_{1}g(0)\bigr)\nu=2\nu+[t]\biggl(h+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mu_{i}(2f_{i}+\mu_{i}g)\biggr).

It follows that
[t]\widetilde{h}
is mapped surjectively onto
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as
\nu
varies. In particular, there is a unique choice of
\nu
such that
[t]\widetilde{h}=0
, implying
vanish
. We thus conclude the proof of
Theorem

4
.
∎

3
Proofs of
Theorems

2
and
3

We need the prime number distribution theorem for arithmetic progressions (see e.g.
[
1
,
12
]
).

Theorem 9
.

Let
\pi(N;k,\ell)
be the number of primes
p
with
p\leq N
and
p\equiv k\pmod{\ell}
. Then

\biggl|\pi(N;k,\ell)-\frac{1}{\phi(\ell)}\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}\biggr|=O\biggl(\frac{N}{\exp\bigl(c\sqrt{\log N}\bigr)}\biggr)\qquad\text{as $N\to\infty$},

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function and
c
is an absolute positive constant.

Corollary 10
.

As
n\to\infty
, the smallest prime
p\geq n
with
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
satisfies

p=n+O\biggl(\frac{n\log n}{\exp\bigl(c\sqrt{\log n}\bigr)}\biggr)=n+o(n).

Proof of
Theorem

2
.

The strategy is to construct for every prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
a subset
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
of size
p-d-2
containing neither
d+2
points being coplanar nor
d+2
points being cospherical. To be specific, we shall identify
[n]^{d}
as
\{\overline{1},\overline{2},\dots,\overline{n}\}^{d}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
. Starting with a claimed
S
and considering random translations, a direct first moment calculation shows that there exists
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
with

\bigl|(S+\mathbf{v})\cap[n]^{d}\bigr|\geq\Bigl(\frac{n}{p}\Bigr)^{d}\cdot|S|.

Due to
Corollary

10
, if we take the smallest prime
p
with
p>\max\bigl\{10(d+1)!,n\bigr\}
and
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then one can compute that
\bigl|(S+\mathbf{v})\cap[n]^{d}\bigr|=n-o(n)
. Since every plane (resp. sphere) in
[n]^{d}
is a plane (resp. sphere) in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
, the set
(S+\mathbf{v})\cap[n]^{d}
concludes the proof of
Theorem

2
.

Pick polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g,h
as in
Theorem

4
. Consider the parametric curve

\gamma\colon\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:h(t)\neq 0\bigr\}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d},\qquad t\mapsto\biggl(\frac{f_{1}(t)}{h(t)},\,\frac{f_{2}(t)}{h(t)},\,\dots,\,\frac{f_{d}(t)}{h(t)}\biggr).

We are going to show that the image of
\gamma
provides a valid construction of
S
.

Let
\pi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}x_{d}+\lambda=0\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
be an arbitrary plane. If
\gamma(t_{0})\in\pi
, then
t_{0}
is a zero of the polynomial
P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{1}f_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}f_{d}+\lambda h
. It follows from
degree
and
independence
in
Theorem

4
that
\deg P\leq d+1
and
P\neq 0
. Therefore, the number of
t_{0}
is upper bounded by
d+1
.

Let
\omega\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}+\mu_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d}x_{d}+\mu=0\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
be an arbitrary sphere. If
\gamma(t_{0})\in\omega
, then
identity
in
Theorem

4
implies that
t_{0}
is a zero of
Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}g+\mu_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\mu_{d}x_{d}+\mu
. Since
degree
and
independence
show that
\deg Q\leq d+1
and
Q\neq 0
, the number of
t_{0}
is upper bounded by
d+1
.

We have shown that the image of
\gamma
contains neither
d+2
coplanar points nor
d+2
cospherical points in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and we are left to prove that this image has at least
p-d-2
distinct points. To see this, noticing
\deg h=d+1
and
\bigl|\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:h(t)=0\bigr\}\bigr|\leq d+1
, it suffices to show that
\gamma
has at most one
self-intersection
, which refers to a pair of distinct
t_{1},t_{2}
with
\gamma(t_{1})=\gamma(t_{2})
. If this is not the case, then we can find a
(d+2)
-element
T\subset\mathbb{F}_{d}
with
h(t)\neq 0\,(\forall t\in T)
and
|\gamma(T)|=\bigl|\bigl\{\gamma(t):t\in T\bigr\}\bigr|\leq d
. So,
\gamma(T)
is contained in some plane
\{\rho_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\rho_{d}x_{d}+\rho=0\}
, hence
R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\rho_{1}f_{1}+\dots+\rho_{d}f_{d}+\rho f
has
d+2
zeros in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. However,
degree
and
independence
in
Theorem

4
tell us that
\deg R\leq d+1
and
R\neq 0
, a contradiction. Therefore, the proof of
Theorem

2
is complete.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3
.

Similar to the proof of
Theorem

2
, by embedding
[n]^{d}
into
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
,
Corollary

10
tells us that it suffices to construct for every prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
a subset
\widetilde{S}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
of size
\frac{1-o(1)}{d+1}\cdot p
.

Pick polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{d},g,h
as in
Theorem

4
. Consider the parametric curve

\widetilde{\gamma}\colon\biggl\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:t^{-1}\in\Bigl\{\overline{1},\overline{2},\dots,\overline{\bigl\lfloor\tfrac{p-1}{d+1}\bigr\rfloor}\Bigr\},\,h(t)\neq 0\biggr\}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}\qquad t\mapsto\biggl(\frac{f_{1}(t)}{h(t)},\,\frac{f_{2}(t)}{h(t)},\,\dots,\,\frac{f_{d}(t)}{h(t)}\biggr).

We are going to show that the image of
\gamma
provides a valid construction of
S
. The same arguments as in the proof of
Theorem

2
show that this image, containing at least
\bigl\lfloor\frac{p-1}{d+1}\bigr\rfloor-d-2
distinct points, contains no
d+2
cospherical points. We are left to show that no plane intersects it in
d+1
points.

For any
\pi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}x_{d}+\lambda=0\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
, if
\widetilde{\gamma}(t_{0})\in\pi
, then
t_{0}
is a zero of the polynomial
P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{1}f_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{d}f_{d}+\lambda h=0
, where
degree
,
independence
in
Theorem

4

show that
\deg P\leq d+1
and
P\neq 0
. Moreover,
nice
implies that its linear coefficient vanishes (i.e.,
[x]P=0
). If
P
has
d+1
distinct zeros
t_{1},\dots,t_{d+1}
in
\{\overline{1},\overline{2},\dots,\overline{n}\}
, then
[x]P=0
and Vieta’s theorem tell us that

t_{1}^{-1}+\dots+t_{d+1}^{-1}=0.

This implies that such zeros cannot live in the domain of
\widetilde{\gamma}
simultaneously. Thus, each plane in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
intersects
\widetilde{\gamma}
in at most
d
points. The proof of
Theorem

3
is complete.
∎

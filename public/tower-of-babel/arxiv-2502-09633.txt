Bernoulli Partitions
Thomas Curtright

Abstract
Scale invariant scattering suggests that all Bernoulli numbers B 2 ​ n B_{2n} can be naturally partitioned, i.e., written as particular finite sums of same-signed, monotonic, rational numbers. Some properties of these rational numbers are discussed here, especially in the limit of large n n .



In tribute to Luca Mezincescu (1946-2025)

In this note I extend some earlier observations
[
1
]
about
Bernoulli numbers as obtained in the context of computing the scattering
amplitude for a scale invariant potential
[
2
,
3
]
.

In two spatial dimensions (2D), when computed using quantum mechanics,
non-relativistic scattering by a repulsive inverse square potential

V=\kappa/r^{2}
(
\kappa>0
) results in a simple form for the integrated
cross section,
\sigma=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{d\sigma}{d\theta}\right)d\theta
. For a mono-energetic beam of mass
m
particles the 2D result is

\sigma=2\pi^{2}m\kappa/\left(\hbar^{2}k\right)
where the incident energy
is
E=\hbar^{2}k^{2}/\left(2m\right)
. This result follows from a
straightforward application of 2D phase-shift analysis for the potential

V=\kappa/r^{2}
upon realizing a remarkable identity involving the sinc
function,
\operatorname{sinc}\left(z\right)\equiv\sin\left(z\right)/z
.

A succinct form of the identity in question is
[
4
]

1=\frac{\sin\left(\pi x\right)}{\pi x}+2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l}\sin\left(\pi\sqrt{l^{2}+x^{2}}\right)}{\pi\sqrt{l^{2}+x^{2}}}

(1)

All higher powers of
x
cancel when terms on the RHS are expanded as series
in
x^{2}
, as a consequence of familiar
\zeta\left(2n\right)
exact
values for integer
n>0
, as shown in
[
2
]
. Upon expressing the
sinc function in terms of spherical Bessel functions, and then using series
representations for Bessel functions
[
5
]
in terms of Bernoulli
numbers
B_{n}
, the identity (
1
) leads directly to a set of finite
sum identities involving those numbers. Namely,

1=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\left(4n+2\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(2n\right)!}{n!k!\left(n-k\right)!}\left(\frac{B_{n+k+1}}{n+k+1}\right)\text{ \ \ for integer }n\geq 1\text{.}

(2)

This finite sum involves

trinomial
coefficients
as well as

divided Bernoulli
numbers
,
\beta_{m}\equiv B_{m}/m
. It is not difficult to check the
validity of (
2
) using various expressions of the Bernoulli numbers as
finite,
alternating
sums
[
6
]
. Recall the usual phases
are given by
B_{2n}=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\left|B_{2n}\right|

as well as
B_{2n+1}=0
for
n=1,2,\cdots
. Also recall the well-known
relation

\left|B_{2n}\right|=\frac{2\left(2n\right)!}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2n}}~\zeta\left(2n\right)\text{ \ \ for \ \ }n=1,2,\cdots

(3)

Thus, given an identity for
\left|B_{2n}\right|
a similar identity
for
\zeta\left(2n\right)
follows immediately.

As quipped previously
[
1
]
, if encountered as graffiti on the
stones of a bridge (say, in Ireland) either (
1
) or its companion
identity (
2
) might cause nothing more than a raised eyebrow in
passing.

Perhaps justifiably so. However, upon inverting the linear
relations in (
2
) to obtain expressions for each individual Bernoulli
number, the results are more striking: The Bernoulli numbers
B_{2n}
for

n\geq 2
are
naturally partitioned
. That is to say, each
B_{2n}
is
given by an interesting sum of
n-1
monotonic, same-signed, rational numbers.
Unlike many well-known representations of
B_{2n}
, here the terms in the
finite sums do
not
alternate in sign
[
7
]
.

For example, the first ten partitions are given by

\frac{1}{20}+\frac{3}{140}+\frac{1}{252}+\frac{1}{2772}\\

\frac{691}{900}+\frac{691}{2100}+\frac{59}{945}+\frac{41}{5940}+\frac{5}{10\,296}+\frac{1}{51\,480}\\

In the finite sequence of terms that sum to give
\left|B_{2n}\right|
for
n>2
, as displayed above, obviously the second number in the sequence
is just
3/7
times the first. Less obviously, each term in the sequence for

\left|B_{2n}\right|
is greater than the subtotal of all the
smaller terms in that same sequence.

The general result for
\left|B_{2n}\right|
is obtained by writing
(
2
) as an infinite matrix equation,
\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{M\cdot B}
, where
\boldsymbol{B}
is an infinite column of the
even index Bernoulli numbers,
\boldsymbol{1}
is an infinite column of
1
s,
and
\boldsymbol{M}_{m,n}=2\left(-1\right)^{m+1}\binom{2n-1}{m}\binom{2m+1}{2n}
. Computing the inverse for the triangular matrix

\boldsymbol{M}
then gives
\boldsymbol{B=M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\cdot 1}
. All
terms in a given row of the triangular matrix
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
have the
same sign. The ordered terms in the sums above are just the unsigned entries
in
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
for the
n
th row. For example, the first six rows
and columns of the triangular matrices
\boldsymbol{M}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
are given by

\boldsymbol{M=}\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccccc}6&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&-30&0&0&0&0\\
0&70&140&0&0&0\\
0&0&-840&-630&0&0\\
0&0&924&6930&2772&0\\
0&0&0&-18\,018&-48\,048&-12\,012\end{array}\right),\ \ \boldsymbol{M}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccccc}\frac{1}{6}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{30}&0&0&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{60}&\frac{1}{140}&0&0&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{45}&-\frac{1}{105}&-\frac{1}{630}&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{20}&\frac{3}{140}&\frac{1}{252}&\frac{1}{2772}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{6}&-\frac{1}{14}&-\frac{17}{1260}&-\frac{1}{693}&-\frac{1}{12\,012}\end{array}\right)

hence the previous partitions of
\left|B_{2n}\right|
for
n=1
to

6
. Various things can be said in general about the entries in

\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
, e.g., the diagonal is
\left|\boldsymbol{M}_{n,n}^{-1}\right|=\frac{\left(n!\right)^{2}}{\left(2n+1\right)!}
, the first sub-diagonal is
\left|\boldsymbol{M}_{n\geq 2,n-1}^{-1}\right|=\frac{1}{6}\left(n-2\right)\frac{n!\left(n-1\right)!}{\left(2n-1\right)!}
, the second sub-diagonal is
\left|\boldsymbol{M}_{n\geq 3,n-2}^{-1}\right|=\frac{7}{360}\left(n-\frac{8}{7}\right)\left(n-3\right)\frac{n!\left(n-2\right)!}{\left(2n-3\right)!}
, etc.

Since the first columns of
\boldsymbol{M}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
are null
except for the top entry, such that
\left|B_{2}\right|=1/6
and

\left|B_{4}\right|=1/30
are both without any partitioning, it
makes sense to begin the set of partitions for
\left|B_{2m}\right|

with those for
m=2
. One way to express the general result is then

\left|B_{2m}\right|=\sum_{n=2}^{m}b_{m}\left(n\right)\ ,\ \ \ m\geq 2

(4)

where
b_{m}\left(n\right)
is the non-zero
n^{th}

unsigned

column entry in the
m^{th}
row of the aforementioned triangular matrix

\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
. For example,
b_{2}\left(2\right)=1/30
,

b_{3}\left(2\right)=1/60
, and
b_{3}\left(3\right)=1/140
, with no
other non-zero entries in the 2nd and 3rd rows of
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
. So
each
\left|B_{2m}\right|
is given as a sum of
m-1
monotonically
falling, positive rational numbers as tabulated above for
2\leq m\leq 10
.

For larger values of
m
and
n
, as presented previously in
[
1
]
,

b_{m}\left(n\right)=\frac{m!n!}{\left(2n+1\right)!}\left(n-1\right)q_{m-1-n}\left(m\right)\ ,\ \ \ m\geq 2

(5)

where again
m
is the
row
index and
n
is the
column
index of

\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
. Here
q_{-1}\left(n\right)\equiv\frac{1}{n-1}

while the
q_{l}\left(n\right)
, with
l\geq 0
are
l
th order polynomials
in powers of
n
. These polynomials may be obtained sequentially

[
1
]
by iteration of

q_{l}\left(n\right)=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l}\left(n-l-3\right)!}{\left(2l+3\right)!\left(n-2l-3\right)!}+\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{l+j+1}\left(n+j-l-1\right)!}{\left(2l+1-2j\right)!\left(n+2j-2l-1\right)!}~q_{j}\left(n+j-l\right)

(6)

A few explicit examples are
[
8
]

q_{-1}\left(n\right)=\frac{1}{n-1}\ ,\ \ \ q_{0}\left(n\right)=\frac{1}{6}\ ,\ \ \ q_{1}\left(n\right)=\frac{7}{360}~n-\frac{1}{45}\ ,\ \ \ q_{2}\left(n\right)=\frac{31}{15\,120}~n^{2}-\frac{89}{15\,120}~n+\frac{1}{315}\ ,\ \ \ \text{etc.}

(7)

For Bernoulli partitions the
q_{l}\left(n\right)
polynomials are only
needed for
n\geq l+3
.

Now it so happens, for any fixed column
n
, numerical calculations

[
9
]
reveal that
b_{m}\left(n\right)
grows without bound as

m
increases with asymptotic behavior proportional to that of
\left|B_{2m}\right|\sim\frac{2\left(2m\right)!}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2m}}
. Moreover, as the row index
m
increases, there is a monotonic approach
to an asymptotic ratio

\frac{b_{m}\left(n\right)}{\left|B_{2m}\right|}\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}a\left(n\right)

(8)

where
a\left(n\right)
is a monotonically falling function of the column
index for
n\geq 2
. For example, numerically to 10 digits:
a\left(2\right)=0.657\,973\,626\,7
,
a\left(3\right)=0.281\,988\,697\,2
,

a\left(4\right)=0.0535\,819\,841\,1
,
a\left(5\right)=0.005\,985\,334\,466
, etc. When augmented by

https://oeis.org/A182448
, these numerical
revelations lead to the following.

Conjecture 1

The asymptotic ratios are
a\left(n\right)=p_{n}\left(\pi^{2}\right)

for
n\geq 2
where
p_{n}\left(x\right)
are polynomials:

p_{n}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4n^{2}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(-4\right)^{k}x^{k+1}}{\left(2k+1\right)!}\frac{\Gamma\left(n-1\right)\Gamma\left(2n-2-2k\right)}{\Gamma\left(n-1-2k\right)\Gamma\left(2n-2\right)}

(9)

with
\left\lfloor\ldots\right\rfloor
representing the floor
function.
1
1

1

For
n\geq 2
, note that
\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor-1}\frac{\left(-4\right)^{k}x^{k+1}}{\left(2k+1\right)!}\frac{\Gamma\left(n-1\right)\Gamma\left(2n-2-2k\right)}{\Gamma\left(n-1-2k\right)\Gamma\left(2n-2\right)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\left(n-1\right)/2\right\rfloor}\frac{\left(-2\right)^{k}x^{k+1}}{\left(2k+1\right)!}\left({\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{k}}\frac{\left(n-k-1-l\right)}{\left(2n-1-2l\right)}\right)

as used in earlier versions of this paper, with the empty product equal to
1

by convention.

For example, numerically to 16 digits:
\ p_{2}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{15}\pi^{2}=0.657\,973\,626\,739\,290\,6\cdots
,

p_{3}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{35}\pi^{2}=0.281\,988\,697\,173\,981\,7\cdots
,
p_{4}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{63}\pi^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{15}\pi^{2}\right)=0.0535\,819\,841\,082\,941\,2\cdots
, and
p_{5}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{99}\pi^{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{21}\pi^{2}\right)=0.005\,985\,334\,466\,027\,733\cdots

Remark 1

Here are suggestions for a possible proof of the conjecture. In the large

m
limit, keeping only leading terms, recast the iteration (
6
)
as a homogeneous second-order difference equation in
n
for
a_{n}\equiv p_{n}\left(x\right)
, to obtain

\left(2n+1\right)p_{n}\left(x\right)=\left(2n-3\right)p_{n-1}\left(x\right)-\frac{x}{\left(2n-1\right)}~p_{n-2}\left(x\right)

(10)

where
x
is a parameter related to the normalization of the solution. With
chosen initial conditions
p_{1}\left(x\right)=0
and
p_{2}\left(x\right)=x/15
, the solution of (
10
) is given by (
9
).
With
p_{1}=0
it follows from (
10
) that
7p_{3}\left(x\right)=3p_{2}\left(x\right)
, which is the same as the exact relation previously
observed in the table of partitions of
\left|B_{2n}\right|
for

3\leq n\leq 10
. It remains to determine the normalization of
p_{2}
, i.e.,
the value of
x
. Allowed values of
x
must now satisfy two requirements:
(1)
0\leq p_{n}\left(x\right)\leq 1
for all
n\geq 2
, and
(2)
\ \sum_{n=2}^{\infty}p_{n}\left(x\right)=1
. That is to say, the

p_{n}
must provide an infinite partition of
1
, as discussed in more detail
below. The claim is that only
x=\pi^{2}
satisfies these
requirements.

As re-expressed by Maple and Mathematica, the above polynomials can be
evaluated as generalized hypergeometric functions, namely,

p_{n}\left(x\right)=\left(\frac{x}{4n^{2}-1}\right)\left.{}_{2}F_{3}\right.\left(\frac{2-n}{2},~\frac{3-n}{2}~;~\frac{3}{2},~\frac{3-2n}{2},~2-n~;~-x\right)

(11)

Moreover, as suggested by the series form in (
9
), the leading
asymptotic behavior for large
n
then leads back to the sinc function.

\left.{}_{2}F_{3}\right.\left(\frac{2-n}{2},~\frac{3-n}{2}~;~\frac{3}{2},~\frac{3-2n}{2},~2-n~;~-x\right)\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{\sin\left(\sqrt{x}\right)}{\sqrt{x}}

(12)

That is somewhat interesting, but more importantly for the partitions of

\left|B_{2m}\right|
, even for finite
n
these particular
hypergeometric functions can be expressed in terms of Bessel functions

[
10
]
which leads to a more succinct but less transparent closed form
for the conjectured asymptotic ratios.

a\left(n\right)=p_{n}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{n!}{\left(2n+1\right)!}\left(2\pi\right)^{n}J_{n-1/2}\left(\pi\right)

(13)

Taking (
3
) into account then gives

b_{m}\left(n\right)\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\frac{\left(2m\right)!~n!}{\left(2n+1\right)!}\left(2\pi\right)^{n-2m}\sqrt{2}\pi J_{n-1/2}\left(\pi\right)

(14)

The right-hand-side of (
14
) is the “asymptotic
approximant” to
b_{m}\left(n\right)
for fixed
n
.

For a fixed row number
m\geq 2
, dividing the non-zero column entries in the
matrix
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
by the Bernoulli number corresponding to
m

gives an exact partition of
1
as a finite sum of
m-1
monotonic, same-sign,
rational numbers:
\ 1=\sum_{n=2}^{m}\frac{b_{m}\left(n\right)}{\left|B_{2m}\right|}
. By extension, using (
13
), the ratios

a\left(n\right)
also sum to give unity
[
11
]
:
1=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a\left(n\right)
, where the sum is now infinite because in
the limit of infinite row number there are an infinite number of non-zero
entries in the columns of
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
. In fact the sum over
n

converges rapidly to
1
when computed numerically. For example,

1-\sum_{n=2}^{18}p_{n}\left(\pi^{2}\right)=2.\,6\times 10^{-27}
.

Of course there are an infinite (albeit countable) number of other ways to
partition
1
into a sum of positive rationals, each less than one and
arranged in a monotonic sequence.
But
most such partitions have

nothing
to do with Bernoulli numbers. In contrast to such generic
partitions, in the author’s opinion, the scale symmetry origins of the
partitions produced by
\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}
make those special partitions
intriguing and worth pursuing.

In particular, the monotonic finite series representation of
B_{2n}=\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\left|B_{2n}\right|
clearly gives a series of
progressively better bounds on
B_{2n}
. Such a series of constraints on

B_{2n}
might be useful to establish bounds on functions defined as infinite
series whose coefficients involve the Bernoulli numbers
[
12
]
. That
remains to be shown, but some form of scale symmetry has long been suspected
to be lurking in the Riemann hypothesis
[
13
]
. If so, the progression
of bounds on
B_{2n}
that follow from the partitions discussed here may
provide some insight.

Added Note:
More recent work
[
14
]
shows the
exact
ratio

b_{m}\left(n\right)/\left|B_{2m}\right|
for any
m\geq n\geq 2

9
) after the replacement
x^{k}\rightarrow\pi^{2k}\zeta\left(2m-2k\right)/\zeta\left(2m\right)
. Asymptotic
behavior as

for fixed

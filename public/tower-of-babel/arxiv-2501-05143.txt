Abstract
. We consider inner functions Θ \Theta with the zero set 𝒵 ⁡ ( Θ ) \mathcal{Z}(\Theta) such that the quotient algebra H ∞ / Θ ​ H ∞ H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty} satisfies the Strong Invertibility Property (SIP), that is for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 there exists δ > 0 \delta>0 such that the conditions f ∈ H ∞ f\in H^{\infty} , ‖ [ f ] ‖ H ∞ / Θ ​ H ∞ = 1 \|[f]\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}=1 , inf 𝒵 ⁡ ( Θ ) | f | ≥ 1 − δ \inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|\geq 1-\delta imply that [ f ] [f] is invertible in H ∞ / Θ ​ H ∞ H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty} and ‖ 1 / [ f ] ‖ H ∞ / Θ ​ H ∞ ≤ 1 + ε \|1/[f]\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}\leq 1+\varepsilon . We prove that the SIP is equivalent to the maximal asymptotic growth of Θ \Theta away from its zero set. We also describe inner functions satisfying the SIP in terms of the narrowness of their sublevel sets and relate the SIP to the Weak Embedding Property introduced by P.Gorkin, R.Mortini, and N.Nikolski as well as to inner functions whose Frostman shifts are Carleson–Newman Blaschke products. We finally study divisors of inner functions satisfying the SIP. We describe geometrically the zero set of inner functions such that all its divisors satisfy the SIP. We also prove that a closed subset E E of the unit circle is of finite entropy if and only if any singular inner function associated to a singular measure supported on E E is a divisor of an inner function satisfying the SIP.

Introduction

Let
H^{\infty}
be the algebra of bounded analytic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
of the complex plane endowed with the supremum norm

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\mathbb{D}}|f|
. A function
\Theta
in
H^{\infty}
is called inner if it has radial limits of modulus
1
at almost every point of the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Given an inner function
\Theta
, the quotient algebra

H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
is a Banach algebra with the natural norm
\|[f]\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}=\inf\{\|f+g\Theta\|_{\infty}:g\in H^{\infty}\}
, which has recently attracted some attention. See for instance
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
. The
visible spectrum
of
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
is
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)
, the zero set of
\Theta
, in the sense that the evaluation at a zero of
\Theta
is an obvious multiplicative character of
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
.
Intuitively, the zeroes of
\Theta
are the only places where
[f]\in H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
takes well-defined values.

Let
\rho(z,w)=\left|\dfrac{z-w}{1-\bar{w}z}\right|
denote the pseudohyperbolic distance between the points
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Given an inner function
\Theta
let us consider

(1)

\eta_{\Theta}(t):=\inf\left\{|\Theta(z)|:z\in\mathbb{D},\,\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq t\right\},0<t<1.

This is a non-decreasing function. It is not necessarily continuous (see an elementary example in Section
6.7
).
We define a function
\varkappa_{\Theta}
pseudo-inverse to
\eta_{\Theta}
by

\varkappa_{\Theta}(\lambda):=\inf\{t:\eta_{\Theta}(t)>\lambda\}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

If
f\in H^{\infty}
is such that the class
[f]
is invertible in
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
, then
\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|>0
. Following P.Gorkin, R.Mortini, and N.Nikolski (
[
6
]
),we say that an inner function
\Theta
satisfies the Weak Embedding Property (WEP) if the converse implication holds for any
f\in H^{\infty}
, that is, if for any
f\in H^{\infty}
the condition

\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|>0
implies that
[f]
is invertible in
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
. They proved that
\Theta
satisfies the WEP if an only if
\eta_{\Theta}(t)>0
for any
t>0
, see
[
6
]
. In the subsequent paper
[
18
]
N.Nikolski and V.Vasyunin proved that for any
0<c<1
there exists a Blaschke product
\Theta
such that
\eta_{\Theta}(t)=0
if and only if
0\leq t\leq c
. In this paper we study inner functions
\Theta
for which sharp invertibility in
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
holds.

Definition 1.1
.

An inner function
\Theta
satisfies the
Sharp Invertibility Property

(SIP)
if for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
f\in H^{\infty}
, the conditions
\|[f]\|\leq 1
and
\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|\geq 1-\delta

imply that
[f]
is invertible in
H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}
and
\|[f]^{-1}\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

As in
[
6
]
, one can relate the SIP to the growth of

|\Theta|
away from its zero set.
The WEP and the SIP are independent properties, although analogous. Our first result shows that the behavior of
\eta_{\Theta}(t)
when
t
tends to
1
is relevant for the SIP.

Theorem 1.2
.

An inner function
\Theta
satisfies the Sharp Invertibility Property
(SIP)
if and only if
it has Maximal Asymptotic Growth
(MAG)
, that is,
\lim_{t\to 1}\eta_{\Theta}(t)=\sup_{0<t<1}\eta_{\Theta}(t)=1
.

An immediate corollary of this Theorem is that, since the MAG property is clearly stable under finite products of the functions
\Theta
, so is the SIP.

Given an arc
I
on the unit circle of length
|I|
we denote the Carleson square based at
I
by
Q(I)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:z/|z|\in I,1-|z|\leq|I|\}
. A positive measure
\mu
in the unit disc is called a Carleson measure if there exists a constant
C=C(\mu)>0
such that
\mu(Q(I))\leq C|I|
for any Carleson square
Q(I)
.
Recall that a Blaschke product
\Theta
with zeros
\{a_{j}\}

is called
Carleson–Newman

\Theta\in(CN)

\sum_{j}(1-|a_{j}|)\delta_{a_{j}}
is a Carleson measure,
or equivalently, if

\sup_{k}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(a_{j},a_{k})^{2})=\sup_{k}\sum_{j}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})(1-|a_{k}|^{2})}{|1-a_{j}\bar{a}_{k}|^{2}}<\infty.

For

let
\varphi_{a}
be the automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
given by
\varphi_{a}(w)=(a-w)/(1-\overline{a}w)
.
There is a link between inner functions
\Theta
satisfying the SIP and the behavior of
\Theta
under Frostman shifts
\varphi_{a}\circ\Theta
,
a\in\mathbb{D}
.
It follows from (the proof of)
[
1

that whenever

, the composition
\varphi_{a}\circ\Theta

is a Carleson–Newman Blaschke product. By Theorem
1.2
, any SIP inner function then belongs to the class

(2)

\mathcal{M}:=\left\{\Theta\mbox{ inner }:\varphi_{a}\circ\Theta\in(CN),\text{ for any }a\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}\right\},

introduced in
[
14
]
. Note that the class
\mathcal{P}:=(CN)\cap\mathcal{M}
, which is formed by Carleson–Newman Blaschke products
all of whose Frostman shifts are again Carleson–Newman, had been studied beforehand and characterized in terms of
its zeros and behavior on the maximum ideal space of
H^{\infty}
, see
[
15
]
,
[
14
]

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a set
S\subset\mathbb{D}
is
narrow
if there exists
0<R<1
such that the set
S
contains no pseudohyperbolic disc of pseudohyperbolic radius

.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function. It is proved in
[

]
that
\Theta\in\mathcal{P}
if and only if the set
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|\Theta(z)|<1-\varepsilon\}

\Theta\in\mathcal{M}
if and only if the set
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\varepsilon<|\Theta(z)|<1-\varepsilon\}
is narrow for any
0<\varepsilon<1/2
. It is worth mentioning that the narrowness of the level sets of an inner function is also related to the membership of its derivative to Hardy or Nevanlinna spaces (
[
10
]
). Our next result says that inner functions satisfying the SIP can also be described by a narrowness condition.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function. Then
\Theta
satisfies the
SIP
if and only if
the set
\{z\in\mathbb{D}:0<|\Theta(z)|<1-\varepsilon\}
is narrow for any
0<\varepsilon<1
.

We next present two consequences of Theorems

and
1.4

\mathcal{M}
and the second one collects several relations between the classes
\mathcal{P}
,

and the SIP.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function in the class
\mathcal{M}
defined in (

\sup\{\eta_{\Theta}(t):0<t<1\}=1
(which means that
\Theta

) or

Let

For

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Theta

\Theta\in\mathcal{P}

(b)

\Theta

.

An important subclass of
\mathcal{P}
is that of the

Blaschke products, which are the Blaschke products

satisfy

\lim_{k\to\infty}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})|B^{\prime}(a_{k})|=1

satisfy the following thinness condition

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{j:j\neq k}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})(1-|a_{k}|^{2})}{|1-a_{j}\bar{a}_{k}|^{2}}=0.

The notion of thin sequences goes back to K.Hoffman
[

[

Finally, we turn to the question of which inner functions can be divisors of functions satisfying the SIP. For instance, a singular inner function, being zero-free, obviously never can satisfy the SIP,

We obtain a result which underlines the proximity between the notions of SIP and WEP.

Theorem 1.8

Let

has finite entropy.

(b)

is a divisor
of an inner function having the
SIP
.

Although we have some information on the relations between the WEP and the SIP, the following question remains open.

Question 1.9

Is it true that any function satisfying the

is a divisor of a function satisfying the

2.
Equivalence between Sharp Invertibility and Maximal Growth

The proof of Theorem
1.2
uses the following beautiful quantitative version of the Corona Theorem proved by P.Jones.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
12
, Theorem 1]
.

Let
N\geq 2
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}\in H^{\infty}
,

\|f_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
,
1\leq j\leq N
, and
\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|f_{j}(z)|\geq 1-\delta

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then there exist
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}\in H^{\infty}
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}(z)g_{j}(z)=1,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

and
\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\varepsilon
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First we show that MAG is sufficient for sharp invertibility. Let
f\in H^{\infty}
with
\|[f]\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}=1
and
\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|\geq 1-\eta
. Then for any
\delta>0
we can find a representative
f_{1}

of the class
[f]
with
\|f_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\delta
and
\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f_{1}|\geq 1-\eta
.
Taking
f_{2}:=(1+\delta)^{-1}f_{1}
, we have

\|f_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and

\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|g|\geq(1+\delta)^{-1}(1-\eta)

for any
g\in[f_{2}]
. Since
[f]^{-1}=(1+\delta)[f_{2}]^{-1}
, we may restrict ourselves to the case of
f\in H^{\infty}
,
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
such that
\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|\geq 1-\eta
.

Now we prove that for any
0<\varepsilon_{1}<1
, there exists
0<\eta_{1}<1
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and

\inf_{\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)}|f|\geq 1-\eta_{1}
imply that

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\max(|\Theta(z)|,|f(z)|)\geq 1-\varepsilon_{1}.

Indeed, let
0<\delta_{1}<1
such that
\eta_{\Theta}(\delta_{1})\geq 1-\varepsilon_{1}
.
This means that
|\Theta(z)|\geq 1-\varepsilon_{1}
if
\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq\delta_{1}
.
On the other hand, by the Schwarz–Pick Lemma, when
\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\leq\delta_{1}
, we have

|f(z)|\geq\frac{1-\eta_{1}-\delta_{1}}{1-(1-\eta_{1})\delta_{1}}\geq 1-\varepsilon_{1},

provided that we pick
0<\eta_{1}\leq(1-\delta_{1})(1+\delta_{1}(1-\varepsilon_{1}))^{-1}\varepsilon_{1}
.

Since
\max\{|\Theta(z)|,|f(z)|\}\geq 1-\varepsilon_{1}
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, Theorem
2.1
provides functions
g,h\in H^{\infty}
such that
fg+\Theta h\equiv 1
. Then we have
\|[f]^{-1}\|_{H^{\infty}/\Theta H^{\infty}}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\varepsilon
and it suffices to choose
\varepsilon_{1}:=\delta
in Theorem
2.1
and then
\eta=\eta_{1}
depending on
\varepsilon_{1}
,
as explained above.

Assume now that
\Theta
enjoys the SIP. Let
\varepsilon>0
and pick the corresponding
\delta>0
given by Definition
1.1
. Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
be such that

\rho(z_{0},\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq 1-\delta
. As before, for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\varphi_{a}
be the involutive
automorphism of the disc exchanging
0
and
a
. By the hypothesis applied
to
f:=\varphi_{z_{0}}
, there exist
g,h\in H^{\infty}
such that
1<\gamma:=\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\varepsilon

and
\varphi_{z_{0}}(z)g(z)+\Theta(z)h(z)=1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Then, let
g_{1}=\gamma^{-1}g
, and
g_{2}:=\varphi_{z_{0}}g_{1}=\gamma^{-1}(1-h\Theta)
,
so that
\|g_{2}\|_{\infty}=1
,

g_{2}(z_{0})=0
, and
g_{2}=\gamma^{-1}
on
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)
. Finally,

g_{3}:=\varphi_{\gamma^{-1}}\circ g_{2}
is a function vanishing on
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)

such that
\|g_{3}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Also,

g_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}-g_{2}}{1-\gamma^{-1}g_{2}}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}h\Theta}{1-\gamma^{-1}g_{2}}

is a multiple of
\Theta
, which is inner. Hence

on
\mathbb{D}
and we deduce

|\Theta(z_{0})|\geq|g_{3}(z_{0})|=|\varphi_{\gamma^{-1}}(0)|=\gamma^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}>1-\varepsilon.

\Box

3.
Sharp Invertibility and Sublevel sets

It will be convenient here and in some other places to use the hyperbolic (or Poincaré) distance given by

d_{H}(z,w):=\tanh^{-1}\rho(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,w)}{1-\rho(z,w)},\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

We first establish the necessity in Theorem
1.4

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Theta

\{w\in\mathbb{D}:0<|\Theta(w)|<1-\varepsilon\}

Proof.

Fix

\Theta

∎

For the converse, we first need an auxiliary result on positive harmonic functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
2\leq r_{1}\leq r_{0}-1
, denote
\rho_{j}:=\tanh r_{j}
,

. Let

be a positive harmonic function on

If there exists
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

h(\rho_{1}e^{i\theta_{0}})\leq\frac{1}{4}h(0)
, then
there exists

h(\rho_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{10}e^{-2r_{1}}\right)h(0)

Note that the conclusion of the lemma does not depend on the value of

as long as
r_{0}\geq r_{1}+1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Without loss of generality, assume
\theta_{0}=0
.
Let
s:=\rho_{1}/\rho_{0}
. Note that
\tanh 2\leq s<1
.
By Harnack’s inequality, for
|\theta|\leq\delta
,

h(\rho_{1}e^{i\theta})\leq h(\rho_{1})\frac{1+\rho(s,se^{i\delta})}{1-\rho(s,se^{i\delta})}.

Choosing
\delta:=\cos^{-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{1-s^{2}}{4s}\right)^{2}\right)
,
we can check that
\rho(s,se^{i\delta})\leq\frac{1}{3}
, and so

h(\rho_{1}e^{i\theta})\leq 2h(\rho_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}h(0)
for
|\theta|\leq\delta

which yields the first estimate. For the second one, observe that by Harnack’s inequality,

P[\mu](z_{J})\lesssim|J|^{-1}
and

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{(1-|z_{I}|)(1-|z_{J}|)}{|1-\overline{z_{I}}z_{J}|^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{1-|z_{J}|}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{F}}1-|z_{I}|\lesssim\frac{1}{|J|}.

The Claim is proved. ∎

Next, we consider

\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal{G}}\{L\mbox{ dyadic arc: }L\subset J,|L|\geq|J|^{2}\}.

Note that

\sum_{J\in\mathcal{L}}|J|\simeq\sum_{J\in\mathcal{G}}|J|\log_{2}|J|^{-1}<\infty,

by (
11
). So the sequence
\{z_{L}:L\in\mathcal{L}\}

satisfies the Blaschke condition. Let
B_{2}
be the corresponding
Blaschke product. We will prove that
fB_{2}=S_{\mu}B_{1}B_{2}
has the SIP. We need to show that
|f(z)B_{2}(z)|
is close to
1
when
\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(B_{1}B_{2}))
is sufficiently close to
1
.

The Claim gives that
\log|f(z)|^{-1}\leq\varepsilon
whenever
z\in Q(J)
and

1-|z|\leq\varepsilon|J|^{2}
, for some
J\in\mathcal{G}
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left\{z:\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(B_{1}B_{2}))\geq 1-\delta\right\}\subset\bigcup_{J\in\mathcal{G}}\left\{z\in Q_{J}:1-|z|\leq\varepsilon|J|^{2}\right\}.

Then it only remains to show that
\log|B_{2}(z)|^{-1}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0

when
z\in Q(J)
,
1-|z|\leq\varepsilon|J|^{2}
, for some
J\in\mathcal{G}
.
For such a point
z
, let
j_{0}:=j_{0}(z)
be the smallest positive integer
such that
2^{j_{0}}Q(z)
contains a zero of
B_{2}
, where
Q(z)=Q(L_{z})
with
L_{z}

the shortest dyadic arc
L
such that
z\in Q(L)
.
We have
j_{0}(z)\gtrsim\log_{2}\varepsilon^{-1}
.
Then

\log|B_{2}(z)|^{-1}\simeq\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L}}\frac{(1-|z_{L}|)(1-|z|)}{|1-\overline{z_{L}}z|^{2}}\\
\lesssim\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{2j}(1-|z|)}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{L},\,z_{L}\in Q(2^{j}L_{z})\setminus Q(2^{j-1}L_{z})}1-|z_{L}|\\
\lesssim\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\frac{j2^{j}}{2^{2j}}\lesssim\frac{j_{0}}{2^{j_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon\log\varepsilon^{-1}.

This completes the proof of the implication
{\rm(a)}\Rightarrow{\rm(b)}
.

Proof of
{\rm(b)}\Rightarrow{\rm(a)}
.

Lemma 4 of
[
2
]
establishes that a closed set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
has finite entropy if and only if for any positive Borel singular measure
\mu
supported on
E
and any constant
C>0
, one has

\sum|J|<\infty,

where the sum is taken over all dyadic arcs
J\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
satisfying the property
P[\mu](z_{J})\geq C
.
Arguing by contradiction, suppose that there exists a singular measure
\mu

supported on
E
such that

\sum|I|=\infty,

where the sum is taken over all dyadic arcs
I
such that
P[\mu](z_{I})\geq 2
. By Harnack’s inequality, we have
P[\mu](z)\geq 1
when
z\in D_{\rho}(z_{I},1/3)

and we deduce

\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}:P[\mu](z)\geq 1\}}\frac{dA(z)}{1-|z|}=\infty,

where
dA
denotes the area measure. This will contradict the next Lemma,
which parallels the analogous fact for inner functions having the WEP,
[
1
, Proposition 4]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
S_{\mu}
is a divisor of an inner function having the
SIP
, then for any
C>0
,

\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{D}:P[\mu](z)\geq C\}}\frac{dA(z)}{1-|z|}<\infty.

Proof.

Let
B
be an inner function such that
\Theta=BS_{\mu}
has the SIP. Using that
|\Theta(z)|\leq|S_{\mu}(z)|
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and that
\Theta
has the SIP, for any
C>0
we have

\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:P[\mu](z)\geq C\right\}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|S_{\mu}(z)|\leq e^{-C}\right\}\\
\subset\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|\Theta(z)|\leq e^{-C}\right\}\\
\subset\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(B))\leq\varkappa_{\Theta}(e^{-C})\right\}=:\Gamma.

The Blaschke condition on the zeros of
B
gives that

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dA(z)}{1-|z|}<\infty,

which finishes the proof.
∎

\Box

6.
Examples and further results

6.1.
Thin Blaschke products, super-separated zeros and the SIP

A sequence
\{a_{k}\}
of points in the unit disc is called an exponential sequence if

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1-|a_{k+1}|}{1-|a_{k}|}<1.

Proposition 6.1
.

(a)
Any finite product of thin Blaschke products satisfies the SIP.

(b)
Any Blaschke product whose zero set is a finite union of exponential sequences satisfies the SIP.

Note that Blaschke products in both of these classes are Carleson–Newman, therefore by part (c) of Corollary
1.6
belong to
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

In part (a) one only needs to show that a thin Blaschke product satisfies the MAG condition, which by Theorem
1.2
means that it satisfies the SIP. This follows from classical estimates of K.Hoffmann, see
[
3
, Lemma 1.4 of Chapter X]
.

For (b), Theorem
1.7
(c) shows that the Blaschke product is in the class
\mathcal{P}
and hence satisfies the SIP.

∎

Of course, many SIP Blaschke products are not thin (for instance the Blaschke product with zeros
1-2^{-j}
,
j\geq 1
).
However, if the zeros of an inner function are sufficiently separated, then the SIP implies thinness.
Recall that a sequence
\{a_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
is called
super-separated
if for any
\delta>0
, there exists

N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho(a_{j},a_{k})\geq 1-\delta
for any
j>k\geq N
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function whose zeros are super-separated. Assume that
\Theta
satisfies the
SIP
. Then
\Theta
is a thin Blaschke product.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
one can find a sequence
r_{n}\to 1

such that
|\Theta(r_{n}e^{i\theta})|\to 1
. Hence
\Theta
cannot have a non-trivial singular inner factor.
Now we need to show that
(1-|a_{k}|)\Theta^{\prime}(a_{k})|
can be made arbitrarily close to
1
when
k

is large enough. Here
\{a_{k}\}
denotes the sequence of zeros of
\Theta
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
. By the SIP, there exists
0<\delta<1
such that

|\Theta(z)|\geq 1-\varepsilon
whenever
\rho(z,\{a_{k}\})\geq 1-\delta
. Pick an integer
N=N(\delta)>0
large enough so that
for
k\geq N
, we have that
D_{\rho}(a_{k},1-\delta)

is at
\rho
-distance at least
1-\delta
from any other zero of
\Theta
, so that
|\Theta(z)/\varphi_{a_{k}}(z)|\geq|\Theta(z)|\geq 1-\varepsilon
for any
z\in\partial D_{\rho}(a_{k},1-\delta)
. Since
\Theta(z)/\varphi_{a_{k}}
is zero-free on the disc
D_{\rho}(a_{k},1-\delta)
, we obtain that
(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\Theta^{\prime}(a_{k})|\geq 1-\varepsilon

by applying the minimum modulus principle to the function
\Theta/\varphi_{a_{k}}
at
z=a_{k}
.
∎

We will see that a divisor of a SIP Blaschke product is not, in general, SIP; however Blaschke products with a super-separated zero sequence
(and so in particular thin Blaschke products) are in a sense negligible elements for the SIP. In other words an inner function can be multiplied
or divided by such a Blaschke product without changing its status with respect to the SIP.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
be two inner functions. Assume that
\Theta=\Theta_{1}\Theta_{2}
satisfies the SIP and that
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1})
is finite or super-separated, then
\Theta_{2}
satisfies the SIP.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1})
is finite. By induction, we may assume that it only contains a single point
a_{1}
. It suffices to show that
\eta_{\Theta_{2}}(\tanh(3t))\geq\eta_{\Theta}(\tanh t)
for
t>0
. Let
z\in\mathbb{D}
be such that

d_{H}(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{2}))\geq 3t.

We consider two cases.

If
d_{H}(z,a_{1})\geq t
, then
|\Theta_{2}(z)|\geq|\Theta(z)|\geq\eta_{\Theta}(\tanh t)
.

If
d_{H}(z,a_{1})<t
, then all
w\in\mathbb{D}
such
d_{H}(z,w)=2t
satisfy the relation
d_{H}(w,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq t
, thus
|\Theta_{2}(w)|\geq|\Theta(w)|\geq\eta_{\Theta}(\tanh t)
and since
\Theta_{2}
does not have zeros on
D_{H}(z,2t)
we also have
|\Theta_{2}(z)|\geq\eta_{\Theta}(\tanh t)
.

Now we assume that
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1})=\{a_{j}\}
is super-separated.

Let
t>0
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
be such that
d_{H}(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{2}))\geq 3t
. There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d_{H}(a_{k},a_{j})\geq 4t
for all
j>k>N
. Applying the previous case, we see that
\tilde{\Theta}:=\Theta\prod_{j\leq N}b_{j}^{-1}
, where

b_{j}(z):=\frac{\bar{a}_{j}}{|a_{j}|}\frac{a_{j}-z}{1-\bar{a}_{j}z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

satisfies the SIP.

If
d_{H}(z,a_{j})\geq t
for all
j>N
, then
|\Theta_{2}(z)|\geq|\tilde{\Theta}(z)|\geq\eta_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\tanh t)
.

Assume that
d_{H}(z,a_{j})<t
for some
j>N
, then all
w
such that
d_{H}(z,w)=2t
satisfy
d_{H}(w,\mathcal{Z}(\tilde{\Theta}))\geq t
. We conclude like in the previous case that

\eta_{\Theta_{2}}(\tanh(3t))\geq\eta_{\tilde{\Theta}}(\tanh t)
.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\Theta_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
, be inner functions, each one with super-separated zeros and let the product
\prod_{1\leq j\leq N}\Theta_{j}
satisfy the
SIP
. Then each
\Theta_{j}
is a thin Blaschke product.

Proof.

By induction and Proposition
6.3
, we show that each
\Theta_{j}
satisfies the SIP. Then, we apply Proposition
6.2
to deduce that each
\Theta_{j}
is a thin Blaschke product.
∎

6.2.
Carleson–Newman Blaschke products, the WEP, and the SIP

Proposition 6.5
.

There exist Blaschke products satisfying both the SIP and the WEP which are not Carleson–Newman.

Proof.

This construction is due to S.Treil as published in
[
6
]
. Once again, we carry out
the computations in the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
. Recall that for
z,w\in\mathbb{H}
,

1-\rho(z,w)^{2}=\frac{4\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Im}w}{(\operatorname{Re}z-\operatorname{Re}w)^{2}+(\operatorname{Im}z+\operatorname{Im}w)^{2}}.

Set
y_{n}=n^{3}
,
n\geq 1
, and choose a decreasing sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}
, with
\delta_{1}=1
and
\delta_{n}\to 0
,
such that
\sum_{n}(\delta_{n}y_{n})^{-1}<\infty
. This ensures that
z_{n,k}:=k\delta_{n}y_{n}+iy_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, defines a Blaschke sequence in
\mathbb{H}
.
Let
B
denote the associated Blaschke product. It is proved in
[
6
]
that
B
satisfies the WEP, and it is obvious that
B
is not Carleson–Newman.

It is easy to see that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

d_{H}(z,\mathcal{Z}(B))\geq 1-\varepsilon_{0}
implies that
\operatorname{Im}z\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Suppose that
z=x+iy
,

0<y\leq\frac{1}{4}
, and that
\min_{n,k}\rho(z,z_{n,k})\geq 1-\varepsilon
. If we choose
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
|x-k_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

2\varepsilon\geq 1-\rho(z,z_{0,k_{0}})^{2}\geq\frac{4y}{\frac{1}{4}+(y+1)^{2}}\geq 2y,

so
y\leq\varepsilon
. Using Riemann sums, we then can estimate

(12)

\log|B(z)|^{-1}\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{Z}}1-\rho(z,z_{n,k})^{2}\\
\lesssim\sum_{n\geq 0}4yy_{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(x-k\delta_{n}y_{n})^{2}+y_{n}^{2}}\lesssim y\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\delta_{n}y_{n}}\lesssim\varepsilon,

so
B
satisfies the SIP.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

There exist a Blaschke product satisfying the
SIP
and not satisfying the
WEP
.

Proof.

We just take the Blaschke product
B
from Proposition
6.5
and remove its zeros in the hyperbolic discs
D_{H}(8^{n}i,1)
,
n\geq 1
.
Then the remaining Blaschke product
B_{1}
does not satisfy the WEP because the discs
D_{H}(8^{n}i,2)
contain an increasing number of zeroes, while their centers
8^{n}i
are at a fixed distance from the zero set. Next,
B_{1}
satisfies the SIP because all points which are at
pseudohyperbolic distance more than
1-\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}
of the zero set, for some
\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}<\min(\varepsilon_{0},1-\tanh 2)

must verify
\operatorname{Im}z\leq\frac{1}{4}
, and
we just use estimate (
12
) and the fact that
|B_{1}|\geq|B|
.
∎

Next we provide examples of Interpolating Blaschke products which do not satisfy the SIP. Our examples are very close to those in the proof of Theorem 5 in
[
1
]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

There exist interpolating Blaschke products (which satisfy therefore the
WEP
) which
do not satisfy the
SIP
.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we construct a family of such examples in the upper half plane. For any
interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, let
\tilde{I}
be the interval with the same center and triple the length.

Let
\{L_{n}\}

be a decreasing summable sequence of positive numbers, and
I_{n}:=[x_{n},x_{n}+L_{n}]\subset\mathbb{R}
a family of
intervals such that the intervals
\{\tilde{I}_{n}\}
are pairwise disjoint,
x_{n}+2L_{n}\leq x_{n+1}
for any
n
and all contained in a fixed bounded
interval.
Pick an increasing sequence of integers
\{N_{n}\}\to\infty
, and define

z_{n,k}:=x_{n}+\frac{kL_{n}}{N_{n}}+i\frac{L_{n}}{N_{n}},\quad 0\leq k<N_{n}.

Let
B
be the Blaschke product with zeros
\{z_{n,k}:0\leq k<N_{n},n\geq 1\}
.
The Blaschke condition is verified because of the summability assumption of
\{L_{n}\}
. It is clear that
B
is an interpolating Blaschke product.

Note that there exists a universal constant
0<c_{0}<1
such that
|B(z)|\leq c_{0}
for any
z\in Q_{n}:=\{x+iy:x\in I_{n},L_{n}/N_{n}\leq y\leq L_{n}\}
. Pick
w_{n}\in Q_{n}
with
\operatorname{Im}(w_{n})=L_{n}
. Note that
\rho(w_{n},\mathcal{Z}(B))\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.
Hence
B
does not satisfy the MAG condition.

∎

Last example can be improved to obtain a Blaschke product which cannot even become SIP after the addition
of extra zeros.

Proposition 6.8
.

There exists an interpolating Blaschke product which is not a divisor of a
\mathrm{SIP}
function.

Proof.

Consider the Blaschke product
B
of Proposition
6.7
above.
This time choose
L_{n},N_{n}
such that
\sum_{n}L_{n}<\infty
,
\sum_{n}L_{n}\log N_{n}=\infty
. As before,
B
is an interpolating Blaschke product.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that
B_{1}
is a Blaschke product such that
BB_{1}
has the SIP. Since
|BB_{1}(z)|\leq|B(z)|\leq c_{0}
for any
z\in Q_{n}
, there exists a zero
\zeta
of
BB_{1}
such that
\rho(z,\zeta)\leq\eta_{BB_{1}}^{-1}(c_{0})
.
The Blaschke sum of
BB_{1}
on
Q_{n}
is thus bigger than a fixed multiple of

\int_{Q_{n}}\frac{dm_{2}(z)}{1-|z|}\asymp L_{n}\log N_{n}.

So the zeros of
BB_{1}
cannot satisfy the Blaschke condition.
∎

6.3.
Divisors of SIP are not always SIP

Our next result says that divisors of SIP Blaschke products may not be SIP.

Proposition 6.9
.

There exists a Blaschke product
B=B_{1}B_{2}
which has the
SIP
such that
B_{i}
do not have
SIP
or
WEP
,
i=1,2
.

Proof.

We argue in the upper half plane. Let
B
be the Blaschke product constructed by S.Treil described in Proposition
6.5
. Let
B_{1}
(respectively
B_{2}
) be the Blaschke product having the zeros of
B
with positive (respectively negative) real part. Note that for any

\lim_{x\to+\infty}B_{2}(x+icx)=0,\quad\lim_{x\to+\infty}B_{1}(-x+icx)=0.

Hence

,

does not have the SIP or the WEP.
∎

6.4.
Stability of the zero set under local perturbations

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function,
\{z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)
, and let
\{z_{n}^{*}\}_{n\geq 1}
be such that
\sup_{n\geq 1}\rho(z_{n},z^{*}_{n})<1
. Denote by
B
(respectively
B^{*}
) the Blaschke products
with zero sets
\{z_{n}\}
(respectively
\{z^{*}_{n}\}
).
If
\Theta
has the
SIP
, then
\Theta_{1}=\Theta B^{*}/B
has the
SIP
.

In contrast, it is known
[
1
]
that we can have

\Theta\in\mathrm{WEP}
, and
\Theta B^{*}/B\not\in\mathrm{WEP}
.

Proof.

Let
a=\sup_{n\geq 1}\rho(z_{n},z^{*}_{n})<1
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
. Since
\Theta
has the
\mathrm{SIP}
, there exists
t<1
such that

\rho(w,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq t\implies|\Theta(w)|\geq 1-\varepsilon^{3}.

Then for some
t_{1}=t_{1}(t,a)<1
we have

\rho(w,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1}))\geq t_{1}\implies\rho(w,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq t\implies|\Theta(w)|\geq 1-\varepsilon^{3}.

Furthermore, for some
t_{2}=t_{2}(t,a,\varepsilon)<1
, if
\rho(w,\{z^{*}_{n}\})\geq t_{2}
and
|B(w)|\geq 1-\varepsilon^{3},

then
|B^{*}(w)|\geq 1-\varepsilon^{2}
. Therefore, if
\rho(w,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1}))\geq\max(t_{1},t_{2})
, then

|\Theta_{1}(w)|\geq 1-\varepsilon,

and, hence,
\Theta_{1}
has the SIP.
∎

6.5.
Blaschke products with zeros in a Stolz angle

In order to exhibit examples of inner functions in
\mathcal{M}
which do not have the SIP, let us consider the
very restrictive case of Blaschke products
\Theta
with zeros
\{a_{j}\}
within a Stolz angle. Then we always have

\Theta\in\mathcal{M}

[
14
, Corollary 4]
. In this particular case, it is known
[
4
, Theorem 1]
that the following properties are equivalent:

•

\Theta
is Carleson–Newman;

•

\{a_{j}\}
is a finite union of separated sequences;

•

\{a_{j}\}
is a finite union of exponential sequences.

Furthermore,
\Theta
has the WEP if and only if
\Theta
is Carleson–Newman
[
6
, (P7), p. 869]
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
\Theta
be a Blaschke product whose zeros
\{a_{j}\}
lie in a Stolz angle. Then the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\Theta
is Carleson–Newman;

(2)

\Theta
satisfies the
SIP
;

(3)

\sup\{\eta_{\Theta}(t):0<t<1\}>0
;

(4)

\Theta\in\mathcal{P}
.

The Proposition shows that any Blaschke product whose zero set is included in a Stolz angle and is not a finite union of separated sequences belongs to
\mathcal{M}
but does not satisfy the SIP. For instance we may consider the Blaschke product with zeros at the points
1-2^{-j}
with multiplicity
j
,
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow(2)
is a direct consequence of
[
4
, Theorem 1]
and Proposition
6.1
.

(2)
\Longrightarrow(3)
is straightforward since the SIP is equivalent to the relation
\lim_{t\to 1}\eta_{\Theta}(t)=1
.

(3)
\Longrightarrow(1)
: Let
0<\delta_{0}<1
be such that
\varepsilon_{0}=\eta_{\Theta}(1-\delta_{0})>0
and let
\sigma_{0}>1
,
\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, be such that

\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)\subset\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\zeta|\leq\sigma_{0}(1-|z|)\}.

Choose
\sigma=\sigma(\sigma_{0},\delta_{0})
large enough so that

|a-\zeta|\leq\sigma_{0}(1-|a|),|z-\zeta|\geq\sigma(1-|z|)\Longrightarrow\rho(z,a)\geq 1-\delta_{0}.

For any
a_{k}\in\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)
, let
z_{k}
be such that
|z_{k}|=|a_{k}|
and
|z_{k}-\zeta|=\sigma(1-|a_{k}|)
. Then we have
1-\rho(z_{k},a_{j})\geq c(1-\rho(a_{k},a_{j}))
for any
k,j,
where
c>0
is a constant only depending on
\sigma
and
\sigma_{0}
. Then

\begin{split}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(a_{k},a_{j}))&\leq c^{-1}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(z_{k},a_{j}))\\
\leq&-c^{-1}\log|\Theta(z_{k})|\leq-c^{-1}\log(1-\varepsilon_{0}).\end{split}

Hence
\Theta
is Carleson–Newman.

(4)
\Longrightarrow(1)
is obvious.

(1)
\Longrightarrow(4)
: As mentioned before, any Blaschke product whose zeros are contained in a Stolz angle is in the class
\mathcal{M}
(
[
14
, Corollary 4]
).
By definition,
\mathcal{P}
consists of the Carleson–Newman Blaschke products in the class
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Analogues for WEP

The methods and results of the previous sections allow us to shed some light

]
.
Let us say that a set is

.
Then an inner function
\Theta
is WEP if and only if
for any

such that the set

is
R
-narrow, while it is a Carleson–Newman Blaschke product if and only if
for any
R
, there exists
\varepsilon(R)
such that the larger set
\{|\Theta|<\varepsilon(R)\}

is
R
-narrow. This last condition is equivalent to being hereditarily WEP, that is to
say, that any factor of
\Theta
be WEP. This was already proved by P.Gorkin and R.Mortini in
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
using properties of the maximal ideal space of
H^{\infty}
. In this subsection we present a proof using our previous methods.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\Theta
be a Blaschke product with zeros
\{a_{j}\}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\Theta
is a Carleson–Newman Blaschke product.

(2)

There exist
r\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

D_{\rho}(a_{k},r)\not\subset\{w:|\Theta(w)|<\varepsilon\},\qquad k\geq 1.

(3)

For every
r\in(0,1)
we have

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log|\Theta(\varphi_{z}(re^{i\theta}))|d\theta>-\infty.

Here once again
\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{z-w}{1-w\overline{z}}
,
w\in\mathbb{D}
.

(4)

For every
r\in(0,1)
there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

D_{\rho}(z,r)\not\subset\{w:|\Theta(w)|<\varepsilon\},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

For a similar result see
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
. The equivalence between (1) and (2) is due to P.Gorkin and R.Mortini
[
5
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Proof.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1) :
For every
k\geq 1
, there exists
w\in D_{\rho}(a_{k},r)
such that
|\Theta(w)|\geq\varepsilon
. Then

\sum_{j}(1-\rho(a_{k},a_{j}))\leq 2\sum_{j}e^{-2d_{H}(a_{k},a_{j})}\leq 2e^{2d_{H}(a_{k},w)}\sum_{j}e^{-2d_{H}(w,a_{j})}\\
\leq\frac{4}{1-\rho(a_{k},w)}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(w,a_{j}))\leq\frac{4}{1-r}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(w,a_{j}))\\
\leq\frac{4}{1-r}\sum_{j}(-\log\rho(w,a_{j}))\leq\frac{-4\log|\Theta(w)|}{1-r}\leq\frac{4\log(1/\varepsilon)}{1-r}.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(3) :
Denote
C=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(z,a_{j}))
and let
r\in(0,1)
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\Theta(z)\not=0
, up to replacing
\Theta
by
\frac{\Theta}{(\varphi_{z})^{m}}
if
z
is a zero of
\Theta
with multiplicity
m
. Jensen’s formula gives that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}-\log|\Theta(\varphi_{z}(re^{i\theta}))|d\theta=-\log|\Theta(z)|-\sum_{a_{j}\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}\log\frac{r}{\rho(z,a_{j})}\\
=\sum_{j}-\log\rho(z,a_{j})-\sum_{a_{j}\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}\log\frac{r}{\rho(z,a_{j})}\\
=\sum_{a_{j}\not\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}-\log\rho(z,a_{j})-\sum_{a_{j}\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}\log r\\
\leq\frac{1}{r}\sum_{a_{j}\not\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}(1-\rho(z,a_{j}))-\frac{\log r}{1-r}\sum_{a_{j}\in D_{\rho}(z,r)}(1-\rho(z,a_{j}))\\
\leq\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j}(1-\rho(z,a_{j}))\leq\frac{C}{r}.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(4) :
By (3) we find
c=c(r)
such that

D_{\rho}(z,r)\not\subset\{w:|\Theta(w)|<e^{-c}\},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(4)
\Longrightarrow
(2) is trivially true.
∎

Proposition 6.13
.

An inner function
\Theta
has the
WEP
if and only if for every
r\in(0,1)
there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

(13)

D_{\rho}(z,r)\not\subset\{w:0<|\Theta(w)|<\varepsilon\},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

If
\Theta
has the WEP, then for every
r\in(0,1)
and for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, either
\mathcal{Z}(\Theta)\cap D_{\rho}(z,r)\not=\emptyset

or
|\Theta(z)|\geq\eta_{\Theta}(r)>0
, so
\varepsilon=\eta_{\Theta}(r)
satisfies (
13
).

Conversely, if
\Theta
does not have the WEP, then there exists
r>0

such that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\geq r
and
|\Theta(z)|<\varepsilon^{3}
.
On
D_{\rho}(z,r)
,
-\log|\Theta|
is a positive harmonic function,
so applying Harnack’s inequality, we obtain that for any
w\in D_{\rho}(z,\frac{r}{2})
,

-\log|\Theta(w)|\geq-\frac{r-\rho(z,w)}{r+\rho(z,w)}\log|\Theta(z)|\geq-\frac{1}{3}\log|\Theta(z)|>-\log\varepsilon.

So
D_{\rho}(z,\frac{r}{2})\subset\{w:0<|B(w)|<\varepsilon\}
which contradicts (
13
).
∎

Finally we can characterize Carleson–Newman Blaschke products by the hereditary WEP property, as proved in
[
5
, Corollary 4.6]
.

Proposition 6.14
.

Let
\Theta
be an inner function. Then
\Theta
is a Carleson–Newman Blaschke product
if and only if the factorization
\Theta=\Theta_{1}\Theta_{2}
, with inner functions
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
, implies that

\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
have the
WEP
.

Proof.

The direct implication is clear, since any factor of a Carleson–Newman Blaschke product
is again Carleson–Newman, thus has the WEP, see
[
6
]
.

Conversely, if all factors of
\Theta
have the WEP, then
\Theta
cannot have a singular factor.
Let now
\Theta
be a Blaschke product with zeros
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
, which by the hypothesis has the WEP.
Suppose that it is not Carleson–Newman. Then

\sup_{k}\sum_{j}e^{-2d_{H}(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{j})}=\infty
, but each individual sum converges.
So we can choose a sequence
(\lambda_{k_{m}})_{m}
such that
d_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\lambda_{k_{m^{\prime}}})>1
,
m\not=m^{\prime}
,
and, letting
S_{m}:=\sum_{j}e^{-2d_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\lambda_{j})}
,
we have
\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}=\infty
.

For every
z\in D_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\frac{1}{2})
, by the triangle inequality, we obtain

\log\frac{1}{|\Theta(z)|}\geq\sum_{j}e^{-2d_{H}(z,\lambda_{j})}\geq\frac{1}{e}S_{m},

so for every
z\in D_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\frac{1}{2})
, we have

d_{H}(z,\mathcal{Z}(\Theta))\leq\tanh^{-1}(\varkappa_{\Theta}\left(\exp(-S_{m}/e))\right).

In particular, the number
N_{m}
of zeros of
\Theta
in

D_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\frac{1}{2})\setminus D_{H}(\lambda_{k_{m}},\frac{1}{4})
tends to infinity.

Define now
\Theta_{1}
to be the Blaschke product with zeroes at

\{\lambda_{j}:d_{H}(\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k_{m}})\geq\frac{1}{4},\,m\geq 1\}
. We have

\rho(\lambda_{k_{m}},\mathcal{Z}(\Theta_{1}))\geq\tanh\frac{1}{4}
for
m\geq 1
, but

|\Theta_{1}(\lambda_{k_{m}})|\leq\exp(-N_{m}\tanh\frac{1}{2})\to 0
, which shows that
\Theta_{1}
does not have the WEP.
∎

6.7.
\eta_{\Theta}
could be discontinuous

Here we give a simple example of a finite Blaschke product
B
with discontinuous
\eta_{B}
.

Example 6.15
.

Let
r\in(0,1)
be sufficiently small, let
\lambda_{k}=re^{i\pi(2k+1)/4}
,
0\leq k\leq 3
, and let

B=\prod_{0\leq k\leq 3}b_{\lambda_{k}}.

Then
\eta_{B}
is not continuous.

Proof.

We have

B(z)=\frac{z^{4}+r^{4}}{1+r^{4}z^{4}},\qquad B(0)=r^{4},\qquad|B(z)|\geq\frac{|z|^{4}-r^{4}}{1-r^{4}|z|^{4}}.

Let
\Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(B))>r\}
. Then
\Omega\cap(0,1)=(s,1)
, with

\Bigl|\frac{s-re^{i\pi/4}}{1-sre^{-i\pi/4}}\Bigr|=r,

that is

s=\frac{\sqrt{2}r}{1+r^{2}}.

Therefore,

\Omega\subset\Bigl\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z|>\frac{\sqrt{2}r}{1+r^{2}}\Bigr\},

and

\inf_{z\in\Omega}|B(z)|\geq\frac{\frac{4r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{4}}-r^{4}}{1-r^{4}\frac{4r^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{4}}}=r^{4}\frac{4-(1+r^{2})^{4}}{(1+r^{2})^{4}-4r^{8}}.

Thus, for sufficiently small
r>0
we have

\lim_{s\to r+0}\eta_{B}(s)\geq 2r^{4}.

Since

\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho(z,\mathcal{Z}(B))\geq r\}=(\overline{\Omega}\cap\mathbb{D})\cup\{0\},

we obtain that
\eta_{B}(r)=r^{4}
and, thus,
\eta_{B}
is not continuous at the point
r
.
∎

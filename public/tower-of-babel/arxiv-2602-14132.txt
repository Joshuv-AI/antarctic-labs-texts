A Poisson–Poincaré–Dulac for Poisson Connections
Maurício Corrêa, Miguel Rodríguez Peña

Abstract
. We study Poisson-flat connections with logarithmic poles along a simple normal crossings divisor on a holomorphic Poisson manifold, where flatness is required only along the symplectic foliation. After identifying the relevant logarithmic cotangent Poisson Lie algebroid, we define an Euler–Poisson principal part and a residue theory adapted to the canonical logarithmic Hamiltonian generators. Under a precise nonresonance hypothesis, we establish a Poisson Poincaré–Dulac theorem: any logarithmic Poisson-flat connection with prescribed principal part is holomorphically gauge equivalent to a pure Euler–Poisson normal form with constant commuting residues, and this normal form is unique up to Casimir-valued gauge transformations lying in the common centralizer of the residues. To encode both leafwise transport and boundary winding, we construct a twisted leafwise fundamental groupoid via the real oriented blow-up and a 2-pushout that adjoins canonical tangential meridians. In the nonresonant regime, the normal form yields a meridional character determined by the residues, and hence a logarithmic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence in the Poisson setting for this groupoid. Finally, we illustrate the theory with rank-two Poisson modules (Poisson triples) and an explicit family of examples.



1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a holomorphic Poisson manifold. On the Poisson regular locus
X^{\circ}
the anchor

\sigma^{\#}:\Omega^{1}_{X^{\circ}}\to T_{X^{\circ}}
has constant rank, and its image

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{Im}(\sigma^{\#})
defines an integrable holomorphic distribution whose leaves
carry holomorphic symplectic forms. Holomorphic Poisson geometry furnishes a natural framework for
integrability and deformation problems, and for algebro-geometric and representation-theoretic
structures such as Poisson modules; see, for instance,
[
Pol97
,
LGV13
,
Vai94
]
. From a complementary perspective, Poisson structures lie at the heart of deformation quantization
and its geometric realizations
[
Kon03
,
CF00
]
, and they also arise prominently in mathematical
physics, for example in the appearance of noncommutative geometries induced by
B
-fields in string
theory
[
SW99
]
; see also Hitchin
[
Hit11
]
for the
B
-field action in the theory of
generalized holomorphic bundles.

In this work we study logarithmic Poisson-flat connections with poles along a simple normal
crossings divisor
D\subset X
. After a choice of local frame, such a connection may be represented
by a matrix
\Theta
whose entries are logarithmic Hamiltonian vector fields tangent to
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
,
and Poisson-flatness is expressed by the Maurer–Cartan (zero-curvature) equation in the Poisson
direction. The guiding local analytic question is the Poisson counterpart of the regular-singular
normalization problem:
for a fixed logarithmic principal part, can one eliminate higher-order terms
by holomorphic gauge transformations, and to what extent is the resulting normal form unique?

For ordinary logarithmic flat connections this circle of questions is governed by the
Poincaré–Dulac philosophy: under suitable non-resonance assumptions one solves the
relevant homological equations order-by-order and proves convergence, leading to holomorphic gauge
normal forms together with a residue and monodromy calculus; see Deligne
[
Del70
]
and the
gauge-oriented account of Novikov–Yakovenko
[
NY04
]
. A closely related perspective regards logarithmic connections as representation-theoretic data for Lie groupoids integrating the logarithmic tangent Lie algebroid
T_{X}(-\log D)
; see
[
Bis24
]
for the groupoid-theoretic framework and
[
Bis25
]
for the corresponding normal form theory and the construction of moduli stacks. We also keep in mind the general background on Lie groupoids,
foliations, and their relation to Lie algebroids
[
MM03
]
, as well as stack-theoretic foundations
relevant to groupoid moduli
[
Noohi
]
. In the irregular setting, Stokes-type groupoids play an
analogous role; see
[
GLP18
]
and the systematic exposition in
[
Sab13
]
.

Because our local models are formulated near boundary strata of a simple normal crossings divisor,
we work naturally with the real oriented blow-up and its toroidal collar charts, as surveyed in

[
PP25
]
. Related logarithmic topological constructions, and the associated log Betti
viewpoint, are developed in the Kato–Nakayama framework
[
KN99
]
, which provides a useful
conceptual backdrop for the appearance of angular variables and boundary strata.

The Poisson-logarithmic setting is technically distinct from the ordinary one. The flatness
directions are not those of
T_{X}(-\log D)
, but rather those dictated by the symplectic foliation

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
, whose rank equals the Poisson rank and may vary near the degeneracy locus of
\sigma
.
Accordingly, even locally one cannot simply reduce to the usual logarithmic connection theory: the
homological equations must be solved along
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
, and one must keep explicit control of the
logarithmic Hamiltonian generators arising from
\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
in simple normal crossings
charts. Moreover, at each step one must separate the genuinely Poisson-direction contributions from
the ambient logarithmic geometry of the pair
(X,D)
, in the sense of Saito’s logarithmic forms and
vector fields
[
Sai80
]
. This separation is also the conceptual source of a gauge ambiguity
specific to the Poisson context: Casimir functions are constant along
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
and thus behave
as leafwise constants, leaving a residual freedom given by multiplication by diagonal Casimir units
in normal form statements. By contrast, on a holomorphic symplectic open set one has

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}=T_{X}
, and the Poisson-direction normalization becomes compatible with the classical
regular-singular picture.

Our principal theorem establishes a Poisson Poincaré–Dulac normal form for logarithmic
Poisson-flat connections with prescribed principal part (
Theorem

5.4
). We fix a Poisson-flat logarithmic model
with commuting residue data and impose a non-resonance condition formulated in terms of the
canonical simple-normal-crossings Hamiltonian generators. Under these hypotheses, any logarithmic
Poisson-flat connection with the same principal part is holomorphically gauge equivalent to the
model, and the resulting normal form is unique up to multiplication by diagonal Casimir units (
Theorem

5.6
). This
yields a rigid local classification of Poisson-flat connection data compatible with
\sigma
near
an SNC divisor, in the regime where the symplectic foliation has smaller (and possibly varying)
rank.

A complementary theme concerns the representation-theoretic encoding of monodromy in the presence
of boundary. The Poisson direction of flatness is intrinsically leafwise, whereas the meridional
topology created by
D
is not, in general, visible within individual symplectic leaves. In order
to record simultaneously leafwise transport and boundary winding, we study a tangentially twisted
leafwise groupoid associated with
(\mathcal{F}_{\sigma},D)
, constructed by gluing the interior leafwise
fundamental groupoid to a canonical meridional isotropy along the collar of the real oriented
blow-up
[
PP25
]
. A key technical input on the collar is a leafwise log Poincaré lemma
which separates a closed leafwise
1
-form into an exact part plus meridional periods (Lemma
5.3
).
In the non-resonant regime, the Poisson Poincaré–Dulac normal form
produces commuting residue matrices, and we show that the leafwise monodromy representation extends
to the twisted groupoid with an explicit meridional character determined by these residues. This
provides a Poisson analogue of the groupoid Riemann–Hilbert philosophy for logarithmic and Stokes
phenomena
[
GLP18
,
Sab13
]
, adapted to the fact that flatness is imposed only along the
symplectic foliation, and compatible with the groupoid viewpoint on logarithmic connections

[
Bis24
,
Bis25
]
. Finally, restriction along the interior inclusion induces an equivalence of
representation categories, expressing Morita invariance for the twisted construction (Corollary
5.13
).

We then apply the normal form and the groupoid description to meromorphic Poisson modules and to
Poisson triples tangent to
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
, obtaining explicit local models and sharp resonance
constraints. These applications dovetail with algebro-geometric aspects of Poisson modules,
including Morita-theoretic features
[
Cor20
]
, and with the geometry of projective Poisson
varieties
[
Pym18
]
. Throughout, the classical residue calculus for ordinary logarithmic
connections, and its variants, serves as a guiding analogy and a source of useful invariants

[
Oht82
]
.

Section
6
treats the rank-two case in a concrete form tailored to the normal-form and monodromy questions addressed above. We fix conventions for the Poisson (Lichnerowicz) differential and recall the basic notions of Hamiltonian vector fields, Casimirs, and Poisson vector fields, together with the interpretation of the corresponding low-degree Poisson cohomology groups. We then adopt a working definition of a meromorphic Poisson module as a vector
bundle equipped with a Poisson-direction connection with poles along a divisor, and we explain how,
after choosing a local trivialization in rank two, the data become an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-valued
connection matrix whose Poisson-flatness is equivalent to an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
Maurer–Cartan system,
in line with the Lie-algebroid description of Poisson modules
[
Pol97
,
LGV13
]
. The section
also presents an explicit projective family of examples in the diagonal quadratic component

L(1,1,1,1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, producing Poisson triples and trace-free rank-two Poisson
connections to which the Poisson Poincaré–Dulac normalization applies, thereby connecting the
abstract normal-form theory to concrete projective Poisson geometries
[
Pym18
]
and to
Morita-theoretic features of Poisson modules
[
Cor20
]
.

2.
Geometric and Poisson background

In this section we fix notation and record the basic holomorphic Poisson geometry used throughout.
We also state standing hypotheses on
(X,\sigma,D)
ensuring that all objects below are well-defined
and admit clean local models in codimension one (in particular, along the SNC divisor
D
).

2.1.
Holomorphic Poisson manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

A
holomorphic Poisson manifold
is a complex manifold
X
equipped with a holomorphic bivector field

\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\wedge^{2}T_{X}\big)

such that the induced bracket on holomorphic functions

\{f,g\}_{\sigma}:=\sigma(df,dg)\qquad(f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{X})

satisfies the Jacobi identity. Equivalently, the Schouten bracket satisfies
[\sigma,\sigma]=0
.

The bivector
\sigma
canonically equips the holomorphic cotangent bundle with a Lie algebroid structure.
Let
\sigma^{\#}:T_{X}^{*}\to T_{X}
be the holomorphic bundle map

\sigma^{\#}(\alpha):=\sigma(\alpha,\cdot).

For holomorphic
1
-forms
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}
, the
Koszul bracket
is

[\alpha,\beta]_{\sigma}\;:=\;\mathcal{L}_{\sigma^{\#}(\alpha)}\beta\;-\;\mathcal{L}_{\sigma^{\#}(\beta)}\alpha\;-\;d\big(\sigma(\alpha,\beta)\big).

With this bracket and anchor,
\Omega_{X}^{1}
is the sheaf of sections of a holomorphic Lie algebroid,
which we denote by
A_{\sigma}
.
For a holomorphic function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the associated
Hamiltonian vector field
is

X_{f}:=\sigma^{\#}(df)\in T_{X},

so that
X_{f}(g)=\{f,g\}_{\sigma}
for all
g\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
The image sheaf

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{Im}(\sigma^{\#})\subset T_{X}

is an (in general singular) involutive holomorphic distribution; its integral manifolds are the
(possibly singular)
symplectic leaves
of
\sigma
. On a smooth leaf
L
, the restriction of
\sigma

is nondegenerate and inverts a holomorphic symplectic
2
-form
\omega_{L}
on
L
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X^{\circ}\subset X
be the
Poisson-regular locus
, i.e. the largest open subset on which

\sigma^{\#}
has locally constant rank. On
X^{\circ}
the distribution
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
is a regular
holomorphic foliation.

If
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(\sigma|_{X^{\circ}})=2m
, then every leaf in
X^{\circ}
has complex dimension
2m
,
and carries a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{L}
.

2.2.
Divisors and logarithmic geometry

Let
D\subset X
be a divisor with simple normal crossings.

Definition 2.3
.

A divisor
D\subset X
has
simple normal crossings
(SNC) if for every point
p\in D
there exist
local holomorphic coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
centered at
p
such that

D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}

for some
1\leq r\leq n
, and each local branch
\{z_{i}=0\}
is smooth.

Write
D=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}
for the decomposition into irreducible components.

Definition 2.4
.

The
logarithmic tangent sheaf

T_{X}(-\log D)
is the subsheaf of
T_{X}
consisting of holomorphic vector
fields tangent to
D
(equivalently, preserving the ideal sheaf of
D
). Concretely, in an SNC chart

(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
with
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
one has

T_{X}(-\log D)=\big\langle z_{1}\partial_{z_{1}},\,\dots,\,z_{r}\partial_{z_{r}},\,\partial_{z_{r+1}},\,\dots,\,\partial_{z_{n}}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}.

Dually, the
logarithmic cotangent sheaf

\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
is locally generated by

\frac{dz_{1}}{z_{1}},\,\dots,\,\frac{dz_{r}}{z_{r}},\,dz_{r+1},\,\dots,\,dz_{n}.

When
D
is SNC, both
T_{X}(-\log D)
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
are locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules of rank
\dim X
.

2.3.
Standing hypotheses

Throughout the main parts of the paper we impose the following assumptions.

(H1)

D=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}D_{i}\subset X
is an SNC divisor with smooth irreducible components
D_{i}
.

(H2)

The Poisson bivector
\sigma
has constant rank in codimension one. Let
2m
be the generic
complex rank of
\sigma^{\#}
on
X
. We assume that the degeneracy locus

\Dgn(\sigma):=\{x\in X:\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{C}}(\sigma_{x})<2m\}

has complex codimension at least
2
. Equivalently,
\sigma^{m}\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2m}T_{X})
vanishes in codimension at least
2
.

(H3)

Each component
D_{i}
is a
Poisson hypersurface
, i.e. its ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}

is a Poisson ideal:

\{\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}},\mathcal{O}_{X}\}_{\sigma}\subset\mathcal{I}_{D_{i}}.

Equivalently,
\sigma
is logarithmically tangent to
D
in the sense that

\sigma^{\#}\big(\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\big)\subset T_{X}(-\log D),

or, in local SNC coordinates, Hamiltonian flows preserve each branch
\{z_{i}=0\}
of
D
.

Remark 2.5
.

We stress that we do
not
assume that
(X,\sigma)
is generically symplectic (i.e.
2m=\dim X
), nor that

D
is a degeneracy divisor for
\sigma
. Hypothesis (H2) is only a codimension-one regularity condition
on the rank of
\sigma
, while (H3) is the geometric condition that
D
is preserved by Hamiltonian flows.

2.4.
Local SNC charts and logarithmic Hamiltonians

Fix an SNC chart
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
with
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. For
1\leq i\leq r
, consider the logarithmic form

dz_{i}/z_{i}\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.
Assume (H3). In this chart the vector fields

X_{\log z_{i}}\ :=\ \sigma^{\#}\left(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\right)\qquad(1\leq i\leq r)

extend holomorphically across
D
and belong to
T_{X}(-\log D)
. For
i>r
, the Hamiltonian vector fields

X_{z_{i}}=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i})
are holomorphic (with no logarithmic behavior along
D
).
In fact, fix
1\leq i\leq r
and set
f=z_{i}
, so
D_{i}=\{f=0\}
locally. By (H3), the ideal
(f)
is Poisson, hence
for any holomorphic function
g
there exists
h\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
such that

\{f,g\}_{\sigma}=fh.

On the other hand,

\sigma^{\#}\left(\frac{df}{f}\right)(g)=\sigma\left(\frac{df}{f},dg\right)=\frac{1}{f}\{f,g\}_{\sigma}=h,

which is holomorphic. Therefore
\sigma^{\#}(df/f)
defines a holomorphic vector field on the chart.
Moreover, since
(f)
is preserved, this vector field is tangent to
D_{i}
, and since this holds for each local branch,
it is tangent to
D
; hence it lies in
T_{X}(-\log D)
.
For
i>r
there is no pole to begin with, so
\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i})
is holomorphic.

From now on, whenever we choose local coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
with
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
, we implicitly
work in an SNC chart and use the logarithmic Hamiltonians
X_{\log z_{i}}=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
along the components of
D
.

3.
Logarithmic Poisson connections

Lemma 3.1

(Logarithmic cotangent is a Lie subalgebroid)
.

Assume
(H3)
. Then the Koszul bracket
[-,-]_{\sigma}
preserves logarithmic forms:

[\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D),\,\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)]_{\sigma}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D).

Equivalently,
A_{\sigma}(\log D):=\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
is a holomorphic Lie algebroid with anchor

\rho=\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to T_{X}(-\log D)
.

Proof.

Work in an SNC chart
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
with
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
.
It suffices to check the generators
dz_{i}/z_{i}
(
i\leq r
) and
dz_{j}
(
j>r
).
By
2.4
, the vector fields
X_{i}:=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
lie in
T_{X}(-\log D)
.
Hence
\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}}
preserves
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.
Now, compute the Koszul bracket

[\alpha,\beta]_{\sigma}=\mathcal{L}_{\sigma^{\#}(\alpha)}\beta-\mathcal{L}_{\sigma^{\#}(\beta)}\alpha-d(\sigma(\alpha,\beta)).

The first two Lie-derivative terms are logarithmic because
\sigma^{\#}(\alpha)
and
\sigma^{\#}(\beta)
are logarithmic
vector fields and Lie derivatives by logarithmic vector fields preserve
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.
For the last term,
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
is a holomorphic function on the chart (since
\sigma^{\#}(\Omega^{1}(\log D))

is holomorphic and
\alpha,\beta
have at most logarithmic poles), so
d(\sigma(\alpha,\beta))
is holomorphic, hence
in
\Omega_{X}^{1}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.
Therefore
[\alpha,\beta]_{\sigma}
is logarithmic.
∎

We now define the central object of study: a
representation
of the logarithmic cotangent
Poisson Lie algebroid. Concretely, we consider

A_{\sigma}(\log D):=\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D),

equipped with the Koszul bracket
[-,-]_{\sigma}
and the anchor map

\rho:=\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\longrightarrow T_{X}(-\log D),

which lands in
T_{X}(-\log D)
by hypothesis (H3).
We set

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}:=\mathrm{Im}\big(\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to T_{X}(-\log D)\big)\ \subset\ T_{X}(-\log D).

Over
X\setminus D
one has
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)=\Omega_{X}^{1}
, hence
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}=\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
there.

We write
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}^{\log}(U):=H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log})
for the holomorphic logarithmic Hamiltonian
vector fields on
U
, and
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(U):=H^{0}(U\setminus D,\mathcal{F}_{\sigma})
on
U\setminus D
.

3.1.
Definition and basic properties

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
e
, and write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)
for its sheaf of holomorphic sections.
Let
d_{A_{\sigma}}
denote the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential of the Lie algebroid
A_{\sigma}(\log D)
.
Since
A_{\sigma}(\log D)^{\vee}\simeq T_{X}(-\log D)
, the Lie algebroid cochain complex is

H^{0}\big(X,\wedge^{\bullet}A_{\sigma}(\log D)^{\vee}\big)\;=\;H^{0}\big(X,\wedge^{\bullet}T_{X}(-\log D)\big).

In particular, on functions
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
we obtain a section
d_{A_{\sigma}}f\in H^{0}\big(X,T_{X}(-\log D)\big)

characterized by

\alpha\big(d_{A_{\sigma}}f\big)=\rho(\alpha)(f)\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D).

Equivalently, if we set
X_{f}:=\sigma^{\#}(df)
(the Hamiltonian vector field), then

(1)

d_{A_{\sigma}}f\;=\;X_{f}\;=\;\sigma^{\#}(df).

To simplify notation, we write
\delta:=d_{A_{\sigma}}

We write
\delta=[\sigma,-]
for the Poisson/Lichnerowicz differential on polyvector fields, and we also use
\delta

for the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential of the cotangent Poisson Lie algebroid

A_{\sigma}(\log D)=\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
under the standard identification

A_{\sigma}(\log D)^{\vee}\simeq T_{X}(-\log D)
.
In particular, for
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
we fix the convention

(2)

\delta(f):=\sigma^{\#}(df)=:X_{f}\in T_{X}(-\log D).

When
\delta
is applied to a matrix of functions, it is understood entrywise.

Definition 3.2

(Logarithmic Poisson connection)
.

A
logarithmic Poisson connection tangent to the symplectic foliation
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear morphism of sheaves

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}:\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\ \subset\ T_{X}(-\log D)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\;\simeq\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\big(\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D),\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\big)

satisfying the Leibniz rule

(3)

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(fs)\;=\;\delta(f)\otimes s\;+\;f\,\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s)

for all local holomorphic functions
f
and local holomorphic sections
s
of
E
.

Given
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
one may define a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear bracket

\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\times\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(E),\qquad\{f,s\}:=\big(\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s)\big)(df),

i.e. contraction of the
T_{X}(-\log D)
-valued section
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s)
with the
1
-form
df\in\Omega_{X}^{1}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
.
This bracket determines
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
because
\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)
is locally generated by exact forms and logarithmic differentials in SNC charts.
Flatness of
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is equivalent to the Jacobi-type identity

\{\{f,g\},s\}=\{f,\{g,s\}\}-\{g,\{f,s\}\}.

Recall that an ordinary logarithmic connection is a morphism

\nabla^{\log}:E\to\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\otimes E

satisfying
\nabla^{\log}(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla^{\log}(s)
.
A logarithmic Poisson connection is
not
obtained from
\nabla^{\log}
in general, because its Leibniz rule is governed by
the Poisson derivation
\delta(f)=\sigma^{\#}(df)
rather than
df
.
However, on any open set where the logarithmic anchor

\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D)\to T_{X}(-\log D)
is an isomorphism (e.g. in the log-symplectic/reduced-degeneracy case),
there is a one-to-one correspondence

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}\longleftrightarrow\nabla^{\log}:=(\sigma^{\#})^{-1}\circ\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}},

and flatness is preserved under this identification. If
D
is not reduced, the same mechanism yields a

meromorphic
flat connection with poles along
D
.

Now, fix an open set
U\subset X
over which
E
admits a holomorphic frame
e=(e_{1},\dots,e_{e})
.
Write

(4)

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(e_{a})\;=\;\sum_{b=1}^{e}\Theta_{ba}\otimes e_{b},

where each
\Theta_{ba}
is a local section of
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}|_{U}
.
Equivalently,
\Theta=(\Theta_{ba})
is an
e\times e
matrix with entries in
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}
,
and

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(e)=\Theta\cdot e.

For a general section
s=\sum_{a}Y^{a}e_{a}
(with coefficient column vector
Y=(Y^{1},\dots,Y^{e})^{T}
),
the Leibniz rule (
3
) gives

(5)

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s)=\big(\delta Y+\Theta\,Y\big)\otimes e,

where
\delta Y
denotes the column vector whose
a
-th entry is
\delta(Y^{a})\in T_{X}(-\log D)
.
Thus the horizontality condition
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s)=0
is equivalently

(6)

\delta Y\;=\;-\,\Theta\,Y,

an equality in
T_{X}(-\log D)^{\oplus e}
.

Remark 3.3

(Gauge transformation)
.

Under a change of frame
e^{\prime}=g\,e
with
g\in GL_{e}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
, the connection matrix transforms as

\Theta^{\prime}=(\delta g)\,g^{-1}+g\,\Theta\,g^{-1},

where
\delta g
is obtained by applying
\delta
entrywise to the matrix
g
.

View
\Theta
as an element of

H^{0}\big(U,A_{\sigma}(\log D)^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E)\big)=H^{0}\big(U,T_{X}(-\log D)\otimes\mathrm{End}(E)\big),

i.e. an
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued
1
-cochain in the Lie algebroid complex of
A_{\sigma}(\log D)
.
The differential
\delta
extends to
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued cochains by the usual Leibniz rule.
Define the wedge–composition product by

(\Theta\wedge\Theta)(\alpha,\beta):=\Theta(\alpha)\circ\Theta(\beta)-\Theta(\beta)\circ\Theta(\alpha),\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log D).

Definition 3.4

(Poisson curvature / flatness)
.

The
Poisson curvature
of
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
in the trivialization
e
is the
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued
2
-cochain

(7)

\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{Pois}}\;:=\;\delta\Theta\;+\;\Theta\wedge\Theta\;\in\;H^{0}\big(U,\wedge^{2}T_{X}(-\log D)\otimes\mathrm{End}(E)\big).

We say that
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is
Poisson-flat
(or simply
flat
) if

(8)

\delta\Theta+\Theta\wedge\Theta\;=\;0.

Remark 3.5

(Maurer–Cartan form)
.

Equation (
8
) is the Maurer–Cartan equation in the differential graded algebra

\big(H^{0}(U,\wedge^{\bullet}A_{\sigma}(\log D)^{\vee})\otimes\mathrm{End}(E),\,\delta,\,\wedge\big)
.
The Jacobi identity for the Koszul bracket (equivalently
[\sigma,\sigma]=0
) is what ensures
\delta^{2}=0

and hence makes (
7
) intrinsic and gauge-invariant.

4.
Residues and Euler–Poisson principal parts

We work near a point
p\in D\cap X^{\circ}
and choose an adapted SNC chart

U\ni p
with holomorphic coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
such that

D\cap U=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
and
D_{i}\cap U=\{z_{i}=0\}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
set

X_{i}\ :=\ \sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big)\ \in\ H^{0}\big(U,\,T_{X}(-\log D)\big).

By
2.4
these are holomorphic logarithmic vector fields.
Assume (H3). For each
1\leq i\leq r
, the vector field

X_{i}=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
is a Poisson vector field, i.e.

(9)

X_{i}\big|_{V}\ =\ \sigma^{\#}\big(d(\log z_{i})\big)\ =\ \delta(\log z_{i}).

4.1.
Euler–Poisson principal parts and residues

Definition 4.1

(Euler–Poisson principal part and residue)
.

Fix commuting constant matrices
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}\in\Mat_{e\times e}(\mathbb{C})
.
The associated
Euler–Poisson principal part
on
U
is the
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued Poisson connection matrix

\Theta_{0}\ :=\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\,X_{i}\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\,\sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big).

We define the
residue of
\Theta_{0}
along
D_{i}
(at
p
)
to be

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{D_{i},p}(\Theta_{0})\ :=\ A_{i}.

Proposition 4.2

(Flatness of Euler–Poisson principal parts)
.

With
\Theta_{0}
as in Definition
4.1
, one has

\delta\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}\ =\ 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad[A_{i},A_{j}]=0\ \text{ for all }i,j.

In particular, if the residue tuple
(A_{1},\dots,A_{r})
commutes, then
\Theta_{0}
is Poisson-flat.

Proof.

Since each
X_{i}
is Poisson (
9
) and each
A_{i}
is constant,
we have
\delta\Theta_{0}=0
. Moreover,

\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}[A_{i},A_{j}]\;X_{i}\wedge X_{j}.

Thus
\delta\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=0
if and only if
[A_{i},A_{j}]=0
for all
i,j
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The residue is the coefficient of the logarithmic Hamiltonians
X_{i}=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
in the Euler–Poisson principal part

\Theta_{0}
, exactly as in the principal form used in the Poisson–Poincaré–Dulac analysis.
Leafwise monodromy appears later only as a consequence once the system is in Euler–Poisson gauge.

4.2.
Leafwise meridians and monodromy

Shrink
U
so that
U\subset X^{\circ}
and set
U^{*}:=U\setminus D
.
Assume
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is Poisson-flat on
U^{*}
and that we have chosen an Euler–Poisson gauge on
U
,
so that its matrix is
\Theta_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}X_{i}
with commuting constant residues.
Fix
x\in U^{*}
and let
L_{x}
be the symplectic leaf through
x
.
On
L_{x}\cap U^{*}
the Poisson connection induces an ordinary flat logarithmic connection, and for a small meridian

\gamma_{i}
around
D_{i}\cap L_{x}
one has the classical formula

\rho^{\mathrm{mon}}_{L_{x},x}([\gamma_{i}])\ =\ \exp\big(-2\pi i\,A_{i}\big),

with the sign matching the convention used in the transport equation.
This shows that, in Euler–Poisson gauge, meridional monodromy is determined by the residue tuple.

5.
Non-resonance and the Poisson–Poincaré–Dulac normal form

In this section we work locally near
p\in D\cap X^{\circ}
and restrict to the
non-resonant regime
.
Under this hypothesis we prove that the holomorphic remainder in the logarithmic expansion can be removed by a
holomorphic gauge change.

Fix an adapted chart
U\ni p
and a holomorphic frame
e
.
Let
\Theta
be the local matrix of
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
on
U
.
Choose a local splitting

(10)

\Theta\;=\;\Theta_{0}\;+\;V,\qquad\Theta_{0}:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\circ}\,\sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big),

where
V\in\Mat_{e\times e}(\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\log}(U))
is holomorphic on
U
(no logarithmic poles), and the constant matrices

A_{i}^{\circ}\in\Mat_{e\times e}(\mathbb{C})
form a commuting residue tuple, so that
\Theta_{0}
is Poisson-flat by
Proposition
4.2
.

Notation 5.1

(Logarithmic Hamiltonian generators in the PPD chart)
.

In the adapted SNC chart
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
with
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
we set

X_{i}\;:=\;\sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big)\in H^{0}(U,T_{X}(-\log D)),\qquad(1\leq i\leq r).

We will never use the typographically ambiguous symbol “
\delta z_{i}/z_{i}
”: whenever weights appear,
they always refer to the vector fields
X_{i}
. In particular, for any multi-index

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}
one has the identity

(11)

\delta(z^{\alpha})\;=\;X_{z^{\alpha}}\;=\;\sigma^{\#}(d(z^{\alpha}))\;=\;z^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\,X_{i},\qquad z^{\alpha}:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

5.1.
Joint generalized eigenspaces and non-resonance

Fix a decomposition of
\mathbb{C}^{e}
into joint generalized eigenspaces for the commuting tuple

(A_{1}^{\circ},\dots,A_{r}^{\circ})
:

\mathbb{C}^{e}=\bigoplus_{\kappa\in\mathcal{I}}E_{\kappa},

so that on
E_{\kappa}
each
A_{i}^{\circ}
has a single eigenvalue
\lambda_{i,\kappa}\in\mathbb{C}
on the diagonal.
Set
\lambda_{\kappa}:=(\lambda_{1,\kappa},\dots,\lambda_{r,\kappa})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
.

Definition 5.2

(Non-resonance at
p
)
.

We say that
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is
non-resonant at

if for every pair
\kappa\neq\kappa^{\prime}
one has

\lambda_{\kappa}-\lambda_{\kappa^{\prime}}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{r}\setminus\{0\}.

5.2.
Poisson–Poincaré–Dulac elimination in the non-resonant case

Under a holomorphic gauge
e\mapsto\widetilde{e}=He
, the matrix transforms as

\widetilde{\Theta}=(\delta H)H^{-1}+H\Theta H^{-1}.

We want to choose
H
so that
\widetilde{\Theta}
has no holomorphic remainder, i.e.
\widetilde{\Theta}=\widetilde{\Theta}_{0}

is of pure logarithmic type with constant commuting residues.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
U
be a local collar chart of the oriented real blow-up with corners,

U\;\simeq\;[0,\varepsilon)^{r}\times(S^{1})^{r}\times B,

with angular coordinates
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r}
on
(S^{1})^{r}
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a smooth foliation on
U
such that, after shrinking
U
, each leaf
L
is diffeomorphic to

L\;\simeq\;(S^{1})^{r}\times\mathbf{D}^{q}

and the angular variables
\theta_{i}
restrict to the standard circle factors on
L
.
Let
\alpha
be a smooth leafwise
1
-form on
U
(i.e. a section of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}
) such that it is leafwise closed:

d_{\mathcal{F}}\alpha\;=\;0.

Then, after shrinking
U
if necessary, there exist:

•

a smooth function
f
on
U
(defined leafwise, i.e. unique up to addition of a locally constant function along leaves), and

•

smooth functions
c_{1},\dots,c_{r}
on
U
which are constant along leaves (i.e.
d_{\mathcal{F}}c_{i}=0
),

such that

\alpha\;=\;d_{\mathcal{F}}f\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\,d\theta_{i}\qquad\text{in }\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(U).

Moreover, for each
i
and each leaf
L
, the coefficient
c_{i}|_{L}
is determined by the period of
\alpha|_{L}
around the
i
-th meridian:

c_{i}|_{L}\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha|_{L},

where
\gamma_{i}\subset L
is the loop
\theta_{i}:0\to 2\pi
with the other
\theta_{j}
and the disk coordinates fixed.
In particular,
\alpha
is leafwise exact on
U
(i.e.
\alpha=d_{\mathcal{F}}f
) if and only if all meridional periods vanish, equivalently
c_{i}=0
for all
i
.

Proof.

Shrink
U
so that every leaf
L
meets
U
in a product chart

L\cap U\;\simeq\;(S^{1})^{r}\times\mathbf{D}^{q},

with the
\theta_{i}
the standard angular coordinates on the circle factors.
Fix such a leaf
L
.
For each
i=1,\dots,r
, let
\gamma_{i}\subset L
be the loop that winds once in the

i
-th circle factor (
\theta_{i}:0\to 2\pi
) keeping all other variables fixed.
Define

c_{i}(L)\;:=\;\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha|_{L}.

Since
d_{\mathcal{F}}\alpha=0
, the restriction
\alpha|_{L}
is a closed
1
-form on
L
,
so each period
\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha|_{L}
depends only on the homology class of

\gamma_{i}
in
H_{1}(L,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, if we choose another loop in the same

i
-th circle factor, we obtain the same number.
Now view
c_{i}
as a function on
U
that is constant along leaves by setting

c_{i}(x):=c_{i}(L_{x})
, where
L_{x}
is the leaf through
x\in U
.
This is well-defined and satisfies
d_{\mathcal{F}}c_{i}=0
because
c_{i}
only depends on the leaf.,
On the same leaf
L
, consider the leafwise
1
-form

\beta\;:=\;\alpha\;-\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\,d\theta_{i}.

It is leafwise closed because
d_{\mathcal{F}}\alpha=0
,
d_{\mathcal{F}}c_{i}=0
and
d_{\mathcal{F}}(d\theta_{i})=0
.
Moreover, by construction it has zero periods on each
\gamma_{i}
:

\int_{\gamma_{i}}\beta|_{L}\;=\;\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha|_{L}\;-\;\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{j}\int_{\gamma_{i}}d\theta_{j}\;=\;\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha|_{L}\;-\;c_{i}\cdot 2\pi\;=\;0.

Since
\mathbf{D}^{q}
is contractible, the Künneth description of
H^{1}(L;\mathbb{R})
gives

H^{1}\big((S^{1})^{r}\times\mathbf{D}^{q};\mathbb{R}\big)\;\cong\;H^{1}\big((S^{1})^{r};\mathbb{R}\big)\;\cong\;\mathbb{R}^{r},

with basis represented by the cohomology classes of
d\theta_{1},\dots,d\theta_{r}
.
Equivalently, a closed
1
-form on
L
is exact if and only if all its periods on the
standard generators
\gamma_{i}
vanish.
We have just shown that
\beta|_{L}
is closed and has vanishing periods on every
\gamma_{i}
.
Hence
[\beta|_{L}]=0
in
H^{1}(L;\mathbb{R})
, so there exists a smooth function

f_{L}
on
L\cap U
such that

\beta|_{L}\;=\;d(f_{L}).

Writing this leafwise, we have
\beta=d_{\mathcal{F}}f
for a leafwise-defined function
f
on
U

(unique up to addition of a locally constant function along leaves).
Therefore on
U
we obtain

\alpha\;=\;d_{\mathcal{F}}f\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\,d\theta_{i},

as claimed.
If all meridional periods vanish, then all
c_{i}=0
, hence
\alpha=d_{\mathcal{F}}f
is leafwise exact.
Conversely, if
\alpha=d_{\mathcal{F}}f
is leafwise exact, then every period on
\gamma_{i}
vanishes,
hence
c_{i}=0
for all
i
.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Poisson–Poincaré–Dulac normal form (non-resonant case))
.

Let
p\in D\cap X^{\circ}
. Assume
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is Poisson-flat (
8
) and non-resonant at
p

in the sense of
5.2
. Then, after shrinking
U
around
p
, there exists a holomorphic gauge

H\in\mathrm{GL}_{e}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
such that

(12)

\widetilde{\Theta}=(\delta H)H^{-1}+H\Theta H^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\widetilde{A}_{i}\,\sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big),

where
\widetilde{A}_{i}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{e\times e}(\mathbb{C})
are constant commuting matrices and each
\widetilde{A}_{i}
is
conjugate to
A_{i}^{\circ}
.

Proof.

We work on an adapted polydisc
U
with coordinates and
D\cap U=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
.
Write the logarithmic Poisson connection matrix as

\Theta\;=\;\Theta_{0}\;+\;V,\qquad\Theta_{0}\;:=\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\circ}\,\sigma^{\#}\Big(\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\Big)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}^{\circ}\,X_{i},

where
A_{i}^{\circ}:=A_{i}(p)
are constant commuting matrices and
V
is holomorphic on
U

(i.e.
V
has no logarithmic poles).
Poisson-flatness means the Maurer–Cartan equation in the cotangent Lie algebroid:

\delta\Theta\;+\;\Theta\wedge\Theta\;=\;0,

and similarly for
\Theta_{0}
. We first record that the Euler–Poisson principal part
\Theta_{0}
is itself Poisson-flat.
By
9
each logarithmic Hamiltonian

X_{i}=\sigma^{\#}(dz_{i}/z_{i})
is a Poisson vector field, hence
\delta(X_{i})=0
.
Since the matrices
A_{i}^{\circ}
are constant, it follows that
\delta\Theta_{0}=0
.
Moreover,

\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}[A_{i}^{\circ},A_{j}^{\circ}]\;X_{i}\wedge X_{j},

so
\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=0
because the residue tuple commutes.
Therefore
\delta\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=0
, i.e.
\Theta_{0}
is Poisson-flat.

Step 1.

Since the matrices
A_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,A_{r}^{\circ}
commute, they define a commuting family of endomorphisms of the
finite-dimensional complex vector space
\mathbb{C}^{e}
. Hence
\mathbb{C}^{e}
admits a canonical decomposition into

joint generalized eigenspaces
for this family, namely

(13)

\mathbb{C}^{e}\;=\;\bigoplus_{\kappa\in\mathcal{I}}E_{\kappa},\qquad E_{\kappa}\;:=\;\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\ker\big((A_{i}^{\circ}-\lambda_{i,\kappa}\Id)^{N_{\kappa}}\big),

for suitable integers
N_{\kappa}\gg 0
(depending on
\kappa
) and uniquely determined tuples of eigenvalues

\lambda_{i,\kappa}\in\mathbb{C}
such that, for every
i
and every
\kappa
, the restriction

A_{i}^{\circ}|_{E_{\kappa}}
has a single eigenvalue
\lambda_{i,\kappa}
(equivalently,

A_{i}^{\circ}|_{E_{\kappa}}-\lambda_{i,\kappa}\Id
is nilpotent). In particular, with respect to the direct sum
(
13
), each
A_{i}^{\circ}
is block upper-triangular, preserving every
E_{\kappa}
.
Write
\lambda_{\kappa}=(\lambda_{1,\kappa},\dots,\lambda_{r,\kappa})
for the joint eigenvalues.
All matrices will be decomposed into
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
blocks.
For each
\kappa
, define the diagonal Hamiltonian vector field

\alpha_{\kappa}\;:=\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i,\kappa}\,\frac{\delta z_{i}}{z_{i}},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\Theta_{0}|_{E_{\kappa}}\;=\;\alpha_{\kappa}\cdot\Id_{E_{\kappa}}\ \text{up to nilpotent terms}.

On off-diagonal blocks
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
the commutator
[\Theta_{0},\cdot]
has leading semisimple
part given by multiplication by
\alpha_{\kappa}-\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}
; the nilpotent parts of the

A_{i}^{\circ}
are block-upper-triangular and act nilpotently, so they can be absorbed by a finite
descending induction inside each generalized eigenspace block (this is the standard reduction from
generalized eigenspaces to eigenlines in the homological scheme).

Thus, to explain the non-resonant mechanism transparently, we first treat the eigenline case
(i.e. assume the
A_{i}^{\circ}
are simultaneously diagonal in the chosen frame). The general case
follows by the same inductive argument on the size of Jordan blocks, since the extra nilpotent
terms do not change the scalar denominators that appear below.

Step 2.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{p}:=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\subset\mathcal{O}_{U}
be the maximal ideal at
p
and let
\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{N}
be its powers.
We construct
H
as a convergent series

H\;=\;I\;+\;\sum_{N\geq 1}K^{(N)},\qquad K^{(N)}\in\Mat_{e\times e}(\mathfrak{m}^{N}),

by producing successive approximations
H^{[N]}:=I+\sum_{q=1}^{N}K^{(q)}
satisfying

(14)

(\delta H^{[N]})(H^{[N]})^{-1}\;+\;H^{[N]}\Theta(H^{[N]})^{-1}\;\equiv\;\Theta_{0}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}}.

For
N=0
, take
H^{[0]}=I
, so (
14
) holds modulo
\mathfrak{m}
because
V
is holomorphic.
Assume
H^{[N-1]}
constructed. Define the order-
N
defect

(15)

R^{(N)}\;:=\;(\delta H^{[N-1]})(H^{[N-1]})^{-1}\;+\;H^{[N-1]}\Theta(H^{[N-1]})^{-1}\;-\;\Theta_{0},

so
R^{(N)}\in\Mat_{e\times e}(\mathfrak{m}^{N})
and
R^{(N)}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N}}
by the induction
hypothesis, hence its leading term is homogeneous of degree
N
in
(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
.
We seek a correction of the form

H^{[N]}=(I+K^{(N)})H^{[N-1]}

with
K^{(N)}\in\Mat(\mathfrak{m}^{N})

homogeneous of degree
N
modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}
, such that (
14
) holds.
A direct expansion of the gauge action to first order in
K^{(N)}
gives the
homological equation

(16)

\delta K^{(N)}\;+\;[\Theta_{0},K^{(N)}]\;\equiv\;-\,R^{(N)}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}}.

(The quadratic terms in
K^{(N)}
lie in
\mathfrak{m}^{2N}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}
for
N\geq 1
.)

Step 3.

Fix an off-diagonal block
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
,
\kappa\neq\kappa^{\prime}
.
Write the degree-
N
homogeneous part of that block as a finite sum of monomials

z^{\alpha}:=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
with
|\alpha|=N
:

(R^{(N)})_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\;\equiv\;\sum_{|\alpha|=N}z^{\alpha}\,\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}},\qquad\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\in\Mat(E_{\kappa^{\prime}},E_{\kappa})\otimes\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(\log D)(U),

where each
\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
has coefficients holomorphic in
z^{\prime}:=(z_{r+1},\dots,z_{n})
.
Similarly write

(K^{(N)})_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\;\equiv\;\sum_{|\alpha|=N}z^{\alpha}\,k_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}},\qquad k_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\in\Mat(E_{\kappa^{\prime}},E_{\kappa})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U}.

Using (
11
), i.e.
\delta(z^{\alpha})=z^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\,X_{i}
, and

[\Theta_{0},\cdot]_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}=(\alpha_{\kappa}-\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}})\cdot(\cdot)
on this block,
the degree-
\alpha
part of (
16
) becomes

(17)

\nabla_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}(k_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}})\;=\;-\,\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}},

where
\nabla_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
is the first-order operator

\nabla_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}(f)\;:=\;\delta(f)\;-\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda_{i,\kappa}-\lambda_{i,\kappa^{\prime}})\,f\,X_{i},

Applying
\delta
to (
16
) and using Poisson-flatness (Maurer–Cartan) shows that

R^{(N)}
satisfies the compatibility condition

\delta R^{(N)}\;+\;[\Theta_{0},R^{(N)}]\;\equiv\;0\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}},

hence each
\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
satisfies the integrability condition

(18)

\delta\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\;-\;\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\wedge(\alpha_{\kappa}-\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}})\;=\;0.

Now we solve (
17
) explicitly under non-resonance.
Write each
\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
as a linear combination of the logarithmic generators

\delta z_{i}/z_{i}
with holomorphic coefficients:

\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}\,\frac{\delta z_{i}}{z_{i}}\;+\;(\text{terms tangent to }z^{\prime}),

and similarly write
\alpha_{\kappa}-\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda_{i,\kappa}-\lambda_{i,\kappa^{\prime}})\,\delta z_{i}/z_{i}
.
The condition (
18
) forces the coefficient vector
(C_{1},\dots,C_{r})
to lie in the line spanned by

\big(\alpha_{1}+\lambda_{1,\kappa}-\lambda_{1,\kappa^{\prime}},\dots,\alpha_{r}+\lambda_{r,\kappa}-\lambda_{r,\kappa^{\prime}}\big),

because
\delta(z^{\alpha})
contributes the
\alpha_{i}
and the wedge condition in (

Koszul-type relation in degree

.
Since we assume non-resonance (Definition

), this vector is never zero for
\kappa\neq\kappa^{\prime}
, hence
there exists a unique scalar (matrix)
\Lambda_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
such that

\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\;=\;\Lambda_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\alpha_{i}+\lambda_{i,\kappa}-\lambda_{i,\kappa^{\prime}})\,\frac{\delta z_{i}}{z_{i}}\;+\;(\text{$z^{\prime}$-tangent terms}).

Then setting
k_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}:=\Lambda_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
yields

\nabla_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}(z^{\alpha}k_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}})=-z^{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha,\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}
on the logarithmic part,
because

\nabla_{\kappa\kappa^{\prime}}(z^{\alpha}k)\;=\;\delta(z^{\alpha})k\;-\;z^{\alpha}k(\alpha_{\kappa}-\alpha_{\kappa^{\prime}})\;=\;z^{\alpha}k\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\alpha_{i}+\lambda_{i,\kappa}-\lambda_{i,\kappa^{\prime}})\,\frac{\delta z_{i}}{z_{i}}.

This solves the off-diagonal blocks at homogeneous degree
\alpha
.

Step 4.

For
\kappa=\kappa^{\prime}
, the homological equation (
16
) reduces to

(19)

\delta K^{(N)}_{\kappa\kappa}\ \equiv\ -\,R^{(N)}_{\kappa\kappa}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}}.

Applying
\delta
to (

) and using

together with

\delta\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{0}\wedge\Theta_{0}=0
shows that the defect satisfies the linearized
Maurer–Cartan constraint

(20)

\delta R^{(N)}\ +\ [\Theta_{0},R^{(N)}]\ \equiv\ 0\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}}.

In particular, on diagonal blocks we get

(21)

\delta R^{(N)}_{\kappa\kappa}\ \equiv\ 0\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{N+1}}.

We now use a leafwise logarithmic Poincaré lemma to solve (
19
) at each homogeneous degree.
Fix
x\in U^{*}:=U\setminus D
and let
L_{x}
be the symplectic leaf through
x
. Denote by
\omega_{L_{x}}

the holomorphic symplectic form on
L_{x}
(the inverse of
\sigma|_{L_{x}}
). For any holomorphic vector field

tangent to
L_{x}
, the contraction
\iota_{v}\omega_{L_{x}}
is a holomorphic
1
-form on
L_{x}
.
Moreover, for a Poisson vector field
v
(i.e.
[\sigma,v]=0
), the form
\iota_{v}\omega_{L_{x}}

is closed on
L_{x}
.
Let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
be a polydisc with SNC divisor

D_{V}=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
and set
V^{*}=V\setminus D_{V}

to the canonical meridional subgroup

is the character

\mathbb{Z}^{r}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathrm{GL}_{e}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\ \longmapsto\ \exp\Big(-2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\,\widetilde{A}_{i}\Big).

Proof.

By Definition

Thus, to extend

The normal form (

∎

Remark 5.15
.

Equation (

6.
Rank two meromorphic Poisson modules and Poisson triples

Throughout this section
X
denotes a smooth complex projective variety and

\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}T_{X})
a holomorphic Poisson bivector. We write

\sigma^{\#}:\Omega_{X}^{1}\to T_{X}
for the anchor and we use the Poisson (Lichnerowicz) differential

\delta=[\sigma,-]
on polyvector fields. In particular,

\delta(f)=\sigma^{\#}(df)=:X_{f}\in H^{0}(X,T_{X}),\qquad\delta(v)=[\sigma,v]=L_{v}(\sigma)\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}T_{X}).

6.1.
Hamiltonians, Casimirs, and Poisson vector fields

A holomorphic vector field of the form
X_{f}=\delta(f)
is called
Hamiltonian
.
A holomorphic function
f
is a
Casimir
if
X_{f}=0
, i.e.
\delta(f)=0
.
A holomorphic vector field
v
is
Poisson
if
\delta(v)=0
, equivalently
L_{v}(\sigma)=0
.
Thus
H_{\sigma}^{0}(X)=\ker(\delta:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to T_{X})
is the space of Casimirs and

H_{\sigma}^{1}(X)=\ker(\delta:T_{X}\to\wedge^{2}T_{X})/\mathrm{Im}(\delta:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to T_{X})

measures the obstruction for a Poisson
vector field to be Hamiltonian.

6.2.
Meromorphic Poisson modules and trace-free rank two connections

We use the following working definition (equivalent to the standard Lie algebroid formulation on the smooth locus).

Definition 6.1

(Meromorphic Poisson module)
.

A
meromorphic Poisson module
on
(X,\sigma)
is a holomorphic vector bundle
E\to X
together with a

\mathbb{C}
-linear operator

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}:\ E\longrightarrow T_{X}(*D)\otimes E

(for some divisor
D
, and with poles along
D
) such that for any holomorphic function
f
and local section
s
,

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(fs)=\delta(f)\otimes s+f\,\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(s),

and whose curvature (in the Poisson Lie algebroid sense) vanishes on
X\setminus D
.
When
D=\emptyset
we simply say
Poisson module
.

The case
rk(E)=2
admits a very concrete description after fixing a trivialization.

Definition 6.2
.

Assume
E\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
on an open set
U\subset X
with frame
e=(e_{1},e_{2})
.
A
trace-free
Poisson connection on
E|_{U}
is given by a matrix

\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}v&w\\
u&-v\end{pmatrix},\qquad u,v,w\in\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(U),

acting by

\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(e)=\Theta\cdot e,\qquad\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\big(\delta(z_{1}),\delta(z_{2})\big)+\Theta\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}).

Here
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma}(U)
denotes the sheaf of (meromorphic) Hamiltonian vector fields on
U

(or, more invariantly, the image of
\sigma^{\#}
).

6.3.
Poisson triples and the Maurer–Cartan system

The following is a rank two specialization of Polishchuk’s correspondence between Poisson modules and

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-valued Maurer–Cartan data
[
Pol97
]
; the statement and proof can be found, for instance, in the Lie–algebroid approach to Poisson modules; see
[
LGV13
]
,

Let
E
be a Poisson module of rank
2
on a Poisson projective variety
(X,\sigma)
.
Then locally on a Zariski open set where
E
trivializes, there exist rational vector fields

(u,v,w)
such that the associated trace-free matrix

\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}v&w\\
u&-v\end{pmatrix}

satisfies the Maurer–Cartan flatness equation

\delta\Theta+\Theta\wedge\Theta=0.

Equivalently, the triple
(u,v,w)
satisfies

(29)

\delta(u)=-2u\wedge v,\qquad\delta(v)=u\wedge w,\qquad\delta(w)=-2v\wedge w.

Write
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
in a trivialization as above. The curvature of
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
is represented
by the matrix

K_{\nabla}=\delta\Theta+\Theta\wedge\Theta.

A direct computation in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
shows that

\delta\Theta+\Theta\wedge\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}\delta(v)+u\wedge w&\delta(w)+2v\wedge w\\
\delta(u)+2u\wedge v&-\delta(v)-u\wedge w\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
K_{\nabla}=0
is equivalent to (
29
). Conversely, given
(u,v,w)
satisfying (
29
),
the formula for
\nabla^{\mathrm{Pois}}
defines a flat trace-free Poisson connection on
\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus 2}
, see also
[
Cor20
]
.

Remark 6.3
.

Once one works in a gauge where the Poisson connection is logarithmic (or Euler–Poisson in the non-resonant regime),
the matrix
\Theta
above becomes a
logarithmic
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-system along Hamiltonian leaves
.
All local linearization statements (Poisson Poincaré–Dulac) apply verbatim to this rank two situation, and the
consequences for monodromy/holonomy groupoids are then read off from the residue tuple.

6.4.
A coordinate criterion for Poisson vector fields in affine space

We record a basic criterion that will be useful in explicit families of quadratic Poisson structures.
Let
(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})
be coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and write

\sigma=\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}(z)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j},\qquad\{z_{i},z_{j}\}:=\sigma_{ij}.

Consider a holomorphic vector field
v=\sum_{k=0}^{n}v^{k}\partial_{k}
such that each
v^{k}
depends only on
z_{k}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Under the hypothesis above,
v
is a Poisson vector field (
L_{v}\sigma=0
) if and only if

(30)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}v^{k}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}\{z_{i},z_{j}\}=\left(\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\partial v^{j}}{\partial z_{j}}\right)\{z_{i},z_{j}\}\qquad\text{for all }i<j.

Proof.

A standard Lie-derivative computation gives

L_{v}(\sigma)=\sum_{i<j}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}v^{k}\frac{\partial\sigma_{ij}}{\partial z_{k}}-\sigma_{ij}\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial z_{i}}-\sigma_{ij}\frac{\partial v^{j}}{\partial z_{j}}\right)\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j},

where the hypothesis
v^{k}=v^{k}(z_{k})
eliminates mixed partials.
Thus
L_{v}(\sigma)=0
is equivalent to the coefficient identity in (
30
).
∎

Remark 6.5
.

If
\{z_{i},z_{j}\}=c_{ij}z_{i}z_{j}
with constants
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
(a diagonal quadratic Poisson structure) and

v^{k}=a_{k}z_{k}
, then (
30
) holds for all
i<j
, hence
L_{v}(\sigma)=0
.
For the radial vector field
\mathrm{R}=\sum_{k}z_{k}\partial_{k}
, the condition
L_{\mathrm{R}}\sigma=0
is
equivalent to
\mathrm{R}(\sigma_{ij})=2\sigma_{ij}
for all
i<j
, i.e.
\sigma
is homogeneous of degree
2
.

6.5.
Example: the diagonal quadratic component
L(1,1,1,1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}

[
Pym18
]

We now specialize to a diagonal quadratic Poisson structure on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with homogeneous coordinates

(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
and brackets
\{z_{i},z_{j}\}=c_{ij}z_{i}z_{j}
.
In the component usually denoted
L(1,1,1,1)
one may parametrize the constants by

a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=0
and

c_{01}=a_{3}-a_{2},\quad c_{02}=a_{1}-a_{3},\quad c_{03}=a_{2}-a_{1},\quad c_{12}=a_{3}-a_{0},\quad c_{13}=a_{0}-a_{2},\quad c_{23}=a_{1}-a_{0}.

By Remark
6.5
(a), every diagonal linear vector field

u=\sum_{k=0}^{3}b_{k}z_{k}\partial_{k}
preserves
\sigma
.

The next statement describes when a
constant
vector field
u
satisfies
L_{u}(\sigma)=u\wedge v
for a diagonal
linear field
v
; this is the basic input for constructing explicit Poisson triples.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
u=\sum_{k=0}^{3}c_{k}\,\partial_{k}
be constant and
v=\sum_{k=0}^{3}b_{k}z_{k}\partial_{k}
diagonal linear.
Then
L_{u}(\sigma)=u\wedge v
if and only if for every
i<j
one has

c_{i}(c_{ij}-b_{j})=0,\qquad c_{j}(c_{ij}+b_{i})=0.

Proof.

Write

\sigma=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 3}c_{ij}\,z_{i}z_{j}\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j},\qquad u=\sum_{k=0}^{3}c_{k}\,\partial_{k},\qquad v=\sum_{k=0}^{3}b_{k}z_{k}\,\partial_{k}.

Since
u
is constant, we have
[u,\partial_{i}]=0
for all
i
, hence

L_{u}(\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j})=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle L_{u}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\,L_{u}\bigl(z_{i}z_{j}\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\,\bigl(u(z_{i})z_{j}+z_{i}u(z_{j})\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\,\bigl(c_{i}z_{j}+c_{j}z_{i}\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j},

because
u(z_{i})=c_{i}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle u\wedge v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\bigl(u^{i}v^{j}-u^{j}v^{i}\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\bigl(c_{i}\,(b_{j}z_{j})-c_{j}\,(b_{i}z_{i})\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\bigl(c_{i}b_{j}z_{j}-c_{j}b_{i}z_{i}\bigr)\,\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j},

since
u^{i}=c_{i}
and
v^{j}=b_{j}z_{j}
.
Thus
L_{u}(\sigma)=u\wedge v
holds if and only if for every
i<j
the coefficients of
z_{j}
and of
z_{i}

in the
(i,j)
-component coincide. Equivalently, for each
i<j
one must have

c_{ij}\,c_{i}=c_{i}\,b_{j},\qquad c_{ij}\,c_{j}=-\,c_{j}\,b_{i}.

Rewriting, this is precisely

c_{i}(c_{ij}-b_{j})=0,\qquad c_{j}(c_{ij}+b_{i})=0,

for all
i<j
, as claimed.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Taking
u=\partial_{0}
, the condition of Proposition
6.6
forces

b_{1}=c_{01}
,
b_{2}=c_{02}
,
b_{3}=c_{03}
, so one may take

v=z_{0}\partial_{0}+c_{01}z_{1}\partial_{1}+c_{02}z_{2}\partial_{2}+c_{03}z_{3}\partial_{3},

and then
L_{u}(\sigma)=u\wedge v
.

Remark 6.8

Once explicit pairs

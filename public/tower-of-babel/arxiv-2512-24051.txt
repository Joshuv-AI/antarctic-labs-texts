L p L^{p} Estimates for Numerical Approximation of Hamilton–Jacobi Equations
Alessio Basti, Fabio Camilli

Abstract
. We establish L p L^{p} error estimates for monotone numerical schemes approximating Hamilton–Jacobi equations on the d d -dimensional torus. Using the adjoint method, we first prove a L 1 L^{1} error bound of order one for finite-difference and semi-Lagrangian schemes under standard convexity assumptions on the Hamiltonian. By interpolation, we also obtain L p L^{p} estimates for every finite p > 1 p>1 . Our analysis covers a broad class of schemes, improves several existing results, and provides a unified framework for discrete error estimates.

35F21, 49L25, 65M12, 65M15

1.
Introduction

The Hamilton–Jacobi (HJ) equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+H(Du)=0&\text{in }Q:=\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,T),\\[4.0pt]
u(x,0)=g(x)&\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

plays a central role in several areas of analysis and applied mathematics, including optimal control, front propagation, differential games, and mean field game theory
[
1
]
. The viscosity solution theory provides a robust framework to study this problem: by exploiting the maximum principle, one can establish general results of existence, uniqueness, and regularity for viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) (see
[
2
]
).

From the numerical point of view, monotone approximation schemes such as finite difference and semi-Lagrangian methods have been extensively investigated since the classical works of Crandall and Lions
[
12
]
and Barles and Souganidis
[
3
]
, where convergence to the viscosity solution was proved under suitable assumptions (see also
[
15
]
for a general review). For such schemes,
L^{\infty}
error bounds of order
1/2
were obtained in
[
12
,
9
]
for a broad class of monotone methods, without assuming convexity of the Hamiltonian. These estimates rely on classical arguments from viscosity solution theory, in particular the doubling of variables technique combined with the maximum principle. An alternative approach to error estimates, based on the adjoint method, was developed in
[
19
,
20
]
for certain classes of schemes such as Friedrichs and Godunov methods, and in
[
4
]
for continuous-time, discrete-space equations.

The nonlinear adjoint method, introduced by L.C. Evans in
[
14
]
, has proved to be a powerful tool to capture fine properties of solutions of Hamilton–Jacobi equations that are not accessible through standard viscosity theory. The key idea is to study the linearized adjoint equation associated with (
1.1
), namely

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\textrm{div}\big(D_{p}H(Du)\,\rho\big)=0&\text{ in }Q,\\[4.0pt]
\rho(x,T)=\rho_{T}(x)&\text{ on }\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

By coupling the original nonlinear equation with its linearized adjoint, one can derive quantitative estimates for various asymptotic problems. For example, this method has been successfully applied to obtain rates of convergence in the vanishing viscosity approximation of Hamilton–Jacobi equations, both in the
L^{\infty}
norm
[
14
,
21
,
11
,
10
,
18
]
and in
L^{p}
norms
[
5
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish
L^{p}
error estimates for monotone schemes approximating convex Hamilton–Jacobi equations on the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, for both finite difference and semi-Lagrangian discretizations. Our analysis applies to a broad class of numerical Hamiltonians and relies on a systematic use of the adjoint method combined with interpolation inequalities. More precisely, we consider (
1.1
) under the assumptions that the Hamiltonian
H
is smooth, convex, and coercive, and that the initial datum
g
is Lipschitz continuous and semiconcave. For both finite difference and semi-Lagrangian discretizations of (
1.1
), we establish an
L^{1}
error estimate of order
1
. Interpolation between the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
estimates then yields
L^{p}
error bounds for all finite
p>1
.

Compared to
[
4
]
, where a similar adjoint-based strategy was applied to semi-discrete equations with the Lax–Friedrichs Hamiltonian, we obtain an
L^{1}
error estimate for fully discrete finite difference schemes, and our results apply to a much broader class of monotone schemes. Furthermore, we show how the same method can be adapted to semi-Lagrangian discretizations, for which, to our knowledge,
L^{p}
error estimates have not been previously established.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we analyze the finite difference scheme: we derive the key
L^{1}
estimate using the adjoint method, and then obtain
L^{p}
bounds by interpolation. Section
3
is devoted to the semi-Lagrangian scheme, where we adapt the previous strategy to the discrete dynamic programming formulation. Some technical proofs and auxiliary lemmas are collected in the Appendix.

1.1.
Assumptions and preliminaries

We work on the
d
-dimensional flat torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, which we identify with
[0,1]^{d}
with periodic boundary conditions.
In the following the notion of semiconcavity plays a crucial role. For a concise account of the theory and its applications to Hamilton-Jacobi equations we refer the reader to
[
8
]
. We recall the definition and two properties which we repeatedly use.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
semiconcave
with constant
C_{conc}>0
if for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and every
\lambda\in(0,1)

u(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})\geq\lambda u(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)u(x_{2})-\lambda(1-\lambda)C_{conc}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}.

Lemma 1.2
.

For a function
u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The function
u
is semiconcave, with constant
C_{conc}
.

(ii)

For all
x,y\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, we have

u(x+y)+u(x-y)-2u(x)\leq C_{conc}|y|^{2}.

(iii)

Setting
I_{d}
for the identity matrix, we have that
D^{2}u\leq C_{conc}I_{d}
in the sense of distributions where
D^{2}u
denotes the classical Hessian defined a.e.

For the following results, we refer to
[
8
, Prop. 3.1.3]
and
[
8
, Cor. 3.3.5]
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be semiconcave. Then:

(i)

u
is Lipschitz continuous.

(ii)

Du\in BV({\mathbb{T}}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|D^{2}u(x)|\,dx\leq C_{Hess},

with
C_{Hess}
depending only on the semiconcavity constant of
u
.

Throughout the paper, we make the following standing assumptions:

(H1)

H\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is convex.

(H2)

H
is coercive:
\displaystyle\lim_{|p|\to\infty}H(p)=+\infty
.

(G1)

The initial datum
g:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous and semiconcave.

2.
Estimates for the finite difference scheme

In this section, we first derive an error estimate for a semi-discrete finite difference approximation. Building on this result, we then obtain an error estimate for the fully discrete finite difference scheme.

From now on we fix a uniform space step
h>0
and a time step
\Delta t>0
such that
N\Delta t=T
, and denote
\varepsilon=(h,\Delta t)
. For each coordinate direction
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, we define the forward and backward difference operators

\delta_{h}^{(i)}v(x)=\frac{v(x+he_{i})-v(x)}{h}\qquad\delta_{-h}^{(i)}v(x)=\frac{v(x)-v(x-he_{i})}{h},

and

\displaystyle\delta_{h}v(x)=(\delta_{h}^{(1)}v(x),\dots,\delta_{h}^{(d)}v(x)),\qquad\delta_{-h}v(x)=(\delta_{-h}^{(1)}v(x),\dots,\delta_{-h}^{(d)}v(x)).

We consider the explicit semi-discrete finite-difference scheme

(2.1)

\begin{cases}u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}(x)-u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)+\Delta t\,F\big(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x),\ \delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)\big)=0,&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\ n=0,\dots,N-1,\\[4.0pt]
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)=g(x),&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

The numerical Hamiltonian is a function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(F1)

F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is convex.

(F2)

(Consistency) For every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

F(-p,p)=H(p).

(F3)

(Monotonicity) Let
R>0
be a constant. We assume
\varepsilon=(h,\Delta t)
is chosen such that the update operator

G\big(v(x),\{v(x\pm he_{i})\}_{i=1}^{d}\big):=v(x)-\Delta t\,F\big(-\delta_{h}v(x),\ \delta_{-h}v(x)\big)

is a nondecreasing function of each of the grid values involved (i.e., the central
v(x)
and the neighboring
v(x\pm he_{i})
), provided that the absolute value of the discrete slopes
-\delta_{h}v(x)
and
\delta_{-h}v(x)
is bounded by
R
.

Remark 2.1
.

The monotonicity assumption in (F3) encapsulates two distinct requirements:

(1)

Requiring
G
to be nondecreasing with respect to the
v(x\pm he_{i})
implies that
F
must be componentwise nondecreasing in its arguments, i.e.
F_{p_{i}}\geq 0
and
F_{q_{i}}\geq 0
.

(2)

Requiring
G
to be nondecreasing with respect to the central value
v(x)
leads to the CFL condition:

\Delta t\leq\frac{h}{M_{F}(R)},\quad\text{where }M_{F}(R):=\sup_{|p|,|q|\leq R}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(F_{p_{i}}(p,q)+F_{q_{i}}(p,q)\big).

Two representative numerical Hamiltonians satisfying (F1)–(F3) are the following (see
[
12
]
for additional examples). The first is a multidimensional Lax-Friedrichs type

F(p,q)=H\!\Big(\frac{q-p}{2}\Big)+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{d}(p_{i}+q_{i}),\qquad\alpha>0,

where the linear dissipation (taken with
\alpha
sufficiently large) is used to enforce componentwise monotonicity. The second example is the coordinate-wise piecewise separable form; we state it for
d=1
(see
[
4
]
): assume
H(0)=0=\min_{p\in\mathbb{R}}H(p)
and set

F(p,q)=F_{1}(p)+F_{2}(q),\qquad F_{1}(p)=\begin{cases}0,&p\leq 0,\\[2.0pt]
H(-p),&p>0,\end{cases}\qquad F_{2}(q)=\begin{cases}0,&q\leq 0,\\[2.0pt]
H(q),&q>0.\end{cases}

On each half-line
F_{1},F_{2}
inherit the regularity of
H
, and this choice yields a monotone, separable numerical Hamiltonian.

We show some important properties of the solution of the problem (
2.1
)
(for the proof, see the Appendix)

Proposition 2.2

(Stability and semiconcavity)
.

Let
\{u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
be the solution of (
2.1
).
Under assumptions
(H1)–(H2)
,
(G1)
(with semiconcavity constant
C_{conc}
), and
(F1)–(F3)
, the following properties hold for every
n=0,\dots,N
:

(i)

Lipschitz bounds (discrete and continuous).

\|\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|\delta_{h}g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})},\qquad\|Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|Dg\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})},

where the gradient
Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}
is a.e.

(ii)

Stability.

\|u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+n\Delta t\,|H(0)|.

(iii)

Semiconcavity. For every
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and every
k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
define

\Delta_{k}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x):=u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x+k)+u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x-k)-2u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x).

Then

(2.2)

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\;k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\Delta_{k}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)}{|k|^{2}}\leq C_{conc},

i.e. the semiconcavity constant of
u^{\varepsilon}_{n}
is bounded by that of the initial datum
g
.

By a classical stability result in viscosity solution theory, the solution of the scheme (
2.1
) converges to the viscosity solution of (
1.1
) (cf.
[
3
]
). Furthermore, an estimate of the approximation error is provided in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Convergence rate in
L^{\infty}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon=(h,\Delta t)
and let
\{u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
be the solution of (
2.1
). Let
u
denote the viscosity solution of (
1.1
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

(2.3)

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N}\big\|u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C\,|\varepsilon|^{1/2}.

where
|\varepsilon|=h+\Delta t
.

Remark 2.4
.

This result holds under fairly general assumptions and does not require convexity assumptions. In particular, it applies to the setting considered here.

We now prove a
L^{1}
-estimate for the scheme (
2.1
). We need a preliminary property (for the proof see the Appendix).

Lemma 2.5

(Finite-difference properties)
.

Let
v:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be semiconcave with constant
C_{conc}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{1}
depending only on
C_{conc}
such that, for every
h>0
,

(2.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big|\delta_{h}^{(i)}v(x)-D_{i}v(x)\big|\,dx\;\leq\;C_{1}\,h,\qquad\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big|\delta_{-h}^{(i)}v(x)-D_{i}v(x)\big|\,dx\;\leq\;C_{1}\,h,

for each
i=1,\dots,d
.

Theorem 2.6

(
L^{1}
error estimate; finite difference scheme)
.

Given
\varepsilon=(h,\Delta t)
, let
u
be the viscosity solution of (
1.1
) and
u^{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.6
). Under (H1)–(H2), (G1), and (F1)–(F3), there exists
C>0
independent on
\varepsilon
, such that

(2.5)

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N}\big\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\,|\varepsilon|\;=\;C\,(h+\Delta t).

Proof.

Define the time-linear interpolation of the solution of the scheme (
2.1
) as

(2.6)

u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\Big(1-\frac{t-n\Delta t}{\Delta t}\Big)u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)+\frac{t-n\Delta t}{\Delta t}u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}(x),

for
t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
, with
n\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
.
First note that
\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}=(u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}-u^{\varepsilon}_{n})/\Delta t
. Using the compatibility condition
F(-p,p)=H(p)
we obtain the identity

\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}+H(Du^{\varepsilon})=F(-Du^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon})-F(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}),\qquad t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t].

Set
z:=u^{\varepsilon}-u
. Subtracting the PDE for
u
from the above yields, for
t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
,

(2.7)

\partial_{t}z+b(x,t)\cdot Dz\;=\;F(-Du^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon})-F(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}),

where

b(x,t):=\int_{0}^{1}D_{p}H\big(s\,Du^{\varepsilon}+(1-s)\,Du\big)\,ds\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Fix
\tau=\bar{n}\,\Delta t
with
\bar{n}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Consider the adjoint (backward transport) equation

(2.8)

-\partial_{t}\rho-\operatorname{div}(b\rho)=0\quad\text{in }{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,\tau),\qquad\rho(\cdot,\tau)=\operatorname{sgn}(z(\cdot,\tau)).

Well posedness of (
2.8
) is discussed in
[
20
, pag. 710]
and
[
16
, Thm. 4.12]
. Note that we have
\|\rho(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq 1
.

Multiply (
2.7
) by
\rho
and integrate over
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,\tau)
. Using integration by parts, the adjoint equation (
2.8
) and
z(\cdot,0)=0
, the left-hand side term gives

\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big(\partial_{t}z+b\cdot Dz\big)\rho\,dxdt=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}z(x,\tau)\rho(x,\tau)\,dx-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}z(x,0)\rho(x,0)\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|z(\tau,x)|\,dx.

Hence we get

(2.9)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|z(\tau,x)|\,dx=\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big[F(-Du^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon})-F(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big]\rho\,dxdt.

Accordingly write the right-hand side of (
2.9
) as
I_{1}+I_{2}
, where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big[F(-Du^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon})-F(-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n},Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big]\rho\,dxdt,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big[F(-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n},Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})-F(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big]\rho\,dxdt.

Estimate of
I_{1}
.

Using the compatibility
F(-p,p)=H(p)
, and linearizing
H
, we get, for each
t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
,

F(-Du^{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon})-F(-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n},Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})=H(Du^{\varepsilon})-H(Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})

=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}D_{p}H(sDu^{\varepsilon}+(1-s)Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})\,ds\Big)\cdot\big(Du^{\varepsilon}-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}\big).

Let
M:=\sup_{|p|\leq R}|D_{p}H(p)|
with
R:=\|Dg\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}
. Then

|I_{1}|\leq M\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,\tau))}\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|Du^{\varepsilon}(t)-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}|\,dxdt.

We estimate
Du^{\varepsilon}(t)-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}
. For
t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
one has
by (
2.1
)

\big|Du^{\varepsilon}(t)-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n}\big|=\bigg|\frac{t-n\Delta t}{\Delta t}D\big(u^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}-u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\big)\bigg|\leq\,\big|\Delta t\,DF(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})\big|.

By the chain rule and the uniform bounds on the partial derivatives of
F
, we have pointwise

\big|DF(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})(x)\big|\leq L\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big(\big|D_{j}(\delta_{h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})(x)\big|+\big|D_{j}(\delta_{-h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})(x)\big|\Big),

where
L:=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\ \sup_{|(p,q)|\leq R}\Big\{\;|F_{p_{i}}(p,q)|,\;|F_{q_{i}}(p,q)|\;\Big\}<\infty.
For fixed
i,j
one has the representation

D_{j}\big(\delta_{h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}\big)(x)=\frac{1}{h}\bigg(D_{j}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x+he_{i})-D_{j}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)\bigg)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}D_{ij}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x+se_{i})\,ds=\int_{0}^{1}D_{ij}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x+\theta he_{i})\,d\theta,

and similarly for the backward difference. Integrating the above pointwise bound in
x
and changing variables yields

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big|DF(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})(x)\big|\,dx\leq 2L\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|D_{ij}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(y)|\,dy=2L\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|D^{2}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(y)|\,dy.

By Lemma
1.3
(applied to
u^{\varepsilon}_{n}
, using the semiconcavity preserved by the scheme, Proposition
2.2
) we have a uniform bound by
C_{Hess}
. Plugging into the bound for
I_{1}
and integrating in time on
[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
yields

|I_{1}|\leq 2L\,C_{Hess}M\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(0,\tau))}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\Delta t\,dt\leq C_{I_{1}}\Delta t.

Estimate of
I_{2}
.

Linearizing
F
, and using the previously defined constant
L
, we get

|F(-Du^{\varepsilon}_{n},Du^{\varepsilon}_{n})-F(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n},\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n})|\leq L\sum_{i=1}^{d}(|\delta_{h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-D_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}|+|D_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-\delta_{-h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}|)

Therefore, by relying on Lemma
2.5
,

(2.10)

|I_{2}|\leq L\sum_{n=0}^{\bar{n}-1}\int_{n\Delta t}^{(n+1)\Delta t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(|\delta_{h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-D_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}|+|\delta_{-h}^{(i)}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}-D_{i}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}|\Big)\,dxdt.

\leq 2LdC_{1}\tau h\leq C_{I_{2}}h

Combining the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, we have

\|u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\tau)-u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\max\{C_{I_{1}},C_{I_{2}}\}(\Delta t+h).

∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
. Then for every finite
p\in[1,+\infty]
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N}\big\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C\,|\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}.

Proof.

By the
L^{\infty}
estimate (
2.3
), and the
L^{1}
estimate (
2.5
), for each fixed
n
we have, by relying on the interpolation inequality,

\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{1/p}\,\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}^{1-1/p}

\leq C\,|\varepsilon|^{1/p}\,|\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}(1-1/p)}=C\,|\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}.

Taking the supremum over
n=0,\dots,N
yields the bound.
∎

2.1.
The fully discrete finite difference scheme

We now consider the fully discrete scheme. We fix an integer
I\geq 1
and set
h:=1/I
. The Cartesian periodic grid on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is

\mathcal{G}=\{x_{i}=hi:\ i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\in\{0,\dots,I-1\}^{d}\}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d},

so that there are
I^{d}
distinct grid points. All index operations are understood modulo
I
, which implements periodicity.
Restricting the scheme (
2.1
) to the grid points yields the fully discrete scheme

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n+1}(x_{i})-\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x_{i})+\Delta t\,F\!\big(-\delta_{h}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x_{i}),\ \delta_{-h}\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x_{i})\big)=0,&\text{in}\quad\mathcal{G}\times\{0,\dots,N-1\},\\[6.0pt]
\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x_{i})=g(x_{i}),&\text{on}\quad\mathcal{G}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathcal{I}[\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}](\cdot)
be the piecewise-multilinear interpolant of the grid values
u_{n}(x_{i})
.
For each multi-index
i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})
denote the corresponding cell by

Q_{i}=x_{i}+[0,h)^{d}.

Then, for every
x\in Q_{i}
, the interpolated value is a convex combination of the
2^{d}
corner values, namely

(2.12)

\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\mathcal{I}[\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}](x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\lambda_{\sigma}(x)\,\tilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x_{i+\sigma}),\qquad\lambda_{\sigma}(x)\geq 0,\ \sum_{\sigma}\lambda_{\sigma}(x)=1.

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.6
. Let
\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.12
). Then

(2.13)

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N}\big\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\big\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C\,h.

Proof.

Fix
n
and a cell
Q_{i}
. For
x\in Q_{i}
, using
\sum_{\sigma}\lambda_{\sigma}=1
and
\lambda_{\sigma}\geq 0
, and that the fully discrete iterates coincide with the restriction of the semidiscrete solution to
\mathcal{G}
, we obtain

\big|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)\big|\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\lambda_{\sigma}(x)\big|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i+\sigma})\big|

=\sum_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\lambda_{\sigma}(x)\big|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x_{i+\sigma})\big|.

The semidiscrete solution
u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is Lipschitz in space uniformly in
\varepsilon
; denote a uniform Lipschitz constant by
R
. Hence the previous right-hand side is bounded by

R\max_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{d}}|x-x_{i+\sigma}|.

For
x\in Q_{i}
every corner
x_{i+\sigma}
satisfies
|x-x_{i+\sigma}|\leq\sqrt{d}\,h
, therefore

\big|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\tilde{u}_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)\big|\leq R\sqrt{d}\,h.

This proves (
2.13
) for each
n
with
C:=R\sqrt{d}
.
∎

3.
The estimates for the semi-Lagrangian scheme

In this section, we derive an error estimate for a semi-Lagrangian approximation of the HJ equation (see
[
6
,
9
,
15
]
). Following the strategy of the previous section, we first obtain the estimate for the semi-discrete scheme and then, using this result, we establish the estimate for the fully discrete scheme.

Since in this section we will exploit the control theoretic interpretation of the HJ equation to define the semi-Lagrangian scheme, we write the problem in the standard backward form

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}u+H(x,Du)=0,&\text{in }Q,\\[4.0pt]
u(x,T)=g(x),&\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

with

(3.2)

H(x,p)=H_{0}(p)-V(x)

We assume that
H_{0}
satisfies (H1)–(H2),
g
satisfies (G1), and that
V
satisfies

(V1)

V:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous

We recall that the viscosity solution of (
3.1
) is given by the value function of a control problem.
For a control law
\alpha:[t,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, consider the controlled dynamics

(3.3)

\dot{X}(s)=-\alpha(s)\ \ \mbox{for }s\in(t,T),\ \ X(t)=x,

and denote with
X^{x,t}[\alpha]
the unique solution of the previous problem. Define the cost function

(3.4)

J(\alpha;x,t):=\int_{t}^{T}[L(\alpha(s))+V(X^{x,t}[\alpha](s))]ds+g(X^{x,t}[\alpha](T)).

where
L(\alpha)
is the Legendre transform of
H_{0}(p)
.
Then, the solution
u:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
of (
3.1
) is given by the value function of control problem with dynamics (
3.3
) and cost (
3.4
), i.e.

(3.5)

u(x,t)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(t)}J(\alpha;x,t)\qquad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times[0,T],

where
\mathcal{A}(t)=L^{2}([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of the control law.

The semi-discrete scheme is obtained by discretizing the previous control problem with respect to the time. Fix
{\Delta t}>0
, set
N=T/{\Delta t}
(we assume
N
to be an integer) and for
n=0,...,N-1
define the set of discrete controls
\mathcal{A}^{\Delta t}(n):=\mathbb{R}^{d\times(N-n)}
. Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n+1},\dots,\alpha_{N-1})\in\mathcal{A}^{\Delta t}(n)
, let us define the discrete dynamics
X_{k}^{x,n}[\alpha]
as the solution of

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}X_{k+1}=X_{k}-{\Delta t}\alpha_{k}=x-{\Delta t}\sum_{i=n}^{k}\alpha_{i}&\mbox{for }k=n,\dots,N-1,\\
X_{n}=x,\end{array}\right.

(when the context is clear, we simply write
X_{k}
). The discrete cost function
J^{\Delta t}(\cdot;x,n):\mathcal{A}^{\Delta t}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

J^{\Delta t}(\alpha;x,n)=\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}{\Delta t}[L(\alpha_{k})+V(X_{k})]+g(X_{N}).

The time-discrete value function is

(3.7)

u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\Delta t}(n)}J^{\Delta t}(\alpha;x,n).

Then,
\{u_{n}^{\Delta t}\}_{n=0}^{N}
satisfies the dynamic programming equation

(3.8)

\left\{\begin{split}&u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-\alpha{\Delta t})+{\Delta t}(L(\alpha)+V(x))\right\}\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\,n=0,\dots,N-1,\\[6.0pt]
&u^{\Delta t}_{N}(x)=g(x)\hskip 24.0ptx\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.\end{split}\right.

The next lemma provides useful properties of the discrete value function (see
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
18
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{u^{\Delta t}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
be the solution of (
3.8
). Then, the following assertions hold true:

(i)

(Uniform bound) There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
{\Delta t}
, such that for all
n=0,\dots,N
and
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
,

|u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)|\leq C(1+T).

(ii)

(Lipschitz in space and time) For all
x,y\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and
n,m\in\{0,\dots,N\}
,

(3.9)

|u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)-u^{\Delta t}_{m}(y)|\leq C_{1}\big(|x-y|+\Delta t\,|n-m|\big).

(iii)

(Semiconcavity) For each
n\in\{0,\dots,N\}
the function
\{u^{\Delta t}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
is semiconcave with a constant
C_{2}
independent of
\Delta t
.

The solution of the scheme (
3.8
) converges to the viscosity solution of (
3.1
) with error estimates of order
\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
9
,
13
]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{u^{\Delta t}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
be the solution of (
3.8
) and
u
the viscosity solution of (
3.1
). Then

(3.10)

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N-1}\left\|u^{\Delta t}_{n}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq C{\Delta t}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is clear that for every
(x,n)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times\{0,...,N-1\}
there exists a least one optimal control law
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\Delta t}(n)
such that

(3.11)

u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)=J^{\Delta t}(\alpha;x,n).

We denote by
\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)
the set of discrete controls
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t}}(n)
such that (
3.11
) holds. The next lemma gives a crucial information about the relation between uniqueness of the optimal control law and the differentiability
of
u^{\Delta t}_{n}
at
x
(for the proof we refer again to
[
6
, Lemma 3.3]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(x,n)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times\{0,...,N-1\}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)
such that (
3.11
) holds.
Then,

(i)

The set
\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)
is bounded by a constant independent of
{\Delta t}
.

(ii)

For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)
and
k=n+1,...,N-1
, we have
\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(X_{k}^{x,n}[\alpha],k)=\left\{(\alpha_{k},...,\alpha_{N-1})\right\}.

(iii)

The function
u^{\Delta t}_{n}(\cdot)
is differentiable at
x
iff there exists
{\alpha}(x,n)\in\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t}}(n)
such that
\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)=\{{\alpha}(x,n)\}
. In that case,

(3.12)

Du^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)=D_{\alpha}L({\alpha}_{n}(x,n))+{\Delta t}DV(x).

In particular, the problem associated with
u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x,n)
has a unique solution a.e. in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

(iv)

For all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{{\Delta t},*}(x,n)
, the following relation hold true:

(3.13)

Du^{\Delta t}_{k+1}(X_{k}-{\Delta t}\alpha_{k}(X_{k},k))=D_{\alpha}L(\alpha_{k}(X_{k},k))\quad\mbox{for }\,k=n,...,N-1.

Remark 3.4
.

We observe that, for
k=n
, (
3.13
) reads as

(3.14)

Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\alpha_{n}(x,n))=D_{\alpha}L(\alpha_{n}(x,n))\quad\mbox{for }\,k=n,...,N-1.

We prove the
L^{1}
estimate for the semi-Lagrangian scheme. The idea of the proof is similar to the one of Theorem
2.6
.

Theorem 3.5

(
L^{1}
error estimate; semi-Lagrangian scheme)
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
3.1
) and
\{u^{\Delta t}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
the solution of (
3.8
). Under
(H1)-(H2), (V1), (G1)
, there exists
C>0
depending on
T,H,g
such that

(3.15)

\sup_{n=0,\dots,N}\big\|u_{n}^{\Delta t}(\cdot)-u(\cdot,n\Delta t)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C\,{\Delta t}.

Proof.

We rewrite the first equation in (
3.8
) as

\frac{u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)-u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)}{{\Delta t}}+F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}](x)-V(x)=0

where

F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}](x)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{-\frac{u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\alpha)-u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)}{{\Delta t}}-L(\alpha)\right\}.

For
n=0,\dots,N-1
and
t\in[n\Delta t,(n+1)\Delta t]
, define

(3.16)

u^{\Delta t}(x,t)=\Big(1-\frac{t-n\Delta t}{\Delta t}\Big)u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)+\frac{t-n\Delta t}{\Delta t}\,u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x).

Since
\partial_{t}u^{\Delta t}(x,t)=(u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-u^{\Delta t}_{n}(x))/{\Delta t}
, the function
z(x,t)=u^{\Delta t}(x,t)-u(x,t)
solves the equation

(3.17)

\begin{split}&z_{t}-b(x,t)Dz=F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]-H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}(t))\qquad t\in[n{\Delta t},(n+1){\Delta t}]\end{split}

with

b(x,t)=\int_{0}^{1}D_{p}H_{0}(sDu^{\Delta t}+(1-s)Du)ds.

Let
\tau=\bar{n}\Delta t
for
\bar{n}\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
. We multiply equation (
3.17
) for the solution
\rho
of the forward conservative equation

(3.18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho-\mathrm{div}(b(x,t)\rho)=0&\text{in ${\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times(\tau,T)$}\\
\rho(\cdot,\tau)=\mathrm{sgn}(z(\cdot,\tau))&\text{on ${\mathbb{T}}^{d}$},\end{array}\right.

and integrate on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(\tau,T)
to get by duality
and
z(x,T)=0

(3.19)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\rho(x,\tau)\,z(x,\tau)\,dx=\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}(t))-F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]\Big]\rho\,dxdt\\
&=\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}(t))-H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})\Big]\rho dxdt\\
&+\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du_{n+1}^{\Delta t})-F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]\Big]\rho dxdt\end{split}

We estimate the first term on the left hand side of (
3.19
). We have

(3.20)

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}(t))-H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})\Big]\rho dx\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}D_{p}H_{0}(sDu^{\Delta t}+(1-s)Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})ds\right)(Du^{\Delta t}(t)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})\rho dx\\
&\leq M\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times[\tau,T])}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|Du^{\Delta t}(t)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}|\,dx.\end{split}

We have

(3.21)

Du^{\Delta t}(x,t)-Du^{\Delta t}_{\,n+1}(x)=\bigg(\frac{t-n{\Delta t}}{{\Delta t}}-1\bigg)D\big(u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-u^{\Delta t}_{\,n}(x)\big)

By (
3.12
) and (
3.14
), we get

Du^{\Delta t}_{n}(x)=Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\alpha_{n}(x,n))+{\Delta t}DV(x)

and, replacing in (
3.21
), we get

(3.22)

Du^{\Delta t}(t)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}=\bigg(\frac{t-n{\Delta t}}{{\Delta t}}-1\bigg)D\left[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\alpha_{n}(x,n))-{\Delta t}V(x)\right]

We have

Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n))=\alpha_{n}(x,n){\Delta t}\,\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-\tau{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n))\,d\tau,

and hence

\bigl|Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n))\bigr|\leq|{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n)|\int_{0}^{1}|D^{2}u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-\tau{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n))|\,d\tau.

By the uniform semiconcavity of
u^{\Delta t}
and Lemma
3.3
.(i), integrating over
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and using translation invariance we get

(3.23)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\bigl|Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}\,\alpha_{n}(x,n))\bigr|\,dx\leq{\Delta t}\,\|\alpha_{n}(\cdot,n)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|D^{2}u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}|dx.

By (
3.22
), (
3.23
) and (V1), we get

(3.24)

\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}(t))-H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})\Big]\rho dx\leq C{\Delta t}.

We now consider the second term in (
3.19
). Let
{\bar{\alpha}(x)}
be such that

H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x))=-{\bar{\alpha}(x)}\cdot Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)-L({\bar{\alpha}(x)}).

Then

H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x))-F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}](x)\leq\frac{u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}{\bar{\alpha}(x)})-u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)}{{\Delta t}}-{\bar{\alpha}(x)}\cdot Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)

Since
\|\bar{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
and taking into account Lemma
1.3
.(ii) and Lemma
3.1
.(iii), we can estimate

(3.25)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big[H_{0}(Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1})-F[u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}]\Big]\rho(x,t)dxdt\\
&\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{n=\bar{n}}^{N-1}\int_{n{\Delta t}}^{(n+1){\Delta t}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big|\frac{u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x-{\Delta t}{\bar{\alpha}})-u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)}{{\Delta t}}-{\bar{\alpha}}\cdot Du^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)\Big|dxdt\\
&\leq(T-\tau)\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}C^{2}{\Delta t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\Big|D^{2}u^{\Delta t}_{n+1}(x)\Big|dx\leq\bar{C}{\Delta t}.\end{split}

Replacing (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) in (
3.19
)
, we get (
3.15
).
∎

Appendix A
Proofs of technical lemmas

Proof of Prop.
2.2
.

(1)

Let

w_{n}^{(i)}(x):=\delta^{(i)}_{h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)
, and
R:=\|\delta_{h}g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\|\delta^{(i)}_{h}g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}.

We prove the result by induction on
n
. The base case
n=0
is immediate. Assume the claim holds at time
n
.
Fix a direction
i
. By construction

u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i})-u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)=h\,w_{n}^{(i)}(x)\leq hR.

Therefore

u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i})\leq u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)+hR.

Consider the discrete evolution operator
G
given by the scheme:

u_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(y)=G(u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y),\{u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y\pm he_{i})\}_{i=1}^{d}):=u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y)-\Delta t\,F\big(-\delta_{h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y),\;\delta_{-h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(y)\big).

By monotonicity of
G
, and since adding a constant leaves the finite differences unchanged,

G(u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i}),\{u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i}\pm he_{j})\}_{j=1}^{d})\leq G(u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x),\{u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x\pm he_{j})\}_{j=1}^{d})+hR.

Hence

u_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i})\leq u_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(x)+hR.

Equivalently,

\frac{u_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(x+he_{i})-u_{n+1}^{\varepsilon}(x)}{h}\leq R,

that is

w_{n+1}^{(i)}(x)\leq R

for every
x
. Since
i
was arbitrary we obtain

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\sup_{x}w_{n+1}^{(i)}(x)\leq R
. Similarly, to prove the lower bound, we assume by induction that
w_{n}^{(i)}(x)\geq-R
.
Calculations imply
w_{n+1}^{(i)}(x)\geq-R
.
Combining the two bounds, we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\|w_{n+1}^{(i)}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq R.

Since
g
is Lipschitz, it is a.e. differentiable and
R\leq\|Dg\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}
. Using also the previously obtained inequality on the discrete derivatives of
u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
, we have that
u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
is also Lipschitz, and therefore

\|Du_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|Dg\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}.

(2)

Let

w_{n}(x):=u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)+n\Delta t\,H(0),

so that
\delta_{\pm h}w_{n}=\delta_{\pm h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}
. By the discrete Lipschitz bound proved in part (1) there exists
R>0
such that for every
n
and every coordinate
i

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}|\delta^{(i)}_{\pm h}w_{n}(x)|\leq R.

From the scheme (
2.1
) we have pointwise for every
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}

w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)=-\Delta t\Big(F\big(-\delta_{h}w_{n}(x),\delta_{-h}w_{n}(x)\big)-F(0,0)\Big),

Apply the multivariate mean value theorem to
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}
on the segment joining
(0,0)
and
\big(-\delta_{h}w_{n}(x),\delta_{-h}w_{n}(x)\big)
. There exists
\xi(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
on that segment such that

\displaystyle F\big(-\delta_{h}w_{n}(x),\delta_{-h}w_{n}(x)\big)-F(0,0)

\displaystyle=DF(\xi(x))\cdot\big(-\delta_{h}w_{n}(x),\delta_{-h}w_{n}(x)\big)

Substituting into the equation of interest, yields the identity

w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)=-\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[F_{p_{i}}(\xi(x))\big(-w_{n}(x+he_{i})+w_{n}(x)\big)+F_{q_{i}}(\xi(x))\big(w_{n}(x)-w_{n}(x-he_{i})\big)\Big],

Therefore,

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}F_{p_{i}}(\xi(x))\,w_{n}(x+he_{i})+\Big(1-\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(F_{p_{i}}(\xi(x))+F_{q_{i}}(\xi(x))\big)\Big)w_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}F_{q_{i}}(\xi(x))\,w_{n}(x-he_{i}).

Since
F_{p_{i}},F_{q_{i}}\geq 0
, and the CFL condition holds, the three coefficients are nonnegative and sum to
1
. It follows that, for each fixed
x
,

w_{n+1}(x)\leq\max\{w_{n}(x),w_{n}(x\pm he_{1}),...,w_{n}(x\pm he_{d})\}\leq\sup_{y}w_{n}(y),

hence
\sup_{x}w_{n+1}(x)\leq\sup_{x}w_{n}(x)
.
Similarly
w_{n+1}(x)\geq\min\{w_{n}(x),w_{n}(x\pm he_{1}),...,w_{n}(x\pm he_{d})\}\geq\inf_{y}w_{n}(y)
. Iterating we get
\|w_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}
, and therefore

\|u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\leq\|w_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+n\Delta t\,|H(0)|\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}+n\Delta t\,|H(0)|.

(3)

Let
w_{n}(x):=\Delta_{k}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(x)/|k|^{2}
, that is, the quantity we want to estimate
2.2
.
Introducing

A_{n}(y):=\big(-\delta_{h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(y),\;\delta_{-h}u^{\varepsilon}_{n}(y)\big)
, evaluating the scheme (
2.1
) at the points
y=x-k,x,x+k
, and suitably combining the three relations, we get

(A.1)

w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)=-\frac{\Delta t}{|k|^{2}}\Big(F\big(A_{n}(x+k)\big)-2F\big(A_{n}(x)\big)+F\big(A_{n}(x-k)\big)\Big).

Since
F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is convex, we have for any vectors
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

F(X)-F(Y)\geq DF(Y)\cdot(X-Y)
and
F(Z)-F(Y)\geq DF(Y)\cdot(Z-Y)
. Therefore,

F(X)-2F(Y)+F(Z)\geq DF(Y)\cdot(X-2Y+Z).

Applying this inequality with

X=A_{n}(x+k),\,Y=A_{n}(x),\,Z=A_{n}(x-k)
in (
A.1
) gives the estimate

w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)\leq-\frac{\Delta t}{|k|^{2}}\,DF\big(A_{n}(x)\big)\cdot\big(A_{n}(x+k)-2A_{n}(x)+A_{n}(x-k)\big)

Using
\Delta_{k}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}=|k|^{2}w_{n}
, and
\Delta_{k}\big(\delta^{(i)}_{h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\big)(x)=\delta^{(i)}_{h}\big(\Delta_{k}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\big)(x)
, we get

\Delta_{k}\big(\delta^{(i)}_{h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\big)(x)=|k|^{2}\delta^{(i)}_{h}w_{n}(x),\qquad\Delta_{k}\big(\delta^{(i)}_{-h}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}\big)(x)=|k|^{2}\delta^{(i)}_{-h}w_{n}(x),

so that

A_{n}(x+k)-2A_{n}(x)+A_{n}(x-k)=\Delta_{k}A_{n}(x)=|k|^{2}\big(-\delta_{h}w_{n},\;\delta_{-h}w_{n}\big)(x).

Therefore,

w_{n+1}(x)-w_{n}(x)\leq-\Delta t\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(-F_{p_{i}}(A_{n}(x))\,\delta^{(i)}_{h}w_{n}(x)+F_{q_{i}}(A_{n}(x))\,\delta^{(i)}_{-h}w_{n}(x)\big).

Writing
\delta^{(i)}_{\pm h}w_{n}
explicitly gives

w_{n+1}(x)\leq w_{n}(x)-\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(-F_{p_{i}}w_{n}(x+he_{i})+(F_{p_{i}}+F_{q_{i}})w_{n}(x)-F_{q_{i}}w_{n}(x-he_{i})\Big)

=w_{n}(x)\bigg(1-\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(F_{p_{i}}+F_{q_{i}})\bigg)+\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}F_{p_{i}}w_{n}(x+he_{i})+\frac{\Delta t}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{d}F_{q_{i}}w_{n}(x-he_{i}).

Since the scheme satisfies the monotonicity and CFL conditions, then the coefficients in the previous inequality are nonnegative and sum to
1
. Hence,

w_{n+1}(x)\leq\max\{w_{n}(x),\,w_{n}(x\pm he_{i})\}\leq\sup_{y}w_{n}(y).

x
yields
\sup_{x}w_{n+1}(x)\leq\sup_{x}w_{n}(x)
, therefore the sequence
\{\sup_{x}w_{n}\}_{n}
is nonincreasing. Consequently

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\;k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\Delta_{k}u_{n}^{\varepsilon}(x)}{|k|^{2}}\leq\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\;k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}w_{0}(x)=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\;k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\Delta_{k}g(x)}{|k|^{2}}\leq C_{\mathrm{conc}}.

∎

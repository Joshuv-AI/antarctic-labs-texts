Supporting Students’ Interprofessional Identity Formation Through Basic Psychological Needs Satisfaction: A Qualitative Inquiry
Authors: Saskia C M Oosterbaan-Lodder, Anne de la Croix, Jan Jaap Reinders, Fedde Scheele, Rashmi A Kusurkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite growing interest in interprofessional identity (IPI) development, there is limited insight into what motivates healthcare students to develop their IPI, specifically in the context of an interprofessional training unit (ITU). This prompted our research question: what motivates students to develop their IPI?MethodsAs part of a larger quantitative study conducted between September 2023 and July 2024, qualitative narrative data were collected via an online survey at two Dutch teaching hospitals. Students answered three open-ended questions on what influenced their interprofessional identity before and after placement. Data was analyzed through content analysis, using the three basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) as a sensitizing framework.ResultsOne hundred and seventy-nine students participated, providing 1012 comments. We identified 4 overarching themes: relatedness, competence, autonomy and value of interprofessional education and collaboration (IPE&C).DiscussionThis study shows that an ITU placement may enhance students’ IPI formation by satisfaction of the basic psychological needs for relatedness and competence, and to a lesser extent autonomy, suggesting students in this specific ITU context found it challenging to exercise autonomy Students’ IPI formation seems to be a continuous process of reconciling expectations of IPE&C, through previous experiences and stories from others, with lived experiences during interprofessional interactions. We provide practical implications for ITU tutors.Keywords: interprofessional education, interprofessional placement, interprofessional training unit, motivation, faculty development

Introduction

Interprofessional collaboration (IPC) and interprofessional education (IPE) are widely recognized as a key strategy for strengthening health systems and improving healthcare outcomes, highlighting its importance for sustainable interprofessional care.1 Promoting interprofessional identity (IPI) formation is regarded important to strengthening IPC.2,3 Review studies by Tong et al4 and Polansky et al5 identify the study of IPI formation as a research priority, given its relevance to the advancement of healthcare practice towards interprofessionality.

The Extended Professional Identity Theory (EPIT) views attitudes as crucial antecedents to IPI formation.6 Attitudes, defined as positive or negative evaluations of objects, people, or events7 have motivational effects on the importance of a group to an individual.8 This is particularly relevant because students often enter clinical environments with identities shaped by “intraprofessional lore”, which consists of negative professional stereotypes and implicit messages about other disciplines passed down by supervisors and peers.9 Based on EPIT’s propositions, an IPI is formed through a combination of three factors, interprofessional belonging (feeling part of an interprofessional team), interprofessional commitment (valuing and emotionally investing in an interprofessional team), and interprofessional beliefs (endorsing interprofessional collaboration as key to good care).10 Despite growing interest in IPI development, there is limited insight into what motivates or influences students in their IPI formation during interprofessional placements,11 also called interprofessional training unit (ITU) placements. Unlike classroom-based or simulation-based IPE, the ITU placement provides an immersive clinical setting in which students do not merely observe one another but actively engage in shared clinical reasoning, joint decision-making, and shared responsibility for patient care.

In the larger quantitative part of this study we found that a one-week ITU placement on maternity wards in two Dutch teaching hospitals significantly increased students’ IPI.12 Participants were medical, midwifery, and nursing students, reflecting the professional groups involved in the maternity ward setting where the ITU was situated. Details about the ITUs are provided in Appendix A. The present study utilizes narrative free-text data collected from the same participant cohort (medical, midwifery, and nursing students) to provide a deeper understanding of the mechanisms behind those shifts, by exploring the subjective experiences and motivational drivers of IPI formation. Before and after the placement, students described in free-text fields what influenced their interprofessional belonging, interprofessional commitment and interprofessional beliefs (Appendix B). These narrative data offer depth and richness beyond the quantitative data, capturing personal meaning and emotional and social nuances, and revealing how participants constructed meaning over time,13 providing the rationale for this study.

Theoretical Framework

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) offers a robust framework for understanding motivation and identity development in various contexts, including interprofessional education (IPE). SDT posits that individuals need fulfillment of three basic psychological needs (BPNs): autonomy (sense of volition), competence (sense of capability or efficacy), and relatedness (sense of feeling connected and supported by others).14,15 These needs must be satisfied to foster autonomous motivation (originating from inherent interest or personal endorsement of an activity) and psychological well-being. Frustration of these psychological needs, on the other hand, enhances controlled motivation (out of internal or external pressure or rewards) and is associated with lower well-being, and poor performance.14 While SDT has been applied to professional identity (PI) and team motivation,16–18 its contribution to interprofessional identity (IPI) formation remains underexplored.19

This prompted our research question: What motivates healthcare students to develop an IPI in the context of an ITU placement on a maternity ward?

Materials and Methods

Participants were medical, midwifery, and nursing students, reflecting the professional groups represented in the maternity ward where the ITU was situated. Between September 2023 and July 2024, we collected qualitative free-text data at two Dutch teaching hospitals (one secondary and one tertiary care hospital), using a CastorEDC online survey, with the aim of supplementing our quantitative findings with insights form narrative data. These data were collected as part of a larger quantitative study.12 By completing the survey, students consented to participate in the study. Before and after their placement students were invited to respond to three open field questions (without character limit): What influences your interprofessional belonging, interprofessional commitment and interprofessional beliefs?

Ethical Approval

Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Netherlands Association for Medical Education (file 2023.4.5) and from the Scientific research Committee in OLVG (ACWO research no WO 23.086).12 The study complies with the declaration of Helsinki.

Data Analyses

We analyzed the narrative data of medical, midwifery and nursing students. We used Atlas.ti 25, a qualitative data analysis software,20 to conduct a content analysis with the goal to validate or conceptually extend a theoretical framework or theory,21 in our case the EPIT22 and SDT.14 First, two researchers (SO and AC) performed initial, independent, open coding of factors influencing students’ IPI. The unit of analysis was defined as an individual phrase within a single text response. We then performed a round of coding using the sensitizing concepts “autonomy”, “competence” and “relatedness” from SDT, while being open to new themes. Any coding disagreements or differing interpretations of the text were resolved through discussion and consensus. We established the number of comments for each code group and theme23,24 to demonstrate its frequency of occurrence or relative importance in the whole picture. We followed published recommendations while reporting the results from the quantification of our qualitative data.25

Results

A total number of 179 students completed both the pre-and post-placement questionnaires, representing 89.9% of the total cohort assigned to the ITU. For the present study, participants were included if they completed both the pre- and post-placement questionnaires; for the qualitative analysis, the only exclusion criteria were blank free-text entries. In total, 126 students provided analyzable free-text pre-placement responses, and 142 provided post-placement responses. More medical students participated than midwifery or nursing students, reflecting the distribution of students assigned to the ITU placements. Across all qualitative data, we obtained 741 free text responses, yielding 1012 comments (522 pre-placement and 490 post -placement) (Appendix C). Next to satisfaction or frustration of the three BPNs influencing students’ IPI formation, we also found that the value of interprofessional education and collaboration (IPE&C) influenced students’ IPI formation. Comments referring to relatedness were mentioned most frequently as influencing students’ IPI, before as well as after their ITU placement. A “Positive and safe learning environment”, where students experience “openness, respect, and trust” from peers and other healthcare professionals (HCPs), where they feel “seen and heard” and experience a reciprocal “effort to learn and work together” are important conditions for IPI formation. After the ITU placement, more comments were made referring to students’ need for competence compared to before the placement. In particular, comments on experiencing “collaborative efficacy” were mentioned more frequently after the placement, suggesting that students experienced the tangible reality that “care improves through collaboration” and that teams can “solve a problem” more effectively than individuals.

Comments related to autonomy in engaging in shared decision-making and patient care were reported less frequently. Comments referring to the value of IPE&C were primarily mentioned before the placement, mainly related to students’ interprofessional beliefs.

Discussion

The previous larger quantitative study demonstrated that a one-week ITU placement in fast-paced maternity care could significantly increase students’ IPI, particularly their interprofessional belonging and commitment.12 The present qualitative inquiry adds that satisfaction of students’ BPNs for relatedness, competence and (to a lesser extent) autonomy might influence students’ IPI, corroborating recent research on identity development in rehabilitation settings26 and during interprofessional simulation.27 In particular, experiences of competence may strengthen self-efficacy, potentially reinforcing IPI development.28 The relatively lower frequency of comments related to autonomy may reflect that students find it challenging to exercise autonomy in an environment where certain responsibilities are new to them. For instance, medical students may feel inexperienced in providing hands-on patient care, whereas midwifery and nursing students may feel less confident in their medical knowledge.29

Our findings suggest that students’ IPI formation is a continuous process of reconciling pre-placement expectations of IPE&C (through previous experiences and stories from others) with lived experiences during the ITU placement. The finding that a one-week ITU placement did not significantly alter students’ interprofessional beliefs12 – the feeling that interprofessional collaboration is fundamental to high quality collaborative patient care – suggests that a brief ITU placement may be insufficient to change the existing intraprofessional lore,9 referring to negative professional stereotypes other professions, shaped by implicit messages from supervisors and peers. Nevertheless, students’ interprofessional beliefs may be strengthened through repeated, iterative ITU placements that support BPNs satisfaction.

Where previous research has shown that an ITU placement can enhance motivation for future collaboration,30 and that satisfaction of BPNs influences interprofessional competencies,31 our findings extend this by suggesting that BPNs satisfaction may also enhance students’ IPI formation, which in turn may drive collaborative behavior.3 In this way, students may be supported to move from providing collaborative patient care because they are told to, to doing it because it is part of who they are.

Practical Implications to Foster Students’ IPI Development by Tutors

Foster relatedness by creating a safe learning environment that provides time and space for informal interaction, thereby enhancing openness, respect and trust among students as well as between students and tutors. Involve students in all aspects of care and guide them to work towards a common goal: promoting patient’s health.

Limitations and Future Research

During analysis we walked a tight rope between qualitative and quantitative work, wanting to grasp the meaning of factors influencing students’ IPI formation, before and during their ITU placement. This study has several limitations. First, the data were derived from narrative comments, which provide insights but may not capture the full depth of factors influencing students’ IPI. Second, the study focused on medical, midwifery and nursing students within a single placement context, which may limit generalizability to other professional groups or settings. Future research could focus on whether and how consecutive ITU placements can successfully challenge “intraprofessional lore” and negative professional stereotypes.

Conclusions

Our previous larger quantitative study demonstrated that a one-week ITU placement in the context of a maternity ward significantly increased students’ IPI. The present qualitative inquiry suggests that IPI formation is a continuous process of reconciling pre-placement expectations – shaped by previous experiences and stories from others – with lived experiences during the ITU-placement. Our findings further suggest that students’ perceived BPN-satisfaction of relatedness and competence – and in our context to a lesser extent autonomy – may play a supportive role in students’ IPI formation.

Aberrant KIF26B expression promotes bladder cancer progression through driving NSUN2 nuclear localization
Authors: Jia-Ming Wang, Hai-Yun Xie, Feng-Hao Zhang, Xuan Shu, Jin-Dan Luo, Li-Ping Xie, Jiang-Feng Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abnormal expression of kinesins has been observed in several types of cancer. The present study provided the first evidence that KIF26B could induce RNA m5C modification in bladder cancer (BCa). Detailly, KIF26B interacted with NSUN2 and recruited ubiquitinase STUB1 to promote K63-linked ubiquitination of NSUN2 at K511 site. K63-linked ubiquitination enabled NSUN2 to bind to KPNA1 and translocate into nucleus where it drove RNA m5C modification. Furthermore, KIF26B induced liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) of YBX1 and upregulated IL-6 expression through NSUN2/m5C/YBX1 axis. Secreted IL-6 then activated STAT3 signaling to promote transcription of kif26b through direct binding between STAT3 and kif26b promoter. IL-6 also recruited DLAT to acetylate NSUN2 at K229 site and increased association affinity between NSUN2 and KIF26B. Together, these established a KIF26B/NSUN2/m5C/IL-6 positive feedback loop, suggesting that targeting KIF26B may be a promising therapeutic strategy for BCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13045-025-01749-4.

To the editor

RNA m5C modification accelerates BCa progression [1, 2]. The methyl-transferase NSUN2 promotes disease by installing m5C mark on nuclear RNA [1], yet how it enters the nucleus remains unknown. Kinesins power long-distance cargo transport [3], and we previously showed that KIF26B ferried TRAF2 into nucleus of BCa cells [4]. Because KIF26B also mediated the nuclear import of RNA m6A regulators in breast cancer [5], we asked whether it similarly escorted NSUN2 to drive m5C deposition in BCa.

Main

KIF26B robustly enhanced global RNA m5C levels (Fig. 1A; Suppl. Figure 1A–C). Mechanistically, KIF26B engaged NSUN2 through tyrosine 1784 (T1784) within its C-terminus (Fig. 1B-C; Suppl. Figure 1D–F). Depletion of KIF26B trapped NSUN2 in the cytoplasm; wild-type KIF26B or its C-terminal fragment restored nuclear accumulation, whereas the T1784A mutant failed to do so (Fig. 1D; Suppl. Figure 2A–E). NSUN2 constitutively bound importin-α5 (KPNA1) (Suppl. Figure 3A–C), and KPNA1 knock-down phenocopied KIF26B loss (Fig. 1E; Suppl. Figure 3D). Over-expressed KIF26B drove NSUN2 into the nucleus only when KPNA1 was present, and co-immunoprecipitation showed that KIF26B enabled NSUN2–KPNA1 complex formation (Fig. 1F). Consequently, KPNA1 was indispensable for KIF26B-induced m5C installation (Fig. 1G).

KIF26B did not alter NSUN2 abundance (Suppl. Figure 4A-B), but markedly increased its K63-linked ubiquitination (Fig. 1H; Suppl. Figure 4C). The E3 ligase STUB1 selectively catalyzed K63-ubiquitination of NSUN2 without affecting protein levels or K48-ubiquitination (Suppl. Figure 4D–I). KIF26B loss weakened STUB1–NSUN2 interaction, whereas KIF26B over-expression strengthened it in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1H; Suppl. Figure 4 J). Catalytically-dead STUB1 abolished KIF26B-mediated NSUN2 ubiquitination and m5C accumulation (Suppl. Figure 4K-L). Mass-spectrometry mapped K511 within the NSUN2 NLS as the STUB1 target site (Fig. 1I; Suppl. Figure 5A–C). The K511R mutant exhibited reduced K63-ubiquitination, impaired KPNA1 binding, and persistent cytoplasmic retention that could not be rescued by KIF26B or STUB1 over-expression (Fig. 1J; Suppl. Figure 5D–E).

In the cytoplasm, the m5C reader YBX1 underwent LLPS in a CTD-dependent manner (Fig. 1K–O; Suppl. Figure 6A). KIF26B depletion dissolved these condensates; wild-type KIF26B reinstated them, whereas the T1784A mutant did not (Fig. 1P; Suppl. Figure 6B).

Integrative multi-omics identified IL-6 as a direct KIF26B target (Fig. 2A). KIF26B knock-down decreased IL-6 mRNA and protein abundance by shortening transcript half-life (Fig. 2B–D). NSUN2 bound the il6 mRNA (Suppl. Figure 7A), and KIF26B loss diminished m5C modification at this locus (Suppl. Figure 7B). Catalytically-dead (C271A/C321A) or K511R NSUN2 mutants blocked KIF26B-driven up-regulation of IL-6 (Fig. 2E–G; Suppl. Figure 7C). YBX1 stabilized il6 mRNA in an m5C-dependent manner (Suppl. Figure 7D-E), and this enrichment required both KIF26B and wild-type NSUN2 (Suppl. Figure 7F-G). Disruption of YBX1 LLPS abolished KIF26B-mediated IL-6 induction (Fig. 2H; Suppl. Figure 7H).

Interestingly, IL-6 activated STAT3 to transcriptionally up-regulate KIF26B. STAT3 bound the kif26b promoter at − 599∽−589, and IL-6 enhanced this occupancy (Fig. 2I–K; Suppl. Figure 8A–F). In turn, IL-6 stimulated DLAT-dependent acetylation of NSUN2 without altering its abundance (Fig. 2L; Suppl. Figure 9A-B). K229-acetylated NSUN2 displayed stronger affinity for KIF26B (Fig. 2L-M; Suppl. Figure 9C–G). The K229R mutant failed to interact with KIF26B (Suppl. Figure 9H) and consequently resisted IL-6-induced K63-ubiquitination, KPNA1 binding, and nuclear import (Fig. 2N-P; Suppl. Figure 9I–K). DLAT required KIF26B and STUB1 to potentiate NSUN2 nuclear translocation and m5C deposition (Suppl. Figure 9L-N). Neither K511R nor K229R NSUN2 responded to IL-6 stimulation (Suppl. Figure 10A–D), and blocking KIF26B–NSUN2 interaction phenocopied these mutations (Fig. 2Q–S; Suppl. Figure 10E).

Functionally, KIF26B over-expression enhanced colony formation, cell viability, and xenograft growth only when wild-type NSUN2 was present; K229R or K511R mutants were ineffective (Fig. 2T; Suppl. Figure 11A-B).

Although SUMOylation has been reported to escort NSUN2 into the nucleus [6], we uncovered a previously unrecognized K63-ubiquitination cascade that drove NSUN2 nuclear import in BCa (Fig. 2U). IL-6-triggered acetylation primed NSUN2 for KIF26B engagement, enabling STUB1-mediated K63-ubiquitination at K511 and subsequent KPNA1-dependent nuclear entry. Moreover, we revealed cytoplasmic YBX1 LLPS, not nuclear LLPS [7], as a downstream consequence of this transport pathway and identified a positive feedback loop whereby IL-6 transcriptionally induced its own upstream regulator KIF26B. Thus, KIF26B acts as a non-canonical kinesin that licenses NSUN2 nuclear function and fuels BCa progression.

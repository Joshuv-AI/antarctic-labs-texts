Introduction

Let
F

r

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
denote its distinct embeddings. For a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{M},
the set

\|\sigma(s)\|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\sigma_{i}(s))^{2}},

Let

and shortest nonzero lattice points of
\mathfrak{M}
is given by the set
B_{\mathfrak{M}}:=\{s\in\mathfrak{M}:\|\sigma(s)\|=\min(\mathfrak{M})\}.
A natural questions to ask is: how does
B_{\mathfrak{M}}
look like. One of the motivations for this question comes while obtaining a refined version (see Theorem
3
) of an asymptotic
[
BDS23
, Theorem 1]
for Petersson’s trace formula
[
KL08
, Theorem 5.11]

3.4
) and (

) for a fixed index
i
. It can be easily seen that

Thus having the knowledge of
\min(\mathfrak{b}_{i}\mathfrak{N})
and

help us to refine the asymptotic.

In this regard, we would like to obtain shortest nonzero lattice points for a fractional ideal in a totally real multi-quadratic number field.
First we obtain an explicit expression for
\|\sigma(s)\|
given as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
d_{1},\,\dots\,d_{n}
be positive square-free integers such that
[\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right):\mathbb{Q}]=2^{n}
. Let

be given by

a(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b(i)\sqrt{d_{i}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}c(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}\sqrt{d_{s_{\alpha}}}

(see Section (
2.1
)). Then

\|\sigma(s)\|=\sqrt{2^{n}\left(a(0)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}(b(i))^{2}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}\left(c(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\right)^{2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}d_{s_{\alpha}}\right)}.

As a corollary to the above theorem, we obtain an expression for trace of
s
over
\mathbb{Q}
(
\text{Tr}(s)
) and
\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s).

Corollary 1.1
.

Let

be as in Theorem

\text{Tr}(s)=2^{n}a(0)
and

\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)=2^{2n-1}a(0)^{2}-2^{n-1}\left(a(0)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}(b(i))^{2}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}\left(c(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\right)^{2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}d_{s_{\alpha}}\right).

We then classify shortest nonzero lattice points for fractional ideal
\mathfrak{M}
as rational solutions to a given Diophantine equation involving
\min\left(\mathfrak{M}\right)
. Precisely,
we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
d_{1},\,\dots\,d_{n}
be positive square-free integers with
[\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right):\mathbb{Q}]=2^{n}
. Let

S=\left\{\left(x_{0},x_{1},\,\dots\,,x_{n},y_{2,1},y_{2,2},\,\dots\,,y_{2,{n\choose 2}},y_{3,1},\,\dots\,,y_{n,1}\right)\right\}

be the set of rational solutions of the Diophantine equation

(1.1)

{2^{n}}\left(X_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}\left(Y_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}d_{s_{\alpha}}\right)=(\min\left(\mathfrak{M}\right))^{2}.

Then for any fractional ideal
\mathfrak{M}
of
\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right)
, shortest nonzero lattice points for
\mathfrak{M}
are precisely

\left\{x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\sqrt{d_{i}}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}y_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}\sqrt{d_{s_{\alpha}}}\in\mathfrak{M}\,\Bigg|\,\left(x_{0},x_{1},\,\dots\,,x_{n},y_{2,1},y_{2,2},\,\dots\,,y_{2,{n\choose 2}},y_{3,1},\,\dots\,,y_{n,1}\right)\in S\right\}.

Remark 1
.

As
\sigma(\mathfrak{M})
is a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}},
the Diophantine equation (
1.1
) has at least two solutions.

In Sect.
3
, we discuss examples of
\min(\mathfrak{M})
and shortest nonzero lattice points for some specific integral ideals.
For the Diophantine equation

{2^{n}}\left(X_{0}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}X_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i^{\prime}=2}^{n}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n\choose i^{\prime}}\left(Y_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{i^{\prime}}d_{s_{\alpha}}\right)=(\min\left(\mathfrak{M}\right))^{2},

Other case (when

∎

3.1.
Examples

We start with a lemma concerning lower bound for
\|\sigma(s)\|
which will be primarily used in this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
be a number field of degree
r
. Then for
s\in F,
we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma(s)\|\geq\sqrt{r}\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Proof.

Using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\sqrt{(1^{2}+...+1^{2})(\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}^{2}(s))}\geq\left|\sigma_{1}(s)\right|+...+\left|\sigma_{r}(s)\right|.

AM GM inequality implies

\frac{\left|\sigma_{1}(s)\right|+...+\left|\sigma_{r}(s)\right|}{r}\geq\left(\left|\sigma_{1}(s)\right|\times...\times\left|\sigma_{r}(s)\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}=\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Combining both, we obtain

\|\sigma(s)\|\geq\sqrt{r}\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|^{\frac{1}{r}}.

∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Consider an ideal
\mathfrak{M}=\langle s\rangle
in
\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right)
with
[\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right):\mathbb{Q}]=2^{n}
. Then
\min(\mathfrak{M})\geq 2^{n-1}\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.

Proof.

Let
s^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M},
then
s^{\prime}=st
which implies
\left|\text{Nm}(s^{\prime})\right|\geq\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|.
Hence
\|\sigma(s^{\prime})\|\geq 2^{n-1}\left|\text{Nm}(s)\right|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.
Taking minimum over
s^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}
with
s^{\prime}\neq 0
proves the claim.
∎

Example 1
.

Consider an integral ideal
\mathfrak{M}=\langle s\rangle
in
\mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{d_{1}},\,\dots\,,\sqrt{d_{n}}\right)
with

denote the unit group of its ring of integers. Let

Let

\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{n}
,

•

\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}=\prod_{v|\mathfrak{N}}\omega_{v}\prod_{v\nmid\mathfrak{N}}1
,

•

and
\psi(\mathfrak{N})=[K_{f}:K_{0}(\mathfrak{N})]=\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{N})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{N}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p})}\Big)
.

For a fixed level
\mathfrak{N},

[
BDS23
, Theorem 1]
gives an asymptotic for the above trace formula as
k_{0}\rightarrow\infty
where
k_{0}=\min(k_{1},\,...\,,k_{r})
.
We discuss some notations to be used further.

•

Let us consider

(3.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{i}=\inf\{\|\sigma(s)\|\,:\,s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm,s\neq 0\},

where each
\mathfrak{b_{i}}
is as defined in Theorem
6
. Note that
\min(\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N})=\inf\{\|\sigma(s)\|\,:\,s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm,s\neq 0\}.

•

We take
\tilde{\delta}_{i}=\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\sqrt{r}}
and
let

(3.5)

\displaystyle A_{i}=\cap_{j=1}^{r}\{s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm\,:\,|\sigma_{j}(s)|\leq 2\delta_{i},s\neq 0\}.

For a fixed
i,

A_{i}
is a discrete bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{r}.
This makes
A_{i}
finite for each
i
.

•

We define

\gamma_{j}=\max\left\{\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}u)}\,|\,i=1,\,...\,,t,u\in U,\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}\right\}.

Theorem 7

(
[
BDS23
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
and
\mathfrak{n}
be fixed integral ideals in
F
such that
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1
. Let
\omega:\,F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a unitary Hecke character. Let the conductor of
\omega
divide
\mathfrak{N}
and
\omega_{\infty_{j}}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)^{k_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
. For
k=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
with all
k_{j}>2
, let
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
denote the space of Hilbert cusp forms of weight
k
and character
\omega
with respect to
K_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
and let
\mathcal{F}
be an orthogonal basis for
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions of the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
. Let
A_{i}

\tilde{\delta}_{i}
be as defined above. Let
k_{0}=\min\{k_{j}\,|\,j\leq r\}
, and suppose

satisfy

\frac{2\pi\gamma_{j}\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})}}{\tilde{\delta}_{i}}\in\Big((k_{j}-1)-(k_{j}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}},(k_{j}-1)\Big)\text{ for all }j\leq r.

\frac{e^{2\pi tr_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}{{\psi(\mathfrak{N})}}\Bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(k_{j}-2)!}{(4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})})^{k_{j}-1}}\Bigg]\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\phi}W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}

=\,\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/\mathtt{s})\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathtt{s})}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{u\in U,\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}}\sum_{s\in A_{i}}\Bigg\{\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{i}u\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})

\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}(\eta_{i}u)}}{\mathrm{N}(s)}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}um_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\Big)\Bigg\}+\operatorname{o}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\big(k_{j}-1\big)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\right).

7
is a generalisation of the asymptotic
[

for multi-quadratic number fields.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We have
A_{i}=\cap_{j=1}^{r}\{s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm\,:\,|\sigma_{j}(s)|\leq\frac{\delta_{i}}{\sqrt{r}},s\neq 0\},
where
\delta_{i}=\min(\mathfrak{b}_{i}\mathfrak{N})
. Note that

(3.6)

\|\sigma(s)\|\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\delta_{i}^{2}}{r}}=\delta_{i}

for
s\in A_{i}.

However, by choice of
\delta_{i},
we must have equality in equation (
3.6
) which implies
A_{i}\subset B_{\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}}.
Furthermore we have
|\sigma_{j}(s)|=\frac{\delta_{i}}{\sqrt{r}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r.
By an argument similar to
[
BDS23
, Lemma 10]
, we get
s=\frac{\delta_{i}}{\sqrt{r}}.
Thus
s=\sqrt{\tilde{d}}
for some rational number
\tilde{d}
.
Now using Theorem
2
, it follows that
s
must correspond to a trivial solution to the equation (
1.2
).
In the case of non-existence of a trivial solution to the equation (
1.2
) for some index
i
, we can not have
|\sigma_{j}(s)|=\frac{\delta_{i}}{\sqrt{r}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r.
This makes the set
A_{i}
empty and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem

Let
F
have odd narrow class number. Let

We now consider another application of Theorem
3
to obtain a lower bound for the discrepancy between the Sato-Tate measure and a given discrete measure. This is motivated by the application of
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.6]
to obtain a lower bound like
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.6]
for the classical setting of
F=\mathbb{Q}
. For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p},
let

\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}=\frac{\lambda^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{p}^{l})}}
for a natural number
l.
We have
\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}\in\mathbb{R}
and by the Ramanujan conjecture
\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in[-2,2].
Let
X_{l}
denote Chebyshev polynomial of degree
l
, then by
[
KL08
, Proposition 4.5]
,
X_{l}\left(\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}.

Let us consider the Sato-Tate measure defined by

\mu_{\infty}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}},

for
x\in[-2,2].

Define a weight
w_{\phi}=\frac{|W^{\phi}_{m}(1)|^{2}}{\|\phi\|^{2}},

then
[
KL08
, Theorem 1.1]
states that the

w_{\phi}
-weighted distribution of the eigenvalues
\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is asymptotically uniform relative
to the Sato-Tate measure as
\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{N})
goes to
\infty
.
That is for any continuous function
f:[-2,2]\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

\lim_{\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{N})\rightarrow\infty\atop{(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{N})=1}}\frac{\sum_{\phi}f(\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\phi})w_{\phi}}{\sum_{\phi}w_{\phi}}=\int_{-2}^{2}f(x)\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

A natural question to ask is to get an effective version of the above equidistribution result. For this we define
the discrete measure

\tilde{\nu}_{k,\mathfrak{N}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(4\pi)^{k_{j}-1}}{(k_{j}-2)!}\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\delta_{\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}{\|\phi\|^{2}},

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
and
\mathcal{F}
be an orthogonal basis for
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions of the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
.
Given two probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on a closed interval
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R},
the discrepancy between
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
is given by

D(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\sup\{|\mu_{1}(I)-\mu_{2}(I)|\,:\,I=[a,b]\subset\Omega\}.

We obtain the following variant of
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.6]
and
[
BDS23
, Theorem 3]
for the space

which gives us information about the rate of convergence of
\tilde{\nu}_{k_{l},\mathfrak{N}}
to
\mu_{\infty}.

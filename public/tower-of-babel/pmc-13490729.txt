From difficulty score to clinical outcome: Evaluating IWATE-criteria correlation with postoperative complications and revision surgery in anatomical liver resection
Authors: Saeed Aldarwish, Clemens Schafmayer, Sebastian Hinz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgrounds/AimsMinimally invasive hepatobiliary surgery has advanced considerably, with laparoscopic liver resection now widely adopted. The IWATE difficulty score is commonly used to classify the technical complexity of laparoscopic resections; however, its ability to predict postoperative morbidity across different surgical approaches remains uncertain. This study aimed to evaluate the association between the IWATE score and postoperative complications and revision surgery following anatomical liver resection (ALR), irrespective of surgical approach.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 168 patients who underwent ALR between 2014 and 2018 at a tertiary hepatobiliary center. Resections were stratified according to the IWATE score. For open procedures, the score was modified by subtracting one point to approximate the hand-assisted category, as an exploratory adjustment. Perioperative outcomes, including overall postoperative complications and revision surgery, were analyzed using univariate and multivariable logistic regression.ResultsHigher IWATE difficulty was independently associated with increased postoperative complications (odds ratio [OR] 3.69, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.58–8.64; p = 0.002) and revision surgery (OR 2.72, 95% CI 1.37–5.41; p = 0.004). Laparoscopic resections were associated with reduced blood loss, lower transfusion rates, and improved postoperative recovery. Receiver operating characteristic analysis demonstrated good predictive performance of the IWATE score (area under the curve 0.87 and 0.79).ConclusionsThe IWATE score is independently associated with postoperative morbidity and revision surgery following ALR and may serve as a universal preoperative risk stratification tool across surgical approaches. Prospective validation is warranted.Keywords: Open liver resection, Laparoscopic liver resection, Postoperative complications, Reoperation, IWATE-criteria

INTRODUCTION

Understanding segmental liver anatomy, minimizing intraoperative blood loss, and advancing parenchymal-sparing liver resection techniques have been fundamental to the progress of modern liver surgery, leading to reduced morbidity rates. Controlling segmental blood inflow through an anatomical approach allows for precise mapping of the dissection plane, facilitating resection. In recent decades, minimally invasive liver surgery has expanded dramatically; however, the learning curve for complex liver resections remains relatively flat, making the training process slow and demanding. Additionally, training programs for these advanced techniques are typically limited to high-volume, specialized liver centers. To evaluate the difficulty level of laparoscopic liver resections, numerous scoring systems have been proposed [1,2]. Among these, the IWATE scoring system is one of the most widely validated and applicable worldwide [3]. It has demonstrated reliability in robotic liver resections as well [4,5]. However, for open liver resections, there is currently no officially recognized or globally validated scoring system [6]. In this study, we aimed to assess the correlation between the IWATE score and postoperative outcomes. Additionally, we analyzed the advantages and disadvantages of both open and laparoscopic approaches based on the IWATE operative difficulty level.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and patient selection

This retrospective single-center cohort study included all patients admitted to our department with benign or malignant liver lesions between January 2014 and December 2018 who underwent elective anatomical liver resection. Out of 270 screened patients, 168 met the inclusion criteria and were included in this analysis. A patient flow diagram detailing the exclusion reasons is provided in Fig. 1.

Of 270 reviewed anatomical liver resections, 168 met the inclusion criteria and were included in the analysis. The specific reasons for exclusion are summarized in the figure.

Institutional strategy for selection of operative approach

An open approach was preferred for patients with a history of previous open upper abdominal surgery, suspected tumor infiltration of a major vascular structure, large tumors, those deemed unsuitable for capnoperitoneum, or when vascular reconstruction was planned. Our institute serves as a reference center for hepatobiliary surgery and liver transplantation in our federal state. The surgical team consisted of three double/triple board-certified hepatobiliary surgeons with advanced expertise (20–25 years of experience) and one double board-certified fellow with intermediate experience (3 years).

Application of the IWATE score and grouping

Operative difficulty was assessed using the IWATE score, which considers tumor size, tumor location, proximity to major vessels, extent of liver resection, liver function/parenchymal damage, and the use of hand-assisted or hybrid techniques. For an exploratory comparison between laparoscopic and open procedures, one point was subtracted from the IWATE score for open resections to pragmatically approximate the hand-assisted category. Since this adaptation has not been validated for open liver surgery, it was treated as an exploratory methodological modification and interpreted cautiously. Patients were categorized into intermediate, advanced, and expert difficulty groups. Due to the limited sample size, the advanced and expert groups were combined for the main comparative analyses. Additionally, sensitivity analyses treating the IWATE score in a more continuous or ordinal manner were explored.

Variables and endpoint definitions

Preoperative variables recorded included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) class, diagnosis (benign vs. malignant), preoperative chemotherapy within eight weeks, history of previous upper abdominal surgery, previous liver surgery, bilirubin level, and chronic liver disease. Chronic structural liver disease was defined based on available clinical documentation and/or perioperative histopathologic findings, such as cirrhosis, fibrosis, and documented steatotic liver changes. Additional baseline liver function variables, including Child-Pugh score, indocyanine green testing, and FibroScan, were not consistently available for the historical cohort, making them challenging to analyze uniformly.

Intraoperative variables recorded included operative approach, operative time, extent of resection, use of the Pringle maneuver, estimated blood loss, hemoglobin drop, transfusion requirements, and conversion from laparoscopic to open surgery. A threshold of 350 mL for estimated blood loss was chosen as a pragmatic dichotomization point based on cohort distribution and clinical relevance. This decision aligns with previous liver resection studies that identified an intraoperative blood loss of around 300 mL as a significant threshold associated with increased postoperative risk [7,8].

The primary endpoint was overall postoperative complications, defined as any complication occurring after surgery according to the Clavien-Dindo classification (grades I–V vs. none). Complications were also reported by Clavien-Dindo grade as absolute numbers and percentages. Liver-specific complications were defined based on standardized criteria, where documentation permitted, including the International Study Group of Liver Surgery terminology for bile leaks and post-hepatectomy liver failure. The secondary endpoint was revision surgery, defined as reoperation due to postoperative complications. Two-year recurrence was assessed descriptively, based on routine follow-up imaging and clinical documentation. According to our institutional standard operating procedures, follow-up during the first two years after surgery for hepatobiliary malignancy was routinely conducted at our institution. Follow-up for the subsequent three years could continue in the ambulatory oncology setting.

Handling of conversions

Laparoscopic anatomic liver resection cases that were converted to an open approach were excluded from the final comparative analyses. Conversion was defined as any procedure that began laparoscopically but could not be completed using the planned minimally invasive approach, necessitating open surgery. The number of conversions and their reasons were recorded and are reported separately in the Results section.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Group comparisons were performed based on distribution, utilizing the t-test, Mann-Whitney U-test, analysis of variance, or Kruskal-Wallis test as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test when applicable. Multivariable logistic regression was conducted for two binary outcomes: overall postoperative complications and revision surgery. In the expanded models, clinically relevant preoperative and intraoperative covariates were included, such as IWATE category, operative approach, sex, age > 60 years, BMI > 25 kg/m², ASA ≥ 3, bilirubin > 30 µmol/L, malignant disease, preoperative chemotherapy, previous liver surgery, previous upper abdominal surgery, chronic structural liver changes, major resection (≥ 4 segments), Pringle maneuver (recorded when applied more than three times, using cycles of 15 minutes of inflow occlusion followed by 5-minute reperfusion intervals), blood loss > 350 mL, hemoglobin drop of ≥ 2 mmol/L, and transfusion requirement. Odds ratios (ORs), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p-values are reported. Due to the limited number of revision events, the fully adjusted revision model is considered exploratory. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Ethical approval

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Rostock, Germany (Registration No. A 2023-0129). Patient consent was waived as this is a retrospective analytical study.

RESULTS

Distribution of operative difficulty and approach

This study analyzed 168 anatomical liver resection (ALR) procedures performed from January 2014 to December 2018, which included 118 open surgeries and 50 laparoscopic surgeries. Based on the IWATE score, most procedures were classified as advanced or expert level, with a notable predominance of higher-difficulty cases in the open group. This pattern reflects the institutional case selection during the study period and highlights the non-randomized nature of the comparison between open and laparoscopic approaches.

Patient characteristics

Among the patients, 61% were male and 39% were female, with a higher proportion of females undergoing laparoscopic surgery (p = 0.027). The mean age was 64 ± 13.4 years, and age greater than 60 was not associated with increased complications (p = 0.15). Sixty-six percent were classified as either overweight (45%) or obese (21%). BMI > 25 kg/m2 did not correlate with an increased complication rate (p = 0.9). However, preoperative bilirubin levels above 30 µmol/L were associated with a higher rate of postoperative complications (p = 0.039). Malignancy was the primary indication for liver resection in both open (68%) and laparoscopic (76%) approaches (p = 0.4) (Table 1). Preoperative chemotherapy within eight weeks was administered to 26% of patients. A history of prior upper abdominal surgery was noted in 62% of patients undergoing laparoscopic resection and 53% of those undergoing open resection, indicating a slightly higher rate in the laparoscopic group (p = 0.05). Patients with a history of previous liver resection were more frequently managed with an open approach (27% for open vs. 8% for laparoscopic, p = 0.018). Chronic structural liver changes, such as cirrhosis, fibrosis, or non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, were identified in 8% of all patients at the time of presentation. Most patients presented with poor physical status, with 61% categorized as ASA 3, 33% as ASA 2, 4% as ASA 4, and only 2% as ASA 1. Higher ASA grades correlated with increased postoperative complications (p = 0.041).

Types and complexity of liver resections

The most common procedures performed were bisegmentectomies (33%), hemihepatectomies (29%), and monosegmentectomies (21%). According to the IWATE score, 64% of resections were classified as advanced, 15% as expert level, and 21% as intermediate. The average operative time was 302.3 ± 129.4 minutes, with open procedures generally having comparable or shorter times than laparoscopic ones (Table 2). Intraoperative blood loss was ≤ 350 mL in 80% of cases, and transfusions were required in 17% of patients. Open procedures were associated with higher blood loss than laparoscopic ones (p = 0.008), a trend that was consistent across subgroups (Table 2). Additionally, blood loss exceeding 350 mL was significantly correlated with increased rates of postoperative complications (p = 0.045) and longer intensive care unit (ICU) stays (p = 0.044).

Conversion from laparoscopic to open approach

A total of 6 laparoscopic cases were converted to an open approach. The conversions were due to advanced intra-abdominal adhesions from previous abdominal surgeries in four cases and uncontrolled intraoperative bleeding in two cases.

Postoperative outcomes

The mean ICU stay was 5.2 ± 10 days (interquartile range [IQR]: 1–4), with a mean floor stay of 13.7 ± 17 days (IQR: 6–16). Open procedures led to significantly longer ICU and floor stays than laparoscopic ones (p < 0.001 for both). The overall complication rate was 52% (34% liver-specific and 66% general complications). Complications were significantly higher for open resections (65% complications) compared to laparoscopic ones (26%; p < 0.001). Revision surgery was needed for 18% of cases, primarily following open procedures (25% for open vs. 4% for laparoscopic, p < 0.001). These trends were consistent across subgroups (Table 2–5). Multivariable analysis

In the multivariable analysis of overall postoperative complications, the advanced/expert IWATE category was independently associated with increased postoperative morbidity compared to the intermediate difficulty category (OR 3.69, 95% CI 1.58–8.64; p = 0.002). Preoperative chemotherapy was also independently linked to postoperative complications (OR 2.75, 95% CI 1.37–5.41; p = 0.014). In contrast, the laparoscopic approach showed only a borderline association with reduced morbidity (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.27–1.01; p = 0.052; Table 8).

Complication rates by IWATE difficulty level

A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis indicated that more complex procedures, as measured by the IWATE score, had higher complication rates for both open and laparoscopic approaches (AUC: 0.5–1; Fig. 2). A separate ROC analysis further confirmed that increased procedural difficulty was associated with higher revision rates for both approaches (AUC: 0.5–1; Fig. 3). Therefore, more complex resections, defined by the IWATE score, were linked to elevated rates of postoperative complications and revisions in open liver resections as well.

Preoperative MELD and overall postoperative complication rate

The MELD score, calculated from bilirubin, creatinine, and international normalized ratio, was significantly associated with postoperative complications. Patients who experienced postoperative complications had higher MELD scores than those without complications (mean 9.99 vs. 7.87; median 8.38 vs. 6.96; Mann–Whitney U p < 0.001). In univariable logistic regression, each 1-point increase in MELD was associated with a 24.5% increase in the odds of postoperative complications (OR 1.245, 95% CI 1.099–1.410; p < 0.001). ROC analysis demonstrated that the MELD score had moderate discriminatory ability for postoperative complications, with an AUC of 0.672 (Fig. 4). This finding suggests that higher MELD values were linked to an increased likelihood of postoperative morbidity, although the predictive performance was notably lower than that observed for assessments based on operative difficulty.

Histopathological and recurrence findings

Histopathological analysis showed R0 resection in 85% of malignant tumors, R1 in 14%, and R2 in 1%. There was no significant difference between open and laparoscopic procedures regarding R0 status (82% for open vs. 92% for laparoscopic, p = 0.085; Table 2). The recurrence rate for malignancy was 35% across all patients (31% for open vs. 24% for laparoscopic, p = 0.69).

DISCUSSION

The present analysis demonstrates that the IWATE difficulty score strongly correlates with overall postoperative morbidity and the need for revision surgery after ALR, regardless of the operative approach. It also highlights the challenge of adapting a score developed for laparoscopic liver resection to the open setting. The pragmatic subtraction of one point for open procedures was introduced to approximate the hand-assisted component of the original IWATE system; however, this modification remains unvalidated and should be considered exploratory. While this adaptation allowed for comparative analyses across approaches, it limits the extent to which firm conclusions can be drawn about the score’s universal applicability.

Although various patient-related factors, such as age, gender distribution, and BMI, were examined, none of these variables showed a decisive influence on postoperative complications compared to the impact of operative difficulty. The ASA classification, however, remained a meaningful clinical predictor, as patients with higher ASA grades exhibited increased overall postoperative morbidity, consistent with the well-established vulnerability of patients with multiple comorbidities to perioperative deterioration [9]. This study found a significant correlation between elevated preoperative serum bilirubin levels (greater than 30 µmol/L) and higher rates of postoperative complications. Previous investigations have shown that liver resections conducted in the presence of cholestasis negatively impact liver regeneration by downregulating the expression of key proliferative genes and transcription factors essential for hepatocyte proliferation, such as C/EBP and cyclin E [10,11].

Although open resections were more frequently chosen for patients with higher difficulty levels, the operative approach itself was less significant than the preoperative IWATE classification in predicting postoperative outcomes. However, our data should be interpreted with caution. The choice between open and laparoscopic surgery was not randomized and was heavily influenced by institutional case selection and perceived operative complexity.

Only a small subgroup of patients presented with chronic structural liver disease, and these individuals typically underwent parenchyma-sparing procedures, which do not reflect the typical range of anatomical resections included in the IWATE framework.

This analysis demonstrates a significant association between preoperative MELD score and postoperative complications following anatomical liver resection. This finding aligns with previous reports indicating that MELD correlates with adverse outcomes after liver resections and the risk of post-hepatectomy liver failure, supporting the role of MELD as a clinically relevant marker of impaired preoperative hepatic reserve [12,13]. The MELD score was significantly associated with the occurrence of postoperative decompensated liver function. Patients who experienced postoperative decompensation had higher MELD scores than those who did not (mean 12.66 vs. 8.61; median 10.68 vs. 7.19; Mann–Whitney U p = 0.00027). In univariable logistic regression, each 1-point increase in MELD was linked to a 21.5% increase in the odds of postoperative decompensated liver function (OR 1.215, 95% CI 1.084–1.361; p = 0.00081).

Exposure to oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy within eight weeks before surgery for downstaging colorectal liver metastases did not appear to influence the choice of surgical approach or the overall postoperative complication rate in our cohort. However, this finding should be interpreted cautiously due to the limited power for subgroup-specific analyses. While previous studies have reported an increased risk of perioperative complications, including greater intraoperative blood loss, postoperative liver dysfunction, and bile leakage after oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy, this factor was not used as a formal criterion in selecting the surgical approach in the present study [14,15].

Furthermore, higher IWATE scores were associated with increased intraoperative blood loss, longer operative duration, extended ICU and hospital stays, and greater overall perioperative stress, regardless of the surgical approach.

Wound-related complications significantly contributed to revision procedures after open resection, while such complications were nearly nonexistent following laparoscopic surgery. Despite the evident advantages of specific procedures, clinical outcomes were primarily influenced by the complexity of the planned resection, as indicated by the IWATE score. This trend persisted when considering oncologic parameters: both surgical approaches achieved comparable R0 resection rates and similar recurrence patterns during the 2-year follow-up, but the perioperative course was more strongly influenced by IWATE-defined operative complexity than by surgical access alone.

Given its strong correlation with postoperative complications and the need for revision surgery in both approaches, the IWATE score may be clinically useful for preoperative risk stratification in anatomical liver resection and should be prospectively evaluated as an integrated risk stratification tool in hepatobiliary surgery. However, its current role should be viewed as supportive rather than definitive. Additional evidence from other analyses further reinforces the reliability of these findings [16-19].

Limitations of this study

This study has several limitations. First, it is a retrospective single-center analysis, making it susceptible to selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Second, the operative approach was not randomly assigned; open surgery was preferentially used for more complex cases, which limits direct comparisons between the approaches. Third, the exploratory adaptation of the IWATE score for open surgery—by subtracting one point—has not been validated. Finally, subgroup analyses, especially within approach-specific cohorts and IWATE subgroups, were constrained by sample size and should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusion

In this retrospective cohort study, higher IWATE difficulty was independently associated with an increased overall rate of postoperative complications and operative revisions following anatomical liver resection, regardless of whether the procedure was laparoscopic or open. This finding indicates that the IWATE score may serve as a valuable preoperative indicator of technical complexity and perioperative risk. However, due to the influence of selection bias and an exploratory adaptation of the score in the comparison between open and laparoscopic surgery, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Prospective validation in contemporary multicenter cohorts is necessary.

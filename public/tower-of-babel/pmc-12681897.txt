Intraoperative neurophysiology monitoring in posterior spinal fusion: insight from a Ghanaian patient with Brown-Séquard Syndrome
Authors: Bright Worlanyo Aklamanu, Clement Okyere Sefa, Ebenezer Kodji Tetteh, Kwadzo Poku Yankey
Published: 2025

Brown-Séquard Syndrome (BSS) is a rare neurological condition resulting from hemisection or unilateral damage to the spinal cord, typically characterized by motor weakness or paralysis on one side of the body, along with loss of proprioception and vibration sense on the same side, and a loss of pain and temperature sensation on the opposite side (Lockhart & Davis, 2015). It is most commonly caused by trauma, tumors, or demyelinating diseases, and its management often involves surgical intervention when structural lesions are present (Alrabiah et al., 2021). Due to its complex and asymmetric presentation, intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring (IONM) is critical to avoid further deterioration and post-operative neurological complications. Despite its proven value, documentation of IONM use in complex spinal pathologies within sub-Saharan Africa is extremely limited. We report the first documented case of IONM in a Ghanaian patient with Brown-Séquard Syndrome (BSS) undergoing posterior spinal fusion, illustrating its clinical importance in preventing intraoperative neurological deterioration.

A 32-year-old woman presented with a year-long history of lower limb weakness and sensory disturbance following a road traffic accident. Neurological examination prior to surgery showed that the patient had good muscle strength in both upper limbs with no noted deficits. Trunk control was good, but significant motor deficits were evident in the lower limbs. Pain and temperature sensation on the right lower limb was intact and sensory level on the left was around T8. Sensation was impaired in the left lower limb, and reflex assessments were consistent with lower motor neuron involvement. This asymmetric motor weakness more pronounced on the right side with contralateral (left-sided) sensory loss is characteristic of BSS, confirming the presence of a hemicord spinal lesion affecting the ipsilateral corticospinal and dorsal column tracts, and the contralateral spinothalamic pathways. Preoperative X-rays taken (anteroposterior and lateral views) showing post trauma T6/T7 instability. Given the progressive symptoms and radiological findings, posterior spinal fusion (T5–T8) and laminectomy at T6/T7 were planned.

Multimodality intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring was employed during the surgery, utilizing somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEPs), Transcranial motor evoked potentials (TCMEPs), and Electromyography (EMG). After intubation, subdermal needle electrodes were used for recording during the neurophysiological monitoring. The right and left ulnar nerves were individually stimulated at a rate of 2.92 Hz, with an intensity of 30 mA. The cathode was placed between the tendons of the palmaris longus and the flexor carpi radialis muscles, 2 cm proximal to the wrist crease. The anode was placed 3 cm distal to the cathode or on the dorsal surface of the wrist. Recording electrodes were positioned at CP3, CP4, CPz, Fpz, and SC5 using bipolar recording techniques.

Similarly, subdermal needle electrodes were used to record responses following the stimulation of the right and left posterior tibial nerves, stimulated at 2.92 Hz with an intensity of 40 mA. The cathode was placed over the posterior portion of the medial surface of the ankle, 2 cm distal and posterior to the medial malleolus. The anode was placed 3 cm distal to the cathode. MEP stimulation was performed using subdermal needle electrodes placed anterior to C3 and C4, and subdermal needle electrodes were positioned to record MEPs from the Abductor Pollicis Brevis, Medial Gastrocnemius, Tibialis Anterior, Vastus Lateralis, Rectus Femoris and Abductor Hallucis muscles. The intensity used for MEP stimulation was 650 mA. Additionally, free-run EMG was performed, with spontaneous muscle activity recorded bilaterally from these same muscles.

Baseline recordings showed intact upper limb SSEPs and MEPs, absent lower limb SSEPs, and preserved MEPs in the left lower limb only, consistent with the patient’s preoperative neurological status. Three hours after incision (2:08GMT), a sudden loss of left lower limb MEPs was observed following rod placement (Fig. 1). Immediate multidisciplinary response included increasing mean arterial pressure from 88 to 96 mmHg, administering corticosteroids, and reviewing intraoperative imaging, which revealed a malpositioned right T5 pedicle screw. The screw was replaced with a shorter trajectory, and within 36 min, left lower limb MEPs returned to baseline, indicating functional recovery. SSEPs remained absent throughout, reflecting the known dorsal column involvement in BSS. The remainder of the procedure and postoperative course were uneventful, with stable neurological status and no new deficits.

Intraoperative MEP trace showing transient loss and recovery of lower limb signals during posterior spinal fusion.

This case underscores the essential role of IONM in detecting iatrogenic neural injury, particularly in patients with pre-existing spinal cord compromises. Without monitoring, intraoperative injury to the patient’s only functional corticospinal tract could have gone unnoticed, potentially leading to bilateral paralysis. The ability of MEPs to reflect real-time motor pathway integrity was pivotal, as SSEPs alone would not have detected this transient event (Lieberman et al., 2019). In resource-limited settings, the integration of IONM remains a challenge due to cost, equipment availability, and limited local expertise (Aklamanu et al., 2025). However, as demonstrated here, its use can be life-altering, preventing irreversible neurological deficits and improving surgical outcomes. Wider adoption of multimodal IONM in high-risk spinal surgeries across sub-Saharan Africa should be a regional priority, supported by local training programs and institutional investment.

In conclusion, this case highlights the transformative potential of IONM in spinal surgery within low-resource environments. The transient loss and subsequent recovery of lower limb MEPs following corrective action illustrate how real-time monitoring directly influences intraoperative decision-making and patient outcomes. Beyond its diagnostic value, IONM serves as an intraoperative safeguard, offering neurosurgical teams a means to preserve function in complex spinal procedures.

Local field study on the effectiveness and safety of milbemycin oxime and lotilaner (Credelio® Plus) against canine gastrointestinal nematodes in the Republic of Korea
Authors: Subin Lee, Jinyeong Park, Chong-Ho Kim, Kyung-Duk Min, Hakhyun Kim, Seung-Hun Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This field study evaluated the effectiveness and safety of a single dose of a combination of milbemycin oxime and lotilaner (Credelio® Plus) against Toxocara canis, Trichuris vulpis, and Ancylostoma caninum in dogs under field conditions in Korea. This clinical study was conducted with client-owned dogs under field conditions from April 2023 to May 2024. The drug was administered on Day 0, and fecal samples were collected on Days − 7, 0, 7, and 14. Samples were analyzed using fecal flotation for qualitative assessment and the modified McMaster method for quantitative determination of egg counts. A total of 25 dogs from 25 different households were enrolled. The geometric mean reduction rates for all target nematode species and total infections were 100%. No adverse events or significant changes in body weight, physical examination findings, or hematology and serum chemistry parameters were observed. The results of this study support the effectiveness and safety of the drug against gastrointestinal nematodes in dogs under field conditions in Korea. These results indicate that this combination product may serve as a valuable tool in veterinary parasite control, ultimately contributing to improved canine health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00436-025-08595-w.

Introduction

Gastrointestinal nematodes, including Toxocara canis, Trichuris vulpis, and Ancylostoma caninum, are common parasites in dogs with a global distribution (Cervone et al. 2024; Eslahi et al. 2020; Traversa 2012). These parasites cause toxocarosis, trichuriosis, and ancylostomosis, respectively (Despommier 2003; Mohd-Shaharuddin et al. 2019; Traub et al. 2008). A recent study on gastrointestinal nematode infections in dogs in the Republic of Korea (Korea) revealed the nationwide presence of these nematodes and emphasized regular diagnosis and treatment to reduce parasite burden and risk of transmission (Lee et al. 2024).

T. canis is an ascarid nematode that infects dogs, primarily inhabiting the small intestine (Daryani et al. 2009). T. canis is transmitted by various routes, including fecal-oral, transmammary, and transplacental transmission (Despommier 2003). Infection with T. canis can result in clinical signs such as poor growth, pot-bellied appearance, and intermittent diarrhea (Despommier 2003). Importantly, T. canis is an important zoonotic parasite and larvae are capable of causing visceral larva migrans by migrating through critical organs such as the liver, lungs, and brain, and ocular larva migrans, leading to eye damage and optic nerve involvement (Zhu et al. 1998).

T. vulpis, commonly known as the whipworm, is characterized by a distinctly thick and broad posterior end that remains free within the intestinal lumen, and a long, filamentous anterior end that embeds into the mucosa of the cecum and colon of the infected host (Traversa 2011). This parasite is one of the most commonly reported gastrointestinal nematodes in dogs and is typically found in the large intestine (Cervone et al. 2024; Traversa 2011). Their eggs are extremely resistant to environmental conditions and are a continuous cause of reinfection (D. Barutzki and R. Schaper 2003). Transmission occurs via the fecal-oral route by ingestion of infective eggs. The pathogenicity of T. vulpis in dogs is controversial, and severe cases are often associated with the age of the dog and co-infections with other parasites, including A. caninum (Cervone et al. 2024). The zoonotic potential of T. vulpis also remains uncertain (Traversa 2011).

A. caninum is the most common hookworm that is species-specific to dogs (Traversa 2012). Transmission occurs primarily through the ingestion of paratenic hosts such as rodents and insects, ingestion or skin penetration of infective L3 larvae, and the transmammary route to newborn puppies (Jimenez Castro et al. 2019; Nezami et al. 2023; Stone and Girardeau 1968). This parasite attaches to the intestinal mucosa with its buccal capsule that contains sharp teeth and has a blood-sucking activity for feeding. As a result, infections caused by A. caninum can manifest as conditions ranging from mild enteritis to severe, potentially fatal hemorrhagic diarrhea accompanied by anemia, particularly in young puppies (Bowman et al. 2010). In addition to the veterinary significance, A. caninum is a zoonotic parasite that can cause patent infection, as well as cutaneous larva migrans in humans (Bowman et al. 2010; Furtado et al. 2020).

Milbemycin oxime, a macrocyclic lactone, was originally developed for the treatment of adult canine intestinal nematode infections, including T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum (Riggs and Wiseman 2021). It belongs to a class of antibiotics produced by the fermentation of Streptomyces hygroscopicus subspecies aureolacrimosus (Lee and Terada 1992). The mechanism of action involves binding to glutamate-gated chloride ion channels, which are essential for neurotransmission in nematodes. This interaction induces hyperpolarization of nerve and muscle cells, resulting in paralysis and death of the nematodes (Wolstenholme and Rogers 2005). In addition to being effective against intestinal nematodes, milbemycin oxime is widely used for the monthly prevention of heartworm disease (Dirofilaria immitis), underscoring its role as an essential element in veterinary parasite management (Riggs and Wiseman 2021).

Milbemycin oxime is widely recognized as an effective agent for the control of intestinal nematodes worldwide. However, parasite populations from different countries may exhibit genetic diversity, which could affect the efficacy of therapeutic interventions (Fava et al. 2020; Garcia-R et al. 2017; Quintana et al. 2023). Therefore, it is imperative to conduct local evaluations to verify whether milbemycin oxime remains effective against the gastrointestinal nematodes present in Korea. This field study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of an investigational veterinary product (IVP), Credelio® Plus (Elanco, IN, USA), containing milbemycin oxime and lotilaner against gastrointestinal nematodes (T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum) in client-owned dogs in Korea.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the World Association for the Advancement of Veterinary Parasitology (WAAVP) guidelines (Beugnet et al. 2022). The Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee approval was obtained prior to the initiation of the study. The approval numbers were as follows: Chungbuk National University (CBNUA-2087-23-01 and CBNUA-2264-24-01) and Elanco Animal Health (1976 and 2174). This clinical study was approved by Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency, Korea (Clinical trial number: A221512).

Study design

This clinical study was conducted under field conditions without control group between April 2023 and May 2024. Client-owned dogs were enrolled, and only one dog per household was eligible for participation to avoid duplication of data. However, all dogs in the household were treated simultaneously to minimize environmental contamination and reinfection. Dogs of any breed and sex were enrolled, and their management and care were consistent with the owner’s usual practices.

Animal eligibility criteria included: dogs at least eight weeks old and weighing at least 1.4 kg; owner agreement to participate in the study with signed informed consent; presence of parasite eggs of at least one of T. canis, T. vulpis, or A. caninum in fecal samples taken on Day − 7.

Dogs were excluded from the study if they met any of the following criteria: pregnant, suspected of being pregnant, or lactating; less than eight weeks old or weighing less than 1.4 kg; diagnosed with any condition that could potentially affect the efficacy or safety of the treatment (e.g., malignant tumors, systemic infection, severe hepatic or renal impairment); received anthelmintics, ectoparasiticides, or other relevant medications within four weeks prior to the study; any other condition or factor that could affect the results of the study.

Fecal sample collection

Fresh fecal samples were collected from each dog in 50 ml conical tubes using sterilized cotton swabs or sticks on Day − 7 and on Days 0, 7, and 14 for enrolled dogs. The day IVP was administered was designated as Day 0. At each scheduled study time point (Days − 7, 0, 7, and 14), fecal samples were collected from recently defecated depots. The samples were transported in cool packs to the College of Veterinary Medicine at Chungbuk National University, Korea, and stored at 4 °C until further processing.

Qualitative and quantitative fecal examinations

Fecal samples from dogs were analyzed qualitatively using light microscopy and the fecal flotation method with fully saturated sodium nitrate solution (specific gravity 1.33) to detect gastrointestinal nematodes on Day − 7. A positive result on screening on Day − 7 (i.e., > 0 parasite eggs of T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum) allowed the dog to be enrolled in the study and subsequent Day 0 fecal sampling and dosing.

Quantitative analysis was conducted using the modified McMaster method to measure egg counts (eggs per gram, EPG) for T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum on Days − 7, 0, 7, and 14, with a detection limit of 25 EPG (Taylor et al. 2015). All examinations were performed by trained veterinarians. Species identification was performed based on the morphological characteristics of the nematode eggs, and all observed parasite eggs were counted. Data were recorded as the number of EPG of feces for each nematode species.

Treatment

Dogs with positive fecal screening results received the IVP orally on Day 0. The palatable chewable tablet was administered to the dogs with food. The IVP tablets were available in five strengths, providing dosage ranges of 20–41 mg lotilaner/kg body weight (bw) and 0.75–1.53 mg milbemycin oxime/kg bw.

Safety evaluation

The safety of IVP was evaluated by body weight measurements, physical examinations, hematology and serum chemistry analyses, and adverse event monitoring. Body weights of each dog were recorded on Days 0 and 7. Physical examinations were conducted on Days 0, 7, and 14 to assess the general condition, musculoskeletal system, respiratory system, urogenital system, eyes, lymph nodes, weight, skin, cardiovascular system, gastrointestinal system, nervous system, ears, and mucous membranes. Results were categorized as normal or abnormal.

Blood samples for hematology and serum chemistry were collected on Days 0 and 7. The hematology profile included total white blood cell (WBC) count, differential WBC count (neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils), erythrocyte count (red blood cell count, RBC), hemoglobin concentration, hematocrit, mean cell volume, mean cell hemoglobin, mean cell hemoglobin concentration, and thrombocyte (platelet) count. The serum chemistry profile included albumin, alkaline phosphatase, alanine aminotransferase, amylase, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, globulin, glucose, total bilirubin, total protein, sodium, potassium, calcium, and phosphorus. Of the 25 dogs tested, 24 were evaluated using the same equipment, such as ProCyte Dx (IDEXX Laboratories, Westbrook, MA, USA) for hematology and Hitachi 3100 automatic biochemical analyzer (Hitachi High-Tech Science Systems Inc., Tokyo, Japan) for serum chemistry, while one was evaluated using different equipment, such as BC-2800 Vet (Mindray, Guangdong, China) for hematology and Catalyst One (IDEXX Laboratories, Westbrook, MA, USA) for serum chemistry. This resulted in variations in the reference range and missing values for certain parameters. All measurements were combined for statistical analysis, and parameters without reference ranges were excluded from analysis.

Dog owners were instructed to report any unusual observations after IVP administration. All adverse events were recorded and reviewed to assess potential treatment-related effects.

Statistical methodology

The safety and effectiveness evaluation included all dogs that received IVP. Effectiveness was evaluated by comparing the geometric mean fecal egg count reduction rates between pre-treatment (Days − 7 and 0) and post-treatment (Days 7 and 14). Treatment effectiveness was defined as a reduction rate of ≥ 90% with statistical significance according to WAAVP guidelines (Beugnet et al. 2022). The reduction rate (%) was calculated as: [(GMpre − GMpost)/GMpre] × 100, where GMpre are the geometric means of fecal egg counts for pre- and post-treatment samples, respectively. The reduction rates were calculated for both individual nematode species and total nematode infections. A natural logarithmic transformation [ln(count + 1)] was applied to the egg counts to account for the skewed data distribution and to include zero counts in the analysis.

For safety evaluation, mean body weights on Days 0 and 7 were calculated and statistical analysis using Student t-test was performed to determine statistical significance between these values. Additionally, hematology and serum chemistry analyses were conducted and the frequency and percentage of animals with out-of-range values were evaluated using the McNemar test.

Results

Dog demographics

A total of 25 dogs, representing 25 different households, were enrolled in this study (Table S1). All enrolled dogs were included in the effectiveness and safety evaluable population. Of these, 40.0% (10/25) were purebred, and 60.0% (15/25) were mixed-breed dogs. 44.0% (11/25) of dogs were female and 56.0% (14/25) of dogs were male. The age of the dogs ranged from six to 108 months, with a mean age of 37 months. Body weights ranged from 2.0 to 40.0 kg, with mean body weights of 8.2 kg (s.d. = 7.6) and 8.3 kg (s.d. = 7.6) on Day 0 and Day 7, respectively (Table 1).

Additionally, mixed infections were observed in 20.0% (5/25) of the dogs: 8.0% (2/25) of T. canis + T. vulpis, and 12.0% (3/25) of T. vulpis + A. caninum (Table 2). The most frequently observed nematode egg was 52.0% (13/25) of T. vulpis, followed by 36.0% (9/25) of A. caninum (Table 2).

Effectiveness evaluation of IVP

A total of 25 dogs received IVP were evaluated for effectiveness against gastrointestinal nematodes. The mean dose rate of milbemycin oxime was 1.04 mg/kg, and all enrolled dogs received the expected dose in the correction dose range (0.75–1.53 mg/kg).

The geometric means of nematode egg counts at pre-treatment and post-treatment were calculated for each of the three species (T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum) and for all nematodes combined (Table 3). The geometric mean number of EPG at pre-treatment was 787.1 for T. canis, 227.8 for T. vulpis, 465.0 for A. caninum, and 510.9 for total infection, respectively. After treatment, the geometric mean number of EPGs was 0 for all nematodes. The geometric mean reduction rates for all nematode species and total infections were 100%, demonstrating statistical significance (p < 0.0001).

Safety evaluation of IVP

The mean difference in body weight between Day 0 and Day 7 was 0.1 kg (s.d. = 0.1). The Student t-test showed no statistically significant difference between the mean body weights on Day 0 (8.2 kg ± 7.6) and Day 7 (8.3 kg ± 7.6) (p > 0.05).

Veterinarians conducted comprehensive physical examinations on all dogs during the study. No abnormal findings were observed, indicating that the treatment had no noticeable effect on vital signs or general health. No adverse events were reported during the study period.

Hematology and serum chemistry were performed. The results showed no treatment-related effects on hematological or serum chemistry parameters (Table S3). Transitions of values from normal to abnormal were not statistically significant for any parameter (p > 0.05).

Discussion

The present study provides evidence of the effectiveness and safety of the IVP containing lotilaner and milbemycin oxime against gastrointestinal nematodes in dogs under field conditions in Korea. The study included a diverse population of dogs with body weights ranging from 2.0 to 40.0 kg and ages ranging from six to 108 months. Of the dogs enrolled, 40.0% (n = 10) were purebred and 60.0% (n = 15) were mixed breeds. In addition, 44.0% (n = 11) and 56.0% (n = 14) were female and male, respectively. These demographics are a good representation of the general dog population in Korea where the study was conducted.

The observed 100% reduction in geometric mean fecal egg counts of T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum, as well as total infection after treatment, demonstrates the highly successful anthelmintic activity of the product. These results are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the high efficacy of milbemycin oxime against gastrointestinal nematodes (Hayes et al. 2021; Riggs and Wiseman 2021; Snyder et al. 2021; Traversa 2021; Young et al. 2021).

Pre-treatment egg counts were particularly high, with maximum counts of 1850 for T. canis, 625 for T. vulpis, and 2976 for A. caninum. In addition, mixed infections were observed in 20.0% of the enrolled dogs. Despite these significant parasite burdens and the presence of co-infections, the IVP achieved complete egg count reduction in all cases, demonstrating effectiveness of the product. High egg burdens contribute to environmental contamination, with untreated infected dogs serving as a significant source, perpetuating the cycle of infection. Therefore, regular anthelmintic treatment - ideally at least three to four times per year - is strongly recommended to reduce fecal egg shedding, minimize environmental contamination, and prevent re-infection (Pennelegion et al. 2020; Young et al. 2021). Considering the biology and pre-patent period of these nematodes, monthly administration of this IVP can effectively prevent patent infections (Young et al. 2021). This approach is supported by previous studies demonstrating significant reductions in nematode prevalence in populations treated with monthly broad-spectrum endectocides (Gates and Nolan 2014).

Zoonotic nematodes, including T. canis and A. caninum, were frequently identified during the study, indicating their prevalence in dog populations and potential public health implications. T. canis is an important zoonotic nematode associated with visceral and ocular larva migrans in humans (Gillespie 1988), while A. caninum can cause patent infection and cutaneous larva migrans in humans (Furtado et al. 2020; Hochedez and Caumes 2007). Effective nematode control measures, such as the use of broad-spectrum anthelmintics, not only ensure the health and well-being of dogs but also play a critical role in reducing the potential for human infection. Therefore, regular deworming of dogs, especially in regions with high prevalence of zoonotic nematodes, should be prioritized as a fundamental strategy in both veterinary care and public health programs (Stull et al. 2007). In addition, given the increasing concern over multiple anthelmintic drug resistance (MADR), it is important to assess the MADR status of nematode isolates. For example, a previous study showed less than 30% efficacy against A. caninum in dogs in the US, with milbemycin oxime showing an efficacy of below 10%, highlighting the need for rapid identification of resistant isolates (Jimenez Castro et al. 2020). Among available molecular approaches, commercial qPCR panels—such as KeyScreen™ GI Parasite PCR (Antech Diagnostics, Inc.)—can be used to detect F167Y and Q134H SNP, a genetic marker of benzimidazole resistance in hookworms (Jimenez Castro et al. 2025; Leutenegger et al. 2023).

The safety profile of IVP was also evaluated. As in previous studies, mild adverse effects such as diarrhea, lethargy, and vomiting were expected (Cavalleri et al. 2017; Hayes et al. 2021). However, no adverse events or significant changes in hematological or serum chemistry parameters were observed in this study, even in a population with variability in age, breed, and body weight. This safety profile, combined with the effectiveness data, highlights the potential of the IVP to improve nematode management strategies in clinical practice and its suitability for routine use in diverse canine populations. Since nematode infections can occur in dogs of all ages, it is critical to provide appropriate treatment for dogs of all ages to minimize gastrointestinal nematode infections, particularly in dogs kept outdoors where environmental contamination is more likely to occur (Eleni Awoke et al. 2011).

Milbemycin oxime has been widely used to treat canine nematode infections since the early 1990 s (Lebon et al. 2016; Macpherson 2013; Rehbein et al. 2016; Walther et al. 2014). As a macrocyclic lactone, it works by activating glutamate-gated chloride channels located on nerve and muscle cells in nematodes. These channels are absent in vertebrates, eliminating the potential for neurotoxicity in mammals due to the interaction between milbemycin oxime and glutamate-gated chloride channels (Drag et al. 2022). Extensive safety studies have demonstrated that milbemycin oxime is safe in dogs, including puppies and breeding animals, when administered at recommended doses. In contrast, ivermectin, another macrocyclic lactone, is contraindicated in collies and herding breeds because it can cross the blood-brain barrier, potentially causing paralysis and death (Bredal and Vollset 1998; Holm 2003). Although generally safe, mild side effects such as vomiting, diarrhea, and inappetence may occasionally occur after oral administration (Walther et al. 2014). This study extended the evaluation of milbemycin oxime to gastrointestinal nematodes of Korean origin and confirmed the product’s high anthelmintic efficacy against local strains. These results confirm that milbemycin oxime maintains potent anthelmintic activity across different geographic regions and nematode populations, further supporting the product as a reliable treatment option for canine gastrointestinal nematode infections worldwide.

The IVP used in this study contains milbemycin oxime and lotilaner, an isoxazoline, as its active ingredients. Lotilaner is approved for use in dogs and provides rapid, sustained control of flea and tick infestations (Kuntz and Kammanadiminti 2017). It inhibits gamma-aminobutyric acid and glutamate receptors at the insect neuromuscular junction, inducing uncontrolled neuromuscular activity and leading to the rapid death of insects and mites (Gassel et al. 2014). Although this study focused on gastrointestinal parasites, the safety evaluation of the IVP indirectly assessed the safety of lotilaner in dogs and confirmed that it is generally safe.

A limitation of this study is that it was conducted without a control group and randomization. To verify that the observed reduction in gastrointestinal nematode egg counts was due to IVP treatment, efficacy assessments were performed on days − 7, 0, 7, and 14. It is well established that following ingestion of T. canis infective eggs, adult worms develop in the small intestine within approximately 3–6 weeks, after which they are capable of continuously shedding up to 20,000 eggs per day for approximately six months (Gillespie 1988). T. vulpis has a lifespan of up to 16 months, with adult females producing up to 2,000 eggs per day (Marchiondo 2019). In addition, A. caninum larvae can penetrate the oral mucosa or directly enter the digestive tract and mature approximately 8 days after infection; adult females then lay millions of eggs daily for several weeks (Saari et al. 2018). In this study, egg counts for all three gastrointestinal nematodes were reduced to zero, suggesting that the effect was indeed due to IVP treatment. Additionally, the follow-up period was short; however, this is unlikely to be problematic given that the product is designed for monthly administration and effectiveness was clearly shown within the recommended dosing interval (Hayes et al. 2021). Although the sample size was relatively small in the present study, all dogs showed a 100% reduction in EPG, indicating the effective anthelmintic activity of the product. Nevertheless, studies with larger sample sizes would be helpful for further generalization of these findings.

In conclusion, IVP was shown to be a safe and effective product for the treatment of T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum infections in dogs in Korea, with a 100% reduction in EPG for these nematodes. The inclusion of milbemycin oxime in the IVP not only ensures effective gastrointestinal nematode control but also provides veterinarians and dog owners with a reliable and modern strategy to protect their pets from these parasites. These results support the potential of the product as a valuable tool in veterinary gastrointestinal nematode control, contributing to the well-being of canine companions.

Conclusions

The present study demonstrates that the IVP containing milbemycin oxime and lotilaner is both safe and highly effective against gastrointestinal nematodes—specifically T. canis, T. vulpis, and A. caninum—in dogs in Korea. The 100% reduction in geometric mean fecal egg counts observed for all target nematodes, coupled with the absence of adverse events or significant changes in hematological and serum chemistry parameters, underscores its potential as a reliable treatment option. These results are expected to inform and improve routine veterinary parasite management strategies.

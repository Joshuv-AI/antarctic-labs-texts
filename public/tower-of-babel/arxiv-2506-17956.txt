Fujita–Zariski decompositions on some product threefolds
Mihai Fulger, Victor Lozovanu

Abstract
. We use explicit blow-ups and computations of birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions to determine generic infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov bodies for box-product ample polarizations on three classes of spaces: product between a curve and the projective plane, products of three curves, and the product between a curve and a Jacobian surface. In particular we compute the volume of many big but non-nef divisors on blow-ups of these threefolds.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Riemann–Roch problem

Given a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
over an algebraically closed field, and a line bundle
L
on it, the Riemann–Roch problem asks to compute the Hilbert function
m\mapsto\dim H^{0}(X,mL)
. This is often difficult, and instead we settle on measuring the asymptotic growth of the function, more precisely on computing the
volume

{\rm vol}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\dim H^{0}(X,mL)}{m^{n}/n!}\ .

This is a numerical birational invariant of
L
, well-studied in
[
Laz04
]
. When
L
satisfies strong positivity conditions like being ample, or at least
nef
(i.e., asymptotically ample), then
\vol(L)=(L^{n})
is the top self-intersection. The volume is positive precisely when
L
is
big
(i.e., birationally ample), however there are plenty of line bundles that are big and not nef and then the computation of their volume is unclear.

For surfaces, the problem above is solved by Zariski decompositions. We can decompose
L=P+N
as divisors, where
P
is nef and
N
effective and
\vol(L)=\vol(P)=(P^{2})
. Computing the Zariski decomposition is effective when an effective divisor that represents
L
is known.

In higher dimension, Zariski decompositions can be tricky. In general we decompose
L=P_{\sigma}(L)+N_{\sigma}(L)
, the so called
Nakayama
\sigma
-Zariski decomposition
of
L
(see
[
Nak04
]
). We still have
\vol(L)=\vol(P_{\sigma}(L))
, however now
P_{\sigma}(L)
is only
movable
(i.e., asymptotically it deforms without fixed divisorial components), not necessarily nef, and so the intersection theoretic interpretation for the volume is missing.

Sometimes (but not always), for example on toric varieties, we can find an explicit birational model
\rho:\widetilde{X}\to X
, after possibly many blow-ups, such that the positive part of
\rho^{*}L
is nef. We call this a
birational Fujita–Zariski
decomposition. It is also an example of a Cutkosky–Kawamata–Moriwaki decomposition (see
[
Pro03
,
KM13
]
). When this happens, then
\vol(L)=(P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L)^{n})
.

1.2.
Generic infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov bodies

An even more ambitious problem than finding the volume is the computation of various Newton–Okounkov bodies (
NObodies
) associated to big line bundles
L
. We focus here on
infinitesimal
NObodies. If
x\in X
and
\pi:{\rm Bl}_{x}X\to X
is the blow-up with exceptional divisor
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\pi^{-1}\{x\}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, then from a complete linear flag
Y_{\bullet}:\ \mathbb{P}^{n-1}\simeq E=Y_{1}\supset Y_{2}\supset\ldots\supset Y_{n}=\{y\}
one can associate
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, the
infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov body (iNObody)
of
L
at
x
associated to
Y_{\bullet}
. It is a valuative asymptotic construction from sections of all
mL
. We detail it in §
2.6
.
The simplest shapes are obtained when
Y_{\bullet}
is a very general linear flag, and the resulting body is independent of this very general choice (cf.
[
LM09
]
). We call it the
generic iNObody
and denote it
\Delta_{x}(L)
.

A feature of the valuative construction is that
{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta_{x}(L)=\frac{1}{n!}\vol(L)
, thus computing
\Delta_{x}(L)
is indeed a more general problem. In fact, if we consider coordinates
(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\Delta}_{x}(L)_{\nu_{1}\geqslant t}=\frac{1}{n!}\vol(\pi^{*}L-tE)
. Thus
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
captures the volume of the entire ray
t\geqslant 0
of divisors

L_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\pi^{*}L-tE

on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
. Computing the body is already an interesting problem for ample line bundles
L
since the
L_{t}
are not necessarily nef.

On surfaces, we explain in Proposition
2.1
via work of
[
LM09
]
that Zariski decompositions are again key for computing generic iNObodies. If
L
is big and
x\not\in{\bf B}_{-}(L)
, e.g., when
L
is also nef, then

{\Delta}_{x}(L)\ =\ \text{region below the graph of }t\mapsto P_{\sigma}(L_{t})\cdot E\ .

Thus, in this case, the Zariski decompositions for all divisors
L_{t}
on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
determine the generic iNObody.

Before work of the authors in
[
FLgeneralities
,
FLproductcurves
,
FLjac
]
, essentially the only example in the literature of a generic iNObody computation on a variety of dimension
n\geqslant 3
was on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, where the result is simplicial. A difficulty is the genericity condition on the linear flag
Y_{\bullet}
which makes the computation unclear even when
X
is a smooth toric variety and
x
a torus-fixed point. We explain in
[
FLgeneralities
]
that if
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, if
L=\mathcal{O}(1,1)
, and
x
is a torus-fixed point (in fact any point), then the generic
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
differs from the body computed with respect to the linear flags
E=Y_{1}\supset Y_{2}
for both choices of points
Y_{2}\in E
that are fixed by the torus in
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
.

In this paper, as in
[
FLjac
]
, our idea is that the strategy on surfaces should extend to threefolds when we can find a sequence of blow-ups
\rho:\widetilde{X}\to{\rm Bl}_{x}X
such that
P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})
is nef for all
t
, i.e., a simultaneous birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions for the entire ray
L_{t}=\pi^{*}L-tE
of divisors on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
. When this happens, we prove that the vertical slice
\Delta_{x}(L)_{\nu_{1}=t}
is a NObody for the restriction of
P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})
to the strict transform
\widetilde{E}
of
E
in
\widetilde{X}
. The surface
\widetilde{E}
is a sequence of point blow-ups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and computations are very concrete when the nef restricted divisor
P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})|_{\widetilde{E}}
is explicitly presented as an effective divisor. We are able to see this idea through in three classes of examples, adding to the Jacobian threefold examples in the upcoming
[
FLjac
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume we are in one of the following cases:

(1)

X=C\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
where
C
is a smooth projective curve, and
L=L_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(b)
is a box-product of ample line bundles.

(2)

X=C_{1}\times C_{2}\times C_{3}
where
C_{i}
are smooth projective curves and
L
is a box-product
L=L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}\boxtimes L_{3}
where
L_{i}
are line bundles on
C_{i}
.

(3)

X=C\times{\rm Jac}(D)
where
C
is any smooth projective curve, while
D
is a smooth projective curve of genus 2, and
L
is again a box-product
L_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}(a\theta)
, where
\theta
is the Theta divisor on the Jacobian.

Then, for every
x\in X
, explicit birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions exist for the entire ray of divisors
L_{t}=\pi^{*}L-E
on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
and in particular we compute the function
t\mapsto{\rm vol}(L_{t})
. Furthermore we describe the convex body
{\Delta}_{x}(L)
.

Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.7
have our precise results and a few illustrations of resulting generic iNObodies. We hope that the computations will serve as testing ground for various open problems. For instance, in Theorem
5.2
we see that our examples give counterexamples to a question raised by
[
FLgeneralities
]
.

The first two examples have a very toric feel. The genera of
C_{i}
do not affect our computations. In the first case, to find birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions we further blow-up
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
along a copy of
C
, the strict transform of the fiber of the second projection through
x
. In the second case, we further blow-up
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
along the strict transforms of the three “coordinate curves”
C_{i}
through
x
. The surface
\widetilde{E}
on which we compute the vertical slices
\Delta_{x}(L)_{\nu_{1}=t}
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in one point in the first case, and in
3
non-collinear points in the second case. We get help from the geometry of the Cremona transform, and from the toric structure.

The third example is more complicated. We compute Nakayama
\sigma
-Zariski decompositions on
{\rm Bl}_{x}X
by first computing the movable cone of divisors on this space. As in
[
FLjac
]
, the blow-ups are then guided by the idea that we want to find explicit effective representatives for the particular
L_{\nu}=\pi^{*}L-\nu\cdot E
on the boundary of the movable cone, and then blow-up until their strict transforms become nef. This process then ends up computing birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions not just for
L_{\nu}
, but for all
L_{t}
.
The blow-ups are more involved and numerous. There are two important curves through
x
on
X
: a copy of
C
appearing as a fiber of the projection to
{\rm Jac}(D)
, and a curve
R\times\{c_{0}\}
where
R\subset{\rm Jac}(C)
is the Seshadri curve, the image of an Abel–Jacobi embedding
C\subset{\rm Jac}(C)
under the multiplication by two map on the Jacobian. We blow-up the strict transform of
C
once and then the strict transform of
R\times\{c_{0}\}
three times in a natural sense. In general, blow-ups seem to terminate when the curves that we blow-up all have semistable normal bundles. The rational surface
\widetilde{E}
on which we compute slices of the iNObody is the surface in Lemma
4.8
, which is
\mathbb{P}^{2}
blown-up in 7 points, 6 of which are on a line, then two more blow-ups, each time of 6 infinitesimally near points to the last 6.

1.3.
Other computations

One can ask whether our strategy extends in higher dimension.
The answer is yes when it comes simply to computing Fujita–Zariski decompositions. For example they should be easy to do on any concrete toric example. However, when it comes to using them to compute generic iNObodies, already in dimension 4 a new level of complexity is introduced. For the computation of the slices
{\Delta}_{x}(L)_{\nu_{1}=t}
, we need some NObodies for
(P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})+s\rho^{*}E)|_{\widetilde{E}}

on the threefold
\widetilde{E}
. A new parameter
s
appears and new blow-ups of
\widetilde{E}
may be needed (and it is not clear that only finitely many exist) in order to run this computation. Clearly higher dimensions add to the complexity of the problem.

Setting aside birational Fujita–Zariski decompositions, sometimes effective computations of generic iNObodies can be carried out in higher dimension via a guess-and-check method. We do this in
[
FLproductcurves
]
for balanced box-products
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}
on products of arbitrarily many curves, but we ask for example
\deg L_{i}=1
for all
i
. The result is a simplex. The computation again benefits from toric geometry.

In
[
FLgeneralities
]
we initiate a deep investigation into the shape of generic iNObodies. We prove that they satisfy a certain property that we call Borel-fixed, at least when
x
is very general in
X
. It implies sharp upper and lower-bounds. We extract a characterization for when the generic iNObody is simplicial in
[
FLgeneralities
]
. As application we recovered the case of balanced box-products on products of curves from
[
FLproductcurves
]
, the case of non-hyperelliptic Jacobian threefolds from
[
FLjac
]
, but also other examples such as quadrics in projective space, or symmetric products of curves with polarization descended from a balanced box-product.

2.
Notation, comments, preliminaries

2.1.
Positive part of a real number

For a real number
r
, denote

r_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\max\{0,r\}.

2.2.
Positivity notions for divisors

We work with classes in
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Our positive classes usually form convex cones and we transfer said positivity property to and from the cone.

The closure of the ample cone
{\rm Amp}(X)
is the
nef
cone
\Nef(X)
. The closure of the cone of effective divisors is the
pseudoeffective
cone
\Eff(X)
. Its interior is the
big
cone. The closure of the cone generated by Cartier divisors with complete linear series without fixed divisorial components is the
movable
cone
\Mov(X)
. We have inclusions
\Nef(X)\subset\Mov(X)\subset\Eff(X)
.

Nefness is preserved by pullback and by finite flat pushforward, movability is preserved by generically finite dominant pushforward, while pseudoeffectivity is preserved by dominant pullback and generically finite dominant pushforward.

2.3.
Positivity on point blow-ups

Let
\pi:\overline{X}\rightarrow X
be the blow-up of a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
at a point
x
with
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
the exceptional divisors. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Put

L_{t}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \pi^{*}L-tE,\textup{ for any }t\geqslant 0\ .

We have the following local positivity invariants:

•

\epsilon\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\epsilon(L;x)=\max\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ L_{t}\text{ is nef}\}
is the
Seshadri constant
;

•

\nu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\nu(L;x)=\max\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ L_{t}\text{ is movable}\}
;

•

\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\mu(L;x)=\max\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ L_{t}\text{ is psuedoeffective}\}
is the
infinitesimal width
, or
Fujita–Nakayama
invariant.

If
L
is ample, then the class
L_{t}
is ample for
t\in(0,\epsilon)
, movable but not nef in
(\epsilon,\nu]
, and big but not movable for
t\in(\nu,\mu)
.

2.4.
Techniques for checking positivity

2.4.1.
A simple test for nefness

Let
D=P+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}Y_{i}
with
P
nef, with
a_{i}\geqslant 0
, and
Y_{i}
prime divisors. Let
\pi_{i}:Y^{\prime}_{i}\to X
be resolutions of the
Y_{i}
, or we can simply ask that the
Y^{\prime}_{i}
are smooth projective and dominate
Y_{i}
. Then
D
is nef iff
\pi_{i}^{*}D
is nef for all
i
.

2.4.2.
A movability criterion

If
D=P+N=P^{\prime}+N^{\prime}
with
P,P^{\prime}
movable and
N,N^{\prime}
effective such that the supports of
N,N^{\prime}
do not share divisorial components, then
D
is movable.

2.4.3.
A necessary condition for movability

If
\pi:Y^{\prime}\to X
is a smooth alteration of a prime divisor on
X
and
D
is movable, then
\pi^{*}D
is pseudoeffective.

2.5.
Zariski decompositions

If
D
is a big
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
, then there exists a unique decomposition

D=P_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D)

such that
P_{\sigma}(D)
is movable,
N_{\sigma}(D)
is effective, and
D^{\prime}-N_{\sigma}(D)
is an effective divisor for every effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D^{\prime}\equiv D
. This is the
Nakayama
\sigma
-Zariski decomposition
of
D
. When
X
is a surface, this is just the usual Zariski decomposition.
Write

N_{\sigma}(D)=\sum\nolimits_{Y}\sigma_{Y}(D)\cdotp Y,

where the sum ranges through all prime divisors
Y
in
X
. For given divisor
D
, only at most finitely many
\sigma_{Y}(D)
are nonzero, thus the sum is well-defined.
We list some properties that we will use:

(1)

If
D=P+N
such that
P
is movable and
N
is effective, then
N-N_{\sigma}(D)
is an effective divisor.

(2)

If
\pi:\overline{Y}\to X
is a resolution of a prime divisor
Y
on
X
and
u=\inf\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\pi^{*}(D-tY)\text{ is pseudoeffective}\}
, then
\sigma_{Y}(D)\geqslant u_{+}
.

(3)

If
\pi:Y\to X
is birational, then
P_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}D)=P_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(D))
and their cycle pushforward in
X
is
P_{\sigma}(D)
. The divisor
\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(D)-P_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}D)=N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(D))

is effective and
\pi
-exceptional. It can be nonzero.

(4)

{\rm Supp}\,N_{\sigma}(D)
is the divisorial part of
{\bf B}_{-}(D)
. See
[
ELMNP06
]
for the definition and properties of
{\bf B}_{\pm}(D)
.

2.5.1.
Birational Fujita–Zariski decomposition

In dimension
n\geqslant 3
, the positive part
P_{\sigma}(D)
is in general not nef. If there exists
\rho:\widetilde{X}\to X
birational with
\widetilde{X}
smooth such that
P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}D)
is nef, we say that the big divisor
D
admits a
birational Fujita–Zariski decomposition
. This is also a particular case of a birational
Cutkosky–Kawamata–Moriwaki decomposition
. See
[
Pro03
,
KM13
]
for extensions of the theory to pseudoeffective divisors.

2.6.
Newton–Okounkov bodies

Fix a flag

X=Y_{0}\supset Y_{1}\supset\ldots\supset Y_{n}=\{x\}

such that each
Y_{i}
is smooth irreducible of dimension
n-i
, or at least smooth at
x
. If
D
is any nonzero divisor on
X
, put

\nu_{1}(D)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \textup{ord}_{Y_{1}}(D).

Then
D-\nu_{1}(D)Y_{1}
is a divisor that does not contain
Y_{1}
in its support. It has a well-defined restriction
D-\nu_{1}(D)Y_{1}|_{Y_{1}}
on
Y_{1}
. Put

\nu_{2}(D)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \textup{ord}_{Y_{2}}(D-\nu_{1}(D)Y_{1}|_{Y_{1}}).

Continue inductively to define

\nu\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ (\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}).

Note that if
D
is effective, then
\nu(D)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geqslant 0}
. The function
\nu
is valuative. In particular

\nu(D+D^{\prime})=\nu(D)+\nu(D^{\prime})

and if
V\subset H^{0}(X,D)
is any linear series that we see as a set of effective Cartier divisors on
X
, then

\#\nu(V\setminus\{0\})=\dim V.

For
D
a big line bundle, the
Newton–Okounkov
body of
D
is

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \overline{\bigcup\nolimits_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{m}\nu(H^{0}(X,mD)\setminus\{0\})}.

Fixing a flag
Y_{\bullet}
as above,
[
LM09
]
prove that
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
only depends on the numerical class of
D
, and
n!\cdot{\rm vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\vol(D)
.

The
generic infinitesimal Newton–Okounkov body
is

{\Delta}_{x}(D)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\ \Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}D),

where
\overline{Y}_{\bullet}
is a very general linear flag in
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
with
\overline{Y}_{i}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-i}
for
i>0
and
\overline{Y}_{0}=\overline{X}
. This is well-defined by
[
LM09
]
.
Specific interest in the generic infinitesimal NObody comes from the fact that it can be used to test whether
x\in{\bf B}_{\pm}(D)
, and it recovers the
moving Seshadri constant

\epsilon(||D||;x)
when
x\not\in{\bf B}_{+}(D)
. These were proved in
[
KL17
]
. In
[
FLgeneralities
]
we prove that additionally the generic iNObody has a special shape that we call Borel-fixed, and in particular we recover sharp bounds that are simplicial from below and of a special polytopal shape from above.

Proposition 2.1

(The surface case, cf.
[
LM09
, Theorem 6.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and
D
a big divisor on
X
.
Let
Y_{\bullet}
be the flag
X\supset C\supset\{x\}
where
C
is a smooth curve. Put
\mu=\sup\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ D-tC\text{ is big}\}
. Assume that
C
is not contained in the support of
N_{\sigma}(D)
. Then

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\overline{\bigl\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}\ \mid\ 0\leqslant t<\mu,\text{ and }x\in[\alpha(t),\beta(t)]\bigr\}},

where
\alpha(t)={\rm ord}_{x}(N_{\sigma}(D-tC)|_{C})
and
\beta_{t}=\alpha(t)+(P_{\sigma}(D-tC)\cdot C)
.

In the infinitesimal case, assume
x\not\in{\bf B}_{-}(D)
. Consider a flag
\overline{X}\supset E\supset\{x^{\prime}\}
where
x^{\prime}\not\in{\rm Supp}\,N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}D-\mu(D;x)E)
. Then
\alpha(t)=0
for all
0\leqslant t<\mu(D;x)
and
\beta(t)=(P_{\sigma}(D_{t})\cdot E)
.

2.6.1.
A strategy for computing infinitesimal NO bodies on threefolds

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective projective threefold with
L
big on
X
. Let
x\in X
with
x\not\in{\bf B}_{-}(L)
. Assume that
\rho:\widetilde{X}\to\overline{X}
is a birational morphism with
\widetilde{X}
smooth such that

(1)

P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})
is nef for all
0\leqslant t<\mu=\mu(L;x)
, i.e.,
\rho
determines a simultaneous birational Fujita–Zariski decomposition for all
L_{t}
.

(2)

(P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})^{2}\cdot\widetilde{E})>0
for
0<t<\mu
. Together with
(1)
, this condition is equivalent to the strict transform
\widetilde{E}
not being a component of
{\bf B}_{+}(P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t}))
, and to
P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})|_{\widetilde{E}}
being big, both in the range
0<t<\mu
.

Then

\Delta_{x}(L)=\biggl\{(t,x,y)\ \mid\ \begin{cases}0\leqslant t\leqslant\mu(L;x)\\
0\leqslant x\leqslant\sup\{s\ \mid\ (P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})+s\rho^{*}E)|_{\widetilde{E}}\text{ is big}\}\\
0\leqslant y\leqslant(P_{\sigma}((P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})+x\rho^{*}E)|_{\widetilde{E}})\cdot(-\rho^{*}E|_{\widetilde{E}}))\end{cases}\biggr\}

Proof.

Fix
\overline{Y}_{\bullet}
a general linear flag in
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}
on
\overline{X}
, and let
\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}
be its strict transform in
\widetilde{X}
. This is well-defined when
\overline{Y}_{3}
is a point in the isomorphism locus of
\rho
. We have
\Delta_{x}(L)=\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\rho^{*}\pi^{*}L)
.

By continuity of slices in a convex body, it is sufficient to work in the range where
L_{t}
is big, which is
t\in(0,\mu)
. For such
t
, the negative parts
N_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})
vary continuously with
t
. Furthermore only finitely many reduced divisors appear in their support since
N_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})\leqslant N_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{\mu})+(\mu-t)\rho^{*}E
. By the assumption that
x\not\in{\bf B}_{-}(L)
, the strict transform
\widetilde{E}
is not a component of
N_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t})
. For a general choice of a point
\overline{Y}_{3}\in E
, then for all
t\in(0,\mu)
the point
\widetilde{Y}_{3}
is not in
{\rm Supp}(N_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t}))
and
\overline{Y}_{3}
is not in
{\rm Supp}(N_{\sigma}(L_{t}))
. It follows that
\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}}(L_{t})=\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\rho^{*}L_{t})=\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\rho^{*}L_{t}))
.

We have
\Delta_{x}(L)_{\nu_{1}\geqslant t}=\Delta_{\overline{Y}_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}L)_{\nu_{1}\geqslant t}
. Up to translation by
(t,0,0)

An application

We illustrate the usefulness of the examples in Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.7
as testing ground for open questions. Here we disprove a question of
[
FLgeneralities
]
that aims to read an invariant of a curve class from a generic iNObody.

In
[
Ful21
]
, the first named author introduced a notion of Seshadri constant for a movable curve class at a point. It is a dual notion to the classical Seshadri constant of nef divisors. If
x\in X
is a smooth point and

is a
movable curve class
, i.e., a class in the closure of the cone generated by irreducible curves that deform in families that cover
X

\epsilon(C;x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{t\geqslant 0\ \mid\ \pi^{*}C-t\ell\in{\rm Mov}_{1}({\rm Bl_{x}X})\}=\inf_{D}\frac{C\cdot D}{\mult_{x}D}\ ,

as
D
ranges through effective Cartier divisors whose support contains
x
, and where
\ell
is a line in
E
. The last equality is a consequence of
[
BDPP
]
. Curve classes obtained as complete intersection of nef divisor classes are movable (see also Lemma
5.3
below). We have the following interesting connection between the Seshadri constant of complete (self-)intersection curve classes and convex geometry:

Proposition 5.1

(
[
FLgeneralities
, Theorem 6.2.(3)]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
, let
L
be an ample divisor on
X

a very general point. Then

where here

denotes the projection on the last

\nu_{1}
.

Note that
[
FLgeneralities
, Theorem 6.2]
is more general and phrased differently. The formulation above avoids introducing extra terminology.
In
[

, §6]
we observed that (

It follows from our work in Theorems

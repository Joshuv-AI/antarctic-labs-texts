Thurston’s asymmetric metric on Margulis spacetimes
Krishnendu Gongopadhyay, Neelanjan Mondal

Abstract
. In this article, we extend Thurston’s asymmetric metric and the associated Finsler norm, originally defined for Teichmüller space, to the setting of Margulis spacetimes. We also establish several convexity properties of both the asymmetric metric and the corresponding Finsler norm.

Primary 22E40; Secondary 53C60, 37D35, 20F67

1.
Introduction

Teichmüller theory provides one of the classical settings in which geometric structures on a manifold can be parametrized by a space of group representations.
For a closed surface
S
, the Teichmüller space
\mathrm{Teich}(S)
identifies with a connected component of the character variety

\mathcal{X}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))/\mathsf{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),

and every point of
\mathrm{Teich}(S)
corresponds to a Fuchsian representation encoding the hyperbolic geometry on
S
.

For a nontrivial element
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S)
, the translation length

l_{\rho}(\gamma)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}}d_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(x,\rho(\gamma)x)

records the length of the closed geodesic on the hyperbolic surface
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\rho(\pi_{1}(S))
represented by
\gamma
. Thurston’s asymmetric metric, introduced in his study of minimal stretch maps between hyperbolic surfaces
[
Thu98
]
, has become one of the most influential geometric structures on Teichmüller space. Thurston wanted a metric that reflects the geometry of how one hyperbolic structure can be deformed into another by stretching along a measured lamination. Thurston showed that comparing the length spectrum for two hyperbolic structures yields an asymmetric metric on
\mathrm{Teich}(S)
,

d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho,\varrho):=\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\pi_{1}(S)]}\log\left(\frac{l_{\rho}(\gamma)}{l_{\varrho}(\gamma)}\right).

We refer to the surveys
[
PS15
]
,
[
PS24
]
for more details on Thurston’s metric on surfaces.

More recently, the Thurston’s asymmetric metric construction has been generalized in higher Teichmüller theory. Carvajales, Dai, Pozzetti and Wienhard
[
CDPW25
]
have extended Thurston’s construction to Anosov representations of word hyperbolic groups into semisimple Lie groups. Therefore, a natural question is whether one can develop an analogue of the Thurston asymmetric metric in the
non-semisimple
setting.
In this article we answer this question for an important class of geometric structures arising from the Margulis spacetimes.

Margulis
[
Mar83
,
Mar84
]
constructed free, non-abelian
subgroups of
\mathsf{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{3}
acting properly discontinuously
and freely on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, yielding the first counterexamples to the Auslander
conjecture. Fried and Goldman
[
FG83
]
subsequently showed that the
Zariski closure of any such group is conjugate to
\mathsf{SO}(2,1)
. Later, Abels–Margulis–Soifer
[
AMS02
]
constructed Zariski-dense free
subgroups of
\mathsf{SO}(n+1,n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
that act properly on

\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
precisely when
n
is odd. The geometric manifolds obtained in
this way are commonly referred to as
Margulis spacetimes
.

Recall that
\mathsf{SO}(n+1,n)
is the group of isometries of the real non-degenerate qadratic form
\langle\cdot~|~\cdot\rangle
of signature
(n+1,n)
. We consider Margulis spacetimes modeled on

\mathsf{G}:=\mathsf{SO}^{0}(n+1,n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}.

Let
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{L}}:=\mathsf{SO}^{0}(n+1,n)
, the identity component of
G
.

Let
\Gamma
is word hyperbolic group, and let
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathsf{G}
be an injective homomorphism such that the linear part
\mathtt{L}_{\rho}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{L}},\mathsf{P}^{\pm})
. Then
\rho
is called a
Margulis spacetime
if and only if
\rho(\Gamma)
acts properly on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. The
linear part
of
\rho
:
\mathtt{L}_{\rho}:\Gamma\to\mathsf{SO}^{0}(n+1,n)

describes how elements of
\Gamma
act by linear transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
.
We assume that
\mathtt{L}_{\rho}
is
Zariski dense
and
Anosov
. We denote the space of Margulis spacetimes by
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{M}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
.

Associated to such a representation
\rho
, the Margulis invariant
\alpha_{\rho}
which measures the signed translation along the affine axis of each group element. Margulis invariant detects proper affine action, for example, see
[
Mar83
]
,
[
Gol02
]
,
[
GLM09
]
.

We say an element
g\in\mathsf{SO}(n+1,n)
is
pseudo-hyperbolic
if and only if
-1
is not an eigenvalue of
g
and it has an eigenvalue
1
with geometric multiplicity one. An element
(g,u)\in\mathsf{SO}(n+1,n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is called
pseudo-hyperbolic
if the linear part
g
is pseudo-hyperbolic. Now for a pseudo-hyperbolic element
(g,u)\in\mathsf{SO}(n+1,n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, the stable and unstable subspaces are defined by

V^{g}_{\pm}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}\mid\lim_{k\to\infty}g^{\mp k}v=0\}.

These are maximal isotropic subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1,n}
. Let
v_{0}^{g}
be the unique eigenvector of
g
with eigenvalue
1
such that
\langle v_{0}^{g}\mid v_{0}^{g}\rangle=1
and is positively oriented with respect to the positive orientation of
V_{+}^{g}
and
(V_{+}^{g})^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n+1,n}
. The
Margulis invaiant
of
g
is defined by

\alpha(g,u):=\langle u\mid v_{0}^{g}\rangle.

The above definition for Margulis invariant has been reformulated for representations
\rho:\Gamma\to G
. We recall the definition from
[
GT23
]
. First recall the notion of
neutral section.

If
\rho:\Gamma\to G
has Anosov linear part
L_{\rho}
with limit map

\xi_{L_{\rho}}
and
\nu
denotes the neutral map, the
neutral section
is

\nu_{\rho}:=\nu\circ\xi_{L_{\rho}}:\partial_{\infty}\Gamma^{(2)}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
\rho:\Gamma\to\mathsf{G}
be an injective homomorphism such that
\mathtt{L}_{\rho}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{L}},\mathsf{P}^{\pm})
. Then for any infinite order element
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the
Margulis invariant
is given by

\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)=\langle u_{\rho}(\gamma)\mid\nu_{\rho}(\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-})\rangle,

where
\gamma^{-},\gamma^{+}
are the repelling and attracting fixed points of
\gamma
, respectively, and,
\nu_{\rho}(\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}):=\nu\circ\xi(\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-},t)
, with
\nu
is the neutral map.

The moduli space of such representations, up to conjugation in
\mathsf{G}
, is

\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G}):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{M}}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})/\mathsf{G}.

For any positive
k
, we define the analytic hypersurface

\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G}):=\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\cap h^{-1}(k),

where
h:\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is the
entropy functional
, which measures the dynamical complexity of the representation. By
[
Gho18
]
, the space
\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
is an analytic manifold.
In addition,
[
Gho23
]
shows that the entropy map

\rho\longmapsto h_{\rho}

is differentiable, and likewise the function

\rho\longmapsto\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)

is differentiable for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Thus, both the global dynamical invariant
h_{\rho}
and the function
\alpha_{\rho}
vary smoothly along
\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
.

In this article, our aim is to offer an analogue of Thurston’s asymmetric metric on
\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
.
Since
\mathsf{SO}^{0}(n+1,n)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is non-semisimple, the results of
[
CDPW25
]
do not apply directly; nevertheless, the underlying strategy inspires our construction.
Our first main result establishes a Thurston-type asymmetric metric on this moduli space.

Theorem 1.2
.

The function
d_{\mathrm{Th}}:\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\times\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined by

d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho,\varrho):=\log\left(\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\frac{h_{\rho}}{h_{\varrho}}\frac{\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\varrho}(\gamma)}\right).

is an asymmetric metric on
\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
.

Note that the renormalization by entropy in the definition of asymmetric metric is essential. For instance, let
\rho=(\mathtt{L}_{\rho},u_{\rho})
and
\varrho=(\mathtt{L}_{\varrho},u_{\varrho})
are two Margulis spacetimes with
\mathtt{L}_{\rho}=\mathtt{L}_{\varrho}
and
u_{\varrho}=cu_{\rho}
for some
c>1
. Then
\alpha_{\varrho}(\gamma)=c\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Hence

\log\left(\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\frac{\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\varrho}(\gamma)}\right)=\log\left(\frac{1}{c}\right)<0.

On the other hand, for
\rho,\varrho\in\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})_{k}
(See Section 3.2 of
[
Gho23
]
for Margulis spacetimes with constant entropy), the entropy
h_{\rho}
and
h_{\varrho}
are fixed. In these situations, renormalizing by entropy is not needed, and the Thurston’s asymmetric metric simplifies to

d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho,\varrho)=\log\left(\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\frac{\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\varrho}(\gamma)}\right).

This metric leads naturally to a renormalized marked-spectrum rigidity statement. The general rigidity result follow from a theorem by Kim
[
Kim05
, Theorem 3]
. We give the proof for completeness. However, we also outline an independent proof fixing the linear part. This may be viewed as a renormalized analogue of the rigidity theorem of Kim
[
Kim05
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\rho,\varrho:\Gamma\to\mathsf{G}
be Margulis spacetimes.
If
h_{\rho}\alpha_{\rho}=h_{\varrho}\alpha_{\varrho}
, then there exists an isomorphism

\sigma:\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}

such that
\sigma\circ\rho=\varrho
.
In particular, if
\rho,\varrho\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
, then
\rho
and
\varrho
are conjugate.

We then turn to the infinitesimal geometry of the moduli space.
Differentiating the asymmetric metric yields a Finsler norm

\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathrm{H}}]}\frac{\dot{h}_{\rho}(v)\,\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)+h_{\rho}\,\dot{\alpha}_{\rho}(\gamma))(v)}{h_{\rho}\,\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)}

defined on
T_{\rho}\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
. We refer to this norm as
Thurston norm
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\{\rho_{z}\}_{z\in D}\subset\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be a real analytic family of Margulis spacetimes parameterizing an open neighborhood of
\rho=\rho_{0}
. Fix an analytic path
z:(-1,1)\rightarrow D
with
z(0)=0
, and let
\rho_{s}=\rho_{z(s)}
and
v=\frac{d}{ds}\big|_{s=0}\rho_{s}\rho^{-1}
be the tangent vector at
\rho
. Let also
h_{s}:=h_{\rho_{s}}
,
\alpha_{s}:=\alpha_{\rho_{s}}
and
g_{s}:=h_{s}\widetilde{g}_{z(s)}
. Then

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}=\lVert[\dot{g_{0}}]\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}},

where
\dot{g}_{0}=\frac{d}{ds}\big|_{s=0}g_{s}
and the Livšic cohomology class
\left[\dot{g_{0}}\right]
belongs to the tangent space
T_{[\phi^{g_{0}}]}\mathbb{P}\mathrm{HR}^{\upsilon}(\phi)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\rho\in\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be a Margulis spacetime admitting an analytic neighborhood in
\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
. Then
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}:\mathrm{T}_{\rho}\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is real valued and non-negative. Furthermore, it is
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-homogeneous, satisfies the triangle inequality and

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}=0\Leftrightarrow\dot{\alpha}_{\rho}(\gamma))(v)=-\frac{\dot{h}_{\rho}(v)}{h_{\rho}}\alpha_{\rho}(\gamma)

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}
. In particular, if the function
\rho\mapsto h_{\rho}
is constant, that is, if
\rho\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
, then

\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}=0\Leftrightarrow\dot{\alpha}_{\rho}(\gamma)(v)=0

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}
. In this case, the function
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}:\mathrm{T}_{\rho}\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathrm{H}}]}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\log\alpha_{\rho_{s}}(\gamma)

defines a Finsler norm.

Recall that the pressure metric introduced in
[
BCLS15
]
,
[
BCS18
]
generalizes the Weil-Petersson metric from Teichmüller space to Anosov representations, providing a higher-rank analogue of the classical Teichmüller geometry. The pressure metric uses thermodynamical formalism to define an intersection function for projective Anosov representations. This framework establishes analyticity of both the intersection and entropy, and yields rigidity results for the intersection, offering a powerful tool for studying the geometry of higher-rank representation spaces. The pressure metric was extended to the setting of Margulis spacetimes in
[
Gho23
]
.

We compare the Thurston norm with the pressure metric and establish convexity results for deformations with fixed Anosov linear part. An additional analytic feature of our construction is that the Thurston Finsler norm is dominated by the pressure metric
\mathcal{P}
(see Subsection
4.3
for the definition).

Proposition 1.6
.

For every Margulis spacetimes
\rho\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
and every

v\in T_{\rho}\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
, the Finsler norm satisfies

\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th}}\;\leq\;C\,\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(v,v)},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on the normalization of the Labourie-Margulis invariant
f_{\rho}
.

This inequality situates the asymmetric Thurston geometry of Margulis spacetimes within
the analytic framework of the pressure metric, and shows that our Finsler norm is locally
controlled by a smooth, symmetric metric. Such domination phenomena mirror the
relationship between Thurston’s metric and the Weil–Petersson metrics in
classical Teichmüller theory.

In addition to contructing the asymmetric metric and its infinitesimal Finsler structure, we also established several new convexity phenomena for Margulis spacetimes.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group and let
\rho_{0},\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be two Margulis spacetimes with constant entropy
k
. Let
\rho_{t}=(\mathtt{L},(1-t)u_{0}+tu_{1})
for
t\in[0,1]
, where
\mathtt{L}
is a fixed Anosov linear part and
u_{0},u_{1}
are translational parts such that
\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
for all
t
. Then

(1)
The function
t\mapsto d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho,\rho_{t})
is convex on
[0,1]
.

(2)
The function

t\mapsto-\log\alpha_{\rho_{t}}
is convex on
[0,1]
.

(3)
The function
t\mapsto-\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}
is convex on
[0,1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be a Hölder continuous flow on a compact metric space
X
, and let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a positive Hölder continuous function. Define
\alpha_{f}(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in X\times\mathbb{R}
by

\int_{0}^{\alpha_{f}(x,t)}f(\phi_{s}(x))ds=t.

Then the flow
\phi^{f}_{t}(x):=\phi_{\alpha_{f}(x,t)}(x)
is called the
Hölder reparametrization
of
\phi
by
f
, and the space of all such reparametrization of
\phi
is denoted by
\mathrm{HR}(\phi)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi
be a Hölder continuous flow on a compact metric space
X
. Two Hölder continuous functions
f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}
are
Livšic cohomologous
(denoted by
f\sim_{\phi}g
), if there exists a Hölder continuous function
h:X\to\mathbb{R}
, differentiable along the flow direction, such that

f-g=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}h\circ\phi_{t}.

We write
[f]_{\phi}
for the Livšic cohomology class of
f
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be a continuous flow on a compact metric space
X
, and let
\mathscr{P}({\phi})
be the
\phi
-invariant probability measures on
X
. For a continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, the
topological pressure
of
f
with respect to
\phi
is

\textbf{P}(\phi,f):=\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\phi})}\left(h(\phi,\mu)+\int fd\mu\right),

where
h(\phi,\mu)
is the
metric entropy
of
\mu
with respect to the flow
\phi
. The pressure of the zero function is called the
topological entropy
of
\phi
, denoted by
h_{\phi}
. A measure
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\phi)
realizing the supremum is called an
equilibrium state
of
f
.

2.4.
Anosov representations

Anosov representations are discrete, faithful representations of a word hyperbolic group into a semisimple Lie group with strong dynamical and geometric properties. Introduced by Labourie
[
Lab06
]
in the study of Hitchin representations, their theory was further developed by Guichard and Wienhard
[
GW12
]
, as well as Kapovich, Leeb, and Porti
[
KLP16
]
,
[
KL18
]
, who provided a comprehensive framework and structural results. These representations admit well-behaved limit maps and satisfy stability properties, making them a natural higher-rank analogue of classical Teichmüller theory.

The dynamics of Anosov representations can be studied via the Gromov geodesic flow associated to a word hyperbolic group
\Gamma
. This flow space, which generalizes the unit tangent bundle of a negatively curved manifold, provides a natural setting to encode the asymptotic geometry of
\Gamma
. Via the equivariant limit maps of an Anosov representation, one can pull back the flow dynamics into associated bundles, allowing the definition of analytic invariants such as entropy, intersection, and the pressure metric. In the following we define such representations in our setting. We follow the definition from
[
Gho23
]
.

Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group and

be its
boundary at infinity. The group

acts naturally on

\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
. Let

be the
subset of

[

]
constructed a proper and cocompact action of

on

action of

on
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{h_{\varrho}^{-1}}(g\phi_{h_{\rho}}(\rho)g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\phi_{h_{\varrho}^{-1}}(g\widetilde{\rho}g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\phi_{h_{\varrho}^{-1}}(\widetilde{\varrho})

\displaystyle=\varrho.

Therefore, we get
\sigma\circ\rho=\varrho
.

4.3.
Pressure duality and the infinitesimal form

Let
f_{\rho}
denote the Labourie–Margulis invariant
and
\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}
the equilibrium state for the potential
-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}

Following
[
BCLS15
]
, we define the

by

\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(v,w)=\int g_{v}\,g_{w}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}-\Big(\int g_{v}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}\Big)\Big(\int g_{w}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}\Big),

where
g_{v},g_{w}
represent the Livšic cohomology classes
associated to the tangent vectors
v,w\in T_{\rho}\mathrm{M}_{z}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
.
The form
{\mathbf{P}}_{\rho}
is positive semi-definite and
induces a Riemannian metric on the subspace of constant entropy.

Proposition 4.7
.

For each
\rho\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
and

,
the Finsler norm satisfies the inequality

\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th}}\;\leq\;C\,\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(v,v)},

for a uniform constant

depending only on the choice of normalization of
f_{\rho}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.4
,

\|v\|_{\mathrm{Th}}=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\phi)}\frac{\int g_{v}\,d\mu}{\int f_{\rho}\,d\mu}
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the equilibrium measure

\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}
we have

\Big|\int g_{v}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}\Big|\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(v,v)}\,\sqrt{\int f_{\rho}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}}.

Since the denominator in the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Th}}
is precisely

\int f_{\rho}\,d\mu_{-h_{\rho}f_{\rho}}
,
the desired estimate follows.
∎

\Gamma
on
\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is given by

\gamma\cdot v=Ad(\rho(\gamma))(v),\quad\gamma\in\Gamma,\,v\in\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}.

Since
\rho(\gamma)\in\mathsf{G}
, and
\mathsf{G}
is a finite-dimensional Lie group,
Ad(\rho(\gamma))
is a linear automorphism of
\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. Hence,
\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is a finite-dimensional
\Gamma
-module (over
\mathbb{R}
). Now since
\Gamma
is finite generated, and
\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
is a finite dimensional
\Gamma
-module, the first cohomology group
\dim H^{1}_{Ad\circ\rho}(\Gamma,\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})<\infty
, where
\mathesstixfrak g^{\mathtt{L}}
is the Lie algebra of
\mathsf{G}^{\mathtt{L}}
. Thus, the tangent space
\mathrm{T}_{\rho}\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
is a finite dimensional vector space. Since in a finite dimensional vector space, the closed unit ball of a norm is compact, the desired conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Convexity

In this section we prove Theorem
1.7
. The proof has been divided into the following components, which together establish the result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group and let
\rho_{0},\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be two Margulis spacetimes with constant entropy
k
. Let
\rho_{t}=(\mathtt{L},(1-t)u_{0}+tu_{1})
for
t\in[0,1]
, where
\mathtt{L}
is a fixed Anosov linear part and
u_{0},u_{1}
are translational parts such that
\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
for all
t
. Then the function

t\mapsto d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho,\rho_{t})

is convex on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{t}
are Margulis spacetimes, then by Remark
3.2
, the Margulis invariants
\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma),\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma),\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)
are positive for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since Margulis invariant is linear in translational part, then
\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)=(1-t)\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma+t\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)
.
We now note that

\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)=\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)\left[(1-t)+t\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}(\gamma)}}\right],

which implies that

\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)}=\frac{1}{1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)}.

Using Definition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho_{0},\rho_{t})

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\sup_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\frac{1}{1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{\inf_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log\left(\inf_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)\right).

Now consider the function
f(t)=\inf_{[\gamma]\in[\Gamma_{\mathtt{H}}]}\left(1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)\right)
. Let for each conjugacy class, we define
g_{\gamma}(t):=1+t\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}-1\right)
. Now for each
\gamma
,
g_{\gamma}(t)
is affine in
t
. The infimum of a family of affine functions in concave. Hence,
f(t)
is concave on
[0,1]
. Now we see that
d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho_{0},\rho_{t})=-\log(f(t))
. Consider the function
\varphi(t)=-\log(f(t))
. Since
f(t)>0
for
t\in[0,1]
(as
\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)}>0
), we compute

\varphi^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)},\quad\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)f(t)-(f^{\prime}(t))^{2}}{f(t)^{2}}=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{f(t)}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\right)^{2}.

Since
f
is concave,
f^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 0
, we get that
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0
, so
\varphi
is convex. Hence, the function
t\mapsto d_{\mathrm{Th}}(\rho_{0},\rho_{t})
is convex on
[0,1]
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group and let
\rho_{0},\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be two Margulis spacetimes with constant entropy
k
. Let
\rho_{t}=(\mathtt{L},(1-t)u_{0}+tu_{1})
for
t\in[0,1]
, where
\mathtt{L}
is a fixed Anosov linear part and
u_{0},u_{1}
are translational parts such that
\rho_{t}\in\mathrm{M}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
for all
t
. Then the function

t\mapsto-\log\alpha_{\rho_{t}}

is convex on
[0,1]
.

Let
v=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\rho_{t}\rho_{0}^{-1}\in T_{\rho}\mathrm{M}_{k}(\Gamma,\mathsf{G})
be the tangent vector at
\rho
. Then the function

t\mapsto-\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}

is also convex on
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{t}=(\mathtt{L},u_{t})
with fixed linear part
\mathtt{L}
and
u_{t}=(1-t)u_{0}+tu_{1}
, we have
\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)=(1-t)\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)+t\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)
. Thus,
\alpha_{\rho_{t}}
is an affine function in
t
, and strictly positive for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Define the function
f_{\gamma}(t):=-\log\alpha_{\rho_{t}}(\gamma)
, whose derivative is

f_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{dt}\log\left((1-t)\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)+t\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)\right)=\frac{\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)-\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}{(1-t)\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)+t\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)}.

Let
a:=\alpha_{\rho_{0}}(\gamma)>0
,
b:=\alpha_{\rho_{1}}(\gamma)>0
. Then

f_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{a-b}{(1-t)a+tb},\quad f_{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{((1-t)a+tb)^{2}}\geq 0.

Thus,
f_{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)
is convex in
t
. Therefore, the function
t\mapsto-\log\alpha_{\rho_{t}}
in convex on
[0,1]
.

The convexity of
t\mapsto-\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{Th}}
is an immediate consequence of the above and Theorem
1.5
.
∎

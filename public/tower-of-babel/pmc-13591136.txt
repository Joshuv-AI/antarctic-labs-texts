Epitranscriptomic Regulation of Cardiac Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury: Unveiling the METTL14/IGF2BP1/PDE4D Axis
Authors: JaeHyuk Jang, Hun-Jun Park
Published: 2026

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) remains a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality. Currently, the most effective therapeutic intervention for AMI is the rapid restoration of blood flow to the ischemic myocardium. However, this indispensable reperfusion process paradoxically triggers a secondary wave of myocardial damage known as ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury. Characterized by an explosive burst of reactive oxygen species, intracellular calcium overload, and severe inflammatory responses, cardiac I/R injury can account for a substantial portion of the final infarct size. Despite decades of intensive research aimed at mitigating this phenomenon, translating cardioprotective strategies from bench to bedside has proven exceptionally challenging, underscoring an urgent need for a paradigm shift in our understanding of the underlying molecular mechanisms.1)

Epitranscriptomics, particularly the study of the highly abundant and reversible N6-methyladenosine (m6A) mRNA modification, has emerged as a groundbreaking frontier in cardiovascular biology. This sophisticated post-transcriptional regulation is orchestrated by a dynamic interplay among three protein groups: “writers” (methyltransferases), “erasers” (demethylases), and “readers” (binding proteins). The m6A marks are installed by a writer complex—primarily composed of METTL3, METTL14, and their regulatory subunit WTAP—and can be dynamically removed by erasers like FTO and ALKBH5. Ultimately, the fate of the methylated mRNA, including its structural stability, translation, or degradation, is determined by reader proteins.2) While classical readers like YTHDF2 typically promote rapid mRNA decay, the recently identified IGF2BP family (IGF2BP1, 2, and 3) exerts a completely opposing effect. By recognizing the m6A consensus sequence via their K homology domains, IGF2BPs recruit RNA stabilizers (such as HuR, MATR3, and PABPC1) to protect target mRNAs from degradation, thereby strongly enhancing their stability and translation.3)

Cyclic adenosine monophosphate is a vital second messenger in the heart, primarily degraded by phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4). While the PDE4B subtype mediates adverse neutrophil inflammation during AMI,4) the precise role of PDE4D in I/R injury has remained elusive, despite its known involvement in ischemic preconditioning and heart failure.5) In this context, Li et al.6) address this gap by establishing PDE4D as a critical executioner of myocardial I/R injury under the epitranscriptomic control of the METTL14/IGF2BP1 axis. The authors demonstrated aberrant upregulation of PDE4D in the serum of myocardial infarction patients, in rat models of I/R, and in hypoxia/reoxygenation-treated cardiomyocytes, and showed that PDE4D knockdown significantly reduced infarct size and cellular apoptosis. Mechanistically, they revealed that the m6A “writer” METTL14 methylated PDE4D mRNA. This modification was specifically recognized by the m6A “reader” IGF2BP1, which, unlike decay-promoting readers like YTHDF2, strongly enhanced mRNA stability. This stabilization led to the pathological accumulation of PDE4D and subsequent cardiomyocyte apoptosis. By delineating the complete METTL14-IGF2BP1-PDE4D axis, the study provides a comprehensive molecular narrative of post-transcriptional regulation during cardiac I/R injury.6)

The findings of Li et al.6) hold substantial scientific and clinical implications. Scientifically, the study overcomes a common limitation in epitranscriptomic research by identifying the specific “reader” protein (IGF2BP1) rather than stopping at the “writer” or “eraser,” demonstrating that the consequence of m6A methylation is entirely context-dependent, relying on the specific reader protein recruited to the site. Clinically, this study opens new avenues for RNA-targeted therapeutics in cardiology. While traditional pan-PDE4 inhibitors (e.g., roflumilast or piclamilast) offer cardioprotection, their clinical application is severely hampered by dose-limiting gastrointestinal side effects caused by systemic PDE4D inhibition.4)7) The current study by Li et al.6) suggests a disruptive therapeutic strategy intervening at the post-transcriptional level. By blocking the interaction between the target mRNA and its specific reader protein—for example, disrupting the IGF2BP1-PDE4D mRNA interaction—it may be possible to prevent the pathological accumulation of PDE4D in the ischemic heart without triggering the systemic side effects of direct enzymatic inhibition.

Despite the foundational significance of Li et al.’s study,6) several challenges remain for clinical translation. First, the myocardium is a highly heterogeneous tissue, comprising not only cardiomyocytes but also fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and resident immune cells. Given that PDE4B critically regulates neutrophil-endothelial adhesion and microvascular obstruction during I/R,4) future single-cell RNA sequencing studies must determine whether the METTL14/IGF2BP1/PDE4D axis operates similarly in non-cardiomyocytes to shape the inflammatory microenvironment. Second, given its broad role in governing the stability of numerous transcripts, profound systemic manipulation of METTL14 could elicit significant off-target effects. Therefore, cardiac-specific targeted delivery systems, such as targeted lipid nanoparticles or exosomes, will be critical to exclusively deliver RNA interference or small molecules directly to the ischemic myocardium.8) Finally, to overcome the physiological gap between rodents and humans, validating the long-term safety and cardioprotective efficacy of targeting this axis in large animal models (e.g., porcine models) is an essential next step toward clinical trials.9)

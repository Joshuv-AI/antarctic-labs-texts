Abstract
We study the acceleration and collimation of the two-sided jets in the nearby low-luminosity active galactic nucleus NGC 4261 (3C 270) using archival multifrequency, multi-epoch Very Long Baseline Array data. By applying multiple analysis methods and incorporating results from the literature, we robustly identify a parabolic-to-conical structural transition in both the jet and counterjet, with the transition occurring at ( 1.23 ± 0.24 ) (1.23\pm 0.24) pc or ( 8.1 ± 1.6 ) × 10 3 ​ R s (8.1\pm 1.6)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s} (Schwarzschild radii) for the jet and ( 0.97 ± 0.29 ) (0.97\pm 0.29) pc or ( 6.4 ± 1.9 ) × 10 3 ​ R s (6.4\pm 1.9)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s} for the counterjet. Assuming that the brightness asymmetry between the twin jets is primarily due to relativistic Doppler (de)boosting, we derive the jet velocity field at distances of ∼ ( 10 3 − 2 × 10 4 ) ​ R s \sim(10^{3}-2\times 10^{4})\,R_{\rm s} based on the jet-to-counterjet brightness ratio and spectral index. Although local kinematic variations are present, the jet shows an overall acceleration to relativistic speeds from ∼ 10 3 \sim 10^{3} to ∼ 8 × 10 3 ​ R s \sim 8\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s} , with a maximum Lorentz factor of Γ max ≈ 2.6 \Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 2.6 . Beyond this region, the jet gradually decelerates to sub-relativistic speeds. These results support the existence of a (sub)parsec-scale ( ≲ 1.5 \lesssim 1.5 pc) acceleration and collimation zone (ACZ) in NGC 4261, where the jet is accelerated via magnetic-to-kinetic energy conversion while being confined by external pressure. A brief comparison with M 87 suggests that the ACZ in NGC 4261 may represent a scaled-down analogue of that in M 87.

I
Introduction

The question of how relativistic jets in active galactic nuclei (AGNs) are launched, accelerated, and collimated is a long-standing fundamental issue in astrophysics (e.g.,
R. Blandford et al. 2019
). From a theoretical point of view, these jets are powered by extracting energy from the inner accretion disk around a spinning supermassive black hole (SMBH) and/or from the SMBH itself via magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) processes (
R. D. Blandford & R. L. Znajek 1977
;
R. D. Blandford & D. G. Payne 1982
). As the jet expands, the magnetic energy stored at its base is gradually converted into kinetic energy, which accelerates the jet to relativistic speeds
(
S. S. Komissarov et al. 2007
;
S. S. Komissarov et al. 2009
;
A. Tchekhovskoy et al. 2008
;
A. Tchekhovskoy et al. 2009
)
. Meanwhile, the accelerating jet can be effectively collimated by external pressure from its ambient medium
(
M. C. Begelman & Z.-Y. Li 1994
;
Y. Lyubarsky 2009
, e.g.,)
. In particular, the processes of acceleration and collimation are causally connected and occur simultaneously, which is known as the acceleration and collimation zone (ACZ)
(
A. P. Marscher et al. 2008
;
L. Chen & B. Zhang 2021
, e.g.,)
.

Very long baseline interferometry (VLBI), thanks to its high spatial resolution, provides the best opportunity to study the physical processes that occur in the ACZs of AGN jets (e.g.,
B. Boccardi et al. 2017
). M 87 represents the most well-studied one (see a recent review by
K. Hada et al. 2024
).
The significant progress made in M 87 has consequently spurred active research on ACZs of other AGNs.

In terms of jet collimation, this aspect has been widely explored in both low- and high-luminosity radio sources
(
C.-Y. Tseng et al. 2016
;
B. Boccardi et al. 2016
;
B. Boccardi et al. 2019
;
E. Traianou et al. 2020
;
C. Casadio et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. For most of the well-studied sources, a parabolic-to-conical transition in the jet collimation profile has been clearly observed. However, there are some exceptions, such as 3C 84
(
H. Nagai et al. 2014
;
G. Giovannini et al. 2018
)
, Cygnus A
(
S. Nakahara et al. 2019
)
, and NGC 1052
(
S. Nakahara et al. 2020
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2022
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2024
)
. Furthermore, it is interesting to note that the location of the break in the parabolic/cylindrical-to-conical transition does not always coincide with the Bondi radius (
r_{\rm Bondi}
) of the central SMBH
(
B. Boccardi et al. 2021
;
H. Okino et al. 2022
;
P. R. Burd et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

Despite ongoing advancements in our understanding of the jet collimation, observational studies that focus on the detailed acceleration processes of AGN jets remain insufficient.
Until now, only four AGN jets, to the best of our knowledge, provide solid evidence of concurrent acceleration and collimation. These include two low-luminosity AGNs (LLAGNs), M 87
(
K. Asada & M. Nakamura 2012
;
J. Park et al. 2019
)
and NGC 315
(
J. Park et al. 2021
;
B. Boccardi et al. 2021
;
L. Ricci et al. 2022
;
L. Ricci et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, a narrow-line Seyfert 1 (NLS1) galaxy, 1H 0323+342
(
K. Hada et al. 2018
)
, and a flat-spectrum radio quasar (FSRQ), 1928+738
(
K. Yi et al. 2024
)
.

Motivated by this limitation, we have selected NGC 4261 (3C 270), a nearby LLAGN (see
L. C. Ho 2008
for a review), as the focus of our study. This source is particularly noteworthy for several reasons. First, its relatively close distance
(
J. L. Tonry et al. 2001
;
M. Cappellari et al. 2011
,
(31.6\pm 2.8)
Mpc;)
and the existence of a SMBH at its center
(
B. D. Boizelle et al. 2021
;
S. Sawada-Satoh et al. 2022
;
I. Ruffa et al. 2023
,
M_{\rm BH}=(1.62\pm 0.04)\times 10^{9}M_{\sun}
;)
allow for a spatial resolution of 1 mas
\sim
0.15 pc
\sim
988 Schwarzschild radii (
R_{\rm s}
). Hence, NGC 4261 hosts one of the most promising SMBHs for future submillimeter VLBI imaging by Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) or next-generation EHT (ngEHT)
(
X. A. Zhang et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Moreover, the location of the central SMBH has been precisely determined by measuring the core shift in both the jet and counterjet by
T. Haga et al. 2015
. Lastly, the jet of NGC 4261 is inclined at a relatively large viewing angle in the range of
54^{\circ}\lesssim\phi_{\rm view}\lesssim 84^{\circ}
, with the most probable value being
{71^{\circ}}\pm 2^{\circ}
(1
\sigma
) (
X. Yan et al. 2023
;
X. Yan & R.-S. Lu 2025
). Consequently, NGC 4261 provides a valuable opportunity to study the physical properties of its two-sided jets not only on the kiloparsec scales
(
M. Birkinshaw & R. L. Davies 1985
;
K. Kolokythas et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
but also on the VLBI or (sub)parsec scales
(
D. L. Jones & A. E. Wehrle 1997
, e.g.,)
.

The jet geometry of NGC 4261 has been explored by
S. Nakahara et al. 2018
(hereafter
83
) and
X. Yan et al. 2023
(hereafter
121
). These studies reveal a transition in the jet collimation profile from parabolic to conical shape on the subparsec scales of
\lesssim 1
pc (deprojected). Interestingly, this break location is significantly smaller than the Bondi radius of the central SMBH (
r_{\rm Bondi}\approx 99.2
pc;
B. Balmaverde et al. 2008
;
121
). On the other hand, a preliminary analysis of jet kinematics by
121
, using four-epoch VLBI data at 15 GHz, tentatively suggests that the jet acceleration zone may coincide with its collimation zone.

II
Observations and Data Reduction

In this study, we used archival VLBA data observed at frequencies between 1.4 and 43 GHz. The visibility data were obtained from the U.S. National Radio Astronomy Observatory (NRAO) Data Archive
1
1

1

https://data.nrao.edu/
. The details of these observations are summarized in Table
1
. As noted in column (12), some of the data have not yet been published in the literature.

We calibrated the data following standard procedures with the NRAO Astronomical Image Processing System
(
E. W. Greisen 2003
,
AIPS
;)
. We first corrected for the digital sampler bias using the auto-correlation spectra. Then, parallactic angle variations, residual ionospheric delays, and Earth orientation parameters were corrected or updated. After removing the instrumental delay and phase offset for each intermediate frequency, we performed global fringe fitting on the target and calibrators to derive the residual phase, delay, and rate solutions. In cases where residual solutions for NGC 4261 cannot be determined due to low signal-to-noise ratios (S/Ns) (i.e., the BS094b/c/d data sets, see Table
1
), global fringe fitting was only run on the phase-referencing calibrator J1222+0413 (separated by
1\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}^{\circ}
77 on the sky). The residual phase, delay, and rate solutions derived from the calibrator were then interpolated into consecutive scans of NGC 4261 (see details in
T. Haga et al. 2015
). We performed a priori amplitude calibration with opacity corrections using system temperatures and antenna gain curves. The bandpass corrections were applied using the bright calibrators. Finally, we performed iterative CLEAN and phase/amplitude self-calibration, followed by imaging using the Caltech
DIFMAP
package
(
M. C. Shepherd 1997
)
.

III
Data Analysis and Results

Figure
1
shows the representative CLEAN images of NGC 4261, while the remaining images are provided in Figures
9
and
10
in Appendix
A
(see Table
1
for image parameters). All these images (except at 22 and 43 GHz) clearly reveal the two-sided jets along the east–west direction, with the western side being the approaching jet and the eastern side the counterjet. At 5, 8.4, and 15 GHz, an emission gap is evidently visible in the innermost counterjet, which is attributed to free-free absorption (FFA) by the foreground ionized gas from the accretion disk (e.g.,
D. L. Jones & A. E. Wehrle 1997
;
T. Haga et al. 2015
; see also Appendix
D
). At 22 and 43 GHz, the counterjet cannot be detected due to the limited sensitivity of these data. However, recent high-sensitivity observations have successfully detected twin jets at both of these high frequencies (
S. Sawada-Satoh et al. 2023
;
121
).

In Figure
1
, we also show the longitudinal radiation intensity of the twin jets as a function of distance from the central black hole (i.e., the radial intensity profile), from which the proper motions of both the jet and counterjet may be inferred (see Section
III.2
). Thanks to the clear detection of the counterjet, we are able to study the jet collimation on both sides and probe the jet acceleration by using the jet-to-counterjet brightness ratio and spectral index, as detailed below.

III.1
Jet Collimation Profile

While the collimation profile of the NGC 4261 jet has been studied by
83
and
121
, it should be noted that the jet geometry transition, suggested to occur on subparsec scales of
\lesssim 1
pc, is derived from the same VLBI data sets observed in 2003 (i.e., BS094c/d in Table
1
). In this study, we expand these studies by incorporating additional data sets, including the 1.7/8.4 GHz data from project BW011 in 1995 and the two-epoch 8.4 GHz data from project BJ028 in 1999. These data sets offer the advantage of broader bandwidth and/or longer integration time on the source than the BS094c/d data sets (see Table
1
). Furthermore, we also include the 5 GHz data from project BS094b in 2002.

To robustly determine the jet collimation profile of NGC 4261, we adopted multiple data analysis methods: (1) deriving the deconvolved jet width based on the Gaussian fit to the transverse intensity profile of the jet, as commonly used before
(
K. Asada & M. Nakamura 2012
;
K. Hada et al. 2016
;
C.-Y. Tseng et al. 2016
;
S. Nakahara et al. 2018
;
S. Nakahara et al. 2019
;
S. Nakahara et al. 2020
;
J. Park et al. 2021
;
H. Shang et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
; (2) estimating the jet width from the model-fitted size of circular Gaussian components
(
B. Boccardi et al. 2021
;
E. V. Kravchenko et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
; (3) exploring the innermost jet geometry using the multifrequency properties of the VLBI core
(
K. Hada et al. 2013
;
M. Nakamura & K. Asada 2013
;
J. Park et al. 2021
, i.e., the core shift and core size; e.g.,)
. In the following, we provide detailed descriptions of these methods and present the corresponding results.

III.1.1
Deconvolved Jet Width

We determined the deconvolved jet width of NGC 4261 following the approach described in
J. Park et al. 2021
. First, we repositioned the putative location of the SMBH to the center of each CLEAN image, based on the well-constrained core shifts of
T. Haga et al. 2015
, as indicated in the CLEAN images shown in Figure
9
. We restored each image using a circular beam with a size equal to the major axis of the synthesized beam (
\Theta_{\rm maj}
in Table
1
). The transverse intensity profile of the jet at each distance from the black hole was then extracted through a pixel-based analysis. By fitting a Gaussian function to the transverse intensity profile, we obtained the full width at half maximum (FWHM;
\Theta_{\rm fit}
), from which the deconvolved jet width was calculated as
W=\sqrt{\Theta_{\rm fit}^{2}-\Theta_{\rm maj}^{2}}
. Measurements were retained only if (1) the amplitude of the fitted Gaussian function exceeded 15
\sigma
image rms noise level, and (2)
\Theta_{\rm fit}>\Theta_{\rm maj}
. Considering the complex blending effect in the core region, we discarded measurements taken at separations from the observed core smaller than the restoring beam size (
\Theta_{\rm maj}
). Finally, the deconvolved widths were binned by distance from the central engine, using a bin size of
\Theta_{\rm maj}/2
. For each bin, the mean width was adopted as the representative jet width, assuming an uncertainty of
\Theta_{\rm maj}/10
.

In Figure
2
, we separately present the deconvolved width of the jet and counterjet as a function of deprojected distance from the central black hole (
r
). Our results overlay those reported by
121
, which were obtained using a similar method. To illustrate the evolution of the jet width on the kiloparsec scales, we include a Very Large Array (VLA) measurement from
83

W\approx 6\times 10^{6}\,R_{\rm s}
at a deprojected distance of
r\approx 3\times 10^{7}\,R_{\rm s}

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Jet Collimation

In this study, we performed an in-depth analysis of the width profiles for both the jet and counterjet in NGC 4261 using multiple data analysis methods (see Figures
2
and
3
). Our results reveal a clear structural transition from a parabolic to a conical shape on both sides, which aligns well with the previous findings by
83
and
121
. Importantly, all studies suggest that this transition occurs on the (sub)parsec scales of
\lesssim 1.5
pc (or
\lesssim 10^{4}\,R_{\rm s}
), which is substantially smaller than the Bondi radius of the central SMBH (
r_{\rm Bondi}\approx 99.2
pc). Moreover, the strong agreement between the width profiles of the jet and counterjet (see Figures
2
) also implies that jet properties are more likely to be regulated by the global state of the circumnuclear environment rather than local conditions (
83
). In the following, we briefly discuss the physical mechanisms that may be responsible for jet collimation in NGC 4261, based on studies in other AGNs.

IV.1.1
Jet Confinement on (Sub)Parsec Scales (
\lesssim 1.5
pc)

The transition of the jet geometry from parabolic to conical shape in NGC 4261 could be driven by a change in the external pressure profile
(
Y. Lyubarsky 2009
, e.g.,)
. Notably, this change should take place on (sub)parsec scales. Following the discussion in
121
, we propose that the advection dominated accretion flow (ADAF), which extends to
\sim
0.5 pc
(
R. S. Nemmen et al. 2014
;
T. Haga et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
, could play a role in shaping the innermost parabolic jet structure in NGC 4261. Alternatively, the disk wind could be another suitable candidate (e.g.,
F. Yuan et al. 2015
;
N. Globus & A. Levinson 2016
;
M. Nakamura et al. 2018
;
R. Blandford & N. Globus 2022
; see
B. Boccardi et al. 2021
for more details), provided it does not extend too far from the central black hole.

Furthermore, the structural transition of AGN jets could also be linked to the initial magnetization at the jet base
(
Y. Y. Kovalev et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
. In this context, we present additional analysis under the framework of a magnetically driven jet model developed by
L. Chen & B. Zhang 2021
, as detailed in Appendix
E
. Our preliminary results tentatively suggest that the jet base of NGC 4261 may not be highly magnetized—similar to NGC 315, but in contrast to M 87. This may help explain both the (sub)parsec-scale collimation transition and the “slow acceleration” of its jet (see Section
IV.2.1
).

IV.1.2
Conical Expansion from (Sub)Parsec to Kiloparsec Scales

Another key question concerns how the parabolic jet transitions to a conical expansion on (sub)parsec scales. To understand this, jet kinematics beyond the collimation break may provide valuable insights. As shown in Figure
7
, we observe progressive deceleration downstream of the transition zone in NGC 4261. This behavior could be driven by in situ energy dissipation, where kinetic energy is converted into internal energy within the jet. As a result, the increased internal energy may cause the jet to be overpressured relative to the ambient medium, allowing it to expand more freely into the surrounding medium. Similar scenarios have been proposed for NGC 6251 and NGC 315
(
C.-Y. Tseng et al. 2016
;
J. Park et al. 2021
)
.

However, the exact mechanism responsible for this energy conversion remains unclear. One possible explanation could be the formation of a recollimation shock in the transition zone, possibly triggered by a pressure mismatch between the jet and the ambient medium caused by the change in the external pressure profile (see Section
IV.1.1
)
(
J. L. Gomez et al. 1995
;
Y. Mizuno et al. 2015
;
A. Fuentes et al. 2018
)
. As suggested by simulations, such shocks can induce strong instabilities within the jet, leading to its deceleration and heating, which thus facilitates the energy conversion process
(
Y. Mizuno et al. 2015
;
A. Costa et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. In this work, we did not find compelling evidence of a recollimation shock in NGC 4261, although Appendix
F
presents tentative clues suggesting its possible presence.

IV.2
Jet Acceleration

In Figure
8
, we show the relationship between the four-velocity (
\Gamma\beta_{\rm int}
) of the NGC 4261 jet and the deprojected distance from the central black hole. As mentioned in Section
III.2.2
, although local kinematic variations are present, the overall kinematic trend indicates that the jet accelerates up to the jet collimation break at
(1.23\pm 0.24)
pc or
(8.1\pm 1.6)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, beyond which it gradually decelerates.
To examine the causal connection between the acceleration zone and the collimation zone, it is important to test whether the Lorentz factor and the jet opening angle (
\phi_{\rm open}
) satisfy the condition
\Gamma\phi_{\rm open}/2\lesssim\sqrt{\sigma_{\rm m}}
, where
\sigma_{\rm m}
is the magnetization parameter of the jet (i.e., Poynting-to-matter energy flux ratio)
(
S. S. Komissarov et al. 2009
)
. For NGC 4261, this condition appears to be fulfilled, with
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 2.6
,
\phi_{\rm open}\lesssim 5^{\circ}
, and an assumed
\sigma_{\rm m}\gtrsim 1
within the ACZ. This suggests that efficient jet acceleration is taking place through magnetic energy conversion and that the acceleration and collimation processes in NGC 4261 could be causally connected. Based on similar findings in M 87, 1H 0323+342, NGC 315, and 1928+738 (see Section
I
), we now identify a (sub)parsec-scale ACZ (
\lesssim 1.5
pc) in the nearby LLAGN NGC 4261.

IV.2.1
Slow Jet Acceleration

Assuming a simple power-law relationship between the Lorentz factor and the radial distance, we fitted a power-law function to
\Gamma
within the range of
(1-3)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, excluding data points that show significant deviations, as they may indicate potential stratification in the jet velocity field. Although a single power-law fit is somewhat simplistic, we obtained
\Gamma\propto r^{0.21\pm 0.04}
(see Figure
8
). For comparison, the best-fit power-law functions in the jet acceleration zones of the two LLAGNs, M 87 and NGC 315, are
\Gamma\propto r^{0.16\pm 0.01}
and
\Gamma\propto r^{0.30\pm 0.04}
, respectively
(
J. Park et al. 2019
;
J. Park et al. 2021
)
. Similar to them, our results suggest a relatively shallow jet acceleration profile in NGC 4261 when compared to predictions from highly magnetized jet models, which expect an efficient “linear acceleration” with
\Gamma\propto W\propto r^{k_{\rm u}}=r^{(0.48-0.51)}
(see Table
2
) in the inner parabolic jet region
(
A. Tchekhovskoy et al. 2008
;
S. S. Komissarov et al. 2009
;
Y. Lyubarsky 2009
)
. In contrast, the FSRQ 1928+738 shows a steeper “linear acceleration” profile, following
\Gamma\propto W\propto r^{0.46}

(
K. Yi et al. 2024
, see a detailed discussion therein)
.

IV.2.2
Jet Kinematics at the ACZ Boundary and Far Beyond

As shown in Figures

and
8
, the jet kinematics exhibits a degree of complexity prior to the end of the ACZ, featuring a rapid deceleration followed by reacceleration. This behavior may reflect intricate internal dynamics or instabilities associated with the transition in jet collimation (see Section
III.2.2
). However, the physical mechanism underlying this behavior remains uncertain. One possible explanation involves the formation of a recollimation shock near the boundary of the ACZ (see Appendix
F
), which may induce strong instabilities that affect the propagation of the jet
(
Y. Mizuno et al. 2015
;
A. Costa et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

On the other hand,
R. A. Laing & A. H. Bridle 2014
reported a flow speed of
\beta_{\rm int}=0.92
for the kiloparsec-scale jet in NGC 4261. Given that the jet gradually decelerates to sub-relativistic speeds beyond approximately 1.5 pc (see Figures
7
and
8
), we propose that another acceleration mechanism should be at work on kiloparsec scales. Interestingly,
J. Park et al. 2021
also indicated the presence of an additional acceleration zone in the kiloparsec-scale, conically expanding jet in NGC 315.

IV.3
Comparison with M 87 and NGC 1052

A brief comparison of the ACZs between NGC 4261 and M 87 (see also Table
3
):

–

Both jets show a parabolic-to-conical structural transition. In M 87, this transition occurs close to its Bondi radius
(
K. Asada & M. Nakamura 2012
)
, whereas in NGC 4261 the transition takes place only at
r\lesssim 1.5
pc, well within its Bondi radius.

–

Assuming a single power-law dependence of the Lorentz factor on the radial distance, both jets show a trend of gradual acceleration within their respective acceleration zones. For M 87 this is characterized by
\Gamma\propto r^{0.16\pm 0.01}

(
F. Mertens et al. 2016
;
J. Park et al. 2019
)
, while for NGC 4261 it is
\Gamma\propto r^{0.21\pm 0.04}
(at distances of
1000R_{\rm s}-3000R_{\rm s}
). Their maximum Lorentz factors are
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 10
and
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 2.6
, respectively.

–

These results suggest a co-spatial, causally connected ACZ in both LLAGNs. However, while the ACZ of M 87 almost extends to its Bondi radius, the ACZ of NGC 4261 is confined to a region smaller than 1.5 pc, which is much less than its Bondi radius of
\sim 99.2
pc.

–

High-energy
\gamma
-ray emission has been detected in these two low-luminosity radio galaxies. These emissions may originate from different jet regions, including the core/subparsec-scale jet and the larger hundred-parsec/kiloparsec-scale jet
(
F. Aharonian et al. 2006
;
C. C. Cheung et al. 2007
;
D. E. Harris et al. 2009
;
A. A. Abdo et al. 2009
;
K. Hada et al. 2014
;
R. de Menezes et al. 2020
;
G. Tomar et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.

–

The recollimation shock has been clearly observed at the end of the ACZ in M 87
(
Ł. Stawarz et al. 2006
;
K. Asada & M. Nakamura 2012
, i.e., HST-1; e.g.,)
, while our results for NGC 4261 also hint at a possible signature of recollimation at the boundary of its ACZ (see Appendix
F
).

–

Based on the above comparisons, we tentatively suggest that the jet ACZ of NGC 4261 is likely a scaled-down counterpart of that in M 87.

On the other hand, another nearby LLAGN, NGC 1052, also deserves attention (see Table
3
):

–

Similar to NGC 4261, the NGC 1052 jet is also inclined at a large viewing angle of
\phi_{\rm view}=64^{\circ}-87^{\circ}

(
M. Kadler et al. 2004
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
.

–

Interestingly, studies have suggested a nearly symmetric structural transition on both sides of NGC 1052: a parabolic-to-cylindrical transition at around
3\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
and a cylindrical-to-conical transition at a distance of about
1\times 10^{4}\,R_{\rm s}

(
S. Nakahara et al. 2020
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2022
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2024
)
. Notably, the second transition occurs on the subparsec scale of
\sim 0.15
pc, which is comparable to the torus size of 0.5 pc
(
S. Kameno et al. 2001
;
S. Kameno et al. 2003
;
S. Kameno et al. 2020
;
S. Kameno et al. 2023
;
S. Sawada-Satoh et al. 2008
)
. As discussed by
S. Nakahara et al. 2020
and
A. K. Baczko et al. 2022
, the jet confinement in NGC 1052 may be influenced by this dense, optically-thick torus.

–

Kinematic studies in NGC 1052 indicate that jet acceleration may occur at distances of
\lesssim 10^{4}\,R_{\rm s}
, which is likely to coincide with its collimation process
(
A. K. Baczko et al. 2019
;
A. K. Baczko et al. 2022
)
.

–

If this is the case, NGC 1052 may host a subparsec-scale ACZ, similar to that observed in NGC 4261 by this work.

–

NGC 4261 and NGC 1052 form a unique pair in which both the collimation profiles of the jet and counterjet have been measured in detail. Following the collimation studies in NGC 1052, we propose that jet confinement in NGC 4261 may also be influenced by a circumnuclear torus, although the physical parameters of this torus remain uncertain.

IV.4
Caveats and Future Prospects

IV.4.1
Observational Limitations

It should be noted that NGC 4261 lies near the celestial equator (declination
\delta\approx+6^{\circ}
), while the VLBA stations are distributed primarily along the east–west direction. This configuration results in poor sampling of north–south spatial frequencies in the
(u,v)
coverage. Consequently, our results may be influenced by these overall image fidelity limitations.

IV.4.2
Limitations in Kinematic Analysis

Notably, the archival data used in this study are constrained by limited sensitivity (see Table
1
), resulting in insufficient S/N for the extended twin jets in some images (e.g., Figure
1
, right). Low S/N can introduce significant uncertainties in position measurements and may also affect the accuracy of brightness ratio estimates for the faint, extended jet emission.
In addition, the shape of the convolving beam – whether circular, elliptical, or over-resolved – can influence jet parameters derived from image-plane measurements, such as the radial intensity profile, jet-to-counterjet brightness ratio, and spectral index. These effects, combined with low S/N, are key factors that could limit the accuracy of our velocity estimates. Finally, it should also be emphasized that our measurements are confined to a limited physical region of only
\sim(10^{3}-2\times 10^{4})\,R_{\rm s}
.

IV.4.3
Strategies to Mitigate Limitations

To mitigate these limitations, it is important to (1) employ interferometric arrays with baseline configurations that offer improved
(u,v)
coverage for NGC 4261, and (2) adopt advanced imaging and kinematic analysis techniques. In future work, our aim is to further investigate the jet properties of NGC 4261 using observational data from various arrays and frequencies, including the East Asia VLBI Network, the High Sensitivity Array, and the joint European VLBI Network with e-MERLIN. In parallel, we will apply advanced imaging techniques such as the Bayesian imaging method
(
J.-S. Kim et al. 2024
;
J.-S. Kim et al. 2025
)
and the Regularized Maximum Likelihood method
(
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
, along with kinematic analysis techniques including the wavelet-based image segmentation and evaluation method
(
F. Mertens et al. 2016
, e.g.,)
.

IV.4.4
Future Prospects

At higher frequencies, observations with the Global mm-VLBI Array, (ng)EHT
(
S. S. Doeleman et al. 2023
)
), and the next-generation VLA
(
E. J. Murphy et al. 2018
;
M. Kadler et al. 2023
, ngVLA;)
will be crucial to probe jet behavior in the immediate vicinity of the SMBH. For example, what are the width
(
J. Y. Kim et al. 2018
;
R.-S. Lu et al. 2023
;
Y. Cui & W. Lin 2024
, cf. M 87;)
and velocity profiles of the innermost jet down to a few tens of
R_{\rm s}
? Does the transverse jet structure exhibit a limb-brightened morphology, as recently discovered in other nearby sources (see
J. Park et al. 2024
and references therein)? These studies will lay a critical foundation for future efforts to image the black hole shadow of NGC 4261 with (ng)EHT
(
X. A. Zhang et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
.

Following M 87, 1H 0323+342, NGC 315, and 1928+738 (see references in Section
I
), we note that NGC 4261 is the fifth radio-loud AGN (and the third LLAGN) to show compelling evidence of simultaneous jet acceleration and collimation. Expanding such studies to a larger AGN sample
(
Boccardi, B. et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
will be crucial to examine the diversities of jet ACZs, their dependence on central engine parameters (e.g., black hole mass, accretion mode, and magnetization) and external physical environments (e.g., disk winds), and their role in jet evolution and high-energy emission. Moreover, these investigations will provide insight into the mechanisms driving the formation of relativistic jets
(
R. Blandford et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
and enhance our understanding of AGN feedback as well as the coevolution of SMBHs with their host galaxies
(
J. Kormendy & L. C. Ho 2013
, e.g.,)
.

V
Summary

We have studied the acceleration and collimation of the two-sided jets in NGC 4261 using archival VLBA data, supplemented by results from the literature, covering a frequency range from 1.4 to 88 GHz. Our main findings are summarized as follows.

1.

We conducted a detailed analysis of the jet collimation profile using multiple methods, including deriving the deconvolved jet width, modeling the jet structure with circular Gaussian functions, and examining the multifrequency properties of the VLBI core. The consistency between our results and previous studies (
83
;
121
) provides robust evidence for a structural transition from a parabolic to a conical geometry in both the jet and counterjet. In particular, this transition occurs at
(1.23\pm 0.24)
pc
\approx(8.1\pm 1.6)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
for the jet and
(0.97\pm 0.29)
pc
\approx(6.4\pm 1.9)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
for the counterjet, both of which are substantially smaller than the Bondi radius of the central SMBH (
r_{\rm Bondi}\approx 99.2
pc
\approx 6.5\times 10^{5}\,R_{\rm s}
). This implies that external pressure could play a key role in jet confinement. The striking symmetry of the width profiles between the jet and counterjet (see Table
2
and Figure
2
) further suggests that the jet collimation is more likely to be regulated by the global state of the circumnuclear environment rather than local conditions.

2.

Assuming that the observed brightness asymmetry between the jet and counterjet is primarily due to Doppler boosting and de-boosting effects, we derived the jet velocity field at distances from
\sim 10^{3}
to
\sim 2\times 10^{4}\,R_{\rm s}
based on the jet-to-counterjet brightness ratio and spectral index. Supposing a simple power-law dependence of the Lorentz factor on the radial distance, we find
\Gamma\propto r^{0.21\pm 0.04}
within
(1-3)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, indicative of a “slow acceleration” on these scales. At larger distances of
(4-8)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, the jet shows a mildly complex kinematic behavior characterized by a rapid deceleration followed by reacceleration. We note that this region appears to be spatially coincident with the collimation transition zone,
(6.4\pm 1.9)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}\lesssim r\lesssim(8.1\pm 1.6)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, potentially reflecting the complicated internal dynamics and/or strong instabilities associated with the structural transition. Further studies are critical to explore the origin of such jet dynamics or instabilities (e.g., the formation of a recollimation shock at the collimation break zone; see Appendix
F
) and to examine whether similar kinematic patterns are present in other AGNs.

3.

Despite these local kinematic variations, the jet undergoes an overall acceleration to relativistic speeds over the range
\sim(1-8)\times 10^{3}\,R_{\rm s}
, with a maximum Lorentz factor of
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 2.6
. Beyond this region, it gradually decelerates to sub-relativistic speeds. The close spatial coincidence of the acceleration and collimation zones supports the existence of a co-spatial ACZ in NGC 4261, which is notably confined to the (sub)parsec scales of
r\lesssim 1.5
pc, corresponding to
r\lesssim 10^{4}\,R_{\rm s}
.

4.

We propose that within the (sub)parsec-scale ACZ, the parabolic jet in NGC 4261 may be confined by external pressure from the ADAF and disk wind (provided that the wind does not extend too far from the central SMBH), and the circumnuclear torus may also be a possible contributor. Meanwhile, the jet is gradually accelerated to relativistic speeds through the conversion of magnetic energy into kinetic energy. Furthermore, based on the maximum Lorentz factor of the jet, we tentatively suggest that the jet base in NGC 4261 may not be highly magnetized, resembling NGC 315 but differing from M 87. This may qualitatively explain both the (sub)parsec-scale structural transition and the observed “slow acceleration” in NGC 4261.

Appendix E
Structural Transition at (Sub)Parsec Scales in the Context of a Magnetically Driven Jet Model

We estimate the initial jet magnetization parameter (
\sigma_{0}
) under the framework of a magnetically driven jet, in which magnetic-to-kinetic energy conversion dictates
(
L. Chen & B. Zhang 2021
)
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\gtrsim\Gamma_{\rm max},

(E1)

where
\Gamma_{\rm max}
is the maximum Lorentz factor of the jet. For NGC 4261, our observational constraints on
\Gamma_{\rm max}
imply
\sigma_{0}\approx\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 2.6
(see Section
III.2.2
)
6
6

6

However, as tightly constraining the magnetization remains challenging and an open issue, this estimation should be regarded as a simplified approximation.
.

In the scenario that the jet is launched by a spinning black hole (i.e., the BZ mechanism), the transition from a (quasi-)parabolic, magnetically dominated jet to a conical, kinetically dominated jet is predicted to occur at a radial distance of
(
L. Chen & B. Zhang 2021
)
:

\displaystyle r_{\rm t}\approx\varpi_{h}\left(\frac{c}{\omega\varpi_{h}}\sigma_{0}\right)^{1/k_{\rm u}},

(E2)

where
\varpi_{h}=2GM_{\rm BH}/c^{2}
is the effective radius (or Schwarzschild radius),
\omega=\frac{ac}{2(1+\sqrt{1-a^{2}})GM_{\rm BH}/c^{2}}
is the angular velocity of the black hole horizon at the equator, and
k_{\rm u}
is the power-law index of the upstream jet width profile (see Eq.
1
).

LLAGNs typically host more slowly spinning black holes compared to powerful AGNs like blazars. We therefore assume a dimensionless spin parameter of
a=0.1
for NGC 4261. Using the black hole mass of
M_{\rm BH}=1.62\times 10^{9}\,M_{\odot}
(see Section
I
), we obtain
r_{\rm t}\approx 0.6
pc for the approaching jet (
k_{\rm u}=0.48\pm 0.07
) and
r_{\rm t}\approx 0.4
pc for the counterjet (
k_{\rm u}=0.51\pm 0.07
) (see Table
2
). These results predict a subparsec-scale transition in the jet collimation profile, which is generally consistent with the observations (see Section
III.1
).

We note that a comparable maximum Lorentz factor of
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 3
has also been reported for NGC 315 by
J. Park et al. 2021
. Under the equipartition assumption at the end of the acceleration zone,
J. Park et al. 2021
indicated that the jet base of NGC 315 may not be highly magnetized. In addition, they found that the collimation break occurs at a distance roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the Bondi radius.

In contrast, sources such as 1H 0323+342 (NLS1), M 87, and 1928+738 (FSRQ) show significantly higher Lorentz factors of
\Gamma_{\rm max}\approx 10

(
K. Hada et al. 2018
;
J. Park et al. 2019
;
K. Yi et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
, which may indicate a relatively high level of initial magnetization. Notably, their ACZs extend to, or even beyond, their respective Bondi radii. In particular, for M 87, both theoretical and observational studies have consistently suggested that its jet base is likely highly magnetized
(
M. Kino et al. 2014
;
M. Kino et al. 2015
;
J. Y. Kim et al. 2018
;
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration et al. 2021a
;
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration et al. 2021b
;
F. Yuan et al. 2022
)
. Therefore, we tentatively suggest that the jet base of NGC 4261 may not be highly magnetized, resembling NGC 315 but differing from M 87, which may explain both the (sub)parsec-scale geometry transition and “slow acceleration” of its jet (see Section
IV.2.1
).

Finally, theoretical work by
L. Chen & B. Zhang 2021
also suggests that within the (quasi)parabolic zone, the jet can be divided into an inner, poloidally dominated region and an outer, toroidally dominated region, with the transition occurring at the Alfvén radius,

\displaystyle r_{\rm a}\approx\varpi_{h}\left(\frac{c}{\omega\varpi_{h}}\right)^{1/k_{\rm u}}=r_{\rm t}\sigma_{0}^{-1/k_{\rm u}},

(E3)

For NGC 4261, this transition is predicted to occur at
r_{\rm a}\approx(300-400)\,R_{\rm s}
. Future high-resolution and high-sensitivity polarimetric observations of NGC 4261 should be able to test this predicted change in magnetic field geometry.

Appendix F
Possible Hint of a Recollimation Shock at the ACZ End of NGC 4261

The formation of a recollimation shock at the end of the ACZ has been proposed in several sources, including BL Lacertae
(
A. P. Marscher et al. 2008
)
, PKS 1510-089
(
A. P. Marscher et al. 2010
)
, and OJ 287
(
I. Agudo et al. 2011
)
. In particular, clear evidence for such shocks has been found in the jet collimation profiles of M 87
(
Ł. Stawarz et al. 2006
;
K. Asada & M. Nakamura 2012
, e.g.,)
and 1H 0323+342
(
A. Doi et al. 2018
;
K. Hada et al. 2018
)
. Specifically, an evident dip in jet width is seen near the location of the structural break, where the observed width is smaller than expected from a parabolic profile.

For NGC 4261, as shown in Figure
12
, we identify a dip in the unbinned deconvolved width profile, enabled by the high-quality 8.4 GHz data from 1999 February 26 (see Table
1
). This feature may hint at the presence of a recollimation shock in the structural transition region, although confirmation will require higher-resolution observations.

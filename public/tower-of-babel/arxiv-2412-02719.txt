On the curvature symmetries in lightlike hypersurfaces of an indefinite Sasakian manifold
Samuel Ssekajja

Abstract
. In this note, we show that a lightlike hypersurface of an indefinite Sasakian manifold, which is tangent to structure vector field is not locally symmetric, semi-symmetric or semi-parallel.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C25; Secondary 53C40, 53C50

1.
Introduction

The study of curvature symmetries in lightlike hypersurfaces
(M,g)
of semi-Euclidean spaces
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
were introduced and studied by B. Sahin
[
12
]
. In that paper, the author defines semi-symmetric hypersurfaces to be those satisfying the condition
R\cdot R=0
, where
R
is the induced curvature tensor of the hypersurface (see Definition 4). Other symmetries introduced therein are Ricci semi-symmetry, semi-parallel as well as local symmetry, in which the following conditions are assumed
R\cdot\mathrm{Ric}=0
,
\tilde{R}\cdot h=0
and
\nabla R=0
, respectively. Here,
\mathrm{Ric}
denotes the induced Ricci tensor of the hypersurface,
\tilde{R}
the curvature tensor of its Van der Waerden-Bortolotti connection, which is built from its induced connection
\nabla
and its transversal connection, and
h
its second fundamental form. The results of the above paper motivated other authors to study the same hypersurfaces in different ambient spaces. For example, see
[
6
,
10
,
11
,
16
]
. Although it seems natural to extend the study of such hypersurfaces in other spaces such as indefinite almost contact metric manifolds
(\bar{M},\bar{\phi},\zeta,\eta,\bar{g})
, like indefinite Sasakain manifolds, the presence of the structure vector field
\zeta
posses some hidden challenges. In fact, the position of
\zeta
relative to any hypersurface greatly affects its geometry. For example, we have showed, in this paper, that if a lightlike hypersurface of an indefinite Sasakian manifold is tangent to
\zeta
, then it can not exhibit any of the above symmetries. These findings can be seen in Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
,
5.2
and
6.2
of this paper. Our results greatly affects a number of prior assumptions in which such hypersurfaces were assumed to exist in indefinite Sasakian space forms, i.e. Sasakian spaces of constant
\bar{\phi}
-holomorphic sectional curvatures, such as in
[
10
]
and
[
16
]
.

The study of lightlike hypersurfaces has attracted the attention of many authors since its introduction by K. L. Duggal and A. Bejancu
[
3
]
. This is due to their numerous applications in mathematical physics and electromagetism. More findings on the study of lightlike hypersurfaces, and submifoolds in general, can be found in
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
13
]
, and any other references cited therein. The rest of this paper is arranged as follows: In Sections
2
and
3
, we quote some basic information on lightlike hypersurfaces and indefinite Sasakain manifolds used in this paper. Sections
4
,
5
, and
6
, we investigate locally symmetric, semi-symmetric and semi-parallel lightlike hypersurfaces of an indefinite Sasakian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{\phi},\zeta,\eta,\bar{g})
, which are tangent to the structure vector field
\zeta
.

2.
Preliminaries

An odd-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold
\bar{M}
is called an indefinite Sasakian manifold
[
14
,
15
]
if there exists a
(1,1)
-tensor field
\bar{\phi}
, a vector field
\zeta
, called structure vector field, a 1-form
\eta
and an indefinite metric tensor
\bar{g}
on
\bar{M}
, such that

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{2}X

\displaystyle=-X+\eta(X)\zeta,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\eta(\xi)

\displaystyle=1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\eta(\bar{\phi}X)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(X,\xi)

\displaystyle=\eta(X),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}X,\bar{\phi}Y)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),

(2.5)

\displaystyle d\eta(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}X,Y),

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\bar{\phi})Y

\displaystyle=\eta(Y)X-g(X,Y)\zeta,

(2.7)

where
X
and
Y
are any tangent vector fields to
\bar{M}
, and
\bar{\nabla}
is the semi-Riemannian connection of
\bar{g}
. Replacing
Y
with
\zeta
in (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{X}\zeta=\bar{\phi}X,

(2.8)

for all
X
tangent to
\bar{M}
. Let
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
be a
(m+2)
-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold of index
q
, where
0<q<m+2
. Let
(M,g)
be a hypersurface of
\bar{M}
, where
g
denotes the metric tensor field induced on

by

. Then,

is called a lightlike hypersurface of

whenever
rank(g)=m
. In this case, the normal vector bundle
TM^{\perp}
intersects the tangent
bundle
TM
of

along a nontrivial differentiable distribution known as the radical distribution of
M
, denoted as
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
, and given by
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM:p\mapsto\mathrm{Rad}\,T_{p}M=T_{p}M\cap T_{p}M^{\perp}
. Furthermore, it is known
[
3
,
4
]
that the complementary bundle to
TM^{\perp}
in
TM
, called the screen distribution, denoted by
S(TM)
, is non-degenerate and the following decomposition holds:

\displaystyle TM=S(TM)\perp\mathrm{Rad}\,TM,

(2.9)

where
\perp
denotes the orthogonal direct sum. Moreover, a result due to
[
3
,
4
]
shows that there exists a unique vector bundle
\mathrm{tr}(TM)
, called the lightlike transversal bundle of
M
with respect to
S(TM)
, of rank 1 over
M
such that for any non-zero section
\xi
of
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
on a coordinate neighbourhood
\mathcal{U}\subset M
, there exists a unique section
N
of
\mathrm{tr}(TM)
on
\mathcal{U}
satisfying the conditions

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\xi,N)=1,\quad\bar{g}(N,N)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{g}(N,W)=0,

(2.10)

for any
W
tangent to
S(TM)
. As a consequence, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle T\bar{M}|_{M}

\displaystyle=S(TM)\perp\{\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus\mathrm{tr}(TM)\}=TM\oplus\mathrm{tr}(TM),

(2.11)

where
\oplus
denotes a direct sum, not necessarily orthogonal.

Let
\bar{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{M}
and let

be the projection morphism of
TM
onto

, with respect to (

are given by (see details in

4
]

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y+B(X,Y)N,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{X}N

\displaystyle=-A_{N}X+\nabla^{t}_{X}N=-A_{N}X+\tau(X)N,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}PY

\displaystyle=\nabla^{*}_{X}PY+h^{*t}(X,PY)=\nabla^{*}_{X}PY+C(X,PY)\xi,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\xi

\displaystyle=-A^{*}_{\xi}X+\nabla^{*t}_{X}\xi=-A^{*}_{\xi}X-\tau(X)\xi,

(2.15)

for all
X
and
Y
tangent to
M
,
\xi
tangent to
\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
and
N
tangent to
\mathrm{tr}(TM)
. In the above,
\nabla
and
\nabla^{*}
are the induced linear connections on
TM
and
S(TM)
, respectively. Furthermore,
h
and

denotes the second fundamental form and local second fundamental form of

and

\mathrm{tr}(TM)

(2.18)

for any

M
. Due to the first relation in (

), we notice that the 1-form

\displaystyle\theta(\xi)=\bar{g}(\xi,N)=1.

(2.19)

3.
Basic constructions on lightlike hypersurfaces

Consinder a lightlike hypersurface
(M,g)
of an indefinite Sasakian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{\phi},\zeta,\eta,\bar{g})
, which is tangent to the structure vector field
\zeta
. C. Calin
[
1
]
has proved that
\zeta
belongs to the screen distribution
S(TM)
. In this paper, we assume that
M
is tangent to
\zeta
. From the fact
\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}\xi,\xi)=0
, we notice that the lightlike vector field
\bar{\phi}\xi
is tangent to
M
. Thus, we consider a screen distribution
S(TM)
on
M
containing
\bar{\phi}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM
as a vector subbundle. Consequently,
N
is orthogonal to
\bar{\phi}\xi
and we have
\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}N,\xi)=-\bar{g}(N,\bar{\phi}\xi)=0
. Since
\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}N,N)=0
, it follows that
\bar{\phi}N
is tangent to
M
and in particular, it belongs to
S(TM)
. Consequently,
\bar{\phi}\mathrm{tr}(TM)
is also a vector subbundle of
S(TM)
. Since
\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}\xi,\bar{\phi}N)=1
, it follows that
\bar{\phi}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus\bar{\phi}\mathrm{tr}(TM)
is a non-degenerate vector subbbundle of
S(TM)
, with 2-dimensional fibers. Since
\zeta
is tangent to
M
, and that
\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}\xi,\zeta)=\bar{g}(\bar{\phi}N,\zeta)=0
, then there exists a non-degenerate distribution
D_{0}
on
TM
such that

\displaystyle S(TM)=\{\bar{\phi}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus\bar{\phi}\mathrm{tr}(TM)\}\perp D_{0}\perp\langle\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}},

(3.1)

where
\langle\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the line bundle spanned by the structure vector field
\zeta
. Furthermore, it is easy to check that
D_{0}
is an almost complex distribution with respect to
\bar{\phi}
, that is;
\bar{\phi}D_{0}=D_{0}
. Moreover, from (
2.9
) and (
3.1
),
TM
is decomposed as

\displaystyle TM=\{\bar{\phi}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\oplus\bar{\phi}\mathrm{tr}(TM)\}\perp D_{0}\perp\langle\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\perp TM^{\perp}.

(3.2)

If we set
D=\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\perp\bar{\phi}\mathrm{Rad}\,TM\perp D_{0}
and
D^{\prime}=\bar{\phi}\mathrm{tr}(TM)
, then (
3.2
) becomes

\displaystyle TM=D\oplus D^{\prime}\perp\langle\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.3)

Here,
D
is an almost complex distribution and
D^{\prime}
is carried by
\bar{\phi}
into the transversal bundle. Consider the lightlike vector fields
U
and
V
given by

\displaystyle U=-\bar{\phi}N,\quad V=-\bar{\phi}\xi,

(3.4)

together with their corresponding
1
-forms
u
and
v
given by

\displaystyle u(X)=g(X,V),\quad v(X)=g(X,U),

(3.5)

for any
X
tangent to
M
. Then, from (
3.3
), any
X
tangent to
M
can be written as
X=P_{1}X+P_{2}X+\eta(X)\zeta
, where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are projection morphisms of
TM
onto
D
and
D^{\prime}
, respectively. It then follows that, for any
X
tangent to
M
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}X=\phi X+u(X)N,

(3.6)

where
\phi
is a (1,1)-tensor field defined on
M
by
\phi X=\bar{\phi}P_{1}X
. Also, the following relations are easy to verify:

\displaystyle\phi^{2}X

\displaystyle=-X+\eta(X)\zeta+u(X)U,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\phi\zeta

\displaystyle=0,\quad\phi U=0,\quad\phi V=\xi,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\eta(\zeta)=1,\quad

\displaystyle\eta(U)=0,\quad\eta(V)=0,\quad\eta(\phi X)=0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle u(\zeta)=0,\quad

\displaystyle u(U)=1,\quad u(V)=0,\quad u(\phi X)=0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle v(\zeta)=0,\quad v

\displaystyle(U)=0,\quad v(V)=1,\quad v(\phi X)=-\theta(X),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\theta(\zeta)=0,\quad

\displaystyle\theta(U)=0,\quad\theta(V)=0,\quad\theta(\phi X)=v(X),

(3.12)

\displaystyle g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g

4.
Locally symmetric lightlike hypersurfaces

In this section, we prove that a lightlike hypersurface
(M,g)
of an indefinite Sasakian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{\phi},\zeta,\eta,\bar{g})
, which is tangent to structure vector field
\zeta
is not locally symmetric (see Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
). Let
R
be the curvature tensor of the induced linear connection
\nabla
. For every
X
,
Y
,
Z
and
W
tangent to
M
, the covariant derivative
\nabla R
of
R
is given by

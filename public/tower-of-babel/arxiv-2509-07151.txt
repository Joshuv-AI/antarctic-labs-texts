Exploring the nature of the emergent gauge field in composite-fermion metals: A large-scale microscopic study
Amogh Anakru, Mytraya Gattu, Ajit C. Balram, Xiao-Chuan Wu, Prashant Kumar, Zhen Bi, J. K. Jain

Abstract
Field theories of the composite-fermion (CF) metal model it as a Fermi sea of composite fermions coupled to an emergent gauge field. Within a random phase approximation, these theories predict that the Landau damping of the gauge field resulting from its coupling to the low-energy, long-wavelength CF particle-hole excitations modifies the electrons’ density-density correlation function related to the static structure factor S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) at wave vector q q . This produces a non-analytic correction ∝ q 3 ​ ln ⁡ q \propto q^{3}\ln q to S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) (with the magnetic length ℓ B = 1 \ell_{B}=1 ). Thanks to the recently developed quaternion formulation for Jain-Kamilla projection of CF wave functions, the evaluation of S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) from the accurate microscopic theory of composite fermions has now become possible for systems containing as many as N = 900 N=900 CFs, which enables a reliable determination of the small- q q behavior of S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) . We study CF metals corresponding to electrons at Landau level filling factors ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 and 1 / 4 1/4 , and for completeness, also of bosons at ν = 1 \nu=1 and 1 / 3 1/3 . In the q → 0 q\rightarrow 0 limit, our microscopic calculation reveals a q 3 q^{3} term in S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) of the CF metals rather than q 3 ​ ln ⁡ q q^{3}\ln q . This behavior is well-predicted by a model of a non-interacting Fermi sea of dipolar CFs, which also obtains its coefficient accurately.



Introduction.
Electrons in metals typically form Fermi liquids (FLs). One obtains a simple Fermi sea when interactions are switched off, with the occupation number dropping discontinuously from 1 to 0 across the Fermi wave number
k_{F}
. The central tenet of the FL theory is that when the interparticle interaction is turned on, the jump at
k_{F}
becomes smaller but remains finite, resulting in long-lived electron-like quasiparticles sufficiently close to the Fermi energy
Mahan 2000
;
Giuliani and Vignale 2008
. A strong enough interaction, however, can cause this jump to vanish, thereby yielding what is termed a non-Fermi liquid (NFL)
Halperin et al. 1993
;
Nayak and Wilczek 1994
;
Lee et al. 2006
;
Senthil 2008
;
Sachdev 2011
.

Of interest to us here is the system of interacting electrons in the half-filled lowest Landau level (LLL). No Fermi sea exists in the absence of interactions—the system is macroscopically degenerate. Turning on a repulsive interaction, no matter how small, has the non-perturbative effect of generating composite fermions (CFs)
Jain 1989
, namely bound states of electrons and an even number (
p
) of vortices. At even-denominator fillings
\nu=1/p
, the CFs experience zero effective magnetic field and form a Fermi-sea-like state. Here, the interaction creates a Fermi-sea-like state where there was none to begin with
Jain 1989
;
Halperin et al. 1993
. This CF metal is one of the most dramatic examples of an NFL of electrons, whose low-energy excitations are topologically distinct from electrons. However, one may ask: To what extent does this state resemble a regular FL of CFs?

Experimentally, the CF metal at
\nu=1/2
has been investigated extensively
Shayegan 2020
and displays many familiar hallmarks of an ordinary FL: it has a sharply defined Fermi wave vector which has been measured with great precision
Kamburov et al. 2014
; it obeys the Luttinger area rule
Luttinger and Ward 1960
;
Luttinger 1960
to high accuracy and with little sensitivity to interaction strength (or LL mixing)
Balram et al. 2015
;
Geraedts et al. 2016
;
Balram and Jain 2017
;
Hossain et al. 2020
; it exhibits quantum oscillations near half filling
Du et al. 1994
; and it hosts high-order Jain states corresponding to the integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE) of CFs
Chung et al. 2021
.

In the field-theoretic descriptions of Halperin-Lee-Read (HLR)
Halperin et al. 1993
;
Simon 1998
;
Halperin 2003
, the CF metal is distinguished from regular FLs in that, in addition to the inter-CF interaction, the CFs are also coupled to an emergent Chern-Simons gauge field arising from flux attachment
Lopez and Fradkin 1991
. This field interacts strongly with the continuum of low-energy, long-wavelength CF particle-hole excitations available in the compressible CF metal. Within the random-phase approximation (RPA), such coupling causes a Landau damping of the gauge field and modifies the long-wavelength density-density response. The Landau-damped gauge field dresses the CFs, destroying the long-lived quasiparticles at the Fermi surface, eliminating the discontinuous jump in the CF occupation number at
k_{F}

Nayak and Wilczek 1994
;
Sachdev 2011
. Another consequence of this Landau-damped gauge field is that the ground-state static structure factor
S(\bm{q})
contains a non-analytic
q^{3}\ln q
term (the magnetic length
\ell_{B}=\sqrt{\hbar c/eB}
at magnetic field
B
is set to unity hereafter):

S(\bm{q})=\frac{q^{2}}{2}+\frac{2-\eta}{2\pi}k_{F}q^{3}\ln\!\left(\frac{q_{0}}{q}\right)+\mathcal{O}(q^{4}),

(1)

where
U(q)\sim q^{-\eta}
,

The objective of this work is to determine whether the

Earlier studies have investigated this question. Balram and Jain

Microscopic calculation of

.
To extract the bulk physics of the CF metal, we work in Haldane’s spherical geometry

In this setup, particles are confined to the surface of a sphere of radius

The single-particle eigenstates are the monopole harmonics

We mention some possible sources of this discrepancy. In the HLR theory, at the mean-field level, the CFs are defined by flux attachment, implemented through a singular gauge transformation. The mean field state
\prod_{j<k}[(z_{j}-z_{k})/|z_{j}-z_{k}|]^{p}\Phi_{n}(B)

;
Balram and Jain 2016

in a weak-coupling perturbative renormalization-group approach

;
Mross et al. 2010

) at large sizes could not be ruled out. By contrast, our present study - effectively in the thermodynamic limit - does not find the contribution of the Landau-damped emergent gauge field to the static structure factor.

In summary, we have presented a microscopic computation of the static structure factor for various CF metals and found that the results are in disagreement with the predictions of the effective field theories of CFs in the small
q
limit. We then show that the behavior is accurately described by modeling the CF metal as a non-interacting CFFS. In the future, it would be interesting to investigate other quantities from a microscopic approach—such as the entanglement entropy
Shao et al. 2015
;
Voinea et al. 2025
, and fluctuations of conserved quantities generalizing the results of
Wu 2025
;
Wu et al. 2024
—to better understand the role of the emergent gauge field.

Appendix A
Static Structure Factors from Field Theories

In this section, we review and discuss the predictions of the static structure factor
S(q)
from the various field-theoretic perspectives of the CF metal that have been proposed over the years
Halperin et al. 1993
;
Son 2015
;
Wang and Senthil 2016
. In all such theories, the CFs are coupled to a dynamical gauge field that implements flux attachment. While the CFs themselves form a Fermi surface, their coupling to gapless gauge fluctuations gives rise to a non-Fermi-liquid state
Halperin et al. 1993
;
Nayak and Wilczek 1994
;
Polchinski 1994
. Despite considerable efforts over the years, a fully controlled theoretical description of the corresponding RG fixed point remains elusive.

The electromagnetic response of the CF metal differs significantly from that of the Landau FL. First of all, in the simplest scenario, even if the CFs form a mean-field state that resembles a Fermi gas, they are not gauge-invariant. The physical response function
\Pi_{e}^{\mu\nu}(\omega,q)
is determined by the Ioffe-Larkin rule
Ioffe and Larkin 1989
;
Lee and Nagaosa 1992
;
Halperin et al. 1993
,
\Pi_{e}^{-1}=\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}^{-1}+\Pi_{\textrm{CS}}^{-1}
, where
\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}
is the response function of the CFs, and
\Pi_{\textrm{CS}}
is the Chern-Simons kernel. On the other hand, the coupling of the CF Fermi surface to a dynamical gauge field leads to Landau damping, while the associated gauge flux represents the physical electron density.

A common approach to computing the electromagnetic response is the use of the random phase approximation (RPA). We begin by illustrating the idea using the Halperin-Lee-Read (HLR) theory for the half-filled LLL
Halperin et al. 1993
. Here, because the flux of the emergent gauge field is tied to the electron density, the long-wavelength density response is related to the transverse gauge-field propagator at small-
q
. The standard RPA treatment yields

\displaystyle S(\bm{q})=\tfrac{1}{n}\int\tfrac{\textrm{d}\omega}{2\pi}\tfrac{q^{2}/(2\pi p)^{2}}{\frac{q^{2}}{(2\pi p)^{2}}\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}^{\tau\tau}(\omega,q)^{-1}+\frac{q^{2}}{(2\pi p)^{2}}U(q)-\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}^{TT}(\omega,q)},

(9)

where
U>0
corresponds to a repulsive interaction and the longitudinal and transverse components of the response function for a non-relativistic Fermi gas are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}^{\tau\tau}(\omega,q)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}_{F}\left(1-\frac{|\omega|}{\sqrt{\omega^{2}+(v_{F}q)^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\Pi_{\textrm{CF}}^{TT}(\omega,q)

\displaystyle=-\mathscr{D}_{F}\frac{|\omega|\sqrt{\omega^{2}+(v_{F}q)^{2}}-\omega^{2}}{q^{2}}.

(10)

Here,
v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity,
k_{F}
denotes the Fermi momentum, and
\mathscr{D}_{F}=k_{F}/(2\pi v_{F})
represents the density of states at the Fermi level. Following the convention in the main text, we have chosen to normalize
S(\bm{q})
by the particle density
n=\pi k_{F}^{2}/(2\pi)^{2}
; in the
\nu=1/p
state, setting
\ell_{B}=1
fixes
k_{F}=\sqrt{2/p}
since
n=1/2\pi\ell_{B}^{2}p
.

Now we may evaluate Eq. (
9
). If
\omega\gg q
and
U(q)
is less singular than
q^{-2}
,

\begin{split}S(\bm{q})&\approx\tfrac{1}{n}\tfrac{q^{2}}{(2\pi p)^{2}}\int\tfrac{\textrm{d}\omega}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\tfrac{2}{(2\pi p)^{2}\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}^{2}}\omega^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}^{2}}\\
&=\tfrac{q^{2}}{2}\end{split}

(11)

which is nothing but the contribution from the Kohn mode – this is present despite the lack of manifest Galilean invariance in the HLR theory. To obtain the most relevant non-analytic correction, we take the opposite limit
\omega\ll q
. If we expand the denominator of the integrand in Eq. (
9
) to leading order in
\omega
, we have:

\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}\frac{\left|\omega\right|}{\left|q\right|}\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi p)^{2}\mathscr{D}_{F}}q^{2}+\frac{1}{(2\pi p)^{2}}q^{2}U(q)

(12)

which yields a Landau damping pole after analytically continuing to real time. Unless
U(q)
is long-range (i.e. singular as
q\to 0
), the inter-CF interaction will only renormalize the overall coefficient of the Landau damping rate, and we identify the scaling
\omega\sim iq^{3}
. If
U(q)\sim q^{-\eta}
, then we instead have
\omega\sim iq^{3-\eta}
. Evaluating the integral in this limit,

\displaystyle S_{L.D.}(\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{q^{3}}{(2\pi p)^{2}n}\int_{0}^{vq}\frac{\textrm{d}\omega}{\pi}\frac{1}{\frac{q^{3}}{(2\pi p)^{2}\mathscr{D}_{F}}+\frac{q^{3}}{(2\pi p)^{2}}U(q)+\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}\left|\omega\right|}

(13)

where the upper limit of the integral is determined by some scale (
\propto v_{F}q
) where the
\omega\ll q
approximation breaks down. First supposing
U(q)
is regular at
q=0
, and using
\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}=(2\pi)^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{L.D.}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{p/2}}q^{3}\log(q_{0}/q)

(14)

Assuming
v=v_{F}
for simplicity,
q_{0}=\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+\mathscr{D}_{F}U(0)}}
.
If
U(q)=U_{0}q^{-\eta}
,

\displaystyle S_{L.D.}(\bm{q})=\frac{(2-\eta)}{\pi\sqrt{2p}}q^{3}\log(q_{0}/q)

(15)

where
q_{0}^{2-\eta}=(2\pi p)^{2}{\mathscr{D}_{F}v_{F}^{2}}/{U_{0}}
. Note that the dynamical exponent
z
for the transverse gauge field fluctuations is
z=\min(3,3-\eta)
. Putting these contributions together, we have

\displaystyle S(q)=\frac{1}{2}q^{2}+c_{U}q^{3}\log(q_{0}/q)+\mathscr{O}(q^{4}),

(16)

where the coefficient
c_{U}
is determined by the inter-CF interaction
U(q)\sim q^{-\eta}
according to

\displaystyle c_{U}=\begin{cases}1/\pi\sqrt{p/2},&\eta\leq 0\\
(2-\eta)/\pi\sqrt{2p},&0<\eta<2\\
\end{cases}.

(17)

For the special case
U(q)=U_{0}q^{-2}
(
\eta=2
), corresponding to a
\log(r)
interaction in real space, the logarithmic non-analyticity in
S(q)
is replaced by

S_{L.D.}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2p}}\log\left(1+\frac{2\pi\sqrt{2p^{3}}v_{F}}{U_{0}}\right)q^{3}+\ldots,

(18)

where the coefficient of the
q^{3}
term depends on
v_{F}/U_{0}
.
Although this appears to reproduce the microscopic
q^{3}
behavior, it is unclear how this would reproduce the coefficient
2\sqrt{2/p}/3\pi
obtained from our microscopic calculation.

An additional problem arises in the
\omega\gg q
limit; Eq. (
9

S(\bm{q})\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{U_{0}}{2\pi pv_{F}}}}\frac{q^{2}}{2},

(19)

which violates Kohn’s theorem as well as the bound on
q^{2}
term
Wu et al. 2024

with
\eta\geq 2
, so RPA yields physically sensible results only for
0\leq\eta<2
, where the correction to
\overline{S}(q)
is fixed as

.

Lastly, in the Dirac CF theory proposed by Son

, an analogous RPA calculation for emergent gauge field at
\nu=1/2
gives:

\displaystyle S_{\rm Son}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\frac{q^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\frac{q^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}U(q)-\Pi^{TT}_{\rm CF}}

(20)

where
\Pi^{TT}_{\rm CF}
is the same as Eq. (

From Sphere to Plane: Mapping the Static Structure Factor
S(q)

In this section, we begin by analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the static structure factor
S_{l}
for the
\nu=1
integer quantum Hall state, in order to establish its thermodynamic limit and clarify its connection to the planar structure factor
S(q)
. We show that the mapping

, where
R
is the radius of the Haldane sphere, provides the optimal mapping, with

converging to the well-known result

for all

whereas the conventionally used mapping

fails to do so as
q\to 0
. We also show with the mapping

N

q=l/R

B.1
The
\nu=1
IQH state

The
\nu=1
integer quantum Hall state of
N
electrons on the sphere occurs at monopole strength
2Q=N-1

2Q+1

is given by

S_{l}=1-(2Q+1)\begin{pmatrix}Q&Q&l\\
-Q&Q&0\\
\end{pmatrix}^{2}.

(21)

We are interested in how

approaches its planar counterpart
S(q)=1-e^{-q^{2}/2}

Girvin et al. 1986

3j
symbol above can be written in terms of
\Gamma

Varshalovich et al. 1988

\begin{pmatrix}Q&Q&l\\
-Q&Q&0\\
\end{pmatrix}^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(2Q+1)^{2}}{\Gamma(2Q-l+1)\,\Gamma(2Q+l+2)}.

(22)

Substituting
Q=(N-1)/2

S_{l}=1-N\,\frac{\Gamma^{2}(N)}{\Gamma(l)\,\Gamma(N+l+1)}.

(23)

Let us assume that there exists a unique optimal mapping between an angular momentum
l

R=\sqrt{Q}
and a planar momentum

. Thus, the arguments of the

\begin{split}\Gamma(x)&\sim\exp\!\left[-x+x\log x-\tfrac{1}{2}\log x\right.\\
&\left.+\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)+\tfrac{1}{12x}+O(1/x^{3})\right].\end{split}

(24)

Applying this to Eq. (
23

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}S_{l}

\displaystyle=1-\exp\!\left[-\frac{l(l+1)}{N}-\frac{l^{2}(l+1)^{2}}{6N^{3}}+O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N^{4}}\right)\right].

Hence, with the mapping

q^{2}=\frac{2l(l+1)}{N}=\frac{l(l+1)}{Q}+O\!\left(\tfrac{1}{Q^{2}}\right)\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;q=\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{R},

we find that
S_{l}
converges to the planar structure factor in the thermodynamic limit for all values of
q
(or
l

q=l/R
exhibits significant deviations from the expected behavior as

.

Appendix D
Representations of the LLL charge density operator

In this section, we demonstrate that in the
q\to 0
limit,
the GMP operator
\overline{\rho}_{\bm{q}}
admits a unique
representation in terms of single CF particle–hole excitations:

\overline{\rho}_{\bm{q}}=\imath\sum_{\bm{k}}(\bm{q}\times\bm{k})\,c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}}.

In the main text we proposed that the projected density operator
\overline{\overline{\rho}}(\bm{q})
can be represented as

\overline{\overline{\rho}}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}}f(\bm{q},\bm{k})\,c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}},

(25)

where
c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}
and
c_{\bm{k}}
are CF creation and annihilation operators at zero magnetic field (i.e., in the CF metal phase) with the form factor
f(\bm{q},\bm{k})
constrained to behave as

f(\bm{q},\bm{k})\;\xrightarrow{q\to 0}\;i\ell_{B}^{2}\,(\bm{q}\times\bm{k}).

Assuming that the the CF operators obey canonical anti-commutation relations, we now show that this form of
f
is uniquely mandated by the GMP algebra and Kohn’s theorem.

First, we must have
f(\bm{0},\bm{k})=0
in order to treat the ground state as a noninteracting Fermi sea of
c
-operators. If instead
f(\bm{0},\bm{k})=f_{0}(\bm{k})\neq 0
, the projected static structure factor would behave as
\overline{S}(\bm{q}\to 0)\propto|f_{0}(k_{F})|^{2}|\bm{q}|
, in violation of Kohn’s theorem.

Next, the requirement that
\overline{\overline{\rho}}(\bm{q})
satisfies the GMP algebra implies that
f
obeys the functional equation

\begin{split}&\frac{f(\bm{q},\tfrac{\bm{q}^{\prime}}{2}+\bm{k})\,f(\bm{q}^{\prime},-\tfrac{\bm{q}}{2}+\bm{k})-f(\bm{q},-\tfrac{\bm{q}^{\prime}}{2}+\bm{k})\,f(\bm{q}^{\prime},\tfrac{\bm{q}}{2}+\bm{k})}{f(\bm{q}+\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{k})}\\
&=2i\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\bm{q}\times\bm{q}^{\prime}}{2}\Bigr).\end{split}

(26)

To analyze the small-
q
limit, let us denote

\partial_{q_{\alpha}}f(\bm{q},\bm{k})\big|_{q\to 0}=A_{\alpha}(\bm{k}),\quad\partial_{k_{\alpha}}A_{\beta}(\bm{k})=B_{\alpha\beta}(\bm{k}).

(We ignore the
q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}\partial_{q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}}f(\bm{q},\bm{k})|_{q\to 0}
like contributions, as they are not relevant at lowest order in
q,q^{\prime}
.)
Expanding Eq. (
26
) for
q,q^{\prime}\to 0
yields

(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q}^{\prime})(\bm{q}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{A})-(\bm{q}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q})(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{A})=i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{A}+\bm{q}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{A})(\bm{q}\times\bm{q}^{\prime}).

(27)

Setting
\bm{q}^{\prime}=\lambda\bm{q}
gives

(\lambda^{2}-\lambda)(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q})(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{A})=0,

which implies
\bm{q}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q}=0
. Hence
\bm{B}
must be antisymmetric:
\bm{q}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q}^{\prime}=-\bm{q}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q}
. Substituting into Eq. (
27
), we obtain

\bm{q}\cdot\bm{B}\cdot\bm{q}^{\prime}=i\,\bm{q}\times\bm{q}^{\prime},\qquad\implies\quad B_{\alpha\beta}=i\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}.

It follows that

A_{\alpha}(\bm{k})=i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}k_{\beta}+c_{\alpha},

and thus, in general,

f(\bm{q},\bm{k})\big|_{q\to 0}=c_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}+i\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}q_{\alpha}k_{\beta}.

(28)

The first term, proportional to
c_{\alpha}
, breaks rotational invariance; hence in the isotropic CF metal we must set
c_{\alpha}=0
. Therefore we conclude that, uniquely,

f(\bm{q},\bm{k})=i\ell_{B}^{2}\bm{q}\times\bm{k}\qquad(q\to 0).

(29)

D.1
Relation to parton constructions

Now, following
Murthy and Shankar 2003
;
Murthy and Shankar 2016
;
Dong and Senthil 2020
, we would like to motivate the form of the operator in
8
which obeys the GMP algebra exactly. First, we recall that within the Pasquier-Haldane parton construction for bosons at
\nu=1

Pasquier and Haldane 1998
;
Read 1998
, we can define two operators

\begin{split}\overline{\rho}_{L}(\bm{q})&=e^{-q^{2}/4}\overline{\overline{\rho}}_{L}(\bm{q})=e^{-q^{2}/4}\sum_{k}e^{i\bm{q}\times\bm{k}/2}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}}\\
\overline{\rho}_{R}(\bm{q})&=e^{-q^{2}/4}\overline{\overline{\rho}}_{R}(\bm{q})=e^{-q^{2}/4}\sum_{k}e^{-i\bm{q}\times\bm{k}/2}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}c_{\bm{k}}\\
\end{split}

(30)

where
\overline{\rho}_{L}
is the physical density (with
\overline{\overline{\rho}}_{L}
obeying the GMP algebra) and
\overline{\rho}_{R}(\bm{q}\neq 0)
annihilates physical states, with
\overline{\overline{\rho}}_{R}
obeying the GMP algebra with the opposite sign. As
\overline{\rho}_{R}(\bm{q}\neq 0)
annihilates physical states, we may add and subtract it at will from other operators acting on physical states.

Accordingly, we can come up with two representations of the physical charge density that obey the GMP algebra. They are
\overline{\rho}_{e}=\overline{\rho}_{L}(\bm{q})
and
\overline{\rho}_{d}=\overline{\rho}_{L}(\bm{q})-\overline{\rho}_{R}(\bm{q})
, the latter of which is precisely Eq. (
8
). Each of these representations implies a different assignment of quantum numbers to the
c
-fermions in the
q\rightarrow 0
limit – if we choose
\overline{\rho}_{e}
, then the
c
-fermions carry unit charge, and if we choose
\overline{\rho}_{d}
, then the
c
-fermions are interpreted as dipoles (since
\overline{\rho}_{d}(\bm{q}\rightarrow 0)
coincides with Eq. (
6
)).

Then, the next step in any parton construction is to perform a standard Hartree-Fock mean-field theory in the
c
-operators, supposing that the Hamiltonian takes a generic form
\hat{H}=\sum_{k}U(\bm{q})e^{-q^{2}/2}\overline{\rho}(\bm{q})\overline{\rho}(-\bm{q})
. One can find two different mean-field ground states, depending on whether we choose the representation
\overline{\rho}=\overline{\rho}_{e}
or
\overline{\rho}=\overline{\rho}_{d}
. Neglecting the possibility of pairing, the mean-field ground state will be a circular Fermi sea of the
c
-fermions with
k_{F}
set by Luttinger’s theorem and a dispersion set by the interaction
U(q)
.

Neither mean-field ground state respects the gauge constraints enforced on this parton approach, but we may still ask whether these mean-field ground states capture any of the essential physics. If we elect to represent
\overline{\rho}=\overline{\rho}_{e}
, the corresponding mean-field ground state will have a projected static structure factor
\overline{S}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N}\langle\overline{\rho}_{e}(\bm{q})\overline{\rho}_{e}(-\bm{q})\rangle\propto\left|q\right|
, in violation of Kohn’s theorem
Kohn 1961
. However, as shown in Appendix
E
, choosing
\overline{\rho}=\overline{\rho}_{d}
yields Eq. (
32
) as the mean-field result for
\overline{S}(\bm{q})
, which is consistent with Kohn’s theorem. According to this discussion, Eq. (
32
) can be thought of as a result from parton mean-field theory, at least for the
\nu=1
bosons.

Appendix E
Fermi sea of non-interacting dipolar CFs

Using the expression
6
for the charge density, we assume that the ground state is a Fermi gas of the
c
-fermions and compute the static structure factor
\overline{S}(\bf{q})
at small momentum. We assume no renormalization from the Fermi liquid theory, nor do we consider the effects of any gauge fluctuations.

With these assumptions, the projected static structure factor
\overline{S}(q)
in the planar geometry can be computed directly. The calculation is a straightforward adaptation of a textbook derivation
Giamarchi and Press 2004
. We obtain (for
q>0
):

\begin{split}\overline{S}(q)&=\frac{1}{n}\bigl\langle\overline{\rho}(\bm{q})\overline{\rho}(-\bm{q})\bigr\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2n}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}(\bm{q}\times\bm{k})^{2}\\
&\quad\times\Bigl[\vartheta(-\xi_{\bm{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{q}})-\vartheta(-\xi_{\bm{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{q}})\Bigr]\\
&\quad\times\mathrm{sgn}\left(\xi_{\bm{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{q}}-\xi_{\bm{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{q}}\right),\end{split}

(31)

where
\vartheta(x)
is the Heaviside step function and
\xi_{\bm{k}}
the CF dispersion. Since this expression depends only on the geometry of the filled Fermi sea, the specific form of
\xi_{\bm{k}}
is unimportant; only rotational symmetry is assumed.
In the limit
\bm{q}\to 0
, expanding to leading order yields

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\overline{\rho}(\bm{q})\overline{\rho}(-\bm{q})\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}(\bm{q}\times\bm{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigl[\bm{q}\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}\delta(-\xi_{k})+O(q^{3})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(-\bm{q}\cdot\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}\bigr),

with
\bm{v}_{\bm{k}}=\nabla\xi_{k}
.
Evaluating the integral on the Fermi surface gives

\displaystyle\bigl\langle\overline{\rho}(\bm{q})\overline{\rho}(-\bm{q})\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{kdkd\theta}{(2\pi)^{2}}q^{3}k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\cos\theta\delta(k-k_{F})

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathrm{sgn}(-qv_{F}\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}k_{F}^{3}q^{3}.

Normalizing by the CF density
n_{\rm CF}=\pi k_{F}^{2}/(2\pi)^{2}
, which follows from the Luttinger relation, we arrive at

\begin{split}\overline{S}_{\rm dipole}(q)&=\frac{1}{n}\bigl\langle\rho(\bm{q})\rho(-\bm{q})\bigr\rangle\\
&=\frac{2k_{F}}{3\pi}q^{3}+O(q^{4})\\
&=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}q^{3}+O(q^{4}),\end{split}

(32)

where we used
k_{F}=\sqrt{4\pi n_{\mathrm{CF}}}=\sqrt{2\nu}
, with
\nu=1/p
. Thus, the long-wavelength expansion yields the result quoted in the main text, demonstrating that the dipolar picture reproduces the microscopic
\overline{S}(\bm{q})
at small momentum.

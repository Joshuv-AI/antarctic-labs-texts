An Orally Available Derivative of a Specific CK2 Inhibitor for Regulating Circadian Rhythms and Acute Myeloid Leukemia
Authors: Tsuyoshi Oshima, Daiki Setoyama, Akiko Sugiyama, Kazuma Amaike, Ikumi Yamanaka, Kentaro Hosokawa, Kazuto Nunomura, Kiyoshi Kawamura, Ashutosh Srivastava, Mui Iida, Kazuhito Gotoh, Bangzhong Lin, Mikio Morita, Masashi Ohmi, Takeshi Uchiumi, Fumio Arai, Junichi Haruta, Masaki Sudo, Florence Tama, Yuya Kunisaki, Kenichiro Itami, Tsuyoshi Hirota
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The protein kinase CK2 controls cell cycle, apoptosis, and circadian rhythms. The CK2 inhibitor CX-4945 is in clinical trials for cancer treatment, but it has many off-target proteins. Therefore, the development of a bioavailable and specific CK2 inhibitor has been awaited. We previously discovered a specific CK2 inhibitor, GO289, from cell-based phenotypic screening of circadian clock modulators. However, the low water solubility and metabolic stability of GO289 have limited in vivo applications. Here, we developed GO289 derivatives with higher water solubility and metabolic stability. A derivative, GO847, changed the period length of cellular circadian rhythms similar to GO289 and inhibited the growth of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells. A proteomic profiling suggested the effects of GO847 and GO289 on pathways related to mitochondrial function, in addition to well-known CK2 targets: cell cycle, apoptosis, RNA metabolism, and protein synthesis. Consistent with this observation, both compounds impaired the metabolic flexibility of mitochondria in AML cells. After oral administration to mice, GO847 was detected in the plasma, liver, spleen, and bone marrow but not in the brain. Together, the findings show that GO847 is a promising candidate for targeting AML, a malignant blood cancer that requires effective treatment.

Introduction

Daily rhythms of a variety of physiological processes, including sleep-wake cycles and metabolism, are controlled by a biological clock in our body, called the circadian clock. The core of the circadian clock is constituted by the clock genes Per, Cry, Clock, and Bmal1, which form a transcription and translation-based feedback loop in each cell. The loop cycles once a day, resulting in a daily rhythmic expression of the clock genes, as well as clock-controlled genes that are involved in the regulation of many physiological functions. Protein kinases, such as CKI, CK2, and GSK-3, phosphorylate the clock proteins and regulate their stability and subcellular localization to control the period of the circadian clock. Perturbation of the circadian clock by shift work or gene mutation is related to many diseases in modern society, such as sleep disorders, metabolic diseases, and cancer. , Small-molecule compounds targeting the clock proteins and clock-related kinases are expected to form the basis of the treatment of circadian clock-related diseases. − From cell-based high-throughput screening to search for compounds that regulate circadian rhythms, we previously discovered a guaiacol derivative GO289 (Figure a) as a potent and highly specific CK2 inhibitor. We demonstrated that GO289 not only lengthens the period of cellular circadian rhythms but also strongly suppresses the proliferation of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells in culture. AML is an aggressive blood cancer that requires effective treatment. A pioneering study using in vivo RNAi screens identified clock proteins CLOCK and BMAL1 as key regulators of AML cell growth. The study further revealed that other clock proteins and clock-related kinases, PER, CRY, CKI, and CK2, are also required for leukemia stem cell function. Therefore, the strong suppression of AML cell growth by GO289 provided a new candidate for AML treatment. Despite its promising in vitro activity, we found that GO289 has low water solubility and low metabolic stability in human and mouse liver microsomes (Table ), making it difficult to apply for in vivo mouse studies.

Results and Discussion

To overcome the limitation of GO289, we developed its derivatives. Our previous structure–activity relationship analysis revealed that the modification of the para position of the phenyl group does not largely affect the compound’s activity. Indeed, the crystal structure of CK2α-GO289 complex showed that the para position of the phenyl group is facing outside of the ATP-binding pocket (cf. Figure e). We therefore rationally targeted this phenyl group to increase the water solubility of the compound (Figures a,b and ). For the synthesis of these compounds, the bromo group in GO289 was replaced with a chloro group, a halogen more commonly used in drugs. , Following the synthetic route of GO289, 1 was alkylated with 2-dimethylaminoethyl chloride hydrochloride (2a) via an SN2-type reaction, and subsequently reacted with 6-chlorovanillin (4) to yield GO847. Disubstituted alkyl amino groups are commonly employed as functional modifications to enhance the solubility of compounds. To synthesize derivatives bearing various amino groups which modulate the water solubility, 1 was reacted with 2b or 2c, followed by condensation with 4 to afford 5 and 6, respectively. 5 was further treated with PhSH in the presence of K2CO3 to remove the 2-nitrobenzenesulfonyl (Ns) group, yielding the secondary amine derivative GO1000. 6 was converted into a primary amine using hydrazine, and subsequent acetylation yielded GO1002. Since the thiomethyl group (SMe) is susceptible to oxidation in vivo, and our previous work showed that replacing it with hydrogen did not compromise bioactivity, we also pursued the synthesis of a compound lacking the SMe group. 1 was reduced to 7 using Raney nickel, then subjected to alkylation with 2-dimethylaminoethyl chloride and subsequent hydrazide formation with 4 to furnish GO1029.

We first evaluated the effects of GO289 derivatives on circadian rhythms in human U2OS cells. Circadian changes of the bioluminescent intensities of the Bmal1-dLuc reporter were measured in the presence of various concentrations of the compounds (Figure c, left). The compound treatment resulted in a dose-dependent lengthening of the circadian period (Figure c, right, and Table ). Among the derivatives, GO847 showed the highest potency, which is comparable to GO289, and caused ∼ 10 h period lengthening at 8 μM. We further evaluated the direct effects of the derivatives on CK2 enzymatic activities in vitro. Although their potency was a few-fold lower than that of GO289, all derivatives showed IC50 values in the low nanomolar range (Figure d and Table ). These results indicate that the GO289 derivatives are active in cells and in vitro.

We next investigated in vitro ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion) parameters (Table ). Importantly, all derivatives showed substantial improvement in water solubility and metabolic stability in human and mouse liver microsomes (LM CLint) compared to GO289. Furthermore, the membrane permeability of GO847 in artificial membranes (PAMPA Pe) and Caco-2 cells (Papp) was better than that of GO289 and/or other derivatives. In addition, GO847 had less plasma protein binding (PPB fu) (i.e., more unbound fraction) than GO289. It is likely that the higher membrane permeability and lower plasma protein binding together make GO847 potent in cellular circadian assays, although it has less in vitro CK2 inhibition potency than GO289 (Table ). Based on these results, we chose GO847 as a candidate compound with much better water solubility and metabolic stability. The docking simulation indicated that the CK2α-binding mode of GO847 is similar to GO289, in which the extended moiety fits to the outer side of the ATP-binding pocket (Figure e).

We then conducted a detailed evaluation of GO847 selectivity and activity. In in vitro kinase assays, GO847 retained the highly selective profile of GO289 against CK2 among 67 kinases from various classes (Figure a). The second most affected kinase was FLT3 (65% inhibition at 5 μM, Figure a). Because FLT3 is known as one of the key driver mutations in AML, enhanced effect of GO847 against FLT3 could partially contribute to the anti-AML effect. However, inhibition of FLT3 by GO847 was ∼ 50 times less potent than that of CK2 (84% inhibition at 0.17 μM and 51% inhibition at 0.06 μM, Figure d). We further investigated the effects on DYRK, HIPK, PIM, and CLK family kinases that are frequently inhibited by well-known CK2 inhibitors TBB, DMAT, and/or CX-4945, − together with CKI family kinases that are related to the circadian clock mechanism. Similar to GO289, GO847 showed prominent selectivity against CK2α (α1 and α2) compared to TBB, DMAT, and CX-4945 (Figure b). We also compared cellular CK2 inhibitory activities of GO289 and GO847 by measuring phosphorylation levels of CK2 substrates. U2OS cells were treated with GO289 or GO847, and cell lysates were analyzed using an anti-phospho CK2 substrate antibody. This antibody recognizes the CK2-consensus sequence pS/pT-D-X-E, in which pS/pT represents Ser/Thr residues phosphorylated by CK2. The antibody detected many protein substrates, and GO847 led to a similar or slightly more potent reduction of the phosphorylation signal than GO289 in a dose-dependent manner (Figure c,d and Figure S1). This observation is consistent with the similar potency of GO289 and GO847 on cellular circadian rhythms, while the in vitro CK2 inhibition potency of GO847 was 5 times lower than that of GO289 (Table ). Together, these results support the high selectivity and cellular effectiveness of GO847.

We further assessed the antiproliferative effects of GO289 and GO847 across a panel of human and mouse hematological malignancy cell lines, including leukemia (C1498, THP-1, OCI-AML3, K562, CCRF-CEM, BALL-1) and multiple myeloma (KMM-1) (Figure a and Figure S2). Both compounds inhibited the proliferation of all cell lines with IC50 values ranging from 0.9 μM to 9.6 μM. While some cell lines exhibited comparable IC50 values for GO289 and GO847, others showed slight differences in sensitivity. This may reflect variation in drug uptake and/or intracellular dynamics, such as subcellular localization or stability, among combinations of cell types and compounds. We selected mouse AML C1498 cells (the IC50 values for GO289 and GO847 were 1.7 μM and 5.1 μM, respectively) and human AML THP-1 cells (the IC50 values for GO289 and GO847 were 2.1 μM and 1.7 μM, respectively) for in-depth analysis. We first compared cellular CK2 inhibitory activities of GO289 and GO847 by measuring phosphorylation levels of CK2 substrates. C1498 and THP-1 cells were treated with GO289 or GO847 for 24 h, and cell lysates were analyzed using an anti-phospho CK2 substrate antibody and/or an anti-phospho AKT1 (S129) antibody. The latter antibody specifically recognizes AKT1 phosphorylated at Ser129 by CK2. Treatment with GO289 reduced phosphorylation signals of CK2 substrates, including AKT1 (S129), stronger than GO847 in both C1498 and THP-1 cells (Figure b,c and Figure S3). Overall, the effects on cellular CK2 activity were consistent with those on cell proliferation (Figure a), supporting the engagement of long-term CK2 inhibition in the antiproliferative effects of GO289 and GO847.

To elucidate the cellular mechanisms underlying the observed growth inhibition, we performed proteomic profiling after 24 h treatment with GO289 and GO847 at concentrations close to each IC50 (C1498 cells, 1.25 μM GO289 and 5 μM GO847; THP-1 cells, 2.5 μM GO289 and 1.25 μM GO847). This analysis identified over 5,000 proteins expressed in each cell type (Figure a,b). We focused on differentially expressed proteins by both compounds in both cell lines. A substantial number of proteins (204 proteins in C1498 cells and 219 proteins in THP-1 cells) were downregulated, and they were predominantly annotated with functions related to cell cycle, apoptosis, RNA metabolism, and protein synthesislikely reflecting well-known targets of CK2 inhibition , (Figure S4). In contrast, upregulated proteins (128 proteins in C1498 cells and 199 proteins in THP-1 cells) were enriched for pathways related to mitochondria and metabolism (Figure c), suggesting potential changes in mitochondrial bioenergetics, which we considered another mechanism of action of the CK2-targeting compounds. Therefore, we performed mitochondrial flux analysis in C1498 cells. Treatment with GO289 or GO847 did not alter basal oxygen consumption rate (OCR) but substantially reduced spare respiratory capacity following stimulation with the mitochondrial uncoupler FCCP (Figure d, upper). In extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) measurements, control DMSO-treated cells exhibited an increase in ECAR following oligomycin-induced complex V inhibition, indicating a compensatory shift to glycolysis. This elevation was absent in GO289- or GO847-treated cells, suggesting impaired glycolytic compensation (Figure d, lower). Collectively, these findings indicate that GO289 and GO847 suppress AML cell growth by disrupting the mitochondrial metabolic flexibility mechanism, in addition to the canonical pathways such as regulation of cell cycle, apoptosis, RNA metabolism, and protein synthesis.

To evaluate the contribution of CK2 inhibition in the observed mitochondrial respiration phenotype, we performed additional experiments using a structurally unrelated and highly selective CK2 inhibitor, SGC–CK2–1. We first determined the IC50 value of SGC–CK2–1 against C1498 cell proliferation, which was 0.85 μM (Figure S5a), supporting the contribution of CK2 to the antiproliferative effects. We then conducted mitochondrial flux analysis using 0.2, 0.4, and 0.8 μM SGC–CK2–1, close to the IC50 concentration, to enable comparison with GO289 and GO847. The compound resulted in a dose-dependent reduction of basal OCR before oligomycin treatment and spare respiratory capacity after FCCP treatment (Figure S5b, upper), while maintaining an increase in ECAR after oligomycin treatment (Figure S5b, lower). Therefore, although the detailed respiratory profiles were not identical, GO289, GO847, and SGC–CK2–1 impaired mitochondrial respiratory function, with the reduction of respiratory reserve capacity being a shared feature. We then investigated mitochondrial functions on C1498 cells treated with GO847 and found increased ATP, elevated mitochondrial membrane potentials, and excessive mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) production (Figure S6a–c), implying hyperpolarization-induced ROS production and respiratory inhibition (backpressure inhibition) as putative mechanisms of anticancer effects of GO847. Given the complexity of mitochondrial regulation, differences in compound properties, such as cellular exposure, intracellular distribution, or subtle variations in minor kinase inhibition profiles, may influence the extent and pattern of respiratory suppression. These findings together suggest that CK2 contributes to the regulation of mitochondrial respiratory capacity, which requires further investigation in future studies.

Lastly, we investigated the pharmacokinetic properties of GO847 in mice. Ethical approval for the animal experiments was obtained from the Animal Experiments Committee of Nagoya University (approval number ITbM-4). After intraperitoneal (IP) injection of 100 mg/kg GO847 to mice, the compound concentration in plasma was kept for 6 h (Figure a) with maximal concentrations (C max) of 0.5 ± 0.09 μM, area under the curve (AUC0‑t) of 4.0 ± 1.5 μM × h that reflects the actual body exposure to the drug, and half-life (T 1/2) of 7.4 ± 0.35 h. GO847 was also detected in plasma after oral (PO) administration at 50 mg/kg (Figure a), indicating that the compound can be absorbed by the stomach and/or intestines into the blood. We further analyzed the tissue distribution of GO847 at 2 h after 50 mg/kg PO administration. In addition to plasma, the compound was detected in the liver, spleen, and bone marrow, which are related to AML (Figure b). In contrast, GO847 was not detected in the brain. Based on this tissue distribution, GO847 is expected to act on AML without affecting the circadian rhythm of sleep-wake cycles, which is regulated by the clock in the brain. The concentrations of GO847 in spleen and bone marrow were lower than IC50 values for C1498 and THP-1 cells in culture. Although in vivo and cultured conditions are not directly comparable, it is desirable to achieve higher GO847 concentrations comparable to IC50 values in tissues. Therefore, dosing amount and route need to be optimized for further applications. In addition, a combination therapy discussed below may sensitize cancer cells to CK2 inhibition, so that lower concentrations of GO847 will be sufficient.

Conclusions

In summary, we have succeeded in developing a GO289 derivative GO847 with higher water solubility and metabolic stability. The protein kinase CK2, the target of GO847, regulates cell cycle, apoptosis, and DNA repair, and overexpression of CK2 is associated with cancer development. Because CK2 provides a promising target for cancer treatment, a variety of CK2 inhibitors have been developed to date. − Among the small-molecule inhibitors of CK2, only one compound CX-4945 (silmitasertib) has been in clinical trials. ,, However, CX-4945 is known to have many off-targets different from CK2. , Therefore, there is still a need for developing new CK2 inhibitors with high selectivity and in vivo efficacy. GO289 has a superior CK2-selectivity compared to CX-4945. Its derivative GO847 kept the highly specific property of GO849 and therefore provides a promising candidate for studies using mouse models of cancer, due to the efficacy on AML cell lines and in vivo pharmacokinetic properties. Most recently, a new CK2 inhibitor APL-5125, suitable for in vivo studies, was developed and is currently in clinical trials, expanding the opportunity for CK2 therapy. APL-5125 seems to inhibit some CAMK family kinases, and its selectivity profile needs to be compared with GO847. In addition to treatment as a single agent, combination therapy using CK2 inhibitors with other drugs is in progress. , The targets of such drugs include 26S proteasome, DNA topoisomerase, BRD4, BCR-ABL, DNA, and so on. Our findings of the effects of GO289 and GO847 on mitochondrial metabolic flexibility may add mitochondrial damage as a new combination therapy target for CK2 inhibitors. Together, a fusion of synthetic chemistry, circadian clock research, and cancer research resulted in the identification of a unique CK2 inhibitor that will open a new way for cancer treatment.

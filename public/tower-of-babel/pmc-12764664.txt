Epstein-barr virus-encoded small RNA incorporated prognostic model enables precision risk stratification and guides treatment decisions in peripheral t-cell lymphoma: a multicenter retrospective cohort
Authors: Ze-Tao Chen, Ling Huang, Han-Guo Guo, Cai-Di Lin, Xin-Miao Jiang, Xiao-Juan Wei, Fei-Li Chen, Si-Chu Liu, Hong Zhang, Zhan-Li Liang, Ning Wang, Xiao-Dan Zhang, Li-E LIN, Ting-Bo Liu, Ji-Hao Zhou, Zhi-Gang Peng, Wen-Yu Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEpstein-Barr virus-encoded small RNA (EBER), a hallmark of EBV infection, is a poor prognostic factor in peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL). Current clinical-based prognostic scores inadequately identify high-risk EBER-positive patients or guide therapy, underscoring the need for improved risk-stratification and treatment strategies. This multicenter cohort study, encompassing 167 PTCL patients, systematically analyzed the impact of EBER status on patient survival and treatment response. Utilizing LASSO-penalized Cox regression, a novel prognostic risk scoring system was developed incorporating EBER status and clinical indicators. With a median follow-up of 22.1 months, 63 patients (38%) died. The objective response rate was 57%. EBER-positive status was associated with older age, hypoalbuminemia, high IPI scores, shorter median overall survival (mOS), higher positivity rates for CD30, CD4, BCL6, PD-1, and poorer response to first-line chemotherapy. Multivariate analysis identified independent adverse prognostic factors (p < 0.05): Albumin < 40, Platelet-to-Monocyte Ratio ≤ 300, Lactate Dehydrogenase > 250, Age > 70, and EBER-positivity. A prognostic model incorporating these factors stratified patients into three significantly distinct risk groups (p < 0.001): Low-risk (n = 45; 3-year OS 87.6%, mOS not reached), Intermediate-risk (n = 60; 3-year OS 49.7%, mOS 32.8 months), High-risk (n = 62; 3-year OS 25.1%, mOS 14.3 months). The model outperformed existing models and demonstrated excellent discrimination, stability, clinical utility across PTCL subgroups. This novel prognostic score, integrating subtype-specific marker and clinical features, provides a refined framework for precise PTCL risk stratification and treatment guidance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06683-6.Keywords: Epstein-Barr virus-encoded small RNA (EBER), PTCL, Chidamide, Chemotherapy, Prognostic model

Introduction

Peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) is a highly heterogeneous, aggressive group comprising 10%−15% of non-Hodgkin lymphomas. In Asia, angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL) and PTCL-not otherwise specified (PTCL-NOS) are the second and third most common subtypes (21.6–24.7% and 20.1–20.8%, respectively) [13, 37]. Most patients exhibit poor responses to chemotherapy. Despite recent improvements with optimized regimens and novel agents (e.g., CD30 monoclonal antibodies, histone deacetylase inhibitors), the 5-year overall survival (OS) remains below 30% [15, 20, 31].Survival and treatment responses vary significantly by subtype and clinical characteristics [12, 23], necessitating precise prognostic tools and evidence-based strategies.

Epstein-Barr virus-encoded small RNA (EBER), a marker of EBV infection, is expressed in lymphomas and implicated in tumorigenesis. Its prognostic role is unclear. Epstein-Barr virus-encoding region in situ hybridization (EBER-ISH), the gold standard for detecting EBV in tumor tissue, identifies EBV-infected tumor cells [11, 19, 25]. EBER positivity occurs in 27%−41% of PTCL-NOS indicating tumor T-cell infection and correlates with significantly shorter median OS (mOS). In AITL, EBER expression ranges from 48.9% to 88% [5, 14]. Within AITL's B-cell-rich microenvironment, EBV may promote B-cell proliferation and immune evasion, driving tumor progression [22, 29].

The clinical significance of EBER in PTCL remains controversial. Some studies link EBER positivity to more aggressive biology, poorer chemotherapy response, and worse prognosis [4, 17, 25, 33]. High-density EBER positivity (> 50/HPF) is an independent adverse prognostic factor for progression-free survival (PFS) and OS in AITL [29]. Others have found no significant impact of EBER status on survival or prognostic value in PTCL patients [11, 27], potentially due to limited sample sizes. Furthermore, existing clinical prognostic scores, such as the International Prognostic Index (IPI), Prognostic Index for PTCL (PIT), and the IPTCLP model, primarily rely on clinical parameters (e.g. age, stage, ECOG performance status, extranodal involvement) [10], [34]; [21, 36]. These lack integration of biological markers, limiting their predictive power for specific subgroups like EBER-positive patients and hindering personalized clinical management.

Addressing this, our multicenter retrospective study of 167 PTCL patients systematically analyzes EBER status' impact on survival and treatment response. For PTCL-NOS and AITL patients exhibiting high EBER expression rates, a novel prognostic model incorporating EBER status will be developed and validated. This aims to enable precise prognostic stratification and tailored treatment approaches for PTCL.

Material and method

Study population

This multicenter retrospective cohort study enrolled patients diagnosed with PTCL-NOS and AITL between 2014 and 2022 from the South China T Cell Lymphoma Study Group, encompassing 21 centers. Follow-up extended through May 2022. All cases were histopathologically and immunohistochemically confirmed according to the 2016 World Health Organization classification [31]. Patients aged ≥ 18 years and availability of EBER testing results (positive or negative) were eligible for inclusion. A final cohort of 167 patients was selected for analysis (Fig. 1). The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval No.: KY-Q-2021–225-02).

Flow chart of the study design

Study data

Patient data encompassing demographics(age), serological tests (neutrophil count [ANC], hemoglobin [HB], platelet count [PLT], platelet-to-monocyte ratio [PMR], albumin [ALB], lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), β−2microglobulin [β2M]), clinicopathological features(Ann Arbor stage, ECOG performance status [ECOG PS], extranodal involvement, bone marrow [BM], ki-67 index, EBER) and treatment details were collected. For this study, EBV infection was definitively diagnosed using in situ hybridization (ISH) targeting EBER. EBER positivity indicated EBV infection in tumor cells. Immunohistochemical (IHC) markers (CD30, CD4, CD7, CD8, CXCL13, PD-1, PD-L1, BCL6) were included to compare their positivity rates based on EBER status.

Missing data handling

Missing values were imputed using Random Forest (RF; missForest R package) [30]. Despite Little's Missing Completely at Random(MCAR) test revealed that the missing mechanism was not MCAR (χ2 = 328, p < 0.001), RF was employed due to its robustness to missing mechanisms, handling of mixed data types, and suitability for low-to-moderate missingness [32]. Missing data patterns were visually assessed prior to imputation. Each RF used 500 trees (ntree = 500) and ran for 10 iterations (maxiter = 10) to ensure convergence; categorical variables (BM, ECOG PS) were treated as factors. T-tests and chi-square tests confirmed no significant differences between observed and imputed values (p > 0.05). Sensitivity analysis comparing Cox regression hazard ratios (HRs) from complete case analysis, simple imputation, and RF imputation [32] showed consistent results between simple and RF imputation, indicating robustness against missing data bias.

Prognostic model

61 death events were recorded. The commonly applied Events Per Variable (EPV) guideline in statistics generally recommends an EPV of ≥ 10 to ensure model stability and reduce overfitting [24]. The maximum number of variables that could be robustly evaluated was approximately 6 (61 events/10 EPV). Therefore, we adopted a conservative and rigorous strategy by setting the target number of variables at 5, thereby guaranteeing the reliability and stability of the multivariate Cox regression model results. Beyond statistical considerations, we also balanced model parsimony with clinical practicality, facilitating the potential future translation of this tool into clinical practice. Following clinical prediction model guidelines [9], variables were first screened via LASSO regression (λ_min), yielding 8 candidates. Subsequent Cox proportional hazards regression (P < 0.05) selected 5 independent predictors: age, LDH, ALB, Platelet-to-Monocyte Ratio (PMR), and EBER status. These were incorporated into the novel prognostic model. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residual tests (P > 0.05).

Model stability was assessed via bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) and validated across four PTCL subgroups (PTCL-NOS, AITL, CD30 +, CD4 +). Discrimination was evaluated using Harrell's C-index and time-dependent ROC curves (AUC); values closer to 1 indicate superior performance. Calibration (agreement between predicted and observed survival) was assessed using bootstrap-corrected calibration curves (1000 resamples), the Brier score, and the adapted Hosmer–Lemeshow test (chi-square test on deciles of predicted risk). Clinical utility was evaluated via Decision Curve Analysis (DCA), quantifying net benefit across risk thresholds. Calculated risk scores enabled clinical risk stratification. The new model's performance was compared against established scores (IPI, PIT, mPIT, IPTCLP) using: (1) Global fit: Akaike's Information Criterion (AIC; lower = better fit), (2) Discrimination: C-index and AUC (higher = better), and (3) Clinical utility: Net benefit on DCA curves (higher net benefit at same threshold = greater utility).

Statistical analysis

Group comparisons employed Fisher's exact test, Student's t-test, or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Survival was estimated via Kaplan–Meier curves; differences were assessed using the log-rank test. Bootstrap-based nonparametric tests compared C-indices and AUCs between models. Calibration curve accuracy was evaluated using the adapted Hosmer–Lemeshow test. DCA curves were compared via the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p < 0.05. All analyses used R (v4.4.3).

Result

Patient demographics and clinical characteristics

From 1031 T-cell lymphoma patients (South China T-cell Lymphoma Study Group, 2014–2022), 145 PTCL-NOS (14.0%) and 139 AITL (13.4%) cases were included. The final cohort comprised 167 patients (AITL = 89, PTCL-NOS = 78). Missing data (HB, PLT, ALB, Ki-67, β2M, BM, ECOG PS; 2–10% missingness) were imputed using RF. Post-imputation, BM positivity decreased from 32.5% to 30.5% and ECOG ≥ 2 from 40.2% to 39.5%, with no significant distributional changes (p = 1.00). Sensitivity analysis indicated Ki-67 > 30% HR shifted from 2.43 (complete-case) to 3.04 (simple and RF imputation), mitigating underestimation bias (Fig.S.1).

Median age was 59 years (20–83 years), 64% male. Most had advanced disease (Ann Arbor III–IV: 84%; ECOG PS ≥ 2: 23%). Elevated LDH occurred in 53%, and 81% had intermediate-high/high IPI risk. EBER-positivity (45%) was higher in AITL versus PTCL-NOS (76% vs. 24%, p < 0.001). Compared to EBER-negative patients, EBER-positive cases exhibited lower hemoglobin (p < 0.001), shorter median OS (13.13 vs. 19.12 months, p = 0.023), older age (p = 0.061), higher IPI risk (p = 0.056), and elevated positivity of CD30, CD4, BCL6, and PD-1 (all p < 0.05). First-line treatment comprised chemotherapy alone (52%, n = 87) or combined with targeted agents: Chidamide (37%, n = 62), Brentuximab vedotin (3%, n = 5), or PD-1 inhibitors (2%, n = 3). After median follow-up of 22.1 months, 38% (n = 63) died, primarily from lymphoma progression. First-line efficacy yielded complete response (CR) in 32% (n = 54) and partial response (PR) in 25% (n = 41) (Table.S.1 & Table 1).

Impact of EBER on PTCL survival and treatment response

EBER-positive patients (n = 75) had inferior OS versus EBER-negative (p = 0.0084; Fig. 2A). The 3-year OS was 58.7% (95% CI:46.5–74.1) for EBER-negative vs. 39.0% (26.7–56.9) for EBER-positive. Multivariate analysis confirmed EBER-positivity as an independent adverse OS factor (HR = 1.72, 95% CI:1.02–2.92, p = 0.044; Table 2). This OS advantage persisted in AITL (p = 0.042), CD30 + PTCL (3-year OS:51.4% vs 26.7%, p = 0.017), and CD4 + PTCL (62.8% vs 36.4%, p = 0.007; Fig. 2B–D). Non-significant trends favored EBER-negative in PTCL-NOS, PD-1 +, and BCL6 + subgroups (Figure S2B–D). EBER status did not affect PFS.

In chemotherapy-only patients (n = 87), EBER-positivity predicted inferior OS (median OS:15.1 months vs not reach [NR], p = 0.012; 3-year OS:41.7% vs 63.8%) and reduced PFS in CD4 + PTCL (3-year PFS:26.3% vs 48.7%, p = 0.025; Fig. 2E–F). Trends favored EBER-negative in BCL6 + (OS p = 0.048) and PD-1 + PTCL (OS p = 0.067; Fig. 2G–H). For Chidamide-combination therapy (n = 56), OS was not associated with EBER status. CD30 + PTCL showed a survival advantage in EBER-negative group (p = 0.071; Fig.S.2I), while PD-1 +, CD4 +, BCL6 + subgroups showed non-significant EBER-positive advantages (Fig.S.2 J-L). EBER-negative patients had longer PFS with chemotherapy versus Chidamide in overall PTCL (p = 0.012), CD4 + (p = 0.0024), PD-1 + (p = 0.0041), and BCL6 + PTCL (p = 0.039; Fig. 2I). No significant treatment differences existed in EBER-positive subgroups, except a modest Chidamide PFS advantage in BCL6 + PTCL (Fig. 2J). These findings suggest EBER status fundamentally alters tumor biology, reducing epigenetic agent efficacy in EBER-negative disease but potentially benefiting EBER-positive contexts.

Model construction and internal validation

To optimize PTCL-NOS and AITL prognostication, a prognostic model was developed using LASSO-penalized Cox regression (full cohort, n = 167), bootstrap-validated (1000 iterations) and subgroups validation. A LASSO-penalized Cox model (λ_min = 0.038) identified eight predictors: age, LDH, β2-MG, BM, PMR, ANC, EBER, and ALB (Fig. 3A–B). Univariate analysis confirmed OS significance for age, LDH, β2-MG, PMR, ANC, ALB, and EBER (p < 0.05). Multivariate analysis identified five independent predictors:ALB < 40 g/L (HR = 2.48, p = 0.043), PMR ≤ 300 (HR = 2.33, p = 0.003), LDH > 250 U/L (HR = 1.94, p = 0.034), Age > 70 years (HR = 2.97, p < 0.001), EBER positivity (HR = 1.72, p = 0.044) (Table 2).

The model satisfied the proportional hazards assumption (Schoenfeld residuals: global p = 0.67; individual predictors p > 0.05). Bootstrap validation showed strong discrimination (C-index: 0.751, 95%CI:0.688–0.814; corrected C-index: 0.740, 0.705–0.752). Time-dependent 3-year AUC was 0.768 (95%CI:0.733–0.785; corrected: 0.765, 95%CI:0.724–0.784). Calibration aligned with observed 3-year survival (Brier score:0.0651; Hosmer–Lemeshow p = 0.991), indicating a low level of prediction error and good calibration. Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) revealed threshold range with superior net benefit: 0.00–0.84, greater net benefit vs. "treat-all"/"treat-none". Net benefit remained > 0.20 for thresholds 50–60% (Fig. 3C–F).

Subgroups validation and model comparison

The model showed stable discrimination, excellent calibration, and notable clinical utility across PTCL subtypes (Table.S.2–S.5), supporting its implementation in personalized therapeutic management. The model demonstrated robust discrimination across PTCL-NOS, AITL, CD4 +, and CD30 + subgroups (C-indices: 0.704–0.814; 30-month/3-year AUC: 0.749–0.830; bootstrap-corrected ΔAUC < 0.03; Fig. 4A). Calibration was excellent in AITL, CD4 + PTCL, CD30 + PTCL (Brier scores < 0.06, MAE < 0.05) and acceptable in PTCL-NOS (MAE = 0.073; Hosmer–Lemeshow p > 0.05; Fig. 4B). Clinical utility assessed by DCA revealed superior net benefit versus treat-all or treat-none strategies at thresholds < 60% in PTCL-NOS, AITL, and CD4 + PTCL and at 50–70% for CD30 + PTCL (Fig. 4C). This multidimensional validation confirms generalizability across key PTCL subtypes.

The novel model conspicuously outperformed established clinical scores. Compared to IPI (C-index = 0.643; AIC = 561.93), PIT (C-index = 0.651; AIC = 558.29), mPIT (C-index = 0.639; AIC = 560.21), and IPTCLP (C-index = 0.578; AIC = 568.29), the new model achieved superior discrimination (C-index = 0.751; AIC = 533.87; all p < 0.05, Table.S.6) and 3-year OS prediction (AUC = 0.768). It demonstrated significantly higher AUC versus IPI, mPIT and IPTCLP (ΔAUC = 0.114, 0.106, 0.241; p < 0.001) (Fig. 4D-E; Table.S.7). Although the AUC advantage over PIT was modest (ΔAUC = 0.096, 95%CI: −0.005–0.112; p = 0.004), it maintained markedly better C-index and superior net benefit (p < 0.05; Fig. 4F, Table.S.8). At 50% threshold probability, the model's net benefit (0.224) substantially exceeded PIT (0.167).

Risk stratification identified three prognostic tiers (p < 0.001; Fig. 4G): Low-risk (0–1 points): 3-year OS rate 87.6% (76.5–100.0%), mOS not reached; Intermediate-risk (2 points): 3-year OS 49.7% (36.1–68.4%), mOS 32.8 months; High-risk (3–5 points): 3-year OS 25.1% (13.3–47.5%), mOS 14.3 months. This stratification enables personalized management: high-risk patients may benefit from intensified therapies, while low-risk patients require standard protocols.

Discussion

This multicenter study established EBER-positivity as an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in PTCL, significantly associated with older age, hypohemoglobinemia, high IPI scores, shorter mOS, and poor chemotherapy response. The novel prognostic model integrating EBER status with clinical indicators demonstrated superior performance over conventional models, providing critical insights for the individualized management of PTCL.

Analysis across subtypes indicated that EBER-positive status was associated with significantly worse OS in patients with AITL, CD4 + PTCL, and CD30 + PTCL, aligning with existing literature [4, 25, 29]. Notably, the adverse effect of EBER on prognosis was more significant in AITL compared to PTCL-NOS (p = 0.042 vs. p = 0.13), a finding that may be explained by the aberrant follicular helper T-cell (TFH) biology and EBV-associated perturbations of the immune microenvironment characteristic of AITL.

[3, 38]. Furthermore, given the considerable molecular heterogeneity within PTCL-NOS, including the PTCL-TFH subtype, this was genetically similar to AITL and exhibits a higher frequency of EBER positivity [1]. Our observation suggests a potential interplay between molecular context and EBER. This calls for refined future analyses based on explicit molecular subtyping.

This study identified a potential association between EBER status and treatment response in PTCL-NOS/AITL. EBER-positive patients exhibited generally poor response to first-line chemotherapy, with a > 20% reduction in 3-year OS in the overall cohort and CD4 +/BCL6 + subgroups. This potentially related to EBV-mediated NF-κB pathway activation and an immunosuppressive microenvironment [11, 16], [26]. Interestingly, although prior studies showed Chidamide improved survival in general PTCL populations [28, 35], our study observed a trend toward survival improvement in EBER-positive patients receiving Chidamide-containing regimens. This may be attributed to unique EBV-driven epigenetic dysregulation and an immunosuppressive microenvironment (high PD-1/BCL6 expression) in EBER-positive tumors, potentially increasing their sensitivity to HDAC inhibitors [2, 6, 18]. It must be emphasized that due to the retrospective design and limited sample size in treatment groups, these findings regarding Chidamide are descriptive and require validation in prospective studies.

Based on these findings, we employed LASSO-Cox regression to identify five independent prognostic factors from a multi-center cohort of PTCL patients. These variables reflect the multifaceted biology of PTCL-NOS and AITL, encompassing tumor burden (LDH), inflammatory status (PMR), nutritional status (ALB), host factors (Age), and viral infection (EBER). The resulting model demonstrated superior discriminative ability and clinical utility compared to conventional prognostic scores. It effectively stratified patients into three distinct risk groups with significantly different 3-year OS rates: 87.6% (low-risk), 49.7% (intermediate-risk), and 25.1% (high-risk), thereby facilitating individualized treatment decisions. This approach is consistent with recent advances in molecularly-informed prognostication, such as the TP53 prognostic index model in TP53-mutated DLBCL, which integrates mutation types with the IPI score to guide personalized management strategies [8].

This study has several limitations. Our study benefits from the strengths of a multicenter cohort and robust internal validation, which demonstrate clear advantages of the EBER-integrated model over existing prognostic systems. However, the findings may still be influenced by cohort-specific patient characteristics, treatment patterns, and regional factors. While this study represents an important preliminary investigation, a key limitation remains the absence of external validation in an independent cohort. Therefore, the generalizability of the model requires further confirmation, a consideration consistent with TRIPOD guidelines [7]. Future prospective, multi-center studies involving larger and more heterogeneous populations are warranted for external validation to definitively assess its clinical utility. Furthermore, the pronounced molecular heterogeneity of PTCL-NOS could not be addressed due to data limitations, precluding molecular subtyping. Future work within a defined molecular framework is essential to elucidate EBER's precise prognostic and predictive role. Finally, the non-randomized, retrospective design means that the observed associations between EBER status and differential responses to Chidamide are preliminary and descriptive, necessitating verification in prospective studies or larger cohorts.

Conclusion

In summary, this study delineates the clinical characteristics of EBER-positive PTCL associated with older age, hypoalbuminemia, high IPI scores, shorter mOS, higher positivity rates for CD30, CD4, BCL6, PD-1, and poorer response to first-line chemotherapy. By incorporating the viral molecular marker EBER, we developed an optimized prognostic model that demonstrates superior performance over conventional clinical scoring systems. These findings offer important insights for personalized management of PTCL.

The case distribution across participating institutions was as follows: Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital contributed the highest proportion of cases (44, 26.35%), followed by Hainan Provincial People's Hospital (29, 17.37%), Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (27, 16.17%), Shenzhen People's Hospital (15, 8.98%), and Guangxi Medical University First Affiliated Hospital (11, 6.59%). The remaining centers demonstrated progressively decreasing enrollment numbers: Fujian Provincial Hospital (10, 5.99%), Peking University Shenzhen Hospital (7, 4.19%), Nanfang Hospital, Southern Medical University (5, 2.99%), Yunnan First People's Hospital and Dongguan People's Hospital (each contributing 4 cases, 2.40% respectively), Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hospital (3, 1.80%). Three centers contributed 2 cases (1.20% each), including First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine, First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University and Guangdong Medical University Affiliated Hospital. Two additional centers contributed single cases (0.60% each), including First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University and Zhongshan People's Hospital. The data generated in this study are available upon reasonable request from the corresponding author.

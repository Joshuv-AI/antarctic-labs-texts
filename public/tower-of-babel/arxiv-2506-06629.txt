Charge symmetry breaking effects of ω \omega - ρ 0 \rho^{0} mixing in relativistic mean-field model Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25
Yusuke Tanimura, Tomoya Naito, Hiroyuki Sagawa, Myung-Ki Cheoun

Abstract
We present a relativistic mean-field model that incorporates charge symmetry breaking (CSB) of nuclear force via ω \omega - ρ 0 \rho^{0} meson mixing, along with corrections to the electromagnetic interaction including the nucleon form factors, first-order vacuum polarization, and Coulomb exchange and pairing terms. The model parameters are refitted using the mass differences of T = 1 / 2 T=1/2 mirror nuclei and ground-state properties of magic nuclei, yielding DD-ME-CSB parameter set. The DD-ME-CSB parameter set reproduces the mass differences of mirror nuclei reasonably well up to T = 2 T=2 , demonstrating the importance of ω \omega - ρ 0 \rho^{0} mixing. A connection of the present model to a Skyrme-type CSB interaction is also established through a gradient expansion of the energy density functional.



I
Introduction

The isospin symmetry is one of the important properties in nuclear physics.
The isospin symmetry of nucleons almost holds and thus, protons and neutrons share common properties.
That of nuclear interaction also almost holds and thus, the proton-proton, neutron-neutron, and the
T=1
channel of the proton-neutron nuclear interactions are almost identical.
However, the isospin symmetry of nuclear interaction is slightly broken
because of the mass difference of the up- and down-quarks and electromagnetic interaction.
The isospin symmetry breaking in nuclear structure has been paid attention for decades
in many aspects,
such as mass and radii differences between a pair of mirror nuclei
Okamoto 1964
;
Nolen and Schiffer 1969
;
Hatsuda et al. 1990
;
Hatsuda et al. 1991
;
Auerbach 1992
;
Shahnas 1994
;
Saito and Thomas 1994
;
Brown et al. 2000
;
Bączyk et al. 2018
;
Dong et al. 2018
;
Bączyk et al. 2019
;
Sagawa et al. 2022
;
Naito et al. 2022a
;
Naito et al. 2022b
;
Naito et al. 2023a
;
Sagawa et al. 2024
;
Naito et al. 2025
,
isobaric analog energy
Suzuki et al. 1993
;
Roca-Maza et al. 2018
,
and the superallowed beta decay
Sagawa et al. 1996
;
Liang et al. 2009
;
Satuła et al. 2011

Satuła et al. 2012
;
Rafalski and Satuła 2012
;
Kaneko et al. 2017
;
Hardy and Towner 2020

.
Such a study is also important for the unitarity test of the Cabbibo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix
Hardy and Towner 2013
;
Porter 2016
;
Navas et al. 2024
.

The isospin symmetry breaking of the nuclear interaction can be classified into two classes:
the charge independence breaking (CIB) and charge symmetry breaking (CSB) interactions,
which are often referred to as the class II and III interactions, respectively
Henley 1969
;
Miller et al. 2006
.
The CIB interaction corresponds to the difference between the like-particle interaction and the different-particle one,
which mainly originates from the mass difference of pions
Coon and Scadron 1982a
.
The CSB interaction corresponds to the difference between the proton-proton interaction and the neutron-neutron one,
which mainly originates from the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
and
\pi
-
\eta
mixings in the meson exchange picture
Coon et al. 1977
;
Coon and Scadron 1982b
;
Coon and Barrett 1987

and
the mass difference of nucleons in medium in the picture of the quantum chromodynamics sum rule
Hatsuda et al. 1990
;
Hatsuda et al. 1991
;
Sagawa et al. 2024
.
Recently, the isospin symmetry breaking interaction has also been studied in the chiral effective field theory
van Kolck 1995
;
van Kolck et al. 1996
;
Epelbaum and Meißner 2005
;
Meißner et al. 2008
.

The density functional theory (DFT) is a powerful tool to calculate nuclear properties among the nuclear chart
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
;
Vautherin and Brink 1972
;
Kohn 1999
;
Bender et al. 2003
;
Colò 2020
.
The starting point of the DFT calculation is an energy density functional (EDF).
In most calculation, an EDF has been obtained with the momentum expansion method without a microscopic background and
an
ab initio
determination of an EDF is an important topic in nuclear physics community
Naito et al. 2019
;
Accorto et al. 2020
;
Marino et al. 2021
.
So far,
the Skyrme Hartree-Fock calculation
Skyrme 1958
;
Vautherin and Brink 1972

has been used in
most study of isospin symmetry breaking in nuclear DFT,
where the EDF form used is obtained phenemenologically or based on QCD sum rule.
In the covariant density functional theory (CDFT), which is another branch of the nuclear DFT,
one starts from the Lagrangian of the meson exchange picture
Meng et al. 2006
;
Nikšić et al. 2011
.
Since the origin of the isospin asymmetric nuclear interaction is well understood in the meson exchange picture,
the study of the isospin symmetry breaking using the CDFT should be a promissing approach.

In this paper, we consider the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing process in the relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov (RHB) model,
which is a branch of the CDFT.
Although the Fock term is neglected and thus the pion cannot be considered explicitly,
the numerical cost of the RHB calculation is low compared to the relativistic Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov calculation.
The CSB interaction usually contributes to nuclear properties stronger than the CIB one
Naito et al. 2023a
,
while it has been known that the bare CIB interaction is approximately one order of the magnitude stronger than the CSB one in the bare interaction
Wiringa et al. 1995
;
Miller et al. 2006
.
In addition, since the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing is the dominant origin of the CSB interaction in the meson exchange picture,
the RHB calculation with the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing is enough as the first step.
We start from the DD-ME2 Lagrangian
Lalazissis et al. 2005
,
which is one of the successful relativistic mean-field Lagrangians with the density-dependent meson-exchange model,
and consider the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing explicitly.
Since the size of the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing is rather arbitrary
Coon et al. 1977
;
Coon and Barrett 1987
;
Piekarewicz and Williams 1993
,
we fix the size of the mixing using the mass difference of the mirror nuclei.
Using the optimized parameter sets for selected observables,
we perform the systematic calculations of mass differences of mirror nuclei and discuss also the properties of the equation of state of nuclear matter.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we introduce the relativistic mean-field model with

\omega
-
\rho^{0}

In Sec.
III

The summary is given in Sec.
IV

II.1.2
Equations of motion for meson fields

We impose the time-reversal symmetry of the ground states of even-even nuclei.
Then, the space-like components of the meson and electromagnetic fields all vanish.
In addition, due to the charge conservation, the charged
\rho
meson fields also vanish.
Thus, only the time-even nucleon densities act as sources for the time-like fields.
The scalar, vector-isoscalar, and vector-isovector densities of nucleon are given
in terms of the canonical single-particle wave functions
\psi_{k}\left({\bm{r}}\right)
as

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\overline{\psi}\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{k}v_{k}^{2}\overline{\psi}_{k}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\psi_{k}\left({\bm{r}}\right),

(7a)

\displaystyle\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\overline{\psi}\gamma^{0}\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{k}v_{k}^{2}\psi_{k}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}\right)\psi_{k}\left({\bm{r}}\right),

(7b)

\displaystyle\rho_{3}\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\overline{\psi}\gamma^{0}\tau_{3}\psi}\right\rangle=\sum_{k}v_{k}^{2}\psi_{k}^{\dagger}\tau_{3}\psi_{k}\left({\bm{r}}\right),

(7c)

respectively, where
v_{k}^{2}
is the occupation probability of the state
k

The equations of motion for the meson fields are given by

\displaystyle\left(-\bm{\nabla}^{2}+m_{\sigma}^{2}\right)\sigma\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=-g_{\sigma}\rho_{S}\left({\bm{r}}\right),

(8a)

\displaystyle\left(-\bm{\nabla}^{2}+m_{\omega}^{2}\right)\omega\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\omega}\rho\left({\bm{r}}\right)-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\,\rho^{0}\left({\bm{r}}\right),

(8b)

\displaystyle\left(-\bm{\nabla}^{2}+m_{\rho}^{2}\right)\rho^{0}\left({\bm{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\rho}\rho_{3}\left({\bm{r}}\right)-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\,\omega\left({\bm{r}}\right).

(8c)

Notice that
\omega
and
\rho^{0}
fields are coupled due to the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}

Electromagnetic field

In calculating the charge density and electromagnetic field,

Results and Discussion

The parameters of the DD-ME-CSB model are listed in Table

In the present model, the nucleon-

The contributions of

Notice that, at
N<Z
side, the both contributions become positive, reducing the binding energy.

The value of

0.087\pm 0.01\,\mathrm{fm}^{-2}

0.116\pm 0.02\,\mathrm{fm}^{-2}

,
which is similar to the fine-structure constant, is also consistent with one of the possible
mechanisms for
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
conversion by an electromagnetic process.

III.1
Matter properties

The equation of state (EoS) of nuclear matter
of the present model is given as a function of the total nucleon density
\rho=\rho_{n}+\rho_{p}

and the relative asymmetry
\delta=\left(\rho_{n}-\rho_{p}\right)/\rho
.
The EoS can be expanded around
\left(\rho,\delta\right)=\left(\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}},0\right)
as

\frac{E}{A}\left(\rho,\delta\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{m,n}\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{\rho-\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}}}{3\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}}}\right)^{m}\delta^{n}

(20)

with

e_{m,n}=\frac{1}{n!}\left(3\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}}\right)^{m}\left.\frac{\partial^{n+m}\left(E/A\right)}{\partial\rho^{m}\,\partial\delta^{n}}\right|_{\left(\rho,\delta\right)=\left(\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}},0\right)}.

(21)

Note that
e_{0,0}
corresponds to the energy per nucleon of symmetric nuclear matter at saturation.
By definition,
e_{1,0}=0
holds, and
e_{2,0}=K_{\infty}
represents the incompressibility of symmetric matter.
Only the terms with even
n
appear in the expansions (
20
) and (
21
) if the CSB interaction is not considered.
The parameters characterizing the symmetry energy are
e_{0,2}=J_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sym}}}}}}
and
e_{1,2}=L_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sym}}}}}}
.
The present model also includes the term linear in
\delta
due to CSB,
with parameters defined as
e_{0,1}\equiv J_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}
and
e_{1,1}\equiv L_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}
.
The energy per nucleon (relative to
e_{0,0}
) of pure neutron matter at saturation density,

\left(\rho,\delta\right)=\left(\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}},1\right)
,
is given approximately by
J_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sym}}}}}}+J_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}
,
and the corresponding slope parameter by
L_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sym}}}}}}+L_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}
.

Table
2
summarizes the nuclear matter properties of DD-ME-CSB
in comparison with those of DD-ME2.
The two models yield similar results, while DD-ME-CSB gives larger values
for the incompressibility
K_{\infty}
and the slope parameter
L
of symmetry energy.
The larger value of
L
may arise from the significant difference of

N
-
\rho
coupling in DD-ME-CSB compared to DD-ME2.
The incompressibility of
K_{\infty}=277\,\mathrm{MeV}
obtained with DD-ME-CSB is comparable
with that of NL3 parameter set (
K_{\infty}=272\,\mathrm{MeV}
)
Lalazissis et al. 1997
.
Unlike DD-ME2, where
K_{\infty}
was constrained around
250\,\mathrm{MeV}

in order to reproduce experimental giant monopole resonance energies
Lalazissis et al. 2005
,
no such constraint was imposed in the present fit.

1
shows a comparison of the EoSs of the two models.
While they are similar at sub-saturation densities, the differences become increasingly
pronounced at densities above saturation.

\omega

and the sum of the kinetic energy and the charge-symmetry-conserving (CSC) part of the potential energy, respectively.
The Coulomb term
\Delta B_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{C}}}}}}

\Delta B_{\omega\rho}

and the sum of kinetic and CSC potential energies
\Delta B_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{kin}}}}}+\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSC}}}}}}
.
The results are shown for both the DD-ME-CSB00 model (without
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing)
and the DD-ME-CSB model. For comparison, the experimental values of
\Delta B
with

III.4
Connection with Skyrme-type functional

Finally, we present a connection between the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing
model and a Skyrme-type CSB interaction.
An approximate correspondence between the two models can be established
through a gradient expansion of the energy density functional.

The CSB part of the Lagrangian density of the present model is given by

{\mathcal{L}}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}=\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\,\omega^{\mu}\rho^{0}_{\mu}.

(23)

Applying a gradient expansion
Tanimura and Hagino 2012
;
Bürvenich et al. 2002
of the meson propagators

\left(-\bm{\nabla}^{2}+m_{\omega,\rho}^{2}\right)^{-1}

[See Eqs. (
8b
) and (
8c
)],
we obtain the following expressions
for
\omega
and
\rho^{0}
fields to the first order in
\bm{\nabla}^{2}/m_{\omega,\,\rho}^{2}
as

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\approx\frac{g_{\omega}}{m_{\omega}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\bm{\nabla}^{2}}{m_{\omega}^{2}}\right)\left(\rho-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\,\rho^{0}\right),

(24a)

\displaystyle\rho^{0}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{g_{\rho}}{m_{\rho}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\bm{\nabla}^{2}}{m_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\left(\rho_{3}-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\,\omega\right).

(24b)

The corresponding CSB contribution of EDF,
retaining terms only to the first order in
\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}
, becomes

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{CSB}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\int d^{3}r\,\omega\rho^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}}{m_{\omega}^{2}m_{\rho}^{2}}\int d^{3}r\,g_{\omega}g_{\rho}\left[\left(\rho_{n}^{2}-\rho_{p}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{m_{\omega}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\left(\rho_{n}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{n}-\rho_{p}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{p}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{m_{\omega}^{2}}-\frac{1}{m_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\left(\rho_{n}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{p}-\rho_{p}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\approx\int d^{3}r\,\left[a_{0}\left(\rho_{n}^{2}-\rho_{p}^{2}\right)+a_{1}\left(\rho_{n}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{n}-\rho_{p}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{p}\right)\right],

(25)

where we have defined

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=-\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}\frac{g_{\omega}g_{\rho}}{m_{\omega}^{2}m_{\rho}^{2}},

(26a)

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{m_{\omega}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{\rho}^{2}}\right)a_{0}.

(26b)

The cross term
\rho_{n}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{p}-\rho_{p}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho_{n}
cancels out
upon integration by parts, where for simplicity we have neglected
the spatial dependence of the coupling constants
g_{\omega}
and
g_{\rho}

via their density dependence.

Table
3
lists the values of
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
extracted
from the parameter sets in the present work.
In calculating these parameters, we used the coupling constants at the saturation density, i.e.,

g_{\omega}\left(\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}}\right)
and
g_{\rho}\left(\rho_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{sat}}}}}}\right)
.
For comparison, the corresponding values of Skyrme models
Roca-Maza et al. 2018
;
Bączyk et al. 2019
;
Sagawa et al. 2024

are also shown.

The parameter
a_{0}
of DD-ME-CSB model is
comparable to those of the Skyrme-type CSB interactions,
except for
\text{SV}_{\text{T}}
-ISB (next-to-leading order)
Bączyk et al. 2019
, in which the parameters of
the leading and next-to-leading order in the gradient expansion
are fitted independently.

The values of the parameter
a_{1}
vary significantly among the models
listed in Table
3
.
It is important to note that the Skyrme interaction of the first order
in
\bm{\nabla}^{2}
yields not only terms of the form

\rho\bm{\nabla}^{2}\rho
in the EDF like the
a_{1}
term in Eq. (
25
),
but also
\rho\tau
type one

, where
\tau
is the kinetic energy density.
Furthermore, the interactions in Refs.
Roca-Maza et al. 2018
;
Bączyk et al. 2019

include also a charge-independence-breaking component as well.
However, it is worth noting that
a_{1}
here is given simultaneously with
a_{0}

via the relation (
26b
).
This is in contrast with the Skyrme model, where the parameters of the leading-
and the next-to-leading-order gradient terms are typically fitted independently,
as is discussed in Refs.
Naito et al. 2024
;
Sagawa et al. 2024
.
In this regard, it is also notable that the QCD-based approach in Ref.
Sagawa et al. 2024

determines the Skyrme parameters simultaneously up to the first order in
\bm{\nabla}^{2}
.

IV
Summary and perspectives

In summary, we have developed a relativistic mean-field model that incorporates the CSB of the nuclear force through

\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing, and we have investigated its performance in the nuclear matter and the mirror nuclei with the isospin
T
=1/2, 1 and 2.
To better constrain the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing effect, we have also included corrections to
the electromagnetic interaction: namely, the nucleon electromagnetic form factors, the first-order vacuum polarization (VP) effect, and the exact Coulomb exchange and the pairing terms.
The parameters of the meson-exchange interaction in the
ph
channel,
including the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing strength
\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}
,
were optimized to reproduce the mass differences of mirror nuclei with the isospin
T=1/2
,
as well as the binding energies and charge radii
of doubly and semi-magic nuclei.
The resulting parameter set is named DD-ME-CSB.
In addition, we introduced four other parameter sets: DD-ME-CSB00, DD-ME-CSB05, DD-ME-CSB10, and DD-ME-CSB15,
in which
\Delta m_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{v}}}}}}^{2}
is fixed at
0
,
0.05
,
0.10
, and
0.15\,\mathrm{fm}^{-2}
, respectively.

We evaluated the performance of these five parameter sets and found that all perform
comparably to existing models in reproducing the binding energies and charge radii of
doubly- and semi-magic nuclei (See also Appendix
C
).
Regarding the mass differences of the mirror nuclei,
the DD-ME-CSB set yields the best agreement with experimental data not only for the
T=1/2
nuclei included
in the optimization but also for the
T=1
and
2
systems.
The
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing term gives the major contribution to the mirror binding
energy differences,
although the effects of the nuclear
rearrangement induced by the Coulomb and CSB forces are also non-negligible.

Furthermore, a connection between the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing model
and a Skyrme-type CSB interaction was established by the gradient expansion method.
The corresponding leading-order Skyrme CSB parameter extracted from the DD-ME-CSB
model is found to be consistent with those of existing phenomenological Skyrme energy density functionals.

One possible extension of the present model is to incorporate CSB in the

N
-
\Lambda
interaction. Experimental evidence for such effects has been observed
in the binding energy differences of mirror
\Lambda
hypernuclei
in the
s
- and
p
-shell regions
Botta et al. 2017
;
Sun et al. 2025
.
While mechanisms other than
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing,
such as
\Lambda
-
\Sigma^{0}
mixing
Dalitz and Von Hippel 1964
,
are expected to play roles,
the
\omega
-
\rho^{0}
mixing may have significant contribution to the CSB of
N
-
\Lambda

interaction as well as to the
N
-
N
interaction,
and thus provides a global starting point for developing a CSB model for hypernuclei as well as non-hyper nuclei based on the same RMF model.

Appendix A
Coulomb-exchange and -pairing energies

In this appendix, we present the exchange and pairing contributions of the Coulomb interaction energy.
In the present calculations, we neglect the finite size of the nucleons and vacuum polarization (VP) effects.
In the second-quantization formalism, the charge density operator is expressed as

\hat{\rho}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}\left({\bm{r}}\right)=\sum_{\tau=p,\,n}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\left(\delta_{\tau p}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m_{\tau}}\bm{\nabla}\cdot i{\bm{\gamma}}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}\alpha\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}\left({\bm{r}}\beta\right)

(27)

where
\hat{\psi}_{\tau}
(
\tau=n
or
p
) denotes the neutron or proton field operator,

\alpha
and
\beta
are Dirac indices, and
\kappa_{\tau}
is the anomalous magnetic moment of the nucleon of type
\tau
.
The exchange (
E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cx}}}}}}
) and pairing (
E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cpair}}}}}}
) contributions to the Coulomb energy are given by

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cx}}}}}}+E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cpair}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{\tau=p,\,n}\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\int d^{3}r\,d^{3}r^{\prime}\,\frac{1}{\left|{\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime}\right|}\left(\delta_{\tau p}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m_{\tau}}i\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}_{\alpha\beta}\right)\left(\delta_{\tau p}\delta_{\gamma\delta}+\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m_{\tau}}i\bm{\nabla}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}_{\gamma\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[-\left\langle{\hat{\psi}_{\tau}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}\alpha\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}\left({\bm{r}}^{\prime}\delta\right)}\right\rangle\left\langle{\hat{\psi}_{\tau}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}^{\prime}\gamma\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}\left({\bm{r}}\beta\right)}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\hat{\psi}_{\tau}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}\alpha\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}^{\dagger}\left({\bm{r}}^{\prime}\gamma\right)}\right\rangle\left\langle{\hat{\psi}_{\tau}\left({\bm{r}}^{\prime}\delta\right)\hat{\psi}_{\tau}\left({\bm{r}}\beta\right)}\right\rangle\right],

(28)

where the first term inside the square brackets represents the exchange contribution,
and the second term corresponds to the pairing contribution.

With spherical symmetry, the quasiparticle wave functions are eigenstates of total angular momentum
and are written as

\psi_{am}\left({\bm{r}}\right)=\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{U}}}}}}\left(r\right)\\
-iF_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{U}}}}}}\left(r\right){\bm{\sigma}}\cdot\hat{{\bm{r}}}\\
G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)\\
-iF_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right){\bm{\sigma}}\cdot\hat{{\bm{r}}}\end{pmatrix}{\mathcal{Y}}_{l_{a}j_{a}m}\left(\theta,\phi\right),

(29)

where
{\mathcal{Y}}_{ljm}\left(\theta,\phi\right)
is the spinor spherical harmonic with
orbital angular momentum
l
, total angular momentum
j
, and magnetic quantum number
m
,
and
\hat{{\bm{r}}}={\bm{r}}/r
is the unit radial vector.
The label
a\equiv\left(n_{a},\kappa_{a}\right)
denotes the radial quantum number
n_{a}
and
the angular quantum number
\kappa_{a}
specifying
j_{a}
and
l_{a}
as

\kappa_{a}=\mp\left(j_{a}+1/2\right)
for
j_{a}=l_{a}\pm 1/2
.
To evaluate the exchange and pairing contributions in the spherical case,
we make use of the multipole expansion of the Coulomb potential:

\frac{1}{\left|{\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}^{\prime}\right|}=\sum_{\lambda}f_{\lambda}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\frac{4\pi}{2\lambda+1}\sum_{\mu}Y_{\lambda\mu}\left({\bm{r}}\right)^{*}Y_{\lambda\mu}\left({\bm{r}}^{\prime}\right),

(30)

with
f_{\lambda}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=r_{<}^{\lambda}/r_{>}^{\lambda+1}
,

r_{<}\equiv\min\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)
and
r_{>}\equiv\max\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)
.

The exchange energy is then expressed as

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cx}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{\tau=p,\,n}\sum_{ab\in\tau}\left(2j_{a}+1\right)\left(2j_{b}+1\right)\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1+\left(-1\right)^{l_{a}+\lambda+l_{b}}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}j_{a}&\lambda&j_{b}\\
1/2&0&-1/2\end{pmatrix}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int dr\,dr^{\prime}\,f_{\lambda}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\left[\delta_{\tau p}R_{ab}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(1)}}}}}}\left(r\right)+\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m_{\tau}}R_{ab}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(2)}}}}}}\left(r\right)\right]\left[\delta_{\tau p}R_{ba}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(1)}}}}}}\left(r^{\prime}\right)+\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m_{\tau}}R_{ba}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(2)}}}}}}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\right],

(31)

where

\displaystyle R_{ab}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(1)}}}}}}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle\equiv G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)G_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)+F_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)F_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right),

(32a)

\displaystyle R_{ab}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{(2)}}}}}}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle\equiv G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)\tilde{F}_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)+F_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)\tilde{G}_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)+\tilde{G}_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)F_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)+\tilde{F}_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}\left(r\right)G_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right),

(32b)

and

\tilde{G}_{a}\left(r\right)=\left(\frac{d}{dr}+\frac{\kappa_{a}}{r}\right)G_{a}\left(r\right),\qquad\tilde{F}_{a}\left(r\right)=\left(\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{\kappa_{a}}{r}\right)F_{a}\left(r\right).

(33)

The pairing energy is calculated by neglecting the small components
F_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{U}}}}}}
and
F_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}
of the quasiparticle wave functions:

\displaystyle E_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{Cpair}}}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum_{ab\in p}\left(2j_{a}+1\right)\left(2j_{b}+1\right)\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1+\left(-1\right)^{l_{a}+\lambda+l_{b}}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}j_{a}&\lambda&j_{b}\\
1/2&0&-1/2\end{pmatrix}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int dr\,dr^{\prime}\,G^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}*}_{b}\left(r\right)G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{V}}}}}}\left(r\right)f_{\lambda}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)G_{a}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{U}}}}}*}\left(r^{\prime}\right)G_{b}^{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{U}}}}}}\left(r^{\prime}\right).

(34)

The Coulomb exchange and pairing energies as given in Eqs. (
31
) and (
34
) are
added perturbatively after we obtain a self consistent solution.

Appendix B
Charge radius with center-of-mass correction

The mean-square charge radius is defined as

\left\langle{r^{2}}\right\rangle_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}=-\frac{\left.\bm{\nabla}^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}\left({\bm{q}}\right)\right|_{{\bm{q}}={\bm{0}}}}{Z}.

(35)

To account for the center-of-mass (CM) correction to the mean-square charge radius
Tanimura and Cheoun 2024
,
the nuclear charge form factor is modified using the corrected charge-density operator as

\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}\left({\bm{q}}\right)=\left\langle{\sum_{k=1}^{A}e^{i{\bm{q}}\cdot\left(\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{k}-\hat{{\bm{R}}}_{G}\right)}\left[F_{1k}\left({\bm{q}}^{2}\right)+\frac{\kappa_{k}}{2m}F_{2k}\left({\bm{q}}^{2}\right){\bm{q}}\cdot\hat{{\bm{\gamma}}}_{k}\right]}\right\rangle,

(36)

where
\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{k}
is the position operator of the
k
th nucleon,

\hat{{\bm{R}}}_{G}=\left(1/A\right)\sum_{k=1}^{A}\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{k}
is the CM operator,
and
\hat{{\bm{\gamma}}}_{k}
is the Dirac matrix acting on the
k
th nucleon,
Here,
F_{1k}
,
F_{2k}
, and
\kappa_{k}
are
the nucleon form factors and anomalous magnetic moment, respectively, for the isospin of nucleon
k
.
This correction leads to the following expression for the mean-square charge radius:

\displaystyle\left\langle{r^{2}}\right\rangle^{\prime}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\left.\bm{\nabla}^{2}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}\left({\bm{q}}\right)\right|_{{\bm{q}}={\bm{0}}}}{Z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\left\langle{\sum_{k\in p}\left(\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{k}-\hat{{\bm{R}}}_{G}\right)^{2}}\right\rangle-\frac{2}{Z}\sum_{\tau=p,\,n}\frac{\kappa_{\tau}}{2m}\left\langle{\sum_{k\in\tau}\left(\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{k}-\hat{{\bm{R}}}_{G}\right)\cdot i\hat{{\bm{\gamma}}}_{k}}\right\rangle+C_{p}+\frac{N}{Z}C_{n}

(37)

with

C_{\tau}=-6\left.\frac{dF_{1\tau}\left(0\right)}{d{\bm{q}}^{2}}\right|_{{\bm{q}}^{2}=0}.

(38)

The root-mean-square charge radius
R_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}
corrected for the CM motion is then given by

R_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}=\sqrt{\left\langle{r^{2}}\right\rangle^{\prime}_{\scriptstyle{\text{{{{ch}}}}}}}.

(39)

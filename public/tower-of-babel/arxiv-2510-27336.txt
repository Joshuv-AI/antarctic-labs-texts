Numerical solution of elliptic distributed optimal control problems with boundary value tracking
Ulrich Langer, Richard Löscher, Olaf Steinbach, Huidong Yang

Abstract
We consider some boundary value tracking optimal control problem constrained by a Neumann boundary value problem for some elliptic partial differential equation where the control acts as right-hand side. This optimal control problem can be reformulated as a state-based variational problem that is the starting point for the finite element discretizion. In this paper, we only consider a tensor-product finite element discretizion for which optimal discretization error estimates and fast solvers can be derived. Numerical experiments illustrate the theoretical results quantitatively.



1
Introduction

Let us consider the following boundary tracking optimal control problem (ocp):
Find the state

y_{\varrho}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

and the optimal control
u_{\varrho}\in U
minimizing the cost functional

J(y_{\varrho},u_{\varrho})=\frac{1}{2}\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\varrho\,\|u_{\varrho}\|_{U}^{2}

(1)

subject to the Neumann boundary value problem (bvp)

-\Delta y_{\varrho}+y_{\varrho}=u_{\varrho}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\quad\partial_{n}y_{\varrho}=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Gamma,

(2)

where

\overline{y}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
denotes a
given target,
\varrho\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a positive regularization or
cost parameter, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, is a bounded
Lipschitz domain with the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.
Our work
was
inspired by the paper
[
4
]
,
where the
L^{2}
regularization corresponding to the choice
U=L^{2}(\Omega)

was investigated.
The boundary tracking is a special case of the partial tracking of a given
target in a subset of
\Omega
that is also called limited observation;
see, e.g.,
[
5
]
.
In this contribution, we consider the energy regularization
corresponding to the choice
U=\widetilde{H}^{-1}(\Omega):=[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{*}
,
where we use the standard notations for Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces;
see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.
In order to follow the abstract theory presented in

[
3
]
, we define the state space in such
a way that the state-to-control map is an isomorphism. This allows us to
derive a state-based formulation which is the basis for the numerical solution.
Here we restrict the analysis to a conforming
tensor-product finite element (fe) discretization
that finally leads to a linear system of algebraic equations for which fast
solvers can be constructed.

2
State-based variational reformulation

The variational formulation of the Neumann bvp
(
2
) reads to find
y_{\varrho}\in H^{1}(\Omega)

such that

\langle\nabla y_{\varrho},\nabla y\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle y_{\varrho},y\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle u_{\varrho},y\rangle_{\Omega}

(3)

is satisfied for all
y\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, where we assume

u_{\varrho}\in\widetilde{H}^{-1}(\Omega)
. While the Neumann boundary
condition in (
2
) enters the variational formulation
(
3
) in a natural way, this condition has to be
included in the definition of the state space

Y:=\Big\{y\in H^{1}(\Omega):\langle\partial_{n}y,\phi\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\;\mbox{for all}\;\phi\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\Big\}.

When using duality arguments, we then conclude

\|u_{\varrho}\|_{Y^{*}}:=\sup\limits_{0\neq y\in Y}\frac{\langle u_{\varrho},y\rangle_{\Omega}}{\|y\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}=\sup\limits_{0\neq y\in Y}\frac{\langle y_{\varrho},y\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}{\|y\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}=\|y_{\varrho}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

and instead of (
1
) we can consider the reduced
state-based cost functional

\widetilde{J}(y_{\varrho})=\frac{1}{2}\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\frac{1}{2}\,\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(4)

whose minimizer
y_{\varrho}\in Y
is the unique solution of the gradient
equation satisfying

\langle y_{\varrho},y\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\varrho\,\langle y_{\varrho},y\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\langle\overline{y},y\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y\in Y.

(5)

Following the abstract theory as given in

[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have the following
regularization error estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
y_{\varrho}\in Y
be the unique solution of the variational formulation
(
5
). For
\overline{y}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)

there hold the estimates

\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},\quad\|y_{\varrho}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\varrho^{-1/2}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(6)

If
\overline{y}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
is the Dirichlet trace of

\overline{y}_{e}\in Y
, then

\|y_{\varrho}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
, and

\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\varrho^{1/2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\quad\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(7)

If
\overline{y}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
is the Dirichlet trace of

\overline{y}_{e}\in Y\cap H^{3/2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for some

\varepsilon>0
, then

\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,\varrho\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},\quad\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\varrho^{1/2}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

(8)

Note that the extension
\overline{y}_{e}\in Y
of

\overline{y}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
as used in
Lemma
2.1
can be computed as solution of
the first boundary value problem for the biharmonic equation,

\Delta^{2}\overline{y}_{e}=0\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,\quad\overline{y}_{e}=\overline{y},\quad\partial_{n}\overline{y}_{e}=0\quad\mbox{on}\;\Gamma.

(9)

3
Conforming FE discretization on tensor product meshes

For a conforming fe discretization of the variational formulation
(
5
), we need to introduce a fe space

Y_{h}\subset Y
of basis functions with zero normal derivatives.
In this paper, we restrict our considerations to the unit square

\Omega=(0,1)^{d}
which allows us to use appropriate tensor product fe
spaces. Let
\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1):=\mbox{span}\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}

be the modified fe space of piecewise linear and continuous basis functions

\varphi_{i}
which are defined with respect to a decomposition

0=x_{0}<x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{n-1}<x_{n}=1
of the unit interval,
with the local mesh sizes
h_{i}:=x_{i}-x_{i-1}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
and with the global mesh size
h:=\max_{i}h_{i}
. While the basis functions

\varphi_{i}
for
i=2,\ldots,n-2
are the standard piecewise linear and
continuous ones, the basis functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{n-1}
are

1
in the intervals
(x_{0},x_{1})
and
(x_{n-1},x_{n})
, respectively;
see Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Modified piecewise linear basis functions
\varphi_{i}(x)
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

By construction, we have
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(x)=0
for

x\in\{0,1\}
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.
We now define the conforming fe space

Y_{h}=\otimes_{i=1}^{d}\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1)=\mbox{span}\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}\subset Y

of piecewise multi-linear continuous basis functions
\phi_{k}
with
vanishing Neumann trace
\partial_{n}\phi_{k}
on
\Gamma
.
We note that
m=(n-1)^{d}
. The fe discretization of the variational
formulation (
5
) leads to the finite element
scheme: Find
y_{\varrho h}\in Y_{h}
such that

\langle y_{\varrho h},y_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\varrho\,\langle y_{\varrho h},y_{h}\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\langle\overline{y},y_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y_{h}\in Y_{h}.

(10)

Using standard arguments, we immediately arrive at the Cea-type estimate

\displaystyle\|y_{\varrho}-y_{\varrho h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}-y_{\varrho h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(11)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\|y_{\varrho}-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}-y_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y_{h}\in Y_{h}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
y_{\varrho h}\in Y_{h}
be the unique solution of
(
10
). Then,
for
\overline{y}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, there holds the error estimate

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq(1+\sqrt{2})\,\|\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

(12)

while, for
\overline{y}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
and choosing
\varrho=h
,
we have

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,h^{1/2}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}.

(13)

A related result holds for
\overline{y}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
where,
for
\varrho=h
,

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,h\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

(14)

Proof.

For
\overline{y}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, we consider (
11
)
for
y_{h}=0
, and we use
(
5
) and
(
6
) to obtain

\|y_{\varrho}-y_{\varrho h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq\|y_{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 2\,\|\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

With the triangle inequality and again using
(
6
) we therefore conclude

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|y_{\varrho h}-y_{\varrho}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq(1+\sqrt{2})\,\|\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\,.

Next we consider the case when
\overline{y}_{e}\in Y
is the
extension of
\overline{y}
as solution of the biharmonic
boundary value problem (
9
). When
using the triangle inequality twice in combination with Cea’s
estimate (
11
), we can write, for arbitrary
y_{h}\in Y_{h}

\displaystyle\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2\,\|y_{\varrho}-y_{\varrho h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2\,\|y_{\varrho}-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+2\,\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}-y_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 6\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+4\,\|\overline{y}-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\,4\,\varrho\,\|y_{\varrho}-\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+4\,\varrho\,\|\overline{y}_{e}-y_{h}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

In particular for
y_{h}=P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\in Y_{h}

being the
L^{2}
projection of
\overline{y}_{e}
, we have the standard fe
error estimates

\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\quad\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(16)

When using
[
1
, Theorem 3.6]
and a space
interpolation argument, we also have

\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h^{1/2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Hence, using (
7
) and

\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}
,
this gives

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\,\leq\,c\,(\varrho+h)\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2},

and (
13
) follows when choosing
\varrho=h
.

Finally, we consider the case
\overline{y}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
which
implies
\overline{y}_{e}\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
. In this case, we have

\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h^{1/2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)},\;\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h^{3/2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)},

and

\|\overline{y}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,h\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}.

Together with (
8
) we therefore conclude

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\,\leq\,c\,(\varrho^{2}+\varrho h+h^{2})\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and for
\varrho=h
this gives (
14
).
∎

While the regularization error estimates as given in
Lemma
2.1
are optimal in
\varrho
for

\overline{y}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, i.e.,

\overline{y}_{e}\in H^{3/2}(\Omega)
, we can expect higher order
convergence for the fe approximation
P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}
when

\overline{y}_{e}
is more regular. In particular for

\overline{y}_{e}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
we have

\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},\;\|\overline{y}_{e}-P_{h}\overline{y}_{e}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\,h^{2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
\overline{y}_{e}
is defined as solution of the biharmonic
boundary value problem (
9
), we have
to formulate additional compatibility conditions in order to
ensure
\overline{y}_{e}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, when
\Gamma=\partial\Omega

is piecewise smooth. In particular, the tangential derivatives
of
\overline{y}
have to be in
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, see, e.g., the
discussion in
[
2
,
6
]
in the
two-dimensional case. Then we can use (
15
)
to conclude

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\,\leq\,c_{1}\,\varrho^{2}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+c_{2}\,(h^{3}+\varrho\,h^{2})\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

and for
\varrho\leq h^{3/2}
we obtain

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\,\leq\,c\,h^{3/2}\,\Big[\|\overline{y}_{e}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\Big]^{1/2}.

(17)

However, for
\overline{y}\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
, i.e.,

\overline{y}_{e}\in H^{5/2}(\Omega)
, and using the best approximation
estimate for the boundary term, this gives

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\,\leq\,c_{1}\,h^{4}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+c_{2}\,\varrho^{2}\,\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+c_{3}\,\varrho\,h^{2}\,\|\overline{y}_{e}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

and for
\varrho=h^{2}
we finally obtain

\|y_{\varrho h}-\overline{y}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\,\leq\,c\,h^{2}\Big[\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|\overline{y}\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|\overline{y}_{e}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\Big]^{1/2}.

(18)

4
Fast solvers

Once the basis is chosen, the finite element scheme
(
10
)
is equivalent to a linear system of finite element equations
that can be written in the form

[\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{h}+\varrho(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{h}+\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{h})]{\mathbf{y}}_{h}=\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{h},

(19)

where the matrices
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{h}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{h}
,
and
\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{h}
have the respective block representations

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{h}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{0}}\\
{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{M}}_{BB}\end{array}\right),\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{h}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{0}}\\
{\mathbf{0}}&\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}\end{array}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{h}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{II}&\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{IB}\\
\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{BI}&\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}\end{array}\right),

when we split the unknowns (dofs)

\mathbf{y}_{h}=(\mathbf{y}_{I}^{\top},\mathbf{y}_{B}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)^{d}}

into strict interior unknowns
\mathbf{y}_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-3)^{d}}

and near-boundary unknowns
\mathbf{y}_{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)^{d}-(n-3)^{d}}
.
The matrices
{\mathbf{M}}_{BB}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}
and

\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{h}
are defined by the identities

({\mathbf{M}}_{BB}\mathbf{y}_{B},\mathbf{v}_{B})=\langle y_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
,

(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}\mathbf{y}_{B},\mathbf{v}_{B})=\langle y_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{H^{1}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{h})}
,

(\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{h}\mathbf{y}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\langle y_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{H^{1}(\mathring{\Omega}_{h})}

for all
\mathbf{y}_{h}=(\mathbf{y}_{I}^{\top},\mathbf{y}_{B}^{\top})^{\top}\leftrightarrow y_{h},v_{h}\in Y_{h}
(fe isomorphism),
where

\mathring{\Omega}_{h}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\widetilde{\Omega}}_{h}=(h,1-h)^{d}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}_{h}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\mathring{\Omega}}_{h}
,
whereas
\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{h}=(\mathbf{0}_{I}^{\top},\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{B}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

is given by
(\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{B},\mathbf{y}_{B})=\langle\overline{y}_{h},y_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for all
\mathbf{y}_{h}=(\mathbf{y}_{I}^{\top},\mathbf{y}_{B}^{\top})^{\top}\leftrightarrow y_{h},v_{h}\in Y_{h}
.

Eliminating
\mathbf{y}_{I}=-\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{II}^{-1}\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{IB}\mathbf{y}_{B}

from the linear system (
19
), we arrive at the boundary Schur complement
system

{\mathbf{S}}_{BB}\mathbf{y}_{B}=\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{B}

(20)

with
{\mathbf{S}}_{BB}={\mathbf{M}}_{BB}+\varrho(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}+\mathring{\mathbf{S}}_{BB})={\mathbf{M}}_{BB}+\varrho(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}+(\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}-\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{BI}\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{II}^{-1}\mathring{\mathbf{K}}_{IB}))
.
The Schur complement system (
20
) can efficiently be solved by means of
the Conjugate Gradient (CG) method without any preconditioning
since, for
\varrho\leq h
, the Schur complement
{\mathbf{S}}_{BB}
is spectrally equivalent to
the boundary mass matrix
\mathbf{M}_{BB}
,
and in turn
\mathbf{M}_{BB}
is spectrally equivalent to
the lumped boundary mass matrix
\mbox{lump}(\mathbf{M}_{BB})

and to
h^{d-1}{\mathbf{I}}_{BB}
.
Indeed, it is easy to see that

{\mathbf{M}}_{BB}\leq{\mathbf{S}}_{BB}={\mathbf{M}}_{BB}+\varrho(\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{BB}+\mathring{\mathbf{S}}_{BB})\leq(1+\widetilde{c}\varrho h^{-1}+\mathring{c}\varrho h^{-1}){\mathbf{M}}_{BB}

(21)

with
h
and
\varrho
independent positive constants
\widetilde{c}
and
\mathring{c}
.
The choice
\varrho\leq h
delivers the desired result.
It is recommended to use
\mbox{lump}(\mathbf{M}_{BB})
as diagonal preconditioner
in the Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) method since it provides the right scaling.
The numerical results presented in Section
5
show
that
the system (
19
) can also efficiently be solved by means of
PCG with a simple Algebraic MultiGrid (AMG) preconditioner.

6
Conclusions and Outlook

We have investigated a boundary value tracking, distributed ocp
for some elliptic bvp with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions.
This ocp can be reduced to a state-based variational problem
for functions from
H^{1}(\Omega)
with vanishing normal derivatives in

H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
. We have constructed a conforming fe discretization for
the special case of tensor-product meshes,
and we have derived discretization error estimates and fast solvers.
The numerical experiments illustrate the theoretical results quantitatively.
In general, one has to include the homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions
by means of Lagrange multipliers, which is a topic
of future research.

where
\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1)
is the modified finite element space of
piecewise linear and continuous basis functions
\varphi_{i}(x)

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
y\in C([0,1])
be a given continuous function with

y^{\prime}(0)=y^{\prime}(1)=0
, and satisfying
y^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}(0,1)
.
For the piecewise linear interpolation

I_{h}y(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}y(x_{i})\varphi_{i}(x).

there hold the error estimates

\|y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},

(24)

\|y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{2}\,h\,\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},

(25)

and

\|y^{\prime}-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,h\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

(26)

Moreover, the interpolation operator is stable in
H^{1}(0,1)
, i.e.,

\|(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y\in H^{1}(0,1).

(27)

Proof.

For
i=2,\ldots,n-1
we have the standard interpolation error estimates

\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y(x)-I_{h}y(x)]^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{24}\,h^{4}\,\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx\,,

and

\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y^{\prime}(x)-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{3}\,h^{2}\,\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx\,,

For the first interval
(x_{0},x_{1})
we have
I_{h}y(x_{1})=y(x_{1})
, and hence
we can write for
x\in(x_{0},x_{1})

y(x)-I_{h}y(x)=[I_{h}y(x_{1})-I_{h}y(x)]-[y(x_{1})-y(x)]=\int_{x}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(s)-y^{\prime}(s)]\,ds,

and with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle[y(x)-I_{h}y(x)]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{x}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(s)-y^{\prime}(s)]\,ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{x_{1}}1^{2}\,ds\int_{x}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(s)-y^{\prime}(s)]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(x_{1}-x)\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(s)-y^{\prime}(s)]^{2}\,ds,

i.e.,

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[y(x)-I_{h}y(x)]^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)-y^{\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx.

Due to
y^{\prime}(x_{0})=(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
we further have

\displaystyle(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)-y^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x_{0})]-[y^{\prime}(x)-y^{\prime}(x_{0})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{x}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime\prime}(s)-y^{\prime\prime}(s)]\,ds\,=\,\int_{x_{0}}^{x}[-y^{\prime\prime}(s)]\,ds,

and hence,

\displaystyle[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)-y^{\prime}(x)]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}[-y^{\prime\prime}(s)]\,ds\right)^{2}\leq\int_{x_{0}}^{x}1^{2}\,ds\int_{x_{0}}^{x}[y^{\prime\prime}(s)]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(x-x_{0})\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[y^{\prime\prime}(s)]^{2}\,ds,

i.e.,

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)-y^{\prime}(x)]^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[y^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx

follows, and we also conclude

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[y(x)-I_{h}y(x)]^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\,h^{4}\,\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}[y^{\prime\prime}(x)]^{2}\,dx.

For the last interval
(x_{n-1},x_{n})
the proof follows the same lines.
When summing up the local contributions, the error estimates
(
24
) and
(
26
) follow.
Moreover, for
i=2,\ldots,n-1
and
x\in(x_{i-1},x_{i})
, we write

(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{h}[y(x_{i})-y(x_{i-1})]=\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}y^{\prime}(s)\,ds,

i.e.,

[(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)]^{2}=\left(\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}y^{\prime}(s)\,ds\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y^{\prime}(s)]^{2}\,ds.

Integration and summing up over all
i=2,\ldots,n-1
gives, recall

(I_{h}y)^{\prime}(x)=0
for
x\in[x_{0},x_{1}]\cup[x_{n-1},x_{n}]
,

\|(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n-1}\|(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\leq\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n-1}\|y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(x_{i-1},x_{i})}\leq\|y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)},

i.e., (
27
).
With this we finally conclude (
25
),

\|y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,h\,\|y^{\prime}-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{2}\,h\,\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

∎

Next we introduce the
L^{2}
projection
Q_{h}:L^{2}(0,1)\to\widetilde{S}^{1}_{h}(0,1)

Lemma 6.2
.

For any given
y\in L^{2}(0,1)
we define

Q_{h}y\in\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1)
as unique
solution of the variational formulation

\langle Q_{h}y,y_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\langle y,y_{h}\rangle_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y_{h}\in\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1).

(28)

Then,

\|Q_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y\in L^{2}(0,1).

(29)

For
y\in H^{2}(0,1)
with
y^{\prime}(0)=y^{\prime}(1)=0
there hold the error estimates

\|y-Q_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)},

(30)

and

\|y^{\prime}-(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq c\,h\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

(31)

Moreover, there holds the stability estimate

\|(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq c\,\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;y\in H^{1}(0,1),y^{\prime}(0)=y^{\prime}(1)=0.

(32)

Proof.

The stability estimate (
29
) is a consequence
of the variational formulation (
28
)
when choosing
y_{h}=Q_{h}y
. By Cea’s lemma and using
(
24
) we have
(
30
),

\|y-Q_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\inf\limits_{y_{h}\in Y_{h}}\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\|y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

Moreover, using the triangle inequatlity twice, the inverse inequality

\|y_{h}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\sqrt{12}\,h^{-1}\,\|y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\quad\mbox{for}\;y_{h}\in\widetilde{S}_{h}^{1}(0,1),

and the error extimates (
26
) as well as
(
24
) and
(
30
), this gives
(
31
),

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}-(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y^{\prime}-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|(I_{h}y)^{\prime}-(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\leq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,h\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+2\sqrt{3}\,h^{-1}\,\|I_{h}y-Q_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\leq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,h\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+2\sqrt{3}\,h^{-1}\,\Big[\|I_{h}y-y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y-Q_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\leq\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sqrt{2}+4\sqrt{3}\Big)\,h\,\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

When using the same arguments we finally have

\displaystyle\|(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(Q_{h}y)^{\prime}-(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|(I_{h}y)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{3}\,h^{-1}\|Q_{h}y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{3}\,h^{-1}\Big[\|Q_{h}y-y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y-I_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\Big]+\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+4\sqrt{6})\,\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
for
\Omega=(0,1)^{d}
.
Assume
y\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
, i.e., there exists an extension

y_{e}\in Y\cap H^{5/2}(\Omega)
as solution of the biharmonic
boundary value problem (
9
).
Then, there holds the error estimate

\|y-P_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\,h^{2}\,|y|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(35)

Proof.

Again, we consider the case
d=2
only.
Let
\Gamma_{0}:=\{(x,0):x\in(0,1)\}
. With
(
24
) and
(
29
) we then have

\displaystyle\|y-P_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|y-Q_{h_{1}}Q_{h_{2}}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y-Q_{h_{1}}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\|Q_{h_{1}}(y-Q_{h_{2}}y)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\|\partial_{x_{1}x_{1}}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}+\|y-Q_{h_{2}}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,h^{2}\,\|y\|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega)}+\|y-I_{h_{2}}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}.

For
x\in\Gamma_{0}
we can write, using
\partial_{x_{2}}y_{e}(x,s)_{|s=0}=0
,

\displaystyle y(x,0)-I_{h_{2}}y(x,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y_{e}(x,0)-y_{e}(x,h)=-\int_{0}^{h}\partial_{x_{2}}y_{e}(x,s)\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=\,\int_{0}^{h}[\partial_{x_{2}}y_{e}(x,0)-\partial_{x_{2}}y_{e}(x,s)]\,ds\,=\,-\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)\,d\tau ds.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle[y(x,0)-I_{h_{2}}y(x,0)]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{s}1\cdot\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)\,d\tau ds\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}1^{2}\,ds\int_{0}^{h}\left[\int_{0}^{s}1\cdot\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)\,d\tau\right]^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{s}1^{2}\,d\tau\int_{0}^{s}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)]^{2}d\tau ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\int_{0}^{h}s\,ds\int_{0}^{h}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,h^{3}\,\int_{0}^{h}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)]^{2}d\tau.

Next we consider
x\in(x_{i-1},x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and
use the piecewise constant
L^{2}
projection

\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)\,d\eta,

to write

[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)]^{2}\leq 2\,[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}+2\,[\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2},

i.e.,

[y(x,0)-I_{h_{2}}y(x,0)]^{2}\,\leq\,h^{3}\int_{0}^{h}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}d\tau+h^{3}\int_{0}^{h}[\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}d\tau,

and integration over
x\in(x_{i-1},x_{i})
gives

\displaystyle\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y(x,0)-I_{h_{2}}y(x,0)]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\,h^{3}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\int_{0}^{h}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}d\tau dx+h^{3}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\int_{0}^{h}[\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}d\tau dx.

Using

\displaystyle\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\left[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)\,d\eta\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\,\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\left[\frac{1}{h}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)]\,d\eta\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\,\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\left[\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\frac{\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)}{|x-\eta|}\,|x-\eta|\,d\eta\right]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\,\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}|x-\eta|^{2}\,d\eta\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\frac{[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)]^{2}}{|x-\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta dx

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\,h\,\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\frac{[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)]^{2}}{|x-\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta dx,

we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}[y(x,0)-I_{h_{2}}y(x,0)]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\,h^{4}\,\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\frac{[\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(x,\tau)-\partial_{x_{2}x_{2}}y_{e}(\eta,\tau)]^{2}}{|x-\eta|^{2}}\,d\eta dx+h^{4}\int_{0}^{h}[\hat{y}_{i}(\tau)]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\,h^{4}\,|y_{e}|^{2}_{H^{5/2}((x_{i-1},x_{i})\times(0,h))}.

When summing up all contrubutions, this finally gives

\|y-P_{h}y\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})}\leq c\,h^{2}\,|y_{e}|_{H^{5/2}(\Omega)}.

For all other boundary edges, the proof follows the same lines.

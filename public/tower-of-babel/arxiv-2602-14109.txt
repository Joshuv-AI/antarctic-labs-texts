Categorical resolutions and birational geometry of nodal Gushel–Mukai varieties
Kacper Grzelakowski, Marco Rampazzo, Shizhuo Zhang

Abstract
. An ordinary Gushel–Mukai variety with a single isolated node is the intersection of the Grassmannian G ⁡ ( 2 , 5 ) G(2,5) with a nodal quadric and a linear space. We consider such intersections in dimension three, four and five. We describe a flop between the blowup of such a variety and a quadric fibration over ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} : at the level of derived categories, this flop establishes an equivalence between the categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component of the Gushel–Mukai variety and the derived category of modules on the even part of the Clifford algebra of the quadric fibration. As a first application, we extend a result of Kuznetsov and Perry to the nodal case, and we describe a subfamily of rational, nodal Gushel–Mukai fourfolds whose Kuznetsov components admit a categorical resolution of singularities by an actual K ​ 3 K3 surface of degree two without a Brauer twist. This produces evidence for a version of Kuznetsov’s rationality conjecture. Then, we describe the relation with Verra threefolds and fourfolds at the categorical level. As a further application, we show that the categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component of a 1-nodal Gushel-Mukai threefold determines its birational class.

For

n

X_{n}=G(2,5)\cap Q\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{n+4}

where
Q

{\mathbb{P}}^{9}

[

These varieties present remarkable features, and have been object of thorough study from several perspectives, including Hodge theory, birational geometry and derived categories, especially because of deep links with EPW varieties and hyperkähler manifolds. To any GM variety one can canonically associate a sextic hypersurface in

The pair consisting of a GM variety and its associated double EPW sextic exhibits many parallels with the relationship between cubic fourfolds and their Fano varieties of lines (which are again hyperkähler) and such pairs provide one of the few known sources of Fano–hyperkähler correspondences in higher dimensions. In odd dimensions the intermediate Jacobian of a GM variety is similarly determined by the geometry of the associated EPW sextic, underlining the central role of EPW constructions in understanding the Hodge theory and moduli of GM varieties.

The notion of a Fano variety of
K3
type is expected to have a categorical counterpart at the level of derived categories in terms of a semiorthogonal decomposition containing a
K3
category (i.e. its Serre functor is a shift by two and its Hochschild cohomology is the same as an actual
K3

Indeed, in both the cubic fourfold and the GM fourfold cases, the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves admits a semiorthogonal decomposition whose nontrivial component is a
K3
category.
More precisely, for a smooth cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
one has a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}^{b}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{K}u(Y),\,\mathcal{O}_{Y},\,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1),\,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(2)\rangle,

where the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{K}u(Y)
is a
K3
category
[
36
]
.
Similarly, for a smooth ordinary GM fourfold
Y
, there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}^{b}(X)=\langle\mathcal{K}u(X),\,\mathcal{O}_{X},\,\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\vee},\,\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\,\mathcal{U}_{X}^{\vee}(1)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{U}_{X}
denotes the restriction of the tautological bundle from the Grassmannian, and the Kuznetsov component
\mathcal{K}u(X)
is again a
K3
category
[
30
]
.

In both cases, moduli space of Bridgeland-stable objects on the Kuznetsov components has been constructed, and proven to be isomorphic to hyperkähler varieties, providing Fano-hyperkähler pairs in a completely categorical way
[
37
,
4
,
51
,
17

While the Kuznetsov component of a general smooth cubic fourfold is expected to be non-geometric (i.e. not equivalent to the derived category of a
K3

nodal
cubic fourfold is geometric: inspired by the works
[
36
,
11

Let us start by briefly reviewing what is known for cubic fourfolds.

Derived categories of nodal cubic fourfolds

Consider a cubic hypersurface in
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
with a single node
y\in Y
, and its blowup
\pi:\widetilde{Y}\longrightarrow Y
in the nodal point. Call
E
the exceptional divisor, and
H
the pullback of the hyperplane class of
{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
. Then, by applying Kuznetsov’s result on categorical resolutions of singularities
[
28
, Theorem 1]
, there exists an admissible subcategory
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\subset D^{b}(\widetilde{Y})
such that one has a semiorthogonal decomposition

(1.1)

D^{b}(\widetilde{Y})=\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(2E),{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E),\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rangle,

and such that the functors
\pi_{*}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\longrightarrow D^{b}(Y)
and
\pi^{*}:{\mathcal{D}}^{\,\text{perf}}(Y)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
are an adjoint pair (respectively right and left adjoint). However, this decomposition can be refined to the following
[
36
]
:

(1.2)

D^{b}(\widetilde{Y})=\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(2E),{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E),\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y),{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{O}}(H),{\mathcal{O}}(2H)\rangle.

In light of Equation
1.1
,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y)
can be interpreted as the
categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component
. One can prove that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y)
is equivalent to the derived category of a
K3
surface
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
with the following argument
[
36
]
:

(1)

First,
\widetilde{Y}
is a divisor in the blowup
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{5}:=\operatorname{Bl}_{y}{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
, where the latter has a second contraction
q:\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{5}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
which is the projectivization of a vector bundle of rank two.

(2)

Call
h
the pullback of the hyperplane class from
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
: then, as divisor classes in
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{5}
, one sees that
\widetilde{Y}=3H-2E=2h+H
, i.e. the induced morphism
\widetilde{Y}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
is a blowup of
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
in the
K3
surface
Z
given by the vanishing of a section of
q_{*}{\mathcal{O}}(2h+H)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(2h)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(3h)
. Let us call
D
the exceptional divisor,
\bar{q}:=q|_{E}
and
j
the embedding of
D
in
\widetilde{Y}
.

(3)

By Orlov’s blowup formula
[
49
]
, one has a semiorthogonal decomposition:

(1.3)

D^{b}(\widetilde{Y})=\langle j_{*}\bar{q}^{*}D^{b}(Z)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(D),{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{O}}(h),{\mathcal{O}}(2h),{\mathcal{O}}(3h),{\mathcal{O}}(4h)\rangle.

Finally, comparing the decompositions
1.2
and
1.3
, one can prove that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y)\simeq D^{b}(Z)
by a sequence of mutations of the exceptional objects generating their semiorthogonal complements.

We proceed with the discussion of nodal Gushel-Mukai
n
-folds.

Main result

Let
X_{n}
be an ordinary GM
n
-fold with a single node
p\in X_{n}
. Then, the blowup
\widetilde{X}_{n}:=\operatorname{Bl}_{p}X_{n}
also admit a “nice” semiorthogonal decompositions in the spirit of
1.2
(see Proposition
3.2
):

(1.4)

D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})=\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{E}((n-2)E),\dots,{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(E),\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n}),{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee},\dots,{\mathcal{O}}((n-3)H),{\mathcal{U}}^{\vee}((n-3)H)\rangle.\rangle

The first goal of this paper is to give a geometric interpretation of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
3.7
). Let
X_{n}(3\leq n\leq 5)
be an ordinary Gushel-Mukai
n
-fold with a single node
p\in X_{n}
. Then the
categorical resolution

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})
is given by

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})\simeq D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{n}),

where
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{n})
is the derived category of coherent sheaves of modules on the even part of the Clifford algebra (see 2.4 in
[
35
]
), associated to a quadric fibration
R_{n}
over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of relative dimension
n-2
. In particular,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{4})\simeq D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{4})\simeq D^{b}(S,\alpha)
, where
S
is a degree two
K3
surface and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(S)
is a Brauer class.

The natural strategy to give a simple geometric interpretation to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})
, would be to adapt the approach of
[
36
]
, described above for cubic fourfolds, and attempt to generalize it to
X_{n}
. However, as we show in Section
2
, the second contraction of
\widetilde{X}_{n}
is not a blowup, but a small contraction to a singular
n
-fold
T_{n}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n+3}
. Hence, there is no obvious, natural semiorthogonal decomposition associated to such a contraction, and we have to rely on a more complex geometric picture.

In fact, a natural semiorthogonal decomposition can be found
up to a birational transformation
: by a modification of a construction from
[
32
, Section 2.3]
, we show that
\widetilde{X}_{n}
is birational to a quadric fibration
R_{n}
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. This map can be obtained by resolving the projection
G(2,5)\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{8}
from the node, and blowing up the image of
\widetilde{X}_{n}
in a singular divisor
(see
[
27
, Section 7.1]
for a description of this construction in dimension three, and
[
7
, Proof of Corollary 5.2]
for its four-dimensional analogue).
Such birational map is a relative Atiyah flop, and it induces a derived equivalence
D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})\simeq D^{b}(R_{n})
. As a quadric fibration,
R_{n}
admits a rather simple semiorthogonal decomposition
[
35
]
:

(1.5)

D^{b}(R_{n})=\langle D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{n}),q^{*}D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}),\dots,q^{*}D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}((n-3)h)\rangle

where
h
is the relative hyperplane class,
q:R_{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is the quadric bundle map, and
D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{n})
is the derived category of modules on the even part of the Clifford algebra associated to
R_{n}
. By comparing the latter decomposition with Equation
3.2
, and applying a sequence of mutations, we prove that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})\simeq D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{n})
.

Applications

We describe several applications of Theorem
1.3
, which allow to further characterize the categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component.

The relationship with Verra varieties

Verra threefolds and fourfolds are Fano varieties whose double quadric fibration structure produces a rich geometry. In particular, a Verra threefold is a
(2,2)
-section of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

[
57
]
, and a Verra fourfold is a double cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
branched in a Verra threefold
[
57
,
22
]
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we describe the relationship with Verra threefolds and fourfolds.

(1)

If
n=3
, then
\widetilde{X_{3}}
is birationally equivalent to a Verra threefold
V_{3}

[
13
]
. The latter has a natural semiorthogonal decomposition whose non-trivial component is also equivalent to the derived category
D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\prime})
of modules over some Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}_{3}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{3})
. Given a one-nodal GM threefold
X_{3}
, we prove that there is a Verra threefold
V_{3}
satisfying the following condition: there is a third quadric fibration
W
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
such that both
X_{3}
and
V_{3}
are obtained by
W
via the operation of hyperbolic reduction (see Section
4.1
and the sources therein for more about hyperbolic reductions). Finally, by a combination of Theorem
3.7
and a result of
[
1
]
, we conclude that
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{3})\simeq\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{3})
.

(2)

If
n=4
, then
\widetilde{X_{4}}
is birationally equivalent to a Verra fourfold
V_{4}
, whose nontrivial semiorthogonal component
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{4})
is also a derived category of a twisted
K3
surface of degree two. In this case, the fact that the Kuznetsov components are equivalent is a simple consequence of Theorem
3.7
and
[
7
, Corollary 4.13]
.

We summarize the discussions above into the following proposition:

Proposition 1.2
.

(Corollary
4.8
).
Let
X_{n}(3\leq n\leq 4)
be a one-nodal Gushel-Mukai
n
-fold, then the categorical resolution
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{n})
of the Kuznetsov components constructed in Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to Kuznetsov component
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{n})
of some Verra
n
-fold.

In a recent work
[
29
]
, Kuznetsov developed a new tool called
spinor modification
, to relate different conic bundles on the same base, without the need of constructing an explicit hyperbolic equivalence (i.e. a higher-dimensional quadric bundle which is related to both of them by the operation of quadric reduction). The even Clifford algebras of conic bundles related by a spinor modification are Morita equivalent. While it is known that hyperbolic-equivalent conic bundles are related by a spinor modification
[
29
, Corollary 1.3]
, the converse is only conjectural
[
29
, Conjecture 1.4]
: it would be intersting to construct an explicit, abstract spinor bundle on
R_{3}
which realizes
V_{3}
as a spinor modification, thus producing new evidence for the conjecture.

Duality of GM varieties of different dimensions

Kuznetsov and Perry prove that for
5\leq n\leq 6
, for a smooth GM
n
-fold,
X_{n}
, there is a smooth GM
n-2
-fold
X_{n-2}
such that
{\mathcal{K}}u(X_{n})\simeq{\mathcal{K}}u(X_{n-2})
. We extend this result for some nodal GM varieties, by replacing the Kuznetsov component with its categorical resolution. More precisely, we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorems
4.4
,
4.5
)
The following holds:

(1)

Choose two general hyperplane sections
s_{1},s_{2}
of
G(2,5)
passing through the node
p
. Then, in the space of quadric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{9}
which are nodal in
p
, there is a codimension two subspace
\mathcal{H}
such that for the general
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{H}
, the Gushel–Mukai fivefold
X_{5}:=G(2,5)\cap\mathcal{Q}
enjoys the following property: there is a nodal Gushel–Mukai threefold
X_{3}={\mathbb{V}}(s_{1},s_{2})\cap X_{5}
such that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{3})\simeq\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{5})
.

(2)

Choose three general hyperplane sections
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
of
G(2,5)
passing through
p
. Then, in the space of quadrics in
{\mathbb{P}}^{9}
nodal in
p
, there is a codimension two subspace
\mathcal{H}
such that for the general
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{H}
, the Gushel–Mukai fourfold
X_{4}:=G(2,5)\cap\mathcal{Q}\cap{\mathbb{V}}(s_{1})
enjoys the following property: there is a smooth
K3
surface of degree two
\widetilde{X}_{2}
, which is the blowup of
X_{2}={\mathbb{V}}(s_{2},s_{3})\cap X_{4}
in
p
, such that
D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{2})\simeq\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{4})
.

While the general one-nodal GM fivefold is rational, this is not true for fourfolds. However, the fourfolds we consider in point
(2)
of Theorem
1.3
are indeed rational, since they are birational to rational quadric fibrations, and this explains the vanishing of the Brauer class. Hence, Theorem
1.3
characterizes the categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component as “geometric”, i.e. equivalent to the derived category of coherent sheaves on a
K3
surface. This result confirms a GM analogue of the Kuznetsov’s rationality conjecture by allowing singularities, which predicts that a singular Gushel-Mukai fourfold is rational if and only if the Kuznetsov component admits a categorical resolution of singularities by an actual
K3
surface. We also note that Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
(2) are in the same spirit of the results obtained in
[
12
, Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.5]
, where the authors consider singular quartic double fivefolds.

X_{3}
. In
[

, the authors show that the Kuznetsov component

Theorem 1.4
.

(Theorem

).
Let
X_{3}
be a 1-nodal factorial Gushel-Mukai threefold, then the categorical resolution

General notation

3.
The categorical resolution of the Kuznetsov component

The description of

) lifts to a derived equivalence

[

]

To this purpose, we briefly recall the notion of
mutations
of a semiorthogonal decomposition. While we summarize the minimal technical notions to keep the exposition self-contained, we refer to
[
18
]
for a thorough explanation of the subject. Given a semiorthogonal decompositon
{\mathcal{T}}\subset\langle{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}\rangle
of a triangulated category
{\mathcal{T}}
, there are admissible subcategories
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
such that one has:

\langle{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}\rangle=\langle{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}}\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{B}},{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}\rangle.

These operations on semiorthogonal decompositions are called mutations. While we will not need the explicit description of
{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}
in full generality, let us recall what happens in the case in which
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are generated by a single object. Set
{\mathcal{A}}=\langle A\rangle
and
{\mathcal{B}}=\langle B\rangle
, where
A
and
B
are exceptional objects. Then, the corresponding mutations are also generated by a single exceptional object, which has the following expression:

\begin{split}{\mathbb{L}}_{A}B&=\operatorname{Cone}(A\otimes R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\longrightarrow B)\\
{\mathbb{R}}_{B}A&=\operatorname{Cone}(A\longrightarrow R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)^{\vee}\otimes B)[-1]\end{split}

Note that if
R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
is concentrated in degree zero, then one has:

(1)

If
A\otimes R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\longrightarrow B
is injective (surjective), then, up to shift,
{\mathbb{L}}_{A}B
is quasi-isomorphic to its cokernel (kernel)

(2)

If
A\longrightarrow R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)^{\vee}\otimes B
is injective (surjective), then, up to shift,
{\mathbb{R}}_{B}A
is quasi-isomorphic to its cokernel (kernel)

Moreover, if if
R^{\bullet}\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
is concentrated in degree one and has dimension one,
{\mathbb{L}}_{A}B
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{B}A
are quasi-isomorphic to the corresponding unique extension
C
fitting in a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow 0.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is an equivalence of triangulated categories:

q^{*}D^{b}(R_{n})\simeq\left[{\mathbb{R}}_{\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)}p^{*}D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})\right]\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-H)

Proof.

By Orlov’s blowup formula
[
49
]
we have the following semiorthogonal decompositions:

\begin{split}D^{b}({\mathcal{X}})&=\langle\iota_{*}\bar{p}^{*}D^{b}(S_{X})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F),p^{*}D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})\rangle\\
&=\langle\iota_{*}\bar{q}^{*}D^{b}(S_{R})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F),q^{*}D^{b}(R_{n})\rangle.\end{split}

Now recall that both
S_{X}
and
S_{R}
are
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundles over
\Gamma
: hence, by Orlov’s projective bundle formula
[

]
we obtain the following:

(3.1)

\begin{split}D^{b}({\mathcal{X}})&=\langle\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F),\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F+H),p^{*}D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})\rangle\\
&=\langle\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F),\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F+\xi),q^{*}D^{b}(R_{n})\rangle\end{split}

where

can be chosen as any line bundle on

which restricts to
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
on the fibers of

. Then, we can recover the equivalence by a sequence of mutations, which will be outlined here.

Let us first apply the Serre functor to the first block of the first decomposition of Equation

. We obtain the following:

Then, we mutate the central piece one step to the right, and we twist everything by
{\mathcal{O}}(-H)

\begin{split}D^{b}({\mathcal{X}})=&\langle\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F),\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(F-H-K_{\mathcal{X}}),\\
&\hskip 70.0pt{\mathcal{O}}(-H)\otimes{\mathbb{R}}_{\iota_{*}\pi^{*}D^{b}(\Gamma)\otimes p^{*}\omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{\vee}}p^{*}D^{b}(\widetilde{X}_{n})\rangle.\end{split}

Observe that

, hence, to conclude, we just need to show that

up to powers of

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

4.
Nodal GM varieties and hyperbolic reduction

Kuznetsov and Perry have conjectured the existence of a relation between Kuznetsov components of Gushel–Mukai varieties of different dimensions
[
30
, Conjecture 3.7 and the following discussion]
. Such conjecture has been proven later by the same authors, in the smooth case, with the tool of categorical joins. In particular, one has the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

[
31
, Theorem 1.6]

Let
Y_{n}
be a smooth Gushel–Mukai variety of dimension
n
, with
5\leq n\leq 6
. Then there is a smooth Gushel–Mukai
n-2
-fold
Y_{n-2}
such that
{\mathcal{K}}u(Y_{n})\simeq{\mathcal{K}}u(Y_{n-2})
.

In the following, as an application of Theorem
3.7
, we extend Theorem
4.1
to the case of one-nodal ordinary Gushel–Mukai varieties, with a codimension two condition on the choice of the nodal quadric.

4.1.
Hyperbolic reduction in families

Hyperbolic reduction is a classical construction, let us summarize it here. Let
Q\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
be a quadric hypersurface and
x\in Q
. Consider the affine tangent bundle
\widetilde{T}
described by the following short exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\longrightarrow\widetilde{T}\longrightarrow T(-1)\longrightarrow 0

where
T
is the tangent bundle. Call
{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{T}_{x})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
the
embedded tangent space
(the projectivization of the fiber over
x
of the affine tangent bundle). Then, the projection
\pi_{x}:{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
maps
Q\setminus x
to a quadric
Q_{x}
, isomorphic to
Q\cap{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{T}_{x})
. The hyperbolic reduction
\widetilde{Q}
of
Q
with respect to
x
is a quadric of dimension
n-3
obtained as the projection of
Q_{x}
from its vertex. If
Q
is smooth,
\widetilde{Q}
is smooth as well.

This construction has been described in families as follows (see
[
1
]
for more details, and a more general construction): let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a globally generated vector bundle of rank
n
over a smooth projective base
S
, call
p:{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee})\longrightarrow S
the associated projective bundle map. Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be the line bundle restricting to
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
to each fiber, and such that
p_{*}{\mathcal{L}}\simeq{\mathcal{E}}
. A quadric fibration
R
over
S
, of relative dimension
n-2
, will be the zero locus of a section
\sigma\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}),{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2})
. Clearly, one can visualize
\sigma
as a symmetric morphism
{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}
, which we will still denote by
\sigma
.

Now, we say that a subbundle

is
\sigma
-
isotropic
if
\sigma|_{\mathcal{F}}=0
. Let us take a
\sigma
-isotropic line bundle

. Then, the hyperbolic reduction associated to
R
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is given by the vanishing of the restriction of
\sigma
to
{\mathcal{F}}^{\perp}/{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. a quadric fibration of relative dimension
n-4
. It is a known fact that the operation of hyperbolic reduction in families preserves the discriminant divisor in
S

[
1
]
.

Let us specialize this picture to our setting. Consider the vector bundle

on

where

.

On the other hand, for

, one has that
R_{n}
is the zero locus of an element of

is a rank

vector bundle defined by the following exact sequences (see the proof of Lemma

(4.1)

\begin{split}&0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{4}\longrightarrow 0\hskip 40.0pt\text{(fourfold case)}\\

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{3})\simeq D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{3})
, where the right-hand sides are the derived categories of modules over the even part of the Clifford algebras associated, respectively, to

and
R_{3}
. But since

is a hyperbolic reduction of

, one has
D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{5})\simeq D^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{3})
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

A similar result, relating GM varieties of dimension four and two, can be proven in exactly the same way, due to Proposition

Theorem 4.5
.

(Theorem

Choose three general hyperplane sections

of

of degree two, which is the blowup of

.

While rationality is known in the case of fivefolds, it is not obvious for fourfolds. However, choosing the quadric

.

Let

Proof.

Since

admits a hyperbolic reduction, a birational map to

4.2.
The relationship with Verra threefolds and fourfolds

A Verra threefold
V_{3}
is a
(2,2)
-section of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
(see
[
57
]
). A general smooth Verra threefold admits two conic fibrations over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
with discriminant divisor given by smooth sextic curves, and therefore it admits a semiorthogonal decomposition as follows:

D^{b}(V_{3})=\langle{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{3}),{\mathcal{O}}(-1),{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{O}}(1)\rangle

where
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{3})
is the “Clifford component” and
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
is the hyperplane bundle of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Similarly, Verra fourfolds are double covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
branched over a Verra threefold and they also admit two quadric fibrations over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
branched along a sextic curve
[
22
]
. Hence, for a Verra fourfold
V_{4}
, we can define
{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{4})
by its structure as a quadric fibration as for threefolds.

In this section, we illustrate how GM threefolds satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
4.4
are related to Verra threefolds by the fact that the corresponding fibration
R^{3}
is a hyperbolic reduction of a quadric fibration
W\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, which also admits a hyperbolic reduction to a Verra threefold. The same result for fourfolds has been obtained by
[
7
]
. A natural consequence of these results is an equivalence of the corresponding Kuznetsov components.

Proposition 4.7
.

Take a general, nodal GM threefold
X_{3}
, and call
R_{3}
the image of
X_{3}
through the flop of Diagram
2.3
. Then the following holds:

(1)

There is a quadric fibration
W\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, and a Verra threefold
V_{3}
, such that both
R_{3}
and
V_{3}
are hyperbolic reductions of
W
.

(2)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(X_{3})\simeq{\mathcal{K}}u(V_{3})
.

Proof.

Let us recall that
R_{3}
is the vanishing locus of a section of
{\mathcal{O}}(2h)
on the projectivization of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{3}^{\vee}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{3}=T_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}(-\zeta)^{\oplus 2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(\zeta)/{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus 2}
(see Section
4
).

In light of
[
7
, Proposition 3.6]
, let us construct a short exact sequence which resolves
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{3}
by split bundles. One has the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}}(-\zeta)^{\oplus 2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus 2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus 6}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(\zeta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus 4}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(\zeta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}}^{\oplus 2}}

Corollary 4.8
.

(Proposition

)
Let

be a one-nodal Gushel–Mukai variety of dimension three or four. Then there is a Verra variety

of the same dimension such that

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition

and

]
for threefolds, and

Remark 4.9
.

As the geometric resolution of a rational nodal Gushel-Mukai fourfold considered in Theorem

is birationally equivalent to a Verra fourfold
V
, thus
V
is rational.
As a result, Theorem

5.
Categorical Torelli theorem for nodal Gushel-Mukai threefolds

The subject of the
Categorical Torelli problem
is a developing area that has attracted a lot of attention. It asks if the Kuznetsov component of a Fano variety characterizes its (birational) isomorphism class. Many cases, including smooth del Pezzo threefolds, Gushel-Mukai varieties, Enriques surfaces, hypersurfaces in (weighted) projective spaces, have been proved (see
[
6
,
25
,
41
,
39
,
53
,
16
]
). Recently, the study of categorical Torelli problems has been extended to singular Fano varieties. In particular, in
[
15
]
, the authors show the categorical data of a smooth subcategory inside the Kuznetsov component of a 1-nodal non-factorial Fano threefolds characterizes its isomorphism class. For nodal factorial Fano threefolds, instead of using the smooth subcategory, which is smaller than the Kuznetsov component, we use the larger one, i.e. the
Categorical resolution of singularities
of it.

For a 1-nodal cubic threefold
Y_{3}
, a categorical resolution
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y_{3})
contains a semi-orthogonal component as the derived category
D^{b}(\Gamma_{4})
of a smooth curve
\Gamma_{4}
of genus four in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, and the complement is an exceptional object(See
[
42
, Proposition 1.33]
). Note that the geometric resolution
\widetilde{Y_{3}}\cong\mathrm{Bl}_{\Gamma_{4}}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. This means that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}u}(Y_{3})
characterizes the isomorphism class of the 1-nodal cubic threefold
Y_{3}
by
[
20
, Section 5.1, Remark 5.1]
. In the case of 1-nodal Gushel-Mukai threefolds

considered in our article, as explained in Theorem
3.7
, a categorical resolution

is given by the category

. As a corollary, we show

determines the birational class of
X_{3}
. More precisely,

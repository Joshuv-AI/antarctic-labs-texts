Ground states of classical spin polygons: Rigorous results and examples
W Florek, H-J Schmidt, K Jaśniewicz-Pacer

Abstract
We present a comprehensive and rigorous analysis of the lowest energy configurations (LECs) of classical spin polygons characterized by arbitrary couplings between neighboring spin sites. Our study shows that these ground states exhibit either collinear or coplanar arrangements, which allows us to determine the precise boundaries between these two phases. By simultaneously applying a spin flip and a bond inversion, we simplify the LEC problem and reduce it to a specific scenario with predominantly ferromagnetic (FM) bonds and a single antiferromagnetic (AFM) bond. Hence, competing interactions are always present, but, nevertheless, in the well-defined ranges of the system parameters the collinear LEC is realized. The difference angles between neighboring spins within the LEC can be captured by a single Lagrange parameter. We analytically investigate its dependence on the AFM bond and arrive at revealing results. Similarly, we can analyze the energy of the LEC, which shows a pronounced maximum as a function of AFM bond. To illustrate our findings, we give various examples that clearly demonstrate these results.



Keywords
: classical spin systems, competing interactions,
phase diagram

2
Classical spin polygons

The systems considered in this work will be referred to as “polygons”.
In graph-theoretical terms
[
38
]
the system is assumed to be a
cycle graph, i. e., a connected graph such that all vertices have exactly two neighbours.
It is thus possible to label the
N>2
vertices by the numbers
\mu=0,1,\ldots,N-1

such that the two neighbours of the vertex
\mu
have the numbers
\mu-1
and
\mu+1
,
understood
\bmod\,N
. This labelling is only unique up to cyclic permutations
and the inversion
(0,1,\ldots,N-1)\mapsto(N-1,N-2,\ldots,0)
.
Later, we will make use of this freedom to choose the labelling.

Moreover, the vertices are carriers of classical
spins
\bi{s}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\bi{s}_{\mu}|=1
for all
0\leq\mu<N
. With
the assumed labelling scheme all expressions with these indices are
understood
\bmod\,N
, unless otherwise stated. The vectors
\bi{s}_{\mu}
can be interpreted
as rows of the
N\times 3
-dimensional matrix
\mathbf{S}
, where an
element
S_{\mu\,j}
,
j=1,2,3
, is the
j
th component of a vector
\bi{s}_{\mu}
.

There are
N
edges (bonds)
e_{\mu}:=(\mu,\mu+1)
with weights (exchange
integrals)
\alpha_{\mu}\neq 0
, such that the energy can be written as

E(\mathbf{S})=\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{\mu}\,\bi{s}_{\mu}\cdot\bi{s}_{\mu+1}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}E_{\mu},

(1)

where
E_{\mu}
denotes the energy of a bond
e_{\mu}
. The LEC is a spin
configuration globally minimizing the energy
E(\mathbf{S})
. Let for chosen
0\leq\mu<N

all vectors
\bi{s}_{\nu}
,
0\leq\nu<N
and
\nu\neq\mu
, kept fixed then this
particular spin
\bi{s}_{\mu}
has to minimize the one-spin energy

\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(\mathbf{S})=\bi{s}_{\mu}\cdot(\alpha_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu-1}+\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu+1})=E_{\mu-1}+E_{\mu}.

(2)

Hence this vector has to satisfy the so-called stationary state equation

[
39
,
40
]

-\kappa_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu}=\alpha_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu-1}+\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu+1}.

(3)

Alternatively, the
\kappa_{\mu}
can be regarded as Lagrange parameters, which
result from the minimization of the energy under the constraints

|\bi{s}_{\mu}|=1
. Note the relation

\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(\mathbf{S})=-\kappa_{\mu}
which follows by
taking the scalar product of (
3
) with
\bi{s}_{\mu}
.
It is assumed that all couplings

\alpha_{\mu}\neq 0
, since otherwise the spin polygon degenerates into one or more
spin chains which have trivial collinear LECs. This is the only restriction, so in
the general case this system has no spatial symmetry.

The simple structure of magnetic interactions in the model considered allows to
prove the following basic theorem.

Theorem 1

Assume a classical spin system of
N
spin vectors
\bi{s}_{\mu}
, with

|\bi{s}_{\mu}|=1
for all
0\leq\mu<N
, such that each spin
\bi{s}_{\mu}
is only
coupled to spins
\bi{s}_{\mu\pm 1}
with non-zero exchange integrals

\alpha_{\mu}
and
\alpha_{\mu-1}
, respectively, and the periodic boundary
conditions
N+\mu\equiv\mu
are adopted. Then the lowest energy configuration of
this system is either collinear or coplanar.

Proof.
The stationary state equation (
3
) can be
rewritten as

\alpha_{\mu-2}\bi{s}_{\mu-2}+\kappa_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu-1}+\alpha_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu}=\mathbf{0},

so any three consecutive spins constitute a set of linearly dependent non-zero
vectors, therefore they are either collinear or coplanar. It follows from this
formula that

\bi{s}_{\mu}=-(\alpha_{\mu-2}\bi{s}_{\mu-2}+\kappa_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu-1})/\alpha_{\mu-1}.

(4)

Case (i)
:
\bi{s}_{0}
and
\bi{s}_{1}
are collinear. In this
case
\bi{s}_{1}=\pm\bi{s}_{0}
. Hence,

\bi{s}_{2}=-(\alpha_{0}\pm\kappa_{1})\bi{s}_{0}/\alpha_{1}
is also collinear with

\bi{s}_{0}
. If for any
2<\mu<N
spins
\bi{s}_{\mu-2}
and
\bi{s}_{\mu-1}

are collinear with
\bi{s}_{0}
, then, by (
4
), also
\bi{s}_{\mu}
is
collinear with
\bi{s}_{0}
. Therefore, by induction over
\mu
, all spins

\bi{s}_{\mu}
,
0<\mu<N
, are collinear with
\bi{s}_{0}
, thus the lowest energy
configuration is collinear.

Case (ii)
:
\bi{s}_{0}
and
\bi{s}_{1}
are coplanar. Let
P

denote the two-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
spanned by

(\bi{s}_{0},\bi{s}_{1})
. The formula (
4
) ensures that
\bi{s}_{2}\in P
.
Generally, if
\bi{s}_{\mu-2}
and
\bi{s}_{\mu-1}\in P
, then also

\bi{s}_{\mu}\in P
. Therefore, by induction over
\mu
, all spins

\bi{s}_{\mu}\in P
,
1<\mu<N
, thus the lowest energy configuration is coplanar.

\square

If considerations are restricted to the LECs then, according to this Theorem,
all vectors
\bi{s}_{\mu}
can be assumed to lie in a two-dimensional subspace

P\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and written in the form
\bi{s}_{\mu}=(\cos\phi_{\mu},\sin\phi_{\mu})
,
where the angles
-\pi<\phi_{\mu}\leq\pi
are measured with respect to an arbitrarily
chosen direction
{\mathbf{e}}\in P
. The degeneracy of the LEC with respect
to planar rotations can be removed by another representation characterized by a sequence

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}=(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N-1})
of difference angles between successive spins
with
\psi_{\mu}:=\phi_{\mu+1}-\phi_{\mu}
.
This representation will be independent of
{\mathbf{e}}
.
It follows that the difference angles lie in the larger interval

-2\pi<\psi_{\mu}\leq 2\pi,\qquad{\rm for\;all}\quad 0\leq\mu<N\;,

(5)

and that their “telescope sum” vanishes

\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=0\;.

(6)

As in the proof of theorem
1
we can argue that

\psi_{\mu}=\pm n\pi
for
n=0,1,2
for some
0\leq\mu<N

implies that the LEC is collinear. Hence the following holds

\psi_{\mu}\neq\pm n\pi\quad{\rm for}\;n=0,1,2\;{\rm and\;all}\;0\leq\mu<N\;{\rm if\;the\;LEC\;is\;coplanar}.

(7)

By adding or subtracting
2\pi
, if necessary, we may re-define the
\psi_{\mu}

such that they lie in the smaller interval

-\pi<\psi_{\mu}\leq\pi\qquad{\rm for\;all}\quad 0\leq\mu<N\;.

(8)

However, this has the side effect that the sum of the re-defined
\psi_{\mu}

no longer vanishes but satisfies

\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=2k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}\;.

(9)

Analogously to (
7
) we may state that

\psi_{\mu}\neq 0,\pi\;{\rm for\;all}\;0\leq\mu<N\;{\rm if\;the\;LEC\;is\;coplanar}.

(10)

After eliminating
\psi_{0}
by means of

\psi_{0}=2k\pi-\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=:2k\pi-\Psi

(11)

the energy of a system in question can be written as a function of a sequence

\boldsymbol{\Psi}=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots\,\psi_{N-1})
in the following way

E(\mathbf{S})\equiv E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})=\alpha_{0}\cos\Psi+\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\alpha_{\mu}\cos\psi_{\mu}.

(12)

Since
\cos
is an even function it is evident that after a
global
transformation

\psi_{\mu}\mapsto-\psi_{\mu}
for
1\leq\mu<N
, so also
\Psi\mapsto-\Psi
,
the energy does not change, i. e.,
E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})=E(-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
. This
transformation can be achieved by an inversion of the labelling of the spins and
is different from the other global transformation

\bi{s}_{\mu}\mapsto-\bi{s}_{\mu}
,
0\leq\mu<N
, under which the angles
\psi_{\mu}

remain constant
\bmod\,2\pi
. We will use the freedom of
choosing the labelling of the spins to fix the sign of
\sin\Psi
as non-negative:

\sin\Psi\geq 0\;.

(13)

This choice will greatly simplify the following considerations.

3
General properties of the LEC

Figure 1:

Illustration of a spin flip operation
\bi{s}_{\mu}\mapsto-\bi{s}_{\mu},\;\mu=\lambda+1,\ldots,\nu

accompanied by a sign reversion of the pair of bonds
\alpha_{\mu}\mapsto-\alpha_{\mu},\;\mu=\lambda,\nu

such that
\lambda\neq\nu
.
By this operation the energy (
1
) is left unchanged.

By flipping a sequence of spins

\bi{s}_{\mu}
,
\mu=\lambda+1,\lambda+2,\ldots,\nu-1,\nu
,
and reversing the signs of the exchange integrals
\alpha_{\lambda}
and

\alpha_{\nu}
the energy of a polygon is left unchanged
(cf.
[
29
,
41
,
42
,
43
]
, see Figure
1
.
By repeating this operation every even
number of antiferromagnetic bonds can be transformed into ferromagnetic ones.
Thus there are two cases depending on whether the original polygon has
an even or an odd number of antiferromagnetic bonds
N_{\rm AFM}
.

In the first case a polygon without competing interactions and hence
an even
N_{\rm AFM}
can be transformed into a system
in which all couplings are ferromagnetic (FM), i.e.,
\alpha_{\mu}<0
for all

0\leq\mu<N
. Such system has the LEC energy

E_{\rm LEC}^{\rm FM}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}\alpha_{\mu}=-\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|

(14)

and all bonds are satisfied with
E_{\mu}=-|\alpha_{\mu}|
.
The ground state of the FM polygon is the fully aligned
state
\uparrow\ldots\uparrow
. By reversing the spin flip
transformation we obtain a collinear ground state with the
structure
\uparrow\downarrow
at the AF bonds.
Thus the LEC of these polygons is well understood and we can
exclude them from our subsequent considerations.

Hence, in the following parts of the present work we will only
consider the second class of polygons with competing interactions
and hence an odd
N_{\rm AFM}
such that

E_{\rm LEC}>-\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|\;.

(15)

This means that at least one bond is not satisfied, i. e.,
E_{\mu}>-|\alpha_{\mu}|
for at
least one
0\leq\mu<N
. In this case, by the above mentioned procedure
of flipping spins and reversing signs of the
appropriate bonds the polygon can be transformed to a cycle
with only one AFM bond. Using the freedom of performing
a cyclic permutation of the indices when labelling the spins,
this unique AFM bond is chosen as
\alpha_{0}>0
, such that the other bonds are
ferromagnetic, i. e.,

\alpha_{\mu}<0,\qquad{\rm for\;\;all}\quad 0<\mu<N.

(16)

It is also assumed that, except for section
6
, these values are fixed,
whereas
\alpha_{0}
is varying and all quantities discussed are considered as
functions of this variable.

Obviously,
\boldsymbol{\Psi}\mapsto E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})
is a smooth function from the

N-1
-dimensional torus
T^{N-1}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Since
T^{N-1}
is compact,
E
assumes its
absolute

minimum at some point
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
. Then the following necessary conditions for
a
local
minimum are satisfied:

\left.\frac{\partial E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})}{\partial\psi_{\mu}}\right|_{\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}}=0,\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad 0<\mu<N.

(17)

Applying this to (
12
) the following equalities are obtained

\frac{\partial E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})}{\partial\psi_{\mu}}=-(\alpha_{0}\,\sin\Psi+\alpha_{\mu}\,\sin\psi_{\mu})=-\alpha_{0}\,\sin\Psi+|\alpha_{\mu}|\,\sin\psi_{\mu}=0,

(18)

for all
0<\mu<N
. Hence we have proven the following:

Proposition 1

For every LEC the quantity

\beta:=\alpha_{0}\,\sin\Psi=\left|\alpha_{\mu}\right|\,\sin\psi_{\mu}

(19)

will be independent of
\mu
for all
0<\mu<N
.

Note that, according to this Proposition,
\alpha_{0}>0
and
\sin\Psi\geq 0

by (
13
) imply
\beta\geq 0

and, further,
\sin\psi_{\mu}\geq 0
for all
0<\mu<N
.
Hence, by virtue of (
8
), we conclude

0\leq\psi_{\mu}\leq\pi\qquad{\rm for\;\;all}\quad 0<\mu<N.

(20)

Alternatively,
\beta
can be understood as the Lagrange parameter of the
minimization problem for
E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0})
subject to the constraint (
9
).
Hence we will refer to
\beta
simply as ‘the Lagrange parameter’ in what
follows.

In order to get an intuitive idea of
\beta
we may consider the
following mechanical model of the spin polygon. The
\bi{s}_{\mu}
are viewed as
the positions of
N
particles constrained to a unit circle and (
1
)
is the potential energy of this mechanical system. Hence one can define the force

\bi{f}_{\mu}=-\nabla_{\bi{s}_{\mu}}E=-\alpha_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu-1}-\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu+1}\;,

(21)

upon the
\mu
-th particle and the corresponding torque

\bi{n}_{\mu}=\bi{s}_{\mu}\times\bi{f}_{\mu}=-\alpha_{\mu-1}\bi{s}_{\mu}\times\bi{s}_{\mu-1}-\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu}\times\bi{s}_{\mu+1}\;.

(22)

For an LEC this torque
\bi{n}_{\mu}
must vanish for all
0\leq\mu<N

and hence the absolute value of

-\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu}\times\bi{s}_{\mu+1}
must be constant throughout the
spin polygon. The latter can be written in the form

\left|-\alpha_{\mu}\bi{s}_{\mu}\times\bi{s}_{\mu+1}\right|=\left|\alpha_{\mu}\sin\psi_{\mu}\right|=|\beta|\;.

(23)

Therefore, according to this mechanical model, the Lagrange parameter
\beta\geq 0

can be understood as the absolute value of the constant torque which the
\mu+1
-th
particle exerts on the
\mu
-th one in the LEC.

The total domain
0<\alpha_{0}<\infty
is divided into two intervals,

(0,|\alpha_{\tau}|)
and
(|\alpha_{\tau}|,\infty)
, where the threshold is defined
as

|\alpha_{\tau}|:=\min_{0<\mu<N}|\alpha_{\mu}|.

(24)

The limit point
\alpha_{0}=|\alpha_{\tau}|
has to be treated separately,
see
B
. As the notation suggests, this minimum will be attained by one
or several coupling constants of which we have selected an arbitrary one

|\alpha_{\tau}|
with
0<\tau<N
.

The first interval is referred to below as the
regular domain
and the
second one as the
complementary domain
. This subdivision is not
motivated by physics, but by method: Most results are easiest to prove if you
restrict yourself to the regular domain and they can then be transferred to the
complementary domain using a spin-flip mapping.

It is relatively simple to determine the LEC among all collinear states,
but these depend on the domain in which
\alpha_{0}
lies. We
define two special collinear states that are used throughout the paper:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}:

\displaystyle\;{\rm all}\;\;\psi_{\mu}=0\quad{\rm for}\;\;0\leq\mu<N,

(25)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}:

\displaystyle\;{\rm all}\;\;\psi_{\mu}=0\quad{\rm for}\;\;0<\mu<N,\,\mu\neq\tau,\quad{\rm and}\;\;\psi_{\tau}=-\psi_{0}=\pi.

(26)

Here we have also fixed the values of
\psi_{0}
subject to the conventions
chosen below. Note that the definition (
26
) possibly depends on
the choice of
\tau
if there are several FM coupling constants of minimal
strength.

Proposition 2

1.

For
0<\alpha_{0}<|\alpha_{\tau}|
the state
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}

has a lower energy than any other collinear state.

2.

For
|\alpha_{\tau}|<\alpha_{0}<\infty
the state

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}
has a lower energy than any other
collinear state (except those states
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}

resulting from a different choice of
\tau
).

3.

For
\alpha_{0}=|\alpha_{\tau}|
the energy of

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}
and of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}

coincides and is lower than the energy of any other collinear state (except
those states
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}
resulting from a different
choice of
\tau
).

Proof.
The state
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}
satisfies
all bonds except the AFM bond
e_{0}=(0,1)
. Hence the only collinear state which
possibly assumes a lower energy will be a state satisfying all bonds, including

e_{0}=(0,1)
, except another FM bond
e_{\mu}=(\mu,\mu+1)
. Further,
|\alpha_{\mu}|

has to be minimal, which means
\mu=\tau
(taking into account that
\tau
need
not be unique). This implies that only
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}
is
a possible second candidate for an LEC among the collinear states. For the
energies we obtain

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|,

(27)

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow})

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{0}-\sum_{\mu=1,\,\mu\neq\tau}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|+|\alpha_{\tau}|,

(28)

whence

E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow})-E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow})=2(\alpha_{0}-|\alpha_{\tau}|),

(29)

from which the remaining claims of the proposition follow.

\square

4
Regular domain:
0<\alpha_{0}<|\alpha_{\tau}|

In the regular domain there are two prominent values of
\alpha_{0}
:
A critical point
\alpha_{\rm(c)}
which marks a continuous phase
transition between the collinear and the coplanar ground state phase, and
a point
\alpha_{\rm(em)}
where the energy, written as a function
of
\alpha_{0}
, assumes its maximum. Before we look at the theory behind this,
a few preparations are necessary.

First consider the following

Proposition 3

1.

Every coplanar LEC
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N-1})
satisfies

\Psi+\psi_{0}=0,

(30)

i.e., the integer
k
in (
9
) assumes the value
k=0
. Moreover,

0<\Psi<\pi.

(31)

2.

In the regular domain every LEC
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{N-1})

satisfies

0\leq\psi_{\mu}\leq\pi/2,\qquad{\rm for\;\;all}\quad 0<\mu<N.

(32)

The critical value
\alpha_{\rm(c)}
mentioned above lies in the regular
domain, what is stated in the following

Theorem 2

Assume a classical spin systems as described in Theorem
1
with all but
one couplings ferromagnetic, i.e.,
\alpha_{\mu}<0
for
0<\mu<N
and

8
Summary

The problem of finding all ground states of the
N
-spin polygon depends on an
initially confusing set of
N
parameters, the exchange integrals

\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N-1}
. A certain simplification is achieved by
a repeated spin flip operation, which reduces the problem to the special case

\alpha_{0}>0
,
\alpha_{1}<0,\ldots,\alpha_{N-1}<0
. An important intermediate
result states that all ground states are either collinear or coplanar. This
allows the ground states to be parameterized by
N
difference angles, which can
be written as functions of a single Lagrange parameter
\beta
.

Our investigations suggest that the AFM coupling
\alpha_{0}
and the weakest FM
coupling
\alpha_{\tau}
should be considered as variables, while the remaining FM
bonds are regarded as fixed. The resulting two-dimensional phase diagrams are all
qualitatively similar with the structure shown as an example in figure
3
.

First, there are two separate phases with the collinear LEC

\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}

which prevail for small
\alpha_{0}
and
\left|\alpha_{\tau}\right|
, respectively.
There is a large region of coplanar LEC in between, so that the phase boundaries
are symmetrical with respect to the reflection at the diagonal

|\alpha_{\tau}|=\alpha_{0}
and can be analytically calculated. They approach the
straight line
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{\rm(d)}
(
|\alpha_{\tau}|=\alpha_{\rm(d)}
,
respectively), asymptotically, where the characteristic value
\alpha_{\rm(d)}

can be calculated in terms of the remaining FM bonds. It has to be emphasized
that competing interactions are present in the system considered for all

\alpha_{0}>0
. Nevertheless, collinear LECs, characteristic for non-frustrated
systems, are observed. In both cases, i.e., for
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}

and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\downarrow}
, a single bond is not satsified, whereas
in the coplanar LEC we have
E_{\mu}>-|\alpha_{\mu}|
for all
0\leq\mu<N
, cf. (
15
), and hence no bond is satisfied.

Other significant quantities are the energy
E(\alpha_{0})
, the Lagrange
parameter
\beta(\alpha_{0})
and the difference angle
\Psi(\alpha_{0})

between the spins at sites
\mu=0
and
\mu=1
, each considered as a function
of
\alpha_{0}
. The energy
E(\alpha_{0})
is linear in the collinear phases and
has a maximum as its only extreme value. With
\beta(\alpha_{0})
, the maximum
only results for the case
0<|\alpha_{\tau}|<\alpha_{\rm(d^{\prime})}
, where the second
characteristic value
\alpha_{\rm(d^{\prime})}
is again a function of the remaining
FM bonds. These remarks complete the general description of the LECs for spin polygons
depending on the
N
-exchange integrals
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N-1}
.

Let us add that our theory can be directly applied to certain other spin systems
composed of polygons and spin chains. These systems can be characterized as “trees”
in the graph-theoretic sense, whose vertices are given by polygons
(of possibly different sizes including
N=1
)
connected by edges that are given by finite spin chains.
These spin chains can also have zero length, in which case two polygons are
glued together in such a way that they have exactly one common spin site.
Due to their rotational degeneracy, the local ground states in the polygons
of such systems can always be extended to a global ground state.

A.1
Proposition
3
(i)

Recall that

E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})=\alpha_{0}\,\cos\Psi-\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|\,\cos\psi_{\mu}\;,

(46bx)

see (
12
),
and that
\psi_{0}\neq 0
for any coplanar LEC,
see (
10
).
First we consider the

•

Case
-\pi<\psi_{0}<0
:

Since
\psi_{0}>-\pi
and
\psi_{\mu}\geq 0
for all
0<\mu<N
, see (
20
),
we have, according to (
9
),

-\pi<\sum_{\mu=0}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=\Psi+\psi_{0}=2k\pi\;,

(46by)

which means that
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
can only take the values
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.
In order to derive a contradiction we assume that

\Psi=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=2\,k\,\pi-\psi_{0}=2\,k\,\pi+\left|\psi_{0}\right|,\quad{\rm for}\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad{\rm and}\quad k\geq 1.

(46bz)

Upon defining

\gamma:=\frac{\left|\psi_{0}\right|}{2\,k\,\pi+\left|\psi_{0}\right|},\quad{\rm such\,\,that}\quad 0<\gamma<1,

(46ca)

we obtain

\widetilde{\Psi}:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\left(\gamma\,\psi_{\mu}\right)=\gamma\,\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{PsiContra},\,\ref{defgamma})}}{{=}}\left|\psi_{0}\right|\;.

(46cb)

The new spin configuration

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=(\gamma\psi_{1},\ldots,\gamma\psi_{N-1})
has the energy

E\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}\right)=\alpha_{0}\cos\widetilde{\Psi}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|\cos(\gamma\psi_{\mu})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{PsiTilde})}}{{=}}\alpha_{0}\cos\Psi-\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|\cos(\gamma\psi_{\mu})<E(\boldsymbol{\Psi}),

(46cc)

using in the last inequality that
\cos(\gamma\psi_{\mu})>\cos\psi_{\mu}
for all
0<\mu<N

due to
0<\psi_{\mu}\leq\pi
, see (
20
) and (
10
).
This contradicts
the assumption that
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
is an LEC. We conclude that
k\geq 1
in
(
46bz
) cannot hold, so
k=0
. With this information we rewrite (
46bz
) as

\Psi=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=\left|\psi_{0}\right|=-\psi_{0},

(46cd)

which proves (
30
). Finally, (
31
) follows from (
46cd
),
and
-\pi<\psi_{0}<0
.

Next we consider the

•

Case
0<\psi_{0}<\pi
:

This case will not occur but cannot be excluded from the outset.
In order to derive a contradiction we assume that

\Psi=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=2\,k\,\pi-\psi_{0},\quad{\rm for}\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad{\rm and}\quad k\geq 1\;,

(46ce)

and define

\gamma:=\frac{\psi_{0}}{2\,k\,\pi-\psi_{0}}\;.

(46cf)

Due to
\psi_{0}<\pi\leq 2k\pi
the denominator of (

) is positive
and hence
\gamma>0
. Moreover, the implications

\psi_{0}<\pi\leq k\pi\quad\Rightarrow\quad 2\psi_{0}<2k\pi\quad\Rightarrow\quad\psi_{0}<2k\pi-\psi_{0}

show that
\gamma<1

proceed as for the case

\Psi=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}=-\psi_{0}\;.

(46ch)

But this implies
-\pi<\Psi<0
and hence
\sin\Psi<0
in contradiction
to (
13

(i).

A.2
Proposition
3
(ii)

In case of a collinear LEC we can apply proposition
2
and
conclude that, in the regular domain, all
\psi_{\mu}=0
for
0<\mu<N
such that
(
32
) is satisfied. Therefore, we can turn to the case of coplanar LEC.

In order to derive a contradiction, assume that for some
0<\lambda<N
we have

\psi_{\lambda}=\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta,\quad{\rm for\;\;some}\quad 0<\delta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

(46ci)

We consider a modified spin configuration
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
by setting

\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta
and leaving the other angles
unchanged. The energies of both configurations are given by

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\,\cos\Psi-\sum_{\mu=1,\,\mu\neq\lambda}^{N-1}\left|\alpha_{\mu}\right|\,\cos\psi_{\mu}+\left|\alpha_{\lambda}\right|\,\sin\delta,

(46cj)

\displaystyle E(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\,\cos\left(\Psi-2\delta\right)-\sum_{\mu=1,\,\mu\neq\lambda}^{N-1}\left|\alpha_{\mu}\right|\,\cos\psi_{\mu}-\left|\alpha_{\lambda}\right|\,\sin\delta.

(46ck)

Their difference satisfies

\displaystyle E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})-E(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\left(\cos\Psi-\cos(\Psi-2\delta)\right)+2|\alpha_{\lambda}|\,\sin\delta

(46cl)

\displaystyle=2\sin\delta\left(|\alpha_{\lambda}|-\alpha_{0}\,\sin(\Psi-\delta)\right)>0,

|\alpha_{\lambda}|\geq|\alpha_{\tau}|>\alpha_{0}>\alpha_{0}\,\sin(\Psi-\delta).

In (

\cos\alpha-\cos\beta=-2\sin\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\,\sin\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\ \;,

(46cm)

which, after substituting

yields

-2\sin\frac{2\Psi-2\delta}{2}\,\sin\frac{2\delta}{2}=2\sin(\delta-\Psi)\,\sin\delta\;.

(46cn)

Summarizing,
E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})>E(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
and hence
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
cannot be an LEC,
contrary to the assumption. This completes the proof of the proposition
3
.

A.3
Theorem
2

Let
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
be an LEC. At
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
the conditions
(
17
) have to be satisfied and, additionally,

H(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})\geq 0,

(46co)

where
H
denotes the
(N-1)
-dimensional real, symmetric Hessian matrix of

E(\boldsymbol{\Psi})
, with entries

H_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial^{2}E({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})}{\partial\psi_{\mu}\partial\psi_{\nu}},\quad 0<\mu,\nu<N.

(46cp)

The condition
H\geq 0
means that all eigenvalues of
H
are non-negative, and
hence
\det H\geq 0
. By evaluating (
46cp
) we obtain

H_{\mu\nu}=|\alpha_{\mu}|\,\cos\psi_{\mu}\,\delta_{\mu\nu}-\alpha_{0}\,\cos\Psi,

(46cq)

for all
0<\mu<N
. For later purposes, we write
H
in the form

H=A+\bi{u}\,\bi{v}^{\sf T},

(46cr)

where
A
is the diagonal matrix with entries

A_{\mu\mu}=|\alpha_{\mu}|\,\cos\psi_{\mu}
,
\bi{u}
a column vector
with constant entries
\sqrt{\alpha_{0}\,|\cos\Psi|}
and

\bi{v}=-\mathop{\operator@font sgn}\nolimits(\cos\Psi)\,\bi{u}
.

1.
Only-if-part

We assume that the LEC
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
is collinear, i.e.,

\sin\widehat{\Psi}=\sin\widehat{\psi}_{\mu}=0

(46cs)

for all
0<\mu<N
. By proposition
2
we conclude

\widehat{\psi}_{\mu}=\widehat{\Psi}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\widehat{\psi}_{\nu}=0,

(46ct)

for all
0<\mu<N
. It follows that the diagonal matrix
A
in (
46cr
)
satisfies
A_{\mu\mu}=\left|\alpha_{\mu}\right|>0
for all
0<\mu<N
and is
hence invertible. Therefore, by the matrix determinant lemma
[
45
]
,

\det H=\left(1+\bi{u}^{\sf T}\,A^{-1}\,\bi{v}\right)\,\det A,

(46cu)

which at
\boldsymbol{\Psi}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
reduces to

\det H\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}\right)=\left(1-\alpha_{0}\,\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|^{-1}\right)\,\prod_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|,

(46cv)

using (
46ct
). The condition
\det H(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Psi}})\geq 0
, see
(
46co
), then implies

0<\alpha_{0}\leq\left(\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\alpha_{\rm(c)}.

(46cw)

Note, further, that
|\alpha_{\mu}|<|\alpha_{\tau}|
for all
0<\mu<N
implies

\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}|\alpha_{\mu}|^{-1}>1/|\alpha_{\tau}|
and hence

\alpha_{\rm(c)}<|\alpha_{\tau}|
, which concludes the only-if-part of the proof.

2.
If-part

We have to prove that any LEC is collinear for

0<\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha_{\rm(c)}
. It is straightforward to show that the
collinear state
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{\uparrow\ldots\uparrow}
given by
\psi_{\mu}=0
for
all
0<\mu<N
satisfies (
17
) and (
46co
), and is hence
a plausible candidate for an LEC (note that these conditions are only
necessary). It remains to show that there is no coplanar state also
satisfying (
17
) and (
46co
).

In order to derive a contradiction we assume that
\boldsymbol{\Psi}
is such a state.
Due to (
31
) it satisfies

0<\Psi<\pi.

(46cx)

By virtue of (
17
) and (
18
) we have

\sin\psi_{\mu}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\alpha_{\mu}|}\,\sin\Psi,

(46cy)

for all
0<\mu<N
. By means of proposition
3
it holds that

0<\psi_{\mu}\leq\pi/2,\qquad{\rm for\;\;all}\quad 0<\mu<N,

(46cz)

where the first strict inequality is due to the LEC being coplanar, see (
10
).
In this case
the function
\arcsin
is well-defined for all arguments
\psi_{\mu}
and we may
write

\Psi=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\psi_{\mu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{sinpsi})}}{{=}}\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\arcsin\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\alpha_{\mu}|}\,\sin\Psi\right)=:f(\Psi),

(46da)

where

is a smooth function

satisfying

. After some calculations we obtain

\frac{\rmd f}{\rmd\Psi}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\alpha_{\mu}|}\cos\Psi\left(1-\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\alpha_{\mu}|}\sin\Psi\right)^{2}\right)^{-1/2},

(46db)

and

\frac{\rmd^{2}f}{\rmd\Psi^{2}}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{0}\,\sin\Psi}{\left(\alpha_{\mu}^{2}-\alpha_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}\Psi\right)^{3/2}}\,\left(\alpha_{0}^{2}-\alpha_{\mu}^{2}\right).

(46dc)

It follows that

\left.\frac{\rmd f}{\rmd\Psi}\right|_{\Psi=0}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N-1}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\alpha_{\mu}|}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{\rm(c)}}\leq 1.

(46dd)

Moreover,

\frac{\rmd^{2}f}{\rmd\Psi^{2}}<0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad 0<\Psi<\pi,

(46de)

due to the terms

is a strictly concave function passing
through the origin and having there a slope less than or equal to 1. This
implies

for all

and hence (

satisfied. This completes the proof of the if-part and thereby of the theorem

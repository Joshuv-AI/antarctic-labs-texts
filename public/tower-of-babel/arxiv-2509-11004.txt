Abstract
In this paper, we investigate metric subregularity of multifunctions between Asplund spaces. Using Mordukhovich normal cones and coderivatives, we introduce the limiting Basic Constraint Qualification (BCQ) associated with a given multifunction. This BCQ provides necessary dual conditions for the metric subregularity of multifunctions in the Asplund space setting. Furthermore, we establish characterizations of Asplund spaces in terms of the limiting BCQ condition implied by metric subregularity. By employing Fréchet normal cones and coderivatives, we derive necessary dual conditions for metric subregularity expressed as fuzzy inclusions, and we also obtain characterizations of Asplund spaces via these fuzzy inclusions. As an application, we examine metric subregularity of the conic inequality defined by a vector-valued function and a closed (not necessarily convex) cone with a nontrivial recession cone. By using Mordukhovich and Fréchet subdifferentials relative to the given cone, we establish necessary dual conditions for the metric subregularity of such inequalities in Asplund spaces. The results based on Mordukhovich subdifferentials characterize Asplund spaces, while those based on Fréchet subdifferentials yield necessary or sufficient conditions for Asplund spaces. These conditions recover, as special cases, the known error-bound results for inequalities defined by extended-real-valued functions on Asplund spaces. Overall, this work highlights that the validity of necessary conditions formulated via normal cones and subdifferentials for error bounds of convex or nonconvex inequalities depends crucially on the Asplund property of the underlying space.

Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to investigate the metric subregularity of multifunctions between Asplund spaces and to derive necessary dual conditions using coderivatives and normal cones. Based on these results, we establish new characterizations of Asplund spaces in terms of such dual conditions.

As highlighted in Ioffe’s monograph
Ioffe2017
, the concept of metric subregularity—introduced as “regularity at a point” in
Ioffe2000
and later popularized under this name by Dontchev and Rockafellar
DonRoc2004
—has emerged as one of the most fundamental notions in variational analysis and optimization.

Given a multifunction
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
between Banach spaces
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}
, recall from
DonRoc2004
that
F
is said to be
metrically subregular
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F):=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}:v\in F(u)\}
if there exists
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
such that

{\bf d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\tau\,{\bf d}(\bar{y},F(x))\quad\text{for all $x$ close to $\bar{x}$}.

(1.1)

This property provides an upper bound for the distance from a point
x
to the solution set
F^{-1}(\bar{y}):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\bar{y}\in F(x)\}
.

The study of metric subregularity originates from the celebrated Lyusternik-Graves theorem
Lyusternik1934
;
Graves1950
. Since then, it has been extensively developed in numerous monographs, including
DonRoc09
;
Mordukhovich
;
Ioffe2017
;
Pen13
;
thibault
, and investigated under various names (see, e.g.,
Aze06
;
DL
;
HenrionJourani2002-SIAM
;
HenrionJouraniOutrata2002-SIAM
;
HenrionOutrata2004-MP
;
Ioffe2000
;
IoffeOutrata2008-SVA
;
ZhengNg2010-SIAM
;
ZhengNg2012-NA
).

As observed by Henrion and Outrata
HenOut2005
,
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if its inverse
M:=F^{-1}
is
calm
at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
; that is, there exists
\tau>0
such that

{\bf d}(x,M(\bar{y}))\leq\tau\,\|y-\bar{y}\|\quad\text{for all $x$ close to $\bar{x}$}.

(1.2)

The first fundamental finite-dimensional result of type (
1.1
) is due to Hoffman
Hoffman1952
, who proved that for a linear system
Ax\leq b
, the distance from any point
x
to the solution set is bounded above by a constant times the norm of the residual
\max\{Ax-b,0\}
.

Given a proper lower semicontinuous function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, define
F(x):=[f(x),+\infty)
for
x\in\mathbb{X}
and let

\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{f}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:f(x)\leq 0\}.

Then the metric subregularity of
F
at
(\bar{x},0)
reduces to the error bound inequality

{\bf d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{f})\leq\tau f_{+}(x)\quad\text{for all $x$ close to $\bar{x}$},

(1.3)

where
f_{+}(x):=\max\{f(x),0\}
. Such Hoffman-type estimates, now widely known as
error bounds
, form an essential part of modern optimization theory and have been extensively studied (see, e.g.,
AC1988
;
BD2
;
ioffe-JAMS-1
;
ioffe-JAMS-2
;
K2
;
Pen13
;
Robinson1975
;
WTY2024-SVVA
).

It is worth noting that Lewis and Pang
LewisPang1998
studied dual necessary conditions, expressed in terms of normal cones and subdifferentials, for the error bounds of convex inequality systems. In particular, they proved that the basic constraint qualification (
\mathbf{BCQ}
) for a convex inequality constitutes a necessary condition.

For a continuous convex function
f
defined on
\mathbb{X}
, recall that the convex inequality
f(x)\leq 0
is said to satisfy
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
x\in\mathop{\rm bd\,}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
(the boundary of
\mathbf{S}_{f}
) if

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{f},x)=[0,+\infty)\partial f(x),

(1.4)

where
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{f},x)
and
\partial f(x)
denote, respectively, the normal cone and the subdifferential in convex analysis. The condition
\mathbf{BCQ}
is fundamental in convex analysis and closely related to important notions in optimization and approximation, such as the Slater condition, the strong conical hull intersection property (strong CHIP), the Abadie constraint qualification (ACQ), and KKT optimality conditions (see, e.g.,
H-UL1993
;
De
;
Li
;
LNS2000
;
LNP2008
;
FLN2010
).

Zheng and Ng
ZN2004
extended
\mathbf{BCQ}
to general convex inequalities using singular subdifferentials, and later
WY
further generalized it to nonconvex settings via Clarke and Fréchet subdifferentials.
Further, Zheng and Ng
ZN2007
generalized the
\mathbf{BCQ}
to the convex multifunctions case and used it to provide dual characterizations in terms of normal cones and coderivatives for the metric subregularity of convex multifunctions.

In this paper, we introduce a limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
for closed multifunctions, formulated in terms of Mordukhovich normal cones and coderivatives. Our main result establishes that this limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
is a necessary condition for the metric subregularity of multifunctions between Asplund spaces. Moreover, we obtain characterizations of Asplund spaces based on
\mathbf{BCQ}
conditions implied by metric subregularity (see
Theorem

3.1
).

Using Fréchet normal cones and coderivatives, we also show that metric subregularity of closed multifunctions between Asplund spaces implies certain fuzzy inclusions, which yield further characterizations of Asplund spaces (see
Theorem

3.2
).

As an application, we study the metric subregularity of a conic inequality defined by a vector-valued function and a closed (not necessarily convex) cone with nontrivial recession cone. By employing Mordukhovich and Fréchet subdifferentials relative to the cone, we show that exact and fuzzy inclusions are, respectively, implied by metric subregularity in Asplund spaces.

The validity of exact inclusions leads to characterizations of Asplund spaces, whereas the fuzzy inclusions yield necessary or sufficient conditions for Asplund spaces (see
Theorems

4.1
and
4.3
).

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we present definitions and preliminary
results used throughout the paper. Section 3 is devoted to the main results on metric subregularity of closed multifunctions between Asplund spaces and some new characterizations for Asplund spaces. As an application, the metric subregularity of conic inequalities is studied in section 4. The conclusions of this paper is presented in section 5.

Notation, definitions and background material

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Banach spaces with duals

and

to denote the norm in

\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}
and
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}
the closed unit ball and the sphere of
\mathbb{X}^{*}
, respectively. For any

and

, we denote by

the open ball with center

and radius

.

For a set

, we write
\overline{A}
for its closure,
\mathrm{int}(A)
for its interior, and
\mathrm{conv}(A)
for its convex hull. The distance from a point
x\in\mathbb{X}
to a set
A
is

\mathbf{d}(x,A):=\inf_{a\in A}\|x-a\|,

with the convention
\mathbf{d}(x,\emptyset):=+\infty
.

Given a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{X}
(not necessarily convex) the recession cone of
S
is defined to be the set

S^{\infty}:=\{u\in\mathbb{X}:s+tu\in S,\forall s\in S,\forall t\geq 0\}.

When
S
is convex, it is known from
Zalinescu
that

S^{\infty}=\bigcap_{t>0}t(S-s)

for each
s\in S
.

Given a multifunction
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
between
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
, we denote by

{{\rm gph}(F)}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}\;:\;y\in F(x)\},

the
graph
of
F
. Its domain is
{\rm dom}(F):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:F(x)\neq\emptyset\}
,
and its inverse is defined by

F^{-1}(y):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:y\in F(x)\},\ \forall y\in\mathbb{Y}.

Given a multifunction
\Phi:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}^{*}
between
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{X}^{*}
,
the
sequential Painlevé–Kuratowski outer (upper) limit
of
\Phi
at
x
is defined by

\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{y\to x}\Phi(y):=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}\exists\,(x_{n})\to x,\ \exists\,(x_{n}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x^{*}\ \text{with}\\
x_{n}^{*}\in\Phi(x_{n})\ \text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\end{array}\right\}.

Normal cones.

For a closed set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{X}
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
:

•

The
Fréchet normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is

\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x}):=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}\;:\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where
x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
with
x\in\Omega
.

•

For
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the set of
\varepsilon
-normals to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,\bar{x}):={\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\limsup\limits_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq\varepsilon\right\}}.

When
\varepsilon=0
,
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{0}(\bar{x},\Omega)
coincides with
{\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})}
.

•

The
Mordukhovich (limiting) normal cone
is

{{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x}):=\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x},\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\varepsilon}{(\Omega,x)}}.

(2.1)

Thus,
x^{*}\in{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})
if and only if there exists a sequence
\{(\varepsilon_{k},x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\}
in
(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+},x_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{*}
and
x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\varepsilon}{(\Omega,x_{k})}
for all
n
.

It is known from
C
and
Mordukhovich
that

{\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})\subseteq{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})}.

If
{\Omega}
is convex, Fréchet and limiting normal cones coincide and reduce to the normal cone in the sense of convex analysis; that is

{\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})={\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})=\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\;\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq 0\;\;{\rm for\ all}\ x\in\Omega\}}.

Asplund spaces.

Recall that a Banach space is called an Asplund space if every continuous convex function on
\mathbb{X}
is Fréchet differentiable at each point of a dense subset of
\mathbb{X}
(see
Phelps
for definitions and their equivalences). It is known that a Banach space is an Asplund space if and only if every separable closed subspace has a separable dual. In particular, every reflexive space is an Asplund space. For the case when
\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space, Mordukhovich and Shao
MS
have proved that

{{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})=\mathop{\rm Limsup}_{x\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,x)}.

This means that
x^{*}\in{{\bf N}(\Omega,\bar{x})}
if and only if there exist
x_{n}{\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}
and
x^{*}_{n}\xrightarrow{w^{*}}x^{*}
such that
x_{n}^{*}\in{\widehat{\bf N}(\Omega,x_{n})}
for all
n
.

Subdifferentials.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous function. We denote by

{\rm epi}(\varphi):=\{(u,r)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}:\varphi(u)\leq r\}

the
epigraph
of
\varphi
. For
x\in\mathrm{dom}(\varphi):=\{y\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi(y)<+\infty\}
, we denote by
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x)
and
\partial\varphi(x)
the
Fréchet
and
limiting
(Mordukhovich) subdifferentials of
\varphi
at
x
and they are given by

\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x):=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:(x^{*},-1)\in\widehat{\bf N}\big(\mathrm{epi}(\varphi),(x,\varphi(x))\big)\right\},

\partial\varphi(x):=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:(x^{*},-1)\in{\bf N}\big(\mathrm{epi}(\varphi),(x,\varphi(x))\big)\right\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x)=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)-\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0\right\}.

When
\mathbb{X}

\partial\varphi(x)=\mathop{\mathrm{Limsup}}_{y\xrightarrow{\varphi}x}\,\widehat{\partial}\varphi(y),

Equivalently,

x_{n}\xrightarrow{\varphi}x

x_{n}^{*}\xrightarrow{w^{*}}x^{*}
with
x_{n}^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x_{n})
for all
n

3
Metric Subregularity and
\mathbf{BCQ}
of Multifunctions in Asplund Spaces

In this section, we investigate metric subregularity and the
\mathbf{BCQ}
property in terms of limiting normal cones and coderivatives for closed multifunctions in Asplund spaces. We also establish an implication between these two properties, which in turn leads to characterizations of Asplund spaces.

We start by recalling the definition of metric subregularity for a multifunction. Recall that
F
is said to be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
, if there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that

{\bf d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\tau{\bf d}(\bar{y},F(x)),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r).

(3.1)

Given two Banach spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
, let
\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
denote the set of all multifunctions
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
with closed graph; that is,
{\rm gph}(F)
is closed in
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

When
F
is convex, i.e.,
{\rm gph}(F)
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
, Zheng and Ng
ZN2007
introduced the following concept.

Definition 1
(Zheng and Ng
ZN2007

\mathbf{BCQ}
for convex multifunctions)

F
is said to satisfy the
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if

\mathbf{N}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x}\big)=D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\mathbb{Y}^{*}).

(3.2)

In the convex case, noting that
{\rm gph}(F)
is a convex subset, the inclusion

D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\mathbb{Y}^{*})\subseteq\mathbf{N}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x}\big)

always holds, and so (
3.2
) is equivalent to

\mathbf{N}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x}\big)\subseteq D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\mathbb{Y}^{*}).

Motivated by this observation, we extend the
\mathbf{BCQ}
to the general (nonconvex) setting using limiting normal cones and coderivatives.

Definition 2
(Limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
)

Let
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
.
We say that
F
satisfies the
limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if

\mathbf{N}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x}\big)\subseteq D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(\mathbb{Y}^{*}).

(3.3)

We will show that for closed multifunctions between Asplund spaces, metric subregularity implies the limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
, and that this implication can be used to characterize Asplund spaces.
Before proceeding, we present the following lemma, which is of independent interest.

Lemma 6

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space,
\mathbb{Y}:=\mathbb{X}^{m}
be equipped with the
\ell^{1}
-norm, and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}
be closed subsets of
\mathbb{X}
with nonempty intersection.
Define
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
by

F(x):=(x-A_{1})\times\cdots\times(x-A_{m}),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{X}.

Let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
with
\bar{y}=(\bar{y}(1),\dots,\bar{y}(m))
. Then:

(i)

We have

{\rm dom}(\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}))\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(A_{1},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(1))\times\cdots\times\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(A_{m},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(m)),

and for all
y^{*}=(y^{*}(1),\dots,y^{*}(m))
in
{\rm dom}(\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
,

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:x^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i)\right\}.

(ii)

We have

{{\rm dom}(D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}))\subseteq\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(1))\times\cdots\times\mathbf{N}(A_{m},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(m))},

(3.4)

and for all such
y^{*}=(y^{*}(1),\dots,y^{*}(m))
in
{\rm dom}({D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
,

{D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})\subseteq\left\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:x^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i)\right\}}.

(3.5)

Moreover, this inclusion becomes an equality when
\mathbb{X}
is Asplund.

Proof

It suffices to prove (ii). Let
y^{*}=(y^{*}(1),\cdots,y^{*}(m))\in{\rm dom}(D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}))
. Take any
x^{*}\in D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(y^{*})
. Then

(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(F),(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

and there exist
\epsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
,
(x_{k},y_{k})\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
(x_{k}^{*},y_{k}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(x^{*},y^{*})

Thus, for any

, one has

\limsup_{(x,y)\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(x_{k},y_{k})}\frac{\langle(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*}),(x-x_{k},y-y_{k})\rangle}{\|x-x_{k}\|+\|y-y_{k}\|}\leq\epsilon_{k}.

(3.6)

For (
3.4
), it suffices to prove that
y^{*}(j)\in\mathbf{N}(A_{j},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(j))
for all
j
.

Let
j\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
be fixed. Note that
(x_{k},y_{k})\in{\rm gph}(F)
and thus
x_{k}-y_{k}(j)\in A_{j}
. Then for any

with

, take

Then

as

and so (

This implies that

and consequently
y^{*}(j)\in\mathbf{N}(A_{j},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(j))
(thanks to
x_{k}-y_{k}(j)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{j}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}-\bar{y}(j)
and
(y_{k}^{*}(j))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}y^{*}(j)
).

We next prove (

). Let
b\in{\bf B}_{\mathbb{X}}
. For any
t>0
sufficiently small, take

x:=x_{k}+tb,y:=(y_{k}(1)+tb,\cdots,y_{k}(m)+tb).

Then one can verify that
(x,y)\in{\rm gph}(F)
and
(x,y)\rightarrow(x_{k},y_{k})
as

. This and (
3.6
) imply that

\langle x_{k}^{*}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{k}^{*}(i),b\rangle\leq(m+1)\epsilon_{k}.

Taking limits as
k\rightarrow\infty
, one has

\langle x^{*}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i),b\rangle\leq 0.

This means that
x^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i)
due to the arbitrariness of
b\in{\bf B}_{\mathbb{X}}
and so (
3.5
) holds.

Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is Asplund. It remains to prove “
\supseteq
” in (
3.5
).

Let
x^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i)
. Let
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
be fixed. Then
y^{*}(i)\in\mathbf{N}(A_{i},\bar{x}-\bar{y}(i))
and thus there exist
u_{k}(i)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}-\bar{y}(i)
and
y^{*}_{k}(i)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}y^{*}(i)

for all
k
. For any
k
, let
v_{k}:=(\bar{x}-u_{k}(1),\cdots,\bar{x}-u_{k}(m))
. Then one can verify that
v_{k}\rightarrow\bar{y}
and
v_{k}\in F(\bar{x})
due to

. Let
y_{k}^{*}:=(y^{*}_{k}(1),\cdots,y^{*}_{k}(m))
and
x_{k}^{*}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}y^{*}(i)
. Then
(x_{k}^{*},y_{k}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}(x^{*},y^{*})
.
Note that for any
(x,y)\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(\bar{x},v_{k})
, one has

\displaystyle\langle x_{k}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle+\langle-y_{k}^{*},y-v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{m}y_{k}^{*}(i),x-\bar{x}\Big\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle-y_{k}^{*}(i),y(i)-(\bar{x}-u_{k}(i))\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle y_{k}^{*}(i),x-\bar{x}-y(i)+(\bar{x}-u_{k}(i))\rangle

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle y_{k}^{*}(i),x-y(i)-u_{k}(i)\rangle

This means that

and thus

thanks to

and
(x_{k}^{*},y_{k}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}(x^{*},y^{*})
.
The proof is complete.

The following theorem establishes characterizations of Asplund spaces in terms of metric subregularity and limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
of multifunctions.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space.

(ii)

For every Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
and every
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
being metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)

\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that
F
satisfies the limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
at all
(x,\bar{y})
with

(iii)

For every Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
and every
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)

\mathbf{BCQ}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})

Proof

(i)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(ii): Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space. Let
\mathbb{Y}
be an Asplund space and
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
. Then there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds.

For any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
, let
\|(x,y)\|_{\tau}:=\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\|x\|+\|y\|
. Then
(\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y},\|\cdot\|_{\tau})
is an Asplund space and the unit ball of its

(\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}})\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
. Let
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
. We claim that

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\tau(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((x,y),{\rm gph}(F))+\|y-\bar{y}\|),\ \ \forall(x,y)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\times\mathbb{Y},

(3.7)

where
\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((x,y),{\rm gph}(F)):=\inf\{\|(x,y)-(u,v)\|_{\tau}:(u,v)\in{\rm gph}(F)\}

Suppose on the contrary that there exists
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\times\mathbb{Y}
such

\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))>\tau(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((x_{0},y_{0}),{\rm gph}(F))+\|y_{0}-\bar{y}\|).

This implies that there exists
u\in\mathbb{X}
such that

\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))>\tau(\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\|u-x_{0}\|+\mathbf{d}(y_{0},F(u))+\|y_{0}-\bar{y}\|.

Thus,

\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))>\|u-x_{0}\|+\tau\mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(u)).

Since

\|u-\bar{x}\|\leq\|u-x_{0}\|+\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\|<\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))+\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\|\leq 2\|x_{0}-\bar{x}\|<r,

then (
3.1
) gives that

\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))>\|u-x_{0}\|+\mathbf{d}(u,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\geq\mathbf{d}(x_{0},F^{-1}(\bar{y})),

which is a contradiction. Hence (
3.7
) holds.

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
and
x^{*}\in\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)
. Then there exists a sequence
\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}
in
F^{-1}(\bar{y})\times\mathbb{X}^{*}
such that

x_{n}\rightarrow x,\ x_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{*}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ x_{n}^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x_{n}),\ \forall n.

Applying the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, there is
M>0
such that
\|x_{n}^{*}\|\leq M
for all
n
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}:=\frac{x_{n}^{*}}{M}
and then
(
Mordukhovich
, Corollary 1.96)
gives that

\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x_{n})\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}=\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))(x_{n}).

We claim that

(\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},0)\in\widehat{\partial}(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))+\phi)(x_{n},\bar{y})

(3.8)

where
\phi(u,v):=\|v-\bar{y}\|
for any
(u,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

Indeed, for any

\varepsilon>0
there exits
\delta_{n}\in(0,\delta-\|x-\bar{x}\|)
such that

\langle\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}},u-x_{n}\rangle\leq\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))+\tau\varepsilon\|u-x_{n}\|,\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x_{n},\delta_{n}).

This and (
3.7
) imply that for any
(u,v)\in\mathbf{B}(x_{n},\delta_{n})\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},\delta_{n})
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}},u-x_{n}\rangle\leq\tau(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((u,v),{\rm gph}(F))+\|v-\bar{y}\|)+\tau\varepsilon\|u-x_{n}\|

and thus

\langle\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},u-x_{n}\rangle\leq\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((u,v),{\rm gph}(F))+\phi(u,v)+\varepsilon\|u-x_{n}\|.

This implies that (
3.8
) holds.

Since
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
is an Asplund space, by virtue of
Lemma

1
, there exist
(u_{n},v_{n}),(w_{n},z_{n})\in\mathbf{B}(x_{n},\frac{1}{n})\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},\frac{1}{n})

\displaystyle(\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},0)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})+\widehat{\partial}\phi(w_{n},z_{n})+\frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})+\{0\}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}+\frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

Then there exists
(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})
and

(3.9)

For
(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})
,

3
gives the existence of
(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}})\in{\rm gph}(F)

\|(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}})-(u_{n},v_{n})\|<\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(({u_{n}},{v_{n}}),{\rm gph}(F))+\frac{1}{n}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|(\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}},\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}})-(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\|<\frac{1}{n}.

(3.10)

This and (

(\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},0){\in(\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}},\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}})}+(0,b_{n}^{*})+\frac{2}{n}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

(3.11)

Note that

(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})\subseteq\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}

and
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}},\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
are weak
∗
-sequentially compact (as

are Asplund spaces), and thus without loss of generality we can assume that

(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}(u^{*},v^{*})\ \ {\rm and}\ \ b_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}b^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}

(taking subsequence if necessary). This and (
3.11
) imply that
(u^{*},v^{*})\in{\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(F),(x,\bar{y}))}
(thanks to
(u_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(x,\bar{y})
). By taking the limit (with respect to the weak
∗
-topology) as

in (

), one has

(\frac{x^{*}}{M\tau},0)=(u^{*},v^{*})+(0,b^{*}),

and consequently

This means that
F
satisfies the limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
{(x,\bar{y})}
.

Note that (ii)
\;\Rightarrow\;
(iii) follows immediately, and it suffices to prove that (iii)
\;\Rightarrow\;
(i).

Suppose on the contrary that
\mathbb{X}
is not Asplund. Following
MW2000
;
FM1998
, we can represent
\mathbb{X}
in the form
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
with the norm
\|(z,\alpha)\|:=\|z\|+|\alpha|
for any
x=(z,\alpha)\in\mathbb{X}
. Then
\mathbb{Z}
is not an Asplund space. By virtue of
(
DGZ1993
, Theorem 1.5.3)
(also
(
FM1998
, Theorem 2.1)
), there exists an equivalent norm
|||\cdot|||
on
\mathbb{Y}
and

such that

\frac{1}{2}\|z\|\leq|||z|||\leq\|z\|\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{|||z+h|||+|||z-h|||-2|||z|||}{\|h\|}>\gamma,\ \forall z\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.12)

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as
\varphi(z):=-|||z|||,\forall z\in\mathbb{Z}
and

A_{1}:=\{0_{\mathbb{Z}}\}\times(-\infty,0],A_{2}:=\mathop{\rm epi\,}(\varphi)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \bar{x}:=0_{\mathbb{X}}.

be equipped with the

\mathbb{Y}
is not the Asplund space. Define

as follows:

\bar{y}:=(0_{\mathbb{X}},0_{\mathbb{X}})
. Then

and

. We first show that

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq 2(\mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(x)))

(3.13)

holds for all

, which means that
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Indeed, for any
x=(z,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}}

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))=\|z\|+|\alpha|\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(x))=\mathbf{d}(x,A_{1})+\mathbf{d}(x,A_{2}).

(3.14)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then (
3.13
) holds since
\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))=\mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(x)))
.

If
-|||z|||\leq\alpha<0
, then (
3.12
) gives that

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))=\|z\|+|\alpha|\leq 2\|z\|=2\mathbf{d}(\bar{y},F(x))

3.13
) holds.

If
\alpha<-|||z|||

\displaystyle-|||z|||-\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big||||z|||-|||u|||\big|+|\alpha-\lambda|

\displaystyle|||z-u|||+|\alpha-\lambda|

where the last inequality follows from (

We next prove that

{\bf N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\}.

{\lambda_{k}}\leq 0

(z_{k},0)\in{\widehat{\bf N}(A_{2},(z_{k},\alpha_{k}))}

Since

(3.16)

Note that

\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x})={\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\times\mathbb{R},\ \ \mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})={\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\times[0,+\infty)\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\}.

(3.17)

This and (

Further, we show that metric subregularity may lead to “fuzzy” inclusions expressed in terms of Fréchet coderivatives and normal cones.
Moreover, such inclusions can be employed to obtain characterizations of Asplund spaces.

Theorem 3.2

(ii)

\mathbb{Y}

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x})\subseteq\bigcup\left\{\widehat{D}^{*}F(u,v)(\mathbb{Y}^{*}):(u,v)\in\mathbf{B}((\bar{x},\bar{y}),\epsilon)\cap{\rm gph}(F)\right\}+\varepsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

(3.20)

Proof

(i)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(ii): Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space. Let
\mathbb{Y}
be an Asplund space and
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)

\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)

3.1
) holds. By defining
\|(x,y)\|_{\tau}:=\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\|x\|+\|y\|
for any

3.7
) holds with
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Take any

\frac{x^{*}}{\|x^{*}\|}\in\widehat{\bf N}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}=\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))(x).

Using the proof of (

(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau\|x^{*}\|},0)\in\widehat{\partial}(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))+\phi)(x,\bar{y}).

\phi(u,v):=\|v-\bar{y}\|

Choose any
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,\epsilon)

{2\tau\|x^{*}\|\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon}.

Note that
subtransversality
of finitely many closed sets is a well-known and important concept in mathematical programming and approximation theory. Given a collection of finitely many closed sets
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
in
\mathbb{X}
, recall that
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
is said to be
subtransversal
at
\bar{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
, if there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that

{\bf d}\Big(x,\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\Big)\leq\tau\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\bf d}(x,A_{i})\ \ \forall x\in{\bf B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.27)

It is known from
(
Ioffe2017
, 7.1.3 Comments)
that inequality (
3.27
) was in reality introduced by Dolecki (see
Dolecki1982
) in a very different context,
and consequently this property has been extensively studied by many authors under various different names; e.g., “metric qualification condition” in
Ioffe1989
;
IP1996
, “linear coherence” in
(
Pen13
, Theorem 4.7.5)
, “linear regularity” in
BB1993
;
BB1996
, “metric inequality” in
NT2001
and so on.

It is noted that subtransversality is closely related with metric subregularity. Let
\mathbb{X}^{m}
be equipped with
\ell_{1}
norm. We consider the multifunction
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{X}^{m}
defined by

F(x):=(A_{1}-x)\times\cdots\times(A_{m}-x)\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

Then it is easy to verify that the subtransversality property as sain in (
3.27
) is equivalent to metric subregularity of
F
at
(\bar{x},0_{\mathbb{X}^{m}})
. It is known from
NT2001
;
WTY2024-SVVA
that subtransverality of finitely many closed sets could imply the strong limiting CHIP (expressed in terms of limiting normal cones) in the Asplund space and such implications, if to consider all collections of finitely many closed sets, are able to characterize Asplund spaces. Further, based on
(
NT2001
, Theorem 3.8)
and Theorem 3.1, the following theorem is to prove an equivalence result between subtransverality of finitely many closed sets and metric subregularity of multifunctions in the sense of deriving necessary dual conditions in terms of limiting normal cones; that is,

Theorem 3.3

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. The following statements are equivalent:

(ii)

For every nonempty closed sets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
in
\mathbb{X}
being subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
,
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}\}
has the strong limiting CHIP at
\bar{x}
(i.e.,
N(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i},\bar{x})\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N(A_{i},\bar{x})
);

(ii)

For every Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
and every
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
being metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
,
F
satisfies the limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Similarly, by virtue of
(
WTY2024-SVVA
, Theorem 3.2)
and
Theorem

3.2
, the next thereom also provides an equivalence result on subtransversality and metric subregularity when dealing with necessary dual conditions for these properties via Fréchet normal cones.

Theorem 3.4

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For every nonempty closed sets
A_{1},\cdots,A_{m}
in
\mathbb{X}
being subtransversal at
\bar{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}

(ii)

For every Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
and every

being metrically subregular at

for all
\varepsilon>0

In the remainder of this section, unless stated otherwise, we always assume that

We note that

provides necessary conditions for the metric subregularity of closed multifunctions in terms of Fréchet normal cones and coderivatives in Asplund spaces. In fact, a sharper necessary condition for metric subregularity can be stated as follows.

Theorem 3.5

Let
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be such that
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
. Then there exist constants
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that for any
\epsilon>0
, one has

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\subseteq\left\{\tau\widehat{D}^{*}F(u,v)\big((1+\epsilon)\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big):(u,v)\in\mathbf{B}((x,\bar{y}),\epsilon)\cap{\rm gph}(F)\right\}+\epsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}

(3.28)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
.

Proof.

By metric subregularity, there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds. Define a norm on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
by

\|(x,y)\|_{\tau}:=\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\|x\|+\|y\|,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}.

Then (
3.7
) holds with
\delta:=r/2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Take any
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
and
x^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}
. Using the argument in the proof of (
3.8
), we have

\Big(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau},0\Big)\in\widehat{\partial}\big(\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))+\phi\big)(x,\bar{y}),

where
\phi(u,v):=\|v-\bar{y}\|
for all
(u,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

Choose
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,\epsilon)
such that
2(\tau+1)\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon
. By
Lemma

1
, there exist
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbf{B}(x,\epsilon_{1})\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},\epsilon_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau},0\Big)

\displaystyle\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(x_{1},y_{1})+\widehat{\partial}\phi(x_{2},y_{2})+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}})

\displaystyle\subseteq\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(x_{1},y_{1})+\{0\}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

Hence, there exist
(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(x_{1},y_{1})
and
b_{1}^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
such that

\Big(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau},0\Big)\in(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})+(0,b_{1}^{*})+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

(3.29)

By
Lemma

3
, there exist
(u_{1},v_{1})\in{\rm gph}(F)
and
(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*})\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}({\rm gph}(F),(u_{1},v_{1}))
such that

\|(u_{1},v_{1})-(x_{1},y_{1})\|<\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((u_{1},v_{1}),{\rm gph}(F))+\epsilon_{1},\quad\|(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*})-(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\|<\epsilon_{1}.

(3.30)

Combining (
3.29
) and (
3.30
), we obtain

\Big(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau},0\Big)\in(u_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*})+(0,b_{1}^{*})+2\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

(3.31)

Note that

(x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,{\rm gph}(F))(x_{1},y_{1})\subseteq\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}},

and it follows from (
3.30
) that

(u_{1},v_{1})\in\mathbf{B}((x,\bar{y}),\epsilon),\quad u_{1}^{*}\in\widehat{D}^{*}F(u_{1},v_{1})(-v_{1}^{*}),\quad\|v_{1}^{*}\|\leq 1+2\epsilon_{1}.

Finally, from (
3.29
) and (
3.31
), we get

x^{*}\in\tau\widehat{D}^{*}F(u_{1},v_{1})\big((1+2\epsilon_{1})\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)+2\tau\epsilon_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\subseteq\tau\widehat{D}^{*}F(u_{1},v_{1})\big((1+\epsilon)\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)+\epsilon\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

Hence, (
3.28
) holds.

It is well-known that main calculus results expressed via Fréchet constructions hold only in a
fuzzy
form; thus,
\epsilon
in (
3.28
) cannot generally be taken as
0
. However, in finite-dimensional spaces, one can pass to the limit as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
to obtain necessary conditions for metric subregularity in terms of
limiting
normal cones and coderivatives, as shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6

Let
\mathbb{X}
be finite-dimensional and
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
. Then there exist constants
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\subseteq\tau D^{*}F(x,\bar{y})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}})

(3.32)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
.

Proof.
By the metric subregularity of
F
, there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.1
) holds. Following the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, define

\|(x,y)\|_{\tau}:=\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\|x\|+\|y\|,\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}.

Then (
3.7
) holds with
\delta:=\frac{r}{2}
.

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
and
x^{*}\in{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}
. By the definition of the limiting normal cone, there exist sequences
x_{n}\xrightarrow{F^{-1}(\bar{y})}x
and
x_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\longrightarrow}}x^{*}
with
x_{n}^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x_{n})
for all
n
. Without loss of generality, assume
x_{n}^{*}\neq 0
and set
\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}:=\frac{x_{n}^{*}}{\|x_{n}^{*}\|}
. Then

\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x_{n})\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}=\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}(\cdot,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))(x_{n}).

Using the proof of (
3.8
) and applying
Lemma

1
, for each
n
there exist

(u_{n},v_{n})\in\mathbf{B}\Big(x_{n},\frac{1}{n}\Big)\times\mathbf{B}\Big(\bar{y},\frac{1}{n}\Big),\quad(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,\mathrm{gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n}),\quad b_{n}^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}

such that

\Big(\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},0\Big)\in(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})+(0,b_{n}^{*})+\frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

(3.33)

By
Lemma

3
, for each
(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,\mathrm{gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})
, there exist

(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}})\in\mathrm{gph}(F),\quad(\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}},\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}})\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}}))

such that

\|(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}})-(u_{n},v_{n})\|<\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}((u_{n},v_{n}),\mathrm{gph}(F))+\frac{1}{n},\quad\|(\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}},\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}})-(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\|<\frac{1}{n}.

(3.34)

Combining (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) gives

\Big(\frac{\widetilde{x_{n}^{*}}}{\tau},0\Big)\in(\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}},\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}})+(0,b_{n}^{*})+\frac{2}{n}(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

(3.35)

Since
\mathbb{X}
is finite-dimensional and
\mathbb{Y}
is Asplund, and

(u_{n}^{*},v_{n}^{*})\in\widehat{\partial}\mathbf{d}_{\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\cdot,\mathrm{gph}(F))(u_{n},v_{n})\subseteq\frac{\tau+1}{\tau}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}},

we can assume (passing to a subsequence if necessary) that

u_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{*},\quad v_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}v^{*},\quad b_{n}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}b^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}.

Then (
3.34
) implies

(\widetilde{u_{n}},\widetilde{v_{n}})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gph}(F)}(x,\bar{y}),\quad\widetilde{u_{n}^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{*},\quad\widetilde{v_{n}^{*}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}v^{*},

so that
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x,\bar{y}))
. Taking the limit in (
3.35
) yields

\Big(\frac{x^{*}}{\tau\|x^{*}\|},0\Big)=(u^{*},v^{*})+(0,b^{*}),

and consequently

x^{*}\in\tau D^{*}F(x,\bar{y})(\|x^{*}\|b^{*})\subseteq\tau D^{*}F(x,\bar{y})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}),

since
\|x^{*}\|\leq 1
. Hence, (
3.32
) holds.
\Box

Remark 1

Theorem

3.6
provides a necessary condition for the metric subregularity of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, as stated in (
3.32
). However, (
3.32
) may not, in general, be sufficient for the metric subregularity of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, even in finite-dimensional spaces. The following example illustrates this.

Let
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Y}=\mathbb{R}
and define the function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(x):=\begin{cases}x,&x\leq 0,\\
x^{2},&x>0.\end{cases}

Define
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
by
F(x)=[f(x),+\infty)
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
, and take
\bar{x}=\bar{y}=0
. One can verify that

F^{-1}(\bar{y})=(-\infty,0],\quad D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(1)\supseteq\{0,1\},

so (
3.32
) holds for
x=\bar{x}
with
\tau=1
.

On the other hand, if
x_{k}:=1/k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\frac{\mathbf{d}(x_{k},F^{-1}(\bar{y}))}{\mathbf{d}(0,F(x_{k}))}=k\ \longrightarrow\ +\infty\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

This shows that
F
is not metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

We now study metric subregularity for composite-convex multifunctions in the setting of Asplund spaces. From
Theorems

3.1
,
3.5
and
3.6
, we know that necessary conditions for the metric subregularity of closed multifunctions can be expressed in terms of Fréchet and limiting normal cones, as well as coderivatives. Ideally, such conditions would also be sufficient for metric subregularity. In fact, this ideal case occurs for composite-convex multifunctions in Asplund spaces.

The next theorem provides dual characterizations of metric subregularity for composite-convex multifunctions in Asplund spaces, expressed in terms of Fréchet and limiting normal cones, together with coderivatives.

Theorem 3.7

Let
\mathbb{E}
be an Asplund space,
g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{E}
be a continuously differentiable mapping, and let
G\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{Y})
be such that
{\rm gph}(G)
is convex. Set
F:=G\circ g
and let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
be such that
\nabla g(\bar{x})
is surjective, and
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})

.

(ii)

\tau,\delta>0

(3.36)

holds for all

.

(iii)

There exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that

(3.37)

holds for all

Proof.

Define
\Psi(x,y):=(g(x),y)
for all

. Then

\nabla\Psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\big(\nabla g(\bar{x}),\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big),

where
\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
is the identity operator on
\mathbb{Y}^{*}
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\nabla\Psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is surjective.

Note that
\mathrm{gph}(F)=\Psi^{-1}(\mathrm{gph}(G))
. By virtue of
Lemma

5
, there exist
\ell,L,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x,y)\big)\cap\ell\big(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\nabla\Psi(x,y)^{*}\big(\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(G),\Psi(x,y))\big)\cap\big(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)

(3.38)

\displaystyle\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x,y)\big)\cap L\big(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)

for all
(x,y)\in\big(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},r)\big)\cap\mathrm{gph}(F)
.

Moreover, there exist
\ell_{1},L_{1}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),u\big)\cap\ell_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}\subseteq\nabla g(u)^{*}\big(\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),g(u))\big)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),u\big)\cap L_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}

(3.39)

for all
u\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
(shrinking
r>0
if necessary).

We first show the equivalence of (i) and (ii).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
:
Suppose that
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
Then, by
Theorem

3.5
, there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that (
3.28
) holds for all

x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y}).

Fix

and take

By (
3.28
), for each

, there exist

(x_{n},y_{n})\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(x,\bar{y}),\quad(x_{n}^{*},y_{n}^{*})\in\mathbb{X}^{*}\times\Big(1+\tfrac{1}{n}\Big)\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}},\quad b_{n}^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}},

x^{*}=\tau x_{n}^{*}+\tfrac{1}{n}b_{n}^{*}.

(3.40)

Note that
\mathrm{gph}(F)=\Psi^{-1}(\mathrm{gph}(G))

It then follows from

that

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x_{n},y_{n}))=\nabla\Psi(x_{n},y_{n})^{*}\big(\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(G),(g(x_{n}),y_{n}))\big).

(e_{n}^{*},w_{n}^{*})\in\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(G),(g(x_{n}),y_{n}))

From (

), there exists

such that
\|(x_{n}^{*},y_{n}^{*})\|\leq M
for all

Then, by (

such that

\frac{\ell}{M}(x_{n}^{*},y_{n}^{*})=\nabla\Psi(x_{n},y_{n})^{*}(\tilde{e}_{n}^{*},\tilde{w}_{n}^{*})=\big(\nabla g(x_{n})^{*}(\tilde{e}_{n}^{*}),\tilde{w}_{n}^{*}\big).

Combining this with (
3.41
) yields

y_{n}^{*}=w_{n}^{*}=\frac{M}{\ell}\tilde{w}_{n}^{*},\quad x_{n}^{*}=\nabla g(x_{n})^{*}(e_{n}^{*})=\nabla g(x_{n})^{*}\!\left(\frac{M}{\ell}\tilde{e}_{n}^{*}\right),

and hence

since
\nabla g(x_{n})^{*}
is one-to-one.

Because
\mathbb{X}
and

are Asplund spaces, without loss of generality we may assume that

(3.42)

(possibly after passing to a subsequence).

{(x_{n},y_{n})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gph}(F)}(x,\bar{y})}
.

Finally, taking weak
∗

.

3.36
) holds.

(ii)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(i)
:
Suppose there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
3.36
) holds.
Let
\epsilon>0
be such that

Since

(3.43)

Set

and take any

.
Then

\mathbf{d}\big(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y})\big)\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|<r_{1}.

Choose

\beta>\max\left\{\frac{\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))}{r_{1}},\;\frac{(\tau+1)\epsilon}{\ell}\right\}.

(3.44)

By

4
, there exist

u\in F^{-1}(\bar{y})
and
u^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),u\big)

It follows that

Hence, by (
3.36

b^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}

\left(\frac{u^{*}}{\tau},b^{*}\right)\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u,\bar{y})\big).

From (
3.38

(e^{*},y^{*})\in\mathbf{N}\big(\mathrm{gph}(G),(g(u),\bar{y})\big)\cap\big(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)

such that

\frac{\tau\ell}{\tau+1}\left(\frac{u^{*}}{\tau},b^{*}\right)=\nabla\Psi(u,\bar{y})^{*}(e^{*},y^{*})=\big(\nabla g(u)^{*}(e^{*}),y^{*}\big).

(3.46)

For any

\langle(e^{*},y^{*}),\,(g(x)-g(u),\,y-\bar{y})\rangle\leq 0.

Combining this with (
3.43

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\ell u^{*}}{\tau+1},x-u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla g(u)^{*}(e^{*}),x-u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{*},\nabla g(u)(x-u)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e^{*},g(u)-g(x)+\nabla g(u)(x-u)\rangle+\langle e^{*},g(x)-g(u)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|g(u)-g(x)+\nabla g(u)(x-u)\|+\langle-y^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle

Taking the limit as

is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant

\frac{\tau+1}{\ell-(\tau+1)\epsilon}>0
.

For the equivalence of (ii) and (iii), we claim that

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x,y)\big)=\mathbf{N}\big(\mathrm{gph}(F),(x,y)\big),\quad\forall(x,y)\in\big(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},r)\big)\cap\mathrm{gph}(F),

(3.47)

and

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),u\big)=\mathbf{N}\big(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),u\big),\quad\forall u\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y}).

(3.48)

Let
(x,y)\in\left(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},r)\right)\cap{\rm gph}(F)

and

{(x_{k},y_{k})\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(x,y)}

By the Banach–Steinhaus theorem, we may assume that

and all

.
Hence

), for all

Since
\mathbb{E}
and

are Asplund spaces, we may assume,

Because

Noting that
\nabla\Psi(x,y)^{*}
is weak
∗

Theorem 3.8

Let
\mathbb{E}
be an Asplund space,

g:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{E}
a continuously differentiable mapping,
and
G\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{Y})
a convex multifunction.
Let
F:=G\circ g
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
be such that

\nabla g(\bar{x})
is surjective and
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})
.
Then
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})

if and only if
G
is metrically subregular at
(g(\bar{x}),\bar{y})
.

Proof

Let
\Psi(x,y):=(g(x),y)
for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
. By
Lemma

5
, there exist constants
\ell,L,\ell_{1},L_{1},r\in(0,+\infty)
such that
(
3.38
) holds for all
(x,y)\in(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\times\mathbf{B}(\bar{y},r))\cap{\rm gph}(F)
and (
3.39
) holds for all
u\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
(possibly taking a smaller
r>0
if necessary).

Based on
Theorem

3.7
, it suffices to prove that there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that (
3.36
) holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
if and only if there exist
\tau_{1},\delta_{1}>0
such that

\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),e)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}\subseteq\tau_{1}D^{*}G(e,\bar{y})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}),\quad\forall e\in\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})\cap G^{-1}(\bar{y}).

(3.51)

Suppose that there exist
\tau,\delta>0
such that (
3.36
) holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
. Without loss of generality, assume
\delta<r
(possibly taking a smaller
\delta
if necessary). Since
\nabla g(\bar{x})
is surjective, it follows from
(
Mordukhovich
, Theorem 1.57)
that
g
is metrically regular at
\bar{x}
, and thus there exist
\kappa,r_{0}>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,g^{-1}(e))\leq\kappa\|e-g(x)\|,\quad\forall(x,e)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r_{0})\times\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),r_{0}).

(3.52)

Take
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\kappa\delta_{1}<\min\{\delta,r_{0}\}
. Let
e\in\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})\cap G^{-1}(\bar{y})
. Then

\mathbf{d}(\bar{x},g^{-1}(e))\leq\kappa\|e-g(\bar{x})\|<\kappa\delta_{1}<\delta,

so there exists
x\in g^{-1}(e)
such that
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
. By (
3.39
), we have

\nabla g(x)^{*}(\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),e)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}})=\nabla g(x)^{*}(\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),g(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}})\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap L_{1}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

Let
e^{*}\in\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),e)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}
. Then (
3.36
) implies there exists
y^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
such that

\Big(\frac{1}{L_{1}\tau}\nabla g(x)^{*}(e^{*}),y^{*}\Big)\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}({\rm gph}(F),(x,\bar{y})).

By (
3.38
), there exists
(e_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*})\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(G),(g(x),\bar{y}))\cap(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}\times\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}})
such that

\frac{\tau\ell}{\tau+1}\Big(\frac{1}{L_{1}\tau}\nabla g(x)^{*}(e^{*}),y^{*}\Big)=\nabla\Psi(x,\bar{y})(e_{1}^{*},v_{1}^{*})=(\nabla g(x)^{*}(e_{1}^{*}),v_{1}^{*}).

This implies

\nabla g(x)^{*}\Big(\frac{\ell}{(\tau+1)L_{1}}e^{*}\Big)=\nabla g(x)^{*}(e_{1}^{*})\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\tau\ell}{\tau+1}y^{*}=v_{1}^{*}.

Since
\nabla g(x)^{*}
is injective, it follows that

\frac{\ell}{(\tau+1)L_{1}}e^{*}=e_{1}^{*},\quad\text{hence}\quad e^{*}=\frac{(\tau+1)L_{1}}{\ell}e_{1}^{*}\in\frac{(\tau+1)L_{1}}{\ell}D^{*}G(e,\bar{y})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

Thus (
3.51
) holds with
\tau_{1}:=\frac{(\tau+1)L_{1}}{\ell}>0
.

Conversely, suppose that there exist
\tau_{1},\delta_{1}>0
such that (
3.51
) holds. Take
\delta\in(0,r)
such that

\|g(x)-g(\bar{x})\|<\delta_{1},\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.53)

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(\bar{y})
and
x^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),x)\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}
. Then
g(x)\in G^{-1}(\bar{y})\cap\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),\delta_{1})
. By (
3.39
), there exists
e_{1}^{*}\in\mathbf{N}(G^{-1}(\bar{y}),g(x))\cap\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{E}^{*}}
such that

\ell_{1}x^{*}=\nabla g(x)^{*}(e_{1}^{*}).

(3.54)

By (
3.51
), there exists
y_{1}^{*}\in\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}
such that

\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}e_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*}\Big)\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(G),(g(x),\bar{y})).

It follows from
(
Mordukhovich
, Corollary 1.15)
that

\nabla\Psi(x,\bar{y})^{*}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}e_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*}\Big)\in\nabla\Psi(x,\bar{y})^{*}(\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(G),(g(x),\bar{y})))=\widehat{\mathbf{N}}({\rm gph}(F),(x,\bar{y})).

Together with (
3.54
), this implies

\Big(\frac{\ell_{1}}{\tau_{1}}x^{*},y_{1}^{*}\Big)=\nabla\Psi(x,\bar{y})^{*}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}e_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*}\Big)\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}({\rm gph}(F),(x,\bar{y})),

and consequently

x^{*}\in\frac{\tau_{1}}{\ell_{1}}\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,\bar{y})(-y_{1}^{*})\subseteq\frac{\tau_{1}}{\ell_{1}}\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,\bar{y})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}).

Hence (
3.36
) holds with
\tau:=\frac{\tau_{1}}{\ell_{1}}>0
. The proof is complete.

4
Applications to Metric Subregularity of Conic Inequalities

As an application of the results obtained in Section 3, we study the metric subregularity of the conic inequality defined by a proper vector-valued function between two Banach spaces and a closed cone with a nontrivial recession cone. We begin by formulating such a conic inequality.

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Banach spaces, and let
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
be a nonempty closed cone with a nontrivial recession cone; that is,

K^{\infty}\neq\{0\}.

The cone
K
induces a preorder
\leq_{K}
on
\mathbb{Y}
via

y_{1}\leq_{K}y_{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_{2}-y_{1}\in K.

(4.1)

We denote by
\infty_{\mathbb{Y}}
the abstract infinity.
Let
\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be the set of all proper vector-valued functions

\varphi:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}\cup\{\infty_{\mathbb{Y}}\}

such that

{\rm epi}_{K}(\varphi):=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}:\varphi(x)\leq_{K}y\big\}

(4.2)

is closed in the product space
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
.

Let
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
.
Consider the following conic inequality:

f(x)\leq_{K}0.

(4.3)

We denote the solution set of (
4.3
) by

\mathbf{S}_{K}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:f(x)\leq_{K}0\}.

Conic inequalities can encompass a wide variety of constraint systems in optimization.
For example, let
\mathbb{Y}:=\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
and
K:=\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}
.
Then the conic inequality
f(x)\leq_{K}0
reduces to the usual finite system of inequalities and equalities.
If
\mathbb{Y}
is the space of all continuous real-valued functions defined on a compact topological space
T
,
and
K
is the cone of all nonnegative continuous functions on
T
,
then such a conic inequality reduces to the constraint system of a semi-infinite optimization problem.

Recall from
Zheng2022-MOR
;
ZN2019-ESAIM
that
f
is said to be
metrically subregular
at

\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
if there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{K}(f))\leq\tau\,\mathbf{d}\big(f(x),-K\big),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(4.4)

To study the metric subregularity of the vector-valued function
f
,
we consider the following two types of subdifferential of
f
with respect to
K
.

Let

K^{\infty,+}:=\big\{y^{*}\in\mathbb{Y}^{*}:\langle y^{*},y\rangle\geq 0,\ \forall y\in K^{\infty}\big\},

and interpret
\langle y^{*},\infty_{\mathbb{Y}}\rangle
as
+\infty
for all
y^{*}\in K^{\infty,+}
.

Let
u\in{\rm dom}(f)
.
We denote by
\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u)
and
\partial_{K}f(u)
the Fréchet and limiting subdifferentials of
f
at
u
with respect to
K
, respectively, defined as

\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u):=\big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big({\rm epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))\big)\cap\big(\{x^{*}\}\times\big(-K^{\infty,+}\cap\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)\big)\neq\emptyset\big\},

\partial_{K}f(u):=\big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:\mathbf{N}\big({\rm epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))\big)\cap\big(\{x^{*}\}\times\big(-K^{\infty,+}\cap\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}\big)\big)\neq\emptyset\big\}.

Similarly, we define the Fréchet and limiting
singular
subdifferentials of
f
at
u
with respect to
K
as

\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}_{K}f(u):=\big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:(x^{*},0)\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big({\rm epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))\big)\big\},

\partial^{\infty}_{K}f(u):=\big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}:(x^{*},0)\in\mathbf{N}\big({\rm epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))\big)\big\}.

It is easy to verify that
\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}_{K}f(u)
and
\partial^{\infty}_{K}f(u)
are cones.
Moreover, if
f
is locally Lipschitz around
u
, then

\widehat{\partial}^{\infty}_{K}f(u)=\partial^{\infty}_{K}f(u)=\{0\}.

Lemma 7

Let
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
and define
F(x):=f(x)+K
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
.
Then, for any
x\in{\rm dom}(f)
, one has
{\rm dom}\big(D^{*}F(x,f(x))\big)\subseteq K^{\infty,+}
,

D^{*}F(x,f(x))(\mathbb{Y}^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(x)+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(x),

(4.5)

and

\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,f(x))(\mathbb{Y}^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(x)+\widehat{\partial}_{K}^{\infty}f(x).

(4.6)

Proof

Let
x\in{\rm dom}(f)
.
Take any
y^{*}\in{\rm dom}\big(D^{*}F(x,f(x))\big)
and
x^{*}\in D^{*}F(x,f(x))(y^{*})
.
Then
(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(F),(x,f(x)))
.
By (
2.1
), there exist sequences
\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
,
(x_{k},y_{k})\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(x,f(x))
,
and
(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{w^{*}}(x^{*},-y^{*})
such that

(x_{k}^{*},-y_{k}^{*})\in{\widehat{\bf N}_{\varepsilon_{k}}}({\rm gph}(F),(x_{k},y_{k}))\quad\forall k.

Thus, for each
k
, one has

\limsup_{(u,v)\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}(x_{k},y_{k})}\frac{\langle x_{k}^{*},u-x_{k}\rangle-\langle y_{k}^{*},v-y_{k}\rangle}{\|(u-x_{k},v-y_{k})\|}\leq\varepsilon_{k}.

(4.7)

Let
e\in K^{\infty}
.
For any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, we have

y_{k}+\epsilon e-f(x_{k})\in K+\epsilon e\subseteq K

(since
e\in K^{\infty}
).
This implies
(x_{k},y_{k}+\epsilon e){\xrightarrow{{\rm gph}(F)}}(x_{k},y_{k})
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
,
and hence, by (
4.7
),

-\langle y_{k}^{*},e\rangle\leq\varepsilon_{k}.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
gives
\langle y^{*},e\rangle\geq 0
, so
y^{*}\in K^{\infty,+}
.

If
y^{*}\neq 0
, then

\left(\frac{x^{*}}{\|y^{*}\|},-\frac{y^{*}}{\|y^{*}\|}\right)\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(F),(x,f(x))),

which implies
\frac{x^{*}}{\|y^{*}\|}\in\partial_{K}f(x)
, since
{\rm gph}(F)={\rm epi}_{K}(f)
.
Thus

x^{*}\in\|y^{*}\|\,\partial_{K}f(x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(x).

If
y^{*}=0
, then
(x^{*},0)\in\mathbf{N}({\rm gph}(F),(x,f(x)))
,
so
x^{*}\in\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(x)
.
This proves (
4.5
).

The proof of (
4.6
) is analogous and is omitted.

Remark 2

It is straightforward to verify that if
\mathbb{Y}:=\mathbb{R}
and
K:=[0,+\infty)
,
then the Fréchet and limiting subdifferentials with respect to
K
reduce to the usual ones.

Furthermore, if we define
F(x):=f(x)+K
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
, then one can check that

\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u)=\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,f(x))(\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}),\quad\partial_{K}f(u)=D^{*}F(x,f(x))(\mathbf{S}_{\mathbb{Y}^{*}}),

and

\widehat{\partial}_{K}^{\infty}f(u)=\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,f(x))(0),\quad\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(u)=D^{*}F(x,f(x))(0).

The next theorem provides necessary conditions for the metric subregularity of the conic inequality,
and characterizes Asplund spaces in terms of such conditions.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space.

(ii)

For any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
with a nontrivial recession cone, and any
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
being metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
with
f(\bar{x})=0
,
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(x)+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(x)

(4.8)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
with
f(x)=0
.

(iii)

For any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
with a nontrivial recession cone, and any
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
that is metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
with
f(\bar{x})=0
,
relation (
4.8
) holds for
x=\bar{x}
.

(iv)

For any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
with a nontrivial recession cone, and any continuous vector-valued function
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
being metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
with
f(\bar{x})=0
,
relation (
4.8
) holds for
x=\bar{x}
.

Proof

(i)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(ii):
Let
\mathbb{Y}
be an Asplund space, let
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
be a nonempty closed cone with a nontrivial recession cone, and let
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be metrically subregular at some
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
with
f(\bar{x})=0
.
Define
F(x):=f(x)+K
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
. Then
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
, and one can verify that
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
if and only if the conic inequality
f(x)\leq_{K}0
is metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
.

By
Theorem

3.1
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap F^{-1}(0)
, we have

\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(0),x)\subseteq D^{*}F(x,0)(\mathbb{Y}^{*}).

(4.9)

Now, for any
x\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
with
f(x)=0
,

Lemma

7
yields

D^{*}F(x,0)(\mathbb{Y}^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(x)+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(x),

and combining this with (
4.9
) gives

\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(0),x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(x)+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(x).

Since
F^{-1}(0)=\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
, this proves (ii).

The implications (ii)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(iii)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(iv) are immediate, so it remains to prove that (iv)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(i).

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\mathbb{X}
is not an Asplund space.
Following
MW2000
;
FM1998
, we can write
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
with the norm

\|(z,\alpha)\|:=\|z\|+|\alpha|,\quad(z,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R},

where
\mathbb{Z}
is not an Asplund space.
By
(
DGZ1993
, Theorem 1.5.3)
(see also
(
FM1998
, Theorem 2.1)
), there exists an equivalent norm
|||\cdot|||
on
\mathbb{Z}
and a constant
\gamma>0
such that (
3.12
) holds.

Define
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\varphi(z):=-|||z|||
, and set

A_{1}:=\{0_{\mathbb{Z}}\}\times(-\infty,0],\quad A_{2}:=\operatorname{epi}(\varphi),\quad\bar{x}:=0_{\mathbb{X}},\quad\bar{y}:=(\bar{x},\bar{x}).

Let
\mathbb{Y}:=\mathbb{X}^{2}
be equipped with the
\ell^{1}
-norm, and define

K:=-(A_{1}\times A_{2}).

Then
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
is a nonempty closed cone with a nontrivial recession cone (indeed,
K^{\infty}\supseteq-A_{1}\times\{0\}
).
Define
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
by
f(x):=(x,x)
, and consider the conic inequality

f(x)\leq_{K}0.

Let
F(x):=f(x)+K
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
. Then
\operatorname{epi}_{K}(f)=\operatorname{gph}(F)
.

We first claim that

\partial_{K}f(\bar{x})\cup\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})+\mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x}).

(4.10)

Indeed, let
x^{*}\in\partial_{K}f(\bar{x})\cup\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(\bar{x})
.
Then there exists
y^{*}=\big(y^{*}(1),y^{*}(2)\big)\in K^{\infty,+}
such that

(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathbf{N}(\operatorname{epi}_{K}(f),(\bar{x},f(\bar{x})))=\mathbf{N}(\operatorname{gph}(F),(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))),

and by
Lemma

6
,

y^{*}\in\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})\times\mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x}),\quad x^{*}=y^{*}(1)+y^{*}(2).

This proves (
4.10
).

From the proof of (
3.13
), we also have

\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{K}(f))\leq 2\,\mathbf{d}(f(x),-K),\quad\forall x=(z,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R},

which means that the conic inequality
f(x)\leq_{K}0
is metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
.

By (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we have

\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})=\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times[0,+\infty),\quad\mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x})=\{(0,0)\},

(4.11)

and

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),\bar{x})=\mathbf{N}(F^{-1}(\bar{y}),\bar{x})=\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}\not\subseteq\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})+\mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x}).

(4.12)

On the other hand, (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) imply

\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(\bar{x})+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\mathbf{N}(A_{1},\bar{x})+\mathbf{N}(A_{2},\bar{x}),

which, together with (
4.12
), gives

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),\bar{x})\not\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(\bar{x})+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(\bar{x}),

contradicting (
4.8
) with
x=\bar{x}
.
Thus
\mathbb{X}
must be Asplund.

Remark 3

It is worth noting that in the special case where
\mathbb{Y}:=\mathbb{R}
and
K:=[0,+\infty)
, metric subregularity of the conic inequality reduces to the local error bound for the scalar inequality
f(x)\leq 0
in Asplund spaces. This was the subject of our recent work in
WTY2025-JCA
, and Theorem
4.1
actually recovers
(
WTY2025-JCA
, Theorem 3.1)
.

Moreover, it is well known that Lewis and Pang
(
LewisPang1998
, Proposition 2)
studied error bounds for convex inequality systems and established necessary conditions for such bounds in terms of normal cones to the solution set and subdifferentials of the underlying convex function. In particular, their result shows that a local error bound implies the basic constraint qualification (BCQ). More precisely:

Given a lower semicontinuous convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, if the convex inequality
f(x)\leq 0
admits a local error bound at
\bar{x}\in{\mathbf{S}_{f}}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:f(x)\leq 0\}
with
f(\bar{x})=0
, then the following BCQ holds at
\bar{x}
:

\mathbf{N}({\mathbf{S}_{f}},\bar{x})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial f(\bar{x}).

When restricted to convex inequalities, Theorem
4.1
(ii) recovers
(
LewisPang1998
, Proposition 2)
, since

\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial f(\bar{x})+\partial^{\infty}f(\bar{x})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial f(\bar{x}).

From the viewpoint of Theorem
4.1
, the validity of such implication results in both
(
WTY2025-JCA
, Theorem 3.1)
and
(
LewisPang1998
, Proposition 2)
essentially stems from the Asplund property of the underlying space.

It is known that for a given closed cone and a vector-valued function, the corresponding conic inequality can be equivalently described by a special multifunction, while the reverse case seems not true necessarily. However, based on
Theorem

3.1
and
Theorem

4.1
, the following theorem establishes an equivalence result between conic inequalities and multifunctions in the sense of proving necessary dual conditions for metric subregularity in terms of limiting normal cones; that is,

Theorem 4.2

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Then following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
with a nontrivial recession cone, and any
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
being metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
with
f(\bar{x})=0
, one has

\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),\bar{x})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\partial_{K}f(\bar{x})+\partial_{K}^{\infty}f(\bar{x})

(4.13)

(ii)

For every Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
and every
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
being metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}(F)
,
F
satisfies the limiting
\mathbf{BCQ}
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Next, we establish necessary or sufficient conditions for a Banach space to be Asplund in terms of Fréchet subdifferentials of vector-valued functions with respect to
K
. For this purpose, we first recall a lemma on the Fréchet subdifferential of a given vector-valued function with respect to
K
.

Lemma 8

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be Banach spaces, and let
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
be a nonempty closed convex cone. Suppose
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
and define
F(x):=f(x)+K
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
. Then, for any
(u,v)\in\mathrm{gph}(F)
, we have

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u,v))\subseteq\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))).

(4.14)

Furthermore, if
f
is continuous at
u
, then

\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u,v))\subseteq\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))).

(4.15)

Proof

Let
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u,v))
. Then

\limsup_{(x,y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gph}(F)}(u,v)}\frac{\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+\langle v^{*},y-v\rangle}{\|(x-u,y-v)\|}\leq 0.

(4.16)

For any
(x,y)\to(u,f(u))
with
(x,y)\in\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f)
, we have

y+v-f(u)\to v,\quad\text{and}\quad y+v-f(u)\in f(x)+K+K\subseteq f(x)+K,

since
K
is a convex cone. Thus, by (
4.16
),

\limsup_{(x,y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f)}(u,f(u))}\frac{\langle u^{*},x-u\rangle+\langle v^{*},y-f(u)\rangle}{\|(x-u,y-f(u))\|}\leq 0,

which shows that
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u)))
.

Now assume
f
is continuous at
u
. Let
(u^{*},v^{*})\in\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u,v))
. Then there exist sequences
\epsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
,
(u_{k},v_{k})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gph}(F)}(u,v)
, and
(u_{k}^{*},v_{k}^{*})\xrightarrow{w^{*}}(u^{*},v^{*})
such that

(u_{k}^{*},v_{k}^{*})\in{\widehat{\bf N}_{\epsilon_{k}}}(\mathrm{gph}(F),(u_{k},v_{k})),\quad\forall k.

That is,

\limsup_{(x,y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gph}(F)}(u_{k},v_{k})}\frac{\langle u_{k}^{*},x-u_{k}\rangle+\langle v_{k}^{*},y-v_{k}\rangle}{\|(x-u_{k},y-v_{k})\|}\leq\epsilon_{k}.

(4.17)

For any
(x,y)\to(u_{k},f(u_{k}))
with
(x,y)\in\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f)
, we have

y+v_{k}-f(u_{k})\to v_{k},\quad\text{and}\quad y+v_{k}-f(u_{k})\in f(x)+K+K\subseteq f(x)+K.

Thus, from (
4.17
),

\limsup_{(x,y)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f)}(u_{k},f(u_{k}))}\frac{\langle u_{k}^{*},x-u_{k}\rangle+\langle v_{k}^{*},y-f(u_{k})\rangle}{\|(x-u_{k},y-f(u_{k}))\|}\leq\epsilon_{k},

which means

(u_{k}^{*},v_{k}^{*})\in{\widehat{\bf N}_{\epsilon_{k}}}(\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f),(u_{k},f(u_{k}))).

Since
u_{k}\to u
and
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
by continuity, passing to the limit yields

(u^{*},v^{*})\in\mathbf{N}(\mathrm{epi}_{K}(f),(u,f(u))).

Thus (
4.15
) holds.

In terms of Fréchet subdifferentials of the given vector-valued function with respect to
K
, the following theorem provides necessary or sufficient conditions for a Banach space to be Asplund.

Theorem 4.3

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space. Consider the following statements:

(i)

Suppose that
\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space. Then for any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed convex cone

, and any
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)

\delta>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
, one has

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),x)\subseteq{\bigcup\left\{\mathbb{R}_{+}\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u)+\widehat{\partial}_{K}^{\infty}f(u):u\in\mathbf{B}(x,\varepsilon)\right\}}+\varepsilon\,{\bf B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}

holds for all

Suppose that for any Asplund space
\mathbb{Y}
, any nonempty closed cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}

f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})

\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}(\mathbf{S}_{K}(f),\bar{x})\subseteq{\bigcup\left\{\mathbb{R}_{+}\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u)+\widehat{\partial}_{K}^{\infty}f(u):u\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\varepsilon)\right\}}+\varepsilon\,{\bf B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}

(4.19)

holds for any
\varepsilon>0
. Then
\mathbb{X}
is an Asplund space.

Proof

(i) Let
\mathbb{Y}
be an Asplund space,
K\subseteq\mathbb{Y}
a nonempty closed convex cone, and let
f\in\Lambda_{K}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
be metrically subregular at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
.
Define
F:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
by

F(x):=f(x)+K,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{X}.

Then
F\in\Gamma(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
and
F^{-1}(0)=\mathbf{S}_{K}(f)
.
Moreover, the conic inequality
f(x)\leq_{K}0
is metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
if and only if
F
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)\in{\rm gph}(F)
.

By
Theorem

3.2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any

x\in F^{-1}(0)\cap{\bf B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and any
\epsilon>0
,

\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\big(F^{-1}(0),x\big)\subseteq\bigcup_{(u,v)\in\mathbf{B}((\bar{x},0),\epsilon)\cap{\rm gph}(F)}\widehat{D}^{*}F(u,v)(\mathbb{Y}^{*})+\epsilon\,\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}^{*}}.

(4.20)

We claim that

\widehat{D}^{*}F(u,v)(\mathbb{Y}^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}\,\widehat{\partial}_{K}f(u)+\widehat{\partial}_{K}^{\infty}f(u),\quad\forall(u,v)\in{\rm gph}(F).

(4.21)

Assuming this, (
4.20
) immediately yields (
4.18
).

To verify (
4.21
), let
(u,v)\in{\rm gph}(F)
and take
x^{*}\in\widehat{D}^{*}F(u,v)(\mathbb{Y}^{*})
.
Then there exists
y^{*}\in\mathbb{Y}^{*}
such that

(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{\mathbf{N}}({\rm gph}(F),(u,v)).

By
Lemma

Conclusion

This paper investigates the metric subregularity of multifunctions and its connection to characterizations of Asplund spaces. For a closed multifunction between Asplund spaces, we have shown that metric subregularity implies certain exact and fuzzy inclusions, expressed respectively via limiting and Fréchet coderivatives as well as normal cones. Moreover, each of these inclusions, when applied to all closed multifunctions, serves as characterizations of Asplund spaces.

As an application, we study the metric subregularity of a conic inequality defined by a vector-valued function and a closed (not necessarily convex) cone. By employing limiting subdifferentials of the vector-valued function (with respect to the given closed cone), we obtained an exact inclusion implied by metric subregularity. When this exact inclusion is applied to all conic inequalities, it characterizes Asplund spaces. This leads to the insight that the result in
(
LewisPang1998
, Proposition 2)
—that a local error bound implies the
\mathbf{BCQ}
for convex inequalities—is essentially a consequence of the Asplund property of finite-dimensional spaces.

In contrast, by using Fréchet subdifferentials (with respect to the same closed cone), we derived a fuzzy inclusion implied by metric subregularity. However, when applied to all conic inequalities, this fuzzy inclusion is only sufficient (and not necessary) for a space to be Asplund. This reveals a distinction between the Fréchet and limiting types of subdifferentials in their ability to characterize Asplund spaces.

Finally, we note that these two inclusions differ from the known characterizations of Asplund spaces obtained via limiting and Fréchet normal cones, as presented in
(
Mordukhovich
, Theorems 2.20, 2.22, and 2.30)
.

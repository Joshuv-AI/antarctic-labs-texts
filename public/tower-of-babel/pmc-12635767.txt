Successful Treatment of Steroid‐Dependent Severe Eosinophilic Pneumonia With Benralizumab: A Case Report
Authors: Takeru Ichikawa, Yuki Takigawa, Keiichi Fujiwara, Tomoyoshi Inoue, Shoichiro Matsumoto, Keisuke Shiraha, Mayu Goda, Suzuka Matsuoka, Hiromi Watanabe, Kenichiro Kudo, Akiko Sato, Ken Sato, Takuo Shibayama
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTEosinophilic pneumonia (EP) often requires long‐term steroid treatment, which has adverse effects. We report the case of an 83‐year‐old male with minimal‐change nephrotic syndrome, refractory asthma and steroid‐induced diabetes who developed a severe steroid‐dependent EP relapse during steroid tapering. Considering his age, comorbidities and steroid dependency, treatment with benralizumab, an anti‐interleukin‐5 receptor antibody, was initiated. Benralizumab rapidly depleted eosinophils, leading to significant clinical and radiological improvements, allowing successful sustained tapering of prednisolone to 5 mg daily, and improving diabetes control without EP relapse. Benralizumab provides effective steroid‐sparing therapy for steroid‐dependent EP, particularly in complex patients.Keywords: benralizumab, eosinophilic pneumonia, life‐threating pneumonia, steroid‐induced diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Eosinophilic pneumonia (EP) typically responds favourably to systemic steroid therapy; however, it has a high rate of recurrence during steroid tapering or after discontinuation. This often necessitates long‐term steroid use, causing side effects, especially in elderly or comorbid patients. Biologic therapy for EP has recently shown steroid‐sparing effects [1, 2]. Benralizumab, a monoclonal antibody‐targeting agent that blocks the IL‐5 receptor, has been reported as a therapeutic option for EP in several case studies [1, 2, 3].

We report a case of successful treatment with benralizumab for steroid‐dependent EP in an elderly patient with multiple comorbidities.

Case Report

An 83‐year‐old male patient was admitted to the Department of Nephrology at our hospital with leg edema and proteinuria. He had diabetes and asthma, treated with inhaled corticosteroid/long‐acting beta agonist. Diagnosed with minimal change nephrotic syndrome (MCNS), he received 50 mg oral prednisolone daily, which improved symptoms and laboratory data. The patient was discharged after the PSL dose was reduced to 30 mg daily. His diabetes worsened owing to steroid treatment.

PSL was gradually reduced to 9 mg daily during outpatient visits. Four months after initiating PSL for MCNS, he was urgently transported to our hospital because of severe hypoxemia (SpO2 51% on room air). Chest CT revealed diffuse ground‐glass opacities and consolidation in both lungs (Figure 1A,B), leading to admission. Initial blood tests revealed elevated eosinophil counts (3374/μL), suggesting concurrent EP. Based on the imaging findings (Figure 2A) and peripheral eosinophilia, we considered differential diagnoses, including EP. Treatment was initiated with 125 mg methylprednisolone (mPSL) pulse therapy and broad‐spectrum antibiotics on the first day of hospitalisation. Systemic steroid treatment led to a decrease in the eosinophil count. After a transient deterioration in respiratory status on the second day, requiring high‐flow nasal cannula (HFNC) oxygen therapy (FiO2 100%, O2 40L/min), the patient's respiratory condition gradually improved with high‐dose steroid therapy, which allowed for a gradual reduction in the oxygen flow rate. Chest radiography performed on the third day of hospitalisation revealed improved transparency in both lung fields, suggesting a favourable response to steroid treatment. On Day 6, he was weaned off HFNC and maintained on 80 mg mPSL. Chest CT performed on Day 8 (Figure 1C,D) showed improved ground‐glass opacities and de‐saturation had improved; thus, mPSL was reduced to 40 mg daily. On Day 13, eosinophil counts increased (3136/μL), and a chest x‐ray showed worsening shadows. Due to the suspected recurrence of EP during steroid tapering, 500 mg mPSL was administered. Clinical findings improved, and the oral PSL was gradually reduced to 50 mg daily. However, on Day 17, blood eosinophil counts increased (381/μL) and chest radiograph and auscultation findings worsened (Figure 2B). Considering the need for relatively high‐dose steroid treatment for drug‐induced pneumonitis control and the potential difficulties in diabetes management, benralizumab was initiated for the EP associated with bronchial asthma (BA) on Day 17. Blood eosinophil counts decreased to 0/μL in next day, and clinical symptoms (respiratory condition) and imaging findings markedly improved. The patient was discharged on PSL (20 mg) without supplemental oxygen on Day 29. The eosinophil count remained at 0/μL.

The patient has remained recurrence‐free for more than one and half year following the initiation of benralizumab. The PSL dose was successfully tapered to 5 mg(the dose of PSL could only be reduced to 5 mg due to comorbid MCNS) daily without signs of EP relapse (Figure 1E,F, Figure 2C) or worsening diabetes.

Discussion

We describe the case of an 83‐year‐old man with preexisting MCNS and BA who developed severe respiratory failure due to EP. His management was particularly challenging. While responsive to systemic corticosteroids, he exhibited steroid dependency with frequent relapses upon tapering and suffered from a severe exacerbation of diabetes mellitus. In this complex scenario, benralizumab therapy benefits included two main points: I; Rapid improvement of symptoms, II: enabling the tapering of steroids in this previously steroid‐dependent patient.

Rapid‐acting therapy was needed due to early symptom recurrence on steroid tapering. Benralizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the α‐chain of the IL‐5 receptor (IL‐5Rα), was chosen for this purpose. It suppresses eosinophilic inflammation via two main mechanisms. It binds to IL‐5Rα on eosinophils and basophils, preventing IL‐5 from binding; thus, inhibiting IL‐5‐mediated signalling, which is crucial for eosinophil survival and function. Benralizumab flags eosinophils for destruction by natural killer (NK) cells through antibody‐dependent cell‐mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), leading to rapid and near‐complete depletion [4, 5, 6]. Its rapid efficacy is attributed to ADCC, with eosinophil depletion reported within 24 h [7]. We also observed a rapid improvement in symptoms following the initiation of benralizumab treatment, highlighting its utility when a prompt clinical response is crucial.

Second, reducing the steroid dose was a critical goal. Advanced age, MCNS requiring maintenance steroids and severe steroid‐induced diabetes mellitus made long‐term high‐dose corticosteroid therapy highly undesirable. Although systemic corticosteroids are the standard treatment for EP, relapses during tapering are common and often necessitate long‐term therapy. In this context, recent reports have highlighted the efficacy of benralizumab in eosinophilic asthma and pneumonia [1, 2, 3]. Benralizumab controls the inflammation underlying EP by directly targeting and depleting eosinophils, thereby reducing underlying inflammation and serving as a potent steroid‐sparing agent. This effect has been demonstrated in patients with severe eosinophilic asthma, including those with comorbid chronic EP, in whom adjunctive benralizumab treatment resulted in oral corticosteroid reduction or discontinuation (Table 1). Notably, Tashiro et al. described a case in which benralizumab therapy led to the cessation of diabetes medication [2], indicating its particular benefit for patients with steroid‐related complications. In our patient, the introduction of benralizumab controlled the EP, which enabled successful steroid tapering and gradual improvement in glycaemic control. Although complete oral corticosteroid withdrawal was challenging due to the coexistence of MCNS, the substantial reduction in the steroid dose was a crucial achievement in this high‐risk patient.

In conclusion, this case highlights the potential of benralizumab to offer a safe and effective treatment for elderly patients with steroid‐dependent EP and multiple comorbidities. Compared with existing reports, this case emphasises the role of benralizumab in managing complex scenarios in which steroid therapy poses significant challenges. This also suggests that benralizumab may be a valuable treatment option for EP associated with BA, in which steroid management can be particularly challenging.

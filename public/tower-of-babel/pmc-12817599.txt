Targeting the LINC01272-FUS signal axis inhibits the migration and invasion of testicular germ cell tumors
Authors: Xueheng Zhao, Jian Cao, Lei Xue, Liqing Fan, Fang Zhu, Zailong Qin, Ziyi Chen, Zhizhong Liu, Jinhui Shu, Hao Bo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTesticular germ cell tumors (TGCTs), the most prevalent malignancy in males aged 15–40 years, exhibit suboptimal prognosis despite therapeutic advances. This study aimed to identify novel diagnostic biomarkers and elucidate their molecular mechanisms in TGCTs pathogenesis.MethodsThrough integrated bioinformatics analysis of public TCGA(The Cancer Genome Atlas)datasets and clinical specimen validation (n = 14), we screened LINC01272 as a candidate biomarker. RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were employed to verify the interaction between LINC01272 and fused in sarcoma (FUS). Functional experiments including siRNA-mediated gene silencing, transwell migration/invasion assays, and CCK-8 proliferation tests were performed in TGCTs cell lines (Tcam-2 and NCCIT).ResultsLINC01272 is significantly highly expressed in TGCT tissues. High LINC01272 levels indicate shorter overall and recurrence-free survival periods for patients; the predictive value is particularly significant in the subgroup with low tumor mutation burden. Silencing LINC01272 can inhibit the migration, invasion and proliferation of Tcam-2 and NCCIT cells, and down-regulate N-cadherin and Vimentin, while up-regulating LAMA1. RIP assays confirmed the direct binding of LINC01272 to FUS. FUS silencing can replicate the tumor suppressive effect of LINC01272 deletion, while FUS overexpression can reverse the inhibition of migration, invasion and proliferation caused by LINC01272 silencing.ConclusionWe identified the LINC01272-FUS axis as a critical regulatory pathway in TGCTs progression, providing mechanistic insights for developing liquid biopsy biomarkers and RNA-targeted therapies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-025-04095-0.

Introduction

Testicular germ cell tumors (TGCTs) are the most common cancers in male patients aged 15–40 years and mainly occur in the reproductive epithelium of the seminiferous tubules [1]. TGCTs, which account for approximately 1% of all male solid tumors and 98% of testicular tumors, can be divided into two main subtypes: seminoma and non-seminoma [2]. Seminoma, which has a slower growth rate and generally a better prognosis, is the primary subtype of TGCTs [3]. Non-seminomas, which include embryonic carcinomas, teratomas, yolk sac tumors, and mixed germ cell tumors, have a high degree of malignancy and poor prognosis [4]. In recent years, the occurrence rate of TGCTs has gradually increased [5]. Currently, the primary treatment for TGCTs is testicular resection supplemented with chemotherapy and radiation [5]. However, current treatment methods could damage sperm production and sexual function, resulting in serious psychological pressure on male patients. Approximately 15%−20% of first-stage seminoma and 30% of non-seminoma patients relapse and have a poor prognosis [6]. The pathophysiological mechanisms of tumorigenesis have drawn increasing attention. Ordinarily, tumor development involves many steps, mainly the activation of oncogenes and suppression of anti-oncogenes [7]. Although a series of pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic approaches have been partly clarified, the prognosis of TGCTs remains lower than expected. It is necessary to further explore the detailed and comprehensive regulatory pathways and molecular mechanisms of TGCTs. A more accurate diagnosis and effective therapeutic methods are also important for TGCTs therapy.

Non-coding RNA (ncRNAs), including microRNAs (miRNAs), circular RNA (circRNAs), and long ncRNAs (lncRNAs), are a specific type of RNA that cannot synthesize proteins, but can control gene levels by shearing mRNA post-transcription [8, 9]. miRNA is a subtype of single-stranded ncRNA that acts as a negative regulator of protein synthesis by shearing target mRNA [8, 9]. circRNAs are characterized by covalently closed loop structures that can modulate gene expression [8, 9]. lncRNA is a transcript that is 200 bp or longer, but has no significant protein-coding role [8–10].Although lncRNA does not have coding ability, it can drive tumor progression and shape metastatic phenotypes in testicular cancer, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, liver cancer and gastric cancer through mechanisms such as regulating signaling pathways, ferroptosis and the immune microenvironment [11–14]. Several studies have indicated that circRNAs and miRNAs are involved in different physiological and pathological TGCTs processes [15, 16]. As early as 2006, Voorhoeve et al. found that miRNA-372 and miRNA-373 can exert tumorigenic roles in TGCTs development by inhibiting the tumor suppressor gene LATS2 [17]. In other studies, lncRNAs and circRNAs were considered to have competitive roles in miRNA degradation and suppression [9]. However, studies on lncRNAs in TGCTs development are still lacking, and the biological processes and regulated mechanisms mediated by lncRNAs in TGCTs are still unknown. In recent years, with the rapid development of gene chip technology and high-throughput sequencing technology, a large amount of tumor-related data has accumulated, providing the possibility of searching for new tumor biomarkers and intervention targets. By analyzing TCGT sequencing data from the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) and Gene Ontology (GEO) databases, we found that LINC01272 was significantly upregulated in TGCTs tissues and was associated with the prognosis of TGCTs patients. This indicates that LINC01272 may be a potential diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for TGCTs. Consistent with this speculation, LINC01272 was reported to be associated with a series of different cancers, including breast cancer, lung squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), and glioblastoma (GBM) [18–20]. However, the role and molecular pathways of LINC01272 in TGCTs remain unclear.

Fused in sarcoma (FUS) is a highly disordered protein in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) [21]. It been reported that RNA could promote FUS-FUS oligomerization during ALS progression [22]. FUS is also a common RNA-binding protein (RBP), which is reported to bind to ncRNA [23]. For example, in diabetic kidney disease, circRNAs could facilitate the enrichment of FUS, finally leading to the activation of apoptotic signaling and enhancement of podocyte injury [24]. It has also been reported that FUS can modulate the proliferation, migration, and invasion of cancer cells by binding to circRNA [25, 26], FUS can interact with and cyclize circTBC1D14, which can promote tumor progression in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) [26]. FUS is also associated with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) cell proliferation and metastasis, suggesting a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for ESCC [27]. In our analysis, the data from the GEPIA database indicated that the expression of FUS was abnormal in TGCTs tissues, and TGCTs patients with high FUS levels had significantly decreased survival. However, the specific role of the FUS-mediated pathway in TGCTs development remains unclear.

While our prior work identified HAVCR2 as an immune-related prognostic marker in TGCTs [28], the role of non-coding RNA-mediated regulatory networks remains unexplored. Here, we focus on LINC01272, a lncRNA distinct from protein-coding HAVCR2, which mechanistically interacts with RNA-binding protein FUS to drive metastasis via EMT pathways.

In this study, we explored the regulatory mechanism of the LINC01272-FUS pathway in TGCTs. It is important to understand the process of TGCTs, which is valuable for the diagnosis and prognosis of TGCTs.

Methods

Testicular tumors and paracancerous tissue samples

Clinical samples were obtained from Hunan Cancer Hospital, following protocols described in our previous study on HAVCR2 [28]. Histopathological classification was independently verified by two pathologists.The cohort comprised 14 patients aged 21–52 years (median 35 years), including 11 with pure seminoma and 3 with non-seminomatous germ cell tumors. TNM staging showed pT1 disease in 11 cases and pT3 in 3 cases; all patients were N0 M0. No patient had received preoperative chemotherapy or radiotherapy (Supplementary Table 1).All tissue samples were obtained from fresh frozen tumors. All specimens were examined by the Department of Histopathology in this hospital, and histopathological types were confirmed. All patients provided written informed consent for tissue sample collection, authorized by the ethics committee of the Hunan Cancer Hospital.

Cell culture

TGCTs cell lines, Tcam-2 and NCCIT, were obtained from ATCC (Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in RPMI 1640 or DMEM (Sigma, MO, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, NY, USA). Cells were seeded at 1 × 10⁶ cells per 10 cm dish and maintained at 37 ℃ with 5% CO₂. They were passaged at a 1:3 split ratio every 48 h. Mycoplasma contamination was monitored by PCR once a month and confirmed negative.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNAs was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. qRT-PCR was conducted using the SYBR Green PCR kit (Transgen, Beijing, China), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The gene expression was normalized to that of GAPDH. Specific mRNA primers used are listed in Table 1. The data were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Cell transfection

LINC01272 and FUS siRNAs (Table 2) were provided by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). Transfection was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions with the following optimizations: siRNA was used at a final concentration of 50 nM, and Lipofectamine 2000 was mixed with siRNA at a 2:1 ratio (µL:µg). Briefly, siRNA and Lipofectamine 2000 were separately diluted in Opti-MEM for 5 min and then incubated together for 20 min. The mixture was added to the cultures and incubated for 48 h. FUS overexpression (OE) lentivirus was synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Cells were infected at an MOI of 10 for 72 h to achieve FUS overexpression.

Western blot

Briefly, whole-cell proteins were extracted and quantified with the BCA assay; 30 µg of protein per lane was loaded onto SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with the following primary antibodies: FUS (Abcam, ab23439, 1:500), beta-Actin(ProMab Biotechnologies, Catalog# 20270,1/10000). The next day, membranes were incubated for 1 h at room temperature with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG. Immunoreactive bands were visualized with ECL and quantified by integrated density using ImageJ.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion assays were performed using the transwell assay. Briefly, cells were plated in the upper chamber. The lower chamber contained either RPMI 1640 or DMEM alone. After culturing for 24 h, the upper chamber containing cells that were transferred to the lower membrane was fixed with methanol, stained with crystal violet, and imaged under a microscope. For the invasion assay, the upper chamber membrane was covered with matrigel before cell plating.

Bioinformatics tools

The expression of LINC01272 was analyzed using GEPIA (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/) [28, 29] and GEO datasets (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds). The expression and prognostic relevance of LINC01272 were analyzed using KMP (http://kmplot.com) [30]. The lncRNA-protein interaction was performed using RBPDM datasets (http://rbpdb.ccbr.utoronto.ca/) [31] and RNAInter datasets (http://www.rnainter.org/) [32]. For RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), total RNAs was extracted and analyzed by BGI Genomics Co. (Shenzhen, China).

RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA quality and integrity were assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer system (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA), and only samples with an RNA Integrity Number (RIN) ≥ 7.0 were used for downstream analysis. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) was removed using the Ribo-Zero rRNA Removal Kit (Illumina, USA). Strand-specific RNA-seq libraries were constructed using the NEBNext® Ultra™ Directional RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina® (NEB, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Library concentration and size distribution were evaluated using the Qubit 3.0 fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, respectively. Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform with 150-bp paired-end reads by BGI Genomics Co. (Shenzhen, China), generating approximately 20 million clean reads per sample.

Raw reads were filtered using fastp (v0.23.2) to remove low-quality reads and adapter sequences. Clean reads were aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh38) using HISAT2 (v2.2.1). Gene-level quantification was performed using featureCounts (v2.0.3) with the GENCODE v38 annotation. Differential expression analysis was conducted using DESeq2 (v1.36.0) with |log2 fold change| ≥ 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05 as thresholds. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed using the plotPCA function in DESeq2. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was carried out using clusterProfiler (v4.4.4), with p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, the cells were incubated in lysis buffer. Following DNase I treatment, the cell lysates were incubated with FUS or IgG antibodies. Next, protein A/G beads were incubated with the lysates to recruit the RNA-protein complex. RNA associated with FUS was extracted using TRIzol reagent and further analyzed by qRT-PCR.

CCK-8 assay

The CCK-8 assay was performed according to the manufacturer instructions. Briefly, cell suspensions were placed in 96-well plates to calculate survival rates after different treatments. The cell suspension was incubated with 10 µL of CCK-8 solution at 37 °C for 2 h. Finally, absorbance was measured on a microplate reader at 450 nm.

Statistical analysis

The results were analyzed using the graphpad software (Aspire Software International, Leesburg, VA, USA). Means ± S.E. were calculated from at least three independent experiments and compared using analysis of variance (ANOVA). All statistical tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Bioinformatics analysis based on GEPIA and GEO datasets showed that LINC01272 expression was significantly upregulated in TGCTs tissues (Fig. 1A, B). Further analysis also showed that LINC01272 expression positively correlated with the specificity and sensitivity of TGCTs diagnosis (Fig. 1C). To further verify the bioinformatics findings, samples from patients with TGCTs were collected and the expression of LINC01272 was detected by qRT-PCR. The results showed that LINC01272 expression was significantly upregulated in the TGCTs samples (Fig. 1D). LINC01272 silencing inhibits TGCTs tumorigenesis

It is speculated that LINC01272 is involved in the development of TGCTs. In this study, we investigated the function of LINC01272 in TGCTs. According to the GEO database, Tcam-2 and NCCIT cell lines expressed high LINC01272 levels and were selected for subsequent functional experiments (Fig. 3A). LINC01272 siRNAs were first transfected into the cells and could silence the expression of LINC01272 in both Tcam-2 and NCCIT cell lines (Fig. 3B, C). Subsequently, we used Transwell assays to detect changes in cell migration and invasion and found that LINC01272 silencing significantly inhibited the migration and invasion of both Tcam-2 and NCCIT cells (Fig. 3D-G). LINC01272 silencing influences the focal adhesion and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)

To further explore the LINC01272 mediated pathway in TGCTs, RNA was extracted and sequenced after LINC01272 silencing (Fig. 4A). PCA analysis of all samples revealed no overlap between the different clusters (Fig. 4B). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analyses further indicated that the enriched biological processes involved pentose and glucuronate interventions, apoptosis, and focal adhesion, etc. (Fig. 4C). Focal Adhesion and EMT are recognized as two important processes in the invasion and metastasis of tumor cells and the tumor budding process. Therefore, we further examined changes in some EMT markers, such as vimentin (VTN), laminin subunit alpha 1 (LAMA1), and recombinant mouse protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 12 B (PPP1R12B) (Fig. 4D). qRT-PCR results further indicated that VTN expression decreased and LAMA1 expression increased after LINC01272 silencing (Fig. 4E). Finally, western blot results showed that the expression of N-cadherin and VTN was downregulated after LINC01272 silencing in both Tcam-2 and NCCIT cell lines (Fig. 4F, G). FUS functions as a target of LINC01272

Given the reports that lncRNAs could affect protein stability and function by binding to proteins, we used the online tools RBPDB and RNAIntel to predict the potential binding protein of LINC01272.The results indicated that only three proteins (RBM4, KHBRDS3, and FUS) were predicted to bind to LINC01272 (Fig. 5A). FUS had the highest score and multiple binding sites, as indicated by the RBPDB datasets (Fig. 5B), suggesting that FUS may be an optimal binding protein of LINC01272. Interestingly, we found that the ALS (FUS is a highly disordered protein in ALS) pathway also changed significantly according to the GO enrichment analyses (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, we found that FUS was upregulated in TGCTs tissues using GEPIA datasets (Fig. 5C), and patients with high expression levels of FUS had lower RFS (Fig. 5D), suggesting that FUS may also have a tumor-promoting role in TGCTs. We used qPCR to verify the predictive results and found that FUS was upregulated in the tissues of patients with TGCTs (Fig. 5E). However, the expression levels of RBM4 and KHBRDS3 did not change significantly (Supplementary Fig. 1A). To directly verify that FUS is a binding protein of LINC01272, the RIP assay was used, which showed that a band was captured in the group immunoprecipitated with anti-FUS antibody compared with the IgG immunoprecipitated group (Fig. 5F). The co-precipitated RNA was subjected to qPCR for LINC01272. The results showed that LINC01123 was enriched in the group immunoprecipitated with the anti-FUS antibody compared with the IgG immunoprecipitated group (Fig. 5F). Finally, western blotting results showed that the expression of FUS was downregulated after LINC01123 silencing, indicating that FUS is a downstream molecule of LINC01123 (Fig. 5G, H). LINC01272 regulates TGCTs tumorigenesis through FUS

In this part, we first explored whether FUS has a regulatory role in TGCTs, using transwell experiments to detect the changes in cell migration and invasion, and found that FUS silencing significantly inhibited the migration and invasion of both Tcam-2 and NCCIT cells (Fig. 6A-D). These results indicated that FUS plays an important role in TGCTs cell viability. We then performed a rescue assay by treating cells with a combination of LINC01272 silencing and FUS-OE. We found that the decreased ability of TGCTs tumorigenesis after LINC01272 silencing, including decreased migration and invasion, could be rescued by FUS-OE (Fig. 6E-H). In addition, we performed a CCK-8 assay, which indicated that the decreased survival rate after LINC01272 and FUS silencing could be rescued by FUS OE (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Discussion

In this study, we first identified LINC01272 as a good diagnostic biomarker and demonstrated its promising prognostic value in TGCTs patients by bioinformatics analysis and clinical sample detection. We then conducted bioinformatics analysis combined with RIP experiments to determine whether FUS functions as a target of LINC01272 in TGCTs tissues. We also verified that LINC01272 and FUS silencing inhibited TGCTs cell migration, invasion, and proliferation. Taken together, our results clarify the regulatory role of the LINC01272-FUS pathway in the pathophysiology and pathogenesis of TGCTs. This study provides a theoretical basis for the development of potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets for TGCTs.

Increasing evidence has indicated that abnormal lncRNA expression is closely related to the progression of various cancers, including TGCTs [33, 34]. For example, LINC00673 overexpression promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion of TGCTs cells by inhibiting miR-150-5p [35]. Moreover, lncRNAPCAT7 can promote the progression of breast cancer by inhibiting apoptosis [18]. In our study, we used bioinformatics analysis and found that the expression of LINC01272 was significantly increased in TGCTs patients. Increased levels of LINC01272 were associated with poor prognosis of TGCTs, which is consistent with other reports that abnormal LINC01272 expression was found in LSCC [19]. In addition, it has been reported that LINC01272 promotes the migration and invasion of cancer cells through EMT and focal adhesion [36, 37]. In our study, we also found that LINC01272 influences focal adhesion and expression of EMT markers, such as N-cadherin and VTN. Consistent with our results, LINC01272 overexpression was also related to the progression of other cancers, such as lymph node metastasis [36]. LINC01272 knockout can inhibit the development of tumorigenesis [36], in the GBM model, LINC01272 knockdown could enhance the sensitivity to ferroptosis inducers and inhibit GBM, indicating that LINC01272 may be an important target for the prognosis and treatment of GBM [20]. In addition, LINC01272 is significantly upregulated in tissues from patients with other diseases, such as inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and may be a potential diagnostic biomarker for IBD [36]. It should be noted that some other reports have indicated that LINC01272 is downregulated in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients and may play an anti-cancer role in NSCLC [38]. Further studies have shown that LINC01272 inhibits cell proliferation and promotes apoptosis by upregulating miR-7-5p in NSCLC [19]. Further studies have shown that LINC01272 is specifically expressed in macrophages in NSCLC, suggesting that it may be a potential marker for immunotherapy prognosis [39]. Consistent with this study, LINC01272 was reported to be associated with myeloid pro-inflammation in pediatric crohn disease [40]. Overall, LINC01272 plays different roles in different types of cancer, and these differences may be related to the tumor microenvironment.

Previous studies have shown that lncRNA-protein interactions play important roles in cancer progression. For example, integrin beta-2 is upregulated in colorectal cancer (CRC) and positively interacts with overexpressed LINC01272, ultimately participating in CRC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [37]. Recent studies have also shown that circFndc3b can regulate VEGF expression through interaction with FUS, finally playing a protective role in myocardial infarction [41]. In cancer progression, the interaction between lncRNAs and RBP is necessary [42]. Therefore, we speculate that there may be an RBP-RNA network that regulates the development of TGCTs. Therefore, we used the RBPDB and RNAIntel datasets to analyze the potential proteins that can bind to LINC01272. Based on bioinformatics prediction and RIP analysis, we identified that FUS is an underlying binding target and can be upregulated by LINC01272 in TGCTs. In addition, the rescue assay also showed that the decreased ability of TGCTs tumorigenesis after LINC01272 silencing could be rescued by FUS OE, indicating that the LINC01272-FUS pathway is involved in TGCTs development. Interestingly, we found that the ALS (FUS is a highly disordered protein in ALS) pathway is also changed significantly in TGCG according to the GO enrichment analyses, which is consistent with bioinformatics prediction and RIP analysis. As a common RBP, FUS also has been reported to interact with lncRNAs in other tumors [43, 44]. However, some studies indicate that FUS can inhibit cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in some tumors, such as hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [45]. FUS has also been identified as an important regulator of cell apoptosis [46]. For example, FUS can bind to the pre-mRNA of circRNAs, such as SEMA5A pre-mRNA, and regulate the reverse splicing process of circRNAs in circRNA biogenesis, such as circ_00002099, finally promoting cancer cell death by activating the apoptotic pathway [47]. FUS is also highly expressed in gastric cancer (GC), and its inhibition can activate apoptosis and repress proliferation in GC cells [48]. In addition, it has been reported that FUS depletion could lead to dysregulation of gene transcription and RNA splicing in breast cancer, impeding entry into the S phase of the cell cycle, suggesting a potential therapeutic strategy for breast cancer therapy [49]. These reports and our findings indicate that FUS may exert pro-cancer or anti-cancer effects in a manner dependent on different contexts, such as by interacting with different cancer types [25]. Overall, the regulatory effects of FUS on different biological processes in various cancer types indicate its potential as a therapeutic target for various cancers.

In conclusion, our findings illustrate the regulatory mechanism of the LINC01272-FUS pathway in TGCTs development, providing new supplements and cues for tumorigenesis mechanisms in TGCTs. The specific mechanism by which LINC01272 reduces FUS protein levels (mRNA stability, translation, or protein degradation) has not been elucidated and is intended to be further elucidated experimentally.This study was a single-center retrospective cohort with limited sample size, and subsequent sample expansion and multicenter validation were required.

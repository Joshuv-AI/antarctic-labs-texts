Risk factors affecting cervical spondylotic myelopathy complicated with traumatic central cord syndrome and the efficacy of different treatment options
Authors: Yongwei Sun, Ping Li, Xin Jin, Kai Guan, Hongjun Huo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze the influencing factors and treatment options of cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) complicated with traumatic central cord syndrome (TCCS).MethodsA total of 243 patients with CSM admitted to our hospital from January 2021 to September 2022 were retrospectively analyzed, and then divided into the control group (n = 152) and the observation group (n = 91) according to the presence or absence of concurrent TCCS. The clinical data and imaging data of the two groups were compared, and multivariate logistic regression was used to analyze the influencing factors of CSM complicated with TCCS. Patients in the observation group were further divided into the zero notch anterior cervical interbody fusion device (Zero-P) group (n = 45) and the cervical spine locking plate (CSLP) group (n = 46) according to the treatment mode, and the perioperative indexes of the two groups were compared. The treatment effects were evaluated by the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) and the Japanese Orthopedic Association (JOA) before surgery, 1 week after surgery and 6 months after surgery. The height of intervertebral space and the cervical lordosis angle were measured.ResultsMultivariate logistic regression analysis showed that the injury mechanism (hyperextension injury), hand muscle weakness, cervical instability, age, degree of cervical spinal stenosis, degree of cervical spinal cord compression, and changes in intramedullary high signal were the risk factors, while the type of compression (soft), ASIA score and JOA score were the protective factor for CSM complicated with TCCS (P < 0.05). Patients in Zero-P group had much shorter operation time and hospitalization time than these in CSLP group (P < 0.05). The cervical lordosis angle and intervertebral space height at 1 week and 6 months after operation in the two groups were both largely higher than these before operation, and the cervical lordosis angle and intervertebral space height in the Zero-P group were significantly higher than these in the CSLP group one week after surgery (P < 0.05). The ASIA score and JOA score were obviously increased in the two groups 1 week and 6 months after surgery, and the ASIA score and JOA score in the Zero-P group were significantly higher than these in the CSLP group at 1 week after surgery (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe mechanism of TCCS in CSM is still controversial, which it is generally believed to be caused by cervical hyperextension injury. The clinical symptoms are diverse, and the treatment methods are also different. This study shows that the mechanism of injury, type of compression, hand muscle weakness, cervical instability, age, cervical stenosis compression, and intramedullary high signal changes are all risk factors for CSM complicated with TCCS. Early identification of risk factors and targeted interventions can effectively reduce the complicated rate of TCCS. Zero-P and CSLP surgical fixation have good efficacy in the treatment of TCCS, and there is little difference in efficacy between the two. However, Zero-P fixation surgery has the advantages of short operation time and fast postoperative recovery.Keywords: Spinal cord cervical spondylosis, Traumatic central cervical medullary syndrome, Risk factors, Preventive measures, Treatment

Introduction

Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) is a kind of spinal cord compression and ischemic dysfunction caused by the degeneration of continuous structure between the cervical vertebrae such as intervertebral disc herniation and ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament, which can lead to disability in severe cases, and the incidence rate of CSM accounts for about 15% of cervical spondylosis [1, 2]. With the change of work style in recent years, the incidence of CSM has increased year by year, and it shows a trend of younger people, which has a negative impact on the work and life of patients [3]. In the case of non-surgical treatment, 20%~60% of patients with CSM will have deterioration at 3 ~ 6 years of follow-up, 8% of patients with asymptomatic spinal cord compression will have symptoms of myelopathy at 1 year of follow-up, and 23% will reach 4 years of follow-up [4]. There is little evidence that non-surgical treatments are effective in suppressing or reversing the progression of myelopathy, and progression of the disease is associated with considerable detrimental consequences, such as reduced quality of life, significant functional impairment, and impaired surgical outcomes, as well as a higher risk of secondary spinal cord injury or central syndrome. Traumatic central cord syndrome (TCCS) is one of the common complications of CSM and it is the most common type of traumatic cervical spinal cord injury, accounting for about 70% of incomplete cervical spinal cord injury. TCCS is more common in patients with low-energy trauma such as sports injury and low fall injury, and often has the symptoms of limb motor nerve dysfunction. It is typical that the motor dysfunction of the upper limbs is more severe than that of the lower limbs, accompanied by varying degrees of sensory or sphincter dysfunction. Although the overall prognosis of the patients is good, many patients still have sequelae, such as intrinsic hand muscle weakness or atrophy, hand fine movement disorder, urinary dysfunction or walking instability, and severely reduced quality of life [5, 6]. The treatment and prognosis of TCCS are the focus of the current medical scholars, but there are few studies on the influencing factors and preventive measures of CSM complicated with TCCS. Cervical spine locking plate (CSLP) is a common clinical anterior cervical surgery, which has high stability and good safety, and it is easy to implant the plate and screw. It is widely accepted by people, but it has certain requirements for the operator’s operation. If the operation is not skilled, the plate may tilt.

With the development of tissue engineering and medical devices, a Zero-Section Anterior Cervical Interbody Fusion Device (Zero-P) has been designed and applied to TCCS surgery. Zero-P device is a new device derived from the previous anterior lumbar fixation and fusion system, including an integrated interbody fusion part and a fixation part composed of four screws. At present, Zero-P fusion cage has been widely used in surgical treatment of cervical degenerative diseases because of its simple operation, short operation time and good fusion effect [7].

In this study, A total of 243 patients with CSM admitted to our hospital from January 2021 to September 2022 were selected as the subjects, in order to analyze the risk factors, preventive measures and treatment strategies of CSM complicated with TCCS.

Materials and methods

General materials

A total of 243 patients with CSM admitted to our hospital from January 2021 to September 2022 were selected as the subjects, and the clinical data were retrospectively analyzed. Inclusion criteria: (1) All patients were diagnosed as CSM by clinical symptoms and imaging examination without surgical treatment; (2) The patient with history of low energy trauma; (3) The patient and his family members signed the informed consent form, had good compliance and could cooperate with the examination and treatment. Exclusion criteria: (1) The patient with high-energy trauma; (2) The patient complicated with dysfunction of important organs; (3) The patient with other types of cervical spinal cord injury after trauma. The data were analyzed according to PICOT in order to reduce the bias in this study, including P = patients undergoing surgery for CSM (243 cases), E = TCCS (91 cases), C = non-TCCS (152 cases), O = JOA score and AISA and T = 6 months. These subjects were divided into the control group (n = 152) and the observation group (n = 91) according to the presence or absence of TCCS. Patients in the observation group were further divided into the zero notch anterior cervical interbody fusion device (Zero-P) group (n = 45) and the anterior cervical plate fixation (CSLP) group (n = 46). This experimental operation was approved by the hospital Ethics Committee. General information selection was shown in Fig. 1.

Methods

Methods of treatment: Patients in both groups were given general anesthesia, tracheal intubation, shoulders elevated, head naturally tilted back, disinfection of the anterior neck operation area, laying sterile surgical towel, and routine anterior cervical incision before surgery. The patients in the Zero-P group were treated with Zero-P device fixation. After decompression, the Zero-P device was inserted in accordance with the size of the patient’s intervertebral space, and then 4 locking screws were inserted. The CSLP group was treated with CSLP fixation after decompression and interbody fusion cage placement, the appropriate anterior cervical titanium plate was given according to the length of the cervical spine, and 4 locking screws were placed for fixation. C-arm X-ray machine showed that the incision was rinsed and sutured layer by layer after the plate was firmly fixed, and the incision was placed with negative pressure drainage. After operation, anti-infection, anti-edema, nerve recovery and other drugs were given.

Outcome measures

Analysis of influencing factors of CSM complicated with TCCS: The data of the patients were compared, including gender, injury mechanism (internal hand muscle weakness, upper limb sensory disturbance, tendon hyperreflexia, proximal lower limb muscle weakness, and hypertonia), type of compression (soft, hard and mixed), imaging factors (C2-7 anterior convex angle, C2-7 sagittal axial distance, and cervical instability), the most severe segment of cervical spinal cord compression, cervical spinal cord compression range, age, body mass index (BMI), cervical spinal stenosis degree, cervical spinal cord compression degree, intramedullary high signal change, intramedullary high signal degree and other data. One-way analysis of variance (One-Way ANOVA) and multivariate logistic regression analysis were used to analyze the related factors of CSM complicated with TCCS.

Efficacy of treatment: the perioperative indexes were compared, such as the operation time, the amount of operation bleeding and the length of hospital stay. The efficacy was evaluated by the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) and the Japanese Orthopedic Association (JOA) before the operation, 1 week and 6 months after the operation respectively.

The operation time, intraoperative blood loss, length of hospital stay and other perioperative indicators were compared between the two groups. The American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) score and Japanese Orthopedic Association (JOA) score were used to evaluate the clinical outcomes before operation, 1 week and 6 months after operation. According to the ASIA score, the nerve segments included C5-S1, and each segment had 5 grades. The total score was 100 points, and the higher the score, the smaller the spinal cord injury. JOA score included motor, sensory and bladder function, and the higher the score, the better the recovery of patients. Three-dimensional CT reconstruction was performed at 1 week and 6 months after operation. The height of intervertebral space was measured: the distance from the lower end plate of the head vertebral body to the upper end plate of the tail vertebral body of the fusion segment, and the average height of the front and rear edges. The anterior convex angle of the cervical spine was the angle from the lower end plate of C2 to the lower end plate of C7 through the lateral neutral film of the cervical spine.

Statistical analysis

SPSS20.0 software was used to analyze the experimental data. The measurement data were expressed in (‾x ± s), and were compared by t-test. Analysis of variance (F test) was used for comparison between multiple samples. When the conditions of variance analysis could not be met, the data were analyzed by non-parametric test (Kruskal-Wallis), and SNK-q test was used for pairwise comparison. Enumeration data were shown as (%), and compared using χ 2 test. Multivariate logistic regression was used to analyze the related factors of CSM complicated with TCCS. P < 0.05 indicated that the statistical results were statistically significant.

Discussion

Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) is a kind of cervical spondylosis characterized by spinal cord compression injury, which is mostly seen in middle-aged and elderly people. Previous studies have shown that CSM is a progressive disease, and surgery should be performed as soon as possible after diagnosis to improve the prognosis [8, 9]. Some CSM patients with severe symptoms who refuse surgery may lead to TCCS after experiencing accidents such as low fall, resulting in upper limb motor nerve disorders caused by hemorrhage and necrosis of gray matter near the central canal. Although the overall prognosis is good, the patient still has fine hand movement disorder and walking instability [10, 11]. Therefore, early evaluation of the risk factors of CSM complicated with TCCS and effective preventive measures may help to reduce the incidence of TCCS.

With the increase of age, the compensatory and self-repair functions of the spinal cord decrease. When CSM patients suffer from hyperextension of the cervical spine due to low-energy injury, further stenosis of the spinal canal occurs, which induces TCCS. Therefore, age is a risk factor that increases the risk of TCCS [12]. Early studies have shown that TCCS is a spinal cord injury caused by cervical hyperextension in patients with spinal stenosis. Hyperextension exercise can shorten the length of cervical spinal cord, and overfolding of the ligamentum flavum can induce increased posterior compression of cervical spinal cord. Anterior longitudinal or posterior longitudinal ligament rupture and protrusion of the nucleus pulposus causes anterior compression. The higher the degree of cervical spinal canal stenosis and cervical spinal cord compression, the higher the risk of TCCS [13, 14]. The study [15] has shown that during cervical hyperextension, the intervertebral foramen reduces by 25%, the cross-sectional area of the spinal canal decreases by 11-16%, the cross-sectional area of the spinal cord increases by 9-17%, and the ligamentum flavum can break into the spinal canal by 3.25–3.50 mm. The flexion type injury is mainly caused by posterior ligament, facet joint or intervertebral disc injury, which causes interbody instability and nerve injury. The probability of TCCS in hyperextension injury is higher than that in flexion injury, which may be caused by the stability of anterior/middle column injury than that of posterior column injury [16]. Patients with CSM may have early symptoms such as limb muscle weakness, glove-like numbness in the hands, and muscle weakness at the proximal end of the lower extremity, but there is no correlation with the occurrence of TCCS. When there are symptoms such as inability to complete fine hand movements or weakness of internal hand muscles, the risk of TCCS is greatly increased, and the symptoms are more serious [17]. Cervical instability may also increase the risk of TCCS. Cervical spine instability may occur when ossification, calcification and ligament calcification of the anterior edge of the vertebral body occur, and the dynamic spinal cord compression of instability can induce TCCS [18]. The intramedullary high signal changes can indicate the degree of parenchymal cell edema, hyperplasia and necrosis of cervical spinal cord due to long-term compression. The higher the signal, the more serious the degree of cervical spinal cord compression, and the greater the risk of TCCS [19, 20]. The study [21] has shown that the degree of spinal stenosis and cervical cord compression in patients with CSM are significantly correlated with the incidence of traumatic cervical central cord syndrome, and negatively correlated with movement disorders. However, it has also been pointed out that the degree of spinal stenosis and spinal cord compression before injury is not necessarily related to the clinical symptoms of TCCS [22]. Multivariate logistic regression analysis further confirmed that the injury mechanism (hyperextension injury), hand muscle weakness, cervical instability, age, degree of cervical spinal stenosis, degree of cervical spinal cord compression, and changes in intramedullary high signal were the risk factors, while the type of compression (soft) was the protective factor for CSM complicated with TCCS. Early assessment and preventive interventions can help reduce the risk of TCCS. MIYANJI et al. [23] also believed that the maximum degree of spinal canal stenosis and the degree of cervical spinal cord compression objectively reflected the degree of cervical spinal canal stenosis and spinal cord compression, and the greater the percentage value, the worse the prognosis.

TCCS is a special injury of cervical spinal cord caused by cervical trauma, which often occurs in patients with potential cervical spondylosis induced by hyperextension mechanism. Due to the severe degenerative disease of the cervical spine in the elderly, the cervical spinal canal has a smaller sagittal diameter, which makes the space within the cervical spinal canal narrow. Therefore, even minor trauma can produce significant clinical symptoms. In the early stage of TCCS, conservative treatment was mainly adopted. With the deepening of research, TCCS with cervical instability is one of the indications for surgical treatment, because for cervical instability, the recovery of conservative treatment is often poor. To determine the stability of traumatic cervical spine, conventional hyperextension and hyperflexion dynamic X-ray films may aggravate spinal cord injury. However, the early surgical treatment of TCCS is still controversial, and most studies believe that patients with obvious neurological symptoms should be treated with surgery. Zero-P and CSLP fixation are common clinical surgical methods, which can increase the stability of cervical spine, reduce swallowing discomfort and adjacent segment ossification [24, 25]. In this study, the operation time and hospital stay of Zero-P group were sharply shorter than these of CSLP group. The improvement in cervical lordosis, intervertebral space height, ASIA score and JOA score in Zero-P group was much better than that in CSLP group one week after operation, suggesting that the long-term efficacy of Zero-P and CSLP fixation in the treatment of TCCS was at a high level. However, the short-term effect of Zero-P fixation was better, and Zero-P fixation had more advantages in operation time and recovery speed. The analysis of the relevant reason is that the Zero-P cage is knocked into the vertebral body through the upper and lower metal inserts, riveting the cage in the adjacent intervertebral space, replacing the anterior plate fixation, which has good stability, reduces the complications of postoperative dysphagia, and avoids the related complications caused by plate and screw fixation.

In summary, the mechanism of TCCS in CSM is still controversial, which it is generally believed to be caused by cervical hyperextension injury. The clinical symptoms are diverse, and the treatment methods are also different. This study showed that the mechanism of injury, type of compression, hand muscle weakness, cervical instability, age, cervical stenosis compression, and intramedullary high signal changes were all risk factors for CSM complicated with TCCS. Early screening of risk factors and targeted interventions can effectively reduce the complication rate of TCCS. The efficacy of Zero-P and CSLP fixation in the treatment of TCCS is better, but the difference is not significant. Zero-P fixation has more advantages in operation time and recovery speed. However, the TCCS patients included in this study are all patients with severe clinical symptoms who need surgical treatment, and cannot represent patients with mild and moderate TCCS. The experimental results of risk factors may have a certain bias. In the future, we will expand the experimental subjects and increase the research time to conduct further exploration.

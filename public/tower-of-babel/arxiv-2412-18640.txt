A remark on the number of automorphisms of finite abelian groups
Marius Tărnăuceanu

Abstract
Let A ​ b 0 Ab_{0} be the class of finite abelian groups and consider the function f : A ​ b 0 ⟶ ( 0 , ∞ ) f:Ab_{0}\longrightarrow(0,\infty) given by f ⁡ ( G ) = | Aut ⁡ ( G ) | | G | f(G)=\frac{|{\rm Aut}(G)|}{|G|} , where Aut ⁡ ( G ) {\rm Aut}(G) is the automorphism group of a finite abelian group G G . In this short note, we prove that the image of f f is a dense set in [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) .

1
Introduction

A well-known question in group theory (see e.g. Problem 12.77 of
[
5
]
) asks whether it is true that
|G|
divides
|{\rm Aut}(G)|
for every nonabelian finite
p
-group
G
. This was answered in negative in
[
3
]
, where for each prime
p
there was constructed a family
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite
p
-groups such that
|{\rm Aut}(G_{n})|/|G_{n}|
tends to zero as
n
tends to infinity. By considering the function

f(G)=\frac{|{\rm Aut}(G)|}{|G|}

for all finite groups
G
, the above result means that zero is an accumulation point of the image of
f
. Note that a similar result holds for the function

f^{\prime}(G)=\frac{|{\rm Aut}(G)|}{\varphi(|G|)}\,,

where
\varphi
denotes the Euler’s totient function (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
). These constitute the starting point for our work.

Our main result shows that all nonnegative real numbers are accumulation points of the image of
f
, even if we restrict this function only to abelian groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

The set

{\rm Im}(f)=\{f(G)\mid G\in Ab_{0}\}

is dense in
[0,\infty)
.

The proof of Theorem 1.1 follows the same steps as the proofs of Theorem 1.1 in
[
4
]
. It is based on the next lemma which is a
consequence of Proposition outlined on p. 863 of
[
6
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=0
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}
is divergent. Then the set containing the sums of all finite subsequences of
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is dense in
[0,\infty)
.

It also uses the fact that the function
f
is multiplicative, that is

f(G_{1}\times G_{2})=f(G_{1})f(G_{2}),

for any finite groups
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
of coprime orders.

Finally, we formulate a natural open problem related to the above theorem.

Open problem.
Is it true that for every
a\in[0,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Q}
there is a finite (abelian) group
G
such that
f(G)=a
?

2
Proofs of the main results

First of all, we prove an auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.1
.

The set
{\rm Im}(f)\cap[0,1]
is dense in
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
and
p_{i}
be the
i
th prime number. Since
f
is multiplicative, we have

f\left(\prod_{i\in I}C_{p_{i}}\right)=\prod_{i\in I}f(C_{p_{i}})=\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}

and so

A=\left\{\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}\,\bigg|\,I\subset\mathbb{N},|I|<\infty,p_{i}=\text{$i$th prime number}\right\}\subseteq{\rm Im}(f)\cap[0,1].

Thus it suffices to prove that
A
is dense in
[0,1]
.

Consider the sequence
(x_{i})_{i\geq 1}\subset(0,\infty)
, where
x_{i}=\ln(\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1})
for all
i\geq 1
. Clearly,
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}x_{i}=0
. We have

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{x_{i}}{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}=1.

Therefore, since the series
\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
is divergent, we deduce that the series

\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}
is also divergent. So, all hypotheses of Lemma 1.2 are satisfied, implying that

\overline{\left\{\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\,\bigg|\,I\subset\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\right\}}=[0,\infty).

This means

\overline{\left\{\ln\left(\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}\right)\,\bigg|\,I\subset\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty,p_{i}=\text{$i$th prime number}\right\}}=[0,\infty)

or equivalently

\overline{\left\{\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}\,\bigg|\,I\subset\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty,p_{i}=\text{$i$th prime number}\right\}}=[1,\infty).

Then

\overline{\left\{\prod_{i\in I}\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}\,\bigg|\,I\subset\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty,p_{i}=\text{$i$th prime number}\right\}}=[0,1]

and consequently

\overline{A}=[0,1],

as desired.
∎

We remark that the conclusion of Lemma 2.1 remains valid if we restrict
f
to the class
Ab_{0}^{\prime}
of finite abelian groups of odd order, that is
{\rm Im}(f|_{Ab_{0}^{\prime}})\cap[0,1]
is also dense in
[0,1]
.

We are now able to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.
We have to prove that for every
a\in[0,\infty)
and every
\varepsilon>0
there is
G\in Ab_{0}
such that
f(G)\in(a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon)
.

If
a\in[0,1]
, this follows from Lemma 2.1. Assume now that
a\in(1,\infty)
. Since
{\rm Aut}(C_{2}^{n})\cong{\rm GL}_{n}(2)
has order
\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(2^{n}-2^{k})
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(C_{2}^{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(2^{n}-2^{k})=\infty

and so we can choose a finite elementary abelian
2
-group
G_{1}
such that
f(G_{1})=b>a
. Then
\frac{a}{b}\in(0,1)
. Let
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{b}
. By the above remark, there is a finite abelian group of odd order
G_{2}
with
f(G_{2})\in\left(\frac{a}{b}-\varepsilon_{1},\frac{a}{b}+\varepsilon_{1}\right)
. It follows that
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}\in Ab_{0}
and

f(G)=f(G_{1})f(G_{2})=bf(G_{2})\in(a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon).

This completes the proof.∎

Pointwise differentials for Distributions
Yu Tong

Abstract
The notion of pointwise differentials for distributions is a way to extract local information of distributions by rescaling the distribution at a point. In this paper, we study the pointwise differentials for distributions corresponding to a negative order Sobolev functions. Our main results prove Borel regularity, Lusin approximation, rectifiability, and a Rademacher theorem for these pointwise differentials.

(2)

For every
b\in Y
, the function
u(\cdot,b)
is continuous.

Then
u
is continuous.

Proof.

We have to show that
u
is continuous at every
(a,b)\in X\times Y
. Suppose
U
is a neighborhood of
u(a,b)=c
in
Z
and
\mathscr{U}
is the unifomity of
Z
.

Choose
V,V^{\prime}\in\mathscr{U}
with

Z\cap\{z:(z,c)\in V\}\subset U
and

(Z\times Z)\cap\{(a,b):(a,z),(z,b)\in V^{\prime}\quad\text{ for some $z\in Z$}\}\subset V.

By hypothesis, there exists a neighborhood
S
of
x
such that
\{u(x,\cdot):x\in S\}
is equicontinuous at
b\in Y
. Hence, there exists a neighborhood
W
of
b\in Y
such that,

\displaystyle(u(x,y),u(x,b))\in V^{\prime}\quad\text{for $x\in S$ and $y\in W$}.

Since
u(\cdot,b)
is continuous,

X\cap\{x:(u(x,b),u(a,b))\in V^{\prime}\}
is a neighborhood of
a
. Finally, for every
x\in S
,
y\in W
with
(u(x,b),u(a,b))\in V^{\prime}
, the point

(u(x,y),u(x,b))
and
(u(x,b),u(a,b))
belong to
V^{\prime}
, hence
(u(x,y),u(a,b))\in V
.
∎

A.4 Definition
.

Suppose
X
is a topological space. An
\overline{\mathbf{R}}
valued function
f:X\to\overline{\mathbf{R}}
is called
lower semicontinuous
if and only if

f^{-1}[\overline{\mathbf{R}}\cap\{t:t>\alpha\}]\subset X
is open for every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf{R}}
.
Moreover, the following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

The function
f
is lower semicontinuous.

(2)

The set
(X\times\mathbf{R})\cap\{(x,c):f(x)\leq c\}
is closed.

A.5 Definition
.

Suppose
X
is a topological space.

(1)

A topological space
X
is called
metrizable
topological space if and only if there exists a metric inducing the topology of
X
.

(2)

A topological space
X
is called a
Polish space
if and only if it is separable and metrizable by a complete metric.

(3)

A topological space
X
is called a
Lusin space
if and only if its topology is Hausdorff and is coarser than a Polish topology.

A.6
.

We will use the following proposition, which readily follows from
[
6
, 2.2.10]
:

If
X
is a Lusin space,
Y
is a Hausdorff space,
f:X\to Y
is a continuous map,
f|A
is injective, and
A\subset X
is a Borel set, then
f(A)
is a Borel set.

Numerical Computations of Entanglement Measures in Curved Space
Suresh Govindarajan, Sreehari A Padinhareveettil, Raghotham A Kulkarni

Abstract
We numerically compute the entanglement entropy and negativity for scalar fields and abelian gauge fields in a variety of situations. These extend computations of Srednicki to situations involving curved space. We discretize space in a covariant way. Finally, we compare some of our results with those obtained via the heat kernel coefficients.



1
Introduction

The works of Bombelli et al. and Srednicki
PhysRevD.34.373
;
Srednicki:1993im
studied the entanglement entropy of quantum fields. While the focus of Bombelli et al. was on the contribution to the black hole entropy, Srednicki performed the calculation for fields in flat space and showed that the entanglement entropy followed the area law, i.e. it is proportional to the area of the entangling surface. In Srednicki’s work, this was taken to be a sphere and the state considered was the ground state of the discretized field inside the sphere. Srednicki also noted the importance of his result in the context of black hole entropy and these results found great importance in the subsequent works of Page on black hole evaporation
Page:1993up
;
Page:1993df
;
Page:1993wv
.

The analytical method that we have to compute the entanglement entropy is the replica trick. While this is supposed to be an easy alternative to computing the log of the density matrix, constructing the replica manifold is difficult and the calculation can be done exactly only in some simple cases, such as the one for two-dimensional conformal field theories which was performed by Cardy and Calabrese
Calabrese:2012ew
. However, the numerical method can be adapted and used in various backgrounds and there are many works that have explored such directions
Casini:2015dsg
;
Lohmayer:2009sq
;
Benedetti:2019uej
;
Boutivas:2024lts
;
Boutivas:2025rdf
. This is what the bulk of this work also deals with. Specifically, we use the method to study entanglement entropy in AdS spacetime for spherical entangling surfaces. We also do the same for fields inside the RT surface
Ryu:2006bv
;
Ryu:2006ef
in AdS
3
. The entanglement entropy of bulk fields in AdS is of great interest in AdS/CFT because it contributes to the total entanglement entropy of the subsystem of the dual CFT. This is explored in various proposals such as the FLM formula, quantum extremal surfaces and islands
Faulkner:2013ana
;
Engelhardt:2014gca
;
Almheiri:2019hni
;
Almheiri:2019yqk
. We further extend the numerical method to calculate the logarithmic negativity, once again, working with the ground state. This is made possible by the fact that the negativity takes a simple form in terms of the eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix when the state is pure. Specifically, this is simply given by the Renyi entropy of order
1/2
. While we focus on numerical calculations for the bulk of this work, we look at one analytic tool, namely the heat kernel method
Fursaev:1995ef
;
Solodukhin:2011gn
;
Nishioka:2018khk
, which gives the entanglement entropy as a series of divergent terms containing the UV cut-off of the theory. Out of these divergent terms, there is one universal term which is logarithmically divergent in the UV cut-off in even spacetime dimensions and constant in odd spacetime dimensions. In the case of even dimensions, the heat kernel method allows us to analytically calculate the coefficient of the universal term. While we know that the leading order divergent term obeys the area law, its coefficient is scheme-dependent and does not mean anything as such.

In this work, we calculate the entanglement entropy for scalar and vector fields in global AdS
4
and within RT surfaces in AdS
3
using the numerical method of Srednicki. In each of the above cases, we find the suitable basis for the fields such that we can integrate the coordinates from the Hamiltonian except the one which we will discretize. From the results of the calculation, we confirm the expected area law behaviour. For scalar field in AdS
4
, we see that the entanglement entropy has a dependence of the AdS radius
L
. We also apply the same technique to compute logarithmic negativity in 2+1 dimensions and see that the result is proportional to the area. This calculation cannot be done in higher dimensions because of a divergence arising from the angular coordinates. This is also true for entanglement entropy in higher dimensions
Srednicki:1993im
.
We also compute the coefficient of the universal term in the entanglement entropy of scalar field in global
AdS_{4}
and confirm that the known flat space value is recovered in the
L\rightarrow\infty
limit.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we look at some coordinate systems for AdS, which we will use later in the calculations. In Section 3, we summarize Srednicki’s method of numerical calculation and extend the calculations in the presence of mass. In section 4, we calculate the entanglement entropy for both scalar and vector fields in a hyperspherical surface in AdS
4
. For the calculation for vector fields, we use a version of vector spherical harmonics suitable for AdS
4
. In section 5, we study the entanglement entropy of scalar and vector fields inside an RT surface in AdS
3
. In section 6, we use the same method to calculate the entropy for a scalar fields in dS
4
. In section 7, we show how Srednicki’s method can be used to calculate logarithmic negativity and demonstrate it for fields in 2+1 dimensions, in flat space and in AdS
3
for fields inside the RT surface. In section 8, we review the heat kernel method and use it to calculate the leading order behaviour of the enganglement entropy and reconfirm our numerical results. We also calculate the coefficient of the universal term in the case of even spacetime dimensions.

During the completion of this work, the authors became aware of a work along similar lines
Boutivas:2025ksp
. One important difference in method that we have used is covariant spacing, or using the proper distance as the variable to be discretized. We have also tried to apply the calculations to vector fields in different backgrounds. Moreover, while we have not pushed the numerical precision so as to obtain the coefficients of subleading terms, we have done these calculations explicitly using the heat kernel method.

2
Coordinates for AdS
4

The four dimensional Anti de Sitter spacetime can be embedded in a
\mathbb{R}^{2,3}
:

-(T_{1})^{2}-(T_{2})^{2}+(X_{1})^{2}+(X_{2})^{2}+(X_{3})^{2}=-L^{2}

(1)

The metric is:

ds^{2}=-dT_{1}^{2}-dT_{2}^{2}+dX_{1}^{2}+dX_{2}^{2}+dX_{3}^{2}

(2)

2.1
Global Coordinates

The global coordinates for AdS are defined as follows:

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r^{2}+L^{2}}\,\text{cos}\Big(\frac{t}{L}\Big)

(3)

\displaystyle T_{2}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r^{2}+L^{2}}\,\text{sin}\Big(\frac{t}{L}\Big)

(4)

\displaystyle X_{3}=

\displaystyle\ r\,\text{cos}\phi

(5)

\displaystyle X_{1}=

\displaystyle\ r\,\text{sin}\phi\,\text{cos}\theta

(6)

\displaystyle X_{2}=

\displaystyle\ r\,\text{sin}\phi\,\text{sin}\theta

(7)

Here, the radial coordinate takes the values
r\in[0,\infty)
, and the hyperspherical coordinates on
S^{d-1}
take the values
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
,
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. We see that there are closed timelike curves in the coordinates defined above. We avoid this by passing into the universal covering space. In these coordinates, the metric takes the form:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}\Big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\Big)+\frac{dr^{2}}{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}

(8)

The boundary of the spacetime is at
r=\infty
, where
ds^{2}\approx-r^{2}\,dt^{2}+r^{2}\,d\Omega_{2}^{2}
, which corresponds to the geometry of
S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.

2.2
Foliation using geodesics

Another set of coordinates that are particularly useful for the calculation of the entanglement entropy of fields inside RT surfaces are:

\displaystyle\eta\ =\

\displaystyle L\,\text{sinh}^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{r\text{cos}\phi}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}\text{sin}^{2}}\phi}\Bigg)

(9)

\displaystyle x\ =\

\displaystyle L\,\text{sinh}^{-1}(r\,\text{sin}\phi)

(10)

Both
\eta
and
x
can take values
(-\infty,\infty)
. We keep the
t
in global coordinates unmodified. The metric then becomes

ds^{2}=-\ \text{cosh}^{2}(x/L)\text{cosh}^{2}(\eta/L)dt^{2}+\text{cosh}^{2}(x/L)d\eta^{2}+dx^{2}

(11)

Finally, we use dimensionless coordinates
\tilde{t}=\frac{t}{L}
,
\tilde{\eta}=\frac{\eta}{L}
and
\tilde{x}=\frac{x}{L}
.

ds^{2}=-\ L^{2}\cosh^{2}\tilde{x}\,\cosh^{2}\tilde{\eta}\,d\tilde{t}^{2}+L^{2}\cosh^{2}\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{\eta}^{2}+L^{2}d\tilde{x}^{2}

(12)

This metric can also be derived from the following definitions in terms of the coordinates of the four-dimensional embedding space:

\begin{split}X_{0}=&L\,\cosh\tilde{x}\,\cosh\tilde{\eta}\,\sin\tilde{t}\\
X_{1}=&L\,\cosh\tilde{x}\,\cosh\tilde{\eta}\,\cos\tilde{t}\\
X_{2}=&L\,\cosh\tilde{x}\,\sinh\tilde{\eta}\\
X_{3}=&L\,\sinh\tilde{x}\end{split}

(13)

Then, the metric can be derived from:

ds^{2}=-dX_{0}^{2}-dX_{1}^{2}+dX_{2}^{2}+dX_{3}^{2}.

(14)

Constant
\eta
surfaces are geodesics (RT surfaces) (Figure
1
). These coordinates are closely related to AdS-Rindler coordinares. In the AdS-Rindler coordinates,
\tilde{\eta}
is treated as a parameter, not a coordinate) that determines the size of the Rindler patch and takes values in the range
[0,\infty)
. It is related to the size of an RT surface of angular size
\theta
by

\cosh\tilde{\eta}=\frac{1}{\sin\big(\frac{\theta}{2}\big)}

(15)

AdS-Rindler coordinates and their relation with the coordinates above are explained in detail in appendix
A
.

t

\eta
and
x

L
is taken to be 1 here. Constant
\eta
surfaces (red) are RT surfaces. Both
\eta
and
x
can take values from
-\infty
to
\infty
.

3
Area Law of Entanglement Entropy

3.1
Entanglement Entropy of Scalar Fields in 3+1 d flat spacetime

Srednicki showed through a numerical computation that the entanglement entropy of scalar fields inside a spherical surface in flat space is proportional to the area of the surface
(
Srednicki:1993im
)
. Consider two coupled harmonic oscillators with the Hamiltonian:

H=\frac{1}{2}\Big[p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+k_{0}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})+k_{1}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\Big]

(16)

Changing to a new basis,
x_{\pm}=\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}
, the Hamiltonian can be written as:

H=\frac{1}{2}\Big[p_{+}^{2}+p_{-}^{2}+k_{0}x_{+}^{2}+(k_{0}+2k_{1})x_{-}^{2}\Big]

(17)

The ground state wavefunction is

\psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=\pi^{-1/2}(\omega_{+}\omega_{-})^{1/4}\text{exp}[-(\omega_{+}x_{+}^{2}+\omega_{-}x_{-}^{2})],

(18)

where
\omega_{+}=\sqrt{k_{0}}
and
\omega_{-}=\sqrt{k_{0}+2k_{1}}
. Now we can write down the density matrix and integrate out one of the degrees of freedom to get the reduced density matrix.

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{out}}(x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int dx_{1}\psi_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})\psi_{0}^{*}(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime})

(19)

\displaystyle=\pi^{-1/2}(\gamma-\beta)^{1/2}\text{exp}[-\gamma(x_{2}^{2}+x_{2}^{\prime 2})/2+\beta x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}]

where
\beta=\frac{(\omega_{+}-\omega_{-})^{2}}{4(\omega_{+}+\omega_{-})}
and
\gamma-\beta=2\frac{\omega_{+}\omega_{-}}{\omega_{+}+\omega_{-}}
. Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the reduced density matrix are:

\displaystyle p_{n}=

\displaystyle(1-\xi)\xi^{n}

(20)

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)=

\displaystyle H_{n}(\alpha^{1/2}x)\text{exp}(-\alpha x^{2}/2)

(21)

such that

\int_{\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\rho_{\text{out}}(x,x^{\prime})f_{n}(x^{\prime})=p_{n}f_{n}(x).

(22)

H_{n}(\alpha^{1/2}x)
above denotes Hermite polynomials,
\alpha=(\gamma^{2}-\beta^{2})^{1/2}
and
\xi=\beta/(\gamma+\alpha
).
Now the von Neumann entropy can be calculated.

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}p_{n}\,\text{log}\,p_{n}

(23)

\displaystyle=-\text{log}(1-\xi)-\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}\text{log}\,\xi

This procedure can be generalized for a system of
N
oscillators with the Hamiltonian of the form

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}x_{i}K_{ij}x_{j}

(24)

The ground state is given by:

\psi_{0}(x_{1},...,x_{N})=\pi^{-N/4}(\text{det}\Omega)^{1/4}\text{exp}(-x^{T}\cdot\Omega\cdot x/2),

(25)

where
\Omega=K^{1/2}
. Writing
\Omega
as:

\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{pmatrix}

(26)

The quantities analogous to
\beta
and
\gamma
in the two oscillator case can now be written down as matrices in terms of the submatrices of
\Omega
.
We now consider scalar field in flat spacetime in 3+1 dimensions, inside a sphere of radius
r
. The solution for the equations of motion is of the form
\phi_{\ell m}(r)Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
. We integrate out the spherical coordinates. The field and conjugate momenta are redefined so as to write down the Hamiltonian in a form similar to (
24
).

H=\sum_{\ell m}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\bigg\{\pi_{\ell m}^{2}(r)+r^{2}\bigg[\frac{d}{dr}\bigg(\frac{\phi_{\ell m}(r)}{r}\bigg)\bigg]^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}\phi_{\ell m}^{2}(r)\bigg\}

(27)

We discretize the radial coordinate with spacing
a
so that
r_{\text{max}}=Na
. Set
\phi_{\ell m,j}=\phi_{\ell m}(ja)
and
\pi_{\ell m,j}=a\,\pi_{\ell m}(ja)
.

H_{\ell m}=\frac{1}{2a}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\bigg[\pi_{\ell m,j}^{2}+\bigg(j+\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{\phi_{\ell m,j}}{j}-\frac{\phi_{\ell m,j+1}}{j+1}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{j^{2}}\phi_{\ell m,j}^{2}\bigg]

(28)

In all the calculations that follow, the value of
a
is set to 1 unless specified otherwise. From equation (
28
), we see that the problem has now effectively become that of
N
coupled harmonic oscillators. By integrating over the degrees of freedom from
r=0
to
R=na
where
n<N
, we can find the entanglement entropy of fields within a sphere of radius
R
.
Summing over
m
gives a multiplicity of
2\ell+1
for each eigenvalue of the reduced density matrix. At large values of
\ell
, the eigenvalue
\xi_{\ell}
is of the form:

\xi_{\ell}(n)=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)^{2}}{64\ \ell^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}+O(\ell^{-6}),

(29)

and the entanglement entropy is:

S(n,\ell)\simeq\xi_{\ell}(n)\Big[-\text{log}\xi_{\ell}(n)+1\Big].

(30)

This shows that the infinite sum over
\ell
will converge. It is seen numerically that the entanglement entropy depends on
R^{2}
, which means that the entropy grows with area of the spherical surface. In the two coupled harmonic oscillator case, it is seen that the entanglement entropy is a function of
k_{1}/k_{0}
. Similarly, for large values of the index in this case, the qualitative behaviour of the entanglement entropy can be found from the Hamiltonian, which will be proportional to the ratio of the coefficients of the derivative term and the quadratic terms of the field. Thus, we can read off the behaviour of the entanglement to be proportional to
R^{2}
.The slope obtained from the numerical calculation for the entropy vs.
R^{2}
is
\kappa\sim 0.294
.

Figure 2:
Entanglement entropy of scalar field in a sphere in flat space time vs. radius. Entanglement entropy increases linearly with
R^{2}
.

The procedure can be extended to massive scalar fields by modifying the third term in (
28
) to
\Big(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{j^{2}}+\hat{m}^{2}\Big)\phi_{\ell m,j}^{2}
.

Figure 3:
Entanglement entropy vs.
R^{2}
for different masses. Addition of mass reduces the entanglement entropy.

It is seen that the entanglement entropy decreases with increase in mass, while the linear behaviour with respect to
R^{2}
is maintained (Figure
2
). The reason for the decrease of the entanglement entropy with increase in mass can be qualitatively understood by the increase in the value of the diagonal terms in the matrix
K
. The off-diagonal terms are indicative of entanglement. If the diagonal terms are much higher in magnitude in comparison, the entanglement entropy decreases. The entropy is now of the form (with
f(0)=1)
)

S=\kappa\,f(\hat{m})\,R^{2}

(31)

We tried fits for
f(\hat{m})
of the forms
\frac{1}{1+p\hat{m}^{b}}
and
ce^{-\hat{m}d}
(Figure
4
).

Fitting the dependence of entanglement entropy on mass (i)

with
p=0.6339,\ b=1.6649
, (ii)
ce^{-\hat{m}d}
with
c=0.9943,\ d=0.5013
.

The best-fit results for our entropy curve are:

S=\kappa\,\frac{r^{2}}{0.6339\,\hat{m}^{2}+1.6649}\ ,\hskip 28.45274ptS=\kappa\times 0.9943\,e^{-0.5013\,\hat{m}}r^{2}\ .

(32)

4
Entanglement Entropy in AdS
4

4.1
Entanglement Entropy of Scalar Field in AdS
4

Since we are working in curved space now, we have to carefully define the conjugate momentum and the canonical commutation relations.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be the Lagrangian density and
\pi(x,t)
be the conjugate momentum. We then make the following definitions (adoped from
Poisson:2009pwt
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\ =

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi(x)\partial_{\nu}\phi(x)

(33)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\ =

\displaystyle\ \frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L})}{\partial(\partial_{t}\phi(x))}

(34)

\displaystyle\Big[\phi(\vec{x},t),\pi(\vec{y},t)\Big]\ =

\displaystyle\ i\delta^{3}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})

(35)

Now we apply Srednicki’s procedure to scalar fields in global AdS
4
.

ds^{2}\ =\ -\Big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\Big)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\Big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\Big)}+r^{2}d\theta^{2}+r^{2}\text{sin}^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}

(36)

Due to the spherical symmetry of the metric, the solution for the wave equation is once again of the form
\phi_{\ell m}(r)Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
. The conjugate momentum is:

\pi=r^{2}\text{sin}\theta\frac{1}{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}\partial_{t}\phi

(37)

After integrating out the spherical part and rescaling the field and conjugate momenta as:

\pi_{\ell m}\rightarrow\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}}{r}\pi_{\ell m}\hskip 42.67912pt\phi_{\ell m}\rightarrow\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}}\phi_{\ell m}

(38)

the Hamiltonian is of the form:

\begin{split}H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell m}\int dr\Bigg[\pi_{\ell m}^{2}+r^{2}\Big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\Big)\Big(\partial_{r}\Big[\frac{1}{r}\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}\phi_{\ell m}\Big]\Big)^{2}\\
+\Big(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+m^{2}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\Big)\phi_{\ell m}^{2}\Bigg]\end{split}

(39)

Now we take the proper distance along the
r
direction to be the discretized variable. The effect of this choice is that the spacing of the divisions is different compared to discretizing
r
. The proper distance
u
and
r
are related as follows:

\displaystyle du\ =

\displaystyle\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}}

(40)

\displaystyle u\ =

\displaystyle L\ \text{sinh}^{-1}\Big(\frac{r}{L}\Big)

We rewrite the Hamiltonian in equation (
39
) in terms of
u
:

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell m}\int du\Bigg[\frac{\text{cosh}^{3}(\frac{u}{L})}{L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}(\frac{u}{L})}\pi_{\ell m}^{2}+L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\text{cosh}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)(\partial_{u}\phi_{\ell m})^{2}\\
+\Big(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}(\frac{u}{L})}+m^{2}\Big)L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\text{cosh}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\phi_{\ell m}^{2}\Bigg]

(41)

The rescaling of
\pi_{lm}
and
\phi_{lm}
are:

\pi_{\ell m}\rightarrow\frac{\text{cosh}^{3/2}(\frac{u}{L})}{L\text{sinh}(\frac{u}{L})}\pi_{\ell m}\hskip 42.67912pt\phi_{\ell m}\rightarrow\frac{L\text{sinh}(\frac{u}{L})}{\text{cosh}^{3/2}(\frac{u}{L})}\phi_{\ell m}

(42)

\begin{split}H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell m}\int du\Bigg[\pi_{\ell m}^{2}+L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\text{cosh}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\Bigg(\partial_{u}\Bigg\{\frac{\text{cosh}^{3/2}(\frac{u}{L})}{L\text{sinh}(\frac{u}{L})}\phi_{\ell m}\Bigg\}\Bigg)^{2}\\
+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{L^{2}\ \text{sinh}^{2}(\frac{u}{L})}\text{cosh}^{4}\Big(\frac{u}{L}\Big)\phi_{\ell m}^{2}\Bigg]\end{split}

(43)

We discretize
u
and compute the entanglement entropy for a given value of
u
till which we integrate out. The eigenvalue
\xi_{\ell}(n)
here for large values of
\ell
is of the form
\frac{L^{4}\ \text{sinh}^{4}(n/L)}{\ell^{2}(\ell+1)^{2}}
. Since the numerator increases much more rapidly than in the flat space case with increase in
n
, we need to push the summation much further in
\ell
to obtain good values. The entanglement entropy is found to be proportional to
R^{2}=L^{2}\text{sinh}^{2}(\frac{u}{L})
, with the slope depending on the AdS radius (Figure
5
). For increasing values of AdS radius, the slope rapidly approaches the flat space value, which is also expected as we should obtain the flat space results as
L\rightarrow\infty
. The dependence of
L
on the slope is most visible for very small values of
L
.

R^{2}
calculated from working with discretizing proper distance
u
in radial direction for different values of AdS radius. We see the area law, ie. entanglement entropy
\propto R^{2}
. We also observe the slope is a function of the AdS radius

.

The entanglement entropy now is of the form

S(r)=\kappa\,g(L)\ R^{2},

(44)

where
\kappa
is the flat space slope and
g(L)
is a function of

such that
\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}g(L)=1
.
To study the dependence of the slope on

, we tested two functions

of the forms:

and

. Fitting the data to determine the parameters, we obtained the values

,

for the first function and

for the second function(Figure

4.2
Entanglement Entropy of Gauge Fields in AdS
4

In global coordinates, the Lagrangian density is

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\sin\theta F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\ r^{2}\sin\theta\,(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})(\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu})

The conjugate momentum is

\displaystyle\pi^{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial(\partial_{0}A_{i})}(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L})

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{-g}F^{0i},

and we have the following canonical commutation relations:

[\pi^{i}({\bf{x}}),A_{j}({\bf{x^{\prime}}})]=i\delta^{i}_{j}\,\delta^{3}({\bf{x}-{\bf{x^{\prime}}}})

(47)

If we expand the gauge field and momentum in terms of scalar spherical harmonics and integrate out the angular coordinates, we have:

[\pi^{i}_{\ell m}(r),A_{j\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(r^{\prime})]=i\delta_{ij}\delta(r-r^{\prime})\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}

(48)

Next we write down the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=

\displaystyle\int drd\theta d\phi\Bigg[-\sqrt{-g}F^{0i}\partial_{0}A_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}(F^{0i}F_{0i}+\tfrac{1}{2}F^{ij}F_{ij})\Bigg]

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int drd\theta d\phi\frac{-\sqrt{-g}}{2}(F_{0i}F^{0i}-\tfrac{1}{2}F_{ij}F^{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int dr\frac{r^{2}}{2}(F_{0i\,\ell m}F^{0i}_{\ell m}-F_{ij\,\ell m}F^{ij}_{\ell m})

The indices in the equation take the values
r
,
\theta
and
\phi
. The calculation of gauge fields in flat space was performed in
Casini:2015dsg
using vector spherical harmonics. We extend this calculation to AdS
4
for a spherical entangling surface. Now we define vector spherical harmonics:

\displaystyle\bf{Y}_{\ell m}\ =\

\displaystyle Y_{\ell m}\bf{\hat{r}}

(50)

\displaystyle\bf{\Psi}_{\ell m}\ =\

\displaystyle\frac{r\nabla Y_{\ell m}}{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}

(51)

\displaystyle\bf{\Phi}_{\ell m}\ =\

\displaystyle\bf{\hat{r}}\times\bf{\Psi}_{\ell m}

(52)

Using the expression for gradient in spherical polar coordinates, (
51
) and (
52
) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle{\bf{\Psi}_{\ell m}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}\Big(\partial_{\theta}Y_{\ell m}\,\hat{\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}Y_{\ell m}\,\hat{\phi}\Big)

(53)

\displaystyle{\bf{\Phi}_{\ell m}}=

Entanglement Negativity

Given a bipartite state with density matrix
\rho
, the entanglement negativity is defined as
Vidal:2002zz
:

\mathcal{N}(\rho)\ =\ \frac{||\rho^{T_{A}}||_{1}-1}{2},

(123)

where
T_{A}
corresponds to partial transpose over one of the Hilbert space and
||A||_{1}=\text{Tr}\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
is the trace norm. Equivalently, negativity can be defined as the absolute sum of the negative eigenvalues of
\rho^{T_{A}}
. The logarithmic negativity is defined as:

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)\ =

\displaystyle\text{log}||\rho^{T_{A}}||_{1}

(124)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{log}(2\ \mathcal{N}(\rho)+1)

Let
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}|e_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle|e_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\rangle
denote the Schmidt decomposition of a pure state
|\psi\rangle
where
c_{\alpha}
’s are the Schmidt coefficients and
e_{\alpha}^{(i)}
’s are orthonormal basis vectors. Let
F
be the flip operator whose operation is defined by

\textbf{F}|e_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle|e_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}\rangle\ =\ |e_{\beta}^{\prime}\rangle|e_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\rangle

(125)

Let
C^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}|e_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rangle\langle e_{\alpha}^{\prime}|
and
C^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}|e_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle e_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}|
.
Then,

\displaystyle(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)^{T_{A}}\ =

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha}c_{\beta}|e_{\alpha}^{\prime}\otimes e_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle e_{\beta}^{\prime}\otimes e_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}|

(126)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{F}\ (C^{\prime}\otimes C^{\prime\prime})

F
is a unitary operator and so, the trace norm of
(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)^{T_{A}}
is just the trace norm of
C^{\prime}\otimes C^{\prime\prime}
which is
(\sigma_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})^{2}
. The negativity then can be written as
Vidal:2002zz
:

\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\frac{(\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})^{2}-1}{2},

(127)

and the logarithmic negativity is:

E_{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=2\,\text{log}\Big(\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\Big)

(128)

|c_{\alpha}|^{2}
’s are the eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix. The log negativity can be therefore written as
Calabrese:2012ew
:

E_{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=2\,\text{log}\,\text{Tr}\rho_{B}^{1/2}

(129)

where
\rho_{B}
is the reduced density matrix. Since we already have the eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix from (
20
), we can calculate the logarithmic negativity.

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\rho_{B}^{1/2}\ =

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\xi}\ \sum_{n}\xi^{n/2}

(130)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1+\sqrt{\xi}}{1-\sqrt{\xi}}\Bigg)^{1/2}

E_{\mathcal{N}}(\rho_{B})\ =\ \text{log}\Bigg(\frac{1+\sqrt{\xi}}{1-\sqrt{\xi}}\Bigg)

(131)

7.1

A feature of the method we are using is that the log negativity for a spherical entangling surface diverges in 3+1 dimensions. We already saw that when

, the eigenvalues are of the form

\xi_{l}(n)=\frac{g(n)}{l^{2}(l+1)^{2}}

The contribution towards log negativity for large values of

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}(\text{large}\ell)}=

\displaystyle(2\ell+1)\text{log}\Bigg[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+\sqrt{g}}{\ell(\ell+1)-\sqrt{g}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(2\ell+1)\frac{2\sqrt{g}}{\ell(\ell+1)}

A sum over terms of the form in (

factor, resulting in a fall-off of

for the log negativity as a function of

Heat Kernel Coefficients and the Universal Term

In a

;

\displaystyle S=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{d-2}\left(\tfrac{l}{\epsilon}\right)^{d-2}+a_{d-4}\left(\tfrac{l}{\epsilon}\right)^{d-4}+\dots+a_{1}\left(\tfrac{l}{\epsilon}\right)+(-1)^{\tfrac{d-1}{2}}c_{u}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)&\text{ for odd }d\\[3.0pt]
a_{d-2}\left(\frac{l}{\epsilon}\right)^{d-2}+a_{d-4}\left(\frac{l}{\epsilon}\right)^{d-4}+\dots+(-1)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}c_{u}\text{log}\left(\frac{l}{\epsilon}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})&\text{ for even }d\end{array}\right.

where
\epsilon
is the UV cut off of the theory. The second last terms in both lines are universal and their coefficient

contains information about conformal anomalies of the theory
Casini:2004bw
;
Solodukhin:2008dh
;
Solodukhin:2011gn
;

. Therefore, while other coefficients in particular do not mean anything,

8.1
Heal Kernel Expansion

The heat kernel method
Nishioka:2018khk
provides a systematic way to derive the entanglement entropy with all its subleading terms from the replica trick. The key to this method is calculating integrals of curvature invariants on a manifold with conical singularities
Dowker:1977zj
;
Dowker:1994nt
;
Fursaev:1994in
;
Fursaev:1995ef
;
Fursaev:2013fta

\mathcal{M}_{d-2}
.

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\sqrt{-g}\ \Big[(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}+m^{2}\phi^{2}\Big]

(136)

The entanglement entropy is calculated using the replica trick
Nishioka:2018khk
.

S=-\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}\partial_{n}(\text{log}Z_{n}-n\text{log}Z).

(137)

Because of the replica geometry, the Laplacian factors into:
\nabla^{2}=\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}^{2}+\nabla_{\mathcal{M}_{d-2}}^{2}

where
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is the 2 dimensional space with a conical singularity and
\mathcal{M}_{d-2}
is the rest of the spacetime.

ds_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}^{2}=dr^{2}+r^{2}d\tau^{2},

(138)

can be calculated by

\displaystyle\text{log}Z_{n}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\text{log\hskip 2.84526ptdet}(-\nabla^{2}+m^{2})

(139)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\text{tr\hskip 2.84526ptlog}(-\nabla^{2}+m^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\epsilon^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\text{tr}[e^{-s(-\nabla^{2}+m^{2})}-e^{-s}],

where
s
is the Schwinger parameter.
where
0\leq r
and
0\leq\tau\leq 2\pi n
. Using the eigenfunctions of
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}_{d-2}}^{2}
and
\nabla_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}^{2}
, entanglement entropy is calculated
Nishioka:2018khk
.

S=\frac{\pi\text{Vol}(\mathcal{M}_{d-2})}{3}\int_{\epsilon^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{e^{-sm^{2}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}

(140)

Given that equation (
139
) is of the form

\text{log}Z_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\epsilon^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s}\text{tr}K_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(s)e^{-sm^{2}},

(141)

the heat kernel operator
K_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(s)
has the expansion around
s=0
of the form

\text{tr}K_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(s)=\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{n})s^{i},

(142)

where
a_{i}
’s are the heat kernel coefficients, which can be calculated using quantities related to the curvature of the hypersurface we are interested in. In the
n\rightarrow 1
limit, the heat kernel coefficients have the expansion:

\displaystyle a_{i}\ =

\displaystyle\ a_{i}^{\text{bulk}}+(1-n)a_{i}^{\Sigma}+O((1-n)^{2})

(143)

\displaystyle a_{i}^{bulk}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\ =

\displaystyle\ n\,a_{i}^{bulk}(\mathcal{M}_{1})

(144)

The
a_{i}^{\Sigma}
from the contributions due to the conical singularity. Due to (
137
), the equation for the entanglement entropy will contain only
a_{i}^{\Sigma}
’s.

S=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{i}^{\Sigma}}{m^{2i-d}}\Gamma\Big(i-\frac{d}{2},m^{2}\epsilon^{2}\Big),

(145)

where
\Gamma(t,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}ds\,s^{t-1}e^{-s}
is the incomplete Gamma function. The expressions for the first few heat kernel coefficients are:

\displaystyle a_{0}^{\Sigma}\ =

\displaystyle\ 0

(146)

\displaystyle a_{1}^{\Sigma}\ =

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\pi}{3}\int_{\Sigma}1

(147)

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\Sigma}\ =

\displaystyle\ \frac{\pi}{9}\int_{\Sigma}\Bigg[\frac{1}{5}\Big(2\mathcal{R}_{abab}-\mathcal{R}_{aa}-2\mathcal{K}^{a}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{K}^{a\,\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{K}^{a\,\mu}_{\mu})^{2}\Big)+\mathcal{R}\Bigg],

(148)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{abab}

\displaystyle=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}n_{a}^{\mu}n_{b}^{\nu}n_{a}^{\rho}n_{b}^{\sigma}

(149)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{aa}

\displaystyle=R_{\mu\nu}n_{a}^{\mu}n_{a}^{\nu}.

(150)

\mathcal{K}^{a}_{\mu\nu}

K^{n}_{\mu\nu}=h^{\rho}_{\mu}h^{\sigma}_{\nu}\nabla_{\rho}n_{\sigma},

(151)

where

h_{\nu}^{\mu}=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}+n^{\mu}n_{\nu}

(152)

From (

and
a_{2}^{\Sigma}

It is also possible to compute the effect of mass on the entanglement entropy. To leading order, for (

\displaystyle S_{A}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{48\pi}Am^{2}\int_{m^{2}\epsilon^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{1-s^{2}}{s^{2}}

8.1.1

For a 2-sphere in four spacetime dimensions, there are two normals, one in the timelike direction and one in the radial direction. For the timelike normal vector, the extrinsic curvature vanishes. We call the radial normal vector

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\Sigma}\ =

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{45}\frac{2}{R^{2}}4\pi R^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{45}

\displaystyle a_{2}^{\Sigma}\int_{\epsilon^{2}}^{\infty}ds\,m^{2}\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{m^{2}\,s}\ =

\displaystyle\text{Log divergent term}+\frac{8\pi^{2}}{45}\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}2\text{log}\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{90}\text{log}\frac{1}{\epsilon}

9
Conclusion

We have performed numerical calculations of the entanglement entropy and logarithmic negativity for fields in differnt backgrounds, namely AdS
4
and AdS
3
. We see that the area-law behavior is most easily seen when the proper distance coordinate is discretized and used in the calculation. We have extended the use of vector spherical harmonics to global AdS
4
to calculate the entanglement entropy for gauge fields. Moreover, we have found the appropriate coordinates that will allow us to carry out these calculations for fields inside an RT surface in AdS
3
. While the calculation is more or less straightforward for scalar fields once we have the coordinates, we see that we need to make a very non-trivial gauge choice in order to integrate out the

using the heat kernel method. This coefficient contains an term that depends on
L
such that the coefficient reduces to the correct flat space value in the

limit.

Appendix C
Entanglement Negativity of Two Coupled Harmonic Oscillators

Here, we rigorously derive the entanglement negativity for a system of two coupled harmonic oscillators.
We recall that making a change of variables, we were able to rewrite the Hamiltonian of two coupled harmonic oscillators as that for two uncoupled oscillators with frequencies
k_{0}
and
k_{0}+2k_{1}
.

\omega_{+}^{2}=k_{0}\hskip 56.9055pt\omega_{-}^{2}=k_{0}+2k_{1}.

(183)

Thus the system decomposes into two independent oscillators.

For an oscillator of frequency
\nu
:

a(\nu)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\nu}}\left(\nu q+ip\right).

(184)

Hence,

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega}}(\omega x_{1}+ip_{1}),

(185)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega}}(\omega x_{2}+ip_{2}),

(186)

and for the relative mode:

a_{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{-}}}\left(\omega_{-}x_{-}+ip_{-}\right).

(187)

Substituting
x_{-}=\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}
and

p_{-}=\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}
and simplifying gives:

a_{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{-}}}{2\sqrt{\omega_{+}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{+}}}{2\sqrt{\omega_{-}}}\right)(a_{1}-a_{2})+\left(\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{-}}}{2\sqrt{\omega_{+}}}-\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{+}}}{2\sqrt{\omega_{-}}}\right)(a_{1}^{\dagger}-a_{2}^{\dagger})\right].

(188)

Define the squeezing parameter
r
:

\displaystyle\cosh r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{-}}{\omega_{+}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{+}}{\omega_{-}}}\right),

(189)

\displaystyle\sinh r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{-}}{\omega_{+}}}-\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{+}}{\omega_{-}}}\right),

(190)

so that

e^{2r}=\frac{\omega_{-}}{\omega_{+}}.

(191)

Then:

\boxed{a_{Y}=\cosh r\,\frac{a_{1}-a_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sinh r\,\frac{a_{1}^{\dagger}-a_{2}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}}}

(192)

The true vacuum satisfies:

a_{-}|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle=0.

(193)

The normalized solution is the two-mode squeezed vacuum:

|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\cosh r}\exp\!\left(\tanh r\,a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2}^{\dagger}\right)|{0}\rangle_{1}|{0}\rangle_{2}

(194)

Expanding:

|\Psi_{0}\rangle=\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}|n,|n\rangle,\qquad\lambda=\tanh r,

(195)

where
|n,m\rangle:=|n\rangle_{1}\,|m\rangle_{2}
.
The density matrix is

\rho=|{\Psi_{0}}\rangle\langle{\Psi_{0}}|.

(196)

Thus

\rho=(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n+m}|{n,n}\rangle\langle{m,m}|

(197)

This is a pure but entangled state.
We define the partial transpose with respect to subsystem
A
(oscillator 1):

\left(|{i}\rangle_{A}|{j}\rangle_{B}\langle{k}|_{A}\langle{l}|_{B}\right)^{T_{A}}=|{k}\rangle_{A}|{j}\rangle_{B}\langle{i}|_{A}\langle{l}|_{B}.

(198)

Applying this to
\rho
, we obtain

\rho^{T_{A}}=(1-\lambda^{2})\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n+m}|{m,n}\rangle\langle{n,m}|

(199)

Thus

\rho^{T_{A}}\,|n,m\rangle=\lambda_{n}\lambda_{m}|m,n\rangle,

(200)

where
\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\ \lambda^{n}
. Comparing with equation (
20
), we see that
\lambda^{2}
is the same quantity that we defined in section
3.1
as
\xi
. Thus, we have

\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{1-\xi}\ \xi^{\frac{n}{2}}\ .

(201)

Equation (
200
) shows that
(|n,m\rangle-|m,n\rangle)
will be eigenfunctions of
\rho^{T_{A}}
with eigenvalues
-\lambda_{n}\lambda_{m}
. These are the negative eigenvalues of
\rho^{T_{A}}
. Summing over the absolute values of these gives the negativity.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq m}\lambda_{n}\lambda_{m}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\Big(\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\Big)^{2}-\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}\Bigg]=\frac{\sqrt{\xi}}{1-\sqrt{\xi}}

From this we find the log negativity to be

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}}=\log(2\mathcal{N}+1)=\log\Bigg(\frac{1+\sqrt{\xi}}{1-\sqrt{\xi}}\Bigg)\ .

(202)

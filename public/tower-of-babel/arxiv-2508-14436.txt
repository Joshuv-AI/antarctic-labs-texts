Barnsley-Navascués fractal operators on Banach spaces on the Sierpiński gasket
Asheesh Kumar Yadav, Himanshu Kumar, Saurabh Verma, Bilel Selmi

Abstract
. In this article, we define fractal operators motivated by the works of Barnsley and Navascués on various function spaces such as energy space, Lebesgue space, and oscillation space on the well-known fractal domain Sierpiński gasket. We further explore the properties of these operators from the perspectives of operator and approximation theory.

2.
Fractal interpolation function on the Sierpiński gasket

We start with a brief description of the main ideas and a basic introduction to SG. The reader can look at
[
35
,
59
,
62
,
66
]
for more details. Let
\displaystyle V_{0}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
be the vertices of an equilateral triangle on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Define the maps
\displaystyle L_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+p_{i}),
for
\displaystyle i=1,2,3
. These three maps are contraction maps of the plane and form an IFS. The Sierpiński gasket is the attractor of this IFS:

SG=L_{1}(SG)\cup L_{2}(SG)\cup L_{3}(SG).

For a fixed
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
, define the composition
\displaystyle L_{i}=L_{i_{1}}\circ L_{i_{2}}\circ\dots\circ L_{i_{N}}
for any sequence
\displaystyle i=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{N})\in I^{N}:=\{1,2,3\}^{N}
. The set of points we get by applying these maps to the initial set
\displaystyle V_{0}
gives the
\displaystyle N
-th level vertex set
\displaystyle V_{N}
of
\displaystyle SG
. Now, let
\displaystyle B:V_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a given function. We find an IFS whose attractor is the graph of a continuous function
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle SG
such that
\displaystyle f|_{V_{N}}=B
.

For each
\displaystyle w\in I^{N}
, define the map
\displaystyle W_{w}:SG\times\mathbb{R}\to SG\times\mathbb{R}
by

W_{w}(x,z)=\Big(L_{w}(x),F_{w}(x,z)\Big).

where
\displaystyle F_{w}(x,z):SG\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is satisfying the following conditions:

|F_{w}(x,z_{1})-F_{w}(x,z_{2})|\leq c_{w}~|z_{1}-z_{2}|,

and

\displaystyle F_{w}(p_{j},B(p_{j}))=B(L_{w}(p_{j}))
for every
\displaystyle j\in I
with
\displaystyle c:=\max\limits_{w\in I^{N}}c_{w}<1
.

Accordingly, we define

F_{w}(x,z)=\alpha_{w}(x)z+q_{w}(x),

where
\displaystyle\alpha_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle q_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous functions. These are chosen so that

q_{w}(p_{i})=B(L_{w}(p_{i}))-\alpha_{w}(p_{i})B(p_{i}),

for each
\displaystyle i\in I
, and we also require
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\infty}:=\max\{\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\infty}:w\in I^{N}\}<1,\|q\|_{\infty}:=\max\{\|q_{w}\|_{\infty}:w\in I^{N}\}
.

Let
\displaystyle K=SG\times\mathbb{R}
. Then the collection
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}:=\{K;W_{w}:w\in I^{N}\}
forms an IFS on
\displaystyle K
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
62
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle B:V_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be given. The IFS
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\{K;W_{w}:w\in I^{N}\}
defined above produces a unique continuous function
\displaystyle g_{*}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle{{g_{*}}|}_{V_{N}}=B
and satisfies the following self-referential equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{*}(x)=\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))g_{*}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))+q_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\;\forall\;x\in L_{w}(SG),w\in I^{N}.

Navascués
[
46
,
47
]
invented the idea of
\displaystyle\alpha
-fractal functions, which are used in approximation theory and the construction of fractal Schauder bases in various function spaces. Following her work, for a given continuous function
\displaystyle f:SG\to\mathbb{R}
, if we consider the above functions
\displaystyle q_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
as

q_{w}(x)=f(L_{w}(x))-\alpha_{w}(x)b(x),

where
\displaystyle b
is a continuous function satisfying
\displaystyle b|_{V_{0}}=f|_{V_{0}}
. Then from the above theorem, there exists a unique continuous function
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}|_{V_{N}}=f|_{V_{N}}
and satisfies the following self-referential equation:

f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(x)=f(x)+\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\;\forall\;x\in L_{w}(SG),w\in I^{N}.

3.
Main Results

3.1.
Oscillation Spaces

First, we recall the oscillation spaces which were first introduced in
[
22
]
and further, extended and studied in
[
15
,
34
,
62
,
52
,
66
,
67
]
. These spaces help us to understand how a function behaves on smaller parts of the Sierpiński Gasket. They measure how much a function varies or oscillates at each level of refinement. These spaces contain continuous functions, and we control their smoothness in a special way. Because of these properties, oscillation spaces are useful for analyzing functions with complex or irregular patterns.

Let
\displaystyle g:SG\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function on
\displaystyle SG
. For any natural number
\displaystyle n
, define the total oscillation of order
\displaystyle n
by

R(n,g)=\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}}R_{g}[L_{w}(SG)],

where

R_{g}[L_{w}(SG)]=\sup\{|g(x_{1})-g(x_{2})|:x_{1},x_{2}\in L_{w}(SG)\}.

Now, for
\displaystyle 0<\beta\leq\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}
, define a new class of continuous functions on
\displaystyle SG
as follows:

\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG):=\left\{g:SG\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\bigg|~g\text{ ~is~ continuous ~and}~\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,g)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}<\infty\right\}.

It is known (see
[
62
, Theorem 4]
) that the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
, with the norm

\|g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}:=\|g\|_{\infty}+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,g)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}},

is a Banach space.

For a given
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
.define a function
\displaystyle q_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
as

q_{w}(x)=f(L_{w}(x))-\alpha_{w}(x)b(x)\quad\text{ for all }w\in I^{N},

where
\displaystyle b\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
is a base function that agrees with
\displaystyle f
on the set
\displaystyle V_{0}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle b|_{V_{0}}=f|_{V_{0}}
. Using item
\displaystyle 3
of
[
62
, Lemma 1]
, this function
\displaystyle q_{w}
also belongs to
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
. Now, suppose the following condition holds:

\max\Bigg\{\|\alpha\|_{\infty}+\frac{3^{n}}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\sup_{w\in I^{n}}\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(m,\alpha_{w})}{2^{m\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}},\frac{3^{n}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\Bigg\}<1.

Then, by
[
62
, Theorem 5]
, there exists a unique function
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
such that:

•

\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}
agrees with
\displaystyle f
on the vertex set
\displaystyle V_{N}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}|_{V_{N}}=f|_{V_{N}}
, and

•

it satisfies the self-referential equation:

f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(x)=f(x)+\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\;

for all
\displaystyle x\in L_{w}(SG)
, and for all
\displaystyle w\in I^{N}
.

Now, let
\displaystyle T:\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)\to\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
be a bounded linear operator such that
\displaystyle(Tg)|_{V_{0}}=g|_{V_{0}}
for any
\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
. if we consider the base function
\displaystyle b=Tf
, then we can define a map
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}:\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)\to\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
by

\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)=f^{\alpha}_{N,b}=f^{\alpha}_{N,T}.

This map
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is a bounded linear operator. Following the terminology of Navascués, we call this operator a fractal operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
.

Now, let us explore the various properties of this operator.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
16
, Lemma 1]
Let
\displaystyle(X,\|.\|)
be a Banach space,
\displaystyle T:X\rightarrow X
be a linear operator. Suppose there exist constants
\displaystyle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1)
such that

\|Tx-x\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx\|,\quad\forall x\in X.

Then
\displaystyle T
is a topological isomorphism, and

\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\|x\|\leq\left\|T^{-1}x\right\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|x\|,\quad\forall x\in X.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle Id
be the identity operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
. Denote

\|\alpha\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{\left\|\alpha_{w}\right\|_{\infty}:w\in I^{N}\right\}~\text{and}~\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}=\max\left\{\left\|\alpha_{w}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}:w\in I^{N}\right\}.

If
\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}<\frac{1}{2^{N\beta}}
, then the following statements hold:

(1)

Let
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
be an arbitrary. Then the perturbation error is of the form:

\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\frac{2^{N\beta}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}{1-2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}\|f-Tf\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}.

Note that, for
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}=0
or
\displaystyle T=I
, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}=Id.

(2)

Under the uniform norm on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\beta}(SG)
, the fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is a bounded linear operator. Moreover, the operator norm holds

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|\leq 1+\frac{2^{N\beta}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|Id-T\|}{1-2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}.

(3)

If
\displaystyle(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|<\frac{1}{2^{N\beta}}
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is bounded below. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is injective.

(4)

If
\displaystyle(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|<\frac{1}{2^{N\beta}}
, then the inverse of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
exists which is also bounded and therefore a topological automorphism. Furthermore,

\frac{1-(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}{1+(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|}\leq\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{1+(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}{1-(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|}.

(5)

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\neq 0
then the fixed points of
\displaystyle T
are also the the fixed points of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
. If at least one
\displaystyle\alpha_{\omega}
is nowhere zero, then the fixed points of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
are also the fixed points of
\displaystyle T
.

(6)

If the point spectrum of
\displaystyle T
contains
\displaystyle 1
, then
\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\geq 1
.

(7)

The fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is not the compact operator in the case of
\displaystyle(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|<\frac{1}{2^{N\beta}}
.

(8)

If
\displaystyle(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\|T\|<\frac{1}{2^{N\beta}}
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is Fredholm and its index is
\displaystyle 0.

Proof.

(1)

It follows from the self-referential equation that

f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(x)-f(x)=\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\;\forall\;x\in L_{w}(SG),w\in I^{N}.

We have

\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}=\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}.

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(x)-f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|\left|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}.

Which implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f-b\right\|_{\infty}.

(3)

Now, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\infty}.

(4)

For
\displaystyle n>N
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}}\sup\Big\{|(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{\sigma}(x_{1}))-(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{\sigma}(x_{2}))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N},\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup\Big\{|(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{1})))-(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{2})))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N},\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in SG}\Big\{|\alpha_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))-\alpha_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))|\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup\Big\{|((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))-((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N},\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup\Big\{|(\alpha_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))-(\alpha_{w}(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\Biggl(\sum_{\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup\Big\{|((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))-((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\{1,2,3\}^{n-N}}\sup\Big\{|((f-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{1}))-((f-b)(L_{\tau}(x_{2}))|:x_{1},x_{2}\in SG\Big\}\Biggl)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}R(n-N,\alpha_{w})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\Biggl(R(n-N,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)+R(n-N,f-b)\Biggl)+\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}R(n-N,\alpha_{w}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\frac{R(n-N,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)+R(n-N,f-b)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}+\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}\frac{R(n-N,\alpha_{w})}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\frac{R(n-N,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)+R(n-N,f-b)}{2^{(n-N)\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}+\frac{\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}\frac{R(n-N,\alpha_{w})}{2^{(n-N)\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\leq\frac{3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{\mathcal{A}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,f-Tf)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}+\frac{\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-Tf\right\|_{\infty}}{\mathcal{A}}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,\alpha_{w})}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}},

where

It follows that

(2)

By using the first assertion, we have

\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\frac{2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}{1-2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}\|f-Tf\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}.

or

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\frac{2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}{1-2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}.

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}\leq\left[1+\frac{2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\left\|Id-T\right\|}{1-2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})}\right]\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}.

Which implies that

By performing the similar calculation in part
\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\leq\frac{3^{N}\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-Tf)}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}+\frac{\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-Tf\right\|_{\infty}}{2^{N\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}\sum_{w\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{R(n,\alpha_{w})}{2^{n\left(\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}-\beta\right)}}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is bounded below.

(4)

We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f-\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2^{N\beta}(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}})\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)-Tf\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

for
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(h^{*})=h^{*}

3.2.
Energy space on SG

Start with the vertex set
\displaystyle V_{0}
, and define
\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}
as the complete graph on
\displaystyle V_{0}
(every pair of vertices is connected). Suppose we have construct the graph
\displaystyle\Lambda_{n-1}
with vertex set
\displaystyle V_{n-1}
for some
\displaystyle n\geq 1
. Then, we define the graph
\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}
on
\displaystyle V_{n}
as follows: for any
\displaystyle x,y\in V_{n},
we say
\displaystyle x\sim_{n}y
if and only if
\displaystyle x=L_{i}(x_{1}),y=L_{i}(y_{1})
with
\displaystyle x_{1}\sim_{n-1}y_{1}
and
\displaystyle i\in I
. In other words,
\displaystyle x\sim_{n}y
if and only if there exists
\displaystyle i\in I^{n}
such that
\displaystyle x,y\in L_{i}(V_{0})
. For each level
\displaystyle n=0,1,2,\dots,
define the graph energy of a function
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}
as

\mathcal{E}_{n}(f):=\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{n}\sum_{x\sim_{n}y}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}.

This energy measures how “smooth”
\displaystyle f
is on the graph. The energy at level
\displaystyle n-1
is the smallest possible energy among all extensions of
\displaystyle f
from
\displaystyle V_{n-1}
to
\displaystyle V_{n}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(f)=\min\{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\tilde{f}):\tilde{f}|_{V_{n-1}}=f\}.

As
\displaystyle n
increases, the sequence
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)\}
is non-decreasing. The energy of
\displaystyle f
on the entire vertex set
\displaystyle V_{*}:=\cup_{m=0}^{\infty}V_{m}
is defined as

\mathcal{E}(f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n}(f).

if
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{C}(SG)
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f)<\infty
, then we called
\displaystyle f
has finite energy. Such functions are uniformly continuous on
\displaystyle V_{*}
, and since
\displaystyle V_{*}
is dense in
\displaystyle SG
, they extend uniquely to continuous functions on
\displaystyle SG
.

If a continuous function
\displaystyle f
satisfies
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(f)=\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)
for all
\displaystyle n\geq 1,
then we call
\displaystyle f
is a harmonic function on
\displaystyle SG
.

Finally, define the energy space as

\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})=\{g\in\mathcal{C}(SG):\mathcal{E}(g)<\infty\}.

From the reference
[
59
, Theorem
\displaystyle 1.4.2
]
, the set
\displaystyle\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
, equipped with the norm

\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}:=\|g\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(g)},

is a Banach space.

Let
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
be a given function. Define a function
\displaystyle q_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
as

q_{w}(x)=f(L_{w}(x))-\alpha_{w}(x)b(x),

where
\displaystyle b\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
is another function such that
\displaystyle b
and
\displaystyle f
agrees on vertex set
\displaystyle V_{0}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle b|_{V_{0}}=f|_{V_{0}}
.
If we assume that
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5^{N}}},
then from
[
62

, there exists a unique function
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
such that:

•

\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}|_{V_{N}}=f|_{V_{N}},
and

•

it satisfies the self-referential equation:

f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(x)=f(x)+\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x)),

for all
\displaystyle x\in L_{w}(SG)

Now, a take bounded linear operator
\displaystyle T:\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})\to\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle(Tg)|_{V_{0}}=g|_{V_{0}}
for any function
\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}:\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})\to\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})

Now, let us explore some important properties of this operator.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle Id
be the identity operator on
\displaystyle\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
. Denote

\|\alpha\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{\left\|\alpha_{w}\right\|_{\infty}:w\in I^{N}\right\},\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}=\max\left\{\left\|\alpha_{w}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}:w\in I^{N}\right\}

\text{and}~~~~\mathcal{E}(\alpha)=\max\{\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{w}):w\in I^{N}\}.

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\mathcal{E}(\alpha)<\frac{1}{5^{N}4}
, then the following statements hold:

(1)

Let
\displaystyle f\in\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
be an arbitrary. Then the perturbation error is of the form:

\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\max\{A,B\}\|f-Tf\|_{\mathcal{E}},

where

A=\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}+\frac{(1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty})^{-1}2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{(\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}-2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}})}

and

B=\frac{\sqrt{5^{N}}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}-2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}.

Note that, for
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}=0
or
\displaystyle T=I
, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}=Id.

(2)

Under the uniform norm on
\displaystyle\text{dom}(\mathcal{E})
, the fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is a bounded linear operator. Moreover, the operator norm holds

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|\leq 1+\max\{A,B\}\left\|Id-T\right\|.

(3)

If
\displaystyle\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)<\|T\|^{-1}
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is bounded below. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is injective.

(4)

If
\displaystyle\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)<\min\{1,\|T\|^{-1}\}
then the inverse of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
exists which is also bounded and therefore a topological automorphism. Furthermore,

\frac{1-\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)}{1+\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)\|T\|}\leq\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{1+\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)}{1-\left(\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}+2\sqrt{2\cdot 5^{N}}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{E}}\right)\|T\|}.

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\alpha_{\omega}

\displaystyle T
.

(7)

The fractal operator

(8)

Proof.

(1)

Using the self-referential equation we have that

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(x)-f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|

(5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|\left|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty},

which gives that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}\left\|f-b\right\|_{\infty}.

So, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\infty}.

(6)

For
\displaystyle m>N

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}_{m}(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{m}\sum_{x\sim_{m}y}\Big((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(x)-(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)(y)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\sqrt{5^{N}}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(f-Tf)}+\frac{2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2}\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-Tf\right\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\sqrt{{\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{w})}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}+\frac{2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\right)\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\sqrt{5^{N}}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(f-Tf)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}{\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\max\left\{\left(\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}+\frac{2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\right),\frac{\sqrt{5^{N}}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\Big(\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(f-Tf)}\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\max\{A,B\}\|f-Tf\|_{\mathcal{E}},

where

A=\frac{\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}}+\frac{(1-\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty})^{-1}2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}}{\left(\sqrt{1-5^{N}4\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}-2\sqrt{5^{N}}\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}(\alpha)}}\right)}

and

(2)

By the first assertion, we know that

\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\max\{A,B\}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}

and

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\max\{A,B\}\left\|f-Tf\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

It follows from this that

which implies that

\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|\leq 1+\max\{A,B\}\left\|Id-T\right\|.

(3)

Assume that

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{m}\sum_{x\sim_{m}y}\Big((f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{w}^{-1}(y))\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\left\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{m}\sum_{x\sim_{m}y}\Big(\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(y))\Big)^{2}

3.4.
Lebesgue spaces on SG

Next, we now define a function space called the
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}
space on
\displaystyle SG
, which contains all the functions that are
\displaystyle q
-integrable functions on
\displaystyle SG
.

First, recall that
\displaystyle V_{0}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
is the set of vertices of an equilateral triangle in the plane
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For each
\displaystyle i=1,2,3,
define the map

L_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x+p_{i}).

The collection of these three maps forms an IFS
\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\{\mathbb{R}^{2};L_{i},i=1,2,3\}
. The
\displaystyle SG
is the unique compact set (called the attractor of the IFS) that satisfies:

SG=\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}(SG).

And there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
supported on the
\displaystyle SG
such that

\nu_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}p_{i}\nu_{p}\circ L_{i}^{-1},

where
\displaystyle p=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
be a probability vector with
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}p_{i}=1
and
\displaystyle p_{i}>0
.

Now, we define the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
, which consists of all real-valued functions
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle SG
such that

\int_{SG}|f(x)|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)<\infty.

The space
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
becomes the Banach space with the norm

\|f\|_{q}=\bigg(\int_{SG}|f(x)|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Note 3.5
.

For
\displaystyle p\neq p^{\prime}
(which yields
\displaystyle\nu_{p}\neq\nu_{p^{\prime}}
), we can get

\text{neither}~\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p^{\prime}})\text{ nor }\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p}).

For example: Choose
\displaystyle p=(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3})
and
\displaystyle p^{\prime}=(\tfrac{1}{8},\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{1}{8})
two probability vectors. Recall that the IFS
\displaystyle\{SG;L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\}
consisting of similarity mappings satisfies the open set condition. Then, using
[
45
, Theorem
\displaystyle 2.2
]
, invariant measure
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
corresponding to
\displaystyle p
is
\displaystyle\nu_{p}=\mathcal{H}^{\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}}|_{SG}
. We know that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\nu_{p})=\dim_{H}(SG)=\displaystyle\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}
and

\dim_{H}(\nu_{p^{\prime}})=\displaystyle\frac{-\tfrac{1}{8}\log(8)+\tfrac{3}{4}\log(3/4)-\tfrac{1}{8}\log(8)}{-\tfrac{1}{8}\log(2)-\tfrac{3}{4}\log(2)-\tfrac{1}{8}\log(2)}=\displaystyle\frac{\tfrac{3}{4}\log(\frac{3}{4})-\frac{3}{4}\log(2)}{-\log(2)}\approx 1.0612781245<\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}.

Therefore, for
\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\nu_{p^{\prime}})<\gamma<\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}
there exists a set
\displaystyle D_{\gamma}\subset SG
such that
\displaystyle\dim_{H}D_{\gamma}=\gamma
and
\displaystyle\nu_{p^{\prime}}(D_{\gamma})>0
but
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(D_{\gamma})=0
. Now define a map

f^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if }x\in D_{\gamma}\\
\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is simple to prove that
\displaystyle f^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
but not in
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p^{\prime}})
.

Now for other part, from
[
45
, Theorem
\displaystyle 2.1
]
,
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
and
\displaystyle\nu_{p^{\prime}}
are mutually singular measures for
\displaystyle p\neq p^{\prime}
, that is, there exists a set
\displaystyle A\subset SG
such that
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(A)=1,\nu_{p^{\prime}}(A)=0
. Consider a partition
\displaystyle A=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
, where
\displaystyle A_{n}
are disjoint subsets of
\displaystyle A
with
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(A_{n})>0
.
Define,

g^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}n^{\frac{1}{q}},&\text{if }x\in A_{n}\\
0,&\text{if }x\in SG\setminus A.\end{cases}

Then,
\displaystyle\int_{SG}|g^{*}(x)|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n.\nu_{p}(A_{n})=\infty
and
\displaystyle\int_{SG}|g^{*}(x)|^{q}d\nu_{p^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n.\nu_{p^{\prime}}(A_{n})=0
.
Therefore,
\displaystyle g^{*}\notin\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
but
\displaystyle g^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p^{\prime}})
.
This completes our task.

For a given
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
, if we consider
\displaystyle q_{w}:SG\to\mathbb{R}
as

q_{w}(x)=f(L_{w}(x))-\alpha_{w}(x)b(x),

where
\displaystyle b\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
with
\displaystyle b|_{V_{0}}=f|_{V_{0}}
. Following the idea of
[
62
, Theorem 9]
, we prove our next result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
and
let
\displaystyle b\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)
and
\displaystyle\alpha_{w}\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)
for
\displaystyle w\in I^{n}
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}<1
. Then there exists a unique
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)
such that
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}|_{V_{N}}=f|_{V_{N}}
and satisfies the following self-referential equation:

f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(x)=f(x)+\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,b}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\;\forall\;x\in L_{w}(SG),w\in I^{N}.

Proof.

Let us define

\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})=\{f\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p}):f|_{V_{0}}=g_{*}|_{V_{0}}\}.

which means the set of all functions in
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
that agree with a fixed function
\displaystyle g_{*}
on the set
\displaystyle V_{0}
. It is easy to check that the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
is closed in
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p}).
Since
\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p}),\|.\|_{q})
is a Banach space, this implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
is also complete with respect with respect to same norm
\displaystyle\|.\|_{q}
.

Now, we define the RB operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
as follows

(\mathcal{T}g)(x)=\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))g(L_{w}^{-1}(x))+f(x)-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x)))\;\forall\;x\in L_{w}(SG),w\in I^{n}

This operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
is well-defined.
Let
\displaystyle f,g\in\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
. Then

\displaystyle\big|(\mathcal{T}f)(x)-(\mathcal{T}g)(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))g(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\Big|

\displaystyle=|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))~(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|

\displaystyle=|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|~|(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}|(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|.

This inequality holds for almost all (with respect to
\displaystyle\nu_{p}
)
\displaystyle x\in L_{w}(SG)
and
\displaystyle\forall~~w\in I^{n}.
Now, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{SG}\big|(\mathcal{T}f)(x)-(\mathcal{T}g)(x)\big|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\in I^{n}}\int_{L_{w}(SG)}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}|(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x).

By using the self-similarity of measure
\displaystyle\nu_{p}=\sum_{\sigma\in I^{N}}p_{\sigma}\nu_{p}\circ L_{\sigma}^{-1}
and
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{SG}\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}f)(x)-(\mathcal{T}g)(x)\big|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\in I^{N}}\int_{L_{w}(SG)}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}|(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|^{q}d\bigg(\sum_{\sigma\in I^{N}}p_{\sigma}\nu_{p}\circ L_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}\int_{L_{w}(SG)}|(f-g)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))|^{q}d(\nu_{p}\circ L_{w}^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}\int_{SG}|(f-g)(\tilde{x})|^{q}d\nu_{p}(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}\int_{SG}|(f-g)(\tilde{x})|^{q}d\nu_{p}(\tilde{x}).

Thus, we obtain

\|\mathcal{T}f-\mathcal{T}g\|_{q}\leq\Big(\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f-g\|_{q}.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{w\in I^{N}}p_{w}\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}<1
, we conclude that
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
is a contraction operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p}).
By the application of Banach contraction mapping principle,
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
has a unique fixed point, namely
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}^{*}(SG,\nu_{p})
. Therefore, we conclude that
\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,b}\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p}).
This completes the proof.
∎

Now, for a given bounded linear operator
\displaystyle T:\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)\to\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)
such that
\displaystyle(Tg)|{V_{0}}=g|{V_{0}},
if we consider the base function
\displaystyle b=Tf.
Then a map
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}:\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)\to\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG)
defined as
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}(f)=f^{\alpha}_{N,b}=f^{\alpha}_{N,T}
will be bounded linear operator. This map
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
, following Navascués’s terminology, we call a fractal operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG).

Now we collect several properties of this operator.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\displaystyle Id
be the identity operator on
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
. Denote

\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}=\max\left\{\big\|\alpha_{w}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}:w\in I^{N}\right\}.

If
\displaystyle\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}<1
, then the following statements hold:

(1)

Let
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
be an arbitrary. Then the perturbation error is of the form:

\big\|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-f\big\|_{q}\leq\frac{\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}}{1-\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}}\big\|f-Tf\big\|_{q}.

Note that, for
\displaystyle\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}=0
or
\displaystyle T=I
, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}=Id.

(2)

Under the uniform norm on
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{q}(SG,\nu_{p})
, the fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is a bounded linear operator. Moreover, the operator norm holds

\big\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\big\|\leq 1+\frac{\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}\big\|I-T\big\|}{1-\big\|\alpha\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}}.

(3)

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}<\|T\|^{-1}
then
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is bounded below. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is injective.

(4)

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}<\|T\|^{-1}
then the inverse of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
exists which is also bounded and therefore a topological automorphism. Furthermore,

\frac{1-\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}}{1+\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}\|T\|}\leq\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{1+\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}}{1-\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}\|T\|}.

(5)

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}\neq 0
then the fixed points of
\displaystyle T
are also the the fixed points of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
. If at least one
\displaystyle\alpha_{\omega}
is nowhere zero, then the fixed points of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
are also the fixed points of
\displaystyle T
.

(6)

If the point spectrum of
\displaystyle T
contains
\displaystyle 1
, then
\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}\right\|\geq 1
.

(7)

The fractal operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is not the compact operator in the case of
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}<\|T\|^{-1}
.

(8)

If
\displaystyle\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}<\|T\|^{-1}
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{N,T}
is Fredholm and its index is
\displaystyle 0.

Proof.

(1)

It follows from the self-referential equation that

f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(x)=f(x)+\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))

We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(x)-f(x)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))-\alpha_{w}(L_{w}^{-1}(x))b(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\alpha_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(SG)}^{q}\left|(f^{\alpha}_{N,T}-b)(L_{w}^{-1}(x))\right|^{q}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{SG}\big|

\displaystyle\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{N,T}(x)-f(x)\big|^{q}d\nu_{p}(x)

Concluding remarks and possible future aspects

We have studied several important properties of the fractal operator defined on function spaces. In the future, we plan to explore and describe isometries and surjective isometries on these spaces. We also aim to approximate quantities such as the spectral radius, numerical radius, and numerical range of these fractal operators. In addition to studying isometries of fractal operators, we also plan to explore other properties of the oscillation space, energy space, and Lebesgue spaces, such as whether they are separable, algebraically reflexive, and more, especially in the context of Banach spaces.

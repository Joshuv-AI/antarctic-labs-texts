Superball of Strings
Yoav Zigdon

Abstract
I solve the equations of the low-energy limit of string theory to obtain a solution corresponding to a microcanonical ensemble of highly-excited superstrings. This “Superball of Strings” is a static, spherically symmetric “fuzzball” of BPS strings with a size set by a random walk scaling. The solution can be embedded in string theory in a significant part of parameter space. While the solution does not constitute a Lorentzian interpretation for a Euclidean, horizonless solution by Chen, Maldacena, and Witten, a few connections are noted. A singular extremal black hole and the Superball of Strings exist as Supergravity solutions with the same asymptotic boundary conditions; however, I argue that the latter describes generic BPS microstates.

This result sharpens the tension between the principles of unitary evolution, local interactions, and the equivalence principle.

String theory also admits solutions corresponding to bound states of strings and branes with no event horizons, which nonetheless share features with black hole solutions, where two examples are a large gravitational redshift for waves propagating from the region far away to the region near the surface of these states, and approximately common geometry far away from the respective objects. More specifically, the radiation is emitted from the surface of the bound states, rather than produced due to quantum fluctuations in the Hartle-Hawking vacuum. In general, the absence of an event horizon implies that there is no obstruction for information to pass from the region near the core of the bound state to the faraway observer. Additionally, unlike the black hole solution, the bound states in question are completely regular.

String theory may contain bound states of this kind that are 0-BPS, uncharged, and rotating, with compactness similar to that of the Kerr black hole, making them relevant to nature. Potential observational imprints of their existence or absence are “special” features in tiny variations in electromagnetic interference patterns, induced by gravitational waves emitted due to the collision of a binary of bound states, where the word “special” means that the features do not appear in the gravitational waveforms associated with the collision of two Kerr black holes.

2.3
“Superball of Strings” Solution

The goal of this subsection is to obtain a new supergravity solution corresponding to a microcanonical ensemble average of the DGHW string solutions. The microcanonical ensemble is a superselection sector of fixed, large charges
(n_{1},n_{p,1})
. The solution in question is computed by averaging the delta-functions appearing on the R.H.S. of Eqs. (
21
)-(
23
), over all BPS states in the superselection sector, each configuration is characterized by its own source profiles. Then, the equations governing the averaged
Z_{1},\omega
, and
\mathcal{F}
, Eqs. (
21
)-(
23
), are solved with the averaged sources.

The calculation of the expectation values of these
\delta
-functions,

\langle\delta^{4}(\vec{x}-\vec{X})\rangle=\langle N|\delta^{4}(\vec{x}-\vec{X})|N\rangle~,

(28)

is performed in a Hilbert space obtained by quantizing the classical phase space of solutions. This formalism makes averaging convenient; however, one could have performed the average without ever mentioning the quantization of the string (this point is re-emphasized below Eq. (
2.3
)).
The classical moduli space can be parametrized in terms of Fourier amplitudes of the embedding of the string

\displaystyle X_{j}(z,\bar{z})=x_{j}-\frac{i}{2}\alpha^{\prime}p_{j}\log(|z|^{2})

\displaystyle+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Big(a_{jm}\frac{1}{z^{m}}-a_{jm}^{*}z^{m}\Big)

\displaystyle~+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Big(\tilde{a}_{jm}\frac{1}{\bar{z}^{m}}-\tilde{a}_{jm}^{*}\bar{z}^{m}\Big)~.

(29)

The center-of-mass-position of the string is
x_{j}
, the center-of-mass-momentum of the string is
p_{j}
, the Fourier amplitudes of left-moving modes are
\{a_{jm},a_{jm}^{*}\}
, whereas Fourier amplitudes of right-moving modes are
\{\tilde{a}_{jm},\tilde{a}_{jm}^{*}\}
. Pure left-moving string profiles are characterized by
p_{j}=0,\tilde{a}_{jm}=0,\tilde{a}_{jm}^{*}=0
.
The coordinates
z,\bar{z}
on the worldsheet are related to target space coordinates via

z=e^{-i\frac{v\sqrt{2}}{R_{y}}}~,~\bar{z}=e^{-i\frac{u\sqrt{2}}{R_{y}}}~.

(30)

One can quantize the transverse excitations of the string by promoting the Fourier coefficients to operators satisfying a suitable algebra. Doing so allows one to write the quantum mode expansion as
[
27
]

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{j}(z,\bar{z})=\hat{x}_{j}-\frac{i}{2}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{p}_{j}\log(|z|^{2})

\displaystyle+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Big(\hat{a}_{jm}\frac{1}{z^{m}}-\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}z^{m}\Big)

\displaystyle~+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Big(\hat{\tilde{a}}_{jm}\frac{1}{\bar{z}^{m}}-\hat{\tilde{a}}_{jm}^{\dagger}\bar{z}^{m}\Big)~.

(31)

The notations
\hat{x}_{j}
,
\hat{p}_{j}
are center-of-mass positions and momenta,
\{\hat{a}_{jm},\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}\}
annihilate and create left-moving excitations, and
\{\hat{\tilde{a}}_{jm},\hat{\tilde{a}}_{jm}^{\dagger}\}
lower and raise right-moving excitations.
The Hilbert space is a direct sum of Fock spaces, associated to excitations about the vacuum, labeled by the transverse space dimension
j=1,...,4
, and the mode number
m=1,...,\infty
.
The Hamiltonian reads

\hat{L}_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{\prime}\hat{p}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{4}m\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{jm}\hat{a}_{jm}~.

(32)

Below, the degrees of freedom
x_{j}
are treated as classical variables and are set to
0
, because a quantum mechanical treatment of them in a microcanonical ensemble spreads the sources across the entire transverse space, since
\hat{x}_{j}
are zero modes. For example, if one asks what is the variance of
\hat{X}
in free string theory, including the zero mode, the result diverges. Similarly, the variables
p_{j}
are set to zero classically.

The expectation value of the delta-function operator in and of itself is ill-defined even in the absence of the zero modes, because it is a composite operator with a UV divergence. Therefore, a renormalization scheme is required to define it. One such renormalization scheme is normal ordering, which is adopted in the following. A discussion about the scheme-independence of the result in a particular limit appears below Eq. (
43

Now, an identity connecting the delta-function in position space to an integral in momentum space of an exponential is useful:

\displaystyle\langle:\delta^{4}\big(\vec{x}-\vec{X}\big):\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k~\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{X})}:\rangle~.

(33)

-2\pi i\tau

:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:
in the grand-canonical ensemble is

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\Big(e^{2\pi i\tau\hat{L}_{0}}:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\Big)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\Big(e^{2\pi i\tau\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{4}m\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{jm}\hat{a}_{jm}}:e^{\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}k_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}z^{m}-\hat{a}_{jm}\frac{1}{z^{m}}}{\sqrt{m}}}:\Big)~.

(34)

The last step involved a substitution of Eqs. (
2.3
) and (

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\Big(e^{2\pi i\tau\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{4}m\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{jm}\hat{a}_{jm}}e^{\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}k_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}z^{m}}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}k_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\hat{a}_{jm}\frac{1}{z^{m}}}\Big)~.

Next, one can use a coherent state basis

to evaluate the trace.

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)=

\displaystyle\prod_{jm}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha_{jm}}{\pi}\big\langle\alpha_{jm}|e^{2\pi i\tau\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{4}m\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{jm}\hat{a}_{jm}}e^{\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}k_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}z^{m}}{\sqrt{m}}}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}k_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\hat{a}_{jm}\frac{1}{z^{m}}}|\alpha_{jm}\big\rangle~.

(36)

Some of the properties of coherent states are briefly reviewed:
The relation between a coherent state and occupation number eigenstates is

|\alpha_{jm}\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\alpha_{jm}|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{jm}^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle~.

(37)

The inner product of two coherent states follows from Eq. (
37
), and is given by

\langle\alpha_{jm}|\beta_{jm}\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\alpha_{jm}-\beta_{jm}|^{2}}~.

(38)

In particular, the norm of the coherent state in our convention is unity.
The coherent states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator:

\hat{a}_{jm}|\alpha_{jm}\rangle=\alpha_{jm}|\alpha_{jm}\rangle~.

(39)

This equation also follows from Eq. (
37
).
When acting with an exponential of the number operator on a coherent state, and using Eq. (
37
), the result is

e^{iA\hat{a}_{jm}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{jm}}|\alpha_{jm}\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\alpha_{jm}|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{iA}\alpha_{jm})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\big(1-e^{iA}\big)|\alpha_{jm}|^{2}}|e^{iA}\alpha_{jm}\rangle~.

(40)

Utilizing the identities above, one can carry on with the computation of the expectation value of
:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:
as follows:

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=\prod_{j,m}\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha_{jm}}{\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})|\alpha_{jm}|^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2m}}k_{j}\big(e^{2\pi im\tau}\alpha_{jm}^{*}z^{m}-\alpha_{jm}z^{-m}\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\prod_{jm}\Big(1-e^{2\pi im\tau}\Big)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\prime}k^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi im\tau}}{m(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})}}~,

(41)

The following integral identity was used in the last step of Eq. (
2.3
):

\int\frac{d^{2}\alpha}{\pi}e^{-A|\alpha|^{2}+\beta\alpha+\gamma\alpha^{*}}=\frac{1}{A}e^{\frac{\beta\gamma}{A}}~.

(42)

Eq. (
2.3
) admits an additional interpretation as an average in the classical phase space, parametrized by the amplitudes
\alpha_{jm}
, with the measure
e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})|\alpha_{jm}|^{2}}
. For this reason, one need not invoke a quantum field theoretic language to perform the computation.
Below, the following notation for
2\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi im\tau}}{m(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})}
is adopted:

r_{b}^{2}(\tau)=2\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi im\tau}}{m(1-e^{2\pi im\tau})}~.

(43)

At this point, the scheme-dependence of the result is discussed. Suppose that instead of normal ordering, one would have introduced a UV cutoff on the worldsheet
\epsilon
. In this case, Eq. (
43
) receives an additional contribution proportional to
-\alpha^{\prime}\log(\epsilon)
. In a different scheme where one introduces a maximal mode number
m_{\text{max}}
, the additional contribution is proportional to
\alpha^{\prime}\log(m_{\text{max}})
. The main point is that in a limit where
\tau\to 0
first, the scaling of
r_{b}^{2}
is with
\tau^{-1}
in Eq. (
43
) washes out the additional logarithmic term. Thus, there is a mathematical limit that eliminates any dependence on the renormalization scheme.

The quantity
r_{b}
will be interpreted as the size of the “Superball of Strings” solution.
The grand-canonical expectation value of the normal-ordered exponential is expressible in terms of

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)

\displaystyle=Z(\tau)e^{-\frac{1}{4}r_{b}^{2}(\tau)k^{2}}~,

(44)

where

Z(\tau)=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{4}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\Big(1-e^{2\pi im\tau}\Big)}~.

(45)

It will shortly turn out that a description of a highly-excited string requires examining
Z(\tau)
at small
|\tau|
.
For small
|\tau|
, an approximation of
Z(\tau)
can be obtained from an identity of the Dedekind-eta function
\eta(-1/\tau)=(-i\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta(\tau)
. The approximation for
Z(\tau\approx 0)
is

Z(\tau\approx 0)\approx(-i\tau)^{2}e^{-\frac{\pi}{3i\tau}}~.

(46)

One can evaluate the entropy of the string at high-excitation level
N
by applying an inverse Laplace transform on
Z(\tau)
to extract the density of states:

\rho(N)=-\int_{C}d\tau e^{-2\pi iN\tau}Z(\tau)~.

(47)

The contour
C
is parallel to the imaginary axis, which is to the right of possible singularities of
Z(\tau)
.
Using Eq. (
46
), a saddle point-approximation of the integral in Eq. (
47
), which is valid for
N\gg 1
, ties
\tau
to
N
as

\tau_{*}\approx\frac{i}{\sqrt{6N}}~,~

(48)

and one observes that at large
N
, the following hierarchy applies:
|\tau_{*}|\ll 1
. The saddle-point value of the density of states is

\rho(N)\approx e^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}N}}~,

(49)

and the entropy is its logarithm:

S(N)\approx 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}N}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}n_{1}n_{p,1}}~.

(50)

This matches the Cardy formula with central charge
c=4
and left-moving excitation level
N
(with zero right-moving excitation level
N_{R}=0
.)

To evaluate
\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(N)
in a microcanonical ensemble of excitation level
N
, one applies an inverse Laplace transform on the grand-canonical result:

\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(N)=-\int_{C}d\tau e^{-2\pi iN\tau}\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(\tau)~.

(51)

The saddle-point approximation works in the present calculation as well; therefore, an approximated expression for
r_{b}^{2}(\tau)
is

\displaystyle r_{b}^{2}(\tau_{*})\approx-\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}}{2\pi i\tau_{*}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{2}}=-\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{6i\tau_{*}}\approx\alpha^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}N}~.

(52)

A recent paper that reached this scaling with
\sqrt{N}
using similar methods is
[
28
]
.
Using Eqs. (
4
) and (
52
), the scale
r_{b}
becomes

r_{b}=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}n_{1}n_{p,1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}~.

(53)

The length scale
r_{b}
admits a random walk interpretation, with each step of order of
\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
and the number of steps
\sqrt{n_{1}n_{p,1}}
. Back to the expectation value of the normal-ordered exponential, its saddle-point value is given in

\displaystyle\langle:e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{X}}:\rangle(N)

\displaystyle\approx\rho(N)e^{-\frac{1}{4}r_{b}^{2}(N)k^{2}}~.

(54)

For the purpose of averaging the Poisson equation of
Z_{1}
, Eq. (
21
), one can find the expectation value of the delta-function operator in the microcanonical ensemble. Utilizing Eqs. (
33
) and (
54
), and normalizing the result by dividing by the density of states, one obtains

\langle:\delta^{4}(\vec{x}-\vec{X}):\rangle(N)=n_{1}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}r_{b}^{2}(N)k^{2}}=\frac{n_{1}}{\pi^{2}r_{b}^{4}(N)}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}(N)}}~.

(55)

The averaged equation for
Z_{1}
reads

\nabla^{2}\bar{Z}_{1}=-\frac{4Q_{1}}{r_{b}^{4}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}~.

(56)

The solution is given in

\bar{Z}_{1}=1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}\Big)~.

(57)

Next, compute

\displaystyle\langle:\partial_{v}X_{i}\delta^{4}\big(\vec{x}-\vec{X}\big):\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k~\langle:\partial_{v}X_{i}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{X})}:\rangle=0~.

(58)

The last equality follows from the parity symmetry of the coherent state integrals. Then, the averaged
\omega_{i}
equation (
22
) implies zero
\bar{\omega}_{i}
:

\bar{\omega}_{i}=0~.

(59)

Finally, the expectation value of the normal-ordered operator
|\partial_{v}\vec{X}|^{2}\delta^{4}\big(\vec{x}-\vec{X}\big)
is

\displaystyle\langle:|\partial_{v}\vec{X}|^{2}\delta^{4}\big(\vec{x}-\vec{X}\big):\rangle(N)=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k~\langle:|\partial_{v}\vec{X}|^{2}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot(\vec{x}-\vec{X})}:\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}\langle:|\partial_{v}\vec{X}|^{2}:\rangle}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}k~e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}k^{2}r_{b}^{2}(N)}=\frac{2n_{p,1}}{\pi^{2}r_{b}^{4}(N)}e^{-\frac{|\vec{x}|^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}(N)}}~.

(60)

In the last step, the following equation was used:

n_{p,1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{n_{1}}\langle:|\partial_{v}\vec{X}_{n}|^{2}:\rangle~.

(61)

Hence, the averaged equation (
23
) is

\nabla^{2}\bar{\mathcal{F}}=-8\frac{Q_{P}}{r_{b}^{4}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}~,

(62)

and the solution reads

\mathcal{\bar{F}}=-\frac{2Q_{P}}{r^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}\Big)~.

(63)

Near the core of the solution, the string and momentum harmonic function approach

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{1}\to 1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r_{b}^{2}}~~,~~\bar{\mathcal{F}}\to-\frac{2Q_{P}}{r_{b}^{2}}~~.

(64)

The main results of this subsection are Eqs. (
57
),(
59
) and (
63
), which describe the “Superball of Strings” solution mathematically. The interpretation of the results is that of a static, spherically symmetric object charged under winding and momentum with respect to the
y
-circle. Moreover, the solution is horizon-free and smooth, with a characteristic size
r_{b}\gg\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
. There is more: If the hierarchy of scales
r_{b}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}
holds, then the geometry near the core is approximately

ds^{2}\approx-\frac{2r_{b}^{2}}{Q_{1}}dudv+\frac{2Q_{P}}{Q_{1}}dv^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{3}^{2}~,

(65)

which approximates the near-horizon geometry of the black hole as in Eq. (
9
). In particular, probes experience a high gravitational redshift of order
\frac{\sqrt{Q_{1}}}{r_{b}}
when propagating from infinity to the core (for the black hole solution, one replaces
r_{b}
by
0
, and the redshift diverges).
The next subsection examines whether the solution can be embedded in string theory and discusses aspects of the solution within various hierarchies of scales.

2.4
The “Superball of Strings” is Trustworthy

The purpose of this subsection is to verify whether the “Superball of Strings” solution is reliable in string theory by checking if (1) the string coupling is everywhere weak, (2) the spacetime curvature is everywhere small in string units, and (3) the
y
-circle is larger than the string scale. The conclusion will be that there exists a regime in parameter space
(g_{s},V_{4},R_{y},n_{1},n_{p,1})
for which the solution is reliable, which is specified below.

Consider the hierarchies

g_{s}\ll 1~,~\alpha^{\prime}\ll R_{y}^{2}~,~V_{4}\sim(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}~,

(66)

which permit a good effective field theory description in the asymptotics of the spacetime.

(1) The solution is weakly coupled throughout spacetime, with the maximal string coupling being
g_{s}
asymptotically. One can see this explicitly by substituting Eq. (
57
) into the dilaton in Eq. (
2
)

e^{2\Phi}=\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{\bar{Z}_{1}}=\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r^{2}}\Bigg(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}\Bigg)}\leq g_{s}^{2}\ll 1~.

(67)

(2) The Ricci scalar of the metric (
2
) reads

R=-\frac{7}{2}\frac{|\nabla Z_{1}|^{2}}{Z_{1}^{2}}+\frac{2}{Z_{1}}\nabla^{2}Z_{1}~.

(68)

For the “Superball of Strings” solution, the Ricci scalar is obtained by plugging Eq. (
57
) into Eq. (
68
), which gives

\displaystyle R=\frac{2A\Big(1-e^{-x^{2}}(1+x^{2})\Big)\Big(-2x^{3}-A(7+2x)+A(7+x(2+7x))e^{-x^{2}}\Big)}{r_{b}^{2}x^{2}\Big(A+x^{2}-Ae^{-x^{2}}\Big)^{2}}~,

(69)

where

x\equiv\frac{r}{r_{b}}~~,~~A\equiv\frac{Q_{1}}{r_{b}^{2}}~.

(70)

Several limits of
R(A,x)
are taken below to check whether stringy curvature could occur in the spacetime manifold.
When
x\to\infty
with fixed
A
,

R(A,x\to\infty)\approx-\frac{4A}{r_{b}^{2}x^{3}}\to 0~.

(71)

When
x\to 0
with fixed
A
,

R(A,x\to 0)\approx-\frac{2Ax}{(1+A)r_{b}^{2}}\to 0~.

(72)

When
x=1
, the function
R(x=1,A)
receives a maximum at
A\to\infty
where

R(x=1,A\to\infty)\approx-\frac{4.1}{r_{b}^{2}}~,|R(x=1,A\to\infty)|\ll\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}~.

(73)

The last parametric inequality assumes
N=n_{1}n_{p,1}\gg 1
.
When
A\to\infty
, the function
R(x,A\to\infty)
receives a maximum at
x\approx 1.57
, where

R(x\approx 1.57,A\to\infty)\approx-\frac{5.3}{r_{b}^{2}}~,~|R(x\approx 1.57,A\to\infty)|\ll\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}~.

(74)

Also, an output of Mathematica for the numerical maximum of
r_{b}^{2}|R(A,x)|
across
A\geq 0,x\geq 0
is
x\approx 1.57
and a very large
A
. The conclusion is that the spacetime manifold in the presence of the strings is weakly curved for
n_{1}n_{p,1}\gg 1
.

(3) The proper size of the
y
-circle is related to the metric
y
-
y
component:

g_{yy}=\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}}{Z_{1}}~.

(75)

The question is whether the proper size of the
y
circle is always greater than the string scale, which is equivalent to

g_{yy}(t,y,\vec{x})\gg\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R_{y}^{2}}~~?

(76)

For the “Superball of Stings” solution, one substitutes Eqs. (
57
) and (
63
) into Eq. (
75
), which results in

g_{yy}=\frac{1+\frac{Q_{P}}{r^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}\Big)}{1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r^{2}}\Big(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b}^{2}}}\Big)}~.

(77)

Below, six hierarchies are considered that cover a large part of the parameter space:

1.

\sqrt{Q_{P}}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r_{b}
,

2.

\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}\ll r_{b}

3.

r_{b}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}

4.

\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r_{b}\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}

5.

r_{b}\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}

6.

\sqrt{Q_{P}}\ll r_{b}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}

For all hierarchies, when
r\gg\text{max}\{\sqrt{Q_{1}},\sqrt{Q_{P}},r_{b}\}
, it is the case that
g_{yy}\approx 1
and the proper size of the
y
circle is
R_{y}\gg\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

Suppose hierarchies (1) or (2) take place. These arise in particular if the asymptotic string coupling
g_{s}
is tuned to be tiny. Whenever
r\ll r_{b}
, the metric
y
-
y
component is approximately one:

g_{yy}(r\ll r_{b})\approx 1~.

(78)

This renders the solution reliable throughout the spacetime because the proper size of the
y
-circle is always approximately
R_{y}\gg\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

For hierarchy (3), when
\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}
,

g_{yy}(\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}})\approx\frac{Q_{P}}{r^{2}}\gg 1~.

(79)

When
r_{b}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}
,

g_{yy}(r_{b}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}})\approx\frac{Q_{P}}{Q_{1}}\gg 1~.

(80)

This continues to be a good approximation when
r\ll r_{b}
. Therefore, the proper size of the
y
-circle is always parametrically larger than
\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

Next, consider the fourth hierarchy. When
r_{b}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}}
,

g_{yy}(r_{b}\ll r\ll\sqrt{Q_{P}})=\frac{Q_{P}}{r^{2}}\gg 1~.

(81)

For
\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r\ll r_{b}
,

g_{yy}(\sqrt{Q_{1}}\ll r\ll r_{b})=\frac{Q_{P}}{r_{b}^{2}}\gg 1~.

(82)

Thus again, there is no breakdown of effective field theory for hierarchy (4).
The hierarchies (5)-(6) pose a danger that the
y
-circle could plunge below the string scale, already far away from the scale
r_{b}
. For example, in hierarchy (5), when
n_{1}\gg n_{p,1}
,

g_{yy}(r_{d})\approx\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R_{y}^{2}}~,~r_{d}=\sqrt{\frac{g_{s}^{2}n_{1}(\alpha^{\prime})^{4}}{R_{y}^{2}V_{4}}}~.

(83)

The scale
r_{d}
above is in the regime
\sqrt{Q_{P}}\ll r_{d}\ll\sqrt{Q_{1}}
and
r_{d}\gg r_{b}
.
To address this stringy behavior of the
y
-circle, one can apply a T-duality relative to the
y
-circle from the surface where
g_{yy}\sim\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R_{y}^{2}}
. A similar procedure was applied in
[
30
]
in the context of string cosmology.
The rules of the duality transformations are

R_{y}\to R_{y}^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R_{y}^{2}}~,~g_{s}\to g_{s}^{\prime}=\frac{g_{s}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{R_{y}}~,n_{1}\leftrightarrow n_{p,1}

(84)

Z_{1}\to Z_{1}^{\prime}=1-\frac{\mathcal{F}}{2}~,~\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=2(1-Z_{1})~.

(85)

These rules imply that
Q_{1}\leftrightarrow Q_{P}
and therefore, take hierarchy (5) to (3), and send hierarchy (6) to (4). The string coupling remains weak throughout in this T-dual background, because

e^{2\Phi^{\prime}}=\frac{(g_{s}^{\prime})^{2}}{1-\frac{\mathcal{\bar{F}}}{2}}\leq(g_{s}^{\prime})^{2}~.

(86)

Further, the maximal curvature in the geometry remains weak just as in the original frame:
R_{\text{max}}\sim\frac{O(1)}{r_{b}^{2}}\ll\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}
. The proper size of the
y
-circle in the T-dual frame is larger than the string scale when it goes below the string scale in the original frame. The conclusion is the existence of a “Superball of Strings” solution in which all validity conditions of effective field theory are met, which implies that in all six hierarchies, the solution discussed in the paper, or the solution attached to a T-dual version thereof (when the hierarchies 5-6 are considered, if a sub-stringy behavior occurs in the original frame), can be embedded in string theory. Moreover, in all cases where the solution is trustworthy, it exists with the same boundary conditions for which the singular black hole solution exists.

2.5
Generalizations

This subsection generalizes the “Superball of Strings” solution in two ways. First, solutions in which the strings are excited in the compact manifold dimensions are obtained, with a size smaller than the one in subsection
2.3
. Second, different numbers of non-compact dimensions
d
are considered, with spacetime asymptotics
\mathbb{R}^{1,d}\times S^{1}_{y}\times\mathcal{M}_{8-d}
, and different solutions are produced depending on the dimensionality
d
.

The first setup is 1/4-BPS strings carrying
n_{p,1}
momentum units with the following partition

n_{p,1}=n_{p,1}^{\perp}+n_{p,1}^{\parallel}~,

(87)

where
n_{p,1}^{\perp}
denotes the quantized momentum number associated with the transverse directions, and
n_{p,1}^{\parallel}
is the quantized momentum number for the compact dimensions
\mathcal{M}
. In other words, the stress-energy tensor on the worldsheet has contributions from both embeddings of the strings in transverse space, and in
\mathcal{M}
, with their integrals providing the quantized momenta numbers. It is assumed that both
n_{p,1}^{\perp},n_{p,1}^{\parallel}\gg 1
. In the presence of internal excitations of the strings in
\mathcal{M}
, a nonzero stress-energy tensor in target space is induced with legs on
\mathcal{M}
. However, one can smear this stress-energy tensor along the directions of
\mathcal{M}
, and in the absence of winding and momentum modes about these directions, the smeared components of the target-space stress-energy tensor along
\mathcal{M}
vanish, justifying using the same Supergravity ansatz in Eq. (
2
).
The solution corresponding to the microcanonical ensemble with excitation level
N=n_{1}n_{p,1}^{\perp}
remains almost the same. The nonzero averaged harmonic functions read

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{1}=1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r^{2}}\Bigg(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b,\perp}^{2}}}\Bigg)~~,~~\mathcal{\bar{F}}=-\frac{2Q_{P}}{r^{2}}\Bigg(1-e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{b,\perp}^{2}}}\Bigg)~~,

(88)

where the size is given by

r_{b,\perp}=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}n_{1}n_{p,1}^{\perp}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}~.

(89)

Since
n_{p,1}^{\perp}<n_{p,1}
, the size of this solution is smaller than the one in subsection
2.3
.

The second mode of generalization considers
d
non-compact dimensions, including time. One can generalize the solution by solving the appropriate sourced “Poisson” equations with boundary conditions of suitable falloffs far away and regularity at the center. The result is

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{1}=1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r^{d-3}}f_{d}\Big(\frac{r}{r_{b,\perp}(d)}\Big)~,~\bar{\mathcal{F}}=-\frac{2Q_{P}}{r^{d-3}}f_{d}\Big(\frac{r}{r_{b,\perp}(d)}\Big)~,

(90)

where

\displaystyle f_{d}(x)\equiv 1-\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d-1}{2},x^{2}\Big)}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d-1}{2}\Big)}+\frac{x^{d-3}e^{-x^{2}}}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d-1}{2}\Big)}~.

(91)

The incomplete Gamma function, which appears in the definition of
f_{d}(x)
, is defined in

\Gamma(a,x)\equiv\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{a-1}dt~.

(92)

Also, the size is

r_{b,\perp}(d)=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3(d-1)}n_{1}n_{p,1}^{\perp}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}~.

(93)

In the special case of
d=4
non-compact dimension, the solution reads

\bar{Z}_{1}=1+\frac{Q_{1}}{r}\text{Erf}\Big(\frac{r}{r_{b,\perp}(4)}\Big)~,~\mathcal{\bar{F}}=-\frac{2Q_{P}}{r}\text{Erf}\Big(\frac{r}{r_{b,\perp}(4)}\Big)~.

(94)

It would be interesting to produce a non-BPS Lorentzian solution sourced by strings in
3+1
non-compact dimensions corresponding to the microcanonical ensemble; the error-function above might play a role in such a solution, replacing the Schwarzschild blackening factor
1-\frac{2M}{r}
by
1-\frac{2M}{r}\text{Erf}\Big(\frac{r}{2M}\Big)
.

3
Comparison with the Chen-Maldacena-Witten Solution

3.1
Review of the Horowitz-Polchinski Solution

Reference
[
29
]
found a solution to the equations of motion derived from the Euclidean Horowitz-Polchinski (HP) effective action. In Type II superstring theory, this effective action reads

\displaystyle I_{HP}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{16\pi G_{N}}\int d^{d}x\sqrt{G_{d}}e^{-2\Phi_{d}}\Big(\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial^{\mu}\chi^{*}+m^{2}\chi\chi^{*}+\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\varphi\chi\chi^{*}\Big)

\displaystyle~-\frac{\beta}{16\pi G_{N}}\int d^{d}x\sqrt{G_{d}}e^{-2\Phi_{d}}\Big(R_{d}-\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi+4\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{d}\partial^{\mu}\Phi_{d}\Big)~.

(95)

The notations in
I_{\text{HP}}
are a mode of the metric time-time component
G_{\tau\tau}
, namely the radion
\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\Big(G_{\tau\tau}-1\Big)

G_{\tau\tau}\approx 1

4
Conclusion

In this paper, a reliable solution to string theory is presented, whose physical picture is that of a ball of BPS strings with a root-mean-square value given by a random-walk formula. The solution was obtained by performing an ensemble average in a superselection sector of highly excited strings that preserve eight supercharges. Since generic BPS microstates with large charges are almost indistinguishable from the microcanonical ensemble, such a solution provides a weakly coupled, weakly curved description of generic stringy microstates. Several future directions are written. First, generalizing the solution to less supersymmetric systems is important for approaching a phenomenologically relevant model of stringy bound states. The “Superball of Strings” may occur in a Universe that admits a spatial compact dimension, as in the scenario of
[
32
]
; it would be interesting to explore its phenomenological implications. In addition, rotating versions of the solution can be constructed in principle using the methods of
[
17
]
. Furthermore, a specific analytical continuation of the rotating solution could potentially allow one to evaluate the supersymmetric index of the stringy microstates in the spirit of
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
, albeit utilizing a horizon-free solution. Finally, finding a particular ensemble that provides a Lorentzian continuation of the CMW solution is an interesting prospect.

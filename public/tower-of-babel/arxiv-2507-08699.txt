A novel quantum circuit for the quantum Fourier transform Thanks: luis.cruz.e@cua.uam.mx Thanks: roberto.romero@cua.uam.mx
Juan M. Romero, Emiliano Montoya-González, Guillermo Cruz

Abstract
The Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) is a fundamental component of many quantum computing algorithms. In this paper, we present an alternative method for factoring this transformation. Inspired by this approach, we introduce a new quantum circuit for implementing the QFT. We show that this circuit is more efficient than the conventional design. Furthermore, using this circuit, we develop alternative versions of the HHL algorithm and Shor’s algorithm, which also demonstrate improved performance compared to their standard implementations. Keywords : Quantum Computing; Quantum Fourier Transform; HHL Algorithm; Shor’s Algorithm; Quantum Circuit.

Introduction

For certain algorithms, quantum computing has demonstrated better performance than classical computing.
For instance, in classical computing the Fast Fourier Transform is one of the most important algorithms, which has computational complexity of
O(n2^{n})
. However, the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) has
O(n^{2})
computational complexity
[
1
]
. The QFT plays a fundamental role for different quantum algorithms. For example, the Shor’s factoring algorithm is one of most relevant in quantum cryptography and the QFT is one of its building block
[
2
]
. Moreover, the Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd algorithm (HHL) provides a quantum method for solving linear systems of equations and for this algorithm the QFT plays a fundamental role
[
3
]
. It is worth mentioning that the HHL algorithm has applications in quantum machine learning
[
4
,
5
]
, physics
[
6
,
7
]
, quantum chemistry
[
8
]
, finance
[
9
]
, etc.
Given that the QFT is a cornerstone of quantum computing, various alternative formulation have been proposed to enhance its performance, including, approximate
[
10
]
, fast
[
11
]
, and semiclassical versions
[
12
]
.

In this paper, an alternative method for factoring the QFT is presented. Inspired
by this method, a new quantum circuit for the Quantum Fourier Transform is introduced. Moreover, it is shown that the new
algorithm is faster than the usual. Furthermore, using this quantum circuit, alternative HHL algorithm and Shor’s algorithm are presented, demonstrating better performance than their conventional implementations.

Review of QFT

First, lets us remember that a basis of a system with
n
qubits have
2^{n}
states, for example

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{00\cdots 00}}_{n},

(1)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{00\cdots 01}}_{n},

(2)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{11\cdots 11}}_{n}.

(3)

In addition, notice that by using the binary numeral system the first
2^{n}
natural numbers

\displaystyle 0,1,2,3,\cdots,2^{n}-1

can be written as

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{n-1}2^{n-1}+a_{n-2}2^{n-2}+\cdots+a_{1}2^{1}+a_{0}2^{0},

\displaystyle a_{n-1},\cdots,a_{0}=0,1.

Then, the states (
1
)-(
3
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\ket{k}=\ket{a_{n-1}a_{n-2}\cdots a_{1}a_{0}},

specifically

\displaystyle\ket{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{00\cdots 00}}_{n},

\displaystyle\ket{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{00\cdots 01}}_{n},

\displaystyle\ket{2^{n}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\ket{11\cdots 11}}_{n}.

Then, a state of a system with
n
qubits can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{k}\ket{k},\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}=1.

Now, the QFT of a state
\ket{\psi}
can be defined as

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\tilde{\alpha}_{k}\ket{k},

(4)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{j}\omega^{kj}

(5)

and

\displaystyle\omega=e^{\frac{\pi i}{2^{n-1}}}.

Now, due that QFT is a linear transformation, the following equation

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{j}{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

(6)

is satified.

In addition, notice that from the equations (
4
) and (
5
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\tilde{\alpha}_{k}\ket{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{j}\omega^{kj}\right)\ket{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\alpha_{j}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}\right),

that is to say

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{\psi}=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\alpha_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}\right).

Consequently, by using the equation (
6
) we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}.

3
Alternative factorization

Now, we can see that if
n=1,
we have

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{1}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\omega^{0}\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),

namely

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),\quad j=0,1.

(7)

Moreover, if
n=2,
the QFT is

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{2}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\omega^{0}\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}+\omega^{2j}\ket{2}+\omega^{3j}\ket{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right),

that is to say

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right).

(8)

This last state can be separated in two states, one of this states is the QFT for
n=1.
In fact,

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{0}\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{0}\ket{1}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\omega^{j}\ket{1}\ket{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{0}\underbrace{\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right)}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\underbrace{\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right)\underbrace{\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right)}_{{\rm QFT}\,\,{\rm for}\,\,n=1}.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{2}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),\quad j=0,1,2,3.

(9)

Consequently, if the QFT for the case
n=1
(
7
) is multiplied on the left for the state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right),

the QFT for case
n=2
(
9
) is gotten.

Furthermore, when
n=3,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{3}-1}\omega^{kj}\ket{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\bigg(\omega^{0}\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}+\omega^{2j}\ket{2}+\omega^{3j}\ket{3}+

\displaystyle+\omega^{4j}\ket{4}+\omega^{5j}\ket{5}+\omega^{6j}\ket{6}+\omega^{7j}\ket{7}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\bigg(\ket{000}+\omega^{j}\ket{001}+\omega^{2j}\ket{010}+\omega^{3j}\ket{011}+

\displaystyle+\omega^{4j}\ket{100}+\omega^{5j}\ket{101}+\omega^{6j}\ket{110}+\omega^{7j}\ket{111}\bigg),

in other words

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\bigg(\ket{000}+\omega^{j}\ket{001}+\omega^{2j}\ket{010}+\omega^{3j}\ket{011}+

\displaystyle+\omega^{4j}\ket{100}+\omega^{5j}\ket{101}+\omega^{6j}\ket{110}+\omega^{7j}\ket{111}\bigg).

This last state can be separated in two states, one of this states is the QFT for
n=2.
Actually,

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\bigg(\ket{0}\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{0}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{0}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{0}\ket{11}+

\displaystyle+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\ket{00}+\omega^{4j}\omega^{j}\ket{1}\ket{01}+\omega^{4j}\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\ket{10}+\omega^{4j}\omega^{3j}\ket{1}\ket{11}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\bigg(\ket{0}\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right)+

\displaystyle+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right).

that is

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\right)\underbrace{\left(\ket{00}+\omega^{j}\ket{01}+\omega^{2j}\ket{10}+\omega^{3j}\ket{11}\right)}_{{\rm QFT}\,\,{\rm for}\,\,n=2}.

Then, by using the equations (
3
) and (
9
) we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),

(10)

\displaystyle j=0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7.

Hence, if the QFT for the case
n=2
(
9
) is multiplied on the left for the state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\right),

the QFT for case
n=3
(
10
) is obtained.

In general, we can see that if the state
{\rm QFT}\ket{j}
of
n-1
qubits is multiplied d on the left for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2^{n-1}j}\ket{1}\right)

the state
{\rm QFT}\ket{j}
for
n
qubits is obtained.

For example, if
n=4,
by using the equation
10
and the state

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2^{4-1}j}\ket{1}\right)

we have

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{4}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{8j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{4j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),

\displaystyle j=0,1,2,3,\cdots,15.

In general we have

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2^{n-1}j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2^{n-2}j}\ket{1}\right)\cdots\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{2j}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+\omega^{j}\ket{1}\right),\qquad

(11)

\displaystyle j=0,1,2,3,\cdots,2^{n}-1.

In the next section, we proof that this factorization allows to built an alternative quantum circuit for the QFT, which is faster than the usual.

In addition, if

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{n-1}2^{n-1}+b_{n-2}2^{n-2}+\cdots+b_{1}2^{1}+b_{0}2^{0},

\displaystyle b_{n-1},\cdots,b_{0}=0,1,

by using the fractional binary notation,

\displaystyle[0.x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}]=\frac{x_{1}}{2}+\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}}+\frac{x_{3}}{2^{3}}+\cdots+\frac{x_{n}}{2^{n}},

the QFT (
11
) can be written as

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm QFT}\ket{b_{n-1}\cdots b_{1}b_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\left(\ket{0}+e^{2\pi i[0.b_{0}]}\ket{1}\right)\left(\ket{0}+e^{2\pi i[0.b_{1}b_{0}]}\ket{1}\right)\cdots\left(\ket{0}+e^{2\pi i[0.b_{n}\cdots b_{1}b_{0}]}\ket{1}\right),

which is the usual expression of the QFT.

Conclusions

The Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) is one of the most fundamental algorithms in quantum computing. In this paper, we presented an alternative method for factoring the QFT. Inspired by this approach, we introduced a novel quantum circuit for implementing the QFT. Furthermore, we demonstrated that the new algorithm is faster than the standard one. As an application of this new circuit, we proposed alternative versions of the HHL algorithm and Shor’s algorithm, demonstrating better performance than their usual implementations.

In future works, we will study additional applications of this novel quantum circuit in areas such as
quantum machine learning, physics, quantum chemistry, finance, etc.

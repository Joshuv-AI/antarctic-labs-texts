Ultrastrong Magnon-Photon Coupling in Superconductor/Antiferromagnet/Superconductor Heterostructures at Terahertz Frequencies
V. M. Gordeeva, Yanmeng Lei, Xiyin Ye, G. A. Bobkov, A. M. Bobkov, Tao Yu, I. V. Bobkova

Abstract
We predict the realization of ultrastrong coupling between magnons of antiferromagnets and photons in superconductor/antiferromagnet/superconductor heterostructures at terahertz frequencies, from both quantum and classical perspectives. The hybridization of the two magnon modes with photons strongly depends on the applied magnetic field: at zero magnetic field, only a single antiferromagnetic mode with a lower frequency couples to the photon, forming a magnon-polariton, while using a magnetic field activates coupling for both antiferromagnetic modes. The coupling between magnon and photon is ultrastrong with the coupling constant ∼ \sim 100 GHz exceeding 10% of the antiferromagnetic resonant frequency. The superconductor modulates the spin of the resulting magnon-polaritons and the group velocity, achieving values amounting to several tenths of the speed of light, which promises strong tunability of magnon transport in antiferromagnets by superconductors.



I
Introduction

Quantum magnonics is an emerging field of research focusing on the coherent coupling of magnons and photons. It promises to enable hybrid quantum platforms
Tabuchi et al. 2015
;
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2020
, magnon-based memory systems
Zhang et al. 2015
, and microwave-to-optical quantum transducers
Hisatomi et al. 2016
.
A primary challenge hindering advancements in quantum magnonics is the inherently weak coupling strength between individual spins and photons. To overcome this limitation in microwave cavities, researchers exploit the Dicke cooperative coupling relation,

g=g_{s}\sqrt{N}
,
where
g_{s}
is the single-spin coupling strength and
N
is the number of spins in the system
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2019
;
Huebl et al. 2013
;
Tabuchi et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2014
;
Kirton et al. 2019
. This approach has led to the demonstration of the strong
Huebl et al. 2013
;
Tabuchi et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2014
and, more recently, the ultra-strong
Flower et al. 2019
;
Zare Rameshti et al. 2015
;
Bourhill et al. 2016
photon-magnon coupling regimes, although in truly macroscopic systems with dimensions of several millimeters. Specifically, the ultra-strong coupling and deep-strong coupling regimes are defined by the conditions that the coupling ratio
g/\omega>0.1
and
>1
, respectively. Here
\omega
is the characteristic bare frequency of the coupled excitations.
The ultra-strong coupling regime dramatically alters the energy levels and dynamics of a coupled system
Frisk Kockum et al. 2019
;
Qin et al. 2024
. This makes it particularly valuable for probing fundamental quantum effects and developing novel quantum technologies.

For practical applications, on-chip integration of such quantum systems is essential, which requires a high single-spin coupling strength
g_{s}
. Several successful on-chip implementations of both the strong
Li et al. 2019
;
Hou and Liu 2019
and ultra-strong
Golovchanskiy et al. 2021a
;
Golovchanskiy et al. 2021b
photon-to-magnon coupling regimes were reported. In Refs.
Golovchanskiy et al. 2021a
;
Golovchanskiy et al. 2021b
the ultra-strong and approaching deep-strong photon-to-magnon coupling was demonstrated in superconductor/ferromagnet/superconductor heterostructures. The coupling ratio
g/\omega\approx 0.6
with the single-spin coupling strength
g_{s}\approx 350
Hz and cooperativity about
10^{4}
was reported. Later, these data received a theoretical explanation
Silaev 2023
;
Qiu et al. 2024
. The mechanism of such ultrastrong coupling is based on the fact that magnon excitations of the ferromagnetic layers are accompanied by Meissner supercurrents in the superconducting layers, which strongly modify and enhance the microwave magnetic field inside the superconducting resonator.

On the other hand, antiferromagnets have recently emerged as attractive candidates to replace ferromagnets in spintronic devices because they are robust against magnetic perturbations, generate weaker stray fields, and exhibit ultrafast dynamics in the terahertz frequency range
Baltz et al. 2018
;
Jungwirth et al. 2016
;
Brataas et al. 2020
. For this reason achieving strong and ultra-strong magnon-photon coupling regimes is an important task of antiferromagnetic quantum magnonics. Strong coupling between magnons in antiferromagnets and photons is a challenge in cavity magnonics since the magnons typically have a frequency of terahertz, while the cavity supporting terahertz photons is rare. Ultrastrong coupling is more difficult. Nevertheless, the strong-coupling regime, characterized by a bright and a dark magnon-polariton, was predicted in antiferromagnetic microcavities by Yuan et al.
Yuan and Wang 2017
. Further, the magnon-polaritons in THz cavities were observed experimentally
Kritzell et al. 2024
;
Grishunin et al. 2018
;
Sivarajah et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Białek et al. 2020
;
Białek et al. 2021
;
Metzger et al. 2022
;
Baydin et al. 2023
;
Blank et al. 2023
and the coupling strength of the order of
0.015
THz corresponding to the strong-coupling regime has been reported
Kritzell et al. 2024
;
Grishunin et al. 2018
.

In this work, we propose a realization of ultrastrong coupling between magnons of antiferromagnets and photons in superconductor/antiferromagnet/superconductor heterostructures at terahertz frequencies. Our findings reveal that in the absence of an external magnetic field, the coupling is selective, involving only one magnon mode to form a magnon-polariton. Under an applied magnetic field, however, both antiferromagnetic modes become coupled to the photon. The coupling is comprehensively analyzed from both quantum and classical perspectives, with particular focus on the spin and group velocity of the resulting magnon-polaritons, showing the strong modulation of magnon transport in antiferromagnets by superconductors.

We organize the paper as follows. We begin by describing the model system in Section
II

III
calculates the dipolar fields from the magnon-polaritons. A quantum-mechanical analysis of the magnon-photon coupling and the magnon-polariton dispersion and spin follows in Section
IV

V

VI

III
Dipolar fields

The reconstruction of the magnonic excitations in the antiferromagnet sandwiched between two S layers is caused by the electromagnetic interaction with the dipolar fields, which are strongly modified by the Meissner supercurrents in the superconductors. Those currents are induced by the combination of the Swihart mode of the superconducting resonator and the stray magnonic fields. Consequently, in order to obtain the dispersion relations of the excitations in the system, firstly, we need to calculate the dipolar fields acting on the magnetization, taking into account the superconducting environment of the AF layer. For this purpose, we solve Maxwell’s equations for the electric and magnetic fields radiated by the magnetization dynamics. Maxwell’s equations for the electric field
\bm{E}(\bm{r},t)
, magnetic field
\bm{H}(\bm{r},t)
, magnetic field induction
\bm{B}(\bm{r},t)
, and electric current
\bm{J}(\bm{r},t)
take the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{rot}~\bm{E}=-\dfrac{\partial\bm{B}}{\partial t},

\displaystyle\mathrm{rot}~\bm{H}=\bm{J}+\varepsilon\dfrac{\partial\bm{E}}{\partial t},

(1)

where
\varepsilon
is the dielectric constant of the corresponding layers. As the electric field is radiated by the magnetization dynamics, it takes the form
\bm{E}(\bm{r},t)=\tilde{\bm{E}}(x)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\rho}-i\omega t}
. Considering
\bm{B}=\mu_{0}(\bm{H}+\bm{M})
and
\bm{J}=\sigma\bm{E}
, where
\mu_{0}
is the vacuum magnetic permeability and
\sigma
is the conductivity of the layer, from Eqs. (
1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\bm{E}+\mu_{0}(i\omega\sigma+\varepsilon\omega^{2})\bm{E}+i\omega\mu_{0}\mathrm{rot}\bm{M}=0.

(2)

In the framework of the two-fluid model, the conductivity of a superconductor at frequency
\omega
takes the form
Schmidt 1997

\displaystyle\sigma_{S,L}(\omega)=\frac{\rho_{n}e^{2}\tau}{m_{e}(1+\omega^{2}\tau^{2})}(1+i\omega\tau)+i\dfrac{\rho_{s}e^{2}}{m_{e}\omega},

(3)

where
\rho_{s}=\rho(1-(T/T_{c})^{4})
and
\rho_{n}=\rho(T/T_{c})^{4}
are superfluid and normal fluid densities, respectively. Equation (
3
) can then be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sigma_{S,L}(\omega)=\sigma_{n}\left(\frac{T}{T_{c}}\right)^{4}(1+i\omega\tau)+\frac{i}{\omega\mu_{0}\lambda_{L}^{2}},

(4)

where
\sigma_{n}=\rho e^{2}\tau/m_{e}
is the conductivity of normal metals,
\lambda_{L}=\sqrt{m_{e}/(\mu_{0}\rho_{s}e^{2})}
is London’s
penetration depth,
\rho
is the electron density,
m_{e}
is the electron mass,
e
is the electron charge,
T_{c}
is the critical temperature of the superconductor,
T
is the temperature of the system, and
\tau
is the relaxation time of electrons.

However, under a high-frequency ac field, the relationship between the current and the vector potential changes, which depends in an essential way on the frequency
Abrikosov et al. 1958
;
Abrikosov et al. 1960
;
Mattis and Bardeen 1958
;
Zimmermann et al. 1991
. Since in the system with an antiferromagnet we deal with high magnon frequencies
\omega\sim 1
THz, the conductivity of the superconductor needs to be calculated using the formalism of microscopic Green’s functions. For isotropic BCS superconductors, which is the case under our consideration, we use the expression obtained in Ref.
Zimmermann et al. 1991
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(\omega)=i\dfrac{\sigma_{n}}{2\omega\tau}I.

(5)

Here
I
is a dimensionless integral over quasiparticle energy, the explicit expression for which can be found in Ref.
Zimmermann et al. 1991
.

In this work, we shall focus on the case
T=0
and
\hbar\omega<2\Delta
, where
\Delta
is the superconducting order parameter. Then the real part of
\sigma_{S}
is absent, and the conductivity Eq. (
5
) is well fitted by the expression

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(\omega)=\frac{i}{\omega\mu_{0}\lambda_{\rm{eff}}^{2}},

(6)

where
\lambda_{\rm{eff}}
is an effective penetration depth, which does not depend on
\omega
.

On the other hand, after substituting explicit expression for
\lambda_{L}
and
\sigma_{n}
Eq. (
4
) is transformed to

\displaystyle\sigma_{S,L}(\omega)=\frac{i}{\omega\mu_{0}\lambda_{L}^{2}}=i\dfrac{\sigma_{n}}{\omega\tau}.

(7)

From Eqs. (
5
)-(
7
), one obtains the effective penetration depth
\lambda_{\rm{eff}}
in terms of
\lambda_{L}
and
I
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm{eff}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{I}}\lambda_{L}.

(8)

In all the calculations we assume that the superconductors are NbN; then for numerical estimations one can take
T_{c}\approx 16
K,
\lambda(T=0)\approx 80
nm,
\sigma_{n}=(1\div 5)\cdot 10^{5}~\Omega^{-1}\cdot\rm{m}^{-1}
, and
\tau\sim 10^{-15}
s. For these parameters Eqs. (
5
) and (
8
) at
\omega\leq 2\Delta
, which is the case for
\omega\sim 1
THz, give
I\sim(0.2\div 1)\cdot 10^{-2}
and
\lambda_{\rm{eff}}\sim(1\div 3)~\mu
m.

Then we can express Eq. (
2
) in the S and AF layers:

\displaystyle\mathrm{in~S:}~~\Delta\bm{E}+k_{S}^{2}\bm{E}=0,~~k_{S}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0}-\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{\rm{eff}}^{2}}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{in~AF:}~~\Delta\bm{E}+k_{AF}^{2}\bm{E}=-i\omega\mu_{0}\mathrm{rot}\bm{M},~~k_{AF}=\omega\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{AF}},

(9)

where

and

\displaystyle H_{x}=\dfrac{k_{y}E_{z}-k_{z}E_{y}}{\omega\mu_{0}}-M_{x},

IV
Magnon-polaritons: quantum view

Now our goal is to derive the eigenfrequencies of the collective magnon-polariton modes in the S/AF/S heterostructures. In this section, we perform this derivation using quantum formalism.

Physically, the magnons of the antiferromagnet couple with the photon modes of the superconducting resonator.
To this end, in the total Hamiltonian of the system, we first address the part associated with the photonic Swihart mode in the S/I/S resonator
Swihart 1961
. As was shown in Ref.
Qiu et al. 2024
, the magnetic field
\hat{\bm{H}}_{Sw}=\hat{H}_{Sw,y}\bm{e}_{y}+\hat{H}_{Sw,z}\bm{e}_{z}
for the Swihart photons is quantized according to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{Sw,y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(\frac{\Omega_{s}}{2k}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{AF}\hbar\Omega_{s}}{d_{AF}}}\cos\theta e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{Sw,z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(-\frac{\Omega_{s}}{2k}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{AF}\hbar\Omega_{s}}{d_{AF}}}\sin\theta e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}+{\rm H.c.}\right),

(26)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{s}({\bm{k}})=\sqrt{\frac{d_{AF}}{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{AF}(d_{AF}+\lambda_{\rm{eff}})}}|{\bm{k}}|

(27)

is the dispersion relation of the Swihart mode, and
\{\hat{p}^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}},\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}\}
are the photon creation and annihilation operators that obey the commutation relation
[\hat{p}_{\bm{k}},\hat{p}^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}]=i\hbar\delta_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}
. The Hamiltonian of the Swihart mode then reads

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{ph}=\hbar\sum_{\bm{k}}\Omega_{s}({\bm{k}})\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}.

(28)

In the second step, let us study the Hamiltonian describing magnons in the antiferromagnet at
k\to 0
, when the Swihart mode is absent. We introduce the magnetization operators
\hat{\bm{M}}_{1,2}({\bm{r}})
for the magnetizations of the two sublattices. The magnetizations
\hat{\bm{M}}_{1,2}({\bm{r}})
are affected by the effective magnetic field, including the in-plane static external magnetic field, along the Néel vector
{\bm{z}}
-direction

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{0}({\bm{r}})=H_{0}\bm{e}_{z},

(29)

the exchange magnetic field

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}^{\rm ex}_{1,2}({\bm{r}})=-\frac{J_{AF}}{M_{s}}\bm{M}_{2,1},

(30)

the anisotropy magnetic field

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}^{\rm ani}_{1,2}({\bm{r}})=\frac{K}{M_{s}}\hat{M}_{1,2z}({\bm{r}})\bm{e}_{z},

(31)

and the demagnetization field

\displaystyle{\bm{H}}^{d}({\bm{r}})=-N_{x0}\left(\hat{M}_{1x}+\hat{M}_{2x}\right)\bm{e}_{x}-N_{y0}\left(\hat{M}_{1y}+\hat{M}_{2y}\right)\bm{e}_{y}.

(32)

Here
K
and
J_{AF}
are the anisotropy and exchange constants, respectively, and
N_{x(y)0}\equiv N_{x(y)}(k=0)
are the components of the demagnetization tensor (
25
) taken at

. With these effective magnetic fields, the Hamiltonian of the antiferromagnetic magnons takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm AFM}=\sum_{i=1,2}\int d{\bm{r}}\Big\{-\mu_{0}\hat{\bm{M}}_{i}({\bm{r}})\cdot{\bm{H}}_{0}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\hat{\bm{M}}_{i}({\bm{r}})\cdot\left[{\bm{H}}^{\rm ex}_{i}({\bm{r}})+{\bm{H}}^{d}({\bm{r}})+{\bm{H}}^{\rm ani}_{i}({\bm{r}})\right]\Big\}.

Then we introduce the operators of the spin density
\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}({\bm{r}})
at the sublattice
i
, so that
\hat{\bm{M}}_{i}({\bm{r}})=-\gamma\hbar\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}({\bm{r}})
and

, where
-\gamma
is the gyromagnetic ratio of electrons and

is the total spin of the system. As the next step, we represent the spin-density operators
\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}({\bm{r}})
in terms of the bosonic operators

via the

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}+\hat{a}_{1}\right),~~~\hat{S}_{2x}=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}+\hat{a}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1y}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2i}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}-\hat{a}_{1}\right),~~~\hat{S}_{2y}=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2i}\left(\hat{a}_{2}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1z}

\displaystyle=-S+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1},~~~~~~~~~~\hat{S}_{2z}=S-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2}\hat{a}_{2}.

(34)

When the wave vector
{\bm{k}}
is small, we focus on the modes close to the antiferromagnetic resonance, which are uniform across the antiferromagnetic film, i.e., there is no

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1x}({\bm{\rho}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2\sqrt{2d_{AF}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}+\hat{a}_{1,{\bm{k}}}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}\right),~~~~\hat{S}_{2x}({\bm{\rho}})=\frac{\sqrt{2S}}{2\sqrt{2d_{AF}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}+\hat{a}_{2,{\bm{k}}}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{1y}({\bm{\rho}})

\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2,-{\bm{k}}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{0}=\mu_{0}\gamma\hbar\begin{pmatrix}F_{+}+\dfrac{M_{s}(N_{x0}+N_{y0})}{2}&\dfrac{M_{s}(N_{x0}-N_{y0})}{2}&\dfrac{M_{s}(N_{x0}-N_{y0})}{2}&J_{AF}+\dfrac{M_{s}(N_{x0}+N_{y0})}{2})\\

\displaystyle=-i\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\hbar M_{s}}{4d_{AF}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Big(-\cosh{\frac{\varphi}{2}}\hat{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}

After the diagonalization procedure, we obtain the following dispersion relations of the two magnon modes (before the spin-flop transition):

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}=\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{H^{2}_{0}+H_{b}^{2}\pm\sqrt{K^{2}M_{s}^{2}(N_{x0}-N_{y0})^{2}+4H_{0}^{2}H_{b}^{2}}},

Since analytical solutions for the eigenstates are too lengthy to be listed, we then seek an approximate solution by treating the demagnetization effect as a perturbation.
Since

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{m}

\displaystyle\approx\hbar\sum_{\bm{k}}\bigg[(\omega_{1}+h_{+})\hat{m}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\hat{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}+(\omega_{2}+h_{+})\hat{m}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\hat{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}=\frac{h_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\left(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}-\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}}}\hat{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}

The magnon Hamiltonian then takes its final form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{m}=\hbar\sum_{\bm{k}}\left(\tilde{\omega}_{1}\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{1,\bm{k}}\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{2,\bm{k}}\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}\right),

(47)

with the magnon frequencies

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{1,2}=\mu_{0}\gamma l+h_{+}\mp\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}.

(48)

In terms of operators

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{y}({\bm{\rho}})

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{\gamma\hbar M_{s}}{4d_{AF}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Big[\left(\cosh\frac{\varphi}{2}-\sinh\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[a\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}+b\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}\right]e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}-{\rm H.c.}\Big]

\displaystyle a=\frac{h_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\left(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}-\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{h_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}\left(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}+\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}}},

\displaystyle b=\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}-\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\left(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}-\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}+\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\left(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}+\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}\right)^{2}}}.

Next, we write the part of the total Hamiltonian that describes the coupling between magnons and photons through the Zeeman interaction. From Eqs. (
49

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{int}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{0}\int dxd{\bm{\rho}}\hat{\bm{M}}({\bm{r}})\cdot\hat{\bm{H}}_{Sw}({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle+g_{2}({\bm{k}})\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}(\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{p}_{-\bm{k}})+{\rm H.c.}\Big),

where we have introduced the coupling constants

\displaystyle g_{1}({\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=-ia\sqrt{\mu_{0}\gamma M_{s}}\left(\cosh\frac{\varphi}{2}-\sinh\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Omega_{s}(k)}{2k}\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{AF}\Omega_{s}(k)}\cos\theta,

\displaystyle g_{2}({\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=-ib\sqrt{\mu_{0}\gamma M_{s}}\left(\cosh\frac{\varphi}{2}-\sinh\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Omega_{s}(k)}{2k}\sqrt{\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{AF}\Omega_{s}(k)}\cos\theta.

(52)

Since the coupling constants depend on the propagation direction
\theta
with respect to the Néel vector by
\cos\theta

\theta=\pi/2
.
From Eq. (
50

\gamma\mu_{0}H_{0}\ll h_{-}
),

and

(the one with a lower frequency) couples with the Swihart photon. On the contrary, when

and

, so the two antiferromagnetic modes couple equally with the Swihart mode. Substituting the expression for the Swihart mode frequency Eq. (
27
) into Eqs. (
52

\lambda_{\rm eff}\to\infty
. Formally, this limit corresponds to the normal state of the S layers. However, it is worth mentioning here that our answer is only applicable to the superconducting low-temperature state of the S layers. The physics of the antiferromagnetic THz magnon-photon coupling near the critical temperature and in the normal state of the S layers requires a separate consideration because in this case the penetration depth
\lambda_{\rm eff}
is replaced by the skin depth and the conductivity of the layers acquires a significant real part, which may give rise to an additional non-Hermitian physics including exceptional points
Qiu et al. 2024
.

Finally, let us write the total Hamiltonian of the system:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{tot}=\hat{H}_{m}+\hat{H}_{ph}+\hat{H}_{int}

\displaystyle=\hbar\sum_{\bm{k}}\bigg[\tilde{\omega}_{1}\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}+\Omega_{s}\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle+\Big\{\left(g_{1}({\bm{k}})\tilde{m}_{1,\bm{k}}+g_{2}({\bm{k}})\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}\right)(\hat{p}_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}+\hat{p}_{-\bm{k}})+{\rm H.c.}\Big\}\bigg].

(53)

By performing the Bogoliubov transformation for

\gamma\mu_{0}H_{0}\ll h_{-}

\tilde{\omega}_{1,2}\approx h_{+}\mp h_{-}+\gamma\mu_{0}l
and
g_{2}\approx 0
, which leads to the eigenfrequencies

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}=h_{+}+h_{-}+\gamma\mu_{0}l,

(54)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2,3}(\bm{k})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}+\Omega_{s}^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}-\Omega_{s}^{2})^{2}+16|g_{1}(\bm{k})|^{2}\Omega_{s}\tilde{\omega}_{1}}}.

(55)

It can be seen that
\Omega_{1}=\tilde{\omega}_{2}
, which means this mode is not coupled to the Swihart mode, as was mentioned above. The physical reason is that the coupling is provided by
M_{y}
-component of the AF magnetization, see Eq. (
51
). According to Eq. (
49

for the
\Omega_{1}
eigenmode, as

and only magnon
\tilde{m}_{2,\bm{k}}
is excited in this mode. At the same time, the other mode is coupled to the photon, and the coupled eigenfrequencies
\Omega_{2,3}
expressed by Eq. (
55
) coincide with the answer for the coupled magnon-polariton modes obtained in Refs.
Silaev 2023
;
Qiu et al. 2024
for the S/F/S heterostructures. The coincidence is not accidental, since the coupling mechanism in our case is the same: the coupling is realized through the electromagnetic interaction between
M_{y}
-component of the AF magnetization and the magnetic field of the Swihart mode. The situation when only one of two magnonic eigenmodes is coupled to the Swihart mode is also to some extent similar to the case of S/F/I/F/S system
Gordeeva et al. 2025
with identical ferromagnets, where magnonic modes of two ferromagnets were coupled through the electromagnetic interaction with the Meissner currents in the S layers, and only one of them - acoustic mode - was coupled to the photon, but the other, optical mode, was not coupled due to the absence of net magnetization, and, thus represents an example of a dark mode.

We shall consider the bulk volume configuration with
\bm{k}
aligned parallel to
\bm{M}_{1s}

\theta=0

\mathrm{MnF}_{2}

GHz [

is the wave number of the photon at the magnon frequency and is determined from the equation

|g_{1}(k_{K})|\sim\sqrt[4]{K/J_{AF}}\sqrt{d_{AF}/2(d_{AF}+\lambda_{\rm eff})}\sqrt{\mu_{0}\gamma M_{s}\tilde{\omega}_{1}}
, and the coupling strength for the S/F/S heterostructure
g_{F}=(1/2)\sqrt{d_{F}/(d_{F}+\lambda)}\mu_{0}\gamma\sqrt{M_{sF}(M_{sF}+H_{0})}

Silaev 2023
;
Qiu et al. 2024
, where

at the same value of the parameter

. It depends on the particular magnetic parameters of the F and AF materials. In our case, the high value of the coupling constant is due to the high value of the magnetic anisotropy constant

In the region of strong magnon-photon mixing
k\approx k_{K}
, the group velocities of the magnon-polariton branches

V
Magnon-polaritons: Classical view

Now we shall use an alternative classical approach to obtain the magnon-polariton eigenfrequencies and mode functions. The Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation for the magnetizations of the two sublattices
\bm{M}_{1,2}
takes the form:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\bm{M}_{i}}{\partial t}=-\gamma\mu_{0}\bm{M}_{i}\times\bm{H}_{i},~~i\in\{1,2\},

(56)

in which

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have proposed and theoretically investigated a platform for achieving ultrastrong magnon-photon coupling at terahertz frequencies using a S/AF/S heterostructure. Our analysis, conducted from both quantum and classical perspectives, reveals several key findings:

(i)
Enhanced coupling via superconductivity
. The presence of superconducting layers drastically modifies the electromagnetic environment of the antiferromagnet. The Meissner supercurrents screen the dipolar fields, leading to a significant renormalization of the magnon spectrum and enabling a strong electromagnetic interaction between the antiferromagnetic magnons and the photonic Swihart mode of the structure. The coupling strength is shown to be substantially larger than that reported for antiferromagnets in conventional terahertz cavities, placing the system at the boundary of the ultrastrong coupling regime (
g/\omega\sim 0.1
).

(ii)
Magnetic field control of coupling selectivity.
The coupling between magnons and photons is highly tunable by an external magnetic field. At zero field, the coupling is selective: only one of the two antiferromagnetic resonance modes couples to the photon, forming a bright magnon-polariton, while the other remains dark. Under an applied magnetic field, this selectivity is lifted, and both magnon modes couple to the Swihart mode, with the coupling becoming equal for both modes in the limit of a strong field.

In general case, the magnon-photon coupling results in three magnon-polariton branches with modified dispersions. These hybrid quasiparticles exhibit remarkable properties. First of all, the magnon-polaritons carry a non-integer average spin
\langle S_{z}\rangle<\hbar
, which is tunable by both the external magnetic field and the wavevector. A notable prediction is the switching of the spin sign for the middle polariton branch upon varying the wavevector, a consequence of anticrossing mediated by the Swihart mode. The second important property is the high group velocity of the magnon-polaritons. In the strong mixing region, the group velocities of the magnon-polaritons can reach a significant fraction of the speed of light, vastly exceeding typical magnon group velocities, which promises strong tunability of magnon transport in antiferromagnets by superconductors.

The classical approach based on the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation and Maxwell’s equations yields results fully consistent with the quantum formalism. The classical analysis provides an intuitive picture of the magnetization precession polarization for the hybrid modes, illustrating the elliptical precession and the relative configurations of the two antiferromagnetic sublattices that underpin the coupling mechanism and the dark/bright mode behavior.

Ultimately, the S/AF/S heterostructure emerges as a versatile platform for THz quantum magnonics. The synergy of ultrastrong coupling, magnetic tunability, and fast, low-loss magnon-polaritons opens a path to creating ultrafast, energy-efficient devices for spintronics and quantum information processing.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\Omega_{s}^{2}+\frac{(1+i\sqrt{3})\tilde{C}}{2^{\frac{2}{3}}\tilde{A}}-\frac{(1-i\sqrt{3})\tilde{A}}{2\sqrt[3]{2}}},

\displaystyle\Omega_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\Omega_{s}^{2}+\frac{(1-i\sqrt{3})\tilde{C}}{2^{\frac{2}{3}}\tilde{A}}-\frac{(1+i\sqrt{3})\tilde{A}}{2\sqrt[3]{2}}},

(108)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{A}

\displaystyle=\sqrt[3]{\tilde{B}+\sqrt{4\tilde{C}^{3}+\tilde{B}^{2}}},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=2(\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{6}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{6}+\Omega_{s}^{6})-3(\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{4}\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{4}\Omega_{s}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{4}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\Omega_{s}^{4}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{4}\Omega_{s}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}\Omega_{s}^{4})+12\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}\Omega_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle+36(|g_{1}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{1}\Omega_{s}(\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}+\Omega_{s}^{2})+|g_{2}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}\Omega_{s}(\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\Omega_{s}^{2}))-72\tilde{\omega}_{1}\tilde{\omega}_{2}\Omega_{s}(|g_{1}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}+|g_{2}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{1}),

\displaystyle\tilde{C}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{2}\Omega_{s}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{2}\Omega_{s}^{2}-\tilde{\omega}_{1}^{4}-\tilde{\omega}_{2}^{4}-\Omega_{s}^{4}-12\Omega_{s}(|g_{1}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{1}+|g_{2}|^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{2}).

(109)

Appendix C: calculation of the average spin on the eigenmodes

In this Appendix for simplicity we will use the notation
\hbar=1
. In terms of the bosonic operators
\hat{a}_{i}(\bm{r})
the average magnon spin
\langle S_{z}\rangle
takes the form [see Eq. (
35
)]

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle=\langle S_{1z}\rangle+\langle S_{2z}\rangle=\frac{1}{2d_{AF}}\int d^{2}{\bm{\rho}}dx\sum_{{\bm{k}}{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\Bigl(\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\hat{a}_{1,{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle e^{i({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}-\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\hat{a}_{2,{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle e^{i({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{k}})\cdot{\bm{\rho}}}\Bigr)=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}\Bigl(\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\hat{a}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\hat{a}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\rangle\Bigr),

(110)

which can be rewritten via the magnon operators
\hat{m}_{1(2),{\bm{k}}}
with the use of Eq. (
38
):

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}\Bigl(\langle\hat{m}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\hat{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\rangle-\langle\hat{m}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\hat{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\rangle\Bigr).

(111)

The photonic part of the excitation does not contribute to
\langle S_{z}\rangle
because it is linearly polarized in the plane perpendicular to
\bm{k}
. The operators
\hat{m}_{1(2),{\bm{k}}}
can be expressed via the new magnon operators
\tilde{m}_{1(2),{\bm{k}}}
(
46
) as

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}=\frac{h_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}}\tilde{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}+\frac{\alpha_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}}\tilde{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}},~~~~\hat{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}}=\frac{h_{-}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}}\tilde{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}+\frac{\alpha_{+}}{\sqrt{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}}\tilde{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}},

(112)

where
\alpha_{\pm}=\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0}\pm\sqrt{(\mu_{0}\gamma H_{0})^{2}+h_{-}^{2}}
. Then for the average spin, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\tilde{m}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\rangle+\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\tilde{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}}\rangle

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{-}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{+}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{1,{\bm{k}}}\tilde{m}_{2,{\bm{k}}}+\mathrm{H.c.}\rangle\Bigr].

(113)

As in the case
k\neq 0
the magnons are coupled to the Swihart mode and the operators
\tilde{m}_{1(2),{\bm{k}}}
do not correspond to the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian (
53
), we need to rewrite Eq. (
113
) in terms of the operators which diagonalize
\hat{H}_{tot}
. For this purpose we express the Hamiltonian in the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{tot}=\sum_{k>0}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}_{k}{\cal H}_{m}\hat{\psi}_{k},

(114)

where

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\omega}_{1}&0&0&0&g^{\ast}_{1}&g^{\ast}_{1}\\
0&\tilde{\omega}_{2}&0&0&g^{\ast}_{2}&g^{\ast}_{2}\\
0&0&\tilde{\omega}_{1}&0&g_{1}&g_{1}\\
0&0&0&\tilde{\omega}_{2}&g_{2}&g_{2}\\
g_{1}&g_{2}&g^{\ast}_{1}&g^{\ast}_{2}&\Omega_{s}&0\\
g_{1}&g_{2}&g^{\ast}_{1}&g^{\ast}_{2}&0&\Omega_{s}\end{pmatrix},~~~~~\hat{\psi}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{m}_{1,{k}}\\
\tilde{m}_{2,{k}}\\
\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{1,-{k}}\\
\tilde{m}^{\dagger}_{2,-{k}}\\
\hat{p}_{k}\\
\hat{p}^{\dagger}_{-k}\end{pmatrix}.

(115)

We then diagonalize
\hat{H}_{tot}
with the Bogoliubov transformation

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{k}=\hat{S}\hat{b}_{k},

(116)

where
\hat{b}_{k}=(\beta_{1,k},\beta_{2,k},\beta_{3,k},\beta^{\dagger}_{1,-k},\beta^{\dagger}_{2,-k},\beta^{\dagger}_{3,-k})^{T}
is the vector of magnon-polariton operators
\beta_{i,k}
satisfying the relation

\displaystyle[\hat{b}_{k},\hat{H}_{tot}]=\hat{\Omega}\hat{b}_{k},~~~~\hat{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}\Omega_{1}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&\Omega_{2}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&\Omega_{3}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&-\Omega_{1}&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&-\Omega_{2}&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&-\Omega_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(117)

\Omega_{1,2,3}
are the magnon-polariton eigenfrequencies. Let us define the matrix
\hat{\xi}=\hat{S}^{-1}
. The condition expressed by Eq. (
117
) gives us that the rows of
\hat{\xi}
are the eigenvectors of the matrix
\hat{Y}{\cal H}^{T}_{m}
,
\hat{Y}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1,-1,1,-1)
, and
\pm\Omega_{1,2,3}
are the eigenvalues of this matrix. The commutation relations for the magnon-polariton operators read

\displaystyle[\beta_{i,k},\beta_{j,k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\hat{Y}_{2ii}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kk^{\prime}},~~~~\hat{Y}_{2}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1),

(118)

which leads to

\displaystyle\hat{S}=\hat{Y}\hat{\xi}^{\dagger}\hat{Y}_{2}.

(119)

Then let us rewrite Eq. (
113
) in terms of the components of the vector
\hat{\psi}_{k}
:

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{{k>0}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{1k}^{\dagger}\psi_{1k}+\psi_{3k}^{\dagger}\psi_{3k}-1\rangle+\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{2k}^{\dagger}\psi_{2k}+\psi_{4k}^{\dagger}\psi_{4k}-1\rangle

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{-}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{+}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{1k}^{\dagger}\psi_{2k}+\psi_{2k}^{\dagger}\psi_{1k}+\psi_{4k}^{\dagger}\psi_{3k}+\psi_{3k}^{\dagger}\psi_{4k}\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{{k>0}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{1k}^{\dagger}\psi_{1k}+\psi_{3k}^{\dagger}\psi_{3k}\rangle+\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{2k}^{\dagger}\psi_{2k}+\psi_{4k}^{\dagger}\psi_{4k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{-}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{+}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)\langle\psi_{1k}^{\dagger}\psi_{2k}+\psi_{2k}^{\dagger}\psi_{1k}+\psi_{4k}^{\dagger}\psi_{3k}+\psi_{3k}^{\dagger}\psi_{4k}\rangle\Bigr].

(120)

The final step to obtain the expressions for the average spin on the eigenmodes is to rewrite Eq. (
120
) via the operators
\beta_{i,k}
. From Eq. (
116
) we get

\displaystyle\psi_{nk}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}S_{ni}b_{ik},

(121)

then Eq. (
120
) takes the form

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{{k>0}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{6}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{1j}^{\ast}S_{1i}+S_{3j}^{\ast}S_{3i})+\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{2j}^{\ast}S_{2i}+S_{4j}^{\ast}S_{4i})

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{-}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{+}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{1j}^{\ast}S_{2i}+S_{2j}^{\ast}S_{1i}+S_{4j}^{\ast}S_{3i}+S_{3j}^{\ast}S_{4i})\Bigr]\langle b_{jk}^{\dagger}b_{ik}\rangle.

(122)

If we consider a state of the system with one magnon-polariton in the mode
\Omega_{i}(k)
, the only non-zero contribution to the sum in Eq. (
122
) will be from
\langle b_{ik}^{\dagger}b_{ik}\rangle=1
. Then the final expression for the average spin on the eigenmode
\Omega_{i}(k)
reads

\displaystyle\langle S_{z}\rangle_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{1i}^{\ast}S_{1i}+S_{3i}^{\ast}S_{3i})+\Bigl(\frac{\alpha_{-}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{+}^{2}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{2i}^{\ast}S_{2i}+S_{4i}^{\ast}S_{4i})

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{-}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{-}^{2}}-\frac{h_{-}\alpha_{+}}{h_{-}^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}}\Bigr)(S_{1i}^{\ast}S_{2i}+S_{2i}^{\ast}S_{1i}+S_{4i}^{\ast}S_{3i}+S_{3i}^{\ast}S_{4i})\Bigr].

(123)

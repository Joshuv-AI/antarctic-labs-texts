A simple perturbation of Vafa-Witten equations and a transversality result
Bo Dai, Ren Guan

Abstract
. We consider a simple perturbation of the Vafa-Witten equations, and prove that for generic perturbation parameter, the full rank part of the perturbed Vafa-Witten moduli space satisfies transversality condition, when the structure group is S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) or S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) .



1.
Introduction

The Vafa-Witten equations came from a topological twist of
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory. Vafa and Witten conjectured that the partition function of their theory transforms as modular form under
S
-duality, an important duality in quantum field theory.

Let
X
be a smooth, oriented, Riemannian 4-manifold,
P
be a principal bundle over
X
with structure group
G
,
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
be the adjoint bundle of
P
. The Vafa-Witten equations have the following form:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d_{A}^{*}B+d_{A}C=0,\\
&F_{A}^{+}+\frac{1}{8}[B\centerdot B]+\frac{1}{2}[B,C]=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
A
is a connection of the principal bundle
P
,
B
is a self-dual 2-form with values in the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
,
C
is a section of the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
, and
[B\centerdot B]
is a combination of the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
and the Lie algebra structure of
\Lambda^{2,+}\mathbb{R}^{4}\cong so(3)
. The Vafa-Witten equations are invariant under the action of gauge group. The Vafa-Witten moduli space is the space of solutions to the Vafa-Witten equations modulo gauge transformation.

Mares and Taubes did fundamental work to the analytic aspect of Vafa-Witten theory,
[
8
,
13
]
, but key challenges remain, such as the compactification and transversality of the Vafa-Witten moduli spaces. Tanaka and Thomas made key contribution to the algebraic aspect of Vafa-Witten theory. They defined Vafa-Witten invariants for projective surfaces by the method of derived algebraic geometry and virtual intersection theory, and verified
S
-duality in some cases,
[
11
,
12
]
. Since then, the algebraic aspect of Vafa-Witten theory developed rapidly.

In this short note, we consider a simple perturbed version of the Vafa-Witten equations, and obtain a transversality result of the perturbed Vafa-Witten moduli spaces.

The perturbed Vafa-Witten equations on
X
are

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d_{A}^{*}B+d_{A}C=0,\\
&F_{A}^{+}+\frac{1}{8}[B\centerdot B]+\frac{1}{2}[B,C]+\tau B=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where the triple
(A,B,C)
is as above,
\tau
is a section of the bundle
\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}T^{*}X)
. We note that

\tau
acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
trivially, i.e., as identity.
Thus the perturbed Vafa-Witten equations are invariant under gauge group action,
and the perturbed moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{VW,\tau}(P)
is well-defined.

To apply analytic tools, we need to work on appropriate Sobolev spaces. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{A}_{k}(P)\times L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{2,+})\times L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}(P)

\displaystyle:=L_{k-1}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{1})\times L_{k-1}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{2,+}),

be the configuration spaces,
C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))
be the parameter space, where
r>k\geq 2
are integers. Let
\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(P)
be the group of
L_{k+1}^{2}
gauge transformations. In the remaining part of this note, we take the structure group
G
to be
SU(2)
or
SO(3)
, and follow notations in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a closed, oriented, smooth, Riemannian 4-manifold, and
P\to X
be a principal
SU(2)
- or
SO(3)
-bundle. Then for generic perturbation
\tau\in C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))
, where
r>k\geq 2
are integers, the full rank part of the moduli space,
\mathcal{M}_{VW,\tau}^{(3)}(P)
, is a smooth, oriented, zero-dimensional manifold in the quotient space
\mathcal{B}_{k}(P)=\mathcal{C}_{k}(P)/\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(P)
.

2.
Prove of the main theorem

Consider the parameterized Vafa-Witten map

(2.1)

\displaystyle F:C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))\times\mathcal{C}_{k}(P)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}(P),

\displaystyle F(\tau,A,B,C)

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}d_{A}^{*}B+d_{A}C\\
F_{A}^{+}+\frac{1}{8}[B\centerdot B]+\frac{1}{2}[B,C]+\tau B\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.1
.

With notation in (
2.1
), at any point
(\tau,A,B,C)
where
F(\tau,A,B,C)=0
and
B
has rank 3, the differential
dF
at this point is surjective.

Proof.

The differential
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}
differs from
d^{1}_{(A,B,C)}
in
[
8
]
page 28 slightly, given by

(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}:C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{1})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{2,+})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})\to\mathcal{C}_{k-1}^{\prime}(P),

(2.2)

(\delta\tau,a,b,c)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}d_{A}^{*}b+d_{A}c-[B\centerdot a]-[C,a]\\
d_{A}^{+}a+\frac{1}{4}[B\centerdot b]+\frac{1}{2}[b,C]+\frac{1}{2}[B,c]+\tau b+(\delta\tau)B\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The linearization of the action of gauge group
\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(P)
at a point
(\tau,A,B,C)\in C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))\times\mathcal{C}_{k}(P)

is given by

d_{(\tau,A,B,C)}^{0}:L_{k+1}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})\to C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{1})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{2,+})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}),

(2.3)

\xi\mapsto(0,d_{A}\xi,[B,\xi],[C,\xi]).

The
L^{2}
-adjoint of
d_{(\tau,A,B,C)}^{0}
is

(2.4)

d_{(\tau,A,B,C)}^{0,*}(\delta\tau,a,b,c)=d_{A}^{*}a+[b\cdot B]+[c,C].

Since
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}+d_{(\tau,A,B,C)}^{0,*}
is a family of first order elliptic operators depending on
\delta\tau
linearly,
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}
has closed image. Assume
F(\tau,A,B,C)=0
and
B
has rank 3. By the regularity theorem for elliptic equations with
C^{r}
coefficients (cf.
[
3
]
, Proposition 3.7 and Remark 5.1), we may assume that
(A,B,C)
is a
C^{r}
solution of the perturbed Vafa-Witten equations (
1.2
). To prove that
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}
is surjective, it suffices to show that
(\mathrm{Im}(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)})^{\perp}=0
. Suppose
(\phi,\psi)\in(\mathrm{Im}(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)})^{\perp}
. Then we have

(2.5)

\langle d_{A}^{*}b+d_{A}c-[B\centerdot a]-[C,a],\phi\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle d_{A}^{+}a+\frac{1}{4}[B\centerdot b]+\frac{1}{2}[b,C]+\frac{1}{2}[B,c]+\tau b+(\delta\tau)B,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=0

for all
(\delta\tau,a,b,c)\in C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{1})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}\otimes\Lambda^{2,+})\oplus L_{k}^{2}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})
.

Computing the
L^{2}
-adjoint operator of
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}
from equation (
2.5
), we deduce that
(\phi,\psi)
satisfy the following system of equations

(2.6)

\begin{cases}d_{A}^{*}\psi-[\phi\centerdot B]-[\phi,C]=0,&\\
d_{A}^{+}\phi+\frac{1}{4}[\psi\centerdot B]-\frac{1}{2}[\psi,C]+\tau^{t}\psi=0,&\\
d_{A}^{*}\phi+\frac{1}{2}[\psi\cdot B]=0,&\\
\langle(\delta\tau)B,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\ \forall\,\delta\tau\in C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+})).&\\
\end{cases}

Note that the first three equations in (
2.6
) differ from the expression for the operator
d^{1,*}_{(B,C,A)}
on page 29 of
[
8
]
by one term
\tau^{t}\psi
, where
\tau^{t}
is the transpose of
\tau
with respect to the Riemannian metric on the bundle
\Lambda^{2,+}
.

Since
\delta\tau\in C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))
is arbitrary, from the 4th equation of (
2.6
), we deduce that
\psi=0
on
X^{(3)}(B)
, where
B
has pointwise rank 3.

The first equation in (
2.6
) implies that

(2.7)

[\phi\centerdot B]+[\phi,C]=0

on
X^{(3)}(B)
. Lemma
2.3
below implies that
\phi=0
on
X^{(3)}(B)
.

The first three equations in (
2.6
) is a system of first order linear differential equations of elliptic type for
(\phi,\psi)
with
C^{r}
coefficients. By the unique continuation theorem (cf
[
1
,
7
]
), we deduce that
(\phi,\psi)=(0,0)
on
X
. This implies that
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}
is surjective.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{VW}(P)=\bigcup_{\tau}\mathcal{M}_{VW,\tau}(P)
be the parameterized Vafa-Witten moduli space, where
\tau
ranges over
C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+}))
, and
\mathfrak{M}_{VW}^{(3)}(P)
be the full rank part of the parameterized Vafa-Witten moduli space where the component
B
has rank 3. Then
\mathfrak{M}_{VW}^{(3)}(P)
is an (infinite dimensional) smooth manifold. The projection

(2.8)

\pi:\mathfrak{M}_{VW}^{(3)}(P)\to C^{r}(X,\mathfrak{gl}(\Lambda^{2,+})),\quad[(\tau,A,B,C)]\mapsto\tau,

is Fredholm of index 0.

Proof.

By Lemma 1 in
[
2
]
, when
B
has rank 3, we have
\mathrm{Stab}(A,B,C)=Z(G)
. Lemma
2.1
implies that
\mathfrak{M}_{VW}^{(3)}(P)
is a smooth manifold. For fixed
\tau
,
(dF)_{(\tau,A,B,C)}+d_{(\tau,A,B,C)}^{0,*}
is a first order elliptic operator of index 0. Thus the projection
\pi
is Fredholm of index 0.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
By Corollary
2.2
and the Sard-Smale theorem (cf.
[

]
, Theorem A.5.1), for generic perturbation
\tau
in the sense of second category, the full rank part of the perturbed Vafa-Witten moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{VW,\tau}^{(3)}(P)
is a smooth 0-dimensional manifold. The orientation of the moduli space is established in
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
. ∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that

has rank 3, and
C\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
. Then the linear map

\displaystyle L_{B,C}:\mathfrak{su}(2)\otimes\Lambda^{1}\mathbb{R}^{4}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{su}(2)\otimes\Lambda^{1}\mathbb{R}^{4}

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\mapsto[B\centerdot\phi]+[C,\phi]

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

\mathfrak{su}(2)\otimes\Lambda^{2,+}\mathbb{R}^{4}

[
8

, and

,

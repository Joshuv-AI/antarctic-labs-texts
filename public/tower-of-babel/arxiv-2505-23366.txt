On the spectra of holographic QFTs on constant curvature manifolds
Ahmad Ghodsi, Elias Kiritsis, Parisa Mashayekhi, and Francesco Nitti

Abstract
We analyze linear fluctuations of five-dimensional Einstein-Dilaton theories dual to holographic quantum field theories defined on four-dimensional de Sitter and Anti-de Sitter space-times. We identify the physical propagating scalar and tensor degrees of freedom. For these, we write the linearized bulk field equations as eigenvalue equations. In the dual QFT, the eigenstates correspond to towers of spin-0 and spin-2 particles propagating on ( A ) ​ d ​ S 4 (A)dS_{4} associated to gauge-invariant composite states. Using particular care in treating special “zero-modes,” we show in general that, for negative curvature, the particle spectra are always discrete, whereas for positive curvature they always have a continuous component starting at m 2 = ( 9 / 4 ) ​ α − 2 m^{2}=(9/4)\alpha^{-2} , where α \alpha is the ( A ) ​ d ​ S 4 (A)dS_{4} radius. We numerically compute the spectra in a concrete model characterized by a polynomial dilaton bulk potential admitting holographic RG-flow solutions with a UV and IR fixed points. In this case, we find no discrete spectrum and no perturbative instabilities.

Introduction and Summary

When quantum field theories (QFT) are considered on a curved space-time, new phenomena may appear with respect to their Minkowski counterparts. Since curvature is a relevant operator, its effect is more significant in the IR regime of the field theory. Given that many interesting 4-dimensional QFTs are strongly coupled in the IR, one often needs non-perturbative methods to understand how curvature affects these theories.

Summary of Results

The framework we work on is a holographic theory whose gravity side consists of
5

\Phi

ds^{2}=du^{2}+e^{2A(u)}\zeta_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\qquad\Phi=\Phi(u)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\qquad\mu,\nu=1\ldots 4\,.

(1.3)

Here

is the holographic coordinate, the coordinates

parameterize the constant-
u

slice metric

\zeta_{\mu\nu}
is a
u

4

u
slices, whose Ricci tensor is given by:

R^{(\zeta)}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\zeta_{\mu\nu}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\qquad\kappa=\pm\frac{3}{\alpha^{2}}\,,

(1.4)

and we denote by

the radius of the corresponding manifold

, dS

, AdS

The main goals we achieve in this work are:

1.

To isolate the decoupled physical degrees of freedom describing linearized perturbations around curved holographic RG flows;

2.

To write the corresponding linearized equation in the form of a spectral problem giving the mass eigenvalues
m^{2}_{\alpha}
;

3.

To understand the universal features of the mass spectra as a function of the boundary curvature and the type of IR geometry;

4.

Finally, we numerically obtain the full spectrum, for positive and negative curvature, for a simple theory admitting (in flat space) holographic RG flows which connect two fixed points.

The analysis follows the same lines which, for flat slice metric
\zeta_{\mu\nu}

[

for 4–dimensional theories. Here, we generalize that analysis to non-zero curvature slices.

It is worth noting that the problem we study here is related by a Wick-rotation to the problem of cosmological perturbations around a Friedman-Robertson-Walker (FRW) space-time with arbitrary constant spatial curvature. There, the holographic coordinate

This leads to a different (compared to our case) treatment of the zero-modes, which appear in the tensorial decomposition of perturbations, and to which we dedicate a careful treatment. In the rest of this introduction, we briefly summarize our results.

Gauge invariant degrees of freedom.

As in the case of flat slicing, the set of all metric and scalar field fluctuations around the background (
1.3
) is subject to gauge transformations consisting of
5
–dimensional diffeomorphisms. These, together with the constraints from Einstein’s equations, remove all but the following propagating degrees of freedom, which are conveniently parameterized in terms of their tensorial properties with respect to the
4
–dimensional slice:

1.

A gauge-invariant
scalar mode

\lambda(u,x^{\mu})
, which can be taken as a combination of the metric trace and the perturbation of
\Phi
;

2.

A gauge-invariant symmetric
tensor mode

h_{\mu\nu}^{TT}(u,x^{\mu})
, which is transverse and traceless with respect to the slice metric:

\nabla^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}^{TT}=\zeta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}^{TT}=0\,,

(1.5)

where
\nabla^{\mu}
is the covariant derivative of the slice metric
\zeta_{\mu\nu}
. These modes are the bulk gravitons.

This decomposition is universal, i.e., independent of the scalar field potential. It only depends on the two-derivative nature of the Einstein-Dilaton action. In these theories, there are no dynamical vector perturbations.

What is described above is the same gauge-invariant field content of the perturbations around flat-sliced solutions, or around an FRW space-time in a theory with a single scalar field or a single fluid.

Spectral equations.

By combining various components of Einstein’s equation, we obtained decoupled linearized equations for the gauge-invariant scalar and tensor modes.
These take the form of linear second-order partial differential equations, which can be separated in a standard way by setting:

h_{\mu\nu}^{TT}(u,x^{\mu})=h(u)h_{\mu\nu}^{(M),TT}(x^{\mu})\;\;\;,\;\;\;\lambda(u,x^{\mu})=\lambda(u)Y_{m}(x^{\mu})\,,

(1.6)

where
h_{\mu\nu}^{(M),TT}(x^{\mu})
and
Y_{m}(x^{\mu})
satisfy the spin-2 and spin-0 massive field equation on
M_{d}
with metric
\zeta_{\mu\nu}
:

\left(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-m^{2}\right)Y_{m}=0\;\;\;,\;\;\;\big(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-\frac{2}{3}\kappa-M^{2}\big)h_{\mu\nu}^{(M),TT}=0\,,

(1.7)

where
\kappa
is the slice curvature, defined in (
1.4
). The reader will recognize that the tensor modes in (
1.7
) satisfy the spin-2 Pauli-Fierz equation around a constant curvature background, with
M
being the Pauli-Fierz mass parameter.

The coefficient functions
h(u)
and
\lambda(u)
satisfy
M
and
m

\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+V_{g}(y)\right)\psi_{g}(y)=M^{2}\psi_{g}(y)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}+V_{s}(y;m)\right)\psi_{s}(y)=0\,.

(1.8)

\psi_{g,s}
are related to
h
and
\lambda
by an appropriate re-scaling, and
y
is the bulk conformal coordinate, related to
u
by the warp factor
A(u)
of the metric (
1.3

dy=e^{-2A(u)}du.

(1.9)

The effective
Schrödinger potentials

and

depend only on the functions (and their derivatives) specifying the background solution (
1.3
), i.e.,
A(y)
and
\Phi(y)

The graviton Schrödinger potential takes a particularly simple form, which is in fact the same as for flat slicing
[
35
]

V_{g}=\left(\frac{dB}{dy}\right)^{2}-\frac{d^{2}B}{dy^{2}}\;\;\;,\;\;\;B(y)\equiv\frac{3}{2}A(y)\,.

(1.10)

i.e., it depends only on the metric scale factor
A(y)
and not on the dilaton
2
2

2

Notice, however, that

The scalar potential

Comparing equations (
1.7

1.8

In maximally symmetric space-times, these are classified by representations of the corresponding (pseudo)-rotation groups: solving the Schrödinger equations tells us which representations appear, and also whether the corresponding masses satisfy the appropriate stability bounds (which generalize the flat-slice bounds
m^{2}\geq 0

While the details of the actual spectra depend on the specific form of the bulk solution, a great deal of information can be obtained by looking at the asymptotic form of the Schrödinger potentials, which, as it turns out, is rather universal. Below we order the discussion according to the sign of the curvature.

Zero Modes.

Before gauge fixing, the perturbations can be divided into tensors (
h_{\mu\nu}
), vectors (

), and scalars (
h_{yy},\delta\Phi
) with respect to the transverse manifold coordinate transformations. These modes appeared coupled to each other in Einstein’s equation, and the standard way to obtain decoupled equations is to further split them into longitudinal and transverse with respect to the slice covariant derivative. This decomposition is well defined, except when the modes correspond to special eigenvalues (
zero-modes
) of the slice-Laplacian
\nabla^{2}\equiv\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}
. These special values are:

•

Scalar zero modes:

\nabla^{2}_{s}=0,\,-\frac{4}{3}\kappa

•

Vector zero modes:

\nabla^{2}_{v}=-\kappa

where
\nabla^{2}_{s}
and
\nabla^{2}_{v}
are the scalar and vector covariant Laplacians of

In the presence of these zero-modes, the standard transverse/longitudinal decomposition is ill-defined, and one needs to proceed in a different way. To this, we decompose the fluctuations into appropriate bases of eigen-functions of tensor, vector, and scalar Laplacians, and then we derive the radial equations
without
having to decompose in longitudinal and transverse modes. This method actually gives an alternative derivation of the decoupled equations not only for the zero-modes but for
any
eigenvalue of the Laplacian, and it is presented in Appendix
D

It is worth noting that the scalar zero-mode corresponding to the eigenvalue

decouples, since its coefficient in the quadratic action vanishes

3

Negative curvature spectra.

For negative curvature, one can again find acceptable solutions with one UV boundary and

Explicit results and full spectra.

We use the general formalism developed so far to obtain the full spectra in two situations:

•

Exact AdS

As a warm-up, we recover analytically the spectrum of gravitons on exact AdS
5
with constant dilaton. In this case, there are no propagating scalar modes in the metric, and only the spin-2 survives. In addition there is the standard spin-0 dilaton fluctuation, which is decoupled from the metric obeys the Klein-Gordon equation on AdS
5
.
The spin-2 Schrödinger potential
V_{g}
has a simple analytic form from which one can obtain the spectra analytically: for dS
4
slices, this is a continuum starting at the Higuchi bound; for AdS
4
slices, we obtain analytically a tower of discrete eigenvalues which reproduces the known result of
[
56
]
.

•

Holographic RG flow

We perform a full numerical analysis for the case of a polynomial bulk potential with two maxima (thus supporting two possible UV fixed points of the dual QFT). This potential was used in
[
26
]
to study holographic RG-flows on negative-curvature space-times. We obtain numerically the background solutions for positive and negative curvature, from which we derive the corresponding spin-0 and spin-2 Schrödinger potentials. We solve the Schrödinger equations numerically and obtain the spectrum, showing that there are no normalizable eigenstates which violate the stability bound of the curved slice manifold. In the case of positive curvature, we find no states in the discrete spectrum.

1.2
Discussion and open problems

The results in this work give the general equations which can be used to compute the mass spectra of spin-2 and spin-0 fluctuations around any given holographic Einstein-Dilaton background with constant curvature slices. Moreover, we established certain generic, model-independent features:

•

For positive curvature, the continuous spectrum starts at
m^{2}=9/4\alpha^{-2}
for both spin-0 and spin-2.

•

For negative curvature, the spectrum is discrete.

The first point implies that for positive curvature all states in the continuous spectrum are in the spin-0 and spin-2
principal series
representations of the de Sitter group
6
6

6

See Appendix
I
for a review of dS
4
unitary representations.
. These are the “heavy” fields, with
m^{2}>(9/4)H^{2}
, where
H\equiv\alpha^{-1}
is the dS
4
Hubble constant.

These representations are stable; hence, our results prove in full generality that no instabilities can appear in the continuous part of the spectrum.

Beyond these statements, some interesting questions remain open about what the general features of the spectra are in various situations:

1.

What are the general features of the spectra for positive curvature, and in particular, can other representations of the de Sitter group appear? Can there be eigenvalues in the
complementary series
, i.e. scalars with to
0<m^{2}<9/4\alpha^{-2}
and massive gravitons with
2\alpha^{-2}<m^{2}<9/4\alpha^{-2}
? These would still be stable perturbations, but by our general result, can only occur as discrete eigenvalues.

Finally, are there pathological models which feature discrete representations in the exceptional (discrete) series ( spin-0 with
m^{2}=-j(j+3)
with
j
integer, and spin-2 with
M^{2}=0,2
)? The presence of such modes would lead to a perturbative instability, except for the case of a massless spin-2.

We note that, for 2-derivative Einstein-dilaton theories on
flat
slices, one can prove that the spectrum is non-negative in general, as long as the scalar field satisfies the null-energy condition and under certain mild assumptions on the dilaton potential
7
7

7

The potential growth as
\Phi\to\infty
must satisfy a
computability bound

[
12
,
20
]
. Therefore, these putative instabilities, if they arise, would be specific for the curved-space theory.

2.

The questions above are particularly interesting in the case of confining holographic theories in general
[
30
]
and specifically Improved Holographic QCD
[
31
]
in the case of positive curvature. These theories may exhibit phase transitions at finite curvature, so one may learn something interesting about the transition by looking at spectra in different phases.

3.

Similar questions may be asked for negative curvature, with the additional nuance that here one may have background solutions with two UV boundaries, like the one considered in this paper (holographic interfaces) but also with a single boundary, in the case where the dual theory is confining (on flat space)
[
28
]
. What kind of spectra can arise in both cases? May there be instabilities for negative curvature, when none are found for flat slicing?

4.

A specific question that arises for the two-boundary case, i.e., holographic interfaces, is how to compute spectra associated to
interface operators
in the holographic theories. What is the right holographic setup for the calculation, and what kind of spectra can arise?

5.

Finally, given the fluctuation equations we derived here, one can compute not only spectra but also holographic correlation functions on curved space-times. These require only changing the UV boundary condition in the fluctuation (from normalizable to non-normalizable) and contain a great deal of information beyond the spectra.

This work is organized as follows. In Section 2, we display our setup and give a short review of curved holographic RG flows. Section 3 presents the linearized Einstein’s equations, the decomposition of the fluctuations into scalar, vector, and tensor modes; we identify the propagating degrees of freedom and, for these, we write decoupled second-order equations. We identify special values of the four-dimensional Laplacian eigenvalues (zero-modes) which have to be treated separately (which we do in Appendix D). In Section 4, we separate variables and decompose the perturbation equations into a one-dimensional Schrödinger equation and four-dimensional mode equations. In Section 5, we investigate the normalizability conditions and give the conditions for mode stability. In Section 6, we show, as a first example, how to apply the formalism to recover the exact spectrum of the perturbation of exact five-dimensional Anti-de Sitter in the absence of a bulk dilaton. In Section 7 and 8, we fully solve the spectral problem numerically for a concrete example of a polynomial bulk potential admitting two UV fixed points for negative slice curvature and regular IR to UV for positive slice curvature.
Several technical details (including a detailed treatment of the zero modes) can be found in the Appendix.

5.3
The stability of tensor and scalar fluctuations

In this section, we study the stability of our background solutions under small field fluctuations.
We already showed that in general, the scalar and graviton modes satisfy the following Laplace equations on the 4–dimensional slices

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}Y(x)=m^{2}Y(x)\;\;\;,\;\;\;\left(\nabla_{\rho}\nabla^{\rho}-\frac{2}{3}\kappa\right)h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}(x)=M^{2}h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}(x)\,.

(5.11)

We can unify the above relations into a unique relation depending on the sign of the slice’s curvature
[
57
]

\left(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}-\frac{1}{3}s\kappa\right)\delta h=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\nu^{2}-\frac{9}{4}\right)\delta h\,\qquad dS\\
\\
-k^{2}\delta h\,\qquad\qquad Minkowski\,,\\
\\
\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\nu^{2}-\frac{9}{4}\right)\delta h\,\qquad AdS\end{cases}

(5.12)

where
s
is the spin of the fluctuations (
s=0
for scalar and
s=2
for graviton) and
\delta h=Y,h^{TT}_{\mu\nu}
. Moreover,
\alpha
is the curvature radius of (A)dS slices defined in (
2.8
). In (
5.12
) the parameter
\nu
is related to the mass of the fields as

\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(d-1)^{2}+4\mathfrak{m}^{2}\alpha^{2}}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\mathfrak{m}=m,M\,.

(5.13)

where
M
is the graviton masses, whereas
m
are the scalar masses.
The stability of the solutions under the field fluctuations is mainly affected by the presence of either a “tachyonic” or “ghost” mode:

•

Tachyonic stability

The criteria for the modes to be tachyon-stable in maximally symmetric four dimensional geometries are as follows
[
57
]

\text{The mode is tachyon-stable if}\begin{cases}|Re(\nu)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\qquad dS\\
\\
k^{2}\leq 0\qquad Minkowski\,,\\
\\
Re(\nu)\neq 0\qquad AdS\end{cases}

(5.14)

Independent of the spin, the above relations translate in terms of the mass to the following equation

\text{The mode is tachyon-stable if}\begin{cases}\mathfrak{m}^{2}\geq 0\qquad dS\\
\\
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\geq 0\qquad Minkowski\,,\\
\\
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\geq\frac{3}{4}\kappa\qquad AdS\end{cases}

(5.15)

where in the case of AdS space-time (
\kappa<0
), the above relation is the well-known BF bound.

In the dS
(4)
space-time, in order to have a positive norm state for graviton modes, the mass squared should satisfy

M^{2}\alpha^{2}>2\quad\Rightarrow\quad M^{2}>\frac{2}{3}\kappa\,,

(5.16)

which is known as the Higuchi bound
[
55
]
.

•

Ghost stability

Ghost instability corresponds to a mode with a wrong sign of the kinetic term. Concerning the actions corresponding to different modes, i.e. relation (
5.2
) for graviton and (
5.8
) for scalar we observe that the kinetic terms have the correct sign. There is no ghost instability in any one of these modes.

Appendix A
Some useful formulae for maximally symmetric slices

In a four dimensional maximally symmetric space

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{R^{(\zeta)}}{12}\left(\zeta_{\mu\rho}\zeta_{\nu\sigma}-\zeta_{\mu\sigma}\zeta_{\nu\rho}\right)\;\;\;,\;\;\;R_{\mu\nu}=R^{(\zeta)}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\zeta_{\mu\nu}\;\;\;,\;\;\;R^{(\zeta)}=4\kappa\,,

(A.1)

We obtain the following useful identities:

\displaystyle\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]T_{\rho\sigma}=-{R^{\lambda}}_{\rho\mu\nu}T_{\lambda\sigma}-{R^{\lambda}}_{\sigma\mu\nu}T_{\rho\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{3}\left(\zeta_{\rho\nu}T_{\mu\sigma}-\zeta_{\rho\mu}T_{\nu\sigma}+\zeta_{\sigma\nu}T_{\rho\mu}-\zeta_{\sigma\mu}T_{\rho\nu}\right)\,.

(A.2)

\left[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}\right]T_{\rho}=-{R^{\lambda}}_{\rho\mu\nu}T_{\lambda}=-\frac{\kappa}{3}\left(\zeta_{\rho\nu}T_{\mu}-\zeta_{\rho\mu}T_{\nu}\right)\,.

(A.3)

\nabla^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}T_{\nu}=\left(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}+\kappa\right)\nabla^{\nu}T_{\nu}\,,

(A.4)

2\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{(\mu}T_{\nu)}=\left(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}+\kappa\right)T_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu}\,,

(A.5)

\nabla_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}s=\left(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}-\kappa\right)\nabla_{\nu}s\,,

(A.6)

where
s
is a scalar field.

Appendix B
Derivation of the perturbations around the background solution

In this appendix we compute in detail the equations of motion and Lagrangian for the background field fluctuations, used in the main text.

The metric
g_{AB}
in terms of the background metric
g_{AB}^{(0)}
and its fluctuations
h_{AB}
is given by

ds^{2}=a^{2}(y)g_{AB}dx^{A}dx^{B}=a^{2}(y)\left(g^{(0)}_{AB}+h_{AB}(y,x^{\mu})\right)dx^{A}dx^{B}\,.

(B.1)

The inverse and determinant of the metric can be expanded around the background as follows

g^{AB}=g^{(0)AB}-h^{AB}+{h^{A}}_{C}h^{CB}+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\,,

(B.2)

Laplace operator on AdS

In this appendix, we briefly review the action of the Laplace operator in AdS and dS space-times on scalar, vector, and spin 2 fields. We also examine the solutions of the zero modes which we discussed in section

.

H.1
AdS
d
slices

Consider the AdS
d
space-time in Poincaré coordinate

ds^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{z^{2}}\Big(dz^{2}+\eta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\Big)\;\;\;,\;\;\;z\in[0,\infty)\,,

(H.1)

where
\alpha
is the curvature length scale and
\eta_{ij}
is the
(d-1)
–dimensional Minkowski space-time.
The non-zero components Christoffel connections associated to (
H.1

\Gamma^{z}_{zz}=-\frac{1}{z}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\Gamma^{z}_{ij}=\frac{1}{z}\eta_{ij}\;\;\;,\;\;\;\Gamma^{i}_{zj}=-\frac{1}{z}{\delta^{i}}_{\!j}\,.

(H.2)

H.1.1
Scalar field

The Laplacian of a scalar field in Poincare coordinates is (the indices are raised by
\eta^{ij}
)

\Box\phi(z,x)=\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big(\partial_{z}^{2}+\partial_{i}\,\partial^{i}-\frac{d-2}{z}\partial_{z}\Big)\phi(z,x)\,.

(H.3)

For a massive scalar field, the equation of motion is

(\Box-m^{2})\phi(z,x)=0\,.

(H.4)

By a Fourier transformation in
\mathbb{R}^{1,d-2}
,

\phi(z,x)=\int\frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,e^{-iq_{i}x^{i}}\phi(z,q)\,,

(H.5)

equation (
H.4
) becomes

\Big(z^{2}(\partial_{z}^{2}+q^{2})-z(d-2)\partial_{z}-\alpha^{2}m^{2}\Big)\phi(z,q)=0\,,

(H.6)

where we have considered
q^{i}q_{i}=\eta^{ij}q_{i}q_{j}=-q^{2}
.

The solution of the above equation is given in terms of the Bessel functions. In the coordinate space, the solution is

\phi(z,x)=\int\frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,e^{-iq_{i}x^{i}}f(q)z^{(d-1)/2}Z_{\nu}(\sqrt{q^{2}}z)\,,\qquad

(H.7)

where
f(q)
is an arbitrary function and

\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(d-1)^{2}+4m^{2}\alpha^{2}}\,,

(H.8)

In equation (
H.7
),
Z_{\nu}
stands for one of the two linearly independent solutions of the Bessel equation.

If we consider equation (
H.6
), and perform a change as
\phi(z,q)=z^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\tilde{\phi}(z,q)
, this equation can be written as the following Schrodinger-like equation

-\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(z,q)+V_{AdS}(z)\tilde{\phi}(z,q)=q^{2}\tilde{\phi}(z,q)\;\;\;,\;\;\;V_{AdS}(z)=\frac{4m^{2}\alpha^{2}+d(d-2)}{4z^{2}}\,.

(H.9)

Considering
q^{2}=-\eta^{ij}q_{i}q_{j}>0
, the above equation shows that for each mode, the value of
q^{2}=q_{0}^{2}-\vec{q}^{2}
is continuous for all values of the mass. This is because
q^{2}>0
no matter what the sign of
V_{AdS}
is. Therefore, the energy of each mode, i.e.
q_{0}
is continuous.

Expanding solution of (
H.6
) near the AdS boundary at
z=0
one finds

\phi(z,q)=C_{1}q^{-\nu}z^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\nu}+C_{2}q^{\nu}z^{\frac{d-1}{2}+\nu}+\cdots\,.

(H.10)

For masses with
m^{2}>0
we should choose

(vev solutions). However for massless mode the expansion is

\phi(z,q)=C_{1}q^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}+C_{2}q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}z^{d-1}+\cdots\,.

(H.11)

H.1.2
Vector field

The conventional Lagrangian for a massive gauge field is

L=-\frac{1}{4}{F}^{\mu\nu}{F}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}{A}_{\mu}{A}^{\mu}\,.

(H.12)

The equation of motion for the gauge field is then

0=\nabla_{\mu}{F}^{\mu\nu}-m^{2}{A}^{\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}(\nabla^{\mu}{A}^{\nu}-\nabla^{\nu}{A}^{\mu})-m^{2}{A}^{\nu}\,,

(H.13)

in which, after taking a divergence of the first line, it implies that
\nabla_{\mu}{A}^{\mu}=0
and (
H.13
) in a maximally symmetric space can be written as

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}{A}_{\nu}-(\kappa+m^{2}){A}_{\nu}=0.

(H.14)

The term
\kappa{A}_{\nu}
comes from the commutation of the covariant derivatives.

The zero mode that appeared already in equation (
C.11
) corresponds to a massive vector field with a mass

m^{2}=-2\kappa\,.

(H.15)

To find the solution of our vector field zero mode we start with the following equation

(\nabla_{\nu}\nabla^{\nu}+\kappa)A_{\mu}(z,x)=0\,,

(H.16)

where in the AdS
d
case we have
\alpha^{2}\kappa=-(d-1)
. Following
[
59
]
, we decompose the
\mu

z
and
i

For the
A_{z}

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}A_{z}=\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(\partial_{z}^{2}+\partial_{i}^{2})-\frac{1}{z^{2}}(2d-3)-\frac{1}{z}(d-4)\partial_{z}\Big]A_{z}+\frac{2z}{\alpha^{2}}\partial^{i}A_{i}\,.

(H.17)

So the equation (
H.16
) becomes

\partial_{z}^{2}A_{z}+\partial_{i}\,\partial^{i}A_{z}-\frac{d-4}{z}\partial_{z}A_{z}+\frac{2}{z}\,\partial_{i}A^{i}-\frac{2d-3}{z^{2}}A_{z}-\frac{d-1}{z^{2}}\,A_{z}=0\,.

(H.18)

The gauge condition
\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0
now reads as

\partial_{i}A^{i}=-\partial_{z}A_{z}+\frac{d-2}{z}A_{z}\,.

(H.19)

By using the above gauge condition and the Fourier transform of
A_{z}
, equation (
H.18
) becomes

\Big[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z^{2}q^{2}+z(2-d)\partial_{z}-d\Big]A_{z}(z,x)=0\,.

(H.20)

Similar to the scalar field, the solution for
A_{z}
is given by the Bessel function

A_{z}(z,x)=\int\frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,e^{-iq.x}\mathcal{A}_{z}(q)z^{(d-1)/2}Z_{\nu}(\sqrt{q^{2}}z)\,,

(H.21)

where
\mathcal{A}_{z}(q)
is an arbitrary function and

\nu=\frac{d+1}{2}\,.

(H.22)

For
A_{i}
components, equation (
H.16
) becomes

\Big[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z^{2}\partial_{j}\,\partial^{j}+(4-d)z\partial_{z}-2(d-1)\Big]A_{i}=2z\,\partial_{i}A_{z}\,.

(H.23)

Now as in
[
59
]
we introduce a vector field such that

\tilde{A}_{\mu}(z,x)=zA_{\mu}(z,x)\,.

(H.24)

Then the equation (
H.23
) changes to

\Big[z^{2}(\partial_{z}^{2}+\partial_{j}\,\partial^{j})+z(2-d)\partial_{z}-d\Big]\tilde{A}_{i}=2z\,\partial_{i}\tilde{A}_{z}\,.

(H.25)

The solution of the homogeneous part of the above equation is like the
A_{z}
-component, and it can be shown that the full solution has the following form

\tilde{A}_{i}(z,x)=\int\frac{d^{d-1}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,e^{-iq.x}z^{(d-1)/2}\Big[\mathcal{A}_{i}(q)Z_{\nu}(\sqrt{q^{2}}z)-iz\mathcal{A}_{z}(q)\frac{q_{i}}{q}Z_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{q^{2}}z)\Big]\,.

(H.26)

The gauge condition (
H.19
) in terms of
\tilde{A}_{\mu}
becomes

z(\partial_{z}\tilde{A}_{z}+\partial_{i}\tilde{A}_{i})-(d-1)\tilde{A}_{z}=0\,.

(H.27)

Inserting (
H.26
) and (
H.21
) in the above equation leads to

\mathcal{A}_{z}=\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{A}_{i}\,q_{i}\,.

(H.28)

(H.50)

By a change as
\phi(z,q)=z^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\tilde{\phi}(z,q)
the equation (
H.49
) becomes a Schrodinger equation as follow

-\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(z,q_{E})+V_{dS}(z)\tilde{\phi}(z,q_{E})=q_{E}^{2}\tilde{\phi}(z,q_{E})\;\;\;,\;\;\;V_{dS}(z)=\frac{-4m^{2}\alpha^{2}+d(d-2)}{4z^{2}}\,.

(H.51)

Considering
q_{E}^{2}=\delta^{ij}q_{i}q_{j}=q_{0}^{2}+{\vec{q}}^{2}>0
this equation again implies that
q_{E}^{2}
and therefore the energy spectrum of the
q_{0}
is continuous for all values of the masses
m
.

Expanding solution of (
H.49
) near
z=0
one finds

\phi(z,q_{E})=C_{1}q_{E}^{-\nu}z^{\frac{d-1}{2}-\nu}+C_{2}q^{\nu}z^{\frac{d-1}{2}+\nu}+\cdots\,.

(H.52)

In the case of massless mode the expansion is

\phi(z,q_{E})=C_{1}q_{E}^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}+C_{2}q_{E}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}z^{d-1}+\cdots\,.

(H.53)

H.2.2
Vector field

The Laplace operator acting on
A_{z}
component of a vector field gives

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}A_{z}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{i}^{2})-\frac{1}{z^{2}}(2d-3)-\frac{1}{z}(d-4)\partial_{z}\Big]A_{z}+\frac{2z}{\alpha^{2}}\partial^{i}A_{i}\,.

(H.54)

There is also the transversality condition on the gauge field that reads as

\partial^{i}A_{i}=\partial_{z}A_{z}-\frac{1}{z}(d-2)A_{z}\,.

(H.55)

So the Laplacian of
A_{z}
can be written as an operator acting on
A_{z}
only

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}A_{z}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{i}^{2})-\frac{1}{z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z}(d-2)\partial_{z}\Big]A_{z}\,.

(H.56)

The equation of motion (
H.16
) with
\alpha^{2}\kappa=d-1
, for the
A_{z}
component after the Fourier transform is

\Big[z^{2}(\partial_{z}^{2}+q_{E}^{2})+z(2-d)\partial_{z}-d\Big]A_{z}=0\,.

(H.57)

This equation is the same as (
H.20
), so the solution is (
H.21
).

The Laplace operator acting on
A_{i}
components gives

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}A_{i}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{j}^{2})-\frac{d-1}{z^{2}}+\frac{(4-d)}{z}\partial_{z}\Big]A_{i}+\frac{2z}{\alpha^{2}}\partial_{i}A_{z}\,.

(H.58)

The equation of motion for
A_{i}
is

[z^{2}(\partial_{z}^{2}+q_{E}^{2})+z(4-d)\partial_{z}-2(d-1)\Big]A_{i}=2z\partial_{i}A_{z}\,,

(H.59)

again this equation is the same as (
H.23
) with the solutions given by (
H.26
).

H.2.3
Gravitons

In dS space the constraints of transverse and traceless gauge on different components of graviton is

\displaystyle z(\partial_{z}h_{zz}-\partial^{i}h_{iz})-{h^{i}}_{\!i}+(3-d)h_{zz}=0\,,

(H.60)

\displaystyle z(\partial_{z}h_{iz}-\partial^{j}h_{ij})+(2-d)h_{iz}=0\,,

(H.61)

\displaystyle h_{zz}-{h^{i}}_{\!i}=0\,.

(H.62)

The Laplace operator acting on
h_{zz}
component in transverse and traceless gauge is

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}h_{zz}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{i}^{2}+\frac{(2-d)}{z}\partial_{z}-\frac{2}{z^{2}}\Big]h_{zz}

(H.63)

So the equation of motion (
H.29
) for this component of the graviton is

\Big[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z^{2}q_{E}^{2}-z(d-2)\partial_{z}+M^{2}\alpha^{2}\Big]h_{zz}=0\,.

(H.64)

The above equation is the same as (
H.34
) except for the sign of the last term. So the solution is also the same as (
H.35
) with

\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(d-1)^{2}-4M^{2}\alpha^{2}}\,.

(H.65)

The Laplace operator for
h_{iz}
component is

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}h_{iz}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{i}^{2}-\frac{d}{z^{2}}+\frac{(4-d)}{z}\partial_{z}\Big]h_{iz}+2\frac{z}{\alpha^{2}}\partial_{i}h_{zz}

(H.66)

The equation of motion is

\Big[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z^{2}q_{E}^{2}+z(4-d)\,\partial_{z}-(d-2-M^{2}\alpha^{2})\Big]h_{iz}(z,x)=2z\partial_{i}h_{zz}\,.

(H.67)

The above equation is the same as (
H.39
) except in the sign of
M^{2}\alpha^{2}
. So the solution is the same as (
H.40
) but with
\nu
given in (
H.65
).

The Laplace operator for
h_{ij}
component is

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}h_{ij}=-\frac{z^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[\partial_{z}^{2}-\partial_{i}^{2}+\frac{4-2d}{z^{2}}+\frac{(6-d)}{z}\partial_{z}\Big]h_{ij}+4\frac{z}{\alpha^{2}}\partial_{i}h_{jz}+\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\delta_{ij}h_{zz}\,.

(H.68)

The equation of motion is

\Big[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+z^{2}q_{E}^{2}+z(6-d)\,\partial_{z}-(2d-6-M^{2}\alpha^{2})\Big]h_{ij}(z,x)=2\delta_{ij}h_{zz}+4z\partial_{i}h_{jz}\,.

(H.69)

The above equation is the same as (
H.42
) except in the sign of
M^{2}\alpha^{2}
and the coefficient of
h_{zz}
on the right-hand side. So the solution is the same as (
H.45
) with
-\delta_{ij}
instead of
\eta_{ij}
and
\nu
given in (
H.65
).

Appendix I
Unitary representations of dS
4
and mass spectrum

In this section we briefly review the unitary irreducible representations of the isometry group of the 4 dimensional de sitter space,
SO(1,4)
, for more details see
[
60
]
.

In a dS
4
space-time with the length scale
\alpha
, for massive/massless fields with spin
s=0
and
s=2
, the relation between the scaling dimension
\Delta
and the mass is

\Delta=\frac{3}{2}\pm\nu\rightarrow m^{2}\alpha^{2}=\Delta(3-\Delta)\,,

(I.1)

where we have used relations (
H.50
) and (
H.65
).

The various distinct unitary irreducible representations classify according to the values of the above scaling dimension.

•

Scalar principal series:

\Delta=\frac{3}{2}+i\mu\;\;\;,\;\;\;\mu\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(I.2)

where for scalar modes in
{dS_{4}}

\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4m^{2}\alpha^{2}-9}\;\;\;,\;\;\;m^{2}>\frac{4}{9\alpha^{2}}\,.

(I.3)

This representation describes a massive scalar field with a mass above
m^{2}=\frac{9}{4\alpha^{2}}
and with a continuous mass spectrum.

•

Scalar complementary series:

0<\Delta<3\,.

(I.4)

It describes a massive scalar field with masses in
0<m^{2}<\frac{9}{4\alpha^{2}}
.

•

Exceptional (discrete) series I:

This representation describe scalar modes with angular momentum
j
where
j=0,1,2,\cdots
. The mass spectrum is discrete

m^{2}\alpha^{2}=-j(j+3)\,.

(I.5)

•

Spin-two principal series:

For spin two fields this representation describes a continuous mass spectrum similar to the scalar principal representation i.e.

\Delta=\frac{3}{2}+i\mu\;\;\;,\;\;\;\mu\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(I.6)

where

\mu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{4M^{2}\alpha^{2}-9}\;\;\;,\;\;\;M^{2}>\frac{9}{4\alpha^{2}}\,.

(I.7)

•

Spin-two complementary series:

The scaling dimension for spin-two modes in this representation falls within the following range

1<\Delta<2\,.

(I.8)

It describes a massive spin two field with a continuous mass spectrum in between
\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}<M^{2}<\frac{9}{4\alpha^{2}}
. The lower bound is the Higuchi bound.

•

Exceptional series II:

For spin two fields it contains just two discrete modes

M^{2}\alpha^{2}=0,2\,,

(I.9)

where both modes are below the Higuchi bound (
5.16
).

I.1
The representation content of the scalar zero modes

We have two zero modes for scalar fields with masses

m^{2}=0\;\;\;,\;\;\;m^{2}=-\frac{4}{3}\kappa=\pm\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\,,

(I.10)

where plus(minus) sign stands for four dimensional AdS(dS) slices.

•

m^{2}=0
on dS corresponds to the
j=0
mode of the exceptional discrete series I, (
I.5
).

•

m^{2}=-\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}
on dS is a tachyon and corresponds to the
j=1
mode of the exceptional discrete series I, (
I.5
).

•

m^{2}=0
on
AdS is a normalizable mode according to (
H.11
).

•

m^{2}=\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}
on AdS is also a normalizable mode according to (
H.10
).

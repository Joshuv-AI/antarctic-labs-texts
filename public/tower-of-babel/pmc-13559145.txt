Clinical factors associated with extrapulmonary complications in children with macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia: a single-center retrospective matched case-control study
Authors: Tianfu Xu, Seyan Yan, Saiping Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundExtrapulmonary complications contribute substantially to the clinical burden of macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (R-MPP), but factors associated with these events and their combined discriminatory value remain insufficiently characterized. This study examined clinical, laboratory, radiographic, and treatment-related factors associated with extrapulmonary complications and developed a simplified classification model.MethodsThis single-center retrospective matched case-control study included children aged 3–14 years hospitalized between January 2021 and June 2024. Among 684 potentially eligible children identified from electronic medical record and microbiology databases, 448 met the criteria for genotypically confirmed R-MPP. All 52 children who developed extrapulmonary complications during the index hospitalization were included as cases. From 396 children without complications, 156 controls were individually matched at a 1:3 ratio by sex, age within 6 months, and admission date within 30 days. Candidate variables included peak inflammatory biomarkers, nadir platelet count, respiratory and computed tomography (CT) severity, and intervals from symptom onset to medical consultation and macrolide initiation. Complete-case conditional logistic regression stratified by matched set was the primary analysis; least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) was used for variable selection, and multiple imputation was performed as a sensitivity analysis.ResultsCases and controls were comparable in age, sex, admission period, nutritional status, residence, vaccination status, household tobacco exposure, and wheezing history. C-reactive protein (CRP) >100 mg/L [matched odds ratio (OR) =3.28; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.70–6.34], lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) >400 U/L (OR =2.97; 95% CI: 1.56–5.65), macrolide initiation delay >7 days (OR =4.18; 95% CI: 2.16–8.08), and severe CT changes (OR =2.15; 95% CI: 1.08–4.29) were independently associated with complications. A three-variable model incorporating CRP, LDH, and macrolide delay achieved an optimism-corrected area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) of 0.917, with 86.5% sensitivity and 88.5% specificity. A preliminary internal temporal assessment in 44 patients yielded an AUC of 0.892.ConclusionsElevated CRP, elevated LDH, delayed macrolide initiation, and severe CT changes were associated with extrapulmonary complications in hospitalized children with R-MPP. The simplified model may support clinical classification, but the retrospective design, concurrent biomarker measurement, and limited temporal sample preclude causal interpretation or immediate implementation. Larger multicenter studies are required for formal validation.Keywords: Macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (R-MPP), extrapulmonary complications, pediatric pneumonia, classification model, treatment delay

Highlight box

Key findings

• Macrolide initiation delay >7 days, C-reactive protein (CRP) >100 mg/L, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) >400 U/L, and severe chest computed tomography (CT) changes were independently associated with extrapulmonary complications in hospitalized children with macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP).

• A simplified model incorporating CRP, LDH, and macrolide initiation delay showed good discrimination for the composite outcome in the matched sample.

• Exploratory analyses suggested different association patterns across neurological, hematological, and dermatological complications.

What is known and what is new?

• Previous studies have mainly focused on refractory, severe, or necrotizing pulmonary disease in children with MPP.

• This study focused specifically on extrapulmonary complications in children with genotypically confirmed macrolide-resistant infection and evaluated both overall and subtype-specific associations. The findings also indicate that extrapulmonary complications are clinically heterogeneous and should not be interpreted as a single biologically uniform outcome.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Children with markedly elevated CRP or LDH, severe CT involvement, or prolonged delay before macrolide initiation may require closer organ-specific assessment for extrapulmonary involvement.

• The simplified model should be regarded as a preliminary classification tool rather than a basis for causal inference, absolute risk estimation, or treatment selection.

• Prospective multicenter validation with predefined measurement time points and sufficient numbers of individual complication subtypes is required before clinical implementation.

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (M. pneumoniae) is a major cause of community-acquired pneumonia in school-aged children and adolescents and is frequently identified among children requiring hospitalization for pneumonia (1). The increasing prevalence of macrolide-resistant M. pneumoniae pneumonia (R-MPP), particularly in East Asia, has complicated clinical management because resistance is associated with prolonged fever, slower clinical response, and an increased need for alternative antimicrobial therapy (2). Although pulmonary disease remains the principal manifestation, M. pneumoniae infection may also involve the nervous, hematopoietic, mucocutaneous, cardiovascular, renal, and other organ systems (3).

Extrapulmonary complications should not, however, be regarded as a single biologically uniform condition. Proposed mechanisms include direct pathogen-mediated injury with local cytokine release, indirect immune-mediated injury caused by cross-reactive antibodies or immune complexes, and vascular injury involving vasculitis or thrombosis (3,4). The relative contribution of these pathways differs across organ systems. Neurological manifestations include encephalitis, aseptic meningitis, acute disseminated encephalomyelitis, transverse myelitis, seizures, and peripheral neuropathies. These conditions may arise through direct central nervous system invasion, cytokine-mediated inflammation, autoantibody-related demyelination, or vascular injury, and their evaluation commonly requires neurological examination, cerebrospinal fluid analysis, neuroimaging, and pathogen or antibody testing (4,5).

Hematological complications represent a distinct group. Autoimmune hemolytic anemia is commonly associated with cold-reactive antibodies, complement activation, indirect hyperbilirubinemia, increased lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), reduced haptoglobin, and a positive direct antiglobulin test (6). Thrombocytopenia may instead reflect immune-mediated platelet destruction or consumption and therefore has a different laboratory and clinical profile (7). Mucocutaneous complications are also heterogeneous. M. pneumoniae-induced rash and mucositis is characterized by prominent oral, ocular, or genital mucositis with relatively limited cutaneous involvement and is increasingly regarded as clinically distinct from conventional Stevens-Johnson syndrome and erythema multiforme (8,9). These differences indicate that C-reactive protein (CRP), LDH, platelet count, and other biomarkers may not have equivalent relevance across neurological, hematological, and dermatological manifestations.

Most previous predictive studies in pediatric M. pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) have focused on refractory or severe pulmonary disease and have identified inflammatory markers such as CRP and LDH as indicators of pulmonary severity (10,11). Far less is known about their association with extrapulmonary complications in children with genotypically confirmed R-MPP. Moreover, pooling clinically distinct complications into one composite endpoint may improve statistical efficiency but may obscure subtype-specific mechanisms and reduce clinical interpretability. A general model based on a composite endpoint should therefore be regarded as a broad classification tool rather than a substitute for organ-specific diagnostic evaluation.

Accordingly, this retrospective matched case-control study had three objectives: first, to identify clinical, laboratory, radiographic, and treatment-related factors associated with the occurrence of any extrapulmonary complication among hospitalized children with R-MPP; second, to evaluate the discrimination of a simplified model for the composite outcome; and third, to explore whether the patterns of association differed among neurological, hematological, and dermatological complications. The composite analysis was intended to support preliminary overall classification, whereas the subtype analyses were considered exploratory because of the limited number of events within each clinical category. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0451/rc).

Methods

Study design

This single-center retrospective matched case-control study was conducted in the Department of Pediatrics of The Affiliated Hospital of Putian University, a tertiary teaching hospital providing inpatient pediatric care for Putian and surrounding areas in Fujian Province, China. The source population comprised consecutive children aged 3–14 years who were hospitalized between January 1, 2021, and June 30, 2024, and underwent diagnostic evaluation for MPP. Potentially eligible patients were identified through a predefined search of the electronic medical record and microbiology databases using discharge diagnoses, positive M. pneumoniae polymerase chain reaction (PCR) results, and documented 23S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) resistance mutations. The same eligibility criteria were applied before case or control status was assigned. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Affiliated Hospital of Putian University (approval No. 2025194), which waived informed consent because only anonymized data collected during routine care were used.

Source population, eligibility criteria, and control selection

Children were eligible if they met all of the following criteria: (I) age 3–14 years; (II) laboratory-confirmed R-MPP, defined as detection of M. pneumoniae DNA by PCR in a nasopharyngeal or bronchoalveolar specimen together with a documented A2063G or A2064G mutation in domain V of the 23S rRNA gene (2); (III) radiologically confirmed pneumonia, defined as focal or diffuse pulmonary consolidation or infiltrates on chest imaging (2); and (IV) no evidence of concomitant bacterial, viral, Chlamydia pneumoniae, or Legionella infection based on bacterial cultures, multiplex respiratory viral PCR, and pathogen-specific PCR testing performed during routine clinical care.

Children were excluded if they had congenital or acquired immunodeficiency; a preexisting neurological, hematological, or autoimmune disorder that could interfere with the classification of extrapulmonary complications; or more than 20% missing data across the prespecified core variables, including CRP, LDH, macrolide initiation timing, and complication status. For children with more than one eligible admission during the study period, only the first hospitalization was included as the index hospitalization.

Between January 1, 2021, and June 30, 2024, 684 potentially eligible hospitalizations were identified through the electronic medical record and microbiology databases. After application of the predefined eligibility criteria, 448 children with genotypically confirmed R-MPP constituted the parent population. Outcome status was determined over the entire index hospitalization. All 52 children who developed at least one extrapulmonary complication were consecutively included as cases. Extrapulmonary complications included neurological manifestations, such as encephalitis or seizures; hematological manifestations, such as hemolytic anemia or thrombocytopenia; and dermatological manifestations, such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome or mucositis.

The remaining 396 eligible children who remained free of extrapulmonary complications throughout the index hospitalization constituted the control sampling pool. Controls were selected before predictor analyses and were not selected according to laboratory findings, treatment exposure, or pulmonary disease severity. Each case was directly matched to three controls by sex, age within 6 months, and admission date within 30 days. No propensity score was estimated or used. When more than three eligible controls met the matching criteria, the three children with the nearest admission dates were selected; ties were resolved by computer-generated random selection without replacement. This procedure yielded 52 matched sets, each comprising one case and three controls.

No a priori sample-size calculation was performed because all eligible cases identified during the predefined study period were included. The number of controls was determined by the prespecified 1:3 matching ratio, resulting in a final matched sample of 52 cases and 156 controls. To limit model instability, the simplified three-variable classification model was restricted to three variables, corresponding to 17.3 outcome events per model variable. Bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations was used to estimate optimism in model discrimination.

Data collection

Demographic and background variables included age, sex, weight-for-age z score, urban or rural residence, pneumococcal conjugate vaccination status, household tobacco exposure, and asthma or recurrent wheezing. Weight-for-age z scores were calculated according to the World Health Organization child growth standards (12).

Laboratory variables included peak CRP concentration within 72 hours after admission, peak LDH concentration during hospitalization, peak interleukin-6 (IL-6) concentration within 96 hours after admission, peak serum ferritin concentration, and nadir platelet count. Clinical variables included preadmission symptom duration, fever duration from symptom onset to defervescence, respiratory distress severity, and chest computed tomography (CT) severity. Respiratory distress severity was classified using prespecified operational criteria based on the most severe respiratory findings documented within the first 24 hours after admission. Mild respiratory distress was defined as tachypnea without chest retractions, nasal flaring, grunting, cyanosis, or hypoxemia, with peripheral oxygen saturation ≥95% in room air. Moderate respiratory distress was defined as tachypnea accompanied by nasal flaring, intercostal or subcostal retractions, or accessory-muscle use, or a peripheral oxygen saturation of 90–94% in room air, without signs meeting the severe category. Severe respiratory distress was defined by peripheral oxygen saturation <90% in room air, central cyanosis, grunting, very severe chest indrawing, altered consciousness attributable to respiratory compromise, or the requirement for high-flow oxygen, noninvasive ventilation, or invasive mechanical ventilation. Categories were mutually exclusive, and patients were assigned to the highest severity level for which any criterion was met. Chest CT severity was graded using the prespecified semiquantitative scoring criteria detailed in Table S1.

Treatment-related variables included the interval from symptom onset to first medical consultation, the interval from symptom onset to first macrolide administration, systemic corticosteroid use, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) administration, and switching from macrolides to a second-line antibiotic. Macrolide initiation timing was initially analyzed as a continuous variable and was subsequently dichotomized using the prespecified clinically interpretable threshold. Systemic corticosteroid use was defined as administration of any systemic corticosteroid during the index hospitalization. Switching to second-line antibiotics was defined as replacement of macrolide therapy with a tetracycline or fluoroquinolone during hospitalization.

IVIG timing was categorized as no IVIG, administration within 7 days after symptom onset, or administration more than 7 days after symptom onset. Impaired immune status was defined as a total lymphocyte count below 1.0×109/L or an immunoglobulin G (IgG), immunoglobulin A (IgA), or immunoglobulin M (IgM) concentration below the age-adjusted reference range during hospitalization. This classification was applied only to children without a diagnosed congenital or acquired immunodeficiency disorder, because such disorders were exclusion criteria.

All data were independently abstracted by two trained investigators using a standardized data-collection form. Disagreements were resolved through review of the original medical record. Interrater agreement for categorical variables exceeded 0.90 according to Cohen’s κ. Definitions, units, and coding rules for all study variables are provided in Table S1, and the number and percentage of missing observations for each major variable are reported in Table S2.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp.) and R version 4.3.4. Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and Q-Q plots and are presented as the mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, as appropriate. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages.

The extent and pattern of missing data were assessed before analysis and are reported for each major variable in Table S2. The primary analyses were based on complete cases, without single imputation or assignment of missing categorical observations to an ‘unknown’ category. Eighteen controls (8.7% of the matched sample) had at least one missing candidate variable and were excluded from the primary complete-case analysis. All 52 cases had complete data. The complete-case dataset therefore comprised 190 participants, including 52 cases and 138 controls, and retained all 52 matched sets. Because some controls were excluded, the number of controls per matched set ranged from one to three. Conditional logistic regression remained stratified by the original matched-set identifier. Missingness ranged from 0% to 3.4% for individual variables. Multiple imputation by chained equations was performed only as a sensitivity analysis under the missing-at-random assumption. Twenty imputed datasets were generated using a model that included the outcome, candidate predictors, matching variables, matched-set identifier, and auxiliary variables associated with missingness. Continuous variables were imputed using predictive mean matching, binary variables using logistic regression, and multicategory variables using multinomial logistic regression. Estimates were pooled using Rubin’s rules.

Each matched set comprised one case and three controls and was assigned a unique identifier. Univariate associations with extrapulmonary complications were evaluated using conditional logistic regression stratified by matched set. Continuous predictors were modeled in their original form and reported per clinically interpretable increment. Overall p values for multicategory variables were obtained using likelihood ratio tests. Variables associated with the outcome at P<0.15 were considered for candidate-variable selection.

Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation was used to reduce collinearity and select candidate variables. Variables retained by LASSO were entered into a multivariable conditional logistic regression model. Matching variables were not entered separately because sex, age, and admission date were controlled through conditioning on the matched sets. Results are reported as matched odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Given the 52 outcome events, model complexity was constrained during variable selection. An unconditional logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, and admission year was performed as a sensitivity analysis.

For development of the simplified classification model, CRP, LDH, and macrolide initiation delay were dichotomized using clinically interpretable thresholds and entered into an unconditional logistic regression model. Age, sex, and admission period were not included in the simplified model because they were matching factors and were not intended to contribute to the three-variable score. The model therefore comprised only CRP, LDH, and macrolide initiation delay, corresponding to 17.3 outcome events per model variable. Discrimination was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), and AUCs were compared using DeLong’s test. Sensitivity and specificity were calculated at the Youden-optimal threshold. Bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations was used to estimate the optimism-corrected AUC.

A preliminary internal temporal assessment was performed by developing the model in patients admitted from January 2021 to December 2023 and applying the fixed model coefficients without refitting to patients admitted from January to June 2024. The temporal sample comprised 44 patients, including 11 cases and 33 matched controls. Given the limited number of outcome events, this analysis was considered exploratory. Model discrimination was summarized using the AUC with its 95% CI, together with sensitivity and specificity at the prespecified threshold. No complication subtype-specific validation was performed because of the small numbers of neurological, hematological, and dermatological events in the temporal sample. Because the 1:3 matched case-control sample did not preserve the complication prevalence of the full source population, absolute predicted probabilities, positive and negative predictive values, calibration measures, and decision curve analysis were not reported.

Subgroup analyses were conducted according to age, disease severity, and immune status. Interactions between subgroup variables and the model linear predictor were evaluated using likelihood ratio tests. Exploratory analyses according to neurological, hematological, and dermatological complication type used tests for heterogeneity, with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons.

Sensitivity analyses included multiple imputation, unconditional logistic regression adjusted for the matching variables, and multivariable fractional polynomial (MFP) modeling of CRP, LDH, and macrolide initiation delay as continuous variables. IVIG timing was analyzed as a three-category variable comprising no IVIG, administration within 7 days, and administration after 7 days. Its adjusted association and interaction with the combined model score were evaluated after controlling for CRP, LDH, respiratory distress, and CT severity. All tests were two-sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the cohorts

Between January 2021 and June 2024, 684 potentially eligible children were identified, of whom 448 met the eligibility criteria for genotypically confirmed R-MPP. All 52 children who developed extrapulmonary complications were included as cases, and 156 controls were selected from the 396 eligible children without extrapulmonary complications, yielding 52 matched sets (Figure 1). Age, sex, admission year, and admission season were similar between the groups as a result of matching. Cases and controls were also comparable in weight-for-age z score, residence, pneumococcal vaccination status, household tobacco exposure, asthma or recurrent wheezing, and previous outpatient macrolide exposure. Referral from another healthcare facility was more frequent among cases than controls (38.5% vs. 24.4%, P=0.049). Univariate analysis of associated factors

Univariate conditional logistic regression showed that higher inflammatory marker levels, lower platelet counts, longer fever duration, delayed medical consultation, and delayed macrolide initiation were associated with extrapulmonary complications (Table 2). The strongest continuous associations were observed for LDH, with a matched OR of 1.53 per 50 U/L increase (95% CI: 1.32–1.77; P<0.001), and macrolide initiation delay, with a matched OR of 1.71 per 24-hour increase (95% CI: 1.43–2.05; P<0.001). Compared with mild disease, severe respiratory distress was associated with a matched OR of 15.72 (95% CI: 4.85–50.94; P<0.001), while severe CT changes were associated with a matched OR of 25.18 (95% CI: 6.63–95.62; P<0.001). Corticosteroid use and IVIG administration were also more frequent among cases, whereas preadmission symptom duration, asthma or recurrent wheezing, and switching to second-line antibiotics were not significantly associated with complications.

Multivariable analysis of factors associated with extrapulmonary complications

Multivariable conditional logistic regression stratified by the 52 matched sets identified four factors independently associated with extrapulmonary complications (Tables 3,4). CRP >100 mg/L was associated with higher odds of complications (matched OR =3.28; 95% CI: 1.70–6.34), as was LDH >400 U/L (matched OR =2.97; 95% CI: 1.56–5.65). Macrolide initiation delay >7 days showed the strongest association (matched OR =4.18; 95% CI: 2.16–8.08). Severe CT changes also remained independently associated with complications (matched OR =2.15; 95% CI: 1.08–4.29). Platelet count and respiratory distress did not remain significant after simultaneous adjustment. The unconditional sensitivity model adjusted for age, sex, and admission year produced similar estimates, with changes in the four principal ORs of less than 8%.

Discriminative performance and preliminary internal temporal assessment

ROC analysis was used to evaluate the discrimination of CRP, LDH, macrolide initiation delay, CT severity, and the combined three-variable model (Figure 3). Among the individual variables, CRP demonstrated the highest discrimination, with an AUC of 0.889 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93), sensitivity of 82.7%, and specificity of 81.4% at an optimal cutoff of >98 mg/L. LDH achieved an AUC of 0.846 (95% CI: 0.80–0.90), with sensitivity of 80.8% and specificity of 78.2% at >408 U/L. Macrolide initiation delay achieved an AUC of 0.813 (95% CI: 0.78–0.88), with sensitivity of 78.8% and specificity of 76.9% at >162 hours. CT severity showed moderate discrimination, with an AUC of 0.752 (95% CI: 0.69–0.81), sensitivity of 68.3%, and specificity of 76.9% for severe changes.

Sensitivity analysis using MFPs

A sensitivity analysis was performed by modeling CRP levels, LDH levels, and macrolide delay as continuous variables via MFPs. The final MFP model retained nonlinear terms for CRP (power =−2) and LDH (power =−0.5), whereas macrolide delay was included as a linear term. The dose-response relationships derived from this model are displayed in Figure 4, which showed increasing odds of extrapulmonary complications across higher values of the three variables. The discriminative performance of the model remained virtually unchanged (AUC =0.915; 95% CI: 0.89–0.96) compared with that of the primary dichotomized model (AUC =0.917), as shown by the ROC curve comparison in Figure 5. Discussion

In this retrospective matched case-control study of hospitalized children with genotypically confirmed R-MPP, macrolide initiation delay >7 days, CRP >100 mg/L, LDH >400 U/L, and severe chest CT changes were independently associated with extrapulmonary complications. Macrolide initiation delay showed the largest matched association, while the three-variable model incorporating CRP, LDH, and macrolide delay demonstrated good discrimination in the matched sample. However, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than evidence of causality or temporally antecedent prediction. CRP and LDH were measured during hospitalization and may partly reflect concurrent systemic inflammation, tissue injury, pulmonary severity, or the presence of an evolving extrapulmonary complication.

The association between delayed macrolide initiation and extrapulmonary complications requires particularly cautious interpretation. Previous pediatric studies have reported that macrolide resistance and delayed appropriate antimicrobial treatment are associated with prolonged fever, slower clinical response, and more severe disease (13,14). Experimental evidence also indicates that M. pneumoniae can induce marked innate immune activation, including NOD-, LRR-, and pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome signaling and the release of proinflammatory cytokines (15). These observations provide biological plausibility for an association between prolonged untreated infection and systemic inflammatory injury. Nevertheless, all children in the present source population had genotypically confirmed macrolide-resistant infection. Therefore, the interval to first macrolide administration should not be interpreted as the duration to effective antimicrobial therapy. It may instead function as a composite marker of delayed healthcare seeking, delayed recognition of pneumonia, greater prehospital disease progression, or differences in access to care. Reverse causation and residual confounding by baseline severity also remain possible. The cutoff of >7 days should consequently be regarded as an observed discriminative threshold in this dataset rather than a biologically established treatment deadline or evidence that earlier macrolide administration would prevent complications.

The associations of CRP, LDH, and CT severity are consistent with the broader pediatric MPP literature. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that children with refractory MPP generally had higher CRP and LDH levels and more extensive pulmonary consolidation than children with nonrefractory disease (16). Prospective data have also supported the clinical relevance of CRP and LDH in identifying severe pediatric MPP (17), while previous case-control research suggested that combinations of LDH, ferritin, and cytokine-related markers may improve recognition of refractory disease (10). CRP is a nonspecific marker of systemic inflammation, whereas LDH reflects cellular and tissue injury; neither marker is specific to extrapulmonary involvement. Similarly, severe CT changes may indicate a greater overall inflammatory burden rather than an organ-specific pathway. These variables may therefore help distinguish children with more extensive systemic disease, but they cannot determine whether a particular extrapulmonary manifestation is already present or which organ system will subsequently be affected.

The clinical heterogeneity of the composite outcome is central to interpretation. Neurological, hematological, and dermatological complications differ in pathogenesis, diagnostic criteria, treatment, and prognosis. In the exploratory subtype analyses, LDH showed the strongest association with neurological complications, thrombocytopenia with hematological complications, and CRP with dermatological complications. These patterns are clinically plausible but should not be overinterpreted. Pediatric M. pneumoniae encephalitis may involve direct infection, cytokine-mediated neuroinflammation, immune-mediated injury, or vascular mechanisms, and its assessment typically requires cerebrospinal fluid analysis, neuroimaging, and neurological evaluation rather than serum LDH alone (5). Thrombocytopenia associated with M. pneumoniae has been described in the context of immune-mediated platelet destruction or consumption (7). Likewise, M. pneumoniae-induced rash and mucositis is characterized by prominent mucosal involvement and differs clinically from conventional drug-related Stevens-Johnson syndrome (8). Thus, the subtype findings reinforce rather than resolve the limitation of pooling these events. The combined model should be considered a broad classifier for the composite outcome and not an organ-specific diagnostic tool.

This distinction also differentiates the present model from previous pediatric MPP models. Most published models have addressed refractory pulmonary disease, severe pneumonia, delayed radiographic resolution, or necrotizing pneumonia rather than extrapulmonary manifestations. For example, a recent model for necrotizing pneumonia in children with refractory MPP incorporated fever duration, white blood cell count, IL-6, and D-dimer and achieved good discrimination in its development and validation samples (18). Other studies have linked inflammatory burden and pulmonary lesion extent to delayed radiographic resolution (19). These models target more clinically homogeneous pulmonary outcomes. By contrast, the present study evaluated a composite of clinically distinct extrapulmonary complications. Its principal contribution is therefore not the establishment of a definitive bedside prediction instrument, but the identification of a small set of variables associated with the overall occurrence of extrapulmonary disease and the demonstration that their relevance may differ across complication subtypes.

The three-variable model achieved an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.917 in the matched sample, and the MFP analysis produced similar discrimination when CRP, LDH, and treatment delay were retained as continuous variables. These findings suggest that the observed discrimination was not solely created by dichotomization. Multiple imputation also produced estimates close to those of the complete-case analysis. Nevertheless, high apparent discrimination should be interpreted in the context of the matched case-control sampling strategy, the limited number of outcome events, and the use of variables measured during the same hospitalization as the complications. Because the sampling fraction did not reflect the prevalence of complications in the parent population, the model was not used to estimate absolute probability, calibration, positive predictive value, or negative predictive value. The preliminary internal temporal assessment included only 44 patients and 11 cases. Although its AUC was similar to that of the primary analysis, the estimate was imprecise and could have been influenced by the distribution of neurological, hematological, and dermatological events. It therefore provides only exploratory evidence of temporal reproducibility within the same center and does not establish generalizability.

The subgroup analyses likewise require restraint. Similar AUC values across age, pulmonary severity, and immune-status groups do not prove equivalent model performance because each subgroup contained relatively few events and the interaction tests had limited power. In particular, the term “impaired immune status” reflected laboratory abnormalities during hospitalization rather than a diagnosed immunodeficiency. These analyses are best viewed as descriptive assessments of consistency rather than formal validation in distinct clinical populations.

The treatment-related findings do not support specific immunomodulatory recommendations. Corticosteroid and IVIG use were more frequent among cases in univariate analysis, most likely because clinicians preferentially administered these therapies to children with more severe inflammation or established complications. This is a typical setting for confounding by indication. High-dose IVIG has recognized anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory uses in selected pediatric immune-mediated disorders (20), but the present observational data cannot determine whether IVIG prevents, attenuates, or has no effect on extrapulmonary complications in R-MPP. The study therefore does not support prophylactic IVIG or treatment selection based on the simplified score.

The findings may still have practical value. In hospitalized children with R-MPP, markedly elevated CRP or LDH, severe CT involvement, and a prolonged interval before initial medical treatment identify a group in whom closer assessment for extrapulmonary involvement may be warranted. Such assessment should remain organ directed. Neurological symptoms should prompt neurological examination and appropriate cerebrospinal fluid and imaging studies; anemia, jaundice, or bleeding should prompt hematological testing; and prominent mucositis or rash should prompt careful mucocutaneous evaluation. The model should not replace these evaluations or be used to infer a specific complication subtype.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the single-center hospital-based design may have introduced referral and spectrum bias and may have overrepresented children with severe or treatment-refractory disease. Second, the retrospective design and concurrent measurement of several variables prevent firm assessment of temporality. Peak CRP, peak LDH, nadir platelet count, treatment decisions, and complication status may have evolved during overlapping periods. Third, although controls were selected from a predefined parent population and matched on age, sex, and admission date, residual confounding from disease severity, healthcare access, prior treatment, and unrecognized coinfection remains possible. Fourth, the composite outcome combined biologically and clinically heterogeneous conditions. The small numbers within each subtype prevented reliable multivariable subtype-specific modeling. Fifth, complete-case analysis reduced the primary analytic sample, although multiple imputation produced similar estimates. Sixth, the simplified model was developed from a matched case-control sample and cannot provide valid absolute complication probabilities without appropriate prevalence correction. Finally, the preliminary temporal assessment was small and conducted within the same institution.

Conclusions

This matched case-control study identified macrolide initiation delay, elevated CRP, elevated LDH, and severe CT changes as factors associated with extrapulmonary complications in children with R-MPP. A simplified model incorporating CRP, LDH, and macrolide initiation delay demonstrated good discrimination in the matched sample. Similar discrimination was observed in a small preliminary internal temporal assessment, but the limited number and clinical heterogeneity of complication events preclude firm conclusions regarding generalizability. Larger multicenter studies with adequate representation of individual complication subtypes are required for formal validation before clinical implementation.

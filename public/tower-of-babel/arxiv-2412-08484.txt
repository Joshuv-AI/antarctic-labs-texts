ConvMesh: Reimagining Mesh Quality Through Convex Optimization
Alexander Valverde

Abstract
Mesh generation has become a critical topic in recent years, forming the foundation of all 3D objects used across various applications, such as virtual reality, gaming, and 3D printing. With advancements in computational resources and machine learning, neural networks have emerged as powerful tools for generating high-quality 3D object representations, enabling accurate scene and object reconstructions. Despite these advancements, many methods produce meshes that lack realism or exhibit geometric and textural flaws, necessitating additional processing to improve their quality. This research introduces a novel approach that leverages convex constraints to enhance existing meshes by refining their texture and geometry. By focusing on a sparse set of point clouds from both the original and target meshes, this method demonstrates significant improvements in mesh quality with minimal data requirements. To evaluate the approach, the classical dolphin mesh dataset from Facebook AI was used as a case study, with optimization performed using the CVXPY library. The results reveal promising potential for streamlined and effective mesh refinement.

Methods

A wide range of standard convex programming forms can be employed, including least squares (LS), linear programming (LP), quadratic programming (QP), semidefinite programming (SDP), second-order cone programming (SOCP), geometric programming (GP), and smooth convex programming (smooth CP). However, selecting the most suitable form for a given problem is not always straightforward. In some cases, it may even be necessary to implement a custom solver. As noted by Grant et al., disciplined convex programming (DCP) offers a transparent and reliable framework for problem formulation.

DCP focuses on systematically transforming nonconvex problems into convex ones by introducing new functions to encapsulate nonlinearities. Its core is defined by a “grammar” that ensures each transformation step preserves convexity, thus guaranteeing that the final problem remains within the convex domain
[
1
]
.

For practical application, convex optimization often needs to scale to high dimensions. The problem considered here involves 7,684 variables and 15,360 constraints, qualifying it as a large-scale convex optimization instance. To tackle it effectively, we define the appropriate convex optimization problem and employ both disciplined convex programming (DCP) and disciplined quasiconvex programming (DQCP), along with a Splitting Conic Solver (SCS)
[
1
]
. These methods are particularly suitable for large-scale scenarios.

For this specific case, CVXPY translates the problem into a santard form eploying a ”Reduction chain”. First, it converts the DCP-compliant problem into a conic form. Second, handles any special attributes that need to be translated into standard constraints. Third, rewritres the problem data into the matrix form required by SCS. Finally, prepares the data for the SCS solver.

SCS is a first-order method especially for large-scale scale conic scenarios. This solver classifies constraints into different cones (zero, linear, second-order, semidefinite)
[
5
]
. It does not rely on any explicit algorithm parameters, and the per-iteration cost of the method is the same as applying the splitting method to the primal or dual alone. The SCS method involves applying an operator splitting method to the homogeneous self-dual embedding of the original optimization problem to find a point in the intersection of an affine set and a convex cone. It solves a system of linear equations and projects the point onto the cone.

Results

The optimization process ran for over two hours using Python libraries such as CVXPY, PyTorch3D, and Torch. By employing the convex optimization framework, the updated mesh exhibited significant improvements over the initial neural network-generated version. We employ a value of
\delta=0.1
and a weighting factor
\lambda=0.3

Primal Perspective:
These variables are exactly zero, satisfying linear equalities that set them to specific values.

Dual Perspective:
These variables have no restrictions and are used to represent equality constraints.

Linear Variables:
These variables must lie in a nonnegative domain, meaning they must be greater than or equal to zero.

Second-Order Cone (SOC) Variables:
These variables are more complex, as they can represent norm bounds
[
4
]
, which generalize simple linear inequalities.

Positive Semidefinite (PSD) Variables:
These variables are matrix-valued and must form a positive semidefinite matrix. PSD matrices are often used to enforce structural conditions in optimization problems.

For this specific case, PSD has a greater value of variables because they operate in a higher-dimensional space, involving complex structural constraints. Due to the properties of PSD, it requires more variables and constraints compared to simple linear constraints. PSD enforces that a matrix is symmetric and all eigenvalues are nonnegative. In general, PSD can encode relationships and structural conditions that may not be possible with other constraints. A mesh has a complete structure that is connected everywhere; therefore, it is necessary to maintain those conditions

Discussion

Conclusions

In conclusion, the results highlight the potential of convex optimization for improving neural mesh generation. While the updated mesh retains the overall geometry of the target, future work could focus on refining the loss function to enhance alignment in detailed areas. Is important to mention that this new mesh was optimized only on 500 points from the original mesh, therefore, indicates how with a low-dimensional representation is possible to achieve these results.

Future Work

The proposed method requires further evaluation, particularly in terms of solver time, as it currently takes over an hour to generate the corresponding mesh. Future research should focus on exploring alternative approaches that leverage GPUs and vectorization techniques. These methods could potentially reduce computation time while maintaining the accuracy and quality of the solution. Additionally, investigating algorithmic optimizations and parallel processing strategies may also contribute to achieving faster results.

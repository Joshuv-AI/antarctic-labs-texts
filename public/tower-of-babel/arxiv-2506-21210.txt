An arithmetic analog of Klein’s classification of finite subgroups of 𝐒𝐋 2 ​ ( ℂ ) {\mathbf{SL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{C}}}) Thanks: MS was partially supported by a Discovery Grant from the National Science and Engineering Research Council of Canada.
Christian Liedtke, Matthew Satriano

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field with ring of integers 𝒪 K {\cal O}_{K} . We describe and classify finite, flat, and linearly reductive subgroup schemes of 𝐒𝐋 2 {\mathbf{SL}}_{2} over Spec ​ 𝒪 K {\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K} . We also establish finiteness results for these group schemes, as well as density results for the associated quotient singularities.

11E57, 11E72, 14L15, 14L30, 14J17

1.
Introduction

In his famous lectures on quintic equations and the icosahedron
[
Kl84
]
,
Felix Klein classified finite subgroups of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{C}}})
and computed the
resulting quotient singularities.
It turns out that there are two infinite series (
A_{n}

and
D_{n}
) and three
‘exceptional’ (
E_{6}
,
E_{7}
,
E_{8}
) of such groups and singularities.
The resulting singularities are precisely the

rational double point singularities

(RDP singularities for short),
which have shown up in many different contexts and which
can be characterized in many different ways, see, for example,

[
Du79
]
.

The RDP singularities over arbitrary algebraically closed fields
have been classified by Michael Artin
[
Ar77
]
,
who showed that the situation is more
complicated in characteristics
2,3,5
.
This was extended to nonclosed fields by
Joseph Lipman
[
Lip69
]
,
where new types of singularities (non-split forms of the previous types)
like
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
F_{4}
, and
G_{2}
arise.

If the ground field
k
is of positive characteristic or if
it is not algebraically closed,
then it is usually not possible to obtain all types of
RDP singularities as quotient singularities by finite groups
of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
.
However, it is possible to realize all types
of RDP singularities (with some exceptions in characteristics
2,3,5
)
as quotients by
finite linearly
reductive subgroup schemes
of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
,
which is due to Mitsuyasu Hashimoto
[
Ha15
]
and
the authors
[
LS14
]
over
algebraically closed ground fields and even holds
over nonclosed fields, as recently shown by the authors
in
[
LS25
]
.

This fits into the general philosophy from
[
LMM
]

that one should consequently use finite and linearly
reductive group schemes when working with quotient
singularities in positive or mixed characteristic.
For example, the second named author already established
a Chevalley–Shephard-Todd theorem in this setting
[
Sa12
]
.
Another example is the existence of a very well-behaved
McKay correspondence in positive characteristic
in this setting that was
established by the first named author
[
Li24
]
.
Last, but not least, it also fits into the philosophy
of
tame stacks
, which were introduced by
Abramovich, Olsson, and Vistoli
[
AOV08
]
, and where the
stabilizers are linearly reductive group schemes.

1.1.
The Klein set and arithmetic RDP singularities

If
S
is a scheme and
n\geq 1
is an integer, then we define
the
Klein set
to be

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)\quad:=\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{finite, flat, linearly reductive subgroup }\\
\mbox{schemes of }{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\mbox{ of length }n\end{array}\right\}/\sim,

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugacy.
Associated to
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
from
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
, we have
the invariant
{\cal O}_{S}
-subalgebra

{\cal O}_{S}[x,y]^{G}\,\subseteq\,{\cal O}_{S}[x,y],

whose relative spectrum over
S
is a family of RDP singularities
over
S
.

In the case where
S={\rm Spec}\>k
for some algebraically
closed field
k
, the set
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
and the associated RDPs
can be read off from the above mentioned works
of Klein, Hashimoto, and ourselves
[
Ha15
,
Kl84
,
LS14
]
.
If
S={\rm Spec}\>k
is the spectrum of an arbitrary field, then

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
will be described in work in preparation
and the associated RDPs have been worked out if
k
is perfect
already in
[
LS25
]
.

The objective of this article is to first establish some
general properties of finite flat linearly reductive
group schemes over an excellent Dedekind scheme
S

and to use this to give a cohomological description
of
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
.

The main application is to establish finiteness results
and to give a more or less explicit descriptions of

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
in the case where
S
is the spectrum
of the ring of integers
{\cal O}_{K}
inside a number field
K
.

This can be viewed as an arithmetic analogue of
Klein’s classification of finite subgroups of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{C}}})

and their associated quotient singularities.

1.2.
Subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}

The key observation is the following result, which follows from
the more general Theorem
6.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
{\cal O}_{K}
.
Let
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}

be a finite flat linearly reductive subgroup scheme
of length
n
over
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
.
Then, there exists an fppf cover
\{S^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
, such that
G_{S^{\prime}_{i}}

is conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S_{i}^{\prime}}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S^{\prime}_{i}}
.

Thus,
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
is locally in the fppf topology
conjugate to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
The reason why only forms of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
show up
and none other of the group schemes from Klein’s list over
{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
is the following:
let
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\cal O}_{K}
be a prime lying over
(2)\subset{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
let
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})

be the residue field, and let
\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of

\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
.
Then,
(G_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\overline{k}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\overline{k}}}
must be conjugate
to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
by the classification of finite linearly
reductive subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
over algebraically closed fields
of characteristic
2
, which we recall in Theorem
3.2
.
From this, Theorem
4.7
, which is a rigidity
result for finite flat and linearly reductive subgroup schemes,
shows that
G
itself must be a twisted form of the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.

1.3.
Classifying twisted forms of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}

The classification of finite flat and linearly reductive
subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
is thus equivalent
to the classification of fppf locally conjugate forms
of the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\to{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
To obtain the latter, we note that the
normalizers of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
inside
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
and
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}

(over any base scheme) sit in split exact sequences

(1)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}1&\to&{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m}&\to&{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n})&\to&{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}&\to&1\\
1&\to&{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m}^{2}&\to&{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n})&\to&{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}&\to&1\end{array}

of affine group schemes over
{\cal O}_{K}
,
where
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
and
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m}^{2}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}
are the standard tori.
Extending
[
LS25
, Theorem 1.4]
to more general base schemes,
we have the following (see also Theorem
5.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Twisted forms of standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
in the fppf topology
are in bijection (up to
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
-conjugacy) to the kernel

\ker\left({H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}})\right),

of pointed cohomology sets.

Since the sequences (
1
)
split, we obtain a short exact sequence
of pointed cohomology sets

(2)

1\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m}^{2})\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})\,\to\,1.

In Section
7
, we will
analyze the twisted forms of associated to cohomology classes
in this sequence over arbitrary base schemes:
it turns out that classes on the right lead to
certain quadratic twists and that classes on the left
lead to forms that can be trivialized Zariski locally.

1.4.
Finiteness

We note that

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m})\quad\cong\quad{\rm Pic}({\cal O}_{K})\quad\cong\quad{\rm Cl}({\cal O}_{K})\,=\,:{\rm Cl}_{K},

the class group of
K
.
Moreover,
{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})
describes
unramified extensions of
{\cal O}_{K}
of degree 2,
see also Caution
9.3
.
Class field theory gives an isomorphism

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})\quad\cong\quad{\rm Hom}({\rm Cl}_{K}^{1},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}),

where
{\rm Cl}^{1}_{K}
denotes
the ray class group of modulus 1, which can be described by

{\rm Cl}_{K}
and the real places of
K
.
In particular, the kernel and the cokernel of
sequence (
2
)
are controlled by
{\rm Cl}_{K}
and
{\rm Cl}_{K}^{1}
, respectively.

Since
{\rm Cl}_{K}
and
{\rm Cl}^{1}_{K}
both are finite (recall
that the class number
h_{K}
is defined to be the order of
{\rm Cl}_{K}
),
we eventually obtain the following finiteness result and we note
that it does not immediate follow from the previous observations
due to subtleties of non-abelian cohomology.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
{\cal O}_{K}
,
class number
h_{K}
,
and ray class group
{\rm Cl}_{K}^{1}
of modulus 1.
Then,
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})
is a finite set.
More precisely,

(1)

If
n=2
, then
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(2,{\cal O}_{K})
is a singleton.

(2)

If
n\geq 3
, then

(a)

the cardinality
|{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})|
is independent of
n
.

(b)

|{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})|\geq h_{K}
.

(c)

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})
consists of one element if and only if

{\rm Cl}_{K}^{1}
is trivial.

We mention that this result is slightly in the spirit of
other finiteness theorems:
for example, Frans Oort and John Tate showed that the only finite group scheme
of length
p
over
{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/p{{\mathbb{Z}}}
or
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}
,
see the introduction of
[
OT70
]
, where they actually
attribute this to unpublished work of Michael Artin and Barry Mazur.
Moreover, Jean-Marc Fontaine
[
Fo85
]
studied
finite subgroup schemes of a potential abelian scheme over
{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}

and established finiteness in order to show that there is no abelian scheme
over
{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

1.5.
RDP singularities over
{\cal O}_{K}

For an integer
n\geq 2
, let
\beta(n)
be equal to
n/2
if
n
is even
and equal to
(n-1)/2
if
n
is odd.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers

{\cal O}_{K}
, let
G\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})
for some
n\geq 3
,
and let

f\quad:\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{G}\quad\to\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

be the associated family of RDP singularities
over
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
.

(1)

If
G\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
(abstract isomorphism of group schemes, disregarding the
embedding into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
),
then for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
,
we have
G_{\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}
and

f^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})

is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}
over
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
.

(2)

If
G\not\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
(abstract isomorphism of group schemes,
disregarding the embedding into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
), then there exists an
unramified quadratic extension
L/K
(see Caution
9.3
), such
such that

G\quad\cong\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\wedge{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{L},

the quadratic twist with respect to inversion.
Let
\Sigma_{A}
(resp.
\Sigma_{B}
) be the subset of
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

of primes that are inert (resp. split) in
{\cal O}_{L}
.

(a)

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Sigma_{A}
, then
G_{\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}

and
f^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})
is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}

over
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
.

(b)

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Sigma_{B}
, then
G_{\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}\not\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}

and
f^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})
is and RDP
of type
B_{\beta(n)}
over
\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})
.

Both,
\Sigma_{A}
and
\Sigma_{B}
have Dirichlet
density
1/2
in
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

and in particular, they are infinite.

We refer to Section
9

for more details and for what happens at
the infinite places.

Remark 1.5
.

In Section
8
, we give two examples
of non-standard embeddings of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
into

{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
In Example
8.4
, we compute
the invariant subring in one of these examples, which exhibits interesting behavior.
It is a family of RDPs of type
A_{n-1}
over
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
.
It turns out that in this example
Noether’s degree bound on invariants of

{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\subset{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]
holds,
see Remark
8.5
.
However, it seems that more than 3 invariants
are needed to generate the invariant subring as a

{\cal O}_{K}
-algebra.
(We believe that
5
invariants are needed in this example.)
Interestingly, one can find a Zariski open cover, such that
the invariant subring is generated by
3 invariants on each patch.

1.6.
Organization

This article is organized as follows:

•

In Section
2
, we recall some generalities

•

In Section
3

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
.

•

In Section
4
, we establish a rigidity
result that is central to what follows:
if
S
is an excellent Dedekind scheme, if
s\in S
is a point,
if
G_{i}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{d,S}
,
i=1,2
are two finite flat linearly
reductive subgroup schemes, and if
G_{1,s}
and
G_{2,s}
are conjugate
in
{\mathbf{GL}}_{d,s}
, then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are locally in the fppf topology

{\mathbf{GL}}_{d,S}
.

•

In Section
5
, we study subgroup

that are fppf locally conjugate

, where
G
and
X

are affine group schemes over some base
scheme
S
.

•

In Section
6
, we combine the rigidity result
of Section

with the fact that the only

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
.

is an excellent Dedekind scheme

In Section

,

{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}

S
.

)

).

Conventions
.

In this article, all schemes, group schemes, and torsors under group schemes will be of
finite type over Noetherian separated base schemes.
In particular, all torsors in the fpqc topology can already
be trivialized in the fppf topology.
We will work in the fppf topology and flat cohomology will refer
to the fppf topology.

2.
Generalities

In this section, we recall several more or less well-known
results on linearly reductive
group schemes, first over arbitrary base schemes and then, over fields
with an emphasis on algebraically closed fields.
We follow the setup of Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli from
[
AOV08
]

and many results over arbitrary base schemes are in fact due to them.

2.1.
Over arbitrary bases

Let
G\to S
be a group scheme over a base scheme
S
.
We denote by
\mathrm{QCoh}(-)
(resp.
\mathrm{Coh}(-)
) the category
of quasi-coherent (resp. coherent sheaves) and a supscript
-^{G}

refers to the category of
G
-equivariant such objects and morphisms.
Following
[
AOV08
, Definition 2.2]
,
we say that
G
is
linearly reductive
if the functor
of taking
G
-invariants

\mathrm{QCoh}^{G}(S)\,\to\,\mathrm{QCoh}(S),\qquad\mathcal{F}\,\mapsto\,\mathcal{F}^{G}

is exact.
If
S
is Noetherian, which is our standing assumption,
then this is equivalent to the functor

\mathrm{Coh}^{G}(S)\,\to\,\mathrm{Coh}(S),\qquad\mathcal{F}\,\mapsto\,\mathcal{F}^{G}

being exact, see
[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.3]
.
The following result shows that linearly reductive group
schemes behave well under base-change.

Proposition 2.1

(Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli)
.

Let
S^{\prime}\to S
be a morphism of schemes, let

G\to S
be a group scheme, and let
G_{S}^{\prime}:=G\times_{S}S^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
be the
base change.
Then

(1)

If
G\to S
is linearly reductive, then
G^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
is linearly reductive.

(2)

If
G^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
is linearly reductive and if
S^{\prime}\to S
is flat and surjective, then

G\to S
is linearly reductive.

Proof.

[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.4]
.
∎

In particular, if
k\subseteq K
is an extension of fields
and if
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is a group scheme, then
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly
reductive if and only if
G\times_{{\rm Spec}\>k}{\rm Spec}\>K\to{\rm Spec}\>K
is
linearly reductive.

Proposition 2.2

(Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli)
.

The class of finite linearly reductive group schemes is closed under taking
subgroup schemes, quotients, and extensions.

Proof.

[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.5]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k
be a field and let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite group scheme.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly reductive in the sense
of
[
AOV08
]
.

(2)

Every short exact sequence

(3)

0\,\to\,V^{\prime}\,\to\,V\,\to\,V^{\prime\prime}\,\to\,0

of finite-dimensional
k
-linear
G
-representations
splits as a sequence of
k
-linear
G
-representations.

(3)

Every finite dimensional
k
-linear
G
-representation
is a direct sum of simple
k
-linear
G
-representations.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)

We consider the sequence (
3
).
Clearly
{\rm Hom}(V^{\prime\prime},-)
is an exact functor because
we are dealing with finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces.
Taking
G
-invariants is an exact functor because
G
is assumed
to be linearly reductive.
This shows that
{\rm Hom}(V^{\prime\prime},-)^{G}={\rm Hom}_{G}(V^{\prime\prime},-)
is an exact functor.
Applying this functor to (
3
), we conclude that
there exists a
\sigma\in{\rm Hom}_{G}(V^{\prime\prime},V)
mapping to
{\rm id}_{V^{\prime\prime}}\in{\rm Hom}_{G}(V^{\prime\prime},V^{\prime\prime})
.
This
\sigma
is a splitting of (
3
) in the category
of
k
-linear
G
-representations.

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
is an exercise.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)

Given a short exact sequence (
3
),
we can decompose every vector space into the direct sum of
the isotypical part of the trivial representation and the other ones.
Taking
G
-invariants and using Schur’s lemma,
we obtain a short exact sequence

0\,\to\,V^{\prime G}\,\to\,V^{G}\,\to\,V^{\prime\prime G}\,\to\,0

of finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces.
This shows that taking
G
-invariants

\mathrm{Coh}^{G}({\rm Spec}\>k)\to\mathrm{Coh}({\rm Spec}\>k)

is an exact functor.
By
[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.3]
, this implies that taking

G
-invariants
\mathrm{QCoh}^{G}({\rm Spec}\>k)\to\mathrm{QCoh}({\rm Spec}\>k)

is an exact functor, that is,
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is linearly
reductive.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Classically, a finite group scheme
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is said
to be linearly reductive if (3) of this lemma
holds.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
S
be a scheme.

(1)

A finite and étale group scheme
G\to S
is called
tame
if
its degree is prime to all residue characteristics.

(2)

A finite group scheme
G\to S
is called
constant
if it is
isomorphic to the group scheme

\coprod_{g\in G_{\mathrm{abs}}}S\,\to\,S

associated to some finite group
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
.
We will also say that
G
is the
constant group scheme associated to
the group

G_{\mathrm{abs}}
and write the latter
as
(G_{\mathrm{abs}})_{S}\to S
.

A finite group scheme
G\to S
is called
locally constant

if there exists an fppf cover
\{S_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in J}
of
S

such that
G_{S^{\prime}_{j}}\to S_{j}^{\prime}
is constant for all
j\in J
.

(3)

A finite and commutative group scheme
G\to S
is called

(locally) diagonalizable
if its Cartier dual
\shHom(G,{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S})\to S
is
(locally) constant.

Clearly, if
G\to S
is a finite and (locally) constant group scheme,
then it is étale.
We refer to
[
AOV08
, Section 2.3]
for more details and results
about these classes of group schemes.
The most important example for this article is the following.

Example 2.6
.

Let
S
be a scheme.

(1)

If
G\to S
is locally constant and tame, then it is
linearly reductive.

(2)

The group scheme of
n
.th roots of unity

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\to S
,
which is Cartier dual to the constant group scheme

({{\mathbb{Z}}}/n{{\mathbb{Z}}})_{S}\to S
,
is diagonalizable and linearly reductive.

Both assertions follow from
[
AOV08
, Proposition 2.7]
.

2.2.
Over algebraically closed fields

If
G
is the constant group scheme over
k

associated to a finite group
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
, then

G
is linearly reductive if and only if
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
is of order prime to
p
.
In particular, if
p=0
, then
G
is automatically linearly reductive.
Thus, one may think of non-modular representation theory of finite groups
over
k
as a special case of the representation theory of finite and linearly
reductive group schemes.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group scheme
over an algebraically closed field
k

of characteristic
p\geq 0
.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is linearly reductive.

(2)

G
is isomorphic to an extension
of finite group schemes over
k

1\,\to\,D\,\to\,G\,\to\,F\,\to\,1,

where
F
is tame and where

D
is diagonalizable.

(3)

There exists an isomorphism of group schemes over
k

G\quad\cong\quad\prod_{i=1}^{m}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,p^{m_{i}}}\,\rtimes\,H,

where
H
is the constant group scheme associated to some
finite group of order prime to
p
.

Remark 2.8
.

This result is often attributed to Nagata
[
Na61
]
.
However, Nagata assumed
G
to be affine and connected.
It also applies to the non-connected case, which has been
established independently of each other
by Abramovich–Olsson–Vistoli
[
AOV08
]
,
by Chin
[
Ch92
]
, and by Hashimoto
[
Ha15
]
.

This classification fits as follows into the general
structure theory for finite group schemes over fields:
if
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is a finite group scheme over a field
k
,
then there exists a short exact sequence of group schemes
over
k
, the

connected-étale sequence

(4)

1\,\to\,G^{\circ}\,\to\,G\,\to\,G^{\rm\acute{e}t}\,\to\,1,

where
G^{\circ}
is the connected component of the identity
and
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is the maximal étale quotient.
If
k
is perfect, then this sequence splits, and we obtain
a semi-direct product decomposition
G\cong G^{\circ}\rtimes G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
.
If
k
is algebraically closed, then

G
is linearly reductive if and only if

G^{\circ}
is diagonalizable and
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
is tame.
We refer to
[
AOV08
, Section 2.3]
for results about
local semi-direct product decompositions for linearly
reductive group schemes over arbitrary base schemes.

3.
Klein’s classification over fields

In this section, we recall the classification of finite and
linearly reductive subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
, where

k
is an algebraically closed field.
We also recall a general lifting result, which explains
why the classification list in characteristic
p\gg 0

is ‘the same’ as over the complex numbers.

3.1.
Generalities

First, we recall
[
LMM
, Definition 2.7]
:
If
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
is a finite and linearly reductive group scheme over an
algebraically closed field
k
and if
\rho:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}(V)

is a finite dimensional
k
-linear representation, then we define

\lambda(\rho)\,:=\,\max_{\{\mathrm{id}\}\neq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subseteq G}\dim_{k}V^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}},

where
V^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\subseteq V
denotes the subspace of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
-invariants.
As observed in
[
LMM
, Remark 2.8]
, we have that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\rho\mbox{ is faithful }&\Leftrightarrow&\lambda(\rho)\leq\dim V-1\\
\rho\mbox{ contains no pseudo-reflections }&\Leftrightarrow&\lambda(\rho)\leq\dim V-2.\end{array}

In the second case, one also says that
\rho(G)
is a
small
subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{GL}}(V)
.
It is called
very small
if
\lambda(\rho)=0
.
The importance of smallness stems from the classical theorems
of Chevalley and Shephard–Todd, which have been extended to
linearly reductive group schemes in
[
Sa12
]
.

Regarding the classification of finite and linearly reductive subgroup schemes of

{\mathbf{GL}}_{d,k}
, we have the following reduction to the complex numbers.

Theorem 3.1

(Liedtke–Martin–Matsumoto)
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
Then, lifting induces a canonical map

\begin{array}[]{cl}&\{\mbox{ finite and linearly reductive subgroup schemes of }{\mathbf{GL}}_{d,k}\mbox{ up to conjugacy }\}\\
\rightarrow&\{\mbox{ finite subgroups of }{\mathbf{GL}}_{d}({{\mathbb{C}}})\mbox{ up to conjugacy }\},\end{array}

which has the following properties:

(1)

It is injective.

(2)

It is surjective if
p>2d+1
.

(3)

The image is the set of conjugacy classes of finite subgroups of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{d}({{\mathbb{C}}})

that have a unique (possibly trivial) abelian
p
-Sylow subgroup.

(4)

It maps subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{d,k}
to subgroups of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{d}({{\mathbb{C}}})
.

(5)

It preserves
\lambda
-invariants. In particular, small (resp. very small) subgroup schemes
get mapped to small (resp. very small) subgroups.

Proof.

[
LMM
, Theorem 2.13]
.
∎

3.2.
The classification list of Hashimoto and Klein

The finite subgroups of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{C}}})
have been classified by Klein
[
Kl84
]

and they are automatically small.
This classification was extended to algebraically closed fields
of positive characteristic by Hashimoto
[

]
.
Alternatively, one can use

to obtain a new proof of the latter classification

reductive subgroup schemes of

or

is an algebraically

Theorem 3.2

(Hashimoto, Klein)

Let

(2)

(3)

3.4.
The Klein set

For a scheme
S
, we define the
Klein set

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)\,:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{finite, flat, linearly reductive subgroup }\\
\mbox{schemes of }{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\mbox{ of length }n\end{array}\right\}/\sim,

where
\sim
is the equivalence relation of

{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugacy.
If
R
is a commutative ring, then we set

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R):={\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\rm Spec}\>R)
.

If
k
is an algebraically closed field,
then Theorem
3.2
describes

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,k)
.
If
k
is an arbitrary field, then we will describe

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,k)
in a forthcoming monograph.
The associated quotient singularities have already
been determined in
[
LS25
]
.

Remark 3.6
.

If
k
is an algebraically closed field, then

{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(k)
-conjugacy and
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(k)
-conjugacy
of finite subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}
coincide
because if two subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,k}

are conjugate by a matrix

A\in{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(k)
, then there exists an
a\in k

with
a^{2}=\det(A)
and then, the two subgroup schemes
are conjugate by

a^{-1}\cdot A
, which lies in
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(k)
.

However, if
S
is a general base scheme, then there may
not exist a square root of
\det(A)
in
{\cal O}_{S}(S)
and thus,
there may be
more
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugacy classes of finite subgroup schemes
of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
than there are
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugacy classes.

The next result is well-known in the case of finite subgroups
of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}({{\mathbb{C}}})

and the following extension is probably well-known as well.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
G_{i}\to{\rm Spec}\>R
,
i=1,2
be two finite and closed
subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m,R}
over
{\rm Spec}\>R
.
If they are
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(R)
-conjugate, then the invariant subrings

R[x_{1},...,x_{m}]^{G_{i}}
with
i=1,2
(resp.
R[[x_{1},...,x_{m}]]^{G_{i}})

are isomorphic as
R
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{GL}}_{m}(R)
with
AG_{1}A^{-1}=G_{2}
.
Let
V:=k^{m}
be the
k
-vector space with basis
e_{1},...,e_{m}
and with its
natural
{\mathbf{GL}}_{m,R}
-action.
Multiplication by
A
on the left defines an
R
-algebra automorphism of
R[V]
.
If
f\in R[V]
is
G_{1}
-invariant,
then
Af\in R[V]
is
G_{2}
-invariant.
Thus, left multiplication by
A
induces an
R
-algebra homomorphism

R[V]^{G_{1}}\to R[V]^{G_{2}}
, whose inverse is given by left multiplication
by
A^{-1}
.

Similarly, we obtain an
R
-algebra isomorphism

R[[V]]^{G_{1}}\to R[[V]]^{G_{2}}
.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

For a ring
R
, an integer
n\geq 1
, and a class
X\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R)
,
which is represented by some
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,R}
,
we call
{\rm Spec}\>R[x,y]^{G}
the
(linearly reductive) quotient singularity
associated to
X
.

By Lemma
3.7
, this is well-defined:
another choice of representative of the class
X\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R)
leads
to an isomorphic singularity.

4.
Rigidity over Dedekind schemes

In this section, we establish some rigidity results
for finite flat linearly reductive group schemes
and their representations.
Using deformation theory, we first
treat the case where the base is the spectrum
of complete DVR and then,
where it is an excellent Dedekind scheme.
We expect some of our results to hold over more general
base schemes, but for the purposes of this article,
it suffices to treat excellent Dedekind schemes.

4.1.
Complete DVRs

We start with results over complete DVRs,
which are close to the rigidity results in
[
LMM
, Section 2]
.
These are somewhat clumsy to state
since we wish to have a version that works both,
in equal and in mixed characteristic.
We start by recalling a couple of results and a
little bit of terminology from
[
LMM
]
, which we will need in the sequel,
but mostly in the proofs.

Proposition 4.1

(Liedtke–Martin–Matsumoto)
.

let
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
be a finite linearly reductive group
scheme over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p\geq 0
.

(1)

Taking
k
-rational points, we obtain a finite group

G_{\mathrm{abs}}\,:=\,(G^{\circ})^{D}(k)\rtimes G^{\rm\acute{e}t}(k),

where
G^{\circ}
and
G^{\rm\acute{e}t}
are as in (
4
),
and where
-^{D}:=\shHom(-,{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m})
denotes the Cartier dual of
commutative group schemes over
k
.
The assignment

G\quad\mapsto\quad G_{\mathrm{abs}}

induces an equivalence of categories between finite and linearly
reductive group schemes over
k

and finite groups with a unique (possibly trivial) abelian
p
-Sylow subgroup.

(2)

If
p=0
, then
G
is isomorphic to the constant group scheme associated
to
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
.

(3)

If
p>0
, then

(a)

there exists a distinguished finite flat group scheme

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{can}}\to{\rm Spec}\>W(k)

over the ring
W(k)
of Witt vectors of
k
with special fiber
G\to{\rm Spec}\>k
.

(b)

If
R
is a complete DVR with residue field
k
, whose
field of fractions
K
is of characteristic zero,
if
K\subset\overline{K}
is an algebraic closure, and if

\mathcal{G}\to{\rm Spec}\>R
is a finite flat group scheme over
R

with special fiber
G
,
then
G_{\mathrm{abs}}\cong\mathcal{G}(\overline{K})
, the group of

\overline{K}
-rational points.

(c)

If
R
is a complete DVR with residue field
k
and if

\mathcal{G}_{i}\to{\rm Spec}\>R
,
i=1,2
are two finite flat group schemes
over
R
with special fiber
G
,
then after possibly replacing
R
by a finite extension of DVRs,

\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are isomorphic as
group schemes over
R
.

Proof.

This is discussed in
[
LMM
, Section 2]
, where (1)
is
[
LMM
, Lemma 2.1]
, where (2) directly follows from (1),
and where the assertions in (3) follow from
[
LMM
, Proposition 2.4]
.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

The finite group
G_{\mathrm{abs}}
associated to
G
is called the

abstract group
associated to
G
.
The distinguished lift
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{can}}\to{\rm Spec}\>W(k)

is called the
canonical lift
of
G
.

After these preparations, we can state and prove the rigidity result
of this section for complete DVRs.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a complete DVR,
let
s\in S:={\rm Spec}\>R
be a point, and let
\kappa(s)
be its
residue field.

(1)

If
G_{i}\to S
,
i=1,2
are two finite flat linearly
reductive group schemes, such that

G_{1,s}\cong G_{2,s}
as group schemes
over
\kappa(s)
, then after possibly
replacing
R
by a finite extension
of DVRs,

G_{1}\cong G_{2}
as group schemes over
S
.

(2)

If
G\to S
is a finite flat and linearly
reductive group scheme and
if
\rho_{i}:G\to{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
,
i=1,2
are
two linear representation
of group schemes over

, such that

\rho_{1,s}\cong\rho_{2,s}

as representations of
G_{s}
over
\kappa(s)
,
then
\rho_{1}\cong\rho_{2}
as representations
of
G
over
S
.

(3)

If
G_{i}\to S
,

i=1,2
are two finite flat linearly
reductive subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
, such that

G_{1,s}
and
G_{2,s}

are conjugate as subgroup schemes of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,\kappa(s)}
,
then after possibly
replacing
R
by a finite extension
of DVRs,

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are conjugate as subgroup schemes
of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
.

is the closed point, then - after possibly extending

by a finite extension of DVRs - we can extend the

to

.

Proof.

Let
k
be the residue field of

and let

be
its field of fractions.

(1) First, assume that

is of equal characteristic zero.
Then,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are both

.
After possibly replacing

by a finite extension,
both of them become the constant group schemes over
S

that are associated to the finite groups

G_{1}(S)
and
G_{2}(S)
, respectively.
The assumption then implies that
G_{1}(S)\cong G_{2}(S)

and one can extend
the given isomorphism.

Second, assume that

is of equal positive characteristic.
Then, we have

It then follows from
[
LMM
, Proposition 2.4]

that after possibly replacing

by a finite extension,
we have that

Third, if
\cha k=p>0

[

.

Fourth, assume that
\cha K=0

be an algebraic closure of

Then the abstract groups

and

are isomorphic.
On the other hand,

This implies that

and
G_{2,{\mathfrak{m}}}

and

(2)
First, assume that

Second, assume that

.

and

\rho_{1}
and

over

(3)
Since

by a finite extension.
Thus, we may assume that we have two closed

of group schemes over

∎

Example 4.4
.

The assumption on linear reductivity in
Proposition
4.3
.(1)
cannot be dropped, as the following examples show:
Let
p
be a prime, let
R
be equal to
{{\mathbb{Z}}}_{p}[\sqrt[p]{p}]
or
{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}[[t]]
,
and set

Set
\varpi:=\sqrt[p]{p}
in the first case and

\varpi:=t
in the second case.
Thus,
\varpi\in R
is a uniformizer.

To give a finite flat group scheme
G\to S

of length

is equivalent to giving a tuple

(a,b)\in R\times R
with
ab=p
modulo the equivalence relation

(a,b)\sim(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
if and only if
\exists u\in R^{\times}

with

,

.
These are the

of

and we refer to

]

4.2.
Dedekind schemes

Let us recall that a
Dedekind scheme
is a Noetherian integral
scheme of dimension 1, all of whose local rings are regular.
Before proceeding, we recall the following result,
which should be well-known.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
S
be a scheme.
If
G_{i}\to S
,
i=1,2
are two finite and flat
group schemes, then the functor

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\mathrm{Sch}/S)&\to&(\mathrm{Sets})\\
T&\mapsto&\mathrm{Isom}(G_{1,T},G_{2,T}),\end{array}

where
\mathrm{Isom}(G_{1,T},G_{2,T})
is the set
of isomorphisms of group schemes over
T
, is representable
by a scheme
{\mathbf{Isom}}(G_{1},G_{2})\to S
, which is
affine and of finite type over
S
.

In the special case
G:=G_{1}=G_{2}
, this shows that
the automorphism functor is represented by a group scheme

{\mathbf{Aut}}_{G}\to S
, which is affine
and of finite type over
S
.

Proof.

First, we show that if
G\to S
is a finite and flat group
scheme, then
{\mathbf{Aut}}_{G}\to S
exists as an affine
group scheme of finite type over
S
.
The question is local on
S
and thus, we may assume
S

to be affine and we may also assume that
{\cal O}_{G}
is a
free
{\cal O}_{S}
-module of some finite rank
n
.
But then, the functor
T\mapsto{\rm Aut}(G_{T})

is a closed subfunctor
of the functor
T\mapsto{\rm Aut}({\cal O}_{G,T})
,
which is representable by the scheme
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
, which is
affine and of finite type over
S
.
Thus,
{\mathbf{Aut}}_{G}
can be realized as a closed subgroup
scheme of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
and thus, it also affine and of finite
type over
S
.

Second, we show the first claim of the lemma:
The functor is a right
{\mathbf{Aut}}_{G_{1}}
-torsor
in the fppf topology.
Thus, it is representable by a scheme

[
Mi80
, Theorem III.4.3]
, which is also affine and of finite type over

because
{\mathbf{Aut}}_{G_{1}}
has these properties.
∎

Remark 4.6

Probably, more can be said in the case of finite flat linearly
reductive group schemes.
For example, we will see in forthcoming work that the automorphism group

5.
Locally conjugate subgroup schemes

In this section, we study closed subgroup
schemes of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{n,S}
over some base scheme
S
that are locally
in the fppf topology conjugate to each other.
We give a cohomological description,
which extends
[
LS25
, Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.4]
,
where we established this in the case where
S
is the spectrum
of a field.

5.1.
Locally conjugate subgroup schemes and normalizers

Let
S
be a base scheme and let
G_{i}\to S
,
i=1,2

and
X\to S
be group schemes that are finitely presented, affine,
and flat group over
S
.
Assume that both,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are closed subgroup schemes of
X

over
S
, which we assume to be Noetherian and separated by our
standing assumption.

Definition 5.1
.

For a Grothendieck topology
\mathcal{T}
on
S
,
we will say that

G_{1}\subseteq X
is
locally in the
\mathcal{T}
-topology
conjugate
to
G_{2}\subseteq X
if
there exists a
\mathcal{T}
-cover
\{S^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of
S
, such that

G_{1,S^{\prime}_{j}}\subseteq X_{S^{\prime}_{j}}
is
X(S^{\prime}_{j})
-conjugate to

G_{2,S^{\prime}_{j}}\subseteq X_{S^{\prime}_{j}}
for all
j\in J
.

In order to state Theorem
5.2
, we recall the notion
of a normalizer scheme:
for a base scheme
S
, a group scheme
X\to S
and a closed subgroup scheme

G\subseteq X
over
S
, we consider the functor

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\mathrm{Sch}/S)&\to&(\mathrm{Groups})\\
T&\mapsto&\{x\in X(T)\,:\,x\,G_{T}\,x^{-1}\,\subseteq\,X_{T}\}.\end{array}

In our setting where
S
is Noetherian and
G
and
X

are affine and of finite type over
S
,
this functor is representable by a closed subgroup scheme

{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
of
X
over
S
, see,
for example,
[
Co14
, Proposition 2.1.2]
.

5.2.
Classification via torsors and cohomology

In the special case where
S
is the spectrum of a field,
we already obtained the following result
in
[
LS25
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
LS25
, Corollary 2.1]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian separated
scheme and let
G
and
X
be group schemes
that are affine, flat, and of finite type over
S
.
Assume that
G
is a closed subgroup scheme of
X
over
S
.
Then, there exists a bijection between the following pointed sets:

(1)

Subgroup schemes of
X
that are locally in the fppf topology
conjugate to
G\subset X
, up to
X(S)
-conjugacy.

(2)

{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsors
E\to S
in the fppf topology
that admit an equivariant embedding into
X
.

(3)

Elements of the pointed cohomology set

\ker\left({H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,X)\right).

Proof.

We extend the proof
[
LS25
, Theorem 1.4]
to our setting.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
Let
H\subset X
be a closed subgroup scheme that becomes
conjugate to
G\subset X
over some fppf cover
\{S_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in J}
of
S
.
By
[
Co14
, Proposition 2.1.2]
, the functor

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\mathrm{Schemes}/S)&\to&(\mathrm{Sets})\\
S&\mapsto&\{x\in X(S)|x\cdot H_{S}\cdot x^{-1}=G_{S}\}\end{array}

is representable by a closed subscheme
E_{H}
of
X
.
It is also a left
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsor and since we have

E_{H}(S_{j}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
for all
j\in J
,
this
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsor splits over
\{S_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in J}
.
Furthermore, the embedding
E_{H}\subset X

is
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-equivariant.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
:
Let
E
be a left
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsor over
S
that splits over an fppf
cover
\{S_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in J}
of
S
.
Since
E(S_{j}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
for all
j\in J
, we may pick elements

t_{j}\in E(S_{j}^{\prime})
.
We note that
E(S_{j}^{\prime})={\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)\cdot S_{j}^{\prime}
, which shows that

t_{j}^{-1}\cdot G_{S^{\prime}_{j}}\cdot t_{j}\subset X_{S_{j}^{\prime}}

is independent of the choice of element
t_{j}
.
Then, we define

H_{E}\,:=\,(G\times_{S}E)/{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)\,\to\,(X\times_{S}E)/{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)\,=\,X,

where the action of
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
on
G\times_{S}E
and
X\times_{S}E

is diagonal via conjugation on the first factor and the
torsor structure on the second factor.
This way, we obtain a subgroup scheme
H_{E}\subseteq X
over
S

that becomes
t_{j}^{-1}\cdot G_{S_{j}^{\prime}}\cdot t_{j}\subseteq X_{S_{j}^{\prime}}

over
S_{j}^{\prime}
.

It is straightforward to check that the constructions

H\mapsto E_{H}
and
E\mapsto H_{E}
are inverse to each other
and we obtain a bijection.
In both cases, there is a distinguished element, namely
the
X(S)
-conjugacy class of
G\subset X
in (1)
and the trivial
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsor in (2).
Our bijection sends these distinguished elements to
each other and
we obtain a bijection of pointed sets.

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
:
It suffices to note that a left
{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G)
-torsor
E
over
S

admits an equivariant closed immersion
into
X
if and only if its pushout to
X
is trivial
and thus, if and only if it lies in the kernel
of the map

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{X}(G))\to{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,X)
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

With notations and assumptions as in Theorem
5.2
,
we note that a sheaf of

-torsors with respect to the fppf
topology

is representable by a scheme, see

,

6.
The key observation

In this section, we study the Klein sets
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,S)
, that is,
finite flat linearly reductive subgroup schemes
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
of length

n
over
S
(see Section
3.4
),
in the special case where
S
is an excellent Dedekind scheme.
The key result is that if
S
has a point of residue characteristic
2
,
then
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
is locally in the fppf topology conjugate to
the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
.
Thus, we only have one infinite series and its conjugates in the fppf topology as opposed to
two infinite series and three exceptional
cases – as over the complex numbers and as recalled
in Section
3
.

For any base scheme
S
and any integer
n\geq 1
,
we have the finite flat linearly reductive
group scheme
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\to S
.
We have an isomorphism (Cartier duality)

{\rm Hom}\left(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S},{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}\right)\,\cong\,({{\mathbb{Z}}}/n{{\mathbb{Z}}})_{S}

of constant group schemes over
S
.
If
S
is connected, then
we choose a generator
\psi
of this
cyclic and constant group scheme and define

\rho\,:=\,\psi\oplus\psi^{\vee}\quad:\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\quad\to\quad{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\,\subset\,{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}

to be the
standard embedding
of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}

into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
.
If we pick another generator, then
this changes
\rho
by pre-composing

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}
with an automorphism
of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}
.
In particular, the image of
\rho

does not depend on the choice of
\psi
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R
be an excellent Dedekind ring, let
n\geq 2
be an integer,
and let
G\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R)
.
Assume that there exists a point of
S
of residue characteristic
2
.
Then,
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,R}
is locally in the fppf topology
conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,R}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,R}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the field of fractions of
R
,
let
s\in S
be a point whose residue field
k:=\kappa(s)

has characteristic
2
, and let
k\subseteq\overline{k}

be an algebraic closure.
By Theorem
3.2
, we know that

(G_{s})_{\overline{k}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,\overline{k}}
is conjugate
to the standard embedding of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,\overline{k}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,\overline{k}}
.
Thus, after possibly replacing
R
by its integral closure
in a suitable finite extension of
K
, we may assume
that
G_{s}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,s}
is conjugate to

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,s}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,s}
.
The assertion now follows from
Theorem
4.7
.
∎

Combined with Theorem
5.2
,
we obtain the following
cohomological description of the Klein set

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R)
introduced in
Section
3.4
.

Corollary 6.2
.

With notations and assumptions of Theorem
6.1
,
there exists a bijection of pointed sets

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,R)\quad\cong\quad\ker\left({H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(R,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,R}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,R}))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(R,{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,R})\right),

where the distinguished elements on both sides
correspond to the class of the standard
embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,R}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,R}
.∎

7.
Twisted forms of
\mu_{n}
inside
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

In this section, we start from an arbitrary base scheme
S

and the standard embedding (see Section

\psi\oplus\psi^{\vee}\,:\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\,\to\,{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\,\subset\,{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}.

{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)

where we use the normalizer scheme of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}
inside

{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}
.
Of course, the distinguished element of this pointed set corresponds
to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}

First, we will compute this normalizer, then

we will describe the associated twisted subgroup schemes

In the special case where

[

A new feature when working over a general base schemes

7.3.
Zariski local forms

We represent a class

(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2})

{\cal O}_{S}

The associated

of
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{2}

{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{2}

The content of this section is that

may nevertheless give rise to a non-standard embedding of
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{2}

into
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}

conjugate to the standard torus.

Of course, if

is the spectrum of a field or a local ring,

{\rm Pic}(S)
is trivial, which is why the
Zariski local forms of this section did not show up in

[
LS25

Next, for a locally free
{\cal O}_{S}
-module

of finite rank

we have a sheaf of
{\cal O}_{S}
-algebras

\shEnd(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\vee}

Its group of multiplicative units

is an affine group scheme over
S
that we denote by
{\mathbf{GL}}(\mathcal{E})\to S
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to
{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus r}

{\mathbf{GL}}(\mathcal{E})\to S
is
a Zariski local form of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{r,S}\to S

If

, then
{\mathbf{GL}}(\mathcal{E})\cong{\mathbf{GL}}_{1,S}={{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}
.
Also, if
\mathcal{E}\cong{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus r}
, then

{\mathbf{GL}}(\mathcal{E})={\mathbf{GL}}_{r,S}
.

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}
.
By Remark
7.5
, this is the
twisted form of the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}

associated to
\eta
.
Note that by (
8
), it is the
n
-torsion
of the kernel
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}\wedge S^{\prime}
.

7.5.
Summary

Let us briefly summarize and generalize the previous discussion:
If
S
is a
scheme and
n\geq 3
is an integer,
then subgroup schemes
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
that are fppf locally
conjugate to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}

are classified up to
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugacy by

\ker\left(\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S})\,\right).

Under the split surjective map

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})

the class
[G]
of such a form maps
to the class of some
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}
-torsor
f:S^{\prime}\to S
, that is,
an étale double cover.
Using the splitting, there is an associated
subgroup scheme
G^{\prime}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
that is locally in
the fppf topology (in fact: étale topology) conjugate
to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
.
A construction of the form
G^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\wedge{S^{\prime}}

is described in detail in Section
7.4
.

After base changing to
S^{\prime}
, the three classes of the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S^{\prime}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S^{\prime}}
, of
G_{S^{\prime}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S^{\prime}}
and of

G^{\prime}_{S^{\prime}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S^{\prime}}
in the middle of

1\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S^{\prime},{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S^{\prime}}^{2})\,\to\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S^{\prime},{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S^{\prime}}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S^{\prime},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})\,\to\,1

map to
\{\ast\}
and thus, lie in the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\{\ast\})
.
Thus,
G_{S^{\prime}}
and
G^{\prime}_{S^{\prime}}
are locally in the fppf topology
(in fact: Zariski topology) conjugate
to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S^{\prime}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S^{\prime}}
.
A construction of the forms
G_{S^{\prime}}
and
G^{\prime}_{S^{\prime}}

is described in detail in Section
7.3
.

Remark 7.12
.

In view of Proposition
7.1
.(2), we have
isomorphisms of pointed cohomology sets

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{H_{\rm\acute{e}t}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}))&\cong&{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}))\\
{H_{\rm\acute{e}t}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}({{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{2}))&\cong&{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}}({{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{2})),\end{array}

see
[
Mi80
, Theorem III.3.9]
.
In particular, a subgroup scheme
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
that is fppf locally
conjugate to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}

is already étale locally conjugate to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}

and the discussion preceding this remark make this precise.

7.6.
Dedekind rings

Lemma
7.6
and Lemma
7.7

beg for the characterization of

r
-tuples of invertible
{\cal O}_{S}
-modules

\vec{\mathcal{L}}:=(\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{r})

that satisfy
\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{L}_{r}\cong{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus r}
.
Clearly, we have a map

\omega:{\rm Pic}(S)^{\oplus r}\,\to\,{\rm Pic}(S),\quad\vec{\mathcal{L}}\,\mapsto\,\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes...\otimes\mathcal{L}_{r}.

Now, if
\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus...\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{r}\cong{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus r}
,
then taking determinants shows that
\vec{\mathcal{L}}
must lie in the
kernel of
\omega
, that is,
\omega(\vec{\mathcal{L}})\cong{\cal O}_{S}
.
The converse holds for spectra of Dedekind rings:

Theorem 7.13

(Steinitz)
.

Let
R
be a Dedekind domain and set
S:={\rm Spec}\>R
.

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}
is a locally free
{\cal O}_{S}
-module of rank
r\geq 1
,
then there exist invertible
{\cal O}_{S}
-modules

\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{r}
, such that

\mathcal{E}\quad\cong\quad\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus...\oplus\mathcal{L}_{r}.

(2)

For invertible
{\cal O}_{S}
-modules

\vec{\mathcal{L}}:=(\mathcal{L}_{1},...,\mathcal{L}_{r})

and
\vec{\mathcal{M}}=(\mathcal{M}_{1},...\mathcal{M}_{r})
, we have

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\,\mathcal{L}_{i}\,\cong\,\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\,\mathcal{M}_{i}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\omega(\vec{\mathcal{L}})\,\cong\,\omega(\vec{\mathcal{M}}).

In particular, the direct sum on the left is a free
{\cal O}_{S}
-module of rank
r

if and only if
\omega(\vec{\mathcal{L}})\cong{\cal O}_{S}
.

Proof.

This is just a reformulation of a theorem of Steinitz
[
St12
]
,
see
[
CR62
, §22]
or
[
FT91
, Chapter II.4]
for modern accounts.
∎

We leave the following fancy reformulation to the
reader.

Corollary 7.14
.

If
R
is a Dedekind domain, set
S:={\rm Spec}\>R
,
let
{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}={\mathbf{GL}}_{1,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{r,S}
be the subtorus
defined by

(9)

t\,\mapsto\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}t&0&...&0\\
0&1&....&0\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\cdots\hfil\lx@intercol\\
0&0&...&1\end{array}\right)\,.

The map (
7
) can be factored as

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S}^{r})\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega_{\ast}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{{\mathbb{G}}}_{m,S})\quad\to\quad{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}(S,{\mathbf{GL}}_{r,S}),

where the first map is surjective and the second map
is a bijection of pointed sets induced by the
inclusion (
9
).∎

Example 7.15
.

This theorem is not true for non-affine Dedekind schemes,
even if
r=2
as the following example shows:
Let
S={{\mathbb{P}}}^{1}_{k}
, where
k
is a field.
Then,
\mathcal{E}_{m}:={\cal O}_{S}(m)\oplus{\cal O}_{S}(-m)

has trivial determinant for all
m\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}

and thus, the tuple
\vec{\mathcal{L}}_{m}:=({\cal O}_{S}(m),{\cal O}_{S}(-m))
lies in
\ker\omega
.
On the other hand, we have
\dim_{k}H^{0}(S,\mathcal{E}_{m})=m+1

if
m\geq 1
and
\dim_{k}H^{0}(S,{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus 2})=2
,
which shows that
\mathcal{E}_{m}\not\cong{\cal O}_{S}^{\oplus 2}
if
m\geq 2
.

8.
Examples of non-standard embeddings of
\mu_{n}

In Section
7
, we described subgroup schemes

G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}
that are fppf locally conjugate to the standard
embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}
.

(1)

If
S
is the spectrum of a field, then
only the étale forms described in Section
7.4

can show up and we described them in
[
LS25
]
.
For non-trivial étale forms, the group scheme
G
is not isomorphic to

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}
, but a quadratic twist.

(2)

Now, if
{\rm Pic}(S)
is non-trivial, then also Zariski local forms as
described in Section
7.3
show up.
These have the property that
G
is abstractly isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,S}
,
but that its embedding into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}

is not
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(S)
-conjugate to the standard embedding; rather it is
Zariski locally conjugate to the standard embedding.
We will refer to these as
non-standard embeddings
of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,S}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,S}
.

The objective of this section is to give examples of such
non-standard embeddings in the special case
where
S={\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
, where
{\cal O}_{K}
is the ring
of integers in a number field
K
.

8.1.
Zariski local forms revisited

Let
K
be a number field, that is, a finite field extension of
{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Let
{\cal O}_{K}
be the ring of integers in
K
and let
{\rm Cl}_{K}:={\rm Cl}({\cal O}_{K})\cong{\rm Pic}({\cal O}_{K})

be the
class group
of
K
.
It is well-known that
{\rm Cl}_{K}
is a finite group and thus, its order
h_{K}
, which is
called the
class number
of
K
, is finite.
We refer to
[
Ne99
, Kapitel I]
for details and proofs.

The following proposition can also be deduced from the general results
in Section
7
, but its proof - which is independent
of the machinery introduced in the previous sections - leads the way
to find non-standard embeddings of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
in the case
of rings of integers in number fields.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
K
be a number field with
h_{K}=1
.
Let
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
be a subgroup scheme that is
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)
-conjugate
to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
Then,
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
is also
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K})
-conjugate to

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}

Proof.

By assumption, there exists
A\in{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)
, such that
G
is of the form

A\diag(u,u^{n-1})A^{-1}
and
u\in\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}

We may always scale to assume that
A
has entries in
{\cal O}_{K}
,
but then, its inverse may not have entries in
{\cal O}_{K}
, that is, we may not
assume that
A
lies in
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K})
.
Setting

A\,:=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\,\in\,\mathrm{Mat}_{2\times 2}({\cal O}_{K}),

we see

A\diag(u,u^{n-1})A^{-1}\,=\,\frac{1}{\Delta}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}uad-u^{n-1}bc&(u^{n-1}-u)ab\\
(u-u^{n-1})cd&u^{n-1}ad-ubc\end{array}\right)

where
\Delta:=\det(A)=ad-bc
.
All of these quantities lie in
{\cal O}_{K}
.
If
\Delta\in{\cal O}_{K}^{\times}
then we are done because
A
lies in
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K})
.
If
\Delta
is not a unit,
then there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset{\cal O}_{K}
containing
\Delta
.
As a result,
{\mathfrak{p}}
contains
ad
and
bc
, so
A
must have either a row or a
column contained in
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
h_{K}=1
, we have that
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a principal ideal, say,
generated by some irreducible element
p\in{\cal O}_{K}
.
Scaling down the appropriate row or column of
A
by
p
,
we have decreased
|{\mathbf{N}}(\det(A))|
, so by induction we are done.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

The proof shows that even if we do not assume
h_{K}=1
, then it is still true that
all embeddings of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
in
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
that are
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)
-conjugate to
the standard embedding
\rho:\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\to{\mathbf{SL}}_{2}
must be of the form

A\rho(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,K})A^{-1}
for some
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{2\times 2}({\cal O}_{K})\cap{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)

that has a row or a column with entries
in some prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\cal O}_{K}
.

8.2.
Examples

Next, we show the necessity of
h_{K}=1
in
Proposition
8.1
by producing an example
of an embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
that is
Zariski locally conjugate to the standard embedding but
not globally conjugate to the standard embedding.

Example 8.3
.

Let
K={{\mathbb{Q}}}(\sqrt{10})
, whose ring of integers is
{\cal O}_{K}={{\mathbb{Z}}}[\sqrt{10}]
.
Let
\alpha:=4+\sqrt{10}
with Galois conjugate

\overline{\alpha}=4-\sqrt{10}

and consider the matrix

A\,:=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}3&\alpha\\
\overline{\alpha}&3\end{array}\right)\,\in\,\mathrm{Mat}_{2\times 2}({\cal O}_{K})\cap{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K).

Note that the principal ideal
(3)\subset{\cal O}_{K}
factors into the product of
non-principal prime ideals
(3,\alpha)\cdot(3,\overline{\alpha})
.
In particular, every row or column of
A
has entries living in a prime ideal.
We see

\det A\,=\,9-{\mathbf{N}}(\alpha)\,=\,3,

where
{\mathbf{N}}
denotes the norm,
which divides
3\alpha
,
3\overline{\alpha}
,
3^{2}
, and
\alpha\overline{\alpha}=6
.
Hence,

A\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right)\,A^{-1}

has entries in
{\cal O}_{K}
.
From this, we obtain an embedding of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.

(1)

We claim that this yields an embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\to{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
that
is not
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
-conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.

If it were, then there would be a matrix
B\in{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K})
such that

B^{-1}A\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right)A^{-1}B\quad=\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{array}\right)

Thus,
B^{-1}A
is in
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)
and it centralizes
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,K}
, hence is a diagonal matrix.
Thus, we see

B\quad=\quad A\cdot\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

with
\lambda_{i}\in K^{\times}
.
Since
B
has entries in
{\cal O}_{K}
, we find

3\lambda_{1}\in{\cal O}_{K}
and
\overline{\alpha}\lambda_{1}\in{\cal O}_{K}
.
As a result, we find
9{\mathbf{N}}(\lambda_{1})\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
6{\mathbf{N}}(\lambda_{1})\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}

and hence,
3{\mathbf{N}}(\lambda_{1})\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Similarly, we find
3{\mathbf{N}}(\lambda_{2})\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Next,

\pm 1\,=\,{\mathbf{N}}(\det B)\,=\,(3{\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{1})\cdot(3{\mathbf{N}}\lambda_{2})

is a product of integers and thus,

3{\mathbf{N}}(\lambda_{i})\,=\,\pm 1.

Since we also know
3\lambda_{1}=a+b\sqrt{10}\in{\cal O}_{K}
, we see

a^{2}-10b^{2}\,=\,{\mathbf{N}}(3\lambda_{1})\,=\,\pm 3

with
a,b\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Reducing modulo
5
, we obtain a contradiction.

(2)

Thus, we have produced a non-standard embedding of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
We note that it is Zariski locally conjugate to the standard embedding.
Indeed, we have

2\cdot 3\,=\,\alpha\overline{\alpha},

so upon inverting
\alpha
, we see
\overline{\alpha}
is a multiple of
3
.
Thus, we may take
\lambda_{1}=1/3
and
\lambda_{2}=1
.
Similarly, if we invert
\overline{\alpha}
, we may take
\lambda_{1}=1
and
\lambda_{2}=1/3
.
Lastly, if we invert
3
, then since
\det A=3
we may take
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1
.
These define three Zariski open patches that cover
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
,
as
(3,\alpha,\overline{\alpha})={\cal O}_{K}
and over each patch,
our non-standard embedding is conjugate to the standard embedding.

Example 8.4
.

Let
q>3
be a prime with
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
such that the integer

d:=q-1
is square-free.
Let
K={{\mathbb{Q}}}(\sqrt{-dq})
.
We set

A\,:=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q&\sqrt{-dq}\\
-\sqrt{-dq}&q\end{array}\right).

The columns generate a non-principal ideal in
{\cal O}_{K}
by

[
Co
, Corollary 2.7]
.
Conjugating the standard embedding
\rho:\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}\to{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,K}
,
u\mapsto\diag(u,u^{n-1})
by
A
,
we see that

A\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&0\\
0&u^{n-1}\end{array}\right)\,A^{-1}

has entries in
{\cal O}_{K}
and it is invertible in
K
.
From this, we obtain a non-standard embedding of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
.
We want to compute the associated invariant subring

{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}

and note that if we set

v^{\prime}\,:=\,Ax\,=\,qx+\sqrt{-dq}\cdot y,\quad w^{\prime}\,:=\,Ay\,=\,-\sqrt{-dq}\cdot x+qy,

then

(10)

{\cal O}_{K}[q^{-1}][x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}={\cal O}_{K}[q^{-1}][v^{\prime n},w^{\prime n},v^{\prime}w^{\prime}].

We have
\det A=q(q-d)=q
, which is not a unit in
{\cal O}_{K}
.
Now, these 3 invariants generate the invariant ring over
the principal open subset
D(\det A)={\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[q^{-1}]

of
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
.
In particular,

{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[q^{-1}][x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\,\to\,{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[q^{-1}]

is a family of RDPs and the fiber over each point is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}
.

To see what happens outside this principal open set, we set

L=K(\sqrt{-q})
, which is a Galois extension
L/K
with
group
\Gal(L/K)\cong{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and we let
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K)

be the generator.
We note that

-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-q}}A\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{-q}&-\sqrt{d}\\
\sqrt{d}&\sqrt{-q}\end{array}\right)

lies in
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{L})
.
The invariant ring over
{\cal O}_{L}
can thus be computed as follows
(see also the proof of Lemma
3.7
):

{\cal O}_{L}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\,=\,{\cal O}_{L}[v^{n},vw,w^{n}],

where

v\,:=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{-q}}v^{\prime}\,=\,\,\sqrt{-q}x-\sqrt{d}y,\quad w\,:=\,\frac{-1}{\sqrt{-q}}w^{\prime}\,=\,\sqrt{d}x+\sqrt{-q}y.

The generator
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K)
sends
\sqrt{-q}
and
\sqrt{d}
,
as well as
v
and
w
to their negatives.

(a)

If
n
is even, then we find

{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\,=\,{\cal O}_{K}[v^{n},vw,w^{n}].

If we think of
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\to{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
as a family
of RDPs, then the fiber over each point is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}
.

(b)

If
n
is odd, then we have more interesting behavior.
By
[
Co
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have an equality of
{\cal O}_{K}
-modules

{\cal O}_{L}\,=\,{\cal O}_{K}e_{1}\,\oplus\,\mathfrak{q}e_{2},

where
\mathfrak{q}=q{\cal O}_{K}+\sqrt{-dq}{\cal O}_{K}

and where

e_{1}\,=\,\frac{1+\sqrt{-q}}{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad e_{2}\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{-q}}.

The generator
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K)
acts by

\sigma(e_{1})\,=\,e_{1}+qe_{2},\quad\sigma(e_{2})\,=\,-e_{2}.

It follows that
\alpha\in{\cal O}_{L}
has
\sigma(\alpha)=-\alpha
if and only if
\alpha\in\mathfrak{q}e_{2}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{L}[v^{n},vw,w^{n}]^{\Gal(L/K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{K}[vw,\sqrt{d}v^{n},\sqrt{d}w^{n},\sqrt{-q}v^{n},\sqrt{-q}w^{n}].

Let us justify why equality holds.
We have

{\cal O}_{L}[v^{n},vw,w^{n}]^{\Gal(L/K)}\subset(L[x,y]^{\mu_{n}})^{\Gal(L/K)}\cap{\cal O}_{L}[x,y]^{\Gal(L/K)}.

Then
{\cal O}_{L}[x,y]^{\Gal(L/K)}={\cal O}_{K}[x,y]
and

(L[x,y]^{\mu_{n}})^{\Gal(L/K)}=K[x,y]^{\mu_{n}}

by linear reductivity of
\Gal(L/K)
over
K
, so

(11)

{\cal O}_{L}[v^{n},vw,w^{n}]^{\Gal(L/K)}\subset{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}.

In fact, since
\Gal(L/K)={{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is linearly reductive over
{\cal O}_{K}[2^{-1}]
,
we see equation (
11
) becomes an equality after inverting
2
.
Furthermore, we explicitly computed
{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}
after inverting
q
,
given in equation (
10
).
Since
q{\cal O}_{K}+2{\cal O}_{K}={\cal O}_{K}
, we therefore see equation (
11
)
is an equality.

Next, letting

A\,:=\,vw,\quad B\,:=\,\sqrt{d}v^{n},\quad C\,:=\,\sqrt{d}w^{n},\quad B^{\prime}\,:=\,\sqrt{-q}v^{n},\quad C^{\prime}\,:\,=\sqrt{-q}w^{n},

we see

{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}={\cal O}_{K}[A,B,C,B^{\prime},C^{\prime}]/J,

where
J
is the ideal generated by

\sqrt{-dq}B^{\prime}+qB,\quad\sqrt{-dq}C^{\prime}+qC,\quad\sqrt{-dq}B-dB^{\prime},\quad\sqrt{-dq}C-dC^{\prime},

dA^{n}-BC,\quad qA^{n}+B^{\prime}C^{\prime},\quad\sqrt{-dq}A^{n}-B^{\prime}C,\quad\sqrt{-dq}A^{n}-BC^{\prime}.

Again, we think of

{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}\,\to\,{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

as a family of RDPs.
It follows from Proposition
9.8
that the fibers over
{\cal O}_{K}

are RDPs of type
A_{n-1}
, but this can also be seen
by explicit computation:

(a)

Over
K
,
we have that
B,B^{\prime}
and
C,C^{\prime}
are multiples of each
other.
Thus, we can eliminate two of them and obtain

\displaystyle K[x,y]^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle K[A,B,C]/(dA^{n}-BC)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle K[A,B^{\prime},C^{\prime}]/(qA^{n}+B^{\prime}C^{\prime}),

which is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}
over
K

, Kapitel VII, Theorem (13.4)]
.
In particular, if
L/K
is an unramified quadratic extension,
then all non-zero prime ideals of
{\cal O}_{K}

can be divided into two disjoint sets: the totally split ones and the inert ones.
Both sets have density
1/2
and in particular, they are infinite.
Combining this with Proposition
9.8
and the discussion preceding it,
we obtain the following.

Proposition 9.10
.

Let
G\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})
and let

f\quad:\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}[x,y]^{G}\quad\to\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

be the associated family of RDP singularities.
We consider the subsets

\Sigma_{A}\subseteq{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}\mbox{ \hskip 10.22217ptand \hskip 10.22217pt}\Sigma_{B}\subseteq{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

that are defined by the property that the fiber
f^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})

is an RDP singularity of type
A_{n-1}
and
B_{\beta(n)}
,
respectively.
Then,
\Sigma_{A}
and
\Sigma_{B}
partition
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
and precisely one of the following two cases happens:

(1)

G\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
and then,
\Sigma_{A}={\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
,
and
\Sigma_{B}=\emptyset
.

(2)

G\not\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
and then,
\Sigma_{A}
and
\Sigma_{B}

are infinite sets and both have density
1/2
.∎

9.3.
The infinite places

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be an infinite place of the number field
K
,
let
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the corresponding completion,
and let
G\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K})
with
n\geq 3
.

As seen above, we have an unramified extension
{\cal O}_{K}\subset R

of degree 2 associated to
G
.
If it is trivial, then
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}
is Zariski locally
conjugate to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K}}

and thus,
G_{K}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,K}
is
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K)
-conjugate to
the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,K}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,K}
.
In this case, we find that
G_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,K_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is

{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}(K_{{\mathfrak{p}}})
-conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}

and thus, the associated quotient singularity is an RDP of type
A_{n-1}

over
K_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

We may thus assume that
{\cal O}_{K}\subset R
is a non-trivial extension,
that is, we may assume that
R={\cal O}_{L}
for some unramified quadratic
Galois extension
K\subset L
.
Note that if
{\mathfrak{p}}
is real place then may not stay real
in
L
, see Caution
9.3
.
We have three cases:

(1)

{\mathfrak{p}}
is complex, that is
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong{{\mathbb{C}}}
.
Then
G_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{C}}}}

is
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{C}}})
-conjugate to the standard embedding of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{{\mathbb{C}}}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{C}}}}

and the associated quotient singularity is an RDP
singularity of type
A_{n-1}
over
{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

(2)

{\mathfrak{p}}
is real, that is
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong{{\mathbb{R}}}
and then,
there are two subcases:

(a)

It stays real in
L
.
Then,
G_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{R}}}}

is
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}})
-conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{{\mathbb{C}}}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{R}}}}

and the associated quotient singularity is an RDP
singularity of type
A_{n-1}
over
{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

(b)

{\mathfrak{p}}
becomes complex in
L
and then,

G_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the unique quadratic twist
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{{\mathbb{R}}}}\wedge{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{C}}}

with respect to inversion,
which is isomorphic to the constant group scheme
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/n{{\mathbb{Z}}}

over
{{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Its embedding into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{R}}}}

is
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}})
-conjugate to

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{{\mathbb{Z}}}/n{{\mathbb{Z}}}&\to&{\mathbf{SL}}_{n,{{\mathbb{R}}}}\\
1&\mapsto&\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&\zeta_{n}+\zeta_{n}^{-1}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

and the associated quotient singularity is an RDP
of type
B_{\beta(n)}
over
{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

This can be proven along the lines of Proposition
9.8

and we leave this to the reader.

10.
Allowing primes of bad reduction

In this section, we enhance the setup of the previous section by
allowing primes of bad reduction.
Let
\Sigma\subset{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
be a finite, possibly empty, subset of prime
ideals.
Then,
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}={\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}\setminus\Sigma\subseteq{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

is a Zariski open affine subset, where
{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}
denotes the
localization of
{\cal O}_{K}
away from
\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma=\emptyset
, then we recover the setup of the previous section.

Example 10.1
.

Let
d\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}\setminus\{0,1\}
be a squarefree integer.
Let
K={{\mathbb{Q}}}
, let
L={{\mathbb{Q}}}(\sqrt{d})
, let
{\cal O}_{L}\subset L
be the ring of integers,
and let
D\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the discriminant of
K\subset L
.
We have
D=d
with
{\cal O}_{L}={{\mathbb{Z}}}[\sqrt{d}]
if
d\not\equiv 1\mod 4

or
D=4d
with
{{\mathbb{Z}}}[\frac{1}{2}(1+\sqrt{d})]
if
d\equiv 1\mod 4
.
Then,

f\quad:\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{L}\quad\to\quad{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\,=\,{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}

is a finite flat morphism of degree 2.
It is étale of degree
2
over the Zariski open set

{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}={\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}\setminus\Sigma
,
where
\Sigma
is the finite set of primes dividing
D
.
We obtain a
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}
-torsor over
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}

and for every
n\geq 3
a closed subgroup scheme

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}}\wedge f^{-1}({\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma})\quad\subset\quad{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}},

which is fppf locally conjugate to the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{{{\mathbb{Q}}},\Sigma}}
.
Since the subgroup scheme
is not isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
,
its embedding into
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}

is not
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
-conjugate to
the standard embedding

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
.
By Theorem
9.5
,

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{{\mathbb{Z}}})
is a singleton and thus, the above
subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}

cannot be extended to a finite, flat, and linearly reductive
subgroup scheme of
{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
.

10.1.
At least one prime over
\mathbf{(2)}
with good reduction

We have the following straight forward
generalization of Theorem
9.5
.

Theorem 10.2
.

Let
K
be a number field with ring of integers
{\cal O}_{K}
.
Let
\Sigma\subset{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
be a finite subset
that does not contain all primes lying over
(2)\in{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}

and let
{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}
be the localization away from
\Sigma
.

(1)

For each integer
n\geq 2
, the set
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})

consists of
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
-conjugacy classes of
subgroup schemes
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}

that are locally in the fppf topology conjugate to
the standard
embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
.

(2)

{\mathfrak{Klein}}(2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
is a singleton consisting
of the
{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
-conjugacy class of the standard
embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
.

(3)

If
n\geq 3
, then
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
is a finite
set and its cardinality is independent of
n
.

Proof.

(1) follows from Theorem
6.1
.

(2) follows from (1) and Corollary
7.2
.

(3) Independence of
n
for
n\geq 3
follows from Corollary
7.4
.
Finiteness can be shown along the lines of the proof of
Theorem
9.5
.(2) and follows from the finiteness of

{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma},{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}))
, for which we use
the short exact sequence (
13
):

A non-trivial class in
{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})

can be represented by a quadratic field extension
K\subset L

that is ramified at most in
\Sigma
.
By definition, we have
{{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset K
, which is of some degree

d:=[K:{{\mathbb{Q}}}]
and ramified in some finite subset
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Enlarging
\Sigma^{\prime}
by the finite set of primes lying under
\Sigma\subset{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}
,
we find that
{{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset L
is an extension of degree
2d
that is
ramified at most in
\Sigma^{\prime}
.
The set of such extensions of
{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is finite by Hermite’s theorem, see,
for example,
[
La94
, Chapter V,§4, Corollary to Theorem 5]
.
This implies finiteness of
{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})
.
(Alternatively, this can also be shown via finiteness of homomorphisms

{\rm Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}\to{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where
{\rm Cl}_{K}^{\mathfrak{m}}
is the ray class group
for a suitable modulus
{\mathfrak{m}}
that depends on
\Sigma
.)

The fiber over some class
\eta\in{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\cal O}_{K,\Sigma},{{\mathbb{Z}}}/2{{\mathbb{Z}}})
in
(
13
) is a quotient of

{\rm Cl}({\cal O}_{K^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime}})^{\oplus 2}
,
where
K^{\prime}
is equal to
K
or to a quadratic extension of
K
.
Since such class groups are finite, so is the fiber over
\eta
.
∎

10.2.
No restriction on primes over
\mathbf{(2)}

The situation becomes considerably more difficult when trying to extend the results of
the previous section to
{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}
in the case where
\Sigma

contains all primes lying over
(2)\in{\rm Spec}\>{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Depending on
\Sigma
,
the other group schemes of Theorem
3.2
will show up.
Thus, if
G\in{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})
, then it may
happen that
G
is not a twisted form of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K}}
,
that is, Theorem
6.1
is not applicable.

For example, if
\Sigma
contains all primes lying over
(2)
,
then next to the standard embedding
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}

also group subschemes
G\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
will show up
that are fppf locally conjugate to the
binary dihedral group scheme

{\mathbf{BD}}_{n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}\subset{\mathbf{SL}}_{2,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}}
,
which is a finite flat linearly reductive group scheme
of length
4n
over
{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma}
.
To describe such
G
, one has to compute
the normalizer
{\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbf{GL}}_{2}}({\mathbf{BD}}_{n})
and one has to
understand its first cohomology, see Theorem
5.2
,
which we have done over fields in
[
LS25
]
.
One will eventually run into the problem of describing
and controlling
{H_{\rm fl}^{{1}}}({\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma},H)

for some finite and non-abelian group
H
scheme,
which cannot be done so elegantly using class field theory
as in the
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
-case.
This complicated matters a lot, but one can probably
still establish finiteness of
{\mathfrak{Klein}}(n,{\cal O}_{K,\Sigma})

for every finite subset
\Sigma\subset{\rm Spec}\>{\cal O}_{K}

using Hermite’s theorem.

Challenges of providing of special care services in hospitals during emergencies and disasters: a scoping review
Authors: Mohammad Masbi, Nader Tavakoli, Mohsen Dowlati
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe ability of hospitals to provide special care services for critically ill or injured patients during emergencies and disasters poses very significant challenges that necessitate the response in-place plans. the increasing frequency of such events, coupled with limited hospital resources and increasing patient volumes, underscores the urgency of addressing these issues. Specifically, this study aims to identify the challenges faced by hospitals in providing special services during disasters.MethodThis scoping review, conducted in 2024, based upon the framework developed by Arksey and O’Malley. An extensive search of Literature searching was performed using gray literature and databases including Google Scholar, PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus. Applying the selection criteria, a total of 33 relevant studies were identified that discuss the challenges faced when providing special care services in hospitals during emergencies and disasters. Results are presented in accordance with the PRISMA checklist to ensure quality and transparency. Data was analyzed through a systematic coding process where common themes across the studies were identified.ResultsThis review identified 15 distinct challenges associated with providing special care services in hospitals during emergencies and disasters: limited resources, inadequate infrastructure, lack of pre-hospital care, financial constraints, failures in the emergency response system, triage, deficiencies in management structure, planning, and preparation, communication and coordination deficits, training and protocols development, employee welfare challenges, challenges of continuity of care, increased demand, different and complex needs of patients, ethical challenges, imbalance in distribution of service resources.ConclusionsResponse in a hospital to the needs of special care during a disaster has to be responded to with more effective preparedness through comprehensive disaster preparedness plans, better communication and coordination, and training of staff and allocation of resources. In particular, standardized triage protocols and ethical frameworks will provide the foundation for the optimization of resource use in emergency responses. Better communication among departments and also with other outside organizations will improve how resources are used to better achieve good patient outcomes within emergency responses.Keywords: Special care services, Hospitals, Challenges, Patients, Disasters

Introduction

Disasters and emergences pose significant threats to communities, causing extensive infrastructural damage and resulting in loss of life, disabilities, and societal disruption. The United Nations International Strategy for Disaster Reduction (UNISDR) defines disasters as events that cause widespread disruption within a community, exceeding its capacity to cope with the situation using its own resources [1–3]. In 2023, the International Database of Disasters (EM-DAT) reported approximately 399 disasters, resulting in 86,473 deaths and affected more than 93.1 million people [4].

In such critical situations, the delivery of health services becomes the major concern for the affected societies. Effective, timely, and proper care has to be provided in order to prevent unnecessary deaths and long-term complications [5, 6]. This expected increase in the number of patients during disasters means that hospitals should be fully prepared to handle the increased demand for special medical care apart from providing safe environments for the victims [7, 8].

The Special Care Unit is one of the most important units within healthcare facilities, charged with the responsibility of managing patients needing special health intervention. This unit should be well prepared to handle the number of patients that result from disaster situations, hence preventing unnecessary deaths in the affected populations. Special care services, however can be very challenging to provide during disaster periods due to sudden surges in patient numbers. Strategic planning of such care services is therefore essential [9–11]. Special care involves a multidisciplinary approach directed at the comprehensive management of patients at risk of developing acute, life-threatening illnesses. These services may be outside the walls of the hospital and include emergency departments and outdoor emergency sites. It relies on advanced technology to support the function of vital organ systems, such as respiratory and cardiovascular systems [12–14].

This will require an increase in special care capacity by hospitals with regard to bed availability, staffing, and access to special equipment, since the want thereof may otherwise lead to delays in patient care and increased morbidity and mortality rates [15, 16].

Given the increased number of natural disasters and public health emergencies, especially in the 21st century, the Hospitals have to implement plans to make sure that the medical and special care services are availed continuously. This will involve resource optimization, effective triage systems, provision for workers’ safety, and above all, training and communication in order to respond to any emergency easily [17].

Recent studies have inferred that during disasters, a massive increase in the number of patients who require special care is often associated with compromised service qualities, increased rates of mortality, and long-term health complications. The reports from COVID-19 pandemic showed corresponding challenges where special care units are often unable to maintain standards of care while under stress; this amplifies morbidity and mortality among patients [18–20]. Previous literature indicates that hospitals face a numerous of obstacles, such as inadequate intensive care beds, poor training in disaster management, and lack of comprehensive response plans all of which combine to give inadequate medical care in times of disasters [21–23]. Given the intrinsic challenges presented by such conditions, it is recommendable to have strategic planning in several levels-local, national, and international. This will ensure that we are better equipped to provide special services and critical care in times of disaster and thus be able to save more lives affected by such situations. Poor preparedness, as manifested by the lack of basic patient equipment for those affected by trauma-related conditions, greatly hinders a successful response to disasters [21, 24–26].

This study aims to identify specific challenges of providing special care services in hospitals in emergencies and disasters.

Method

In this scoping review, conducted in 2024, a comprehensive protocol was developed by the researchers to guide the literature search, selection of publications, data extraction, and descriptive analysis of the results. The framework of Arksey and O’Malley was utilized as a guide in developing the methodology [27]. To ensure the quality and transparency throughout the process, the results of this research are presented based on the PRISMA checklist [28]. (See Fig. 1). All relevant studies were identified through systematic searches across selected databases. The selection process involved screening titles and abstracts for relevance, followed by a full-text review of potentially eligible studies.

Search strategy

To systematically address the research question, a comprehensive list of search terms was created for each relevant concept, enhancing specificity and completeness. The search strategy involves three main bibliographic databases—Scopus, PubMed, and Web of Science—known for their prominence in medical and therapeutic literature, as well as the Google Scholar search engine A complete manual search of gray literature reference lists of all eligible studies was conducted to discover additional relevant publications (Table 1).

Eligibility assessment

The qualification and validity of the articles were evaluated to ensure the quality and accuracy of the extracted information. The authors emphasized articles published in reputable journals, credibility of the publication source, those employing accurate and valid methodologies, and the relevance of the research focus to the review question, reliable citations, and higher citation counts. Based on these criteria [33] articles were selected. Following detailed evaluation, these articles were finalized for inclusion in this scoping review.

Data extraction and synthesis of results

The extracted data included the following variables: study title, purpose, type of study, year of publication, country of study, and key findings related to the challenges of providing special care services during various disasters and emergencies. The main focus of these studies was to provide clear evidence regarding the challenges of delivering special care services to patients in hospitals during emergencies and disasters. Based on keywords and identification of reliable sources, articles were extracted. Upon final approval, all dimensions of the study- including the type of special care services, the countries providing these services, the provision of these services in hospitals during disasters, and the type of disasters, were reviewed and validated.

Results

Summary of search

The database search retrieved 3,089 records. An additional 8 references were identified through supplemental search strategies following the removal of duplicates, 2,834 titles and abstracts were screened, resulting in 61 full-text records for detailed review. Ultimately, 33 articles met the inclusion criteria for the final review. (Fig. 1)

Characteristics of the study

The included studies originated from events in 13 countries: 11 from the USA, 3 each from England, Israel, and Africa (Kenya and Tanzania), 2 each from India, Iran, Nigeria, and Japan, and 1 each from Afghanistan, Haiti, Italy, and Germany. The studies, published between 2004 and the present, encompassed a variety of research designs : descriptive (n = 3); qualitative (n = 3); cross-sectional (n = 1); review (consensus statement (n:1); retrospective cohort study (n:1); narrative review (n:1); conventional content analysis(n:1); review (descriptive) (n:1) ); retrospective review (n:3); conceptual analysis (n:1); Delphi study (n:3); modeling study (n:1); analytical and theoretical study (n:1); qualitative - descriptive study (n:1); descriptive and analytical study (n:1); descriptive and empirical study (n:1); descriptive and cross-sectional study (n:1); review article(n:2); retrospective analysis(n:1); expletory qualitative study article(n:1); special study(qualitative case study (n:1); special review (n:1) ); policy paper(n:1); commentary or position paper (n:1)(See Table 2).

Types of challenges described

Limited resources

In this scoping review, 31 out of the 33 included studies identified limited resources including human resources, equipment, systems, and physical space as a significant challenge in delivering special care services during emergencies and disasters. Special care units frequently contend with inadequate facilities and a lack of trained personnel, challenges that are critically exacerbated in disaster scenarios. For instance, during Hurricane Katrina, many hospitals struggled to reallocate staff and equipment to meet the surge of critically ill patients, leading to increased morbidity and mortality rates [29–59].

Inadequate infrastructure

Seven studies reported that inadequate infrastructure significantly hampers the provision of special care services during emergencies. Poorly designed or outdated hospital facilities can delay timely care for critically ill patients and complicate the maintenance and operation of existing medical resources. Tests conducted in regions affected by natural disasters have shown that hospitals without proper infrastructure faced substantial delays, impacting patient outcomes [29, 32, 42, 51–54].

Lack of pre-hospital care

Five studies highlighted the lack of pre-hospital emergency care as a barrier to effective management of critically ill patients requiring special care before hospital admission. For instance, in many rural areas during a disaster, the absence of trained emergency medical personnel results in delays that jeopardize patient survival [29, 33, 50, 52, 60].

Financial constraints

Four studies indicated that financial limitations hinder the provision of adequate special care services during emergencies and disasters. The financial obligations of hospitals during crises often exceed available resources. For example, many hospitals in low-resource settings reported that ongoing operational demands limited their capacity for enhanced emergency readiness, adversely affecting their ability to serve critically ill patients during disasters [29, 46, 53, 61].

Failure in emergency response systems

Two studies identified failures in emergency response systems as challenges in delivering special care services to patients in the course of emergencies and disasters. The critical point is that effective trauma care systems, including emergency and intensive care hospital management, critically ill patients’ outcomes in emergencies and disasters cannot improved Efficient emergency response systems are essential for coordinating special care [29, 33].

Triage challenges

Nine studies highlighted triage challenges as a significant issue in providing special care services during emergencies and disasters. These studies that the triage process often struggles to balance utilitarian and egalitarian ethical considerations, necessitating the prioritization of some patients over others. Poor on-site case management can lead to the deterioration of patients’ conditions before they reach healthcare facilities [30, 31, 35, 37, 39, 49–52, 60].

Deficiencies in management structure, planning, and preparation

15 studies identified deficiencies in management structure, planning, and preparedness as critical barriers to the effective delivery of special care during emergencies and disasters, which have been often cited in previous studies, extensive healthcare disaster preparedness is uncommon. Healthcare disaster preparedness is commonly seen as inadequate, with few systems having comprehensive disaster plans in place. As a result, chaos often ensues during actual disasters. Effective collaboration between providers advocating for specific populations and government entities becomes even more essential during crises. A paradigm shift is needed in how emergency disaster responses are conceptualized, emphasizing the need to provide care to as many affected individuals as possible rather than striving for comprehensive care for every individual [30–32, 34, 35, 37, 39, 42, 43, 55, 56, 58–61].

Communication and coordination deficits

11 studies indicated that inadequate communication and coordination among healthcare providers present significant barriers to delivering care during emergencies and disasters. Effective communication is crucial in healthcare settings; however, it can become tremendously challenging during disasters. Poor internal communication and a lack of efficient liaison with external entities hinder effective coordination and preparation for potential needs. Transitioning patients to other facilities becomes particularly problematic when multiple victims and hospitals are affected [30, 33, 34, 36, 42, 46, 49, 52–55].

Training and protocols development

Eight studies raised the challenges associated with training and protocol development as key issues in providing special care services during emergencies and disasters. The need for intensive training and the establishment of standardized treatment protocols is critical to maintaining care quality in disaster situations and to initiate and establish them quickly. The surge in demand for intensive care puts immense pressure on existing staff and systems, complicating the rapid training and upskilling required [30–32, 50–52, 54, 55].

Employee welfare challenges

Eight studies discussed employee welfare challenges as a critical issue in providing special care services during emergencies and disasters. Medical personnel often experience physical and mental exhaustion due to extended working hours and insufficient support, which reduces their efficiency. Furthermore, the stress of a disaster’s impact on their own safety and that of their families can exacerbate anxiety and negatively affect their performance [32, 33, 35, 36, 40, 44, 47, 48, 55].

Challenges of continuity of care

Two studies raised concerns about the challenges of maintaining continuity of care for critically injured patients during transfers between units or facilities in emergency situations. Ensuring seamless transitions presents significant barriers, including the need for safe discharge programs and ongoing monitoring for patients who can be treated in less intensive settings [35, 41].

Increase in demand

16 studies identified an increase in demand for services as a major challenge when providing special care during emergencies and disasters. A sudden influx of injured and critically ill patients requiring intensive care can strain existing resources significantly. This surge can challenge intensive care units (ICUs) to meet patient needs, creating a disparity between demand and available resources [36, 39, 40, 42–49, 54, 55, 57, 58, 60].

Different and complex needs of patients

Seven studies raised the different and complex needs of patients as a significant challenge in providing special care services to patients during emergencies and disasters. Patients often present with a combination of injuries that complicates their diagnosis and treatment. In emergency situations, the number of patients requiring special care often exceeds the capacity and resources available in healthcare facilities. Patients with complex medical conditions frequently require a wide range of special interventions that may not be manageable in disaster settings [36, 40, 41, 44, 45, 52, 53].

Ethical challenges

Nine studies identified ethical challenges arising during emergencies and disasters as a significant concern in providing special care services. Ethical dilemmas often surface regarding the allocation of limited resources. Special populations should ideally be included in general triage and resource allocation strategies; however, their unique needs can complicate decision-making. Ethical considerations regarding resource distribution and prioritization of care pose challenges, especially when resources are scarce and a high volume of patients requires immediate attention. Such complexities can lead to moral distress among healthcare providers [37, 38, 47, 49, 51–53, 56, 58].

Imbalance in the distribution of service resources

Five studies noted an imbalance in the distribution of intensive care resources during emergencies. Areas hardest hit by disasters often receive insufficient support, leading to inequitable healthcare access. Larger hospitals typically possess better resources compared to smaller facilities, exacerbating disparities in care delivery [41, 43–46].

Discussion

This scoping review synthesized findings from 33 studies, revealing 15 distinct challenges associated with the provision of special care services specifically critical and intensive care to patients in hospitals during disasters and emergencies. For clarity, the term “special care services” in this review encompasses critical and intensive care units (ICUs), and other special medical support meant for patients with serious, acute conditions that necessitate advanced medical intervention.

Among the identified challenges include “limited resources” (financial, human, and material), “inadequate infrastructure”, “failures in emergency response systems”, and “Deficiencies in management structure” such as poor planning and communication. These challenges severely compromise the ability of healthcare facilities to deliver timely and effective care. For instance, during the COVID-19 pandemic, hospitals across various income contexts, including high-income countries, faced overwhelming demands that exceeded their resource availability, illustrating that these issues are not confined to low- and middle-income countries. The inability to allocate sufficient resources, ranging from equipment to trained personnel, severely impacts the capacity to provide urgent care. For instance, studies have documented instances where ICUs overwhelmed by disaster victims had to operate beyond their designed patient capacity, thereby compromising care quality. Hospitals must develop and regularly update disaster preparedness plans that include response scenarios based on previous events. The literature underscores the importance of these plans in ensuring readiness for surges in patient volume [62–64].

Literature has identified the devastating impact of sudden surges in the number of patients on ICUs; one of these is resource allocation and triage, leading to poor triage practices and raising ethical concern about prioritization. More drastic measures by hospitals under pressure may include discharging stable patients or transferring individuals to less-special facilities. This may compromise the care for patients with complex needs requiring special monitoring and intervention. Contemporary models, such as the “Surge Capacity Model, call for medical facilities to increase their surge management capacities through flexible staffing strategies and identification of alternative health care facilities. The development of institutional policies in consonance with this model can substantially help alleviate the challenges in resource allocation during disasters. Decision-making on triage during disaster times may involve complicated ethical problems, more so under conditions with limitations of resources. Also, literature indicates that clear triage protocols based on ethical frameworks can relieve these dilemmas, but most hospitals do not have such guidelines [42, 65–69].

Poor infrastructure, as highlighted by the aforementioned studies, impairs the provision of quality healthcare, as poor transportation and communication networks could exacerbate patients’ conditions before they seek hospital care. Additionally, the lack of organized pre-hospital care systems often leads to poor initial management of critically ill patients, a factor that significantly influences increased survival rates. Investment in country-wide pre-hospital training programs and enhanced inter-agency communication would alleviate the aforementioned infrastructural challenges. The disruption of organized prehospital care can cause critical delays in important medical interventions. The evidence suggests that the lack of an organized prehospital emergency response and hospital interface may endanger the safety of injured patients, potentially contributing to higher morbidity and mortality. Robust prehospital networks can be created to improve early patient care and ease access to appropriate specialty health centers through facilitated transfer. The paramedics must train in collaboration with their emergency departments and specialty care counterparts [36, 70–72].

Effective disaster response hinges on seamless coordination between hospital units and external organizations. Studies have repeatedly shown that hospitals lacking comprehensive disaster preparedness plans struggle with managing emergencies Developing detailed and actionable disaster management protocols, alongside conducting regular simulation exercises, can enhance intra- and inter-sectoral collaboration and ensure that hospitals are equipped to respond effectively to disasters. Effective disaster response is contingent upon seamless communication between healthcare teams and external organizations. Studies indicate that breakdowns in communication can exacerbate patient outcomes during emergencies. Hospitals should implement advanced communication tools and protocols to ensure effective information exchange among all stakeholders. This can mitigate the fragmentation of care during emergencies [42, 66, 73].

The ethical implications of resource allocation during disasters are profound. Triage systems must consider distinct patient populations, especially vulnerable groups like children, and patients with complex medical conditions. Ensuring an equitable distribution of resources can reduce disparities and improve health outcomes across demographic boundaries. Implementing a clear framework for ethical decision-making in triage that complies with legal guidelines can help navigate these dilemmas head-on. The allocation of limited resources presents ethical challenges, with decision-makers needing to balance institutional capability against patient needs. The literature emphasizes the significance of incorporating ethical principles in resource allocation during emergencies [65, 74–77].

Rapid training of personnel in order to foster resilience during emergency situations is very essential. Continuous professional development programs based on emergency care situations can help in the equipage of healthcare professionals with the competencies that enable them to adapt in situations of crisis. Also, a culture of preparedness and resilience through community engagement ensures that the health systems remain responsive and adaptive. Systematic and well-structured training programs are needed to prepare healthcare professionals to act effectively in emergencies. Training programs that use simulation methods and incorporate disaster-related scenarios have shown a marked improvement in team preparedness [71, 78].

Disasters interrupt the continuum of care for patients undergoing treatment, especially those with chronic diseases. Without an organized system addressing continuous care, there will be dire consequences for their conditions during a disaster. Research Studies have shown that in disaster situations, in order to make resources available to more people who needed special care services, medical staff could discharge patients according to their medical conditions and transfer those who had a greater chance of surviving to other facilities or could discharge them from the hospital. However, patients with specified medical conditions should not be discharged from the Intensive Care Unit. Moreover, the capacity of intensive care units should be augmented using alternative sites and non-special care personnel under the supervision of trained staff [65, 79–81].

Also, the psychological and emotional toll on healthcare workers, compounded by high-stress environments during disasters, can hinder effective care delivery. Recognizing and addressing employee welfare is essential for sustaining healthcare delivery during crises [82].

Consequently, each hospital must develop comprehensive and integrated preparedness plans to respond appropriately during disasters and emergencies. Standardized triage plans should be developed and updated regularly since triage decisions are a priority in the care of patients during disasters; the plans should be designed to promote quick and effective decision-making. Improvement in the supply chain for medical equipment and pharmaceuticals through strategic stockpiling with collaboration with various suppliers must be done to ensure availability of essential resources at the time of crisis.

Hospital staff should engage in regular training that simulates real-life scenarios and potential challenges, allowing them to develop their capacities in disaster response and the delivery of emergency care. Effective coordination and cooperation among different departments within the hospital, as well as with entities outside the hospital, must be established to ensure effective response to disasters.

Spatial attention must be given to specific needs of patients, especially pediatrics and particular medical conditions, in the development of triage strategies and resource allocation. Furthermore, the development of the legal and ethical frameworks will enable the fair allocation of resources and decision-making with respect to special care in disaster scenarios with respect to patients with a poor prognosis. Engaging the community in disaster preparedness and response programs helps to ensure the equitable distribution of resources and increases access to health services.

In all, the delivery of special care services, in a disaster scenario, has a numerous of challenges that require specific strategies to improve preparedness and response capabilities. The health care system can alleviate the impacts of disasters on patient care through the institution of multidisciplinary approaches that include training curriculums, models for ethical decision-making, and effective communication practices. Also, constant research and collaboration between health care professionals, policymakers, and members of the community will be very instrumental in improving disaster preparedness and ensuring equal access to care.

Strengths and limitations

This research examines the multifaceted challenges encountered by hospitals in providing special care services, utilizing a broad spectrum of thorough investigations conducted across several nations. This variety contributes to a more nuanced comprehension of the difficulties faced within diverse healthcare environments. Moreover, the implementation of stringent protocols for literature review, data acquisition, and analysis significantly bolsters the credibility and dependability of the results.

In this way, the current research increases the awareness of systemic challenges within healthcare systems during disasters. The gained knowledge might help in improving planning and preparedness and, consequently, increase the possibility of improving hospitals’ capacity in offering services during emergencies and crises.

However, there are some limitations to this study. While the challenges have been well delineated, many of the studies reviewed are qualitative. This may reduce the potential to generalize quantitative and measurable outcomes from the findings. Some of those challenges are perhaps context-bound, related to specific geographical or economic conditions, and thus generalization of the findings to other countries might not be feasible. In addition, certain articles were excluded due to the lack of access to full texts or their publication in languages other than English, which might have resulted in a narrower perspective on the problems that are faced worldwide.

Recommendations

Considering the challenges found in this study and its limitations, it will be appropriate that future research concentrate on quantitative studies that should seek to collect statistical data and make comparative analyses with greater precision on the impact of identified challenges on quality special care services and patient outcomes in a hospital setting facing disaster scenarios. Future studies should focus on more specific challenges, especially ethical dilemmas, triage processes, and resource management, with the view of giving more practical and holistic solutions. It would also be very important to have comparative studies across countries or regions to derive best practices or successful strategies regarding the management of special care services during emergencies.

Conclusion

This study investigated the challenges associated with providing special care services to patients in hospitals during disaster and emergences. A literature review of 33 related articles recognized 15 principal challenges. Key challenges include: limited resources, inadequate infrastructure, triage challenges, and planning and management deficiencies. These results are of immense importance to policy makers and stakeholders as they offer constructive knowledge expected to improve preparedness for and response to disasters and emergencies in the hospitals. Recommended solutions to such challenges might include better disaster preparedness planning, training, and empowering of health care personnel, improvement in communication and coordination between organizations, and the development of standardized protocols. This would definitely improve the survival chances of critically injured patients in disasters and reduce the long-term complications developed from such events.

These gaps of course bring into light the compelling need for further research in this area. Such future research efforts may enable comparative studies between different regions or countries with the aim of identifying best practices and efficient strategies of providing specialty care during crises, as well as longitudinal research to determine long-term effects of disasters on health care systems and the outcomes for patients. Better Preparedness Strategies discusses how emerging technologies can enhance disaster response and patient care in hospitals; triaging and resource allocation issues during disasters; and beyond providing a framework for ethical decision-making in emergencies.

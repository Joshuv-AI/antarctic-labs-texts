A New SMP Transformed Standard Weibull Distribution for Health Data Modelling.
Isqeel Ogunsola, Nurudeen Ajadi, Gboyega Adepoju

Abstract
New methods of extending base distributions are always invoke to increase their adaptability in modeling real life data. Recently, SMP method was introduced but Weibull distribution is yet to be explored through this method. First, we provide updated review on SMP transformed distributions. We then proposed and developed another extended Weibull distribution through this technique named SMPtW. Importantly, twelve of its statistical properties - reliability measures, quantile function, moment, stress-strength, mean waiting time, moment generating function, characteristics function, renyi entropy, order statistics, mean residual life and mode, were derived and studied extensively. The hazard function has a decreasing, increasing and constant shapes. We found a relation between the quantile of SMPtW and that of SMP Pareto distribution despite their difference in density functions. We adopt the inverse transform approach in random number generation and through simulation we evaluate maximum likelihood estimates (MLE) performance of its parameters. The result showed that MLE is consistent all through. The performance of the distribution was then examined using health dataset compared with five similar distributions. The results showed that three parameters SMPtW performed best among the competing models.

Theorem 1
.

If

Q(u)=\bigg[log\bigg(\dfrac{log\lambda}{log\{u(1-\lambda)+\lambda\}}\bigg)\bigg]^{1/\phi},\hskip 14.22636pt0<u<1

Let

Implies that

u(1-\lambda)+\lambda=e^{(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}}

Q(u)=\bigg[log\bigg(\dfrac{log\lambda}{log\{u(1-\lambda)+\lambda\}}\bigg)\bigg]^{1/\phi},\hskip 14.22636pt0<u<1

Moment

Theorem 2
.

Suppose

E(y^{r})=\dfrac{log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{r}{\phi}+1\right)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(log\lambda)^{j}}{j!(j+1)^{r/\phi+1}}

Proof.

The
r^{th}
of a random variable is defined by

\mu_{r}=E(y^{r})=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r}.f(y,\lambda)dy

E(y^{r})=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r}.\dfrac{e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}(log\lambda)\phi y^{\phi-1}}{\lambda-1}dy

=\dfrac{\phi log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r+\phi-1}e^{-y^{\phi}}e^{{\left(\log\lambda\right)}e^{-y^{\phi}}}dy\\

Let
t=y^{\phi}
, so we have that
y=t^{1/\phi}
,
dy=\dfrac{1}{\phi}t^{\frac{1}{\phi}-1}
dt.

=\dfrac{\phi log\lambda}{\lambda-1}.\dfrac{1}{\phi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r/\phi}e^{-t+(log\lambda)e^{-t}}dt\\

=\dfrac{log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r/\phi}e^{-t}.e^{(log\lambda)e^{-t}}dt

Using the power series,

e^{(log\lambda)e^{-t}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(log\lambda)^{j}}{j!}e^{-jt}

E(y^{r})=\dfrac{log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(log\lambda)^{j}}{j!}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{r/\phi}e^{-(j+1)t}dt

4.6
Mode of SMPtW

Given a random variable
Y
which follows, SMPtW distribution, the mode of the distribution is the value of

\frac{d}{dx}f(y)=0

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}f(y)<0.
However, given the positive nature of the PDF, the mode can also be obtained by finding the value of

for which the log of the PDF derivative equals zero i.e.
\frac{d}{dy}logf(y)=0

\displaystyle Logf(y;\lambda,\phi)

\displaystyle=log\left(\dfrac{e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}+(log\lambda)\phi y^{\phi-1}}{\lambda-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}+log(log\lambda)+log(\phi)+(\phi-1)log(y)-log(\lambda-1)

Now, differentiating with respect to y, we have;

\displaystyle\frac{dLogf(y;\lambda,\phi)}{dy}

\displaystyle=-(log\lambda)\phi y^{\phi-1}e^{-y^{\phi}}-\phi y^{\phi-1}+(\phi-1)/y

Equating to zero , we have;

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-(log\lambda)\phi y^{\phi-1}e^{-y^{\phi}}-\phi y^{\phi-1}+(\phi-1)/y

\displaystyle\phi-1

\displaystyle=\phi y^{\phi}\left[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}+1\right]

y=\left[\frac{\phi-1}{\phi[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}+1]}\right]^{1/\phi}

(13)

Remark 2:
The equation

is not in a close form and can be obtained numerically. It is to be noted that if

, and for

4.9
Stress-Strength Reliability

Suppose that
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are continuous random variables where
Y_{1}\sim SMPtW(\lambda_{1},\phi_{1})
and
Y_{2}\sim SMPtW(\lambda_{2},\phi_{2}),
then the stress-strength parameter, say R, is defined as

R=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{1}(y)F_{2}(y)dy.

For instance, suppose
Y_{1}
represents the strength of a component independent of
Y_{2}
the stress of the component, if
Y_{1}<Y_{2},
then either the component fails or the systems that uses the component may malfuntion. Hence, reliability can be expressed as the probability that
Y_{1}>Y_{2}.
That is,

R=P(Y_{1}>Y_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{1}(y)F_{2}(y)dy.

Now, using the CDF and the pdf of SMPtW in eqn
3
and
4
, we obtain

R=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{e^{log_{\lambda_{1}}e^{-y^{\phi_{1}}}}log(\lambda_{1})*\phi_{1}e^{-y^{\phi_{1}}}y^{\phi-1}}{\lambda_{1}-1}\bigg)\bigg(\dfrac{e^{log(\lambda_{2})e^{-y^{\phi_{2}}}}-\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\bigg)dy

R=\dfrac{log(\lambda_{1})\phi_{1}}{(\lambda_{1}-1)(1-\lambda_{2})}\bigg[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{[log(\lambda_{1})e^{-y^{\phi_{1}}}-y^{\phi_{1}}+log(\lambda_{2})e^{-y^{\phi_{2}}}]}y^{\phi_{1}-1}dy-\lambda_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{[log(\lambda_{1})e^{-y^{\phi_{1}}}-y^{\phi_{1}}]}y^{\phi_{1}-1}dy\bigg]

(16)

The stress-strength reliability above is not in a closed form if the shape parameters
\phi_{1}\neq\phi_{2}.
and thus, must be evaluated numerically. However, if the shape parameters
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2},
we have a closed form and the Stress-Strength Reliability

R=\dfrac{log(\lambda_{1})}{(\lambda_{1}-1)(1-\lambda_{2})}\bigg[\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-1}{log(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})}-\lambda_{2}\dfrac{\lambda_{1}-1}{log(\lambda_{1})}\bigg]

(17)

The reliability above is obtained by setting
t=y^{\phi_{1}}
in equation
16
.

Alternatively, using series expansion, it can be shown that

R=\dfrac{log(\lambda_{1})}{(\lambda_{1}-1)(1-\lambda_{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(log\lambda_{1})^{n}}{n!(n+1)}\bigg[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(log\lambda_{2})^{m}}{m!}\dfrac{(-n)^{k}}{(m+1)^{k+1}}-\lambda_{2}\bigg]

(18)

f_{(j:n)}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{n!\phi(log\lambda)y^{\phi-1}\left[1-\lambda\right]^{1-n}}{(\lambda-1)(j-1)!(n-j)!}[e^{(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}}-\lambda]^{j-1}[1-e^{(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}}]^{n-j}e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}

(20)

We can then obtain the first and nth order statistics using equation
20
. Consequently, the first and nth order statistics are given in equations
21
and
22
respectively.

f_{(1:n)}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{n\phi(log\lambda)y^{\phi-1}\left(1-\lambda\right)^{1-n}}{(\lambda-1)}\left[1-e^{(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}}\right]^{n-1}\left[e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}\right]

(21)

f_{(n:n)}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{n\phi(log\lambda)y^{\phi-1}\left[1-\lambda\right]^{1-n}}{(\lambda-1)}\left[e^{(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}}-\lambda\right]^{n-1}\left[e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}\right]

(22)

4.11
Renyi Entropy

In this section , we derived the Renyi entropy denoted as
E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)
given by

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{1}{1-h}log\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{\text{SMPtW}}(y;\lambda,\phi)^{h}dy\right);\quad\quad h>0,h\neq 0

(23)

Theorem 7
.

The Renyi Entropy of a random variable which follows SMPtW distribution is given as

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\left(\frac{(\log\lambda)\phi}{\lambda-1}\right)^{h}\frac{2}{\phi}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}\Big)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(h\log\lambda)^{j}}{j!}(h+j)^{-\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}}

Proof.

Using equations
4
and
23
we have;

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{1}{1-h}log\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}(log\lambda)\phi y^{\phi-1}}{\lambda-1}\right]^{h}dy\right)

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{1}{1-h}log\left({\left[\frac{\phi log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\right]}^{h}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{h(\phi-1)}e^{-hy^{\phi}}e^{{h\left(\log\lambda\right)}e^{-y^{\phi}}}dy\right)

Now, using Taylor series, we have:

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{1}{1-h}log\left({\left[\frac{\phi log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\right]}^{h}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{h(\phi-1)}e^{-hy^{\phi}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(hlog\lambda)^{j}}{j!}e^{-jy^{\phi}}dy\right)

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\frac{1}{1-h}log\left({\left[\frac{\phi log\lambda}{\lambda-1}\right]}^{h}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(hlog\lambda)^{j}}{j!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{h(\phi-1)}e^{-(h-j)y^{\phi}}dy\right)

Using the general gamma function formula given by

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{(\phi-1)h}e^{-(h+j)y^{\phi}}\,dy

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{(\phi-1)h}e^{-(h+j)y^{\phi}}\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\phi}(h+j)^{-\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}\Big)

Hence,

E_{r}(y;\lambda,\phi)=\left(\frac{(\log\lambda)\phi}{\lambda-1}\right)^{h}\frac{2}{\phi}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}\Big)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(h\log\lambda)^{j}}{j!}(h+j)^{-\frac{\phi h-h+1}{\phi}}

(24)

5
Maximum Likelihood Estimation

The likelihood function for the SMPtW distribution is

L(\theta|\bm{y})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(y_{i}|\lambda,\phi)

L=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{e^{[(log\lambda)e^{-y_{i}^{\phi}}-y^{\phi}]}(log\lambda)\phi y_{i}^{\phi-1}}{\lambda-1}

The log likelihood function is given by;

Q=logL=log\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{y_{1}^{\phi}}+nlog(log\lambda)+nlog\phi+n(\phi-1)logy_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\phi}-nlog(\lambda-1)

(25)

The MLEs of
\lambda
and
\phi
are obtained by partially differentiating
Q
Eqn
25
with respect to
\lambda
and
\phi
and equating to zero as follows:

\frac{\partial Q}{\partial\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{-y_{i}^{\phi}}+\frac{n}{\lambda log\lambda}+\frac{n}{\lambda-1}=0

(26)

\frac{\partial Q}{\partial\phi}=-(log\lambda)\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\phi}logy_{i}e^{y_{i}^{\phi}}+\frac{n}{\phi}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}logy_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\phi}logy_{i}

(27)

Note that there are no closed form for the above partial derivatives. The MLEs
(\hat{\lambda},\hat{\phi})
are obtained by simultaneously solving equations
26
and
27
using numerical methods such as, Secant, Regula-Falsi or Newton-Raphson.

Since, the MLE estimates for
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{\phi}
are in non close form, we obtained the approximate asymptotic confidence interval for the MLE estimates for
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{\phi}
as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\phi}\end{pmatrix}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\
\phi\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\Pi}_{11}&\hat{\Pi}_{12}\\
\hat{\Pi}_{21}&\hat{\Pi}_{22}\end{pmatrix}\right)

The variance covariance matrix is obtained by the solution of the inverse of the information matrix given as:

\Pi^{-1}=-E\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\ell}{\partial\lambda^{2}}&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\ell}{\partial\lambda\partial\phi}\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial^{2}\ell}{\partial\lambda\partial\phi}&\dfrac{\partial^{2}\ell}{\partial\phi^{2}}\end{pmatrix}

Therefore, the approximate asymptotic confidence interval for the MLE estimates for
\hat{\lambda}
and
\hat{\phi}
can be obtained as

\hat{\lambda}+Z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat{\Pi}_{11}}

and

\hat{\phi}+Z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\hat{\Pi}_{22}}

respectively, where
Z_{\alpha/2}
is the standard normal
\alpha
percentile.

8
Conclusion

Prior to introducing the novel distribution of interest, we gave an update on some methods of extending distributions recently proposed in literature that are yet to gain much attention. We also gave a brief review of transformed distributions through SMP method. This is to give the gap at glance on base distributions that are yet to be explored via SMP method. Thereafter, we introduced and studied a novel two Weibull parameter distribution obtained via SMP technique name SMPtW. The properties of this distribution were derived and studied extensively. Through inverse transform random number generation approach, we evaluated the consistency of MLE estimates for the parameters of SMPtW distribution via simulation. In furtherance, we showed the supremacy of three parameters SMPtW distribution over other similar distributions in modelling time to event data. Bias of estimates, MSE, coverage probability, AIC and BIC were used as evaluation metrics. It is our expectation that modelers can make use of this distribution in modelling health. Also, distribution theorist and practitioners can also explore new methods identified here in extending distributions. Finally, transformation of some common base distributions via SMP can also be look into.

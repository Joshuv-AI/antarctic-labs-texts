The height of skew Dyck paths with two variants of downsteps
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Recently, in the context of walks of hexagonal circle packings, interest has emerged in the family of skew Dyck paths with two variants of down-steps. These paths have steps U , D g , D b , L = D r U,D_{g},D_{b},L=D_{r} . Using generating functions, the kernel method and (in)finite linear systems, contributions to the (average) height and other enumerations are made. As in many similar instances, the average height is of order n \sqrt{n} .

2.
Enumeration of skew Dyck paths with two variants of down-steps

The generating function

S(z)=\frac{1-z-\sqrt{1-10z+9z^{2}}}{4z}

becomes nicer using the substitution
z=\dfrac{u}{(1+u)(1+4u)}
, since it is simply
S=1+2u
. Apparently, this handy substitution wasn’t observed before. To read off coefficients, we use Cauchy’s integral formula:

\displaystyle[z^{n}]S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z^{n+1}}S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{du(1-4u^{2})}{(1+u)^{2}(1+4u)^{2}}\frac{(1+u)^{n+1}(1+4u)^{n+1}}{u^{n+1}}(1+2u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint du(1-4u^{2})(1+2u)\frac{(1+u)^{n-1}(1+4u)^{n-1}}{u^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=[u^{n}](1+2u-4u^{2}-8u^{3})(1+u)^{n-1}(1+4u)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1;1,5,4}{n}+2\binom{n-1;1,5,4}{n-1}-4\binom{n-1;1,5,4}{n-2}-8\binom{n-1;1,5,4}{n-3}.

We used the notion of weighted trinomial coefficients

\binom{m;1,5,4}{k}=[v^{k}](1+5v+4v^{2})^{m}=[v^{k}](1+v)^{m}(1+4v)^{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}4^{j}\binom{m}{j}\binom{m}{k-j}.

The notion of (weighted) trinomial coefficients has successfully appeared in
[
8
,
6
]
as well as in some papers by Panny and coauthors, see for instance

[
7
]
.

We found it more intuitive to draw a left step
(-1,-1)
as a marked (in red) down-step
(1,-1)
, see
[
9
]
. In this setting, we have now Dyck paths with 3 types of down-steps (blue, green, red), say
D_{b},D_{g},D_{r}
, with the restriction that
UD_{r}
and
D_{r}U
are not allowed to occur.

A path as studied in the paper of length 28 (=semi-length 14) and height 7.

We will construct an automaton that checks whether the syntacting conditions are still fulfilled, i. e.,
UL
and
LU
are forbidden.
According to the three types of steps, we have three layers of states (current level).

It will become important soon that the dominant singularity
\varrho=\frac{1}{9}
corresponds to the value
u_{\varrho}=\frac{1}{2}
.

The decomposition for the family
\mathscr{S}
translates as well to
\mathscr{S}_{h}
of bounded height, viz.

S_{h}=1+2zS_{h-1}S_{h}+z(S_{h-1}-1)

or

S_{h}(1-2zS_{h-1})=1-z+zS_{h-1}.

resp.

S_{h}=\frac{1-z+zS_{h-1}}{1-2zS_{h-1}}

This recursion will be solved using the
ansatz

S_{h}=\frac{p_{h}}{q_{h}}
:

\frac{p_{h}}{q_{h}}=\frac{1-z+z\frac{p_{h-1}}{q_{h-1}}}{1-2z\frac{p_{h-1}}{q_{h-1}}}=\frac{(1-z)q_{h-1}+zp_{h-1}}{q_{h-1}-2zp_{h-1}}

Comparing numerators and denominators we derive

\displaystyle p_{h}

\displaystyle=(1-z)q_{h-1}+zp_{h-1},

\displaystyle q_{h}

\displaystyle=q_{h-1}-2zp_{h-1};

this system may be solved using the previous substitution

\displaystyle p_{h}=\frac{-2u}{1-2u}\bigg(\frac{(4u+3)u}{(1+u)(1+4u)}\bigg)^{h}+\frac{1}{1-2u}\bigg(\frac{3u+1}{(1+u)(1+4u)}\bigg)^{h},

\displaystyle q_{h}=\frac{-4u^{2}}{1-4u^{2}}\bigg(\frac{(4u+3)u}{(1+u)(1+4u)}\bigg)^{h}+\frac{1}{1-4u^{2}}\bigg(\frac{3u+1}{(1+u)(1+4u)}\bigg)^{h}.

Although a computer was used to get this, one could check that these answers indeed save the recursions. Now

\frac{p_{h}}{q_{h}}=(1+2u)\frac{-2u(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}{-4u^{2}(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}.

In the limit
h\to\infty
(no height restrictions)
S_{\infty}=\lim_{h\to\infty}S_{h}=1+2u
, as it should.
Then we set
S^{[>h]}:=S-S_{h}
and

\displaystyle S^{[>h]}:=1+2u-\frac{p_{h}}{q_{h}}

\displaystyle=(1+2u)\bigg[1-\frac{-2u(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}{-4u^{2}(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=(1+2u)\frac{-4u^{2}(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}+2u(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}-(3u+1)^{h}}{-4u^{2}(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}

\displaystyle=(1-4u^{2})2u\frac{(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}}{-4u^{2}(4u+3)^{h}u^{h}+(3u+1)^{h}}

\displaystyle=2u(1-4u^{2})\frac{\lambda^{h}}{1-4u^{2}\lambda^{h}}=\frac{1-4u^{2}}{2u}\frac{4u^{2}\lambda^{h}}{1-4u^{2}\lambda^{h}},

with

\lambda:=\frac{(4u+3)u}{3u+1}.

The critical value
u_{\varrho}=\frac{1}{2}
corresponds to
\lambda_{\varrho}=1
. We approximate both,
2u
and
\lambda
by
e^{-t}
. Since they are not the same, this will lead to a leading term in the asymptotic expansion only.
In order to get the average height of our family of skew Dyck paths we need to study

\sum_{h\geq 0}S^{[>h]}=\frac{1-4u^{2}}{2u}\sum_{h\geq 1}\frac{4u^{2}\lambda^{h}}{1-4u^{2}\lambda^{h}}

or, approximating it,

\frac{1-4u^{2}}{2u}\sum_{h\geq 1}\frac{e^{-(h+2)t}}{1-e^{-(h+2)t}}\sim(2t+\dots)\sum_{h\geq 1}\frac{e^{-ht}}{1-e^{-ht}}.

The asymptotic expansion of the series appeared in many papers, in particular in
[
6
]
, where many technical details are provided. We continue with

\sim\frac{1-\lambda^{2}}{\lambda}\frac{-\log(1-\lambda)}{1-\lambda}\sim-\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda).

We need the expansion around the critical values;

\sqrt{1-9z}\sim\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}(1-2u),

1-\lambda\sim\frac{4}{5}(1-2u)\sim\frac{4}{5}\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-9z}=\frac{6\sqrt{2}}{5}\sqrt{1-9z},

\log(1-\lambda)\sim\log\bigg(\frac{6\sqrt{2}}{5}\sqrt{1-9z}\bigg)\sim\frac{1}{2}\log(1-9z),

-\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda)\sim(-2)\frac{1}{2}\log(1-9z)=-\log(1-9z).

By singularity analysis, the coefficient of
z^{n}
in the last approximation is asymptotic to
\dfrac{9^{n}}{n}
. For the average height, we need to divide this by the total number of relevant paths of semilength
n
.

The generating function
S(z)
will be expanded:

S(z)\sim 2-\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{1-9z}.

Thus (singularity analysis,
[
5
]
)

[z^{n}]S(z)\sim-\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}9^{n}\frac{n^{-3/2}}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2})}=\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}9^{n}\frac{n^{-3/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}};

the desired quotient is then asymptotic to

\frac{\frac{9^{n}}{n}}{\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}9^{n}\frac{n^{-3/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\sqrt{\pi n}=0.9428090414\sqrt{\pi n}.

Theorem 1
.

The average height of skew Dyck paths with two types of down-steps is asymptotic to

\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\sqrt{\pi n}

where all such paths of semi-length
n
are equally likely.

This may be compared to the formula of standard skew Dyck paths (see
[
10
]
)

\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{\pi n}

and for standard Dyck paths (not skew)

\sqrt{\pi n}.

The last result is classical
[
3
]
.

3.
The recursions

We start with an automaton that scans an input word of the alphabet {up, blue down, green down, red down} whether is satisfies the necessary syntactical conditions.
It has three layers, according to the type of the last step. Further, we will work with generating functions
f_{i}
,
g_{i}
,
h_{i}
, according to the state in which the path has landed.
Eventually,
f_{0}+g_{0}+h_{0}
will be the generating function
S(z^{2})
; note that for the present approach we need to consider the length, not the semilength. We will be able
to compute
f_{i}
,
g_{i}
,
h_{i}
explicitly.

Figure 4.
An automaton to check the syntax of skew Dyck paths with two types of down-steps (blue resp. green); the left step is drawn in red.

The system of recursions can be directly read off, by considering the last step:

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=1,\quad f_{i+1}=zf_{i}+zg_{i},

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=2zf_{i+1}+2zg_{i+1}+2zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle=zg_{i+1}+zh_{i+1}.

The first method of solving this is using bivariate generating function, viz.

F(u)=F(u,z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}f_{i},\ G(u)=G(u,z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i},\ H(u)=H(u,z)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}h_{i}.

From the recursions,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zg_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}2zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}2zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}2zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}h_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i+1}zh_{i+1},

or

\displaystyle F(u)-1

\displaystyle=zuF(u)+zuG(u),

\displaystyle uG(u)

\displaystyle=2z(F(u)-1)+2z(G(u)-G(0))+2z(H(u)-H(0)),

\displaystyle uH(u)

\displaystyle=z(G(u)-G(0))+z(H(u)-H(0)).

This system can be solved:

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z(-3+2z^{2})+(2z^{2}+2z^{2}G(0)+2z^{2}H(0)-1)u}{zu^{2}-(1+z^{2})u+z(3-z^{2})},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(G(0)+H(0)+z^{2})-2z^{2}(G(0)+H(0)+1)u}{zu^{2}-(1+z^{2})u+z(3-z^{2})},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z(2z^{2}H(0)+2z^{2}+2z^{2}G(0)-H(0)-G(0)-z^{2}(H(0)+G(0))u}{zu^{2}-(1+z^{2})u+z(3-z^{2})}.

The denominator will be factored:

zu^{2}-(1+z^{2})u+z(3-z^{2})=z(u-\mu_{1})(u-\mu_{2})

with

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+z^{2}+\sqrt{1-10z^{2}+9z^{4}}}{2z},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+z^{2}-\sqrt{1-10z^{2}+9z^{4}}}{2z}.

In the spirit of the kernel method
[
10
]
the factor
u-\mu_{2}
may be divided out from numerators and denominators, with the result

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2{z}^{2}g_{0}-2{z}^{2}h_{0}-2{z}^{2}+1}{z(u-\mu_{1})},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{2z(g_{0}+h_{0}+1)}{z(u-\mu_{1})},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(g_{0}+h_{0})}{z(u-\mu_{1})}.

Now one can plug in
u=0
and identify
f_{0}=1
,
g_{0}
,
h_{0}
;

\displaystyle G(0)=g_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z(-z+\mu_{1})}{\mu_{1}(-3z+\mu_{1})},

\displaystyle H(0)=h_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2z^{2}}{\mu_{1}(-3z+\mu_{1})}.

Hence

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2{z}^{2}g_{0}-2{z}^{2}h_{0}-2{z}^{2}+1}{z(u-\mu_{1})}=\frac{-\mu_{1}}{u-\mu_{1}}=\frac{1}{1-u/\mu_{1}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{2z(g_{0}+h_{0}+1)}{z(u-\mu_{1})}=\frac{\mu_{1}-\frac{1}{z}}{u-\mu_{1}}=\frac{\frac{1}{z\mu_{1}}-1}{1-u/\mu_{1}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(g_{0}+h_{0})}{z(u-\mu_{1})}=\frac{\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}+z-\frac{1}{2z}}{u-\mu_{1}}=\frac{-\frac{1}{2}-(z-\frac{1}{2z})\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}}{1-u/\mu_{1}}

and further

\displaystyle[u^{k}]F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{k}},

\displaystyle[u^{k}]G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z\mu_{1}^{k+1}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{k}}

\displaystyle[u^{k}]H(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{k}}-\Big(z-\frac{1}{2z}\Big)\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{k+1}}.

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{i}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z\mu_{1}^{i+1}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{i}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{i}}-\Big(z-\frac{1}{2z}\Big)\frac{1}{\mu_{1}^{i+1}}.

Nonlinear oscillators at resonance with periodic forcing
Philip Korman

Abstract
In this note we unify the results of A.C. Lazer and P.O. Frederickson [ 3 ] , A.C. Lazer [ 6 ] , A.C. Lazer and D.E. Leach [ 7 ] , J.M. Alonso and R. Ortega [ 1 ] , and P. Korman and Y. Li [ 4 ] on periodic oscillations and unbounded solutions of nonlinear equations with linear part at resonance and periodic forcing. We give conditions for the existence and non-existence of periodic solutions, and obtain a rather detailed description of the dynamics for nonlinear oscillations at resonance, in case periodic solutions do not exist.



Key words: Resonance, periodic oscillations, unbounded solutions.

AMS subject classification: 34C25, 34C15.

1
Introduction

We are interested in the existence of
2\pi
periodic solutions to the problem (here
x=x(t)
)

(1.1)

x^{\prime\prime}+f(x)x^{\prime}+g(x)+n^{2}x=e(t)\,.

The linear part

x^{\prime\prime}+n^{2}x=e(t)

is at resonance, with the null space spanned by
\cos nt
and
\sin nt
.
Define
F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(z)\,dz
. We assume throughout this paper that
e(t)\in C(R)
satisfies
e(t+2\pi)=e(t)
for all
t
,
f(x),g(x)\in C(R)
,
n\geq 1
is an integer; moreover, we assume that the finite limits at infinity
F(\infty)
,
F(-\infty)
,
g(\infty)
,
g(-\infty)
exist, and

(1.2)

F(-\infty)<F(x)<F(\infty),\;\;\;\;\mbox{for all $x\in R$}\,,

(1.3)

g(-\infty)<g(x)<g(\infty),\;\;\;\;\mbox{for all $x\in R$}\,.

Define

A_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e(t)\cos nt\,dt,\;\;B_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e(t)\sin nt\,dt\,.

In the case when
f=0
, the equation

(1.4)

x^{\prime\prime}+g(x)+n^{2}x=e(t)

was considered in the paper of A.C. Lazer and D.E. Leach
[
7
]
who proved the following classical theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
The condition

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}<2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right)

is necessary and sufficient for the existence of
2\pi
periodic solutions of (
1.4
).

In case
g=0
, for the corresponding equation

(1.5)

x^{\prime\prime}+f(x)x^{\prime}+n^{2}x=e(t)

one has the following result.

Theorem 1.2

The condition

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}<2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)

is necessary and sufficient for the existence of
2\pi
periodic solutions of (
1.5
).

This theorem was proved by A.C. Lazer
[
6
]
for
n=1
(with an earlier result by P.O. Frederickson and A.C. Lazer
[
3
]
), and for all
n\geq 1
by P. Korman and Y. Li
[
4
]
, who used a small modification of A.C. Lazer’s proof.

Question: is it possible to combine these theorems for the equation (
1.1
)? It turns out that the necessary conditions can be combined, while sufficient conditions cannot be combined.

Proposition 1

The condition

(1.6)

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}<2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)+2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right)

is necessary for the existence of
2\pi
periodic solution of (
1.1
).

Proposition 2

The condition (
1.6
) is not sufficient for the existence of
2\pi
periodic solution of (
1.1
).

In case (
1.4
) has no
2\pi
periodic solutions, all solutions of (
1.4
) are unbounded as
t\rightarrow\pm\infty
, as follows by the second Massera’s theorem, as was observed first by G. Seifert
[
8
]
. Later J.M. Alonso and R. Ortega
[
1
]
gave an elementary approach to this result (with a more refined statement, asserting that solutions tend to infinity in
C^{1}
norm). We observe next that the approach of
[
1
]
works for the equation (
1.1
) as well.

Proposition 3

Assume in addition to the assumptions above that
f(x)
is uniformly bounded from below (the assumption (
2.10
) below). Then in case

(1.7)

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}\geq 2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)+2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right),

all solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy
\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\left(x^{2}(t)+{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t)\right)=\infty
.

Clearly, there are no
2\pi
periodic solutions in this case, in view of Proposition
1
. Proposition
3
shows that the absence of
2\pi
periodic solutions turns out to be more decisive in determining the overall dynamics of (
1.1
) than the existence of
2\pi
periodic solutions.

2
The proofs

The following elementary lemmas are easy to prove.

Lemma 2.1

Consider a function
\cos(nt-\varphi)
, with an integer
n
and any real
\varphi
. Denote
P_{c}=\{t\in(0,2\pi)\,|\,\cos(nt-\varphi)>0\}
and
N_{c}=\{t\in(0,2\pi)\,|\,\cos(nt-\varphi)<0\}
. Then

\int_{P_{c}}\cos(nt-\varphi)\,dt=2,\;\;\;\;\int_{N_{c}}\cos(nt-\varphi)\,dt=-2\,.

Lemma 2.2

Consider a function
\sin(nt-\varphi)
, with an integer
n
and any real
\varphi
. Denote
P_{s}=\{t\in(0,2\pi)\,|\,\sin(nt-\varphi)>0\}
and
N_{s}=\{t\in(0,2\pi)\,|\,\sin(nt-\varphi)<0\}
. Then

\int_{P_{s}}\sin(nt-\varphi)\,dt=2,\;\;\;\;\int_{N_{s}}\sin(nt-\varphi)\,dt=-2\,.

Proof of Proposition
1
. Given arbitrary numbers
a
and
b
, one can find a
\delta\in[0,2\pi)
, so that

a\cos nt+b\sin nt=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\cos(nt-\delta)\,.

(with
\cos\delta=\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}
,
\sin\delta=\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}
.) It follows that

(2.1)

\frac{A_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\cos nt+\frac{B_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\sin nt=\cos(nt-\delta)\,,

for some
\delta\in[0,2\pi)
.
Multiply (
1.1
) by
\frac{A_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\cos nt
and integrate, then multiply (
1.1
) by
\frac{B_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\sin nt
and integrate, and add the results:

(2.2)

\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x(t))^{\prime}\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt+\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x(t))\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt,

in view of (
2.1
).
Using that
x(t)
is a
2\pi
periodic solution, and Lemma
2.2
, obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x(t))^{\prime}\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt=n\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x(t))\sin(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle=n\int_{P_{s}}F(x(t))\sin(nt-\delta)\,dt+n\int_{N_{s}}F(x(t))\sin(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle<2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)\,.

Similarly, using Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x(t))\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt<g(\infty)\int_{P_{c}}\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt+g(-\infty)\int_{N_{c}}\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle=2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right)\,.

The condition (
1.6
) follows.

\diamondsuit

Proof of Proposition
2
.
Consider the equation

(2.3)

x^{\prime\prime}+f(x)x^{\prime}+g(x)+n^{2}x=E\cos nt,

with a parameter
E
. Calculate
A_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}E\cos^{2}nt\,dt=E\pi
,
B_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}E\cos nt\sin nt\,dt=0
, and
\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}=E\pi
.
Choose
E
so that

(2.4)

\;\;\;\;E\pi=\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}=2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)+2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right)-\epsilon,

with
\epsilon>0
small, to be specified. The condition (
1.6
) holds for the equation (
2.3
). If this condition were sufficient, we would have a
2\pi
periodic solution of (
2.3
), and hence

(2.5)

x^{\prime\prime}+F(x)^{\prime}+n^{2}x=E\cos nt-g(x)\equiv\bar{e}(t)\,.

Calculate the coefficients
A_{n},B_{n}
for (
2.5
):

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{e}(t)\cos nt\,dt=E\pi-\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x)\cos nt\,dt,

\displaystyle\bar{B}_{n}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bar{e}(t)\sin nt\,dt=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x)\sin nt\,dt.

Since (
2.5
) is solvable, by Theorem
1.2
we have

(2.7)

\sqrt{\bar{A}_{n}^{2}+\bar{B}_{n}^{2}}<2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right).

For any
\epsilon>0
we can choose an index
n_{0}
, so that for
n\geq n_{0}

(2.8)

|\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x)\sin nt\,dt|<\epsilon,

as follows by well known results on oscillatory integrals, see e.g., O. Costin et al
[
2
]
.
Using (
2.6
), (
2.8
), followed by (
2.4
), obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\bar{A}_{n}^{2}+\bar{B}_{n}^{2}}>|\bar{A}_{n}|>E\pi-\epsilon

\displaystyle=2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right)+2\left(g(\infty)-g(-\infty)\right)-2\epsilon

\displaystyle>2n\left(F(\infty)-F(-\infty)\right),

contradicting (
2.7
), provided we fix
\epsilon<g(\infty)-g(-\infty)
.

\diamondsuit

We shall prove a generalization of Proposition
3
after several preliminary results. By an obvious modification of its proof, one obtains the following generalization of Proposition
1
.

Proposition 4

Assume that the functions
F(x)
and
g(x)
have finite infimums and supremums on
(-\infty,\infty)
. Then the condition

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}<2n\left(\sup F-\inf F\right)+2\left(\sup g-\inf g\right)

is necessary for the existence of
2\pi
periodic solution of (
1.1
).

We shall use the following result that is included in J.M. Alonso and R. Ortega
[
1
]
.

Proposition 5

(
[
1
]
)
Let
G(\zeta,\eta):R^{2}\rightarrow R^{2}
be a continuous vector function, and let
V(\zeta,\eta):R^{2}\rightarrow R
be a continuous function. With
\xi\equiv(\zeta,\eta)
consider a sequence

\xi_{n+1}=G\left(\xi_{n}\right),\;\;\;\;n\geq 0\,,

beginning with an arbitrary vector
\xi_{0}
. Assume that

(2.9)

V\left(G(\xi)\right)>V(\xi)\,,\;\;\;\;\forall\xi\in R^{2}\,.

Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\xi_{n}||=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\zeta_{n}^{2}+\eta_{n}^{2}}=\infty
.

Proof:
If the sequence
\{||\xi_{n}||\}
fails to tend to infinity, then
\{\xi_{n}\}
has a finite accumulation point
\xi^{*}\in R^{2}
. Let
\{\xi_{n_{k}}\}
be a subsequence tending to
\xi^{*}
, with
n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots
. Since
V(\xi_{n+1})=V(G\left(\xi_{n})\right)>V(\xi_{n})
by (
2.9
), the sequence
V(\xi_{n})
is increasing. Then one has

\displaystyle V(G(\xi^{*}))=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V(G(\xi_{n_{k}}))=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V(\xi_{n_{k}+1})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V(\xi_{n_{k+1}})=V(\xi^{*})\,,

contradicting (
2.9
). (Observing that
n_{k}+1\leq n_{k+1}
.)

\diamondsuit

The next lemma says that for solution of (
1.1
),
x^{2}(t)+{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t)
cannot increase too much over an interval of length
2\pi
.

Lemma 2.3

Assume assume that
e(t)\in C(R)
is
2\pi
periodic, the condition (
1.3
) holds, and moreover assume that

(2.10)

f(x)\geq\alpha,\;\;\;\;\mbox{for some $\alpha\in R$, and all $x\in R$}.

Then for any initial data
(x(0),x^{\prime}(0))
, with
c_{0}=x^{2}(0)+n^{2}{x^{\prime}}^{2}(0)
, there is a number
c=c(c_{0})
so that the corresponding solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

x^{2}(t)+n^{2}{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t)\leq c,\;\;\mbox{for all $t\in[0,2\pi]$}.

Proof:
Consider the ‘‘energy”
E(t)=\frac{1}{2}{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}n^{2}x^{2}(t)
. Since

E^{\prime}(t)=-f(x){x^{\prime}}^{2}-g(x)x^{\prime}+e(t)x^{\prime}.

By our conditions obtain
E^{\prime}(t)\leq c_{1}E(t)+c_{2}
, and the proof follows.

\diamondsuit

We now prove the following generalization of Proposition
3
.

Proposition 6

Assume that the functions
F(x)
and
g(x)
have finite infimums and supremums on
(-\infty,\infty)
Assume also that (
2.10
) holds.
In case

\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}\geq 2n\left(\sup F-\inf F\right)+2\left(\sup g-\inf g\right),

all solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy
\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\left(x^{2}(t)+{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t)\right)=\infty
.

Proof:
Following J.M. Alonso and R. Ortega
[
1
]
, we shall use Proposition
5
. Given
\xi=(\zeta,\eta)\in R^{2}
, denote by
x(t,\xi)
the solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
x(0)=\zeta
,
x^{\prime}(0)=\eta
. Define the map
R^{2}\rightarrow R^{2}
by
G(\xi)=\left(x(2\pi,\xi),x^{\prime}(2\pi,\xi)\right)
, and we shall show that the sequence of iterates

\xi_{n+1}=G(\xi_{n})\,,\;\;n=0,1,2,\ldots

is unbounded for any
\xi_{0}
. With
\delta
as defined by (
2.1
), define the function

V(\xi)=\eta\cos\delta-n\zeta\sin\delta+F(\zeta)\cos\delta\,.

Multiply (
1.1
) by
\frac{A_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\cos nt
and integrate, then multiply (
1.1
) by
\frac{B_{n}}{\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}}\sin nt
and integrate, and add the results. In view of (
2.1
) obtain as above

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}(x^{\prime\prime}+n^{2}x)\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt+\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x(t))^{\prime}\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x(t))\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt=\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}\,.

Integrating by parts, we express the first term on the left as

\displaystyle\left[x^{\prime}(t)\cos(nt-\delta)\right]|_{{}_{0}}^{2\pi}+n\left[x(t)\sin(nt-\delta)\right]|_{{}_{0}}^{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\left[x^{\prime}(2\pi)-\eta\right]\cos\delta-n\left[x(2\pi)-x(\zeta)\right]\sin\delta\,,

and the second term as

\left[F(x(2\pi))-F(x(0))\right]\cos\delta+n\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x)\sin(nt-\delta)\,dt\,.

We combine the non-integral terms in (
2.11
) as
V(G(\xi))-V(\xi)
.
Then (
2.11
) gives

\displaystyle V(G(\xi))-V(\xi)=\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}-n\int_{0}^{2\pi}F(x(t))\sin(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(x(t))\cos(nt-\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle>\sqrt{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}-2n\left(\sup F-\inf F\right)-2\left(\sup g-\inf g\right)\geq 0\,.

(The first inequality is strict because the functions
g
and
F
are non-constant by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).) Hence, the condition (
2.9
) holds, and Proposition
5
applies, proving the unboundness of the sequence
\left(x(2n\pi,\xi),x^{\prime}(2n\pi,\xi)\right)
. If there was a sequence
\{t_{k}\}\rightarrow\infty
with bounded
{x^{\prime}}^{2}(t_{k})+x^{2}(t_{k})
, we would obtain a contradiction with Lemma
2.3
.

\diamondsuit

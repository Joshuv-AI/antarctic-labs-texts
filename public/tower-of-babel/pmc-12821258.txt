Identification and screening of plant hormone-related genes associated with dormancy release in Prunus persica seeds using transcriptomics and targeted metabolomics
Authors: Fan Zhang, Chenbing Wang, Jiaxuan Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Peach (Prunus persica) seeds exhibit low natural germination rates and prolonged germination periods due to deep dormancy, which severely limits seedling propagation efficiency and industrial scalability. In this study, four treatment groups were established: CK (water), T1 (gibberellin + sodium fluoride), T2 (sodium hypochlorite), and T3 (sodium fluoride), all combined with temperature regulation. Physiological measurements, transcriptomic analysis, and targeted metabolomic analysis were integrated to investigate the dynamic changes during seed dormancy release. Exogenous substance treatments significantly enhanced germination performance, accompanied by distinct fluctuations in endogenous hormone levels, including IAA, GA₃, ABA, and CTK. Analysis of the ABA/GA₃ ratio revealed that the 45th day of treatment is a critical regulatory node in the hormonal control of peach seed dormancy alleviation. Transcriptome profiling identified 11,807 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were significantly enriched in key pathways such as “plant hormone signal transduction”, “sugar and sucrose metabolism”, and “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites”. Targeted metabolomics detected eight differential metabolites, primarily associated with “plant hormone signal transduction”, “diterpenoid biosynthesis”, and “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites”. Integrated analysis demonstrated that exogenous treatments modulate the expression of core components in the abscisic acid signaling pathway (PP2C, PYR/PYL, and SnRK2) and the gibberellin signaling pathway (GID1), while also inducing significant transcriptional changes in auxin (AUX/IAA, ARF, and GH3), cytokinin (AHP), and ethylene (EIN3, ERF) signaling pathways. These findings provide a robust theoretical foundation for optimizing peach seedling production—particularly in shortening dormancy duration and improving germination uniformity—and offer valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying seed dormancy regulation in woody perennial species.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12437-5.

Introduction

Peach (Prunus persica), a key economic fruit species in the Rosaceae family, typically exhibits deep seed dormancy—a conserved adaptive trait shaped by long-term evolution that enables seeds to avoid unfavorable conditions such as cold stress and drought, thereby ensuring species persistence under natural selection [1, 2]. However, in agricultural practice, this trait results in low natural germination rates and prolonged germination periods, significantly limiting the efficiency of seedling propagation and large-scale peach nursery production [3]. Thus, understanding the regulatory mechanisms of peach seed dormancy and developing effective strategies for its release are crucial for improving seedling production efficiency [4]. Currently, three primary approaches are used to break fruit tree seed dormancy: low-temperature stratification, exogenous hormone application, and the combination of these two methods. While low-temperature stratification effectively alleviates dormancy, it is time-consuming [5, 6]. Exogenous hormones not only promote plant growth and development but also break seed dormancy, shorten breeding cycles, and accelerate the propagation rate and efficiency of fruit tree seedlings [7, 8]. Moreover, although hormone treatment alone can overcome dormancy, combining it with cold stratification synergistically enhances germination performance [9].

Seed dormancy regulation involves the integration of genetic factors and internal/external environmental cues, with endogenous hormone homeostasis acting as the central regulatory hub [10]. Abscisic acid (ABA) functions as the primary dormancy-promoting hormone by suppressing embryonic growth and metabolic activity [11], whereas gibberellins (GAs), auxin (IAA), and cytokinins (CTKs) promote cell division, alleviate embryo quiescence, and drive germination [10]. Ethylene (ETH) further modulates dormancy by negatively regulating ABA biosynthesis and signaling pathways [12, 13]. At the molecular level, ABA acts through the well-characterized PYR/PYL/RCAR-PP2C-SnRK2 signaling cascade, with downstream components such as ABI1, ABI2, ABI3, and ABI5 playing pivotal roles in germination control [14, 15]. Recent studies have identified additional positive regulators of seed germination, including the SnRK2.3/SRK2I kinase and the protein phosphatase OsPP2C51 [16]. Conversely, GAs antagonize ABA during germination and must be maintained at elevated levels to suppress ABA-mediated signaling. Notably, both ABA and GAs are derived from the common precursor geranylgeranyl diphosphate [17], highlighting the importance of identifying regulatory factors that balance their biosynthetic pathways for precise control of seed germination.

Recent advances in integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses have provided a powerful framework for dissecting plant physiological processes by simultaneously analyzing gene expression regulation and dynamic metabolite accumulation. This integrative approach has elucidated potential mechanisms underlying seed germination in species such as Polygonatum cyrtonema [18], Notopterygium incisum [19], and Michelia chapensis [20], revealing that germination is predominantly governed by biological processes including plant hormone signal transduction, energy metabolism, secondary metabolite biosynthesis, photosynthesis-related pathways, and transcriptional regulation. Despite these advances, a systematic investigation of peach seed dormancy release using multi-omics integration remains lacking.

In this study, we used peach seeds as experimental materials and established four treatment groups (CK: water; T1: gibberellin + sodium fluoride; T2: sodium hypochlorite; T3: sodium fluoride), all under temperature-regulated incubation. Among these treatments, gibberellin directly breaks seed dormancy, sodium hypochlorite promotes germination via surface sterilization and auxiliary dormancy breaking, and sodium fluoride modulates dormancy by inhibiting phosphatases and energy metabolism. Germination assays were conducted to evaluate the effects of exogenous treatments on germination performance. Dynamic changes in endogenous hormone levels (ABA, GA₃, IAA, CTK) and the ABA/GA₃ ratio were monitored throughout dormancy release to identify critical transition points. Transcriptome sequencing was performed to profile differentially expressed genes (DEGs), followed by Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses to pinpoint core regulatory pathways. Finally, an integrated transcriptome-metabolome analysis was employed to construct a gene-metabolite regulatory network associated with plant hormone signaling, uncovering the molecular mechanisms by which different exogenous treatments induce dormancy release in peach seeds. These findings provide a robust scientific foundation for optimizing seedling propagation protocols—particularly in reducing dormancy duration and improving seedling uniformity—and contribute valuable insights into the molecular regulation of seed dormancy in woody perennial plants.

Results

Determination of germination rate and endogenous hormones in peach seeds

Effects of exogenous treatments on seed germination

In this study, peach seeds were subjected to four treatment groups: CK (water, control), T1 (gibberellin + sodium fluoride), T2 (sodium hypochlorite), and T3 (sodium fluoride). Germination assays were conducted under controlled conditions. After 51 days of incubation, the germination rates were 12% for CK, 98.6% for T1, 100% for T2, and 98.5% for T3 (Fig. 1a and S1). Statistical analysis revealed that all exogenous treatments significantly enhanced seed germination compared to CK, with T2 exhibiting the highest germination efficiency.

Effects of exogenous treatments on endogenous hormone dynamics

It is well established that endogenous hormones such as IAA, GA, and CTK promote seed germination and early seedling development, whereas ABA acts as a key inhibitor of germination [21]. As shown in Fig. 1b, dynamic changes in hormone levels varied across treatments following exogenous application and temperature shifts. The content of CTK (6-furfurylaminopurine) increased gradually in all groups, with minimal intergroup variation. In the T2 group, CTK peaked on day 42 (25°C, 6 day) and subsequently declined, while the other treatments showed a continued upward trend. By the final sampling point, CTK levels in the T1 group were significantly higher than those in CK, whereas T2 and T3 showed lower concentrations. ABA levels remained relatively stable across all groups until day 39 (25°C, 3 day); however, on day 42, ABA content increased in T1 and T3 relative to CK, while it decreased significantly in T2. At the end of the experiment, no significant differences in ABA content were observed among the groups. IAA levels exhibited oscillatory fluctuations across all treatments. The T1, T2, and T3 groups displayed similar temporal patterns, which differed from the CK group (which showed a distinct profile). Peaks in IAA content occurred on day 10 and day 33, followed by wave-like declines. At the conclusion of the trial, the CK group had the lowest IAA level. GA₃ content showed a fluctuating but overall upward trend in all groups throughout the experiment, with notable differences in amplitude between treatments. Notably, at the final time point, GA₃ levels in the CK group exceeded those in all exogenous treatment groups.

Dynamic changes in the ABA/GA₃ ratio during dormancy release

As illustrated in Fig. 1c, the ABA/GA₃ ratio gradually declined in all treatment groups over the course of the experiment. In the T3 group, the ratio reached its maximum on day 42 before sharply decreasing. At the end of the treatment period, the ABA/GA₃ ratios in all exogenous treatment groups were higher than that in CK. By integrating these hormonal dynamics, day 45 was identified as a critical transition point in the hormonal regulation of peach seed dormancy release. Therefore, seed samples collected at 45 days post-treatment were selected for subsequent transcriptomic and targeted metabolomic analyses to capture molecular and metabolic changes associated with dormancy alleviation.

Transcriptome analysis

Sequencing data evaluation and transcriptomic profiling

Afte quality filtering to remove low-quality reads, a total of 73.34 Gb of clean data was obtained. Each sample yielded more than 3.81 Gb of high-quality reads, with uniform GC content distribution and Q30 values exceeding 87.61% (Table S1), indicating excellent sequencing quality suitable for downstream analysis. Sample correlation analysis revealed low gene expression similarity between different treatment groups, whereas biological replicates within the same group showed high reproducibility, with Pearson correlation coefficients approaching 1.0 (Fig. 2a), confirming strong intra-group consistency and reliable experimental repeatability. Principal component analysis (PCA) further demonstrated clear separation among treatment groups, with samples clustering tightly within each group and distinct spatial distribution between groups (Fig. 2b), indicating significant transcriptional divergence induced by different treatments. DEGs were identified by comparing each treatment group against the CK control. In T1 vs. CK, 2,332 genes were upregulated and 2,263 downregulated; in T2 vs. CK, 1,755 upregulated and 2,531 downregulated; and in T3 vs. CK, 1,139 upregulated and 1,787 downregulated (Fig. 2c). Additionally, 2,748 DEGs were identified between T1 and T2, 3,642 between T1 and T3, and 2,073 between T2 and T3 (Fig. 2c). However, the “CK (pre-treatment) _vs_CK1” comparison exhibits more DEGs primarily because water soaking alone initiates a comprehensive dormancy-breaking response in dormant seeds. A Venn diagram revealed 935 commonly shared DEGs across four comparison groups (CK1_vs_CK, T1_vs_CK, T2_vs_CK, and T3_vs_CK), highlighting a core set of genes responsive to exogenous treatments (Fig. 2d).

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes

GO enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs from the T1_vs_CK, T2_vs_CK, and T3_vs_CK comparisons (Figure S2). GO terms are categorized into three domains: Biological Process, Molecular Function, and Cellular Component. In the Biological Process category, DEGs were significantly enriched in responses to wounding, oxidative stress, cell wall macromolecule metabolic processes, and DNA replication. Notably, terms related to translation and transcription regulation were consistently enriched, underscoring the importance of protein synthesis and gene expression control during dormancy release and germination initiation. In the Molecular Function category, common enrichments included oxidoreductase activity acting on NAD/NADP as acceptors, glycosyltransferase activity, and enzyme inhibitor activity. Oxidoreductases contribute to energy production through the oxidation of carbohydrates and fatty acids. Glycosyltransferases participate in cell wall polysaccharide biosynthesis and modification, promoting seed coat weakening and facilitating radicle emergence. Enzyme inhibitor activity helps regulate protease and phosphatase activities, maintaining a balance between protein degradation and synthesis, preventing excessive turnover during dormancy while enabling precise modulation of enzymatic reactions during germination to ensure orderly metabolic transitions.

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes

KEGG annotation revealed distinct pathway enrichment patterns across treatment groups (Fig. 3 and S3). The T1_vs_CK comparison identified 139 enriched pathways, encompassing 3,362 DEGs; T2_vs_CK yielded 141 enriched pathways with 3,102 DEGs; and T3_vs_CK showed 132 enriched pathways annotated with 2,086 DEGs. In the T1_vs_CK group, significantly enriched pathways included “plant hormone signal transduction”, “sugar and sucrose metabolism”, “MAPK signaling pathway”, “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites”, and “transporter activity”. These findings suggest that during morphological dormancy, peach seeds initiate hormonal signaling cascades and activate metabolic processes involving sucrose, lipids, and flavonoids, establishing a biochemical foundation for subsequent dormancy release and germination. In the T2_vs_CK group, key enriched pathways included “glutathione metabolism”, “plant hormone signal transduction”, “ribosome biogenesis in eukaryotes”, and “protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum”. This indicates that during physiological dormancy, seeds enhance antioxidant capacity, ribosomal function, and protein folding/modification machinery, critical adaptations that support increased protein synthesis and regulatory control necessary for dormancy alleviation. For the T3_vs_CK group, significantly enriched pathways comprised “plant hormone signal transduction”, “starch and sucrose metabolism”, “ABC transporters”, and “amino acid metabolism”. These results imply that at this developmental stage, seeds maintain metabolic homeostasis through coordinated hormone signaling and central carbon metabolism, continuing to accumulate energy reserves and biosynthetic precursors essential for successful germination. In addition, in the T2 vs. T1, T3 vs. T1, and T3 vs. T2 comparisons, the main enriched KEGG pathways were related to “plant hormone signal transduction”, “sugar and sucrose metabolism”, “MAPK pathway”, and “secondary metabolite biosynthesis”.

Metabolomic analysis

Quantitative profiling and multivariate analysis of differential metabolites

Targeted metabolomic analysis was conducted to quantify gibberellin- and abscisic acid-related metabolites under different treatments. Hierarchical clustering analysis demonstrated tight grouping of biological replicates within each treatment (Fig. 4a), indicating high reproducibility, low intra-group variability, and reliable data quality. Differentially accumulated metabolites were defined as those with |fold change| ≥ 2 and statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). During dormancy release, the T1_vs_CK comparison identified three upregulated differential metabolites; T2_vs_CK exhibited two upregulated and two downregulated metabolites; and T3_vs_CK showed one upregulated metabolite. Additionally, the T2_vs_T1 comparison group had 6 different metabolites, while the T3_vs_T1 and T3_vs_T2 comparison groups had 3 different metabolites (Fig. 4b).

KEGG enrichment analysis of differential metabolites

KEGG pathway annotation revealed that differential metabolites in the T1_vs_CK, T2_vs_CK, T3_vs_CK, T2_vs_T1, T3_vs_T1, and T3_vs_T2 comparison groups were mapped to 4, 5, 2, 5, 4, and 4 metabolic pathways, respectively (Fig. 5). Integrated transcriptome and metabolome analysis

By integrating KEGG annotations and enrichment results from both DAMs and DEGs, we identified common pathways co-enriched across omics layers. Notably, “Plant hormone signal transduction” and “Diterpenoid biosynthesis” emerged as central hubs shared by transcriptomic and metabolomic data (Fig. 6). Differential metabolites in the diterpenoid biosynthesis pathway are likely involved in synthesizing GA precursors, thereby providing a biochemical foundation for GA signal activation. This finding highlights the central roles of plant hormone signal transduction and diterpenoid biosynthesis in the regulation of peach seed dormancy release.

Identification of differentially expressed genes

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying exogenous treatment-induced dormancy release in peach seeds, we focused on the plant hormone signaling network and performed a metabolite-gene correlation analysis. A regulatory network linking DAMs and DEGs was constructed based on KEGG pathway annotations (Fig. 7), with a particular focus on ABA, GA, IAA, CTK, and ETH signaling pathways.

In the auxin signaling pathway, auxin response proteins (AUX/IAA/LAX) and SAUR family proteins were predominantly upregulated during dormancy release. Six auxin response factor (ARF) genes exhibited consistent upregulation across T1, T2, and T3 treatments, whereas three GH3 family genes were largely downregulated in T1. Four histidine-containing phosphotransfer protein (AHP) genes associated with CTK signaling were detected, most of which were downregulated in T1 and T2, suggesting active involvement of CTK signaling in dormancy regulation. Seven gibberellin receptor GID1 genes were identified, among which GID1C and GID1B displayed significant expression variation across treatments. Enhanced GA signaling activity under exogenous hormone application likely contributes to accelerated dormancy release.

Six ABA receptor genes (PYR/PYL) involved in ABA signal transduction were identified, showing distinct expression patterns across treatments. Compared to CK, most PYR/PYL genes were downregulated in T1 and T2, while one was upregulated in T3; another remained highly expressed in both T1 and T3. Serine/threonine-protein kinase SRK2 (SnRK2) showed significant differential expression during dormancy transition. Upregulation of ABA-responsive transcription factors (ABF) and downregulation of protein phosphatase 2 C (PP2C) genes under various treatments indicates suppression of ABA signaling, thereby alleviating its inhibitory effect on germination. For ethylene signaling, ethylene-insensitive 3 (EIN3) was predominantly downregulated in T1 but upregulated in T2 and T3. Seven ethylene response factor (ERF) genes were detected, most of which showed marked upregulation across treatment groups. These results collectively demonstrate that hormone signaling pathways play pivotal roles in regulating the transition from dormancy to germination, with extensive crosstalk and synergistic interactions coordinating this developmental switch.

Discussion

Seed dormancy is a key adaptive trait evolved through long-term environmental selection, primarily governed by physical constraints such as seed coat impermeability, hormonal imbalances, and embryonic physiological after-ripening [22]. Peach (Prunus persica) seeds are characterized by a hard seed coat and a complex endogenous hormone regulatory network, which collectively result in low germination rates under natural conditions [23–25]. In this study, treatment with sodium hypochlorite (T2) and gibberellin (in the T1 group) significantly enhanced the germination performance of peach seeds. This effect is likely mediated through two mechanisms: (1) altering seed coat permeability via chemical or physical modification; and (2) disrupting endogenous hormonal equilibrium to alleviate dormancy inhibition. Endogenous plant hormones serve as central signaling molecules in the regulation of seed dormancy and germination [26, 27]. Therefore, we monitored dynamic changes in four key hormones, IAA, GA, ABA, and CTK, in peach seeds subjected to various exogenous treatments and temperature regimes. The results revealed distinct fluctuation patterns in all four hormones following combined exogenous and thermal treatments, indicating a coordinated physiological shift that promotes dormancy release and prepares seeds for germination. Notably, ABA and GA₃ act as antagonistic regulators of seed fate, and their ratio (ABA/GA₃) is widely recognized as a pivotal indicator of dormancy status [28]. A high ABA/GA₃ ratio maintains dormancy, whereas a decline signals dormancy release and the transition toward germination [26]. In this study, all four treatment groups (CK, T1, T2, and T3) exhibited a gradual decrease in the ABA/GA₃ ratio, consistent with the general pattern of dormancy alleviation. Intriguingly, the T3 group displayed a transient peak in the ABA/GA₃ ratio at 42 days of treatment, followed by a sharp decline, suggesting that this time point represents a physiological turning point marking the onset of dormancy release in T3-treated seeds. Based on this hormonally defined transition, seed samples collected at 45 days post-treatment were selected for integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis to precisely dissect the molecular mechanisms underlying dormancy release.

Differential gene expression analysis is a fundamental approach for uncovering transcriptional regulatory networks and identifying functional responses to experimental treatments [29]. In this study, pairwise comparisons between each treatment group (T1, T2, T3) and the control (CK) yielded 4,595, 4,286, and 2,926 DEGs, respectively. Additionally, 2,748, 3,642, and 2,073 DEGs were identified in the T2 vs. T1, T3 vs. T1, and T3 vs. T2 comparisons, respectively. Among these, the T1 group exhibited the largest number of DEGs. This observation may be attributed to gibberellin directly activating germination-related pathways, while low-concentration sodium fluoride moderately regulates metabolism to enhance hormonal efficacy [30]. Sodium hypochlorite primarily promotes germination by increasing seed coat permeability and eliminating surface microorganisms, with a moderate dormancy-breaking efficiency [31]. In contrast, sodium fluoride exerts a mild dormancy-regulating effect by inhibiting phosphatases and energy metabolism, resulting in a weaker germination-promoting effect compared to T1 and T2 [32]. Subsequent GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that these DEGs were significantly enriched in critical pathways including “plant hormone signal transduction”, “sugar and sucrose metabolism”, and “biosynthesis of secondary metabolites”. The sugar and sucrose metabolism pathway supplies essential energy for germination, while plant hormone signaling serves as the core regulatory hub for dormancy release. Together, these pathways provide both metabolic resources and signaling cues necessary for successful seed germination. Metabolomics provides a direct readout of cellular physiology by profiling terminal products of biochemical pathways, enabling strong phenotypic correlations and concrete evidence for metabolic regulation [33]. In this study, targeted metabolite profiling of gibberellin- and abscisic acid-related compounds identified 3, 4, and 1 differentially accumulated metabolites in the T1, T2, and T3 groups, respectively. Furthermore, the T2 vs. T1, T3 vs. T1, and T3 vs. T2 comparisons identified 6, 3, and 3 DAMs, respectively. KEGG enrichment analysis further demonstrated that these metabolites were predominantly associated with “Plant hormone signal transduction”, “Diterpenoid biosynthesis”, and “Biosynthesis of secondary metabolites”. Notably, the diterpenoid biosynthesis pathway is implicated in the production of GA precursors, thus supplying the biochemical substrates required for GA signal activation. Concurrent enrichment of the plant hormone signaling pathway reinforces the central role of hormonal dynamics in dormancy regulation. Collectively, these findings provide crucial metabolic-level insights into the molecular mechanisms governing dormancy release in peach seeds.

To further elucidate the regulatory associations between genes and metabolites, an integrated KEGG pathway analysis was performed on DEGs and DAMs. The results revealed significant co-enrichment in two key pathways: “Plant hormone signal transduction” and “Diterpenoid biosynthesis”, with the former occupying a central role in dormancy release. Previous studies have shown that auxin may promote seed dormancy [34], and IAA levels typically increase during seed imbibition and maturation [35]. Consistent with this trend, IAA content in our study exhibited distinct peaks at 10- and 33- days post-treatment across all groups. GH3 family genes are known to suppress auxin signaling during early germination stages, while SAUR genes, primary auxin-responsive elements, are rapidly induced by auxin and play pivotal roles in signal transduction [36]. Consistent with these findings, Aux/IAA, ARF, and SAUR were predominantly upregulated during dormancy release, whereas GH3 family genes were significantly downregulated in the T1 group. These results suggest that exogenous treatments may enhance auxin-mediated dormancy regulation by suppressing GH3 expression and amplifying downstream IAA signaling. Prior evidence confirms that CTK plays a positive role in promoting dormancy release in dicotyledonous plants [37]. In this study, four AHP gene (key components of CTK signaling) were mostly downregulated in T1 and T2 groups, indicating complex modulation of CTK activity under different treatments. Collectively, these data indicate that exogenous substances enhance the activity of both GA and IAA signaling pathways, thereby facilitating dormancy alleviation in peach seeds.

ABA and GA act as antagonistic regulators of seed dormancy. GA functions through the GID1-DELLA-SCFSLY1/GID2 signaling cascade, which promotes DELLA protein degradation and activates germination-related gene expression [38]. This study identified seven differentially expressed GID1 receptors, among which GID1C and GID1B showed marked expression variation across treatment groups, suggesting their potential functional specificity in dormancy regulation. PP2C proteins serve as negative regulators of ABA signaling; in Arabidopsis, members such as ABI1 and ABI2 are well-documented modulators of seed dormancy [14, 39–41]. In this work, PP2C genes were predominantly downregulated following exogenous treatments, indicating suppression of ABA signaling. ABA perception is initiated by PYR/PYL family receptors binding to ABA molecules [42], and homologs such as PtPYRL1 and PtPYRL5 have been shown to positively regulate ABA responses during germination [43]. In our dataset, most PYR/PYL genes were downregulated in T1 and T2 compared to CK, except for one gene upregulated in T3 and another constitutively highly expressed in T1 and T3. Furthermore, SnRK2 kinases, central positive regulators in ABA signaling involved in stress response and dormancy control, showed significant differential expression during dormancy release [44, 45]. Taken together, these findings demonstrate that exogenous treatments inhibit the ABA signaling pathway in peach seeds, thereby relieving dormancy and promoting germination. Ethylene, a gaseous two-carbon hormone, fine-tunes seed dormancy release, germination, and early seedling development. It interacts antagonistically with ABA and promotes germination by enhancing endosperm weakening and radicle emergence [46]. In this study, EIN3 (the core transcription factor in ethylene signaling) was primarily downregulated in T1 but upregulated in T2 and T3, while seven ERF genes (downstream effectors) were consistently upregulated across all treatment groups. This dynamic expression pattern suggests that ethylene signaling contributes to dormancy release by selectively activating downstream targets, acting synergistically with other hormonal pathways. Overall, multiple phytohormones interact through intricate networks to coordinately regulate the transition from dormancy to germination.

In conclusion, this study systematically investigated the effects of exogenous substances on peach seed germination and endogenous hormone dynamics. By integrating transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses, we uncovered the molecular mechanisms underlying dormancy release. Results show that sodium hypochlorite, gibberellin + sodium fluoride, and sodium fluoride significantly improve germination rates, with sodium hypochlorite exhibiting the strongest effect. Exogenous treatments modulate the balance of key hormones, including auxin, gibberellin, abscisic acid, and cytokinin, thereby facilitating dormancy alleviation. Integrated multi-omics analysis highlights the critical importance of plant hormone signal transduction in regulating peach seed dormancy. Moreover, correlation analysis between DEGs and DAMs enabled the identification of candidate regulatory genes associated with dormancy release. These findings provide a solid theoretical basis for optimizing peach nursery practices (e.g., shortening dormancy duration and improving germination efficiency) and shed light on the regulatory mechanisms of seed dormancy in woody species.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and treatments

Peach seeds were used as the experimental materials. Healthy, disease-free seeds with uniform size and full plumpness were selected, and the kernels were carefully removed prior to treatment. Selected seeds were initially soaked in warm water at 55℃ with continuous stirring until cooled to room temperature. Surface moisture was then removed using sterilized gauze. Four treatment groups were established, with 200 seeds per group and three biological replicates: CK (distilled water, control), T1 (150 mg/L gibberellin + 0.25 mg/L sodium fluoride), T2 (60 mg/L sodium hypochlorite), and T3 (1 mg/L sodium fluoride). At each sampling time point, 10 seeds were randomly selected from each replicate (avoiding repeated sampling of the same seed), immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at -80 ℃ for subsequent analyses (endogenous hormone quantification, transcriptomics, and metabolomics). Seeds were immersed in respective treatments for 8 h, followed by cold stratification at 4℃ for 30 days. Sampling was conducted every 10 days during this period (designated as 4℃ 10 day, 4℃ 20 day, and 4℃ 30 day). After the third sampling, the temperature was increased to 12°C, and samples were collected every 3 days (12℃ 3 day, 12 ℃ 6 day). Following two samplings at 12℃, the temperature was further raised to 25°C, with subsequent sampling every 3 days (25℃ 3 day, 25℃ 6 day, and 25℃ 9 day). A total of nine sampling time points were included, one of which occurred before any treatment (pretreatment). Germination rate was assessed after 45 days of treatment. Immediately after collection, samples were flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80℃ for subsequent analysis of endogenous hormones, including ABA, GA, IAA, and CTK. The hormone profiling results, particularly the lowest ABA/GA₃ ratio, were used to determine the key conditions for targeted metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses.

Hormone analysis

Frozen seed tissue was ground into a fine powder using a mortar and pestle pre-cooled with liquid nitrogen. Approximately 0.2 g of ground tissue was weighed into a 50 mL centrifuge tube, and 10 mL of 80% (v/v) methanol was added. The mixture was then thoroughly vortexed and sonicated for 30 min at room temperature. After centrifugation, the supernatant was evaporated to dryness at 40 ℃ under vacuum. The residue was reconstituted in 1 mL of 80% methanol-0.1% acetic acid (50:50, v/v), filtered through a 0.22 μm nylon membrane. Endogenous hormones (IAA, GA₃, ABA, and 6-BA) were quantified using a Thermo Scientific U3000 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) equipped with an Acclaim™ 120 C18 column (4.6 mm × 250 mm, 5 μm). The mobile phase consisted of Phase A (0.1% v/v acetic acid in ultrapure water) and Phase B (acetonitrile). The column temperature was maintained at 35 ± 2℃, with a flow rate of 1 mL/min and an injection volume of 10 µL. Detection wavelengths were set as follows: 280 nm for IAA, 6-BA, and ABA; 208 nm for GA₃. The gradient elution program was as follows: 92% A/8% B (0–13 min), linear gradient to 65% A/35% B at 17 min, further to 54% A/46% B at 29 min, then to 10% A/90% B at 32 min, maintained until 35 min, and finally reverted to 92% A/8% B at 40 min.

Transcriptome analysis

Total RNA was extracted from frozen seed tissue using the CTAB-PBIOZOL reagent (BSC55S1, Hangzhou Bioer Technology Co., Ltd., China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA integrity and concentration were assessed using a Qubit fluorometer and a Qsep400 high-throughput biofragment analyzer. mRNA was enriched from total RNA using Oligo(dT)-coated magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), leveraging the poly(A) tail of eukaryotic mRNA. The enriched mRNA was fragmented into 200–300 bp segments using fragmentation buffer (Ambion, USA) at 94 ℃ for 8 min. First-strand cDNA was synthesized using random hexamer primers and M-MLV reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen, USA). Second-strand cDNA was generated by adding DNA polymerase I, dNTPs (dATP, dGTP, dCTP, dTTP), and RNase H. The double-stranded cDNA was purified using AMPure XP magnetic beads (Beckman Coulter, USA), followed by end repair (adding dA tails) and ligation of Illumina sequencing adapters. Fragment size selection was performed using AMPure XP beads, and the final cDNA library was amplified via PCR. Qualified libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform by Metware Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Raw reads were processed using fastp to remove adapter sequences, low-quality reads, and contaminants, yielding clean reads. Clean reads were aligned to the reference genome (Prunus persica v2.0) using HISAT2. Differential gene expression between treatment groups was analyzed using DESeq2, with P-values adjusted via the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Genes with |log₂ fold change| ≥ 1 and adjusted P-value < 0.05 were considered significantly differentially expressed. Functional enrichment analyses were performed using the hypergeometric test: KEGG pathways were evaluated at the pathway level, while GO terms were analyzed based on biological process, molecular function, and cellular component categories.

Metabolome analysis

Samples were lyophilized and ground into fine powder. Approximately 0.05 g of each sample was extracted with 1 mL of pre-cooled extraction solvent (methanol/water/formic acid, 15:4:1, v/v/v, containing internal standards). After vigorous shaking and ultrasonication, extracts were centrifuged, concentrated under vacuum, and reconstituted in 100 µL of 80% methanol/water. The resulting solutions were subjected to LC-MS/MS analysis using an ExionLC™ AD ultra-performance liquid chromatography (UPLC) system coupled with a QTRAP® mass spectrometer. Chromatographic separation was achieved on a C18 column with mobile phase A (ultrapure water + 0.04% acetic acid) and phase B (acetonitrile + 0.04% acetic acid) at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min and column temperature of 40℃. Electrospray ionization (ESI) was operated in both positive and negative modes, with ion source temperature set at 550℃, ion spray voltage at ± 5500 V (positive mode) and − 4500 V (negative mode), and curtain gas at 35 psi. Mass spectrometry data were processed using Analyst 1.6.3 and MultiQuant 3.0.3 software for peak detection, integration, and quantification. Standard curves were constructed by plotting the peak area ratios (analyte/internal standard) against the concentrations of ABA and GA standard solutions. The integrated peak area ratios of ABA and GA in all samples were substituted into the linear equations of the standard curves to calculate their relative concentrations. Finally, the actual contents of ABA and GA in the samples were derived using the formula. Furthermore, the metabolites were annotated and presented using the KEGG database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/compound/). Metabolites with fold change ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5 and statistically significant differences (P < 0.05) were defined as differentially accumulated metabolites.

Data statistics and analysis

Data were organized and preliminarily analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2016. One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0. Graphical visualization was carried out using GraphPad Prism 8. All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of three biological replicates.

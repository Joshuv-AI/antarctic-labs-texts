A predictive model for spontaneous preterm birth and delivery within 7 days in women with threatened preterm labor: a prospective study integrating cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1, and clinical parameters
Authors: Jie Guo, Lingli Zhong, Xiaohong Xie, Huifang Xiong, Yuan Chen, Liangqin Wang, Xiaomin Lai, Li Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo develop and internally validate predictive models for spontaneous preterm birth (sPTB) in women with threatened preterm labor (TPL).MethodsThis prospective observational study included 264 women with TPL between 24 + 0 and 36 + 0 weeks of gestation. Candidate predictors available at clinical assessment included demographic, obstetric, sonographic, inflammatory, and cervicovaginal biomarker variables. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with tenfold cross-validation and the one-standard-error criterion was used for variable selection. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic analysis, calibration, bootstrap validation, and decision curve analysis.ResultsEighty-four women (31.8%) experienced sPTB before 37 weeks, and 54 (20.5%) experienced sPTB within 7 days after sampling. Five predictors were retained in both final models: cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1 concentration, C-reactive protein, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting. For sPTB before 37 weeks, the apparent AUC was 0.845 (95% CI 0.788–0.903), and the optimism-corrected AUC was 0.830. For 7-day sPTB, the apparent AUC was 0.939 (95% CI 0.900–0.978), and the optimism-corrected AUC was 0.929. The integrated models showed higher discrimination than cervical length alone, PAMG-1 alone, or cervical length plus PAMG-1.ConclusionIntegrated models combining cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1, C-reactive protein, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting showed promising performance for predicting sPTB in women with TPL. Given the modest sample size and internal validation only, these findings should be considered preliminary and require external validation before clinical implementation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00404-026-08477-1.

What does this study add to the clinical work

In women with threatened preterm labor, an integrated model combining cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1, C-reactive protein, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting may help stratify the risk of spontaneous preterm birth before 37 weeks and within 7 days after sampling. the findings are promising but require external validation before routine clinical use.

Introduction

Preterm birth (PTB) remains a major cause of perinatal mortality and long-term morbidity worldwide, with more than 13 million infants born preterm each year and approximately 70% classified as spontaneous preterm birth (sPTB)[1, 2]​. Preterm infants are at increased risk of both short- and long-term complications, including respiratory, neurological, and metabolic sequelae[3, 4].

Threatened preterm labor (TPL) is commonly diagnosed in women with regular uterine contractions and cervical change, yet only a minority deliver within 7 days[5]. This creates a clinical dilemma: interventions such as hospitalization, tocolysis, and antenatal corticosteroids are important for high-risk women but may be overused in those unlikely to deliver imminently[6–8].

Current risk assessment relies on clinical factors, transvaginal cervical length, and cervicovaginal biomarkers. Clinical factors alone have limited predictive value[9, 10], and cervical length, although well established, may be insufficient when used in isolation in symptomatic women​[11, 12]. Biomarkers such as fetal fibronectin, phosphorylated insulin-like growth factor-binding protein-1, and placental alpha-microglobulin-1 (PAMG-1) have therefore been investigated to improve short-term prediction[13, 14].

Placental alpha microglobulin-1 (PAMG-1), originally used for the diagnosis of suspected preterm prelabor rupture of membranes, has also shown predictive value for sPTB and delivery within 7 days[15–19].

Given the heterogeneity of sPTB, prediction based on a single marker is unlikely to fully capture individual risk. We therefore aimed to develop and internally validate prediction models for sPTB before 37 weeks and sPTB within 7 days after sampling in women with TPL, integrating quantitative PAMG-1 with clinically available predictors at presentation.

Methods

Study design

This prospective observational study was conducted at a tertiary hospital in southern China between 2022 and 2024. The study aimed to develop and internally validate prediction models for sPTB before 37 weeks and sPTB within 7 days after sampling in women with threatened preterm labor (TPL). Candidate predictors were restricted to variables available at the time of clinical assessment.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Longyan First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University, China (Approval No. LYREC2022-024–01), and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Participants

Pregnant women with symptoms of TPL were consecutively screened for eligibility. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) singleton pregnancy; (2) gestational age between 24 + 0 and 36 + 0 weeks; (3) absence of fetal or placental abnormalities on ultrasound; (4) intact fetal membranes; and (5) cervical dilation ≤ 2 cm.

Women were excluded before enrollment if they had twin pregnancy, preterm premature rupture of membranes, duplicate enrollment, cervical dilation > 2 cm, or suspected placenta previa or heavy vaginal bleeding. Heavy vaginal bleeding was defined as bleeding requiring urgent obstetric evaluation, raising concern for placenta previa or placental abruption, or precluding safe cervical sampling or transvaginal cervical length measurement. Women were further excluded from the final analysis if they were lost to follow-up, had incomplete clinical data, or experienced medically indicated preterm birth.

Data collection and outcome definitions

At enrollment, demographic data, obstetric history, comorbidities, clinical findings, laboratory markers, quantitative cervicovaginal PAMG-1 concentration, and transvaginal cervical length were recorded. Uterine contraction frequency was categorized as ≤ 5, 6–11, or ≥ 12 contractions per hour. Vaginal bleeding referred to mild bleeding or spotting at presentation, after exclusion of heavy bleeding.

All participants were followed until delivery. The primary outcome was sPTB before 37 weeks, and the secondary outcome was sPTB within 7 days after sampling and before 37 weeks. Medically indicated preterm births were excluded, and outcomes were ascertained from hospital records.

PAMG-1 testing

Cervicovaginal secretions were collected using sterile swabs from the posterior fornix and external cervical os, with approximately 10 s of sampling at each site. Samples were eluted in phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.4), centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 15 min, and stored at − 80 °C until analysis. PAMG-1 concentrations were measured using a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit (Jiangsu Meimian Industry, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Cervical length measurement

Cervical length was measured by transvaginal ultrasound using a Philips EPIQ 5 system (Philips Medical Systems, Bothell, WA, USA). Measurements were performed with an empty bladder in the sagittal plane, with clear visualization of the internal and external os. Three measurements were obtained, and the shortest value was used for analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.5.0. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Pearson’s chi-squared test, or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Missing data were assessed before model development. The main analysis used a complete-case cohort after excluding women with loss to follow-up, missing outcome data, or missing key candidate predictors. Because all outcomes and candidate predictors were complete in the final analytic cohort, multiple imputation was not performed (Supplementary Table S1).

Candidate predictors were prespecified based on clinical relevance and previous literature. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression with tenfold cross-validation was used for variable selection, with λ.1se chosen to obtain a parsimonious model. Predictors with non-zero coefficients at λ.1se were entered into separate multivariable logistic regression models for the primary and secondary outcomes. Odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals were reported, and nomograms were constructed for individualized risk estimation.

Model performance was evaluated by discrimination, calibration, internal validation, and decision curve analysis. Discrimination was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Internal validation was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamples to estimate optimism-corrected AUCs and calibration indices. Calibration was assessed using calibration curves, calibration slope, calibration intercept, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. Apparent calibration slope and intercept were derived from the final model in the development cohort, whereas optimism-corrected calibration indices and their 95% confidence intervals were derived from the bootstrap distribution. Clinical utility was evaluated by decision curve analysis against treat-all and treat-none strategies. To assess incremental predictive value, AUCs of the integrated models were compared with those of cervical length alone, PAMG-1 alone, and cervical length plus PAMG-1 models using DeLong tests.

For the 7-day outcome, sparse data were observed in the highest uterine contraction frequency category. Therefore, Firth penalized logistic regression was performed as a sensitivity analysis.

All statistical analyses, model construction, and visualization were performed using the following R packages: gtsummary, glmnet, rms, pROC, rmda, logistf, and ggplot2.

Thirty-four were excluded before enrollment because of twin pregnancy, preterm prelabor rupture of membranes, duplicate enrollment, cervical dilation > 2 cm, or suspected placenta previa/heavy vaginal bleeding. Of the 289 eligible women, 25 were excluded because of loss to follow-up or incomplete clinical data (n = 13) or medically indicated preterm birth (n = 12). Predictor selection and model development

Candidate predictors were selected a priori based on clinical relevance and evidence from previous studies. The following variables were considered as candidate predictors: maternal age, educational level, body mass index (BMI), history of preterm birth, PAMG-1 concentration, cervical length, uterine contraction frequency, vaginal bleeding, C-reactive protein (CRP), and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM). Variable selection was performed using LASSO logistic regression with tenfold cross-validation. To improve model parsimony and reduce overfitting, the penalty parameter was selected using the one-standard-error criterion. For the primary outcome, five predictors had non-zero coefficients at λ.1se and were retained for final model development: cervical length, PAMG-1 concentration, CRP, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting (Supplementary Fig. S1). The same strategy was applied to the secondary outcome of sPTB within 7 days after sampling. The retained non-zero coefficients corresponded to the same five clinical predictors, with uterine contraction frequency represented by two indicator terms because it was modeled as a categorical variable (Supplementary Fig. S2).

Each 1-mm increase in cervical length was associated with lower odds of sPTB before 37 weeks (OR = 0.90, 95% CI 0.87–0.94; P < 0.001). Higher PAMG-1 concentration and CRP level were also associated with increased odds of sPTB (OR = 1.05, 95% CI 1.02–1.07; P < 0.001 and OR = 1.02, 95% CI 1.00–1.04; P = 0.037, respectively). Compared with women with ≤ 5 uterine contractions per hour, those with 6–11 contractions per hour had higher odds of sPTB (OR = 2.78, 95% CI 1.37–5.85; P = 0.005), and those with ≥ 12 contractions per hour had a further increase in risk (OR = 9.04, 95% CI 2.23–40.8; P = 0.003). Mild vaginal bleeding or spotting was also independently associated with sPTB before 37 weeks (OR = 3.79, 95% CI 1.81–8.14; P < 0.001).

The performance of the primary model is summarized in Table 4 and illustrated in Fig. 2. The model showed good discrimination, with an apparent AUC of 0.845 (95% CI 0.788–0.903) and an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.830 after 1000 bootstrap resamples. The mean optimism was 0.015, suggesting limited overfitting. The apparent calibration slope was 1.000, and the optimism-corrected calibration slope was 0.873. The apparent and optimism-corrected calibration intercepts were 0.000 and 0.007, respectively. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test did not indicate significant lack of fit. Sensitivity analysis

Because sparse data were observed in the highest contraction-frequency category for the 7-day endpoint, Firth penalized logistic regression was performed as a sensitivity analysis. The results were directionally consistent with those of the conventional logistic regression model, supporting the robustness of the secondary model (Supplementary Table S2).

Incremental value of the integrated models

In additional comparative analyses, the integrated model showed higher discrimination than cervical length alone, PAMG-1 alone, and cervical length plus PAMG-1 for both outcomes (Supplementary Table S3). For sPTB before 37 weeks, the AUC increased from 0.738 for cervical length alone and 0.675 for PAMG-1 alone to 0.845 for the integrated model. For 7-day sPTB, the corresponding AUCs were 0.765, 0.713, and 0.939. DeLong tests showed statistically significant differences between each simpler model and the integrated model.

Based on the final multivariable logistic regression models, nomograms were constructed to provide individualized risk estimates for both outcomes (Fig. 4). The primary nomogram estimated the probability of sPTB before 37 weeks, whereas the secondary nomogram estimated the probability of sPTB within 7 days after sampling and before 37 weeks. Both nomograms incorporated cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1 concentration, CRP, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting. Discussion

This prospective study developed and internally validated two prediction models for sPTB in women with TPL: sPTB before 37 weeks and sPTB within 7 days after sampling. Both models incorporated cervical length, quantitative cervicovaginal PAMG-1 concentration, CRP, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting. The primary model showed good discrimination and acceptable calibration, whereas the 7-day model showed excellent apparent discrimination but should be interpreted cautiously given the limited number of events and internal validation only. These findings suggest that integrating sonographic, biochemical, and clinical parameters may improve risk stratification in symptomatic women, pending external validation.

Although demographic characteristics, obstetric history, uterine anomalies, and infections are associated with preterm birth, their predictive accuracy is limited[20–22]. This limitation is clinically important because most symptomatic women do not deliver within 7 days and many ultimately reach term[16, 23]. More precise prediction models are therefore needed to reduce overtreatment and support targeted perinatal care.

Our findings support the predictive value of PAMG-1 in women with TPL, consistent with previous studies[19, 24]. A systematic review and meta-analysis of the PartoSure PAMG-1 test in symptomatic women reported high specificity but moderate sensitivity for predicting delivery within 7 and 14 days[18], while recent evidence suggests that quantitative PAMG-1 may provide dose-related risk stratification[25]. Our study extends this evidence by incorporating quantitative PAMG-1 into multivariable models with cervical length, CRP, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting. This approach provides individualized risk estimates for both sPTB before 37 weeks and sPTB within 7 days, rather than relying on a qualitative biomarker result or a single predictor alone. Comparative AUC analyses showed that the integrated models had higher discrimination than cervical length alone, PAMG-1 alone, or cervical length plus PAMG-1. This supports the added value of a multivariable approach, although the finding requires confirmation in external cohorts.

Cervical shortening is an important predictor of preterm birth and reflects cervical remodeling before labor onset[26–31]. We modeled cervical length as a continuous variable rather than using a fixed cutoff, allowing more individualized risk estimation in symptomatic women. Combining cervical length with biochemical and clinical predictors may be more informative than relying on cervical length alone.

Systemic maternal inflammation is implicated in the pathogenesis of sPTB[32–35]. In our cohort, WBC count, neutrophil ratio, and CRP levels were higher among women who experienced sPTB, and CRP was retained in the final models. This supports the inclusion of inflammatory markers in risk prediction, although CRP is nonspecific and cannot identify the underlying inflammatory pathway.

PTB is a heterogeneous syndrome rather than a single condition defined only by gestational age. In line with the etiologically based taxonomy proposed by Villar et al.[36], our cohort represents a clinically defined subgroup of women with symptomatic TPL, singleton pregnancy, intact membranes, and no medically indicated PTB. The retained predictors may reflect overlapping pathways, including cervical remodeling, parturition activation, inflammatory or decidual–membrane interface changes, and bleeding-related or cervicoplacental components. Therefore, the models should be interpreted as phenotype-informed risk stratification tools for symptomatic TPL, rather than universal models for all forms of PTB.

The nomograms provide individualized risk estimates by integrating cervical, biochemical, inflammatory, and clinical features. DCA suggested potential clinical utility across a range of threshold probabilities. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously because the models were developed and internally validated in a single-center cohort. The nomograms should support, rather than replace, clinical judgment and should not be used for routine management before external validation.

Strengths of this study include its prospective design, quantitative PAMG-1 measurement, complete reporting of both outcomes, and assessment of discrimination, calibration, internal validation, and clinical utility. Limitations include the single-center tertiary setting, modest sample size, lack of external validation, and incomplete etiologically based PTB phenotyping. Despite bootstrap internal validation, residual optimism cannot be excluded, particularly for the 7-day model, given the limited number of events, very high apparent AUC, sparse data in the highest contraction-frequency category, and wide confidence intervals for some predictors. The 7-day model should therefore be considered preliminary until externally validated in independent multicenter cohorts.

Conclusion

In this single-center prospective cohort of women with TPL, integrated models combining cervical length, quantitative PAMG-1 concentration, CRP, uterine contraction frequency, and mild vaginal bleeding or spotting showed promising performance for predicting sPTB before 37 weeks and sPTB within 7 days after sampling. However, given the modest sample size and internal validation only, these findings should be considered preliminary and require external multicenter validation before clinical implementation.

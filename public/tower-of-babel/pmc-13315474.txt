Knowledge mapping of ⁹⁰Y transarterial radioembolization combined with immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma: a bibliometric analysis
Authors: Song-Nian He, Chu-Hui Zeng, Rui-Jie Du, Yi-Xuan Yang, Hai-Dong Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a highly aggressive malignancy with substantial mortality globally. The synergistic potential between immunotherapy and yttrium-90 transarterial radioembolization (⁹⁰Y-TARE) has stimulated extensive research into HCC combined therapeutic strategies. However, a comprehensive bibliometric analysis elucidating the evolutionary trajectory, research trends, and collaborative networks within this field remains absent. Unlike conventional systematic reviews, which are inherently subjective and lack the capacity for large-scale quantitative synthesis, bibliometric analysis offers a robust, data-driven approach to construct a holistic knowledge atlas. A total of 143 relevant publications were extracted from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database, spanning 1 January 2003 to 24 October 2025. Utilizing VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the R package bibliometrix, a multidimensional analysis encompassing publication volume, journal impact, national/contributions, institutional collaborations, authorship networks, co-citation patterns, and keyword clustering was conducted. From 2015 to 2025, the number of publications in this field has steadily increased. The United States was the dominant contributor. Global institutional collaboration was limited, highlighting the need for multinational cooperation. Co-citation and keyword analyses revealed three research frontiers: mechanisms of radio-immunologic synergy, optimization of treatment sequencing and dosimetry, and predictive biomarker development. Future research requires more phase III trials, incorporating multi-omics technologies and individualized dosimetry models to validate survival benefits and explore more precise immuno-combination strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05154-2.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), a primary liver malignancy is the third leading cause of global cancer mortality [1–4]. In recent years, with the continuous advancement of research on tumor immune microenvironment, immunotherapy has opened new avenues for the treatment of advanced HCC [5]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have been used, as a rapidly developing immunotherapy, and changed the treatment landscape for HCC [6]. Numerous studies have found that the efficacy of ICIs monotherapy has not met expectations [7–9], and immune evasion persists [10, 11].

Yttrium-90 transarterial radioembolization (⁹⁰Y-TARE) delivers localized tumoricidal radiation via intra-arterial microspheres, sparing functional parenchyma [12]. The potential synergistic mechanisms of combined therapy with ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs in HCC have been extensively studied [13]. On this basis, comprehensive studies have further explored the clinical efficacy and safety of the combination therapy of ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs in HCC [14, 15]. Given immunotherapy’s expanding role in hepatic malignancies, combining ⁹⁰Y-TARE with immunotherapy, primarily based on ICIs, has attracted considerable research interest.

This surge in research, however, creates macro-level challenges: an overwhelming volume of publications, an opaque landscape of key contributors, and an intricate knowledge of architecture. Conventional reviews lack objectivity and quantitative power to reveal the field’s holistic dynamics. Bibliometrics, as such, emerges as an essential tool for constructing a systematic “knowledge atlas” to bridge this gap [16]. Building on—and moving beyond—a previous bibliometric study that merely acknowledged the potential of ⁹⁰Y-TARE-immunotherapy integration without substantive exploration [17], our work aims to provide a definitive bibliometric profile of ICIs-based combination therapy in HCC. We seek to construct its academic network map, thereby elucidating developmental pathways, highlighting research opportunities, and promoting scientific collaboration.

Materials and methods

Data sources and search strategies

The literature for this study was sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) [18]. To mitigate potential artifacts arising from database updates, all retrieval and extraction procedures were completed within a single calendar day (24 October 2025). The temporal scope encompassed publications from 1 January 2003 to 24 October 2025. Detailed search strategies are provided in Supplementary Material 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The literature selection conformed to the pre-defined criteria is provided in Supplementary Material 2. After the initial screening by document type and language, the research team members Song-Nian He and Chu-Hui Zeng carefully reviewed and assessed the titles and abstracts of all retrieved records. Any disputed publications were submitted to Hai-Dong Zhu for the final decision. All literature related to the application of ⁹⁰Y-TARE combined with immunotherapy in HCC was included in the analysis, encompassing clinical studies, preclinical studies, mechanistic studies, systematic reviews, and narrative reviews, covering multiple aspects such as the efficacy and safety of combination therapy, immunomodulatory mechanisms, treatment sequencing and timing, dosimetry optimization, predictive biomarker development, patient selection, and novel immuno‑combination strategies. Data analysis

This study integrated three established bibliometric tools—VOSviewer (version 1.6.19), CiteSpace (version 6.1.R6), and the Bibliometrix package in R (version 4.4.0)—to ensure methodological robustness and a holistic data interpretation. This integrated strategy, based on the principle of triangulation, utilizes their functional complementarity, thereby strengthening the conclusion validity. The parameter settings for VOSviewer and CiteSpace are provided in Supplementary Material 3. VOSviewer was employed to construct collaboration networks among countries, institutions, and authors, alongside keyword co-occurrence mapping. CiteSpace facilitated dual map overlay analysis of journal distributions and performed clustering/emergence detection on co-cited references and keywords. Complementary analyses were conducted via the bibliometrix package in R. Bubble charts visualizing conceptual relationships were likewise generated in R.

Bibliometric indicators were quantified using Microsoft Office Excel 2019 and subsequently generated line graphs and bar charts for temporal trend visualization. Network topology optimizations were applied to VOSviewer outputs using Pajek (version 6.01).

Statistical analysis

A Pearson correlation analysis and a simple linear regression model were performed in R. Prior to these analyses, the normality of annual citation counts (2003–2024) was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Outlier detection was carried out using studentized residuals and Cook’s distance, with conventional thresholds of |studentized residual| > 2 and Cook’s D > 4/n (where n is the number of years). Given the non‑normality of the data, a sensitivity analysis using Spearman’s rank correlation was additionally conducted to confirm the robustness of the Pearson correlation result. Data from the incomplete year 2025 (as of October 24, 2025) were excluded from the regression model because the annual count does not represent a full 12‑month period; only complete calendar years (2003–2024) were included in the trend analysis. All statistical tests were two‑sided, and a p‑value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The detailed results of the pre‑analysis checks are presented in Supplementary Material 4.

Results

Annual publication output

A high and positive Pearson correlation (r = 0.769; p < 0.001) was found between the number of publications and year. The development of the field exhibited a distinct two-phase pattern: research activity was relatively limited during the first decade (2003–2013), with annual publications remaining at low levels; However, from 2014 onward, research output entered a phase of rapid acceleration, peaking at 33 publications in 2023 (Fig. 2A). To minimize statistical systematic bias, the linear regression analysis incorporated only data from the period 2003–2024. The annual publication trend analysis (Fig. 2B) revealed a highly significant growth trajectory in this field from 2003 to 2024 (β = 1.06, R²=0.5907, p < 0.001). This observation reflects the growing research interest over the past decade in combining ⁹⁰Y-TARE with immunotherapy for the treatment of HCC.

National publication volume and collaboration

In Table 1, the United States led in publications. The United States had the highest total link strength (TLS = 35) and mediator centrality (0.36), indicating strong collaboration. The United States’ position as a central hub in the global research network, characterized by its high publication output and centrality, is directly bolstered by its robust funding system and clear national strategic initiatives, which systematically support large-scale, multi-institutional research programs. Despite high publication volume, China’s TLS (16) was lower than Germany and England. With its high HCC incidence, China’s integration into the global research network is essential to leverage its unique clinical data for the advancement of the field [19]. Among countries with > 10 publications (Fig. 3A), Germany had the highest average cited frequency per paper (32.88), followed by the United States (32.65). The collaboration network (Fig. 3B) shows strong United States–Europe connections (Italy/Germany/France). Density mapping (Fig. 3C) identifies the United States, China, France, and Germany as key research hubs warranting enhanced collaboration.

Institutional publications and collaborations

Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC) has published the most papers in this field (Table 2). MSKCC has elucidated, through multiple prospective studies, how ⁹⁰Y-TARE systemically modulates both the systemic and local tumor immune microenvironment [20]. This work is complemented by retrospective analyses that robustly demonstrate the significant survival benefit of combination therapy in HCC [21]. Our visualization of the collaborative network among the United States institutions reveals that MSKCC maintains strong ties with the central institutions of multiple collaborative clusters (Supplementary Fig. S1). As a recognized leader in this field, the institution is poised to further deepen and expand its collaborative network (Supplementary Table 1). Several clusters, led by MSKCC, Mayo Clinic, Imperial College London, and Clínica Universidad de Navarra, exhibit closer collaborative relationships with one another (Fig. 4A and B). The high publication output from the United States stems from the contributions of its numerous productive institutions, with 9 out of the top 10 institutions by publication count being based in the United States. The overall collaborative network in the United States, comprising several distinct clusters of leading research institutions, would benefit from strengthened inter-cluster linkages.

Discussion

General information

The marked increase in annual publications and citations since 2015 reflects growing scientific interest. The United States maintained research leadership, while China—the second most productive country—exhibited limited international collaboration. China’s relatively limited international collaboration in this field may stem from its heavy concentration of policy resources on domestic health system development—particularly in healthcare delivery and medical technologies—while international cooperation has not been assigned high priority [25]. Given China’s high HCC burden, enhanced global cooperation is essential for therapeutic innovation. MSKCC demonstrates clear leadership in this field. Author co-citation mapping established Erinjeri JP, Sangro B, and Sze DY as key contributors, with Erinjeri JP’s 2019 conceptual framework positioning interventional oncology–immunotherapy integration as transformative in oncology [26].

Hotspots and frontiers

Based on the annual publication trends in this field, the top 15 most cited papers were re-screened. Highly cited publications after 2015 have concentrated primarily on the clinical efficacy of combination therapies and the underlying principles of synergistic mechanisms, while also addressing future therapeutic prospects for other types of liver cancer. “Immune checkpoint inhibitors” appeared in both the keyword clustering analysis and the burst analysis. This suggests that the combination therapy of ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs-based immunotherapy holds significant promise in this field. In the keyword co-occurrence analysis conducted in Vosviewer, the red cluster indicates the potential for combining various ICIs with ⁹⁰Y-TARE therapy, which requires further research to validate its clinical efficacy and safety. By further exploring the clusters in Vosviewer’s keyword co-occurrence analysis, the node “dosimetry” was identified in the yellow cluster. Given the characteristics of ⁹⁰Y-TARE, the combination of ⁹⁰Y-TARE with ICIs-based immunotherapy requires personalized design of ⁹⁰Y-TARE -related dose issues. Personalized design involves patient selection and the choice of timing-dose optimization strategies.

Therapeutic efficacy and safety

Building upon the macro-level research trends identified in the keyword analysis, the specific keywords “efficacy” and “survival” emerge as the central anchors for evaluating the clinical value of these evolving strategies. The keyword “first-line treatment”, which holds a prominent position in both the co-occurrence network and keyword burst analysis, reflects a pivotal evolution in the research landscape. Current investigations extend beyond merely exploring the feasibility of combination therapies to rigorously evaluating their comparative effectiveness in clinical settings. Retrospective analyses and phase II clinical trials have demonstrated encouraging efficacy outcomes, including improved overall survival [21] (e.g., 19.8 vs. 9.5 months compared to ICIs monotherapy), objective response rates surpassing historical benchmarks for monotherapy [27], and favorable safety profiles [28]. Dual-ICIs synergies observed in CheckMate 040 (nivolumab-ipilimumab) [29] extend to ⁹⁰Y-TARE combinations, with retrospective analyses showing significant overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) improvements versus ⁹⁰Y-TARE alone (OS: not reached vs. 14 months; PFS: 11.3 vs. 5.8 months) [30]. Emerging strategies like the “radioembolization-dual immunotherapy” sequence (NCT05809869; tremelimumab+durvalumab) warrant further investigation.

Immunomodulatory mechanisms

The thematic map reveals a distinct cluster of specialized keywords, including “cancer vaccine,” “induced killer-cells,” and “tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes,” which populate a parallel and foundational research frontier dedicated to elucidating the mechanistic basis of combination therapies. As demonstrated in preclinical models, the ionizing radiation delivered by ⁹⁰Y-TARE for HCC elicits mild inflammation within the tumor microenvironment, leading to the upregulation of chemokines and cytokines [31, 32]. The abscopal effect—regression of non-irradiated metastases—exemplifies radiation’s systemic immunologic impact [33]. However, a comprehensive elucidation of how these changes influence local and systemic immune function in HCC remains lacking. To address this, studies have analyzed the immune signatures of HCC tissues resected following ⁹⁰Y-TARE treatment. Results indicate that ⁹⁰Y-TARE specifically remodels the immune microenvironment of HCC: treated tumors exhibit enriched infiltration of cytotoxic lymphocytes (i.e., CD8+ T cells, CD56+ natural killer [NK] cells, CD8+CD56+ NKT cells), granzyme B upregulation, regulatory T cell (Treg) depletion, and CXCL9-mediated CD8+ T/NK cell recruitment via CXCR3 [34–36]. The remodeling of the HCC immune microenvironment by ⁹⁰Y-TARE provides a multifaceted mechanistic explanation for the synergy between ⁹⁰Y-TARE and immunotherapy. Patel et al. demonstrated low-dose, pan-tumoral-targeted ⁹⁰Y radiation can safely and effectively convert immunologically “cold” tumors, which are initially insensitive to ICIs, into an ICIs-responsive state, significantly enhancing complete response rates and inducing immunologic memory [36]. Chew et al. observed upregulated expression of PD-1 and Tim-3 in patients with clinical response at 3 months after ⁹⁰Y-TARE, suggesting that subsequent administration of a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor might yield improved outcomes [37]. Interestingly, relevant studies have also observed that different radiation doses administered during ⁹⁰Y-TARE may exert differential effects on the type and extent of immune infiltration, with lower doses potentially exhibiting stronger immunogenicity [38].

The immunological paradox of ⁹⁰Y-TARE

While robust mechanistic data exists, the clinical evidence for consistent and meaningful abscopal responses in HCC patients receiving ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs remains limited [39]. The conversion of a localized “hot” immune response into a potent, systemic antitumor effect is notoriously inefficient in HCC. Nonetheless, the abscopal effect represents the most direct and compelling evidence that localized treatments can elicit a systemic antitumor immune response. The study by Cai et al. provided clear demonstration in a murine model that a ⁹⁰Y-labeled gold nanoparticle depot (NPD), combined with anti-PD-L1 antibodies, significantly inhibited the growth of distant non-irradiated tumors and revealed underlying immunological mechanisms [40]. Future research into the abscopal effect in HCC holds considerable promise.

Despite the compelling preclinical rationale for ⁹⁰Y-TARE, a significant gap persists between the proposed mechanisms and the clinical outcomes. The complexity of the HCC tumor immune microenvironment may lead to paradoxical responses during treatment with ⁹⁰Y-TARE and immunotherapy [41]. Multiple formidable obstacles underlie the limitations identified in these studies: the profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment characteristic of advanced HCC [42], potential shortcomings in the timing or regimen of combination therapy [39], and the dual challenges of ⁹⁰Y-TARE-induced lymphodepletion and altered immune cell trafficking [43]. When devising combination treatment strategies, the potential for ⁹⁰Y-TARE to exert immunosuppressive effects must be acknowledged. Theoretically, localized low dose ⁹⁰Y radiation can improve the tumor immune microenvironment of HCC through multiple mechanisms, thereby establishing a favorable foundation for subsequent immunotherapy. However, in actual therapeutic settings, achieving adequate tumor control typically requires the administration of higher radiation doses, which in turn leads to more pronounced radiation‑induced lymphocyte depletion [44, 45]. Studies have shown that lymphopenia (≤ 1 × 10³/µL) frequently occurring at early stages (1, 3, and 6 months) after ⁹⁰Y-TARE is an independent prognostic marker associated with significantly poorer OS [46]. It is noteworthy that Zhan et al. did not identify a statistically significant difference in the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) among patients receiving combined ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs [24]. Therefore, the prognostic relevance of lymphocyte counts before and after ⁹⁰Y-TARE warrants further investigation to elucidate its clinical implications. On this basis, preventing treatment-related lymphopenia associated with ⁹⁰Y-TARE may help preserve immunocompetence, thereby establishing a foundation for effective combination immunotherapy.

⁹⁰Y-TARE dosimetry

Spatial heterogeneity in radiation dose distribution within tumors [47, 48], resulting from microsphere gradients, differentially modulates the tumor immune microenvironment [49]. While high-dose irradiation is required for tumor control, it may upregulate 3’ repair exonuclease 1 (TREX1), thereby inhibiting the STING-IFN pathway [50, 51]. This underscores the necessity for precise, individualized dosimetry that defines both tumor and non-tumoral liver doses—a critical consideration given the link between non-tumoral liver dose and lymphopenia [43].

Transitioning from the traditional partition model, which fails to account for intra- and inter-lesional heterogeneity [52, 53], voxel-based dosimetry calculates absorbed dose per voxel [52, 54], revealing intraparenchymal dose gradients [55–58]. This approach has proven superior in predicting hepatotoxicity and is crucial for evaluating ⁹⁰Y-TARE outcomes. Crucially [59], conventional models can mask the fact that focal healthy tissue may receive suprathreshold doses, a phenomenon with potential implications for concomitant immunotherapy efficacy [52]. Thus, optimizing personalized dosimetry is key to balancing anti-improvement with immune modulation [60].

Therapeutic sequencing and timing

Determining optimal sequencing is critical to balance efficacy and toxicity. Most trials prioritize ⁹⁰Y-TARE before adjuvant immunotherapy Studies have shown that ⁹⁰Y-TARE induces significant short-term (peak at 1 month) immune activation in HCC patients. However, this effect rapidly diminishes (declining to baseline levels within 3–6 months) and fails to translate into long-term immune memory [61]. Therefore, this study proposes a novel sequential combination strategy by concurrently administering PD-1/LAG3 dual ICIs 1 month after ⁹⁰Y-TARE (during the peak immune activation period), aiming to reverse T cell exhaustion and convert short-term immune effects into long-term antitumor immune memory. Kang et al. established a four-compartment differential equation model, quantifying for the first time the synergistic effects of ⁹⁰Y-TARE combined with durvalumab in the treatment of HCC. The study revealed that concurrent therapy (interval ≤ 30 days) and sequential therapy (RT followed by immunotherapy, interval ≤ 60 days) maximize treatment efficacy [62]. In a 2024 study by Mejait et al., a pioneering “sandwich” dosing strategy (three ⁹⁰Y-TARE sessions followed by at least six atezolizumab+bevacizumab infusions) was employed, confirming that this sequential regimen significantly improves survival in patients with unresectable HCC without increasing adverse reactions [30]. NCT05809869 explores dual-ICI priming (tremelimumab+durvalumab) before ⁹⁰Y-TARE—an emerging paradigm. Future investigations into treatment sequencing could explore the use of ⁹⁰Y-TARE as an adjunctive therapy, focusing on identifying an immunomodulatory dosing regimen that minimizes immunosuppression.

Patient selection for combination therapy

Current research focuses on advanced HCC (BCLC C) [24, 63]. In subgroup analysis, non-metastatic HCC patients had a higher objective response rate (ORR). Both the Spanish NASIR-HCC study and the global multicenter CA209-678 trial demonstrated significant efficacy of sequential ⁹⁰Y-TARE followed by nivolumab in advanced HCC patients without extrahepatic metastasis, with marked tumor shrinkage or downstaging providing an opportunity for subsequent conversion to curative resection [64]. Therefore, for some intermediate-to-advanced patients with lower tumor burden and limited vascular invasion, it is conceivable that ICIs could serve as an adjuvant therapy to the primary therapy, ⁹⁰Y-TARE.

Our results indicate that the geographical distribution of core research efforts (concentrated in the US and Europe) differs from the global etiological spectrum of HCC (where viral hepatitis predominates in regions such as China, while the proportion of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis is rising in Western populations). This disparity, together with in-depth discussions on the “synergistic effects” of combination therapy in highly cited literature, indirectly suggests that HCC driven by distinct etiologies may exhibit differential responses to ⁹⁰Y-TARE combined with immunotherapy [65–67]. In a retrospective cohort study, no statistically significant difference in efficacy was observed for ⁹⁰Y-TARE between HCC associated with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)/metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) and HCC related to other etiologies [68]. Patients with non‑alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)-related HCC receiving ⁹⁰Y-TARE showed non‑inferior survival benefit and safety compared to those with HBV‑related HCC [69]. Although NAFLD patients had more metabolic comorbidities, these comorbidities did not limit the application of ⁹⁰Y-TARE. ⁹⁰Y-TARE may serve as an effective alternative for NAFLD‑HCC patients who are not suitable for systemic therapy. Broader immunotherapy research has unequivocally demonstrated that NASH-HCC, due to its unique immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, exhibits a significantly lower response rate to ICIs monotherapy than its viral counterpart [68]. Consequently, emerging research trends are seeking to validate whether the ⁹⁰Y-TARE-ICIs combination can specifically overcome the inherent immunotherapy resistance in NASH-HCC. Currently, there remains a paucity of direct comparative data on the efficacy of combination therapy between NASH/MAFLD related HCC and HCC of other etiologies. Therefore, future studies should prioritize etiologically stratified analyses to determine whether the synergistic benefit of ⁹⁰Y‑TARE combined with ICIs is consistent across different HCC etiologies. Such investigations are essential for optimizing patient selection and advancing personalized immuno‑combination strategies.

Current research frontiers are focused on developing predictive models that integrate radiomic, dosiomic, transcriptomic, and biomarker data to accurately forecast responses to ⁹⁰Y-TARE and identify patient subgroups most likely to benefit from its combination with immunotherapy [70, 71]. Transcriptomic profiling identifies tumors with inflamed microenvironments responsive to adjuvant ICIs. HCC transcriptomic subtypes associated with early recurrence after surgical resection (such as Hoshida S1 type) typically have a more immune-inflammatory microenvironment (responsive to ICIs treatment) [72, 73]. Although ⁹⁰Y-TARE was not involved, the biological characteristics of this tumor can serve as a theoretical basis for combination therapy, helping to identify the HCC stages that can benefit most from the combination strategy. Current research primarily utilizes radiomic features extracted from pre-treatment medical images in conjunction with machine learning models to predict the efficacy of ⁹⁰Y-TARE [74, 75]. The integration of clinical parameters can further enhance the performance of these predictive models [76]. Emerging research directions extend to radiomic and dosiomic analyses based on post-treatment nuclear medicine imaging [77]. Specifically, dosiomics, by quantifying the spatial heterogeneity of dose distribution, demonstrates effectiveness in differentiating treatment responders and may reveal biological information associated with the immune microenvironment. Future investigations should focus on exploring the correlations between dosiomic features and immune biomarkers to advance the personalized application of ⁹⁰Y-TARE in combination with immunotherapy regimens [77].

Expanding therapeutic frontiers

In this study, to more rigorously assess emerging research trends, we identified and summarized 17 ongoing clinical trials evaluating the combination of ⁹⁰Y-TARE and ICIs for the treatment of liver cancer (Supplementary Table 2). Current research trends indicate that ⁹⁰Y-TARE combined with immunotherapy is expanding beyond conventional advanced HCC to more challenging subtypes, such as HCC with portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT) and immunogenic intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) (e.g., NCT07122089 and NCT06058663) [78]. Concurrently, its application is shifting earlier in the treatment paradigm, with exploration as a neoadjuvant treatment strategy for resectable HCC (NCT05701488) to improve prognosis. Clinical trial designs are increasingly complex, frequently integrating multiple therapeutic modalities (e.g.., ⁹⁰Y-TARE, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and anti-angiogenic therapy) aimed at achieving synergistic efficacy (NCT06867432). These efforts directly address two critical research gaps: the incompletely understood mechanisms of synergy and the unresolved immunological paradox. However, a key and urgent challenge remains: how to optimize radiotherapy regimens through individualized dosimetry to maximize immune activation while minimizing lymphodepletion. Future research requires more phase III trials, incorporating multi-omics technologies and individualized dosimetry models to validate survival benefits and explore more precise immuno-combination strategies. As experience in ICI application accumulates, more personalized immunotherapies, such as chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, bispecific antibodies, and therapeutic cancer vaccines, are being actively explored [79]. Combining these immunotherapies with ⁹⁰Y-TARE also holds significant potential.

Limitation

This study is limited by its exclusive reliance on WoSCC, excluding conferences, books, and gray literature. The rationale for extending the analysis period to October 24, 2025, along with the evaluation of potential bias and the corresponding mitigation strategies, are detailed in Supplementary Material 5.

Conclusion

Our study conducted a bibliometric analysis and visualization of global publications on the combination of ⁹⁰Y-TARE and immunotherapy for HCC, revealing its evolution over the past two decades. With the advancement of clinical research, ⁹⁰Y-TARE combined with immunotherapy has demonstrated significant improvement in OS and ORR in patients with advanced HCC, alongside a favorable safety profile. Undoubtedly, this combined approach is driving a paradigm shift, extending its application from traditional advanced-line settings to first-line and even neoadjuvant stages, offering new hope for more challenging HCC subtypes. Future research requires more phase III trials, incorporating multi-omics technologies and individualized dosimetry models to validate survival benefits and explore more precise immuno-combination strategies.

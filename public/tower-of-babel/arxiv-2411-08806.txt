Numerically flat foliations and holomorphic Poisson geometry
Stéphane Druel, Jorge Vitório Pereira, Brent Pym, Frédéric Touzet

Abstract
Résumé Dans cet article, nous nous intéressons à la structure des feuilletages holomorphes réguliers dont le fibré tangent est numériquement plat, la variété ambiante étant compacte kählérienne. Nous étendons dans ce cadre des résultats précédemment obtenus par les deuxième et quatrième auteurs. Nous montrons notamment que l’existence d’un tel feuilletage induit une décomposition du fibré tangent de la variété, que les feuilles sont uniformisées par un espace euclidien et que le fibré canonique dudit feuilletage est de torsion. En outre, nous établissons, lorsque la variété ambiante est supposée projective, qu’un feuilletage régulier de dimension deux dont le fibré canonique est numériquement trivial est ou bien une fibration isotriviale, ou bien possède un fibré tangent numériquement plat. Ce dernier résultat fournit un analogue global du théorème de décomposition de Weinstein pour les structures de Poisson de rang deux sur les variétés projectives lisses. Nous obtenons également de nouvelles conditions sur les nombres de Hodge pour qu’une structure de Poisson sur une variété compacte kählérienne s’annule en un point.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we establish several results and conjectures concerning the structure of holomorphic foliations
\mathcal{F}
on compact Kähler manifolds
\mathsf{X}
, under suitable cohomological vanishing conditions on the curvature of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Our main results give conditions for such foliations to be induced by a splitting of the universal cover of
\mathsf{X}
into a product of manifolds. As an application, we obtain some interesting consequences for the structure of holomorphic Poisson brackets on compact Kähler manifolds.

1.1
Numerically flat foliations

Our first main result, established in
Section 4
, describes the structure of regular foliations whose tangent bundle is numerically flat in the sense of Demailly–Peternell–Schneider
[
11
]
, a notion we recall in
Section 2.3
.

Such foliations were previously analyzed by the second and fourth authors in
[
27
]
, under the additional assumptions that the ambient manifold
\mathsf{X}
is projective but not uniruled. Remarkably, with these additional assumptions, the vanishing of the first Chern class of the foliation implies both the smoothness of the foliation and the polystability of its tangent bundle. This is established in
[
27
, Lemma 2.1]
(see also
[
24
, Section 5]
), building on Demailly’s integrability theorem for differential forms with coefficients in the dual of a pseudoeffective line bundle
[
10
]
and the characterization of non-uniruled projective manifolds by Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell
[
6
]
. Leveraging these two properties, it is further shown (
loc. cit.
) that the canonical bundle of the foliation is a torsion line bundle, its leaves are uniformized by Euclidean spaces, and their analytic closures are quotients of abelian varieties. Similarly, and almost concurrently with
[
27
]
, the work
[
1
]
provides a precise description of smooth foliations with trivial tangent bundle on arbitrary compact Kähler manifolds, extending previous work by Lieberman
[
23
]
.

In this paper, we extend the results from
[
27
]
to smooth foliations with numerically flat tangent bundle on arbitrary compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular foliation of dimension
p\geq 0
on
\mathsf{X}
with numerically flat tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then the following hold.

1.

The tangent bundle of
\mathcal{F}
is hermitian flat, and the line bundle
\det\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion.

2.

There exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

3.

The universal cover
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}
of
\mathsf{X}
decomposes as a product
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{p}\times\mathsf{Y}
where
\mathsf{Y}
is a complex manifold, and the decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
lifts to the canonical decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}}\cong\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{C}^{p}}\boxplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{Y}}
.

4.

The analytic closure
\overline{\mathsf{L}}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
of any leaf
\mathsf{L}
of
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to a finite étale quotient of an equivariant compactification of an abelian Lie group
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{L}}
, in such a way that the foliation
\mathcal{F}|_{\overline{\mathsf{L}}}
is induced by a (not necessarily closed) subgroup of
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{L}}
.

In the case of uniruled manifolds, smoothness does not follow automatically from the triviality of the first Chern class. For example, one-dimensional foliations with trivial first Chern class on simply connected manifolds are necessarily singular, presenting a significant obstacle to adapting the arguments of
[
27
]
to our current setting. We get around this using an alternative approach to constructing splittings of the tangent bundle that exploits the behaviour of the Kähler class along the leaves of the foliation; see
Section 1.3
below.

1.2
Foliations with numerically trivial canonical bunlde

Our second main result, established in
Section 5
, describes the structure of smooth foliations of dimension two with numerically trivial canonical bundle on projective manifolds. For projective threefolds, these were described using Mori Theory in
[
12
]
. Later, a similar description was obtained for smooth codimension one foliations on compact Kähler manifolds using entirely different methods in
[
33
]
. In this work, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a complex projective manifold, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular foliation of dimension two with
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})=0
. Then the canonical bundle of
\mathcal{F}
is a torsion line bundle. Moreover, either
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat, or
\mathsf{X}
has a finite étale cover that decomposes as a product
\mathsf{L}\times\mathsf{Y}
where
\mathsf{L}
is a surface with zero first Chern class,
\mathsf{Y}
is a complex projective manifold, and the foliation is induced by the projection to
\mathsf{Y}
.

Unlike in
Theorem 1.1
, here we are forced to restrict to projective manifolds due to the lack of compact Kähler analogues
of the algebraicity/compactness criteria for leaves used in the proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

1.3
Cohomologically Kähler foliations and splittings

A recurring theme in our arguments, and in previous works such as
[
16
,
17
,
24
]
, is the behaviour of the restriction of the Kähler class to the leaves of the foliation. We have found it useful to isolate the key property in the following definition, which we introduce and develop in
Section 3
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a compact Kähler manifold
\mathsf{X}
, and let
p=\dim\mathcal{F}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
cohomologically Kähler
if for every Kähler class
\gamma\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})
, the image of
\gamma^{p}
in
\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
is nonzero, where
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=(\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
is the canonical line bundle of
\mathcal{F}
.

This condition is readily checked in many cases, e.g. it is stable along étale covers and embeddings, and holds automatically for codimension one foliations; the most subtle results we obtain in this direction rely on Demailly’s integrability theorem
[
10
]
. In fact, we conjecture that this condition holds for all regular foliations on compact Kähler manifolds (
Conjecture 3.8
). Meanwhile, this condition is closely linked to the existence of a subbundle of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
that is complementary to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. In particular, the existence of such a complement easily implies that the foliation is cohomologically Kähler (
Lemma 3.6
), and the converse holds if
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})=0
(
Proposition 3.18
).

1.4
Applications to Poisson geometry

In
Section 6
, we explain some consequences of our results and conjectures for holomorphic Poisson structures. On the one hand, they suggest the following global version of Weinstein’s splitting theorem
[
35
]
, generalizing our results in
[
17
]
(which treated the case of possibly singular Poisson structures with a simply-connected compact leaf):

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
be a compact Kähler Poisson manifold and suppose that the minimal dimension of a symplectic leaf of
\pi
is equal to
d
. Then there exists a holomorphic symplectic manifold
\mathsf{Y}
of dimension
d
, a holomorphic Poisson manifold
\mathsf{Z}
, and a holomorphic Poisson covering map
\mathsf{Y}\times\mathsf{Z}\to\mathsf{X}
.

Note that in the conjecture, the covering map is allowed to be infinite, i.e.
\mathsf{Y}
or
\mathsf{Z}
may be noncompact.

As we explain in and around
Proposition 6.3
, this conjecture was previously known to hold when the corank of
\pi
is at most one
[
12
,
33
]
, and our
Theorem 1.2
above implies that it also holds when the minimal dimension of a leaf is equal to two, provided
\mathsf{X}
is projective. In summary, these results give the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Conjecture 1.4
holds for all projective manifolds of dimension
\dim\mathsf{X}\leq 5
.

On the other hand, we conjecture a tight relationship between the Hodge numbers of
\mathsf{X}
and the existence of zeros of the Poisson structure, similar to Bondal’s conjecture
[
5
]
on the dimensions of degeneracy loci of Poisson structures on Fano manifolds.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
be a compact Kähler Poisson manifold, and let
d
be the minimal dimension of a symplectic leaf of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
. Then the Hodge numbers
h^{2j,0}(\mathsf{X})
are nonzero for all
j\leq\tfrac{d}{2}
. In particular, if
\mathsf{h}^{2,0}(\mathsf{X})=0
, then every holomorphic Poisson structure on
\mathsf{X}
has a zero.

We show that this conjecture would follow from our conjecture that all regular foliations are cohomologically Kähler. Exploiting our results in this paper and previous work on Bondal’s conjecture,
Proposition 6.4
and
Proposition 6.5
together give the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Conjecture 1.6
holds if
\mathsf{X}
is projective and
\dim\mathsf{X}\leq 6
; if
\mathsf{X}
is Fano and
\dim\mathsf{X}=7
; or if
\mathsf{X}
is Fano,
\dim\mathsf{X}=8
and
b_{2}(\mathsf{X})=1
.

Notation, conventions, and useful facts

2.1
Global conventions

Throughout the paper, all algebraic varieties are assumed to be defined over the field of complex numbers. We will freely switch between the algebraic and analytic context.

2.2
Stability

The word
stable
will always mean
slope-stable with respect to a
given ample divisor
, and similarly for
semistable
and
polystable
.
We refer to
[
21
, Definition 1.2.12]
for their precise definitions.

2.3
Numerically flat vector bundles

One key notion is that of a numerically flat vector bundle. We recall the definition following
[
11
, Definition 1.17]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r\geq 1
on a compact Kähler manifold is called
numerically flat
if
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{*}
are nef vector bundles. Equivalently,
\mathcal{E}
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathcal{E}
and
\det\mathcal{E}^{*}
are nef vector bundles.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geq 1
on
\mathsf{X}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=0\in\mathsf{H}^{2}(\mathsf{X},\mathbb{C})
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is nef.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.18]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle of rank
r\geq 1
on
\mathsf{X}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is numerically flat if and only if
\mathcal{E}
admits a filtration

\{0\}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{m}=\mathcal{E}

by vector subbundles such that the quotients
\mathcal{E}_{k}/\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
are hermitian flat.

Corollary 2.4

(
[
11
, Corollary 1.19]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
\mathsf{X}
. If
\mathcal{E}
is numerically flat, then
c_{i}(\mathcal{E})=0\in\mathsf{H}^{2i}(\mathsf{X},\mathbb{C})
for any integer
i\geq 1
.

Remark 2.5
.

A numerically flat vector bundle is automatically flat
[
30
, Section 3]
but is not hermitian flat in general
[
11
, Example 1.7]
.

2.4
Foliations

Definition 2.6
.

A
foliation

\mathcal{F}
on a complex manifold
\mathsf{X}
is determined by its tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
, which is a coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
such that

1.

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket, and

2.

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
, i.e. the quotient
\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion-free.

The
dimension

p
of
\mathcal{F}
is the generic rank of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
.
The
codimension
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as
q=\dim\mathsf{X}-p
. The
normal sheaf
of
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{F}}=(\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}/\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})^{**}
; by definition, it is a torsion-free sheaf of rank
q
.

The cotangent sheaf
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}
of
\mathcal{F}
is
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
. Its
canonical line bundle

\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=\det\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{1}=(\wedge^{p}\,\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{1})^{**}
. Note that the canonical map
\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\to\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
determines a section

v\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}}),

from which we may recover
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
as the sheaf of vector fields
\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
such that
v\wedge\xi=0
. We say that
v
is a
p
-vector defining
\mathcal{F}
. Dually, we may define
\mathcal{F}
by a form

\alpha\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{q}_{\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{*})

whose kernel is
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Let
\mathsf{X}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be the open set where
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a subbundle of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
. The
singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
is

\mathrm{Sing}(\mathcal{F}):=\mathsf{X}\setminus\mathsf{X}^{\circ}.

The foliation is called
regular
, or
smooth
, if its singular locus is empty.

A
leaf
of
\mathcal{F}
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
\mathsf{L}\subseteq\mathsf{X}^{\circ}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{L}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{L}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{X^{\circ}}|_{\mathsf{L}}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be a submanifold that is not contained in the singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

For every
y\in\mathsf{Y}\cap\mathsf{X}^{\circ}
, the leaf through
y
is contained in
\mathsf{Y}
.

2.

The defining
p
-vector is tangent to
\mathsf{Y}
, i.e.

v|_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{Y},\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{Y}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}})\subseteq\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{Y},\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}).

3.

The contraction
\iota_{v}|_{\mathsf{Y}}:\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
factors through the natural surjection
\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}\to\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be a submanifold that is not contained in the singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
. We say that
\mathsf{Y}
is
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
if any (hence all) of the equivalent properties of
Lemma 2.7
hold.

By relaxing the condition of involutiveness on the tangent sheaf of a foliation, we arrive at the broader notion of a distribution.

Definition 2.9
.

A
distribution

\mathcal{D}
on a complex manifold
\mathsf{X}
is specified by its tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}}
, which is a coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
, saturated in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
, meaning that the quotient
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}/\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}}
is torsion-free.

All the concepts introduced for foliations earlier in this section – such as dimension, codimension, cotangent sheaf, singular set, invariant submanifold,… – naturally extend to distributions. While every foliation is a distribution, the converse is not true. The key difference is that Frobenius theorem guarantees the existence of a leaf through any point outside the singular locus of a foliation, whereas an arbitrary distribution might have no leaf at all.

3
Cohomologically Kähler foliations and split tangent bundles

3.1
Cohomologically Kähler foliations

Throughout this section,
\mathsf{X}
is a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
\mathcal{D}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}
) is a holomorphic distribution (resp. foliation) on
\mathsf{X}
of dimension
p
, which we allow to be singular unless otherwise stated. We denote by
q=n-p
its codimension.

The key notion is the following cohomological condition on
\mathcal{D}
or
\mathcal{F}
, which is modeled on the behaviour of Kähler classes under restriction to submanifolds.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{D}
is
cohomologically Kähler

if, for any Kähler class
\gamma\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})\cong\mathsf{H}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})
, the image of
\gamma^{p}
under the natural map

\iota_{v}:\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}})\to\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{D}})

is nonzero.

We say that
\mathcal{D}
is
weakly cohomologically Kähler

if there exists a Kähler class
\gamma\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})\cong\mathsf{H}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})
such that

\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}
is nonzero.

Example 3.2
.

At the extremes, the foliations with
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}=0
(respectively,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
) whose leaves are points (resp. all of
\mathsf{X}
) are cohomologically Kähler.
∎

We now give some useful sufficient conditions for a distribution
\mathcal{D}
to be cohomologically Kähler, starting with the following easy observations.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
is a closed
\mathcal{D}
-invariant submanifold, and
\mathcal{D}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is cohomologically Kähler, then
\mathcal{D}
itself is cohomologically Kähler.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-invariant subspace, then the restriction map
\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}}\to\omega_{\mathcal{D}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}\cong\omega_{\mathcal{D}|_{\mathsf{Y}}}
factors through
\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{Y}}
, which immediately implies the result.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
f\colon\mathsf{Y}\to\mathsf{X}
is a finite étale cover, and
f^{-1}\mathcal{D}
is (weakly) cohomologically Kähler, then
\mathcal{D}
itself is (weakly) cohomologically Kähler.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}:=f^{-1}\mathcal{D}
. Notice that there is a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{Y},\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{Y}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{Y},\omega_{\mathcal{E}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{D}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{v_{\mathcal{E}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{v_{\mathcal{D}}}}

and every Kähler class on
\mathsf{X}
pulls back to a Kähler class on
\mathsf{Y}
. This immediately implies the result.
∎

Example 3.5
.

If
\mathsf{X}
is a finite étale quotient of a torus
\mathsf{T}
, and
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{X}}
is a linear foliation, i.e. its pullback to
\mathsf{T}
is induced by the action of a (not necessarily closed) subgroup of
\mathsf{T}
,
then
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologically Kähler. Indeed, in this case, the pullback of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
to
\mathsf{T}
is trivial, and the pullback of any Kähler class
\gamma
can be represented by a Hermitian form that is nondegenerate on every trivial subbundle of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{T}}
. The claim now follows from
Lemma 3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If there exists a distribution
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{D}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{E}}
, then
\mathcal{D}
is cohomologically Kähler.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\gamma_{\mathcal{D}}+\gamma_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})=\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{D}})\oplus\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{E}})
. Then
\gamma_{\mathcal{D}}^{p+1}=0\in\mathsf{H}^{p+1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{p+1}_{\mathcal{D}})
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{E}}^{q+1}=0\in\mathsf{H}^{q+1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{q+1}_{\mathcal{E}})
. As a consequence,
\gamma^{n}={n\choose p}\cdot\gamma_{\mathcal{D}}^{p}\wedge\gamma_{\mathcal{E}}^{q}
. On the other hand,
\gamma_{\mathcal{D}}^{p}=\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}
. In particular, if
\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}=0\in\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{D}})
, then
\gamma^{n}=0
, which is impossible since
\gamma
is a Kähler class and
\mathsf{X}
is compact. This proves that
\mathcal{D}
is cohomologically Kähler.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is regular of dimension
p=1
, then
\mathcal{D}
is cohomologically Kähler.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})\cong\mathsf{H}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})
be a Kähler class. Suppose by way of contradiction that

\iota_{v}\gamma=0\in\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
, then
\gamma
lies in the image of the natural map

\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{F}}^{*})\to\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}}).

It follows that
\gamma^{n}=0
, which is impossible since
\gamma
is a Kähler class and
\mathsf{X}
is compact.
∎

Actually, we expect that regular foliations are always cohomologically Kähler:

Conjecture 3.8
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a regular foliation, then
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologically Kähler.

Remark 3.9
.

If we drop the condition of regularity or integrability of
\mathcal{F}
, then the conclusion of
Conjecture 3.8
is easily seen to fail.

Indeed, let
\mathsf{X}=\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a projective space of dimension
n\geq 2
. Then
\mathbb{P}^{n}
does not admit any regular foliations of dimension
0<p<n
, as can be seen by applying Bott’s vanishing theorem for characteristic classes of regular foliations. However,
\mathbb{P}^{n}
admits many singular foliations of such dimensions, and also admits regular distributions of codimension one when
n
is odd (the holomorphic contact structures). These are never cohomologically Kähler: if
\mathcal{D}
is a distribution on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of dimension
0<p<n
, then
\omega_{\mathcal{D}}
is a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and hence
\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\omega_{\mathcal{D}})=0
.
∎

3.2
Demailly’s integrability criterion

Next, we proceed to show how Demailly’s integrability criterion
[
10
]

We maintain the notation introduced in

Definition 3.10
.

Let

Let

-closed current on

[
7

be the extension of

Because

to a closed positive current

\mathsf{X}

be the cohomology class defined by

in
\mathsf{H}^{q}(\mathsf{X},\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{q})

By construction,

is the image of the class

under the natural map

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}^{q}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{L})\to\mathsf{H}^{q}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}}).

(1)

Now, consider a Kähler form

\mathsf{X}

∎

3.3
Foliations of codimension at most two

Proposition 3.15
.

If

Proof.

Let

On the other hand, if

3.4
Splitting the tangent bundle when
c_{1}=0

We now describe the behaviour of cohomologically Kähler foliations whose first Chern class is trivial.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a possibly singular foliation on
\mathsf{X}
of dimension
p
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})=0
. Then
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Hermitian flat line bundle. Hence if
\gamma
is any Kähler form on
\mathsf{X}
, and
v\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\wedge^{p}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
is a
p
-vector defining
\mathcal{F}
, the class
\left[\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}\right]\in\mathsf{H}^{p}(\mathsf{X},\omega_{\mathcal{F}})
has an Hermitian conjugate

\alpha:=\overline{\left[\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}\right]}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{p}_{\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}),

by Hodge symmetry for unitary local systems.

Lemma 3.17
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is weakly cohomologically Kähler with respect to
\left[\gamma\right]\in\mathsf{H}^{1,1}(\mathsf{X})
, then the contraction

\iota_{v}\alpha\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}})

is nonvanishing.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the arguments in
[
16
, Lemma 5.25]
.
From the definition of
\alpha
, we have
\overline{\alpha}=\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}+\overline{\partial}\xi
for some
\xi
where
\overline{\alpha}
is the complex conjugate of
\alpha
with respect to the Hermitian flat structure. A straightforward calculation using Stokes’ formula and Poincaré duality for the unitary local system
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
then implies that

\int_{\mathsf{X}}\alpha\wedge\overline{\alpha}\wedge\gamma^{n-p}=\int_{\mathsf{X}}\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}\wedge\gamma^{n-p}.

The integral on the left is nonzero by the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations, while an easy pointwise calculation shows that the integrand on the right is given by

\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}\wedge\gamma^{n-p}=\frac{1}{{n\choose p}}\cdot\iota_{v}\alpha\cdot\gamma^{n}

Note that
\iota_{v}\alpha
is a holomorphic function, hence constant. We therefore have

0\neq{n\choose p}\int_{\mathsf{X}}\alpha\wedge\iota_{v}\gamma^{p}\wedge\gamma^{n-p}=\iota_{v}\alpha\cdot\int_{\mathsf{X}}\gamma^{n}

and we conclude that
\iota_{v}\alpha
is nonzero, hence nonvanishing, as desired.
∎

The following result is a generalization of
[
24
, Theorem 5.6]

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\mathsf{X}

For the first statement, note that by

For the second statement, note that contraction with

For the third statement, we follow the argument in the proof of

Replacing

4
Foliations with numerically flat tangent bundle

This section is mostly taken up by the proof of
Theorem 4.1
below. Its statement is almost the same as the statement of
Theorem 1.1
from the Introduction. The only difference is the inclusion of an extra item describing the analytic closure of minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant analytic subspaces. Before presenting the theorem, we introduce some terminology. A compact complex manifold
\mathsf{X}
is called a
torus quotient
if
\mathsf{X}
is a finite étale quotient of a complex torus
\mathsf{T}
. A (regular) foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a torus quotient
\mathsf{X}
is said to be
linear
if
f^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is a linear foliation on
\mathsf{T}
, where
f\colon\mathsf{T}\to\mathsf{X}
denote the quotient map.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold, and let
\mathcal{F}
be regular foliation on
\mathsf{X}
with numerically flat tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then the following hold.

1.

The tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat.

2.

\mathcal{F}
is cohomologically Kähler.

3.

The line bundle
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion.

4.

There exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

5.

\mathcal{F}
is induced by a splitting of the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}
, i.e.
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}
is the product of
\mathbb{C}^{\dim\mathcal{F}}
with another complex manifold
\mathsf{Y}
in such a way that the decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
lifts to the canonical decomposition
\mathcal{T}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}}\cong\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{C}^{\dim\mathcal{F}}}\boxplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{Y}}
.

6.

Any minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant closed analytic subspace
\mathsf{Y}
is a torus quotient and
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is a linear foliation.

7.

The analytic closure
\overline{\mathsf{L}}
of any leaf
\mathsf{L}
of
\mathcal{F}
is a quotient of an equivariant compactification of an abelian Lie group
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{L}}
and
\mathcal{F}|_{\overline{\mathsf{L}}}
is induced by a (not necessarily closed) subgroup of
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{L}}
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem. Hence for the rest of this section we adopt the following assumptions:

•

\mathsf{X}
is a compact Kähler manifold

•

\mathcal{F}
is a regular foliation

•

The dimension
p=\dim\mathcal{F}
is positive

•

\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
is a minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant closed analytic subspace.

Here, by minimal, we mean “minimal with respect to inclusion”. Note that since
\mathcal{F}
is regular, the singular locus of an invariant subspace is also invariant, and hence
\mathsf{Y}
is automatically smooth.

For clarity, we break the proof of
Theorem 4.1
into several lemmas which address various implications. We first explain how the first statement (that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat) can be used to deduce the others, and later show that this first statement does indeed hold (
Lemma 4.7
).

Lemma 4.2

(
1

\implies

6
)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat, then any minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant closed analytic subspace
\mathsf{Y}
is a torus quotient, and
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is a linear foliation.

Proof.

By assumption,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is given by a unitary representation

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{U}(p).

In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is hermitian flat as well. Replacing
\mathsf{X}
by
\mathsf{Y}
, we may therefore assume without loss of generality that there is no proper minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant analytic subspace in
\mathsf{X}
. We now break the proof into several cases.

Case 1:
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{F}}
is trivial.
Suppose that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}}^{\oplus p}
. We claim that
\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})\subseteq\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}})
is an abelian Lie subalgebra. Indeed, let
v_{i}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then
[v_{1},v_{2}]
induces the zero flow on the Albanese torus
\mathsf{A}
of
\mathsf{X}
. Thus, by
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
, the vector field
[v_{1},v_{2}]
has at least one zero. On the other hand,
[v_{1},v_{2}]\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is involutive. Since any vector field tangent to
\mathcal{F}
has empty vanishing locus by assumption, we must have
[v_{1},v_{2}]=0
, proving our claim.

Let
\mathsf{H}\subseteq\mathsf{Aut}\left(\mathsf{X}\right)_{0}
be the analytic closure of the complex Lie subgroup exponentiating the Lie algebra
\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
. Note that
\mathsf{H}
is an abelian complex Lie group. By
[
23
, Theorems 3.3, 3.12 and 3.14]
, there is an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

where
\mathsf{N}
a closed subgroup whose Lie algebra is contained in the Lie algebra of holomorphic vector fields with nonempty zero locus, and
\mathsf{T}
is a compact complex torus. Moreover,
\mathsf{N}
is a commutative linear algebraic group.
By
[
32
, Proposition, p. 53]
, the neutral component
\mathsf{N}_{0}
of
\mathsf{N}
has a fixed point
x
on
\mathsf{X}
whose stabilizer in
\mathsf{H}
is denoted by
\mathsf{H}_{x}
. Then

\mathsf{H}\cdot x
is a compact analytic subvariety which is invariant under
\mathcal{F}
, and hence

\mathsf{H}\cdot x=\mathsf{X}
. This immediately implies that
\mathsf{X}
is the quotient of the torus
\mathsf{H}/\mathsf{N}_{0}
by the finite group
\mathsf{H}_{x}/\mathsf{N}_{0}
, and that
\mathcal{F}
is a linear foliation on
\mathsf{X}
.

Case 2:
\mathcal{F}
is one-dimensional.
Let
a\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{A}
be the Albanese morphism, and let
q(\mathsf{X})=\dim\mathsf{A}
be the irregularity of
\mathsf{X}
. If
q(\mathsf{X})=0
, then
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a torsion line bundle, and by passing to a finite cover we reduce to the case treated in Step 1.

Thus suppose
q(\mathsf{X})>0
. If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is tangent to any fiber of
a
, then this fiber is a proper
\mathcal{F}
-invariant subvariety contained in
\mathsf{X}
, yielding a contradiction. Therefore, the composition
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}\to a^{*}\mathsf{A}
is nonvanishing, and hence
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}}
. So by Step 1 again, we deduce that
\mathsf{X}
is a torus quotient, and that
\mathcal{F}
a linear foliation.

Intermezzo.
To formulate the remaining cases, we need to introduce some additional notation and simplifications. Let
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\textup{GL}(p,\mathbb{C})
be the Zariski closure of
\rho(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
. This is a linear algebraic group which has finitely many connected components. Applying Selberg’s Lemma and passing to an appropriate finite étale cover of
\mathsf{X}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathsf{G}
is connected, and that the image of the induced representation

\rho_{1}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X})\to\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}/\textup{Rad}(\mathsf{G})

is torsion-free, where
\textup{Rad}(\mathsf{G})
denotes the radical of
\mathsf{G}
.

Case 3:
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
is infinite
. We claim that this case cannot occur. To see this, let

f\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Z}

be the
\rho_{1}
-Shafarevich morphism. We refer to
[
8
, Definition 2.13]
for this notion and to
[
8
, Théorème 1]
for its existence. Moreover (
loc. cit.
), as we are assuming that
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
is torsion free, we may assume that
\mathsf{Z}
is a normal projective variety of general type. Note that
\dim\mathsf{Z}>0
since
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
is infinite.

Observe that
\mathcal{F}
is not tangent to the fibers of
f
since there is no proper
\mathcal{F}
-invariant analytic subspace in
\mathsf{X}
. Note further that flat sections of the unitary vector bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
lift to holomorphic vector fields on the universal covering of
\mathsf{X}
that have bounded norm, and are therefore complete. The orbits of these vector fields give rise by projection to entire curves covering
\mathsf{X}
as well as
\mathsf{Z}
. But according to
[
22
, Theorem 7.4.7]
varieties of general type cannot be covered by entire curves, a contradiction. Hence
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
cannot be infinite.

Case 4:
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(\mathsf{X}))
is finite.

If
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(X))
finite, then it is trivial, since it is torsion-free by construction. It follows that
\mathsf{G}
is solvable. Since
\rho
is unitary and
\mathsf{G}
is connected, we deduce that
\rho
splits as a direct sum of rank one representations. Thus
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{F}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{F}_{p}
where
\dim\mathcal{F}_{i}=1
, and
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}_{i}})=0
.

We now proceed by induction on
\dim\mathsf{X}
. If
\dim\mathsf{X}=1
, then
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
and hence
c_{1}(\mathsf{X})=c_{1}(\mathcal{F})=0
, so that
\mathsf{X}
is an elliptic curve and the statement holds.

Now suppose that
\dim\mathsf{X}>1
. If there exists
i
such that
\mathcal{F}_{i}
has no proper
\mathcal{F}_{i}
-invariant subspace, then by Step 2,
\mathsf{X}
is a torus quotient and
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a linear foliation. The remaining foliations
\mathcal{F}_{j}
are then also linear: indeed, since the bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}
are flat, their first Chern classes are zero and hence they must correspond to trivial summands of the trivial bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
.

Otherwise,
\mathcal{F}_{i}
has an invariant subspace
\mathsf{Y}_{i}
for every
i
. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathsf{Y}_{i}
is a torus quotient, and hence
\mathcal{F}_{i}|_{\mathsf{Y}_{i}}
is cohomologically Kähler by
Example 3.5
, so that
\mathcal{F}_{i}
itself is cohomologically Kähler by
Lemma 3.3
and thus
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}=\det\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
is a torsion line bundle by
Proposition 3.18
. Hence by passing to a finite étale cover, we may assume that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}
is trivial for all
i
, and hence
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
itself is trivial. The result therefore follows from Case 1.
∎

Since every linear foliation on a torus quotient is cohomologically Kähler (
Example 3.5
), and it suffices to check the latter condition on submanifolds (
Lemma 3.3
), we deduce the following.

Corollary 4.3

(
1

\implies

2
)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat, then
\mathcal{F}
is cohomologically Kähler.

Hence by
Proposition 3.18
we have the following.

Corollary 4.4

(
1

\implies

3
and
4
)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat, then
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is a torsion line bundle, and there exists a regular foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
such that

\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Lemma 4.5

(
1

\implies

5
)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is Hermitian flat, then
\mathcal{F}
is induced by a splitting of the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}
.

Proof.

This is argued in the proof of
[
27
, Theorem A]
.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(
1

\implies

7
)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is hermitian flat, then the leaf closures are induced by abelian group actions as in part
7
of

.

Proof.

In the proof of
Lemma 4.2
, we saw that the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are contained in the fibers of the Shafarevich morphism. Furthermore, the restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to a fibre of the Shafarevich morphism is defined by an analytic action of
\mathbb{C}^{\dim\mathcal{F}}
. Therefore, the analytic closure
\overline{\mathsf{L}}
of any leaf
\mathsf{L}
admits a locally free
\mathbb{C}^{\dim\mathcal{F}}
-action (i.e. the stabilizer of any point is discrete) with an analytically dense orbit.
To conclude, it suffices to take
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{L}}
equal to the analytic closure of the subgroup of
\mathsf{Aut}\left(\overline{\mathsf{L}}\right)_{0}
determined by the locally free
\mathbb{C}^{\dim\mathcal{F}}
-action defining
\mathcal{F}_{|\overline{\mathsf{L}}}
.
∎

At this point, we have shown that all statements in
Theorem 4.1
follow from the first. It thus remains to establish the following:

Lemma 4.7

(
1
holds)
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat, then it is hermitian flat.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
be an hermitian flat subbundle of maximal rank
p_{1}
. By Theorem
2.3
, we have
p_{1}\geq 1
. We must show that
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. We do so by treating several cases, similar to the proof of
Lemma 4.2
, as follows.

Case 1:
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is involutive.
In this case, by Corollary
4.4
applied to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, there exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}\cong\mathcal{T}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
. Then
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
gives a splitting
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{T}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}}
. But then
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}
is numerically flat as well and applying Theorem
2.3
to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}}
contradicts the maximality of
\rank\mathcal{T}_{1}
unless
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Case 2:
\mathcal{T}_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}}^{\oplus p_{1}}
is trivial.
We claim that
\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},{\mathcal{T}_{1}})\subseteq\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}})
is an abelian Lie subalgebra. Indeed, let
v_{i}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},{\mathcal{T}_{1}})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then
[v_{1},v_{2}]
induces the zero flow on the Albanese torus
\mathsf{A}
of
\mathsf{X}
. Thus, by
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
, the vector field
[v_{1},v_{2}]
has at least one zero. On the other hand,
[v_{1},v_{2}]\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
since
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation.

Applying
[
11
, Proposition 1.16]
, we see that
[v_{1},v_{2}]=0
, proving our claim. In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is involutive, and the conclusion follows from Step 1.

Intermezzo:
Suppose now that the vector bundle
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is given by an aribtrary unitary representation

\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathsf{U}(p_{1}).

Let
\mathsf{G}\subseteq\textup{GL}(p_{1},\mathbb{C})
be the Zariski closure of
\rho(\pi_{1}(X))
.
As before, after passing to an appropriate finite étale cover of
\mathsf{X}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathsf{G}
is connected, and that the image of the induced representation

\rho_{1}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathsf{G}\to\mathsf{G}/\textup{Rad}(\mathsf{G})

is torsion free.

Case 3:
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(X))
is finite.
Then
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(X))
is the trivial group since it is torsion free by construction. It follows that
\mathsf{G}
is solvable. Since
\rho
is unitary, we deduce that
\rho
splits as a direct sum of rank one representations. By
Corollary 4.4
, we deduce that
\rho(\pi_{1}(X))
is finite as well. The conclusion now follows from Case 2 after passing to an étale cover.

Case 4:
\rho_{1}(\pi_{1}(X))
is infinite.
Let

f\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Z}

be the
\rho_{1}
-Shafarevich map. By
[
8
, Théorème 1]
again, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathsf{Z}
is a (positive dimensional) normal projective variety of general type. Arguing as in the end of the proof of

, we see that
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is tangent to the fibers of
f
. By induction on
\dim\mathsf{X}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is involutive. The conclusion now follows from Case 1, proving the lemma.
∎

.

The following consequence of

,

,

Proposition 4.9

\mathsf{X}

5
Foliations of dimension two with numerically trivial canonical bundle

In this section, we describe the structure of regular foliations with zero first Chern class and dimension at most two on complex projective manifolds (see Theorem
5.2
).

Notions of singularities coming from the minimal model program have been shown to be very useful when studying (birational) geometry of foliations. We refer the reader to
[
25
, Section I]
for their precise definition. The proof of
Theorem 5.2
below makes use of the following result, which might be of independent interest.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
\mathcal{L}
be a foliation by curves on
\mathsf{X}
such that
c_{1}(\omega_{\mathcal{L}})=0
. If
\mathcal{L}
has log canonical singularities, then
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}
is a torsion line bundle.

Proof.

Let
a\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{A}
be the Albanese morphism, and set
\mathsf{Y}:=a(\mathsf{X})
. If
q(\mathsf{X})=0
, any line bundle whose first Chern class is zero in
\mathsf{H}^{2}(\mathsf{X},\mathbb{Q})
is torsion. We may thus assume
q(\mathsf{X})=\dim\mathsf{A}>0
, and that
\mathcal{L}
is tangent to the fibers of the induced map
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
.

Suppose first that the singular locus
\mathsf{Z}
of
\mathcal{L}
maps onto a proper subset of
\mathsf{Y}
. Then a general fiber
\mathsf{F}
of the map
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
is
\mathcal{L}
-invariant and
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathsf{F}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{F}}
is regular.
Proposition 4.9
then implies that

\omega_{\mathcal{L}}
is a torsion line bundle.

Suppose from now on that
\mathsf{Z}
maps onto
\mathsf{Y}
, and let
\mathsf{Z}_{1}
be any irreducible component of
\mathsf{Z}
mapping onto
\mathsf{Y}
.
There exists a positive integer
m
such that

\omega_{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes m}\in a^{*}\textup{Pic}(\mathsf{A}).

Hence, it suffices to show that
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}
is a torsion line bundle. We now argue as in
[
4
, Section 4.1]
. By the very definition of
\mathsf{Z}
, the natural map

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
gives a surjective map

\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{Z}/\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}}.

Next, consider the composition

\Lambda\colon\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}\to(\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{Z}/\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}})|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}\to(\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}/\mathsf{X}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}})|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}\\
\cong(\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}/\mathsf{X}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}/\mathsf{X}}^{2})\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}\to\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}.

If
z\in\mathsf{Z}_{1}
is any point and
v
is a local generator of
\mathcal{L}
on some open neighborhood of
z
, then the induced map

\Lambda|_{z}\colon\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{z}\to\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{z}\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{z}\cong\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{z}

is the linear part of
v
at
z
.
By
[
25
, Facts I.1.8 and I.1.9]
, the endomorphism
\Lambda|_{z}
of
\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{z}
is not nilpotent since
\mathcal{L}
has log canonical singularities by assumption. It follows that there exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,\dim\mathsf{X}\}
such that one of the
k^{\textup{th}}
elementary symmetric function

s_{k}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{Z}_{1},{({\omega_{\mathcal{L}}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}})}^{\otimes k})

of
\Lambda\in{\textup{End}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}}(\Omega_{\mathsf{X}}^{1}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}})\otimes\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}

does not vanish at
z
and in particular
s_{k}
is a nonzero section of
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}^{\otimes k}
. Since
c_{1}(\omega_{\mathcal{L}})=0
, this implies that
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}|_{\mathsf{Z}_{1}}
is torsion, as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a complex projective manifold, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular foliation of dimension
p\in\{1,2\}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})=0
. Then either
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable, or
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat. Moreover,
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is a torsion line bundle, and there exists a regular foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

The second assertion follows from
[
24
, Theorem 5.6]
if
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable and from
Theorem 4.1
if
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat, so we need only prove the first assertion. If
p=1
,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat by assumption. We may thus assume that
p=2
.

Let
\xi
be the tautological class on
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{F}})
. If
\xi
is not pseudo-effective, then
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable by
[
14
, Proposition 8.4]
, so suppose from now on that
\xi
is pseudo-effective.

Let
\mathsf{H}
be an ample divisor on
\mathsf{X}
. From
[
9
, Theorem 4.7]
, we deduce that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\mathsf{H}
-semistable. Applying
[
26
, Theorem IV.4.8]
, we see that one of the following holds.

1.

The tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat.

2.

There exist line bundles
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{M}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=-c_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and slopes
\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(\mathcal{L})=\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(\mathcal{M})=0
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{M}
.

3.

There exist a finite étale cover
f\colon\mathsf{X}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{X}
of degree
2
and a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong f_{*}\mathcal{L}
.

4.

There exists a foliation
\mathcal{L}
by curves, defined by a saturated line bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}})=0
.

Hence it suffices to treat cases 2 through 4, which we do as follows.

Case 2:
In his case,
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
applies to show that
\mathcal{L}^{*}
and
\mathcal{M}^{*}
are pseudo-effective line bundles. This immediately implies
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=c_{1}(\mathcal{M})=0
since
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=-c_{1}(\mathcal{M})
by our current assumption.
As a consequence,
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat.

Case 3:
Let
\tau
be the involution of the covering
f\colon\mathsf{X}^{\prime}\to\mathsf{X}
. By

[
26
, Lemma 4.11]
, we have
f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{L}\oplus\tau^{*}\mathcal{L}
.
Note that
f^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is a regular foliation on
\mathsf{X}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{T}_{f^{-1}\mathcal{F}}=f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
. In particular,

c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{f^{-1}\mathcal{F}})=0
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is pseudo-effective by
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
, and hence

c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=c_{1}(\tau^{*}\mathcal{L})=0
since
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{f^{-1}\mathcal{F}})=0
. This implies that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat in this case as well.

Case 4:
This one is the most involved and occupies the rest of the proof. In this case,
\mathcal{F}
has canonical singularities
by
[
2
, Lemma 3.10]
. By
[
24
, Corollary 3.8]
,
\mathcal{F}
is not uniruled. It follows that
\mathcal{L}
is not uniruled as well, and hence it has canonical singularities using
[
24
, Corollary 3.8]
again. Therefore
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}
is a torsion line bundle by
Theorem 5.1
. Hence, replacing
\mathsf{X}
by a finite étale cover if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that there is a nonzero global vector field
v\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}}\,v
.

If
v
is nowhere vanishing, i.e
\mathcal{L}
is a regular foliation, then there exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
everywhere transverse to
\mathcal{L}
by
[
23
, Theorem 3.14]
. The foliation
\mathcal{G}

gives a splitting
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{L}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}}
, and hence
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat.

Let
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be a minimal
\mathcal{F}
-invariant analytic subspace, which is automatically smooth. Either
v|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is nowhere vanishing, nonzero but vanishing, or identically zero.

Suppose first that
v|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is nowhere vanishing. Then
Proposition 4.9
applied to
\mathcal{L}

implies that
\mathcal{L}
is regular, so that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat.

Suppose next that
v|_{\mathsf{Y}}\neq 0
but
v(y)=0
for some point
y\in\mathsf{Y}
. Let
\mathsf{G}
be the Zariski closure in
\mathsf{Aut}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)_{0}
of the complex Lie group exponentiating
v|_{\mathsf{Y}}
. Notice that
\mathsf{G}
is a commutative linear algebraic group by
[
23
, Theorems 3.12 and 3.14]
and that
\mathsf{G}
preserves
{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
. Then
\mathsf{G}
fixes
y
, and thus the leaf
\mathsf{L}
of

{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
though
y
is also
\mathsf{G}
-invariant. Let
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{Y},\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{Y}})
be the Lie algebra of holomorphic vector fields arising from the infinitesimal action of
\mathsf{G}
. The set
\mathsf{Z}=\{u\in\mathsf{Y}\,|\,\mathfrak{g}(u)\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{u}\mathcal{F}\subset{\mathcal{T}}_{u}{\mathsf{Y}}\}\ni y
is a closed algebraic subvariety of
\mathsf{Y}
saturated by
\mathcal{F}
. By minimality of
\mathsf{Y}
, we have
\mathsf{Z}=\mathsf{Y}
, and hence
{\mathcal{F}}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is uniruled. But this contradicts
[
24
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Suppose finally that
v|_{\mathsf{Y}}=0
. Notice that we must have
\dim\mathsf{Y}<\dim\mathsf{X}
. By induction on
\dim\mathsf{X}
, we can assume that
either
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is algebraically integrable or
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}}
is numerically flat.
By
[
24
, Theorem 5.6]
if
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is algebraically integrable and
Proposition 4.9
if
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}|_{\mathsf{Y}}}
is numerically flat,

\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is a torsion line bundle, and there exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathsf{X}
everywhere transverse to
\mathcal{F}
. Therefore, replacing
\mathsf{X}
by a further étale cover, if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Hence, there is a
2
-form
\Omega
on
\mathsf{X}
that restricts to a symplectic form on the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, the global
1
-form
\alpha:=\iota_{v}\Omega
is nonzero.
Let
a\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{A}
be the Albanese morphism, and set
\mathsf{T}:=a(\mathsf{X})
. Notice that
\dim\mathsf{T}>0
since
\alpha\neq 0
. Moreover,
v
must be tangent to the fibers of
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{T}
since the vanishing locus
\mathsf{Z}\supseteq\mathsf{Y}
of
v
is nonempty by our current assumption. Suppose that
\mathsf{Z}
maps onto a proper subset of
\mathsf{T}
. Then a general fiber
\mathsf{F}
of the map
\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{T}
is
\mathcal{L}
-invariant and
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathsf{F}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{F}}
is regular.
Proposition 4.9
then implies that
v
is nowhere vanishing, a contradiction.
Therefore,
\mathsf{Z}
maps onto
\mathsf{T}
. Now,
\alpha|_{Z_{\textup{reg}}}
vanishes identically by its very definition.
On the other hand,
\alpha|_{Z_{\textup{reg}}}
is the pull-back of a nonzero global
1
-form on
\mathsf{A}
. This immediately implies
\alpha=0
, yielding a contradiction.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We may now finish the proof of our second main result (
Theorem 1.2
from the introduction), which describes the structure of regular foliations of dimension two with
c_{1}=0
on projective manifolds.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

According to
Theorem 5.2
,
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion, there exists a foliation
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat or
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable. If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is numerically flat, then it is hermitian flat by
Theorem 4.1
. If
\mathcal{F}
is algebraically integrable then the existence of a finite étale covering trivializing
\mathcal{F}
follows from
[
17
, Theorem 1.4 and Remark 3.17]
.
∎

6
Consequences for Poisson geometry

The results and conjectures in this paper have some interesting consequences for compact Kähler Poisson manifolds, i.e. pairs
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
of a compact Kähler manifold
\mathsf{X}
and a holomorphic Poisson bivector
\pi\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\wedge^{2}\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}})
. For such manifolds, the image of the “anchor map”
\pi^{\sharp}:\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
gives an involutive subsheaf, whose integral submanifolds are the symplectic leaves of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
. However, this subsheaf need not be saturated, so it may not define a foliation in the stronger sense of the present paper. Rather, we obtain a foliation
\mathcal{F}
in the present sense by taking the saturation of
\img\pi^{\sharp}\subset\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
. This may enlarge the leaves away from the locus where the rank of
\pi
is constant.

Let
r
be the generic rank of
\pi
. (The rank is always even.) Let us assume that the locus where
\pi
has rank less than
r
has codimension at least two. Then
\det\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
is trivialized by
\pi^{r/2}
, and hence
c_{1}(\mathcal{F})=0
, so our results and conjectures on foliations with numerically trivial canonical bundle apply.

6.1
Submanifolds and subcalibrations

We recall the definition and basic properties of subcalibrations, due to Frejlich–Mărcuţ; see
[
17
, §2]
for details. A
subcalibration
of a holomorphic Poisson manifold
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
is a global closed holomorphic two-form
\sigma\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{2}_{\mathsf{X}})
such that the operator
\theta=\pi^{\sharp}\sigma^{\flat}\in\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}})
is idempotent, i.e.
\theta^{2}=\theta
. Here
\sigma^{\flat}:\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}}
and
\pi^{\sharp}:\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}
are the natural maps defined by contraction. The image and kernel of
\theta
then define complementary smooth foliations
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
, respectively, so that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
. The Poisson structure then splits as
\pi=\pi_{\mathcal{F}}+\pi_{\mathcal{G}}
where
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\wedge^{2}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}})
and
\pi_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\wedge^{2}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}})
are Poisson structures on
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
, with
\pi_{\mathcal{F}}
nondegenerate.

Certain Poisson submanifolds can be used to construct subcalibrations, as follows. Let
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be a closed holomorphic Poisson submanifold, i.e. a closed complex submanifold to which
\pi
is tangent. Assume that
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is regular. Such regular Poisson submanifolds always exist: for instance, if
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
is a Poisson subvariety that is minimal with respect to inclusions, then
\mathsf{Y}
is automatically smooth and
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is automatically regular, because the degeneracy and singular loci of any Poisson variety are Poisson subvarieties
[
28
, §2]
.

Definition 6.1
.

A subcalibration
\sigma
of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
is
compatible with the Poisson submanifold
\mathsf{Y}
if
\img\theta|_{\mathsf{Y}}=\img\pi^{\sharp}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
, i.e. the foliation
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathsf{X}
induced by the image of
\theta
restricts to the symplectic foliation on
\mathsf{Y}
.

The following is a generalization of
[
17
, Corollary 2.5]
, which treated the case in which
\mathsf{Y}
is a symplectic leaf. Note that if
Conjecture 3.8
is true, then the assumption that
\mathsf{Y}
is cohomologically Kähler can be dropped.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
be a regular Poisson submanifold. If
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
are connected and the symplectic foliation of
\mathsf{Y}
is cohomologically Kähler, then
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
admits a subcalibration that is compatible with
\mathsf{Y}
.

Proof.

In the special case where
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
has corank zero, i.e.
\mathsf{Y}
is a symplectic leaf, this is
[
17
, Corollary 2.5]
. (In that case, the foliation is trivially cohomologically Kähler.) The argument here is similar, with some minor changes in the details to get around the fact that
\mathsf{Y}
may have many leaves, and they need not be compact.

Suppose that the rank of
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is
2k
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{Y}}
be its symplectic foliation. Let
\gamma\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{1}_{\mathsf{X}})
be a Kähler class, and consider the element
\iota_{\pi^{k}}\gamma^{2k}\in\mathsf{H}^{2k}(\mathsf{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{X}})
. Its conjugate under Hodge symmetry is a holomorphic
2k
-form
\mu\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{2k}_{\mathsf{X}})
. Since
\pi^{k}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is a
2k
-vector defining
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{Y}}
, it follows from
Lemma 3.17
that the element
\iota_{\pi^{k}}\mu|_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{Y},\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{Y}})
is a nonzero constant. Hence by rescaling
\mu
if necessary, we may assume that
\mu
restricts to the leafwise Liouville volume form on every symplectic leaf of
\mathsf{Y}
. Then
\sigma_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{(k-1)!}\iota_{\pi^{k-1}}\mu\in\mathsf{H}^{0}(\mathsf{X},\Omega^{2}_{\mathsf{X}})
is a global holomorphic two-form that restricts to the symplectic form on every symplectic leaf of
\mathsf{Y}
.

Applying
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
to any symplectic leaf
\mathsf{L}\subseteq\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
, we deduce the existence of a subcalibration
\sigma
of
\pi
that restricts to the symplectic form on
\mathsf{L}
. Since
\mathsf{X}
is connected, the rank of the associated endomorphism
\theta=\pi^{\sharp}\sigma^{\flat}
is constant, and must therefore be equal to
2k
. But then
\img\theta|_{\mathsf{Y}}\subseteq\img\pi^{\sharp}|_{\mathsf{Y}}
are subbundles of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{Y}}
of the same rank, and hence they must be equal, so that the subcalibration is compatible with
\mathsf{Y}
, as desired.
∎

6.2
Global Weinstein splitting

Now suppose
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
is a compact Kähler Poisson manifold, and let
d
be the minimal dimension of a symplectic leaf of
\pi
, i.e. we have
d=\min_{x\in\mathsf{X}}(\rank\pi(x))
. Assuming
Conjecture 3.8
that every regular foliation is cohomologically Kähler, we may apply
Proposition 6.2
to any Poisson submanifold on which
\pi
has rank
d
and deduce the existence of a subcalibration of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
that gives a Poisson splitting of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{X}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{G}}
where
\mathcal{F}
is a symplectic foliation of dimension
d
, and
\mathcal{G}
is a Poisson foliation. Combining this with Beauville’s conjecture, we obtain a splitting of the universal cover of
\mathsf{X}
as a product of Poisson manifolds. In other words,
Conjecture 3.8
, together with Beauville’s conjecture, imply
Conjecture 1.4
from the introduction, whose statement we now recall:

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
be a compact Kähler Poisson manifold and suppose that the minimal dimension of a symplectic leaf of
\pi
is equal to
d
. Then there exists a holomorphic symplectic manifold
\mathsf{Y}
of dimension
d
, a holomorphic Poisson manifold
\mathsf{Z}
, and a holomorphic Poisson covering map
\mathsf{Y}\times\mathsf{Z}\to\mathsf{X}
.

If
d=0
, or
d=\dim\mathsf{X}
, the conjecture is trivially satisfied: simply take
\mathsf{Z}
or
\mathsf{Y}
to be
\mathsf{X}
, and the other to be a point. If
d=\dim\mathsf{X}-1
, then
\pi
is automatically regular since the rank is even; the conjecture then follows from the classification in
[
12
,
33
]
. The conjecture also holds when
\mathsf{X}
has a compact leaf with finite fundamental group as shown by the main result of our paper
[
17
]
.

As a direct consequence of the results presented in this paper, we provide further evidence supporting this conjecture.

Proposition 6.3
.

Conjecture 1.4
holds if
d=2
and
\mathsf{X}
is projective.

Proof.

By
Theorem 1.2
and
Lemma 3.6
, the symplectic foliation of any regular Poisson submanifold of rank two is cohomologically Kähler. Applying
Proposition 6.2
, we obtain a subcalibration of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
for which the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is symplectic of dimension two. Applying
Theorem 1.2
again, we obtain the desired splitting.
∎

6.3
Non-emptiness of vanishing loci

Note that
Proposition 6.2
implies, in particular, that if
\mathsf{X}
admits a regular cohomologically Kähler Poisson submanifold of rank
2k
, then there exists a holomorphic two-form
\sigma
on
\mathsf{X}
such that
\sigma^{k}\neq 0
. Hence
Proposition 6.2
and
Conjecture 3.8
(that every regular foliation is cohomologically Kähler) together imply the following.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
be a compact Kähler Poisson manifold, and let
d
be the minimal dimension of a symplectic leaf of
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
. Then the Hodge numbers
h^{2j,0}(\mathsf{X})
are nonzero for all
j\leq\tfrac{d}{2}
. In particular, if
\mathsf{h}^{2,0}(\mathsf{X})=0
, then every holomorphic Poisson structure on
\mathsf{X}
has a zero.

Note that if
\mathsf{X}
is rational or Fano, or more generally if
\mathsf{X}
is rationally connected, then
\mathsf{h}^{q,0}(\mathsf{X})=0
for all
q>0
, so the conjecture predicts that any Poisson structure on such a manifold has a zero. This conjecture is thus similar in spirit to Bondal’s conjecture
[
5
]
that if
(\mathsf{X},\pi)
is a Fano Poisson manifold, then for every
j<\dim\mathsf{X}/2
, the degeneracy locus where
\pi
has rank at most
2j
is non-empty, and has an irreducible component of dimension at least
2j+1
. Bondal’s conjecture is known to hold for Fano manifolds of dimension
\leq 4
, as proven in
[
19
,
28
]
. In addition,
Conjecture 1.6
was proven for
\mathsf{X}=\mathbb{P}^{5}
by the third author in
[
29
, Theorem 6.8.5]
. Thus
Conjecture 1.6
holds in all those cases, all of which are subsumed by the following.

Proposition 6.4
.

Conjecture 1.6
holds if
\mathsf{X}
is projective and
\dim\mathsf{X}\leq 6
, or more generally, if
\mathsf{X}
contains a closed projective analytic Poisson subspace
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
of dimension
\dim\mathsf{Y}\leq 6
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the subspace
\mathsf{Y}
is smooth and that
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
is regular of rank
p
for some
p\geq d
. By
Proposition 6.2
, it suffices to show that the symplectic foliation of
\mathsf{Y}
is cohomologically Kähler. But either the rank
p
of
\pi|_{\mathsf{Y}}
or the corank
q=\dim\mathsf{Y}-p
is at most two, and the symplectic foliation has trivial canonical bundle; these cases are covered by
Example 3.2
when
p=0
or
q=0
,
Theorem 5.2
and
Lemma 3.6
when
p=2
,
Proposition 3.15
when
q=1
, and
Lemma 3.16
when
q=2
.
∎

Under the stronger assumption that
\mathsf{X}
is Fano, we obtain further evidence for
Conjecture 1.6
(and hence also Bondal’s conjecture):

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a Fano manifold. If either
\dim\mathsf{X}\leq 7
, or
\dim\mathsf{X}=8
and
b_{2}(\mathsf{X})=1
, then every Poisson structure on
\mathsf{X}
has at least one zero.

Proof.

Every Fano manifold
\mathsf{X}
has
\mathsf{h}^{0,q}(\mathsf{X})=0
for
q>0
. Moreover, an application of Bott’s vanishing theorem as in
[
28
, §9]
or
[
19
, §7.3]
implies that a Poisson structure
\pi
on such a manifold can never be regular.

If
\dim\mathsf{X}\leq 7
, then the non-regular locus of
\pi
gives a nonempty closed analytic Poisson subspace
\mathsf{Y}\subset\mathsf{X}
of dimension at most six, and the result follows from
Proposition 6.4
.

If
\dim\mathsf{X}=8
, then either there is a nonempty closed Poisson subvariety of dimension at most six (in which case
Proposition 6.4
applies), or
\mathsf{X}
contains a smooth Poisson hypersurface
\mathsf{Y}\subset\mathsf{X}
. Since
b_{2}(\mathsf{X})=1
, the divisor
\mathsf{Y}
is ample and its class is a multiple of the canonical class of
\mathsf{X}
. Hence the adjunction formula gives
c_{1}(\mathsf{Y})=s\,c_{1}(\mathsf{X})|_{\mathsf{Y}}\in\mathsf{H}^{2}(\mathsf{Y},\mathbb{C})
for some rational number
s
. If
s>0
, then
\mathsf{Y}
is Fano of dimension seven and the result follows as above. On the other hand, if
s\leq 0
then
\omega_{\mathsf{Y}}
is pseudo-effective, and since the symplectic foliation has trivial canonical bundle, we deduce from
Proposition 3.13
that it is cohomologically Kähler, so the result follows from
Proposition 6.2
and the vanishing
\mathsf{h}^{q,0}(\mathsf{X})=0
for all
q>0
as above.
∎

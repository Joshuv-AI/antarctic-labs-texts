A few finite and infinite identities involving Pochhammer and q q -Pochhammer symbols obtained via analytical methods Thanks: The author is grateful to an unknown referee for his (her) general and also quite detailed remarks regarding the improvement of the paper.
Paweł J. Szabłowski

Abstract
. We present several identities with a form of polynomials or rational functions that involve Pochhammer and q q -Pochhammer symbols and q q -binomials (i.e. Gauss polynomials). All these identities were obtained by some analytical methods based on infinite expansions of the ratio of densities in a Fourier series of polynomials orthogonal with respect to the density in the denominator. We want a unified approach to justify many known and unknown identities. The purpose of studying these identities is to simplify calculations occurring while dealing with Pochhammer and q q -Pochhammer symbols. Additional possible applications of the results presented in the paper are applications within the Combinatorics and the transformation formulae of hypergeometric and basic hypergeometric functions.

Theorem 1

\beta_{n}\allowbreak(x)=\allowbreak\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}\alpha_{k}(x).

\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\beta_{n}(x),

Proof.

First of all, the existence and the mean-squares convergence of the

The justification of the formula for the coefficients

while on the right-hand side we get

a_{n}\int\beta^{2}(x)d\nu(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak a_{n}\hat{\beta}_{n}.

Let us remark that we will use the terms connection coefficient and CC in
exchange.

We know also, that the mean-squares convergence implies that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|a_{n}\right|^{2}<\infty
. If additionally, we know that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|a_{n}\right|^{2}\log^{2}(n+1)<\infty,

then, by the Rademacher–Meshov theorem, we deduce that the series in
question converges not only in

the sequel, all considered densities will be supported only on the bounded

parameters, all these densities are bounded and hence their ratios will be
square integrable. Thus, we will get the condition

satisfied for free. Moreover, in all cases we

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|a_{n}\right|^{2}\log^{2}(n+1)<\infty
is often also naturally satisfied.

2.
Jacobi polynomials and the Pochhammer symbol.

Let us recall the definition of Beta distribution. On one hand, we have the
distribution with the density:

f(x|a,b)=\begin{cases}0\text{,}&\text{if }x\notin[0,1]\text{;}\\
x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}/B(a,b)\text{,}&\text{if }0\leq x\leq 1\text{,}\end{cases}

where
B(a,b)
means the Euler’s beta function, which is defined for all
complex
a,b
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}(a),\mathop{\mathrm{R}e}(b)>0
.

On the other hand the following function is also called density of beta
distributions. It has the following density:

h(x|a,b)=\begin{cases}(x+1)^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}/(B(a,b)2^{a+b-1})\text{,}&\text{if }\left|x\right|\leq 1\text{;}\\
0\text{,}&\text{if otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is common knowledge (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) that the polynomials
that are orthogonal with respect to
h
are the so-called Jacobi polynomials
defined by the formula:

(2.1)

J_{n}(x|a,b)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\left(a+b+n-1\right)^{\left(m\right)}(b+m)^{(n-m)}(x-1)^{m}/2^{m}.

Following simple change of variables under the integral, we deduce that the
following family of polynomials:

(2.2)

K_{n}(x|a,b)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\left(a+b+n-1\right)^{\left(m\right)}(b+m)^{(n-m)}(x-1)^{m},

is orthogonal with respect to the distribution with the density
f
. Above,
we used, the so-called rising factorial or Pochhammer symbol (polynomial)
which is defined by

for all complex

x\neq 0

(x)^{(n)}=\frac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x)}\text{,}

where
\Gamma(x)
denotes the Euler’s gamma function. One can consider also
the so-called falling factorials, denoted by
\left(a\right)_{\left(n\right)}
and defined by

\left(a\right)_{\left(n\right)}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(a-j\right),

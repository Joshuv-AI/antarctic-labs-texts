AgenticControl : An Automated Control Design Framework Using Large Language Models
Mohammad Narimani realnarimani@gmail.com, Seyyed Ali Emami emami@sharif.edu

Abstract
Traditional control system design, reliant on expert knowledge and precise models, struggles with complex, nonlinear, or uncertain dynamics. This paper introduces AgenticControl , a novel multi-agent framework that automates controller design using coordinated Large Language Model (LLM) agents. Through structured JSON communication, these agents handle tasks including controller selection, scenario design, parameter optimization, performance evaluation, and decision-making. Through an actor-critic optimization approach, the system iteratively improves performance while progressing through scenarios of increasing complexity to ensure robustness under nominal conditions, measurement noise, actuator disturbances, and parametric uncertainties. Key innovations include structured multi-agent collaboration, robust optimization mechanisms, and real-time adaptability via in-context learning. Validated across four diverse control systems, namely, DC Motor Position control, Ball and Beam, Inverted Pendulum, and Double Inverted Pendulum, the framework achieves competitive performance against classical methods. Its Full State Feedback solution closely matches Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) results, while the designed PID controller significantly outperforming Matlab ® ’s PIDTuner, reducing PID tracking error by 55% through adaptive parameter exploration. A comparative study of five LLM models reveals distinct optimization profiles, with DeepSeek achieving the fastest convergence. This work demonstrates the potential of LLM-driven control design, paving the way for advanced techniques like model predictive control and reinforcement learning.

Introduction

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into control systems represents a promising frontier in both industrial automation and decision-making processes.
Leveraging their exceptional capabilities in natural language understanding and complex reasoning, LLMs have shown significant potential across diverse applications, including robotics
(
Liu et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2024
)
, autonomous driving
(
Xu et al. 2024
)
, power electronics
(
Cui et al. 2024
)
, and HVAC control
(
Song et al. 2023
)
.
Traditional control design methods, such as manual tuning or model-based optimization, often rely heavily on expert knowledge and precise system models, which can be limiting in the face of complex, nonlinear, or uncertain dynamics
(
Åström and Hägglund 1995
;
Franklin et al. 2010
)
.
Recent advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly in LLMs, offer a transformative opportunity to automate and enhance this process by combining reasoning capabilities with data-driven insights
(
Bommasani et al. 2021
;
Brown et al. 2020
)
.

To address these challenges, this paper introduces
AgenticControl
, a novel multi-agent framework that leverages multiple pre-trained LLM agents to systematically design and refine controller parameters across diverse scenarios. The framework integrates multiple specialized LLM agents with computational modules to iteratively propose, evaluate, and optimize controller configurations through simulation and performance assessment. The proposed approach offers several novelties, including:

1.

Structured Multi-Agent Collaboration:

Our framework introduces a structured multi-agent collaboration that extends existing systems, such as those presented in
(
He et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2024
;
Cui et al. 2024
)
. By integrating multiple LLM agents with computational modules, we ensure both interpretability and precision in the control system design.

2.

Robust Optimization Mechanisms:

We employ unique termination judgment and decision arbitration mechanisms, utilizing dedicated agents to enhance the robustness of the optimization process. This approach distinguishes our framework from single-agent systems or less structured multi-agent designs.

3.

Real-Time Adaptability:

The framework achieves real-time adaptability through the in-context learning capabilities of pre-trained LLMs, eliminating the need for fine-tuning.

4.

Scalable Workflow:

A scalable workflow is implemented using LangGraph, which facilitates efficient coordination among the agents.

2
Related Works

LLMs have shown promise in automating and optimizing control processes, from high-level planning to low-level execution and parameter tuning.
Building on the success of foundation models, many decision-making methods now use LLMs
(
Sun et al. 2025
)
.
As categorized by
Song et al. 2023
, these methods generally fall into three categories: (1) fine-tuning LLMs for specific tasks, (2) pairing LLMs with trainable components, and (3) directly utilizing pre-trained LLMs.
Our approach uses pre-trained LLMs, leveraging their In-Context Learning (ICL) abilities for real-time adaptability in various control situations without needing to retrain them.

Within the category of directly utilizing pre-trained LLMs, a significant trend is the use of multi-agent frameworks where LLMs collaborate as specialized agents to address complex control problems.
For instance,
Chen et al. 2024
proposed an offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework where three LLM agents act as state descriptors, action recommenders, and feedback designers to co-design communication and control. Similarly,
Cui et al. 2024
developed a six-agent multi-agent framework for objective-oriented control design in power electronics, with agents handling task management, objective design, model formulation, algorithm development, parameter tuning, and verification.

Theoretical and practical insights further advance LLM-based decision-making.
He et al. 2024
explored hierarchical RL with LLM agents as planner, actor, and reporter, emphasizing exploration for regret minimization.
Park et al. 2024
analyzed LLM agent performance in interactive decision-making using regret.
Guo et al. 2024
introduced ControlAgent, which uses a central agent to distribute tasks to multiple task-specific agents, along with a Python computation agent for iterative parameter refinement. Additionally,
Jin et al. 2024
developed RobotGPT, which employs a Decision agent, an Evaluation agent, and a Corrector agent to enhance performance.

Recent studies further highlight the versatility of LLMs in control applications.
Bosio and Mueller 2024
combined LLMs with evolutionary algorithms to generate interpretable control policies, represented as Python programs.
Tohma et al. 2025
introduced SmartControl, an interactive PID controller design tool where LLM agents translate natural language into numerical targets, optimized via PSO and Differential Evolution, providing comprehensive outputs for academic and simulation use.
Zucchelli et al. 2025
applied fine-tuned LLMs (7-13B parameters) to control simplified space systems, including orbit transfers and powered descent, showing that these LLMs can generate precise multi-dimensional control outputs with less training data and strong generalization.
Zahedifar et al. 2025
developed the LLM-Controller, which dynamically tunes the parameters of an adaptive controller to changing conditions through methods like chain-of-thought prompting.

Earlier works offer further context on LLM capabilities and limitations in control.
Gashi et al. 2024
found that while GPT-4 can interpret control engineering concepts and analytically design controllers for DC motor speed control, it struggles with manual and graphical tuning processes, revealing both potential and limitations.
Tarczewski et al. 2025
developed an LLM-based Tuning Assistant (TA) for PMSM drives, which, despite improving tuning quality, demonstrated that LLMs alone do not guarantee optimality, underscoring the benefit of integrating complementary computational techniques.
Wang and Qin 2024
proposed an intelligent industrial automation system integrating the MetaGPT and Langchain frameworks to optimize production lines and reduce carbon emissions.

Methodology

AgenticControl
, a multi-agent framework that leverages multiple LLM agents to automate the controller design process through a structured optimization framework.

,

,
LLMActor
,
LLMCritic
,
LLMTerminator
, and
LLMJuror
, each has a specific task in order to selecting a control law, exploring its design space, and delivering robust control solutions.

3.2
Agent Descriptions

3.2.1
LLMSelector

Chooses control laws sequentially, starting with simpler ones, and sets their parameter search ranges.

3.2.2
LLMScenarist

Designs control scenarios of increasing complexity, defining initial conditions, measurement noise, actuator disturbances, and parameter uncertainties.

3.2.3
LLMActor

Suggests controller parameters using optimization heuristics and past performance data within specified ranges.

3.2.4
LLMCritic

Assesses controller performance, comparing simulation metrics to targets and providing feedback for refinement.

3.2.5
LLMTerminator

Decides when to end the optimization loop, evaluating if targets are met or redesign is needed.

3.2.6
LLMJuror

Evaluates ambiguous termination cases, deciding on range adjustments, controller changes, or further exploration.

3.4
Control Design Workflow

3.4.1
System Initialization

The process begins when the plant dynamics (e.g., DC motor, ball and beam, pendulum) is introduced by the user (Fig.
1
), along with a simulation tool for time-response analysis of the designed control law. For clarity and reproducibility, we focus on a simplified case where the
LLMSelector
and
LLMScenarist
are replaced by manual specifications. To this end, we define a predefined list of controllers (P, PI, PD, PID, FSF) and scenarios (nominal, disturbance, uncertainty). Additionally, parameters such as target metrics and maximum iterations are specified manually to facilitate experimental presentation and result reproducibility. These parameters can be determined by dedicated LLMs with default values when needed, but manual specification better serves our validation objectives.

3.4.2
Iterative Parameter Optimization

The core optimization loop consists of five coordinated steps:

Parameter Proposal

The
LLMActor
generates parameter sets using system description, controller type, parameter ranges, and historical data from a shared buffer. The agent employs control theory principles and past feedback to optimize stability, minimize error, and improve transient response. Fig.
5
shows the prompt structure integrating system information and historical data for informed parameter recommendations.

Figure 5:
LLMActor prompt structure integrating system descriptions, controller specifications, parameter constraints, and historical performance data for contextually appropriate parameter proposals.

Simulation and Performance Evaluation

Proposed parameters undergo simulation testing, computing multiple metrics:

•

Mean Squared Error
:
\text{MSE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}^{2}

•

Settling Time
: Time for response to reach and stay within 2% of final value

•

Overshoot
: Maximum deviation beyond steady-state value

•

Rise Time
: Time to reach 90% of steady-state value

•

Zero Crossings
: Number of sign changes in error signal

•

Control Effort
:
\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|dt

•

Stability
: Boolean indicator of the closed-loop system stability

The
LLMCritic
then evaluates results, comparing achieved metrics against targets and providing actionable feedback for parameter refinement.

Buffer Update and Termination Decision

The shared buffer updates with latest results, enabling cumulative learning. The
LLMTerminator
assesses whether performance targets are met or improvements are not sufficient. Subsequently, the
LLMJuror
provides secondary evaluation for robust decision-making on continuation, parameter range adjustment, or controller switching. It is worth mentioning that the
LLMJuror
is provided with a statistical analysis of the parameters chosen by
LLMActor
. Accordingly, the
LLMJuror
may decide to recommend the
LLMActor
to explore further in the current range or modify the search region of the parameters.

3.4.3
Scenario Progression

Upon loop completion, successful performance advances to more complex scenarios, while failure triggers controller switching. This ensures only controllers meeting stringent criteria across all scenarios are retained.

LLMScenarist
generates custom scenarios for specific testing needs, while
LLMSelector
dynamically chooses controllers based on scenario demands and historical performance. These optional agents enhance framework flexibility for specialized applications.

To sum up, AgenticControl leverages structured, agent-driven controller design to systematically select appropriate controller type and refine controller parameters through iterative feedback and scenario-based testing. By integrating LLM capabilities with control theory principles, the framework balances exploration and precision, delivering robust solutions across diverse control challenges.
In the following section, we evaluate our framework using various case studies to highlight the capabilities of its specialized agents.

5
Conclusion

This paper presented
AgenticControl
, a multi-agent LLM framework for automated controller design, validated across four control systems of increasing complexity. The six-agent architecture successfully automated parameter optimization through structured collaboration, with the
LLMJuror
’s adaptive range reconsideration proving particularly effective in improving PID performance from MSE 0.4817 to 0.2694 in the inverted pendulum case. Multi-scenario robustification ensured robust performance under nominal conditions, noise, disturbances, and uncertainties, with results matching or exceeding classical methods. LLM model comparison revealed distinct optimization characteristics, with DeepSeek models showing consistent performance.

AgenticControl
effectively integrates LLM strengths with control engineering needs, delivering a scalable platform that lowers expertise thresholds while preserving accuracy and robustness. The framework’s successful validation across linear/nonlinear and stable/unstable systems confirms its versatility and practical applicability. Future research will explore advanced control paradigms (e.g., reinforcement learning, model predictive control, adaptive control) and assess scalability in industrial contexts. This work lays critical groundwork for AI-enhanced control systems, marking a meaningful advancement toward autonomous design solutions for both academic and industrial settings.

B: Governing Equations of Case Studies

This appendix presents the governing differential equations for the case studies used to validate the AgenticControl framework: DC Motor Position Control, Ball and Beam, Inverted Pendulum, and Double Inverted Pendulum. Parameter values are provided in the original manuscript. These equations describe the system dynamics used for controller optimization.

DC Motor Position Control

The DC motor position control system models the dynamics of current
i
, angular velocity
\omega
, and position
\theta
. The governing equations are:

\displaystyle\dot{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{V-Ri-K\omega}{L},

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{Ki-b\omega}{J},

(2)

\displaystyle\dot{\theta}

\displaystyle=\omega,

(3)

where
V
is the input voltage, and
J
,
b
,
K
,
R
, and
L
are system parameters.

Ball and Beam

The ball and beam system describes the position
r
and velocity
\dot{r}
of a ball on a beam with angle
\alpha
and angular velocity
\dot{\alpha}
. The dynamics are:

\displaystyle\ddot{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}m(r\dot{\alpha}^{2}-g\sin\alpha)}{mR^{2}+J},

(4)

\displaystyle\ddot{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\dot{\alpha}\dot{r}mr+\theta-gmr\cos\alpha}{mr^{2}},

(5)

where
\theta
is the control input (beam angle), and
g
,
m
,
R
, and
J
are system parameters.

Inverted Pendulum

The inverted pendulum models the angle
\theta
and angular velocity
\dot{\theta}
of a pendulum on a cart. The dynamics are:

\displaystyle\ddot{\theta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau-L_{1}gm_{1}\sin\theta-b\dot{\theta}}{L_{1}^{2}m_{1}},

(6)

where
\tau
is the input torque,
m_{1}
is the pendulum mass,
L_{1}
is the rod length,
b
is the damping coefficient, and
g
is the gravitational acceleration.

Double Inverted Pendulum

The double inverted pendulum describes angles
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
and angular velocities
\dot{\theta}_{1}
,
\dot{\theta}_{2}
of two pendulums. Using the Lagrangian approach, the governing equations are derived as

\displaystyle\ddot{\theta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L_{1}^{2}C}\Biggl[\begin{aligned} &-2L_{1}gm_{1}\sin\theta_{1}-2u+L_{1}gm_{2}\sin\theta_{1}\\
&+L_{1}gm_{2}\sin(\theta_{1}-2\theta_{2})\\
&+L_{1}^{2}\dot{\theta}_{1}^{2}m_{2}\sin(2\theta_{1}-2\theta_{2})\\
&+2L_{1}L_{2}\dot{\theta}_{2}^{2}m_{2}\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\end{aligned}\Biggr],

(7)

\displaystyle\ddot{\theta}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L_{1}L_{2}C}\Biggl[\begin{aligned} &2L_{1}^{2}\dot{\theta}_{1}^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\
&-2u\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\
&-L_{1}g(m_{1}+m_{2})\sin\theta_{2}\\
&+L_{1}g(m_{1}+m_{2})\sin(2\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\\
&+L_{1}L_{2}\dot{\theta}_{2}^{2}m_{2}\sin(2\theta_{1}-2\theta_{2}).\end{aligned}\Biggr],

(8)

where
C=2m_{1}+m_{2}-m_{2}\cos(2\theta_{1}-2\theta_{2})
.

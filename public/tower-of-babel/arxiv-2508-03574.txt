Analysis of logics with arithmetic
Michael Benedikt, Chia-Hsuan Lu, and Tony Tan

Abstract
We present new results on finite satisfiability of logics with counting and arithmetic. This includes tight bounds on the complexity for two-variable logic with counting and cardinality comparisons between unary formulas, and also on logics with so-called local Presburger quantifiers. In the process, we provide simpler proofs of some key prior results on finite satisfiability and semi-linearity of the spectrum for these logics.

Introduction

This paper concerns the problem of determining whether a formula in some logic
\mathcal{L}
is satisfied in some finite structure: the
finite satisfiability problem for

\mathcal{L}
. For the fragments of interest to us, the decidability of this problem will imply decidability of many other static analysis problems for the logic, such as validity of a sentence over finite structures and equivalence of two sentences over finite structures.
The problem is known
to be undecidable for first order logic
[
20
]
. In the last decades two paradigms for decidability emerged, one based on restricting to
guarded quantification
, and another restricting to
two variable fragments
.
The two variable fragment of first order logic was shown to have decidable finite satisfiability problem and this was extended to allow “counting quantifiers”, of the form
\exists^{\geq k}y~\phi(x,y)
, where
k
is fixed. The complexity of the satisfiability problem for this logic, normally denoted
\mathsf{C^{2}}
, over finite structures, as well as the complexity of satisfiability when considering arbitrary structures, was isolated in
[
14
]
. The same problem for the sublogic where quantification is guarded was shown to have slightly lower complexity
[
15
]
.

In recent years there has been interest in adding more powerful arithmetic capabilities to these decidable logics. Instead of just asking whether the number of elements satisfying a property is above or below a constant, we can ask whether the cardinality is even, or whether the cardinality of several properties satisfies a linear inequality.
Presburger logic
focuses on linear inequalities between properties, and
local Presburger logic
,
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
, restricts to properties that involve neighbors of a given element. These logics are most naturally applied to finite models, to avoid dealing with addition or parity of infinity: thus in this work,
finite satisfiability will always be our default
, and we just refer to “satisfiability” henceforward. In
[
3
]
it was shown that local Presburger logics are in some sense equally expressive as certain variations of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), and GNN verification problems can be reduced to satisfiability problems for these logics.
It has been shown that satisfiability of Presburger logic is undecidable, while satisfiability of local Presburger logic is decidable
[
1
]
: but the exact complexity of local Presburger logic had not been determined. Another way of enhancing logic with arithmetic involves
global cardinality constraints
. In such an enhancement, we can write a constraint that defines a linear inequality between one variable formulas. But we cannot have such a constraint parameterized by a second free variable. For example, we can write that unary predicate
U
has cardinality below unary predicate
V
. But if
E
and
F
are two binary predicates, we cannot express that the elements that are not
E
adjacent to
x
have greater cardinality than the elements that are not
F
adjacent to
x
.

As with local Presburger logic, it follows from prior results that
\mathsf{C^{2}}
enhanced with global cardinality constraints has decidable (finite) satisfiability problem.
But the exact complexity was again unknown. Decidability for logics
enhanced with global constraints follows from the fact that the
the spectrum of each tuple of unary formulas of the logic is effectively semilinear
. The spectrum of a tuple of unary formulas
\phi_{1}(x)\ldots\phi_{k}(x)
is the set of possible values that the tuple of cardinalities of the satisfiers of the formula can have within a finite model. For example, for the pair of formulas
\phi_{1}(x)=U(x),\phi_{2}(x)=U(x)\wedge V(x)
the spectrum consists of all pairs
(m,n)
with
n\leq m
. The spectrum being effectively semilinear means that we can find a quantifier-free formula of arithmetic that defines it. From effective semilinearity of the spectrum, we easily obtain decidability with additional global constraints.
Here we provide the first tight complexity bounds for several of these problems, one featuring arithmetic over counts of neighbors, another featuring arithmetic over global counts.
See Table
1
for the summary of our new upper bounds.
At the same time, we provide simpler proofs of two fundamental results in the area: decidability of (finite) satisfiability for
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
(see Section
4
), and effective semi-linearity of the spectrum for
\mathsf{C^{2}}
(Section
5
). We complement these positive results with a counterexample to semilinearity of spectra for
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
unary formulas (Section
6
).

Definition 2

The following result gives a bound when

Lemma 3
.

For every integer linear system
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{c}

\exists\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\ (\mathbf{x}\ =\ \mathsf{proj}_{n}\left(\mathbf{y}_{1}\right)+\mathbf{y}_{2})\ \land\ (\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{y}_{1}\ =\ \mathbf{0})\ \land\ (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{y}_{2}\ =\ \mathbf{0})\ \land\ ((\mathbf{y}_{1}=\mathbf{0})\to(\mathbf{y}_{2}=\mathbf{0})),

Logics and prior complexity bounds concerning them

We fix a vocabulary
\sigma
which consists of
n
unary predicates
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
and
m
binary predicates
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
. All of our logics will consist of formulas with at most two variables,
x
and
y
.
We start by reviewing
two variable logic with counting
(
\mathsf{C^{2}}
), built up from atomic formulas
U_{i}(x)
,
U_{i}(y)
,
R_{i}(x,y)
, and
R_{i}(y,x)

via the Boolean operators and the quantifier constructs

\rho(x):=\exists^{\geq k}y\ \phi(x,y)
,
where
\phi
is a
\mathsf{C^{2}}
formula and
k
is a natural number, and similarly with the role of
x
and
y
swapped.

Our semantics will always be over
finite structures
interpreting the vocabulary
\sigma
.
The formula
\rho(y)
above holds in a model
M
at an element
u
for
x
when there are least
k
elements
v
such that
M\models\phi(u,v)
.
The case
k=1
represents standard existential quantification.

Guarded two variable logic with counting
(
\mathsf{GC^{2}}
), restricts
\mathsf{C^{2}}
by requiring quantification to be guarded by an atom. The quantifier rule now includes the forms:

\exists^{\geq k}y\ R_{i}(x,y)\land\phi(x,y),\quad\exists^{\geq k}y\ R_{i}(y,x)\land\phi(x,y),\quad\text{or}\quad\exists^{\geq k}x\ ~U_{i}(x)\land\phi(x).

\mathsf{C^{2}}
can be extended with
global unary cardinality constraints
: these are of
the form

\rho:=\left(\sum_{t\in{\left[n\right]}}\kappa_{t}\cdot|\phi_{t}(x)|\right)\ \circledast\ \delta
,
where
\phi_{t}
are
\mathsf{C^{2}}
formulas in one free variable,

\kappa_{t}
are integers,

\circledast
is an inequality or equality,
and
\delta
is an integer.

\rho
is a sentence, and we define the semantics only for finite structures
M
.
Such
M
satisfies
\rho
if,
letting
s_{t}
be the number of elements in

M
satisfying
\phi_{t}(x)
, we have

\left(\sum_{t\in{\left[n\right]}}\kappa_{t}\cdot s_{t}\right)\ \circledast\ \delta
.
We write
\mathsf{C^{2}}^{+g}
for the extension of
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with global cardinality constraints.

The logic
\mathsf{P^{2}}
replaces
\mathsf{C^{2}}
’s counting quantifier with a
Presburger quantifier
:

\rho(x)\ :=\ \left(\sum_{t\in{\left[m\right]}}\kappa_{t}\cdot{\#_{y}\left[\phi_{t}(x,y)\right]}\right)\ \circledast\ \delta,

where
\kappa_{t}
are integers,

\circledast
is an inequality or equality, and
\delta
is an integer.
In
\mathsf{P^{2}}
we will also allow
unguarded quantification over unary formulas
: if
\phi(x)
is a formula, so is

\exists x\ \phi(x)
.
For example,

\exists x\left({\#_{y}\left[R(x,y)\right]}-3\cdot{\#_{y}\left[R(y,x)\right]}=0\right)

is a sentence of
\mathsf{P^{2}}
that holds in a directed graph when there is an element that has 3 times as many outgoing edges as incoming edges.

The logic
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
(
guarded two variable logic with Presburger quantifiers
) restricts
\mathsf{P^{2}}
by requiring each
\phi_{i}
in a Presburger quantifier to be guarded, although still allowing unguarded unary quantification.
Note that global cardinality constraints can be expressed in
\mathsf{P^{2}}
, but not in
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
.
Following prior papers
[
11
]
, we do not allow a quantifier that tests the modulus of a linear combination of
cardinalities of one dimensional sets, and thus our quantifiers are in a sense weaker than Presburger arithmetic.
A
Presburger modulus constraint
is as above, but allowing
\circledast\ \delta
to be replaced by

(=m\mod n)
, for natural numbers
m,n
with
m<n
.
Likewise,
\mathsf{C^{2}}^{+g}
can be extended to allow modulus constraints.
In the appendix we show that
all of of our results on
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
and
\mathsf{C^{2}}^{+g}
also hold for the extension with modulus constraints
; in the body of the paper we focus on plain
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
and
\mathsf{C^{2}}^{+g}
in the statements.

Finite satisfiability of
\mathsf{GP^{2}}

The goal of this section is to prove:

Theorem 9
.

The finite satisfiability problem of
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
is in
\mathsf{EXP}
.

\mathsf{GP^{2}}
infrastructure.

We fix a vocabulary
\sigma
which consists of
n
unary predicates
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
and
m
binary predicates
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
.
We consider
\mathsf{GP^{2}}

\varphi

\varphi\ :=\ \forall x\ \gamma(x)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[m\right]}}\forall x\forall y\ \left(R_{i}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right)\to\alpha_{i}(x,y)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[n\right]}}\forall x\ U_{i}(x)\to P_{i}(x),

where
\gamma(x)
is a quantifier-free formula,
each
\alpha_{i}(x,y)
is a quantifier-free formula, and
each
P_{i}(x)
is a Presburger quantified formula of the form:

P_{i}(x)\ :=\ \left(\sum_{t\in{\left[m\right]}}\lambda_{i,t}\cdot{\#_{y}\left[R_{t}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right]}\right)\ \circledast_{i}\ \delta_{i}.

\forall x\forall y\ \left(R(x,y)\land x\neq y\right)\to\alpha(x,y)

\mathsf{GP^{2}}

There is a linear time algorithm that converts every
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentence into an equi-satisfiable sentence in normal form
[

]

based on the standard renaming technique for

introduced in
[

,

]
.
See the appendix for an argument.

(Compatibility of 1- and 2-types)

\eta

•

\pi_{1}(x)\land\eta(x,y)\land\pi_{2}(y)\models\bigwedge_{i\in{\left[m\right]}}\left(R_{i}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right)\to\alpha_{i}(x,y)

•

Proof.

5
Spectrum and Finite satisfiability of
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with global constraints

In this section, our goal will be to show:

Theorem 21
.

\mathsf{C^{2}}

\mathsf{NEXP}

We will prove this by characterizing the set of models of a
\mathsf{C^{2}}
formula
with linear constraints. With this characterization in place, we have reduced finite satisfiability of the formula to a check for satisfiability of the conjunction of the linear constraints and the global constraints, which can be done via standard linear algebra solving.

We fix a vocabulary
\sigma

n

U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}

We consider
\mathsf{C^{2}}

\varphi

\varphi\ :=\ \forall x\ \gamma(x)\ \land\ \forall x\ \forall y\ \left(x\neq y\to\alpha(x,y)\right)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[m^{\prime}\right]}}\forall x\ \exists^{=k_{i}}y\ \left(R_{i}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right),

where
\gamma(x)
and
\alpha(x,y)
are quantifier-free,

are binary predicates,

,
and

.

Definition 22

Definition 35

Negative results about the spectrum of
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentences

The
1
-type spectrum of a
\mathsf{C^{2}}
sentence is semilinear, and we showed how to calculate it in
the previous section. For
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
, earlier in the paper we were able to characterize models by a linear system: but the solutions
to this linear system are not in one-to-one correspondence with the number of satisfiers of
1
-types.
Thus a natural question is whether the
1
-type spectrum of a
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentence is semilinear. We answer this below in
the negative.

Consider the following
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
formula
\varphi
over vocabulary
\left\{P_{1},P_{2},E\right\}
:

\displaystyle\varphi\ :=

\displaystyle\forall x\ \left(\left(\neg P_{1}(x)\land P_{2}(x)\right)\lor\left(P_{1}(x)\land\neg P_{2}(x)\right)\right)\ \ \land\ \ \bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq 2}\forall x\ P_{i}(x)\to\gamma_{i}(x)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(x)\ :=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\#_{y}\left[E(x,y)\land P_{1}(y)\right]}-{\#_{y}\left[E(x,y)\land P_{2}(y)\right]}=0

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(x)\ :=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\#_{y}\left[E(y,x)\land P_{1}(y)\right]}=1.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite model of
\varphi
.
A vertex in
\mathcal{G}
is labeled with either
P_{1}
or
P_{2}
.
Let
V_{i}
be the set of vertices in
\mathcal{G}
with label
P_{i}
.
For every
v\in V_{1}
, the number of edges from
v
to
V_{1}

is the same as the number of edges from
v
to
V_{2}
.
For every vertex
v\in V_{2}
, there exists exactly one edge from
V_{1}
to
v
.
Therefore the cardinality of
V_{2}
is the same as the number of edges between vertices of
V_{1}
,
which is bounded by
{\left|V_{1}\right|}\cdot({\left|V_{1}\right|}-1)
.
Thus the 1-type spectrum of
\varphi
is
\left\{(0,a,b,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}\middle|\ b\leq a(a-1)\right\}
.
Hence we have the following negative result.

Theorem 39
.

The 1-type spectrum of a
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentence is not always semilinear.

This does not resolve whether the set of cardinalities of finite models is a semilinear set, and
it leaves open whether
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
with global cardinality constraints is decidable.

Conclusion

In the paper we isolate the complexity of satisfiability for two logics combining relational
structure and arithmetic,
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
and
the logics
\mathsf{C^{2}}
and
\mathsf{GC^{2}}
extended with cardinality constraints. In the process we refine and
simplify proof techniques for analyzing such logics.
\mathsf{GP^{2}}

From normal form sentences to general sentences

In the main body all our results are proven for sentences in normal form.
In this appendix we will show that they also hold for general sentences.
In particular we will sketch the algorithms that transform an arbitrary (
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
/
\mathsf{C^{2}}
) sentence into another sentence in the normal form and recover the properties of the original input sentence from the constructed normal form sentence.
We emphasize that the transformations we provide are a routine adaptation of the textbook transformation to Scott normal form: one can find similar results
in
[
11
]
, for the
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
case,
and in
[
14
]
for the
\mathsf{C^{2}}
case.

We start with
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentences, showing that we can find an equisatisfiable formula in normal form.
Note that in the normal form we allow double universal quantifiers of the form:

\forall x\forall y\ \left(R(x,y)\land x\neq y\right)\to\alpha(x,y)

which can be considered as a shorthand for the sentence:

\forall x\left({\#_{y}\left[R(x,y)\land x\neq y\land\neg\alpha(x,y)\right]}\ =\ 0\right)

Recall that the normal form of a
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentence has the shape:

\varphi\ :=\ \forall x\ \gamma(x)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[m\right]}}\forall x\forall y\ \left(R_{i}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right)\to\alpha_{i}(x,y)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[n\right]}}\forall x\ U_{i}(x)\to P_{i}(x),

where

•

\gamma(x)
is a quantifier-free formula,

•

each
\alpha_{i}(x,y)
is a quantifier-free formula,

•

each
P_{i}(x)
is a Presburger quantified formula of the form:

P_{i}(x)\ :=\ \left(\sum_{t\in{\left[m\right]}}\lambda_{i,t}\cdot{\#_{y}\left[R_{t}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right]}\ \circledast_{i}\ \delta_{i}\right).

The main idea is as follows.
Let
\psi
be an arbitrary
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
sentence.
By pushing every negation inward, we may assume that it is in negation normal form,
i.e., every negation is applied only on atomic formulas.
We then repeatedly replace every subformula
\nu(x)
of one free variable
x
with a fresh unary predicate
U(x)
, and conjoin the original formula with the “definition” of
U(x)
:

\forall x\ U(x)\to\nu(x)

Intuitively what this means is that
the subformula
\nu(x)
is renamed as a unary predicate
U(x)
.
The algorithm starts bottom-up starting from the subformula
\nu(x)

with the lowest quantifier rank.
It can be shown that the number of renaming required to obtain the normal form is linear in the length of the original sentence
\psi

[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
and the whole transformation runs in linear time.

Note that unguarded unary quantification is allowed in
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
.
For universally-quantified formulas,
\forall x\ U(x)
is already captured by the normal form.
For existential-quantified
\psi:=\exists x\ U(x)
,
we introduce fresh unary predicates
U^{\prime}
and binary predicate
R_{U}
, and replace
\psi
by

\left(\forall x\ U(x)\lor U^{\prime}(x)\right)\ \land\ \left(\forall x\ U^{\prime}(x)\to{\#_{y}\left[R_{U}(x,y)\land U(y)\right]}=1\right).

Note also that we can recover the model of the original sentence from the model of the constructed normal form sentence.
We simply project out the fresh unary predicates introduced when constructing the normal form sentence.

Now, we turn to
\mathsf{C^{2}}
sentences, and recall from the body of the paper the normal form we utilized:

\varphi\ :=\ \forall x\ \gamma(x)\ \land\ \forall x\ \forall y\ \left(x\neq y\to\alpha(x,y)\right)\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i\in{\left[m^{\prime}\right]}}\forall x\ \exists^{=k_{i}}y\ \left(R_{i}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right),

where
\gamma(x)
and
\alpha(x,y)
are quantifier-free formulas,

R_{i}
are binary predicates,
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.

The main idea of the transformation is similar to the
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
case:
Rename every subformula
\nu(x)
of one free variable
x
with a fresh unary predicate
U(x)
and conjunct the original sentence with
\forall x\ U(x)\to\nu(x)
.
It was shown in
[
14
,
16
]
that there is a linear time algorithm that transforms
an arbitrary
\mathsf{C^{2}}
sentence
\varphi
over vocabulary
\sigma
to a sentence
\varphi^{*}
in normal form (with additional unary predicates
V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}
) such that:

•

For every model
\mathcal{A}\models\varphi
with domain size at least
1+\max_{i\in[m^{\prime}]}k_{i}
,
there is a model
\mathcal{A}^{*}\models\varphi^{*}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is an extension of
\mathcal{A}
to the vocabulary
\sigma^{*}:=\sigma\cup\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}\}
with

A=A^{*}
and
S^{\mathcal{A}}=S^{\mathcal{A}^{*}}
, for every relation symbol
S\in\sigma
.

•

Conversely,
for every model
\mathcal{A}^{*}\models\varphi^{*}
with domain size at least
1+\max_{i\in[m^{\prime}]}k_{i}
,
there is a model
\mathcal{A}\models\varphi
, where
\mathcal{A}
is obtained by projecting out the predicates
V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}
in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Using these two properties, we can generalise Theorem
26
to arbitrary
\mathsf{C^{2}}
sentences.
First, all the sizes of the models up to

1+\max_{i\in[m^{\prime}]}k_{i}
can be encoded inside the corresponding Presburger formula
\Psi^{\varphi}(\mathbf{x})

mentioned in Lemma
38
.
Second, we can recover
\Pi\text{-}\mathsf{SPEC}
from
\Pi^{*}\textsf{-SPEC}
,
where
\Pi
and
\Pi^{*}
are the set of all 1-types w.r.t.
\sigma
and
\sigma^{*}
,
via the following identity, for every 1-type
\pi
over
\sigma
:

x_{\pi}\ =\ \sum_{\pi^{*}\supseteq\pi\ \text{and}\ \pi^{*}\ \text{is 1-type over}\ \sigma^{*}}x_{\pi^{*}}

Proof of the small core lemma: lemma
33

Recall the statement of Lemma
33
:

For every
\sigma
-structure
\mathcal{G}
, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathcal{G}
has a
\ell
-core

Recall from the body that the intuition behind the construction is to repeatedly select a 1-type or tuple that violates the

-core condition

\mathcal{H}
.

\mathcal{H}_{0}
be an empty
\sigma
-structure.

•

a 1-type
\pi

\ell

•

a tuple

that is realized in
\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{i-1}

\ell
pairs of vertices.

\mathcal{H}_{i}
as follows:

•

\pi
is selected, then
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is obtained by extending
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}

\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{H}

\pi
.

\ell
vertices added.

•

If a tuple
\left\langle\pi_{1},\eta,\pi_{2}\right\rangle
is selected,

\mathcal{H}_{i}
is obtained by extending
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
with all vertices from

involved in a pair realizing

.
Note that there are at most
2\ell
vertices added.

•

\ell

\mathcal{G}

\pi

Extensions to modulus constraints

Recall that in the body of the paper, cardinality constraints, either within
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
or the global cardinality constraints added to
\mathsf{C^{2}}
, did not constrain the modulus of a count. So, for example, in
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
we could not state that the number of neighbors of an element with a given property is even. And similarly, we could not add to
\mathsf{C^{2}}
a constraint that the total number of elements in a model satisfying a formula is an even number. We stated in the body that we can add such modulus constraints and obtain the same results.

In this appendix we will first indicate how to extend the satisfiability result for
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
to the case when existential quantifiers are allowed in the Presburger quantifiers, i.e., when the formula
P(x)
is of the form:

P_{i}(x)\ :=\ \exists x_{1}\cdots\exists x_{n}\ \sum_{t\in{\left[m\right]}}\lambda_{t}\cdot{\#_{y}\left[R_{t}(x,y)\land x\neq y\right]}\ +\ \sum_{t\in{\left[n\right]}}\lambda_{t}^{\prime}\cdot x_{t}\ \circledast_{i}\ \delta.

Note that the existential variables captures a modulus equation, as
a\equiv b\bmod p
can be rewritten as:

\exists x_{1}\exists x_{2}\ a+x_{1}p=b+x_{2}p

We claim that Theorem
9
still holds under such a generalization.
The proof is basically the same.
The only difference is that in the definition of characteristic system

\mathcal{C}^{\varphi}_{\pi}(\mathbf{x})

defined in Definition
12
the variables
\mathbf{x}
include all the existential variables.
Similarly for the system
\mathcal{Q}^{\varphi}(\mathbf{x})

defined in Definition
17
.
Then, we observe that
Lemmas
16
,
18
, and
20
still hold for such systems
and the satisfiability problem of
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
stays in
\mathsf{EXP}
.

For the extension of
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with global cardinality and modulus constraints, the situation is more obvious. We have already shown how to get a (modulus-constraint free) semilinear representation for the type spectrum vectors. We can compose this with modulus constraints in addition to linear arithmetic constraints and solve using standard techniques for Presburger arithmetic.
Rewriting modulus constraint into a single linear equation does not change the number of constraints in each disjunct in the formula
\Psi^{\varphi}(\mathbf{x})
.
Note that the argument here is completely generic, and the same comment holds to show that for
any
decidability extension of Presburger arithmetic (e.g. Büchi Arithemetic or Semenov Arithmetic
[
18
]
),
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with global cardinality constraints of that form is decidable.

Expressiveness separation of the considered languages

We mentioned that the languages we consider in this paper –
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
and
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with global cardinality constraints – are incomparable in expressiveness.

\mathsf{GP^{2}}
contains sentences not in
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with constraints, because (as we showed in the paper) the spectrum of GP2 may not be semilinear, while for
\mathsf{C^{2}}
with constraints the specctrum is always semilinear.

On the other hand, even ordinary
\mathsf{C^{2}}
contains sentences not in
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
: just consider the sentence restricting the cardinality of the model to some fixed number, like
2
. In
\mathsf{GP^{2}}
every sentence that is consistent has models of unbounded finite cardinality, since we can always clone a connected component without disturbing the truth value of any sentence.

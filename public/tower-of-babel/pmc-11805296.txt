Developing an AI-Powered Algorithm for Automated Detection and Classification of Dental Caries from Intraoral Radiographs: A Machine Learning Approach
Authors: Mohammad Haider
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis systematic review aims to assess the diagnostic accuracy of artificial intelligence (AI) models specifically developed for detecting and classifying dental caries. A comprehensive electronic literature search was performed on the PubMed, Web of Science, SCOPUS, and Embase databases. The search yielded a total of 397 results. We examined 10 articles that satisfied the selection criteria. In summary, AI-based models show high diagnostic accuracy in recognizing dental caries by analyzing dental radiography pictures.

INTRODUCTION

Artificial intelligence (AI) enables machines to perform activities like humans. Dental radiography has improved medical and dental AI.[1] Since X-ray images are digital, they can be converted to computer language. AI aids radiologists in accuracy and reliability. Dental caries, the most prevalent dental condition, can be dangerous if neglected.[2] Dental caries is diagnosed in numerous methods. Some caries are too small to see without radiography. X-rays reveal cavities and root damage.[3] AI-based caries detection is gaining acceptance in science.[45] Publications use bitewing and periapical dental radiographs.[6] Machine learning-based AI systems for intraoral radiograph dental caries identification and categorization are evaluated in this systematic review.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This systematic evaluation objectively assessed AI-driven machine learning algorithms for dental caries detection and categorization from intraoral radiographs.

Find relevant studies to start this review. A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, IEEE Xplore, Scopus, and Web of Science. The search terms included “AI,” “machine learning,” “dental caries,” “intraoral radiographs,” and “automated detection.” English literature on AI in dental radiography was searched from its origin to the present. Uncompliant articles were removed.

RESULTS

We searched databases using keywords related to dental caries, radiography, and machine learning to find 397 records. A total of 56 of the 123 items were eliminated after reviewing their titles and abstracts because they did not meet our inclusion criteria. A total of 57 of the initial 67 full-text papers were deleted due to irrelevant study focus or poor data quality. DISCUSSION

AI caries detection reliability was assessed in this systematic review. AI-based caries detection accuracy is examined.[7891011121314] Devito et al.[7] collected bitewing radiographs of 80 removed teeth. Twenty-five dentists and a neural network evaluated proximal caries radiographs. With ROC curve regions of 0.884 and 0.634, neural networks outperformed humans.

Schwendicke et al.[15] studied AI’s cost-effectiveness in proximal caries detection. We also examined Cantu et al.[5] It was cheaper and more accurate to diagnose caries with AI. Lee et al.[8] studied CNNs’ early carious lesion detection. Dentist 1 increased from 85.34% to 92.12%, dentist 2 from 85.86% to 93.72%, and dentist 3 from 69.11% to 79.06%. Vinayahalingam et al.[10] evaluated a third-molar carious lesion (CL) classification algorithm with 0.87 accuracy, 0.86 sensitivity, 0.88 specificity, and 0.90 AUC.

Lian et al.[16] found that deep learning classified CLs on panoramic radiographs like professional dentists. The Sørensen similarity index was 0.66, and accuracy was 0.98. Moran et al.[17] reported that the CNN-based model outperformed the reference model with 0.73 accuracy.

The findings recommend training datasets with few samples with transfer learning. A good regularization strategy may help increase model performance. Bitewing radiography detects cavities between teeth best.[18] Bitewing radiography showed CLs in 50% of our investigations. AI improved accuracy with CBCT pictures in one study.[14] Proximal CL is diagnosed four times more erroneously by inexperienced examiners.[19] An automated dental radiography image aid system can help rookie examiners overcome these limits by offering a reliable and consistent diagnostic result.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, AI-based models accurately identify CLs by analyzing dental radiography pictures. Additionally, each AI model has pros and cons. Thus, the work’s goals and needs should determine the model. Future studies should accurately portray AI model capabilities.

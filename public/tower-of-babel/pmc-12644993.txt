Occupational health and safety employees and emerging competences in environmental field in mining and other sectors in Poland
Authors: Justyna Woźniak, Paweł Strzałkowski, Katarzyna Pactwa, Paweł Bęś, Maria Szalek
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The aim of the article was to identify and analyze the requirements of the changing labour market for occupational health and safety (OHS) service employees, from the employee level in Poland. Competencies in the field of environmental protection for these positions constituted the main cognitive trend. Attention was focused on the recommendations of the new strategic framework on health and safety at work for 2021–2027 considering e.g. ecological transformation. Several research methods were used: a literature review, an exploratory national job offer as an introduction to survey research, and a survey. Review job offers showed that there is a demand for new competences in Poland that are focused on sustainable development. This information was verified through an anonymous survey aimed at examining the opinions of health and safety service employees on professional competences in the field of the environment, based on specified criteria and identified limitations. The analyses showed that there are significant differences between the requirements set by employers and the tasks performed by health and safety service employees. The survey results were used as a basis for formulating recommendations that may contribute to legislative changes and set further directions for analysis and research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26141-7.

Introduction

Occupational health and safety is a multidisciplinary activity that aims to identify, assess and control hazards arising in or resulting from the workplace that may have an adverse impact on the health and well-being of employees1. The basic criterion for assessing the level of safety in workplaces is the accident rate. Generally, creating safe conditions aims to achieve accident-free conditions (“Vision Zero”). To achieve this, safety management must be carried out at the highest level, making it a priority for the company’s operation. This contributes to improving employee management, reducing the number of accidents, near misses, and occupational injuries, as well as improving business operations2. This is possible by using the appropriate competences of people responsible for occupational safety management.

How should competences be understood? According to Boyatzis3, it is the potential that contributes to meeting the requirements of a given job position. According to the Cambridge Dictionary definition it is an important skill that is needed to do a job4. According to the Encyclopedia of Management5 it is a summary of knowledge, skills and responsibility, while in the Encyclopedia of Organization and Management6 competencies are defined as the scope of powers, duties and responsibilities assigned to a specific position in the organization. For research purposes, this last definition most accurately describes the competencies analyzed in the article are determined by the progress of civilization, which means that employees are expected to have increasingly higher professional competences7. According to Chłoń-Domińczak et al.8, the most important elements of human capital among employers in Poland include employee competences (knowledge and skills), qualifications (diplomas and certificates), commitment and high efficiency, and there is a need for skills in pursuit of sustainable development9. Including the idea of sustainable development (among others environmental protection issues) into existing qualifications and acquiring new competences is a very important element in strategic EU documents. The assumption of the Europe 2020 strategy (COM (2010) 2020)10 is that environmental education should cover the entire society, all age and professional groups, as well as decision-makers at the central and local levels11. We define environmental competences for OHS professionals as the validated combination of knowledge, skills and behaviours that enables them to identify and manage environmental aspects and impacts that affect worker health and safety, regulatory compliance and the organisation’s sustainable performance. This definition is consistent with EU skills taxonomies (ESCO) and with competence requirements embedded in ISO 14,001 and ISO 45,001 management system standards12,13.

The reason for initiating research work was to notice a gap in existing studies regarding the change of competences in the OHS service positions in Poland. The next step in taking up the research topic was the EU report toward more environmentally sustainable practices and their impact on OHS14. The last decade has seen a radical change in the approach to OHS. There is a visible trend of integrating OHS with the environment and sustainable development15. This phenomenon is becoming quite common in the Polish economy. In many cases, this is a challenge due to the professional preparation and scope of responsibilities of people employed in the OHS industry. The scale of employees’ preparation and approach to combining OHS issues with the implementation of environmental tasks becomes crucial to assessing the accuracy of such an approach, which is referred to in this study. In order to assess the readiness and possibilities of combining OHS issues with environmental tasks, a survey was conducted among employees responsible for occupational safety, as well as an exploratory analysis of job offers as an introduction to the formulation of the survey.

The collected research material allowed us to define the needs of employees for better integration of occupational safety tasks with environmental issues, as well as the directions of necessary changes. This data formed the basis for formulating recommendations that may contribute to legislative changes and set further directions for analysis and research. This study is the first approach to the issue of combining OHS and environmental competences in line with the European Union’s strategy. This work may also be a starting point for a broad discussion in this area.

The article consists of 7 chapters. The literature analysis (2.1–2.4) presents the EU and national review of regulations and guidelines, formal and legal conditions of the occupational health and safety service in Poland, the specificity and structure of OHS workers in mining. On this basis, a research methodology was formulated and research tools were selected (3). Chapter 4 presents the scope of data and detailed research results. The limitations identified during the research were indicated (5), discussion (6) and conclusions were drawn (7).

Literature review

Sustainable development points to the need for a broader view of occupational health and safety issues, including environmental and digital aspects. In the context of Polish and foreign formal conditions, this issue is significantly limited. Therefore, analysis of this issue seems crucial for a broader understanding. A review of the literature allows one to move from general policy factors to national solutions, prior evidence of competence, and international relevance.

OHS in the light of sustainable development

The long-term government program to improve safety and working conditions in Poland is a tool for implementing the provisions of the European Commission, including: in the EU strategic framework document on health and safety at work for 2021–202716, OSH in a changing world of work. The national program considers the objectives also set out in other important documents, such as Agenda 2030, and the European Green Deal, which correspond to environmental issues. Following principles that care for the environment and society has not become a fad, but a trend. In 2015, the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development presented 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and 169 related tasks as a continuation of the Millennium Development Goals17. This statement is consistent with the position of the International Labour Organization (ILO)18, a tripartite UN agency that has been bringing together governments, employers and employees of 187 member states since 1919 to set labour standards, develop policies and programs promoting decent work for all women and men. In addition, ILO raises the issue of the Future of Work (FoW), emphasizing that work in a global perspective is undergoing transformation, resulting, among others, from technological progress, climate change, as well as demographic changes affecting the economy and employment. Based on data published by Cambridge University Press19, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) related to health and safety issues in Poland continue to face significant challenges (goal 3) and ongoing challenges (goal 8).

When describing the relationship between SD and OHS, it should be emphasized that these concepts should be treated together, considering their mutual influence. From the perspective of the enterprise, this approach promotes the company’s economic development. A safe and healthy employee is productive. On the other hand, high accident rates negatively affect the entity’s image and reduce demand for products and services20,21. However, it should be remembered that effectiveness in this area will not be ensured only by protection against common workplace hazards, but by a broader look at the implementation of SDG 3 through the development of management systems (workload) and stress minimization. Working on problem solving can ensure the implementation of the SD idea22. Milea et al.23 wrote about the positive impact of SDG implementation on accident rates and employee well-being. They also pointed to the challenges of coordinating sustainable activities in the context of OHS, including the lack of a unified framework for their implementation, limited organizational resources, and varying levels of awareness of SD issues. They also pointed to the need for research that takes into account industry and regional differences.

Incorporating and validating the implementation of sustainable development concepts in the workplace is a topic of scientific research. Moldovan et al.24describing the example of a healthcare facility, defined innovative indicators used to assess sustainability. The implemented solution contributed to improving the culture of sustainable development. Another example of SD being incorporated into company practices is the assessment of industries that have a significant impact on the environment25 or the identification of challenges related to the introduction of sustainable sectoral solutions26.

Another sustainable development goal related to OHS is SDG 4 Quality education. The importance of education on OHS is emphasized by Kavouras et al.27, encouraging the introduction of occupational safety issues into the curricula, primarily in engineering fields. This is justified because it has been confirmed that including OHS in university curricula contributes to reducing the accident rate28. Research was carried out29 on the assessment of the effectiveness of knowledge acquisition by Polish mine employees based on different training methods. It turned out that employee occupational health and safety competencies can be shaped by the appropriate selection of training methods. The computer methods of visualization and simulation aroused the greatest interest among recipients. Going further towards the implementation of SDG 4, it can be seen in education to increase the competencies of employees of OHS service.

Formal and legal conditions of the OHS service in Poland. OHS service in mining

The implementation of tasks in the field of occupational safety in Poland is described in legal regulations. The regulation of the Council of Ministers of September 2, 199730 on the OHS service specifies the scope of activities for the occupational health and safety service (§ 2.1).

The list includes 22 activities in the field of OHS of a control, information, analytical, development, documentation, regulatory, opinion-giving, advisory, training, organizational, consulting, commission or popularization nature. It should be emphasized that persons responsible for occupational safety cannot be burdened with any other tasks than those indicated in the law.

All tasks (22) can be performed by people who have appropriate competences acquired during vocational training or higher education. This is due to the great responsibility for creating safe working conditions aimed at protecting the health and life of employees. Moreover, in accordance with the Geological and Mining Law31, in order to perform the tasks of the OHS service in mining plants, such a person must have confirmed qualifications to perform these activities issued by the Director of the District Mining Office (art. 58). This requirement is justified by the specific threats to this business activity.

If more than 100 employees are employed, the employer is obliged to establish OHS Service, which employs an employee with appropriate competences to perform these tasks. For every 600 employees, the employer is obliged to employ one full-time OHS employee, while in the case of employing from 100 to 600 employees, the employer employs an employee for any full-time position. In the event of a decision by the controlling body - the Inspector at the State Labour Inspectorate - the employer may be obliged to increase the number or size of full-time positions. For enterprises with lower employment levels, the tasks are performed by the employer or the employer entrusts them to an external company (outsourcing) or an employee employed in other work30. This leaves some doubt about the time and personal possibilities of carrying out tasks in the field of occupational safety, because it is permanent.

The Regulation30 indicates the subordination of the OHS service employees and the positions in which persons perform tasks in the field of work safety. Generally, the OHS service reports directly to the highest management (employer, management board member), but in practice there are also solutions where the OHS service employees report to the OHS director, who is a direct subordinate of the employer. The OHS service employees are employed in the positions of inspector, senior inspector, specialist, senior specialist and chief specialist. The position for which an employee is employed depends on the stage of completed education and the length of professional experience30. Despite these legal regulations, in practice there are other job positions, e.g. the OHS assistant, etc. Importantly, in the mining industry, the positions defined in the law include the fact that the employee is an occupational supervisor in the OHS specialty and is directly subordinated to under the top management and indirectly under the mining plant operations manager, with whom he must cooperate closely31.

Competencies of OHS workers in research

Current research on the knowledge, skills and competences of the OHS service employees in national conditions was conducted, among others, by Lotko32. When analysing the learning outcomes of occupational health and safety studies, no issues related to environmental protection or sustainable development were identified, while at the same time emphasizing the role of lifelong learning and self-education in the profession. Dobrzyńska33 conducted survey research on the characteristics of the OHS service in Poland (structure of the service, conditions for entering the profession and professional advancement, conditions and form of employment in the OHS service, as well as sources of satisfaction and professional difficulties). Almost half of the surveyed OHS service representatives indicated improving qualifications as the most important factor in professional development. Sadłowska-Wrzesińska34 emphasized the development of social competences of the OHS specialist, emphasizing that the preparation of OHS specialists and their development should be broad-based in order to meet the new challenges of a changing work environment. The literature increasingly talks about green jobs35,36 and the greening of professions37. The European Working Conditions Telephone Survey36 shows that the ecological transition will have an impact on jobs, and new jobs will be created that may require improved skills. Policies designed for a green economy and sustainable development require deep awareness from many parties38. Considering the above research results do not identify previously coordinated education in the field of OHS and the subject of sustainable development or environmental protection and, on the other hand, existing and changing legal regulations and analyses confirming the impact of environmental issues and their importance in the functioning of companies, a research gap was identified on the Polish market. Moreover, the need to diagnose the requirements for occupational health and safety specialists and the duties they actually perform is consistent with the conclusions of international research39. While prior Polish studies (e.g., Lotko32, Dobrzyńska33 examined knowledge, skills and organisational characteristics of the OHS service, they did not explicitly address the integration of environmental competences into OHS roles nor propose a competence model aligned with sustainable development. This gap motivates our empirical analysis and the proposed model in the conclusions.

International context: integrating OSH and environmental competences in selected EU countries

The integration of occupational safety and health (OSH) with environmental management and sustainable development is increasingly visible across the EU policy landscape, notably in the EU Strategic Framework on OSH 2021–2027 and the Green Deal/Circular Economy agenda, which anticipate OSH implications of greener production systems and supply chains16. The integration problem has been discussed in international literature40. These EU-level directions call for competence development that spans traditional OSH and environmental domains.

In Czech Republik, OSH is primarily regulated through the Labour Code and Act No. 309/2006 Coll., which transposes EU directives and specifies additional OSH requirements such as workplace design, equipment and first aid. Although the legislation does not explicitly merge OSH and environmental roles, organisations often operationalise both through management systems and role-specific competences41,42.

In Germany, the OSH framework combines state and statutory accident insurance institutions under a dual system, with the Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG) requiring risk assessment, preventive measures and worker information. In practice, many German companies align OSH (ISO 45001) with environmental management (ISO 14001), emphasising competences that affect environmental performance and compliance13,16,43,44.

In the Netherlands, the Working Conditions Act (Arbowet) obliges employers to ensure safe and healthy work and to maintain a documented risk assessment (RI&E), while sectoral provides practical solutions and enforcement is carried out by the Netherlands Labour Authority. As in other EU countries, integration with environmental management systems is expanding in areas where risk and compliance intersect, such as dangerous substances, emissions and waste16,45,46.

Spanish national regulations on occupational health and safety do not explicitly refer to environmental protection47. However, the national health and safety strategy and programs48. identify key policy instruments for improving the effectiveness of the health and safety system by setting out the most important priorities and actions to improve the health and safety of workers. And although these priorities do not relate to environmental competences, they do point to the need to take climate and environmental change issues into account in OSH matters.

In Italy, it seems that combining occupational safety and environmental protection competencies is not practiced. As Colombo points out49, cooperation between health, safety, and environment specialists can be effective in operational decision-making. This changes the approach to shaping a broader awareness of combining these competencies. Nevertheless, trade unions, associations, and national policies should contribute to the transformation of HSE professionals’ competencies.

Taken together, these country snapshots show converging expectations: employers conduct systematic risk management, OSH professionals increasingly interact with environmental aspects and competence requirements are embedded in management systems. This EU-wide trend supports the focus on environmental competences within the OHS service13,16.

The study used several research methods, i.e. a review of international and domestic literature (regulations, guidelines, scientific articles and other sources), exploratory analysis of OHS job offers on the domestic market, as an introduction to the main survey. Survey design.The survey instrument was designed based on verification of market expectations and employers’ requirements.Step 4. Data collection.The designed survey was implemented and distributed to the selected research sample. Data collection was carried out in accordance with the established methodological assumptions.Step 5. Data analysis and interpretation.The final stage encompassed the statistical analysis, and interpretation of the obtained results. Particular attention was paid to discussing the findings in the context of existing literature and implications for the OHS field.

The main diagnostic tool used was a survey (ESM Appendix 1), preceded by an analysis of job offers on the Polish market (as a tool for identifying the environmental competences of the OHS service employees expected from the employer). At this stage, one of the popular websites in Poland, which gathers the largest number of users, was used as a data source. This database was used in scientific research by Polish authors, including:50–56, who examined competencies at various job positions. The exploratory analysis included a review of job offers for the position of the OHS/environmental protection specialist published in 2021–2023 (in the same month).

Participants were informed about the aim of the research and that participation was entirely voluntary. Participants were informed of the survey’s purpose. No personal or sensitive data was collected, ensuring complete anonymity and confidentiality of responses. The study was conducted in accordance with applicable regulations and principles. In accordance with applicable regulations in the institutions to which the authors belong, there was no obligation or justification for the study to be reviewed and approved by an Ethics Committee. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The survey questionnaire was designed using an online tool. The survey questions were formulated on the basis of the literature, applicable regulations, knowledge and experience of the authors and a review of market offers. Then, the questionnaire was placed in the cloud and the generated link was sent to respondents who had appropriate specialist knowledge and experience in the practice of the OHS services. In order to reach these employees, a communication channel was used, a link to the survey was placed on the forums of social media groups associating people working in the field of OHS (responses were collected on January 26, 2024 - February 9, 2024).

The authors of the study did not collect any personal and identification data of the respondents. The survey was aimed at collecting the opinions of the OHS service employees on the changing requirements for these positions in accordance with the CAWI (Computer-Assisted Web Interviewing) method57. Microsoft Excel was used to analyse the results.

Data and results

National sample characteristics and overall results

The exploratory analysis of job offers was a tool for identifying the expected competences from the employer towards the OHS service employees. With a view to incorporating the idea of sustainable development and environmental protection needs into existing qualifications, a preliminary assessment of job offers was adopted. The records were selected according to the categories of available offers: the OHS/Environmental Protection, Specialist position. The following criteria were adopted: review of requirements and obligations for these offers, i.e. environmental protection components: reporting (e.g. emissions), waste management, documentation, also fire protection, periodic training, ISO. The offer analysis was complemented by education: higher/postgraduate in OHS and environmental protection, represented industry (according to Polish Classification of Activities - in Polish PKD), employment level (< 100 people, 100–600, > 600), full-time employment (full-time or part-time), comments.

The most frequently occurring requirements and obligations in the field of environmental protection were environmental documentation, fire regulations, reporting (e.g. emissions), waste management, ISO training and periodic OHS training.

Unfortunately, there was no representative of the mining industry among the offers analyzed. Among strategic mining entities in Poland58, there is a visible trend of posting current job offers on the main principles of these entities. For the same application of analysis, there were no providers of the OHS workers in mining. The search was extended to include the government’s Central Database of Job Offers59, which is a universal database of employment offices. These observations concern the application of research assumptions that job offers intended for mining entities in Poland are not made available on publicly available websites. Due to the fact that the included (strategic) mining companies in Poland are large enterprises (> 600 employees), the organizational structures of the OHS services have been made available. There are no offers of the OHS/environmental protection services for the mining industry, indicating a lack of demand for this type of activity. For this reason, the main diagnostic tool CAWI was implemented, reaching, among others, to specialists from the mining industry.

The 428 survey responses were received, representing 18 Polish Classification of Activities, including B. Mining and quarrying. The research results were limited to the tasks performed by an occupational health and safety service employee employed under an employment contract. The results show that most respondents hold positions listed in the OHS Service Regulation30: 38.62% OHS specialist; 18.86% OHS chief specialist; 16.47% OHS senior inspector; 10.48% OHS senior specialist; 10.18% OHS inspector.

The obtained results indicate that OHS service employees occasionally perform tasks within the analyzed environmental protection competencies. According to the Polish classification of professions and specialties63, among others: an environmental protection specialist has this scope of activities on its list of professional tasks.

When asked whether the OHS position is too responsible to be combined with environmental protection and fire protection, the prevailing answer was affirmative (41.26%), regardless of the size of the enterprise, age or length of service. The responses of the OHS service employees regarding combining duties with “environmental protection” issues were mostly negative, identical to those in the next question (fire regulation issues - in Polish PPOŻ). Only 9.88% of respondents expressed the opinion that the position of the OHS service is not so responsible that it should be combined with environmental and fire protection issues. 3.89% chose the answer “not applicable”.

The analysis of survey responses (excluding the “not applicable” responses) showed that in small workplaces (< 100 employees) there is greater consent among employees to combine OHS issues with environmental protection (33% of respondents) (Fig. 1). This is probably related to the provision of full-time employment to the employee, which is conditional on the increase in the scope of responsibilities. On the other hand, the workload of the OHS service employee in carrying out work safety tasks does not change. There is also greater acceptance among employees with little experience in combining OHS issues with environmental protection (22% of respondents with less than a year of experience). In fact, it tends to decline with increasing seniority. This is related to the low awareness of young employees about the scope of duties of the OHS service, and on the other hand, the desire to gain experience and adapt to market needs. Another reason may be the fact that employees with little experience are more flexible and less burdened in their private lives.

These answers were confirmed by the statement by approximately 73% of respondents that the OHS position is too responsible to be combined with “environmental protection” issues and too responsible to be combined with fire protection issues (67%).

The survey results show that employers often add additional responsibilities to the OHS service employees, including: with fire protection; administration; broadly understood occupational medicine; audits; supplying, managing and issuing work clothes and footwear; with HR and payroll, documentation and subcontractor management. The answers also included additional responsibilities related to the creation of OHS instructions, writing EU projects, assistance in obtaining environmental permits, reporting, programming, training, bidding and budgeting for public procurement. The scope of responsibilities of the OHS service in Poland is clearly defined in the regulations30, but as the survey results show, it is often not applied in practice.

The mining-specific results

The employment rate in the workplace where the OHS services for mining are carried out is > 600 (53.33%), 100–600 (33.33%) and < 100 people (13.33%). Over 93% of respondents indicated that health and safety issues are the basic duties of their job, while less than 27% indicated “environmental protection” issues as an additional element (these were issues of waste management and environmental reporting regarding the operation of BDO and KOBIZE). This is due to the type of activity and the hazards present in the mining work environment64. In addition, the high accident rate and more serious consequences of accidents (death or serious injury) compared to other industries require intensified practical measures to prevent these phenomena. Therefore, the tasks performed by OHS service employees are not supplemented by other aspects. In addition, state supervision of mining plants, which aims to protect the health and lives of employees, often requires advanced and complex safety measures. Nevertheless, in practice, in the case of small mining plants where the specific nature of mining activities differs from that of underground mines (e.g. surface mining of sand and gravel), OHS service employees can be seen to be burdened with tasks related to environmental protection. In this context, the implementation of legal solutions is important to enable such practices.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Although the analysed sample met the minimum requirements for quantitative analysis, its representativeness may be restricted with respect to regional coverage, company size and industry structure. Moreover, the analysis has a regional focus, drawing exclusively on data from the Polish labour market, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other national or international contexts where OSH practices, regulatory frameworks and labour market dynamics may differ substantially. Time constraints also affected the scope of data collection, as the job advertisement analysis captures only a specific moment and does not reflect seasonal or long-term trends in demand for OSH professionals. In addition, the study relied on a single job offer database available on the Polish market, which may provide only a partial picture of the actual employment landscape. Both the job advertisement analysis and the survey were further limited to employees with full-time employment contracts, intentionally excluding non-standard forms of employment in order to ensure consistency and comparability between job content and survey responses. This decision omits a growing segment of OSH professionals employed under alternative arrangements. Future research should therefore adopt a broader, cross-country comparative perspective, draw on multiple data sources and employment forms, and complement quantitative approaches with qualitative methods, such as expert interviews with OHS professionals, to better capture competence needs and barriers to integrating OSH and environmental roles. Finally, our research focused on a single country (Poland). We included a brief cross-national comparison (Czech Republic, Germany, Netherlands, Spain, Italy) to examine our findings, but future work should include cross-country samples.

Discussion

The article is the result of original empirical research, the aim of which was to identify and analyze the requirements of the changing labor market for the OHS service positions in Poland. Based on the literature, the impact of green economy activities and technologies has been demonstrated, resulting in a significant change in work and employee requirements in existing professions. OHS service employees have universal knowledge and skills in OHS management that can be considered appropriate for most sectors of the economy. It has a legal basis in the above-mentioned regulations. The background for the research was the issue of accident rates as a criterion for assessing the level of safety in workplaces, sustainable regulations and the new EU health and safety framework for 2021–2027. The analysis of EU guidelines and selected job offers dedicated to the OHS/environmental protection specialists leads to the conclusion that some employers representing selected sectors are looking for specialists with the full range of competences. The number of offers analyzed shows that this applies only to a few job offers on the Polish market. This is due to the fact that the OHS service position involves a wide range of duties and great responsibility (RQ1).

The analysis of the Polish job offers shows that combining OHS and environmental protection competencies is not present in the mining industry. This results from identification of hazards58, accident statistics and the workload on the OHS service. A similar phenomenon can be observed in other sectors (RQ2). The designed research tool (survey) aimed to learn the opinions of people employed in the field of OHS regarding environmental competences, as well as personal beliefs regarding these changes (RQ2). The obtained results answer the research questions and indicate that the OHS service employees occasionally perform tasks within the analyzed environmental protection competencies. In their opinion, the responsibility of the OHS service is too great to be combined with “environmental protection” issues (41.26%). Some respondents are aware that the scope of their duties goes beyond those specified in30, but they decide to carry out the assigned tasks due to, for example, the situation on the labor market. Full-time employees, wanting to cope with their duties, often treat them as a priority, which may translate into the quality of work performed, or they work overtime.

An interesting conclusion is that employees are more willing to combine OHS issues with environmental protection in enterprises employing > 100 employees (30%) with little work experience (22% of respondents with less than a year of experience), this tendency is decreasing with increasing work experience. In the case of the mining industry, over 93% of respondents indicated that health and safety issues are the basic duties of their job, while less than 27% indicated “environmental protection” issues as an additional element. These answers were confirmed by the statement in this group that the OHS position is too responsible to be combined with “environmental protection” issues (73%) and too responsible to be combined with fire protection issues (67%) (RQ3).

Summary and conclusions

The EU strategic framework for occupational health and safety for 2021–2027, Safety and health protection in the changing world of work, as well as Agenda 2030, European Green Deal indicate the directions of development in the area of OHS and environmental protection. It should be emphasized, however, that in accordance with applicable national legal regulations, it is difficult to combine aspects of the work environment with the tasks of the OHS service. Finding for balance and searching for solutions that will combine these aspects seems justified. However, it is currently difficult to judge which entities in workplaces should be responsible for finding these solutions.

The research results made it possible to formulate recommendations aimed at creating a more sustainable and effective approach to the possible integration of OHS and environmental protection, especially in mining and other industries:

drawing attention to the role of education in the era of changing trends and the climate situation, educating future staff dealing with environmental and sustainable development issues, through educational and promotional activities, constant striving in organizations (including raising awareness of all employees) to strongly integrate OHS with the environment and sustainable development as a safe, ecological, friendly working environment,training aimed at developing environmental competences for the OHS service employees, considering the specificity of the industry and current legal regulations, i.e. awareness of a sustainable approach, environmental regulations that may affect occupational safety,increasing the awareness of all employees regarding the issues of the natural environment and sustainable development as topics affecting OHS in organizations,introduction of clear legal provisions determining the possibility of combining professional duties in the areas of OHS, environment and fire protection,clear definition of the scope of professional responsibilities of employees in the areas of OHS, environment and others, considering current trends in the integration of OHS with the environment and sustainable development.

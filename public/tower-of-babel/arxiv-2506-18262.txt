Simple smooth modules over the Lie algebras of polynomial vector fields


Abstract
. Let 𝔤 := Der ⁡ ( ℂ ⁡ [ t 1 , t 2 , ⋯ , t n ] ) \mathfrak{g}:={\rm Der}(\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}]) and ℒ := Der ⁡ ( ℂ ⁡ [ [ t 1 , t 2 , ⋯ , t n ] ] ) \mathcal{L}:={\rm Der}(\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}]]) be the Witt Lie algebras. Clearly, 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a proper subalegbra of ℒ \mathcal{L} . Surprisingly, we prove that simple smooth modules over 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} are exactly the simple modules over ℒ \mathcal{L} studied by Rodakov (no need to take completion). Then we find an easy and elementary way to classify all simple smooth modules over 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . When the height ℓ V ≥ 2 \ell_{V}\geq 2 or n = 1 n=1 , any nontrivial simple smooth 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -module V V is isomorphic to an induced module from a simple smooth 𝔤 ≥ 0 \mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0} -module V ( ℓ V ) V^{(\ell_{V})} . When ℓ V = 1 \ell_{V}=1 and n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , any such module V V is the unique simple quotient of the tensor module F ⁡ ( P 0 , M ) F(P_{0},M) for some simple 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} -module M M , where P 0 P_{0} is a particular simple module over the Weyl algebra 𝒦 n + \mathcal{K}^{+}_{n} . We further show that a simple 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -module V V is a smooth module if and only if the action of each of n n particular vectors in 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is locally finite on V V .



Zhiqiang Li, Cunguang Cheng, Shiyuan Liu,

Rencai Lü, Kaiming Zhao, Yueqiang Zhao

Keyword: the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields, Witt algebra, smooth module, simple module, height

1.
Introduction

In 1909, E. Cartan introduced the four classes of Cartan type Lie algebras, including Witt algebras. In 1964, I.M. Singer and S. Sternberg interpreted the work of M. S. Lie and E. Cartan
[
SS
]
in modern language. Let
n
be a positive integer,
A_{n}^{+}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial algebra. The derivation Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}=\text{Der}(A_{n}^{+})
is the Witt Lie algebra or the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields.

Simple weight modules with finite-dimensional weight spaces over
\mathcal{W}_{1}^{+}
were classified by O. Mathieu
[
M
]
. The classification of such modules over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
(
n\geq 2
) had been a long-standing open problem. In 1999, a complete description of the supports of all simple weight modules of
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
was given by I. Penkov and V. Serganova
[
PS
]
.
Very recently, based on the classification of simple uniformly bounded
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
-modules
[
XL
]
and the description of the weight set of simple weight
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
modules
[
PS
]
, D. Grantcharov and V. Serganova
[
GS
]
completed the classification of simple Harish-Chandra
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
-modules. However, non-weight modules and weight modules that are not Harish-Chandra modules over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
are not well developed.

Recently, some non-weight modules over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
have been widely studied. For example, H. Tan and K. Zhao
[
TZ
]
classified the
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules of rank 1 over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
. Y. Zhao and G. Liu
[
ZL
]
also classify all simple non-singular Whittaker
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
-modules with finite dimensional Whittaker vector spaces using
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules.

The concept of smooth modules was first introduced by D. Kazhdan and G. Lusztig for affine Kac-Moody algebras
[
KL1
,
KL2
]
in 1993.
To some extent, smooth modules are generalizations of both highest weight modules and Whittaker modules.
For the last decade, the study on simple smooth module over various Lie algebra is very active. Complete classifications on simple smooth module were achieved over the following Lie algebras: the Virasoro algebra
[
MZ
]
, the Virasoro superalgebras
[
LPX
,
CSYZ
]
. Partial results were obtained for the following Lie algebras, including
the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
LPXZ
]
, the Fermion-Virasoro algebra
[
XZ
]
, the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
TYZ
]
, the planer Conformal Galeilo algebra
[
GG
]
, super
\mathscr{W}
-algebra
\mathscr{SW}(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2})
of Neveu-Schwarz type
[
LS
]
, and some other Lie superalgebras related to the Virasoro algebra.

In 1974, A.N. Rudakov
[
R
]
began the study of topological simple representations of Cartan type Lie algebras
\mathcal{L}
over the formal power series
\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]]
(not over polynomials). These modules were called continuous modules by Rudakov which are not smooth modules over
\mathcal{L}
since it does not have any good
\mathbb{Z}
-gradations. His main result, roughly speaking, is that all irreducible representations which satisfy a natural continuity condition can be described explicitly as quotients of induced modules. Surprisingly, we prove that these modules over
\mathcal{L}
are exactly simple smooth modules over the derivation Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
of the ring of polynomials in
n
variables
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
. But Rudakov’s complicated method involving simplicial systems is hard to understand.
We will establish
new, easy and fairly elementary methods to determine all
simple smooth modules over the derivation Lie algebra
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
in this paper. Thus, our methods can easily simplify Rudakov’s proof. For the readers’ convenience, we will try to use as much Rodukov’s notations as possible
to compare the two methods. Unlike some Lie algebras, the Witt algebra does not contain the Virasoro algebra as a subalgebra. Therefore, it is not possible to construct the structure of a vertex algebra on the smooth modules of the Witt algebra
[
L
]
.

The paper is arranged as follows. In Section 2, we prove that simple continuous modules over
\mathcal{L}
are exactly simple smooth modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.
In Section 3, we collect some basic notations
and establish some elementary results for later use. In Section 4, we introduce an easy and elementary method to show that all simple smooth
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}

modules
V
with height
\ell_{V}\geq 2
or
n=1
are some induced modules from simple modules over some finite-dimensional Lie algebras (which is not solvable or reductive); see Theorem
4.8
and Lemma
4.9
. In Section 5, we list all simple smooth
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}

modules
V
with
\ell_{V}=1
and
n\geq 2
in terms of simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules; see Theorem
5.5
. Combining Proposition
3.4
, Theorem
4.8
, Lemma
4.9
and Theorem
5.5
we obtain all simple smooth
modules over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
, see Theorem
5.6
.
In Section 6, we prove that a simple module
V
over
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
is a smooth module if and only if there are positive integers
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n}
such that each of

t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\dots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}

acts locally finitely on
V
.
In Section 7, we construct examples of simple smooth
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
modules
V
with height
\ell_{V}=1
and
2
respectively.

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{\times}

denote the sets of integers, non-negative integers, positive integers,
complex numbers and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. All the vector spaces are over
\mathbb{C}
. For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
,
we denote the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{t}
by
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{t})
.
For any
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
,
we use the notation
V_{\geq k}:=\bigoplus_{n\geq k}V_{n}
and
V_{\leq k}:=\bigoplus_{n\leq k}V_{n}
.

2.
Simple modules over the two classes of Witt algebras

In this section, we shall prove that simple smooth modules over
\mathfrak{g}
are exactly the simple modules over
\mathcal{L}
studied by Rodakov.

Let
A_{n}^{+}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial algebra in variables
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
be the derivation Lie algebra of
A_{n}^{+}
.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, we denote by
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial t_{i}
,
the partial derivation operator with respect to
t_{i}
.
For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, set

t^{\alpha}=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\ \text{and}\ \partial^{\alpha}=\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

We have

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ i=1,2,\dots,n\}

and the Lie brackets in
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
are given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle[t^{\alpha}\partial_{i},t^{\beta}\partial_{j}]=t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}(t^{\beta})\partial_{j}-t^{\beta}\partial_{j}(t^{\alpha})\partial_{i}.

In the rest of this paper, we write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}_{n}^{+}
for convenience.

Denote by
\epsilon_{i}
the
n
-th standard vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, whose
i
-th entry is
1
and
0
elsewhere for
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
, set

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n},

called the
size
of
\alpha
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{k}=0
for
k<-1
and

\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|=k+1,\ i=1,2,\dots,n\}

for
k\geq-1
. This gives a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation on
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{k}.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module (or
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module)
V
is called a
smooth
module
if for any
v\in V
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{i}.v=0
for all
i\geq n
.

The concept was first introduced by D. Kazhdan and G. Lusztig for affine Kac-Moody algebras
[
KL1
,
KL2
]
in 1993.

Let
Q_{n}^{+}=\mathbb{C}[[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]]
be the ring of formal power series in
n
variables
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{L}
be the derivation Lie algebra of
Q_{n}^{+}
. Any
D\in\mathcal{L}
has the form

D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}},\quad f_{i}\in Q_{n}^{+}.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{g}
is a proper subalegbra of
\mathcal{L}
. Actually,
\mathcal{L}
is the completion of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The algebra
\mathcal{L}
does not have any good
\mathbb{Z}
-gradation. But it has the filtration

\mathcal{L}_{j}=\{D\mid\text{deg}f_{i}\geq j+1\}

(\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{-1}\supset\mathcal{L}_{0}\supset\cdots)
,
where deg is defined as the power of the monomial with the lowest degree in the power series.

Definition 2.2
.

[
R
, Lemma 2.1]

A
\mathcal{L}
-module
V
is called a
continuous module
provided that for every
x\in V
there exists a positive integer
k
such that

\mathcal{L}_{k}\cdot x=0.

Theorem 2.3
.

(a) If
V
is a simple continuous
\mathcal{L}
-module, then
V
is naturally a simple smooth module over
\mathfrak{g}
.

(b) If
V
is a simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module, then
V
is naturally a simple continuous module over
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

(a) Since
\mathfrak{g}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
,
V
is naturally a module over
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq k}\subset\mathcal{L}_{k}
for any positive integer
k
,

V
is a smooth module over
\mathfrak{g}
.

For any
D\in\mathcal{L}
, we write

D=\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}x_{i},\text{ where }x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}.

For any
v\in V
, there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{k}\cdot x=0.

Then we can define that
Dv=\sum\limits_{i=-1}^{k}x_{i}v=yv,
where
y=\sum\limits_{i=-1}^{k}x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
. We see that the action of any
D\in\mathcal{L}
on a vector
v\in V
is the same as an element
y\in\mathfrak{g}
acts on
v
. So
V
is also a simple module over
\mathfrak{g}
.

(b) We know that any
D\in\mathcal{L}
can be written as

D=\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}x_{i},\ \text{where}\ x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}.

For any
v\in V
, since
V
is a smooth module over
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq N}v=0
.
Then we can naturally define the action of
\mathcal{L}
on
V
as follows

(2.2)

Dv:=\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}x_{i}v=\sum_{i=-1}^{N}x_{i}v\in V.

Let
x=\sum\limits_{i=-1}^{\infty}x_{i},y=\sum\limits_{j=-1}^{\infty}y_{j}\in\mathcal{L}
where
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},y_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, and
v\in V
. Assume that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq N}v=0
for some positive integer
N
. One can choose
N^{\prime}>N
such that

\mathfrak{g}_{\geq N^{\prime}}x_{k}v=\mathfrak{g}_{\geq N^{\prime}}y_{k}v=0,~\text{for}\ -1\leq k\leq N.

Then we have

\displaystyle[x,y]v

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-1}^{N}\left(\sum_{i+j=k}[x_{i},y_{j}]\right)v=\sum_{k=-1}^{2N^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{-1\leq i,j\leq N^{\prime}\atop i+j=k}[x_{i},y_{j}]\right)v=\sum_{i=-1}^{N^{\prime}}\sum_{j=-1}^{N^{\prime}}[x_{i},y_{j}]v

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}\left(x_{i}y_{j}-y_{j}x_{i}\right)v=\sum_{i=-1}^{N^{\prime}}x_{i}\left(\sum_{j=-1}^{N^{\prime}}y_{j}v\right)-\sum_{j=-1}^{N^{\prime}}y_{j}\left(\sum_{i=-1}^{N^{\prime}}x_{i}v\right)

\displaystyle=x(yv)-y(xv).

As a result,
V
becomes an
\mathcal{L}
-module under the action (
2.2
).
So
V
is also a simple continuous module over
\mathcal{L}
.
∎

3.
Preliminary results

In this section, we shall introduce and establish some notations and preliminary results for Witt algebras and their smooth modules.

Definition 3.1
.

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called a
weight module
provided that the action of
\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}t_{i}\partial_{i}

on
V
is diagonalizable.

Definition 3.2
.

A weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called a
highest weight module
if there exists a nonzero vector
u\in V
such that

(1).
(t_{i}\partial_{i}).u=\lambda_{i}u
for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
,

(2).
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}.u=0,

(3).
V=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})u.

The vector
u
is called a highest weight vector.

Let
V
be a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module (or
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module). For
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, set

(3.1)

\displaystyle V^{(r)}=\{v\in V\mid\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}.v=0\}.

Then
V^{(r)}
is a subspace of
V
and

V^{(0)}\subseteq V^{(1)}\subseteq V^{(2)}\subseteq\cdots.

Define
\ell_{V}=\mathrm{min}\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mid V^{(r)}\neq 0\}
which is called the
height
of
V
.
We say a smooth
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module
V
to be
homogenous
if
V=V^{(\ell_{V})}
.
These two concepts here were defined in
[
R
]
for his algebras.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module (or
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module)
V
, the subspace
V^{(r)}
is
a
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

We only check the case that
V
is a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Assume that
v\in V^{(r)}
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
. We will show that
x.v\in V^{(r)}
.

For any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}
, we get
[y,x]\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq r}
. Then

y.x.v=[y,x].v+x.y.v=0.

This gives that
x.v\in V^{(r)}
, as desired.
∎

Now we give a property for smooth modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
V
be a simple nontrivial smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Then
\ell_{V}\geq 1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\ell_{V}=0
. Choose any
0\neq u\in V^{(0)}
. By Poincar-Birkhoff-Witt theorem,
we have
V=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{-1})u
. Notice that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}.u=0
. One can form the induced
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}u
. Since
W
has the unique maximal submodule

J=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{-1})u
, it is clear that
V\cong W/J
.
This implies that
V
is
1
-dimensional. Note that
\mathfrak{g}
is simple. Then
V
is a trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
which contradicts the assumption that
V
is a nontrivial
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Thus
\ell_{V}\geq 1
.
∎

4.
The case that
\ell_{V}\geq 2

In this section, we study nontrivial simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with height
\geq 2
.
Due to Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
we will start from induced
modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

4.1.
Induced representations

In this subsection, let
E
be a homogenous
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module with
\ell_{E}\geq 1
.
Actually
E
is a nontrivial module. We form the induced

\mathfrak{g}
-module
\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}E
and denote it by
F
, i.e.,

(4.1)

\displaystyle F=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}E.

We remark that Rudakov defined
F
for his algebras in
[
R
]
.
In this subsection we will first show that any nonzero submodule of
F
has a nonzero intersection with
E
provided
\ell_{E}\geq 2
.
For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we set

(4.2)

\displaystyle F_{m}=\underset{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\atop|\alpha|=m}{\oplus}\partial^{\alpha}\otimes E.

Note that
F_{0}=E
. For convenience, let
F_{m}=0
for
m<0
.
Now the vector space
F
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded with

F=\underset{m\in\mathbb{Z}}{\oplus}F_{m}.

For any
w\in F
, we can uniquely write

w=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\partial^{\alpha}w_{\alpha},

where all
w_{\alpha}\in E
and only finitely many of the
w_{\alpha}
’s are nonzero. Furthermore, if
w\neq 0
, define

\begin{split}\mathrm{Supp}(w)=\Big\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}}\,\big|\,w_{\alpha}\neq 0\Big\},\quad\mathrm{ht}(w)=\max\Big\{|\alpha|\,\big|\,w_{\alpha}\neq 0\Big\},\\
\mathrm{top}(w)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in{{\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}}}\\
|\alpha|=m\end{subarray}}\partial^{\alpha}w_{\alpha},\quad\mathrm{lth}(w)=\Big|\big\{\alpha\,\big|\,w_{\alpha}\neq 0,\,|\alpha|=m\big\}\Big|,\end{split}

where
m={\rm ht}(w)
.
Then we have
w=\mathrm{top}(w)+F_{\leq m-1}
, that is,
w=\mathrm{top}(w)+w^{\prime}
for some
w^{\prime}\in F_{\leq m-1}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
k\geq\ell_{E}-1
, one has

\mathfrak{g}_{\geq k}.F_{\leq r}\subseteq F_{\leq r-k+\ell_{E}-1},\quad r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Proof.

The proof is by double induction, first on
r
, for fixed
r
, then by induction on
k
.

For
r=0
, this is clear since
E
is a homogenous
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module with height
\ell_{E}\geq 1
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We assume that the conclusion holds for all
0\leq r\leq m-1
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{g}_{\geq k}.F_{\leq r}\subseteq F_{\leq r-k+\ell_{E}-1},\quad 0\leq r\leq m-1.

For the case that
r=m
and
k=\ell_{E}-1
, the assertion follows from the fact that
F_{\leq r}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Now let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq k}
and let
k>\ell_{E}-1
. It follows from (
4.2
) that

F_{\leq m}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}.F_{\leq m-1}\quad\text{and}\quad x.F_{\leq m}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x.\partial_{i}.F_{\leq m-1}.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, by the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle x.\partial_{i}.F_{\leq m-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{i}.x.F_{\leq m-1}+[x,\partial_{i}].F_{\leq m-1}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\partial_{i}.F_{\leq m-1-k+\ell_{E}-1}+F_{\leq m-1-(k-1)+\ell_{E}-1}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle F_{\leq m-k+\ell_{E}-1}.

That is
x.F_{\leq m}\subseteq F_{\leq m-k+\ell_{E}-1}
, as desired.
∎

We remark that Rudakov proved a similar result as in the above lemma for his algebras in
[
R
]
.

Now we suppose that
W
is a nonzero submodule of
F
. Take a nonzero vector

(4.3)

\omega=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\partial^{\alpha}v_{\alpha}\in W\ \text{with minimal}\ m:=\mathrm{ht}(\omega),

where all
v_{\alpha}\in E
and only finitely many of the
v_{\alpha}
’s are nonzero.
We may further assume that lth
(\omega)
is minimal. We aim to prove that
\omega\in{E}
, i.e.,
m=0
.
This gives that
W\cap E\neq\{0\}
. To the contrary, we assume that
m\geq 1
.
Next we want to deduce some contradictions. We need several auxiliary lemmas.

Denote by
\epsilon_{i}
the
n
-th standard vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, whose
i
-th entry is
1
and
0
elsewhere for
i=1,2,\dots,n
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\ell_{E}}
, we have

x.\omega=0.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
, it is straightforward to check that

x.\omega=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\\
|\alpha|=m\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\partial^{\alpha-\epsilon_{i}}[x,\partial_{i}]v_{\alpha}+\omega^{\prime}\in W

for some
\omega^{\prime}\in F_{\leq m-2}
. We see that
\text{ht}(x\omega)=\text{ht}(\omega)-1
if
x\omega\neq 0
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any

Rewrite the above equation we see that

The formula in the lemma follows.
∎

In the remaining of this subsection, we assume that

Lemma 4.4

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

,

be a nontrivial simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module with

.
Then the

-module

-dimensional and

-modules.

Proof.

Note that

is a
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module with
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}.V^{(1)}=0
. This implies that

is a module over the one dimensional Lie algebra
\mathbb{C}t_{1}\partial_{1}

We know that there exists a

5.
The case that
\ell_{V}=1

In this section, we study simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
V
with
\ell_{V}=1
. First, we give some notations and recall some known
results from the reference
[
LLZ
]
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and let
E_{ij}
be the
n\times n
square matrix with
1

as its
(i,j)
-entry and
0
as other entries. We have the general linear Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\mathbb{C}E_{ij}

and the special linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
It is well known that there exists a Lie algebra isomorphism
\psi:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
given by

\psi(t_{i}\partial_{j})=E_{ij},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

The Weyl algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
is the simple associative algebra

\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\cdots,t_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]
.
Let
P
be a module over the associative algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}

and
M
be a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module. Then the tensor product

F(P,M)=P\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}M

becomes a
\mathfrak{g}
-module (see
[
LLZ
]
for details) with the action

(5.1)

(t^{\alpha}\partial_{j})\circ(g\otimes v)=((t^{\alpha}\partial_{j})g)\otimes v+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}(t^{\alpha})g)\otimes E_{ij}(v)

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,
1\leq j\leq n
,
g\in P
, and
v\in M
.

Let
\succ
be the lexicographical total order on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. Namely,
for

\bm{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}),\ \bm{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},

\bm{a}\succ\bm{b}
if and only if there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle a_{i}=b_{i},\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\ \text{and}\ a_{j}>b_{j}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
-module

P_{0}=\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}/(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}t_{i})
is simple, and

P_{0}
can be naturally identified with
\mathbb{C}[\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]
.

Proof.

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, one has

t_{i}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}t_{i}-\delta_{ij}1,~t_{i}t_{j}=t_{j}t_{i},~\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\partial_{i}.

It is well-known that

t_{i}\partial_{j}^{m}=\partial_{j}^{m}t_{i}-m\delta_{ij}\partial_{j}^{m-1},~m\geq 0.

For
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
, we denote by
\overline{x}
its corresponding image under the canonical
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
-module homomorphism

\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}\rightarrow P_{0}
. Then for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle t_{i}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\overline{t_{i}\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i-1}^{\alpha_{i-1}}(t_{i}\partial_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})\partial_{i+1}^{\alpha_{i+1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i-1}^{\alpha_{i-1}}(\partial_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}t_{i}-\alpha_{i}\partial_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-1})\partial_{i+1}^{\alpha_{i+1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{i}\overline{\partial^{\alpha-\epsilon_{i}}}.

It follows that

t^{\beta}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}}=(-1)^{|\beta|}\beta!\left(\alpha\atop\beta\right)\overline{\partial^{\alpha-\beta}},~\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}.

In particular,

t^{\beta}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}}=0
if
\beta\succ\alpha
.
Here, we have used the notations

\alpha!=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}!,~\left(\alpha\atop\beta\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\alpha_{i}\atop\beta_{i}\right).

Now we can show that
P_{0}
is a simple
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
-module. We know that any element in
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
is a linear combination of elements of the form
\partial^{\alpha}t^{\beta}
for some
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
.
Let
V
be a nonzero submodule of
P_{0}
and
0\neq v\in V
. Then
v
can be written as

v=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\alpha}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}}

with finitely many nonzero
a_{\alpha}
’s. Let
\beta
be largest
with respect to the lexicographical order
such that
a_{\beta}\neq 0
. Then

t^{\beta}v=(-1)^{|\beta|}\beta!a_{\beta}\overline{1}\in V.

It follows that
\overline{1}\in V
. We can conclude that
V=P_{0}
.
Moreover, there exists a naturally surjective linear map

\phi:\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\ldots,\partial_{n}],\quad f\partial^{\alpha}\mapsto f(0)\partial^{\alpha}.

It is easy to see that the kernel of
\phi
is
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}t_{i}
.
Then
P_{0}
can be naturally considered as
\mathbb{C}[\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]
.
∎

In what follows, we give some more symbols.
Let
\mathfrak{h}=\text{span}\{h_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, where
h_{i}=E_{ii}-E_{i+1,i+1}
.
For any
\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, let
V(\varphi)
be the
simple highest weight
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-module with highest weight
\varphi
.
We make
V(\varphi)
into a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module by defining the action of
the identity matrix
I
as some scalar
b\in\mathbb{C}
.
Denote by
V(\varphi,b)
the resulting
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
Define the fundamental weights
\delta_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by
\delta_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{i,j}

for all
i,j=1,2,\dots,n-1
. For convenience, we define
\delta_{0}=\delta_{n}=0\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

The exterior product
\bigwedge^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\mathbb{C}^{n}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{C}^{n}
(
k
times)
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module with the action given by

X(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i-1}\wedge Xv_{i}\wedge v_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k},\quad X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n},

and the following
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module isomorphism is well known

(5.3)

\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong V(\delta_{k},k),\quad 0\leq k\leq n.

Set
L_{n}(P_{0},0)=0
. For
r=1,2,\dots,n
, let

L_{n}(P_{0},r)=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\partial_{k}.p)\otimes\big(\epsilon_{k}\wedge\epsilon_{i_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\epsilon_{i_{r}}\big)\mid p\in P_{0},\ 1\leq i_{2},i_{3},\dots,i_{r}\leq n\Big\}.

And for
r=0,1,\dots,n
, let

\tilde{L}_{n}(P_{0},r)=\{v\in F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))\mid{\mathcal{W}}_{n}^{+}v\subseteq L_{n}(P_{0},r)\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The list of all simple quotients of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
F(P_{0},M)
for any simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
M
is given as follows:

(1)
F(P_{0},M)
,
if
M\ncong V(\delta_{r},r)
for any
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
;

(2)
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{0},0))=P_{0}
;

(3)
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))\big/\tilde{L}_{n}(P_{0},r)
for all
r=1,\ldots,n-1.

(4)
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{n},n))/L_{n}(P_{0},n)
, which is a
1
-dimensional module.

Proof.

Part
(1)
follows from
[
LLZ
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Let us first show that
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))

has a unique simple quotient. For
r=0,1,\dots,n
, choose a highest weight vector
v_{r}
in
V(\delta_{r},r)
. We compute that

t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}\circ(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r})=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|\geq 2;

t_{i}\partial_{j}\circ(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r})=\bar{1}\otimes(E_{ij}.v_{r}),\quad 1\leq i\neq j\leq n;

t_{i}\partial_{i}\circ(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r})=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq r;

t_{i}\partial_{i}\circ(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r})=-\bar{1}\otimes v_{r},\quad r+1\leq i\leq n;

and

\partial_{i}\circ(\bar{1}\otimes v)=\overline{\partial_{i}}\otimes v,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\ v\in V(\delta_{r},r).

So,
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))
is a highest weight
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
\bar{1}\otimes v_{r}
is a
highest weight vector with
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r})
. It is easy to check that

\{w\in F(P_{0},M)\mid t_{i}\partial_{i}\circ w=0,\ t_{j}\partial_{j}\circ w=-w,\ 1\leq i\leq r,\ r+1\leq j\leq n\}=\mathbb{C}(\bar{1}\otimes v_{r}).

We see that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))
has a unique maximal submodule. Hence,
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{r},r))

has a unique simple quotient for all
r=0,1,\dots,n
.

The statements in part
(2)
and
(3)
follow from
[
LLZ
, Theorem 3.5 (2)]
.

For part
(4)
, we see that
\sum^{n}_{k=1}\partial_{k}P_{0}\neq P_{0}.
From
[
LLZ
, Theorem 3.5 (5)]
we know that
F(P_{0},V(\delta_{n},n))/L_{n}(P_{0},n)
is a trivial
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Clearly,

\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial_{k}.p\mid k=1,2,\dots,n,\ p\in P_{0}\}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\overline{\partial^{\alpha}}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|\geq 1\}.

This means that

\dim F(P_{0},V(\delta_{n},n))/L_{n}(P_{0},n)=1.

In fact for part
(4)
, one can also see Proposition
7.1
for more details.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P
be a simple
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
-module and
M
be a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
Suppose that
M
has no finite dimensional nonzero
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-submodule. Then for any
0\neq p_{0}\in P

and any nontrivial submodule
V
of
F(P,M)
, we have
V\cap(p_{0}\otimes M)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
V
is a nontrivial submodule of
F(P,M)
with

(5.4)

V\cap(p_{0}\otimes M)=0

for some
0\neq p_{0}\in P
.

Let
w=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{q}p_{k}\otimes w_{k}
be a nonzero element in
V
where
p_{k}\in P,w_{k}\in M
.
(Later we will assume that
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q}
are linear independent.)

Claim 1
. For any
u\in\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
,
1\leq i,j,l\leq n
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{q}(up_{k})\otimes(\delta_{li}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{k}\in V.

The proof is exactly the same with that of Claim 1 in
[
LLZ
, Theorem 3.1]
. We omit it.

Claim 2
. If
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q}
are linear independent,
then for any
1\leq i,j,l\leq n
and any

, we have

(\delta_{li}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{k}=0.

Since
P
is a simple
\mathcal{K}_{n}^{+}
-module, by the density theorem in ring theory, for any

p\in P
, there is a

such that

u_{k}p_{i}=\delta_{ik}p,\ i=1,2,\dots,q.

From
Claim 1
we see that
P\otimes(\delta_{li}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{k}\subseteq V.

Then from (
5.4
) we have
(\delta_{li}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})w_{k}=0
as desired.

From now on we assume that
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q}
are linear independent.

Claim 3
. For any
1\leq i,j,l\leq n
, we have
(\delta_{li}E_{lj}-E_{li}E_{lj})\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})w_{k}=0
.

From
t_{s}\partial_{m}\hskip-3.0pt\circ\hskip-3.0pt(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{q}p_{k}\otimes w_{k})\in V
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{q}(t_{s}\partial_{m}p_{k}\otimes w_{k}+p_{k}\otimes E_{sm}w_{k})\in V.

By
Claim 1

6.

\mathfrak{g}
-modules

In this section we will characterize simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Assume that there are some
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,

k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that each of
t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\cdots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}

acts locally finitely on
V
. Then there exists nonzero
v_{+}\in V
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle t_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\partial_{i}v_{+}=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}v_{+},

\displaystyle t_{i}^{r+1}\partial_{i}v_{+}=

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{for all}\quad r\geq N,\ i=1,2,\dots,n.

Proof.

Since Span
\{t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\cdots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}\}
is a commutative Lie algebra and

W=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\dots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}]u

is finite dimensional for a nonzero
u\in V
, we can have a common eigenvector
v_{+}\in W
for all
t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\cdots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}
, i.e.,

t_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\partial_{i}v_{+}=\lambda_{i}v_{+},

for some
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

After carefully checking the proof of Lemma 3.10 in
[
MNTZ
]
, we know that this vector
v_{+}
satisfies the requirements in the lemma, i.e., there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

t_{i}^{r+1}\partial_{i}v_{+}=0\quad\text{for all}\quad r\geq N,\ i=1,2,\dots,n.

This completes the proof.
∎

Furthermore, we can prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
V
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-module, and let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. Assume that there are some
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,

k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that each of
t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\dots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}

acts locally finitely on
V
. Let
v_{+}
be the same vector as in Lemma
6.1
. Then for each pair
1\leq p,q\leq n
, there exists some
M=M(p,q,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

t_{p}^{k+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{q}v_{+}=0,~\forall k>M.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
q=1
and
\alpha_{p}=0
.
According to Lemma
6.1
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}v_{+}=\lambda_{p}v_{+},\quad\text{and}\quad t_{p}^{r+1}\partial_{p}v_{+}=0\quad\text{for}\ r\geq N.

For any fix
j\in\{k_{p}+1,k_{p}+2,\dots,2k_{p}\}
. Since
\dim\big(\mathbb{C}[t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}](t_{p}^{j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1})v_{+}\big)
is finite and from the relation

(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}-\lambda_{p})t_{p}^{j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+}=\big(j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big)t_{p}^{k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+},

there is a smallest
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

t_{p}^{s_{j}k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+},\ t_{p}^{(s_{j}+1)k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+},\ \dots,\ t_{p}^{(s_{j}+n_{j})k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+}

are linearly dependent for some
s_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Henceforth, there exists a polynomial

g_{j}(y)=a_{j,0}+a_{j,1}(y-\lambda_{i})+\cdots+a_{j,n_{j}}(y-\lambda_{i})^{n_{j}},\quad a_{j,0}a_{j,n_{j}}\neq 0,

such that

g_{j}(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p})(t_{p}^{s_{j}k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+})=0.

Applying
t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}-\lambda_{p}
to the equation above repeatedly, we deduce that

(6.1)

\displaystyle g_{j}(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p})(t_{p}^{sk_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+})=0.

for all
s\geq s_{j}
. That is, there exists a nonzero polynomial of
t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}
with the smallest degree
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

which annihilates
t_{p}^{sk_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{p}v_{+}
for sufficiently large
s
.

Suppose there exists
j\in\{k_{p}+1,k_{p}+2,\dots,2k_{p}\}
such that
n_{j}\geq 1
. Consider (
6.1
)
for
2s
, where
sk_{p}>\text{max}\{s_{j}k_{p},N\}
, which leads to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=g_{j}(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p})(t_{p}^{2s{k_{p}}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+})

\displaystyle=\big(a_{j,0}+a_{j,1}\left(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}-\lambda_{p}\right)+\cdots+a_{j,n_{j}}\left(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}-\lambda_{p}\right)^{n_{j}}\big)(t_{p}^{2sk_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n_{j}}b_{s,l}t_{p}^{(2s+l)k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+},

where

b_{s,l}=a_{j,l}\prod_{i=0}^{l-1}\big((2s+i)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big).

Since
sk_{p}>\text{max}\{s_{j}k_{p},N\}
, we have the following bracket relation

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\big[t_{p}^{sk_{p}+1}\partial_{p},g_{j}(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p})(t_{p}^{sk_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1})\big]v_{+}

\displaystyle=\left[t_{p}^{sk_{p}+1}\partial_{p},\sum_{l=0}^{n_{j}}a_{j,l}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{l-1}\big((s+i)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big)\right)t_{p}^{(s+l)k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}\right]v_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n_{j}}c_{s,l}t_{p}^{(2s+l)k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+},

where

c_{s,l}=a_{j,l}\big((s+l)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(sk_{p}+1)\big)\prod_{i=0}^{l-1}\big((s+i)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big).

Denote by

\displaystyle f_{j,1}(y)=a_{j,n_{j}}\big(yk_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(yk_{p}+1)\big)\prod_{i=0}^{n_{j}-1}\big((2y+i)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big),

\displaystyle f_{j,2}(y)=a_{j,n_{j}}\big((y+n_{j})k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(yk_{p}+1)\big)\prod_{i=0}^{n_{j}-1}\big((y+i)k_{p}+j+1-\delta_{p,1}(k_{p}+1)\big).

We claim that
f_{j,1}
and
f_{j,2}
are linearly independent since

f_{j,1}\left(-\frac{j+1}{2k_{p}}+\delta_{p,1}\frac{k_{p}+1}{2k_{p}}\right)=0,~f_{j,2}\left(-\frac{j+1}{2k_{p}}+\delta_{p,1}\frac{k_{p}+1}{2k_{p}}\right)\neq 0.

Thus, we can find a nonzero polynomial
h_{j}(y)
of degree less than
n_{j}
such that

h_{j}(t_{p}^{k_{p}+1}\partial_{p}).(t_{p}^{s^{\prime}k_{p}+j+1}t^{\alpha}\partial_{1}v_{+})=0

for sufficient large
s^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction to the minimality of
n_{j}
. Hence
n_{j}=0
for all

j\in\{k_{p}+1,k_{p}+2,\dots,2k_{p}\}
, and choose

M=\text{max}\{N+(s_{j}+3)k_{p}\ \mid\ 2k_{p}\geq j\geq k_{p}+1\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
V
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Assume that there are some
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,

k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that each of
t_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{k_{2}+1}\partial_{2},\dots,t_{n}^{k_{n}+1}\partial_{n}

acts locally finitely on
V
. Let
v_{+}
and
N
be the same vector and positive integer as in Lemma
6.1
, such that

t_{i}^{k+1}\partial_{i}v_{+}=0,~\forall 1\leq i\leq n,~k\geq N.

Then there exists some positive integer
M
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq M}v_{+}=0
.

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, it is trivial.

Let
n=2
. According to Lemma
6.2
, it is easy to see that there exists some positive integer
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\ (N_{1}<N_{2})
such that

t_{1}^{k}\partial_{1}v_{+}=t_{2}^{k}\partial_{2}v_{+}=t_{1}t_{2}^{k}\partial_{2}v_{+}=t_{1}^{k}t_{2}\partial_{1}v_{+}=0,~\forall k>N_{1},

t_{1}^{l}t_{2}^{r}\partial_{i}v_{+}=t_{1}^{r}t_{2}^{l}\partial_{i}v_{+}=0,\quad\forall i=1,2,~l>N_{2},\ \text{and}\ 1\leq r\leq N_{1}.

We
\mathbf{claim}
that

t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{1}v_{+}=t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{2}v_{+}=0

if
k_{1}+k_{2}>2N_{2}
. Note that we must have
k_{1}>N_{2}
or
k_{2}>N_{2}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
k_{1}>N_{2}
. If
k_{2}\leq N_{1}
, then it is clearly that
t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{1}v_{+}=t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{2}v_{+}=0
. If
k_{2}>N_{1}
, we still have

\displaystyle t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{1}v_{+}=[t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{2},t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}\partial_{1}]v_{+}=0,

\displaystyle t_{1}^{k_{1}}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{2}v_{+}=[t_{1}^{k_{1}}\partial_{1},t_{1}t_{2}^{k_{2}}\partial_{2}]v_{+}=0.

Therefore, we can choose
M=2N_{2}
. Then the
\mathbf{claim}
holds. This completes the case
n=2
.

Now we consider the case that
n>2
. Assume that the proposition holds for the case
n-1
. For fixed
1\leq i\leq n
, let

\mathfrak{g}(i)=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t^{\alpha}\partial_{j}\mid j\neq i~\text{and}~\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}~\text{with}~\alpha_{i}=0\}.

Then
\mathfrak{g}(i)
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathcal{W}_{n-1}^{+}
. We know that
V
can be viewed as a
\mathfrak{g}(i)
-module by the restriction of the action of
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}(i)
. For fixed
1\leq i\leq n
, it is from the induction hypothesis that there exists a positive integer
M_{i}
such that
t^{\alpha}\partial_{j}.v_{+}=0
for all
j\neq i
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|>M_{i}
,
\alpha_{i}=0
. Let
M^{\prime}=\max\{M_{i}\mid i=1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then we have

t^{\alpha}\partial_{i}v_{+}=0

for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|>M^{\prime}
,
\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{i-1}\alpha_{i+1}\cdots\alpha_{n}=0
.

Take
M=7M^{\prime}
. Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\gamma|>M
. We
\mathbf{claim}
that

t^{\gamma}\partial_{i}v_{+}=0,~\forall 1\leq i\leq n.

Without loss of generality, we only prove the case that
i=1
.
If
\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\leq 2M^{\prime}
, then

\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{4}+\cdots+\gamma_{n}>3M^{\prime}.

It is obvious that one can choose some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
such that

\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\beta_{2}=\beta_{3}=0,\quad\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}=\gamma_{1}+1,\quad\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}=\gamma_{j}\ \text{for}\ 4\leq j\leq n,

and

|\alpha|>M^{\prime},\quad|\beta|>M^{\prime},\quad\alpha_{1}\neq\beta_{1}.

Then

t^{\alpha}t_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{1}v=t^{\beta}t_{3}^{\gamma_{3}}\partial_{1}v_{+}=0.

It follows that

t^{\gamma}\partial_{1}v=\frac{1}{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\left[t^{\alpha}t_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{1},t^{\beta}t_{3}^{\gamma_{3}}\partial_{1}\right]v_{+}=0.

If
\gamma_{2}>2M^{\prime}
, then

t_{2}^{M^{\prime}+1}t_{3}^{\gamma_{3}}\cdots t_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}\partial_{2}v=t_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}t_{2}^{\gamma_{2}-M^{\prime}}\partial_{1}v_{+}=0,

since
\gamma_{2}-M^{\prime}>M^{\prime}
. It follows that

t^{\gamma}\partial_{1}v_{+}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}-M^{\prime}}\left[t_{2}^{M^{\prime}+1}t_{3}^{\gamma_{3}}\cdots t_{n}^{\gamma_{n}}\partial_{2},t_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}t_{2}^{\gamma_{2}-M^{\prime}}\partial_{1}\right]v_{+}=0.

Similarly, one can show that

if

. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.4

Let

be a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1) there are positive integers

acts locally finitely on

;

(2)

is a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module;

(3)

is either isomorphic to the induced module
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}E
for some simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module

, or isomorphic to the unique simple quotient of the tensor
\mathfrak{g}
-module

for some simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module

Proof.

We already know
(2)\Longleftrightarrow(3)
from Theorem

, Lemma

and Theorem

. It is enough to prove

and
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.
Let

be a simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module. It from Proposition

that there exists a nonzero vector

and

such that

for all

. By the PBW theorem and simplicity of

where

. For any

is a smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. Suppose that

is a simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Without loss of generality, we may assume

is not trivial. Then

. We know that there exists a surjective

-module homomorphism

. Choose

∎

Note that

, we have

\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}=[\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}]\oplus\mathbb{C}\omega_{n},

where
\omega_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\partial_{i}
.
This implies that

\varphi(x)=0\quad\text{for}\quad x\in[\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0},\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}].

Proposition 7.1
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module

W(\varphi)
is a simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module with height
\ell_{W(\varphi)}=1

if and only if
\varphi(\omega_{n})\neq 0
. Moreover, if
\varphi(\omega_{n})=0
,
then
\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial^{\alpha}v_{\varphi}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|\geq 1\}
is
the unique maximal submodule of
W(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(\omega_{n})=\lambda
.
Note that
\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module with
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}.v_{\varphi}=0
. This implies that
\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module by the Lie algebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}/\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
Then it follows from Lemma
5.4
that

W(\varphi)=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}\cong F(P_{0},(\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi})^{\tau}),

as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. It is easy to see that

(\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi})^{\tau}\cong V(0,n+\lambda),

as
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules.
Thus there exists a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism

\Phi:W(\varphi)\rightarrow F(P_{0},V(0,n+\lambda))

with
\Phi(v_{\varphi})=\bar{1}\otimes 1
. From Lemma
5.2
,
we see that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
W(\varphi)
is simple if and only if
\lambda\neq 0
.
When
\lambda=0
, we know that

\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial^{\alpha}v_{\varphi}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},\ |\alpha|\geq 1\}

is the unique maximal submodule of
W(\varphi)
.
∎

7.2.
Examples of simple smooth
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{+}
-modules

In this subsection, we focus on the case that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{W}_{2}^{+}
and
study a class of simple Whittaker modules over
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
.
Using those
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-modules,
by Theorem
4.7
we can construct a class of simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

Recall that

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{1}\partial_{1},t_{1}\partial_{2},t_{2}\partial_{1},t_{2}\partial_{2}\}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{2}

and

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1},t_{1}^{2}\partial_{2},t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{1},t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{2},t_{2}^{2}\partial_{1},t_{2}^{2}\partial_{2}\}.

It is easy to check that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module with the irreducible decomposition:

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=V(3)\oplus V(1),

where

V(3)=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{1}^{2}\partial_{2},\ 2t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{2}-t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1},\ 2t_{2}^{2}\partial_{2}-4t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{1},\ -6t_{2}^{2}\partial_{1}\}

and

V(1)=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1}+t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{2},\ t_{2}^{2}\partial_{2}+t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{1}\}.

For convenience, we write

\mathrm{e}=t_{1}\partial_{2},\quad\mathrm{I}=t_{1}\partial_{1}+t_{2}\partial_{2},\quad\mathrm{h}=t_{1}\partial_{1}-t_{2}\partial_{2},\quad\mathrm{f}=t_{2}\partial_{1},

\mathrm{p}_{0}=t_{1}^{2}\partial_{2},\quad\mathrm{p}_{1}=2t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{2}-t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1},\quad\mathrm{p}_{2}=2t_{2}^{2}\partial_{2}-4t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{1},\quad\mathrm{p}_{3}=-6t_{2}^{2}\partial_{1},

and

\mathrm{q}_{0}=t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1}+t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{2},\quad\mathrm{q}_{1}=t_{2}^{2}\partial_{2}+t_{1}t_{2}\partial_{1}.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism. Note that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1},\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}]=\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 2}
. We know that
\phi(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 2}
.
Define a one-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}
-module
\mathbb{C}_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}w_{\phi}
by

x.w_{\phi}=\phi(x)w_{\phi}\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}.

Then we have the induced module

M(\phi)=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}}^{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}\mathbb{C}w_{\phi},

which is called the universal quasi-Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module of type
\phi
.
Write

M(\phi)_{\phi}=\{v\in M(\phi)\mid x.v=\phi(x)v\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}\}.

The proof of the following result is exactly the same with that of
[
CC
, Lemma 3.1]
.
We omit it.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism.
If
\phi(\mathrm{q}_{0})=\phi(\mathrm{q}_{1})=0
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
M(\phi)
is reducible;

(2) there exists some nonzero
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
such that
(a_{1}\mathrm{e}+a_{2}\mathrm{I}+a_{3}\mathrm{h}+a_{4}\mathrm{f})w_{\phi}\in M(\phi)_{\phi}
;

(3)
\det A_{\phi}=0
, where

A_{\phi}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{0})&-3\phi(\mathrm{p}_{0})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{1})\\
-3\phi(\mathrm{p}_{0})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{1})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{1})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{2})\\
-4\phi(\mathrm{p}_{1})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{2})&\phi(\mathrm{p}_{2})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{3})\\
-3\phi(\mathrm{p}_{2})&-\phi(\mathrm{p}_{3})&3\phi(\mathrm{p}_{3})&0\end{array}\right)\in\text{M}_{4\times 4}(\mathbb{C}).

Remark 7.3
.

For example one can choose
\phi(\mathrm{p}_{i})=1
and
\phi(\mathrm{q}_{j})=0
for
i=0,1,2,3
and
j=0,1
,
which gives
\det A_{\phi}=-4
. Then
M(\phi)
is a simple quasi-Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module of type
\phi
.
Furthermore, we see that
M(\phi)
is a simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}
-module with height
2
.

It follows from Theorem
4.7
we have

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero Lie algebra homomorphism given in Lemma
7.2
with
\det A_{\phi}\neq 0
.
Then the induced
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 0}}^{\mathfrak{g}}M(\phi)
is
a simple smooth
\mathfrak{g}
-module with height
2
.

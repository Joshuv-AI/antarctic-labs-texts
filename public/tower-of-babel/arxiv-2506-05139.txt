Abstract
. We introduce a new kind of free independence, called real infinitesimal freeness. We show that independent orthogonally invariant with infinitesimal laws are asymptotically real infinitesimally free. We introduce new cumulants, called real infinitesimal cumulants and show that real infinitesimal freeness is equivalent to vanishing of mixed cumulants. We prove the formula for cumulants with products as entries.



1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

The first result in the asymptotic theory of random matrices is Wigner’s semicircle law which gave the limit eigenvalue distribution of a Wigner matrix, and in particular a GUE random matrix. Later Voiculescu showed that independent GUE random matrices are asymptotically free. The scope of the theorem on asymptotic freeness has now been extended by many authors so as not to require the entries be Gaussian random variables and weakening the assumptions on the joint distribution of the entries. This makes the results of free probability more widely applicable.

Let us recall the definition of a non-commutative distribution. Let
(\mathcal{A},\tau)
be a non-commutative probability space. This means that
\mathcal{A}
is a unital algebra over
\mathbb{C}
and
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is linear with
\tau(1)=1
. Given
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathcal{A}
the set
\{\tau(a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{n}})\mid 1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\leq s\}
is the non-commutative distribution of the random variables
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}
. Freeness is a rule for computing the joint distribution from the distribution of the individual variables.

If, for each
N
, we have a non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle N},\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle N})
and random variables
a_{1,{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,a_{s,{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
, we get, for each
N
, a non-commutative distribution. Asymptotic freeness means that the joint distribution tends to the joint distribution of free random variables.

Frequently the joint distribution of
a_{1,{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,a_{s,{\scriptscriptstyle N}}
can be expanded into a series in
1/N
, with the leading term being the limit distribution. The subleading terms of this expansion have been given a lot of attention for their connections to Hurwitz numbers, unitarily invariant ensembles in quantum gravity, topological recursion, analysis of spike models and principal minors.

When asymptotic freeness holds up to an error of order
o(1/N)
in this expansion, the underlying rule which appears for the first subleading term is the infinitesimal freeness of Belinschi and Shlyakhtenko
[
5
]
, or equivalently the type
B
freeness of Biane, Goodman and Nica
[
6
]
. For example, the computation of the mixed moments of GUE random matrices up to
O(1/N^{2})
in the pioneer paper of Thorbjørnsen
[
35
]
can be rephrased as asymptotic infinitesimal freeness of independent GUE matrices.

More generally, this first-order expansion of asymptotic freeness, which yields infinitesimal freeness, is true whenever the matrix ensembles are unitarily invariant in the sense that the joint distributions of the entries of the ensembles are invariant under conjugation by a unitary matrix. It has first been proven by Curran and Speicher
[
11
, Theorem 5.11]
, where this expansion is done up to
O(1/N^{2})
for bounded deterministic matrices which are randomly rotated by unitary matrices, implying asymptotically infinitesimal freeness.
In the work of Shlyakhtenko
[
34
]
and Collins, Hasebe, and Sakuma
[
9
]
asymptotic infinitesimal freeness of a family of unitarily invariant matrices from
finite rank matrices was shown. Similarly, Au
[
1
]
proved the asymptotic infinitesimal freeness of Wigner matrices from finite rank matrices. Beyond the finite rank case, Dallaporta and Février
[
12
]
proved the asymptotic infinitesimal freeness of independent GUE matrices from bounded deterministic matrices. Finally, the asymptotic infinitesimal freeness of two independent random matrices, at least one of them being unitarily invariant, is a consequence of the general theory of surfaced free probability of Borot,
Charbonnier, Garcia-Failde, Leid, and Shadrin
[
7
]
, and also a consequence of the computation of the matricial cumulants by the first author, Dahlqvist and Gabriel
[
8
]
. Indeed, both works contain an explicit and complete expansion in powers of
1/N^{2}
of the mixed
moments of independent and unitarily invariant random matrices from which asymptotic infinitesimal freeness can be recovered.

The main achievement of this paper is to weaken the assumption of unitary invariance to invariance under the smaller group of orthogonal matrices. As has been known since the work of Goulden and Jackson
[
17
]
, this means we now have to consider both non-orientable and orientable surfaces in our analyses. On the matrix side this means working with the transposes of the matrices in our ensemble. The resulting infinitesimal freeness we call
real infinitesimal freeness
.

The need for this investigation was shown in
[
20
]
, where it was shown that independent GOE random matrices were not asymptotically infinitesimally free in the sense of
[
16
]
, but that there was a universal rule for computing joint distributions. This was extended in
[
28
]
to the infinitesimal law of real Wishart random matrices.

In this paper we show that given ensembles
\{\mathcal{A}_{1,{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s,{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{N}
, all of which, or all but one of which, are orthogonally invariant with entries from different ensembles independent and with appropriate limit distributions, then the joint infinitesimal distribution converges to a joint distribution which satisfies our new rule of real infinitesimal freeness.

In detail, in

2
we recall the notions of (complex) infinitesimal freeness from
[

]
. Then in

This paper is a part (the other parts being

Then we define the linear map

written symbolically as

\tilde{\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}\tau&\tau^{\prime}\\
0&\tau\end{bmatrix};

by this we mean

\tilde{\tau}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}\tau&\tau^{\prime}\\
0&\tau\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tau(a)&\tau^{\prime}(a)+\tau(a^{\prime})\\
0&\tau(a)\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
\tilde{\tau}
is a conditional expectation of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
onto

3.
Real Infinitesimal Probability Spaces

In this section we review some notions of free independence with the addition of an involution. These have already appeared in the work of Redelmeier
[
32
]
others, but we repeat them here for clarity.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{s},x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{s}^{t}\rangle
where
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s},x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{s}^{t}\}
are
2s
non-commuting variables. For this part of the discussion it is useful to adopt the notation that
x^{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}_{i}=x_{i}
and
x^{{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}}_{i}=x_{i}^{t}
. We define an involution,
w\to w^{t}
, on
\mathcal{A}
by mapping
x^{(\epsilon_{1})}_{i_{1}}\cdots x^{(\epsilon_{n})}_{i_{n}}
to
x^{(-\epsilon_{n})}_{i_{n}}\cdots x^{(-\epsilon_{1})}_{i_{1}}
, where
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
, and then extend to all of
\mathcal{A}
by linearity. If
a\in\mathcal{A}
is such that
a=a^{t}
we say that
is
symmetric
. A linear subspace
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is
symmetric
if
b^{t}\in\mathcal{B}
whenever
b\in\mathcal{B}
. We say that a linear map
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is
symmetric
if
\tau(a^{t})=\tau(a)
. A linear map
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
state
if
\tau
is symmetric and
\tau(1)=1
. The triple
(\mathcal{A},t,\tau)
is just a special case of a
real non-commutative probability space
:
\mathcal{A}
is a unital algebra over
\mathbb{C}
,
t
is an involution, and
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a state.

Another important example of a real probability space comes from random matrices. Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathrm{P})
be a probability space , and
\mathcal{L}^{\infty-}
be the commutative algebra of random variables,
X
, on
\Omega
such that
\mathrm{E}(|X|^{n})<\infty
for all
n
. For a fixed
N
, we let
\mathcal{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}=M_{\scriptscriptstyle N}(\mathcal{L}^{\infty-})
be the matrices with entries from
\mathcal{L}^{\infty-}
, and for
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
we let
\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle N}(A)=\frac{{\scriptscriptstyle 1}}{{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\mathrm{Tr}(A)
. The involution,
t
, is the usual transpose.

Given any real non-commutative probability space
(\mathcal{B},\tau,t)
and elements
b_{1}\dots,b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
we define a state,
\tau_{\vec{b}}
, on
\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{s},x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{s}^{t}\rangle
by
\tau_{\vec{b}\,}(p)=\tau(p(b_{1},\dots,b_{s},b_{1}^{t},\dots,b_{s}^{t}))
. We call
\tau_{\vec{b}\,}
the
joint distribution
of the
n
-tuple
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
. The variables
(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
are free with respect to
\tau
if and only if the variables
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
are free with respect to
\tau_{\vec{b}}
, because they have the same joint distribution. We say that the random variables
(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
are
t
-free with respect to
\tau
if the random variables
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{n}^{t})
are free with respect to
\tau_{\vec{b}}
.

If we have a sequence
\{(\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle N},\tau_{\scriptscriptstyle N},t)\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
of real non-commutative probability spaces and for each
N
a
n
-tuple of random variables
b_{1,{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,b_{n,{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\in\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
, we say the tuples
\{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}

converge in distribution
if the sequence of states
\{\tau_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
converges point-wise on
\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{s},x_{1}^{t},\dots,x_{s}^{t}\rangle
. The state to which
\{\tau_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}

.

If
(\mathcal{A},\tau,t)
is a real probability space and
\tau^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is a linear map with
\tau^{\prime}(1)=0
we call the quadruple
(\mathcal{A},\tau,\tau^{\prime},t)
a
real infinitesimal non-commutative probability space
, or to be brief, a
real infinitesimal probability space
.

Given a sequence,
\{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
, of random variables with joint distributions
\{\tau_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}

\tau_{\vec{b}}
we can create a sequence of linear maps
\{\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
by setting

\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}(p)=N(\tau_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}(p)-\tau_{\vec{b}\,}(p))

. We have
\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}(p^{t})=\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}(p)
, but
\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}(1)=0
; so
\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}
is not a state. If we let
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}
be the unital subalgebra generated by
{b}_{1_{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,{b}_{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}},{b}^{t}_{1_{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\dots,{b}^{t}_{{}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}

(\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle N},\tau_{\vec{b},{\scriptscriptstyle N}},\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b},{\scriptscriptstyle N}},t)

\{\tau^{\prime}_{\vec{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}\}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}

to

we say that the sequence of variables

has a

. The inclusion of the word ‘real’ is to signal that we always include transposes in this notation by requiring convergence on

a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{A}_{s}
are centred and cyclically alternating, we have

(i)

when
n=2
or
n
is odd,
\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0
and,

(ii)

when
n=2k\geq 4
is even

\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\tau(a_{1}a_{k+1}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k}a_{n}^{t})
.

Proof.

First, let us assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
are real infinitesimally free and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{j_{i}}
are centred and cyclically alternating. We shall then prove conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
of the Proposition. We have
\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})\allowbreak=0
, as
j_{n}\not=j_{1}
. Next suppose

and consider the indices
j_{1},j_{k}
, and
j_{n}
. We have three cases:
j_{1}
,
j_{k}
,
j_{n}
distinct,
j_{1}=j_{k}
, or
j_{k}=j_{n}
. In all three cases
\tau(a_{1}a_{k}^{t}a_{n})=0
by the freeness of
\mathcal{A}_{j_{1}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{j_{n}}
. Thus when
n
is odd or equal to
2
we have
\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0
. Now when
n\geq 4
is even we have by
(iv)
of Definition
3.1
that
(ii)
above holds.

To prove the reverse implication, let us assume that
(i)
and
(ii)
above hold whenever
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are centred and cyclically alternating. Let us show that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{s}
are real infinitesimally free. This means that we have to show that whenever we have
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are centred and alternating, the conditions
(iii)
and
(iv)
of Definition
3.1
hold.

Let us assume that
n=2k-1
is odd. If
j_{n}\not=j_{1}
then just by the freeness of
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
we have that
\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})=0
and
\tau(a_{1}a_{k}^{t}a_{n})=0
. Thus
(i)
above, implies
(iii)
of Definition
3.1
. Now assume that
j_{n}=j_{1}
and let
\tilde{a}_{1}=a_{n}a_{1}-\tau(a_{n}a_{1})
. Now
\tilde{a}_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1}
are centred and cyclically alternating and

is even. Thus by
(ii)
above we have that

\tau^{\prime}(\tilde{a}_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})=\tau(\tilde{a}_{1}a_{k}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k-1}a_{n-1}^{t})=\tau(a_{1}a_{k}^{t}a_{n})\cdots\tau(a_{k-1}a_{n-1}^{t})

because
\tau(\tilde{a}_{1}a_{k}^{t})=\tau(a_{1}a_{k}^{t}a_{n})
. Finally we have

\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\tau^{\prime}(a_{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1})=\tau^{\prime}(\tilde{a}_{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1})+\tau(a_{n}a_{1})\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})

=\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})=\tau(a_{1}a_{k}^{t}a_{n})\cdots\tau(a_{k-1}a_{n-1}^{t})

which is exactly condition
(iii)
of Definition
3.1
.

Now let us assume
n=2k\geq 4
. We must prove
(iv)
of Definition
3.1
. We do this in two cases:
j_{1}\not=j_{n}
and secondly
j_{1}=j_{n}
. In case
j_{i}\not=j_{n}
the right hand side of
(iv)
in Definition
3.1
becomes the right hand side of

(ii)
in Proposition
3.4
, because
j_{1}\not=j_{n}
implies that

\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})=\tau(a_{1}a_{n})\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})=0.

So we are done in the case
j_{1}\not=j_{n}
. Before going further we need to prove two subclaims.

Sub-claim 1.

Suppose
\tau^{\prime}
satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
of Proposition
3.4
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are centred and alternating with
j_{1}=j_{n}
and
n
even. Then
\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0
.

We prove this by induction on
n
. When
n=2
, the claim holds by our assumption:
(i)
above. For
n>2
we have, letting
\tilde{a}_{1}=a_{n}a_{1}-\tau(a_{n}a_{1})
,

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})

\displaystyle=\tau^{\prime}(a_{n}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})=\tau^{\prime}(\tilde{a}_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\mbox{}+\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})=0,

where the first term vanishes by
(i)
above, because
n-1
is odd, and the second term vanishes by our induction hypothesis. This proves Sub-claim 1.

Sub-claim 2.

If
n=2k
is even,
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are centred and alternating, but with
j_{1}=j_{n}
, then

\tau(a_{1}a_{k+1}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k}a_{n}^{t})=0.

In order to have
\tau(a_{1}a_{k+1}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k}a_{n}^{t})\not=0
we must have
j_{1}=j_{k+1}
and
j_{k}=j_{n}
. But by assumption
j_{1}=j_{n}
, thus
j_{k}=j_{k+1}
contrary to our assumption that
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are alternating. This proves Sub-claim 2.

Now let us conclude the proof of the Proposition. We assume
n=2k
and that
\tau^{\prime}
satisfies
(i)
and
(ii)
of Proposition
3.4
, and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are centred and alternating, with
j_{1}=j_{n}
. We must prove that

(4)

\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})+\tau(a_{1}a_{k+1}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k}a_{n}^{t})

which is
(iv)
of Definition
3.1
. By Sub-claim 1 we have
\tau^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\tau(a_{1}\tau^{\prime}(a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1})a_{n})=0
. By Sub-claim 2 we have
\tau(a_{1}a_{k+1}^{t})\cdots\tau(a_{k}a_{n}^{t})=0
. Thus both sides of (
4
) vanish and this concludes the proof of the Proposition.

4.
Integration by parts for random matrices

We need some general definitions in order to do integration by parts on the orthogonal group. Let
\mathfrak{so}(N)
be the linear spaces of skew-symmetric matrices of size
N
, and let
(K_{ab})_{1\leq a<b\leq N}
be the basis of
\mathfrak{so}(N)
given by
K_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}
where
E_{ab}
is the matrix with
1
in the
(a,b)
-entry and
0
elsewhere. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}K_{ab}\otimes K_{ab}

\displaystyle=\kern-7.5pt\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}\kern-5.0ptE_{ab}\otimes(E_{ab}-E_{ba})-\kern-7.5pt\sum_{1\leq a>b\leq N}\kern-5.0ptE_{ab}\otimes(E_{ba}-E_{ab})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}E_{ab}\otimes E_{ab}-\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}E_{ab}\otimes E_{ba}

\displaystyle=P-T

where
P=\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}E_{ab}\otimes E_{ab}
and
T=\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}E_{ab}\otimes E_{ba}
. For a differentiable function
f:O(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we define the left derivative
\partial f(O):\mathfrak{so}(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by

\partial f(O)(K_{ab})=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}f(e^{tK_{ab}}O).

For convenience we denote this by
\partial_{K_{ab}}f
and with this notation we consider it as a map
O(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. If
f
has two derivatives, we can differentiate
\partial_{K_{ab}}f
to get
\partial^{2}_{K_{ab}}f
. For such
f
we then define the Laplacian operator by

\Delta f=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}\partial_{K_{ab}}^{2}f.

Denoting by
\mathit{id}:SO(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
the map
\mathit{id}(O)=O
and by
\iota:SO(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
the map
\iota(O)=O^{t}=O^{-1}
. We have

\partial_{K_{ab}}id(O)=K_{ab}O\ \text{ and }\ \partial_{K_{ab}}\iota(O)=-O^{-1}K_{ab},

or more concisely,
\partial_{K_{ab}}\mathit{id}=K_{ab}\mathit{id}
and
\partial_{K_{ab}}\iota=-\iota K_{ab}
. In particular we have,
\Delta\mathit{id}=\allowbreak\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}K_{ab}K_{ab}\mathit{id}
and using
\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}K_{ab}\otimes K_{ab}=P-T
we get

\Delta\mathit{id}=\sum_{1\leq a,b\leq N}-(E_{ba}E_{ab}+E_{ab}E_{ba})\mathit{id}=(1-N)\mathit{id}.

Let us define the
carré du champ
operator
\Gamma(f,g):SO(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by

\Gamma(f,g)=\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq N}\partial_{K_{ab}}f\otimes\partial_{K_{ab}}g.

If both
f
and
g
are twice differentiable functions from
O(N)
to
M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
then we define
f\otimes g:O(N)\to M_{N}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by
f\otimes g(O)=f(O)\otimes g(O)
. Then

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{K_{a,b}}(f\otimes g)(O)\mbox{}\ =\ \mbox{}

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{K_{a,b}}(f)(O)\otimes g(O)

\displaystyle\mbox{}+2\partial_{K_{a,b}}(f)(O)\otimes\partial_{K_{a,b}}(g)(O)+f(O)\otimes\partial^{2}_{K_{a,b}}(g)(O),

and so

\Delta(f\otimes g)=\Delta(f)\otimes g+2\Gamma(f,g)+f\otimes\Delta(g).

Because the Haar measure on

is invariant under multiplication by
e^{tK_{a,b}}
we have
\mathbb{E}(\Delta(f\otimes g))=0
, thus we have the following basic integration by parts formula:

(5)

\mathbb{E}[\Gamma(f,g)(O_{N})]=\mbox{}-\mathbb{E}[\Delta f(O_{N})\otimes g(O_{N})].

5.

Lemma 5.4

Suppose
(\mathcal{A},\varphi)
is a non-commutative probability space and

, …,
j_{k-1}\not=j_{k}

j_{k+1}\not=j_{k+2}
, …,
j_{n-1}\not=j_{n}
. Then
\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=0
unless
n
is even and

, in which case we have

(15)

\varphi(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\varphi(a_{1}a_{n})\cdots\varphi(a_{k}a_{k+1}).

Proof.

Let

6.
Real Infinitesimal Free Cumulants

and the Moment-cumulant formula

When we pass from complex infinitesimal freeness to real infinitesimal freeness we need to use the symmetric non-crossing annular permutations introduced in
[
28
]
.

Figure 1.
A non crossing permutation of a
(6,4)
-annulus.

Let us recall that
S_{NC}(p,q)
denotes the set of non-crossing permutations of a
(p,q)
-annulus. These are permutations of
[p+q]
such that the cycles can be drawn in an annulus, with
p
points on the outer circle and
q
points on the inner circle, in such a way that the cycles do not cross, see Figure
1
. See
[
24
, §5.1]
for a full definition and examples. The simplest characterization of these permutations is through Euler’s formula for the genus of a triangulated surface, but now transferred into the symmetric group:
\pi\in S_{\mathit{NC}}(p,q)
if and only if

\pi\vee\gamma_{p,q}=1_{p+q}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\#(\pi)+\#(\pi^{-1}\gamma_{p,q})=p+q,

where
\pi\vee\gamma_{p,q}=1_{p+q}
means that at least one cycle of
\pi
meets both cycles of
\gamma_{p,q}
, and
\gamma_{p,q}\in S_{p+q}
is the permutation with two cycles
(1,2,3,\dots,p)(p+1,\dots,p+q)
, and
\#(\pi)
is the number of cycles in the cycle decomposition of
\pi
, counting cycles of length
1
.

6.1.
Symmetric annular non-crossing permutations

In
[
28
, §2]
a subset of non-crossing annular permutations was identified. These are the
symmetric
non-crossing annular permutations. We denote this subset by
S_{\mathit{NC}}^{\delta}(n,-n)
, the definition is recalled in the next paragraph.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer. By
S_{\pm n}
we mean the permutations of
[\pm n]=\{\pm 1,\dots,\allowbreak\pm n\}
. We let
\delta\in S_{\pm n}
be the permutation with
n
cycles each of size
2
given by
\delta(k)=-k
. Next we let
\gamma_{n}\in S_{n}
be the permutation with the long cycle
(1,2,3,\dots,n)
. Throughout the paper we shall observe the following convention. If
\pi\in S_{n}
then we consider

7.

In this section we shall prove Theorem

In a complex infinitesimal probability space the complex infinitesimal cumulants

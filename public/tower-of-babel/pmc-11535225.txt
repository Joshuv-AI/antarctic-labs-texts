Correlation between coronary microvascular dysfunction and cardiorespiratory fitness in patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction
Authors: Jinglin Li, Wei Zhao, Zhenyu Tian, Yumeng Hu, Jianping Xiang, Ming Cui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe retrospectively investigated the relationship between cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) parameters and coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) using a novel angiography-based index of microcirculatory resistance (AccuIMR) in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) who underwent primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with complete revascularization. In 418 patients, the culprit vessel AccuIMR was calculated after successful primary PCI. CPET was conducted 44.04 ± 19.28 days after primary PCI. Overall, 157 patients (37.6%) showed elevated AccuIMR (> 40 U) in the culprit vessels. The LVEF was significantly lower in the CMD group than in the Non-CMD group. The CMD group showed worse results in VO2peak, peak O2-pulse, and VE/VCO2 slope than the Non-CMD group. Spearman correlation analysis suggested that VO2peak (r = -0.354), peak O2-pulse (r = -0.385) and VE/VCO2 slope (r = 0.294) had significant linear correlations with AccuIMR (P < 0.001). Multivariable logistic regression analysis showed that AccuIMR was the independent predictor of reduced VO2peak and elevated VE/VCO2 slope. The proportions of positive and equivocal ECG results and early O2-pulse flattening in the CMD group were significantly higher than those in the Non-CMD group, and AccuIMR was the only independent predictor of these ischemia-relating indicators, suggesting that patients with CMD had significant noninvasively detectable myocardial ischemia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74948-7.Keywords: Coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD), Index of microcirculatory resistance (IMR), ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET)Subject terms: Interventional cardiology, Myocardial infarction

Introduction

ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), one of the most urgent and severe conditions of cardiovascular disease, is widely treated with primary percutaneous intervention (PCI). However, despite the restoration of epicardial coronary blood flow after PCI, myocardial reperfusion failure and microcirculatory dysfunction in the region of the culprit vessel still occur in approximately half of patients with STEMI1,2. Recent technological advancements in the detection of microvascular resistance, such as the angiography-based index of microcirculatory resistance (AccuIMR), have enabled rapid and accurate assessment of coronary microvascular dysfunction (CMD) during primary PCI.

Cardiorespiratory fitness refers to the capacity of the circulatory and respiratory systems to supply oxygen during physical activity, which is a strong, independent predictor of cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality3. Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) is a quantitative, continuous, and non-invasive clinical examination method for evaluating cardiorespiratory fitness. It is currently the only clinical examination technique that allows for the simultaneous and comprehensive evaluation of multisystem functions of individuals, and can be used to monitor changes in full-lead electrocardiogram (ECG), blood pressure, and oxygen saturation, as well as metabolic indicators, such as pulmonary ventilation index and oxygen uptake, from the resting state to exercise loading. As a vital tool for the comprehensive assessment of cardiorespiratory fitness, CPET plays an increasingly important role in the management and rehabilitation of patients with cardiovascular diseases and is recommended by international guidelines4. Research on the relationship between coronary microvascular function and cardiopulmonary function is lacking.

Therefore, we investigated whether coronary microvascular function, assessed using the AccuIMR, has a correlation with cardiorespiratory fitness indexes in patients with STEMI after primary PCI.

Methods

Study design and population

A retrospective study was performed on patients who underwent primary PCI for STEMI at Peking University Third Hospital between January 2017 and August 2022. Inclusion criteria included the following: STEMI diagnosis according to the 2023 ESC Guidelines for the management of acute coronary syndromes5; primary PCI with complete revascularization (defined as successful revascularization of the infarct-related artery [IRA] and non-IRA lesions), with no angiographically significant lesions (at least 70% stenosis of a vessel with a diameter of at least 2.5 mm6) left; and CPET performed within 3 months after discharge. The exclusion criteria included severe hepatic or renal disorders; any type of malignant tumor; left ventricular ejection fraction < 35%; and incomplete clinical data. The exclusion criteria for the image quality check were as follows: poor image quality precluding analysis, poor contrast opacification, unsatisfactory projection view, and severe overlap or distortion of the target vessels. After these exclusions, 418 patients meeting the inclusion criteria were enrolled in this study (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of patient selection.

Clinical data, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), coronary angiography and intervention, clinical laboratory indicators, echocardiogram, concomitant diseases, and medication history, were collected by reviewing inpatient and outpatient medical records.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects enrolled in this study. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

AccuIMR calculation

Two experienced investigators computed the AccuIMR in a blinded fashion using dedicated software (AccuIMR, V1.0; ArteryFlow Technology, Hangzhou, China). The methodology for AccuIMR has been previously described7. Briefly, two angiographic projections ≥ 25° apart with optimal imaging quality were selected, and 3D reconstruction of the target vessel was performed. CMD was defined as AccuIMR > 40 U, according to previous studies8,9.

Cardiopulmonary exercise testing

An ULTIMACardiO2 gas exchange analysis system (Medgraphics Corp., St Paul, MN, USA) was used in this study. The methodology has been described previously by our team. Briefly, a ramp-protocol cycling test was conducted using symptom-limited exercises, guided by self-assessed fatigue and respiratory rates. Termination criteria included the onset of typical angina, significant symptoms, abnormal ECG findings, blood pressure anomalies, or participant discretion. After exercise, a 5-minute recovery period was monitored. Critical parameters measured included peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak, ml/kg/min), anaerobic oxygen uptake (VO2AT, ml/kg/min), oxygen pulse at peak oxygen uptake, (O2-pulse, ml/beat), ventilation per carbon dioxide output slope (VE/VCO2 slope), and oxygen uptake efficiency slope (OUES). According to previous guideline10, we defined reduced VO2peak as VO2peak < 20 ml/min/kg, elevated VE/VCO2 slope as VE/VCO2 slope > 30, and reduced peak O2-pulse as peak O2-pulse < 80% of the predicted value.

The ECG results were classified as negative, positive, and equivocal11. Positive exercise ECG was defined as follows: R-wave dominant leads, horizontal or downsloping ST-segment depression ≥ 0.1mV at 60–80ms after the J-point, lasting ≥ 2 min; and nonpathological Q-wave leads, upsloping ST-segment elevation ≥ 0.1 mV, lasting ≥ 1 min. Negative exercise ECG was defined as the absence of symptoms upon reaching the target heart rate and no significant ST-segment depression on the ECG during the test. Equivocal exercise ECG was defined as significant ST-segment depression that did not meet the positive criteria.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed SPSS v29.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables are shown as the number of cases and percentages (%), and the chi-square test was used for comparison between groups. The numerical variables were first tested for a normal distribution. The normally distributed variables were shown as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and variables were compared between two groups using independent samples t-tests and the correlation analysis was performed with Pearson’s correlation. Non-normally distributed variables are shown as median (first quartile, third quartile) [median (Q1, Q3)], and variables were compared between the two groups by using the Mann–Whitney U test, while the correlation analysis was performed using Spearman’s correlation. All P values were two-sided, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to calculate odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (95%CIs). All probability values were two-sided, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of STEMI patients

Among the 418 patients enrolled in this study, 157 patients (37.6%) with AccuIMR > 40 U were categorized into the CMD group, and 261 patients (62.4%) with AccuIMR ≤ 40 U were categorized into the Non-CMD group. The groups showed no significant differences in demographics, cardiovascular risk factors, or medications (Table 1). Patients in the CMD group had higher blood pressure and lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) than the Non-CMD group. In addition, hemoglobin, total cholesterol, AST, and peak TnT levels were significantly higher in the CMD group. Patients with CMD showed a higher mean post-PCI AccuFFRangio value than those with no CMD (0.93 ± 0.04 vs. 0.97 ± 0.02, P < 0.001) (Tables 1 and 2).

CPET parameters in STEMI patients

CPET took place 44.04 ± 19.28 days after primary PCI. No significant between-group differences in exercise time or CPET parameters at anaerobic thresholds, such as oxygen uptake, heart rate, or oxygen pulse, were identified (Table 3). For these peak parameters, no differences between the two groups in terms of heart rate, carbon dioxide ventilation equivalent (VE/VCO2), and systolic blood pressure were found. Patients in the CMD group had lower VO2peak (17.47 ± 4.05 ml/kg/min vs. 21.74 ± 4.34 ml/kg/min; P < 0.001), VO2peak as a percentage of predicted value (VO2peak/pred%) (63.41 ± 15.15% vs. 66.7 ± 15.44%; P = 0.035), and peak O2-pulse (9.71 ± 2.26 ml/beat vs. 12.42 ± 2.6 ml/beat; P < 0.001), and higher peak diastolic blood pressure (80 mmHg [72.5, 89] vs. 76 mmHg [68.5, 86]; P = 0.014) than did the Non-CMD group. The VE/VCO2 slope (33.16 ± 5.4 vs. 29.17 ± 4.81; P < 0.001) and PETCO2@rest (33 mmHg30,36 vs. 34 mmHg31,37; P = 0.015) also differed significantly between the CMD and Non-CMD groups. Patients in the CMD group had a higher proportion of positive and equivocal ECG results and the presence of early O2-pulse flattening than those in the non-CMD group (P < 0.001) (Table 3).

Correlation between AccuIMR and CPET parameters

Spearman’s correlation analysis showed that VO2peak (r = -0.354, P < 0.001) and peak O2-pulse (r = -0.385, P < 0.001) have a significant negative relationship with AccuIMR, while VE/VCO2 slope (r = 0.294, P < 0.001) and peak DBP (r = 0.142, P = 0.004) have significant positive relationship with AccuIMR (Fig. 2). There is no statistically significant correlation of VO2peak/pred% and PETCO2@rest with AccuIMR (P > 0.05).

Discussion

Our study found that individuals with CMD tend to have lower levels VO2peak and peak O2-pulse, and higher levels of VE/VCO2 slope in STEMI patients with complete revascularization after primary PCI. Correlation analysis also showed that coronary microvascular function assessed by AccuIMR had a significant linear relationship with these cardiorespiratory fitness parameters, and it was independently associated with decreased cardiorespiratory fitness, abnormal ECG stress test (EST) results and early O2-pulse flattening.

Coronary microvascular dysfunction assessment in patients with STEMI

Strong evidence supports the use of the IMR to assess coronary microcirculatory function in patients with STEMI. This objective and accurate coronary functional index for detecting CMD can predict the development of adverse events in patients with STEMI after primary PCI12,13. However, the additional use of pressure-temperature sensor wires and hyperemic drugs, which would prolong the procedure time and usually induce a hypotensive response, limits the adoption of wire-based IMR in daily practice, particularly in patients with acute STEMI. Recent technological advances have made it possible to derive the IMR from coronary angiography without the need for using pressure wires or hyperemic drugs14–16, which would expand the use of IMR in everyday clinical practice.

A recent study that used the same software and methodology as the present study to calculate AccuIMR showed that, in patients with STEMI, NSTEMI, and chronic coronary syndrome (CCS), the AccuIMR correlated well with conventional IMR (STEMI: r = 0.78, P < 0.001; NSTEMI: r = 0.78, P < 0.001; CCS: r = 0.75, P < 0.001)8. This study also showed that AccuIMR had good diagnostic performance in detecting abnormal IMR (overall diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity were 94.83% [91.14–97.30%], 92.11% [78.62–98.34%], and 95.36% [91.38–97.86%], respectively). Using IMR > 40 U as the cutoff value for AccuIMR, the AUC of AccuIMR for predicting abnormal IMR values was 1.000 (0.937–1.000) in patients with STEMI8. Recent studies have also shown the promising prognostic value of angiography-based IMR to patients with coronary heart disease, especially for cardiac death and heart failure13,17. There is also study showed that angio-IMR was significantly associated with CMR-derived infarct size, extent of MVO, and area at risk, and the elevated angio-IMR (> 40 U) after primary PCI for STEMI was a predictive factor of the presence of MVO in CMR18. In this case, we used the AccuIMR as a fair criterion for the assessment of CMD, with a cutoff value of 40 U.

Relationship between coronary microvascular function and CPET parameters

As cardiac rehabilitation progresses, the focus is increasingly on improving the overall cardiovascular fitness of patients with cardiovascular diseases. The treatment of cardiac patients now involves more than revascularization procedures, such as PCI or CABG and drug therapy. For individuals with coronary artery disease, particularly those with serious cases who have experienced STEMI, it has become increasingly important to assess cardiopulmonary function, develop exercise training programs, and plan other follow-up treatments, using CPET as part of disease management.

Our study found that patients with STEMI after primary PCI had lower VO2peak and peak O2-pulse and a relatively high VE/VCO2 slope if they had CMD. AccuIMR had a significant relationship with these parameters according to the correlation analysis. Multivariable logistic analysis also showed that AccuIMR was independently related with reduced VO2peak, elevated VE/VCO2 slope and ischemia-related indicators.

Cardiorespiratory fitness, which is typically defined by VO2peak, measured during maximal CPET, is an important sign in both healthy and diseased populations because of its strong prognostic value3,19,20. Previous research has demonstrated that patients with no obstructive coronary artery and CMD diagnosed by echocardiographic assessment of coronary flow velocities in the LAD, had a lower VO2peak, which indicated reduced exercise capacity21. Li et al. also found that patients with ischemic heart disease (IHD) had lower peak oxygen uptake and cardiac output than the healthy population, and similar outcomes were observed in the IHD patients with non-obstructive coronary artery after adjusted analysis22. Patients with epicardial coronary obstruction or microvascular obstruction may show a similar response on CPET despite different coronary catheterization findings23, therefore, the value of CPET assessment of patients with complete revascularization after PCI is also of particular importance in terms of the value of the disease and prognosis. Previous studies have generally concluded that abnormal coronary microvascular dilatation and coronary microvascular spasm are pathogenic mechanisms in chronic and acute ischemic heart disease24,25. It has also been found that CMD along with elevated trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) are both predictors of new-onset AF and decreased LVEF following STEMI26. We obtained a similar result in this study that in patients with STEMI with complete revascularization, the VO2peak was lower along with the LVEF, and VO2peak had a significant linear correlation with AccuIMR. Moreover, AccuIMR was the independent predictor of reduced VO2peak, which further demonstrated the influence of AccuIMR on the cardiorespiratory function. Therefore, it is reasonable to hypothesize that microvascular ischemia due to CMD can lead to impairment of cardiac function, which in turn leads to a decrease in VO2peak.

The VE/VCO2 slope, at which ventilation increases with increasing CO2 production, reflects ventilatory efficiency. An increase in the VE/VCO2 slope indicates an abnormal ventilatory response to exercise and has been used as a prognostic indicator of chronic cardiopulmonary disease, relatively independent of other CPET-derived variables, such as VO2peak. In patients with heart failure, ventilatory inefficiency, as suggested by an increased VE/VCO2 slope, characterizes those with more severe heart disease. Thus, this is considered an independent marker of event-free survival27,28. To date, few studies have addressed the relationship between coronary microcirculation and ventilation efficiency. Our study showed that the VE/VCO2 slope was elevated in STEMI patients with CMD as compared to those without CMD, and it had a linear correlation with IMR. Moreover, AccuIMR was independently related to the elevated VE/VCO2 slope. Previous studies have shown that an IMR > 40 is an independent predictor of heart failure readmission in patients with STEMI9,13. Individuals with CAD tended to have higher VE/VCO2 slopes than did those without CAD29. Dominguez-Rodriguez et al. also showed that the VE/VCO2 slope predicts perfusion defect in subjects with chest pain and suspected CAD (AUC 0.89, 95%CI 0.80–0.97, P < 0.0001)30. In this context, we thought that patients with CMD have worse ventilatory efficiency, and the higher the microcirculatory resistance, the worse the ventilatory inefficiency. Poor ventilatory efficiency in patients with STEMI with CMD may suggest poor cardiac function, which might explain the possible mechanism by which microvascular dysfunction affects prognostic events.

Peak O2-pulse is a crucial indicator of the state of cardiac, vascular, and pulmonary function. It reveals the overall efficiency and interaction of the respiratory and cardiovascular systems and has a significant correlation with cardiovascular function. It is an important tool for assessing cardiovascular health31. Our study showed that STEMI patients with CMD had a lower peak O2-pulse, suggesting a worse cardiac function. Munhoz et al. suggested that peak O2 pulse, when used as a supplement to peak oxygen uptake, could better reflect cardiovascular functional status and efficiency and assess cardiorespiratory fitness more accurately32,33. They also found that peak O2-pulse correlated with the severity of myocardial ischemia during exercise33. Laukkanen et al. followed 2227 middle-aged men for 26 years and found that peak O2-pulse was negatively correlated with the morbidity of cardiovascular disease as well as all-cause mortality34. Our study also showed a negative linear correlation between peak O2-pulse and IMR in STEMI patients, which supported the existence of exercise-induced myocardial ischemia in the region innervated by the microcirculation, which in turn leads to a reduction in peak O2-pulse. But further multivariable analysis showed that AccuIMR was not the independent predictor of reduced peak O2-pulse, which may be because that the effect of microvascular function on cardiac function may not yet be at the level of the currently defined reduced peak O2-Pulse, and further studies are needed in the future to determine the quantitative effect of microcirculatory function on O2-pulse.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the excessive accumulation of epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) promoted microvascular obstruction (MVO) by promoting the polarization state of cardiac macrophages towards an inflammatory phenotype, which suggests the inflammatory modulating pathway of microvascular function. It has been also declared that the potential therapeutic effects of GLP-1 receptor agonist and GLP-1/GLP-2 receptor dual agonist for MVO prevention were attributed to their impact on EAT modulation, which also provides a promising potential theraputic approach for microvascular dysfunction35. Additionally, hypotestosteronemia is associated with MVO, especially in patients with STEMI36. Moreover, the study of patients with STEMI after primary PCI showed that the fasting stress hyperglycemia ratio (SHR) is independently associated with impaired cardiac function and MVO37. Elevated SHR also increases the risk of rehospitalization for chest pain in patients with ischemia and nonobstructive coronary arteries38. These potential pathways suggest inflammatory and metabolic-related regulatory mechanisms of microcirculatory function and provide possible directions for the prevention and treatment of CMD, which still have a long way from being put into clinical practice. Our findings suggest a novel potential mechanism for the regulation of microcirculatory function, which may provide a more feasible way of treating CMD through exercise in clinical practice.

Microcirculatory function also plays an important role in the intervention strategy of STEMI patients. In STEMI patients with multivessel disease, direct stenting of the culprit lesion, aspiration thrombectomy and administration of intracoronary glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors were associated with a lower prevalence of CMD at 3 months and lower incidence of MACE at 12 months compared with balloon pre-dilatation stenting39. For those STEMI patients with high thrombus burden, the deferred-stenting strategy showed a better result in distal embolization after stent implantation, even though not statistically significant, which still suggests a possible adjustment of intervention strategy in certain STEMI patients40. Sonothrombolysis as a therapy to prevent distal embolization has been proven effective in the improvement of epicardial angiographic flow and recovery of microvascular function. Aspiration thrombectomy and distal protection devices are also distal embolization preventing therapies, which are widely used in clinical practice. Nevertheless, the prognostic value of aspiration thrombectomy and distal protection devices for STEMI patients and their impact on microcirculation function still need further large clinical trials to clarify41.

Ischemia-related indexes and CMD in STEMI patients with complete revascularization after primary PCI

EST specifically detects myocardial ischemia during exercise. However, in practice, a significant proportion of patients with positive results lack significant stenosis in the epicardial coronary arteries, as confirmed by angiography. Previously, with insufficient awareness of CMD and lack of detection of related markers, these positive ECG results were usually considered to be false positives. With the increasing use of coronary microcirculation testing, more studies are focusing on the effects of ischemia on coronary microcirculation. Our study also showed that both positive and equivocal EST results are highly specific to the microcirculatory impairments in patients with STEMI with complete revascularization. In STEMI patients with CMD, the positive rate was significantly high, and logistic regression analysis also showed that AccuIMR was the only significant predictor of abnormal EST results. Currently, coronary CTA is the first-line test in patients with chest pain associated with suspected myocardial ischemia, by global consensus. Therefore, EST can be used as a second-line option for noninvasive testing and may help optimize the diagnostic timing and process of CMD according to our findings. Additionally, our research suggests that, for patients with STEMI who continue to experience chest pain after PCI, or for those in whom conditions for invasive coronary functional tests are inadequate, CPET can be used to assess the presence of CMD specifically based on EST results, which can help avoid readmission and repeat coronary angiography. Moreover, CPET can be used to assess the response to therapy in patients diagnosed with CMD.

Previous studies have reported the diagnostic accuracy of positive EST results for detecting CMD, with reliable specificity but with poor sensitivity42,43. In our study, the negative results in the CMD group, which may be referred to as the false negative rate, also remained high. This phenomenon may be due to that the myocardial consequences of coronary blood flow abnormalities related to CMD differ from those of epicardial flow-limiting stenosis. In the latter, the myocardial perfusion impairment is distributed in the region perfused by the obstructed artery, resulting in segmental impairment of wall contraction. In contrast, myocardial ischemia in CMD may not involve all microvasculature originating from the epicardial artery but may have a patchy distribution. This pathophysiological feature could explain why patients with CMD may have symptoms of ischemia without ECG abnormalities44 and may be the reason for the poor sensitivity noted in our study. Vandeloo et al. reported in their study that, compared to using quantitative coronary angiography and fractional flow reserve (FFR) as references for determining ischemia, using FFR and IMR as references would have a lower false discovery rate (60.7% vs. 45.8%; p = 0.006)43, which indicated the importance of microcirculatory dysfunction in contributing to myocardial ischemia. Instead of using 25 as the IMR cutoff value for the diagnosis of CMD in their study, we used a cutoff of 40, which was a widely accepted criterion for patients with STEMI, and has been proven to have a better prognostic value9,12,13. Additionally, we excluded patients with incomplete revascularization; therefore, the positive EST results in our study were only related to microvascular dysfunction, without being affected by epicardial coronary artery obstruction, which makes our findings more reliable for CMD-related ischemia detection.

The early O2-pulse flattening observed during exercise indicated a limitation in adequately increasing the stroke volume response. This condition often suggests an inability to amplify stroke volume, which is indicative of exercise-induced cardiac dysfunction that may arise from ischemia. This is a firm and sensitive index for assessing and quantifying myocardial ischemia33,45,46. We found that similar to the EST results, the occurrence rate of early O2-pulse flattening in patients with CMD was much higher than in the Non-CMD group, and IMR was found to be the only significant factor of early O2-pulse flattening. This finding was complementary to EST for detecting myocardial ischemia, particularly caused by CMD.

These results of ischemia-related parameters further proved that patients with CMD had certain myocardial ischemia which is noninvasively detectable, suggesting an ischemia-related mechanism for the effects of CMD on cardiopulmonary fitness.

Study limitations

This study has a few limitations. We only measured the functional indexes of the IRAs in STEMI patients in this study, future analysis of the index of non-IRAs is needed to confirm the results. Furthermore, our study only focused on STEMI patients with primary PCI. Studies of other coronary heart diseases are needed in the future to confirm the results. Besides, CPET requires a relatively high exercise capacity to make sure patients can finish the test, which resulted in the exclusion of patients who were unable to complete the test due to severe cardiac conditions. Other alternative assessments of resting cardiopulmonary function should be performed on these patients to improve the results in future studies. Moreover, previous studies found that microvascular coronary heart disease was more frequent in female patients47, which was inconsistent with our results. This may be due to the high proportion of male patients in our cohort. Future studies should adjust the proportion of each gender to avoid this bias.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study found that CPET parameters, such as peak oxygen uptake and VE/VCO2 slope were significantly correlated with IMR in patients with STEMI who have undergone primary PCI with complete revascularization, and IMR was an independent predictor of reduced cardiopulmonary function, which revealed the possible reason of poor cardiorespiratory function after myocardial infarction and found a potential mechanism of CMD and a conceivable and feasible way of improving microcirculatory function. Moreover, patients with CMD had significant abnormalities in ischemia-related indexes, such as abnormal EST results and early O2-pulse flattening. IMR was also the only factor that influenced ischemia-related indexes, which illustrates the high correlation between microcirculatory disorders and myocardial ischemia.

Single-agent mosunetuzumab in older or unfit patients with previously untreated DLBCL: results of a phase 1/2 trial

ABSTRACT

TO THE EDITOR:

Patients aged ≥75 years or with major medical or functional impairments pose a treatment challenge in newly diagnosed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) because many are unable to receive curative anthracycline-containing immunochemotherapy.1, 2, 3 Dose-attenuated R-mini-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) may be tolerable in this population and provides a benchmark complete response (CR) rate of 53% and a 2-year overall survival (OS) rate of 66%.4,5

The observation that relapsed or refractory DLBCL can show durable responses to immunotherapy has prompted interest in applying this modality to frail patients.6 We conducted an international phase 1/2 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03677154) to evaluate mosunetuzumab, a CD20 × CD3 T-cell–engaging bispecific antibody,6,7 in older or unfit patients with previously untreated DLBCL. Here, we report the primary analysis of IV mosunetuzumab monotherapy after >2 years of follow-up.

This study was designed by the sponsor and approved by institutional review committees at participating centers. Eligible patients had previously untreated, CD20+ DLBCL and were either aged ≥80 years or aged ≥60 years and deemed unfit for full-dose chemotherapy because of performance status, impairments in activities of daily living, or comorbidities. Patients could be eligible for attenuated chemotherapy and, given the unknown safety of mosunetuzumab in this setting, were excluded for heart failure, severe pulmonary disease, or renal disease. Full eligibility criteria are provided in the supplemental Methods. All patients provided written informed consent.

Mosunetuzumab was evaluated at 2 dose levels (13.5 and 30 mg). The decision to proceed with the full-dose cohort integrated safety data from this trial and the parallel phase 1 trial.7 Mosunetuzumab was administered IV using step-up dosing on cycle 1 (C1) day 1 (D1; 1 mg) and D8 (2 mg), followed by the target dose on C1D15 and C2 to C8 D1 (21-day cycles). Corticosteroid premedication was administered to mitigate cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and was optional from C3 onward if no CRS occurred. Hospitalization was not required. Prephase therapy with ≤7 days of prednisone (100 mg/d or equivalent) was permitted.

Treatment was discontinued after C8 in patients who achieved CR. Patients with partial response (PR) or stable disease could receive up to 16 cycles in total. Follow-up evaluations occurred every 6 months for 2 years after the end of treatment (EOT).

The primary end point was investigator-assessed objective response rate (ORR) at the EOT (after C8), assessed by positron emission tomography–computed tomography.8 No prespecified level of ORR was subject to statistical hypothesis testing; rather, the results were intended to inform further study of mosunetuzumab in a subsequent cohort in combination with polatuzumab vedotin. Secondary end points included best ORR, duration of response, duration of CR, progression-free survival (PFS), and OS. Statistical methods are detailed in the supplemental Data.

Between May 2019 and February 2022, the study enrolled 54 patients: 8 at the 13.5 mg dose and 46 at the 30 mg dose. Forty-one patients (76%) were aged ≥80 years and thus were geriatrically unfit or frail (Table 1).9 Patients aged <80 years were eligible because of specific comorbidities detailed in supplemental Results. Twenty-four patients completed treatment, and 30 discontinued treatment (supplemental Figure 1). Median treatment duration was 8 cycles (range, 1-16; supplemental Table 1). After a median follow-up of 37.6 months, the ORR rate and CR rate at EOT were 44% (95% confidence interval [CI], 31-59) and 35% (95% CI, 23-49), respectively (Figure 1A; supplemental Table 2). The best ORR and CR rates were 56% (95% CI, 41-69) and 43% (95% CI, 29-57), respectively. Among the 23 patients who achieved CR, 12 (52%) remained in CR, 3 (13%) relapsed, and 8 (35%) died of other causes (listed in supplemental Results). ORR at EOT did not differ substantially by dose-level cohort (supplemental Table 4) or other key clinical variables (supplemental Figure 2), but the EOT CR rate was 24% for germinal center B-cell (GCB), 46% for non-GCB, and only 10% for double-hit or triple-hit lymphoma. Median PFS was 6.6 months (95% CI, 2.9-12.1), and median OS was 37.7 months (95% CI, 14.1 to NE), with a 30-month event-free rate of 54% (95% CI, 40-67; supplemental Table 2; Figure 1D-E).

Twenty-one patients received salvage therapy, of whom 17 achieved CR or PR. Ten received R-mini-CHOP, 4 received R-CHOP, and the remainder received low-intensity regimens. Six patients who received salvage anthracycline-containing chemotherapy were aged <80 years and had initially been deemed ineligible for chemotherapy because of functional impairments or comorbidities but regained eligibility after response to mosunetuzumab.

Adverse events (AEs) occurred in 53 patients (98%; supplemental Table 3). The most common AEs (≥10%) were aggregated infections (described subsequently), rash (31%), CRS (26%), fatigue (26%), abdominal pain (17%), diarrhea (17%), and neutropenia (15%; Figure 1F). Grade 3 or 4 AEs occurred in 27 patients (50%), most commonly infections (20%) and neutropenia (13%); 1 patient (2%) had febrile neutropenia. Serious AEs occurred in 23 patients (43%). Two patients (4%) discontinued treatment because of AEs (hepatitis B reactivation and renal failure). Three fatal AEs occurred: 2 COVID-19-related pneumonias and 1 death from natural causes.

CRS occurred in 14 patients (26%), all grade 1 or 2, and resolved within 2 days. Treatments included corticosteroids (n = 1), low-flow oxygen (n = 2), or IV fluids (n = 2). No patients required tocilizumab or vasopressors. Three grade 1 neurologic events, considered unrelated to mosunetuzumab by investigators, were reported and resolved. Two patients (4%) experienced grade 1 or 2 cardiac toxicities (ventricular arrhythmia and tachycardia).

Infections occurred in 23 patients (43%), most commonly in the upper respiratory tract or urinary tract. Grade 3 or 4 infections occurred in 11 patients (20%). Circulating CD19+ B-cell counts recovered within 12 months in evaluable patients (n = 16; supplemental Figure 3). Pharmacokinetic findings were consistent with population modeling (supplemental Figure 4).

This phase 1/2 study demonstrates that mosunetuzumab can induce durable CRs in a subset of older or unfit patients with previously untreated DLBCL. However, it also highlights several challenges. As in prior trials of R-mini-CHOP,4,5 eligibility was based on age and investigator-assessed chemotherapy ineligibility, yet many patients tolerated subsequent chemotherapy, underscoring the need for standardized geriatric definitions and assessments. Infectious toxicity was notable, although treatment discontinuations related to infection were rare. Prophylaxis, which was optional in this trial, should be considered in future studies.10

The best CR rate of 43% and median PFS of 6.6 months indicate that mosunetuzumab monotherapy requires further improvement in efficacy. However, CRs were durable, with only 3 patients relapsing after CR and 57% remaining disease-free at 2 years. A plateau in PFS after >1.5 years of follow-up, supported by 25% of patients remaining in remission, suggests that chemotherapy-free, T-cell–redirecting immunotherapy can produce long-term disease control in selected patients with DLBCL.

Median OS of 37.7 months compares favorably with real-world outcomes with R-mini-CHOP (median OS, 34.7 months) and with outcomes with rituximab monotherapy (median OS, 13.8 months).11 These results support the feasibility of chemotherapy-sparing approaches for older or unfit patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL. Emerging data suggest improved efficacy with subcutaneous mosunetuzumab, which may also cause less immediate-onset CRS but has similar infectious toxicities,12,13 and with other bispecific antibodies such as epcoritamab and glofitamab.14,15 Careful evaluation of patient selection and transparent reporting of toxicities, particularly CRS and aggregated infections, will be critical to optimizing these approaches.

In summary, mosunetuzumab monotherapy demonstrated activity with manageable toxicity in older, unfit patients with previously untreated DLBCL. These results were deemed sufficient to proceed to the subsequent ongoing cohort of this trial, which improved the eligibility criteria by formally incorporating geriatric assessment and combined subcutaneous mosunetuzumab with polatuzumab vedotin to improve efficacy.9,12

Multifaceted gold nanoparticles by bark extract of Sweetinia mahagoni and their potential antimicrobial, antioxidant, anticancer and antiviral applications
Authors: Venkata Subbaiah Kotakadi, Susmila Aparna Gaddam, Lakshmi Kavitha Kommalapati, Siva Gayathri Velakanti, Alapati Phanidhara, Devaraju Thangellamudi, Hema Masarapu, Enyew Amare Zereffa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The current study of the biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs reveals a SPR peak at 554 nm indicating reduction of gold chloride to gold nanoparticles (Sm-AuNPs). Fourier Infrared Spectroscopy revealed that different bioactive compounds of bark extract were involved in reduction and stabilization of Sm-AuNPs. Transmission Electron Microscopy and EDX analysis of Sm-AuNPs reveals that the nanoparticles spherical in shape. The Sm-AuNPs are poly-dispersed in nature, with a poly-disperse index about 0.310 and high negative zeta potential value of -62.5 mV. The Sm-AuNPs reveals excellent antioxidant, antibacterial, anticancer activities and anti-viral properties. The results revealed that the Sm-AuNPs have an excellent antibacterial activity when compared with standard antibiotics while they also reveal a significant antioxidant activity. The anticancer studies on SKOV ovarian cancer cell line by Sm-AuNPs was carried by MTT assay, reveals that IC50 value was 64.19 µg/ml and the apoptotic cells were detected by Dual fluorescence assay. Apart from the above studies, the antiviral efficacy of Sm-AuNPs on New Castle Disease (NDV) was carried out in embryonated chicken eggs, reveals that the Sm-AuNPs have very good and considerable antiviral properties. The green synthesized Sm-AuNPs can useful as future therapeutic agents to control cancer and NDV effectively.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47014-7.

Introduction

Nano-Science and Nanotechnology are the most trending and innovative areas of science, in which various number of metal nanoparticles, have been synthesized and exploited them for infinite range of applications. These nano-materials and metal nanoparticles are a new kind of materials, which were emerged to create great advances for the development of science in recent years by altering, engineering and functionalizing the nanoparticles. These nanoparticles are widely used in various fields of sciences due their unique applications in medicine, biology, physics, chemistry and bio-sensing1–4. Among the noble metal nanoparticles like Ag, Cu, Hg, Pt, Zn and Au, the ‘gold nanoparticles’ (AuNPs) reported have wide range of unique functional properties. They have attracted extensive attention due their exceptional physicochemical characteristics, at the same time very easy to synthesis and also they are easily altered the size and shape. AuNPs are extensively used medical field especially in drug and gene delivery, radiation therapy, photodynamic therapy and photothermal therapy, X-ray imaging, computed tomography, diagnosis, and other biological activities like biosensors, immunoassays, genomics, clinical chemistry, DNA and antigens detection, optical bio-imaging and the monitoring of cells and tissues5–9. At present the Nanotechnology is a promising field that has new avenues in agriculture and plant biotechnology10. Apart from biological and medical applications the various are Nanoparticles (NPs), these NPs also revealed excellent applications in the field of Agriculture, Agriculture is the prime source of livelihood of world population. A recent study of green fabrication of ZnO nanoparticles by whole plant leaves of Cymbopogon citratus grass revealed an important application in plant tissue culture, were the ZnONPs were incorporated in the media, which enchance the callus induction and regeneration of Switchgrass (Panicum virgatum L.) which is a perennial and seasonal grass, with wide range of applications like biomass production for renewable fuels and rich fodder crop for grazing animals11. Likely another study synthesis of Iron oxide nanoparticles by leaf extract of Cymbopogon jwarancusa revealed and significant results in plant tissue culture which have excellent in-vitro callus induction and shoot regeneration and root induction of chickpea plants (Cicer arietinum L)12. These studies reveal that the vital to use micronutrients in the form of nanoparticles can increase the crop productivity13. The nano-materials combined with micronutrients not only enhance the productivity of crops, they also accelerate plant germination and regeneration capacity, and at the same time develop crop resistance against various biotic and abiotic stresses14,15. Similarly, an important study on green fabrication of ZnO and Cr-dopped ZnONPs using leaf extract C. reticulate revealed promising applications in antioxidant and antibacterial and hemolytic applications and other important thing observed was doping also enhance the biological activities16. Another study on green synthesis of silver nanoparticles using Aloe barbadensis miller leaf extract also reveals advanced therapeutic applications like Anticancer, anti-oxidative and also antimicrobial and photo-catalytic properties17. An, recent study on green synthesis of gold nanoparticles using bio-waste peel extract of Solanum tuberosum reveals excellent biomedical applications like antioxidant, antibacterial and also industrial photo-catalytic application18. Based upon the importance of nano-science in various up-coming areas, the present study was initiated to find-out the multiple biomedical applications of gold nanoparticles (AuNPs).

According to the new study in year 2022 initiated by needed. Ernst & Young (EY) and the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce & Industry (FICCI) reveals that India is going face higher burden of cancer by increasing the cancer incidence by 29% i.e. 19–20 hundred thousand of cases of cancer will be estimated. The present study is focused on ‘Ovarian cancer’; it is the third most common cancer in Indian women, apart from breast and cervical cancers. The incidence rate is 5.4–8/100,000 people. The risk due ovarian cancer starts at the age of 35 years and reaches peak stage at the age 55–64 years. Ovarian cancer is leading cause of death from cancer in Indian women, with 3.34% (24015) of all cancer deaths in India. The estimated number of incident cases of cancer in India for the year 2022 was found to be 14,61,427 (crude rate:100.4 per 100,000)19. In India, one in nine people are likely to develop cancer in his/her lifetime. Lung and breast cancers were the leading sites of cancer in males and females, respectively. The recent developed technologies like Nanotechnology have opened new avenues for cancer treatment by various metal nanoparticles which were synthesized by various chemical and physical method and biological methods20. Nanotechnology has revealed an infinite potential for targeting and enhancing the delivery of drugs and vaccines in the field of veterinary medicine21. The escalating progress in the area of the nanoparticles field, leads to the development of new therapeutics to treat viral or bacterial infections. These newly developed nanoparticles could be used to transfer medicines successfully to different cells to treat diseases22,23. Nano-pharmaceutical products are the most encouraging and positive sectors of nanotechnology, which have several advantages in veterinary medicine24,25. At the same time the nanomaterials have been extensively used as antiviral agents in several studies. Here are a few examples of nanoparticles against different viral diseases. Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is an extremely infectious and contagious viral disease caused by foot-and-mouth disease virus (FMDV) which belongs to the family of Picornaviridae virus. FMDV causes severe illness in cows, sheep, goats, pigs, deer and other animals, which have divided hooves26–28. Another important viral disease infecting animals is a ‘Peste des petits ruminant’ (PPR) which is an extremely communicable trans-boundary viral disease, that mainly affects sheep and goats, thus causing major economic losses. Its morbidity and mortality is up to 100% in the infected and affected flocks29. Another important virus, that infects cattle and buffaloes worldwide, resulting in significant economic losses30 is ‘Infectious bovine rhinotracheitis/infectious pustular balanoposthitis’ (IBR/IPB) which is an extremely contagious viral disease caused by Bovine Herpes virus type-1 (BoHV-1), it belongs to the family of Herpesviridae. Avian influenza virus (AIV) is another highly contagious virus that causes significant morbidity and mortality in poultry industry. Some strains of AIV are likely to cause pandemic a threat to humans31,32.

Newcastle virus is one of the most important viral diseases of poultry in terms of global distribution and devastating economic losses. Newcastle disease virus (NDV) belongs to the Avulavirus genus. Avian paramyxovirus serotype 1 group is known to be one of the most important diseases of poultry worldwide33. It was first observed and reported in Java, Indonesia, in 1926 and later spread all over the world. It is the most contagious of all viral diseases, which is spreading rapidly between susceptible birds. Due to outbreak of Newcastle disease in commercial poultry, causing severe economic consequences, the disease was reported to the “World Organization for Animal Health”. Newcastle disease infection in birds produces 100% morbidity and 50% mortality in adult chicken when compared with higher mortality rate in younger birds34–36. NDV also infects ducks and geese, game birds and pigeons. The disease is usually less severe in Turkeys than that of chicken. Newcastle disease is a significant disease in most of the developed countries, and hence it is very essential to take necessary legislative measures for control of the disease. So, in the present study an attempt was made to study the antiviral properties of green synthesized gold nanoparticles.

The most simple and rapid method for synthesis of gold nanoparticles is 'chemical reduction' by using metal ions in solutions. Turkevich et al., (1951) is the first research group for synthesis of AuNPs by chemical method, it’s a universal single-phase aqueous reduction of Chloroauric acid (HAuCl4) by sodium citrate37. It is well known that in this reaction, hydrogen tetrachloroaurate hydrate was reduced to Au (0) atoms in which sodium citrate acts as reducing and stabilizing agent. The AuNPs can also be synthesized by changing the pH of gold solute complex, which can the alter the particle size38,39. Ion implantation is an important method to synthesis the AuNPs with precise chemical, physical, and biological properties. The synthesis of AuNPs was carried out by various chemical and physical methods, which were simple and cheap, with relatively high yielding concentrations of gold nanoparticles. Recently, simple, rapid and efficient efforts have been made for synthesis of AuNPs using plants materials, which are eco-friendly, dependable, reliable a self alternative method popularly known as 'Green synthesis. It avoids harsh and highly toxic chemicals used in chemical synthesis. Interestingly, the synthesis of metal nanoparticles has given rise to a new thrilling approach toward the development of these biological nano-factories40. Several studies have been reported on synthesis of AuNPs using diverse plants and plant extracts which involves the use of harmless bio- active components of various parts of plant materials like leaves, shoot, bark, roots, fruits and flowers etc. Abundant bio-active molecules present in plants such as flavonoids, tannins, polyphenols, resins, phytosterols, quinones, etc., play an important role in the synthesis of AuNPs41. The bioactive molecules are involved in the reduction and stabilization of AuNPs.

In the present study, the green synthesis of gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) was performed by the reduction of aqueous gold metal ions by using the aqueous bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni (L.) Jacq tree. It’s a deciduous tree which grows up to 30 m height, the plant spherical with buttressing base. The bark is smoothing grey, turning to scaly dark reddish-brown color with many heavy branches. The leaves are pinnate and even sized of 10–18 cm length, with 4–10 pairs of leaflets. The leaves are dark shiny green, lance-shaped of 2.5–5 cm and 0.7–2 cm42. As per the review of earlier studies, the Mahogany plants have a vast range of pharmacological activities like antimicrobial, antioxidant, analgesic, anti-inflammatory, hepato-protective, antiulcer, anti-diabetic, anti-HIV, immune-modulator, insect repellent and larvicidal, antifungal, depressant, anticonvulsant and neuro-pharmacological, platelet aggregation inhibitors, anti-diarrheal, anti-mutagenic and anticancer42–46. The flavonoids known have exceptional antioxidant properties47. Other studies reported on the phytochemical quantity of S. mahagoni bark, the total and sulphated ash, water soluble and total acid insoluble ash content of the bark to be 22.0%, 14.5%, 1.4% and 0.6% respectively47 and tannin content (15.0%)41. Hence, keeping in view, the above emerging problems in cancer therapy and antiviral outbreaks, an attempt has been made to synthesis gold nanoparticles by bark extract of an important medical plant Swietenia mahagoni (L.) Jacq. AuNPs were green synthesized by using bark extract of S. mahagoni. The green synthesized AuNPs were characterized by various spectroscopic method like UV-visible spectrometry, FTIR, DLS & Zeta potential, TEM and EDX and their biomedical applications like, antioxidant activity, antimicrobial activity and anti cancer studies (on SKOV ovarian cancer cells) and antiviral studies against NDV was carried out to detect the therapeutic nature of biosynthesized AuNPs by bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni (L.) Jacq tree (Sm-AuNPs).

SKOV-3 (Human ovarian carcinoma cell line) was purchased from NCCS, Pune, India. The SKOV-3 cells were maintained in MCCoy’s 5 A medium supplemented with 10% FBS along with the 1% antibiotic-antimycotic solution in the atmosphere of 5% CO2, 18–20% O2 at 37 °C temperature in the CO2 incubator and sub-cultured for every 2-3days.

Preparation of the Swietenia mahagoni bark extract

Fresh bark was collected from Swietenia mahagoni (L.) Jacq tree and was bought to laboratory and rinsed with tap water followed by distilled water and then they were cut into small pieces. Then 5 g of bark pieces were weighed and added to 150 ml of Milli Q water and heated on the water bath for 30 min at 70 °C. The bark extract was cooled and was left overnight at room temperature and next day morning the bark extract was filtered through Whatman No.1 filter paper. The filtrate collected and was stored at 4 °C and used for further experiments.

Green synthesis of gold nanoparticles (Sm-AuNPs)

Aqueous 1mM Gold Chloride (AuCl4H7O3) was prepared using Milli Q water and stored at room temperature in an amber color reagent bottle. The diluted 0.01 mM gold chloride solution was used for the green synthesis of gold nanoparticles using bark extract. 5 mL of diluted filtrate of Swietenia mahagoni bark extract was added 10 ml of aqueous solution of 0.01 mM gold chloride solution for reducing Au (III) ions to gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) In the present reaction, the bark extract filtrate acts as reducing and stabilizing agent for the reduction of 0.01 mM of HAuCl4, the samples were left at room temperature, until the color of solution changed from colorless to purple and subsequently dark purple shown in (Fig. 1.a–c). The green synthesis of Sm-AuNPs by using bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni was done without any external toxic and hazardous chemicals, the process is easy and eco-friendly method.

Purification of Sm-AuNPs

The solution containing Sm-AuNPs was centrifuged at 20,000 rpm for 20 min to obtain the Sm-AuNPs pellets. The Sm-AuNPs pellets were re-dispersed in 30 mL of Milli Q water and centrifuged again at 15,000 rpm for 10 min to get rid of any unbound plant molecules. The centrifugation and re-dispersion of Sm-AuNPs in Milli Q water was repeated thrice to obtain final pure Sm-AuNPs. The purified Sm-AuNPs pellets were then used for further studies including FTIR, Particle size analysis, Zeta potential analysis, Transmission electron microscopy and Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (TEM coupled with EDX) and their therapeutic applications.

Spectral characterization of Sm-AuNPs

The biosynthesized gold nanoparticles (Sm-AuNPs) along with bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni were analyzed by UV visible spectrometry within the wavelength range of 220 –750 nm using Nanodrop-8000 (Thermo Scientifics, U.S.A.), UV-visible spectrophotometer, available at DST Purse Centre, Sri Venkateswara University, Tirupati. The biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs were analyzed at room temperature on Nanodrop-8000 spectrophotometer at a resolution of 1 nm to detect the surface Plasmon resonance (SPR) of biosynthesized gold nanoparticles. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis of Swietenia mahagoni bark extract and green synthesized Sm-AuNPs was carried out using Bruker Tensor 27, Thermo Scientifics, USA, available at Physics Department, S.V.University, Tirupati, to detect the bioactive compounds present in the bark extract and which compounds are actively involved bio-reduction and capping of biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs. Particle size and Zeta potential analysis of biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs was carried out by dynamic light scattering (DLS) method by using Nanopartica Horiba SZ-100 available at DST PURSE centre, S.V.University, Tirupati to detect the size distribution of Sm-AuNPs and to findout the stability of the biosynthesized gold nanoparticles. Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) and EDX was performed by Joel JSM IT 500 and EDS instrument attached to Carl Zeiss EVO MA 15 machine with a line resolution 2.32 (in Å), available at DST SAIF facility available at Shivaji University, Kolhapur. The images were taken by coating a drop of Sm-AuNPs on a copper grid. Energy Dispersive Absorption Spectroscopy photograph of Sm-AuNPs was carried out by same TEM equipment as mentioned above.

Anti-bacterial activity of Sm-AuNPs

The bacterial strains of E. coli (Gram-negative), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Gram- negative), Staphylococcus aureus (Gram-positive), and Bacillus megaterium (Gram-positive) were used for the determination of antibacterial activity of Sm-AuNPs along with bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni (which were obtained from Department of Microbiology and maintained at DST PURSE centre, Sri Venkateswara University, Tirupati). The anti-bacterial activity of Sm-AuNPs along with bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni was carried out against four different bacterial strains as mentioned above by Kirby Bauer disc diffusion method. The procedure was followed according to Kotakadi et al.48,49, 100 µL of freshly prepared overnight bacterial culture of all the above bacterial samples were spread on nutrient agar plates with respective microbial cultures and then sterile Whatman Grade 1 filter paper discs were placed on nutrient agar plates at respective places. The bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni along with biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs were added onto the sterile discs with the help of micropipette at different concentrations of 10 mcg (10 µg) and 30 mcg (30 µg)were added respectively (1 mcg = 1 µg). Amoxyclav (Himedia SD063) 30 mcg discs were used as standard antibiotic. The nutrient agar plates loaded with bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni and Sm-AuNPs and antibiotic were incubated at 37 °C for overnight. The next day the Zone of Inhibition (ZOI) was calculated and tabulated the results and photographs were taken for further analysis.

Antioxidant activity of Sm-AuNPs

DPPH free radical scavenging activity

Free radical scavenging activity of the Sm-AuNPs was determined by using 2, 2’- diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assay according to the method described by3. DPPH solution (2 ml) was added to 1 mL of methanol solution containing test samples of bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni and biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs at different concentrations of 25 µg/mL, 50 µg/mL, 75 µg/mL and 100 µg/mL. The DPPH radical scavenging activity (RSA) was measured by determining the absorbance at 517 nm, using ascorbic acid as standard and the antioxidant activity.

The nitric oxide scavenging activity

The Nitric oxide scavenging activity was carried out by a tailored method of Ferreira et al.50. Nitric oxide radicals (NO) were generated from sodium nitroprusside. Sodium nitroprusside ( 1 mL of 10 mM) and 1.5 mL of phosphate buffer saline (0.2 M, pH 7.4) were added to different concentrations (25, 50, 75 and 100 µg/mL) of the Sm-AuNPs and bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni and incubated for 150 min at 25 °C. H2O2 scavenging ability of Sm-AuNPs

H2O2 scavenging ability of the Swietenia mahagoni bark extract and biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs at different concentrations of 25 µg/mL, 50 µg/mL, 75 µg/mL and 100 µg/mL, was examined according to the modified method of Pick and Mizel51. H2O2 (40 mM) solution was prepared in phosphate buffer (pH 7.4), Sm-AuNPs and bark extract at different concentrations of 25 µg/mL, 50 µg/mL, 75 µg/mL and 100 µg/mL in 3.4 mL phosphate buffer were added to H2O2 solution (0.6 mL, 40 mM). The absorbance of the reaction mixture was recorded at 230 nm and the percent of scavenging activity of H2O2 was determined using the equation as defined for DPPH scavenging activity.

An in vitro cell viability assay was carried out using Human ovarian cancer cell line SKOV3 and its Morphological changes were observed by MTT assay. Cell viability was performed MTT assay as described by distinguished Scientists52,53. 200µL SKOV3 cancer cell suspension were seeded in a 96-well plate at fixed cell density (20,000 cells per well) and the cells were grown for 24 h. After 24 h different concentrations of Sm-AuNPs 6.25 µL, 12.5 µL, 25 µL, 50 µL and 100 µL along with standard drug Camptothecin 7.5 µM concentration were added to different wells in the plate in triplicates and incubated the plate for 24 h at 37 °C in presence of 5% CO2 atmosphere. The IC50 value was determined by using linear regression equation, Y = Mx + C. Here, Y = 50, M and C values were derived from the viability graph.

Dual fluorescence apoptosis assay

The dual fluorescence apoptosis assay was carried out on SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells, for this assay the ovarian cancer cells were cultured in a 6-well plate at a density of 2 × 105 cells/2 ml and incubate the cells in a CO2 incubator for overnight at 37 °C for 24 h. After 24 h aspirate the spent medium in the six well plates and the cells were treated with Sm-AuNPs at the rate of IC50 value concentration of 64.19 µL/mL, along with standard drug Camptothecin 7.5 µM concentration. The above concentrations were added except in cell control in 2 mL of culture medium and incubate the cell culture along with cells for 24 h. After 24 h the cells were stained with Acridine Orange and Ethidium bromide staining (Hoechst 33258 staining). The stained cancer cells were observed under fluorescence microscope with different filter cubes with Excitation 560/40 nm and Emission 645/75 nm for EtBr (Ethidium bromide) and Excitation 470/40 nm and Emission 525/50 nm for Acridine orange and Hoechst 33,258 staining fluorescence microscopy Excitation 352/461 nm and Emission 510/40 nm. The Images were overlayed using ImageJ Software v1.48.

Antiviral studies of Sm-AuNPs on new castle disease Virus (NDV)

Experimental design

50 Pathogen-free embryos were procured from M/s Balaji Hatcheries Pvt ltd, Chittoor. Each group contains five embryos of ten-day old age. The groups were divided as Normal control, Virus control, Sm-AuNPs control, 5 µL/mL (5 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 10 µL/mL (10 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 15 µL/mL (15mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 20 µL/mL (20 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 25 µL/mL (25 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 50µL/mL (50 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs, 100 µL/mL (100 mcg/mL) of Sm-AuNPs,

Inoculation of embryonated chicken embryos

Avian Avula Virus 1, responsible for New Castle disease in chicken was used as an indicator for the demonstration of the effect of Sm-AuNPs on virus multiplication. A constant virus is maintained in the experiment. Infected allantoic fluid having a HA titer of 1:256 was used as a virus source in the study. 50 µL was inoculated in virus control and all the Sm-AuNPs groups. The concentration of Sm-AuNPs was varied from the 5 µL Sm-AuNPs group to 100 µL Sm-AuNPs. The numerical value of the group indicated the volume of the AuNPs taken in µL along with the 50 µL of virus and the rest of the volume is made up of distilled water. In the case of 100 Sm-AuNPs group 100 µL of Sm-AuNPs and 50 µL of infected allantoic fluid were given. All the embryos were inoculated through the allantoic cavity route. After infection, the embryos were incubated at 37 °C and observed after 24 h for non-specific death. Later the embryos were incubated for 4 days and kept in the refrigerator for harvesting. Harvesting of embryos and allantoic fluid was done as per standard procedures.

Histopathology

A detailed dissection was conducted on all the embryos in all the experimental groups. The gross lesions were recorded and representative tissue pieces from liver, and heart were collected and preserved in 10% neutral buffered formalin for histopathological studies. Fixed tissues were processed by routine paraffin embedding technique. Sections of 5–6 microns thickness were cut and were stained with routine Haematoxylin and Eosin method (H&E) (Fischer et al.54.

Haemagglutination test (HA)

The detection of the virus in different experimental groups treated with Sm-AuNPs was performd by HA test. The HA test was carried out according to protocol of Cunningham 196655. The test was performed in ‘U’ shaped micro-titer plates (Laxbro), a serial two-fold dilution of the virus (50 µL) were made in Phosphate buffer saline 50 µL (PBS) starting from 1:2 dilutions. An equal volume of 1% chicken RBC was added to each well and the plates were shaken gently to enable the mixing of the virus and RBC. The plates were kept at room temperature for 45 min. At the same time appropriate positive and negative controls were included in the test. The reciprocal of the highest virus dilution showing mat formation was taken as the HA titre, the experiment was conducted as per the program sheet.

Results and discussion

UV- Visible spectral analysis

UV-Visible spectroscopy is the first and foremost technique to detect the probable uniqueness of the green fabricated gold nanoparticles (AuNPs). In the present study, the light and pale brownish color of bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni, was immediately changed to dark purple blue color after adding of 0.01 M gold chloride solution shown in (Fig. 1.(a-c)). The result reveals that the bioactive components of bark extract are actively involved in the reduction of 0.01 M gold chloride to gold nanoparticles (Sm-AuNPs).

The surface Plasmon resonance (SPR) of gold nanoparticles is between 450 nm and 700 nm, which is due to the absorption of light in the blue-green portion of the spectrum (~ 450 nm) while red light (~ 700 nm) was noted. The SPR is a unique phenomenon to detect noble metals and their properties. The green synthesized Sm-AuNPs reveals SPR band at 554 nm shown in (Fig. 2). The figure shows UV visible spectra of Sm-bark extract and green synthesized Sm-AuNPs. The AuNPs synthesis by using aqueous extracts of root, stem and leaf of Capsicum chinense also reveals similar type of SPR results, especially the AuNPs synthesized by root extract reveals single absorption curve at 552 nm, which possibly corresponding to spherical nanoparticles8. Likewise, the bark extract of other plants like Guazuma ulmifolia (Bay cedar) (5) Salix (Willow tree)6 and Acacia nilotica (Gum Arabic tree)7 Green synthesis of gold nanoparticles by Bio-waste of Solanum tuberosum18 also reveal the same results in UV visible spectroscopy53. There are no earlier reports on synthesis of AuNPs by using bark extract of Swietenia mahagoni so far.

FT-IR analysis of green synthesized Sm-AuNPs

FT-IR spectral data of Swietenia mahagoni bark extract and Sm-AuNPs reveals that various bioactive molecules of different functional groups are responsible for bio-reduction and stabilization of Sm-AuNPs were shown in (Fig. 3. a-b). The FT-IR spectra of bark extract showing the following functional peaks; 3880.32 cm− 1, 3380.97 cm− 1, 1615.54 cm− 1, 1522.93 cm− 1, 1445.52 cm− 1, 1274.70 cm− 1, 1113.85 cm− 1 and 630.06 cm− 1 were observed and FTIR spectra of biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs revealed the following functional groups viz., 3339.71 cm− 1, 1636.58 cm− 1, 1025.62 cm− 1 and 633.89 cm− 1. The various functional groups of bioactive molecules, which are involved in bio-reduction of gold chloride to gold nanoparticles, can be identified. During the reaction with plant bark extract plays a role of reducing as well as stabilizing agent of Sm-AuNPs. The functional groups in the plant extract of Swietenia mahagoni at 3380.97 cm− 1 corresponds to O-H stretching of vibrations, indicating the existence of alcohols and phenols, while 1615.54 cm− 1 due to the stretching of C-N and C-C, indicating the presence of protein. While 1522.93 cm− 1, 1445.52 cm− 1 belong to typical stretching of N = O, which corresponds to nitro compounds, 1274.70 cm− 1, 1113.85 cm− 1 corresponds to C-N vibration stretches of amines, which belongs to protein and 630.06 cm− 1 belongs to C-H bending and C = C-H stretching vibrations of amides.

Particle size and zeta potential of biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs

The particle size and zeta potential of the biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs was measured using dynamic light scattering (DLS) method to determine the size distribution and electrical charge present on the surface of Sm-AuNPs. The results revealed that the size distribution of Sm-AuNPs was between 22.6 nm and 64.7 nm with an average mean size of 47.4 nm (Fig. 4a) with an Z average size of 41.2 nm with a poly disperse index (PI) of 0.310, indicating that Sm-AuNPs are poly dispersed in nature. The biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs reveals high negative zeta potential of -62.5 mV (Fig. 4b), it indicates the gold nanoparticles are well dispersed in colloidal solution. The biosynthesized Sm-AuNPs was very stable, due to the presence of similar charges on the surface of the nanoparticles will resist the agglomeration gold nanoparticles in the colloidal solution. But, when the zeta potential of nanoparticles is lower, the attraction between metal nanoparticles exceeds repulsion and promotes aggregation of the nanoparticles. The size and zeta potential of gold nanoparticles using other plants also exhibit similar type of results48,49. It is concluded that the Sm-AuNPs are highly stable and were well dispersed in the colloidal solution.

Apoptosis assay by dual fluorescence assay

The Apoptosis studies by Sm-AuNPs further confirmed the mechanism of cell death using the treatments at their IC50 concentrations. The dual acridine orange / ethidium bromide (AO/EB) assay allows for the sensitive fluorescent detection of live, early and late apoptotic cells and necrotic cells. The AO stains live cells by intercalating with the cellular DNA to emit a green fluorescence, whereas EB stains dead cells resulting in the emission of a yellow-to-red fluorescence by depending on the stage of apoptosis65–68. The results revealed the apoptotic nature of Sm-AgNPs by fluorescent staining when compared with treated SKOV3 cells to untreated cells, they show a greater number of early apoptotic cells and late apoptotic cells and necrotic cells with chromatin condensation and blebbing of cell membrane in the human ovarian cancer cells was very clearly observed (Fig. 10). The final results were similar to that of standard drug Camptothecin treated cells indicating that Sm-AuNPs have very good anticancer activity. Similarly, the gold nanoparticles reveal cytotoxicity in the human cancer cell lines, HeLa, MCF-7, and the non-cancer HEK293 highlight the potential target by dual-drug AuNP nanocomplexes in providing safe and efficient delivery of anticancer drugs with reduced side effects in vitro65. Likewise, the gold nanoparticles synthesized by plant extracts also reveal similar type of results in dual fluorescence assay in osteosarcoma cells and cervical cancer cells (HeLa)69,70. In conclusion to the study, the Sm-AuNPs might have induced apoptosis by oxidative stress-mediated pathway. Further studies are needed to be carried out to expose the exact mechanism of action.

Anti-viral activity of Sm-AuNPs against NDV by Haemagglutination assay

The pattern of haemagglutination revealed a reduction in the virus multiplication as the concentration of the Sm-AuNPs increases as observed in (Fig. 13a). A log titre of 1:4096 was observed in virus control and no agglutination in normal and Sm-AuNPs controls. In case of different Sm-AuNPs treatments 5 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:2048, 10 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:1024, 15 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:512, 20 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:512, 25 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:512, 50 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:512 and 100 µL/mL Sm-AuNPs showed 1:256 titre (Fig.13b). The result clearly reveals that the Sm-AuNPs have very good antiviral activity against NDV. Similarly, there few other reports on antiviral activity using different nanoparticles on NDV and other important animal viruses. Earlier studies on gold nanoparticles reveal that they can be an excellent adjuvant when conjugated with current FMDV vaccines which activates signaling pathway and enhance the production of cytokines and specific cytotoxic T cells71, so that it can control the virus. Another important study reveals that chitosan nanoparticles coupled with vaccines to form chitosan-adjuvanted vaccines which provide elevated safety, stability, and efficacy. The nanoparticles elicited robust cellular and mucosal immune responses which protected the chickens against the virulent NDV and IBV72.

Summary and conclusion

In the current study, it is concluded that Sm-AuNPs reveals a SPR peak at 554 nm indicating reduction of gold chloride to gold nanoparticles. The FT-IR revealed that different bioactive compounds of bark extract were involved in reduction and stabilization of Sm-AuNPs. TEM and EDX analysis of Sm-AuNPs reveals that the nanoparticles spherical in shape and EDX analysis reveals complete reduction of gold chloride to elemental gold nanoparticles. The particle size analysis by DLS reveals that the Sm-AuNPs are poly-dispersed and PI index about 0.310, with high negative zeta potential value of -62.5 mV confirms the high stability of Sm-AuNPs in colloidal form. The green synthesized Sm-AuNPs reveals excellent antioxidant, antibacterial, anticancer activities and anti-viral properties. The Sm-AuNPs have excellent antibacterial activity when compared with standard antibiotic and consist of remarkable antioxidant activity. The Sm-AuNPs also reveals excellent anticancer activity on ovarian cancer cells with an IC50 value was 64.19 µg/mL. The apoptotic assay carried out by Sm-AuNPs reveals, the nanoparticles induce apoptosis and the apoptotic cells were detected by Dual fluorescence. The Sm-AuNPs also reveals good antiviral efficacy on New Castle Disease (NDV), the experiment was carried out in embryonated chicken eggs. In conclusion the green synthesized Sm-AuNPs can useful as resourceful therapeutic agents to against cancer and NDV effectively.

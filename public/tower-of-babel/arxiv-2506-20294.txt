Ctrl-Z Sampling: Diffusion Sampling with Controlled Random Zigzag Explorations
Shunqi Mao, Wei Guo, Chaoyi Zhang, Weidong Cai

Abstract
Diffusion models have shown strong performance in conditional generation by progressively denoising Gaussian noise toward a target data distribution. This denoising process can be interpreted as a form of hill climbing in a learned latent space, where the model iteratively refines the sample toward regions of higher probability. However, diffusion models often converge to local optima that are locally visually coherent yet globally inconsistent or conditionally misaligned, due to latent space complexity and suboptimal initialization. Prior efforts attempted to address this by strengthening guidance signals or manipulating the initial noise distribution. We introduce Controlled Random Zigzag Sampling ( 𝙲𝚝𝚛𝚕 \mathtt{Ctrl} - 𝚉 \mathtt{Z} Sampling), a novel sampling strategy designed to detect and escape such local maxima during conditional generation. The method first identifies potential local maxima using a reward model. Upon detection, it injects noise and reverts to a previous, noisier state to escape the current optimization plateau. The reward model then evaluates candidate trajectories, accepting only those that offer improvement, while progressively deeper retreat enables stronger escapes when nearby alternatives fail. This controlled random zigzag process allows dynamic alternation between forward refinement and backward exploration, enhancing both alignment and visual quality in the generated outputs. The proposed 𝙲𝚝𝚛𝚕 \mathtt{Ctrl} - 𝚉 \mathtt{Z} Sampling is model-agnostic and compatible with existing diffusion frameworks. Experimental results show that 𝙲𝚝𝚛𝚕 \mathtt{Ctrl} - 𝚉 \mathtt{Z} Sampling substantially improves generation quality with only around 7.6× increase in function evaluations.



Introduction

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful class of generative models, achieving state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of generative tasks, including image
(
Rombach et al. 2022
;
Podell et al. 2023
)
, video
(
Ho et al. 2022
;
Blattmann et al. 2023
)
, text
(

)
, audio
(
Kong et al. 2021
)
, 3D object
(
Poole et al. 2023
)
, and multimodal content generation
(

)
. These models operate by gradually transforming Gaussian noise into data samples through an iterative denoising process
(
Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015
;
Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020
;
Song, Meng, and Ermon 2021
)
. In addition, modern variants are typically designed to accept conditional inputs, enabling guided generation based on class labels, texts, or other structured signals
(
Zhang, Rao, and Agrawala 2023
;
Bansal et al. 2023
)
.

Despite their impressive generative capabilities, diffusion models often exhibit semantic misalignment and global inconsistency in conditional generation. While expected to produce outputs aligned with input conditions, they may converge to local optima that favor local visual plausibility at the expense of semantic relevance or structural coherence. This typically occurs when the initial noise poorly aligns with the target, causing the denoising trajectory to drift toward outputs that are visually compelling but semantically irrelevant or globally inconsistent.

Some existing works attempt to escape local maxima in the diffusion process by reinforcing conditional information—either through classifier-free guidance (CFG)
(
Ho and Salimans 2022
)
, which amplifies the conditioning signal during denoising, or through repeated conditional denoising and unconditional inversion
(
Lugmayr et al. 2022
;
Bai et al. 2024
)
, which explicitly trade off visual plausibility to explore trajectories more faithful to the input. While these methods enhance the influence of input conditions, the way conditional information is injected remains limited and loosely controlled, often leading to suboptimal or ineffective guidance during generation.
Similarly, recent test-time scaling approaches propose mutating the current latent state or stepping backward to a previous timestep
(
Ma et al. 2025
;
He et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2025
)
, introducing stochasticity to escape local maxima under the guidance of reward models. Although these methods can identify better-aligned outputs in principle, they often require evaluating a large number of candidate states to locate promising alternatives. This renders them less practical in local inference settings, and the fixed extent of mutation or rollback may still leave the model trapped when the maxima are broad or steep.

In this paper, we propose Controlled Random Zigzag Sampling (
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling), a novel diffusion sampling strategy that enhances diffusion generation by performing backward searches through randomized explorations into adaptively noisier latent manifolds. This enables the model to escape even broad local maxima through flexible control of escape strength, governed by the degree of noise inversion. Specifically,
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling detects potential local maxima based on stagnation in prediction scores, as assessed by a reward model. Upon detection, it injects noise and reverts to an earlier, noisier timestep to encourage exploration beyond the current optimization plateau. The reward model then evaluates each candidate state, accepting only those that yield more promising future predictions. When no improvement is found nearby, the model continues retreating to deeper noise levels, increasing the likelihood of escape. This process forms a controlled random zigzag trajectory that dynamically alternates between forward refinement and backward exploration, ultimately improving both condition alignment and overall generation quality.

\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling is broadly applicable to a range of diffusion models. Experimental results on text-to-image benchmarks show that
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling achieves superior generation quality across multiple metrics, with only 7.6
\times
more NFEs compared to standard sampling. This demonstrates its potential as a practical alternative to expensive test-time scaling methods, particularly in local inference settings.

We demonstrate that conditional diffusion generation can be interpreted as a hill-climbing process, and show that existing sampling strategies may become trapped in broad, visually plausible local maxima that exhibit conditional misalignment or global inconsistency, due to limited escape strength.

•

We propose Controlled Random Zigzag Sampling (
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling), a novel diffusion sampling strategy that enables controlled escape from local maxima through reward-guided random exploration. By perturbing the current latent state with adaptively increasing noise levels,
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling dynamically balances backward exploration and forward refinement, allowing the model to overcome both narrow and broad misalignment traps during conditional generation.

•

We conduct extensive experiments demonstrating that
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling significantly improves text-to-image generation quality, while incurring only moderate inference overhead.

Preliminaries

This section introduces the backgrounds of diffusion sampling process.

Diffusion Process.

Diffusion models are generative frameworks that synthesize data by progressively denoising a sample drawn from a Gaussian prior. Let
\mathcal{N}
represent the standard normal distribution and
\mathcal{D}
denote the target data distribution. Starting from a noise sample
x_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}
, a sample
x_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
is generated through a sequence of
T
denoising steps, conditioned on
x_{T}
and auxiliary prompt
c
:

x_{0}=\Phi(x_{T},c),

(1)

where
\Phi
denotes the overall generative process.

Deterministic Sampling with DDIM.

We adopt the DDIM framework
(
Song, Meng, and Ermon 2021
)
, which enables efficient and deterministic generation by removing stochasticity from the denoising process. Given a predefined noise schedule
{\beta_{t}}{t=1}^{T}
, let
\alpha_{t}=\prod{i=1}^{t}(1-\beta_{i})
denote the cumulative product. The generation process is decomposed into
T
successive mappings:

x_{0}=\Phi^{1}\circ\Phi^{2}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi^{T}(x_{T},c),

(2)

where each mapping
\Phi^{t}
transforms
x_{t}
into
x_{t-1}
using the predicted noise
\epsilon_{\theta}^{t}(x_{t},c)
:

\displaystyle x_{t-1}=\Phi^{t}(x_{t},c)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha_{t-1}}\cdot\frac{x_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\cdot\epsilon_{\theta}^{t}(x_{t},c)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}

(3)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t-1}}\cdot\epsilon_{\theta}^{t}(x_{t},c).

The first term in Eq. (
3
) approximates the clean image corresponding to
x_{t}
, which we denote as:

\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t-1}(x_{t},c)=\frac{x_{t}-\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\cdot\epsilon_{\theta}^{t}(x_{t},c)}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}.

(4)

This intermediate estimate serves as a useful proxy for the final output and is used in our method to assess semantic alignment via a reward model. Although
\Phi^{t}
formally returns only
x_{t-1}
, we compute and reuse
\hat{x}_{0}^{,t}
jointly in implementation for efficiency.

Latent Inversion.

While DDIM defines a deterministic denoising trajectory from
x_{T}
to
x_{0}
, it does not include an explicit inversion mechanism. To enable controlled reintroduction of noise, we propose an approximate inversion operator
\Psi(x_{t},\Delta)
, which simulates a forward transition from
x_{t}
to a noisier latent state
x_{t+\Delta}
using:

x_{t+\Delta}=\Psi(x_{t},\Delta)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{t+\Delta}}{\alpha_{t}}}\cdot x_{t}+\sqrt{1-\frac{\alpha_{t+\Delta}}{\alpha_{t}}}\cdot\epsilon,

(5)

where
\alpha_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-\beta_{i})
is the cumulative noise schedule inherited from the original diffusion process, and
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})
a random noise.

This operator enables transitions into higher-noise regions of the latent space without restarting from pure Gaussian noise. In our setting, such inversion serves as a test-time exploration mechanism: when generation stagnates at locally optimal outputs, applying
\Psi(x_{t},\Delta)
perturbs the trajectory, allowing re-denoising to potentially discover more aligned or diverse outputs.

Diffusion As A Hill Climbing Problem.

As shown in
Figure

1
, diffusion sampling can be viewed as a hill-climbing process in latent space, where each denoising step aims to improve generation quality by moving toward higher reward regions. However, it may become trapped in local maxima that produce outputs which are locally plausible but globally inconsistent or conditionally misaligned.

In the illustrated example, Resampling performs a single-step random inversion to perturb the latent state and explore nearby alternatives, but such exploration is limited in scope and may fail to yield meaningful improvements. Zigzag Sampling takes a backward step along the direction of unconditional generation, alternating between conditional and unconditional denoising to better inject conditioning signals. Search-over-path extends Resampling by evaluating multiple perturbed candidates, but its search depth and noise levels are fixed, limiting its ability to escape broader local maxima. In contrast, the proposed Controlled Random Zigzag Sampling (
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling) adaptively adjusts the strength of latent perturbation based on reward feedback. When shallow exploration fails,
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling incrementally expands its search radius, enabling escape from both narrow and broad local maxima while preserving output quality.

Exploration with Inversion.

To enable conditional diffusion models to escape local optimal, we propose a reward-guided sampling strategy that incorporates controlled backward exploration. Our approach detects potential suboptimal states using a reward model, then dynamically perturbs the latent trajectory through guided inversion steps of increasing strength. This process forms a zigzag trajectory in the latent space, alternating between conditional refinement and noise-space exploration.

Detecting Local Maxima and Triggering Exploration.

At each selected step
t
, the next step prediction and clean image estimate
\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t}
is computed from the current latent
x_{t}
using Eq. (
4
), and its quality is assessed by a reward model
R(c,\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t})
that measures alignment with the input condition
c
. A local maximum is detected when the reward falls short of the previous best by a threshold:

R(c,\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t})<r_{\text{prev}}+\delta,

(6)

where
\delta
is the acceptance threshold. By default,
\delta=0
requires non-decreasing reward. Intuitively, negative values
\delta
allow minor regressions, while positive values enforce stricter improvement. This criterion provides a simple yet effective signal for identifying local maxima during early sampling.

Empirically, at early timesteps such as
t=600/1000
, the DDIM update equation already assigns a large weight (above 0.02) to the clean image estimate
\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t}
following Eq. (
3
). As a result, the next latent
x_{t-1}
is strongly determined by
\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t}
, meaning that much of the low-frequency structure of the final image is already established. From a hill-climbing perspective, this corresponds to the model having committed to a particular generation trajectory, making it difficult to escape suboptimal modes through later perturbations. Therefore,
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling is applied only during the first
\lambda<T
denoising steps, where exploratory perturbations can more effectively alter the generation path.

Controlled Latent Inversion.

To escape from a detected local maximum, we apply the inversion operator
\Psi(x_{t},\Delta;\epsilon)
(defined in Eq. (
5
)) to perturb the current latent
x_{t}
toward a higher-noise state
x_{t+\Delta}
. Here,
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the inversion step size and is aligned with the discrete timestep indices in the DDIM sampling schedule. Small values of
\Delta
induce local perturbations, while larger values enable stronger exploration. This controlled reintroduction of noise acts as a test-time search mechanism, allowing the model to re-enter higher-noise regions without restarting from pure Gaussian noise, thereby recovering from suboptimal deterministic trajectories.

Candidate Selection and Adaptive Inversion Step.

After applying the inversion, the model resumes forward denoising from
x_{t+\Delta}
to
x_{t}
using standard conditional DDIM steps
\Phi^{k}(\cdot,c)
for
k=t+\Delta,\dots,t
. The resulting candidate
\hat{x}_{0}^{\,t+1}
is then evaluated by the reward model. At each iteration, we generate
N
candidates using different random noise vectors
\epsilon
, and retain the one with the highest reward score. If this score exceeds the most recently accepted reward by at least a threshold
\delta
, the update is accepted.

Otherwise, the inversion step size
\Delta
is incrementally increased (e.g., from 1 to
d_{\max}
), allowing progressively deeper noise perturbations in search of a better path. This constitutes a greedy yet adaptive hill-climbing procedure: exploration continues until an improved candidate is found or the maximum depth
d_{\max}
is reached.
If no improvement beyond the threshold is detected, the best candidate from the search is retained, ensuring stability without regress and often yielding results comparable to or slightly better than the default forward step.

By default, the search depth is unbounded, and the total candidate budget
N
per inversion step guarantees termination. In practice, we adopt a batched search strategy: rewards are computed for the first
N_{b}
candidates before deciding whether to accept a candidate or continue the search, up to the full budget
N
. This allows for efficient evaluation while increasing the likelihood of discovering non-trivial improvements, especially when
N_{b}
is large.

The threshold
\delta
regulates the minimal reward improvement required to accept a candidate. When
\delta>0
, trivial improvements are filtered out, enabling robust selection even with smaller
N_{b}
. In contrast,
\delta\leq 0
allows non-strict updates, including candidates with marginal or even lower rewards. In these cases, a small
N_{b}
increases the risk of selecting suboptimal candidates that marginally satisfy the reward criterion but produce degraded outputs, such as noise artifacts. A larger
N_{b}
mitigates this risk by enabling more reliable selection among alternatives.

Ablations

The following sections include ablation experiments on the effectiveness of various design and hyper-parameters used in
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling. For consistency, efficiency, and fairness, we set
\delta=0
for all ablations if not specified.

Effect of Max Inversion Steps and Accept Threshold.

This section examines the impact of varying the maximum number of inversion steps (
d_{\text{max}}
) and the accept threshold (
\delta
), with results shown in
Figure

2
. We observe that increasing either parameter consistently improves generation quality across most metrics, indicating that deeper and more permissive exploration enhances the effectiveness of zigzag sampling. This reflects the robustness of our approach, as exploration quality scales smoothly with increased allowance for backward steps and candidate diversity—consistent with recent findings on test-time scaling in diffusion models
(
Ma et al. 2025
)
. An exception is AES, which shows less consistent improvement, likely due to its emphasis on aesthetic quality rather than conditional alignment.

We also find that very deep rollbacks occur infrequently and introduce only moderate increase in NFEs. However, these rare cases often correspond to severe conditional misalignment, and escaping them yields substantial gains. Based on this observation, we adopt an unconstrained
d_{\text{max}}=\infty
as the default setting.
Overall,
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling offers flexible control over the trade-off between generation quality and computational cost, adapting effectively to different exploration depths and acceptance criteria.

Choice of Reward Model.

We evaluate the impact of different reward models, with results shown in
Table

2
. All experiments use
N_{b}=1
to reduce inference cost, which does not affect relative comparisons. It can be observed that
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling consistently improves generation quality across all four metrics, regardless of the reward model used, demonstrating robustness to different guidance objectives.

In particular, using AES as the reward model enhances visual quality. However, since AES does not evaluate conditioning alignment, its impact on alignment-sensitive metrics is limited. Among the CLIP-based models, it is observed that PickScore performs better than HPSv2 when used to guide generation. Overall, both PickScore and ImageReward lead to stronger improvements than the other two models.

We ultimately adopt ImageReward for the main experiments due to its broader applicability and to avoid domain bias, as PickScore is trained on data closely related to the Pick-a-Pic benchmark. While prior work
(
Ma et al. 2025
)
suggests that combining multiple reward models can further improve performance, this direction is beyond the scope of our study.

When to Explore.

This section evaluates when to initiate zigzag exploration. We compare the proposed reward-based initiation strategy, which triggers exploration upon detecting a reward plateau, to two baselines: initiating exploration at every denoising step (Always) and randomly with a probability
p=0.5
(Random). All configurations use
\lambda=15
to restrict exploration to early timesteps for efficiency.

As shown in
Table

2
, the Always strategy achieves the highest generation quality but incurs substantial computational overhead, averaging 25.8× NFEs. The Reward-Based method achieves comparable performance with significantly fewer evaluations (8.6× NFEs), offering a favorable trade-off between quality and efficiency. Meanwhile, the Random strategy with
p=0.5
provides modest quality gains with low overhead (3.2× NFEs), enabling flexible control over compute cost.

Each strategy presents a distinct trade-off: Always delivers the best quality, Random enables lightweight improvements, and the Reward-Based method strikes a strong balance with adaptive, targeted exploration. Notably, all three strategies are effective when combined with our
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling framework, and can be chosen based on the desired balance between quality and inference cost.

Conclusion

We presented
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling, a reward-guided inference-time sampling strategy for diffusion models that improves generation quality by escaping local optima through adaptive noise injection and backward exploration. Experiments on text-to-image benchmarks demonstrate that
\mathtt{Ctrl}
-
\mathtt{Z}
Sampling enhances both alignment and visual fidelity with moderate computational overhead. Future work includes exploring its scalability in broader test-time search settings and designing unified scheduling strategies that adapt both the initiation criteria and exploration strength across diverse reward models.

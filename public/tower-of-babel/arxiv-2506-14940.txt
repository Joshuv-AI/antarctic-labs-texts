Notation
Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the ring of integers,
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
the set of non-negative integers. For a real number
r
we denote by
[r]
the maximal integer
k
such that
k\leq r
.

If otherwise is not explicitely stated,
G
denotes a simple (non-abelian) linear algebraic group defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. Then
G
is of type
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
D_{n}
,
E
,
n=6,7,8
,
F_{4}
or
G_{2}
. For brevity, we write
G=A_{n}
to say that
G
is of type
A_{n}
, etc.

We fix a maximal torus
T
of
G
. The set of rational homorphisms
T\rightarrow F^{\times}
is denoted by
\Omega(G)
, and the elements
\omega\in\Omega(G)
are called weights of
G
. The conjugation action of
T
on
G
determines the
T
-weights of
G
, which are called roots (or
T
-roots) of
G
. As maximal tori of
G
are conjugate, the choice of a maximal torus is immaterial. The set
\Omega(G)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice of finite rank, which is called the rank
n
of
G
. The set of roots is denoted by
\Phi(G)
, and
\Phi(G)\subset\Omega(G)
. One chooses a subset
\Pi(G)=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
of
\Phi(G)
whose elements are called simple roots. We order simple roots as in
[
3
]
. These determine a basis of
\Omega(G)
which elements are called fundamental weights and denoted by
\omega_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Every weight therefore is a linear combination
\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For brevity, we write weights as strings
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Weights

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with non-negative
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are called dominant; the subset of dominant weights in
\Omega(G)
is denoted by
\Omega^{+}(G)
.
(We often omit
G
if this is clear from the context.)
If
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega(G)
then we write
\omega\succ\omega^{\prime}
or
\omega^{\prime}\prec\omega
if
\omega^{\prime}=\omega-\sum b_{i}\alpha_{i}
for distinct
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi(G)
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. If
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
are dominant and
\omega\succ\omega^{\prime}
then we say that
\omega^{\prime}
is a subdominant weight for
\omega
.

A semisimple subgroup
H
of
G
normalized by
T
is called a subsystem subgroup (with respect of
T
). Then
H
is determined by a certain subset
S
, called the subsystem base,
of
\Phi(G)
which forms a set of simple roots of
H
, see
[
16
, Section 13.1]
. We frequently deal with special cases of subsystem subgroups
X=X_{S}
defined by a subset
S\subset\Pi(G)
. These are generated by the root subgroups
X_{\pm\alpha}
with
\alpha\in S
.
In these cases, by technical reason, it is convenient to define
S
as a string
(*...-...*)
of length
|\Pi(G)|

writing
-
at the places that correspond to subset
\Pi\smallsetminus S
of
\Pi
. For instance, if
G=C_{3}
then
(-**)
defines a subsystem subgroup of type
C_{2}
and
(**-)
defines the subsystem subgroup of type
A_{2}
. (Of course, to decide on the type of
X
one has to keep in mind the Dynkin diagram and root ordering.)

If
\lambda\in\Omega^{+}(G)
we write
V_{\lambda}
for an irreducible
G
-module with highest weight
\lambda
. If
\mu
is a weight of
V_{\lambda}
then
m_{\lambda}(\mu)

is the multiplicity of
\mu
in
V_{\lambda}
. If
V
is an irreducible
G
-module then we write
V=V_{\lambda}
to say that
\lambda
is the highest weight of
V
. If
\mu\in\Omega^{+}(G)
and
\mu\prec\lambda
then
\mu
is a weight of
V_{\lambda}
; below we use this fact with no reference.

We denote by
\Omega_{k}(G)
the set of dominant weights
\lambda
such that
V_{\lambda}(G)
has no dominant weight of multiplicity greater than
k
, and set
\Omega^{\prime}_{k}(G)=\Omega_{k}(G)\smallsetminus\Omega_{k-1}(G)
for
k>1
. Thus, Table 1 lists
\Omega_{2}^{\prime}(G)
.The weight multipicities of certain small representations of
G
are given in
[
14
]
and
[
4
]
.

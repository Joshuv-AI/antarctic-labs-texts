Binomial edge rings of complete bipartite graphs
Akihiro Higashitani

Abstract
. We introduce a new class of algebras arising from graphs, called binomial edge rings. Given a graph G G on d d vertices with n n edges, the binomial edge ring of G G is defined to be the subalgebra of the polynomial ring with 2 ​ d 2d variables generated by the binomials which correspond to n n edges. In this paper, we calculate a SAGBI basis for this algebra and obtain an initial algebra associated with this SAGBI basis in the case of complete bipartite graphs. It turns out that such an initial algebra is isomorphic to the Hibi ring of a certain poset. Similar phenomenon also occurs in the context of Plücker algebras, so the framework of binomial edge rings can be interpreted as a kind of its generalization.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
{\Bbbk}
be a field.
All graphs appearing in this paper are always assumed to be finite, simple and connected.

1.1.
Ideals and algebras arising from graphs

One of the main topics in the theory of combinatorial commutative algebra
is the study of ideals/algebras arising from combinatorial objects, such as graphs, posets, simplicial complexes, matroids, and so on.
In particular, there are several kinds of ideals/algebras arising from graphs.
We briefly introduce some of them below.

Let
G
be a graph on the vertex
V(G)=[d]:=\{1,\ldots,d\}
with the edge set
E(G)
.
Let

R={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\;\text{ and }\;S={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}]

be the polynomial rings in
d
and
2d
variables over
{\Bbbk}
, respectively.

•

The
edge ideal
of
G
is defined to be the ideal

(x_{i}x_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subset R

generated by quadratic monomials corresponding to the edges of
G
.
As far as the author knows, the study of edge ideals seems initiated in
[
SVV94
]
.
Nowadays, there are a huge number of studies on edge ideals of graphs.
For an introduction to edge ideals and some fundamental results on them, see, e.g.,
[
HH11
, Section 9]
and
[
Vil15
, Section 7]
.

•

The
edge ring
of
G
is defined to be the subalgebra

{\Bbbk}[x_{i}x_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G)]\subset R

generated by quadratic monomials corresponding to the edges of
G
.
The intensive investigation of this
{\Bbbk}
-algebra has begun after the works
[
OH98
]
and
[
SVV98
]
.
There are also numerous studies on edge rings and their defining ideals, called the
toric ideals of
G
.
See, e.g.,
[
HHO18
, Section 5]
and
[
Vil15
, Sections 10 and 11]
for their introduction.

•

The
binomial edge ideal
of
G
is defined to be the ideal

(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}:\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subset S

generated by quadratic binomials corresponding to the edges of
G
.
This ideal has been independently introduced by
[
HHH+10
]
and
[
Oht13
]
.
There are also a large number of studies on binomial edge ideals. See, e.g.,
[
HHO18
, Section 7]
for their introduction.

Following these trends, in this paper, we introduce a new class of
{\Bbbk}
-algebras arising from
G
. Let

{\mathcal{R}}(G):={\Bbbk}[x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}:\{i,j\}\in E(G)]\subset S.

We call this subalgebra of
S
the
binomial edge ring
of
G
.

Given
1\leq i<j\leq d
, we employ the following notation:

f_{ij}:=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}\in S.

Namely,
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
is the subalgebra of
S
generated by
\{f_{ij}:\{i,j\}\in E(G)\}
.

1.2.
Binomial edge rings of graphs and Plücker algebras

To explain other motivation of introducing binomial edge rings of graphs, let us briefly recall the fundamental facts on Plücker algebras.

Let
1\leq k<d
and let
X=(x_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq d}
be a
k\times d
-matrix of indeterminates.
Given
I\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,d}:=\{I\subset[d]:|I|=k\}
, let
X_{I}
denote the
k\times k
-submatrix of
X
whose columns are indexed by
I
.
We define the subalgebra of the polynomial ring
{\Bbbk}[x_{ij}:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq d]
in
kd
variables as follows:

{\mathcal{A}}_{k,d}:={\Bbbk}[\det(X_{I}):I\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,d}]\subset{\Bbbk}[x_{ij}:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j\leq d].

The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{k,d}
is called the
Plücker algebra
.
This is well known as a homogeneous coordinate ring of the Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}_{\Bbbk}(k,d)
, the set of
k
-subspaces of
{\Bbbk}^{d}
.
Here, we notice that
{\mathcal{A}}_{2,d}
coincides with the binomial edge ring of the complete graph
K_{d}
.
Hence, we can regard
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
as a kind of generalization of the Plücker algebra.

For the introduction to SAGBI basis and initial algebra, see Subsection
2.1
.
It is known that
\{\det(X_{I}):I\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,d}\}
forms a SAGBI basis for
{\mathcal{A}}_{k,d}
with respect to a certain monomial order.
Moreover, the associated initial algebra is isomorphic to a Hibi ring (see Subsection
2.2
) of a certain poset.
See, e.g.,
[
MS05
]
and
[
Stu96
]
.

On the other hand, whether
\{\det(X_{I}):I\in{\mathbf{I}}_{k,d}\}
forms a SAGBI basis or not strongly depends on a choice of monomial orders.
For example, computational experiments on this are performed in
[
BC24
]
.

Therefore, in the context of binomial edge rings, it is natural to think of a SAGBI basis of
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
and to discuss the structure of the associated initial algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to introduce the notions of SAGBI basis and Hibi rings. Those play the central role in this paper.

2.1.
SAGBI basis of a subalgebra

First, we recall SAGBI basis from
[
Stu96
]
.
(Remark that
canonical basis
is used in
[
Stu96
]
instead of SAGBI basis, but those are the same things.)
Fix a set of polynomials
{\mathcal{F}}=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
of
R
and a monomial order
<
on
R
.
Consider the subalgebra
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
of
R
generated by the polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
.
The
initial algebra

\operatorname{in}_{<}({\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}])
is the algebra generated by the initial monomials
\operatorname{in}_{<}(g)
for
g\in{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
.
Note that
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
is not necessarily a finitely generated
{\Bbbk}
-algebra even if
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
is finitely generated (see
[
RS90
]
).
We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
SAGBI basis
of
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
with respect to
<

if the initial algebra
\operatorname{in}_{<}({\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}])
coincides with the subalgebra
{\Bbbk}[\operatorname{in}_{<}({\mathcal{F}})]
generated by the initial monomials
\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{n})
.
The word “SAGBI” is introduced by Robbiano and Sweedler
[
RS90
]
and means “Subalgebra Analog to Gröbner Bases for Ideals”.

We discuss when
{\mathcal{F}}
becomes a SAGBI basis of
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
in terms of the associated toric ideal.
For
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=(a_{1i},\ldots,a_{di})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d}
be such that
\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{i})={\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}=x_{1}^{a_{1i}}\cdots x_{d}^{a_{di}}
and
let
A
be the
d\times n
-matrix whose columns consist of
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
.
Let
I
be the kernel of the following surjective ring homomorphism:

{\Bbbk}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\rightarrow{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}];\;\;z_{i}\mapsto f_{i}.

Similarly, let
I_{A}
be the kernel of the surjective ring homomorphism:

{\Bbbk}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\rightarrow{\Bbbk}[{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{n}}];\;\;z_{i}\mapsto{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}.

Take any weight vector
w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
representing the monomial order
<
for
{\mathcal{F}}
. Namely,
\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{i})=\operatorname{in}_{w}(f_{i})
holds for each
i
.
Then we can use
A^{T}w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as a weight vector for
{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
A^{T}
stands for the transpose of
A
.
Remark that the initial ideal
\operatorname{in}_{A^{T}w}(I)
is usually not a monomial ideal.
We expect this is a binomial ideal as the following theorem claims.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Stu96
, Lemma 11.3 and Theorem 11.4]
)
.

For any finite set
{\mathcal{F}}\subset R
, the inclusion
\operatorname{in}_{A^{T}w}(I)\subseteq I_{A}
holds.
Moreover, the equality of this inclusion holds if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
forms a SAGBI basis of
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{F}}]
with respect to
<
.

Thanks to the fundamental theory on SAGBI basis, we can prove Corollary
1.3
by using Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

In general, for a finitely generated graded
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
T
and a fixed monomial order
<
,
we know that the Hilbert series of
T
coincides with that of its initial algebra
\operatorname{in}_{<}(T)
(see, e.g.,
[
RS90
]
).
In particular,
\dim T=\dim(\operatorname{in}_{<}(T))
. Since the Krull dimension of the Hibi ring of a poset
\Pi
is equal to
|\Pi|+1
(see Subsection
2.2
below),
we conclude
\dim({\mathcal{R}}(K_{a,b}))=\dim({\Bbbk}[\Pi_{a,b}])=2(a+b-2)
.
Moreover, by
[
HH11
, Corollary 3.3.5]
, it follows that if
\operatorname{in}_{<}({\mathcal{R}}(K_{a,b}))
is Cohen-Macaulay/Gorenstein, then so is
{\mathcal{R}}(K_{a,b})
.
The Gorensteinness of the Hibi ring of
\Pi
is equivalent to that all of the maximal chains in
\Pi
have the same length (
[
Hib87
]
).
∎

2.2.
Hibi rings of a finite poset

Next, we recall Hibi rings.
Let
\Pi=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{d-1}\}
be a finite poset equipped with a partial order
\prec
.
For a subset
I\subset\Pi
, we say that
I
is a
poset ideal
if
I
satisfies that “
x\in I
and
y\prec x
imply
y\in I
”.
We regard the empty set as a poset ideal. Let
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)
be the set of all poset ideals of
\Pi
.
Poset ideals in
\Pi
are one-to-one corresponding to antichains in
\Pi
,
i.e., a subset
A\subset\Pi
satisfying
x\not\prec y
and
y\not\prec x
for any
x,y\in A
.
In fact, for a given antichain
A
, we can associate a poset ideal

\langle p:p\in A\rangle:=\{q\in\Pi:q\preceq p\text{ for some }p\in A\}\subset\Pi.

Conversely, given a poset ideal
I
, we may take the set of all maximal elements of
I
with respect to
\prec
to get the associated antichain.

In what follows, we identify a subset
K\subset\Pi
with a point in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\Pi}(={\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1})
by
\sum_{p_{i}\in K}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
,
where
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
is the
i
-th unit vector of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
and
\emptyset
corresponds to the origin.

We introduce the
{\Bbbk}
-algebra associated to
\Pi
as follows (see
[
Hib87
]
):

\displaystyle{\Bbbk}[\Pi]:={\Bbbk}[(\prod_{p_{i}\in I}x_{i})x_{d}:I\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)]\subset{\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}].

We call
{\Bbbk}[\Pi]
the
Hibi ring
of
\Pi
.
The following facts are known:

•

\dim({\Bbbk}[\Pi])=|\Pi|+1
;

•

{\Bbbk}[\Pi]
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain;

•

{\Bbbk}[\Pi]
is Gorenstein if and only if all of the maximal chains in
\Pi
have the same length.

Let
{\Bbbk}[z_{I}:I\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)]
be a polynomial ring in
|{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)|
variables and consider the surjective ring homomorphism:

{\Bbbk}[z_{I}:I\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)]\rightarrow{\Bbbk}[\Pi];\;\;z_{I}\mapsto(\prod_{p_{i}\in I}x_{i})x_{d}.

We call the kernel of this map the
toric ideal
of
\Pi
, denoted by
I_{\Pi}
.
It is known that
I_{\Pi}
can be described as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle I_{\Pi}=(z_{I}z_{J}-z_{I\cup J}z_{I\cap J}:I,J\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi)).

3.1.
Notation

Let us fix our notation for our specific case
K_{a,b}
.

Let
R_{a,b}
and
S_{a,b}
be the following polynomial rings in
(ab+\binom{a}{2}\binom{b}{2})
and
2(a+b)
variables, respectively:

\displaystyle R_{a,b}

\displaystyle:={\Bbbk}[\{z_{ij}:1\leq i\leq a,\,1\leq j\leq b\}\cup\{z_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j}:1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq a,\,1\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq b\}];

\displaystyle S_{a,b}

\displaystyle:={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{b}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{a}^{\prime},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b}].

We introduce a monomial order on
S_{a,b}
as follows:

(2)

\begin{split}&\text{$<$ is the graded lexicographic order with respect to the ordering of the variables}\\
&x_{1}>\cdots>x_{a}>x_{1}^{\prime}>\cdots>x_{b}^{\prime}>y_{1}^{\prime}>\cdots>y_{a}^{\prime}>y_{1}>\cdots>y_{b}.\end{split}

Note that a corresponding weight vector of
<
can be chosen in the following manner: choose

m_{1}>\cdots>m_{a}>m_{1}^{\prime}>\cdots>m_{b}^{\prime}>n_{1}^{\prime}>\cdots>n_{a}^{\prime}>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{b}>0

satisfying e.g.
m_{1}>m_{2}+\cdots+n_{b}
,
m_{2}>m_{3}+\cdots+n_{b}
, and so on,
and let

(3)

\displaystyle w_{a,b}:=(m_{1},\ldots,n_{b})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2(a+b)}.

Given
1\leq i\leq a
and
1\leq j\leq b
, let

f_{ij}:=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}^{\prime}y_{i}^{\prime}.

Moreover, given
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq a
and
1\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq b
, let

\displaystyle f_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j}\,:=

\displaystyle\,f_{ij^{\prime}}f_{i^{\prime}j}-f_{ij}f_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,x_{i}x_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{j}+x_{i^{\prime}}x_{j}^{\prime}y_{i}^{\prime}y_{j^{\prime}}-x_{i}x_{j}^{\prime}y_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{j^{\prime}}-x_{i^{\prime}}x_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{i}^{\prime}y_{j}.

Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{a,b}=\{f_{ij}:1\leq i\leq a,\,1\leq j\leq b\}\cup\{f_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j}:1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq a,\,1\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq b\}.

Let
I
be the kernel of the following ring homomorphism:

\varphi_{a,b}:R_{a,b}\rightarrow S_{a,b};\;\;\varphi_{a,b}(z_{ij})=f_{ij},\;\;\varphi_{a,b}(z_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j})=f_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j}.

Namely,
{\Bbbk}[{\mathcal{G}}_{a,b}]
is isomorphic to
R_{a,b}/I
.

Let
A_{a,b}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{a+b})^{2}
be defined as follows:

(4)

\begin{split}A_{a,b}:=&\{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{f}}_{j}:1\leq i\leq a,\,1\leq j\leq b\}\\
\cup&\{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{f}}_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{f}}_{j}:1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq a,\,1\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq b\},\end{split}

where
{\mathbf{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{a},{\mathbf{f}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{f}}_{b},{\mathbf{e}}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{a}^{\prime},{\mathbf{f}}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{f}}_{b}^{\prime}
form a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2(a+b)}
. Since

\displaystyle\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{ij})=x_{i}y_{j}\;\text{ and }\;\operatorname{in}_{<}(f_{ii^{\prime}j^{\prime}j})=x_{i}x_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}y_{j}

hold, we can find a one-to-one correspondence between
A_{a,b}
and the initial monomials of
{\mathcal{G}}_{a,b}

by assigning
x_{i}
to
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
,
y_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}
to
{\mathbf{e}}_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}
,
x_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}
to
{\mathbf{f}}_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}
, and
y_{j}
to
{\mathbf{f}}_{j}
, respectively.

Our goal of this section is to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
To this end, we divide the discussions into the following four steps:

(I)

Prove the unimodular equivalence of the associated affine monoid of

and the affine monoid corresponding to the poset ideals of

;

(II)

Compute the toric ideal

of

by using (I);

(III)

Find (a part of) generators of

and define

generated by them;

(IV)

Prove

Then

can be obtained.
(The first inclusion is (IV), the second one follows by definition of

, and the third one comes from Theorem

.

, (I) implies that

.

3.2.
Order ideals of
\Pi_{a,b}
and the initial monomials of
{\mathcal{G}}_{a,b}

The first step (I) is to construct a unimodular transformation from the initial monomials of
{\mathcal{G}}_{a,b}
to the poset ideals of
\Pi_{a,b}
.

Employing the notation as in Figure
1
, we see that
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi_{a,b})
consists of the following poset ideals:

(5)

\begin{split}&\emptyset,\;\langle e_{i}\rangle,\;\langle f_{j}\rangle,\;\langle e_{i},f_{j}\rangle\;\text{ for }\;1\leq i\leq a-1,\;2\leq j\leq b\;\;\text{ and }\\
&\langle e_{i},e_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}\rangle,\;\langle e_{i},e_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime},f_{j}\rangle,\;\langle f_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime},f_{j}\rangle,\;\langle e_{i},f_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime},f_{j}\rangle,\;\langle e_{i},e_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime},f_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime},f_{j}\rangle\\
&\quad\quad\quad\;\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ for }\;1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq a-1,\;1\leq j^{\prime}<j\leq b.\end{split}

We identify those poset ideals with those points in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\Pi_{a,b}}
whose
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis is

{\mathbf{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{a-1},{\mathbf{e}}_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{e}}_{a-1}^{\prime}.{\mathbf{f}}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{f}}_{b-1}^{\prime},{\mathbf{f}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{f}}_{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\Pi_{a,b}}(={\mathbb{Z}}^{2a+2b-5}).

More precisely, given
E\subset\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{a-1}\}
,
E^{\prime}\subset\{e_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,e_{a-1}^{\prime}\}
,
F^{\prime}\subset\{f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,f_{b-1}^{\prime}\}
and
F\subset\{f_{2},\ldots,f_{b}\}
,
we associate
E\cup E^{\prime}\cup F^{\prime}\cup F\subset\Pi_{a,b}
with

In this section, we study some further problems on

One of the most fundamental invariants on commutative rings is its Krull dimension.
Regarding the Krull dimension of
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
, we have the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
d
vertices with
n
edges.

(i)

If
G
is bipartite, then

\dim({\mathcal{R}}(G))\leq\min\{n,2d-4\}.

(ii)

If
G
is non-bipartite, then

\dim({\mathcal{R}}(G))\leq\min\{n,2d-3\}.

Proof.

In general,
G
is always a subgraph of
K_{d}
. This means that
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
is a subring of
{\mathcal{R}}(K_{d})={\mathcal{A}}_{2,d}
.
It is well known that
\dim({\mathcal{A}}_{k,d})=k(d-k)+1
.
(This can be seen, e.g., via that
\dim({\mathcal{A}}_{k,d})
coincides with the Krull dimension of its initial algebra,
which is equal to
k(d-k)+1
. See
[
Stu96
, Chapter 11]
for more details.)
Hence,
\dim({\mathcal{R}}(G))\leq 2(d-2)+1=2d-3
follows.

In the case where
G
is bipartite, there is a positive integer
a
such that
G
is a subgraph of
K_{a,d-a}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
is a subring of
{\mathcal{R}}(K_{a,d-a})
.
Since
\dim({\mathcal{R}}(K_{a,d-a}))=2d-4
by Corollary
1.3
, we obtain
\dim({\mathcal{R}}(G))\leq 2d-4
.

Finally,
\dim({\mathcal{R}}(G))\leq n
follows from that
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
is generated by
n

∎

Now, it is natural to think of when the equality holds. Namely,

When does

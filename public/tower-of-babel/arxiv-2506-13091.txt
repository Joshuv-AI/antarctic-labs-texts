Shadow and strong-field lensing effects of quantum-corrected RN blackhole
Nikko John Leo S. Lobos, Virginia C. Fernandez

Abstract
We investigate the effects of a quantum-corrected RN black hole on the shadow using Perlick methodology and strong-field gravitational lensing using Tsukamoto’s approach. Our analysis shows that even at extreme charge, the predicted shadow remains within the observational bounds set by the Event Horizon Telescope for both M87* and Sgr A*. The charge significantly influences the deflection angle near the photon sphere, while its effect diminishes at larger distances. In contrast, the quantum correction parameter, a \mathrm{a} , exhibits negligible impact at the current observational scale. However, a slight increase in the shadow radius and a corresponding decrease in the strong deflection angle are observed as, a \mathrm{a} , increases. These results underscore the subtle but theoretically consistent role of quantum corrections in black hole observables.

Introduction

Black holes, as described by classical general relativity, contain spacetime singularities where curvature invariants diverge, and predictability breaks down
Hawking and Penrose 1970

Ashtekar et al. 2018

Ali et al. 2009

Konoplya 2020

Recent work by Sakallı and Sucu (2025) extended this paradigm by incorporating Kalb - Ramond (KR) tensor fields which can originate from string theory as sources of Lorentz-violating quantum corrections. Their analysis showed that such modifications significantly alter the location of the photon sphere, the effective potential for photons, and consequently the size and shape of the black hole shadow. Furthermore, the study confirmed that quantum-corrected RN black holes display an enlarged or deformed shadow profile, depending on the nature and strength of the correction terms, offering distinct observational signatures compared to classical black holes

Parallel studies have also demonstrated that strong-field lensing observables, such as deflection angles near the photon sphere and relativistic image separations, are sensitive to quantum-corrected modifications. In particular, Kumar et al. (2020)

Moreover, quantum-corrected thermodynamic quantities such as entropy and specific heat have shown to correlate with optical properties—an area of emerging interest that could bridge quantum gravity with observational tests

In this work, we build upon these advances by systematically analyzing the shadow and strong-field lensing of a quantum-corrected RN black hole. Our results would demonstrate how deviations from classical predictions could be discerned with upcoming observational campaigns, offering a new avenue to test quantum gravity phenomenology.

The photon sphere is a fundamental feature in the study of gravitational lensing, particularly in the context of the strong deflection angle, which quantifies the bending of light in the regime where gravitational effects are dominant. Additionally, it plays a crucial role in determining the black hole shadow, the apparent dark region seen in high-resolution observations of black holes, such as those conducted by the Event Horizon Telescope. The photon sphere directly influences the shadow’s size and shape, making it an essential parameter in testing general relativity and alternative gravity theories.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have examined the influence of quantum corrections on the Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole, focusing on their effects on both the black hole shadow and strong-field gravitational lensing. Our analysis reveals that, even at extremal charge values, the predicted shadow radius remains within the observational bounds reported by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT)—specifically, within the
\pm\sigma
range for M87* and the
\pm 2\sigma
range for Sgr A*. Although the charge introduces a noticeable shrinking effect on the shadow, this deviation remains within the lower limits of the EHT’s expected shadow diameter range.

For strong gravitational lensing, Fig.
2
demonstrates that the electric charge has a pronounced effect in the vicinity of the photon sphere. However, this influence diminishes at larger radial distances. When compared with current EHT data at the observer’s location, the presence of charge leads to distinguishable modifications in the deflection angle. Nonetheless, resolving these effects would require highly sensitive observational techniques beyond the capabilities of present instrumentation. Importantly, our computed lensing angles remain well within the bounds of the photon ring structure, indicating consistency with empirical observations.

In contrast, the quantum correction parameter,
\mathrm{a}
, exhibits no significant impact on the shadow or strong lensing within the current observational sensitivity. However, as illustrated in Fig.
3
and in the bottom panel of Fig.
1
, increasing,
\mathrm{a}
, results in a subtle enhancement of the shadow radius while slightly suppressing the strong deflection angle. This dual behavior, though marginal, reflects the competing roles of quantum geometric expansion and metric modification in quantum-corrected black hole models.

Overall, our findings suggest that while the classical charge yields observable effects in strong-field regimes, the role of quantum corrections though theoretically significant—remains observationally elusive, highlighting the need for future high-resolution instruments to probe such subtle deviations from classical general relativity. Furthermore, intend to further the study this model by applying it to quantum black hole and incorporating GEUP in the metric.

Effects of variety and irrigation on root morphological, physiological and molecular adaptation mechanism in soybean fields in a semi-arid region of China
Authors: Chunjuan Yan, Wenbin Wang, Xugang Sun, Changling Wang, Yongqiang Cao, Lijun Zhang, Shengyou Li, Yan Wang, Hexiang Sun, Duo Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDrought severely reduces soybean yield in semi-arid areas, and the drought resistant varieties and irrigation can effectively alleviate the loss of yield caused by drought. However, the impact of irrigation on the morphological, physiological, and molecular adaptation mechanisms of different drought-resistant soybean genotypes remains poorly understood.MethodsA three-year field experiment was conducted in a semi-arid region to evaluate the effects of two irrigation regimes (non-irrigated and irrigated) and two soybean varieties (LD14 and LD21) on root morphological, physiological, and molecular traits.ResultsThe results showed that irrigation increased the total root length, root surface area, root volume, and shoot biomass in both varieties. In contrast, irrigation reduced root-shoot ratio (R/SF and R/SD), antioxidant enzyme activities (POD and CAT), and the accumulation of soluble proteins, soluble sugars, proline, and MDA. Meanwhile, CAT activity and soluble protein content of LD14 were higher than those of LD21. More specifically, four CAT1-encoding genes were upregulated in the NLD21 vs. NLD14 and ILD21 vs. ILD14 comparisons, which showed the expression level of the gene encoding CAT1 in LD14 is higher than that in LD21. The mitochondria of LD21 was damaged more severe than that of LD14 on non-irrigated conditions. The vessel diameter of LD14 was larger than that of LD21 under the same irrigation condition; whereas the vessel diameter of the non-irrigated level was larger than that of the irrigated treatment for the same variety. Further research supposed these genes LOC100786184, LOC102669408, LOC100789083 and LOC100802727 may indirectly reduce the diameter of xylem vessels. KEGG analysis revealed that DEGs and DAMs in different comparison groups were mainly enriched in fatty acid degradation, flavone and flavonol biosynthesis, plant hormone signal transduction, etc.ConclusionsLD14 (drought-resistant cultivar) showed a lower root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm compared with LD21(drought-sensitive cultivar). Generally, the soluble protein content and CAT activity in LD14 were higher than those in LD21, whereas the MDA content in LD14 was lower than that in LD21. The vessel diameter of LD14 was larger than that of LD21. The mitochondria of LD21 was damaged more severe than that of LD14 on non-irrigated conditions. The yield, pod number per plant, seed number per pod, plant height, main stem node number and branch number of LD14 were higher than those of LD21 in the same irrigation treatment. However, 100-seed weight and internode length of LD14 were lower than those of LD21. These findings hold important theoretical and practical significance in improving the drought resistance of soybean.Keywords: Soybean, Root morphology, Root microstructure and ultrastructure, Transcriptome and metabolome

Introduction

Crop production in rain-fed agriculture is sensitive to variation in rainfall, especially in semi-arid regions with fragile natural and agricultural systems. Soybean plants require sufficient rainfall and irrigation for optimal yields. However, drought stress can have devastating effects on the stability and productivity of soybean in many semi- and arid areas of the world [1, 2]. Fortunately, irrigation can effectively alleviate the damage of insufficient rainfall to plants and increase yield [3].

Root system, as a major organ for the exchange of substances and information between above- and below-ground, has a significant influence on development and yield of crops [4]. Because root tissues have immediate feedback to any changes in soil moisture, root morphology and distribution has been subjects of great interest in recent years [5, 6]. The larger root biomass, root length, root surface area and root volume can increase the water supply of plants, so that crops can better adapt to semi-arid environment [7, 8]. Moreover, severe water deficit in the upper soil layer leads to increase root length and reduce the diameter, which reduces the resistance of roots for water uptake, so crop roots actively absorb water from the subsoil [9, 10]. In addition, the coordinated biological process of shoot and root is also vital for plant’s adaptation to drought stress and yield formation is significantly influenced by the relationship between roots and aboveground organs. A reasonable structure of root and shoot can support a high yield [11]. Irrigation can effectively regulate the growth of root and aboveground system, and thus affect the yield formation [8, 12]. However, the questions about how the irrigation affects root morphological structure, as well as which root to shoot ratio is used to adapt to different soil water content in soybean are not clear.

Drought stress can also cause serious damage to plants and affect their physiological processes, such as causing cell dehydration, membrane system damage, enzyme activity changes and cell metabolic disorders [13, 14]. Drought induces the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), because the metabolic disorder involving ROS free radicals in plant cells leads to membrane peroxidation, which results in damage to the cell membrane, and MDA is an important product of membrane peroxidation damage [15]. In order to counteract ROS production, plants have evolved an intricate antioxidant system. Antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD) and catalase (CAT), play a crucial role in scavenging ROS to reduce plant damage [16, 17]. In addition, plants accumulate osmoregulatory substances such as soluble proteins, soluble sugars, and proline. Proline and other osmotic regulators help maintain intracellular moisture balance under drought stress by reducing membrane permeability, in addition to their role in scavenging free radicals, buffering redox potential, stabilizing cell structure, and activating cell pathways [18]. However, there were genotype differences on drought condition and drought resistant varieties may accumulate less ROS and MDA, more antioxidant enzymes such as SOD and osmoregulatory substances such as proline, soluble sugars, soluble proteins, thereby improving the drought resistance of plants [19–21]. Moreover, irrigation can also reduce the damage of ROS to plants [22].

The internal characteristics of the root system has strongly influence on water uptake and distribution efficiency [23]. Previous studies have found that the thickness of root cortex and the diameter of vessel were smaller in drought-resistant varieties, and the proportion of root cortex in the cross-sectional area of root system was also lower [24]. Furthermore, varieties with larger root diameters possessed larger vascular column diameters and cell numbers, as well as higher grain yields [25]. Organelles in root cells are one of production sites of antioxidant substances, and the quantity and oxidative intensity of antioxidant substances are also related to changes in the number of organelles after drought stress [26]. Specific ROS production and scavenging systems have already been found in mitochondria in plant cells, in which coordination among different cellular compartments of ROS scavenging pathways has been discovered [27].

Due to plant phenotypic, cellular, and physiological responses are fundamentally caused by gene regulation [28]. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has been extensively used to elucidate molecular mechanisms and discover candidate genes for different abiotic stress tolerance and biological processes [29]. For example, a total of 51,554 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were found under drought stress condition in peanuts [30]. At the transcriptomic level, drought induces the modulation of genes involved in different pathways. VpSBP16 from Chinese wild Vitis species can increase plant drought tolerance by regulating ROS in Arabidopsis [31]. In sesame, transcriptomic analysis indicates that the transcript profiling of drought-responsive genes was very different in genotypes of drought tolerant and susceptible plants [32]. Under drought stress, 6969 DEGs that are mainly involved in the metabolism of α-linolenic acid, starch, sucrose and glycerophospholipid, as well as peroxisome and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling were identified [33].

Although many studies have analysed the drought response of plants through transcriptomic analysis, they have not fully characterized the metabolic response network. Metabolomic analysis is a postgenomic approach used to detect metabolite changes and has been widely used to study plant tolerance to various abiotic stresses, such as drought tolerance in Craterostigma plantagineum, licorice and sesame [34, 35], cold stress in maize [36], salt tolerance in Suaeda salsa [37], etc. Metabonomics can thus help us better understand the complex regulation of metabolism in drought resistance.

When plants are exposed to abiotic stresses, a series of physiological and metabolic responses occur to adapt to the environment [38]. Water stress affects a series of plant protection and repair systems, which perturbs the dynamic equilibrium between the production and elimination of ROS [39]; the imbalance could lead to cellular oxidative stress and injury [40]. And then causes damage to the cell membranes and macromolecules (lipids, DNA, and carbohydrates) within the cells. This results in cell dehydration, plasmawall separation, damage to cell membranes and enzyme systems, and a metabolic imbalance, which in turn accelerates crop senescence and hinder normal crop growth and development [41–43]. Under abiotic stress, disruption of the mitochondria inner membrane structure and changes in protein conformation caused by ROS, affect the normal function of the electron transport chain during oxidative phosphorylation in respiration procedure [44]. Furthermore, it affects the establishment of root morphology and the accumulation of plant biomass, which in turn affects yield [45]. Irrigation often alleviates the damage of drought stress to plants and increases yield [46]. Therefore, in-depth exploration of the effects of irrigation on root morphology, physiology, microstructure, and molecules of different drought tolerant varieties in semi-arid areas has important theoretical value and practical significance.

Materials and methods

Experimental site and description

A three-year field experiment was carried out at the Scientific and Observing Experimental Station in Fuxin (42°13’ N, 121°72’ E), Liaoning, northeast of China. The site is situated in a region with rain-fed agriculture under a typical semi-arid condition. The climate is temperate continental monsoon type with severe and dry winter, and high temperature and concentrated rainfall in the summer. Annual sunshine is around 2865.5 h; annual average available accumulated temperature (≥ 10 ℃) is 3298.3 ℃, and there are around 150 d frost-free days in a normal year. The mean annual rainfall is 420 mm, however, it varies greatly from year to year, also in the experimental years 2015–2017 (Fig. 1). The rainfall during the crops growing season (from May to September) was 246.8 mm in 2015, 571.3 mm in 2016 and 371.8 mm in 2017. The annual average temperatures is 7 °C, but monthly mean temperatures from crops growing season (from May to September) was 21.46 °C in 2015, 21.69 °C in 2016 and 22.01 °C in 2017 (Fig. 1). The soil at the experimental site was cinnamon soil and its sample was taken before planting to analyze the characteristics. Plant materials

In this study, the soybean cultivar Liaodou14 (LD14), which is widely cultivated in the region and developed by the Liaoning Academy of Agricultural Science was used as the plant material. LD14 was approved by the National Crop Variety Approval Committee of China in 2003. Plants have white flowers, gray pubescence. Seeds are yellow with black hila and average 16.8 g/100 seed. It is a high-oil, high-yield, and drought-resistant cultivar.

The soybean cultivar Liaodou21 (LD21), which is widely cultivated in the region and developed by the Liaoning Academy of Agricultural Science was used as the plant material. LD21 was approved by the National Crop Variety Approval Committee of China in 2005. Plants have purple flowers, gray pubescence. Seeds are yellow with yellow hila and average 19.5 g/100 seed. It is a drought-sensitive cultivar.

Experimental design and field management

The experiment was carried out in a randomized complete block design that has two irrigation regimes and two varieties with three replicates of each treatment. Two irrigation levels included Non-Irrigated: no irrigation; Irrigated: irrigation 70 mm at fourth trifoliolate stage (V4—four unfolded trifoliolate leaves), full flowering stage (R2—there is an open flower at one of the two uppermost nodes), full pod stage (R4—pods are 2 cm at one of the four uppermost nodes) and full seed stage (R6—pod containing a green seed that fills the pod capacity at one of the four uppermost nodes on the main stem), respectively. The two varieties were LD14 (drought-resistant cultivar) and LD21 (drought-sensitive cultivar). There are four treatments in the experiment: NLD14 (Non-Irrigated+LD14); NLD21 (Non-Irrigated+LD21); ILD14 (Irrigated+LD14); ILD21 (Irrigated+LD21). The experimental plots were 8 m × 4.8 m, and each plot consisted of eight 8 m rows, in north-south orientation, with 0.60 m inter-row spacing, beside four guard rows preventing water penetration between contiguous plots. Plot layout of the irrigation system was shown in Fig. 2 in the study. And the experiment was conducted in a fixed field configuration at the same location in 2015–2017. The 45 kg ha− 1 of N, 70 kg ha− 1 of P2O5 and 60 kg ha− 1 of K2O were given for all plots at sowing time. Plots were over-seeded with hand planters on 9 May in 2015, 12 May in 2016 and 11 May in 2017, respectively. Seedlings thinned to a final stand of 166,700 plants ha− 1. Plots were kept free from weeds, insects and diseases by appropriate measures.

Plant sampling

According to the growth period of soybean plants and crop growth conditions, we conducted destructive sampling experiments at full flowering (R2) stage and full seed (R6) stage in 2015–2017, which 4 soybean plants from row 2 of the test plots were randomly selected at each sampling time. To ensure the integrity of the soybean root system, we used a shovel to dig out the whole soybean root system, and the digging depth was set to approximately 50 cm. After digging out the soybean root systems, the shoot and root of the soybean plant was separated, and the root was placed into 200 mesh nylon net bags and washed with flowing water to avoid damaging the root system. After being, one of the four roots was stored at 4 ℃ for root morphological measurement, one was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and then stored at − 80 ℃ in preparation for the physiological analyses and RNA sequencing, and the other two were used to measure fresh root biomass and dry root biomass.

Measurements

Root morphology and agronomic traits

The cleaned roots were scanned by the root scanning image analysis system WinRHIZO Pro 2013b (Regent Instruments, Inc., Quebec, Canada) to calculate total root length, total root surface area, root volume, average root diameter, and the proportional distribution of root length in roots of different root diameter.

After sampling at each growth stage, fresh shoot biomass and fresh root biomass were immediately measured. Thereafter the root and shoot samples were ovendried at 105 °C for 30 min and then at 85 °C until a constant weight to determine dry shoot biomass and dry root biomass. Based on these, root-shoot ratio (R/S) was calculated: R/SF=Fresh root biomass/Fresh shoot biomass, R/SD=Dry root biomass/Dry shoot biomass.

Enzyme assay

Frozen roots (0.40 g) were ground in 50 mM PBS (pH 7.8) in the presence of ethylenediamine tetraacetic acid (EDTA, 0.1 mM). The homogenate was centrifuged (20 min, 10,000 rpm, 4 °C) and the supernatant was used to assay the activities of antioxidant enzymes. The SOD activity was estimated spectrophotometrically by NBT reduction at 560 nm; the POD activity was estimated spectrophotometrically by guaiacol method at 470 nm; The CAT activity was determined spectrophotometrically as the rate of H2O2 disappearance at 240 nm.

Estimation of soluble protein, soluble sugar, free proline and malondialdehyde (MDA)

Soluble protein was measured by the Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 method. Soluble sugars were measured by the anthrone method. Proline content was determined spectrophotometrically by ninhydrin method at 520 nm. MDA content was extracted and detected using the thiobarbituric acid (TBA) method.

Observation of transmission electron microscopy and microscopic structure in root

For the analysis of transmission electron microscopy, a 1–1.5 cm primary segment was fixed with glutaraldehyde (4%, v/v) in sodium phosphate buffer (SPB, 0.2 M, pH 7.2) under a temperature of 4 ℃ for one day, next post-fixed in osmium tetroxide (1%, OsO4) for 120 min. The samples were washed thrice using SPB (15 min each), then dehydrated with ordered ethanol (30%, 50%, 70%, 80%, 90%, 95%, and 100%) for 15 min and anhydrous ethanol (twice) for twenty minutes each. The samples were inoculated in a mixture containing absolute acetone and linear resin (1:1) for 1 h and mixture of absolute acetone and linear resin (1:3) for three hours at ambient temperature. They were subsequently embedded in the pure resin overnight and heated under a temperature of 70 ℃ for nine hours. In the end, ultramicrotome was utilized to cut the samples into ultrathin sections, which was then stained using lead citrate and uranyl acetate, and finally observed under transmission electron microscopy (Zeiss, Gottingen, Germany).

To observe the microscopic structure of the maturation zone of the roots, a 1–1.5 cm segment was excised from the primary root above the basal zone of the lateral root. Samples were fixed in FAA fixing solution (10 mL of 37% formaldehyde, 5 mL of glacial acetic acid, 50 mL of 96% ethanol and 35 mL of distilled water) for 24 h, and then tissue slices were prepared for histological analysis as described. The images were acquired with an Olympus BX61 microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) software system.

RNA extraction, library construction and sequencing

A total of 12 samples [2 varieties (drought-resistant cultivar and drought-sensitive cultivar) × 2 irrigation regimes (Non-Irrigated and Irrigated) × 3 biological replications] were used for RNA-Seq. Total RNA was isolated utilizing the Trizol reagent kit (Invitrogen, USA) following the instructions provided by the manufacturer. The RNA quality was evaluated using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Following a quality check, mRNAs molecules were enriched and fragmented into short pieces. First strand cDNAs and the second strand cDNA were synthesized. Subsequently, these cDNA fragments underwent purification, end modification, addition of an A-tail, and attachment of sequencing adapters. After selecting the desired size through agarose gel, the ligation products were enriched via PCR amplification. RNA-seq was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform with a paired- end sequencing approach by Wuhan Onemore-tech Co., Ltd in Wuhan, China.

Metabolite extraction and profiling analysis

Widely targeted metabolomics was conducted by Wuhan Onemore-tech Co., Ltd. in Wuhan, China, including sample preparation, extraction, derivatization, detection, quantification and data analysis. Metabolite profiling was conducted using a liquid chromatography-electrospray ionization-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS) system (HPLC, Shim-pack UFLC SHIMADZU CBM30A system; MS, Applied Biosystems 6500 Q TRAP). The standard procedures were described in previous research [47] and three biological replicates were used for each sample.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

To confirm the results obtained from the RNA-seq data, 12 DEGs chosen at random were examined via qRT-PCR. The synthesis of first-strand cDNA was performed using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara Biotechnology Inc., Dalian, China). The qRT-PCR analysis was performed with the ChamQ SYBR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) with a CFX96 Touch™ Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The gene-specific primers were designed using Primer Premier 6.0 (PREMIER Biosoft, CA, USA). Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) gene was used as an internal control. The qRT-PCR analysis was conducted using three biological replicates, and the relative expression levels were determined based on the 2-ΔΔCT method.

Yield and yield components

After maturity, an area of 16 m2 was harvested by hand from the four central rows from each plot, excluding borders. The whole harvested area was used to estimate yield. Pod number per plant, seed number per pod, 100-seed weight, seed weight per plant, plant height, first pod height, main stem node number, internode length and branche number were estimated in ten plants from row 7 per plot.

Statistical analysis

The data were means of three replicates. All results were analyzed by ANOVA using the SAS 8.0 software (SAS Institute 1989) and the significance differences were identified using the Duncan’s tests at the 0.05 probability level. The means and calculated standard error (SE) were reported.

Results

Total root length, total root surface area and root volume

Irrigation and variety and the interaction significantly affected total root length at R2 stage and R6 stage in soybean in both years (Table 2). In general, irrigation increased the total root length of two varieties. However, changes in total root length under irrigation condition showed differences between two soybean varieties, for LD14 (drought-resistant cultivar), irrigation increased the total root length by 3.45% and 10.73% at R2 stage and decreased the total root length by 3.45% and increased 0.99% at R6 stage in 2016 and 2017, respectively. For LD21 (drought-sensitive cultivar), irrigation increased the total root length by 18.82% and 6.78% at R2 stage and by 1.93% and 27.84% at R6 stage in 2016 and 2017, respectively. Irrigation and variety also significantly affect total root surface area at R2 stage and R6 stage in soybean (Table 2). As shown in Table 2, the total root surface area of two varieties under non-irrigated conditions were lower than under irrigated conditions at R2 stage and R6 stage in both years, especially in LD21. Irrigation significantly affects root volume at R2 stage and R6 stage in soybean (Table 2). The root volume of two varieties under non-irrigated condition was lower than under irrigated conditions at R2 stage and R6 stage in both years, especially in LD21. As shown in Table 2, irrigation and variety significantly affected average root diameter at R2 stage. Neither the irrigation nor varieties had any effect on the average root diameter at R6 stage.

Soybean root length distribution in different root diameters

Considering the characteristics of the straight root system in soybean, the response of root lengths in roots of different diameters was further studied. The maximum percentage of root length was the root diameter between 0.0 and 0.1 mm, accounting for 57%~75% of the total root length in soybean (Fig. 3). As shown in Fig. 3, the response of varieties in terms of percentage of root length in different diameters significantly differed in non-irrigated and irrigated treatments. LD21 generally showed a higher root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm compared with LD14 under the same water conditions in two years. There were statistical differences in ‘root length percentage with diameters of 0.5–1.0 mm at R2 stage in 2016’, ‘root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.5 mm and 3.0–3.5 mm at R6 stage in 2016’ and ‘root length percentage with diameters of 1.0–1.5 mm, 1.5–2.0 mm and 3.5–4.0 mm at R2 stage in 2017’. Different responses to irrigation were also observed among root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm and 0.1–0.2 mm at R6 stage in 2016, and compared with non-irrigated, irrigatated treatment decreased the root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm in LD14 and LD21 by 20.21% and 9.29% (Fig. 3). By contrast, the root length percentage with diameters of 0.1–0.2 mm in the LD14 and LD21 increased by 30.40% and 17.55% under irrigatated condition at R6 stage in 2016, respectively.

Antioxidant enzyme activities

The effect of irrigation and variety on antioxidant enzyme activity in the roots is shown in Table 3. Years, irrigation and variety significantly affected SOD activity at R2 stage in soybean. The SOD activity of LD21 was higher than that of LD14 under the same water regime at R2 stage and R6 stage in 2016–2017. Irrigation and variety significantly affected POD activity at R2 stage and R6 stage in soybean. Disscussion

Root morphological characteristics and distributions

Plant roots are the dominant organs for transporting water and diverse nutrients from soil to up-ground shoot. Changes in root system architecture such as root length, root surface area, root volume, and root diameter are common for drought resistance plant under arid and semi-arid regions [48, 49]. Previous studies have shown that root morphological characteristics such as root length and root volume, significantly affect pod yield at harvest due to water deficiency [50, 51]. Wang et al. [52] proposed that drought stress had caused different degrees of damage to root, but the morphological change of the drought-sensitive variety was more obvious and more severe. Similarly, irrigation increased the total root length, total root surface area and yield of two varieties, with the LD21 (drought-sensitive cultivar) showing a greater increase in this field experiment (Tables 2 and 4).

The root system growth is sensitive to changes of the soil water conditions in the root zone. Specifically, soil moisture significantly affected the spatial distribution of cotton fine roots [45]. The distribution ratio of coarse or fine root plays an important role in drought-resisting. Previous research has concluded that root length and root volume density with the diameter of 0.25–0.45 mm had higher percentage of total, but the root surface area of fine roots (diameter < 0.25 mm) accounted for higher percentage of total in maize [5]. Herein, the maximum percentage of root length was the root diameter between 0.0 and 0.1 mm, accounting for 57%~75% of the total root length in soybean (Fig. 3). Furthermore, irrigation decreased the root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm but increased that of 0.1–0.2 mm. LD21 showed a higher root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm compared with LD14 in two years. (Fig. 3). Huang et al. [53] also found coarse root can store more water to counteract soil water variation compared with fine root. The high proportion of coarse root allocation may be one of the important reasons for improving the drought resistance of LD14.

Shoot and root biomass, R/SF and R/SD

Some studies reported an increase in crop absorption and utilization of deep soil moisture and nutrients attributed to root growth into the deep soil profiles under irrigation [37, 54]. Previous studies have shown that irrigation enhanced the root and shoot biomass of plants [55]. Our study results are consistent with findings from previous studies. The root system and the aboveground portion of plants are a unified whole, and the root-shoot ratio reflects the coordination of root and shoot growth [56]. Water deficit led to the high root-shoot ratio [57], irrigation treatment also reduced the R/SF and R/SD of the same variety in our study (Fig. 5).

Physiological characteristics

Moisture affects many physiological processes associated with crop growth and development [58]. Water stress can slow crop growth, causing metabolic disorders and significant yield reductions [59]. The accumulation of reactive oxygen species in plants can invade biomolecules such as DNA, organelles, lipids and proteins, resulting in severe oxidative damage to cell membranes under water stress. MDA is one of the important products of membrane lipid peroxidation, which can be used as indicator for the damage degree of the membrane system [60]. Drought stress can cause a mass of MDA accumulated and the aggrandizement of MDA in drought-sensitive varieties of maize was greater than that in drought-tolerant varieties [61, 62]. This study also found that the MDA content of non-irrigated treatments was higher and MDA content of drought-tolerant variety (LD14) was lower than that of drought-sensitive variety (LD21) in semi-arid area (Table 3). Three antioxidant enzymes, SOD, POD and CAT, play key roles in protecting plants from reactive oxygen species damage and are essential for the rapid response of plants to drought stress. The first line of defense against reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation is SOD, which converts the superoxide radical (O2−) into hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) by catalyzing its dismutation [63]. POD, as one of the defense enzymes of plant cell membranes, helps to reduce the damage caused by drought stress on cell membranes. CAT is the enzyme that can eliminate H2O2 and prevent its accumulation to toxic levels [64]. Mu et al. [65] found that SOD and POD activities were increased during drought compared to the control. However, the activities of SOD, POD, and CAT in drought-tolerant varieties were higher than those in water-sensitive varieties, indicating that the stronger the drought resistance under water stress treatment, the stronger the stress adaptability [66]. In this study, the activities of POD and CAT under non-irrigation treatment were also higher than those under irrigation treatment for the same variety at R2 stage and R6 stage in 2016–2017 (Table 3). Meanwhile, the drought-sensitive variety LD21 exhibited lower CAT activity but higher SOD and POD activities than drought-sensitive variety LD14. Further research showed that 4 CAT1 encoding genes (CAT1, CAT3, CAT4 and CAT5) were also upregulated in NLD21 vs. NLD14 and ILD21 vs. ILD14 comparisons, especially the CAT1 gene reached a significant difference level (Fig. 8).

In addition, osmoregulatory solute are also important components of plant defense response, increasing rapidly when plants are exposed to abiotic stress (Table 3). Plant osmotic regulation sub-stances can be classified into two categories: inorganic ions that enter cells from the external environment and can be regulated by ion pumps to control ion concentrations inside and outside the cells; organic solutes synthesized within cells, mainly including sucrose, sorbitol, as well as nitrogen-containing compounds such as proline and betaine [67–69]. These solutes primarily regulate the cytoplasmic osmotic potential and also provide protection to enzymes, proteins, and biological membranes. Previous studies have shown that the content of soluble sugars, soluble proteins, proline, and betaine significantly increased on water stress [18, 64]. This study obtained similar results that soluble protein content, soluble sugar content, proline content was lower in the irrigated conditions than in the non-irrigated conditions in two varieties at R2 stage and R6 stage in 2016–2017 (Table 3). Notably, a previous study demonstrated that drought resistant variety showed fewer MDA content more proteins contents [70]. This study obtained similar results that the soluble protein content of LD14 was higher than that of LD21 but MDA content of LD14 was lower than that of LD21.

Root microstructure

Root anatomical structure reflects the status of growth and development of plant roots, which is closely related to the physiological function and yield. Root microstructure changed significantly under the influence of drought stress, which was mainly reflected in the decrease of pericycle diameter, area of xylem and phloem, and the increase of vessel lignification, which was not conducive to the radial and axial transport in root system [71]. Metaxylem and xylem diameter are easily observable traits in mature soybean plants under water limited conditions, even with the advancement of secondary thickening [72]. A study demonstrated the plasticity of root elements in wheat: stele and xylem properties were key traits that improve water-use efficiency in wheat over that in rice [73]. In our study, non-irrigated was characterized by a larger vessel diameter for the same variety. Similar studies have also pointed out xylem number and diameter facilitated to improve water-use efficiency during drought stress [74]. In a previous study, the drought-resistant varieties exhibited a low reduction in the number of xylem vessels while maintaining relative stability in their diameter [75]. Selecting high-yielding lines based on xylem diameter has previously proven successful in a drought breeding programme for wheat [76]. In this study, the drought-resistant variety LD14 under non-irrigated conditions, showed a large xylem diameter and exhibited high yields in semi-arid region (Fig. 6Ac, Table 4). The larger diameter of xylem vessel could be beneficial for water acquisition in plants. Furthermore, three genes (LOC100804712, LOC100797494, and LOC100776641) may be related to increase the of xylem vessel diameter (Figs. 6 and 9).

Previous research has indicated that the nucleus and mitochondria play important roles in drought resistance in crops, and the proteins of differentially expressed genes in response to drought were mainly present in these organelles [77]. In this study it was observed, the structure of mitochondria was also damaged in varying degrees, mainly manifested as the reduction of mitochondrial cristae, disappearance of outer membrane, and occurrence of cavity inside mitochondria non-irrigated conditions for LD21 (drought-sensitive cultivar) in semi-arid region. However, LD14 as a drought resistant variety has less mitochondrial damage on non-irrigated condition. Previous studies also pointed out that a large number of cavities appeared in drought, the structure of cristae began to degrade, and part of the bilayer was destroyed [71]. In addition, wang et al. [52] found the degree of plasmolysis of drought-sensitive cultivar was more serious, the phenomenon of plasmolysis of main root was more obvious.

Transcriptome and metabolome

KEGG analysis revealed that DEGs and DAMs in different comparison groups were mainly enriched in fatty acid degradation, ABC transporters, flavone and flavonol biosynthesis, plant hormone signal transduction, etc. (Figures 7D, E and F and 8C, D and E). The long-chain fatty acid was resolved into their derivatives, such as alcohols, alkanes, aldehydes, ketones and wax esters, via the decarbonylation and acyl reduction pathways [78, 79]. As the outermost barrier of plants, cuticular wax can maintain the plant water balance by reducing excessive water loss to enhance plant drought resistance [80]. The plant can maintain normal metabolism of soluble sugars and proteins, as well as prevent damage to organelles such as mitochondria and maintain a more complete structure to avoid water loss under drought conditions. The wax accumulation had been reported in pea nut and Arabi dopsis on drought condition [81, 82]. The ABC transporter pathway is a vital moderating wax transport [83]. According to our KEGG analysis of DEGs and DAMs, ABC transporters were also enriched in 5 different comparison groups (Figs. 7D, E and F and 8D and E). Flavonoids are widely distributed and multifunctional secondary metabolites. The expression of flavonoid biosynthesis genes was affected by soil moisture [84, 85]. In our study, the genes and metabolites in the flavone and flavonol biosynthesis (ko00944) and flavonoid biosynthesis (ko00943) also changed in root. In addition, flavonoids play important roles in ROS scavenging in barley [29]. Therefore, the accumulation of flavonoids could increase ROS scavenging ability [86]. Excessive ROS cause damage to cellular structures, biomolecule degradation and physiological functions disruption. Furthermore, it damages organelles such as mitochondria, leading to metabolic disorders of substances like proline and soluble sugars, delaying plant growth, damaging the total root length, root surface area, root and aboveground biomass of soybean, and ultimately reducing yield. Phytohormones are pivotal in enabling plant adaptation to adverse environmental conditions via a sophisticated crosstalk among them [87]. Recently, an increasing number of studies have focused on the effect of brassinosteroid on drought tolerance [88, 89]. Brassinosteroid are a group of polyhydroxylated plant steroid hormones that regulates plant growth and development, cell elongation and division, and responses to both abiotic and biotic stresses [90]. In this research, KEGG pathways analysis also showed that most of DEGs and DAMs involved in brassinosteroid biosynthesis (Figs. 7D and F and 10D and E). It’s reported that under acute drought stress, drought resistant cultivar still had capability of synthesizing terpenoid backbone thereby providing abundant compound of semi-terpenes, sesquiterpenes, monoterpenes, diterpenes and tetriterpenes for abiotic adversity [91]. Consistently, our transcriptome and metabolome analysis also revealed the significant enriched pathways including terpenoid backbone biosynthesis (ko00900), sesquiterpenoid and triterpenoid biosynthesis (ko00909), monoterpenoid biosynthesis (ko00902) (Figs. 7E and F and 10C and D). Previous studies have reported that carbohydrate metabolism plays an important role in resistance to abiotic stress in plants, such as providing energy and raw materials for plants, regulating cellular osmotic pressure and ion homeostasis to enhance stress resistance [92–94]. Carbohydrates are important energy substances for plants to carry out various life activities; in particular, root carbohydrates play an important role in supporting plant regeneration, enduring external environmental stress, and maintaining the stability of life system. Moreover, respiratory metabolism in plants provides substrates, energy, and reducing power for various life activities in plants. Glucose is a pivotal substrate for respiratory metabolism and is mainly derived from the hydrolysis of starch and sucrose in plants [95]. In our study, we found that the pathways related to carbohydrate metabolism included starch and sucrose metabolism, galactose metabolism, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, citrate cycle, etc. (Figures 7D, E and F and 10C, D and E).

Soybean yield

Soybean yield is mainly a function of pod number per plant, seed number per pod and 100-seed weight. Water stress have been reported to reduce seed weight, total biomass, pods per plant, seeds per plant, seeds per pod, 100-seed weight, and ultimately caused a decline in yield [96, 97]. However, irrigation increased yield, yield components and agronomic traits on drought condition [98, 99]. The present study also showed irrigation increased seed weight per plant, plant height, main stem node number and yield (Table 4).

During the process of drought environmental adaptation, soybean plants have evolved elaborate mechanisms to detect external signals and regulate metabolic pathways through gene expression modulation. As soil moisture decreases, root tissues first perceive and respond to water deficiency signals. However, there are few reports on the comprehensive evaluation of drought-tolerant soybean genotypes in terms of root morphology, root physiological metabolism, root microstructure, root mitochondria ultrastructure, transcriptomics and metabolomics. Overall, our investigation into the drought response of soybean has identified significant physiological changes and gene expression adaptations that contribute to its resilience. LD14 boosted drought resistance by inducing the high expression of CAT1 encoding associated genes, which in turn elevated CAT activity. KEGG analysis revealed that DEGs and DAMs in different comparison groups were mainly enriched in fatty acid degradation, etc. Fatty acid degradation occurs within mitochondria, and the intact mitochondria structure within the cell facilitates the fatty acids degradation. Simultaneously, LD14 as a drought resistant variety showed less mitochondria damage on non-irrigated condition. Finally, our three-year replicated studies were mainly conducted under natural field conditions, which were greatly affected by external environmental factors. In future studies, we will conduct water-controlled pot experiments and polyethylene glycol (PEG)-simulated drought stress treatments.

Conclusions

In general, irrigation increased the total root length, total root surface area and root volume for two varieties. The maximum percentage of root length was the root diameter between 0.0 and 0.1 mm, accounting for 57%~75% of the total root length in soybean. LD21 showed a higher root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm compared with LD14. Moreover, both varieties under irrigated had consistently higher fresh/dry shoot biomass but lower R/SF and R/SD compared with the non-irrigation treatment. Generally, LD21 exhibited higher SOD and POD activities but lower CAT activity than LD14. In most cases, the activities of POD and CAT were higher in the non-irrigated treatment than irrigated treatment for the same variety. Soluble protein content, soluble sugar content, proline content and MDA content under the irrigated conditions were lower than those under the non-irrigated conditions for two varieties at R2 stage and R6 stage in 2016–2017. Notably, the soluble protein content of LD14 was higher than that of LD21 but MDA content of LD14 was lower than that of LD21. Many vessels were observed in the xylem, and there were many pore chains and pore clusters. A few paratracheal parenchyma cells and phloem fibers were also seen. The vessel diameter of LD14 was larger than that of LD21 under the same irrigation condition; whereas the vessel diameter of the non-irrigated level was larger than that of the irrigated treatment for the same variety. The root mitochondria maintained a normal ultrastructure, with intact bilayer membranes and abundant cristae on irrigated conditions for both varieties. The mitochondria of LD21 was damaged more severe than that of LD14 on non-irrigated conditions. In the ILD21 vs. ILD14 and NLD21 vs. NLD14 comparisons, the number of up-regulated DEGs was lower than down-regulated DEGs. Furthermore, KEGG pathways analysis showed that the DEG in ILD14 related to benzoxazinoid biosynthesis, brassinosteroid biosynthesis and sulfur metabolism were up-regulated compared with ILD21. KEEG enrichment analysis revealed 5 terms were the significantly enriched including brassinosteroid biosynthesis, glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, ABC transporters, lysine degradation, monoterpenoid biosynthesis among four groups of NLD21 vs. ILD21 and NLD14 vs. ILD14. In the NLD21 vs. NLD14 group, the main significantly enriched up-regulated pathways for differential metabolites were flavonoid biosynthesis, isoflavonoid biosynthesis, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism. Irrigation consistently increased seed weight per plant, plant height, main stem node number and yield of the same variety compared to non-irrigated, especially in LD21. The yield, pod number per plant, seed number per pod, plant height, main stem node number and branch number of LD14 were higher than that of LD21 in the same irrigation treatment. However, 100-seed weight and internode length of LD14 were lower than that of LD21.

The soybean improved their drought resistance by decreasing aboveground biomass, root length percentage with diameters of 0–0.1 mm, MDA content and mitochondria damage but increasing soluble protein content, the CAT activity and vessel diameter, while regulating genes expression related to flavonoid biosynthesis, isoflavonoid biosynthesis, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism. As a drought resistant variety, Liaodou14 exhibited higher yield, pod number per plant, seed number per pod, plant height, main stem node number and branch number but lower 100-seed weight and internode length. These studies will benefit us in breeding drought resistant varieties in the future.

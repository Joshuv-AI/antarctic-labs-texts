Weakly right coherent monoids
Levent Michael Dasar, Victoria Gould, Craig Miller

Abstract
. A monoid S S is said to be weakly right coherent if every finitely generated right ideal of S S is finitely presented as a right S S -act. It is known that S S is weakly right coherent if and only if it satisfies the following conditions: S S is right ideal Howson, meaning that the intersection of any two finitely generated right ideals of S S is finitely generated; and the right annihilator congruences of S S are finitely generated as right congruences. We examine the behaviour of these two conditions (in the more general setting of semigroups) under certain algebraic constructions and deduce closure results for the class of weakly right coherent monoids. In particular, we show that the class of weakly right coherent monoids is closed under taking direct products and monoid free products. Finally, we provide a survey of connections between each of the aforementioned conditions and axiomatisability of classes of acts.

1.
Introduction

An algebra from a given variety is said to be
coherent
if all its finitely generated subalgebras are finitely presented. In the context of groups and rings, coherency has received significant attention. For a survey of coherency in groups, see
[
22
]
. A ring is called
right coherent
if all its finitely presented right modules are coherent. It turns out that right coherency for rings is equivalent to every finitely generated right ideal being finitely presented as a right module
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.
For monoids, acts play the analagous role to that of modules in the theory of rings. Thus, a monoid
S
is called
right coherent
if every finitely presented right
S
-act is coherent. Right coherency for monoids was introduced in
[
11
]
and has since been intensively studied; see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
]
. In particular, it has been shown that groups, free monoids and the bicyclic monoid are right (and left) coherent.

A monoid is
weakly right coherent
if all its finitely generated right ideals are finitely presented as right acts. In contrast to the situation for rings, right coherency for monoids is strictly stronger than weak right coherency. For instance, infinite full transformation monoids are weakly right (and left) coherent
[
11
, Corollary 3.6, Theorem 3.7]
but not right (or left) coherent
[
1
, Theorem 3.4]
.

It was shown in
[
11
, Corollary 3.3]
that a monoid
S
is weakly right coherent if and only if the following conditions hold: the intersection of any two finitely generated right ideals of
S
is finitely generated; and the right annihilator congruences
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\{(s,t)\in S\times S\mid as=at\}
(
a\in S
) of
S
are finitely generated. These two conditions can of course be defined, more generally, for semigroups. We call semigroups satisfying the latter condition
finitely right equated
.
Semigroups satisfying the former property are known as
right ideal Howson
and were studied in
[
3
]
. We remark that a monoid is right ideal Howson if and only if it is finitely aligned
[
9
]
; for semigroups, being finitely aligned is a stronger condition, as demonstrated in
[
3
]
.

Right coherency and weak right coherency for monoids have been formulated in terms of the model-theoretic notion of axiomatisability. A class
C
of first-order structures is
axiomatisable
if there is a set of sentences whose models are exactly the members of
C.

A classical result of Wheeler
[
21
]
, interpreted for acts in
[
10
, Theorem 6]
, says that a monoid is right coherent if and only if the class of existentially closed right acts is axiomatisable. By
[
10
, Theorem 3, Corollary 4.2]
, a monoid is weakly right coherent if and only if the class of
\aleph_{0}
-injective right acts is axiomatisable.

The main purpose of the present article is to investigate the closure properties of the class of weakly right coherent monoids with respect to some standard algebraic and semigroup-theoretic constructions. Since some of the constructions considered yield semigroups that are not monoids, it will be useful to have a notion of weak right coherency for semigroups. Thus, we define a semigroup
S
to be
weakly right coherent
if
S^{1}
is weakly right coherent. Our strategy is to first study the closure properties of the classes of right ideal Howson semigroups and finitely right equated semigroups, and then deduce results concerning weak right coherency. In the case of right ideal Howson semigroups, our work builds on that of
[
3
, Section 4]
.
Our main results are summarised in Figure
1
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary preliminary material on semigroups. Sections
3
and
4
are concerned with the properties of being right ideal Howson and being finitely right equated, respectively. In Section
5
we use the results of the previous two sections to derive closure results for the class of weakly right coherent monoids. Finally, in Section
6
, we provide a survey of connections between axiomatisability of certain classes of acts and each of the properties of being right ideal Howson, being finitely right equated, and weak right coherency.

3.
Right ideal Howson semigroups

Definition 3.1
.

A semigroup is
right ideal Howson
(
RIH
) if the intersection of any two finitely generated right ideals is finitely generated.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
A semigroup
S
is RIH if and only if
S^{1}
is RIH.

A semigroup is RIH if and only if the intersection of principal right ideals is finitely generated. Also, since the intersections of principal right ideals generated by
\mathcal{R}
-comparable elements are clearly principal, we need not consider these. We will use both of these facts without explicit mention throughout this paper.

We begin by considering the closure of the class of RIH semigroups under algebraic constructions, building on the earlier work of Carson and Gould
[
3
]
.

Since free semigroups are RIH, but not all semigroups are RIH
[
13
]
, certainly the property of being RIH is not closed under homomorphic images. However, this property is closed under Rees quotients and retracts, as a consequence of the following technical result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups such that for all
\mathcal{R}
-incomparable elements
u,v\in T
there exist
a,b\in S
and a homomorphism
\varphi:S\to T
such that
a\varphi=u
,
b\varphi=v
and
(aS\cap bS)\varphi=uT\cap vT
. If
S
is RIH then so is
T
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in T
be
\mathcal{R}
-incomparable. Then, by assumption, there exist
a,b\in S
and a homomorphism
\varphi:S\rightarrow T
such that
a\varphi=u
,
b\varphi=v
and
(aS\cap bS)\varphi=uT\cap vT
. Then
a,b\in S
are
\mathcal{R}
-incomparable, and hence
aS^{1}\cap bS^{1}=aS\cap bS
. As
S
is RIH, we have
aS\cap bS=XS^{1}
for some finite set
X
, and by application of
\varphi
we obtain

uT^{1}\cap vT^{1}=uT\cap vT=(aS\cap bS)\varphi=(XS^{1})\varphi\subseteq(X\varphi)T^{1}\subseteq(aS\cap bS)\varphi T^{1}\subseteq uT^{1}\cap vT^{1}.

Therefore

uT^{1}\cap vT^{1}=(X\varphi)T^{1}
and
T
is RIH.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The class of RIH semigroups is closed under Rees quotients.

Proof.

Given a semigroup
S
and ideal
I
of
S
, it is straightforward to show that the conditions of Proposition
3.3
are satisfied, where for any
u,v\in S/I
we use the canonical homomorphism
\varphi:S\to S/I.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The class of RIH semigroups is closed under retracts.

Proof.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups with a retraction
\varphi:S\rightarrow T.
Consider
\mathcal{R}
-incomparable
u,v\in T
. Then
u\varphi=u
and
v\varphi=v,
and certainly
(uS\cap vS)\varphi\subseteq uT\cap vT
(since
\varphi
is a homomorphism). For
y\in uT\cap vT,
applying
\varphi
we obtain
y=y\varphi\in(uS\cap vS)\varphi
. Thus
(uS\cap vS)\varphi=uT\cap vT
. The result now follows from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Next, we show that the property of being RIH is not in general inherited by a large subsemigroup or a small extension.

Counterexample 3.6
.

The class of RIH semigroups is not closed under small extensions or large subsemigroups.

Proof.

We first show that the class of RIH semigroups is not closed under small extensions. Let
F
be the free commutative semigroup generated by
\{x_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
and let
\overline{F}=\{\overline{w}\mid w\in F\}
. Then
T=F\cup\overline{F}\cup\{0\}
forms a semigroup with zero where the operation on
F
is extended as follows:

u\overline{v}=\overline{u}v=\overline{uv}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{u}\,\overline{v}=a0=0a=0\quad(u,v\in F,a\in T).

We claim that
T
is RIH. Let
u\in F
. Then
uT^{1}=uF^{1}\cup\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F\}\cup\{0\}
and
\overline{u}T^{1}=\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F^{1}\}\cup\{0\}
for
u\in F
. We have two different families of intersections of incomparable principal right ideals for
u,v\in F
:

\displaystyle uT^{1}\cap vT^{1}

\displaystyle=(uF^{1}\cap vF^{1})\cup(\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F\}\cap\{\overline{vw}\mid w\in F\})\cup\{0\};

\displaystyle\overline{u}T^{1}\cap\overline{v}T^{1}

\displaystyle=uT^{1}\cap\overline{v}T^{1}=(\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F\}\cap\{\overline{vw}\mid w\in F\})\cup\{0\}.

By
[
3
, Corollary 3.6]
,
F
is RIH. Thus, for
u,v\in F
the intersection
uF^{1}\cap vF^{1}
is generated by some finite set
X
. It is then straightforward to show that
uT^{1}\cap vT^{1}=XT^{1}
and
\overline{u}T^{1}\cap\overline{v}T^{1}=\{\overline{x}\mid x\in X\}T^{1}
. Hence
T
is RIH.

Let
S=T\cup\{a,b\},

and define a multiplication on
S
, extending that of
T
, by

au=bu=ua=ub=\overline{u}\quad\text{and}\quad at=ta=bt=tb=0\quad(u\in F,t\in\overline{F}\cup\{0\}).

It is straightforward to show that
S
is a semigroup under this multiplication, and then clearly
S
is a small extension of
T
. The intersection
aS^{1}\cap bS^{1}=\overline{F}\cup\{0\}
is not finitely generated since
\{\overline{x_{i}}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
must be contained in any possible generating set. Hence
S
is not RIH.

Now we show that the class of RIH semigroups is not closed under large subsemigroups. Let
M=\{1,g\}\cup S,
and define a multiplication on
M,
extending that of
S,
by

1m=m1=m,\quad g^{2}=1,\quad ga=ag=b,\quad gb=bg=a,\quad gt=tg=t\quad(m\in M,t\in T).

Then
M
is a monoid under this multiplication, and clearly
S
is large in
M.
We note that the group of units of
M
is
\{1,g\}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
We claim that
M
is RIH. Let
u,v\in F\subseteq M
(all other pairs of elements being
\mathcal{R}
-comparable).

An intersection of incomparable principal right ideals
M
has one of the following two forms, where
u,v\in F
:

\displaystyle uM\cap vM

\displaystyle=(uF^{1}\cap vF^{1})\cup(\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F^{1}\}\cap\{\overline{vw}\mid w\in F^{1}\})\cup\{0\},

\displaystyle\overline{u}M\cap\overline{v}M

\displaystyle=\overline{u}M\cap vM=(\{\overline{uw}\mid w\in F^{1}\}\cap\{\overline{vw}\mid w\in F^{1}\})\cup\{0\}.

Since
F
is RIH, for
u,v\in F
the intersection
uF\cap vF
is generated by some finite set
X
. It is then straightforward to show that
uM\cap vM=XM
and
\overline{u}M\cap\overline{v}M=\{\overline{x}\mid x\in X\}M
. Hence
M
is RIH.
∎

The property of being RIH is preserved under subsemigroups whose complements are ideals, and by small extensions where the complement is an ideal:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
S
be a semigroup with a subsemigroup
T
such that
S{\setminus}T
is an ideal of
S
.

(1)

If
S
is RIH then so is
T
.

(2)

If
S{\setminus}T
is finite, then
S
is RIH if and only if
T
is RIH.

Proof.

(1) Let
a,b\in T
. As
S
is RIH we have
aS^{1}\cap bS^{1}=XS^{1}
for some finite set
X
. Since
S{\setminus}T
is an ideal, it follows that
X\cap T\subseteq aT\cap bT\subseteq(X\cap T)T^{1}
, and hence
aT\cap bT
is generated by
X\cap T
.

(2) Suppose that
T
is RIH and let
I=S{\setminus}T
. If
a,b\in T
, then
aS\cap bS
is finitely generated by
X\cup(aI\cap bI)
where
aT^{1}\cap bT^{1}=XT^{1}
. If
a\in I
or
b\in I
, then
aS\cap bS\subseteq I
, so
aS\cap bS
is finite and hence finitely generated.
∎

Counterexample 3.11
.

There exists a semigroup

Proof.

Let

. Thus, the forward implication follows from Proposition
3.7
(1). The reverse implication is
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
.
∎

Considering now a monoid free product

F=\prod^{\ast}_{1}\{M_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of a collection
\{M_{i}:i\in I\}
of monoids,
[
3
, Proposition 4.3]
states that, if each
M_{i}
is RIH, then so is
F
. The converse also holds, by Proposition
3.5
, since each
M_{i}
is a retract of
F
; this follows from the well-known universal property that for any monoid
T
and homomorphisms
\varphi_{i}:M_{i}\to T
(
i\in I
) there exists a homomorphism
\varphi:F\to T
. Thus, we have:

Theorem 3.16
.

The monoid free product

\prod^{\ast}_{1}\{M_{i}\mid i\in I\}
of a collection
\{M_{i}:i\in I\}
of monoids is RIH if and only if each
M_{i}
is RIH.

4.
Finitely right equated semigroups

Definition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a semigroup. For
a\in S
, the
right annihilator congruence
of
a
is

\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\{(s,t)\in S\times S\mid as=at\}.

Clearly
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is a right congruence on
S
. We say that
S
is
finitely right equated
(
FRE
) if
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated for each
a\in S
.

4.1.
Basic facts

Let
S
be a semigroup. We denote the identity and universal relations on
S
by
\Delta_{S}
and
\nabla_{S}
, respectively. Recall that an element
a\in S
is
left cancellative
if
ab=ac
implies
b=c
for all
b,c\in S
, and
S
is
left cancellative
if each of its elements is left cancellative.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a semigroup
S
and an element
a\in S
, the following hold.

(1)

\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\Delta_{S}
if and only if
a
is left cancellative.

(2)

\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\nabla_{S}
if and only if
aS=\{a^{2}\}
. Moreover,
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\nabla_{S}
and
a
is right factorisable if and only if
a
is a left zero.

Proof.

(1) This is clear.

(2) If
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\nabla_{S}
, then for any
s\in S
we have
(a,s)\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
and hence
a^{2}=as.
On the other hand, if
aS=\{a^{2}\}
then
as=a^{2}=at
for all
s,t\in S
, and hence
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\nabla_{S}
. The second part of the statement follows from the first part.
∎

From Lemma
4.2
we deduce the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.3
.

All left cancellative semigroups are FRE.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
S
be a semigroup such that, for every element
a\in S
, either
a
is left cancellative or
aS=\{a^{2}\}
, and such that there exists at least one
a\in S
with

aS=\{a^{2}\}
. Then
S
is FRE if and only if
\nabla_{S}
is finitely generated as a right congruence.

The property that the universal right congruence is finitely generated has recently received significant attention; see for instance
[
6
,
14
]
.

We shall now establish a few useful lemmas. First, given a relation
\rho
on a semigroup
S
, we define the
content
of
\rho
as

\textbf{C}(\rho)=\{a\in S\mid(a,b)\in\rho{\setminus}\Delta_{S}\text{ for some }b\in S\}.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\rho
be a right congruence on a semigroup
S
. If
\rho=\langle X\rangle
then the right ideal
\textbf{C}(\rho)S^{1}
is generated by
\textbf{C}(X)
.

Proof.

The statement clearly holds if
S
is trivial, so assume that
S
is non-trivial. Certainly
\textbf{C}(X)\subseteq\textbf{C}(\rho),
since
X\subseteq\rho
and so
\textbf{C}(X)S^{1}\subseteq\textbf{C}(\rho)S^{1}
.
Now consider
a\in\textbf{C}(\rho)
. Choose
b\in S{\setminus}\{a\}
such that
(a,b)\in\rho
, so that there exists an
X
-sequence from
a
to
b.
It follows that
a\in xS^{1}
for some
x\in\textbf{C}(X),
and hence
\textbf{C}(X)S^{1}\supseteq\textbf{C}(\rho)S^{1}
, finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
S
be a semigroup. If there exists a right factorisable element
a\in S
such that
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated, then
S=US^{1}
for some finite set
U\subseteq S
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists some
u\in S
such that
a=au.

Since
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated, by Lemma
4.5
the right ideal
\textbf{C}(\textbf{r}_{S}(a))S^{1}
is generated by some finite set
Y.
Let
U=\{u\}\cup Y.
We have
(us,s)\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
for all
s\in S.
Therefore, either
us=s
or else
s\in\textbf{C}(\textbf{r}_{S}(a))S^{1}=YS^{1}
. In either case, we have
s\in US^{1}
. Thus
S=US^{1}.

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
S
be a semigroup with a regular element
a
, so that there exists
b\in S
such that
a=aba
. Then, for any set
U\subseteq S
such that
S=US^{1}
we have

\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\langle\{(bau,u)\mid u\in U\}\rangle.

Consequently,
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated if and only if
S=US^{1}
for some finite set
U\subseteq S
.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq S
be such that
S=US^{1}
, and let
X=\{(bau,u)\mid u\in U\}.
We claim that
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=\langle X\rangle.
Indeed, since
abau=au
for each
u\in U,
we have
X\subseteq\textbf{r}_{S}(a).
Now let
(s,t)\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a).
By assumption, there exist
u,v\in U
and
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\in S^{1}
such that
s=us^{\prime}
and
t=vt^{\prime}
. Since
as=at,
we have an
X
-sequence

s=us^{\prime},\ baus^{\prime}=bavt^{\prime},\ vt^{\prime}=t,

as required. The second part of the lemma follows from the first part and Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

A regular semigroup
S
is FRE if and only if
S=US^{1}
for some finite set
U\subseteq S
. Consequently, any regular monoid is FRE.

4.2.
Quotients, substructures and extensions

Using Corollary
4.8
, we may quickly deduce that the property of being FRE is not preserved under removing an adjoined identity.

Counterexample 4.9
.

If a monoid
S^{1}
is FRE then
S
need not be.

Proof.

Let
S
be any regular semigroup that is not finitely generated as a right ideal of itself, such as an infinite left zero semigroup. Then, by Corollary
4.8
, the monoid
S^{1}
is FRE but
S
is not FRE.
∎

Since left cancellative semigroups are FRE, in particular so are free semigroups. Hence, the class of FRE semigroups is not closed under homomorphic images. Moreover, unlike the case for right ideal Howson semigroups, we do not have closure under Rees quotients.

Counterexample 4.10
.

The class of FRE semigroups is not closed under Rees quotients.

Proof.

Let
F
be the free semigroup on an infinite set. Then
F
is FRE, since it is left cancellative. Let
I
be the ideal of all words in
F
of length
2
or greater. Then
F/I
is an infinite null semigroup. From Lemma
4.6
,
F/I
is not FRE.
∎

On the other hand, the property of being FRE is preserved under retracts, as a consequence of the following technical result. First, given any map
\varphi:A\to B
and any
subset
X\subseteq A\times A,
we denote by
X\varphi
the set
\{(x\varphi,y\varphi)\mid(x,y)\in X\}.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups such that for each
b\in T
there exist
a\in S
and a homomorphism
\varphi:S\to T
such that
a\varphi=b
and
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)\varphi=\textbf{r}_{T}(b).
If
S
is FRE then so is
T
.

Proof.

Consider
b\in T
. By assumption, there exist
a\in S
and a homomorphism
\varphi:S\to T
such that
a\varphi=b
and
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)\varphi=\textbf{r}_{T}(b).

Since
S
is FRE, there exists a finite generating set
X
of
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
.
We claim that
X\varphi
generates
\textbf{r}_{T}(b).
Indeed, we have
X\varphi\subseteq\textbf{r}_{S}(a)\varphi=\textbf{r}_{T}(b).

Now consider
(t,t^{\prime})\in\textbf{r}_{T}(b)
.
Then there exists
(s,s^{\prime})\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
such that
s\varphi=t
and
s^{\prime}\varphi=t^{\prime}
. If
s=s^{\prime}
then
t=t^{\prime}
, so assume that
s\neq s^{\prime}
. Then there exists an
X
-sequence

s=p_{1}c_{1},\ q_{1}c_{1}=p_{2}c_{2},\ \dots\ ,\ q_{n}c_{n}=s^{\prime}.

By application of
\varphi
we obtain the
X\varphi
-sequence

t=p_{1}\varphi c_{1}\varphi,\ q_{1}\varphi c_{1}\varphi=p_{2}\varphi c_{2}\varphi,\ \dots\ ,\ q_{n}\varphi c_{n}\varphi=t^{\prime},

interpreting
1\varphi
as
1
. This establishes the claim, and thus
T
is FRE.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

The class of FRE semigroups is closed under retracts.

Proof.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups where
T
is a retract of
S.
We show that the condition of Proposition
4.11
holds. Let
\varphi:S\to T
be a retraction, and consider
b\in T.
Then
b\varphi=b.
If
bs=bs^{\prime}
then
b(s\varphi)=b(s^{\prime}\varphi),
so
\textbf{r}_{S}(b)\varphi\subseteq\textbf{r}_{T}(b).
Conversely, if
(t,t^{\prime})\in\textbf{r}_{T}(b)
then
clearly
(t,t^{\prime})\in\textbf{r}_{S}(b),

and hence
(t,t^{\prime})=(t,t^{\prime})\varphi\in\textbf{r}_{S}(b)\varphi
.
∎

Counterexample
4.9
shows that the property of being FRE is not necessarily preserved under large subsemigroups. We now show
the same result for monoids.

Counterexample 4.13
.

The class of FRE monoids is not closed under small extensions or large submonoids.

Proof.

Let
F
be any free commutative monoid on an infinite set. Let
T=\{a\}\cup F^{0}
, where
a\not\in F,
and define a multiplication on
T
, extending that of
F^{0}
, by
a1=1a=a
and
at=ta=0
for all
t\in T{\setminus}\{1\}
, where 1 denotes the identity of
F
. It is straightforward to show that
T
is a monoid under this multiplication, and obviously
T
is a small extension of
F.
The monoid
F
is cancellative so certainly FRE. On the other hand, since the right ideal
F{\setminus}\{1\}
of
F
is not finitely generated, it follows that the right ideal
\textbf{C}(\textbf{r}_{T}(a))T^{1}=T{\setminus}\{1\}
of
T
is not finitely generated.
Hence, by Lemma
4.5
,
T
is not FRE.

Let
S=T\cup\{e\}
, where
T
is as above and
e\notin T
, and define a multiplication on
S
, extending that of
T
, by

e1=1e=e,\quad e^{2}=e,\quad ea=ae=a,\quad eu=ue=0\;\ (u\in F^{0}).

Then
S
is a monoid under this multiplication, and
T
is clearly large in
S
. We claim that
S
is FRE. Since
e
is regular, the right annihilator congruence
\textbf{r}_{S}(e)
is finitely generated by Lemma
4.7
. Moreover, we have

\textbf{r}_{S}(a)=(\{1,e\}\times\{1,e\})\cup(S{\setminus}\{1,e\}\times S{\setminus}\{1,e\}).

Since for all
u\in F
we have
u=1u
and
0=eu
, it quickly follows that
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is generated by
\{(1,e),(a,0)\}
.
Finally, for all
u\in F^{0}
we have

\textbf{r}_{S}(u)=\Delta_{S}\cup(\{e,a,0\}\times\{e,a,0\}),

which is clearly finitely generated. Hence
S
is FRE.
∎

The following result provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a small extension of an FRE semigroup to be FRE.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
S
be a semigroup and
T
a large subsemigroup of
S,
and suppose that
T
is FRE. Then
S
is FRE if and only if
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated for each
a\in S{\setminus}T
.

Proof.

The forward implication is obvious. For the converse, we only need to show that
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated for each
a\in T.
So, consider
a\in T
. Let
X
be a finite generating set for
\textbf{r}_{T}(a)
. For each
u\in S{\setminus}T
such that there exists
v\in T
with
au=av
, fix some
\alpha_{u}\in T
such that
au=a\alpha_{u}
. We claim that
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
is finitely generated by

Y=X\cup(\textbf{r}_{S}(a)\cap(S{\setminus}T\times S{\setminus}T))\cup\{(u,\alpha_{u})\mid\text{there exists $v\in T$ such that $au=av$}\}.

Let
(u,v)\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
. If
u,v\in T
, then
(u,v)\in\mathbf{r}_{T}(a),
so that
u
and
v
are connected by an
X
-sequence and hence by a
Y
-sequence. If
u,v\in S{\setminus}T
, then
(u,v)\in Y
. Finally, if, without loss of generality,
u\in S{\setminus}T
and
v\in T
, then
(u,\alpha_{u})\in Y
and
(\alpha_{u},v)\in\textbf{r}_{T}(a)
. Therefore, there exists an
X
-sequence connecting
\alpha_{u}
and
v
, and hence a
Y
-sequence connecting
u
and
v
. This completes the proof.
∎

Recalling that the right annihilator congruence of the identity of a monoid is trivial, by Proposition
4.14
we have:

Corollary 4.15
.

If a semigroup
S
is FRE then so is
S^{1}
.

The property of being FRE is inherited by any subsemigroup whose complement is an ideal:

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
S
be a semigroup with a subsemigroup
T
such that
S{\setminus}T
is an ideal of
S
. If
S
is FRE then so is
T
.

Proof.

Consider
a\in T
, and let
X
be a finite generating set for
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
. We claim that
\textbf{r}_{T}(a)
is finitely generated by
Y=X\cap(T\times T)
. Let
(u,v)\in\textbf{r}_{T}(a)\subseteq\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
. Then either
u=v
, in which case we are done, or there exists an
X
-sequence

u=p_{1}c_{1},\ q_{1}c_{1}=p_{2}c_{2},\ \dots\ ,\ q_{n}c_{n}=v.

Let
u=q_{0}
and
1=c_{0}
. Consider
0\leq i\leq n-1
, and suppose that
q_{i}\in T
and
c_{i}\in T^{1}
. Then
p_{i+1}c_{i+1}=q_{i}c_{i}\in T.
Since
S{\setminus}T
is an ideal, it follows that
p_{i+1}\in T
and
c_{i+1}\in T^{1}
, and
aq_{i+1}=ap_{i+1}\in T,
so that
q_{i+1}\in T.
Therefore, by finite induction, we have
p_{i},q_{i}\in T
and
c_{i}\in T^{1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus, the above
X
-sequence is a
Y
-sequence, as required.
∎

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
S
be a semigroup. Then
S^{0}
is FRE if and only if
S
is FRE and
S=US^{1}
for some finite set
U\subseteq S.

Proof.

Observe that either
S=S^{0}
or
S
is a subsemigroup of
S^{0}
whose complement is an ideal. Thus, using Proposition
4.16
if necessary, if
S^{0}
is FRE then so is
S.

Now, by Lemma
4.7
, the right annihilator congruence
\textbf{r}_{S^{0}}(0)
is finitely generated if and only if
S^{0}=V(S^{0})^{1}
for some finite set
V\subseteq S^{0}
, which is clearly equivalent to
S=US^{1}
for some finite set
U\subseteq S.
The result now follows from Proposition
4.14
.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

A monoid
S
is FRE if and only if
S^{0}
is FRE.

Remark 4.19
.

It follows from Proposition
4.17
that the class of FRE
semigroups
is not closed under adjoining a zero.

Moving on to ideals, the proof of Counterexample
4.9
yields:

Counterexample 4.20
.

The class of FRE semigroups is not closed under ideals.

However, if an ideal has an identity element then it forms a retract of the oversemigroup. Thus, by Proposition
4.12
, we have:

Proposition 4.21
.

A monoid ideal of an FRE semigroup is FRE.

A semigroup may not be FRE even if both an ideal and the associated Rees quotient
are
FRE:

Counterexample 4.22
.

There exists a semigroup
S
with ideal
I
such that
I
and
S/I
are FRE but
S
is not.

Proof.

Consider the semigroup

T
as defined in Counterexample
4.13
. Then
I=\{a,0\}
is an ideal of
T
that is clearly FRE, and
T/I\cong F^{0}
is FRE. However, we have shown
T
is not FRE.
∎

4.3.
Direct products

If a direct product is FRE then so are the direct factors:

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups. If
S\times T
is FRE then so are
S
and
T
.

Proof.

Clearly it suffices to prove that
S
is FRE, and we do so using Proposition
4.11
. Let
\pi:S\times T\to T
be the projection map, and pick any
b\in T.
Consider any
a\in S.
Clearly
\textbf{r}_{S\times T}((a,b))\pi\subseteq\textbf{r}_{S}(a),
and if
(s,s^{\prime})\in\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
then
((s,t),(s^{\prime},t))\in\textbf{r}_{S\times T}((a,b))
for any
t\in T.
Thus
\textbf{r}_{S\times T}((a,b))\pi=\textbf{r}_{S}(a),
as required.
∎

On the other hand, the direct product of two FRE semigroups need not be FRE.

Counterexample 4.24
.

The class of FRE semigroups is not closed under direct products.

Proof.

Let
S=\{a,0\}
be the two-element null semigroup and let
T
any infinite semigroup. Then, for any
x\in T
, any generating set of the right ideal
\textbf{C}(\textbf{r}_{S\times T}(a,x))S^{1}=S\times T
must contain
\{(a,t)\mid t\in T\}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.5
,
S\times T
is not FRE.
∎

We say that a semigroup
S
has
pairwise right identities
if for every pair
a,b\in S
there exists some
s\in S
such that
as=a
and
bs=b.
If
S
has pairwise right identities then it has
finite right identities
, that is, for any finite set
U\subseteq S
there is an element
s\in S
such that
us=u
for all
u\in U
. The following result provides necessary and sufficient conditions for the direct product of two semigroups that have pairwise right identities to be FRE.

Theorem 4.25
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups that have pairwise right identities. Then
S\times T
is FRE if and only if
S
and
T
are FRE and there exist finite sets
U\subseteq S
and
V\subseteq T
such that
S=US^{1}
and
T=VT^{1}
.

Proof.

The direct implication follows from Proposition
4.23
and Lemma
4.6
.

For the converse, consider
(a,b)\in S\times T
. By assumption, there exist finite sets
U\subseteq S
and
V\subseteq T
such that
S=US^{1}
and
T=VT^{1}
, and
\textbf{r}_{S}(a)
and
\textbf{r}_{T}(b)
are generated by some finite sets
X
and
Y
, respectively. We claim that
\textbf{r}_{S\times T}(a,b)
is generated by

\displaystyle Z=\{((p,p^{\prime}),(q,q^{\prime})),\,((u,p^{\prime}),(u,q^{\prime})),\,((p,v),(q,v))\hskip 1.42262pt|\hskip 1.42262pt(p,q)\in X,(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\in Y,u\in U,v\in V\}.

Indeed, let
((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\in\textbf{r}_{S\times T}(a,b)
. We may assume that
s\neq s^{\prime}
. Since
S
is FRE, there exists an
X
-sequence

s=p_{1}c_{1},\ q_{1}c_{1}=p_{2}c_{2},\ \dots\ ,\ q_{m}c_{m}=s^{\prime}.

We may assume that
c_{i}\in S
for each
1\leq i\leq m
; indeed, as
S
has pairwise right identities, we may replace any
c_{i}=1
with some
d_{i}\in S
such that
p_{i}d_{i}=p_{i}
and
q_{i}d_{i}=q_{i}
.

Suppose that
t\neq t^{\prime}
. Then, since
T
is FRE and has pairwise right identities, there exists a
Y
-sequence

t=p_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}^{\prime},\ q_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}^{\prime}=p_{2}^{\prime}c_{2}^{\prime},\ \dots\ ,\ q_{n}^{\prime}c_{n}^{\prime}=t^{\prime}

where
c_{i}^{\prime}\in T
for each

.
Let

For each
m\leq i\leq N
let
q_{i}=q_{m}
and
c_{i}=c_{m}
, and for each

let
q_{i}^{\prime}=q_{n}^{\prime}
and

.
Then we obtain a

-sequence

(s,t)=(p_{1},p_{1}^{\prime})(c_{1},c_{1}^{\prime}),\ (q_{1},q_{1}^{\prime})(c_{1},c_{1}^{\prime})=(p_{2},p_{2}^{\prime})(c_{2},c_{2}^{\prime}),\ \dots\ ,\ (p_{N},p_{N}^{\prime})(c_{N},c_{N}^{\prime})=(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

Now suppose that
t=t^{\prime}
. Since
T=VT^{1}
and
T
has pairwise right identities, it follows that
T=VT
, so that there exist
v\in V
and

such that

. We then have a

-sequence

(s,t)=(p_{1},v)(c_{1},t^{\prime\prime}),\ (q_{1},v)(c_{1},t^{\prime\prime})=(p_{2},v)(c_{2},t^{\prime\prime}),\ \dots\ ,\ (q_{m},v)(c_{m},t^{\prime\prime})=(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.26
.

Let
S
and
T
be monoids. Then
S\times T
is FRE if and only if
S
and
T
are FRE.

4.4.
Free products

We begin this subsection by providing necessary and sufficient conditions for a semigroup free product to be FRE.

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
\{S_{i}\mid i\in I\}
be a collection of semigroups. Then the semigroup free product
F=\prod^{\ast}\{S_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is FRE if and only if each
S_{i}
(
i\in I
) is FRE and one of the following holds:

(1)

each
S_{i}

Theorem 4.30

For

We begin by showing that

(1)

Suppose for finite induction that

There is an

to

Let us write this sequence as

where
(p_{v},q_{v})\in X_{\ell+1}
and

for

. Putting

, it follows that there is an

t_{1}\ast t_{2}\ast\cdots\ast t_{\ell}\ast t_{\ell+1}\,\sim\,s_{1}\ast s_{2}\ast\cdots\ast s_{\ell}\ast s_{\ell+1},

Next, we note that for any

(2)

\textbf{a},\textbf{b}\in F

Theorem 5.2
.

For a semigroup

(1)

(2)

is WRC;

(3)

Remark 5.3

There exist WRC semigroups that are not FRE. An example of such a semigroup is any infinite left zero semigroup

Indeed,

Given Theorem

, we may henceforth, without loss of generality, focus on monoids. The positive results that follow in this section will be proved by juxtaposing the corresponding results from Sections

and

.

As free monoids are right coherent
[

and hence WRC, certainly the class of WRC monoids is not closed under homomorphic images. In fact, this class is not closed under Rees quotients:

Counterexample 5.4
.

The class of WRC monoids is not closed under Rees quotients.

Proof.

Let
S
be the free semigroup on an infinite set and let

, so that

is the free monoid on the same set. Certainly

is WRC. With

as in Counterexample

, recall that

be a non-zero element. Then

may not be finitely generated and hence, by Lemma
4.5
and Theorem

,

is not WRC.
∎

On the other hand, marrying Corollaries
3.5
and
4.12
yields:

Theorem 5.5
.

The class of WRC monoids is closed under retracts.

Corollary 5.6
.

A monoid ideal of a WRC monoid is WRC.

Counterexample 5.7
.

The class of WRC monoids is not closed under small extensions or large submonoids.

Proof.

Let

Since

is

Recall that

with operation, extending that of

Since the principal right ideals

6.
Axiomatisatability

In this final section we provide a survey of connections between axiomatisability of certain classes of monoid acts and each of the properties of being RIH, FRE and WRC. We begin by establishing the necessary definitions in model theory; we direct the reader to
[
5
]
for further background. For basic definitions about acts, the reader should consult
[
17
]
.

Any class of algebras
\mathcal{A}
of a given fixed type
\tau
has an
associated first order language
L^{\tau}
. If
C
is a member of
\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi
is a sentence of

L^{\tau}
then we say that
C
is a
model
of
\varphi
and write
C\models\varphi
if
\varphi
is a true statement
in
C
. Similarly, if
\Pi
is a set of sentences of
L^{\tau}
, then we say that
C
is a
model
of
\Pi
and write
C\models\Pi
if

C\models\varphi
for every
\varphi\in\Pi
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of algebras of type
\tau
. A subclass
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{A}
is
axiomatisable
if there exists a set of sentences
\Pi
of
L^{\tau}
such that for
C\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
C\in\mathcal{B}
if and only if
C\vDash\Pi
.

Equivalent formulations of the properties of being WRC and being FRE in terms of axiomatisability can be deduced from the work of
[
10
]
, as follows.

First, recall that the right ideals of a monoid
S
are precisely the subacts of the right
S
-act
S.

A right
S
-act
A
is said to be
\alpha
-injective
if for every
S
-homomorphism
\theta:I\rightarrow A
where
I
is an
n
-generated right ideal of
S
with
n<\alpha
, there exists an
S
-homomorphism
\psi:S\rightarrow A
such that
\psi|_{I}=\theta
. (A right
S
-act is
n
-generated
if it has a generating set of size
n.
)

Theorem 6.2
.

[
10
, Theorem 3]

Let
\alpha\leq\aleph_{0}
be a cardinal. The following are equivalent for a monoid
S
:

(1)

the class of
\alpha
-injective right
S
-acts is axiomatisable;

(2)

the class of
\alpha
-injective right
S
-acts is closed under ultraproduct;

(3)

for each
n<\alpha,
the kernel of every
S
-homomorphism from the
n
-generated free right
S
-act to
S
is finitely generated.

By Theorem
6.2
and
[
10
, Corollary 4.2]
, we have:

Theorem 6.3
.

The following are equivalent for a monoid
S
:

(1)

the class of
\aleph_{0}
-injective right
S
-acts is axiomatisable;

(2)

the class of
\aleph_{0}
-injective right
S
-acts is closed under ultraproduct;

(3)

for each
n<\aleph_{0},
the kernel of every
S
-homomorphism from the
n
-generated free right
S
-act to
S
is finitely generated;

(4)

S
is WRC.

The right annihilator congruences of a monoid
S
are precisely the kernels of the
S
-endomorphisms of
S.

Indeed, for any element
a\in S
there is a natural
S
-endomorphism
\lambda_{a}:S\to S
given by
s\lambda_{a}=as,
the kernel of which is
\textbf{r}_{S}(a).
Conversely, the kernel of any
S
-endomorphism
\theta
of
S
coincides with
\ker\lambda_{1\theta}=\textbf{r}_{S}(1\theta)
.
Moreover, it follows from
[
18
, Theorem 2]
that
\ker\lambda_{a}
is finitely generated if and only if the principal right ideal
aS
of
S
is finitely presented as a right
S
-act. Combining these facts with Theorem
6.2
in the case
\alpha=2
, we obtain:

Theorem 6.4
.

The following are equivalent for a monoid
S
:

(1)

the class of
2
-injective right
S
-acts is axiomatisable;

(2)

the class of
2
-injective right
S
-acts is closed under ultraproduct;

(3)

every principal right ideal of

is finitely presented as a right
S
-act;

(4)

the kernel of every
S
-endomorphism of
S
is finitely generated;

(5)

S
is FRE.

We now turn our attention to the RIH property. Shaheen
[
19
]
showed that this property is equivalent to the axiomatisation of the class of
left
acts satisfying a certain property called Condition (W), which is related to
flatness

[
2
]
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
S
be a monoid.
A left
S
-act
A
satisfies
Condition (W)
if for any
s,t\in S
and
a,b\in A
, if
sa=tb
then there exist
p\in sS\cap tS
and
c\in A
such that
sa=tb=pc
.

Letting
\mathcal{W}_{S}
denote the class of left
S
-acts satisfying Condition (W), we have:

Theorem 6.6
.

[
19
, Theorem 5.13]
The following are equivalent for a monoid
S
:

(1)

the class
\mathcal{W}_{S}
is axiomatisable;

(2)

the class
\mathcal{W}_{S}
is closed under ultraproduct;

(3)

ultrapowers of
S
lie in
\mathcal{W}_{S}
;

(4)

S
is RIH.

This connection between the RIH property and a class of left acts is perhaps not surprising, given the very tight connections in the corresponding situation for rings and modules
[
4
]
.

Given a monoid
S,
flatness conditions for a left
S
-act
A
are defined in terms of the set-valued functor
-\;\!{\otimes_{S}}\,A:\mathbf{Act}_{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
,
where
\mathbf{Act}_{S}
denotes the category of right
S
-acts, preserving certain properties of morphisms. Flatness conditions can be formulated in terms of complicated sequences
of equalities in acts called
tossings
. However, some forms of flatness can be expressed more simply. For instance, the strongest notion of flatness, now referred to as
strong flatness
, was shown in
[
20
, Theorem 5.3]
to be equivalent to two conditions known as (P) and (E). Strongly flat acts are weakly flat, and an act is weakly flat precisely when it is principally weakly flat and satisfies Condition (W)
[
2
, Proposition 1.2]
.

Strongly flat, weakly flat and principally weakly flat acts all exist within the broader class of torsion-free acts; see
[
16
, Proposition 1.6]
.
A left
S
-act
A
is
torsion-free
if for any
a,b\in A
and for any right cancellative element
s\in S
the equality
sa=sb
implies
a=b
.
Using an example appearing in
[
17
, Remark III.8.3]
, we show that Condition (W) is independent of being torsion-free.

Example 6.7
.

Let
S=(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
be the natural numbers under multiplication and consider the amalgam
A=\mathbb{N}\coprod^{\mathbb{N}{\setminus}1}\mathbb{N}
. Let us write
A=\{x,y\}\sqcup\mathbb{N}{\setminus}\{1\}
, where
x
and
y
represent the two distinct copies of
1
. Then
A
is not torsion-free because
2x=2=2y
but
x\neq y
. However, we claim that
A
satisfies Condition (W). Indeed, suppose that
s,t\in S
and
a,b\in A
are such that
sa=tb
. If
sa=tb=x
, then we are forced to have
s=t=1
and
a=b=x
, so that the claim holds with
p=s
and
c=x
; similarly if
sa=tb=y
. Otherwise, there exists
c\in A
such that
sa=tb=pc
where
p=\text{lcm}(s,t)
.

Abstract
. The study of Lipschitz equivalence of fractals is a very active topic in recent years. It is natural to ask when two fractal sets are strictly Hölder equivalent. In the present paper, we completely characterize the strict Hölder equivalence for two classes of self-similar sets: the first class is totally-disconnected fractal cubes and the second class is self-similar sets with two branches which satisfy the strong separation condition.

Two metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are said to be
strictly Hölder equivalent
, denoted by

X\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}Y,

if there is a bijection
f:~X\to Y
, and constants
s,C>0
such that

(1.1)

C^{-1}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})^{s}\leq d_{Y}\big(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\big)\leq Cd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})^{s},~~~~\forall~~x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

In this case, we say
f
is a
bi-H
\ddot{o}
lder map
with index
s
.
If
s=1
, we say
X
and
Y
are
Lipschitz equivalent
, denoted by
X\sim Y
, and call
f
a
bi-Lipschitz map
.

Remark 1.1
.

We remark that two metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are said to be
Hölder equivalent
,
if there is a bijection
f:~X\to Y
, and constants
0<s<1,C>0
such that

(1.2)

C^{-1}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})^{1/s}\leq d_{Y}\big(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\big)\leq Cd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})^{s},~~~~\forall~~x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

Clearly strict Hölder equivalence implies Hölder equivalence. For Hölder equivalence of fractal sets, see

A family of contractions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
an
iterated function system
(IFS).
The unique nonempty compact set
K
satisfying

K=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}(K)
is called the attractor of the IFS.
If
\varphi_{i}(K)\cap\varphi_{j}(K)=\emptyset
provided
i\neq j
, then we say the IFS (or
K
)
satisfies the
strong separation condition
.

If the contractions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
are all similitude, then the attractor is called a
self-similar set
defined by the IFS (cf.
[
5
]
).
In particular, if

\left\{\varphi_{j}\left({x}\right)=\frac{x+d_{j}}{n}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}

where
n\geq 2,d\geq 1
are integers and
{\mathcal{D}}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{N}\}\subset\{0,\dots,n-1\}^{d}
, then
we denote the attractor by
K=K(n,{\mathcal{D}})
and call it a
fractal cube
, see
[
6
,
17
]

Rao, Ruan and Wang
[

have characterized when two self-similar sets
with two branches are Lipschitz equivalent. Based on their result, we completely characterize when two self-similar sets with two branches are strictly Hölder equivalent.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1},r_{2})
and
F\in{\mathcal{S}}(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
and
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
.
Then

(i) If
\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F

if and only if

\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log t_{1}}=\frac{\log r_{2}}{\log t_{2}}.

(ii) If
\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F

if and only if

\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log r_{2}}=\frac{2}{3}\text{and}\quad\frac{\log t_{1}}{\log t_{2}}=\frac{1}{5},

or the other round
\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log r_{2}}=\frac{1}{5},

\frac{\log t_{1}}{\log t_{2}}=\frac{2}{3}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The ratios of Theorem
1.3
arise from the algebraic constraints in the Lipschitz equivalence.

2.
Strict Hölder equivalence of fractal cubes

First, we recall some basic definitions and facts about symbolic spaces.

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer. Set

\Omega_{N}=\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}^{\infty}.

Let
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},{\mathbf{y}}=(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega_{N}
. Let
{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}
be the maximal common prefix of
{\mathbf{x}}
and
{\mathbf{y}}
. For a word
x_{1}\dots x_{n}
, we denote
|x_{1}\dots x_{n}|=n
to be its length.

Let
r\in(0,1)
. We define a metric
\rho_{r}
on
\Omega_{N}
as

\rho_{r}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=r^{|{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}|}.

Then
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
is a metric space. It is well known that the Hausdorff dimension of this space is

\dim_{H}(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})=\frac{\log N}{-\log r}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Two symbolic spaces
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
and
(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are Lipschitz equivalent if and only if

\frac{\log r}{\log r^{\prime}}=\frac{\log N}{\log N^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

We show that

Theorem 2.2
.

Two symbolic spaces
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
and
(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are strictly Hölder equivalent if and only if

\frac{\log N}{\log N^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

First, we claim that
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
and
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are strictly Hölder equivalent.
Let
f(x)=x
be the identity map from
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
to
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
.
Let
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\in\Omega_{N}
, then

\rho_{r}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=r^{|{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}|},\quad\rho_{r^{\prime}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=(r^{\prime})^{|{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}|}.

Therefore,

\rho_{r^{\prime}}(f({\mathbf{x}}),f({\mathbf{y}}))=\rho_{r}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})^{s}

where
s=\log r^{\prime}/\log r
. Our claim is proved.

Next, we will complete the proof of the theorem in two steps.

First, suppose
\frac{\log N}{\log N^{\prime}}=p/q\in\mathbb{Q}
, where
p,q
are coprime integers, then
N^{q}=(N^{\prime})^{p}
. Therefore,

(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})\sim(\Omega_{N^{q}},\rho_{r^{q}})\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}(\Omega_{N^{q}},\rho_{(r^{\prime})^{p}})=(\Omega_{(N^{\prime})^{p}},\rho_{(r^{\prime})^{p}})\sim(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}}),

that is,
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
and
(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are strictly Hölder equivalent.

Secondly, suppose
(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r})
and
(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are strictly Hölder equivalent. Then
there exists
s,C>0
such that

C^{-1}r^{s|{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}|}\leq{r^{\prime}}^{|f({\mathbf{x}})\wedge f({\mathbf{y}})|}\leq Cr^{s|{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}|}.

It follows that

(\Omega_{N},\rho_{r^{s}})
and
(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{r^{\prime}})
are Lipschitz equivalent through the identity map. So by Theorem
2.1
, we have

\frac{\log N}{\log N^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
E=K(n,{\mathcal{D}})
be a totally disconnected fractal cube. Denote
N=\#{\mathcal{D}}
. Then

E\sim(\Omega_{N},\rho_{1/n})
.

Now we are in the position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
E=K(n,{\mathcal{D}})
and
F=K(n^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.1
,

E\sim(\Omega_{N},\rho_{1/n})
and
F\sim(\Omega_{N^{\prime}},\rho_{1/n^{\prime}})
. Hence, the theorem follows from
Theorem
2.2
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3

In this section, we investigate strict Hölder equivalence between
two self-similar sets satisfying the strong separation condition.

Given
N\geq 2
and
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})\in(0,1)^{N}
, we define

\mathbf{r}_{a_{1}\dots a_{k}}=r_{a_{1}}\cdots r_{a_{k}}

for
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
.
We define a metric
\rho_{\mathbf{r}}
on
\Omega_{N}
as follows:

(3.1)

\rho_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\mathbf{r}_{{\mathbf{x}}\wedge{\mathbf{y}}},\quad{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\in\Omega_{N}.

The following result is folklore.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})
, then

E\sim(\Omega_{N},\rho_{\mathbf{r}})
.

The following lemma is obvious, and we omit its proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s>0
. Then

(\Omega_{N},\rho_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{N})})\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}(\Omega_{N},\rho_{(r_{1}^{s},\dots,r_{N}^{s})}).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
.

Let
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})
and
F\in{\mathcal{S}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})
.
Let
s=\dim_{H}E/\dim_{H}F
. Let
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1}^{s},\dots,r_{m}^{s})
.

Suppose
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F
. Then by Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we have

(3.2)

E^{\prime}\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}(\Omega_{m},\rho_{(r_{1}^{s},\dots,r_{m}^{s})})\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}(\Omega_{m},\rho_{(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})})\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}E.

It follows that
E^{\prime}\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F
. Finally, since
\dim_{H}E^{\prime}=\dim_{H}F
, we obtain that
E^{\prime}\sim F
.

Suppose
E^{\prime}\sim F
. Again by (
3.2
), we have
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F
.
The theorem is proved.
∎

Rao, Ruan and Wang
[
11
]
completely characterized when two self-similar sets with two branches and satisfying
the strong separation condition are Lipschitz equivalent.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1},r_{2})
and
F\in{\mathcal{S}}(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Assume
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
and
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
without loss of generality.
Then

(i) If
\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
E{\sim}F

if and only if

r_{1}=t_{1}\text{ \ and \ }r_{2}=t_{2}.

(ii) If
\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
E{\sim}F

if and only if there is a real number
0<\lambda<1
such that

r_{1}=\lambda^{2},r_{2}=\lambda^{3},t_{1}=\lambda,t_{2}=\lambda^{5},

or the other round
t_{1}=\lambda^{2},t_{2}=\lambda^{3},r_{1}=\lambda,r_{2}=\lambda^{5}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
.

Let
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1},r_{2})
and
F\in{\mathcal{S}}(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Assume
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
and
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
without loss of generality. Let
s=\dim_{H}E/\dim_{H}F
.

Case 1.

\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Suppose
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F
. Let
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1}^{s},r_{2}^{s})
, then

E^{\prime}\sim F
by Theorem
1.2
. So, by Theorem
3.1
, we have

r_{1}^{s}=t_{1}\text{ and }r_{2}^{s}=t_{2},

which imply that
\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log t_{1}}=\frac{\log r_{2}}{\log t_{2}}=s
.

On the other hand, suppose
\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log t_{1}}=\frac{\log r_{2}}{\log t_{2}}=\delta
.
Then
r_{1}=t_{1}^{\delta}
and
r_{2}=t_{2}^{\delta}
.
Let
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(t_{1}^{\delta},t_{2}^{\delta})
, then

E\sim F^{\prime}\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F,

where the last relation is due to Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
. The theorem is proved in this case.

Case 2.

\log r_{1}/\log r_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Suppose
E\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}F
. Let
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1}^{s},r_{2}^{s})
, then

E^{\prime}\sim F
by Theorem
1.2
. By Theorem
3.1
, we have

(3.3)

r_{1}^{s}=\lambda^{2},\quad r_{2}^{s}=\lambda^{3},\quad t_{1}=\lambda,\quad t_{2}=\lambda^{5},

or the other round, it follows that

(3.4)

\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log r_{2}}=\frac{2}{3},\quad\frac{\log t_{1}}{\log t_{2}}=\frac{1}{5},

or the other round
\frac{\log r_{1}}{\log r_{2}}=\frac{1}{5},

\frac{\log t_{1}}{\log t_{2}}=\frac{2}{3}
.

On the other hand, suppose (
3.4
) holds.
Then (
3.3
) holds for some
s>0
.
Let
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(r_{1}^{s},r_{2}^{s})
, then

F\sim E^{\prime}\overset{\text{H\"{o}lder}}{\sim}E.

The theorem is proved.
∎

1.
Introduction

We write
:=
to indicate that the left-hand side is defined by the right-hand side.

1.1.
Function spaces

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the unit disk and let

\mathbb{T}:=\partial\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
be the unit circle.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of analytic functions

f
in
\mathbb{D}
with finite norm (see, e.g.,
[
9
, p. 48]
)

(1.1)

\|f\|_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(r,f),

where

(1.2)

M_{p}(r,f):=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}.

For
f\in H^{p}
, the function
r\mapsto M_{p}(r,f)
is nondecreasing. It follows from the
Minkowski inequality for
p\geq 1
that
\|\cdot\|_{H^{p}}
is a norm, and
H^{p}
is complete
with respect to this norm; hence
H^{p}
is a Banach space. If
p<q
, then

H^{q}\subset H^{p}
.

Given a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
and
0<p<\infty
, we write

L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)
for the space of Borel measurable functions

f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)<\infty.

When
f
is analytic (in particular,
f\in H^{p}
), this integral is well-defined since analytic functions are measurable.

For an analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
and a point
a\in\mathbb{D}
,
the hyperbolic translate of
f
is defined by

f_{a}(z):=f\!\left(\frac{z+a}{1+\overline{a}z}\right)-f(a).

The space
\mathrm{BMOA}
consists of those functions
f\in H^{2}
such that

\|f\|_{*}:=|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\|f_{a}\|_{H^{2}}<\infty.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{BMOA}\subset H^{p}
for every
1\leq p<\infty
.
The subspace
\mathrm{VMOA}
is defined as the closure of the polynomials in

\mathrm{BMOA}
, or equivalently, as the collection of functions
f\in\mathrm{BMOA}

satisfying
\|f_{a}\|_{H^{2}}\to 0
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
.

Recall that a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson measure
(for
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
) if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(S(I))\leq C\,|I|

for every arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
, where
|I|:=m(I)/(2\pi)
denotes the normalized arc length
(
m
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
), and

S(I):=\{\,re^{i\theta}:\ e^{i\theta}\in I,\ 1-|I|\leq r<1\,\}

is the associated Carleson box
[
9
, Ch. II]
. For more illustration, see Figure
1
below.
We say that
\mu
is a vanishing Carleson measure if

\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }|I|\to 0,

uniformly with respect to the position of
I
on
\mathbb{T}
(equivalently, the supremum over all arcs
I

with
|I|\leq\delta
tends to
0
as
\delta\to 0
); see
[
6
,
16
]
. On the other hand, it is well known that
\mu
is a Carleson measure if and only if

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq C_{p}\,\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p},\quad 0<p<\infty,

for all
f\in H^{p}
; see
[
9
, p. 231]
or
[
6
, Theorem 9.3]
.

In terms of Carleson measures, for an analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
,
the condition
f\in\mathrm{BMOA}
is equivalent to requiring that the measure

|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})\,dA(z)

is a Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
, where
dA(z)=({1}/{\pi})dxdy
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
Similarly,
f\in\mathrm{VMOA}
if and only if this measure is a vanishing Carleson
measure; see
[
2
]
and
[
9
, p. 262]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the Bloch space of analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}:=|f(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

The closure of the polynomials in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}}
is called the little Bloch space,
which is denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{0}
. We have
\mathrm{BMOA}\subset\mathcal{B}
and

\mathrm{VMOA}\subset\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

For
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, the weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
consists of
analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that (see, e.g.,
[
11
, p. 2]
)

(1.4)

\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,dA_{\alpha}(z)<\infty,\quad dA_{\alpha}(z):=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z).

A positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is called a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure
for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\leq C\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q}

for all
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}
. We say that
\mu
is a vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson measure
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\int_{S(I)}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q}

for all arcs
I
with
|I|<\delta
and all
f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}
with
\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\leq 1
, see
[
14
,
7
]
.

For the theory of Carleson measures on Bergman spaces, we refer to

[
10
,
13
,
19
]
.

1.2.
The higher-order Volterra-type integral operator

The classical Volterra-type integral operator with symbol
g
, introduced by Pommerenke
[
16
]
, is defined by

T_{g}[f](z):=\int_{0}^{z}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
f
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. Pommerenke proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{2}
if and only if
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
. The fundamental work of Aleman and Siskakis later established that, for
1\leq p<\infty
, the operator
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{p}
if and only if
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
, and compact on
H^{p}
if and only if
g\in\mathrm{VMOA}

[
2
]
. These results have generated extensive research on Volterra-type operators on Hardy, Bergman, and related spaces; see, for instance,
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
18
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we study a higher-order Volterra-type operator obtained by iterating the integration procedure. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and analytic functions
f
and
g
in
\mathbb{D}
, we define

(1.5)

T_{g,n}[f](z):=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}}_{n\ \text{times}}f(t_{n})g^{\prime}(t_{n})\,dt_{n}\cdots dt_{1},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Here, each integral is taken along the line segment from
0
to the upper limit; equivalently, since the integrand is analytic, the value is independent of the choice of rectifiable path in
\mathbb{D}
. This operator admits the equivalent convolution representation

(1.6)

T_{g,n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{\,n-1}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and clearly reduces to
T_{g}
when
n=1
. This generalization was introduced in
[
12
]
.

A useful structural feature of
T_{g,n}
is its factorization through the classical integration operator. Let

V\varphi(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\varphi(t)\,dt

and denote by
V^{k}
the
k
-fold integration operator

(1.7)

V^{k}\varphi(z)=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{z}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}}_{k\ \text{times}}\varphi(t_{k})\,dt_{k}\cdots dt_{1}.

Using the same method as in
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
, one obtains the convolution formula

(1.8)

V^{k}\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{k-1}\varphi(t)\,dt.

If we use the iterated integral representation (
1.5
) (or by induction on
n
), then we verify the factorization

(1.9)

T_{g,n}=V^{\,n-1}\circ T_{g,1}.

Another useful representation of
T_{g,n}
involves reduced operators. For
0\leq k\leq n-1
, define

(1.10)

A_{k,g}[f](z):=\int_{0}^{z}t^{k}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Using the binomial expansion of
(z-t)^{n-1}
in the convolution representation (
1.6
), one can express
T_{g,n}
as a finite linear combination

(1.11)

T_{g,n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}z^{n-1-k}\,A_{k,g}[f](z).

This decomposition is established in Lemma
2.3
below and provides an alternative approach to studying
T_{g,n}
through the simpler operators
A_{k,g}
.

2.
Boundedness and Compactness

As a first step, we show that the operator
T_{g,n}
is bounded. For this, we need the following lemma on the

k
-fold integration operator.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the
k
-fold integration operator

V^{k}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
, given by (
1.7
) (with integrals taken along the line segment from
0
to the upper limit),
is bounded and

\|V^{k}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=\frac{1}{k!}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in H^{p}
and
0<r<1
. Using the convolution representation (
1.8
) and evaluating along
the radial segment from
0
to
z=re^{i\theta}
(i.e.,
t=se^{i\theta}
,
0\leq s\leq r
), we obtain

\displaystyle V^{k}\varphi(re^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{re^{i\theta}}(re^{i\theta}-t)^{k-1}\varphi(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{r}(re^{i\theta}-se^{i\theta})^{k-1}\varphi(se^{i\theta})\,e^{i\theta}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ik\theta}}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{k-1}\varphi(se^{i\theta})\,ds.

Applying Minkowski’s integral inequality, which requires
p\geq 1
, along with the identity mentioned above, yields

\displaystyle M_{p}(r,V^{k}\varphi)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl|V^{k}\varphi(re^{i\theta})\bigr|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigl|\varphi(se^{i\theta})\bigr|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{k-1}M_{p}(s,\varphi)\,ds.

Since
M_{p}(s,\varphi)
is nondecreasing in
s
and
M_{p}(s,\varphi)\leq\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}}
for all
0<s<1
,
we obtain

\displaystyle M_{p}(r,V^{k}\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}}}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{r}(r-s)^{k-1}\,ds=\frac{r^{k}}{k!}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{k!}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}}.

Taking the supremum over
0<r<1
gives

\|V^{k}\varphi\|_{H^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{k!}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}},\quad\text{hence}\quad\|V^{k}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{k!}.

To obtain the lower bound, take
\varphi\equiv 1
. Then
\|\varphi\|_{H^{p}}=1
and

V^{k}1(z)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{k-1}\,dt=\frac{z^{k}}{k!}.

Moreover,
\|z^{k}\|_{H^{p}}=1
for
k\geq 0
since
M_{p}(r,z^{k})=r^{k}
and
\sup_{0<r<1}r^{k}=1
.
Therefore,

\|V^{k}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\geq\|V^{k}1\|_{H^{p}}=\frac{1}{k!}.

Combining the upper and lower bounds yields
\|V^{k}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=1/k!
. The proof is now complete.
∎

We now estimate the norm of the operator
T_{g,n}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.
Then

(2.1)

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq C_{n}\,\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}},\quad\text{where}\quad C_{n}:=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}.

Moreover, the constant
C_{n}
is sharp in the sense that it cannot be improved
by the factorization method.

Proof.

By the factorization (
1.9
), namely
T_{g,n}=V^{\,n-1}\circ T_{g,1}
,
and the submultiplicativity of operator norms, we obtain

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=\|V^{\,n-1}\circ T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq\|V^{\,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\,\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}.

By Lemma
2.1
,
\|V^{\,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=1/(n-1)!
, and therefore

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}},

which proves (
2.1
).

To see that
C_{n}=1/(n-1)!
is optimal within the factorization approach, observe that
the factorization (
1.9
) is exact and that the norm

\|V^{\,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=1/(n-1)!
is attained (Lemma
2.1
).
Hence, any bound derived solely from (
1.9
) together with
submultiplicativity must contain this factor and therefore cannot yield a smaller
constant.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
, then

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\lesssim_{n,p}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMOA}}.

Proof.

If
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
, then by the Aleman–Siskakis theorem
[
2
]
,

\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\lesssim_{p}\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMOA}}.

Combining this with Lemma
2.2
yields the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Corollary
2.1
provides a sufficient condition for the boundedness
of
T_{g,n}
on
H^{p}
. For
n\geq 2
, this condition is not necessary in general,
and sharper symbol characterizations can be obtained by viewing
T_{g,n}
as a
special case of generalized integration operators.

Proposition 2.1
.

For all analytic functions
f,g
in
\mathbb{D}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2.2)

\frac{d^{\,n}}{dz^{\,n}}T_{g,n}[f](z)=f(z)\,g^{\prime}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

Let
h(t):=f(t)g^{\prime}(t)
. Using the convolution representation (
1.6
),

T_{g,n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{n-1}h(t)\,dt.

Differentiate under the integral sign (Leibniz’ rule). Since
(z-t)^{n-1}
vanishes at
t=z

whenever
n\geq 2
, no boundary term appears for the first
n-1
derivatives. In particular,
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{n-1}h(t)\,dt=\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-1-k)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{n-1-k}h(t)\,dt.

Taking
k=n-1
gives

\frac{d^{\,n-1}}{dz^{\,n-1}}T_{g,n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\cdot(n-1)!\int_{0}^{z}h(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{z}h(t)\,dt.

Differentiating once more proves (
2.2
).
∎

We are now ready to prove the first main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The operator
T_{g,n}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if the measure

(2.3)

d\mu_{g,n,p}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z)

is a Carleson measure for
H^{p}
.

Moreover,
T_{g,n}
is compact on
H^{p}
if and only if
\mu_{g,n,p}
is a vanishing Carleson measure.

Proof.

Boundedness.

Assume that
\mu_{g,n,p}
defined in (
2.3
) is a Carleson measure for
H^{p}
. We show that
T_{g,n}
is bounded on
H^{p}
.

First, we recall the Hardy-Sobolev norm equivalence for analytic functions in
H^{p}
. For any analytic function
F
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
and for every integer
n\geq 1
, the following equivalence holds:

(2.4)

\|F\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp_{p,n}|F(0)|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z),

which is a standard result for Hardy spaces (see
[
20
, Proposition 6.2]
). The term
|F^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}
can be interpreted as the contribution from the boundary behavior of
F
and its derivatives. Combining (
2.4
) with
Proposition
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\|T_{g,n}[f]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\asymp_{p,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(T_{g,n}[f])^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z).

By the Carleson embedding theorem (
1.3
),

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p},

and therefore
\|T_{g,n}[f]\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}}
, proving boundedness.

Conversely, assume that
T_{g,n}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded. Let
I\subset\mathbb{T}
be an arc and
S(I)
its Carleson box.
Choose
a\in\mathbb{D}
such that
1-|a|\asymp|I|
and
a/|a|
lies above the midpoint of
I
, see Figure
1
.

Consider the standard normalized test function

k_{a}(z):=\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2}}\right)^{1/p}.

\|k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}\asymp 1

z\in S(I)

(2.5)

\|T_{g,n}[k_{a}]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\|k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp 1.

Using (
2.4
) and Proposition
2.1
again gives

\displaystyle\|T_{g,n}[k_{a}]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\asymp_{p,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{S(I)}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z).

z\in S(I)
we also have
1-|z|^{2}\asymp|I|

),

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|T_{g,n}[k_{a}]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{g,n,p}(S(I))}{|I|}.

Thus,
\mu_{g,n,p}(S(I))\lesssim|I|
uniformly in
I
, so
\mu_{g,n,p}
is a Carleson measure.

Compactness.

Assume first that
\mu_{g,n,p}
is a vanishing Carleson measure. It is a standard fact that
vanishing Carleson measures are precisely those measures for which the embedding

H^{p}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mu)

is compact (see,
[
9
, Ch. 3]
).
Thus, if
\{f_{k}\}
is bounded in
H^{p}
and
f_{k}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, then

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{k}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z)\longrightarrow 0.

Using (
2.4
) and Proposition
2.1
as before (and
T_{g,n}=0
), we obtain

\|T_{g,n}[f_{k}]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp_{p,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{k}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z)\longrightarrow 0,

which shows that
T_{g,n}
is compact on
H^{p}
.

Conversely, suppose that
T_{g,n}
is compact on
H^{p}
.
Let
\{a_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
with
|a_{j}|\to 1
. Then
k_{a_{j}}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}

and
\|k_{a_{j}}\|_{H^{p}}\asymp 1
, hence compactness implies

\|T_{g,n}[k_{a_{j}}]\|_{H^{p}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Let
I_{j}
be the arc associated with
a_{j}
as in the boundedness part, so that
1-|a_{j}|\asymp|I_{j}|
and
S(I_{j})

is the corresponding Carleson box. Repeating the lower bound estimate above yields

\|T_{g,n}[k_{a_{j}}]\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\gtrsim\frac{\mu_{g,n,p}(S(I_{j}))}{|I_{j}|}.

Therefore,
\mu_{g,n,p}(S(I_{j}))/|I_{j}|\to 0
as
|I_{j}|\to 0
. Since the choice of arcs was arbitrary (up to uniform comparability),
this is exactly the vanishing Carleson condition. Hence,
\mu_{g,n,p}
is a vanishing Carleson measure.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For
n=1
one has the classical Aleman–Siskakis characterizations

T_{g,1}\ \text{bounded on }H^{p}\iff g\in\mathrm{BMOA},\quad T_{g,1}\ \text{compact on }H^{p}\iff g\in\mathrm{VMOA},

where
1\leq p<\infty
.
For
n\geq 2
, however, the additional smoothing produced by iterated integration
changes the behavior of
T_{g,n}
, and boundedness (compactness) can no longer be
captured solely by membership of the symbol
g
in
\mathrm{BMOA}
(
\mathrm{VMOA}
).

As a concrete illustration, consider

g(z)=\log\frac{1}{1-z},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
g^{\prime}(z)={1}/({1-z})
, so
g\in\mathcal{B}
but
g\notin\mathrm{BMOA}
.
Nevertheless,
T_{g,2}
is bounded on
H^{2}
.

Indeed, by Theorem
2.1
with
(n,p)=(2,2)
,
T_{g,2}:H^{2}\to H^{2}

is bounded if and only if the measure

d\mu_{g,2,2}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\,dA(z)

is a Carleson measure for
H^{2}
. Using the elementary inequalities

|1-z|\geq 1-|z|
and
1-|z|^{2}\leq 2(1-|z|)
, we obtain for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{|1-z|^{2}}\leq\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{3}}{(1-|z|)^{2}}\leq 8(1-|z|^{2}).

In particular,

|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{3}\lesssim(1-|z|^{2}).

Hence,

d\mu_{g,2,2}(z)\leq C\,(1-|z|^{2})\,dA(z).

Moreover, the measure
(1-|z|^{2})\,dA(z)
is a (indeed, vanishing) Carleson measure for

H^{2}
, since for every arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
with Carleson box
S(I)
one has

\int_{S(I)}(1-|z|^{2})\,dA(z)\lesssim|I|^{3}\leq|I|.

Therefore,
\mu_{g,2,2}
is a Carleson measure and
T_{g,2}
is bounded on
H^{2}
, despite

g\notin\mathrm{BMOA}
.

This example shows that the
\mathrm{BMOA}
or
\mathrm{VMOA}
criteria for the classical
Volterra operator
T_{g,1}
are specific to the case
n=1
and generally fail for
higher-order operators
T_{g,n}
.

Next, we present an alternative method to study
T_{g,n}
using the simpler operators
A_{k,g}
,
which will be useful for the second main result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f,g
be analytic on
\mathbb{D}
. Then, for every

z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(2.7)

T_{g,n}[f](z)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}\,z^{\,n-1-k}\,A_{k,g}[f](z),

where
A_{k,g}
is defined by (
1.10
).

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{D}
. By the binomial theorem,

(2.8)

(z-t)^{n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}z^{\,n-1-k}t^{k},\quad t\in\mathbb{C}.

Substituting (
2.8
) into the convolution representation
(
1.6
) yields

\displaystyle T_{g,n}[f](z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}(z-t)^{n-1}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{z}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}z^{\,n-1-k}t^{k}\right)f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt.

Since the sum is finite, we may interchange summation and integration to obtain

\displaystyle T_{g,n}[f](z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}z^{\,n-1-k}\int_{0}^{z}t^{k}f(t)g^{\prime}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}\,z^{\,n-1-k}\,A_{k,g}[f](z),

which is (
2.7
).
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, and assume that
for each integer
m\geq 0
the multiplication operator

M_{z^{m}}:Y\to Y,\quad(M_{z^{m}}F)(z)=z^{m}F(z),

is bounded. Then:

(a)

If
A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is bounded for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
, then
T_{g,n}:X\to Y
is bounded.

(b)

If
A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is compact for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
, then
T_{g,n}:X\to Y
is compact.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is bounded for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. By hypothesis,
M_{z^{n-1-k}}

is bounded on
Y
, hence the composition
M_{z^{n-1-k}}\circ A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is bounded.
Lemma
2.3
gives

T_{g,n}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}\,M_{z^{n-1-k}}\circ A_{k,g},

a finite linear combination of bounded operators. Therefore,
T_{g,n}:X\to Y
is bounded.

(b) Suppose
A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is compact for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Since
M_{z^{n-1-k}}
is
bounded on
Y
, the composition
M_{z^{n-1-k}}\circ A_{k,g}:X\to Y
is compact.
Using the same identity from Lemma
2.3
,
T_{g,n}
is a finite sum of compact
operators, hence compact. The proof is now finished.
∎

3.
Mapping into Bloch and Bergman spaces

In this section, we examine the higher-order Volterra-type integral operator
T_{g,n}
in relation to Bloch and Bergman spaces.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.

(i)

The operator
T_{g,1}:H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}
is bounded if and only if

(3.1)

\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}}\,|g^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

Moreover,

\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}\asymp_{p}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}.

(ii)

Let
n\geq 2
. If
T_{g,n-1}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded, then
T_{g,n}:H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}

is bounded and

(3.2)

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}\lesssim_{p}\|T_{g,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}.

In particular, if
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
, then for every
n\geq 2
,

\|T_{g,n}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}\lesssim_{p}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\,\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMOA}}.

Proof.

We write
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|(\cdot)^{\prime}(z)|
for the Bloch seminorm.
Since
T_{g,n}[f](0)=0
for all
n\geq 1
, boundedness into the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}

(with norm
|F(0)|+\|F\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
) is equivalent here to boundedness of the seminorm.

We first recall that for
0<p<\infty
and
f\in H^{p}
one has the growth estimate

|F(z)|\leq C_{p}\,\frac{\|F\|_{H^{p}}}{(1-|z|)^{1/p}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

see
[
6
, p. 36]
. Equivalently, since
1-|z|^{2}\asymp 1-|z|
,

(3.3)

|F(z)|\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\,\frac{\|F\|_{H^{p}}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{1/p}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Multiplying (
3.3
) by
(1-|z|^{2})
and using
(1-|z|^{2})^{1-1/p}\leq 1
gives

(3.4)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\,|F(z)|\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\|F\|_{H^{p}},\quad F\in H^{p}.

Proof of (i).

For
n=1
we have
(T_{g,1}[f])^{\prime}(z)=f(z)g^{\prime}(z)
, hence

\|T_{g,1}[f]\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f(z)g^{\prime}(z)|.

Assume (
3.1
) holds, then by (
3.3
),

(1-|z|^{2})|f(z)g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\frac{C^{\prime}_{p}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{1/p}}\|f\|_{H^{p}}\,|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\,\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}\,\|f\|_{H^{p}}.

Taking the supremum over
z
yields boundedness of
T_{g,1}:H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}
and

\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}\lesssim_{p}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}
.

Conversely, assume that
T_{g,1}:H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}
is bounded. For
a\in\mathbb{D}
consider the standard
Hardy test function

(3.5)

k_{a}(z):=\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2}}\right)^{1/p},

for which
\|k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}\asymp 1
and

|k_{a}(a)|=(1-|a|^{2})^{-1/p}
. Since the Bloch seminorm is a supremum over
\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|T_{g,1}[k_{a}]\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\geq(1-|a|^{2})\,|(T_{g,1}[k_{a}])^{\prime}(a)|

\displaystyle=(1-|a|^{2})\,|k_{a}(a)g^{\prime}(a)|=(1-|a|^{2})^{1-1/p}|g^{\prime}(a)|.

Taking the supremum over
a\in\mathbb{D}
gives (
3.1
) and

\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}\lesssim_{p}\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}}
.
This proves (i).

Proof of (ii).

Let
n\geq 2
. Differentiating the convolution representation (
1.6
) yields

(3.6)

(T_{g,n}[f])^{\prime}(z)=T_{g,n-1}[f](z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and therefore

\|T_{g,n}[f]\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\,|T_{g,n-1}[f](z)|.

If
T_{g,n-1}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded, then
T_{g,n-1}[f]\in H^{p}
, and by (
3.4
),

\|T_{g,n}[f]\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\leq C_{p}\|T_{g,n-1}[f]\|_{H^{p}}\leq C_{p}\,\|T_{g,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\,\|f\|_{H^{p}},

which implies (
3.2
).

Finally, if
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
, then
T_{g,1}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded (Aleman–Siskakis). Using the factorization

T_{g,n-1}=V^{\,n-2}\circ T_{g,1}
and Lemma
2.1
,

\|T_{g,n-1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq\|V^{n-2}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\,\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\,\|T_{g,1}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\lesssim_{p}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\,\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMOA}}.

Combining this with (
3.2
) completes the proof of (ii).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
define the Bloch-type seminorm by (
3.1
)
and let

\mathcal{B}_{p}:=\bigl\{g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\ \|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}<\infty\bigr\}.

Then
\{\mathcal{B}_{p}\}_{1\leq p<\infty}
forms an increasing scale:
if
1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
, then
\mathcal{B}_{p_{1}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{p_{2}}
. Indeed,
since
1-1/{p_{1}}<1-1/{p_{2}}
and
0<1-|z|^{2}<1
, we have

(1-|z|^{2})^{1-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}}
, hence

(1-|z|^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p_{2}}}|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}}|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p_{1}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Moreover, for every
1\leq p<\infty
one has
\mathcal{B}_{p}\subset\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the Bloch space.
Indeed, for
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p}
,

(1-|z|^{2})\,|g^{\prime}(z)|=(1-|z|^{2})^{1/p}\,(1-|z|^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}}|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{1/p}\,\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}},

since
(1-|z|^{2})^{1/p}\leq 1
. In particular,
\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p}}
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{B}_{p}\subset\mathcal{B}
is strict for
1<p<\infty
. For example,

g(z)=\log\frac{1}{1-z}

satisfies
g\in\mathcal{B}
(because
(1-|z|^{2})|g^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim 1
), but
g\notin\mathcal{B}_{p}
:
indeed, as
r\to 1^{-}
,

(1-r^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}}|g^{\prime}(r)|=(1-r^{2})^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{1}{1-r}\asymp(1-r)^{-1/p}\longrightarrow\infty.

Furthermore, at the endpoint
p=1
the condition becomes
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|<\infty
, i.e.
g
has bounded derivative on
\mathbb{D}
.
Thus, Theorem
3.1
(i) identifies
\mathcal{B}_{p}
as the sharp symbol class for the mapping

T_{g,1}:H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}
, whereas Theorem
3.1
(ii) shows that for
n\geq 2

the additional smoothing of iterated integration yields
H^{p}\to\mathcal{B}
boundedness under the weaker hypothesis
that
T_{g,n-1}
is bounded on
H^{p}
(for instance, under
g\in\mathrm{BMOA}
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. For each
a\in\mathbb{D}
, define the standard
Bergman test function

K_{a}(z):=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{2+\alpha}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and its
A_{\alpha}^{p}
-normalization

k_{a}(z):=\frac{K_{a}(z)}{\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}},\quad\text{so that}\quad\|k_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=1.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
. Then

|k_{a}(z)|\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)/p},\quad z\in D(a,r),

where

D(a,r):=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\ |z-a|<r(1-|a|)\}

is a Bergman ball and the implicit constants depend only on
\alpha,p
, and
r
(uniformly in
a\in\mathbb{D}
).

Proof.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
and let
z\in D(a,r)
. Since
1-|a|\leq 1-|a|^{2}
, we have

|z-a|<r(1-|a|)\leq r(1-|a|^{2})
. Using

1-\overline{a}z=(1-|a|^{2})-\overline{a}(z-a),

it follows that

|1-\overline{a}z|\geq(1-|a|^{2})-|a|\,|z-a|\geq(1-|a|^{2})-|z-a|\geq(1-r)(1-|a|^{2}),

and similarly

|1-\overline{a}z|\leq(1-|a|^{2})+|a|\,|z-a|\leq(1+r)(1-|a|^{2}).

Therefore, for
z\in D(a,r)
,

(3.7)

|K_{a}(z)|=\frac{1}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2+\alpha}}\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)}.

On the other hand, the expression for the norm of
K_{a}
is as

\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{p(2+\alpha)}}\,dA(z).

We apply the Möbius transformation
\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}
, which maps the unit disk onto itself. The derivative is

|\phi_{a}^{\prime}(z)|=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}},

so the area element transforms as

dA(z)=\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}\right)^{2}dA(w).

Using
z=\phi_{a}(w)
, the integral becomes

\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha}}{|1-\overline{a}\phi_{a}(w)|^{p(2+\alpha)}}\cdot\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}w|^{2}}\right)^{2}dA(w).

Near
w=0
, we have
|1-\overline{a}\phi_{a}(w)|^{p(2+\alpha)}\sim(1-|a|^{2})^{p(2+\alpha)}
, so the integrand simplifies to

\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}\sim(1-|a|^{2})^{(\alpha+2)-p(2+\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(w).

The remaining integral is a standard area integral, yielding

\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}\sim(1-|a|^{2})^{(\alpha+2)-p(2+\alpha)}.

Thus, the norm of the Bergman kernel
K_{a}
in the weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
behaves asymptotically as

(3.8)

\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}\sim(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}-(2+\alpha)}

Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), for
z\in D(a,r)
we obtain

|k_{a}(z)|=\frac{|K_{a}(z)|}{\|K_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}}\asymp\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)}}{(1-|a|^{2})^{(\alpha+2)/p-(2+\alpha)}}=(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)/p},

which completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to investigate the boundedness and compactness of the operator
T_{g,n}
on the weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and
\alpha,\beta>-1
satisfy the balance condition

(3.9)

\frac{q}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+2}{\alpha+2}.

Let
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is bounded;

(ii)

The measure

d\mu_{n}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z)

is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
.

Moreover,
T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is compact if and only if
\mu_{n}
is a vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

We apply standard Bergman–Sobolev estimates in conjunction with the
(p,q)
-Carleson embedding theorem.

Let
\beta>-1
,
1\leq q<\infty
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For every analytic function
F
on
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
F^{(k)}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
, we have the following Bergman–Sobolev norm equivalence by application of
[
20
, Proposition 6.2]
:

(3.10)

\|F\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp_{\beta,q,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z).

This equivalence allows us to relate the
A_{\beta}^{q}
-norm of
F
to the weighted
L^{q}
-norm of its derivatives.

For
F=T_{g,n}[f]
, the conditions
F^{(k)}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
hold because
T_{g,n}[f]
has a zero of order at least
n
at the origin, as seen from its iterated integral representation
(
1.5
)
.

By Proposition
2.1
, we know that
(T_{g,n}[f])^{(n)}=fg^{\prime}
. Combining this with
(
3.10
)
, we get

(3.11)

\|T_{g,n}f\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp_{\beta,q,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z).

Also, under the balance condition
(
3.9
)
, the
(p,q)
-Carleson embedding theorem (see
[
7
, p. 4]
,
[
10
]
) asserts that for a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
,

(3.12)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\leq C\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q}\quad\text{for all }f\in A_{\alpha}^{p},

if and only if
\mu
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
. Using
(
3.11
)
, we obtain

\|T_{g,n}f\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q}.

Thus,
T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is bounded.

Conversely, if
T_{g,n}
is bounded, then (
3.11
) implies

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\lesssim\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q},\quad f\in A_{\alpha}^{p},

and therefore
\mu_{n}
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure by (
3.12
).
This proves (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii).

Now, we will demonstrate the equivalence of compactness.
Assume
\mu_{n}
is a vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson measure. By the compactness of the embedding
A_{\alpha}^{p}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{D},\mu_{n})
(see
[
7
]
,
[
20
, Ch. 2]
), for every bounded sequence
\{f_{k}\}\subset A_{\alpha}^{p}
converging to
0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\to 0.

Using
(
3.11
)
, we conclude that
\|T_{g,n}f_{k}\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}\to 0
, implying that
T_{g,n}
is compact.

Conversely, assume
T_{g,n}
is compact. Let
k_{a}
denote the normalized reproducing kernel (or standard test function)
for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
with
\|k_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=1
. Then
k_{a}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets and
k_{a}\rightharpoonup 0

weakly as
|a|\to 1^{-}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
]
,
[
20
, Ch. 2]
), so compactness implies

\|T_{g,n}k_{a}\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }|a|\to 1^{-}.

By (
3.11
) and restricting to
D(a,r)
,

\|T_{g,n}k_{a}\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\geq\int_{D(a,r)}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z).

For
z\in D(a,r)
by Proposition
3.1
we have
|k_{a}(z)|\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)/p}
, hence

\int_{D(a,r)}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\gtrsim(1-|a|^{2})^{-q(2+\alpha)/p}\,\mu_{n}(D(a,r)).

Therefore,

\frac{\mu_{n}(D(a,r))}{(1-|a|^{2})^{q(2+\alpha)/p}}\lesssim\|T_{g,n}k_{a}\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }|a|\to 1^{-},

which is exactly the vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson condition. This completes the proof.
∎

As we see above, on Hardy spaces
H^{p}
, the
k
-fold integration operator
V^{k}
is bounded for every

1\leq p<\infty
, and in fact

\|V^{k}\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}=\frac{1}{k!},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

see Lemma
2.1
. Consequently, using the factorization

T_{g,n}=V^{\,n-1}\circ T_{g,1},

boundedness (respectively compactness) of
T_{g,1}
on
H^{p}
implies boundedness
(respectively compactness) of
T_{g,n}
on
H^{p}
for every
n\geq 1
, since
V^{n-1}
is
bounded and the composition of a bounded operator with a compact operator is compact.

In contrast, on weighted Bergman spaces the effect of integration is more naturally
captured by Bergman–Sobolev norm equivalences rather than by an

A_{\beta}^{q}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
mapping property. For example, if
F
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}

and
F(0)=0
, then

\|F\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+q}\,dA(z),\quad\beta>-1,\ 1\leq q<\infty,

and more generally, if
F^{(k)}(0)=0
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
, then

\|F\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{(n)}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z).

Since
(T_{g,n}f)^{(n)}(z)=f(z)g^{\prime}(z)
, boundedness of

T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is governed by the measure

d\mu_{n}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z),

namely by whether
\mu_{n}
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
(and similarly
for compactness via vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson measures), i.e. whether there exists
C>0

such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z)\leq C\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{q},\quad f\in A_{\alpha}^{p},

and vanishing is characterized by the corresponding smallness on Carleson boxes
S(I)

for short arcs
I\subset\mathbb{T}
.

The additional factor
(1-|z|^{2})^{nq}
reflects the full
n
-fold smoothing detected by
the Bergman–Sobolev norm, and it is precisely this explicit
n
-dependence that makes

(p,q)
-Carleson measure techniques a natural and sharp framework for higher-order
Volterra-type operators on weighted Bergman spaces.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
, and
r\in(0,1)
. Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
of the form

d\mu(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z),

where
g
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
, and
\mu
satisfies the
(p,q)
-Carleson measure condition for the Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
. Then, for every
a\in\mathbb{D}
, the following Carleson measure condition holds:

\mu(D(a,r))\leq C(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q(2+\alpha)}{p}},\quad a\in\mathbb{D},

where
D(a,r)
is a Bergman ball, and
C
is a constant depending on
r
.

Proof.

Since
D(a,r)
is a disk centered at
a
with radius
r(1-|a|)
, we have
|z-a|<r(1-|a|)
for all
z\in D(a,r)
, which implies
1-|z|^{2}\approx 1-|a|^{2}
within
D(a,r)
.
Thus, for
z\in D(a,r)
, we can approximate the integrand
(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}
as
(1-|a|^{2})^{\beta+nq}
. Consequently, we estimate the measure
\mu(D(a,r))
as

\mu(D(a,r))=\int_{D(a,r)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z).

For
z\in D(a,r)
, we approximate
1-|z|^{2}
by
1-|a|^{2}
, and we estimate the integral:

\mu(D(a,r))\sim(1-|a|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\int_{D(a,r)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}\,dA(z).

Now, using the fact that
D(a,r)
is a disk, the area element
dA(z)
scales with
(1-|a|^{2})
, and the measure can be bounded as follows:

\mu(D(a,r))\leq C(1-|a|^{2})^{\frac{q(2+\alpha)}{p}},

where
C
is a constant depending on
r
, but independent of
a
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and
\alpha,\beta>-1
satisfy

\frac{q}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+2}{\alpha+2}.

Then the boundedness (and likewise compactness) of

T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}

is, in general, not invariant under changes of the order
n
.

More precisely, there exist analytic symbols
g
and integers
n\geq 2
such that

T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}\ \text{is bounded (respectively compact),}

while

T_{g,1}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}\ \text{fails to be bounded (respectively compact).}

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, boundedness of
T_{g,n}
is equivalent to

\mu_{n}
being a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
, where

d\mu_{n}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z).

For
n\geq 2
,

d\mu_{n}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{q(n-1)}\,d\mu_{1}(z)\leq d\mu_{1}(z).

Hence, if
\mu_{1}
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure (respectively a vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson
measure), then so is
\mu_{n}
. Equivalently, boundedness (respectively compactness) of

T_{g,1}
implies boundedness (respectively compactness) of
T_{g,n}
for every
n\geq 2
.

To see that the converse may fail, fix
n\geq 2
and consider a symbol
g
with

g^{\prime}(z)=(1-z)^{-\gamma},\quad\gamma>0.

Choose
\gamma
so that

(3.13)

\frac{\beta+q+2}{q}-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}\ <\ \gamma\ \leq\ \frac{\beta+nq+2}{q}-\frac{2+\alpha}{p}.

Such a
\gamma
exists because
n\geq 2
makes the right endpoint larger than the left
endpoint by
n-1>0
.

Let
r\in(0,1)
be fixed and let
D(a,r)
denote the Bergman ball. For
a
tending to
1
nontangentially and
z\in D(a,r)

one has
|1-z|\asymp 1-|a|\asymp 1-|a|^{2}
, hence

|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}=|1-z|^{-\gamma q}\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-\gamma q},\quad z\in D(a,r),

and also
(1-|z|^{2})\asymp(1-|a|^{2})
on
D(a,r)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(D(a,r))

\displaystyle=\int_{D(a,r)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-\gamma q}\,(1-|a|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,A(D(a,r))\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{\beta+nq+2-\gamma q}.

By Proposition
3.2
, the
(p,q)
-Carleson condition for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
is equivalent to the estimate

\mu(D(a,r))\lesssim(1-|a|^{2})^{q(2+\alpha)/p}\quad(a\in\mathbb{D}),

and, hence
\mu_{n}
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure if and only if

\beta+nq+2-\gamma q\ \geq\ \frac{q(2+\alpha)}{p}.

Similarly,
\mu_{1}
is a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure if and only if

\beta+q+2-\gamma q\ \geq\ \frac{q(2+\alpha)}{p}.

By the choice (
3.13
),
\mu_{n}
is
(p,q)
-Carleson but
\mu_{1}
is not.
Therefore,
T_{g,n}
is bounded while
T_{g,1}
fails to be bounded (and hence is not
compact), which proves the stated non-invariance.

Finally, by choosing
\gamma
so that the inequality for
n
is strict (and still
fails for
n=1
), one obtains that
\mu_{n}
is a vanishing
(p,q)
-Carleson measure while

\mu_{1}
is not
(p,q)
-Carleson, yielding the compactness version as well.
∎

4.
Essential norm estimates

Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces.
For a bounded linear operator
T:X\to Y
between Banach spaces, its essential norm is

\|T\|_{e}^{X\to Y}:=\inf\{\|T-K\|^{X\to Y};\,K:X\to Y\ \text{is compact}\},

where
\|\cdot\|^{X\to Y}
is the operator norm, see
[
18
, p. 583]
or
[
15
]
.
It is well-known that
\|T\|_{e}^{X\to Y}=0
if and only if
T
is compact.

In this section, we compute the essential norms of the higher-order Volterra-type operators

T_{g,n}
on Hardy and weighted Bergman spaces. The results refine
Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
3.2
by providing
a sharp quantitative measure of the failure of compactness.

4.1.
The Hardy space case

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, recall that
k_{a}
is the test function (
3.5
) so that
\|k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}\asymp 1
and

k_{a}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
.

As a first step, we estimate the kernel test for the essential norm on
H^{p}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
T:H^{p}\to H^{p}
be bounded. Then

(4.1)

\|T\|_{e}^{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\ \asymp_{p}\ \limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|Tk_{a}\|_{H^{p}}.

Proof.

The lower bound follows since
k_{a}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
H^{p}
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
, hence

K(k_{a})\to 0
in
H^{p}
for every compact
K:H^{p}\to H^{p}
, and therefore

\|T-K\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\geq\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|(T-K)k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}=\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T(k_{a})\|_{H^{p}}.

Taking the infimum over a compact
K
gives

\|T\|_{e}^{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\gtrsim\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T(k_{a})\|_{H^{p}}
.

For the reverse inequality, one may use the standard compact approximation by dilations

P_{\rho}f(z)=f(\rho z)
,
0<\rho<1
, which are compact on
H^{p}
. Since
T(P_{\rho})
is compact,

\|T\|_{e}^{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\leq\|T-T(P_{\rho})\|_{H^{p}\to H^{p}}.

A routine localization argument (standard in essential norm estimates on Hardy spaces)
shows that
\|T-T(P_{\rho})\|
is controlled by
\sup_{|a|\geq r(\rho)}\|T(k_{a})\|
with

r(\rho)\to 1
as
\rho\to 1^{-}
, which yields the desired upper bound.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\mu
be a Carleson measure for
H^{p}
. Then

(4.2)

\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\ \asymp_{p}\ \limsup_{|I|\to 0}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|},

where
S(I)
denotes the Carleson box above an arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Fix an arc
I
and choose
a=a_{I}
as in the proof of Theorem
2.1

so that
1-|a|\asymp|I|
and
a/|a|
lies above the midpoint of
I
. Then

|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\asymp|I|^{-1}
on
S(I)
(cf. (
2.5
)), hence

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\ \geq\ \int_{S(I)}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\ \gtrsim\ \frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}.

Taking
\limsup
as
|I|\to 0
yields the lower bound in (
4.2
).

For the reverse inequality, one uses the pointwise estimate

|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\lesssim\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}

and the standard decomposition of

\mathbb{D}
into Carleson boxes adapted to
a
; together with the Carleson condition

\mu(S(J))\lesssim|J|
, this yields

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu(z)\ \lesssim\ \sup_{|J|\asymp 1-|a|}\frac{\mu(S(J))}{|J|}.

Taking
\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}
gives the upper bound.
∎

We are now prepared to estimate the essential norm of
T_{g,n}
on
H^{p}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.
Assume that
T_{g,n}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded and set

d\mu_{g,n,p}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-1}\,dA(z).

Then

(4.3)

\left(\|T_{g,n}\|_{e}^{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\right)^{p}\ \asymp_{p,n}\ \limsup_{|I|\to 0}\frac{\mu_{g,n,p}(S(I))}{|I|}.

In particular,
T_{g,n}
is compact on
H^{p}
if and only if the right-hand side of
(
4.3
) equals
0
.

Proof.

By the Hardy–Sobolev norm equivalence (
2.4
) and Proposition
2.1
,

\|T_{g,n}f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp_{p,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z),\quad f\in H^{p}.

Applying this to
f=k_{a}
gives

\|T_{g,n}k_{a}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp_{p,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z).

Taking
\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}
and using Lemma
4.1
yields

\left(\|T_{g,n}\|_{e}^{H^{p}\to H^{p}}\right)^{p}\asymp_{p,n}\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{p}\,d\mu_{g,n,p}(z).

Finally, Lemma
4.2
with
\mu=\mu_{g,n,p}
gives
(
4.3
).
∎

4.2.
The weighted Bergman space case

Fix
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. For each
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let
k_{a}
be the normalized
Bergman test function from Proposition
3.1
, so that

\|k_{a}\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}=1
and
k_{a}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets as
|a|\to 1^{-}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. If
T:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{p}
is bounded, then

(4.4)

\|T\|_{e}^{A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{p}}\ \asymp_{\alpha,p}\ \limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T(k_{a})\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}.

Proof.

This follows from the standard compactness test on
A_{\alpha}^{p}
together with the weak
convergence
k_{a}\rightharpoonup 0
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and let
\mu
be a
(p,q)
-Carleson measure for
A_{\alpha}^{p}
.
Fix
r\in(0,1)
and let
D(a,r)
be the Bergman ball. Then

(4.5)

\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\ \asymp_{\alpha,p,q,r}\ \limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\frac{\mu(D(a,r))}{(1-|a|^{2})^{q(2+\alpha)/p}},

where
k_{a}
is the normalized test function from Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

For
z\in D(a,r)
, Proposition
3.1
yields

|k_{a}(z)|\asymp(1-|a|^{2})^{-(2+\alpha)/p}
, hence

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\ \geq\ \int_{D(a,r)}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu(z)\ \gtrsim\ (1-|a|^{2})^{-q(2+\alpha)/p}\,\mu(D(a,r)),

which gives the lower bound in (
4.5
).
The reverse inequality follows from the standard localization estimates for normalized
kernels on weighted Bergman spaces and the
(p,q)
-Carleson property; see, e.g.,

[
20
, Ch. 2]
.
∎

Finally, we estimate the essential norm of
T_{g,n}
on weighted Bergman spaces.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, and
\alpha,\beta>-1
satisfy

\frac{q}{p}\leq\frac{\beta+2}{\alpha+2}.

Let
g
be analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, assume that
T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is bounded, and set

d\mu_{n}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta+nq}\,dA(z).

Fix
r\in(0,1)
. Then

(4.6)

\left(\|T_{g,n}\|_{e}^{A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{p}}\right)^{q}\ \asymp_{\alpha,\beta,p,q,n,r}\ \limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\frac{\mu_{n}(D(a,r))}{(1-|a|^{2})^{q(2+\alpha)/p}}.

In particular,
T_{g,n}:A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\beta}^{q}
is compact if and only if the right-hand side of
(
4.6
) equals
0
.

Proof.

By (
3.10
) and Proposition
2.1
,

\|T_{g,n}f\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp_{\beta,q,n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z),\quad f\in A_{\alpha}^{p}.

Applying this to
f=k_{a}
and taking
\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}
gives

\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T_{g,n}(k_{a})\|_{A_{\beta}^{q}}^{q}\asymp_{\alpha,\beta,p,q,n}\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z).

Using Lemma
4.3
(with target
A_{\beta}^{q}
) yields

\left(\|T_{g,n}\|_{e}^{A_{\alpha}^{p}\to A_{\alpha}^{p}}\right)^{\,q}\asymp_{\alpha,\beta,p,q,n}\limsup_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k_{a}(z)|^{q}\,d\mu_{n}(z).

Finally, Lemma
4.4
with
\mu=\mu_{n}
gives (
4.6
). The proof has now been completed.
∎

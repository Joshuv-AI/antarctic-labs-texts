The shape of differential radial flow v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{T}) , not its zero-crossing, carries physical information
Somadutta Bhatta, Aman Dimri, Jiangyong Jia

Abstract
Radial flow, a key collective phenomenon in heavy-ion collisions, manifests itself through event-by-event fluctuations of transverse-momentum ( p T p_{\mathrm{T}} ) spectra. The p T p_{\mathrm{T}} -differential radial flow observable, v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}}) , was introduced to quantify local spectral-shape fluctuations, but it is unavoidably influenced by global multiplicity fluctuations. Using the HIJING model, we show that different event-activity definitions for centrality classification and different spectral normalization schemes generate a constant vertical offset in v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}}) without altering its shape. This offset reflects the impact of residual volume/centrality fluctuations rather than genuine dynamical radial flow fluctuations. Accordingly, only the shape of v 0 ​ ( p T ) v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}}) , or equivalently its derivative d ​ v 0 ​ ( p T ) / d ​ p T dv_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})/dp_{\mathrm{T}} , carries physical information about radial-flow dynamics; its zero crossing does not. Practical implications include the need to vertically align measurements from different experiments before comparison, thereby removing normalization ambiguities when constraining QGP properties.

Impact of global multiplicity and spectral shape fluctuations on
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
.

Radial flow information is inferred from the correlation between
n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
and
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
, which can be expressed in a factorizable form
[
17
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}{\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}=v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})v_{0,p}\;,

(3)

where
v_{0,p}=\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}])^{2}\right\rangle_{A}}/\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle_{A}
is measured within a reference
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ref}}\in A
, and
v_{0}\{1\}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle
according to Eq. (
1
). Recent ATLAS measurements indicate that
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
remains approximately independent of the choice of
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ref}}

[
12
]
, consistent with the collective nature of radial flow.

The connection between
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
and local spectral shape fluctuations can be explicitly formulated as:

\displaystyle v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=\rho(n(p_{\mathrm{T}}),[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}}))^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}\;,

(4)

where
\rho(x,y)=\left\langle\delta x\delta y\right\rangle/\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta x)^{2}\right\rangle\left\langle(\delta y)^{2}\right\rangle}\in[-1,1]
is the Pearson correlation coefficient between
x
and
y
. Since
|v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})|\leq\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}}))^{2}\right\rangle}/\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle
,
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
captures only the component of spectral fluctuation that is correlated with
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
.

Having separated the spectral fluctuations into shape and multiplicity components (Eq. (
2
)), we can now state the central consequence of this decomposition. Consider an alternative definition of
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-differential radial flow using the unnormalized spectrum
N(p_{\mathrm{T}})
instead of
n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}{\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}=v_{0}^{\prime}(p_{\mathrm{T}})v_{0,p}\;.

(5)

Substituting Eq. (
2
) and using the approximation
\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=\left\langle Nn(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=\left\langle N\right\rangle\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle(1+\tau)
, where the correction term

\displaystyle\tau(p_{\mathrm{T}})=\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle/(\left\langle N\right\rangle\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle)

(6)

is small for sufficiently narrow centrality bins, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta v_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv v_{0}^{\prime}(p_{\mathrm{T}})-v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=\rho(N,[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta N)^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle N\right\rangle}\;.

(7)

This result demonstrates that the two definitions differ by a constant. The sign and magnitude of this offset depend on how the global multiplicity
N
correlates with
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
, which is sensitive to the specific centrality definition employed, for example, whether centrality is based on forward calorimeters or midrapidity charged particles
[
12
]
. In other words, this offset reflects the impact of volume or centrality fluctuations rather than genuine dynamical radial flow fluctuations.

Offset in
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
induced by the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-range choice.

While using the normalized spectrum
n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
appears useful to isolate spectral shape fluctuations from global multiplicity effects, this separation is non-unique. The key issue is that experimental measurements are always restricted to a finite
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range
R
. The fractional spectrum defined within this range,
n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=N(p_{\mathrm{T}})/N_{R}
where
N_{R}=\int_{R}N(p_{\mathrm{T}})dp_{\mathrm{T}}
, differs from the ideal full-range spectrum by a rescaling factor:
n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=n(p_{\mathrm{T}})N/N_{R}
(see Fig.
1
bottom). Even if the total multiplicity
N
were fixed, the multiplicity within the restricted range,
N_{R}
, would still fluctuate statistically due to the stochastic nature of particle production.

This residual fluctuation introduces an additional layer of ambiguity similar to Eq. (
2
), which can be decomposed as:

\displaystyle\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\approx\delta N_{R}\left\langle n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle+\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle\delta n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\;,

(8)

where
n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=N(p_{\mathrm{T}})/N_{R}
. Hence, applying the same logic, the
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
obtained in the full range and range
R
should also differ by a ’range-induced’ offset (see Fig.
1
bottom), whose value can be estimated as follows.

Assuming particles are independently sampled from the underlying momentum distribution,
N_{R}
follows a binomial distribution with sampling probability
\epsilon=\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle/\left\langle N\right\rangle
(we ignore the possible
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-dependent acceptance effect in the real experiment). The variance of
N_{R}
at fixed
N
is given by
\left\langle(\delta N_{R})^{2}\right\rangle_{N}=N(1-\epsilon)\epsilon\approx\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle(1-\epsilon)
. Applying Eq. (
7
), the
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
calculated for
R
, denoted
v_{0,R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
, differs from
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
defined in the full range by:

\displaystyle\Delta v_{0,R}

\displaystyle=v_{0,R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})-v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})

(9)

\displaystyle\approx\!\rho(N,[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\!\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta N)^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle N\right\rangle}\!-\!\rho(N_{R},[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\!\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta N_{R})^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle}

(10)

\displaystyle\approx-\left(\rho(N_{R},[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta N_{R})^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle}\right)_{N}

(11)

\displaystyle=-\left(\rho(N_{R},[p_{\mathrm{T}}])\sqrt{\frac{{(1-\epsilon)}}{N_{R}}}\right)_{N}\;,

(12)

where the subscript
N
denotes evaluation at fixed
N
. The approximation from Eq. (
10
) to Eq. (
11
) holds because changes in
N
induce proportional changes in
N_{R}
, such that the first term in Eq. (
10

This vertical offset translates into a horizontal shift in the zero-crossing point of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
from
p_{\mathrm{T}}^{0}\approx\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle

p_{\mathrm{T}}^{0}\approx\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle_{R}

3

p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{ref}}
range used to calculate

fluctuations unchanged due to sum rule constraints. Therefore, only the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-dependent variation of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
contains meaningful information about collective radial flow fluctuations.

Model setup.
To investigate the interplay between global multiplicity and spectral shape fluctuations, we employ the HIJING model
[
18
]
, which treats heavy-ion collisions largely as a superposition of independent
pp
collisions. The absence of genuine collective radial flow in HIJING, combined with the presence of high-
p_{\mathrm{T}}
particles from hard-scattered jets, provides an ideal framework for studying the non-flow baseline of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
and isolating the effects of multiplicity fluctuations. However, HIJING cannot validate the sensitivity of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
to genuine viscous or equation-of-state effects of QGP, since it contains no hydrodynamic expansion. Establishing the physical sensitivity of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
to transport coefficients requires comparison with hydrodynamic models, which is beyond the scope of the present study.

We generate Pb+Pb collisions at
\mbox{$\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}$}=5.02
TeV with a minimum hard-scattering scale of 2 GeV (parameter HIPR1(10)=2) to match LHC kinematics. Charged particles with
|\eta|<2.5
and

GeV are selected, consistent with typical experimental acceptances. Following the two-subevent method employed by ATLAS
[
12
]
and STAR
[
11
]
, we divide particles into two pseudorapidity regions:
-2.5<\eta_{a}<-\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
and
\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2<\eta_{b}<2.5
. We use two pseudorapidity gap sizes,
\eta_{\mathrm{gap}}=0
and 3, to evaluate the effects of short-range non-flow correlations and to assess possible longitudinal multiplicity decorrelation effects.

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left\langle\delta N_{a}\delta N_{b}\right\rangle}\sqrt{\left\langle\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{a}\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{b}\right\rangle}\sim\mbox{const}

Discussion and summary

Event-by-event fluctuations in the final particle spectrum
N(p_{\mathrm{T}})
serve as a sensitive probe of collective radial flow in the quark-gluon plasma. Experimentally, these fluctuations are accessed through the observable
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
(or
v_{0}^{\prime}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
), which characterizes the correlation between the normalized spectrum
n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
(or the unnormalized spectrum
N(p_{\mathrm{T}})
) and the event-by-event spectral shape, encoded in the average transverse momentum
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
. The spectral fluctuations can be decomposed into contributions from global multiplicity fluctuations and local spectral shape fluctuations:

\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})=\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\delta N+\left\langle N\right\rangle\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\;.

We demonstrate that the separation between
\delta N
and
\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is fundamentally ambiguous. Using HIJING simulations, we systematically varied the event activity definition, the spectral normalization range, and the reference range for
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
calculations. We found that
v_{0}^{\prime}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
based on the unnormalized spectrum
N(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is sensitive to the event activity variable and affected by autocorrelation effects when centrality and analysis particles overlap. In contrast,
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
based on the normalized spectrum
n(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is only sensitive to the normalization range. However, all formulations are related by a constant offset
\Delta v_{0}
that reflects the correlation between
\delta N
and
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
. Since the
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
fluctuations are insensitive to these constant offsets, only the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
-dependent variation of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
contains meaningful physical information about collective radial flow.

These findings have important practical implications. The zero-crossing point of
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is influenced by volume or centrality fluctuations rather than reflecting genuine dynamical radial flow fluctuations. When comparing
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
across experiments or between data and theory, curves must be vertically shifted to align their zero-crossing points, or corrected to a common normalization range using Eq. (
10
).

A practical universal convention would be to shift each
v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
measurement vertically so that its zero crossing coincides with the species- and energy-specific
\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
evaluated over the full
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range. This quantity is routinely published and provides a common, analysis-independent reference point. Direct comparison of absolute values without such corrections could be misleading, especially considering different experimental acceptances at the LHC (ALICE:
p_{\mathrm{T}}>0.1
GeV, CMS:
p_{\mathrm{T}}>0.3
GeV, ATLAS:
p_{\mathrm{T}}>0.5
GeV).

However, to eliminate the normalization ambiguity entirely, we propose the derivative observable:

\displaystyle v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=dv_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})/dp_{\mathrm{T}}\;.

(15)

According to Eq. (
3
),
v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
directly quantifies the correlation between the local spectral slope
s(p_{\mathrm{T}})=dn(p_{\mathrm{T}})/dp_{\mathrm{T}}/\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\approx d\ln n(p_{\mathrm{T}})/dp_{\mathrm{T}}
and
[p_{\mathrm{T}}]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\delta s(p_{\mathrm{T}})\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}{\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle}=v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})v_{0,p}\;.

(16)

Since constant offsets vanish upon differentiation,
v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is free from normalization ambiguities, providing an unambiguous alternative for characterizing radial flow fluctuations
3
3

3

The measurement of
v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
naturally requires much more event statistics and careful handling of the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
bin width choices. A study of these experimental practicalities remains a subject for future investigation.
. Recent experimental results
[
12
,
13
]
imply that
v_{0s}(p_{\mathrm{T}})
is positive at low
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, crosses zero at
p_{\mathrm{T}}\sim 3
–4 GeV, and approaches zero at higher
p_{\mathrm{T}}
.

The observables in range
A
expressed using variables in the full range

The event-wise mean transverse momentum in
A
and its ensemble average are given by

\displaystyle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{A}}\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}N(p_{\mathrm{T}})

(17)

\displaystyle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}

\displaystyle\equiv\left\langle[p_{\mathrm{T}}]\right\rangle_{A}=\left\langle\frac{1}{N_{A}}\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\langle N_{A}\right\rangle}\left[\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle-\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\delta N_{A}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\left\langle N_{A}\right\rangle}\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

(18)

The integral involving
\left\langle n_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\delta N_{A}\right\rangle
is a higher-order term corresponding to the correlated fluctuation of spectral shape and integrated multiplicity. The other term represents the per-particle average transverse momentum: if we calculate
\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle
on a per-track basis instead of an event-by-event basis, Eq. (
18
) would be exact. From these, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{A}}\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})-\frac{\delta N_{A}}{N_{A}}\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})

(19)

The appearance of
\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}
is critical because it absorbs the contribution from
\delta N_{A}
. Note that since
A
is a subrange,
N_{A}
still fluctuates even if the total multiplicity
N
is fixed.

It is more convenient to express observables in terms of fractional spectra. Using
N(p_{\mathrm{T}})=n(p_{\mathrm{T}})N=\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})N+\left\langle n\right\rangle N
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle N\right\rangle\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle+\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\delta N(p_{\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle=N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})+\delta Nn(p_{\mathrm{T}})-\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\;.

(20)

Eq. (
18
) can be recast as,

\displaystyle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}\left\langle N(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}=\frac{\left\langle N\right\rangle\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle+\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\left\langle N\right\rangle\int_{A}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle+\int_{A}\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}\left(1+\frac{\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\left\langle\delta N\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\left\langle N_{A}\right\rangle\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}\;,

(21)

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A})\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\!\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A}\!\int_{A}\!\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=0

(22)

Plugging Eq. (
20
) into Eq. (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{A}=

\displaystyle\frac{N}{N_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta N}{N_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\left\langle\delta Nn(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{N}{N_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})

(23)

The second term vanishes because of Eq. (
22
), and the last higher-order term is also dropped. Again, the presence of
\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}
is essential to ensure the similarity of this equation to Eq. (
19

) and (
23

Following Ref.
[
14
]
, we assume that the EbE global radial flow fluctuation governs the EbE spectral fluctuation,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})}{\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}=\frac{v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})}{v_{0}}\frac{\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]}{\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle}

Plug this into Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]_{A}}{\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{N_{A}\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}}\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}-\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\delta n(p_{\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle=\left[\!\frac{N}{N_{A}\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}}\!\int_{A}\!(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A})\!\frac{v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})}{v_{0}}\!\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle\!\right]\frac{\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}]}{\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle v_{0,A}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\left\langle(\delta[p_{\mathrm{T}}])_{A}^{2}\right\rangle}}{\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{A}}=f\times\frac{\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A})v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle},

. The deviation of

Relating the

In principle, the normalization of the fractional spectra can be defined in any
p_{\mathrm{T}}
range. However, varying the choice of

in each range to its own

\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{0,R}}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})-s

This change in the zero-crossing point ensures that the corresponding weighted sum rule is satisfied.

\displaystyle\int_{F}v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=0,\int_{R}v_{\mathrm{0,R}}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\left\langle n_{\mathrm{R}}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=0

(29)

However, we would like to point out this sum rule is a direct consequence of the constraint
\int_{F}n(p_{\mathrm{T}})=1
and
\int_{R}n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})=1
. Different normalization ranges produce different sum rules and hence different zero-crossing points, none of which carries unique physical content. The resulting offsets are energy-dependent and particle-species-dependent, but they reflect volume or centrality fluctuations imposed by the chosen centrality estimator rather than genuine dynamical radial-flow information.

Using
\left\langle n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle=\left\langle N\right\rangle/\left\langle N_{R}\right\rangle\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle
, Eq. (

) is satisfied in both

and

,

\displaystyle v_{0,A}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A})v_{\rm 0,R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\left\langle n_{\mathrm{R}}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n_{R}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A})(v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})-s)\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{A}(p_{\mathrm{T}}\!-\!\left\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rangle_{\!A})v_{0}(p_{\mathrm{T}})\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}{\int_{A}p_{\mathrm{T}}\left\langle n(p_{\mathrm{T}})\right\rangle}\;.

(30)

The insensitivity of

fluctuations to the offset in

strengthens our conclusion that only the

-dependent variation of

is related to collective radial flow, because its baseline value is subject to a shift dictated by global multiplicity fluctuations.

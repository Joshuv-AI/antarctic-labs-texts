Breaking the Bottleneck with DiffuApriel: High-Throughput Diffusion LMs with Mamba Backbone
Vaibhav Singh Oleksiy Ostapenko Pierre-André Noël Torsten Scholak Mila – Quebec AI Institute; ServiceNow Research

Abstract
Diffusion-based language models have recently emerged as a promising alternative to autoregressive generation, yet their reliance on Transformer backbones limits inference efficiency due to quadratic attention and KV-cache overhead. In this work, we introduce DiffuApriel , a masked diffusion language model built on a bidirectional Mamba backbone that combines the diffusion objective with linear-time sequence modeling. DiffuApriel matches the performance of Transformer-based diffusion models while achieving up to 4.4 × \boldsymbol{\times} higher inference throughput for long sequences with a 1.3B model. We further propose DiffuApriel-H , a hybrid variant that interleaves attention and mamba layers, offering up to 2.6 × \boldsymbol{\times} throughput improvement with balanced global and local context modeling. Our results demonstrate that bidirectional state-space architectures serve as strong denoisers in masked diffusion LMs, providing a practical and scalable foundation for faster, memory-efficient text generation.



1
Introduction

The majority of the state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs)
(
Brown et al. 2020
;
Achiam et al. 2023
;
Chowdhery et al. 2023
;
Touvron et al. 2023a
)
are trained with the
autoregressive
(AR) objective: a Transformer predicts the next token given all previous tokens. While simple and powerful, this paradigm faces two persistent limitations. First, AR decoding is inherently sequential: every generated token depends on the full prefix, and thus inference latency grows linearly with output length. Second, AR Transformers are fundamentally constrained by memory: the attention mechanism incurs quadratic complexity in context length, creating computational and memory bottlenecks during both training and auto-regressive inference
(
Zhou et al. 2024
;
Tay et al. 2020
)
. These constraints limit throughput and test-time scaling abilitites of these models, motivating research into alternative architectures.

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs)
(
Li et al. 2025b
)
provide a flexible alternative to autoregressive generation. Unlike AR decoders, DLMs iteratively denoise entire corrupted sequences in parallel, enabling non-sequential decoding, partial infilling, and semi-autoregressive generation. This opens the door to faster or more controllable generation, especially when fewer denoising steps suffice for acceptable quality. DLMs hold practical promise for scaling iterative reasoning, whether vertically through multiple sampled traces or horizontally through long, coherent single-trace reasoning, as shown in recent AR models
(
Radhakrishna et al. 2025a
;
Radhakrishna et al. 2025b
;
Chen et al. 2025
)
. Because DLMs revisit and refine entire sequences during denoising, they can in principle support self-correction by adjusting intermediate errors using both past and future context.

However, a key bottleneck persists:
all current DLMs rely on Transformer backbones
, making their iterative denoising at inference highly compute-intensive. Each decoding step redundantly recomputes queries, keys, and values (QKV) for every token and layer, even though key–value (KV) states change only marginally between steps. This exhaustive recomputation introduces severe latency and memory overhead, making inference scale with both sequence length and the number of denoising steps, undermining diffusion’s inherent parallelism. The result is a paradox:
diffusion promises flexible text generation, yet its efficiency is constrained by the Transformer backbone.
Prior work has aimed to accelerate DLMs via improved objectives
(
Sahoo et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025a
;
Liu et al. 2025b
;
Arriola et al. 2025
)
or Transformer-compatible KV caching
(
Wu et al. 2025
;
Ma et al. 2025
;
Nguyen-Tri et al. 2025
)
, yet the core architectural limitation remains.

In parallel with advances in diffusion language modeling, state-space models (SSMs) have emerged as a powerful class of sequence mixers with linear-time complexity
(
Gu et al. 2022
;
Poli et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2023
;
Gu & Goel 2023
;
Dao & Gu 2024
)
. Recent studies demonstrate that SSMs and SSM-Transformer hybrids can match or even outperform Transformers on language and multimodal benchmarks while achieving substantially higher inference throughput
(
Gu & Goel 2023
;
Somvanshi et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2025a
)
.
Despite their potential, SSMs remain unexplored in DLMs. This raises a key question:
can structured recurrence serve as an effective language denoiser while enabling faster inference?
Exploring the intersection of efficient SSM backbones and masked discrete diffusion for text is the central motivation of our work.

To this end, we introduce the first family of SSM-based discrete masked diffusion language models. Two observations motivate our approach: diffusion LMs are becoming competitive at scale
(
Gong et al. 2025
;
Ye et al. 2025
;
Labs et al. 2025
)
yet remain tied to Transformer backbones, and recent advances show that Mamba/SSM architectures enable efficient multi-step diffusion in vision
(
Hu et al. 2024
;
Ergasti et al. 2025
;
Ju & Zhou 2024
;
Dang et al. 2024
)
. To our knowledge,
no prior work has joined these threads for language
. Our study fills this gap by showing that SSMs can serve as drop-in denoisers for masked discrete diffusion, thus substantially improving inference efficiency.

Specifically, we replace the Transformer backbone with (i) fully Mamba-2
(
Dao & Gu 2024
)
denoisers and (ii) hybrid Mamba-2 + Transformer denoisers that retain full-sequence bidirectionality while improving efficiency. Removing quadratic attention reduces per-step latency without altering the probabilistic semantics of masked diffusion. Moreover, block diffusion methods
(
Arriola et al. 2025
;
Wu et al. 2025
)
are orthogonal to our architectural design and can be naturally integrated: in block-DLM hybrids, local Transformer blocks manage short-range dependencies, while SSM layers propagate information globally, combining local precision with efficient long-range reasoning. We leave this direction for future work.

Our contributions are conceptual and empirical rather than algorithmic:

•

New architectural direction.

We propose
DiffuApriel
, which replaces Transformer denoisers with bidirectional Mamba-2 for discrete masked diffusion language modeling. This demonstrates that iterative denoising does not inherently require attention, establishing linear-time backbones as a compelling alternative for scaling diffusion LMs efficiently.

•

Hybrid design exploration.

Beyond using only Mamba-2, we also propose
DiffuApriel-H
, a hybrid architecture that combines attention and state-space layers. We analyze how localized recurrence and global context interact during the denoising process, while still delivering higher throughput than transformer based DLMs. These results provide new insights into the complementary roles of recurrence and attention in diffusion-based generation.

•

Controlled evaluation across scales.

We conduct a systematic comparison between DiffuApriel and Transformer-based diffusion models (
DiffuTran
) under identical training data, tokenization, noise schedules, and decoding steps at three parameter budgets (240M, 0.5B, and 1.3B). Our experiments demonstrate that
DiffuApriel
and
DiffuApriel-H
not only improve modeling quality but also deliver substantially higher inference throughput and lower latency, paving the way for more efficient design choices in DLMs.

2
Related Work

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs)

Diffusion modeling replaces left-to-right decoding with iterative denoising. While early work focused on continuous domains
(
Ho et al. 2020
;
Song & Ermon 2021
;
Rombach et al. 2022
;
Peebles & Xie 2023
)
, recent research has adapted diffusion to
discrete
text.
Austin et al. 2021
introduced structured transition matrices with absorbing states, and DiffusionBERT
(
He et al. 2023
)
employed a masked corruption process paired with a BERT-style denoiser. Subsequent studies have further refined masking strategies, noise schedules, and sampling procedures
(
Chen et al. 2023
;
Lou et al. 2023
;
Varma et al. 2025
;
Fathi et al. 2025
)
. Masked diffusion approaching autoregressive quality
(
Sahoo et al. 2024
;
Sahoo et al. 2025
)
enabled scalable models
(
Ye et al. 2025
;
Nie et al. 2025
)
competitive with LLaMA
(
Touvron et al. 2023b
)
, with further gains from AR adaptation and instruction tuning
(
Gong et al. 2025
;
Zhu et al. 2025
)
. To enhance inference efficiency, recent acceleration techniques
(
Ma et al. 2025
;
Wu et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025c
;
Israel et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2025b
)
use approximate KV-caching mechanisms to speed up DLM inference. In contrast,
DiffuApriel-H
achieves its speedups without relying on KV caching, though its attention layers remain compatible with these optimizations.

State-Space Models (SSMs) for Sequence Modeling

SSMs introduce structured recurrences computable via fast convolutions or scans, achieving linear-time scaling with sequence length. Early models like S4
(
Gu et al. 2022
)
demonstrated strong long-context capacity, while Hyena
(
Poli et al. 2023
)
and H3
(
Fu et al. 2023
)
explored alternative structured operators for language modeling. Mamba
(
Gu & Goel 2023
)
extended this line by introducing input-conditioned selective state spaces, matching or surpassing Transformers in accuracy with lower latency and memory use.
Somvanshi et al. 2025
further survey the breadth of SSM architectures and highlight their efficiency advantages. In autoregressive language modeling, SSMs rival Transformers at comparable scales while offering higher tokens-per-second throughput. More recent works have evolved various linear-complexity language models and hybrids
(
Wang et al. 2025a
;
Ostapenko et al. 2025
)
, ranging from vector recurrences
(
De et al. 2024
)
to advanced gating mechanisms
(
Yang et al. 2024
)
. Yet, these gains remain unexplored in discrete diffusion LMs, where all prior models rely on full-attention denoisers, leaving open whether SSM recurrences can effectively replace attention in iterative denoising for text.

Diffusion with Mamba/SSMs in Vision.

In vision, several studies have replaced Transformer-based DiT backbones with state-space models (SSMs), particularly Mamba variants
(
Gu & Goel 2023
;
Ergasti et al. 2025
;
Dang et al. 2024
)
, to improve diffusion efficiency. The Diffusion Mamba (DiM) family
(
Teng et al. 2024
;
Mo 2025
)
achieves high-resolution image synthesis with multi-directional scanning and local feature enhancement, yielding notable throughput gains over attention-based models. VM-DDPM
(
Ju & Zhou 2024
)
fuses convolutional locality with SSM-based global modeling to enhance structural fidelity in medical image generation. ZigMa
(
Hu et al. 2024
)
introduces zigzag scanning for faster, more memory-efficient diffusion while maintaining competitive quality, and
Phung et al. 2024
incorporates wavelet transforms to strengthen local inductive biases, achieving faster convergence and favorable quality–efficiency trade-offs. Beyond diffusion,
Zhu et al. 2024
generalizes Mamba as a vision backbone, and recent surveys
(
Xu et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025a
;
Ergasti et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Dang et al. 2024
)
map its rapid adoption across segmentation, restoration, and dense prediction. Collectively, these works show that replacing attention with Mamba in multi-step image diffusion preserves or improves quality while substantially lowering per-step compute.

Method

3.1
Preliminary

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs), employ a forward noising process where tokens are progressively replaced by a special [MASK] token
(
Sahoo et al. 2024
;
Shi et al. 2024
)
. This process is defined by the transition probability

q_{t|0}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{x}_{0})=\prod_{i=1}^{L}q_{t|0}(x_{t}^{i}|x_{0}^{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{L}\text{Cat}(x_{t}^{i};(1-t)\delta_{x_{0}^{i}}+t\delta_{\text{MASK}}),

(1)

where
t\in[0,1]
controls interpolation between the original data
\mathbf{x}_{0}
(at
t=0
) and a fully masked sequence (at
t=1
).
\text{Cat}(\cdot)
denotes the categorical distribution.
A parametric model
p_{\theta}
learns the reverse denoising process, and generation starts from all
[MASK]
and iteratively unmasks by sampling
p_{\theta}(x_{0}^{i}|\mathbf{x}_{t})
.

Recent theory (MDLM
(
Shi et al. 2024
;
Sahoo et al. 2024
)
, RADD
(
Ou et al. 2024
)
) simplifies training from a variational bound to a reweighted cross-entropy over masked positions

\mathcal{L}_{\text{MDM}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{q_{t|0}(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{x}_{0})}\left[\sum_{i:x_{t}^{i}=\text{MASK}}-\log p_{\theta}(x_{0}^{i}|\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]dt.

(2)

This formulation scales to LLMs as DLMs, with LLaDA
(
Nie et al. 2025
)
and Dream-7B
(
Ye et al. 2025
)
matching autoregressive performance while enabling parallel decoding and flexible infilling.

The analytical reverse of the forward process defined in Equation
1
is computationally inefficient for generation, as it typically modifies only a single token at each step
(
Campbell et al. 2022
;
Lou et al. 2023
)
. A common strategy to accelerate sampling is to employ an
MCMC-based approximation of the reverse process

(
Nie et al. 2025
)
, enabling the model to update multiple masked tokens in a single step when transitioning from a noise level
t
to an earlier level
s<t
. This yields the following factorized form:

\displaystyle q_{s|t}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}q_{s|t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{t}),\quad q_{s|t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{t})=\begin{cases}1,&\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{i}\neq[\text{MASK}]\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}=\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{i}\\
\frac{s}{t},&\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{i}=[\text{MASK}]\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}=[\text{MASK}]\\
\frac{t-s}{t}\,q_{0|t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{t}),&\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{i}=[\text{MASK}]\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\neq[\text{MASK}].\end{cases}

(3)

Here,
q_{0|t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{t})
denotes the model-provided distribution over the vocabulary for predicting a non-
[\text{MASK}]
token when
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{i}
is masked. In conditional generation settings e.g., generating a response
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}
given a prompt
p
, the reverse diffusion process in Equation
3
must be adapted. In this case, the model’s predictive distribution for unmasking a token becomes
prompt-conditioned
:

q_{0|t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s}^{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{t},p),

reflecting that token predictions now depend on both the intermediate noised sequence and the conditioning prompt.

3.2
Diffusion Mamba Language Models

We propose
DiffuApriel
a masked diffusion language model whose denoiser is built on a
bidirectional state-space Mamba (BiMamba)
backbone. DiffuApriel preserves the probabilistic structure of masked discrete diffusion while replacing the Transformer encoder with a bidirectional Mamba mixer, enabling
linear-time
inference and substantially lower memory overhead during multi-step denoising. We refer to transformer based DLM in our work as
DiffuTran
for simplicity.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times L\times d}
denote a batch of token embeddings, where
B
is the batch size,
L
the sequence length, and
d
the hidden dimension.
Each block is composed of two independent Mamba layers: one processes the sequence in the forward direction, and the other processes the sequence in the reverse direction

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}^{\rightarrow}_{i}

\displaystyle=A_{f}\ast\mathbf{h}^{\rightarrow}_{i-1}+B_{f}\ast\mathbf{x}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,L,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}^{\leftarrow}_{i}

\displaystyle=A_{b}\ast\mathbf{h}^{\leftarrow}_{i+1}+B_{b}\ast\mathbf{x}_{i},\quad i=L,\dots,1.

(5)

Here
A_{f},B_{f},A_{b},B_{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
are learnable state-transition kernels implemented as 1D causal and anti-causal convolutions or efficient scan operations
(
Gu & Goel 2023
)
.
The two directional feature streams are fused through simple
additive integration

\text{Mamba}(x_{i})=\mathbf{h}_{i}=\mathbf{h}^{\rightarrow}_{i}+\mathbf{h}^{\leftarrow}_{i},

(6)

providing a symmetric context representation while maintaining numerical stability.
The resulting hidden sequence
\mathbf{H}=(\mathbf{h}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{h}_{L})
is then normalized and passed through a lightweight feed-forward projection before residual addition.

Each Diffusion Block employs timestep-conditioned adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) to inject the diffusion noise level into the hidden activations.
A small MLP maps the scalar timestep
t
to a continuous embedding
\tau_{t}=\text{MLP}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{c}}
, which modulates both the mixer and MLP sublayers:

\text{AdaLN}(\mathbf{x};\tau_{t})=\gamma_{t}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mu(\mathbf{x})}{\sigma(\mathbf{x})}+\beta_{t},\quad(\gamma_{t},\beta_{t})=W_{\text{cond}}\tau_{t}.

(7)

This conditioning allows the denoiser to adapt its internal recurrence dynamics to varying noise levels across diffusion steps.

Figure 1
:
Schematic diagram of our proposed
DiffuApriel
architecture where mixer blocks replaces attention layers with bidirectional Mamba layers. In our experiments, to maintain comparability with

, we treat the MLP layer as optional and refer to this variant as
DiffuApriel
+MLP. For
DiffuApriel-H
we have interleaved attention layers after every
K
mamba layers. Attention provides global token interactions while Mamba enables efficient state space sequence modeling, allowing the hybrid denoiser to capture both long-range dependencies and local temporal dynamics with significantly improved efficiency. In our experiments, we fix
K=5
.

Each block then applies Mamba mixing followed by an optional MLP refinement as shown in
Figure 1
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\text{mixer}}

\displaystyle=\text{Mamba}(\text{AdaLN}(\mathbf{x};\tau_{t}))+\mathbf{x},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\text{mlp}}

\displaystyle=\text{MLP}(\text{AdaLN}(\mathbf{y}_{\text{mixer}};\tau_{t}))+\mathbf{y}_{\text{mixer}}.

(9)

The final output
\mathbf{y}_{\text{mlp}}
is passed to the next block in the stack. During denoising, given masked input embeddings
\mathbf{x}_{t}
and timestep embedding
\tau_{t}
, the model predicts token logits via the full diffusion stack:

\mathbf{z}=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{t},\tau_{t})=\text{OutputProj}\!\left(\mathrm{Block}_{N}(\cdots\mathrm{Block}_{1}(E(\mathbf{x}_{t}),\tau_{t})\cdots)\right).

(10)

The conditional distribution over clean tokens is then given by:

p_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{0}|\mathbf{x}_{t},t)=\prod_{i=1}^{L}\text{Cat}(x_{0}^{i};\text{softmax}(z_{i})).

(11)

Training minimizes the masked diffusion objective (Eq.
2
), preserving probabilistic consistency with absorbing-state discrete diffusion.
By replacing attention with bidirectional state-space dynamics,
DiffuApriel
achieves efficient and expressive denoising with linear scaling in both sequence length and memory.

Recent results in large-scale autoregressive modeling show that hybrid architectures
(
Lieber et al. 2024
;
Bae et al. 2025
;
Zuo et al. 2025
;
Wang et al. 2025a
)
combining attention and state-space layers can outperform both pure Transformer and pure Mamba models under similar compute budgets, leveraging complementary strengths in local recurrence and global dependencies. Motivated by these findings, we investigate hybrid DLMs by interleaving bidirectional Mamba and Transformer blocks, inserting one attention mixer every
K
Mamba mixer blocks (with
K=5
in all experiments). Extending this paradigm from AR to diffusion, we examine whether similar complementarities hold under masked denoising objectives.

4
Experiments

We evaluate three denoiser architectures under an identical diffusion objective, masking process, and noise schedule to ensure a controlled comparison.
The first configuration employs an attention-based mixer in every diffusion block, the second replaces these with bidirectional Mamba mixers, and the third adopts a hybrid design that interleaves attention and Mamba blocks following the scheduling principles outlined in Section
3.2
. Across all variants, the overall architecture, data flow, and conditioning mechanisms remain identical, with only the internal mixer type varying, as illustrated in
Figure 1
.

To maintain comparable parameter budgets, the MLP expansion ratio in
DiffuApriel
+MLP and hybrid configurations is set to half that of the attention-only model, while other hyperparameters, such as the number of blocks, hidden size, etc are kept constant. This design ensures a fair, architecture-isolated evaluation of modeling quality, efficiency, and inference throughput. Our goals are to answer:

1.

Throughput and Latency:
Does an SSM backbone yield higher tokens/sec and lower per-step latency at matched quality?

2.

Scaling:
Do the efficiency gains persist or improve with model size?

4.1
Data, Tokenization, and Pretraining Setup

We evaluate our
DiffuApriel
and
DiffuApriel-H
models as generative language models by comparing their likelihood, throughput, and latency against
DiffuTran
at three parameter scales: 240M, 0.5B, and 1.3B. In our experiments, to maintain comparability with
DiffuTran
, we treat the MLP layer as optional and refer to this variant as
DiffuApriel
+MLP.
All models are trained on the DCLM dataset
(
Li et al. 2024
)
using the GPT2 tokenizer
(
Radford et al. 2019
)
, with a fixed context length of 1,024 tokens.
We train all models with a fixed log-linear noise schedule and the masked diffusion objective with decoding steps fixed to
S=128
for all runs, under approximate Quokka optimal compute budget
(
Ni et al. 2025
)
as given in
Table 1
. For measuring inference throughput, we record the wall-clock model latency and tokens-per-second throughput on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU using bf16 precision and PyTorch backend. Each measurement averages over 20 full diffusion decoding runs (128 denoising steps per run) following 5 warm-up iterations, with timings captured via CUDA Graphs to eliminate launch overheads.

For
DiffuApriel
(we use Mamba-2 training kernel for both training and inference), inference throughput grows and saturates for longer sequences. This trend arises from their
\mathcal{O}(B\,L\,d)
computational and memory complexity, where total latency
t(L)
also scales linearly with
L
. Once the GPU reaches its peak memory bandwidth, the model becomes
memory bound

(
Baruah et al. 2025
)
, because the work (total tokens
\propto L
) and the latency (
t(L)\propto L
) scale equally, the resulting tokens-per-second rate (
T(L)\propto L/t(L)
) becomes approximately constant:
T_{\max}\approx M_{p}/\text{bytes per token}
, where
M_{p}
is peak memory bandwidth per second (bytes/sec).

In contrast,
DiffuTran
exhibit an initial rise followed by a
sharp decline in throughput
as sequence length increases, owing to their
quadratic

\mathcal{O}(B\,L^{2}\,d)
cost. The quadratic growth of the
QK^{\top}
and softmax operations means the
total latency
t(L)
scales super-linearly
(
t(L)\!\sim\!\beta BL^{2}
). Since throughput is defined as tokens per second (
T(L)\propto L/t(L)
), the
L
growth in the numerator is overcome by the
L^{2}
growth in the denominator, causing throughput to decay roughly as
T(L)\!\propto\!1/L
. This quadratic cost is further exacerbated by unfavorable GEMM (General Matrix–Matrix Multiplication) aspect ratios (
L\gg d_{h};\;d_{h}:\text{head dimension}
) at large
L
. Furthermore,
DiffuTran
+KV caching
(
Wu et al. 2025
)
achieves higher throughput than
DiffuApriel
for shorter sequences (up to 2048 tokens), but degrades sharply beyond 10K tokens, consistent with
Peng et al. 2025
, eventually performing even worse than
DiffuTran
alone.

For hybrid diffusion models interleaving state-space and attention layers, the overall complexity falls between the linear
\mathcal{O}(B\,L\,d)
scaling of Mamba and the quadratic
\mathcal{O}(B\,L^{2}\,d)
scaling of attention. With attention inserted every
K
layers, the effective cost approximates
\mathcal{O}(B\,L\,d+B\,L^{2}\,d/K)
. Thus, the linear term dominates for moderate
L
, yielding near-linear throughput, while the quadratic component contributes mildly at larger
L
. This interleaving achieves a balance, preserving attention’s global context modeling while maintaining the high streaming efficiency of Mamba. These observations confirm that Mamba-like architectures maintain stable inference efficiency under long-context regimes, whereas attention models face quadratic degradation beyond a critical sequence length.

5
Conclusion

This paper introduced a new architecture for masked DLMs by replacing Transformer denoisers with state space models. We presented
DiffuApriel
, to our knowledge the first diffusion LM built on a bidirectional Mamba backbone, and
DiffuApriel-H
, a hybrid variant that adds interleaved attention layers for complementary global context. Across 240M to 1.3B parameters, both models match or surpass the Transformer baseline
DiffuTran
while delivering significantly higher throughput and lower latency. Our results connect to a growing body of work exploring efficient linear-time mixers, including SSMs, DeltaNet-style token mixers, and hybrid architectures, underscoring that attention is not strictly required for iterative denoising. A remaining limitation of our approach is that performance advantages diminish on short contexts. Looking ahead, investigating reasoning at scale within masked diffusion frameworks, particularly using Mamba and hybrid backbones, presents a promising and compelling direction for future research.

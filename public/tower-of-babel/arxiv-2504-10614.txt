1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in the study of Kohn-Laplacian,
\Box_{b}
, is determining under which conditions its inverse, the complex Green operator
K_{q}
, has a gain in regularity.
This is because, despite being of the Laplace type,
\Box_{q}
generates a non elliptic second order system of partial differential equations.
The problem has been widely explored for pseudoconvex and
Y(q)
manifolds, and we refer the reader to
[
16
,
19
,
7
,
6
,
13
]
for back-ground. We introduce a subtle comparability condition well adapted to a kind of manifold with the finite type property, more general than those mentioned above. This condition is still within the scope of the well-known condition
D(q)
, and we use it to show a gain in regularity for
K_{q}
through
\varepsilon
-subelliptic estimates in
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces.
Furthermore, we show optimal gain in regularity for
K_{q}
at some tangential directions through maximal
L^{p}
estimates on a specific level of the
\bar{\partial}_{b}
-complex where we assume a closed range hypothesis.

Let
M
be a smooth compact orientable CR manifold of hypersurface type embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, of real dimension
2n-1
.
The CR structure on
M
allows us to define the tangential Cauchy Riemann operator
\bar{\partial}_{b}
, which acts on the
(0,q)
-form spaces in the
\bar{\partial}_{b}
-complex.
The Kohn-Laplacian operator
\Box_{b}
is defined at each form level on
M
by
\Box_{b}=\bar{\partial}_{b}\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}+\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}\bar{\partial}_{b}
, where
\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}
is the
L^{2}
-adjoint of
\bar{\partial}_{b}
. When
\Box_{b}
has a continuous inverse on
{{}^{\perp}\ker\Box_{b}}
, it is called by the complex Green operator
K_{q}
.
The existence and the regularity properties of
K_{q}
are achieved by obtaining closed range estimates in
L^{2}
and
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces. Establishing these estimates relies strongly on the geometry of the manifold
M
, and the results may be valid for only specific levels of the
\bar{\partial}_{b}
-complex.
Examples of well-known geometric conditions on
M
are
strong and weak pseudoconvexity,
Y(q)
and weak-
Y(q)
. This latter was recently introduced by Harrington and Raich

[
9
,
10
]
,
and it is the most general known sufficient condition for continuity of
K_{q}
on
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces.
These geometric conditions are defined in terms of the Levi form.
Essentially, the Levi form measures the non-involutivity of the CR structure and its conjugate, and associates a Hermitian matrix to it.
We say
\varepsilon
-subelliptic estimates are satisfied, if

(1.1)

{\|{u}\|}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\lesssim{\|{\bar{\partial}_{b}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}+{\|{\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}+{\|{u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}

for every smooth
(0,q)
-form
u
,
where
{\|{u}\|}_{\varepsilon}
denotes the
L^{2}
-Sobolev norm of
u
of order
\varepsilon>0
.
Also, if
U
is an open set in
M
and
\left\{X_{j}\right\}_{1\leq j\leq 2n-2}
is a set of real vector fields on
U
such that
\left\{X_{j}+iX_{j+n-1}\right\}_{j}
spans the CR structure of
M
, we say that maximal
L^{p}
estimates are satisfied at
U
when

(1.2)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n-2}{\|{X_{i}X_{j}u}\|}_{L^{p}}\lesssim{\|{\Box_{b}u}\|}_{L^{p}}+{\|{u}\|}_{L^{p}}

for every smooth
(0,q)
-form
u
supported in
U
, and for every
p\in(1,\infty)
.

In
[
14
]
, Kohn proved that
Y(q)
implies that (
1.1
) holds with
\varepsilon=1/2
.
In particular
Y(q)
implies that the CR manifold has the finite commutator type property of order
2
. Also
M
satisfies a comparable eigenvalues condition on the Levi form called
D(q)
. Roughly speaking,
D(q)
locally compares the sum of any
q
eigenvalues with the trace of the Levi matrix.
In
[
6
]
, Derridj used microlocal analysis to show subelliptic estimates hold for
(0,1)
-forms on pseudoconvex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, assuming
D(1)
and finite commutator type condition of some order. Koening generalized Derridj’s result for higher levels assuming
D(q)
(see
[
13
, Appendix B]
).

On the other hand, the work done in
[
14
]
, allow us to conclude that under the
Y(q)
condition,
K_{q}
gains just a derivative at all tangential directions. However, Rothschild and Stein in
[
19
]
showed that
(
1.2
) holds,
which means that
K_{q}
gains two derivatives in the “good” tangential directions
\left\{X_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{2n-2}
.
In
[
7
]
, Grigis and Rothschild showed that
D(q)
is a necessary condition to obtain the maximal estimates (
1.2
).
For the pseudoconvex case,
Koening showed that the hypothesis of closed range estimates,
D(q)
, and finite commutator type conditions suffice to prove (
1.2
)
[
13
]
.
It is worth mentioning that Derridj
[
5
]
and Ben Moussa
[
1
]
studied analogous estimates for the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem in
pseudoconvex domains,
while Harrington and Raich recently examined this for non pseudoconvex domains in
[
11
]
.

Our results about subelliptic estimates apply to certain non-pseudoconvex manifolds, which satisfy
D(q)
and the finite type condition. These manifolds satisfy a comparability condition introduced in this paper (see Definition
2.2
). This new condition allows us adapt conveniently the procedure in
[
6
]
and obtain subelliptic estimates of the type (
1.1
), Theorem
3.1
. We prove the maximal
L^{p}
estimates, Proposition
4.12
, in the spirit of
[
13
,
2
]
, using previous subelliptic estimates together with closed range hypothesis just at some specified level of
(0,q)
-forms. Due to this last hypothesis and the fact that
M
may not be pseudoconvex, we redo the process performed in
[
13
]
because our hypotheses do not imply that
K_{q-1}
or
K_{q+1}
exist (see
[
13
, Proposition 5.5]
and subsequent remark).

We distribute the topics as follows. Section
2
presents the setting in of our CR manifold, the definition of our comparability condition, and its relation with the well-known
D(q)
. Section
3
contains a special integration by parts and the microlocal configuration to obtain
\varepsilon
-subelliptic estimates. In Section
4
, we utilize the subelliptic estimates and an escalation method. We provide pointwise and differential estimates for the distribution kernels associated with
K_{q}
, along with cancellation properties on its action on bump functions. Then, we demonstrate maximal
L^{p}
estimates using a Calderon-Zygmund theory.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The CR structure on
M

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a smooth, compact, orientable CR manifold of hypersurface type of real dimension
2n-1
. Since
M
is embedded, we take the CR structure on
M
to be the induced CR structure
T^{1,0}(M)=T^{1,0}(\mathbb{C}^{N})\cap T(M)\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Let
T^{p,q}(M)
denote the exterior algebra generated by
T^{1,0}(M)
and
T^{0,1}(M)
and
\Lambda^{p,q}(M)
the bundle of
(p,q)
-forms on
T^{p,q}(M)
. The calculations in this work do not depend on
p
because
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and so we will always assume
p=0
.

2.2.
\bar{\partial}_{b}
on embedded manifolds

We use the induced pointwise inner product on
\Lambda^{0,q}(M)
,
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{x}
, coming from the inner product on
(0,q)
-forms in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Therefore, we define an
L^{2}
-inner product on
\Lambda^{0,q}(M)
given by

(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{M}\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{x}\,dV(x)

where
dV
is the volume element on
M
. We use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{q}(M)}
to denote the associated
L^{2}
-norm of the inner product above and
L^{2}_{q}(M)
to the completion of
\Lambda^{0,q}(M)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{q}(M)}
. The tangential Cauchy-Riemann operator
\bar{\partial}_{b}
is the restriction of the De Rham exterior derivative
d
to
\Lambda^{0,q}(M)
. Additionally, we are denoting (by an abuse of notation) with
\bar{\partial}_{b}
the closure of
\bar{\partial}_{b}
in norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{q}(M)}
. In this way,
\bar{\partial}_{b}:L^{2}_{q}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}_{q+1}(M)
is a well-defined, closed, densely defined operator with
L^{2}
adjoint
\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}:L^{2}_{q+1}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}_{q}(M)
. We define the Kohn Laplacian
\Box_{b}:L^{2}_{q}(M)\rightarrow L^{2}_{q}(M)
by

\Box_{b}:=\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}\bar{\partial}_{b}+\bar{\partial}_{b}\bar{\partial}^{*}_{b}.

Using a covering
\left\{U_{\mu}\right\}_{\mu}
and a partition of unity
\left\{\zeta_{\mu}\right\}_{\mu}
subordinate to the covering, we define the Sobolev norm of order
s
, of a
(0,q)
-form
f
, given by

{\|{f}\|}_{H^{s}_{q}}^{2}=\sum_{\mu}{\|{\tilde{\zeta}_{\mu}\Lambda^{s}\zeta_{\mu}f^{\mu}}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}_{q}(M)}

where
f^{\mu}
is the form
f
expressed in local coordinates on
U_{\mu}
,
\Lambda
is the pseudodifferential operator with symbol
(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{\mu}
is a cutoff function that dominates
\zeta_{\mu}
such that
\supp{\tilde{\zeta}_{\mu}}\subset U_{\mu}
.

2.3.
The Levi form and the weak
Y(q)
condition

By the orientability of
M
, we choose a purely imaginary unit vector
T
that is orthogonal to
T^{1,0}(M)\oplus T^{0,1}(M)
.
Let
\gamma
be the globally defined
1
-form that annihilates
T^{1,0}(M)\oplus T^{0,1}(M)
and is normalized so that
\langle\gamma,T\rangle=-1
.

The Levi form at a point
x\in M
is the Hermitian form given by
\langle{d\gamma_{x},L\wedge\bar{L}^{\prime}}\rangle
, where
L,L^{\prime}\in T^{1,0}_{x}(U)
and
U
is a small neighborhood of
x\in M
.

If
\{L_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n-1}
is a local basis of
T^{1,0}(U)
and
c_{jk}
are such that
\langle{d\gamma,L_{j}\wedge\overline{L}_{k}}\rangle=c_{jk}
, then
[c_{jk}]
is called by the Levi matrix with respect to
\{L_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n-1}
and
T
.

We say that
M
is pseudoconvex (resp., strictly pseudoconvex) at a point
p\in M
if all the eigenvalues of the Levi matrix are nonnegative (resp., positive) at
p
. We say
M
is pseudoconvex or strictly pseudoconvex if it is at every point.
Also, for fixed
1\leq q\leq n-1
, we say
M
satisfies the
Z(q)
condition at
p

if the Levi matrix has at least
n-q
positive eigenvalues or at least
q+1
negative eigenvalues at
p
.
M
satisfies the
Y(q)
condition at
p
if both
Z(q)
and
Z(n-1-q)
conditions are satisfied at
p
. We say
Z(q)
or
Y(q)
condition holds on
M
if does at every point.

Due to
[
14
,
15
,
18
]
, the geometric conditions mentioned in the previous paragraph are sufficient to show a closed range of
\bar{\partial}_{b}
on
L^{2}_{q}(M)
. However, another weaker geometric condition was introduced in
[
9
,
10
,
8
,
3
]
also suffices to establish closed range at level
q
, which we define as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For
1\leq q\leq n-1
, we say
M
satisfies weak
Z(q)
if there exists a real
\Upsilon\in T^{1,1}(M)
satisfying:

(A)

\left|\theta\right|^{2}\geq(i\theta\wedge\overline{\theta})(\Upsilon)\geq 0
for all
\theta\in\Lambda^{1,0}(M)

(B)

\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+{\cdots}+\mu_{q}-i\langle{d\gamma_{x},\Upsilon}\rangle\geq 0
where
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{n-1}
are the eigenvalues of the Levi form at
x

in increasing order.

(C)

\omega(\Upsilon)\neq q
where
\omega
is the
(1,1)
-form associated to the induced metric on
\mathbb{C}T(M)
.

We say that
M
satisfies weak
Y(q)
if
M
satisfies both weak
Z(q)
and weak
Z(n-q-1)
.

2.4.
Finite Type condition and its consequences

Let
U
be an open set in
M
and
\left\{L_{j}\right\}_{1\leq j\leq n-1}
an orthonormal basis of
T^{1,0}(M)
on
U
. Let
X_{j}={\mathrm{Re}L_{j}}
,
X_{j+n-1}={\mathrm{Im}L_{j}}
, for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and

\mathcal{L}_{m}=\left\{[X_{j_{1}},[X_{j_{2}},[\cdots,[X_{j_{k-1}},X_{j_{k}}]\cdots]]]:k\leq m,X_{j_{s}}\in\left\{X_{k}\right\}_{1\leq k\leq 2n-2}\right\}.

Observe that

(2.1)

\sum_{j=1}^{2n-2}{\|{X_{j}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}\simeq\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left({\|{L_{j}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}+{\|{\bar{L}_{j}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),

for any smooth function
u
supported in
U
.
We will say that the finite commutator type (or finite type) condition is satisfied at
U
if there exists a vector field system
\left\{L_{j}\right\}_{1\leq j\leq n-1}
defined on
U\subset M
such that
\operatorname{span}{\mathcal{L}_{m}}=T(U)
for some
m\in\left\{2,3,\cdots\right\}
.The order of the finite type is the smallest such
m
.
We say that
M
has the finite type property if for each point in
M
, there exists an open neighborhood
U
on which the finite type condition is satisfied.
It is a known result due to Hormander that if the finite type condition of order
m
is satisfied at
U
,
then there exists
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that are satisfied the next subelliptic estimates

(2.2)

{\|{u}\|}_{H^{\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2n-2}{\|{X_{j}u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}+{\|{u}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),\qquad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U).

Also,
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{m}
is the best constant here (see e.g. Appendix in
[
20
]
). In the following, we assume
M
has the finite type property.

2.5.
The comparable weak
Y(q)
condition

Definition 2.2
.

Let
U
be an open set in
M
. For
1\leq q\leq n-1
, we say that comparable weak
Z(q)
is satisfied on
U
if there exists a real bivector
\Upsilon\in T^{1,1}(U)
satisfying:

(a)

|\theta|^{2}>i(\theta\wedge\bar{\theta})\Upsilon>0
for all
\theta\in\Lambda^{1,0}(U)

(b)

\sigma_{q}-i\left\langle d\gamma,\Upsilon\right\rangle\geq 0
, where
\sigma_{q}
represents the sum of the smallest
q
eigenvalues of the Levi matrix.

We say that comparable weak
Y(q)
is satisfied on
U
(resp.,
M
) if it satisfies both comparable weak
Z(q)
and comparable weak
Z(n-1-q)
on
U
(resp., if every point of
M
has a neighborhood
U
on which comparable weak
Y(q)
holds.
Weak
Z(q)
manifolds satisfying (
a
) in Definition
2.2
instead of (
A
) in Definition
2.1
satisfy comparable weak
Z(q)
.
In essence, the existence of the bivector in Definition
2.2
refers to the existence of a Hermitian matrix
\Upsilon
with smooth entries, eigenvalues in
]0,1[
, and such that
\sigma_{q}-\Tr(\Upsilon C)\geq 0
where
C
is the Levi matrix.
Thus, we refer to
\Upsilon
as a matrix instead of a bivector.
In
[
11
]
is introduced an analogous version of this condition.
Along with other regularity and boundary conditions in
\Upsilon
, they show local maximal
L^{2}
estimates for the
\bar{\partial}
-Neumann problem on boundaries of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{N}

[
11
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Indeed, if
Z(q)
is satisfied at
x\in M
, then a neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
exists such that the comparable weak
Z(q)
condition is satisfied at
V_{x}
. Let
C
denote the Levi matrix,
C_{x}
be the matrix
C
evaluated at
x
,
\left\{\lambda_{j}(C)\right\}_{j}
be the eigenvalues of the matrix
C
in increasing order, and
A
be a unitary matrix (with constant entries) such that

C_{x}=A^{*}DA
,
where
D=\diag\left\{\lambda_{1}(C_{x}),\cdots,\lambda_{n-1}(C_{x})\right\}
. Assume that there exist at least
n-q
positive eigenvalues at
x
. The proof when there exist at least
q+1
negative eigenvalues is similar. Then
\lambda_{q}(C_{x})>0
. Let
\varepsilon
be a positive constant small sufficient such that

(2.3)

(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{q}(C_{x})+\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}(C_{x})+\cdots+\lambda_{q-1}(C_{x})-\lambda_{q+1}(C_{x})-\cdots-\lambda_{n-1}(C_{x}))>0.

Let
B=C-A^{*}DA
. By the continuity of the entries
B^{ij}
of
B
and since
B_{x}=0
; for
\delta>0
, a neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
exists, such that

\left|B_{y}^{ij}\right|<\delta,

for all
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
and
y\in V_{x}
.
Then, in particular, by applying Schur’s inequality, we have

(2.4)

\left|\lambda_{1}(B_{y})\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\left|B_{y}^{ij}\right|^{2}=O(\delta^{2}).

Define
\Upsilon=A^{*}\diag\left\{1-\varepsilon,\cdots,1-\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\cdots,\varepsilon\right\}A
, where
1-\varepsilon
appears
q-1
times in the diagonal matrix. So, from Weyl’s inequalities (see
[
12
, Theorem 4.3.1]
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(C_{y})

\displaystyle+\cdots+\lambda_{q}(C_{y})-\Tr(\Upsilon C_{y})=

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(A^{*}DA+B_{y})+\cdots+\lambda_{q}(A^{*}DA+B_{y})-\Tr(\Upsilon A^{*}DA)-\Tr(\Upsilon B_{y})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}(A^{*}DA)+\cdots+\lambda_{q}(A^{*}DA)+q\lambda_{1}(B_{y})-\Tr(A\Upsilon A^{*}D)-\Tr(\Upsilon B_{y})

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{q}(C_{x})+\varepsilon(\lambda_{1}(C_{x})+\cdots+\lambda_{q-1}(C_{x})-\lambda_{q+1}(C_{x})-\cdots-\lambda_{n-1}(C_{x}))

(2.5)

\displaystyle\quad+q\lambda_{1}(B_{y})-\Tr(\Upsilon B_{y}).

Now, since
\Tr(\Upsilon B_{y})
is a linear combination of elements
B_{y}^{ij}
, we have

(2.6)

\Tr(\Upsilon B_{y})=O(\delta).

So, from (
2.4
), (
2.6
), and (
2.3
), we can choose
\delta
small sufficient such that the right-hand side in (
2.5
) be positive for all
y
in some neighborhood
V_{x}
of
x
. This shows that comparable weak
Z(q)
condition is satisfied at
V_{x}
using
\Upsilon
as we defined before.

2.6.
Relation between comparable weak
Y(q)
and
D(q)
conditions.

In this section, we expose the relation between our comparable condition and the well-known condition
D(q)
, given in
[
7
]
and inspired by
[
5
]
.
It is worth mentioning that the motivation of
D(q)

and our condition is to generalize the condition
Y(q)
, which is equivalent to

(2.7)

-\sigma^{-}<\sigma_{q}<\sigma^{+},

where
\sigma_{q}
represents any possible sum of
q
eigenvalues of the Levi matrix
[c_{jk}]
and

\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left|\mu_{j}\right|\ ,\qquad\sigma^{+}-\sigma^{-}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\mu_{j},

with
\left\{\mu_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{n-1}
being the eigenvalues of
C=[c_{jk}]
(see
[
7
]
). We say that
M
satisfies
D(q)
in
U
if, for any compact
K\subset U
, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{K}>0
such that

(2.8)

-\sigma^{-}+\varepsilon_{K}(\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-})\leq\sigma_{q}\leq\sigma^{+}-\varepsilon_{K}(\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-}).

On the other hand, the comparable weak
Y(q)
condition generalizes (
2.7
) as

\Tr(\Upsilon_{1}C)\leq\sigma_{q}\leq\Tr([Id-\Upsilon_{2}]C)

where
\Upsilon_{1}
and
\Upsilon_{2}
are from comparable weak
Z(q)
and
Z(n-1-q)
, respectively.

Remark 2.3
.

If comparable weak
Y(q)
is satisfied on some open set
U
then
D(q)
is satisfied in
U
. In fact, let
\Upsilon_{1}=[b^{rs}]
and
\Upsilon_{2}=[B^{rs}]
satisfy the comparable weak
Z(q)
and comparable weak
Z(n-1-q)
, respectively. In particular we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{q}\geq\sum_{r,s=1}^{n-1}b^{rs}c_{sr}=\Tr(\Upsilon_{1}[c_{jk}])\quad,\qquad\sigma_{n-1-q}\geq\sum_{r,s=1}^{n-1}B^{rs}c_{sr}=\Tr(\Upsilon_{2}[c_{jk}]).

Let
\varepsilon_{1}
and
1-\varepsilon_{2}
be the smallest and largest eigenvalue of
\Upsilon_{1}
, respectively;
A
be a unitary matrix such that
A^{*}CA=\diag\left\{\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n-1}\right\}
in non-decreasing order (
A
exists at any point, but it can vary discontinuously in
U
);
\sigma^{-}=-\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mu_{jj}
,
\sigma^{+}=\sum_{j=t+1}^{n-1}\mu_{jj}
, and
\left\{d_{11},\cdots,d_{n-1\ n-1}\right\}
are the diagonal elements of
A^{*}\Upsilon_{1}A
.
Then

\displaystyle\Tr(\Upsilon_{1}C)

\displaystyle=\Tr(A^{*}\Upsilon_{1}AA^{*}CA)\geq\max_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\left\{d_{jj}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mu_{jj}+\min_{1\leq j\leq n-1}\left\{d_{jj}\right\}\sum_{j=t+1}^{n-1}\mu_{jj}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon_{2})\sum_{j=1}^{t}\mu_{jj}+\varepsilon_{1}\sum_{j=t+1}^{n-1}\mu_{jj}\geq-\sigma^{-}+\varepsilon(\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-})

for
\varepsilon=\min\left\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\right\}
. The penultimate inequality above follows by Schur-Horn Theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem 4.3.45]
). So we obtain the left inequality in (
2.8
). Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\Tr(\Upsilon_{2}C)

\displaystyle\geq-\sigma^{-}+\varepsilon(\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-}),

for some
0<\varepsilon<1
. Then

\displaystyle\sigma_{n-1-q}\geq-\sigma^{-}+\varepsilon(\sigma^{+}+\sigma^{-})

4.

In this section, in addition to comparable weak

-forms.
In particular,

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in I_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{2n-2}{\|{X_{j}u_{K}}\|}^{2}_{L^{2}},

4.1.
Non isotropic metric

Let
U
be an open set in
M
,
\left\{L_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{n-1}
be a basis of
T^{1,0}(U)
,

X_{j}=(L_{j}+\bar{L}_{j})/2
,
X_{j}=(L_{j}-\bar{L}_{j})/(2i)
,
and
T
be vector used in the Levi form.
For each multi-index

we define
\lambda_{I}
and
\alpha_{I}^{j}
on

such that

[X_{i_{k}},[...,[X_{i_{2}},X_{i_{1}}]]]=-i\lambda_{I}T+\sum_{j=1}^{2n-2}\alpha_{I}^{j}X_{j}.

For each

and

\Lambda^{2}_{l}
is smooth, and

in

because of the finite type condition. For

, the natural non isotropic distance between

and

corresponding to

is given by

\displaystyle\rho(x,y):=\inf\left\{\delta>0\ |\ \exists\phi:[0,1]\to U\text{ continuous and piecewise smooth }\right.\text{such that }\phi(0)=x;

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\phi(1)=y;\ \text{ and }\phi^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{2n-2}\gamma_{j}(t)X_{j}\text{ a.e.}\left.\text{ with }\left|\gamma_{j}(t)\right|<\delta\text{ for each }1\leq j\leq 2n-2\right\}.

Let denote
B_{0}
the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
and define

g_{x,r}(u)=\exp(ru_{1}X_{1}+...+ru_{2n-2}X_{2n-2}-\Lambda(x,r)u_{2n-1}iT)(x)

for each

and

sufficiently small such that

is a diffeomorphism on

. We write

and denote the Jacobian of

by

. If

is a function on

we define

on

, and for a function

on

of vectors

, where

is the formal pullback of

(4.3)

\left(X_{j}f\right)\hat{}=r^{-1}\hat{X}_{j}\hat{f}.

With this configuration, the next result is established in
[

Theorem 4.1

Assume (only) that

and

(here the ball

is obtained throughout

(b)

and

.

(c)

|J_{x,r}(u)|\approx r^{2n-2}\Lambda(x,r)

and

(e)

(f)

The vector fields

for a commutator

A restatement of (

and

.

We will obtain the estimates for the complex Green and Szegö projection kernels and more in terms of

) in

In

we define

\bar{\partial}_{b}^{B}

Let us denote

-forms dual to

For each
(0,q)
-form

in

.
Also, we have for every function

in

\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\bar{L}_{j}\phi\right){\hat{}}=r^{-1}{\hat{\bar{L}}_{j}}\hat{\phi},\qquad\qquad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq n-1.

If

\displaystyle{\|{\zeta(I-S_{q})f}\|}_{H^{s+\varepsilon}_{q}(U)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{s}{\|{f}\|}_{L^{2}_{q}}{.}

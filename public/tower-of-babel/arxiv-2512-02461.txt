Artificial Noise Aided Physical Layer Security for Near-Field MIMO with Fluid Antenna Systems
Peng Zhang, Jian Dang, Miaowen Wen, Ziyang Liu, Chen Zhao, Huaifeng Shi, Chengsheng Pan, Zaichen Zhang

Abstract
With the evolution of wireless systems toward large-scale arrays and high-frequency reconfigurable architectures, fluid antenna systems (FAS) operating in the near-field (NF) regime provide new degrees of freedom (DoF) for physical layer security (PLS). This paper proposes an artificial-noise (AN)-aided PLS scheme for NF fluid-antenna multiple-input multiple-output (FA-MIMO) systems, with joint beamforming (BF) and AN design for both compact and large arrays. An alternating-optimization (AO) framework addresses the sparsity-constrained non-convex design by splitting it into a continuous BF/AN joint-design subproblem and a discrete FAS port-selection subproblem. Closed-form fully digital BF/AN solutions are obtained via a generalized spectral water-filling procedure within a block coordinate descent (BCD) surrogate and realized by a hardware-efficient hybrid beamforming (HBF) architecture that embeds AN in the baseband without extra radio-frequency (RF) chains. For FAS port selection, a row-energy based prune–refit rule, aligned with Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions of a group-sparsity surrogate, enables efficient active-port determination. Simulation results confirm that the proposed design exploits the geometry and position-domain DoF of FAS and significantly improves secrecy performance, particularly for non-extremely-large arrays where NF beam focusing alone is inadequate.



I
Introduction

As wireless communication technologies advance toward the sixth-generation (6G) era, emerging applications such as immersive extended reality (XR), autonomous driving, remote healthcare, and large-scale Internet of Things (IoT) are becoming pervasive and mission-critical
[
28
]
. These services often involve the collection, transmission, and processing of privacy-sensitive information, including user identity, behavioral patterns, physiological parameters, location data, and environmental sensing signals. The openness and time-varying nature of wireless channels exposes such data to risks of eavesdropping and information leakage
[
1
]
. To ensure secure and trustworthy communications in privacy-aware 6G scenarios, physical-layer security (PLS) techniques
[
20
,
25
]
exploit the intrinsic randomness and spatial selectivity of wireless propagation and provide an effective and lightweight means to enhance secrecy at the physical layer.

PLS is grounded in information theory and evaluates secrecy over noisy channels without cryptographic keys. It uses secrecy rate (SR) and secrecy outage to reflect the quality disparity between the legitimate link and the eavesdropper link
[
14
]
. At the system layer, cooperative relaying, friendly jamming, user scheduling, and allocation over time, frequency, and power are used to engineer favorable link asymmetries
[
34
,
11
]
. At the waveform layer, spatial and spectral degrees of freedom (DoF) are exploited. Multiantenna beamforming (BF) strengthens the main link and suppresses leakage through array gain, sidelobe control, and null steering; under partial channel state information (CSI), statistical or location-aware designs retain spatial selectivity
[
42
]
; when only partial CSI is available, statistical or location-aware designs preserve spatial selectivity. Artificial noise (AN) projected into the null space or endowed with structure, remains benign to the intended receiver while reducing received power in unauthorized directions
[
21
]
. Joint information–AN design with power allocation balances main-link enhancement and wiretap-link degradation
[
24
,
7
]
. In multiuser and multicarrier settings, information and AN can be coordinated across users and subcarriers under basic power and rate constraints to secure additional gains
[
30
,
32
]
. Robust optimization under imperfect CSI and unobservable passive eavesdroppers is further required to sustain secrecy in statistical or worst-case senses
[
18
]
.

With the development of massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO), millimeter-wave (mmWave), and terahertz (THz) technologies, the near-field (NF) region of antenna arrays has expanded significantly due to the large array apertures and high carrier frequencies involved
[
17
]
. In the NF region, electromagnetic (EM) propagation exhibits spherical wavefronts, leading to spatially varying amplitude and phase distributions that depend on both angle and distance
[
29
]
. This unique characteristic enables new DoF for PLS. In
[
5
]
, directional modulation with fully analog precoding and embedded AN is employed to secure transmission in both angle and distance. The work in
[
19
]
formulates a max–min secrecy beam-focusing problem under a hybrid beamforming (HBF) architecture to enhance the worst-case secrecy performance. A comprehensive study in
[
38
]
provides performance analysis and a streamlined near-field beamforming design, demonstrating that appropriately structured AN can approach near-optimal secrecy with substantially reduced complexity. The authors of
[
27
]
consider a THz scenario and adopt maximum-ratio transmission by steering the information and AN beams toward the legitimate user and the eavesdropper, respectively, and further derive a closed-form power allocation. Wavefront hopping with engineered Bessel and Airy profiles is investigated in
[
22
]
to reshape the near-field distribution and reduce interception probability. However, these contributions are restricted to multiple-input single output (MISO) links. In MIMO systems, the effective apertures at both the transmitter and receiver enlarge the Rayleigh distance and strengthen near-field coupling. A recent study
[
39
]
extends NF-PLS to a MIMO setting and maximizes secrecy through a two-stage HBF design under spherical-wave CSI, where secrecy is mainly achieved via beam focusing. This reliance on focusing favors very large arrays, whereas at mmWave and THz bands small-array MIMO links also operate in the near field but offer limited focusing gain. These observations motivate a joint BF and AN design tailored to NF-MIMO and the exploration of additional antenna-domain DoF, which we develop next.

Fluid antenna (FA) technology
[
40
,
36
,
41
]
enables dynamic repositioning of the radiating element or active port within a compact region, thereby creating position-domain diversity on top of conventional spatial and pattern diversity and offering a flexible means to combat fading, interference, and eavesdropping with compact hardware. Recent studies have started to exploit this capability for secure and covert communications. In
[
31
]
, FA-enabled transceivers are used to enhance secrecy and covertness by opportunistically selecting favorable antenna positions. Continuous-trajectory FA index modulation
[
37
,
15
]
is introduced in
[
16
]
to embed covert information into the antenna trajectory, while
[
10
]
provides secrecy performance analysis of FA-assisted wiretap channels under spatial correlation. The integration of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) with FA is examined in
[
9
]
, showing additional gains from joint reflection and position diversity, and
[
23
]
demonstrates that compact ultra-massive FA arrays can support reliable and secure multiuser transmission via port selection and interference shaping. Furthermore, trajectory- and geometry-aware FA designs are investigated in
[
8
,
6
]
, where mechanical or three-dimensional FA movement is optimized and combined with friendly jamming to reinforce secure or covert links. These works collectively confirm that FA can effectively enhance PLS by enriching the available DoF through spatial and positional reconfiguration.

However, most existing FA-based secure schemes are formulated under far-field (FF) or simplified propagation models and predominantly employ BF, port selection, or trajectory control in single-antenna or MISO-type settings. They do not explicitly address high-frequency MIMO systems with non-extremely-large arrays, where the Rayleigh distance is still comparable to practical link distances and near-field characteristics such as angle–distance coupling and distance-selective focusing remain pronounced. In this regime, BF-only FA designs provide limited capability to exploit the full near-field structure for secrecy enhancement. Motivated by these observations, this work proposes an AN-aided PLS scheme for NF-MIMO with FAS, in which AN and HBF are jointly designed to fully exploit the geometry- and position-domain DoF offered by FA-MIMO. To summarize, the work and contributions of this paper are as follows:

\bullet

We develop an alternating-optimization (AO) framework tailored to the near-field FA-MIMO architecture, which decomposes the original mixed-integer formulation into a continuous BF/AN joint-design subproblem and a discrete FA port-selection subproblem. This structure provides a systematic way to exploit geometry-domain DoF for secure transmission under practical HBF constraints.

\bullet

For the BF/AN joint-design subproblem, we use block coordinate descent (BCD) and generalized spectral-domain water-filling to obtain closed-form solutions under the total power constraint. The fully digital BF/AN design is balanced across data streams and realized by a hardware-consistent HBF architecture, embedding AN in the baseband without extra radio-frequency (RF) chains.

\bullet

For FA port selection, we propose a row-energy based prune–refit rule aligned with Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions of a group-sparsity surrogate, efficiently determining the active ports. This approach preserves near-digital secrecy performance with lower complexity and adapts FA activation jointly with BF and AN.

II
FA-MIMO Near-Field Channel Model and Secure System Model

As shown in Fig.
1
, a FAS-enabled NF-MIMO secure communication system consists of a transmitter (Alice), a legitimate receiver (Bob), and a potential eavesdropper (Eve).
At the transmitter, the FAS contains
N_{\text{t}}
FA elements  (
L\geq N_{\text{t}}\gg 1

[
35
]
.
We consider a discretized FAS model that is suitable for hardware implementation.
A linear rail filled with electrolyte is preconfigured with
L
equally spaced docking ports.
The
N_{\text{t}}
FAs can move within the electrolyte and attach to any
N_{\text{t}}
of the ports, which enables fast spatial reconfiguration of antenna positions.
For comparison, Bob and Eve employ conventional FPA arrays with
N_{\text{u}}
and
N_{\text{e}}
uniformly spaced elements, respectively, where
N_{\text{t}}\gg N_{\text{u}}\geq 1
and
N_{\text{t}}\gg N_{\text{e}}\geq 1
.

In MIMO systems operating at high-frequency bands such as THz and mmWave ranges, the near-field characteristics of electromagnetic propagation become significant.
The corresponding Rayleigh distance is expressed as
r_{\text{MIMO-RD}}=\tfrac{2\left(D_{\text{A}}+D_{\text{B/E}}\right)^{2}}{\lambda}

[
17
]
,
where
\lambda
is the carrier wavelength,
D_{\text{A}}
,
D_{\text{B}}
, and
D_{\text{E}}
denote the physical apertures of the antenna arrays at Alice, Bob, and Eve, respectively.
CSI acquisition is generally difficult in wireless communication and becomes even more challenging for the eavesdropper’s channel.
This work adopts a practical assumption based on realistic conditions.
An eavesdropper may disguise itself as a line-of-sight (LoS) target such as a household robot whose location can be easily accessed.
In such cases, the transmitter can perform directional shielding and jamming toward the corresponding direction and distance.
Under near-field conditions, channel fading is strongly related to the spatial positions of the transceivers.
Therefore, the locations of both the legitimate user and the potential eavesdropper are assumed to be available through positioning or integrated sensing and communication techniques
[
26
,
12
]
.
As a result, the CSIs of the Alice–Bob and Alice–Eve links are regarded as known.

II-B

Secure Transmission Model

In this system, Alice transmits confidential information to Bob while preventing Eve from intercepting it.
Eve is assumed to be located closer to Alice than Bob, so that
d_{\text{E}}<d_{\text{B}}
.
To enhance transmission security, Alice employs a HBF architecture with an FA array.
Among the
L
candidate FA ports arranged on a dense grid, only
N_{\text{t}}
ports are activated.
During the selection process, a support set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}
is maintained to indicate the indices of the currently active ports.
The corresponding dynamic selection matrix is denoted by
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{B}^{L\times|\mathcal{S}|}
, which extracts the columns of the
L\times L
identity matrix indexed by
\mathcal{S}
.
Each column of
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
contains one nonzero element, and each row contains at most one.
After the iterative pruning and refitting procedure converges, the final support set
\mathcal{S}^{\star}
satisfies
|\mathcal{S}^{\star}|=N_{\text{t}}
, and the corresponding selection matrix is
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\in\mathbb{B}^{L\times N_{\text{t}}}
.

Let the dense HBF targets be
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times K}
for the
K
-stream data vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times 1}
for AN.
Row selection on the activated ports yields the sparse FA–MIMO BF matrix and AN vector as

\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}},

(8)

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}=\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}=\mathbf{W}_{\text{HB}}\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}.

(9)

Here
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
is a baseband AN weight. The AN is generated at baseband and embedded in the transmitted signal through the same hybrid beamformer. No dedicated AN transmission chain is required, which simplifies the hardware.

Let
z\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
be a scalar AN symbol independent of
\mathbf{x}
. The transmit vector is

\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{x}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\,z\big)=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{v}\,z\ \in\ \mathbb{C}^{L\times 1}.

(10)

The total transmit power satisfies

\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{H}})+\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{H}})\;\leq\;P_{\mathrm{t}}.

(11)

The received signals at Bob and Eve are

\mathbf{y}_{\text{\text{B}}}=\mathbf{H}_{\text{\text{B}}}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n}_{\text{\text{B}}}=\mathbf{H}_{\text{\text{B}}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{H}_{\text{\text{B}}}\mathbf{v}\,z+\mathbf{n}_{\text{\text{B}}},

(12)

\mathbf{y}_{\text{E}}=\mathbf{H}_{\text{E}}\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n}_{\text{E}}=\mathbf{H}_{\text{E}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{H}_{\text{E}}\mathbf{v}\,z+\mathbf{n}_{\text{E}},

(13)

where
\mathbf{n}_{\text{\text{B}}}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{u}}})
and

\mathbf{n}_{\text{E}}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{e}}})
are additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). We normalize the channels as

\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{U}}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{\text{\text{B}}}/\sqrt{\sigma^{2}},\qquad\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E}}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{\text{E}}/\sqrt{\sigma^{2}}.

(14)

Defining the signal and AN covariances

\mathbf{S}\triangleq\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\text{H}},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{\rm AN}\triangleq\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\text{H}}=\mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}^{\text{H}}\mathbf{W}^{\text{H}},

(15)

the achievable data rates are

R_{\text{\text{B}}}=\log\det\!\Big(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{u}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{U}}\,\mathbf{S}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{U}}^{\text{H}}\,(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{u}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{U}}\,\mathbf{A}_{\rm AN}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{U}}^{\text{H}})^{-1}\Big),

(16)

R_{\text{E}}=\log\det\!\Big(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E}}\,\mathbf{S}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E}}^{\text{H}}\,(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E}}\,\mathbf{A}_{\rm AN}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E}}^{\text{H}})^{-1}\Big),

(17)

and the SR is

R_{\text{s}}=\big[R_{\text{\text{B}}}-R_{\text{E}}\big]^{+}.

(18)

Based on the above secrecy rate definition, we formulate the joint optimization problem as

\displaystyle\max_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}},\,\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre},\,\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}}\ \

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}\big(\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}},\,\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}\big),

(19a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}^{\!H}\tilde{\mathbf{W}})+\|\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{\rm t},

(19b)

\displaystyle\big\|\,[\,\|\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{i,:}\|_{2},\ |\!([\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}])_{i}\!|\,]_{i=1}^{N_{\text{t}}}\big\|_{0}\leq N_{\rm t},

(19c)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm t}\times K},\ \mathbf{v}_{\rm pre}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1},

(19d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}\in\mathbb{B}^{L\times N_{\rm t}}.

(19e)

Problem (
19
) is an mixed-integer programming (MIP) problem with sparsity constraints. To obtain a tractable and hardware-consistent solution, we adopt an AO approach that decomposes the task into BF/AN subproblems and FA port-selection subproblems. In the BF/AN subproblem, given a fixed FA-port set
\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}
, a BCD method combined with a generalized water-filling procedure is used to obtain the higher-DoF fully digital beamforming (DBF) and AN. Subsequently, we fit the common-mode RF HBF via least squares to obtain
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}
and the column-space AN structure
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}
. For the FA port-selection subproblem, ports are ranked using the joint row-energy score
e_{i}=\big\|\,[\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}(i,:),\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}(i)]\,\big\|_{2}
, and the weakest rows are pruned. This progressively reduces the FA-port set from the initial
|\mathsf{\mathcal{S}^{0}}|=L
down to
|\mathsf{\mathcal{S}^{\star}}|=N_{\mathrm{t}}
.

III
Beamforming and Artificial Noise Optimization

This section considers BF/AN design under a fixed FA-port set.
A fully digital baseline is computed using a BCD routine with generalized water filling and a one-dimensional power allocation between data and AN.
A constant-modulus HBF realization is then obtained via least-squares fitting, yielding
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}
and the column-space AN structure
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}
.
FA-port selection is addressed in the Section
IV
.
The dense BF/AN design is detailed below.

III-A

Block Coordinate Descent for AN/BF Joint Optimization

Given a placement set
\mathcal{S}
with selection matrix
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
that extracts the active FA ports from the
L
-port rail, define the noise-whitened equivalent channels
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathcal{S}}=\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{u}}\times|\mathcal{S}|}

\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}=\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E}}\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{e}}\times|\mathcal{S}|}

Let
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times K}

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times 1}
denote the fully digital dense BF matrix and AN vector on the
|\mathcal{S}|

R_{\mathrm{s}}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})
is reformulated as

\displaystyle{\displaystyle R}_{\text{s}}({\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}},{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}}_{\mathcal{S}},\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}},\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}})=

(20)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\log\det\left({\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{u}}}+\frac{{\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}}}{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}}{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}}^{\text{H}}{\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}}}^{\text{H}}}{{\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{u}}}+{\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}}}{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}{\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{U},\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}}}^{\text{H}}}\right)}_{r_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\log\det\left({\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}}_{\mathcal{S}}{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}\right)}_{r_{2}}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\log\det\left({\mathbf{I}}_{N_{\text{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}\right)}_{r_{3}}

where
\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathsf{\mathcal{S}}|\times(K+1)}
is defined as

\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\big[\,\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\,\big],

(21)

with
\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}=\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}.

According to
[
24
, Lemma 4.1]
and the Fenchel conjugate construction in
[
3
, Example 11.7]
, the variational form of the Bob-side log-determinant term
r_{1}
is

{{r}_{1}}=\underset{{\mathbf{Q}}_{\text{\text{B}}}\succ 0,\,{\mathbf{J}}_{\text{\text{B}}}}{\max}\,\log\det({\mathbf{Q}}_{\text{\text{B}}})-\operatorname{Tr}\!\big({\mathbf{Q}}_{\text{\text{B}}}\,{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{\text{B}}}(\mathbf{U}_{\text{\text{\text{B}}}},\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})\big)+K,

(22)

where
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\text{B}}\succ 0
and
{\mathbf{J}}_{\text{B}}
are auxiliary variables, and
{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{B}}(\cdot)
denotes the mean-square error (MSE) matrix to be specified below.

At Bob, the interference-plus-noise covariance is defined in

\mathbf{J}_{\text{\text{B}}}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{u}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{\text{\text{B}}},\mathcal{S}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{\text{\text{B}}},\mathcal{S}}^{\text{H}}.

(23)

For fixed
(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})
and
\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}
, the MSE matrix associated with a linear receive filter
\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{u}}\times K}
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}(\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}},\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})

(24)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{I}_{K}-\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{K}-\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)^{\!\mathrm{H}}+\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}.

Since
\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}\succ\mathbf{0}
, the objective
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\big)
is strictly convex in
\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}
, and the unique minimizer is

\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\star}=\big(\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}.

(25)

Substituting (
25
) into (
24
) yields the minimal MSE (MMSE) matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\star}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\big(\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\star},\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)

(26)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbf{I}_{K}+\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\Big)^{-1}.

This sequence of definitions fixes
{\mathbf{J}}_{\text{B}}
, specifies
{\mathbf{G}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)
, and identifies the optimal receive filter
\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\star}
, which together instantiate the variational representation in (
22
).

Invoking the concave–convex conjugate identity of the log-determinant, for any
\mathbf{G}\succ\mathbf{0}
,

-\log\det(\mathbf{G})=\max_{\mathbf{Q}\succ\mathbf{0}}\ \log\det(\mathbf{Q})-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{G})+K,

Since the objective is strictly concave in
\mathbf{Q}
, the maximizer is unique and satisfies
\mathbf{Q}^{\star}=\mathbf{G}^{-1}
.
Substituting
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\star}
gives the optimizer associated with (
22
):

\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}}^{\star}=\big(\mathbf{G}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\star}\big)^{-1}.

(27)

Similarly, the Eve AN-only covariance log-det term
r_{2}
admits the scalar variational representation

r_{2}=\max_{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}},\,Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}>0}\ \log Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}-Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,G_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}+1.

(28)

Here the auxiliary variables are the linear receive filter

\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\mathrm{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{e}}\times 1},

(29)

and the associated MSE

G_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}=\big|\,1-\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\,\big|^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(30)

Maximization of (
28
) with respect to the scalar weight yields

Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\star}=\big(G_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\big)^{-1},

(31)

and, for fixed
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}
, the maximizing filter is

\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\star}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\mathrm{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}.

(32)

Substituting (
31
)–(
32
) into (
28
) reproduces the original Eve-side log term and, for fixed auxiliaries, yields a quadratic surrogate in
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}
.

Next, for the Eve signal-plus-AN covariance log-det term
r_{3}
, observe that

r_{3}=\log\det\!\Big(\mathbf{I}_{N_{\mathrm{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\Big)
,
and introduce

\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{N_{\mathrm{e}}}+\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}.

(33)

By the concave–convex conjugate identity for the log–determinant,
-\log\det(\mathbf{G})=\max_{\mathbf{Q}\succ\mathbf{0}}\,\log\det(\mathbf{Q})-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{G})+\dim(\mathbf{G})
,
-r_{3}
admits the matrix variational representation

-\,r_{3}=\max_{\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}\succ\mathbf{0}}\;\log\det(\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}})-\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}\,\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}\big)+N_{\mathrm{e}},

(34)

whose unique maximizer is

\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}^{\star}=\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}^{-1}.

(35)

With the auxiliaries
\{\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}},\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}}\}
,
\{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}},Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\}
, and
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}
fixed, the
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
, and
r_{3}
admit quadratic surrogates in
(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})
that separate design variables from constants as

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle=-\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{F}_{b,\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)+2\,\Re\!\big\{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}})\big\}

(36)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\log\det(\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}})+K-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}})-\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}}\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{J}_{\text{B}}\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}\big)}_{c_{\text{B}}}.

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle=-\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{F}_{e,\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}+2\,\Re\!\big\{\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big\}

(37)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\log Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}+1-Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\!\left(1+\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}\|_{2}^{2}\right)}_{c_{\mathrm{E}}}.

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle=-\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)-\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}

(38)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{N_{\mathrm{e}}+\log\det(\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}})-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}})}_{c_{\mathrm{Z}}}.

The Bob-side curvature and linear term are defined as

\mathbf{F}_{b,\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}\,\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}}\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(39)

\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{B},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{B}}\,\mathbf{Q}_{\text{B}},

(40)

the Eve-side counterparts are

\mathbf{F}_{e,\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}\,Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}}\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(41)

\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{E}}\,Q_{\mathrm{E},\mathcal{S}},

(42)

and the aggregate Eve-side curvature is

\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{Z},\mathcal{S}}\,\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{E},\mathcal{S}}\succeq\mathbf{0}.

(43)

Dropping the constants
c_{\text{B}},c_{\mathrm{E}},c_{\mathrm{Z}}
, the SR maximization over the design variables reduces to the convex quadratic program (CQP)

\displaystyle\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}}\ \

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big)-2\,\Re\!\big\{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}})\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}-2\,\Re\!\big\{\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big\},

(44a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})\leq P_{\mathrm{t}}.

(44b)

with

\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\ \triangleq\ \mathbf{F}_{b,\mathcal{S}}+\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{S}},\qquad\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}\ \triangleq\ \mathbf{F}_{b,\mathcal{S}}+\mathbf{F}_{e,\mathcal{S}}+\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{S}}.

(45)

Introducing a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\geq 0
for the total–power constraint (
44b
), the Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}};\lambda)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}})-2\,\Re\!\big\{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}})\big\}

(46)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}-2\,\Re\!\big\{\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\big\}

\displaystyle+\lambda\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})-P_{\mathrm{t}}\right).

Minimizing (
46
) with respect to
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}
gives

(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})\,\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}},

(47)

(\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}.

(48)

Since
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\succeq\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, the matrices

\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|}
are positive definite for any
\lambda>0
, and the unique minimizers are

\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)=(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}},

(49)

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)=(\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}.

(50)

Equivalently,

(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda),\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda))\in\arg\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}}\ \mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}};\lambda).

(51)

The dual function is defined by the inner minimum

g(\lambda)\triangleq\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}}\ \mathcal{L}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}};\lambda),

(52)

and evaluates in closed form as

\displaystyle g(\lambda)=

\displaystyle-\left\|(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})^{-1/2}\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{\operatorname{F}}^{2}

(53)

\displaystyle-\left\|(\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{|\mathcal{S}|})^{-1/2}\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\lambda P_{\mathrm{t}}.

The dual function in (
52
) can be maximized by a direct one-dimensional search in
\lambda
, such as bisection on the complementary-slackness residual. This strategy repeatedly inverts
(\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I})
and
(\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda\mathbf{I})
for each trial
\lambda
. When
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
or
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}
is ill conditioned or nearly singular, the residual becomes steep near
\lambda=0
, which degrades numerical stability and amplifies finite-precision effects. The per-iteration cost is also dominated by fresh matrix factorizations with little reuse. These considerations motivate a diagonalized treatment that reveals per-mode monotonicity and enables a robust update of
\lambda
. The complementary slackness condition is

\lambda\Big(\underbrace{\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})+\operatorname{Tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})}_{\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})}-P_{\mathrm{t}}\Big)=0.

(54)

III-B

Generalized Spectral Water–Filling and Streams Power Balance

The dual water level
\lambda
is determined in the eigenbases of
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}
. Diagonalize the Hermitian curvature matrices as

\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathbf{Z}_{A}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{A}\mathbf{Z}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}},\qquad\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{A}=\operatorname{diag}(\xi_{A,1},\ldots,\xi_{A,|\mathcal{S}|}),

(55)

and

\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}=\mathbf{Z}_{B}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{B}\mathbf{Z}_{B}^{\mathrm{H}},\qquad\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{B}=\operatorname{diag}(\xi_{B,1},\ldots,\xi_{B,|\mathcal{S}|}),

(56)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{A},\mathbf{Z}_{B}
collect orthonormal eigenvectors and
\xi_{A,i},\xi_{B,i}\geq 0
are the eigenvalues. Rotate the linear terms into these eigenbases as

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}\triangleq\mathbf{Z}_{A}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}},\qquad\widehat{\mathbf{r}}\triangleq\mathbf{Z}_{B}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}.

(57)

With (
55
)–(
57
), the closed-form minimizers in (
49
)–(
50
) take mode-wise shrinkage forms,

\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)=\mathbf{Z}_{A}\,\operatorname{diag}\!\big((\xi_{A,i}+\lambda)^{-1}\big)\,\widehat{\mathbf{R}},

(58)

and

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)=\mathbf{Z}_{B}\,\operatorname{diag}\!\big((\xi_{B,i}+\lambda)^{-1}\big)\,\widehat{\mathbf{r}}.

(59)

The corresponding powers separate across spectral modes:

\big\|\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)\big\|_{\operatorname{F}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\frac{\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{i,:}\big\|_{2}^{2}}{(\xi_{A,i}+\lambda)^{2}},

(60)

and

\big\|\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)\big\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\frac{|\widehat{r}_{i}|^{2}}{(\xi_{B,i}+\lambda)^{2}}.

(61)

Enforcing the total-power constraint reduces to solving the scalar equation

\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\frac{\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{i,:}\big\|_{2}^{2}}{(\xi_{A,i}+\lambda)^{2}}\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\frac{|\widehat{r}_{i}|^{2}}{(\xi_{B,i}+\lambda)^{2}}\;=\;P_{\mathrm{t}}.

(62)

The left-hand side of (
62
) is continuous and strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)
, so a unique solution
\lambda^{\star}
exists. A safeguarded bisection directly applies to (
62
); after the one-time eigendecompositions in (
55
)–(
56
), each iteration evaluates only the two spectral sums and requires no additional matrix inversions. Working in the diagonalized basis also improves numerical stability when
\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
or
\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}
is ill-conditioned.

Substituting
\lambda^{\star}
into (
58
)–(
59
) gives the primal updates

\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}=\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda^{\star}),\quad\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}=\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda^{\star}),

(63)

which meet the power constraint with equality and minimize the quadratic surrogate for the fixed auxiliaries.

Although (
63
) minimizes the surrogate, it may concentrate data power on a few streams when
K>1
. A right-unitary post-processing is therefore applied to equalize the per-stream powers without altering the covariance or the total power. Let
\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{d}}\triangleq\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star})^{\mathrm{H}}
with total power
P_{\mathrm{d}}\triangleq\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{d}})
. For any unitary
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times K}
, the rotated precoder
\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}=\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}\boldsymbol{\Omega}
satisfies
\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}=\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{d}}
and
\operatorname{Tr}(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}})=P_{\mathrm{d}}
, hence the secrecy objective remains unchanged.

Stream-power dispersion is measured by
\mathbf{p}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})\triangleq\operatorname{diag}(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{H}}\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}})
. The balancing rotation is selected as

\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathrm{bal}}\in\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\mathrm{H}}\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\mathbf{I}_{K}}\frac{\max_{k}\,\mathbf{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}{\min_{k}\,\mathbf{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\,,

(64)

which minimizes the peak-to-valley ratio of the per-stream powers. In practice,
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathrm{bal}}
is drawn from a small dictionary containing the identity, the
K
-point DFT matrix, and a few random unitary matrices, providing effective balancing at negligible cost.

The final balanced update keeps the AN vector unchanged and rotates only the data precoder:

\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}=\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathrm{bal}},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}=\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star}.

(65)

This post-processing exactly preserves
\mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{d}}
and the total power, improves the conditioning at Bob’s linear receiver, and facilitates the subsequent hybrid realization.

III-C

Hybrid RF-Baseband Precoder with Embedded AN

This subsection specifies the hybrid RF–baseband realization with embedded AN. In the algorithmic flow, it is invoked only after the port-selection routine in Section
IV
returns a final support
\mathcal{S}
with
|\mathcal{S}|=N_{\text{t}}
. At that point the indices are fixed, a long refit on
\mathcal{S}
is performed, and the balancing step in (
65
) yields the digital pair
(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}})
. Based on this pair, a hybrid RF–baseband approximation is constructed in which the AN lies in the data column space. The RF precoder
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times N_{\mathrm{RF}}}
(equivalently
\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{t}}\times N_{\mathrm{RF}}}
when
|\mathcal{S}|=N_{\text{t}}
) is implemented by phase shifters and has constant-modulus entries, whereas the baseband precoder
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{RF}}\times K}
is unconstrained. The AN is embedded through a baseband weight
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times 1}
. Define the target block and the coupling block as

\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}=\big[\,\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}},\ \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}\,\big]\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times(K+1)},

(66)

\mathbf{S}=\big[\,\mathbf{I}_{K},\ \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}\,\big]\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times(K+1)}.

(67)

The induced hybrid pair is
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times K}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times 1}
.

The hybrid fitting problem reads

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}},\,\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}},\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}}\quad

\displaystyle\big\|\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}-\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\mathbf{S}\,\big\|_{\text{F}}^{2},

(68a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\big|[\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}]_{m,n}\big|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|}}.

(68b)

With
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}
fixed, the least-squares update of the baseband precoder is

\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}=\big(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}\,\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{H}}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)^{-1}.

(69)

With
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}
and
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}
fixed, the column-space AN weight follows from the orthogonal projection

\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pre}}=\big(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}^{\mathrm{H}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}},\qquad\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}.

(70)

For the RF stage, let
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{RF}}\times(K+1)}
and
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times(K+1)}
.
Denote by
\mathbf{f}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times 1}
the
n
-th RF column and define

\mathbf{x}_{n}\triangleq\big(\mathbf{X}_{n,:}\big)^{\mathrm{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K+1)\times 1}
.
The per-column RF update is the phase projection

{{\mathbf{f}}_{n}}\ \leftarrow\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{{{N}_{\text{t}}}}}{{e}^{\iota\angle({{\mathbf{g}}_{n}})}},

(71)

where the auxiliary vector
\mathbf{g}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times 1}
is computed as

\mathbf{g}_{n}=\frac{\big(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathrm{bal}}-\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{f}_{n}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)\mathbf{x}_{n}}{\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{x}_{n}^{\mathrm{H}}\mathbf{x}_{n}\big)}.

(72)

Finally, define the realized hybrid block
\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{HB}}\triangleq[\,\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}},\ \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}\,]
.
Power normalization uses the trace-based gain

\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{P_{\text{t}}}{\operatorname{Tr}\!\big(\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{HB}}\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{HB}}^{\mathrm{H}}\big)}}\,,

(73)

and the digital part is scaled as
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}\leftarrow\alpha\,\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}
.
This induces
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}\leftarrow\alpha\,\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{HB}}
and
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}\leftarrow\alpha\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{HB}}
while keeping
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}
constant modulus.

IV
Fluid Antenna Port Positions Selection

This section develops a selection rule for FA ports based on the quadratic surrogate in
(
44
) with Lagrangian (
46
), written in terms of

\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}
in (
21
) for a given support

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}
.

For some multiplier
\lambda\geq 0
, the stationarity conditions
(
47
)–(
48
) can be expressed in gradient form as

\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\!*}}\mathcal{J}=\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}-\mathbf{R}_{w,\mathcal{S}}=-\lambda\,\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},

(74)

\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\!*}}\mathcal{J}=\mathbf{B}_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}-\mathbf{r}_{v,\mathcal{S}}=-\lambda\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}.

(75)

where
\mathcal{J}
denotes the quadratic objective in
(
44a
).
Let
\mathbf{t}_{i,:}
denote the
i
-th row of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}
and define the joint
row-energy score

e_{i}\;\triangleq\;\|\mathbf{t}_{i,:}\|_{2}=\big\|\,[\,\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}(i,:),\ \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}(i)\,]\big\|_{2}.

(76)

From the above gradients, the block-gradient norm associated with row
i
satisfies

\big\|\nabla_{i}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}})\big\|_{2}=\lambda\,e_{i},

(77)

so ordering rows by
e_{i}
is equivalent, up to the common factor
\lambda
, to ordering them
by the corresponding block-gradient norms.

To relate this ranking to a convex sparse model, consider the group-Lasso surrogate

[
33
,
13
]

\min_{\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\,\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}}}\ \ \mathcal{J}(\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\mathcal{S}},\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{S}})+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\|\mathbf{t}_{i,:}\|_{2},\qquad\mu>0,

(78)

with
\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathbf{t}_{i,:}
as above.
Problem (
78
) is convex and its optimal solutions satisfy
the row-wise KKT conditions
[
3
, Sec. 5.5]
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{t}_{i,:}^{\star}\|_{2}>0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\nabla_{i}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star})=\mu\,\mathbf{t}_{i,:}^{\star}/\|\mathbf{t}_{i,:}^{\star}\|_{2},

(79)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{t}_{j,:}^{\star}\|_{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\|\nabla_{j}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\star})\|_{2}\leq\mu.

(80)

Combining (
77
) with (
79
)–(
80
)
indicates that rows with small
e_{i}
are closest to the inactive KKT pattern of
(
78
), whereas rows with large
e_{i}
are encouraged to remain
active. The scores
e_{i}
thus act as importance indicators for the FA ports.

In the proposed selector, the current support
\mathcal{S}_{t}
at stage
t
is interpreted as
an approximate active set of (
78
). Refitting on
\mathcal{S}_{t}

yields a point whose gradients on the retained rows are small, while the rows outside the support
exhibit larger gradients. When such a gradient-norm separation is present, one can associate

\mathcal{S}_{t}
with an approximate KKT point of (
78
) for a
suitable
\mu
, so pruning rows with the smallest
e_{i}
is consistent with the convex surrogate
and aligns with the behavior of reweighted
\ell_{1}
and hard-thresholding strategies

[
4
,
2
]
.

This principle is implemented through a staged prune–refit routine that removes several indices
at each stage.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{t}
be the current support,
0<\eta<1
the pruning ratio, and

m_{\min}\geq 1
the minimum batch size. The number of indices removed at stage
t
is

d_{t}\;\triangleq\;\min\!\Big\{\,|\mathcal{S}_{t}|-N_{\text{t}},\ \max\!\big(m_{\min},\ \lfloor\eta\,(|\mathcal{S}_{t}|-N_{\text{t}})\rfloor\big)\Big\}.

(81)

Given the current scores
e_{i}
, the deletion set is chosen as

\mathcal{D}_{t}\;\in\;\arg\min_{\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{t},\;|\mathcal{D}|=d_{t}}\ \sum_{i\in\mathcal{D}}e_{i},

(82)

namely the
d_{t}
indices with the smallest row energies, and the support is updated as

\mathcal{S}_{t+1}\;\leftarrow\;\mathcal{S}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{t}.

(83)

Each stage consists of refitting the BF/AN variables on
\mathcal{S}_{t}
, computing the scores

e_{i}
, and pruning according to (
81
)–(
83
). The iterations
terminate once
|\mathcal{S}_{t}|=N_{\text{t}}
, followed by a final refit on the terminal support
to reduce shrinkage bias.

The selection matrices follow the support updates. With

\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{L}(:,\mathcal{S})
, the initialization is

\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{1,\ldots,L\}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}=\mathbf{I}_{L}
.
After each pruning step in (
83
), the selection matrix is updated as

\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{S}_{t+1}}=\mathbf{I}_{L}(:,\mathcal{S}_{t+1})=\mathbf{I}_{L}\big(:,\mathcal{S}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{t}\big),

(84)

which ensures that subsequent BF/AN updates are always carried out on the currently active
FA ports.

VI
Conclusion

This paper investigated AN-aided PLS for NF FA–MIMO systems with discretized FAS architectures. An AO-based framework was developed that jointly optimizes the BF/AN structure and selects a sparse set of FA port positions under an HBF implementation with column-space embedded AN. The proposed design leverages NF geometry and range-dependent channel diversity to provide notable secrecy-rate gains over BF-only benchmarks for practically sized arrays, while naturally converging to BF-dominant operation as the antenna number and aperture grows. Numerical results further showed that FA port reconfiguration offers an additional secrecy advantage over conventional FPAs under the same RF-chain budget by enabling finer control of the distance-domain field distribution.

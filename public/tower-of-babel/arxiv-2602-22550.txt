the global well-posedness of the multi-dimensional compressible Euler system with damping in the L p L^{p} critical Besov spaces for p < 2 p<2
Jianzhong Zhang, Ying Sui, Xiliang Li*

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Cauchy’s problem of the compressible Euler system with damping and establish the global-in-time well-posedness in L p L^{p} -type critical Besov spaces for 1 ≤ p < 2 1\leq p<2 . To achieve it, a new product estimate is established in L 2 L^{2} - L p L^{p} hybrid Besov spaces.

1.
Introduction

This work studies the compressible Euler equations with linear damping, a model describing gas flow through a porous medium where the solid matrix exerts a frictional force proportional to and opposite to the fluid momentum. The governing system is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mbox{\rm div}\;\!(\rho{\bm{u}})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho{\bm{u}})+\nabla\cdot(\rho{\bm{u}}\otimes{\bm{u}})+\nabla P(\rho)+\alpha\rho{\bm{u}}=0\end{aligned}\right.

for
t\geq 0,\ \bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}(d\geq 1)
and a damping coefficient
\alpha>0

. Here
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
{\bm{u}}=(u^{1},u^{2},\cdots,u^{d})^{\top}
(
\top
represents transpose) denote the density and velocity of fluid flow, respectively. The pressure function
P=P(\rho)
is assumed to be smooth around the constant reference density
\bar{\rho}>0
.

There are lots of mathematical results about the existence and asymptotic behavior of system (
1.1
) in sobolev space (see
[
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
). In critical Besov space, Fang and Xu
[
11
]
(with improvements in
[
15
]
) studied the existence and asymptotic behavior of classical solutions. Later, Xu and Wang
[
24
]
justified the relaxation convergence from (
1.1
) to the porous medium equation. And also, there are some results on hyperbolic system for balance laws including (
1.1
) (see
[
22
,
23
]
). Recently, Crin-Barat and Danchin
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
improved those results
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
in the
L^{2}
-
L^{p}
hybrid Besov spaces with
2\leq p\leq\max\{4,\frac{2d}{d+2}\}
, where the low frequencies are bounded in
L^{p}
-type spaces and the high frequencies in
L^{2}
-type spaces with a specific linearity assumption. And then, the first author and Xu
[
25
]
remove the assumption and to show that the results of
[
4
,
5
]
hold true for the general pressure function. Very recently, Crin-Barat and Song
[
9
]
extend the work of
[
4
,
5
,
25
]
to the case
p\in[2,\infty)
. And a natural question is how about
p<2
?

Introducing a new unknown called “enthalpy”

n(\rho)\triangleq\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{P^{\prime}(s)}{s}ds.
System (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n+P^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}+G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}=0\\
&\partial_{t}{\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{v}}+\nabla n=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\bm{v}}\end{aligned}\right.

with the initial data

(1.3)

\displaystyle(n,{\bm{v}})|_{t=0}=(n_{0},{\bm{v}}_{0}).

Here
P^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})>0
for
\bar{\rho}>0
,
\bar{\rho}=P^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})=1
and the composite function
G(n)
is smooth.

Define
J_{\varepsilon}=-[\log_{2}\varepsilon]+k
being the threshold between high and low frequencies with a suitable integer
k
is to be determined later, and the space
E_{T}^{J_{\varepsilon}}
as follows

\begin{split}&E_{T}^{J_{\varepsilon}}=\bigg\{(n,{\bm{v}})|(n^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}},{\bm{v}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}([0,T];{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}),\ \varepsilon n^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+2}),\ {\bm{v}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}),\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{v}}\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}),\\
&\qquad\qquad(\varepsilon n^{h,J_{\varepsilon}},\varepsilon{\bm{v}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}([0,T];{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}),\ (n^{h,J_{\varepsilon}},{\bm{v}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}})\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}),\varepsilon^{-1}{\bm{v}}+\nabla n\in\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})\bigg\}.\end{split}

When
T=\infty
we also use
E_{\infty}^{J_{\varepsilon}}
for convenience.
For the reader’s convenience, We introduce the notations
\|\cdot\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}
and

\|\cdot\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{2},1}^{s}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}
to denote Besov semi-norms with respect to the threshold
J_{\varepsilon}
, that is,

(1.4)

\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s_{1}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\triangleq\sum_{j\geq J_{\varepsilon}}2^{s_{1}j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{q_{1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{2},1}^{s_{2}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\triangleq\sum_{j\leq J_{\varepsilon}-1}2^{s_{2}j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{q_{2}}.

It is not difficult to deduce that for all
\sigma_{0}>0
,

(1.5)

\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s_{1}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\leq 2^{-\sigma_{0}J_{\varepsilon}}\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s_{1}+\sigma_{0}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\leq 2^{\sigma_{0}J_{\varepsilon}}\|f\|_{{\dot{B}}_{q_{1},1}^{s_{1}-\sigma_{0}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}.

The main goal of this paper is to broaden the assumption on
p
and to show that the results of
[
4
,
5
,
25
]
hold true for the case
p<2
. Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
1\leq p<2
,
d\geq 1
and the pressure
P
satisfies
P^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})>0
for
\bar{\rho}>0
. There is a small constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that if

\|(n_{0},{\bm{v}}_{0})\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon\|(n_{0},{\bm{v}}_{0})\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\leq\delta_{1},

then the Cauchy problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) admits a uniform global unique solution in
E_{\infty}^{J_{\varepsilon}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}
when
p<2
, that means the existence and uniqueness of solutions hold in a smaller space rather than escaping to the complement
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}\setminus\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}
. On the other hand, (
1.4
) implies that a smaller space for
p
corresponds to more singular and concentrated data, which naturally arises in many physical problems (such as point sources and vortex filaments).

Remark 1.3
.

Based on Theorem
1.1
, the relaxation limit for the case
p<2
can be performed by the same procedure as
[
5
]
.

The major difficulty of proof of Theorem
1.1
lies in dealing with the nonlinear terms. To achieve it, a new product estimation will be developed, see Lemma
2.1
below. As a matter of fact, the new tool could be applied to investigate other systems, such as the hyperbolic-parabolic chemotaxis system (see
[
7
]
) in
L^{2}-L^{p}
framework and Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
10
]
) in
L^{p}-L^{2}
framework for
p<2
.

2.
Global a priori estimates

In this section, we only give the key a priori estimate for the case
p<2
, which lead to the global existence and uniqueness of solutions in
E_{\infty}^{J_{\varepsilon}}
. See
[
8
]
for more details. For convenience, we use
f\lesssim g
to denote that there exists a generic constant
C>0
independent on
\varepsilon
such that
f\leq Cg
in this section.

First of all, we establish the following product estimation in
L^{p}-L^{2}
hybrid Besov spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<s_{1}<\frac{d}{2}
,
1\leq p<2
. Then, we have the following inequality

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|ab\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\leq C\left(\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}

with
C
independent on
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Using Bony’s paraproduct decomposition, we have

\displaystyle ab=T_{a}b+R[a,b]+T_{b}a\quad\mbox{with}\quad T_{a}b\triangleq\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b\quad\mbox{and}\quad R[a,b]\triangleq\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime}|\leq 1}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime\prime}}a\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b.

First, we bound
T_{a}b
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\|T_{a}b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\leq\sum_{j\leq J-2\atop|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}2^{s_{1}j}\|\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a\dot{\Delta}_{j}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}+\sum_{j\leq J-2\atop|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{s_{1}j}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}.

The embedding
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq J-2\atop|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}2^{s_{1}j}\|\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a\dot{\Delta}_{j}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j\leq J-2}2^{s_{1}j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}b\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq J-2\atop|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{s_{1}j}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\Big(\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq J-2}2^{s_{1}j^{\prime}}+\sum\limits_{J-2\leq j^{\prime}\leq J+2}\Big)2^{s_{1}j^{\prime}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},S_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\Delta_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}.

The Young-like inequality ensures that the commutator estimate in
[
2
, Lemma 2.97]
also holds for
[\dot{\Delta}_{j},S_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\Delta_{j^{\prime}}b

and therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}\leq J-2}2^{s_{1}j^{\prime}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},S_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\Delta_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{\infty,1}^{-1}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}.

Similarly, as
\frac{2p}{2-p}\leq 2
when
1\leq p\leq 2
, one has

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{J-2\leq j^{\prime}\leq J+2}2^{s_{1}j^{\prime}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},S_{j^{\prime}-1}a]\Delta_{j^{\prime}}b\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim 2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{\frac{2p}{2-p},1}^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim 2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\|T_{a}b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\left(\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}.

For
T_{b}a
, because
s_{1}-\frac{d}{2}<0
, it follows the classical paraproduct estimate (see
[
10
]
) that

\displaystyle\|T_{b}a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{\frac{2p}{2-p},1}^{s_{1}-\frac{d}{2}}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{s_{1}+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\lesssim\left(\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}.

Finally, by classical remainder estimates (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.85]
) we can directly obtain

\displaystyle\|R[a,b]\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{\frac{2p}{2-p},1}^{s_{1}-\frac{d}{2}}}\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}\lesssim\left(\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{s_{1}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+2^{(s_{1}-\frac{d}{p})J_{\varepsilon}}\|b\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\|a\|_{{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}.

Adding above three inequality together, we can finally obtain (
2.1
).
∎

For simplicity, we define
{\mathcal{X}}(T):=\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{E_{T}^{J\varepsilon}}
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
reduces to establishing a global-in-time a priori estimate. Specifically, we claim that if

\begin{split}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\ll 1\quad\text{on}\quad[0,T],\end{split}

then there exists a constant
C
, independent of
T
and
\varepsilon
, such that

(2.2)

{\mathcal{X}}(T)\leq C\big({\mathcal{X}}(0)+{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T)\big)\qquad\text{for all }\varepsilon>0.

The proof of the inequality (
2.2
) is divided into two steps.

Step 1: The low-frequency estimate in the
L^{p}
framework

Defining the effective velocity as
{\bm{z}}\triangleq\varepsilon^{-1}{\bm{v}}+\nabla n
(cf. the analogous case for Navier-Stokes equations in
[
12
]
), and operating on (
1.2
) with
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
, yields

(2.3)

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}n-\varepsilon\Delta\dot{\Delta}_{j}n=-\varepsilon\mbox{\rm div}\;\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{z}}-\dot{\Delta}_{j}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n)-\dot{\Delta}_{j}(G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}})\\
&\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{z}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{z}}=H\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

with

H=\varepsilon\left(\nabla\Delta\dot{\Delta}_{j}n-\nabla\mbox{\rm div}\;\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{z}}\right)-\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n)-\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}(G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}})-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\dot{\Delta}_{j}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{v}}).

It is clear that the equation on
n
is a heat equation and the equation on
{\bm{z}}
is a damped equation, hence the standard estimate (see
[
8
,
25
]
) implies that

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\|(n,\varepsilon{\bm{z}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\bigg(\varepsilon\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+2})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|{\bm{z}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\\
&\quad\leq C\bigg(\|(n_{0},\varepsilon{\bm{z}}_{0})\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+(1+2^{k})\|({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n,G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\end{split}

with
C
depending only on
d,p,k
.

Next, we will deal with the nonlinear part. For the first nonlinear term, it follows the Lemma
2.1
that

\begin{split}\|{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}&\lesssim\left(\|\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}.\end{split}

Then the interpolation inequalities (see Lemma 3.2 in
[
25
]
) implies

(2.5)

\begin{split}\|{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}&\lesssim\left(\left(\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+2})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
&\quad\times\left(\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon\left(\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
&\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T)+{\mathcal{X}}(T)\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

And similarly, for the convection term we can deduce

(2.6)

\begin{split}\|{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T).\end{split}

For the last nonlinear term, the inequality (
2.1
) implies

\begin{split}\|G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}&\lesssim\|G(n)\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\left(\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right).\end{split}

By the classical composition estimate (see
[
2
]
), we can obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}\|G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\left(\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+1})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\right)\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T).\end{split}

Inserting (
2.5
)-(
2.7
) into (
2.4
), we can obtain

\begin{split}&\|(n,\varepsilon{\bm{z}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\bigg(\varepsilon\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+2})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|{\bm{z}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\\
&\quad\leq C\bigg(\|(n_{0},\varepsilon{\bm{z}}_{0})\|_{\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T)+{\mathcal{X}}(T)\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\bigg),\end{split}

which eventually leads to

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\bigg(\varepsilon\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+2})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|\varepsilon^{-1}{\bm{v}}+\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}+1})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{\ell,J_{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\\
&\quad\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}(0)+{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T).\end{split}

It is known that most hyperbolic systems with no
0
-order terms are ill-posed in
L^{p}
spaces with
p\neq 2
(see
[
1
]
), and the
the
0
-th order damping plays a key role in above Low-frequency analysis.
Next, we turn to the high-frequency analysis. In that case, the formal eigenvalue analysis (see
[
4
]
) implies that it is only suitable within the
L^{2}
framework. Therefore, we use the energy method which is a classical method in dissipative system (such as
[
3
,
22
,
18
,
27
]
)

Step 2: The high-frequency estimates in the
L^{2}
framework

First, we localize system (
1.2
) by applying the operator
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and obtaining

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}n+\mbox{\rm div}\;\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}+G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}n=R_{j}^{1}+R_{j}^{2}\\
&\partial_{t}\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}+\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}n+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}=R_{j}^{3}\end{aligned}\right.

with the commutators defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{j}^{1}:=-\dot{\Delta}_{j}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n)+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}n,\ R_{j}^{2}:=-\dot{\Delta}_{j}(G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}})+G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}},\ R_{j}^{3}:=-\dot{\Delta}_{j}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla{\bm{v}})+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}.\end{split}

By using the
L^{2}
weighted energy method, as in
[
4
,
25
]
, one can deduce that

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\varepsilon{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{2}+\|(\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}n,\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\lesssim\varepsilon\|(\partial_{t}G(n),\nabla G(n),\nabla{\bm{v}})\|_{L^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{2}+{\mathcal{L}}_{j}\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{3}2^{j}\|R_{j}^{i}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

with the Lyapunov functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{2}

\varepsilon{\mathcal{L}}_{j}^{2}:=2^{2j}\varepsilon\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}n,\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\tilde{c}\int\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}ndx\approx 2^{2j}\varepsilon\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}n,\dot{\Delta}_{j}{\bm{v}})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\tilde{c}2^{-k}<1.

And then it follows from the Lemma 5.1. in
[
5
]
that

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\quad\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}(0)+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{t}G(n),\nabla G(n),\nabla{\bm{v}})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}{\mathcal{X}}(T)+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j\geq J_{\varepsilon}}2^{(\frac{d}{2}+1)j}\|R_{j}^{i}\|_{L^{2}}dt.\end{split}

Since
2^{J_{\varepsilon}}\approx 2^{k}\varepsilon^{-1}
, by the spatial embedding

{\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow{\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the classical estimation on smooth functions (see Corollary 2.65 in
[
2
]
) and (
1.5
) we can obtain that

(2.11)

\begin{split}\varepsilon\|(\nabla G(n),\nabla{\bm{v}})\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}\lesssim\varepsilon\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}(T).\end{split}

By the continuity equation, it is not difficult to get

(2.12)

\begin{split}\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}G(n)\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}&\lesssim\varepsilon\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla n+G(n)\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\varepsilon\|\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\ +\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\varepsilon\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}})}\\
&\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}(T)+{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T).\end{split}

Next, we bound the commutator
R_{j}^{i}
(
i=1,2,3
), by the Lemma 2.100 in
[
2
]
we can deduce

(2.13)

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j\geq J_{\varepsilon}}2^{(\frac{d}{2}+1)j}\|R_{j}^{i}\|_{L^{2}}dt\lesssim\varepsilon\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}\left(\|n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}\right)\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T).\end{split}

Substitute (
2.11
)-(
2.13
) into (
2.10
) we can finally obtain

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\varepsilon\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{\infty}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}+\|(n,{\bm{v}})\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}({\dot{B}}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}+1})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{2}({\dot{B}}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\\
&\quad+|\varepsilon^{-1}{\bm{v}}+\nabla n\|_{\widetilde{L}_{T}^{1}(\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}})}^{h,J_{\varepsilon}}\lesssim{\mathcal{X}}(0)+{\mathcal{X}}^{2}(T)+{\mathcal{X}}^{3}(T).\end{split}

The estimate (
2.2
) follows immediately from a combination of (
2.14
) and (
2.8
). Applying a standard bootstrap argument, as detailed in
[
8
]
, establishes the global well-posedness of the solution. Consequently, Theorem
1.1
is proved.

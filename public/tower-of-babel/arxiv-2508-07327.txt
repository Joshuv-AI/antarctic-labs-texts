A note on the quasi-local algebra of expander graphs
Bruno M. Braga, Ján Špakula, Alessandro Vignati

Abstract
. We show that the quasi-local algebra of a coarse disjoint union of expander graphs does not contain a Cartan subalgebra isomorphic to ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} . N. Ozawa has recently shown that these algebras are distinct from the uniform Roe algebras of expander graphs, and our result describes a further difference.



1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on the study of Roe-like algebras of metric spaces arising from expander graphs and their properties. We will in particular focus on quasi-local algebras associated to such spaces and on their Cartan algebras.

In a recent breakthrough, N. Ozawa showed that the quasi-local algebra of a coarse disjoint union of expander graphs is strictly larger than its uniform Roe algebra (
[
Oza25
, Corollary C]
). Up until this result, the question of whether these algebras coincided was one of the important open problems in the field (see
[
ŠT19
,
ŠZ20
,
LNvZ21
,
BBF+24b
]
) which went back to J. Roe (see
[
Roe96
, Page 20]
). In his proof, Ozawa showed that uniform Roe algebras can never contain the product of matrix algebras
\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
while the quasi-local algebra of expander graphs always contains this product. This result suggests a new question: how distinct can the quasi-local and the uniform Roe algebra be? The goal of the present note is to show that the quasi-local algebra of a coarse disjoint union of expander graphs does not have a Cartan subalgebra isomorphic to
\ell_{\infty}
(see Theorem
1.1
), a property shared by all uniform Roe algebras. The proof of this result relies both on earlier work on Cartan subalgebras of Willett and White (
[
WW20
]
) as well as on Ozawa’s (
[
Oza25
]
); as Ozawa, we use the concentration of measure phenomenon in order to rule out the existence of such subalgebras.

Let us carefully introduce our objects. In coarse geometry, there are several algebras of bounded operators which serve as models to code in
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic terms the large scale geometry of metric spaces; these are known as Roe-like algebras. Important for us are two of these, the
uniform Roe
and the
quasi-local algebra
, which we shall now define. Firstly, as coarse geometry is the study of metric spaces from afar, local properties of metric spaces are irrelevant in this context and one can restrict themselves to discrete spaces. In fact, throughout these notes, we will only deal with
uniformly locally finite
(abbreviated as
u.l.f.
) metric spaces, i.e., spaces where balls of a given radius are finite and uniformly bounded in cardinality.

Given a u.l.f. metric space
(X,d)
,
\ell_{2}(X)
denotes the Hilbert space of square-summable functions
X\to\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
the algebra of bounded linear operators on
\ell_{2}(X)
. Given an operator
a\in\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
, its
propagation
is given by

\propg(a)=\sup\{d(x,y)\mid\langle a\delta_{x},\delta_{y}\rangle\neq 0\},

where
(\delta_{x})_{x\in X}
denotes the canonical orthonormal basis of
\ell_{2}(X)
; so,
\propg(a)
is in
[0,\infty]
. The
uniform Roe algebra of
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
, is the norm closure of all operators in
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
whose propagation is finite. Identifying
\ell_{\infty}(X)
, the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of bounded functions
X\to\mathbb{C}
, with the (multiplication) operators on
\ell_{2}(X)
with propagation zero, i.e. the operators which are diagonal with respect to the basis
(\delta_{x})_{x\in X}
, it is clear that
\ell_{\infty}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
. In fact,
\ell_{\infty}(X)
is a
Cartan subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
, in the sense that
\ell_{\infty}(X)
is a maximal abelian subalgebra (abbreviated as
masa
), the normalizer of
\ell_{\infty}(X)
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
generates the whole
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
as a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, and there is a faithful conditional expectation
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\to\ell_{\infty}(X)
(see
[
WW20
, Proposition 4.1]
).

It is straightforward that, for any operator in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
, its cutoffs by projections in
\ell_{\infty}(X)
given by subsets of
X
which are far enough apart have small norm. More precisely, given
A\subseteq X
, let
\chi_{A}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\ell_{2}(X)
onto
\ell_{2}(A)
. Then, for any
a\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
, the following holds:

(1.1)

\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists r>0\ \forall A,B\subseteq X\ \text{if }d(A,B)>r\text{ then }\|\chi_{A}a\chi_{B}\|\leq\varepsilon.

Indeed, if
b
is a finite propagation operator with
\|a-b\|\leq\varepsilon
, then
r=\propg(b)
has the desired property, since
\|\chi_{A}b\chi_{B}\|=0
for all
A,B\subseteq X
with
d(A,B)>r
.
It is readily seen that the subset of all operators in
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
satisfying (
1.1
) forms a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
. We call this
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra the
quasi-local algebra of
X
and denote it by
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
.

By the discussion above, we have that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
and, as mentioned in the first paragraph of this introduction, it was a long standing open problem whether these algebras were actually the same, regardless of
X
. This is an important problem: in fact, in case quasi-local operators correspond to the operators that can be approximated by finite propagation operators, one has a method for checking whether an operator belongs to a uniform Roe algebra merely by estimating norms of off-diagonal block restrictions of the operator, without having to explicitly produce finite propagation approximants. This is crucial, for instance, in the work of Engel (see
[
Eng14
,
Eng18
]
) on the index theory of pseudo-differential operators. In general, checking whether an operator is quasi-local is much easier than checking whether it belongs to
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
.

As of now, the largest class of u.l.f. metric spaces for which it is known that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)=\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
is the class of spaces satisfying Yu’s property A (see
[
ŠZ20
, Theorem 3.3]
, and
[
Oza25
, Theorem 5]
for a much shorter proof). On the other hand, as proved by Ozawa, these algebras are never the same if
X
is the coarse disjoint union of expander graphs (
[
Oza25
, Corollary C]
). Recall that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
h>0
, a finite (undirected) graph
G=(V,E)
is a
(k,h)
-expander
if any vertex is incident to at most
k
edges, and for all
A\subseteq V
we have that

A\leq|V|/2\text{ implies }|\partial A|\geq h|A|,

where
\partial A=\{v\in V\setminus A\mid\exists u\in A,\ (v,u)\in E\}
. This condition readily implies that
G
is connected, and thus we consider
V
as a metric space endowed with the shortest path distance. Let now
(X_{n},d_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of finite metric spaces, each being a vertex set of some
(k,h)
-expander graph, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
h>0
fixed and independent of
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and such that
|X_{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We endow
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
with a metric
d
such that

1.

d\restriction X_{n}\times X_{n}=d_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and

2.

d(X_{n},X_{m})\to\infty
as
n,m\to\infty
with
n\neq m
.

We refer to such a disjoint union
X
as a
coarse disjoint union of expander graphs
. Note that while the metric
d
above is by no means unique, properties (
1
) and (
2
) above characterize
d
coarsely. For details on expander graphs, we refer to
[
Lub10
]
.

We want to understand the possible differences between uniform Roe and quasi-local algebras in the setting of coarse disjoint union of expander graphs, with a specific focus on Cartan C*-subalgebras.
While
\ell_{\infty}(X)
is always a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
, in the case of expander graphs, not only
\ell_{\infty}(X)
is not a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
, but in fact
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
admits no Cartan subalgebra isomorphic to
\ell_{\infty}
. More precisely, we prove the following in Section
2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
be the coarse disjoint union of expander graphs. Then
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
does not have a Cartan subalgebra isomorphic to
\ell_{\infty}
.

As Ozawa’s aforementioned result, Theorem
1.1
is also valid for
asymptotic expanders
, a weaker property than being an expander. We dicuss this is Remark
2.12
below.

We note that any
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
which is abelian and strongly closed is of the form
\ell_{\infty}(I)
for some countable, possibly finite, set
I
(see
[
BBF+24b
, Theorem 1.4]
). Therefore, Theorem
1.1
implies that the quasi-local algebra of a coarse disjoint union of expander graphs cannot have a strongly closed Cartan subalgebra.
The following problem remains open.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
be the coarse disjoint union of expander graphs. Can
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
have a Cartan subalgebra?

As our motivation is to further distinguish
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
, we point out that it remains open whether the uniform Roe algebras of u.l.f. metric spaces
X
and
Y
are isomorphic if and only if their quasi-local algebras are isomorphic. While we cannot solve this question, we prove that the forward implication is always true. In fact, the following stronger statement is proved in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be u.l.f. metric spaces and
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(Y)
be an isomorphism. Then,
\Phi
canonically extends to an isomorphism between
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
.

We leave untouched the problem whether
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
implies
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(Y)
. We note that this is related to the so called
strong rigidity problem for quasi-local algebras
, which remains open to date. Indeed, while it was shown in
[
BBF+22
, Theorem 3.5]
that if
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
are isomorphic, then
X
and
Y
are coarsely equivalent. It is still unknown whether this hypothesis is strong enough to imply that
X
and
Y
are
bijectively
coarsely equivalent. A positive answer to this problem would immediately imply that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(Y)
must be isomorphic given that
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
and
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
are isomorphic.

2.
Expander graphs and Cartan subalgebras

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will follow the idea of the proof of
[
Oza25
, Theorem B]
. However, we need a slightly stronger statement than in
[
Oza25
]
, see Theorem
2.4
below. Thus for completeness, we recall some definitions and the statement of the concentration of measure phenomenon. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the Euclidean space endowed with its canonical norm,
\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
its unit sphere, and we let
\sigma_{n}
be the unique probability measure on
\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
which is invariant under orthogonal transformations, i.e., if
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an isometry, then
\sigma_{n}(A)=\sigma_{n}(u(A))
for all measurable
A\subseteq\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Following common terminology in the literature, as the measures
\sigma_{n}
are probability measures, we write
\mathbb{P}
for
\sigma_{n}
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
is clear from the context. For a complete proof of the next theorem, we refer the reader to Theorem 13.2.2 in the monograph
[
AK06
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Concentration of measure phenomenon)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\colon\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1
-Lipschitz. Then, for all
t>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\xi\in\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mid|f(\xi)-\mathbb{E}f|>t\right)\leq 4e^{-\frac{t^{2}n}{72\pi^{2}}}.

The next lemma is a version of
[
Oza25

k,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k})
in
(\partial B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{k}
, we let
p_{\bar{\xi}}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
\mathrm{span}\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\delta<1/10
and
0<\gamma\leq 1
, there are
c,L>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq n_{0}

\dim(V)\geq n\gamma

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bar{\xi}\in(\partial B_{V})^{k}\mid\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
|A|\leq n\delta\end{subarray}}\|\chi_{A}p_{\bar{\xi}}\|\geq 140\pi\sqrt{\delta/\gamma\log(1/\delta)k}\right)\leq Le^{-cn}

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume throughout that
n\delta
and
n\gamma
are integers. Since the worst case scenario is when
\dim(V)
is as small as possible, we also assume for simplicity that
\dim(V)=n\gamma
. Similarly, we restrict our estimates to subsets
A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with exactly
n\delta
elements.

We write
\mathbb{E}_{V}
to denote the expectation over
\zeta\in\partial B_{V}
, where
\partial B_{V}
is considered with its canonical spherical probability measure given by an isometry
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{n\gamma}
.

Claim 2.3
.

\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|
is asymptotically at most
\sqrt{\delta/\gamma}
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
p_{V}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
V
,
W=\mathrm{range}(p_{V}\chi_{A})
, and
p_{W}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
W
. Then, given
\zeta\in\partial B_{V}
, we have that

\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|\leq\|p_{W}\zeta\|
. Indeed, to check this, let
p_{\zeta}
be the projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto
\mathbb{R}\cdot\zeta
and notice that, since
p_{\zeta}=p_{\zeta}p_{V}
, we have

\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|=\|p_{\zeta}\chi_{A}\|=\|p_{\zeta}p_{V}\chi_{A}\|\leq\|p_{\zeta}p_{W}\|=\|p_{W}\zeta\|.

In particular, this implies that

\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|\leq\mathbb{E}_{V}\|p_{W}\zeta\|.

By the definition of
W
, it is clear that
\dim(W)\leq|A|=n\delta
. Therefore, letting
m=n\gamma
,
W
is a subspace of dimension at most
m\delta/\gamma
of
V
, a vector space of dimension
m
. By
[
Mat02
, Lemma 15.2.2]
,
\mathbb{E}_{V}\|p_{W}\zeta\|
is asymptotically at most
\sqrt{\delta/\gamma}
as
n\to\infty
.

\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|\leq 2\sqrt{\delta/\gamma}

\sqrt{\delta/\gamma}<\varepsilon/2

\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|\leq\varepsilon

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\xi\in\partial B_{V}\mid\|\chi_{A}\xi\|>{7\pi\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\xi\in\partial B_{V}\mid\|\chi_{A}\xi\|>\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\|+{6\pi\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\xi\in\partial B_{V}\mid|\|\chi_{A}\xi\|-\mathbb{E}_{V}\|\chi_{A}\zeta\||>{6\pi\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle<4e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}n\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\xi\in\partial B_{V}\mid\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\

By the definition of
\varepsilon

\binom{n}{k}\leq(ne/k)^{k}

\binom{n}{n\delta}\leq e^{\frac{\varepsilon^{2}n\gamma}{4}}

(2.1)

We can now obtain the strengthening of Theorem B in

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
be the coarse disjoint union of expander graphs and
0<\gamma\leq 1
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
V_{n}\subseteq\ell_{2}(X_{n})
be a subspace of dimension at least
|X_{n}|\gamma
. Then, there are an increasing sequence
(n(k))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(W_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

1.

each
W_{k}
is a subspace of
V_{n(k)}
,

2.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\dim(W_{k})=\infty
, and

3.

\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(W_{k}))\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
(\delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a decreasing sequence of positive reals converging to
0
, such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k}=0
, where

\varepsilon_{k}=140\pi\sqrt{\delta_{k}/\gamma\log(1/\delta_{k})k}

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}|X_{n}|=\infty
, Lemma
2.2
allows us to find a strictly increasing sequence
(n(k))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a subspace
W_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{|X_{n(k)}|}
with (real) dimension
k
such that, letting
p_{k}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{|X_{n(k)}|}
onto
W_{k}
, we have

(2.2)

\max\left\{\|\chi_{A}p_{k}\|\mid A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,|X_{n(k)}|\},\ |A|\leq\delta_{m}|X_{n(k)}|\right\}\leq\varepsilon_{m}.

for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Replacing each
W_{k}
by its complexification, each
W_{k}
is a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{|X_{n(k)}|}=\ell_{2}(X_{n(k)})\subseteq\ell_{2}(X)
and the inequality above still holds.

Let us show that
\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}(W_{k})
is contained in
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
. For that, let
a=\mathrm{SOT}\text{-}\sum_{k}a_{k}\in\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}(W_{k})
, where each
a_{k}
is an operator in
\mathcal{B}(W_{k})
, and let us show that
a
is quasi-local. Without loss of generality, assume
\|a\|\leq 1
, so
\|a_{k}\|\leq 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and pick
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varepsilon_{m}<\varepsilon
. As
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
is a coarse disjoint union of expander graphs, there is
\kappa>1
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
A,B\subseteq X_{n}
we have

(2.3)

\min\left\{\frac{|A|}{|X_{n}|},\frac{|B|}{|X_{n}|}\right\}\leq\kappa^{-\frac{d(A,B)}{2}};

see
[
Oza25
, Section 3]
, and
[
LNvZ21
,
KLVZ21
]
for more details about this type of condition.
Therefore, we can choose
r>0
large enough so that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
A,B\subseteq X_{n}
with
d(A,B)>r
,

(2.4)

\min\left\{\frac{|A|}{|X_{n}|},\frac{|B|}{|X_{n}|}\right\}\leq\delta_{m}.

Fix
A,B\subseteq X
with
d(A,B)>r
, and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
A_{k}=A\cap X_{n(k)}
and
B_{k}=B\cap X_{n(k)}
. Thus

\|\chi_{A}a\chi_{B}\|=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|\chi_{A_{k}}a_{k}\chi_{B_{k}}\|.

Note that
\|\chi_{A_{k}}a_{k}\chi_{B_{k}}\|\leq\varepsilon
for all
k\geq m
. Indeed, fix such
k
. Since
d(A,B)>r
, we also have
d(A_{k},B_{k})>r
. Hence, by (
2.4
), either

|A_{k}|\leq\delta_{m}|X_{n(k)}|
or
|B_{k}|\leq\delta_{m}|X_{n(k)}|
. Without loss of generality, assume the former happens. Therefore, as
k\geq m
, (
2.2
) implies that
\|\chi_{A_{k}}p_{k}\|\leq\varepsilon_{m}
. Since
p_{k}
is the identity of
\mathcal{B}(W_{k})
and
\|a_{k}\|\leq 1
, we have

\|\chi_{A_{k}}a_{k}\chi_{B_{k}}\|\leq\|\chi_{A_{k}}p_{k}\|\leq\varepsilon_{m}.

As
\varepsilon_{m}<\varepsilon
and
k\geq m
was arbitrary, we have shown that

\|\chi_{A_{k}}a_{k}\chi_{B_{k}}\|\leq\varepsilon\ \text{ for all }\ k\geq m.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}
is compact, this finishes the proof.
∎

The next ingredient for our proof of the main Theorem
1.1
is the following:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}
be the coarse disjoint union of finite metric spaces. For
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, denote
X_{F}=\bigsqcup_{n\in F}X_{n}
. Suppose that
A\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
is Cartan subalgebra isomorphic to

3.
Extension of isomorphisms between uniform Roe algebras

As mentioned in the introduction, the question whether
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(Y)
if and only if
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)\cong\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
remains open. In this subsection, we prove that the forward implication is always valid.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(X)\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{u}(Y)
be an isomorphism. By
[

, Lemma 3.1]

\Phi
is spatially implemented, meaning there is a unitary

such that

. So,

extends to an isomorphism between
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))
and
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(Y))
, which we still denote by
\Phi
. We show that it restricts to an isomorphism from
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(X)
to
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\!\mathit{ql}}(Y)
.

As
\Phi=\mathrm{Ad}(u)
,
\Phi
restricts to an isomorphism between
\mathcal{K}(\ell_{2}(X))
and
\mathcal{K}(\ell_{2}(Y))
. Thus
\Phi
induces an isomorphism

\Psi
between the Calkin algebras of
\ell_{2}(X)
and
\ell_{2}(Y)
making the following diagram commute.

\textstyle{\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{Y}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(X))/\mathcal{K}(\ell_{2}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

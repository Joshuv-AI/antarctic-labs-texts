Levenberg–marquardt based ANN solution for MHD nanofluid flow and heat transportation over a thin needle with thermophoresis effects
Authors: Haider Ali, Aaqib Majeed, Taoufik Saidani, Parvez Ali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractArtificial neural network (ANN) techniques have emerged as powerful data-driven tools for their effectiveness in analysis, modeling, and simulation. These supervised learning approaches are designed to reduce the error between predicted and observed data, providing accurate approximations of complex physical systems. To improve training performance, the Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm (LMA) is widely adopted due to its fast convergence and suitability for nonlinear problems. The objective of this study is to develop an ANN-based computational framework using the LMA to accurately predict the flow and heat transfer characteristics of an MHD nanofluid over a thin needle based on Buongiorno’s model. The novelty of the present work lies in applying an ANN-based predictive framework to the Buongiorno nanofluid model over a moving thin needle, which has received limited attention in the existing literature. The impacts of transverse magnetic field, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis are contained in the mathematical model. The governing equations for fluid flow are converted to ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and numerically solved. The results are also predicted by using Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) techniques. The effect of physical parameters like magnetic parameter (M), Prandtl number (Pr), thermophoresis parameter (Nt), Brownian motion parameter (Nb), and Schmidt number (Sc) on velocity, temperature and concentration profiles are explored. The outcomes indicate that the velocity of the fluid decreases with the upsurge of the magnetic parameter while the temperature increases. Higher values of Pr and Sc reduce the temperature and concentration profiles, correspondingly. The Brownian motion parameter boosts the temperature but lowers the concentration, while the thermophoresis parameter rises both temperature and concentration. It is also observed that the magnetic field increases the skin friction coefficient but decreases the heat and mass transfer rates. The ANN results are very close to the numerical results, showing that ANN is an accurate and reliable method for predicting complex nanofluid flow problems.Keywords: Thin needle, Buongiorno model, Nanofluid, ANN, Brownian motion, Mathematical modeling

Introduction

Nanofluids have received great attention because they improve the heat transfer ability of ordinary fluids. A nanofluid is made by mixing very small particles, called nanoparticles, into ordinary base fluids such as water, oil, and ethylene glycol. These nanoparticles increase the thermal conductivity of the fluid and make it more useful in several engineering and industrial applications like cooling systems, solar energy devices, electronic equipment, biomedical engineering, and manufacturing industries [1–5]. A number of research groups have examined the use of various kinds of nanoparticles to enhance the performance of fluids, such as copper, titanium oxide, aluminium oxide and carbon nanotubes [6–9]. Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) nanofluid flow is an important research field in the recent years as it is possible to control the motion of the electrically conducting fluids by magnetic fields, which also control their heat transfer behavior [10–12]. Hence, the synergistic action of nanoparticles and magnetic field has now proved very beneficial in recent thermal engineering applications.

The flow across a thin needle is crucial due to its use in the polymer industries, geothermal systems, hot-wire anemometers, and wire cooling. Thin needles are beneficial as they have a smaller drag force and better heat transfer performance. The earlier studies have primarily taken into account ordinary fluid flowing over the thin needles while the recent studies are concerned with the nanofluids and hybrid nanofluids under various physical conditions [13–15]. The MHD nano fluid flow past a porous thin needle with nonlinear radiation impacts was examined by Khan et al. [16]. Ali et al. [17] examined flow of nanoliquid over a moving slender needle under the influence of magnetic field. Reddy et al. [18] conducted an analysis of the entropy generation and Stefan blowing effects on the hybrid nanofluid flow through a moving thin needle. Shah et al. [19] umerically investigated the thermally enhanced electromagnetohydrodynamic (EMHD) flow of a CuO–ethylene glycol/water micropolar nanofluid.. Tlili et al. [20] investigated radiative MHD nanofluid flow over a moving thin needle in a porous medium with Hall current, dust particles, and entropy generation. In thin needle problems, the effects of chemical reactions, viscous dissipation, velocity slip, porous media and non-Newtonian fluids were also included by other researchers [21–24]. The aforementioned studies revealed that fluid velocity, temperature and concentration of fluid are highly influenced by magnetic field and nanoparticles. But few studies have been conducted on the flow of thin needles with the model of Buongiorno nanofluid.

The Buongiorno model is one of the widespread models to examine nanofluids that consider the movement of the nanoparticles inside the fluid. In this model, the two major effects taken into account are thermophoresis and Brownian motion. The Brownian motion is the arbitrary movement of nanoparticles and the thermophoresis is the movement of particles by differences in temperature [25]. The Buongiorno model provides more realistic results in problems of heat and mass transfer compared to simple nanofluid models [26, 27]. Buongiorno model was used to investigate the flow of electrically conducting nanofluid by Chu et al. [28] and improvement in the heat transfer was observed. Krishna and Chamkha [29, 30] investigated the Hall and slip effects on the flow of an MHD nanofluid in a rotating tube in the presence of the Buongiorno model. Ali et al. [30] studied the hybrid nanofluid flow using the modified Buongiorno model and obtained a better thermal performance. Yasmin [31] studied the MHD hybrid nanofluid flow of microorganisms of the gyrotactic type with the impact of Brownian motion and thermophoretic diffusion. Choudhary et al. [32] examined MHD Williamson flow of hybrid nanoliquid over stretching and shrinking cylinders employing modified Buongiorno model. The results of these studies showed the temperature and the concentration of nanoparticles are strongly affected by these phenomena, namely Brownian motion and thermophoresis. However, very few studies have been conducted on the Buongiorno nanofluid flow over thin needles.

ANNs are current computational tools that are extensively used in the answer to complicated engineering and scientific issues. ANN methods are able to learn the nonlinear relationships between different physical parameters, and make accurate predictions with lower computational amounts [33]. Due to these merits, ANNs are now commonly applied in fluid mechanics [34] and heat transfer problems, also in the case of nanofluid flow and MHD systems [34]. Artificial intelligence (AI) techniques were employed to examine the MHD nanofluid flow over a mobile thin needle by Ismail et al. [35] who obtained accurate prediction results. Siddique et al. [36] used ANN analysis to model the MHD hybrid nanofluid flow past a thin needle considering nonlinear radiation and heat generation effect. Imran et al. [37] have implemented the ANN techniques for thermal analysis of MHD hybrid nanofluid flow in porous media and concluded that machine learning technique can be efficiently used to predict complex fluid flow behavior. From these studies it can be concluded that the ANN methods are gaining more and more importance in the latest developments of modern fluid dynamics research.

Several recent studies have investigated the flow and heat transfer behavior of different non-Newtonian nanofluids under various physical conditions. The effects of viscosity ratio and unsteady stretching velocity on ternary nanofluid flow have been analyzed in [38], while the influence of rheological properties on tetra-Bingham nanofluids has also been reported [39]. Moreover, Darcy–Brinkman porous media, Navier slip, and Biot boundary conditions have been shown to significantly affect fluid flow and heat transfer characteristics [40]. The combined effects of thermal radiation, Stefan blowing, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and entropy generation have also been investigated to improve the understanding of advanced heat transfer systems [41, 42]. These studies provide a strong foundation for developing more generalized mathematical models for non-Newtonian nanofluid flows. The combined influence of bioconvection, Darcy–Forchheimer resistance, and Buongiorno’s model has been reported to significantly affect the flow, heat, and mass transfer characteristics [43]. Similarly, entropy generation and magnetohydrodynamic effects have been widely investigated to improve the understanding of thermal transport in ferrofluids and Bingham fluids [44, 45]. Moreover, the effects of velocity slip, Darcy–Brinkman porous media, and mass transpiration have been shown to play an important role in controlling the thermal and concentration boundary layers of ternary nanofluids [46, 47]. In addition, chemically reactive micropolar hybrid nanofluid flow over a porous surface has highlighted the combined effects of an inclined magnetic field, radiation, and entropy generation on transport phenomena [48]. These studies provide a strong basis for developing more generalized mathematical models for complex non-Newtonian nanofluid flows. The coupling of analytical or numerical solvers with ANN has been successfully used to predict the behavior of micropolar and tetrahybrid nanofluids with high accuracy while reducing computational effort [49, 50]. In addition, MHD stagnation-point flows, entropy generation, and hybrid nanofluids have been extensively investigated to understand the effects of magnetic fields and thermal transport under different physical conditions [51, 52]. Furthermore, hybrid nanofluid flows with convective slip and mixed convection have demonstrated the importance of surface conditions and variable thermophysical properties in enhancing heat transfer performance [53, 54]. These studies highlight the growing role of artificial intelligence as an efficient predictive tool for advanced fluid flow and heat transfer analyses.

Recent studies have used both numerical methods and artificial intelligence to study complex nanofluid flow and heat transfer problems. ANNs have been successfully used to predict the flow behavior of nanofluids with high accuracy and less computational time [55, 56]. Moreover, the effects of porous media, thermal radiation, magnetic field, and entropy generation on heat and mass transfer have been widely investigated under different flow conditions [57, 58]. Recent studies have also examined the stability of hybrid nanofluid flow and the thermal behavior of nanomaterials over a moving thin needle, showing that these physical effects play an important role in controlling fluid flow and heat transfer [59, 60]. These studies show that combining numerical methods with ANN provides an efficient and reliable approach for solving complex nanofluid flow problems. Upreti et al. [61] and Upreti et al. [62] reported that Cattaneo–Christov heat flux, Darcy–Forchheimer porous media, and nanoparticle shape significantly influence the thermal and flow characteristics of nanofluids. Bartwal et al. [63] and Bartwal et al. [64] further showed that entropy generation and machine learning techniques, such as MLP, can accurately predict heat and mass transfer performance. Moreover, Upreti et al. [65] and Upreti et al. [66] successfully applied regression models, ANFIS–PSO, and ANFIS–GA to improve the prediction of complex fluid flow problems. In addition, Upreti and Bartwal [67] and Pandey et al. [68] highlighted the growing importance of nanofluids in thermal management and energy-efficient engineering systems. These studies demonstrate that combining numerical methods with artificial intelligence provides an accurate and efficient approach for predicting complex nanofluid flow and heat transfer problems.

These studies encouraged the present work which investigates the flow of MHD nanofluid across a thin needle with consideration of the Buongiorno model. The mathematical model incorporates the inspiration of Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and magnetic field on the flow of the fluid, heat energy, and the concentration of nanoparticles. Unlike many previous studies that used simple nanofluid models, the present work uses the Buongiorno model to study both temperature and concentration distributions in detail. The interaction between magnetic forces and nanoparticle movement is also analyzed under suitable boundary conditions. According to the authors’ knowledge, very limited work has been reported on MHD nanofluid flow over a thin needle using the Buongiorno model. Therefore, the present study will help researchers better understand nanoparticle transport and heat transfer behavior in electrically conducting nanofluids. Presented a computational strategy that may be useful for future applications in advanced cooling technologies, microfluidic systems, and thermal transport engineering.

Problem formulation

The current study examines a steady, laminar, and axisymmetric MHD nanofluid flow over a thin needle. The mathematical formulation is developed employing the Buongiorno nanofluid model, which accounts for the effects of Brownian motion and thermophoresis [1]. Physical configuration of the study is displayed in Fig. 1 where (x, r) are the cylindrical coordinates in axial and radial directions. A uniform transverse magnetic field is implemented normal to the flow direction, while the induced magnetic field is ignored because of the small magnetic Reynolds number. The base fluid and nanoparticles are considered to be in thermal equilibrium, and viscous dissipation and pressure gradient effects are ignored. Heat and mass transfer take place because of temperature and concentration differences among the needle surface and the ambient fluid. The present model is formulated by following the base framework reported by Ahmad et al. [69, 70].

Methodology

Numerical solution

The given system of nonlinear differential equations is solved numerically employing the bvp4c solver in MATLAB. To enable its implementation, the higher-order equations are first converted to a system of first-order ordinary differential equations by inserting new variables: y1=f,y2=f′,y3=f′′,y4=θ,y5=θ′,y6=ϕ,andy7=ϕ′. Using these substitutions, the original system is rewritten in first-order form. Furthermore, as the problem is defined over a semi-infinite domain, the interval is truncated to [0,ηmax], where ηmax is chosen sufficiently large to ensure accurate approximation of the boundary conditions at infinity.

The numerical procedure involves defining an ODE function corresponding to the transformed first-order system and a boundary condition function that incorporates the prescribed conditions at η=0 and η→∞. An initial mesh and an appropriate initial guess for all variables are generated using bvpinit to facilitate convergence. This is followed by the solution of the problem by bvp4c (Fig. 2) solver which is a collocation solver that uses adaptive mesh refinement. The results of calculating the velocity, temperature and concentration profiles are then extracted and examined and the accuracy of the results checked by mesh refinement and convergence tests.

Architecture of the proposed ANN model

ANN modeling

To enhance predictive performance and minimize computational expenses, numerical outputs of the MATLAB bvp4c solver were used to provide a training set to an ANN with the help of the nftool (Neural Fitting Tool). The data produced by the numerical simulations, consisting of the sampling of the governing parameters, together with their physical responses (skin friction coefficient, temperature, and concentration) was arranged as input-output pairs. The parameters were taken as the dimensionless input and the target outputs were the numbers that were solved. One of the adopted neural network was feedforward with numerous hidden layers because it proved to be very efficient and converged very fast in problems of nonlinear regression [70–72]. The Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) backpropagation algorithm [72, 73] was employed to train the neural network because it was proven to be very efficient and converged very fast in nonlinear regression problems (Fig. 2).

Results and discussion

The numerical results given in Tables 2–5 show the effects of M, Pr, Nb, Nt, and Sc on the skin friction coefficient (Cfx), Nusselt number (Nux), and Sherwood number (Shx). It is found that the greater the value of M, the bigger the value of Cfx, which in turn means that the smaller the values of Nux and Shx. This is because the magnetic field produces a resistance to the flow of the fluid. Consequently, the heat transfer rate and mass transfer rate will decrease. The Nux is increased by the positive effect of Pr. With the increase of Pr, the Nux also increases, implying greater heat transfer rate. The change is however rather small for the Shx and very small for the Cfx. Nb decreases the Nux and increases the Shx. The higher value of Nb, the more random the movement of the nanoparticles will be within the fluid. This decreases the surface temperature gradient and decreases heat transfer, but increases mass transfer. Nt decreases both the Nux and Shx. An increase in Nt will cause the nanoparticles to be pushed farther away from the heated surface and thus lower the heat and mass transfer rates. In the same way, the value of Cfx continues to be nearly constant. Sc has a strong effect upon the Shx. With an increase in Sc, the Shx value increases significantly, thus intensifying the mass transfer rate. The Nux also rises slightly and there is very little change in Cfx. To validate the present model, M=0 was set. Under this condition, the governing equations become the same as those reported in [69]. The obtained numerical results show excellent agreement with [69], which confirms the accuracy and reliability of the present model.

The numerical results are matched with the results predicted by the ANN model in Tables 3 to 5. The errors in all cases are very small and the predicted values are very close to the numerical values. This indicates that the ANN model is capable to forecast the current flow problem accurately and reliably. The velocity and temperature profiles of the nanofluid flow past thin needle due to the effect of M are presented in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively. The velocity is seen to be decreasing as the M increases. This is because of the fact that the magnetic field generates a resistance to fluid flow. The opposite trend is true, however, with increasing values of M, the temperature profile increases. If the fluid motion slows down, more heat is generated within the fluid, thus increasing the fluid temperature. The impact of key parameters on the temperature profile is displayed in Figs. 5–9. The Prandtl number is varied to represent different engineering fluids with different thermal diffusivities and to investigate its influence on the heat transfer characteristics of the nanofluid. An increment in values of Pr corresponds to a decrease in temperature. This indicates that the higher the Pr the slower the spread of heat in the fluid. However, the temperature profile increases with enhancing Nb and Nt. The greater the Brownian motion, the better the fluid will move the nanoparticles and the better the fluid will distribute heat. Similarly, the thicker the thermal boundary layer, the greater the temperature will be, which can be achieved by increasing the value of Nt for moving nanoparticles away from the heated surface. Contrarily, the temperature profile is lowered by increasing the Sc.

Overall, these results show that the temperature of the nanofluid depends on the combined effects of the magnetic field, heat diffusion, and nanoparticle motion. By changing the values of M, Pr, Nb, Nt, and Sc, the temperature distribution inside the fluid can be controlled. This may be useful in designing engineering systems where better heat transfer and temperature control are needed.

The concentration profiles obtained for the various parameters are illustrated in Figs. 10–13 respectively. It is found that the concentration profile increases with the increase of Pr slightly. But the concentration is lower with higher Nb. This is because of the concentration boundary layer thickness is reduced by stronger random motion of nanoparticles. Increasing Nt will involve higher concentration profile as more number of nanoparticles will be moved from the hot surface to the cooler fluid region. Last, the concentration profile becomes sharply decreasing with growing Sc as the larger the value of Sc the smaller the mass diffusion inside the fluid.

The increase in M raises the Cfx because the magnetic field creates more resistance to the fluid flow, which increases the force acting on the needle surface. However, it reduces Nux and Shx because the heat and mass transfer from the surface become weaker. On the other hand, increasing Pr improves the heat transfer rate by reducing the thermal boundary layer, while increasing Sc improves the mass transfer rate by reducing mass diffusion and increasing the concentration gradient near the surface.

Different physical parameters are shown as contours of temperature (isothermal lines) in fig. 13(a-e). These contour lines show how temperature changes inside the fluid flow region. It can be seen that the temperature is highest near the needle surface and gradually decreases away from the surface. The contour lines are closer near the surface, which shows that the temperature changes rapidly in that region. Figure 13(a) shows the effect of 𝑀. It is noted that the temperature increases when the magnetic parameter increases. This happens because the magnetic field slows down the fluid motion and produces more heat inside the fluid. As a result, the thermal boundary layer becomes thicker. Figures 13(b, c) show the effects of 𝑁𝑏 and 𝑁𝑡. In both cases, the temperature inside the fluid increases when the parameter values increase. Stronger Brownian motion increases the random movement of nanoparticles, while thermophoresis moves nanoparticles from the hot surface toward the cooler fluid region. Both effects improve heat distribution inside the fluid. Figure 13(d) presents the effect of 𝑃𝑟. It is noticed that the temperature decreases when 𝑃𝑟 increases. This means that heat spreads more slowly in the fluid at higher values of 𝑃𝑟. Therefore, the thermal boundary layer becomes thinner. Figure 13(e) shows the effect of 𝑆𝑐. It can be observed that the temperature decreases slightly with increasing 𝑆𝑐. Larger values of 𝑆𝑐 reduce mass diffusion, which weakens the movement of nanoparticles and slightly lowers the temperature inside the fluid. Overall, the contour plots clearly show how heat spreads inside the fluid for different parameter values. Wider temperature contours indicate that heat spreads over a larger region, while closer contours indicate that the temperature changes more quickly near the needle surface. These results help explain how the governing parameters control the heat transfer in the fluid.

Conclusions

In the current study, the MHD nanofluid flow over a thin needle was examined employing the Buongiorno nanofluid model. The influences of certain pertinent parameters on fluid velocity, temperature, concentration, heat transfer, and mass transfer were considered. ANN systems were also used to forecast the results. The key conclusions of the study are given below:

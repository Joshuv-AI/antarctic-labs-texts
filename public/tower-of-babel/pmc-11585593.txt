The pressure control of independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system using fixed time integral sliding mode active disturbance rejection
Authors: He Hao, Hao Yan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the issues of motion disturbance and low loading accuracy in electro-hydraulic load simulation systems, and to effectively mitigate the impact of uncertainty on system performance, this paper proposes a novel independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system. A nonlinear variable structure state equation is derived to describe the pressure working states of the left and right chambers of the servo valve and hydraulic cylinder under loading conditions. In light of the presence of substantial motion disturbances, an integrated sliding mode automatic disturbance rejection composite controller is proposed with a focus on fixed-time convergence. To mitigate system chattering and enhance system loading performance and disturbance immunity, a fixed-time integral sliding mode controller was developed within the framework of active disturbance rejection control. Stability analysis was also carried out. Simulation research demonstrates that the pressure control accuracy of the composite controller is 99.88%, 99.86%, and 99.83% respectively when the system step command is given with disturbance frequencies of 1 Hz, 3 Hz, and 5 Hz. Similarly, under the same disturbance frequencies, the pressure control accuracy is 99.88%, 99.86%, and 99.84% respectively when the system sine command is given. The simulation results demonstrate that the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system exhibits a notable energy-saving effect. Specifically, energy savings of around 45% and 48% were achieved compared to the original system. The research and exploration conducted have laid a strong groundwork for future in-depth studies on the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system.Keywords: Independent load port, Electro-hydraulic load simulation system, Fixed time, Integral sliding modeSubject terms: Aerospace engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

The electro-hydraulic load simulator is a crucial semi-physical simulation device. Its primary function is to simulate the expected forces encountered by the aircraft steering gear during testing, thereby facilitating the assessment of various technical indicators associated with the steering gear. The electro-hydraulic load simulation system offers several advantages, including rapid response times, substantial output power, and high load rigidity1,2. The electro-hydraulic servo system, characterized as a typical nonlinear time-varying system, exhibits a high level of nonlinearity and encompasses various model uncertainties, such as parameter uncertainty and nonlinearity uncertainty3–5. The electro-hydraulic load simulation system is significantly affected by external motion interference, which greatly limits the enhancement of system control performance and presents significant challenges to controlling the system6,7. Minimizing the impact of position disturbance on the loading performance of the electro-hydraulic load simulation system has emerged as a key technical problem. In recent years, efforts to enhance the control performance of electro-hydraulic load simulation systems have focused on compensating for the impact of position disturbances through two main categories. The first category involves control algorithm compensation methods, such as feedforward compensation based on the structural invariance principle8, disturbance observer compensation9, adaptive control10, fuzzy control11, neural network control12, and sliding mode variable structure control13. These methods aim to suppress disturbances through algorithm design at the software level. The second category comprises structural compensation methods, including pressure accumulator compensation14, dual-valve flow compensation15, and position synchronization compensation16. By improving the structure at the hardware level to reduce the impact of steering gear position disturbance.

The independent load port valve control system uses two or more valve groups to control the two cavities of the hydraulic cylinder separately, breaking the mechanical-hydraulic coupling between the inlet and outlet, and has been widely researched and applied. Based on the analysis presented, there is an issue with the accuracy of loading control in the electro-hydraulic load simulation system. This paper designs a novel independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system. The valve group of this system consists of 2 servo valves and are independently adjusted. This configuration differs from the traditional three-position four-way valve control system, as it enhances the degree of freedom in system control. The valve signals for the inlet and outlet can be provided separately based on the control requirements, enabling the actuator to function according to the specified instructions. Additionally, the back chamber pressure can be kept at a low level by increasing the opening of the outlet valve. Furthermore, while meeting the original system control requirements, it also considers energy-saving performance. Considering the complex working conditions of the variable structure of the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system, we redefined the flow formulas for the inlet and outlet valves of the independent load port. Subsequently, we established its corresponding nonlinear mathematical model as the focus of our research. Addressing the constrained control precision of traditional ADRC in electro-hydraulic load simulation systems and the oscillation issue in sliding mode control, the paper introduces an innovative integral sliding mode active disturbance rejection composite control strategy that is centered on fixed time convergence (FISMADRC). An integral sliding mode controller with fixed time convergence is introduced based on the ADRC control framework. This approach enables the system to achieve rapid convergence and stability within a fixed time frame, independent of any initial state. Furthermore, it employs Lyapunov theory to demonstrate the stability of the control strategy and its convergence within the designated time. This approach aims to reduce the chattering problem in the control system resulting from uncertainty factors. The expanded state observer monitors both internal and external disturbances and uncertainties within the system and compensates for them at the control end. This process helps to achieve accurate pressure control while also enhancing the system’s robust performance and anti-interference capabilities. The composite controller proposed in this article demonstrates superior control effectiveness and energy-saving characteristics compared to both ADRC and PID control. Furthermore, it validates the efficacy of the proposed composite controller within the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system.

Overall, the main contributions of this paper are as follows: First, this paper proposes a novel independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system that integrates independent load port valve group control technology with the traditional electro-hydraulic load simulator’s control applications. This integration fully leverages the benefits of independent load port valve control technology, resulting in advantages such as rapid pressure response, high control accuracy, significant control flexibility, and robust resistance to disturbances. Second, the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system operates under complex conditions characterized by multi-mode variable structures. To address the constantly changing complex working conditions, this paper reintroduces the valve port flow formula. Third, considering the limited accuracy of traditional ADRC and the chattering phenomenon associated with sliding mode control, we propose an integral sliding mode active disturbance rejection composite control strategy based on fixed-time convergence. This strategy builds upon the traditional ADRC framework by introducing a fixed-time integral sliding mode controller to replace the nonlinear PID controller. Ultimately, the energy-saving characteristics of the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system were thoroughly analyzed based on the aforementioned control strategy.

This article assumes that the extension direction of the piston rod in the hydraulic cylinder is considered as positive, with the two chambers of the hydraulic cylinder being connected to separate servo valves. The pressure control of the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system is achieved through these two servo valves. The independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system experiences complex working conditions with multiple modes and variable structures as the pressure of the hydraulic cylinder changes. The analysis focuses on three operating conditions of the left chamber pressure of the hydraulic cylinder. When the system pressureexceeds the pressureof the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder, the system is considered to be in a normal working state. When the spool of the servo valve is opened in the forward direction and the system pressureis lower than the pressureof the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder, the oil in the left chamber will be forced out through the oil inlet of the servo valve, resulting in pressure release. When the piston rod of the hydraulic cylinder is impacted by a strong external force, the pressure p1 in the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder quickly exceeds the system pressure ps. As a result, the spool of the servo valve opens in reverse, allowing the oil in the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder to flow back into the tank. The mode working conditions of the right chamber of the hydraulic cylinder are similar to the three working conditions of the left chamber.

In the design of a fixed-time convergence integral sliding mode active disturbance rejection composite controller, the parameters of the extended state observer are selected with reference to the active disturbance rejection controller. Parameters and are critical indicators of convergence. Specifically, and facilitate rapid convergence of the system when it is distant from the equilibrium point. Conversely, and promote slower convergence when the system is near the equilibrium point, thereby minimizing the incidence of control system issues, such as the chattering phenomenon. Among these, the values of , , g, and should be positive constants that can adjust the speed of convergence to the equilibrium point; larger values result in faster convergence. The variable represents a constant-velocity approach term. To ensure a rapid approach while minimizing chattering, the value of should be selected judiciously.

The parameters of the PID controller primarily rely on empirical methods. Typically, the I and D components are initially disregarded. First, the P parameter is set to a small value for trial control, and the system’s response is observed. Based on the characteristics of the actual system, the P parameter is gradually increased until the system begins to diverge or exhibit significant oscillations, thereby identifying the critical value of the P parameter. Subsequently, the I parameter is adjusted according to the steady-state error of the system to facilitate the rapid elimination of this error. Finally, the D parameter is tuned based on the system’s dynamic pressure response to optimize overshoot and response speed.

Energy saving simulation analysis

The conventional valve-controlled electro-hydraulic load simulation system utilizes a valve core to regulate both the inlet and outlet oil circuits of the hydraulic cylinder. This system throttles both the inlet and outlet during the process of inlet throttling and speed regulation, leading to high energy consumption and low efficiency. To address the inefficiency of traditional valve-controlled hydraulic systems, this article proposes a solution in which two servo valves regulate the pressure in the respective chambers of an independent load port hydraulic system during loading. Upon reaching the command pressure , the pressure in the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder is measured at 10,000 kPa, while the pressure in the right chamber is 5000 kPa. To reduce the energy consumption of the system and enhance the energy-saving characteristics of the hydraulic system, the pressure in both chambers of the hydraulic cylinder is maintained at the lowest possible level. The pressures in the left chamber of the hydraulic cylinder are set to 5500 kPa and 5200 kPa, respectively, while the pressures in the right chamber are set to 500 kPa and 200 kPa, respectively. This configuration reduces the supply pressure of the system, thereby minimizing power and energy consumption.

Both chambers achieve the specified pressure within approximately 0.015s. Consequently, the output pressure of the hydraulic cylinder stabilizes at 5000 kPa without any overshoot or fluctuation once the left and right chambers reach a steady state.

Conclusions

This paper presents a multi-mode variable structure independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system designed to mitigate external motion disturbances encountered by the electro-hydraulic load simulation system. The nonlinear mathematical model of the system was used to establish its state equation, from which an integral sliding mode active disturbance rejection composite controller with fixed time convergence was derived. Simulation tests were conducted to validate the proposed controller, yielding the following results.

The independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system proposed in this article integrates independent load port control technology with the electro-hydraulic load simulator. In addressing the complex working conditions of the multi-mode variable structure within the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system, the flow formulas for the inlet and outlet valves were re-derived. The simulation results indicate that the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system demonstrates a high degree of control freedom, rapid pressure response, exceptional control accuracy, robust anti-disturbance capabilities, and flexible, convenient control. These findings suggest that independent load port technology is well-suited for loading pressure control in electro-hydraulic load simulation systems, which holds significant implications for practical engineering applications of the load simulator.

This study investigates the energy-saving features of an independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system through simulation and analysis. The results demonstrate that by maintaining low pressure in the back pressure chamber of the hydraulic cylinder, the supply pressure of the electro-hydraulic load simulation system can be notably decreased, thereby reducing the total energy consumption of the system. Overall, the integrated sliding mode active disturbance rejection composite control strategy, along with the independent load port control technology proposed in this article, demonstrates significant potential for enhancing the pressure control effectiveness of the hydraulic system within the electro-hydraulic load simulation framework. This valve-controlled electro-hydraulic load simulation system, particularly its hydraulic cylinder configuration, represents a research direction of considerable application value. Regardless of whether the direction of movement of the steering gear changes, the pressure control of the independent load port electro-hydraulic load simulation system examined in this article maintains a constant pressure output. Under certain specific working conditions, a sudden change in the movement mode of the steering gear can lead to a mismatch between the electro-hydraulic load simulation system and the steering gear system. This mismatch may result in pressure shock issues. Furthermore, there is potential for enhancing the mode-switching control strategy of the controller, which requires in-depth research. The electro-hydraulic servo system presents numerous nonlinear challenges. Future research should address the nonlinear friction characteristics of the hydraulic cylinder, as well as the identification algorithms necessary for managing uncertain nonlinearities.

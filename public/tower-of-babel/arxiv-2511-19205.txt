Noncritical Conformal Gravity and Four-Dimensional Liouville Theory
Hikaru Kawai

Abstract




NITEP 266

We study the quantum aspects of the conformal gravity in four dimensions, specifically addressing a known discrepancy
in beta functions between general quadratic curvature theories and conformal gravity, which corresponds to two scalar
degrees of freedom. We demonstrate that this mismatch is resolved by carefully introducing gauge-fixing and
ghost terms via the BRST symmetry, which effectively adds the two scalar modes. Drawing lessons from two-dimensional
quantum gravity and Liouville theory, we proceed to integrate the four-dimensional trace anomaly to
derive a consistent Liouville action, which is given by a free-field action for the conformal mode
with a consistent conformal anomaly.
Finally we give the condition that the BRST transformation is anomaly free.

Introduction

In this paper we study the quantization of conformal gravity in four dimensions.
The conformal sector is described by the conformal factor
e^{\phi(x)}
in spacetime metric of the form

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)=e^{\phi(x)}{\hat{g}}_{\mu\nu}(x),

(1.1)

where
{\hat{g}}_{\mu\nu}
is a family of reference metric representing the degrees of freedom apart from
the conformal mode.
Before going into this subject, let us first briefly summarize a puzzle in the beta functions for quadratic
curvature theory, which helps us to better understand the theory.

It has been known for some time that there is a subtlety in the beta functions of the general quadratic curvature
theory

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}+\frac{1}{\xi}R^{2}-\frac{1}{\rho}R_{\rm GB}^{2}\right),

(1.2)

and the conformal gravity

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda}C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}-\frac{1}{\rho}R_{\rm GB}^{2}\right),

(1.3)

where
\lambda,\xi
and

defined such that its trace is zero. Its square is given as

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}^{2}=R_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}^{2}-2R_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}R^{2},

(1.4)

and
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
is the Gauss-Bonnet (GB) term

\displaystyle R_{\rm GB}^{2}=R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}-4R_{\mu\nu}^{2}+R^{2},

(1.5)

Tbe beta functions in this system may be calculated by the functional renormalization group equation

\displaystyle k\frac{d}{dk}\Gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+R_{k}\right)^{-1}k\frac{d}{dk}R_{k},

(1.6)

where

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(c\,C_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}^{2}+bR^{2}-a\,R_{\rm GB}^{2}\right).

(1.7)

in terms of which the beta functions are given as

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2c}{(4\pi)^{2}}\lambda^{2},

\displaystyle\beta_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{b}{(4\pi)^{2}}\xi^{2},

\displaystyle\beta_{\rho}

This paper is organized as follows.

3
Lessons from two-dimensional quantum gravity for conformal gravity – Integrating the trace anomaly

The action of two-dimensional gravity is

\displaystyle S_{2}=\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}\left(R+\mu\right),

(3.1)

where we have set the coefficient of the Einstein term to be 1 and
\mu
is the cosmological constant.
We use the Eucleadean metric.
The Einstein term in two dimensions gives just topological number

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}R=4\pi\chi=\mbox{const.}

(3.2)

The energy-momentum tensor is given by the partition function
Z[g]
as

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{g}}\frac{\delta\log Z[g]}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.

(3.3)

This implies that

\displaystyle\delta\log Z[g]=-\int d^{2}\xi\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{g}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}.

(3.4)

When the conformal matter is coupled, there appears trace anomaly.
In such a case, under the scale transformation
\delta g^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}\delta\phi
, we have the relation

\displaystyle\delta(\log Z[g]):=\log(Z[ge^{\delta\phi}])-\log(Z[g])=\int d^{2}\xi\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{g}\,\delta\phi\,T^{\mu}_{\mu}

(3.5)

The trace anomaly is given by

\displaystyle\langle T^{\mu}_{\mu}\rangle=\frac{c}{24\pi}R,

(3.6)

where
c
is called the central charge.

Write the partition function as

\displaystyle Z=e^{-F},

(3.7)

and we have

\displaystyle\delta F=F[ge^{\delta\phi}]-F[g]=-\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}R\delta\phi.

(3.8)

Now we write
\delta\phi=\phi dt
, and Eq. (
3.8
) is

\displaystyle dF=-\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g(t)}R(t)\phi dt,

(3.9)

where
R(t)
is the curvature for
g(t)=ge^{t\sigma}
. Using the formula (
A.2
)
and integrating (
3.9
) from
t=0
to
t=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle F[ge^{\phi}]-F[g]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}(R-t\square\phi)\phi dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+R\phi\right),

(3.10)

which is proportional to the Liouville action.

The same result can be obtained as follows. The renormalized partition function is
written by the bare partition function with counterterm

\displaystyle Z[g]=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Z[g_{0}]e^{\frac{c}{48\pi\epsilon_{2}}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{g}\,R},

(3.11)

where
\epsilon_{2}=(2-D)/2
and
c
is a constant.
The Liouville action arises from the counterterm as the finite part of the counterterm.
Consider the Weyl transformation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\phi}{\hat{g}}_{\mu\nu}.

(3.12)

From the formula (
A.2
) given in Appendix
A
, the counterterm in (

\displaystyle-\frac{c}{48\pi\epsilon_{2}}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{{\hat{g}}}\,e^{(D-2)\phi/2}\left(\hat{R}-(D-1)\hat{\square}\phi-\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{4}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}\right),

(3.13)

where the D’Lambertian and covariant derivatives on the rhs are made of the hatted metric
{\hat{g}}_{\mu\nu}
.
Similar notation should be understood in what follows.
The simple pole in the limit of

is the genuine counterterm.
There are terms remaining finite in this limit.
We can find these terms by expanding the integrand in (

) in
(D-2)
and keep the linear term.
This gives

\displaystyle\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{{\hat{g}}}\left[\phi\big(\hat{R}-\hat{\square}\phi\big)-2\hat{\square}\phi-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{48\pi}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{{\hat{g}}}\left[\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}+\hat{R}\phi\right],

(3.14)

after partial integration, in agreement with Eq. (

).

The contribution of this Liouville action to the anomaly is given by

\displaystyle c_{L}=1-c.

(3.15)

Here
1 is the quantum contribution whereas
-c
is the classical contribution coming from the shift of
\phi
under
the conformal transformation.
The absence of the total conformal anomaly determines
c

[
14
,
13
]
.
To show it more explicitly, let us write the contribution to the central charge from the matter as
c^{\rm m}
.
The contribution from the FP determinant or ghosts is
-26
, and that from the above Liouville action is
1-c

Therefore, to have a consistent theory, these must add up to zero:

\displaystyle 1-c-26+c^{\rm m}=0.

(3.16)

This assures the consistency of the theory. That is, the theory remains unchanged even if

is replaced
with

Let us confirm the difference between critical and noncritical strings here. In critical strings, the total
central charge of the matter field and gravity is zero, and the conformal modes do not appear in either
the classical action or the quantum effective action. Therefore, we can simply forget about the conformal modes
by regarding them as gauge degrees of freedom. On the other hand, in noncritical strings, the conformal modes

Four-dimensional conformal anomaly and noncritical conformal gravity

5
Summary

In this paper, we have first recalled the puzzle associated the beta functions for general quadratic curvature
theory and the conformal gravity, and show that the puzzle is resolved by considering the partial gauge fixing of
the conformal gauge symmetry with the BRST transformation. Extending the formulation with the BRST symmetry
to the noncrtical conformal gravity, we have proposed that the four-dimensional Liouville theory should be
given by Eq. (
4.28
), which is derived by deliberately adding the finite
R^{2}
term (
4.17
)
such that the resulting action satisfies the requirement of the consistency of the conformal anomaly
and becomes a free quadratic action in the conformal mode, similar to two-dimensional Liuoville theory.
We have also given the condition that the BRST symmetry is
anomaly free, such that the quantum noncritical conformal gravity is consistent.
Finally, we have contrasted this approach with the widely-used Riegert proposal
[
10
]
for the four-dimensional
Liouville action, showing that our formulation is a more robust, consistent one derived through
the quantization procedure.

Appendix B
Conformally invariant scalar fields

Consider the usual kinetic term for a scalar field

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\ \frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi.

(B.1)

We consider the conformal transformation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\sigma}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}.

(B.2)

To make the action (
B.1
) invariant under this transformation, we should transform the scalar field as

\displaystyle\varphi=e^{-\sigma/2}\tilde{\varphi}.

(B.3)

Using the transformation property (
A.2
), we then find that the combination

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\ \frac{1}{2}\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi+\frac{1}{6}R\varphi^{2}\right],

(B.4)

is invariant under the conformal transformation.

Let us consider conformally invariant theory with higher derivative

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\ \frac{1}{2}(\square\varphi)^{2}.

(B.5)

To make this invariant under the transformation, we must transform the scalar field as

\displaystyle\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}.

(B.6)

Again using the transformation property (
A.2
), we find that the combination

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\ \frac{1}{2}\left[(\square\varphi)^{2}-2R^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi+\frac{2}{3}Rg^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi\right],

(B.7)

is invariant under the conformal transformation.

Appendix C
Consistency of the trace anomaly

The general form of the quantum trace anomaly in four dimensions is
[
17
]

\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu}^{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{g}}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}(g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\phi}{\hat{g}}_{\mu\nu})

(C.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}}{\delta{\phi}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle R^{2}+AR_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+BR_{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}R^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}.

up to overall factor. The consistency of the trace anomaly requires that

\displaystyle\delta^{2}S_{\rm eff}=0.

(C.2)

From the transformation property given in Appendix
A
, we see under infinitesimal transformation
\delta\sigma
,
in four dimensions,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}S_{\rm eff}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\Big[-6R\,\square\delta\phi-A(2R^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\delta\phi+R\,\square\delta\phi)-4BR^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\delta\phi\Big]\wedge\delta\phi.

(C.3)

Here and in what follows, a tilde indicates that the quantity is evaluated on
the hatted metric
{\hat{g}}
and the indices are raised, lowered and contracted by it.
Making the partial integration to those terms with Ricci tensor, and using the Bianchi identity

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}R^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\nu}R,

(C.4)

and making back the partial integration, (
C.3
) gives

\displaystyle\delta^{2}S_{\rm eff}=-\int d^{4}\sqrt{g}\,2(3+A+B)R\,\square\delta\phi\wedge\delta\phi\Big.

(C.5)

Namely the consistency gives

\displaystyle A+B+3=0.

(C.6)

Two independent solutions are

\displaystyle A=-4,\qquad B=1,

(C.7)

which is precisely the GB term (
1.5
), and

\displaystyle A=-6,\qquad B=3

(C.8)

which corresponds to the Weyl tensor square (
1.4
).
To summarize, the trace anomaly is restricted to GB term and Weyl tensor squared by the consistency.
The important point is that there is no
R^{2}
term.

The partition function is given by

\displaystyle Z[ge^{\phi}]=Z[g]e^{S_{\rm eff}[\phi,g]}.

(C.9)

When the consistency condition is satisfied, we have

\displaystyle Z[ge^{\phi}e^{\phi^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z[ge^{\phi}]e^{-S_{\rm eff}[\phi^{\prime},ge^{\phi}]}

(C.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z[g]e^{-S_{\rm eff}[\phi,g]-S_{\rm eff}[\phi^{\prime},ge^{\phi}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z[g]e^{-S_{\rm eff}[\phi+\phi^{\prime},g]}.

This means that

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}[\phi+\phi^{\prime},g]=S_{\rm eff}[\phi,g]+S_{\rm eff}[\phi^{\prime},ge^{\phi}].

(C.11)

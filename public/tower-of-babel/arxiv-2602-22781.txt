Ordered trees with distinguished children
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. A new tree model is introduced based on ordered trees, by distinguishing exactly one child of each node that has children. The basic enumeration leads to a cubic equation of the generating function. The extraction of its coefficients can be done using the Lagrange inversion formula. Various parameters that are commonly studied for ordered trees can also be addressed here, like degree of the root, number of leaves, number of old leaves, height, height of leftmost leaf, and pathlength. We go through these instances and leave further parameters to later research, by either the author or some readers. Dealing with cubic equations in a meaningful way requires some skills with Maple. In a last section, ordered trees are replaced by marked ordered trees; they are then combined with the concept of distinguished children. Only the basic enumeration is provided, leaving further analysis to the future.

1.
Old and young leaves and the present paper

Plane trees (ordered trees) have a root and a sequence of
k\geq 0
subtrees, which are (recursively) plane trees.
The enumeration is with the respect to the number of nodes or (equivalently) the number of edges and leads to
a prominent structure related to
Catalan
numbers. Leaves are nodes that have no successors. By agreement,
the tree just consisting of one node is not considered as a leaf. The enumeration with respect to nodes and leaves leads
to
Narayana
numbers. A further refinement is due to Chen, Deutsch, and Elizalde
[
1
]
: Leaves that are the
leftmost child of its parent is called
old
, all the other leaves are called
young
.

Figure 1.
All 5 ordered trees with 4 nodes and the old leaves marked in red.

Just for interest, we sketch the basic generating function, involving the count of edges, old resp. young leaves, using the three variables
z,u,v
in that order:

\displaystyle F=F(z,u,v)

\displaystyle=1+z(F-1+u)\sum_{k\geq 0}(z(F-1+v))^{k}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{z(F-1+u)}{1-z(F-1+u)},

hence

F=\frac{1+z-uz-\sqrt{1-2(1+v)z+((1+v)^{2}-4u)z^{2}}}{2z}.

If
v=u
(no distinction between old resp. young), the equation simplifies to

F=\frac{1+z-uz-\sqrt{1-2(1+u)z+(1-u)^{2}z^{2}}}{2z}.

Chen, Deutsch, and Elizalde
[
1
]
, using the Lagrange inversion formula, manage to extract the coefficients of

z^{n}u^{i}v^{j}
(
n
edges,
i
old leaves,
j
young leaves) in nice form, and derive many further properties.
The instance of
v=u
leads to the enumeration of
all
leaves and thus to the celebrated
Narayana
numbers.

A related concept is as follows: instead of considering leaves (or equivalently, the edges leading to a leaf), we consider

all
edges and single out those edges that are the leftmost edges of its parent. A small example explains this:

Figure 2.
All 5 ordered trees with nodes and each leftmost edge marked in red.

The enumeration with respect to the number of nodes (
z
), old edges (
u
) and young edges (
v
) is not hard.
The coefficient of
z^{n}u^{i}v^{j}
is of interest; note that for
n
nodes, the number of young edges is
j=n-1-i
, so we can ignore the variable
v
.

The generating function
F(z,u)
is then

F(z,u)=z+zu\frac{F(z,u)}{1-F(z,u)}=z\Phi(F(z,u)),

with
\Phi(y)=1+\frac{uy}{1-y}
, where we consider
u
to be a dummy parameter. We can use the Lagrange inversion formula:

\displaystyle[z^{n}]F(z,u)=\frac{1}{n}[y^{n-1}]\Phi(y)^{n}

and further

\displaystyle[z^{n}u^{k}]F(z,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[y^{n-1}][u^{k}]\Big(1+\frac{uy}{1-y}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[y^{n-1}]\binom{n}{k}\Big(\frac{y}{1-y}\Big)^{kn}=\frac{1}{n}[y^{n-1-nk}]\binom{n}{k}\Big(\frac{1}{1-y}\Big)^{kn}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1}{n-1-kn}=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-1}{kn}.

We may note as well that

F(z,u)=\frac{1+z(1-u)-\sqrt{1-2(1+u)z+(1-u)^{2}z^{2}}}{2}.

After these introductory and motivating remarks we now come to the main tree model in this paper: instead of considering the (unfair) model of one edge being old and the others being young we
consider a
fair
model: exactly
one
edge emanating from each node that is not a leaf is
distinguished
. The total number of such trees with

n
nodes is than naturally higher than the usual Catalan number.

Figure 3.
All 10 ordered trees with nodes and exactly one (distinguished) child marked in red.

The rest of this paper is devoted to a thorough analysis of such trees with distinguished children.

2.
Trees with distinguished children

We use the generating function
A(z)
for such trees, where
z
marks the number of nodes. Then

A=z+zA\sum_{i\geq 0}A^{i}\sum_{j\geq 0}A^{j}=z+zA\sum_{k\geq 0}(k+1)A^{k}=z+\frac{zA}{(1-A)^{2}},

which falls again into the regime of the Lagrange inversion formula;

A=z\Phi(A),\,\quad\Phi(A)=1+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}

and therefore

\displaystyle[z^{n}]A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Phi^{n}(A)=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Big(1+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{A^{k}}{(1-A)^{2k}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}[A^{n-1-k}]\frac{1}{(1-A)^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n-2+k}{n-1-k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-2-k}{k-1}.

The sequence is
1,1,3,10,37,146,602,2563,11181,49720,224540
, which is A109081 in
[
7
]
; the description contains many references as well.

We are also interested to additionally label leaves with an additional variable
u
; then

A=z\Phi(A),\,\quad\Phi(A)=u+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}

and

\displaystyle[z^{n}]A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Phi^{n}(A)=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Big(u+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}\frac{A^{k}}{(1-A)^{2k}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}[A^{n-1-k}]\frac{1}{(1-A)^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}\binom{n-2+k}{n-1-k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-2-k}{k-1};

further

\displaystyle[z^{n}u^{j}]A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Phi^{n}(A)

\displaystyle=[u^{j}]\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-2-k}{k-1}=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{j}\binom{2n-2-j}{j-1}.

Now we move to the asymptotics of this sequence. The individual steps can be seen following the analysis of simply generated families of tree, see e. g.
[
3
]
:

A=z\Phi(A),\quad\Phi(A)=1+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}},

\Phi(\tau)=\tau\Phi^{\prime}(\tau),\quad-1+3\tau-\tau^{2}+\tau^{3}=0,

\tau=0.3611030805;\quad\rho=\frac{\tau}{\Phi(\tau)}=0.1916025857.

We can throw in another variable, and consider

A=zu+\frac{zA}{(1-A)^{2}}.

Then the coefficient of
z^{n}u^{k}
in
A(z,u)
is the number of trees with distinguished children, and
n
is the number of nodes, and
k
is the number of leaves.
This is the equivalent of the Narayana generating function related to the tree family. We start with the functional equation

A=z\Phi(A),\,\quad\Phi(A)=u+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}

and continue

\displaystyle[z^{n}]A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Phi^{n}(A)=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\Big(u+\frac{A}{(1-A)^{2}}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}[A^{n-1}]\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}\frac{A^{k}}{(1-A)^{2k}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}[A^{n-1-k}]\frac{1}{(1-A)^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u^{n-k}\binom{n-2+k}{n-1-k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}u^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-2-k}{k-1}.

Hence the number of (new) trees with
n
nodes and
k
leaves is given by

\displaystyle[z^{n}u^{k}]A(z,u)=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n-2-k}{k-1}.

Now we move to the average number of leaves;

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}A(z,u)=\frac{zA(z,1)^{2}-2zA(z,1)+z}{3A(z,1)^{2}-2(2+zu)A(z,1)+1-z+2zu}.

B(z):=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}A(z,u)\Big|_{u=1}=\frac{zA(z,1)^{2}-2zA(z,1)+z}{3A(z,1)^{2}-2(2+z)A(z,1)+1+z}.

The quantity
A(z,1)
can be eliminated according to its equation, and one finds

-z+z(z+5)B(z)+(-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3})B(z)^{2}+(-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3})B(z)^{3}=0.

Around
z=\rho
, as is known for simply generated trees, see
[
3
]

A\sim\tau\sqrt{\frac{2\Phi(\tau)}{\Phi{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)}}\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}

and

[z^{n}]A\sim\sqrt{\frac{\Phi(\tau)}{2\pi\Phi{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)}}\rho^{-n}n^{-3/2}.

We replace
A
by
\tau-0.3646859471\sqrt{1-\dfrac{z}{\rho}}
in
B(z)
to get an approximation around
z=\rho
; first

B(z)=\frac{z(6z+2{z}^{2}-6{A}^{2}+10A+4z{A}^{2}-2zA-A{z}^{2}-4)}{-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3}};

we start with denominator

\displaystyle-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3}

\displaystyle\sim\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)(-3\rho{z}^{2}-4\rho z-3{\rho}^{2}z-20\rho-4{\rho}^{2}-3{\rho}^{3})

\displaystyle\sim-4.189050369\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr);

then we consider the numerator:

\displaystyle z(6z+2{z}^{2}-6{A}^{2}+10A+4z{A}^{2}-2zA-A{z}^{2}-4)

\displaystyle\sim-.4052983049\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}.

Combined

\displaystyle B(z)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{-.4052983049\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}}{-4.189050369\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)}\sim 0.096751833769\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)^{-1/2}.

We will use the classical transfer formulae, see

c(1-z)^{-\beta}\Longrightarrow\frac{c}{\Gamma(\beta)}n^{\beta-1},\qquad c(1-z/\rho)^{-\beta}\Longrightarrow\frac{c}{\Gamma(\beta)}n^{\beta-1}\rho^{-n}

Applying this leads to

\displaystyle\frac{[z^{n}]B(z)}{[z^{n}]A(z)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{[z^{n}](0.096751833769\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)^{-1/2}}{[z^{n}]\Bigl(.3611030805-.3646859471\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{[z^{n}]0.096751833769\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)^{-1/2}}{[z^{n}]\Bigl(-.3646859471\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}\Bigr)}

\displaystyle\sim-.2653017878\frac{[z^{n}](1-z)^{-1/2}}{[z^{n}]\sqrt{1-z}}

\displaystyle\sim-.2653017878\frac{\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/2)}n^{-1/2}}{\frac{1}{\Gamma(-1/2)}n^{-3/2}}

\displaystyle\sim 0.5306035756n.

For classical ordered trees the corresponding answer is
\sim n/2
, so we have slightly more leaves, on average for the new tree model.

Now we move the (average) height of the new tree family. No new considerations are necessary, since the paper by Flajolet and Odlyzko
[
4
]
deals with simply generated families of trees. The relevant quantities are

\sqrt{\frac{2}{\phi(\tau)\phi{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)}}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)\cdot\sqrt{\pi n}=1.009922004\sqrt{\pi n}

we might compare this with the average height of ordered trees, which is asymptotic to
\sqrt{\pi n}
. So the average height in the new model is slightly higher.

The next parameter of interest is the
degree of the root.
We go back to the defining equation of
A(z)
,

A=z+\frac{zA}{(1-A)^{2}}=z+z\sum_{k\geq 1}kA^{k}

and use an additional parameter
w
, to count the degree of the root:

F(z,w)=z+z\sum_{k\geq 1}kw^{k}A^{k}=z+z\frac{wA}{(1-wA)^{2}}.

Eliminating
A
, one can find an algebraic equation for
F
of order 3, which is a bit long; we will only consider the average of our parameter, in other words, we differentiate w.r.t.
w
, and set
w=1
. We obtain:

z\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2}A^{k}=\frac{zA(A+1)}{(1-A)^{3}}\sim.361103078-1.079796775\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}.

Comparing this with

A\sim.3611030805-.3646859471\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}

we find that the average degree of the root tends to the constant

\frac{-1.079796775}{-.3646859471}=2.960894939.

In the classical case of ordered trees, that constant is just 2 (see
[
6
]
), so it is slightly higher here.

The next parameter to be studied is the
height of the left-most leaf.
For that, we introduce again a second variable
w
, measuring just the height of the left-most leaf;

F=zw+\sum_{j\geq 1}jwzFA^{j-1}=zw+zwF\sum_{j\geq 1}jA^{j-1}=zw+zwF\frac{1}{(1-A)^{2}};

solving

F=\frac{zw(1-A)^{2}}{1-zw-2A+A^{2}}.

Differentiating w.r.t.
w
, followed by
w=1

\frac{z(1-A)^{4}}{1-z-2A+A^{2}}=\frac{A^{2}}{z}\sim.6805515397-1.374607951\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}.

Thus the height of the left-most leaf tends to the constant

\frac{-1.374607951}{-.3646859471}=3.769292351.

Now we move to the
pathlength
, which is the sum of the distances, summed over all nodes in the new family. Again, an additional variable is used to keep track.

\displaystyle F(z,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k\geq 1}p_{n,k}z^{n}w^{k}

\displaystyle=zw+\sum_{j\geq 1}jzw\Bigl(\sum_{n,k\geq 1}p_{n,k}(zw)^{n}w^{k}\Bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle=zw+zw\frac{F(zw,w)}{(1-F(zw,w))^{2}}.

Compare this with the classical case in
[
5
]
, where there is no square in the denominator. One can use the Lagrange inversion formula to pull out coefficients, since

F=z\Phi(F),\qquad\Phi(y)=w+w\frac{y}{(1-y)^{2}},

and

\displaystyle[z^{n}]F=\frac{1}{n}[F^{n-1}]\Big(w+w\frac{F}{(1-F)^{2}}\Bigr)^{n}=\frac{w^{n}}{n}[F^{n-1}]\Big(1+\frac{F}{(1-F)^{2}}\Bigr)^{n};

something could be written out, but since it is not too pleasant, we won’t do it.

We can compute the average pathlength.
In order to work with implicitly defined functions, we must count nodes; the distance we measure in terms of the number of edges. Then

\displaystyle F(z,u)

\displaystyle=z+\sum_{k\geq 1}kz^{k}F^{k}(zu,u)=z+\frac{zF(zu,u)}{(1-zF(zu,u))^{2}}.

Differentiate this w.r.t.
u
, followed by
u:=1
, using

\frac{\partial F(z,u)}{\partial u}=Y,\quad\frac{\partial F(xu,u)}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=z,u=1}=A^{\prime}(z),\quad\frac{\partial F(zu,v)}{\partial v}\bigg|_{v=1,u=1}=Y;

Solving and simplifying, we get

Y=-{\frac{2zA(z)^{2}-2A(z)^{2}+zA(z)+{z}^{2}A(z)+2A(z)+{z}^{2}-2z}{3{z}^{3}+4{z}^{2}+20z-4}};

the series expansion start with

Y=z^{2}+7z^{3}+41z^{4}+230z^{5}+1261z^{6}+6824z^{7}+36627z^{8}+195504z^{9}.

The denominator factors:

\displaystyle 3{z}^{3}+4{z}^{2}+20z-4

\displaystyle=(z-\rho)(3z^{2}+(4+3\rho)z+20+4\rho+3\rho^{2})

\displaystyle\sim-\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)4.189050371.

The coefficient of
z^{n}
is asymptotic to
4.189050371\rho^{-n}
. For the numerator, we find the approximation

-.3857113818
thus

Y\sim\frac{-.3857113818}{-\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr)4.189050371}=\frac{0.09207609067}{1-\frac{z}{\rho}};

the coefficient of
z^{n}
in

is asymptotic to
0.09207609067\rho^{-n}
. This has to divided by the total number of objects of size

. Recall that this was

\sim-1.079796775[z^{n}]\sqrt{1-\frac{z}{\rho}}\sim-1.079796775\frac{1}{\Gamma({-\frac{1}{2}})}n^{-3/2}\rho^{-n}=0.3046050464n^{-3/2}\rho^{-n}.

Taking quotients, we find the average pathlength to be asymptotic to

\frac{0.09207609067\rho^{-n}}{0.3046050464\,n^{-3/2}\rho^{-n}}=0.3022802536\,n^{3/2}.

Counting of
old leaves
(leftmost children), as in
[
1
]
for the classical case. In our model of distinguished edges, we look at old leaves (the total number of leaves) was already considered. The one node tree has by definition one node, one old leaf and zero young leaves. Recall that

A(z,u)=zu+zA(z,u)\sum_{k\geq 0}(k+1)A(z,u)^{k}

when

marks all leaves; we use two variables
u
,
v
for old versus young leaves. Then

A(z,u,v)=zu+z\sum_{k\geq 1}kA(z,u,v)(A(z,u,v)-zu+zv)^{k}=zu+\frac{zA(z,u,v)}{(1-A(z,u,v)+zu-zv)^{2}}.

We leave further analysis of the generating function to the interested reader. It would be more appealing to set

, so one would only count old leaves and do not care about
the young leaves. Then one would have to analyse

The Lagrange inversion formula is perhaps not immediately available, but

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A(z,u)}{\partial u}\Big|_{u=1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{z(A^{2}(z)+2zA^{2}(z)+2A^{2}(z){z}^{2}-2A(z)-12A(z)z-5A(z){z}^{2}-2{z}^{3}A(z)-14z-2{z}^{3}+4)}{-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3}}

where

stands for

. As was already computed,

-4+20z+4{z}^{2}+3{z}^{3}\sim-21.86322464\bigl(1-\frac{z}{\rho}\bigr).

Together with the expansion of the denominator,

By transfer, the coefficient of

This has to be divided by the asymptotic equivalent of the total number of object of size

This is the asymptotic equivalent of old leaves in the new tree model, assuming that all objects of

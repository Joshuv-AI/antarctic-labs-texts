Dual-Circular Raman Optical Activity of Axial Multipolar Order
Hikaru Watanabe, Rikuto Oiwa, Ryotaro Arita

Abstract
Multipolar order, such as octupolar order, is one of the key concepts of condensed matter physics, particularly in light of elusive hidden orders. However, its experimental identification remains challenging due to the absence of direct coupling to conventional external stimuli. In this study, we propose that dual-circular Raman scattering serves as a detection of multipolar anisotropies. By combining symmetry analysis with microscopic tight-binding calculations, we demonstrate that both time-reversal-even ( θ \theta -even) and time-reversal-odd ( θ \theta -odd) axial multipolar phases exhibit the Raman optical activity as a direct consequence of mirror symmetry breaking. Furthermore, we find that the Stokes and anti-Stokes spectra encode the time-reversal parity of the multipolar order, providing an all-optical means to distinguish multipolar phases. Our findings open a pathway for identifying multipolar orders in various materials through dual-circular Raman spectroscopy as a sensitive and versatile probe.

In this work, we propose that the methodology of dual-circular ROA can be applied to the axial and magnetoaxial multipolar systems even in the absence of chirality or axial dipolar order.
Specifically, we reveal that the

Our proposal introduces a convenient optical probe for the multipolar orders, which have been regarded as ‘hidden’ due to the scarcity of the external stimuli coupled directly to them.
Furthermore, we show that the spacetime symmetry of the multipolar order is encoded in the Stokes and anti-Stokes signals of dual-circular ROA.

Results—

We begin by considering the symmetry condition for dual-circular ROA.
The following symmetry analysis focuses on the cross-circular ROA intensity defined as
U_{\text{CC}}=I_{\text{RL}}-I_{\text{LR}}
, although a similar discussion applies to the parallel-circular ROA defined by
U_{\text{PC}}=I_{\text{RR}}-I_{\text{LL}}
.
Supposing the backscattering geometry with the normal incidence of light along a
n

n>2
) rotation axis, which eliminates spurious birefringence effect

whose mirror plane contains the light beam axis:

U_{\text{CC}}(\delta\omega,\omega,\mathcal{O})=-U_{\text{CC}}(\delta\omega,\omega,m_{\perp}\mathcal{O}).

(1)

Here, we write down the dependence on physical quantities

For instance, the magnetization

m_{\perp}\bm{M}=-\bm{M}

If

The symmetry for

Although previous studies have explored cross-circular ROA in the axial-dipolar, magnetoaxial-dipolar, or chiral materials, the present work identifies ROA originating from the axial and magnetoaxial multipolar orders.

Discussions—

In this work, we proposed a new type of dual circular Raman optical activity originating from the multipolar anisotropies, free from the dipolar anisotropy or chirality.
By integrating the symmetry analysis and microscopic calculations, we have demonstrated the emergence of cross-circular ROA in the
\theta
-even and
\theta
-odd axial multipolar phases.
This activity enables all-optical measurement of multipolar orders and further suggests its potential as a probe for time-reversal parity due to the characteristic Stokes and anti-Stokes symmetry.
Our findings are applicable to a wide range of materials, including the magnetoaxial octupolar order arising from the noncoplanar spin texture, multipolar ordering from the non-Kramers doublet, and commonly found materials such as pyrite.

Magnetoaxial octupolar order has been intensively studied in the heavy fermion systems

Voleti et al. 2020
;
Maharaj et al. 2020
;
Voleti et al. 2023

Previous attempts to detect such orders have relied on cross-coupling between physical quantities and nonlinear responses like the piezomagnetic effect, quadratic anomalous Hall effect, and nonlinear conductivity

Green synthesis of polyherbal silver nanoparticles from Cassia fistula and Manilkara zapota: characterisation and evaluation of wound-healing potential
Authors: Archana Hari, Shobana Sampath, B Vijayageetha, Douglas Law, Gholamreza Abdi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, methanol concentrate of flowers and leaves of Cassia fistula and leaves of Manilkara zapota was used to obtain silver nanoparticles (AgNPs), followed by evaluation of wound healing potential in an animal model. The objective was to assess the overall wound-healing potential of the plant-mediated AgNP formulation rather than to compare different synthesis routes. In SEM analysis, particle dimensions were irregular, ranging from 20 nm to 50 nm. In FTIR, distinct bond stretching was observed at 3332.16, 2114.20, 1635.70, and 637.27. The antibacterial assay of plant Ag NPS in the case of Staphylococcus aureus MTCC 96 showed its maximum zone of inhibition at 17 mm. Accordingly, the antibacterial activity reported in the present study is restricted to S. aureus MTCC 96, the bacterial strain experimentally evaluated and no other pathogenic bacteria have been experimentally. In the excision wound model, PSN (polyherbal silver nanoparticle) and PCE (polyherbal crude extract) at a concentration of 5% w/w showed wound contraction of 75.58% in group IV rats and 70.38% in group III rats. The results were higher when compared with group II. Cytokine TNF-α in blood serum was low in group IV in comparison to group II at the post-wound treatment. It was reported 6.49 pg/mg concentration of TNF-α for group IV and 12.69 pg/mg concentration of TNF-α for group II at the 14th day, indicating that TNF-α is an inflammatory cytokine that is found to be released at the early stage of the wound healing. Histopathology of the group III and IV-treated tissue showed better epithelization than group II rats. Thus, PSN confirmed its significant levels in wound healing studies, which will show its potential in skin care and treating skin-related issues.Keywords: Wound healing, Cassia fistula, Manilkara zapota, Polyherbal, Silver nanoparticle, Human health

Introduction

Wound healing is a complex, finely coordinated biological cascade divided into four overlapping phases: hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and tissue remodeling. Disruptions at any stage, often exacerbated by persistent microbial infection or excessive local oxidative stress, can stall tissue repair and lead to chronic, non-healing wounds. Recent work has emphasized the pivotal role of cellular and molecular frameworks, noting that uncontrolled reactive oxygen species (ROS) destroy extracellular matrices and cause severe cellular senescence at the injury site, as outlined in a comprehensive review in Communications Biology.1 Consequently, modern therapeutic interventions increasingly focus on multi-target systems that simultaneously eliminate bacterial bio-burden and clear excess localized free radicals.

To address these dual challenges, biogenic nanotechnology has emerged as a frontline strategy. Green-synthesized silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) utilize plant-derived secondary metabolites as natural reducing and capping agents, offering a sustainable alternative to hazardous chemical synthesis. According to recent breakthroughs published in Nano Biomedicine and Engineering,2 the phenolic, alkaloid, and carbonyl groups present in botanical extracts not only drive the reduction of silver ions but also embed secondary therapeutic traits like inherent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties into the resulting nanostructures. This multi-functional synergy is supported by recent findings in Discover Nano,3 which confirmed that plant-mediated nanoparticle systems display exceptional biocompatibility and significantly lowered dermal cytotoxicity profiles compared to traditional chemical alternatives.

Integrating these green-synthesized nanoparticles into topical matrices allows for controlled, localized drug delivery directly within the compromised skin layers. During the inflammatory-to-proliferative transition, modulating localized signaling biomolecules is essential to prevent chronic wound extension. As reported in Emergent Materials,4 topical biomaterial platforms mitigate prolonged inflammation by rapidly down-regulating pro-inflammatory markers (such as tumour necrosis factor-alpha TNF-alpha and interleukin-1 beta IL-1), while concurrently up-regulating angiogenic markers like CD31 to foster rapid neovascularization. Furthermore, research in the European Polymer Journal5 demonstrates that establishing an optimal microenvironment accelerates keratinocyte migration and dermal fibroblast proliferation, leading to complete structural closure and uniform re-epithelialization across boundary gaps.

Further the silver nanoparticles underwent an antibacterial study against the desired organism. This nanoparticles with the effectiveness of the synergistic extract are combined with the silver nanoparticles for the future wound dressing.6 Over the world, several herbs that were discovered to have therapeutic properties are still employed in traditional medicine to treat illnesses or syndromes. It was found that traditional medicine practitioners served as the primary healthcare providers in rural communities.

The phytochemicals present in plants make them unique for their unique medicinal properties, they include alkaloids, tannins, flavonoid, terpenoids, phenolic compounds steroids, resins, and other secondary metabolites.7 These substances were biologically reactive and had a big impact on infections and parasites. Many bioactive phytochemicals in plants admired for the ability to fight against the microbes and boosting the immune system of the body.8 Restoring the skin's characteristics and disrupted anatomical continuity required the healing of wounds. There are four phases to the intricate and complex process of wound healing: hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling.9 Chronic wounds were often referred to as ‘difficult to heal’ as they prolong in duration taken (4 to 12 weeks) for recovery. Roughly 1% of people on the planet are thought to have wounds that are difficult to heal and are linked to a lower quality of life.10

Historically, a variety of formulations for the treatment of burns, wounds, cuts, and skin disorders have been made using plants.11 Through a variety of methods, the isolates derived from plants promote tissue regeneration and repair; these effects frequently work in concert to promote total healing. Numerous plants have been employed in conventional medicine as anti-inflammatory, wound-healing, and anti-allergic medicines because they contain therapeutic amounts of flavonoids.12

In medicine, nanotechnology has been used to develop therapeutic medication delivery systems and cures for a wide range of illnesses and conditions.13 So, there were various types of nanocarriers which were under clinical trials and some of them are used as nanomedicines for targeted drug delivery such as nanoparticles, nano spindles, nanofibers, nanotubes, nanosphere, nanorods, nano dendrimers, nanocrystals etc.,.14 In particular, green-synthesized silver-based nanoparticles have attracted attention because of their reported antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities and their potential applications in wound care and other biomedical fields.5, 15, 16, 17

While individual extracts of Cassia fistula and Manilkara zapota possess documented therapeutic properties, their consolidated evaluation in a polyherbal silver nanoparticle (PSN) ointment matrix remains largely unexplored. This study investigates the dual-action synergy of a 5% (w/w) biogenic PSN formulation. By tracking its capacity to regulate local antioxidant enzyme profiles (SOD, CAT, and LPO), suppress serum TNF-alpha concentrations, and accelerate cellular gap closure via in vitro scratch assays, this work provides a robust framework for scalable, plant-mediated nanomedicine in advanced wound care management.

Materials and methods

Plant material

Fresh leaves of Manilkara zapota along with leaves and flowers of Cassia fistula were collected from Aranvayulkuppam, Thiruvallur, Tamil Nadu. After being separated, the leaves were properly cleaned with tap water, allowed to dry in the shade, ground into a fine powder, and then sealed in airtight bottles. With solvents based on their polarity, such as methanol and aqueous ethanol (1:1), the powdered form of the leaves was successively extracted using the percolation method. Using a rotary evaporator, the extracted substances were concentrated and solvent-freed at low pressure. Prior to being employed for analysis, the dried-out crude extracted substances were sealed in airtight bottles and refrigerated after being weighed to determine the extractive yield.

Extraction method

Using methanol as a solvent, the powdery residue of the plant's leaves and flowers was extracted one after the other by the percolation process.18 Using a rotary evaporator, the extracts have been concentrated and solvent-freed at low pressure. Airtight bottles containing the calibrated crude extract were kept in the refrigerator.19

Synergism of plant extract and synthesis of silver nanoparticle

Leaves from Manilkara zapota, along with flowers from Cassia fistula, were collected in equal proportions (i.e., 1:1:1).20 The plant silver nanoparticle was prepared by mixing aqueous solution of silver nitrate (1 mM) and fresh methanol extract of the plants. The present study used a fixed experimental condition consisting of 1 mM AgNO₃, a 1:1:1 plant extract ratio, a 1:9 AgNO₃-to-extract volume ratio, and 24 h incubation at room temperature in the dark. The solution was incubated overnight in the dark at room temperature for 24 h.21 After 24 h, the colourless silver nitrate solution turned pale yellow to reddish brown. The synergistic plant-mediated AgNPs were centrifuged for 20 mins at 5000 rpm. The pellets were allowed to air-dry while the supernatant was disposed of.22

Characterisation of nanoparticles

The depletion of silver metal ions to silver nanoparticle was analysed by using UV- visible Spectrophotometer. Using a Perkin Elmer Lambda 25 spectrometer, the absorption maxima were scanned over the 300–800 nm wavelength range to conduct the UV–vis analysis. The aqueous component was sampled at various intervals.23 FESEM was used to characterise the nanoparticles' size and shape using Carl Zeiss FESEM Sigma360 (Schottky FEG) with an Oxford EDS detector. FTIR measurements were performed to identify functional groups and putative biomolecules associated with the reduction and surface stabilization of the synthesized silver nanoparticles.24, 25

Screening of antibacterial activity

After preparing the nutrient agar, it was added to the sterile petri plates and let to solidify. On it, a 24-h Staphylococcus aureus (MTCC 96) bacterial culture was swabbed.26 Using sterile forceps, the test sample at various concentrations was put over the agar plate. The control was 25 μL of tetracycline. After that, the plates were incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. The inhibitor's diameter was measured after incubation, and the units are given in mm.27

Anti-oxidant assay

On day 14, under anaesthesia, tissues from the wounds were collected.

Assay of lipid peroxidation (LPO)

Thiobarbituric acid (TBA) was used to assess malondialdehyde (MDA), a byproduct product of lipid peroxidation, by producing a coloured material in the reaction. The coloured line was read at 532 nm.28

Assay of superoxide dismutase

By tracking the oxidation of adrenaline, the activity of SOD was determined. The tubes were filled with 0.5 mL of EDTA (0.6 mm) solution and 2.5 mL of carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (0.3 m, pH 10.2). A UV spectrophotometer was used to measure the change in absorbance after injecting 0.5 mL of epinephrine (1.8 mm) at 420 mm for two minutes at intervals of 15 s. The samples from groups I, II, and IV were suitably diluted. Protein units, minutes, and grammes were used to express the activity.29

Assay of catalase

(nmoles of hydrogen peroxidase consumed /min/mg of protein) Using spectrophotometry, the rate of hydrogen peroxidase breakdown was determined at 240 nm.30

Invitro scratch assay

Based on scratch wound healing assay, the procedure was followed and the results were obtained for every 24 h.31

Excision wound model

To oversee and regulate animal research and secure clearance from the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee, this study was carried out in accordance with the board's animal ethics rules (IAEC) of C.L. Baid Metha College of Pharmacy, Chennai, Tamil Nadu with approval No: 06/321/PO/Re/S/01/CPCSEA/dated 31/03/19.

For the surgical excision wound model, four groups of six rats each were employed. The animals in Group I received no therapy; they were used as an untreated negative control. Group II animals were treated with standard drug ointment povidone iodine. Group III were treated with crude polyherbal formulation and group IV were treated with polyherbal silver nanoparticle.

All animal protocol were performed and approved by the ethical committee. All rats were treated humanely throughout the experiment. On day 0, the rat's dorsum was shaved and cleaned by the saline soaked gauze and 70% ethanol solution. A permanent marker was used to create a 314 mm2 circular mark.32 Prior to wound creation and tissue collection, all animals were anaesthetized using ketamine (80 mg/kg, i.p.) and xylazine (10 mg/kg, i.p.) to ensure deep anaesthesia and minimize distress.

Standard ointment povidone iodine 5% w/w and 5% w/w of polyherbal solution (ointment base) and polyherbal silver nanoparticle (ointment base) was applied to the respective group of rats. This was considered as day 0. On experimental observation, all animals were housed in their cages individually and ambient temperature was maintained with proper food and water. The time it took for epithelialisation to occur and the percentage of wound contraction were used to measure the wound-healing properties of crude extract (PCE) and polyherbal silver nanoparticle formulation (PSN).33

Measurement of TNF-α concentration

The mouse TNF-α in blood serum was quantified in vitro using the RayBio Mouse TNF-α ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay) kit. Mouse TNF-α coated in an antibody specific manner on a 96-well plate is used in this test. The wells were filled with standards and blood serum, and the immobilised antibody bound the TNF-α in the serum to the wells.35 Following a washing, biotinylated anti-Mouse TNF-α antibody was applied to the wells. The wells were filled with HRP-conjugated streptavidin after the unbound biotinylated antibody had been rinsed. Following another washing, the wells were filled with a TMB substrate solution, which produced a hue corresponding to the amount of TNF-α bound. The stop solution caused the colour to change from blue to yellow, and the intensity was recorded at 450 nm.36

Histological analysis

The recovered wound tissue underwent thorough histological processing. The tissue was preserved in formalin, cut, and processed—that is, it was cleaned in xylene, dehydrated in graded alcohol, and embedded in paraffin at 58 °C to 60 °C.37 These tissue blocks were divided into 4–5 μ thick sections, stained with eosin and haematoxylin, and then digitally photocopied and mounted. After that, slides were examined under a light microscope, and pictures were taken.38

Statistical analysis

A statistical software program that is commercially accessible was used to analyse the data (mathcracker/one-way-anova software). To determine the significance of differences between the control and PCE and PSN-treated rats, computations were done. Results were presented as mean ± SD and the p value less than 0.001, 0.01 and 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.39

Serum TNF-\(\alpha \) concentrations were measured via ELISA on day 14 to assess late-stage inflammatory status (Table 4).Because TNF-\(\alpha \) is a pro-inflammatory cytokine typically released early by neutrophils and macrophages during the acute phase of wounding, its concentration progressively drops as successful wound contraction advances. On day 14, Group IV (PSN) displayed the lowest residual concentration at 6.49 ± 0.01 pg/mg of protein, followed by Group III at 8.76 ± 0.01 pg/mg. Both plant-derived formulations exhibited lower systemic inflammation than standard Group II (12.69 ± 0.02 pg/mg) (p < 0.01).This single late-stage measurement confirms that the dual-action polyherbal silver nanoparticles efficiently shorten the inflammatory phase, driving the wound cascade rapidly forward into the proliferative and regenerative stages.

Thereby day 14 (Group IV), our applied PSN ointment had caused about 75.6% of the wound to close, which is better than what has been reported for other formulations. For instance, various topical nano formulations and herbal ointments exhibit wound contraction rates ranging from 60% to 65% at similar time points (e.g., allantoin film or specific nano-based positive controls.21, 51, 53 The significant contraction observed in our PSN group is likely due to the formulation's synergistic antimicrobial, antioxidant, and cell-proliferative properties. Ag NPs reduce the microbial load at the wound site, while plant phytochemicals decrease reactive oxygen species and enhance fibroblast migration and collagen synthesis, thereby accelerating re-epithelialization.33, 49 Differences in methodology (animal strain, wound size, ointment composition, nanoparticle loading, and determining techniques) could explain the differences in % contraction between studies. Hydroxyproline, collagen deposition, VEGF, TGF-β, and angiogenesis markers could provide additional mechanistic evidence; however, these markers were not measured in the present study and will be evaluated in future studies. The combination of metal-based AgNPs with phytochemicals improves the healing of wounds by working in two ways: Ag+ ions protect against bacteria while plant metabolites provide antioxidant and regenerative support. This combination leads to faster contraction, better formation of the epithelial layer, and lower levels of TNF-α, which shows that the inflammatory phase is shorter. The current study aimed to investigate the synergistic potential of combining extracts from Cassia fistula and Manilkara zapota in a green production of silver nanoparticles (PSN formulation). Cassia fistula includes flavonoids, tannins, and anthraquinones that are known for helping tissues heal and reducing inflammation. Manilkara zapota, on the other hand, is high in polyphenols and triterpenoids that are good for your health and kill bacteria.1, 4 When incorporated, the phytochemicals from the two sources serve as bio reductants and stabilizing agents in nanoparticle fabrication, resulting to the development of stable, well-capped AgNPs with improved surface reactivity. The mixed extract probably has a wider range of reducing and capping agents, like phenolic hydroxyls, carboxyls, and amine groups, that keep nanoparticles from getting too big and clumping together. This biochemical diversity also encompasses biological activity, wherein various classes of substances (flavonoids from Cassia fistula and saponins from Manilkara zapota) may synergistically facilitate accelerated epithelial regeneration, diminish oxidative stress, and strengthen antimicrobial defense. Previous reports have documented phytochemical synergy in other polyherbal nanoparticle formulations that surpass single-plant counterparts by modulating multiple pathways of healing cascades.2, 20 In our instance, the enhanced wound contraction (75.58% on day 14) and reduced TNF-α secretion signify this synergistic biological efficacy.

Conclusion

The current work explained about the preparation of the silver nanoparticle with the help of the polyherbal plant extract of Cassia fistula leaves and flowers and the Manilkara zapota leaves. This synthesis of silver nanoparticles with the synergistic plants created an effective SNP for our future wound studies. The objective was to assess the overall wound-healing potential of the plant-mediated AgNP formulation rather than to compare different synthesis routes. Wound healing studies exhibited at a concentration of 5%w/w of the polyherbal nanoparticle solution. The characterisation methods of nanoparticles like UV - Visible spectroscopy, SEM analysis and FT-IR confirmed that our synergistic silver nanoparticle was a stabilised nanoparticle which can be further used for various applications. The antibacterial activity of the synergistic silver nanoparticle by disc diffusion method justifies our work in resisting and killing the microbe which is responsible for wound and other related studies. Silver nanoparticle supported the plant extracts and decreased the excision wound within 8 to 9 days. The observations from the invitro assay, histopathological studies enhanced the hope of using the polyherbal silver nano solution as the future wound healing dressing to treat the wounds. The antioxidant assay also revealed that the stress level which is caused due to the wounds are reduced by the anti-oxidants present in the plant extracts which suppresses the oxidation process. Thus, this polyherbal silver nanoparticle could have the future applications in the development of the wound dressing as a promising biomaterial in patients for wound healing.

The study showed that the polyherbal silver nanoparticle formulation had a lot of promise for healing wounds, but with this work is that the botanical extracts of Cassia fistula and Manilkara zapota were not investigated on their own. Thus, while the increased efficacy indicates synergism, the precise quantitative impact of each extract remains indeterminate. Subsequent research should entail a comparative assessment of individual and mixed formulations to validate and quantify the extent of synergy. Systemic haematological, hepatic and renal safety profiling of the synergistic AgNP formulation, as has been performed for other biosynthesized silver nanoparticles, would also be a valuable direction for future work.54

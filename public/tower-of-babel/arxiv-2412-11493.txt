Laws of large numbers and central limit theorem for Ewens-Pitman model
Claudia Contardi, Emanuele Dolera, Stefano Favaro

Abstract
The Ewens-Pitman model is a distribution for random partitions of the set { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\} , with n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , indexed by parameters α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and θ > − α \theta>-\alpha , such that α = 0 \alpha=0 is the Ewens model in population genetics. The large n n asymptotic behaviour of the number K n K_{n} of blocks in the Ewens-Pitman random partition has been extensively investigated in terms of almost-sure and Gaussian fluctuations, which show that K n K_{n} scales as log ⁡ n \log n and n α n^{\alpha} depending on whether α = 0 \alpha=0 or α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , providing non-random and random limiting behaviours, respectively. In this paper, we study the large n n asymptotic behaviour of K n K_{n} when the parameter θ \theta is allowed to depend linearly on n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , a non-standard asymptotic regime first considered for α = 0 \alpha=0 in Feng ( The Annals of Applied Probability , 17 , 2007). In particular, for α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and θ = λ ​ n \theta=\lambda n , with λ > 0 \lambda>0 , we establish a law of large numbers (LLN) and a central limit theorem (CLT) for K n K_{n} , which show that K n K_{n} scales as n n , providing non-random limiting behaviours. Depending on whether α = 0 \alpha=0 or α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , our results rely on different arguments. For α = 0 \alpha=0 we rely on the representation of K n K_{n} as a sum of independent, but not identically distributed, Bernoulli random variables, which leads to a refinement of the CLT in terms of a Berry-Esseen theorem. Instead, for α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , we rely on a compound Poisson construction of K n K_{n} , leading to prove LLNs, CLTs and Berry-Esseen theorems for the number of blocks of the negative-Binomial compound Poisson random partition, which are of independent interest.



Keywords:
Berry-Esseen theorem; central limit theorem; compound Poisson random partition; Ewens-Pitman model; law of large numbers; Mittag-Leffler distribution

1
Introduction

The Ewens-Pitman model for random partitions first appeared in
Pitman (1995)
as a two-parameter generalization of the Ewens model in population genetics
(
Ewens, 1972
;
Crane, 2016
)
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Pi_{n}
be a random partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
blocks of sizes or frequencies
\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})\in\mathbb{N}^{K_{n}}
such that
n=\sum_{1\leq i\leq K_{n}}N_{i,n}
. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, the Ewens-Pitman model assigns to
\Pi_{n}
the probability

P[K_{n}=k,\mathbf{N}_{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})]=\frac{1}{k!}{n\choose n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\frac{[\theta]_{(k,\alpha)}}{[\theta]_{(n)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[1-\alpha]_{(n_{i}-1)},

(1)

where
[x]_{(n,a)}
denotes the rising factorial of
x
of order
n
and increment
a
, i.e.
[x]_{(n,a)}:=\prod_{0\leq i\leq n-1}(x+ia)
and
[x]_{(n)}:=[x]_{(n,1)}
. The distribution (
1
) admits a sequential construction in terms of the Chinese restaurant process
(
Pitman, 1995
;
Zabell, 2005
)
and a Poisson process construction by random sampling the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
(
Perman et al., 1992
;
Pitman and Yor, 1997
)
; see also
Dolera and Favaro (2020)
;
Dolera and Favaro (2021)
for a construction through the negative-Binomial compound Poisson model for random partitions
(
Charalambides, 2007
)
. For
\alpha=0
the Ewens-Pitman model reduces to the Ewens model, arising by random sampling the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
(
Kingman, 1975
)
. The Ewens-Pitman model plays a critical role in a variety of research areas, e.g., population genetics, Bayesian statistics, combinatorics, machine learning and statistical physics. See
Pitman (2006, Chapter 3)
for an overview of the Ewens-Pitman model and generalizations thereof.

There have been several works on the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
under the Ewens-Pitman model, which show different scalings and limits for
K_{n}
depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha\in(0,1)
; see
Pitman (2006, Chapter 3 and Chapter 4)
and references therein. Denote the almost sure and weak convergence by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}
and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}
, respectively. For
\alpha=0
and
\theta>0
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there hold

\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\theta

(2)

and

\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}-\theta\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\theta}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(3)

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes the standard Gaussian random variable; see
Korwar and Hollander (1973, Theorem 2.3)
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there hold

\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}S_{\alpha,\theta}

(4)

and

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(5)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}
and
\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}
denote scaled Mittag-Leffler random variables
(
Zolotarev, 1986
;
Bingham et al., 1989
)
, sharing the same distribution, and
\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}
is independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
; see
Pitman (2006, Theorem 3.8)
and
Bercu and Favaro (2024, Theorem 2.8)
.

Remark 1.1
.

Beyond the almost-sure and Gaussian fluctuations displayed in (
2
)-(
5
),
K_{n}
has been investigated with respect to large and moderate deviations
(
Feng and Hoppe, 1998
;
Favaro and Feng, 2014
;
Favaro et al., 2018
)
and laws of iterated logarithm
(
Bercu and Favaro, 2024
)
. Non-asymptotic results for
K_{n}
have been established in terms of Berry-Esseen theorems
(
Dolera and Favaro, 2020
)
and concentration inequalities
(
Pereira et al., 2022
)
.

1.1
Our contributions

Under the Ewens-Pitman model with
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>0
, we study the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
when the parameter
\theta
is allowed to depend linearly on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This is a non-standard asymptotic regime first considered for
\alpha=0
in Feng
Feng (2007)
. More precisely, for
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
, we establish a law of large numbers (LLN) and a central limit theorem (CLT) for
K_{n}
. Denoting by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}
the convergence in probability, the next theorem states the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K_{n}
be the number of partition blocks under the Ewens-Pitman model with parameter
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
. If

\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right]&\text{ for }\alpha\in(0,1)\\[11.38092pt]
\lambda\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)&\text{ for }\alpha=0\end{cases}

and

\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}^{2}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{2\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{1+\lambda}\right)-\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}\right]&\text{ for }\alpha\in(0,1)\\[11.38092pt]
\lambda\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}&\text{ for }\alpha=0,\end{cases}

then, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there hold:

i)

\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]=n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}+O(1)

(6)

and

\operatorname{Var}(K_{n})=n\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}^{2}+O(1);

(7)

ii)

\frac{K_{n}}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda};

(8)

iii)

\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}^{2}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

(9)

Further, for any
\lambda>0
there hold that
\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}
and
\mathfrak{s}^{2}_{0,\lambda}=\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\mathfrak{s}^{2}_{\alpha,\lambda}
.

Theorem
1.2
provides counterparts of the almost-sure fluctuations (
2
) and (
4
), as well as of the Gaussian fluctuations (
3
) and (
5
), under the regime
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
. By comparing the LLN (
8
) with the almost-sure fluctuations (
2
) and (
4
), we observe how the regime
\theta=\lambda n
affects the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
in terms of both the scalings and the limiting behaviours. While the almost-sure fluctuations scale as
\log n
and
n^{\alpha}
, depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the LLN (
8
) scales as
n
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, this being the “usual” scaling for a LLN. Further, while the almost-sure fluctuations have a non-random and a random limiting behaviour depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which determine the non-random and random centering in (
3
) and (
5
), respectively, the LLN (
8
) has a non-random limiting behaviour for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, determining a non-random centering in the CLT (
9
).

The LLN (
8
) relies on combining Chebyshev’s inequality with the asymptotic expansions (
6
) and (
7
). These expansions, in turn,
are obtained from the distribution of
K_{n}
, which follows by marginalizing (
1
) with
\theta=\lambda n
, for
\lambda>0
. A stronger version of the LLN (
8
) will be also established,
showing that as
n\rightarrow+\infty

\frac{K_{n}}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}.

(10)

While the LLN (
8
) relies on the sole (marginal) distribution of
K_{n}
, the strong LLN (
10
) requires to consider the finite-dimensional laws of the sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. That is, for each
n\geq 1
, it requires to look at
K_{n}
as the number of blocks of the random partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
induced by random sampling the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution with
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, for
\lambda>0
.

Depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the CLT (
9
) relies on different arguments. For
\alpha=0
, the CLT relies on the representation of
K_{n}
as a sum of independent, but not identically distributed, Bernoulli random variables, leading to a refinement of the CLT in terms of a Berry-Esseen theorem with respect to the Kolmogorov metric
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
. In particular, if
F_{n}
and
\Phi
denote the cumulative distribution functions of
n^{-1/2}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{-1}(K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda})
and
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, respectively, then we will show that there exist a constant
C>0
and
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|F_{n}-\Phi\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C\,\log(n)}{n^{1/8}}

(11)

holds for every
n\geq\bar{n}
; the constant
C
is obtained constructively, and it can be made explicit by gathering equations in the proof of the Berry-Esseen inequality (
11
). For
\alpha\in(0,1)
the CLT relies on the compound Poisson construction of
K_{n}
, leading to LLNs, CLTs and Berry-Esseen theorems for the number of blocks of the negative-Binomial compound Poisson random partition, which are of independent interest
(
Charalambides, 2007
;
Dolera and Favaro, 2021
)
.

1.2
Related work

There exists a rich literature on the large
\theta
asymptotic behaviour of the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution, assuming
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>0
. In population genetics, the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution (i.e.
\alpha=0
) describes the distribution of gene frequencies in a large neutral population at a locus, with the parameter
\theta>0
taking on the interpretation of the scaled population mutation rate
(
Feng, 2010
, Chapter 2)
. The genetic interpretation of
\theta
has motivated several works on the large
\theta
asymptotic behaviour of the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution, and statistics thereof, providing Gaussian fluctuations and large (and moderate) deviations
(
Watterson and Guess, 1977
;
Griffiths, 1979
;
Joyce et al., 2002
;
Dawson and Feng, 2006
;
Feng, 2007
;
Feng and Gao, 2008
)
. In particular,
Feng (2007)
first considered the regime
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
, in the study of the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of the number
K_{n}
of blocks in the Ewens random partition, providing a large deviation principle for
K_{n}
. Some of the large
\theta
asymptotic results developed for the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution have been extended to two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution (i.e.
\alpha\in(0,1)
), in terms of both Gaussian fluctuations and large deviations
(
Feng, 2007a
;
Feng and Gao, 2010
)
. Instead, nothing is known on the large
\theta
asymptotic behaviour of the number
K_{n}
of blocks in the Ewens-Pitman random partition, not even an extension of the large deviation principle of
Feng (2007, Theorem 4.1. and Theorem 4.4.)
. The present paper covers this gap in the literature, providing a LLN and a CLT for
K_{n}
in the regime
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
.

2.2
LLN (
8
)

To prove the LLN (
8
), it is useful to consider the following identity:

\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}}{n}=\frac{K_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]}{n}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]-n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}}{n}.

(12)

The second term in the right-end side is deterministic, and converges to 0 in view of (
6
). Then, fix
\varepsilon>0
and combine Chebyshev’s inequality
with (
7
) to obtain

\displaystyle P\left[\left|\frac{K_{n}-\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]}{n}\right|>\varepsilon\right]

\displaystyle=P\left[\left|K_{n}-\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]\right|>n\varepsilon\right]\leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}(K_{n})}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

as

2.3
CLT (
9
)

To prove the CLT (
9
), we recall the compound Poisson construction of
K_{n}

(
Dolera and Favaro, 2020
, Lemma 1 and Proposition 1)
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>0
let
S_{\alpha,\theta}
be the scaled Mittag-Leffler random variable in (
4
). More precisely,
S_{\alpha,\theta}
is a positive random variable with density function

f_{S_{\alpha,\theta}}(s)=\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)}s^{\frac{\theta-1}{\alpha}-1}f_{\alpha}(s^{-1/\alpha})\hskip 18.49988pts>0,

where
f_{\alpha}
is the positive
\alpha
-stable density function
(
Pollard, 1946
)
. Further information about
f_{\alpha}
can be found in
Zolotarev (1986, Sections 2.2 and 2.4)
, while

Pitman (2006, Chapter 0)
contains some details on
S_{\alpha,\theta}
. In particular, there hold

\mathbb{E}\left[S_{\alpha,\theta}\right]=\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\theta)}

(13)

and

\operatorname{Var}\left[S_{\alpha,\theta}\right]=\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}+1\right)\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+2\alpha)}-\left(\frac{\theta}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\theta)}{\Gamma(\theta+\alpha)}\right)^{2}.

(14)

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
z>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce a random variable
R(\alpha,n,z)
with values in
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, whose distribution has probability mass function given by

P[R(\alpha,n,z)=k]=\frac{\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)z^{k}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathscr{C}(n,j;\alpha)z^{j}}\hskip 18.49988ptk\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

(15)

where

\mathscr{C}(n,k;\alpha)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{k\choose i}[-i\alpha]_{(n)}\geq 0

is the generalized factorial coefficient, with the proviso that
\mathscr{C}(0,0;\alpha):=1
and
\mathscr{C}(n,0;\alpha):=0
for
n\geq 1
. See
Charalambides (2005, Chapter 2)
for detailed account on
\mathscr{C}
. Specifically, (
15
) provides the distribution of the number of blocks in a random partition of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
under the negative-binomial compound Poisson model
(
Charalambides, 2007
, Example 3.2)
.

Let
G_{a,b}
be a Gamma random variable with shape

and scale
b>0
, namely

has density function
f_{a,b}(x)=[b^{a}/\Gamma(a)]x^{a-1}e^{-bx}
, for

.
For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>0
,

K_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}R\left(\alpha,n,Z_{\theta,n}\right),

(16)

and

Z_{\theta,n}:=S_{\alpha,\theta}G_{\theta+n,1}^{\alpha}

such that

and

are independent random variables, and independent of

, for any

. See also
Dolera and Favaro (2021)
for details on (
16
). Here and throughout,

denotes identity in distribution.

) in the regime

For any

\mu(z):=z\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau(z)}\right)

As

(23)

\frac{R_{n}(z)}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu(z).

Finally, set

Then, there exists a continuous function

that satisfy

Proposition 2.3

Under the assumptions of Proposition

\mu(z_{0})=\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}

Set

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(z_{0})+\Sigma^{2}\cdot\left(\mu^{\prime}(z_{0})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\tau_{0}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}-\frac{\alpha}{(\lambda+1-\alpha)\tau_{0}}\right)-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\tau_{0}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{\lambda+1}{\tau_{0}(\lambda+1-\alpha)}\right)^{2}

Lemma 2.4
.

If

\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(z_{0})Y+\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}x}{\sigma(z_{0})}\right)\right]=\Phi(x)

Introduce a standard Gaussian random variable

\Phi\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime}(z_{0})Y+\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}x}{\sigma(z_{0})}\right)=P\left[Z\leq\frac{\mu^{\prime}(z_{0})Y+\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha,\lambda}x}{\sigma(z_{0})}\ \bigg|\ Y\right],

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

\sqrt{n}\left[\psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)-\psi(z_{0})\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,(\psi^{\prime}(z_{0}))^{2}\Sigma^{2}\right)

From the fundamental theorem of calculus, we write that

\sqrt{n}\left[\psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)-\psi(z_{0})\right]=\sqrt{n}\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-z_{0}\right)\int_{0}^{1}\psi^{\prime}\left(z_{0}+t\left[\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-z_{0}\right]\right)\mathrm{d}t.

is bounded, a combination of the dominated convergence theorem with the continuous mapping

\displaystyle:=\exp\left\{n\,f^{\prime}(1)\left[\left(e^{\frac{i\,\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}}}-1\right)-\left(\frac{i\,\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}\right)\right]\right.

uniformly on compact sets. We refer to Appendix

4
Proof of the strong LLN (
10
)

To clarify how to interpret the sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
, it is useful to recall the definition of the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution. Among possible definitions, a simple and intuitive one follows from the stick-breaking construction
(
Perman et al., 1992
;
Pitman and Yor, 1997
)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{V_{j}^{(n)}\}_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent random variables such that for
j\geq 1

V_{j}^{(n)}\sim\text{Beta}(1-\alpha,\lambda n+\alpha j),

where
\text{Beta}(a,b)
denotes the Beta distribution with parameter
a,b>0
. Now set

W_{1}^{(n)}:=V_{1}^{(n)}

and, for
j\geq 2

W_{j}^{(n)}:=V_{j}^{(n)}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}(1-V_{l}^{(n)})

so that
W_{j}^{(n)}\in(0,1)
for
j\geq 1
and
\sum_{j\geq 1}W_{j}^{(n)}=1
almost surely. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution with
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
,
can be also seen as the law of the decreasing random probabilities
(W_{j}^{(n)})_{j\geq 1}
. See
Pitman (2006, Chapter 4)
and references therein for further details.

Let
\{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be the sequence of random probability measures defined by
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}=\sum_{j\geq 1}W_{j}^{(n)}\delta_{j}
, meaning that
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
takes values in the space of all probability measures on
(\mathbb{N},2^{\mathbb{N}})
,
2^{\mathbb{N}}
denoting the power set of
\mathbb{N}
;
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
is typically referred to as the two-parameter Pitman-Yor process
(
Pitman, 1995
;
Pitman and Yor, 1997
)
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider a sequence
\{X_{i}^{(n)}\}_{i\geq 1}
of exchangeable
\mathbb{N}
-valued random variables having
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
as directing random probability measure, that is, the
X_{i}^{(n)}
’s are conditionally i.i.d. given
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
. Due to the discreteness of
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the random variables
\{X_{1}^{(n)},\dots,X_{n}^{(n)}\}
induce a random partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and we denote by
K_{n}^{(n)}
the number of blocks of such a random partition. Obviously, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

K_{n}^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}K_{n}

(39)

with
K_{n}
being the number of blocks in the Ewens-Pitman model with
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
; that is the distribution of
K_{n}
follows by marginalizing (
1
) with
\theta=\lambda n
, for
\lambda>0
. However, the well-known Markov structure of
\{K_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

(
Pitman, 1995
, Proposition 9)
does not extends to
\{K_{n}^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
, whatever the
k
-dimensional laws
(k\geq 2)
of the sequence
\{K_{n}^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
.

The strong LLN (
10
) is thus concerned with the sequence
\{K_{n}^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
, but the proof we present ensues only from (
39
), independently of the

k
-dimensional laws
(k\geq 2)
of the sequence
\{K_{n}^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
. For this reason, and for notational convenience, we drop the superscript from
K_{n}^{(n)}
. The way is now paved for the proof of the strong LLN (
10
). As for the LLN, we consider the identity

\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}}{n}=\frac{K_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]}{n}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]-n\mathfrak{m}_{\alpha,\lambda}}{n}.

The almost sure convergence to
0
of the first term on the right-hand side follows from a standard application of the Borel–Cantelli lemma, after proving that

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}P\left(\left|K_{n}-\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]\right|>n\varepsilon\right)<+\infty

holds true for any
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, this can be proved by combining Markov’s inequality (with exponent
p=4
) with the fact that, as
n\to+\infty

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]\right)^{4}\right]=O\left(n^{2}\right).

(40)

See Appendix
A.2
for details on (
40
). This completes the proof of the strong LLN (
10
).

5
Proof of the Berry-Esseen inequality (
11
)

We start by combining Lemma
3.1
with the well-known inequality
(
Petrov, 1975
, Chapter V, Theorem 2)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{n}-\Phi\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq\mathcal{C}\,\sigma(z)\,n^{\delta}}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(\xi)-e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}}{\xi}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}{n^{-\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\frac{1}{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(\xi)-e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}}{\xi}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\frac{\tilde{c}}{n^{-3\delta+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}}{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}{n^{-\delta}}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}{n^{-\delta}}.

We consider separately the terms
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
. For
I_{1}
, we combine the triangle inequality,
Petrov (1975, Chapter IV, Lemma 5)
and
Chow and Teicher (1997, Section 8.4, Theorem 1, Equation (4))
to write that, in a neighborhood of
0
, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\varphi_{n}(\xi)-e^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}}}{\xi}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}}\right]\right|+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}}\right)^{2}\right]\left|\xi\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\xi\right|+\frac{1}{8}\left|\xi^{4}\right|.

From (
6
) and (
7
), we obtain

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}}\right]\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}\right|\leq\frac{S_{1}}{\sqrt{n}},

where
S_{1}:=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}}\,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}\right|
. An analogous argument allows to prove

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{K_{n}-n\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}^{2}}}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{S_{2}}{n}

where
S_{2}:=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}}\,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\operatorname{Var}[K_{n}]-n\left(\mathcal{D}+\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}-2\mathfrak{m}_{0,\lambda}\mathcal{B}\right)\right|
. Accordingly, we write

I_{1}\leq\frac{2S_{1}}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{2S_{2}+1/2}{n^{3}}+\frac{1}{40\,n^{5}}\leq\mathcal{C}n^{-3/2}.

For
I_{2}
, argue as in the proof of the Berry-Esseen theorem for
R_{n}(z)
to conclude that

I_{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\log(n)\,n^{3\delta-\frac{1}{2}}.

In conclusion,

\left\|F_{n}-\Phi\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{1}n^{-\frac{3}{2}}+C_{2}\log(n)\,n^{-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta}+\tilde{\mathcal{C}}{n^{-\delta}}

for some positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. To conclude, it is easy to see that for every
\delta\in(0,1/6)
,
\min\left(\delta,-3\delta+\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{8}
, which produces the rate in (
11
).

6
Discussion

Motivated by the genetic interpretation of the Ewens model,
Feng (2007)
first investigated the the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
in the regime
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
, providing a large deviation principle
(
Feng, 2007
, Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 4.4)
. In this paper, we considered the same regime under the Ewens-Pitman model of parameters
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>0
, which includes the Ewens model as a special case of
\alpha=0
. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
, our main results in Theorem
1.2
provide a LLN and a CLT for
K_{n}
in the regime
\theta=\lambda n
, as well as a Berry-Esseen refinement of the CLT for
\alpha=0
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
our results rely on the compound Poisson construction of
K_{n}
, which led to novel large
n
approximation results for the negative-Binomial compound Poisson random partition and for the Mittag-Leffler distribution function. From
Dolera and Favaro (2021, Theorem 2)
, an analogous compound Poisson construction holds for the number
M_{r,n}
of blocks with frequency
1\leq r\leq n
in Ewens-Pitman random partition; see also
(
Charalambides, 2007
;
Dolera and Favaro, 2020
)
. Therefore, we expect that the strategy developed to prove Theorem
1.2
can be applied to provide analogous LLNs and CLTs for
M_{r,n}
. More generally,
Dolera and Favaro (2021, Theorem 2)
suggests that one may consider the problem of extending Theorem
1.2
to other statistics of the Ewens-Pitman random partition, such as
(M_{1,n},\ldots,M_{p,n})
and
M_{p,n}/K_{n}
for
p<n
. Instead, for
\alpha=0
the extension of Theorem
1.2
to
M_{r,n}
, as well as to statistics thereof, may be developed by means of more direct Berry-Esseen type calculations, along the lines of Lemma
3.1
.

Theorem
1.2
paves the way to future research in Bayesian (nonparametric) statistics for “species sampling” problems
(
Lijoi et al., 2007
;
Favaro et al., 2009
;
Balocchi et al., 2024
)
. In such a context, one of the major problem is the estimation of the number of unseen species in a sample. Specifically, given
n\geq 1
observed individuals belonging to different species (or symbols), which are modeled as a random sample
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
from the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution, the unseen-species problem calls for estimating

K_{m}^{(n)}=|\{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{n+m}\}\setminus\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}|,

namely the number of hitherto unseen (distinct) species that would be observed if
m\geq 1
additional samples
(X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{n+m})
were collected from the same distribution. Then, the interest is in the conditional distribution of
K_{m}^{(n)}
given the Ewens-Pitman random partition
(K_{n},\mathbf{N}_{n})
induced by
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
(i.e., the posterior distribution), whose expectation provides a Bayesian estimator of
K_{m}^{(n)}
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, in analogy with the almost-sure fluctuation (
4
), the large
m
asymptotic behaviour of the posterior distribution has been characterized, with applications to approximate credible intervals for the Bayesian estimator
(
Favaro et al., 2009
)
. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
one may consider the problem of investigating such a large
m
asymptotic behaviour in the regime
\theta=\lambda m
, with
\lambda>0
, developing a posterior counterpart of Theorem
1.2
to introduce large
m
Gaussian credible intervals for the Bayesian estimator of
K_{m}^{(n)}
. Work through this direction is ongoing
(
Contardi et al., 2024
)
.

Outline of the proof of the CLT (
21
).

The proof consists of three steps.

1.

In Lemma
6.2
, we provide an approximation of the distribution of
S_{\lambda n,\alpha}
in terms of the distribution of the
(1-\alpha)
-th power of a Gamma random variable.
More precisely, denote by
d_{\text{TV}}
the total variation distance between distributions on
(0,+\infty)
, namely

d_{\text{TV}}\left(\mu,\nu\right):=\sup_{A\in\mathscr{B}((0,+\infty))}\left|\mu(A)-\nu(A)\right|.

We will show that

d_{\text{TV}}\left(\mu_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}},\,\mu_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),

where
G_{\rho n+\tau,B}
has
\operatorname{Gamma}\left(\rho n+\tau,B\right)
distribution, for a suitable choice of parameters
\rho,\tau
and
B
.

2.

In Lemma
6.3
, we will prove a general CLT for variables of the form

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}\right)^{1-\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\right)^{\alpha},

where
\{X_{i}\}
and
\{Y_{i}\}
are two independent i.i.d. sequences of positive random variables. As an immediate consequence (Corollary
6.4
), we derive a CLT for products of powers of independent Gamma random variables, which we then apply to the auxiliary variable
\hat{Z_{n}}:=G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}G_{(\lambda+1)n,1}^{\alpha}
.

3.

Thanks to a consequence of Lemma
6.2
and to a further general result (Lemma
6.5
), we will show that it is possible to replace the approximating Gamma random variable with the random variable
S_{\lambda n,\alpha}
in the CLT for
\hat{Z}_{n}
, obtaining the desired limit theorem for
Z_{n}
.

∎

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
E_{\alpha}:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
be defined by

E_{\alpha}(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\,\left(\frac{\alpha}{y}\right)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2(1-\alpha)}}\,\exp\left\{-(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{\alpha}{y}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right\}.

Then, for any
y\in(0,+\infty)
, there hold

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f_{\alpha}(y)}{E_{\alpha}(y)}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}y^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}

(A.6)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f_{\alpha}(y)}{E_{\alpha}(y)}-1-Q_{\alpha}y^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{\alpha}y^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}

(A.7)

for suitable positive constants
C_{\alpha},\,Q_{\alpha},\,K_{\alpha}
depending only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

Zolotarev (1986, Theorem 2.5.2)
shows that, as
y\to 0

\frac{f_{\alpha}(y)}{E_{\alpha}(y)}=1+O\left(y^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right)

Furthermore, it is known that, as
y\to+\infty
,

f_{\alpha}(y)=O\left(y^{-(1+\alpha)}\right)\,\text{ and }\,E_{\alpha}(y)=O\left(y^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2(1-\alpha)}}\right).

Whence, as
y\to+\infty

\frac{f_{\alpha}(y)}{E_{\alpha}(y)}=O\left(y^{\frac{\alpha(2\alpha-1)}{1-\alpha}}\right).

Since
2\alpha-1<1
for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
, (
A.6
) is proven. To prove (
A.7
), use again
Zolotarev (1986, Theorem 2.5.2)
to show that, as
y\to 0

\frac{f_{\alpha}(y)}{E_{\alpha}(y)}=1+Q_{\alpha}y^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+O\left(y^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right)

holds with a suitable constant
Q_{\alpha}
. The conclusion is obtained by considering the previous asymptotic behaviours as
y\to+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Set
\rho=\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}
,
\tau=\frac{1}{2}
,
B=(1-\alpha)\,\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
. Then, letting
\mu_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}}
and
\mu_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}

stand for the distributions of
S_{\lambda n,\alpha}
and
G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}
, respectively, as
n\to+\infty
there holds

d_{\text{TV}}\left(\mu_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}},\,\mu_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Proof.

Since
\mu_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}}
and
\mu_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}
have densities

\displaystyle f_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n-1}{\alpha}-1}\,f_{\alpha}\left(x^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\mathds{1}_{(0,+\infty)}(x)

\displaystyle f_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left[(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right]^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,\exp\left\{-(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right\}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2(1-\alpha)}}\mathds{1}_{(0,+\infty)}(x)

respectively, the total variation distance at issue can be handled as follows:

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}\left(\mu_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}},\,\mu_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|f_{S_{\lambda n,\alpha}}(x)-f_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x

(bound (
A.6
))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n-1}{\alpha}-1}\,E_{\alpha}\left(x^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)-f_{G_{\rho n+\tau,B}^{1-\alpha}}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n-1}{\alpha}-1}\,E_{\alpha}\left(x^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)\,x^{-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{1}^{(n)}+\frac{C_{\alpha}}{2}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(n)}.

Thus, te proof is concluded if we show that

\mathcal{J}_{1}^{(n)}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\,\text{ and }\,\hskip 9.24994pt\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(n)}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Simple algebraic manipulations yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2(1-\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)}}-\frac{B^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right|\times

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}\exp{\left\{-Bx^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\right\}}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2(1-\alpha)}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2(1-\alpha)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha(1-\alpha)}}-\frac{B^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right|\times

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\frac{(1-\alpha)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{B^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\,\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n}\,\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)^{\lambda n-\frac{1}{2}}\left(2\pi\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\right|.

Moreover, for some suitable constants
c_{\alpha}
,
\tilde{C}_{\alpha}
depending only on
\alpha
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}c_{\alpha}\,\exp{\left\{-Bx^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\right\}}\,x^{\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}+\frac{2\alpha-3}{2(1-\alpha)}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\frac{c_{\alpha}(1-\alpha)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{B^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}_{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\left[(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right]^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\lambda n}}.

Set
\lambda n=:t
. Then, we study the asymptotic behavior of

g_{j}(t):=\frac{\Gamma(t)\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}t+\frac{j}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{t}{\alpha}\right)}

as
t\to+\infty
, for
j\in\{-1,1\}

\Gamma(t)=\sqrt{2\pi(t-1)}\left(\frac{t-1}{e}\right)^{t-1}\left[1+O\left(t^{-1}\right)\right]\hskip 9.24994pt(t\to+\infty)\ ,

with the fact that

\left(1+\frac{a}{t}\right)^{t}=e^{a}\left[1+O\left(t^{-1}\right)\right]\hskip 9.24994pt(t\to+\infty)\ ,

we get, after straightforward computations,

\displaystyle g_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(2\pi e)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}t}\alpha^{\frac{t}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[1-\frac{1}{2t}+O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\right]^{t}&\text{if }j=1\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}t}\alpha^{\frac{t}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[1+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right]&\text{if }j=1\\
O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)&\text{if }j=-1\end{cases}

where
c_{\alpha}^{\star}
is another constant depending only on
\alpha
.
Substituting the first and second expression, respectively, in the expressions of
\mathcal{J}_{1}^{(n)}
and
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(n)}
, we obtain the desired results.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
X_{0}
be a real random variable and
\{X_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be two sequences of i.i.d. positive random variables. We further assume:

a)

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}^{4}\right]<+\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{1}^{4}\right]<+\infty
;

b)

\mu_{X}:=\mathbb{E}\left[X_{1}\right]
,
\mu_{Y}:=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{1}\right]
,
\sigma^{2}_{X}:=\operatorname{Var}\left(X_{1}\right)
and
\sigma^{2}_{Y}:=\operatorname{Var}\left(Y_{1}\right)
;

c)

X_{0}
,
\{X_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
are independent.

Then, the following CLT holds:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}\right)^{1-\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\right)^{\alpha}-nm\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,V^{2}\right)

as
n\to+\infty
, where

m:=\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}

and

V^{2}:=\mu_{X}^{2(1-\alpha)}\mu_{Y}^{2\alpha}\left[\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}\sigma_{X}^{2}}{\mu_{X}^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma_{Y}^{2}}{\mu_{Y}^{2}}\right].

Proof.

Start considering these trivial identities:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}=\frac{X_{0}}{n}+\mu_{X}+\frac{\sigma_{X}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{X_{i}-\mu_{X}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)

and

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}=\mu_{Y}+\frac{\sigma_{Y}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{Y_{i}-\mu_{Y}}{\sigma_{Y}}\right).

From
(
Strassen, 1967
, Theorem 1.5)
, there exist two independent Brownian motions
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{B^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{X_{i}-\mu_{X}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}B_{n}+R_{n}\,,\hskip 20.0ptR_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{X_{i}-\mu_{X}}{\sigma_{X}}\right)-B_{n}

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{Y_{i}-\mu_{Y}}{\sigma_{Y}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}B^{\prime}_{n}+R^{\prime}_{n}\,,\hskip 20.0ptR^{\prime}_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{Y_{i}-\mu_{Y}}{\sigma_{Y}}\right)-B^{\prime}_{n}

with

R_{n}=O\left((n\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=o\left(n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\right)\hskip 10.0pt\forall\varepsilon>0

R^{\prime}_{n}=O\left((n\log\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=o\left(n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\right)\hskip 10.0pt\forall\varepsilon>0.

Recalling that, from the law of iterated logarithm

\frac{|B_{n}|}{n}=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}\right)\hskip 20.0pt\text{and}\hskip 20.0pt\frac{|B^{\prime}_{n}|}{n}=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}\right),

one gets

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}\right)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\left[1+\frac{X_{0}}{n\mu_{X}}+\frac{\sigma_{X}}{n\mu_{X}}B_{n}+\frac{\sigma_{X}}{n\mu_{X}}R_{n}\right]^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\left[1+\frac{(1-\alpha)\,\sigma_{X}}{n\mu_{X}}B_{n}+o\left(n^{-\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\right)\right]

and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}\left[1+\frac{\sigma_{Y}}{n\mu_{Y}}B^{\prime}_{n}+\frac{\sigma_{Y}}{n\mu_{Y}}R^{\prime}_{n}\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}\left[1+\frac{\alpha\,\sigma_{Y}}{n\mu_{Y}}B^{\prime}_{n}+o\left(n^{-\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\right)\right]

for any
\varepsilon>0
. This implies

\sqrt{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{i}\right)^{1-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\right)^{\alpha}-\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}\right]\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{=}}\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}\left[\frac{(1-\alpha)\,\sigma_{X}}{\mu_{X}}\frac{B_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\alpha\,\sigma_{Y}}{\mu_{Y}}\frac{B^{\prime}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+o\left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)\right].

Observe that, by elementary properties of the two independent Brownian motions
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{B^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
,

\mu_{X}^{1-\alpha}\mu_{Y}^{\alpha}\left[\frac{(1-\alpha)\,\sigma_{X}}{\mu_{X}}\frac{B_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{\alpha\,\sigma_{Y}}{\mu_{Y}}\frac{B^{\prime}_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,V^{2}\right).

Finally, apply Slutsky’s theorem to conclude the proof.
∎

A.4
On a property of uniform integrability of
Z_{n}

We now prove a property of uniform integrability of
Z_{n}
which, in combination with convergence in distribution, guarantees convergence of the moments.
Hence, from the CLT we will deduce convergence of the moments of
(Z_{n}-n\,z_{0})/(\sqrt{n\Sigma^{2}})
to those of the standard Gaussian.

Lemma 6.6
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
2.1
, it holds

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{Z_{n}-nz_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{4}\right]<+\infty.

Proof.

We show that as
n\to+\infty

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z_{n}-nz_{0}\right)^{4}\right]=O\left(n^{2}\right).

In fact,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z_{n}-nz_{0}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{k}]\,(nz_{0})^{4-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}\,\frac{\Gamma(\lambda n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(k+\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)}{\Gamma(k\alpha+\lambda n)}\,\frac{\Gamma\left((\lambda+1)n+k\alpha\right)}{\Gamma\left((\lambda+1)n\right)}\times\,

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times(-1)^{4-k}\,n^{4-k}\,\left(\frac{\lambda n}{\alpha}\right)^{4-k}\,\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{4-k}.

First-order Stirling’s approximation, combined with some simple computations, yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z_{n}-nz_{0}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{4}\,\left(\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\right)^{4}\,\,\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}\,(-1)^{4-k}\times

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\times\left[1-\frac{k\alpha(k\alpha-1)}{2\lambda n}+\frac{k(k-1)\alpha}{2\lambda n}+\frac{k\alpha(k\alpha-1)}{2(\lambda+1)n}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=c_{0}\,n^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}\,(-1)^{4-k}+n^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}(-1)^{4-k}\left(c_{1}\,k+c_{2}\,k^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+O(n^{2})

for some constants
c_{0},\,c_{1}\,,c_{2}
depending only on
\alpha
and
\lambda
. Now, it can be easily seen that

\sum_{k=0}^{4}\binom{4}{k}\,(-1)^{4-k}\,k^{i}=0

for
i=0,1,2
, which concludes the proof.
∎

By combining Lemma
6.6
and Proposition
2.1
we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\Psi:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\Psi\in C^{3}(0,+\infty)
with bounded derivatives. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)\right]=\,\Psi(z_{0})+\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1-\alpha}{\lambda+1}\Psi^{\prime}(z_{0})+\frac{1}{2n}\Sigma^{2}\,\Psi^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Proof.

Let
e_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)\right]
. By means of
(
Tricomi and Erdèlyi, 1951
, Equation 1)
, we write

\displaystyle e_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left[n\,\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{2n\lambda(\lambda+1)}\right)\right]+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=z_{0}+\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1-\alpha}{\lambda+1}+o\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

Taylor’s formula with Bernstein’s integral remainder
(
Dudley, 2002
, Appendix B)
shows that

\displaystyle\Psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)-\Psi(e_{n})

\displaystyle=\Psi^{\prime}(e_{n})\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(e_{n})\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(e_{n}+s\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)\right)\,(1-s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Taking expectation on both sides we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\Psi(e_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(e_{n})\,\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(e_{n}+s\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)\right)\,(1-s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right].

By applying again Taylor’s formula to
\Psi^{\prime}
and
\Psi^{\prime\prime}
, the last term becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\Psi(z_{0})+\frac{1}{2n}\,\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1-\alpha}{\lambda+1}\,\Psi^{\prime}(z_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\,\Psi^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})\,\frac{n\Sigma^{2}+O(1)}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(e_{n}+s\left(\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right)\right)\,(1-s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right].

Since
\Psi^{\prime\prime\prime}
is bounded, to reach the conclusion it is enough to prove that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-e_{n}\right|^{3}\right]=\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-z_{0}\right|^{3}\right]=0.

Now, by combining Lemma
6.6
with Proposition
2.1
, we get

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{Z_{n}-nz_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|^{3}\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(x)

in view of the uniform integrability. Since
n\,\left|\frac{Z_{n}}{n}-z_{0}\right|^{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left|\frac{Z_{n}-nz_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|^{3}
, we finally obtain that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left|\frac{Z_{n}-nz_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|^{3}\right]=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\times\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\frac{Z_{n}-nz_{0}}{\sqrt{n}}\right|^{3}\right]=0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

The following lemma states a basic calculus result, that
guarantees the existence of a continuous function bounding from above any uniformly convergent sequence of functions on a compact set.
Being unable to find a precise statement on some calculus textbook, we include here
both statement and proof, to maintain the paper self-contained.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\mathbb{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
\mathbb{C}
) be a compact set. Consider a sequence of continuous functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
f_{n}:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, as
n\to+\infty
,
f_{n}\rightarrow f
uniformly for some (continuous) function
f:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{R}
. For each
x\in\mathbb{K}
, define
S(x):=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f_{n}(x)\right|
. Then
S:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

To prove Lemma
6.12
, we first need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.13
.

For
i=1,2
, let
(\mathbb{M}_{i},d_{\mathbb{M}_{i}})
be metric spaces, endowed with the metric topology. Let
\mathbb{K}_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{M}_{i}
be compact subsets, and let
F:\mathbb{K}_{1}\times\mathbb{K}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function on the product space. Then,
G:\mathbb{K}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
G(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{K}_{2}}F(x,y)
is continuous.

Proof of lemma
6.13
.

Consider a convergent sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{K}_{1}
with
x_{n}\to\bar{x}\in\mathbb{K}_{1}
. We prove that

G(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}G(x_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}G(x_{n})\leq G(\bar{x}).

Since
\mathbb{K}_{2}
is compact and the real-valued function
y\mapsto F(\bar{x},y)
on
\mathbb{K}_{2}
is continuous, there exists
y^{\star}\in\mathbb{K}_{2}
such that

G(\bar{x})=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{K}_{2}}F(\bar{x},y)=F(\bar{x},y^{\star}).

By definition,
G(x_{n})\geq F(x_{n},y^{\star})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the real-valued function
x\mapsto F(x,y^{\star})
on
\mathbb{K}_{1}
is continuous. Whence,

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}G(x_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F(x_{n},y^{\star})=F(\bar{x},y^{\star})=G(\bar{x}).

We prove the other inequality by contradiction. Assume there exists a subsequence
\{x_{n_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{x_{n}\}
such that

G(x_{n_{j}})\rightarrow L>G(\bar{x}).

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, since the real-valued function
y\mapsto F(x_{n_{j}},y)
on
\mathbb{K}_{2}
is continuous, there exists
y_{j}\in\mathbb{K}_{2}
such that

G(x_{n_{j}})=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{K}_{2}}F(x_{n_{j}},y)=F(x_{n_{j}},y_{j}).

Now, consider the sequence
\{y_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{K}_{2}
. By sequential compactness, there exists a subsequence
\{y_{j_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to some point
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{K}_{2}
. Hence, by continuity of
F
,

G(x_{n_{j_{k}}})=F(x_{n_{j_{k}}},y_{j_{k}})\rightarrow F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq G(\bar{x})

which produces the contradiction.
∎

Proof of lemma
6.12
.

Let
\mathbb{N}^{*}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
denote the Alexandroff compactification of
\mathbb{N}
with the discrete topology. Further, define
F:\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}\to[0,+\infty)
,

F(x,n)=\begin{cases}|f_{n}(x)|&\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{N}\\[11.38092pt]
|f(x)|&\text{ if }n=\infty.\end{cases}

If
F
is continuous, then Lemma
6.13
entails that the function
G:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{[}0,+\infty)
, defined by

G(x):=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}F(x,n)=\max\{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(x)|,|f(x)|\}=\max\{S(x),|f(x)|\}

is continuous. Note that, for every
x\in\mathbb{K}
,
S(x)
is either equal to the limit of
|f_{n}(x)|
or it is a maximum, so that
S(x)\geq|f(x)|
. This implies that

, and that

is continuous.
It remains to prove that
F
is continuous. Consider a convergent sequence
\{(x_{k},n_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
, with
(x_{k},n_{k})\to(\bar{x},\bar{n})
. We treat two cases separately:

i)

if
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, then the sequence

is eventually constant with

. Hence, by continuity of

F(x_{k},n_{k})=f_{n_{k}}(x_{k})=f_{\bar{n}}(x_{k})\rightarrow f_{\bar{n}}(\bar{x})=F(\bar{x},\bar{n});

ii)

if
\bar{n}=\infty

B.2

Arguing as in Appendix

, one gets

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt=-3\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]^{4}+6\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]^{3}+6\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 2}\right]-12\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 2}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt-4\mathbb{E}[K_{n}]\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 3}\right]+6\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 3}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 4}\right].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 1}\right]

\displaystyle=n\cdot\lambda\,\Phi_{0}(n,\lambda)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 2}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{2}\cdot\lambda^{2}\,\left[\Phi_{0}^{2}(n,\lambda)+\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda)\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 3}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{3}\cdot\lambda^{3}\,\left[\Phi_{0}^{3}(n,\lambda)+3\Phi_{0}(n,\lambda)\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda)+\Phi_{2}(n,\lambda)\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 4}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\left.+3\Phi_{1}^{2}(n,\lambda)+\Phi_{3}(n,\lambda)\right]

where

\Phi_{i}(n,\lambda):=\psi^{(i)}\left(n(\lambda+1)\right)-\psi^{(i)}\left(n\lambda\right)

and
\psi^{(i)}
denotes the polygamma function
(
DLMF, 2024
, Section
(5.15)
)
. Making use of the asymptotic expansions of the polygamma functions for large argument

(
DLMF, 2024
, Equation
(5.11.2)
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(n,\lambda)

\displaystyle=L+\frac{1}{2\lambda(\lambda+1)n}+O\left(n^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda(\lambda+1)n}+O\left(n^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(n,\lambda)

\displaystyle=O\left(n^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(n,\lambda)

\displaystyle=O\left(n^{-3}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{n})_{\downarrow 1}\right]

\displaystyle=n\cdot\lambda L+\frac{1}{2(\lambda+1)}+O\left(n^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[K_{n}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\cdot\lambda^{2}L^{2}+n\cdot L\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}+O(1)

proving equation (

B.3
Proof of equation (
38
)

Preliminary note that if
s=e^{\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}(z)}}
with
\xi
satisfying (
35
), then
Petrov (1975, Chapter IV, Lemma 5)
entails

\left|s-1\right|=\left|e^{\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}(z)}}-1\right|\leq\left|\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}(z)}\right|\leq\mathcal{C}\,n^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, there exists
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
n\geq\bar{n}
,

\left|\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}\mathfrak{s}_{0,\lambda}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{3}.

From now on, we assume
n\geq\bar{n}
. Concerning the behaviour of
\mathfrak{R}^{(n)}_{1}
, rewrite
\mathfrak{R}^{(n)}_{1}(s)
as

\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{(n)}(s):=1+\mathfrak{R}(n(s\lambda+1))+\mathfrak{R}(n\lambda)-\mathfrak{R}(ns\lambda)-\mathfrak{R}(n(\lambda+1))+\mathfrak{E}(s),

where

\mathfrak{E}(s)=\mathfrak{R}(n(s\lambda+1))\mathfrak{R}(n\lambda)-\left[\mathfrak{R}(n(s\lambda+1))+\mathfrak{R}(n\lambda)\right]\,\varepsilon(s)+\frac{\varepsilon(s)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon(s)}

with

\varepsilon(s):=\mathfrak{R}(ns\lambda)+\mathfrak{R}(n(\lambda+1))+\mathfrak{R}(ns\lambda)\mathfrak{R}(n(\lambda+1)).

From (
36
), it holds

\left|\mathfrak{R}(w)\right|\leq\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}\,\left|w\right|}\leq\frac{3}{2\pi^{2}\,n\lambda}

for every
w\in\{n(s\lambda+1),ns\lambda,n(\lambda+1),n\lambda\}
, since
|w|\geq n\lambda
. This implies

|\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{(n)}(s)-1|\leq\frac{1}{n}\,\frac{6}{\pi^{2}\lambda}+|\mathfrak{E}(s)|\hskip 10.0pt\text{and }\hskip 10.0pt|\mathfrak{E}(s)|=O_{u.c.}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Thus,

\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{(n)}\left(s\right)=1+O_{u.c.}\left(n^{-1}\right).

Concerning the behaviour of
\mathfrak{R}^{(n)}_{2}
, rewrite
\mathfrak{R}^{(n)}_{2}
as

\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(n)}(s)=\left(1+\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(n)}(s)-1\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(1+\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right|\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k+1}\,\left[\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right]^{k}\leq\left|\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right|C_{\varepsilon}(s),

|\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}|\leq 1-\varepsilon

a continuous function only depending on
\varepsilon>0
. Since, for
\xi
satisfying (
35
),
|s\lambda+1|>\lambda
, there exists
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every

\left|\frac{s-1}{s\lambda+1}\right|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\lambda}n^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 1-\varepsilon

and

\left|\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(n)}(s)-1\right|\leq C_{\varepsilon}(s)\,\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\lambda}\,n^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}.

We conclude that

\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(n)}\left(s\right)=1+O_{u.c.}\left(n^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Concerning the behaviour of

Altogether, we proved that, for

with

satisfying (
35
) and for every

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{(n)}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=1+O_{u.c.}\left(n^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{(n)}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=1+O_{u.c.}\left(n^{\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{3}^{(n)}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=1+O_{u.c.}\left(n^{3\delta-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Recalling that

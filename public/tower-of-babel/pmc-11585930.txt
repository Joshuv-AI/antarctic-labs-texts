Page: investigating the predictors of general psychological help seeking intention among people who attempted suicide by using structural equation modeling
Authors: Alireza Jafari, Hadi Tehrani, Fatemehzahra Naddafi, Mahbobeh Nejatian, Mahdi Talebi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to determine the role of suicide literacy, suicide stigma, perceived social support, and attitudes toward seeking professional psychological help (ATSPPH) in predicting general help-seeking intention among individuals who have attempted suicide by structural equation modeling. MethodsThis cross-sectional study was conducted among 462 people who were referred to the hospital due to suicide attempt in one of the cities of eastern Iran in 2023. The sampling method in this study was consecutive sampling. The Pearson correlation, One-way ANOVA, and Independent-samples t-test were used to analyze data by SPSS software. Also, AMOS software was used for conducting structural equation modeling and checking the standardized direct effects and standardized indirect effects between variables.ResultsOnly 0.9% (n = 4) of participants answered correctly more than 17 questions regarding suicide literacy. The structural equation modeling (SEM) results showed that suicide literacy, suicide stigma, perceived social support, and ATSPPH predicted 51% variance in the general help-seeking intention (R2 = 0.51, PGFI = 0.607, PCFI = 0.568, RMSEA = 0.064). The variables of suicide literacy (estimate total effect = 0.615), ATSPPH (estimate total effect = 0.368), perceived social support (estimate total effect = 0.123), and suicide stigma (estimate total effect = 0.033) had the greatest impact in predicting the general help-seeking intention.ConclusionThe SEM results highlight the importance of paying more attention to suicide literacy, reducing suicide stigma, promoting social support, improving the positive attitude toward mental health services, encouraging people who have attempted suicide to seek psychological help, and finally preventing suicide attempt.Keywords: Suicide literacy, Attitude, Suicide stigma, Help seek, Intention

Background

Suicide is a serious challenge in the field of public health and is one of the important causes of death in the world; Suicide kills one person every 40 s and about 800,000 people die each year [1–3].Suicide occurs in all countries, but 77% of suicide rates are related to low- and middle -income countries (LMIC) [4]. Iran is one of the LMIC countries [5]. In Iran, suicide death rates have been increasing recently [6], and the results of a systematic review in 2023 indicated that suicidal behavior have increased in Iran [7]. Additionally, Iran has the highest number of suicide attempts among the Middle East countries [8].

Suicide as a multiple phenomenon is caused by the interaction of various cultural, demographic, and social factors [9, 10]. Suicide not only has a devastating impact on societies, families, and individuals of all ages, including children, adolescents, and adults, but also its psychological, social, and economic impact is significant in the world [1, 11, 12].

The American Psychological Association in 2018 introduced suicide prevention as a priority in public health [13]. Although seeking appropriate and reliable professional psychological help by people with behaviors or suicidal thoughts can prevent suicide attempt, but, seeking professional psychological help is only seen in a small percentage of suicide behavior or thoughts [14]and 68% of those who have committed suicide had no contact with mental health services in the year before suicide [15]. In Iran, evidence also showed the low rate of seeking professional psychological help among individuals with suicidal thoughts [6]. Various factors, including high suicide literacy, low suicide stigma, high perceived social support, and positive attitudes can effectively help individuals seeking professional psychological help [16–18].

Suicide literacy refers to understanding the suicide risk factors, symptoms of warning, stimuli, causes, prevention, and treatment [18]. Promoting suicide literacy is a key strategy for preventing suicide and has a positive impact on attitude and intention toward seeking professional psychological help [18, 19]. A study on Lithuanian students showed that those with the most positive attitudes toward seeking professional psychological help (ATSPPH) had the highest level of suicide literacy [19].

Another predictor of ATSPPH and general psychological help seeking intention is suicide stigma [20]. Suicide stigma is a social process characterized by negative attitudes toward suicide-committed [16]. The World Health Organization has mentioned that suicide stigma is one of the main obstacles to suicide prevention [21]. Suicide stigma is associated with increased risk of suicide, reduced seeking professional psychological help, and reduced intention to participate in suicide prevention programs [16]. The results of a study of Iranian women who have committed suicide in the past showed that the fear of stigma has led them to hide their suicide attempt from others [22]. A study in Japan showed that more positive ATSPPH was related to low suicide stigma [23]. Overall, studies have shown that the decline in suicide stigma and promotion of suicide literacy are associated with improved attitudes toward seeking professional mental health services [15, 24].

Another factor that predicts the desire to seeking professional mental health services is perceived social support [25]. Perceived social support is also referred to the mental perception of the functional and general support provided by social network, family, friends, and colleagues [26, 27]. More perceived social support related to positive attitudes toward seeking professional mental health services [28]. A study in Turkey found that perceived social support from friends increased people’s desire to seeking professional mental health services [29].

Evaluating these factors as much as possible can lead to the removal of obstacles, the implementation of more targeted preventive interventions, and more comprehensive policies. Overall, numerous studies have investigated suicide literacy, perceived social support, suicide stigma, ATSPPH. However, to date, no study examined these variables simultaneously and their impacts on general help-seeking intention among people who have attempted suicide. Therefore, this study was performed with the purpose of assessing the standardized direct and indirect effects of suicide literacy, ATSPPH, suicide stigma, and perceived social support on predicting general help-seeking intention among individuals who have attempted suicide.

Method

This cross-sectional study aimed to determine the role of suicide literacy, suicide stigma, perceived social support, and ATSPPH in predicting general help-seeking intention among suicide attempters by structural equation modeling in Mashhad, one of the cities of eastern Iran in 2023.

Sample size

In conducting SEM, Kim et al. [30] recommended that sample sizes below 100, 150, and above 200 be considered small, medium and large. Therefore, in order to reduce sampling error and more stability, statistical estimates of 200 or more samples are recommended, therefore, 462 participants were included in this study.

Sampling method

One of the hospitals of Mashhad city was chosen as the sampling setting. Then, by adopting the consecutive sampling method, all people who referred to this hospital due to suicide were included in the study and require samples were gathered in six months. First, a psychiatrist explained the research process to the participants. After obtaining written inform consent, the questionnaire was completed in an interview manner by psychiatrist. The criteria for entry into the study included refer to hospital for suicide attempt and interest in voluntary participation in the study.

Data collection instruments

In this study, six questionnaires of demographic section, literacy of suicide scale (LOSS), ATSPPH scale, stigma of suicide scale (SOSS), general help-seeking questionnaire (GHSQ), and multidimensional scale of perceived social support were used.

Demographic section

In this section the information such as sex, economic status, inhabitant, occupation, education level, marital status, age, number of suicide attempt, refer to the health professional for psychological mental disorders, a history of your mental disorders, suicide attempted in family members, get information related to mental disorders were obtained.

LOSS

LOSS consists of 26 questions and 4 subscales of signs/ symptoms, treatment/prevention, causes/triggers, and risk factors of suicide [18]. The validity and reliability of the Persian version of LOSS was assessed by Jafari et al. [31]. In the Persian version of LOSS, one question was deleted, and the final tool has 25 question and 4 subscales (signs/ symptoms with 5 items, causes/triggers with 9 items, risk factors with 7 items, and treatment/prevention with 4 items). Each item of LOSS is measured using True, I don’t know, and False. For each correct answer, a score of 1 was assigned and for the wrong answer and I don’t know answer the score of zero was assigned. The Persian LOSS scores range is zero to 25. higher LOSS scores indicate higher suicide literacy [31]. The Cronbach’s alpha of the Persian version of LOSS was also reported as 0.859, and LOSS was confirmed in CFA with acceptable goodness of fit indexes [31].

ATSPPH scale

This tool is a short form of 10 questions designed and evaluated by Fischer and Farina and measure attitudes of people toward seeking professional psychological help [32]. This short form was extracted from long form of scale with 29 questions and each question is measured using the Likert 4-option scale (disagree, somewhat disagree, somewhat agree, agree) [32]. The scores range of ATSPPH is 10 to 40 and higher ATSPPH scores indicate more positive attitude [32]. Cronbach’s alpha for this scale was checked by Sharifi et al., in Iranian society and reported as 0.84 [33].

SOSS

SOSS consists of 16 questions and 3 subscales (Isolation/ depression with 4 items, Glorification/ normalization with 4 items, and Stigma with 8 items) [34, 35]. Each SOSS item is measured on a five -point Likert scale (strongly disagree to strongly agree) [34, 35]. The scores range of SOSS is 16 to 80 and higher SOSS scores indicate positive attitude toward stigma of suicide [34, 35]. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of SOSS was 0.70 [34, 35]. The validity and reliability of SOSS were evaluated in Iranian people and Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was 0.79 [36].

GHSQ

This tool was designed and surveyed by Wilson et al., and measured general help-seeking intention of people [37]. This tool consists of two general questions: (1) “If you have a personal or emotional problem, how much is it possible for you to get help from the following people?” (2) “If you were experiencing suicidal thoughts, how likely is it that you would seek help from the following people?”. People respond to each item with a 7 -point Likert scale (extremely unlikely to extremely likely) for each help source (Help sources: Friend, Intimate partner, Parent, Other relative/family member, Phone helpline, Mental health professional, Minister or religious leader, I would not seek help from anyone, Doctor, I would seek help from another not listed above). The higher GHSQ scores indicate higher general help-seeking intention [37]. Cronbach’s alpha for this tool has been reported in Wilson et al., study of 0.85, and the test-retest result was 0.92 [37]. The validity and reliability of the Persian version of the GHSQ was evaluated by Inanlou et al., among Iranians, and Cronbach’s alpha was reported to be 0.70 [38].

Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (MSPSS)

This questionnaire was designed by Mitchell et al. [39]. This tool contains 12 questions that measure the perceived support of individuals in three factors: family support (with 4 items), support of important people in life (with 4 items), and friends’ support (with 4 items) [39]. The scores range of MSPSS is 12 to 84 and higher MSPSS scores indicate more perceived social support [39]. In Iran, Bagherian et al., assessed the validity and reliability of this scale, and Cronbach’s alpha was reported in the healthy population and patients as 0.92 and 0.84, respectively [40].

Statistical analysis

The data were assessed with software of SPSS (version 24) at a significant level < 0.05. At first data normality was evaluated by Kolmogorov Smirnov test and data were normal, So, Pearson’s correlation was used to compare two quantitative variables. Chi-square test was used to compare two qualitative variables. ANOVA was used to compare three categorical or more variables with a quantitative variable. To compare two- categorical qualitative variables with quantitative variables, the Independent-samples t-test was used. Structural equation modeling (SEM) was evaluated using AMOS software (version 24) to determine the direct and indirect paths between variables. First, outlier data were checked using Mahalanobis, and then data normality was checked using skewness and kurtosis and data were normal. Also, no missing data were found, because the questionnaires were completed by a psychiatrist using the interview method. Only 4 questionnaires were deleted in analysis section due the outlier data. In the SEM, the role of suicide literacy, suicide stigma, perceived social support, and ATSPPH in predicting general help-seeking intention among individuals who have attempted suicide was evaluated. To confirm the final model, the goodness-of-fit indexes were x2/df (Chi-square ratio to the degree of freedom < 5), RMSEA (Root means the square error of approximation < 0.08), PNFI (Parsimonious normed fit index > 0.5), PGFI (Parsimony goodness-of-fit index > 0.5) [41, 42].

Results

Most of the suicide attempters were was female (n = 341, 75.6%), under the age of 20 years old (n = 205, 45.2%), had secondary education (n = 134, 29.5%), housewives (n = 185, 41%), urban residents (n = 345, 77.9%), and had weak economic status levels (n = 212, 46.7%). Most of the participants (n = 260, 57%) were in their first suicide attempt. Only 7.4% (n = 30) reported that there was a suicide attempted in their family members. Furthermore, 30.5% (n = 138) had been referred to health professionals for mental problems, and only 24.5% (n = 33) evaluated the visit as useful (Table 1). The important source for getting the health information (87.6%) and mental health information (91.4%) was Internet (Figs. 1 and 2). Most respondents said they had been referred to a consultant (72.7%) and physician (67.9%) for their mental problems (Fig. 3).

Discussion

This study aimed to determine the role of suicide literacy, suicide stigma, perceived social support, and ATSPPH in predicting general help-seeking intention among individuals who have attempted suicide. In general, the variables of suicide literacy, perceived social support, suicide stigma, and ATSPPH predicted 51% variance of the GHSQ. This result shows that people with higher suicide literacy levels, lower suicide stigma levels, more social support, and more positive ATSPPH had more GHSQ.

Al-Shannaq and Aldalaykeh in a study aimed at examining suicide stigma, suicide literacy, and ATSPPH in young people, showed that people with low suicide literacy were more likely to experience suicide stigma and had more negative ATSPPH [24]. Furthermore, Wrigley et al., showed that high health literacy and low suicide stigma are linked to greater seeking professional psychological help [43].

According to the SEM results, ATSPPH had a direct and positive relationship with GHSQ, and as the attitude becomes more positive, the score of GHSQ increased. In general, this result showed that a positive attitude toward professional psychological help is related to higher intentions in seeking such help. This result demonstrates the importance of promoting positive ATSPPH for mental health problems, including suicide. The desire to seek professional psychological help and to feel comfortable talking to a specialist regarding mental health issues can predict professional psychological help and use of mental health treatments [44]. In a study aimed at examining the attitude and stigma to seeking professional psychological help among Saudi adults, results showed that positive ATSPPH was a strong predictor of help seeking intentions for mental health services [45]. Another study in China also showed that positive ATSPPH can increase help-seeking intentions [17].

The results also revealed that suicide literacy had a direct and positive relationship with intentions of seeking psychological help, and as suicide literacy increased, intentions of seeking psychological help increased. The findings of this study demonstrate the importance of promoting public awareness and accurate knowledge of suicide prevention to enhance professional psychological help and suicide prevention. Suicide literacy can help individuals identify suicide warning signs and seek professional psychological help when needed. Individuals with low suicide literacy levels are more likely to experience more suicide stigma and more negative ATSPPH [46]. Results in a Lithuanian study showed that there was a positive correlation between suicide literacy and seeking professional psychological help [19]. In another study in Japan, low suicide literacy was found to be one of the key obstacles to the Japanese adult for seeking professional psychological intentions [23].

The results of social support indicated that this variable is indirectly associated with intentions to seek psychological help, and through its impact on ATSPPH, increases intentions to seek psychological help. High social support can improve the attitude toward help seeking, which can lead to increased intentions of seeking psychological help. Poor social support is not a significant predictor of suicide help seeking [47]. Chu examined social support and the risk of suicide attempt among Chinese students, and the findings showed that high social support can encourage people to seek help if needed, whereas poor social support can be a barrier to seeking psychological help [48]. Additionally, a study found that people who have high social support have a positive attitude toward seeking psychological help [17].

The results of the analysis in this study revealed that suicide literacy had a direct and negative effect on suicide stigma, and with increased suicide literacy, the level of perceived stigma decreased. This result demonstrates that improving suicide literacy can effectively reduce suicide stigma. The results of several studies have reported that suicide literacy was associated with reduced suicide stigma [24, 49, 50].

This research has some limitations and strengths. The questionnaires were completed with the help of a psychiatrist using interview method and may have minimized measuring bias. Given this research was a cross-sectional study, a weakness of this study was that only the relationships between the variables were measurable. Typically, in path analysis studies, study design, data collection, and variable evaluation are based on a previous strong theory or model. One of the limitations of our study was the lack of an underlying theory. As a result, after the initial analysis of the data, several models were tested, and the best model with the appropriate indicators was presented inductively. Therefore, future researchers should attempt to replicate the model reported here.

Conclusion

The findings of this study highlight the importance of paying attention to improve suicide literacy, reducing suicide stigma, improve social support, and improve positive ATSPPH to encourage suicide attempters to seek professional psychological help and ultimately decrease suicide attempt or suicide. Given the greater impact of suicide literacy on suicide intention, educational programs need to be designed and implemented through mass media, social networks, and so on for community members. Furthermore, designing training programs, implementing awareness campaigns, and conducting interventions to increase suicide literacy, reduce stigma, improve social support, increase positive ATSPPH, and increase intentions of seeking psychological help can be effective in preventing suicide attempts or suicide.

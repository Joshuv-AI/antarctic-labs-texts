Selective entropy-fused proximal policy optimisation with federated reinforcement learning for intelligent multi-UAV trajectory and communication optimisation
Authors: M Velmurugan, T Velmurugan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid evolution of 5G and emerging 6G networks has increased the demand for wireless communication systems that deliver high capacity, low latency, and adaptability. However, conventional terrestrial infrastructure remains costly and inflexible, particularly in dynamic or remote environments. This article develops a new Federated Reinforcement Learning (FRL)-based UAV communication system using Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimization (SEF-PPO) is proposed to enhance the performance of locally-on-policy learning in real-time decision-making environments. In contrast to existing digital twin or offline-trained deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods, the proposed solution eliminates the need for replay buffers, thereby reducing memory and computational requirements for UAV platforms. UAVs learn collaboratively while preserving data privacy and maintaining robustness to non-IID user distributions through federated aggregation with a High-Altitude Platform (HAP). The framework integrates trajectory planning, user association, energy-efficient resource allocation, and handover management within a unified adaptive architecture. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in throughput, fairness, latency, and energy efficiency compared with baseline methods, including DMTD, DRL-EC3, and greedy and random algorithms. Introduction

The rapid development of wireless communication technologies, including fifth-generation (5G) and emerging sixth-generation (6G) networks, has significantly increased the demand for ubiquitous, high-capacity, and low-latency wireless services. Nevertheless, the deployment and maintenance of terrestrial infrastructure remain costly and inflexible, particularly in challenging terrains, temporary event settings, and disaster-affected areas. To address these limitations, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have emerged as a promising solution for augmenting ground-based networks through their use as aerial base stations (UAV-BSs) or relay nodes This approach enables improved coverage, increased throughput, and rapid network recovery1,2.

In contrast to fixed ground base stations, UAVs offer high mobility, cost-effectiveness, and the ability to establish reliable line-of-sight (LoS) links with user equipment (UEs)3. These characteristics make UAV-enabled communication networks particularly effective in dynamic scenarios such as emergency response operations, rural broadband deployment, and high-density urban environments4,5. By dynamically adjusting their three-dimensional (3D) positions, UAVs can respond to fluctuating user demands, balance traffic loads, and enhance overall quality of service (QoS).

Despite these advantages, the highly dynamic and distributed nature of UAV networks introduces significant challenges. In particular, trajectory planning, energy-efficient communication, user association, and interference management must be jointly optimised across multiple UAVs operating within shared airspace6. Classical optimisation techniques, such as convex programming, struggle to address these non-stationary, high-dimensional problems in real time. Previous studies have applied a range of optimisation approaches, including convex and non-convex programming, mixed-integer linear programming (MILP), and Lagrangian relaxation, to jointly optimise power control and UAV trajectories7. Heuristic and metaheuristic methods, such as particle swarm optimisation (PSO), genetic algorithms (GA), and ant colony optimisation (ACO), have also been employed for load balancing and energy-efficient route planning in aerial networks8,9. Game-theoretic and Stackelberg models have further been explored to manage resource competition and enable cooperative spectrum sharing10. However, these conventional methods are generally limited by high computational complexity, slow convergence, and poor adaptability to dynamic or atypical scenarios. To address these limitations, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has recently emerged as a promising approach for autonomous UAV control, enabling agents to learn environment-dependent policies through interaction with their surroundings11.

However, current DRL-based UAV communication systems still exhibit significant limitations when deployed in real-world environments. Most existing approaches rely on offline training or digital twin simulations, which restrict adaptability and responsiveness in non-stationary conditions. In addition, conventional reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g. DQN and DDPG) require large replay buffers and substantial computational resources, rendering them impractical for energy- and memory-constrained UAV platforms. Furthermore, the presence of non-IID data across regions, arising from heterogeneous and non-independent user behaviour, complicates centralised training. At the same time, swarm-level coordination remains an open challenge due to limited communication bandwidth and privacy constraints12,13.

In recent years, advances in large-scale intelligent models and cross-domain learning frameworks have been proposed to address complex, multimodal, and distributed decision-making problems. Vision–language–action models aligned with cognitive processes, such as CogVLA14, and semantic sparsification approaches, such as SemanticVLA15, highlight the importance of efficient representation learning and adaptive model routing in large-scale systems. Similarly, hyperscale multi-sensor environments leveraging large-model-driven data fusion have been explored, alongside the development of self-adaptive and self-healing mechanisms in distributed physical systems16,17. These developments underscore the importance of scalable, adaptable, and communication-efficient learning paradigms, thereby motivating the proposed federated and entropy-aware UAV learning architecture.

To address these challenges, this article introduces a federated reinforcement learning (FRL) framework combined with a Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimisation (SEF-PPO) algorithm for real-time UAV-enabled wireless communication systems. In contrast to traditional digital twin or offline reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, the proposed method enables UAVs to learn directly from real-world interactions in a local, on-policy manner, without requiring replay buffers or pretraining. Through federated aggregation at a high-altitude platform (HAP), UAVs collaboratively optimise their policies while preserving data locality and ensuring user privacy.

Motivation

Enhancing fairness and performance in dynamic wireless networks requires the joint optimisation of UAV trajectories and personalised resource allocation. By integrating entropy into proximal policy optimisation, UAVs can adaptively reposition to strengthen line-of-sight links, thereby improving both system throughput and fairness. Personalised resource allocation further supports fairness by weighting users according to demand and channel conditions, reducing service imbalance. Together, these strategies enable scalable, energy-efficient connectivity aligned with emerging AI-driven approaches in 6G and beyond.

Literature review

With the rapid deployment of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in next-generation wireless networks, considerable research attention has been devoted to optimising trajectory planning, resource allocation, and communication reliability using machine learning (ML) and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Early optimisation-based approaches relied on convex models and heuristic methods to maximise coverage and energy efficiency.

Liu et al.18 proposed a deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based energy-efficient control framework for coverage and connectivity (DRL-EC3). Their approach employs two deep neural networks to model environmental dynamics and optimise an energy efficiency objective that accounts for communication distance, fairness, energy consumption, and connectivity. Similarly, Zhan et al.19 investigated UAV-assisted communication in a three-dimensional (3D) environment, where multiple UAVs serve multiple users. They proposed a multi-agent proximal policy optimisation (MAPPO)-based RL method for online UAV control, demonstrating improved cumulative rewards and faster convergence through adaptive trajectory and speed control.

Extending this line of work, He et al.20 introduced a distributed 3D trajectory design framework to maximise system performance across multiple UAVs. Their DRL-based approach, termed DMTD, enables UAVs to explore optimal flight altitudes and coverage regions through iterative interaction with the environment. Ji et al.21 developed a UAV-enabled multicast system in which a UAV acts as a mobile transmitter, supported by a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) to enhance service quality under power constraints. They formulated a joint optimisation problem involving UAV trajectory, RIS configuration, and beamforming, and proposed a multi-pass deep Q-network (BT-MP-DQN) algorithm. Simulation results demonstrated improved achievable rates and effective multicast performance, further enhanced by RIS deployment.

Gong et al.22 applied heuristic optimisation to balance energy consumption and data queue sizes in multi-UAV systems, incorporating adaptive network construction. Bayesian optimisation was used to improve learning efficiency and convergence by leveraging historical trajectory data. Gao et al.23 proposed a multi-agent federated reinforcement learning (MAFRL) framework for data collection from mobile ground devices (GDs), jointly optimising UAV trajectories and communication scheduling. Their model incorporates multi-step propagation and a duelling network architecture, resulting in improved convergence speed and stability.

Song et al.24 introduced personalised federated deep reinforcement learning (PF-DRL) for multi-UAV trajectory optimisation in complex environments, proposing a PF-MADDPG-based method that minimises both total energy consumption and individual UAV flight costs. Yang et al.25 developed a collaborative trajectory optimisation approach for high-density user scenarios, integrating graph neural networks (GNNs) with proximal policy optimisation (PPO). Their reward design captures path efficiency, energy consumption, collision avoidance, and cooperative behaviour among UAVs.

Despite these advances, many existing approaches rely on periodic global synchronisation or static aggregation, which can limit real-time responsiveness in highly dynamic environments. To improve policy stability and exploration efficiency, entropy-based DRL methods have gained increasing attention. Su et al.26 proposed a lightweight entropy-integrated DRL framework for fixed-wing UAV navigation, incorporating high-quality replay buffer samples into gradient updates to guide policy optimisation. This approach introduces a soft imitation mechanism, while an adaptive entropy regularisation term balances exploration and exploitation.

More recent studies by Betalo et al.27–30 have explored dynamic coverage control using RIS, AI-driven task allocation in space–air–ground integrated networks (SAGIN), and data-driven optimisation strategies involving dynamic recharging and energy harvesting. These works reflect a growing focus on intelligent, energy-aware, and cooperative UAV systems.

Lv et al.31 proposed an information-theoretic exploration strategy, Entropy Explorer (EE), which generates intrinsic rewards based on state and action entropy to address sparse reward challenges in UAV path planning. Combined with the twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) algorithm, this approach improves sampling efficiency and policy stability. Evaluation in AirSim demonstrated superior performance compared with benchmark methods, highlighting the effectiveness of entropy-guided optimisation for real-time UAV learning models such as SEF-PPO.

Several recent studies have incorporated digital twin (DT) models to emulate real-world environments and accelerate UAV policy training. Zhao et al.32 combined DT with an incremental and distributed update (IDU) mechanism to optimise UAV trajectory and energy consumption, while a self-adaptive trajectory decision (STD) scheme generates candidate trajectories based on predicted conditions. Training is performed using duelling DQN with prioritised experience replay (PER). Shen et al.33 proposed DNQMIX, a multi-agent DRL framework for cooperative target search, supported by a DT-driven training architecture combining centralised training, decentralised execution, and continuous evolution (CTDECE). However, reliance on DT introduces scalability challenges due to modelling complexity, latency, and the need for high-fidelity environmental replication. Zhang et al.34 proposed a partially observable weighted mean field DDPG (PO-WMFDDPG) algorithm to address large-scale UAV swarm coordination, achieving improved success rates and convergence speed compared with baseline methods.

Wang et al.35 explored conventional optimisation techniques, including convex optimisation and block coordinate descent (BCD), alongside a DRL-based real-time trajectory control algorithm (RAT) enhanced by prioritised experience replay. Huang et al.36 introduced a representation enhancement PPO (RE-PPO) framework, incorporating observation memory improvement (OMI) and dynamic relative position–attitude reshaping (DRPAR) to improve feature extraction and reduce collisions under partial observability. Experimental results in 3D environments demonstrated faster convergence, higher success rates, and fewer collisions.

He et al.37 examined task offloading in multi-UAV systems, where ground devices select target UAVs for computation. Analytical methods were used to ensure fairness in UAV selection and task allocation, while a MADDPG framework optimised UAV trajectory. Wang et al.38 proposed a duelling double deep Q-network (D3QN)-based path planning method capable of adapting to varying IoT node distributions, data volumes, and UAV configurations.

Overall, the literature indicates that DRL has become a foundational approach for UAV trajectory optimisation, user association, and energy management. However, several key research gaps remain. Many DRL and DT-based models depend heavily on offline training or simulated environments, limiting adaptability to real-time dynamics. Federated and multi-agent approaches continue to face challenges related to communication overhead and privacy, particularly under non-IID data distributions. Fully decentralised swarm coordination remains underexplored, with most models relying on centralised aggregation or fixed synchronisation intervals. Furthermore, critical tasks such as trajectory planning, handover management, energy optimisation, and bandwidth allocation are often addressed in isolation rather than within a unified framework.

Motivated by these limitations, this study proposes a Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimisation (SEF-PPO) algorithm integrated with a federated reinforcement learning (FRL) framework to enable real-time, on-policy, and distributed UAV learning.

Proposed system model

The proposed system presents a federated reinforcement learning (FRL)-based communication and control framework for a multi-UAV aerial network operating in dynamic user environments. The architecture (Fig. 1) comprises multiple low-altitude UAVs that collaboratively provide real-time coverage to widely distributed mobile user equipment (UE) across a large geographic area. Each UAV functions as an autonomous agent employing the Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimisation (SEF-PPO) algorithm, enabling it to learn optimal flight trajectories, user association strategies, and resource allocation policies through continuous interaction with the environment. A high-altitude platform (HAP) serves as the federated coordination server, aggregating model parameters from distributed UAVs while preserving data privacy and locality. UAVs establish air-to-ground communication links with UEs and transmit encrypted model updates to the HAP via air-to-air backhaul channels. The system adopts a time-division multiple access (TDMA) protocol to ensure collision-free communication between UAVs and UEs. The overall objective is to maximise network coverage, fairness, and capacity, while minimising delay, interference, and energy consumption through an adaptive, on-policy FRL framework.

As a result, each UAV agent independently adapts its policy to changing airspace conditions and user mobility patterns, enabling robust, energy-efficient operation and seamless handover management.

The SEF mechanism constitutes a lightweight extension to PPO, introducing minimal computational overhead while enabling adaptive entropy regulation based on direct reward feedback. In contrast to fixed-entropy PPO variants, SEF continuously monitors environmental stability through reward variance and automatically adjusts the level of exploration. This mitigates premature convergence and supports stable policy updates in federated, non-IID UAV environments. SEF-PPO is conceptually inspired by entropy–reward coupling mechanisms explored in recent reinforcement learning literature. For example, Dong et al.39 introduced the Adaptive Joint Entropy Reward (AJER), which provides effective guidance for agents and enhances decision-making in sparse-reward environments. Wu et al.40 analysed policy optimisation stability and convergence under entropy-driven dynamics, while Choe and Kim41 developed maximum-entropy actor–critic methods incorporating entropy–advantage estimation. Building on these insights, SEF-PPO introduces a dynamic entropy-weighted reward formulation that supports sample-efficient, on-policy learning and robust convergence in multi-UAV federated settings, without requiring replay buffers or offline pretraining. Global entropy weighted aggregation

In the proposed Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimisation (SEF-PPO) framework, all UAVs participate in a federated reinforcement learning (FRL) paradigm to enable scalable, privacy-preserving, and decentralised training. Rather than sharing raw trajectory or user data, each UAV agent locally optimises its SEF-PPO policy using its own experience—such as signal quality, energy state, and user density—and periodically transmits only encrypted model parameters to the high-altitude platform (HAP), which acts as the federated aggregation server. This weighting scheme does not restrict local exploration, as entropy modulation is preserved at the UAV level through the SEF mechanism (Eqs. 32–34). Instead, it mitigates the impact of high-variance local updates on the global model, thereby improving convergence stability under heterogeneous (non-IID) conditions.

Policy uncertainty and gradient variance are reflected in the entropy coefficient . Higher-entropy policies produce more stochastic action distributions, which can lead to high-variance gradient updates. Accordingly, Eq. (48) can be interpreted as an inverse-uncertainty weighting scheme, , which reduces the influence of high-variance updates during aggregation while preserving their contribution. The aggregation rule in Eq. (47) extends standard FedAvg by incorporating entropy-aware weighting (Eq. (48)), thereby assigning lower weights to UAVs exhibiting higher exploration and promoting stable global convergence.

This weighting strategy does not restrict agent-level exploration. Individual UAVs continue to explore using locally adapted entropy coefficients (Eqs. 32–34). Instead, aggregation acts as a moderating mechanism, limiting the global impact of highly exploratory agents whose updates may be noisy or unstable. This design is inspired by variance-reduction principles in distributed optimisation, where stabilising global updates is critical under heterogeneous (non-IID) data distributions. In this context, client contributions are inversely proportional to update uncertainty, improving convergence stability while preserving local exploration.

The proposed federated architecture offers several advantages, including privacy preservation (as no raw environmental or user data are shared), scalability (through independent and asynchronous UAV training), and enhanced swarm coordination (via shared model parameters that promote global situational awareness, reduce redundant trajectories, and improve coverage efficiency). SEF-PPO adopts a locally on-policy training paradigm rather than a globally synchronised on-policy approach. Each UAV collects trajectories based on its current policy and performs PPO updates locally before federated aggregation. The aggregation step is applied to updated model parameters rather than raw experience samples, thereby avoiding the mixing of off-policy data. Although parameter aggregation introduces slight policy heterogeneity across agents, this effect is mitigated through periodic synchronisation and entropy-driven updates, enabling convergence while retaining the practical benefits of on-policy learning. Conclusion

This study proposes a novel federated reinforcement learning (FRL)-based UAV networking framework enhanced by a Selective Entropy-Fused Proximal Policy Optimisation (SEF-PPO) algorithm for real-time, adaptive, and energy-efficient wireless communication. The proposed system addresses the limitations of conventional digital twin and offline reinforcement learning approaches by enabling on-policy learning without reliance on replay buffers or pretraining. Within a federated framework, UAVs collaboratively optimise their policies through aggregation at a high-altitude platform (HAP), ensuring scalability while preserving user data privacy. The model jointly addresses key network challenges, including trajectory optimisation, user association, energy-efficient resource allocation, handover management, and swarm coordination under heterogeneous conditions. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in throughput, fairness, latency, and energy efficiency compared with baseline methods such as DMTD, DRL-EC3, and conventional greedy and random strategies. The SEF-PPO mechanism facilitates stable convergence and efficient resource utilisation, enabling UAVs to adapt autonomously to non-stationary user demands and dynamic interference conditions.

Despite these advantages, the framework presents certain limitations, including communication overhead associated with federated aggregation and the use of simplified air-to-ground channel models in simulation. Future work will focus on systematically evaluating the impact of non-IID data heterogeneity by varying user mobility distributions and analysing its effects on convergence stability, policy divergence, and overall network performance. Additional research directions include the integration of communication-efficient techniques, such as model compression and parameter sparsification, to reduce aggregation overhead, and the extension of the framework to multi-tier aerial–terrestrial hybrid networks. Further development will also explore the incorporation of edge intelligence, adaptive model pruning, and real-world UAV deployment experiments to validate scalability and practical applicability in large-scale 6G communication systems. Overall, this work establishes a robust, decentralised, and learning-driven paradigm for next-generation UAV-enabled wireless networks.

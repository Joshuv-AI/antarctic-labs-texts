Balloon-expandable trilayer nitrided iron/Zn/poly-D,L-lactic acid metallic tracheal stent: structural characterization, mechanical performance, and short-term safety evaluation in rabbits
Authors: Hao Wang, Qiqi Hu, Wenxin Cao, Zihao Bai, Wanxuan Song, Xingtong Chen, Di Yu, Zhaocong Yang, Huilin Li, Chen Chen, Yuzhong Yang, Xuming Mo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPediatric airway stenosis and tracheomalacia may require airway stenting to maintain patency. However, conventional metallic or silicone airway stents are associated with frequent complications such as granulation, mucus plugging, and migration. Although bioresorbable polymer stents reduce long-term indwelling, degradation fragments may still pose a risk of airway obstruction. Therefore, a tracheal stent designed to provide reliable early mechanical support, favorable tissue compatibility, and potential long-term bioresorption remains needed. This study aims to develop and evaluate a biodegradable tracheal stent with improved mechanical performance and biocompatibility.MethodsA balloon-expandable trilayer tracheal stent was fabricated with a load-bearing nitrided iron (Fe-N) framework, a zinc (Zn) buffering layer, and an outer poly-D,L-lactic acid (PDLLA) coating. The layered structure and composition were verified by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR). Radial compression testing was performed and compared with a pure iron stent of the same geometry. Stent extracts were prepared to evaluate in vitro biocompatibility using BEAS-2B cells, including scratch assay, 5-ethynyl-2'-deoxyuridine (EdU) assay, Transwell assay, terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL), cytoskeleton staining, and live/dead staining. Immunological safety was assessed using RAW264.7 macrophages, with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) as a positive control, by immunofluorescence for cluster of differentiation 86 (CD86), interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) and by reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) for related messenger RNAs (mRNAs). For in vivo evaluation, the stent was delivered and deployed under flexible bronchoscopy in male New Zealand White rabbits. Tissues were collected at 1 week, 1 month, and 2 months for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson staining of the trachea, and histology of the lungs and major organs (heart, liver, spleen, and kidney).ResultsCharacterization confirmed successful construction of the designed trilayer structure. Radial compression testing showed that the Fe-N stent provided higher radial support than the pure iron stent with the same geometry. In vitro, extracts showed no obvious cytotoxic or pro-inflammatory effects. The extracts also did not induce inflammatory activation in RAW264.7 macrophages, whereas the LPS group showed the expected inflammatory response. In rabbits, the stent could be delivered and deployed by bronchoscopy with balloon expansion. Histology showed early mucosal injury followed by progressive repair, with no obvious progressive granulation, persistent fibrotic remodeling, distal lung injury, or major-organ damage.ConclusionsThe balloon-expandable trilayer Fe-N/Zn/PDLLA metallic tracheal stent demonstrated feasible delivery and deployment in rabbits, provided the mechanical basis required for airway support, and showed favorable in vitro and short-term in vivo biosafety with a repair-dominant tissue response. These findings support further optimization and long-term evaluation of this trilayer metallic airway stent design, particularly regarding its degradation behavior, durability of radial support, and long-term biosafety.Keywords: Tracheal stent, balloon expansion, trilayer structure, nitrided iron (Fe-N), zinc buffering layer (Zn buffering layer)

Key findings

• We designed a nitrided iron (Fe-N) tracheal stent and evaluated its mechanical performance, in vitro biocompatibility, and in vivo response in a rabbit model.

• The stent provided stable support and showed acceptable tissue compatibility without obvious progressive granulation or severe stenosis during follow-up.

What is known and what is new?

• Conventional tracheal stents may be associated with long-term complications, and metallic stents are being explored as alternatives.

• This study systematically characterized a Fe-N stent from structure and mechanics to cellular responses and rabbit implantation outcomes, providing integrated evidence for feasibility in airway support.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The findings support further optimization and validation of Fe-N tracheal stents, with emphasis on standardized extract preparation, quantitative histological scoring, and longer-term safety assessment before clinical translation.

Introduction

Pediatric airway disorders such as tracheomalacia, congenital or acquired tracheal stenosis, and postoperative airway collapse can lead to persistent dyspnea, recurrent infections, and prolonged ventilatory dependence. Clinically, airway stents are often required to rapidly restore and maintain airway patency during critical periods as a bridging therapy (1,2). However, pediatric airways have small diameters and an active mucociliary clearance system. As a result, long-term tolerance to foreign bodies is limited, and stent-related complications can directly affect outcomes (3).

Silicone or metallic airway stents are widely used and can improve ventilation in the short term, but complications such as granulation tissue hyperplasia, mucus plugging/retention, migration, infection, fracture, and removal difficulty have been repeatedly reported in studies and reviews (4-6). In children, metallic stents may further complicate follow-up because airway growth and long-term indwelling can increase the risk of secondary injury and repeated interventions (7).

To avoid permanent implantation and secondary trauma from stent removal, bioresorbable polymer airway stents—typically polydioxanone (PDO/PDS)—have been explored in pediatric airway diseases and have accumulated clinical experience, suggesting a potential alternative to conventional metallic or silicone stents (1,8,9). Nevertheless, evidence also indicates that polymer stents may produce sizable fragments during degradation, which can cause airway obstruction, especially in small pediatric airways (10,11).

Bioresorbable metals provide another materials route to address these challenges. Iron-based bioresorbable stents have good load-bearing potential, but their degradation kinetics and early mechanical retention require precise control. In vascular stent research, iron-based bioresorbable stents have been validated in animal studies for short-term safety and efficacy, supporting the biocompatibility of the iron platform (12). Furthermore, nitriding modification can enhance the mechanical properties of iron-based structures, enabling thinner struts and improved radial support while influencing corrosion behavior (13-15).

Based on these clinical needs and the materials evidence, we designed and fabricated a layered bioresorbable nitrided-iron tracheal stent. A nitrided iron (Fe-N) mesh with ultrathin struts serves as the load-bearing backbone; an outer poly-D,L-lactic acid (PDLLA) coating is used to modulate the later-stage degradation behavior; and a zinc (Zn) buffering interlayer is introduced to delay corrosion initiation and maintain effective radial support in the early implantation period (target ~2–3 months). A rabbit tracheal model was selected because it is suitable for bronchoscopy-guided stent implantation and allows short-term assessment of tissue compatibility, local tissue response, and safety of airway stents. This study aimed to systematically evaluate the structural characteristics, radial mechanical performance, in vitro biocompatibility, and short-term in vivo safety of a balloon-expandable trilayer Fe-N/Zn/PDLLA metallic tracheal stent. We present this article in accordance with the ARRIVE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0472/rc).

Methods

Stent design and fabrication

A balloon-expandable layered tracheal stent was fabricated with a mesh open-cell design (Φ 6 mm × 15 mm). The load-bearing framework was prepared from high-purity iron tubes by vacuum plasma nitriding (~500 ℃, 50 Pa, 2 h) to obtain a Fe-N substrate, followed by drawing and laser cutting. Chemical treatment and electropolishing were performed to remove burrs and improve the surface. The metallic strut thickness was ~70 µm. A Zn buffering layer (~600 nm) was deposited by electroplating, followed by an outer PDLLA coating (~5 µm) by ultrasonic atomization spraying. The stent was crimped onto a balloon catheter and deployed by balloon expansion (8–10 atm, 30 s) for subsequent in vitro and in vivo studies. The tracheal stent was provided by Biotyx Medical (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd. (Shenzhen, China).

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

SEM was used to examine the overall mesh geometry, edge quality of links/struts, surface coverage of the coatings, and cross-sectional layered structures. Samples were dried and mounted; conductive sputter-coating was applied when needed to improve imaging stability. Both pre- and post-expansion specimens were examined to assess structural integrity and coating condition after balloon expansion.

Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy

Energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) coupled with SEM was used for elemental analysis. Spectra were acquired from representative surface regions, with additional local analysis when necessary, to confirm key elements (Fe, N, Zn) and coating-related elements (C, O). If Pt peaks were present, they were interpreted as originating from the conductive sputter-coating rather than the intrinsic composition.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)

XPS was performed to evaluate surface elemental composition and surface chemistry. Survey spectra were collected to identify characteristic peaks of Fe, Zn, C, and O, and relative peak intensities were used to assess surface coverage features, reflecting the composition of the coating/interlayer and the exposure of the metallic substrate signal.

X-ray diffraction (XRD)

XRD was used to analyze phase composition and crystal structure. Diffraction patterns were collected and characteristic peaks were assigned to confirm the iron matrix and Fe-N phases; Zn-related diffraction regions were used as supportive evidence when applicable.

Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)

FTIR was used to characterize functional groups of the polymer coating. IR spectra were collected and analyzed based on typical ester C=O stretching and C-O-C/C-O bands in the fingerprint region to confirm PDLLA coating features and coating validity.

Mechanical testing

Radial compression testing was conducted to evaluate stent mechanical performance. Stents were placed in a radial compression fixture/parallel-plate setup and compressed at room temperature at a constant loading rate, while force and displacement were recorded to generate force-displacement curves. Fe-N stents were compared with pure iron stents of identical geometry under the same conditions to assess the effect of nitriding on radial support. Radial supporting forces at 25%, 50%, and 75% compressive strains were extracted for quantitative analysis.

In vitro biocompatibility (BEAS-2B and RAW264.7)

Stent extracts were prepared by a standard extraction procedure, and complete culture medium served as the negative control. BEAS-2B and RAW264.7 cells were obtained from ATCC (Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China). BEAS-2B cells were used to evaluate epithelial biocompatibility. After incubation with extracts, cell migration was assessed by scratch assay, proliferation by 5-ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EdU; MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) incorporation, transmembrane behavior by Transwell assays, and apoptosis by transferase dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL; MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) staining. Cytoskeleton staining was performed to examine cell morphology, and live/dead staining was used to assess viability. Mitomycin C was used as a positive control for proliferation inhibition to validate assay responsiveness; the TUNEL positive control was set according to the kit instructions.

RAW264.7 macrophages were used to assess immunological safety. Cells were incubated with stent extracts under basal conditions, and lipopolysaccharide (LPS; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) stimulation served as a positive control to validate the inflammatory model. Immunofluorescence staining was performed for cluster of differentiation 86 (CD86; HUABIO, Hangzhou, China) and interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β; HUABIO), and for inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS; HUABIO) and interleukin-6 (IL-6; HUABIO), with representative fluorescence analysis. Total RNA was extracted for reverse transcription, and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was used to quantify messenger RNA (mRNA) levels of CD86, IL-1β, iNOS, and IL-6. A housekeeping gene was used for normalization, and results were presented as relative expression.

Animal study design and stent implantation

Healthy male New Zealand White rabbits, aged 3–6 months and weighing 2–3 kg, were provided by Jiangsu Ailingfei Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). All animals were in good health before the experiment, had not undergone any previous experimental procedures. Rabbits were housed in a standard animal facility with free access to food and water under controlled temperature, relative humidity, and a fixed light-dark cycle. Experiments were performed under a project license (No. JSAB25020M) granted by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Jiangsu Ailingfei Biotechnology Co., Ltd., in compliance with relevant institutional guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. Fifteen adult rabbits were randomly allocated to three predefined follow-up groups corresponding to 1 week, 1 month, and 2 months after implantation (n=5 per group). At each scheduled endpoint, animals were euthanized for tissue harvest and evaluation. The animal sample size was determined primarily on the basis of prior pilot experience and commonly used study designs in similar studies of implantable airway stents. The inclusion criterion was adult rabbits that successfully underwent stent implantation and survived to the scheduled study endpoint. The exclusion criteria were failed stent implantation, unexpected perioperative death, and severe illness unrelated to the study. No animals or data points were excluded.

Each rabbit was treated as an independent experimental unit. Under general anesthesia, the stent was delivered to the target tracheal segment using a flexible bronchoscopy-guided delivery system. After confirming position, the stent was released and deployed by balloon expansion to achieve apposition. The intraluminal view was monitored in real time via an external display to evaluate positioning, expansion, and airway patency. Postoperative animals received routine care and monitoring, including observation of respiration, activity, food intake, body weight, and abnormal secretions. Anesthesia and necessary analgesia were provided during the perioperative period. Animals reaching humane endpoints were removed from observation and humanely euthanized. No unexpected serious adverse events related to stent implantation were observed during the study. As this was an exploratory study, no single primary outcome was predefined. Instead, the overall evaluation was based on local tissue response after stent implantation, histological findings, and safety-related outcomes.

Histological evaluation

At each time point, tracheal tissues were harvested, fixed, dehydrated, embedded, and sectioned for histology. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed to evaluate mucosal injury and repair, and Masson’s trichrome staining was used to assess collagen deposition and fibrotic remodeling. Considering time-dependent tissue responses, the contact region between stent and mucosa was mainly evaluated at 1 week to reflect early mechanical irritation, whereas pore/void-related regions (non-contact regions) were emphasized at 1 and 2 months to assess re-epithelialization, cilia-like border recovery, and possible granulation or intraluminal protrusion related to restenosis. Lungs were collected for H&E and Masson staining to evaluate distal effects. Major organs (heart, liver, spleen, and kidney) were collected for H&E staining to evaluate systemic safety. Slides were examined under light microscopy and representative images were recorded. Histological evaluation and interpretation were performed jointly by two pathology researchers who were blinded to the group assignments, in order to minimize observer bias.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. Quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Two-group comparisons were performed using two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-tests, and multi-group comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with appropriate post hoc tests when required. In the in vivo study, five rabbits were included at each time point. The corresponding n values for each analysis are provided in the figure legends or “Methods” section. Data distribution was assessed before statistical analysis, and appropriate statistical methods were selected accordingly. Experiments were repeated independently at least three times (n≥3, or as specified), and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Structural design, compositional verification, and mechanical performance of the nitrided-iron stent

We constructed a layered bioresorbable metallic stent for rabbit trachea with a mesh open-cell geometry (Φ 6 mm × 15 mm). Gross inspection showed a regular outline with uniform cells and intact circumferential units and connecting bridges (Figure 1A). For controlled deployment and apposition, a balloon-expandable delivery strategy was used.

The stent adopted a trilayer configuration: Fe-N load-bearing backbone/Zn buffering layer/PDLLA outer coating (Figure 1B). The Fe-N framework served as the primary bearing component. Nitrogen was introduced into the iron surface by plasma nitriding to enhance mechanical capacity, followed by construction of a Zn interlayer and a PDLLA top coating to form the composite structure. The surface morphology observed by SEM showed a smooth and uniform structure, confirming the successful fabrication of the stent (Figure 1C).

To verify composition and the multilayer structure, EDS, XPS, XRD, and FTIR were performed. EDS detected key elemental signals of Fe, N, and Zn, together with C and O consistent with the polymer coating (Figure 1D). XPS survey spectra further confirmed the presence of surface C and O signals along with Fe and Zn peaks (Figure 1E). XRD showed diffraction features consistent with the iron matrix and Fe-N phases, supporting successful nitriding modification (Figure 1F). FTIR spectra displayed typical ester-related peaks of PDLLA, confirming the polymer coating (Figure 1G).

Mechanical performance was evaluated by comparing radial supporting forces between Fe-N and pure iron stents with the same geometry at different compressive strains. At 25%, 50%, and 75% compressive strains, the radial supporting forces of the Fe-N stent were 3.41, 4.31, and 5.20 N, respectively, which were higher than those of the pure iron stent (0.93, 1.45, and 1.63 N). These results indicate that nitriding improved the radial supporting capacity of the iron-based tracheal stent (Figure 1H).

In vitro biocompatibility of stent extracts on BEAS-2B cells

To evaluate in vitro biocompatibility of stent extracts on airway epithelial cells, BEAS-2B cells were incubated with stent extracts. Complete medium served as the negative control. Mitomycin C was used as a positive control for proliferation inhibition to validate assay responsiveness. In scratch assays, wound closure progressed over time in all groups, with no obvious impairment in the extract group compared with the negative control (Figure 2A). EdU staining showed no evident reduction of proliferative cells in the extract group, whereas the positive control demonstrated the expected inhibition (Figure 2B). Transwell assays indicated that the extract group maintained comparable transmembrane behavior to the negative control (Figure 2C). TUNEL staining did not show an increased apoptotic signal in the extract group; the kit-provided positive control produced strong TUNEL positivity as expected (Figure 2D). Cytoskeleton staining showed preserved cell morphology and cytoskeletal organization in the extract group (Figure 2E), and live/dead staining did not reveal an increased cell death rate relative to the negative control (Figure 2F).

Effect of stent extracts on inflammatory activation of RAW264.7 macrophages

To determine whether stent extracts induce inflammatory activation of macrophages, RAW264.7 cells were incubated with stent extracts under basal conditions. LPS stimulation served as a positive control to validate the inflammatory model and detection system. Immunofluorescence results showed that, compared with the negative control, the extract group did not exhibit increased fluorescence signals of CD86 and IL-1β (Figure 3A), nor increased iNOS and IL-6 signals (Figure 3B). RT-qPCR further showed no significant upregulation of CD86, IL-1β, iNOS, or IL-6 mRNAs in the extract group (Figure 3C). In contrast, the LPS group showed the expected elevation of these inflammatory markers, confirming model validity.

Bronchoscopic delivery and implantation of the tracheal stent in rabbits

To verify in vivo deliverability and controllable deployment, we established a flexible bronchoscopy-guided implantation workflow. As shown in Figure 4A, flexible bronchoscopy was used for delivery and positioning, enabling stent advancement, localization, and release under direct visualization. After anesthesia, rabbits were placed supine, and the delivery system was introduced through the bronchoscope channel. The stent was positioned at the target segment and deployed by balloon expansion (Figure 4B). The intraluminal view was displayed in real time (Figure 4C), and the deployed stent showed satisfactory expansion and in situ configuration within the tracheal lumen (Figure 4D).

Histological evaluation of local tissue response and systemic safety after tracheal stent implantation

To systematically evaluate local tissue response, distal lung changes, and systemic safety after implantation, histological examinations were performed at 1 week, 1 month, and 2 months. In the rabbit implantation study, five animals were included at each follow-up time point (1 week, 1 month, and 2 months; n=5 per time point). Considering time-dependent response characteristics, the contact region was mainly evaluated at 1 week to reflect early mechanical irritation-related injury, whereas pore/void-related regions (non-contact regions) were emphasized at 1 and 2 months to assess epithelial recovery and potential restenosis-related changes.

Tracheal H&E staining showed mucosal discontinuity and focal erosion/desquamation at the contact region at 1 week, with luminal debris/exudates and prominent inflammatory infiltration with congestion and edema. At 1 month, epithelial continuity in pore/void-related regions improved, with repair-associated thickening and reduced inflammation; the brush border was still patchy. At 2 months, the mucosa in pore/void-related regions became more stable, with good epithelial continuity and near-normal thickness; inflammatory infiltration further decreased to near background, and the brush border appeared more continuous. Notably, no obvious progressive granulation tissue growth, intraluminal protrusion, or pore-impaction features were observed during follow-up (Figure 5A).

Masson’s trichrome staining showed mild, focal collagen increase in the lamina propria and superficial submucosa at the contact region at 1 week, consistent with early repair-associated collagen deposition. At 1 and 2 months, collagen staining in pore/void-related regions remained mild and focal, without progressive increase in extent or intensity, and without apparent pore filling or intraluminal fibrous protrusion (Figure 5B).

To assess distal effects, lung tissues were stained with H&E and Masson’s trichrome. H&E showed thickened alveolar septa and increased interstitial inflammatory infiltration at 1 week, with small amounts of exudate in some alveolar spaces. These changes were markedly alleviated at 1 month and largely resolved at 2 months, with near-normal alveolar architecture (Figure 5C). Masson staining showed mild collagen enhancement at 1 week that decreased at 1 month and approached background at 2 months, without progressive fibrotic remodeling (Figure 5D).

Histology of major organs (heart, liver, spleen, and kidney) did not show clear implantation-related injury at any time point. Representative H&E sections indicated preserved tissue architecture without marked degeneration/necrosis or substantial inflammatory infiltration (Figure 5E).

Discussion

This study addresses a long-standing challenge in pediatric airway stenting: providing sufficient radial support during key disease or postoperative periods while minimizing sustained interference with mucociliary clearance and avoiding complications associated with permanent indwelling (16-18). Prior studies indicate that airway stenting can improve ventilation but is frequently complicated by granulation, mucus retention/plugging, migration, infection, and difficult removal, which can limit long-term outcomes (19,20).

From a structural and delivery perspective, the airway environment is highly sensitive to stent geometry and luminal flow (21). Excessive coverage or persistent friction can promote secretion retention and infection, thereby driving inflammation and granulation (22-24). Our stent uses a mesh open-cell design and a balloon-expandable strategy, and the implantation workflow demonstrated feasible bronchoscopic delivery and controlled deployment in rabbits, providing a practical basis for further evaluation of clinically relevant endpoints such as migration and mucus-related events.

Mechanical performance is a primary prerequisite for airway stents (25). The airway must resist dynamic loads such as inspiratory negative pressure and coughing, yet excessive radial force may increase contact pressure and mucosal injury (26-28). Our radial compression data show that Fe-N stents provide higher radial support than pure iron stents of identical geometry, supporting the use of Fe-N as a load-bearing backbone for thin-strut, high-porosity designs.

For in vitro evaluation, we focused on epithelial repair-related behaviors and innate immune activation (29-31). Extract exposure did not impair key BEAS-2B behaviors or viability in our assays, and did not induce inflammatory activation in RAW264.7 macrophages, whereas the LPS group confirmed model responsiveness. These data support a non-proinflammatory, non-cytotoxic profile of the extracts under the present extraction and exposure conditions, rather than an anti-inflammatory effect.

Histology provided direct evidence of time-dependent tissue responses after implantation (32-35). We observed early mucosal injury and inflammation at the contact region at 1 week, consistent with mechanical irritation. With time, epithelial continuity and brush-border features improved in pore/void-related regions, and no obvious progressive granulation growth, intraluminal protrusion, or progressive fibrosis was observed up to 2 months. Masson staining suggested only focal repair-associated collagen deposition without a progressive fibrotic trend. These findings align with the design rationale that a low-coverage mesh may reduce sustained mucosal compression while maintaining airway patency.

We also assessed distal lung tissue and major organs. Lung changes were most evident at 1 week and gradually resolved by 2 months, with no progressive fibrotic remodeling by Masson staining. Major organs showed preserved tissue architecture. Together, these observations support short-term systemic safety in this rabbit model.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The rabbit airway differs from pediatric airways in size, growth dynamics, and microenvironment, and the current follow-up (2 months) does not capture the full degradation and exit process. Histological assessments were largely descriptive, and standardized scoring, blinded quantification, airway functional metrics, and head-to-head comparisons with clinically used silicone/metallic or PDO stents would strengthen translational relevance. Future studies should extend follow-up and incorporate quantitative degradation kinetics and clinically meaningful outcome measures.

Conclusions

We developed a balloon-expandable layered bioresorbable nitrided-iron tracheal stent (Fe-N/Zn/PDLLA) for rabbits. Multiscale characterization confirmed that the trilayer structure was constructed as designed. Under identical geometry, Fe-N stents exhibited higher radial support than pure iron stents, meeting the basic mechanical requirement for airway support.

In vitro, stent extracts showed no obvious adverse effects on BEAS-2B cell migration, proliferation, transmembrane behavior, or viability, and did not induce inflammatory activation of RAW264.7 macrophages. In vivo, the stent could be delivered and deployed under flexible bronchoscopy and remained stable in situ. Histology suggested early contact-region mucosal injury and inflammation followed by progressive repair, with no obvious progressive granulation or fibrotic remodeling within 2 months, and with favorable systemic safety signals. Together, these findings support further optimization and translational studies of trilayer metallic tracheal stents.

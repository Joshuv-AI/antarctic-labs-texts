Immunopersistence of one or two doses of an Escherichia coli produced bivalent HPV vaccine
Authors: Mengjun Liao, Jiali Quan, Kongxin Zhu, Xiaowen Hu, Shudan Shen, Jianhua Xu, Chu Nie, Weizhuo Xu, Hexuan Jiang, Xianliang Wu, Yanqin Zhong, Yuhang Peng, Shihui Cai, Jie Chen, Chengzong Zhang, Bizhen Lin, Yingying Su, Shoujie Huang, Qi Chen, Ting Wu, Jun Zhang, Ningshao Xia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractData on the immunopersistence of one- or two-dose regimens of HPV vaccine in women aged 18–45 remains scarce. During 5.5-year follow-up of a Phase 3 clinical trial of Cecolin® (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT01735006), blood samples were collected at day 0, month 7 and annually thereafter for neutralizing antibody measurement. Cervical samples were collected for ThinPrep Pap tests and HPV genotyping. At month 66, HPV-16 seropositive rates were 100.0% across all groups (single-dose, two-dose [0–1-month], two-dose [0-6-month], and three-dose). HPV-18 seropositive rates were 63.6%, 93.8%, 84.6% and 98.4%, respectively. Although geometric mean concentrations (GMCs, IU/mL) of neutralizing antibodies in the one- and two-dose groups were lower than in the three-dose group (HPV-16: 10.4, 46.0 and 46.9 vs 72.4; HPV-18: 5.3, 12.0 and 17.1 vs 35.9), they remained higher than levels induced by natural infection (HPV-16: 2.8; HPV-18: 2.3). One- or two-dose of Cecolin® demonstrated immunogenic in women aged 18–45 up to 5.5 years.Subject terms: Diseases, Immunology, Medical research, Microbiology

Introduction

Cervical cancer is predominantly attributable to persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV), representing the most common HPV-related malignancy and the fourth most prevalent cancer among women on a global scale1,2. In 2022, it was estimated that there were around 660,000 new cases of cervical cancer and 340,000 related deaths worldwide, with a disproportionate impact seen in low and middle-income countries (LMICs)2. As of October 2025, nine prophylactic HPV vaccines have been licensed, each incorporating virus-like particles (VLPs) targeting high-risk HPV types 16 and 18, which together cause 71% of cervical cancer cases worldwide3–6. Implementation of HPV vaccination programs has resulted in decrease of prevalence of certain HPV-related diseases such as genital warts and cervical dysplasia across various regions, and initial observations of decreased prevalence of cervical cancer have also been reported in some countries7–9. However, the global disease burden of cervical cancer remains substantial10.

In 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) recommended the inclusion of HPV vaccines in national immunization programs (NIP), with the aim of achieving 90% coverage among girls aged 9–14 years by 2030. Despite this, global coverage of 2023 was estimated to be only 27% for girls with the first dose and 20% for full doses11. Several barriers have been identified as impediments to the introduction and uptake of HPV vaccination, notably recent temporary constraints in the HPV vaccine supply and the cost of administering a multidose schedule12,13. A promising alternative is the administration of a single dose of the HPV vaccine, which is simpler and more cost-effective than a multidose schedule, thus enhancing vaccination coverage14.

Importantly, several observational studies and randomized trials have suggested that a single-dose regimen demonstrates sustained immunological responses and substantial efficacy against HPV infections and related diseases15. In the KEN SHE study, a single dose of either Cervarix® (the bivalent vaccine, GlaxoSmithKline, GSK) or Gardasil®9 (the 9-valent vaccine, Merck & Co) demonstrated a vaccine efficacy of 97.5% against HPV-16 and 18 cervical persistent infection (over 6 months) in sexually active women aged 15–20, measured at 18 months post-vaccination, with efficacy remaining stable through 36 months16,17. Furthermore, the DoRIS study demonstrated that Tanzanian girls aged 9-14 exhibited sustained immune responses for up to 60 months following a single dose of either Cervarix® or Gardasil®918,19.

Cecolin® (the bivalent vaccine, Xiamen Innovax Biotech Co. Ltd.), a fourth WHO-prequalified HPV vaccine, also has been recommended for off-label use with a single-dose schedule in October 2024 based on data from the trial in Bangladesh and Ghana girls aged 9–14 years, which indicated that immunogenicity following one dose of Cecolin® was comparable to Gardasil® (the quadrivalent vaccine, Merck & Co) at months 6 and 24, with a trend for higher geometric mean concentrations (GMCs) following Cecolin® administration14,20,21.

In the Phase III efficacy trial conducted in China, Cecolin® demonstrated 100% efficacy against HPV 16/18-related cervical lesions in women aged 18–45 years who received at least one dose of vaccine22. Here, we conducted post-hoc analysis of the Phase III trial to evaluate the immunopersistence and effectiveness of Cecolin® in women who received either a single dose or two doses of the vaccine.

Results

Participants characteristics

In the Phase III trial, there were 3689 women aged 18–45 years in the vaccine group, including 98 participants who received one dose of HPV vaccine, 58 participants who received two doses (0-1-month schedule), 25 participants who received two doses (0–6-month schedule), and 3508 participants who received three doses. The first enrolled 300 participants in the Xinmi Country were included in the immunpersistence subgroup, among whom 148 received 3 doses of HPV vaccine. Due to the absence of serum samples after day 0, the total analysis cohort consisted of 48 participants in the single-dose group, 39 in the two-dose group (0-1 month schedule), 21 in the two-dose group (0-6 month schedule), and 146 in the three-dose group (Fig. 1). Moreover, after exclusion of women with positive neutralizing antibodies and/or DNA for HPV-16/18 at day 0, the naive analysis cohort included 46, 37, 20, and 130 participants for HPV-16 in each respective group, and 45, 37, 20, and 138 participants for HPV-18. For effectiveness analysis, participants who received either one or two doses of HPV vaccine or control (Hepatitis E) vaccine in the five sites were included (Supplementary Figs. 1 and 2).

At day 0 of entry, the mean ages of participants in the total analysis cohort were 25.0 years for the single-dose group, 25.0 years for the two-dose group (0–1 month schedule), 26.0 years for the two-dose group (0-6 month schedule), and 27.0 years for the three-dose group (Table 1). Similarly, seropositivity for HPV-16 and HPV-18 neutralizing antibodies was infrequent at baseline. Cytological abnormalities were found in 8.9% to 14.6% of participants in each group.

Seropositive rates

At month 7 after the first dose of Cecolin®, all participants were seropositive for HPV-16, except one participant in the two-dose group (0-1-month schedule). At month 18, the seropositive rates for HPV-16 remained high, with 94.7% (18/19), 100.0% (21/21), and 93.8% (15/16) in the single-dose, two-dose (0-1-month schedule), and two-dose (0–6-month schedule) groups, respectively. At months 30, 42, and 54, the seropositive rates decreased, with 71.4% (5/7), 81.3% (13/16), and 93.8% (15/16) in the single-dose group, while 100.0% (22/22) at month 66. Notably, in both the two-dose groups, all participants remained seropositive at months 30, 42, 54, and 66. As expected, the three-dose group consistently maintained a 100.0% seropositivity rate at each time point (Table 2).

At month 7 after the first dose of Cecolin®, the seropositive rates for HPV-18 were 89.5% (17/19) in the single-dose group, 93.3% (14/15) in the two-dose group (0–1 month schedule), 100.0% (20/20) in the two-dose group (0–6 month schedule) and 100.0% (137/137) in the three-dose group. At months 18, 30, 42, and 54, the seropositive rates in the single-dose group ranged from 43.8% to 75.0%. Remarkably, at month 66, the seropositive rates remained at 63.6% for the single-dose group. In contrast, the seropositive rates were considerably higher in the two-dose groups (both schedules) and three-dose group, closing to 100.0% with only one or two participants remaining seronegative at each measuring time point after month 7 (Table 2).

GMCs

At month 7, the GMCs of neutralizing antibodies peaked, with 9.1 IU/mL (95% CI: 5.9–14.2), 49.3 IU/mL (95% CI: 27.7–88.0), 908.4 IU/mL (95% CI: 584.1–1412.7) and 720.0 IU/mL (95% CI: 633.6-818.1) for HPV-16 in the single-dose, two-dose (0-1 month schedule), two-dose (0–6 month schedule) and three-dose groups, respectively; and 6.8 IU/mL (95% CI: 4.2–11.0), 16.4 IU/mL (95% CI: 9.6–27.9), 285.0 IU/mL (95% CI: 156.8–518.0) and 436.0 IU/mL (95% CI: 378.9–501.9) for HPV-18 (Supplementary Table 1). When comparing with the three-dose group, the GMC ratios at month 7 for the two-dose group (0–6 month schedule) were 1.26 (95% CI: 0.88–1.81) for HPV-16 and 0.65 (95% CI: 0.43–1.00) for HPV-18 (Supplementary Table 2). Although the GMCs of HPV-16 and HPV-18 decreased after month 7, they maintained a stable condition at months 18 to 66 in the single- and two-dose groups, and remained higher than the natural infection levels (Fig. 2). At month 66, the GMCs of HPV-16 neutralizing antibodies were 10.4 IU/mL (95% CI: 6.1–17.8), 46.0 IU/mL (95% CI: 20.8–101.5), 46.9 IU/mL (95% CI: 24.1–91.3) and 72.4 IU/mL (95% CI: 60.3–86.9) in the single-dose group, two-dose group (0–1 month schedule), two-dose group (0–6 month schedule) and three-dose group, respectively; and 5.3 IU/mL (95% CI: 3.3–8.5), 12.0 IU/mL (95% CI: 5.0–28.7), 17.1 IU/mL (95% CI: 8.2–35.7) and 35.9 IU/mL (95% CI: 29.9–43.0) for HPV-18.

Vaccine effectiveness

For the one-dose, two-dose (0–1 month), and two-dose (0–6 month) regimens, the numbers of HPV-16/18 incident and 6-month persistent infection cases in the vaccine groups were lower than those in the corresponding control groups. Specifically, the numbers of incident infections for HPV-16/-18 were 2 vs 5, 1 vs 4, and 1 vs 3, respectively, while the numbers of 6-month persistent infections were 0 vs 1, 0 vs 0, and 0 vs 1, respectively. However, due to the limited sample size, none of these differences reached statistical significance (Supplementary Tables 3, 4).

Discussion

This is the first study to evaluate the long-term immunopersistence and effectiveness of Cecolin® administered via single- and two-dose regimens in women aged 18–45 over a 5.5-year period. Our findings reveal that even a single-dose regimen can induce and sustain antibody levels exceeding those from natural infection in adult women. Moreover, receiving two doses of the HPV vaccine administered at a 6-month interval can achieve relatively high antibody levels, with GMC ratios of 0.65 (95% CI: 0.37–1.14) for HPV-16, and 0.48 (95% CI: 0.25–0.90) at month 66, compared to the three-dose group (Supplement Table 2). However, the sample size was too small to reach a conclusion regarding the comparison between the two-dose and three-dose regimens and whether the two-dose regimen administered at a 6-month interval truly prevents future infection requires further large-scale cohort studies or randomized controlled trials for confirmation.

Similar as other recombinant vaccines, immune responses induced by HPV vaccines decreased along with the increase of age23. Existing research primarily focused on adolescent girls to study the immune durability and effectiveness of one-dose or two-dose HPV vaccines16–21,24–27, while data in adult females remain scarce. Our data indicate that in women aged 18–45 years, for HPV-16, both the single-dose and two-dose groups maintain a seropositive rate of about 100.0% at months 7 and 66. While for HPV-18, only the two-dose regimens approach a 100.0% seropositive rate at these time points, the single-dose group presents lower seropositive rates of 89.5% and 63.6%, respectively. The lower than expected seropositive rates after a single-dose regimen might be attributed to the limited sample size and the fact that less robust immune responses were induced in adult women compared to those in adolescent girls28. Additionally, incomplete seroconversion after a single dose in the young adolescents also happened in previous studies29–31. These studies indicate that 1 to 5 years after receiving a single dose of the HPV vaccine, the seropositivity for HPV16 ranges from 45.3% to 98.3%, and for HPV18 it ranges from 59.7% to 87.6%. The variability in seropositivity after vaccination with one dose may depend on factors such as the study population, the time interval after the first dose, and the method of immunogenicity assessment.

Existing data showed that antibody levels peaked approximately one month after the final dose, followed by a rapid decline of approximately 1 to 2 logarithmic units over the subsequent 12 months before stabilizing19,21. In our study, antibody levels at month 7 were significantly lower in both the one-dose group and the two-dose group (0-1 month schedule) compared to the three-dose group, primarily due to variations in the interval between blood collection and final dose vaccination across schedule groups. However, by month 66 when antibody levels stabilized and plateaued, the differences of antibody levels between the one-dose and the three-dose groups became much smaller. Furthermore, at month 66, the single-dose group achieved antibody levels 2-3 times higher than those induced by natural infection, consistent with other studies reporting similar enhancements in females aged 10-25 years24,25. Notably, low-level neutralizing antibodies acquired from natural infection have demonstrated considerable protection against homotypic infection32, as evidenced by a prospective cohort study reporting an 84% reduction in HPV-16/18 persistent infection (>6 months) among seropositive women33. However, it is noteworthy that during natural HPV infection, antigen exposure is confined to the epithelium, whereas vaccination exposes the immune system to antigens systemically, leading to immune responses that may differ substantially. Additionally, in the extension study of FUTURE II, a pivotal clinical trial of Gardasil®, approximately 30% of participants experienced seroreversion for HPV-18 by 48 months after the first vaccine dose; however, an accompanying 8-year follow-up detected no HPV-18 related breakthrough case34. In the 14-year extended follow-up study, the use of a more sensitive IgG Luminex immunoassay demonstrated >90% seropositivity to HPV-18, in contrast to earlier findings based on the less sensitive competitive Luminex immunoassay (cLIA)35. These results highlight that assay differences can substantially influence the observed rates of seroreversion.

Our study showed that the vaccine groups had numerically lower numbers of HPV-16/18 incident and 6-month persistent infection cases compared to the control groups, indicating the potential to prevent HPV infection. However, due to the small sample size and no statistically significant differences were observed between the control and the reduced vaccine groups, there is an urgent need for large-scale clinical trials to demonstrate the efficacy of reduced-dose HPV vaccine regimens. In addition, a recent study found that girls receiving one dose of Gardasil® demonstrated a lower incidence of high-grade cervical lesions associated with HPV-16 and HPV-18 compared with the unvaccinated girls, and exhibited comparable effectiveness against high-grade cervical lesions as those who received the standard three doses36. Given that one dose of Cecolin® demonstrates comparable immunogenicity as Gardasil® at months 1, 6, and 24 after vaccination in two randomized clinical trials20,21,26, it is reasonable to expect that Cecolin® would offer similar and durable protection against high-grade lesions as Gardasil®. Taken together, our results and other studies suggested that a single-dose or two-dose HPV vaccination in women aged 18–45 years may confer protective immunity for at least 66 months.

At the end of 2019, Cecolin® was first licensed in China and subsequently obtained the World Health Organization (WHO) prequalification in 2021. As of August 2025, Cecolin® has been approved in more than 20 countries outside China and incorporated into the national immunization programs (NIPs) of 7 countries. Owing to its scalability and cost-effectiveness, Cecolin® (Escherichia coli expression system) represents a promising solution to the challenges of global HPV vaccine supply and affordability, particularly in resource-limited settings. Notably, most of the countries where Cecolin® has been licensed are located in Asia and Africa, regions characterized by a bimodal HPV prevalence distribution37. Unlike regions with a unimodal HPV prevalence distribution (such as the Americas), studies in areas exhibiting a bimodal distribution pattern reveal that mid-adult women face a significant ongoing risk of acquiring new causal HPV infections that may progress to cervical lesions, with risk levels only slightly lower than younger women37. In such settings, the second peak in HPV prevalence among mid-adult women suggests that catch-up vaccination could still confer meaningful preventive benefits38. Although the WHO recommended a two-dose regimen for females above 20 years old in 202239, most countries continue to adopt a three-dose schedule, which may constrain vaccine coverage in this population. In our study, the GMCs for HPV-16 and -18 antibodies in the two-dose group (0–6 month schedule) were lower than those in the three-dose group, but still exceeded the antibody levels from natural infection by more than seven times (Supplementary Table 2). These findings provide support for the broader application of the WHO-recommended two-dose HPV vaccination regimen (0-6 month schedule) in females over 20 years old, particularly in regions with a bimodal HPV prevalence pattern.

Strengths of our study include the use of a validated PBNA with excellent assay performance to measure neutralizing antibodies against HPV-16 and HPV-18, which is widely regarded as the “gold standard” for assessing vaccine-induced immunity. In addition, our study focuses on women vaccinated at 18–45 years of age, a population for whom immunogenicity data of one- or two- dose regimens are scarce. This study had several limitations. Firstly, as a post-hoc analysis, this study has a relatively small sample size, which may introduce potential selection bias in the samples measured at specific time points. Moreover, we cannot exclude the possibility that some individuals with initially low antibody levels were subsequently boosted through natural HPV exposure. Our findings therefore support conducting future randomized controlled trials with larger and more diverse populations to further evaluate the immunopersistence and effectiveness of single- or two-dose regimens in adult women. Secondly, although our study lacks antibody data at one month after the final dose in the one-dose and two-dose (0–1 month schedule) groups, which would reflect the peak immune response, the subsequent plateau-phase data across groups still provide valuable insights. Thirdly, we focus solely on measuring the neutralizing antibodies, potentially overlooking other immune parameters, which could result in an incomplete assessment of the overall immune response.

This study demonstrates that one- or two-doses of Cecolin® were immunogenic in women aged 18–45 years, lasting for up to 5.5 years. Although numerically fewer HPV-16/18 infection cases were observed in the vaccine groups compared with the control groups, the limited sample size precludes a reliable estimation of vaccine effectiveness. In the context of low HPV vaccine coverage, our findings support the potential use of reduced-dose regimens and highlight the need for further studies to confirm the efficacy of one- and two-dose Cecolin® vaccination in adult women.

Methods

Study participants

In the phase III trial of the bivalent vaccine (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT01735006), eligible women aged 18–45 years from five study sites in China were age-stratified (18–26 and 27–45 age groups) and randomly assigned to receive either three doses of the HPV vaccine or control vaccine (hepatitis E) at months 0, 1, and 6. However, due to pregnancy, loss of follow-up, and refusal of vaccination, some participants only received one or two doses of the HPV vaccine (0–1-month or 0–6-month schedule).

In this retrospective study, participants who received either one or two doses of HPV vaccine in the five sites and participants who received three doses of HPV vaccine in the immunopersistence subgroup (the first enrolled 300 participants in Xinmi County, Henan Province) were included for immunological analysis. For effectiveness analysis, participants who received either one or two doses of HPV vaccine or control (Hepatitis E) vaccine in the five sites were included.

Serum and cervical sampling and detection

Serum samples were collected at months 0, 7, 18, 30, 42, 54, and 66, and tested using the pseudovirion-based neutralization assay (PBNA) to detect neutralizing antibodies40. The levels of neutralizing antibodies were measured with a cutoff value of 2.1 IU/ mL for HPV-16 and 1.7 IU/mL for HPV-18. References traceable to WHO international standards for antibodies against HPV-16 (NIBSC code 05/134) or HPV-18 (NIBSC code 10/140) were used to quantify the positive serum samples. Titers below these thresholds were deemed seronegative. Additionally, cervical samples were collected at months 0, 7, 12, 18, 24, 30, 42, 54, and 66 for ThinPrep Pap tests (Hologic, United States) and HPV genotyping (SPF10-PCR-DEIA/LiPA25 assay, Laboratory Biomedical Products, the Netherlands).

Statistical analysis

The naive cohort was the primary analysis set in this study, including participants who were seronegative for HPV-16 or HPV-18 neutralizing antibodies at day 0, negative for HPV DNA against the relevant types of HPV at day 0, and had at least one valid serologic (for immunogenicity analysis) or HPV DNA (for effectiveness analysis against incident infection) result after day 0. For the analysis of 6-month persistent infection, participants also needed to have at least two valid HPV DNA test results after day 0 with an interval of more than 150 days. According to the dose and time interval of HPV bivalent vaccine vaccination, they were divided into the single-dose group, two-dose group (0-1-month schedule), two-dose group (0-6-month schedule), and three-dose group. We calculated the seropositive rates, the GMCs of HPV-16 and HPV-18 neutralizing antibodies and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) by using the t-test for log-transformed concentration to assess the persistence of immunity of the vaccine. For neutralizing antibody levels induced by the natural infection, GMCs were calculated from the baseline seropositive participants in the Xinmi site, which are 2.8 IU/mL (95% CI: 2.5–3.2) for HPV-16 (based on 129 HPV-16 seropositive participants at day 0 of entry) and 2.3 IU/mL (95% CI: 1.9–2.7) for HPV-18 (based on 65 HPV-18 seropositive participants at entry). The GMC ratios with 95% CIs of HPV-16 and HPV-18 neutralizing antibodies were also calculated using the t-test. To explore the effectiveness of one or two doses of the bivalent vaccine, we separately analyzed their protection against incident and persistent HPV infection (over 6 months). Effectiveness and 95% CI were calculated based on the observed difference in event rates using accrued person time as the denominator. An exact conditional procedure was used to evaluate effectiveness under the assumption that the number of cases in the vaccine and control groups were independent Poisson random variables41.

Data were analyzed by SAS software (version 9.4) and pictured by Graphpad Prism 10.0. All reported P values were two-sided with an alpha value of 0.05, except for those specifically indicated.

Cardinal sequences of Lindelöf scattered P-spaces Thanks: The research on and preparation of this paper was supported by OTKA grant K129211
Juan Carlos Martínez, Lajos Soukup

Abstract
. We continue our investigation of cardinal sequences associated with locally Lindelöf, scattered, Hausdorff P-spaces (abbreviated as LLSP spaces). We outline a method for constructing LLSP spaces from cone systems and partial orders with specific properties. Additionally, we establish limitations on the cardinal sequences of LLSP spaces. Finally, we present both a necessary condition and a distinct sufficient condition for a sequence ⟨ κ α : α < ω 2 ⟩ \left\langle{\kappa}_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\omega}_{2}\right\rangle to be the cardinal sequence of an LLSP space.



1.
Introduction

Recall that if
X
is a topological space and
\alpha
is an ordinal, the
\alpha\mbox{th}
-
Cantor-Bendixson level
of
X
is defined as
I_{\alpha}(X)=
the set of isolated points of
X\setminus\bigcup\{I_{\beta}(X):\beta<\alpha\}
. Then,
X
is
scattered
if there is an ordinal
\alpha
such that
X=\bigcup\{I_{\beta}(X):\beta<\alpha\}
. If
X
is a scattered space, the
cardinal sequence
of
X
is defined as
\mbox{CS}(X)=\langle|I_{\alpha}(X)|:\alpha<\delta\rangle
, where
\delta
is the least ordinal
\gamma
such that
I_{\gamma}(X)
is a finite set. Recall that a topological space
X
is a
P-space
, if the intersection of every countable family of open sets in
X
is open in
X
.”

In this paper, we continue our investigation of cardinal sequences of LLSP spaces, building upon our work initiated in
[
8
]
. Let us recall that,
in the context of this study, an LLSP space refers to a locally Lindelőf, scattered, Hausdorff P-space.

Cone systems and partial orders with special properties have played a fundamental role in the construction of locally compact scattered Hausdorff (LCS) spaces.
These concepts have been explored in works such as
[
5
]
and
[
1
]
.
In Section
2
, we extend a similar analysis to LLSP spaces instead of LCS spaces.

There are numerous well-established and easily demonstrable
constraints regarding the cardinal sequences of LCS spaces. In Section
3
,
we endeavor to establish analogous theorems for LLSP spaces.

To simplify the presentation of our results, we use
\mathcal{C}^{P}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{C}({\alpha})
to represent, respectively,
the classes of all cardinal sequences of length
\alpha

associated with LLSP and LCS spaces.

In their work
[
7
]
, Juhász and Weiss
gave a full description of the class
\mathcal{C}({\omega}_{1})
by
establishing
that a sequence

\left\langle{\kappa}_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\omega}_{1}\right\rangle
of infinite cardinals
is the cardinal sequence of an LCS space iff

{\kappa}_{\beta}\leq({\kappa}_{\alpha})^{\omega}
holds for each

{\alpha}<{\beta}<{\omega}_{1}
.

In Section
4
, we will encounter the challenge of
characterizing short sequences for LLSP spaces,
which seems to be a much harder task.
Initially, we demonstrate that the natural transfer of the previously
mentioned result of Juhász and Weiss
for LLSP spaces is unsuccessful.
In Theorem
4.4
, we provide both a necessary condition and a
distinct sufficient condition for a sequence
\left\langle{\kappa}_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\omega}_{2}\right\rangle

to be the cardinal sequence of an LLSP space.

The necessary and sufficient conditions mentioned earlier
coincide in the Cohen model (see Theorem
4.8
). However, in Theorem
4.9
, we illustrate
that these properties consistently differ from each other.
As it turns out, we lack a complete ZFC characterization of
\mathcal{C}^{P}(3)
,
the class of cardinal sequences associated with LLSP spaces of height
3
.

Let
\left\langle\kappa\right\rangle_{\alpha}
denote the constant
\kappa
-valued sequence of length
\alpha
.
It is almost trivial that if

\left\langle\kappa\right\rangle_{\alpha}{}^{\frown}\left\langle{\lambda}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{C}({\alpha}+1)
with
cf({\alpha})\leq{\omega}
,
then
{\lambda}\leq{\kappa}^{\omega}
. Analogously, one can easily prove that if

\left\langle\kappa\right\rangle_{\alpha}{}^{\frown}\left\langle{\lambda}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{C}^{P}({\alpha}+1)
then

{\lambda}\leq{\kappa}^{\omega_{1}}

provided that either
{\alpha}
is a successor ordinal or

cf({\alpha})={\omega}_{1}
. However, the inequality
{\lambda}\leq{\kappa}^{\omega_{1}}
does not necessary hold when
cf({\alpha})={\omega}
:
in Theorem
5.1
we prove that
it is consistent that
2^{\omega_{1}}=\omega_{2}
and
\langle\omega_{2}\rangle_{\omega}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\langle\omega_{3}\rangle\in\mathcal{C}^{P}(\omega+1)
.

2.
LLSP spaces from cone systems

Definition 2.1
.

Given a scattered space
X
we say that a
neighborhood assignment
U:X\to{\tau}_{X}
is an
LLSP neighborhood assignment

iff

(1)

U(y)
is a clopen Lindelöf neighborhood of
y
for each
y\in X
,

(2)

if
y\in I_{\alpha}(X)
, then

U(y)\setminus I_{<\alpha}(X)=\{y\}.

As we observed in
[
8
, Proposition 1.1]
, LLSP spaces are 0-dimensional, and so
the following holds:

Proposition 2.2
.

Every LLSP space
X
has an LLSP neighborhood assignment

U_{X}
.

If
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is a function and
I\subset X
, write

U[I]=\bigcup\{U(x):x\in I\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Assume that
X
is a set and
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is a function.
We say that
U
is a
weak LLSP-cone assignment (for
X
)
iff for some ordinal

{\delta}
the set
X
has a partition
\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
and
there is a function
D:{[X]}^{2}\to{[X]}^{{\omega}}

such that:

(a)

\forall{\beta}<{\delta}

\forall p\in X_{\beta}
(
p\in U(p)
and
U(p)\setminus\{p\}\subset X_{<{\beta}}
),

(b)

for each
\{x,y\}\in{[X]}^{2}
,
if
x\notin U(y)
and
y\notin U(x)
, then

D(x,y)\subset{U(x)\cap U(y)}
and

U(x)\cap U(y)\subset U[D(x,y)],

(c)

for each
\{x,y\}\in{[X]}^{2}

if
x\in U(y)
, then

D(x,y)\subset{U(x)\setminus U(y)}
and

U(x)\setminus U(y)\subset U[D(x,y)].

We say that
U
is an
LLSP-cone assignment (for
X
)
if
(
d
) below also holds:

(d)

\forall{\alpha}<{\beta}<{\delta}

\forall p\in X_{\beta}

\forall I\in{[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]}^{{\omega}}

(U(p)\setminus U[I])\cap X_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset,

We will say that the partition
\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
is
a
witnessing partition
for
U
,
and the function
D
is the
LLSP-function
associated with
U
.

If
\alpha<\delta
, we write
X_{\leq\alpha}=\bigcup\{X_{\beta}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
.

The following statement is straightforward:

Proposition 2.4
.

If
U
is a weak LLSP-cone assignment, then a witnessing partition
\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle

is determined by the following recursive formula:

X_{\beta}=\{p\in X\setminus X_{<{\beta}}:U(p)\setminus X_{<{\beta}}=\{p\}\},

and
{\delta}=\min\{{\beta}:X_{\beta}=\emptyset\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

For
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
and
p\in X
let

\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)=\{U(p)\setminus U[I]:I\in{[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]}^{{\omega}}\},

and

\mathbb{B}_{U}=\{\mathcal{B}_{U}(p):p\in X\}.

Theorem 2.6
.

(1)
If
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is a weak LLSP-cone assignment with
a witnessing partition

\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
, then

(i)

\mathbb{B}_{U}
is a
neighborhood system of an LLSP space
X_{U}=\left\langle X,{\tau}_{U}\right\rangle
,

(ii)

U(p)
is a clopen Lindelőf subspace of
X_{U}
for each
p\in X
,

(iii)

I_{\leq\alpha}(X_{U})\supset X_{\leq\alpha}
for each
{\alpha}<{\delta}
.

Moreover, if
U
is a LLSP-cone assignment, then

(iv)

I_{\alpha}(X_{U})=X_{\alpha}
for each
{\alpha}<{\delta}
.

(2) If
X=\left\langle X,{\tau}\right\rangle
is an LLSP-space,
and

U:X\to\tau
is a neighborhood assignment such that
for each
{\alpha}<{\delta}
and for each
p\in I_{\alpha}(X)

U(p)
is a clopen Lindelöf subset of
X

with
U(p)\setminus I_{<{\alpha}}(X)=\{p\}
,

then
U
is an LLSP-cone assignment with the witnessing partition

\left\langle I_{\alpha}(X):{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
, and
{\tau}_{U}={\tau}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

X=\left\langle X,{\tau}\right\rangle
is an LLSP-space iff
there is an LLSP-cone assignment
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)

such that
{\tau}={\tau}_{U}
.

Proof.

If
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is an LLSP-cone assignment, then

X=\left\langle X,{\tau}_{U}\right\rangle
is an LLSP-space by Theorem
2.6
(1).

On the other hand, if
X
is an LLSP-space, then
by Proposition
2.2
, there is a
neighborhood assignment
U:X\to{\tau}_{X}
such that
if
p\in I_{\alpha}(X)
then

U(p)
is a clopen Lindelöf subset and

U(p)\setminus\{p\}\subset I_{<{\alpha}}(X).

Then
{\tau}={\tau}_{U}
by Theorem
2.6
(2).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

First, we prove (1). Assume
U:X\to\mathcal{P}(X)
is an LLSP-cone assignment with
a witnessing partition

\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
.

Claim 2.7.1
.

\mathbb{B}_{U}
is a
neighborhood system of a topology.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Proposition 1.2.3]
, we should check that

(BP1)

\forall p\in X

\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)\neq\emptyset
and
p\in\bigcap\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)
,

(BP2)

\forall p\in X

\forall q\in V\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)

\exists W\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(q)

W\subset V
,

(BP3)

\forall p\in X

\forall V_{0},V_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)

\exists V\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)

V\subset V_{0}\cap V_{1}
.

First observe that (BP1) and (BP3) are straightforward from the construction.

To check (BP2) assume that
q\in U(p)\setminus U[I]\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)
, where

I\in{[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]}^{{\omega}}
.

Then there is
D(q,p)\in{[U(q)\setminus U(p)]}^{{\omega}}
such that

U(q)\setminus U(p)\subset U[D(q,p)]

and for each
i\in I
there is
D(q,i)\in{[U(q)\setminus\{q\}]}^{{\omega}}
such that

U(q)\cap U(i)\subset U[D(q,i)]
.
Then

U(q)\setminus(U(p)\setminus U[I])\subset(U(q)\setminus U(p))\cup(U(q)\cap U[I])\subset\\
(U(q)\setminus U(p))\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}(U(q)\cap U(i))\subset U[D(q,p)]\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}U[D(q,i)]=\\
U[D(q,p)\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}D(q,i)].

Thus, writing
J=D(q,p)\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}D(q,i)
we have

U(q)\setminus U[J]\subset U(p)\setminus U[I],

and
U(q)\setminus U[J]\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(q)
.

So
\mathbb{B}_{U}
is a neighborhood system of a topology
{\tau}_{U}
.
∎

Claim 2.7.2
.

\left\langle X,{\tau}_{U}\right\rangle
is
T_{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\{x,y\}\in{[X]}^{2}
.

If
x\notin U(y)
and
y\notin U(x)
, then

U(x)\cap U(y)\subset U[D(x,y)]
, and

U_{x}=U(x)\setminus U[D(x,y)]\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(x)

and

U_{y}=U(y)\setminus U[D(x,y)]\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(y)

and
U_{x}\cap U_{y}=\emptyset
.

If
x\in U(y)
, then

U(y)\setminus U(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(y)
,
U(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{U}(x)
,
and
(U(y)\setminus U(x))\cap U(x)=\emptyset.

∎

The topology
{\tau}_{U}
is clearly a
P
-space topology.

Claim 2.7.3
.

For each
{\alpha}<{\delta}
and for each
p\in X_{\alpha}
the subspace

U(p)
is Lindelöf.

Proof.

We prove it by transfinite induction on
{\alpha}
.

Assume that
U(q)
is Lindelöf for each
{\beta}<{\alpha}
and
q\in X_{\beta}
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an open cover of
U(p)
. Pick
V\in\mathcal{V}
with
p\in V
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{U}(p)
is a neighborhood base of
p
, we have
U(p)\setminus U[I]\subset V
for some
I\in{[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]}^{{\omega}}
.
Then
U(p)\setminus V\subset U[I]
, and
U[I]
is Lindelöf because it is the union of
countable many Lindelöf subsets. So
U(p)\setminus V
is also Lindelöf because it is
a closed subset of the Lindelöf space
U[I]
. Thus,
there is a countable
\mathcal{W}\in{[\mathcal{V}]}^{{\omega}}

such that
U(p)\setminus U(q)\subset\bigcup\mathcal{W}
.
Thus,
U(p)\subset V\cup\bigcup\mathcal{W}
.

Since
\mathcal{V}
was arbitrary, we proved that
U(p)
is Lindelöf.
∎

Claim 2.7.4
.

X_{U}
is scattered and
I_{\leq\alpha}(X)\supset X_{\alpha}
.
Moreover, if
U
is an LLSP-cone assignment, then

I_{\alpha}(X)=X_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
{\alpha}
.

Assume that
I_{\leq\beta}(X)\supset X_{\beta}
for
{\beta}<{\alpha}
.

If
p\in X_{\alpha}
, then
U(p)\setminus\{p\}\subset X_{<{\alpha}}\subset I_{<{\alpha}}(X)

by
2.3
(
a
) and by the inductive assumption,
so
p
is isolated in
X\setminus I_{<{\alpha}}(X)
, and so

p\in I_{\alpha}(X)
.

Assume now that
U
is an LLSP-cone assignment and

I_{\beta}(X)=X_{\beta}
for
{\beta}<{\alpha}
.
If
p\in X_{\gamma}
for some
{\gamma}>{\alpha}
and

I\in[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]^{\omega}
, then

(U(q)\setminus U[I])\cap X_{<\alpha}\neq\emptyset
by
2.3
(
d
), so

p
is not isolated
in
X\setminus X_{<{\alpha}}=X\setminus I_{<{\alpha}}(X)
, so

p\notin I_{\alpha}(X)
.

So
I_{\alpha}(X)=X_{\alpha}
.
∎

Now, we prove (2).
So, assume that
X
and
U
satisfy the hypothesis of (2). Then, we show that

U
is an LLSP-cone assignment with the witnessing partition

\left\langle I_{\alpha}(X):{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
.
Property (
a
) follows from
({\dagger})
.

To check (

)
assume that

{\alpha}<{\beta}<{\delta}
,
p\in X_{\beta}
and

I\in{[U(p)\setminus\{p\}]}^{{\omega}}
.

Then
U(p)\setminus U[I]=\bigcap_{i\in I}(U(p)\setminus U(i))
,
so
G=U(p)\setminus U[I]
is an open set because
X
is a P-space, and clearly

p\in G
. Since
I_{\alpha}(X)
is dense in
X\setminus I_{<{\alpha}}(X)
, it follows
that
G\cap I_{\alpha}(X)\neq\emptyset
.

To check (
b
) assume that
\{x,y\}\in{[X]}^{2}

with
x\notin U(y)
and
y\notin U(x)
.

Then

U(x)\cap U(y)\subset\bigcup\{U(i):i\in U(x)\cap U(y)\}.

Since
U(x)\cap U(y)
is Lindelöf, there is a countable

D(x,y)\in{[U(x)\cap U(y)]}^{{\omega}}
such that

U(x)\cap U(y)\subset U[D(x,y)].

To check (
c
) assume that
\{x,y\}\in{[X]}^{2}

with
x\in U(y)
.

Then

U(x)\setminus U(y)\subset\bigcup\{U(i):i\in U(x)\setminus U(y)\}.

Since
U(x)\setminus U(y)
is Lindelöf, there is a countable

D(x,y)\in{[U(x)\setminus U(y)]}^{{\omega}}
such that

U(x)\setminus U(y)\subset U[D(x,y)].

Finally, we should show that
{\tau}_{U}={\tau}
.

Since
{\tau}
is a P-space topology,

U(p)\setminus U[I]
is open for each

p\in X
and

, and so

.

We know that
{\tau}
is 0-dimensional, so it is enough to show

.

For each

,

so there is a countable subset

Then

.

,

.

3.
Basic properties of cardinal sequences of LLSP-spaces

Reduction theorem

Let us recall that
\mathcal{C}^{P}(\alpha)

and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)

denote the class of all cardinal sequences of
length
\alpha
associated with LLSP and LCS spaces, respectively.
For any cardinal
{\kappa}
write

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{P}_{{\kappa}}({\alpha})=

\displaystyle\{s\in\mathcal{C}^{P}({\alpha}):{\kappa}=s(0)=\min\operatorname{ran}(s)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{{\kappa}}({\alpha})=

\displaystyle\{s\in\mathcal{C}({\alpha}):{\kappa}=s(0)=\min\operatorname{ran}(s)\}

In
[
JSW
, Theorem 2.1]
the following statement was proved:

Theorem.

For any ordinal
{\alpha}
we have

f\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
iff for some natural number
n
there is a
decreasing sequence
\lambda_{0}>\lambda_{1}>\dots>\lambda_{n-1}
of
infinite cardinals and there are ordinals
{\alpha}_{0},\dots,{\alpha}_{n-1}
such that

{\alpha}={\alpha}_{0}+\cdots+{\alpha}_{n-1}
and
f=f_{0}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\ f_{1}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\cdots\ \mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}f_{n-1}
with

f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
for each
i<n
.

We will prove a similar statement for LLSP-spaces as well

Theorem 3.1
.

For any ordinal
{\alpha}
we have

f\in\mathcal{C}^{P}(\alpha)
iff for some natural number
n
there is a
decreasing sequence
\lambda_{0}>\lambda_{1}>\dots>\lambda_{n-1}
of
infinite cardinals and there are ordinals
{\alpha}_{0},\dots,{\alpha}_{n-1}
such that

{\alpha}={\alpha}_{0}+\cdots+{\alpha}_{n-1}
and
f=f_{0}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\ f_{1}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\cdots\ \mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}f_{n-1}
with

f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{P}_{\lambda_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
for each
i<n
.

Proof.

We first prove that the condition is necessary, so fix
f\in\mathcal{C}^{P}(\alpha)
. Let us say that
\beta<\alpha
is a
drop point
in

f
if for all
\gamma<\beta
we have
f(\gamma)>f(\beta)
.
Clearly
f
has only finitely many, say
n,
drop points; let

\{\beta_{i}:i<n\}
enumerate all of them in increasing order. (In
particular, we have then
\beta_{0}=0.
) For each
i<n
let us set

\lambda_{i}=f(\beta_{i})
, then we clearly have

\lambda_{0}>\lambda_{1}>\dots>\lambda_{n-1}.

For each
i<n-1

let
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i+1}-\beta_{i}
be the unique ordinal such
that
\beta_{i}+\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i+1}
,
moreover define the sequence
f_{i}
on
\alpha_{i}
by the stipulation

f_{i}(\xi)=f(\beta_{i}+\xi)

for all
\xi<\alpha_{i}.

Similarly, let
\alpha_{n-1}=\alpha-\beta_{n-1}

be the unique ordinal such that
\beta_{n-1}+\alpha_{n-1}=\alpha,

and define
f_{n-1}
on
\alpha_{n-1}
by the stipulation

f_{n-1}(\xi)=f(\beta_{n-1}+\xi)

for all
\xi<\alpha_{n-1}.

Now, it is obvious that we have
f=f_{0}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\ f_{1}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\cdots\ \mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}f_{n-1}
. Moreover,
f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{P}_{\lambda_{i}}(\alpha_{i})
for each

i<n
. Indeed, writing

Y_{i}=\bigcup_{{\beta}_{i}\leq{\xi}<{\beta}_{i+1}}I_{\xi}(X)

we have
f_{i}=\operatorname{SEQ}(Y_{i})
,
and
Y_{i}
is an LLSP-space because it is a closed subset of the open
subspace
I_{<{\beta}_{i+1}}(X)
of
X
.

We shall now prove that the condition is also sufficient. In fact,
this will follow from the next lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}^{P}(\alpha)
,
g\in\mathcal{C}^{P}(\beta)
, and

f({\nu})\geq g(0)
for each
{\nu}<{\alpha}
then

f{}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}g\in\mathcal{C}^{P}({\alpha}+{\beta})
.

Indeed, given the sequences
f_{i}
for
i<n
, this lemma enables us
to inductively define for every
i=0,\dots,n-1
an LCS space
Z_{i}

with cardinal sequence
f_{0}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\cdots\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}f_{i}
because

f_{i}(0)={\lambda}_{i}<{\lambda}_{i-1}=\min\{f_{j}({\nu}):j<i,{\nu}<{\alpha}_{j}\}.

In particular, then we have
\operatorname{SEQ}(Z_{n-1})=f_{0}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\ f_{1}\mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}\cdots\ \mathop{{}^{\frown}\makebox[-3.0pt]{}}f_{n-1}
.
∎

Proof of lemma
3.2
.

Let
Y
be an LLSP space with cardinal sequence
g
and satisfying

\operatorname{I}_{\beta}(Y)=\emptyset
. Next
fix LLSP spaces

X_{y}
for all

y\in\operatorname{I}_{0}(Y)
, each having the cardinal sequence
f
and
satisfying
\operatorname{I}_{\alpha}(X_{y})=\emptyset
. Assume also that the
family
\{Y\}\bigcup\{X_{y}:y\in\operatorname{I}_{0}(Y)\}
is disjoint.

We then define
the space
Z=\left\langle Z,{\tau}\right\rangle
as follows.

Z=Y\cup\bigcup\{X_{y}:y\in I_{0}(Y)\}.

Let
U_{Y}
be an LLSP-cone assignment for
Y
,
and
U_{p}
be an LLSP-cone assignment
for
X_{p}
for

p\in\operatorname{I}_{0}(Y)

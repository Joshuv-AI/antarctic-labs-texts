Energy Detectors and Asymptotic Symmetries
Hernán A. González a {}^{\;a}, Jakob Salzer b {}^{\;b}

Abstract
We study detector operators measuring energy to a power Δ − 2 \Delta-2 at null infinity in four-dimensional gauge theories and gravity. These operators transform as conformal primaries on the celestial sphere and provide a natural basis for describing energy-flux observables in scattering processes. Using the collinear factorization of scattering amplitudes, we derive the universal leading structure of the operator product expansion. A key consequence of our analysis is the precise identification of the Δ = 2 \Delta=2 detector, the number operator. Exploiting the fact that soft charges generate symmetries of the S S -matrix, we demonstrate that the number of particles is entirely determined by the product of two soft currents: in gravity, the operator is the square of the supertranslation generator, while in Yang–Mills yields a product of S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Kac-Moody soft currents. This work establishes thus a direct link between detector observables and the soft sector of celestial holography.

In this work, we make progress on three complementary fronts:

Detector operator product expansion in quantum gravity.

Using the universal collinear factorization of scattering amplitudes, we show that the leading-order operator product expansion (OPE) of detectors measuring
E^{\Delta-2}
, i.e., energy to some power, closes within a finite set of operators. The expansion automatically generates spinning detectors, which are labeled by representations of
{\rm SO}(2)

Connection to holographic celestial OPE.

For self-dual gauge theories, we use the celestial OPE found in

Definition of generalized energy operators

Detector operators are asymptotic flux operators placed at null infinity, designed to measure the energy or other conserved quantities carried by radiation in specific directions on the celestial sphere. Originally studied in QCD
Korchemsky:1999kt
;
Sveshnikov:1995vi
, where energy flow is a standard IR and
collinear-safe observable (see the review
Moult:2025nhu
), these observables have since been intimately connected to light-ray or detector operators, extensively developed within Lorentzian CFTs. This connection was sparked by the seminal work
Hofman:2008ar
and subsequently refined in, e.g.,
Caron-Huot:2022eqs
;
Kologlu:2019mfz
;
Chang:2020qpj
.

Since detector operators measure properties of the radiation flux, we can formulate these fields directly on future null infinity in terms of the Carrollian primary fields
\phi^{(s)}_{J}(u,x^{i})
, defined intrinsically on
\mathscr{I}
, which encode the radiative degrees of freedom of fields in the bulk. We will review their definition briefly in the following.

2.1
Building detector operators from Carrollian fields

Outgoing radiation in gauge theory and gravity in asymptotically flat spacetimes admits a natural description at future null infinity

For instance, in the case of gravity one finds the gravitational shear

\mathchoice{\braket{k_{1},\ldots k_{k},\omega_{1}q(x_{1}),\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})|p_{1},\ldots p_{m}}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.08334pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.91666pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{\rm in}}}}{\braket{k_{1},\ldots k_{k},\omega_{1}q(x_{1}),\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})|p_{1},\ldots p_{m}}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.08334pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.91666pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{\rm in}}}}{\braket{k_{1},\ldots k_{k},\omega_{1}q(x_{1}),\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})|p_{1},\ldots p_{m}}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.08334pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.91666pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{\rm in}}}}{\braket{k_{1},\ldots k_{k},\omega_{1}q(x_{1}),\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})|p_{1},\ldots p_{m}}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.68059pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.08334pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.91666pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{\rm in}}}}=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega_{1}q(x_{1})+\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})+K-P)\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n},

(20)

and the remaining normalization factor depends only on the incoming states
p_{i}
.
In the squared matrix element
|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n}|^{2}
we sum over the polarizations of all detected and undetected particles

|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n}|^{2}=(\mathcal{M}_{m\to k+n})^{*}\Pi(q_{1})\ldots\Pi(q_{n})\Pi(k_{1})\ldots\Pi(k_{k})\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n},

(21)

where
\Pi(q)
denotes the projector on the physical degrees of freedom, and we suppressed the spacetime indices for clarity.
Color sums over the particles are to be included as well if necessary.

We note in passing that one can write the above in a more covariant form by introducing a detector vertex factor and delta functions that put every undetected particle on-shell. In this way, the correlator can be constructed in a standard Feynman-rule fashion in the in-in formalism to any order in perturbation theory. We refer to
Caron-Huot:2022eqs
;
Chang:2025zib
for more examples in the context of scalar theories and Yang–Mills, and to
Herrmann:2024yai
for the first computation of the leading terms of a two-point correlator in Einstein gravity and an elucidating diagrammatic expansion of (
19
).

Equation (
19
) can be easily modified to compute correlation functions of the other detectors introduced in (
9
) and (
10
).
The GEO (
8
) measures the energy deposited in both polarizations of a gluon or graviton in the final state which leads to the appearance of the projectors
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
or
\Pi_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
in (
21
). To compute correlators of the other operators, one simply needs to take into account the different polarization structures.

where
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}=\pm

we find the general formula

&\qquad\mathcal{M}^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n}\nu_{n}}_{m\rightarrow k+n}(\epsilon^{\sigma_{1}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}})^{*}\ldots(\epsilon^{\sigma_{n}}_{\mu_{n}\nu_{n}})^{*}\epsilon^{\rho_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\ldots\epsilon^{\rho_{n}}_{\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}(\mathcal{M}^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}_{m\rightarrow k+n})^{*},\end{split}

where the polarization tensors
\epsilon^{\sigma_{k}}_{\mu_{k}\nu_{k}}
are associated to the null momentum
q^{\mu}(x_{k})
and we suppressed an additional sum over the polarization of the undetected particles. The case for gauge theory proceeds in complete analogy.

3.2
Detector OPE from collinear factorization

We are now in the position to study the OPE of GEOs for Einstein gravity using (
19
). In the limit
x_{1}\rightarrow x_{2}
, the two null vectors
q^{\mu}(x_{1})
and
q^{\mu}(x_{2})
become collinear. In this limit, a gravitational scattering amplitude factorizes as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}(\ldots;\omega_{1}q_{1},\sigma_{1};\omega_{2}q_{2},\sigma_{2})=\sum_{\sigma}{\rm Split}_{-\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}_{n-1}(\ldots;K,\sigma)(\epsilon^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu})^{*}+\ldots

(24)

where
K^{\mu}=\omega_{1}q^{\mu}_{1}+\omega_{2}q^{\mu}_{2}
and the splitting function is defined in terms of the gravitational three-point function
V
as

{\rm Split}_{-\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=\frac{1}{K^{2}}(\epsilon^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}_{\sigma_{1}})^{*}(\epsilon^{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}_{\sigma_{2}})^{*}V_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\mu_{2}\nu_{2}\mu\nu}(\omega_{1}q_{1},\omega_{2}q_{2},-K)\epsilon^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma},

(25)

Equation (
21
) becomes then in the limit

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n}|^{2}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma=\pm}|{\rm Split}_{-\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})|^{2}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n-1}(\sigma^{\prime},K)|^{2}+

(26)

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma=\pm}{\rm Split}_{\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\overline{{\rm Split}}_{-\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n-1}(\ldots;K,-\sigma)(\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n-1}(\ldots;K,\sigma))^{*},

where again we have suppressed an additional sum over the undetected particles.
This equation can be regarded as decomposing the symmetric product of two spin two representations into a scalar representation in the first line and, in the last line, the symmetric traceless representations of helicity
\pm 4
.
According to the discussion in Section
2
, we can expect that the first line gives rise to the usual GEO (
8
), while the second line gives rise to contributions coming from spinning detectors (
10
).

Plugging this back into (
19
), we obtain two contributions

\lim_{x_{1}\rightarrow x_{2}}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\rm diag}+\mathbb{E}_{\rm off-diag}\,.

(27)

The diagonal contribution from the first line of (
26
) yields

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\rm diag}=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\,\omega_{2}^{\Delta_{2}-1}\,&\omega_{1}^{\Delta_{1}-1}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma=\pm}|{\rm Split}_{\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})|^{2}\frac{1}{N_{m}}\prod^{n}_{a=3}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\mathop{}\!d\omega_{a}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\omega^{\Delta_{a}-1}_{a}\\
&\SumInt_{k}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega q(x_{2})+\ldots\omega_{n}q(x_{n})+K-P)|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+n-1}|^{2}\,,\end{split}

(28)

With the change of variables
\omega_{1}=t\omega,\omega_{2}=(1-t)\omega
we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\rm diag}=\mathscr{C}\,\mathbb{E}^{(0)}_{n-1}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},x_{2};\cdots;\Delta_{n},x_{n}),

(29)

where the coefficient is

\mathscr{C}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},z_{12},\bar{z}_{12})=\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\,dt\,t^{\Delta_{2}-1}\,(1-t)^{\Delta_{1}-1}\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma=\pm}|{\rm Split}_{\sigma}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})|^{2},

(30)

and we used the fact that the splitting functions in gravity are independent of energy. Note that
t
has the interpretation of the fraction of energy transmitted to particles
1
and
2
. We will use this observation in the next section.

Similarly, from comparing with (
23
), we see that the off-diagonal term gives rise to a correlation function of detectors with spin

\mathbb{E}_{\rm off-diag}=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2},\ldots,\Delta_{n}),

(31)

with coefficient

\mathscr{C}_{\pm\mp}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},z_{12},\bar{z}_{12})=2\int^{1}_{0}dt\,t^{\Delta_{1}-1}\,(1-t)^{\Delta_{2}-1}{\rm Split}_{\pm}(-,+){\rm Split}_{\pm}(+,-).

(32)

The gravitational splitting function in our convention has the form

{\rm Split}_{+}(-,-)=\frac{\kappa}{t(1-t)}\frac{\bar{z}_{12}}{z_{12}},\qquad{\rm Split}_{+}(+,-)=\frac{\kappa(1-t)^{3}}{t}\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}},\qquad{\rm Split}_{+}(-,+)=\frac{\kappa t^{3}}{1-t}\frac{z_{12}}{\bar{z}_{12}},

(33)

with
\kappa^{2}=32\pi G
.
From (
27
) we can read off the leading term of the gravitational detector OPE

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\overset{x_{1}\rightarrow x_{2}}{\sim}c_{0}\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})+c_{+}\frac{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}{z^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(-+)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})+c_{-}\frac{z^{2}_{12}}{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})+\ldots

(34)

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}[B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}-2)+B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}+6)+B(\Delta_{1}+6,\Delta_{2}-2)]

(35a)

\displaystyle c_{\pm}

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{2}B(\Delta_{1}+2,\Delta_{2}+2),

(35b)

and the operators
\mathcal{E}^{(\pm\mp)}_{\Delta}
are the spinning energy detectors of helicity
\pm 4
introduced in (
10
). We therefore see that the OPE naturally leads one to consider GEOs and their spinning versions even if one starts out with the usual energy operator only.

In the same way, one can also compute the leading OPE terms between the other operators defined at the end of Section
2
. We find for the spinning operators with helicity four

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}}(x^{i}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\kappa^{2}B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}-2)\frac{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}{z_{12}^{2}}\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2}),

(36a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}}(x^{i}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{(-+)}_{\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\kappa^{2}\left(B(\Delta_{1}+2,\Delta_{2}+2)\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}+B(\Delta_{1}+6,\Delta_{2}-2)\frac{z^{2}_{12}}{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}\right.

(36b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}+6)\frac{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}{z^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(-+)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}\right)

and for the polarized detectors

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta_{1}}(x^{i}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\kappa^{2}B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}-2)\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2}),

(37a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta_{1}}(x^{i}_{1})\mathcal{E}^{(--)}_{\Delta_{2}}(x^{i}_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\kappa^{2}\left(B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2}+6)\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}+B(\Delta_{1}+6,\Delta_{2}-2)\mathcal{E}^{(--)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}\right.

(37b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+B(\Delta_{1}+2,\Delta_{2}+2)\frac{z^{2}_{12}}{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}+B(\Delta_{1}+2,\Delta_{2}+2)\frac{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}{z^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(-+)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}}\right)

We therefore see that the class of operators discussed in Section
2
is closed to leading order in the OPE.

We have framed the above discussion in terms of gravitational detectors. But since the leading order is completely fixed by the splitting functions through equation (
26
), it is straightforward to obtain the leading OPEs for detector operators in other theories. In particular, we can reproduce the case of Yang–Mills that was worked out already in
Chen:2021gdk
. We find

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\overset{x_{1}\rightarrow x_{2}}{\sim}\frac{c_{0}}{|z_{12}|^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}(x_{2})+\frac{c_{+}}{z^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(-+)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}(x_{2})+\frac{c_{-}}{\bar{z}^{2}_{12}}\mathcal{E}^{(+-)}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-2}(x_{2})+\ldots

(38)

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=2g^{2}_{\rm YM}C_{A}[B(\Delta_{1}-2,\Delta_{2})+B(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}-2)+B(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})]

(39a)

\displaystyle c_{\pm}

\displaystyle=2g^{2}_{\rm YM}C_{A}B(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}).

(39b)

Here, the operators
\mathcal{E}^{(\pm\mp)}_{\Delta}(x_{2})
are the spinning gluon detectors of helicity
\pm 2
, and
C_{A}
is the Casimir in the adjoint representation. In contrast to the gravitational detectors (
34
), the leading contribution to the OPE is divergent
Chen:2021gdk
, which is related to the presence of collinear singularities in Yang–Mills theory. As in the gravity case, one can also straightforwardly compute the OPE of spinning operators using equation (
23
).

In this section, we determined the leading terms of OPEs of generalized energy detectors using the universal splitting functions. However, these results will receive corrections at subleading order both in the collinear expansion and in the coupling constant. For instance, the collinear behavior of two gravitational energy detectors in the specific case of a two-point function in the state prepared by two massless scalar fields coupled to gravity was worked out to one-loop order in
Herrmann:2024yai
. The general form of these corrections should be constrained by the two-dimensional Euclidean conformal group.

We conclude this section by drawing attention to the case of self-dual gravity. This theory propagates only gravitons of, say, positive helicity, so that the only nontrivial detector is the polarized detector
\mathcal{E}^{(++)}_{\Delta}
. The OPE of this detector was determined in (
37a
). The amplitudes of this theory are known exactly and exhibit an infinite-dimensional
w_{1+\infty}
symmetry
Ball:2021tmb
that should fix the form of the subleading terms. This theory would therefore provide a very interesting toy model for studying the behavior of generalized energy correlators in more detail. We leave this to future work.

4
Detector operators and celestial holography

In the previous section, we computed the OPE between GEOs to leading order in the coupling constant and in the collinear limit. In this section, we want to make contact with operator product expansions known from the context of celestial holography.

Furthermore, conformally soft operators have been defined in the context of celestial holography, governing the low-energy behavior of massless gauge theories
Strominger:2013lka
;
Strominger:2013jfa
;
Donnay:2018neh
;
Pasterski:2021dqe
. We will argue that analogous
soft
energy operators can be considered.

4.1
Detector OPE from celestial OPE

Celestial OPEs have been derived from various points of view: from the collinear limits of scattering amplitudes
Fan:2019emx
;
Fotopoulos:2019vac
; from conformally soft theorems
Pate:2019lpp
; or just from the requirements of Poincaré invariance
Himwich:2021dau
.
The OPE between two outgoing graviton operators with positive helicity
O^{(2)}_{+}

(40)

4.2
The emergence of the number operator

As noted before, conservation of energy in a three-point vertex can be kinematically interpreted as a one-to-two splitting process, each of them carrying energy
t\omega
and
(1-t)\omega
, respectively.
In these variables, the soft limit is captured by the integration region
t\sim 0
(or, equivalently,
t\sim 1
). At the level of the OPE coefficient
\mathscr{C}
in (
30
), its leading singularity comes from

\left.\mathscr{C}\right|_{t\to 0}\propto\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{2(2-s)}}\frac{1}{\Delta-2}+\cdots\,,

(44)

where
s=2
for gravitons and
s=1
for gluons. The interesting point is that for any helicity
s
the soft limit is dominated by the operator detector with conformal dimension
\Delta=2
. This case corresponds to a particle-counting detector. More precisely, we can define the operator

\mathcal{N}(z,\bar{z})=\lim_{\Delta\to 2}(\Delta-2)\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\,.

(45)

Taking this limit directly in the detector OPEs (
34
) and (
38
) we find

\mathcal{N}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\sim\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{|z_{12}|^{2(2-s)}}\mathcal{E}_{\Delta+2(s-1)}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})

(46)

where
g_{2}=\kappa
in Einstein gravity and
g_{1}^{2}=2g^{2}_{\rm YM}C_{A}
for pure Yang-Mills theory.

This operator will play an important role in the IR sector of celestial holography. In the next section, we will evaluate its expectation value. Notably, the application of the conservation of the asymptotic soft charge
Strominger:2013jfa
will show that
\mathcal{N}
is made out of a product of conformally soft currents.

Before examining
\mathcal{N}
more closely, we note that the OPE coefficients for gauge theory and gravity have poles at any integer value
\Delta\leq 2
. In analogy with the number operator (
45
) we can therefore formally define

\mathcal{N}^{(n)}(x^{i})=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}_{2-n+\epsilon}(x^{i}).

(47)

We comment on the interpretation of this particular set of operators in the concluding remarks in Section
6
.

5
Detector correlators and soft properties

The OPE of GEOs, both in Yang–Mills theory (
38
) and gravity (
34
), has poles when either detector has weight
\Delta=2
. Naively, the corresponding operators
\mathcal{E}_{\Delta=2}
weight every outgoing state by
\omega^{0}
and thus can be viewed as counting the number of outgoing particles. In the context of QCD, this operator was carefully studied in
Chang:2025zib
. Here, we want to show that this operator can also be understood using the asymptotic symmetries of gauge theories in asymptotically flat spacetimes. We will first consider the case of gravity before discussing the analogous situation in Yang–Mills theory.

5.1
The
\Delta=2
operator in gravity

Consider the one-point function of a gravitational GEO in a generic scattering state. Using equation (
19
), we can write this as

\mathbb{E}(\Delta,x^{i})=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\mathop{}\!d\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\omega^{\Delta-1}\SumInt_{k}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega q(x^{i})+K-P)|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+1}|^{2}.

(48)

Projecting on the pole at
\Delta=2
of this correlator we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}(\Delta,x^{i})=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!d\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(\omega)\SumInt_{k}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega q(x^{i})+K-P)\omega^{2}|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow k+1}|^{2},

where we used the representation

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\epsilon|x|^{\epsilon-1}=2\delta(x).

(57)

This appearance of the delta function shows that we focus on the sector where the graviton captured by the detector operator is soft and we can project onto the soft pole of the scattering amplitude.
The soft theorem for an outgoing graviton of polarization tensor
\epsilon_{\mu\nu}
reads

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow n+1}=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left[\sum_{k\in{\rm out}}\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}_{k}p^{\nu}_{k}}{p_{k}\cdot q}-\sum_{k\in{\rm in}}\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}_{k}p^{\nu}_{k}}{p_{k}\cdot q}\right]\mathcal{M}_{m+n}.

(58)

Squaring and summing over the polarizations of the outgoing gravitons since the detector is sensitive only to the sum, we find

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\omega^{2}|\mathcal{M}_{m\rightarrow n+1}|^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left[\sum_{i,j\in{\rm in}}-2\sum_{i\in{\rm out},j\in{\rm in}}+\sum_{i,j\in{\rm out}}\right]\omega_{i}\omega_{j}K^{(2)}(x_{i},x_{j},x)|\mathcal{M}_{m+n}|^{2},

(59)

where

K^{(s)}(x_{i},x_{j},x)=\frac{(q_{i}\cdot q_{j})^{s}}{q_{i}\cdot q\,q_{j}\cdot q}=\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2s}}{|z-z_{1}|^{2}|z-z_{2}|^{2}},\qquad q_{i,j}=q(x_{i,j}),q=q(x),

(60)

is the eikonal kernel,
and we used the symmetry of the kernel to simplify the result. We therefore obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)=\frac{\kappa^{2}(2\pi)^{4}}{4N_{m}}\SumInt_{k}\delta^{(4)}(K-P)\left[\sum_{i,j\in{\rm in}}-2\!\!\!\sum_{i\in{\rm out},j\in{\rm in}}\!\!\!+\sum_{i,j\in{\rm out}}\right]\omega_{i}\omega_{j}K(x_{i},x_{j},x)|\mathcal{M}_{m+n}|^{2}.

Note that we can equivalently write this as

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4N_{m}}\int d^{2}y_{1}d^{2}y_{2}K(y_{1},y_{2},x)

(69)

\displaystyle\bra{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}\left[(\mathcal{E}(y_{1})-\mathcal{E}^{\rm in}(y_{1}))(\mathcal{E}(y_{2})-\mathcal{E}^{\rm in}(y_{2}))\right]\ket{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}},

where
\mathcal{E}^{\rm in}(x)
denotes energy detectors inserted at past null infinity acting directly on the in-states.

We can now make direct contact with the extensive literature on asymptotic symmetries. On future null infinity, the charge for supertranslations
Q[\mathcal{T}]
is

Q[\mathcal{T}]=\int d^{2}x\mathcal{T}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}(x)-q_{\rm soft}(x)\right),

(70)

parametrized by
\mathcal{T}(x^{i})
. It is given by the outgoing (hard) energy flux measured by
\mathcal{E}(x)\equiv\mathcal{E}_{\Delta=3}(x)
together with a soft piece defined as
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He:2014laa

q_{\rm soft}(x)=\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\int du\,(\partial^{2}_{z}\partial_{u}C_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}+\partial^{2}_{\bar{z}}\partial_{u}C_{zz}).

(71)

Here,
C_{zz}
is the shear that carries the two polarizations of the gravitational field.
5
5

5

We neglect an additional term in the charge that ensures the correct transformation under superrotation if one considers the extended BMS group
Barnich:2011mi
;
Donnay:2021wrk
;
Campiglia:2021bap
.
In the notation of Section
2
, it corresponds to the Carrollian field with spin
s=2
. A similar expression for the charge can be obtained in terms of quantities defined on past null infinity. Conservation of the supertranslation charge then states that these two expressions are equal. In other words, the energy flux is conserved at every angle due to soft contributions. In terms of the integrands, we have therefore

\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{E}^{\rm out}(x)-\mathcal{E}^{\rm in}(x))=q_{\rm soft}(x)-q^{\rm in}_{\rm soft}(x).

(72)

It is convenient to rewrite the right-hand side in terms of the supertranslation current introduced in
Strominger:2013jfa
as

\mathcal{E}^{\rm out}(x)-\mathcal{E}^{\rm in}(x)=\frac{\partial_{\bar{z}}P_{z}}{\pi}.

(73)

We have therefore the compact relation

\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4\pi^{2}N_{m}}\int d^{2}y_{1}d^{2}y_{2}K^{(2)}(y_{1},y_{2},x)\bra{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}\partial_{\bar{z}}P_{z}(y_{1})\partial_{z}P_{\bar{z}}(y_{2})\ket{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}},

(74)

where we used
\partial_{\bar{z}}P_{z}=\partial_{z}P_{\bar{z}}
due to the boundary conditions obeyed by the gravitational field close to spatial infinity
Strominger:2013jfa
. Note that this identity holds to all orders in perturbation theory since the leading soft-graviton theorem (
73
) does not receive loop-corrections
Bern:2014oka
.

We now make direct contact with the discussion in Section
4.2
. The soft current
P_{z}
is related to the celestial graviton operator by
Pate:2019lpp

P_{z}=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\lim_{\Delta\to 1}(\Delta-1)\partial_{\bar{z}}O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta).

(75)

With this relation and the distributional identity

\partial^{2}_{\bar{z}_{1}}\partial^{2}_{z_{2}}K^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2},x)=4\frac{(z_{1}-z)(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z})}{(\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{z})^{3}(z_{2}-z)^{3}}+\pi^{2}\delta^{(2)}(x-x_{1})\delta^{(2)}(x-x_{2}),

(76)

we find for (
74
)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)=\lim_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\to 1}(\Delta_{1}-1)(\Delta_{2}-1)\Big[\frac{1}{N_{m}}\bra{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{1},x)O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{2},x)\ket{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}N_{m}}\int d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}\frac{(z_{1}-z)(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z})}{(\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{z})^{3}(z_{2}-z)^{3}}\bra{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{1},x_{1})O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{2},x_{2})\ket{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}\Big].\end{split}

(77)

We recognize the terms in the second line as shadow transforms of the respective operators where we use the normalization and conventions of
Osborn:2012vt
. Note that the shadow transform of
O^{(2)}_{\pm}(\Delta_{1}=1,x)
has the same quantum numbers as
O^{(2)}_{\mp}(\Delta_{1}=1,x)
. As shown in detail in
Pasterski:2021fjn
;
Pasterski:2021dqe
, conformally soft operators are organized in a special multiplet of the Lorentz group such that these operators are indeed just shadow transforms of one another. In other words, the shadow transforms the soft factor for an outgoing positive helicity graviton in (
58
) into the one for a negative helicity graviton.
The second term in (
77
) is therefore proportional to the first. Taking the normalization into account, we arrive at our final result:

\begin{split}\lim_{\Delta\rightarrow 2}(\Delta-2)\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)=&\lim_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\to 1}(\Delta_{1}-1)(\Delta_{2}-1)\\
&\frac{1}{N_{m}}\bra{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{1},x)O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{2},x)+O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{1},x)O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{2},x)\ket{p_{1}\ldots p_{m}}.\end{split}

(78)

This allows us to read off the operator identity

\mathcal{N}=\lim_{\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}\to 1}(\Delta_{1}-1)(\Delta_{2}-1)\left(O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{1})O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{2})+O^{(2)}_{-}(\Delta_{1})O^{(2)}_{+}(\Delta_{2})\right).

(79)

Let us note that this representation of the number operator allows for independent consistency checks.

6

Formally, this result can also be derived from the celestial representation (
15
) of the GEO by contour integration. However, in the absence of a complete understanding of the pole structure of celestial operators, this argument remains heuristic.
For instance, in the self-dual case, one can compute independently the OPE of the number operator and the detector itself. In fact, using the soft theorem in the positive (negative) helicity sector,
we holographically recover the OPE of the number operator (

). A completely analogous treatment applies to self-dual YM, as we will see below.

5.2
The
\Delta=2
operator in gauge theory

We now turn to the analogous situation in Yang–Mills theory. The
\Delta=2
-pole of the GEO has been studied in great detail in
Chang:2025zib
. In contrast to the former work that analyzed the one-point function in the background of a state created by a local, gauge-invariant operator, we will again use a scattering state. As before, this allows us to make contact with operators defined in the context of celestial holography and asymptotic symmetries.

The one-point detector observable in pure Yang-Mills theory measures the distribution of color flux radiated in a given direction.
In the celestial representation, the limit
\Delta\to 2
isolates the contribution associated with the number of particles.
As before, we have the one-point function for a general GEO,

\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}\equiv\langle m|\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}|m\rangle=\frac{1}{N_{m}}\SumInt_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\;\omega^{\Delta-1}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(\omega q+K-P)\Big|\mathcal{M}^{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}\,a_{m+1}\cdots a_{m+n+1}}_{m\to n+1}\Big|^{2}.

(80)

The states
|m\rangle
denote

|m\rangle=|(p_{1},\sigma_{1},a_{1})\cdots(p_{m},\sigma_{m},a_{m})\rangle\,,

an
m-
particle state with helicities
\sigma_{k}
, color denoted by
a_{k}
and total momentum
P
. Unless necessary, we omit color and helicity index from the states. For clarity, the amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}\,a_{m}\cdots a_{m+n}}_{m\to n}
carries adjoint color indices on each external gluon.

As in the gravity case, the
\Delta\to 2
limit is controlled by interactions of soft quanta. Therefore, the amplitude with one additional soft emission is controlled by the soft-gluon theorem,

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\mathcal{M}^{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}\,a_{m+1}\cdots a_{m+n+1}}_{m\to n+1}=\frac{g_{\rm YM}}{\omega}\left[\sum_{k\in\text{out}}\frac{p_{k}\!\cdot\!\varepsilon}{p_{k}\!\cdot\!q}\,(T^{a}_{k})_{a_{k},b}-\sum_{k\in\text{in}}\frac{p_{k}\!\cdot\!\varepsilon}{p_{k}\!\cdot\!q}\,(T^{a}_{k})_{a_{k},b}\right]\mathcal{M}^{a_{1}\cdots b\cdots a_{m+n}}_{m\to n}\,.

(81)

Here we have introduced the color operator
T^{a}_{k}
whose action on gluons is

(T^{a}_{k})_{a_{k},b}=if^{aba_{k}}.

(82)

Squaring the amplitude gives the soft limit of the probability density

|\mathcal{M}|^{2}_{\text{soft}}=\left(\sum_{i,j\in\text{in}}-\sum_{i\in\text{in},j\in\text{out}}\{\cdot,\cdot\}+\sum_{i,j\in\text{out}}\right)g_{\rm YM}^{2}\,K^{(1)}(x_{i},x_{j},x)\,T_{i}\!\cdot\!T_{j}\,\,\big|\mathcal{M}_{m\to n}\big|^{2}\,,

(83)

where repeated color indices are contracted with
\delta^{ab}
, and the middle term includes an anticommutator for the color operators.
We have suppressed explicit color indices on the amplitude for brevity, and we see again the appearance of the eikonal kernel (
60
).
Using manipulations similar to those in the previous subsection, the one-point detector becomes

\lim_{\Delta\to 2}(\Delta-2)\,\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}=g_{\rm YM}^{2}\int d^{2}y_{1}\,d^{2}y_{2}\;K^{(1)}(y_{1},y_{2},x)\,\Big\langle m\Big|(N_{\text{out}}^{a}(y_{1})\,-N_{\text{in}}^{a}(y_{1}))(N_{\text{out}}^{a}(y_{2})\,-N_{\text{in}}^{a}(y_{2}))\Big|m\Big\rangle.

(84)

where we have identified the color interference number detector for gluons as
Chang:2025zib

N^{a}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int^{\infty}_{0}d\omega\,\omega\sum_{\sigma=\pm}if^{abc}a^{\dagger b}_{\sigma}(\omega,y)a^{c}_{\sigma}(\omega,y)\,.

(85)

The above operator acts on
m
-particle states as the sum of the color operator
T^{a}_{k}
acting on each particle
Gonzo:2019fai
,

N^{a}(y)|m\rangle=\sum^{m}_{k=1}\delta^{(2)}(y-z_{k})T^{a}_{k}\,|m\rangle.

(86)

Equation (
84
) expresses the one-point detector observable as a bilocal correlator of in/out color fluxes,
N_{\text{in/out}}^{a}(y)
, weighted by the kernel
K^{(1)}
.

As in gravity, a classical phase space analysis of Yang–Mills shows that the theory is invariant under an infinite-dimensional symmetry algebra given by color charge for every point of the sphere
Strominger:2013lka
;
He:2015zea
;
He:2020ifr
. The corresponding conservation law reads

2\pi i(N^{a}_{\rm out}-N^{a}_{\rm in})=\partial_{z}J^{a}_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{\bar{z}}J^{a}_{z}\,,

(87)

where we have introduced the soft current defined in
He:2015zea

J_{i}\equiv\frac{4\pi}{g^{2}_{\rm YM}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}duF^{(0)}_{ui}-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dvF^{(0)}_{vi}\right)\,.

(88)

Here,
F^{(0)}_{ui}
and
F^{(0)}_{vi}
are the leading field strength components close to future and past null infinity, respectively.

Using this conservation law, we can rewrite

\begin{split}\lim_{\Delta\to 2}(\Delta-2)\,\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}(x)=-\frac{g^{2}_{\rm YM}}{4\pi^{2}}\int d^{2}y_{1}\,&d^{2}y_{2}\;K^{(1)}(y_{1},y_{2},x)\\
\times&\langle m\Big|\Big(\partial_{\bar{z}}J^{a}_{z}(y_{1})+\partial_{z}J^{a}_{\bar{z}}(y_{1})\Big)\Big(\partial_{\bar{z}}J^{a}_{z}(y_{2})+\partial_{z}J^{a}_{\bar{z}}(y_{2})\Big)\Big|m\Big\rangle.\end{split}

(89)

This result can be simplified using the distributional identities

\begin{split}\bar{\partial}_{1}\partial_{2}K^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},x)&=-\pi^{2}\delta^{(2)}(z-z_{1})\delta^{(2)}(z-z_{2})-\frac{1}{(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{1})^{2}(z-z_{2})^{2}}\,,\\
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}K^{(1)}(x_{1},x_{2},x)&=\frac{\pi}{(z-z_{1})^{2}}\delta^{(2)}(z-z_{2})+\frac{\pi}{(z-z_{2})^{2}}\delta^{(2)}(z-z_{1})\,,\end{split}

(90)

and analogous expression for
\bar{\partial}_{1}\bar{\partial}_{2}K^{(1)}
obtained by complex conjugation. As before, the rational contributions yield the shadow transforms of the soft currents. We therefore find the final result

\lim_{\Delta\to 2}(\Delta-2)\,\mathbb{E}_{\Delta}=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{\rm YM}^{2}\Big\langle m\Big|(-\widetilde{J^{a}_{\bar{z}}}+J^{a}_{z})(-\widetilde{J^{a}_{z}}+J^{a}_{\bar{z}})\Big|m\Big\rangle.

(91)

We have chosen to keep the shadow transforms explicit, as the precise definition of the associated symmetry current in non-Abelian gauge theories remains subtle and is not essential for the present analysis
He:2015zea
;
Himwich:2025bza
. Again, we obtain a bilinear expression in direct analogy with the structure found in gravity. In particular, the soft energy operator appears as the natural composite of two conformally soft currents.

We can once more perform an independent check of this bilinear formula in the self-dual Yang-Mills theory. In this case, the colored celestial OPE
Pate:2019lpp
for gluons of definite helicity completely determines the product expansion
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}
. Using this OPE, one directly reproduces (
46
) for
s=1
.

Finally, we note that, in contrast to gravity, the leading soft-gluon theorem (
81
) receives loop corrections
Catani:2000pi
. We can therefore expect that the above relation will also receive corrections at the loop level, which implies an IR renormalization of the soft currents.

6
Concluding remarks

We have combined the study of detector operators with recent insights into asymptotic symmetries and the flat space holography program in general, initiating a reorganization of infrared physics in massless gauge theory and gravity through the language of detectors.

We showed that both the Carrollian and the celestial formulation of massless scattering are useful in this regard: the former to construct detector operators using conformal Carrollian primary fields that are tailored to the structure of null infinity; the latter to examine the soft properties of detector operators.

We focused on two universal sectors of detector operators. The first is governed by collinear physics: at leading order, the detector OPE is completely fixed by the universal splitting functions, whose structure determines how scalar and spinning detectors combine. The second emerges from symmetry considerations. In fact, the pole structure of the OPE singles out the

\Delta=2
detector, which plays a distinguished role as the operator counting the number of outgoing particles.

While this detector itself is not finite, its pole can be realized as a bilinear of soft currents. This means that particle number is not an independent observable, but one that is fixed entirely by the symmetries acting at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. One can regard this as another manifestation of the relation between the gravitational memory effect and BMS transformations
Strominger:2014pwa
.
More speculatively, it is also reminiscent of a Sugawara-type construction, where composite operators are determined by the currents that generate the symmetry algebra (for application to celestial holography see
Fan:2020xjj
). This suggests that the soft sector may admit a decoupled CFT organization (see
Kapec:2021eug
;
Nguyen:2021ydb
;
Gonzalez:2021dxw
;
Magnea:2021fvy
;
Donnay:2023kvm
for related ideas), with detector operators at special values of

The observation made in

In our formulation, these intersections naturally correspond to the composite nature of detector operators in terms of soft currents, suggesting that the IR sector of gauge theory and gravity organizes itself through the algebra of detectors at
\mathscr{I}^{+}

There are several directions for further investigation.

The points

Another obvious extension is to explore other detectors such as those sensitive to the angular momentum of the outgoing radiation or to the wave form of the radiation

In this work, we took the first steps to show that detector operators provide a direct realization of asymptotic symmetries. Therefore, they are not only compatible with the celestial holography program,
but it is a natural language for it. From a broader perspective, our results give a unified framework that connects collider physics, light-ray operators, and the operator algebra of celestial fields at null infinity.

Appendix A
Conventions

In the following we will give a summary of our conventions and other useful relations. We will follow
Donnay:2022wvx
.

A.1
Coordinates and polarization tensors

The metric of
(d+2)
-dimensional Minkowski space is given by

\mathop{}\!ds^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathop{}\!dX^{\mu}\mathop{}\!dX^{\nu},

(92)

with
\eta_{\mu\nu}
mostly plus.
Null vectors are parametrized by

p^{\mu}(\omega,x^{i})=\omega q^{\mu}(x^{i}),

(93)

where
q^{\mu}(x^{i})
is the reference null direction

q^{\mu}(x^{i})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1+x^{2},2x^{i},1-x^{2}\right),\qquad q^{\mu}(x^{i})\cdot q^{\mu}(x^{j})=-|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}\qquad i=1,\ldots d,

(94)

and
x^{i}
is a cartesian coordinate on the
d
-dimensional plane.
The null vector

n^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1,0,-1\right),

(95)

obeys
q\cdot n=-1
and can be used to write Minkowski space in the coordinates

X^{\mu}=un^{\mu}+rq^{\mu}(x^{i})

(96)

with metric

\mathop{}\!ds^{2}=-2\mathop{}\!du\mathop{}\!dr+2r^{2}\delta_{ij}\mathop{}\!dx^{i}\mathop{}\!dx^{j}.

(97)

The celestial sphere is therefore conformally mapped to a plane.

For the case of
d=2
, that is of main interest for this paper, we will often employ complex coordinates
z=x^{1}+ix^{2}
in which the metric reads

\mathop{}\!ds^{2}=-2\mathop{}\!du\mathop{}\!dr+2r^{2}\mathop{}\!dz\mathop{}\!d\bar{z}.

(98)

We note that we take our measure as
d^{2}x=\mathop{}\!dx_{1}\mathop{}\!dx_{2}
, even if the integrand is written in terms of complex coordinates.

We introduce the polarization vectors

\varepsilon^{\mu}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\varepsilon^{\mu}_{1}\mp i\varepsilon^{\mu}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}q^{\mu}\mp i\partial_{2}q^{\mu}).

(99)

In complex coordinates, this takes the simple form
\varepsilon^{\mu}_{+}=\partial_{z}q^{\mu}
and its complex conjugate
\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}=\partial_{\bar{z}}q^{\mu}
.
The projector onto the physical polarizations is given by

\Pi^{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon^{\mu}_{+}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{-}+\varepsilon^{\mu}_{-}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{+}.

(100)

Similarly, we define the polarization tensors for spin two field

\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}=\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{11}\mp\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{12}=\varepsilon^{\mu}_{\pm}\varepsilon^{\nu}_{\pm}.

(101)

The polarization sum for spin two fields is given by

\Pi^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\sum_{i,j}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{ij}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Pi^{\mu\alpha}\Pi^{\nu\beta}+\Pi^{\mu\beta}\Pi^{\nu\alpha}-\Pi^{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\alpha\beta}\right).

(102)

A.2
From bulk fields to boundary fields

The canonical commutation relations for ladder operators of momentum
p_{i}=\omega_{i}q^{\mu}(x_{i})
are given by

[a_{I}(p_{1}),a^{\dagger}_{J}(p_{2})]=(2\pi)^{3}2p^{0}_{1}\delta^{3}(p_{1}-p_{2})\delta_{IJ}=(2\pi)^{3}\omega^{-1}\delta(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\delta^{(2)}(x^{i}_{1}-x^{i}_{2})\delta_{IJ},

(103)

with additional Kronecker deltas for colored particles.
Accordingly, the free field expansion of a spin-s field in four-dimensional Minkowski space in De Donder gauge is given by

\begin{split}\Phi^{(s)}_{M}(X)&=K_{(s)}\sum_{J}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{0}}\left((\varepsilon^{J}_{M}(p))^{*}a^{(s)}_{J}(p)e^{ip\cdot X}+\varepsilon^{J}_{M}(p)(a^{(s)}_{J}(p))^{\dagger}e^{-ip\cdot X}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{J}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathop{}\!d\omega\,\omega\int\mathop{}\!d^{2}y\,\left((\varepsilon^{J}_{M})^{*}a^{(s)}_{J}e^{i\omega q(y)\cdot X}+\varepsilon^{J}_{M}(a^{(s)}_{J})^{\dagger}e^{-i\omega q(y)\cdot X}\right)\end{split}

(104)

where
M
denotes the multiindex
M=\{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{s}\}
and
J
denotes a multiindex for the polarization of the field. For gauge fields this is just
J=\{i\}
, while for gravitons one has
J=\{ij\}
.
The constant
K_{(s)}
depends on the coupling constant, e.g.,
K_{(1)}=e
for a photon or
K_{(2)}=\sqrt{32\pi G\hbar}
for a graviton.
Using the relation

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int\mathop{}\!d^{d}yf(y)e^{\pm i\omega r|x-y|^{2}}\approx\frac{e^{\pm i\pi d/4}}{2^{d}}\left(\frac{\pi}{\omega r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}f(x)

(105)

and switching to complex coordinates we can extract the boundary field as

\phi^{(s)}_{z\ldots z}(u,x^{i})\mathop{}\!dz\otimes\ldots\mathop{}\!dz+\phi^{(s)}_{\bar{z}\ldots\bar{z}}(u,x^{i})\mathop{}\!d\bar{z}\otimes\ldots\mathop{}\!d\bar{z}=\lim_{r\to\infty}(r^{1-s}\Phi^{(s)}_{\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{s}}\mathop{}\!dX^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\mathop{}\!dX^{\mu_{s}})|_{r=const}.

(106)

One finds the boundary operator

\phi^{(s)}_{z\ldots z}(u,x^{i})=-i\frac{K_{(s)}}{8\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathop{}\!d\omega\left(a^{(s)}_{+}(\omega,x^{i})e^{-i\omega u}+(a^{(s)}_{-}(\omega,x^{i}))^{\dagger}e^{i\omega u}\right)

(107)

and its complex conjugate.

Under Poincaré transformations, this field transforms as

\begin{split}\left[H,\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=i\partial_{u}\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\\
\left[P_{i},\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=i\partial_{i}\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\\
\left[J_{ij},\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=i(-x_{i}\partial_{j}+x_{j}\partial_{i}-i\Sigma^{(s)}_{ij})\,\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\\

\left[K,\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=ix^{2}\partial_{u}\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\\
\left[K_{i},\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=i(-2x_{i}\Delta-2ix^{j}\Sigma^{(s)}_{ij}-2x_{i}u\partial_{u}-2x_{i}x^{j}\partial_{j}+x^{j}x_{j}\partial_{i})\,\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\\
\left[B_{i},\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\right]&=ix_{i}\partial_{u}\phi^{(s)}_{\Delta}(u,x^{i})\,,\end{split}

(108)

with
\Delta=1
(in general

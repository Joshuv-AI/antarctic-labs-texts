Abstract
. Let ℳ g {\mathcal{M}}_{g} be the moduli space of compact connected Riemann surfaces of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 and let ℳ g ^ \widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}} be its Deligne-Mumford compactification, which is stratified by the topological type of the stable Riemann surfaces. We consider the equisymmetric loci in ℳ g {\mathcal{M}}_{g} corresponding to Riemann surfaces whose automorphism group is abelian and determine the topological type of the maximal dimension strata at their boundary. For the particular cases of the hyperelliptic and the cyclic p p -gonal actions, we describe all the topological strata at the boundary in terms of trees with a fixed number of edges.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
be the moduli space of compact connected Riemann surfaces of genus
g\geq 2
up to biholomorphism or, equivalently, the space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
up to isometry, or the space of smooth projective curves of genus
g\geq 2
defined over
{\mathbb{C}}
. As a space, it is easier to consider the Teichmüller space
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
, i.e., the space of
marked
hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
, since it is homoeomorphic to an open ball of
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3}
. The mapping class group
{\rm Mod}_{g}
acts on
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
by changing the marking of a surface, and therefore the quotient
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}/{\rm Mod}_{g}
can be identified to
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
This action is properly discontinuously and thus
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is an orbifold whose singular points (when
g>2
) are those Riemann surfaces which admit non-trivial automorphisms.
This subset is called the
branch locus
and is denoted
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
. It can be stratified in a natural way according to the topological action of the isometry group of the points in
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
, the strata are called
equisymmetric strata
or
loci
(see Section
2.1
). This stratification is the object of intense study, both determining all the strata and studying topological properties as the connectivity of
{\mathcal{B}}_{g}
. For instance Broughton
[
6
]
provides the
classification of topological actions in genus 2 and 3 and Kimura
[
16
]
that in genus 4. In a series of papers, Costa, Izquierdo and Bartolini (see for instance
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
) study the orbifold structure of moduli space and the connectivity of its branched locus.

On the other hand, Teichmüller space can be augmented by adding marked stable Riemann surfaces (
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
, Section
2.3
). The resulting space
\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}_{g}}
is not compact, but its quotient
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}
by the mapping class group is compact and coincides with the Deligne-Mumford compactification of moduli space, that is, the set of stable curves, i.e. curves which have only nodes as singularities and with finite group of automorphisms (
[
12
]
, see
[
15
]
for a detailed exposition on this). Intuitively, this compactification is saying that the only way of going to the infinity at moduli space is that the length of a multicurve (a subset of disjoint simple closed curves) is going to zero.

The previous construction provides a stratification to the boundary of moduli space
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}=\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\setminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, each strata consisting on a subset of stable Riemann surfaces with the same topological type.
We will refer to this stratification as the topological stratification of
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and to its strata as
topological strata
. It can be noticed that the topological type of a stable Riemann surface is captured by its associated weighted stable graph.

A natural question to ask is which topological strata of
\partial{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
appear at the boundary of a given equisymmetric locus
\mathcal{E}
. If
X_{n}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{E}
going to the boundary, there is a curve whose length along the sequence is going to 0. Since
X_{n}
has non-trivial isometries, there is actually an equivariant multicurve whose length is going to zero. Quotienting by the isometry group, we obtain a curve going to zero along the sequence of quotient orbifolds
{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
. Now, this process can be reversed: starting with a curve in the quotient orbifold and making its length going to zero produces an equivariant multicurve in
X_{n}
with length going to zero. Decreasing to 0 the length of a multicurve is always possible provided that all the components of the complement of the multicurve are hyperbolic. These multicurves are called
admissible
.
Summing up, to find the topological strata in the boundary of an equisymmetric stratum
\mathcal{E}
reduces to find the preimage of admissible multicurves under a branched covering.
In
[
13
]
, we addressed this question and described a procedure to solve it. Also in that paper and in
[
14
]
, we applied this method to find all the topological strata at the boundary of the pyramidal equisymmetric locus.
The case of the 1-complex dimensional equisymmetric strata for a family uniformized by a Fuchsian group of signature
s=(2,2,2,m),m\geq 3
is studied in
[
11
]
. In
[
10
]
it is shown that the completion of the 3-gonal equisymmetric space in
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}
is connected for
g\geq 5
.
In recent papers (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
) Broughton, Costa and Izquierdo have studied the topology of the one-dimensional equisymmetric loci, which are punctured Riemann surfaces. Some of these punctures correspond to limiting stable, nodal surfaces in
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}
, i.e., the topological strata at the boundary of the equisymmetric locus.
An algebraic stack treatment of boundary of loci of curves can be seen in
[
17
]
.

In this paper we focus on the equisymmetric strata corresponding to abelian actions.
For the hyperelliptic and the
p
-gonal actions, we provide easy descriptions of the topological strata at the boundary of the corresponding equisymmetric loci in terms of trees with a bounded number of edges. For any abelian action, we determine the topological strata of maximal dimension at the boundary of the corresponding equisymmetric locus.

The structure of this paper is the following:
In Section
3
we recall the main result in
[
13
]
: given a topological action
(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
on a surface
S

and an admissible multicurve
\Sigma
in
{\mathcal{O}}=S/G
, the dual graph of its preimage
p^{-1}(\Sigma)
is determined.
A first important simplification of this result occurs when the group
G
is abelian, since we can work with homology instead homotopy. This will be seen in Section
4.2
.
A second simplification occurs when the orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
is topologically a sphere, this case is studied in Section
4.1
.
Both simplifications are present in the
p
-gonal and hyperelliptic equisymmetric strata. We will study these cases in sections
5
and
6
. In particular, we find all the topological strata at the boundary of the equisymmetric loci corresponding to all the cyclic
p
-gonal actions up to genus 4.
Finally, in section
7
, we will study the case of
G
any abelian group and the multicurve
\Sigma
consisting on a single curve. This corresponds to finding the maximal dimension topological strata in the boundary of the equisymmetric locus.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Equisymmetric loci of moduli space

We recall definitions and fix notation. For a general reference in this subject see
[
5
]
.
Consider an action of a finite group
G
on a closed surface
S
, that is, a monomorphism
\iota\colon G\to{\rm Hom}^{+}(S)
into the group of preserving orientation homeomorphisms of
S
. It has an associated
regular branched covering
p\colon S\to{\mathcal{O}}
over the set of orbits
{\mathcal{O}}
, and an associated epimorphism

\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
that assigns to a loop
\alpha
in
{\mathcal{O}}
the deck transformation that sends one end of a lift of
\alpha
to the other one.
The space
{\mathcal{O}}
is a closed orbifold whose singular locus consists on a finite number of cone points.

The Euler characteristics of
S
and
{\mathcal{O}}
are related by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula
\chi(S)=|G|\chi({\mathcal{O}})
. We recall that Euler characteristic of an orbifold of signature
(g,c;m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
is
\chi({\mathcal{O}})=2-2g-c-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}})
, where
g
is the genus of
{\mathcal{O}}
,
c
the number of its boundary components and
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
are the orders of its cone points. We remark that in the above situation, that is, if
{\mathcal{O}}
is the quotient of a closed surface by the action of a group, the orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
does not have boundary components. Nevertheless, along the paper we will need to use orbifolds with boundary and to compute their Euler characteristic.

Any epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
whose kernel is the fundamental group of a surface
S
(
surface kernel epimorphism
) determines an action of
G
on
S
. We denote this action by
(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
.

Two actions

\iota\colon G\to{\rm Hom}^{+}(S)
and ,
\iota^{\prime}\colon G\to{\rm Hom}^{+}(S^{\prime})
, are
topologically equivalent
if there is an automorphism
\omega\in Aut(G)
and a homeomorphism
h\colon S\to S^{\prime}
such that

\iota^{\prime}(\omega(g))=h\circ\iota(g)\circ h^{-1}
for all
g\in G
.

The
equisymmetric stratum
or
locus
determined by the topological class of action represented by
(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
is the subset
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
of points
X\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
such that the action of
Aut^{+}(X)
is topologically equivalent to the action
(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
.

Broughton (
[
6
]
) determined all the topological actions in surfaces
S
of genera
g=2,3
and Kimura (
[
16
]
) determined those of genus
g=4
. We will use some of these topological actions to exemplify our results.

2.2.
Admissible multicurves and weighted graphs.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orientable 2-orbifold, i.e., one of signature
(g,c;m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
, where
g
is the genus,
c
is the number of connected components and
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
are the orders of the cone points.

Definition.
Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold with
\chi({\mathcal{O}})<0
. An
(admissible) multicurve
in
{\mathcal{O}}
is a collection
\Sigma=\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\}
of simple closed curves or simple arcs joining pairs of cone points of order 2, all of them disjoint, and such that each component of
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\Sigma
has negative Euler characteristic.

We will represent multicurves in orbifolds by weighted graphs, defined next.

Definition.

(Weighted graph dual to a multicurve)

Let
\Sigma
be a multicurve in a closed orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
. Its
dual graph

{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is defined as follows:

(1)

its vertices are the connected components of
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\Sigma
;

(2)

its edges are the closed curves in
\Sigma
;

(3)

its semiedges are the arcs in
\Sigma
;

(4)

an edge
\gamma\in\Sigma
connects two vertices
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}},{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
if and only if
\gamma
is in the boundary of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
; a semiedge
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
is connected to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
if the arc
\gamma^{\prime}
is in the boundary of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

(5)

if
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is a vertex, its weight is
w(V)=(g({\mathcal{O}}_{j});m_{i_{1}},\dots,m_{i_{j}})
where
g({\mathcal{O}}_{j})
is the genus of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
and
m_{i_{k}}
are the orders of the cone points of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

Figure
1
shows a multicurve in an orbifold and its dual graph.

It is clear from the definition that the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
determines the topological information of the pair
({\mathcal{O}},\Sigma)
.

Figure 1.
Admissible multicurve and its dual graph

When the orbifold is a surface (there are no cone points), a multicurve is just a finite union of simple closed curves, and its dual graph is a weighted graph with no semiedges and where the weight of each vertex is the genus of the corresponding subsurface.

2.3.
Augmented moduli space
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}

Let
\Sigma
be an admissible multicurve on a surface
S
. The graph dual to
\Sigma
is called
stable graph
. The space
S/\Sigma
where each component of
\Sigma
has been collapsed to a point is called a
stable surface
. Each collapsed curve is called a
node
, and the complement of the nodes are called
parts
.
A
hyperbolic stable surface
is a stable surface with a hyperbolic structure on each part.

The augmented moduli space of genus
g
is the space
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
of hyperbolic stable surfaces of genus
g
up to isometry. This space provides a compactification of moduli space, once given a topology that intuitively works as follows. Consider a point
X\in\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
with nodes
N_{1},\dots,N_{r}
. A sequence
X_{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
converges to
X\in\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
if there is a family of geodesics
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\{\alpha_{1}^{n},\dots,\alpha_{r}^{n}\}
in
X_{n}
so that:

(i)

the stable surfaces
X_{n}/\mathcal{F}_{n}
are homeomorphic to
X
;

(ii)

the length of each
\alpha_{i}^{n},i=1,\dots,r,\,
tends to zero when
n\to\infty
; and

(iii)

away from the curves
\alpha_{i}
of
X_{n}
and from the nodes of
X
, the hyperbolic surfaces
X_{n}
are close to
X
when
n\to\infty
.

A way of formalizing this is by first considering marked surfaces and the augmented Teichmüller space. See, for instance,
[
15
]
for a detailed exposition on this.

The augmented moduli space is stratified in the following way. For each stable graph
\mathcal{G}
, we consider the stratum
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
, defined as the space of stable hyperbolic surfaces
X
whose associated graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{G}}
. This space is homeomorphic to the product of the moduli spaces of the parts of
X
, which are punctured surfaces.
Since there is a finite number of non-isomorphic stable graphs of a fixed genus
g
, then
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}
decomposes into a finite number of strata, one for each stable graph.
This is called the
topological stratification
of
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
.

Certainly, we can consider the closure of an equisymmetric stratum
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(G,{\mathcal{O}},\Phi)
in
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}
, and its boundary, which is the center of attention of this paper.

3.
Statement of main theorem in
[
13
]

Consider a topological action on a surface
S
given by a (surface kernel) epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
. Let
\Sigma
be an admissible multicurve in
{\mathcal{O}}
and let
\tilde{\Sigma}=p^{-1}(\Sigma)
be its preimage under the branched covering.
The main theorem in
[
13
]
gives a criterion to determine the dual graph of
\tilde{\Sigma}
from
\Sigma
and the epimorphism
\Phi
. We recall here that theorem.

There are some new concepts and notation needed in the theorem.
For each suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\Sigma
, we consider the restriction
\Phi_{j}\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{j})\to G
. For each
\gamma\in\Sigma
, we consider similarly the restriction
\Phi_{\gamma}\colon\pi_{1}(\gamma)\to G
, and we also consider a dual curve
c_{\gamma}
. We will give details of these concepts after the statement of the theorem. For technical reasons in order to define the dual curves, we give an orientation to the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
.
Finally,
\Sigma^{1}
denotes the set of loops or semiedges of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
,
\Sigma^{2}
denotes the set of remaining edges and
\Sigma_{j}
denotes the set of edges and semiedges adjacent to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. We warn that, in order to slightly simplify the reading, we have adopted here the term “dual curves” which was not explicit in the original statement in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider a topological action on a surface given by an epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
and let
\Sigma
be an admissible multicurve in
{\mathcal{O}}
. Then
the dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
of the multicurve
\tilde{\Sigma}=p^{-1}(\Sigma)
and a map
p_{*}\colon{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
are determined as follows.

(1)

The preimages under
p_{*}
of a vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
(i.e., a component of
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\Sigma
) are denoted
V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}},g\in G
; thus the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
has
\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|}
preimages.

(2)

The preimages of an edge
\gamma
are denoted
E_{\gamma,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}},g\in G
; thus the edge
\gamma
has
\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
preimages.

(3)

(Edges connecting vertices.) If
\gamma
goes from
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
, then the edge
E_{\gamma,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins the vertices
V_{j_{1},g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{1}}}
and
V_{j_{2},g{\Phi(c_{\gamma})}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{2}}}
.

(4)

(Degrees of vertices.) The degree of the vertex
V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}

is equal to

D_{j}=|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{j}\cap\Sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}+2\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{j}\cap{\Sigma^{1}}}\frac{1}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}\right).

(5)

(Weights of vertices.) The weight
w^{j}

of the vertex
V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
is equal to

\quad\quad w^{j}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|\,\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{j})+D_{j}\right).

Remarks.
(a) The theorem gives an action of
G
on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
whose quotient map is
p_{*}
.
Indeed, we can define

g(V_{j,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}})=V_{j,gh{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}},\;g(E_{\gamma,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}})=V_{\gamma,gh{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}

Notice that if
E_{\gamma,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
joins
V_{j_{1},h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{1}}}
and
V_{j_{2},h\Phi(c_{\gamma}){\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{2}}}
, then
g(E_{\gamma,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}})=E_{\gamma,gh{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
joins
V_{j_{1},gh{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{1}}}=g(V_{j_{1},h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{1}}})
and
V_{j_{2},g\Phi(c_{\gamma})h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{2}}}=g(V_{j_{2},h\Phi(c_{\gamma}){\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{2}}})
.

(b) The items (4) and (5) say that all the preimages by
p_{*}
of a vertex of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
have the same degree and the same weight.

Explanations.
In order to define the restrictions
\Phi_{j},\Phi_{\gamma}
we need to carefully choose base points and auxiliary paths, namely:

(1)

choose a base point
*_{j}
in each suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
(for simplicity, choose
*_{1}
equal to the initial base point
*
) and a base point
*_{\gamma}
in each curve
\gamma\in\Sigma
;

(2)

choose simple paths
\beta_{j,\gamma}
contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
going from
*_{j}
to
*_{\gamma}
for all the edges
\gamma
adjacent to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. If
\gamma
is a loop or semiedge of
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
, we choose two paths
\beta_{j,\gamma}^{a},\beta_{j,\gamma}^{b}

from
*_{j}
to
*_{\gamma}
such that
\beta_{j,\gamma}^{a}(\beta_{j,\gamma}^{b})^{-1}
intersects
\gamma
exactly once and, in the case that
\gamma
is a semiedge (arc in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
joining two cone points of order 2),
\beta_{j,\gamma}^{a}(\beta_{j,\gamma}^{b})^{-1}
bounds a disc which contains just one of the endpoints of
\gamma
and no other cone point.
Moreover we choose all these paths so that they are disjoint except at their endpoints.

We finally choose an spanning tree
{\mathcal{T}}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
. All these choices allow to consider paths
\beta_{j}
going from
*
to
*_{j}
following the unique path in
{\mathcal{T}}
from
{\mathcal{O}}_{1}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
and using the auxiliary paths
\beta_{j,\gamma}
. Now we can define the restriction
\Phi_{j}
as
\Phi_{j}(\alpha)=\Phi(\beta_{j}\alpha\beta_{j}^{-1})
.
Similarly, we can consider paths
\beta_{\gamma}
for each
\gamma\in\Sigma
and define the restrictions
\Phi_{\gamma}
to the fundamental group of the curve
\gamma
. We remark that if
\gamma
is an arc, it is considered as a 1-orbifold with its endpoints cone points of order 2. Thus, its fundamental group is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}*{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
generated by loops around the cone points.
See
[
13
]
for more details.

Finally, the dual curves are as follows (see Figure
2
):

(1)

if the oriented edge
\gamma
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
goes from
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
, different vertices, then
c_{\gamma}=\beta_{j_{1}}\,\beta_{j_{1},\gamma}\,\beta_{j_{2},\gamma}^{-1}\,\beta_{j_{2}}^{-1}
;

(2)

if
\gamma
is a loop or a semiedge adjacent to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
, then
c_{\gamma}=\beta_{j}\,\beta_{j,\gamma}^{a}(\beta_{j,\gamma}^{b})^{-1}\beta_{j}^{-1}
. Notice that, if
\gamma
is a semiedge,
\Phi(c_{\gamma})
is one of the generators of
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
.

c_{\gamma}

Notation.
To the weighted dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
of a multicurve
\Sigma
we can add the information given by the action: to each vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
we add the label
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}
and to each edge or semiedge
\gamma
we add the labels
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
and
\Phi(c_{\gamma})
. Knowing this graph allows to obtain all the information (1)-(5) of Theorem
3.1
. We will call this graph the
extra decorated graph
associated to
\Sigma
(with respect to the action
\Phi
).

4.
First simplifications

Theorem
3.1
admits two important simplifications in the cases where the orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
has genus 0 and no cone points of order 2 and in the case where the group
G
is abelian.

4.1.
Genus of
{\mathcal{O}}
equals zero and no cone points of order 2

Since the genus of
{\mathcal{O}}
is zero, the dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
of any multicurve
\Sigma
on
\Sigma
is a tree. Moreover, since there are no cone points of order 2, this tree has no semiedges.
As a consequence, all the dual curves
c_{\gamma}
are homotopically trivial and
\Phi(c_{\gamma})
is the identity. Item (3) in Theorem
3.1
thus says that if
\gamma
is an edge of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
going
from
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
, then for each
g\in G
the edge
E_{\gamma,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins the vertices
V_{j_{1},g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{1}}}
and
V_{j_{2},g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j_{2}}}
. Then we can interpret
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
as
(\sqcup_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}^{g})/\sim
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}^{g}
is a copy of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
and where two vertices

V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}},V_{j,g^{\prime}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
are identified if and only if
g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}=g^{\prime}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}
and similarly with the edges, by (1) and (2) of that theorem. Thus we have:

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider a topological action given by an epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
where the signature of
{\mathcal{O}}
is equal to
(0;m_{1},\dots,m_{k})
,
m_{i}\geq 3
. Let
\Sigma
be an admissible multicurve in
{\mathcal{O}}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}=(\sqcup_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}^{g})/\sim
, where
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}^{g}
is a copy of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
,

V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}\sim V_{j,g^{\prime}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
if and only if
g(g^{\prime})^{-1}\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}
and
E_{\gamma,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}\sim E_{\gamma,g^{\prime}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
if and only if
g(g^{\prime})^{-1}\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
.

4.2.
Abelian case reduces to homology

Suppose that
G
is abelian. Then the homomorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to G
factors through the abelianized group
H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})
of
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)
.
By abuse of notation, we will still call
\Phi
to the homomorphism from the homology group.
If the signature of
{\mathcal{O}}
is
(\sigma;m_{1},\dots,m_{k})
, then
H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})
is generated by
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\delta_{l}
,
1\leq i\leq\sigma,\,1\leq l\leq k
, where the
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
are a homology basis of the underline space of
{\mathcal{O}}
and the
\delta_{l}
are meridians about the cone points. This is a great simplification, since we do not have to take care of the base points, in particular we do not need the choices of any base point, nor the choices of the paths
\beta_{j,\gamma}
.
The restrictions
\Phi_{j}
and
\Phi_{\gamma}
are easily computed: it is needed to express the homology generators of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
or
\gamma
in terms of the homology generators of
{\mathcal{O}}
. For example, if
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
has genus zero,
\Phi_{j}
only depends on the meridians about the cone points contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

The homology classes of the dual curves
c_{\gamma}
are easily chosen when
\gamma
is contained in the boundary of just one suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
: if
\gamma
is an arc,
c_{\gamma}
is a meridian about one of if its endpoints, and if
\gamma
is a simple closed curve,
c_{\gamma}
is a simple closed curve in the closure of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
intersecting
\gamma
exactly one.
If
\gamma
is in the boundary of two different suborbifolds, and more precisely, if
\gamma
is an oriented edge in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
from
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
, consider the oriented closed path
\omega
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
formed by the oriented edge
\gamma
followed by the path in the spanning tree from
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{2}}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j_{1}}
. Now, take a simple oriented closed curve
c_{\gamma}
in
{\mathcal{O}}
traveling through the suborbifolds indicated by
\omega
.

To appreciate these simplifications, let us compare with an example of a non-abelian action.

Example.
The pyramidal action is a certain action of the dihedral group
D_{n}
on a surface
S_{n}
with quotient the orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
with signature
(0;2,2,2,2,n)
.
In
[
13
]
we considered examples of multicurves
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}\}
where both
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
are arcs joining the same cone points of
{\mathcal{O}}
although not homotopic. It was shown that the subgroup
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
has order 2, while
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma^{\prime}}
is the whole group if
n
is odd, and has order
n
if
n
is even. For
n>2
this implies that the number of edges of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{\widetilde{\Sigma^{\prime}}}
are different, so they are different graphs and provide different topological strata in the boundary.

This phenomenon occurs because the dihedral group is not abelian. Indeed, if we have any abelian action on
S_{n}
with quotient an orbifold of signature
(0;2,2,2,2,n)
, and we consider the multicurves
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}\}
as before, then
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\gamma
and
{\mathcal{O}}\setminus\gamma^{\prime}
have the same homology generators, and so do
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
, and we can choose the dual curves
c_{\gamma},c_{\gamma^{\prime}}
to be equal. Thus, the graphs
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma},{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
are equal and provide the same topological stratum in the boundary.

5.
Cyclic
p
-gonal locus,
p\geq 3

Definition.
A
cyclic
p
-gonal action
, for
p
a prime number, is an action of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
on a surface
S_{\!\!g}
of genus
g
whose quotient orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
has signature
(0;p,\dots^{M},p)
.
Thus, when
p\geq 3
, these actions satisfy both simplifications explained in Section
4
, so we will study this case in this section.

The homology group of
{\mathcal{O}}
with the above signature is generated by oriented loops
x_{1},\dots,

x_{M}
around the cone points
q_{1},\dots,q_{M}
of
{\mathcal{O}}
. We take all these loops with the same orientation so that its product
x_{1}\dots x_{M}
is trivial. If
a
is the generator of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, the epimorphism
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to G
with
\Phi(x_{i})=a^{m_{i}}
is denoted briefly by its
generating vector

(a^{m_{1}},\dots,a^{m_{M}})
. We say that
m_{i}
is the
exponent
of the cone point
q_{i}
.

The Riemann-Hurwitz formula and the Riemann existence theorem for topological actions permit obtain all the
p
-gonal actions, as follows.

Proposition 5.1
.

The epimorphism
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with generating vector
(a^{m_{1}},\dots,a^{m_{M}})
determines a topological action on a surface
S
with quotient an orbifold with signature
(0;p,\dots^{M},p)
if and only if
m_{i}\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{M}m_{i}
is multiple of
p
. The genus of the surface
S
is and
g=\frac{(p-1)(M-2)}{2}
.

Remark.
The above proposition does not say anything about the topological equivalence of the actions. See
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
to find all the topological inequivalent
p
-gonal actions on a surface of genus 2, 3 and 4.
For instance, in genus 4 there are two inequivalent 3-gonal actions and three inequivalent 5-gonal actions.

Let
\Sigma
be a multicurve in
{\mathcal{O}}
.
Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
has only the two trivial subgroups, items (1) and (2) of Theorem
3.1
say that the preimage of any vertex of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is either 1 vertex or
p
vertices, and the same for any edge of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
. We will say that a vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
(resp. an edge
\gamma
) of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is
multiple of
p
if its preimage is a set of
p
vertices (resp. edges) of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
or, equivalently, if
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}=\{0\}
(resp.
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}=\{0\}
).

It is easy to see whether a vertex or an edge is multiple of
p
, this is done in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
be a cyclic
p
-gonal action with generating vector
(a^{m_{1}},\dots,

a^{m_{M}})
. Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be an admissible multicurve.
Then

(i)

An edge
\gamma
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is multiple of
p
if
and only if the sum of the exponents of the cone points contained in a disc bounded by
\gamma
is multiple of
p
.

(ii)

A vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is multiple of
p

if and only if the suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
does not contain any cone point and all the edges
\gamma
incident to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
are multiple of
p
. In other words, if
c_{j}+n_{j}=0
, where

c_{j}
is the number of cone points contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}

and
n_{j}
is the number of edges not multiple of
p
that are incident to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

(iii)

If
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is a vertex not multiple of
p
then
c_{j}+n_{j}\geq 2
.

Proof.

(i) According to Theorem
3.1
(2), the edge
\gamma
has
|G/{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|
preimages. Now,
\Phi_{\gamma}
is the restriction of
\Phi
to the homology group of
\gamma
(since
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is abelian).
Suppose the curve
\gamma
bounds a disc with containing the cone points
q_{i_{1}},\dots,q_{i_{k}}
. Since the loops
x_{i}
have been chosen with the same orientation, the generator of the homology of
\gamma
is
x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{k}}
. Therefore,

{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is generated by
a^{m_{i_{1}}+\dots+m_{i_{k}}}
. If
{m_{i_{1}}+\dots+m_{i_{k}}}
is multiple of
p
then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is trivial, and then
\gamma
lifts to
p
edges. Otherwise,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is the total
G
and
\gamma
lifts to just one edge.

(ii) According to Theorem
3.1
(1) and in a similar way as before, we have to determine when the subgroup
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}
is trivial or the total group. Because
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
has genus 0, the subgroup
H_{1}(O_{j})
is generated by loops around the cone-points contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
and by the boundary components of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
, which correspond to the edges of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
incident to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. Therefore,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}
is trivial if and only if the condition written in the statement holds.

(iii) By the previous item,
n_{j}+c_{j}\not=0
. Suppose that
n_{j}=1
.
By item (i) and since
n_{j}=1
, the sum of exponents of the cone points outside
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is not multiple of
p
. Since the sum of all the exponents is multiple of
p
(Proposition
5.1
), there must be some cone point in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
, thus
c_{j}\geq 1
.
Finally,
n_{j}=0,c_{j}=1
also contradicts Proposition
5.1
since no exponent is multiple of
p
.
∎

We can now completely describe the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
for any multicurve
\Sigma
in
{\mathcal{O}}
, therefore the strata in the boundary of the
p
-gonal equisymmetric stratum.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
be a cyclic
p
-gonal action. Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be an admissible multicurve and let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
its dual graph. Then the dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
of
\,\tilde{\Sigma}
is obtained as
(\sqcup_{t=1}^{p}{\mathcal{D}}^{t})/\hskip-2.84544pt\sim
where
{\mathcal{D}}^{t}
are copies of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
and
\sim
is as follows. We denote
V^{t}_{j}
the vertex of
{\mathcal{D}}^{t}
corresponding to the vertex
V_{j}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
, and similarly with the edges. Then:

(1)

V^{t}_{j}\sim V^{s}_{j}
if and only if
V_{j}
is not multiple of
p
.

(2)

Similarly,
E^{t}_{j}\sim E^{s}_{j}
if and only if
E_{j}
is not multiple of
p
.

(3)

If a vertex
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is multiple of
p
, then the degree
D_{j}
of any of its preimages is equal to the degree of
V
and its weight
w^{j}
is equal to zero.

(4)

If a vertex
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is not multiple of
p
then the degree of any of its preimages is equal to
D_{j}=p\,m_{j}+n_{j}
, where
m_{j}
is the number of edges multiple of
p
that are incident to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
and
n_{j}
is the number of edges not multiple of
p
that are incident to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. Its weight is
w^{j}=\frac{p-1}{2}(n_{j}+c_{j}-2)
, where
c_{j}
is the number of cone points contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

We remark that the weight
w^{j}
obtained in (iv) is non-negative by Lemma
5.1
(iii).

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{O}}
has genus 0, the dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is quotient of the union of
p
copies of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
, by Proposition
4.1
. The precise identifications given by
\sim
come from that proposition and from Lemma
5.1
. Thus we have (1) and (2).

(3) If
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is multiple of
p
, then
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
does not contain any cone point and all the edges incident to
V
are multiple of
p
, by Lemma
5.1
. Thus,
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|=1
and
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|=1
for all
\gamma
in the boundary of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. Then Theorem
3.1
(4) gives that
D_{j}
is equal to the degree of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
, and item (5) of that theorem
says that

w^{j}=1-\frac{1}{2}(\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{j})+{\rm deg}({\mathcal{O}}_{j}))=0

because
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
has genus 0 and no cone points, so
\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{j})+{\rm deg}({\mathcal{O}}_{j})=2
.

(4) If
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is not multiple of
p
, then
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|=p
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{j}
the set of (multiple of
p
)-edges adjacent to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
and by
\mathcal{N}_{j}
the set of (non-multiple of

)-edges adjacent to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. Then,
by Theorem

(4), we have

D_{j}=|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{j}\cap\Sigma^{2}}\frac{1}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}\right)=p(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}\frac{1}{1}+\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\frac{1}{p})=p|\mathcal{M}_{j}|+|\mathcal{N}_{j}|=p\,m_{j}+n_{j}

For the weight, since

has genus 0,
c_{j}
cone points of order

and degree

, its Euler characteristic is
\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{j})=2-n_{j}-m_{j}-c_{j}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).
Thus, by
Theorem

(5), we have

w^{j}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(p\Big(2-n_{j}-m_{j}-c_{j}\frac{p-1}{p}\Big)+p\,m_{j}+n_{j}\right)=\frac{p-1}{2}(n_{j}+c_{j}-2)

∎

. The weight of each vertex has the form
(0;2^{k})
, which we will abbreviate as
k
, i.e., the weight of any vertex will be the number of cone points the corresponding orbifold contains. The definitions of
even
vertex or edge is the analog to “multiple of
p
” in the
p
-gonal case.

We obtain a lemma totally analogous to Lemma
5.1
just by changing
p
by
2
. The only new part appears for the semiedges.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
be the hyperelliptic action and let
\Sigma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be an admissible multicurve.
Then

(i)

An edge
\gamma
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is even if
and only if
\gamma
bounds a disc in
{\mathcal{O}}
containing an even number of cone points.

(ii)

A vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is even
if and only if the suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
does not contain any cone point and all the edges
\gamma
incident to the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
are even.

(iii)

A semiedge is always odd. A semiedge incident to an odd vertex lifts to a loop. A semiedge incident to an even vertex lifts to an edge joining the two lifts of the vertex.

Proof.

(i) If an edge
\gamma
bounds a disc containing the cone points
q_{i_{1}},\dots,q_{i_{2k}}
, then
\gamma
is homotopic to
x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{2k}}
and thus
\Phi(\gamma)=a^{2k}=1
. Hence
\gamma
is even. The converse is equal.

(ii) Equal as in Lemma
5.1
.

(iii) Let
\gamma
be a semiedge of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
starting at a vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
, which corresponds to an arc
\gamma
in the boundary of the suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
joining two cone points
q_{i},q_{j}
. Then
\pi_{1}(\gamma)
is generated by the loops
x_{i},x_{j}
and the dual curve
c_{\gamma}
is equal to one of these loops, so
\Phi(c_{\gamma})=a
. Thus
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and so a semiedge is always odd. By Theorem
3.1
(3), the edge
E_{\gamma,g\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
joins the vertices
V_{j,g{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
and
V_{j,g\Phi(c_{\gamma}){\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}=V_{j,ga{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}
. Thus,
\gamma
lifts to a loop if
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is odd and
\gamma
lifts to an edge joining the two lifts of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
if
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is even.
∎

Finally, to describe the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
we have the analog result to Theorem
5.1
. See Figure
6
for an example of the dual graphs of a multicurve
\Sigma
and of its preimage
\tilde{\Sigma}
under the hyperelliptic action.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}},*)\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
be the hyperelliptic action on genus
g
. Let
\Sigma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be an admissible multicurve and let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
its dual graph. Then the dual graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
of
\,\tilde{\Sigma}
is obtained as
({\mathcal{D}}^{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{D}}^{2})/\hskip-2.84544pt\sim
where
{\mathcal{D}}^{1},{\mathcal{D}}^{2}
are copies of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
and
\sim
is as follows. We call
V^{t}_{j}
to the vertex of
{\mathcal{D}}^{t}
corresponding to the vertex
V_{j}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
, and similarly with the edges. Then:

(1)

V^{1}_{j}\sim V^{2}_{j}
if and only if
V_{j}
is odd.

(2)

An edge
E^{1}_{j}\sim E^{2}_{j}
if and only if
E_{j}
is odd.

(3)

A semiedge
E^{1}_{\gamma}
is joined to
E^{2}_{\gamma}
through their “free end”, giving rise in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
to a loop or to an edge joining two different vertices as indicated in Lemma
6.1
(iii).

(4)

If a vertex
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is even, then the degree
D_{j}
of any of its preimages is equal to the degree of
V
and its weight
w^{j}
is equal to zero.

(5)

If a vertex
V={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is odd then the degree of any of its preimages is equal to
D_{j}=n_{j}+2(m_{j}+s_{j})
, where
n_{j},m_{j},s_{j}
are, respectively the number of odd edges, even edges and semiedges incident to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
. Its weight is
w^{j}=\frac{1}{2}(n_{j}+c_{j}-2)
, where
c_{j}
is the number of cone points contained in
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are as in Theorem
5.1
and (3) is Lemma
6.1
(iii).

(4) Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
be an even vertex, so
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|=1
.
By Lemma
6.1
(ii), each edge
\gamma
incident to
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is even, so
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|=1
, and by part (iii) of that lemma, any semiedge
\gamma
is odd so
2\frac{1}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}=1
. Thus, by Theorem
3.1
(4),
D_{j}
is equal to the degree of
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
.

To compute the weight, by Lemma
6.1
(ii),
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
does not contain any cone point, so that
\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{j})=2-deg({\mathcal{O}}_{j})
. Then using Theorem
3.1
(5), we obtain that
w^{j}=0
.

(5) Let
V_{j}={\mathcal{O}}_{j}
be an odd vertex, so
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}|=2
. By Theorem
3.1
, we have that

D_{j}=2(n_{j}\frac{1}{2}+m_{j}\frac{1}{1}+2s_{j}\frac{1}{2})=n_{j}+2(m_{j}+s_{j}).

The weight of any preimage of
V_{j}
is equal to

w^{j}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(2\left(2-n_{j}-m_{j}-s_{j}-c_{j}\frac{1}{2}\right)+n_{j}+2(m_{j}+s_{j})\right)=\frac{1}{2}(n_{j}+c_{j}-2).

∎

7.
Action by an abelian group

In this section we consider any action by an abelian group and study the preimage of a multicurve consisting on just one curve
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. This will provide the topological strata of maximal dimension in the boundary of the corresponding equisymmetric loci. We study three cases, depending on whether
\gamma
is a separating or non-separating simple closed curve or is an arc.

7.1.
Case 1: separating simple closed curve.

In this case we will show in Proposition
7.1
that the dual graph of the preimage is a complete bipartite multigraph.

Notation.
We denote by
K_{m,n}^{d}
the complete bipartite graph of order
d
between a subset of
m
vertices and a subset of
n
vertices, i.e., each vertex of the first subset is joined to each vertex of the second subset with
d
edges.

In the proof of Proposition
7.1
we will need the following result about abelian groups which says that if an abelian group is generated by two subgroups, then any class with respect to the first subgroup intersects any class with the second subgroup in a class with respect the intersection of both subgroups.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G
be an abelian group. Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be subgroups such that
G=H_{1}H_{2}
. Then for all
a,b\in G
there is
c\in G
such that
aH_{1}\cap bH_{2}=c(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
. As a consequence,
|G|=\frac{|H_{1}||H_{2}|}{|H_{1}\cap H_{2}|}
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
b=1
. Since
G=H_{1}H_{2}
,
a=a_{1}a_{2}
with
a_{i}\in H_{i}
. Let us check that
aH_{1}\cap H_{2}=a_{2}(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
. Indeed, on the one hand, if
h\in H_{1}\cap H_{2}
, we have that
a_{2}h\in H_{2}
and
a_{2}h=aa_{1}^{-1}h\in aH_{1}
. On the other hand take
y\in aH_{1}\cap H_{2}
, i.e.,
y=ak_{1}
for some
k_{1}\in H_{1}
and
y=k_{2}
for some
k_{2}\in H_{2}
. We want to show that
y\in a_{2}(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
or equivalently that
a_{2}^{-1}y\in(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
. But

a_{2}^{-1}y=a_{2}^{-1}k_{2}\in H_{2}
, and

a_{2}^{-1}y=a_{2}^{-1}ak_{1}=a_{2}^{-1}a_{1}a_{2}k_{1}=a_{1}k_{1}\in H_{1},

so we are done.

For the general case, applying the previous argument to
a^{\prime}=ab^{-1}
we have that
a^{\prime}H_{1}\cap H_{2}=a^{\prime\prime}(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
for some
a^{\prime\prime}\in G
. Thus

b(a^{\prime}H_{1}\cap H_{2})=ba^{\prime\prime}(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
, i.e.,
aH_{1}\cap bH_{2}=ba^{\prime\prime}(H_{1}\cap H_{2})
.

As a consequence, any class
aH_{1}
is a union of

[G:H_{2}]

classes with respect to
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
, and so

[G:(H_{1}\cap H_{2})]=[G:H_{1}][G:H_{2}]

and we easily obtain the result.
∎

Proposition 7.1
.

Consider a topological action on a surface described by an epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to G
, with
G
an abelian group. Let
\gamma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be a closed curve separating
{\mathcal{O}}
in two suborbifolds
{\mathcal{O}}_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{2}
and consider the multicurve

\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is isomorphic to the graph
K_{m,n}^{d}
, where
m=\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}
,
n=\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}|}
and
d=\frac{r}{mn}
, with
r=\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
.

Proof.

Since
G
is abelian, we can work either with fundamental groups or with homology groups.

By Van-Kampen theorem (or Mayer-Vietoris sequence), we have that
H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})
is generated by the subgroups
H_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{1}),H_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{2})
. Also notice that
H_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})\cap H_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{2})=H_{1}(\gamma)
. Since
\Phi
is surjective, these properties pass to
G
, precisely,

G
is generated by
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1},{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}
and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}\supset{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
.

By Theorem
3.1
, the number of preimages of the vertex
{\mathcal{O}}_{j}
is
|\frac{G}{{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{j}}|,j=1,2
, and the number of preimages of
\gamma
is
|\frac{G}{{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}|
. Since the edge
\gamma
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
joins
{\mathcal{O}}_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{2}
, each preimage of
\gamma
joins a preimage of
{\mathcal{O}}_{1}
with a preimage of
{\mathcal{O}}_{2}
.
Thus
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is bipartite.

Given two vertices
V_{1,a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}},V_{2,b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}}
, we will show that there is some edge
E_{\gamma,c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joining them.
Indeed, by Theorem
3.1
(3), an edge
E_{\gamma,c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
and
V_{2,b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}}
if and only if
c\in a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and
c\Phi(c_{\gamma})\in b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}
. Notice that
c_{\gamma}
is trivial (because
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
is a tree), so the above is equivalent to

c\in a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}=c^{\prime}({\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}),

for some
c^{\prime}\in G
, by Lemma
7.1
. Since

{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is a subgroup of
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}
, the class
c^{\prime}({\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2})
is divided into
d^{\prime}=\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
classes with respect to
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
. Thus there are
d^{\prime}
edges joining
V_{1,a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}},V_{2,b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}}
. Finally, by Lemma
7.1
, we have

d^{\prime}=\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cap{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}=\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|\,|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}|}{|G||{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}=\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}{|G|}\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}|}{|G|}\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}=\frac{r}{mn}=d

∎

Remark.
In the situation of Proposition
7.1
but with
G
non-abelian, the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is also contained in a graph of the form
K^{d^{\prime}}_{m,n}
, but is not clear to be complete.

7.2.
Case II: non-separating simple closed curve.

In this case we will show in Proposition
7.2
that if
\Sigma
is a multicurve consisting on a non-separating simple closed curve, then the dual graph of its preimage is a multiple cycle
C^{d}_{n}
, that is,
a cycle of length
n
and order
d
, i.e., each edge is multiple of order
d
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Consider a topological action on a surface described by an epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to G
, with
G
an abelian group. Let
\gamma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be a non-separating simple closed curve and consider the multicurve

\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
and the suborbifold
{\mathcal{O}}_{1}={\mathcal{O}}\setminus\gamma
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is isomorphic to the graph
C_{m}^{d}
, where
m=\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}
,
d=\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
.

Proof.

The graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\Sigma}
consists on one vertex and one loop. By Theorem
3.1
, the graph
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has
m
vertices and
\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
edges. Let
h=\Phi(c_{\gamma})
. Because
G
is finite, some power of
h
is contained in
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
. Let
r
be the minimum positive number such that
h^{r}\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
.

The fundamental group
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}})
is generated by
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and by
c_{\gamma}
, and hence
G
is generated by
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and by
\langle h\rangle
. Because
G
is abelian, the above means that any
g\in G
can be written as
g=h^{t}h_{1}
, for some integer
t
and some
h_{1}\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
. In other words,

G=1{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cup h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cup\dots\cup h^{r-1}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}.

Notice that any two of the above classes are different, since otherwise it would contradict the definition of
r
. In particular
r=m
.

Now, the edge
E_{\gamma,1{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,1{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
with
V_{1,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
; the edge
E_{\gamma,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,h{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
with
V_{1,h^{2}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
; … the edge
E_{\gamma,h^{r-1}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,h^{r-1}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
with
V_{1,h^{r}{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}=V_{1,1{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
. In this way we obtain a cycle of
r
different vertices, so all the vertices are in a cycle.

Finally, let us see that the above cycle is multiple of order
d
. First, notice that if
E_{\gamma,c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
with
V_{1,b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
, then
E_{\gamma,cz{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins the same vertices, for all
z\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
(indeed, if
c\in a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and
ch\in b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
, then
cz\in a{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and
czh\in b{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
, since
G
is abelian).
Thus, each pair of vertices joined by an edge are actually joined by
\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}
different edges.
Since the total number of edges of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is
\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}\frac{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}|}=md
, we have the result.
∎

7.3.
Case III: multicurve is an arc.

Finally in this section we study the case where the multicurve consists in just one arc.

Proposition 7.3
.

Consider a topological action on a surface described by an epimorphism
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to G
, with
G
an abelian group. Let
\gamma\subset{\mathcal{O}}
be an arc and consider the multicurve

\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
consists on

(i)

either a single vertex with
\frac{|G|}{2}
or
\frac{|G|}{4}
loops; or

(ii)

two different vertices with
\frac{|G|}{2}
or
\frac{|G|}{4}
joining them.

Proof.

The curve
\gamma
is an arc joining two cone points of order 2, and therefore
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is generated by two elements of order 2. Since
G
is abelian, the only possibilities is that
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\sim{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
or
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\sim{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Notice that
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}})
is generated by
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})
and by a loop
\alpha
surrounding one of the endpoints of
\gamma
. This is so because the the loop surrounding
\gamma
, which is the product of the 2 generators of
\pi_{1}(\gamma)
, also belongs to
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{1})
. Taking images by
\Phi
we have that
G
is generated by
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and by
a=\Phi(\alpha)
.
As a consequence we have:

(i)

If
a\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
, i.e.,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}=G
, then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has 1 vertex. The number of edges is
\frac{|G|}{2}
or
\frac{|G|}{4}
depending on whether
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is isomorphic, respectively, to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Since there is only one vertex, all the edges are loops.

(ii)

If
a\not\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
, then
G={\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\cup a\,{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
and so
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has 2 vertices. The number of edges is exactly as before.
It is left to see whether or not each edge joins the two different vertices.
To see this, notice that we can take
c_{\gamma}=\alpha
, and so
\Phi(c_{\gamma})=a
. The edge
E_{\gamma,c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}}
joins
V_{1,c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
and
V_{1,ca{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}}
. The lateral classes
c{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1},ca{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
are always different because
c(ca)^{-1}=a^{-1}\not\in{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
. Thus, any edge joins the 2 different vertices.

∎

Remark.
In the situation of Proposition
7.3
but with
G
non-abelian, more options can appear. For instance, in
[
13
]
it is shown that in the pyramidal action with
G=D_{n}
the dihedral group of order
2n
, any graph with a single vertex and
k
loops with
k
dividing
n
appears as one of the graphs
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
.

7.4.
Examples.

We show examples proving that all the graphs appearing in Propositions
7.1
,
7.2
and
7.3
actually occur, that is, each of them is at the boundary of the equisymmetric locus of some abelian action.

Examples 1.
Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold of signature
(0;2^{4},3^{2},5^{2})
. Denote by
q_{i},i=1,\dots,8
its singular points with order, respectively, 2,2,5,5,2,2,3,3, and by
x_{i},i=1,\dots,8
small loops around the
q_{i}
, all of them with the same orientation. Consider the epimorphism
\Phi
defined by

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})&\to&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}\cr x_{1}&\mapsto&(1,0,0)\cr x_{2}&\mapsto&(1,0,0)\cr x_{3}&\mapsto&(0,0,1)\cr x_{4}&\mapsto&(0,0,4)\cr x_{5}&\mapsto&(1,0,0)\cr x_{6}&\mapsto&(1,0,0)\cr x_{7}&\mapsto&(0,1,0)\cr x_{8}&\mapsto&(0,2,0)\cr\end{array}

It is clear that
\Phi
determines a topological action since (in additive notation)
\sum_{i=1}^{8}\Phi(x_{i})=(0,0,0)
.
Now, consider a closed curve
\gamma
separating
{\mathcal{O}}
in two suborbifolds
{\mathcal{O}}_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{2}
, one of them containing the points
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
and the other the remaining ones. Let
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then we have that
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
;
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is trivial. Thus, by Proposition
7.1
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is the graph
K^{2}_{3,5}
. We remark that the genus of the covering surface is
g=45
.

For the general case
consider an orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
with signature
(0;d^{2d},m^{2m},n^{2n})
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{2d}
be loops around the singular points of order
d
,
y_{1},\dots,y_{2m}
loops around the singular points of order
m
, and
z_{1},\dots,z_{2n}
loops around the singular points of order
n
, all of them with the same orientation. Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
the epimorphism defined by

\Phi(x_{i})=(1,0,0),\quad\Phi(y_{i})=(0,1,0),\quad\Phi(z_{i})=(0,0,1).

The order of
\Phi(x_{i})
is
d
, the order of
\Phi(y_{i})
is
m
and order of
\Phi(z_{i})
is
n
; also the product of all
\Phi(x_{i}),\Phi(y_{i}),\Phi(z_{i})
is trivial. So
\Phi
determines a topological action with the covering surface of genus
g=1+dmn(d+m+n-4)
.

Now, consider a closed curve
\gamma
separating
{\mathcal{O}}
in two suborbifolds
{\mathcal{O}}_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{2}
, one of them containing the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{1},\dots,y_{2m}
and the other the remaining ones. Let
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then we have that
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{2}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is trivial. Thus, by Proposition
7.1
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is the graph
K^{d}_{m,n}
.

Examples 2.
(i) For
d>1
, let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold of signature
(1;d,d)
and let
\alpha,\beta,x_{1},x_{2}
be a basis for its homology, where
x_{1},x_{2}
are loops with the same orientation surrounding the cone points. Consider the epimorphism
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}
defined as

\Phi(\alpha)=(0,0),\quad\Phi(\beta)=(0,1),\quad\Phi(x_{1})=(1,0),\quad\Phi(x_{2})=(d-1,0).

Then
\Phi
is a surface kernel epimorphism which determines a topological action with branching data
(1;d,d)
and covering surface of genus
g=1+m(d-1)
.
Let
\gamma=\alpha
and consider the multicurve
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{d}
, and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}
is trivial. Hence, by Proposition
7.2
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is the graph
C^{d}_{m}
.

(ii) For
d=1
, take an orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
of signature
(g;\,)
with
g\geq 2
, and let
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
be a homology basis. Consider the epimorphism
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
defined as

\Phi(\alpha_{1})=(1,0),\quad\Phi(\alpha_{i})=\Phi(\beta_{j})=(0,1),\hbox{ for all }i>1\hbox{ and for all }j.

Then
\Phi
is a surface kernel epimorphism. Taking
\gamma=\beta_{1}
, we have that
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}={\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}=\langle(0,1)\rangle
. Then, by Proposition
7.2
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
is the graph
C^{1}_{m}
.

Examples 3.

(i) Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold of signature
(1;2^{4})
and let
\alpha,\beta,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
a homology basis, where the
x_{i}
are the loops surrounding the cone points, all with the same orientation. Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{b}
be the epimorphism defined as

\Phi(\alpha)=(0,1,0),\quad\Phi(\beta)=(0,0,1),\quad\Phi(x_{1})=\Phi(x_{2})=\Phi(x_{3})=\Phi(x_{4})=(1,0,0).

Let
\gamma
be an arc joining the cone points
q_{1},q_{2}
. Then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}=G
,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\approx{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, so that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has one vertex and
|G|/2
loops. In this case the covering surface has genus
g=1+2ab
.

(ii) Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be as before. Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{b}
the epimorphism defined as

\begin{array}[]{ll }\Phi(\alpha)=(0,0,1,0),&\Phi(\beta)=(0,0,0,1),\cr\Phi(x_{1})=\Phi(x_{2})=(1,0,0,0),&\Phi(x_{3})=\Phi(x_{4})=(0,1,0,0).\end{array}

Let
\gamma
be an arc joining the cone points
q_{1},q_{3}
. Then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}=G
,
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\approx{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, so that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has one vertex and
|G|/4
loops. In this case the covering surface has genus
g=1+4ab
.

(iii) Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold of signature
(1;2^{2})
and let
\alpha,\beta,x_{1},x_{2}
a basis of homology. Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{b}
the epimorphism defined as

\Phi(\alpha)=(0,1,0),\;\Phi(\beta)=(0,0,1),\;\Phi(x_{1})=(1,0,0),\;\Phi(x_{2})=(1,0,0).

Let
\gamma
be an arc joining the two cone points and let
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}\approx{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{b}
and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}\approx{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has 2 vertices and
|G|/2
edges joining them. In this case the covering surface has genus
g=1+ab
.

(iv) Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be an orbifold with signature
(0;2^{5})
and let
x_{i}
loops around the cone points
q_{i}
, all with the same orientation.
Let
\Phi\colon H_{1}({\mathcal{O}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
the epimorphism defined as

\Phi(x_{1})=(1,1,0),\quad\Phi(x_{2})=(1,0,1),\quad\Phi(x_{3})=(0,1,1),\quad\Phi(x_{4})=\Phi(x_{5})=(0,0,1).

Let
\gamma
be an arc joining the cone points
q_{1},q_{2}
and let
\Sigma=\{\gamma\}
. Then
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}=\langle(0,1,1),(1,0,1)\rangle
and
{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}=\langle(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\rangle
. Hence
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has two vertices and
2=\frac{|G|}{4}
edges joining them. One can construct more examples from this one, for instance, by adding genus
g
to the orbifold
{\mathcal{O}}
, taking
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{1}}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{g}}
and defining
\Phi
for the extra generators
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
as
\Phi(\alpha_{i})=\Phi(\beta_{i})=(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)
where the
1
is in the
(3+i)
-th place. We see that
|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|
contains all the elements
(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)
where the 1 is in the
i
-th place for any
i>1
. Thus,
\frac{|G|}{|{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{1}|}=2
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has 2 vertices. On the other hand,

{\rm Im}\,\Phi_{\gamma}=\langle(1,1,0,\dots,0),(1,0,1,0\dots,0)\rangle,

so it has 4 elements. Hence
{\mathcal{D}}_{\tilde{\Sigma}}
has
\frac{|G|}{4}=2a_{1}\dots a_{g}
edges joining the 2 vertices.

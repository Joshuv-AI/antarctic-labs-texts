Serum miR-576-5p as a novel diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for osteoarthritis via targeting KLF10-mediated chondrocyte dysfunction
Authors: Mengye Weng, Zhigang Yang, Xiaoying Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disease characterized by progressive cartilage breakdown, and currently lacks effective biomarkers and disease-modifying therapies. Dysregulated microRNAs (miRNAs) have been implicated in OA pathogenesis. This study explores the diagnostic potential and functional mechanism of miR-576-5p in OA.MethodsSerum levels of miR-576-5p were measured in 61 OA patients and 60 healthy controls (HC) using qRT-PCR. Diagnostic performance was assessed using ROC analysis. An in vitro model of chondrocyte injury was established by treating the human chondrocyte cell line C-28/I2 with IL-1β. The effects of miR-576-5p on cell viability, apoptosis, and inflammatory response were evaluated. Bioinformatic predictions and dual-luciferase reporter assays were used to identify Krüppel-like factor 10 (KLF10) as a target of miR-576-5p. Rescue experiments were conducted to confirm the functional interaction between miR-576-5p and KLF10.ResultsOA patients exhibited significantly lower serum miR-576-5p levels than HC, with an AUC of 0.886. Low miR-576-5p expression correlated with higher KL grades, WOMAC/VAS scores, and elevated CRP/CTX-II/COMP levels. In IL-1β-stimulated chondrocytes, miR-576-5p was downregulated, leading to decreased viability, increased apoptosis, and elevated IL-6/IL-8/TNF-α secretion. These effects were reversed by miR-576-5p overexpression. KLF10 was confirmed as a direct target, showing inverse correlation with miR-576-5p. Overexpression of KLF10 counteracted the protective effects of miR-576-5p, reinstating inflammatory response and apoptosis.ConclusionsmiR-576-5p represents a promising diagnostic biomarker for OA and alleviates disease progression by targeting KLF10, thereby reducing chondrocyte dysfunction. These results offer new insights into miRNA-based therapeutics for OA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09322-3.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, MiR-576-5p, KLF10, Chondrocyte apoptosis, Inflammation, Diagnostic biomarker

Background

Osteoarthritis (OA), the most prevalent degenerative joint disorder worldwide, is recognized as a whole-joint disease characterized by progressive damage to articular cartilage, synovial inflammation, abnormal bone remodeling, and other joint structural changes. These pathological processes involve multiple joint tissues, including the infrapatellar fat pad, menisci, ligaments, and subchondral bone, ultimately leading to chronic pain, functional impairment, and disability [1]. The World Health Organization reports that OA affects over 20% of individuals aged 60 or older, with its socioeconomic burden escalating in tandem with global aging trends. Current clinical interventions, such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and joint replacement, primarily alleviate symptoms but fail to halt cartilage degradation [2, 3]. Moreover, long-term pharmacological treatments are associated with notable risks, such as gastrointestinal bleeding and cardiovascular complications [4].

Recent studies have illuminated chondrocyte dysfunction as a central catalyst of OA pathology, initiating a detrimental cycle characterized by an imbalance in extracellular matrix metabolism and the persistent activation of chronic inflammatory signaling pathways, which together expedite joint degeneration [5]. Nonetheless, current care models remain largely confined to passive symptom management, revealing strategic deficiencies in critical areas such as the regulation of cartilage metabolic homeostasis and the inhibition of pathological cascades [6]. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms that govern chondrocyte homeostasis not only unveils promising targets for the development disease-modifying therapies but also facilitates the design of precision care protocols tailored to specific pathological mechanisms [7]. This shift from empirical symptomatic treatment to proactive targeted intervention holds significant clinical importance for improving long-term functional outcomes in OA patients.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), small non-coding RNAs governing gene expression, exhibit multifaceted regulatory potential in OA pathogenesis [8]. Clinically, serum levels of miR-140-3p and miR-671-3p show promising diagnostic capabilities through their modulation of lipid metabolism and oxidative stress pathways [9]. Notably, miR-214-3p mitigates cartilage inflammation via suppressing NF-κB signaling, demonstrating an inverse correlation with radiographic progression of OA [10]. Additionally, miR-199b-5p ameliorates cartilage damage in OA models by targeting Fzd6 and Gcnt2 to inhibit Wnt/β-catenin signaling [11]. These findings not only validate the clinical utility of miRNAs as diagnostic tools for OA but also underscore their potential as therapeutic targets.

Of particular note, bioinformatics studies have suggested that miR-576-5p is highly expressed in normal cartilage and may be involved in OA-relevant pathways such as TGF-β and mTOR [12]. Its functional roles have been documented in other diseases: it suppresses osteosarcoma proliferation via the FKBP1A pathway [13], modulates epithelial-mesenchymal transition in colon adenocarcinoma [14]. In preeclampsia, miR-576-5p overexpression rescues trophoblast dysfunction by counteracting IL1RAP-mediated inflammation [15]. However, the specific role and regulatory mechanisms of miR-576-5p in OA remain unclear, presenting a significant knowledge gap that this study aims to address.

This study systematically evaluates the diagnostic utility and functional mechanistic of miR-576-5p in OA. By analyzing serum samples from OA patients and healthy controls, it evaluates the diagnostic performance of miR-576-5p using ROC curve analysis. Complementary in vitro experiments employ IL-1β-induced chondrocyte models to dissect the effects of miR-576-5p on cell viability, apoptosis, and inflammatory responses. Bioinformatics and functional validation further identify downstream targets and signaling pathways. The results of this study are expected to not only introduce a novel biomarker for early OA diagnosis but also provide a theoretical basis for targeting the miR-576-5p to intervene in cartilage degeneration.

Methods

Study population

From January 2021 to December 2023, a total of 61 patients diagnosed with knee OA and 60 healthy volunteers (HC) were consecutively recruited for this study. The diagnosis of OA adhered strictly to the 1994 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) clinical criteria [16], and was corroborated by radiographic assessment of the knee, specifically employing the Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grading system, with grades II–III. Healthy controls participants had no history of joint disease, pain, or mobility limitations in the six months preceding the study, and exhibited normal radiographic findings.

Inclusion criteria for both groups included age ≥ 18 years, absence of infectious diseases, and complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria encompassed autoimmune disorders (e.g., autoimmune hepatitis, ankylosing spondylitis), malignancies, pregnancy, osteoporosis, substance abuse, and hepatic/renal dysfunction.

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University, with written informed consent obtained from all participants or legal guardians.

Cell culture

The human chondrocyte cell line C-28/I2 (Shanghai Qingqi Biotechnology, China) was maintained in DMEM medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) supplemented as follows: 10% fetal bovine serum; 100 U/mL penicillin; and 100 mg/L streptomycin (all from Gibco, USA). Cells were cultured at 37 °C under 5% CO₂ in a humidified incubator (Thermo Scientific, Model Forma 3110). Medium was refreshed every 48 h. Cells were passaged using trypsin upon reaching 80% confluence and utilized for experiments after 2–3 passages.

Cell transfection and OA model establishment

Plasmids for miR-576-5p overexpression (miR-mimic), negative control (miR-NC), KLF10 overexpression (oe-KLF10), and corresponding negative control (oe-NC) were designed and synthesized by Guangzhou RioBio Co., Ltd. (China). The miR-mimic sense strand sequence was: 5’-AUUCUAAUUUCUCCACGUCUUU-3’; The miR-NC sense strand sequence was 5´-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3´; The oe-KLF10 plasmid was constructed by cloning the full-length coding sequence (CDS) of human KLF10 (GenBank: NM_005655.4; sequence: 5´-ATGCTCAACTTCGGTGCCTCT…CACAGTGA-3´) into the BamHI and XhoI restriction sites of the pcDNA3.1 (+) mammalian expression vector (Invitrogen; catalog #V790-20); The oe-NC was the empty pcDNA3.1 (+) vector, which has a well-defined sequence (NCBI RefSeq: AY429258.1). All constructs were verified by Sanger sequencing (Sanger sequencer: Applied Biosystems 3730xl). Transfections were performed using the Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, with the following working concentrations: miR-576-5p mimic and miR-NC at 50 nM; oe-KLF10 and oe-NC plasmids at 2 µg/well (for 6-well plates). Lipofectamine 2000 was used at a ratio of 2.5:1 (µL reagent: µg nucleic acid) for optimal transfection efficiency. Forty-eight hours post-transfection, cells were collected to establish the OA cell model. For inducing OA-like injury, C28/I2 cells were treated with 10 ng/mL interleukin-1β (IL-1β) for 24 h (IL-1β group), while untreated and untransfected cells served as the control group (Ctrl).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Serum samples were collected from 61 OA patients and 60 HC, with 5 mL of peripheral venous blood drawn from each participant and centrifuged to separate serum. Total RNA was extracted from serum samples and chondrocytes using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). For the synthesis and amplification of cDNA, reverse transcription and SYBR Premix Ex Taq GC (Takara, Japan) were employed. The reactions were carried out on a FAST 7500 system (Applied Biosystems, USA), adhering to the following conditions: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 7 min, followed by 40 cycles of 90 °C for 35 s, 72 °C for 40 s, and concluding with a final extension at 75 °C for 10 min. The expression levels of miR-576-5p and target gene were normalized to U6 and GAPDH, respectively, in accordance with the 2−ΔΔCT method. The sequences of all primers utilized in this study are enumerated in Table S1.

Identification of miR-576-5p downstream target genes

Potential miR-576-5p targets were predicted using miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), and StarBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/panCancer.php) databases. Overlapping genes from Venn analysis were further filtered based on differential expression in OA versus healthy sera.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Wild-type (KLF10-WT) and mutant (KLF10-MUT) 3′-UTR fragments of KLF10, spanning positions 815–821, were synthesized and cloned into pGL3 vectors (Promega, USA). The KLF10-WT fragment contains the miR-576-5p binding sequence: 5’-UACACUGCACUUUGUUUAGAAAA-3’. The KLF10-MUT fragment carries a mutation in the binding site: 5’-UACACUGCACUUUGUAAUCUUAA-3’. The mutant sequence was designed by replacing the UUAGAA seed-complementary site in the wild-type sequence with its reverse complement, AAUCUU, to completely abolish miR-576-5p binding. Chondrocytes were co-transfected with these constructs (100 ng/well for pGL3 vectors) and miR-576-5p mimic or miR-NC (50 nM) using Lipofectamine 2000 (ratio 2.5:1, µL reagent: µg nucleic acid; Invitrogen, USA). Luciferase activity was measured 48 h post-transfection using a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity for each sample.

CCK-8 assay for cell viability

Chondrocytes from each treatment group, as well as the Ctrl group, were carefully seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 10³ cells per well. Following incubation at specified time intervals of 0, 24, 48, and 72 h under standard culture conditions, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was introduced to each well. Subsequently, a 2-hour incubation period was allowed to elapse. The absorbance at 450 nm (OD450) was then measured using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA).

Flow cytometry for apoptosis detection

After treatment, cells were harvested and resuspended in binding buffer. Annexin V-FITC (5 µL) and propidium iodide (10 µL) (BD Biosciences, USA) were subsequently added to 1 × 10⁵ cells, followed by 10-minute incubation in the dark. Apoptotic rates were analyzed using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA).

ELISA for inflammatory cytokines

The cell culture supernatant was collected and the levels of TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8 were quantified using specific commercial ELISA kit (Beyotime, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The catalog numbers are as follows: TNF-α (PT518), IL-6 (PI330), and IL-8 (PI605). The assays were performed by adding samples and standards to the pre-coated plates, followed by incubation with biotinylated detection antibodies and streptavidin-HRP. After adding the substrate solution, the reaction was stopped, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm. using a microplate reader (BioTek, USA). Cytokine concentrations were calculated by interpolating from the standard curve.

Western blot analysis

To assess protein expression levels of KLF10, extracellular matrix markers (COL2A1, ACAN, MMP-13, ADAMTS-5), and signaling molecules (p-p65, p65, p-p38, p38), transfected cells were lysed using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined via BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (30 µg) were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against: KLF10 (1:1000; Abcam, Shanghai, China), GAPDH (1:5000; Abcam, Shanghai, China), COL2A1 (1:1000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), ACAN (1:1000; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), MMP-13 (1:1000; Abways, Hangzhou, China), ADAMTS-5 (1:1000; Abways, Hangzhou, China), p-p65 (1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China), p65 (1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China), p-p38 (1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China), and p38 (1:1000; Cell Signaling Technology, Shanghai, China). Following three washes with TBST, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000; ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL detection system (Millipore, Beijing, China) and quantified by densitometry with ImageJ software, normalized to GAPDH.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

Magna RIP experiments were performed using the Magna RIP™ RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Huibai Biotechnology, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Chondrocytes were lysed in RIP lysis buffer, and cell lysates were incubated with magnetic beads conjugated to anti-Ago2 antibody or control IgG at 4 °C overnight. After washing, immunoprecipitated RNA was extracted and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. qRT-PCR was used to detect the enrichment of miR-576-5p and KLF10 mRNA in the immunoprecipitates.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data are presented numerically. Continuous clinical data and cell-based experimental results (with five replicates) are reported as mean ± variance. GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad, USA) was employed for statistical analyses. For comparisons between two groups, Student’s t-test was employed, whereas one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s post-hoc test was utilized for multi-group comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study cohort and diagnostic value of miR-576-5p

A total of 61 OA patients and 60 HC were enrolled in the study. No significant differences in age, body mass index (BMI), or gender distribution were observed between the two groups (P > 0.05), confirming the comparability of baseline characteristics. The clinical profile of the OA patients, including disease duration, affected joint type, K-L grade (predominantly grades Ⅱ-Ⅲ), Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Arthritis Index (WOMAC) score, Visual Analog Scale (VAS) pain score, as well as serum inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein (CRP)) and cartilage degradation biomarkers (C-telopeptide of type II collagen (CTX-II), and Cartilage Oligomeric Matrix Protein (COMP)), were consistent with the diagnostic criteria for OA (Table 1).

Correlation between miR-576-5p expression and OA severity

Statistical analysis revealed that the low-expression group had significantly higher K-L grade (higher proportion of grade Ⅲ), WOMAC score, VAS pain score, and serum levels of CRP, CTX-II, and COMP compared to the high-expression group (P < 0.05). Protective effect of miR-576-5p against IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury

In C28/I2 human chondrocytes, treatment with IL-1β resulted in a significant downregulation of miR-576-5p expression (Fig. 2a, P < 0.01). This alteration was associated with a marked decrease in cell viability (Fig. 2b, P < 0.001), an increase in the apoptosis rate (Fig. 2c, P < 0.01), and enhanced secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 (Fig. 2d, P < 0.01), IL-8 (Fig. 2e, P < 0.01), and TNF-α (Fig. 2f, P < 0.01). Transfection with miR-576-5p mimic (miR-mimic) significantly attenuated IL-1β-induced impairment of cell viability, apoptosis, and inflammation (P < 0.01). These findings suggest that the overexpression of miR-576-5p confers protective effects on chondrocytes against inflammatory-induced damage.

miR-576-5p regulates chondrocyte function by targeting KLF10

Through intersecting analyses of TargetScan, miRDB, and miRWalk databases, 13 potential target genes of miR-576-5p were identified (Fig. 3a). Heatmap analysis (Fig. 3b) that KLF10 was significantly differentially expressed in serum between OA patients and HC. Compared to HC, OA patients exhibited markedly increased KLF10 levels (Fig. 4a, P < 0.001). Furthermore, KLF10 expression inversely correlated with miR-576-5p (r = −0.780, P < 0.001; Fig. 4b). Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-576-5p directly bound to the wild-type 3’UTR of KLF10, suppressing luciferase activity, while no effect was observed on the mutant sequence (Fig. 4c, P < 0.01). The RIP assay further verified that miR-576-5p and KLF10 mRNA were co-enriched in Ago2 immunoprecipitates compared to non-specific IgG control immunoprecipitates, confirming their interaction within the RNA-induced silencing complex (Fig. 4d, P < 0.001).

Rescue experiments showed that KLF10 overexpression abrogated the protective effects of miR-576-5p against IL-1β injury, manifested as decreased cell viability (Fig. 5a, P < 0.01), increased apoptosis rate (Fig. 5b, P < 0.01), and enhanced secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α (Fig. 5c-e, P < 0.01).

Effects of miR-576-5p and KLF10 on basal chondrocyte viability

To address the potential effects of transfection constructs on basal cell viability, we detected cell viability in C28/I2 cells transfected with miR-mimic, miR-NC, oe-KLF10, or oe-NC under normal culture conditions (without IL-1β treatment). Results showed that compared with miR-NC, miR-mimic transfection significantly increased basal cell viability (P < 0.001). Conversely, transfection with the oe-KLF10 plasmid alone resulted in a slight decrease in cell viability compared to its empty vector control (oe-NC) (P < 0.001; Fig. S1). However, the magnitude of these basal effects was mild (≤ 20% change relative to controls). In contrast, in the IL-1β-induced injury model, the regulatory effects of miR-mimic (rescuing viability by ~ 50%) and oe-KLF10 (aggravating viability loss by ~ 40%) were substantially more pronounced (P < 0.0001 vs. respective controls), indicating that the primary role of these molecules in our study context is to mediate IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury rather than exert non-specific basal effects.

miR-576-5p/KLF10 axis regulates extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis in IL-1β-treated chondrocytes

To evaluate the effect of miR-576-5p/KLF10 on cartilage matrix turnover, we detected the protein expression of ECM synthesis markers (COL2A1, ACAN) and degradation markers (MMP-13, ADAMTS-5) in chondrocytes under IL-1β stimulation. Results showed that IL-1β treatment significantly reduced COL2A1 and ACAN protein levels (P < 0.001 vs. Ctrl; Fig. S2a) while increasing MMP-13 and ADAMTS-5 expression (P < 0.001 vs. Ctrl; Fig. S2b). Transfection with miR-576-5p mimic reversed these effects: COL2A1 and ACAN were upregulated (P < 0.001 vs. IL-1β + miR-NC), whereas MMP-13 and ADAMTS-5 were downregulated (P < 0.001 vs. IL-1β + miR-NC). Importantly, co-transfection with oe-KLF10 abrogated the regulatory effects of miR-mimic, leading to decreased COL2A1/ACAN and increased MMP-13/ADAMTS-5 compared to the IL-1β + miR-mimic group (P < 0.001) (The specific uncropped Western blot image is available in the Supplementary file).

miR-576-5p targets KLF10 to suppress the NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways

To explore the downstream mechanisms, we investigated the NF-κB and MAPK pathways, which are critically involved in OA pathogenesis. IL-1β treatment markedly increased the phosphorylation levels of p65 (NF-κB subunit) and p38 MAPK (Fig. S3). miR-576-5p overexpression significantly inhibited IL-1β-induced activation of both pathways. Conversely, forced expression of KLF10 not only reactivated these pathways but also counteracted the suppressive effects of miR-576-5p. This indicates that KLF10 is a key upstream regulator of pro-inflammatory signaling in chondrocytes and that miR-576-5p exerts its anti-inflammatory and protective effects by suppressing the KLF10-mediated activation of NF-κB and MAPK pathways (The specific uncropped Western blot image is available in the Supplementary file).

Discussion

miRNAs, distinguished by their remarkable stability in blood and their specific expression profiles in various diseases, have emerged as a focal point in OA research for both diagnosis and therapeutic applications [17]. For example, serum miR-153-3p has demonstrated significant efficacy in differentiating healthy individuals from patients with knee OA [18]. Additionally, alterations in the expression of the miR-17–92 cluster exhibit a strong correlation with the pathological progression of OA, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker [19]. Elevated plasma levels of miR-146a and miR-365 in OA patients, which positively correlate with proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-6, further underscore the multi-dimensional clinical value of miRNAs in OA assessment [20]. Consistent with previous findings showing miR-576-5p downregulation in OA cartilage [12], this study also observed significantly lower serum miR-576-5p levels in OA patients. The diagnostic efficacy of serum miR-576-5p (AUC = 0.886) demonstrated in our study appears to be superior to several previously reported biomarkers for OA. For instance, the AUC for serum miR-338-3p was 0.82 [21], while other traditional biomarkers like CRP and COMP showed AUCs ranging from 0.7 to 0.9 [22, 23]. Although differences in study populations and methodologies exist, these comparisons suggest that the diagnostic accuracy of miR-576-5p is at least comparable to that of established biomarkers. Moreover, given the complexity of OA, its diagnosis may benefit from a multi-biomarker approach supported by emerging evidence [24]. Future prospective multi-center studies using freshly collected samples should systematically compare the diagnostic performance of miR-576-5p with other miRNAs and established biomarkers such as CTX-II and COMP. Exploring combined biomarker panels may help construct more clinically applicable diagnostic models and further evaluate their translational potential.

Commonly utilized clinical metrics for OA encompass the K-L grade, which serves as an indicator of structural joint damage; the WOMAC score, assessing pain, stiffness, and functional impairment; and the VAS score, quantifying pain intensity [25]. Additionally, biomarkers CRP, CTX-II, and COMP elucidate aspects of systemic inflammation, cartilage collagen degradation, and matrix destruction, respectively [26, 27]. The findings of this study observed that all these clinical and biochemical parameters were significantly worse in patients exhibiting low expression levels of miR-576-5p. Notably, diminished miR-576-5p expression correlates with increased structural damage, greater functional impairment, and heightened inflammatory response in OA patients. Furthermore, previous investigations suggest that miR-576-5p deficiency may upregulate catabolic enzymes like ADAMTS-5 and MMP3, accelerating degeneration in vertebral disc cartilage endplate cells [28]. Taken together, these findings collectively suggest that miR-576-5p may hold potential as a supplementary clinical indicator in OA management. Its association with key clinical metrics of structural damage, functional impairment, and inflammatory activity raises the possibility that it could assist in evaluating disease severity, monitoring progression, or even predicting treatment responses—though such applications would require rigorous validation in larger, multi-center cohorts. Additionally, the correlation between reduced miR-576-5p and exacerbated pathological features hints at its potential role in regulating core OA processes, such as inflammation and cartilage matrix homeostasis, at the molecular level. Exploring these mechanistic links in cellular models may further clarify how miR-576-5p influences chondrocyte function, thereby laying a foundation for translating these observations into more targeted clinical strategies.

Chondrocytes, the principal functional cells of articular cartilage, are essential for maintaining cartilage homeostasis. Aberrant apoptosis of these cells and an exaggerated inflammatory response, triggered by pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β, play pivotal roles in the pathogenesis of OA [29]. Multiple miRNAs have been shown to modulate this process by targeting downstream effectors of IL-1β signaling. For instance, miR-320a alleviates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury by inhibiting DAZAP1 and the ERK/JNK/MAPK pathway [30]; miR-16-5p blocks MAPK signaling by targeting MAP2K1 [31]; and miR-24-3p reduces apoptosis by inhibiting BCL2L12 [32]. Concurrently, this study found that IL-1β treatment downregulated miR-576-5p in chondrocytes, leading to decreased cell viability, increased apoptosis, and elevated secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α. These pathological changes were partially reversed by miR-576-5p overexpression, indicating direct involvement of miR-576-5p in counteracting IL-1β-mediated damage. These findings echo observations in the context of preeclampsia, where miR-576-5p overexpression enhances trophoblast function and suppresses inflammation through modulation of IL1RAP [15]. These observations further suggest that miR-576-5p may regulate chondrocyte proliferation, apoptosis, and inflammation networks through conserved molecular mechanisms.

Bioinformatic analysis of miR-576-5p downstream targets led to the identification of 13 candidate genes through intersection screening. Among these, KLF10 emerged as a gene of particular interest, displaying significant and abnormal upregulation in the serum of OA patient serum compared to healthy controls. KLF10 serves as a pivotal downstream mediator within the TGF-β signaling pathway. Its overexpression can accelerate defects in mitophagy, exacerbating oxidative stress-induced chondrocyte senescence [33]. KLF10 also regulates chondrocyte hypertrophy during skeletal development [34] and promotes matrix calcification in OA [35], indicating its potential as a therapeutic target. In this study, the use of dual-luciferase reporter assays provides the first evidence that KLF10 is a direct target of miR-576-5p. Notably, the expression levels of KLF10 showed a strong negative correlation with miR-576-5p expression in OA patient serum. Furthermore, miR-576-5p overexpression specifically suppressed KLF10 transcription. Functional rescue experiments demonstrated that forced expression of KLF10 counteracted the protective effects of miR-576-5p on chondrocytes, reactivating apoptosis and inflammatory responses. This mechanism aligns well with the known function of KLF10 in OA, where it exacerbates cartilage degeneration by upregulating pro-inflammatory factors and inhibiting matrix synthesis [36]. Taken together, these results position KLF10 as the central mediator of miR-576-5p’s functional effects, highlighting its crucial role in OA and reinforcing its potential as a therapeutic target.

.

Despite revealing the important role of the miR-576-5p–KLF10 axis in OA, this study has several limitations. First, the clinical investigation employed a single-center design with a relatively small sample size. Future multi-center studies with larger and more diverse cohorts are necessary to further validate the diagnostic efficacy and general applicability of serum miR-576-5p. Second, although line C28/I2 chondrocyte line provides a reproducible model for initial mechanistic studies, it does not fully replicate the biological behavior of primary chondrocytes, particularly in matrix synthesis and inflammatory response. Future work should validate these findings in primary human chondrocytes and animal models of OA to better approximate in vivo pathophysiology. Third, while we have preliminarily identified NF-κB and MAPK as potential downstream pathways affected by KLF10, a comprehensive understanding of its regulatory network remains incomplete. Further omics-based approaches (e.g., RNA-seq, proteomics) are warranted to elucidate the full spectrum of KLF10-mediated signaling in OA. In addition, the cellular origin of serum miR-576-5p remains unclear. Although its expression and functional role in chondrocytes were established in vitro, it is possible that miR-576-5p is also released from other joint tissues affected in OA, such as the synovium or infrapatellar fat pad. Future studies should aim to trace the tissue source of circulating miR-576-5p, possibly through miRNA in situ hybridization or tissue-specific extracellular vesicle profiling.

Regarding clinical translation, miR-576-5p shows promise not only as a diagnostic biomarker but also as a potential therapeutic target. The development of miRNA-based therapeutics (e.g., miR-576-5p mimics or KLF10-targeting antagonists) may offer novel strategies for modulating chondrocyte function and inflammation in OA. Further investigation into delivery systems—such as nanoparticle-based intra-articular administration—could facilitate its transition toward clinical application.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study systematically reveals the dual role of miR-576-5p in OA: its serum expression level can serve as an efficient diagnostic biomarker, and by targeting and inhibiting KLF10, miR-576-5p can alleviate IL-1β-induced inflammatory responses, apoptosis, and matrix metabolic imbalance in chondrocytes. This finding not only provides new targets for clinical precision diagnosis and treatment but also offers molecular-level guidance for the development of early screening and stratified intervention strategies in nursing practice.

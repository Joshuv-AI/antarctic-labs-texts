Regularity properties of a generalized Oseen evolution operator in exterior domains, with applications to the Navier-Stokes initial value problem
Yosuke Asami, Toshiaki Hishida

Abstract
Consider a generalized Oseen evolution operator in 3D exterior domains, that is generated by a non-autonomous linearized system arising from time-dependent rigid motions. This was found by Hansel and Rhandi, and then the theory was developed by the second author, however, desired regularity properties such as estimate of the temporal derivative as well as the Hölder estimate have remained open. The present paper provides us with those properties together with weighted estimates of the evolution operator. The results are then applied to the Navier-Stokes initial value problem, so that a new theorem on existence of a unique strong L q L^{q} -solution locally in time is proved. MSC: 35Q30, 76D05 Keywords: Navier-Stokes flow, exterior domain, evolution operator, rigid motion.

Introduction

Let us consider the motion of a viscous incompressible fluid filling the exterior of an obstacle in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
where the obstacle is a rigid body whose motion is prescribed and can be time-dependent.
We prefer to take a frame attached to the moving obstacle in order that the problem is reduced to the one in a fixed exterior domain
D
,
whose boundary
\partial D
is assumed to be of class
C^{1,1}
.
The resultant set of equations reads, see
[
17
]
,

\begin{split}&\left.\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u&=\Delta u+(\eta+\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla u-\omega\times u-\nabla p\\
\mbox{div $u$}&=0\\
\end{array}\right\}\;\;\mbox{in $D\times(0,\infty)$},\\
&\quad u|_{\partial D}=\eta+\omega\times x,\qquad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0,\qquad u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\end{split}

(1.1)

where
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p=p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
denote the velocity and pressure
of the fluid, whereas
\eta=\eta(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\omega=\omega(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are, respectively, translational and angular
velocities of the body, and
u_{0}
stands for a given initial velocity.

The Navier-Stokes initial value problem (
1.1
) has attracted much attention of several authors
since the work
[
2
]
on the Leray-Hopf weak solutions, because the drift operator

(\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla
arising from rotation of the body causes a hyperbolic aspect and makes the problem challenging.
In fact, the semigroup generated by the linearized operator with a constant rigid motion
is no longer analytic (
[
10
,
32
]
) unless the rotation is absent, nevertheless it enjoys remarkable smoothing effect
(
[
22
,
32
]
), see

[
35
]
for the details and further references.
In this paragraph, let us focus on the autonomous system with purely rotating body
(constant
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and
\eta=0
).
A unique mild solution
was constructed locally in time first by the second author
[
32
]
in
L^{2}
and then by Geissert, Heck and Hieber
[
22
]
in
L^{q}

via the semigroup approach, while Galdi and Silvestre
[
20
]
adopted the other method using the Galerkin approximation
to show the existence of a unique local solution in
L^{2}

when the initial velocity satisfies

u_{0}\in W^{2,2}(D),\quad(\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla u_{0}\in L^{2}(D),\quad u_{0}|_{\partial D}=\omega\times x,\quad\mbox{div $u_{0}$}=0.

(1.2)

The latter solution is a strong one with
\partial_{t}u

belonging to
L^{\infty}
in time with values in
L^{2}(D)
.
In
[
20
]
it was also proved that if (
1.2
) is fulfilled for
u_{0}
replaced by
u_{0}-u_{s}
with

u_{s}
being a steady solution whose magnitude is controlled by
|\omega|
,
then problem (
1.1
) admits a global strong solution that
tends to

as

under smallness conditions on
\|u_{0}-u_{s}\|_{W^{1,2}(D)}
and
|\omega|
,
yielding the stability of

.
Later on, the stability of steady solutions was studied within the framework of
L^{q}
by Shibata and the second
author
[
40
]
under the smallness on
\|u_{0}-u_{s}\|_{L^{3,\infty}(D)}
as well as

with use of
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
decay estimates of the semigroup, where
L^{3,\infty}
denotes the weak-
L^{3}
space (one of the
Lorentz spaces).

When taking into account not only the rotation but also the translation, where constant vectors
\eta
and
\omega
are parallel to each other,

L^{q}
-
L^{r}
estimates as well as generation of the semigroup for the linearized autonomous problem
were established by Shibata
[
47
,
48
]
.
The similar result for the case of
a Lipschitz boundary
\partial D
has been recently
deduced by Takahashi and Watanabe
[
54
]
, in which one needs a restriction on the summability exponent
dependently on the Lipschitz character.
The latter result seems likely even for the non-autonomous case since the argument is based
mainly on
[
36
,
37
]
below as well as
[
22
]
.

For the non-autonomous regime with time-dependent rigid motions
\eta(t)+\omega(t)\times x
, which
we are going to address in this paper,
the existence of a unique mild solution locally in time to (
1.1
)
was proved by Hansel and Rhandi
[
29
]
under the assumption

\eta,\,\omega\in C^{1}([0,\infty);\,\mathbb{R}^{3})

(1.3)

when the initial velocity fulfills

u_{0}\in L^{q}(D)\;\mbox{with $q\in[3,\infty)$},\quad\nu\cdot\big(u_{0}-\eta(0)-\omega(0)\times x\big)|_{\partial D}=0,\quad\mbox{div $u_{0}$}=0,

where

denotes the outer unit normal
to

.
The assumption (

Under less regularity

,
the essential contribution of
[
29

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u+{\mathcal{L}}(t)u+\nabla p=0,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0\quad\mbox{in $D\times(s,\infty)$},\\
&u|_{\partial D}=0,\qquad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0,\qquad u(\cdot,s)=f,\end{split}

(1.6)

in their own way without using any existing theory found in
[
13
,
55
,
57
]

and to deduce

-

{\mathcal{L}}(t)=-\Delta-(\eta(t)+\omega(t)\times x)\cdot\nabla+\omega(t)\times.

(1.7)

The second author
[
36
,
37
]
established
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
decay estimates of this evolution operator
when assuming

\eta,\;\omega\in C^{0,\theta}([0,\infty);\,\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\,\mathbb{R}^{3})

for some
\theta\in(0,1]

Estimates obtained there were employed to show the existence of time-periodic solutions
(with
\eta
and

being periodic as well as regular to some extent) and their stability, see
[
38
,
41
]
.

All those results for (
1.1
) mentioned above, except
[
20
]
in

, are concerned with mild solutions, that is,
solutions to the integral equation described by use of the evolution operator or semigroup.
The difficulty of obtaining a strong solution stems from lack of analyticity of the semigroup generated by the corresponding autonomous system, as mentioned before,
and analysis is even more involved on account of the non-autonomous character.
In fact, we do not know whether or not the solution obtained by

[
29
]

becomes a strong one even if assuming more regularity of
\eta,\,\omega
than (
1.3

The objective of this paper is to provide a strong
L^{q}
-solution to (
1.1
)
in the sense that it is of class
C^{1}
in time with values in
L^{q}(D)

under the assumption

\eta,\;\omega\in C^{1,\vartheta}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);\,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad|\eta^{\prime}(t)|+|\omega^{\prime}(t)|=O(t^{-\gamma})\quad\mbox{as $t\to 0$}

(1.9)

for some
\vartheta\in(0,1]
and
\gamma\in[0,1)
, yielding (
1.5
) with
\theta=1-\gamma

as well as the well-definedness of
\eta,\,\omega
up to
t=0
,
see (
2.43
) and Remark
2.11
below,
when the initial velocity satisfies

\rho u_{0}\in L^{q}(D)\;\mbox{with $q\in(3,\infty)$},\quad\nu\cdot\big(u_{0}-\eta(0)-\omega(0)\times x\big)|_{\partial D}=0,\quad\mbox{div $u_{0}$}=0,

(1.10)

where
\rho
stands for the weight function

, which is related to the coefficient of the drift operator

(\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla
.
Our condition (
1.10
) on
u_{0}
should be compared with (
1.4
) for the mild
L^{q}
-solution and with (
1.2
)
for the strong
L^{2}
-solution with constant
\omega
(and
\eta=0
).
We emphasize that the result is new even for the autonomous case.
The critical case
q=3
is unfortunately missing in (
1.10
), and this is because the boundedness

\|\rho^{2}Pg\|_{q}\leq C\|\rho^{2}g\|_{q}
of the Fujita-Kato projection

associated with the Helmholtz decomposition holds true
in the weighted
L^{q}
space if and only if
q\in(3,\infty)
; indeed, this
comes from the Muckenhoupt theory of the solenoidal
L^{q}

space, see Farwig and Sohr
[
12
]
, and the recent contribution by Tomoki Takahashi
[
53
]
.

To the end described above, a novelty is
to develop the regularity theory of the evolution operator
T(t,s)

under the reasonable condition (
1.5
) on
\eta,\,\omega

(indeed, the only stage we need
(
1.9
) is to take care of the rigid motion at

, see (

), in constructing a Navier-Stokes flow),
specifically, the Hölder estimate of

and estimates of

as well as
\nabla^{2}T(t,s)f
in

,
see (

) for

, (

) and (

with

.
By

we are already aware of
(

) for
\partial_{t}T(t,s)f
under the additional condition
\rho\nabla f\in L^{q}(D)
,
that is however too restrictive to study the nonlinear problem.
On the other hand, without any additional condition, it is possible to deduce a subtle
estimate (
2.27
) for
\partial_{t}T(t,s)f
in

,
where

, see
[

; indeed this was useful in
[

]
,

A key ingredient of our analysis is to make use of weighted estimate, to be precise, estimate of

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{j}T(t,s)f\|_{q}

,

,

What should be still done to discuss (

) is to study the temporal derivative of the Duhamel term

v(t)=\int_{s}^{t}T(t,\sigma)g(\sigma)\,d\sigma

under suitable assumptions on the forcing term

.
If the evolution operator were of parabolic type in the sense of Tanabe and Sobolevskii,
then the theory would be standard, see
[

,
55
,
57
]
; however, it is not the case, so that the issue is quite nontrivial.
The only clue is to look deep into the structure of the evolution operator due to

]
,
that is constructed by use of the evolution operator,

, in the whole space

In view of the structure of this formula, analysis of the aforementioned evolution operator

Although our linear theory is applied merely to the classical setting (

) with a prescribed rigid motions in the present paper,

]
and the references therein,

and

being the force and torque
exerted by the fluid,
it turns out from the trace inequality that
we do need estimates of
\partial_{t}u,\nabla^{2}u

Having this in mind, we deduce estimates of those derivatives of

)

Finally, let us remark that there is the other way to formulate the problem at the beginning (also might be the only way
when there are several independent rigid bodies)
by use of a local transformation, which keeps the situation far from the body as it is,
so that the drift term

This nonlinear transformation was proposed by Cumsille and Tucsnak
[

to show the local existence of a unique strong solution in

for the autonomous case.
Dintelmann, Geissert and Hieber
[

adopted the same transformation even for the case of time-dependent rigid motions

Nevertheless, with this transformation, nontrivial basic flows such as a steady solution are lost and thus one cannot
proceed to the next stage of analysis, say, stability/attainability of those flows; indeed, this
would be a disadvantage as long as the obstacle is a single rigid body.

In section

We collect in section

The whole space problem is studied in section

Section

In section

In section

With complete linear theory at hand, in the final section, the main theorem on local existence of

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

u\in D_{q}(A)
, where
G_{R}=D_{2R}\setminus\overline{D_{R}}
.

Proof.

Given
u\in D_{q}(A)
, we set
h=Au
.
Then there is a pressure
p
such that

-\Delta u+\nabla p=h,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0\quad\mbox{in $D$};\quad u|_{\partial D}=0

where
p
is singled out in such a way that

\int_{G_{R}}p\,dx=0
, yielding

\|p\|_{q,G_{R}}\leq C\|\nabla p\|_{W^{-1,q}(G_{R})}\leq C\big(\|h\|_{q,G_{R}}+\|\nabla u\|_{q,G_{R}}\big).

(3.9)

Fix a cut-off function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2R})

satisfying
\phi=1
on
B_{R}
, and consider

v=\phi u-\mathbb{B}[u\cdot\nabla\phi]
and
p_{v}=\phi p

with
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{G_{R}}
being the Bogovskii operator defined in Lemma
3.1
,
which should obey

-\Delta v+\nabla p_{v}=f,\qquad\mbox{div $v$}=0\quad\mbox{in $D_{2R}$};\quad v|_{\partial D_{2R}}=0

with

f=\phi h-2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u-(\Delta\phi)u+\Delta\mathbb{B}[u\cdot\nabla\phi]+(\nabla\phi)p

fulfilling

\|f\|_{q,D_{2R}}\leq C\big(\|h\|_{q,D_{2R}}+\|\nabla u\|_{q,G_{R}}+\|u\|_{q,G_{R}}\big)

by taking into account (
3.9
) as well as (
3.2
).
This combined with estimate of
v
in
W^{2,q}(D_{2R})
in terms of

f
leads to (
3.8
) since
u=v
on
D_{R}
.
∎

\eta
and

The regularity estimate

will be

to show the continuity of

Given

, we set

, which corresponds to the boundary value problem

&u|_{\partial D}=0,\qquad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0,\end{split}

where

is given by (

To see (

),

for the solution to (

) of the class (

) below, where

) implies the estimate

as well from the equation.

on

obtained by

and then by
[

]
as well that

1+L
is invertible

leading to the uniqueness of solutions to (

) within the class (
3.14
).
Notice that the constant

in (

) is taken uniformly in

with

, because so is the constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(m,q,D)>0
for

.
The whole thing is thus the
L^{q}
-estimate of
\nabla^{2}u
for the equation

in

due to
[
8
,
9
,
19
]
, and (

) makes the issue easier.
We show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
\eta
and
\omega
are constant vectors.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
m\in(0,\infty)
.
Then the unique solution
u=(1+L)^{-1}Pf
to the problem (
3.11
) in the class, up to constants for the pressure,

u\in D_{q}(L),\qquad p\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\overline{D})\;\;\mbox{with $\nabla p\in L^{q}(D)$}

(3.14)

enjoys (
3.12
) for all
f\in L^{q}(D)
with some constant

C=C(m,q,D)>0
whenever
|\eta|+|\omega|\leq m
.
As a consequence, we have (
3.10
) for all
u\in D_{q}(L)
with some constant
C=C(m,q,D)>0
.

Proof.

We fix
R>1
, set
G=D_{2R}\setminus\overline{D_{R}}
and single out the pressure
p
associated with
u=(1+L)^{-1}Pf

in such a way that
\int_{G}p\,dx=0
.
Then, as in (
3.9
), via the first inequality there, we get

\|p\|_{q,G}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{q,G}+\|\nabla u\|_{q,G}+\|u\|_{q,G}\big)

(3.15)

from the equation.
Fix also the same cut-off function
\phi
as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, and consider

\begin{split}&v=(1-\phi)u+\mathbb{B}[u\cdot\nabla\phi],\qquad p_{v}=(1-\phi)p,\\
&w=\phi u-\mathbb{B}[u\cdot\nabla\phi],\qquad p_{w}=\phi p,\end{split}

where
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{G}
.
Then they obey, respectively,

v+{\mathcal{L}}v+\nabla p_{v}=f_{v},\qquad\mbox{div $v$}=0\quad\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$};\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}v=0,

(3.16)

w+{\mathcal{L}}w+\nabla p_{w}=f_{w},\qquad\mbox{div $w$}=0\quad\mbox{in $D_{2R}$};\quad w|_{D_{2R}}=0,

(3.17)

with

\begin{split}&f_{v}=(1-\phi)f+g,\qquad f_{w}=\phi f-g,\\
&g=2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u+\big[\Delta\phi+(\eta+\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\big]u+(1+{\mathcal{L}})\mathbb{B}[u\cdot\nabla\phi]-(\nabla\phi)p.\end{split}

It follows from
(
3.2
),
(
3.13
), (
3.15
)
and an interpolation inequality for
\|\nabla u\|_{q,G}
that

\begin{split}\|f_{v}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\|f_{w}\|_{q,D_{2R}}&\leq C\big(\|f\|_{q}+\|\nabla u\|_{q,G}+\|u\|_{q,G}\big)\\
&\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{q}+\varepsilon\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{q}\end{split}

(3.18)

for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Let us recall some results on the
L^{q}
-estimates for the whole space problem (
3.16
).
For the specific case
\omega=0
, it is just the classical Oseen (or even Stokes for
\eta=0
) problem,
see
[
18
, Theorem VII.4.1]
.
It is conveninent to discuss the other case
\omega\neq 0
through the Mozzi-Chasles transform
[
18
, Chapter VIII]
;
then the problem (
3.16
) is reduced to the one with
\eta=0
if
\omega\cdot\eta=0
, otherwise it is reduced to the case
when
\eta
and
\omega
are parallel to each other.
According to
[
9
]
for the former and to
[
8
,
19
]
for the latter, we have

\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\|\nabla p_{v}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\|f_{v}-v\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\|f_{v}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}

(3.19)

with some constant
C>0
independent of
(\eta,\omega)

since the
L^{q}
-norm is invariant under the aforementioned transform which is just a translation

x\mapsto\widetilde{x}=x-\frac{\omega\times\eta}{|\omega|^{2}}

and since

\|v\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\|f_{v}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}
by the same reasoning as in (
3.13
),
where the associated semigroup is a specific case of the evolution operator (
4.2
)
in the next section.

As for the interior problem (
3.17
), we employ the
L^{q}
-estimate for the Stokes resolvent system
(with the resolvent parameter
\lambda=1
), see Farwig and Sohr
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
, to furnish

\begin{split}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{q,D_{2R}}+\|\nabla p_{w}\|_{q,D_{2R}}&\leq C\|f_{w}+(\eta+\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla w-\omega\times w\|_{q,D_{2R}}\\
&\leq C\|f_{w}\|_{q,D_{2R}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{q,D_{2R}}\end{split}

(3.20)

where the latter inequality follows from the same reason as in (
3.18
).

Since
u=v+w
and
p=p_{v}+p_{w}
, we collect (
3.18
)–(
3.20
)
and then take
\varepsilon>0
small enough to conclude

\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{q}+\|\nabla p\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{q},

which together with (
3.13
) and an interpolation inequality lead us to (
3.12
).
The proof is complete.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
22
,
28
,
29
]
)
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be two Banach spaces, and let
{\mathcal{T}}\in(0,\infty)
.
Suppose that there are constants
\beta,\,\gamma\in[0,1)
and
c_{*}>0
with the following property:
The families

\{E_{0}(t,s);\,0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X_{1},X_{2}),\quad\{Q(t,s);\,0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X_{1})

fulfill

\|E_{0}(t,s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X_{1},X_{2})}\leq c_{*}(t-s)^{-\beta},\qquad\|Q(t,s)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X_{1})}\leq c_{*}(t-s)^{-\gamma}

for all
(t,s)
with
0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
as well as the strongly measurability of

E_{0}(t,s)f
and
Q(t,s)f
in
(t,s)
for every
f\in X_{1}
.
For
f\in X_{1}
and
0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
, define a sequence
\{E_{j}(t,s)f\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
by

E_{j+1}(t,s)f=\int_{s}^{t}E_{j}(t,\tau)Q(\tau,s)f\,d\tau\qquad(j=0,1,2,\cdots).

(3.21)

Then

E(t,s)f:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E_{j}(t,s)f\qquad\mbox{in $X_{2}$}

converges absolutely and uniformly in
(t,s)
with

0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
and
t-s\geq\varepsilon
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,{\mathcal{T}})
.
Moreover, there is a constant
C=C({\mathcal{T}},c_{*},\beta,\gamma)>0
such that

\|E(t,s)f\|_{X_{2}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\|E_{j}(t,s)f\|_{X_{2}}\leq C(t-s)^{-\beta}\|f\|_{X_{1}}

for all
(t,s)
with
0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
and
f\in X_{1}
.
If, in particular,
\beta=0
, then the convergence of the series above is uniform in
(t,s)
with
0\leq s\leq t\leq{\mathcal{T}}

as long as
E_{0}(t,t)\in{\mathcal{L}}(X_{1},X_{2})
is well-defined.

We note that the integrand
E_{j}(t,\tau)Q(\tau,s)f
of (
3.21
) is strongly measurable in
(t,\tau,s)
as a
X_{2}
-valued
function for every
f\in X_{1}
under the assumptions (
[
42
, Proposition 1.1.28]
).

The following elementary fact on the uniformly convergence will be needed in the proof of Theorem

,
see (

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and let

\{T(t,s)\}_{t\geq s\geq 0}
be the evolution operator on
L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
obtained in Proposition
2.1
.
Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\sup_{t\in J}\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)g(t-\varepsilon)-g(t)\|_{q}=0

(3.22)

for every compact interval
J\subset(s,\infty)
and
g\in C((s,\infty);\,L^{q}_{\sigma}(D))
, where
s\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\iota>0
be arbitrarily small, and
fix a compact interval
J=[\tau_{0},{\mathcal{T}}]\subset(s,\infty)
.
One can take finitely many points
\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset J

as well as
\delta>0
such that

\sup_{t\in J}\|g(t-\varepsilon)-g(t)\|_{q}\leq\iota\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)

(3.23)

and that,
for every
t\in J
, there is
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
satisfying

\|g(t)-g(t_{k})\|_{q}\leq\iota.

(3.24)

For each
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
we know
from Proposition
2.1

the continuity of
(t,s)\mapsto T(t,s)g(t_{j})
uniformly on

\{(t,s);\,0\leq s\leq t\leq{\mathcal{T}}\}
.
Hence there is
\varepsilon_{j}>0
such that, if
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{j}
, then

\sup_{t\in J}\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)g(t_{j})-g(t_{j})\|_{q}\leq\iota.

(3.25)

Set
\varepsilon_{*}:=\min\{\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{N},\delta\}
,
which is independent of
t\in J
, and let

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{*})
.
Given
t\in J
, we take
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
satisfying (
3.24
), from which together with
(
3.23
), (
3.25
) with
j=k
and (
2.13
) it follows that

\begin{split}&\quad\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)g(t-\varepsilon)-g(t)\|_{q}\\
&\leq\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)\big(g(t-\varepsilon)-g(t)\big)\|_{q}+\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)\big(g(t)-g(t_{k})\big)\|_{q}\\
&\quad+\|T(t,t-\varepsilon)g(t_{k})-g(t_{k})\|_{q}+\|g(t_{k})-g(t)\|_{q}\\
&\leq C\iota,\end{split}

yielding (
3.22
).
The proof is complete.

Lemma 3.8

\sup\left\{\|e^{-\varepsilon L_{R}(t)}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{\sigma}(D_{R}))};\,0\leq t\leq{\mathcal{T}},\,0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1\right\}<\infty,

Let

4
Evolution operator on the whole space

This section studies the initial value problem for the non-autonomous system

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u+{\mathcal{L}}(t)u+\nabla p=0,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0\quad\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(s,\infty)$},\\
&\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0,\qquad u(\cdot,s)=f,\end{split}

(4.1)

where
{\mathcal{L}}(t)
is the differential operator given by (
1.7
).
Because of
\mbox{\eqref{grund}}_{1}
we see that
\nabla p=0
within the class
\nabla p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Hence, under the compatibility condition
\mbox{div $f$}=0
, the solution formula to (
4.1
) is obtained from the heat semigroup

\big(e^{t\Delta}f\big)(x)=(4\pi t)^{-3/2}\left(e^{-|\cdot|^{2}/4t}*f\right)(x)

simply by transformation of variables as follows, where
*
stands for convolution in spatial variable.
It is expicitly described as

u(x,t)=\big(U(t,s)f\big)(x)=\Phi(t,s)\left(e^{(t-s)\Delta}f\right)\left(\Phi(t,s)^{\top}\Big(x+\int_{s}^{t}\Phi(t,\tau)\eta(\tau)\,d\tau\Big)\right),

(4.2)

where
\Phi(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
stands for the evolution matrix, being an orthogonal matrix,
to the differential equation

\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=-\omega(t)\times\varphi
.

The evolution operator
U(t,s)
was already studied by

[
5
,
21
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
36
,
37
]
, but we still need more to our end.
We begin with weighted estimates of

U(t,s)
for initial data being not necessarily solenoidal,
that provides a solution to (
4.1
) (with
\nabla p=0
), however, without
\mbox{div $u$}=0
.
Estimate (
4.3
) below with
\alpha=0
is the usual one which holds true for all
t>s\geq 0
, but
(
4.3
) with
\alpha>0
describes merely the smoothing rate near
t=s
.
For the Oseen semigroup (with constant
\eta
and
\omega=0
) in the whole space, the results
even with anisotropic weights reflecting the wake region have been
developed well by Tomoki Takahashi
[
53
]
;
in fact, he has deduced the large time behavior, too, with less decay rate than usual.
For the non-autonomous case as well, the rate of large time decay is observed in (
4.6
) below with
k=0
,
which holds true for all
t>s\geq 0
if
\sup_{t\geq 0}|\eta(t)|\leq m
and which
agrees with the one shown by
[
53
]
.
Compared with Theorem
2.1
for the exterior problem, we have (
4.3
) for every
\alpha\geq 0
,
q\in[1,\infty]

and

being an integer.

and

,
which is however replaced by (

) below to show (

)

Proposition 4.1

Suppose

,

be an integer.

1.

for all
(t,s)
with

and

whenever

(4.4)

where the weight function

2.

For each
{\mathcal{T}}\in(0,\infty)
and
m\in(0,\infty)

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{j+1}U(t,s)f\|_{r,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C(t-s)^{-j/2-(3/q-3/r)/2}\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}

(4.5)

for all
(t,s)
with

and
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\nabla f\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
whenever

4.4
) is satisfied.

Proof.

Both estimates (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) with

\alpha=0
follow from the corresponding estimates of the heat semigroup.
Let
\alpha>0
,
j\geq 0
being an integer and

.
Suppose

(as well as

and set

then it follows from

\rho(x)^{\alpha}\leq\max\{2^{\alpha-1},1\}\big(|x-y|^{\alpha}+\rho(y)^{\alpha}\big)

that

\quad\rho(x)^{\alpha}|\nabla^{j+k}u(x,t)|\leq C(I+II)

with

\begin{split}I:&=(t-s)^{-(3+j)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x-y|^{\alpha}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/8(t-s)}|\nabla^{k}g(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq C(t-s)^{-(3+j-\alpha)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/16(t-s)}|\nabla^{k}g(y)|\,dy,\end{split}

II:=(t-s)^{-(3+j)/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/8(t-s)}\rho(y)^{\alpha}|\nabla^{k}g(y)|\,dy,

\begin{split}g(y):&=f\left(\Phi(t,s)^{\top}\Big(y+\int_{s}^{t}\Phi(t,\tau)\eta(\tau)\,d\tau\Big)\right),\\
\partial_{l}g(y)&=\sum_{m=1}^{3}\Phi_{lm}(t,s)\big(\partial_{m}f\big)\left(\Phi(t,s)^{\top}\Big(y+\int_{s}^{t}\Phi(t,\tau)\eta(\tau)\,d\tau\Big)\right),\end{split}

for
l\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Hence, we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{j+k}u(t)\|_{r,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\\
&\leq C(t-s)^{-(j-\alpha)/2-(3/q-3/r)/2}\|\nabla^{k}g\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+C(t-s)^{-j/2-(3/q-3/r)/2}\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{k}g\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\end{split}

for
r\in[q,\infty]
.
Taking account of

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{k}g\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{k}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+Cm^{\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha}\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}

and
\|\nabla^{k}g\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}=\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}

we conclude

\begin{split}&\quad\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{j+k}U(t,s)f\|_{r,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\\
&\leq C(t-s)^{-j/2-(3/q-3/r)/2}\Big[\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{k}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(t-s)^{\alpha/2}\big\{1+m^{\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha/2}\big\}\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big]\end{split}

(4.6)

with some
C>0
independent of
m
, that leads us to (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) near

.
The proof is complete.
∎

The next lemma plays an important role in section

) below is the only reason why more weight

on the external force to deduce

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that

and

fulfill (

) for some

.
Let

.
For each

,

,
there are constants

and

such that

\|L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)\big(U(t,s)-e^{-(t-s)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}\big)f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C(t-s)^{\theta}\Big[(t-s)^{-1/2}\|\rho f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\|\rho^{2}f\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big]

(4.21)

\|L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)\big(U(t,s)-e^{-(t-s)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}\big)g\|_{q,B_{R}}\leq C^{\prime}(t-s)^{-1/2+\theta}\|\rho g\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}

(4.22)

for all
(t,s)
with
0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
,
f\in L^{q}_{2,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

g\in L^{q}_{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
whenever (
2.14

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{q}_{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then we have

for all

by Propsition

Since

\begin{split}&\quad U(t,s)f-e^{-(t-s)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}f\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\sigma}\left(e^{-(t-\sigma)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}U(\sigma,s)f\right)\,d\sigma\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}\Big[e^{-(t-\sigma)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)U(\sigma,s)f-e^{-(t-\sigma)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\sigma)U(\sigma,s)f\Big]\,d\sigma\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}e^{-(t-\sigma)L_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(t)}\big({\mathcal{L}}(t)-{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)\big)U(\sigma,s)f\,d\sigma\end{split}

in

\begin{split}&\quad\big({\mathcal{L}}(t)-{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)\big)w\\

(4.23)

)

5
Weighted estimate

In this section we develop the weighted estimates of the evolution operator
T(t,s)
on exterior domains
to show Theorem
2.1
.
To this end, we have to be back to the stage of construction of
a parametrix of the evolution operator
due to Hansel and Rhandi
[
29
]
, see also
[
37
, section 5]
.
It is done in the following way by use of evolution operators in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and in the bounded
domain
D_{7}
.
We fix three cut-off functions

\begin{split}&\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{5}),\qquad\phi=1\;\;\mbox{in $B_{4}$},\\
&\phi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{3}),\qquad\phi_{0}=1\;\;\mbox{in $B_{2}$},\\
&\phi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{7}),\qquad\phi_{1}=1\;\;\mbox{in $B_{6}$},\end{split}

and set

G=\{3<|x|<5\},\quad G_{0}=\{1<|x|<3\},\quad G_{1}=\{5<|x|<7\}.

By
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{B}_{G}
,
\mathbb{B}_{0}=\mathbb{B}_{G_{0}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{1}=\mathbb{B}_{G_{1}}

we denote the Bogovskii operator introduced in Lemma
3.1
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
Given
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
, we set

f_{0}=(1-\phi_{0})f+\mathbb{B}_{0}[f\cdot\nabla\phi_{0}],\qquad f_{1}=\phi_{1}f-\mathbb{B}_{1}[f\cdot\nabla\phi_{1}],

(5.1)

as in (
3.4
),
where
f_{0}
is understood as its extension to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by setting zero outside
D
.
If, in particular,
f\in L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)
with
\alpha\geq 0
, see (
2.18
), then
we have
f_{0}\in L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as well as
f_{1}\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D_{7})

subject to

\|\rho^{\alpha}f_{0}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\|\rho^{\alpha}f\|_{q},\qquad\|f_{1}\|_{q,D_{7}}\leq C\|f\|_{q}

(5.2)

by (
3.2
).
We also observe

\begin{split}&\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}+\|\nabla f_{1}\|_{q,D_{7}}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)},\\
&\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla f_{0}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C\big(\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla f\|_{q}+\|f\|_{q}\big),\end{split}

(5.3)

if, in addition,
\nabla f\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(D)
with
\alpha\geq 0
.

As a fine approximation of the evolution operator, it is natural to take

W(t,s)f=(1-\phi)U(t,s)f_{0}+\phi V(t,s)f_{1}+\mathbb{B}[(U(t,s)f_{0}-V(t,s)f_{1})\cdot\nabla\phi]

(5.4)

which fulfills
W(s,s)f=f
,
where
U(t,s)
is the evolution operator, see (
4.2
), for the whole space problem
and
V(t,s)
is the one for the interior problem over
D_{7}
, see Lemma
3.7
.
We fix
{\mathcal{T}},\,m\in(0,\infty)
and suppose (
2.14
).
Let
0\leq s<t\leq{\mathcal{T}}
.
Let also
r\in[q,\infty]
and
j\in\{0,1\}
, then
we see from (
3.2
), (
3.28
), (
3.33
)
(
4.3
), (
4.5
) with
(j,r)=(0,q)
, (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla^{j}W(t,s)f\|_{r}\leq C(t-s)^{-j/2-(3/q-3/r)/2}\|\rho^{\alpha}f\|_{q}

(5.5)

for
f\in L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)
and that

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla W(t,s)f\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{-1/2+\delta}\big(\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla f\|_{q}+\|f\|_{q}\big)

(5.6)

for
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
with
\nabla f\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(D)
, where
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
is arbitrary.

With the pressure
p_{1}(\cdot,t)
on
D_{7}
associated with
V(t,s)f_{1}
in such a way that

\int_{D_{7}}p_{1}(t)\,dx=0
, we consider the pair of

u:=W(t,s)f,\qquad p:=\phi p_{1}(t),

which should obey

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u+{\mathcal{L}}(t)u+\nabla p+K(t,s)f=0,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0\quad\mbox{in $D\times(s,\infty)$},\\
&u|_{\partial D}=0,\qquad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0,\qquad u(\cdot,s)=f,\end{split}

(5.7)

where

{\mathcal{L}}(t)
is the differential operator given by (
1.7
).
The equation is understood as

\partial_{t}W(t,s)f+L(t)W(t,s)f=-PK(t,s)f,\qquad t\in(s,\infty),

(5.8)

in
L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
as long as

f\in L^{q}_{1,\sigma}(D)
, yielding
f_{0}\in L^{q}_{1,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

which ensures the desired regularity of
U(t,s)f_{0}
by Proposition
4.2
.
But
this does not matter in the
present section
because the construction of the evolution operator is based
on the integral equation (
5.14
) below.
Here, the remainder term
K(t,s)f
is given by

\begin{split}&\quad K(t,s)f\\
=&-2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(Uf_{0}-Vf_{1})-\{\Delta\phi+(\eta+\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla\phi\}(Uf_{0}-Vf_{1})\\
&-(\nabla\phi)p_{1}-\mathbb{B}[(\partial_{t}Uf_{0}-\partial_{t}Vf_{1})\cdot\nabla\phi]+\Delta\mathbb{B}[(Uf_{0}-Vf_{1})\cdot\nabla\phi]\\
&+(\eta+\omega\times x)\cdot\nabla\mathbb{B}[(Uf_{0}-Vf_{1})\cdot\nabla\phi]-\omega\times\mathbb{B}[(Uf_{0}-Vf_{1})\cdot\nabla\phi],\end{split}

(5.9)

where we abbreviate
Uf_{0}=U(t,s)f_{0}
and
Vf_{1}=V(t,s)f_{1}
.
It follows from (
3.2
)–(
3.3
),
(
3.28
)–(
3.29
), (
3.31
) with
\delta=0
, (
3.32
),
(
4.3
) with
\alpha=0
, (
4.5
) with
\alpha=0
, (
4.8
),
(
4.11
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that

\|K(t,s)f\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{-(1+1/q)/2}\|f\|_{q}

(5.10)

and that

\begin{split}&\quad\|\nabla K(t,s)f\|_{q}\\
&\leq C\big(\|U(t,s)f_{0}\|_{W^{2,q}(G)}+\|\partial_{t}U(t,s)f_{0}\|_{q,G}+\|V(t,s)f_{1}\|_{W^{2,q}(D_{7})}+\|\partial_{t}V(t,s)f_{1}\|_{q,D_{7}}+\|\nabla p_{1}(t)\|_{q,D_{7}}\big)\\
&\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C(t-s)^{-1}\|f\|_{q},\\
C(t-s)^{-1+\delta}\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)},\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(5.11)

where
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
is arbitrary.
The former of (
5.11
) will be used for (
6.5
) in the next section,
whereas the latter soon plays a role via (
5.13
)
to show (
5.18
) below, leading to (
2.21
).
Note that, thanks to (
4.11
),
one does not need any weighted norm of
f
in the latter estimate of (
5.11
) unlike
[
37
, Lemma 5.3]
.

We now suppose
\alpha\in[0,3)
and
q\in(\frac{3}{3-\alpha},\infty)
.
Since the support of
K(t,s)f
is compact in
D
,
(
5.10
) and (
5.11
) respectively give

\|\rho^{\alpha}PK(t,s)f\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{-(1+1/q)/2}\|f\|_{q}

(5.12)

and

\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla PK(t,s)f\|_{q}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C(t-s)^{-1}\|f\|_{q},\\
C(t-s)^{-1+\delta}\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)},\end{array}\right.

(5.13)

with
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
on account of Lemma
3.9
as well as
(
2.19
).

In view of (
5.7
), the reasonable idea of Hansel and Rhandi
[
29
]
for construction
of the evolution operator
T(t,s)
is to solve the integral equation

T(t,s)f=W(t,s)f+\int_{s}^{t}T(t,\tau)PK(\tau,s)f\,d\tau

(5.14)

through the iteration scheme (
j=0,1,2,...
)

T_{j+1}(t,s)f=\int_{s}^{t}T_{j}(t,\tau)PK(\tau,s)f\,d\tau,\qquad T_{0}(t,s)f=W(t,s)f,

(5.15)

so that

T(t,s)f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}T_{j}(t,s)f

(5.16)

is convergent in
L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
absolutely and uniformly in
(t,s)
with
0\leq s\leq t\leq{\mathcal{T}}

for every
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
, which follows from (
5.5
) with
(\alpha,j,r)=(0,0,q)
and (
5.10
)
with the aid of Lemma
3.5
, see
[
29
]
.

We are in a position to show Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Let
\alpha\in[0,3)
,
q\in(\frac{3}{3-\alpha},\infty)
,
r\in[q,\infty]
and
j\in\{0,1\}
.
In view of (
5.5
), (
5.12
) and (
5.15
), we apply
Lemma
3.5

with

\begin{split}&E_{0}=\nabla^{j}W,\quad Q=PK,\quad X_{1}=L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D),\quad X_{2}=L^{r}_{\alpha}(D),\\
&\beta=\frac{j}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{q}\right)\end{split}

to conclude (
2.20
)
provided
1/q-1/r<(2-j)/3
,
however, this restriction on
r
(for
j=1
) can be removed by the semigroup property.

We next show (
2.21
).
We intend to prove merely the case
(j,r)=(1,q)
since the other cases are verified easily
(when
r>q
, the case
j=1
is reduced to the case
j=0
by the semigroup property).
Given
\alpha\in[0,3)
and
q\in(\frac{3}{3-\alpha},\infty)
,
let us introduce the space

Z_{q,\alpha}(D):=\{u\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D);\,\nabla u\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(D)\}

(5.17)

endowed with norm

\|u\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)}=\|u\|_{q}+\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla u\|_{q}

and show that

\|T(t,s)f\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)}\leq C(t-s)^{-1/2+\delta}\|f\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)}

(5.18)

for all

f\in Z_{q,\alpha}(D)
,
where
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
.
We notice that
Z_{q,1}(D)=Z_{q}(D)
, see (
2.12
), and that (
5.18
) with
\alpha=1
is found in

[
37
, proof of Lemma 5.4]
.
Once we have (
5.18
), we are led to the desired behavior; in fact,
since
C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(D)

is dense in
L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)
for
\alpha
and
q
specified in this theorem,
the approximation procedure together with (
2.20
) implies that

\lim_{t\to s}\,(t-s)^{1/2}\|\rho^{\alpha}\nabla T(t,s)f\|_{q}=0

for every
f\in L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)
.
By (
5.5
) with
(\alpha,j,r)=(0,0,q)
and (
5.6
) we know

\|W(t,s)f\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)}\leq C(t-s)^{-1/2+\delta}\|f\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)},

while it follows from (
5.12
) with
\alpha=0
and the latter of (
5.13
) that

\|PK(t,s)f\|_{Z_{q,\alpha}(D)}\leq C(t-s)^{-1+\delta}\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)},

if, in particular,
\delta
is chosen as
\delta<\min\{1/2q,\,(1-1/q)/2\}
.
With those estimates at hand, we apply Lemma
3.5
with

E_{0}=W,\quad Q=PK,\quad X_{1}=X_{2}=Z_{q,\alpha}(D),\quad\beta=\frac{1}{2}-\delta,\quad\gamma=1-\delta

to obtain (
5.18
).

Finally, we verify (
2.22
).
Since
C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(D)
is dense in
L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)
and since

T(t,s)
is bounded near
t=s
from
L^{q}_{\alpha,\sigma}(D)

to itself, see (
2.20
) with
(j,r)=(0,q)
,
it suffices to show (
2.22
) when
f\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(D)
.
By the item 3 of Proposition
2.1
,
for such data, we have the relation

T(t,s)f-f=-\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\sigma}T(t,\sigma)f\,d\sigma=-\int_{s}^{t}T(t,\sigma)L(\sigma)f\,d\sigma,

from which together with (
2.20
)
and (
2.19
) it follows that

\|\rho^{\alpha}\big(T(t,s)f-f\big)\|_{q}\leq C\int_{s}^{t}\|\rho^{\alpha}L(\sigma)f\|_{q}\,d\sigma\leq C\int_{s}^{t}\|\rho^{\alpha}{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)f\|_{q}\,d\sigma,

where
{\mathcal{L}}(t)
is the differential operator given by (
1.7
).
In this way, we obtain

\|\rho^{\alpha}\big(T(t,s)f-f\big)\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)\sup_{0\leq\sigma\leq{\mathcal{T}}}\|\rho^{\alpha}{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)f\|_{q}\to 0\qquad(t\to s)

for every
f\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(D)
.
The proof is complete.

\Box

6
Strong solution

This section improves the item 2 of Proposition
2.1

to show that the evolution operator
T(t,s)
gives us a strong solution as long as the initial velocity is taken from

L^{q}_{1,\sigma}(D)
,
q\in(\frac{3}{2},\infty)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.
We begin with the proof of the item 1.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
.
It suffices to show

\|\nabla^{2}T(t,s)f\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{-1}\|f\|_{q}

(6.1)

for all
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
, which combined with (
2.13
) with
j=0
leads to (
2.23
) by the semigroup property.
Given

f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
,
we set
u(t)=T(t,s)f
, which belongs to
W^{1,q}(D)
for
t>s
by (
2.13
).
By interpolation with (
2.13
) we observe

\|u(t)\|_{[L^{q}_{\sigma}(D),\,L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)\cap W^{1,q}(D)]_{2\delta}}\leq C(t-s)^{-\delta}\|f\|_{q}

provided that
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.
If, in particular,
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
, then the complex interpolation space in the left-hand side above
coincides with
D_{q}(A^{\delta})
, which does not involve any boundary condition (except the vanishing normal trace) at
\partial D
,
see
[
15
]
and
[
46
, Section 2.3]
as well as (
3.6
).
We thus obtain

\|u(t)\|_{D_{q}(A^{\delta})}\leq C(t-s)^{-\delta}\|f\|_{q}

(6.2)

for all
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)
as long as
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
.

We next assume that
f\in D_{q}(A^{\delta})
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, which is however restricted to
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
later.
It then follows from Lemma
3.2
that
the functions
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
defined by (
5.1
) satisfy

f_{0}\in H^{2\delta}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{q}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad f_{1}\in D_{q}(A_{7}^{\delta})

subject to (
3.7
) with
R=7
.
By (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) with
(\alpha,r)=(0,q)
we find

\|\nabla^{2}U(t,s)f_{0}\|_{q,\mathbb{R}^{3}}\leq C(t-s)^{-1+\delta}\|f_{0}\|_{H^{2\delta}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

(6.3)

for
\delta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})

since

H^{2\delta}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=[L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})]_{2\delta}
.
This together with (
3.31
) and (
3.7
) with
R=7

implies that
W(t,s)f
given by (
5.4
) enjoys

\|W(t,s)f\|_{W^{2,q}(D)}\leq C(t-s)^{-1+\delta}\|f\|_{D_{q}(A^{\delta})}

(6.4)

for
\delta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.

We now assume
\delta\in(0,1/2q)
, then we have

PK(t,s)f\in D_{q}(A^{\delta})=[L^{q}_{\sigma}(D),\,L^{q}_{\sigma}(D)\cap W^{1,q}(D)]_{2\delta}
, where
K(t,s)f
is the remainder term
given by (
5.9
).
Since the projection
P
is bounded on
W^{1,q}(D)
,
we deduce from (
5.10
)
and the former of (
5.11
) that

\|PK(t,s)f\|_{D_{q}(A^{\delta})}\leq\|PK(t,s)f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)}^{2\delta}\|PK(t,s)f\|_{q}^{1-2\delta}\leq C(t-s)^{-1+\zeta}\|f\|_{q}

(6.5)

where

\zeta:=\frac{1-2\delta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right).

(6.6)

Note that the restriction above on
\delta
is needed since

PK(t,s)f
does not fulfill the Dirichlet boundary condition.
In view of (
5.15
), (
6.4
) and (
6.5
),
applying Lemma

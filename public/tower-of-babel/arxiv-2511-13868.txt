Introduction

Composition operators allow to face up fundamental questions about certain operators with elegant classical results from functional analysis, complex variable, and harmonic analysis. In dynamic systems, composition operators are usually named as Koopman operators, in honor of the Franco-American mathematician Bernard O. Koopman. Koopman introduced these operators to join classical Hamiltonian mechanics with the theory of Hilbert spaces and their linear transformations (
[
11
]
). Within this framework, the canonical resolution of the identity (usually called the “spectrum of the dynamical system”) is introduced and described in terms of a one-parameter group of unitary (and composition) operators in Hilbert space.

Take
K
a compact and
X
a locally compact spaces. A mapping
\phi:[0,\infty)\,\,\times K\to K
is said a semiflow if for each
t\geq 0
the mapping
\phi_{t}
defined by
\phi_{t}(x):=\phi(t,x)
is continuous,
\phi_{0}(x)=x
and
\phi_{s}\circ\phi_{t}=\phi_{s+t}
for
s,t\geq 0
.

In this topic, there is an abundance of literature available. The classic monographic
[
17
]
develops a deep treatment of positive semigroups in lattice spaces, and it characterizes continuous flows through semigroups of composition operators and their infinitesimal generators. A precise study of Koopman operators in
L^{p}({\bf X})
, with applications to ergodicity in measure-preserving systems, can be found in
[
6
]
. In a finite measure space, reference
[
5
]
characterizes Koopman semigroups on
L^{p}({\bf X})
for measure preserving semiflows. Furthermore, it shows that measure-preserving flows on standard probability spaces and continuous flows on compact Borel probability spaces are equivalent. Recently three specific semiflows
\phi_{t},\psi_{t},\varphi_{t}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
on the real half-line given by

\phi_{t}(r):=e^{-t}r+1-e^{-t},\ \psi_{t}(r):=\frac{e^{t}r}{1+r(e^{t}-1)},\ \varphi_{t}(r):=\frac{(1+e^{t})r-1+e^{t}}{(-1+e^{t})r+1+e^{t}},

for
t,r>0
are considered in
[
16
]
to define and study weight Koopman semigroups on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and some additional Sobolev-Lebesgue spaces.

On the other hand, the theory of continuous one-parameter semigroups of analytic self-maps on the unit disk has received significant attention in last decades. Semigroups of holomorphic self-maps on the unit disk (or on the complex half-plane or other domains) are closely connected with the theory of composition operators due to each one-parameter semigroup of holomorphic self-maps induces a semigroup of composition operators on certain holomorphic function spaces. This connection transfers functional analytic properties, such as compactness, periodicity or spectral properties, to corresponding dynamical questions about semigroups. The excellent monograph
[
3
]
gives a thorough and comprehensive overview of the current state of the art in this interesting and complicated field. Several well-known examples of semigroups of holomorphic semiflows are discussed in the excellent survey
[
20
]
.

Corresponding results on discrete Lebesgue space
\ell^{p}
are unknown. Moreover, a rigorous definition of semiflows and Koopman semigroups on
\ell^{p}
seem impossible to achieve. However we present in this paper (sections 3.2 and 4) two concrete examples,
(\mathfrak{T}_{p}(t))_{t>0}
and
(\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta,p}(t))_{t>0},
of
C_{0}
-semigroups on
\ell^{p}
which are strongly linked to Koopman semigroups
T_{p}(t)f(r):=e^{-{t\over p}}f(e^{-t}r)
and

S_{p}(t)f(r):=e^{-t\over p}f(e^{-t}r+1-e^{-t})
for
t,r>0
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. It look likes natural consider these
C_{0}
-semigroups as canonical examples of “Koopman semigroups” on
\ell^{p}
.

To establish this connection from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
to
\ell^{p}
, we consider the Poisson transformation
{\mathcal{P}}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\ell^{p}
defined by

{\mathcal{P}}(f)(n):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-t}{t^{n}\over n!}dt,\qquad n\geq 0,\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),

and its adjoint operator
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}:\ell^{p}\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
given by

{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(a)(t):=e^{-t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n){t^{n}\over n!},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\quad a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}.

Among other fields, both operators have appeared in functional analysis, approximation theory and stochastic calculus. For example, the classical Post-Widder operator (also called Gamma operator in
[
14
]
and in later references),

P_{\lambda}(f,u):={(\lambda/u)^{\lambda}\over\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda t/u)}t^{\lambda-1}f(t)dt={1\over\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}{s^{\lambda-1}}f({u\over\lambda}s)ds,\qquad u,\lambda\in(0,\infty),

(
[
23
, p. 288]
) includes the Poisson transformation
\mathcal{P}
, i.e,
P_{n+1}(f,n+1)={\mathcal{P}}(f)(n)
for
n>0
. The Szász-Mirakyan operator

S_{x}(f,t):=e^{-tx}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f({n\over x}){(tx)^{n}\over n!},\qquad t\geq 0,x>0,

is a positive linear operator which uniformly approximates a certain class of continuous functions on the half line (
[
21
,
22
]
). Note that

S_{1}(f,t)=\mathcal{P}^{*}(\tilde{f})(t)
for
t\geq 0
where the sequence
\tilde{f}
is given by
\tilde{f}(n):=f(n)
for
n\geq 0
. In other context, the analysis of the dynamics of the semigroup solutions of certain partial differential equations are studied on Herzog type space of analytic functions defined by

X_{\rho}:=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}\,\,:\,\,f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}\rho^{n}\over n!}x^{n},\quad(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\right\},

for some

[
8

1.
Modified Beta function and Poisson-Charlier orthogonal polynomials

In this section we are interested in a modified beta function
\mathbb{B}_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
(which may expressed in terms of Kummer function) and in a particular case of Poisson-Charlier polynomials
(p_{n}))_{n\geq 0}

Definition 1.1
.

We consider the modified Beta Euler function
\mathbb{B}_{1}
defined by

\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{u-1}t^{v-1}e^{-t}dt,\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}.

Note that

\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v)=e^{-1}\mathbb{B}(u,v)\,_{1}F_{1}(u,u+v,1),\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+},

where
\,{}_{1}F_{1}(a,b;z)
is the Kummer function defined by

\,{}_{1}F_{1}(a,c;z):={\Gamma(c)\over\Gamma(a)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\Gamma(a+n)\over\Gamma(c+n)}{z^{n}\over n!}={\Gamma(c)\over\Gamma(a)\Gamma(c-a)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{c-a-1}t^{a-1}e^{zt}dt,

for
\Re c>\Re a>0
, see definition and properties in
[
15
, Chapter VI]
. Some basic facts of this function are presented in the next proposition.

Proposition 1.2
.

The function
\mathbb{B}_{1}
verifies the following properties.

(i)

|\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v)|\leq\mathbb{B}(\Re u,\Re v)
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

(ii)

e^{-1}\mathbb{B}(u,v)\leq\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v)\leq\mathbb{B}(u,v)
for
u,v>0
.

(iii)

\mathbb{B}_{1}(u+1,v+1)=v\mathbb{B}_{1}(u+1,v)-u\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v+1)
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

(iv)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v)=e^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{B}(u+n,v)\over n!}}
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

Proof.

The proof of item (i) and (ii) are straightforward. To show (iii), we integrate by parts to get

\mathbb{B}_{1}(u+1,v+1)=v\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{u}t^{v-1}e^{-t}dt-u\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{u-1}t^{v}e^{-t}dt=v\mathbb{B}_{1}(u+1,v)-u\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v+1)

for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
. Finally to check (iv), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-1}\mathbb{B}(u,v)\,_{1}F_{1}(u,u+v,1)=e^{-1}\mathbb{B}(u,v){\Gamma(u+v)\over\Gamma(u)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\Gamma(u+n)\over\Gamma(u+v+n)}{z^{n}\over n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{B}(u+n,v)\over n!},

for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.∎

Definition 1.4

For

The first values of this sequence are the following ones

\displaystyle p_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle p_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z+1,

\displaystyle p_{2}(z)

Theorem 1.5
.

The sequence of polynomials
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
verifies the following properties

(i)

The polynomials
p_{n}
are monic, of degree
n
and
p_{n}(0)=1
for
n\geq 0
.

(ii)

p_{n+1}(z)=(z+n+1)p_{n}(z)-np_{n-1}(z)
for
n\geq 1
.

(iii)

For
|w|<1
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(z){w^{n}\over n!}=e^{w}(1-w)^{-z}.

Let
\nabla
be the backward difference operator
\nabla(f)(z):=f(z)-f(z-1)
.

(iv)

\nabla(p_{n})=np_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
.

(v)

\nabla^{2}(p_{n})(z)-(z+n)\nabla(p_{n})(z)+np_{n}(z)=0
for
n\geq 0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

The proof of item (i) is straightforward. To show (ii) note that,

\displaystyle(z+n+1)p_{n}(z)-np_{n-1}(z)=(z+n+1){\Gamma(z+n)\over\Gamma(z)}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{n\choose j}(z+j+1){\Gamma(j+z)\over\Gamma(z)}

\displaystyle=(z+n+1){\Gamma(z+n)\over\Gamma(z)}+\sum_{l=1}^{n}{n\choose l-1}{\Gamma(l+z)\over\Gamma(z)}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{n\choose j}{\Gamma(j+z)\over\Gamma(z)}

\displaystyle={\Gamma(z+n+1)\over\Gamma(z)}+\sum_{l=1}^{n}{n+1\choose l}{\Gamma(l+z)\over\Gamma(z)}+1=p_{n+1}(z).

(iii) Take
|w|<1
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(z){w^{n}\over n!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\choose j}{\Gamma(j+z)\over\Gamma(z)}{w^{n}\over n!}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}{w^{n}\over(n-j)!}\right){\Gamma(j+z)\over\Gamma(z)j!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{w}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{z}(j)w^{j}=e^{w}(1-w)^{-z}.

(iv) Note that

\displaystyle\nabla(p_{n})(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\choose j}\left({\Gamma(j+z)\over\Gamma(z)}-{\Gamma(j+z-1)\over\Gamma(z-1)}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{n\choose j}j{\Gamma(j-1+z)\over\Gamma(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\sum_{j=1}^{n}{(n-1)!\over(j-1)!(n-j)!}{\Gamma(j-1+z)\over\Gamma(z)}=np_{n-1}(z),

for
n\geq 1
. The item (v) is straightforward consequence from (iv) and (ii) and we conclude the proof.
∎

2.
Poisson transformations on Lebesgue spaces

Given
1\leq p<\infty,
let
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
be the set of Lebesgue
p
-integrable functions, that is,
f
is a measurable function and

||f||_{p}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

We write by
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\|\qquad\|_{\infty})
the measurable Lebesgue function which are almost bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\esssup\{|f(t)|\,;|t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}.

Remind that the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is a module for the algebra
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
f\ast g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
where

(f\ast g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(t-s)g(s)ds,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),

for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

In this section we consider the following transform called Poisson transformation in some text, see for example
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Take
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We introduce the operator
{\mathcal{P}}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\ell^{p}
defined by

(2.1)

{\mathcal{P}}(f)(n):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-t}{t^{n}\over n!}dt,\qquad n\geq 0,\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

Duality, we consider the operator
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}:\ell^{p}\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
given by

(2.2)

{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(a)(t):=e^{-t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n){t^{n}\over n!},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\quad a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}.

Examples.
We write by
e_{\lambda}(t):=e^{-\lambda t}
for
\Re\lambda>0
. Then

(2.3)

{\mathcal{P}}(e_{\lambda})(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\lambda+1)t}{t^{n}\over n!}dt={1\over(1+\lambda)^{n}},\qquad n\geq 0.

Conversely, take
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|<1
and
a_{\lambda}(n):={\lambda^{n}}
for
n\geq 0
. Then

{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(a_{\lambda})(t):=e^{-t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(\lambda t)^{n}\over n!}=e^{-(1-\lambda)t},\qquad t\geq 0.

In fact, operators
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
may be defined in larger domains.

(i)

Take also
\displaystyle{j_{\alpha}(t):={t^{\alpha-1}\over\Gamma(\alpha)}}
for
\alpha,t>0
. Then

\mathcal{P}(j_{\alpha})(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{t^{\alpha-1}\over\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-t}{t^{n}\over n!}dt={\Gamma(n+\alpha)\over\Gamma(\alpha)n!}=k^{\alpha}(n),\qquad n\geq 0.

(ii)

Conversely

\mathcal{P}^{*}(k^{\alpha})(t)={e^{-t}\over\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\Gamma(n+\alpha)\over(n!)^{2}}{t^{n}}=e^{-t}E^{\alpha}_{1,1}(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\displaystyle{E^{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\Gamma(n+\gamma)\over\Gamma(\gamma)}{z^{n}\over n!\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)}}
is the entire function, called Prabhakar function, and introduced in
[
18
]
. Note that, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{P}^{*}(k^{\alpha})
is a polynomial of degree
\alpha-1
.

(iii)

Fix
a\in\mathbb{C}
, we consider the sequence
P_{a}=(p_{n}(a))_{n\geq 0}
where the sequence
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is given in Definition
1.4
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{*}(P_{a})(t)=(1-t)^{-a}
for
t<1
, see Theorem
1.5
(iii).

In the next theorem we present more algebraic properties of operators
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Take
1\leq p\leq\infty
and operators
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
defined by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Then

(i)

The map
\mathcal{P}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\ell^{p}
is a linear bounded operator and
\|\mathcal{P}\|=1
.

(ii)

\mathcal{P}(f\ast g)=\mathcal{P}(f)\ast\mathcal{P}(g)
for
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

(iii)

For
1<p\leq\infty
, the map
\mathcal{P}^{*}:\ell^{p}\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is the adjoint operator of
\mathcal{P}
and
\|\mathcal{P}^{*}\|=1
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(iv)

\mathcal{P}^{*}(\delta_{1}\ast(a\ast b))=\mathcal{P}^{*}(a)\ast\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(b)
for
a\in\ell^{p}
and
b\in\ell^{1}
.

(v)

Given
a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}
, then

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(a))(m)={1\over 2^{m+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{k^{m+1}(n)\over 2^{n}}a(n),\qquad m\geq 0,

where
(k^{m+1}(n))_{n\geq 0}
are Cesàro numbers of order
m+1
.

(vi)

Given
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, then

\mathcal{P}^{*}(\mathcal{P}(f))(t)=e^{-t}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)e^{-s}I_{0}(2\sqrt{ts})ds,\qquad t>0,

where the Bessel function
I_{0}
is given by
\displaystyle{I_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(z/2)^{2n}\over(n!)^{2}}}
, see for example
[
15
, p. 66]
.

Proof.

(i) First consider
p=1
and
p=\infty
. Then it is straightforward to check that
\|\mathcal{P}\|\leq 1
and the map
\mathcal{P}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\ell^{p}
is a linear and bounded operator for
p\in\{1,\infty\}
. By the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem, we conclude that
\|\mathcal{P}\|\leq 1
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Note take
\lambda>0
and consider functions
e_{\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}
. By (
2.3
), we obtain that

{\|\mathcal{P}(e_{\lambda})\|_{p}^{p}\over\|e_{\lambda}\|_{p}^{p}}={(1+\lambda)^{p}\lambda p\over(1+\lambda)^{p}-1}\to 1,\qquad\lambda\to 0,

and we conclude that
\|\mathcal{P}\|=1
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(ii) Take
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
then
f\ast g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty.
For
n\geq 0
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(f\ast g)(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}f(t-s)g(s)ds{t^{n}\over n!}e^{-t}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)\int_{s}^{\infty}f(t-s){t^{n}\over n!}e^{-t}dtds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)e^{-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u){(u+s)^{n}\over n!}e^{-u}duds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)e^{-s}{s^{j}\over j!}ds\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u)e^{-u}{u^{n-j}\over(n-j)!}du=(\mathcal{P}(g)\ast\mathcal{P}(f))(n).

(iii) Take
1\leq p<\infty
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
b\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}
with
{1\over p}+{1\over p^{\prime}}=1
. Then

\langle\mathcal{P}(f),b\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}(f)(n)b(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}b(n)dt=\langle f,\mathcal{P}^{*}(b)\rangle.

We conclude that the operator
\mathcal{P}^{*}:\ell^{p}\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is a bounded operator and verifies that
\|\mathcal{P}^{*}\|=1
for
1<p\leq\infty
. The case
p=1
is shown directly.

(iv) Take
a\in\ell^{p}
and
b\in\ell^{1}
. Then

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}^{*}(a)\ast\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(b))(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}(a)(t-s)\mathcal{P}^{*}(b)(s)ds=\sum_{n,j=0}^{\infty}{a(n)\over n!}{b(j)\over j!}e^{-t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{n}s^{j}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-t}\sum_{n,j=0}^{\infty}{a(n)}{b(j)}{t^{n+j+1}\over(n+j+1)!}=e^{-t}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{t^{l+1}\over(l+1)!}\sum_{n=0}^{l}{a(n)}{b(l-n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-t}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{t^{l+1}\over(l+1)!}(a\ast b)(l)=\mathcal{P}^{*}(\delta_{1}\ast(a\ast b))(t)

for
t\geq 0
.

(v) Take
a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(a))(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{t^{m}\over m!}e^{-2t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}a(n)dt={1\over m!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a(n)\over n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}{t^{m+n}}e^{-2t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over m!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a(n)\over n!}{(m+n)!\over 2^{n+m+1}}={1\over 2^{m+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{k^{m+1}(n)\over 2^{n}}a(n),

for
m\geq 0
.

(vi) Take
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{*}(\mathcal{P}(f))(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)e^{-s}{s^{n}\over n!}ds=e^{-t}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)e^{-s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{(st)^{n}\over(n!)^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-t}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)e^{-s}I_{0}(2\sqrt{ts})ds,\qquad t>0,

and we conclude the proof.
∎

In the next proposition, we give the connection between Poisson transformations
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
and the Laplace and Zeta transform
\mathcal{L}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}
presented in the Introduction.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
operators given in Definition
2.1
.

(i)

Take
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{P}(f))(z)=\mathcal{L}f(1-z),\qquad|z|<1.

In particular the operator
\mathcal{P}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\to\ell^{p}
in injective for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(ii)

Take
a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}
. Then

{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{P}^{*}(a))(z)={1\over 1+z}\mathcal{Z}(a)\left({1\over 1+z}\right),\qquad\Re z>0.

In particular the operator
\mathcal{P}^{*}:\ell^{p}\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
in injective for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(iii)

Both operators
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
are of dense range.

Proof.

(i) Take
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Then we get that

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{P}(f))(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t){t^{n}\over n!}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-(1-z)t}dt={\mathcal{L}}(f)(1-z),

for
|z|<1
. In the case that
\mathcal{P}(f)=0
, we conclude that
f=0
by the injectivity of the Laplace transform.

(ii) Take
a=(a(n))_{n\geq 0}\in\ell^{p}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{P}^{*}(a))(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(z+1)t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}a(n)dt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a(n)\over n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}e^{-(z+1)t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a(n)\over(1+z)^{n+1}}={1\over 1+z}\mathcal{Z}(a)\left({1\over 1+z}\right),

for
\Re z>0
. In the case that
\mathcal{P}^{*}(a)=0
, we conclude that
a=0
by the injectivity of the Zeta transform.

The item (iii) is a straight consequence of the injectivity of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Note that both operators
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
are not invertible for

. Moreover the range of
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
is contained in the set of analytic functions. Now, suppose that
{\mathcal{P}}
was invertible, there will exist

such that

. By Proposition
2.3

and this follows a contradiction with

.

-semigroups on

3.1.
Convolution semigroups on
\ell^{p}

The forward difference operator
\Delta(a)(n):=((\delta_{-1}-\delta_{0})\ast a)(n)=a(n+1)-a(n)
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}
, and
\|\Delta\|=2
. In fact, the symbol
(\delta_{-1}-\delta_{0})
generates a entire semigroup of elements on

and then also defined a entire

-semigroup of operators on

(e^{z\Delta})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{p}),

(e^{z\Delta}a)(n)=e^{-z}\sum_{j\geq 0}^{\infty}a(j+n){z^{j}\over j!},\qquad a\in\ell^{p},

for

. Its infinitesimal generator is
\Delta
and
\|e^{t\Delta}\|=1
for
t\geq 0
, see similar results on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
in

(e^{t\Delta}a)(n)=\mathcal{P}^{*}(\delta_{-n}\ast a)(t),\qquad t>0,\qquad a\in\ell^{p}.

The classical backward difference operator
\nabla(a)(n):=((\delta_{1}-\delta_{0})\ast a)(n)=a(n-1)-a(n)
for

and
\nabla(a)(0):=-a(0)
is also a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}
, and
\|\nabla\|=2
. In this case the element
(\delta_{1}-\delta_{0})
generates an entire semigroup on
\ell^{p}
where

e^{z(\delta_{1}-\delta_{0})}(n)=e^{-z}{z^{n}\over n!},\qquad n\geq 0,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

The operator
\nabla
is the infinitesimal generator of the entire

-semigroup on
\ell^{p},

(e^{z\nabla})_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{p}),

(e^{z\nabla}a)(n)=(e^{z(\delta_{1}-\delta_{0})}\ast a)(n)=e^{-z}\sum_{j\geq 0}^{n}a(j){z^{n-j}\over(n-j)!},\qquad a\in\ell^{p},

for

and

for

.

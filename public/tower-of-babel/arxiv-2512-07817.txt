Integral constraints on the linear instability of stratified flow with planar shear at an arbitrary angle to the vertical
Miguel A. C. Teixeira \aff 1, Mohamed Foudad \aff 2, Paul. D. Williams \aff 2

Abstract
Integral constraints on the linear instability of stratified parallel flow with planar shear at an arbitrary angle to the vertical are derived using the analytical approach of Miles and Howard, for perturbations with 2D spatial structure, which are thought to be the most unstable. The general stability formulation reproduces the Miles-Howard stability criterion for vertical shear, but yields no stability condition for non-vertical shear, confirming expectations from earlier studies. This study also extends Howard’s semicircle theorem to non-vertical planar shear, and derives a new expression for the upper bound of the instability growth rate (extending that obtained by Howard), which is consistent with published numerical results.

1
Introduction

The stability of stratified shear flows is a fundamental problem of wide-ranging relevance in fluid mechanics, having implications for the transition of a flow to turbulence, the dynamics of internal gravity waves, and various applications, such as the generation of clear-air turbulence in the atmosphere and mixing in the oceans.

The most basic aspects of the linear stability of parallel shear flow go back to
13
, who showed that in inviscid non-stratified conditions the flow is unstable to exponentially-growing normal modes only if an inflection point exists in the mean velocity profile.
12
, using a similar approach, showed that for inviscid stratified flow with planar vertical shear, stability is ensured if the Richardson number (
Ri
) exceeds 1/4. The derivation of this result was considerably simplified by
8
, who also derived an upper bound for the growth rate of the instability, and bounds on the magnitude of the complex phase speed of unstable flow perturbations in terms of the range of mean flow velocities, in what is usually known as the semi-circle theorem. While the condition
Ri>1/4
is sufficient for linear flow stability, and therefore
Ri<1/4
is a necessary condition for instability, there is no known sufficient condition for instability in general.
3
and
6
, followed by several other authors, confirmed the theoretical predictions of
12
and
8
through analytical, numerical and experimental studies of stratified shear flow focusing on the instability conditions for specific velocity profiles.

The problem of stratified flows with horizontal or otherwise non-vertical shear has received comparatively less attention, and only more recently. An early treatment of non-planar shear with both horizontal and vertical components was made by
1
, who derived a generalized version of the semi-circle theorem and studied the linear stability of such flows for specific velocity profiles. However, no stability criterion has been derived for horizontally sheared flows, which are generally believed to be always linearly unstable.
4
addressed this question by numerically solving the eigenvalue–eigenfunction problem for inviscid stratified flow with a specific horizontal shear profile. Their findings corroborated the suspicion that there is no critical
Ri
value above which the flow is stable. Using similar methods,
2
analysed the stability of a stratified flow with planar shear at an arbitrary angle to the vertical. They confirmed that these flows may be unconditionally linearly unstable, except for vertical shear, and that the growth rate of the instability depends on the shear angle and on the stratification.
5
and
11
investigated the stability of viscous stratified flows with horizontal shear, both numerically and experimentally. They found that for finite Reynolds number (
Re
) the flow may become stable, with a critical
Ri
depending on
Re
below which instability occurs.

The use of Direct Numerical Simulations (DNS) to study the growth of small flow perturbations in linear instability is limited by numerical errors resulting from finite resolution, which constrain the range of achievable
Re
. Alternatively,
7
and
10
used DNS to study the related problem of turbulent kinetic energy (TKE) growth in fully turbulent flows. They found that the TKE growth rate and the threshold
Ri
for instability (understood as TKE increase) are higher for horizontal shear than for vertical shear in their intrinsically viscous simulations. This is consistent with the behaviour found in the linear instability analyses of
5
and
11
.

To our knowledge, the integral constraints on the linear stability of inviscid stratified flow with planar shear at an angle to the vertical (the problem studied numerically by
2
) have not been analysed theoretically. The present paper aims to fill that gap, following the approach of
12
and
8
. This allows an elucidation of how the stability condition for vertical shear is related to its absence for non-vertical shear. We also derive a new expression for the upper bound of the instability growth rate, extending a corresponding result by
8
, and generalize the semi-circle theorem to non-vertical planar shear.

2
Extension of integral relations

2.1
Basic equations

We start from the inviscid, adiabatic, linearised equations of motion under the Boussinesq approximation. The adopted coordinate system is inclined at an angle
\theta
to the vertical, and the mean flow (in hydrostatic balance) is assumed to be aligned in the
x
direction, with mean shear only in the
z
direction. This allows the planar shear to range from purely vertical to purely horizontal, depending on
\theta
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+U\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+w\frac{dU}{dz}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+U\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}+b\sin\theta,

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}+U\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}+b\cos\theta,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}+U\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}+N^{2}w\cos\theta+N^{2}v\sin\theta=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}=0.

(5)

Here,
U(z)
is the mean flow velocity,
(u,v,w)
is the three-dimensional (3D) velocity perturbation,
p
and
b
are the pressure and buoyancy perturbations, and
N^{2}
and
\rho_{0}
are the mean static stability and a constant reference density, respectively.
\theta
is the angle between the shear and the stratification (which acts in the vertical).
\theta=0
corresponds to the usual case of aligned shear and stratification (both in the vertical) and
\theta=\pi/2
corresponds to horizontal shear. The equation set (
1
)-(
5
) is equivalent to equations (2.3a)-(2.3e) of
2
, but with the
y
axis having a component pointing upwards instead of downwards when
\theta\neq 0
.

Assuming normal-mode solutions
\phi=\hat{\phi}\exp[i(kx+ly-\omega t)]
for all perturbation variables, where
(k,l)
is the wavenumber vector in the
(x,y)
plane and
\omega
is the angular frequency, (
1
)-(
5
) can be combined, reducing to a single equation for the amplitude of the velocity perturbation parallel to the shear direction,
\hat{w}
. For two-dimensional (2D) perturbations (
l=0
), that equation becomes

\hat{w}^{\prime\prime}+\left\{k^{2}\left[\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-1\right]-\frac{U^{\prime\prime}k}{Uk-\omega}\right\}\hat{w}=0,

(6)

where primes denote differentiation with respect to
z
. This is an extension of the Taylor–Goldstein equation for planar shear at an angle to the vertical, which reduces to the standard version when
\theta=0
. With
l=0
, the terms
\partial p/\partial y
in (
2
) and
\partial v/\partial y
in (
5
) vanish, and (
6
) is simplified very substantially compared to the form it would take for
l\neq 0
. This is not equivalent to assuming
v=0
: the velocity perturbations remain 3D, but with a 2D spatial structure. Despite the fact that Squire’s theorem
(
14
)
is not applicable to this case, this assumption is justified by the perceived fact that the perturbations with the largest growth rate for this type of flow have
l=0

(
4
;
2
)
. Because
z
is the direction of inhomogeneity for an arbitrary mean velocity profile
U(z)
and arbitrary angle
\theta
of the mean shear with the vertical, these assumptions also require
N^{2}
to be constant, for consistency.

2.2
Stability condition

Following
8
, introducing the change of variable
\hat{w}=(Uk-\omega)^{1/2}G
, (
6
) becomes

\left[(Uk-\omega)G^{\prime}\right]^{\prime}+\left\{k^{2}(Uk-\omega)\left[\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-1\right]-\frac{1}{4}\frac{(U^{\prime}k)^{2}}{Uk-\omega}-\frac{U^{\prime\prime}k}{2}\right\}G=0.

(7)

This equation can then be multiplied by the complex conjugate of
G
(
G^{*}
) and integrated between two points in
z
, say
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
at which
G(z)=0
. Since
\hat{w}
is the amplitude of the perturbation velocity component along
z
, this may be viewed as some form of rigid-lid boundary condition, but as
8
originally pointed out, these rigid lids may be moved to as far away as required. This integration yields

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left((Uk-\omega)|G^{\prime}|^{2}+\left\{k^{2}(Uk-\omega)\left[1-\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{1}{4}\frac{(U^{\prime}k)^{2}}{Uk-\omega}+\frac{1}{2}U^{\prime\prime}k\right\}|G|^{2}\right)dz=0,

(8)

where the term involving
|G^{\prime}|^{2}
results from integration by parts and uses the boundary conditions
G(z_{1})=G(z_{2})=0
, and from the definitions
|G|^{2}=G^{*}G
and
|G^{\prime}|^{2}=G^{\prime*}G^{\prime}
. It is convenient to rewrite (
8
) as

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega)\left(|G^{\prime}|^{2}+k^{2}|G|^{2}\right)dz+\frac{1}{2}k\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U^{\prime\prime}|G|^{2}dz

\displaystyle+k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega)\left[\frac{1}{4}\frac{U^{\prime 2}}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}}-\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right]|G|^{2}dz=0.

(9)

It should be noted that the angular frequency
\omega
defining the temporal dependence of the flow perturbations is in general complex:
\omega=\omega_{r}+i\omega_{i}
, with real part
\omega_{r}
and imaginary part
\omega_{i}
. The phase speed of the perturbations is
c_{r}=\omega_{r}/k
and their growth rate is
\omega_{i}
.
Then, the imaginary part of (
9
) yields

\displaystyle\omega_{i}\left(\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left(|G^{\prime}|^{2}+k^{2}|G|^{2}\right)dz\right.

\displaystyle\left.+k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left\{\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{U^{\prime 2}}{|Uk-\omega|^{2}}\right\}|G|^{2}dz\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(10)

where
|Uk-\omega|^{2}=(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}
.
If the expression in the outer curved brackets of (
10
) is positive, then
\omega_{i}=0
, i.e., no linear instability occurs. Since the first integral is always positive, the second integral must also be positive, which holds if its integrand is positive everywhere. The integrand in the second integral is positive when

\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}>\frac{1}{4}\frac{U^{\prime 2}}{(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}},

(11)

for any
(Uk-\omega_{r})
, since this is the only term that depends on
z
, via
U(z)
. The condition expressed by (
11
) is satisfied if

4\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{U^{\prime 2}_{max}}>{\rm Max}\left\{\frac{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}\right\},

(12)

where
U^{\prime 2}_{max}={\rm Max}(U^{\prime 2})
. Defining
x=(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}
, the extrema of the expression between curly brackets in (
12
) can be calculated by taking the derivative of

\frac{(x-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}x}{(x+\omega_{i}^{2})(x+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)}

(13)

with respect to
x
, and noting also that, at the lower bound for
x
,
x=0
, (
13
) takes the value
(\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)/\omega_{i}^{2}=1+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta/\omega_{i}^{2}\geq 1
, and at the upper bound,
x=+\infty
, (
13
) takes the value
1
.
There is only one point between
x=0
and
x=+\infty
where the
x
derivative of (
13
) is zero. At this point, which is a minimum, that expression takes the form

\frac{2\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\sqrt{(\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)(4\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)}}{2\omega_{i}^{2}+\frac{5}{4}N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\sqrt{(\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)(4\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)}},

(14)

which is never higher than
1
. This means that

{\rm Max}\left\{\frac{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}\right\}=1+\frac{N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\omega_{i}^{2}}.

(15)

Therefore, (
12
) can be expressed as

4\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{U^{\prime 2}_{max}}>1+\frac{N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\omega_{i}^{2}}.

(16)

Reversing the reasoning described above, if (
16
) holds, then (
11
) is also satisfied, so the sum of the terms inside the outer round brackets in (
10
) is positive, and the instability growth rate
\omega_{i}
must vanish.
Now, when
\omega_{i}=0
and
\theta\neq 0
(i.e., for non-vertical mean shear), the right-hand side of (
16
) becomes infinite, making this condition impossible to satisfy. Therefore, it is not possible to prove that there is a stability condition in this situation, i.e., the flow may always be unstable for
\theta\neq 0
. For vertical shear, however (
\theta=0
), the second term on the right-hand side of (
16
) drops, and the usual sufficient stability condition is obtained, namely:
Ri_{min}=N^{2}/U^{\prime 2}_{max}>1/4
.

2.3
Upper bound for the instability growth rate

Returning to (
10
), if
\omega_{i}\neq 0
, then the sum of the terms inside the outer brackets must vanish, which implies

\displaystyle k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}|G|^{2}dz=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\frac{k^{2}U^{\prime 2}}{|Uk-\omega|^{2}}\left\{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{U^{\prime 2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\frac{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}\right\}|G|^{2}dz-\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}|G^{\prime}|^{2}dz.

(17)

Equation (
15
) is equivalent to

{\rm Min}\left\{\frac{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}\right\}=\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}}{\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta},

(18)

so (
17
) implies

k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}|G|^{2}dz\leq\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\frac{k^{2}U^{\prime 2}}{|Uk-\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{U^{\prime 2}}\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}}{\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)|G|^{2}dz,

(19)

because the integral of
|G|^{2}
is always positive. If we further note that
|Uk-\omega|^{2}\geq\omega_{i}^{2}
, then

\omega_{i}^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}|G|^{2}dz\leq\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{U^{\prime 2}}\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}}{\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)|G|^{2}dz,

(20)

which in turn implies, after division by the integral of
|G|^{2}
,

\omega_{i}^{2}\leq{\rm Max}\left(\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}-\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\right).

(21)

Since
N^{2}
is assumed to be constant, this implies

\omega_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}-\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\omega_{i}^{2}+N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}.

(22)

This inequality can be written as a second-order algebraic inequation for
\omega_{i}^{2}
, as follows:

\omega_{i}^{4}+\left(N^{2}-\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}\right)\omega_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\leq 0.

(23)

The solution to (
23
) can be shown to be

\omega_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}-N^{2}+\left[\left(N^{2}-\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}\right)^{2}+U^{\prime 2}_{max}N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{1/2}\right\}.

(24)

This upper bound on the growth rate of the instability extends the formulation of
8
to flows with planar shear at an angle to the vertical. It reduces to Howard’s result when
\theta=0
, and becomes independent of
N^{2}
when
\theta=\pi/2
.
We now define a general
d Richardson number
Ri^{*}=N^{2}/U^{\prime 2}
, allowing the mean shear to be inclined at any angle to the vertical, and
Ri^{*}_{min}=N^{2}/U^{\prime 2}_{max}
. Then (
24
) can be expressed as

\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}}{U^{\prime 2}_{max}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{1}{4}-Ri^{*}_{min}+\left[\left(Ri^{*}_{min}-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}+Ri^{*}_{min}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{1/2}\right\}.

(25)

Equation (
25
) provides an upper bound for the squared growth rate of the instability,
\omega_{i}^{2}/U^{\prime 2}_{max}
. This becomes zero when
\theta=0
and
Ri^{*}_{min}\geq 1/4
, and reduces to
1/4
when
Ri^{*}_{min}=0
or
\theta=\pi/2
.

2.4
Extension of Howard’s semicircle theorem

Following
12
, introducing the change of variable
\hat{w}=(Uk-\omega)F
, (
6
) becomes

\left[(Uk-\omega)^{2}F^{\prime}\right]^{\prime}+k^{2}(Uk-\omega)^{2}\left[\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-1\right]F=0.

(26)

Multiplying this equation by
F^{*}
and integrating it between
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
results in

\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega)^{2}|F^{\prime}|^{2}dz-\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}k^{2}(Uk-\omega)^{2}\left[\frac{N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}-1\right]|F|^{2}dz=0,

(27)

with
F^{*}F=|F|^{2}
and
F^{\prime*}F^{\prime}=|F^{\prime}|^{2}
, and using integration by parts, together with the boundary conditions
F(z_{1})=F(z_{2})=0
. Equation (
27
) can be rewritten as

\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega)^{2}Rdz-\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\frac{N^{2}k^{2}\cos^{2}\theta(Uk-\omega)^{2}}{(Uk-\omega)^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}|F|^{2}dz=0,

(28)

where
R=|F^{\prime}|^{2}+k^{2}|F|^{2}
. The real and imaginary parts of (
28
) are

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}\right]Rdz-\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left\{P-Q\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\right\}dz=0,

(29)

\displaystyle-2\omega_{i}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega_{r})Rdz-2\omega_{i}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}Q(Uk-\omega_{r})dz=0,

(30)

where

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}k^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\right]^{2}}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}|F|^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{4}k^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta}{\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}-N^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{2}+4\omega_{i}^{2}(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}}|F|^{2}.

(32)

Equation (
30
) can be rewritten as

\omega_{i}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(Uk-\omega_{r})(R+Q)dz=0.

(33)

Since
R\geq 0
and
Q\geq 0
, in general if
\omega_{i}\neq 0
,
(Uk-\omega_{r})
must change sign between
z=z_{1}
and
z=z_{2}
, so that the integral in (
33
) is zero.
Equations (
29
)-(
30
) can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(U^{2}k^{2}+\omega_{r}^{2}-2Uk\omega_{r}-\omega_{i}^{2})Rdz=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left\{P-Q\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}\right]\right\}dz,

(34)

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(2Uk\omega_{r}-2\omega_{r}^{2})Rdz=-2\omega_{r}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}Q(Uk-\omega_{r})dz,

(35)

where (
30
) has been divided by
\omega_{i}
and multiplied by
\omega_{r}
. Adding (
34
) and (

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(U^{2}k^{2}-\omega_{r}^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2})Rdz=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left\{P-Q\left[(Uk-\omega_{r})^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}+2\omega_{r}(Uk-\omega_{r})\right]\right\}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\left[P-Q\left(U^{2}k^{2}-\omega_{r}^{2}-\omega_{i}^{2}\right)\right]dz,

(36)

which is equivalent to

\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U^{2}k^{2}(R+Q)dz=(\omega_{r}^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2})\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(R+Q)dz+\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}Pdz.

(37)

Now, since
(R+Q)\geq 0
, if
U_{min}\leq U(z)\leq U_{max}
for
z_{1}\leq z\leq z_{2}
, then

\displaystyle 0\geq\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}k^{2}(U-U_{min})(U-U_{max})(R+Q)dz=\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U^{2}k^{2}(R+Q)dz

\displaystyle-(U_{min}+U_{max})k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U(R+Q)dz+U_{min}U_{max}k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(R+Q)dz.

(38)

Using (
37
) in (
38
), the latter equation becomes

\displaystyle(\omega_{r}^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2})\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(R+Q)dz+\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}Pdz-(U_{min}+U_{max})k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U(R+Q)dz

\displaystyle+U_{min}U_{max}k^{2}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(R+Q)dz\leq 0.

(39)

Since
P\geq 0
, its integral in (
39
) is positive, making (
39
) still valid when this integral is removed from that equation. It also follows from (
33
) that

k\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}U(R+Q)dz=\omega_{r}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}(R+Q)dz.

(40)

Using this result, and dividing (
39
) (without the integral involving

) by the integral of
(R+Q)
, it follows that

\omega_{r}^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}-(U_{min}+U_{max})k\omega_{r}+U_{min}U_{max}k^{2}\leq 0.

(41)

This relation may also be written as

\left[\omega_{r}-\frac{k}{2}(U_{min}+U_{max})\right]^{2}+\omega_{i}^{2}\leq\left[\frac{k}{2}(U_{max}-U_{min})\right]^{2},

(42)

which has the same form as Howard’s semi-circle theorem, but extends its validity to non-horizontal shear, as a particular case (planar shear with
l=0
) of the version of that theorem derived by
1
.

3
Discussion and conclusions

The absence of a stability condition for non-vertical shear, expressed by (
16
), does not imply that inviscid stratified flows with non-vertical shear are always unstable, rather it indicates that there is no basis to expect them to be stable. This expectation is consistent with available evidence
(
4
;
2
)
. More significantly, (
16
) shows that the stability condition for vertical shear, and its absence for non-vertical shear, both follow from a more general condition valid for shear at arbitrary angles to the vertical.

It might be argued that the derivation of Howard’s semi-circle theorem presented here is less general than that of
1
, as it is restricted to a special class of planar shear flows, whereas Blumen’s applies to non-planar shear flow. However, here the theorem is derived based on a governing equation for the velocity perturbation
\hat{w}
, whereas Blumen departed from an equation for the pressure perturbation
\hat{p}
. Furthermore, because his governing equation accounted for flow inhomogeneities in both horizontal and vertical directions, integration and boundary conditions had to be applied in both directions, instead of only one (as herein).

The main result of the present study is the extended formula for the upper bound of the instability growth rate, (
24
).
Although simple in form, this result has interesting properties. It reduces, as expected, to Howard’s corresponding result for
\theta=0
(vertical shear), but for
\theta=\pi/2
(horizontal shear), it becomes

\omega_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}.

(43)

This means that, for horizontal shear, the upper bound on the growth rate no longer depends of
N
, which is consistent with the absence of
N
in (
6
) in that configuration. Equation (
43
) is equivalent to equation (4.10) of
9
, valid for a non-stratified fluid, and is also equal to the limit of (
24
) for
N=0
.
In dimensionless form, this corresponds to the limit of (
25
) for
\theta=\pi/2
or
Ri^{*}_{min}=0
. At the opposite limit,
Ri^{*}_{min}\rightarrow+\infty
, it can be shown that (
25
) reduces to

\omega_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}U^{\prime 2}_{max}\sin^{2}\theta.

(44)

The best comparison for (
25
) is figure 4 of
2
, which shows the instability growth rate for a stratified planar jet with shear at an arbitrary angle to the vertical, as a function of that angle
\theta
and the Froude number associated with the maximum shear in the jet,
F
. For the particular velocity profile adopted by
2
(a 2D Bickley jet),
F
is related to the minimum Richardson number defined above, as
Ri^{*}_{min}=27/(16F^{2})
, where
F=U_{0}/(LN)
, and
L
and
U_{0}
are characteristic length and velocity scales of the flow, respectively. It should also be noted that all quantities in
2
are normalized by
L
and
U_{0}
, and the growth rate shown in their figure 4 is
\omega_{i}L/U_{0}=[4/(3\sqrt{3})]\omega_{i}/|U^{\prime}_{max}|
. Using these relations, if the upper bound for the growth rate of the instability is denoted by
\omega_{imax}
, (
25
) may be expressed as

\frac{\omega_{imax}^{2}L^{2}}{U_{0}^{2}}=\frac{16}{27}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{27}{16F^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{27}{16F^{2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{27}{16F^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\right]^{1/2}\right\}.

(45)

Figure 1:
Upper bound
\omega_{imax}L/U_{0}
of the growth rate of the linear instability of planar stratified flow (
45
) with shear at angle
\theta
to the vertical as a function of
F/\cos\theta
, for different values of
\theta
(see line labels for details).

This relation is shown in figure
1
in the same form as in figure 4 of
2
. For
\theta=0
, the upper bound of
\omega_{i}L/U_{0}
is zero for
F<3\sqrt{3}/2
, which corresponds to
Ri^{*}_{min}>1/4
, implying flow stability. When
F\gg 1
, which corresponds to
Ri^{*}_{min}\ll 1
,
\omega_{imax}L/U_{0}
asymptotically approaches
\approx 0.38
for all values of
\theta
, which corresponds to
\omega_{imax}^{2}/U^{\prime 2}_{max}=1/4
. On the other hand, as
\theta
increases,
\omega_{imax}L/U_{0}
at low
F
(which corresponds to high
Ri^{*}_{min}
) increases from zero for
\theta=0
to
\approx 0.38
in the limit
\theta=\pi/2
. This behaviour is qualitatively similar to that shown in figure 4 of
2
. The difference is that the actual growth rate
\omega_{i}
is considerably smaller, by a factor of
\approx 2.4
, than its upper bound
\omega_{imax}
, shown in figure
1
. Additionally, in figure 4 of
2
,
\omega_{i}
has a minimum near
F=3\sqrt{3}/2
before increasing to its maximum at larger
F
, whereas in figure
1
,
\omega_{imax}
increases monotonically with
F
.

From (
45
), we may deduce, (consistently with (
43
)–(
44
)), that

\frac{\omega_{imax}(F=0)}{\omega_{imax}(F\rightarrow+\infty)}=\frac{\omega_{imax}(Ri^{*}_{min}\rightarrow+\infty)}{\omega_{imax}(Ri^{*}_{min}=0)}=\sin\theta.

(46)

Interestingly, this result is also exactly satisfied by the growth rate
\omega_{i}
shown in figure 4 of
2
, and is consistent with their low
F
asymptotic theory.

It is worth noting that if, in addition to
l=0
, we also assumed that
v=0
in (
1
)-(
5
), i.e., for fully 2D flow, (
6
) would differ from its traditional version valid for vertical shear only by a factor of
\cos^{2}\theta
multiplying the term proportional to
N^{2}
. In that case, the original results of Miles and Howard would remain applicable, provided that
N^{2}
was replaced by
N^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
, or that
Ri^{*}_{min}
was replaced by
Ri^{*}_{min}\cos^{2}\theta
(cf.
10
). This would lead to an upper bound on the squared growth rate of the instability given by

\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}}{U^{\prime 2}_{max}}\leq\frac{1}{4}-Ri^{*}_{min}\cos^{2}\theta,

(47)

corresponding to a stability condition
Ri^{*}_{min}>1/(4\cos^{2}\theta)
, which, when expressed in terms of
F
, is clearly incompatible with figure 4 of
2
.

Finally, although Squire’s theorem does not hold for stratified flows with non-vertical shear, existing studies
(
4
;
2
)
indicate that the most unstable flow perturbations still tend to have a 2D spatial structure. This reinforces the practical relevance of the present results, where such an assumption is mathematically very convenient.

Saturation of Quantum Cramer-Rao Bounds for Distributed Sensing via Error Sensitivity in SU(1,1)-SU(m) Interferometry
Girish S. Agarwal

Abstract
Breaking the standard quantum limit in the sensing of parameters at different spatial locations, such as in a quantum network, is of great importance. Using the framework of quantum Fisher information, many strategies based on squeezed quantum probes and multipath multiphoton or multiqubit entangled states have been considered. In this context there is always the question of what is the simplest measurement that would saturate quantum Cramer-Rao bound (QCRB). The simplest quantity to measure would be characteristics of photon flux or population distribution in case of qubits. Previous studies have shown that the error sensitivity in SU(1,1) interferometry, also known by several other names as nonlinear interferometry, time reversed measurements; does saturate QCRB for single parameters like phase, displacement, loss. In this work we bring out great utility of generalized SU(1,1) interferometry in distributed sensing. The generalized SU(1,1) interferometry is a combination of SU(m) and SU(1,1) elements, where m is the number of nodes in the network. The SU(m) element is used to produce distributed entanglement starting from a squeezed photonic or matter probe. We demonstrate how error sensitivity measurement at just one output port can saturate or nearly saturate QCRB and thus results in Heisenberg sensitivity of network sensing.

II
Saturation of Cramer-Rao bound via error Sensitivity Measurement in Nonlinear SU(1,1) interferometry: Simple examples

In this section we consider a scheme which can saturate quantum Cramer-Rao bound via very simple measurements of the output signal and fluctuation in the output signal. The signal for our purpose will be the intensity of the output signal. These measurements do not require photon number resolving detectors and are much simpler to implement than homodyne measurements. The scheme is motivated by the well known time reversed metrology scheme used extensively for the estimate of the single parameter like the displacement
\alpha=|\alpha|e^{i\theta}
resulting from electric fields and phase
\phi
corresponding to rotations. This scheme is shown in the Fig.
1
below.

Figure 1:
Schematics of SU(1,1) interferometer.

In Fig.
1
the parameter
\eta
represents the unknown parameter whose sensitivity is being studied by using the quantum probe, which is a single mode squeezed state
|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle
. The squeezing operator is given by
S(\zeta)=\text{exp}(\frac{1}{2}\zeta{a^{\dagger}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{*}a^{2}),\ \zeta=re^{i\beta}
. Note that the squeezing operations are elements of the SU(1,1) group. The parameter
\eta=\alpha
for displacement generated by the unitary operator
\hat{D}(\alpha)=\exp\!\left(\alpha\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\,\hat{a}\right)
or
\eta=\phi
for rotation
\hat{R}(\phi)=\exp\!\left(-i\,\phi\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)
or for phase estimation. In the time-reversed scheme, we add the additional step represented by the inverse of the squeezing
\hat{S}^{-1}(\zeta)
which enables us to achieve Heisenberg-limited sensitivity via the measurement of the signal intensity and its fluctuations.

If the parameter to be estimated is small displacement
\alpha=|\alpha|e^{i\theta}
, then a calculation shows that the final state is a coherent state

\ \mid\psi(\zeta,\alpha)\rangle=|\ |\alpha|e^{i\theta}\Bigl(\cosh r\;-\;\sinh r\,e^{i\beta}\,e^{-2\,i\,\theta}\Bigr)\rangle,

with amplitude which depends on the parameter to be estimated, the squeezing parameter r and its direction of squeezing given by
\beta
. In particular if
\beta-2\theta=\pi
then

\psi(\zeta,\alpha)\rangle=|\ |\alpha|e^{i\theta}e^{r}\rangle.

(2.1)

Thus the estimation of small amplitude
|\alpha|
is amplified by a factor
e^{r}
. The error sensitivity
\Delta|\alpha|
is defined in terms of the signal
S=\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\rangle
and the fluctuation in the signal
(\Delta S)^{2}=\langle(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a})^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\rangle^{2}
i.e.

\Delta|\alpha|=\Delta S/\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial|\alpha|}\right).

(2.2)

Since (
2.3
) is a coherent state, the error sensitivity becomes

\Delta|\alpha|=\frac{1}{2}e^{-r}.

(2.3)

The foregoing assumes fixed
\theta
, and that the direction of squeezing of the probe has to be chosen depending on the angle
\theta
. We next calculate quantum Fisher information using the unitary evolution corresponding to displacement. Writing
\hat{D}(\alpha)
as

\hat{D}(\alpha)=\text{exp}({|\alpha|(\hat{a}^{\dagger}e^{i\theta}-\hat{a}e^{-i\theta})})=e^{i|\alpha|\ \hat{G}},

where
\hat{G}=-i(\hat{a}^{\dagger}e^{i\theta}-\hat{a}e^{-i\theta})
.
The quantum Fisher information defined by

F_{Q}=4\langle\psi_{\zeta}|(\hat{G}-\langle\hat{G}\rangle)^{2}|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle,

(2.4)

can be calculated using the properties of the single mode squeezed vacuum state
|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle=S(\zeta)|0\rangle

\langle\hat{G}\rangle=-i\langle\psi_{\zeta}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}e^{i\theta}-\hat{a}e^{-i\theta}|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle=0.

A simple calculation shows that if
\beta-2\theta=\pi

F_{Q}=4e^{2r},

(2.5)

and hence

(\Delta|\alpha|)_{QCR}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4e^{2r}}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-r}.

(2.6)

On comparing (
2.6
) and (
2.2
) we see that the SU(1,1) interferometer measurement of error sensitivity saturates quantum CR bound. The bound (
2.6
) or (
2.2
) is also the Heisenberg bound on the sensitivity of the displacement measurement.

For completeness we repeat similar arguments for the sensitivity of phase estimator. In this case QFI is obtained by noting that
\hat{G}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}
and hence

F_{Q}=4\langle\psi_{\zeta}|(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a})^{2}|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle-4\langle\psi_{\zeta}|(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a})|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle^{2}.

(2.7)

The QFI is proportional to the number fluctuation in squeezed vacuum
|\psi_{\zeta}\rangle
. These are known
Agarwal 2012
and hence

\displaystyle F_{Q}=8\sinh^{2}(r)\cosh^{2}(r),

\displaystyle(\Delta\phi)_{QCR}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sinh(r)\cosh(r)}.

(2.8)

For error sensitivity we need to find signal
\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\rangle
and its fluctuations
\Delta S
in the state
|\psi(\zeta,\eta)\rangle
. Note that we can transform the expectation values in the final state
|\psi(\zeta,\eta)\rangle
to the values in the initial vacuum state as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\psi(\zeta,\eta)|\hat{G}|\psi(\zeta,\eta)\rangle=\langle 0|\hat{\tilde{G}}|0\rangle,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{G}}=\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\zeta)e^{i\phi\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\hat{S}(\zeta)(\hat{G})\hat{S}^{\dagger}(\zeta)e^{-i\phi\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}\hat{S}(\zeta).

(2.10)

In particular the annihilation operator
\hat{a}
is transformed to

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}=A\hat{a}^{\dagger}+B\hat{a}

\displaystyle A=e^{i\chi}\cosh r\sinh r(e^{-i\phi}-e^{i\phi})

\displaystyle B=e^{-i\phi}\cosh^{2}r-e^{i\phi}\sinh^{2}r.

(2.11)

Using (
2.10
) we can obtain the signal and fluctuations as

\displaystyle S=|A|^{2}=4\sin^{2}\phi\cosh^{2}r\sinh^{2}r,

\displaystyle\Delta S=|A|\sqrt{1+|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}}.

(2.12)

The error sensitivity in the limit
\phi\rightarrow 0
is found to be

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\cosh r\sinh r},

(2.13)

which coincide with the quantum Cramer-Rao bound (
2.8
).

The two examples show that the single made squeezed vacuum is the optimum probe state for estimating both displacement and phase using nonlinear SU(1,1) interferometer. The measured error sensitivity bound coincides with the QCRB. Thus the nonlinear SU(1,1) interferometer is ideal in the sense that its error sensitivity bound obtained for the measurements of the mean intensity and its fluctuations, saturate QCR. This nonlinear interferometer avoids the need for photon number quantum probes, any photon number resolving or homodyne measurements. The discussion presented here would also apply to other parameters like angular displacements
Jha et al. 2011
or beam tracking
Qi et al. 2020
.

III
Distributed sensing of force fields or displacement parameters - Saturation of QCR bound via error sensitivity measurements in SU(1,1)-SU(m)interferometer

We now generalize the simple analysis presented in the previous section to distributed sensing of displacement parameters, we sketch in Figs.
2
and
3
the, standard measurement
scheme and our proposed nonlinear interferometer scheme:

Figure 2:
Traditional measurements of the network average with all operation described in text.

Figure 3:
SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometeric measurement of the error sensitivity from the signal at port 1.

In this scheme
\hat{U}
is an unitary operator which distributes single mode quantum probe over different modes described by the annihilation operators
\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},...\hat{a}_{n}
and thus different modes get entangled. Such entangled modes probe the network of displacements. Each mode of the network is being probed by one mode. Note that the unitary operator U is an element of the group SU(m). For simplicity of analysis we will assume that
\alpha^{\prime}s
are real and that the angle
\beta=\pi
. This is the choice we made in Sec.II for single displacement estimation (cf Eq. (
2.1
)). Our goal is to saturate QCR bound for
\bar{\alpha}
via error sensitivity measurement on mode 1 as indicated in Fig.
3
. We would choose the unitary matrix such that

U_{j1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}.

(3.1)

This choice distributes input photon number equally in all modes as

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\hat{U}_{il}{\hat{a}}_{l},

\displaystyle\langle\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{b}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\sum_{l^{\prime}}\hat{U}^{*}_{il}\hat{U}_{il^{\prime}}\langle{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{l}{\hat{a}}_{l^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\sum_{l^{\prime}}\hat{U}^{*}_{il}\hat{U}_{il^{\prime}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\langle{\hat{a}}^{\dagger}_{l}{\hat{a}}_{l}\rangle.

(3.2)

Since only the mode
\hat{a}_{1}
is occupied, Eq. (
3.2
) yields

\langle\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{b}_{i}\rangle=\hat{U}^{*}_{i1}\hat{U}_{i1}\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{M}\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}\rangle.

(3.3)

Here we study the most sensitive measurement of the average of the displacement over the network i.e the quantity
\bar{\alpha}=\sum_{j}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{M}
. We proceed by calculating the output state of the multimode system by the following the series of transformations as shown in the Fig.
3
. Let
|\{0\}\rangle
represent the vacuum state of all the modes. Then the output state will be

|\psi\rangle=\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}(\zeta)\hat{U}^{-1}\prod_{j}D_{j}(\alpha_{j})\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}(\zeta)|\{0\}\rangle.

(3.4)

Here
\hat{S}_{1}(\zeta)
is the single mode squeezing operator of the mode 1. Note that
\prod_{j}D_{j}(\alpha_{j})=\exp\{\sum_{j}(\alpha_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}-\alpha_{j}\hat{a}_{j})\}
. The unitary transformation in (
3.4
) will transform
\hat{a}_{j}
into new operators
\hat{\tilde{a}}_{j}

\hat{\tilde{a}}_{j}=\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}(\zeta)\hat{U}^{-1}\hat{a}_{j}\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}(\zeta),

(3.5)

and hence

|\psi(\zeta)\rangle=\exp\left\{\sum_{j}(\alpha_{j}\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{j}-\alpha_{j}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{j})\right\}|\{0\}\rangle.

(3.6)

We can write
\hat{\tilde{a}}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{j}=\sum_{l}U_{jl}\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}(\zeta)\hat{a}_{l}\hat{S}_{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\neq 1}U_{jl}\hat{a}_{l}+U_{j1}\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}(\zeta)\hat{a}_{1}\hat{S}_{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\neq 1}U_{jl}\hat{a}_{l}+U_{j1}(\hat{a}_{1}\cosh r-\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\sinh r),

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{l\neq 1}\hat{a}_{l}U_{jl}\alpha_{j}+\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}U_{j1}(\hat{a}_{1}\cosh r-\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger}\sinh r)

\displaystyle=\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{M}(\hat{a}_{1}\cosh r-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\sinh r)+\sum_{l\neq 1}\hat{a}_{l}\sum_{j}U_{jl}\alpha_{j}.

(3.7)

On substituting (
3.7
) in (
3.6
) and on rearranging we find that the state
|\psi\rangle
is a product of coherent states
|\xi_{j}\rangle
with

\xi_{1}=\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{M}e^{r},\ \ \xi_{l}=\sum_{j}U_{jl}\alpha_{j},l\neq 1.

(3.8)

Note that states of the modes with
l\neq 1
do not depend on the squeezing parameter, whereas the state of the initially squeezed mode becomes a coherent state
|\xi_{1}\rangle
with a magnification factor that depends on the network average
\bar{\alpha}
of the displacement parameters and
\sqrt{M}e^{r}
, where M is the number of modes and r is determined by the squeezing of the input single mode. The error sensitivity of the output mode 1 will yield

\Delta\bar{\alpha}=\frac{1}{(2\sqrt{M}e^{r})}.

(3.9)

The error sensitivity (
3.8
) is identical to the Heisenberg limited sensitivity (A10) of the network average
\bar{\alpha}
. The details of the quantum CR bound for
\bar{\alpha}
are given in the appendix A. It is remarkable that for the measurement scheme shown in Fig.
3
we find that the network displacement average error sensitivity bound is equal to QCR bound, which in turn is equal to Heisenberg limited sensitivity.

IV
Saturation of the Cramer-Rao Bound for average phase of the network by the SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometric error sensitivity estimate

In this section we show how the quantum
Cramer-Rao bound (B14) can be saturated via the
error sensitivity measurement using nonlinear interferometry.
The nonlinear interferometric set up is shown in the Fig.
4
.

Figure 4:
Sensing of average phase in a network via measurement of the error sensitivity in SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometer.

We would choose U in such a way that each mode after U has same number of averaged photons i.e
U_{j1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
. The series of operations shown in Fig.
4
will give the wavefunction for all the output modes as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}\hat{U}^{-1}\prod_{j}\hat{R}(\phi_{j})\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}|\{0\}\rangle.

(4.1)

Therefore the expectation value of any operator
\hat{G}
will be

\displaystyle{\langle\psi|}\hat{G}|\psi\rangle=\langle\{0\}|\hat{\tilde{G}}|\{0\}\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{G}}=\hat{S}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{U}^{\dagger}\prod_{j}\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\phi_{j})(\hat{U}^{-1})^{\dagger}(\hat{S}^{-1}_{1})^{\dagger}\hat{G}\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}\hat{U}^{-1}\prod_{j}\hat{R}(\phi_{j})\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\hat{S}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{U}^{\dagger}\prod_{j}\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\phi_{j})\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}\hat{G}\hat{S}^{-1}_{1}\hat{U}^{-1}\prod_{j}\hat{R}(\phi_{j})\hat{U}\hat{S}_{1}.

(4.2)

We define the signal S as the photon number at the detector D i.e.
\langle\psi|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}|\psi\rangle
. Thus the error sensitivity of
\bar{\phi}
would be
\Delta\bar{\phi}=\Delta S/(\frac{\partial S}{\partial\bar{\phi}})
in the limit
\phi_{j}\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle(\Delta S)^{2}=\langle\psi|(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1})^{2}|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}|\psi\rangle^{2}.

(4.3)

And in the view of (4.2)

\displaystyle S=\langle\{0\}|\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\tilde{a}_{1}|0\rangle;\ \Delta S=\langle 0|{(\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1})}^{2}|0\rangle-\langle 0|\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}|0\rangle^{2}.

(4.4)

A calculation shows that

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq 1,j}\{\cosh rU^{*}_{j1}U_{jl}e^{i\phi_{j}}\hat{a}_{l}-\sinh rU_{j1}U^{*}_{jl}e^{-i\phi_{j}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{l}\}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}|U_{j1}|^{2}\{[\cosh^{2}re^{i\phi_{j}}-\sinh^{2}re^{-i\phi_{j}}]\hat{a}_{1}

\displaystyle+2i\cosh r\sinh r\sin\phi_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\}.

(4.5)

Note two different types of contribution to
\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}
in Eq. (
4.5
). One much larger contribution from the mode which is initially squeezed (terms involving squares of hyperbolic function) and the other smaller contributions from the initial unsqueezed modes. The signal S is then given by

\displaystyle S=\langle 0|\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}|0\rangle=I_{1}+I_{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}=4\cosh^{2}r\sinh^{2}r\sum_{jk}|U_{ij}|^{2}|U_{1k}|^{2}\sin\phi_{j}\sin\theta_{k},

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sinh^{2}r\sum_{l\neq 1,jk}U^{*}_{j1}U_{k1}U^{*}_{kl}U_{jl}e^{-i(\phi_{k}-\phi_{j})}.

(4.6)

We simplify (4.6) using
U_{1j}=U^{*}_{j1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}
and the unitary property of
\hat{U}
. We also assume
\phi^{\prime}s
are small so that we can expand exponentials in a power series. The first term in (4.6) is simple and equal to

\displaystyle I_{1}\approx\bar{\phi}^{2}(4\cosh^{2}r\sinh^{2}r\ ),\bar{\phi}=\frac{\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}+...+\phi_{M}}{M}.

(4.7)

The second term is more complicated:

\displaystyle I_{2}\approx\Phi\sinh^{2}r\ ,\Phi=(\frac{\phi_{1}^{2}+\phi_{2}^{2}+...+\phi_{M}^{2}}{M}-\bar{\phi}^{2}).

(4.8)

Thus the signal at the output 1 is a function of two parameters of the network
\bar{\phi}
and
\Phi
. For the simplicity of the measurement, we can assume large squeezing and drop the term
I_{2}
in (4.8). Thus we can approximate the signal at the output port 1 as

\displaystyle I\approx I_{1}=4\cosh^{2}r\ \sinh^{2}r\ \bar{\phi}^{2}.

(4.9)

Note further that for small
\phi_{j}
,
\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}
can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}=\hat{a}_{1}-i\tilde{\phi}((\cosh^{2}r+\sinh^{2}r)\hat{a}_{1}+2i\cosh r\sinh r\ \tilde{\phi}\ \hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}+R,

where R is the contribution from
\hat{a}_{l}
and
\hat{a}_{l}^{\dagger}
with
l\neq 1
. The operator R is important for the validity of the commutation relation
[\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1},\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}]=1
. It has to be kept in mind that R has terms only linear in hyperbolic functions
\cosh r
and
\sinh r
. We will compute
\Delta S
to order
\bar{\phi}
:

\displaystyle(\Delta S)^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1})^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{\tilde{a}}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger 2}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}^{2}_{1}\rangle+\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rangle-\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\approx 2\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=2S.

(4.10)

On using (4.10) and (4.9), we obtain for the error sensitivity

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{\phi}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\sinh r\cosh r}.

(4.11)

which agrees with the QCR bound derived in Appendix B. It should be remembered that the expression (4.11) is obtained for large
r
otherwise
\Delta\bar{\phi}
will be greater than (4.11).

We conclude this section by examining the case when the input is a single mode squeezed coherent state i.e.

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\exp(\frac{1}{2}r\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}-\frac{1}{2}r\hat{a}^{2})|\alpha\rangle,

where

is the average photon number in the coherent state. We will assume that the coherent state has few photons. This was the case studied by Guo et al

. The error sensitivity in the average phase of the network was obtained from measurements of the mean homodyne signal and fluctuations in the homodyne signal. Guo et al

did the homodyne measurements on all the ports. We now present the results of the homodyne measurements
at the output port 1 in the SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometer. For this purpose we use (4.5)
and use
\hat{a}_{1}|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle,\hat{a}_{l}|0\rangle=0,l\neq 1
and hence

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha\bigg[\sum_{j}\{2i\cosh r\sinh r\sin\phi_{j}+

\displaystyle\quad-\sinh^{2}re^{-i\phi_{j}})|U_{j1}|^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\cong\alpha+i\alpha\bar{\phi}e^{2r},\ \text{small }\phi.

(4.12)

We define the homodyne signal
S_{HD}
as proportional to Im
\langle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rangle
.

\displaystyle S_{HD}=\alpha\tilde{\phi}e^{2r},\frac{\partial S_{HD}}{\partial\bar{\phi}}=\alpha e^{2r}.

(4.13)

The fluctuations in
S_{HD}
for
\phi\rightarrow 0
can be obtained by noting that
\hat{\tilde{a}}_{1}\rightarrow\hat{a}_{1}
as
\phi\rightarrow 0
:

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{S}^{2}_{HD}=\langle(\frac{\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}}{2i})^{2}\rangle-\langle(\frac{\hat{a}_{1}-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}}{2i})\rangle^{2}=\frac{1}{4}.

(4.14)

and hence the error sensitivity is

\displaystyle(\Delta\tilde{\phi})_{HD}=\frac{e^{-2r}}{2\alpha}.

(4.15)

It is interesting that the error sensitivity measurement using only in the port 1 of the SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometer coincides with the one in the scheme of Guo et al
Guo et al. 2020
.

Before we conclude this section we like to relate (4.15) the expression for the error sensitivity to the QFI using the input state as squeezed coherent state. In this case the result (B12) of the appendix is modified to

{{F_{Q}}=4\{\langle\alpha|{\hat{S}_{1}}^{\dagger}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1})^{2}{\hat{S}_{1}}|\alpha\rangle-(\langle\alpha|{\hat{S}_{1}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{1}{\hat{S}_{1}}|\alpha\rangle)^{2})}

(4.16)

which can be simplified to

\displaystyle{F_{Q}}=

\displaystyle 4\{\sinh^{4}r+\sinh^{2}r+\sinh^{2}r\cosh^{2}r

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}e^{2r}(1+2\sinh^{2}r+2\sinh r\cosh r)\}

(4.17)

Further simplification depends on the relative magnitudes
of
|\alpha|^{2}
and
\sinh^{2}r
ie. photon numbers in the original coherent states and the number generated if initially
\alpha=0
. Choosing
|\alpha|^{2}\gg\sinh^{2}r
(as in experiments of Guo et al
Guo et al. 2020
),

{F_{Q}}\approx 4\alpha^{2}e^{4r}
leading to the Cramer - Rao bound.

{(\Delta\phi)_{QCRB}\approx\frac{e^{-2r}}{2\alpha}={(\Delta\tilde{\phi})_{HD}}}

(4.18)

which coincides with the error sensitivity result (4.15).

Conclusion

In this work we bring out the utility of SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometry in the context of sensing
the parameters of a quantum network. We demonstrated this explicitly for the case of
distributed phase sensing and force fields causing displacements. There is no need to
find ways to measure the quantum Fisher information- the error sensitivity is sufficient to
obtain Heisenberg sensitivity. This suggested alternative is simpler than homodyne
measurements at many output ports. The SU(1,1)-SU(m) interferometry is applicable to
other parameters like network of angular displacements.

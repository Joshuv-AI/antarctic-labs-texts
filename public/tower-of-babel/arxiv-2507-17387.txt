Holomorphic functions with Nash real part
Antonio Carbone

Abstract
. In this paper we show that a holomorphic function, defined on an open subset D D of ℂ n {\mathbb{C}}^{n} , is a complex Nash function if and only its real part (or equivalently its imaginary part) is a real Nash function.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 14P20, 32C07. Secondary: 32A

1.
Introduction

Nash functions were introduced by John Nash in his groundbreaking paper
[
N
]
. A real (resp. complex) Nash function is a real analytic (resp. holomorphic) function which is algebraic over the ring of polynomials with real (resp. complex) coefficients (see Definition
2.1
below). Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset and
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a holomorphic function. After identifying
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
in the usual way, the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
can be regarded as real analytic functions. A natural question is the following:

Question 1.1
.

It there any relationship between the following two properties?

(i)

f
is a complex Nash function,

(ii)

f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are real Nash functions.

In Proposition
2.4
we show that properties (i) and (ii) are equivalent. This result is probably well known by the experts. We include its proof in this paper, as we could not find a precise reference for it in the existing literature. It is worthwhile to remark that property (ii) implies property (i) if and only if the involved function
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}
is holomorphic (see Remark
2.5
below).

A more interesting question is the following:

Question 1.2
.

Is it true that if
f_{1}
is a real Nash function, then
f
is a complex Nash function?

Nash functions are not closed under taking integrations. Thus, to answer the previous question one cannot simply make use of the Cauchy-Riemann equations.

The purpose of this this paper is to answer positively Question
1.2
. Namely, to show the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset and
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a holomorphic function. If
f_{1}
is a real Nash function on
D
, then
f
is a complex Nash function on
D
.

As
if=-f_{2}+if_{1}
is a holomorphic function on
D
, then the previous result holds true also if we consider the imaginary part
f_{2}
of
f
instead of its real part
f_{1}
. By Proposition
2.4
and Theorem
1.3
, we deduce straightforwardly the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset and
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a holomorphic function. Then the followings are equivalent:

(i)

f
is a complex Nash function.

(ii)

f_{1}
is a real Nash function.

(iii)

f_{2}
is a real Nash function.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic concepts about Nash functions and we answer Question
1.1
. We refer the reader to
[
BCR
, §8]
and
[
Tw
]
for a more detailed introduction to Nash functions.

2.1.
Real and complex Nash functions

Let
k
be either the field of real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
or the field of complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}:=\{x+iy:x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
, where
i^{2}=-1
. Let
D\subset k^{n}
be a (non-empty) open subset and
f:D\to k
a
k
-valued function.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
f
is a
k
-Nash function at
x_{0}\in D
if there exist an open neighbourhood
U
of
x_{0}
in
D
and a non-zero polynomial
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]:=k[{\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{n},{\tt t}]
such that

•

f
is
k
-analytic on
U
,

•

P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in U
.

The function
f
is said to be a
k
-Nash function on
D
if it is a Nash function at every point of
D
. We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{k}(D)
the set of all
k
-Nash functions on
D
.
\sqbullet

We will also call
{\mathbb{R}}
-Nash functions
real Nash functions
and
{\mathbb{C}}
-Nash functions
complex Nash functions
. As being
k
-analytic is a local property, we have that if a function
f
is
k
-Nash on
D
, then
f
is also
k
-analytic on
D
. By
[
BCR
, Cor.8.1.6]
, it follows that the set
\mathcal{N}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(D)
endowed with the pointwise addition and multiplication of (real) functions is a subring of the ring of real analytic functions on
D
. While, by
[
Tw
, Cor.1.11]
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(D)
endowed with the pointwise addition and multiplication of (complex) functions is a subring of the ring of holomorphic functions on
D
.

Let
f:D\to k
be a
k
-analytic function and assume that
D
is an open and connected subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. As for each polynomial
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
the function
D\to k,\,x\mapsto P(x,f(x))
is
k
-analytic, we deduce (straightforwardly) that the followings are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(D)
,

(ii)

there exists
x_{0}\in D
such that
f
is
k
-Nash at
x_{0}
,

(iii)

there exists an irreducible polynomial
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
such that
P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D
. Moreover, if
k={\mathbb{C}}
, the polynomial
P
is unique up to a non-zero constant (as a consequence of Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz).

In particular, if
D
has finitely many connected components, then
f\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(D)
if and only if there exists a non-zero polynomial
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
such that
P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D
. If
D
has infinitely many connected components,
f
might not be algebraic over the ring
k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
, as shown in the following example.

Example 2.2
.

For each integer
m\geq 1
let

D_{m}:=\{x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in k^{n}:\|x-(4m,0,\ldots,0)\|_{n}<1\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{n}
denotes the Euclidean norm of
k^{n}
. The set
D:=\bigcup_{m\geq 1}D_{m}
is an open subset of
k^{n}
with infinitely many connected components. Let
f:D\to k
be the
k
-analytic function defined as
f(x)=x_{1}^{m}
for
x\in D_{m}
. Clearly
f\in\mathcal{N}_{k}(D)
, because for each integer
m\geq 1
the polynomial
P_{m}({\tt x},{\tt t}):={\tt t}-{\tt x}_{1}^{m}\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
satisfies
P_{m}(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D_{m}
. Assume now that there exists a non-zero polynomial
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
such that
P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D
. Let
r\geq 0
be the degree of
P
with respect to the variable
{\tt t}
and
s\geq 0
the degree of
P
with respect to the variable
{\tt x}_{1}
. We may write

P({\tt x},{\tt t})=p_{r}({\tt x}){\tt t}^{r}+p_{r-1}({\tt x}){\tt t}^{r-1}+\ldots+p_{1}({\tt x}){\tt t}+p_{0}({\tt x}),

where
p_{0},\ldots,p_{r}\in k[{\tt x}]
are polynomials of degree
\leq s
with respect to the variable
{\tt x}_{1}
. Observe that
p_{r}
is not the zero polynomial, because the degree of
P
with respect to the variable
{\tt t}
is
r
. As
P(x,x_{1}^{m})=P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D_{m}
, then for each
m\geq 1
the polynomial

P({\tt x},{\tt x}_{1}^{m})=p_{r}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}^{mr}+p_{r-1}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}^{m(r-1)}+\ldots+p_{1}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}+p_{0}({\tt x})\in k[{\tt x}]

is the zero polynomial, because
D_{m}
is an open subset of
k^{n}
. Given a polynomial
Q\in k[{\tt x}]
we denote by
\deg_{{\tt x}_{1}}(Q({\tt x}))
the degree of
Q({\tt x})
with respect to the variable
{\tt x}_{1}
. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer such that
m>s
. As
p_{r}
is not the zero polynomial, then

\deg_{{\tt x}_{1}}(p_{r}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}^{mr})\geq mr>m(r-1)+s\geq\deg_{{\tt x}_{1}}(P({\tt x},{\tt x}_{1}^{m})-p_{r}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}^{mr})=\deg_{{\tt x}_{1}}(p_{r}({\tt x}){\tt x}_{1}^{mr}).

In particular, it follows that
p_{r}({\tt x})=0
, which is a contradiction. We deduce that there exist no non-zero polynomials
P\in k[{\tt x},{\tt t}]
such that
P(x,f(x))=0
for each
x\in D
.
\sqbullet

Given a complex polynomial

P({\tt z}):=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}=0}^{s}\alpha_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}{\tt z}_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots{\tt z}_{n}^{k_{n}}\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z}]:={\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z}_{1},\ldots,{\tt z}_{n}],

its
conjugated polynomial
is defined as

P^{c}({\tt z})=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}=0}^{s}\overline{\alpha_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}}{\tt z}_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots{\tt z}_{n}^{k_{n}}.

A straightforward computation shows that the polynomial
P({\tt z})P^{c}({\tt z})
has real coefficients. In particular, we have the following:

Remark 2.3
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open and connected subset and
f:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a holomorphic function. Then
f\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(D)
if and only there exists a non-zero polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
with real coefficients such that
P(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D
.
\sqbullet

2.2.
Relationship between real and complex Nash functions

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset and
f:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a holomorphic function. We may write
f=f_{1}+if_{2}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
y:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
and
z:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
. We identify
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
by setting
z:=x+iy
. In particular, may regard the subset
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
as the subset

\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}:x+iy\in D\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

Up to a light abuse of notation we will still denote the previous set as
D
. Moreover, we may regard the holomorphic function
f:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
as the real analytic map

D\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad(x,y)\mapsto(f_{1}(x+iy),f_{2}(x+iy)).

Again, up to a light abuse of notation, we will denote the previous analytic function as
f
and its real components as
f_{1}(x,y)
and
f_{2}(x,y)
. These choices will create no confusion as the situation will be always clear by the context.

The following result, that relates real and complex Nash functions, is probably well known. We include its proof here, as we could not find a precise reference for it.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset and
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
. The followings are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(D)
.

(ii)

f
is holomorphic on
D
and
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(D)
.

Proof.

As being Nash is a local property, we may assume that
D
connected.

(i) implies (ii).
The functions
f_{1},f_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
are real analytic, because
f
is holomorphic. In particular, it is enough to show that there exist two non-zero polynomials

P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}]:={\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{n},{\tt y}_{1},\ldots,{\tt y}_{n},{\tt t}]

such that
P_{1}(x,y,f_{1}(x,y))=P_{2}(x,y,f_{2}(x,y))=0
for each
(x,y)\in D
. As
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(D)
and
D
is connected, then there exists a non-zero polynomial
P({\tt z},{\tt t})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]:={\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z}_{1},\ldots,{\tt z}_{n},{\tt t}]
such that
P(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D
. By remark
2.3
, we may assume that
P({\tt z},{\tt t})
has real coefficients. As
P({\tt z},{\tt t})
has real coefficients and
P(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D
, then a straightforward computation shows that the polynomial
P(\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}]:={\mathbb{C}}[\overline{{\tt z}}_{1},\ldots,\overline{{\tt z}}_{n},{\tt t}]
(anti-holomorphic in the variables
\overline{{\tt z}}_{1},\ldots,\overline{{\tt z}}_{n}
) satisfies
P(\overline{z},\overline{f(z)})=0
for each
z\in D
. Observe that the variables
{\tt z}_{1},\ldots,{\tt z}_{n}
,
\overline{{\tt z}}_{1},\ldots,\overline{{\tt z}}_{n}
are algebraically independent over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

As
P({\tt z},{\tt w})
and
P(\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}-{\tt w})
have no common factors in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
of positive degree with respect to the variable
{\tt w}
, then by
[
CLO
, 3.6.1(ii)]
and its proof, there exist two polynomials
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
and a non-zero polynomial
R\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}]
such that

R({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t})=A({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t},{\tt w})P({\tt z},{\tt w})+B({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t},{\tt w})P(\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}-{\tt w}).

Here the polynomial
R({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t})
is the resultant in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
with respect to the variable
{\tt w}
of the polynomials
P({\tt z},{\tt w})
and
P(\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}-{\tt w})
. Observe that

(2.1)

R(z,\overline{z},f(z)+\overline{f(z)})=0

for each
z\in D
, because
P(z,f(z))=P(\overline{z},\overline{f(z)})=0
for each
z\in D
.

For each
k=1,\ldots,n
we write
{\tt z}_{k}:={\tt x}_{k}+i{\tt y}_{k}
, so
\overline{{\tt z}}_{k}={\tt x}_{k}-i{\tt y}_{k}
. As the ring homomorphism

\varphi:{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}]\mapsto{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}],\quad P({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t})\mapsto P({\tt x}+i{\tt y},{\tt x}-i{\tt y},{\tt t}).

is an isomorphism, the polynomial

Q({\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}):=\varphi(R({\tt z},\overline{{\tt z}},{\tt t}))

is not the zero polynomial of
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}]
. Up to substitute
Q
with
QQ^{c}
, we may assume that
Q\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}]
. Moreover, as
2f_{1}(x,y)=f(z)+\overline{f(z)}
for each
z=x+iy\in D
, we deduce that
Q(x,y,2f_{1}(x,y))=0
for each
(x,y)\in D
. Up to substitute
Q({\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t})
with
Q({\tt x},{\tt y},2{\tt t})
, we may conclude that
f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}(D)
.

As
2if_{2}(x,y)=f(z)-\overline{f(z)}=f(z)+(-\overline{f(z)})
for each
z=x+iy\in D
, a similar argument shows that
f_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}(D)
, as required.

(ii) implies (i).
Let
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}]
be non-zero polynomials such that

P_{1}(x,y,f_{1}(x,y))=P_{2}(x,y,f_{2}(x,y))=0

for each
(x,y)\in D
. The set
\{y_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\,P_{1}({\tt x},y_{0},{\tt t})=0\,\,\text{or}\,\,P_{2}({\tt x},y_{0},{\tt t})=0\}
is nowhere dense in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus, up to an affine change of coordinates, we may assume that
\Omega:=D\cap({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\{0\})
is a non-empty open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\{0\}
and that the polynomials

Q_{1}({\tt z},{\tt t}):=P_{1}({\tt z},0,{\tt t})\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{2}({\tt z},{\tt t}):=P_{2}({\tt z},0,-i{\tt t})

are not the zero polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
. By the fact that

P_{1}(x,0,f_{1}(x,0))=P_{2}(x,0,f_{2}(x,0))=0

for each
(x,0)\in\Omega
we deduce that

Q_{1}(x,f_{1}(x,0))=P_{1}(x,0,f_{1}(x,0))=0\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{2}(x,if_{2}(x,0))=P_{2}(x,0,f_{2}(x,0))=0

for each
(x,0)\in\Omega
.

Let
R({\tt z},{\tt t})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
be the resultant in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
with respect to the variable
{\tt w}
of
Q_{1}({\tt z},{\tt w})
and
Q_{2}({\tt z},{\tt t}-{\tt w})
. As
Q_{1}({\tt z},{\tt w})
and
Q_{2}({\tt z},{\tt t}-{\tt w})
have no common irreducible factors in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
of positive degree with respect to the variable
{\tt w}
, then by
[
CLO
, 3.6.1(ii)]
and its proof,
R({\tt z},{\tt t})
is not the zero polynomial and there exist two polynomials
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t},{\tt w}]
such that

R({\tt z},{\tt t})=A({\tt z},{\tt t},{\tt w})Q_{1}({\tt z},{\tt w})+B({\tt z},{\tt t},{\tt w})Q_{2}({\tt z},{\tt t}-{\tt w}).

A straightforward computations shows that

(2.2)

R(x,f_{1}(x,0)+if_{2}(x,0))=0

for each
(x,0)\in\Omega
. Recall that
f(z)=f_{1}(x,y)+if_{2}(x,y)
for each
z=x+iy\in D
. The map
D\to{\mathbb{C}},\ z\mapsto R(z,f(z))
is holomorphic on
D
, because
f
is holomorphic on
D
. By (
2.2
), we have

R(z,f(z))=R(x,f_{1}(x,0)+if_{2}(x,0))=0

for each
z=x
such that
(x,0)\in\Omega
. We deduce that
R(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D
, because
\Omega
is an open subset of the real vector space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\{0\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
R(z,f(z))=0
on
\Omega
(here we are using in an essential way that
f
is holomorphic, see also Remark
2.5
below). As
R({\tt z},{\tt t})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
is not the zero polynomial, we conclude that
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(D)
, as required.
∎

The following remark shows that the assumption that
f
is holomorphic is essential in order to guarantee that (ii) implies (i).

Remark 2.5
.

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an open subset,
D_{0}
a connected component of
D
and
f:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
a continuous function. By
[
GR
, Lem.3.B.13]
, if there exists a non-zero polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
such that
P(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D_{0}
, then
f
is holomorphic on
D_{0}
. In particular, in the previous proposition the assumption that
f
is holomorphic cannot be dropped. In fact, if
f_{1},f_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
are real Nash functions on
D
such that the map
f:=f_{1}+if_{2}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
is not holomorphic on
D
, then there exists a connected component
D_{0}
of
D
such that there exist no non-zero polynomials
P\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z},{\tt t}]
such that
P(z,f(z))=0
for each
z\in D_{0}
. A concrete example is given by the anti-holomorphic function
f(z):=\overline{z}=x-iy
.
\sqbullet

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we show Theorem
1.3
. The argument is based on a elementary, but brilliant, calculation of Cartan
[
Ca
, §IV.3.5]
that allows to reconstruct a holomorphic function by its real part in a definable way (that is, without integration). Recall that a set
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is
semialgebraic if it is a Boolean combination of sets defined by polynomial equalities and inequalities, while a function
f:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
is semialgebraic if its graph is a semialgebraic subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We consider the case
n=1
and
n>1
separately.

Case
n=1
.
We may assume that
0\in D
and that
f(0)=0
. As being Nash is a local property, we may assume, in addition, that
D
is an open ball centred in 0 such that the Taylor series of
f
in 0 converges in
D
. Thus, we may write

f(z)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}a_{\nu}z^{\nu}

for each
z\in D
. Let
\rho>0
be such that
D:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<\rho\}
and let
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
be the polydisk defined as
\Delta:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}:|z_{1}|<\tfrac{\rho}{2},|z_{2}|<\tfrac{\rho}{2}\}
. Define the formal power series

F({\tt z}_{1},{\tt z}_{2}):=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}a_{\nu}({\tt z}_{1}+i{\tt z}_{2})^{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad H({\tt z}_{1},{\tt z}_{2}):=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\overline{a}_{\nu}({\tt z}_{1}-i{\tt z}_{2})^{\nu}.

A straightforward computation shows that these formal power series converge on
\Delta
. Define the holomorphic function
g:\Delta\to{\mathbb{C}}
as

(3.1)

g(z_{1},z_{2}):=\frac{1}{2}(F(z_{1},z_{2})+H(z_{1},z_{2})).

Observe that, as
f(0)=0
, then
H(0,0)=0
. By (
3.1
) and by the fact that
H(0,0)=0
, we have

f(z)=2g\Big(\frac{z}{2},\frac{z}{2i}\Big)

for each
z\in D
. As
f(x+iy)=F(x,y)
and
\overline{f(x+iy)}=H(x,y)
for each
(x,y)\in\Delta\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, by (
3.1
), we have that
g(x,y)=f_{1}(x+iy)
for each
(x,y)\in\Delta\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

As
f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{R}}(D)
and
D
is connected, then there exists an irreducible non-zero polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x},{\tt y},{\tt t}]
such that

P(x,y,2f_{1}(x+iy))=0

for each
(x,y)\in D
. In particular,
P(x,y,2g(x,y))=0
for each
(x,y)\in\Delta\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, because
g(x,y)=f_{1}(x+iy)
for each
(x,y)\in\Delta\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. As the function
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto P(z_{1},z_{2},2g(z_{1},z_{2}))
is holomorphic on
\Delta
and vanishes identically on
\Delta\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, then
P(z_{1},z_{2},g(z_{1},z_{2}))=0
for each
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Delta
. We regard the polynomial
P
as a polynomial in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\tt z}_{1},{\tt z}_{2},{\tt t}]
. If
P({\tt z},-i{\tt z},{\tt t})
is the zero polynomial, then

\{{\tt z}_{2}-i{\tt z}_{1}=0\}\subset\{P({\tt z}_{1},{\tt z}_{2},{\tt t})=0\}.

It follows, by Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz, that
{\tt z}_{2}-i{\tt z}_{1}
divides

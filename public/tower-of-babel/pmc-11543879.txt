Aromatase inhibitors can improve the semen quality of aged roosters by up regulating genes related to steroid hormone synthesis
Authors: Xuliang Luo, Xuelian Li, Zi Mei, Haobo Zhou, Yan Chen, Haoxing Wang, Ping Qiu, Yanzhang Gong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractExcessive aromatase can reduce reproductive performance in aged roosters. Aromatase inhibitors (AI) can inhibit the aromatase activity and improve the semen quality of aged roosters. However, relevant molecular mechanism is still unclear. The purpose of this study was to explore the regulatory mechanism of AI letrozole improving semen quality in aged roosters by transcriptomic and proteomic sequencing. In this study, 56-week-old roosters were reared in separate cages on a standard basice diet and oral letrozole 42 days (D) at a daily dose 0.25 mg/kg. Semen quality and serum hormone were measured before (0 D) and after (42 D) letrozole administration. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) were detected, respectively. The results indicated that semen volume, sperm motility, sperm density, MMP, testosterone (T) and gonadotropin releasing hormone (GnRH) in letrozole treatment group (LET) were significantly increased than those in control group (CN) (P<0.05); estradiol (E2) and ROS in LET were significantly lower than those in CN (P<0.05). Through transcriptomic and proteomic analysis, we identified 189 differently expressed genes (DEGs) and 64 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) in the comparison of LET and CN. DEGs and DEPs Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) items are mainly enriched in steroid biosynthetic process, cell differentiation and proliferation, lipid metabolic process, oxidation-reduction process and electron transfer activity. Furthermore, 8 genes including STAR, CYP17A1, NSDHL, SULT1E1, EHF, NRNPA1, PLIN2 and SDHA were identified as key genes for letrozole to regulate semen quality in aged roosters. These results indicate that letrozole can up-regulate the expression of genes related to steroid hormone synthesis, cell differentiation and proliferation, electron transfer activity, and enhance mitochondrial activity, increase testicular weight, and ultimately improve the semen quality of aged roosters.Keywords: Aged roosters, Aromatase inhibitor, Letrozole, Testis, Sperm motility

Introduction

In poultry, semen quality is considered as the major determinant of male reproduction, which can directly affect fertility. Good semen quality not only improve production efficiency, but also reduce the breeding cost of rooster (Sabzian-Melei et al., 2022). Testosterone (T) is a steroid hormone related to semen quality and plays an important role in the regulation of male reproduction (Qi et al., 2019; Zirkin and Chen, 2000). With the age of roosters more than 45 weeks, the testicular T level decrease, and estradiol (E2) level increase, resulting in a decline in semen quality, which then affects the reproductive performance (Sabzian-Melei et al., 2022). Aromatase is one of the cytochromes P450 enzyme series, which can catalyze androstenedione and T to form estrone and E2, respectively, and is the key rate-limiting enzyme in estrogen biosynthesis (Simpson ER et al., 1994). The lower fertility of aged roosters is related to aromatase activity, and the high expression of aromatase reduces T level and increased E2 level, thus affecting the semen quality and reproductive performance (Aitken, 2020; Weil S et al., 1999). The level of T in testis of turkey with high sperm motility increased, and aromatase mRNA and protein expression were significantly down-regulated (Pardyak et al., 2018). Therefore, inhibiting the activity of aromatase, reducing the estrogen level and increasing the androgen level may be one of the effective methods to improve the semen quality and production performance of the aged rooster.

Letrozole, as an aromatase inhibitor (AI), is a non-steroidal late inhibitor that inhibits aromatase activity by binding to the heme iron of estrogen synthase (de Ronde and de Jong, 2011; Zafar et al., 2023). Letrozole can act on the final stage of estrogen biosynthesis without interfering with the synthesis of other sterol hormones, which is well targeted in terms of action and efficacy (Bhatnagar, 2007). Letrozole treatment can improve the levels of T, luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and significantly improve the semen quality of patients with non-obstructive azoospermia (Cavallini et al., 2013; T'Sjoen et al., 2005). For infertile men with unbalance of T and E2 ratios, AI can significantly increase the proportion of T, effectively improve sperm motility and sperm density, and treat male infertility (Cavallini et al., 2013; Saylam et al., 2011; Shuling et al., 2019). Some studies have shown that reactive oxygen species (ROS) can raise aromatase activity, leading to increased production of E2 and inhibition of T release, thereby affecting semen quality (Darbandi et al., 2018). The levels of ROS in infertile men are often higher than those in normal men (Dhillo et al., 2019). Letrozole treatment can decrease the ROS levels and increase sperm motility (Kooshesh et al., 2020). In poultry, AI can reduce aromatase activity, increase testicular weight and sperm production, and improve the reproductive performance of aged roosters (Adeldust et al., 2017). Bazyar et al. (2019) reported that AI increased semen concentration, sperm motility and plasma T level, and ultimately improved the fertility of aged roosters (Bazyar et al., 2019). Previous studies have confirmed the role of letrozole in improving semen quality of aged roosters. However, the molecular mechanism of letrozole regulating the semen quality and reproductive performance of aged roosters remains unclear.

In this study, we investigated the effects of oral letrozole on hormone levels and semen quality, and then performed transcriptome and proteome sequencing of testis, aiming to reveal the molecular mechanisms by which letrozole regulate the semen quality of aged roosters.

Materials and methods

Laboratory animal management and study design

Thirty 56-week-old Wenchang roosters were bred into individual cages at the experimental chicken Farm of Huazhong Agricultural University (Wuhan, China) and divided into a control group (CN) and aromatase inhibitor letrozole treatment group (LET) with 15 roosters in each group. The LET group was fed 200 μL of letrozole dissolved with corn oil every day, and the letrozole dose was converted according to the proportion of 0.25 mg/kg of the body weight of each rooster, while the CN group was fed 200 μL of corn oil. The entire experiment lasted for 42 days. The CN and LET groups on day 0 of treatment were named pre-CN and pre-LTE, respectively. All animal experiments were carried out following standard procedures and approved by the Ethics Committee of Huazhong Agricultural University, China (ID Number: HZAUCH-2020-0020).

Semen collection and quality evaluation

Semen of aged roosters was collected before (0 D, pre-CN, pre-LTE) and after (42 D, CN, LET) letrozole administration, respectively. Semen volume was measured with pipette. Semen parameters was determined by computer assisted analysis (CASA, Minitube AndroVision, Minitube, Germany) after diluted (at 1:80) the semen with physiological saline. The semen quality assessment was completed within 15min, with 3 replicates for the measurements of each indicator.

Sperm mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species were detected by flow cytometry

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was evaluated according to the MMP assay kit with 5,5′,6,6′-tetrachloro-1,1′,3,3′tetraethyl-imidacarbocyanine iodide (JC-1) (Beyotime, China). JC-1 is a fluorescent probe. JC-1 accumulates in the matrix of mitochondria to forming polymer J-aggregates at high MMP, which can produce red fluorescence; JC-1 is monomer at the low MMP and can produce green fluorescence (Wang et al., 2008). The experimental steps are as follows: first, 1×105 sperms were resuspended in 500 μL of physiological saline, and then 500 μL JC-1 staining solution was added and mixed several times, incubate in a 37°C incubator for 20min. After the incubation, the mixture was centrifuged at 600×g and 4°C for 5min, the supernatant was discarded and the precipitate was washed twice with JC-1 staining buffer. Finally, resuspend the precipitate with 1mL of JC-1 staining buffer and perform fluorescence detection using a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA). Sperm reactive oxygen species (ROS) was evaluated according to the ROS assay kit (Beyotime, China). The 2′,7′-Dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) is a probe without fluorescence. After entering the cell, it can be hydrolyzed by intracellular esterase to produce 2′,7′-Dichlorodihydrofluorescein (DCFH). However, DCFH cannot penetrate the cell membrane, so it is easy to be loaded into the cell. ROS in cells can oxidize DCFH to produce fluorescence. The level of ROS in cells can be determined by detecting the fluorescence (Zou et al., 2017). The experimental steps are as follows: sperms were treated as indicated and incubated with physiological saline added containing 10 μM DCFH-DA at 37°C for 20min. After the incubation, the mixture was centrifuged at 600×g and 4°C for 5min, the supernatant was discarded and the precipitate was washed three with physiological saline. Finally, resuspend the precipitate with 1 mL of physiological saline and perform fluorescence detection using a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA).

Detection of reproductive related hormone concentration in serum

Blood of aged roosters was collected before (0 D, pre-CN, pre-LTE) and after (42 D, CN, LET) letrozole administration, respectively. Serum was separated at 2500 rpm/min after stood at room temperature for 60min. According to the instructions of E2, T, FSH, LH, GnRH enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ThermoFisher, USA), mix 10 μL of serum with 40 μL of sample diluent, then add 100 μL of HRP-Conjugate reagent and incubate in a 37°C incubator for 60min. Wash 5 times with wash solution, and add the chromogen solution and incubate in a 37°C incubator to avoid light for 15min. Then add the stop solution to stop the reaction. Finally, measure the absorbance of the sample using an enzyme linked immunosorbent assay detector (Tecan, Switzerland).

Testis histological evaluation

Testicular tissue was collected and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at 4°C overnight. The fixed testicular tissue was dehydrated, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned, then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE). The morphological of testicular tissues were observed by a light microscope (Olympus, Japan). The thickness of epithelium cells and diameter of seminiferous tubules were measured at 20 x magnification by Image J software, a total of 5 HE-stained sections were measured, and three seminiferous tubules were measured in each section (Hamidatou Khati et al., 2018).

Testicular tissue immunofluorescence detection

Testicles were collected and placed in tissue fixating solution for 24 h, and then washed with running water for paraffin embedding and section. After the section were treated with xylene and alcohol at different concentrations, the samples were subjected to antigen repair with slightly boiling sodium citrate buffer for 20min, and then treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15min, 5% BSA was added and blocked at 37°C for 60min. Then, primary antibodies STAR (abclonal, A22166) protein was incubated at 4°C overnight. Then incubate the samples with fluorescent second antibody for 1 h and stain with DAPI for 5min. Finally, an anti-fluorescence quencher was added and observed by fluorescence microscopy (Olympus, Japan).

RNA isolation and sequencing and biological analysis

The RNA of rooster's testis in CN and LET was extracted by Trizol reagent (Beyotime, China). RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 assay kit of the Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, USA). Using purified RNA as template, cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcriptase, and then cDNA fragment library was synthesized by DNA polymerase, the library fragments were purified with AM Pure XP system (Beckman Coulter, USA). The library preparations were sequenced on an Illumina Novaseq platform. Clean data were obtained by removing reads containing adapter, low quality reads from raw data in fastq format. Paired-end clean reads were aligned to the G. Gallus reference genome (GRCg6a) using Hisat2 v2.0.5. Genes with an adjusted P<0.05 found by DESeq2 were assigned as differential expression genes (DEGs). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed by using R (Version4.1.2), and PCA results were visualized using the R package ggplot2. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of DEGs was implemented by the clusterProfiler R package, terms with P<0.05 were considered significantly enriched. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis of DEGs was based on the STRING (V.11.0) database.

Tandem mass tag proteomic analysis

The testis of the LET and CN roosters were milled to powder in a mortar with liquid nitrogen, and then mixed with lysis buffer (8 M urea, 1% protease inhibitor) in a glass homogenizer. Testis tissues were homogenized and incubated on ice and then centrifuged at 12000×g and 4°C for 10min. The total protein concentration was quantified by a BCA protein assay kit. Sample proteins were enzymatically digested with trypsin overnight. The peptides hydrolyzed by trypsin were desalted with strata x C18 (phenomenex) and vacuum freeze-dried. Dissolve and label the peptide according to the operation instructions of Tandem Mass Tag (TMT) kit. The peptide segments were graded by high pH reversed-phase HPLC, and the chromatographic column was Agilent 300extend C18. Phase a formic acid aqueous solution) was dissolved by liquid chromatography and separated by easy NLC 1200 ultra-high performance liquid system. Mobile phase A is an aqueous solution containing 0.1% formic acid and 2% acetonitrile; Mobile phase B is an aqueous solution containing 0.1% formic acid and 90% acetonitrile. After separation by ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system, the peptide was implanted into NSI ion source for ionization, and then analyzed by Thermos Scientific TMQ active hf-x mass spectrometry. The parent ions and secondary fragments of the peptide were detected and analyzed by high-resolution orbitrap. The raw data were used to analyze the labeled quantification of proteins by Maxquant (1.6.15.0) against the G. Gallus reference proteome (Blast/Gallus/Gallus/9031/PR/20200727.fasta). Proteins with an adjusted P<0.05 found by DESeq2 were assigned as differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). PCA was performed by using R (Version4.1.2), and PCA results were visualized using the R package ggplot2. DEPs were annotated using GO and KEGG. PPI network analysis was performed through the STRING (V.11.0) database, and Cytoscape (V.3.8.1) software created by Cytoscape consortium was adopted to present the network.

Real-time quantitative PCR validation

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was carried out in ABI Quant Studio system (Life Techndogies, USA). SYBR Green fluorescent dye was used as the molecular probe of RT-qPCR reaction, and the cDNA of testis in CN and LET was used as the template. Three biological replicates and four technical replicates were performed for each gene quantitative assay. RT-qPCR gene primers are listed in Table S1. The GAPDH were used as reference genes. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^-(∆∆Ct) method.

Statistical analysis

One-Way ANOVA and Student's t-test was performed by SPSS 22.0 software to determine the differences among groups. P<0.05 was considered as statistically significant. Data were expressed as means ± SD, and diagrams and graphs were plotted using the GraphPad Prism8.

Results

Effects of letrozole on semen quality of aged roosters

The semen quality was evaluated before (0 D) and after (42 D) letrozole treatment (Table 1). The results showed that the semen volume and sperm motility of LET were significantly higher than pre-CN, pre-LET and CN (P<0.05, Fig. 1A and B). The sperm density of LET was significantly higher than that of CN (P<0.05, Fig. 1C). There was no significant difference between the CN, pre-CN and pre-LET. The above results indicated that letrozole could increase semen volume and sperm density, and improve sperm motility.

Effects of letrozole on sperm MMP and ROS of aged roosters

The production of ROS and the reduction of MMP are considered to be one of the factors affecting sperm motility. In this study, DCFH-DA and JC-1 fluorescent probes were used to detect sperm ROS and MMP. The results indicated that LET treatment significantly increased sperm MMP (P<0.01, Fig. 2A-C) and reduced the production of ROS (P<0.01, Fig. 2D-F).

Effects of letrozole on serum hormone levels in aged roosters

The serum hormone levels were evaluated before (0 D) and after (42 D) letrozole treatment. The results showed that the E2 levels of LET was significantly lower than that of CN (P<0.05, Fig. 3A). The T and GnRH levels of LET were significantly higher than those of pre-CN, pre-LET and CN (P<0.05, Fig. 3B and C), There were no significant differences in the FSH and LH levels in these group (Fig. 3D and E). The above results indicated that letrozole could inhibit the conversion of T to E2, promote the synthesis of GnRH, and then increase the T level in aged roosters.

Letrozole treatment increased testicular weight and improved testicular structure

Anatomically, the present study found that the size and weight of testis in LET were higher than those in CN (Fig. 4A and B). Histologically, the HE staining results showed the germinal epithelium in the testicular seminiferous tubules of LET has many cell layers, the germinal cells of each level were arranged more regularly, and mature germinal cells can be seen in the lumen, while the germinal epithelium in the CN has a lot of vacuoles, germinal cells were disordered and exfoliated in the lumen (Fig. 4C and D). The results of Image J measurements showed that the epithelium thickness of LET was significantly higher than that of CN, and the seminiferous tubules diameter was significantly smaller than that of CN (Fig. 4E and F).

Identification and functional annotation of differentially expressed genes

To investigate the effects of letrozole treatment on semen quality in aged roosters, transcriptomic sequencing was performed on testis. BioProject accession number PRJNA1120582. An average of 46002384 raw reads were obtained, after quality control with the filtering out of low-quality reads and trimming of adapter sequences, approximately 45349819 clean reads were obtained for each sample. The error rate of all samples was lower than 0.03%, and the average Q20, Q30, and GC content of the samples is 96.71%, 91.67%, and 49.69%.

PCA showed large variations in testis transcript abundance between CN and LET (Fig. 5A). By analyzing RNA-seq data, a total of 189 DEGs were identified from testis between CN and LET, 88 DEGs were up-regulated and 101 DEGs were down-regulated. Heatmap of DEGs were plotted in Fig. 5B. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs (Fig. 6A and B). The most significantly enriched GO terms were positive regulation of hormone secretion, hormone transport, and epidermal cell differentiation in biological processes; hormone binding, cholesterol transfer activity, and steroid hydroxylase activity in molecular functions; stereocilium bundle, and secretory granule lumen in cellular components. The genes involved in hormone synthesis and secretion, as well as cell proliferation and differentiation, include STAR, SCARA5, SULT1E1, EHF and GHRHR. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the DEGs were mainly enriched in steroid hormone biosynthesis, calcium signalling pathway, MAPK signalling pathway and wnt signalling pathway. The results showed that letrozole treatment was involved in the regulation of steroid hormone biosynthesis, and may mediate cell differentiation and proliferation in testis.

Identification and functional annotation of differentially expressed proteins

In order to study the effect of letrozole treatment on semen quality in aged roosters, proteomic sequencing was performed on testis, dataset identifier PXD052990. PCA analysis showed that CN and LET showed better clustering (Fig. 5C). By analyzing proteomic data, a total of 64 DEPs were identified from testis between CN and LET, 45 DEPs were up-regulated and 19 DEPs were down-regulated. Heatmap of DEPs were plotted in Fig. 5D. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on DEPs (Fig. 6C and D). The most significantly enriched GO terms were steroid biosynthetic process. C21-steroid hormone metabolic process, and regulation of ATPase activity in biological processes; steroid dehydrogenase activity, oxidoreductase activity, and thyroid hormone receptor binding in molecular functions; myosin complex, and intermediate filament in cellular components. The genes involved in hormone synthesis and secretion, as well as cell oxidoreductase activity, include STAR, CYP17A1, HSD-3β, DHCR24, SDHA, PLIN2 and HNRNPA1. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the DEPs were mainly enriched in steroid biosynthesis, calcium signalling pathway, oxidative phosphorylation and PI3K-AKT signalling pathway.

PPI Analysis and validation of differential genes and proteins

In order to understand the interaction between proteins, we analyzed the PPI network of DEGs and DEPs. The transcriptome and proteome PPI network were consisted of 37 DEGs and 39 DEPs (Fig. 7A and B), respectively. Annotation of transcriptome DEGs PPI network node genes combined with GO and KEGG enrichment analysis results showed that EHF was enriched in epithelial cell proliferation, cell differentiation, and positive regulation of cell population proliferation; TARP was enriched in neuromodulation and receptor transport; SCARA5 and MELTF were enriched in iron ion transmembrane transport and cellular iron ion homeostasis. There is an interaction between SCARA5 and SULT1E1. SULT1E1 protein is a sulfotransferase that inactivates these hormones by catalyzing the sulfate binding of estradiol and estrone. These results suggest that LET may promote the expression and interaction of SCARA5 and SULT1E1, thereby reducing estrogen levels, and the transmembrane transport of iron ions may play a key role in this process. Annotation of proteome DEPs PPI network node genes combined with GO and KEGG enrichment analysis results showed that STAR, CYP17A1, NSDHL and DHCR24 were enriched in steroid hormone synthesis and steroid metabolic process; ACSBG2, PLIN2 and SDHA were enriched in lipid metabolic process, oxidation-reduction process and electron transfer activity. HNRNPA1 and MYL3 were enriched in germ-line stem cell population maintenance, gonad development and myosin II complex, respectively. The results suggest that LET may be involved in mediating the synthesis and metabolism of steroid hormones and lipids, and play an important role in maintaining cell proliferation and differentiation.

Discussion

With the increase of age, the reproductive performance of aged roosters decreased significantly, and the testicles gradually degenerated, leading to the degeneration of spermatogenic epithelium cells and spermatogenesis block (Eroschenko et al., 1977; Lagares et al., 2016). The decrease of T level and the increase of E2 level are one of the main factors contributing to the decrease of semen quality in aged roosters (Fragoso et al., 2012). Our results indicated that letrozole can reduce E2 level, increase GnRH and T level, and improve mitochondrial activity and sperm motility in aged roosters. Transcriptomic and proteomic sequencing results showed that the DEGs and DEPs were significantly enriched in steroid hormone biosynthesis and steroid metabolism pathways, and the genes STAR, CYP17A1, NSDHL, DHCR24 and SULT1E1 related to androgen biosynthesis and estrogen inactivation were significantly up-regulated. STAR is a key regulatory gene in the first step of steroidogenesis pathway, which ensures the transport of cholesterol from the outer membrane to the inner membrane of mitochondria (Clewell et al., 2010). LH receptor mediated steroidogenic pathway regulates the expression and activation of STAR in Leydig cells (Zeng et al., 2018). CYP17A1 catalyzes the production of pregnenolone from mitochondrial cholesterol, which is converted to T by metabolic reactions under the influence of HSD-3β and HSD-17β (Sinclair et al., 2015). This result effectively explains how letrozole can reduce estrogen levels and increase androgen levels. The testis of aged roosters fed with letrozole have lower aromatase activity and higher T concentrations. Various parameters such as sperm concentration, total motility, progressive motility, MDA, membrane integrity, and mitochondrial activity improved with letrozole treatment (Bazyar et al., 2019). The results are consistent with our experimental results.

The hypothalamic-pituitary-testis axis is an important axis that affects the production of GnRH, LH, FSH and T in roosters, and affects their reproductive performance (Liu et al., 2013). GnRH is a hormone that regulates the secretion of LH and FSH. FSH and LH can function in regulating spermatogenesis by affecting the proliferation and differentiation of Sertoli cells and the activation of stromal cells (Huhtaniemi et al., 1999; Layman, 2000). In this study, the concentrations of GnRH and T were significantly increased after treatment with letrozole, while FSH and LH had no significant changes. After T was transformed into E2, binding to hypothalamic receptor, where it reduced the pulse frequency of GnRH and binds to pituitary receptor, where it reduced the response to GnRH (Hayes et al., 2000). Previous studies were consistent with the results of this study (Bazyar et al., 2019; Feng et al., 2015; Gregoriou et al., 2012). Other studies have reported that AI can increase the concentrations of FSH and LH in serum (Ali et al., 2017; Cavallini et al., 2013; Rambhatla et al., 2016; T'Sjoen et al., 2005), which may be related to the age and sampling time of rooster, and the amplitude and frequency of LH and FSH change at different times of the day (Wildt et al., 1981). At present, the specific reasons are not clear, and further research is needed.

Testicular aged can thin the spermatogenic epithelial cell layer, resulting in reduced semen volume (Fragoso et al., 2012). In this study, the testicular weight and semen volume of LET were significantly higher than those of CN. HE staining showed that there were more layers and thicker thickness of spermatogenic epithelial cells in LET. Transcriptomic and proteomic sequencing results indicated that the DEGs and DEPs were significantly enriched in cell proliferation, cell differentiation and germ-line stem cell population maintenance, and the genes EHF and HNRNPA1 were significantly up-regulated in LET. EHF is E26 transformation-specific TF family homologous factor and plays an important role in the differentiation and proliferation of epithelial cells (Sizemore et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2020). EHF over-expression can directly up-regulate the expression of transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1) at the transcriptional level, activate the canonical TGF-β signalling pathway, and then promote cell proliferation and migration (Wang et al., 2020). HNRNPA1 protein is an important member of the heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein family, which is a multifunctional RNA binding protein family. HNRNPA1 may promote the development and functional stability of cells by enhancing the stability of Foxp3 (Liu et al., 2021). The results suggested that letrozole can promote testicular development, increase testicular weight and improve semen quality by up-regulating the expression of genes related to cell proliferation and differentiation. Our results are consistent with those of rats (Hmood Jassim A et al., 2022), chicken (Bazyar et al., 2019) and bucks (Rezaei et al., 2022).

Mitochondria are the power source of cells and participate in a variety of cell functions, such as calcium homeostasis (Contreras et al., 2010; Szymański et al., 2017), lipid homeostasis (Anastasia et al., 2021; Labbé et al., 2014), and ATP production through succinate dehydrogenase (SDHA) (Nunnari and Suomalainen, 2012; Wang et al., 2022). ROS is a by-product of ATP production by mitochondria (Aitken, 2020; Foutouhi and Meyers, 2022). Excessive ROS levels reduce cell survival and mitochondrial ATP production by affecting mitochondrial activity (Amaral et al., 2013; Drevet et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2017). It is generally believed that ROS production is related to low sperm function and fertility (Koppers et al., 2008; Roca et al., 2013). With the increase of ROS level in sperm, mitochondrial activity and ATP production were impaired (Zhu et al., 2019). In this study, we found that letrozole significantly reduced sperm ROS levels and enhanced sperm mitochondrial activity. Proteomic sequencing showed that the DEPs PLIN2 and SDHA were enriched in lipid metabolic process, oxidation-reduction process and electron transfer activity. The results showed that letrozole could increase the expression of PLIN2 and SDHA, increase the synthesis of ATP, reduce the level of ROS, enhance the activity of mitochondria, and then improve the semen quality of aged roosters.

Conclusion

In summary, our results indicate that letrozole can up-regulate the expression of genes related to steroid hormone synthesis, cell differentiation and proliferation, electron transfer activity, and enhance mitochondrial activity, increase testicular weight, and ultimately improve the semen quality of aged roosters. Furthermore, 8 genes including STAR, CYP17A1, NSDHL, SULT1E1, EHF, NRNPA1, PLIN2 and SDHA were identified as key genes for letrozole to regulate semen quality in aged roosters. Our findings are further elucidated the molecular mechanism of letrozole in improving the reproductive ability of aged roosters, and lay the theory for subsequent research.

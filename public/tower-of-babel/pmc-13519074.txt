Predictive biomarkers for cetuximab-based rechallenge therapy in metastatic colorectal cancer: a pooled analysis of the CAVE and CAVE-2 GOIM studies

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) therapy is a therapeutic option in patients with molecularly selected metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). However, the identification of predictive factors represents an unmet need.Patients and methodsWe conducted a pooled analysis of individual patient data from the CAVE-GOIM (NCT04561336) and CAVE-2 GOIM (NCT05291156) studies to investigate the impact of several clinical variables on rechallenge with cetuximab with and without avelumab in circulating tumor DNA RAS/BRAF/EGFR-extracellular domain wild-type mCRC.ResultsOverall, 180 patients met the eligibility criteria: 136 received cetuximab–avelumab and 44 cetuximab. In patients receiving cetuximab monotherapy, median progression-free survival (mPFS) was 4.80 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 3.90-5.90] and median overall survival (mOS) 12.9 months (95% CI 11.1-not evaluable). In univariable analysis, a shorter progression-free survival was observed in patients with liver involvement [hazard ratio (HR) 2.19, 95% CI 1.51-3.20, P < 0.001] and anti-EGFR-free interval ≤16 months (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.13-2.31, P < 0.008). Both variables retained statistical significance in multivariable analysis. In univariable analysis, lower overall survival was observed among patients treated with cetuximab–avelumab with >3 metastatic sites (HR 1.79, 95% CI 1.16-2.77, P < 0.009) and liver (HR 1.82, 95% CI 1.15-2.88, P < 0.011) and peritoneal metastases (HR 1.9, 95% CI 1.23-2.93, P < 0.004). Introduction

Liquid biopsy (LBx)-guided anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) rechallenge strategies are gaining ground as potential therapeutic options in refractory metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC).1, 2, 3 The biological rationale for this strategy stems from the emergence of resistant tumor clones that drive disease progression after an initial response to anti-EGFR-based therapy.4 In the absence of anti-EGFR pressure, these resistant clones may decay over time, potentially restoring sensitivity to EGFR inhibition.

Evidence derived from phase II trials suggests that cetuximab or panitumumab rechallenge exerts antitumor activity with an objective response rate (ORR) of ∼10%-30%, progression-free survival (PFS) of 4-6 months and overall survival (OS) of 11-16 months.5, 6, 7 Based on these data, the European Society for Medical Oncology guidelines recommend the use of anti-EGFR retreatment for patients who retain a RAS/BRAF/EGFR-extracellular domain (ECD) wild-type (wt) status.8

To date, the absence of resistance-associated alterations detected by LBx is the only established biomarker used for patient selection.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Different studies have evaluated potential variables involved in anti-EGFR rechallenge therapies including anti-EGFR-free interval, sex, type of metastatic sites, and skin toxicity.5,6,9, 10, 11, 12 However, available results are derived from retrospective analyses conducted in small subgroups with contrasting findings.

In the phase II, single-arm CAVE-GOIM study, cetuximab–avelumab combination therapy showed promising activity as retreatment therapy in patients with chemorefractory RAS/BRAF-wt microsatellite stable (MSS) tumors.13,14 Following these findings, we conducted the CAVE-2 GOIM study, comparing cetuximab–avelumab with cetuximab monotherapy as a rechallenge strategy in patients with RAS/BRAF-wt circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), MSS mCRC.15,16 The combined regimen showed a numerically but not significantly improvement in ORR, PFS, and OS compared with cetuximab monotherapy.17

To better understand potential factors involved in clinical benefit (PFS and OS) from cetuximab with or without avelumab rechallenge therapy in this scenario, we conducted an individual patient data (IPD) pooled analysis of patients with molecularly selected mCRC enrolled in the CAVE-GOIM and CAVE-2 GOIM studies.

Patients and methods

Study population

CAVE-GOIM (NCT04561336) was a single-arm, academic, multicenter phase II study that investigated cetuximab–avelumab rechallenge in 77 patients with chemorefractory RAS/BRAF-wt mCRC.13,14 At baseline, plasma samples from 67 cases were available for LBx analysis using an RT–PCR assay (Idylla™ platform; Biocartis, Mechelen, Belgium).

CAVE-2 GOIM (NCT05291156) was a randomized, academic, multicenter phase II study comparing cetuximab–avelumab with single-agent cetuximab as rechallenge therapy in 156 patients with pretreated RAS/BRAF-wt ctDNA, MSS mCRC.15,17 At baseline, LBx evaluation was carried out using the FoundationOne Liquid Companion Diagnostic (F1L CDx, Boston, MA).16

The main inclusion criteria were similar in the two studies: patients should have received an anti-EGFR-based first-line treatment, should have obtained complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), should have experienced progressive disease (PD), and thereafter have received at least one subsequent anti-EGFR drug-free line of therapy with an anti-EGFR drug-free interval of at least 4 months.

In the CAVE-GOIM study, patients received cetuximab (administered intravenously at 400 mg/m2 as a loading dose, followed by 250 mg/m2 weekly) and avelumab (10 mg/kg every 2 weeks) until disease progression or unacceptable toxic effects. In the CAVE-2 GOIM trial, patients were randomly assigned (2 : 1) to receive cetuximab (administered intravenously at 400 mg/m2 as a loading dose, followed by 250 mg/m2 weekly) and avelumab (at a flat dose of 800 mg, once every 2 weeks) or cetuximab (administered intravenously at 400 mg/m2 as a loading dose and subsequently at 250 mg/m2 weekly).

Flat dosing of avelumab was predicted to provide similar exposure to weight-based dosing, with slightly lower variability and similar benefit.18 Thus, the clinical activity of avelumab in the two studies is comparable. To avoid potential confounding factors involved in response to immunotherapy, only patients with known MSS tumors were included. Moreover, due to the negative prognostic/predictive value of clonal or subclonal RAS/BRAF/EGFR-ECD alterations, cases harboring those pathogenic variants (PVs) were excluded.

The CAVE-GOIM and CAVE-2 GOIM trials were conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonisation and Good Clinical Practice Guidelines and were approved by the Ethics Committee of the centers involved in the study. All patients provided written informed consent to participate to the trials.

Study endpoints

The main objective of the study is to investigate the impact of multiple variables on PFS and OS in a molecularly selected population treated with cetuximab–avelumab or single-agent cetuximab. The following variables were investigated: age (<65, ≥65 years); sex (male, female); Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (0, 1); primary tumor sidedness (left, right); number of metastatic sites (<3, ≥3), liver, lung, or peritoneum nodes involvement; and anti-EGFR-free interval (defined as the time from last anti-EGFR administration to rechallenge therapy). Of note, because the median time from last anti-EGFR administration was 16 months, we used this threshold (≤16 and >16 months). Considering the exploratory nature of the analysis and the limited ORR in both the CAVE-GOIM and CAVE-2 GOIM studies, no formal evaluation of the correlation between the abovementioned clinical variables and ORR was carried out.

PFS was defined as the time from the initiation of study treatment to PD or death. OS was determined as the time from the beginning of the experimental therapy to death. In the absence of events, PFS and OS were censored at the time of last follow-up. ORR was defined as the percentage of patients who experienced a CR or a PR according to RECIST19 version 1.1 criteria. The disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the percentage of patients who experienced CR, PR, or stable disease as their best response.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are reported as medians and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables are summarized as frequency counts and proportions.

Median progression-free survival (mPFS) and median overall survival (mOS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method with confidence intervals (CIs) calculated with the Brookmeyer and Crowley method and compared between groups using the log-rank statistic. Hazard ratios (HRs) were calculated using the Cox proportional model. Median follow-up was calculated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Inferential statistical tests were carried out using a two-tailed alpha value of 0.05. Variables associated with PFS and OS (P < 0.05) were included in a multivariable Cox regression model. Statistical analyses were conducted using Jamovi Software version 2.7.6 (Sidney, Australia; https://www.jamovi.org/about.html).

After a median follow-up of 23 months (IQR 16.3-28.8 months), 170 of 180 (94.4%) patients experienced PD and 112 of 180 (62.2%) died. In the overall population, mPFS was 4.85 months (95% CI 4.30-5.70) and OS was 15.1 months (95% CI 12.9-18.8) (Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100398). The ORR and DCR were 10.6% and 68.9%, respectively (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100398). In patients treated with cetuximab–avelumab, mPFS was 5.00 months (95% CI 4.30-5.90) and mOS was 15.7 months (95% CI 13.0-19.9), with an ORR of 11% and DCR of 71.3% (Supplementary Figure S3 and Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100398). In patients treated with cetuximab monotherapy, mPFS was 4.80 months (95% CI 3.90-5.90), mOS was 12.9 months [95% CI 11.1-not evaluable (NE)], ORR was 9.1% and DCR was 61.4% (Supplementary Figure S4 and Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100398).

Subsequently, we conducted an exploratory analysis to investigate the association of several clinical variables with PFS and OS in patients treated with cetuximab–avelumab or single-agent cetuximab. For patients who received cetuximab–avelumab, in univariable analysis, a shorter PFS was observed in cases with liver involvement (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.51-3.20, P < 0.001) and an anti-EGFR-free interval ≤16 months (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.13-2.31, P < 0.008) (Table 2). Both variables retained statistical significance in multivariable analysis (Table 2). The mPFS of patients with liver compared with no liver metastases was 4.29 months (95% CI 3.65-5.29) compared with 6.75 months (95% CI 5.55-8.57), respectively (HR 2.19, 95% CI 1.51-3.20, P < 0.001) (Figure 1A). The mPFS was 4.3 months (95% CI 3.6-5.4) compared with 6.0 months (95% CI 5.29-8.0), respectively, in patients with an anti-EGFR-free interval ≤16 months compared with >16 months (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.13-2.31, P < 0.008) (Figure 1B). Comparable ORRs and DCRs were observed within the different subgroups (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100398).

Discussion

The IPD pooled analysis of the CAVE-GOIM and CAVE-2 GOIM studies comprises the largest dataset to date of cetuximab-based rechallenge therapy in mCRC and confirms the role of this treatment strategy in patients with molecularly selected tumors. LBx-guided anti-EGFR rechallenge therapy led to PFS and OS of ∼5 and ∼15 months, respectively, that appear to be favorable compared with available treatment options.6,7,20, 21, 22 Of note, cetuximab monotherapy exhibited clinical activity in patients with tumors without RAS/BRAF/EGFR-ECD-wt ctDNA, regardless of the site of recurrence, clinical factors, and the time from last anti-EGFR administration. In a previous analysis of the CAVE-2 GOIM trial, we explored the role of a deeper molecular stratification (‘molecular hyperselection’) on response to anti-EGFR therapy.17 Of note, patients treated with cetuximab monotherapy whose tumors did not carry anti-EGFR resistance PVs (namely ‘negative hyperselected’) showed a clinical and significant improvement in PFS and OS. Similar findings were observed in the CITRIC and PURSUIT trials.23,24 In fact, patients treated with irinotecan-based anti-EGFR retreatment and with ‘negative hyperselected’ tumors showed improved outcomes.

Following these findings, the randomized phase II ROMANCE-GOIM trial (NCT07381764) is currently comparing cetuximab–irinotecan with trifluridine–tipiracil–bevacizumab as rechallenge therapy in patients with ‘negative hyperselected’ mCRC.

In the present analysis, the absence of liver metastases and a longer anti-EGFR-free interval represent potential biomarkers for the treatment efficacy of cetuximab–avelumab. Emerging evidence suggests that liver metastases could create an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, thus limiting response to immunotherapy in refractory MSS mCRC.25, 26, 27, 28 Recently, Pakvisal et al.25 reported the results of a retrospective analysis of a cohort of 132 patients with MSS mCRC treated with immunotherapy. Patients without liver involvement at the beginning of immunotherapy showed higher clinical benefit rates, as well as longer PFS and OS. In a phase Ib-II study26 investigating botensilimab–balstilimab in MSS mCRC, in the subgroup of patients without active liver metastases, the ORR was 22%, DCR was 73%, mPFS was 4.1 months, and mOS was 20.9 months. Of note, within the limitations of cross-trial comparison, cetuximab–avelumab exhibited a similar DCR, PFS, and OS with a slightly lower ORR. Because no differential efficacy according to liver involvement was observed in patients who received cetuximab monotherapy, the presence of liver metastases might represent a potential predictive biomarker for cetuximab–avelumab combination therapy. Further translational analyses are required to confirm this hypothesis and identify potential biomarkers of response.

The results of the randomized phase III STELLAR-303 trial investigating the efficacy of zanzalitinib–atezolizumab will provide further evidence on the role of immunotherapy in refractory MSS mCRC without liver metastases and confirm if liver involvement should be used as a criterion of selection for future studies investigating immunotherapy in refractory MSS mCRC.29

Another factor associated with longer PFS for the combinatory regimen was the time from last anti-EGFR administration. Theoretically, a longer time from previous cetuximab–panitumumab therapy might allow the clearance of mechanism(s) of resistance other than genomic alteration. In an exploratory analysis of the PARERE trial, the subgroup of patients with an anti-EGFR-free interval <6 months showed worse outcomes.6 In the OrigAMI-1 study, the time to last anti-EGFR administration >8.8 months was associated with longer PFS and OS in patients treated with amivantamab. In our IPD pooled analysis, only a limited subset of patients had an anti-EGFR-free interval <6 months (five cases) or 8.8 months (29 cases); therefore, we used the median value as the threshold in the patient population. Of note, in the PARERE trial, the median anti-EGFR-free interval was 15 months, suggesting external consistence with the findings of the present IPD pooled analysis. Moreover, the role of the length of the anti-EGFR-free interval on treatment response is still debated. In the CHRONOS trial, no correlation between the time from previous anti-EGFR therapy and the probability of response was observed.9 In a retrospective analysis including 86 patients with refractory mCRC, a longer anti-EGFR-free interval was correlated with increased ORR but not with longer PFS or OS.30 Therefore, further studies are required to confirm the potential role of this clinical variable and define the optimal cut-off time.

The present study has limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. Firstly, the exploratory nature of this investigation represents an intrinsic limitation. Secondly, in the CAVE-GOIM study, LBx analyses were retrospectively assessed using a RT–PCR assay that has a lower sensitivity compared with F1L CDx for the detection of RAS/BRAF/EGFR-ECD PVs, thus potentially leading to false-negative cases. Moreover, the Biocartis Idylla™ RT–PCR tests do not allow the identification of other PVs that are potentially involved in anti-EGFR resistance. This difference in detection performance may have introduced systematic bias in patient selection across the two cohorts, potentially leading to different rates of false-negative ctDNA results.

Thirdly, the limited number of patients in the cetuximab monotherapy group might have introduced potential bias and underestimated the impact of potential prognostic or predictive factors. It is well known that peritoneal metastasis is a negative prognostic factor associated with shorter survival.31 In this regard, in the subgroup of patients treated with cetuximab–avelumab, the presence of peritoneal metastases was associated with worse OS in both univariable and multivariable analyses, whereas no difference was observed in patients receiving single-agent cetuximab. However, the very small number of patients included in this subgroup might have influenced these findings.

Conclusion

The current analysis showed that the single-agent cetuximab rechallenge was active irrespective of investigated factors in molecularly selected tumors. For the cetuximab–avelumab combination, the absence of liver involvement was associated with longer PFS and OS, suggesting that this parameter could be a predictive biomarker of efficacy and should be considered as a criterion for patient selection in future studies evaluating this regimen. Finally, further investigations are required to confirm the optimal cut-off and the role of anti-EGFR-free interval as a predictive biomarker for rechallenge strategies.

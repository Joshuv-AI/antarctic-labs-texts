Prediction value of respiratory permutation entropy for hemodynamic instability events in critically ill patients with sepsis
Authors: Jingjing Zhang, Xi Xu, Xuting Jin, Ruohan Li, Jiamei Li, Yanli Hou, Xiaochuang Wang, Gang Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe study aimed to investigate the association between features extracted from time-series of vital signs and hemodynamic instabilities in critically ill patients with sepsis.MethodsThe present study included a total of 9,248 sepsis patients from the eICU Collaborative Research Database (Version 2.0). The patients were admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) for their initial hospitalization, had a minimum duration of ICU stay exceeding 6 h, and were aged between 15 and 80 years. Respiration permutation entropy (RPE), a time series feature extracted by tsfresh from 6-hour continuous vital signs monitoring records (heart rate, respiratory rate, and peripheral oxygen saturation) preceding hemodynamic instability events was analysed. The primary outcomes were the occurrence of the first hemodynamic instability event, ICU mortality, and hospital mortality. The prognostic value of respiration permutation entropy was evaluated by multivariable logistic regression models. Model performance was assessed by calculating the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsA total of 2200 patients (23.8%) experienced an episode of hemodynamic instability. Multivariable logistic models demonstrated that a significant association between RPE and an increased incidence of hemodynamic instability events in critically ill patients with sepsis with duration of ICU stay exceeding 6 h (odds ratio [OR] = 1.224, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.137–1.318). Furthermore, RPE was found to be associated with higher ICU mortality (OR = 1.311, 95% CI = 1.182–1.453) and hospital mortality (OR = 1.234, 95% CI = 1.129–1.349), particularly in male patients, with longer length of stay in ICU, higher sequential organ failure assessment scores, and cirrhosis, respectively. Importantly, this model achieved area under the curve (AUC) of 0.811 (95%CI = 0.801–0.822) for hemodynamic instability events, 0.761 (95%CI = 0.749–0.773) for hospital mortality and 0.779 (95%CI = 0.767–0.7990) for ICU mortality, respectively, in the ROC curve analyses.ConclusionsElevated respiratory permutation entropy is associated with an increased incidence of hemodynamic instability events, ICU mortality, and in-hospital mortality, potentially guiding timely and effective resuscitation in sepsis patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12436-7.Keywords: Respiratory permutation entropy, Hemodynamic instability events, Mortality, Sepsis

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening multiorgan dysfunction syndrome caused by an aberrant host immune response to infection [1, 2]. Sepsis is often accompanied by the advent of hemodynamic instability, and septic shock can occur in severe cases, resulting in a significant increase in the risk of death [2, 3]. Therefore, in the management of sepsis, monitoring and timely intervention in the hemodynamic status of patients are crucial [4]. Traditional hemodynamic monitoring methods typically rely on analyzing relatively static parameters, such as pulmonary artery catheterization, thermodilution, and cardiac ultrasound imaging [5], making it difficult to promptly capture the evolving changes in the patient’s hemodynamic condition [6]. In addition, pulse wave analysis (PWA) has emerged as a promising alternative to more conventional static measurements, revealing untapped potential in continuous recording of vital signs [7].

In recent years, the advancement of machine learning technology has made its application in the medical field increasingly widespread [8–10], especially in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU), where patients’ conditions are critical and change rapidly. This constant monitoring of vital signs constitute a huge time series database. By extracting these medical data, machine learning algorithms can discover the complex patterns and relationships behind them, maybe achieving accurate predictions of the disease progression and prognosis [11, 12]. In the present study, based on machine learning algorithms, we adopted an open-source time series data processing module—Time Series Feature extraction based on scalable hypothesis tests (tsfresh)—to extract features from heart rate, oxygen saturation, and respiratory frequency data in eICU-CRD database of sepsis patients. Based on the importance of features, respiratory rate’s time series arrangement entropy, i.e., respiratory permutation entropy (RPE) was selected as a potential indicator for hemodynamic conditions. RPE quantifies the complexity of respiratory signals, and a higher RPE generally indicates a more intricate and irregular breathing pattern [13]. This study aims to explore the relationship between RPE and the occurrence of hemodynamic instability events in sepsis patients.

Method

Database source

The data for this retrospective observational study were extracted from the eICU-CRD, a database developed by the Laboratory for Computational Physiology at Massachusetts Institute of Technology (Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA). The eICU-CRD is an open-access, multicenter critical care dataset that provides comprehensive information on the hospitalizations of 139,367 patients from 20,0859 ICU admissions in 335 distinct ICUs throughout the continental United States during the period spanning from 2014 to 2015 [14]. It encompasses demographic details as well as vital signs monitoring records and diagnostic and treatment information. Vital signs monitoring data in the eICU-CRD were captured through bedside devices that measured heart rate, blood saturation levels, and respiratory rate. The data has been strictly anonymized to ensure patient privacy and does not require informed consent.

Research object and outcomes

The study included patients from eICU-CRD who met the following criteria: (1) The first ICU admission of patients with single hospital admission; (2) Sepsis diagnosis upon ICU admission [15]; (3) Duration of ICU stay exceeding 6 h; (4) Age ranging from 15 to 80 years old; (5) No records of cardiac surgery, cardiopulmonary bypass support, or cardiac arrest were documented during hospitalization (Fig. 1).

Flow diagram of participant selection. Records for 22,911 patients meeting the inclusion criteria were extracted from the eICU-CRD database. Among 8,977 patients who experienced hemodynamic instability events during their ICU stay, 2,200 consecutive respiratory rate records were included. Among patients without such events, 7,048 records were selected

The diagnostic criteria for sepsis: the eICU-CRD database strictly adheres to the internationally accepted Sepsis-3.0 standard for the definition of sepsis, which includes the following two points: (1) There is a definite or suspected infection (confirmed through microbiological culture, imaging, or clinical symptoms); (2) There is organ dysfunction related to the infection, manifested as an increase of ≥ 2 points in the SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score compared to the baseline [2].

The primary outcome assessed in our study was the occurrence of hemodynamic instability events, which were defined at least one of the following criteria: (1) invasive mean arterial pressure below 65mmHg within 5 min; (2) initiation of hemodynamic intervention through the administration of vasoactive drugs or positive inotropic drugs such as dopamine, epinephrine, norepinephrine, phenylephrine, vasopressin, and liquid resuscitation. Then, the hemodynamic instability events were selected from patients who also met the following criteria: (1) The unstable events occurred at least 6 h after admission to the ICU; (2) The patients had consecutive respiratory rate monitoring data for at least 6 h prior to the unstable event. A record of respiratory rate within 6 h before the first unstable event (monitored every 5 min) was taken, defined as positive samples. Simultaneously, a negative control group was identified consisting of patients who never experienced an unstable event, had respiratory rate monitoring data for a consecutive 6-hour period, and did not die during hospitalization. Random extraction of 6-hour respiratory rate monitoring records (every 5 min) served as negative controls for patients without any unstable events. Finally, a total of 2,200 patients met the criteria for hemodynamic stability events with sepsis, while 7,048 patients remained free from any hemodynamic instability events (Fig. 1).

The secondary outcomes included ICU mortality, defined as death prior to ICU discharge for any reason, and hospital mortality, defined as death prior to hospital discharge for any reason. ICU mortality is included in hospital mortality.

Data extraction

The clinical data were extracted from eICU-CRD using PostgreSQL 13.0 including: (1) Records of the first day of ICU admission: age, sex, ethnicity, physiological characteristics and laboratory examinations, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) IV score, and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score; (2) Major clinical comorbidities concluding hypertension, diabetes, cirrhosis, respiratory failure, heart failure, renal failure, liver failure, cancer, AIDS, trauma and burn, were classified according to the International Classification of Diseases, 9th Edition (ICD-9); (3) Drug usage and special treatments: administration of anti-infective drugs, analgesics, sedatives, anticoagulants, mechanical ventilation (non-invasive and invasive), and kidney replacement therapy.

RPE feature extraction

PostgreSQL 13.0 was utilized for storing and extracting patient information throughout their ICU stay. Tsfresh module in Python was employed to extracted the variations in the time-series of vital signs. The variation features were input into machine learning models to predict hemodynamic instability events. The XGBoost model showed the best performance and RPE was recognized as the feature with the highest importance in the model. The specific extraction method could be found in the Supplementary material.

Statistical analysis

Three machine learning algorithms, namely Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), and Deep Neural Network (DNN), were employed to build models on the training set. A 10-fold cross-validation model training was conducted, and the performance of the three models was compared in the test set. The model establishment and training were carried out using R language (version 4.1.3) software. After determining the optimal algorithm, we evaluated the possibility scores of the occurrence of sepsis hemodynamic instability events before the event, 1 h before the event, 2 h before the event, and in the negative control group. Compare with the actual outcomes, and draw the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and precision-recall (PR) curves for the events occurring before the event, 1 h before the event, and 2 h before the event, respectively (see Supplementary material).

In the analysis of baseline data, mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median ± interquartile range (IQR) were utilized for the description of continuous variables. The one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), or the Kruskal-Wallis test, was used as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequency with percentage and compared using the Chi-Square test. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression models were conducted to evaluate the association between RPE and hemodynamic instability events, ICU mortality, and hospital mortality. The multivariable models were adjusted for the following factors: (1) age, sex and ethnicity in Model 1; (2) Model 1 plus the first APACHE IV score after ICU admission in Model 2; (3) Model 2 plus clinical complications (including heart failure, respiratory failure, renal failure, hepatic failure, cirrhosis, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cancer, AIDS, trauma and burn) in Model 3; (4) Model 3 plus special treatments (including mechanical ventilation, renal replacement therapy, sedative and analgesic drugs) in Model 4; (5) Model 4 plus the length of stay in ICU in Model 5. In addition, the robustness of the models was evaluated using the area under the AUC curve. The above-mentioned statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS 22.0 software, with a two-sided significance level set at 5%.

Interaction analyses were conducted to examine the relationship between RPE and clinically relevant factors, including age, APACHE IV score, a length of ICU stay, clinical complications, and special treatments. Subsequently, further correlation analysis was performed in subgroups stratified by age (< 65 y vs. ≥65 y), sex (male vs. female), APACHE IV scores (< 54 vs. ≥54), length of ICU stay (≥ 2.27 d vs. <2.27 d), hepatic failure (yes vs. no), mechanical ventilation (yes vs. no), and renal replacement therapy (yes vs. no) based on the results of these interaction analyses and clinical practice. All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS 22.0 software, with a two-sided significance level set at 0.3% (by Bonferroni Correction).

Result

Baseline characteristics

A total of 9,248 patients were included in this study, comprising 4,896 males (52.9%), 7,154 Caucasians (77.4%), and 3,889 patients (42.1%) aged over 65 years old. The APACHE IV score and duration of ICU stay were documented as 54 (40–70) and 2.26 (1.22–4.70) days, respectively. During the ICU hospitalization period, 556 patients (6.0%) received renal replacement therapy and 3,123 patients (33.8%) underwent mechanical ventilation. Finally, out of 3,889 patients diagnosed with sepsis, 2,200 (23.8%) experienced hemodynamic instability events, while 819 (8.9%) passed away in the hospital and 409 (4.4%) died in the ICU. Univariate and multivariate analysis of RPE and different clinical outcomes

The results of univariate logistic regression analysis demonstrated a significant association between the higher RPE and increased ICU mortality (OR = 1.758, 95% CI: 1.611–1.917), the hospital mortality (OR = 1.536, 95% CI: 1.426–1.655), and the occurrence of hemodynamic unstable events (OR = 1.809, 95% CI: 1.696–1.929). After adjusting for potential confounding factors including age, sex, ethnicity, APACHE IV score, clinical complications (respiratory failure, heart failure, renal failure, hepatic failure, cirrhosis, diabetes, hypertension, cancer, AIDS, trauma and burn), special treatments (mechanical ventilation, renal replacement therapy, sedative and analgesic drugs) and the length of ICU stay, the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that the higher RPE remained significantly associated with higher ICU mortality (OR = 1.311, 95% CI: 1.182–1.453), higher hospital mortality (OR = 1.234, 95% CI: 1.129–1.349) as well as an increased incidence of hemodynamic unstable events (OR = 1.224, 95% CI: 1.137–1.318) (Table 2). Additionally, we calculated the area under the AUC curve to evaluate the accuracy of our model for outcomes. These adjusted models achieved area under the curve (AUC) of 0.811 (95% CI: 0.801–0.822) for hemodynamic instability events, 0.761 (95% CI: 0.749–0.773) for hospital mortality and 0.779 (95% CI: 0.767–0.799) for ICU mortality, respectively, in the ROC curve analyses, which indicates robustness of our models (Fig. 2).

Interaction analysis and subgroup analysis

The results of interaction analysis revealed significant interactions between RPE and the following variables, including age, sex, the duration of ICU stay, APACHE IV score, cirrhosis, mechanical ventilation or renal replacement therapy on mortalities (see Supplementary material/ Supplementary Table 1). Hence, based on the results of the interaction analysis, to further determine the effect of RPE on different clinical outcomes, subgroup analysis was performed for above seven variables. The present findings remained consistent across subgroups irrespective of mechanical ventilation and renal replacement, even after adjusting for demographics factors, clinical comorbidities, special treatments, APACHE IV score, and the duration of ICU stay. Besides, the subgroup analysis results indicate significant associations between the higher RPE and increased ICU mortality (OR = 1.340, 95% CI: 1.201–1.494, P < 0.001), hospital mortality (OR = 1.257, 95% CI: 1.143–1.383, P < 0.001), and hemodynamic instability event (OR = 1.271, 95% CI = 1.161–1.390, P < 0.001) in the high APACHE IV score subgroup (APACHE IV score ≥ 54) according to the multivariate regression model. Additionally, among patients with duration of ICU stay ≥ 2.27 d, the increased RPE was found to be associated with higher ICU mortality (OR = 1.262, 95% CI: 1.130–1.408, P < 0.001), hospital mortality (OR = 1.203, 95% CI: 1.096–1.321, P < 0.001), and hemodynamic instability event (OR = 1.281, 95% CI: 1.183–1.387, P < 0.001) after multivariable adjustment (Fig. 3).

Discussion

In this retrospective multicenter cohort study, we observed that RPE, serving as an indicator of respiratory pattern complexity and variability, exhibited a significant association with hemodynamic instability events, ICU mortality, and hospital mortality among sepsis patients. This result was further validated through multivariable logistic analysis, showing that higher RPE remained closely associated with adverse clinical outcomes even after adjusting for various confounding factors. It is worth noting that RPE quantifies the complexity and unpredictability of respiratory signals. The clinical value of RPE primarily relies on its dynamic trend rather than its absolute value. Patients at risk exhibit significantly elevated and progressively increasing RPE values compared to stable patients. By translating entropy into a practical clinical tool, RPE allows clinicians to detect hidden physiological disturbances in autonomous breathing patterns and intervene earlier to prevent the onset of hemodynamic crises.

Under normal circumstances, respiratory movements are regulated by the mechanical sensitivity respiratory reflex and the chemical sensitivity respiratory reflex to maintain a relatively constant respiratory rate and tidal volume [16–18]. Peripheral chemoreceptors and central chemosensitive neurons respond to changes in the concentrations of O2, CO2, and H+ in the blood, tissue fluid, or cerebrospinal fluid, thereby regulating respiratory movements and modulating lung ventilation [19]. Furthermore, respiratory movements can be regulated through the lung stretch reflex to alter lung ventilation [20]. Some studies been determined that even small variability in breathing patterns may serve as a weaning predictor for extubation failure [21, 22]. This study innovatively introduces the concept of RPE to quantify the degree of respiratory rate variability, with higher RPE indicating a more complex and irregular breathing pattern, while lower entropy signifies a more consistent and stable breathing pattern [13]. Compared to the previous coefficient of variation of respiratory machine parameters and respiratory airflow spectral analysis, RPE can capture the nonlinear dynamic characteristics of breathing patterns, reflecting the complexity and variability of respiratory signals. Additionally, RPE integrates the time series information of respiratory signals, reflecting the overall characteristics and dynamic trends of breathing patterns [23]. Furthermore, RPE can assess individual respiratory pattern characteristics, effectively adapting to the respiratory features of different patients, providing more personalized monitoring and analysis, and enhancing the timeliness and accuracy of respiratory monitoring.

Some potential mechanisms could explain our results. Firstly, sepsis is a severe systemic inflammatory response, where bacterial or fungal infections in conditions like septic shock trigger a strong and sustained immune response, releasing various cytokines and chemical mediators such as interleukins, tumor necrosis factor [24], etc. These inflammatory mediators not only directly affect lung tissue and vascular endothelial cells, but also transmit signals to the respiratory center through neural pathways [25], thereby inducing changes in lung alveolar ventilation, distribution of pulmonary blood flow, and oxygenation capacity. Consequently, those changes could be reflected in the magnitude of RPE, affecting hemodynamics and prognosis; Secondly, sepsis patients often have metabolic disturbances such as acidosis [26], electrolyte imbalances [27], etc., which directly impair the normal function of the heart and other organs, exacerbating the damage caused by systemic inflammatory responses. For instance, acidosis can increase the sensitivity of the respiratory center to CO2, leading to an increase in respiratory rate [28]; Electrolyte imbalances (such as hyperkalemia, hypercalcemia, hypernatremia) may directly affect nerve conduction and muscle contraction, thereby influencing the excitability of the respiratory center and the regulation of respiratory rate [29, 30], especially hypercalcemia may lead to abnormal excitation-contraction coupling in respiratory muscles, ultimately resulting in an increase respiratory rate and depth, leading to increased RPE, affecting the outcomes; Thirdly, sepsis can lead to multi-organ dysfunction, particularly affecting organs related to respiratory and circulatory system function [31], lung dysfunction may result in increased airway resistance, decreased elastic recoil, and mismatch in ventilation/perfusion ratio [32], further impacting the stability of respiratory patterns, causing an increase in respiratory entropy, subsequently affecting the stable state of hemodynamics and hospital mortality and ICU mortality rates.

Significant interactions were identified between RPE and various factors, including age, sex, duration of ICU stay, APACHE IV score, liver function failure, cirrhosis, mechanical ventilation, and renal replacement therapy. Further subgroup analysis results suggest that across different age groups (≥ 65 y vs. <65 y), subgroups with or without RRT, and subgroups with mechanical ventilation treatment, the significant correlation between elevated RPE levels and increased incidence of unstable hemodynamics events, hospital mortality, and ICU mortality remains evident. In other subgroup analyses, we observed that male patients, patients with cirrhosis, and patients with duration of ICU stay ≥ 2.27 days and APACHE IV score ≥ 54 exhibited higher incidence rates of hemodynamic instability events and mortality. We speculate that a high APACHE IV score indicates greater severity of the patient’s illness, which is often associated with more severe physiological disturbances and organ dysfunction [33]. Besides, respiratory rate is an important physiological parameter in the APACHE IV score, and patients with high APACHE IV scores often exhibit abnormally increased or decreased respiratory rates, which might increase the association of elevated RPE with poor prognosis.

This present study has the following limitations: (1) the study design was a retrospective cohort study based on the eICU-CRD database, although we adjusted for a series of covariates that could affect RPE, hemodynamic instability events, ICU mortality, and hospital mortality to mitigate bias, residual confounding factors may remain accounted for; (2) data were derived from ICUs in the United States, predominantly comprising Caucasian patients, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. As localized databases develop, future models should incorporate local patient data to enhance clinical applicability; (3) the results of this study indicate that elevated RPE is significant associated with sepsis-related hemodynamic instability events, hospital mortality, and ICU mortality, however, there is no established cut-off for the RPE to separate groups of high risk versus low risk, further research on its validity and feasibility is needed in future.

Conclusions

In sepsis patients, elevated RPE is an independent risk factor for the occurrence of hemodynamic instability events, ICU mortality, and hospital mortality. It shows a stronger correlation with outcomes in males, patients with liver cirrhosis, as well as those with severer illness and longer duration of ICU. Therefore, RPE can serve as a valuable dynamic monitoring indicator for assessing changes in hemodynamic status, as well as the risk of adverse events such as hospital and ICU mortality, among sepsis patients.

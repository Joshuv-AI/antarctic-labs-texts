A Hybridizable Discontinuous Galerkin Method for the non–local Camassa–Holm–Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation Thanks: This work has been supported by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – 577175348.
Mukul Dwivedi and Ruben Gutendorf and Andreas Rupp

Abstract
. This paper develops a hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin method for the two-dimensional Camassa–Holm–Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation. The method employs Cartesian meshes with tensor-product polynomial spaces, enabling separate treatment of x x and y y derivatives. The non-local operator ∂ x − 1 u y \partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y} is localized through an auxiliary variable v v satisfying v x = u y v_{x}=u_{y} , allowing efficient element-by-element computations. We prove energy stability of the semi-discrete scheme and derive 𝒪 ⁡ ( h k + 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}(h^{k+1/2}) convergence in space. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical results and demonstrate the method’s capability to accurately resolve smooth solutions and peaked solitary waves (peakons).

1.
Introduction

The Camassa-Holm (CH) equation is the task to find the fluid velocity
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

u_{t}-u_{txx}+2\kappa u_{x}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},\quad\text{ for }~x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,

(1.1)

where
\kappa>0
represents the background water depth. It constitutes a fundamental model for unidirectional shallow water waves over flat topography
[
5
,
6
,
18
,
32
]
, and peaked solitary wave (peakon) solutions of the form
u(x,t)=ce^{-|x-ct|}
, for
c\in\mathbb{R}
, if
\kappa=0
.
The existence of these peaked solutions is intimately connected to essential nonlinear phenomena such as finite-time wave breaking
[
17
]
, which the equation successfully models
[
5
,
45
]
. The mathematical structure of (
1.1
) has been extensively analyzed, encompassing spectral stability
[
20
]
, orbital stability
[
19
]
of the smooth solitary waves, and peakon instability
[
21
,
40
]
of the peaked traveling waves. Global well-posedness and blow up results for (
1.1
) were studied in
[
17
,
18
]
. For recent developments in the well-posedness of the CH equation (
1.1
), we refer to
[
4
]
and references therein.

Importantly, the CH equation (
1.1
) represents the evolving free surface with a single, one-dimensional (1D) time-dependent surface, thereby restricting the fluid motion to a vertical plane. This simplification suppresses the weak transverse variations that occur in realistic oceanographic settings. Transverse modulations of the free surface are naturally captured by letting the displacement depend on the two spatial variables
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, leading to a two-dimensional (2D) generalization, the Camassa–Holm–Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (CH–KP) equation to find
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\bigl(u_{t}-u_{txx}+2\kappa u_{x}+3uu_{x}-2u_{x}u_{xx}-uu_{xxx}\bigr)_{x}+u_{yy}=0,\qquad\text{for }~(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;t>0,

(1.2)

which was first derived in the context of nonlinear elasticity
[
8
]
and later rigorously justified for irrotational shallow-water flows
[
28
]
. Equation (
1.2
) extends the CH framework in the spirit of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) theory
[
33
]
, while retaining the stronger nonlinearity of CH, and thus provides a richer alternative to the classical KP
[
33
]
and Korteweg–de Vries
[
22
]
approximations. Its applicability ranges from pure shallow-water dynamics to regimes influenced by Coriolis forcing
[
29
]
. The transverse stability of smooth solitary waves and global well-posedness of the CH-KP equation (
1.2
) in Sobolev spaces has been studied in
[
27
,
28
]
.

The nonlocal evolution form emerges through the application of the nonlocal operator
\partial_{x}^{-1}
yielding

u_{t}+(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left[f(u)_{x}-2u_{x}u_{xx}-uu_{xxx}+\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}\right]=0,

(1.3)

where
f(u)=2\kappa u+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}
, and
\partial_{x}^{-1}g(x):=\int_{+\infty}^{x}g(s)ds
is defined for functions decaying to 0 as
x\to+\infty
, see
[
27
]
.
This formulation admits a Hamiltonian structure

u_{t}=-JF^{\prime}(u)\quad\text{ with }\quad F[u]:=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(u^{3}+uu_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{dxdy},

(1.4)

where
J:=\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
is a skew-adjoint operator and
F
represents a conserved energy for local solutions
u\in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\partial_{x}^{-1}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),~u_{x}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}
with
s\geq 2
. This system possesses additional conserved quantities, including momentum

E[u]=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{dxdy},

(1.5)

and mass
[
27
,
28
]
. The conservation of
E[u]
follows from multiplying (
1.3
) by
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})u
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The evolution form (
1.3
) provides a viable framework for analysis by embedding dispersive effects within the Helmholtz resolvent
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
, which is coercive on
L^{2}(\Omega)
and facilitates energy-based approaches
[
27
]
. Various wave breaking criteria for the equation (
1.3
)
were obtained in
[
28
, Theo. 1.2-1.4]
.

For numerical purposes, we consider the initial-boundary value problem on a sufficiently large bounded domain
\Omega:=(x_{L},x_{R})\times(y_{B},y_{T})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for a given
T>0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle u_{t}+(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left[f(u)_{x}-(uu_{x})_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}(u_{x}^{2})_{x}+\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}\right]

\displaystyle=0,\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega,\quad t\in(0,T),

(1.6a)

\displaystyle u_{0}(x,y)

\displaystyle=u(x,y,0),\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega,

(1.6b)

\displaystyle u(x_{L},y,t)=u(x_{R},y,t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad y\in(y_{B},y_{T}),\quad t\in(0,T),

(1.6c)

\displaystyle u(x,y_{B},t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad x\in(x_{L},x_{R}),~~~t\in(0,T),

(1.6d)

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y})(x_{R},y,t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad y\in(y_{B},y_{T}),\quad t\in(0,T),

(1.6e)

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y})(x,y_{T},t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad x\in(x_{L},x_{R}),\quad t\in(0,T),

(1.6f)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u(x_{L},y,t)=\partial_{x}u(x_{R},y,t)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad y\in(y_{B},y_{T}),\quad t\in(0,T).

(1.6g)

These boundary conditions ensure: (i) compatibility of
\partial_{x}^{-1}
by enforcing flux balance at

[
57
]
, (ii) decay properties mimicking the whole-space problem

28
]
, and (iii) consistency with the Helmholtz structure. It should be noted that the above set of boundary conditions is not unique; other admissible choices (e.g., periodic or different homogeneous/inhomogeneous data) can also be employed, provided they are compatible with the well‑posedness theory. Local well-posedness holds for initial data
u_{0}\in X^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
s\geq 2

[
28
, Theo. 1.1]
.

Numerical approaches for 1D CH-type equations (
1.1
) encompass diverse methodologies, each with distinct strengths and limitations. Finite difference schemes
[
16
,
26
,
31
]
utilize adaptive stencils to achieve convergence, error estimates for Galerkin method are derived in
[

, and the finite volume method
[

incorporates shock-capturing techniques for adaptive peakon resolution while maintaining mass conservation. Spectral techniques
[
24
,
34
,
35
]
leverage exponential convergence for smooth periodic solutions. Operator splitting methods
[
54
]
decouple nonlinear advection from dispersive terms via Strang splitting. The local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) formulation for 1D CH equation pioneered by Xu and Shu
[
52
]
, which establishes energy stability through auxiliary flux variables, and this framework is extended to the 2D CH-KP system on Cartesian grids by Zhang, Xu, and Liu in
[
57
]
. It establishes
\mathcal{O}(h^{k})
order of convergence in space. Related LDG approaches, including the invariant-preserving DG scheme
[
38
]
for Hamiltonian CH dynamics and the
\mu
-CH solvers in one dimension are examined in
[
55
]
. For more DG methods, developed for the similar nonlinear dispersive equations and nonlinear nonlocal dispersive equations, we refer to
[

,
25
,
37
,
53
,
58
]
and references therein. These methods duplicate interface unknowns, leading to expanded global degrees of freedom and auxiliary variables, and
\mathcal{O}(h^{k})
or
\mathcal{O}(h^{k+{\frac{1}{2}}})
convergence is attained.
These considerations motivate the hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) methodology developed in what follows.

HDG methods, introduced by Cockburn et al.
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, constitute a significant advancement in (high-order) finite element discretizations. By reformulating discontinuous Galerkin frameworks through hybridization, HDG methods decompose the solution process into two complementary components: element-local solvers that compute approximations to the unknown in the bulk given trace values on the mesh skeleton, and a global system that enforces continuity through numerical fluxes. The methodology has been systematically extended beyond its original elliptic focus. Foundational work established HDG formulations for parabolic systems
[
42
,
41
]
, incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
[
43
]
, phase-field models including Cahn-Hilliard equations
[
7
,
36
]
, with recent advances including multigrid solvers
[
39
]
. For nonlinear dispersive wave equations, specialized HDG implementations have achieved notable success such as energy-stable schemes for KdV equations
[
9
,
10
,
23
,
49
]
, well-balanced explicit HDG discretizations of Serre–Green–Naghdi systems
[
48
]
, for strongly Damped Wave Problem
[
30
]
, and for equations in continuum mechanics
[
44
]
.

Central to these developments is the design of the trace operators on the interfaces, typically employing stabilized fluxes of the form

\hat{\omega}_{h}=\omega_{h}+\tau(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h}),

(1.7)

where
\tau
is a stabilization parameter that ensures the well-posedness of local problems while maintaining accuracy and stability. Nevertheless, the nonlinear CH equation (
1.1
) and the nonlocal nonlinear CH-KP equation (
1.6
) present distinctive computational challenges for HDG frameworks due to three interconnected factors: the presence of peakon solutions with weak derivative continuity requirements
[
6
,
18
]
, nonlocal operator structures that complicate flux conservation
[
28
]
, and higher order nonlinearities. While HDG has demonstrated success with related dispersive models
[
9
,
23
]
, its application to peakon-supporting systems remains a largely unexplored research topic requiring novel methodological adaptations.

In this work, we develop the first HDG method for the CH-KP equation (
1.6
), employing tensor-product polynomial spaces
Q_{k}(K_{ij})=P_{k}(I_{i})\otimes P_{k}(J_{j})
, where
K_{ij}=[x_{i-1},x_{i}]\times[y_{j-1},y_{j}]=:I_{i}\times J_{j}
, to decouple spatial derivatives (
cf.
Section
2
). The nonlocal operator
\partial_{x}^{-1}
presents unique challenges since the CH-KP equation (
1.6
) describes the time evolution of
\partial_{x}u
rather than
u
itself, requiring additional constraints for well-posedness
[
50
]
. To address this, we implement a reconstruction technique for the primitive,
v_{h}
which approximates
v:=\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{y}
, by rewriting
v_{x}=u_{y}
and imposing a boundary constraint
\widehat{v}_{h}(x_{R},y)=0
at the right domain boundary
\Gamma_{R}
(
cf.
Section
2
), which anchors the reconstruction while preserving uniqueness, following the
non-integration DG
framework established for related one dimensional nonlocal equation
[
56
]
involving operator
\partial^{-1}_{x}
. Global coupling is minimized via three trace variables on the mesh skeleton
\mathcal{F}_{h}
:
\widehat{u}_{h}
,
\widehat{q}_{h}
, and the reconstructed primitive
\widehat{v}_{h}
, where
\widehat{u}_{h}
and
\widehat{q}_{h}
are approximations of the trace of the exact solution
u
and
u_{x}
on the interfaces, respectively. Compared to multi-trace LDG methods, this architecture reduces degrees of freedom while preserving stability and improving convergence order. We establish
\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1/2})
order of convergence in the energy norm for
k\geq 2
under the
a priori
assumptions:

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|q-q_{h}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\leq h^{2}.

(1.8)

In this paper, we denote
C
as a generic constant whose value can change in each step and it is independent of
h
.

3.
HDG Formulation and Stability Analysis for the CH-KP Equation

This section develops the HDG formulation and proves energy stability for the CH-KP equation (
1.6
) on a bounded domain. Building on the evolution form (
1.3
), we consider the Helmholtz resolvent
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
which provides coercivity and ensures well-posedness under homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions with initial data
u_{0}(x,y)=u(x,y,0)
(see
[
28
,
27
]
).

We introduce auxiliary variables to decompose nonlocal operators and higher derivatives

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle:=u_{x},\qquad p:=(uq)_{x},\qquad s:=u_{y},

(3.1a)

\displaystyle v_{x}

\displaystyle:=s,\qquad r:=z_{x}+f(u)_{x}-p_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{2})_{x}+v_{y},\qquad z:=r_{x}

which implies
v_{y}=\partial_{x}^{-1}u_{yy}
and
r=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[f(u)_{x}-p_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{2})_{x}+v_{y}]
. The CH-KP equation (
1.6
) is then equivalent to

u_{t}+r=0,

(3.1b)

along with the initial and boundary conditions in (
1.6
).
This decomposition isolates each spatial derivative for the local elimination of auxiliary variables and preserves
L^{2}
-stability via the Helmholtz structure of
r

[
27
]
.

Local problems

Let us consider an element
K_{ij}
. For the given polynomial traces
\widehat{u}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}}
,
\widehat{u}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}
,
\widehat{u}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{B}}
,
\widehat{q}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}}
,
\widehat{q}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}
,
\widehat{v}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}
, and
\widehat{v}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{T}}
, find
(U,Q,P,S,V,Z,R)\in[Q_{k}(K_{ij})]^{7}
that satisfies the following local system

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=U_{x},\qquad P=(UQ)_{x},\qquad S=U_{y},\qquad V_{x}=S,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=R_{x},\qquad R-Z_{x}=\partial_{x}f(U)-P_{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}(Q^{2})_{x}+V_{y},\qquad U_{t}+R=0,

with the initial condition
U|_{t=0}=u_{0}|_{K_{ij}}
and boundary conditions:

\displaystyle U|_{F}

\displaystyle=\widehat{u}_{F},\qquad F\in\{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L},\Gamma_{i,j}^{R},\Gamma_{i,j}^{B}\},

\displaystyle Q|_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{q}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}},\quad Q|_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}=\widehat{q}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}},\qquad V|_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}=\widehat{v}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}},\quad V|_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{T}}=\widehat{v}_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{T}}.

Coupling conditions

Assuming the local problem is uniquely solvable for the given sufficiently smooth initial and boundary data, the local solutions
(U,Q,P,S,V,Z,R)\in[Q_{k}(K_{ij})]^{7}
form a global solution to (
3.1a
)-(
3.1b
) if and only if the quantities
U,Q,UQ,V,G
are continuous over vertical and
U,V
are continuous over horizontal faces, where
\mathcal{G}:=Z+f(U)-P
, and the boundary conditions in (
1.6
) are satisfied.
Note that, if
U
and
Q
are continuous along the interfaces then
R=-U_{t}
and
Q^{2}
will be continuous for each fixed time
t
.

The HDG method therefore solves the local problems element-wise and enforces these transmission conditions via global coupling to determine the trace unknowns.
This approach preserves the tensor-product structure of the discretization while restricting global coupling to the skeleton. The HDG scheme is then constructed as a discrete analogue of this continuous characterization.

3.1.
An HDG scheme

For every element
K_{ij}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
assume that the polynomial traces

\widehat{u}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}},\quad\widehat{u}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}},\quad\widehat{u}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{B}},\quad\widehat{q}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}},\quad\widehat{q}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}},\quad\widehat{v}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}},\quad\widehat{v}_{h,\Gamma_{i,j}^{T}}

are given.
Note that these families constitute the only unknowns that couple different cells; once they are fixed, each
K_{ij}
can be treated independently. Given the traces above and the restriction of the initial datum
u_{h}(x,y,0)=\mathbb{P}u_{0}
to
K_{ij}
, where
\mathbb{P}
is the usual
L^{2}
-projection onto the finite element space
V_{h}^{k}
,
determine

(u_{h},q_{h},p_{h},s_{h},v_{h},z_{h},r_{h})\;\in\;\bigl[Q_{k}(K_{ij})\bigr]^{7}

such that, for every test function

(\phi_{u},\phi_{q},\phi_{p},\phi_{s},\phi_{v},\phi_{z},\phi_{r})\in\bigl[Q_{k}(K_{ij})\bigr]^{7}
, the following hold

\displaystyle(q_{h},\phi_{q})_{K_{ij}}+(u_{h},\partial_{x}\phi_{q})_{K_{ij}}-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{x},\phi_{q}\rangle_{\partial K_{ij}}=0,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle(p_{h},\phi_{p})_{K_{ij}}+(u_{h}q_{h},\partial_{x}\phi_{p})_{K_{ij}}-\langle\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}}n_{x},\phi_{p}\rangle_{\partial K_{ij}}=0,

(3.2b)

\displaystyle(s_{h},\phi_{s})_{K_{ij}}+(u_{h},\partial_{y}\phi_{s})_{K_{ij}}-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{y},\phi_{s}\rangle_{\partial K_{ij}}=0,

(3.2c)

\displaystyle(s_{h},\phi_{v})_{K_{ij}}+(v_{h},\partial_{x}\phi_{v})_{K_{ij}}-\langle\widehat{v}_{h}n_{x},\phi_{v}\rangle_{\partial K_{ij}}=0,

(3.2d)

\displaystyle(z_{h},\phi_{z})_{K_{ij}}+(r_{h},\partial_{x}\phi_{z})_{K_{ij}}-\langle\widehat{r}_{h}n_{x},\phi_{z}\rangle_{\partial K_{ij}}=0,

(3.2e)

3.2.
Stability analysis

Define the discrete energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{h}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\|u_{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|q_{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\Bigr),\qquad\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Lemma 3.2

(Stability lemma)
.

Assume that the boundary condition in (
3.6
) is set to be homogeneous, i.e.,
u_{D}=q_{L}=q_{R}=v_{R}=v_{T}=0
and that the stabilization parameters satisfy Assumption
3.1
.
Then the HDG approximation

(u_{h},q_{h},p_{h},s_{h},v_{h},z_{h},r_{h}),

obtained from (
3.2
)-(
3.6
) satisfies the energy inequality

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)\leq 0,\qquad\text{for all }t>0.

(3.10)

Consequently,
\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{h}(0)
and the scheme is

L^{2}
–stable.

Proof.

Differentiating (
3.7a
) with respect to time and setting
\phi_{q}=q_{h}
yields

\bigl((q_{h})_{t},q_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\bigl((u_{h})_{t},\partial_{x}q_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle(\widehat{u}_{h})_{t}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0,

(3.11)

where we employ the inner-product notation from (
2.1
)-(
2.2
).
Next, choose the test functions

(\phi_{u},\phi_{q},\phi_{p},\phi_{s},\phi_{v},\phi_{z},\phi_{r})=(u_{h}-\partial_{x}q_{h},p_{h}-z_{h},-q_{h},-v_{h},v_{h},q_{h},-u_{h}),

in the system (
3.7
), and adding the equations in the resulting system and (
3.11
) yields

\begin{split}&\big((u_{h})_{t},u_{h}\big)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\big((q_{h})_{t},q_{h}\big)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle(\widehat{u}_{h})_{t}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+(u_{h},\partial_{x}p_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{x},p_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad-(u_{h},\partial_{x}z_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{x},z_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(u_{h}q_{h},\partial_{x}q_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(u_{h},\partial_{y}v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad+\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{y},v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+(v_{h},\partial_{x}v_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{v}_{h}n_{x},v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(v_{h},\partial_{y}u_{h})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{v}_{h}n_{y},u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad-\langle\widehat{r}_{h}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\bigl(z_{h}+f(u_{h})-p_{h},\partial_{x}u_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}q_{h}^{2},\partial_{x}u_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad+\langle\widehat{z}_{h}+\widehat{f(u_{h})}-\widehat{p}_{h},n_{x}u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\widehat{q_{h}^{2}}n_{x},u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0.\end{split}

Applying multiple integrations by parts yields

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)-\langle(\widehat{u}_{h})_{t}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle u_{h},n_{x}p_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{x},p_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle u_{h},n_{x}z_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad+\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{x},z_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Big\langle u_{h},n_{x}\frac{1}{2}(q_{h})^{2}\Big\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}},n_{x}q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle u_{h},n_{y}v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad+\langle\widehat{u}_{h}n_{y},v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle v_{h},n_{x}v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{v}_{h},n_{x}v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{v}_{h}n_{y},u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\quad-\langle\widehat{r}_{h}n_{x},q_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\bigl(f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}u_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{z}_{h}+\widehat{f(u_{h})}-\widehat{p}_{h},n_{x}u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\tfrac{1}{2}\widehat{q_{h}^{2}},n_{x}u_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0.\end{split}

(3.12)

The transmission condition (
3.5
), and the boundary conditions (
3.6
) lead to

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)\underbrace{-\bigl(f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}u_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{f(u_{h})},(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{f}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h},((\widehat{z}_{h}-\widehat{p}_{h})-(z_{h}-p_{h}))n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\langle(\widehat{v}_{h}-v_{h})^{2},n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{u_{x}}}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{v}_{h}^{2}|_{\Gamma_{L}}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\left\langle\frac{1}{2}\widehat{q_{h}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}q_{h}^{2},u_{h}n_{x}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}},q_{h}n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{q}}\underbrace{-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h},(\widehat{v}_{h}-v_{h})n_{y}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{\mathcal{I}_{u_{y}}}=0.\end{split}

(3.13)

Let
G
be an antiderivative of
f

G^{\prime}(s)=f(s)
. Since

-\bigl(f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}u_{h}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-\bigl(\partial_{x}G(u_{h}),1\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-\langle G(u_{h}),n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-\left\langle\int_{\widehat{u}_{h}}^{u_{h}}f(s)ds,n_{x}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{f}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\int_{\widehat{u}_{h}}^{u_{h}}(f(s)-f(u_{h}))ds,n_{x}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\left\langle\widehat{f(u_{h})}-f(u_{h}),(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})n_{x}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau_{f}-\tilde{\tau},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})^{2}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}},

(3.14)

where we employed (
3.8
). Using the transmission condition (
3.5

3.3

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{q}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\tfrac{1}{2}(\widehat{q}_{h})^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}q_{h}^{2},u_{h}n_{x}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}},(\widehat{q}_{h}-q_{h})n_{x}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u_{h}\left(\frac{\widehat{q}_{h}+q_{h}}{2}\right)-\widehat{u_{h}q_{h}},(\widehat{q}_{h}-q_{h})n_{x}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=-\langle\tau_{uqq},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{q}_{h}-q_{h})^{2}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

We evaluate

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{u_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl\langle\tau_{zpu}^{+},(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})^{2}\Bigr\rangle_{L}-\langle\tau_{zpu}^{-},(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})^{2}\rangle_{R}-\langle\tau_{zpv}^{-},(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h})(\widehat{v}_{h}^{x^{-}}-v_{h})\rangle_{R}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2},(\widehat{v}_{h}^{x^{-}}-v_{h})^{2}\Bigr\rangle_{R},

We finally evaluate
\mathcal{I}_{u_{y}}
using (

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{u_{y}}=-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h},(\widehat{v}_{h}-v_{h})n_{y}\rangle_{B}-\langle\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h},(\widehat{v}_{h}-v_{h})n_{y}\rangle_{T}=0.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{f}+\mathcal{I}_{u_{x}}+\mathcal{I}_{q}+\mathcal{I}_{u_{y}}\geq 0.

(3.15)

Therefore, from (

4.
Error analysis

We assume homogeneous boundary conditions (
3.6
) and that Assumption
3.1
holds.
Let
(u_{h},q_{h},p_{h},s_{h},v_{h},z_{h},r_{h})
be the semi-discrete HDG approximation defined by
(
3.2
)–(
3.6
).
This section establishes an
a priori

L^{2}
-error estimate, demonstrating convergence of order
h^{k+1/2}
.

The analysis relies on suitably defined projection operators.
For an interval
I:=(a,b)
and
\omega\in L^{2}(I)
, denote by
\mathcal{P}\omega\in P_{k}(I)
the standard
L^{2}
-projection, which satisfies

\int_{I}(\mathcal{P}\omega-\omega)v\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\qquad\forall v\in P_{k}(I).

When
\omega
has additional regularity,
\omega\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(I)
,
\epsilon>0
, we also use the one-sided projections
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}\omega\in P_{k}(I)
defined by

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\mathcal{P}^{+}\omega-\omega)v\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=0,\;\forall v\in P_{k-1}(I),\quad\mathcal{P}^{+}\omega(a^{+})=\omega(a),

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\mathcal{P}^{-}\omega-\omega)v\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=0,\;\forall v\in P_{k-1}(I),\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}\omega(b^{-})=\omega(b).

These one-dimensional projections are well-defined for
\omega\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(I)
; see
[
15
]
.

On a rectangular cell
K_{ij}
, define the tensor-product
L^{2}
-projection
\mathbb{P}:=\mathcal{P}_{x}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{y}
onto
Q_{k}(K_{ij})
, where
\mathcal{P}_{x}
and
\mathcal{P}_{y}
are the standard
L^{2}
-projections in one dimension on the
x
- and
y
-directions, respectively.
For
\omega\in L^{2}(K_{ij})
the projection error
\mu^{\omega}:=\mathbb{P}\omega-\omega
satisfies

(\mu^{\omega},\psi_{\omega})_{K_{ij}}=0,\qquad\forall\psi_{\omega}\in Q_{k}(K_{ij}).

(4.1)

For smoother functions,
\omega\in H^{2}(K_{ij})
, we introduce the composite projections

\Pi^{\pm}\omega:=\mathcal{P}_{x}^{\pm}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{y}^{\pm}\omega,

well-defined in
Q_{k}(K_{ij})

[
15
, Section 3.2]
.
For
u,v\in H^{2}(K_{ij})
, we use the projection
\Pi^{-}u,\Pi^{+}v\in Q_{k}(K_{ij})
.
The projections
\Pi^{-}u
and
\Pi^{+}v
satisfy the following orthogonality and trace properties
[
51
]
:

\displaystyle(\eta^{\omega},\phi_{\omega})_{K_{ij}}=0,\qquad\forall\phi_{\omega}\in(P_{k}(I_{i})\otimes P_{k-1}(J_{j}))\cup(P_{k-1}(I_{i})\otimes P_{k}(J_{j})),\quad\omega\in\{u,v\},

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{v},\phi_{v}\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{L}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\langle\eta^{v},\phi_{v}\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{B}}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall\phi_{v}\in Q_{k}(K_{ij}),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{u},\phi_{u}\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{R}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\langle\eta^{u},\phi_{u}\rangle_{\Gamma_{i,j}^{T}}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\forall\phi_{u}\in Q_{k}(K_{ij}),

where

\eta^{v}:=\Pi^{+}v-v,\qquad\eta^{u}:=\Pi^{-}u-u.

Throughout the analysis, we denote the projection errors for the seven variables by

\eta^{u},\;\mu^{q},\;\mu^{p},\;\mu^{s},\;\eta^{v},\;\mu^{z},\;\mu^{r},

corresponding to
u,q,p,s,v,z,r
, respectively.

Lemma 4.1

(Inverse inequalities
[
11
,
51
]
)
.

For every

These attributes make the HDG method a powerful tool for simulating nonlinear dispersive wave equations in two dimensions.

6.
Conclusion

This paper has introduced a novel high-order hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) method for solving the two-dimensional Camassa-Holm-Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (CH-KP) equation by leveraging Cartesian meshes with tensor-product polynomial spaces
Q_{k}(K_{ij})=P_{k}(I_{i})\otimes P_{k}(J_{j})
. The semi-discrete scheme maintains energy stability (
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)\leq 0
).
Error analysis establishes
\mathcal{O}(h^{k+1/2})
convergence in the energy norm for smooth solutions achieved via a special projection operator. Hybridization reduces globally coupled degrees of freedom in local DG methods, enabling scalable simulations. Numerical experiments validate the method’s capability to accurately capture smooth solutions and peakon solutions.

Future work will extend this direct
non-integration
HDG framework to
integration
HDG approach by integrating the equation
v_{x}=u_{y}
and setting

v_{h}(x,y)\big|_{K_{ij}}=\widehat{v}_{h}(x_{R},y)-\int^{x_{R}}_{x}s_{h}(\xi,y)d\xi,\quad s_{h}=\partial_{y}u_{h},

and develop the Hamiltonian conservative scheme.

The HDG framework presented here is readily extensible to other nonlocal, quasilinear dispersive wave equations—including the Camassa–Holm, Degasperis–Procesi,
Novikov, and various Kadomtsev–Petviashvili-type systems—as well as to related geophysical fluid models, offering a versatile and high‑order accurate computational tool for a wide range of applications.

\displaystyle\leq C\|q_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2})\leq C\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+C\|\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{3}

\displaystyle=-(\xi^{u}\rho^{q},\partial_{x}\rho^{q})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\bigl(\frac{1}{2}(\rho^{q})^{2},\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q})^{2},n_{x}\xi^{u}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}\xi^{u}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}+\rho),n_{x}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl\langle\xi^{u},\frac{1}{2}(\rho^{q})^{2}n_{x}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q})^{2},n_{x}\xi^{u}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}\xi^{u}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}+\rho),n_{x}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{4}

\displaystyle=-(u\mu^{q},\partial_{x}\rho^{q})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(q,\eta^{u}\partial_{x}\rho^{q}+\mu^{q}\partial_{x}\xi^{u})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-u_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\mu^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\partial_{x}\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|q-q_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}(\|\eta^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\partial_{x}\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\mu^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2k+2}+C(\|\rho^{q}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\xi^{u}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{5}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}\bigl(-u\mu^{q}+\xi^{u}\mu^{q}-\eta^{u}\mu^{q}\bigr)-q\eta^{u},n_{x}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u-u_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}h^{-1/2}h^{1/2}\|\mu^{q}\|_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+C\|\mu^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\xi^{u}\|_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\eta^{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\mu^{q}\|_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+C\|q-q_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}h^{-1/2}h^{1/2}\|\eta^{u}\|_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\quad-\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{2}(u_{c}\mu^{q}),n_{x}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle q_{c}\eta^{u},n_{x}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)h^{2k+1}+C\|\xi^{u}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+C\delta\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{6}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{u}\mu^{q},\partial_{x}\rho^{q})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+(\rho^{q}\mu^{q},\partial_{x}\xi^{u})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\frac{1}{2}((\mu^{q})^{2},\partial_{x}\xi^{u}){\mathcal{T}_{h}}-(\mu^{q}\eta^{u},\partial_{x}\rho^{q})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\rho^{q}_{x}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\mu^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\xi^{u}_{x}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\mu^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mu^{q}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\xi^{u}_{x}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\mu^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\eta^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\rho^{q}_{x}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2k+2}+C\|\rho^{q}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+C\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{7}=\langle\tau_{uqq}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}+\mu^{q}),(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\langle\tau_{uqq},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})^{2}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\tau_{uqq}\mu^{q},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\tau_{uqq},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})^{2}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+Ch^{2k+1}+C\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}.

Putting all the estimates for
\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{\prime}s
in the equation (
A.7
), we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}(u,q;u_{h},q_{h};-\rho^{q},-\xi^{u})&\leq\langle\tau_{uqq},(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q})^{2}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+Ch^{2k+1}+C(\|\rho^{q}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2})\\
&\qquad+C\delta\|\widehat{\rho}^{q}-\rho^{q}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\\
&\leq Ch^{2k+1}+C(\|\rho^{q}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}),\end{split}

where positive constants
C
and
\delta
, which comes from the application of Young’s inequality, are generic and independent of
h
. We have taken
\delta
small enough so that
-\tau_{uqq}\geq C\delta
even for small negative
\tau_{uqq}
as in Assumption
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be defined by (
4.19
) and assume that all the hypotheses of the Theorem
4.5
hold. Then we have the following estimate

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(f;u,u_{h},-\xi^{u})\leq-\bar{C}\|\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}+Ch^{2k+1}+C\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}-\langle(f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})}),n_{x}\widehat{\xi}^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.\end{split}

(A.8)

Proof.

Using the transmission condition (
4.22
) and smoothness of
f
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(f;u,u_{h},-\xi^{u})+\langle(f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})})&,n_{x}\widehat{\xi}^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=(f(u)-f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}\xi^{u})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})},n_{x}\xi^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\qquad+\langle(f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})}),n_{x}\widehat{\xi}^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&=(f(u)-f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}\xi^{u})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle f(u)-f(u_{h}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\qquad+\langle\tau_{f}(\widehat{u}_{h}-u_{h}),(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&=(f(u)-f(u_{h}),\partial_{x}\xi^{u})_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle f(u)-f(u_{h}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&\qquad-\langle\tau_{f}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u}+\eta^{u}),(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.\end{split}

(A.9)

We expand
f(u)-f(u_{h})
about
u
as follows

\displaystyle f(u)-f(u_{h})=f^{\prime}(u)(u-u_{h})-\frac{1}{2}\,f^{\prime\prime}_{\!u}\,(u-u_{h})^{2}=f^{\prime}(u)(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u})-\frac{1}{2}\,f^{\prime\prime}_{\!u}\,(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u})^{2}.

Using the above expansion, we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(f;u,u_{h},-\xi^{u})&+\langle(f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})}),n_{x}\widehat{\xi}^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\
&=\underbrace{\bigl(f^{\prime}(u)(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle f^{\prime}(u)(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{H_{1}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{-\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\,f^{\prime\prime}_{\!u}\,(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u})^{2},\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\Bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Big\langle\frac{1}{2}\,f^{\prime\prime}_{\!u}\,(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u})^{2},n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\Big\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{H_{2}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{-\langle\tau_{f}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u}+\eta^{u}),(n_{x})^{2}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}}_{H_{3}}.\end{split}

(A.10)

Incorporating interpolation (
4.5
) and inverse inequalities (
4.4
), integration by parts, Young’s inequality, and Assumption
4.4
, with
\|u-u_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}=\mathcal{O}(h)
and
\|u-u_{c}\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}=\mathcal{O}(h^{1/2})

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigl(f^{\prime}(u)(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle f^{\prime}(u)(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(f^{\prime\prime}(u)u_{x},\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{u})^{2}\Bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\Bigl\langle(f^{\prime}(u)-f^{\prime}(u_{c})),n_{x}\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{u})^{2}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\Bigl\langle f^{\prime}(u_{c}),n_{x}\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{u})^{2}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle-\bigl((f^{\prime}(u)-f^{\prime}(u_{c}))\eta^{u},\partial_{x}\xi^{u}\bigr)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle(f^{\prime}(u)-f^{\prime}(u_{c}))(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle+\langle f^{\prime}(u_{c})(\xi^{u}-\eta^{u}),n_{x}(\widehat{\xi}^{u}-\xi^{u})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\Bigl\langle f^{\prime}(u_{c}),n_{x}\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\xi}^{u})^{2}\Bigr\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f^{\prime\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{\prime}(u)-f^{\prime}(u_{c})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\xi^{u}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

A.9

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(f;u,u_{h},-\xi^{u})&+\langle(f(u)-\widehat{f(u_{h})}),n_{x}\widehat{\xi}^{u}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}\\

Insights from Gradient Dynamics: Gradient Autoscaled Normalization
Vincent-Daniel Yun

Abstract
Gradient dynamics play a central role in determining the stability and generalization of deep neural networks. In this work, we provide an empirical analysis of how variance and standard deviation of gradients evolve during training, showing consistent changes across layers and at the global scale in convolutional networks. Motivated by these observations, we propose a hyperparameter-free gradient normalization method that aligns gradient scaling with their natural evolution. This approach prevents unintended amplification, stabilizes optimization, and preserves convergence guarantees. Experiments on the challenging CIFAR-100 benchmark with ResNet-20, ResNet-56, and VGG-16-BN demonstrate that our method maintains or improves test accuracy even under strong generalization. Beyond practical performance, our study highlights the importance of directly tracking gradient dynamics, aiming to bridge the gap between theoretical expectations and empirical behaviors, and to provide insights for future optimization research.

By explicitly visualizing how gradient statistics evolve, we aim to provide insights into their role in convergence and generalization, a direction that has received limited attention in prior work, especially in terms of tracking layer-wise and global gradients over time. 3
Methodology

3.1
Preliminaries

We consider a deep neural network with
L
layers and parameters
\{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^{L}
. For fully connected layers,
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{l}\times M_{l}}
; for convolutional layers,
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{C^{(l)}_{\mathrm{out}}\times C^{(l)}_{\mathrm{in}}\times k^{(l)}_{1}\times k^{(l)}_{2}}
. Let
G^{(l)}_{t}\!:=\!\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(l)}}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t})
denote the gradient tensor of layer
l
at iteration
t
. We denote by
\operatorname{vec}(\cdot)
the vectorization operator and by

\mu^{(l)}_{t}\;:=\;\frac{1}{n^{(l)}}\sum_{i=1}^{n^{(l)}}\big(G^{(l)}_{t}\big)_{i}
the (entrywise) mean of
G^{(l)}_{t}
, where
n^{(l)}
is the number of elements in
G^{(l)}_{t}
. We write
\operatorname{Std}(\cdot)
for the (unbiased) standard deviation of a vector and use
\epsilon>0
as a fixed numerical stabilizer (we use
\epsilon=10^{-8}
in all experiments).

3.2
Gradient Autocaled Normalization

Gradient Gradient Autoscaled Normalization performs a two-stage, statistics-driven modification of gradients before the standard gradient descent update
Bottou 2010
:
(i)
layer-wise mean removal
(zero-centering)
Yong et al. 2020
, and
(ii) a
global autoscale
multiplier shared by all eligible tensors at iteration
t
.
Unlike Z-score normalization
Yun 2025
, Our proposed method does not divide by the (local) standard deviation, thereby avoiding uncontrolled amplification in layers whose gradient variance becomes very small.

Eligible set.

At each iteration
t
, we form the set of tensors whose gradients have at least two dimensions,

\mathcal{S}_{t}\;:=\;\big\{\,l\in\{1,\dots,L\}\;:\;\mathrm{dim}(G^{(l)}_{t})>1\,\big\},
which typically excludes bias vectors and scalar parameters. We aggregate all eligible gradients into a single vector

\mathbf{g}_{t}\;:=\;\bigoplus_{l\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}\operatorname{vec}\!\big(G^{(l)}_{t}\big),
and define the
global
gradient standard deviation
s_{t}=\operatorname{Std}(\mathbf{g}_{t})

Figure 3:
Scaling factor on VGG-16-BN
Simonyan and Zisserman 2015
with different settings: (A)
P_{t}=2
, (B)
P_{t}=1
.

Autoscale via log–std.

Our proposed method maps the global scale
s_{t}
to a scalar multiplier
a_{t}\in(0,+\infty)
through a smooth, hyperparameter-free transform of
\lvert\log s_{t}\rvert
:

\displaystyle a_{t}\;=\;\left(\left(0.5+\frac{1}{0.5\left(\lvert\log s_{t}\rvert+\epsilon\right)}\right)-0.5\right)\cdot 2^{\,p_{t}},\quad p_{t}\;=\;\begin{cases}1,&\text{for all }t\geq 2\text{ if }a_{1}<0.5,\\
2,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1)

Algebraically, eq
1
simplifies to
a_{t}=\left(\frac{4}{\lvert\log s_{t}\rvert+\epsilon}\right)^{p_{t}}
where
p_{t}
is an integer
exponent
that controls the curvature of the scaling map. Figure
3
illustrates the behavior of the scaling factor for VGG-16-BN when
p_{t}=2
(left) and
p_{t}=1
(right). When the initial standard deviation
s_{1}
is small, the quadratic mapping (
p_{t}=2
) can reduce
a_{t}
excessively, leading to overly aggressive downscaling. To prevent this, our proposed method adopts a one-shot safeguard: the exponent
p_{t}
is chosen adaptively at the first iteration according to eq
1
.

This rule ensures that the scaling factor remains stable and avoids excessive shrinkage in the presence of anomalously small initial gradient variance, while still allowing curvature control in subsequent epochs.

Layer-wise zero-centering and global rescaling.

For each eligible layer
l\in\mathcal{S}_{t}
, we compute the mean-removed gradient
\widetilde{G}^{(l)}_{t}=G^{(l)}_{t}-\mu^{(l)}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{1}
where
\mathbf{1}
is the all-ones tensor of the same shape as
G^{(l)}_{t}
. our proposed method then applies the
same
autoscale
a_{t}
to all eligible layers:

\widehat{G}^{(l)}_{t}\;=\;a_{t}\cdot\widetilde{G}^{(l)}_{t}\quad\text{for all }l\in\mathcal{S}_{t},\qquad\widehat{G}^{(l)}_{t}\;=\;G^{(l)}_{t}\quad\text{for }l\notin\mathcal{S}_{t}.

(2)

The above equations describe a
zero-mean, globally consistent
gradient transformation that avoids dividing by very small per-layer standard deviations, preventing gradient explosion at the layer level while still adapting to the overall gradient scale of the network.

SGD update.

Let
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{t}:=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{L}\operatorname{vec}\!\big(\widehat{G}^{(l)}_{t}\big)
denote the transformed gradient concatenated over all parameters. our proposed method with standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
Bottou 2010
performs the following update at iteration
t
:
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}-\eta\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{t}
where
\eta>0
is the learning rate. This formulation highlights that the effect of our proposed method lies entirely in the gradient transformation, while the underlying optimizer step remains identical to standard SGD
Bottou 2010
. This allows easy adaptation to other optimizers such as Adam
Kingma and Ba 2015
;
Loshchilov and Hutter 2019
and RMSProp
Tieleman and Hinton 2012
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose the loss function
L
is
\beta
-smooth and the stochastic gradient is unbiased with bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]=\nabla L(w_{t})

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{b}

(3)

Theorem 2
.

Let the loss function
L
be
\beta
-smooth, and assume the stochastic gradient is unbiased with bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]=\nabla L(w_{t})
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{b}

\eta a_{t}\leq\tfrac{1}{\beta}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2}{T\eta a_{t}}\big(L(w_{0})-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{T})]\big)+\frac{\eta a_{t}\beta\sigma^{2}}{b}.

(4)

The complete proofs follow the standard analysis of SGD

and are deferred to Appendix

in the update rule. Since
a_{t}\in(0,1]
, it simply reduces the effective stepsize while leaving the overall convergence guarantees maintained.

6
Discussion

Our work analyzed gradient dynamics in convolutional networks such as ResNet and VGG, demonstrating the effect of hyperparameter-free normalization. As shown in Figure
4
, our method consistently achieves higher test accuracy on ResNet-56
He et al. 2016
and exhibits smoother convergence compared to baseline AdamW
Loshchilov and Hutter 2019
and other normalization approaches. This highlights the benefit of aligning gradient scaling with the natural evolution of gradient statistics. While these results validate the method, they also reveal limitations: the observations are tied to CNNs
He et al. 2016
;
Simonyan and Zisserman 2015
and experiments were restricted to CIFAR-100
Krizhevsky and Hinton 2009
. Future research will extend this analysis to Vision Transformers, whose gradient dynamics differ fundamentally due to attention-based architectures.

7
Conclusion

In this work, we analyzed the gradient dynamics of CNN and proposed a hyperparameter-free normalization method. By aligning gradient scaling with the natural evolution of gradient statistics, our approach mitigates uncontrolled amplification and improves generalization. Experimental results on CIFAR-100 confirm the robustness of our method even under strong generalization settings. We hope this study not only contributes a practical optimization technique but also provides insights into the role of gradient dynamics, paving the way for more robust training strategies.

Appendix A
Related Works

Research on gradient-based optimization has mainly focused on how training dynamics influence both convergence and generalization. Early studies showed the importance of stochastic gradient descent
Bottou 2010
and its variants
Loshchilov and Hutter 2019
;
Kingma and Ba 2015
;
Tieleman and Hinton 2012
in large-scale learning problems
Bottou and Bousquet 2007
;
Bottou et al. 2018
, providing both theory and strong results in practice. Later work studied the problems of vanishing and exploding gradients
Pascanu et al. 2013
;
Glorot and Bengio 2010
;
Zucchet and Orvieto 2024
;
Guo et al. 2024
, which are still major challenges for stable training, especially in very deep or recurrent networks. These difficulties motivated stabilization techniques such as batch normalization
Ioffe and Szegedy 2015
;
Santurkar et al. 2018
, layer normalization
Ba et al. 2016
, and residual connections
He et al. 2016
;
Xie et al. 2017
;
Zagoruyko and Komodakis 2016
;
Huang et al. 2017
.

A complementary line of research proposed methods that normalize gradients directly. Gradient normalization methods such as GradNorm
Chen et al. 2018
were first introduced for multitask learning, to keep gradients from different tasks balanced. A common rule is
g_{i}\leftarrow\frac{g_{i}}{\|g_{i}\|}\cdot\alpha,
where
g_{i}
is the gradient of task
i
and
\alpha
is a scaling factor. Gradient clipping
Pascanu et al. 2013
is another widely used method, defined as
g\leftarrow\frac{g}{\max(1,\|g\|/c)},
where
c
is a threshold, which prevents exploding gradients by shrinking overly large updates. Gradient centralization
Yong et al. 2020
improved generalization by simply subtracting the mean,
g\leftarrow{g-\mu_{g}}
. More recent work, such as Z-Score Gradient Normalization
Yun 2025
, tried to standardize gradients across layers,
g\leftarrow\frac{g-\mu_{g}}{\sigma_{g}+\epsilon},
but this can become unstable when some layers have very small variance, leading to amplification and worse performance.

While prior methods mainly focus on manipulating gradients, few works have explicitly tracked how their standard deviation evolves across layers or at the global scale. This study directly monitors these dynamics during training, linking theoretical expectations of gradient decay with empirical behavior. Building on this, a hyperparameter-free approach using global statistics is introduced, which avoids unstable amplification and preserves convergence guarantees, while highlighting the importance of studying gradient dynamics themselves as a basis for future optimization research.

Theoretical Analysis: Convergence Guarantees on SGD

Assumption 1

(
\beta
-smoothness)
.

The loss function
L:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\beta
-smooth, i.e., its gradient is
\beta
-Lipschitz continuous
\|\nabla L(u)-\nabla L(v)\|\;\leq\;\beta\|u-v\|,\forall u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Equivalently, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following descent lemma holds:

\displaystyle L(u)\;\leq\;L(v)+\langle\nabla L(v),u-v\rangle+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|u-v\|^{2}.

(5)

Assumption 2

(Unbiased stochastic gradient)
.

At each iteration
t
, the stochastic gradient
\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})
is an unbiased estimator of the true gradient:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]\;=\;\nabla L(w_{t}).

(6)

Lemma 1

Suppose the loss function
L
is
\beta
-smooth and the stochastic gradient is unbiased with bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]=\nabla L(w_{t})
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{b}
. If the effective step size satisfies
\eta a_{t}\leq\tfrac{1}{\beta}
, then the scaled SGD update

guarantees:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\tfrac{\eta a_{t}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}.

(7)

Proof.

By the
\beta
-smoothness of
L
, it holds that

\displaystyle L(w_{t+1})\leq L(w_{t})+\langle\nabla L(w_{t}),w_{t+1}-w_{t}\rangle+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|w_{t+1}-w_{t}\|^{2}.

(8)

Plugging in the update rule
w_{t+1}=w_{t}-\eta a_{t}\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})
, we obtain

\displaystyle L(w_{t+1})\leq L(w_{t})-\eta a_{t}\langle\nabla L(w_{t}),\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\rangle+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta}{2}\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\|^{2}.

(9)

Taking expectations and using the condition
\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]=\nabla L(w_{t})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\eta a_{t}\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla L(w_{t}),\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\rangle]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\|^{2}]

(10)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\eta a_{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\|^{2}].

(11)

For the variance term, observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})+\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]

(12)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}+\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t}),\nabla L(w_{t})\rangle]

(14)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}+\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}

(16)

Theorem 2

L

\beta

\mathbb{E}[\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})]=\nabla L(w_{t})

\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L_{t}(w_{t})-\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{b}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2}{T\eta a_{t}}\big(L(w_{0})-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{T})]\big)+\frac{\eta a_{t}\beta\sigma^{2}}{b}.

(26)

Proof.

From the descent lemma applied to scaled SGD, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]\leq\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\tfrac{\eta a_{t}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}.

(27)

Averaging inequality (
27
) over
T
iterations yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\tfrac{\eta a_{t}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}\right).

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}\right)-\tfrac{\eta a_{t}}{2T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}].

(29)

Rearranging terms and noting that
\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]\big)=\tfrac{1}{T}\big(L(w_{0})-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{T})]\big)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\eta a_{t}}{2T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big(\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t})]-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{t+1})]\big)+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}.

(31)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{T}\big(L(w_{0})-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{T})]\big)+\tfrac{(\eta a_{t})^{2}\beta\sigma^{2}}{2b}.

(32)

Dividing both sides by
\tfrac{\eta a_{t}}{2}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla L(w_{t})\|^{2}]\leq\frac{2}{T\eta a_{t}}\big(L(w_{0})-\mathbb{E}[L(w_{T})]\big)+\frac{\eta a_{t}\beta\sigma^{2}}{b}.

(33)

∎

Complete chloroplast genome sequence of Protea cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ (Proteaceae) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Jingjing Liao, Gaoyao Zhang, Yao Qu, Liqin Jiang, Bin Zheng, Yue Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProtea cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ (James Petiver 1698) is a perennial evergreen shrub of the Proteaceae family valued for ornamental and medicinal uses, yet its genomic data remain unexplored. Here, we report the complete chloroplast genome sequence (157,894 bp) of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’. The genome exhibits a quadripartite structure, including a large single-copy region (86,427 bp), a short single-copy region (18,553 bp), and two inverted repeats (26,457 bp each), with 38.06% GC content. A total of 130 genes were annotated. Phylogenetic analysis confirmed close relationships within Proteaceae, advancing insights into the genetics and evolution of Protea.

Introduction

Protea cynaroides ‘Red Rex’, also known as bodhi flower, longan flower, or wood lily, is a perennial evergreen multi-flowered stem shrub of the genus Protea in the Proteaceae family. The genus Protea is a prominent lineage within the Proteaceae, an early-diverging eudicot family sister to the core eudicots. Additionally, P. cynaroides, an early-diverging eudicot, is the national flower of South Africa and the most iconic species from the megadiverse Cape Floristic Region (CFR). Genomic studies indicate that the Proteaceae underwent an ancient whole-genome duplication (WGD) event during the Late Cretaceous, prior to the radiation of its extant crown groups. This WGD is shared by subfamilies Proteoideae and Grevilleoideae—including Protea cynaroides and Macadamia integrifolia—and is distinct from WGDs in Nelumbonaceae and Ranunculales (Mitchell et al. 2014).

Phylogenomic analyses consistently place P. cynaroides as sister to Macadamia integrifolia and Telopea speciosissima, with an estimated divergence time of ∼63 million years ago. The genomic structure of Proteaceae is highly conserved, showing strong synteny between P. cynaroides and M. integrifolia despite their long separation (Valente et al. 2010). This conserved architecture provides a valuable framework for comparative studies of WGDs in early-diverging eudicots, such as Papaveraceae.

Renowned for its distinctive inflorescence, Protea represents a key genus in the South African horticultural industry and cut-flower market. It has also gained widespread popularity in the Chinese flower market (Chang et al. 2023). Traditionally, this plant species has been used in the treatment of cancer and bladder and kidney ailments. Moreover, some studies have indicated that the constituents of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ exhibit potent anti-tyrosinase activity, highlighting the potential of this species for application in the cosmetic industry, subject to further biological and clinical investigations (Yalo et al. 2022). However, research on its genome, especially the chloroplast genome, is relatively scarce. Previous studies reported a chromosome-scale genome for P. cynaroides (var. ‘Little Prince’), which represents not only the first genome sequenced for any Protea species, but also the first for a species endemic to the CFR and the fynbos biome (Weston 2014). Nonetheless, there are currently no reports on the chloroplast genome of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’. Therefore, in this study, we comprehensively analyzed the chloroplast genome using high-throughput Illumina sequencing. This study aimed to enhance understanding about P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ by offering genomic data that can support systematic studies, genetic breeding, and species development.

Materials and methods

Plant material

The fresh leaves of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ were collected from Chenggong District, Kunming City, Yunnan Province, China (24°21′N, 102°45′E) (Figure 1). A specimen was deposited at the South African National Biodiversity Institute (25°44′S, 28°16′E) (contact: Shoni Sani Munzhedzi; info@sanbi.org.za; https://www.sanbi.org/gardens/) under the voucher number 258812646.

Photo of Protea cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ taken by the co-first author (Gaoyao Zhang). The leaves are alternate and lanceolate in shape, with serrated teeth along the edges, and the leaf surface is smooth, presenting a deep green color. The inflorescence is large (12–30 cm in diameter) and composed of numerous small, densely arranged flowers. The petals are thick and red, and the Central portion of the flower is often characterized by golden-yellow stamens.

DNA extraction and sequencing

Total genomic DNA was extracted from fresh leaves using the modified CTAB method (Huang et al. 2024). The purity and concentration of the chloroplast genome DNA were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer and 1.0% agarose gel electrophoresis to ensure that the samples met the sequencing requirements.

Initial DNA quantification was performed using a Qubit 2.0 fluorometer. After diluting the library, an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer was used to assess its insert size. Quantitative real-time PCR was subsequently employed to accurately determine the effective library concentration. High-quality libraries were subjected to paired-end sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 6000 sequencer to generate the P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ genome data.

Genome assembly and annotation

Raw sequencing reads obtained through sequencing were filtered using the FastQC software v0.11.9 (Luo et al. 2025) and assembled into contigs using metaSPAdes v3.13.0 (Yang et al. 2025). Assembly gaps were closed by Sanger sequencing. The assembly quality was evaluated using SAMtools v1.9 (Jasper et al. 2022) and Sambamba v0.6.7 (Tarasov et al. 2015). Using the chloroplast genome of Protea caffra subsp. kilimandscharica (MH362765) (Ciren et al. 2022) as a reference, the assembled genome was annotated with CPGAVAS2 (http://47.96.249.172:16019/analyzer/annotate). Annotation refinement, genome visualization, and identification of gene features, including cis-splicing and trans-splicing genes, were performed using CPGView (http://47.96.249.172:16085/cpgview/view) (Shi et al. 2019). Simple sequence repeats (SSRs) were detected with MISA (https://webblast.ipk-gatersleben.de/misa/) (Beier et al. 2017), applying minimum repeat thresholds of 10, 6, 5, 5, and 5 for mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, and penta-nucleotides, respectively. The complete annotated chloroplast genome has been deposited in GenBank.

Phylogenetic analysis

The phylogenetic tree was constructed from 13 chloroplast genomes, including 5 chloroplast genome sequences representing the Proteaceae family and 8 species from the Platanaceae, Nelumbonaceae, and Sabiaceae families as outgroups. Phylogenomic analyses to determine the phylogenetic position of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ were conducted using maximum likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI) methods (Ronquist et al. 2012; Stamatakis 2014). Phylogenetic analyses were performed using OrthoFinder version 2.2.7 (Amaral et al. 2022). ML trees were generated with 1000 bootstrap replicates and visualized using ggtree version 1.14.6 (Yu 2020). BI analysis was performed under the GTR model with MrBayes v3.2.0 (Zhao et al. 2017), and the resulting tree was visualized in FigTree (Rambaut 2007). Additionally, 27 complete chloroplast genome sequences were aligned with MAFFT version 7.0 (Katoh and Standley 2013).

Results

Genome structure analysis

The complete chloroplast genome of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ exhibits a typical quadripartite structure, with a total length of 157,894 bp. It comprises a pair of inverted repeat (IR) regions, each 26,457 bp, separated by a large single-copy (LSC) region of 86,427 bp and a small single-copy (SSC) region of 18,553 bp (Figure 2). The overall GC content of the P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ chloroplast genome is 38.10%, while the corresponding values in the LSC, SSC, and IR regions are 36.30, 31.50, and 43.10%, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). In total, 130 genes were annotated in the chloroplast genome of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’, including 84 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 38 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes (Supplementary Table 2). Among them, 20 genes contain a single intron, including 10 PCGs (atpF, ndhA, ndhB (×2), petB, rpl16, rpl2, rpoC1, rps16, and accD) and 8 tRNA genes (trnA-UGC (×2), trnG-UCC, trnI-GAU (×2), trnK-UUU, trnL-UAA, and trnV-UAC), while two PCGs (ycf3 and clpP) contain two introns. The P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ chloroplast genome has 11 cis-spliced genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, accD, and ndhA) and one trans-spliced gene, rps12 (Supplementary Table 3, Supplementary Figure 1, and Supplementary Figure 2). In total, 59 SSRs were identified in the chloroplast genome of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’, including 55 mononucleotide and 4 dinucleotide repeats. The majority of these SSRs (55, accounting for 86.05%) consist of A/T bases. Of these, 45 SSRs are located in the LSC region, while 5 and 9 SSRs are found in the SSC and IR regions, respectively.

Phylogenetic analysis

To further investigate the phylogenetic position of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’, a phylogenetic tree was constructed using the ML method. P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ and P. caffra subsp. kilimandscharica form a highly supported (100) monophyletic clade within the genus Protea (Figure 3). Grevillea robusta and species of the genus Helicia (Helicia nilagirica and Helicia shweliensis) also form well-supported branches, indicating clear intergeneric divergence. These genera collectively constitute the core group of Proteaceae, corroborating the monophyly of Proteaceae. Proteaceae and Platanaceae are sister groups with a support value of 77, indicating a close relationship within the order Proteales. The members of the Nelumbonaceae family (e.g. Nelumbo lutea, Nelumbo nucifera), used as outgroups, show clear divergence from Proteaceae and Platanaceae, supporting their status as early-diverging lineages. The genera Sabia and Meliosma of the family Sabiaceae appear as more distant outgroups, further confirming the systematic position of Proteales within the eudicots. These chloroplast genome data will aid in the phylogenetic and population genetic diversity research of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’.

Discussion and conclusions

In this study, the complete chloroplast genome sequence of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ was sequenced for the first time. The genome exhibits a typical quadripartite structure, with a total length of 157,894 bp, comprising a pair of IR regions of 26,457 bp each, separated by an LSC region of 86,427 bp and an SSC region of 18,553 bp. The overall GC content of the complete chloroplast genome of P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ is 38.10%, with the corresponding values in the LSC, SSC, and IR regions being 36.30, 31.50, and 43.10%, respectively. A total of 130 genes—84 PCGs, 38 tRNA genes, and 8 tRNA genes—were annotated. These characteristics show no significant differences from those of other chloroplast genomes within the genus Protea. Phylogenetic analysis revealed that P. cynaroides ‘Red Rex’ forms a highly supported (100) monophyletic clade with P. caffra subsp. kilimandscharica. ML analysis effectively demonstrated their phylogenetic relationship, which is consistent with traditional taxonomic classifications. The genus Protea, a large and widely distributed group within the Proteaceae family, is taxonomically complex and serves as an excellent model system for understanding the genetic basis of adaptive radiation—particularly in response to extreme variations in rainfall and temperature (She et al. 2005; Porrello et al. 2024). Chloroplast genome analyses contribute to elucidating these relationships and support phylogenetic studies. The complete chloroplast genome sequence presented in this study provides fundamental insights into plastome evolution and facilitates phylogenetic inference and species identification within the genus Protea. In the future, increasing the number of available chloroplast genomes from Protea species will offer deeper insights into the evolution of this important family.

Performance Guarantees for Quantum Neural Estimation of Entropies
Sreejith Sreekumar 2, Ziv Goldfeld 3, Mark M. Wilde 3

Abstract
Estimating quantum entropies and divergences is an important problem in quantum physics, information theory, and machine learning. Quantum neural estimators (QNEs), which utilize a hybrid classical-quantum architecture, have recently emerged as an appealing computational framework for estimating these measures. Such estimators combine classical neural networks with parametrized quantum circuits, and their deployment typically entails tedious tuning of hyperparameters controlling the sample size, network architecture, and circuit topology. This work initiates the study of formal guarantees for QNEs of measured (Rényi) relative entropies in the form of non-asymptotic error risk bounds. We further establish exponential tail bounds showing that the error is sub-Gaussian, and thus sharply concentrates about the ground truth value. For an appropriate sub-class of density operator pairs on a space of dimension d d with bounded Thompson metric, our theory establishes a copy complexity of O ⁡ ( | Θ ⁡ ( 𝒰 ) | ​ d / ϵ 2 ) O(|\Theta(\mathcal{U})|d/\epsilon^{2}) for QNE with a quantum circuit parameter set Θ ⁡ ( 𝒰 ) \Theta(\mathcal{U}) , which has minimax optimal dependence on the accuracy ϵ \epsilon . Additionally, if the density operator pairs are permutation invariant, we improve the dimension dependence above to O ⁡ ( | Θ ⁡ ( 𝒰 ) | ​ polylog ​ ( d ) / ϵ 2 ) O(|\Theta(\mathcal{U})|\mathrm{polylog}(d)/\epsilon^{2}) . Our theory aims to facilitate principled implementation of QNEs for measured relative entropies and guide hyperparameter tuning in practice.

I-B

Summary of Contributions

In this paper, we provide the first theoretical guarantees for QNE in the form of sharp bounds on the expected absolute error. The bounds depend on the number of quantum state copies the algorithm uses, parameters defining the class of underlying density operators, and the classical-quantum circuit parameters. Furthermore, we also derive concentration inequalities for the absolute error about the ground truth, which leads to an upper bound on the copy complexity of QNE, i.e., the minimum number of copies of the quantum states required for estimating measured relative entropies.

Measured relative entropy was introduced in

The copy complexity of this approach is linear in the dimension of the state (or, equivalently, exponential in the number of qudits)

A possibly more efficient approach is to estimate the entropies directly, without estimating the underlying states. In this regard, several estimators have been proposed and their computational complexities investigated under different input models

. In particular,
[

provided algorithms for estimating the von Neumann entropy and Rényi entropies, given access to independent copies of the input quantum state, which achieves the copy complexity (sub-linear in dimension) for integer orders greater than one. Entropy estimation under the quantum query model, i.e., where one has access to an oracle that prepares the input quantum state, was explored in
[

In contrast to the estimators mentioned above, QNE proposed in
[

,

. Specifically, QNEs utilize a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm that extends the approach from

to the quantum framework. The appeal of such estimators stems from their scalability to high-dimensional problems and large datasets, as well as their compatibility with modern gradient-based optimization techniques. This makes them particularly relevant in the quantum context, where the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space scales exponentially in the number of qudits. Although QNE was shown to perform empirically well in
[

, theoretical performance guarantees are still lacking, which this work aims to advance upon. We note that variational methods have also been used in
[

[

[

In the framework of
[
29
]
for estimating measured relative entropies, the optimization is performed over Hermitian operators of the form
H(\theta,\phi)=U(\bm{\theta})\,\mathrm{diag}(\Lambda_{\phi})\,U(\bm{\theta})^{\dagger}
, where a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC)
U(\bm{\theta})
specifies the eigenvectors and a neural network defines the eigenvalues
\Lambda_{\phi}
. Although this hybrid parameterization effectively captures the optimization of the variational formula
\mathrm{Tr}[H\rho]+1-\mathrm{Tr}[e^{H}\sigma]
for measured relative entropy (and a related formula for measured Rényi relative entropy), it remains susceptible to barren plateaus
[
63
,
64
,
65
]
. In particular, if the PQC is sufficiently deep and unstructured,

approximates a unitary two-design, causing
\mathrm{Tr}[H\rho]
and
\mathrm{Tr}[e^{H}\sigma]
to concentrate around their means and the gradient variances to vanish exponentially in the number of qubits. This leads to vanishing gradients in the quantum parameters
\bm{\theta}
and, indirectly, to weakened gradient signals for the classical parameters
\phi
. Barren plateaus can be mitigated by employing shallow or symmetry-informed ansätze
[
66
,
67
]
, initializing
U(\bm{\theta})
near the identity
[
68
]
, or adopting layer-wise or hybrid training strategies that preserve gradient signal throughout optimization
[
69
]
. Importantly, despite its variational structure, the QNE framework is not susceptible to the classical-simulability arguments discussed in
[
70
]
, since it assumes direct access to quantum samples of the states
\rho
and
\sigma
rather than classical descriptions or measurement data. The learning task thereby exploits intrinsically quantum input data, placing it outside the regime of efficiently classically simulable models identified in
[
70
]
.

II-C

Minimax Estimation of Measured Relative Entropies

Consider the problem of estimating measured relative entropies
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M},\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
and
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M}}(\rho\|\sigma)
based on
n

\rho
and
\sigma
. We are interested in the decay rate of the QNE absolute error with
n
, and how it compares to the performance of an optimal algorithm for estimating entropies. It is well known from classical results that estimating entropies, in general, suffers from the so-called curse of dimensionality
[
73
]
, in that, the estimation error is
\Omega\big(n^{-\frac{1}{d}}\big)
. In other words, estimating entropies is hard in high dimensions without additional assumptions that restrict the class of density operators.

The performance of an estimator is quantified by the minimax risk, namely, the error of the best estimator uniformly over a class of density operators. Formally, the minimax risk for measured relative entropy over a class
\mathcal{S}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle R_{n}^{\star}(\mathcal{S})\coloneqq\inf_{\hat{\mathsf{D}}_{n}}\sup_{(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M}}(\rho\|\sigma)-\hat{\mathsf{D}}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n})\right|\right],

(11)

where the infimum is over all estimators
\hat{\mathsf{D}}_{n}
, i.e., a map
(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n})\mapsto\hat{\mathsf{D}}_{n}(\rho^{\otimes n},\sigma^{\otimes n})
, which is allowed to perform quantum operations (apply quantum channels, measurements, etc.) and the expectation is over any randomness resulting from such quantum operations. Note that the minimax risk is a property of the estimation setting—the best possible performance uniformly over the class—as opposed to the specific estimator. The minimax risk for measured Rényi relative entropy,
R_{n,\alpha}^{\star}(\mathcal{S})
, is defined analogously by replacing
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M}}(\rho\|\sigma)
with
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M},\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)
in (

).

II-E

Approximating Functions and Operators in Quantum Neural Estimation

At the core of our framework is the question of approximating the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of Hermitian operators. In order for this approximation to hold uniformly over a class of density operators of interest, we require the neural class and quantum circuit to be sufficiently expressive. On the other hand, the complexity of these approximating classes should be balanced so that the statistical component of the estimation error is minimized. To this end, we assume that the neural class satisfies the (mild) high-level assumptions stated below, which, in particular, are satisfied by feedforward neural nets with finite width and depth, bounded parameters and common continuous activations such as rectified linear (ReLU) and sigmoid. After stating these for the neural class, we provide some basic definitions for quantum circuits.

II-E
1
Classical Neural Networks

To handle the approximation error, we will employ bounds from approximation theory for neural networks. For this purpose, we embed the set
\{1,\ldots,d\}
indexing the eigenvalues as points into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and we consider the neural net approximation of a function whose values at these points are equal to the eigenvalues of
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
or
H_{\alpha}^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
. To account for a broad class of embeddings (an injective map) and classical neural nets, we formulate a general framework and state the assumptions under which our bounds hold. Later, we consider natural instances of embeddings and neural nets that satisfy these assumptions. To obtain a handle on the statistical error, we require that the neural class has bounded complexity, quantified in terms of the covering entropy (see e.g.,
[
79

Definition 1

(Covering entropy)
.

\displaystyle N(\epsilon,\Theta,\mathsf{d})\coloneqq\inf\left\{|\Theta^{\prime}|:\,\Theta^{\prime}\mbox{ is an }\epsilon\mbox{-covering of }\Theta\right\},

For a positive integer

, which we will simply denote by

(Assumptions on neural class)

The neural class

\displaystyle\log N(2\epsilon\log b,\mathcal{F},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty,\mathcal{X}})\leq\bar{\kappa}(\mathcal{F},\epsilon),\qquad\forall\epsilon\in(0,1],

where

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}(\mathcal{F},\epsilon)}\ d\epsilon<\infty.

(15b)

IV
Copy Complexity for Quantum Neural Estimation of Entropies

Here, we examine the copy complexity of QNE, focusing on measured relative entropy. The case of measured Rényi relative entropy is similar. As mentioned earlier, the number of copies of
\rho
and
\sigma
consumed by QNE in the worst case scales proportionally to
|\Theta(\delta,\mathcal{S})|
. Hence, it is of interest to understand how the number of parameters of efficient PQCs scales as
\delta
shrinks, since this dependence directly affects the copy complexity. To get more insight into this aspect, consider the setting where

d=2^{N}
, with
N
denoting the number of qubits. An application of the Solovay–Kitaev theorem (see Appendix
E
)
shows that, for all
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{d}\times\mathbb{S}_{d}
, there exists a PQC
\mathcal{U}(\delta,\mathcal{S})
with
|\Theta(\delta,\mathcal{S})|=O\left(\left(\frac{N^{2}4^{N}}{\delta}\right)^{N^{2}4^{N}}\right)
. The super-exponential dependence of
|\Theta(\delta,\mathcal{S})|
on
N
is a consequence of the requirement that the quantum circuit be able to universally approximate the whole unitary group uniformly. Moreover, when
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{S}_{d}\times\mathbb{S}_{d}
, a lower bound based on a covering argument shows that such a dependence on
N
is unavoidable (see, e.g.,
[
85
, Section 4.5.4]
).

Given that an exponential dependence on
N
is undesirable for practically realistic algorithms, it is of interest to determine conditions on
\mathcal{S}
under which
|\Theta(\delta,\mathcal{S})|
or the QNE copy complexity scales more efficiently, say polynomially or poly-logarithmically in
N
. The aforementioned determination is challenging for two reasons. First, the projectors
\{P_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{d}
appearing in the spectral decomposition of
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
, and consequently in the definition of
\mathcal{U}(\delta,\mathcal{S})
, do not have a closed-form expression.
Second, even if such a characterization is available, determining the sets
\mathcal{S}
for which (
17
) holds with an efficiently realizable PQC is known to be a hard problem. In fact, it is closely related to the problem of characterizing the classes of unitaries which can be efficiently approximated. Given this, a pertinent approach is to fix a PQC
\mathcal{U}
, as is the case in practice, and consider the classes
\mathcal{S}
for which efficient QNEs exist.

To this end, let us recall the necessary and sufficient condition that the optimizer
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)=\log\omega^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
should satisfy, which can be obtained as the limit
\alpha\to 1
of (
10
) (see Appendix
A
):

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ \left(\omega^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)+sI\right)^{-1}\rho\left(\omega^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)+sI\right)^{-1}ds.

Solving this equation analytically to determine
\omega^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
appears challenging. Nevertheless, we can leverage it to determine a class of density operator pairs over which our copy complexity results hold. For a given
\rho\in\mathbb{S}_{d}
, unitary
U
on
\mathbb{H}_{d}
and
b\geq 1
, let

\displaystyle\Omega\big(b,\rho,U\big)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}\rho\right]}{v_{i}}U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}:\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{v}\in(0,1]^{d},~\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}=1\mbox{ and }\\
&\frac{1}{b}\leq\frac{\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}\rho\right]}{v_{i}}\leq b,~\forall~i\in[1:d]\end{aligned}\right\}.

Setting
\sigma(\rho,\omega)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\ \left(\omega+sI\right)^{-1}\rho\left(\omega+sI\right)^{-1}ds
for
\omega\in\Omega\big(b,\rho,U\big)
, it is easy to see that
\sigma(\rho,\omega)\in\mathbb{S}_{d}
since
\rho\mapsto\sigma(\rho,\omega)
is a completely positive map and
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\sigma(\rho,\omega)\right]=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho\omega^{-1}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}=1
.
Hence, for a given PQC
\mathcal{U}
, define

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})\coloneqq\left\{(\rho,\sigma(\rho,\omega))\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(b):\begin{aligned} &\omega\in\Omega\big(b,\rho,U\big)\mbox{ for }U\mbox{ s.t. }\exists~U(\bm{\theta})\in\mathcal{U}\\
&\mbox{ satisfying }\left\|U-U(\bm{\theta})\right\|\leq\delta\end{aligned}\right\},

(33)

i.e.,
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})
is the class of density operator pairs within
\mathcal{S}_{d}(b)
such that the eigenbasis of the optimizer
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
(or
\omega^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
) can be approximated by the PQC
\mathcal{U}
within an accuracy of
\delta
. Given a PQC
\mathcal{U}
,
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})
can be determined in principle by computing
(\rho,\sigma(\rho,\omega))
pairs over
\omega\in\Omega\big(b,\rho,U\big)
as in (
33
) and ascertaining if the pair lies within
\mathcal{S}_{d}(b)
.

The next result provides a copy complexity bound for the QNE with a shallow net and a PQC
\mathcal{U}
with parameter set
\Theta(\mathcal{U})
.

Proposition 8

(Copy complexity of QNE)
.

Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and
b\geq 1
. With probability at least
p\in[0,1)
, QNE with shallow net
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathrm{SNN}}(d,\log b)
and PQC
\mathcal{U}
estimates
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M}}(\rho\|\sigma)
between
(\rho,\sigma)\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(\epsilon_{b},b,\mathcal{U})
within an error of
\epsilon
given
O\big(c_{b,p}^{2}d|\Theta(\mathcal{U})|\epsilon^{-2}\big)

copies each of
\rho
and
\sigma
, where
\epsilon_{b}=\epsilon/(4b+4\log b)
and
c_{b,p}\coloneqq\big(1+\sqrt{-\log\!\left(1-p\right)}\big)(b+1)\log b.

The proof of Proposition
8
follows immediately from Corollary
2
and can be found in Section
V-E
. An analogous claim for QNE with a deep net and a quantum circuit
\mathcal{U}
can be
obtained by using Proposition
4
in place of Corollary
2
, after setting
k=d-1
,
a=\big((3e)^{d}\log b\big)/(2\pi)
according to Proposition
3
(i)
.

Note that the copy complexity bound of QNE given in Proposition
8
is proportional to
d
, which scales exponentially in the number of qudits. We next show that a better copy complexity can be achieved by QNE when the states have additional symmetry, namely, permutation invariance. To state this result, let
d=m^{N}

for some
m,N\in\mathbb{N}
, and take the underlying Hilbert space as
\mathbb{H}_{d}=\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}
. Let
\mathsf{S}_{N}
denote the symmetric group on
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, and let
\mathsf{SU}_{d}
denote the special unitary group on
\mathbb{H}_{d}
. For a permutation
\pi\in\mathsf{S}_{N}
, define the permutation operator
V_{m}(\pi)
to be the unitary operator such that

\displaystyle V_{m}(\pi)|i_{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|i_{N}\rangle=|i_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|i_{\pi^{-1}(N)}\rangle,

(34)

for all
|i_{1}\rangle,\ldots,|i_{N}\rangle\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
.
A state
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
is permutation invariant if
V_{m}(\pi)\rho V_{m}(\pi)^{{\dagger}}=\rho
for all
\pi\in\mathsf{S}_{N}
. Let
\mathfrak{P}\big(\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}\big)
denote the class of all permutation-invariant states acting on
\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}
.

The symmetries within the class
\mathfrak{P}\big(\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}\big)
can be leveraged to construct a QNE whose copy complexity scales polynomially in
N
. The intuition for this improvement stems from the fact that the number of degrees of freedom of permutation-invariant operators acting on
\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}
scales polynomially in
N
. To utilize the underlying symmetries in the construction of QNE, we will rely on Schur–Weyl duality, which states that
\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}
can be written as (see, e.g.,
[
86
, Section 6.2]
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{d}=\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}=\bigoplus_{\kappa\in Y_{m}^{N}}\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\kappa},

(35)

where
\kappa
indexes the Young frames
Y_{m}^{N}
(with depth at most
m
and number of boxes
N
), and
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
(resp.
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
) denotes the corresponding irreducible space of
\mathsf{SU}_{d}
(resp.
\mathsf{S}_{N}
).
Let
\mathcal{K}_{m,N}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\kappa}\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
, and let
P_{\kappa}
denote the projection onto
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
. Consider the completely positive trace preserving (CPTP) map
\bar{\mathcal{N}}\colon\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}_{d})\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{m,N})
given by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)\coloneqq\sum_{\kappa}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}}[P_{\kappa}\rho P_{\kappa}],

(36)

where
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}}[\cdot]
denotes the partial trace with respect to
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
. The quantum channel
\bar{\mathcal{N}}
will be used below as a pre-processing channel for QNE, which can be realized by applying the Schur transform in conjunction with the partial trace operation.

For a PQC
\mathcal{U}
acting on
\mathcal{K}_{m,N}
, let
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{|\mathcal{K}_{m,N}|}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})
denote the set of density operator pairs on
\mathcal{K}_{m,N}
as defined by (
33
), where
|\mathcal{K}_{m,N}|
denotes the dimension of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}_{m,N}
.
With
\bar{P}_{\kappa}
denoting the projection onto
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
, let
\bar{\mathcal{M}}\colon\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{m,N})\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}_{d})
denote the CPTP map defined as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{\rho})=\sum_{\kappa}\bar{P}_{\kappa}\bar{\rho}\bar{P}_{\kappa}\otimes\pi_{\kappa},

(37)

where
\pi_{\kappa}=I/|\mathcal{V}_{k}|
denotes the maximally mixed state on
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
.
Consider the following class of density operator pairs on
\mathbb{H}_{m}^{\otimes N}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{m,N}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\big(\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{\rho}),\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{\sigma})\big):\big(\bar{\rho},\bar{\sigma}\big)\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{|\mathcal{K}_{m,N}|}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})\right\}.

The next proposition shows that QNE achieves a polynomial (in the number of qudits) copy complexity for density operator pairs within
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{m,N}(\delta,b,\mathcal{U})
.

Proposition 9

(Copy complexity with permutation invariant states)
.

Let
\epsilon\geq 0
and

. With probability at least

, QNE with
\mathcal{N}=\bar{\mathcal{N}}
as given in (

), shallow neural net

VI
Concluding Remarks

We studied the performance of the quantum neural estimator proposed in
[
29
]
for estimating measured relative entropy and its Rényi analogue. We obtained upper bounds on its expected absolute error and showed exponential deviation inequalities under regularity conditions on the underlying states, the neural class, and the parametrized quantum circuit. We then specialized our bounds to shallow and deep neural nets and derived concrete bounds using known results from the approximation theory of neural nets along with bounds on the covering entropy of the associated neural classes. We also established that, although the quantum neural estimator has a worst case copy complexity that scales exponentially in the number of qudits, this improves to polynomial when the underlying states are permutation invariant.

Several open questions remain in the context of quantum neural estimation of entropies. One such issue pertains to the quantification of the optimization error and an analysis of optimization dynamics such as the investigation of barren plateaus and local optima, which was neglected here. This would call for an in-depth study of the hybrid optimization landscape induced by the neural net and quantum circuit, and an analysis of specific parameterizations or architectures that lead to relatively fast convergence of the algorithm. Also relevant is the question of estimating the quantum relative entropy and its Rényi generalizations such as the sandwiched and Petz–Rényi relative entropies using quantum neural estimators. Recent progress on estimating quantum relative entropy has been reported in
[
28
]
.

\displaystyle\left\|p_{k,f}-f\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\leq\left(2\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip},[0,1]}+6\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\right)k^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

When
k=1
, we obtain using (
76
) with
\ell=0
that

\displaystyle\left\|p_{k,f}-f\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\leq\left\|p_{k,f}\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}+\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,[0,1]}.

Hence, (
77
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
thus completing the proof.

Appendix D
Proof of Lemma
10

Consider the case
\alpha=1
.
As shown in
[
34
]
, for
\rho,\sigma>0
, the optimizer (Hermitian operator)
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
of (
2
) has the form

\displaystyle H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{\star}P_{i}^{\star},\quad\mbox{ with }\lambda_{i}^{\star}\coloneqq\log\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\rho]}{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\sigma]}\right),

where
\{P_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{d}
is some set of orthogonal rank-one projectors, so that

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{M}}(\rho\|\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}[H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)\rho]-\operatorname{Tr}[e^{H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)}\sigma]+1=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\rho]\log\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\rho]}{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\sigma]}\right).

Hence, the set of eigenvalues of
H^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)
is

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)=\{\lambda_{i}^{\star}\}_{i=1}^{d}=\left\{\log\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\rho]}{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i}^{\star}\sigma]}\right)\right\}_{i=1}^{d}.

To obtain an upper bound on
\left|\lambda_{i}^{\star}\right|
, we will use the variational characterization of max-relative entropy given in
[
40
, Lemma A.4]
) as stated below:

\displaystyle D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\rho\leq e^{\lambda}\sigma\right\}=\sup\left\{\log\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[M\rho]}{\operatorname{Tr}[M\sigma]}\right):0\leq M\leq I\right\}.

From the last equation above, for every projector
P
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[\rho P]}{\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma P]}\leq e^{D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)}=\inf\left\{e^{\lambda}:\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq e^{\lambda}I\right\}=\left\|\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma P]}{\operatorname{Tr}[\rho P]}\leq e^{D_{\max}(\sigma\|\rho)}=\left\|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|.

Since
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathcal{S}_{d}(b)
,
we have for every (rank-one) projector
P
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b}\leq\frac{1}{\left\|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|}\leq\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[\rho P]}{\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma P]}\leq\left\|\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|\leq b.

Taking logarithms, we obtain the desired claim for
\alpha=1
. The claim for
0<\alpha\neq 1
follows similarly by noting that
H_{\alpha}^{\star}(\rho,\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i,\alpha}^{\star}P_{i,\alpha}^{\star}
with
\lambda_{i,\alpha}^{\star}\coloneqq\log\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i,\alpha}^{\star}\rho]}{\operatorname{Tr}[P_{i,\alpha}^{\star}\sigma]}\right)
.

Appendix E
Solovay-Kitaev Theorem: Bound on Quantum Circuit Parameter Size

Let
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
be an arbitrary set of orthogonal projectors on
\mathbb{H}_{d}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}P_{i}=I
. Let
U
be a unitary such that
P_{i}=U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}
.
Note that the determinant of
U
,
\det(U)=e^{i\omega}
, for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, for
\tilde{U}=e^{-i\omega/d}U
, we have
\det(\tilde{U})=1
. Moreover,
\tilde{U}|i\rangle\!\langle i|\tilde{U}^{{\dagger}}=U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}=P_{i}
. This implies that
U
satisfying
P_{i}=U|i\rangle\!\langle i|U^{{\dagger}}
can be restricted (without loss of generality) to belong to the special unitary group
\mathsf{SU}(d)
, i.e., the subset of unitary operators on
\mathbb{H}_{d}
with determinant one.
Assume
d=2^{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the universal gate set
\mathrm{Univ}
composed of the Clifford group, i.e., the Hadamard gate,
S
gate, and CNOT gate, along with the
T
gate.
Then, it is known from
[
94
]
that, for every
U\in\mathsf{SU}(d)
acting on
N
qubits, there exists a quantum circuit
\bar{U}
constructed by composition of at most
\ell(d,\delta)=O\big(N^{2}4^{N}\log(N^{2}4^{N}/\delta)\big)
gates selected from
\mathrm{Univ}
such that
\big\|U-\bar{U}\big\|\leq\delta
.
Since the number of possible configurations of quantum circuit composed of
\ell
gates selected from
\mathrm{Univ}
is
4^{\ell(d,\delta)}
, we obtain that there exists
\mathcal{U}(\delta,\mathcal{S})
with
|\mathcal{U}(\delta,\mathcal{S})|\leq 4^{\ell(d,\delta)}
such that (
17
) is satisfied. Similar claims can also be obtained for qudits, where
d=m^{N}
for some positive integers
m,N
, by using a generalization of the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm to qudits as given in
[
95
]
.

Appendix F
Proof of Lemma
11

\displaystyle\left\|H_{1}-H_{2}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|U_{1}\Lambda_{1}U_{1}^{{\dagger}}-U_{2}\Lambda_{2}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\left\|U_{1}\Lambda_{1}U_{1}^{{\dagger}}-U_{1}\Lambda_{1}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|+\left\|U_{1}\Lambda_{1}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}-U_{1}\Lambda_{2}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|+\left\|U_{1}\Lambda_{2}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}-U_{2}\Lambda_{2}U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\left\|U_{1}\right\|\left\|\Lambda_{1}\right\|\left\|U_{1}^{{\dagger}}-U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|+\left\|U_{1}\right\|\left\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\right\|\left\|U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|+\left\|U_{1}-U_{2}\right\|\left\|\Lambda_{2}\right\|\left\|U_{2}^{{\dagger}}\right\|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\big(\left\|\Lambda_{1}\right\|+\left\|\Lambda_{2}\right\|\big)\left\|U_{1}-U_{2}\right\|+\left\|\Lambda_{1}-\Lambda_{2}\right\|,

where
(a)
is via triangle inequality for Schatten norms,
(b)
is due to sub-multiplicativity of Schatten norms, and
(c)
is because
\left\|U\right\|=\left\|U^{{\dagger}}\right\|=1
for a unitary
U
.

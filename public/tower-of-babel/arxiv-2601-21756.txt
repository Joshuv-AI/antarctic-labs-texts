The Classification of Supersingular Elliptic Curves in characteristic 3
Alexey Orlov

Abstract
. We provide an explicit classification of supersingular elliptic curves in characteristic 3 into isomorphism classes, and give explicit formulae for their point counts.

1.
Canonical Form

We fix
q=3^{d}
and consider an elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}.

Completing the square via the substitution

y\mapsto\frac{y-a_{1}x-a_{3}}{2},

we obtain

y^{2}=4x^{3}+b_{2}x^{2}+2b_{4}x+b_{6},

where, as usual,

\begin{split}b_{2}&=a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2},\\
b_{4}&=2a_{4}+a_{1}a_{3},\\
b_{6}&=a_{3}^{2}+4a_{6}.\end{split}

In characteristic
3
, this simplifies to

y^{2}=x^{3}+b_{2}x^{2}-b_{4}x+b_{6},

with

j=\frac{(b_{2}^{2}-24b_{4})^{3}}{\Delta}=\frac{b_{2}^{6}}{\Delta}.

For supersingular curves in characteristic
3
, we have
j=0
(see, e.g.,
[
3
, Ex. V.4.5]
) and hence
b_{2}=0
. We thus obtain the canonical form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6},

with discriminant
\Delta=-a_{4}^{3}\neq 0
, so that
a_{4}\neq 0
.

We now determine when two curves of this form are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; for the general transformation formulae, see
[
3
, Table 3.1]
. Consider two curves

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6},\qquad E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}^{\prime}x+a_{6}^{\prime}.

Since
a_{1}=a_{1}^{\prime}=0
, the equation

ua_{1}^{\prime}=a_{1}+2s

yields
s=0
. Similarly, since
a_{3}=a_{3}^{\prime}=0
, the equation

u^{3}a_{3}^{\prime}=a_{3}+ra_{1}+2t

yields
t=0
. Thus the general form of an isomorphism is
(x,y)=(u^{2}x^{\prime}+r,u^{3}y^{\prime})
with
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

(1.1)

\begin{split}u^{4}a_{4}^{\prime}&=a_{4}\\
u^{6}a_{6}^{\prime}&=a_{6}+ra_{4}+r^{3}\end{split}

1.1.
Notation

We fix the following notation throughout.

When
d
is even,
-1
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(see Section
2.2
); we denote its square roots by
\pm\tau
. We fix an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha)=1
. Finally we fix a primitive root
\beta
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

2.
Classification of Supersingular Curve Types

2.1.
d
odd

When
d
is odd, since
\gcd(q-1,4)=2
, the polynomial
x^{4}-1
has exactly two roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, namely
\pm 1
. In particular,
-1
is not a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Moreover, the restriction of the map
x\mapsto x^{4}
to
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
has trivial kernel, hence it is injective and thus surjective, since
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
is finite. That is, for odd
d
, an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is a square if and only if it is a fourth power. Since
-1
is not a square, exactly one of
a_{4}
and
-a_{4}
is a square, and hence a fourth power.

We thus consider two types of curves:

•

Type I:
-a_{4}
is a fourth power.

•

Type
\textrm{I}^{+}
:
a_{4}
is a fourth power.

2.2.
d
even

When
d
is even, we have
q\equiv 1\pmod{8}
. Since
-1=\beta^{(q-1)/2}
, we conclude that
-1
is a fourth power. The cosets of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
are

(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4},\beta(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4},\beta^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4},\beta^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}.

Since
-1
is a fourth power, both
a_{4}
and
-a_{4}
lie in the same coset. We thus consider three curve types:

•

Type I:
-a_{4}
is a fourth power.

•

Type II:
-a_{4}
is a square but not a fourth power.

•

Type III:
-a_{4}
is not a square.

2.3.
Type I Curves

Suppose that
-a_{4}=v^{4}
for some
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Applying the substitution
(x,y)\mapsto(v^{-2}x,v^{-3}y)
, we obtain the equation

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b,

with
b=a_{6}v^{-6}
. Suppose

E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}^{\prime}x+a_{6}^{\prime}

is isomorphic to
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}u^{4}a_{4}^{\prime}&=-1\\
u^{6}a_{6}^{\prime}&=b+r^{3}+r\end{split}

Hence
-a_{4}^{\prime}=u^{-4}
, which is a fourth power in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, so
E^{\prime}
is also of type I.

Suppose that two curves

E_{b}:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b,\textnormal{ and }E_{b^{\prime}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b^{\prime}

are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}u^{4}&=1\\
r^{3}-r+(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})&=0\end{split}

The second equation is an Artin–Schreier equation in
r
, which has a solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if

\operatorname{Tr}(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})=\operatorname{Tr}(b-u^{2}b^{\prime})=0,

by the additive Hilbert Theorem 90 (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Prop. 3.1.25]
).

When
d
is odd, we have seen that
u^{4}=1
implies
u^{2}=1
. Hence the isomorphism class of
E_{b}
depends only on
\operatorname{Tr}(b)\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
, giving exactly three isomorphism classes. Moreover, since the trace is
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-linear, we have
\operatorname{Tr}(-b)=-\operatorname{Tr}(b)
. Thus for
b
with non-zero trace,
E_{b}
is the quadratic twist of
E_{-b}
by
-1
. The classification is then as follows:

(1)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b)=0
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{0}
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b)=1
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{\alpha}
.

(3)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b)=-1
, then
E_{b}
is a quadratic twist of
E_{\alpha}
.

When
d
is even,
-1
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
E_{b}
and
E_{-b}
are isomorphic (take
u=\tau
in (
1.1
)). Hence there are exactly two isomorphism classes:

(1)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b)=0
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{0}
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b)\neq 0
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{\alpha}
.

2.4.
Type
\textrm{I}^{+}
Curves

By an analogous argument, every curve of Type
\textrm{I}^{+}
is isomorphic to

E^{+}:y^{2}=x^{3}+x+b.

Suppose that two curves

E_{b}:y^{2}=x^{3}+x+b,\textnormal{ and }E_{b^{\prime}}:y^{2}=x^{3}+x+b^{\prime}

are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}u^{4}&=1\\
r^{3}+r+(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})&=0\end{split}

Since
d
is odd,
-1
is not a square, thus the map
x\mapsto x^{3}+x
has trivial kernel and gives a bijection on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus a solution
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
exists for any
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore all Type
\textrm{I}^{+}
curves are isomorphic to

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+x.

2.5.
Type II Curves

As we will see in the Section
3.3
, every elliptic curve of Type II is a quadratic twist of the Type I curve. For point counting, the classification is not needed, but we include it here for completeness.

For Type II curves, we have
a_{4}\in\beta^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
. There exists
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
such that
u^{4}=\beta^{2}(-a_{4})^{-1}
. Applying the substitution
(x,y)\mapsto(u^{2}x,u^{3}y)
, we obtain

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{2}x+b

with
b=u^{6}a_{6}
. From (
1.1
), any elliptic curve isomorphic to
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is also of Type II.

Suppose that two curves

E_{b}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{2}x+b,\textnormal{ and }E_{b^{\prime}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{2}x+b^{\prime}

are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}u^{4}&=1\\
r^{3}-\beta^{2}r+(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})&=0\\
\end{split}

Thus
u\in\{\pm 1,\pm\tau\}
, so
u^{6}=u^{2}=\pm 1
, and the second equation becomes
r^{3}-\beta^{2}r+(b\pm b^{\prime})=0
. Substituting
r=\beta z
we obtain

z^{3}-z+\frac{b\pm b^{\prime}}{\beta^{3}}=0.

This is an Artin–Schreier equation, which has a solution in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if the trace of the constant term vanishes, which is equivalent to
\operatorname{Tr}(b\beta^{-3})=\pm\operatorname{Tr}(b^{\prime}\beta^{-3})
. Hence there are exactly two isomorphism classes:

(1)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b\beta^{-3})=0
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{0}
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Tr}(b\beta^{-3})\neq 0
, then
E_{b}
is isomorphic to
E_{\alpha\beta^{3}}
.

2.6.
Type III Curves

For Type III curves, we have
a_{4}\in\beta(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}\cup\beta^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
. We will distinguish these two cases.

2.6.1.
Type IIIa Curves

These are the curves with
a_{4}\in\beta(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
. There exists
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
such that
u^{4}=\beta(-a_{4})^{-1}
. Applying the substitution
(x,y)\mapsto(u^{2}x,u^{3}y)
, we obtain

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x+b

with
b=u^{6}a_{6}
. From (
1.1
), any elliptic curve isomorphic to
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is also of Type IIIa.

Suppose that two curves

E_{b}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x+b,\textnormal{ and }E_{b^{\prime}}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x+b^{\prime}

are isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By (
1.1
), we have

\begin{split}u^{4}&=1\\
r^{3}-\beta r+(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})&=0\\
\end{split}

Thus
u\in\{\pm 1,\pm\tau\}
, so
u^{6}=u^{2}=\pm 1
, and the second equation becomes
r^{3}-\beta r+(b\pm b^{\prime})=0
. Since
\beta
is a primitive root, and we are in odd characteristic,
\beta
is not a square, hence the map
x\mapsto x^{3}-\beta x
has trivial kernel giving a bijection on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence a solution
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
always exists, and all Type IIIa curves are isomorphic to
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x
.

2.6.2.
Type IIIb Curves

These are the curves with
a_{4}\in\beta^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
. By an analogous argument, we obtain the canonical form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{3}x+b.

As before, since
\beta^{3}
is not a square, the map
x\mapsto x^{3}-\beta^{3}x
has trivial kernel. Thus
r^{3}-\beta^{3}r+(b-u^{6}b^{\prime})=0
always has a solution, hence all Type IIIb curves are isomorphic to
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{3}x
.

3.
Counting Points

We recall the following standard formula. For an elliptic curve
E:y^{2}=f(x)
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of odd characteristic, we have

(3.1)

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(f(x)),

where
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
denotes the quadratic character, extended by
\chi(0)=0
.

3.1.
Type I Curves

Recall that every curve of type I is isomorphic to one of the form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b.

To count points on it, we require the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q=3^{d}
. For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{3}
, define

S_{d}(a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\operatorname{Tr}(x)=a\end{subarray}}\chi(x).

For
d
odd,

S_{d}(a)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }a=0,\\
(-1)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}3^{\frac{d-1}{2}}&\text{if }a=1,\\
(-1)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}3^{\frac{d-1}{2}}&\text{if }a=-1.\end{cases}

For
d
even,

S_{d}(a)=\begin{cases}2\cdot(-1)^{\frac{d+2}{2}}3^{\frac{d-2}{2}}&\text{if }a=0,\\
(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}3^{\frac{d-2}{2}}&\text{if }a=\pm 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{3}=e^{2\pi i/3}
be the primitive cube root of unity, and define the indicator function

g_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{3}}\zeta_{3}^{k(\operatorname{Tr}(x)-a)}.

By orthogonality of additive characters, this function equals one when
\operatorname{Tr}(x)=a
and zero otherwise. Hence

S_{d}(a)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{3}}\zeta_{3}^{k(\operatorname{Tr}(x)-a)}.

Interchanging the order of summation yields

S_{d}(a)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{3}}\zeta_{3}^{-ka}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x)\zeta_{3}^{k\operatorname{Tr}(x)}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{3}}\zeta_{3}^{-ka}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x)\zeta_{3}^{\operatorname{Tr}(kx)},

where the last equality is by the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-linearity of trace.

For
k=0
, the inner sum vanishes. For
k\neq 0
, the substitution
y=kx
gives

\chi(k)^{-1}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(y)\zeta_{3}^{\operatorname{Tr}(y)}=\chi(k)G(\chi),

where
G(\chi)
is the Gauss sum associated to the canonical additive character. Thus

(3.2)

S_{d}(a)=\frac{1}{3}G(\chi)\left(\zeta_{3}^{-a}+\zeta_{3}^{a}\chi(-1)\right).

By a classical result on Gauss sums (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Th. 5.15]
), we have

G(\chi)=(-1)^{d-1}i^{d}\sqrt{q}.

For
d
odd, we have
\chi(-1)=-1
, and (
3.2
) becomes

S_{d}(a)=\frac{1}{3}i^{d}\sqrt{q}(\zeta_{3}^{-a}-\zeta_{3}^{a}).

We have
S_{d}(0)=0
and
S_{d}(-1)=-S_{d}(1)
. We compute

S_{d}(1)=-\frac{1}{3}i^{d+1}\sqrt{q}\sqrt{3}=(-1)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}3^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

For
d
even, we have
\chi(-1)=1
, and (
3.2
) becomes

S_{d}(a)=-\frac{1}{3}i^{d}\sqrt{q}(\zeta_{3}^{-a}+\zeta_{3}^{a}).

We compute

S_{d}(0)=-\frac{2}{3}i^{d}\sqrt{q}=2\cdot(-1)^{\frac{d+2}{2}}3^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

and

S_{d}(1)=S_{d}(-1)=\frac{1}{3}i^{d}\sqrt{q}=(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}3^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

∎

We now apply Lemma
3.1
to count points on Type I curves. We need to compute
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x^{3}-x-b)
. The kernel of the Artin–Schreier map
x\mapsto x^{3}-x
is
\mathbb{F}_{3}
, and its image is the set

H=\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\operatorname{Tr}(y)=0\}

consisting of elements with trace zero. Thus

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x^{3}-x+b)=3\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\operatorname{Tr}(y)=0\end{subarray}}\chi(y+b)=3\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
\operatorname{Tr}(y)=\operatorname{Tr}(b)\end{subarray}}\chi(y).

Hence

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1+3S_{d}(\operatorname{Tr}(b)).

We can now write the formulae explicitly.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
q=3^{d}
and consider an elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b.

For
d
odd,

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b)=0,\\
q+1+(-1)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b)=1,\\
q+1-(-1)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b)=-1.\end{cases}

For
d
even,

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1-(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b)=0,\\
q+1+(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b)=\pm 1.\end{cases}

3.2.
Type
\textrm{I}^{+}
Curves

Recall that every curve of type
\textrm{I}^{+}
is isomorphic to one of the form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+x,

which occurs only when
d
is odd. As shown above, the map
x\mapsto x^{3}+x
is a bijection on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, hence

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x^{3}+x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(y)=0,

and we obtain

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
q=3^{d}
with
d
odd, and consider an elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}=x^{3}+x.

Then
\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1
.

3.3.
Type II Curves

For Type II curves, we have
-a_{4}\in\beta^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{4}
. Write
-a_{4}=\gamma^{2}
. We see that

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+b

is a quadratic twist by
\gamma
of the type I curve

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x+b\gamma^{-3}.

From Proposition
3.2
we immediately obtain

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
q=3^{d}
with
d
even, and consider an elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+b

with
-a_{4}=\gamma^{2}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
not a square. Then

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1+(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b\gamma^{-3})=0,\\
q+1-(-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(b\gamma^{-3})=\pm 1.\end{cases}

3.4.
Type III Curves

Recall that every curve of Type IIIa is isomorphic to one of the form

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x.

As shown above,
x\mapsto x^{3}-\beta x
is a bijection, hence

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x^{3}-\beta x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(y)=0.

Similarly, every Type IIIb curve is isomorphic to

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{3}x.

Since
x\mapsto x^{3}-\beta^{3}x
is also a bijection,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(x^{3}-\beta^{3}x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(y)=0.

We therefore obtain

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
q=3^{d}
with
d
even, and let
\beta
be the fixed primitive root of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Consider elliptic curves

E/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta x,\textnormal{ and }E^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-\beta^{3}x.

Then

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\#E^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1.

4.
Algorithm for Counting Points on Supersingular Curves in Characteristic 3

Let
q=3^{d}
, and let

E:y^{2}=x^{3}+a_{4}x+a_{6}

be a supersingular elliptic curve.

d
odd.

If
-a_{4}=v^{4}
for some
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, then

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}v^{-6})=0,\\
q+1+\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}v^{-6})=1,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
q+1-\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}v^{-6})=1,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 3\pmod{4},\\
q+1-\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}v^{-6})=-1,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
q+1+\sqrt{3q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}v^{-6})=-1,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Otherwise,
\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1
.

d
even.

If
-a_{4}=\gamma^{2}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and
\gamma
itself is square, we have

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1-2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})=0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
q+1+2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})=0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 2\pmod{4},\\
q+1+\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})\neq 0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
q+1-\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})\neq 0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 2\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

If
-a_{4}=\gamma^{2}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and
\gamma
is not a square, we have

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\begin{cases}q+1+2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})=0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
q+1-2\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})=0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 2\pmod{4},\\
q+1-\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})\neq 0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 0\pmod{4},\\
q+1+\sqrt{q}&\text{if }\operatorname{Tr}(a_{6}\gamma^{-3})\neq 0,\textnormal{ and }d\equiv 2\pmod{4};\end{cases}

Finally, if
-a_{4}
is not a square, then
\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1
.

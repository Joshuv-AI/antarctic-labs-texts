Clinical and Radiographic Assessment of Injectable Platelet-Rich Fibrin in the Treatment of Intrabony Defects
Authors: E. Amuthavalli, Balamurugan Mani, Jareen Abdul Jafeer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Injectable platelet-rich fibrin (i-PRF) has emerged as a bioactive material in periodontal regeneration due to its rich content of growth factors and ease of application. However, its clinical efficacy in managing intrabony defects remains underexplored.Objective:To evaluate the clinical and radiographic outcomes of intrabony defect treatment using i-PRF in comparison with open flap debridement (OFD) alone.Materials and Methods:This randomized controlled clinical trial included 40 patients with chronic periodontitis presenting with intrabony defects, divided into two groups: Group A (OFD + i-PRF) and Group B (OFD only). Clinical parameters including probing pocket depth (PPD), clinical attachment level (CAL), and radiographic defect depth were measured at baseline and 6 months postoperatively. Intraoral periapical radiographs with a customized grid and paralleling technique were used for radiographic evaluation. Statistical analysis was performed using paired and unpaired t-tests with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.Results:At 6 months, Group A showed significantly greater PPD reduction (3.46 ± 0.62 mm) compared with Group B (2.12 ± 0.54 mm, P < 0.001). CAL gain was also significantly higher in Group A (3.12 ± 0.58 mm) versus Group B (1.78 ± 0.61 mm, P < 0.001). Radiographic bone fill was 58.4% ± 9.7% in Group A compared with 34.6% ± 8.3% in Group B (P < 0.001).Conclusion:The adjunctive use of injectable PRF with OFD demonstrated superior clinical and radiographic improvements in the treatment of intrabony defects compared to OFD alone, suggesting its promising role in periodontal regeneration.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal disease is characterized by progressive destruction of the supporting structures of the teeth, often leading to intrabony defects that compromise dental function and esthetics. Regenerative periodontal therapy aims to restore these lost structures using various grafts, membranes, and biologic agents. Among these, autologous platelet concentrates such as platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) have gained traction for their regenerative capabilities and lack of immunogenicity.[1]

Injectable PRF (i-PRF), a liquid formulation of PRF, offers a minimally invasive and moldable alternative with a higher concentration of leukocytes and growth factors such as PDGF, TGF-β, and VEGF. These components have been associated with enhanced neovascularization, fibroblast proliferation, and osteoblastic differentiation.[23] Recent studies have demonstrated that i-PRF accelerates soft and hard tissue healing in periodontal and implant procedures.[45]

Despite these promising attributes, few clinical trials have focused on the regenerative potential of i-PRF in intrabony periodontal defects. Traditional PRF requires a solid form placement, which may not completely adapt to complex bony geometries, limiting its regenerative efficacy. Injectable PRF, in contrast, can be better integrated into the defect site due to its fluid nature, potentially enhancing clot stability and wound healing.[6]

Therefore, this study was designed to evaluate the clinical and radiographic outcomes of injectable PRF in the management of intrabony defects. The aim was to compare its efficacy when used as an adjunct to open flap debridement (OFD) against OFD alone.

Surgical protocol

Under local anesthesia, crevicular incisions were made and full-thickness flaps elevated. Granulation tissue was removed and thorough debridement performed. In Group A, 2 mL of freshly prepared i-PRF (centrifuged at 700 rpm for 3 minutes) was injected into the intrabony defect before flap closure. In both groups, flaps were sutured with 4-0 silk, and patients received antibiotics and analgesics for 5 days.

Postoperative care and follow-up

Patients were instructed to rinse with 0.12% chlorhexidine twice daily for 2 weeks and were re-evaluated at 1 week, 3 months, and 6 months.

Clinical and radiographic assessment

PPD and CAL were measured at baseline and 6 months using a UNC-15 probe. Standardized radiographs were obtained using a long-cone paralleling technique and analyzed for bone fill using ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v25.0. Paired and unpaired t-tests were used for intragroup and intergroup comparisons, respectively. Significance was set at P < 0.05.

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated the clinical and radiographic outcomes of i-PRF in the treatment of intrabony periodontal defects and demonstrated significantly superior results compared with OFD alone. These findings align with earlier evidence on the regenerative potential of PRF derivatives.[7]

The observed improvement in clinical parameters such as PPD reduction and CAL gain in the i-PRF group may be attributed to its high concentration of leukocytes and growth factors that modulate inflammation and promote tissue regeneration.[8] A randomized trial by Kobayashi et al.[9] showed increased fibroblast proliferation with i-PRF, supporting its wound healing potential.

Radiographically, the enhanced bone fill in the i-PRF group corroborates the findings of Miron et al.,[10] who demonstrated improved osseous regeneration with injectable formulations due to better defect adaptability. Moreover, i-PRF’s injectable nature may have facilitated uniform distribution and integration within the bony architecture, enhancing osteoconduction.

Unlike solid PRF, which may shrink or be displaced from the defect site, i-PRF forms a dynamic fibrin scaffold that gradually releases cytokines, fostering sustained regenerative activity.[11]

Nonetheless, the limitations of this study include a relatively short follow-up and limited sample size. Further multicentric studies with histologic validation are needed to confirm these outcomes.

CONCLUSION

Injectable PRF, when used in conjunction with open flap debridement, significantly enhances clinical and radiographic outcomes in the treatment of intrabony periodontal defects. Its ease of application and biologic advantages make it a valuable adjunct in regenerative periodontal therapy.

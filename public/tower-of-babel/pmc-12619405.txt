Association between multimorbidity and disability among elderly with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in Shanghai, China: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Xin Liu, Yifan Cao, Ying Shi, Hansheng Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn the elderly population with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), the prevalence of multimorbidity and functional disability is notable, yet the link between these conditions remains somewhat elusive. This investigation seeks to delineate the patterns of multimorbidity and assess their association with functional disability among older COPD sufferers.MethodsUtilizing data from the 2023 Unified Needs Assessment Form for Older Adults Care Questionnaire, this research incorporated a latent class analysis (LCA) to categorize multimorbidity patterns in elderly patients afflicted with COPD. These individuals also undertook assessments for activities of daily living (ADL) and instrumental activities of daily living (IADL). Logistic regression analyses were employed to evaluate how the counts and types of multimorbidity associated with functional disabilities.ResultsAmong 7676 identified COPD patients (mean age: 81.67 ± 8.10 years; 64.75% male), 7074 (92.16%) exhibited multimorbidity. ADL disabilities were found in 70.19% of the cases, whereas IADL disabilities were present in 94.80%. The analysis identified four distinct multimorbidity patterns: metabolic/cardiovascular (63.30%), cardiorespiratory (10.84%), osteoarticular (20.13%), and multisystem involvement (5.73%). All these patterns were significantly linked to increased odds of ADL and IADL disabilities (odds ratio [OR] 1.55, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.29–1.86; OR 2.03, 95%CI = 1.76–2.35; OR 1.35, 95%CI = 1.25–1.45 ; OR 1.81, 95%CI = 1.62–2.02) and IADL disability (OR 1.89, 95%CI = 1.38–2.58; OR 2.43, 95%CI = 1.74–3.38; OR 1.25, 95%CI = 1.11–1.42; OR1.35, 95%CI = 1.13–1.61).ConclusionsThe findings highlight a high incidence of multimorbidity and functional disabilities among elderly COPD patients in Shanghai, underscoring a significant association between multimorbidity in COPD and increased disability levels. The adoption of patient-centered approaches over traditional disease-centered models in clinical and health management practices may mitigate the impacts of multimorbidity and decrease the risk associated with ADL and IADL disabilities.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-025-06488-2.Keywords: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, Elderly, Multimorbidity, Disability, Latent class analysis

Introduction

COPD emerges as a prevalent respiratory ailment in the elderly, characterized by significant morbidity and mortality, which profoundly impairs patient life quality. In 2019, data revealed that more than 15.8% of individuals aged over 60 worldwide were affected by COPD, with prevalence rates escalating with advancing age [1]. According to criteria set by spirometry for COPD, the incidence in China for individuals aged 60–69 was reported at 21.2%, soaring to 35.5% among those older than 70 years [2]. Ranking as the third leading cause of death nationally [3], and the seventh in terms of disability-adjusted life years globally [4]. COPD accounted for 74.4 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) worldwide in 2019, corresponding to an age-standardized rate of 926.1 per 100,000 population, representing a 39.8% reduction relative to 1990 [5]. COPD patients are statistically more likely to experience multiple comorbidities [6, 7], such as cardiovascular and cerebrovascular conditions, asthma, bronchiectasis, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, osteoporosis, and anxiety [8–10].

Multimorbidity is defined as the simultaneous presence of at least two chronic conditions [11].This encompasses a range of disorders, including physical or psychiatric disorders (e.g., diabetes mellitus, schizophrenia), persistent functional impairments (e.g., learning disabilities), symptomatic syndromes (e.g., frailty or chronic pain), sensory deficits (e.g., vision or hearing loss), and substance-related disorders. Collectively, these conditions constitute a complex clinical entity whose synergistic effects substantially magnify the disease burden [12]. Multimorbidity was noted in approximately 93.4% of Chinese COPD patients, with 80.62% experiencing two or more concurrent chronic diseases [13]. Notably, the incidence of multimorbidity among the elderly with COPD is markedly higher compared to younger cohorts [14].

Functional disabilities associated with COPD and concurrent health conditions have garnered significant attention. Functional disability in older adults encompasses the progressive, acquired inability to perform basic Activities of Daily Living (ADL) and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) that are indispensable for autonomous living. Population-representative evidence indicates that approximately one-quarter of community-dwelling older adults manifest ADL-related limitations, whereas nearly one-half exhibit clinically significant IADL impairments, thereby substantiating its substantial epidemiological footprint and its centrality in geriatric health outcomes [15]. These disabilities are typically accompanied by a progressive deterioration in patient-reported outcomes, including worsening dyspnea severity, increasing depressive symptoms, and declining perceived health status. These factors, in turn, independently predict an elevated risk of hospitalization and premature mortality [16]– [17]. A longitudinal investigation involving Singaporean adults aged 55 and older indicated a marked increase in the prevalence of both activities of daily living (ADL) and instrumental activities of daily living (IADL) when COPD coexists with frailty [18].This deterioration in functional capacity is closely linked to adverse self-reported outcomes, including diminished life satisfaction and heightened fear of falling, further amplifying psychological distress and healthcare dependency among affected older adults [19]. A Spanish cross-sectional study reported that COPD patients suffering from psychiatric disorders exhibited a higher risk of ADL and IADL disabilities compared to those with COPD alone [20], potentially exacerbating the burden on partners and caregivers [21]. Notably, the current literature primarily addresses the effects of specific multimorbidity combinations without extensive examination of broader multimorbidity patterns [22–26]. Many studies explore the relationship between various patterns of multiple chronic conditions and critical patient-related factors. These investigations commonly examine the relationship between multimorbidity and socio-demographic characteristics, disease severity, quality of life, and healthcare utilization by examining either the total count of concurrent illnesses or specific disease combinations.

These findings challenge the efficacy of conventional single-disease-focused management strategies for COPD, suggesting that they may not adequately address the complex treatment needs of patients with multiple conditions. Simth et al. conducted a systematic review assessing the impact of interventions designed to target specific risk factors associated with multimorbidity or its functional disabilities, finding that tailored interventions could offer greater benefits [27]. This underscores the importance of recognizing and understanding the patterns of multimorbidity in COPD and their implications for health outcomes.

Given the paucity of studies focusing on the nexus between multimorbidity and functional disability among older adults with COPD, this research seeks to investigate these associations in Shanghai’s elderly population over the age of 60, examining the risk of ADL/IADL disabilities.

Methods

Study population and data collection

The research data was based on a cross-sectional survey named Unified Needs Assessment Form for Older Adults Care Questionnaire, conducted between January and May 2023. This extensive survey was designed to evaluate the care requirements of the elderly in Shanghai, focusing on their health statuses and capabilities for self-care. This survey was conducted by two uniformly trained assessors at the participant’s house, including a family doctor well acquainted with the participant’s circumstances. A total of 179,141 participants were initially enrolled from January to May 2023.The inclusion criteria were: (1) older adults aged 60 and above; (2) applying for long-term care insurance in Shanghai and voluntarily participating in this study; (3) individuals identified with COPD. The exclusion criteria were: (1) incomplete chronic disease data and the presence of chronic conditions found in less than 1% of the COPD cohort. Out of 179,141 respondents, this study homed in on 9,058 (5.06%) individuals identified with COPD. The selection excluded 1,382 participants for reasons including incomplete chronic disease data and the presence of chronic conditions found in less than 1% of the COPD cohort. Consequently, the analysis included 7,676 subjects. A comprehensive flow chart illustrating the selection process of the participants is depicted in Fig. 1.

Flow chart of participants selection

Assessors conducted direct interviews, gathered data after evaluated and confirmed by the family doctor, and compiled reports for each participant face-to-face. Prior to commencing the survey, all assessors underwent comprehensive training. Adhering to the established “Shanghai Older Adults Care Needs Assessment Guidelines,” they conducted their evaluations, securing informed consent from all subjects or their legal representatives. During the data input process, dedicated staff conducted quality control checks to maintain data accuracy. The final survey results would be publicly disclosed for a two-week period within the elderly participants’ residential communities, allowing for community-level scrutiny and validation.

Measurement

Chronic conditions and Multimorbidity

In this investigation, a comprehensive enumeration of 16 chronic conditions was compiled, encompassing hypertension, coronary heart disease, COPD, cerebrovascular disease (including cerebral hemorrhage or cerebral infarction), diabetes, Parkinson’s disease, tumor, bone and joint diseases, dementia, chronic renal failure, epilepsy, cirrhosis, thyroid dysfunctions (hyperthyroidism/hypothyroidism), other ailments of the digestive system, viral hepatitis, and sexually transmitted disease. Family doctor systematically inquired about each disease from the list, starting with questions such as, “Do you have hypertension?” followed by “Is this diagnosis confirmed by a physician?” If affirmative, the condition was recorded as present based on participants’ medical records and reports. This method was uniformly applied to verify the presence of each listed condition. Additionally, if COPD was identified, further inquiries about five specific diseases including anxiety or depression, heart failure, pulmonary encephalopathy, respiratory failure, and lung infection were conducted. Multimorbidity is defined here as the simultaneous presence of two or more chronic conditions in an individual [11, 28].

Functional disability

The assessment of functional disability was evaluated and confirmed by family doctor who was well-acquainted with participants’ state of illness. The Barthel Index was employed to measure ADL disability, encompassing aspects such as fecal and urinary incontinence, grooming, toileting, eating, changing positions, walking five meters, dressing, stair navigation, and bathing [29, 30]. Participants were categorized based on their final scores into four levels of dependency: severe (≤ 8), moderate (9–12), mild (13–19), and independent (20) [29]. IADL disabilities were assessed using the Lawton IADL Scale, which evaluates abilities to shop, use transportation, prepare meals, perform housework, handle laundry, operate a telephone, manage medications, and handle finances [31]. The resulting scores allowed for classification into severe (≤ 2), moderate (3–5), mild (6–7), and independent (8). In the study, only 34 participants demonstrated independence in ADL, and 121 in IADL. To ensure robust statistical analysis, severe and moderate dependency levels were grouped as ‘disabled’, while mild and independent statuses were considered ‘non-disabled’.

Covariates

The analysis included socio-demographic variables such as age, gender, educational attainment, primary income sources, and living arrangement. Educational attainment was categorized as follows: no formal education, primary school, junior high school, high school, and college or higher. Primary income sources were predominantly pensions and allowances, which might include financial support from children or other sources. Household composition was classified into several categories: residing alone, living with a spouse only, residing with both spouse and children, living solely with children, and living in a household with other relatives or non-relatives. The treatment needs of COPD participants included no treatment, outpatient treatment, inpatient treatment, emergency treatment, and ineffective treatment. Additionally, among the 7676 participants, 547 had complete records of blood gas analysis and pulmonary function tests to assess the COPD disease severity. For blood gas parameters, values were stratified into three distinct classifications: PaO2 > 60mmHg; PaO2 ≤ 60 mmHg, PaCO2 ≤ 50 mmHg; PaO2 ≤ 60 mmHg, PaCO2 > 50 mmHg. Pulmonary functions were categorized into five standardized grades based on FEV1/FVC ratio and FEV1: FEV1/FVC > 70%, FEV1 ≥ 80%; FEV1/FVC ≤ 70%, FEV1 ≥ 80%; FEV1/FVC ≤ 70%, 50%≤FEV1 < 80%; FEV1/FVC ≤ 70%, 30%≤FEV1 < 50%; FEV1/FVC ≤ 70%, FEV1 < 30%.

Statistical analysis

For descriptive analysis, the study utilized frequency and percentage metrics for categorical variables, employing Chi-squared tests for comparison between distinct groups. To maintain the robustness of the findings, conditions with less than 1.0% prevalence among COPD patients were excluded, narrowing the focus to 15 diseases for final analysis. Latent class analysis (LCA) was implemented to discern patterns of multimorbidity among 7074 COPD subjects [32]. Unlike traditional hierarchical clustering that depends on defining cluster distances and selecting algorithms, LCA employs statistical methods to assess model fit and classifies entities based on probability, enhancing objectivity and rigor [33]. The study utilized Mplus Version 8.3, evaluating models ranging from one to eight classes. The analysis included the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) [34], Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) [35], Vuong-Lo-Mendell-Rubin adjusted likelihood ratio test (VLMR-LRT), entropy [36], and the participant proportion in the smallest class for each model. Lower AIC and BIC scores signify improved model fit. Entropy values range from 0 to 1, with values closer to 1 indicating more precise classifications [37]. Upon selecting an optimal class, participants were assigned to the latent class with the highest calculated membership probability [38]. An average posterior probability exceeding 0.7 was indicative of an optimal fit [39].

Missing data were observed for variables of living arrangement (n = 14) and education level (n = 24). Given the small proportion of missing values and the categorical classification of both variables, missing data were handled by performing mode imputation.

To investigate the relationship between multimorbidity and functional disability, logistic regression analyses were applied. The models included adjustments age, gender, education, primary income sources, living arrangements and treatment needs. Statistical significance was attributed to P-values < 0.05. Additionally, due to statistical sample size requirements, further investigations into the association between COPD multimorbidity patterns and disability were not conducted among the 547 participants with documented COPD severity records.

Latent class analysis was conducted using Mplus Version 8.3, while SPSS Version 29.0 was utilized for all other statistical evaluations.

Results

Participants’ characteristics

The mean age of the 7676 participants was 81.67 ± 8.10 years and 64.75% were male. A significant majority, 66.81%, were literate. The predominant source of income for 96.21% of participants was pension. A small fraction, 8.83%, resided alone, whereas 35.04% lived solely with their spouse. Regarding health status, 72.91% of those with COPD had at least two additional chronic diseases, and 48.03% had three or more concurrent chronic diseases. Concerning treatment needs, 91.8% of subjects needed outpatient treatment. Out of the total participants, 602 (7.84%) were identified with only COPD, while 7074 (92.16%) exhibited multimorbidity. The prevalence rates of ADL disability (71.60%) and IADL disability (95.18%) were notably higher in the multimorbidity group compared to those with only COPD (Table 1). As depicted in Fig. 2, the most frequent chronic conditions accompanying COPD included hypertension (60.84%), coronary heart disease (47.42%), bone and joint diseases (31.06%), lung infections (25.23%), and cerebrovascular diseases (24.22%). Furthermore, among 547 with the records of the severity of COPD, 532 (97.26%) presented with multimorbidity, 72.57% exhibited the PaO2 level less than 60 mmHg and 53.56% demonstrated FEV1 values less than 80% of predicted value (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

This research highlighted that both multimorbidity and functional disability are notably prevalent among elderly individuals with COPD. The study discerned that the quantity and configuration of multimorbid conditions variably influenced ADL disability and IADL disability. This finding implies that certain combinations of chronic conditions could potentially forecast functional disabilities in elderly COPD patients. To our knowledge, this represents the initial exploration into the association between multimorbidity and functional disability among Chinese COPD patients aged 60 years and above, utilizing a substantial dataset.

Our findings indicate that 92.15% of COPD patients aged 60 years and older exhibit multimorbidity, aligning with global data that underscore the widespread nature of multimorbidity in this patient population. For instance, research from Denmark reported that 81% of COPD patients experienced chronic multimorbidity [25], and a multicenter cohort study in Japan noted a 92% prevalence of at least one multimorbidity among COPD patients [23]. Such evidence points to the universal challenge of multimorbidity among COPD patients. In our study, the most prevalent diseases were hypertension (60.84%), coronary heart disease (47.42%), bone and joint diseases (31.06%), lung infection (25.23%), and cerebrovascular disease (24.22%). Comparative findings from the CIRO CO-morbidity study highlighted the common occurrence of hyperglycemia, atherosclerosis, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and osteoporosis [24]. Similarly, the BODE COPD cohort study identified hypertension, hyperlipidemia, coronary artery disease, degenerative joint disease, and benign prostatic hyperplasia as frequent comorbidities [40]. While the findings across studies vary, the high incidence of cardiovascular diseases and bone and joint diseases in COPD patients is consistently noted.

This investigation found a high prevalence of functional disability among elderly COPD patients, with 70.19% experiencing disability in ADL and 94.8% in IADL. Compared to Rodríguez-Rodríguez's study, which reported ADL disability rates of 21.8% for men and 31.9% for women aged 60-79 years, and IADL disability rates of 37.5% for men and 41.5% for women in the same age group [20], our figures are significantly higher. Another study in China noted an ADL disability prevalence of 48.5% among elderly COPD participants [41]. The discrepancy between these studies could stem from differences in the average age of participants, as ours were older and specifically from a subset of the elderly requiring care services. Moreover, variations in the methods used to assess functional disability contribute to these differences. Our study utilized a set of 22 questions to evaluate functional disability, whereas Gong et al. used a 12-question framework [41], influencing the comparative outcomes. Furthermore, a Shanghai study on multimorbidity patterns in the elderly reported ADL and IADL disability rates of 50.7% and 90.6%, respectively [42]. Although this study did not specifically focus on COPD patients, the age structure and disability assessment methods were similar to ours. Nonetheless, our study recorded a higher ADL disability rate, likely reflecting the more pronounced disability prevalence observed in COPD patients compared to non-COPD individuals [43].

This research delineated four distinct multimorbidity patterns among elderly COPD patients: metabolic-cardiovascular, cardiorespiratory, osteoarticular, and multisystem. A systematic analysis of five previous studies focusing on COPD comorbidities consistently revealed patterns of cardiovascular and metabolic diseases, despite variations in disease types and multimorbidity research methodologies [13, 23–26]. Our study found a notably high number of patients categorized under metabolic-cardiovascular and osteoarticular patterns, aligning with findings from Vanfleteren and Jurevičienė [24, 26]. Additionally, the multisystem pattern in our research showed that 12 out of the 15 studied diseases surpassed the prevalence rates among COPD participants, with an average multimorbidity count of 5.48 per patient. This pattern is akin to the 'All Multimorbidity Including Heart Disease' cluster identified in Hansen’s study, which recorded the highest multimorbidity [25]. Unlike prior research which mainly employed hierarchical clustering and often omitted pulmonary diseases, our study identified unique clusters of respiratory failure, lung infection, coronary heart disease, and heart failure, termed as the cardiorespiratory pattern. This could be attributed to the increased muscle load, deteriorating muscle function, and inefficient gas exchange in COPD patients leading to respiratory failure [44, 45]. Additionally, COPD’s links with cardiovascular diseases through pathways like oxidative stress [46], inflammation [47], endothelial dysfunction [48], and aging [49], make it an independent risk factor for cardiovascular events, particularly heart failure [50]. Furthermore, the severity of heart failure exacerbates lung dysfunction due to thoracic volume constraints and heightened lung fluid content affecting gas exchange [51]. Our findings align with multiple studies that reported cardiorespiratory disease patterns but did not specifically label them as 'cardiorespiratory pattern' [52, 53]. The convergence of these factors substantiates the cardiorespiratory pattern observed in our study.

Our research demonstrated a substantial association between multimorbidity in COPD patients and an elevated risk of disability, mirroring findings from a Chinese study that reported a significant association between the number of chronic conditions and disability in ADL and IADL among the elderly over 80 years old [54]. The detrimental impact of multiple concurrent diseases affecting various organ systems may explain this association [55]. Specifically, our study highlighted that the cardiorespiratory pattern, involving high incidences of respiratory failure (92.57%), lung infection (83.70%), coronary heart disease (58.8%), and heart failure (39.11%), was notably linked to both ADL and IADL disabilities. Among 532 participants with documented COPD severity, our study demonstrated that the cardiorespiratory pattern was associated with the highest proportions of both PaO2 <60 mmHg and FEV1 values below 80% of the predicted value, when compared to the metabolic/cardiovascular pattern, osteoarticular pattern, and multisystem pattern. Previous research supports the connection between chronic lung diseases and heart failure with increased disability [56], likely due to patients suffering from low exercise tolerance, skeletal and respiratory myopathy, muscle fatigue, and dyspnea [57]. Additionally, our findings revealed that the multisystem pattern, which includes both somatic and mental health challenges like anxiety or depression (70.86%) and cognitive disabilities (30.62%), also poses a significant disability risk. This aligns with the Quiñones et al. study, which identified depression and cognitive impairments as particularly disabling, more so than purely somatic conditions [58]. Furthermore, the metabolic/cardiovascular pattern was found to increase disability risks, possibly due to factors like elevated albuminuria and C-reactive protein levels in patients with cardiovascular diseases [59].

This investigation capitalized on extensive sample data to explore the relationship between multimorbidity and functional disability among elderly COPD patients in Shanghai, China. Notably, it was the first to identify a cardiorespiratory classification in the study of multimorbidity patterns within this demographic, revealing significantly elevated risks for both ADL and IADL disabilities. Nonetheless, the study's scope is confined by several limitations. Primarily, it encompassed only 15 chronic conditions, excluding potential other chronic illnesses affecting these patients. The influence of different chronic diseases might result in varied clustering and characteristics of clusters, suggesting that future research should utilize datasets that encompass a broader range of chronic conditions to more accurately delineate multimorbidity patterns in COPD patients. Secondly, compared to the general community-dwelling elder adult population, our study cohort exhibited a higher mean age and a greater prevalence of functional disability, and elderly individuals without care needs or those with acute and short-term illnesses may have been underrepresented in this assessment, which could lead to an overestimation of the association among multimorbidity patterns and functional disability. Thirdly, although certain potential confounders were adjusted based on previous research, additional confounding factors were not considered, such as smoking and the severity of COPD. Lastly, the cross-sectional design of this study precludes any conclusions about causal relationships between multimorbidity patterns and functional disability. Prospective studies are warranted to further investigate these relationships and consider the interactive and synergistic effects of COPD multimorbidity patterns on functional disability.

Conclusion

The study indicates a high prevalence of multimorbidity among elderly Chinese COPD patients, which is associated with an enhanced risk of functional disability.These insights are crucial for developing patient-centered healthcare management strategies focused on multimorbidity in COPD patients, aiming to mitigate chronic conditions, avert functional disability, and enhance life quality. To comprehensively understand the causal dynamics between multimorbidity patterns and disability among COPD patients, future research should incorporate longitudinal studies.

Medical image encryption algorithm based on Chen’s chaotic system and the Advanced Encryption Standard algorithm
Authors: Arzigul Hekim, Mamatreyim Yunus, Qingmei Huang, Nadira Mamatreyim, Aliya Imin, Dilara Turhun, Ablat Sulayman, Mutallip Sattar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSingle Chen’s chaotic system or Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) cannot provide sufficient anti-attack performance when separately used for medical image encryption. This study built a hybrid encryption framework combining four-dimensional (4D) Chen hyperchaotic system, Arnold transform, logistic map and AES-256 to fix security flaws of single encryption modules and protect privacy of clinical medical imaging data.MethodsA three-stage encryption workflow driven by logistic chaotic sequences was designed: (I) the logistic map ran 4,300 iterations (initial X=0.1, μ: 3.57–4.00, step =0.0001). The 100th and 1000th sequence values were Arnold mapping parameters; the 1200th, 1500th, 1800th and 2000th outputs served as four initial states of the 4D Chen hyperchaotic system to guarantee high chaotic parameter sensitivity; (II) Arnold transform realized pixel scrambling, followed by exclusive-OR (XOR) diffusion with Chen hyperchaotic sequences to eliminate intra-image pixel correlation; (III) we extracted 256 continuous bits (positions 2050–2305) from logistic sequences as the unique AES-256 secret key, and implemented multiround block encryption under AES Electronic Codebook (ECB) mode to boost cipher randomness. Simulations were run on MATLAB R2019b with two self-generated medical phantom images: a 743×720 liver lesion phantom (46.1 kB) and a 236×180 splenic trauma phantom (24.6 kB).ResultsQuantitative metrics show excellent encryption performance. For liver lesion ciphertext, number of pixels change rate (NPCR) =99.66% and unified average changing intensity (UACI) =34.73%; splenic trauma ciphertext reached NPCR =99.81% and UACI =33.55%, both above the 99.6% and 33.3% security thresholds. Ciphertext χ2 values were 275.5194 (liver lesion phantom) and 243.3932 (splenic trauma phantom), much lower than plaintext values (1.4977×105, 4.4232×106), proving uniform grayscale distribution. Ciphertext information entropy was 7.9953 (liver lesion phantom) and 7.9969 (splenic trauma phantom), nearly the ideal maximum of 8.00. Ciphertext pixel correlation coefficients (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) ranged −0.0042 to 0.0039, close to zero and far smaller than plaintext coefficients (>0.93). Compared with 2025–2026 state-of-the-art chaotic encryption schemes, our method achieved the best NPCR, UACI and correlation results. Extra tests confirmed strong resistance against chosen-plaintext, chosen-ciphertext, noise and occlusion attacks, with stable efficiency for computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, ultrasound and X-ray multimodal medical images.ConclusionsThe proposed linked chaotic-AES hybrid algorithm unifies Arnold parameters, Chen hyperchaotic initial values and AES keys via logistic mapping, overcoming drawbacks of independent random module design. It achieves near-ideal randomness, ultra-low pixel correlation and strong multi-attack resistance versus standalone Chen chaos or AES. This lightweight, low-overhead scheme provides reliable end-to-end security for clinical medical image transmission and storage in smart healthcare systems.

Introduction

The development of the internet has allowed the public access to medical information and for greater volume of medical images to be transmitted over networks. To prevent the private information of patients from being leaked, private medical images must be encrypted.

Chaotic phenomena are complex forms of natural motion characterized by boundedness, aperiodicity, and extreme sensitivity to initial conditions and parameters (1-3). A hyperchaotic system is a chaotic system with more than two positive Lyapunov exponents, in which the phase trajectories diverge in several directions and the chaotic motion is more complex than that in a conventional chaotic system. Information encrypted by simple chaotic systems is insecure because the signals can be decoded with high likelihood when intercepted. High-order hyperchaotic systems can enhance the randomness and unpredictability of chaos, thus providing stricter confidentiality and performance in communication (4-6). Chaotic cryptography was first conceptualized by Matthews et al. (7) who proposed a cryptographic scheme based on chaotic sequences of deformed logistic maps, triggering an upsurge in cryptography research. In recent years, chaotic systems have been widely applied to image encryption. In the digital-image encryption algorithms of Zhao et al. (8), Gao et al. (9), and Huang et al. (10), chaotic sequences replace the parameters in the fusion formula and improve the encryption security by changing the parameters. Wu et al. (11), Zhang et al. (12), and Zhu et al. (13) synchronized two chaotic systems with different dimensions, providing heterogeneous chaotic synchronization systems for communication encryption. Applying Chen’s system, Chai et al. (14) and Chen et al. (15) generated chaotic sequences for diffusing and scrambling plaintext color images and achieving adaptive image encryption. Yan et al. (16) investigated the dynamic behavior of a chaotic system by varying a single unknown exponential parameter and state variables in the nonlinear terms of the system. After examining its structure, they applied the newly constructed chaotic system to an image-encryption system with the same encryption and decryption processes. Zhao et al. (17) proposed a three-dimensional (3D) orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing constellation encryption scheme based on perturbed spatiotemporal chaos. They designed a feedback cellular automaton and a novel 3D 16-ary constellation diagram combining the perturbed spatiotemporal chaos system, thereby achieving achieve modulation encryption of the physical layer via 3D constellation rotation. Chaos is also applicable to medical image encryption. Fractional-order chaos can realize 3D reconstruction and encryption of computed tomography (CT) images. Another encryption technique is chaotic map diffusion (18,19).

Recently, high-dimensional chaos, nonlinear transformations, and intelligent cryptanalysis have become key research directions in this field (20-22). Alexan et al. [2025] devised an encryption scheme combining chaotic systems and nonlinear transformations (20). Alshehri [2025] designed an algorithm based on four-dimensional (4D) chaotic system and cellular automaton (21). Furthermore, Manavalan et al. [2026] adopted quasi-quantum neural networks to conduct cryptanalysis on chaotic image ciphers (22).

As important branches of multimedia security, steganography and watermarking techniques also provide valuable references for medical data protection. A recently developed medical steganography scheme shares identical threat models and evaluation metrics, including histogram distribution, pixel correlation, and information entropy, with medical image encryption, achieving reliable protection of medical private data (23). Quantum-resilient multimedia security and Internet of things–oriented data encryption have also garnered heightened research attention. Although existing quantum-resistant multimedia frameworks have been designed primarily for audio streams, their core essence of ant-quantum encryption and secure multimedia transmission provide important guidance for the future development of our medical image encryption scheme (24).

Our work expands upon the above-mentioned studies, as we constructed a hybrid encryption scheme of Chen’s chaotic system and Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) for medical images. The traditional Data Encryption Standard (DES) and triple DES algorithms cannot keep pace with the rapid development of modern computing power. Therefore, these algorithms cannot serve as long-term encryption standards and have been replaced with the AES algorithm, which offers higher security than does the DES and triple DES algorithms (25). Since its adoption by the National Institute of Standards and Technology in May 2002, the AES algorithm has become one of the most commonly used symmetric encryption algorithms (7). In 2009, Chen et al. (26) combined the AES algorithm with logistic map into an encryption algorithm for medical images, which effectively protects them during transmission. Zhan et al. (27) introduced the processes and principles of the AES and DES algorithms to image encryption. Comparing the encryption efficiencies of the two algorithms under different modes, they found that the AES algorithm achieves higher encryption efficiency than does the DES algorithm. Zheng et al. (28) proposed an image encryption algorithm based on chaos and AES. First, the algorithm optimizes the structure of round operations via a pipeline approach and integrates three substitution processes (byte substitution, row shifting, and column mixing) with lookup tables to improve the data processing speed of the system and increase the data throughput. Second, the algorithm generates chaotic sequences as replacements of round keys to enhance the system’s security.

In summary, a single chaos or AES algorithm is insufficient for satisfying the dual requirements of fast encryption speed and strong encryption effect. Although a number of hybrid schemes combining chaos and AES have been reported, the majority of these merely adopt chaotic sequences to replace AES round keys separately. In contrast, our system uniformly generates the parameters of the Arnold map, the initial values of Chen’s hyperchaotic system, and the initial key of the AES algorithm via the logistic chaos map. This all-linked parameter and key generation mode avoids the independent design of chaotic and AES modules, further enhances the randomness of parameter and key selection, and improves the anti-attack capability of the encrypted images. The results of digital-image encryption demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed encryption method.

Specific decryption process

The image receiver performs reverse operations of the encryption process. First, it uses the logistic chaotic map to iteratively generate the parameters of the Arnold map, the initial values of the Chen’s hyperchaotic system, and the key of the AES algorithm. Second, the AES decryption algorithm and XOR operation are sequentially applied to the ciphertext image. Finally, the inverse operations of the encryption process are carried out on the chaotic sequence generated by the Chen’s hyperchaotic system and the Arnold map. Discussion

In this work, AES was implemented in Electronic Code Book (ECB) mode for image -lock processing. Although the traditional ECB mode has inherent cryptographic limitations for general data encryption, it fits well with the characteristics of medical image encryption. Images consist of a large number of independent pixel blocks, and ECB mode features simple operation and low computational overhead, which guarantees high encryption efficiency for massive medical image data. Moreover, we introduced a Chen hyperchaotic sequence to preprocess image blocks before AES encryption. This preprocessing disrupts the correlation between adjacent blocks and compensates for the security defects of pure ECB mode. Therefore, the combination of ECB-mode AES and chaotic scrambling is a reasonable choice for practical medical image encryption scenarios.

We fully recognize that standalone ECB mode may expose the structural features of plaintext images due to identical ciphertext outputs for identical blocks. However, in our complete framework, multilayer chaotic processing including Arnold parameter scrambling and Chen hyperchaotic sequence diffusion is performed prior to AES encryption. All image blocks are completely randomized and decorrelated before entering the AES-ECB module. As a result, the original block correspondence and structural similarity are thoroughly eliminated, which fundamentally avoids the information leakage risk caused by ECB-mode defects. In this case, the ECB mode only undertakes efficient block encryption without retaining any valid image structure, which resolves the inherent cryptographic weakness of standard ECB encryption.

In this study, we formally defined the attack models including chosen-plaintext attack, chosen-ciphertext attack, noise attack, and occlusion attack. Relying on the pseudo-random characteristics of Chen hyperchaotic sequences, the algorithm fully disrupts the statistical features and pixel correlation of plaintext images. Meanwhile, the hybrid structure of chaotic scrambling and AES block encryption breaks the inherent mapping relationship between plaintext and ciphertext, which effectively resists the above-mentioned common attacks and greatly enhances the overall security of the system. Experimental results indicate that the algorithm maintains stable and excellent encryption performance across different image types, which proves its strong adaptability and practical application potential in a diversity of medical image scenarios.

We further quantitatively evaluated the computational efficiency of the proposed algorithm in terms of execution time, computational overhead, and memory consumption. The encryption and decryption running time for standard medical image sizes was recorded under uniform experimental conditions. The results suggest that the hybrid chaotic-AES encryption structure achieves low computational complexity and stable running efficiency. Moreover, the algorithm has acceptable memory occupancy during processing, which fully meets the real-time transmission and deployment requirements of practical medical imaging systems.

In our study, the logistic map was uniformly adopted to generate parameters for Arnold transform, initial values of Chen chaotic system, and keys for AES encryption. This unified design simplifies the overall system structure and avoids the security risks introduced by multiple independent random number generators. Meanwhile, the inherent chaotic characteristics of logistic map ensure the randomness and unpredictability of all relevant parameters. We also analyzed the parameter coupling effect: the correlated parameters form an associated chaotic constraint relationship, which increases the difficulty of parameter cracking and further enhances the overall security of the encryption scheme.

For better reproducibility, we explicitly summarized key implementation details. All chaotic parameters were set within a fixed valid range, and uniform numerical precision was adopted throughout the program. We applied an iteration discard strategy to remove transient chaotic sequences before formal encryption. In addition, the complete key generation, distribution, and storage procedures have been described clearly. These detailed settings make the whole algorithm easy to reconstruct and run independently.

Currently, artificial intelligence–driven cryptanalysis and quantum-inspired learning have become emerging threats to chaos-based encryption. Studies on this subject have proven that neural networks can learn and approximate partial chaotic mapping rules, which weakens the long-term robustness of traditional chaotic ciphers. To address with this challenge, our scheme combines multisource chaotic sequences and AES block encryption. The coupled parameters and multilayer nonlinear transformation increase the difficulty for intelligent models to fit the encryption logic. Meanwhile, the dynamic key generation mechanism further resists learning-based cracking, ensuring satisfactory long-term security in complex attack environments.

It is important to note that NPCR, UACI, information entropy, and pixel correlation are basic statistical metrics for evaluating diffusion and confusion performance. Beyond these indicators, we further analyzed the performance against classic cryptanalytic attacks. Combined with the structural characteristics of chaotic scrambling and AES encryption, the algorithm can effectively defend against known-plaintext attack, chosen-plaintext attack, chosen-ciphertext attack, linear attack, and differential attack. The multilayer nonlinear transformation and dynamic key mechanism break the linear correlation and differential characteristics between plaintext and ciphertext, and thus the scheme maintains reliable security across an array of attack scenarios.

The rapid development of network technology has increased the use of digital images in daily life. Trusted physicians can be consulted from the homes of potential patients, saving time and enabling cross-regional diagnoses and advice from clinical professional. These shifts in the physician–patient relationship have elevated the demands for security in medical image transmission. The proposed encryption algorithm combines a hyperchaotic system with the AES algorithm. First, it randomly selects six numbers from the values generated by a logistic chaotic map, providing two parameters of the Arnold map and four initial values of Chen’s hyperchaotic system. The medical image scrambled via the Arnold map is encrypted in the first round with a chaotic sequence generated by Chen’s hyperchaotic system. Finally, 256 bits are randomly selected from the logistic map as the key for the AES algorithm, which performs round key encryption on the image. The logistic sequence is generated with initial value X=0.1, control parameter μ varying from 3.57 to 4.00 at a step of 0.0001, and a total of 4,300 iterations. The 256-bit key is extracted from positions 2050–2305 of the generated sequence. The effective key space is dominated by the secret initial seed and parameter μ rather than the extracted 256-bit segment. With the given parameter range, the available values of μ reach 4301. Combined with the random initial seed, the overall key space is sufficiently large to avoid low-entropy risks. In this paper, AES-256 denotes the length of the working key for block encryption, and the underlying security is guaranteed by the large parameter space of the chaotic system. This approach effectively overcomes the shortcomings (small key space and fixed encryption process) of the AES algorithm, thereby enhancing the anti-attack capability of the algorithm. A successful simulation of image encryption demonstrated the potentially broad application prospects of the proposed algorithm.

As for the future work, we will further optimize and improve the proposed medical image encryption scheme based on the current research foundation. First, we will optimize the chaotic sequence generation mechanism and block encryption strategy to reduce computational overhead and enhance the real-time performance of the algorithm so as to better adapt to the fast transmission requirements of medical image data. Second, we will develop adaptive encryption strategies for multimodal medical images including CT, magnetic resonance imaging, and ultrasound, further improving the generalization and robustness of the encryption method. Third, we aim to adopt lightweight deployment optimization for medical edge terminals to promote the practical engineering application of the algorithm in medical privacy protection scenarios. This follow-up research will be centered on the technical framework and application scenarios outlined in this paper, with the aim being to continuously improve the practicability and security of medical image encryption technology.

Medical image encryption is a core component of the smart healthcare ecosystem. Systematic reviews have clarified the technical architecture integrating blockchain for distributed storage and federated learning for privacy-preserving model training in medical scenarios (34,35). Our encryption scheme can be embedded into such end-to-end healthcare data pipelines. Our long-term goal is to combine the proposed algorithm with blockchain and federated learning to build a complete interdisciplinary privacy protection framework for medical data.

Conclusions

This paper presents a hybrid medical image encryption algorithm integrating Chen’s hyperchaotic system and AES-256. In this method, the logistic map uniformly generates all chaotic parameters and AES keys, and multilayer chaotic scrambling offsets the inherent security flaws of AES-ECB mode. Multimodal medical image tests confirmed that the algorithm has outstanding statistical diffusion performance and strong resistance to various common cryptanalytic and interference attacks. It balances low computational overhead and sufficient key space, meeting the real-time privacy protection demands of smart medical imaging systems. Future work will optimize the algorithm’s real-time performance and lightweight deployment and examine its combination with blockchain and federated learning for the purpose of building an integrated medical data security framework.

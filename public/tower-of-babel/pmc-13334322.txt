A retrospective analysis for early-stage non-endometrioid endometrial cancers
Authors: Eunkyoung Lee, Won Kyung Cho, Won Park, Jeong-Won Lee, Yoo-Young Lee, Chel Hun Choi, Tae-Joong Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to investigate treatment outcomes and explore optimal adjuvant treatment options for patients with early-stage uterine serous carcinoma (USC) and clear cell carcinoma (CCC).Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed the records of 63 patients diagnosed with stage I–II (2009 International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics [FIGO]) USC or CCC. Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and comparisons between groups were performed with the log-rank test.ResultsAmong the 63 patients, 36 (57.1%) had USC and 25 (39.7%) had CCC. According to the revised 2023 FIGO staging system, 23 patients (36.5%) were stage IC, and 40 (63.5%) were stage IIC. Adjuvant treatment included no adjuvant therapy (23.8%), chemotherapy alone (39.7%), radiotherapy alone (28.6%), and combined chemotherapy plus radiotherapy (8.0%). After a median follow-up of 54 months (range, 5 to 227 months), the 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) for the entire cohort were 89.5% and 85.7%, respectively. Patients with CCC had a 5-year PFS of 95.8% and OS of 92.3%, whereas those with USC had a 5-year PFS of 85.1% and OS of 81.4%. Most recurrences (7/63, 11.1%) were distant, occurring predominantly in USC cases (6/7) and 2023 stage IIC (7/7).ConclusionIn this retrospective study, early-stage USC and CCC had very favorable PFS and survival outcomes. Thus, future randomized clinical trial or large cohort study is necessary to determine the optimal adjuvant treatments in these patients.

Introduction

Endometrial cancer is the sixth most common gynecologic malignancy worldwide and the incidence continues to rise [1]. While the majority of endometrial cancers are of endometrioid histology and generally have a favorable prognosis, non-endometrioid subtypes are known for their aggressive behavior [2]. Non-endometrioid endometrial cancers including uterine serous carcinoma (USC), clear cell carcinoma (CCC), and carcinosarcomas, account for approximately 10%–15% of all endometrial cancers, but contribute to approximately 40%–50% of endometrial cancer-related deaths [3,4]. These subtypes are characterized by their distinct molecular profiles, rapid progression, and tendency for early metastasis, even in early-stage disease [5,6].

In the revised 2023 International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) system, USC and CCC are grouped as “aggressive histologic types,” and staging differs from that of non-aggressive endometrioid type tumors [7]. Specifically, USC and CCC confined to the endometrium are classified as stage IC, while any degree of myometrial invasion is categorized as stage IIC, regardless of invasion depth. Given their high recurrence rates, adjuvant therapy is commonly considered for early-stage USC and CCC. However, owing to the rarity of these subtypes, most evidence regarding adjuvant treatment comes from small retrospective studies, and published findings have been inconsistent [4,8-12]. Some research suggests that surgery alone may be sufficient for certain stage I patients [13], whereas others demonstrate improved loco-regional control with adjuvant radiotherapy [14]. Furthermore, given the propensity for distant metastasis, many clinicians advocate incorporating adjuvant chemotherapy [15].

In light of these uncertainties, we conducted a retrospective analysis to investigate treatment outcomes and explore optimal adjuvant treatment strategies for early-stage USC and CCC.

Materials and Methods

1. Patient

From January 2001 to December 2020, we retrospectively identified 63 patients diagnosed with stage I–II (FIGO 2009) USC or CCC who underwent total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy at our institution. Pelvic and para-aortic lymphadenectomy, and peritoneal washings were performed at the surgeon's discretion. Staging was conducted according to both the 2009 and the revised 2023 FIGO systems.

2. Adjuvant treatment

Adjuvant treatment was individualized based on tumor characteristics, patient preferences, and physician judgment. The choice between external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) and vaginal brachytherapy was based on risk factors including depth of myometrial invasion, lymphovascular space invasion (LVSI), tumor size, and treating radiation oncologist's assessment. Platinum-based chemotherapy (primarily carboplatin and paclitaxel) was administered for 3–6 cycles. EBRT was delivered to the whole pelvis at a dose of 45–50.4 Gy in 25–28 fractions. Vaginal brachytherapy was delivered as high-dose-rate intracavitary radiation at 30 Gy in 6 fractions or 21 Gy in 3 fractions. For concurrent chemoradiation, weekly cisplatin was administered.

3. Follow-up and outcomes

Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from surgery to the first documented recurrence or death from any cause. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to death from any cause or last follow-up. Loco-regional recurrence was defined as a recurrence at vaginal stump, pelvic or para-aortic lymph nodes, and distant recurrence was defined as metastases outside the pelvis/abdomen or peritoneal seeding. Patients were followed up at regular intervals, typically every 3 months for the 5 years after surgery. Follow-up evaluations included physical examinations, serum tumor marker assessments, and imaging studies including chest and abdomen-pelvis computed tomography scans.

4. Statistical analysis

Survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 28 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

1. Patient characteristics

Among the 63 patients included, the median age at diagnosis was 65 years (range, 43 to 84). A total of 36 patients (57.1%) had USC, 25 (39.7%) had CCC, and two (3.2%) had mixed histology. According to the 2009 FIGO staging system, 45 patients (71.4%) were stage IA, 13 (20.6%) were stage IB, and five (7.9%) were stage II. Under the revised 2023 FIGO system, 23 patients (36.5%) were stage IC, and 40 (63.5%) were stage IIC. LVSI was observed in 12 patients (19.0%). There was no significant difference in tumor characteristics between the USC and CCC groups (Table 1).

2. Treatment

All patients underwent total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. Pelvic lymphadenectomy was performed in 59 patients (93.7%), and para-aortic lymphadenectomy was performed in 30 patients (47.6%). Adjuvant treatment regimens varied: 15 patients (23.8%) received no adjuvant therapy, 25 (39.7%) received chemotherapy alone, 18 (28.6%) received radiotherapy alone, and five (8.0%) received combined chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Among those with CCC, 44% did not receive adjuvant therapy. In contrast, among those with USC, 24 patients (66.7%) underwent chemotherapy (p < 0.001) (Table 1). Peritoneal washing cytology was performed in 56 patients (88.9%) and was positive in three patients. Omentectomy was conducted in nine patients, consistent with institutional practice during which omental assessment was performed selectively based on surgeon discretion and intraoperative findings rather than routinely for all early-stage cases.

Of the patients receiving chemotherapy, the most common regimen was carboplatin plus paclitaxel (24 patients, 80% of those receiving chemotherapy), with a median of 4 cycles (range, 3 to 6). Among the patients receiving radiotherapy, 12 underwent EBRT and 11 received vaginal brachytherapy.

3. Survival outcomes and prognostic factors

The median follow-up duration was 54 months (range, 5 to 227). During follow-up, seven patients (11.1%) experienced disease recurrence, and 11 (17.5%) died. The 5-year PFS and OS rates for the entire cohort were 89.5% and 85.7%, respectively. Stratified by histology, patients with USC had a 5-year PFS of 85.1% and a 5-year OS of 81.4%, while patients with CCC had a 5-year PFS of 95.8% and a 5-year OS of 92.3% (Fig. 1). Most recurrences (5/7, 71.4%) were distant only, with one case being regional plus distant and one being loco-regional and distant (Table 2).

Univariate analysis identified that age ≥70 years (p = 0.025) and stage IIC disease by the 2023 FIGO system (indicating myometrial invasion, p = 0.035) were associated with worse PFS (Table 3). No significant prognostic factors were found for OS. Multivariate analysis was not feasible due to the small number of patients and events.

Table 4 describes the characteristics of patients who experienced recurrence. Of the seven patients who experienced recurrence, six had USC and one had CCC. All of these patients had myometrial invasion (2023 FIGO IIC). Three out of the seven patients exhibited LVSI, the adjuvant treatments they received were varied: two received vaginal brachytherapy (VBT) alone, two received VBT combined with chemotherapy, two received chemotherapy alone, and one received no adjuvant treatment. The most frequent site of distant metastases was lung, occurring in three of the seven patients.

Discussion and Conclusion

This retrospective study evaluated treatment outcomes and adjuvant strategies for early-stage (2009 FIGO I–II) non-endometrioid endometrial cancers, specifically USC and CCC. A significant finding was the difference in outcomes between these two histologic subtypes. Patients with CCC demonstrated a superior 5-year PFS of 95.8%, compared to 85.1% for those with USC, despite almost half of the CCC group not receiving adjuvant treatment. The recurrence rate for CCC was 4%, versus 16.7% for USC, supporting earlier studies that suggest CCC has a lower recurrence rate—one-third that of USC [16-18].

One of the largest studies on early USC, by Kurnit et al. [14], included 737 patients. They performed a subgroup analysis assessing the impact of adjuvant treatment in those USC confined to the endometrium (without myometrial invasion). Of the 250 patients with endometrium-confined USC, no adjuvant therapy demonstrated a significant difference in recurrence-free survival compared to observation alone. However, vaginal cuff brachytherapy was associated with improved OS compared to observation alone (hazard ratio, 0.46; 95% confidence interval, 0.23 to 0.94) [14]. Armbruster et al.'s study [19], the largest on early-stage CCC, included 112 patients. In this study, OS approached 10 years for early-stage CCC patients. Adjuvant treatment was not associated with survival, but age over 70 and LVSI were the only factors associated with worse survival [19].

In our study cohort, there were 23 cases of 2023 FIGO stage IC (confined to the endometrium), comprising 12 cases of USC and 11 of clear cell CCC as detailed in Table 1. Within this IC subgroup, adjuvant treatment included chemotherapy alone in seven patients (six with USC, one with CCC), concurrent chemoradiotherapy in one patient with USC, VBT alone in three patients, and no adjuvant therapy in 12 patients (data not shown). Notably, all recurrent cases had stage IIC (myometrial invasion) and none of IC patients developed recurrence regardless of adjuvant treatment or histologic type (Table 4). This observation supports the utility of the revised 2023 FIGO staging system, which classifies high-risk histology based on the presence of myometrial invasion.

Regarding prognostic factors for survival, older age (>70) and stage IIC were significant factors for PFS. However, we could not conduct a multivariate analysis due to the small number of patients and events. Previous studies have reported that LVSI and tumor size are associated with survival [14,20,21]. In our study, those with LVSI and larger tumor size had numerically lower survival rates, although this was not statistically significant. Furthermore, we did not identify significance in PFS among the various adjuvant treatment groups; however, this finding should be interpreted cautiously given the study’s retrospective design, small sample size, and heterogeneous adjuvant therapies. The patients who received radiotherapy or chemotherapy even had numerically worse PFS compared with those who did not receive such adjuvant treatment (Table 2). This may reflect treatment selection bias, whereby patients with higher-risk features were more likely to be offered adjuvant therapy. Due to the limited sample size and event numbers, multivariate analysis could not be performed to adjust for these confounding variables.

Our study has several limitations, including its retrospective nature, small sample size, and potential for selection bias. The heterogeneity in adjuvant treatments and the extended study period may have introduced confounding factors. Despite these limitations, our findings contribute to the limited body of evidence on early-stage non-endometrioid endometrial cancers. Specifically, the favorable survival outcomes observed in CCC may indicate a relatively indolent disease course, though the role and intensity of adjuvant therapy in these patients remain to be clarified. In contrast, the higher rate of distant failure in USC suggests a potential need for effective systemic treatment strategies. Additionally, given that no recurrence was observed among patients with 2023 FIGO stage IC, further investigation is warranted to determine whether additional treatment might confer clinical benefits regardless of histological subtype at this stage.

In conclusion, this retrospective analysis suggests that patients with early-stage CCC generally demonstrate favorable outcomes, whereas those with USC more frequently experienced distant recurrences, particularly in the presence of myometrial invasion. These findings highlight the importance of considering histologic subtype and myometrial invasion when tailoring adjuvant treatment strategies and are consistent with the rationale of the revised 2023 FIGO staging system, which incorporates depth of invasion in aggressive histologies. Given the retrospective design and limited sample size of this study, the results should be interpreted with caution, and further large-scale prospective studies are warranted to elucidate the optimal adjuvant treatment approach for early-stage USC and CCC.

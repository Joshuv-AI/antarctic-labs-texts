An analytical exploration of dark and bright solitary wave solutions of time-fractional (3+1) -dimensional generalized Painlevé-type equation
Authors: Mohsin Khan, Rashid Ali, Ibraheem M Alsulami, Amer Alsulami
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research study constructs and examines solitary wave solutions to the Katugampola time-fractional -dimensional generalized Painlev’e-type (also known as P-type) equation with the help of the generalized -expansion method and tanh-coth method. The targeted model is extensively used to understand the dynamics of the plasma waves and instabilities in soliton theory, plasma physics and nonlinear wave theory. By offering closed form traveling solutions, the proposed methods transform the desired model into a nonlinear algebraic system. A new set of solutions in terms of trigonometric, rational and hyperbolic functions is obtained by analytically solving the resulting algebraic system using the symbolic computation tool Maple. We provide a range of 3D, density, and 2D visual representations that show the presence of bright and dark solitary wave solutions in the framework of the resulting wave profiles that illustrate the strong dynamics of the model. Also, a more dynamic and practical framework proposed by Katugampola-derivative is used to enhance the validity of the results by investigating how the time-fractional derivative affects the given model’s solutions. Being the initial accounts of developing solutions of the aimed model, our comparison brings out the uniqueness of the obtained solutions. The variegated array of findings also shows that the suggested approaches are useful mathematical tools for solving nonlinear Fractional Partial Differential Equations (FPDEs), with applications in mathematical sciences such as engineering, physics, and biology, as well as in other nonlinear evolution problems.Keywords: Fractional partial differential equations, Generalized Painlevé-type equation, -expansion method, Tanh-coth method, Katugampola fractional derivative, Dark and bright solitary wave solutionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Fractional Calculus (FC) is a subdiscipline of mathematics that examines differential and integral operators of real or complex order. The frameworks of FC are effective in the modeling of systems and processes which are non-local and have memory. The FC started gaining interest soon after the introduction of classical calculus, as Leibniz wrote in a letter to L’Hopital in 1695. In 1730, Euler became the first to examine the topic of the fractional calculus. The derivative of arbitrary order was first treated by S.F. Lacroix in 1819 and a fractional derivative was introduced by P.S. Laplace in 1812 as an integral1. The development of the theory of FC was given much attention by many mathematicians by then2. FC theories and applications have become relatively popular and receive significant interest today3. It is among the novel subdisciplines of applied mathematics, mathematical analysis and mathematical physics. This is because the derivations and modelling of certain nonlinear models can be done using FC to explain phenomena that cannot be derived using integer derivatives, and particularly those of non local and non conservative systems. Also, the application of FC is applied to phenomena in various areas of context, such as quantum systems and space. It has evolved to deal with the dynamics of real world complex systems and innovative ideas on real time data have been implemented and tested. Some of these ideas have gone on to patent some of the ideas, which demonstrates the effectiveness of FC as a tool to be used4. The practical fractional derivative mathematical ideas that are crucial to FC and could be widely applied include Grunwald-Letnikov fractional derivative5, Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative6, Atangana-Baleanu fractional derivative7, Caputo fractional derivative8, Conformable fractional derivative9, He’s fractional derivative10, truncated M-fractional fractional derivative11, Hadamard fractional derivative12, and Katugampola fractional derivative13. All definitions possess specific advantages and are applied to a motley range of fields where the non-integer order systems and phenomena are of paramount importance, such as the sphere of signal processing, physics and engineering. Some uses of these derivatives include understanding viscoelastic materials, modeling electromagnetic systems, making biological systems which have anomalous diffusion, definition of anomalous transport processes, processing of fractal signals and analysis of wave propagation in dispersive media. Fractional derivatives are also added to dynamical system visualization. Although several fractional derivatives are available, the choice of derivative is crucial to modelling physical systems. The Katugampola fractional derivative offers a notable improvement in this regard over other definitions of fractional operators, such as the Caputo fractional derivative and Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative. Unlike other derivatives, KFD combines power-law and logarithmic kernels, enabling it to bridge the gap between different memory effects. This makes it well-adapted to multiscale and fractal-like media which have both local and nonlocal properties. Also, KFD preserves some important structures of classical calculus such as the Leibniz rules, which is more convenient for analytical solution of nonlinear equations14. This is important, as it guarantees a unity and consistency of the application of the principles of classical calculus to higher, more complex applications. However, other derivatives, such as Caputo may be useful for initial value problems, but are not as adaptable to scaling and uneven memory effects. Thus, KFD is selected here for its capacity to preserve more complex wave properties of heterogeneous and fractional media.

The development of fractional derivatives has allowed us to employ the principles of classical calculus to a wide range of scientific and engineering fields such as physics, control theory, biology, signal processing, electromagnetics, and quantum mechanics. The inherent nonlocality and memory characteristics of fractional derivatives make them ideal for generalising classical frameworks, particularly nonlinear Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) into nonlinear Fractional PDEs (FPDEs), where the dynamics of the system are not only driven by the local interactions but also by its past. Consequently, nonlinear FPDEs offer a more accurate model for wave propagation in complex media in which integer-order models are incapable of incorporating memory and nonlocality effects. The nonlinear sciences community has been very interested in nonlinear FPDEs over the last ten years because of the incredible variety of opportunities they offer. In order to understand the mechanisms behind nonlinear FPDEs, it is important to find and study their exact solutions which give direct insight into the role of nonlocality and memory effects on the dynamics. Therefore, in most natural disciplines, the closed-form solutions of nonlinear FPDEs are currently crucial for understanding complex nonlinear wave phenomena. Studying the solitary wave phenomena, which relies on closed form solutions of nonlinear FPDEs, especially the computation of the traveling wave and solitary wave solution, can offer important insights into the dynamics of nonlinear nonlinear FPDEs. Consequently, the study of solitary wave solutions of nonlinear FPDEs is growing considerably. As a result, several useful methods for obtaining solitary wave solutions have been created in order to comprehend the fundamental mechanism of these nonlinear models. In the past few decades there has been a growing interest in the use of symbolic computing techniques to generate exact solitary wave solutions for nonlinear FPDEs that facilitate the tedious algebraic calculations. Many techniques have been developed by researchers to discover solitary wave solutions, such as the exp-function strategy15, F-expansion methodology16, the soliton ansatz method17, extended trial equation technique18, extended tanh function method19, the first integral tool20, Hirota bilinear approach21, the sine-cosine method22, modified simple equation approach23, the Riccatti-Bernoulli sub-ODE technique24, the Jacobi elliptic function method25, the Kudryashov approach26, the Khater approach27, Riccati modified extended simple equation method28, the -expansion approach29,30, the tanh-coth methodology31, the Tan-Cot approach32, extended direct algebraic method33–35, the generalized Kudryashov auxiliary tool36, extended sinh-Gordon equation expansion approach37, unified method38, Lie symmetry technique39 generalized exponential rational function method40, and several other methods41–44.

The -expansion method and the tanh-coth method are prominent analytical techniques that efficiently yield clear, closed-form solutions. Their flexibility in selecting functional forms allows them to generate a wide spectrum of solutions—including solitons, traveling waves, and trigonometric, hyperbolic, and rational functions. A key advantage is that, like other such methods, they circumvent the need for intricate analytical steps such as linearization or perturbation when addressing nonlinear models. Motivated by these merits, researchers have extensively applied these methods to study solitary wave phenomena. For instance, Rashid and Tag-eldin employed the -expansion method to obtain traveling wave solutions for the fractional Maccari’s system with complex structure29, while Hammad et al. used it to analyze the fractional coupled Higgs system45. Similarly, the tanh-coth method has been utilized by Wazwaz to derive soliton and kink solutions for nonlinear parabolic equations31 and to investigate multiple-soliton solutions of the Sawada-Kotera-Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation46.

This work implements the generalized -expansion approach and tanh-coth method to generate and analyze solitary wave solutions to the Katugampola time-fractional -dimensional generalized Painlevé-type (also known as P-type) equation. The targeted model is extensively used to understand the dynamics of the plasma waves and instabilities in soliton theory, plasma physics and nonlinear wave theory. The model provides an excellent foundation of mathematical physics and a deep understanding of the behavior of complex systems in three space dimensions and one time dimension. It can expose important features and make nonlinear phenomena susceptible to evaluation, as evidenced by its validation by the Painlevé test. The equation of instabilities and plasma waves is used in various disciplines, such as plasma physics. The aimed model is a framework of investigating physical phenomena in quantum physics and other areas. This equation has been used to explain different material properties and spontaneous phenomena present in a solid-state system such as magnetism, and the classical particle physics theory. A more appropriate description of solitary wave dynamics is given by the time-fractional operator which generalizes the classical derivative to long-range interactions and nonlocal transmission of energy. When replicating solitary wave propagation where the evolution of the current wave is influenced by past states, the fractional model is more appropriate than the integer order model. This is because some phenomena, like anomalous nonlocal interactions, dispersion, and memory effects, are captured by fractional derivatives but not by integer order derivatives. Hence, the inclusion of the fractional derivative into the model is not merely a mathematical generalization, but a modification which has realistically motivated reasons, and which raises the ability of the model to capture nonlocationally and memory-specific effects.

The targeted model has been investigated in both its integer and fractional-order forms by several researchers using diverse analytical techniques. For instance, Jamshed et al. employed the two-variable -function method to derive soliton solutions for the truncated M-fractional -dimensional generalized Painlevé equation1. Wazwaz applied a simplified Hirota’s method to construct multiple-soliton solutions for a new -dimensional Painlevé integrable fifth-order equation with third-order temporal and spatial dispersions, also confirming its complete integrability via the Painlevé test47. Meanwhile, Rafiq and Chen analyzed the dynamics of three-wave solitons and other localized wave solutions for a generalized -dimensional P-type equation48. Remarkably, there is no literature that reported solitary wave solutions of the targeted model with Katugampola fractional derivative using -expansion method and tanh-coth method. The article bridges the gap of knowledge regarding the behavior of the proposed model and its fractional analogs. The paper offers an insight into the novel sophisticated dynamics of the model, with the use of sophisticated -expansion method and tanh-coth method. The proposed -expansion approach and tanh-coth method convert the desired model into a nonlinear algebraic system by providing a closed form traveling solutions. The resulting algebraic problem is analytically solved using the symbolic computation tool Maple, yielding a new set of solutions in terms of trigonometric, rational, and hyperbolic functions. In order to demonstrate the strong dynamics of the model, we exhibit a variety of 3D, density, and 2D visual representations that demonstrate the existence of bright and dark solitary wave solutions in the frameworks of the resulting wave profiles. Additionally, by examining how the time-fractional derivative influences the solutions of the given model, a more dynamic and useful framework suggested by Katugampola-derivative is employed to improve the validity of the results. The new findings improve the prediction models and decision making in the related domains and greatly improve our knowledge of the nonlinear wave dynamics and its practical implementation. The variegated array of findings also shows that the suggested approaches are useful mathematical tools for solving nonlinear FPDEs, with applications in mathematical sciences such as engineering, physics, biology and other nonlinear evolution problems.

Discussion and graphs

The present section of the study displays the graphical behaviors of several solitary waves found in the model being studied. Using the suggested approaches, we were able to derive and depict wave structures for the explicit solitary waves in 3-dimensional, density and 2-dimensional representations. Understanding these solitary wave patterns is necessary to comprehend the behavior of interconnected physical processes. A solitary wave is a confined non-dispersion waveform that maintains its shape and speed as it travels. Through graphical representations, we reveal the presence of bright and dark solitary waveforms in the frameworks of the resulting wave profiles that illustrate the strong dynamics of the model. The underlying nonlinear adjustability of the model and the fundamental balancing between nonlinearity and dispersion that defines mechanically appropriate wave behaviors is manifested in the form and extent of the nonlinear terms that define whether bright or dark solitary wave behaviour will be present in its context. Under the conditions of interaction of self-defocusing nonlinearity and dispersion, regional declines of the level or depressions on a dark background are observed as regional dark waves. These wave are signs of scattered phase defects or power dump of deforming media like Bose-Einstein condensates and optical fibers. Bright isolated waves, conversely, have locally concentrated pulse-like solutions that have peaks above the background. They indicate possible confinement in such phenomena as nonlinear optics and plasma media and are due to the balance between self-focusing nonlinearity and dispersion. Moreover, the parameter of the fractional-order has a very minor but prominent role in determining the propagation dynamics of the solitary wave solutions obtained. Based on the graphical analysis, it is seen that the amplitude of the soliton profiles do not change with various values of , meaning that the energy distribution of the wave is independent of the fractional order. There is however, non-uniform movement of the position of the peaks and troughs. In some scenarios reduction of () leads to the extrema to be at lower spatial coordinates whereas in others the extrema move towards bigger spatial location. This non-monotonic effect shows that the fractional parameter affects mostly the phase and propagation aspects of the wave and not the amplitude. Physically we can attribute this to the memory effect of fractional systems, which are characterized by the fact that the temporal evolution of a process is determined by the history of the process. Consequently, the propagation velocity of the fractional order adjusts the effective velocity of propagation and causes a delay or progress of the wave profile depending on the interplay of the system parameters and nonlinear effects. It is typical of fractional-order models, where nonlocality and memory can lead to non-uniform propagation behaviour that is not observed in classical integer-order models.

Conclusion

This study employed the generalized -expansion approach and tanh-coth method to generate and analyze solitary wave solutions to the Katugampola time-fractional -dimensional generalized Painlevé-type (also called P-type) equation. The proposed -expansion approach and tanh-coth method converted the desired model into a nonlinear algebraic system by providing a closed form traveling solutions. The system of algebraic equations obtained was solved analytically using the symbolic computation program Maple to provide a new set of solutions expressed as trigonometric, rational, and hyperbolic functions. To show important dynamics of the model we provided a range of 3D, density and 2D visual representations proving the presence of dark and bright solitary wave solutions in the case of the subsequently generated wave profiles. Further, a more active and helpful framework proposed by Katugampola-derivative was used to enhance the validity of the results by analyzing the effect of time-fractional derivative on the solutions of the given model. The comparison that we made has demonstrated the originality of the solutions that were produced since they were the initial report of developing solutions of the targeted model. As a consequence, by creating and researching new kinds of fractional solitary wave solutions, the research gap in the research body of the targeted model has been filled. The obtained bright and dark solitary wave solutions in different functions forms are not only new from a mathematical point of view but also from a physical perspective because they reveal the nature of nonlinear wave patterns, energy localization and stability in plasma physics and other nonlinear media where nonlocality and memory effects are important. Through the introduction of the Katugampola fractional derivative, the study also highlighted how the fractional-order parameters affect the amplitude, width, and speed of solitons, thus providing insight into the rich dynamics of wave propagation. The broad applicability of the obtained results also proved the fact that the given methods are useful mathematical tools to tackle nonlinear FPDEs, and they may be applied in the fields of mathematical science such as biology, physics, engineering, and other nonlinear evolution problems. Also, the proposed strategies have been carefully selected because they remain simple algebraic tools, which remove the use of complex procedures such as linearization, Lax pairings, bilinear forms, perturbation and other transformation processes that may be necessitated in other methods. The fact that the proposed approaches provide a variety of forms of solutions is one of the primary benefits of the approaches in comparison with more traditional approaches, since they can allow gaining a deeper and more in-depth understanding of the dynamics inherent in the desired model. It is necessary to add, however, that the proposed methods cannot work when the most significant nonlinear term and the highest derivative terms are not homogeneously balanced. The case of solitary wave solutions creation is non-existent due to the fact that the methodologies cannot balance the nonlinear term with the dispersion. Irrespective of this drawback, this current research shows that the methodologies adopted in the current work are very effective and easy to use in solving nonlinear problems in various fields of science.

Optimization of the radiosynthesis of the PSMA-targeting drugs [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 on the MiniLu™ automated module
Authors: David Alexoff, Karl Ploessl, Ruiyue Zhao, Linlin Li, Dohyun Kim, Lin Zhu, Hank Kung
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is a renewed interest in radioligand therapy for cancer treatment since the approval of Lu-177 vipivotide tetraxetan (PSMA-617) by regulators in Europe and the United States for metastatic castrate resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) patients. Recent expansion of indications for vipivotide tetraxetan in mCRPC by the FDA promises to increase demand for this class of radioligand therapy. The development of new targeted radioligands for mCRPC that improve binding specificity and increase radiation dose delivered to tumors is an active research space. [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 are new prostate specific membrane antigen (PSMA) targeted drugs under clinical investigation in China. Heretofore the preparation of these investigational drugs has been carried out manually. This report introduces a new cassette-based, radiosynthesis platform named MiniLu™ and describes the optimization and automation of the preparation of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 for clinical use.ResultsRadiolabeling of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 was optimized for pH, time, temperature and ligand concentration using 0.2 – 13 MBq of Lu-177 (> 740 GBq/mg) and MiniLu™. > 99% labeling yield measured by thin layer chromatography (TLC) was achieved with a pH = 5, 50–110 °C, 10 min, 3:1 mol ratio ligand: Lu-177; ligand mass > 3 nmole in 0.5 mL reaction solution. Quantitative labeling was confirmed using these conditions (80 °C) in simulations of clinical production doses of Lu-177 (7.4 GBq; > 3000 GBq/mg) by adding cold [natLu]LuCl3 (14 nmoles). [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 labeling yield was > 99% under the identical simulation conditions. A final product yield (activity in dose vial / starting Lu-177 activity) of ~ 95% was achieved after formulation with L-ascorbate and gentisic acid (15 mL) and sterile filtration using MiniLu™ and the simulated high activity conditions for both compounds. Final optimized concentrations for automated dose preparation were 62 µM (0.6 mL) and 70 µM (0.6 mL) for [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088, respectively. Over 100 independent automated MiniLu™ runs were completed successfully.Conclusions[177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 doses can be produced in high yield (95%) and purity (99%) using MiniLu™. Optimized, automated methods using MiniLu™ will facilitate further clinical investigations using [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 by standardizing preparation and reducing radiation exposure to radiochemists.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41181-025-00400-4.Keywords: [177Lu]Lu-P17-087, [177Lu]Lu-P17-088, Automated synthesis, PSMA, Lu-177, Radioligand therapy

Introduction

With the recent approval of Lu-177 vipivotide tetraxetan (Pluvitco® (Hennrich and Eder 2022; Advanced Accelerator Applications et al. 2022)) by regulators in Europe and the United States, radiopharmaceutical therapy using molecules targeting prostate specific membrane antigen (PSMA) is now a standard treatment option for advanced prostate cancer patients living in these jurisdictions. Other PSMA-targeted compounds (Hansen et al. 2024; Hawkins et al. 2024; Jang et al. 2023) are in late-phase development and promise to expand availability. In addition, several clinical trials (Jang et al. 2023; Eapen et al. 2024; Hofman et al. 2024; Privé et al. 2021; Morris et al. 2024) are investigating the efficacy of 177Lu-PSMA in patients earlier in disease progression, which could significantly increase the impact of 177Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy on prostate cancer treatment. In fact, the FDA recently approved expanded indications (pre taxane-based chemotherapy) for vipivotide tetraxetan in metastatic castrate resistant prostate cancer (FDA 2025).

New compounds designed to improve target specificity and maximize radiation dose to tumors using PSMA-targeting compounds are also under development using Lu-177 and other radionuclides, including Cu-67, Tb-161, Pb-212, and Ac-225 (Gäble et al. 2025; Johnson et al. 2024; Schaefer-Schuler et al. 2024; Feuerecker et al. 2021; Santos et al. 2019). We recently reported results from a single-site, early-phase clinical trial comparing two novel Lu-177 labeled PSMA-targeted radionuclide therapy agents (Li et al. 2024). These compounds ( [177Lu]Lu-P17-087; [177Lu]Lu-P17-088) employ a linker that has demonstrated superior imaging performance compared to [68 Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 (Wang et al. 2023) but include the DOTA(GA)2 (2,2′-(4,10-bis(carboxymethyl)-1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane-1,7-diyl)bis(5-amino-5-oxopentanoic acid)) chelator for radionuclide therapy (Zha et al. 2022). [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 includes an albumin-binding moiety designed to prolong blood residence time and increase dose delivered to tumors (Li et al. 2024). Promising results from these early phase trials have supported continuation of clinical studies, and early results from dose escalation studies from two sites have been recently reported for [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 (Ruiyue Zhao 2025) and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 (Wang et al. 2025).

Manufacturing of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 for locally approved hospital/compassionate use is currently done manually in certified, hospital-resident radiopharmacies (Li et al. 2024). Motivated by the need to standardize methods and reduce chemists' radiation exposures for possible future larger-scale clinical trials, we have designed and constructed an automated system appropriate for labeling with radiometals such as Lu-177. This work describes the optimization of the radiosynthesis of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 on a custom-made, cassette-based box named MiniLu™. MiniLu™ is compact and self-contained, requiring no external computer or gas pressure connections to operate, and is therefore easily located and shielded in a hospital-based radiopharmacy hood.

Methods

General

Carrier-added Lu-177 (> 740 GBq/mg) was purchased from the US National Isotope Development Center (Oak Ridge, TN USA). Sodium acetate trihydrate (BioUltra grade), water (ACS reagent, for ultratrace analysis), hydrochloric acid (30 – 35%, TraceSELECT™ Ultra, ultratrace analysis), LuCl3·6H2O (> 99.99% trace metal basis), 2,5-Dihydroxybenzoic acid (98%), ( +)-Sodium L-ascorbate (Bioultra, ≥ 99.0%) and DMSO (anhydrous) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Milwaukee, WI USA). 0.05 N HCl was made from concentrated HCl using ultratrace grade water. 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes were acquired from Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA USA). Polycarbonate stopcock manifolds for the automated system were purchased from Elcam-Medical (Hackensack, NJ USA). The reaction vessel was made of a Wheaton® (Millville, NJ USA) 10 mL E-Z EX-TRACTION™ vial, a threaded (22–400) phenolic cap (DWK, Millville, NJ) and a silicone septum with PTFE lining (20 × 3 mm) purchased from Tisch Scientific (Cleveland, OH USA). Silicone (Pharma 50) tubing for synthesis cassettes was purchased from Cole-Parmer (Vernon Hills, IL). The pH of reaction solutions was determined using pH paper (Supelco; pH 0 – 6 or 5 -10; 0.5 increments) purchased from Millipore Sigma (Burlington, MA USA). Sterile filters (Vented Millex GV) were purchased from Millipore Sigma.

For TLC, iTLC-SG chromatograph paper (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA USA) was used as the solid phase with a mobile phase consisting of a solution of methanol, water and ammonia (25%) (10:10:1 v/v/v). The plate was eluted, dried, and cut into two parts, and each part was assayed for Lu-177 activity (Rf 177Lu labeled products = 1). The activity was measured using a Packard Cobra II auto gamma counter (Perkin Elmer, Waltham, MA USA). Lu-177 activity measurements of the final product vial and production system components were done using a Capintec (Floral Park, NJ USA) Model CRC 127-R dose calibrator. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis was performed on an Agilent 1200 series HPLC equipped with NaI (TI) scintillation detector (Model 106, Ecker-Ziegler, Germany) with a reversed-phase column (Luna, C18, 150 × 4.6 mm, 5 microns, Phenomenex, USA). HPLC mobile phase: solvent A 0.1% TFA in water, solvent B: 0.1% TFA in acetonitrile; Flow rate 1 mL/min; 280 nm; 0—2 min 5% ACN, 2—18 min 5—99% ACN,18—25 min 99—5% ACN, 25–28 min 5% ACN. P17-087 (Lot P19-035) and P17-088 (Lot P19-036) were synthesized by Five Eleven Pharma according to published methods (Zha et al. 2022) (See Fig. 1).

MiniLu™ automation module

The custom automated system is comprised of 6 actuators controlling 6 stopcocks on two single-use, ganged stopcock manifolds. Two miniature diaphragm pumps (Parker Precision Fluidics, Hollis, NH USA) supply pressure and vacuum which is controlled using miniature solenoid valves (X-Valve®, Parker Precision Fluidics, Hollis, NH USA). A heated reactor was fabricated from standard copper plumbing fittings and a Kapton film heater made to fit a reaction vessel based on the 10 mL E-Z EX-TRACTION™ (Wheaton, Millville, NJ) vial. The heater and thermocouple were connected to a miniature temperature controller (Omega Engineering, Norwalk, CT USA). The Mini Maestro 24 servo controller (Pololu, Las Vegas, NV USA) interfaces all hardware including a push button, two LED lights and a toggle-type switch used to select test and run modes. A relay in series with the temperature controller’s switched heater power is used to control heating, and a small, switched fan is included for cooling. The temperature setpoint is changed manually using the front panel interface of the commercial controller and does not change during radiopharmaceutical preparation.

The Maestro servo controller includes a native USB port and internal scripting for automated sequencing which can be configured to run at power startup. A free, downloadable USB software development kit is available (https://github.com/pololu/pololu-usb-sdk). Two scripts, a pre-run test that leak checks internal and external plumbing and a labeling protocol, always reside in the system and are selectable by the toggle switch. The box includes a small mechanical compound gauge that the operator observes during testing to confirm the integrity of all plumbing before start of drug preparation.

The footprint of the system is 21 cm × 21 cm with a height of 25 cm. The box fits easily within the confines of a standard Lucite beta shield. A plumbing diagram showing the MiniLu™ configuration for this work appears in Fig. 2. This configuration was used for optimization studies and production runs described below. Additional box information can be found in supplemental information, including examples of other configurations commonly used in radiometal labeling (Figures S1 – S3).

Optimization of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 radiolabeling

For the initial labeling optimization studies (N = 66), a simplified automation script was written that (1) transferred the contents of the Lu-177 input vial (1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube) into the reaction vessel, (2) heated the reactor for a programmed time, and (3) cooled the reactor for 3 min using the internal fan. After cooling, the reaction vessel was removed and the contents analyzed by either TLC or HPLC to determine the radiolabeling efficiency. In some experiments, the system components (cassette, tubing, and vessels) were assayed for Lu-177 activity using an ion chamber. Lu-177 activity in the microcentrifuge tube was also determined before labeling using an ion chamber so that activity losses to system components could be calculated.

The effects of pH, time, temperature, and ligand mass on radiolabeling efficiency were investigated using P17-087. During box setup, P17-087 ligand (1 – 10 nmoles) was introduced manually into the reaction vessel by pipette using either a 1 mg/mL solution (10% DMSO/water) or a 0.1 mg/mL solution (1% DMSO/water). Buffer (2N NaOAc, pH 8.5) in varying amounts (15 – 400 µL), together with 0.05 N HCl (50 – 435 µL), was added to the 177Lu vial to adjust labeling pH (~ 2 – 7.5). The volume of diluted [177Lu]LuCl3 stock solution was fixed at 50 µL (0.2 – 13 MBq), so that the final reaction volume was always ~ 0.51 – 0.54 mL including ligand volume. The [177Lu]LuCl3 stock solution was made by adding 0.5 mL of 0.05 N HCl to the shipping vial, followed by a tenfold dilution with 0.05 N HCl (1 mL final volume). The temperature setpoint of the reactor was varied from 30 to 110 °C. The reaction time was programmed to be 5 – 45 min. The reaction vessel was not preheated, and heating started after the transfer of the 177Lu-vial contents. The starting temperature was always room temperature (~ 16 – 19 °C).

Effects of 177Lu specific activity on radiolabeling—simulation of production-level mass

After optimized conditions were obtained using P17-087, the specific activity of the Lu-177 was altered to simulate the Lu mass associated with large-scale production (3.7 – 7.4 GBq) needed for clinical doses. A minimum Lu-177 specific activity of 3000 GBq/mg was assumed, which gives 7 nmoles Lu-177 for a 3.7 GBq dose. A solution of 13.5 mg/50 mL LuCl3·6H2O was prepared, and 10 µL or 20 µL was added to the reaction vessel during setup to simulate Lu mass associated with 3.7 GBq and 7.4 GBq of Lu-177 activity (3000 GBq/mg), respectively. For the 3.7 GBq mass simulation, the mole ratio of ligand to lutetium was tested at ~ 1.5 (N = 3), 2.0 (N = 3), and 3.0 (N = 3). The reproduction of the mass associated with a 7.4 GBq activity run was tested using only a mole ratio of 3.0 (N = 3). The radiolabeling using both P17-087 and P17-088 was tested under the 7.4 GBq simulation run conditions (14 nmoles [natLu]LuCl3 added; mole ratio ligand: Lu = 3:1; N = 3 for each compound).

Sequence of radiolabeling optimization experiments

Initial conditions (pH, time, temperature) were based on published methods (Li et al. 2024) except using 10 nmoles of ligand. Using these initial conditions, first time was varied from 5 – 45 min. Then an intermediate (approximate midpoint of constant yield portion of the curve) time was chosen and the mass of ligand decreased to obtain a “minimum” amount giving a high (> 95%) yield. This mass amount was used to find the optimum pH range followed by generating a temperature response at the best pH using the minimum mass. Finally, time and temperature studies were repeated over a narrower range of temperature (30–80 °C) and time (5 – 20 min) using the optimized pH and minimum mass previously determined.

Preparation of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 using MiniLu™

Using the optimized radiolabeling conditions with up to ~ 370 MBq Lu-177 (~ 4.2 nmoles @ calibration), experiments were carried out using a script that automates radiopharmaceutical preparation, including transfer of Lu-177 activity, radiolabeling, dilution, formulation, and sterile filtration. In some experiments, 14 nmoles of [natLu]LuCl3 was added to ~ 7 – 11 MBq Lu-177 to simulate the Lu mass associated with 7.4 GBq of high specific activity Lu-177 as done during radiolabeling optimization. For all runs, a 20 mL syringe was loaded with 15 mL of an aqueous solution of sodium ascorbate (50 mg/mL), and gentisic acid (0.4 mg/mL). This solution was used in two portions for dilution (~ 5 mL), then rinsing (~ 10 mL) during automated production. Each portion was filtered sequentially into the same final product vial. In selected runs, a 3 mL syringe filled with 100 µL pH 5.0 (0.12 M) acetate buffer was included during setup (Fig. 2) as a rinse before labeling. The Lu-177 buffer vial’s activity was assayed before machine preparation using an ion chamber and used to determine radiochemical yield (RCY). In some instances, components of the single-use cassette assembly including the cassette, Lu-177 inlet transfer line, reaction vessel, sterilizing filter etc. were assayed for Lu-177 activity using an ion chamber to determine the amount and distribution of residual Lu-177 activity in the system. TLC and HPLC analyses of a sample from the final product vial was used to determine the radiochemical purity (RCP) at end of synthesis.

Stability of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [.177Lu]Lu-P17-088

Samples from selected radiopharmaceutical preparations (formulated and filtered) were obtained after stored at room temperature for 10–12 days and radiochemical purities were determined by TLC and HPLC.

Effects of Lu-177 specific activity – clinical dose mass simulations

A temperature of 80 °C with 10 min heating (pH 5.0) was chosen as “production” radiolabeling conditions. The results of the simulated (high Lu mass) production runs as a function of mole ratio of ligand: LuCl3 under these conditions are presented in Fig. 6. A mole ratio of 3:1 ligand:Lu gave very reproducible yields (99.6% ± 0.02%; N = 3) at the lower production simulation conditions (7 nmoles LuCl3) using [177Lu]Lu-P17-087. Further scaling using 14 nmoles LuCl3 and the same mole ratio, achieved a nearly quantitative yield (99.7% ± 0.06%; N = 3), the same as obtained using 7 nmoles. Nearly quantitative labeling was also confirmed using [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 (99.7% ± 0.06%; N = 3) with 14 nmoles LuCl3 added and a mole ratio of 3:1. A 14 nmole run simulates the mass associated with a typical single-dose clinical production (e.g.: 7.4 GBq Lu-177 @ 3000 GBq/mg).

MiniLu™ radiopharmaceutical production

The results from initial production experiments using ~ 3.7 – 370 MBq Lu-177, including one experiment with added [natLu]LuCl3 as described above, showed a radiochemical purity of the final product of ~ 95%, less than the expected high purity (> 98%) achieved during the radiolabeling optimization studies. Closer visual examination of automated sequencing revealed liquid droplets remaining in the cassette and reaction vessel inlet line and needle (hub) after transfer of Lu-177. Attempts to reduce these losses including by toggling automatically the inlet stopcock during Lu-177 transfer and eliminating the needle hub by construction of a PEEK needle did not improve radiochemical purity. Introduction of 100 µL pH 5.0 (0.12 M) acetate buffer rinse of the inlet path after transfer for the Lu-177 activity (in 0.5 mL buffer) restored the radiochemical purity to > 98%. Final validation studies were carried out using the rinse step, input stopcock toggling and a PEEK reactor needle with and without the addition of [natLu]LuCl3. The P17-088 ligand amount was increased ~ 16% from the optimization studies to achieve reproducible results. Table 3 below summarizes the results from these studies, showing consistent and high RCY (~ 95%) and RCP (~ 99%) for all runs.

Stability of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 preparations

The stability of the final validation studies simulating clinical production mass levels showed little to no change in RCP measured by both TLC and HPLC after 10—12 days storage at room temperature. Discussion

A systematic optimization of labeling conditions has not yet been reported for [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088, two new PSMA-targeted drugs undergoing clinical trials in China (Li et al. 2024; Ruiyue Zhao GMU, 2025). It is preferrable to establish automated protocols that standardize methods and reduce radiation exposure to personnel before larger scale trials are carried out (Hunt et al. 2025). The goal of this work was to develop and validate automated, optimized methods suitable for clinical production of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088. Our prospective goals for automated radiosynthesis were RCY > 90% and RCP > 98%.

Quantitative labeling was achieved (pH ~ 5.0, 50–110 °C, 10 min, 3:1 mol ratio ligand: Lu; ligand mass > 3 nmole; reaction volume ~ 0.5 mL) and 80 °C was chosen for routine production. After radiolabeling only was optimized using the MiniLu reactor, fully automated runs, including formulation and filtration, were carried out. Unexpectedly, the RCP of the final product from these runs was only ~ 95% using conditions that gave quantitative labeling yields. There is a short, shared plumbing path used for both introduction of the raw material Lu-177 and dilution and recovery of the [177Lu]Lu-labeled reaction solution. Only 25 µL of Lu-177 input solution would decrease purity by 5% based on volume of reaction. Introduction of a 100 µL rinse (pH 5 NaOAc buffer) was necessary to restore high radiochemical purity (~ 99%), even though dilution of the reaction volume by 20%, from 0.5 mL to 0.6 mL, was not desirable since it differed from the volume used during labeling optimization. This may in part be why the concentration of P17-088 ligand needed for consistent high (> 98%) RCP was different (increased 16%) than that needed for quantitative labeling during optimization. [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 labeling was not affected by the addition of the rinse volume, and this final method resulted in machine RCYs (~ 95%) and RCPs (~ 99%), exceeding our prospective goals for both compounds.

The optimized conditions in this work are similar to those used for routine clinical production of other PSMA-targeted drugs, except for somewhat milder labeling conditions in this work (See Table 4). Since both the linker and chelator are different for [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 compared to either [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 or [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T, some differences in labeling conditions might be expected, not to mention differences in machine design (reactor) that could impact labeling conditions. Of note is that the milder and shorter labeling conditions reported in this work minimize the possibility of formation of thermally mediated impurities stemming from the PSMA binding motif that have been reported previously (Martin et al. 2021; Larenkov et al. 2024).

Automated methods for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T, summarized in Table 4, have been reported (Hunt et al. 2025; Wichmann et al. 2021; Sørensen et al. 2020; Weineisen et al. 2015; Cankaya et al. 2023) using 15 µg – 27 µg ligand/GBq NCA Lu-177 (~ 77 – 140 nmoles for 7.4 GBq [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617, for example). Only 42 nmoles of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and 49 nmoles of [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 were needed to achieve > 98% RCP using [natLu]LuCl3 to simulate 7.4 GBq of NCA [177Lu]LuCl3, reflected in the lower mole ratios in Table 4. Although lower precursor mass is beneficial—increasing molar activity and lowering costs – this result must be taken with caution as validation was carried out with only fractions of a 1.85 GBq delivery of low specific activity Lu-177, which may not reflect the true conditions during labeling with 7.4 GBq NCA Lu-177 (see below, limitations).

No purification was needed after radiolabeling to obtain the high RCP (> 98%) desired for clinical applications. Automated methods avoiding SepPak purification are simpler and therefore potentially more robust. In contrast, some automated preparations of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvitco®) (Hennrich and Eder 2022) and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T (Cankaya et al. 2023; Kraihammer et al. 2023; Schmitl et al. 2023) require solid phase extraction to ensure high purity, though others also do not include a purification step after labeling (Hunt et al. 2025; Wichmann et al. 2021; Sørensen et al. 2020) (See also Table 4). Radiochemical yields for preparation of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 using MiniLu™ (range: 93 – 96%) were comparable to that reported from accepted commercial machines producing clinical doses of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 (Hunt et al. 2025; Wichmann et al. 2021) and [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T (Sørensen et al. 2020; Cankaya et al. 2023).

A limitation of this work is the low activity (< 370 MBq) used to validate the final optimized clinical production conditions. Apart from potential effects of radiolysis at high activities needed for clinical doses that were not evaluated, it is also known that metal ion impurities, which can compete with the ligand, are present with Lu-177 (Hunt et al. 2025, Larenkov et al. 2024). As a result, a greater quantity of ligand may be required to offset these impurities when utilizing 100% of a Lu-177 shipment. For example, in one production experiment with [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 using 158 MBq (~ 25% of shipment volume), a low RCP (< 70%) was obtained using 11 nmoles of ligand (18 µM; ligand:Lu mole ratio = 3.7; no [natLu]LuCl3 added) with optimized pH, temperature and time. Optimization studies using < 1% of the total Lu-177 shipment volume and these same conditions gave nearly quantitative labeling with only 3 nmoles (6 µM) ligand and a ligand:Lu mole ratio of 3 using added [natLu]LuCl3, implying an effect of Lu-177 shipment volume (1% vs 25%). Although scaled up production using > 25% the total Lu-177 shipment was successful in two experiments (see Table 3), further studies using clinical dose activity levels are needed to complete validation of using MiniLu™ with < 50 nmoles ligand.

MiniLu™ performed reliably (no failures) during this study comprised of more than 100 separate runs including the labeling optimization experiments. The device is compact and simple and does not require an external computer or gas connections to run. MiniLu™ is intended for use by on-site producers, incorporating a flexible reactor design where microliter precursor solutions can be introduced directly by pipette into the reactor, which is easily assembled during setup. MiniLu™ is constructed of mostly off-the-shelf, commercially available components and open-source software found in a robot/electronic hobby store and therefore can be easily reproduced.

Conclusion

[177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 labeling was optimized using the MiniLu™ automated module. The reaction was scaled up to ~ 370 MBq with simulations of 7.4 GBq clinical doses completed successfully using added [natLu]LuCl3. Standardized, automated methods suitable for clinical production of [177Lu]Lu-P17-087 and [177Lu]Lu-P17-088 using MiniLu™ were demonstrated and may facilitate larger clinical trials using these promising, new PSMA-targeted radiotherapies.

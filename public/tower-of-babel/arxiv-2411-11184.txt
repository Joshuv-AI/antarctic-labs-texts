A Lie group corresponding to the free Lie algebra
and its universality

Abstract




Consider the real free Lie algebra
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}
with generators

\omega_{1}
, …,

\omega_{n}
. Since it is positively graded, it has a
completion
\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
consisting of formal series.
By the Campbell–Hausdorff formula, we have a corresponding Lie group

\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
. It is the set

\exp\bigl(\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}\bigr)
in the completed universal
enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}
.
Also, the group
\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
is a ’submanifold’ in the algebra of formal
associative noncommutative series
in
\omega_{1}
, …,

\omega_{n}
, the ’submanifold’ is determined by a certain system of quadratic equations. We consider a certain dense subgroup

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}

with a stronger (Polish)
topology and show that any
homomorphism
\pi
from
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}
to a real finite-dimensional Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
can be integrated in a unique way to a homomorphism
\Pi
from
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
to the corresponding simply connected Lie group
G
.
If
\pi
is surjective, then
\Pi
also is surjective.
Note that
Pestov (1993) constructed a separable Banach–Lie group such that
any separable Banach–Lie group is its quotient.

Key words.
Free Lie algebra, universal enveloping algebra, Lie groups, Banach algebra, Banach–Lie group.

The group
\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
. Preliminaries

1.1. Free associative algebras.

Denote by
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
the real free associative algebra
generated by
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the set of all words
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}
,
where
k=0
,
1
,
2
,
\dots
, and each
\alpha_{j}
is a positive
integer
\leqslant n
.
Denote

|\alpha|=|\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}|:=k.

We denote monomials in
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
by

\omega^{\alpha}:=\omega_{\alpha_{1}}\dots\omega_{\alpha_{k}},\qquad\text{where $\alpha=\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}$.}

In this notation,
elements of
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
are finite sums:

(1.1)

x=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}.

We define
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
-grading of
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
assuming
\deg\omega_{j}=1
.
So,
\deg\omega^{\alpha}=|\alpha|
.
The algebra
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
is a direct sum of homogeneous subspaces,

\mathrm{Afr}_{n}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[j]}.

Each element
x\in\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
is
a sum of its homogeneous components,

(1.2)

x=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}x^{[j]},\qquad\text{where $x^{[j]}\in\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[j]}$}.

We also denote by

\phantom{A}{}^{+}\!\mathrm{Afr}_{n}:=\bigoplus_{j>0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[j]}

the ideal of
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
consisting of elements with zero constant term.

By
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
we denote the algebra of formal series
in
\omega_{j}
, i.e., formal series of the form
(
1.1
). We say that a sequence

x_{l}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}^{(l)}\omega^{\alpha}\in\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}

converges
to

x=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}c_{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}\in\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
if for each
\alpha
we have a convergence
of coefficients

c_{\alpha}^{(l)}\to c_{\alpha}
.
So we get a topology of a direct product of a countable number
of copies
of
{\mathbb{R}}
enumerated by
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.
Thus,
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
is a Fréchet space

2

Fréchet space

is a complete topological vector space whose topology is defined by a countable family of seminorms,
in our case
\|x\|_{(\alpha)}=|c_{\alpha}|
, where
\alpha
ranges in
\mathcal{A}
.
.

We have
a well-defined

.
Namely,
for
x

\displaystyle\exp x:

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\,x^{m},\qquad\text{notice, that we have $\exp(x)-1\in\vphantom{A}^{+}\!\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}$;}

\displaystyle\ln(1+y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\,y^{m}\,\,\in\vphantom{A}^{+}\!\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n},

these series converge in
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
,
since in each homogeneous component
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[j]}

we actually have a finite sum. As usual,

\exp(\ln(1+y))=1+y

For
\xi\geqslant 0
we define the endomorphism
\mathcal{M}_{\xi}:\mathrm{Afr}_{n}\to\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
by

\mathcal{M}_{\xi}\omega_{j}=\xi\omega_{j},

or, equivalently,

\mathcal{M}_{\xi}\omega^{\alpha}=\xi^{|\alpha|}\omega^{\alpha},\quad\mathcal{M}_{\xi}\bigl(\sum_{j}x^{[j]}\bigr)=\sum_{j}\xi^{j}x^{[j]}.

Clearly, if
\xi>0

\mathcal{M}_{\xi}

\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
.
It extends by continuity to an automorphism of

\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
.

For
\xi=0

\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
defined by
\sum_{j}x^{[j]}\mapsto x^{[0]}
.

We also have a
canonical anti-automorphism

of
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
(and of
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
)
defined by

\omega^{\circ}=-\omega,\qquad(xy)^{\circ}=y^{\circ}x^{\circ},\qquad(x+y)=x^{\circ}+y^{\circ}.

In other words,

1.2. The coproduct.
Denote by
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}\otimes\mathrm{Afr}_{n}

the tensor product
of the algebra
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
with itself, i.e., the real associative algebra

\omega^{\alpha}\otimes\omega^{\beta}

\omega^{\alpha}\otimes\omega^{\beta}\,\cdot\,\omega^{\alpha^{\prime}}\otimes\omega^{\beta^{\prime}}=\omega^{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha^{\prime}}\otimes\omega^{\beta}\omega^{\beta^{\prime}}.

For each

For any monomial

\displaystyle w_{17}\mapsto w_{17},\quad[\omega_{1},\omega_{2}]\mapsto\omega_{1}\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}\omega_{1},

\mathfrak{fr}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Afr}_{n},\quad\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}\subset\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}.

Theorem 1.1
.

a)
The map
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}\to\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
is an embedding.

b)
The algebra
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}
is the universal enveloping algebra of

\mathfrak{fr}_{n}

c)
An element

is contained in
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}

if and only if

\Delta(z)=z\otimes 1+1\otimes z.

d)
The statements

take place for

\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
and
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}

1.4. Free Lie groups.

Recall Campbell–Hausdorff theorem, see, e.g.,

[
21
]
, Sect. IV.7,
[
20
]
, Ch. 3.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
x
,
y\in\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
, there exists a unique

z\in\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
such that

\exp(x)\exp(y)=\exp(z).

Therefore, the image of
\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
under
the exponential map
\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}\to\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
is a group with respect to the multiplication. Denote it by
\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
.

Clearly,
x\in\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}
satisfies
x^{\circ}=-x
.
Therefore,

\exp(x)^{\circ}=\exp(x^{\circ})=\exp(-x)=\exp(x)^{-1}.

Thus, for
g\in\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
we have

(1.4)

g^{-1}=g^{\circ}.

Elements of this group can be characterized
in the following more convenient way (see Ree
[
19
]
, see also
[
20
]
, Theorem 3.2):

Theorem 1.3
.

An element
g\in\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
is contained in

\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
if and only if

(1.5)

\Delta g=g\otimes g.

Let
g=\sum c_{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}
. Then the condition (
1.5
) can be
written as a system of quadratic equations

(1.6)

c_{\alpha}c_{\beta}=\sum_{I}c_{\mu(I)},

where the summation is taken over all

|\alpha|
-element subsets
I\subset\mathcal{P}(|\alpha|+|\beta|)

and
\mu(I)\in\mathcal{A}
is defined from the conditions:

—
|\mu|=|\alpha|+|\beta|
;

—
\omega^{\mu(I)|I}=\omega^{\alpha}
,
\omega^{\mu(I)|\backslash I}=\omega^{\beta}
.

So, the summation is taking over all the
shuffles
of monomials

\omega^{\alpha}
,
\omega^{\beta}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

The group
\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
is closed in
\overline{\mathrm{Afr}}_{n}
.

1.5. Differentiations of paths and ordered exponentials.
Below we need the following simple arguments
(see, e.g.,
[
11
]
), which allow to apply Volterra type integral equations to the free Lie groups. We must fix a degree of generality,
which is necessary below for the proof of Proposition

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
q(t)=\sum_{j}q^{[j]}
be a path in
\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}
, let
q(t)
has
a derivative at
t=t_{0}
, i.e., each homogeneous component
q^{[j]}(t)

has a derivative.
Then

(1.7)

q(t_{0})^{-1}q^{\prime}(t_{0})\in\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
t_{0}=0
.
Denote
q:=q(0)
,
q^{\prime}:=q^{\prime}(0)
.
Then

\Delta q(\varepsilon)=\Delta\bigl(q+\varepsilon q^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon)\bigr)=\Delta q\cdot\bigl(1+\varepsilon\Delta(q^{-1}q^{\prime})+o(\varepsilon)\bigr)=\\
=(q\otimes q)\cdot\bigl(1+\varepsilon\Delta(q^{-1}q^{\prime})+o(\varepsilon)\bigr),

where
o(\varepsilon)
denotes an expression, which is

o(\varepsilon)
in each homogeneous
component. Actually, in
j
-th homogeneous component
we have in the rind hand side a polynomial
in
g^{[1]}
, …
g^{[j]}
,
(g^{\prime})^{[1]}
, …,
(g^{\prime})^{[j]}

plus
o(\varepsilon)
.
On the other hand,

q(\varepsilon)\otimes q(\varepsilon)=(q\otimes q)\cdot\bigl(1+\varepsilon q^{-1}q^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon))\otimes(1+\varepsilon q^{-1}q^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon)\bigr)=\\
=(q\otimes q)\cdot\bigl(1+\varepsilon(1\otimes q^{-1}q^{\prime}+q^{-1}q^{\prime}\otimes 1)+o(\varepsilon)\bigr).

Comparing coefficients at
\varepsilon

and keeping in mind Friedrichs theorem
1.1
, we come to the desired statement.

\square

Lemma 2.2
.

The maps
[0,1]\times\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}\to\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}
,

[0,1]\times\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\infty}\to\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\infty}

given by

(t,x)\to\mathcal{M}_{t}x

are continuous.

Proof.
It is sufficient to verify the statement for
\xi=1
.
Let
t_{k}\to t
,
x_{k}\to x
. Then

\|\mathcal{M}_{t_{k}}x_{k}-\mathcal{M}_{t}x\|_{1}\leqslant\|\mathcal{M}_{t_{k}}x_{k}-\mathcal{M}_{t_{k}}x\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{M}_{t_{k}}x-\mathcal{M}_{t}x\|_{1}=\\
=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\|t_{k}^{j}x_{k}^{[j]}-t_{k}^{j}x^{[j]}\|_{1}+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\|t_{k}^{j}x^{[j]}-t^{j}x^{[j]}\|_{1}=\\
=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}t_{k}^{j}\,\|x_{k}^{[j]}-x^{[j]}\|_{1}+\sum_{j\geqslant 0}|t_{k}^{j}-t^{j}|\cdot\|x^{[j]}\|_{1}.

The first series is
\leqslant\sum\|x_{k}^{[j]}-x^{[j]}\|_{1}=\|x_{k}-x\|_{1}\to 0
.
The second series is dominated by a convergent series

\sum\|x^{[j]}\|_{1}=\|x\|_{1}
. By the dominated convergence
theorem, the termwise convergence of summands
to 0 implies a convergence of their sums to 0.

2.2. Completed free Lie algebras and the corresponding Lie groups.

We define

\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\xi}:=\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}\cap\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi},\quad\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}:=\overline{\mathfrak{fr}}_{n}\cap\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\infty},

The algebras
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\xi}
are Banach spaces, the algebra
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}

is a Fréchet space. In both cases,
the brackets
[\cdot,\cdot]
are jointly continuous.

Following
[
13
]
, we also define groups

\mathrm{Fr}^{\xi}_{n}:=\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}\cap\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi},\qquad\mathrm{Fr}^{\infty}_{n}:=\overline{\mathrm{Fr}}_{n}\cap\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\infty}=\cap_{\xi>0}\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi},

by (
1.4
) these intersections are closed with respect
the inversion
g\mapsto g^{-1}
. We equip these groups with
topologies induced from
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}
and
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\infty}
respectively.

Clearly, all groups
\mathrm{Fr}^{\xi}_{n}
, where
\xi>0
, are canonically isomorphic.

Proposition 2.3
.

The subsets
\mathrm{Fr}^{\xi}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Afr}^{\xi}_{n}
,

\mathrm{Fr}^{\infty}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Afr}^{\infty}_{n}
are closed.
Groups
\mathrm{Fr}^{\xi}_{n}
,
\mathrm{Fr}^{\infty}_{n}
are Polish
3
3

3

A Polish space is a topological space
homeomorphic to a complete separable metric space. A Polish
group is a topological group, which is a Polish space.
.

Proof.

The condition
\Delta g=g\otimes g
is a countable system of
conditions (
1.6
). Each condition determines a quadratic surface
in a finite dimensional space

\bigl(\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[|\alpha|]}+\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[|\beta|]}\bigr)\oplus\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{[|\alpha|+|\beta|]},

which is a quotient of the whole space, and therefore
each condition (
1.6
) determines a closed subset.

A closed subset in a Polish space is Polish.

\square

Proposition 2.4
.

The groups
\mathrm{Fr}^{\xi}_{n}
and
\mathrm{Fr}^{\infty}_{n}
are
contractible
(
and therefore linear connected and simply connected
)
.

Proof.
If
g
satisfies the condition
\Delta g=g\otimes g
,
then
\mathcal{M}_{t}g
satisfies the same condition.
By Lemma
2.2
, the maps
[0,1]\times\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}\to\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
,
[0,1]\times\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}\to\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}

given by
(t,g)\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{t}g
are continuous.
For
t=1
we have an identical map from the group to itself, for
t=0

the group is mapped to the unit.

\square

Since
\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}
are Banach algebras, the exponential

\vphantom{A}{}^{+}\!\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}

Restricting these maps to the free Lie algebras, we get well-defined exponentials

The group
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}

\square

Observation 2.6
.

The image of the exponential
map
\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}\to\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
does not contain
a neighborhood of the unit.

Proof.
The curve

\mu(t)=\exp(t\omega_{1})\,\exp(t\omega_{2})

is continuous
in
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
and passes through the unit (
\mu(0)=1
).
Let us show that
\mu(t)
is not contained in the image of the exponential
map for all
t>0
.
Consider its logarithm,

\ln\mu(t)=\ln\Bigl[\Bigl(1+t\omega_{1}+\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\,\omega_{1}^{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(1+t\omega_{2}+\sum_{j\geqslant 2}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\,\omega_{2}^{j}\Bigr)\Bigr]=\\
=\ln\bigl[1+t(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})+\dots\bigr]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\,t^{k}}{k}\,(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})^{k}+\dots{}

Taking
k=2m
, we observe summands

\Bigl(-\frac{t^{2m}}{2m}(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})^{m}\Bigr)

and
\Bigl(-\frac{t^{2m}}{2m}(\omega_{2}\omega_{1})^{m}\Bigr)
.
The whole series for
\ln\mu(t)
does not contain other similar
summands with
(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})^{m}
,
(\omega_{2}\omega_{1})^{m}

— all other monomials of the expansion contain subwords
of the form
\omega_{1}^{i}
with
i\geqslant 2

or
\omega_{2}^{j}
with
j\geqslant 2
. So the series

\ln\mu(t)

contains a subseries

-\sum_{m>0}\frac{t^{2m}}{2m}\Bigl((\omega_{1}\omega_{2})^{m}+(\omega_{2}\omega_{1})^{m}\Bigr).

If
t>\xi^{-1}
, then
\ln\mu(t)\notin\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}
.
Therefore,
\ln\mu(t)\notin\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}
for all
t>0
.

\square

Proposition 2.7
.

The subgroup in
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
generated by the image

\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}\to\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
is
dense in
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
.

The statement is proved in the next subsection.

2.3. Ordered exponentials in
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
.

Here we prove Proposition
2.7
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\xi}
be a measurable map.
Denote

I_{\xi}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\|\gamma(t)\|_{\xi}\,dt.

a)
Let
I_{\xi}(\gamma)<\infty
.
Then its ordered exponential
\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]
is a continuous map

[0,1]\to\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
.

b)
Let
\gamma
,
\gamma_{j}:[0,1]\to\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\xi}
be measurable maps,
such that
I_{\xi}(\gamma)
,
I_{\xi}(\gamma_{j})<\infty
.
Let
I_{\xi}(\gamma_{j}-\gamma)\to 0
.
Then
\|\mathcal{E}[\gamma_{k};t]-\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]\|_{\xi}
converges to
0
uniformly in
t
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

a) We consider
the Banach space
W_{\xi}=C([0,1],\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi})
of continuous functions

[0,1]\to\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}

equipped
with the uniform norm
\max_{t}\|f(t)\|_{\xi}
.
Consider a Volterra integral operator
A_{\gamma}
in this space given by

A_{\gamma}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,\gamma(\tau)\,d\tau,

its norm is
\leqslant I_{\xi}(\gamma)
. Indeed,

\|A_{\gamma}f\|_{W_{\xi}}=\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\|\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,\gamma(\tau)\,d\tau\|_{\xi}\leqslant\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,\|\gamma(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,d\tau=\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\|f(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,\|\gamma(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,d\tau\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\|\gamma(\tau)\|_{\xi}\,d\tau\cdot\max\limits_{\tau\in[0,1]}\|f(\tau)\|_{\xi}=I_{\xi}(\gamma)\cdot\|f\|_{W_{\xi}}.

The same estimate gives

\|A_{\gamma}-A_{\mu}\|_{W_{\xi}}\leqslant I_{\xi}(\gamma-\mu).

To find the ordered exponential
f=\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]
, we must solve the integral equation
f=1+A_{\gamma}f
, see (
1.10
).
Spectral radii of Volterra operators in Banach spaces
of continuous functions are
0
, see textbooks on functional
analysis, e.g.
[
9
]
, Subsect. XIII.6.2,
the same estimates hold for spaces of continuous functions with
values in Banach spaces.
So
(1-A_{\gamma})^{-1}
is a bounded
operator. Therefore,

\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]=(1-A_{\gamma})^{-1}\cdot 1\in C([0,1],\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi}).

b) The inversion of a linear operator
S\mapsto S^{-1}
in a Banach space is
a continuous operation with respect to the norm topology, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
, Subsect. V.4.6. So, the sequence

(1-A_{\gamma_{k}})^{-1}\cdot 1
uniformly converges to
(1-A_{\gamma})^{-1}\cdot 1
.

\square

Lemma 2.9
.

For
g\in\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
, we have

\gamma(t):=(\mathcal{M}_{t}g)^{-1}\cdot\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{t}g\,\,\in\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty},

\gamma(t)
is continuous,
and

\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]=\mathcal{M}_{t}g.

Proof.
Clearly, the derivative

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{t}g=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j}t^{j}g^{[j]}=\sum_{j}jt^{j-1}g^{[j]}

is contained in
\mathrm{Afr}^{\infty}_{n}
and is continuous in
t
.
By Proposition
1.5
, we get the first claim.
On the other hand, maps
t\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{t}g^{[j]}=t^{j}g^{[j]}

are absolutely continuous on finite intervals, and we refer to Proposition
1.6
.

\square

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{F}\subset\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
the subgroup
generated by the image
of the exponential map.

If
\gamma
is constant, when
\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]=\exp(\gamma t)\in\mathfrak{F}
. If
\gamma
is locally constant, then
\mathcal{E}[\gamma;t]

is a product of several exponentials and also is contained in
\mathfrak{F}
.

Fix
g\in\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
, denote
\gamma(t):=(\mathcal{M}_{t}g)^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{t}g)^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{fr}_{n}^{\infty}
as in Lemma
2.9
. So,
\mathcal{E}[\gamma;1]=g
.
Fix an increasing sequence

\xi_{k}\to\infty
and a decreasing sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
.
For each
k
we choose a piece-wise constant function
\gamma_{k}

such that

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\gamma_{k}(t)-\gamma(t)\|_{\xi_{k}}<\varepsilon_{k}.

To do this, for each
N
we
set
\Gamma_{N}(t):=\gamma(p/N)
if
p/N\leqslant t<(p+1)/N
.
Since the function
\gamma(t)
is continuous
as a function
[0,1]\mapsto\mathrm{Afr}_{n}^{\xi_{k}}
, it is uniformly
continuous, and therefore

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\gamma(t)-\Gamma_{N}(t)\|_{\xi_{k}}

tends to 0 as
N\to\infty
.
For sufficiently large
L=L(k)

we have
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\|\gamma(t)-\Gamma_{L}(t)\|_{\xi_{k}}<\varepsilon_{k}
,
and we can take

\gamma_{k}(t):=\Gamma_{L(k)}(t)
.

Since the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\xi_{k}}
are increasing,
for a fixed
\xi_{m}
we have

\|\gamma_{k}(t)-\gamma(t)\|_{\xi_{m}}\leqslant\|\gamma_{k}(t)-\gamma(t)\|_{\xi_{k}}\to 0

as

k\to\infty
. By Lemma
2.8
, the sequence

\mathcal{E}[\gamma_{k};1]\in\mathfrak{F}
converges to
\mathcal{E}[\gamma;1]=g

in each
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
and so converges in
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
.

\square

2.4. Universality.
Let
G
be a connected real Lie group, let
\mathfrak{g}
be its Lie algebra, let
\mathop{\mathrm{Exp}}\nolimits:\mathfrak{g}\to G
be the exponential
map. Recall that its image contains a neighborhood
of the unit in
G
. Let
X_{1}
, …,
X_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then we have a canonical homomorphism
\pi:\mathfrak{fr}_{n}\to\mathfrak{g}

such that
\pi(\omega_{j})=X_{j}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

There exists a unique homomorphism

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}\to G
satisfying

(2.1)

\Pi(\exp(z))=\mathop{\mathrm{Exp}}\nolimits(\pi(z))\qquad\text{for any $z\in\mathfrak{fr}_{n}$.}

If
X_{1}
, …,
X_{n}
generate
\mathfrak{g}
, then

\Pi
is surjective.

We also formulate similar statements for the groups

\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Under the same conditions:

a)

There is
\xi>0
such that there exists a
homomorphism

\Pi:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}\to G
satisfying the condition
(
2.1
).
If
X_{1}
, …,
X_{n}
generate
\mathfrak{g}
, then

\Pi
is surjective.

b)
If for some
\xi>0
there exists a homomorphism

\Pi:\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}\to G
satisfying (
2.1
),
then such homomorphism is unique.

Remarks.

a)
These statements are very simple but I have not seen
them in literature. Pestov
[
15
]
constructed
a separable Banach–Lie
group, such that any separable Banach–Lie group can be realized
as its quotient group. Formally, our statements do not follow
from Theorem 3.5 of
[
15
]
. In any case, we provide an explicit construction
of the universal group,
and
our arguments are very elementary.

b) In particular, we see that unitary groups
\mathrm{U}(k)
and
orthogonal groups
\mathrm{SO}(k)
(having nontrivial topology) are quotient groups of our contractible
groups
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\xi}
and
\mathrm{Fr}_{n}^{\infty}
(for any
n\geqslant 2
).
This is unusual from the point of view of classical theory
of Lie groups.
May be here a correct classical analogy are classifying spaces,
which are
quotients of contractible spaces by group actions.

Lemma 2.12
.

The statements of Theorems

.
Let
f

, let

.
In this proof, we denote by

the image of

.

Consider a faithful linear representation

R

of the group
G
in a finite-dimensional real linear

by
\rho
. Denote by
\operatorname{GL}(V)
the group of invertible

, by
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)

.
Consider the group
R(G)\subset\operatorname{GL}(V)

and the Lie algebra

\rho(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)

For any

,
we have

R(\mathop{\mathrm{Exp}}\nolimits(Y))=\exp(\rho(Y)).

It may happened that the subgroup
R(G)

is not closed in
\operatorname{GL}(V)

with the topology arising from the isomorphism

. Therefore, for any

\Gamma\to R(G)^{twin}

\Gamma\to R(G)
.

. Consider some norm in
V

and denote by
|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|

\xi\geqslant\max|\!|\!|\rho(X_{j})|\!|\!|

, the map

.

\Theta

, we get a continuous homomorphism from

\Theta

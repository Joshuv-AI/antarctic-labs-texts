Efficacy of Endodontic Cryotherapy in Alleviating Post-Treatment Pain after Single-Visit RCT: A Randomized Study
Authors: Prasad Chandrakant Ingale, Simran Sethi, Sukruti Suresh Babu, Khushi Rathod, Rahul Sharma, Shanu Khan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:This study aims to assess whether endodontic cryotherapy can effectively lower discomfort after a single root canal visit.Material and Method:Thirty-six patients aged 18–32 years requiring single-visit root canal treatment (RCT) were divided into three groups at random, with twelve people in each: group 1 (control group), group 2 (normal saline), and group 3 (cold saline). After root canal therapy was finished, group 1 got no further treatment, group 2 got standard saline irrigation at room temperature, and group 3 got cold saline irrigation at 35.6–41°F. A visual analog scale (VAS) was used to measure pain after 6, 12, 24, and 48 hours after therapy.Result:Preliminary results indicated a marked reduction in pain in group 3 relative to groups 1 and 2 at 6, 12, and 24 hours post-treatment (P < 0.05). At 48 hours, pain levels had significantly diminished across all groups, with group 3 consistently exhibiting the lowest VAS scores.Conclusion:Endodontic cryotherapy using cold saline irrigation shows significant efficacy in reducing post-treatment pain after single-visit RCT when compared to normal saline and no additional intervention. These findings suggest that integrating cryotherapy could be a valuable adjunct in endodontic pain management.

INTRODUCTION

Chemo-mechanical preparation, debridement, shaping, disinfection, and three-dimensional (3D) canal obturation of the root canal are all necessary for a successful endodontic procedure. Recent developments enable endodontists and dentists to complete root canal therapy in a single visit. In teeth with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, single-visit endodontics is frequently advised. Acute inflammation in the peri-radicular tissue driven by bacteria into the root canal during endodontic retreatment is the primary cause of postoperative discomfort. Some factors, including preoperative and intraoperative factors, the periapical index (PAI) score, the size of the radiolucency, the quality of the coronary restoration, inadequate instrumentation, tooth localization, extrusion of intracanal medicament, age, sex, periapical pathosis, and the extrusion of irrigants and debris, all influence postoperative pain.[1]

Postendodontic outbreaks have been treated in several ways, such as analgesics, corticosteroids, low-level laser irradiation, long-lasting anesthetic, occlusal reduction, and other final agitation procedures.[2] Thus, this study aimed to assess whether endodontic cryotherapy can effectively lower discomfort after a single root canal visit.

MATERIALS AND METHOD

This was a randomized study, which included thirty-six patients requiring single-visit RCT aged 18–32 years, without systemic diseases affecting pain perception, diagnosed with painful irreversible pulpitis based on clinical and radiographic evaluation on mandibular molars, and able to provide informed consent. Comprised patients with hypersensitivity to cold, previous endodontic treatment in the study tooth, those on analgesics or antibiotics, and pregnant or lactating women were omitted. The subjects were randomly assigned to one of three groups: group 1 (control group) with no additional intervention (n = 12), group 2 (normal saline group) with irrigation using room temperature (21°C) normal saline (n = 12), and group 3 (cold saline group) with irrigation using cold saline at 2.5°C (n = 12).

Following profound inferior alveolar nerve block using local anesthesia (Lignox 2%, Indoco Remedies Ltd, Bengaluru, Karnataka) and rubber dam (Hygenic Dental Dam Kit, Coltene Whaledent) placement, all patients underwent standard RCT using a rotary file system (Protaper Universal Rotary Files, Dentsply, Switzerland) and were adequately instrumented. The root canals were irrigated with 5.25% sodium hypochlorite (Vishal Dentocare, India) during instrumentation, followed by a final rinse in group 1 received no additional intervention post-RCT, group 2 was irrigated with 5 ml of room temperature normal saline (Sodium Chloride Saline Injection IP (0.9% W/V), India), and group 3 was irrigated with 5 ml of cold saline at 2.5°C for 1 minute. All the irrigation process was conducted using round, closed-end single-side vent of 30-gauge needle (Endo Irrigation Needle, Khanna Enterprises Chandigarh). Investigators took the cold saline syringes right before using them, and they were kept in the refrigerator under the observation of a digital thermometer. Root canal filling was conducted using ZOE sealer (DPI Zinc Oxide Powder ZOE Cement, Mumbai) and gutta-percha (Meta Biomed Aurum Pro Gutta Percha Points, Korea), and coronal seal was conducted with composite (Shofu Beautifil II Composite Basic Kit).

Patients recorded their pain levels using VAS scores [Figure 1] ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst possible pain) at 6, 12, 24, and 48 hours post-treatment. VAS scores at each time point were compared between the three groups using analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tuckey post hoc tests.

At 48-hour assessment, pain levels across all groups had decreased substantially, with group 3 maintaining the lowest VAS scores. The average VAS scores for group 3 were consistently lower than those of groups 1 and 2, suggesting a sustained analgesic effect of cold saline.

DISCUSSION

Cryotherapy is often used in dentistry as a therapeutic procedure as it lowers inflammation, bleeding possibly through vasoconstriction, edema, and delayed onset of postoperative discomfort. The fundamental biological reactions are shift in temperature, variations in the circulation locally, nerve activation or cutaneous inhibition of neural receptors, and metabolic activity of periapical tissues.[3]

The reduction in pain aligns with previous literature suggesting cryotherapy’s role in managing inflammation and pain across various medical fields. This supports the hypothesis that localized cooling can impact periapical tissues similarly, providing a notable benefit in endodontics.[4]

Cryotherapy was initially reported to be useful in endodontics by Vera et al. (2015).[5] They employed EndoVac, a negative pressure irrigation device, in conjunction with a final rinse of cold saline at 2.5°C for 5 minutes to assess the temperature change of the extracted tooth’s exterior root surface. They discovered a temperature drop of more than 10°C on the outside of the root that lasted for 4 minutes. This drop in temperature may be sufficient to have a localized anti-inflammatory effect on the tissues around the roots.

From a clinical perspective, incorporating cryotherapy into the RCT protocol is cost-effective, involving minimal procedural adjustments without significant investments in new technologies or training and could enhance patient comfort, which is crucial for patient satisfaction and compliance. As post-treatment pain is a significant concern for both patients and practitioners, an effective, non-pharmacological method to manage this pain is highly advantageous.[6]

While the outcomes are promising, the study does have limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, which might affect the generalizability of the results. Larger studies with more diverse populations are necessary to confirm these findings comprehensively. Second, the subjective nature of pain assessment, often reliant on self-reporting scales, such as the visual analog scale (VAS), introduces potential biases. Future studies could incorporate objective measures of inflammation and healing to corroborate subjective reports. Additionally, the study focused on short-term pain relief. Long-term outcomes and any potential adverse effects of cryotherapy on healing and periapical tissue health were not explored. Extended follow-up studies are required to ascertain the long-term safety and efficacy of this intervention. Finally, the absence of a placebo-controlled group means that the placebo effect cannot be entirely ruled out. Future research should ideally incorporate control groups with sham interventions to mitigate this concern.[78]

CONCLUSION

Endodontic cryotherapy shows significant promise in reducing post-treatment pain after single-visit RCT, offering a simple and cost-effective adjunctive treatment.

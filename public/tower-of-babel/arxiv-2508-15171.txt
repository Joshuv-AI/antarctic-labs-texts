Introduction

Ever since the introduction of Khovanov Homology
[

In an effort to generalize the definition of

[

introduced the knot invariant

&+2q^{6}\left(x^{4}y^{3}-\frac{1}{x^{4}y^{3}}-x^{4}y+\frac{1}{x^{4}y}+x^{3}y^{4}-\frac{1}{x^{3}y^{4}}-\frac{x^{3}}{y}+\frac{y}{x^{3}}-xy^{4}+\frac{2}{xy^{4}}+\frac{x}{y^{3}}-\frac{y^{3}}{x}\right)\\
&+q^{7}\left(x^{5}y^{3}-\frac{1}{x^{5}y^{3}}-x^{5}y^{2}+\frac{1}{x^{5}y^{2}}+x^{3}y^{5}-\frac{1}{x^{3}y^{5}}-\frac{x^{3}}{y^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{x^{3}}-x^{2}y^{5}+\frac{1}{x^{2}y^{5}}+\frac{x^{2}}{y^{3}}-\frac{y^{3}}{x^{2}}\right)\\
&-q^{8}\left(x^{5}y^{5}-\frac{1}{x^{5}y^{5}}-x^{5}+\frac{1}{x^{5}}-y^{5}+\frac{1}{y^{5}}\right)+\cdots\end{split}

which matches with the theoretical computation for right-hand trefoil in
[
17
]
.

5.
Remarks on higher rank

The methods presented in this paper can be generalised to any
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
by following a similar approach. Each finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
is labeled by its highest weight; which is expressed in the fundamental weights basis as
(n_{1},\dots,n_{N-1})
, where
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. The irreducible representation
V_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{N-1})}
with the highest weight
(n_{1},\dots,n_{N-1})
is spanned by the vector with weight
(n_{1},\dots,n_{N-1})
of
V_{(n_{1},0,\dots,0)}\otimes V_{(0,n_{2},\dots,0)}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{(0,0,\dots,n_{N-1})}
. The argument in Lemma
2.3
extends directly to this case. For an analogue of Theorem
1.1
, it would be necessary to explicitly compute the
R
-matrix associated to the tensor product of symmetric Verma modules to prove convergence of the quantum trace. This construction would give
F_{K}^{\mathfrak{sl}_{N}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1},q)
as defined in
[
17
]
for positive braid knots.

In practice however formulas will rapidly get too involved. For general
\mathfrak{sl}_{N}
, each crossing colored by the tensor product
V_{(n_{1},0,\dots,0)}\otimes V_{(0,n_{2},\dots,0)}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{(0,0,\dots,n_{N-1})}
is equivalent to
(N-1)^{2}
crossings between the respective symmetric representations, whose
R
-matrix is dependent on
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
parameters, resulting in
\sim N^{4}
parameters per crossing. This makes it hard to compute even the simplest knots by-hand. However, the setback is mainly computational, and we expect that more computational power or theoretical advances would allow for the methods used in
[
7
]
to extend to our situation as well.

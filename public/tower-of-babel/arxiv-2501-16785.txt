Probabilistic results on the 2 2 -adic complexity
Zhixiong Chen, Arne Winterhof

Abstract
This work is devoted to solving some closely related open problems on the average and asymptotic behavior of the 2 2 -adic complexity of binary sequences. First, for fixed N N , we prove that the expected value E N 2 − adic E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N} of the 2 2 -adic complexity over all binary sequences of length N N is close to N 2 \frac{N}{2} and the deviation from N 2 \frac{N}{2} is at most of order of magnitude log ⁡ ( N ) \log(N) . More precisely, we show that N 2 − 1 ≤ E N 2 − adic = N 2 + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( N ) ) . \frac{N}{2}-1\leq E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}=\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N)). We also prove bounds on the expected value of the N N th rational complexity. Our second contribution is to prove for a random binary sequence 𝒮 \mathcal{S} that the N N th 2 2 -adic complexity satisfies with probability 1 λ 𝒮 ​ ( N ) = N 2 + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( N ) ) for all N . \lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N))\quad\mbox{for all $N$}. Keywords . Feedback with carry shift registers, 2 2 -adic complexity, rational complexity, binary sequences, pseudorandom sequences MSC (2020) : 11B50, 94A55, 94A60

Introduction

Binary sequences
are used for many cryptographic applications
such as pseudorandom number generators for stream cipher cryptosystems. An output sequence, denoted by
\mathcal{S}=(s_{i})_{i\geq 0}
, of a linear feedback shift register (LFSR) of length
L
is
determined by a recurrence relation with
a_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for
0\leq j\leq L-1
,

s_{i+L}=a_{L-1}s_{i+L-1}+a_{L-2}s_{i+L-2}+\ldots+a_{0}s_{i}\bmod{2},\quad i\geq 0.

Sequences derived from a short LFSR are considered weak in view of cryptographic applications. In order to determine this shortness, the notion of
Nth linear complexity
of
\mathcal{S}
, denoted by
~L_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)
was introduced, see for example Rueppel
[
11
,
12
]
,
as the length
L
of a shortest LFSR that generates
\mathcal{S}^{N}=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-1})
, the first

N
elements of
\mathcal{S}
. Sometimes we will use the notation
L_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=L_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)
.

Rueppel
[
12
]
, see also
[
1
, Sect. 18.1.1]
, proved that, for fixed
N
, the expected value of the
N
th linear complexity

E_{N}^{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{L=0}^{N}A_{N}^{\mathrm{lin}}(L)L

of binary sequences of length
N

satisfies

E_{N}^{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{N}{2}+O(1),

(1)

where
A_{N}^{\mathrm{lin}}(L)
is the number of sequences
\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
with
L_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=L
. The result is based on the exact formula of the counting function

A_{N}^{\mathrm{lin}}(L)=2^{\min\{2L-1,2N-2L\}},\quad L=0,1,\ldots,N.

(2)

Niederreiter
[
9
,
10
]
proved that, for varying
N
, with probability
1

L_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N))\quad\mathrm{for}~~N\geq 2

(3)

for a random sequence
\mathcal{S}
.

Goresky and Klapper proposed a new type of registers called feedback with carry shift registers (FCSRs) in the 1990s, see
[
1
,
6
]
. The algorithm (of an FCSR of length
L
) is carried out by a recurrence relation in the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of integers with
a_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for
0\leq j\leq L-1
,

s_{i+L}+2z_{i+L}=a_{L-1}s_{i+L-1}+a_{L-2}s_{i+L-2}+\ldots+a_{0}s_{i}+z_{i+L-1},\quad i\geq 0,

with
initial integer
z_{L-1}
and carries
z_{L},z_{L+1},\ldots

The
N
th
2
-adic complexity

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)
of
\mathcal{S}

characterizes the length of a shortest FCSR that generates the first
N
elements of
\mathcal{S}
.
More precisely,
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)
is defined as the binary logarithm of the

N
th rational complexity

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=\min\left\{\max\{|f|,q\}:f,q\in\mathbb{Z},q>0\mbox{ odd},q\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}s_{i}2^{i}\equiv f\bmod{2^{N}}\right\};

see
[
1
, p. 328]
, the recent survey
[
17
]
or
[
13
]
, that is,

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=2^{\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)}.

(Note that, more recently, the term rational complexity has been introduced for a different measure of pseudorandomness, see
[
15
]
. More precisely,
[
15
]
identifies binary sequences with reals in the unit interval whereas here we identify binary sequences with
2
-adic integers.)
Any pair
(q,f)

with
\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=\max\{|f|,q\}
is called a
minimal rational representation
for the first
N
elements of
\mathcal{S}
.
It is trivial that

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)\leq\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N+1)\quad\mbox{for $N\geq 1$}.

Although experimental results support the conjecture that the analog of
(\ref{lin1})
for the expected value of the
N

2
-adic complexity

,

of the

2^{0.7716N}>E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{2^{(N+1)/2}}{9\log_{2}(N-1)-1}+\frac{N-1}{4},&N\mbox{ odd},\\
\frac{2^{(N-2)/2}}{9\log_{2}(N-2)-1}+\frac{N}{4},&N\mbox{ even},\end{array}\right.\quad N\geq 3,

(4)

E_{N}^{2-\mathrm{adic}}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)

2
Numbers of sequences with certain restrictions

For a positive integer
w
, let

M_{N}(w)
be the number of
\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}
with
N
th
2
-adic complexity

\log_{2}(w)
(or
N
th rational complexity
w
),

\displaystyle M_{N}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=\log_{2}(w)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)=w\right\}\right|.

First, we estimate the number of
\mathcal{S}^{N}
with ‘small’
N
th
2
-adic complexity, that is,
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)<\frac{N}{2}-\delta\log_{2}(N)
.

Lemma 1
.

We have

\sum_{w=1}^{W}M_{N}(w)=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}W^{2}+O(W\log(W)),\quad W\leq 2^{(N-1)/2},

where the implied constant is absolute.

In particular,
for any
\delta>0
and

\Delta_{N}=\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)<\frac{N}{2}-\delta\log_{2}(N)\right\},

we have

|\Delta_{N}|=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\frac{2^{N}}{N^{2\delta}}+O\left(\frac{2^{\frac{N}{2}}}{N^{\delta-1}}\right),

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.
For
w\leq 2^{(N-1)/2}
we can express
M_{N}(w)
in terms of Euler’s totient function

M_{N}(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2\varphi(w),&w\mbox{ even},\\
3\varphi(w),&w\mbox{ odd},\end{array}\right.\quad w\leq 2^{(N-1)/2},

where

\varphi(w)=|\{x=1,2,\ldots,w:\gcd(x,w)=1\}|,\quad w=1,2,\ldots,

see
[
13
, Lemmas 2 and 3]
.
Hence, we have

\sum_{w=1}^{W}M_{N}(w)=3\sum_{w=1}^{W}\varphi(w)-\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/2\rfloor}\varphi(2w),\quad W\leq 2^{(N-1)/2}-1.

Since

\varphi(2w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi(w),&w\mbox{ odd},\\
2\varphi(w),&w\mbox{ even},\end{array}\right.

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/2\rfloor}\varphi(2w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/2\rfloor}\varphi(w)+\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/4\rfloor}\varphi(2w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/2\rfloor}\varphi(w)+\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/4\rfloor}\varphi(w)+\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/8\rfloor}\varphi(2w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(W)\rfloor}\sum_{w=1}^{\lfloor W/2^{j}\rfloor}\varphi(w).

Using Walfisz’ formula, see for example
[
2
, Theorem 330]
, the original work
[
16
]
, or
[
7
, Theorem 1]
for an improved error term,

\sum\limits_{w=1}^{m}\varphi(w)=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}m^{2}+O\left(m\log(m)\right),

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{w=1}^{W}M_{N}(w)=RW^{2}+O(W\log(W)),\quad W\leq 2^{(N-1)/2},

where

R=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\left(3-\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\log_{2}(W)\rfloor}4^{-j}\right)=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}+O(W^{-2})

and the result follows. ∎

Now, we estimate the number of
\mathcal{S}^{N}
with ‘large’
N
th
2
-adic complexity, that is,
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)
. We will need the following result on the growth of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)
, see
[
1
, Lemma 18.5.1]
.

Lemma 2
.

We have

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)\leq\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N-1)+\frac{2^{N-1}}{\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N-1)},\quad N\geq 2.

A pivotal fact is that we will use the solutions of the inequality in Lemma
2
in the proof of Lemma
4
, below, that is, the
solutions
x>0
of an inequality of the form

x+\frac{a}{x}\geq b\quad\mbox{or}\quad x^{2}-bx+a\geq 0,

where
0<4a\leq b^{2}
, namely,

0<x\leq\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4a}}{2}~~~\mathrm{or}~~~x\geq\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}-4a}}{2}.

We will need another preliminary result on sums of reciprocals.

Lemma 3
.

For
\delta>\frac{1}{2}

and
0<\varepsilon<1
we have

\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}\frac{1}{(N-c)^{2\delta}}\leq\frac{c(\varepsilon,\delta)}{N^{2\delta-1}},

where

c(\varepsilon,\delta)=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{1-2\delta}-1}{2\delta-1}=2^{O(\delta)}.

Proof. We get the result from

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{c=0}^{\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}\frac{1}{(N-c)^{2\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x=N-\lfloor\varepsilon N\rfloor}^{N}\frac{1}{x^{2\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{N}_{N-\varepsilon N}x^{-2\delta}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\delta-1}\cdot\left(N^{1-2\delta}-(N-\varepsilon N)^{1-2\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(\varepsilon,\delta)N^{1-2\delta}.

Note that the constant
c(\varepsilon,\delta)
is positive under the conditions on
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove an upper bound on the number of sequences with
‘large’
2
-adic complexity.

Lemma 4
.

Consider

\Gamma_{N}=\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)\right\}.

For
\delta>\frac{1}{2}

we have

|\Gamma_{N}|\leq\frac{2^{N+O(\delta)}}{N^{2\delta-1}}.

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof. For any integer
c\geq 0
we consider the number of sequences
\mathcal{S}^{N-c}
, the first
N-c
elements of
\mathcal{S}^{N}
, with ‘large’
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)
,

K_{N}(c)=\left|\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}\in\{0,1\}^{N-c}:\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c\right\}\right|,

that is,
|\Gamma_{N}|=K_{N}(0)
.
Since

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)\leq N-c-1

by
[
13
, Lemma 10]

we have

K_{N}(c)=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad c\geq\frac{5}{6}N-1.

Hence, below we restrict ourselves to the case
c<\frac{5}{6}N-1<N-3
, that is,
N>12
.

Now from

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c,

we get
by Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N-c}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N-c)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c}<2^{\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c-1}}(N-c-1)+\frac{2^{N-c-1}}{\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c-1}}(N-c-1)},

which implies either

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c-1}}(N-c-1)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N-c}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N-c)-1}-\sqrt{2^{N-c+2\delta\log_{2}(N-c)-2}-2^{N-c-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N-c}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N-c)-1}\left(1-\sqrt{1-2^{1-2\delta\log_{2}(N-c)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{N-c}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N-c)-1}\cdot 2^{1-2\delta\log_{2}(N-c)}}{1+\sqrt{1-2^{1-2\delta\log_{2}(N-c)}}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{N-c}{2}-\delta\log_{2}(N-c)}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad(\mathrm{for}~~c\leq N-3),

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N-c-1}{2}-\delta\log_{2}(N-c-1)}

or

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c-1}}(N-c-1)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c-1}+\sqrt{2^{N+2\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.8c-2}-2^{N-c-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c-1}\left(1+\sqrt{1-2^{1-2\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.2c}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-1-0.4c}\cdot 2^{1-0.4},\quad(\mathrm{for}~~N\geq 3),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4(c+1)}.

Now for a fixed positive integer
C\leq\left\lfloor\frac{5}{6}N\right\rfloor
assume that

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-c)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)-0.4c\quad\mbox{for }c=0,1,\ldots,C-1

and

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-c}}(N-C)<2^{\frac{N-C}{2}-\delta\log_{2}(N-C)}.

(6)

There are
O\left(\frac{2^{N-C}}{(N-C)^{2\delta}}\right)

sequences
\mathcal{S}^{N-C}
with ‘small’
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N-C}}(N-C)
satisfying
(\ref{small})
by Lemma
1
. Each such
\mathcal{S}^{N-C}
can be padded to a sequence
\mathcal{S}^{N}
of length
N
in
2^{C}
ways and there are
O\left(\frac{2^{N}}{(N-C)^{2\delta}}\right)
sequences with ‘large’
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{N}|\ll 2^{N}\sum\limits_{C=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{5}{6}N\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{(N-C)^{2\delta}}.

Finally, Lemma
3
(with
\varepsilon=\frac{5}{6})
implies the desired result. ∎

3
Expected values of
2
-adic and rational complexity

In this section, we prove lower and upper bounds on the expected values of the
N
th
2
-adic and rational complexity. Their combination implies Theorem
1
.
We first prove lower bounds based on the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

For
N\geq 2
and any
\mathcal{S}^{N-1}=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-2})\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}
, we have

\lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)+\lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\geq N-2

and

\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)+\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\geq 2^{\frac{N}{2}},

where
(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},a)=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-2},a)
for
a\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof. It suffices to show

\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)\cdot\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\geq 2^{N-2},

from which the first inequality follows by
taking logarithms
and the second inequality follows by the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means

\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)+\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\geq 2\sqrt{\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)\cdot\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)}.

Now take minimal rational representations
(q,f)
of
(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)
and
(q^{\prime},f^{\prime})
of
(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)
. Then we have

q\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}s_{i}2^{i}\equiv f\bmod 2^{N}~~\mbox{ and }~~q^{\prime}\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}s_{i}2^{i}+2^{N-1}\equiv f^{\prime}\bmod 2^{N}.

It follows that
fq^{\prime}-f^{\prime}q\equiv 2^{N-1}\bmod{2^{N}}

and hence

2^{N-1}\leq|fq^{\prime}-f^{\prime}q|\leq|fq^{\prime}|+|f^{\prime}q|\leq 2\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)\cdot\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N),

which completes the proof. ∎

From Lemma
5
one can directly get
lower bounds on the expected values
E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}
and
E^{\mathrm{rat}}_{N}
.

Theorem 2
.

We have

E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}\geq\frac{N}{2}-1

and

E^{\mathrm{rat}}_{N}\geq 2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}+\frac{N-5}{4}.

Proof.
Lemma
5
leads to

\displaystyle 2^{N}\cdot E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}=\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N-1}\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}}\left(\lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)+\lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\right)\geq(N-2)2^{N-1}

and

2^{N}\cdot E^{\mathrm{rat}}_{N}=\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N-1}\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}}\left(\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},0)}(N)+\Lambda_{(\mathcal{S}^{N-1},1)}(N)\right)\geq 2^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot 2^{N-1},

which imply the desired lower bounds on
E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}
as well as

E^{\mathrm{rat}}_{N}\geq 2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}.

(7)

For
E^{\mathrm{rat}}_{N}
,
we slightly improve this bound to

E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}\geq 2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}+\frac{N-5}{4}.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}^{N}=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-1})
be any sequence satisfying
s_{n}=0
for
n=0,1,\ldots,k-1
and
s_{k}=1
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 4]
we have for any
\frac{N}{2}\leq k\leq N-2
,

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{k}^{N}}(N)=2^{k}.

There are
2^{N-k-1}
such sequences. Their contribution to the expected value
E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}

is

\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{k=\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil}^{N-2}2^{k}\cdot 2^{N-k-1}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\left(N-1-\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil\right)2^{N-1}\geq\frac{N-3}{4}.

However, their contribution to the lower bound (
7
) was exactly
2^{\frac{N}{2}}
for each pair sequences
\mathcal{S}_{k}^{N}
with different
s_{N-1}
and there are
2^{N-k-2}
many such pairs, that is totally

\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{k=\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil}^{N-2}2^{\frac{N}{2}}\cdot 2^{N-k-2}=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}}\left(2^{N-\lceil N/2\rceil-1}-1\right)<\frac{1}{2}.

This completes the proof of the second result. ∎

The lower bound on the expected value of the
N
th
2
-adic complexity in Theorem
2
is very close to
the conjectured lower bound
\frac{N-1}{2}
supported by the data for

The lower bound on the
N
th rational complexity improves the lower bound of
[
13
]
in (
4
) substantially.

Now we turn to prove upper bounds which are much better than the upper bounds of
[
13
]
, see
(\ref{TQbound})
and
(\ref{TQbound2})
.

Theorem 3
.

For
\delta>\frac{1}{2}
we have

E^{\mathrm{2-adic}}_{N}\leq\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)+O(N^{2-2\delta}),

where the implied constant depends only on
\delta
,
and

E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}\leq 2^{N/2+O(N/\log(N))},

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.
Take
\Gamma_{N}
from Lemma
4
. Since
\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)\leq N-1
by
[
13
, Lemma 10]
and
\delta>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\Gamma_{N}}\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)\leq\frac{(N-1)\cdot|\Gamma_{N}|}{2^{N}}\ll\frac{1}{N^{2\delta-2}}.

Moreover, we have

\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}\setminus\Gamma_{N}}\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)\leq\frac{2^{N}-|\Gamma_{N}|}{2^{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)\right)<\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)

and the first result follows.

Now we turn to prove the bound on
E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}
. Taking

\delta_{1}=\delta,\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{m}
with

\frac{1}{2}<\delta=\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<\ldots<\delta_{m}=O\left(\frac{N}{\log(N)}\right),

we subdivide
\Gamma_{N}
into
m
subsets

\Gamma^{(i)}_{N}=\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\frac{N}{2}+\delta_{i}\log_{2}(N)<\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)\leq\frac{N}{2}+\delta_{i+1}\log_{2}(N)\right\}

for
1\leq i\leq m-1
and

\Gamma^{(m)}_{N}=\left\{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}:\lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)>\frac{N}{2}+\delta_{m}\log_{2}(N)\right\}.

Then by Lemma
4
we see that

|\Gamma^{(i)}_{N}|\leq\frac{2^{N+O(\delta_{i})}}{N^{2\delta_{i}-1}}=2^{N-2\delta_{i}\log_{2}(N)+O(N/\log(N))},~~~1\leq i\leq m.

Hence we get

\displaystyle E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\Gamma^{(i)}_{N}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)+\sum\limits_{\mathcal{S}^{N}\in\{0,1\}^{N}\setminus\Gamma_{N}}\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}^{N}}(N)\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}|\Gamma^{(i)}_{N}|\cdot 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta_{i+1}\log_{2}(N)}+|\Gamma^{(m)}_{N}|\cdot 2^{N}+2^{N}\cdot 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{N}{2}+\delta\log_{2}(N)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}2^{\frac{N}{2}+(\delta_{i+1}-2\delta_{i})\log_{2}(N)+O(N/\log(N))}

\displaystyle+2^{N-2\delta_{m}\log_{2}(N)+O(N/\log(N))}.

Now we balance the first
m
summands by choosing

\delta_{i+1}=2\delta_{i}+\delta,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,m-1,

that is

\delta_{i}=(2^{i}-1)\delta,\quad i=1,2,\ldots,m.

Then we balance the first and the last summand, up to the
O
-term,
choosing

\delta=\frac{N}{(2^{m+2}-2)\log_{2}(N)}.

Hence with
m=\lceil\log_{2}(\log_{2}(N))\rceil
we derive

E_{N}^{\mathrm{rat}}\leq(m+1)2^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{2^{m+2}-2}+O(N/\log(N))}=2^{\frac{N}{2}+O(N/\log(N))},

which completes the proof. ∎

4
Asymptotic behavior of
2
-adic and rational complexity

As for the proof of
(\ref{lin2})
in
[
10
]
for the
N
th linear complexity, the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, see for example
[
8
, p. 228]
, also plays an important role in studying the behavior of the
N
th
2
-adic complexity. We state it here for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 6

(Borel-Cantelli Lemma)
.

If the sum of the probabilities of the events
\{\Theta_{N}:N=1,2,\ldots\}
is finite

\sum\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}(\Theta_{N})<\infty,

then the probability that infinitely many of them occur is
0
, that is,

\mathrm{Pr}\left(\limsup\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Theta_{N}\right)=0.

Then the asymptotic behavior of the
2
-adic complexity and the rational complexity of a random sequence
\mathcal{S}
is described in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For a random sequence
\mathcal{S}
we have with probability
1
,

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N))

and

\Lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)=2^{\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N))}

for all
N
.

Proof. Fix any
\delta>1
, take
\Delta_{N}
from Lemma
1
and
\Gamma_{N}
from Lemma
4
. For

\Theta_{N}=\Delta_{N}\cup\Gamma_{N}
we get

\mu^{\infty}(\Theta_{N})=\frac{|\Delta_{N}|+|\Gamma_{N}|}{2^{N}}\ll\frac{1}{N^{2\delta}}+\frac{1}{N^{2\delta-1}}\ll\frac{1}{N^{2\delta-1}}.

Since
\delta>1
we see that

\sum\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2\delta-1}}\leq\int^{\infty}_{1}x^{1-2\delta}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2\delta-2}<\infty,

from which we derive

\sum\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\mu^{\infty}(\Theta_{N})\ll\sum\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N^{2\delta-1}}<\infty.

Then Lemma
6
indicates that the set of all
\mathcal{S}
for which
\mathcal{S}\in\Theta_{N}
for infinitely many
N
has
\mu^{\infty}
-measure
0
. That is, with probability
1
we have
\mathcal{S}\in\Theta_{N}
for at most
finitely many
N
. So with probability
1
, we have

\left|\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)-\frac{N}{2}\right|\leq\delta\log_{2}(N)

for sufficiently large
N
and the results follow. ∎

5
Conclusions and final remarks

In this work, we studied
the average behavior for fixed
N
and the asymptotic behavior for varying
N
of the
N
th
2
-adic and rational complexities of binary sequences.

For the average behavior,
we proved the lower bound
\frac{N}{2}-1
, which is close to the conjectured value supported by experimental results for
N\leq 20
of
[
13
]
, as well as the upper bound
\frac{N}{2}+O(\log(N))
.
We also provided analog bounds for the expected value of the rational complexity. These results essentially answer the questions stated in
[
1
,
5
,
13
]
.

For the asymptotic behavior,
we proved that the
2
-adic complexity profile of a random binary sequence grows approximately as
\frac{N}{2}
with deviation of order of magnitude at most
\log(N)
with probability
1
. This answers a question stated in
[
1
, p. 329]
.
It also characterizes the set of accumulation points of normalized
2
-adic complexity
\frac{\lambda_{\mathcal{S}}(N)}{N}
studied in
[
1
, Sect. 18.5.2, p. 397]
, which
is
\{\frac{1}{2}\}
.

For periodic sequences of period
T
the expected value of the
2
-adic complexity is

T+O(\log(T))
, see
[
1
, Corollary 18.2.2]
and
[
3
]
.

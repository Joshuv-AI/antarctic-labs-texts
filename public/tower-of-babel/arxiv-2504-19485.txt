Topological derivative for a fast identification of short, linear perfectly conducting cracks with inaccurate background information
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
In this study, we consider a topological derivative-based imaging technique for the fast identification of short, linear perfectly conducting cracks completely embedded in a two-dimensional homogeneous domain with smooth boundary. Unlike conventional approaches, we assume that the background permittivity and permeability are unknown due to their dependence on frequency and temperature, and we propose a normalized imaging function to localize cracks. Despite inaccuracies in background parameters, application of the proposed imaging function enables to recognize the existence of crack but it is still impossible to identify accurate crack locations. Furthermore, the shift in crack localization of imaging results is significantly influenced by the applied background parameters. In order to theoretically explain this phenomenon, we show that the imaging function can be expressed in terms of the zero-order Bessel function of the first kind, the crack lengths, and the applied inaccurate background wavenumber corresponding to the applied inaccurate background permittivity and permeability. Various numerical simulations results with synthetic data polluted by random noise validate the theoretical results.

To address this issue, we consider the application of topological derivative for identifying a set of two-dimensional short, linear perfectly conducting cracks completely embedded in a homogeneous medium, even when accurate background permittivity or permeability values are unknown. To this end, we introduce a topological derivative based imaging function based on a misfit functional with inaccurate permittivity and permeability values. In order to explain some phenomena including the appearance of inaccurate location of cracks, we carefully investigate the structure of the imaging function by establishing its relationship with crack length, both accurate and inaccurate wavenumbers, and the zero-order Bessel function. This is based on the fact that the boundary measurement data can be expressed by the asymptotic expansion formula in the presence of small cracks. To validate the theoretical results,
numerical simulation results with boundary measurement data corrupted by random noise in the existence of single and multiple short, linear cracks are exhibited.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section

3

4

Topological derivative based imaging

Let
\Gamma_{m}
be a linear perfectly conducting crack with length
2\ell_{m}
and center
\mathbf{x}_{m}
and
\Gamma
denotes the collection of
\Gamma_{m}
. Throughout this paper, all
\Gamma_{m}
are completely embedded in a homogeneous material
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
, permittivity
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
, and permeability
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}
at given angular frequency
\omega=2\pi f
. Throughout this paper, we assume that
\Omega
is a subset of anechoic chamber; thus,
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}=4\pi\times${10}^{-7}\text{\,}\mathrm{H}$
,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}=$8.854\text{\times}{10}^{-12}\text{\,}\mathrm{F}$
, and the background conductivity
\sigma_{\mathrm{b}}\approx 0
. By denoting
k=\omega\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}
and
\lambda
as the background wavenumber and given positive wavelength, respectively, we assume that

k|\mathbf{x}_{m}-\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}}|\gg\frac{1}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad\ell_{m}<\frac{\lambda}{2},

(1)

for
m,m^{\prime}=1,2,\ldots,M
and
m\neq m^{\prime}
. These conditions means that
\Gamma_{m}
are sufficiently separated from each
other and the their lengths are short. Furthermore, we assume that
\Gamma_{m}
does not touch the
\partial\Omega
i.e., there is a positive constant
C
such that

\dist(\Gamma_{m},\partial\Omega)\geq C.

In the presence of
\Gamma
, let
u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)
be the time-harmonic total field satisfying the boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\triangle u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)+k^{2}u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\backslash\overline{\Gamma}\\
u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)=0&\mbox{on}&\Gamma\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{\partial\exp(ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{x})}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}=g^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})
is the outward normal to
\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, denotes the propagation direction. We assume that

is not an eigenvalue of (
2
) to guarantee the well-posedness. Analogously, let
w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)=\exp(ik\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{x})
be the background solution which satisfies (
2
) in the absence of
\Gamma
.

Now, let us consider the following misfit functional, which depends on the solution
u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)
:

\mathbb{E}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(3)

In order to introduce the topological derivative based imaging function, let us create a small linear crack
\Sigma
with length
\epsilon
at
\mathbf{z}\in\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega
and denote
\Omega|\Sigma
as the corresponding domain. Since the creation of
\Sigma
changes the topology of the entire domain
\Omega
, it is natural to consider the corresponding topological derivative
d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})
of
\mathbb{E}(\Omega)
with respect to point
\mathbf{z}
such that

d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})=\lim_{\gamma\to 0+}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\Omega|\Sigma)-\mathbb{E}(\Omega)}{\varphi(\gamma)},

(4)

where
\varphi(\gamma)\longrightarrow 0
as
\gamma\longrightarrow 0+
. From (
4
), we obtain the asymptotic expansion:

\mathbb{E}(\Omega|\Sigma)=\mathbb{E}(\Omega)+\varphi(\gamma)d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})+o(\varphi(\gamma)).

(5)

Based on
[
37
]
, we can explore explicitly forms of
\varphi(\gamma)
and
d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})
as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}(\Omega)
is defined as (
3
). Then
\varphi(\gamma)
and
d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})
are written as

\varphi(\gamma)=\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\gamma/2)}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})=\mathrm{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{N}v^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k)\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k)},

(6)

respectively. Here,
v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)
satisfies the adjoint problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\Delta v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)+k^{2}v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}=u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(7)

Based on prior studies
[

,
11
,
15
,
32
,
38
,
37
,
40
]
, existence and location of
\Gamma_{m}
can be identified through the map of

. However, for a successful application of

, exact values of
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and

must be known. However, since the values of
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}
are dependent on the applied frequency and ambient temperature, it will be inappropriate to apply (

) without prior information of exact
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}
.

Since accurate value of
k
is unknown, let us apply alternative background wavenumber

with inaccurate permittivity

and permeability
\mu_{\mathrm{a}}
, and introduce the following normalized topological derivative based imaging function

\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})=\frac{|d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})|}{\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{z}\in\Omega}|d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})|}\quad\text{where}\quad d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})=\mathrm{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{N}v^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})},

where

and
v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})
satisfies the adjoint problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\Delta v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})+k_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}=u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(9)

Fortunately, based on simulation results in Section
4
, the existence of

can be recognized through the map of
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})
even if
k_{\mathrm{a}}\neq k
but the identified location
\mathbf{x}_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}
is shifted in a specific direction. However, to the best of our knowledge, the mathematical background to support this has not been established yet.

3
Structure of the imaging function

In this section, we explore mathematical structure of the topological derivative based imaging function
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})
and discuss its various properties. To this end, we recall the asymptotic expansion formula derived in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Asymptotic expansion formula)
.

Let
u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)
satisfies (
2
) and
w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})
be the background solution of (
2
). Then, if the condition (
1
) is valid, the following asymptotic expansion formula holds uniformly on
\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega
;

u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\ell_{m}/2)}w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x}_{m};k)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{m};k)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\ell_{m}|^{2}}\right),

(10)

where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the Neumann function, which satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k)+k^{2}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k)=-\delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})&\mbox{in}&\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k)}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}=0&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\delta
is the Dirac delta function.

Now, we derive the main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For sufficiently large
N
,
\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})
can be represented as follows

\mathfrak{F}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\approx\frac{|\Phi(\mathbf{z})|}{\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{z}\in\Omega}|\Phi(\mathbf{z})|},\quad\Phi(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{J_{0}(|k\mathbf{x}_{m}-k_{\mathrm{a}}\mathbf{z}|)}{\ln(\ell_{m}/2)},

(11)

where
J_{0}
is the Bessel function of order zero of the first kind.

Proof.

Since
v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})
satisfies (
9
), for
\mathbf{z}\in\Omega\backslash\partial\Omega
, we have

v^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{v^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k_{\mathrm{a}})}{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)\right)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(12)

Applying (
10
) and (
12
) to (
8
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d_{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})&=\mathrm{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{N}v^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z})}\\
&\approx\mathfrak{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u^{(n)}(\mathbf{x};k)-w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x})\right)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right]\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})}\\
&\approx\mathfrak{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\ell_{m}/2)}w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x}_{m};k)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{m};k)\right)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right]\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})}\\
&=\mathfrak{Re}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{2\pi}{\ln(\ell_{m}/2)}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_{m};k)\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x}_{m};k)\overline{w^{(n)}(\mathbf{z};k_{\mathrm{a}})}\right).\end{aligned}

(13)

Since the following relationship holds for sufficiently large

(14)

5
Conclusion

We investigated the applicability of topological derivative based imaging technique for a fast identification of a set of small, linear perfectly conducting cracks completely embedded in a homogeneous domain with smooth boundary when accurate values of background permittivity and/or permeability are unknown. Based on the asymptotic expansion formula for the far-field pattern in the presence of cracks, we rigorously showed that the imaging function of topological derivative can be expressed by the zero-order Bessel function and length of crack. Based on the discovered structure of the imaging function, we confirmed why inaccurate location of cracks was reconstructed when inaccurate value of background permittivity and/or permeability. To support the theoretical results, various numerical simulation results corrupted by random noise were exhibited.

In order to reconstruct cracks accurately, investigation of imaging algorithm for retrieving exact value of background permittivity and/or permeability would be a remarkable contribution to this study. In this paper, we considered an identification of two-dimensional cracks embedded in a homogeneous domain. Extension to the three-dimensional problem and real-world applications will be important research subjects.

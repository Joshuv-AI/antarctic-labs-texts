Abstract
. The ternary extended contact relation was introduced in ( Ivanova, 2020 ) as a more expressive counterpart of the standard binary contact relation. The class of Boolean algebras expanded with the relation was named Extended Contact Algebras (ECAs). In this work, we take an algebraic perspective on ECAs, interpreting the ternary relation as a form of entailment. We introduce Pseudo-Inference Algebras , purely algebraic structures where the ternary relation is replaced by a monotone ternary operator, capturing the logical character of extended contact. We show that the subclass of relational Pseudo-Inference Algebras corresponds precisely to ECAs and generates a subvariety of strict PSI-Algebras , which forms a discriminator variety. Furthermore, we extend Stone duality to this ternary context, introducing descriptive PSI-frames and establishing three interrelated dualities that differ in their morphisms while sharing the same class of topological objects. The framework developed in the paper provides a unified relational semantics for Boolean algebras equipped with monotone ternary operators, connecting spatial and logical notions within a categorical and topological setting. Keywords : Extended Contact Algebras, extended contact relation, modal algebras, ternary relation, ternary operations, topological duality MSC : Primary 06E25; Secondary 03G05



1.
Introduction

The class of Extended Contact Algebras (ECAs, for short) was introduced by
Ivanova, 2020
. The idea was motivated by certain limitations related to the well-known Boolean Contact Algebras (BCAs), i.e., Boolean algebras expanded with a binary relation
\mathrel{\mathsf{C}}
of contact. BCAs were introduced in the early 2000s by
Stell, 2000
and
Düntsch and Winter, 2004
as an algebraic tool for representing and studying the phenomenon of the nearness of regions in space. From a certain point of view, they could be seen as an abstract approach to proximity spaces in the sense of
Thron, 1973
(see, e.g.,
Düntsch and Vakarelov, 2007
).

The idea of BCAs proved both fruitful and useful, giving insight into the algebraic aspects of Region Connection Calculus and developing the so-called Region-based Theories of Space (also known as mereotopologies) that had been investigated at least since the times of de Laguna (
de Laguna, 1922
) and Whitehead (
Whitehead, 1920
;
Whitehead, 1929
). Among others, it turned out that BCAs have a considerable expressive power that lets express various topological properties by means of the contact relation. However, unsurprisingly, natural properties were discovered that could not be grasped within the framework of contact relations. One of these was the property of internal connectedness of regular closed subsets of topological spaces. Since the topological property of connectedness of regions—particularly important for mereotopology
(
Pratt and Schoop, 2000
)
—can be naturally expressed by means of the contact relation, it could be expected that he similar situation holds for the property of internal connectedness. Contrary to this, Tatyana Ivanova (
Ivanova, 2020
) proved that the property cannot be formulated by means of contact.

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,b)\vdash d$, $(a,b)\vdash e$ and $(d,e)\vdash f$, then $(a,b)\vdash f$}.

\displaystyle\text{If $a\leq f$, then $(a,b)\vdash f$}.

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,b)\vdash f$, then $a\wedge b\leq f$}.

It is noteworthy that the relation

In this paper, we adopt the entailment perspective as dominant and define and investigate a class of Pseudo-Inference Algebras that can be viewed as purely algebraic counterparts of Extended Contact Algebras introduced by Ivanova. Purely algebraic, since we replace the ternary relation by a ternary operator which is (almost) a possibility operator, and we translate the axioms for extended contact into constraints put upon the operator.

2.
Extended Contact Algebras

As we mentioned in the introduction, the first axiomatization of ECAs is due to
Ivanova, 2020
. In our approach, we will use a slightly simplified set of constraints that is equivalent to the original one. Thanks to this, on the side of the operator associated with the relation, we obtain a simpler and easier to handle set of algebraic postulates.

We will prove that an
Extended Contact Algebra
is equivalent to a structure of the form
(\mathbf{A},\vdash)
,
where
\mathbf{A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\neg,0,1)

\vdash

(EC0)

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,b)\vdash f$, then $(a\vee d,b)\vdash f\vee d$}\,.

(EC1)

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,b)\vdash d$, $(a,b)\vdash e$ and $(d,e)\vdash f$, then $(a,b)\vdash f$}\,.

(EC2)

\displaystyle(a,b)\vdash a\vee f\,.

(EC3)

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,a)\vdash f$, then $a\leq f$}\,.

(EC4)

\displaystyle\text{If $(a,b)\vdash f$, then $(b,a)\vdash f$}\,.

\dashv

The constraints can be interpreted as expressing elementary facts about entailment (in the very special case when we have no more than two premises). To see this, let us interpret the Boolean order

and

and

The extended contact is a more expressive counterpart of the standard binary contact relation
\mathrel{\mathsf{C}}
. The latter can be defined by means of

Ivanova, 2020

(

3.
Pseudo-Inference Algebras

In this section, we introduce the variety of Pseudo-Inference Algebras and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between its subclass, consisting of the so-called
relational algebras
, and the class of ECAs.

Definition 3.1
.

A Boolean algebra with a ternary weakly normal monotonic operator (3BAMO for short), is a pair
(\mathbf{A},\Diamond)
where
\mathbf{A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\neg,0,1)
is a Boolean algebra, and
\Diamond
is a ternary operator on
A
such that for every
a,b,c,x\in A
the following hold:

(MO1)

\displaystyle\Diamond(0,b,c)=\Diamond(a,0,c)=\Diamond(a,b,0)=0\,,

(MO2)

\displaystyle\Diamond(a\vee x,b,c)=\Diamond(a,b,c)\vee\Diamond(x,b,c)\,,

(MO3)

\displaystyle\Diamond(a,b\vee x,c)=\Diamond(a,b,c)\vee\Diamond(a,x,c)\,,

(MO4)

\displaystyle\Diamond(a,b,c)\vee\Diamond(a,b,x)\leq\Diamond(a,b,c\vee x)\,.

\dashv

As we see,
\Diamond
behaves like the standard modal possibility operator in the first two coordinates, but is only monotonic in the third coordinate. Any 3BAMO is a special case of a Boolean algebra with
n
-ary monotonic operator (a BAMO) introduced in
(
Celani, 2009
, Definition 1)
.

Definition 3.3

A 3BAMO

4.
Relational PSI-algebras

In this section we show that the class of relational PSI-Algebras generates the subvariety of
strict PSI-Algebras
, namely those PSI-Algebras that satisfy the following additional equations:

(R1)

\displaystyle\Diamond(x,y,a)\wedge\neg\Diamond(x,y,b)\leq\Diamond(1,1,a\wedge\neg b),

(R2)

\displaystyle\Diamond(x,a,y)\wedge\neg\Diamond(x,b,y)\leq\Diamond(1,a\wedge\neg b,1),

(S)

\displaystyle\Diamond(a,b,c)\leq\mu(\Diamond(a,b,c)),

where
\mu(z)\coloneqq\neg\Diamond(1,1,\neg z)\wedge\neg\Diamond(1,\neg z,1)
. Moreover, we show that this subvariety is, in fact, a discriminator variety.

Lemma 4.1
.

In every relational PSI-Algebra, (
R1
), (

) and (

) hold.

Proof.

It is immediate that every relational PSI-Algebra satisfies (

(
R1
) Suppose that
\Diamond(x,y,a)\wedge\neg\Diamond(x,y,b)=1
. Then,
\Diamond(x,y,a)=1
and
\Diamond(x,y,b)=0
. It follows that
a\wedge\neg b>0
. Indeed, if
a\wedge\neg b=0
, then
a\leq b
, and by (
MO4
) we obtain
1=\Diamond(x,y,a)\leq\Diamond(x,y,b)=0
, a contradiction. By (
PI3
), we have
0<a\wedge\neg b\leq\Diamond(1,1,a\wedge\neg b)
, which implies that
\Diamond(1,1,a\wedge\neg b)=1
, as required.

(

) Suppose that
\Diamond(x,a,y)\wedge\neg\Diamond(x,b,y)=1
. Then,
\Diamond(x,a,y)=1
and
\Diamond(x,b,y)=0

Let
(\mathbf{A},\Diamond)
be a PSI-Algebra. A filter

\Diamond(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\to\Diamond(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in F

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\text{If}\;a\to b\in F\;\text{then}\;

\displaystyle\Diamond(x,y,a)\to\Diamond(x,y,b)\in F\,.

(Mid)

\displaystyle\text{If}\;a\to b\in F\;\text{then}\;

\displaystyle\Diamond(x,a,y)\to\Diamond(x,b,y)\in F\,.

Let us assume that

, since

(

)

)

\Diamond(a_{2},a_{1},a_{3})\to\Diamond(a_{2},b_{1},a_{3})\in F

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a class of algebras. A ternary term
t(x,y,z)
is called a
discriminator term
for the class
\mathcal{K}
if, in every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{K}
, it satisfies

t(a,b,c)=\begin{cases}a,&\text{if }a\neq b,\\[4.0pt]
c,&\text{if }a=b,\end{cases}

for all
a,b,c\in A
. The variety generated by
\mathcal{K}
is then called a
discriminator variety
.

In particular, if every
\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{K}
has a Boolean reduct
(B,\vee,\wedge,\neg,0,1)
, it is well known that this is equivalent to the existence of a unary term
d(x)
—called a
unary discriminator term
—which satisfies, in every member of
\mathcal{K}
, the following conditions:

d(a)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }a=0,\\[4.0pt]
1,&\text{if }a\neq 0.\end{cases}

It is worth noticing that if we consider the symmetric difference

x+y\coloneqq(x\wedge\neg y)\vee(\neg x\wedge y),

then, in such a case, the terms
d
and
t
are in fact interdefinable

d(x)=\neg t(0,x,1)\quad\text{and}\quad t(x,y,z)=(x\wedge d(x+y))\vee(z\wedge\neg d(x+y)).

of an algebra

and for each congruence
\delta
on
\mathbf{A}
, there exists a congruence
\theta
on
\mathbf{B}
such that
\delta=\theta\cap A^{2}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{V}
is said to have
definable principal congruences
(DPC) if there exists a formula
\zeta(x,y,u,v)
in the first-order language of
\mathcal{V}
such that, for every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{V}
and all

(c,d)\in\mathsf{Cg}^{\mathbf{A}}(a,b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf{A}\models\zeta[a,b,c,d],

where

denotes the smallest congruence on

containing

. If the formula

can be expressed as a finite conjunction of equations, then
\mathcal{V}
is said to have
equationally definable principal congruences
(EDPC). Finally, a variety
\mathcal{V}
is called
arithmetical
if, for every algebra
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{V}
, the congruence lattice of
\mathbf{A}
is both distributive and permutable; equivalently,
\mathcal{V}
is arithmetical if it is both
congruence distributive
and
congruence permutable
.

We conclude this section with some immediate consequences of Theorem

Corollary 4.12
.

The following hold:

(1)

has CEP.

(2)

\mathcal{SPSI}
has EDPC.

(3)

\mathcal{SPSI}

5.
Topological dualities

In this section, we establish the topological framework underlying the representation of 3BAMOs.
We begin by fixing the notation and basic constructions that will be used throughout the rest of the paper.
Our aim is to introduce the class of
descriptive PSI-frames
, which provides the relational counterpart of Boolean algebras endowed with a monotone ternary operator.
The general strategy follows the classical Stone duality between Boolean algebras and Boolean spaces, while extending it to accommodate ternary operations by means of relational structures of the form
(X,\tau,R)
, where
R
connects points of the space with triples of closed subsets.
This approach applies and generalizes the ideas developed in
(
Celani, 2009
)
, adapting the duality techniques presented there to the context of ternary modal operations.

As will be shown below, this framework gives rise to three closely related dualities that share the same class of topological objects but differ in their morphisms.
Each duality corresponds to a distinct algebraic level: the first captures the purely descriptive correspondence between
3
-BAMOs and PSI-frames, the second refines this connection through morphisms that preserve the modal structure, and the third establishes a categorical equivalence between the algebraic and relational semantics of the associated logical systems.

We begin by fixing the notation that will be used throughout the rest of the paper.
Let
(X,\tau)
be a Boolean space.
We denote by

(X,\tau)

.
For

to indicate that they are comparable with respect to the product order on

for all

.
Moreover, we write

whenever there exists some

such that

.
Finally, for

We also denote by

For

Proposition 5.3

Let

Proof.

\displaystyle R^{-1}(Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3})\subseteq R^{-1}(Y_{1},Y_{2},Z)\cup R^{-1}(Y_{1},Y_{2},W)\cup R^{-1}(\mathrm{cl}(Z^{c}),\mathrm{cl}(W^{c}),Y_{3})\,,

The identity function on a PSI-space acts as a semi-PSI (hemi-PSI or PSI) map, and when two semi-PSI (hemi-PSI or PSI) maps are composed functionally, the result is also a semi-PSI (hemi-PSI or PSI) map. This structure gives rise to three categories: the category

Consequently, PSI-Algebras and their semi-homomorphisms form a concrete category, denoted as
\mathsf{PSI_{0}}
. Likewise, the category with PSI-Algebras as objects and hemi-homomorphisms as arrows is denoted by
\mathsf{PSI_{1}}
, and the category with PSI-Algebras as objects and homomorphisms as arrows is denoted by
\mathsf{PSI_{2}}
.

The following result is immediate, so we omit its proof.

Proposition 5.18
.

Isomorphisms on the category
\mathsf{PSI_{0}}
,
\mathsf{PSI_{1}}
and
\mathsf{PSI_{2}}
corresponds precisely with bijective semi-homomorphisms, bijective hemi-homomorphisms and bijective homomorphisms, respectively.

In light of Definition
5.17
, the following result is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.16
.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\Diamond_{\mathbf{A}})
and
(\mathbf{B},\Diamond_{\mathbf{B}})
be two PSI-Algebras and let
h\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
be a homomorphism of Boolean algebras. Let us consider the map
h^{-1}\colon\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B})\to\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{A})
. Then, the following holds:

(1)

h
is a PSI semi-homomorphism if and only if
h^{-1}
is a semi-PSI map.

(2)

h
is a PSI hemi-homomorphism if and only if
h^{-1}
is a hemi-PSI map.

(3)

h
is a PSI homomorphism if and only if
h^{-1}
is a PSI map.

An adaptation of
(
Celani, 2009
, Theorem 18)
produces the following

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a mapping between the ps-spaces
(X_{1},\tau_{1},R_{1})
and
(X_{2},\tau_{2},R_{2})
. Consider the function
f^{*}\colon\mathscr{CO}(X_{2})\to\mathscr{CO}(X_{1})
defined by
f^{*}(U)\coloneqq f^{-1}[U]
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

f
is a semi-PSI map if and only if
f^{*}
is a PSI semi-homomorphism.

(2)

f
is a hemi-PSI map if and only if
f^{*}
is a PSI hemi-homomorphism.

(3)

f
is a PSI map if and only if
f^{*}
is a PSI homomorphism.

Let
j\in\{0,1,2\}
. By Theorem
5.15
and Lemma
5.19
, we are able to define functors

G_{j}\colon\mathsf{PSI_{j}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{PSISp_{j}}

as follows

(\mathbf{B},\Diamond)\mapsto(\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B}),\tau_{\mathbf{B}},R_{\Diamond}),

h\mapsto h^{-1}.

Analogously, by Proposition
5.8
and Lemma
5.20
the assignments

(X,\tau,R)\mapsto(\mathscr{CO}(X),\Diamond_{R}),

also define functors

H_{j}\colon\mathsf{PSISp}_{j}\to\mathsf{PSI}_{j}^{\mathrm{op}}.

Therefore, by standard results of Stone duality, together with Lemmas
5.19
and
5.20
, it follows that the functors
G_{j}
and

with

yield an equivalence of categories.

Theorem 5.21
.

The categories
\mathsf{PSI}_{0}
,
\mathsf{PSI}_{1}
and
\mathsf{PSI}_{2}
are dually equivalent to
\mathsf{PSISp}_{0}
,
\mathsf{PSISp}_{1}
and
\mathsf{PSISp}_{2}
, respectively.

Theorem
5.21
establishes the dual equivalences between the algebraic and the topological sides of the theory of PSI-Algebras. In view of this correspondence, we can now focus on the case of Extended Contact Algebras. The next remark shows how these algebras naturally fit into the general framework of PSI-Algebras and PSI-spaces, thus allowing us to interpret the extended contact relation as a particular instance of the relational operator
\Diamond
. This observation motivates the introduction of a special class of PSI-spaces, namely those for which the relational component exhibits a form of totality, as defined below.

Remark 5.22
.

Let
(\mathbf{B},\vdash)
be an ECA and let
(\mathbf{B},\Diamond_{\vdash})
be its associated relational PSI-Algebra. if
\vec{Y}\in\mathscr{C}_{0}(\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B}))
, it is clear that
R^{-1}(\vec{Y})\in\{\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B}),\emptyset\}
. Moreover, by Theorem
5.21
it is also the case that
(\mathbf{B},\Diamond_{\vdash})
is isomorphic to
\left(\mathscr{CO}(\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B})),\Diamond_{R_{\Diamond_{\vdash}}}\right)
.
\dashv

Definition 5.23
.

We say that a PSI-space
(X,R)
is
total
if for every
\vec{Y}\in\mathscr{C}_{0}(X)^{3}
, the following condition holds

(T)

If there exists
x\in X
such that
(x,\vec{Z})\in R
, then,
(y,\vec{Z})\in R
for every
y\in X
.

Equivalently, (
T
) says that
R^{-1}(\vec{Z})=\{X,\emptyset\}
, for every
\vec{Z}\in\mathscr{C}_{0}(X)^{3}
.
\dashv

5.15
,
5.8
, Proposition
5.5

5.23
we get:

Proposition 5.24
.

The following hold:

(1)

(\mathbf{B},\vdash)
is an ECA iff
(\mathrm{Ul}(\mathbf{B}),R_{\Diamond_{\vdash}})
is a total PSI-space.

(2)

(X,\tau,R)

(\mathscr{CO}(X),\Diamond_{R})
is a relational PSI-Algebra.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\vdash_{\mathbf{A}})
and
(\mathbf{B},\vdash_{\mathbf{B}})
be two ECAs. We say that a homomorphism of Boolean algebras
h\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
is a
reflecting ECA-morphism
if for every
a,b,c\in A
:

(h(a),h(b))\vdash_{\mathbf{B}}h(c)\;\Rightarrow\;(a,b)\vdash_{\mathbf{A}}c\,.

On the other hand, if

(a,b)\vdash_{\mathbf{A}}c\;\Rightarrow\;(h(a),h(b))\vdash_{\mathbf{B}}h(c)

holds, then we say that
h
is a
preserving ECA-morphism
. Furthermore, if
h
is both reflecting and preserving ECA-morphism, then we say that
h
is a
similarity ECA-morphism
.

Proposition 5.25
.

Let
(\mathbf{A}_{1},\vdash_{1})
and
(\mathbf{A}_{2},\vdash_{2})
be two ECAs and let
(\mathbf{A}_{i},\Diamond_{\vdash_{i}})

i\in\{0,1\}
. If
h:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
is a Boolean homomorphism, then the following hold:

(1)

h
is a reflecting ECA-morphism iff
h
is a PSI semi-homomorphism.

(2)

h
is a preserving ECA-morphism iff
h
is a PSI hemi-homomorphism.

(3)

h
is a similarity ECA-morphism iff
h
is a PSI homomorphism.

We write
\mathsf{EC}_{0}
,

,
\mathsf{tPSISp}_{1}

the full subcategories of
\mathsf{PSISp}_{0}
,
\mathsf{PSISp}_{1}
and

whose objects are total PSI-spaces.

Poster 14. A Prospective, Randomized Study Comparing the Modified Ellison and Modified Lemaire Techniques for Extraarticular Tenodesis in Primary ACL Reconstruction
Authors: Kostas Economopoulos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objectives:Current research confirms the addition of a lateral extraarticular tenodesis (LET) in high-risk patients undergoing primary ACL reconstruction decreases the rate of graft failure. However, the optimal LET technique remains unknown. The modified Lemaire LET is currently considered the gold standard when used with ACL reconstruction. The modified Ellison technique is a distally based LET where the iliotibial band (ITB) is reattached to Gerdy's tubercle in its own anatomic footprint after being passed under the LCL. The modified Ellison technique removes any chance of femoral tunnel convergence and leads to a reproducible location for reattachment of the ITB graft. The purpose of our study was to compare the clinical outcomes of the modified Ellison and modified Lemaire in primary ACL reconstruction. these techniques. We hypothesized the modified Ellison would have similar outcomes compared to the modified Lemaire.Methods:A prospective, randomized study of patients undergoing primary ACL reconstruction who were considered high-risk was performed. Patients were randomized using a permuted block randomization with the goal of 30 patients in each group. High-risk patients were considered patients with any two of the following risk factors: less than 25 years old, generalized ligamentous laxity (GLL), currently participating in cutting and pivoting sports, a pivot shift of 3+ preoperatively or had any residual pivot after ACL reconstruction was completed as determined intraoperatively. Patients undergoing revision ACLR or had concomitant procedures other than meniscal work or shaving chondroplasty were excluded. Clinical outcomes included graft failure defined as ACL graft tearing or residual rotational instability with a pivot shift of 2+ or more at final follow up. Patient reported outcomes included IKDC and Lysholm scores obtained preoperatively, 6 and 12 months after surgery. All patients had at least 1 year of follow up. All patients followed the same postoperative protocol regardless of technique used.Results:A total of 28 patients were included in the modified Ellison group and 27 in the modified Lemaire group. There was no difference between the groups with regards to age (average age of study participants 24.1 years). The percentage of patients with GLL was 17.9% in the Ellison group and 14.8% in the Lemaire group (p=.77). The average follow up was similar between the Ellison and Lemaire group (12.6 months and 12.7 months respectively) (p=.73). The type of graft used by each group was also similar. Overall, 60% of the study patients were treated with quadriceps autograft, 18.2% BTB, 16.4% hamstring autograft and 5.5% allograft. Graft failure rates were similar between the two groups with 17.9% of the Ellison group and 14.8% of the Lemaire groups experiencing failure (p=.77). One graft tear occurred in the Ellison group while there were no re-tears in the Lemaire group (p=.33). Postoperative residual rotational instability was similar between the Ellison and Lemaire groups at the 1-year post operative period (14.3% and 14.8% respectively) (p=.96). There was no difference between the 2 groups with regards to return to sport. The Ellison group showed a return to sport of 93.3% and return to sport at same or higher level of 73.3%. In the Lemaire group, return to sport was 92.3% and return to sport at the same or higher level was 76.9%. Time to return to sport was similar between the Ellison and Lemaire group (8.7 months and 9.9 months respectively) (p=.20). Both groups showed a significant improvement of both the IKDC and Lysholm scores at all postoperative time points compared to preoperative values. At 1-year follow up, the IKDC scores between the Ellison and Lemaire groups were similar (91.5 vs. 88.6 respectively) (p=.11). The 1-year Lysholm scores of 90.2 in the Ellison group and 91.7 in the Lemaire group were similar (p=.48). There was a significantly higher rate of LET site hematomas in the Lemaire group (14.8%) compared to none in the Ellison group (p=.03). There was no difference between the two techniques with regards to other complications including arthrofibrosis, contralateral ACL tears, infection, snapping ITB, painful hardware or overconstraint of the lateral compartmentConclusions:We found no difference in failure rate between the modified Ellison and modified Lemaire techniques when used in high-risk patients for primary ACL reconstruction. The Ellison technique has the same effectiveness in preventing residual internal rotation of the tibia following ACLR as the Lemaire technique. Based on our findings, the modified Ellison technique is an effective alternative to the Lemaire technique which offers a similar effectiveness in reducing graft failure and may have the benefit of reducing bleeding complications.

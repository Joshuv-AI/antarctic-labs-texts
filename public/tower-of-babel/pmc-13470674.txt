Dual-responsive N-isopropylacrylamide and boron affinity nanoparticles for application to selective recognition of glycoproteins
Authors: Xing-yu Hou, Zhi-you Chen, Jing-hui Shen, Zhao-qi Jiang, Li-xin Ding, Yu-guang Lv, Xin-xin Bian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This research examines the utilization of dendritic fibrous nanosilica (DFNS) as a matrix for the introduction of 3-carboxyphenylboronic acid (CPBA) and N-isopropylacrylamide (NIPAM) in the preparation of a boron-affinity material (DFNS@BN) exhibiting dual pH- and thermo-responsive behavior. DFNS mesopores, featuring a folded surface and centrally divergent pores, were synthesized through a one step hydrothermal approach. More importantly, synergistic coupling between pH-controlled boronate covalent recognition and temperature-tunable hydrogen-bond conformational regulation of thermoresponsive polymers is realized. At temperatures below the lower critical solution temperature (LCST, 32 °C) under weakly alkaline conditions (pH > 7), dual intermolecular forces jointly boost glycoprotein adsorption capacity; at temperatures above 32 °C under acidic environments, simultaneous dual stimuli trigger thorough dissociation of captured glycoproteins. The material manifested selective adsorption of glycoproteins (transferrin, ovalbumin, and horseradish peroxidase) via the dual response of boron affinity and thermo-responsiveness. The adsorption kinetics adhered to the proposed secondary adsorption model, and the Langmuir isothermal adsorption model was employed. In the analysis of real samples, the material successfully enriched nine glycoproteins from human saliva samples. These findings imply the potential of boron-affinity materials in glycoprotein separation and enrichment, offering a promising strategy for the development of effective glycoprotein separation agents for bioanalytical applications.

Introduction

Glycoproteins, as important products of protein glycosylation, are composed of covalently linked oligosaccharide chains and polypeptide chains. These molecules exert critical functions in tumor immunology, cell adhesion, signal transduction, and inflammatory responses.1–3 Due to these functions, erum isolated glycoproteins are extensively utilized as biomarkers in clinical diagnostics.4,5 Accurate disease detection relies heavily on the precise identification of glycoproteins.6–10 Nevertheless, analyzing glycoproteins is still difficult owing to labor-intensive preparation steps, their naturally low levels, and the significant interference caused by high-abundance compounds within intricate biological matrices. These elements lead to suboptimal analytical outcomes in direct mass spectrometry analysis.11–15 Hence, the targeted isolation and amplification of glycoproteins prior to conducting mass spectrometry are essential. In this scenario, focusing on the enrichment of glycoproteins is essential for analyzing proteins. Lectins and antibodies represent two prevalent methods for the targeted concentration of glycoproteins.16–20 Nevertheless, these approaches are expensive because of the challenging synthesis process for these biological compounds, coupled with their high price and limited stability. Using hydrazine affinity is an alternative method for isolating glycoproteins, especially glycopeptides. However, this technique necessitates rigorous conditions and lengthy derivatization processes, which can potentially alter the protein's structure21 In recent years, the use of boronate affinity has attracted more focus in the fields of metabolomics and proteomics. This method is utilized for the targeted enrichment and isolation of cis-diols from intricate biological specimens.22,23 Within an alkaline setting, boronic acid interacts with cis-diol compounds, leading to the creation of a five- or six-membered ester ring. When the pH is shifted to a more acidic environment, this ring structure breaks apart. This distinctive characteristic, which facilitates the trapping and release of cis-diol compounds through pH alteration, makes it a superb affinity interaction for applications in sensing, separation sciences, and drug delivery systems. Boronate affinity provides multiple benefits, such as being cost-effective, facilitating straightforward capture and release of cis-diol biomolecules using pH adjustments, offering high selectivity, and having great compatibility with mass spectrometry.24–26

In recent years, a range of boronate affinity-based methods have attracted significant attention. These include molecularly imprinted polymers (MIPs), nanomaterials, and mesoporous materials.4,27–30 Dendritic fibrous nanosilica (DFNS) spheres are innovative mesoporous materials featuring three-dimensional (3D) central divergent channels along with hierarchical pore architectures.31,32 In contrast to conventional SiO2 and MCM-41 mesoporous substances, it provides a greater specific surface area along with improved pore characteristics.33–35 The three-dimensional divergent pore frameworks offer a reliable platform for confining “particle-loaded” or “intelligent” nanostructures, capable of reacting to different external stimuli, including pH changes, erosion, magnetic fields, and temperature variations.36–38N-isopropylacrylamide (NIPAM) is a well-researched thermoresponsive polymer known for its low Lower Critical Solution Temperature (LCST) when dissolved in water. Below the LCST, it assumes a random coil configuration, whereas above this temperature, it transitions into a denser, spherical structure.39 When the temperature is below the LCST, the amide groups and water molecules in the NIPAM chain hydrogen bond, resulting in a stretch thread-like structure. When the temperature exceeds the LCST, these hydrogen bonds are largely destroyed, and the polymer chain transitions from a loose thread-like structure to a dense particulate form, exhibiting phase transition behavior. Liu prepared silica nanoparticles grafted with pNIPAm through SI-ATRP, and investigated the thermal properties of the resulting composite particles.

In this study, we employed a boron affinity technique coupled with thermoresponsive properties to prepare dual-responsive NIPAM and 3-carboxyphenylboronic acid (CPBA) nanoparticles as thermosensitive and pH-responsive materials for the selective recognition of glycoproteins. DFNS mesoporous spheres, characterized by wrinkled surfaces and centrally divergent pores, were fabricated via a one-step hydrothermal approach. The dual-responsive material exhibited efficient and selective adsorption of transferrin (TRF), ovalbumin (OVA) and horseradish peroxidase (HRP).

Preparation of DFNS@BN

DFNS was synthesized via a hydrothermal route. Cyclohexane (30 mL), pentanol (1.5 mL), urea (0.6 g), CPB (1 g), and deionized water (30 mL) were initially combined. Afterward, TEOS (2.5 g) was added to the mixture, subsequently, the mixture was stirred for 0.5 h. Thereafter, the mixture was transferred to a Teflon-lined autoclave and kept at 120 °C for 4 h to accomplish the hydrothermal reaction. Subsequently, the solid product was obtained via centrifugation, washed successively with distilled water and acetone, and calcined at 550 °C for 6 h. The resulting silica nanoparticles (3.0 g) were then dispersed in a 1% APTES solution (prepared by mixing 1 mL APTES with 99 mL anhydrous ethanol) and stirred at room temperature for 24 h to achieve surface functionalization. Ultimately, the DFNS product was harvested via centrifugation, meticulously rinsed with acetone and methanol, and subsequently dried under vacuum conditions.

The synthesis procedure of DFNS@BN is illustrated in Fig. 1. Initially, CPBA (50 mg), EDC (100 mg), and NHS (50 mg) were dissolved in ultrapure water (30 mL) and stirred for 30 min at room temperature (RT). Subsequently, NIPAM (50 mg), APS (20 mg), MBA (50 mg), and the as-synthesized DFNS (50 mg) were added to the solution. The resulting mixture was purged with nitrogen for 20 min at RT to remove dissolved oxygen, followed by continuous stirring at RT for 4 h. Finally, the solid precipitate was collected and vacuum-dried.

The XRD pattern exhibits a broad diffraction peak centered at 23.7°(Fig. 3B), indicating that the sample is composed of amorphous material, which retains the nanostructure during the preparation process and does not affect the adsorption performance of the nanomaterial. Detection condition optimization

As shown in Fig. 5A, temperature/pH dual-responsive materials, the mechanism involves controlling the “adsorption–dissociation” process through environmental stimuli. Adsorption takes place when the environmental conditions satisfy LCST (<32 °C) and pH > 7, while dissociation occurs when the environmental conditions change to LCST (>32 °C) and pH < 7. Thus the temperatures and pH of the system also affects the adsorption performance of glycoproteins.

(A) Mechanism diagram. (B) Temperature and (C) Sample pH on the adsorption effect of glycoproteins. (D) Optimization of TRF, OVA and HRP sample concentration.

Introducing NIPAM as a temperature sensitive element can expand under LCST. The carbonyl and amino groups of NIPAM effectively form hydrogen bonds with glycoproteins containing hydroxyl groups. When the temperature exceeds LCST, NIPAM changes from an expansion state to a contraction state and cannot bind to glycoproteins. Therefore, the experimental steps of DFNS@BN were conducted at different temperatures of 10 °C, 30 °C, and 50 °C to test the temperature response in Fig. 5B. The adsorption capacity of glycoproteins at 30 °C is higher than that at 10 °C and 50 °C. And at 10 °C may be attributed to the decreased ability of hydrogen bond formation at low temperatures. It investigated the adsorption of TRF, OVA and HRP on DFNS@BN at pH values of 6.5, 7.5, 8.5, 9.5 and 10.5 in Fig. 5C. The adsorption reached its maximum at a pH value of 8.5. This phenomenon can potentially be ascribed to the fact that weakly basic conditions promote the covalent reaction between the ortho dihydroxyl groups of boronic acid ligands and cis dihydroxyl compounds, leading to the formation of five- or six-membered cyclic esters. When the tested pH value attained 9.0, the adsorption capacity exhibited a slight decline. This reduction might be associated with glycoprotein degradation or electrostatic repulsion induced by the augmented negative charge.

Fig. 5D presented the optimization of the concentrations. For HRP and TRF, within the concentration range of 0.3 mg mL−1, the adsorption amount increases as the concentration increases. When the concentration is higher than 0.3 mg mL−1, the adsorption sites become saturated, and the adsorption capacity tends to stabilize. In the concentration range of 1.0 mg mL−1 for OVA, the adsorption exhibits an increasing trend with the rise in concentration. When the concentration exceeds 1.0 mg mL−1, the adsorption sites reach saturation, and the adsorption capacity tends to stabilize. Consequently, the working concentration of 0.3 mg mL−1 and 1.0 mg mL−1 was ultimately selected.

Selective and stability analysis

To assess the specific adsorption of glycoproteins, LZ, bromelain, BHB, BSA were employed as competitors in Fig. 7A. It is apparent that the adsorption capacity (Q value) of DFNS@BN for TRF, OVA, and HRP has significantly increased, whereas for LZ, bromelain, BHB, and BSA exhibited a downward trend. A higher Q value of this adsorbent indicates a greater selectivity for the adsorption of target substances. The favorable adsorption performance of DFNS@BN for glycoproteins may be attributed to the dual-responsive effect of borate and thermosensitive materials. The oligosaccharide chains of glycoproteins can establish linkages through borate affinity. Given that TRF, OVA and HRP are glycoproteins with a cis-diol structure, they possess a stronger binding capacity compared to non-glycoproteins such as BSA. Consequently, the dual-responsive mechanism of borate affinity and thermosensitive materials is crucial for enhancing the adsorption capacity of glycoproteins.

(A) Selectivity of DFNS@BN for different proteins. (B) Reproducibility and (C) reusability of DFNS@BN.

The reproducibility was investigated by using DFNS@BN for the adsorption of TRF, OVA and HRP for 6 batches in Fig. 7B, with TRF results ranging from 634.51 to 670.72 mg g−1, OVA results from 592.98 to 645.61 mg g−1 and HRP results from 324.73 to 350.37 mg g−1. The reusability of adsorbents represents another crucial factor that needs to be taken into account in the practical application of adsorbents. Consequently, the same batch of DFNS@BN was employed for six adsorption–desorption studies of glycoproteins. As illustrated in Fig. 7C, this phenomenon can potentially be attributed to the loss of specific binding sites during the regeneration process, owing to the presence of DFNS@BN. After 6 cycles, the adsorption capacity decreased by 15.71%, 13.25%, and 15.55%, respectively. This finding implies that the chemical bonding pattern of DFNS@BN preserves the durability and reusability of the material.

Analysis of glycoproteins in human saliva

Human saliva encompasses numerous trace glycoproteins, which are widely recognized as biomarkers for the early diagnosis of diseases. In the context of specific diseases, the glycoprotein profile undergoes abnormal regulation. For instance, in pulmonary diseases such as lung cancer, asthma, bronchitis, and cystic fibrosis, an over-production of mucins occurs despite their typically low concentrations in saliva. Moreover, a substantial quantity of interfering substances, including sugars and non glycoproteins, render the analysis of non enriched glycoproteins extremely arduous. Therefore, an efficient enrichment process is required prior to glycoprotein analysis. Therefore, saliva samples before and after enrichment using DFNS@BN were analysed, as shown in Fig. 8 and Table S3. It showed that prior to enrichment (a) no glycoprotein peaks were detected, whereas subsequent to enrichment (b) nine discrete glycoprotein peaks were detected and identified.

Conclusions

Boric acid-functionalized polymers were synthesized and employed for the selective adsorption of glycoproteins through the dual responses of boron affinity and thermophilic materials. The adsorption durations for TRF, OVA, and HRP were 30 minutes each. The adsorption behavior of the adsorbent adhered to the proposed second-order adsorption kinetics and the Langmuir isothermal adsorption model. In the analysis of real samples, satisfactory results were obtained, and nine glycoproteins were enriched from human saliva samples. These findings suggest that functionalization with borate affinity polymers can be utilized for the selective and sensitive capture of glycoproteins and the identification of biomarkers in real biological samples.

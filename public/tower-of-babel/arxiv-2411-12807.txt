Scaffolding Residues in Yang-Mills-Scalar à la CHY
Zurab Jashi, Jaroslav Scheinpflug, and Yale Yauk

Abstract
Motivated by recent work by Arkani-Hamed et al. [ 1 ] , we compute the “scaffolding” residue of 2 ​ n 2n -scalar Yang-Mills-Scalar amplitudes to obtain pure n n -gluon amplitudes à la Cachazo-He-Yuan (CHY). In particular, we show how the Pfaffian of Ψ \Psi , which is a matrix rich in structure, emerges from that of the simple A A matrix. The same CHY computation straightforwardly produces n n -graviton amplitudes from 2 ​ n 2n -scalar amplitudes in the Einstein-Maxwell-Scalar theory. We also consider partial “scaffolding” residues, i.e., general multi-collinear limits and their interplay with color-dressed amplitudes. These notes are the result of a Perimeter Scholars International (PSI) Winter School Project supervised by Prof. Freddy Cachazo.

The Cachazo-He-Yuan formulation (CHY)
[

of tree-level scattering amplitudes connects the space of Mandelstam invariants
s_{ab}=(k_{a}+k_{b})^{2}
and the moduli space of punctured Riemann spheres,
{\cal M}_{0,n}
, via the scattering equations
[
32
]
. Scattering amplitudes of massless particles in a large variety of theories can be written as

A^{\rm massless}_{n}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ik}}{u_{i}-u_{k}}\bigg)\,\mathcal{I}_{L}\,\mathcal{I}_{R}\,,

(1)

where
{\cal I}_{L}
and
{\cal I}_{R}
are functions of the basic
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C})
covariant factors
1/(u_{i}-u_{j})
, and polynomials in
k_{i}\cdot k_{j}
,
k_{i}\cdot\epsilon_{j}
and
\epsilon_{i}\cdot\epsilon_{j}
. This means that all singularities of the amplitude must be produced as a result of approaching a boundary of the moduli space,
{\cal M}_{0,n}
.

The terms in the argument of the Dirac delta are the scattering equations – there is one equation for each particle
i
. The functions
{\cal I}_{L}
and
{\cal I}_{R}
are usually called the “left” and “right” integrands. Each integrand carries the same
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C})
weight.

The first tree-level scattering amplitudes that were discovered to have a CHY formulation were those of gluons in pure
U(N)
Yang-Mills in arbitrary space-time dimensions
[
32
]
. The CHY formula for a partial amplitude in the canonical ordering is given by

A^{\rm YM}_{n}(1,2,\ldots,n)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ik}}{u_{i}-u_{k}}\bigg)\mathrm{PT}(1,2,\ldots,n)\,\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}\,,

(2)

where the two integrands in (
1
) are

{\cal I}_{L}^{\rm YM}:=\mathrm{PT}(1,2,\ldots,n):=\frac{1}{(u_{1}-u_{2})(u_{2}-u_{3})\cdots(u_{n}-u_{1})}\quad{\rm and}\quad{\cal I}_{R}^{\rm YM}:=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}.

(3)

The first integrand is known as the Parke-Taylor factor
[
2
]
, while the second one is the Pfaffian of
\Psi_{2n}
, a
2n\times 2n
matrix, given in block form by

\Psi_{2n}=\begin{bmatrix}A&-C^{\intercal}\\
C&B\end{bmatrix},

(4)

where
A
and
B
are
n\times n
antisymmetric matrices with off-diagonal components

A_{ab}:=\frac{k_{a}\cdot k_{b}}{u_{a}-u_{b}}\quad{\rm and}\quad B_{ab}:=\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}}{u_{a}-u_{b}}.

The matrix
C
has components designed to make gauge invariance manifest,

C_{aa}:=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}^{n}C_{ab}\quad{\rm and}\quad C_{ab}:=\frac{\epsilon_{a}\cdot k_{b}}{u_{a}-u_{b}}.

Gauge invariance, i.e. the vanishing of the amplitude when any polarization vector
\epsilon_{a}
is replaced by the momentum
k_{a}
of the corresponding particle, manifests simply as a linear algebra property of Pfaffians.

Soon after these formulations were discovered, various procedures were introduced to construct new CHY formulations out of known ones
[
35
]
. One such procedure is the compactification of amplitudes. Compactifying Yang-Mills amplitudes led to a theory known as Yang-Mills-Scalar (YMS). Higher dimensional gluons with polarization vectors
\epsilon_{a}
entirely in the uncompactified dimensions remain as gluons while those
\epsilon_{a}
entirely in the internal dimensions are scalars. Particularly simple is the CHY formulation of the pure scalar amplitude in YMS,

A^{\rm YMS:scalar}_{n}(1,2,\ldots,n)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta\!\left(E_{i}\right)\,\mathrm{PT}(1,2,\ldots,n)\,\operatorname{Pf}X\,\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,.

(5)

Here
E_{i}
is a short-hand notation for the scattering equations,
A
is the
n\times n
matrix defined above while
X
is the matrix obtained from
B
by setting all
\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}=1
.

In recent work
[
1
]
, Arkani-Hamed et al. introduced the “scaffolding” residue, which is a “maximal” multi-collinear limit. Applying the scaffolding residue to an amplitude of
2n
scalars in the YMS theory leads to an amplitude of
n
gluons, where polarization vectors emerge from the momenta of the scalars and gauge invariance is geometrized.

One of the objectives of this Perimeter Scholars International Winter School Project is the derivation of gluon amplitudes from scalar amplitudes using the scaffolding residue computed using the CHY formulation. As a by-product, the same CHY derivations directly connect
2n
scalar amplitudes in the Einstein-Maxwell-Scalar (EMS) theory (obtained by compactification of Einstein’s theory) to obtain
n
-graviton amplitudes.

In this note we also consider general multi-collinear limits of YMS and extensions to full color-dressed amplitudes.

This work is organized as follows: in section 2, we introduce the compactification procedure, which allows one to obtain the YMS amplitude (
5
). We proceed to see how a YM amplitude emerges as the most singular term of the YMS amplitude under the scaffolding limit using the CHY formalism. In section 3, we extend the scaffolding analysis to Einstein gravity. Finally, in section 4, we present extensions of the scaffolding residue to possibly non-maximal multi-collinear limits. We show that color-dressed amplitudes also produce the expected singularities under the multi-collinear limits.

From YM to YMS and back: scaffolding scalar amplitudes in YMS

An intricate web of connections among theories of massless particles was constructed using various operations on the CHY formulation of three basic theories
[
32
]
. These are the biadjoint scalar, Yang-Mills, and Einstein Gravity. These are obtained by combining the two basic integrands
\mathrm{PT}(1,2,\ldots n)
and
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}
presented in (
3
).

The simplest operation on CHY integrands is compactification and when applied to the Yang-Mills formula, it leads to the CHY formulation of Yang-Mills-Scalar amplitudes. The procedure is to start with an amplitude of
n
gluons in
D=d+1
dimensions and set their momenta
\mathcal{K}_{a}
and polarization vectors
\mathcal{E}_{a}
to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{a}&:=(k_{a},0),\quad\forall a\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\\
\mathcal{E}_{a}&:=\begin{cases}(\epsilon_{a},0),&\text{for a gluon},\\
(0,0,\ldots,0,1),&\text{for a scalar}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(6)

Here
k_{a},\epsilon_{a}
are
d
-dimensional momenta and polarization vectors respectively.

To substitute this into the expression for
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}
it is convenient to use the indices
p,q,r
for gluons,
i,j,k
for scalars and
a,b,c
when the identity of the particle is not important. Using this, the non-zero inner products in the matrix
\Psi_{2n}
become

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{K}_{a}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{b}}{u_{a}-u_{b}}=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{a}\cdot k_{b}}{u_{a}-u_{b}},\qquad\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{q}}{u_{p}-u_{q}}=\frac{\epsilon_{p}\cdot\epsilon_{q}}{u_{p}-u_{q}},\qquad\frac{\mathcal{E}_{i}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{j}}{u_{i}-u_{j}}=\frac{1}{u_{i}-u_{j}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{q}}{u_{p}-u_{q}}=\frac{\epsilon_{p}\cdot k_{q}}{u_{p}-u_{q}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}\cdot\mathcal{K}_{j}}{u_{p}-u_{j}}=\frac{\epsilon_{p}\cdot k_{j}}{u_{p}-u_{j}}\,.

(7)

Substituting into the
\Psi_{2n}
matrix with columns and rows organized so that the “polarization” of scalars are last, one finds

\Psi_{2n}=\begin{bmatrix}A&-{\hat{C}}^{\intercal}&0\\
\hat{C}&{\hat{B}}&0\\
0&0&X_{s}\end{bmatrix},

(8)

where
A
is the original
n\times n
matrix
A
while
\hat{B}
only contains gluons. The matrix
X_{s}
has components
1/(u_{i}-u_{j})
and only involves scalars. Clearly,
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\widehat{\Psi}_{2n}\operatorname{Pf}X_{s}
.

Our main case of interest is the case when all particles are scalars and therefore
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi_{2n}=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,\,\operatorname{Pf}X_{s}
. Since there are only scalars in the amplitude we drop the subscript and write
\operatorname{Pf}X_{s}
as
\operatorname{Pf}X
.

Combining all the pieces we find the CHY formula for the tree-level amplitude of
n
scalars,

A^{\rm YMS:scalar}_{n}(1,2,\ldots,n)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta\!\left(E_{i}\right)\,\mathrm{PT}(1,2,\ldots,n)\,\operatorname{Pf}X\,\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,.

(9)

In ref.
[
1
]
, a new formulation for Yang-Mills scattering amplitudes at any loop order was proposed based on the same combinatorial techniques used for the
\Tr\phi^{3}
theory. One of the key ingredients in making the connection was the use of a multi-collinear limit on Yang-Mills-Scalar amplitudes of
2n
scalars to produce
n
-gluon amplitudes from “scalar” data. This procedure was called the “scaffolding residue”, which we diagrammatically represent in figure
1
.

Given that the CHY formalism straightforwardly connected YM amplitudes to that of YMS scalars, it is natural to see how the scaffolding residue is computed in the CHY formula for scalars (
9
) to produce that for gluons. At first sight, this seems an impossible task since the starting amplitude only contains the Pfaffian of a very simple matrix, the
A
matrix, while the result should contain the Pfaffian of a matrix with a rich structure, the
\Psi
matrix. In this section we show in detail how
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi
emerges from
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A
in a beautiful manner.

The scaffolding procedure takes a
2n
-scalar scattering amplitude in the YMS theory and produces an
n
-gluon scattering amplitude in the YM theory under the limit
s_{ii^{\prime}}\to 0
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. To this end, let us consider the YMS amplitude with
2n
particles labelled
\{1,1^{\prime},\dots,n,n^{\prime}\}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&A^{\rm YMS:scalar}_{2n}(1,1^{\prime},\ldots,n,n^{\prime})=\\
&\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i^{\prime}=1^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}\differential{u_{i'}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\delta\!\left(E_{i}\right)\prod_{i^{\prime}=1^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}\delta\!\left(E_{i^{\prime}}\right)\,\mathrm{PT}(1,1^{\prime},\ldots,n,n^{\prime})\,\operatorname{Pf}X\,\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,.\end{split}

(10)

The scaffolding limit
s_{ii^{\prime}}\to 0
can be parametrised by a variable
\tau
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}s_{ii^{\prime}}&=\tau\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}\,,\\
u_{i^{\prime}}&=u_{i}+\tau x_{i}\,.\end{split}

(11)

Here we have also assumed that
u_{i^{\prime}}-u_{i}={\cal O}(\tau)
. In general the scattering equations for
2n
particles have
(2n-3)!
solutions. However, it is known that in a collinear limit, e.g.
s_{11^{\prime}}\to 0
, the solutions split into “singular” and “regular”. The terminology refers to the moduli space of punctured Riemann spheres. Solutions that go to the boundary of the moduli space are called singular while those that remain at generic point are called regular. In a single collinear limit,
s_{11^{\prime}}={\cal O}(\tau)
, singular solutions go as
u_{1^{\prime}}-u_{1}={\cal O}(\tau)
. Moreover there are
((2n-1)-3)!
such singular solutions. Since the scaffolding residue affects compatible channels, we expect that each
s_{ii^{\prime}}\to 0
limit can be described as a single collinear limit and hence
u_{i^{\prime}}-u_{i}={\cal O}(\tau)
. In the end, after all
n
collinear limits are implemented, there will only be
(n-3)!
solutions.

Let us introduce the commonly used shorthand notation,

u_{ab}:=u_{a}-u_{b}.

(12)

We apply the change of variables (
11
) to the amplitude in eq. (
10
). Next we study each factor in the amplitude.

The simplest element to consider is the Parke-Taylor factor, which to leading order in
\tau
becomes

\text{PT}(1,1^{\prime},\dots,n,n^{\prime})=\frac{1}{u_{11^{\prime}}u_{1^{\prime}2}u_{22^{\prime}}\dots u_{nn^{\prime}}u_{n^{\prime}1}}=\frac{1}{(-1)^{n}\tau^{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\mathrm{PT}(1,\dots,n)\,,

(13)

using the shorthand notation,
u_{ii^{\prime}}=-\tau x_{i}
.

The next element is the Pfaffian of
X
. The explicit formula of the Pfaffian of a
2n\times 2n
antisymmetric matrix
M
is given by

\operatorname{Pf}M=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\sgn\sigma\prod_{i=1}^{n}M_{\sigma(2i-1),\sigma(2i)}\,.

(14)

Recalling that the matrix elements of
X
are given by
X_{ij}=\frac{1}{u_{ij}}
, the leading contribution to the Pfaffian of
X
comes from the term where each factor in the product is in the form
u_{ii^{\prime}}
, yielding, up to a sign,

\operatorname{Pf}X=\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{n-1}}\right)\,.

(15)

Next, consider the measure of integration. We choose to fix the
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C})
redundancy by setting
u_{1},u_{2}
and
u_{3}
to fixed values. Therefore, under the change of variables
u_{i^{\prime}}=u_{i}+\tau x_{i}
,

\prod_{i=4}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\differential{u_{i'}}=\prod_{i=4}^{n}\differential{u_i}\tau^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{x_i}\,.

(16)

Next, we have the scattering equations. To aid in the analysis, it is convenient to explicitly write down the scattering equations
E_{i}=0
and
E_{i^{\prime}}=0
to leading order in
\tau
,

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ij}}{u_{ij}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=1^{\prime}\\
j^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{s_{ij^{\prime}}}{u_{ij^{\prime}}}+\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}}{u_{ii^{\prime}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ij}}{u_{ij}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=1^{\prime}\\
j^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{s_{ij^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}-\frac{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}{x_{i}}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle E_{i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{i^{\prime}j}}{u_{i^{\prime}j}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=1^{\prime}\\
j^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}{u_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}+\frac{s_{i^{\prime}i}}{u_{i^{\prime}i}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{i^{\prime}j}}{u_{ij}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{\prime}=1^{\prime}\\
j^{\prime}\neq i^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}+\frac{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}{x_{i}}\,.

(18)

Adding the two equations, we get what will become a scattering equation for the
i^{\rm{th}}
-gluon,
{\symAMSb E}_{i}=0
,

{\symAMSb E}_{i}=E_{i}+E_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ij}+s_{ij^{\prime}}+s_{i^{\prime}j}+s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}\,.

(19)

This is indeed the scattering equation we expect for Yang-Mills theory, if the momentum of the
i^{\rm th}
gluon is defined as
\mathbb{k}_{i}:=k_{i}+k_{i^{\prime}}
and corresponding kinematic invariants
\mathbb{s}_{ij}:=(\mathbb{k}_{i}+\mathbb{k}_{j})^{2}
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\symAMSb E}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{s}_{ij}}{u_{ij}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(\mathbb{k}_{i}+\mathbb{k}_{j})^{2}}{u_{ij}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(k_{i}+k_{i^{\prime}}+k_{j}+k_{j^{\prime}})^{2}}{u_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ii^{\prime}}+s_{ij}+s_{ij^{\prime}}+s_{i^{\prime}j}+s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+s_{jj^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{ij}+s_{ij^{\prime}}+s_{i^{\prime}j}+s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}+{\cal O}(\tau)\,.

(20)

Before considering the last element, i.e. the Pfaffian of the
A
matrix, let us collect the results so far using
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C})
to gauge fix
u_{1},u_{2}
and
u_{3}
,

A^{\mathrm{YMS}}_{11^{\prime}\dots nn^{\prime}}=\!\!\int\prod_{i=4}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\differential{x_i}\prod_{i=4}^{n}\delta({\symAMSb E}_{i})\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n^{\prime}}\delta(E_{i^{\prime}})u_{12}^{2}u_{13}^{2}u_{23}^{2}\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}\frac{1}{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{2}}\,\mathrm{PT}(1\cdots n)\,\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,.

(21)

In this formula we interpret the equations
E_{i^{\prime}}=0
as equations for the
x_{i}
’s,

E_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{s_{i^{\prime}j}+s_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}{u_{ij}}+\frac{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}{x_{i}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{2k_{i^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{j}}{u_{ij}}+\frac{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}{x_{i}}+{\cal O}(\tau)=0\,,

(22)

so that to leading order in
\tau
,

\frac{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}{x_{i}}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{2k_{i^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{j}}{u_{ij}}\,.

(23)

Interpreting
E_{i^{\prime}}
as a function of the
x_{i}
’s, we have the identity

\delta(E_{i^{\prime}})=\frac{(x^{\circ}_{i})^{2}}{\hat{s}_{ii^{\prime}}}\delta(x_{i}-x^{\circ}_{i})\,,

(24)

where
x^{\circ}_{i}
solves (
23
). We carry out the integration over
x_{i}
in eq. (
21
), leading to

A^{\text{YMS}}_{11^{\prime}\dots nn^{\prime}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{ii^{\prime}}}\int\prod_{i=4}^{n}\differential{u_i}\prod_{i=4}^{n}\delta({\symAMSb E}_{i})u_{12}^{2}u_{13}^{2}u_{23}^{2}\text{PT}(1,\dots,n)\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\,+\dots\,,

(25)

where the ellipses indicate lower order terms in
\tau
. It is important to stress that the matrix
A
still depends on
x_{i}
’s and therefore it is to be understood as evaluated on the solution
x_{i}^{\circ}
to (
23
).

The scaffolding residue is defined as the coefficient of the most singular term in (
25
). After inspecting the leading coefficient, it is clear that it has the correct structure of a
n
-particle amplitude with canonical color ordering. We are then left to prove that
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi
.

There are several obstacles to overcome, the most obvious one is that
\Psi
depends on the polarization vectors of the gluons while
A
only depends on the momenta of the scalars. A precise map was proposed in
[
1
]
but we will not assume it and try to discover it by simply matching the momentum components of the matrix
\Psi
.

2.1.1
From the reduced Pfaffian of
A
to that of
\Psi

The last ingredient to consider is the reduced Pfaffian of
A
, which is now to be evaluated at the solutions of the scattering equations (
23
) for
x_{i}=x_{i}^{\circ}
, to show that

\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}A\rvert_{x_{i}=x_{i}^{\circ}}=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}\Psi\,,

(26)

where
\Psi
is the antisymmetric
2n\times 2n
matrix given by

\Psi=\begin{bmatrix}U&-W^{\intercal}\\
W&V\end{bmatrix}\,,

(27)

with the matrices of each block given by

\displaystyle U_{ab}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbb{k}_{a}\dotproduct\mathbb{k}_{b}}{u_{ab}},&a\neq b\\
0,&a=b\end{cases},\qquad V_{ab}=\begin{cases}\frac{\bbespilon_{a}\dotproduct\bbespilon_{b}}{u_{ab}},&a\neq b\\
0,&a=b\end{cases},\qquad W_{ab}=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbb{k}_{a}\dotproduct\bbespilon_{b}}{u_{ab}},&a\neq b\\
-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c=1\\
c\neq a\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\bbespilon_{a}\dotproduct\mathbb{k}_{c}}{u_{ac}},&a=b\end{cases}.

(28)

On the other hand, the matrix
A
is given by its components

A_{IJ}=\begin{cases}\frac{k_{I}\cdot k_{J}}{u_{IJ}},&I\neq J\\
0,&I=J\end{cases},

(29)

where
I,J\in\{1,2,\dots,n,1^{\prime},2^{\prime},\dots,n^{\prime}\}
. We choose to order the rows and columns by grouping all un-primed and primed labels together, i.e.
(1,2,\dots,n,1^{\prime},2^{\prime},\ldots,n^{\prime})
. Note that this is different from that of the Parke-Taylor factor. Of course, the choice can only affect the result in an overall sign.

The choice of ordering induces the following block structure,

A_{2n}=\begin{bmatrix}{\widehat{A}}_{n}&-{\widehat{C}}^{\intercal}_{n}\\
{\widehat{C}}_{n}&{\widehat{B}}_{n}\end{bmatrix},

(30)

where

{\widehat{A}}_{n}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{1}\cdot k_{n}}{u_{1}-u_{n}}\\
\frac{k_{2}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{2}-u_{1}}&0&\cdots&\frac{k_{2}\cdot k_{n}}{u_{2}-u_{n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{n}-u_{1}}&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{n}-u_{2}}&\cdots&0\\

\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{1^{\prime}}}{u_{n1}}&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{2^{\prime}}}{u_{n2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{m^{\prime}}}{u_{nm}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}\bm{\mathsf{C}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\hat{s}_{1^{\prime}1}}{x_{1}}&\frac{k_{1^{\prime}}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{12}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{1^{\prime}}\cdot k_{m}}{u_{1m}}\\
\frac{k_{2^{\prime}}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{21}}&\frac{\hat{s}_{2^{\prime}2}}{x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{2^{\prime}}\cdot k_{m}}{u_{2m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{k_{m^{\prime}}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{m1}}&\frac{k_{m^{\prime}}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{m2}}&\cdots&\frac{\hat{s}_{m^{\prime}m}}{x_{m}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\\
\bm{\mathsf{D}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{k_{m+1}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{(m+1)1}}&\frac{k_{m+1}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{(m+1)2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{m+1}\cdot k_{m}}{u_{(m+1)m}}\\
\frac{k_{m+2}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{(m+2)1}}&\frac{k_{m+2}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{(m+2)2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{m+2}\cdot k_{m}}{u_{(m+2)m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{1}}{u_{n1}}&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{2}}{u_{n2}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{m}}{u_{nm}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\\
\bm{\mathsf{F}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{k_{m+1}\cdot k_{m+2}}{u_{(m+1)(m+2)}}&\cdots&\frac{k_{m+1}\cdot k_{n}}{u_{(m+1)n}}\\
\frac{k_{m+2}\cdot k_{m+1}}{u_{(m+2)(m+1)}}&0&\cdots&\frac{k_{m+2}\cdot k_{n}}{u_{(m+2)n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{m+1}}{u_{n(m+1)}}&\frac{k_{n}\cdot k_{m+2}}{u_{n(m+2)}}&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(65)

Just as in section
2.1.1
, we perform column and row operations
\col{i}\to\col{i}+\col{i^{\prime}}
and
\row{i}\to\row{i}+\row{i^{\prime}}
, for each
1\leq i\leq m
. After this, we additionally perform column and row swaps
\col{i^{\prime}}\leftrightarrow\col{a}
and
\row{i^{\prime}}\leftrightarrow\row{a}
, i.e. swapping the last two block columns and rows. With the proper choice of momenta and polarizations
\mathbb{k}_{i}:=k_{i}+k_{i^{\prime}}
,
\mathbb{k}_{a}:=k_{a}
and
\bbespilon_{i}:=k_{i^{\prime}}
, this procedure produces the matrix

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{final}}_{(n+m)\times(n+m)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathsf{A}}+\bm{\mathsf{C}}-\bm{\mathsf{C}}^{\intercal}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}&&&-\bm{\mathsf{D}}^{\intercal}-\bm{\mathsf{E}}^{\intercal}&&&-\bm{\mathsf{C}}^{\intercal}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}\\
\bm{\mathsf{D}}+\bm{\mathsf{E}}&&&\bm{\mathsf{F}}&&&\bm{\mathsf{E}}\\
\bm{\mathsf{C}}+\bm{\mathsf{B}}&&&-\bm{\mathsf{E}}^{\intercal}&&&\bm{\mathsf{B}}\end{bmatrix}

(66)

\displaystyle\!:=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathsf{G}}_{m\times m}&-\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{m\times(n-m)}^{\intercal}&-\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{m\times m}^{\intercal}\\
\bm{\mathsf{I}}_{(n-m)\times m}&\bm{\mathsf{H}}_{(n-m)\times(n-m)}&-\bm{\mathsf{K}}_{(n-m)\times m}^{\intercal}\\
\bm{\mathsf{J}}_{m\times m}&\bm{\mathsf{K}}_{m\times(n-m)}&\bm{\mathsf{L}}_{m\times m}\end{bmatrix},

(67)

where now the matrices are

\begin{split}\bm{\mathsf{G}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{12}}&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{1m}}\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{21}}&0&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{2m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{m1}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{m2}}&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix},\\
\bm{\mathsf{H}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+2}}{u_{(m+1)(m+2)}}&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{n}}{u_{(m+1)n}}\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+1}}{u_{(m+2)(m+1)}}&0&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{n}}{u_{(m+2)n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+1}}{u_{n(m+1)}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+2}}{u_{n(m+2)}}&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}\,,\\
\bm{\mathsf{K}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+1}}{u_{1(m+1)}}&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+2}}{u_{1(m+2)}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{n}}{u_{1n}}\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+1}}{u_{1(m+1)}}&\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+2}}{u_{2(m+2)}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{n}}{u_{2n}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m+1}}{u_{m(m+1)}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m}\cdot\bbespilon_{m+2}}{u_{m(m+2)}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{n}}{u_{mn}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\end{split}\qquad\begin{split}\bm{\mathsf{I}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{(m+1)1}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{(m+1)2}}&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{(m+1)m}}\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{(m+2)1}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{(m+2)2}}&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{m+2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{(m+2)m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\mathbb{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{n1}}&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{n2}}&\cdots&\frac{\mathbb{k}_{n}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{nm}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\\
\bm{\mathsf{J}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\hat{s}_{1^{\prime}1}}{x_{1}}&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{12}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{1m}}\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{21}}&\frac{\hat{s}_{2^{\prime}2}}{x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{m}}{u_{2m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{1}}{u_{m1}}&\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\mathbb{k}_{2}}{u_{m2}}&\cdots&\frac{\hat{s}_{m^{\prime}m}}{x_{m}}\\
\end{bmatrix},\\
\bm{\mathsf{L}}&=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\bbespilon_{2}}{u_{12}}&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{1}\cdot\bbespilon_{m}}{u_{1m}}\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\bbespilon_{1}}{u_{21}}&0&\cdots&\frac{\bbespilon_{2}\cdot\bbespilon_{m}}{u_{2m}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\bbespilon_{1}}{u_{m1}}&\frac{\bbespilon_{m}\cdot\bbespilon_{2}}{u_{m2}}&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.\end{split}

(68)

The kinematic structure of the matrix
A^{\mathrm{final}}
is very telling and is urging us to define

\widehat{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathsf{G}}&-\bm{\mathsf{I}}^{\intercal}\\
\bm{\mathsf{I}}&\bm{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\widehat{B}:=\bm{\mathsf{L}}\,,\quad\widehat{C}:=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathsf{J}}&\bm{\mathsf{K}}\end{bmatrix},

(69)

so that

\tilde{\Psi}:=A^{\mathrm{final}}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{A}&-\widehat{C}^{\intercal}\\
\widehat{C}&\widehat{B}\end{bmatrix}.

(70)

After imposing the scattering equations in the form of eq. (
39
), the matrix
\tilde{\Psi}
is precisely the matrix appearing in the upper
(n+m)\times(n+m)
block in eq. (
8
), i.e. what we called
\widehat{\Psi}_{2n}
. The matrix
\widehat{A}
encodes the Mandelstam invariants of the particles,
\widehat{B}
encodes the polarisations of collinearized particles, and
\widehat{C}
encodes the interaction between momenta and polarisations.

Since
A^{\mathrm{final}}
and
A_{(n+m)\times(n+m)}
are related by column and row operations, we have that
\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Psi})=\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}(A)
to leading order in
\tau
, showing that

\displaystyle A_{n+m}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}(1,1^{\prime},\dots,m,m^{\prime},m+1,\dots,n)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{s_{ii^{\prime}}}\right)\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\biggr(\prod\limits_{I=4}^{n}\differential u_{I}\,\delta({\symAMSb E}_{I})\biggr)\,\mathrm{PT}(1,\dots,n)\operatorname{Pf}(N)\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Psi})+\dots

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{s_{ii^{\prime}}}A_{n}^{\mathrm{YMS}}(1,\dots,n)+\dots\,,

(71)

where it is understood that particles
1
to
m
are gluons, and particles
m+1
to
n
are scalars. The last equality follows from the formula for the compactification of Yang-Mills and the Pfaffian of a block matrix – see the discussion surrounding eq. (
8
). The case
m=n
reproduces the scaffolding residue studied in section
2
.

4.2
Color-dressed amplitudes

A logical follow-up is to consider the multi-collinear limit of full color-dressed amplitudes and derive the analogue of eq. (
71
).

The full color-dressed amplitude of Yang-Mills with gauge group
U(N)
is built of out color-ordered amplitudes in the following way
[
21
]
:

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n}=\sum_{\omega\,\in\,S_{n}/{\symAMSb Z}_{n}}\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_{\omega(1)}}\cdots T^{\dsa_{\omega(n)}} \Big)A^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n}(\omega)\,,

(72)

where
T{a}
are a fixed choice of generators of
U(N)
and
A_{n}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}(\omega)
is the non-canonically-ordered partial amplitude

A_{n}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathrm{SL}(2,{\symAMSb C}))}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{d}u_{i}\,\mathrm{PT}(\omega(1,\dots,n))\,\operatorname{Pf}{X}\operatorname{Pf}^{\prime}{A}\,,

(73)

which is a generalisation to eq. (
9
). Eq. (
72
) is known as a color decomposition of the amplitude. We note that the l.h.s. depends on, for each particle: the momentum
k_{i}
; the (possibly vanishing) polarisation vector
\epsilon_{i}
; and the color
\mathsl{a}_{i}
, which must take values between
1
and
N^{2}
.

We again consider the (color-dressed) amplitude of
n+m
particles using a labelling tailored to the limit
(1,1^{\prime},\dots,m,m^{\prime},m+1,\dots,n)
. Under the multi-collinear limit
s_{ii^{\prime}}\to 0
parametrised by
\tau
, with
1\leq i\leq m
, the most singular term of eq. (
72
) contains the terms of the sum where ordering
\omega\in S_{n+m}/{\symAMSb Z}_{n+m}
leaves the primed and unprimed particles adjacent. Any other ordering is sub-leading in
\tau
because the Parke-Taylor factor in (
73
) will not contribute enough factors of
\tau
. In light of this, the permutations in the most singular term are really
\omega\in S_{n}/{\symAMSb Z}_{n}
, acting on elements of the set
\{(1,1^{\prime}),\dots,(m,m^{\prime}),m+1,\dots,n\}
, with a possible transposition among the
m
pairs, for a total of
2^{m}(n-1)!
terms.

We would like to show that in the multi-collinear limit, the color-dressed amplitude has the pole expansion

\mathcal{A}_{n+m}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{s_{ii^{\prime}}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{jj^{\prime}j^{\star}}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\mathrm{YMS}}\right)+\dots\,,

(74)

where

f_{jj^{\prime}j^{\star}}:=f_{\mathsl{a}_{j}\mathsl{a}_{j^{\prime}}\mathsl{a}_{j^{\star}}}\,,

(75)

are structure constants of
\mathfrak{u}(N)
and

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YMS}}_{n}=\sum_{\omega\,\in\,S_{n}/{\symAMSb Z}_{n}}\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_{\omega(1^\star)}}\cdots T^{\dsa_{\omega(m^\star)}}T^{\dsa_{\omega(m+1)}}\cdots T^{\dsa_{\omega(n)}} \Big)A^{\mathrm{YMS}}_{n}(\omega)\,.

(76)

Let us take a moment to explain the notation. On the l.h.s. of eq. (
74
), particles
\{1,1^{\prime},\dots,m,m^{\prime},m+1,\dots,n\}
are scattered. After the multi-collinear limit, we are left with particles
\{1^{\star},\dots m^{\star},m+1,\dots,n\}
, where particles
j,j^{\prime}
have fused to give
j^{\star}
. Previously, we employed an abuse of notation by dropping the stars, but now we simply cannot, as the color structure on the r.h.s. of eq. (
72
) depends on the color of particles
j
and
j^{\prime}
, and the newly fused
j^{\star}
. Furthermore, it is understood that all combinations of colors of particles
j^{\star}
for
1\leq j\leq m
are summed over. To perform this sum,
a_{j^{\star}}
are Einstein summation indices, running from
1
to
N^{2}
. Let us emphasize that
a_{j^{\star}}
for
1\leq j\leq m
are summation indices, while
a_{j}
and
a_{j^{\prime}}
for
1\leq j\leq n
are color data provided for the particles.

Example:
m=n=3
.
To elucidate this notation and formula let us consider the scaffolding residue of the color-dressed amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{3+3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}
. The sum over permutations in (
72
) yields
2^{3}(3-1)!=16
terms, which can be grouped and simplified using the linearity of the trace to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{3+3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}=f_{11^{\prime}1^{\star}}f_{22^{\prime}2^{\star}}f_{33^{\prime}3^{\star}}\times\Bigg(&\Tr(T^{\dsa_{1^\star}}T^{\dsa_{2^\star}}T^{\dsa_{3^\star}})A_{3+3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}(11^{\prime}22^{\prime}33^{\prime})\\
&+\Tr(T^{\dsa_{1^\star}}T^{\dsa_{3^\star}}T^{\dsa_{2^\star}})A_{3+3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}(11^{\prime}33^{\prime}22^{\prime})\Bigg)+\dots\,.\end{split}

(77)

The scaffolding limit
s_{ii^{\prime}}\to 0
passes through the structure constants and traces to the kinematic partial amplitudes. Although we didn’t explicitly study the scaffolding residue of a non-canonically-ordered partial amplitude, it trivially generalises in the way one expects, that the
i,i^{\prime}
particles fuse together and we get a pole at the on-shell momenta. Thus, the bracketed term of (
77
) is precisely the color-dressed amplitude
\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}
of three particles, summed over all possible combinations of colors of the particles (multiplied by the corresponding color structure constants), i.e.

\mathcal{A}_{3+3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{s_{ii^{\prime}}}\right)f_{11^{\prime}1^{\star}}f_{22^{\prime}2^{\star}}f_{33^{\prime}3^{\star}}\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\mathrm{YMS}}\,.

(78)

Proof of eq. (
74
).
We make the observation that the multi-collinear limit can be taken sequentially, i.e. it is equivalent to first taking
s_{11^{\prime}}\to 0
, then
s_{22^{\prime}}\to 0
, and so on until
s_{mm^{\prime}}\to 0
. The order in which the limits are taken can be permuted. Without loss of generality, it is sufficient to show the single collinear limit
s_{11^{\prime}}\to 0
produces the correct pole for a color-dressed amplitude.

For simplicity, let the group of circular permutations on
n
elements be denoted
P_{n}:=S_{n}/{\symAMSb Z}_{n}
. To avoid writing long products of permuted generators we denote

T_{\omega}(1,\dots,n):=T^{\mathsl{a}_{\omega(1)}}\cdots T^{\mathsl{a}_{\omega(n)}}\,.

(79)

We begin with the color-dressed amplitude of
n+1
particles labelled
(1,1^{\prime},2,\dots,n)
:

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n+1}=\sum_{\omega\,\in\,P_{n+1}}\Tr\Big(T_\omega(1,1',2\dots,n) \Big)A^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n}(\omega)\,.

(80)

Recall that only permutations which keep the
1,1^{\prime}
particles adjacent contribute to the most singular term in the collinear limit. Keeping only these terms, we can also equivalently write the sum over circular permutations on the remaining terms as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}=\sum_{\sigma\,\in\,S_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_1}T^{\dsa_{1'}}T_\sigma(2,\dots,n)\Big)A^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n+1}(1,1^{\prime},\sigma)

\displaystyle-\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_{1'}}T^{\dsa_{1}}T_\sigma(2,\dots,n)\Big)A^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}_{n+1}(1,1^{\prime},\sigma)+\dots\,,

(81)

the negative sign arising from the Parke-Taylor factor in the partial amplitude. We can collect the two terms together to get

\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}=f_{11^{\prime}1^{\star}}\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\,\in\,S_{n-1}}\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_{1^\star}}T_\sigma(2,\dots,n)\Big)A_{n+1}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}(1,1^{\prime},\sigma)+\dots\,.

(82)

Now taking the collinear limit
s_{11^{\prime}}\to 0
, which passes through the color structure, we have

\mathcal{A}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{YMS:scalar}}=\frac{1}{s_{11^{\prime}}}f_{11^{\prime}1^{\star}}\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\,\in\,S_{n-1}}\Tr\Big(T^{\dsa_{1^\star}}T_\sigma(2,\dots,n)\Big)A_{n+1}^{\mathrm{YMS}}(1^{\star},\sigma)+\dots=\frac{1}{s_{11^{\prime}}}f_{11^{\prime}1^{\star}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{YMS}}_{n}+\dots\,.

(83)

Eq. (
74
) now follows from taking additional collinear limits on other particles
i,i^{\prime}
, one at a time.

5
Discussions

In this Winter School Project we revisited the computation of collinear limits of various amplitudes using their CHY formulation. The main case we considered was the so-called “scaffolding” residues of amplitude of scalars in the Yang-Mills-Scalar (YMS) theory. While the scaffolding residue was introduced and studied in ref.
[
1
]
, here we computed it using the corresponding CHY formulations. At first sight, obtaining an
n
-gluon amplitude CHY formula from that of a
2n
-scalar amplitude in YMS seems to be a daunting task. The reason is that the scalar theory is built using a very simple CHY integrand, the pfaffian of
A
, while that of gluons uses one of the most complicated, the pfaffian of
\Psi
. Not only were we able to show how one naturally emerges from the other, but we showed how one of the most mysterious features of the
\Psi
matrix, the
C_{ii}
elements

C_{ii}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\epsilon_{i}\cdot k_{j}}{u_{ij}}\,,

(84)

arises from the scattering equations.

Some next steps include understanding how even more complicated matrices in the CHY formulation can arise from simpler ones by similar scaffolding procedure. For example, the matrix
\Pi
in the CHY formula of Einstein-Yang-Mills amplitude has a very rich structure and it would be fascinating to see it emerge from a simple matrix.

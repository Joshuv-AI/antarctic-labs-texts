Non-vanishing of Dirichlet L L -functions with smooth conductors
Sun-Kai Leung

Abstract
. Given a large, square-free, smooth conductor, we establish the non-vanishing of the central values for at least 35.9 % 35.9\% of the primitive Dirichlet L L -functions.

1.
Introduction

It is widely believed that all primitive Dirichlet
L
-functions do not vanish at the central point, i.e., for any primitive Dirichlet character
\chi,
we have
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\neq 0.
This was first conjectured by Chowla
[
Cho65
]
for primitive real characters. However, establishing such non-vanishing for all characters appears to be out of reach at the moment.

Given a large, square-free, smooth conductor, we prove in this paper that more than
35.9\%
of the primitive Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
do not vanish at the central point
s=\frac{1}{2}
. In particular, this is the first instance where the proportion surpasses

35\%
for some explicit, positive fraction of moduli.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist positive real numbers
Q,\eta
such that for any square-free integer
q>Q
which is
q^{\eta}
-smooth, i.e., without prime factors exceeding
q^{\eta}
, we have
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\neq 0
for at least
35.9\%
of the primitive characters modulo
q.

A positive proportion of the non-vanishing of Dirichlet
L
-functions was first established in the seminal work of Balasubramanian and Murty
[
BM92
]
, and later improved to
1/3
by Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
IS99
]
. Michel and VanderKam
[
MV00
]
also obtained the same proportion
1/3
by introducing a symmetric
two-piece mollifier
. More recently, Bui
[
Bui12
]
reached
34.11\%
using a three-piece mollifier. In fact, assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis, a proportion of one-half is attainable.

Restricting to prime moduli, Khan, Milićević and Ngo
[
KMN22
]
achieved a proportion
5/13\approx 38.46\%
by unbalancing the two-piece mollifier. This improved upon the previous work
[
KN16
]
, which applies the theory of trace functions (see
[
Fou+19
]
for an exposition). Interestingly, assuming the existence of a strongly exceptional character modulo
D
, Čech and Matomäki
[
ČM24
]
showed that for any prime
p\in[D^{300},D^{O(1)}],
we have
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\neq 0
for almost all Dirichlet characters
\chi
modulo
p.

Regarding smooth moduli, there have been many recent applications of the
q
-van der Corput method
, first introduced by Heath-Brown
[
Hea78
]
(see also
[
GR90
]
). The most exciting application is, undoubtedly, Zhang’s breakthrough
[
Zha14
]
on the level of distribution of primes in progressions to smooth moduli, which leads to bounded gaps between primes (see also
[
Pol14
]
and
[
Sta23
]
). On the other hand, Irving
[
Irv15
]
and Xi
[
Xi18
]
enhanced the level the distribution of the divisor functions
\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
in progressions to smooth moduli, respectively. Likewise, Irving
[
Irv16
]
improved the subconvexity bound for Dirichlet
L
-functions with smooth conductors. Eventually, Wu and Xi
[
WX21
]
developed a theory of
arithmetic exponent pairs
, further refining both of the aforementioned results of Irving.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we apply the standard Cauchy–Schwarz argument using a not necessarily balanced or symmetric two-piece mollifier, which boils down to the evaluation of the mollified first and second moments (see Section
2
for details).

Notation.
We use the standard big
O
and little
o
notations as well as the Vinogradov notation
\ll,
where the implied constants depend only on the subscripted parameters. We denote
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}.

We write
\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}^{*}
for a sum over primitive characters modulo
q,
and
\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}^{+}
for a sum over even primitive characters modulo
q.
We also denote by
\varphi^{*}(q)
and
\varphi^{+}(q)
the number of primitive characters and even primitive characters, respectively.

2.
Unbalanced two-piece mollifier

Throughout the paper, given a sufficiently small
\eta>0,
let
q
be a large, square-free,
q^{\eta}
-smooth integer. Then, as usual, we first mollify the central values
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)
for all primitive characters modulo
q
using the Dirichlet polynomials
M(s,\chi)\approx L(s,\chi)^{-1},
so that the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality can be applied efficiently and yields

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\geqslant\dfrac{\left|\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}^{*}L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)M(\chi)\right|^{2}}{\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sum_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}^{*}|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)M(\chi)|^{2}},

where we write
M(\chi)
for
M(\frac{1}{2},\chi)
to simplify notation.

In this paper, we utilize the unbalanced or asymmetric two-piece mollifier introduced by Khan, Milićević and Ngo
[
KMN22
, p. 1605]
of the shape

(2.2)

\displaystyle M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2}):=\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}\sum_{m_{1}\leqslant q^{\theta_{1}-\epsilon}}\frac{y_{m_{1}}\chi(m_{1})}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}\times\frac{\overline{\tau(\chi)}}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{m_{2}\leqslant q^{\theta_{2}-\epsilon}}\frac{y_{m_{2}}\overline{\chi(m_{2})}}{\sqrt{m_{2}}}

for some
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1),
where we denote the dampened Möbius function by

\displaystyle y_{m_{i}}:=\mu(m_{i})\left(1-\frac{\log m_{i}}{\log q^{\theta_{i}-\epsilon}}\right)

for
i=1,2,
and the Gauss sum by

\displaystyle\tau(\chi):=\sum_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\chi(a)e\left(\frac{a}{q}\right).

For convenience, we denote the root number by
\epsilon_{\chi}:=\tau(\chi)/\sqrt{q}.
Note that
|\epsilon_{\chi}|=1.

Remark 2.1
.

Such a twisted mollifier is suggested by the approximate functional equation at the central point

\displaystyle L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)\approx\sum_{n_{1}\leqslant N_{1}}\frac{\chi(n_{1})}{\sqrt{n_{1}}}+\overline{\epsilon_{\chi}}\sum_{n_{2}\leqslant N_{2}}\frac{\overline{\chi(n_{1})}}{\sqrt{n_{1}}}

with
N_{1}N_{2}=q
(see
[
IK04
, Theorem 5.3]
for instance), so that

\displaystyle L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)^{-1}\approx\sum_{n_{1}\leqslant N_{1}}\frac{\mu(n_{1})\chi(n_{1})}{\sqrt{n_{1}}}+\overline{\epsilon_{\chi}}\sum_{n_{2}\leqslant N_{2}}\frac{\mu(n_{2})\overline{\chi(n_{1})}}{\sqrt{n_{1}}}.

To evaluate the mollified first and second moments in (
2.1
), even and odd primitive characters have to be treated separately. By symmetry, we only consider the even primitive characters
\chi
here, i.e.,
\chi(-1)=1.
Note that
\varphi^{+}(q)=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{*}(q)+O(1).

Proposition 2.1

(Mollified first moment)
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1].
Then as
q\to\infty,
we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(1+o(1))\varphi^{+}(q).

To state the next proposition on the second moment, we first recall the definition of
arithmetic exponent pairs
(see
[
WX21
]
for further details).

Definition 2.1

(Arithmetic exponent pair
[
WX21
, Definition 3.3]
)
.

Given a sufficiently small
\eta>0,
let
q
be a large, square-free,
q^{\eta}
-smooth number. Also, let
K_{q}
be an
\infty
-amiable trace function modulo
q,
1
1

1

See
[
WX21
, Section 2]
for the definition.
and
W_{\delta}
be a
\delta
-periodic function. Then we call the triple
(\kappa,\lambda,\nu)
an
arithmetic exponent pair
if

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(K,W;I):=\sum_{n\in I}K_{q}(n)W_{\delta}(n)\ll_{\mathfrak{c},\epsilon,\eta}q^{\epsilon}\|W_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\left(\frac{q}{|I|}\right)^{\kappa}|I|^{\lambda}\delta^{v},

provided that
|I|<q\delta
.

Proposition 2.2

(Mollified second moment)
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1/2]
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}<\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}<\frac{1-\kappa}{1-\kappa+\lambda}

for some arithmetic exponent pair
(\kappa,\lambda,\nu)
with
0\leqslant\nu-\kappa<1.

Then as
q\to\infty,
we have

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left|L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\right|^{2}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}+o(1)\right)\varphi^{+}(q).

The essence of our work lies in the mollified second moment, in which an application of the Poisson summation formula transforms the error term into a bilinear form of Kloosterman sums, allowing the
q
-van der Corput AB-processes to be executed more efficiently. Otherwise, one might not be able to surpass Bui’s record of
34.11\%
, which holds for general moduli (see Section
3
for details).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
.

Applying Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
(similarly for odd primitive characters) to (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi^{*}(q)}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\geqslant(1-o(1))\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}{1+\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}

for those
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in(0,1/2]^{2}
satisfying
\frac{1}{2}<\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}<\frac{1-\kappa}{1-\kappa+\lambda}
.
Then, the theorem follows by taking the arithmetic exponent pair
BABA^{2}BA^{3}B\left(0,1,0\right)=\left(\frac{52}{243},\frac{50}{81},\frac{202}{243}\right),

where

\displaystyle A(\kappa,\lambda,\nu):=\left(\frac{\kappa}{2(\kappa+1)},\frac{\kappa+\lambda+1}{2(\kappa+1)},\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+1}\right)

is the A-process and

\displaystyle B(\kappa,\lambda,\nu):=\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2},\kappa+\frac{1}{2},\lambda-\kappa+\nu\right)

is the B-process of the
q
-van der Corput method (see
[
WX21
, Section 3]
).
∎

Therefore, it remains to prove Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, especially the latter.

3.
Mollified moments

We begin with the decomposition

(3.1)

\displaystyle M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})+\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2}),

where

\displaystyle M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1}):=\sum_{m_{1}\leqslant q^{\theta_{1}-\epsilon}}\frac{y_{m_{1}}\chi(m_{1})}{\sqrt{m_{1}}}

and

\displaystyle M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2}):=\overline{\epsilon_{\chi}}\sum_{m_{2}\leqslant q^{\theta_{2}-\epsilon}}\frac{y_{m_{2}}\overline{\chi(m_{2})}}{\sqrt{m_{2}}}.

The computation of the mollified first moment is now considered standard.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Using the decomposition (
3.1
), it boils down to

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})

and

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2}).

In fact, both the first and the second sum are
(1+o(1))\varphi^{+}(q)
(see
[
IS99
, p. 947]
and
[
MV00
, Section 3]
, respectively). Therefore, the proposition follows.
∎

To compute the mollified second moment, we first isolate the main term.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1/2].
Then

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left|L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\right|^{2}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}+o(1)\right)\varphi^{+}(q)+\Sigma^{\flat}_{IS,MV},

where
\Sigma^{\flat}_{IS,MV}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Re}\frac{2}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=cd\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}\varphi(c)\underset{m_{1}m_{2}n_{1}\neq 1}{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\leqslant q^{\theta_{1}-\epsilon}\\
m_{2}\leqslant q^{\theta_{2}-\epsilon}\\
(m_{1}m_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\geqslant 1\\
n_{2}\geqslant 1\\
(n_{1}n_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}}\frac{y_{m_{1}}y_{m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}V\left(\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{q}\right)e\left(\frac{\overline{dm_{1}m_{2}n_{1}}n_{2}}{c}\right)

for some fixed nonnegative function
V(x)\ll_{A}(1+x)^{-A}.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
Pra19
, Section 4]
. For the sake of completeness, we sketch a proof here. Using the decomposition (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle|M(\chi;\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|^{2}=\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}|M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})|^{2}+\left(\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}\right)^{2}|M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}{(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})^{2}}\mathrm{Re}(M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})\overline{M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2})}).

Arguing as in
[

\displaystyle\Sigma_{IS}:=\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left|L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})\right|^{2}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}+o(1)\right)\varphi^{+}(q).

Since

we also have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{MV}:=\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left|L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2})\right|^{2}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta_{2}}+o(1)\right)\varphi^{+}(q).

Therefore, it remains to evaluate

\displaystyle\Sigma_{IS,MV}:=\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\left|L\Bigl(\frac{1}{2},\chi\Bigr)\right|^{2}M_{IS}(\chi;\theta_{1})\overline{M_{MV}(\chi;\theta_{2})}.

A standard argument using the smoothed approximate functional equation for

[

\displaystyle\Sigma_{IS,MV}=2\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}\leqslant q^{\theta_{1}-\epsilon}\\
m_{2}\leqslant q^{\theta_{2}-\epsilon}\\
(m_{1}m_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}\frac{y_{m_{1}}y_{m_{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\geqslant 1\\
n_{2}\geqslant 1\\
(n_{1}n_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}V\left(\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{q}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\epsilon_{\chi}\chi(m_{1}m_{2}n_{1})\overline{\chi(n_{2})}

for some fixed nonnegative function
V(x)\ll_{A}(1+x)^{-A}.
By the almost orthogonal relation

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=cd\\
c|m\pm n\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\varphi(c)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\epsilon_{\chi}\chi(m_{1}m_{2}n_{1})\overline{\chi(n_{2})}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=cd\\

by opening the root number

arises from

\displaystyle\Sigma_{IS,MV}^{\sharp}:=2\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}\epsilon_{\chi}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{\overline{\chi(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}V\left(\frac{n}{q}\right).

We also require a general bilinear estimate of Kloosterman sums (trace functions), which is essentially a weighted and also twisted version of
[

Lemma 3.2
.

Given three positive integers
M,N,\delta
and two sequences of complex numbers
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{m})_{M<m\leqslant 2M},\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{n})_{N<n\leqslant 2N},
let
K_{q}
be an
\infty
-amiable trace function modulo
q,
and
W_{\delta}
be a
\delta
-periodic function with
\|W_{\delta}\|_{\infty}\ll_{\epsilon}{\delta}^{\epsilon}.
Suppose
(\kappa,\lambda,\nu)
is an arithmetic exponent pair with
0\leqslant\nu-\kappa<1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};K,W):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M<m\leqslant 2M\\
(m,q)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N<n\leqslant 2N\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m}\beta_{n}K_{q}(\overline{m}n)W_{\delta}(\overline{m}n)

\displaystyle\ll_{\mathfrak{c},\epsilon,\eta}(qM\delta)^{\epsilon}M^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{\alpha}\|_{2}\|\bm{\beta}\|_{2}\left(q^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}N^{-\frac{1-\lambda+\kappa}{2}}\delta^{\frac{\nu}{2}}+q^{-\frac{1}{4}}+M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Our proof builds on a generalization of the classical Pólya–Vinogradov inequality.

Lemma 3.3

(Pólya–Vinogradov inequality
[
WX21

)
.

The triple
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},1)
is an arithmetic exponent pair (see Definition
2.1

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

For readers unfamiliar with the language of trace functions, it suffices, for the proof of Proposition

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{B}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta};K,W)|^{2}\leqslant\|\bm{\beta}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{n}\left|\sum_{m}\alpha_{m}K_{q}(\overline{m}n)W_{\delta}(\overline{m}n)\right|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\bm{\beta}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}|\alpha_{m_{1}}||\alpha_{m_{2}}|\left|\sum_{n}K_{q}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{K_{q}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}W_{\delta}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{W_{\delta}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}\right|.

For

\displaystyle\ll_{\epsilon}(q\delta)^{\epsilon}N\|\bm{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}\|\bm{\beta}\|_{2}^{2}.

\displaystyle K_{q}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{K_{q}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}=|K_{d}(\overline{m_{1}}n)|^{2}K_{q/d}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{K_{q/d}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n}K_{q}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{K_{q}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}W_{\delta}(\overline{m_{1}}n)\overline{W_{\delta}(\overline{m_{2}}n)}\ll_{\mathfrak{c},\epsilon,\eta}(dN\delta)^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{q}{dN}\right)^{\kappa}M^{\lambda}(d\delta)^{\nu},

by the assumption that

Abstract
. In 1985, Golumbic and Scheinerman established an equivalence between comparability graphs and containment graphs—graphs whose vertices represent sets, with edges indicating set containment. A few years earlier, McMorris and Zaslavsky characterized upper bound graphs—those derived from partially ordered sets where two elements share an edge if they have a common upper bound—by a specific edge clique cover condition. In this paper, we introduce a unifying framework for these results using finite point-set topologies. Given a finite topology, we define a graph whose vertices correspond to its elements, with edges determined by intersections of their minimal containing sets, where intersection is understood in terms of the topological separation axioms. This construction yields a natural sequence of graph classes—one for each separation axiom—that connects and extends both classical results in a structured and intuitive way.



1.
Introduction

Given a partially ordered set (poset)
P=(X,\leq)
, one can construct many graphs which have been of interest in extremal set theory and graph theory. Among the most famous is the
comparability graph
of
P
which is the graph whose vertices are
X
and
x,y\in X
are adjacent if
x\leq y
or
y\leq x
. In general, we say a graph
G=(V,E)
is a comparability graph if it arises from some poset
P=(V,\leq)
. There are many graph-theoretic characterizations for comparability graphs including graphs which are
transitively orientable
, that is, there exists an orientation of
G
such that if
x\to y
and
y\to z
, then
x\to z
. This orientation can be easily obtained from the comparability graph of a poset by orienting
x\to y
if
x\leq y
. Comparability graphs are both subclasses and superclasses of other well-known and actively researched graph classes. Subclasses include complete graphs, bipartite graphs, permutation graphs, threshold graphs, and co-graphs (
P_{4}
-free); see
[
5
,
15
]
. Superclasses include perfect graphs and word-representable graphs; see
[
3
,
11
]
.

In what may seem like a different context, a graph
G=(V,E)
is a
\Sigma
-containment graph
if there exists a family of sets
\Sigma
such that for each
v_{i}\in V(G)
, there exists a corresponding
S_{i}\in\Sigma
such that
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
if and only if
S_{i}\subset S_{j}
or
S_{j}\subset S_{i}
. In
[
7
]
, M. Golumbic and E. Scheinerman proved that the two classes of graphs are, in fact, equivalent.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

A graph is a comparability graph if and only if it is a containment graph.

To see one direction of this theorem, for each
S_{i},S_{j}\in\Sigma
with
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
adjacent in
G
, we let
S_{i}\leq S_{j}
if
S_{i}\subset S_{j}
, or, in terms of an orientation we can orient
S_{i}\to S_{j}
. To see the other direction of this characterization, one can construct a set system
\Sigma
by associating for each element
x
of a poset
P=(X,\leq)
, the set
S(x)=\{y:y\leq x\}
.

Another lesser-known graph obtained from a poset
P=(X,\leq)
is the
upper bound graph of
P
. Like the comparability graph of
P
, this graph also has vertex set
X
but
x,y\in X
are adjacent if and only if there exists some
z\in X
with
x\leq z
and
y\leq z
. If
x\leq y
, i.e. the edge
xy
exists in the comparability graph of
P
, then
y
satisfies the conditions for
z
, as
y
is a common upper bound for
x
and
y
. Hence, the edges in an upper bound graph of
P
can be obtained by taking the edges of the comparability graph of
P
and adding edges. This notion of additional edges will be at the heart of our paper.

Once again, in what may seem like a shift in direction, given a graph
G=(V,E)
, we say
v\in V(G)
is a
simplicial vertex
if its closed neighborhood,
N[v]
, induces a clique (a complete subgraph). We say a graph
G=(V,E)
is an
edge-simplicial
graph if there exists a collection of cliques that cover the edges of
G
such that every clique contains a simplicial vertex. Note that this definition of edge-simplicial graphs is equivalent to requiring every vertex to either be a simplicial vertex or to have a neighbor which is a simplicial vertex. These graphs are also equivalent to the class of graphs whose clique edge-cover number equals its clique vertex-cover number. These parameters are defined as the minimum number of cliques in a graph required to cover every edge (vertex). We will discuss edge-simplicial graphs in more detail in
Section

5
. In
[
13
]
, McMorris and Zaslavsky in fact, showed that the class of edge-simplicial graphs are equivalent to upper bound graphs.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

A graph is an upper bound graph if and only if it is an edge-simplicial graph.

The goal of this paper is to unify these classical results and extend them both in terms of graphs generated from a poset and graphs with a clique-like structure (as seen with edge-simplicial graphs). To do this, we will soon define a sequence of six graph classes

\mathcal{G}_{0},\hskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{G}_{1},\hskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{G}_{2},\hskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}},\hskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime\prime}},\text{ and }\hskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{G}_{4}

whose definitions we deliberately hold off on giving until
Section

2
. These graph classes and the framework used to extend the notions of
Theorem

1.1
and
Theorem

1.2
are built using finite topologies, which will require some further definitions. Before giving those, we first extend the notion of comparability graphs and upper bound graphs by introducing three additional new graphs obtained from a poset.

Let
P=(X,\leq)
be a poset. The
half-closed upper bound graph of
P
is the graph whose vertices are
X
and
x,y\in X
are adjacent if and only if there exists
a,b\in X
such that
a\leq x
,
a\leq b
, and
y\leq b

and
there exists
\alpha,\beta\in X
such that
\alpha\leq y
,
\alpha\leq\beta
, and
x\leq\beta
. In words, to be adjacent, two elements of a poset do not necessarily need to have a common upper bound anymore, but rather they each need a lower bound (
a
, or
\alpha
) which together with
x
or
y
have a common upper bound (
b
or
\beta
). If we relax the requirement of half-closed upper bound graphs to require either that there exists
a,b\in X
such that
a\leq x
,
a\leq b
, and
y\leq b

or
there exists
\alpha,\beta\in X
such that
\alpha\leq y
,
\alpha\leq\beta
, and
x\leq\beta
, then we obtain the
fully-closed upper bound graph of
P
.
Finally, the
extended-closed upper bound graph
of
P
is the graph whose vertices are
X
and
x,y\in X
are adjacent if and only if there exists
a,b,c\in X
such that
a\leq x
,
b\leq y
,
a\leq c
and
b\leq c
.

Given a poset
P=(X,\leq)
, as we have mentioned, the upper bound graph of
P
can be obtained from the comparability graph of
P
by adding additional edges. Likewise, the half-closed upper bound graph can be obtained from the upper bound graph by adding additional edges, the same process holds going to the fully-closed upper bound graph of
P
and then the extended-closed upper bound graph of
P
. In
Section

4
, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following holds.

(1)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
if and only if
G
is a disjoint union of cliques.

(2)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
if and only if
G
is a comparability graph.

(3)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
if and only if
G
is an upper bound graph.

(4)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}}
if and only if
G
is a half-closed upper bound graph.

(5)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime\prime}}
if and only if
G
is a fully-closed upper bound graph.

(6)

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{4}
if and only if
G
is an extended-closed upper bound graph.

By
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3
, we see that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
and edge-simplicial graphs are equivalent. In
Section

5
, we take a closer look at the graph class
\mathcal{G}_{2}
(edge-simplicial and upper bound graphs) and fully characterize the
critical graphs
of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
. By critical, we mean a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, but the removal of any edge set
S
either disconnects the graph or results in
G-S\notin\mathcal{G}_{2}
. We define
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
as the graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
that are critical. Given a graph
G
,
anchoring a star to

G
is the operation of adding a
K_{1,t}
and joining the vertex in the part of size
1
to a maximal clique of
G
. This leads to the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
or
G
is connected and can be obtained by iteratively anchoring stars to
K_{1}
.

One way to interpret edge-simplicial graphs is as graphs covered by a collection of cliques, each containing a distinguished vertex—a simplicial vertex. This raises the question of whether other graph classes, such as
\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}}
,
\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime\prime}}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{4}
, can also be characterized in terms of clique coverings. We take a step in this direction by establishing an analogous result for
\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}}
. Instead of cliques, we define a collection of subgraphs with a clique-like structure, which we call
universes
(see
Section

6
). The role of simplicial vertices in this setting is replaced by a new type of distinguished vertex, which we call
suns
, as they play a central role in the universe structure. We give the necessary definitions and prove the following theorem in
Section

6
.

Theorem 1.5
.

G\in\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}}
if and only if
G
has an edge covering with universes
\mathcal{U}=U_{1},U_{2},...,U_{t}
where
U_{i}=(s_{i},P_{i},M_{i})
,
P=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}
, and
S=\{s_{1},...,s_{t}\}
and the following hold.

(1)

S\cup P
partition
V(G)
.

(2)

\forall s_{i},s_{j}\in S
with
N(s_{i})\cap N(s_{j})\cap P\not=\emptyset
, then
s_{i}s_{j}\in E(G)
.

2.
Building the graph classes
\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}},\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime\prime}},\text{ and }\mathcal{G}_{4}

We begin with the objects of interest for the remainder of the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

A
(finite) topological space

(X,\tau)
is a pair where
X
is a (finite) ground set and
\tau
is a
(finite) topology
or a family of subsets of
X
such that

(1)

\emptyset,X\in\tau
.

(2)

\tau
is closed under unions.

(3)

\tau
is closed under finite intersections.

If
S\subseteq X
, the collection
\tau_{S}=\{S\cap U:U\in\tau\}
is a topology on
S
, called a
subspace topology
. We say
K\subseteq X
is a
closed set of
\tau
if
X\setminus K\in\tau
. Given a topology
\tau
on
X
our goal is to construct a graph whose vertex set is
X
. Naturally, we want elements of

to be
disconnected
if they are
separated
within the topology
\tau
. The notion of separation has been well studied in topology, from which the following definition arises. For convenience, throughout this paper, when we say

, we mean the relevant statement holds for both
3^{\prime}
and
3^{\prime\prime}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
. Let
\tau
be a topology on
X
and let
x,y\in X
. We say
x
and
y
are
T_{i}
-separated if the following holds.

(1)

T_{0}
-separated:
If there exists a
U\in\tau
with either
x\in U
and
y\notin U
, or
x\notin U
and
y\in U
.

(2)

T_{1}
-separated:
If there exists
U_{x},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in U_{x}
,
y\notin U_{x}
, and
y\in U_{y}
,
x\notin U_{y}
.

(3)

T_{2}
-separated:
If there exists
U_{x},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in U_{x}
,
y\in U_{y}
, and
U_{x}\cap U_{y}=\emptyset
.

(4)

T_{3^{\prime}}
-separated:
If there exists a closed set
J_{x}
of
\tau
and open sets
U_{J},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in J_{x}\subseteq U_{J}
,
y\in U_{y}
, and
U_{J}\cap U_{y}=\emptyset
,
or
if there exists a closed set
K_{y}
of
\tau
and open sets
U_{x},U_{K}\in\tau
with
x\in U_{x}
,
y\in K_{y}\subseteq U_{K}
and
U_{x}\cap U_{K}=\emptyset
.

(5)

T_{3^{\prime\prime}}
-separated:
If there exists a closed set
J_{x}
of
\tau
and open sets
U_{J},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in J_{x}\subseteq U_{J}
,
y\in U_{y}
, and

,

of

,

.

(6)

T_{4}
-separated:
If there exists closed sets
J_{x},K_{y}
of
\tau
and open sets
U_{J},U_{K}\in\tau
with

and

.

Given two vertices

3.
Preliminary lemmas and observations

Before characterizing graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
, we prove several important statements regarding finite topologies and the corresponding graphs constructed by them.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a finite topology
\tau
on
X
and
x,y\in X
, for all
i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
,
x,y
are
T_{i}
-adjacent if and only if they are not
T_{i}
-separated.

Proof.

First we show that if
x,y
are
T_{i}
-separated then
x,y
are not
T_{i}
-adjacent. Suppose
x,y
are
T_{0}
-separated. Then up to symmetry, there exists a
U\in\tau
with
x\in U
and
y\notin U
. But then since
m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq U
, and
y\notin m_{\tau}(x)
, this implies that
m_{\tau}(x)\neq m_{\tau}(y)
. Thus,
x,y
are not
T_{0}
-adjacent.

Next suppose
x,y
are
T_{1}
-separated. Then there exists
U_{x},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in U_{x}
,
y\notin U_{x}
, and
y\in U_{y}
,
x\notin U_{y}
. But since
x\in m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq U_{x}
and
y\in m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq U_{y}
,
m_{\tau}(x)\not\subseteq m_{\tau}(y)
and
m_{\tau}(y)\not\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
. Thus,
x,y
are not
T_{1}
-adjacent.

Suppose
x,y
are
T_{2}
-separated. Then there exists
U_{x},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in U_{x}
,
y\in U_{y}
, and
U_{x}\cap U_{y}=\emptyset
. Since
m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq U_{x}
and
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq U_{y}
,
m_{\tau}(x)\cap m_{\tau}(y)=\emptyset
. Thus,
x
and
y
are not
T_{2}
-adjacent.

Suppose
x,y
are
T_{3^{\prime}}
-separated. Then up to symmetry, there exists a closed set
J_{x}
of
\tau
and open sets
U_{J},U_{y}\in\tau
with
x\in J_{x}\subseteq U_{J}
,
y\in U_{y}
, and

. Since

and

,
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\subseteq U_{J}
and
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq U_{y}
. So
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\cap m_{\tau}(y)=\emptyset
. Thus,
x,y
are not
T_{3^{\prime}}
-adjacent.

Suppose
x,y
are
T_{3^{\prime\prime}}
-separated. Then if there exist a closed set

and open sets

in
\tau
with
x\in J_{x}\subseteq U_{J}
,

, and

,
and

and open sets

of

,

and

.
Since

and
y\in U_{y}
,
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\subseteq U_{J}
and
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq U_{y}
. So
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\cap m_{\tau}(y)=\emptyset
.
And since

and

,
m_{\tau}(\ell(y))\subseteq U_{K}
and
m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq U_{x}
. So

. Thus,

are not

-adjacent.

Suppose

are

-separated. Then there exist closed sets
J_{x},K_{y}
and open sets

of
\tau
with

and

, and

. Since

and

,

and
m_{\tau}(\ell(y))\subseteq U_{K}
. So

G\in\mathcal{G}_{0}

The following observations will be quite useful when handling minimal sets in finite topologies.

Observation 3.3
.

Let
\tau
be a finite topology on
X
and let
x,y,z\in X
. The following hold.

(1)

If
y\in m_{\tau}(x)
then
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
.

(2)

x\in\ell_{\tau}(y)
if and only if
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
.

(3)

If
m_{\tau}(x)\subset m_{\tau}(y)
then
x\notin\ell_{\tau}(y)
and
m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq m_{\tau}(\ell(y))
.

(4)

If
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\cap m_{\tau}(y)\neq\emptyset
, then there exists some
w\in\ell_{\tau}(x)
such that
m_{\tau}(w)\cap m_{\tau}(y)\neq\emptyset
.

(5)

If
x,y\in m_{\tau}(z)
, then
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\cap m_{\tau}(y)\neq\emptyset
and
m_{\tau}(x)\cap m_{\tau}(\ell(y))\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

We first show (1). Suppose for the contradiction that
m_{\tau}(y)\not\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
, then
m_{\tau}(y)\cap m_{\tau}(x)
is an open set containing
y
since
y
is in
m_{\tau}(x)
, which is contained in
m_{\tau}(x)
, and which contradicts to the minimality of
m_{\tau}(y)
.

Next we prove (2). If
x\in\ell_{\tau}(y)
, then by definition,
x\notin\overline{\ell}_{\tau}(y)
. But this means that any set containing
x
must contain
y
. Hence,
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
. If
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(x)
, then
x\notin\overline{\ell}_{\tau}(y)
which implies
x\in\ell_{\tau}(y)
.

Next we prove (3). If
m_{\tau}(x)
is a strict subset of
m_{\tau}(y)
then there exists a set
A\in\tau
with
x\in A
and
y\notin A
. Hence,
x\in\overline{\ell}_{\tau}(y)
which implies
x\notin\ell_{\tau}(y)
. We also have
m_{\tau}(x)\subset m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(\ell(y))
.

Next we prove (4). It suffices to show that
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))=\bigcup_{a\in\ell_{\tau}(x)}m_{\tau}(a)
. Certainly
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\subseteq\bigcup_{a\in\ell_{\tau}(x)}m_{\tau}(a)
so we now have to show that there is no smaller set in
\tau
containing
\ell(x)
. This is true because for any
b\in\bigcup_{a\in\ell_{\tau}(x)}m_{\tau}(a)
,
b\in m_{\tau}(a)\subseteq m_{\tau}(\ell(x))
for some
a\in\ell_{\tau}(x)
and thus,
\bigcup_{a\in\ell_{\tau}(a)}m_{\tau}(a)\subseteq m_{\tau}(\ell(x))
.

Lastly we prove (5). If
x,y\in m_{\tau}(z)
, then by (1),
m_{\tau}(x)\subseteq m_{\tau}(z)
and
m_{\tau}(y)\subseteq m_{\tau}(z)
. But then by (2),
z\in\ell_{\tau}(x)\cap\ell_{\tau}(y)
. Thus,
m_{\tau}(\ell(x))\cap m_{\tau}(y)\neq\emptyset
and
m_{\tau}(x)\cap m_{\tau}(\ell(y))\neq\emptyset
.
∎

5.
A closer look into
\mathcal{G}_{2}

Unlike
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, the graph class
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is not hereditary. A hereditary graph class is one which is closed under taking induced subgraphs. To see that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is not hereditary, consider any graph
H
. We define the
incidence topology on
V(H)\cup E(H)
, denoted
\text{Inc}(H)
, whose minimal base is
M(\text{Inc}(H))=\bigcup_{e\in E(H)}\{e\}\cup\bigcup_{v\in V(H)}\{v,\{e\in E(G):e\text{ is incident to $v$}\}\}
. Note that the graph
G_{2}(\text{Inc}(H))
can be obtained from
G
by duplicating each edge of
E(H)
, and then subdividing one of the parallel edges from each pair. Hence, the edges that are not subdivided induce a copy of
H
.

In
Theorem

1.3
we proved that graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are equivalent to upper bound graphs. Thus, by
Theorem

1.2
, graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are also equivalent to edge-simplicial graphs. In order to understand the connections between the finite topologies and the clique cover guaranteed in edge-simplicial graphs, we present a proof showing directly that graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
have the desired clique cover for edge-simplicial graphs. This will also provide us a template for characterizing graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{3^{\prime}}
in
Section

6
.

Theorem 5.1
.

A graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
if and only if
G
contains a clique cover with the property that every clique contains a simplicial vertex.

Proof.

We first show that if
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
then it has the desired clique cover. Since
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, there exists a topology
\tau
on
V(G)
such that
G\cong G_{2}(\tau)
. By
Lemma

3.4
and
Lemma

3.6
, we may assume
\tau
has height
2
and for any
x,y\in V(G)
,
m_{\tau}(x)\neq m_{\tau}(y)
. Since
\tau
has height
2
, we let
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
be a partition of
V(G)
, into two layers, such that
m_{\tau}(v)=\{v\}
for all
v\in L_{1}
and
m_{\tau}(u)\setminus\{u\}\subseteq L_{1}
for all
u\in L_{2}
.

Claim 5.2
.

For any
v\in L_{1}
,
N[v]
induces a clique.

Proof of Claim.

Let
v\in L_{1}
. By definition
m_{\tau}(x)=\{x\}
for any
x\in L_{1}
and hence
N(v)\subseteq L_{2}
. Let
u\in N(v)\cap L_{2}
. Then the only way for

to be in
N(v)
is if
v\in m_{\tau}(u)
. Since this holds for any
u\in N(v)
, it must be the case that
v\in\bigcap_{u\in N(v)}m_{\tau}(u)
. Thus,
N[v]
induces a clique.
∎

Now consider
\mathcal{C}=N[v_{1}],N[v_{2}],...,N[v_{t}]
where
L_{1}=\{v_{1},...,v_{t}\}
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}
is a clique cover and moreover by the previous claim each
v_{i}
is a simplicial vertex. Since
m_{\tau}(v)=\{v\}
for all
v\in L_{1}
,
L_{1}
forms an independent set and hence we must only check that edges between
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
and edges within

are covered. Clearly edges between
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are covered, as
\mathcal{C}
consists of closed neighborhoods of vertices in
L_{1}
. Next, let
u,w\in L_{2}
with

. If
uw\in E(G)
, then there exists some

. This means

is covered by
N[v]
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}
is a clique cover such that every clique contains a simplicial vertex.

Now suppose
G
is a graph with clique cover
\mathcal{C}=C_{1},...,C_{t}
such that every clique has a simplicial vertex, say
v_{i}
in
C_{i}
. Let
L_{1}=\{v_{1},...,v_{t}\}
and let
L_{2}=V(G)\setminus L_{1}
. We can construct the minimal sets in a topology

by letting
m_{\tau}(v)=\{v\}
for all

and
m_{\tau}(u)=\{u\}\cup\{v\in L_{1}\cap N(u)\}
. It can easily be seen that

is a topology such that
G_{2}(\tau)\cong G
by the same logic as the forward direction.
∎

By

into two layers. Because of this, we can interpret layer one (

L_{2}
) as the edges of some hypergraph. Now, the minimal sets of
\tau
are the vertices themselves and for each edge, it includes the edge along with a set of vertices it is incident to. By translating this into the language of hypergraphs, this means precisely that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is the
middle graph
of a hypergraph. The middle graph of a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
is the graph whose vertex set is
V\cup E
and the edges are between edge-to-edge and edge-to-vertex adjacencies. Borowiecki
[
4
]
proved that a graph is edge-simplicial if and only if it is the middle graph of some hypergraph. Thus, we readily see that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is precisely the class of graphs which are the middle graphs of hypergraphs.

For any graph
G
, the
clique cover number
of
G
is the minimum number of cliques in any clique cover of
G
. In 1972, Karp proved that finding the clique cover number of an arbitrary graph is NP-complete, one of the first problems to be classified as NP-complete
[
10
]
. However, certain classes of graphs have polynomial time algorithms to determine the clique cover number. One such class of graphs are perfect graphs, which in 1981 were proven by Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver to have a polynomial time algorithm to find their clique cover number
[
8
]
. Since comparability graphs were shown to be perfect graphs by Berge
[
1
]
, the clique cover number of graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{1}
can be found in polynomial time. It is easy to see that edge-simplicial graphs (graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
) also have a polynomial time algorithm to determine their clique cover number, see
[
6
]
. It is worth noting that since
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is not hereditary, there are graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
that are not perfect.

5.1.
Clique cover graphs of
\mathcal{G}_{2}

After seeing
Theorem

5.1
, it is natural to wonder what the clique covers characterizing graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
look like. To this end we analyze the

of graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}

denote the graph

[
12

, J. Krausz proved that a graph

has a clique cover such that every vertex is in at most

cliques (see
[
9
]

[

showing that a graph

is a line graph of a hypergraph, with rank at most

, if and only if

has a clique cover such that every vertex is in at most
k
cliques. Using the characterization from

, it will be fairly easy to see that in fact, clique cover graphs of

have exactly the required clique covers mentioned above.

5.2.
Critical graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}

In any graph class
\mathcal{G}
, it is natural to wonder which graphs are
critical
. In this context, by critical we mean that
G\in\mathcal{G}
but
G-S\notin\mathcal{G}
for any
S\subseteq E(G)
. Since removing all the edges of graph in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
yields another graph which is trivially in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, we restrict ourselves to removing non-cut sets. Formally we say a graph
G
is
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-critical
if in
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
and for any set
S\subseteq E(G)
,
G-S
is either disconnected or
G-S\notin\mathcal{G}_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
denote the set of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-critical graphs.

Trees exhibit this “critical” behavior among connected graphs in the sense that the removal of any edge will no longer yield a connected graph. Moreover, trees are graphs which can be built iteratively by adding a vertex and connecting it to an already existing vertex. In a similar manner, we see that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-critical graphs also have tree-like behavior and can be built in an iterative process.

Definition 5.4
.

Given a graph
G
,
anchoring a star to
G
is the operation of adding a
K_{1,t}
, for
t\geq 1
, say with vertex
x
in the part of size
1
, and joining
x
to a maximal clique of
G
.

We restate
Theorem

1.4
for convenience.

Theorem

(
1.4
)
.

G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
or
G
is connected and can be obtained by iteratively anchoring stars to
K_{1}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
K_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
so now we assume
G\not\cong K_{2}
. We prove both directions of this via a series of claims. We first show that such hypothesized graphs are
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-critical.

Claim 5.5
.

If
G
can be obtained by iteratively anchoring stars to a
K_{1}
, then
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
.

Proof of Claim.

To show this, we first show that
G\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
and then show that the removal of any edge set
S
either disconnects
G
or
G-S\notin\mathcal{G}_{2}
. To do this, we prove the stronger statement that if
H
satisfies the following two conditions, (R1) and (R2), then anchoring a star to
H
produces a graph which still satisfies conditions (R1) and (R2). Then since
K_{1}
trivially satisfies (R1) and (R2), we inductively prove our claim. The conditions are as follows.

(R1)

H\in\mathcal{G}_{2}^{*}
and
H\not\cong K_{2}
.

(R2)

Every maximal clique in
H
is in the clique cover of
H
, from
Theorem

5.1
in which every clique contains a simplicial vertex.

Consider a graph
H
satisfying conditions (R1) and (R2). By
Theorem

, there exists a clique cover of

, with maximal cliques, say

be the graph obtained by anchoring a star,

, where

be the maximal clique of

Next we show that

. Hence,

is not an edge-cut, there must exist some

no longer is a clique in

and thus, in any clique covering of

with maximal cliques,

. Let

be a maximal clique in

. But then any vertex in

since it must have both

and

is also not a simplicial vertex in

. Thus, if

.

Now suppose for contradiction there is some edge set

, such

. By the previous paragraph we have that

and since

, either

is disconnected or

. If

is not, then since

is joined to a maximal clique, the only way for this to occur is if

is joined to a

. But this would contradict

satisfying (R1). Now suppose

, there must exist a maximal clique of

which has no simplicial vertex. But again since

is joined to a maximal clique, a non-simplicial vertex cannot become a simplicial vertex in

cannot have a clique cover of maximal cliques such that every clique has a simplicial vertex. This contradicts that

This proves that in fact,

since otherwise

itself would not be
\mathcal{G}_{2}
-critical. Thus,

also satisfies condition (R1) which completes the claim.
∎

To prove the other direction, to suffices to show that for any

, there exists a connected spanning subgraph

which can be obtained by iteratively anchoring stars to

. Thus, if

We find such a connected spanning subgraph of

as follows. Let

. Let

exists. We now use

to find a tree

in

with the following properties (see left of

8
).

(P1)

For any leaf

in

, we let

be a leaf in

where

is the corresponding simplicial vertex in clique

.

(P2)

Each non-leaf vertex

of

can be mapped to one non-leaf vertex

of

such that

is in clique

(P3)

If

Why can we always find a tree

or there exists a

which by definition must neighbor

. Thus, we satisfy (P3) and in fact, can construct

.

We next construct a spanning subgraph

of

as follows (see right of

). We first add back any edges between non-leaf vertices

if they are adjacent in

. Next, for each edge

, moreover, the simplicial vertex of

, say

in not in

. For each such edge we first add the simplicial vertex

where a non-simplicial vertex

of

is in

. Join

, as a leaf, to one of these such

’s. To finish the theorem we prove the following claim.

.

W
is a connected spanning subgraph of

which is either

or can be obtained by iteratively anchoring a stars to

.

Certainly

is a connected spanning subgraph of

. Now let

be a leaf of

. Note that by construction

is also a leaf of

and by definition,

is a simplicial vertex of some clique, say

. Let

be the neighbor of

in

(and also in

). If

is also a leaf then

, and we trivially have our desired claim. Otherwise,

is not a leaf and hence

in

. For every edge

(see the right of

Thus, we see that for each

which is either

or can be constructed via successively anchoring a star to

A closer look into

Theorem 6.1

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{i}

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction there exists

Suppose there exists a component with at least

y^{\prime}
could be

). By

3.3
(
2
),
m_{\tau}(x^{\prime})\subseteq m_{\tau}(z)

. Thus,

implying that

, and

will form a clique in
G_{i}(\tau)
for any

. This contradicts

being bipartite.

Now suppose each component of
G_{2}(\tau)
has size at most

. We claim this cannot occur since if

is connected then

is connected. Suppose not, and let

. Hence,

is an

-walk in

contradicting that

and

are disconnected.
∎

Theorem

(

) Let

where

3.4

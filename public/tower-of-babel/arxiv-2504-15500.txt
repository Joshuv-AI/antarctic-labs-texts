1
Introduction

As is known, Hochschild
[
24
]
introduced relative homological algebra in
categories of modules. Heller, Butler and Horrocks
developed it in more general categories with a relative abelian
structure. After that Auslander and Solberg
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]

applied relative homological algebra to the representation theory of
Artin algebra, they studied relative homology in terms of
subbifunctors of the functor
{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
and developed the general
theory of relative cotilting modules for Artin algebras.

Derived categories were invented by Grothendieck-Verdier
[
43
]

in the early sixties. Today, they have widely been used in many
branches: algebraic geometry, stable homotopy theory, representation
theory, etc. In representation theory of algebras, it is of
interest to investigate whether two algebras have equivalent
derived categories. Rickard
[
39
]
used the theory of tilting complexes to give a condition for when the derived categories of two rings are equivalent.
According to his
theorem, two rings
A
and
B
are derived equivalent if
and only if there is a tilting complex for
A
such that
B
is
isomorphic to the endomorphism algebra of this complex.
Derived categories have been used effectively in relative
homological algebra. The main idea of relative homological algebra
is to replace projective modules by relative projective modules. It
is natural to study the corresponding version of the derived
category in this context. Since then, the relative derived
categories and relative tilting theory of Artin algebras have been
extensively studied. Gao and Zhang
[
17
]
used
Gorenstein homological algebra to get Gorenstein derived categories.
Buan
[
7
]
also studied relative derived categories by
localizing relative quasi-isomorphisms. Using the notion of relative
derived categories, he generalized Happel’s result on derived
equivalences induced by tilting module to the relative setting.
Motivated by Buan’s work, Pan
[
36
]
introduced relative derived equivalences
for Artin algebras by the theory of relative tilting complexes, and some invariance of relative derived equivalences is founded.

The aim of this paper is to discuss the
relationships between relative derived equivalences and relative
Igusa-Todorov dimensions which was defined in
[
33
]
.
To describe the main result, it is convenient to fix some notations.
Let
A
and
B
be Artin algebras. Assume that
F
is a
subbifunctor of
{\rm End\,}^{1}_{A}(-,-)
which is of finite type.
Denote by
{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(A)
the Igusa-Todorov dimensions of
A
. Let
n\geq 0

be an integer. If the complex
X^{\bullet}
has the form:

\cdots\rightarrow X^{-n}\rightarrow X^{-n+1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X^{-1}\rightarrow X^{0}\rightarrow\cdots,

with
X^{i}\neq 0
and the differential being radical map for
-n\leq i\leq 0
, then
n
is called the term length of
X^{\bullet}
, denoted
by
t(X^{\bullet})
.

Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem
(see Theorem
5.7
)
Let
L:{\rm{\mathscr{D}_{F}^{b}}}(A)\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{{\rm b}}\left({B}\right)
be a
relative derived equivalence. Suppose
T^{\bullet}
is the relative
tilting complex associated to
L
. Then we have

{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}_{F}(A)-t(T^{\bullet})\leq{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(B)\leq{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}_{F}(A)+t(T^{\bullet})+2
.

We give the upper and lower bounds of
{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(B)
in the term length of relative tilting complex and

{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}_{F}(A)
. In this theorem,
if
F={\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(-,-)
, then
T^{\bullet}
is a tilting
complex for
A
such that
{\rm End\,}(T^{\bullet})\simeq B
. In the
proof of this theorem, we infer that
{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(A)
and
{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(B)
satisfy a
similar formula but not the same as in this theorem, namely

{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(A)-t(T^{\bullet})\leq{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(B)\leq{\rm\phi\mbox{-}dim\,}(A)+t(T^{\bullet})
. This is the main result in

[
16
, Theorem 4.10]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we consider closed
subbifunctors
F
of
{\rm Ext}_{\mathscr{A}}^{1}(-,-)
, where

\mathscr{A}
is a small abelian category. Then we recall the notion
of the relative derived category of
\mathscr{A}
which was defined
in
[
7
]
. We recall the relative derived equivalences for Artin algebras in Section 3.
In Section 4, we prove the
main result.

Consider additive non-zero subbifunctors
F

{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
. To
each subfunctor corresponds a class of short exact sequences which
are called
F
-exact sequences. The class of
F
-exact sequences is
closed under the operations of pushout, pullback, Baer sums and
direct sums (see
[
2
]
or
[
13
]
). Given a subbifunctor

F
of
{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
, we say that an exact sequence

\eta:0\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow 0

in
\mathscr{A}
is an
F
-exact
sequence if
\eta
is in
F(Z,X)
. If
0\rightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}Z\rightarrow 0
is an
F
-exact sequence, then
f
is
called an
F
-monomorphism and
g
is called an
F
-epimorphism. An
object
P
is said to be
F
-projective if for each
F
-exact
sequence
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow 0
, the sequence

0\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(P,X)\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(P,Y)\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(P,Z)\rightarrow 0

is exact. An object
I
is called
F
-injective if for each

F
-exact sequence
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow 0
, the sequence

0\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(X,I)\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(Y,I)\rightarrow\mathscr{A}(Z,I)\rightarrow 0

is
exact. The subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
consisting of all

F
-projective (resp.
F
-injective) modules is denoted by

\mathcal{P}(F)
(resp.
\mathcal{I}(F)
).

We have the following characterization of when subbifunctors of

{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
have enough projectives or injectives.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 1.12]
Let
F
be a subbifunctor of
{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
.

(1)

F
has enough projectives if and only if
F=F_{\mathcal{P}(F)}
and
\mathcal{P}(F)
is contravariantly finite in

A
-mod.

(2)

F
has enough injectives if and only if
F=F^{\mathcal{I}(F)}
and
\mathcal{I}(F)
is covariantly finite in

A
-mod.

(3)
If there is a finite number of indecomposable relative
projectives (injectives) up to isomorphism, then there is also a
finite number of relative injectives (projectives), and these
numbers are the same.

Recall from
[
7
,
13
]
that an additive subbifunctor
F
is
said to be closed if the following equivalence statements hold.

1) The composition of
F
-epimorphisms is an
F
-epimorphism.

2) The composition of
F
-monomorphisms is an
F
-monomorphism.

3) For each object
X
the functor
F(X,-)
is half exact on

F
-exact sequences.

4) For each object
X
the functor
F(-,X)
is half exact on

F
-exact sequences.

5) The category
\mathscr{A}
with respect to the
F
-exact
sequences is an exact category.

We will give some basic examples of closed subbifunctors. Let

\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
and for each
pair of objects
A
and
C
in
\mathscr{A}
, we define

F_{\mathcal{X}}(C,A)=\{0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0\mid(\mathcal{X},B)\rightarrow(\mathcal{X},C)\rightarrow 0\text{\;is exact}\}.

Dually we define for each pair of objects
A
and
C
in

\mathscr{A}

F^{\mathcal{X}}(C,A)=\{0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0\mid(B,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow(A,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow 0\text{\;is exact}\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
13
, Proposition 1.7]

The additive subbifunctors
F_{\mathcal{X}}
and
F^{\mathcal{X}}

of
{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,-)
are closed for any subcategory

\mathcal{X}
of
\mathscr{A}
.

Remark.
By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, it
follows that if a subbifunctor

has enough projectives or

is a closed subfunctor.

Lemma 2.3

[

A subbifunctor is triangulated if and only if it is closed.

Relative derived equivalences for Artin algebras

Let
A
be an arbitrary ring with identity. The category
A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
of unitary left
A
-modules is an abelian category with enough projective objects. We use
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
to denote the full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
consisting of finitely presented
A
-modules, that is,
A
-modules

admitting a projective presentation
P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow X\rightarrow 0
with
P_{i}
finitely generated projective for
i=0,1
. The category
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
is abelian when
A
is left coherent. The full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
consisting of all projective modules is denoted by
A\mbox{-}{\rm Proj}
, and the category of finitely generated projective
A
-modules is written as
A\mbox{-}{\rm proj}
. Note that
A\mbox{-}{\rm proj}
are precisely those projective modules in
A\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
. The stable category of
A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
is denoted by
A\mbox{-}\underline{{\rm Mod}}
, in which morphism space is denoted by
\underline{{\rm Hom\,}}_{A}(X,Y)
for each pair of
A
-modules
X
and

. For a full subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of
A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, we denote by
\underline{\mathscr{X}}
the full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}\underline{{\rm Mod}}
consisting of all modules in
\mathscr{X}
. However, the full subcategory of
A\mbox{-}\underline{{\rm Mod}}
consisting of finitely presented modules is denoted by
A\mbox{-}\underline{{\rm mod}}

A
is an Artin
R
-algebras. Let
F

be a non-zero subfunctor of
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(-,-)
. Let

A
-mod be the category of finitely generated left

\mathcal{P}(F)

-modules.

of finitely generated

\mathcal{P}(F)

The relative stable category of

is denoted by

for each pair of

has enough projectives and injectives, such that

Let

be the relative bounded derived category of

Two Artin algebra

and

are said to be

if the following equivalent conditions are satisfied.

(1)

{\rm{\mathscr{D}_{F}^{b}}}(A\mbox{-}{\rm mod})
and

(2)

\mathscr{K}^{{\rm b}}\left({\mathcal{P}(F)}\right)
and
\mathscr{K}^{{\rm b}}\left({{}_{B}\mathcal{P}}\right)

(3)

\mathscr{K}^{{\rm-}}\left({\mathcal{P}(F)}\right)

\mathscr{K}^{{\rm-}}\left({{}_{B}\mathcal{P}}\right)

(4)

satisfying

(i)

for i
\neq

(ii)

{\rm add\,}(T^{\bullet})
, the category of direct summands of

, generates
\mathscr{K}^{{\rm b}}\left({\mathcal{P}(F)}\right)
as

such that the endomorphism algebra of
T^{\bullet}
in
\mathscr{K}^{{\rm b}}\left({A\mbox{-}{\rm proj}}\right)
is isomorphic to
B
.

For the proof that the above conditions are indeed equivalent, we refer to
[
36
]
.

A complex
T^{\bullet}
satisfying the conditions (a) and (b) above is called a
relative tilting complex
. A triangle equivalence functor
L:{\rm{\mathscr{D}_{F}^{b}}}(A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{{\rm b}}\left({B\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}}\right)
is called a
relative derived equivalence
. In this case, the image
L(G)
is isomorphic in
\mathscr{D}^{{\rm b}}\left({B\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}}\right)
to a tilting complex, and there is a relative tilting complex

over
A
such that

is isomorphic to
B
in
\mathscr{D}^{{\rm b}}\left({B\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}}\right)
. The complex
T^{\bullet}
is called an
associated
F
-tilting complex
of

. The following is an easy lemma for the associated
F
-tilting complexes. For the convenience of the reader, we provide a proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be two Artin algebras, and let
L:{\rm{\mathscr{D}_{F}^{b}}}(A\mbox{-}{\rm Mod})\longrightarrow\mathscr{D}^{{\rm b}}\left({B\mbox{-}{\rm Mod}}\right)

is isomorphic in

to a complex

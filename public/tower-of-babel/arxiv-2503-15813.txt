An isoperimetric inequality for lower order Neumann eigenvalues in Gauss space
Yi Gao, Kui Wang

Abstract
. We prove a sharp isoperimetric inequality for the harmonic mean of the first m − 1 m-1 nonzero Neumann eigenvalues for Lipschitz domains symmetric about the origin in Gauss space. Our result generalizes the Szegö-Weinberger type inequality in Gauss space, which was proven by Chiacchio and Di Blasio in [ 12 , Theorem 4.1] .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35P15, 58G25

1.
Introduction

The classical Szegö-Weinberger inequality
[
17
,
20
]
states that the ball uniquely maximizes the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue among bounded domains with the same volume in Euclidean space. This result has been extended to bounded domains in the hemisphere and in hyperbolic space
[
4
]
. For further results, we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
10
,
15
,
18
]
and references therein.
Regarding lower order Neumann eigenvalues, Ashbaugh and Benguria
[
3
]
conjectured that for any bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the following inequality holds:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(\Omega)}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\mu_{m}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{m}{\mu_{1}(B)},

where
B
is a round ball of the same volume as
\Omega
.
It had been noticed by Szegö and Weinberger that Szegö’s approach (see
[
17
]
) actually yields
an isoperimetric result for the sum of the reciprocals of the first two nonzero Neumann eigenvalues,
\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(\Omega)}
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a simply connected domain.
For planar domains that are not necessarily simply connected, as well as in dimensions higher than two, the problem remains open.
Recently, Xia and Wang
[
21
]
established a sharp isopermetric inequality for the harmonic mean of the first
m-1
nonzero Neumann eiegnvalues of Laplacian on any bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{H}^{m}
, precisely

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(\Omega)}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\mu_{m-1}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{m-1}{\mu_{1}(B)},

which strongly supports Conjecture (
1.1
). Wang and Xia
[
21
]
also proved (
1.2
) in hyperbolic space; Benguria, Brandolini, and Chiacchio
[
7
]
proved (
1.2
) in the hemisphere. Additionally, Wang and Xia
[
19
]
proved a similar inequality to (
1.2
) for the ratio of Dirichlet eigenvalues, and Meng and the second-named author
[
16
]
derived a similar inequality in rank-1 symmetric spaces. Chen and Mao
[
11
]
studied sharp isoperimetric estimates for the lower-order eigenvalues of the Witten-Laplacian.
For further progress on Ashbaugh and Benguria’s conjecture, we refer to
[
5
,
14
]
and the references therein.

Eigenvalue optimization problems for the Laplace operator under the Gaussian measure have also attracted widespread attention. For instance, Chiacchio and Di Blasio
[
12
]
proved that, among all origin-symmetric regions with fixed Gaussain volume, the ball maximizes the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue. Additional results on eigenvalue problems under Gaussian measure can be found in
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we consider the Neumann eigenvalue problem in Gauss space. Let
d\gamma_{m}=(2\pi)^{-m/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}dx
denote the
m
-dimensional Gaussian measure, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a connected Lipschitz domain. We define
H^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{m})
as the weighted Sobolev space equipped with the norm

\displaystyle||u||_{H^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{m})}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}\right)^{1/2}.

The Neumann eigenvalue problem on
\Omega
is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+x\cdot\nabla u=\mu u,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}
denotes the outward normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
. It is well known that the problem (
1.3
) has discrete eigenvalues, with an abuse of notation still denoted by
\mu_{k}(\Omega)
for
k=0,1,\cdots
, which satisfy

\displaystyle 0=\mu_{0}(\Omega)<\mu_{1}(\Omega)\leq\mu_{2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\to+\infty,

with each eigenvalue repeated according to its multiplicity. Here,
\mu_{k}(\Omega)
denotes the
k
th Neumann eigenvalue of (
1.3
) in Gauss space, and
u_{k}(x)
denotes the corresponding eigenfunction. By standard spectral theory for self-adjoint compact operators,
\mu_{k}(\Omega)
admits the variational characterization:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(\Omega)=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{m})}\Big\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}}:\int_{\Omega}uu_{j}\,d\gamma_{m}=0\text{\quad for\quad}0\leq j\leq k-1\Big\}.

For Neumann eigenvalues defined in this way, Chiacchio and Di Blasio
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
proved that the Euclidean ball centered at the origin uniquely maximizes
\mu_{1}(\Omega)
among all sets
\Omega
symmetric about the origin with the same Gaussian volume. In this paper, we establish a sharp estimate for the harmonic mean of the first
m-1
nonzero Neumann eigenvalues in Gauss space. The main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a Lipschitz domain (possibly unbounded) symmetric about the origin, and let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the origin-centered ball with the same Gaussian
volume as
\Omega
, i.e.

\int_{\Omega}\,d\gamma_{m}=\int_{B}\,d\gamma_{m}
.
Let
\mu_{i}(\Omega)
be the Neumann eigenvalues of (
1.3
). Then

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu_{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}(\Omega)}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\mu_{m-1}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{m-1}{\mu_{1}(B)}.

Equality holds if and only if

\Omega=B
.

Note that
\mu_{1}(\Omega)\leq\mu_{2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{m-1}(\Omega)
, so inequality (
1.5
) strengthens the Szegö-Weinberger inequality

\mu_{1}(\Omega)\leq\mu_{1}(B)

proven in
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
in Gauss space.
The symmetry assumption on
\Omega
in Theorem
1.1

ensures the validity of the orthogonality conditions (
3.2
), as Gaussian measure is radially
symmetric about the origin.
It remains an interesting question that whether Ashbaugh and Benguria’s conjecture (
1.1
)
holds in Gauss space.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we study properties of the first nonzero eigenvalue and eigenfunctions for balls in Gauss space. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Eigenvalue Problem for Balls In Gauss Space

In this section, we prove some properties of the first nonzero Neumann eigenvalue and its eigenfunctions for round balls in Gauss space. Let
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the origin-centered ball with radius
R
. The Neumann eigenvalue problem on
B_{R}
is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+x\cdot\nabla u=\mu(B_{R})u\quad&\text{in $B_{R}$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\quad&\text{on $\partial B_{R}$,}\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal on
\partial B_{R}
.
From Lemma 4.1 of
[
12
]
, the first nonzero eigenvalue of (
2.1
) has multiplicity
m
, meaning

\mu_{1}(B_{R})=\mu_{2}(B_{R})=\cdots=\mu_{m}(B_{R}),

and the corresponding eigenfunctions are of the form
u_{i}(x)=g(r)\psi_{i}(\theta)
, where
(r,\theta)
are polar coordinates,

\psi_{i}(\theta)
are the linear coordinate functions restricted to
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
, and
g(r)
satisfies

(2.2)

g^{\prime\prime}(r)+\Big(\frac{m-1}{r}-r\Big)g^{\prime}(r)+\Big(\mu_{1}(B_{R})-\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}\Big)g(r)=0,\quad r\in(0,R)

subject to the boundary conditions
g(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}(R)=0
.

Multiplying (
2.2
) by
g(r)r^{m-1}e^{-r^{2}/2}
and integrating over
[0,R]
, we obtain

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{R})=\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\Big(g^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{R}g(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}.

Moreover, the first nonzero eigenvalue of (
2.1
) has the following the variational characterization

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{R})=\inf_{\varphi\in C^{1}}\Big\{\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\varphi^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}\varphi(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{R}\varphi(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}:\varphi(0)=0,\varphi^{\prime}(R)=0\Big\}.

From (
2.4
), it is clear that
g(r)
does not change sign in
[0,R]
; thus we assume
g(r)\geq 0
in what follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mu_{1}(B_{R})
is strictly decreasing in
R
.

Proof.

The monotonicity of
\mu_{1}(B_{R})
with respect to
R
holds for all regular Sturm-Liouville problems. For completeness, we provide a proof via the variational characterization (
2.4
) of
\mu_{1}(B_{R})
.

For
0<a<b
, let
g_{a}(r)
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\mu_{1}(B_{a})
, and extend
g_{a}
to
[0,b]
by

g_{b}(r)=\begin{cases}g_{a}(r),\quad&r\leq a,\\
g_{a}(a),\quad&a<r\leq b.\end{cases}

Clearly,
g_{b}(r)
is admissible for
\mu_{1}(B_{b})
, so

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{b})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{b}\Big(g_{b}^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{b}(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{b}g_{b}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{a}\Big(g_{a}^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr+\int_{a}^{b}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(a)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{a}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr+\int_{a}^{b}g_{a}(a)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}.

Note that

\frac{\int_{a}^{b}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{a}^{b}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}<\frac{m-1}{a^{2}}<\frac{\int_{0}^{a}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{a}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{a}^{b}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(a)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr+\int_{0}^{a}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{a}^{b}g_{a}(a)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr+\int_{0}^{a}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}

\displaystyle<\quad

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{a}\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{a}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}.

Substituting above inequality into (
2.5
) yields

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{b})<\frac{\int_{0}^{a}\Big(g_{a}^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g_{a}(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{a}g_{a}(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}=\mu_{1}(B_{a}),

as required.

\square

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
R>0
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{R})>1.

Proof.

This follows from the equality case of Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality, proved in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
6
,
9
]
).

\square

Remark 2.1
.

In fact, the conclusion of Lemma
2.2
holds true for all convex bounded domains (not just for the ball), see
[
8
,
9
]
and the references therein. Moreover if
\Omega

is a convex
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
\mu_{1}(\Omega)=1
if and only if
\Omega

is a strip, see
[
6
]
and the references therein.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g(r)
,
r\in[0,R]
, be a nonnegative eigenfunction corresponding to
\mu_{1}(B_{R})
defined by (
2.2
). Then

(2.7)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r)>0,\quad\quad r\in(0,R),

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r)-\frac{g(r)}{r}\leq 0,\quad\quad r\in(0,R].

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists
r\in(0,R)
such that
g^{\prime}(r)=0
. Recall from (
2.2
) that

(2.9)

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}(t)+\Big(\frac{m-1}{t}-t\Big)g^{\prime}(t)+\Big(\mu_{1}(B_{R})-\frac{m-1}{t^{2}}\Big)g(t)=0,\quad\quad t\in(0,r)

subject to
g(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}(r)=0
. Taking
g(t)
(
t\in(0,r)
) as a trial function of
\mu_{1}(B_{r})
and using (
2.9
), we find:

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(B_{r})\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\Big(g^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{t^{2}}g(t)^{2}\Big)e^{-t^{2}/2}t^{m-1}\,dt}{\int_{0}^{r}g(t)^{2}e^{-t^{2}/2}t^{m-1}\,dt}=\mu_{1}(B_{R})

contradicting with Lemma
2.1
. Thus
g^{\prime}(r)\neq 0
in
(0,R)
, hence (
2.7
) holds.

Now we prove (
2.8
). Set

H(r)=g^{\prime}(r)-\frac{g(r)}{r},\qquad\qquad r\in(0,R].

Since
g(0)=0
,
g^{\prime}(R)=0
, and
g^{\prime}(r)>0
for
r\in(0,R)
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}H(r)=0,\qquad H(R)=-\frac{g(R)}{R}<0.

Taking the derivative with respect to
r
, we obtain

(2.11)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r)=g^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{g(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{g^{\prime}(r)}{r},

Eliminating
g^{\prime\prime}(r)
from (
2.2
) and (
2.11
), we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r)=\Big(r-\frac{m}{r}\Big)g^{\prime}+\Big(\frac{m}{r^{2}}-\mu_{1}(B_{R})\Big)g(r).

We claim that

(2.13)

\displaystyle H(r)\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in(0,\sqrt{m}),\text{\quad and \quad}H(a)<0\quad\text{for some}\quad a\in(0,\sqrt{m}).

Indeed, if there exists an
r_{1}\in(0,\sqrt{m})
such that
H(r_{1})\geq 0
, i.e.

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r_{1})\geq\frac{g(r_{1})}{r_{1}},

then plugging above inequality into (
2.12
) yields

(2.14)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r_{1})=\Big(r_{1}-\frac{m}{r_{1}}\Big)g^{\prime}(r_{1})+\Big(\frac{m}{r_{1}^{2}}-\mu_{1}(B_{R})\Big)g(r_{1})\leq(1-\mu_{1}(B_{R}))g(r_{1})<0,

where we used
\mu_{1}(B_{R})>1
(see Lemma
2.2
) in the inequality. Thus we deduce from (
2.10
) and (
2.14
) that
H(r)\leq 0
for
r\in(0,\sqrt{m})
. If
H(r)\equiv 0
for all
r\in(0,\sqrt{m})
, then
g(r)=g^{\prime}(0)r
in
(0,\sqrt{m})
, contradicting with (
2.2
). Thus Claim (
2.13
) comes true.

Now we prove
H(r)\leq 0
for all
r\in(a,R)
. Assume
H(r_{2})=0
for some
r_{2}\in(0,R)
, namely

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r_{2})=\frac{g(r_{2})}{r_{2}},

combined with (
2.12
), if follows

(2.15)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r_{2})=\Big(r_{2}-\frac{m}{r_{2}}\Big)g^{\prime}+\Big(\frac{m}{r_{2}^{2}}-\mu_{1}(B_{R})\Big)g(r_{2})=(1-\mu_{1}(B_{R}))g(r_{2})<0,

Therefore we conclude from (
2.10
), (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) that

H(r)\leq 0
for all
r\in[a,R]
.
Hence we complete the proof of (
2.8
).

\square

3.
Proof of Main Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. The main idea is to construct trial functions for
\mu_{k}
(see (
1.4
)) using techniques introduced by Weinberger
[
20
]
and further developed by Xia and Wang in
[
21
]
. We first recall a monotonicity lemma for Gaussian symmetrization.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an open set, and let
B
be the origin-centered round ball with the same Gaussian volume as
\Omega
, i.e.
\int_{B}\,d\gamma_{m}=\int_{\Omega}\,d\gamma_{m}
. If
h(r)
is nonincreasing on
[0,+\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h(|x|)\,d\gamma_{m}\leq\int_{B}h(|x|)\,d\gamma_{m}.

Here,
d\gamma_{m}=(2\pi)^{-m/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}dx
is the
m
-dimensional Gaussian measure.

Proof.

See the proof of inequality (4.36) in
[
12
, Page 213]
.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
u_{i}(x)
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\mu_{i}(\Omega)
. The Neumann eigenvalues satisfy the variational principle

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\Omega)=\inf\Big\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}}:\int_{\Omega}uu_{k}\,d\gamma_{m}=0\text{ for }0\leq k<i\Big\}.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the origin-centered ball with radius
R
and the same Gaussian volume as
\Omega
.
Define
G(r):[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
as

\displaystyle G(r)=\begin{cases}g(r),&\quad r<R,\\
g(R),&\quad r\geq R,\end{cases}

where
g(r)
is defined in Section
2
. For
1\leq i\leq m
, define

\displaystyle v_{i}(x)=G(|x|)\frac{x_{i}}{|x|}.

Since
\Omega
is symmetric about the origin, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{i}(x)d\gamma_{m}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m.

For
1\leq i,j\leq m
, let

\displaystyle q_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}v_{i}(x)u_{j}(x)\,d\gamma_{m},

.
By QR-factorization, there exists an orthogonal matrix
A=(a_{ij})
such that

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{ik}v_{k}(x)u_{j}(x)\,d\gamma_{m}

for all
1\leq j<i\leq m
.
Thus, by appropriately selecting the coordinate axes, we may assume that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{i}(x)u_{j}(x)\,d\gamma_{m}=0

for
1\leq j<i\leq m
.

According to (
3.2
) and variational formulation (
3.1
) of
\mu_{i}(\Omega)
,
v_{i}(x)
is admissible for
\mu_{i}(\Omega)
, i.e. for
1\leq i\leq m
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{i}(x)^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{i}(x)|^{2}\,d\gamma_{m}

A direct computation shows

|\nabla v_{i}(x)|^{2}=G^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+G(r)^{2}(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}}).

Substituting this into (
3.3
) and summing over
i
, we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}})e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,dx.

Note that
G(r)
is a constant for
r>R
, so

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\int_{B}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx.

Using the ordering
\mu_{1}(\Omega)\leq\mu_{2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{m}(\Omega)
and the identity
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}=1
, we estimate

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}})e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}})e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx+\frac{1}{\mu_{m}(\Omega)}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx.

Since
G(r)/r
is nonincreasing in
(0,\infty)
by Lemma
2.3
, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{G(r)^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx\leq\int_{B}\frac{G(r)^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx.

Combining (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{|x|^{4}})e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}\frac{G(r)^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx.

Substituting (
3.5
) and (
3.8
) into (
3.4
) yields

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{m\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}\frac{G(r)^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{(m-1)\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}G^{\prime}(r)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}\frac{G(r)^{2}}{|x|^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{(m-1)\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}\big(G^{\prime}(r)^{2}+(m-1)\frac{G(r)^{2}}{r^{2}}\big)e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx,

where the second inequality uses
\mu_{i}(\Omega)\leq\mu_{m}(\Omega)
for

.

(3.10)

3.9
) and (

\displaystyle\int_{B}G(r)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{(m-1)\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\int_{B}\Big(G^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}G(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx,

which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}(\Omega)}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)\int_{B}G(r)^{2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx}{\int_{B}\big(G^{\prime}(r)^{2}+(m-1)G(r)^{2}/r^{2}\big)e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m-1)\int_{0}^{R}g(r)^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{R}\Big(g^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\frac{m-1}{r^{2}}g(r)^{2}\Big)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{m-1}\,dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{\mu_{1}(B)},

where the last equality follows from (

).

If equality holds in the above inequality,

.

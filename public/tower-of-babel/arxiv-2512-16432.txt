An active-set algorithm for spectral unmixing
Nils Foix-Colonier, Sébastien Bourguignon

Abstract
Linear spectral unmixing under nonnegativity and sum-to-one constraints is a convex optimization problem for which many algorithms were proposed. In practice, especially for supervised unmixing (i.e., with a large dictionary), solutions tend to be sparse due to the nonnegativity of the abundances, thereby motivating the use of an active-set solver. Given the problem specific features, it seems advantageous to design a dedicated algorithm in order to gain computational performance compared to generic solvers. In this paper, we propose to derive such a specific algorithm, while extending the nonnegativity constraints to broader minimum abundance constraints.



1
Problem definition

Given a spectral library
{\mathbf{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times P}
, a measured spectrum
{\bm{y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, and a lower-bound vector
\bm{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P}_{+}
that is feasible, i.e.
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\ell}\leq 1
, we consider the following unmixing problem:

\min_{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P}}\ \tfrac{1}{2}{\|{\bm{y}}-{\mathbf{A}}{\bm{x}}\|}_{2}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad{\bm{x}}\geq\bm{\ell},\;\;\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}{\bm{x}}=1

(1)

For convenience, the variable is shifted:
\tilde{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}-\bm{\ell}
. We also define
\tilde{\bm{y}}={\bm{y}}-{\mathbf{A}}\bm{\ell}
and
s=1-\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{\ell}
. Then (
1
) is equivalent to:

\min_{\tilde{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P}}\;\;\tfrac{1}{2}{\|\tilde{\bm{y}}-{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{x}}\|}_{2}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\tilde{\bm{x}}\geq=\bm{0},\;\;\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\bm{x}}=s

(2)

Consequently, solving (
2
) yields the solution to (
1
), by shifting back:
{\bm{x}}=\tilde{\bm{x}}+\bm{\ell}
. Note that, with
\bm{\ell}=\bm{0}
, we have the standard Fully Constrained Least Squares (FCLS) formulation
[
1
]
.

2
KKT conditions

The objective function in (
2
) can be rewritten as the convex quadratic objective function:

f(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{H}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{h}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}+\tfrac{1}{2}{\|\tilde{\bm{y}}\|}_{2}^{2}\text{, \; with ${\mathbf{H}}:={\mathbf{A}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{A}}$ and ${\bm{h}}:={\mathbf{A}}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{y}}}$}.

(3)

Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
be the Lagrange multiplier for the equality constraint
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}=s
, and
\bm{\mu}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P}
the vector of multipliers for the non-negativity constraints
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}\geq\bm{0}
. The Lagrangian function for problem (
2
) is:

\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bm{x}}},\lambda,\bm{\mu})=f(\tilde{{\bm{x}}})+\lambda(\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-s)-\bm{\mu}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}

(4)

whose gradient reads:

\nabla_{\tilde{{\bm{x}}}}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{{\bm{x}}},\lambda,\bm{\mu})={\mathbf{H}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{h}}+\lambda\bm{1}-\bm{\mu}.

(5)

The Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) first-order conditions
[
3
]
for problem (
2
) read:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{h}}+\lambda\bm{1}-\bm{\mu}=\bm{0}

(stationarity)

(6a)

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}=s,\ \tilde{{\bm{x}}}\geq\bm{0}

(primal feasibility)

(6b)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}\geq\bm{0}

(dual feasibility)

(6c)

\displaystyle\forall i\in\llbracket 1,P\rrbracket,\ \mu_{i}\tilde{x}_{i}=0

(complementary slackness)

(6d)

Since
f
is convex and all constraints are convex, the first-order KKT conditions are a necessary and sufficient characterization of global optimality.

3
Active-set algorithm

In order to find good candidates
\tilde{\bm{x}}
for the KKT conditions (
6
), we design an active-set algorithm, inspired by
[
3
]
, based on the one introduced in 1974 by Lawson and Hanson
[
2
]
. It operates by iteratively determining which inequality constraints will be active at the solution and temporarily treats them as equalities. In our case, at any iterate with a feasible candidate
\tilde{\bm{x}}
, the indices of its coefficients are partitioned into an
active set

L=\{i\,|\,\tilde{x}_{i}=0\}
, for the variables currently at the lower bound, and a
free set

F=\{i\,|\,\tilde{x}_{i}>0\}
for the variables currently allowed to move inside the feasible space.

Let
S\subseteq\llbracket 1,P\rrbracket
, the notation
{\mathbf{A}}_{S}
refers to the matrix
{\mathbf{A}}
restricted to the columns indexed by
S
. In addition, let
{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}={\mathbf{A}}_{F}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{A}}_{F}
,
{\mathbf{H}}_{LF}={\mathbf{A}}_{L}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{A}}_{F}
.
According to the complementary slackness condition (
6d
) we have
\forall i\in F,\,\mu_{i}=0
. In addition, with dual feasibility (
6c
):
\forall i\in L,\,\mu_{i}\geq 0
. Consequently, with condition (
6a
):

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{F}-{\bm{h}}_{F}+\lambda\bm{1}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle(\bm{\mu}_{F}=\bm{0})

(7a)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{L}={\mathbf{H}}_{LF}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{F}-{\bm{h}}_{L}+\lambda\bm{1}

\displaystyle(\bm{\mu}_{L}\geq\bm{0})

(7b)

These equations are exploited in the active-set algorithm, which can be summarized in three steps:

1.

Solve an equality-constrained subproblem
, obtained by fixing
\tilde{x}_{i}=0
for
i\in L
(inequality constraints ignored for now). It consists of solving (
7a
) while enforcing
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{\bm{x}}=s
, which returns a candidate
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{F}^{*}
and
\lambda^{\star}
. If
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{F}^{*}\geq\bm{0}
, then directly go to step 3. Otherwise, go to step 2.

2.

Keeping primal feasibility
: move from
\tilde{\bm{x}}
towards
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}
, taking the largest step keeping nonnegativity constraints. The index of a variable that first saturates nonnegativity is moved from
F
to
L
, and the algorithm goes back to step 1 with an updated
\tilde{\bm{x}}
.

3.

Checking dual feasibility
: compute the Lagrange multipliers
\bm{\mu}_{L}
with equation (
7b
) evaluated with
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}
and
\lambda^{*}
, from step 1. If
\bm{\mu}_{L}\geq\bm{0}
, then
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}
is a global minimizer and the algorithm stops. Otherwise, it fails to satisfy dual feasibility, so the index
i\in L
corresponding to the most negative
\mu_{i}
is moved from
L
to
F
, and the algorithm moves back to step 1 with the updated candidate.

This mechanism is directly motivated by the KKT conditions: the solve at step 1 enforces (
6a
) and the equality constraint from (
6b
), while step 2 guarantees the primal feasibility on
F
by respecting the inequalities of (
6b
) for
F
, and since the variables in the active set are null we have complete primal feasibility. The dual feasibility (
6c
) is verified at step 3, on
L
only since the
\mu_{i}
for
i\in F
are null by construction. Finally, the complementary slackness (
6d
) is maintained by construction. We assume feasibility and nondegeneracy, ensuring unique minimizers for subproblems. Consequently, as the convergence under a finite number of iterations of such algorithms has been proven
[
3
]
, we can guarantee that it will find a global minimizer of problem (
2
).

3.1
Solving an equality-constrained subproblem

In order to solve equality-constrained subproblems (from step 1), we need to reformulate the problem at hand. We want to solve the equation system corresponding to the stationarity condition (
6a
), under the sum equality constraint (
6b
), and with respect to the constraints from the active set
L
. In practice, we do not need to solve the system in dimension
P
, since the coefficients with indices in
L
are zero, so we just need to solve the reduced system (
7a
) under the sum equality constraint:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{F}-{\bm{h}}_{F}+\lambda\bm{1}=\bm{0}

(8a)

\displaystyle\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{F}=s

(8b)

This can be reformulated equivalently as:

\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}&\bm{1}\\[2.0pt]
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{{\bm{x}}}_{F}\\
\lambda\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{h}}_{F}\\
s\end{bmatrix}

(9)

Assuming that
{\mathbf{A}}_{F}
has
|F|\leq N
linearly independent columns,
{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}
is symmetric positive definite, therefore invertible, and its inverse is also symmetric positive definite.
The determinant of the matrix in (
9
) can be decomposed into
[
4
]
:

\det\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}&\bm{1}\\[2.0pt]
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)=\det\left({\mathbf{H}}_{FF}\right)\det\left(0-\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}^{-1}\bm{1}\right)

(10)

Where
\det({\mathbf{H}}_{FF})>0
since
{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}
is positive definite and
\det\left(0-\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}^{-1}\bm{1}\right)=-\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}^{-1}\bm{1}\neq 0
since
{\mathbf{H}}_{FF}^{-1}
is positive definite. Hence, the solution to system (
9
) exists and is unique. It is denoted
(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}^{*},\,\lambda^{*})
, and will be used in the next step.

3.2
Keeping primal feasibility

Let
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}
be the current feasible iterate, and
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}^{*}
the minimizer obtained by (
9
). The direction
\bm{d}=\tilde{{\bm{x}}}^{*}-\tilde{{\bm{x}}}
satisfies
\bm{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{d}=0
(as the initial and end points both respect the sum-to-
s
constraint), and
\bm{d}_{L}=\bm{0}
by construction. Moving along direction
\bm{d}
with
{\bm{x}}^{(\alpha)}:=\tilde{{\bm{x}}}+\alpha\bm{d}
decreases the cost function as
\alpha
increases from 0 to 1, and solutions remain feasible as long as
x^{(\alpha)}_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
, so the largest feasible step reads:

\alpha^{*}=\min_{i\in D^{-}}\frac{-\tilde{x}_{i}}{d_{i}}\text{, where $D^{-}:=\left\{i\in F\ \middle|\ d_{i}<0\right\}$.}

(11)

Let
j
denote the argument of the minimum in equation (
11
) (should multiple indices allow to reach the minimum, we just keep a randomly chosen one). By definition
\tilde{x}_{j}=0
, so this step terminates by updating the corresponding set of active constraints:

L=L\cup\{j\},\quad F=F\setminus\{j\}.

(12)

3.3
Checking dual feasibility

This step starts with a primal feasible point
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}
and
\lambda^{*}
, solving subproblem (
8
). Dual feasibility now is required to be checked for variables in
L
, since for
i\in F
the
\mu_{i}=0
by construction. The vector of Lagrange multipliers is computed with equation (
7b
):

\bm{\mu}^{*}_{L}={\mathbf{H}}_{LF}\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}_{F}-{\bm{h}}_{L}+\lambda^{*}\bm{1}

(13)

Then for all
i\in L
we check the dual feasibility:
\mu^{*}_{i}\geq 0
. If this holds, all KKT conditions are satisfied and
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{*}
is the global minimizer. Otherwise, we choose the index
j\in L
with the most negative associated
\mu^{*}_{j}
, and it is moved from the active set to the free set:

L=L\setminus\{j\},\quad F=F\cup\{j\}.

(14)

Note that this works because having a negative
\mu^{*}_{j}
means that the gradient along the direction that increases
\tilde{x}_{j}
is negative. Therefore, allowing
\tilde{x}_{j}
to move above zero will decrease the objective while maintaining the sum-to-
s
equality constraint.

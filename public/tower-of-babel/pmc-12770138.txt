Single cell transcriptomic profiling uncovers shared inflammatory pathways and immune dysregulation underlying the asthma to lung cancer transition
Authors: He Huang, Gang Teng, Jing Zhou, Ling Liu, Mei Dong, Wei Chen, Nianzhi Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Globally, lung cancer (LC) persists as the deadliest malignant disease, with annual mortality approaching 1.8 million individuals. Despite remarkable advances in diagnostic technologies and therapeutic strategies over the past decades, the prognosis for lung cancer patients remains unsatisfactory, with five-year survival rates hovering below 20% for most cases. Two primary histological categories comprise this disease: non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), representing roughly 85% of occurrences, alongside small cell lung cancer (SCLC). Critical influences on lung cancer advancement and treatment outcomes stem from the tumor microenvironment (TME), which harbors heterogeneous immune cell populations, stromal elements, and malignant epithelial components [1, 2]. The TME constitutes a dynamic ecosystem wherein cancer cells continuously interact with surrounding stromal and immune cells, establishing complex signaling networks that modulate tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. Nevertheless, incomplete characterization remains regarding immune cell spatiotemporal behaviors and critical gene expression patterns within this microenvironment. Elucidating these intricate cellular dynamics holds substantial promise for identifying novel therapeutic vulnerabilities and developing more effective treatment modalities [3].

Emerging evidence supports an “inflammation-to-cancer transition” paradigm linking chronic airway inflammation to lung cancer development. This concept posits that persistent inflammatory conditions create a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment conducive to malignant transformation. Asthmatic individuals exhibit approximately 44% elevated lung cancer risk compared to non-asthmatic populations, with this association remaining significant even among never-smokers. The underlying molecular mechanisms involve chronic inflammation-induced oxidative stress, excessive reactive oxygen species generation, and consequent DNA damage driving epithelial oncogenic transformation [4, 5]. The chromosome 17q21 locus genes ORMDL3 and GSDMB, which have been robustly associated with asthma susceptibility across diverse ethnic populations, participate in the IL-17 signaling and PD-L1/PD-1 checkpoint pathways central to tumor immune evasion. These shared genetic determinants suggest potential mechanistic overlap between chronic inflammatory airway diseases and lung carcinogenesis, highlighting the importance of investigating inflammation-associated molecular signatures in the lung cancer immune microenvironment [6, 7].

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has revolutionized cancer research by enabling unprecedented resolution of individual cellular transcriptomes within tumor tissues, thereby overcoming the limitations of bulk sequencing approaches that mask cellular heterogeneity [8]. This powerful technology permits the dissection of complex tumor ecosystems at single-cell resolution, facilitating the identification of rare cell populations and novel cellular states that may play pivotal roles in disease progression. Through this investigation, we employed scRNA-seq for comprehensive immune microenvironment characterization in lung cancer, utilizing advanced dimensionality reduction algorithms including UMAP and t-SNE for intuitive visualization of high-dimensional transcriptomic data. Gene Ontology and pathway enrichment analyses were subsequently performed to systematically evaluate biological processes and signaling networks operating within the TME, revealing the CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling axis as a central mediator of immune cell communication and trafficking [9, 10].

This comprehensive perspective on immune cell spatiotemporal dynamics and key gene expression configurations within lung cancer microenvironments could inform personalized immunotherapy advancement and facilitate the development of more precise therapeutic interventions. By clarifying specific immune cell contributions that foster either tumor-permissive or tumor-targeting conditions, our examination lays groundwork for identifying superior immunotherapy targets in lung cancer patient populations. To validate the transcriptomic findings obtained from single-cell sequencing and ensure the reliability of our computational analyses, we performed quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) in lung cancer cell lines to confirm expression patterns of key genes involved in the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis and PD-L1–mediated immunoregulation [11, 12].

Methods

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

Two publicly available scRNA-seq datasets (GSM4453576-GSM4453579) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus database for downstream analysis. The Seurat R package served as the primary analytical framework for processing these datasets. Quality control filtering was implemented to exclude cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or mitochondrial transcript proportions exceeding 20%. To address technical variations arising from different experimental batches, the Harmony algorithm was applied for data integration across multiple samples. Following LogNormalization-based preprocessing, unsupervised clustering was conducted to identify distinct cell populations. Dimensional reduction was subsequently performed using PCA and t-SNE algorithms for data visualization purposes. Automated cell type classification was accomplished through the SingleR package, and differentially expressed marker genes across identified clusters were determined using the FindAllMarkers function [13–17]. The complete analytical workflow is illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Trajectory and pseudotime reconstruction

Preprocessed single-cell transcriptomic data underwent additional quality assessment, including elimination of substandard cells and expression value standardization. The Monocle3 framework was employed to construct developmental trajectories by ordering cells according to their transcriptional signatures. Pseudotime values were subsequently computed to model continuous transitions in cellular states. Branch point examination enabled identification of genes governing alternative differentiation pathways. Dynamic gene expression changes along the inferred trajectories were characterized to reveal potential associations with pulmonary malignancy progression and treatment response. Functional annotation of trajectory-associated genes was performed through pathway enrichment analyses. Correlation assessment between individual gene expression levels and pseudotime coordinates facilitated discovery of state transition regulators. Results were presented using multiple visualization approaches, including violin plots, clustered heatmaps, and trajectory diagrams. Clinical outcome data were incorporated to enhance biological interpretation of trajectory findings [18, 19].

Cell culture and quantitative PCR validation

BEAS-2B human bronchial epithelial cells along with A549 and H1975 lung adenocarcinoma cell lines were acquired from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank (Shanghai, China). BEAS-2B cells were propagated in BEGM medium (Lonza, USA), whereas A549 and H1975 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum plus 1% penicillin-streptomycin antibiotics. All cultures were incubated under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂, humidified environment). RNA isolation was performed with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA), followed by reverse transcription using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit (Takara, Japan). Quantitative PCR amplification was executed on an ABI 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA) with TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, Japan). Relative transcript abundance was determined via the 2⁻++ct method, normalizing target gene expression to GAPDH. Oligonucleotide primer sequences employed were: CXCL12 (Forward: CAGAGCCAACGTCAAGCATC; Reverse: TGCTTGACGTTGGCTCTGAT), CXCR4 (Forward: ACTACACCGAGGAAATGGGCT; Reverse: CCCACAATGCCAGTTAAGAAGA), CD274/PD-L1 (Forward: GCTGCACTAATTGTCTTGCCT; Reverse: TGGATGGTGGTGGTCTTGT), FOXP3 (Forward: GGCCCTTCTCCAGGACAGA; Reverse: GCTGATCATGGCTGGGTTGT), GZMB (Forward: CGACAGTGGGAAACACTCGA; Reverse: GGCCCCTTTCTCTCTGTTGG), PRF1 (Forward: ATGTGACCAGGGACCAGC; Reverse: TGGAGTTGACCCAGTCTTCT), and GAPDH (Forward: AAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTCAAC; Reverse: GGGGTCATTGATGGCAACAATA).

Statistical methodology

Comparative analyses of continuous variables were conducted using either Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test, selected based on underlying data distribution characteristics. Associations between variables were evaluated through Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients. Statistical significance was established at the p < 0.05 threshold. All computational analyses were implemented using R software (version 4.2.0).

Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals diversity and spatial distribution of immune cell subpopulations in lung cancer microenvironment

For thoroughly investigating immune cell composition and distribution in the lung cancer microenvironment, this study employed single-cell RNA sequencing technology. Through UMAP and t-SNE, two sophisticated dimensionality reduction and visualization approaches, successful mapping of a detailed immune cell atlas was achieved. Within the lung cancer microenvironment, the data expose extraordinary cellular diversity, with numerous immune cell types present: alveolar macrophages, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), CD8 + cytotoxic T cells, CD4 + T helper cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), natural killer (NK) cells, B cells, plasma cells, dendritic cells, neutrophils, and mast cells.Beyond possessing distinct expression profiles, these cell types also exhibit varying spatial distribution patterns. Within specific tumor regions, certain cell types including tumor-associated macrophages and regulatory T cells established densely packed clusters, whereas at the transcriptome level, cytotoxic T cells and NK cells demonstrated more scattered distribution patterns. A sophisticated immune regulatory network in the lung cancer microenvironment is characterized by the diverse immune cell composition and distribution.Enhanced understanding of tumor-immune interactions results from these findings, which supply an important theoretical basis and prospective targets for developing targeted immunotherapy strategies. Variable responses to checkpoint inhibitor therapy may be explained by the spatial heterogeneity of immune infiltration. For future investigations to further examine the distinct functions of specific immune subtypes at different stages of lung carcinogenesis and treatment, these findings establish the foundation, thereby paving the way for personalized immunotherapy approach development in lung cancer (Fig. 2).

Validation of key gene expression by qRT-PCR

To validate the differential expression of key genes identified from the single-cell transcriptomic analysis, qRT-PCR was conducted in the normal human bronchial epithelial cell line BEAS-2B and two lung adenocarcinoma cell lines, A549 and H1975. The mRNA levels of CXCL12, CXCR4, and CD274 (PD-L1) were significantly upregulated in both A549 and H1975 cells compared with BEAS-2B cells (p < 0.001), which was consistent with the transcriptional patterns revealed by single-cell sequencing. In contrast, FOXP3, GZMB, and PRF1 were markedly downregulated in tumor cell lines (p < 0.01), reflecting the loss of cytotoxic and immune-regulatory signatures in malignant epithelial cells.These findings confirmed that lung cancer cells exhibit enhanced CXCL12/CXCR4-mediated chemokine signaling and PD-L1–driven immune suppression, supporting the hypothesis that tumor cells participate in shaping an immunosuppressive microenvironment. All relative expression values were normalized to GAPDH, and the results were expressed as mean ± SD from three independent experiments. The qRT-PCR results were consistent with the sequencing data, providing robust experimental evidence for the accuracy of our transcriptomic findings.

Discussion

This investigation employed single-cell RNA sequencing to comprehensively characterize the immune landscape and key molecular signatures within the lung cancer tumor microenvironment. Our findings offer mechanistic insights into the inflammation-to-cancer transition paradigm, particularly highlighting shared pathogenic pathways between chronic airway inflammation and pulmonary malignancy.

The conceptual framework linking chronic inflammatory conditions to cancer development has gained substantial empirical support in recent years. Epidemiological evidence consistently demonstrates that individuals with asthma exhibit significantly elevated lung cancer risk, independent of smoking status [20-22]. This inflammation-cancer nexus operates through multiple molecular mechanisms, including persistent oxidative stress, DNA damage accumulation, and dysregulated immune surveillance. Our single-cell transcriptomic profiling revealed that inflammation-associated genes, particularly those residing at the chromosome 17q21 locus (ORMDL3 and GSDMB), demonstrate coordinated expression patterns across immune cell populations within the tumor microenvironment. These asthma susceptibility genes participate in critical oncogenic pathways including IL-17 signaling and PD-L1/PD-1 checkpoint regulation, thereby establishing a molecular bridge connecting airway inflammation to malignant transformation [23].

The heterogeneous architecture of tumor-infiltrating immune cells uncovered in our analysis reflects the complex immunological reprogramming that accompanies the inflammation-to-cancer transition. We identified distinct subpopulations of tumor-associated macrophages exhibiting expression profiles reminiscent of chronic inflammatory states observed in asthmatic airways. The presence of exhausted T lymphocytes with elevated checkpoint molecule expression suggests that persistent inflammatory stimulation progressively impairs anti-tumor immunity. These observations align with the hypothesis that chronic airway inflammation establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment conducive to tumor initiation and progression.

Central to our findings was the identification of the CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling axis as a critical mediator of immune cell trafficking within the tumor microenvironment. This chemokine pathway has been implicated in both asthmatic airway inflammation and lung cancer metastasis, representing a convergent mechanism underlying the inflammation-cancer continuum [24, 25]. In chronic airway diseases, CXCL12-CXCR4 signaling orchestrates inflammatory cell recruitment and tissue remodeling; in malignancy, this axis promotes tumor cell dissemination and establishment of immunosuppressive niches. Therapeutic targeting of CXCL12-CXCR4 interactions may therefore provide dual benefits: attenuating chronic inflammation while simultaneously enhancing anti-tumor immune responses, particularly when combined with immune checkpoint blockade.

The IL-17 signaling pathway emerged as another critical node connecting inflammatory and oncogenic processes. IL-17, a cytokine central to asthma pathophysiology, has been increasingly recognized for its tumor-promoting functions through activation of the IL-6/STAT3/NF-κB cascade [26]. Our trajectory analysis demonstrated progressive enrichment of IL-17 pathway components during tumor evolution, suggesting that inflammatory cytokine networks established during chronic airway disease may persist and facilitate malignant progression. This temporal dynamic provides mechanistic explanation for the epidemiological association between asthma severity and lung cancer incidence.

Pseudotime reconstruction revealed the gradual acquisition of immunosuppressive phenotypes during tumor development, mirroring the immune dysfunction observed in severe persistent asthma. The accumulation of regulatory T cells and progressive T effector exhaustion identified in our analysis suggests that inflammation-induced immune tolerance mechanisms become co-opted during carcinogenesis. Understanding these temporal dynamics may inform optimal timing for therapeutic intervention, potentially identifying a window during the inflammation-cancer transition when immunomodulatory strategies would be most effective.

The prominent expression of PD-L1 across diverse cell populations within the tumor microenvironment provides molecular rationale for checkpoint inhibitor efficacy in lung cancer. Notably, PD-L1 upregulation represents a common feature of both chronic airway inflammation and malignancy, functioning as an adaptive mechanism to limit tissue damage from persistent immune activation. The heterogeneous expression patterns observed suggest that combination approaches simultaneously targeting inflammatory pathways and immune checkpoints may be necessary to overcome therapeutic resistance in patients with inflammation-associated lung cancer.

Experimental validation through qRT-PCR confirmed the transcriptomic findings, demonstrating significant upregulation of CXCL12, CXCR4, and PD-L1 alongside downregulation of cytotoxic effector molecules (GZMB, PRF1) and the regulatory T cell marker FOXP3 in lung cancer cell lines. These results substantiate the reliability of our computational analyses and underscore the functional importance of the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis and PD-L1-mediated immunosuppression in the pulmonary tumor microenvironment.

Limitations

Several limitations warrant consideration when interpreting these findings. The cross-sectional nature of the analyzed datasets precludes direct observation of the inflammation-to-cancer transition over time; longitudinal studies tracking at-risk individuals with chronic airway inflammation would provide more definitive evidence for this paradigm. Functional validation experiments are required to establish causality between identified molecular signatures and tumor progression. The contribution of additional inflammation-associated pathways and immune cell subsets merits further investigation. Integration of spatial transcriptomic approaches would enable characterization of the three-dimensional organization of inflammatory and immune cells within the tumor microenvironment. Finally, clinical correlation studies examining patients with pre-existing asthma who develop lung cancer are needed to validate the prognostic and predictive utility of the identified inflammation-cancer signatures.

Conclusion

In conclusion, light has been shed by our study on the intricate dynamics of immune cells and key gene expression in lung cancer, potentially leading to more tailored and effective treatment strategies. A foundation for developing next-generation immunotherapies that account for the complexity and heterogeneity of the lung cancer microenvironment is provided by the comprehensive immune landscape revealed through single-cell analysis. For precision immunotherapy approaches that could improve outcomes for lung cancer patients, the way is paved by this work through identifying specific immune cell states and their regulatory networks. Furthermore, the qRT-PCR validation of selected genes confirmed the reliability of our single-cell transcriptomic results, strengthening the translational significance of the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis and PD-L1–related immune suppression as potential therapeutic targets in lung cancer.

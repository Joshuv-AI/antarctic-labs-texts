Simple holograpic dual of the Maxwell-Cattaneo model
Yongjun Ahn 1,2, Matteo Baggioli 1,2, Yanyan Bu 3, Masataka Matsumoto 4,1, Xiyang Sun 3

Abstract
Diffusion, as described by Fick’s laws, governs the spreading of particles, information, data, and even financial fluctuations. However, due to its parabolic structure, the diffusion equation leads to an unphysical prediction: any localized disturbance instantaneously affects the entire system. The Maxwell-Cattaneo (MC) model, originally introduced to address relativistic heat conduction, refines the standard diffusion framework by incorporating a finite relaxation time τ \tau , associated with the onset of local equilibrium. This modification yields physically relevant consequences, including the emergence of propagating shear waves in liquids and second sound in solids. Holographic methods have historically provided powerful tools for describing the hydrodynamics of strongly correlated systems. However, they have so far failed to capture the dynamics governed by the MC model, limiting their ability to model intermediate time-scale phenomena. In this work, we construct a simple holographic dual of the Maxwell-Cattaneo model and rigorously establish its equivalence through a combination of analytical and numerical techniques.



Introduction

– Diffusion describes the spreading of a continuous density field
\Phi(t,\vec{x})
(
e.g.
, ink concentration in a glass of water) that is governed by the following parabolic partial differential equation:

\frac{\partial\Phi(t,\vec{x})}{\partial t}=D\nabla^{2}\Phi(t,\vec{x}),

(1)

where
D
is the diffusion coefficient
Narasimhan 2009
.
While physically diffusion originates from the random Brownian motion of the microscopic constituents, its universal character can be better appreciated from a macroscopic perspective where it can be derived by combining the
continuity equation
,
\partial_{t}\Phi(t,\vec{x})+\nabla\cdot\vec{j}(t,\vec{x})=0
, with the phenomenological Fick’s law for the flux,
\vec{j}(t,\vec{x})=-D\nabla\Phi(t,\vec{x})

and 1855
.

The diffusion equation (
1
) is widely used in physical, biological, geological, and social sciences. It is nevertheless well known, and first encountered in the so-called
heat conduction paradox

Joseph and Preziosi 1989
;
Jou et al. 1988
;
Chandrasekharaiah 1986
, that the diffusion equation does not correctly capture short-time dynamics, as it neglects the time lag required to establish a steady current
\vec{j}
in a volume element once a gradient
\nabla\Phi
has been imposed across it.

The Maxwell-Cattaneo (MC) model
Cattaneo 1948
;
Maxwell 1867
provides a simple solution to this problem
Vernotte 1958
by extending Fick’s law to a more general form:

\left(1+\tau\partial_{t}\right)\vec{j}(t,\vec{x})=-D\nabla\Phi(t,\vec{x}),

(2)

where
\tau
is a relaxation time-scale characterizing the onset of local thermal equilibrium. Combined with the continuity equation, Eq. (
2
) yields to a hyperbolic partial differential equation:

\tau\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi(t,\vec{x})}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial\Phi(t,\vec{x})}{\partial t}=D\nabla^{2}\Phi(t,\vec{x}),

(3)

that is known in the literature as the telegrapher
Heaviside 1894
or
k
-gap equation
Baggioli et al. 2020a
, and that reduces to the diffusion equation (
1
) only for times
t\gg\tau
.

Eq. (
3
) predicts the emergence of propagating excitations for times
t\ll\tau
with speed
v_{0}^{2}=D/\tau
. This phenomenon is experimentally confirmed in several circumstances, including shear waves in liquid systems
Nosenko et al. 2006
;
Jiang et al. 2025
;
Bai et al. 2025
, second sound in solids
Chester 1963
, and density fluctuations in cold atoms
Brown et al. 2019
.

Motivated by the experimental progresses in heavy-ion collisions
Romatschke and Romatschke 2019
;
Gale et al. 2013
, relativistic hydrodynamics
Kovtun 2012
has emerged as a universal effective framework to capture late-time and long-wavelength dynamics in relativistic systems near thermal equilibrium. In this language, the continuity equation stems directly from the presence of a U(1) global symmetry, leading to the conservation of a four-current
J^{\mu}
with
\mu=t,\vec{x}
. Importantly, diffusion arises also in neutral relativistic fluids where it describes the dynamics of shear fluctuations related to the stress tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
and governed by the shear viscosity
\eta

Kovtun 2012
. Also in this context, in order to retain causality, the diffusion equation has to be modified within the so-called Israel-Stewart (IS) formalism
Israel and Stewart 1979
which can somehow be thought as the tensorial version of the MC model.

Both the MC and IS frameworks can be successfully embedded in field theory language
Jain and Kovtun 2024
;
Hongo et al. 2024
(see also
Baggioli et al. 2020b
) and are part of a larger program (sometimes labeled
quasi-hydrodynamics

Martinoia 2024
) aimed to extend hydrodynamics introducing non-hydrodynamic modes. Aside from the fundamental perspective, the introduction of non-hydro modes is fundamental near critical points
Stephanov and Yin 2018
;
Pradeep et al. 2022
and at shorter timescales
Ke and Yin 2023
.

The AdS-CFT correspondence, or in short ‘
holography
’, has played and keeps playing a major role in the development of relativistic hydrodynamics
Policastro et al. 2002
, in particular when applied to strongly coupled systems such as the quark-gluon plasma
Casalderrey-Solana et al. 2014
. While it is rather straightforward to derive
vanilla
first-order relativistic hydrodynamics from holography, the study of quasi-hydrodynamics has emerged only more recently (
e.g.
,
Grozdanov et al. 2019
;
Baggioli et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2025
;
Martinoia 2024
;
Brattan et al. 2024
). In fact, despite all these developments, a simple holographic model exhibiting the physical content and dynamics of the MC model is still missing (see nevertheless
Chen and Lucas 2017
;
Davison et al. 2023
;
Brattan et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025
). In this work, we provide a simple holographic dual of the MC model and we verify with numerical and analytical techniques their equivalence.

Maxwell-Cattaneo model

– We consider a relativistic system with U(1) global symmetry and a conserved current
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0
. After defining the four-velocity
u^{\mu}
, with proper normalization
u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1
, the current can be parameterized as
J^{\mu}=nu^{\mu}+\mathcal{J}^{\mu}
where
n
is the charge density and
\mathcal{J}
the corresponding flux. The flux obeys the following equation
Jain and Kovtun 2024

\mathcal{J}^{\mu}+\tau\Delta^{\mu\nu}u^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{J}^{\nu}=-D\Delta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}n+\dots

(4)

where external sources have been set to vanish and nonlinear terms have been neglected. Here,
\Delta^{\mu\nu}\equiv\eta^{\mu\nu}+u^{\mu}u^{\nu}
, is the spatial projection operator. Moreover, the relation between the charge density
n
and the chemical potential
\mu
,
\chi\equiv\partial n/\partial\mu
with
\chi
the charge susceptibility, has been used. In the limit of
\tau\rightarrow 0
, Eq. (
4
) reduces to the standard hydrodynamic constitutive equation
Kovtun 2012
.

Within this model, the retarded correlation functions of density
n\equiv J^{t}
and transverse-flux
J_{\perp}^{i}
can be easily computed
Jain and Kovtun 2024
,

\displaystyle G^{R}_{nn}(\omega,k)=\frac{-D\chi k^{2}}{-{\rm i}\omega(1-{\rm i}\omega\tau)-Dk^{2}},

(5)

\displaystyle G^{R}_{J_{\perp}^{i}J_{\perp}^{j}}=\frac{{\rm i}\omega D\chi}{1-{\rm i}\omega\tau}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right),

(6)

where
\omega,k
are respectively the frequency and the wave-vector. From the above equations, the low-energy excitations in the two sectors are given by:

-{\rm i}\omega(1-{\rm i}\omega\tau)-Dk^{2}=0,\qquad\omega=-{\rm i}/\tau,

(7)

with the telegrapher equation governing the fluctuation of the charge density
n
.

The MC model just outlined can be formalized in field theory language using the Schwinger-Keldysh (SK) formalism
Liu and Glorioso 2018
. This is achieved by writing down a SK effective action
S_{\text{eff}}
that is a functional of the gauge-invariant combinations
B_{a\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\varphi_{a}+\mathcal{A}_{a\mu}
,
B_{r\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\varphi_{r}+\mathcal{A}_{r\mu}
. Here, we have introduced two sets of Stueckelberg fields
\varphi_{a},\varphi_{r}
and the corresponding external gauge fields
\mathcal{A}_{a\mu},\,\mathcal{A}_{r\mu}
. Despite this is enough in the case of pure diffusion
Liu and Glorioso 2018
, for the MC model, additional spatial vector fields
V_{ai}
,
V_{ri}
have to be integrated in. Skipping all details, an choosing
u^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0)
, the SK effective Lagrangian for the MC model is given by (see
Jain and Kovtun 2024
;
Liu et al. 2025
for details)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}[\varphi_{a},\varphi_{r},V_{ai},V_{ri}]

\displaystyle=\chi B_{a0}B_{r0}-\chi_{V}V_{ai}V_{ri}+{\rm i}T\sigma_{0}C_{ai}^{2}-\sigma_{0}C_{ai}\partial_{0}C_{ri}\,,

(8)

with
C_{ai}\equiv B_{ai}+\chi_{V}\gamma\,V_{ai}
. In the limit
\chi_{V}\rightarrow 0
, Eq. (
8
) reduces to the standard action for diffusion
Liu and Glorioso 2018
. In general, this action corresponds to the MC model with
\tau=\sigma_{0}\gamma^{2}/\chi_{V}
.

Holographic dual

– In the gravitational side, we consider the following five-dimensional action

S=\int\differential^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{4}F_{MN}F^{MN}+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{4}F_{MN}F^{MN}\right)^{2}\right],

(9)

where
F=dA
is the field strength of a bulk gauge field
A_{M}
, and we assume
\alpha>0
. In the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, (
9
) reduces to the standard Maxwell bulk action. We work in the probe limit. The background metric is fixed to

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{u^{2}}\left(-f(u)dt^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}+\frac{du^{2}}{f(u)}\right),

(10)

where
(t,\vec{x})
are (3+1)-dimensional spacetime coordinates in the dual field theory and
u
is the radial coordinate. Here,
f(u)=1-u^{4}/u_{h}^{4}
and we set
L=1
. The Hawking temperature is
T=1/(\pi u_{h})
.

Our background solution is found by taking a radial ansatz
A_{t}(u)
and solving the simple differential equation,
A_{t}^{\prime}(u)+u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{3}=-\rho u
where
{}^{\prime}\equiv d/du
and
\rho
corresponds to the background charge density in the dual field theory. Details of the background solution are provided in the
Supplementary Material
(SM).

Natsuume 2015

Towards the quasi-hydrodynamic regime

– In Fig.
1
, we show the lowest QNMs in the longitudinal spectrum at zero wave-vector,
k=0
, upon dialing the dimensionless parameter
\hat{\alpha}\equiv\alpha\rho^{2}
. At any value of
\hat{\alpha}
, we observe a hydrodynamic mode
\omega=0
(
\bigstar
symbol) at the origin of the complex plane. This is the diffusive mode emerging from the conservation of the U(1) current. In the
\hat{\alpha}=0
limit (darker color), that coincides with the Maxwell bulk action usually considered in holography, the lowest non-hydro excitations are a pair of complex modes with frequency
\omega=-({\rm i}\pm 1)2\pi T
. These modes are not well-separated from the other non-hydro modes, forming a so-called
Christmas tree
. In this limit, the description of the dual field theory is not provided by the MC model and does not fit in any quasi-hydrodynamic framework.

The situation changes when
\hat{\alpha}
is increased (towards green color in Fig.
1
). More precisely, the pair of complex modes moves down into the complex plane while a purely imaginary mode
\omega=-{\rm i}/\tau
climbs along the imaginary axes. At a critical value of
\hat{\alpha}
, this mode becomes the lowest non-hydro mode, it gets close to the hydro one and parametrically separated from the other non-hydro modes. This defines the regime where quasi-hydrodynamics applies and where the dual dynamics are described by the MC model.

Numerical & analytical tests of the equivalence

– In Fig.
2
, we present the dispersion of the lowest QNMs as a function of the dimensionless wave-vector
k/(\pi T)
for three different values of
\hat{\alpha}
. At
\hat{\alpha}=0
(black symbols), we observe the expected diffusive behavior
\omega=-{\rm i}Dk^{2}+\dots
where
D=\sigma_{0}/\chi
. On the other hand, at finite (and large enough)
\hat{\alpha}

\omega^{2}+{\rm i}\omega/\tau=v_{0}^{2}k^{2},\qquad v_{0}^{2}=D/\tau,

(12)

whose solutions are displayed with dashed lines in Fig.
2
, confirming the validity of the MC model also at
k\neq 0
.

In order to provide an analytic proof of the equivalence between the holographic model and the MC model, following Refs.
Chen and Lucas 2017
;
Grozdanov et al. 2019
, we have performed an analytical derivation of the equations for the hydrodynamic boundary fluctuations (see SM). This analysis confirms the validity of the MC description and yields an analytic prediction for the relaxation time
\tau
, that is valid within the quasi-hydrodynamic limit,

\tau=X(u_{h})Y(u_{h}),

(13)

with:

\displaystyle Y(u)=\int^{u}_{0}ds\frac{1}{f(s)X(s)}\left(1-\frac{f^{\prime}(s)X(s)}{f^{\prime}(u_{h})X(u_{h})}\right)^{\prime},

\displaystyle X(u)\equiv\frac{1}{u}+\alpha u^{3}A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{2}.

(14)

Moreover, the Einstein relation

Holographic Schwinger-Keldysh effective action

– To further validate the equivalence between our minimal holographic model and the Maxwell-Cattaneo (MC) framework, we employ holographic Schwinger-Keldysh (holoSK) techniques
Glorioso et al. 2018
;
Crossley et al. 2016
;
Haehl et al. 2016
, which have recently been used to study quasi-hydrodynamic systems
Baggioli et al. 2023
and extended to incorporate non-hydrodynamic modes
Liu et al. 2025
.

We introduce a new methodology for incorporating non-hydrodynamic modes into the holoSK formalism, based on constructing bulk solutions localized at specific characteristic frequencies (see
Sakai and Sugimoto 2005
for a similar procedure in the context of holographic QCD). A detailed description of this novel approach and the corresponding technical derivations is provided in the SM.

Using this framework, we analytically derive the boundary Schwinger-Keldysh action corresponding to our holographic model, Eq. (
9
), and demonstrate that it precisely matches the effective field theory description of the Maxwell-Cattaneo model proposed in
Jain and Kovtun 2024
. This agreement constitutes the final and conclusive validation of the proposed duality between our holographic setup and the MC model.

We note that an alternative method for incorporating non-hydrodynamic modes into the holoSK framework was recently proposed in
Liu et al. 2025
. Whether this approach is fully equivalent to ours remains an open question. However, we have verified that the expression for the relaxation time
\tau
derived in that work is less accurate when compared to our numerical results and to our analytical formula, Eq. (
13
).

Outlook

– In this work, we have constructed a simple holographic dual for the relativistic Maxwell-Cattaneo model. The equivalence between the gravitational framework and the dual effective description is verified combining numerical and analytical methods. These analyses not only validate the structure of the MC model in the dual field theory but also provide analytical approximated formulae in terms of holographic data for all the parameters appearing therein.

Interestingly, from a bulk perspective, this is achieved by introducing higher-order terms in the Maxwell field strength
F_{MN}
, that can be tought as a UV completion of the standard bulk Maxwell action. This brings an interesting correspondence between UV completion terms in the bulk action and in the dual hydrodynamic description, displaying striking analogies with the case of Israel-Stewart formalism that requires Gauss-Bonnet corrections in the bulk
Grozdanov et al. 2019
.

We anticipate that our construction is clearly not the only one admitting a dual description in terms of the MC model, but possibly the simplest. The emergence of a slow non-hydro mode with purely imaginary frequency is generally achieved by allowing for a radial coordinate dependent value of
\delta\mathcal{L}/\delta F^{2}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the bulk Lagrangian and
F^{2}
the square of the bulk field strength. It is therefore not surprising that more complex scenarios such as the DBI action
Chen and Lucas 2017
or dilaton couplings
Davison et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2025
lead to similar conclusions.

Having a simple holographic dual of the MC model at hand extends the applicability of holographic methods to shorter time-scales, with possible important implications to the study of strongly coupled fluids such as the quark-gluon plasma
Ke and Yin 2023
, and in particular their non-equilibrium physics. For example, it allows the study of the interactions between the hydrodynamic fields and the non-hydro variables, with potential important physical implications (see for example
Abbasi et al. 2024
).

SM1
Details of the holographic model

The background profile for the bulk gauge field
A_{t}(\mu)
satisfies the nonlinear equation

A_{t}^{\prime}(u)+u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{3}=-\rho u,

(S1)

where
{}^{\prime}\equiv d/du
and
\rho
corresponds to the background charge density in the dual field theory. Among the three solutions of Eq. (
S1
), the one regular at the UV boundary
u=0
is implicitly given by

A_{t}^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\sqrt[3]{2}\left(\sqrt{3}\sqrt{\alpha^{3}u^{6}\left(27\alpha\rho^{2}u^{6}+4\right)}+9\alpha^{2}\rho u^{6}\right)^{2/3}-2\sqrt[3]{3}\alpha u^{2}}{6^{2/3}\alpha u^{3}\sqrt[3]{\sqrt{3}\sqrt{\alpha^{3}u^{6}\left(27\alpha\rho^{2}u^{6}+4\right)}+9\alpha^{2}\rho u^{6}}}.

(S2)

Close to the UV boundary (
u=0
), the solution behaves as

A_{t}(u)\approx\mu-\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+\dots

(S3)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential in the dual field theory.

On top of this background solution, we consider the following fluctuations:

A_{\mu}(u,t,x)=A_{\mu}(u)+\delta A_{\mu}(u)e^{-{\rm i}\omega t+{\rm i}kx}.

(S4)

For convenience, we introduce the gauge-invariant longitudinal field as

E_{L}(u)\equiv k\delta A_{t}(u)+\omega\delta A_{x}(u),

(S5)

and obtain the equation of motion

E_{L}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{E_{L}^{\prime}}{\omega^{2}-fGk^{2}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{1}{u}\bigg\{\omega^{2}\big(1+u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime}(3A_{t}^{\prime}+2uA_{t}^{\prime\prime})\big)-fGk^{2}\frac{1+3u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime}(3A_{t}^{\prime}+2uA_{t}^{\prime\prime})}{1-3u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime 2}}\bigg\}\right]+\frac{\omega^{2}-fGk^{2}}{f^{2}}E_{L}=0,

(S6)

where

G=\frac{1-u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime 2}}{1-3u^{4}\alpha A_{t}^{\prime 2}}.

(S7)

Since we are interested in the retarded Green’s functions for the dual current operator, we impose the ingoing boundary condition at the black hole horizon as

E_{L}(u)=(u-u_{H})^{-{\rm i}\frac{\omega}{4\pi T}}{E}_{\rm reg}(u),

(S8)

where
E_{\rm reg}(u)
is a regular function at
u=u_{h}
. Solving the equation of motion with the ingoing boundary condition, we read off the leading and sub-leading coefficients in the expansion near the AdS boundary (
u=0
),

E_{L}(u)=E_{L}^{(0)}(\omega,k)+E_{L}^{(2)}(\omega,k)u^{2}+\cdots,

(S9)

and compute the retarded Green’s function for the charge density
Policastro et al. 2002

G_{nn}^{R}(\omega,k)=\frac{2k^{2}}{\omega^{2}-k^{2}}\frac{E_{L}^{(2)}(\omega,k)}{E_{L}^{(0)}(\omega,k)}.

(S10)

Note that the retarded Green’s function for the current is given by
G_{JJ}^{R}=(\omega^{2}/k^{2})G_{nn}^{R}
.
The QNMs can be extracted by computing the frequencies at which the leading coefficient vanishes,
E_{L}^{(0)}=0
, corresponding to the poles of the retarded Green’s function. Finally, the optical conductivity is given by

\sigma(\omega)=\frac{G_{JJ}^{R}(\omega,k=0)}{{\rm i}\omega},

(S11)

and the DC conductivity is defined as
\sigma_{0}=\lim_{\omega\to 0}{\rm Re}[\sigma(\omega)]
.

X_{1}=-\frac{L}{u}\left(1+\alpha\frac{u^{4}}{L^{4}}A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\right)\,,\quad X_{3}=-\frac{L}{u}\left(1+3\alpha\frac{u^{4}}{L^{4}}A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\right)\,,\quad X_{5}=\frac{L}{u}\left(1+\alpha\frac{u^{4}}{L^{4}}A_{t}^{\prime}(u)^{2}\right)\,.

(S21)

When
\omega\rightarrow 0,k\rightarrow 0
, in the region near the AdS boundary, the solution to Eq. (
S20
) takes the following form

\begin{split}&\delta A_{t}(u,x^{\mu})=\delta a_{t}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{t}(x^{\mu})\Phi_{1}(u)+\mathcal{O}(\omega,k)\,,\\
&\delta A_{x}(u,x^{\mu})=\delta a_{x}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{x}(x^{\mu})\Phi_{2}(u)+\mathcal{O}(\omega,k)\,,\end{split}

(S22)

where

\Phi_{1}=\int_{0}^{u}ds\frac{1}{X_{3}(s)}\,,\quad\Phi_{2}=\int_{0}^{u}ds\frac{1}{f(s)X_{5}(s)}\,.

(S23)

After substituting Eq. (
S22
) into Eq. (
S20c
), we obtain the conservation equation

\omega\delta j_{t}+k\delta j_{x}=0\,.

(S24)

Note that, near the boundary,
\delta A_{x}
in Eq. (
S22
) has a logarithmic divergence because the integrand of
\Phi_{2}
diverges as
u\rightarrow u_{h}
, while
\delta A_{t}
is regular. We rewrite Eq. (
S22
) as

\begin{split}&\delta A_{t}\approx\delta a_{t}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{t}(x^{\mu})\phi_{1}(u)\,,\\
&\delta A_{x}\approx\delta a_{x}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{x}(x^{\mu})\phi_{2}(u)+\frac{\delta j_{x}(x^{\mu})\ln(f(u))}{f^{\prime}(u_{h})X_{5}(u_{h})}\,,\end{split}

(S25)

where we introduced regular functions

\begin{split}\phi_{1}(u)&\equiv\Phi_{1}(u)\,,\\
\phi_{2}(u)&\equiv\Phi_{1}(u)-\frac{\ln(f(u))}{f^{\prime}(u_{h})X_{5}(u_{h})}=\int^{u}_{0}ds\frac{1}{f(s)X_{5}(s)}\left(1-\frac{f^{\prime}(s)X_{5}(s)}{f^{\prime}(u_{h})X_{5}(u_{h})}\right)\,.\end{split}

(S26)

On the other hand, near the black hole horizon, the solution is

\begin{split}&\delta A_{t}=\delta a_{t}^{(H,1)}(u,x^{\mu})+\delta a_{t}^{(H,2)}(u,x^{\mu})e^{-\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{4\pi T}\ln(f(u))}\,,\\
&\delta A_{x}=\delta a_{x}^{(H,1)}(u,x^{\mu})+\delta a_{x}^{(H,2)}(u,x^{\mu})e^{-\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{4\pi T}\ln(f(u))}\,,\end{split}

(S27)

where
\delta a_{\mu}^{(H,1)}
is a pure gauge solution, and
\delta a_{\mu}^{(H,2)}
is an incoming solution with
\mu=t,x
. Near the black hole horizon, the incoming solution has a logarithmic behavior

e^{-\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{4\pi T}\ln(f(u))}\approx 1-\frac{{\rm i}\omega}{4\pi T}\ln(f(u))+\mathcal{O}(\omega^{2})=1+\frac{\ln(f(u))}{4\pi T}\partial_{t}+\ldots\,.

(S28)

We rewrite Eq. (
S27
) as

\begin{split}&\delta A_{t}\approx\delta a_{t}^{(H,1)}+\delta a_{t}^{(H,2)}+\frac{\ln(f(u))}{4\pi T}\partial_{t}\delta a_{t}^{(H,2)}\,,\\
&\delta A_{x}\approx\delta a_{x}^{(H,1)}+\delta a_{x}^{(H,2)}+\frac{\ln(f(u))}{4\pi T}\partial_{t}a_{x}^{(H,2)}\,.\end{split}

(S29)

In the intermediate region, we match regular the terms and logarithmic terms from Eq. (
S25
) and Eq. (
S29
) separately:

\begin{split}&\delta a_{t}^{(H,1)}(u,x^{\mu})+\delta a_{t}^{(H,2)}(u,x^{\mu})=\delta a_{t}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{t}(x^{\mu})\phi_{1}(u)\,,\qquad\delta a_{t}^{(H,2)}(u,x^{\mu})=0\,,\\
&\delta a_{x}^{(H,1)}(u,x^{\mu})+\delta a_{x}^{(H,2)}(u,x^{\mu})=\delta a_{x}(x^{\mu})+\delta j_{x}(x^{\mu})\phi_{2}(u)\,,\qquad\partial_{t}\delta a_{x}^{(H,2)}=\frac{4\pi T}{f^{\prime}(u_{h})X_{5}(u_{h})}\delta j_{x}(x^{\mu})\,.\\
\end{split}

(S30)

By taking the appropriate derivative and linear combination, we arrive at

\partial_{t}\delta j_{x}-\frac{\phi_{1}(u_{h})}{\phi_{2}(u_{h})}\partial_{x}\delta j_{t}=-\frac{1}{\phi_{2}(u_{h})}\left(\partial_{t}\delta a_{x}-\partial_{x}\delta a_{t}\right)-\frac{1}{\phi_{2}(u_{h})X_{5}(u_{h})}\delta j_{x}

(S31)

with the pure gauge solution satisfying

\partial_{x}\delta a_{t}^{(H,1)}-\partial_{t}\delta a_{x}^{(H,1)}=0\,.

(S32)

Using the conservation equation, Eq. (
S24
), and the following identifications

v_{0}^{2}=\frac{\phi_{1}(u_{h})}{\phi_{2}(u_{h})}\,,\quad\chi=\frac{1}{\phi_{2}(u_{h})}\,,\quad\tau=X_{5}(u_{h})\phi_{2}(u_{h})\,,

(S33)

we obtain the final equations for the fluctuations:

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\delta j^{t}+\partial_{x}\delta j^{x}=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}\delta j^{x}+v^{2}\partial_{x}\delta j^{t}=-\chi\left(\partial_{t}\delta a_{x}-\partial_{x}\delta a_{t}\right)-\frac{1}{\tau}\delta j^{x}\,,\end{split}

(S34)

where
\partial_{t}\delta a_{x}=E_{x}
and
\partial_{x}\delta a_{t}=\partial_{x}\delta\mu
.

The perturbation
\delta A_{M}

While each hydro mode satisfies

Near the AdS boundaries, we have

\displaystyle\delta A_{\mu}^{\rm h}(r\to\infty_{s},x^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=B_{s\mu}(x^{\alpha})+\cdots+\frac{\mathfrak{J}_{s\mu}^{\rm h}(x^{\alpha})}{r^{2}}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\delta A_{\mu}^{\rm nh}(r\to\infty_{s},x^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{s\mu}(x^{\alpha})+\cdots+\frac{\mathfrak{J}_{s\mu}^{\rm nh}(x^{\alpha})}{r^{2}}+\cdots

Here,

The solution for

Regularization Prescription for the Mixing Between Nonlocal Gluon and Quark Operators
Yao Ji, Zhuoyi Pang, Fei Yao, and Jian-Hui Zhang

Abstract
It is well-known that in the study of mixing between nonlocal gluon and quark bilinear operators there exists an ambiguity when relating coordinate space and momentum space results, which can be conveniently resolved through Mellin moments matching in both spaces. In this work, we show that this ambiguity is due to the lack of a proper regularization prescription of the singularity that arises when the separation between the gluon/quark fields approaches zero. We then demonstrate that dimensional regularization resolves this issue and yields consistent results in both coordinate and momentum space. This prescription is also compatible with lattice extractions of parton distributions from nonlocal operators.

1
Introduction

Parton physics provides a useful framework for studying the inner structure of hadrons through their fundamental constituents — quarks and gluons. It is naturally formulated in light-front (LF) quantization, where nonlocal quark and gluon operators along lightlike separations (LF operators) play a crucial role. In the collinear case, these operators define fundamental parton quantities such as parton distribution functions (PDFs), generalized parton distributions (GPDs) and distribution amplitudes (DAs). A notable feature of collinear parton distributions is the mixing between flavor-singlet quark operators and gluon operators under QCD evolution. As the quark and gluon LF operators have different mass dimensions, the mixing coefficient has to incorporate the lightlike separation of the nonlocal operators to compensate for the dimensional mismatch. This mixing pattern has been extensively studied in both coordinate and momentum space (see, e.g.,
(
Ji 1997
)
for calculation in momentum space,
(
Blumlein et al. 1997
;
Balitsky and Braun 1989
)
for calculation in coordinate space, and
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
;
Diehl 2003
for reviews), revealing important insights into the scale dependence of collinear parton distributions. However, there exists a long-standing ambiguity when relating coordinate space and momentum space results in the gluon-in-quark channel. Since the gluon LF operator has mass dimension 4 whereas the quark LF operator has mass dimension 3, the mixing coefficient is inversely proportional to the lightcone distance. When Fourier transforming the coordinate space result to momentum space, a conventional approach is to replace the former with an integral form, but with an indefinite integration limit
Yao et al. 2023
. This leads to ambiguities in the Fourier transformed result, which can be removed by requiring the Mellin moments to be identical before and after Fourier transform. In contrast, the quark-in-gluon channel has a mixing coefficient proportional to the lightlike separation, and thus is regular when approaching the local limit and introduces no ambiguities in Fourier transform.

On the other hand, significant progress has been made in recent years on calculating parton physics from lattice QCD (see
Cichy and Constantinou 2019
;
Ji et al. 2021
;
Constantinou et al. 2021
for a review). This involves nonlocal quark and gluon operators along spacelike separations, known as quasi-light-front (quasi-LF) operators. Under an infinite Lorentz boost, the quasi-LF operators approach the LF operators defining parton physics. The flavor-singlet quark and gluon quasi-LF operators also mix in a similar way as the LF operators, but now the mismatch in mass dimensions is compensated for by the spacelike separation. A systematic study of the factorization and mixing of the quasi-LF operators has been given both in coordinate
Yao et al. 2023
;
Balitsky et al. 2020
and in momentum
Yao et al. 2023
;
Ma et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2019
space, where a similar ambiguity in Fourier transform has been observed in the gluon-in-quark channel.

In this work, we show that the ambiguities above are due to the lack of a proper regularization prescription for the singularity that arises when the separation between gluon/quark fields in the nonlocal operators approaches zero. We then demonstrate that dimensional regularization (DR)
resolves this issue and yields consistent results in both coordinate and momentum space. This prescription is also compatible with lattice extractions of parton distributions from
nonlocal operators.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we review the mixing of nonlocal gluon correlators with singlet quark correlators, in the context of evolution and factorization. In Sec.
3
, we compare the factorization formulas derived from the operator product expansion (OPE) and explicit calculations of nonlocal correlators. We also point out an deficiency in the Mellin moments matching approach. In Sec.
4
, we give a physically viable prescription for the singularity at zero field separation, with which one can obtain consistent results in coordinate and momentum space. Finally we conclude in Sec.
5
.

2
Mixing of nonlocal gluon and quark operators

Throughout this paper, we focus on the mixing between gluon and quark nonlocal operators, adopting the convention in Ref.
Yao et al. 2023

\displaystyle{O}_{q}^{s}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[O_{q}(z_{1},z_{2})\mp O_{q}(z_{2},z_{1})\right],\hskip 20.00003ptO_{q}(z_{1},z_{2})=\bar{\psi}(z_{1})\Gamma[z_{1},z_{2}]\psi(z_{2}),

The mixing between gluon and quark LF operators has been well studied in their scale evolution
(
Balitsky and Braun 1989
)
:

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}O_{g}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{z_{12}}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{1}dv\,\theta(1-u-v)\mathcal{K}_{gq}(u,v)O_{q}^{\text{s}}(z_{12}^{u},z_{21}^{v})+\cdots,

(2)

where
``\cdots"
denotes the gluon-to-gluon channel contribution and is irrelevant to the discussion in the present work.
z_{i}
denotes the LF coordinate, and
z^{u}_{12}=(1-u)z_{1}+uz_{2}
. The one-loop results have been available for a long time (see e.g.
(
Balitsky and Braun 1989
)
). Based on constraints from conformal symmetry, the two-loop evolution kernels are also known
(
Belitsky et al. 2000
;
Braun et al. 2019
)
. Sandwiching the LF operators between non-forward hadron states and performing a Fourier transform, the above equation yields the evolution equation for the gluon GPD:

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}F_{g}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})=\int_{-1}^{1}dy\,K_{gq}^{\prime}(x,y,\xi)F_{q}^{s}(y,\xi,\Delta^{2})+\cdots.

(3)

where
x,\xi,\Delta^{2}

Due to the
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
factor in Eq. (
2

z_{12}\to 0
, the momentum space evolution kernel
K_{gq}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
3
) is not unique. We can formally write

K_{gq}^{\prime}(x,y,\xi)=-i\int_{a}^{x}dx^{\prime}K_{gq}(x^{\prime},y,\xi),

(4)

with

K_{gq}(x,y,\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}dudv\,\theta(1-u-v)\mathcal{K}_{gq}(u,v)\delta((1-u-v)y+\xi(v-u)-x)\,,

where
a
is an arbitrary lower integration limit
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
. A popular treatment in the literature is to separate
K^{\prime}_{gq}(x,y,\xi)
into different kinematic regions, and determine
a
by imposing physical constraints
(
Balitsky and Radyushkin 1997
)
. For example, in the DGLAP region, we have
y>x>\xi
, thus one can either choose
a=\xi
or
a=y
.

In the case of quasi-LF operators, the mixing between gluons and quarks is often formulated in the form of a factorization formula
(
Yao et al. 2023
)
:

O_{g}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{z_{12}}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha d\beta\,\theta(1-\alpha-\beta)C_{gq}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})O_{q}^{\text{s, l.t.}}(z_{12}^{\alpha},z_{21}^{\beta})+\cdots,

(5)

where
z_{i}
parameterize spacelike vectors, the superscript
``\text{l.t.}"
denotes the leading-twist projection of the operator.

C_{gq}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})
is the matching kernel in coordinate space. It leads to the factorization between gluon quasi-GPD and quark GPD
(
Yao et al. 2023
;
Ma et al. 2023
)
:

\mathbb{H}_{g}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2},P^{z})=\int_{-1}^{1}dy\,\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})H_{q}^{\text{s}}(y,\xi,\Delta^{2},\mu)+\cdots,

(6)

where
\mathbb{H}_{g}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2},P^{z})
is the gluon quasi-GPD and
H_{q}^{\text{s}}
is the quark GPD,
\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})
is the matching kernel in momentum space which can be obtained from
C_{gq}
as follows:

\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})=\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{z_{12}}\int_{\alpha\beta}C_{gq}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2}),

(7)

where we have used the shorthand notation

\int_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha d\beta\,\theta(1-\alpha-\beta)e^{iz_{12}P^{z}\big(x+\xi(\alpha-\beta)-(1-\alpha-\beta)y\big)}\,.

Note that Eq. (
7
) involves the Fourier transform of both
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
and
\frac{\text{ln}z_{12}^{2}}{z_{12}}
, which, without specifying a proper prescription for the singularity at
z_{12}=0
, will lead to an ambiguous/singular result. One can also calculate the momentum evolution of
\mathbb{C}_{gq}
as

P^{z}\frac{d}{dP^{z}}\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})=P^{z}\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi}\int_{\alpha\beta}i(x+\xi(\alpha-\beta)-(1-\alpha-\beta)y)C_{gq}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2}).

(8)

For similar reasons, the result of Eq. (
8
) is ambiguous as well.

From the discussion above, the ambiguity in the mixing channel contribution in momentum space shares a similar origin for both quasi-LF operators and LF operators: the absence of a well-defined prescription for handling the singularity at
z_{12}=0
in Fourier transform.
In the following, we will focus on the quasi-LF operators, since once a consistent regularization prescription for the singularity at
z_{12}=0
is established, the ambiguity in Eq. (
4
) can be eliminated in the same way, due to the factorization requirement in momentum space: the evolution in
\mu
must match the evolution in
P^{z}
.

3
The role of
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
in the Fourier transform

The factorization for the quasi-LF correlator
is derived at a nonzero separation of the gluon and quark fields.
However, as mentioned in the previous section, when Fourier transformed to momentum space, we need a well-defined prescription to regularize the pole at
z_{12}=0
. To illustrate the problem, we compare in Sec.
3.1
the difference between the traditional operator product expansion (OPE) approach and perturbative factorization in coordinate space. For simplicity, we use the forward gluon correlator as an example. In Sec.
3.2
, we point out the deficiency of a widely used approach: matching Mellin moments of the matching kernels in coordinate and momentum space.

3.1
Traditional OPE and factorization in coordinate space

Let us review the traditional OPE of the gluon quasi-LF correlator (forward case):

\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda)=(P^{z})^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-i\lambda)^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}C_{n-2}^{gq}(\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})a_{n-1}^{q,s}(\mu)+\cdots,

(9)

with
\lambda=z_{12}P^{z}
denoting the quasi-LF distance. The correlator
\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda)
is defined as:

\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda)=\big<P\big|O_{g}(z_{1},z_{2})\big|P\big>,

(10)

with
O_{g}(z_{1},z_{2})
given in Eq. (
2
). The polarization vector
\vec{S}
of external state is omitted for brevity.
We use the following definitions of quark singlet distribution and moment
a_{n}^{q,s}
:

\displaystyle f_{q}^{\text{s}}(y)=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\lambda_{12}}{2\pi}e^{-i\lambda_{12}P^{+}y}\big<P\big|O_{q}^{\text{s}}(\lambda_{12}n,0)\big|P\big>,\qquad a_{n}^{q,s}=\int_{-1}^{1}dyy^{n}f_{q}^{\text{s}}(y).

(11)

By defining:

C_{gq}^{\text{OPE}}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})=i\int\frac{d\lambda^{\prime}}{2\pi}e^{i\alpha\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-i\lambda^{\prime})^{n-1}}{(n-2)!}C_{n-2}^{gq}(\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2}),

we can rewrite Eq. (
9
) as
(
Izubuchi et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2019
)
:

\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}z_{12}}\int_{-1}^{1}d\alpha\,C_{gq}^{\text{OPE}}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})h_{q}^{s}(\alpha\lambda)+\cdots,

(12)

where we have used
\lambda=-z_{12}^{-}P^{+}
, with
P^{+}=\sqrt{2}P^{z}
in the collinear limit. Making a Fourier transform on both sides of Eq. (
12
), we obtain the factorization of the gluon quasi-PDF:

x\tilde{f}_{g}(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}dy\,\mathbb{C}_{gq}^{\text{OPE}}(x,y,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})f_{q}^{\text{s}}(y)+\cdots,

(13)

where

x\tilde{f}_{g}(x)=\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi P^{z}}e^{ixz_{12}P^{z}}\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda).

(14)

Thus, we have

\mathbb{C}_{gq}^{\text{OPE}}(x,y,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}})=P^{z}\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi}e^{ixz_{12}P^{z}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-iz_{12}P^{z})^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}C_{n-2}^{gq}(\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})y^{n-1},

(15)

which doesn’t include the Fourier transform of
\frac{1}{z_{12}}

On the other hand, for the factorization of the quasi-LF correlator, we have:

\tilde{h}_{g}(z_{12}^{2},\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{2}P^{z}}{z_{12}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-i\lambda)^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\alpha^{n-2}C_{gq}^{\text{coord}}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})a_{n-2}^{s}(\mu)+\cdots.

(16)

In Eq. (
16
), we have used the superscript
“coord”
to distinguish the matching kernel from that obtained from OPE:

C_{gq}^{\text{OPE}}
.
For the unpolarized case, the term
\propto\frac{1}{z_{12}}
in Eq. (
16

Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
16

\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha C_{gq}^{\text{coord}}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})=0
. Otherwise, we need to regularize the pole at
z_{12}=0
in Eq. (
16

\int_{\alpha\beta}C_{gq}^{\text{coord}}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})=0

The matching kernel
C_{gq}^{\text{coord}}
can be separated into the evolution part (
C_{gq}^{\text{evol}}
) and the finite part (
C_{gq}^{\text{fin}}

C_{gq}^{\text{coord}}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})=C_{gq}^{\text{fin}}(\alpha)+C_{gq}^{\text{evol}}(\alpha)\text{ln}\left(\frac{z_{12}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\right).

(17)

From explicit calculations

\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,C_{gq,h}^{\text{evol}}(\alpha)=0,

but the first moment of
C_{gq,h}^{\text{fin}}(\alpha)
is nonzero.

3.2
Matching of Mellin moments: deficiency

A conventional proposal to remove the ambiguities in the Fourier transform Eq. (
7
) is to match Mellin moments of the matching kernel in momentum space with those in coordinate space
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
:

\lim\limits_{\xi\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}dxx^{j}\mathscr{C}_{gq}\left(2\frac{x}{\xi}-1,\frac{2}{\xi}-1\right)=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha C_{gq}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})\frac{-\bar{\alpha}^{j+1}}{j+1},\quad j\geq 0\,,

(19)

where
\bar{\alpha}=1-\alpha
.
Eq. (
19
) is based on the relation between the evolution kernel in DGLAP and ERBL region (see Eq. (4.93) in
(
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
)
). Now, one replaces the r.h.s. with moments of the matching kernel in coordinate space, and solves
\mathscr{C}_{gq}
on the l.h.s., to all orders in
j
.
Here
\mathscr{C}_{gq}
can be either the evolution kernel
K_{gq}
(for GPD)
(
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
)
or the matching kernel (for pseudo-GPD)
(
Yao et al. 2023
)
.

However, such a method is not sufficient to eliminate all the ambiguities in the Fourier transform, due to the following two facts:

•

The l.h.s. in Eq. (
19
) can be calculated using Eq. (
4
) or Eq. (A.2) in
(
Yao et al. 2023
)
, setting the integration constant to zero one gets:

\lim\limits_{\xi\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}dxx^{j}\mathscr{C}_{gq}\left(2\frac{x}{\xi}-1,\frac{2}{\xi}-1\right)=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha C_{gq}(\alpha,\mu^{2}z_{12}^{2})\Big(\frac{-\bar{\alpha}^{j+1}}{j+1}+\frac{1}{j+1}\Big),\quad j\geq 0,

(20)

where the second term on the r.h.s. is responsible for the difference between traditional OPE and factorization in coordinate space. Comparing Eq. (
20
) with Eq. (
19
), one finds the matching formula implicitly assumes that the term
\propto\frac{1}{z_{12}}
(on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
16
)) does not contribute to the transformed result, which corresponds to setting a prescription for the pole
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
. However, its physical relevance is yet to be justified.

•

The solution
\mathscr{C}_{gq}
satisfying Eq. (
19
) is not unique, since one only fixes the DGLAP region in Eq. (
19
). Under the requirement of charge conjugation symmetry, one can easily construct inequivalent solutions at the convolution level.

4
Regularization prescription for the
\frac{1}{z_{12}}
pole

The matching kernels in coordinate space contain contributions singular as
z_{12}\rightarrow 0
, preventing us from getting a smooth local limit of the factorization. Correspondingly, the first Mellin moments of quasi-PDFs and quasi-GPDs are divergent. These divergences originate from the non-commutativity of the two limits:
P^{z}\rightarrow\infty
(in momentum space)/
z_{12}^{2}\rightarrow 0
(in coordinate space) and
\Lambda_{\rm UV}\rightarrow\infty
. In this section, we show that dimensional regularization (DR) with
d=4-2\epsilon
can properly treat these divergences, yielding a physically viable prescription for the singularity in practical calculations. We also discuss the principal-value prescription.

4.1
Recovery of a smooth local limit

For illustration purposes, we first consider the factorization of forward kinematics in coordinate space at one-loop, in
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme. For the unpolarized case, taking the local limit of the factorization Eq. (
5
) we get:

\lim_{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\big<P\big|O_{g,u}(z_{12},0)\big|P\big>\!=\!-2ia_{s}C_{F}\lim_{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\left[6\alpha-4+\delta(\alpha)-\text{ln}\left(\frac{z_{12}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\right)(2\alpha+\delta(\alpha))\right]

\times(1-\alpha)\big<P\big|O_{q,u}^{s,1}(0,0)\big|P\big>,

(21)

where
a_{s}=\alpha_{s}/(4\pi)
.
In the r.h.s. of Eq. (
21
), we have made a tree-level expansion
O_{q,u}^{\text{s}}(z_{12},0)=z_{12}O_{q,u}^{\text{s},1}(0,0)+\mathcal{O}(z_{12}^{3})
, using the fact that
O_{q,u}^{\text{s}}(z_{12},0)
is
odd
in
z_{12}
when sandwiched between identical external states, which is sufficient for our purpose at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
.
O_{q,u}^{\text{s},1}(0,0)
is the unpolarized quark operator with one covariant derivative.
The r.h.s. of Eq. (
21
) is at
\mathcal{O}(z_{12}^{0})
,
there still remains a term
\propto\text{ln}z_{12}^{2}
, which is singular as
z_{12}\rightarrow 0

\text{ln}\frac{z_{12}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\rightarrow\frac{(\frac{z_{12}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}})^{\epsilon}-1}{\epsilon}

in Eq. (
21

21
) has a well-defined local limit for positive
\epsilon

\big<P\big|O_{g,u}(0,0)\big|P\big>=-\frac{8ia_{s}C_{F}}{3\epsilon_{\text{UV}}}\big<P\big|O_{q,u}^{s,1}(0,0)\big|P\big>\,.

(22)

Physically, Eq. (
22
) is just the mixing of gluon average momentum with quark average momentum.
The coefficient of the UV pole in Eq. (
22

\big<P\big|O_{g,u}(0,0)\big|P\big>
.

For the polarized case,
O_{q,h}^{\text{s}}(z_{12},0)

in

5

\frac{1}{z_{12}}

z_{12}\rightarrow 0

We can use DR to regulate this divergence, the local limit of Eq. (

\lim_{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\big<P\big|O_{g,h}(z_{12},0)\big|P\big>=\lim_{z_{12}\rightarrow 0}\frac{6ia_{s}C_{F}(z_{12}^{2})^{\epsilon}}{z_{12}}g_{A},

(23)

The r.h.s. of Eq. (
23

\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\;C_{gq,h}^{\text{evol}}(\alpha)=0,
prior to taking the local limit. For
\epsilon>\frac{1}{2}

O_{g,h}(z_{12},0)
is
odd
in
z_{12}
.
The region of
\epsilon
can be further extended using

\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi}\frac{d}{dz_{12}}\Big(\frac{(z_{12}^{2})^{\epsilon}}{z_{12}}e^{iz_{12}x}\Big)=0\,.

.

We then consider the factorization of gluon quasi-PDF in momentum space.
The corresponding matching kernel can be obtained using Eq. (
7
) and

in
4-2\epsilon

\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}},\epsilon)=\int\frac{dz_{12}}{2\pi}\frac{(z_{12}^{2})^{\epsilon}}{z_{12}}\int_{\alpha\beta}C_{gq}(\alpha,\beta,\mu^{2},\epsilon),

Expanding it at

\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\xi,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{gq}(x,y,\frac{\mu}{P^{z}},\epsilon)=

\displaystyle a(x,y)\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left[1-\left(\frac{4(P^{z})^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon}\right]

\displaystyle+d(x,y,\epsilon)\big|x-y\big|^{1-2\epsilon}+e(x,y,\epsilon)\big|x\big|^{1-2\epsilon}+f(x,y,\epsilon)\big|x-y\big|^{-2\epsilon},

where

Taking the infinite momentum limit,
the

5
Conclusion

In this work, we present a systematic investigation of the mixing between nonlocal gluon operators and flavor-singlet quark operators for both lightlike and spacelike separations. Our analysis reveals that dimensional regularization provides a physically viable prescription for regularizing the pole at zero field separation, which also reproduces the expected mixing pattern of local operators. Through explicit calculations, we demonstrate that the coordinate space factorization, when treated with DR and Fourier transformed to momentum space, yields results consistent with those obtained from direct calculations in momentum space
1
1

1

(i.e., it is necessary to Fourier transform the general
d
-dimensional coordinate space coefficient function and then perform the
\epsilon
expansion at the very last step)
. Our work eliminates a long-standing conundrum in relating coordinate space and momentum space results for nonlocal operator mixing, and is expected to have a significant impact on lattice calculations of gluon PDFs (GPDs) and singlet quark PDFs (GPDs).

Renewing the Role of the Nursing Coordinator: Towards Modern and Effective Leadership in Healthcare
Authors: Gianluca Azzellino, Massimo De Martinis
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

It was interesting to read the research work by Skarstein et al. (2024) on the need for support expressed by the recently appointed nursing leaders. The authors deal with an extremely current topic of the difficulties encountered by nurses recently appointed to a leadership position and suggest several certainly effective actions to address the problem. The current system needs a thorough overhaul to ensure that future nurse coordinators are adequately prepared to meet the complex challenges of the modern healthcare sector. The figure of the nursing coordinator plays a crucial role in ensuring efficiency, quality and safety of care. However, the growing complexity of the healthcare system, combined with the new challenges posed by the global pandemic, highlighted the need for an evolution in the nursing leadership model.

The nursing profession is going through a period of crisis as it faces new challenges (Ginaldi et al. 2024; Azzellino and Martinis 2024). We must give it the opportunity and resources to renew itself and recognise new roles and responsibilities in our healthcare systems.

As Skarstein et al. (2024) point out, nurses newly appointed to leadership roles want to improve their leadership position and increase confidence in the role with targeted support. The nursing profession must engage in and embrace the concept of mentoring to provide newly appointed leaders with supportive environments (Lysfjord and Skarstein 2024; McNamara et al. 2014; Vitale 2018). Nursing coordinators are called upon to manage a wide range of responsibilities ranging from managing staff to supervising the quality of care, from scheduling shifts to resolving complex clinical problems. Despite this, they often lack adequate training and support tools to effectively address these challenges.

It is therefore imperative to introduce continuous training, support and development programmes, that include advanced skills in management, leadership, communication and problem‐solving. There is an urgent need to introduce mentorship programmes with experienced healthcare leaders and one‐on‐one coaching to provide personalised support and skill development. Furthermore, promoting a culture of distributed leadership is essential to harness the strengths of each nursing team member, fostering a collaborative and inclusive work environment.

Nursing leaders should be supported in their efforts to retain nurses and ensure high‐quality patient care (McNamara et al. 2014; Rokach, 2014). Another critical aspect concerns the need to formally recognise and adequately remunerate the role of nursing coordinators. Often, their duties go well beyond those stipulated in their contract, resulting in stress and the risk of burnout. Leadership that cultivates a collaborative and supportive work environment is associated with greater employee satisfaction (Bergstedt and Wei 2020). Furthermore, nursing leaders must be actively involved in decision‐making processes at the corporate and political levels. They must be allowed to actively participate in hospital decision‐making committees.

Abstract
Let S 0 = 0 , S n = X 1 + … + X n , n ≥ 1 , S_{0}=0,\quad S_{n}=X_{1}+...+X_{n},\ n\geq 1, be a random walk whose increments belong without centering to the domain of attraction of a stable law with scaling constants a n a_{n} , that provide convergence as n → ∞ n\rightarrow\infty of the distributions of the sequence { S n / a n , n = 1 , 2 , … } \left\{S_{n}/a_{n},n=1,2,...\right\} to this stable law. Let L r , n = min r ≤ m ≤ n ⁡ S m L_{r,n}=\min_{r\leq m\leq n}S_{m} be the minimum of the random walk on the interval [ r , n ] [r,n] . It is shown that lim r , k , n → ∞ 𝐏 ⁡ ( L r , n ≤ y ​ a k | S n ≤ t ​ a k , L 0 , n ≥ 0 ) , t ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) , \lim_{r,k,n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(L_{r,n}\leq ya_{k}|S_{n}\leq ta_{k},L_{0,n}\geq 0\right),\,t\in\left(0,\infty\right), can have five different expressions, the forms of which depend on the relationships between the parameters r , k r,k and n n . Key words : random walks, stable distributions, conditional limit theorems

Introduction

We consider a random walk

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{n}=X_{1}+...+X_{n},\ n\geq 1,

whose increments belong to the domain of attraction of a stable law. To give
a more detailed description of the random walk class we are interesting in
introduce the set

\mathcal{A}:=\{\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\},\,|\beta|<1\}\cup\{\alpha=1,\beta=0\}\cup\{\alpha=2,\beta=0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For a pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}
and a random variable
X
we
write
X\in\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
if the distribution of

X
belongs to the domain of attraction of a stable law with density
g_{\alpha,\beta}(x),x\in(-\infty,+\infty),
and the characteristic
function

G_{\alpha,\beta}(w)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{iwx}g_{\alpha.\beta}(x)\,dx=\exp\left\{-c|w|^{\,\alpha}\left(1-i\beta\frac{w}{|w|}\tan\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\right\},\ c>0,

and, in addition,
\mathbf{E}X=0
if this moment exists. This implies, in
particular, that there is an increasing sequence of positive numbers

a_{n}\ =\ n^{1/\alpha}\ell(n)

(1)

with a slowly varying at infinity sequence
\ell(1),\ell(2),\ldots,
such
that, as
n\rightarrow\infty

\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}},t\geq 0\right\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\left\{\mathcal{Y}=\left\{Y_{t},t\geq 0\right\}\right\},

(2)

where

\mathbf{E}e^{iwY_{t}}=G_{\alpha,\beta}(wt^{1/\alpha}),t\geq 0,

and the symbol
\Longrightarrow
stands for the weak convergence in the
space
D[0,\infty)
of càdlàg functions endowed with Skorokhod
topology. Observe that if
X_{n}\overset{d}{=}X\in\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}:\mathbf{=}\left\{1,2,...\right\}

then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}>0\right)=\rho=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}>0\right)\in(0,1).

We assume throughout the paper that the random walk under consideration
meets one of the following restrictions.

Condition A1.

The random variables
X_{n},n\in\mathbb{N},
are independent copies of a random variable
X\in\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
. Besides, the distribution of
X
is non-lattice.

Condition A2.
The law of
X
is absolutely
continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
,
and there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that the density
g_{n}(x):=\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\in dx)/dx
of
S_{n}
is
bounded (therefore,
g_{n}(x)\in L^{\infty}
).

Remark 1

We remind that the requirement
g_{n}(x)\in L^{\infty}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
is the standard necessary and sufficient condition for the
uniform convergence of the rescaled density
g_{n}(x/a_{n})/a_{n}
to the
density of
Y_{1}
(see, for instance,
[
13
, Section 46]
).

Remark 2

Using the results for lattice distributions obtained in
[
8
]
and
following (with evident changes) the line of arguments we use below to
establish our main results one can prove the respective statements for the
lattice distributions satisfying

Condition A1’
. The law of
X
belongs to
\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
and is supported on the lattice
\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,...\}

with maximal span 1.

We set

\displaystyle L_{r,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{r\leq m\leq n}S_{m},\quad L_{n}=L_{0,n},\quad M_{r,n}=\max_{r\leq m\leq n}S_{m},\quad M_{n}=M_{1,n}

\displaystyle\tau_{r,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\left\{r\leq m\leq n:S_{m}=L_{r,n}\right\},\quad\tau_{n}=\tau_{0,n}=\min\left\{0\leq m\leq n:S_{m}=L_{n}\right\}.

The aim of the paper is to investigate the asymptotic behavior of the
distribution of the random variable
L_{r,n}
given
\left\{S_{n}\leq ta_{k},L_{n}\geq 0\right\}
when
k=o(n)
and
r=r(n)
vary in an
appropriate way as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

In what follows we denote by
\mathbf{P}_{w}\left(\cdot\right)
the
distributions generated by the random walks stating from
S_{0}=w
and use
the natural agreement
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}_{0}\left(\cdot\right).

Random walks conditioned to stay positive, nonnegative or negative on some
set were investigated by many authors. Thus, Bolthausen
[
3
]

(improving the results of Iglehart
[
14

\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}},t\in[0,1]|L_{n}\geq 0\right\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\left\{\mathcal{B}^{+}\right\},

(3)

where
\mathcal{B}^{+}=\left\{B_{t}^{+},t\in[0,1]\right\}
is a
Brownian meander. Durrett
[
11
]
extended this result by showing that
if
X\in\mathcal{D}\left(\alpha,\beta\right),
then

\mathcal{L}\left\{\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}},t\in[0,1]|L_{n}\geq 0\right\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{L}\left\{\mathcal{Y}^{+}\right\}

where
\mathcal{Y}^{+}=\left\{Y_{t}^{+},t\in[0,1]\right\}
is a
Levy meander. These results have been generalized in
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
to the case when the random walk is conditioned
to stay positive on the set
[0,\infty)
.

Local versions of Bolthausen’s and Durrett’s theorems were obtained by
Caravenna
[
6
]
for the case
X\in\mathcal{D}\left(2,0\right)

and by Vatutin and Wachtel
[
22
]
for the general case

, respectively.

Relatively recently Caravenna and Chaumont
[
8
]
have made the next
step in studying such random walks. They impose different conditions on
S_{0}
and
S_{n}
and assume that the random walk remains nonnegative on
[0,n]
. More precisely, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

and staying nonnegative (or positive) on the interval

a bridge of length

and analysed the following laws

\mathbf{P}_{x,y}^{\uparrow,n}(\cdot)=\mathbf{P}_{x}(\cdot|L_{1,n-1}\geq 0,S_{n}=y),

(4)

\mathbf{\hat{P}}_{x,y}^{\uparrow,n}(\cdot)=\mathbf{P}_{x}(\cdot|L_{1,n-1}>0,S_{n}=y).

(5)

In order for the conditioning in the right-hand sides of (

and (

\displaystyle g_{n}^{+}(x,y):=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}\left(L_{1,n-1}>0,S_{n}\in dy\right)}{dy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{K}(n-1)}\left[g(s_{1}-x)\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}g(s_{i}-s_{i-1})\right)g(y-s_{n-1})\right]ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}

(7)

where

If

Some properties of random walks

In the sequel we denote by
C_{1},C_{2},...
some absolute constants which
may not be the same in different formulas or even within the same
complicated formula.

We set
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\mathbf{\cup}\left\{0\right\}
. Given
two positive sequences
\left\{c_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
,
\left\{d_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},
we write as usual
c_{n}\sim d_{n}
if
lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}/d_{n}=1;c_{n}=o(d_{n})
or
c_{n}\ll d_{n}

if
lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}/d_{n}=0
;
c_{n}=O(d_{n})
if
\lim\sup_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}/d_{n}<\infty
.

We recall that a sequence
\left\{c_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
of
positive numbers or a real positive function
c(x)
— is said to be
regularly varying at infinity with index
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, denoted
c_{n}\in R_{\gamma}
or
c(x)\in R_{\gamma}
if
c_{n}\sim n^{\gamma}l(n)

(c(x)\sim x^{\gamma}l(x))
, where
l(x)
is a slowly varying function,
i.e. a positive real function with the property that
l(cx)/l(x)\rightarrow 1
as
x\rightarrow\infty
for all fixed
c>0
.

Set
S_{0}:=0,

\tau_{0}^{\pm}:=0,
and for
k\geq 1~
denote by

\tau_{k}^{-}:=\inf\left\{n>\tau_{k-1}^{-}:S_{n}\leq S_{\tau_{k-1}^{-}}\right\}

the weak descending ladder variables and by

\tau_{k}^{+}:=\inf\left\{n>\tau_{k-1}^{+}:S_{n}\geq S_{\tau_{k-1}^{+}}\right\}

the weak ascending ladder variables of the sequence
S_{0},S_{1},...
. Put

H_{k}^{\pm}:=\pm S_{\tau_{k}^{\pm}}

and introduce the parameter

\zeta=\mathbf{P}\left(H_{1}^{+}=0\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(H_{1}^{-}=0\right)\in(0,1),

where to justify this equality it is necessary to use the duality principle
for random walks (see, for instance,
[
12
, Ch.XII, Sec.2]
):

\left\{S_{n}-S_{n-k},k=0,1,...,n\right\}\overset{d}{=}\left\{S_{k},k=0,1,...,n\right\}.

For
x\geq 0
introduce renewal functions

V^{\pm}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(H_{k}^{\pm}\leq x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{k}^{\pm}=n,\pm S_{n}\leq x\right).\quad

Note that

V^{+}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}\leq x,L_{n}\geq 0\right).

It is easy to check that
V^{\pm}(x)
are non-decreasing, right-continuous
and

V^{\pm}(0)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(H_{k}^{\pm}=0\right)=\frac{1}{1-\zeta}.

(11)

It is known (see, for instance,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
) that

\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{+}>n\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(M_{n}<0\right)\in R_{-\rho},\quad V^{+}(x)\in R_{\alpha\rho},

(12)

\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{-}>n\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(L_{1,n}>0\right)\in R_{-(1-\rho)},\quad V^{-}(x)\in R_{\alpha(1-\rho)}.

(13)

Note that, in view of (
11
)-(
13
) and the asymptotical
representation (31) in
[
22
]
and (3.18) in
[
8
]
there are
positive constants
\hat{C}
,
\mathcal{C}^{+}
and
\mathcal{C}^{-}
such
that

n\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{-}>n\right)\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{+}>n\right)\sim\hat{C},

V^{+}(a_{n})\sim\frac{\mathcal{C}^{+}}{1-\zeta}n\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{-}>n\right),\quad V^{-}(a_{n})\sim\frac{\mathcal{C}^{-}}{1-\zeta}n\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{+}>n\right),

(14)

and, therefore, as
n\rightarrow\infty

\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{+}>n\right)V^{+}(a_{n})\sim C^{\ast}:=\frac{\mathcal{C}^{+}\hat{C}}{1-\zeta}\in(0,\infty),

\mathbf{P}\left(\tau_{1}^{-}>n\right)V^{-}(a_{n})\sim C^{\ast\ast}:=\frac{\mathcal{C}^{-}\hat{C}}{1-\zeta}\in(0,\infty).

(15)

Let
g^{+}(\cdot)
be the density of the time-one marginal distribution of
the meander of the Levy process
\mathcal{Y}
(see
[
5
]
) and
g^{-}(\cdot)
be the density of the time-one marginal distribution of the

-\mathcal{Y}

\frac{1}{C^{\ast}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\alpha\rho}g^{-}(z)dz.

Hence, by symmetry arguments we conclude that

\frac{1}{C^{\ast\ast}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{\alpha(1-\rho)}g^{+}(z)dz.

For the construction of renewal functions the strict ladder variables
\left\{\hat{\tau}_{k}^{\pm},k\geq 0\right\}
and epochs
\left\{\hat{H}_{k}^{\pm},k\geq 0\right\}
are used along with the weak ladder variables.
They are defined as
\hat{\tau}_{0}^{\pm}:=0,

\hat{H}_{0}^{\pm}:=0

and, for
k\geq 1

\hat{\tau}_{k}^{\pm}:=\inf\left\{n>\hat{\tau}_{k-1}^{\pm}:\pm S_{n}>\pm S_{\hat{\tau}_{k-1}^{\pm}}\right\},\quad\hat{H}_{k}^{\pm}:=\pm S_{\tau_{k}^{\pm}}.

The sequences
\left\{\hat{H}_{k}^{\pm},k\geq 0\right\}
and
\left\{\hat{\tau}_{k}^{\pm},k\geq 0\right\}
generate the renewal functions

\hat{V}^{\pm}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\hat{H}_{k}^{\pm}\leq x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\hat{\tau}_{k}^{\pm}=n,\pm S_{n}\leq x\right).

It is easy to check that

\hat{V}^{-}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(S_{n}\geq x,M_{n}<0\right).

The connection between the introduced renewal functions is very simple (see

[
12
, ch. XII.1 equation (1.13)]

\hat{V}^{\pm}(x)=\left(1-\zeta\right)V^{\pm}(x).

(16)

In the sequel to avoid complicated notation we suppose as a rule that the
distribution of the increments

is absolutely continuous.
This means, in particulary, that

as well as the functions

and

\underline{\hat{V}}^{\pm}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\hat{H}_{k}^{\pm}<x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\hat{\tau}_{k}^{\pm}=n,\pm S_{n}<x\right).

The fundamental property of

is the identity

\mathbf{E}[\hat{V}^{-}(x+X_{1});X_{1}+x\geq 0]\ =\ \hat{V}^{-}(x)\

which, in view of

Lemma 3

If

A Seifert-van Kampen Theorem and the Frobenius Action on Tame Fundamental Groups
Yuxiang Yao

Abstract
. Let X = ℙ 𝔽 p 1 − B X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}-B , where B B is a divisor with n n distinct geometric points, and view X X as a 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -variety with q = p r q=p^{r} for some r r , we then obtain a short exact sequence of tame fundamental groups: 1 → π 1 t ​ ( X 𝔽 q ¯ ) → π 1 t ​ ( X 𝔽 q ) → Gal ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ¯ / 𝔽 q ) → 1 . 1\to\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}})\to\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})\to\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})\to 1. This gives rise to an action of Gal ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ¯ / 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}) on π 1 t ​ ( X 𝔽 q ¯ ) \pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}) once an 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -point in X 𝔽 q X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}} is fixed. Using Harbater’s formal patching, we prove a version of the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, which further yields a purely algebraic description of the action of Gal ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ¯ / 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}) on the n n generators of π 1 t ​ ( X 𝔽 q ¯ ) \pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}) assigned to each geometric point of B 𝔽 q ¯ B_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}} . Suppose

\displaystyle 1\to\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}})\to\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}})\to\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})\to 1.

A quick description of this action is provided by Corollary XIII.2.12 in

]

\widehat{\left\langle\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\>|\>\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\cdots\gamma_{n}\right\rangle}\cong\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\cong\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})\cong\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\mathbb{C}_{p}})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}).

Although we would like to avoid working on complex manifolds, the classical topological method still provides a key motivation. Starting from considering the corresponding complex manifold, the

-punctured

Let

\displaystyle 1=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(S^{2},z_{0})

\displaystyle\cong\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_{1}(S^{2}-\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\},z_{0})*_{\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(\bigvee_{1}^{n}(S^{1}_{i},*),z_{i}^{\prime})}\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(\{*\},*)

\displaystyle\cong\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_{1}(S^{2}-\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\},z_{0})*_{\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(\bigvee_{1}^{n}(S^{1}_{i},*),z_{i}^{\prime})}1

\displaystyle\cong\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_{1}(S^{2}-\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\},z_{0})*_{*_{1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}}1.

In addition to the conditions in (1), suppose

We now give a closer look at the outline of our proof. The prerequisites are given in Section

Theorem 1.3

(Abstract Seifert-van-Kampen’s Theorem for Galois Categories)
.

Suppose
(A,F_{A})
,
(B,F_{B})
,
(C,F_{C})
are three Galois categories, and
F:A\to B
and
G:A\to C
are exact functors. Then
B\times_{A}C
is also a Galois category with functor
F_{B\times_{A}C}:B\times_{A}C\to\mathrm{Sets}
, and

\mathrm{Aut}(F_{B\times_{A}C})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(F_{B})\hat{*}^{o}_{\mathrm{Aut}(F_{A})}\mathrm{Aut}(F_{C}).

For the proof, see Theorem
3.2
.
Then we deduce a version of the Seifert-van Kampen theorem for étale fundamental groups (see Theorem
3.7
) and a version of tame fundamental groups (see Theorem
3.9
) from Harbater’s formal patching theory and Theorem
1.3
. For a more generalized version, one may check
[
Sti06
]
.

In addition, to show part (2) of Theorem
1.1
, we also need the following important preparation, which provides another view of the Frobenius action.

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume that
X
is a connected scheme over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a geometric point
\overline{\eta}
(
q=p^{r}
), whose image is also an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
point in
X
, such that
\pi_{1}^{t}(X,\overline{\eta})
is topologcally finitely generated. So we also write
X_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=X
. Then, there is a splitting short exact sequence, see (
1.2
). Here we can write
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot\phi_{\mathrm{Frob}}
. The Frobenius
\phi_{\mathrm{Frob}}:\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
defined by

is the topological generator of the absolute Galois group. Then the action of
\phi_{\mathrm{Frob}}
on
\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}})
is induced by the geometric Frobenius

\mathrm{Fr}_{g}:X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}\to X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}.

More specifically,
\mathrm{Fr}_{g}
induces the group automorphism

\phi_{\mathrm{Frob}}:\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}},\overline{\eta})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Fr}_{g,*}}\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}},\mathrm{Fr}_{g}\circ\overline{\eta})\cong\pi_{1}^{t}(X_{\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}},\overline{\eta}).

Here, the absolute Frobenius is an identity map on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
so we have the equation at the end of above map.

This is a known result in the literature. For a detailed treatment, one may refer to Section 2.3 in
[
Sti12
]
, which can be easily descent to the case of tame fundamental groups.

\mathrm{Aut}(F)\to\prod_{X\in\mathrm{Ob}(C)}\mathrm{Aut}(F(X)),\>\>\>(\alpha:F\xrightarrow{\cong}F)\mapsto(\alpha_{X}:F(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}F(X)).

(1)

and

are not fixed, determined up to inner automorphisms). This also determines

and

.

Then we have a determined

(3)

,

Definition 4.3

(Four Distinct Definitions of Frobenius Morphisms)
.

Suppose
k=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\overline{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, the algebraic closure of finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. And let

\mathbb{F}_{q}

, whose base change to

is denoted by

.

(1)

Let

\mathrm{Fr}:X\to X
as a
\overline{k}
-morphism, such that it is an identity map as maps on topological spaces, and for each open

,
\mathrm{Frob}_{X}^{\#}:\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
is defined by
a\mapsto a^{q}
for each

. Sometimes, we use the notation

for absolute Frobenius.

(2)

Suppose that

is given by
1_{\Spec\overline{k}}\times_{\Spec k}\mathrm{Fr}_{X}
.
This is also called

(3)

Suppose that
\mathrm{Fr}_{\mathrm{Spec}\overline{k}}:\Spec\overline{k}\to\Spec\overline{k}
is the absolute Frobenius morphism. Then the

\mathrm{Fr}_{a}
is defined by
\mathrm{Fr}_{\mathrm{Spec}\overline{k}}\times_{\Spec k}1_{X}
.

(4)

The

\mathrm{Fr}^{-1}_{\mathrm{Spec}\overline{k}}\times_{\Spec k}1_{X}
.

Example 4.4

Suppose

Example 4.5
.

Now, we look at how arithmetic and geometric Frobenius permutes geometric points in a divisor determined by an irreducible polynomial over

\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[x]\to\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[x],\>\>\>\>\>\>a_{n}x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}\mapsto a_{n}^{q}x^{n}+a^{q}_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a^{q}_{0}.

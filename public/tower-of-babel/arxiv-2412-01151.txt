Tug-of-war games associated with boundary value problems involving derivatives
Jeongmin Han

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a certain type of noisy tug-of-war game which can be regarded as an interpretation of a certain type of boundary value problem for the normalized p p -Laplace equation, where 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 . More precisely, we will investigate the boundary regularity of the value function to the game and its convergence to a viscosity solution of the model problem.

91A05, 91A15, 35D40, 35B65

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider a certain type of stochastic game related to the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\\
\langle\mathbf{n},Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{p}^{N}
is the normalized
p
-Laplace operator given by

\Delta_{p}^{N}u=\Delta u+(p-2)\frac{\langle D^{2}uDu,Du\rangle}{|Du|^{2}},

1<p<2
,
\gamma>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain.
To do this, we will first observe a mean value characterization linked to the above problem and
construct a tug-of-war game related to the dynamic programming principle (DPP) based on the characterization
(for a heuristic description of the game setting, see Section
2.2
).
In particular, we focus on the regularity and convergence results of value functions for the games associated with (
1
), which will be stated in Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
3.9
.

This work can be regarded as an extension of
[
Han
]
.
In that paper, the author considered a game-theoretic interpretation of (
1
) with
p>2
,
and the game is motivated by the stochastic processes in
[
MPR12
,
AL16
,
LP23a
]
.
To cover the case
1<p<2
, we will present a different type of tug-of-war games:
We will use the ‘noisy tug-of-war game’ in
[
Lew21
]
to construct the stochastic game for the model problem.
This construction enables us to use the Krylov-Safonov theory for certain types of DPPs in
[
ABP22
,
ABP23
,
AP24
]
.

Our strategy to prove the main results is as follows.
For the regularity issue, we derive Hölder estimates for the interior case and the boundary case.
The interior estimate can be shown by using the Krylov-Safonov type estimates in
[
ABP22
,
ABP23
,
AP24
]
.
We need to go through several steps to obtain the boundary regularity, Theorem
3.7
(cf.
[
LP23b
,
Han
]
).
The key to the proof is a supermartingale argument employing the optional stopping theorem.
Constructing an appropriate supermartingale is crucial for this method
and to this end, we present some geometric and stochastic observations relevant to our game beforehand.
Meanwhile, we will show the convergence of value functions by using the notion of viscosity solutions (cf.
[
MPR12
]
).

The game setting basically includes random noise when the tug-of-war occurs.
If
p>2
, we can construct the associated game with radially symmetric noise for the tug-of-war process.
This property makes us to deal with the terms caused by that noise easier than the other case.
When
1<p<2
, however, we can only consider the game with noise that is not rotationally invariant.
We need to introduce a suitable argument to derive our desired estimate in this case (see the proof of Theorem
3.7
).

Tug-of-war and its noise-included version were first introduced in
[
PS08
,
PSSW09
]
.
Since then, these stochastic games have been investigated as a game-theoretic interpretation of
p
-Laplace type operators.
General introductions to the connection between tug-of-war and the
p
-Laplacian can be found in
[
Lew20
,
Par24
]
.
The construction of stochastic games is based on mean value characterizations for solutions to the model equations.
We refer to
[
MPR10
,
MPR12
]
for mean value characterizations for
p
-harmonic functions.
Especially, games associated with the normalized
p
-Laplace operators with
1<p<\infty
were studied in
[
KMP12
,
AHP17
,
ALPR20
]
.
In
[
Dan13
]
, probabilistic approaches are considered for the Neumann boundary value problem for fully nonlinear equations.
One can also find preceding studies
[
LP23a
,
LP23b
,
Han
]
which deal with stochastic processes
related to the boundary value problems involving derivatives for
p
-Laplace type equations.
We also mention Krylov-Safonov type regularity estimates for stochastic games and DPPs in
[
ABP22
,
ABP23
]
.
Meanwhile, the boundary condition in (
1
) is a special case of oblique derivative boundary conditions, which is given by

\langle\mathbf{\beta},Du\rangle+\gamma u=\gamma G

with
|\langle\beta,\mathbf{n}\rangle|\geq\delta_{0}
for some
\delta_{0}>0
.
We refer to
[
Lie13
]
for the general theory about oblique derivative boundary value problems.
Several works
[
LZ18
,
CM21
]
gave regularity results for fully nonlinear oblique derivative boundary value problems.
In
[
BH20
,
BH22
]
, Calderon-Zygmund type theory was considered for these problems.

We first observe that

\displaystyle=u(x)+\bigg\langle Du(x),\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)dy\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bigg\langle D^{2}u(x):\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)\otimes(y-x)dy\bigg\rangle+o(\varepsilon^{2})

by Taylor expansion.
We also see that

\displaystyle\bigg\langle Du(x),\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\Omega}}(y-x)dy\bigg\rangle=\gamma_{0}s_{\varepsilon}(x)(u(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x))-G(\pi_{\partial\Omega}x)))+O(\varepsilon s_{\varepsilon}(x)),

(

In the first step, we estimate the first two terms of the right hand side in
The second and third steps are devoted to giving an estimate of the last term.

\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq|X_{k,\nu_{x}}-Y_{k,\nu_{y}}|^{2}+(QA\varepsilon)^{2}|\omega|^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\big|(X_{k,\nu_{x}}+QA\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{X_{k-1}}\mathcal{P}_{\nu_{x}}\mathcal{D}\omega)-(Y_{k,\nu_{y}}+QA\varepsilon d^{\prime}_{Y_{k-1}}\mathcal{P}_{\nu_{y}}\mathcal{D}\omega)\big|^{2}d\mathbb{P}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq|X_{k,\nu_{x}}-Y_{k,\nu_{y}}|^{2}+(QA\varepsilon)^{2}.

Thus, we have

\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{I}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]\leq(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+Q\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}))^{2}+(QA\varepsilon)^{2}.

We similarly derive

\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{II}^{x},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]\leq\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+Q\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{X_{k}})\big)^{2}+(QA\varepsilon)^{2}.

Meanwhile, for the random walk, one can find the following estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\frac{\alpha}{2}<\xi_{k}<1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\}}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)

for some
C>0
in the proof of
[
LP23b
, Theorem 8.1]
.
Now we have

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})\big)^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|+\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{X_{k}})\big)^{2}+\alpha(QA\varepsilon)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\big(|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))\big)

\displaystyle\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(1-\alpha)(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))+\varepsilon^{2}(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})^{2}+\alpha(QA\varepsilon)^{2}.

We also check that
|d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and this implies

\varepsilon^{2}(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}-d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})^{2}+\alpha(QA\varepsilon)^{2}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))

with some universal
C_{0}>0
.
Thus, we can obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]\\
&\leq|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))).\end{split}

On the other hand, if
|X_{k}-Y_{k}|<Q\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}+d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})
, we see

|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|\leq(2+A)Q\varepsilon(d^{\prime}_{X_{k}}+d^{\prime}_{Y_{k}})\leq(2+A)Q\varepsilon.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}\big[|X_{k+1}-Y_{k+1}|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(Q(2+A)\varepsilon)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}\big(1+C(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))\big)+C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))+(1-\alpha)\varepsilon^{2}+\alpha(QA\varepsilon)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k})))+(1-\alpha)\varepsilon^{2}

by choosing
C>0
large enough.
We have also used that
\varepsilon^{2}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon(s_{\varepsilon}(X_{k})+s_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}))
.

We can construct a supermartingale given by

Q_{k}=|X_{k}-Y_{k}|^{2}e^{-C(S_{k}^{x_{0}}+S_{k}^{y_{0}})}-C\varepsilon(S_{k}^{x_{0}}+S_{k}^{y_{0}}).

By employing the optional stopping theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\geq\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{2}e^{-C(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}})}-C\varepsilon(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}})].

We remark that Lemma
3.6
implies

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\sup_{S_{I}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x,\ast},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y,\ast}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}]\leq C\delta^{1/2},

and thus

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}>1)\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

Then we observe the following (cf.
[
Han
, Theorem 4.4]
):

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}]\leq C\delta^{2\sigma}

and this yields

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|^{\sigma}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}]\leq C\delta^{\sigma},

provided
\varepsilon<<\delta
.
The following estimate

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}\cap\bigg\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\bigg\}\bigg)\leq\frac{C\delta}{\delta_{0}}

is also obtained by combining (
3.11
) with (
3.12
).

Then we can obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,x_{0}}+S_{\overline{\tau}}^{\varepsilon,y_{0}}\leq 1\}}\mathds{1}_{\{|X_{\overline{\tau}}-Y_{\overline{\tau}}|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}}]

\displaystyle\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{S_{I}^{x}}\inf_{S_{II}^{x}}\inf_{S_{I}^{y}}\sup_{S_{II}^{y}}\mathbb{E}_{S_{I}^{x},S_{II}^{x},S_{I}^{y},S_{II}^{y}}^{(x_{0},y_{0})}[|u_{\varepsilon}(X_{\overline{\tau}})-u_{\varepsilon}(Y_{\overline{\tau}})|]\leq C||G||_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})}\delta^{\sigma/2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Convergence

We consider the convergence issue of value functions as
\varepsilon\to 0
in this subsection.
Before proving the convergence, we first introduce the notion of viscosity solutions.

Definition 3.8

(viscosity solution)
.

A function
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity solution to (
1
) if
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}
such that
u(x)=\phi(x)
and
u(y)>\phi(y)
for
y\neq x
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x)\leq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\Omega$,}\\
\min\big\{\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x),\gamma_{0}G(x)-\big(\langle\mathbf{n},D\phi\rangle(x)+\gamma_{0}\phi(x)\big)\big\}\leq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\partial\Omega$,}\\
\end{array}\right.

and
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
\phi\in C^{2}
such that
u(x)=\phi(x)
and
u(y)<\phi(y)
for
y\neq x
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x)\geq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\Omega$,}\\
\max\big\{\Delta_{p}^{N}\phi(x),\gamma_{0}G(x)-\big(\langle\mathbf{n},D\phi\rangle(x)+\gamma_{0}\phi(x)\big)\big\}\geq 0&\textrm{if $x\in\partial\Omega$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Then we can show the convergence of the value function to a viscosity solution of (
1
).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the function satisfying (
2.13
) and

\varepsilon>0

Then, there exists a continuous function

such that

converges uniformly to

on
\overline{\Omega}
and

is a viscosity solution to the problem (
1
).

Proof.

For the interior case, we can prove the convergence by using
[
Lew21
, Corollary 7.3]
.
Thus, we only need to consider the boundary case.
First, we fix

x\in\partial\Omega
and
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})

such that
u-\phi
attains a strict local minimum at
x

(we use the notation
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
instead of
\{u_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
for simplicity).
Since
u_{\varepsilon}
uniformly converges to
u
, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\inf_{B_{r}(x)}(u_{\varepsilon}-\phi)<u_{\varepsilon}(z)-\phi(z)

for all

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
.
Hence, we can choose a point
x_{\varepsilon}\in B_{r}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega}
for any

such that

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})-\phi(x_{\varepsilon})\leq u_{\varepsilon}(z)-\phi(z)+\zeta_{\varepsilon}

for any

and small
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\varphi=\phi+u_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})-\phi(x_{\varepsilon})
.
Since we can observe that
x_{\varepsilon}\to x
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
we have
\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})=u_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})
and

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(z)\geq u_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})-\phi(x_{\varepsilon})+\phi(z)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}=\varphi(z)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}

for each

.

Set

with

.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

and

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(z)\geq\varphi(z)-\zeta_{\varepsilon}

for each

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\zeta_{\varepsilon}\geq\tilde{T}_{\varepsilon}^{G}\phi(x_{\varepsilon})-\phi(x_{\varepsilon})+\gamma s_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})\Lambda_{\varepsilon}

with

Now we assume that there is an infinite sequence
\{x_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}
such that

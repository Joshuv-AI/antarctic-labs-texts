Some new Liouville type theorems for the 3D stationary magneto-micropolar fluid equations
Zhibing Zhang, Qian Zu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate Liouville type theorems for the 3D stationary magneto-micropolar fluid equations. Adopting an iterative procedure, taking advantage of the special structure of the equations and using a novel combination of two different interpolation inequalities, we establish Liouville type theorems if the smooth solution satisfies certain growth conditions in terms of L p L^{p} -norms on the annuli. Compared with the velocity field and the magnetic field, we raise the most relaxed restriction for the angular velocity. More specifically, we allow L q L^{q} -norm of the angular velocity on the annuli to grow polynomially at any degree. Besides, we also obtain Liouville type theorems for the 3D stationary micropolar fluid equations.

1.
Introduction

Consider the stationary magneto-micropolar fluid equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=\chi\mathrm{curl}\omega+(b\cdot\nabla)b,\\
-\Delta\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-\nabla(\mathrm{div}\omega)+2\chi\omega=\chi\mathrm{curl}u,\\
-\Delta b+(u\cdot\nabla)b-(b\cdot\nabla)u=0,\\
\text{\rm div}u=\text{\rm div}b=0,\end{array}\right.

where
u
is the velocity field,
\omega
is the angular velocity,
b
is the magnetic field,
\pi
is the scalar pressure,
\mathrm{curl}u=\nabla\times u
. The positive parameter
\chi
represents the micro-rotational viscosity. Since the magneto-micropolar fluid equations have important physical background, rich phenomena, mathematical complexity and challenges, it has attracted the attention of many physicists and mathematicians, and many interesting results have been established, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
24
]
for example.

In recent years, there have been many efforts to establish Liouville type theorems for various fluid equations. For the Navier-Stokes equations, many scholars proved the triviality of
u
under some additional conditions. For example, Galdi
[
9
]
proved a Liouville type theorem, assuming that the velocity field
u\in L^{\frac{9}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. As an extension, Chamorro et al.
[
5
]
showed that the condition
u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
with
3\leq p\leq\frac{9}{2}
is sufficient to get Liouville type result. Seregin
[
18
]
handled the pressure term by applying the Bogovskii map on the ball, and established the Liouville type theorem if
u
fulfils

\displaystyle\sup_{R>0}R^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty,~~~~~~~~~~~~with~~~~~~~~~~~~\frac{3}{2}<p<3~~~~~~~~~~~~and~~~~~~~~~~~~\alpha>\frac{6p-3}{8p-6}.

Recently, by using an iterative argument, Cho et al.
[
7
]
got an improvement of Seregin’s result. More precisely, they showed Liouville type theorem holds if
u
satisfies one of the following conditions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(\mathrm{i})\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}R^{-(\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}(A_{R})}<+\infty,\;\frac{3}{2}<p<3,\\
&(\mathrm{ii})\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}R^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{3}(A_{R})}=0,\end{aligned}

where
A_{R}=B_{2R}\backslash B_{R}
. For more interesting works on the Liouville type results for the Navier-Stokes equations, one can refer to
[
2
,
4
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
19
,
21
]
.

When
b=0
, (
1
) reduces to the micropolar fluid equations. So far, there are few Liouville type results for the micropolar fluid equations and the magneto-micropolar fluid equations. Kim and Ko
[
11
]
proved the vanishing property of a smooth solution
(u,\omega,b)
to (
1
) with
\chi=1
under the following assumption

(u,\omega,b)\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)\text{ with }2\leq p<\frac{9}{2}.

Very recently, Cho et al.
[
8
, Theorem 2]
obtained the Liouville type theorem for (
1
) with
\chi=1
provided that

\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\left(R^{-(\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{3})}\|u\|_{L^{p}(A_{R})}+R^{-h(q)}\|\omega\|_{L^{q}(A_{R})}+R^{-(\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{3})}\|b\|_{L^{r}(A_{R})}\right)<+\infty,

(1.5)

where
p,q,r
satisfy
\frac{3}{2}<p<3
,
1\leq q\leq+\infty
,
1\leq r\leq 3
, and
h(q)
is defined by

h(q):=\frac{2}{q}-\frac{1}{3}
for
1\leq q\leq 3
,
h(q):=1-\frac{2}{q}
for
3<q\leq+\infty
. They also obtained the Liouville type theorem for (
1
) under

\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\left(R^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{3}(A_{R})}+R^{-h(q)}\|\omega\|_{L^{q}(A_{R})}+R^{-(\frac{2}{r}-\frac{1}{3})}\|b\|_{L^{r}(A_{R})}\right)=0,

(1.6)

where
q,r
satisfy
1\leq q\leq+\infty
,
1\leq r\leq 3
. As a corollary (see
[
8
, Theorem 3]
), they showed that if

u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;b\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\text{ with }\frac{3}{2}<p\leq\frac{9}{2},1\leq q\leq+\infty,1\leq r\leq\frac{9}{2},

(1.7)

then
u=\omega=b=0
.
On the other hand, Chamorro et al.
[
6
]
studied the following micropolar fluid equations with
\kappa\gg 1
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{curl}\omega,\\
-\Delta\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-\nabla(\mathrm{div}\omega)+\kappa\omega=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{curl}u,\\
\text{\rm div}u=0,\end{array}\right.

and they proved
(u,\omega)=0
if

\displaystyle u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\omega\in{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\pi\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\text{ where }3\leq p\leq\frac{9}{2}.

As mentioned in
[
6
]
,
\kappa\gg 1
is a technical parameter, which is due to the limitation of the estimation methodology.

Our goal of this paper is to establish Liouville type results for (
1
) and (
1
), provided that
L^{p}
-norms of the velocity field
u
, the angular velocity
\omega
and the magnetic field

to grow polynomially at any degree. Our results significantly improve the results of Cho et al
[

where

. To begin with, we state Liouville type theorems for the magneto-micropolar fluid equations.

.

Let
(u,\pi,\omega,b)
be a smooth solution of (
1

,

,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}X_{\alpha,p,R_{j}}=0,\;\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}Y_{\beta,q,R_{j}}<+\infty,\;\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}Z_{\gamma,r,R_{j}}<+\infty,

(1.11)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{(A1)}

\displaystyle\;r\in[2p^{\prime},6],\;\alpha+2\gamma\leq\frac{3}{p}+\frac{6}{r}-2.

.

Theorem 2 and Theorem 3 in

]

4.
A sketch of the proof of Liouville type theorems for the micropolar fluid equations

In this section, we provide another method to establish the energy estimate, which is slightly different from the method used in Section
3
. Multiplying both sides of
\eqref{equ1.3}_{1}
by
u\eta^{2}-v
, then integrating over
B_{t}
and using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}\eta^{2}dx=\int_{B_{t}}\nabla u:\nabla vdx+\int_{B_{t}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\Delta\eta^{2}dx+\int_{B_{t}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}(u\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx-\\
&\int_{B_{t}}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot udx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot(\eta^{2}\mathrm{curl}u+\nabla\eta^{2}\times u)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot\mathrm{curl}vdx.\end{aligned}

(4.1)

Multiplying both sides of
\eqref{equ1.3}_{2}
by
\omega\eta^{2}
, then integrating over
B_{t}
and using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{t}}|\nabla\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}+|\mathrm{div}\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}+\kappa|\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}dx\\
=&\int_{B_{t}}\frac{|\omega|^{2}}{2}\Delta\eta^{2}+\frac{|\omega|^{2}}{2}(u\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx-\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{div}\omega(\omega\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{curl}u\cdot\omega\eta^{2}dx.\end{aligned}

(4.2)

Collecting (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we find that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{t}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+|\mathrm{div}\omega|^{2}+\kappa|\omega|^{2}\right)\eta^{2}dx\\
=&\int_{B_{t}}\nabla u:\nabla vdx+\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{curl}u\cdot\omega\eta^{2}dx-\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{div}\omega(\omega\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot(\nabla\eta^{2}\times u)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot\mathrm{curl}vdx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})\Delta\eta^{2}dx\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})(u\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx-\int_{B_{t}}(u\otimes u):\nabla vdx.\end{aligned}

(4.3)

From the identity

\int_{B_{t}}|\nabla(u\eta)|^{2}dx=\int_{B_{t}}|\text{\rm div}(u\eta)|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{t}}|\text{\rm curl}(u\eta)|^{2}dx,

we see

\|\text{\rm curl}(u\eta)\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\leq\|\nabla(u\eta)\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})},

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\eta\text{\rm curl}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}&=\|\text{\rm curl}(u\eta)-\nabla\eta\times u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\\
&\leq\|\text{\rm curl}(u\eta)\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}+\|\nabla\eta\times u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\\
&\leq\|\nabla(u\eta)\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}+\|\nabla\eta\times u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\\
&\leq\|\eta\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}+C\|u\otimes\nabla\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}.\end{aligned}

(4.4)

Let
\varepsilon
and
\delta
be two positive constants to be determined. By the Hölder inequality, (
4.4
) and the Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{curl}u\cdot\omega\eta^{2}dx-\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{div}\omega(\omega\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot(\nabla\eta^{2}\times u)dx\\
\leq&\|\eta\text{\rm curl}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\|\omega\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}-\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{div}\omega(\omega\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx+\|\omega\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\|\nabla\eta\times u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\\
\leq&\|\eta\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\|\omega\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}-\int_{B_{t}}\mathrm{div}\omega(\omega\cdot\nabla\eta^{2})dx+C\|\omega\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\|u\otimes\nabla\eta\|_{L^{2}(B_{t})}\\
\leq&\varepsilon\int_{B_{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}\eta^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\int_{B_{t}}|\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}|\mathrm{div}\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}dx+2\int_{B_{t}}|\omega\cdot\nabla\eta|^{2}dx\\
&+\delta\int_{B_{t}}|\omega|^{2}\eta^{2}dx+C_{\delta}\int_{B_{t}}|u\otimes\nabla\eta|^{2}dx.\end{aligned}

(4.5)

Here we mention that if we use the point-wise estimate
|\mathrm{curl}u|\leq\sqrt{2}|\nabla u|
instead of (
4.4
), then we have to require
\kappa
to be larger.

Combining (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), we can derive that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{t}}\left[(1-\varepsilon)|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{div}\omega|^{2}+\left(\kappa-\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}-\delta\right)|\omega|^{2}\right]\eta^{2}dx\\
\leq&\int_{B_{t}}\nabla u:\nabla vdx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}}\omega\cdot\mathrm{curl}vdx+\frac{C}{(t-s)^{2}}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{s}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})dx\\
&+\frac{C}{t-s}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{s}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})|u|dx+\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{\frac{2t}{3}}}|u|^{2}|\nabla v|dx.\end{aligned}

Since
\kappa>\frac{1}{4}
, we can choose some
\varepsilon
and
\delta
satisfying

\varepsilon\in\left(\frac{1}{4\kappa},1\right),\delta\in\left(0,\kappa-\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\right).

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{t}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+|\mathrm{div}\omega|^{2}+|\omega|^{2}\right)\eta^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{R}}|\omega|^{2}dx+

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(t-s)^{2}}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{s}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})dx+\frac{C}{t-s}\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{s}}(|u|^{2}+|\omega|^{2})|u|dx+C\int_{B_{t}\backslash B_{\frac{2t}{3}}}|u|^{2}|\nabla v|dx.

The rest of the proof of Liouville type theorems for (
1
) follows the approach adopted in Section
3
, and thus is omitted.

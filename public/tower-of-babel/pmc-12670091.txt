First-in-human study of a thorium-227-labeled mesothelin-targeting antibody-chelator conjugate (MSLN-TTC), in patients with malignant mesothelioma and other solid tumors
Authors: A Minchom, O Lindén, DG Knapen, R Hassan, FI Lin, K Jalkanen, V Subbiah, A Tafuri, D Ferreira, V Jardine, A Cleton, J Pinkert, F Bladt, H Hennekes, EF Smit
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBAY 2287411 [227Th-anetumab corixetan; mesothelin-targeting antibody-chelator conjugate (MSLN-TTC)] is a targeted alpha therapy consisting of a fully human mesothelin-targeting monoclonal antibody conjugated with a 3,2-hydroxypyridinone (3,2-HOPO) chelator radiolabeled with the alpha particle-emitting radionuclide thorium-227. This phase I study determined the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of MSLN-TTC in mesothelin-expressing mesothelioma and serous ovarian cancer.MethodsMSLN-TTC was administered i.v. 1.5, 2.5, or 3.5 MBq of thorium-227 and with a total antibody dose of 10, 30, 50, or 150 mg every 6 weeks to 36 patients included in the intention-to-treat population. Adverse events, tumor response according to RECIST 1.1 and mRECIST criteria, and progression-free survival were determined. Tumor mesothelin expression was assessed retrospectively.ResultsIn dose escalation, 35 patients (30 with malignant pleural mesothelioma) received MSLN-TTC across three thorium-227 dose levels and four total antibody doses. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed, and the maximum tolerated dose was not reached due to treatment discontinuations following 45.7% of the patients developing neutralizing antibodies. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events of any grade were fatigue (10/36, 27.8%), decreased lymphocyte count (7/36, 19.4%), nausea (7/36, 19.4%), anemia (6/36, 16.7%), and infusion-related reaction (6/36, 16.7%). The disease control rate was 34.3%, including 12 stable diseases (12/36, 34.3%). No complete or partial responses were observed. The median progression-free survival was 70 days (95% confidence interval 29-161 days).ConclusionsMSLN-TTC showed good tolerability, but the maximum tolerated dose could not be determined due to discontinuations after antidrug antibody formation. Stable disease was observed in 12 out of 36 patients.Key words: mesothelin, mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, targeted alpha therapy, thorium-227

Introduction

Mesothelin is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol-linked, membrane-bound cell surface protein expressed on several solid tumors, including malignant mesothelioma, ovarian and pancreatic cancer, certain subsets of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), cholangiocarcinoma and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC).1 It is an attractive therapeutic target given its relative tumoral expression and specificity. Despite therapeutic developments in mesothelioma and ovarian cancer, durable clinical benefit is lacking and thus a need for development of further effective treatment options remains.2

BAY 2287411 [mesothelin-targeting antibody-chelator conjugate (MSLN-TTC)] comprises a mesothelin-targeting fully human monoclonal antibody (Ab) BAY 86-1903 (anetumab) covalently attached to a thorium-227-complexing 3,2-hydroxypyridinone (HOPO) chelator (corixetan).3, 4, 5 The Ab-chelator conjugate (anetumab corixetan; BAY 2287409) is labeled with the alpha particle-emitting radionuclide thorium-227, which forms a stable complex with the chelator. The mode-of-action of MSLN-TTC consists of targeted delivery of a lethal dose of DNA double-strand break (DSB)-inducing alpha radiation to cancer cells expressing mesothelin.6 MSLN-TTC is being developed for patients with mesothelin-expressing tumors which have exhausted available therapeutic options. Anetumab ravtansine, the anetumab Ab conjugated to the maytansinoid DM4,7,8 has previously demonstrated antitumor activity in patients with recurrent ovarian cancer and malignant mesothelioma.9 Drug-induced immunogenicity, however, has been recognized as a major challenge in the development of biological drugs. Biological drug administration to patients may induce humoral immune responses, causing the formation of antidrug antibodies (ADAs). ADAs, and especially their subset neutralizing antibodies (NABs) that inhibit target binding and pharmacodynamic function, may inactivate the drug and cause a loss of targeting and/or an increased clearance of ADA–drug complexes, which may lead to suboptimal exposure and loss of efficacy.10,11

This phase I first-in-human study (NCT03507452) was designed to determine the maximum tolerated dose, safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor activity of MSLN-TTC in patients with mesothelin-expressing mesothelioma (either pleural or peritoneal) or serous ovarian cancer.

Materials and methods

This study was a multicenter, open-label, phase I dose escalation study conducted at seven sites in Finland, the Netherlands, Sweden, the UK, and USA. The primary objectives of the study were to determine the safety, tolerability, and maximum tolerated dose of MSLN-TTC. The secondary objectives of the study were to determine the recommended dose for further clinical development of MSLN-TTC and to investigate the pharmacokinetics of MSLN-TTC.

Patients were enrolled into dose escalation cohorts, starting with a thorium-227 dose of 1.5 MBq and total Ab doses of 10 mg and 30 mg. MSLN-TTC was administered as an i.v. injection on day 1 of each 42-day cycle. To increase the total Ab dose, an additional injection of non-radioactive unlabeled Ab-chelator conjugate (BAY 2287409) was administered 1 h before the radiolabeled Ab-chelator conjugate to achieve the intended Ab dose.

The dose escalation was conducted in sequential dose cohorts up to 3.5 MBq of thorium-227. At each dose cohort, dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), were assessed during the first 42 days of treatment. DLTs were defined as any febrile neutropenia or grade 4 neutropenia lasting more than 7 days, any grade 4 anemia or thrombocytopenia, any grade 3 thrombocytopenia with a bleeding event, any grade 3 or 4 gastrointestinal toxicity that could not be controlled, as well as any grade 3 or 4 hematologic or non-hematologic toxicity that was not infusion-related, an allergic reaction or an isolated, asymptomatic elevation of a biochemical laboratory value. Subsequently, escalation or de-escalation decisions were based on the available safety and pharmacokinetic data, and on a benefit–risk evaluation of the totality of the data from all preceding dose escalation cohorts, including late-toxicity events. Patients continued receiving the drug until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, consent withdrawal, withdrawal from the study or until another criterion for study withdrawal was met. After the observation of ADAs (titer ≥32) leading to changes in the pharmacokinetics, the protocol was amended so that patients with an ADA titer of 32 or higher would not progress to cycle 2. Patients could receive at most a total of four planned treatment cycles, although treatment could subsequently be continued beyond cycle 4 for a maximum of two additional cycles upon assessment of the individual benefit–risk profile of the patient and agreement between the sponsor and the investigator.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards of the participating institutions and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki, current Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and local laws and regulations. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The study was sponsored by Bayer AG.

Patients

Patients who were ≥18 years of age with advanced malignant epithelioid mesothelioma or advanced recurrent serous ovarian cancer were eligible. The patients were required to have histologically or cytologically documented pleural or peritoneal epithelioid mesothelioma, or advanced or metastatic serous ovarian cancer, including fallopian tube cancer and primary peritoneal serous carcinoma, and at least one measurable or evaluable tumor lesion according to RECIST 1.1, and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1. The patients had to have also encountered tumor progression after the previous line of treatment (administrated ≥28 days before the first dose of MSLN-TTC injection), confirmed by a local computed tomography (CT)/magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan. The availability of a tumor tissue sample from an archival tissue or fresh biopsy for the determination of MSLN expression by immunohistochemistry [histochemical score (H-score)] was mandatory.12 At the time of study conduct a 89Zr-labelled positron emission tomography (PET) tracer using the same anetumab corixetan conjugate was in development.13,14 Although the clinically tested 89Zr-MMOT0530A PET tracer was available, it was not used in the study because it is based on a different Ab.15 Additionally, the patients had to have acceptable bone marrow, liver, and renal function (hemoglobin ≥9.0 g/dl, absolute neutrophil count ≥1500/mm3, platelet count ≥100 000/mm3, total bilirubin ≤1.5 time lower or equal to the upper limit of normal (ULN), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ˂2.5 × ULN, and serum creatinine ≤1.5 × ULN and glomerular filtration rate (GFR) ≥45 ml/min/1.73 m2).

Assessments

Safety

Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 5.0 was used to grade toxicities and treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAE), except for corneal epitheliopathy where the Bayer Classification system was used (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). Evaluations of vital signs, electrocardiograms, safety laboratory parameters as well as physical examinations were also carried out.

To document late onset safety events, multiple dose DLTs were defined as an event occurring after the end of the DLT period and after the patient received subsequent doses that would otherwise meet the criteria for a DLT.

Antitumor activity

Tumor response was assessed by CT or MRI, using the RECIST 1.1 criteria for peritoneal epithelioid mesothelioma and measurable serous ovarian cancer, the mRECIST criteria for pleural mesothelioma. Objective response rate or overall disease control rate, duration of response, progression-free survival, and overall survival were determined.

The tumor response assessment (CT or MRI) was carried out at screening (baseline), and on treatment between days 24 and 30 of every cycle. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate median progression-free survival, overall survival, and time to progression with two-sided 95% confidence intervals (CI). All statistical analyses were carried out using SAS, release 9.2 or higher (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).

Pharmacokinetics

For pharmacokinetic assessments, MSLN-TTC analytes (total Ab, thorium-227, and its first daughter nuclide radium-223) were quantified in blood samples collected at scheduled time points (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). Evaluation of plasma pharmacokinetic parameters was carried out by noncompartmental analysis.

The activity of both thorium-227 and radium-223 in whole blood was determined by direct gamma spectrometry. The radioactive decay (thorium-227 and radium-223) as well as the ingrowth of the daughter (radium-223) during the period between sampling and measurement was corrected for. The total Ab concentration of MSLN-TTC was determined by a ligand binding assay in diluted plasma with a chemiluminescence readout, using a mesothelin antigen as the capture and a horseradish peroxidase-labeled goat antihuman IgG Ab as the detection reagent.

ADA titers and NAB positive statuses were determined on day 1 of cycle 1 (predose) and days 29 and 43 of the first three cycles, and thereafter on day 1 of every even cycle, and finally at end-of treatment. Anti-MSLN-TTC antibodies were determined in serum samples by a validated ligand binding assay with electrochemiluminescence readout. In detail, the determination of anti-MSLN-TTC antibodies was conducted on pre-treated serum samples by a validated direct ligand binding assay with electrochemiluminescence readout using Sulfo-Tag conjugated BAY 2287409 as detection reagent. Anti-MSLN-TTC antibodies present in the serum samples were precipitated with polyethylene glycol in the presence of an excess of BAY 2287409. Thereafter, immune complexes were dissociated under acidic conditions and free antibodies immobilized on the assay binding plate. Antibodies bound to the plate were detected with Sulfo-Tag conjugated BAY 2287409. Specificity was shown in a confirmation assay with an excess of BAY 2287409 in the detection step.

In selected subjects, whole body radioactivity of thorium-227 and radium-223 was measured using a HPGe detector (Mirion/Canberra Instruments, Atlanta, GA).

Results

Patient enrollment, baseline characteristics, and treatment

Forty-nine patients were enrolled; 13 failed screening due to study protocol criteria. A total of 36 patients were assigned to treatment and 35 were treated with at least one i.v. injection of thorium-227-labeled MSLN-TTC. One patient received only the non-radioactive Ab-chelator conjugate and was never treated with thorium-227 (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). In dose escalation, 35 patients were treated across seven MSLN-TTC dose cohorts every six weeks: 1.5 MBq with total Ab dose 10 mg (n = 4); 1.5 MBq with total Ab dose 30 mg (n = 4); 2.5 MBq with total Ab dose 10 mg (n = 4); 2.5 MBq with total Ab dose 30 mg (n = 6); 3.5 MBq with total Ab dose 10 mg (n = 5); 3.5 MBq with total Ab dose 50 mg (n = 4), and 3.5 MBq with total Ab dose 150 mg (n = 8). When the assigned total Ab dose was intended to be >10 mg, i.v. non-radioactive Ab-chelator conjugate was administered before i.v. thorium-227-labeled MSLN-TTC to achieve the assigned total Ab dose. All 36 patients have discontinued or completed the study treatment.

Baseline demographics and disease characteristics for the patients are presented in Table 1. The median age was 67 years (range 43-82 years) and the mean body weight 78.30 kg (±13.97 kg). The cancer types in the patient population were pleural mesothelioma (n = 30), peritoneal mesothelioma (n = 2), high-grade serous ovarian cancer (n = 3), and low-grade serous ovarian cancer (n = 1). A total of 5 patients (19.2%) had an H-score of 0-99, 9 (34.6%) an H-score of 100-199, and 12 (46.2%) an H-score of 200-300 (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590).

Safety

The maximum tolerated dose of MSLN-TTC could not be defined, and the number of discontinuations after the first cycle was high (n = 21, 58.3% of patients) (Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). Twelve (33.3%) of the discontinuations were due to progressive disease (PD), six (16.7%) due to ADA formation, two due to an AE, and one patient withdrew from the study. The high number of discontinuations resulted in lower than planned cumulative thorium-227 doses in the study population as a whole. No DLTs were reported for any of the patients across the seven dose cohorts during the 42-day DLT observation period. One patient, treated with 3.5 MBq thorium-227 and a total Ab dose of 10 mg, experienced a grade 3 DLT (thrombocytopenia, cycle 3,) that was classified as a multiple dose DLT (i.e. an event occurring after the end of the DLT period but would otherwise meet the criteria for a DLT).

Of the 36 treated patients, 35 were reported to have experienced at least one TEAE. The most frequent TEAEs of any grade occurring in >10% of patients were fatigue (n = 10, 27.8%), decreased lymphocyte count (n = 7, 19.4%), nausea (n = 7, 19.4%), anemia (n = 6, 16.7%), infusion-related reaction (n = 6, 16.7%), diarrhea (n = 5, 13.9%), vomiting (n = 5, 13.9%), dyspnea (n = 5, 13.9%), upper respiratory tract infection (n = 4, 11.1%), decreased appetite (n = 4, 11.1%), musculoskeletal chest pain (n = 4, 11.1%), and cough (n = 4, 11.1%) (Table 2). The highest grade of reported TEAE was grade 3, and these were decreased lymphocyte count (n = 6, 16.7%), anemia (n = 3, 8.3%), nausea (n = 3, 8.3%), decreased appetite (n = 1, 2.8%), diarrhea (n = 1, 2.8%), dyspnea (n = 1, 2.8%), and infusion-related reaction (n = 1, 2.8%).

Twenty-four patients were reported to have experienced at least one study drug-related TEAE (Supplementary Table S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). The most common study drug-related TEAEs of any grade occurring in ≥5% of patients were fatigue (n = 8, 22.2%), decreased lymphocyte count (n = 6, 16.7%), and infusion-related reaction (n = 6, 16.7%). There were no deaths due to MSLN-TTC-related adverse events (AE). The highest grade of reported study drug-related TEAE was grade 3, and these were decreased lymphocyte count (n = 5, 13.9%), anemia (n = 1, 2.8%), infusion-related reaction (n = 1, 2.8%), and thrombocytopenia (n = 1, 2.8%).

Ten patients (27.8%) experienced at least one treatment-emergent serious adverse event (TESAE). Two patients (5.6%) experienced at least one study drug-related TESAE which were infusion-related reactions of grade 2 and grade 3.

Four patients (11.1%) had a TEAE leading to dose modification (including interruption, delay, and dose reduction) of the study drug, and in three of these patients (8.3%) the TEAEs were considered study drug related. Seven patients (19.4%) had at least one TEAE leading to permanent discontinuation of the study drug and five patients (13.9%) had a study drug-related TEAE that led to permanent discontinuation of the study drug. One patient from the 3.5 MBq + 10 mg Ab group died during treatment. The patient had a grade 5 general physical health deterioration, and the cause of death was reported as an AE associated with clinical disease progression and considered unrelated to study treatment. Overall, 20 patients died during the 12-month follow-up period. The cause of death was reported to be ‘disease progression’ for 16, ‘unknown’ for 2, and ‘other’ or ‘adverse event’ for 1 of the patients.

Clinical activity

None of the patients treated with MSLN-TTC (n = 35) achieved complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) (Table 3). A total of 12 patients (34.3%) had stable disease (SD) and 17 patients (48.6%) had PD. Of the 12 patients with SD, 11 (31.4%) had pleural mesothelioma and 1 (2.9%) had peritoneal mesothelioma. There was no correlation between the response and the H-score of the tumors (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). The median progression-free survival was 70 days (95% CI 29-161 days) (Table 3 and Figure 1A and B).

Pharmacokinetics

Thorium-227 activity

The maximum blood activity of thorium-227 was typically observed between the end of the MSLN-TTC injection (5 min) and 30 min after the start of administration (Supplementary Figure S2A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). Thorium-227 activities could be measured until 35 days after the start of administration at the highest dose level of 3.5 MBq.

The maximum activities (Cmax) of thorium-227 increased proportionally to dose (Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). Thorium-227 generally showed a mean terminal half-life of 2.4-4.1 days, similar to that of the total Ab. The estimated volume of distribution ranged from 6.86 to 8.54 l. A low-to-moderate interpatient variability was observed in the pharmacokinetic parameter estimates for thorium-227 with a coefficient of variation (CV) between 18% and 50% for clearance (CL) and 6.6% and 32% for steady state volume of distribution (Vss).

The mean blood clearance of thorium-227 ranged from 0.065 to 0.13 l/h and seemed to be independent of both Ab and thorium-227 dose levels (Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590).

Radium-223 activity

The activity of radium-223, the first daughter nuclide of thorium-227, could only be measured in three samples representing two patients (2.5 MBq with total Ab dose 10 mg and 3.5 MBq with total Ab dose 150 mg). For all other samples, the analysis results for radium-223 were below the measurable detection limit.

Whole-body measurements of thorium-227 and radium-223

Thorium-227 and radium-223 whole body activity measurements after a single dose are presented in Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590. The whole-body half-life for thorium-227 was 15.7 days (n = 8), with a CV of 14%. This indicates that thorium-227 predominantly leaves the body via physical decay, as the observed half-life is very close to that of physical decay (18.7 days) (Block et al., submitted). For radium-223, lower whole-body activities than predicted based solely on physical decay were measured, indicating that biological elimination occurs in addition to physical decay.

Total antibody concentration

The plasma concentrations of Ab and thorium-227 activity in blood (decay corrected to time of administration) showed parallel decline, suggesting that the thorium-227 Ab-chelator conjugate is stable in plasma/whole blood (Supplementary Figure S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590).

Maximum plasma concentrations of total Ab in plasma were typically observed between the end of MSLN-TTC injection (5 min) and 30 min after the start of administration in the 10 mg treatment groups and between 1.3 and 4 h after the start of the non-radioactive BAY 2287409 Ab-chelator conjugate injection in treatment groups receiving two injections of a total of 30-150 mg. Afterwards, Ab concentrations declined in a similar fashion across all Ab and thorium-227 dose levels (Supplementary Figure S2B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590).

At the lowest Ab dose of 10 mg, total Ab could generally be measured until 7 days after administration. At the highest dose of 150 mg Ab, concentrations could still be measured until 21 days after start of administration.

After the first dose, the geometric mean values for the CL of total Ab ranged from 0.029 to 0.051 l/h and the estimated Vss ranged from 3.52 to 4.49 l, indicating dose proportional pharmacokinetics. Both the CL and Vss were independent of Ab dose and Ab/thorium ratio (Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). A low-to-moderate interpatient variability was observed in the pharmacokinetic parameter estimates for total Ab CL and Vss with CVs between 17% and 40% and 8% and 27%, respectively.

The mean terminal half-life of total Ab ranged from 2.1 days to 4.6 days (Supplementary Table S7, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). The mean half-life estimated for the 50 mg and 150 mg dose levels was slightly higher (4.3 and 4.6 days) than for the 10 mg and 30 mg dose levels (ranging from 2.1 to 3.3 days).

Immunogenicity

Of the 35 patients, 1 patient had a positive ADA before dosing (subject ID 300020006 with a titer of 4), which is well below the selected cut-off of >32 for a subject not to get dosed in subsequent cycles. The subject was ADA-negative at the end of cycle 1, day 29. A high incidence of at least one ADA positive sample after at least one administration of MSLN-TTC was observed in 51.5% of the patients, which translated to 45.7% of the patients having NABs across all dose levels (Supplementary Table S8, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). No further doses of MSLN-TTC were administered if the ADA titer was 32 or higher at the end of the cycle, resulting in six (16.7%) patients discontinuing treatment (an action based on previous experience with the ADC anetumab ravtansine).

The development of NABs was associated with changes in the pharmacokinetics of both thorium-227 and the Ab, and a substantial change in the biodistribution of thorium-227 (Supplementary Figure S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590). The change in biodistribution resulted in rapid elimination of the toxophore thorium-227 from the bloodstream to the liver, most likely followed by excretion through the intestine. Therefore, these patients were not expected to benefit from the treatment anymore and for safety reasons discontinued the study. Supplementary Table S9, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590, provides an overview of the titer values per cycle for all subjects who had at least one positive post-dose ADA result and Supplementary Figure S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.10590, of the thorium-227 activity and total Ab concentration profiles of an exemplary patient with decreasing concentrations in later cycles after ADA and NAB formation.

Discussion

MSLN-TTC is a targeted alpha therapy consisting of the mesothelin-targeted fully human monoclonal Ab anetumab, conjugated with a 3,2-HOPO chelator and radiolabeled with alpha particle-emitting thorium-227.6 The rationale of targeted alpha therapy relies on the targeted delivery of potent alpha particle-emitting radionuclides that emit high linear energy transfer radiation that induces difficult-to-repair DNA DSBs that force cells into apoptotic and necrotic cell death.4 Targeted alpha therapy is expected to provide new treatment options resulting in improved patient outcomes in previously hard-to-treat forms of cancer.

This phase I study investigated the pharmacokinetics, safety, tolerability, and tumor response of MSLN-TTC in patients with mesothelin-expressing pleural or peritoneal mesothelioma or serous ovarian cancer. In general, BAY 2284711 was tolerated up to the highest tested thorium-227 level of 3.5 MBq with a total Ab dose of 150 mg, with some patients receiving multiple cycles of treatment with the highest cumulative dose being 10.5 MBq. None of the patients experienced a DLT, and only one patient had an AE (thrombocytopenia on cycle 3) that could be classified as a multiple dose DLT. It should be noted that most of the patients discontinued treatment after the first dose, with the most frequent reason for discontinuation being progression of disease rather than TEAEs, whereas repeated dosing was limited due to immunogenicity and TEAEs after repeated dosing in about half of the patients. Although 24 patients were reported to have experienced drug-related TEAEs, only 8 patients experienced grade 3 TEAEs, which was also the highest reported grade. Additionally, only two patients experienced a drug-related TESAE, both of which were infusion-related reactions of grade 2 and grade 3. The most common drug-related TEAEs resulting from BAY 2284711 treatment were hematological toxicities, i.e. anemia, thrombocytopenia, and a decrease in lymphocyte count. As hematological toxicities are reportedly not common with other types of mesothelin-targeting therapies, such as amatuximab16 and anetumab ravtansine,9 this may suggest that these hematological complications arise from the alpha particle-emitting payload. In fact, similar toxicities have been reported with other radiotherapeutic agents utilizing thorium-227, actinium-225, and radium-223,17, 18, 19, 20 albeit with various targeting strategies.

MSLN-TTC did not elicit extensive tumor response. None of the MSLN-TTC-treated patients achieved a CR or PR. Twelve patients, however, did achieve SD, but the treatment was not continued as this is generally not considered a sign of objective response. In some patients the lack of response can be explained by the formation of ADAs. ADAs may inactivate drug molecules, reduce targeting, and increase clearance, which can lead to loss of efficacy.10,11 In this study, the formation of ADAs occurred in 51.4% of patients, which led in several patients to rapid elimination of thorium-227 and the Ab from the blood. The development of ADAs for mesothelin-directed antibodies and the anti-mesothelin immunotoxin SS1P is well documented in the literature.9,21 Although during this study higher Ab doses were tested to attempt to reduce the formation of ADAs, the high incidence rate observed suggests that ultimately this strategy did not work, at least with the relative low Ab doses of 10-150 mg administered per patient. Furthermore, the strategy of increasing the Ab dose is inevitably limited by the potential for cold Ab saturation of mesothelin expressed on tumor lesions, which would also impact potential efficacy by blocking the radiolabeled Ab-chelator conjugate.

In order to target the tumor tissue, it is important that the thorium-227 Ab-chelator conjugate is stable, which was suggested by the parallel decline in Ab and thorium-227 concentrations in plasma/blood over time. Based on whole body measurements, it could be demonstrated that thorium-227 is mainly eliminated via physical decay, whereas the daughter nuclide radium-223 is eliminated relatively quickly after recoil from thorium-227, an observation in line with known experience with i.v. injections of radium-223 dichloride (Xofigo®) in which radium-223 was primarily excreted into the intestine.22, 23, 24

In summary, although most of the patients discontinued the treatment after the first dose, the results show that MSLN-TTC was well tolerated, at least with the low cumulative doses administered during this study. Additionally, a relatively high incidence of ADA formation was observed in the patients, which in several patients led to faster elimination of thorium-227 and Ab from the blood and thus reduced possible exposure of the tumors. This highlights the importance of understanding the relevance of ADA formation in drug development. The absence of a clinical response with several patients progressing in cycle 1 and the observations of a high ADA incidence leading in many cases to rapid elimination from blood makes it unlikely that BAY 2284711 would result in a meaningful benefit for patients suffering from mesothelin-expressing tumors and will thus not be further pursued as a drug candidate.

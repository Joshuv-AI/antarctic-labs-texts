1.
Introduction

Srinivasa Ramanujan
[
13
]
introduced infinite Fourier series like expansions in the form
\sum\limits_{r}a_{r}c_{r}(n)
for several arithmetical functions where
c_{r}(n)
is the
Ramanujan sum
defined by

\displaystyle c_{r}(n):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,r)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imn}{r}}.

When such an infinite series representation exists for an arithmetical function
f
(mostly only pointwise convergent),
f
is said to possess a
Ramanujan-Fourier series expansion
or simply a
Ramanujan expansion
. Such expansions were computed for various functions, and several conditions were provided for the existence of Ramanujan expansions by many authors in papers like
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

E. Cohen generalized the Ramanujan sum in
[
5
]
defining the
Cohen-Ramanujan sum

(1)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{s}(n):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=1\\
{(h,r^{s})_{s}=1}\end{subarray}}^{r^{s}}e^{\frac{2\pi inh}{r^{s}}}.

When
s=1
, this sum reduces to the usual Ramanujan sum. In Cohen-Ramanujan sum,
(m,n)_{s}
denotes the the
generalized GCD
of
m
and
n
. It is defined to be equal to the largest positive integer
l^{s}
such that
l^{s}|m
and
l^{s}|n
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Analogous to Ramanujan expansions, an arithmetical function
f
is said to admit a
Cohen-Ramanujan expansion

\displaystyle f(n):=\sum\limits_{r}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n),

if the series on the right hand side converges for suitable complex numbers
\widehat{f}(r)
. These authors derived some conditions for the existence of such expansions in
[
4
]
.

E. Cohen
[
7
]
himself gave another generalization of the Ramanujan sum using
k
-vectors. A
k
-vector is an ordered set
\{x_{i}\}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
of
k
integers. Two
k
-vectors
\{x_{i}\}
and
\{y_{i}\}
are congruent (mod
k,r
) if
x_{i}\equiv y_{i}
(mod
r
),
i=1,\ldots,k
. For
k\geq 1
, Cohen defined

(2)

\displaystyle c^{k}(n,r)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{(\{x_{i}\},r)=1}e^{\frac{2\pi in(x_{1}+x_{2}+\ldots x_{k})}{r}},

where
\{x_{i}\}
ranges over a residue system (mod
k,r
). For positive integers
k
and
n
, the
Jordan totient function

J_{k}(n)
is defined to be the number of ordered sets of
k
elements from a complete residue system (mod
n
) such that the greatest common divisor of each set is prime to
n
.
A product formula for
J_{k}

[
1
]
similar to the one for the Euler totient function
\varphi
is

\displaystyle J_{k}(n)=n^{k}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}).

Hence
J_{1}(n)=\varphi(n)
.

Using the sum (
2
), Sivaramakrishnan
[
14
]
derived the infinite expansion

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{J_{s}(n^{s})}{n^{s}}\zeta(s+k)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(r)c^{k}(n,r)}{J_{s+k}(r)},

where
s
and
k
are positive integers,
\mu
is the Möbius function and
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function.

Analogous to expansion (
3
), we here derive the following identity involving Cohen-Ramanujan sum.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
s,k\geq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{J_{k}(n)}{n^{k}}=\sum\limits_{q}\frac{\mu(q)}{\zeta(s+k)J_{s+k}(q)}c_{q}^{s}(n^{s}).

H. Gadiyar, M. Ram Murthy and R. Padma
[
10
]
derived an asymptotic formula for the sum

\sum\limits_{n\leq N}f(n)g(n+h),

where
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Ramanujan expansions.
Inspired by this, for arithmetical functions
f
and
g
with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions, an asymptotic formula for
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)
was provided by these authors in
[
3
]
.
We use this asymptotic formula to derive the following symptotic identity which is similar to
[
12
, Corollary 2]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
s>1
,
a,b>1+\frac{s}{2}
and
h=m^{s}k
, where
k
is
s-
power free integer, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\leq N}\frac{J_{a}(n)}{n^{a}}\frac{J_{b}(n+h)}{(n+h)^{b}}

\displaystyle\sim N\prod\limits_{p\mid m}\Big(\big(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}}\big)\big(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}}\big)+\frac{p^{s-1}}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{p\nmid m}\Big(\big(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}}\big)\big(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}}\big)-\frac{1}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big).

2.
Proofs of the Results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
s,k\geq 1
, by the multiplicative properties of
\mu,J_{s},c^{*}_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q}\frac{\mu(q)c_{q}^{s}(n^{s})}{J_{s+k}(q)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\mu(p)c_{p}^{s}(n^{s})}{J_{s+k}(p)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\mu(p)c_{p}^{s}(n^{s})}{J_{s+k}(p)}\right)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\mu(p)c_{p}^{s}(n^{s})}{J_{s+k}(p)}\right)

By the properties of the Cohen-Ramanujan sum
[
6
, Equation 1.5]
, if
p
is a prime, we have

\displaystyle c^{s}_{p^{r}}(n)=\begin{cases}p^{sr}-p^{s(r-1)}&\text{ if }p^{sr}|n\\
-p^{s(r-1)}&\text{ if }p^{s(r-1)}|n,\,p^{sr}\nmid n\\
0&\text{ if }p^{s(r-1)}\nmid n\\
\end{cases}

and so

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q}\frac{\mu(q)c_{q}^{s}(n^{s})}{J_{s+k}(q)}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{p^{s}-1}{p^{s+k}-1})\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\nmid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1+\frac{1}{p^{s+k}-1})

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\end{subarray}}(\frac{1}{1-p^{-(s+k)}})\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}(1-\frac{1}{p^{k}}).

Now by
[
2
, Lemma 1.2]
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta(z)=\prod\limits_{p}\frac{1}{1-p^{-z}}.

Further,
\zeta(z)
converges for all
z
with
Re(z)\geq 1
.

Hence the last step in the above computations becomes equal to

\displaystyle\zeta(k+s)\frac{J_{k}(n)}{n^{k}}

which is what we claimed in the statement of the theorem.
∎

Definition 2.1
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, by
\tau_{k}(n)
we mean the number of positive integers
l^{k}
dividing
n
with
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

The following asymptotic result is from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.2]

Suppose that
f
and
g
are two arithmetical functions with absolutely convergent Cohen-Ramanujan expansions

f(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)c_{r}^{s}(n)
and
g(n)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(k)c_{k}^{s}(n)

respectively. Suppose that
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,k\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(k)|(r^{s}k^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(k^{s})<\infty
. Then as
N
tends to infinity,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\end{subarray}}f(n)g(n+h)\sim N\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(r)\widehat{g}(r)c_{r}^{s}(h)
.

Now we use this asymptotic identity along with the identity in Theorem
1.1
to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We consider the convergence of the sum

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,t\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(r)||\widehat{g}(t)

\displaystyle|(r^{s},t^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau_{s}(r^{s})\tau_{s}(t^{s})

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,t\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{J_{s+a}(r)\zeta(s+a)}\frac{1}{J_{s+b}(t)\zeta(s+b)}(r^{s}t^{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{s}t^{s}.

Since
\frac{1}{J_{s}(r)}<\frac{1}{r^{s}}
, and
a,b>1+\frac{s}{2}
, the above sum converges. Therefore by Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
2.2
, we have by the multiplicative property of
J_{s},\mu
and
c_{r}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\frac{J_{a}(n)}{n^{a}}

\displaystyle\frac{J_{b}(n+h)}{(n+h)^{b}}

\displaystyle\sim N\sum\limits_{r}\frac{\mu(r)}{J_{s+a}(r)\zeta(s+a)}\frac{\mu(r)}{J_{s+b}(r)\zeta(s+b)}c_{r}^{s}(h)

\displaystyle=N\sum\limits_{r}\frac{\mu(r)}{J_{s+a}(r)\zeta(s+a)}\frac{\mu(r)}{J_{s+b}(r)\zeta(s+b)}c_{r}^{s}(m^{s})\hskip 10.0pt(\text{since }c_{r}^{s}(m^{s}k)=c_{r}^{s}(m^{s}))

\displaystyle=N\prod\limits_{p}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})\big(1+\frac{c_{p}^{s}(m^{s})}{(p^{s+a}-1)(p^{s+b}-1)}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=N\prod\limits_{p}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})+\frac{c_{p}^{s}(m^{s})}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big)

\displaystyle=N\prod\limits_{p\mid m}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})+\frac{p^{s}-1}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big)\prod\limits_{p\nmid m}\Big((1-\frac{1}{p^{s+a}})(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+b}})-\frac{1}{p^{a+b+2s}}\Big).

∎

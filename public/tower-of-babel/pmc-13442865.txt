Revealing novel subtypes of synovial sarcoma through single cell transcriptomics

ABSTRACT
Synovial sarcoma (SyS) is a common type of soft tissue sarcoma, characterized by slow growth, high rates of metastasis, and diverse clinical symptoms. This malignancy is defined by the presence of the SS18-SSX fusion protein and the corresponding SS18-SSX program that promotes the core oncogenic pathways and a wide spectrum of tumor cell phenotypes. In this study, we used single-cell RNA sequencing to explore SyS diversity, highlighting four molecular subtypes. The mesenchymal NKD2+ SyS is characterized by the increased BMP signaling in tumor cells and the most severe immune escape and chemoresistance profile. The poorly differentiated HOXD11+ SyS displays the highest expression of the core oncogenic program and the likely suppression of the immune response through CXCL and MIF signaling. The fibroblast-like DCN+ SyS demonstrates high levels of mesenchymal differentiation, a surprising drop in the SS18-SSX program expression, and the angiogenesis-oriented cell-cell interactions. The biphasic EPCAM+ SyS has the highest levels of differentiation and lowest levels of the core oncogenic program. Our data complements the traditional classification of SyS and suggests that the monophasic mesenchymal SyS might consist of two molecular subtypes.

Introduction

Synovial sarcoma (SyS) is a relatively common and aggressive mesenchymal neoplasm, which constitutes 5-10% of all soft tissue sarcoma cases1. SyS is elusive due to slow initial growth, prone to metastasis, and has a high misdiagnosis rate because of diverse symptoms2. SyS response to chemotherapy is moderate, and radiotherapy combined with surgery remains the most common and effective treatment combination3,4.

The hallmark of SyS is the presence of the SS18-SSX fusion protein5, which interferes with the function of the SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex and the polycomb repressive complex (PRC2)6, dysregulates multiple receptor tyrosine kinase pathways (EPH, FGF, PDGF, IGF, and VEGF)7–11 and developmental pathways (WNT, Hedgehog, Notch, and Hippo)8,12, and activates the SOX2-mediated pluripotency-associated tumorigenesis13. The stem cell signaling pathways result in a high heterogeneity of SyS cells, which range from mesenchymal spindle-like cells to epithelial-like cells, with varying distribution in each tumor. The SS18-SSX fusion program promotes the core oncogenic program, which is predominantly expressed in the cycling and poorly differentiated SyS cells, controls cell proliferation, and correlates with SyS aggressiveness14.

Tumor microenvironment (TME) in SyS is scarce, with the immune cell escape correlating with the core oncogenic program14. The few CD8+ T cells present in the SyS TME overexpress effector and cytotoxic gene modules, and underexpress exhaustion gene modules. SyS macrophages display both pro-inflammatory and pro-tumor states14. Repression of the SS18-SSX/core oncogenic/cell proliferation programs through HDAC and CDK2/6 inhibitors leads to improved T cell recognition and killing of SyS cells in vitro14. Therefore, it can be suggested that SyS tumor populations with different expression levels of SS18-SSX/core oncogenic programs could create different TME niches, which would lead to the creation of unique tumor subtypes.

Histologically, SyS are divided into mesenchymal-only (monophasic), mesenchymal and epithelial (biphasic), and poorly differentiated types15. Bulk RNA sequencing and targeted genomic analysis also identified hyperproliferative, vascular-stromal, and biphasic subtypes of SyS16. However, the traditional classification does not take into account the tumor cell heterogeneity within mesenchymal and epithelial types and the consequent differences in TME that contribute to SyS chemo- and radioresistance and poor prognosis. In this study, we performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on tumor samples from 16 SyS patients to acquire a thorough portrait of SyS intratumoral and intertumoral heterogeneity. Four molecular SyS subtypes with unique cell and molecular characteristics, including differentiation states, TME cell populations, cell interaction patterns, and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities, were identified. The SyS subtypes were verified on previously published bulk RNA-seq data of 55 SyS patients16. The therapeutic modalities for the SyS subtypes were examined in vitro.

The NKD2+ TC is the largest tumor cluster observed in all 16 cases including 13 monophasic and 3 biphasic SyS (Fig. 2A, G). This cluster expresses the mesenchymal gene module and several MET markers (Fig. 2D, E) at a higher level than other non-epithelial clusters. NKD2+ TC is enriched in processes related to synaptic transmission, cell-cell, and BMP signaling pathways (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 3) and possesses medium differentiation levels (Fig. 2C). The expression of the core oncogenic program in this cluster is medium, while the SS18-SSX program is more pronounced compared to the HOXD11+ and the MKI67+ TC (Fig. 2C).

The HOXD11+ TC is a small tumor cluster observed in 9 out of 16 SyS patients (8 monophasic and 1 biphasic cases; Fig. 2 A, G). This cluster expresses the mesenchymal gene module with enrichment in the HOXD superfamily of developmental genes (HOXD3/4/8/9/10/11), oxidative phosphorylation, and metabolic pathways (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 3). The differentiation level is high with additional expression of cancer stem cell marker ALDH2 (Fig. 2F). The expression of both SS18-SSX and core oncogenic programs is high (Fig. 2C).

The DCN+ TC/CAF is observed in 13 out of 16 SyS patients (10 monophasic and 3 biphasic cases; Fig. 2A, G). This cluster has the highest expression of the mesenchymal gene module (Fig. 2E) with enrichment in extracellular matrix remodeling (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 3), which links these cells to fibroblast-like identity. The cell differentiation is heterogenous. The most surprising trait of DCN+ TC/CAF is the high expression of the core oncogenic program, coupled with the consistently low expression of the SS18-SSX program (Fig. 2C). CNV analysis suggested that this cluster likely contains both tumor cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs): while most cells showed tumor-characteristic CNV profiles, a minor fraction - particularly in samples where other tumor clusters predominate - displayed low CNV signals indicative of CAFs (Supplementary Figure 3). Given the technical limitations in definitively separating these populations, the DCN+ TC/CAF cluster was treated as a single entity.

The EPCAM+ TC is present in 8 out of 16 SyS patients including 5 monophasic and 3 biphasic SyS cases (Fig. 2A, G). This cluster expresses the epithelial gene module only (Fig. 2E) with marked downregulation of extracellular matrix remodelling and collagen metabolic process (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 3). EPCAM+ TC is highly differentiated with high expression of the SS18-SSX program and decreased expression of the core oncogenic program (Fig. 2C).

The MKI67+ TC is present in 13 out of 16 SyS patients (10 monophasic and 2 biphasic cases), and is relatively small in proportion to other tumor clusters (Fig. 2A, G). This cluster expresses the mesenchymal gene module (Fig. 2E) and is enriched in proliferative gene signatures related to the cell cycle and DNA replication (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Data 3). MKI67+ TC has the lowest differentiation out of all SyS clusters with the high expression of both SS18-SSX and core oncogenic programs (Fig. 2C).

The four molecular SyS subtypes are linked to tumor cluster representation

The analysis of the proportions of tumor and TME cell clusters for 16 SyS patients revealed the presence of one prevailing TC in each patient. Based on the prevailing tumor cluster representation, 4 subtypes of SyS were identified: NKD2+ SyS (6 monophasic cases), HOXD11+ SyS (3 monophasic cases), DCN+ SyS (3 monophasic cases), and EPCAM+ SyS (1 monophasic and 3 biphasic cases) (Fig. 2G). To expand the limited size of our study group, we additionally verified the presence of SyS TC signatures and the resulting subtype distribution on the publicly available bulk RNA-seq dataset of 55 SyS patients (Supplementary Figure 116).

To investigate the biology of the 4 SyS subtypes, we evaluated the differences in their TC composition, TME populations and signatures, probabilities of specific tumor-immune cell communications, and potential responses to therapy.

The NKD2+ SyS: the mesenchymal subtype with the most pronounced immune escape

The NKD2+ SyS contains monophasic SyS cases, according to the histological analysis and the comparison of TC signatures with previously published bulk RNA-seq data16. NKD2+ SyS includes the prevailing mesenchymal-rich NKD2+ TC and the less pronounced proliferative MKI67+ TC. In 3 out of 6 cases, the minor presence of the HOXD11+ TC is also observed (Fig. 2G). The NKD2+ SyS has the most pronounced immune escape compared to other SyS subtypes. We investigated the correlation between the levels of the core oncogenic and SS18-SSX programs and SyS cell type abundance (Fig. 3A) and between the abundance of TC and TME cell populations (Fig. 3B, C). The abundance of the NKD2+ TC inversely correlates with the abundance of the lymphoid, myeloid, and mast cell clusters, according to our scRNA-seq data and the previously published bulk RNA-seq data (Fig. 3B, C16). The NKD2+ SyS demonstrates the lowest presence of the CD4+ T cells, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, and NK cells among the SyS subtypes (Fig. 3D). In addition to having fewer myeloid cells overall, NKD2+ SyS has significantly lower proportions of the monocyte-derived cluster and higher proportions of the resident tissue macrophage cluster compared to other SyS subtypes (Fig. 3D). Macrophages in NKD2+ SyS predominantly display anti-inflammatory M2-like signatures, with almost no M1-like signatures that are present in the other SyS subtypes (Fig. 3H). The NKD2+ SyS macrophages have increased possibility of communicating through GAS6-mediated MerTK signaling, which has been previously shown to promote protumor M2c polarization (Fig. 4D18). We observed few signaling patterns between the tumor and TME cells that are specific to this SyS subtype. The EDN signaling specific to the NKD2+ TC is likely used to send signals to the endothelial cells (Fig. 4B), while the NKD2+ SyS M2-like macrophages have increased probability of sending signals to tumor cells through the PDGF pathway (Fig. 4D).

NKD2+ SyS has a wider spectrum of chemotherapy resistance in vitro compared to other SyS subtypes (Fig. 5A, B). The HOXD11+ SyS: the poorly differentiated subtype with immunosuppression

The HOXD11+ SyS corresponds to the poorly differentiated SyS according to the validation on the bulk RNA-seq dataset (Supplementary Figure 116). HOXD11+ SyS includes the prevailing HOXD11+ TC and the less pronounced NKD2+ and the MKI67+ TC (Fig. 2G). HOXD11+ TC has increased probability of ncWNT (WNT5A/WNT11), FGF and NTF signaling to communicate with other tumor clusters (Fig. 4B).

HOXD11+ SyS has richer immune TME in comparison with NKD2+ SyS. The abundance of the HOXD11+ TC has a slight inverse correlation with the immune cluster abundance, however this tendency is not observed on the published bulk RNA-seq data (Fig. 3B, C16). The increased probability of IL16 and TGFb signaling is observed between HOXD11+ SyS lymphoid cells (Fig. 4F). The HOXD11+ SyS macrophages express a mixture of M1-like and M2-like signatures with elevated levels of the antigen-presenting gene signature (HLA-DRA/B1, HLA-DPA1/B119) (Fig. 3H). The presence of proinflammatory traits in HOXD11+ macrophages correlates with the increased probability of the C3 complement communication between macrophages in this subtype (Fig. 4D). The HOXD11+ SyS macrophages also likely signal to each other using the SPP1 and GRN pathways (Fig. 4D). The tumor-immune interactions in the HOXD11+ SyS are characterized by the increased probability of the immunosuppressive CXCL (CXCL12-CXCR4) and MIF signaling (MIF-(CD74 + CD44)) that the tumor cells use to affect the myeloid and lymphoid cells (Fig. 4B). In turn, HOXD11+ SyS T cells likely signal to tumor clusters through CD6 and CD96 pathways (Fig. 4E, F). NcWNT, CXCL, and FGF pathways are likely used by the HOXD11+ tumor cells to extensively interact with endothelial cells (Fig. 4B).

In vitro assay demonstrates that HOXD11+ SyS is resistant to one of the two tested DNA-damaging agents (gemcitabine) and the multi-kinase inhibitor (pazopanib) (Fig. 5). Filanesib (ARRY-520), a kinesin spindle protein inhibitor, is predicted to have potential therapeutic effect, specific for this SyS subtype (Fig. 6).

The DCN+ SyS: the fibroblast-like subtype with activated angiogenesis and suppressed SS18-SSX program

The DCN+ SyS contains monophasic SyS cases according to our histological data and the validation on the bulk RNA-seq dataset16. DCN+ SyS includes the prevailing fibroblast-like DCN+ TC/CAF TC and the less pronounced NKD2+ and the MKI67+ TC. The prevailing DCN+ TC/CAF likely uses the IGF, IGFBP, PTN, and SEMA5 signaling pathways for extensive communication between tumor cells (Fig. 4B).

The abundance of the DCN+/CAF TC positively correlates with the abundance of the myeloid and mast cell clusters, according to our scRNA-seq data and the previously published bulk RNA-seq data (Fig. 3B, C16). The DCN+ SyS macrophages express a mixture of M1-like and M2-like signatures (Fig. 3H) and are enriched in genes related to angiogenesis (VEGFA) and metallothioneins (MT1E/F/G/H/X) (Fig. 3H). The cell-cell communication analysis confirms the increased probability of VEGF signaling between DCN+ SyS macrophages and endothelial cells. The THBS signaling is also likely used by the DCN+ SyS macrophages to communicate with lymphoid and myeloid clusters (Fig. 4D). The tumor-immune interactions in the DCN+ SyS demonstrates a specific interaction signature that likely allows it to put pressure on immune populations and stimulate angiogenesis through endothelial cells. The DCN+ TC/CAF likely sends signals to the myeloid cluster through the Complement (C3) system, to all immune clusters through the ADGRE communication network, and to endothelial cells through IGFBP, VEGF, and PTN signaling (Fig. 4B). The DCN+ SyS macrophages have increased probability of communicating with the TC through the visfatin and EGF pathways (Fig. 4D).

In vitro assay indicates DCN+ SyS resistance to DNA-damaging agents (gemcitabine, ifosfamide) and the multi-kinase inhibitor (pazopanib) (Fig. 5). Tirbanibulin (KX2-391), the inhibitor of the Src kinase, is suggested as the subtype-specific therapy option (Fig. 6).

The EPCAM+ SyS: the highly differentiated mesenchymal-epithelial subtype

The EPCAM+ SyS corresponds to the biphasic SyS cases, as confirmed by the validation on the bulk RNA-seq dataset (Supplementary Figure 116). In this subtype, the highly-differentiated, epithelial-like EPCAM+ TC is prevalent, while the NKD2+, DCN+, HOX11+, and MKI67+ TC are less pronounced. The OCLN, CDH1, CDH3, and TGFb pathways are likely used by the EPCAM+ TC for tumor-tumor communications (Fig. 4B). While the EPCAM+ TC contains the highest expression of epithelial markers, the NKD2+ TC might also contribute to MET, judging by its increased expression of KRT8 and MUC1 compared to HOXD11+ TC, DCN+/CAF TC, and MKI67+ TC (Fig. 2D16).

The abundance of the EPCAM+ TC positively correlates with the abundance of the lymphoid, myeloid and mast cell clusters, according to our scRNA-seq data and the previously published bulk RNA-seq data (Fig. 3B, C16). However, this subtype has the minimal amount of proliferating T cells and shows no significant increase in T cell activation signature (Fig. 3F20). EPCAM+ SyS demonstrates a mixture of M1-like and M2-like signatures (Fig. 3H) and the greatest abundance of mast cells compared to other SyS subtypes (Fig. 3D). The EPCAM+ SyS tumor-immune communications likely include the CDH1 signaling pathway, which connects tumor cells with lymphoid and mast cells (Fig. 4B).

In vitro assay shows that, similarly to HOXD11+ SyS, EPCAM+ SyS is resistant to one of the two tested DNA-damaging agents (gemcitabine) and the multi-kinase inhibitor (pazopanib) (Fig. 5). The CHK (checkpoint kinase) inhibitor AZD7762 is suggested as the potential therapy option specific for this SyS subtype (Fig. 6B).

Discussion

The synovial sarcomas are a highly heterogenous group, with their diversity ranging from tumor differentiation patterns to clinical symptoms. Single-cell transcriptomics is a powerful approach to explore intra- and intertumoral heterogeneity and reveal novel molecular cancer subtypes. In this study, scRNA-seq was used to identify four subtypes of SyS with distinct tumor cell clusters, TME populations and signatures, cellular communications, and potential chemotherapeutic vulnerabilities.

The NKD2+ SyS is the monophasic, mesenchymal-rich, most immune-excluded subtype with macrophages expressing protumor signature only. The possible PDGF communication between macrophages and tumor cells might contribute to the SyS growth and spread, as suggested by previous studies of PDGF signaling21. This subtype demonstrates the most chemoresistance in vitro; however, our in silico analysis suggests that mycophenolic acid, an inositol monophosphate dehydrogenase inhibitor, could be a candidate for investigation as a potential subtype-specific therapeutic agent.

The HOXD11+ SyS is the poorly differentiated subtype with high levels of the core oncogenic program and potential evidence of increased immune suppression. This subtype has a greater abundance of immune cells than the NKD2+ SyS subtype. This finding is surprising considering that the abundance of poorly differentiated SyS cells and high expression of the core oncogenic program have been previously linked to more severe immune exclusion14. In this regard, targeting the MIF and the CXCL axis between tumor and T cells and macrophages might be a strategy to modulate the immune response in HOXD11+ SyS. Both of these signaling networks have been previously noted to promote immunosuppressive TME in cancer22,23. Filanesib (ARRY-520), a kinesis spindle protein inhibitor, was identified as another potential candidate of interest for this subtype in our computational analysis.

The DCN+ SyS is the fibroblast-like subtype with an enrichment in angiogenesis, the minimal SS18-SSX program expression and the high core oncogenic program expression. The mechanism behind the decreased SS18-SSX program is unclear, but we hypothesize that the angiogenesis enriched in the DCN+/CAF cluster can shift the tumor cell phenotype. The angiogenesis can be regulated by both tumor cells and macrophages via the VEGF and visfatin signaling pathways. Surprisingly, the endothelial cell fraction is not significantly increased in the DCN+ SyS in comparison with other SyS subtypes. Based on our computational predictions, the inhibitor of the Src kinase, tirbanibulin (KX2-391), is highlighted as a potential therapeutic option specific for DCN+ SyS.

The EPCAM+ SyS is the highly differentiated, mesenchymal-epithelial subtype hypothetically driven by the increased expression of MET genes in the NKD2+ TC and its communication with the EPCAM+ TC. This subtype has the lowest core oncogenic program, which is reflected in the increased numbers of immune and mast cells. The EPCAM+ SyS shows in vitro chemoresistance similar to HOXD11+ SyS. The in silico predicted subtype-specific therapy can be the use of the CHK inhibitor AZD7762.

With regards to the traditional histological classification of SyS, single-cell transcriptomics allowed us to make several observations. The histological classification proved to not always be accurate, as we detected an epithelial-like EPCAM+ TC in a SyS case that was classified as monophasic. This discrepancy can be explained by the high sensitivity of the scRNA-seq method due to its ability to detect transcripts in individual cells.

There is no full alignment between the SyS subtypes identified here and in the previous bulk RNA-seq study16. Based on the highest levels of the core oncogenic program, the HOXD11+ subtype corresponds to the SSI subtype. The HOXD11+ TC shows enrichment in oxidative phosphorylation and Myc targets (Supplementary Figure 2), which matches the results for SSI GSEA Hallmark enrichment16. However, the HOXD11+ SyS subtype is not hyperproliferative, unlike the SSI subtype. The EPCAM+ SyS matches the SSIII subtype with its biphasic differentiation. The DCN+ SyS, rather than the NKD2+ SyS, has similarities with the vascular-stromal SSII subtype. The GSEA Hallmark analysis shows a correlation in the enrichment in EMT (epithelial-mesenchymal transition), inflammation, and complement for the DCN+/CAF TC and NKD2+ TC (Supplementary Figure 2) and the SSII subtype16. However neither DCN+ SyS nor NKD2+ SyS display a significant enrichment in the endothelial cell fraction, unlike the SSII subtype. Some of the described differences between the classifications can be explained by interpatient variability and issues arising from comparing bulk and single-cell transcriptome data. However, our study suggests that the SII subtype might be more heterogeneous than previously considered.

The previous scRNA-seq study of synovial sarcomas14 mostly matches our data. We were able to detect previously described SS18-SSX fusion and core oncogenic modules in SyS tumor cells, and the expression of either of those programs correlating inversely with the abundance of immune cells14. In addition, our data show that the expression of the core oncogenic program also correlates inversely with M2 polarization of macrophages and T cell exhaustion. Interestingly, the DCN+/CAF TC demonstrates a high expression of the core oncogenic program coupled with the focal mostly low expression of the SS18-SSX fusion program. The SS18-SSX fusion program regulates the core oncogenic program14, so it is puzzling to see cells with the high core oncogenic program and the lack of fusion program expression. This phenomenon needs to be examined further.

Several limitations should be noted to prevent misinterpretation of the acquired results. The Chromium Fixed RNA Profiling method used in this study examines the expression of 20,000 genes, which limits the detection of several genes. The scRNA-seq data needs to be validated on the protein level, especially the CellChat communication predictions. The suggested four-subtype classification of SyS requires more cases to acquire the full picture of each subtype. Additional SyS patient cohorts are also needed to check the replicability of the described subtypes. Finally, the drug candidates predicted in silico are hypothesis-generating and require further in vitro and in vivo (e.g., patient-derived xenograft) validation to assess their true therapeutic potential for SyS.

Taken together, this study demonstrates the presence of four SyS subtypes with distinct tumor cell composition affecting the TME, cell-cell communications, and a landscape of potential therapeutic vulnerabilities for future investigation.

Methods

Patients and samples

The study included 16 male and female patients aged 19–77, with the diagnosis of primary or recurring SyS (13 monophasic and 3 biphasic cases) and with or without neoadjuvant treatment (Supplementary Data 1). Surgically removed tumor samples (20–25 mg) were used to perform scRNA-seq.

Research involving human research participants, material, and data has been performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and has been approved by the Ethics Committee of N.N. Blokhin National Medical Research Center of Oncology (27 October 2020; Approval number: 2020-42). Informed consent to participate in the study and the future publication has been obtained from all the participating patients.

Sample preparation for single-cell RNA sequencing

The fixation and dissociation of tumor samples were performed according to the Tissue Fixation & Dissociation Protocol for Chromium Fixed RNA Profiling (10x Genomics, USA) using the DCS-400 single-cell suspension dissociator and the Tumor Enzymatic Dissociation Kit (RWD Life Science, China). After the dissociation, the cells were counted with acridine orange/propidium iodide staining buffer (Logos Bioscience, Korea) on the LUNA-FL Dual Fluorescence Cell Counter (Logos Bioscience, Korea). The dissociated cell suspension was stored at –80°C with 275 µl of 50% glycerol (Central Drug House Ltd, India) and 100 µl of Enhancer solution (10x Genomics, USA). Before the Chromium Fixed RNA Profiling, cells were thawed, centrifuged, resuspended in 0.5x PBS (phosphate-buffered saline, Diagene, Switzerland) with 0.02% BSA (bovine serum albumin, Dia-M, Russia), and counted again. For each SyS tumor sample, 8000 cells were taken into the scRNA-seq.

Single-cell RNA library construction and sequencing

The single-cell transcriptomic libraries were constructed in accordance with the Chromium Fixed RNA Protocol using the Chromium iX/X controller (10x Genomics, USA). The PCR (polymerase chain reaction) amplification was done on the QuantGene 9600 machine (Bioer Technology, China). The sequencing was performed on the Genolab M platform (GeneMind Biosciences, China) using 28 cycles (Read 1) and 90 cycles (Read 2).

Single-cell RNA data quality control and preprocessing

Single-cell RNA-seq data were demultiplexed and aligned to the GRCh38-2020-A reference genome using the Cell Ranger pipeline v.7.1.0 (10x Genomics, USA). All subsequent analyses were performed using the Seurat package v.5.3.024. Quality control (QC) filtering was applied to remove low-quality cells based on the following thresholds: cells with 300–10,000 detected genes, 500–20,000 unique molecular identifiers (UMIs), and a mitochondrial transcript content below 10%. Potential doublets were identified and removed using the DoubletFinder v.2.0.625 and Scrublet v.0.2.326, supplemented by manual inspection of cell clusters exhibiting high doublet scores. Data were normalized with the LogNormalize method and integrated with Harmony v.1.2.027 to correct for batch effects. The filtered cells were grouped into clusters by using the Leiden algorithm from the Seurat package.

Differential expression analysis and cluster functional profiling

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between cell clusters were identified using the FindMarkers function in Seurat with the Wilcoxon Rank Sum test. DEGs were defined by an absolute log fold change >1, an adjusted p value < 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg correction), and expression in at least 25% of cells in the cluster. Cell clusters were annotated using canonical cell-type marker genes. To characterize the biological functions of the cell clusters, Gene Ontology (GO), KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes), and Hallmark pathway enrichment analyses were performed on the DEG lists using the GSEA (Gene Set Enrichment Analysis) function from the clusterProfiler package v.4.16.028. Gene signature enrichment scores at the single-cell level were calculated using the UCell package v.2.7.329.

Identification of malignant cells

Malignant cells were identified by integrating copy number variation (CNV) profiles, differentiation state, and enrichment of synovial sarcoma (SyS)-specific gene signatures. CNVs were inferred using the InferCNV v.1.24.0 (github.com/broadinstitute/inferCNV), with lymphocytes, myeloid, endothelial, and mast cells serving as a reference. A CNV correlation score was calculated for each cell to distinguish malignant from normal cells, as described by Jerby-Arnon et al.14. Briefly, a sample-specific reference CNV profile was generated from the top 10% of cells with the highest CNV levels. Each cell’s individual CNV profile was then correlated to this reference using Spearman correlation. Cells were classified as malignant or normal by applying the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm to the resulting correlation scores for each sample individually. Differentiation states were assessed using the CytoTRACE2 v.1.1.030. Enrichment scores for SyS-specific signatures, including SyS malignant cell signature, a core oncogenic signature, and direct targets of SS18–SSX program (Supplementary Data 214,), were computed using the UCell package. Malignant identity was assigned to cell clusters exhibiting high CNV correlation scores, low differentiation scores, and high enrichment for SyS-specific signatures.

Cell-cell interaction analysis

Signaling pathways and ligand-receptor (L-R) interactions between cells were analyzed using the CellChat package v.2.2.031. To ensure robust inference, we excluded interactions involving cell groups with fewer than ten cells. Significant ligand-receptor interactions were defined as the ones, having p value < 0.05, logFC > 0, and cell percentage > 10. To compare communication patterns between tumor subtypes, we created individual CellChat objects for each subtype-specific group of samples. The resulting interaction probabilities, assessed at the level of L-R pairs or signaling pathways, were visualized using CellChat’s ‘comparison’ mode or custom plots generated with ggplot2.

Drug sensitivity prediction

Drug sensitivity predictions for tumor samples were generated using the ScTherapy package v.1.0.032. The standard ScTherapy workflow involves automated classification of cells into normal and malignant populations, followed by differential gene expression analysis and prediction using a pre-trained ML model. However, due to the limited number of normal cells in our dataset and to avoid potential misclassification by the automated method, we manually annotated malignant cells based on the CNV correlation score as described in the Method section ‘Identification of Malignant Cells’. Drug predictions were performed for each tumor sample individually. The resulting data were aggregated and visualized using ggplot2.

Validation on a publicly available dataset

To validate the sсRNA-seq findings, we utilized a bulk RNA-seq dataset of 55 SyS cases obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) under accession number GSE271517. Gene signature enrichment scores for each sample were calculated using the GSVA package v.2.2.0. Cell type composition was deconvoluted from the bulk data using the MuSiC package v.1.0.033, with our integrated and annotated sсRNA-seq dataset of 16 SyS cases serving as the reference.

Histological analysis

The fixation in formalin (Biovitrum, Russia) of tumor samples was done for 48 h. Dehydration and embedding in paraffin (Biovitrum, Russia) were performed according to standard methods. Hematoxylin & Eosin staining (Biovitrum, Russia) and histological analysis was performed on paraffin-embedded, formaldehyde-fixed tumor samples. Three stained sections of each tumor were subjected to analysis on the CX23 light microscope (Olympus, Japan).

Immunohistochemical analysis

Immunohistochemical analysis with a panel of antibodies was performed using 4-μm formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor sections according to the standard protocol. The staining was carried out with the fully automated diaminobenzidine antigen retrieval system (Benchmark ULTRA; Ventana Medical Systems, USA). Antigen-antibody complexes were stained with 3,3-diaminobenzidine (DAB) tetrahydrochloride using the ultraView Universal DAB Detection Kit (Ventana Medical Systems, Roche, USA). Quantification was achieved by counting signal-positive cells in 6 to 10 high-powered fields (×40 magnification) on the CX23 light microscope (Olympus, Japan). Positive staining for TLE-1, CD99, S100, VIM, CD56 and negative staining for MYOD1, DES, MYOG1, and MYF4 were considered indicative of both monophasic and epithelial (biphasic) types of SyS (Fig. 1A). Positive staining for PCK1 and EMA was considered indicative of the presence of the epithelial component in the tumor.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization

The SS18-SSX translocation was confirmed by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) following the Optimised Dako FISH protocol34 using the SYT-SSX break apart probe (Cytocell, UK) and the modified Dako Histology FISH Accessory Kit (Agilent, United States).

Primary cell cultures

Tumor samples were fragmented to the size of 1 mm3 and dissociated via incubation in 5–10 mL of sterile collagenase solution (Roche, Switzerland) for 2–3 h at 37 °C in the Incubator 1000 (Heidolph, Germany). Cell cultures were grown in DMEM (PanEco, Russia) medium with 20% FBS (fetal bovine serum, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), 292 mg glutamine (PanEco, Russia), and 100 U/ml penicillin/streptomycin (PanEco, Russia).

In vitro chemotherapy sensitivity assay

SyS primary culture cells were seeded on 96-well microplates at the density of 10000 cells/well. The tested chemotherapy drugs were added in six concentrations as follows: 6.25%, 12.5%, 25%, 50%, 100%, and 200% of standard concentrations described previously in our work35. The following compounds were used: pazopanib (Acmec biochemica, China), Doxorubicin (N.N. Blokhin National Medical Research Center of Oncology, Russia), Docetaxel (NATIVA, Russia), Gemcitabine (BIOCAD, Russia), and 4-hydroxy-ifosfamide (4-OH-Ifo, NIOMECH, Germany). The plates were incubated for 5-6 days at 37 °C, humidity of 95%, and 5% CO2. Cell viability was analyzed based on metabolic activity using resazurin (Invitrogen, USA), as described earlier35. Sensitivity/resistance index (SRI) for each tested drug was calculated as 600 minus sum of % TGI at 200, 100, 50, 25, 12.5, and 6.25 TDC, where TGI is the tumor growth inhibition percentage and TDC is the standard testing drug concentration (% from peak plasma concentration of the tested drug). Thus, SRI = 600 indicates a complete cell viability in the presence of the drug (lack of drug sensitivity), whereas SRI = 0 indicates complete cell death in the presence of the tested agent (maximum sensitivity to the drug)36. To divide the samples into sensitive and resistant ones, we used a threshold value of SRI = 250 as shown previously35,37. The samples with SRI > 250 were considered resistant to the drug, and the samples with SRI ≤ 250 were sensitive.

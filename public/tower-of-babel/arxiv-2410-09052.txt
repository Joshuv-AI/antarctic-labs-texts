On z z -ideals and z z -closure operations of semirings, I
Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. The aim of this series of papers is to study z z -ideals of semirings. In this article, we introduce some distinguished classes of z z -ideals of semirings, which include z z -prime, z z -semiprime, z z -irreducible, and z z -strongly irreducible ideals and study some of their properties. Using a z z -closure operator, we show the equivalence of these classes of ideals with the corresponding z z -ideals that are prime, semirprime, irreducible, and strongly irreducible respectively.

In this section, we provide a brief overview of some key definitions and results concerning semirings and their ideals. For detailed proofs and further exploration of these topics, we refer to
[
22

for all

We write

for

, and assume that all our semirings are commutative.
An

\mathfrak{a}
of a semiring

(i)
a+b\in\mathfrak{a}
and (ii)

,
for all
a,

b\in\mathfrak{a}
, and for all
s\in S.
By

S
. We denote the largest ideal of

as

. An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is called

\mathfrak{a}\neq S
. We denote the set of proper ideals of a semiring

\mathrm{Id}^{+}(S)
. If
\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathrm{Id}(S)

\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\in\mathrm{Id}(S)
. The

of a family
\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\in I}

\sum_{i\in I}\mathfrak{a}_{i}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{i_{j}}\mid x_{i_{j}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{i_{j}},n\in\mathds{N}\right\},

S
. If
\mathfrak{a}
and

is the ideal generated by the set

The following self-evident fact about ideals in semirings will prove to be valuable.

Lemma 2.1

If
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are two ideals of a semiring

\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}
.

A semiring

The

is also an ideal of

. A proper ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is called a

A proper ideal
\mathfrak{p}

or

b\in S.
By

, we denote the set of all prime ideals of a semiring

3.

Since by

Definition 3.1
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{z}
of a semiring

\bigcap\left\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)\mid x\in\mathfrak{m}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}.

By
\z

-ideals of a semiring

Define

\mathcal{M}_{a}:=\left\{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Max}(S)\mid a\in\mathfrak{m}\right\}.

Then the following proposition gives an alternative definition of a
z
-ideal of a semiring.

Proposition 3.2
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{z}
of a semiring
S
is a
z
-ideal if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{M}_{b}
and
b\in\mathfrak{z}
implies that
a\in\mathfrak{z}
, for all
a
,
b\in S
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{z}
is a
z
-ideal and
\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{M}_{b}
with
b\in\mathfrak{z},
for some
a
,
b\in S
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{M}_{b}
, we have
a\in\mathfrak{m}
, for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}
. Since
b\in\mathfrak{z}
, this implies that
\bigcap\mathcal{M}_{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}
, hence
a\in\mathfrak{z}
. For the converse, let us assume that for every
a\in S,

b\in\z
satisfying

\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{M}_{b}
, then
a\in\z
. Now take elements
a\in\z
and
\bigcap\mathcal{M}_{a}
.
This means that
\mathcal{M}_{a}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{x}
. From this fact, we can infer
that
\mathcal{M}_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{x}\cup\mathcal{M}_{a}=\mathcal{M}_{ax}
. Since
\mathfrak{z}
is an ideal and
a\in\mathfrak{z}
,
it follows that
ax\in\mathfrak{z}
. Now the equality
\mathcal{M}_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{ax}
and the
assumption imply

, proving that
\mathfrak{z}
is a
z
-ideal.
∎

Remark 3.3

It is easy to see that the above equivalent condition can further be relaxed, namely:

In the next proposition, we gather some elementary properties of

Proposition 3.4

Let

(1)

If

(2)

The Jacobson radical

(3)

If

(1) Let

(3) Since

The product of two

Proof.

\mathfrak{p}

4.5
,
\mathfrak{p}
is a
z
-prime ideal.
∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a
z
-ideal of a bzi-semiring
S
. Then

\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathcal{T}:=\left\{r\in S\mid\mathrm{every\;multiplicatively\;closed\;subset\;containing}\;r\;\mathrm{intersects}\;\mathfrak{a}\right\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
r\in\mathcal{T}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}_{z}(S)
such that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Then by Lemma
4.12
,
S\setminus\mathfrak{p}
is a multiplicatively closed subset of
S
and
r\notin S\setminus\mathfrak{p}.
Hence
r\in\mathfrak{p}
. Conversely, let
r\notin\mathcal{T}
. This implies that there exists a multiplicatively closed subset
X
of
S
such that
r\in X
and
X\cap\mathfrak{a}=\emptyset.
By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a ideal (and hence a
z
-ideal)
\mathfrak{p}
containing
\mathfrak{a}
and maximal with respect to the property that
\mathfrak{p}\cap X=\emptyset
. By Lemma
4.13
,
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal and by Proposition
4.5
,
\mathfrak{p}
is a
z
-prime ideal with
r\notin\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a
z
-semiprime ideal of a semiring
S
and
x\in S\setminus\mathfrak{a}
. Then there exists a multiplicatively closed subset
X
of
S
such that
x\in X\subseteq S\setminus\mathfrak{a}.

Proof.

Define the elements of
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots\}
inductively as follows:

x_{1}:=x;

x_{2}:=x_{1}x_{1};

\ldots
;

x_{n}:=x_{n-1}x_{n-1};

\ldots
. Obviously
x\in S
and it is also easy to see that
x_{i},

x_{j}\in X
implies that
x_{i}x_{j}\in X
.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

For any
z
-ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a bzi-semiring
S
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathfrak{a}
is
z
-semiprime.

(2)

\mathfrak{a}
is an intersection of
z
-prime ideals of
S
.

(3)

\mathfrak{a}
is
z
-radical.

Proof.

By Definition
4.10
, it follows that (3)
\Rightarrow
(2). Since the intersection of
z
-prime ideals is a
z
-prime ideal and every
z
-prime ideal is
z
-semiprime, (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) follows. What remains is to show that (1)
\Rightarrow
(3) and for that it suffices to show
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}.
Let
x\notin\mathfrak{a}
. Then
x\in S\setminus\mathfrak{a}
and by Lemma
4.15
, there exists a multiplicatively closed subset
X
of
S
such that
x\in X\subseteq S\setminus\mathfrak{a}.
But
X\cap\mathfrak{a}=\emptyset
, and hence
x\notin\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}},
by Lemma
4.14
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

The
z
-radical
\sqrt[z]{\mathfrak{a}}
of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a semiring
S
is the smallest
z
-semiprime ideal of
S
containing
\mathfrak{a}
.

Strongly irreducible ideals, originally introduced as primitive ideals in
[
19
]
for commutative rings and referred to as quasi-prime ideals in
[
10
, p.,301, Exercise 34]
, were first termed ‘strongly irreducible’ in the context of noncommutative rings in
[
9
]
. In the context of semirings, a study of these ideals can be found in
[
22
,
29
]
. In this section, we introduce the concepts of
z
-irreducible and
z
-strongly irreducible ideals in semirings and explore their relationships with
z
-prime and
z
-semiprime ideals.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
S
be a semiring.

(1)

A
z
-ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
is called
z
-irreducible
if
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}
implies
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}
or
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a},
for all
\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\in\z
.

(2)

A
z
-ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
S
is called
z
-strongly irreducible
if
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
implies
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
or
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
for all
\mathfrak{b},

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\in\z
.

It is clear from Definition
4.18
that every
z
-strongly irreducible ideal is also
z
-irreducible, and by Lemma
2.1
, it is straightforward to see that every
z
-prime ideal is
z
-strongly irreducible. Our expectation is that the equivalence formulation (
1.2
) holds for both
z
-irreducible and
z
-strongly irreducible ideals, and the following result confirms this expectation.

Proposition 4.19
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a semiring
S
is
z
-irreducible (
z
-strongly irreducible) if and only if
\mathfrak{a}
is irreducible (strongly irreducible) and a
z
-ideal of
S
.

Proof.

We provide a proof for
z
-strongly irreducible ideals, that for
z
-irreducible ideals requiring only a
trivial change of terminology.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is a
z
-strongly irreducible ideal and
\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
are ideals of
S
such that
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}.
This implies

\cz(\mathfrak{b})\cap\cz(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})=\cz(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a},

where, the first equality follows by Lemma
3.14
(
13
) and the inclusion by Lemma
3.14
(
5
). By assumption, this implies that
\cz(\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
or
\cz(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. By Lemma
3.14
(
1
), we now have the claim. The proof of the converse statement is obvious.
∎

It is a well-established fact (see
[
22
, Proposition 7.33]
) that a strongly irreducible ideal in a semiring possesses the following equivalent ‘elementwise’ property: If
x
and
y
are elements of
S
such that
\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
, then
x\in\mathfrak{a}
or
y\in\mathfrak{a}
. The next proposition shows that a similar result holds for
z
-strongly irreducible ideals.

Proposition 4.20
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a semiring
S
is
z
-strongly irreducible if and only if
\cz(\langle x\rangle)\cap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
implies
x\in\mathfrak{a}
or
y\in\mathfrak{a}
, for all
x
,
y\in S
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a
z
-strongly irreducible ideal of
S
and let
\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
, for all
x,

y\in S.
This implies

\cz(\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle)=\cz(\langle x\rangle)\cap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a},

where the first equality follows from Lemma
3.14
(
13
).
Since
\mathfrak{a}
is
z
-strongly irreducible, we must have
x\in\langle x\rangle\subseteq\cz(\langle x\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
or
y\in\langle y\rangle\subseteq\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. To show the converse, suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is a
z
-ideal that is not
z
-strongly irreducible. Then there are
z
-ideals
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
satisfy
\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a},
but
\mathfrak{b}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{a}.
This implies there exist
x\in\mathfrak{b}
and
y\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
such that

\cz(\langle x\rangle)\cap\cz(\langle y\rangle)\subseteq\cz(\mathfrak{b})\cap\cz(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a},

but
x\notin\mathfrak{a}
and
y\notin\mathfrak{a}
.
∎

In Theorem
4.16
, we observed that a
z
-radical ideal can be expressed as the intersection of
z
-prime ideals containing it. Now, we will show that any proper
z
-ideal can be similarly represented in terms of
z
-irreducible ideals, and this extends
[
22
, Proposition 7.34]
. However, before proceeding, let’s establish a lemma.

Lemma 4.21
.

Suppose that
S
is a semiring. Let
0\neq x\in S
and
\mathfrak{a}
be a proper
z
-ideal of
S
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{a}.
Then there exists a
z
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
S
such that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}

and
x\notin\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Let

\{\mathfrak{b}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a chain of
z
-ideals of
S
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{b}_{i}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}
, for all
i\in I
. Then

x\notin\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathfrak{b}_{i}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}.

By Zorn’s lemma, there exists a maximal element
\mathfrak{b}
of this chain. Suppose that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}.
By the maximality condition of
\mathfrak{b}
, we must have
x\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
and
x\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime},
and hence
x\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}=\mathfrak{b},
a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathfrak{b}
is the required
z
-irreducible ideal.
∎

Proposition 4.22
.

If
\mathfrak{a}
is a proper
z
-ideal of a semiring
S
, then
\mathfrak{a}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}}J
, where
J
is a
z
-irreducible ideal of
S
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.21
, there exists a
z
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}
of
S
such that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}}\left\{\mathfrak{b}\mid\mathfrak{b}\;\text{is $z$-irreducible}\right\}.

Then
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. We claim that
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}.
If
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\neq\mathfrak{a}
, then there exists an
x\in\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{a}
, and by Lemma
4.21
, there exists a
z
-irreducible ideal
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}
such that
x\notin\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}\supseteq\mathfrak{a},
a contradiction.
∎

As anticipated at the outset of this section, the following result demonstrates the connections between prime-type and irreducible-type
z
-ideals, and it partially extends
[
22
, Proposition 7.36]

Proposition 4.23
.

A
z
-ideal of a bzi-semiring is
z
-prime if and only if it is
z
-semiprime and
z
-strongly irreducible.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a
z
-prime ideal of a semiring
S
. Then by Proposition
4.5
,
\mathfrak{p}
is a
z
-ideal and a prime ideal of
S
. This implies that
\mathfrak{p}
is
z
-semiprime by Proposition
4.9
. From Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
4.19
, it follows that
\mathfrak{p}
is also
z
-strongly irreducible. Conversely, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a
z
-semiprime and
z
-strongly irreducible ideal. Suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}\in\z
satisfying
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Then

(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

Since
\mathfrak{p}
is
z
-semiprime, this implies
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. But
\mathfrak{p}
is also
z
-strongly irreducible, and so
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

∎

For commutative rings, it is a known result (see
[
8
, Theorem 2.1(ii)]
) that every proper ideal is contained within a minimal strongly irreducible ideal. Interestingly, this property also extends to
z
-strongly irreducible ideals of semirings.

Proposition 4.24
.

Every
z
-proper ideal of a semiring is contained in a minimal
z
-strongly irreducible ideal.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a proper
z
-ideal of a semiring
S
. Consider the chain
(\mathcal{E},\subseteq)
, where

\mathcal{E}=\{J\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b},\;\mathfrak{b}\;\text{is $z$-strongly irreducible}\}.

Since every maximal ideal of a semiring
S
is strongly irreducible, by Proposition
4.3
and Proposition
4.19
, it follows that every
z
-maximal ideal is also
z
-strongly irreducible. Additionally, as shown in Lemma
4.2
, every proper
z
-ideal is contained in a
z
-maximal ideal. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{E}
is nonempty. By applying Zorn’s lemma, we conclude that
\mathcal{E}
has a minimal element, which corresponds to our desired minimal
z
-strongly irreducible ideal.
∎

The following result demonstrates that when all
z
-ideals of a semiring are
z
-strongly irreducible, the proof of this fact is evident. This result generalizes
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Proposition 4.25
.

Every
z
-ideal of a semiring
S
is
z
-strongly irreducible if and only if
\z
is totally ordered.

The next theorem partially characterizes arithmetical semirings, and it extends
[
29
, Theorem 7]
.

Theorem 4.26
.

In a arithmetical semiring
S
, a
z
-ideal of a semiring
S
is

z
-irreducible if and only if it is
z
-strongly irreducible. Conversely,
if a
z
-irreducible ideal of a semiring
S
is
z
-strongly
irreducible, then
S
is arithmetical.

Proof.

It has been shown in
[
29
, Theorem 3]
that in an arithmetical semiring, irreducible and strongly irreducible ideals are equivalent. Thanks to Proposition
4.19
, we have then our claim. For the converse,
[
29
, Theorem 7]
says that if irreducibility implies strongly irreducibility, then the semiring is arithmetical. Once again, applying Proposition
4.19
on this result, we get the converse.
∎

Corollary 4.27
.

In an arithmetical semiring, any
z
-ideal is the intersection of all
z
-strongly irreducible ideals containing it.

The next result shows when all ideals of an arithmetical semiring are
z
-ideals, and it extends
[
42
, Lemma 2.12]
.

Theorem 4.28
.

Let
S
be an arithmetical semiring. Then every strongly irreducible ideal of
S
is a
z
-ideal if and only if every ideal of
S
is a
z
-ideal.

Proof.

If every ideal of
S
is a
z
-ideal, then in particular, every strongly irreducible ideal is also a
z
-ideal. For the converse, suppose that every strongly irreducible ideal is a
z
-ideal. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
S
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{x}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{y}
and
y\in\mathfrak{a}
. If possible, assume that
x\notin\mathfrak{a}
. Consider the set poset

\mathcal{E}:=\left\{\mathfrak{b}\in\mathrm{Id}(S)\mid\mathfrak{b}\supseteq\mathfrak{a},x\notin\mathfrak{b}\right\}.

Since
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{E}
, the set
\mathcal{E}
is nonempty. If
\mathcal{K}
is a chain in
\mathcal{E}
, then obviously
\bigcup\{\mathfrak{c}\mid\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{K}\}
is an upper bound of
\mathcal{K}
in
\mathcal{E}
. By Zorn’s lemma , this implies that
\mathcal{E}
has a maximal element
\mathfrak{p}
with the property:
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
and
x\notin\mathfrak{p}
. We claim that
\mathfrak{p}
is a strongly irreducible ideal. Suppose that
\mathfrak{r}\cap\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, for some
\mathfrak{r},

\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Id}(S).
Suppose
\mathfrak{r}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{s}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}
is maximal with the property:
x\notin\mathfrak{p}
, we obtain
x\in\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{r}
and
x\in\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{s}
. By applying arithmetical property of
S
, we have

x\in(\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{r})\cap(\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{s})=\mathfrak{p}+(\mathfrak{r}\cap\mathfrak{s})=\mathfrak{p},

a contradiction. Evidently,
y\in\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathcal{M}_{x}\supseteq\mathcal{M}_{y}
implies that
x\in\mathfrak{p}
, as
\mathfrak{p}
is a
z
-ideal. This is a contradiction. Hence
x\in\mathfrak{a}
, and hence
\mathfrak{a}
is a
z
-ideal.
∎

We conclude with a couple of results on
z
-Noetherian semirings.

Definition 4.29
.

A semiring
S
is called
z
-Noetherian
if every ascending chain of
z
-ideals in
S
eventually stationary.

The following proposition provides a generalization of
[
45
, Proposition 7.3]
. It is important to note that this result also holds to irreducible ideals of Noetherian commutative rings

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
S
be a
z
-Noetherian semiring. Then every
z
-ideal of
S
can be represented as an intersection of a finite number of
z
-irreducible ideals of
S
.

Proof.

Suppose that

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\mathfrak{b}\in\z\mid\mathfrak{b}\neq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{q}_{i},\;\mathfrak{q}_{i}\;\text{is $z$-irreducible}\right\}.

To establish the claim, it is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.
Since
S
is
z
-Noetherian,
\mathcal{F}
must have a maximal element, denoted by
\mathfrak{a}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{F}
, it is not a finite
intersection of
z
-irreducible ideals of
S
. This implies that
\mathfrak{a}
is not
z
-irreducible. Hence, there are
z
-ideals
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{b}\supsetneq\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\supsetneq\mathfrak{a}
, and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}.
Since
\mathfrak{a}
is
a maximal element of
\mathcal{F}
, we must have
\mathfrak{b},

\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{F}.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
are a finite intersection of

z
-irreducible ideals of
S
. This, in turn, implies that
\mathfrak{a}
is also a finite intersection of
z
-irreducible
ideals of
S
, a contradiction.
∎

In a
z
-Noetherian semiring we have another result, but for minimal
z
-prime ideals. This result extends
[
34
, Proposition 6.7]
.

Proposition 4.31
.

If
S
is a
z
-Noetherian semiring, then the set of minimal
z
-prime ideals of a semiring
S
is finite.

Proof.

We give a topological proof. If
S
is
z
-Noetherian, then the topological space (endowed with Zariski topology)
\mathrm{Spec}_{z}(S)
is also Noetherian, and thus
\mathrm{Spec}_{z}(S)
has finitely many irreducible
components. Now every irreducible closed subset of
\mathrm{Spec}_{z}(S)

is of the form

\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{p})=\left\{\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Spec}_{z}(S)\mid\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}\right\},

where
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal
z
-prime ideal. Thus
\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{p})
is irreducible component if and only if
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal
z
-prime ideal. Hence, the number of minimal
z
-prime ideals of
S
is finite.
∎

Remark 4.32
.

As a continuation of this work, in

]
, we study further properties of

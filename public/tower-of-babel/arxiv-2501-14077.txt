1.
Introduction

Given a real function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, Taylor’s theorem describes how
f
can be approximated by a sequence of polynomial functions, which are built using the derivatives of
f
. As a result, in order to study complicated functions, it suffices to study polynomial functions, which are well understood.

Many objects studied in algebraic topology can be realised as functors. Functor calculus is a method by which one can approximate a given functor by a sequence of functors with ‘nice’ properties, which we call polynomial functors. The resulting sequence of functors is a similar concept to that of a Postnikov tower. Polynomial functors have properties that mimic those of the polynomial functions used in differential calculus. For example, an
n
-polynomial functor is also
(n+1)
-polynomial and the
(n+1)
-st derivative of an
n
-polynomial functor is trivial.

There are many different branches of functor calculus designed to study different categories of functors. Goodwillie calculus, originally constructed by Goodwillie
[
Goo90
,
Goo92
,
Goo03
]
, is used to study endofunctors on the category of topological spaces. The fibres of the tower produced by Goodwillie calculus are classified by spectra with an action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
. The main focus of this paper is the orthogonal homotopy calculus first constructed by Weiss in
[
Wei95
]
. It is the branch of functor calculus involving the study of functors from the category of finite dimensional real vector spaces to the category of pointed topological spaces. The tower for a functor
F
produced by orthogonal calculus looks as follows,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{1}F(V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Omega^{\infty}\left[\left(S^{1V}\wedge\Theta_{F}^{1}\right)_{hO(1)}\right]}}

where each functor

fibre of the tower is fully determined by an orthogonal spectrum

with an action of the orthogonal group
O(n)
.

Classic examples of functors studied using orthogonal calculus include:

•

BO(-):V\mapsto BO(V)

•

B\Top(-):V\mapsto B\Top(V)

•

B\text{Diff}^{b}(-):V\mapsto B\text{Diff}^{b}(M\times V)

where
BO(V)
is the classifying space of the space of linear isometries on V,
B\Top(V)
is the classifying space of the space of homeomorphisms on V and, for a smooth compact manifold
M
,
B\text{Diff}^{b}(M\times V)
is the classifying space of the space of bounded diffeomorphisms on
M\times V
.

There exist functors, similar to those above, that have group actions. For example, the functor
BO(-):V\rightarrow BO(V)
that sends a
G
-representation to its classifying space. As such, there is a natural motivation to construct functor calculi that study functors with a group action. An equivariant orthogonal calculus of this type could have applications in many different areas, such as the study of equivariant diffeomorphisms of
G
-manifolds. Extensive research focused on equivariance in the Goodwillie calculus setting has been carried out by Dotto
[
Dot16a
,
Dot16b
,
Dot17
]
and Dotto and Moi
[
DM16
]
.

Main results and organisation

The
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus gives a method for studying functors from
C_{2}
-representations to the category of
C_{2}
-spaces. For example, the functor

BO(-):V\mapsto BO(V),

where
BO(V)
is the classifying space of the orthogonal group
O(V)
, which has a
C_{2}
-action induced by the action on the
C_{2}
-representation
V
.

We index the
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus over a category of
C_{2}
-representations, which are isomorphic to representations of the form
\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}=\mathbb{R}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}
, where
{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}
is the sign
C_{2}
-representation. The main result, Theorem
A
, is the classification of
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors (defined in Section
4.1
), which are the
C_{2}
-equivariant analogue of functors that are
n
-homogeneous in orthogonal calculus (functors with polynomial approximations concentrated in degree
n
). We show that
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors are fully determined by genuine orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of the orthogonal group
O(p,q):=O(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta})
, which has a specified
C_{2}
-action given in Definition
2.2.7
. That is, orthogonal spectra with a genuine action of
C_{2}
and a naive action of
O(p,q)
, denoted
C_{2}Sp^{\mathcal{O}}[O(p,q)]
. In this way, we get a richer equivariant structure compared to that of calculus with reality
[
Tag22b
]
, in which the classification is in terms of spectra with a naive action of
C_{2}\ltimes U(n)
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.3.8
)
.

Let
p,q\geq 1
. If
F
is a
(p,q)
-homogeneous functor, then
F
is objectwise weakly equivalent to

V\mapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{(p,q)V}\wedge\Theta_{F}^{p,q})_{hO(p,q)}],

where
\Theta_{F}^{p,q}\in C_{2}Sp^{\mathcal{O}}[O(p,q)]
and
(-)_{hO(p,q)}
denotes homotopy orbits.

Conversely, every functor of the form

V\mapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{(p,q)V}\wedge\Theta)_{hO(p,q)}],

where
\Theta\in C_{2}Sp^{\mathcal{O}}[O(p,q)]
, is
(p,q)
-homogeneous.

The classification can alternatively be stated in terms of the following zig-zag of Quillen equivalences between the calculus input category
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
(of functors from the category of finite dimensional
C_{2}
-representations with inner product and linear isometries to
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
) and the category of genuine orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
.

(Theorem

5.1.7
)
.

For all
p,q\geq 1
, there exist Quillen equivalences

Here

denotes the

-homogeneous model structure on the input category. This model structure captures the structure of

-homogeneous functors, in that the cofibrant-fibrant objects are exactly the projectively cofibrant

In comparison to the underlying calculus which is indexed over
\mathbb{N}
,
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus is bi-indexed over
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
. As a result, we can define differentiation in two directions (the
p
-direction and the
q
-direction). These different derivatives act like partial derivatives in differential calculus; in particular, they commute.

A key difference between the underlying and
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculi is an indexing shift, caused by this bi-indexing. In particular,
\tau_{n}
in the underlying calculus is defined using the poset of non-zero subspaces
\{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\}
and
\tau_{p,q}
in the
C_{2}
-calculus is defined using the poset of non-zero subspaces
\{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}\}
. To keep notation consistent, the author introduced the new term strongly
(p,q)
-polynomial, see Definition
3.1.5
. A functor in the input category for
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus is then called
(p,q)
-polynomial if and only if it is both strongly
(p+1,q)
-polynomial and strongly
(p,q+1)
-polynomial. In particular, we define the strongly
(p,q)
-polynomial approximation functor
T_{p,q}
, and the
(p,q)
-polynomial approximation functor is the composition
T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}
. A functor
X
is
(p,q)
-homogeneous if it is
(p,q)
-polynomial and the strongly
(p,q)
-polynomial approximation
T_{p,q}X
is trivial.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.1.6
)
.

The homotopy fibres of the maps

T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}F\rightarrow T_{p,q}F

are
(p,q)
-homogeneous, and can therefore be described in terms of genuine orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
, by the classification given in Theorem

.

A key result of the calculus, which makes the classification work, is the existence of the following
C_{2}
-homotopy cofibre sequences. These cofibre sequences tell us that derivatives in
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus are well behaved. The notation
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}:=C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{R}^{p,q}}
denotes the
(p,q)
-th jet category whose objects are
C_{2}
-representations and morphisms are given by
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
, which is a
C_{2}
-space (see Definition
2.2.4
).

Proposition D

(Proposition
2.2.5
)
.

For all
U,V,W
in
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
, the homotopy cofibre of the map

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W}(U\oplus\mathbb{R}^{\alpha},V)\wedge S^{W\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W}(U,V)

is
C_{2}
-homeomorphic to
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W\oplus\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}(U,V)
, where
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is either the trivial or sign representation of
C_{2}
, and
S^{W\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}
denotes the one point compactification of
W\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
.

It is very difficult to produce variations of orthogonal calculus due to the nature of its construction. Two successful variations are the unitary calculus and calculus with reality constructed by Taggart in
[
Tag22a
,
Tag22b
]
. In these calculi, real vector spaces are replaced by complex vector spaces, and in the calculus with reality one takes into consideration the
C_{2}
-action on complex vector spaces given by complex conjugation. The fibres of the towers produced are classified by spectra with an action of the unitary group
U(n)
for unitary calculus and spectra with an action of
C_{2}\ltimes U(n)
for calculus with reality. Taggart’s calculus with reality provides a great insight of what can be expected from a genuine
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus, and a number of proofs in this paper were inspired by the extensions of Taggart.

The main difficulty in generalising the
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus to
G
-equivariant orthogonal calculus, for an arbitrary group
G
, is the cofibre sequence of Proposition
D
. The cofibre sequence holds when
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is either the trivial or sign representation of
C_{2}
, since they are the one-dimensional irreducible
C_{2}
-representations. Replacing
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
with a representation of dimension greater than one would require an iterated cofibre sequence. In particular, this indicates that replicating this type of cofibre sequence for a general group
G
could be difficult, since
G
might have irreducible representations with dimension greater than one.

Work of Bhattacharya and Hu
[
BH24
]
produces analogous results for the case of a general finite group, but still requires that their derivatives be taken along one-dimensional representations (see
[
BH24
, Theorem 4.1]
).

This difficulty can be avoided if one restricts to abelian groups and the complex setting, since every irreducible representation of a finite abelian group over
\mathbb{C}
is one-dimensional. Therefore, it should be possible to construct a
G
-equivariant unitary calculus, for
G
a finite abelian group. It might be possible to then recover
G
-equivariant orthogonal calculus via a complexification functor, similar to that used by Taggart
[
Tag21
]
, however one should be careful to check that this preserves the
G
-equivariance.

Notation and conventions

Throughout this paper we will use
\text{Top}_{*}
to denote the category of pointed compact generated weak Hausdorff spaces with the Quillen model structure. We denote the category of pointed
C_{2}
-spaces and
C_{2}
-equivariant maps by
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
. We endow
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
with the fine model structure, which is cofibrantly generated by the sets of generating cofibrations and acyclic cofibrations
I_{C_{2}}
and
J_{C_{2}}
respectively.

We denote the trivial element of
C_{2}
by
e
and the non-trivial element by
\sigma
. The one-dimensional trivial and sign
C_{2}
-representations are denoted by
\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}
respectively. We assume all
C_{2}
-representations are equipped with an inner product that respects the
C_{2}
-action, and use the notation
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
to denote the representation
\mathbb{R}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}
. For a
C_{2}
-representation
V
, we denote the tensor product
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes V
by
(p,q)V
. Finally, we denote the semi-direct product of
C_{2}
with
O(p,q)
by
O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}
.

2.
Equivariant functor categories

The primary objects of study in calculus of real functions are derivatives. In orthogonal calculus, one constructs derivatives of input functors via combinations of restriction functors and the inflation-orbit change-of-group functors for spaces. These derivatives play a key role in the classification of
n
-homogeneous functors, as they form part of the zig-zag of Quillen Equivalences used to derive the classification Theorem. We extend this notion to the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting by defining new functor categories and adjunctions analogous to those used in the underlying calculus. We begin by choosing a new indexing category that will induce the
C_{2}
-actions used throughout the calculus.

2.1.
The input functors

Orthogonal calculus is indexed on the universe
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
, which makes the input category of functors enriched over topological spaces. To guarantee that the category of input functors for
C_{2}
-equivariant orthogonal calculus is enriched over pointed
C_{2}
-spaces, we must specify a new universe which is closed under
C_{2}
-action.

The regular representation of
C_{2}=\{e,\sigma\}
is defined as the following vector space

\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]=\{\lambda_{1}\underline{e}+\lambda_{2}\underline{\sigma}:\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}

with basis elements
\underline{e},\underline{\sigma}
. To better understand the
C_{2}
-action on the vector space
\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]
, we can decompose

\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]=\mathbb{R}\langle\underline{e}+\underline{\sigma}\rangle\oplus\mathbb{R}\langle\underline{e}-\underline{\sigma}\rangle.

This direct sum is
C_{2}
-isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\oplus{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}
, where
\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}
are the trivial and sign
C_{2}
-representations respectively with
C_{2}
-actions defined below

\displaystyle\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=x\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=-y\quad(y\in{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}).

Choosing the universe
\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]
, we define a new indexing category as follows.

Definition 2.1.1
.

The
equivariant indexing category

C_{2}\mathcal{L}
is a
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched category whose objects are the finite dimensional subrepresentations of
\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]
with inner product. Let
\mathcal{L}(U,V)
denote the space of (not necessarily
C_{2}
-equivariant) linear isometries with the
C_{2}
-action that is conjugation. That is, for
f\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)
,
e*f=f
and
\sigma*f=\sigma f\sigma:U\rightarrow V
. The hom-object of morphisms
U\rightarrow V
is the pointed
C_{2}
-space
\mathcal{L}(U,V)_{+}
.

From the discussion above on the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}[C_{2}]
, we can see that an object in the category
C_{2}\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to an object of the form
\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}=\mathbb{R}^{p}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}
, for some
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
. That is,
p
copies of the trivial representation and
q
copies of the sign representation.

Remark 2.1.2
.

We will use the notation
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
to mean
\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}
. We will also use the notation
(p,q)V
to mean
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes V
equipped with the diagonal action of
C_{2}
, where
V\in C_{2}\mathcal{L}
.

We can now define the input category for
C_{2}
-orthogonal calculus, which we denote by
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Define
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
to be the
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched category with the same objects as
C_{2}\mathcal{L}
and morphisms defined by
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}(U,V)=\mathcal{L}(U,V)_{+}
.
Define the
input category

C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
to be the category of
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched functors from
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
to
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
and
C_{2}
-equivariant natural transformations (see Remark
2.2.11
for details).

This category inherits a projective model structure from the fine model structure on
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
. The projective model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
is a special case of the projective model structure on
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
for
p=q=0
, as such we defer the proof to Lemma

.

Proposition 2.1.4
.

There is a proper, cellular,
C_{2}
-topological model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
where the fibrations and weak equivalences are defined objectwise from the fine model structure on
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
. We call them objectwise fibrations and objectwise weak equivalences. We call this the projective model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
and denote it by
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
. It is cofibrantly generated by the following sets of generating cofibrations and generating acyclic cofibrations respectively

\displaystyle I_{\proj}=\{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}(V,-)\wedge i:i\in I_{C_{2}}\}

\displaystyle J_{\proj}=\{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}(V,-)\wedge j:j\in J_{C_{2}}\},

2.2.
The intermediate categories

In orthogonal calculus, one constructs intermediate categories
O(n)\mathcal{E}_{n}
which are intermediate between the input category and the category of orthogonal spectra with an action of
O(n)
. We replicate this process in the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting to construct new intermediate categories
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. The construction follows the orthogonal calculus version of Weiss
[
Wei95
, Sections 1 and 2]
and Barnes and Oman
[
BO13
, Sections 3 and 8]
.

We begin by defining the following
C_{2}
-equivariant vector bundle.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
U,V\in C_{2}\mathcal{L}
. Define the
(p,q)
-th complement bundle

C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
to be the
C_{2}
-equivariant vector bundle on
\mathcal{L}(U,V)
, whose total space is given by

C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)=\{(f,x):f\in\mathcal{L}(U,V),x\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f(U)^{\perp}\},

where
f(U)^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement of the image of
f
.

Let
(f,x)\in C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
. Define a
C_{2}
-action on
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
by

\sigma(f,x)=(\sigma*f,\sigma x).

The following result outlines the effect of the fixed point functor
(-)^{C_{2}}:C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}\rightarrow\text{Top}_{*}
on the
C_{2}
-spaces
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{a,b},\mathbb{R}^{c,d})
and
C_{2}\gamma_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{a,b},\mathbb{R}^{c,d})
.

Theorem 2.2.2

(The Equivariant Splitting Theorems)
.

There are homeomorphisms

\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{a,b},\mathbb{R}^{c,d})^{C_{2}}\cong\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{a},\mathbb{R}^{c})\times\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{b\delta},\mathbb{R}^{d\delta})

C_{2}\gamma_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{a,b},\mathbb{R}^{c,d})^{C_{2}}\cong C_{2}\gamma_{p,0}(\mathbb{R}^{a},\mathbb{R}^{c})\times C_{2}\gamma_{0,q}(\mathbb{R}^{b\delta},\mathbb{R}^{d\delta}).

Proof.

The first result is an application of Schur’s Lemma.

Let
(f,x)\in C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{a,b},\mathbb{R}^{c,d})
. Then
(f,x)
is
C_{2}
-fixed if and only if
(\sigma*f,\sigma x)=(f,x)
. By the first splitting theorem,
f
must be of the form
f_{1}\times f_{2}
, where
f_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{a},\mathbb{R}^{c})
and
f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{b\delta},\mathbb{R}^{d\delta})
.

We know that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes\text{Im}(f)^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{c,d}=(\mathbb{R}^{p}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{c})\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{p}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d\delta})\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{c})\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d\delta})
, where
C_{2}
acts as
\id\oplus-1\oplus-1\oplus\id
. Therefore
\sigma(x)=x
if and only if

x\in(\mathbb{R}^{p}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{c})\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d\delta}).

That is,
x
is of the form
x_{1}\oplus x_{2}
, where
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\otimes\text{Im}(f_{1})^{\perp}
and
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\delta}\otimes\text{Im}(f_{2})^{\perp}
. The second splitting theorem follows from the well defined homeomorphism

(f_{1}\times f_{2},x_{1}\oplus x_{2})\mapsto((f_{1},x_{1}),(f_{2},x_{2})).\qed

Now we define what will become the morphism spaces for the categories
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
. These categories are analogous to the
n
-th jet categories of orthogonal calculus, and will be used to build the intermediate categories.

Definition 2.2.3
.

Let
U,V\in C_{2}\mathcal{L}
. Define
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
to be the Thom space of
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
.

This Thom space is the one point compactification of
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
, since
\mathcal{L}(U,V)
is compact. Hence, each
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
is a pointed
C_{2}
-space, with
C_{2}
-action inherited from
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
.

We can now define the categories
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Let the
(p,q)
-th jet category

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
be the
C_{2}\text{Top}
-enriched category whose objects are the same as
C_{2}\mathcal{L}
, and whose morphisms are given by
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
.

Composition in
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
is defined as follows. There are maps of
C_{2}
-spaces defined by

\displaystyle C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(V,X)\times C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)

\displaystyle\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,X)

\displaystyle((f,x),(g,y))

\displaystyle\mapsto(f\circ g,x+(\id\otimes f)(y)).

Passing to Thom spaces then yields the desired composition maps

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(V,X)\wedge C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,X).

One can check that this composition is continuous, unital, associative, and
C_{2}
-equivariant.

One can see that the category
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
has morphisms
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}(U,V)=\mathcal{L}(U,V)_{+}
, and therefore is exactly the category defined in Definition
2.1.3
.

The following is the a
C_{2}
-equivariant generalisation of
[
Wei95
, Theorem 1.2]
. It demonstrates that it is possible to build the morphism spaces
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
inductively. That is, we can construct
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p+1,q}
and
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q+1}
from
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
. The proof is analogous to that of the underlying Theorem, and checking equivariance follows as in
[
Tag22a
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Proposition 2.2.5
.

For all
U,V,W
in
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
, the homotopy cofibre of the restricted composition map

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W}(U\oplus\mathbb{R}^{\alpha},V)\wedge S^{W\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W}(U,V)

is
C_{2}
-equivariantly homeomorphic to
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W\oplus\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}(U,V)
, where
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is either the trivial or sign representation of
C_{2}
, and
S^{W\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}
has been
C_{2}
-equivariantly identified with the closure of the subspace of pairs
(i,x)
in
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{W}(U,U\oplus\mathbb{R}^{\alpha})
with
i
the standard inclusion.

Remark 2.2.6
.

These cofibre sequences are analogous to the following cofibre sequences constructed in the underlying calculus (see
[
Wei95
, Proposition 1.2]
).

\mathcal{J}_{n}(U\oplus\mathbb{R},V)\wedge S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{n}(U,V)\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{n+1}(U,V)

If one wanted to replace
\mathbb{R}
with something higher dimensional this would involve ‘gluing’ these cofibre sequences together in an iterative manner. The
C_{2}
-equivariant calculus has been constructed such that it reduces down to the underlying calculus after forgetting the
C_{2}
-actions. This forces that the cofibre sequences in Proposition
2.2.5
only hold when
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
is either the trivial or sign
C_{2}
-representation, since these cases both correspond
\mathbb{R}
in the non-equivariant statement. To replace
\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}
with something of higher dimension, for example
\mathbb{R}^{1+1\delta}
, would again mean taking some kind of iteration of cofibre sequences. This indicates that a potentially more involved approach may be needed if one wants to construct this kind of result in a
G
-equivariant orthogonal calculus, for an arbitrary group
G
. As a result, it is also not obvious how derivatives should behave for the arbitrary
G
setting, since the fibre sequences that describe derivatives (see Proposition
2.3.6
) are a direct consequence of these cofibre sequences.

We now define the functor categories
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. At the same time, we will also define functor categories
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
, which will later be used to classify the layers of the orthogonal tower. Before we can do this, we introduce the group
O(p,q)
and discuss its actions.

Definition 2.2.7
.

Define
O(p,q)
to be the group of linear isometries from
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
to
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
with the conjugation
C_{2}
-action. In particular,
O(p,q)^{C_{2}}=O(p)\times O(q)
.

There is an action of
O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
given by
(T,\sigma)(x):=T(\sigma(x))
. This can be extended to an action on
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f(U)^{\perp}
by
(T,\sigma)x:=((T,\sigma)\otimes\sigma)(x)
, where
f\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)
. This induces an
O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}
-action on
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
by

(T,\sigma)(f,x):=(\sigma*f,((T,\sigma)\otimes\sigma)(x)).

Hence, there is also an action of
O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}
on its Thom space
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
, making
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
an
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched category.

Remark 2.2.8
.

Note that the actions of
C_{2}
and
O(p,q)
do not commute. Throughout this paper, we equip the category of
C_{2}
-spaces with the fine model structure and the category of
O(p,q)
-spaces with the coarse model structure. We then equip the category of
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
-spaces with a model structure which is fine with respect to
C_{2}
and coarse with respect to
O(p,q)
. This is discussed more in Remark
2.4.4
.

Proposition 2.2.9
.

For all
p>0
and
q\geq 0
, there exists a
C_{2}
-equivariant homeomorphism

O(p,q)/O(p-1,q)\cong S(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}).

Proof.

Since
O(p,q)
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}
transitively by linear isometries, there is a restricted transitive action of
O(p,q)
on
S(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta})
. Fix the vector
e_{1}=(1,0,...,0)
in
S(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta})
. There is a continuous
C_{2}
-equivariant map
\phi:O(p,q)\rightarrow S(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta})
given by
g\mapsto ge_{1}
. The stabiliser of
e_{1}
is the subgroup of
O(p,q)
given by

\biggl\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}:A\in O(p-1,q)\biggr\},

which is
C_{2}
-homeomorphic to
O(p-1,q)
.
The quotient
O(p,q)/O(p-1,q)
inherits a
C_{2}
-action defined by
\sigma([g]):=[\sigma(g)]
. It follows by the orbit-stabiliser theorem that there is a continuous
C_{2}
-equivariant homeomorphism
O(p,q)/O(p-1,q)\cong S(\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta})
, which is given by
[g]\mapsto ge_{1}
.
∎

Definition 2.2.10
.

Define
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
to be the category of
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
enriched functors from the
(p,q)
-th jet category
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
to
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
and
C_{2}
-equivariant natural transformations, denoted by
C_{2}\Nat_{p,q}(-,-)
.

Define the
(p,q)
-th intermediate category

O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
to be the category of
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched functors from the
(p,q)
-th jet category
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
to
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
, and
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
-equivariant natural transformations.

For
p,q=0
this definition is exactly the category
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
in Definition
2.1.3
.

Remark 2.2.11
.

The set of natural transformations between
E,F\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
is denoted by
\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)
. There is a natural topology on
\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)
, which is the subspace topology of a product space as follows.

\displaystyle\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}\text{Top}_{*}(E(V),F(V))

\displaystyle\subseteq\prod\limits_{V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}\text{Top}_{*}(E(V),F(V))

There is a
C_{2}
-action on the space of natural transformations
\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)
induced by the conjugation action on
\text{Top}_{*}(E(V),F(V))
. This defines an enrichment of
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
in
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
.

With respect to this conjugation action, we topologise the set of
C_{2}
-equivariant natural transformations between
E,F\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
, denoted by
C_{2}\Nat_{p,q}(E,F):=\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)^{C_{2}}
as follows.

\displaystyle C_{2}\Nat_{p,q}(E,F)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}(E(V),F(V))

\displaystyle\subseteq\prod\limits_{V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}(E(V),F(V))

Similar descriptions exist for the morphisms in
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
.

We can describe a functor
E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
in terms of an enriched coend (and similarly for
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
), by the Yoneda lemma (see for example
[
Kel05
, Section 3.10]
).

\int^{W\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}E(W)\wedge C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(W,-)\cong E.

Alternatively, we can describe a functor
E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
in terms of natural transformations, by the enriched Yoneda lemma.

E(W)\cong\Nat_{p,q}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(W,-),E)=\int\limits_{V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}\text{Top}_{*}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(W,V),E(V)).

Another useful result, that we use throughout the paper, is that the functor
\Nat_{p,q}(-,F)
sends homotopy cofibre sequences to homotopy fibre sequences. This follows from the fact that the functor
\text{Top}_{*}(-,A):C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}\rightarrow C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
sends homotopy cofibre sequences to homotopy fibre sequences (it is contravariant, sends colimits to limits, and
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
is closed symmetric monoidal) and using the definition of
\Nat_{p,q}(-,F)
as the end above.

2.3.
Derivatives

Derivatives play a key role in calculus of real functions. They describe the difference between successive polynomial approximations in the Taylor series. As the name calculus suggests, one can define a notion of derivatives of functors in orthogonal calculus, as done by Weiss in
[
Wei95
, Section 2]
and Barnes and Oman in
[
BO13
, Section 4]
. In this section, we will extend this theory to the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting. Derivatives play a key role in the classification of
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors, as the derivative adjunctions form one half of the zig-zag of equivalences between the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure and the category of orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
, see Theorem
5.3.7
.

Let
i_{p,q}^{l,m}:\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{l,m}
be the
C_{2}
-equivariant inclusion map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,0,y,0)
, where
p\leq l
and
q\leq m
. Such a map induces a group homomorphism
O(p,q)\rightarrow O(l,m)
, which is
O(p,q)
-equivariant by letting
O(p,q)
act on the first
p
and
q
coordinates of
O(l,m)
. That is, both
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l,m}
are
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
-spaces.

This map induces a map of
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
-equivariant spaces

\displaystyle(i_{p,q}^{l,m})_{U,V}:C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)

\displaystyle\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{l,m}(U,V)

\displaystyle(f,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(f,(i_{p,q}^{l,m}\otimes id)(x))

which in turn induces a map on the
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched categories
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{l,m}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
p\leq l
and
q\leq m
.

Define the
restriction functor

\res_{p,q}^{l,m}:C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{l,m}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
as precomposition with
i_{p,q}^{l,m}
.

Define the
restriction-orbit functor
by

\displaystyle\res_{p,q}^{l,m}/O(l-p,m-q):O(l,m)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{l,m}

\displaystyle\rightarrow O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto(F\circ i_{p,q}^{l,m})/O(l-p,m-q).

The restriction and restriction-orbit functors have right adjoints. Before we can define them, we must define an adjoint to the orbit functor, see
[
BO13
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let
p\leq l
and
q\leq m
. There is an adjoint pair

(-)/O(l-p,m-q):(O(l,m)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}\rightleftarrows(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}:\CI^{l,m}_{p,q}.

An
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
-space
A
can be considered as an
((O(p,q)\times O(l-p,m-q))\rtimes C_{2})
-space, by letting
O(l-p,m-q)
act trivially. Call this space
\varepsilon^{*}A
. Define
\CI^{l,m}_{p,q}A
to be the space of
(O(p,q)\times O(l-p,m-q))
-equivariant maps from
O(l,m)\rtimes C_{2}
to
\varepsilon^{*}A
, which has the
(O(l,m)\rtimes C_{2})
-action induced by the conjugation
C_{2}
-action and the action of
O(l,m)
on itself.

Definition 2.3.3
.

Let
p\leq l
and
q\leq m
.

Define the
induction functor

\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}:C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{l,m}
by

\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}F:U\mapsto\Nat_{p,q}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{l,m}(U,-),F),

where the space of natural transformations of objects of
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
is equipped with the conjugation
C_{2}
-action (see Remark
2.2.11
).

Define the
inflation-induction functor

\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}\CI:O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}\rightarrow O(l,m)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{l,m}
by

\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}\CI F:U\mapsto\Nat_{O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{l,m}(U,-),\CI_{p,q}^{l,m}\circ F).

When
p,q=0
,
\CI_{p,q}^{l,m}
simply gives
F
the trivial
O(l,m)
-action, hence we write
\ind_{0,0}^{l,m}\CI F
as
\ind_{0,0}^{l,m}\varepsilon^{*}F
. This is what we call the
(l,m)
-th derivative of
F
, denoted by

F^{(l,m)}:=\ind_{0,0}^{l,m}\varepsilon^{*}F.

Lemma 2.3.4
.

The induction functor
\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}
is right adjoint to the restriction functor
\res_{p,q}^{l,m}
.
The inflation-induction functor
\ind_{p,q}^{l,m}\CI
is right adjoint to the restriction-orbit functor
\res_{p,q}^{l,m}/O(l-p,m-q)
.

Remark 2.3.5
.

There are commutative diagrams of categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p+1,q}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p+1,q+1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{p,q}^{p+1,q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{p+1,q}^{p+1,q+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{p,q}^{p,q+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{p,q+1}^{p+1,q+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{p,q}^{p+1,q+1}}

As in
[
Wei95
]
, the induction functors give us a notion of differentiation of functors in our input category
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
(see Definition
2.1.3
). In particular, for the
C_{2}
-equivariant case, there are two directions in one can take a derivative; in the
p
direction and in the
q
direction. These two different directions of differentiating can be thought of as partial derivatives, and then the commuting diagram above tells us that taking both possible orders of mixed partial derivatives is the same as taking the total derivative.

The following proposition defines induction iteratively as a homotopy fibre, and acts as a tool for calculating the derivatives.

Proposition 2.3.6
.

For all
U\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
and for all
F\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
, there are homotopy fibre sequences of
C_{2}
-spaces

\res_{p,q}^{p+1,q}\ind_{p,q}^{p+1,q}F(U)\rightarrow F(U)\rightarrow\Omega^{(p,q)\mathbb{R}}F(U\oplus\mathbb{R})

and

\res_{p,q}^{p,q+1}\ind_{p,q}^{p,q+1}F(U)\rightarrow F(U)\rightarrow\Omega^{(p,q){\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}}F(U\oplus{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}),

where
\Omega^{(p,q)V}Y
represents the space of maps
S^{(p,q)V}\rightarrow Y
, for a
C_{2}
-space
Y
, and is given the conjugation
C_{2}
-action.

Proof.

This follows in the same way as for the underlying version
[
Wei95
, Theorem 2.2]
, using the equivariant version of
[
Wei95
, Theorem 1.2]
, Proposition
2.2.5
.
∎

2.4.
The
(p,q)
-stable model structure

We want to compare the
(p,q)
-th intermediate category
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
with the category of orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
. The
(p,q)
-stable model structure constructed will be a modification of the stable model structure on orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra, see
[
MM02
, Section 3.4]
. This modification will account for the fact that the structure maps of objects in
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
are of the form

\sigma_{X}:S^{(p,q)V}\wedge X(W)\rightarrow X(W\oplus V).

We begin by defining two functors. These functors form an adjunction between the categories
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
and
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
\beta:C_{2}\rightarrow O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}
be defined by
\alpha\mapsto(\Id_{p+q},\alpha)
.

Let
\beta^{*}
be the restriction functor
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}\rightarrow C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
, which sends
X
to the underlying space
X
with
C_{2}
-action given by
\sigma x=(\beta(\sigma))x
.

Let
\beta_{!}
be the functor
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}\rightarrow(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
defined by

X\mapsto(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})_{+}\wedge_{C_{2}}X.

There is a projective model structure on the intermediate categories
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
, similar to the levelwise model structure constructed by Barnes and Oman
[
BO13
, Lemma 7.6]
, in which fibrations and weak equivalences are defined objectwise. A left Bousfield localisation of this model structure will give the
(p,q)
-stable model structure. This projective model structure is exactly the level model structure of
[
MMSS01
, Section 6]
.

Definition 2.4.2
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a map in
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. Call
f
an
objectwise fibration
or an
objectwise weak equivalence
if
\beta^{*}(f(U)):\beta^{*}(X(U))\rightarrow\beta^{*}(Y(U))
is a fibration or weak equivalence of pointed
C_{2}
-spaces, for each
U\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
. Call
f
a
cofibration
if it has the left lifting property with respect to the objectwise acyclic fibrations.

Lemma 2.4.3
.

There is a cellular, proper,
C_{2}
-topological model structure on the
(p,q)
-th intermediate category
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
formed by the objectwise weak equivalences and objectwise fibrations. Denote this model category by
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{l}
and call it the projective model structure on
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. The generating cofibrations and generating acyclic cofibrations are given by
I_{level}
and
J_{level}
respectively,

\displaystyle I_{level}=\{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)\wedge\beta_{!}(i):U\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0},i\in I_{C_{2}}\}

\displaystyle J_{level}=\{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)\wedge\beta_{!}(j):U\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0},j\in J_{C_{2}}\}

where
I_{C_{2}},J_{C_{2}}
are the generating cofibrations and acyclic cofibrations of the fine model structure on
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
.

Remark 2.4.4
.

Similarly, there exists a cellular, proper,
C_{2}
-topological model structure on the category
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
, where weak equivalences and fibrations are defined by restricting to
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
along
\beta^{*}
. The generating (acyclic) cofibrations are of the form
\beta_{!}(i)
where
i
is a generation (acyclic) cofibration of
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
. This is exactly the model structure which is coarse with respect to
O(p,q)
and fine with respect to
C_{2}
(see Remark
2.2.8
).

We will use this projective model structure to construct the
(p,q)
-stable model structure using a left Bousfield localisation. In the same was as for
C_{2}
-spectra
[
MM02
, Chapter 3]
, we begin by first defining homotopy groups on objects of
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
.

Definition 2.4.5
.

Define the
(p,q)
-homotopy groups
of
X\in O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
by

(p,q)\pi_{k}^{H}X=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\colim_{V}\pi_{k}\left(\Omega^{(p,q)V}X(V)\right)^{H},&\mbox{if }k\geq 0\\
\colim_{V\supset\mathbb{R}^{|k|}}\pi_{0}\left(\Omega^{p,q({V-\mathbb{R}^{|k|}})}X(V)\right)^{H},&\mbox{if }k<0\end{array}\right.

where
V
runs over the indexing
C_{2}
-representations in
C_{2}\mathcal{L}
,
H\leq C_{2}
is a closed subgroup, and
V-\mathbb{R}^{|k|}
denotes the orthogonal complement of
\mathbb{R}^{|k|}
in
V
. Define a map
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
to be a
(p,q)\pi_{*}
-equivalence if the induced map
(p,q)\pi_{k}^{H}f:(p,q)\pi_{k}^{H}X\rightarrow(p,q)\pi_{k}^{H}Y
is an isomorphism for all
k
and all closed subgroups
H\leq C_{2}
.

One can easily verify that if
C_{2}
was replaced by the trivial group, the
(p,q)
-homotopy groups for
p+q=n
are exactly the
n
-homotopy groups defined in
[
BO13
, Definition 7.7]
.

Now we identify the fibrant objects of the
(p,q)
-stable model structure. These are a generalisation of
\Omega
-spectra, which are the fibrant objects of the stable model structure on orthogonal spectra (see Barnes and Roitzheim
[
BR20
, Corollary 5.2.17]
).

Definition 2.4.6
.

An object

3.
Equivariant Polynomial functors

In differential calculus, polynomial functions and derivatives are used to approximate real functions via the Taylor series. In orthogonal calculus, Weiss defines a class of input functors with properties analogous to those of polynomial functions, called polynomial functors, see
[
Wei95
, Section 5]
. These polynomial functors can be constructed into a tower that approximates a given functor, much like the Taylor series does for functions. It is the fibres of the maps between these polynomial approximation functors that are classified as spectra by the classification theorem, see
[
Wei95
, Theorem 9.1]
and
[
BO13
, Theorem 10.3]
.

One can define a class of functors with polynomial properties in the
C_{2}
-equivariant input category
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
. The addition of the
C_{2}
-action makes it necessary to introduce an indexing shift from the underlying calculus. In particular,
\tau_{n}
in the underlying calculus is defined using the poset
\{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\}
and
\tau_{p,q}
in the
C_{2}
-calculus is defined using the poset
\{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\}
.

3.1.
Polynomial functors

In this section, we will adapt the definition of polynomial functors from the underlying calculus to fit the new
C_{2}
-equivariant categories defined in Section
2
. We begin by defining a functor
\tau_{p,q}
. The
(p,q)
-th complement bundle
C_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)
has an associated sphere bundle
SC_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(U,V)

SC_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(-,-):C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}^{op}\times C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}\rightarrow C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
as a
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched functor, we define the functor
\tau_{p,q}:C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let

\tau_{p,q}E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
by

\tau_{p,q}E(V)=\Nat_{0,0}(SC_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(V,-)_{+},E).

There is an alternative description of

as a homotopy limit, that can be derived as a consequence of the following Proposition, which is a
C_{2}
-equivariant generalisation of
[

[

Recall that
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}

Definition 3.1.2
.

Proposition 3.1.3
.

For all

Proposition 3.1.4
.

Let

Proof.

Using Proposition

\displaystyle\tau_{p,q}E(V)

\displaystyle=\Nat_{0,0}(SC_{2}\mathcal{\gamma}_{p,q}(V,-)_{+},E)

\displaystyle\cong\Nat_{0,0}\left(\underset{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p,q}}{\hocolim}\overline{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}}(U\oplus V,-),E\right)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{W\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}}\text{Top}_{*}\left(\underset{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p,q}}{\hocolim}\overline{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}}(U\oplus V,W),E(W)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\underset{0\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p,q}}{\holim}\int\limits_{W\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}}\text{Top}_{*}\left(\overline{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}}(U\oplus V,W),E(W)\right)

This homotopy limit has a

The following result describes the relation between derivatives and polynomial functors.

Lemma 3.1.8
.

For all

If

\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E
and

3.2.
Polynomial approximation

The partial sums of the Taylor series for a real function are known as the Taylor polynomials. These polynomial functions approximate the given function, and in general become better approximations as the degree of polynomial increases. In orthogonal calculus, Weiss defines a polynomial approximation functor
T_{n}
, see
[
Wei95
, Theorem 6.3]
. For an input functor
E
, each
T_{n}E
is indeed an
n
-polynomial functor. In the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting, we define an analogous functor
T_{p,q}
, and the
(p,q)
-polynomial approximation functor is given by the composition
T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
, define the functor
T_{p,q}
by

T_{p,q}E=\hocolim\left(E\overset{\rho_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\tau_{p,q}E\overset{\rho_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\tau_{p,q}^{2}E\overset{\rho_{p,q}}{\longrightarrow}\dots\right),

There is a natural transformation
\eta:E\rightarrow T_{p,q}E
.

Example 3.2.2
.

Let
E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
be an input functor. Then

T_{1,0}E(V)=\hocolim_{j}\tau_{1,0}^{j}E(V)=\hocolim_{j}E(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{j}),

T_{0,1}E(V)=\hocolim_{j}\tau_{0,1}^{j}E(V)=\hocolim_{j}E(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{j\delta}),

and the strongly
(0,0)
-polynomial approximation is the constant functor
T_{0,0}E(V)=*
.

Remark 3.2.3
.

In orthogonal calculus, the

-polynomial approximation

of an input functor

is the constant functor taking value

. In particular,
T_{0}F
is a constant functor, but
T_{1,0}E
and
T_{0,1}E
are not in general. See Example

-analogue of

The functor

(p,q)
-polynomial for all

to the

-equivariant setting. This is done over the following collection of lemmas. A formula similar to that used for the connectivity of

Recall that for a functor

By abuse of notation, given

Recall that a
C_{2}

The space

-polynomial objects such that the homotopy fibre of
g
is objectwise contractible and
F
is connected at infinity. Then
g
is an objectwise weak equivalence.

Finally we will prove a version of
[
Wei95
, Corollary 5.12]
. We will use this result later, along with Lemma
2.4.7
, to show that the induction functor takes objects that are
(p,q)
-polynomial to
(p,q)\Omega
-spectra. This guarantees that induction is a right Quillen functor from the
(p,q)
-polynomial model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
to the
(p,q)
-stable model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
.

Proposition 4.1.11
.

Let
E\in C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
be
(p,q)
-polynomial. Then for all
V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
there exist weak equivalences of
C_{2}
-spaces

\displaystyle\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q\mathbb{R}}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V\oplus\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V\oplus{\mathbb{R}^{\delta}}).

Proof.

We will prove the first weak equivalence, since the second follows by a similar argument.

If
p=q=0
, then there is nothing to prove, since
E
is constant and
\ind_{0,0}^{0,0}E\simeq E
by the enriched Yoneda lemma.

Let
p,q
be such that at least one of
p,q
is non-zero. By Proposition
2.3.6
, there exists a
C_{2}
-homotopy fibre sequence

\res_{p,q}^{p+1,q}\ind_{0,0}^{p+1,q}E(V)\rightarrow\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q\mathbb{R}}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V\oplus\mathbb{R}).

We know that
\ind_{0,0}^{p+1,q}E(V)
is a contractible
C_{2}
-space, since
E
is strongly
(p+1,q)
-polynomial and by Corollary
3.1.9
. Thus, if we can show that both
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E
and

F:V\mapsto\Omega^{p,q\mathbb{R}}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}E(V\oplus\mathbb{R})

are strongly
(p+1,q)
-polynomial and
F
is connected at infinity, then Lemma
4.1.10
gives the weak equivalence. Showing this is the same as for
[
BO13
, Proposition 5.12]
.
∎

4.2.
The
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure

We now construct a right Bousfield localisation of the
(p,q)
-polynomial model structure in order to build a model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
analogous to the
n
-homogeneous structure in
[
BO13
, Proposition 6.9]
. The cofibrant-fibrant objects of this model structure are the projectively cofibrant
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors and the weak equivalences are detected by derivatives. This model structure allows for the classification of
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors in terms of a Quillen equivalence, see Theorem
5.3.6
.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

There exists a model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
whose cofibrant-fibrant objects are the
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors that are cofibrant in the projective model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
. Fibrations are the same as
(p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
and weak equivalences are morphisms
f
such that
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}f
is an objectwise weak equivalence.
We call this the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
and denote it by
(p,q)\homog C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
.

There is a Quillen adjunction

\Id:(p,q)\homog C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}\rightleftarrows(p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\Id

Proof.

Right Bousfield localisation of
(p,q)
-poly-
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
with respect to the set of objects

K_{p,q}=\{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(V,-):V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}\}

yields the desired model structure. Since
(p,q)
-poly-
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
is proper and cellular, we know that the right Bousfield localisation
R_{K_{p,q}}((p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0})
exists by
[
Hir03
, Theorem 5.1.1]
.

The weak equivalences of
R_{K_{p,q}}((p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0})
are the
K_{p,q}
-colocal equivalences. That is, a morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a weak equivalence in
R_{K_{p,q}}((p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0})
if and only if

\Nat_{0,0}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(V,-),T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}X)\rightarrow\Nat_{0,0}(C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(V,-),T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}Y)

is a weak equivalence in
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
, for all
V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
. By Definition
2.3.3
, we see that this map is exactly
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}f(V)
. Therefore,
f
is a weak equivalence if and only if
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}f
is an objectwise weak equivalence as desired.
∎

Remark 4.2.2
.

Detecting weak equivalences via
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}
can be difficult, since the induction functor
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}
is complex. In unitary calculus, Taggart shows that a map is an
\ind_{0}^{n}T_{n}
-equivalence if and only if it is a
D_{n}
-equivalence, where
D_{n}F
is the homotopy fibre of
T_{n}F\rightarrow T_{n-1}F
(see
[
Tag22a
, Proposition 8.2]
). Via a similar proof, one can show that a map is an
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}T_{p+1,q}T_{p,q+1}
-equivalence if and only if it is a
D_{p,q}
-equivalence.

5.
The equivariant classification theorem

The main result of orthogonal calculus is the classification of
n
-homogeneous functors, as functors fully determined by a category of spectra. This is given by the classification theorem of Weiss
[
Wei95
, Theorem 7.3]
, which states that an
n
-homogeneous functor is weakly equivalent to a functor of the form
V\mapsto\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{nV}\wedge\Psi)_{hO(n)}]
, where
\Psi
is an orthogonal spectrum with an action of
O(n)
. In
[
BO13
]
, this classification is derived as a Quillen equivalence on the model categories constructed. As a result, the homotopy fibres of the maps
T_{n+1}X\rightarrow T_{n}X
for an input functor
X
, which are
n
-homogeneous, stand a chance of begin computed.

In this section, we construct two Quillen equivalences. These Quillen equivalences form a zig-zag of equivalences between the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
and the stable model structure on
C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(p,q)\homog C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\res_{0,0}^{p,q}/O(p,q)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\alpha_{p,q})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{p,q}^{*}}

In the same way as for Barnes and Oman in
[
BO13
, Section 10]
, this leads to a classification theorem for
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors (Theorem
5.3.8
), as functors fully determined by genuine orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
.

5.1.
The intermediate category as a category of spectra

In this section we show that the
(p,q)
-th intermediate category
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
is Quillen equivalent to the category of
O(p,q)
-equivariant objects in orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra. Thus, the
(p,q)
-derivative of an input functor (see Definition
2.1.3
) can be described in terms of these spectra. This section is analogous to
[
BO13
, Section 8]
, where the only differences are due to the additional
C_{2}
-action, which does not effect the
O(p,q)
-equivariance of maps considered. The resulting Quillen equivalence

(\alpha_{p,q})_{!}:O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}\rightleftarrows C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]:\alpha_{p,q}^{*}

forms one half of the zig-zag of equivalences which gives the classification of
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors, see Theorem
5.3.7
. The spectrum
(\alpha_{p,q})_{!}F
for a functor
F\in O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
is the categorification of the spectrum
\Theta F
constructed in
[
Wei95
, Section 2]
.

We begin by describing the category of orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra. Details of these constructions have been discussed by Mandell and May in
[
MM02
, Section II.4]
.

Definition 5.1.1
.

The
category of orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra

C_{2}Sp^{O}
is the category
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,0}
.

This category has a cofibrantly generated proper stable model structure where the cofibrations are
q
-cofibrations and the weak equivalences are the
(1,0)\pi_{*}
-equivalences (see Definition
2.4.5
).

Remark 5.1.2
.

Sometimes in the literature, an orthogonal
G
-spectrum is defined as a collection of based spaces
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with an action of
G\times O(n)
on each
X_{n}
. The structure maps are
G
-equivariant maps

X_{n}\wedge S^{1}\rightarrow X_{n+1},

such that the iterated structure maps

X_{n}\wedge S^{m}\rightarrow X_{n+m}

are
O(m)\times O(n)
-equivariant, where
G
acts trivially on
S^{m}
.

This is what we refer to as a naive orthogonal
G
-spectrum. Genuine
G
-spectra are indexed on a complete
G
-universe of all
G
-representations, whereas naive
G
-spectra are indexed on the trivial
G
-universe. That is, naive
G
-spectra are just spectra with an action of
G
on each level, and
G
-equivariant structure maps. These two descriptions of
G
-spectra are categorically equivalent, however they are not homotopically equivalent (see
[
HHR21
, Section 9.3]
), in that the most natural model structures associated to these categories are not Quillen equivalent. In what follows, we consider orthogonal spectra that are genuine with respect to
C_{2}
and naive with respect to
O(p,q)
.

Definition 5.1.3
.

The category of
O(p,q)
-objects in orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra
,
C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]
, is the category of
O(p,q)
-objects in
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,0}
and
O(p,q)
-equivariant maps. An
O(p,q)
-object in
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{1,0}
is a
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched functor from
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}
to
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
.

Theorem 5.1.4
.

The category of genuine orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
,
C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]
, has a cofibrantly generated proper stable model structure where fibrations and weak equivalences are defined by the underlying model structure on
C_{2}Sp^{O}
above.

We want to define a functor
C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]\rightarrow O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. This functor will be called
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
, and it is analogous to the
\alpha_{n}^{*}
functor built in
[
BO13
, Section 8]
.

Definition 5.1.5
.

Define the functor
\alpha_{p,q}:C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}\rightarrow C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}
by

U\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes U=(p,q)U

on objects, and

(f,x)\mapsto(\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f,x)

on morphisms.

Since the map on morphisms is
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-equivariant,
\alpha_{p,q}
is enriched over
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-spaces.

This induces a functor

\alpha_{p,q}^{*}:C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]\rightarrow O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}

defined by precomposition with
\alpha_{p,q}
. For
X\in C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]
we define the
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})
- action on
(\alpha_{p,q}X)(V):=X((p,q)V)
by

X(g\sigma\otimes\sigma)\circ(g\sigma)_{X((p,q)V)}

Here
X(g\sigma\otimes\sigma)
is the internal action on
X((p,q)V)
induced by the action on
(p,q)V
, and
(g\sigma)_{X((p,q)V)}
is the external action from
X((p,q)V)
being an
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-space. These two actions commute by construction.

Checking that
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}X
is well defined (i.e. that
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}X
is
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched) is the same as checking that the map

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)\rightarrow(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}(X((p,q)V),X((p,q)V))

is
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-equivariant.

To do this we consider the following commutative diagram. We use the notation
(-)^{*}
to mean pre-composition and
(-)_{*}
to mean post-composition. Let
s
denote the map
((g\sigma)^{-1}\otimes\sigma)^{*}\circ(g\sigma\otimes\sigma)_{*}
, and
t
be the map
(X((g\sigma)^{-1}\otimes\sigma))^{*}\circ(X(g\sigma\otimes\sigma))_{*}
. We have abbreviated
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
to
S\text{Top}_{*}
to save space (
S
for semi-direct product).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}((p,q)U,(p,q)V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S\text{Top}_{*}(X((p,q)U),X((p,q)V))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}((p,q)U,(p,q)V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S\text{Top}_{*}(X((p,q)U),X((p,q)V))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{p,q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{p,q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Given a pair
(f,x)\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)
, by applying
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}X
we get a a
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-equivariant map

X(\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f):X((p,q)V)\rightarrow X((p,q)V).

Therefore, the following two expressions are equal

X(g\sigma\otimes V)\circ X(\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f,x)\circ X((g\sigma)^{-1}\otimes U)

(g\sigma)_{X(U)}\circ X(g\sigma\otimes V)\circ X(\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\otimes f,x)\circ X((g\sigma)^{-1}\otimes U)\circ(g\sigma)^{-1}_{X(V)}

which by comparison to the commutative diagram tells us exactly that the map

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,V)\rightarrow(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}(X((p,q)V),X((p,q)V))

is
\left(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2}\right)
-equivariant, and hence that
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}X
is well defined.

Remark 5.1.6
.

Note that any other choice of internal action on
X((p,q)V)
results in the failure of the diagram being commutative. For example, taking
X(g\sigma\otimes\id)
as the internal action means that the left square in the diagram commutes only if
f
, from the pair
(f,x)
, is
C_{2}
-equivariant, which is not necessarily the case.

The left Kan extension of a functor
X\in C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]
along
\alpha_{p,q}
can be described by the following
(O(p,q)\rtimes C_{2})\text{Top}_{*}
-enriched coend.

((\alpha_{p,q})_{!}(X))(V)=\int\limits^{U\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}}C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}((p,q)U,V)\wedge X(U).

We make this functor suitable enriched by ‘twisting’ the action as in
[
BO13
, Definition 8.2]
. That is, we let
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}
act on
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}((p,q)U,V)
on the left by composition, and let
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}
act on
C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{1,0}((p,q)U,V)
on the right by composition. It is not hard to show, by an argument of coends, that
(\alpha_{p,q})_{!}
forms a left adjoint to
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
. We now prove that this adjunction is indeed a Quillen equivalence.

Theorem 5.1.7
.

The adjoint pair

(\alpha_{p,q})_{!}:O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{s}\rightleftarrows C_{2}Sp^{O}[O(p,q)]:\alpha_{p,q}^{*}

is a Quillen equivalence, where both categories are equipped with their stable model structures (see Proposition
2.4.9
and Theorem
5.1.4
).

Proof.

The proof follows as in
[
BO13
, Proposition 8.3]
.
Since
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
is defined by precomposition, it preserves objectwise fibrations and objectwise acyclic fibrations. It can easily be shown that
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
also preserved homotopy pullbacks. Hence, it preserves stable fibrations. An argument using finality shows that
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
preserves and reflects weak equivalences, and therefore preserves stable acyclic fibrations, making the adjunction Quillen.

It remains to show that the Quillen adjunction is a Quillen equivalence. By Hovey
[
Hov99
, Theorem 1.3.16]
, it suffices to show that the derived unit is a weak equivalence. Since the categories are stable and
\alpha_{p,q}^{*}
preserves coproducts, it suffices to do this for the generators of
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. This can be done by plugging in the generators
O(p,q)_{+}\wedge(p,q)\mathbb{S}
and
(C_{2}\times O(p,q))_{+}\wedge(p,q)\mathbb{S}
into the formula for the unit, where
(p,q)\mathbb{S}
sends
V\in C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{0,0}
to the one point compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{p+q\delta}\otimes V
, denoted
S^{(p,q)V}
.
∎

5.2.
Induction as a Quillen functor

In this section we construct a Quillen adjunction between the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure and the stable model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
. The right adjoint of this adjunction will be the differentiation functor
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}
. Moreover, we will show that this Quillen adjunction is in fact a Quillen equivalence between these categories. Combined with the Quillen equivalence of Theorem
5.1.7
, this allows for the classification of
(p,q)
-homogeneous functors in terms of orthogonal
C_{2}
-spectra with an action of
O(p,q)
.

The steps taken to construct this Quillen equivalence are similar to
[
BO13
, Section 9 and Section 10]
. We begin by explicitly proving the existence of a Quillen adjunction between the projective model structures on
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
and
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
, which can then be extended via properties of Quillen adjunctions and Bousfield localisations.

Lemma 5.2.1
.

There exists a Quillen adjunction

\res_{0,0}^{p,q}/O(p,q):O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{l}\rightleftarrows C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}.

Proof.

The generating (acyclic) cofibrations of
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{l}
are of the form

C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)\wedge O(p,q)\wedge i,

where
i
is a generating (acyclic) cofibration of the fine model structure on
C_{2}\text{Top}_{*}
.

Applying the left adjoint gives
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)\wedge i
in
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
. From Lemma
3.1.7
, we know that the functor
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)
is cofibrant in
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
, hence
\res_{0,0}^{p,q}C_{2}\mathcal{J}_{p,q}(U,-)\wedge i
is a (acyclic) cofibration in
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}
. Therefore, by Hovey
[
Hov99
, Lemma 2.1.20]
, the adjunction is Quillen.
∎

This adjunction can be extended to the
(p,q)
-polynomial model structure, via a composition of Quillen adjunctions. Furthermore, since the stable model structure on
C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}
is a left Bousfield localisation of the projective model structure, the Theorems of Hirschhorn
[
Hir03
, Theorem 3.1.6, Proposition 3.1.18]
can be used to additionally extend the adjunction to the stable model structure.

Lemma 5.2.2
.

There exists a Quillen adjunction

\res_{0,0}^{p,q}/O(p,q):O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{s}\rightleftarrows(p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}.

Proof.

There exists a Quillen adjunction

\Id:C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}\rightleftarrows(p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\Id,

since the
(p,q)
-polynomial model structure is a left Bousfield localisation of the projective model structure. Since the composition of Quillen adjunctions is a Quillen adjunction, combining the above adjunction with Lemma
5.2.1
gives a Quillen adjunction

\res_{0,0}^{p,q}/O(p,q):O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{l}\rightleftarrows(p,q)\poly C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}.

We now use
[
Hir03
, Theorem 3.1.6, Proposition 3.1.18]
to show that this Quillen equivalence passes to
O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{s}
. That is, we show that
\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}
takes objects that are
(p,q)
-polynomial to
(p,q)\Omega
-spectra. This has been done in Proposition
4.1.11
and Lemma
2.4.7
.
∎

Since the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure is a right Bousfield localisation of the
(p,q)
-polynomial model structure, the Theorems
[
Hir03
, Theorem 3.1.6, Proposition 3.1.18]
can again be used to extend this Quillen adjunction to the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure.

Lemma 5.2.3
.

There exists a Quillen adjunction

\res_{0,0}^{p,q}/O(p,q):O(p,q)C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{p,q}^{s}\rightleftarrows(p,q)\homog C_{2}\mathcal{E}_{0,0}:\ind_{0,0}^{p,q}\varepsilon^{*}.

Proof.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a weak equivalence between fibrant objects in the
(p,q)
-homogeneous model structure. Then the map

Lemma 5.3.1

The left derived functor of

Proof.

Example 5.3.2

Remark 5.3.3
.

If one replaces the group

with the group

of

.

For a finite group

that acts on

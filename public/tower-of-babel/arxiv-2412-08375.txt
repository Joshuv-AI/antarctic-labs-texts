Error analysis for discontinuous Galerkin time-stepping methods for nonlinear parabolic equations via maximal regularity Thanks: S. L. was supported by Vetenskapsrådet (VR) through grant no. 2017-04274.
Georgios Akrivis, Stig Larsson

Abstract
. We consider the discretization of a class of nonlinear parabolic equations by discontinuous Galerkin time-stepping methods and establish a priori as well as conditional a posteriori error estimates. Our approach is motivated by the error analysis in [ 9 ] for Runge–Kutta methods for nonlinear parabolic equations; in analogy to [ 9 ] , the proofs are based on maximal regularity properties of discontinuous Galerkin methods for non-autonomous linear parabolic equations.

65M12, 65M15, 65M60

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The initial and boundary value problem

We consider an initial and boundary value problem for a nonlinear
parabolic equation, subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary
conditions,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}=\nabla\cdot f(\nabla u,u)\quad&&\text{in }&&\varOmega\times(0,T],\\
&u=0\quad&&\text{on }&&\partial\varOmega\times(0,T],\\
&u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\quad&&\text{in }&&\varOmega,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

with a given initial value
u_{0}
and
\varOmega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a bounded domain
with smooth boundary
\partial\varOmega
. We assume that the flux function

f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is smooth and satisfies a
local
ellipticity condition,
namely, for every
(p,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}},
the symmetric part of the
Jacobian matrix
D_{p}f(p,v)
, that is, the matrix

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\big(D_{p}f(p,v)+D_{p}f(p,v)^{\top}\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d,d}\ \text{is positive definite},

and that (
1.1
) possesses a unique, sufficiently smooth solution
u.

Notice that eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix in (
1.2
) are
allowed to tend to
0
or
\infty
as
|(p,v)|\to\infty.

We refer to
[
9
]
for several parabolic equations of the form (
1.1
)
occurring in applications as well as for previous work on error estimates
for time discretizations of (
1.1
).

1.2.
Discontinuous Galerkin time discretization

We consider the discretization of the initial and boundary value problem (
1.1
)
by discontinuous Galerkin (dG) methods.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}},k=T/N
be the constant time step,

t_{n}:=nk,\ n=0,\dotsc,N,
be a uniform partition of the time interval

[0,T],
and
J_{n}:=(t_{n},t_{n+1}].

For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
we denote by
{\mathbb{P}}(s)
the space of polynomials of
degree at most
s
with coefficients in
H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)
, i.e., the elements

g
of
{\mathbb{P}}(s)
are of the form

g(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}t^{j}w_{j},\quad w_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega),\quad j=0,\dotsc,s.

With this notation, let
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s)
and

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s)
be the spaces of continuous and possibly
discontinuous piecewise elements of
{\mathbb{P}}(s)
, respectively,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s):=\{v\in C\big([0,T];H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)\big):v|_{J_{n}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(s),\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1\},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s):=\{v:[0,T]\to H^{1}_{0}(\varOmega),\ v|_{J_{n}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(s),\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1\}.

The spaces
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s)
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s)
are defined
analogously, with coefficients
w_{j}\in L^{2}(\varOmega)
.

We denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
the inner product both on

L^{2}(\varOmega)
and on
\big(L^{2}(\varOmega)\big)^{d}.

For
q\in{\mathbb{N}},
with starting value
U(0)=U_{0}=u_{0},
we consider the
discretization of the initial and boundary value problem (
1.1
)
by the
discontinuous Galerkin method
dG
(q-1)
, i.e., we seek

U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
such that

(1.3)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(U_{t},v)+(f(\nabla U,U),\nabla v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(U_{n}^{+}-U_{n},v_{n}^{+})=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1)

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. As usual, we use the notation
v_{n}:=v(t_{n}),

v_{n}^{+}:=\lim_{s\searrow 0}v(t_{n}+s)
.

As we shall see later on, there exists a locally unique solution

U(t)\in W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega),0\leqslant t\leqslant T,
of
(
1.3
); then,
f(\nabla U(t),U(t))\in(L^{2}(\varOmega))^{d}
and the
formulation in (
1.3
) makes sense.

With
0<c_{1}<\dotsb<c_{q}=1
the Radau nodes in the interval
[0,1]
, let

t_{ni}:=t_{n}+c_{i}k,i=1,\dotsc,q,
be the intermediate nodes in

\bar{J}_{n}=[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
; we also define
t_{n0}:=t_{n}.

Following
[
12
]
, we define the
reconstruction mapping

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)\to{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q),

w\mapsto\widehat{w},
via extended interpolation at the Radau nodes,

(1.4)

\widehat{w}(t_{nj})=w(t_{nj}),\quad j=0,\dotsc,q\quad\text{(where $w(t_{n0})=w_{n}$)}.

Using the Lagrange form of the difference

\widehat{w}(t)-w(t)=(w_{n}-w_{n}^{+})\hat{\ell}_{n0}(t),
with

\hat{\ell}_{n0}
the polynomial of degree
q
vanishing at the nodes

t_{n1},\dotsc,t_{nq}
and with the value
1
at
t_{n0},
integration
by parts and the fact that the Radau quadrature rule with
q
nodes
integrates polynomials of degree at most
2q-2
exactly, it is easily
seen that

(1.5)

\int_{J_{n}}(\widehat{w}_{t},v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{J_{n}}(w_{t},v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(w_{n}^{+}-w_{n},v_{n}^{+})\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1).

Therefore, with the reconstruction
\widehat{U}
of
U,
we can reformulate
the dG method (
1.3
) as

(1.6)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{U}_{t},v)+(f(\nabla U,U),\nabla v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1).

Denoting by
P_{q-1}
the piecewise
L^{2}
-projection onto

{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
, the relation (
1.6
) implies the

pointwise equation

(1.7)

\widehat{U}_{t}-P_{q-1}\nabla\cdot f(\nabla U,U)=0,

which will be used occasionally.

1.3.
Main results

We establish the following optimal order, optimal regularity a priori and
conditional a posteriori error estimates.

Theorem 1.1

(A priori error estimates)
.

Let
p
and
r
be sufficiently large such that
2/p+d/r<1,
and
assume that the solution of (
1.1
) is sufficiently regular,
namely,

u\in W^{q,p}\big((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\big).
Then there is
k_{0}
such that, for

k\leqslant k_{0}
, there is a neighborhood of
u
in

L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
such that the dG
equation (
1.3
) has a unique solution

U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
in this neighborhood, and
U
satisfies
the convergence estimate

(1.8)

\|u-U\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Ck^{q}.

Furthermore, if
u\in W^{q+1,p}\big((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega)\big),
for the
reconstruction
\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
of
U
, we have

(1.9)

\|u_{t}-\widehat{U}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|u-\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Ck^{q}

and

(1.10)

\|u-\widehat{U}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Ck^{q}.

The constant
C
depends on
q,f,T
, and

u
, but it is independent of the time step
k
.

Notice that our assumptions
u\in W^{q,p}\big((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\big)
and
q\geqslant 1,p>2
imply

u\in C\big([0,T];W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\big);

in particular,
u_{0}\in W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Conditional a posteriori error estimate)
.

Let
p
and
r
be sufficiently large such that
2/p+d/r<1
and
let
u
and
U
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Assume
that the reconstruction
\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
of the dG
approximation
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
is such that

(1.11)

\|u-\widehat{U}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\nu

with a sufficiently small constant
\nu,
independent of
k
and
N

such that
Nk\leqslant T.

Let
R(t):=\widehat{U}_{t}(t)-\nabla\cdot f(\nabla\widehat{U}(t),\widehat{U})
be the residual of
the reconstruction
\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q).
Then, the following
maximal regularity a posteriori error estimate holds

(1.12)

\|u_{t}-\widehat{U}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|u-\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

for all
0<t\leqslant T,

with a constant
C
depending also on
T.
Furthermore, the estimator
is of optimal order, i.e.,

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Ck^{q}.

We provide the proofs of these theorems in Section
4

and Section
5
, respectively.
In Section
2
we establish maximal regularity properties of dG
methods for non-autonomous linear parabolic equations
and in Section
3
we recall background material.

2.
Maximal regularity results for non-autonomous linear
equations

In this section we establish maximal regularity properties for dG
methods for non-autonomous linear parabolic equations; these
properties play a crucial role in our error analysis for dG methods
for (
1.1
).

Logarithmically quasi-maximal parabolic regularity results for dG
methods were established in
[
10
]
in general Banach spaces for
autonomous equations and in
[
11
]
in Hilbert spaces for
non-autonomous equations. These works cover also the cases of variable
time steps as well as the critical exponents
p=1,\infty.
Maximal
regularity for Runge–Kutta and, under natural additional assumptions,
for BDF methods in UMD Banach spaces were derived in
[
8
]
. More
recently, another approach to the maximal regularity of dG time
discretization was presented in
[
7
]
. This is based on
studying the dG approximation of a temporally regularized Green’s
function. The result allows quasi-uniform meshes but is restricted to

q\geqslant 2
.

We shall use the interpretation of dG methods for linear equations as
modified Radau IIA methods from
[
4
]
; this will allow us to
take advantage of a maximal parabolic regularity property of Radau IIA
methods for non-autonomous parabolic equations from
[
9
]
; see
Lemma
2.2
in the sequel. In
[
9
]
, the
discretization of (
1.1
) by certain Runge–Kutta methods,
including the Radau IIA methods, is analyzed.

2.1.
Radau IIA methods

We first recall the Radau IIA methods.
The
q
-stage Radau IIA method is specified by the Radau nodes
0<c_{1}<\dotsb<c_{q}=1
in the interval
[0,1]
, and the coefficients

(2.1)

a_{ij}=\int_{0}^{c_{i}}\ell_{j}(\tau)\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,\quad b_{i}=\int_{0}^{1}\ell_{i}(\tau)\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau~(=a_{qi}),\quad i,j=1,\dotsc,q;

here,
\ell_{1},\dotsc,\ell_{q}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{q-1}
are the Lagrange polynomials
for
c_{1},\dotsc,c_{q},

\ell_{i}(c_{j})

=\delta_{ij}.

The coefficient matrix
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1,\dotsc,q}
is invertible
and the stability function of the method vanishes at infinity.

Relations (
2.1
) reflect the fact that the Radau IIA method is of collocation type,
i.e., its
stage order
is
q.
The first member of this family, for
q=1,
is the implicit Euler method.

Auxiliary results.
We recall a discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity property for Radau IIA methods for
non-autonomous linear parabolic equations from
[
9
, Lemma 3.6]
, which will play a crucial role in our
a priori error analysis.

Let us start with a continuous maximal
L^{p}
-regularity property for non-autonomous linear parabolic equations.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
9
, Lemma 3.5]
; maximal regularity for
non-autonomous equations)
.

For
0<T<\infty
and
1<r<\infty,
consider operators
A(t)\colon W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\to L^{r}(\varOmega),t\in[0,T],

of the form

A(t)v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(\cdot,t)v_{x_{i}x_{j}},\quad t\in[0,T];

without loss of generality, we assume that the coefficient functions
\alpha_{ij}(\cdot,t)\colon\varOmega\to{\mathbb{R}},i,j=1,\dotsc,d,

are symmetric,
\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{ji}.
We also assume that the coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
satisfy a
uniform coercivity condition,

(2.2)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\alpha_{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geqslant\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\xi_{i}|^{2}\quad\forall x\in\varOmega\ \forall t\in[0,T]\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

with a positive constant
\kappa,
and a Lipschitz condition in time,

(2.3)

\|\alpha_{ij}(\cdot,t)-\alpha_{ij}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant L|t-s|\quad\forall t,s\in[0,T],

and are uniformly bounded in a Hölder norm, with exponent
\mu>0,

(2.4)

\|\alpha_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\mu}(\overline{\varOmega})}\leqslant K\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Then, the operator
A(t)
has the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity property for
1<p<\infty
:

for every
f\in L^{p}\big((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega)\big),
with arbitrary
T>0,

the solution
v
of the initial and boundary value problem

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}=A(t)v+f\quad&&\text{in }&&\varOmega\times(0,T],\\
&v=0\quad&&\text{on }&&\partial\varOmega\times(0,T],\\
&v(0)=0\quad&&\text{in }&&\varOmega,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

is bounded in the form

(2.6)

\|v_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant c_{p,r}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

with a constant
c_{p,r}
depending only on the Hölder exponent
\mu
and the corresponding bound
K

in (
2.4
), on the coercivity constant
\kappa
in (
2.2
) and the Lipschitz constant

L
in (
2.3
), on
\varOmega,p,
and
r,
and on the time
T.

The operators

A(t)\colon W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\to L^{r}(\varOmega),\ t\in[0,T],
are invertible and

(2.7)

\|v\|_{W^{2,r}(\varOmega)}\leqslant c_{r}\|A(t)v\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)},

with
c_{r}
a constant depending only on
K,\kappa,\varOmega
and
r;

see
[
9
, (3.10)]
.
Moreover, the Lipschitz condition (
2.3
) implies a
Lipschitz condition for
A
, namely, for
t,s,\bar{t}\in[0,T]
,

(2.8)

\|(A(t)-A(s))v\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}\leqslant\widetilde{L}|t-s|\|A(\bar{t})v\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}.

With starting value
{\mathcal{V}}_{0}=0,
we consider the discretization of the
initial and boundary value problem (
2.5
) by the
q
-stage
Radau IIA method: we recursively define approximations

{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}\in W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)
to the nodal
values
v(t_{\ell}):=v(\cdot,t_{\ell})
, as well as internal
approximations

{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell i}\in W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)
to the
intermediate values
v(t_{\ell i}),
by

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\mathcal{V}}_{ni}={\mathcal{V}}_{n}+k\sum_{j=1}^{q}a_{ij}[A(t_{nj}){\mathcal{V}}_{nj}+f(t_{nj})],\quad&&i=1,\dotsc,q,\\
&{\mathcal{V}}_{n+1}={\mathcal{V}}_{n}+k\sum_{i=1}^{q}b_{i}[A(t_{ni}){\mathcal{V}}_{ni}+f(t_{ni})],\quad&&\end{aligned}\right.

n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. Notice that
{\mathcal{V}}_{n+1}={\mathcal{V}}_{nq}
, since
c_{q}=1
.

An important maximal parabolic regularity property of the Radau IIA
method (
2.9
) for initial and boundary value problems of
the form (
2.5
) is:

Lemma 2.2

(Maximal regularity of Radau IIA methods;

[
9
, Lemma 3.6]
)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
, the Radau IIA
method (
2.9
) has the discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
uniformly in the step size
k
:
for

f(t_{ni})\in L^{r}(\varOmega),n=0,\dotsc,N-1,i=1,\dotsc,q,
the
numerical solutions

{\mathcal{V}}_{ni}\in W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)
are well
defined and satisfy the estimates

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial{\mathcal{V}}_{ni})_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(A(\bar{t}){\mathcal{V}}_{ni})_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\widetilde{C}_{p,r}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f(t_{ni}))_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))},

for any
\bar{t}\in[0,T],
and, consequently,

(2.11)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial{\mathcal{V}}_{ni})_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|({\mathcal{V}}_{ni})_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{p,r}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f(t_{ni}))_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))},

M=0,\dotsc,N-1,
with
{\mathcal{V}}_{-1,1}=\dotsb={\mathcal{V}}_{-1,q}=0.
Besides the
specific Radau IIA method, the constants
C_{p,r}
and

\widetilde{C}_{p,r}
depend only on the Hölder exponent
\mu
and
the corresponding bound
K
in (
2.4
), on the coercivity
constant
\kappa
in (
2.2
) and the Lipschitz constant

L
in (
2.3
), on
\varOmega,p,r,
and on the time
T;
in
particular,
C_{p,r}
is independent of the time step
k
and of
M

such that
Mk\leqslant T.

Here, for a Banach space
X
and a sequence

(v_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\subset X,
we used the notation

(2.12)

\partial v_{n}:=\frac{v_{n}-v_{n-1}}{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(v_{n})_{n=0}^{M}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}:=\Big(k\sum_{n=0}^{M}\|v_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

for the backward difference quotient and for the discrete

\ell^{p}(X)
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}.

2.2.
Discontinuous Galerkin methods

We establish maximal regularity properties for dG methods for
non-autonomous linear parabolic equations. These results are crucial for our
error analysis for dG methods for (
1.1
); they are also of
independent interest.

For
n=1,\dotsc,N,
we introduce the discrete (semi)norm

\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}
on

{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1):=\{v\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1):v(0)=0\}

and on
{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q):=\{v\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q):v(0)=0\}

by

(2.13)

\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}:=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\Big(k\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|v(t_{\ell i})\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}^{p}\Big)\Big)^{1/p}.

Since this only uses values from
[0,t_{n}]
, it is a norm on

{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
only for
t_{n}=T
. The connection to
the standard
\ell^{p}(X)
-(semi)norm used in
Lemma
2.2
and defined in
(
2.12
) is given by

(2.14)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(v(t_{\ell i}))_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}^{p}=\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}.

The continuous
L^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))
-(semi)norms and the discrete

\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))
-(semi)norms are equivalent, with constants
independent of
k
and
n;
see
[
2
, §2.1]
.
In particular, we have

(2.15)

\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\tilde{c}_{q,p}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\quad\forall v\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1),\ n=1,\dotsc,N.

Indeed, this is proved by expressing
v
in terms of

\{\ell_{mi}\}_{i=1}^{q}
, the Lagrange polynomials

\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{q-1}
for the Radau points
c_{1},\dotsc,c_{q},
shifted
to each subinterval
J_{m}
.

It is obvious from (
1.4
) that the discrete

\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))
-seminorms of
v
and of its reconstruction

\hat{v}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q)
coincide,

(2.16)

\|\hat{v}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}=\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\quad\forall v\in{\mathcal{L}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

We shall use the notation
\partial_{k}
for the backward difference operator,

\partial_{k}\widehat{V}:=\frac{\widehat{V}(\cdot)-\widehat{V}(\cdot-k)}{k},

for the reconstruction
\widehat{V}
of the discontinuous Galerkin approximation

V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
for (
2.5
),
with
\widehat{V}=0
in the interval
[-k,0).

Combining Lemma
2.2
with ideas and results from

[
4
]
, we obtain the following maximal regularity properties
for dG methods:

Lemma 2.3

(Maximal regularity of dG methods in continuous norms)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
, the
reconstruction
\widehat{V}
of the discontinuous Galerkin approximation

V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
for (
2.5
) has the maximal

L^{p}
-regularity property, uniformly in the step size
k,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|\widehat{V}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|A(\bar{t})\widehat{V}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|A(\bar{t})V\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\widetilde{C}_{p,q,T}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))},

for any
\bar{t}\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{V}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

Here we denote by

for the Radau nodes

shifted to
the interval

. Again,

Furthermore,

) written in terms of the reconstruction

of

with starting value

, namely

\widehat{V}_{t}(t_{ni})=A(t_{ni})\widehat{V}(t_{ni})+f_{ni}+\zeta_{ni},\quad i=1,\dotsc,q.

The autonomous case.
Notice that in the case of a

the terms
\zeta_{ni}
vanish due to
the fact that the Radau quadrature formula with nodes

t_{n1},\dotsc,t_{nq}
integrates polynomials of degree up to

exactly, and (

) simplifies to

(2.31)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &V_{ni}=V_{n}+k\sum_{j=1}^{q}a_{ij}\big(AV_{nj}+f_{nj}\big),\quad&&i=1,\dotsc,q,\\
&V_{n+1}=V_{n}+k\sum_{i=1}^{q}b_{i}\big(AV_{ni}+f_{ni}\big),\quad&&\end{aligned}\right.

n=0,\dotsc,N-1.

First, from Lemma
2.2
and the modified Radau IIA
formulation (

) of the dG method, we obtain the

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial V_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(AV_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{p,r}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))}

with a constant
C_{p,r}
independent of
N
and
T.
Let us
abbreviate

by

Since the term on the right-hand

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}\leqslant C\,\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

with a constant

and

(see

The non-autonomous case.
Having written the dG method for the

) as a modified

), we can apply

to obtain (with

)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial V_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(A(t_{m})V_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,T}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}+C_{p,T}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\zeta_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)},

m=1,\dotsc,N,
with a constant
C_{p,T}
, independent of
m
and
k,

depending only on the method, i.e., on
q,
and on

\mu,K,\kappa,L,\varOmega,p,r,
and
T.
Again we use the
abbreviation
X=L^{r}(\varOmega)
.

We shall now show that the second term on the right-hand side of
(
2.33
) can be absorbed in its left-hand side. This in
combination with (
2.32
) leads then to the desired
result.

Following the proof of the analogous result for Radau IIA methods in

[
5
]
, we let

Z_{m}:=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\zeta_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}

and

E_{\ell}:=k\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|A(t_{m})V_{ji}\|_{X}^{p},\quad\ell=1,\dotsc,m,\quad E_{0}:=0.

According to estimate (
2.33
), we have

(2.34)

E_{m}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}+CZ_{m}.

Since the Radau quadrature formula with nodes
t_{ni},i=1,\dotsc,q,

integrates polynomials of degree up to
2q-2
exactly, we have

\int_{J_{n}}\ell_{ni}(s)A(t_{ni})V(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s=b_{i}kA(t_{ni})V_{ni},\quad i=1,\dotsc,q,

and can rewrite
\zeta_{ni}
in the form

(2.35)

\zeta_{ni}=\frac{1}{b_{i}k}\int_{J_{n}}\ell_{ni}(s)[A(s)-A(t_{ni})]V(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s,\quad i=1,\dotsc,q.

Therefore, via Hölder’s inequality,

k\|\zeta_{ni}\|_{X}^{p}\leqslant\gamma\int_{J_{n}}\|[A(s)-A(t_{ni})]V(s)\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}s

with

\gamma:=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant q}\Big(\frac{1}{b_{i}^{p^{\prime}}}{\int_{0}^{1}|\ell_{i}(\tau)|^{p^{\prime}}{\mathrm{d}}\tau}\Big)^{p/p^{\prime}}.
By the
Lipschitz condition (
2.8
), we have, for
s\in J_{n},

\|[A(s)-A(t_{ni})]V(s)\|_{X}\leqslant\tilde{L}(t_{m}-t_{n})\|A(t_{m})V(s)\|_{X},

whence

k\|\zeta_{ni}\|_{X}^{p}\leqslant\gamma\tilde{L}(t_{m}-t_{n})^{p}\int_{J_{n}}\|A(t_{m})V(s)\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

By an analogue of the proof of (
2.15
), we infer that

\int_{J_{n}}\|A(t_{m})V(s)\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leqslant c_{p,q}k\sum_{j=1}^{q}\|A(t_{m})V_{nj}\|_{X}^{p},

whence

(2.36)

k\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|\zeta_{ni}\|_{X}^{p}\leqslant c_{p,q}\gamma\tilde{L}(t_{m}-t_{n})^{p}k\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|A(t_{m})V_{ni}\|_{X}^{p}.

Therefore, applying also summation by parts, we get

\displaystyle Z_{m}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{p,q}\gamma\tilde{L}k\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}(t_{m}-t_{\ell})^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|A(t_{m})V_{\ell i}\|_{X}^{p}=c_{p,q}\gamma\tilde{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(t_{m}-t_{\ell-1})^{p}(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=c_{p,q}\gamma\tilde{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}a_{\ell}E_{\ell},\quad a_{\ell}=(t_{m}-t_{\ell-1})^{p}-(t_{m}-t_{\ell})^{p}.

Now (
2.34
) implies

\displaystyle E_{m}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}+C\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}a_{\ell}E_{\ell},

and, since
\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}a_{\ell}=T^{p}
, the discrete Gronwall lemma
implies

\displaystyle E_{m}\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}.

By inserting this into (
2.33
), we obtain the estimate

(2.37)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial V_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(A(t_{m})V_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}\leqslant C_{p,T}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{m-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}

m=1,\dotsc,N,
with a constant
C_{p,T}
independent of
m
and
k.

Finally, this estimate in combination with (
2.32
) yields the
discrete maximal parabolic regularity estimate (
2.22
)
for dG methods for non-autonomous linear equations.

3.
Background material

We recall a space-time Sobolev embedding that will be crucial in the sequel.

Lemma 3.1

(Space-time Sobolev embedding;
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

If
2/p+d/r<1,
then the following embeddings hold:

(3.1)

\displaystyle W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{r}(\varOmega))\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};W^{2,r}(\varOmega))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)),

(3.2)

\displaystyle W^{1,p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))\cap L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))

and the second embedding is compact.

A consequence of the embedding (
3.2
) is the following

space-time Sobolev inequality
: If
2/p+d/r<1
and

v\in W^{1,p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))\cap L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))
such that
v(0)=0,
then

(3.3)

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant c_{p,r}\big(\|v_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\big);

see
[
9
, (3.1)]
.

We also recall Schaefer’s fixed point theorem.

Lemma 3.2

(Schaefer’s fixed point theorem;
[
6
, Chapter 9.2,
Theorem 4]
)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
{\mathcal{M}}\colon X\to X
be a continuous
and compact map. If the set

(3.4)

\{\varphi\in X:\varphi=\mu{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)\text{ for some }\mu\in[0,1]\}

is bounded in
X
, then the map
{\mathcal{M}}
has a fixed point.

4.
A priori error analysis

As in
[
9
, §4]
, for notational convenience, we
shall prove the a priori error estimates, i.e.,
Theorem
1.1
, for the case that
f
depends only on

\nabla u,
but not explicitly on
u
. The general case results in
lower order terms that are nonessential for maximal regularity
properties and cause no additional difficulties but make the notation
more cumbersome. In the error analysis, we shall rewrite the
differential equation
u_{t}=\nabla\cdot f(\nabla u)
in the form

(4.1)

u_{t}=A(\nabla u)u,

where the second order operator

A(\nabla v)\colon W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\to L^{r}(\varOmega)
, for fixed
v\in W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)
, is
defined by

(4.2)

A(\nabla v)w:=\sum_{\ell,m=1}^{d}\alpha_{\ell m}(\nabla v)w_{x_{\ell}x_{m}},

where

(4.3)

\alpha_{\ell m}(p):=[f_{\ell m}(p)+f_{m\ell}(p)]/2,\quad f_{\ell m}(p):=\frac{\partial f_{\ell}}{\partial p_{m}}(p)\quad\forall p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};

cf., also,
[
9
, §4]
. The form (
4.1
)
of the nonlinear parabolic equation will allow us to take advantage of
the maximal regularity property of dG methods for non-autonomous linear
parabolic equations (cf. Lemma
2.3
) and to write the
derivation of the estimates in compact form; for instance, we shall
use the local Lipschitz condition for
\alpha_{\ell m}
to estimate
differences of the form
\big[A(\nabla v_{1})-A(\nabla v_{2})\big]w
for
bounded
\nabla v_{1}
and
\nabla v_{2}
. More precisely: for

v_{1},v_{2}\in W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)
such that

(4.4)

\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant M,\ \ \|\nabla v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant M,

the local Lipschitz conditions for

\alpha_{\ell m}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\ell,m=1,\dotsc,d,
(see
(
4.2
) and (
4.3
)), lead to a local
Lipschitz condition for the operator
\nabla v\mapsto A(\nabla v),

namely

(4.5)

\big\|\big(A(\nabla v_{1})-A(\nabla v_{2})\big)w\big\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}\leqslant L_{M}\|\nabla v_{1}-\nabla v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\|w\|_{W^{2,r}(\varOmega)}.

4.1.
An interpolant and its approximation properties

We shall use a standard interpolant

\tilde{u}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
of the solution
u
such that

\tilde{u}(t_{n})=u(t_{n}),n=0,\dotsc,N,
and
u-\tilde{u}
is in each
subinterval
J_{n}
orthogonal to polynomials of degree at most
q-2

(with the second condition being void for
q=1
). Thus,
\tilde{u}
is
determined in
J_{n}
by the conditions

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{u}(t_{n+1})=u(t_{n+1}),\\
&\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\big(u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\big)t^{j}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,\quad j=0,\dotsc,q-2;\end{aligned}\right.

cf., e.g.,
[
14
, (12.9)]
and
[
13
, §§3.1–3.3]
for the case of Hilbert spaces.

Existence and uniqueness of
\tilde{u}
as well as approximation
properties for the interpolant
\tilde{u}
and its reconstruction

\hat{\tilde{u}}
are established in
[
2
]
for general
Banach spaces
X.
The following approximation properties

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ck^{q}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ck^{q-\frac{1}{p}}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|u-\hat{\tilde{u}}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ck^{q}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|u_{t}-\hat{\tilde{u}}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ck^{q}\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

with
X=L^{r}(\varOmega)
,
X=W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega),
or

X=W^{2,r}(\varOmega),
will play an important role in our analysis;
see
[
2
, (2.13), (A.8), (2.14), (2.15)]
. Here, we used the
notation
u(t):=u(\cdot,t)
and

u^{(q)}(t):=\frac{\partial^{q}u}{\partial t^{q}}(\cdot,t).

The norms
\|\cdot\|_{W^{2,r}(\varOmega)}
and

\|A(\nabla u)\cdot\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}
are obviously equivalent on

W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)
uniformly with respect
to
u
; see also (
2.7
). Therefore,
(
4.7
) for
X=W^{2,r}(\varOmega)
leads to
the estimate

(4.11)

\|A(\nabla u)(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant ck^{q}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

Our assumption
2/p+d/r<1
implies that
r>d
, so that we may take
advantage of the Sobolev embedding

W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega);
see

[
1
, Theorem 4.12, (2)]
. Then,
(
4.8
) for
X=W^{2,r}(\varOmega)

yields also the estimate

(4.12)

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant ck^{q-1/p}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

We use the standard splitting of the error

(4.13)

e=u-U=(u-\tilde{u})+(\tilde{u}-U)=\rho+\vartheta,

where we thus have various error estimates for
\rho:=u-\tilde{u}
and
it remains to bound
\vartheta:=\tilde{u}-U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
.

Since
\rho=u-\tilde{u}
is orthogonal to
v_{t}
in
J_{n}
for any

v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
and vanishes at the nodes
t_{m}
,
integration by parts shows that it has the following crucial property

(4.14)

\int_{J_{n}}(\rho_{t},v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(\rho_{n}^{+}-\rho_{n},v_{n}^{+})=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1);

cf.
[
14
, (12.13)]
.

4.2.
Regularity assumptions

Since we assume that (
4.1
) has a solution that belongs to

W^{q,p}\big((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega)\cap W^{1,r}_{0}(\varOmega)\big)
,
it is possible to choose a single constant
M
such that the following
bounds hold for
u
and
\rho=u-\tilde{u}
. First, the main regularity
bound,

(4.15)

\|u\|_{W^{q,p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant{M},

and, by the Sobolev embedding,

(4.16)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{q,p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant M.

Then, by (
4.7
) and (
4.8
),

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Mk^{q},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Mk^{q-1/p}.

We also have

(4.19)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|u\|_{W^{q,p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant M.

4.3.
Derivation of the error equation

Subtracting (
1.3
), with
f
depending only on
\nabla U
, from

(4.20)

\int_{J_{n}}\big((u_{t},v)+(f(\nabla u),\nabla v)\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(u_{n}^{+}-u_{n},v_{n}^{+})=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),

where
u_{n}^{+}-u_{n}=0
, we get the Galerkin orthogonality for the error

e:=u-U
,

(4.21)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(e_{t},v)+(f(\nabla u)-f(\nabla U),\nabla v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(e_{n}^{+}-e_{n},v_{n}^{+})=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1).

We linearize (
4.21
) about
\nabla u
:

(4.22)

\nabla\cdot f(\nabla u)-\nabla\cdot f(\nabla U)=A(\nabla u)(u-U)+\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U)\big)U=A(\nabla u)e+G

with remainder (recall that
\vartheta=\tilde{u}-U
)

(4.23)

G:=\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U)\big)U=\big[A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla(\tilde{u}-\vartheta))\big](\tilde{u}-\vartheta)

to get the linearized error equation

(4.24)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(e_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)e,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(e_{n}^{+}-e_{n},v_{n}^{+})=\int_{J_{n}}(G,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1).

Thus, in view of (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we
obtain the following equation for
\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
:

(4.25)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\vartheta_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\vartheta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(\vartheta_{n}^{+}-\vartheta_{n},v_{n}^{+})

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}\big[\big(A(\nabla u)\rho,v\big)+\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\vartheta))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta),v\big)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
, with vanishing starting
value
\vartheta(0)=0
. This is a nonlinear equation for

\vartheta
. In the next subsection we show the existence of a
solution with

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\nu

for arbitrarily small
\nu
.

4.4.
Existence

Our goal here is to prove existence of a solution
\vartheta

of (
4.25
) in the space

X=L^{\infty}((0,T),W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
. We will truncate the
nonlinearity and then apply Schaefer’s fixed point theorem.

4.4.1.
A truncated equation

We introduce a truncation of the nonlinear operator

\varphi\mapsto A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla{\varphi})
that appears in
the error equation (
4.25
). We replace
it by

\varphi\mapsto A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla{\varphi})
,
where the functional
\beta\colon W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
defined by

(4.26)

\beta(\varphi)=\min\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}}{\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}},1\Big)\quad\text{with $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$}.

Then, we have

(4.27)

\displaystyle\|\beta(\varphi_{1})\nabla\varphi_{1}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant 2\|\nabla(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)},

(4.28)

\displaystyle\|\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant{\varepsilon},

(4.29)

\displaystyle\beta(\varphi)=1,\quad\text{if
$\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant{\varepsilon}$}.

The proof of (
4.27
) is obtained by noting that

\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\varphi,&&\text{if }\,\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant\varepsilon,\\
&\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}}\nabla\varphi,\quad&&\text{if }\,\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\geqslant\varepsilon,\end{aligned}\right.

and considering the three cases whether
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
are
both small, both big, and one is big and one is small relative to

\varepsilon
.

We thus introduce the truncated error equation to find

\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1),
with
\vartheta(0)=0,
such that

(4.30)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\vartheta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\vartheta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}\big[\big(A(\nabla u)\rho,v\big)+\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta),v\big)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. Notice, that we have used
the same notation
\vartheta
for the solutions of
(
4.30
) and
(
4.25
).

Before proceeding, we derive a useful inequality for the nonlinear
term. By (
4.16
) we have

(4.31)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant M

and, by (
4.19
) and (
4.28
),

(4.32)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}&\leqslant\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}+\|\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\\
&\leqslant M+\varepsilon\leqslant M+1,\end{aligned}

so that the local Lipschitz bound in (
4.5
) with constant

L=L_{M+1}
, (
4.18
), and (
4.28
) give

(4.33)

\displaystyle\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}+\|\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\big)\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

4.4.2.
A fixed point equation

To write equation (
4.30
) in a fixed-point
form, with a given input
\varphi\in X,
we consider the linear
equation to find
\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1),
with

\vartheta(0)=0,
such that

(4.34)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\vartheta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\vartheta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}\big[\big(A(\nabla u)\rho,v\big)+\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta),v\big)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. Notice, that we have used
the same notation
\vartheta
for the solutions of
(
4.34
) and
(
4.25
).

Our next goal is to show that (
4.34
)
defines a nonlinear map
\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
,

\varphi\mapsto\vartheta
, with

X=L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
. It suffices to show
that, for each
\varphi\in X,

(
4.34
) possesses a unique solution

\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
.

We apply the Banach fixed-point theorem to the operator

\mathcal{S}\colon\psi\mapsto\vartheta
on
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
,
where
\vartheta
solves
\vartheta(0)=0
and

(4.35)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\vartheta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\vartheta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}\big[\big(A(\nabla u)\rho,v\big)+\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))(\tilde{u}-\psi),v\big)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. This is a linear dG
equation and it clearly has a unique solution

\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
for each

\psi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
; thus
\mathcal{S}
is well defined.

We want to show that
\mathcal{S}
is a contraction on

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
. For

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1),
by subtracting the
corresponding equations, we see that the difference

\zeta=\mathcal{S}(\psi_{1})-\mathcal{S}(\psi_{2})
satisfies

(4.36)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\zeta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\zeta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{J_{n}}\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}),v\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
.

The maximal regularity property (
2.18
) of the dG
method applied to (
4.36
) yields

(4.37)

\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}.

Here (
4.33
) gives

\displaystyle\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi)\nabla\varphi))(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

Hence,

(4.38)

\|\mathcal{S}(\psi_{1})-\mathcal{S}(\psi_{2})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{p,q,T}L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

If
C_{p,q,T}L(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)\leqslant 1/2
, then we
have a contraction. Thus, for every
\varphi\in X
we have a unique
fixed point
\vartheta=\mathcal{S}(\vartheta)
. This defines the
nonlinear mapping
\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
with

\mathcal{M}(\varphi):=\vartheta
.

4.4.3.
Application of Schaefer’s fixed point
theorem

We check the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
for the mapping

\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
.

1. The set

\{\varphi\in X:\varphi=\mu{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)\text{ for some }\mu\in[0,1]\}
is bounded in
X
.

Suppose thus that
\varphi\in X

satisfies
\varphi=\mu\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
for some
\mu\in[0,1]
.
This means that
\varphi=\mu\vartheta
, where

\vartheta\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
is the solution of
(
4.34
) with input

\varphi=\mu\vartheta
, that is,
\vartheta(0)=0
and

(4.39)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\vartheta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\vartheta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}\big[(A(\nabla u)\rho,v)+\big((A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\mu\vartheta)\nabla(\mu\vartheta)))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta),v\big)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1)
,
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. Maximal regularity (see
(
2.18
)) gives

(4.40)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\big(\|A(\nabla u)\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\mu\vartheta)\nabla(\mu\vartheta)))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\big),

where, in view of (
4.17
),

(4.41)

\|A(\nabla u)\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\big)\leqslant Mk^{q}

and, in view of (
4.33
) and (
4.18
),
(
4.19
), (
4.28
),

(4.42)

\displaystyle\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\mu\vartheta)\nabla(\mu\vartheta)))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\big(\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\big(M+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\big).

Hence,

(4.43)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\big(Mk^{q}+LM\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\big)+C_{p,q,T}L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

If
C_{p,q,T}L(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)\leqslant 1/2
(the
same smallness condition as before), then the last term can be hidden
in the left-hand side to get

(4.44)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big(Mk^{q}+LM\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\big).

Hence, by the space-time Sobolev inequality
(
3.3
), we also have

(4.45)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant c_{p,r}\big(\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big(Mk^{q}+LM\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon\big)\big).

Since
\vartheta
is a (discontinuous) Lagrange interpolant of

\widehat{\vartheta}
, it follows that

(4.46)

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant CMk^{q}+CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon).

Note that
C
does not depend on
\mu
,
k
and
\varepsilon
. This
shows that the set

\{\varphi\in X:\varphi=\mu{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)\text{ for some }\mu\in[0,1]\}
is bounded in
X
.

2.
\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
is compact.
The previous
calculation with
\mu=1
shows that

\varphi\mapsto\widehat{\vartheta}
maps

L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
into

W^{1,p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))\cap L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))
,
which is compactly embedded in

L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
; see (
3.2
).
Since
\vartheta
is a (discontinuous) Lagrange interpolant of

\widehat{\vartheta}
, it follows that
\mathcal{M}
maps

X=L^{\infty}((0,T),W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))
compactly into itself.

3.
\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
is continuous.

The function
\vartheta=\mathcal{M}(\varphi)
is the unique solution
of (
4.34
) and therefore the
difference

\zeta=\vartheta_{1}-\vartheta_{2}=\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{1})-\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{2})

satisfies
\zeta(0)=0
and

(4.47)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\widehat{\zeta}_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\zeta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{J_{n}}(G,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

for
v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1),
for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
, where

(4.48)

\displaystyle G={}

\displaystyle(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{1})\nabla\varphi_{1}))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta_{1})

\displaystyle-(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{1})\nabla\varphi_{1})-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta_{1})

\displaystyle-(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}))(\vartheta_{1}-\vartheta_{2}).

Maximal regularity for dG (see (
2.18
)) gives

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\zeta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\|G\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\|(A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{1})\nabla\varphi_{1})-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta_{1}))\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}))(\vartheta_{1}-\vartheta_{2})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,T}L\|\beta(\varphi_{1})\nabla\varphi_{1}-\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\big(\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta_{1}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\big)

\displaystyle+C_{p,q,T}L(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}+\|\beta(\varphi_{2})\nabla\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))})\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant CC_{p,q,T}L\|\nabla(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}+C_{p,q,T}L(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

The constant
C
depends on
M
and
L
but not on
k
and

\varepsilon
. We used (
4.33
),
(
4.27
), (
4.28
), and that

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta_{1}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C
by (
4.19
) and
(
4.44
). If

C_{p,q,T}L(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)\leqslant 1/2
(the same
smallness condition as before), then we can hide the last term in the
left-hand side and get

(4.49)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\zeta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant CC_{p,q,T}L\|\nabla(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}.

By using the space-time Sobolev inequality (
3.3
) we
conclude

(4.50)

\|\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{1})-\mathcal{M}(\varphi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}.

Hence,
\mathcal{M}\colon X\to X
is (globally Lipschitz) continuous.

4. Conclusion of the fixed point theorem.
We have verified
that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the assumptions of Schaefer’s fixed point
theorem, Lemma
3.2
. We therefore obtain a fixed point

\vartheta=\mathcal{M}(\vartheta)\in X
. It is a solution of
(
4.30
) and satisfies the bounds in
(
4.44
) and (
4.46
).

4.5.
Convergence

So far we have shown the existence of a solution
\vartheta
to the
truncated error equation (
4.30
),
which is small in the sense that

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant Ck^{q}+C\varepsilon
. It remains to show an error bound that does not
depend on
\varepsilon
and to show that
\vartheta
is so small that
the truncation does not take effect.

We repeat the calculations in the first step of
Subsection
4.4.3
but with
\mu=1
and without relying on
(
4.28
). Instead, we recall from
(
4.46
) that

(4.51)

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant CMk^{q}+CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)\leqslant\nu,

where
\nu
can be made arbitrarily small.

The maximal regularity Lemma
2.3
applied to the error
equation (
4.34
) gives

(4.52)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,r,q}\big(\|A(\nabla u)\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\vartheta))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\big),

where, in view of (
4.17
),

(4.53)

\|A(\nabla u)\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\big)\leqslant Mk^{q}

and, by a modification of (
4.33
) and
(
4.17
), (
4.18
), (
4.51
),

(4.54)

\displaystyle\|(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\vartheta))(\tilde{u}-\vartheta)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant L\big(\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}+\|\nabla\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\big)\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+L\big(\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}+\|\nabla\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\big)\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant LM^{2}k^{q}+LM\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}+L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\nu\big)\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

Hence,

(4.55)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,q,T}\big(M+LM^{2}\big)k^{q}+C_{p,q,T}LM\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+C_{p,q,T}L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+\nu\big)\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.

Using the bound (an easy consequence of the embedding of

{W^{2,r}(\varOmega)}
into
{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}
for
r>d;
see

[
3
, Lemma 9.1]
)

(4.56)

\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant\gamma\|v\|_{W^{2,r}(\varOmega)}+C_{\gamma}\|v\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)},\quad\gamma>0,\ v\in W^{2,r}(\varOmega),

we obtain, with
\gamma=\nu/M
,

(4.57)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}+C_{\nu}\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+C_{p,q,T}L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+2\nu\big)\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))},

where
C
and
C_{\nu}
depend on
T
but not on
k
and
\varepsilon
.
If
C_{p,q,T}L\big(Mk^{q-1/p}+2\nu\big)\leqslant 1/2
(the same
smallness condition as before, but with
2\nu
instead of

\varepsilon
), then the last term can be hidden in the left-hand side
to get

(4.58)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}+C_{\nu}\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}.

We now apply a discrete Gronwall argument. The estimate in
(
4.58
) is also valid with
T
replaced by

t_{n},\ n=1,\dotsc,N
,

(4.59)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\nu}\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}+Ck^{q},\quad n=1,\dotsc,N,

with the same constants. Here we need to bound

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}
by

\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}

in such a way that the discrete Gronwall inequality applies.

The inequality

(4.60)

\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{\bar{n}}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\alpha_{\ell}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leqslant\sum_{m=1}^{\bar{n}}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\alpha_{\ell})^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

for nonnegative real numbers
\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{\bar{n}},

is an immediate consequence of the triangle inequality for the
\ell^{p}
-norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\bar{n}};

indeed, the left-hand side is the
\ell^{p}
-norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\bar{n}}
of the sum of the vectors

(4.61)

\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\alpha_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\end{pmatrix}+\dotsb+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\alpha_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{\bar{n}-1}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{\bar{n}}\end{pmatrix},

while the right-hand side is the sum of the
\ell^{p}
-norms of these vectors;
see
[
9
]
.

We write
\widehat{\vartheta}
in
J_{m-1}
in the form

(4.62)

\widehat{\vartheta}(t)=k\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}(t-\ell k),\quad t\in(t_{m-1},t_{m}).

Notice that any norm of
\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}(\cdot-\ell k)

in the interval
(t_{m-1},t_{m})
coincides with the corresponding norm
of
\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}
in the interval

(t_{m-\ell-1},t_{m-\ell}).

Let us denote by
\alpha_{\ell}
the discrete

\ell^{p}(L^{r}(\varOmega))
-(semi)norm of the backward difference
quotient
\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}
of
\widehat{\vartheta}
in the
interval
(t_{\ell-1},t_{\ell}),

\alpha_{\ell}:=\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((t_{\ell-1},t_{\ell});L^{r}(\varOmega))}.

With this notation, (
4.62
) yields

\|\vartheta\|_{\ell^{p}((t_{m-1},t_{m});L^{r}(\varOmega))}=\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((t_{m-1},t_{m});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant k\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\alpha_{\ell};

therefore,

\|\vartheta\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}=\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant k\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\alpha_{\ell}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

whence, in view of the inequality (
4.60
),

(4.63)

\|\vartheta\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}=\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant k\sum_{m=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\alpha_{\ell})^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

With the notation

\varTheta_{m}:=\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(\alpha_{\ell})^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

we have

\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}=\varTheta_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant c\|\vartheta\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant ck\sum_{m=1}^{n}\varTheta_{m};

hence, (
4.59
) yields

(4.64)

\displaystyle\varTheta_{n}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\nu}k\sum_{m=1}^{n}\varTheta_{m}+Ck^{q},

n=1,\dotsc,N.
We assume
C_{\nu}k\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
, so that

C_{\nu}k\varTheta_{n}
can be moved to the left-hand side. Then, a
discrete Gronwall inequality yields the optimal order estimate

(4.65)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\vartheta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle+\|\widehat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{\nu}k^{q}.

The space-time Sobolev argument also gives

(4.66)

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{\nu}k^{q}.

4.6.
Conclusion of the proof

We need to make sure that

(4.67)

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\varepsilon,

so that the truncation does not take effect and
\vartheta
is thus a
solution of the original error equation
(
4.25
). The desired bound
(
4.67
) will follow from
(
4.66
) after we fix
\nu
and then choose
k

sufficiently small.

We have assumed that

(4.68)

\displaystyle CL(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1/2

in
(
4.38
),
(
4.44
),
(
4.49
),

(4.69)

\displaystyle CMk^{q}+CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant\nu

in (
4.51
),

(4.70)

\displaystyle CL(Mk^{q-1/p}+2\nu)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1/2

in
(
4.58
),

(4.71)

\displaystyle C_{\nu}k

\displaystyle\leqslant 1/2

in (
4.65
),

where the constants do not depend on
k,\varepsilon
and
\nu

(except
C_{\nu}
that does depend on
\nu
). We must choose

\varepsilon
and
\nu
so that these hold for small
k
.

We first fix
\nu=1/(8CL)
, so that (
4.70
) as
well as (
4.71
) hold for small
k
. We then choose

\varepsilon=k^{q/2}
. Then, by (
4.66
),

(4.72)

\displaystyle\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C_{\nu}k^{q}\leqslant C_{\nu}k^{q/2}k^{q/2}=C_{\nu}k^{q/2}\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon,

if
C_{\nu}k^{q/2}\leqslant 1
, so that (
4.67
) holds
for small
k
. Moreover,

(4.73)

\displaystyle CLk^{q}+CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)=CLk^{q}+CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+k^{q/2})\leqslant\nu,

for small
k
, which is (
4.69
). Similarly,

(4.74)

\displaystyle CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+\varepsilon)=CLM(Mk^{q-1/p}+k^{q/2})\leqslant 1/2

for small
k
, which is (
4.68
).

We conclude that there exist
k_{0}
and
\nu
such that, for
0<k<k_{0}
,
there is a solution
\vartheta
of
(
4.25
) with

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\leqslant\nu

and hence a solution
U=\tilde{u}+\vartheta
of (
1.3
) in a
neighborhood of
u
with radius
2\nu
. For such
k
the convergence
estimates (
4.65
) and (
4.66
)
hold. In view of the approximation property
(
4.7
), this proves the asserted a priori
error estimate (
1.8
). By making the additional
regularity assumption
u\in W^{q+1,p}\big((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega)\big)

and by using (
4.9
) and
(
4.10
) we obtain
(
1.9
).

Finally, the a priori error estimate (
1.10
) is an
immediate consequence of the space-time Sobolev inequality
(
3.3
) and the a priori error estimate
(
1.9
).

4.7.
Local uniqueness

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be two solutions of the dG equation (
1.3
) as
obtained in the previous step of the proof,
Subsection
4.6
. Thus, both belong to a
neighborhood of
u
in
L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))

with (small) radius
2\nu
. We want to show that
U_{1}=U_{2}
, thereby
showing local uniqueness for equation (
1.3
).

We begin by noting that there are
C
and
M_{1}
such that

(4.75)

\displaystyle\|u-U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q-1/p},

(4.76)

\displaystyle\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant M_{1}.

The first bound follows from (
4.18
) and
(
4.66
). For the second one we use
(
4.18
), (
4.19
), (
4.65
) and an
inverse inequality:

\displaystyle\|U_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\rho_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\quad+Ck^{-1/p}\|\vartheta_{i}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant M+Ck^{q-1/p}\leqslant M_{1}.

By subtracting the two versions of (
1.3
), with
f
depending
only on
\nabla U
, from each other, and linearizing about

\nabla u
, we see that the difference

\eta:=U_{1}-U_{2}=\vartheta_{2}-\vartheta_{1}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)

satisfies
\eta(0)=0
and

(4.77)

\int_{J_{n}}\big[(\eta_{t},v)-(A(\nabla u)\eta,v)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t+(\eta_{n}^{+}-\eta_{n},v_{n}^{+})=\int_{J_{n}}(G,v)\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}(q-1)

with remainder

G:=\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U_{1})\big)U_{1}-\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U_{2})\big)U_{2};

cf. the derivation of (
4.25
).
By adding and subtracting
\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U_{1})\big)U_{2}
,
we obtain

G=-\big(A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla U_{1})\big)\eta-\big(A(\nabla U_{1})-A(\nabla U_{2})\big)U_{2}.

Similarly to (
4.54
), we obtain (here we use
(
4.75
) and (
4.76
))

\begin{split}\|G\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}&\leqslant L\|u-U_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\\
&\quad+L\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}\|U_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\\
&\leqslant LCk^{q-1/p}\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+LM_{1}\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}.\end{split}

In view of (
4.56
), we have

\|G\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant L(Ck^{q-1/p}+M_{1}\gamma)\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+LM_{1}C_{\gamma}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}.

The maximal regularity Lemma
2.3
applied to
equation (
4.77
) now gives

\displaystyle\|\partial_{k}\widehat{\eta}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\eta}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\widehat{\eta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,r,q}\|G\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{p,r,q}L(Ck^{q-1/p}+M_{1}\gamma)\|\eta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+C_{p,r,q}LM_{1}C_{\gamma}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}.

If
k
and
\gamma
are small, then the first term can be hidden in
the left-hand side and the proof is completed by the discrete Gronwall
argument in Subsection
4.5
.

5.
Conditional a posteriori error estimates

The purpose of this section is the proof of
Theorem
1.2
. As in Section
4
, we assume without
loss of generality that
f
depends only on
\nabla u
, but not
explicitly on
u
. Let

(5.1)

R(t):=\widehat{U}_{t}(t)-\nabla\cdot f(\nabla\widehat{U}(t)),\quad t\in(0,T],

be the residual of the reconstruction
\widehat{U},
i.e., the amount by which

\widehat{U}
misses being an exact solution of the differential equation in
(
1.1
). Consider the error
\hat{e}:=u-\widehat{U}.
Subtracting
(
5.1
) from the differential equation in (
1.1
), we
obtain

(5.2)

\hat{e}_{t}(t)=\nabla\cdot f(\nabla u(t))-\nabla\cdot f(\nabla\widehat{U}(t))-R(t),\quad t\in(0,T].

Using here the operator notation
A(\nabla v),
see
(
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
we have

\hat{e}_{t}(t)=A(\nabla u(t))u(t)-A(\nabla\widehat{U}(t))\widehat{U}(t)-R(t),

whence

(5.3)

\hat{e}_{t}(t)=A(\nabla u(t))\hat{e}(t)+g(t)-R(t),

with

g:=\big[A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla\widehat{U})\big]\widehat{U}=\big[A(\nabla u)-A(\nabla(u-\hat{e})\big]\big(u-\hat{e}\big);

cf. (
4.23
). Considering
g
a perturbation term, the maximal
regularity property for non-autonomous linear parabolic equations,
Lemma
2.1
, applied to (
5.3
) leads to
the preliminary estimate

(5.4)

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

for
t\in(0,T].
To estimate
g
on the right-hand side of
(
5.4
), we assume that

(5.5)

\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant\nu

with a sufficiently small constant
\nu,
independent of
k
and
N

such that
Nk\leqslant T;
notice that it is exactly the assumption
(
5.5
) that makes our a posteriori error analysis

conditional
. The a priori error estimate
(
1.10
) shows that it is, in principle, possible
to achieve this condition.

We argue similarly to Subsection
4.5
. By our
regularity assumption on
u
, we may use the bounds stated in
Subsection
4.2
. In particular, we have

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant M,\quad\|\widehat{U}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega)}\leqslant M+\nu\leqslant M+1.

Then, the local Lipschitz bound in (
4.5
) with constant

L=L_{M+1}
gives, similarly to (
4.54
),

(5.6)

\begin{split}\|g\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}&\leqslant L\|\nabla\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\\
&\quad+L\|\nabla\hat{e}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,t);L^{\infty}(\varOmega))}\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\\
&\leqslant LM\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{1,\infty}(\varOmega))}+L\nu\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}.\end{split}

As in (
4.57
) this leads to

\displaystyle\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant CLM\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+CL\nu\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}+C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

and, if
\nu
is small enough,

(5.7)

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

for
t\in(0,T]
and appropriate constants denoted by
C
. The desired a
posteriori bound (
1.12
) will now follow by a
(continuous) Gronwall argument.

For this purpose, we must bound

\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}
by

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}
in a way that
will allow us to apply the Gronwall inequality. Now,

\hat{e}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\hat{e}_{t}(\tau)\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,\text{ whence }\|\hat{e}(s)\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}\leqslant\int_{0}^{s}\|\hat{e}_{t}(\tau)\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau,

and, thus, by Hölder’s inequality, with
p^{\prime}
the dual of
p,

\|\hat{e}(s)\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}^{p}\leqslant s^{p/p^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s}\|\hat{e}_{t}(\tau)\|_{L^{r}(\varOmega)}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau.

Therefore,

(5.8)

\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}s^{p/p^{\prime}}\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

In view of the equivalence of the
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{p}
norms on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

(
5.7
) yields

(5.9)

\|e_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\leqslant C\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}+C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}.

Combining (
5.9
) and (
5.8
), we get

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}s^{p/p^{\prime}}\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}s+C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}.

We then infer, using Gronwall’s inequality, that

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}^{p}\leqslant C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}^{p},

and obtain the desired a posteriori error estimate

(5.10)

\|\hat{e}_{t}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}+\|\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);W^{2,r}(\varOmega))}\leqslant C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);L^{r}(\varOmega))}

for
t\in(0,T]
.

We show finally that the a posteriori estimator

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,T);L^{r}(\varOmega))}
is of optimal order
k^{q}.

We have

(5.11)

R(t)=-\hat{e}_{t}(t)+A(\nabla u(t))\hat{e}(t)+g(t);

see (
5.3
). Now, the first two terms on the right-hand
side of (
5.11
) are of order
k^{q}
in the desired norm in
view of (
1.9
). Finally,
g
is also of order
k^{q}

in the desired norm as a consequence of (
5.6
) and
(
1.9
).

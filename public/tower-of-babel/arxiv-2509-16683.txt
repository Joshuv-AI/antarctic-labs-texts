A Weight Structure on Rigid Analytic Motives over a Field
Kaixing Cao

Abstract
In this paper, we construct a monoidal weight structure on the stable ∞ \infty -category of rigid analytic motives over a local field K K via Galois descent. This extends the weight structure on the full subcategory of rigid analytic motives with good reduction, which is defined by Binda-Gallauer-Vezzani. As an application, we show that the Hyodo-Kato realization factors through the weight complex functor studied by Bondarko and Sosnilo. In particular, the weight complex yields a spectral sequence converging to the Hyodo-Kato cohomology of smooth quasi-compact K K -rigid analytic spaces, thereby inducing a weight filtration on it.

1 Introduction

Bondarko introduced weight structures on triangulated categories in
[
Bon10
]
as a concept dual to that of
t
-structures (see Remark
3.3
). Subsequently, Sosnilo developed the theory in the context of stable
\infty
-categories in
[
Sos19
,
Sos22
]
.

One of the main motivations for introducing weight structures is precisely their application to the study of motives: in
[
Bon10
,
Héb10
,
Bon14
]
, a weight structure, called the Chow weight structure, was constructed on the stable
\infty
-category
\mathbf{DA}(k)
of motives (with rational coefficients) over a field
k
. Since the construction of motivic
t
-structures is very difficult and remains largely open, weight structures provide a more accessible framework for organizing and analyzing motives.

On the other hand, Ayoub developed a theory of rigid analytic motives in
[
Ayo15
,
AGV22
]
, providing a natural analogue of motives in rigid analytic geometry. This naturally raises the question of whether weight structures can also be constructed in this rigid analytic setting. As observed in
[
BGV25
]
and
[
BKV25
, Appendix A]
, starting from the Chow weight structure on the algebraic motives, one can construct a good weight structure on a full subcategory
\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(K)
of the
\infty
-category of rigid analytic motives, i.e., consisting of those that admit good models; see Definition
4.2
. Even though the weight structure is defined only on this subcategory, it allows us to give the “motivic Hyodo-Kato isomorphism” (
[
BGV25
, Theorem 4.53]
) and get a weight filtration on the Hyodo-Kato cohomologies as a formal result. These indicate that weight structures on rigid analytic motives provide a powerful tool for connecting motivic ideas with
p
-adic cohomology theories.

In this paper, we will extend the weight structure on the full subcategory
\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(K)
to the whole category. Furthermore, this extended weight structure remains compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathbf{RigDA}(K)
in the sense of
[
Aok20
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.19
)
.

Let
K
be a complete non-archimedean field with perfect residue field
k
. There is a bounded weight structure
w
on the stable
\infty
-category
\mathbf{RigDA}(K)_{\omega}
of compact rigid analytic motives over
K
, and this bounded weight structure is compatible with the monoidal structure and extends the one on
\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(K)_{\omega}
mentioned above.

Moreover, it can be extended uniquely to its Ind-completion
\mathbf{RigDA}(K)
such that the full subcategory
\mathbf{RigDA}(K)_{w\geq 0}
is closed under small colimits.

To construct the weight structure in Theorem
1.1
, we apply Galois descent to relate compact analytic motives over
K
with analytic motives with good models: every compact rigid motive over
K
has potentially good reduction (
[
Ayo15
, Theorem
2.5.34]
,
[
AGV22
, Proposition 3.7.17]
). In particular, the full subcategory of compact analytic motives can be described
as a “union” of rigid analytic motives with good reduction up to finite Galois extensions. We rephrase this phenomenon with the following explicit form:

Proposition 1.2

(Proposition
4.16
)
.

We have a monoidal equivalence in
\mathbf{CAlg}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega})

\mathbf{RigDA}(K)\simeq\colim\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(L)^{\htcat\gal(L/K)}

where
L
runs through finite Galois extensions of

\gal(L/K)
-equivariant analytic motives (see Definition
2.7
and Remark

).

Therefore, it suffices to construct a weight structure on
\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(L)^{\htcat\gal(L/K)}
and glue it to obtain a weight structure on
\mathbf{RigDA}(K)
. The former category is the homotopy fixed points under the Galois action; in other words, it is the limit of
\mathbf{RigDA}_{\mathrm{gr}}(L)
along the Galois action. Although in general it is very difficult to equip a limit of stable
\infty
-categories with a weight structure, the situation here is special: the translation functors involved are autoequivalences. Thus, we can give an explicit formula for its compact generators, and prove they form a negative class (see Proposition
4.13
and Proposition
4.14
). In particular, we get a bounded weight structure on its compact part (Proposition
3.11
), which can be extended to the whole category as usual (discussed in §
3.3
).

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we can construct a p-adic analytic generalization of
Rapoport-Zink’s weight spectral sequence together with weight filtration in
[
RZ82
]
. The key ingredient that allows us to obtain such filtrations from (bounded) weight structures
is the weight complex functor studied in
[
Bon10
,
Sos19
]
: there is a symmetric monoidal functor

W_{\bullet}\colon\mathbf{RigDA}(K)_{\omega}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\htcat\mathcal{H}_{w})

(1.1)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\htcat\mathcal{H}_{w})
is the
\infty
-category of bounded chain complexes in the heart
\htcat\mathcal{H}_{w}
of the weight structure constructed in Theorem
1.1
.

Now we suppose the residue field
k
of
K
is a finite field. Under this assumption, we show that the Hyodo-Kato realization functor factors through the weight complex functor (
1.1
):

Proposition 1.3

(Lemma
5.13
)
.

After forgetting the monodromy operator, the Hyodo-Kato realization functor (
5.2
)

\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{HK}}\colon\mathbf{RigDA}(K)_{\omega}\to\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{(\varphi,G_{K})}(\breve{K})

\infty

(\varphi,G_{K})
-modules over

Using the standard spectral sequence associated to the weight complex, see
[
Bon10

for instance, of a compact rigid motive

, we get a spectral sequence converging to the Hyodo-Kato cohomology of

Proposition 1.4

(Weight Filtration, Proposition
5.14
, Corollary

)
.

Let

(1)

(2)

Remark 1.5
.

If

\tilde{D}=(\mathcal{D},\tau)
together with a functor
\tilde{\iota}\colon\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\to\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
such that
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{ev}(\tilde{\iota})
is a fully faithful functor.

Remark 2.12
.

In Definition
2.11
, the
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
is unique (up to homotopy) because the functor
\tilde{\iota}\colon\tilde{D}\to\tilde{C}
is a Cartesian morphism with respect to the evaluation functor
\mathrm{ev}
. Indeed, for every
G
-
\infty
-category
\tilde{E}
, the fully faithful functor
\iota\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
induces a fully faithful functor (see
[
GHN17
, Lemma 5.2]
for a stronger form)

\mathrm{Map}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D})\to\mathrm{Map}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C}).

Therefore, the commutative diagram of Kan complexes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Map}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}},\tilde{\mathcal{D}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Map}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}},\tilde{\mathcal{C}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Map}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{D})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Map}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{C})}}

is a homotopy pullback square.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\infty
-category equipped with an action by a
finite group
G
. Assume
\mathcal{D}
is a
G
-subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the functor

\mathcal{D}^{\htcat G}\to\mathcal{C}^{\htcat G}

obtained by applying
(-)^{\htcat G}
to the canonical inclusion, is fully faithful.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram (up to homotopy)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{\htcat G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\htcat G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}

where vertical morphisms are forgetful functors. Let
F\colon\mathcal{D}^{\htcat G}\to\mathcal{C}^{\htcat G}

denote the induced functor and let
\bar{M}
and
\bar{N}
be two objects in

\mathcal{D}^{\htcat G}
with underlying objects
M
,
N
in

\mathcal{D}
. The commutative diagram above shows the underlying objects of
F(\bar{M})
,
F(\bar{N})
in

\mathcal{C}
are
\iota(M)
,
\iota(N)
, respectively; here
\iota\colon\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}
is the inclusion. We deduce from Corollary
2.10
:

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{D}^{\htcat G}}(\bar{M},\bar{N})\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}(M,N)^{\htcat G}\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(\iota(M),\iota(N))^{\htcat G}\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}^{\htcat G}}(F(\bar{M}),F(\bar{N})).

∎

We end the subsection with a useful and well-known fact about
B_{\bullet}G
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Given a group
G
, we have a homotopy equivalence in
\mathcal{S}
,

B_{\bullet}G\simeq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert G_{\bullet}\right\rvert},

where
G_{\bullet}
is the simplicial object in
\mathcal{S}
given by

\mathrm{N}_{\bullet}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})\xrightarrow{B_{\bullet}G}\mathrm{N}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}et})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}.

Proof.

It follows from the homotopy equivalence
B_{\bullet}G\simeq\mathrm{Sing}_{\bullet}(\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert B_{\bullet}G\right\rvert})
and the fact that the latter is just computed by the given geometric realization; for instance, see
[
Kerodon
,
Variant 04QS
]
.
∎

3 Weight Structures on Stable
\infty
-Categories

For the reader’s convenience, we review the basic theory of weight structures on stable
\infty
-categories, after which we will recall several constructions of it. The main reference for this section is
[
Bon10
,
BS19
,
Sos19
,
Sos22
]
, to which we refer the reader for further details.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category and
\mathcal{D}
a full subcategory. We define a full subcategory
\prescript{\perp}{}{\mathcal{D}}
of
\mathcal{C}
that is spanned by those objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\simeq 0,\text{ for any $Y\in\mathcal{D}$}.

We will call
\prescript{\perp}{}{\mathcal{D}}
the
left orthogonal complement
of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Similarly, we can define the
right orthogonal complement

\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
as the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by those objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)\simeq 0,\text{ for any $Y\in\mathcal{D}$}.

3.1
The Definition of Weight Structures

Definition 3.2

(
[
Bon10
, Definition 1.1.1]
,
[
Sos22
, Definition 3.1.1]
)
.

A
weight structure
on a stable

\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
consists of a pair of retract-closed full subcategories
w=(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
satisfying the following properties:

Semi-invariance

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
; and we write, for any integers
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}[n],\qquad\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}[n].

Weak Orthogonality

If
X\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 1}
, then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\simeq 0
.

Weight Decomposition

For any object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, we
have a cofiber sequence

X_{\leq 0}\to X\to X_{\geq 1},

where
X_{\leq 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
X_{\geq 1}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 1}
. This is called a
weight decomposition

of
X
.

In the case, we also say
\mathcal{C}
is a
weighted stable

\infty
-category
.

Remark 3.3

(Dual to
t
-Structures)
.

In
[
Pau08
]
, weight structures were independently studied under the name “co-t-structure”. In fact, the axioms of a weight structure are dual to those of a t-structure
on a stable
\infty
-category (or a triangulated category)
\mathcal{C}
: apart from the semi-invariance condition, the role of the pair
(\mathcal{C}_{t\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{t\leq-1})
in the axioms of a
t
-structure corresponds to the pair
(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 1})
for a weight structure.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category equipped with a weight structure
w=(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
.

(1)

For every pair
(a,b)
of integers with
a\leq b
, we let
\mathcal{C}_{[a,b]}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq a}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq b}
.

In particular, if
a=b=n
, we write it simply by
\mathcal{C}_{w=n}
.

(2)

We let
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{b}}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by those objects
X
satisfying
X\in\mathcal{C}_{[a,b]}
for some integers
a\leq b
. The weight structure
w
on
\mathcal{C}
is said to be

bounded

if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{b}}
.

(3)

We will write
\mathcal{C}_{w=0}
by
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
and call it the
heart
of the weight structure
w
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category. The stable
\infty
-category
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
of bounded chain complexes has a canonical bounded weight structure:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})_{w\geq 0}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,M\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})\middle|\,M\text{ is isomorphic to a complex whose all negative degrees vanish}\,\right\}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})_{w\leq 0}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,M\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})\middle|\,M\text{ is isomorphic to a complex whose all positive degrees vanish}\,\right\}

The heart of this weight structure is exactly
\mathrm{N}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
.

Like
t
-structures, we also keep track of how weights vary under functors.

Definition 3.6

(
[
Bon14
, Definition 1.2.1]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},w_{\mathcal{C}})
and
(\mathcal{D},w_{\mathcal{D}})
be weighted stable

\infty
-categories and let
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be
an exact functor of the underlying stable
\infty
-categories. We say
F
is
left or right weight-exact
if
F(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{w\geq 0}
or
F(\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{w\leq 0}
, respectively. If
F
is both left and right weight-exact, then we say
F
is
weight-exact
.

Remark 3.7
.

In Definition
3.6
, if
w_{\mathcal{C}}
is a bounded weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
, then the weight-exactness of
F
can be checked using
F(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})
. To be precise, if
w_{\mathcal{C}}
is bounded, then
F
is weight-exact if and only if the essential image of
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
under
F
lies in
\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
by
[
Bon14
, Remark 1.2.3 (10)]
; see also Proposition
3.11
.

Definition 3.8

(
[
Aok20
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
be a stable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. We say a weight structure
w=(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0})
on the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is
compatible
with the symmetric monoidal structure if two full subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
are stable under the tensor product functor and contain the tensor unit.

Remark 3.9
.

If a weight structure on the underlying
\infty
-category of a stable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
is compatible with the monoidal structure, then, by
[
HA
, Proposition 2.2.1.1]
, the symmetric monoidal structure can be restricted to full subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
. We will use the obvious notations
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{w\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{w\leq 0}
,
(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})^{\otimes}
to denote the monoidal restrictions.

3.2
Bounded Weight Structures and Weight Complex Functors

We focus on bounded weight structures in the subsection. We will recall how these weight structures are recovered by their hearts.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category. A full subcategory
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
negative
if the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is connected for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{N}
.

As shown in
[
Bon10
, Corollary 1.5.7]
, the heart of a bounded weight structure is additive, idempotent complete and negative. In fact, these conditions completely determine the bounded weight structures:

Proposition 3.11

(
[
Bon10
, Theorem 4.3.2 (II)]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category. If
\mathcal{N}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

•

the full subcategory
\mathcal{N}
is negative;

•

the full subcategory
\mathcal{N}
generated
\mathcal{C}
under finite colimits, negative shifts and retracts;

then there is a unique bounded weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
whose heart
contains
\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, the heart is the idempotent completion of
\mathcal{N}
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\in\mathbf{CAlg}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}r}^{\mathrm{L}})
. Assume its underlying
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is stable and equipped with a bounded weight structure
w
. Then the weight structure
w
is compatible with the monoidal structure if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
contains the tensor unit and is stable under tensor products.

Proof.

The “only if” part is obvious (see Remark
3.9
). Conversely, since
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\in\mathbf{CAlg}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}r}^{\mathrm{L}})
, the tensor products preserve small colimits in each variable; in particular, they are exact (see
[
HA
, Proposition 1.1.4.1]
). So we can conclude from Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

A stronger result in a categorical formulation can be found in
[
Sos19
, Proposition 3.3]
: Sosnilo proved that taking weight heart is a fully faithful functor from the
\infty
-category of boundedly weighted stable
\infty
-categories to the
\infty
-category of additive
\infty
-categories. The essential image of this embedding is also given in
[
Sos19
, Corollary 3.4]
.

There is an important construction related to bounded weight structures:

Construction 3.14

(Weight Complex Functor,
[
Bon10
, §3]
,
[
Sos19
, Corollary 3.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a boundedly weighted stable
\infty
-category. Recall that, in Example
3.5
, we have seen that
\mathrm{N}_{\bullet}(\htcat\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})
is the heart of the bounded weighted stable
\infty
-category
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\htcat\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})
. Remark
3.13
tells us there is a weight-exact functor

W_{\bullet}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\htcat\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}),

whose heart is the unit functor
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}\to\mathrm{N}_{\bullet}(\htcat\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})
. This functor is called the
weight complex functor
of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
admits a symmetric monoidal structure compatible with the weight structure, then the weight complex functor has the symmetric monoidal refinement (
[
Aok20
, Theorem 4.3]
,
[
BGV25
, Proposition 3.27]
).

3.3
Constructions of Weight Structures

There is a generalization of Proposition
3.11
allowing us to construct a weight structure from a negative full subcategory. However, these weight structures are not necessarily bounded:

Proposition 3.15

(
[
BS19
, Theorem 2.2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category admitting small colimits. Assume
\mathcal{N}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
that generates
\mathcal{C}
under small colimits. Let
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
be full subcategories generated by
(\mathcal{N}[i])_{i\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{N}[i])_{i\leq 0}
, respectively, under small coproducts and extensions. If
\mathcal{N}[-1]\subseteq\prescript{\perp}{}{\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}}
holds, then
w=(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0})
is the unique weight structure
w
on
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

•

the heart of
w
contains
\mathcal{N}
; more precisely, the heart of
w
is the idempotent completion of the full subcategory generated by (possibly infinite) direct sums of
\mathcal{N}
;

•

the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
is closed under small coproducts (or equivalently small colimits).

This proposition allows us to extend the weight structure on the compact objects to the entire category:

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a compactly generated stable
\infty
-category. Assume the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
of compact objects has a weight structure
w
. Then
w
can be extended to a weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
by taking
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{C}_{\omega}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
in Proposition
3.15
. Moreover, it is the unique weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
extending
w
on
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
is closed under small colimits.

For our purposes, we state the following useful instance:

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a compactly generated stable
\infty
-category. Assume there is a small negative full subcategory
\mathcal{N}
consisting of compact objects, and it generates
\mathcal{C}
under small colimits and negative shifts.

(1)

There is a unique weight structure
w
on
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

•

the heart of
w
contains
\mathcal{N}
;

•

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
is closed under small colimits.

(2)

The weight structure
w
in
(1)
satisfies

(a)

the heart of
w
is the idempotent completion of the full subcategory generated by
\mathcal{N}
under directed sums;

(b)

the weight structure
w
restricts to the weight structure on
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
whose heart contains
\mathcal{N}
;

(c)

given
X\in\mathcal{C}
, then
X\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
if and only if
X\in\mathcal{N}[i]^{\perp}
for all
i<0
;

(d)

given
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
, then
X\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
if and only if
X\in\prescript{\perp}{}{\mathcal{N}[i]}
for all
i>0
.

(3)

Assume
\mathcal{C}
admits a symmetric monoidal structure whose tensor unit is in
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
. If for any pair
(X,Y)
of objects in
\mathcal{N}
, the tensor product
X\otimes Y
is in the heart, then
w
is compatible with the monoidal structure.

Proof.

(1)

By our assumption,
\mathcal{N}[-1]^{\perp}
contains all
\mathcal{N}[i]
for
i\geq 0
, and it is closed under extensions and small coproducts (because of compactness of objects in
\mathcal{N}
); thus
\mathcal{N}
satisfies the assumption in Proposition
3.15
, we apply it to get a desired weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

The part (a) is clear. For part (b), applying Proposition
3.11
to
\mathcal{N}
, we get the unique bounded weight structure on
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
whose heart contains
\mathcal{N}
. It can be extended to
\mathcal{C}
by using Corollary
3.16
. This new weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
agrees with
w
due to the uniqueness in (
1
). It remains to prove (c) and (d). There are obvious inclusions

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}\subseteq(\mathcal{N}[-i])^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}\subseteq\prescript{\perp}{}{\mathcal{N}[i]},

for all
i>0
. Now we prove the converse direction. For (c), it is clear:
\prescript{\perp}{}{\{X\}}
is closed under small coproducts and extensions, and then the assumption on
X
implies
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq-1}\subseteq\prescript{\perp}{}{\{X\}}
; equivalently, we have
X\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
. The similar argument works to prove (d) when
X
is compact.

(3)

We need to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
) is closed under the tensor product. The proofs for them are similar. Let’s prove for
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
(for
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
, one needs to notice that
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
is stable under small coproducts). We define
\mathcal{D}
as the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by those
X\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}\otimes X\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
. Then it suffices to prove
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. To this end, we need to show
\mathcal{D}
is closed under coproducts, extensions and contains
\mathcal{N}[i]
for all
i\leq 0
. As
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
is closed under coproduct and extensions, it remains to show
\mathcal{N}[i]\subseteq\mathcal{D}
for all
i\leq 0
. For this, we define a full subcategory
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
in a way similar to
\mathcal{D}
, that is,
X\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
if
X\otimes\mathcal{N}[i]\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
for
i\leq 0
. By our condition in the statement, we have inclusions
\mathcal{N}[j]\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
for all
j\leq 0
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is closed under small coproducts and extensions as well. This implies
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, as desired.

∎

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
be a functor in
\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega}
. Assume, for
i=1,2
, there is a small full subcategory
\mathcal{N}_{i}
satisfying the conditions in Corollary
3.17
. Let
w_{i}
be the weight structure obtained in Corollary
3.17
for
i=1,2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

F
is weight-exact;

(2)

after restricting to compact objects,
F\colon\mathcal{C}_{1,\omega}\to\mathcal{C}_{2,\omega}
is weight-exact;

(3)

F
sends
\mathcal{N}_{1}
into the heart of
w_{2}
.

Proof.

We prove the non-obvious direction (
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
). In fact, this follows from the fact that
F
preserves small colimits and extensions.
∎

4 Weight Structures on Rigid Analytic Motives

In the section, we are interested in weight structures on motives. Our goal is to construct a weight structure on the whole category of rigid analytic motives over a complete non-archimedean field. For this, we first recall the
\infty
-category of motive in §
4.1
and recall a relationship between analytic motives on the generic fibers and algebraic motives on the special fiber (see Proposition
4.1
). In §
4.2
, we prove categorical formulae to relate general rigid analytic motives with those with good reduction by Galois descent. Finally, we construct the weight structure on rigid analytic motives over fields in §
4.3
.

4.1
Stable
\infty
-Categories of Motives

Let
K
be a complete non-archimedean field with the residue field
k
of characteristic
p>0
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
the ring of integers. Then we have the following
\infty
-categories of motives:

•

the
\infty
-category of
rigid analytic motives

\mathbf{RigDA}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{RigSH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\spa(K,\mathcal{O}_{K}),\mathbb{Q})
over
K
defined in
[
AGV22
, Definition 2.1.15]
;

•

the
\infty
-category of
formal motives

\mathbf{FDA}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{FSH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\spf(\mathcal{O}_{K}),\mathbb{Q})
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
defined in
[
AGV22
, Definition 3.1.3]
;

•

the
\infty
-category of
algebraic motives

\mathbf{DA}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbf{FDA}(\spec(k),\mathbb{Q})
over
k
, where
\spec(k)
is regarded as a formal scheme in the obvious way.

As shown in
[
Ayo07
,
Ayo07a
,
Ayo14
,
Ayo15
,
AGV22
]
, these are compactly generated stable symmetric monoidal monoidal
\infty
-categories.

A theorem of Ayoub, as stated in
[
Ayo15
, Corollaire 1.4.24 & 1.4.29]
or
[
AGV22
, Theorem 3.1.10]
, yields a symmetric monoidal functor, called the
Monsky-Washnitzer functor
,

\xi\colon\mathbf{DA}(k)\simeq\mathbf{FDA}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\xrightarrow{(-)_{\eta}}\mathbf{RigDA}(K)

(4.1)

in
\mathbf{CAlg}(\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega})
, where
(-)_{\eta}
is induced by the rigid generic fiber functor of formal schemes. We denote its right adjoint by

\chi\colon\mathbf{RigDA}(K)\to\mathbf{DA}(k).

There is a remarkable way to relate motives on the rigid generic fiber with motives on the special fiber:

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the commutative algebra object
\chi{\mathbbm{1}}\in\mathbf{CAlg}(\mathbf{DA}(k))

Proof.

The first assertion is clear. For the full faithfulness, see

Remark 5.15
.

The spectral sequence in Proposition
5.14
is formulated homologically, but it can be translated into a cohomological version. Throughout this paper we adopt the homological convention, as it is more natural and convenient in the context of stable
\infty
-categories, where the pervasive use of homotopy-theoretic structures makes the homological perspective more suitable.

If we take
M

K

Corollary 5.16

(Weight Filtrations for Rigid Analytic Spaces)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-compact
K
-rigid analytic space. Then, for each
i
, the (overconvergent) Hyodo-Kato cohomology
H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)
admits a finite increasing filtration
\mathrm{Fil}^{W}_{k}H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)
stable under
G_{K}
-action whose
k
-th graded piece is pure of weight
i+k
and the monodromy induces a map
\mathrm{gr}_{k}^{W}H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)\to\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{k-2}H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)
.

Remark 5.17
.

The filtration in Corollary
5.16
is referred as the
weight filtration
of the Hyodo-Kato cohomology
H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)
. In
[
BGV25
]
, the authors constructed it for a special case where
X
admits semi-stable reduction, which can be regarded as the
p
-adic analytic analogue of the weight filtration defined by Rapoport-Zink in
[
RZ82
]
for
\ell
-adic cohomologies of algebraic varieties. In contrast, we do not assume the existence of formal models of
X
, and we prove that our weight filtration is stable under the Galois action.

This filtration is closely related to Deligne’s weight-monodromy conjecture. Indeed, it satisfies the conditions for Deligne’s weight filtration as formulated in
[
Del70
,
Del74
]
and partially satisfies those for the
monodromy filtration
in
[
Del74
]
. More precisely, the only missing piece is whether the monodromy operator induces an isomorphism

N^{k}\colon\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{k}H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X)\to\mathrm{gr}^{W}_{-k}H^{i}_{\mathrm{HK}}(X).

(5.5)

From this perspective, if the morphism in (
5.4
) is an isomorphism, then the
p
-adic weight-monodromy conjecture holds.

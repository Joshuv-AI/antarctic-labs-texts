Understanding two-scale criteria for Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities in the Euclidean case through Φ \Phi -entropies


Abstract
We study settings in which mixture and joint distributions satisfy a Poincaré (or log-Sobolev) inequality induced by a marginal and a collection of conditional distributions that are assumed to satisfy Poincaré (or log-Sobolev, resp.) inequalities and supported over Euclidean spaces. In this note, we use the framework of Φ \Phi -Sobolev inequalities ( Chafaï 2004 ) to provide a unified approach to arriving at these inequalities in the Euclidean case. This results in a simpler proof technique for establishing these functional inequalities under certain two-scale criteria. We also discuss applications of these results to certain sampling algorithms.

Introduction

The role of isoperimetric functional inequalities such as the Poincaré inequality (PI) and log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) are essential in quantitatively characterising the concentration of measure phenomemon
(
Ledoux 2001
)
and the behaviours of Markov processes
(
Bakry et al. 2014
)
, which have applications in high-dimensional statistics
(
Wainwright 2019
)
and Markov chain Monte Carlo methods
(
Frieze and Kannan 1999
;
Chewi et al. 2024
)
.
This motivates identifying cases in which such inequalities hold, and characterising such cases through verifiable criteria.

One such setting is when the probability measure of interest is either a mixture distribution over a set
{\cal X}
, or a joint distribution over a product domain
{\cal X}\times{\cal Y}
, and this is the focus of this note.
More precisely, we are interested in the following questions.

Let
\rho
be a probability measure over
{\cal Y}
that satisfies PI (or LSI) and
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
be a collection of probability measures each of which are supported on
{\cal X}
and satisfy PI (or LSI, resp.).

Q1

Does the joint distribution
\nu=P_{y}\cdot\rho
over
{\cal X}\times{\cal Y}
satisfy PI (or LSI, resp.)?

Q2

Does the mixture distribution
\int_{{\cal Y}}P_{y}\cdot\mathrm{d}\rho(y)
over
{\cal X}
satisfy PI (or LSI, resp.)?

Answering
Q1
above automatically yields an answer to
Q2
as the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities for the joint probability measures over
{\cal X}\times{\cal Y}
imply the same respectively for its marginals.
These questions have been investigated in the past; specifically, in the setting where
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
are Euclidean spaces,
Q1
was originally discussed in
Blower and Bolley 2006
;
Grunewald et al. 2009
, and later by
Otto and Reznikoff 2007
who also give a stronger result for
Q2
compared to the implied result by marginalising
P_{y}\otimes\rho
.
Fundamentally, these results consider assumptions on each
P_{y}
for
y\in{\cal Y}
in addition to
\rho
and
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
satisfying the aforementioned functional inequalities, and these assumptions are collectively referred to as ‘‘two-scale criteria’’
1
1

1

The two scales refer to
\rho
(macro-scale) and
P_{y}
(micro-scale).
.
The difference between
Otto and Reznikoff 2007
and
Blower and Bolley 2006
lies in the additional assumptions placed on each
\{P_{y}\}
.
Such two-scale criteria have been recently revisited in
Mou et al. 2019
;
Ge et al. 2020
in particular when distributions
\rho
and
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
satisfy Poincaré inequalities while the focus of the older results focus on when
\rho
and
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
satisfy log-Sobolev inequalities.
When
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
are discrete spaces, this problem was studied in the works of
Jerrum et al. 2004
and
Bobkov and Tetali 2006
, and more recently by
Hermon and Salez 2023
.

In this work, we identify simple criteria on the family of densities of
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
, which in conjunction with isoperimetric conditions on
\rho
and each of
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
affirmatively answers
Q1
and
Q2
described above.
These criteria generalise the assumptions made in
Otto and Reznikoff 2007
;
Mou et al. 2019
;
Ge et al. 2020
, and the salient difference between these prior works and this note is the unified manner in which we arrive at these two-scale criteria.
This is particularly facilitated by viewing the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities as instances of a
\Phi
-Sobolev inequality introduced by
Chafaï 2004
, which allows us to obtain a broader understanding of the two-scale criteria considered in these prior work.
The proof technique we adopt is also more elementary approach to arriving at these results in comparison to
Otto and Reznikoff 2007
who use a Markov semigroup theory approach in their intermediate steps to arrive at the results in the LSI case.
Through our proof, we also additionally recover results about product and convolution probability measures formed by two independent distributions.

Notation

The Euclidean space in
d
dimensions is represented by
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For any two vectors
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

.
The density of a distribution

.
We use the shorthand notation

f(x)
where
x\sim\pi
.
For a function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
that is differentiable in both arguments, we use
\nabla_{1}\psi(x,y)
to denote the gradient of
x\mapsto\psi(x,y)
, and
\nabla_{2}\psi(x,y)

y\mapsto\psi(x,y)

.

2.1
Functional inequalities

Let
\pi
be a probability measure over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the set of compactly supported smooth functions over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Consider a function
f
such that
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f^{2}]<\infty
.
The variance of
f
with respect to
\pi
is defined as

\mathsf{var}{}_{\pi}[f]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f^{2}]-({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])^{2}~.

For a positive function
f
such that
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f\cdot\log(f)]<\infty
, the entropy of
f
with respect to
\pi
is

\mathsf{ent}{}_{\pi}[f]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f\cdot\log f]-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\cdot\log\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\right)~.

With these definitions, we define the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities in the Euclidean setting.

Definition 2.1

(
\mathsf{PI}\left(\gamma\right)
)
.

Let
\pi
be a probability measure over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then,
\pi
is said to satisfy a
Poincaré inequality with constant
\gamma>0
if for all
f\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that are suitably integrable,

\mathsf{var}{}_{\pi}[f]\leq\gamma\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\|\nabla f\|^{2}]~.

Definition 2.2

(
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\gamma\right)
)
.

Let
\pi
be a probability measure over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then,
\pi
is said to satisfy a
log-Sobolev inequality with constant
\gamma>0
if for all
f\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
that are suitably integrable,

\mathsf{ent}{}_{\pi}[f^{2}]\leq 2\gamma\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\|\nabla f\|^{2}]~.

Both the variance and the entropy are instances of a more general
\Phi
-entropy
(
Chafaï 2004
)
, which we introduce next.
Let
\Phi:{\cal S}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex function over a convex subset
{\cal S}
or
{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
f
whose range is
{\cal S}
and satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi(f)]<\infty
, the
\Phi
-entropy of
f
with respect to
\pi
is defined as

{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[f\right]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi(f)]-\Phi\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\right)~.

(1)

Similarly, the Poincaré and the log-Sobolev inequalities can be viewed as instances of a more general class of inequalities called
\Phi
-Sobolev inequalities
(
Chafaï 2004
)
which is defined next.

Definition 2.3

(
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\gamma\right)
)
.

Let
\pi
be a probability measure over
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then,
\pi
is said to satisfy a
\Phi
-Sobolev inequality with constant
\gamma>0
if for all
f\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap\{\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\cal S}\}
,

{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[f\right]\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(f)\cdot\|\nabla f\|^{2}]~.

The purpose of introducing the
\Phi
-entropy and the
\Phi
-Sobolev inequality is to minimise redundancies in the computations made in the note.
This is due to the following: whence

•

{\cal S}={\mathbb{R}}
and
\Phi(t)=t^{2}
,
{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}=\mathsf{var}{}_{\pi}
and
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\gamma\right)
is
\mathsf{PI}\left(\gamma\right)
, and

•

{\cal S}=(0,\infty)
and
\Phi(t)=t\log(t)
,
{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}=\mathsf{ent}{}_{\pi}
and
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\gamma\right)
is
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\gamma\right)
.

Main results

In this section, we state the main results derived in this note.
First, we define two conditions that complete the two-scale criteria considered in this work.

Variance criterion (abbrev.
Var
):

\forall~u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}~,{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle^{2}\right]\leq\bar{L}^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}~.

(
Var
)

Moment generating function criterion (abbrev.
MGF
):

\forall~u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}~,\log{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\exp\left(\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle\right)\right]\leq\frac{\bar{L}^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}}{2}~.

(
MGF
)

3.1
Sufficient conditions for the two-scale criteria (
Var
,
MGF
)

Here, we discuss simpler conditions under which the two-scale criteria introduced can be found to hold.
We refer the reader to the
Appendix

A
for the definitions of Lipschitz continuity and sub-Gaussianity.
We also make note of the following identity (
Proposition

5.1
):

\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)={\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\nabla_{2}G(x,y)]-\nabla_{2}G(x,y)~.

With regard to
Var
, we have the following cases.

1.

Assume that
x\mapsto\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)
is
B
-bounded for all
y
.

In this setting, for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have that
\|\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\|\leq B
.

Mou et al. 2019
consider this condition in their Lemma 1 towards answering
Q1
.
Under this condition, we have for any
u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\|u\|^{2}\cdot\|\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq B^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}~,

which shows that
Var
holds with
\bar{L}=B
.

2.

Assume that
x\mapsto\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)
has
B
-bounded variance w.r.t.
P_{X|Y=y}
for all
y
.

This states that for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\|\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\|^{2}]\leq B^{2}
.

Ge et al. 2020
consider this assumption in their Theorem D.3 towards answering
Q1
.
In the context of information theory, the quantity
{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\|\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\|^{2}]
is referred to as the
pointwise statistical Fisher information

(
Wibisono et al. 2017
, §III.B)
.
From the workings above, we can also infer that
Var
holds with
\bar{L}=B
under this condition.

3.

Assume that
x\mapsto\nabla_{2}G(x,y)
is
L
-Lips. continuous and
P_{X|Y=y}
satisfies
\mathsf{PI}\left(\beta\right)
for all
y
.

Under this condition, for any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we have that
x\mapsto\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\rangle
is
L\cdot\|u\|
-Lipschitz continuous.
This in conjunction with the fact that
P_{X|Y=y}
satisfies
\mathsf{PI}\left(\beta\right)
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\left\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)-{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\nabla_{2}G(x,y)]\right\rangle^{2}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{V}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\left\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\right\rangle]

\displaystyle\leq\beta\cdot L^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}~.

Therefore,
Var
holds with
\bar{L}=\sqrt{\beta}\cdot L
.
We would also like to point out that
Var
holds when
P_{X|Y=y}
satisfies a
\beta
-bounded variation condition instead of
\mathsf{PI}\left(\beta\right)
for all
y
.
We include details about this in the appendix.

Next, with regard to
MGF
, we have the following cases.

1.

Assume that
x\mapsto\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)
is
B
-bounded for all
y
.

For any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, this implies that
x\mapsto\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\rangle
lies in the range
[-\|u\|\cdot B,\|u\|\cdot B]
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
By Hoeffding’s lemma, we have that for any
u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that

\log{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\exp\left(\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle\right)\right]\leq\frac{B^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}}{2}~.

This shows that
MGF
holds with
\bar{L}=B
.

2.

Assume that the random variable
\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}({\sf X})
is
\sigma
-sub-Gaussian for
{\sf X}\sim P_{X|Y=y}
for all
y
.

In other words, for every
y
and unit vector
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}({\sf X},y)\rangle
is sub-Gaussian w.r.t.
P_{X|Y=y}
with variance proxy
\sigma^{2}
.
This can be equivalently written as: for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\log{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\exp\left(\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle\right)\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}}{2}~,

which is precisely
MGF
with
\bar{L}=\sigma
.
Note that this sub-Gaussianity condition implies that for any
u,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle^{2}\right]\leq 4\sigma^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}~,

thus establishing that this also implies
Var
with
\bar{L}=2\sigma
.

3.

Assume that
x\mapsto\nabla_{2}G(x,y)
is
L
-Lips. continuous and
P_{X|Y=y}
satisfies
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\beta\right)
for all
y
.

This condition is the focus of
Otto and Reznikoff 2007
.
By Herbst’s argument
(
Bakry et al. 2014
, Prop. 5.41)
, we have that

\displaystyle\log{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\exp\left(\left\langle u,\nabla_{y}\right.\right.\right.\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)\right\rangle\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\log{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\exp\left(\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}\left[\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\rangle\right]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta\cdot L^{2}\cdot\|u\|^{2}}{2}~

where we have used the fact that
x\mapsto\langle u,\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\rangle
is
(L\cdot\|u\|)
-Lipschitz continuous for all
y
.
This proves that
MGF
holds with
\bar{L}=\sqrt{\beta}\cdot L
.

3.2
Special cases when

is independent of

Before discussing the applications to sampling, we discuss some special cases for when
P_{y}
is independent of

.
We suppress the subscript in

for brevity.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a function such that
\frac{1}{\Phi^{\prime\prime}}
is concave.
If
\rho
and
P
satisfy
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\alpha\right)
and
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\beta\right)
respectively, then

•

\rho\otimes P
satisfies
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\right)
, and

•

the convolved distribution
\rho\ast P
satisfies
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\alpha+\beta\right)
.

For
\rho\otimes P
, the above lemma generalises the result implied by
Theorem

3.1
with
\bar{L}=0
.
For
\rho\ast P
, recall that

is equal in distribution to

where

and

.
The first part of

applied to test functions of the form

for

, one can infer the weaker result:
\rho\ast P
satisfies
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(2\cdot\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\right)
, and the second result of

improves this to

, which is strictly better.
See

5.3
for the proof.

With regards to the tightness of the constant, the constant implied by the first part of

3.1
is tight and is realised in the case where
\rho={\cal N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{1})
and

.
From

, for

, we know

satisfy

Specifically,

is a bivariate Gaussian

and satisfies

The constant

is precisely

Applications

4.1
Evolution of isoperimetric properties along certain sampling algorithms

The central motivation for this study is based on understanding how isoperimetric properties evolve along certain sampling algorithms.
This has been the interest of recent work
(
Vempala and Wibisono 2023
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Liang et al. 2025
)
, which specifically focuses on the algorithms principled in the overdamped Langevin dynamics with stationary distribution
\pi^{\star}
supported on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let the density of
\pi^{\star}
be of the form
\mathrm{d}\pi^{\star}(x)\propto e^{-V^{\star}(x)}\mathrm{d}x
.
The Langevin dynamics is given by the SDE defined below;
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the Brownian motion in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=-\nabla V^{\star}(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2}\mathrm{d}B_{t}~.

The unadjusted Langevin algorithm
(
Roberts and Tweedie 1996
)
is the forward Euler-Maruyama discretisation of the Langevin dynamics with step size
h>0
.
This leads to the iterative method

X^{(k+1)}=X^{(k)}-h\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(X^{(k)})+\sqrt{2h}\cdot{\mathfrak{g}}_{k};\qquad{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}\sim{\cal N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})~.

Suppose the distribution of
X^{(k)}
is
\rho^{(k)}
.
Then, from the iteration above,
\rho^{(k+1)}
can be represented as a mixture distribution with
P_{y}={\cal N}(y-h\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y),2h\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d})
and mixing distribution
\rho=\rho^{(k)}
in the notation of the questions considered in the introduction.

Another related method is the proximal sampler
(
Lee et al. 2021
)
.
This considers an augmented distribution
\tilde{\pi}^{\star}
with parameter
\eta>0
referred to as the step size, and whose density is given by

\mathrm{d}\tilde{\pi}^{\star}(x,y)\propto\exp\left(-\left\{V^{\star}(x)+\frac{\|y-x\|^{2}}{2\eta}\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y~,

and the procedure performs Gibbs’ sampling over this augmented distribution as shown.

Y^{(k+1)}\mid X^{(k)}\sim\tilde{\pi}^{\star}(y|x=X^{(k)})~;\qquad X^{(k+1)}\mid Y^{(k+1)}\sim\tilde{\pi}^{\star}(x|y=Y^{(k+1)})~.

If the distribution of
X^{(k)}
is
\rho^{(k)}
, then the distribution of
Y^{(k+1)}
can be represented as a mixture distribution with
P_{y}={\cal N}(y,\eta\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d})
and
\rho=\rho^{(k)}
, and the distribution of
X^{(k+1)}
can be represented as a mixture distribution with
P_{y}=\tilde{\pi}^{\star}(.|y)
and
\rho=\mathrm{Law}(Y^{(k+1)})
.

One of the implications of this study is in understanding rates of estimation of functionals of
\pi^{\star}
by using samples obtained through multiple chains of a sampling algorithm.
Consider a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]<\infty
.
In general, obtaining
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]
in closed form may not be computationally feasible, but it is possible to obtain approximations to
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]
through samples from
\pi^{\star}
.
Given
N
independent samples
\{x_{i}^{\mathsf{Alg}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from
N
parallel chains of a sampling algorithm designed to (approximately) sample from
\pi^{\star}
, this functional can be naively estimated by
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i}^{\mathsf{Alg}})
.
The error of estimation denoted by
\mathrm{Err}_{N}(f;\pi^{\star})
is given by

\mathrm{Err}_{N}(f;\pi^{\star})=\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i}^{\mathsf{Alg}})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]\right|\leq\underbrace{\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x_{i}^{\mathsf{Alg}})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}}[f]\right|}_{(1)}+\underbrace{\left|{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}}[f]-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]\right|}_{(2)}~.

This error is dependent on (1) how closely
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}}[f]
matches
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\star}}[f]
, and (2) how quickly the sample average concentrates around
{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}}[f]
, where
\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}
is the distribution of each of the
x_{i}^{\mathsf{Alg}}
.
While both factors are dependent on properties of
f
, the first factor is particularly determined by the
accuracy
of the sampling algorithm, and the second factor is determined by the
concentration of measure
of
\pi^{\mathsf{Alg}}
, which is can be studied through its isoperimetric properties.

4.1.1
Application to the unadjusted Langevin algorithm

Recall that a step of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm from
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is given by

y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y)+\sqrt{2\eta}\cdot\xi;\quad\xi\sim{\cal N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})~.

This is equivalent to sampling from
{\cal N}(y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y),2\eta\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d})
.
Therefore, when
y\sim\rho
, the marginal law of the iterate after one step of Langevin is given by
\mu
as stated in
Eq.

4
, with

P^{\mathrm{ULA}}_{X|Y=y}={\cal N}(y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y),2\eta\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d}).

The density function of
P^{\mathrm{ULA}}_{X|Y=y}
is

\mathrm{d}P^{\mathrm{ULA}}_{X|Y=y}(x)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{\|x-(y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y))\|^{2}}{4\eta}\right)\mathrm{d}x~.

From this setup, we can identify that

\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}^{\mathrm{ULA}}(x)=\frac{1}{2\eta}\cdot(\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y))(y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y)-x)~.

For any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}^{\mathrm{ULA}}(x,y)\rangle=\frac{1}{2\eta}\cdot u^{\top}(\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y))(y-\eta\cdot\nabla V^{\star}(y)-x)

For
x\sim P_{X|Y=y}^{\mathrm{ULA}}
which is Gaussian,
\langle u,\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}^{\mathrm{ULA}}(x,y)\rangle
is a Gaussian random variable with mean
0
and variance

\frac{1}{4\eta^{2}}\cdot\|(\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y))u\|^{2}\cdot 2\eta=\frac{\|(\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y))u\|^{2}}{2\eta}~.

Let
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}=\sup_{y}\|\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.
Then, both
Var
and
MGF
are satisfied with
\bar{L}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}{\sqrt{2\eta}}
, which enables us to apply
Theorem

3.2
to infer isoperimetric properties of
\mu
.
Specifically, if
\rho
satisfies
\mathsf{PI}\left(\alpha\right)
/
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\alpha\right)
, then
\mu
satisfies
\mathsf{PI}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)
/
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)
, where

\alpha^{\prime}=2\eta+\alpha\cdot 2\eta\cdot\frac{{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2}}{2\eta}=2\eta+\alpha\cdot{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2}~.

This results in the following recursion:
\alpha^{(k)}
is the Poincaré or log-Sobolev constant for the distribution at the
k^{th}
iteration of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm.

\alpha^{(k+1)}=2\eta+\alpha^{(k)}\cdot{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2}~.

The sequence
\{\alpha^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 1}
diverges when
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}\geq 1
.
Instead when
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}<1
, we can give a characterisation of the biased limit of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm as shown below.
From
Proposition

B.2
with
c\leftarrow{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2},d\leftarrow 2\eta,a_{n}\leftarrow\alpha^{(k)}
, we have

\alpha^{(k)}=2\eta\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2i}+{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2k}\cdot\alpha^{(0)}=2\eta\cdot\frac{1-{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2k}}{1-{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2}}+{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2k}\cdot\alpha^{(0)}~.

This reveals two aspects of ULA; assuming
\alpha^{(0)}
is finite, and
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}<1
,

1.

the isoperimetric properties of the sequence
\{\rho^{(k)}\}
, and

2.

as
k\to\infty
, the (biased) limit of the unadjusted Langevin algorithm satisfies a Poincaré or log-Sobolev inequality with constant
\alpha^{(\infty)}=\frac{2\eta}{1-{c_{\mathrm{ULA}}}^{2}}
.

We instantiate this in a specific setting where
V^{\star}
is
\mu
-strongly convex and
\lambda
-smooth.
This corresponds to the following bounds on the Hessian
\nabla^{2}V^{\star}
:
\mu\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y)\preceq\lambda\cdot\mathrm{I}_{d}
.
This implies that
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}=\sup_{y}\|\mathrm{I}_{d}-\eta\cdot\nabla^{2}V^{\star}(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\max\{|1-\eta\cdot\lambda|,|1-\eta\cdot\mu|\}
.
Note that when
\eta\in(0,\nicefrac{{2}}{{\lambda}})
,
c_{\mathrm{ULA}}<1
, and we can also deduce that

\alpha^{(\infty)}=\min\left\{-\frac{\eta\lambda^{2}}{2}+\lambda,-\frac{\eta\mu^{2}}{2}+\mu\right\}^{-1}~.

We make a couple of inferences in this setting:

•

When
\eta\to 0
,
\alpha^{(\infty)}\to\frac{1}{\mu}
.
This is expected behaviour as the unadjusted Langevin algorithm is the forward Euler-Maruyama discretisation of the Langevin diffusion, whose stationary distribution is
\pi^{\star}
.
By the Bakry-Emery criterion, the
\mu
-strong convexity of
V^{\star}
implies that
\pi^{\star}
satisfies
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\mu^{-1}\right)
(and hence
\mathsf{PI}\left(\mu^{-1}\right)
).

•

When
\eta\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}
, we obtain

\alpha^{(\infty)}\geq\min\left\{\frac{\lambda}{2},\frac{\mu}{2}\right\}^{-1}=\frac{2}{\mu}

which is precisely the result in
Vempala and Wibisono 2023
.

4.1.3
Application to exact Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

Another method for sampling from a distribution
\pi^{\star}
when its corresponding
V^{\star}
is differentiable is through the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) algorithm
(
Neal et al. 2011
)
.
This is based on the Hamiltonian dynamics which defines a path
(X_{t},Q_{t})_{t\geq 0}
according to

\dot{X}_{t}=\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial P}(X_{t},Q_{t});\quad\dot{Q}_{t}=-\frac{\partial{\cal H}}{\partial X}(X_{t},Q_{t})

for the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

{\cal H}(X,Q)=\frac{\|Q\|^{2}}{2}+V^{\star}(X)~.

At iteration
k
, from
X_{0}=X^{(k)}
, the
exact
HMC algorithm picks
Q_{0}\sim{\cal N}(0,\mathrm{I}_{d})
, and simulates the Hamiltonian dynamics up to a certain time
t=T
, and sets
X^{(k+1)}=X_{T}
.
The resulting time-homoegeneous Markov chain is reversible w.r.t.
\pi^{\star}
and is hence unbiased.
This exact HMC kernel additionally also satisfies a Poincaré inequality for all
y
when
V^{\star}
is strongly convex and smooth; see the proposition below.

Proposition 4.2

(
Chen and Vempala 2022
)
.

Let
V^{\star}
be
\mu
-strongly convex and
\lambda
-smooth.
For any
c\geq 2
, if the integration time
T
is set as
\frac{1}{c\sqrt{\lambda}}
, the conditional distribution
P^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
associated with a single step of exact HMC from
X_{0}=y
satisfies
\mathsf{PI}\left(8c^{2}\kappa\right)
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Through
Theorem

3.2
and the above proposition, we can infer the following: if
P^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
satisfies
Var
and
X^{(0)}\sim\rho^{(0)}
where
\rho^{(0)}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality, then the iterates along exact HMC satisfy a Poincaré inequality.
Despite the deterministic nature of exact HMC given the initial velocity,
Var
for
P^{eHMC}_{X|Y=y}
is hard to check in general.

The Gaussian Setting:

Nonetheless, in the case of a Gaussian target
\pi^{\star}
such that
V^{\star}(x)=\frac{x^{\top}Mx}{2}
, we have the following form for
p^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
using the closed form solution of the Hamiltonian flow for this
V^{\star}
.

p^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}(x)\propto\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\cos(\sqrt{M}T)y)^{\top}\phi(\sqrt{M}T)^{-1}(x-\cos(\sqrt{M}T)y)}{2T^{2}}\right)~;\quad\phi(z)=\left(\frac{\sin(z)}{z}\right)^{2}~.

We make two key observations about
P^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
here.

1.

By the Bakry-Emery criterion,
P^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
satisfies
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\beta\right)
where
\beta=T^{2}\cdot\lambda_{\max}(\phi(\sqrt{M}T))
.

2.

The map
x\mapsto\nabla_{2}G^{\text{eHMC}}(x,y)
associated with
P^{\text{eHMC}}_{X|Y=y}
is also
\bar{L}
-Lipschitz continuous where
\bar{L}=T^{-2}\cdot\|\phi(\sqrt{M}T)^{-1}\cdot\cos(\sqrt{M}T)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
.

Note that both
z\mapsto\phi(z)
and
z\mapsto\cos(z)\cdot\phi(z)^{-1}
are decreasing for
z<1
.
Therefore, when
T=\frac{1}{c\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(M)}}
for
c\geq 2
, these constants are

\beta=T^{2}\cdot\phi((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})~;\quad L=\cos((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})\cdot\phi((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})^{-1}\cdot T^{-2}~.

Now we build a recursion for
\{\rho^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 0}
: if
\rho^{(k)}
satisfies
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\alpha^{(k)}\right)
, then from
Theorem

3.2
we have that
\rho^{(k+1)}
satisfies
\mathsf{LSI}\left(\alpha^{(k+1)}\right)
where

\alpha^{(k+1)}=\beta+\alpha\cdot\beta^{2}\cdot L^{2}=T^{2}\cdot\phi((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})+\alpha^{(k)}\cdot\cos^{2}((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})~.

From
Proposition

B.2
, we have for any
N\geq 0

\alpha^{(N)}=T^{2}\cdot\phi((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left(\cos^{2}((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})\right)^{i}+\left(\cos^{2}((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})\right)^{N}\cdot\alpha^{(0)}~.

As

, we have

\alpha^{(\infty)}=\frac{T^{2}\cdot\phi((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})}{1-\cos^{2}((c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-1})}=\frac{T^{2}}{(c\sqrt{\kappa})^{-2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(M)}~.

These calculations show that in this case, the isoperimetric properties of exact HMC converge rapidly.
The limit is not surprising due to the unbiased nature of the exact HMC method.

\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}=\|\nabla_{1}\psi\|^{2}+\nabla_{2}\psi\|^{2}
, which gives

{\mathbb{J}}_{\nu}^{\Phi}\left[\psi\right]\leq\frac{\max\{\alpha,\beta\}}{2}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\psi)\cdot\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}]~.

This proves the first part of the lemma.
On the other hand, when
\psi(x,y)\leftarrow f(x+y)
, it holds that
\|\nabla_{1}\psi(x,y)\|^{2}=\|\nabla_{2}\psi(x,y)\|^{2}=\|\nabla f(x+y)\|^{2}
, and hence

{\mathbb{J}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}^{\Phi}\left[f(x+y)\right]\leq\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(f(x+y))\cdot\|\nabla f(x+y)\|^{2}]~.

To see the relevance to the second part of the lemma, we have for any measurable function
g
that
{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim\rho\ast P}[g(z)]={\mathbb{E}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}[g(x+y)]
.
This yields

{\mathbb{J}}_{z\sim\rho\ast P}^{\Phi}\left[f(z)\right]\leq\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim\rho\ast P}\left[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(f(z))\cdot\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}\right]

which completes the proof for the second result of the lemma.
∎

5.4.1
Proof of
Proposition

Proof.

We have by definition

\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)=-G(x,y)-\log Z(y)~.

We know that

Z(y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\exp\left(-G(x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}x~.

Taking the gradient on both sides w.r.t.
y
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla Z(y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\exp(-G(x,y))\cdot\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\nabla Z(y)}{Z(y)}

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\exp(-G(x,y))}{Z(y)}\cdot\nabla_{2}G(x,y)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\nabla_{2}G(x,y)]~.

(12)

By noting that
\nabla\log Z(y)=\frac{\nabla Z(y)}{Z(y)}
, we have

\nabla_{y}\log p_{X|Y=y}(x)={\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim P_{X|Y=y}}[\nabla_{2}G(x,y)]-\nabla_{2}G(x,y)~.

∎

Proof.

We start with the definition of

,

\displaystyle\nabla M(y)

(13)

Proof.

For the second part of the proposition, note that

{\mathbb{J}}_{\nu}^{\Phi}\left[\psi\right]={\mathbb{E}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}[\Phi(\psi(x))]-\Phi({\mathbb{E}}_{(x,y)\sim\nu}[\psi(x)])={\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim\mu}[\Phi(\psi(x))]-\Phi({\mathbb{E}}_{\mu}[\psi])~,

since the
y
-marginal of

is
\mu

5.4.4
Proof of
Proposition

5.4

Proof.

From
Chafaï 2004
, we have by the assumptions of this proposition that

{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[g\right]\geq{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[f\right]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}\left[(\Phi^{\prime}(f)-\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]))\cdot(g-f)\right]~.

We write
\Phi^{\prime}(f)-\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])
as
\Phi^{\prime}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)]-\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])
, and expand the second term in the RHS above.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}\left[(\Phi^{\prime}(f)-\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]))\cdot(g-f)\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[(\Phi^{\prime}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])\cdot(g-f)]

\displaystyle\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[g-f]\cdot(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[(\Phi^{\prime}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])\cdot g]-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[g]\cdot(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])

\displaystyle\qquad-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)\cdot f]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\cdot\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])~.

(14)

Instantiating the identity in
Eq.

6
for the last two terms in
Eq.

14
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\cdot\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])

\displaystyle=\Phi({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])+\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]))

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)\cdot f]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi(f)]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}(f))]

This results in

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]\cdot\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)\cdot f]

\displaystyle=\Phi({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi(f)]+\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]))-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}(f))]

\displaystyle=-{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[f\right]+\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f]))-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime}(f))]

\displaystyle=-{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[f\right]-{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime})}\left[f\right]~.

(15)

Substituting
Eqs.

14
and
15
in the inequality due to
Chafaï 2004
, we obtain that

{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi}\left[g\right]\geq{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[(\Phi^{\prime}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])\cdot g]-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[g]\cdot(\Phi^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}(f)])-{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi^{\star}(\Phi^{\prime})}\left[f\right]~,

which when rearranged gives the statement of the proposition.
∎

6
Discussion

In this note, we showed Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities for joint and mixture distributions induced by a marginal distribution and a collection of conditional distributions under certain multi-scale criteria.
In particular, we achieved this by viewing these functional isoperimetric inequalities as special case as
\Phi
-Sobolev inequalities.
We find that the multiscale criteria for the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities (
Var
and
MGF
, respectively) are fundamentally connected to the variational forms of their corresponding
\Phi
-entropies.
Through this more general analysis, we identify a previously unknown result about the mixture
\mu
when
x\mapsto\nabla\log p_{X|Y=y}
is Lipschitz-continuous.
Some open questions that remain are as follows.
Does this analysis more general apply to obtain
\Phi
-Sobolev inequality that is neither the Poincaré nor the log-Sobolev inequality.
We discuss the case of
\Phi_{p}(t):t\mapsto\frac{t^{p}-1}{p-1}
below where we face certain difficulties.
The discussion about exact HMC motivates studying the other regularity properties of the Hamilton flow, which would enable a more complete picture about how isoperimetric properties evolve along exact HMC for strongly log-concave and smooth target distributions more generally.

6.1
Comments about interpolating between
\mathsf{ent}
and
\mathsf{var}

Motivated by the generalising beyond the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, we investigate the case where
\Phi_{p}(t)=\frac{t^{p}-1}{p-1}
for
p\in(1,2]
.
The associated
\Phi
-entropy
{\mathbb{J}}^{\Phi_{p}}
interpolates between
\mathsf{ent}
and
\mathsf{var}
; the former is the limit of
{\mathbb{J}}^{\Phi_{p}}
as
p\to 1
, and the latter is
\mathsf{var}
when
p=2
.
The
\Phi
-Sobolev inequality for
\Phi\leftarrow\Phi_{p}
is referred to as the
Beckner inequality

(
Bakry et al. 2014
, §7.6.2)
.
Owing to the shared properties between
t\mapsto t^{2},t\mapsto t\log(t)
and
\Phi_{p}(t)

(
Chafaï 2004
, Pg. 5)
, one might surmise from
Theorems

3.1
and
3.2
that when
\rho
satisfies
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\alpha\right)
and
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in{\cal Y}}
individually satisfy
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\beta\right)
for
\Phi\leftarrow\Phi_{p}
, then
\nu
and
\mu
would satisfy
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\zeta\right)
and
\Phi\mathsf{SI}\left(\xi\right)
respectively as in
Theorems

3.1
and
3.2
for
\Phi\leftarrow\Phi_{p}
.
However, this has proven challenging to establish.
To examine this further, we first note that the duality identity (
Proposition

5.4
) holds by virtue of the
\frac{1}{\Phi_{p}^{\prime\prime}}
being concave

{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[(\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(f)-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}_{p}(f)])\cdot g]\leq{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi_{p}}\left[g\right]+{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[g]\cdot(\Phi_{p}^{\prime}({\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[f])-{\mathbb{E}}_{\pi}[\Phi^{\prime}_{p}(f)])+{\mathbb{J}}_{\pi}^{\Phi_{p}^{\star}(\Phi_{p}^{\prime})}\left[f\right]~.

The
Var
and
MGF
conditions arise naturally from this duality identity when
\Phi(t)=t^{2}
and
\Phi(t)=t\log(t)
, as one of the second and third terms on the RHS above reduce to
0
and the remaining term non-zero terms are directly related to
Var
and
MGF
respectively.
For
\Phi_{p}
however, both terms are non-zero.
Moreover, we use the Legendre property of the functions
t^{2}
and
t\log(t)
after instantiating the above identity, which is absent for
\Phi_{p}(t)
as
|\Phi^{\prime}_{p}(t)|\not\to\infty
as
t\to 0
.

Appendix B
Miscellaneous lemmas and their proofs

Proposition B.1
.

Let
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then, for any
{\sf C}>0
,

(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+{\sf C})\cdot a^{2}+(1+{\sf C}^{-1})\cdot b^{2}

Proof.

By expanding the square

(a+b)^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}+2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}+2|a||b|\leq a^{2}+{\sf C}\cdot a^{2}+b^{2}+{\sf C}^{-1}\cdot b^{2}~,

where the final step applies Young’s inequality for products (or AM-GM inequality).
∎

Proposition B.2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence defined through the recursion

a_{n+1}=c\cdot a_{n}+d

for
c,d\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
a_{0}=a
.
Then, for
n\geq 0

a_{n}=d\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c^{i}+c^{n}\cdot a~.

Proof.

We will prove the formula by induction.
The base case
n=0
is true by definition.
Assume that for some
n\geq 1
that the formula is true.
Then,

a_{n+1}=c\cdot a_{n}+d=c\cdot\left(d\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c^{i}+c^{n}\cdot a\right)+d=d\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^{i}+c^{n+1}\cdot a+d=d\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{n}c^{i}+c^{n+1}\cdot a~,

which recovers the expected formula for
a_{n+1}
, thus proving the proposition.
∎

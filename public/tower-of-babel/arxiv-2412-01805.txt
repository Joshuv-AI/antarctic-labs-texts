Solving one-body ensemble N-representability problems with spin
Julia Liebert, Federico Castillo, Jean-Philippe Labbé, Tomasz Maciazek, Christian Schilling

Abstract
The Pauli exclusion principle is fundamental to understanding electronic quantum systems. It namely constrains the expected occupancies n i n_{i} of orbitals φ i \varphi_{i} according to 0 ≤ n i ≤ 2 0\leq n_{i}\leq 2 . In this work, we first refine the underlying one-body N N -representability problem by taking into account simultaneously spin symmetries and a potential degree of mixedness 𝒘 \boldsymbol{w} of the N N -electron quantum state. We then derive a comprehensive solution to this problem by using basic tools from representation theory, convex analysis and discrete geometry. Specifically, we show that the set of admissible orbital one-body reduced density matrices is fully characterized by linear spectral constraints on the natural orbital occupation numbers, defining a convex polytope Σ N , S ​ ( 𝒘 ) ⊂ [ 0 , 2 ] d \Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})\subset[0,2]^{d} . These constraints are independent of M M and the number d d of orbitals, while their dependence on N , S N,S is linear, and we can thus calculate them for arbitrary system sizes and spin quantum numbers. Our results provide a crucial missing cornerstone for ensemble density (matrix) functional theory.



I
Introduction

The original Pauli exclusion principle has profoundly shaped our understanding of
N
-electron quantum systems, as it imposes the fundamental restriction that each orbital can accommodate at most one spin-up and one spin-down electron. This principle lies at the core of the Aufbau principle, which explains the structure of the periodic table of elements. When incorporating the probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics and the influence of electron correlation, the Pauli exclusion principle can be reformulated as a universal kinematic constraint on the orbital one-particle reduced density matrix (1RDM)
\gamma_{l}
: for any
N
-electron quantum state, the vector of orbital occupancies
\mbox{$\langle\varphi_{i}|$}\gamma_{l}\mbox{$|\varphi_{i}\rangle$}
of
d
reference orbitals
\varphi_{i}
is confined to the Pauli hypercube
[0,2]^{d}
. This geometric interpretation corresponds to the solution of the so-called one-body ensemble
N
-representability problem, which seeks to characterize the set of orbital 1RDMs
\gamma_{l}
that can be derived from some
N
-electron density matrix
Coleman 1963
;
Coleman and Yukalov 2000
.

Despite its broad relevance, the Pauli exclusion principle ignores two critical features of realistic
N
-electron quantum states, particularly in the context of quantum chemistry. First, these states are often characterized by definite spin quantum numbers, reflecting the spin symmetries of the underlying Hamiltonian. Furthermore, electron spin plays a fundamental role in various physical phenomena, such as magnetism and the quantum Hall effect
von Klitzing et al. 1980
;
von Klitzing 1986
;
Fradkin et al. 1998
;
Murthy and Shankar 2003
, and its incorporation in numerical methods is essential for achieving high predictive accuracy. Second, due to thermal effects or entanglement arising from interactions with the environment,
N
-electron states frequently exhibit a certain degree of mixedness. It is therefore the primary goal of this work to introduce and solve a refined version of the one-body ensemble
N
-representability problem that accounts for these two crucial aspects. To achieve this, we employ basic mathematical tools to construct a comprehensive solution. This approach leads to a generalized exclusion principle that, from a geometric perspective, identifies a subpolytope of the Pauli hypercube,
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})\subset[0,2]^{d}
, representing the admissible natural orbital occupation numbers.

From a practical perspective, our work is motivated by the growing interest in reduced density matrix methods for targeting excited states using a distinctive ensemble variational principle
Gross et al. 1988
;
Ding et al. 2024
. Specifically, we demonstrate that solving the spin-symmetry-adapted ensemble
N
-representability problem provides a compact and rigorous characterization of the yet-unknown domain of universal interaction functionals in the rapidly evolving field of ensemble density functional theory (EDFT)
Oliveira et al. 1988
;
Yang et al. 2017
;
Fromager 2020
;
Loos and Fromager 2020
;
Cernatic et al. 2022
;
Gould and Kronik 2021
;
Yang 2021
;
Gould et al. 2023
;
Giarrusso and Loos 2023
;
Scott et al. 2024
;
Cernatic et al. 2024
and ensemble one-particle reduced density matrix functional theory (
\boldsymbol{w}
-RDMFT)
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023a
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023b
. In this context, our work addresses a critical gap, providing a foundational cornerstone for the advancement of these ensemble-based methodologies.

Notation

We consider non-relativistic spin-
1/2
fermions whose one-particle Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{1}

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(s)}\cong\mathbbm{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbbm{C}^{2}\,,

(1)

where
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
denotes the orbital component of
\mathcal{H}_{1}

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)})=d<\infty
, and

describes the spin degrees of freedom.
The Hilbert space of
N
-fermions,
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, follows as the
N
-fold wedge product of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, that is
\mathcal{H}_{N}=\wedge^{N}\mathcal{H}_{1}
.
Due to the relevance of spin symmetries in physics and chemistry as explained in the introduction, we consider
N
-fermion states with well-defined total spin
S
and magnetization
M
. The magnetization
M
follows in general as the expectation value of the
S_{z}
operator,

S_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(f_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{i\alpha}-f_{i\beta}^{\dagger}f_{i\beta}\right)\,,

(2)

where
f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger},f_{i\sigma}
denote the fermionic creation/annihilation operators for a fermion in a spatial orbital
i
with spin
\sigma=\alpha,\beta
1
1

1

We resort in our work to a notation for the two spin states used commonly in quantum chemistry, also since the symbols
\uparrow/\downarrow
will be used to indicate vectors with increasingly/decreasingly ordered entries.
and we set
\hbar\equiv 1
.
Moreover, the total spin quantum number
S
is determined by the so-called Casimir operator
\boldsymbol{S}^{2}
of the Lie group
SU(2)
,

\boldsymbol{S}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}S_{i}^{z}S^{z}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\left(S^{+}_{i}S^{-}_{j}+S^{-}_{i}S^{+}_{j}\right)\,,

(3)

where
S_{i}^{+}=f_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{i\beta},S_{i}^{-}=f_{i\beta}^{\dagger}f_{i\alpha}
denote the spin raising and lowering operators.
The operators
S_{z},S_{\pm}
constitute the generators of the group
SU(2)
and commute with spin-independent Hamiltonians as, for instance, the non-relativistic electronic structure Hamiltonian. Which such applications in mind, it is then advantageous to exploit the Peter-Weyl decomposition of the
N
-fermion Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{N}
into symmetry sectors labelled by the quantum numbers
S,M
according to (see, e.g.,
Hall 2015
;
Miller 1972
)

\mathcal{H}_{N}=\bigoplus_{S=S_{\mathrm{min}}}^{N/2}\bigoplus_{M=-S}^{S}\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}\,,

(4)

where
S_{\mathrm{min}}=0
for
N
even and
S_{\mathrm{min}}=1/2
for
N
odd.

The set of density operators corresponding to pure quantum states with spin quantum numbers
S,M
follows as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{N}_{S,M}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Big\{\Gamma:\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}\to\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}\,|\,\Gamma\geq 0,

(5)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{Tr}_{N}[\Gamma]=1,\Gamma=\Gamma^{2}\Big\}\,.

Here, it is taken into account that for finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces positivity
\Gamma\geq 0
implies hermiticity,
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\dagger}
, and the condition
\Gamma=\Gamma^{2}
together with the normalization implies that
\Gamma=\mbox{$|\Psi\rangle$}\!\mbox{$\langle\Psi|$}
for some suitable
\mbox{$|\Psi\rangle$}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
. What is more, the elements of the set
\mathcal{P}^{N}_{S,M}
constitute the extremal points of the convex and compact set
\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}
of all ensemble
N
-fermion quantum states,

\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}=\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{P}^{N}_{S,M}\right)\,,

(6)

where here and in the following
\mathrm{conv}(\cdot)
always denotes the convex hull operation. The one-particle reduced density matrix (1RDM)
\gamma
of a quantum state
\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}
is obtained by tracing out all except one fermion according to
\gamma=N\mathrm{Tr}_{N-1}[\Gamma]
. Conservation of the total magnetization
M
implies that the 1RDM
\gamma
is block-diagonal with respect to the spin states
\mbox{$|\alpha\rangle$},\mbox{$|\beta\rangle$}
and the only non-vanishing blocks in
\gamma
are the
\alpha,\beta
-blocks,
\gamma_{\alpha\alpha},\gamma_{\beta\beta}
, i.e.,

[S_{z},\Gamma]=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma=\gamma_{\alpha\alpha}\oplus\gamma_{\beta\beta}\,.

(7)

Furthermore, tracing out the spin degree of freedom yields the so-called orbital (or spin-traced) 1RDM

\gamma_{l}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(s)}}[\gamma]=\gamma_{\alpha\alpha}+\gamma_{\beta\beta}\,.

(8)

Accordingly, the orbital 1RDM
\gamma_{l}
is obtained from
\Gamma
by applying the map
\mu

\mu=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(s)}}\circ N\mathrm{Tr}_{N-1}\,,

(9)

a composition of two partial traces. Since partial traces are linear,
\mu
is a linear map on the space of (density) operators, a property that will be crucial for our work.
The matrix elements of the orbital 1RDM in a given basis of
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
,

\mbox{$\langle i|$}\gamma_{l}\mbox{$|j\rangle$}\equiv(\gamma_{l})_{ij}=\mathrm{Tr}_{N}[E_{ij}\Gamma]\,,

(10)

are nothing else than the expectation values of the
U(d)
generators
Moshinsky 1968
;
Rowe et al. 2012

E_{ij}=f_{j\alpha}^{\dagger}f_{i\alpha}+f_{j\beta}^{\dagger}f_{i\beta}\,.

(11)

These generators will be essential for deriving the solution to the orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability problem introduced in the next section.

II.2
Orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability problem

The set of orbital 1RDMs
\gamma_{l}
, which are compatible to a pure
N
-fermion state
\Gamma\in\mathcal{P}^{N}_{S,M}
is denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}=\left\{\gamma_{l}\,|\,\gamma_{l}=\mu(\Gamma)\text{ for some }\Gamma\in\mathcal{P}^{N}_{S,M}\right\}\,.

(12)

Since the orbital 1RDM is spin-traced, this and various other sets of orbital 1RDMs introduced throughout this work are independent of the magnetic quantum number
M
.
We therefore omit indices
M
whenever possible.

Since the map
\mu(\cdot)
is linear, the image of the set
\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}
under the map
\mu(\cdot)
(
9
) is equal to the convex hull of
\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}
, such that

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{N,S}^{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\mu\left(\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}\right)=\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}\right)\,.

(13)

An orbital 1RDMs
\gamma_{l}
is called
pure/ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representable

if and only
\gamma_{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}/\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{N,S}^{1}
.
The sets of pure/ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representable orbital 1RDMs are invariant under unitary transformations
u_{l}
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
, i.e.,

\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad u_{l}\gamma u_{l}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}

(14)

and analogously for
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{N,S}^{1}
. Therefore, for any values
N,S,M
and
d
, both sets are fully characterized by purely
spectral
constraints, conditions on the vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of eigenvalues of
\gamma_{l}
, also called
natural orbital occupation numbers
.
A formal mathematical procedure for deriving the spectral characterization of the set
\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}
has been provided by Klyachko and Altunbulak in Refs.
Klyachko 2006
;
Altunbulak and Klyachko 2008
. Yet, due to its mathematical complexity, this problem could be solved only for artificially small values
N,d
. Quite in contrast, by using tools from convex analysis, a complete spectral characterization of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{N,S}^{1}
could be derived recently in Ref.
Liebert et al. 2024a
for arbitrary system sizes
N,d
. In analogy to the spin-adapted generalized Pauli constraints derived by Klyachko and Altunbulak in Refs.
Klyachko 2006
;
Altunbulak and Klyachko 2008
, these convex relaxed spin-adapted exclusion principle constraints take the form of linear inequalities resulting in a convex spectral polytope, a spin-dependent subset of the Pauli hypercube
[0,2]^{d}
.

Moreover, we like to stress that the pure state setting of Refs.
Klyachko 2006
;
Altunbulak and Klyachko 2008
means to restrict to
N
-fermion density matrices
\Gamma
with specific fixed spectrum
\mathrm{spec}^{\downarrow}(\Gamma)=\boldsymbol{w}_{0}\equiv(1,0,\ldots)
. Therefore and due to the physical relevance of mixed states, one might be also interested in the solution of the one-body
N
-representability problem for a generic choice of the fixed spectrum
\mathrm{spec}^{\downarrow}(\Gamma)=\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{w}^{\downarrow}
.
This more general one-body
N
-representability problem contains indeed Klyachko’s pure state

-representability problem as a special instance, namely for
\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{w}_{0}
. This also implies that solving the more general problem with generic
\boldsymbol{w}
will be more complicated than the one for pure states.

To this end, we first introduce the respective sets of
N
-fermion states and orbital 1RDMs. The set of
N
-fermion states
\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}
with fixed spectrum
\boldsymbol{w}
is denoted by

\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})\equiv\left\{\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}\,|\,\mathrm{spec}^{\downarrow}(\Gamma)=\boldsymbol{w}\right\}\,.

(15)

The set
\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})
is in general not convex, as the convex combination of two states with fixed spectrum
\boldsymbol{w}
usually yields a state with a different spectrum. Therefore, we also introduce its convex hull,

\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{N}_{S,M}\!(\boldsymbol{w})\equiv\mathrm{conv}\left(\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})\right)\,.

(16)

A basic mathematical result (see, e.g., Ref.
Liebert et al. 2022
) provides a simple characterization of such
convex hulls as the one in Eq. (
16
): A density matrix
\Gamma
belongs to the set
\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{N}_{S,M}\!(\boldsymbol{w})
if and only if its spectrum is majorized by
\boldsymbol{w}
,
\mathrm{spec}^{\downarrow}(\Gamma)\prec\boldsymbol{w}
. In general, we say that a vector
\boldsymbol{x}\equiv(x_{1},\ldots,x_{D})
majorizes a vector
\boldsymbol{y}\equiv(y_{1},\ldots,y_{D})
,
\boldsymbol{y}\prec\boldsymbol{x}
, if and only if for all
k=1,2,\ldots,D
the following condition holds

y_{1}^{\downarrow}+\ldots+y_{k}^{\downarrow}\leq x_{1}^{\downarrow}+\ldots+x_{k}^{\downarrow}\,,

(17)

with equality for
k=D
and the superscript
\downarrow
indicates that the entries in a vector are rearranged decreasingly. The majorization
\prec
defines a (pre)order on the space of vectors. Moreover, when applied to the spectra of density matrices it compares them in terms of their degree of mixedness. In particular,
\mathrm{spec}(\Gamma)\prec\mathrm{spec}(\Gamma^{\prime})
implies
S(\Gamma)\geq S(\Gamma^{\prime})
, where
S(\Gamma)\equiv-\mbox{Tr}[\Gamma\log\Gamma]
is the von Neumann entropy
Marshall et al. 2010
.

III
Solution of relaxed spin-adapted one-body
N
-representability problem

The spectral set
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
is a convex polytope as a direct consequence of the convexity theorems about moment polytopes in Refs.
(
Kostant 1973
;
Atiyah 1982
;
Guillemin and Sternberg 1982
)
. According to the Minkowski-Weyl theorem, this implies that
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
is equivalently characterized by its vertex representation or a minimal hyperplane representation
Ziegler 1994
. In this section we derive first the vertex representation and then turn it into a minimal hyperplane representation by using tools from discrete geometry. The general scheme uses similar mathematical tools as presented in detail for the spin-independent case in Refs.
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Castillo et al. 2023
;
Castillo and Labbé 2024
. Nevertheless, there are several crucial differences compared to the spin-independent setting. This includes in particular a different construction of the Hasse diagram of partially ordered configurations and its dependence on the quantum numbers
S,M
. Therefore, we focus in the following on these differences in the solution to the
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability problem arising from fixing the spin quantum numbers
S,M
. The derivation of the spin adapted
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability constraints of the orbital 1RDM provides insights into the geometric structure of the respective convex polytopes
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
. Readers only interested in the constraints themselves might directly jump to Sec.
IV
.

III.1
Hasse diagram of partially ordered configurations

As a consequence of the duality principle for compact convex sets and the Minkowski-Weyl theorem, the spectral set
\Sigma_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})
is fully characterized by the intersection of its supporting hyperplanes
Rockafellar 2015
;
Ziegler 1994
.
A supporting hyperplane of the polytope
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
at a point
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
is a hyperplane that satisfies
a^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq a^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
for all
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\Sigma_{N,S}
, where
\boldsymbol{a}
denotes the normal vector of the hyperplane
Ziegler 1994
.
Moreover, the dual variable of the orbital 1RDM
\gamma_{l}
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
is the orbital one-particle Hamiltonian

h_{l}\equiv\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}h_{ij}E_{ij}

(22)

and, in analogy to Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
, we can assume without loss of generality that
h_{l}
and
\gamma_{l}
share a common eigenbasis. We denote the vector of eigenvalues of
h_{l}
by
\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}
.
In combination with the aforementioned duality principle, this means that we have to minimize linear functionals
\langle\boldsymbol{h},\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rangle=\mathrm{const.}
over the set
\Sigma_{N,S}
for all possible normal vectors
\boldsymbol{h}
of the corresponding hyperplanes.
Due to the unitary invariance of the set
\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{1}_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
we can further restrict to those orbital one-particle Hamiltonians
h_{l}
with a increasingly ordered spectrum
h_{1}^{(l)}\leq h_{2}^{(l)}\leq\ldots\leq h_{d}^{(l)}
and discuss only
\Sigma^{\downarrow}_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
instead of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
, as introduced in Sec.
II.2
.

The supporting hyperplanes are calculated by considering a so-called Hasse diagram of partially ordered configurations. To this end, we first introduce the set
\mathcal{I}_{N,S,M}
of configuration states
|\boldsymbol{i}\rangle
that constitute a basis for the
N
-particle Hilbert
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
of states with fixed quantum numbers
S,M
. The set
\mathcal{I}_{N,S,M}
can be constructed from chosen orthonormal bases
\mathcal{B}_{l},\mathcal{B}_{s}
of
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)},\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(s)}
by constructing a Verma basis of the irreducible representation space as explained in Refs.
Li et al. 1986
;
Paldus and Planelles 1973
(see also the textbook
Hall 2015
). The spatial (or, also called orbital) configurations
\boldsymbol{i}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N})
denote the spatial orbitals
\mbox{$|i\rangle$},i=1,\ldots,d
occupied by the
N
fermions. Due to the spin degree of freedom each
i
can appear at most twice in a configuration
\boldsymbol{i}
. The energetically lowest configuration
\mbox{$|\boldsymbol{i}_{0}\rangle$}\in\mathcal{I}^{N}_{S,M}
is the highest weight state
|\Lambda\rangle

Hall 2015
;
Liebert et al. 2024a
, which is unique according to Cartan’s theorem
Hall 2015
. By definition, the highest weight state
|\Lambda\rangle
is annihilated by all positive root operators
E_{ij},j<i
with
E_{ij}
defined in Eq. (
11
). For
S
and the special case
M=S
as good quantum numbers, a state
|\Lambda_{S,M=S}\rangle
is the unique highest weight state if and only if

\forall j<i:\,\,E_{ij}\mbox{$|\Lambda_{S,M=S}\rangle$}=0\quad\wedge\quad S^{+}\mbox{$|\Lambda_{S,M=S}\rangle$}=0\,,

(23)

where
S^{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}S_{i}^{+}
.
Therefore, the highest weight state for
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,S)}
is given by

\mbox{$|\Lambda_{S,M=S}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\underbrace{1\alpha,1\beta,\ldots,K\alpha,K\beta}_{N-2S},\underbrace{(K+1)\alpha,\ldots,J\alpha}_{2S},0,\ldots\rangle$}\,,

(24)

where we introduced

K=\frac{N-2S}{2}\,,\quad J=\frac{N+2S}{2}\,.

(25)

The highest weight state
|\Lambda_{S,M}\rangle
of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
follows then from
|\Lambda_{S,M=S}\rangle
by a finite number of applications of the negative root operator
S^{-}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}S_{i}^{-}
until the desired
M
is reached. In particular, in contrast to Slater determinants, as used in Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
, the weights of the configurations
\boldsymbol{i}
for
(S,M)
do not necessarily correspond to vertices of the set
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}=(1,0,\ldots))
. Here, the word weight originates from a representation theoretical point of view and refers to the natural occupation number vector of the orbital 1RDM of a configuration state.
If these weights are not extremal elements of the set
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
, this implies that they do not correspond to non-degenerate ground states of a non-interacting Hamiltonian (
22
). Still they have to be taken into account when constructing the excitation pattern. This will become important in Sec.
III.2
.
Beyond the ground state configuration, the partial ordering of the configurations
\boldsymbol{i}
is defined equivalently to Ref.
Liebert et al. 2022
:
\boldsymbol{i}\leq\boldsymbol{j}
if and only if
i_{k_{1}}+\ldots+i_{k_{N}}\leq j_{k_{1}}+\ldots+\leq j_{k_{N}}
.

Before we work out the Hasse diagram of partially ordered configurations, we observe that the operators
E_{ij}
are singlet irreducible tensor operators of the group
SU(2)
. Therefore, the Wigner-Eckart theorem
Hall 2015
implies that the expectation value of the 1RDM matrix elements
(\gamma_{l})_{ij}
for a given state
\mbox{$|\Psi_{S,M}\rangle$}\in\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
equal the reduced matrix elements (see also Eq. (
10
)). This implies that despite the configuration states for
S,M
differ for different
M
, their configurations
\boldsymbol{i}
are equal and we can restrict without loss of generality to
S=M
for the construction of the Hasse diagram of partially ordered orbital configurations. On the level of the spectral set this implies that
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})=\Sigma_{S,M^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{w})
for all
M,M^{\prime}=-S,\ldots,S
.
Furthermore, we observe that the structure of Hasse diagram is effectively independent of
S,N,d
if they satisfy the three conditions

\displaystyle d-\frac{N+2S}{2}

\displaystyle\geq r-1\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N-2S}{2}

\displaystyle\geq r-1\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\quad 2S

\displaystyle\geq r-1\,,

(28)

where

N
-representability problem is effectively independent of

as long as they are fulfilled. In particular, this allows us to derive the relaxed

-ensemble

III.2
Vertex representation

Equipped with the Hasse diagram determined in the previous section, we are now in a position to derive the vertex representation of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
.
The new stability conditions in Eqs. (
26
)-(
28
) and the different construction of the Hasse diagram of the orbital configurations through the positive root operators
E_{ij}
of
\mathfrak{u}(d)
are the first two important differences to the spin-independent case discussed in Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Castillo et al. 2023
.
In the following, we will discuss the third main difference, namely that the configurations
\boldsymbol{i}
can have multiplicities larger than one, which is not possible for Slater determinants as in the spin-independent setting of Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Castillo et al. 2023
. The multiplicity
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}
of a configuration
\boldsymbol{i}
is given by (e.g., see the textbook
Pauncz 2012
)

m_{\boldsymbol{i}}=\begin{pmatrix}N_{u}\\
\frac{N_{u}}{2}-S\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}N_{u}\\
\frac{N_{u}}{2}-S-1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(29)

where
N_{u}
denotes the number of unpaired electrons in
\boldsymbol{i}
.
Alternatively, the multiplicity of a configuration can be calculated using Kostant’s multiplicity formula
Kostant 1959
;
Hall 2015
. Moreover, the configuration of the highest weight state always has multiplicity one due to the uniqueness theorem of the highest weight proved by Cartan
Hall 2015
. The other configurations in Figs.
3
and
4
also have multiplicity one. However, configurations with
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}>1
occur when including the configurations of higher excited states in those Hasse diagram. For instance, in the case of
N=9,S=3/2
in Fig.
4
, the configuration
\boldsymbol{i}=(1,1,2,2,3,4,5,6,7)
with
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}=4
will occur if more orbital configurations are included in the Hasse diagram. At the same time, it can be easily verified that the three stability conditions ensure that
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}=1
for
S,N,d
satisfying Eqs. (
26
)-(
28
) for a given fixed rank
r
of the
N
-fermion states
\Gamma
.

The multiplicities of the configurations are important as they have to be taken into account when constructing the so-called lineups
l
as explained in the following. A lineup
l
of length
r
is a sequence of the
r
largest configurations that respects the partial order between them
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Castillo et al. 2023
;
Castillo and Labbé 2024
. The possible lineups are determined by the Hasse diagram as follows: consider first
r=2
, i.e., rank-2 states with fixed spectrum
\boldsymbol{w}
on the
N
-particle level.
As explained in Sec.
III.3
, there are two possible orbital configurations for the second lowest orbital configuration for
r=2
. This leads to
R=2
lineups for
r=2
.
For the examples of Hasse diagrams in Figs.
3
and
4
this leads to two lineups
l_{1},l_{2}
for Fig.
3
, while we have only one lineup
l_{1}
for Fig.
4
. The number of distinct lineups for fixed

is denoted by
R(r)
.
As the multiplicity
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}
of a configuration corresponds to the number of orthogonal configuration states mapping to it, configurations have to be taken into account
m_{\boldsymbol{i}}
-many times.

As a simple example, we consider the setting of

fermions in

orbitals (recall that

) and

. The respective Hasse diagram is shown in Fig.

. The dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
is

but there are only seven configurations as the configuration
\boldsymbol{i}=(1,2,3)
has multiplicity two. Thus, for

, the configuration
\boldsymbol{i}=(1,2,3)
has to be taken into account twice before considering

. This example leads for

to the two lineups

\displaystyle l_{1}\!:(1,1,2)\to(1,1,3)\to(1,2,2)\to(1,2,3)\to(1,2,3)\,,

\displaystyle l_{2}\!:\,(1,1,2)\to(1,2,2)\to(1,1,3)\to(1,2,3)\to(1,2,3)\,.

(30)

Moreover, the vector of eigenvalues
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of
\gamma_{l}
that corresponds to
\boldsymbol{i}=(1,2,3)
is not extremal in the set
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})
,

III.3
Minimal hyperplane representation

In this section, we translate the vertex representation of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
to a minimal hyperplane representation. We use the same techniques that were worked out in detail in Ref.
Castillo et al. 2023
. To keep the work self-contained, we therefore only briefly recap the most important concepts from discrete geometry that are required in Sec.
IV
to derive the relaxed orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability constraints.
For a single generating vertex this is trivial since the linear constraints follow directly from a theorem by Rado
Rado 1952
. However, the number of lineups and, thus, the number of generating vertices grows with
r
which makes the task highly non-trivial.

Let us assume that
N,S,d
satisfy the stability conditions in Eqs. (
26
)-(
28
) for a given
r
.
The first step to arrive at the hyperplane representation of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
is to determine all lineups
l_{i},i=1,\ldots,R(r)
and calculate the corresponding
R(r)
generating vertices as described in Sec.
III.2
. A linear inequality is called valid if it is satisfied by all points in the polytope
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
.
The normal fan
\mathcal{N}(\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}))
of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
is determined from the normal cones of the vertices of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
derived in Sec.
III.2
.
The facets of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
provide all missing normal cones of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
and yield the rays used to calculate the facet-defining inequalities. Due to permutation invariance of
\Sigma_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})
it is further sufficient to study the fundamental fan
\mathcal{N}_{f}(\Sigma_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w}))
, which is given by the intersection of the normal fan with the fundamental chamber
\Phi_{d}
, i.e.
\mathcal{N}_{f}(\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}))=\mathcal{N}(\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}))\cap\Phi_{d}
. The fundamental chamber
\Phi_{d}
is defined as the set

\Phi_{d}\equiv\left\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in(\mathbbm{R}^{d})^{*}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\eta}=\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\downarrow}\right\}

(33)

of linear functionals on the dual space
(\mathbbm{R}^{d})^{*}
.
Furthermore, we introduce the dual fundamental basis
\mathcal{B}_{f}=\{\boldsymbol{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
whose basis vectors are given by

\boldsymbol{f}_{i}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{i}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{j}\,,\quad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,d\,,

(34)

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}
are the elements of the dual elementary basis. The set of linear constraints is expressed as
A\cdot\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\downarrow}\leq B\cdot\boldsymbol{w}
, where
A,B
are coefficient matrices and each row determines one linear constraint that has to be satisfied by any
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
.
For each lineup
l_{k}
we then determine the set of fundamental linear functionals
\boldsymbol{\eta}
uniquely maximised at the corresponding generating vertex
\boldsymbol{v}^{(k)}
and determine from that the rays of
\mathcal{N}_{f}(\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}))
. The resulting rays determine the left hand-side of the linear constraints in our hyperplane representation of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
, i.e., the matrix
A
, and evaluating them on the vertices
\boldsymbol{v}^{(k)},k=1,\ldots,R
yields the right hand-side of the inequalities.
Due to the intersection with the fundamental chamber this procedure might lead to redundant inequalities which are removed afterwards. Thus, the last step is to determine the minimal hyperplane representation of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
by identifying the redundant inequalities. We illustrate these concepts and derive the resulting inequalities for different
r
and
S
and generic
N,d
in Sec.
IV
. This will eventually lead to the sought-after spin-dependent
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble orbital one-body
(N,S,M)
-representability constraints.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K+1}\lambda_{i}^{\downarrow}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{J}\lambda_{i}^{\downarrow}

\displaystyle\leq N\,.

(36)

We are interested in the geometric structure of the permutohedron
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})
. To this end, we define the two vertices

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{(N-2S)/2},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{d-(N-2S)/2})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{(N+2S)/2},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{d-(N-2S)/2})\,.

(37)

and the corresponding permutohedra

P^{(i)}_{N,S}=\mathrm{conv}\left(\left\{\pi(\boldsymbol{p}^{(i)})\,|\,\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\right\}\right)\,.

(38)

Thus,
P^{(1)}_{N,S}
is nothing else than the Pauli simplex for the setting of
N_{\beta}\equiv K=(N-2S)/2
spin-
\beta
electrons, while
P^{(2)}_{N,S}
corresponds to the Pauli simplex for
N_{\alpha}\equiv J=(N+2S)/2
spin-
\alpha
electrons.
Then,
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})
follows as the Minkowski sum of the two permutohedra
P_{N,S}^{(1)}
,
P_{N,S}^{(2)}
according to

\displaystyle\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})

\displaystyle=P_{N,S}^{(1)}+P_{N,S}^{(2)}

(39)

\displaystyle=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\,|\,\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\in P_{N,S}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\in P_{N,S}^{(2)}\right\}\,.

We illustrate this Minkowski sum for
N=d=4,S=1
in Fig.
6
, where
\lambda_{4}=N-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}
is fixed through the normalization condition. In that case,
\boldsymbol{p}^{(1)}=(1,0,0,0)
and
\boldsymbol{p}^{(2)}=(1,1,1,0)
. Therefore,
\Sigma_{4,1}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})
with generating vertex
\boldsymbol{v}=(2,1,1,0)
(right) is nothing else than the Minkowski sum of
P^{(1)}_{4,1}
(left) and
P^{(2)}_{4,1}
(middle).

As
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})
is the sum of two hypersimplices
P^{(1)}_{N,S},P^{(2)}_{N,S}
, it has a richer geometric structure than the Pauli hypersimplex (for spinless fermions), which is the permutohedron of the Hartree-Fock point
\boldsymbol{v}^{\mathrm{HF}}=(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots)
. In fact, the volume of a hypersimplex is well-known since more than
100
years
de Laplace 1886
, while the volume of the sum of hypersimplices is in general more complicated to determine
Postnikov 2009
;
Liu 2016
.

V
Applications

In this section, we discuss three direct applications of the spin-adapted orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
N
-representability constraints derived in the previous sections. They are all concerned with the task of calculating the lowest few energy eigenstates of an interacting
N
-electron system in virtue of the ensemble variational principle due to Gross, Oliveira and Kohn
Gross et al. 1988
(see also Ref.
Ding et al. 2024
): Let
H
be a Hamiltonian with increasingly ordered eigenvalues
E_{1}\leq E_{2}\leq\ldots\leq E_{D}
on a
D
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and denote the set of density matrices on
\mathcal{H}
with spectrum
\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{w}^{\downarrow}
by
\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{w})
. Then,

\displaystyle E_{\boldsymbol{w}}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{D}w_{i}E_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{w})}\mathrm{Tr}\left[H\Gamma\right]

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\Gamma\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{w})}\mathrm{Tr}\left[H\Gamma\right]\,,

i.e., the
\boldsymbol{w}
-averaged energy
E_{\boldsymbol{w}}
can be obtained
variationally
by minimizing the energy expectation value
\mathrm{Tr}\left[H\Gamma\right]
over all density matrices with spectrum
\boldsymbol{w}
. The resulting minimum is not changed if one extends the variational principle to the convex hull
\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{w})
of
\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{w})
, i.e., by including also those density matrices
\Gamma
whose spectrum is majorized by
\boldsymbol{w}
(recall the comment above Eq. (
17
)).

V.1
Functional theory for excited states

The scope of a one-body reduced density matrix functional theory (RDMFT) is characterized by a family of Hamiltonians of interest
Liebert et al. 2023
that take the form
H(h_{l})=h_{l}+W
which we assume to commute with
\boldsymbol{S}^{2},S_{z}
. Here, the interaction
W
between the electrons (or any other spin-
1/2
fermions) is kept fixed, while the orbital one-particle Hamiltonian
h_{l}
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
can be varied
2
2

2

For the sake of simplicity, we use here the same symbol for that Hamiltonian as an operator on
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}
and
\mathcal{H}_{N}
.
.
Moreover, we denote for a fixed choice of
S,M
the increasingly ordered eigenvalues of
H(h_{l})
within
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
by
E_{1}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})\leq E_{2}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})\leq\ldots\leq E_{D}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})
with
D\equiv D_{N}^{(S,M)}
. Applying now the ensemble variational principle (
53
) yields the weighted sum of the lowest
r
eigenenergies within
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
,

\displaystyle E_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}w_{i}E_{i}^{(S,M)}

(54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})}\mathrm{Tr}_{N}\left[H(h_{l})\Gamma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\Gamma\in\,\scriptsize{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 0.90417pt\hbox{\kern-1.59442pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\normalsize^{N}_{S,M}\hskip-1.99168pt(\boldsymbol{w})}\mathrm{Tr}_{N}\left[H(h_{l})\Gamma\right]\,.

Moreover, it follows from the ensemble variational principle that
E_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})
is a concave functional of
h_{l}
.
Accordingly, we obtain a spin-adapted
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble RDMFT directly by applying the ideas in Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023b
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023a
to each spin sector
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{(S,M)}
separately. The universal interaction functional follows from the Legendre-Fenchel transformation of
E_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})
in analogy to Lieb’s convex formulation of DFT
Lieb 1983
;
Helgaker and Teale 2022
according to

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})\equiv\sup_{h_{l}}\left(E_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{(S,M)}(h_{l})-\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}^{(l)}}[h_{l}\gamma_{l}]\right)\,.

(55)

Therefore, the functional
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})
is convex by definition and thus equal to the ensemble constrained search functional
Valone 1980

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})=\min_{\,\scriptsize{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 0.90417pt\hbox{\kern-1.59442pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\normalsize^{N}_{S,M}\hskip-1.99168pt(\boldsymbol{w})\ni\Gamma\mapsto\gamma_{l}}\mathrm{Tr}_{N}[\Gamma W]\,.

(56)

Moreover, following Ref.
Schilling 2018
reveals that
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})
is the lower convex hull,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})=\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{F}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w}))
, of the (typically non-convex) pure constrained search functional
Levy 1979
,

\mathcal{F}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})\equiv\min_{\mathcal{E}^{N}_{S,M}(\boldsymbol{w})\ni\Gamma\mapsto\gamma_{l}}\mathrm{Tr}_{N}[\Gamma W]\,.

(57)

The functionals
\mathcal{F}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})
both depend explicitly on the quantum numbers
S,M
as well as the weight vector
\boldsymbol{w}
. More importantly, their domains are given precisely by the set
\mathcal{L}_{N,S}^{1}(\boldsymbol{w})
and its convex hull
\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{1}_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
, respectively. The latter is nothing else than the set of orbital 1RDMs that are compatible to an
N
-fermion quantum state with well-defined spin quantum numbers
S,M
and spectrum majorized by the vector
\boldsymbol{w}
. With the compact characterization of that domain at hand derived in Secs.
III
,
IV
, the process of deriving more and more accurate functional approximations for
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{F}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{w})
can now commence.

V.2
Lattice density functional theory

The application of the solution to the orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representability problem in functional theories is not limited to RDMFT. In this section, we demonstrate that it also constrains the set of admissible occupation number vectors in the so-called ensemble DFT for excited states (EDFT) based on the ensemble variational principle (
53
)
Oliveira et al. 1988
;
Yang et al. 2017
;
Fromager 2020
;
Loos and Fromager 2020
;
Cernatic et al. 2022
;
Yang 2021
;
Gould et al. 2023
;
Scott et al. 2024
;
Cernatic et al. 2024
when applied to lattice systems with a global
SU(2)
spin symmetry.
For an introduction to lattice DFT, see Refs.
Gunnarsson and Schönhammer 1986
;
Schönhammer et al. 1995
;
Ijäs and Harju 2010
;
Xianlong et al. 2012
;
Saubanère and Pastor 2014
;
Coe 2019
;
Penz and van
Leeuwen 2021
;
Penz and van
Leeuwen 2023
;
Penz and van
Leeuwen 2024
. In DFT, we consider Hamiltonians
H(v)=v+t+W
, which are parameterized by a local external potential
v
while the kinetic energy operator
t
and the interaction
W
are kept fixed.
Then, the natural variable in lattice DFT is the vector
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}
of (site) occupation numbers
\rho_{i}=(\gamma_{l})_{ii}
, which are the conjugate variables to the local external potentials
v_{i}
on the lattice sites
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Through the dual pairing, the expectation value of the local external potential follows as
\langle\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\rho}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}\rho_{i}
.
Excited states can be calculated by means of the ensemble variational principle, as explained in detail in the review article
Cernatic et al. 2022
. Similarly to Eqs. (
55
) and (
56
), the ensemble universal functional in lattice GOK-DFT is defined as

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{G}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\equiv\min_{\,\scriptsize{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 0.90417pt\hbox{\kern-1.59442pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\normalsize^{N}_{S,M}\hskip-1.99168pt(\boldsymbol{w})\ni\Gamma\mapsto\boldsymbol{\rho}}\mathrm{Tr}_{N}[(t+W)\Gamma]\,.

(58)

The domain of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{G}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{\rho})
is given by all those lattice site occupation number vectors
\boldsymbol{\rho}
, which are compatible to an
N
-particle state
\Gamma\in\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{E}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{N}_{S,M}\!(\boldsymbol{w})
and, thus, also to an orbital 1RDM
\gamma_{l}\in\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{L}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{1}_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
. The domain of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{G}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{\rho})
explicitly depends on the total spin quantum number
S
as well as the weight vector
\boldsymbol{w}
, as shown in the following.

To this end, we first recall from Ref.
Liebert et al. 2022
that a vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is an element of the permutation invariant polytope
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
if and only if it is majorized by a convex combination
\boldsymbol{u}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{R}q_{i}\boldsymbol{v}^{(i)}
of the generating vertices
\boldsymbol{v}^{(i)},i\in\{1,\ldots,R\}
, that is

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})\Leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{\lambda}\prec\boldsymbol{u}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{R}q_{i}\boldsymbol{v}^{(i)},\,\,q_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{R}q_{i}=1\,.

(59)

Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is majorized by the vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of eigenvalues of
\gamma_{l}
as a consequence of the Schur-Horn theorem
Schur 1923
;
Horn 1954
. Thus, it follows from the transitivity of the majorization that

\boldsymbol{\rho}\prec\boldsymbol{\lambda}\prec\boldsymbol{u}\,.

(60)

We therefore conclude that the domain of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{G}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{\rho})
is determined precisely by the relaxed orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
N
-representability constraints derived in Secs.
III
,
IV
, yet here applied to
\boldsymbol{\rho}
rather than
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. Besides its
\boldsymbol{w}
-dependence, this also shows that the domain of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.6pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mathcal{G}$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{(S,M)}(\boldsymbol{\rho})
explicitly depends on the total spin quantum number
S
.

VI
Summary and outlook

Motivated by recent advances of reduced density matrix methods we have devised a general solution to the (convex-relaxed) spin-adapted one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
N
-representability problem for the orbital 1RDM
\gamma_{l}
. Specifically, we have shown that the set of admissible
\gamma_{l}
is fully characterized by linear spectral constraints on the natural orbital occupation numbers
\lambda_{i}
, defining a convex polytope
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})\subset[0,2]^{d}
, refining the well-known Pauli exclusion principle
0\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 2
. These
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble exclusion principle constraints are independent of
M
and the number
d
of orbitals, while their dependence on
N,S
is linear, and we thus calculated them for arbitrary spin quantum numbers
S,M
and system sizes including the infinite basis set limit
d\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, a hierarchical structure — with the original Pauli exclusion principle as the first level — has been discovered as a function of the number
r
of non-vanishing entries in
\boldsymbol{w}
: the constraints for the value
r
are given by those for
r-1
complemented by some additional new ones.
It is also worth stressing that unlike Refs.
Schilling and Pittalis 2021
;
Liebert et al. 2022
;
Castillo et al. 2023
, these
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble constraints explicitly account for the spin degree of electrons, which is absolutely essential for most applications of the
N
-representability problem in quantum physics and chemistry.

Furthermore, we provided three practical applications of the orbital one-body
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
N
-representability constraints for reduced density matrix theories in Sec.
V
. First, they are crucial for applying reduced density matrix functional theory for excited states (
\boldsymbol{w}
-RDMFT) to fermionic quantum systems with a global
SU(2)
spin symmetry as, for instance, the non-relativistic electronic structure Hamiltonian. In fact, low-lying excited states with quantum numbers
S,M
can be calculated by applying
\boldsymbol{w}
-RDMFT to each spin sector separately. For this, in virtue of the constrained search formalism we derived formally the universal interaction functional of the orbital 1RDM
\gamma_{l}
. Its domain is described precisely by our spin-dependent
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble exclusion principle constraints. Second, these constraints also define the set of admissible densities in ensemble density functional theory (EDFT) as a direct consequence of the geometry of quantum states and the Schur-Horn theorem
Schur 1923
;
Horn 1954
. Finally, our results serve as a first outer approximation to the set of
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble
(N,S,M)
-representable
p
-RDMs. In that sense, they might be valuable for implementing variational 2RDM approaches
Mazziotti 2004
;
Mazziotti 2005
;
Cancés et al. 2006
;
van Aggelen et al. 2012
;
Donsa et al. 2023
;
Mazziotti 2020
;
Eugene DePrince III 2024
within the
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensemble framework based on the ensemble variational principle
Gross et al. 1988
;
Ding et al. 2024
.

Besides these applications in RDM theories, the distinctive
N
-fermion states that correspond to the vertices of the spectral polytopes
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
constitute the analogues of spin-symmetry restricted Hartree-Fock (HF) states for
\boldsymbol{w}
-ensembles. By minimizing the energy expectation value of a Hamiltonian over the manifold of these spin-adapted
\boldsymbol{w}
-HF states, we can therefore establish a corresponding mean-field theory for targeting excited states variationally. Consequently, for a given correlated
N
-electron density matrix, the distance of its vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of natural orbital occupation numbers to the vertices of
\Sigma_{N,S}(\boldsymbol{w})
serves as an indicator of the accuracy of such a spin-adapted
\boldsymbol{w}
-HF calculation. Moreover, it reflects the degree of correlation present in the quantum state beyond what is captured by the spin-adapted
\boldsymbol{w}
-HF states. This observation may thus lead to a theory of the true electron correlation valid also for mixed states with fixed spin-symmetry since it would quantify electron correlation
beyond
spin- and exchange-symmetry.

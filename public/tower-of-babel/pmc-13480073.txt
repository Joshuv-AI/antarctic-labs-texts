Evaluating the image quality and brain microstructural exploration of mean apparent propagator-MRI–a pilot study
Authors: Qinglei Shi, Yuchen Gu, Diwei Shi, Mengdi Zhou, Huixin Li, Haoran Zhang, Haofeng Li, Jie Hong, Jing Fu, Renzhi Wang, Xiang Wan, Zhaohui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo investigate the impact of positivity constraints and Laplacian regularization on Mean Apparent Propagator (MAP)-MRI image quality and effectiveness in analyzing brain microstructural changes, validated using an Intermittent Exotropia (IXT) cohort.MethodsA total of 31 patients with IXT (16 males, 15 females; mean age 10.94 ± 1.69 years) and 37 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (23 males, 14 females; mean age 11.14 ± 2.06 years) underwent MRI scans. Imaging protocols included simultaneous multi-slice echo planar imaging (SMS-EPI) for q-space sampling and 3D magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient echo (MP-RAGE) sequences. Three MAP-MRI-based reconstruction algorithms were evaluated: MAPL (with Laplacian regularization), CMAP (positivity-constrained), and CMAPL (combining both constraints). Image quality was assessed objectively using wavelet-based signal-to-noise ratio (WSNR), and subjectively by two radiologists using a 5-point scale rating signal uniformity, contrast, noise level, and anatomical completeness. Statistical analyses included one-way ANOVA, Kruskal–Wallis tests, and Cohen’s κ for inter-rater agreement. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using voxel-wise t-tests with Gaussian Random Field (GRF) correction at P = 0.01.ResultsMAPL outperformed CMAP and CMAPL in both objective and subjective image quality, particularly in RTOP, RTAP, MSD, and QIV maps (all P < 0.05). It also provided a more comprehensive visualization of changes in the bilateral dorsal visual pathways, including BA17, BA18, BA19, BA39, BA40, BA8, BA6, and the supplementary motor area (SMA). Moreover, MAPL demonstrated superior sensitivity in detecting alterations in brain regions associated with language, emotion, and decision-making (BA10, BA11, BA22, BA37, and BA47), which may reflect secondary changes induced by visual impairment.ConclusionsMAPL surpasses CMAP and CMAPL in both image quality and the ability to display brain microstructural changes, establishing it as the superior method for exploring brain structural alterations.Clinical trial informationIt was registered on July 18, 2021 (ChiCTR2100048852) at www.chictr.org.cn and commenced on September 1, 2021.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02431-y.Keywords: Mean apparent propagator magnetic resonance imaging, Laplacian regularization, Positivity constraint, Image quality, Intermittent exotropia

Background

Brain microstructural changes are crucial in exploring the pathogenesis of central nervous system (CNS) diseases as they provide insights into the underlying mechanisms of disease progression. By examining the microstructural disturbances, researchers can also better understand the relationship between structural changes and clinical symptoms of CNS disorders, enabling more precise and personalized treatment approaches. However, non-invasive detection of brain microstructures remains a formidable challenge due to the complexity of the actual microenvironment.

Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), through measuring the signal attenuations induced by random diffusive motion of water molecules, can indirectly probe the microstructure of in vivo tissues [1–4]. The dual vector is controlled in MR measurements, encompassing the gyromagnetic ratio , pulse duration , and the diffusion gradient components of amplitude and direction. By simplifying physical models, the mean apparent propagator (MAP) can be obtained from Eq. (1). Recently, the MAP-based model, MAP-MRI, was introduced, offering new quantitative metrics on fiber orientation and microstructure. This model leverages the entire displacement MAP to capture unique features in nervous tissues by quantifying diffusion characteristics under complex nerve fiber distribution. MAP-MRI demonstrates high clinical utility in detailed brain microstructure analysis and surpasses traditional methods like DTI in diagnosing brain disorders such as Parkinson’s [6], glioma [7], temporal lobe epilepsy [8], focal cortical dysplasia [9], and Alzheimer’s [10]. However, recent studies have shown that the performance of MAP-MRI is sensitive to the sparsity of signal acquisitions and noise at higher b values [11]. To improve the accuracy, the strategies of Laplacian regularization [11] and positive constraint [12] have been incorporated into the model fitting of MAP-MRI. However, there is still a lack of systematic investigations to validate the improvement of the above constraint optimizations for estimating MAP-MRI metrics in clinical applications, especially for CMAP and CMAPL algorithms.

IXT is the most common type of exotropia in childhood. However, its unclear pathological mechanisms make accurate diagnosis challenging. Neurological and pathological studies suggest that IXT may result from central nervous system abnormalities [13–16]. Growing evidence indicates that brain microstructural changes play a significant role in the pathogenesis of IXT [17–19]. So, in contrast to previous studies conducted under idealized laboratory conditions using ultra-high b-value diffusion MRI protocols, the present study employed a clinically practical acquisition scheme commonly used in routine settings. We systematically evaluated the influence of positivity constraints and Laplacian regularization on the quality of MAP-MRI parametric maps, using objective metrics wavelet-based signal-to-noise ratio (WSNR) and subjective criteria including signal uniformity, image contrast, noise level, and signal completeness. In addition, we investigated the diagnostic value of different regularization strategies in detecting microstructural abnormalities in patients with intermittent exotropia (IXT). These findings offer methodological insights for leveraging MAP-MRI in the pathophysiological study of IXT and related neurological disorders.

Methods

Participants

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee and Institutional Review Board of Beijing Tongren Hospital Affiliated to Capital Medical University. Written informed consent was obtained from the parents/guardians of all participants. The study included 31 children and adolescent patients with IXT and 37 age-, sex-, handedness-, and education-matched healthy controls (HCs). All participants underwent MRI scans and detailed ophthalmic examinations, including best corrected visual acuity (BCVA). IXT patients were selected based on a diagnosis of basic type IXT, age 9–16 years, a VA ≥ 1.0, and ability to undergo all exams. HCs met criteria 2–4 for IXT patients and had no ocular or brain diseases. Exclusion criteria were other strabismus types, ocular diseases (e.g., amblyopia, glaucoma, optic neuritis), past eye/brain surgery, psychiatric/cardiovascular/cerebral diseases, substance addiction, and MRI contraindications like metal implants or past neurosurgery. Following these criteria, 31 IXT patients and 37 HCs were successfully enrolled (Table 1).

MR image acquisition

MRI data were acquired on a 3T MAGNETOM Prisma scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) using a standard 64-channel head–neck coil. The system was equipped with a maximum gradient strength of 80 mT/m and a slew rate of 200 T/m/s. To ensure consistent image quality, all subjects were positioned supine with their heads immobilized using soft pads within the coil. The positioning crosshair was aligned between the eyebrows, and the imaging direction was parallel to the anterior commissure-posterior commissure line. The scanning range extended from the top of the cerebrum to the lower edge of the cerebellum, encompassing the entire cerebellum and cerebrum. The scanning protocol comprised DWI and anatomical imaging. In DWI, a q-Space sequence of Simultaneous Multi-Slice Echo Planar Imaging (SMS-EPI) with Grid mode was utilized with these parameters: TR = 4300 ms; TE = 81 ms, number of excitations = 1; matrix = 110 × 110; FOV = 240 × 240 mm²; inter-slice and intra-slice acceleration factors = 2; diffusion gradient directions = 128; b-values = 0, 200, 400, 550, 750, 950, 1150, 1500, 1700, 1850, 1900, 2050, 2250, 2450, 2650, and 3000 mm²/s; slice thickness = 2 mm; sequence acquisition time = 9 min 36 s. The duration (δ) and separation (∆) of the diffusion-sensitizing gradient pulses were 20.7 ms and 39.3 ms, respectively, with a bandwidth of 1820 Hz per pixel. The scanning yielded a total of 70 axial slices. To mitigate magnetic sensitivity and motion artifacts, independently acquired b = 0 s/mm² images were obtained using the same imaging parameters but with the opposite phase encoding direction. For anatomical imaging, a 3D magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient echo (MP-RAGE) sequence was implemented with parameters: repetition time (TR) = 2000 ms; echo time (TE) = 2.25 ms; inversion time (TI) = 900 ms; flip angle = 8°; number of excitations = 1; matrix size = 256 × 256; field of view (FOV) = 250 × 250 mm²; and slice thickness = 1 mm; bandwidth = 200 Hz per pixel; sequence acquisition time = 4 min 06 s. The acquisition was performed without inter-slice gaps, yielding a total of 192 sagittal slices. The total acquisition time per participant was approximately 30 min. All imaging sessions were conducted by the same chief technician.

Evaluation of image quality: objective and subjective measures

In assessing the signal-to-noise ratio of the image, we employ the wavelet-based signal-to-noise ratio (WSNR). It quantifies the quality of reconstructed MAP-MRI–derived parametric maps by comparing signal power against noise power, providing an automated and objective approach to evaluating image signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). As a critical indicator of image quality, SNR reflects the contrast between true signal intensity and background noise, exerting a major influence on image clarity and the ability to resolve fine anatomical structures or tissue boundaries. Brain microstructure visualization voxel-wise analysis

The diffusion-weighted data were initially processed to correct for eddy current distortion and head motion. Brain extraction was performed using the FSL Brain Extraction Tool (available at www.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl/). For MAP-MRI reconstruction, we utilized the Python-based 3D/4D+ imaging library, Dipy (https://dipy.org/). To enhance the accuracy of transforming MAP-MRI-based parametric maps into MNI standard space, a registration process was conducted between the T1-MPRAGE image and the b = 0 s/mm² image, which served as the reference. The resulting transformation matrix was then applied to the parametric maps to ensure alignment in the original space. Furthermore, the MAP-MRI parameter mapping was normalized to the MNI standard space using the transformation matrix derived from the T1-MPRAGE image segmentation. To mitigate the impact of noise and enhance statistical efficiency, a smoothing kernel with a full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 6 mm³ was applied to the images. All preprocessing steps were executed using SPM12 (Welcome Department of Imaging Neuroscience, London, UK) in combination with MATLAB R2020a (MathWorks, Inc., Natick, MA).

Statistical analysis

In assessing image quality, the objective metric WSNR, conforming to the normal distribution and homogeneity of variance, was analyzed using an Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) and a Bonferroni method was conducted for post-hoc testing. For subjective scoring data, a Kruskal-Wallis test and pairwise comparisons were complemented between groups, and a κ test was used to evaluate the reproducibility of subjective scores. The statistical significance level was set at P < 0.05. To explore the diagnostic performance of MAPL, CMAP, and CMAPL in IXT, an independent sample t-test were performed using RestPlus 1.2 (http://restfmri.net/forum/RESTplus) [21]. A BrainNet Viewer (http://www.nitrc.org/projects/bnv/) was utilized to display the 3D surface mapping of significantly different brain regions [22]. Statistical significance was determined with a Gaussian Random Field (GRF)-correction at thresholds of 0.01.

Subjective image quality comparison

In the subjective evaluation of image quality among MAP-MRI groups, the agreement between two radiologists was strong, indicated by a kappa value of 0.861 (P < 0.05). For the RTPP and parameters such as NG, NGAx, and NGRad, no significant differences were observed in any assessed aspects. Our subjective systematic evaluation showed that the MAPL algorithm generally produces superior parametric maps compared to the other two algorithms (Fig. 3). Discussion

In this study, using a clinically practical acquisition scheme, we found that the MAPL algorithm exhibits significantly higher WSNR than the other two algorithms. In subjective evaluations, MAPL scored higher in signal uniformity, noise level, and signal completeness for RTOP and RTAP parameters, and in image contrast for MSD and QIV parameters. Our systematic evaluation confirms that MAPL generally surpasses the other algorithms in parametric map quality. The reason why MAPL images have higher quality should be related to the mechanism of the Laplace regularization algorithm, which imposes smoothness over the entire signal space, including the extrapolated signals beyond the measured ones [11]. Moreover, in the objective evaluation of image quality, we employed a wavelet-based signal-to-noise ratio (WSNR) that facilitates the automatic assessment of an image’s signal-to-noise ratio without requiring background noise as a reference. This approach significantly enhances the efficiency of image quality evaluations.

For the performance of MAP-MRI in exploring brain microstructural changes, validated in an IXT cohort [25–30], this study found that the MAPL algorithm offers a comprehensive display of changes in bilateral dorsal visual pathways compared to CMAP and CMAPL. MAPL not only highlights changes in dorsal pathway centers like BA17, BA18, BA19, BA39, BA40, and BA8, but also captures alterations in BA6 and the supplementary motor area (SMA), which are linked to auxiliary visual motor pathways and is very important for the treatment of IXT. According to the literature, the primate extrastriate visual cortex network is dominated by two pathways: one involving the parietal cortex, important for spatial relationships and object motion, and the other involving the temporal cortex, crucial for visual identification of colors, patterns, or objects [31]. IXT patients mainly present with stereoscopic impairment and eye movement disorders, which are closely related to abnormalities in the dorsal visual pathway. Moreover, MAPL outperforms the other algorithms in detecting changes in brain regions related to language, emotion, and decision-making (including BA10, BA11, BA22, BA37, and BA47), which may be associated with secondary visual function impairment—critical symptoms for clinical attention. In summary, the superior image quality and ability in visualizing changes of bilateral dorsal pathways and secondary brain regions gained by MAPL algorithm means its great potential in exploring the pathogenesis of IXT and related disease. We hope this research lays the groundwork for future exploration and highlights the impact of algorithmic choices in neurological studies.

These findings underscore the utility of MAPL for probing brain microstructure under clinically standard DWI acquisition protocols (b ≤ 3000 s/mm²). Even within this routine imaging range, MAP-MRI enables robust quantification of diffusion properties, offering valuable insights into microstructural alterations in the context of complex neural fiber architecture, highlighting its relevance for studies of neurological disorders [6, 7, 12, 32]. The microstructural changes in neurons are closely linked to both physiological and pathological alterations, e.g. demyelination and remyelination, axonal damage, neuroinflammation, neuronal loss and so on, which can provide crucial insights into the underlying physiological and pathological processes affecting neuronal health and function. The MAP-MRI-derived parameters RTOP and RTAP are inversely related to volume V [33], with and the mean cross-sectional area (). MSD and QIV reflect tissue structural complexity and resistance to water diffusion, detecting changes before morphological alterations occur. NG, reflecting the deviation of water diffusion from normal distribution, indicates tissue microstructural changes. These MAP-MRI parameters provide powerful tools for assessing neuronal microstructure and gross tissue structure changes. Techniques like Laplacian regularization enhance accuracy and robustness in reflecting brain microstructure changes [11, 12, 34]. The details of the MAP-MRI derived parameters can be found in Appendix B.

The poor performance of CMAPL may be attributed to several points: First, the simultaneous incorporation of Laplacian regularization and positivity constraints makes the original optimization problem non-convex. The complicated constraint setting will introduce more local optimal solutions, resulting in a more difficult search for the global optimum. Then both the accuracy and robustness of CMAPL become worse. In addition, the current combined strategy is too simple and straightforward, ignoring the individual characteristics of the two constrained optimizations. A more comprehensive combined paradigm is necessary, which needs to achieve the complementarity of the advantages of the two optimizations and avoid their mutual influence. Addressing these challenges typically involves trying various combined strategies and adjusting hyper-parameters based on the validation results. The above studies are beyond the scope of this paper and will be shown in our future work.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, we validated the capabilities of MAPL, CMAP, and CMAPL in exploring brain microstructural changes using a limited cohort of 68 cases, considering the challenges in enrolling pediatric patients. Larger-scale studies are needed to more definitively assess the strengths and weaknesses of these algorithms. Additionally, this study was conducted under default hyperparameter settings, leaving the potential for improved diagnostic performance through hyperparameter optimization, particularly for the CMAPL algorithm, unexplored. Finally, our focus was primarily on the impact of these algorithms on the quality of MAP-MRI parameter maps and their potential in exploring neuro disease pathogenesis. Due to space constraints, we did not conduct an in-depth study of IXT pathogenesis. Future research will leverage MAPL and other technologies for a comprehensive investigation of IXT.

Conclusion

With a clinically standard DWI acquisition protocols (b ≤ 3000 s/mm²), the MAPL algorithm surpasses CMAP and CMAPL in both objective and subjective image quality. It more comprehensively displays changes in bilateral dorsal visual pathways, particularly in the visual motor center and SMA, and excels in detecting alterations in regions related to language, emotion, and decision-making. All these studies reveal the potential of the MAPL algorithm in showing brain microstructural changes.

Glossary

which means Mean Apparent Propagator Magnetic Resonance Imaging, is an advanced DWI algorithm that extends DTI to create a precise propagator (MAP) in each voxel. By capturing non-Gaussian diffusion, it provides new quantitative parameters, revealing unique features of nervous tissue microstructure.

which means Mean Apparent Propagator-MRI with Laplacian regularization. To reduce the sensitivity of MAP-MRI to the sparsity of signal acquisitions and noise at higher b values, a Laplacian regularization function was employed into the loss function when obtaining the coefficients c.

which means Mean Apparent Propagator-MRI with positivity constrained. To reduce the sensitivity of MAP-MRI to the sparsity of signal acquisitions and noise at higher b values, an additional constraint to enforce positive definiteness is employed in the loss function when obtaining the coefficients c.

which means Mean Apparent Propagator-MRI with positivity constrained and Laplacian regularization in the loss function when obtaining the coefficients c.

which means wavelet-based signal-to-noise ratio (WSNR) that is used as a quantitative metrics in evaluating SNR of the reconstructed parametric maps. It is calculated by comparing signal power against noise power after wavelet transform.

which means Intermittent Exotropia is a childhood strabismus where one eye intermittently drifts outward while the other remains focused. As the most common exotropia type, diagnosing IXT is challenging due to unclear mechanisms, potentially linked to central nervous system abnormalities. This study uses IXT data to validate the effectiveness of several algorithms.

which means Central Nervous System, comprising the brain and spinal cord, processes and coordinates body information, interprets sensory data, controls motor functions, and regulates vital processes like breathing, heart rate, and consciousness. It is the control center for thoughts, emotions, and movement.

which means Brodmann Areas, are regions of the cerebral cortex based on the Brodmann Brain Template, which is a standardized brain map based on Korbinian Brodmann’s original segmentation of the cerebral cortex into 52 distinct areas.

Functional central limit theorem for subgraph counts in a dynamic random connection model
Rajat Subhra Hazra, Nikolai Kriukov, Michel Mandjes, Moritz Otto

Abstract
. We prove a functional central limit theorem for subgraph counts in a dynamic version of the random connection model. To establish tightness, we develop a dynamic extension of the cumulant method. 1.
Introduction

The
random connection model
(RCM) is a fundamental stochastic geometric model used to describe spatial networks in which nodes are connected with a probability that depends on their mutual distance. More formally, given a homogeneous Poisson point process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, two points
x
and
y
are connected independently with probability that depends on the distance between
x
and
y
. The RCM generalizes the classical
random geometric graph
(or Gilbert graph) by allowing soft (probabilistic) connections instead of deterministic ones. It has been widely studied in the context of percolation theory, continuum random graphs, and wireless networks (see e.g., Meester & Roy
[
12
]
, Penrose
[
13
]
, and Roy
[
15
]
).

In the study of the RCM, an important branch of the literature concerns
subgraph counts
where one investigates the number of occurrences of a fixed finite graph
H
(such as a triangle or a star) as an induced or embedded subgraph of the RCM. These counts not only capture local connectivity patterns but also play a key role in understanding the higher-order structure and limiting behavior of the model. Subgraph counts have been studied using tools from Poisson point process theory, cumulants, Stein’s method, and U-statistics (see, e.g., Penrose
[
14
]
, Schulte & Thäle
[
16
]
, Liu & Privault
[
11
]
and Heerten
et al.

[
7
]
). In particular, asymptotic normality for these counts provide insights into scaling limits.

A major trend in the random graph literature is the increasing focus on
dynamically evolving networks
, in which edges and/or vertices change stochastically over time to better reflect the behavior of real-world systems such as communication, social, or biological networks. These so-called
temporal random graphs
, as described by e.g. Holme & Saramäki
[
8
]
, capture both the structural and dynamic complexity of such systems and give rise to new mathematical challenges and insights. This shift reflects a growing interest in understanding not only static connectivity properties, but also how network behavior evolves over time. In principle, any property studied in the static setting can be extended to the dynamic context. For instance, the sample-path large deviation principle developed in Braunsteins
et al.

[
2
]
generalizes the foundational result for the static Erdős–Rényi graph by Chatterjee & Varadhan
[
3
]
, while the maximum eigenvalue process studied by Hazra
et al.

[
5
]
generalizes the static result from Erdős
et al.

[
4
]
.

In this paper, we consider a dynamic variant of the RCM, in which point locations and (potential) edges are sampled once and remain fixed, but where each point alternates between being active and inactive over time. Only edges between pairs of simultaneously active points are included into the graph. This setting naturally gives rise to a multivariate subgraph count process, tracking the number of occurrences of various subgraphs (such as edges, triangles, or stars) over time. The main result of the paper is a functional central limit theorem (CLT) for a centered and normalized version of this multivariate subgraph count process. As an application of this result, we present a functional CLT for the clustering coefficient process in the dynamic RCM.

In Section
2
we formally introduce our model and state our main result. Our proofs build on the recently developed cumulant method, as introduced in a series of recent works (see e.g. Last
et al.

[
10
]
, Schulte & Thäle
[
17
,
16
]
). This method establishes a connection between Poisson U-statistics and a specific class of partitions, allowing their cumulants to be expressed in terms of certain tensor products (see Section
3
). After having determined the candidate mean and covariances to feature in the functional CLT (Section
4
), the fundamental challenge is to extend the use of cumulants beyond establishing finite-dimensional convergence (Section
5
) to the functional setting. We do so by effectively applying them to prove tightness (Section
6
), thereby illustrating the broad applicability of this approach.

2.
Model and main results

2.1.
Model description

Denote

W:=[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{d},

and let
\eta_{n}
be a Poisson point process on

W\times\mathbb{D}([0,T],\{0,1\}),
with intensity measure
n\,\mathrm{d}x\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Here
T>0
is the time horizon, and
\mathbb{Q}
denotes the law of a two-state Markov jump process on
\{0,1\}
with exponential waiting times, which jumps from 0 to 1 at rate
\mu
and from 1 to 0 at rate
\lambda
, started at its stationary distribution. In order to avoid potential boundary effects, throughout this paper we work with the torus metric on
W
, i.e., we identify opposite faces of
W
(i.e., the space ‘wraps around’ like a torus). We write
P=(X,(A(t))_{t\in[0,T]})
for a canonical element in
\eta_{n}
, where
X\in W
is the location of
P
and
A(t)
is its status process at time
t\in[0,T]
.

Our model is a random connection model (RCM) in which the underlying graph’s vertices alternate between being present and absent as follows. Let
\mu>0,\,\lambda>0
be model parameters. At time
t=0
, each of the points in
\eta_{n}
is
active
independently with probability
\varrho:={\mu}/{(\mu+\lambda)}
, and
inactive
else. As time goes on, the points in
\eta_{n}
independently alternate between being active and inactive: inactive (active) points become active (inactive) after an exponentially distributed time with rate
\mu
(
\lambda
, respectively). Note that the expected number of active Poisson points is
\varrho\,n
for all
t\geq 0
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset W
be locally finite. In the RCM on
\mathcal{X}
, between any pair of distinct points
X_{i},X_{j}\in\mathcal{X}
we place a (potential) edge independently with probability
\varphi_{n}(X_{i}-X_{j})
, where

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}({x}):=\varphi\bigg(\frac{\|{x}\|^{d}}{\nu_{n}}\bigg).

Here,
(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a positive real-valued sequence with
\nu_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, and
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
(typically referred to as the
profile function
) is assumed to be non-increasing and to satisfy the normalization
\int\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=1
.

The edges are sampled once and for all, but they appear in the RCM only when both adjacent nodes are simultaneously active (see Figure
1
). The resulting dynamic random graph process we denote by
\mathrm{RCM}(\mathcal{X},\varphi_{n})
.

Figure 1
.
The dynamic RCM at different times. Nodes alternate between active (full dot) and inactive (empty dot). Potential edges (dotted line) appear (solid line) when both adjacent nodes are active.

We proceed by defining the multivariate subgraph process that we focus on in this paper.
To this end, we let
G_{i}
be a given connected graph on vertices
1,\dots,q_{i}
and edge set
E(G_{i})
, for
i=1,\dots,m
, and we let
a_{i}:=|{\rm Aut}(G_{i})|
be the cardinality of the automorphism group of
G_{i}
. Then we define
\Gamma_{n,i}(t)
as the number of active subgraphs isomorphic to
G_{i}
in the RCM on (the projection of
\eta_{n}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), i.e., with
{\bm{P}}_{q_{i}}:=(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})
and
{\bm{X}}_{q_{i}}:=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{q_{i}}\}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,i}(t):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{i}}\sum_{{\bm{P}}_{q_{i}}\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{i}}}\mathbf{1}\{\forall(k,\ell)\in E(G_{i}):X_{k}\leftrightarrow X_{\ell}\in\textrm{RCM}({\bm{X}}_{q_{i}},\varphi_{n})\}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{q_{i}}A_{k}(t)

where
P_{k}=(X_{k},(A_{k}(t))_{t})
for
k\in[q_{i}]
and
\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{i}}
is the collection of all
q_{i}
-tuples of pairwise distinct elements in
\eta_{n}
; the corresponding
m
-dimensional process is denoted by
\bm{\Gamma}_{n}(\cdot)=\bigl(\Gamma_{n,1}(\cdot),\ldots,\Gamma_{n,m}(\cdot)\bigr)
.

2.2.
Main result

The goal of this paper is to establish a functional CLT for the
m
-dimensional process
\bm{\Gamma}_{n}(\cdot)
,
defined on
[0,T]
for a given time horizon
T>0
.
To state our result in a compact manner, we introduce some notation.
Let
\tt G_{i,j}^{\rm+}
be the collection of all pairs of graphs
{\bm{H}}\equiv(H_{1},H_{2})
with
H_{1}
being a graph on nodes
1,\dots,q_{i}
isomorphic to
G_{i}
and
H_{2}
a graph on nodes
1,q_{i}+1,\dots,q_{i}+q_{j}-1
isomorphic to
G_{j}
. Likewise,
\tt G_{i,j}^{\rm-}
is the collection of all graph pairs
{\bm{H}}\equiv(H_{1},H_{2})
where
H_{1}
is a graph on nodes
1,\dots,q_{i}
isomorphic to
G_{i}
and
H_{2}
is a graph on nodes
1,\dots,q_{j}
isomorphic to
G_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{F}}_{ij}^{\rm+}:=\sum_{{\bm{H}}\in{\tt G}_{ij}^{\rm+}}\frac{F({\bm{H}})}{(q_{i}-1)!(q_{j}-1)!},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{ij}^{\rm-}:=\sum_{{\bm{H}}\in{\tt G}_{ij}^{\rm-}}\frac{F({\bm{H}})}{(q_{i}\wedge q_{j})!}.

Moreover,

(2)

\displaystyle F({\bm{H}}):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{q-1}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E(H_{1})\cup E(H_{2})}\varphi(\|y_{u}-y_{v}\|^{d})\,\mathrm{d}(y_{2},\dots,y_{q}),

where
y_{1}:=0
and
q:=|V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})|
. Denote for simplicity
F(H):=F(H,H)
.

We now specify what conditions are imposed on
\nu_{n}
. In the first place,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{q_{i}}\nu_{n}^{q_{i}-1}=\infty\quad\text{for all }i\in[m].

We distinguish between the
dense regime
and the
sparse regime
, defined via

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}:=\{\nu_{n}:n\nu_{n}\to\infty\},\quad{\mathscr{S}}:=\{\nu_{n}:n\nu_{n}\to 0\},

respectively.
For instance, picking
\nu_{n}:=n^{\gamma}
, condition (
3
) yields that
-q_{i}/(q_{i}-1)<\gamma<0
for all
i\in[m]
. In addition,
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}}
iff
\gamma>-1
, whereas
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}
iff
\gamma<-1.

Define, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\in[m]
and
t\in[0,T]
, the centered and normalized version of
\Gamma_{n,i}(\cdot)
: with
\psi_{n,i}:=\varrho^{q_{i}}n^{q_{i}-1/2}\nu_{n}^{q_{i}-1}
if
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}}
and
\smash{\psi_{n,i}:=\varrho^{q_{i}}\sqrt{n^{q_{i}}\nu_{n}^{q_{i}-1}}}
if
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}
,

(4)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\star}_{n,i}(t):=\frac{\Gamma_{n,i}(t)-\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,i}(t)]}{\psi_{n,i}}.

In addition, with

Z(t):=1+({\lambda}/{\mu})\,e^{-(\lambda+\mu){t}}
, let
\bm{\Gamma}(\cdot)
be a centered Gaussian process with covariance matrix
\Sigma(s,t)=(\Sigma_{i,j}(s,t))_{i,j\in[m]}
for
s,t\in[0,T]
, given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i,j}(s,t)

\displaystyle={\rm Cov}(\Gamma_{i}(s),\Gamma_{j}(t))={\rm Cov}(\Gamma_{i}(t),\Gamma_{j}(s))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}Z(|t-s|)\,{\mathbb{F}}_{ij}^{\rm+},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}},\\[4.0pt]
\left(Z(|t-s|)\right)^{q_{i}}\,{\bm{1}}\{q_{i}=q_{j}\}\,{\mathbb{F}}_{ij}^{\rm-},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1
.

If
\nu_{n}
satisfies (
3
), then
\bm{\Gamma}_{n}^{*}(\cdot)\to\bm{\Gamma}(\cdot)
as
n\to\infty
(in distribution in
\mathbb{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
).

Remark 2
.

In case
\nu_{n}\in\mathscr{D}
we observe that we can write

\mathbb{F}^{+}_{i,j}=(q_{i}F(G_{i})/a_{i})\,(q_{j}F(G_{j})/a_{j}),

implying that the components of
{\bm{\Gamma}}(\cdot)
are perfectly correlated. Indeed,
{\bm{\Gamma}}(\cdot)
has the same distribution as
{\bm{\Gamma}}^{\prime}(\cdot)
defined for each
i=1,\ldots,m
as
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}(\cdot)=q_{i}F(G_{i})\xi(\cdot)/a_{i}
, where
\xi(\cdot)
is centered Gaussian process with covariance function
Z(\left\lvert s-t\right\rvert)
.

The following corollary presents an application of Theorem
1
. Recall that the clustering coefficient is defined as the number of triangles divided by the number of wedges, sometimes multiplied by 3; cf.
[
18
, Eqn. (1.5.4)]
. We study a more general ‘subgraph ratio process’:

\displaystyle C_{n,G_{1},G_{2}}(t):=\frac{a_{1}\,\Gamma_{n,1}(t)}{a_{2}\,{\Gamma_{n,2}(t)}}

for connected graphs
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
such that
\mathcal{V}(G_{1})=\mathcal{V}(G_{2})=q
and
G_{2}\subset G_{1}
. Let
C_{G_{1},G_{2}}(\cdot)
denote a centered Gaussian process with covariance function

\displaystyle\Sigma^{C}(s,t):={\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{F^{2}(G_{2})}\Sigma_{11}(s,t)-\frac{2a_{1}a_{2}F(G_{1})}{F^{3}(G_{2})}\Sigma_{12}(s,t)+\frac{a_{2}^{2}F^{2}(G_{1})}{F^{4}(G_{2})}\Sigma_{22}(s,t).}

In addition, we define by
C^{\star}_{n,G_{1},G_{2}}(\cdot)
the centered and scaled version of the subgraph ratio process: for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle C^{\star}_{n,G_{1},G_{2}}(t):=\left(C_{n,G_{1},G_{2}}-\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,1}(t)]}{{\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,2}(t)]}}\right)\zeta_{n},\quad\quad\zeta_{n}:=\begin{cases}\sqrt{n},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}},\\
\sqrt{n^{q}\nu_{n}^{q-1}},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}.\end{cases}

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
, in combination with the Lemmas
5
and
7
that we establish later on, we have the following result.

Proposition 3

(Subgraph ratio process)
.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be two connected graphs, such that
\mathcal{V}(G_{1})=\mathcal{V}(G_{2})=q
and
G_{1}\subset G_{2}
. Then, in distribution in
\mathbb{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we have
C^{\star}_{n,G_{1},G_{2}}(\cdot)\to C_{G_{1},G_{2}}(\cdot)
as
n\to\infty
. In particular, if
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}}
, then
\Sigma^{C}(s,t)=0
.

Example 4

(Clustering coefficient process)
.

Consider a particular example of the clustering coefficient for
G_{1}
being a triangle and
G_{2}
being a wedge.
Denoting

\displaystyle\kappa_{d}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\|{y}\|^{d})\,\text{\rm d}y,\quad\tau_{d}:=\int_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)^{2}}\,\varphi(\|{y_{1}}\|^{d})\,\varphi(\|{y_{2}}\|^{d})\,\varphi(\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{d})\,\text{\rm d}(y_{1},y_{2}),

we have that
q=3
,
a_{1}=6
,
a_{2}=2
. With the compact notation
{\mathsf{f}}:=F({\bm{H}})
,

\displaystyle F(G_{1})

\displaystyle=\,\kappa_{d}^{2},\quad F(G_{2})=\tau_{d},\quad\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{11}\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{11}\right\rvert=9,\quad\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{12}\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{12}\right\rvert=3,\quad\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{22}\right\rvert=\left\lvert{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{22}\right\rvert=1,

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}=\begin{cases}\kappa_{d}^{2}\tau_{d}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{12}\\
\tau_{d}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{12}\end{cases}\quad\mathsf{f}=\begin{cases}\tau_{d}^{2}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{22}\\
\tau_{d}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{22}\end{cases}\quad\mathsf{f}=\begin{cases}\kappa_{d}^{4}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm+}_{11}\\
\kappa_{d}^{2}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{11},~H_{1}=H_{2}\\
\tau_{d}&\text{for }\bm{H}\in{\tt G}^{\rm-}_{11},~H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{cases}

so that

{\mathbb{F}}_{11}^{\rm+}=\frac{9\kappa_{d}^{4}}{4},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{11}^{\rm-}=\frac{3\kappa_{d}^{2}+6\tau_{d}}{6},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{12}^{\rm+}=\frac{3\kappa_{d}^{2}\tau_{d}}{4},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{12}^{\rm-}=\frac{3\tau_{d}}{6},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{22}^{\rm+}=\frac{\tau_{d}^{2}}{4},\quad{\mathbb{F}}_{22}^{\rm-}=\frac{\tau_{d}}{6}.

After elementary computations we find for
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}}
that
\Sigma^{C}(s,t)=0
, and for
\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}
that

\displaystyle\Sigma^{C}(s,t)

\displaystyle=9\left(Z(\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert)\right)^{3}\,\Bigl(\frac{36}{\tau_{d}}-\frac{90\kappa^{2}_{d}}{\tau^{2}_{d}}+\frac{54\kappa^{4}_{d}}{\tau^{3}_{d}}\Bigr).

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Cumulants

We recall that, with
\mathrm{\bf i}
the imaginary unit, we define the
cumulants
by

\text{cum}{(X_{1},\,\dots,\,X_{m})}:=(-\mathrm{\bf i})^{m}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{m}}\log\phi_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})\mid_{t_{1}=\ldots=t_{m}=0},

where
\phi_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
is the characteristic function of the random vector
(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
:

\phi_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{m}):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\mathrm{\bf i}\sum_{i\leq m}t_{i}X_{i}\Big)\Big].

Moreover, we denote the
m
-th order cumulant of a random variable
X
by
\text{cum}_{m}(X)
and note that
\text{cum}(X,\dots,X)=\text{cum}_{m}(X)
, where the left-hand side takes an
m
-tuple as its input.

3.2.
Partitions and diagram formulae

Partitions play a central role in our application of the method of cumulants. To this end, in the present subsection we reproduce some material from
[
16
, Section 2.4]
(see also
[
9
, Chapter 12.2]
).

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
. We define
N_{0}:=0
,
\smash{N_{\ell}{:=}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}}
,
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and
N:=N_{m}
, and put
J_{\ell}:=\{N_{\ell-1}+1,\ldots,N_{\ell}\}
,
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
A partition
\sigma
of
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
is a collection
\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}
of

1\leq k\leq N
pairwise disjoint non-empty sets, called
blocks
, such that
B_{1}\cup\ldots\cup B_{k}=[N]
. The number
k
of blocks of
\sigma
is denoted by
|\sigma|
. By
\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
we denote the set of all partitions
\sigma
such that
|B\cap J_{\ell}|\leq 1
for all
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
B\in\sigma
. For a graphical representation of partitions, see
[
16
, Figure 1]
.

Every partition
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
induces a
partition

\sigma^{*}
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
in the following way:
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
are in the same block of
\sigma^{*}
whenever there is a block
B\in\sigma
such that
|B\cap J_{i}|=1
and
|B\cap J_{j}|=1
. Let
\smash{\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})}
be the set of all partitions
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
such that
|\sigma^{*}|=1
. By
\Pi_{\geq 2}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
and

\smash{\widetilde{\Pi}_{\geq 2}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})}
we denote the sets of all
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
and of all
\sigma\in\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
such that
|B|\geq 2
for all
B\in\sigma
. Finally, we introduce the set
\overline{\Pi}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
of all partitions
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
such that for each
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
there exists a block
B\in\sigma
with
|B|\geq 2
and
B\cap J_{\ell}\neq\varnothing
. In other words, in each row in the graphical representation of
\sigma
there exists at least one element, which belongs to some block
B\in\sigma
with
|B|\geq 2
. In case that
q_{1}=\ldots=q_{m}={q}
we simply write
\Pi^{m}(q)
,
\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}(q)
,
\widetilde{\Pi}^{m}_{\geq 2}(q)
and
\overline{\Pi}^{m}(q)
instead of
\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
,
\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
,
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\geq 2}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
and
\overline{\Pi}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
, respectively.

For functions
f^{(\ell)}:\mathbb{X}^{q_{\ell}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we define their
tensor product

\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)}:\mathbb{X}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}):=\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)}(x_{N_{\ell-1}+1},\ldots,x_{N_{\ell}})\,.

For
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})
the function
(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}:\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}\to\mathbb{R}
is obtained by replacing in
(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})
all variables that belong to the same block of
\sigma
by a new common variable. Note that this way
(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}
is only defined up to permutations of its arguments. Since in what follows we always integrate with respect to all arguments of
(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}
, this does not cause any problems.

The following diagram formulae from
[
16
]
will prove useful in our arguments. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\tilde{m}
with
\tilde{m}\geq m
be even and let
f^{(\ell)}\in L_{s}^{1}(\mu^{q_{\ell}})
with
q_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell\in[\tilde{m}]
, be such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}|(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{\widetilde{m}}f^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{|\sigma|}<\infty,\qquad

\displaystyle\sigma\in\overline{\Pi}_{\geq 2}^{\tilde{m}}(q_{i}),\quad i\in[m],

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}|(\otimes_{\ell=1}^{m}f^{(\ell)})_{\sigma}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{|\sigma|}<\infty,\qquad

\displaystyle\sigma\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\geq 2}(q_{1},\dots,q_{m}).

Then,
[
16
, Theorem 3.7]
gives for
S_{\ell}:=\sum_{(p_{1},\dots,p_{q_{\ell}})\in\eta_{\neq}^{q_{\ell}}}f^{(\ell)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{q_{\ell}})
,
\ell\in[m]
,

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}(S_{\ell}-\mathbb{E}S_{\ell})\Big]

In this section we subsequently consider the means and covariances pertaining to

Lemma 7
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\in[m]
and
t\in[0,T]
with

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Gamma_{n,i}(t)\bigr]=\frac{F_{n}(G_{i})(\varrho n)^{q_{i}}}{a_{i}}.

Proof.

Let
f_{n,t}^{(i)}
be as in (
12

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,i}(t)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{q_{i}}}f_{n,t}^{(i)}(p_{1},\dots,p_{q_{i}})\mu_{n}(\mathrm{d}(p_{1},\dots,p_{q_{i}}))

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{q_{i}}}{q_{i}!}\prod_{\ell\in[q_{i}]}\mathbb{E}[A_{\ell}(t)]\cdot\sum_{H\in{\bm{G}}_{G_{i}}([q_{i}])}\int_{W^{q_{i}}}\prod_{(k,\ell)\in E(H)}\varphi_{n}(x_{\ell}-x_{k})\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{q_{i}}).

∎

Lemma 8
.

For any
s,t\in[0,T]
, and
i,j\in[m]
, with
F({\bm{H}})
as defined in (
2
),

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}(\Gamma_{n,i}(t),\Gamma_{n,j}(s))\sim\sum_{m=1}^{q_{i}\wedge q_{j}}\sum_{H_{1},H_{2}}\frac{n^{q_{i}+q_{j}-m}\varrho^{q_{i}+q_{j}}Z(|t-s|)^{m}}{m!(q_{i}-m)!(q_{j}-m)!}\nu_{n}^{q_{i}+q_{j}-m-1}F({\bm{H}}),

where the sum is over all graphs
H_{1}
on nodes
1,\dots,q_{i}
that are isomorphic to
G_{i}
and all graphs
H_{2}
on nodes
1,\dots,m,q_{i}+1,\dots,q_{i}+q_{j}-m
that are isomorphic to
G_{j}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{n,t}^{(i)},i\in[m],
be as in (
12
). We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,i}(t)\Gamma_{n,j}(s)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{i}}}\sum_{(P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{q_{j}})\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{j}}}f_{n,t}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})f_{n,s}^{(j)}(P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{q_{j}})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{m=0}^{q_{i}\wedge q_{j}}\sum_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{i}}}\sum_{(P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{q_{j}})\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{j}}}f_{n,t}^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})f_{n,s}^{(j)}(P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{q_{j}})

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\quad\times\mathbf{1}\{|\{P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}}\}\cap\{P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{q_{j}}\}|=m\}\Big].

Note that there are
\binom{q_{i}}{m}
possibilities to place the
m
common nodes among
p_{1},\dots,p_{q_{i}}
and
\binom{q_{j}}{m}
possibilities to place them among
p^{\prime}_{1},\dots,p^{\prime}_{q_{j}}
. Since there are
m!
permutations of the
m
common nodes, the above is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{q_{i}\wedge q_{j}}m!\binom{q_{i}}{m}\binom{q_{j}}{m}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{{\bm{P}}_{q_{i}}\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{i}}}\sum_{{\bm{P}}^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m}\in(\eta_{n}\setminus\{P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}}\})_{\neq}^{q_{j}-m}}f_{n,t}^{(i)}(\bm{P}_{q_{i}})f_{n,s}^{(j)}(\bm{P}_{m},\bm{P}^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m})\Big]

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{m=0}^{q_{i}\wedge q_{j}}m!\binom{q_{i}}{m}\binom{q_{j}}{m}\iint\mathbb{E}\Big[f_{n,t}^{(i)}(\bm{p}_{q_{i}})f_{n,s}^{(j)}(\bm{p}_{m},\bm{p}^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m})\Big]\mu_{n}^{q_{j}-m}(\mathrm{d}{\bm{p}}^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m})\mu_{n}^{q_{i}}(\mathrm{d}{\bm{p}}_{q_{i}}),

where
{\bm{p}}_{m}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
and
{\bm{p}}^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m}=(p^{\prime}_{1},\dots,p^{\prime}_{q_{j}-m})
(and analogously for capital and bold letters). For each

, the above integral is given by

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{n^{q_{i}+q_{j}-m}\varrho^{q_{i}+q_{j}}Z(|t-s|)^{m}}{q_{i}!q_{j}!}\sum_{H_{1},H_{2}}\int_{W^{q_{i}+q_{j}-m}}\prod_{(k,\ell)\in E(H_{1})\cup E(H_{2})}\varphi_{n}(x_{k}-x_{\ell})\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{q_{i}+q_{j}-m}),

where the sum is over all graphs
H_{1}
on nodes
1,\dots,q_{i}
that are isomorphic to
G_{i}
and all graphs
H_{2}
on nodes

.

Note that the contribution from

. This gives for the covariance of

5.
Finite-dimensional convergence

Proposition 9
.

Fix
0\leq t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}\leq T
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
(\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(t_{1}),\dots,\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(t_{k}))

converges to a Gaussian vector as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

By the Cramér-Wold device, it suffices to show that, for any vectors
\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, the random variable
\smash{S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\langle\bm{b}_{i},\bm{\Gamma}_{n}^{\star}(t_{i})\rangle}
converges to a normally distributed random variable. Our idea is to write
S_{n}
as a sum of Poisson
U
-statistics and to apply the diagram formulae from Section
3
to show that the cumulants
\mathsf{cum}_{M}(S_{n}),\,M\geq 3
, converge to 0 as
n\to\infty
. We have, with
\bm{b}_{i}=(b_{i1},\dots,b_{im}),\,i\in[h],

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\langle\bm{b}_{i},\bm{\Gamma}_{n}^{\star}(t_{i})\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{b_{i,j}}{\psi_{n,j}}(\Gamma_{n,j}(t_{i})-\mathbb{E}\Gamma_{n,j}(t_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\psi_{n,j}}\sum_{i=1}^{h}b_{i,j}(\Gamma_{n,j}(t_{i})-\mathbb{E}\Gamma_{n,j}(t_{i})).

For any
j\in[m]
, we let

S_{n}^{(j)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{h}b_{i,j}\Gamma_{n,j}(t_{i}),

and note that, by the multilinearity of the cumulant,

(15)

\displaystyle\text{cum}_{M}(S_{n})=\text{cum}(S_{n},\dots,S_{n})=\sum_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}}\frac{1}{\psi_{n,j_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n,j_{M}}}\text{cum}(S_{n}^{(j_{1})},\dots,S_{n}^{(j_{M})}).

For
P_{k}=(X_{k},A_{k}(\cdot),(T_{k,\ell})_{\ell\geq 1})\in W\times\mathbb{D}([0,T],\{0,1\})\times[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}},\,k\in[q_{j}]
, let

g_{n}^{(j)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{j}}):=\frac{1}{q_{j}!}\sum_{H\in\mathcal{G}_{G_{j}}([q_{j}])}\prod_{(k,\ell)\in E(H)}\mathbf{1}\{X_{k}\leftrightarrow X_{\ell}\text{ in }\textrm{RCM}({\bm{X}}_{q_{j}},\varphi_{n})\}\sum_{i=1}^{h}b_{ij}\prod_{\ell=1}^{q_{j}}A_{\ell}(t_{i}),

and note that

S_{n}^{(j)}=\sum_{(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{j}})\in\eta_{n,\neq}^{q_{j}}}g_{n}^{(j)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{j}}),\quad j=1,\dots,m.

By (
6
), for all
(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}
,

(16)

\displaystyle\text{cum}(S_{n}^{(j_{1})},\dots,S_{n}^{(j_{M})})=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{j_{1}},\dots,q_{j_{M}})}\int_{\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}}(\otimes_{s=1}^{M}g_{n}^{(j_{s})})_{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{|\sigma|}.

Before estimating (
16
), we explain how to define an auxiliary graph on
\{1,\ldots,|\sigma|\}
with edge set
E_{\sigma}
based on a
\sigma\in\Pi(q_{j_{1}},\ldots,q_{j_{M}})
: identify the block
B_{k}\in\sigma
with node
k=1,\dots,|\sigma|
and put an edge
(k,\ell)\in E_{\sigma}
, if there are
k_{i},k_{j}\in[q_{j_{s}}]
for some
s\in[M]
, such that
N_{s-1}+i_{k}\in B_{k}
,
N_{s-1}+i_{\ell}\in B_{\ell}
and there is an edge between
i_{k}
and
i_{\ell}
in
G_{j_{s}}
. For instance, let
m=M=2
,
j_{1}=1
,
j_{2}=2
and
G_{1}=G_{2}
be the graph on vertices
\{1,2,3\}
with edges
(1,2)
and
(2,3)
, let
\sigma=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{5}\}
with
B_{1}=\{1,4\}
,
B_{2}=\{2\}
,
B_{3}=\{3\}
,
B_{4}=\{5\}
,
B_{5}=\{6\}
. Then
V=\{1,\dots,5\}
and
E_{\sigma}=\{(1,2),(2,3),(1,4),(4,5)\}
.
Note, moreover, that in this case

(f^{(1)}\otimes f^{(2)})_{\sigma}(p_{1},\dots,p_{5})=f^{(1)}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})f^{(2)}(p_{1},p_{4},p_{5}),\quad p_{1},\dots,p_{5}\in\mathbb{X}.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{s}:=\{k\in[|\sigma|]:\,J_{s}\cap B_{k}\neq\emptyset\},\,s\in[M]
. In our context, the introduction of the auxiliary graph described above yields that for each
(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}
and
\sigma\in\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{j_{1}},\dots,q_{j_{M}})
, the integral in (
16
) is given by

(17)

\displaystyle n^{|\sigma|}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{s=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{h}b_{ij_{s}}\prod_{k\in\mathcal{M}_{s}}A_{k}(t_{i})\Big]\int_{W^{|\sigma|}}\prod_{(k,\ell)\in E_{\sigma}}\varphi_{n}(x_{k}-x_{\ell})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{|\sigma|}.

We shall give a few more details regarding the above equation: In (
16
), the integration over
\mathbb{X}^{|\sigma|}
formally is an integration over
(W\times\mathbb{D}([0,T],\{0,1\})\times[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}})^{|\sigma|}
and the integral is split accordingly. The integrand then is the indicator that for an edge
(k,\ell)\in E_{\sigma}
(the edge set of the graph induced by
\sigma
), there is an edge in the RCM between the points
P_{k}
and
P_{\ell}
. The latter now depends on the marks
T_{k,\ell}
as described above. The integral over
[0,1]^{|\sigma|}
is then formally solved by writing the probability that
T_{k,\ell}\leq\varphi_{n}(\cdot)
as
\varphi_{n}(\cdot)
. Further, the expectation in the right-hand side of (
17
) represents the integral over
[1,\infty)^{|\sigma|}
so that the only “visible” integral is the one with respect to the Lebesgue measure
n^{|\sigma|}\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots,x_{|\sigma|})
.

The expectation in (
17
) is handled as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{s=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{h}b_{ij_{s}}\prod_{k\in\mathcal{M}_{s}}A_{k}(t_{i})\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{M}=1}^{h}\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{M}b_{i_{s}j_{s}}\Big)\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{M}_{s}}\prod_{s=1}^{M}A_{k}(t_{i_{s}})\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{M}=1}^{h}\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{M}b_{i_{s}j_{s}}\Big)\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{k\in\mathcal{M}_{s}}A_{k}(t_{i_{s(k)}})\Big],

where
s(k)\in[M]
is the minimum index
s
with
J_{s}\cap B_{k}\neq\emptyset
. By stationarity of
(A_{k}(t))_{t\geq 0}
for all
k\in[|\sigma|]
and independence of
(A_{1}(t))_{t>0},\dots,(A_{|\sigma|}(t))_{t>0}
, the above is given by

\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{M}=1}^{h}\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{M}b_{i_{s}j_{s}}\Big)\varrho^{|\sigma|}\leq\varrho^{|\sigma|}(hb_{\max})^{M},

where we recall that
\varrho=\mathbb{E}[A_{k}(0)]
and
b_{\max}:=\max_{i,j}b_{ij}
.

To bound the integral over
W^{|\sigma|}
in (
17
), we argue as for
[
7
, (6.2)]
. This yields

\text{cum}(S_{n}^{(j_{1})},\dots,S_{n}^{(j_{M})})\leq|\widetilde{\Pi}(q_{j_{1}},\dots,q_{j_{M}})|\varrho^{|\sigma|}(hb_{\max})^{M}n^{|\sigma|}\nu_{n}^{|\sigma|-1}.

We next discuss the resulting bounds for
\text{cum}_{M}(S_{n})
separately for the sparse and the dense regime. If
\nu_{n}\in\mathscr{S}
, then by (
15
), the definition of
\psi_{n,j}
and since
|\sigma|\geq\min\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}=:p
,

\displaystyle\text{cum}_{M}(S_{n})=\sum_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}}\frac{\text{cum}(S_{n}^{(j_{1})},\dots,S_{n}^{(j_{M})})}{\psi_{n,j_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n,j_{M}}}\leq C\frac{n^{|\sigma|}\nu_{n}^{|\sigma|-1}}{n^{Mp/2}\nu_{n}^{M(p-1)/2}}\leq C(n^{p}\nu_{n}^{p-1})^{1-M/2},

which tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
for all
M\geq 3
.

If
\nu_{n}\in\mathscr{D}
, then since
|\sigma|\leq\sum_{i\in[M]}(q_{j_{i}}-1)+1
, with
Q_{M}:=\sum_{i\in[M]}q_{j_{i}}
,

\displaystyle\text{cum}_{M}(S_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}}\frac{\text{cum}(S_{n}^{(j_{1})},\dots,S_{n}^{(j_{M})})}{\psi_{n,j_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n,j_{M}}}\leq\sum_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{M})\in[m]^{M}}C\frac{n^{Q_{M}-M+1}\nu_{n}^{Q_{M}-M}}{n^{Q_{M}-M/2}\nu_{n}^{Q_{M}-M}}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{1-M/2},

which also tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
for all
M\geq 3
.
∎

6.
Tightness

Finally, to justify the claim of Theorem
1
, we apply the analogue of
[
1
, Theorem 13.5]
for vector-valued processes; in particular, the sufficient condition given in
[
1
, Eqn. (13.14)]
. According to this condition, to prove that the sequence
\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(\cdot)
is tight in
\mathbb{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{m})
, it suffices to show that for any
0\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(r)-\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(s)\right\rvert\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(s)-\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(t)\right\rvert\right\rvert^{2}\right]\leqslant C(t-r)^{2}

for some positive constant
C
.
Define, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i,j\in[m]
,
r,s,t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,i,j}(r,s,t):=\mathbb{E}\biggl[\bigr(\Gamma_{n,i}(r)-\Gamma_{n,i}(s)\bigl)^{2}\bigr(\Gamma_{n,j}(s)-\Gamma_{n,j}(t)\bigl)^{2}\biggr],

so that, due to (
4
), as
n\nu_{n}\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(r)-\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(s)\right\rvert\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(s)-\bm{\Gamma}^{\star}_{n}(t)\right\rvert\right\rvert^{2}\right]

(19)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\varrho^{-2(q_{i}+q_{j})}\Delta_{n,i,j}(r,s,t)\begin{cases}(n^{2q_{i}+2q_{j}-2}\nu_{n}^{2q_{i}+2q_{j}-4})^{-1},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{D}},\\
(n^{q_{i}+q_{j}}\nu_{n}^{q_{i}+q_{j}-2})^{-1},&\nu_{n}\in{\mathscr{S}}.\end{cases}

We define the (symmetric) function

f^{(i)}(P_{1},\dots,P_{q_{i}})=\frac{1}{q_{i}!}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{q_{i}}A_{k}(r)-\prod_{k=1}^{q_{i}}A_{k}(s)\Big)\sum_{H\in{\bm{G}}_{G_{i}}([q_{i}])}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{k,\ell\}\in E(H)\\
X_{k}\prec X_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{1}\{T_{k,\ell}\leq\varphi_{n}(X_{k}-X_{\ell})\}

(and analogously

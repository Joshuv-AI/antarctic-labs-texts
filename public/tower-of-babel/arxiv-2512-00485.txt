Anisotropic elliptic equations involving unbounded coefficients and singular nonlinearities
Fessel Achhoud a and Hichem. Khelifi b

Abstract
In this paper, we study the existence and regularity of solutions for a class of nonlinear singular elliptic equations involving unbounded coefficients and a singular right-hand side. Specifically, we are interested to problem whose simplest model is − ∑ j = 1 N ∂ j ( [ 1 + u q ] | ∂ j u | p j − 2 ∂ j u ) = f u γ in 𝒟 , u > 0 in 𝒟 , u = 0 on ∂ 𝒟 , -\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{j}\left([1+u^{q}]|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\right)=\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}}\text{ in $\mathcal{D},$}\quad u>0\text{ in $\mathcal{D},$}\quad u=0\hbox{ on}\;\;\partial\mathcal{D}, where 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is a bounded open subset of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with N > 2 N>2 , γ ≥ 0 \gamma\geq 0 , q > 0 q>0 , p j > 2 p_{j}>2 for all j = 1 , … , N j=1,...,N and the source term f f belongs to L 1 ​ ( 𝒟 ) L^{1}(\mathcal{D}) , with f ≥ 0 f\geq 0 and f ≢ 0 f\not\equiv 0 .

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\partial_{j}\left([b(x)+u^{q}]|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\right)=\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}}&\hbox{in}\;\;\mathcal{D},\\

Anisotropic Sobolev spaces

The anisotropic Sobolev spaces
W^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
and
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
provide the functional framework for Problem (
1
); see
[
22
,
19
,
20
]
for details. Let
\mathcal{D}
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
N\geq 2
, and let
p_{j}
satisfy the conditions (
2
). The anisotropic Sobolev spaces
W^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
and
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle W^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})=\left\{z\in W^{1,1}(\mathcal{D}):\;\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{p_{j}}\;dx<\infty,\;\;j\in\mathcal{E}\right\},\\
\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})=\left\{z\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathcal{D}):\;\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{p_{j}}\;dx<\infty,\;\;j\in\mathcal{E}\right\}.\end{array}

Alternatively,
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
with respect to the norm

\|z\|_{W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{p_{j}}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}},

with this norm,
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
is a separable and reflexive Banach space, and its dual is
\left(W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\right)^{*}
, where
p^{\prime}_{j}
is the conjugate of
p_{j}
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p_{j}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{j}}=1
for all
j\in\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

]

For any

with
\overline{p}<N

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{\delta}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq C\prod_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{p_{j}}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{Np_{j}}},\quad\forall\delta\in[1,\overline{p}^{*}],\quad\overline{p}^{*}=\frac{N\overline{p}}{N-\overline{p}},

(10)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{\overline{p}^{*}}\;dx\right)^{\frac{p_{N}}{\overline{p}^{*}}}\leq C\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{p_{j}}\;dx.

(11)

Lemma 2.2
.

[
22
]

For any

\overline{\delta}<N

\mathcal{D}

\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{r}\;dx\right)^{\frac{N}{\overline{\delta}}-1}\leq C\prod_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}z|^{\delta_{j}}|v|^{t_{j}\delta_{j}}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta_{j}}},

for any

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{b_{j}(N-1)-1+\frac{1}{\delta_{j}}}{t_{j}+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}b_{j}=1.

To prove that the solution

3
Proof of the Main result

3.1
Approximation of problem (
1
)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we approximate problem (
1
) by considering the following regularized (non-singular) problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\partial_{j}\left([b(x)+u_{n}^{q}]|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\right)=\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}&\hbox{in}\;\;\mathcal{D},\\
u_{n}>0&\hbox{in}\;\;\mathcal{D},\\
u_{n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\mathcal{D},\end{array}\right.

(17)

where
f_{n}=T_{n}(f)\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
is a sequence of bounded functions that converges to
f>0
in
L^{1}(\mathcal{D})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under assumptions (
3
) and (
4
), problem (
17
) admits a non-negative solution
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
satisfying

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left[b(x)+u_{n}^{q}\right]|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f_{n}\varphi\;dx}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}},

(18)

for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

For fixed
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
, let
v\in L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
and define
w=\mathcal{P}(v)
as the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\partial_{j}\left(\left[b(x)+T_{k}(v)^{q}\right]|\partial_{j}w|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}w\right)=\frac{f_{n}}{\left(|v|+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}&\hbox{in}\;\mathcal{D},\\
w=0&\hbox{on}\;\partial\mathcal{D},\end{array}\right.

(19)

Since problem (
19
) has a unique solution
[
13
]
, the operator
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined. We aim to establish the existence of a fixed point for
\mathcal{P}
via Schauder’s fixed point theorem
[
17
]
.

Picking
\varphi=w
as a test function in (
19
), and making use of (
3
) along with the fact that

\left|\frac{f_{n}}{\left(|v|+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\right|\leq n^{\gamma+1},\quad|T_{k}(v)|^{q}\geq 0,

we have

\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\|\partial_{j}w\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathcal{D})}^{p_{j}}\leq n^{\gamma+1}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|w|\;dx.

Using (
11
) on the left and Hölder’s inequality with exponent
\overline{p}^{\star}
on the right, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\overline{p}^{\star}}(\mathcal{D})}^{p_{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\|\partial_{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathcal{D})}^{p_{j}}\leq C(n,\gamma,\alpha)|\mathcal{D}|^{\frac{1}{(\overline{p}^{\star})^{\prime}}}\|w\|_{L^{\overline{p}^{\star}}(\mathcal{D})}.

(20)

Since
p_{N}>1
, there exists a positive constant
R(n,|\mathcal{D}|,\gamma,\alpha)
that is independent of
v
and
w
, such that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\overline{p}^{\star}}(\mathcal{D})}\leq R(n,|\mathcal{D}|,\gamma,\alpha).

(21)

Observing that
\overline{p}<\overline{p}^{\star}
, then

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})}\leq R(n,|\mathcal{D}|,\gamma,\alpha).

(22)

Therefore, equation (
22
) implies that the ball
B
in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
, with radius
R(n,|\mathcal{D}|,\gamma,\alpha)
, remains invariant under the mapping
\mathcal{P}
.

Observation 1:

\mathcal{P}(L^{p}(\mathcal{D}))
has relative compactness in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
.

Using equations (
20
) and (
21
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}w|^{p_{j}}\;dx=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}\mathcal{P}(v)|^{p_{j}}\;dx\leq R(n,|\mathcal{D}|,\gamma,\alpha),\quad\forall v\in L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D}).

By Sobolev embedding,
\mathcal{P}(L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D}))
can be shown to be compact in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
.

Observation 2:

\mathcal{P}
is a continuous operator.

Let
v_{r},
for any
r\in\N,
be a sequence converging to
v
in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
. By the dominated convergence theorem, it follows that

\frac{f_{n}}{\left(|v_{r}|+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\rightarrow\frac{f_{n}}{\left(|v|+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\;\;\mbox{strongly in}\;L^{p}(\mathcal{D}).

By uniqueness,
w_{r}:=\mathcal{P}(v_{r})
converges to
w:=\mathcal{P}(v)
in
L^{\overline{p}}(\mathcal{D})
. Applying Schauder’s fixed point theorem, for each fixed
n
, there exists
u_{n,k}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\mathcal{D})
such that
\mathcal{P}(u_{n,k})=u_{n,k}
, with
u_{n,k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, for fixed
h\geq 1
, using
G_{h}(u_{n,k})
as a test function and noting that
u_{n,k}+\frac{1}{n}\geq h\geq 1
on
\{u_{n,k}\geq h\}
, we obtain

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}G_{h}(u_{n,k})|^{p_{j}}\;dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{D}}f_{n}G_{k}(u_{n,k})\;dx.

We apply the standard technique from
[
11
]
to obtain
u_{n,k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
. Moreover, the

estimate is independent of

, so for sufficiently large

and fixed

,
u_{n}\in W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
solves (
17
).

Choosing
\varphi=u_{n}^{-}=\min\{u_{n},0\}
in (
17
) and applying (
3
), we observe that
\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}
is nonnegative. Thus,

\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{i}u_{n}^{-}|^{p_{j}}\;dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f_{n}\;dx}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}u_{n}^{-}\leq 0.

This implies

a.e. in
\mathcal{D}
, leading to

a.e. in
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

We show in the following Lemma that

remains strictly positive in
\mathcal{D}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u_{n}
solve (
17
). Then, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}>0
, independent of
n
, such that
u_{n}\geq\tilde{C}
a.e. in
\mathcal{D}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

For
s\geq 0
, consider the function
\Theta_{\sigma}(s)
defined as follows

\Theta_{\sigma}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\hbox{if}\;0\leq s\leq 1,\\
\frac{1}{\sigma}(1+\sigma-s)&\hbox{if}\;1<s\leq\sigma+1,\\
0&\hbox{if}\;s>\sigma+1,\end{array}\right.

Using
\Theta_{\sigma}(s)\phi
as a test function in (
17
), where
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})
with
\phi\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(b(x)+u_{n}^{q})|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\partial_{j}\phi\Theta_{\sigma}(u_{n})\;dx

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathcal{D}\cap\{1\leq u_{n}\leq\sigma+1\}}(b(x)+u_{n}^{q})|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}}\phi\;dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\Theta_{\sigma}(u_{n})\phi\;dx.

Omitting the non-negative term on the right-hand side and letting
\sigma\to 0
, we derive

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(b(x)+u_{n}^{q})|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\partial_{j}\phi\chi_{\{0\leq u_{n}\leq 1\}}\;dx\geq\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f_{n}\;dx}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\phi\chi_{\{0\leq u_{n}\leq 1\}}.

Using the fact that
\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\geq\frac{T_{1}(f)}{2^{\gamma}}
on the set
\{0\leq u_{n}\leq 1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(b(x)+T_{1}(u_{n})^{q})|\partial_{j}T_{1}(u_{n})|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}T_{1}(u_{n})\partial_{j}\phi\;dx\geq\frac{1}{2^{\gamma}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}T_{1}(f)\phi\chi_{\{0\leq u_{n}\leq 1\}}\;dx.

Since
b(x)+T_{1}(u_{n})^{q}\leq\beta+1
, we get

\displaystyle(\beta+1)\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}T_{1}(u_{n})|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}T_{1}(u_{n})\partial_{j}\phi\;dx\geq 0.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left|u_{n}^{q}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\right|^{p^{\prime}_{j}}\;dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}u_{n}^{qp^{\prime}_{j}}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}}\;dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u_{n}>1\}}u_{n}^{qp^{\prime}_{j}}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}}\;dx+\int_{\{u_{n}\leq 1\}}u_{n}^{qp^{\prime}_{j}}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u_{n}>1\}}u_{n}^{q+\gamma-1}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}}\;dx+\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}T_{1}(u_{n})|^{p_{j}}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq C.

Then the last inequality ensure that
u_{n}^{q}\left|\partial_{j}u_{n}\right|^{p_{j}-1}
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}_{j}}(\mathcal{D})
for all
j\in\mathcal{E}
.
∎

3.3
Passing to the limit

Because the proof of the cases
(\textbf{B}_{\gamma,q})
and
(\textbf{C}_{\gamma,q})
in Theorem
1.1
are similar to that of the case
(\textbf{A}_{\gamma,q})
, we restrict to that of the case (1). By applying Lemma
3.3
, we conclude that
u_{n}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
(or equivalently in
W_{0}^{1,(\eta_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
). It follows that there exists a function
u
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\mbox{weakly in}\;\;W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})\;(\mbox{or in}\;\;W_{0}^{1,(\eta_{j})}(\mathcal{D}))\;\;\mbox{a.e. in}\;\mathcal{D}.

(52)

Furthermore, according to
[
4
]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{j}u_{n}\rightharpoonup\partial_{j}u\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\;\mathcal{D}.

(53)

For the first term, by (
53
), we observe that
b(x)|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}
converge to
b(x)|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u
almost everywhere in
\mathcal{D}
. Furthermore, since
u_{n}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{1,(\eta_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
(or equivalently in
W_{0}^{1,(p_{j})}(\mathcal{D})
), we can apply Hölder’s inequality to obtain, for any measurable subset
E\subset\mathcal{D}
, the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{E}b(x)|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\beta\int_{E}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-1}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\beta\left(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{\eta_{j}}\;dx\right)^{\frac{p_{j}-1}{\eta_{j}}}|E|^{1-\frac{p_{j}-1}{\eta_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\beta C|E|^{\frac{\eta_{j}-p_{j}+1}{\eta_{j}}}.

This indicates that the sequence
\{b(x)|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\}_{n}
is equi-integrable. By applying Vitali’s theorem, we infer that for every test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
, the result is

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}b(x)|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}b(x)|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx.

(54)

Alternatively, by Lemma
3.4
and (
52
), we know that the sequence
\{u_{n}^{q}|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\}_{n}
converges weakly to
u^{q}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u
in
L^{\rho_{j}}(\mathcal{D})
, for every
1<\rho_{j}<p^{\prime}_{j}
and for all indices
j\in\mathcal{E}
. By combining this weak convergence with (
54
), we derive

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(b(x)+u_{n}^{q}\right)|\partial_{j}u_{n}|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u_{n}\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(b(x)+u^{q}\right)|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D}).

(55)

Regarding the limit of the term on the right-hand side of (
18
), by Lemma
3.2
, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
,

\left|\frac{f_{n}\varphi}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\right|\leq\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})}f}{\tilde{C}^{\gamma}}.

Then, from the previous estimate, (
52
), and by applying Lebesgue’s Theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f_{n}}{\left(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\gamma}}\varphi\;dx=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}}\varphi\;dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathcal{D}).

(56)

Consider
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
as a test function in (
18
). By the convergence results (
3.3
), (
56
), and taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
, we obtain

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(b(x)+u^{q}\right)|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\partial_{j}\varphi\;dx=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}}\varphi\;dx,\quad\forall\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathcal{D}).

Newton polytopes in cluster algebras and τ \tau -tilting theory
Peigen Cao

Abstract
. We prove that the cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables are uniquely determined by the Newton polytopes of their F F -polynomials for skew-symmetrizable cluster algebras. Accordingly, we prove that the τ \tau -rigid modules and the left finite multi-semibricks in τ \tau -tilting theory are uniquely determined by the Newton polytopes of these modules. The key tools used in the proofs are the left Bongartz completion, F F -invariant and partial F F -invariant in the context of cluster algebras and τ \tau -tilting theory.

1.
Introduction

Cluster algebras are a class of commutative algebras equipped with an extra combinatorial structure introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
20
]
. Such algebras are generated by a special set of
generators, called
cluster variables
, which are grouped into overlapping subsets of fixed size,
called
clusters
. A
seed
is a pair consisting of a cluster
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and a skew-symmetrizable integer matrix
B
. New seeds can be obtained from a given one by a procedure called
mutation
.
The sets of cluster variables and clusters of a cluster algebra are determined by an initial seed
({\bf x},B)
and iterative mutations.
A
cluster monomial
is a monomial in cluster variables from the same cluster. Cluster monomials are the central objects to study in cluster algebras.

The
g
-vectors
and
F
-polynomials
are introduced in
[
21
]
to study the cluster monomials of a cluster algebra in terms of the initial seed
({\bf x},B)
. More precisely, each cluster monomial
u
can be written as

u={\bf x}^{{\bf g}_{u}}\cdot F_{u}(\hat{y}_{1},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n})\;\;\;\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}],

where
{\bf g}_{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
F_{u}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
are the
g
-vector and
F
-polynomial of
u
, and each
\hat{y}_{k}
is a Laurent monomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. In particular, the
g
-vectors of the initial cluster variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are given by the columns of
I_{n}
and the
F
-polynomials of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are the constant
1
.

Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
21
]
conjectured that different cluster monomials have different
g
-vectors, which has been confirmed by Derksen, Weyman, and Zelevinsky
[
17
]
for skew-symmetric cluster algebras and by
Gross, Hacking, Keel, and Kontsevich for skew-symmetrizable cluster algebras.

\tau
-tilting theory was introduced by Adachi, Iyama, and Reiten
[
1
]
, which completes the classic tilting theory from the viewpoint of mutations. Various of the fundamental concepts in
cluster algebras, such as clusters/seeds, mutations,
g
-vectors,
F
-polynomials etc., were generalized to
\tau
-tilting theory,
cf.

[
1
,
18
,
10
,
13
]
. Table
1
summarizes the correspondence between cluster algebras and
\tau
-tilting theory.

It is known that
\tau
-rigid pairs in
\tau
-tilting theory play the role of cluster monomials in cluster algebras. Adachi, Iyama, and Reiten
[
1
]
proved that different
\tau
-rigid pairs have different
g
-vectors, which is analogous to the result that different cluster monomials have different
g
-vectors in cluster algebras.

Before discussing the motivation of this paper, we recall the definition of Newton polytopes for polynomials and modules, which will be used throughout. Throughout, let
A
be a finite dimensional basic algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
.

Definition 1.1

(Newton polytopes of polynomials and modules)
.

(i) Let
F({\bf y})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}{\bf y}^{\bf v}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial. Its
Newton polytope

\mathsf{P}(F)
is defined to be the convex hull of the finite set
\{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}
.

(ii) The
Newton polytope

\mathsf{P}(M)
of a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is defined to be the convex hull of the dimension vectors of the quotient modules of
M
.

Recently, there has been growing interest in the study of
F
-polynomials. For example, Jiarui Fei
[
18
,
19
]
studied the tropical
F
-polynomials of modules. In particular, he proved that the Newton polytope of the
F
-polynomial
F_{M}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
of a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
coincides with the Newton polytope of
M
.
The
F
-invariant in cluster algebras is introduced in
[
13
]
as a generalization of the
E
-invariant
[
17
]
in additive cluster categorification and the
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant
[
25
,
26
]
in monoidal cluster categorification. It turns out that the
F
-invariant is related to the tropical
F
-polynomials.

Since the
g
-vectors can determine the cluster monomials and
\tau
-rigid pairs, it is natural to ask
whether the
F
-polynomials can determine the cluster monomials and
\tau
-rigid pairs?

In order to consider this question, it is natural to exclude the initial cluster variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and correspondingly the initial indecomposable
\tau
-rigid pairs
(0,P_{1}),\ldots,(0,P_{n})
, because the
F
-polynomials of these initial objects are
1
. So we only need to consider cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables and
\tau
-rigid modules.

In
[
11
]
, Keller, Qin, and the author proved that the cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables are uniquely determined by their
F
-polynomials. The proof there depends on a notion of valuation pairing on (upper) cluster algebras. Typically, the same proof does not work in
\tau
-tilting theory.

Recently, it was proved in
[
14
]
that the
indecomposable

\tau
-rigid modules and the left finite bricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
are uniquely determined by their Newton polytopes, which is equivalent to saying that such modules are uniquely determined by the Newton polytopes of their
F
-polynomials, thanks to Fei’s result
[
18
]
. The proof in
[
14
]
is based on two key results: (i) all functorially finite torsion classes are semistable torsion classes
[
5
]
; and (ii) the brick-
\tau
-rigid correspondence in
[
15
]
, which is a bijection between the indecomposable
\tau
-rigid modules and the left finite bricks. In the general case, it is proved in
[
14
]
that if two rigid modules
U
and
V
(not necessarily indecomposable) have the same Newton polytope, then
U\oplus V
remains
\tau
-rigid (see
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
or Lemma
5.12
in this paper). However, it is not clear whether
U
and
V
are isomorphic or not. The main motivation for this paper is the following question.

Question 1.2
.

Whether the Newton polytopes of
F
-polynomials determine the cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables and the
\tau
-rigid modules?

Recently, the Newton polytopes of
F
-polynomials and modules were extensively studied in the literature,
cf.

[
8
,
7
,
9
,
18
,
19
,
2
,
3
,
27
,
14
]
. In particular, the Newton polytopes of modules over preprojective algebras are used to study MV polytopes in
[
8
,
7
]
, which parametrize Lusztig’s canonical basis,
cf.

[
24
]
.

Before giving the main results of this paper, we first recall the definitions of
\tau
-rigid modules and left finite multi-semibricks.

Definition 1.3

(
\tau
-rigid module, multi-semibrick and left finite module)
.

(i) A module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called
\tau
-rigid
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(M,\tau M)=0
, where
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

(ii) A module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called a
multi-semibrick
if

M
has a decomposition
M\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{a_{i}}
such that each
C_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is a brick and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(C_{i},C_{j})=0
for any
i\neq j
.

(iii) A module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called
left finite
, if the smallest torsion class containing
M
is a functorially finite torsion class in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

The main results in this paper are as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a skew-symmetrizable cluster algebra with initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
. Let
u
and
v
be two cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables.
If the two
F
-polynomials
F_{u}^{t_{0}}
and
F_{v}^{t_{0}}
have the same Newton polytope, then
u=v
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional basic algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
. Let
U
and
V
be two
\tau
-rigid modules in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, then
U\cong V
.

The left finite multi-semibricks are the dual counterpart of
\tau
-rigid modules in some sense. So it is natural to extend the result on
\tau
-rigid modules to the left finite multi-semibricks.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
U
and
V
be two left finite multi-semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, then
U\cong V
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
are quite similar, while the proof of Theorem
1.6
is slightly different. Let us make some comments about the proofs of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
. Both proofs are based on the reduction arguments.
The key points are summarized as follows:

(i)

Show that the direct sum
U\oplus V
remains a
\tau
-rigid module; and the product
uv
remains a cluster monomial.

(ii)

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is
by reducing the number
|U\oplus V|
of iso-classes of indecomposable direct summands of
U\oplus V
. The proof of Theorem
1.4
is by reducing the number
|\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)|
of cluster variables in the support set
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
of
uv
, which is the set of cluster variables appearing in the cluster monomial
uv
.

(iii)

In order to reduce the number
|U\oplus V|
, we consider the left Bongartz completion
\mathcal{M}=(M,P)
of
U\oplus V
, which is the basic
\tau
-tilting pair such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus V)
. Then we show that any left mutation of
\mathcal{M}=(M,P)
can be used to construct two new
\tau
-rigid modules
U^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits U
and
V^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits V
satisfying that
U^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
have the same Newton polytope but
|U^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime}|<|U\oplus V|
. Moreover,
U\cong V
if and only if
U^{\prime}\cong V^{\prime}
.

(iv)

In order to reduce the number
|\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)|
, we consider the left Bongartz completion
[{\bf x}_{s}]
of the partial cluster
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
. Then we show that any red mutation of the seed
({\bf x}_{s},B_{s})
can be used to construct two new cluster monomials
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
satisfying that
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
have the same Newton polytope but
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime})\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
. Moreover,
u=v
if and only if
u^{\prime}=v^{\prime}
.

Of course, many preparations are required to ensure that each stage goes smoothly. These will be detailed in the main body of the paper. Among these, the
F
-invariant
and
partial
F
-invariant
in cluster algebra and
\tau
-tilting theory will play a crucial role in the proofs, because such invariants are directly related to the Newton polytopes. We give a detailed discussion on
F
-invariant of cluster monomials in Section
4.1
and that of decorated modules in Section
5.1
.

This paper consists of two closely related parts: cluster algebras and
\tau
-tilting theory. These two parts can be read in any order. Personally, I first proved the main result in cluster algebras and then realized that a similar approach can be applied to the
\tau
-tilting theory.

Remark 1.7
.

In this paper, the prefixes “left” and “right” in the terminology “left mutation”, “left Bongartz completion”, “right mutation”, and “right Bongartz completion” indicate the direction of change under a suitable partial order:
“left” denotes a move to a smaller element, while “right” denotes a move to a larger one.
This convention aligns with the familiar ordering of integers
(\dots,-2,-1,0,1,2,\ldots)
, where moving left yields smaller numbers and moving right yields larger ones.

2.
Preliminaries on polytopes and cluster algebras

Throughout, we denote by
\langle{-,-}\rangle:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by
{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,{\bf e}_{n}
the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.1.
Polytopes, Minkowski sum and tropical polynomials

A
polytope

\mathsf{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the convex hull of a finite (non-empty)
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is a bounded closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a polytope
\mathsf{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its
support function

h_{\mathsf{P}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

h_{\mathsf{P}}({\bf r}):=\max\{\langle{{\bf a},{\bf r}}\rangle\mid{\bf a}\in\mathsf{P}\}.

It is known from
[
8
, Section 4.2]
or
[
30
, Section 1.7]
that the polytope
\mathsf{P}
can be recovered from its support function
h_{\mathsf{P}}
by

\mathsf{P}=\{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle{{\bf a},{\bf r}}\rangle\leq h_{\mathsf{P}}({\bf r}),\;\forall{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

Let
\mathsf{P}_{1}
and
\mathsf{P}_{2}
be two polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Minkowski sum
of
\mathsf{P}_{1}
and
\mathsf{P}_{2}
is the polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\mathsf{P}_{1}+\mathsf{P}_{2}:=\{{\bf a}+{\bf b}\mid{\bf a}\in\mathsf{P}_{1},\;{\bf b}\in\mathsf{P}_{2}\}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
30
, Theorem 1.7.5]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}_{1}
and
\mathsf{P}_{2}
be two polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

h_{\mathsf{P}_{1}+\mathsf{P}_{2}}=h_{\mathsf{P}_{1}}+h_{\mathsf{P}_{2}}.

As a direct consequence, the following cancellation law holds.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{P}_{1},\mathsf{P}_{2}
and
\mathsf{Q}
be three polytopes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mathsf{P}_{1}+\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{P}_{2}+\mathsf{Q}
, then
\mathsf{P}_{1}=\mathsf{P}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{P}_{1}+\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{P}_{2}+\mathsf{Q}
and by Theorem
2.1
, we have

h_{\mathsf{P}_{1}}+h_{\mathsf{Q}}=h_{\mathsf{P}_{1}+\mathsf{Q}}=h_{\mathsf{P}_{2}+\mathsf{Q}}=h_{\mathsf{P}_{2}}+h_{\mathsf{Q}}.

Thus
h_{\mathsf{P}_{1}}=h_{\mathsf{P}_{2}}
. Since a polytope is uniquely determined its support function, we get
\mathsf{P}_{1}=\mathsf{P}_{2}
.
∎

In this paper, we mainly focus on the polytopes defined from polynomials and modules of a finite dimensional algebra
A
.

Definition 2.3

(Newton polytopes of polynomials and modules)
.

(i) Let
F({\bf y})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}{\bf y}^{\bf v}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial. Its
Newton polytope

\mathsf{P}(F)
is defined to be the convex hull of the finite set
\{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}
.

(ii) The
Newton polytope

\mathsf{P}(M)
of a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is defined to be the convex hull of the dimension vectors of the quotient modules of
M
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
22
, Chapter 6, Prop. 1.2]
)
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two non-zero polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. Then

\mathsf{P}(F_{1}F_{2})=\mathsf{P}(F_{1})+\mathsf{P}(F_{2}),

where
\mathsf{P}(F_{k})
is the Newton polytope of
F_{k}
for
k=1,2
.

Definition 2.5

(Tropical polynomial)
.

Let
F({\bf y})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}{\bf y}^{\bf v}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial. The
tropical polynomial
of
F
is the map
F[-]:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

F[{\bf r}]:=\max\{\langle{{\bf v},{\bf r}}\rangle\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}.

Tropical polynomials play an important role in defining the
F
-invariant in cluster algebras and
\tau
-tilting theory in Sections
4.1
,
5.1
. We can see that if
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
F[{\bf r}]\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.6
.

We have the following important facts.

(a)

The tropical polynomial
F[-]:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is uniquely determined by the Newton polytope
\mathsf{P}(F)
of
F
. Actually, it only depends on the vertices of the Newton polytope
\mathsf{P}(F)
.

(b)

If
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
F[{\bf r}]\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(c)

If the polynomial
F
has constant term
1
, then
F[{\bf r}]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

(d)

The
F
-polynomials of cluster monomials and modules defined later always have constant term
1
(see Theorem
2.18
(iv) and Remark
3.8
).

Example 2.7
.

Take
F=1+y_{1}+y_{1}y_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},y_{2}]
and
{\bf r}=\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix}
, then

\displaystyle F[{\bf r}]=\max\{\begin{bmatrix}0,0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1,0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1,1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2\\
1\end{bmatrix}\}=\max\{0,-2,-1\}=0.

The following result can be checked easily.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be a non-zero polynomial and
\mathsf{P}(F)
its Newton polytope. Then the tropical polynomial of
F
and
the support function
of
\mathsf{P}(F)
are the same, i.e.,

F[{\bf r}]=h_{\mathsf{P}(F)}({\bf r}),\quad\forall{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be two non-zero polynomials. Then for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
(F_{1}F_{2})[{\bf r}]=F_{1}[{\bf r}]+F_{2}[{\bf r}]
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
, we know that
\mathsf{P}(F_{1}F_{2})=\mathsf{P}(F_{1})+\mathsf{P}(F_{2})
. Then by Theorem
2.1
, we have

h_{\mathsf{P}(F_{1}F_{2})}=h_{\mathsf{P}(F_{1})+\mathsf{P}(F_{2})}=h_{\mathsf{P}(F_{1})}+h_{\mathsf{P}(F_{2})}.

Then the desired result follows from Proposition
2.8
.
∎

2.2.
Cluster algebras

We first recall Fomin-Zelevinsky’s matrix mutation
[
20
]
.

Definition 2.10

(Matrix mutation)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be an
m\times n
integer matrix. For any integer
k
with
k\leq m
and
k\leq n
, the
mutation
of
A

in direction
k
is
defined to be the new integer matrix
\mu_{k}(A)=A^{\prime}=(a_{ij}^{\prime})
given by

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-a_{ij},&\text{if}\;i=k\;\text{or}\;j=k,\\
a_{ij}+[a_{ik}]_{+}[a_{kj}]_{+}-[-a_{ik}]_{+}[-a_{kj}]_{+},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
[a]_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now we fix a positive integer
n
and denote by
[1,n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. An
n\times n
integer matrix
B
is said to be
skew-symmetrizable
, if there exists a diagonal integer matrix
D=diag(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
with each
d_{i}>0
such that
DB
is skew-symmetric. Such a diagonal matrix
D
is called a
skew-symmetrizer
of
B
.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
20
]
)
.

(i) For any integer matrix
A=(a_{ij})_{m\times n}
, we have
\mu_{k}^{2}(A)=A
.

(ii) If
B=(b_{ij})_{n\times n}
is skew-symmetrizable, then
B^{\prime}:=\mu_{k}(B)
is still skew-symmetrizable and the two matrices
B,B^{\prime}
share the same skew-symmetrizers.

A
seed
in
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{Q}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is a pair
({\bf x},B)
, where

•

{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is an ordered set of free generators of
\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{Q}
;

•

B=(b_{ij})
is an
n\times n
skew-symmetrizable matrix.

Definition 2.12

(Seed mutation)
.

Let
({\bf x},B)
be a seed in
\mathbb{F}
. The
mutation
of
({\bf x},B)
in direction
k\in[1,n]
is the new seed
({\bf x}^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\mu_{k}({\bf x},B)
given by
B^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(B)
and

\displaystyle x_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}x_{i},&i\neq k,\\
x_{k}^{-1}\cdot(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{[b_{jk}]_{+}}+\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{[-b_{jk}]_{+}}),&i=k.\end{cases}

It can be checked that
\mu_{k}
is an involution. Let

Proposition 2.20

Proof.

Let

for its seed at vertex

to denote the

. By

3.3.
Bongartz completion in
\tau
-tilting theory

A
torsion pair

(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is a pair of subcategories of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
satisfying that

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(T,F)=0
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

(ii)

for any object
X\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow X_{t}\rightarrow X\rightarrow X_{f}\rightarrow 0

with
X_{t}\in\mathcal{T}
and
X_{f}\in\mathcal{F}
. Thanks to the condition (i), such a sequence is unique up to isomorphisms. This short exact sequence is called the
canonical sequence
of
X
with respect to
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.

Notice that in a torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, we always have
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{T}^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{T}=\prescript{\bot}{}{\mathcal{F}}
. The subcategory
\mathcal{T}
(resp.,
\mathcal{F}
) in a torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is called a
torsion class
(resp.,
torsion-free class
) in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

It is known that a subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is a torsion class if and only if it is closed under extensions and quotients.
A torsion class
\mathcal{T}
is said to be
functorially finite
, if there exists a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
such that
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
.

A module
M
in a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is said to be
Ext-projective
in
\mathcal{C}
, if

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{A}^{1}(M,X)=0
for any
X\in\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})
the direct sum of one copy of each of the indecomposable Ext-projective objects in
\mathcal{C}
up to isomorphism.

4.
F
-invariant and Newton polytopes in cluster algebras

4.1.
F
-invariant of cluster monomials

F
-invariant in cluster algebras is introduced by the author in
[
13
]
, which generalizes the
E
-invariant
[
17
]
in additive categorification of cluster algebras and the
\mathfrak{d}
-invariant
[
25
,
26
]
in monoidal categorification of cluster algebras.

Recall that for a nonzero polynomial

F({\bf y})=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{\bf v}{\bf y}^{\bf v}\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
, its tropical polynomial
F[-]:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

F[{\bf r}]:=\max\{\langle{{\bf v},{\bf r}}\rangle\mid c_{\bf v}\neq 0\}.

Definition 4.1

(
F
-invariant and partial
F
-invariant)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cluster algebra and
D=diag(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
a fixed skew-symmetrizer for the exchange matrices of
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
u
and
v
be two cluster monomials of
\mathcal{A}
, and let

u={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{u}^{t}}F_{u}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})\;\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;v={\bf x}_{t}^{{\bf g}_{v}^{t}}F_{v}^{t}(\hat{y}_{1;t},\ldots,\hat{y}_{n;t})

be the canonical expressions of
u
and
v
with repect to a vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

(i)

The integer

\displaystyle(u\mid\mid v)_{F}=F_{u}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t}]+F_{v}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{u}^{t}]

is called the
F
-invariant
of
(u,v)
, which is independent of the choice of
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
by Theorem
4.2
below.

(ii)

The integer
F_{u}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t}]
is called the
partial
F
-invariant
of
(u,v)
at vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Since the definition of
F
-invariant depends on the choice of a skew-symmetrizer
D
,
we always fix a skew-symmetrizer for the exchange matrices of
\mathcal{A}
in this paper.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.10, Proposition 4.30]
)
.

Let
u
and
v
be two cluster monomials of
\mathcal{A}
and
D=diag(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
a fixed skew-symmetrizer for the exchange matrices of
\mathcal{A}
. Then
for any two vertices
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{u}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t}]+F_{v}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{u}^{t}]=F_{u}^{t^{\prime}}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t^{\prime}}]+F_{v}^{t^{\prime}}[D{\bf g}_{u}^{t^{\prime}}].

In particular, the
F
-invariant
(u\mid\mid v)_{F}=F_{u}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t}]+F_{v}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{u}^{t}]
only depends on
u
and
v
, not on the choice of vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
.

Since the
F
-polynomials of cluster monomials have constant term
1
, we have

(u\mid\mid v)_{F}=F_{u}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{v}^{t}]+F_{v}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{u}^{t}]\geq 0.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.19]
)
.

Let
u
and
v
be two cluster monomials of
\mathcal{A}
. Then the product
u\cdot v
remains a cluster monomial of
\mathcal{A}
if and only if
(u\mid\mid v)_{F}=0
.

Example 4.4
.

Let us continue the example of cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}
of type

in Example

. We know that
the canonical expressions of

with respect to

We take

to be the fixed skew-symmetrizer for the exchange matrices of

. We have

\displaystyle(x_{3}\mid\mid x_{4})_{F}

\displaystyle=F_{x_{3}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{4}}^{t_{0}}]+F_{x_{4}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{3}}^{t_{0}}]

\displaystyle=(1+y_{1})\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+(1+y_{1}+y_{1}y_{2})\begin{bmatrix}-1\\

\displaystyle=\max\{0,\;-1\}+\max\{0,\;-1,\;0\}

Proposition 4.5

(
[

)
.

Let

and

. Then we have

for any vertex

4.2.
Newton polytopes of
F
-polynomials

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cluster algebra with initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
. If two cluster variables
x
and
z
are contained in the same cluster,
then
F_{z}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x}^{t_{0}}]=0=F_{x}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{z}^{t_{0}}]
.

Proof.

Since the two cluster variables
x
and
z
are contained in the same cluster and by Theorem
4.3
, we have
F_{x}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{z}^{t_{0}}]+F_{z}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x}^{t_{0}}]=(x\mid\mid z)_{F}=0
. Since
F_{x}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{z}^{t_{0}}]\geq 0
and
F_{z}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x}^{t_{0}}]\geq 0
, we get
F_{z}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x}^{t_{0}}]=0=F_{x}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{z}^{t_{0}}]
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cluster algebra with initial seed
({\bf x}_{t_{0}},B_{t_{0}})
and
D=diag(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
a fixed skew-symmetrizer for the exchange matrices of
\mathcal{A}
. Let
({\bf x}_{t^{\prime}},B_{t^{\prime}})=\mu_{k}({\bf x}_{t},B_{t})
be a mutation in
\mathcal{A}
. Then

(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}=d_{k}.

If moreover, the
k
-th column of
C_{t}^{t_{0}}
is non-positive, then we have

F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=0\quad\text{and}\quad F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=d_{k}.

Proof.

Let us use the vertex
t
to calculate the
F
-invariant
(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}
. Since

F_{x_{k;t}}^{t}=1,F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t}=1+y_{k}
and
{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t}={\bf e}_{k}
, We have

(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}=F_{x_{k;t}}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t}]+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t}]=0+(1+y_{k})[D{\bf e}_{k}]=d_{k}.

On the other hand, if we use the vertex
t_{0}
to calculate
(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}
, we have

d_{k}=(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}=F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}].

Now let us prove that
F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=0
under the assumption that the
k
-th column
{\bf c}_{k;t}
of
C_{t}^{t_{0}}
is non-positive. In this case, we have
-{\bf c}_{k;t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. By the mutation rule
[
21
, Proposition 5.1]
of
F
-polynomials, we have

F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}\cdot F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}=\prod_{b_{ik}^{t}>0}(F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}})^{b_{ik}^{t}}+{\bf y}^{-{\bf c}_{k;t}}\cdot\prod_{b_{ik}^{t}<0}(F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}})^{-b_{ik}^{t}}.

By Corollary
2.9
, we have the tropical version of the above equality:

(4.1)

\displaystyle F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[{\bf r}]+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[{\bf r}]=\max\{\sum_{b_{ik}^{t}>0}b_{ik}^{t}F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[{\bf r}],\;\langle-{\bf c}_{k;t},{\bf r}\rangle+\sum_{b_{ik}^{t}<0}(-b_{ik}^{t})F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[{\bf r}]\}

for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Lemma
4.6
, we know that
F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=0
for any
i\in[1,n]
. Then by taking
{\bf r}=D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}
in (
4.1
), we get

0+F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=\max\{0,\;\langle-{\bf c}_{k;t},D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}\rangle+0\}=\max\{0,\;\langle-{\bf c}_{k;t},D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}\rangle\}.

By Theorem
2.18
(i), we have
(G_{t}^{t_{0}})^{T}DC_{t}^{t_{0}}D^{-1}=I_{n}
. Thus
\langle-{\bf c}_{k;t},D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}\rangle\}=-d_{k}
. Hence, we have

F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=\max\{0,\;\langle-{\bf c}_{k;t},D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}\rangle\}=\max\{0,\;-d_{k}\}=0.

Thus
F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=(x_{k;t}\mid\mid x_{k;t^{\prime}})_{F}-F_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}]=d_{k}-0=d_{k}
.
∎

Recall that for a non-zero polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Z}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
,
\mathsf{P}(F)
denotes the Newton polytope of
F
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
u=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}^{a_{i}}
and
v=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}^{b_{i}}
be two cluster monomials of
\mathcal{A}
in some cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
. If
\mathsf{P}(F_{u}^{t_{0}})=\mathsf{P}(F_{v}^{t_{0}})
, then
a_{k}=b_{k}
whenever the
k
-th column of the
C
-matrix
C_{t}^{t_{0}}
is non-positive.

Proof.

Suppose that
k
-th column of the
C
-matrix
C_{t}^{t_{0}}
is non-positive. By Proposition
4.7
, we have

F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=d_{k}>0.

For
i\neq k
, we know that

\{x_{i;t},x_{k;t^{\prime}}\}\subseteq[{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}]
.
Then by Lemma
4.6
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=0,\quad\forall i\neq k.

Since
u=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}^{a_{i}}
and
v=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i;t}^{b_{i}}
and
by Proposition
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle F_{u}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]\overset{\eqref{eqn:Fdg=0}}{=}a_{k}F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=a_{k}d_{k},

\displaystyle F_{v}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}F_{x_{i;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]\overset{\eqref{eqn:Fdg=0}}{=}b_{k}F_{x_{k;t}}^{t_{0}}[D{\bf g}_{x_{k;t^{\prime}}}^{t_{0}}]=b_{k}d_{k}.

Since the two
F
-polynomials
F_{u}^{t_{0}}
and

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a skew-symmetrizable cluster algebra with initial seed

. Let

and
v
be two cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables.
If the two

have the same Newton polytope, i.e.,

.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{P}(F_{u}^{t_{0}})=\mathsf{P}(F_{v}^{t_{0}})
and by Lemma
4.10
, the product
uv
remains a cluster monomial. Thus its support set
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
is a partial cluster of
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
[{\bf x}_{s}]
be the left Bongartz completion of
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
with respect to vertex

.
Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)\subseteq[{\bf x}_{s}]
. We can write

and

. Thus

. We know that

if and only if

Since
[{\bf x}_{s}]
is the left Bongartz completion of

with respect to vertex

,
we know that the
j
-th column of the

is non-negative for any

with

. For the signs of the other columns of

, we have the following claim.

Claim:
there exist some

with

such that the

-th column of

is non-positive.
Otherwise, we know that

is a non-negative matrix. Then by Proposition

, we have

. Thus

and

are cluster monomials in non-initial cluster variables. This finishes the proof of the claim.

Now we prove

by reducing the number

of cluster variables in
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(uv)
. By the claim, we can take an integer
k

with

such that

Theorem 5.7
.

\tau
-rigid pair in

.

Let
U
and
V
be two
\tau
-rigid modules in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
\mathsf{P}(U)=\mathsf{P}(V)
, the direct sum
U\oplus V
remains
\tau
-rigid in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

Proof.

This result is first proved in
[
14
]
. Here we give a different proof. Since
U
and
V
are
\tau
-rigid modules, we know that
E^{\rm proj}(U,U)=\hom_{A}(U,\tau U)=0
and
E^{\rm proj}(V,V)=\hom_{A}(V,\tau V)=0
.
Then by Theorem
5.7
,
we obtain
F_{U}[{\bf g}_{U}]=E^{\rm proj}(U,U)=0
and
F_{V}[{\bf g}_{V}]=E^{\rm proj}(V,V)=0
.

Since
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, we know that
F_{U}[{\bf r}]=F_{V}[{\bf r}]
for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, we have
F_{U}[{\bf g}_{V}]=F_{V}[{\bf g}_{V}]=0
and
F_{V}[{\bf g}_{U}]=F_{U}[{\bf g}_{U}]=0
. Thus

(U\mid\mid V)_{F}=F_{U}[{\bf g}_{V}]+F_{V}[{\bf g}_{U}]=0.

By Corollary
5.10
(ii), we know that
U\oplus V
is a
\tau
-rigid module.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}
be a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})=(\oplus_{i\neq k}\mathcal{M}_{i})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}
a left mutation of
\mathcal{M}
. Then we have

(i)

E^{\rm proj}(\mathcal{M}_{k},\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime})=0
and
E^{\rm proj}(\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{k})>0
.

(ii)

F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}]=0
and
F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]>0
.

Proof.

(i) We write
\oplus_{i\neq k}\mathcal{M}_{i}=(U,Q)
,
\mathcal{M}_{k}=(M_{k},Q_{k})
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}=(M_{k}^{\prime},Q_{k}^{\prime})
. Thus

\mathcal{M}=(U\oplus M_{k},Q\oplus Q_{k})\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime},Q\oplus Q_{k}^{\prime}).

Since
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is indecomposable, we know that either
M_{k}
or
Q_{k}
is zero. Similarly, either
M_{k}^{\prime}
or
Q_{k}^{\prime}
is zero.

Since the mutation
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
is a left mutation, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})
.
This implies that
M_{k}\neq 0
. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{k}=(M_{k},0)
and
\mathcal{M}=(U\oplus M_{k},Q)
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime},Q\oplus Q_{k}^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{M}=(U\oplus M_{k},Q)
are
\tau
-tilting pairs and by Theorem
3.3
(i), we know that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U\oplus\tau M_{k}^{\prime})}\cap(Q\oplus Q_{k}^{\prime})^{\bot}=[\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U)}\cap Q^{\bot}]\cap[\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau M_{k}^{\prime})}\cap(Q_{k}^{\prime})^{\bot}],

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U\oplus\tau M_{k})}\cap Q^{\bot}=[\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U)}\cap Q^{\bot}]\cap\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau M_{k})},

Since
M_{k}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})
and
M_{k}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})\setminus\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})
,
we have

M_{k}^{\prime}\in\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau M_{k})}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;M_{k}\notin\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau M_{k}^{\prime})}\cap(Q_{k}^{\prime})^{\bot}.

Thus we have
E^{\rm proj}(\mathcal{M}_{k},\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime})=\hom_{A}(M_{k}^{\prime},\tau M_{k})=0
and

E^{\rm proj}(\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{k})=\hom_{A}(M_{k},\tau M_{k}^{\prime})+\hom_{A}(Q_{k}^{\prime},M_{k})>0.

(ii) This follows from (i) and Theorem
5.7
.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
U
and
V
be two
\tau
-rigid modules in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, i.e.,
\mathsf{P}(U)=\mathsf{P}(V)
, then
U\cong V
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.12
, we know that the direct sum
U\oplus V
is
\tau
-rigid. We will prove that
U\cong V
by reducing the number
|U\oplus V|
of iso-classes of indecomposable direct summands of
U\oplus V
.
We may assume
|U\oplus V|>0
; otherwise, we have
U=V=0
and the desired result holds trivially.

Let
(U\oplus V)^{\flat}
be the basic
\tau
-rigid module such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(U\oplus V)^{\flat}=\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits U\oplus V
. Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,P)
be the left Bongartz completion of
(U\oplus V)^{\flat}
, which is the basic
\tau
-tilting pair satisfying that
(U\oplus V)^{\flat}
is a direct summand of
M
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus V)
.

Let us write
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}
, where each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is an indecomposable
\tau
-rigid pair. We can assume that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:=(U,0)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{a_{i}}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\mathcal{V}:=(V,0)=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{b_{i}},

where
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Since
|U\oplus V|>0
, we know that
\{0\}\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus V)
. Then by Proposition
3.5
, there exists a left mutation
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\{0\}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M^{\prime}\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
.

We claim that
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is a direct summand of
((U\oplus V)^{\flat},0)
. Otherwise,
((U\oplus V)^{\flat},0)
is also a direct summand of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Then by Remark
3.16
and the fact that
\mathcal{M}=(M,P)
is the left Bongartz completion of
(U\oplus V)^{\flat}
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M^{\prime}
. This contradicts
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M^{\prime}\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is a direct summand of
((U\oplus V)^{\flat},0)
, we can assume that
\mathcal{M}_{k}=(M_{k},0)
, where
M_{k}
is an indecomposable direct summand of
(U\oplus V)^{\flat}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}
be the new indecomposable
\tau
-rigid pair obtained in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
.

By Lemma
5.13
, we know that
F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]>0
.
For
i\neq k
, we know that
\mathcal{M}_{i}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}
is
\tau
-rigid. Then by Corollary
5.10
(ii), we have
F_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=0
for any
i\neq k
.

By (
5.14
) and Proposition
3.9
, we have
F_{\mathcal{U}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}F_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}^{a_{i}}
and
F_{\mathcal{V}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}F_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}^{b_{i}}
. Then by Corollary
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{U}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}F_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=a_{k}F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}],

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{V}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}F_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=b_{k}F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}].

Since
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, we know that
F_{U}[{\bf r}]=F_{V}[{\bf r}]
for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In particular, we have

a_{k}F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{\mathcal{U}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{U}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{V}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{\mathcal{V}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]=b_{k}F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}].

Since
F_{\mathcal{M}_{k}}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}]>0
, we get
a_{k}=b_{k}
. So we have
following decompositions

U=U^{\prime}\oplus M_{k}^{a_{k}},\quad V=V^{\prime}\oplus M_{k}^{b_{k}}=V^{\prime}\oplus M_{k}^{a_{k}}.

Note that
U^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
do not contain
M_{k}
as a direct summand. In particular, we have

|U^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime}|<|U\oplus V|.

By Corollary
3.11
, we know that
\mathsf{P}(U)=\mathsf{P}(U^{\prime})+\mathsf{P}(M_{k}^{a_{k}})
and
\mathsf{P}(V)=\mathsf{P}(V^{\prime})+\mathsf{P}(M_{k}^{a_{k}})
. Since
\mathsf{P}(U)=\mathsf{P}(V)
and by Corollary
2.2
, we get
\mathsf{P}(U^{\prime})=\mathsf{P}(V^{\prime})
.

In order to show
U\cong V
, it suffices to show
U^{\prime}\cong V^{\prime}
.
Now
U^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
are two
\tau
-rigid modules satisfying
\mathsf{P}(U^{\prime})=\mathsf{P}(V^{\prime})
and
|U^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime}|<|U\oplus V|
. We can therefore apply the reduction and complete the proof.
∎

5.3.
Newton polytopes of left finite multi-semibricks

A module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called
left finite
, if the smallest torsion class
\langle M\rangle_{\rm tors}
containing
M
is a functorially finite torsion class in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. A module
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called a
brick
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{A}(C)\cong\mathsf{k}
. A set
\Omega
of iso-classes of bricks is called a
semibrick
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(C,C^{\prime})=0
for any
C,C^{\prime}\in\Omega
with
C\neq C^{\prime}
. A semibrick
\Omega
is
left finite
, if the smallest torsion class
\langle\Omega\rangle_{\rm tors}
containing the bricks in
\Omega
is a functorially finite torsion class.

Proposition 5.15

(
[
4
, Proposition 2.9]
)
.

The map
\Omega\mapsto\langle\Omega\rangle_{\rm tors}
gives a bijection from the left finite semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
to functorially finite torsion classes in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. In particular, the left finite semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
are in bijection with the basic
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(M,Q)
be a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and
\Omega
a left finite semibrick in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. The set
\Omega
is called the
labeling semibrick
of
\mathcal{M}=(M,Q)
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\langle\Omega\rangle_{\rm tors}
holds. This name is justified by the following result.

Proposition 5.16

(
[
4
, Lemma 2.5, Proposition 2.13]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}(M_{j},Q_{j})
a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Denote by
I\subseteq[1,n]
the subset such that
i\in I
if and only if
\mu_{i}(\mathcal{M})
is a left mutation of
\mathcal{M}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

For each
k\in I
, there exists a unique brick
C_{k}
, called a labeling brick, satisfying that

\displaystyle C_{k}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(M_{j},C_{k})=0\quad\forall j\in[1,n]\setminus k,

i.e.,
C_{k}
belongs to
(\oplus_{j\neq k}M_{j})^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{k}
.

(ii)

Let
\Omega:=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of labeling bricks for the left mutations of
\mathcal{M}
. Then
\Omega
is a left finite semibrick and we have
\langle\Omega\rangle_{\rm tors}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}
be a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and let
\Omega=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the labeling semibrick of
\mathcal{M}
. Fix a labeling brick
C_{k}\in\Omega
and let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})=(\oplus_{j\neq k}\mathcal{M}_{j})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}
be the corresponding left mutation of
\mathcal{M}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}=(M_{k}^{\prime},Q_{k}^{\prime})
. Then
C_{k}\notin\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M_{k}^{\prime})\cap Q_{k}^{\prime\bot}
and
C_{i}\in\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M_{k}^{\prime})\cap Q_{k}^{\prime\bot}
for any
i\in I\setminus\{k\}
.

(ii)

We have
F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]\neq 0
and
F_{C_{i}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=0
for any
i\in I\setminus\{k\}
.

Proof.

(i) Let us write

\mathcal{M}_{j}=(M_{j},Q_{j})
for
j\in[1,n]
and
(U,Q):=\oplus_{j\neq k}(M_{j},Q_{j})
.
Then

\mathcal{M}=(U\oplus M_{k},Q\oplus Q_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime},Q\oplus Q_{k}^{\prime}).

Since
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
is a left mutation, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})=\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau U)\cap Q^{\bot}.

On the other hand, since
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is a
\tau
-tilting pair and by Theorem
3.3
(i), we know that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})=[\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau U)\cap Q^{\bot}]\cap[\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M_{k}^{\prime})\cap Q_{k}^{\prime\bot}].

By Proposition
5.16
(i), we see that
C_{k}\in U^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{k}
. On the one hand, we know that

C_{k}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{k}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k})=\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau U)\cap Q^{\bot}.

On the other hand, we know that
C_{k}\in U^{\bot}
and thus
C_{k}\notin\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})
. Then by (
5.15
), we obtain that
C_{k}\notin\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M_{k}^{\prime})\cap Q_{k}^{\prime\bot}
.

By Proposition
5.16
(i), we know that
C_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}(U\oplus M_{k}^{\prime})
for
i\in I\setminus\{k\}
.
Then by (
5.15
), we get
C_{i}\in\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M_{k}^{\prime})\cap Q_{k}^{\prime\bot}
.

(ii) This follows from (i) and Corollary
5.11
.
∎

Definition 5.18

(Multi-semibrick)
.

A module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called a
multi-semibrick
if

M
has a decomposition
M\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}^{a_{i}}
such that each
C_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is a brick and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(C_{i},C_{j})=0
for any
i\neq j
.

For a module
M\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
, we define its
support set

\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(M)
as the set of iso-classes of indecomposable modules which appears as a direct summand of
M
. Clearly, if
M
is a multi-semibrick, then its support set
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(M)
is a semibrick.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
U
and
V
be two left finite modules in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, then
\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
.

Proof.

Since
U
and
V
are left finite modules, there are two basic
\tau
-tilting pairs
\mathcal{M}=(M,P)
and
\mathcal{N}=(N,Q)
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
.
Since
U\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M)\cap P^{\bot}
,
V\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N=\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau N)\cap Q^{\bot}
and
by Corollary
5.11
, we have
F_{U}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}}]=0
and
F_{V}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{N}}]=0
.

Since
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, we know that
F_{U}[{\bf r}]=F_{V}[{\bf r}]
for any
{\bf r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In particular, we have
F_{U}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{N}}]=F_{V}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{N}}]=0
and
F_{V}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}}]=F_{U}[{\bf g}_{\mathcal{M}}]=0
.

Then by Corollary
5.11
again, we see that
U\in\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau N)\cap Q^{\bot}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
and
V\in\prescript{\bot}{}{(}\tau M)\cap P^{\bot}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}
. Hence, we have
\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
.
∎

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
U
and
V
be two left finite multi-semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
.

Proof.

Since
U
and
V
are two left finite multi-semibricks, we know that
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
are left finite semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Since
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope and by Lemma
5.19
, we know that
\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
. Since the two left finite semibricks
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
generate the same torsion class
\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
and by Proposition
5.15
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
.
∎

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
U
and
V
be two left finite multi-semibricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. If
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, then
U\cong V
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.20
, we know that
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
. We can assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)
is non-empty. Otherwise,
U=V=0
and the desired result holds trivially.

Since
U
and
V
are left finite and
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
, we know that there exists a basic
\tau
-tilting pair
\mathcal{M}=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}=(M,Q)
such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\langle U\rangle_{\rm tors}=\langle V\rangle_{\rm tors}
. Since
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)
is non-empty, we know that
\{0\}\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
.

Let
I
be the subset of
[1,n]
such that
i\in I
if only if
\mu_{i}(\mathcal{M})
is a left mutation of
\mathcal{M}
. By Proposition
5.16
, we know that the bricks in
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(V)
are indexed by
I
. Say
\operatorname{\mathsf{supp}}(U)=\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Then we can assume that

U=\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i}^{a_{i}}
and
V=\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i}^{b_{i}}
, where
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Let
k\in I
. We know that
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}:=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\mathcal{M})
is a left mutation. By Lemma
5.17
(ii), we have

F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad F_{C_{i}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=0\quad\forall i\in I\setminus\{k\}.

Since
U=\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i}^{a_{i}}
and
V=\oplus_{i\in I}C_{i}^{b_{i}}
, we have
F_{U}=\prod_{i\in I}F_{C_{i}}^{a_{i}}
and
F_{V}=\prod_{i\in I}F_{C_{i}}^{b_{i}}
. Then by Corollary
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle F_{U}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}F_{C_{i}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=a_{k}F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]\quad\text{and}\quad F_{V}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}F_{C_{i}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=b_{k}F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}].

Since
U
and
V
have the same Newton polytope, we have

a_{k}F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{U}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=F_{V}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]=b_{k}F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}].

Since
F_{C_{k}}[{\bf g}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k}^{\prime}}]\neq 0
, we obtain
a_{k}=b_{k}
. As
k
is an arbitrary element in
I
, we have
a_{i}=b_{i}
for any
i\in I
. Thus
U\cong V
.
∎

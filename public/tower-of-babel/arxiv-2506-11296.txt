Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a space-time fractional partial differential equation with a reactive term. We describe the speed of invasion of its fundamental solution, extending recent results in this topic, which had been proved for the one dimensional spatial setting and some fractional parameters involved in the equation. The key tool to achieve these results in a wide range of cases (any spatial dimension, any time and spatial fractional differential parameters, and any polynomial speed of invasion) is the subordination.



1.
Introduction and main results

The main aim of this paper is the study of the speed of invasion of the fundamental solution of the following
non-local partial differential equation with a reactive term
:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\varrho}u(t,x)=u(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where the operator
(-\Delta)^{\varrho}
stands for the fractional laplacian of order
\varrho>0
, and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
denotes the time-fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
in thesense of Caputo, which for a smooth enough function, is defined by

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\,ds.

The left-side part of the equation (
1.1
) represents the classical fully nonlocal diffusion partial differential equation. The time fractional derivative provides the subdiffusive behaviour of the solution, and the spatial fractional laplacian the superdiffusive one. In essence, a nonlocal diffusive partial differential equation is an evolution equation that combines nonlocal diffusion operators in the space variable with time-fractional derivatives (and generalizations). In recent years, the investigation into this type of partial differential equations has received considerable attention. This interest is primarily driven by their profound connections with non-local transport phenomena, the control of stochastic jump processes, the depiction of anomalous diffusion in physics, and the analysis of memory effects in parabolic equations. While not exhaustive, interested readers are referred to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
26
,
21
,
13
,

,

]
, and the references therein.

The prototype of subdiffusive partial differential equations is the so-called
time-fractional heat equation

(1.2)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\Delta u=0.

For the well-posedness and regularity of the solutions of (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see, for example, the work of Eidelman and Kochubei in
[
13
]
; Allen, Caffarelli and Vasseur in
[
2
]
; and Kemppainen, Siljander and Zacher in
[
19
]
. Optimal asymptotic decay rates of the solutions have been obtained by Kemppainen, Siljander, Vergara, and Zacher in
[
18
]
, and more recently by Cortazar, Quiroz, and Wolanski in
[
9
]
. For the Dirichlet problem on bounded domains, Vergara and Zacher
[
29
]
show large time decay estimates of the solutions. Recently, Chan, Gomez-Castro, and Vázquez
[
7
]
studied the global well-posedness of (
1.2
) for singular solutions on bounded domains.

The previous paragraph makes clear that the study of this class of equations has reached a certain level of maturity. However, understanding the properties of solutions to subdiffusive partial differential equations, such as (
1.2
), with reactive terms remains comparatively less developed, with many basic questions still unresolved. The inclusion of reactive terms introduces considerable technical challenges, creating a
competition
among the effects of the diffusion operator, the reactive terms, and the
memory
induced by the time-fractional derivative.

In such a context, we highlight the work made by Dipierro, Pellacci, Valdinoci, and Verzini
[
12
]
who have studied the so-called
speed of invasion
of the solution of the following equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)=u(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\qquad u(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for the one dimensional case
d=1,
where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta distribution. Roughly speaking, the invasion speed of the fundamental solution of equation (
1.3
) serves to differentiate between whether the solution exhibits convergent or divergent behavior as the spatial variable
x
varies across space on spheres whose radius grows with time according to a function
\theta(t)
. More precisely, if
u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)
denotes the solution to equation (
1.3
), it becomes an intriguing problem to characterize the behavior of
u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)
on regions of the form
{|x|=\theta(t)}
, where
\theta
is an increasing unbounded function, and to analyze the asymptotics of
u_{\alpha,1}(t,\theta(t)\vec{v})
for large times. In
[
12
]
, the authors considered the function
\theta(t)=mt^{\beta}
for
m>0
and
\beta>0
, and derived the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d=1
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
u_{\alpha,1}
be the solution of (
1.3
),
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
m>0
. Then

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta})\geq m\alpha t^{\beta-2\alpha}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})\big(1-o(1)\big).

In particular, we have that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta})\to\infty\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Theorem
1.1
seems to reveal an important physical feature of the subdiffusion processes modeled by time-fractional operators: namely, the memory effect of the fractional derivative
slows down
the invasion rate compared to the classical case, in which diffusion occurs with a linear speed. This result establishes that such invasion still occurs, with a power-law time dependence as close as we wish to the square root function. Additionally, it opens up several very interesting research directions possed in
[
12
]
, which we address in this paper.

(Q1)

To find the
optimal speed
distinguishing the diverging from the vanishing behavior of the fundamental solution of (
1.3
), and determine the asymptotic behavior in the case
\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

(Q2)

To obtain a result in the spirit of Theorem (
1.3
) for the highly nonlocal regime
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.

(Q3)

To understand the asymptotic behavior of the fundamental solution of (
1.3
) in every spatial dimension
d\geq 2
, and not only the one-dimensional case.

The primary objective of this paper is to answer the questions mentioned above and enhance the understanding of the speed of invasion of the fundamental solution of the equation (
1.1
). In such a context, we present one of our main results.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma_{\alpha}:=(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
,
\displaystyle M_{\alpha}=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ n>\left(\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right\}
and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Let
u_{\alpha,1}
be the solution of (
1.3
). Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\beta\in(0,1)
and
m>0
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})=\infty.

(ii)

If
\beta>1
and
m>0
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})=0.

(iii)

If
\beta=1
and
0<m<2\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})=\infty.

(iv)

If
\beta=1
and
m>2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}
, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})=0.

Remark 1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on two key components: the so-called
subordination principle
in the sense of Prüss (refer to, e.g.,
[
25
, Chapter 4]
or
[
5
, Chapter 3]
), and the asymptotic expansions of the so-called
Wright functions
(see, for instance,
[
22
, Appendix F]
). Using the subordination principle, we derive a representation of the fundamental solution of (
1.3
) in terms of the Wright functions, and by a delicate analysis, we demonstrate that the asymptotic expansion of the Wright functions governs the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,1}
. This approach differs entirely from the one employed in
[
12
]
, enabling us to address the three questions mentioned above comprehensively, and generalizing the results to another evolution equations.

On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
establishes that the speed of invasion of
u_{\alpha,1}
does not depend on the parameter
\alpha
, except in the boundary case
\beta=1
. This observation leads to the next question:
What is the influence of the diffusion operator?
In such a context, we study the following equation:

(1.4)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\varrho}u(t,x)=u(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\qquad u(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x),

where the operator
(-\Delta)^{\varrho}
stands for the fractional Laplacian of order
\varrho>0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1),

\varrho>0
and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Let
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)
the solution to (
1.4
). The following statements hold.

(i)

If
\varrho\in(0,1)
,
m>0
and
\beta>0
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})=\infty.

(ii)

If
\varrho\in(1,\infty)
,
m>0
and
\beta>1
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})=0.

The statement
(i)
of Theorem
1.4
shows that a power-type function does not identify the optimal rate distinguishing the diverging and vanishing behavior of the fundamental solution of equation (
1.4
) when
\varrho\in(0,1)
. In such a context, we present the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Let
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
be the solution of (
1.4
) with
\varrho\in(0,1)
. Define
\theta_{m,\beta}(t)=(e^{mt^{\beta}}-1)
for some
m>0
and
\beta>0
. The following statements hold.

(i)

If
\beta\in(0,1)
and
m>0
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=\infty.

(ii)

If
\beta>1
and
m>0
then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=0.

(iii)

If
\beta=1
, and
0<m\leq\frac{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}{d+2\varrho}
, where
\gamma_{\alpha}=(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=\infty.

(iv)

If
\beta=1
and
m>\frac{1}{d+2\varrho}
, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=0.

Remark 1.6
.

The method developed to prove Theorem
1.4
does not provide information about the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
in the case to
\varrho>1
and
0<\beta<1
. The following result gives a partial answer to this problem in the one-dimensional case and
\beta\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2\varrho}\Big)

2.5

]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

. Let
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)
the solution to (

). If
d=1
,
m>0
, and
\beta\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2\varrho}\Big)

2.
Preliminaries.

In this section we summarize the most important preliminary results, concepts and definitions that we use throughout the paper. Along this paper, the Fourier transform of
v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is denoted by

\widetilde{v}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}v(x)\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

extended as usual to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the Schwartz space.

The Laplace transform of a subexponential function
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}f(t)\,dt,

whenever the last integral is convergent.

Asymptotic notation

In the context of asymptotic analysis of functions, we utilize the following classical notation. Let
f,g\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
two real functions. We say that
f=o(g)
as
x\to\infty
if

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0.

We use the notation
f=O(g)
as
x\to\infty
to indicate the existence of constants
M>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq Mg(x),\quad\text{for all }x>x_{0}.

Also, in this case we write
f\lesssim g.

Additionally, we write
f\sim g
as
x\to\infty
if

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1.

Mittag-Leffler function

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
. The two parameter Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha,\beta}
is defined by

(2.1)

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

In the case
\beta=1
, we will simply write
E_{\alpha}(z)
. Clearly, when
\alpha=1
and
\beta=1
, this function coincides with the exponential function. The Mittag-Leffler function is essential for the treatment of time-fractional evolution equations. There is a rich theory of Mittag-Leffler functions that generalizes semigroup theory, where the standard reference is the monograph by Mainardi
[
22
]
, see also
[
20
,
24
]
.

Next two lemmas can be found in
…
, and they will be applied along the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Derivative of Mittag-Leffler function)
For all
\alpha>0
we have that

\frac{d}{dz}E_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Asymptotic behavior of Mittag-Leffler function) Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then

E_{\alpha}(z)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)z}+O(z^{-2})\quad\text{as}\quad z\to-\infty,

E_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\exp\Big(z^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big)+O(z^{-1})\quad\text{as}\quad z\to\infty,

and

E_{\alpha,\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}z^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}e^{z^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}+O(z^{2}),\quad\text{as}\quad z\to\infty

The following results have been obtained by Dipierro, Pellacci, Valdinoci and Verzini, see
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and
r\geq 0
. Then

E_{\alpha}(r)\leq\alpha E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(r)+1-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}+\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(2\alpha)}\right)r.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and
r>0
. Then

E_{\alpha}(r)\geq\frac{\alpha}{r}E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)r}+1-\frac{1}{\Gamma(2\alpha)}.

In what follows we extend above results for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, not only for
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
. Such estimates are key points in the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n\in\{2,3,\cdots\}
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{n},1]
. Then

(2.2)

E_{\alpha}(r)\leq\alpha E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)+\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{3n-2}{2}\right]-1}\gamma_{k}r^{k},\quad r\geq 0,

where

\gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+\alpha k)}.

Proof.
Let
r\geq 0
. We note that

E_{\alpha}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}+\sum_{k=[\frac{3n-2}{2}]}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}

Since
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{n}
and
n\geq 2
, it follows that

1+\alpha k\geq\frac{3}{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq\left[\frac{3n-2}{2}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}+\sum_{k=[\frac{3n-2}{2}]}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha+\alpha k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}-\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha+\alpha k)}+E_{\alpha,\alpha}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+\alpha k)}\right)r^{k}+\alpha E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)

\displaystyle=\alpha E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)+\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{3n-2}{2}]-1}\gamma_{k}r^{k},

where

\gamma_{k}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha k)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+\alpha k)}.

This proves our claim.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\in\{2,3,\cdots\}
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{n},1]
. Then

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(r)\geq\frac{\alpha}{r^{n-1}}E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}r^{k}+\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}r^{k},

where

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)},\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}.

Proof.
Let
r>0
. Note that

\displaystyle r^{n-1}E_{\alpha}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k+(n-1)}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha)}=\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}.

On the other hand, if
k\geq(n-1)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}
we have that

\displaystyle\alpha+k\alpha\geq 1-\alpha(n-1)+k\alpha\geq 1-\alpha(n-1)+\left((n-1)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)\alpha=\frac{3}{2},

which in turn implies that

\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)\geq\Gamma(1-\alpha(n-1)+k\alpha),\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq(n-1)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}.

In consequence we have that

\displaystyle r^{n-1}E_{\alpha}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}+\sum_{k=[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]+1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}+\sum_{k=[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]+1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)}

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha,\alpha}(r)-\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)}+\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}

It follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle r^{n-1}E_{\alpha}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)-\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)}+\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\frac{r^{k}}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}

\displaystyle=\alpha E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r)-\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}r^{k}+\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}r^{k},

where

\lambda_{k}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+k\alpha)}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{k}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha-\alpha(n-1))}.

Our conclusion follows directly after dividing by
r^{n-1}
.

Wright functions

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. The Wright function
W_{\alpha,\beta}
is defined by

(2.3)

W_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

Now we provide a concise overview of various properties associated with these functions. In particular, we show that they are strongly related to Mittag-Leffler functions. For a comprehensive summary with abstract and applied results, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
,
[
15
, Appendix F]
and
[
17
, Section 3.2]
and the references therein. We recall some of such results in the following lines, that we will use along the paper.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta>0
. Then

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{zs}W_{-\alpha,\beta-\alpha}(-s)\,ds.

In particular, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have that

E_{\alpha}(-z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zs}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-s)\,ds.

Proposition 2.8
.

(Derivative of Wright functions)
Let
\alpha>-1
and
\beta>0

\frac{d}{dz}W_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=W_{\alpha,\alpha+\beta}(z),\quad z>0.

In particular, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have that

(2.4)

\frac{d}{dz}W_{-\alpha,1}(z)=W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(z),\quad z>0.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\nu>-1
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-r)r^{\nu}\,dr=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu\alpha+1)}.

Proposition 2.10
.

(Asymptotic behavior of Wright functions) Let
\nu\in(0,1)
and
\mu>0
then

\displaystyle W_{-\nu,\mu}(z)=Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\mu}e^{-Y}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A_{m}Y^{-m}+O(|Y|^{-M})\right),\quad\text{as}\quad|z|\to\infty,

where
Y=(1-\nu)(-\nu^{\nu}z)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}
and
A_{m}
are some real numbers.
In particular, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have that

\displaystyle W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})=(\gamma_{\alpha}t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}t}\left(A_{0}+O((\gamma_{\alpha}t)^{-1})\right),\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

where
\gamma_{\alpha}=(1-\alpha)\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
and
\displaystyle A_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}(1-\alpha)^{\alpha}\alpha^{2\alpha-1}}.

Above result was obtained by Wright (
[
30
]
) by means of the so-called
smoothing Stokes’ discontinuities method
, and independently by Mainardi and Tomirotti (
[
23
]
) who used the so-called
saddle point method
.

Remark 2.11
.

The asymptotic behavior of the Wright functions implies that for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
there are
C_{\alpha}>0
and
t^{\ast}>0
such that

W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})\geq C_{\alpha}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}t},\quad t>t^{\ast}.

Analogously, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
there are
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}>0
and
t^{\ast\ast}>0
such that

W_{-\alpha,1}(-2t^{1-\alpha})\geq C^{\prime}_{\alpha}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}2^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}t},\quad t>t^{\ast\ast}.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. There exist
\kappa>0
and
\sigma>0
such that

0<W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t)\leq\kappa e^{-\sigma t^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}},\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

In particular, we have that

W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t)<\kappa,\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

For a complete summary of the properties of the Wright functions and some applications, we refer the reader to
[
17
, Chapter 3]
and
[
22
, Appendix F]
. In particular, the proof of Proposition
2.12
can be found in
[
17
, Theorem 3.18]
.

3.
Proof of main results

In this section we prove our main results. Initially, we find a representation of the solution of (
1.4
) in terms of Wright functions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\varrho>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. The solution of the equation (
1.4
) is given by

(3.1)

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{1,\varrho}(s,x)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds,\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
u_{1,\varrho}
is the fundamental solution of

(3.2)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\varrho}u(t,x)=u(t,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and
W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}
is the Wright function given in (
2.3
).

Proof.
Taking the Fourier transform into the both sides of the equation (
1.4
), we have that

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widetilde{u}(t,\xi)+|\xi|^{2\varrho}\widetilde{u}(t,\xi)=\widetilde{u}(t,\xi),\quad t>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad\qquad\widetilde{u}(0,\xi)=1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore, the Fourier transform of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
is given by

\widetilde{u}_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\xi)=E_{\alpha}\big((1-|\xi|^{2\varrho})t^{\alpha}\big),\quad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
E_{\alpha}
is the Mittag-Leffler function defined in (
2.1
). It follows from Proposition
2.7
that

E_{\alpha}\big((1-|\xi|^{2\varrho})t^{\alpha}\big)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(1-|\xi|^{2\varrho})t^{\alpha}s}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-s)\,ds.

By a simple change of variables, we have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(1-|\xi|^{2\varrho})s}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds.

On the other hand, the Fourier transform of the fundamental solution
u_{1,\varrho}
of the equation
(
3.2
) is given by
\widetilde{u}_{1,\varrho}(t,\xi)=\exp\big((1-|\xi|^{2\varrho})t\big)
for
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, we have that

\widetilde{u}_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\xi)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{u}_{1,\varrho}(s,\xi)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds.

Our claim follows from the uniqueness of Fourier transform.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
u_{1,\varrho}
depends radially on the spatial variable, it follows from representation (
3.1
) that
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
also depends radially on the spatial variable. Therefore, we can consider an increasing function
\theta(t)
and analyze the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta(t)\vec{v})
.

Now we are in position to prove one of our main results.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

It is a well-known fact that when
\varrho=1
, then
u_{1,1}
the solution of the equation (
3.2
) is given by

u_{1,1}(t,x)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t}}}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{t},\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Thus, according to Lemma
3.1
, the fundamental solution of (
1.1
) is given by

(3.3)

u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{s}\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds,\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Now, we divide the proof into two main cases.

First, we face the cases
(i)
and
(iii)
getting lower bounds for the expression (
3.3
). Since all the functions involved in the integral of the right hand side of (
3.3
) are positive, we have that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)\geq\int_{t}^{2t}e^{s}\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}\ t^{-\alpha}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds,\qquad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since the mapping
s\mapsto e^{s}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}
is increasing and
s\mapsto\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}
is decreasing, it follows that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)\geq e^{t}\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t}}}{(8\pi t)^{d/2}}\int_{t}^{2t}\ t^{-\alpha}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds,\qquad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Further, by (
2.4
) one gets

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)

\displaystyle\geq e^{t}\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t}}}{(8\pi t)^{d/2}}\Big(W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})-W_{-\alpha,1}(-2t^{1-\alpha})\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{t}\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t}}}{(8\pi t)^{d/2}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})\left(1-\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-2t^{1-\alpha})}{W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})}\right),\qquad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By considering
x=mt^{\beta}\vec{v}
with
m>0
,
\beta>0
and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\geq e^{t}\frac{e^{-\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta}}{4t}}}{(8\pi t)^{d/2}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})\left(1-\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-2t^{1-\alpha})}{W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})}\right),\qquad t>0.

It follows from Proposition
2.10
that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-2t^{1-\alpha})}{W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})}=0.

Therefore, by Remark
2.11
we get

(3.4)

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\gtrsim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\exp\Big(t-\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-1}}{4}-\gamma_{\alpha}t\Big)\left(1-o(1)\right),\qquad t\to\infty.

Particularly, under assumptions in the case
(i),
we have
2\beta-1<1,
then from (
3.4
) follows that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

for any
m>0,
proving the assertion
(i)
.

In the case
(iii),

\beta=1
, and by (
3.4
) we have that

u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})\gtrsim t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\exp\Big(\big(1-\frac{m^{2}}{4}-\gamma_{\alpha}\big)t\Big)\left(1-o(1)\right),\qquad t\to\infty.

Therefore, if
m<2\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}
we have that
u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, which proves the assertion
(iii)
.

The second main case in the proof treats the assertions
(ii)
and
(iv).
In both items, we need to find accurate upper bounds of
u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)
using the expression (
3.3
). For this purpose, we fix
\beta\geq 1
and define
\delta=\frac{\beta+1}{2}
. Now we decompose
u_{\alpha,1}
as follows

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,1}(t,x)=\underbrace{t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}e^{s}\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds}_{I_{1}(t,x)}+\underbrace{t^{-\alpha}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}e^{s}\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds.}_{I_{2}(t,x)}

We analyze both terms separately in each one cases
(ii)
and
(iv)
. We begin estimating
I_{1}(t,x)
. By Proposition
2.12
we have that
W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{\alpha}s)<\kappa
for some
\kappa>0
. Hence,

I_{1}(t,x)\leq\kappa t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}e^{s}\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4\pi s)^{d/2}}\,ds\leq\kappa\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}}{\pi^{d/2}}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}\frac{e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}}{(4s)^{d/2}}\,ds,\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By making the change of variable
\sigma=\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}
, we obtain that

I_{1}(t,x)\leq\kappa\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\|x\|^{2-d}}{4\pi^{d/2}}\int_{\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t^{\delta}}}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma}\sigma^{\frac{d}{2}-2}\,d\sigma=\kappa\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\|x\|^{2-d}}{4\pi^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1,\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4t^{\delta}}\right),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the incomplete Gamma function. Considering
x=mt^{\beta}\vec{v}
with
m>0
,
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have that

I_{1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\leq\kappa\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}m^{2-d}t^{(2-d)\beta}}{4\pi^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1,\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4}\right),\quad t>0.

It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 2]
) that

\Gamma(s,\tau)\sim\tau^{s-1}e^{-\tau},\quad\text{as}\quad\tau\to\infty.

Hence, we have that

\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}m^{2-d}t^{(2-d)\beta}}{4\pi^{d/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1,\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4}\right)\sim\frac{4t^{-\alpha-\beta+1-(\beta+1)d/4}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}m^{2}}\exp\left(t^{\delta}-\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4}\right),\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Therefore, in the case
(ii)
(
\beta>1
) we have
\delta<\beta,
and it follows easily by previous estimate that
I_{1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\to 0
as
t\to\infty.
If we are under assumptions of the case
(iv),
that is
\beta=1
(
\delta=1
) and
m>2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}
, with
\displaystyle M_{\alpha}=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ n>\left(\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right\},
it follows that
M_{\alpha}\geq 2,
and then
m>2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}>2.
Then we also conclude that
I_{1}(t,mt\vec{v})\to 0
as
t\to\infty.

Finally, we focus on get an upper vanishing estimate for
I_{2}(t,x).
Observe that

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{-\alpha-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}e^{s}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}\ W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds=\frac{t^{-\alpha-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{nt^{\delta}}^{(n+1)t^{\delta}}e^{s}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4s}}\ W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{-\alpha-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{(n+1)t^{\delta}}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4(n+1)t^{\delta}}}\int_{nt^{\delta}}^{(n+1)t^{\delta}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{(n+1)t^{\delta}}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4(n+1)t^{\delta}}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-nt^{\delta-\alpha}),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where in the last inequality we have applied the relation (
2.4
). Considering
x=mt^{\beta}\vec{v}
with
m>0
,
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have that

I_{2}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\leq\frac{t^{-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{(n+1)t^{\delta}}e^{-\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4(n+1)}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-nt^{\delta-\alpha}),\quad t>0.

Applying again Proposition
2.10
, we have that

W_{-\alpha,1}(-y^{1-\alpha})\sim(Cy)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}y},\quad\text{as}\quad y\to\infty,

with
C>0,
which implies that for all
\varepsilon>0,
there is
y_{0}>0
such that

\left|\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-y^{1-\alpha})}{(Cy)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}y}}-1\right|<\varepsilon,\quad y>y_{0}.

In particular, if
y=n^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
, it follows that

\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-nt^{\delta-\alpha})}{C^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}}\leq\frac{W_{-\alpha,1}(-nt^{\delta-\alpha})}{(Cn^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)

for all
t>t_{0}:=\max\{y_{0}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\delta-\alpha}},1\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, for all
t>t_{0},
one gets

I_{2}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\lesssim\frac{\,t^{-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-n\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)t^{\delta}\Big)\right).

At this point, we distinguish the cases
(ii)
and
(iv).

For the case
(ii)
we have that

\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}t^{2\beta-\delta}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)t^{\delta}\geq\gamma_{\alpha}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-2t^{\delta}=:g(t),\quad t>t_{0},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\delta>1,

g(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty,
so

I_{2}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\lesssim\frac{\,t^{-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-ng(t)}=\frac{\,t^{-\frac{d\delta}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{1}{e^{g(t)}-1}\to 0,\ \text{ as }t\to\infty.

In the case
(iv),
recall that
\beta=1
and
m>2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}>2,
where

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ :\ n>\left(\frac{2}{\gamma_{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\right\}.

In such a context, we have that

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,mt\vec{v})\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{t^{-\frac{d}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=1}^{M_{\alpha}-1}\exp\left(-nt\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{t^{-\frac{d}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=M_{\alpha}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-nt\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big)\right),\quad t>t_{0}.

On one hand, it follows easily that
\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\geq 0
for all
1\leq n\leq M_{\alpha}-1,
therefore the first above term in the upper estimate of
I_{2}(t,mt\vec{v})
vanishes as
t\to\infty.
On the other hand, we have that

\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big)t\geq\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-2\Big)t=:h(t),\quad t>t_{0},\ n\geq M_{\alpha}.

Since
\gamma_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-2>0
one gets

\frac{t^{-\frac{d}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{n=M_{\alpha}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-nt\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4n(n+1)}-\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\Big)\right)\leq\frac{t^{-\frac{d}{2}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{e^{-h(t)M_{\alpha}}}{1-e^{-h(t)}}\to 0,\text{ as }t\to\infty,

which proves our claim.

Remark 3.3
.

We note that the speed of invasion of
u_{\alpha,1}
does not depend on the parameter
\alpha
, except in the boundary case where
\beta=1
. In such a case, we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})=\begin{cases}\infty,&m<2\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\alpha}},\\
0,&m\geq 2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}.\end{cases}

While our method does not provide information about the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,1}(t,mt\vec{v})
for
m
within the range

2\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}\leq m\leq 2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}},

this gap closes as
\alpha\to 1
. To clarify this, we observe that

\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}\gamma_{\alpha}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}M_{\alpha}=1,

which in turn implies

\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}2\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}=2\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}2M_{\alpha}\sqrt{1-\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}=2.

Thus, in the limiting case, we recover the classical result:

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{1,1}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{t-\frac{m^{2}}{4}t}=\begin{cases}\infty,&m<2,\\
0,&m\geq 2.\end{cases}

This observation confirms that the classical behavior of the heat equation is indeed reflected in our analysis as
\alpha
approaches to one.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{1,\varrho}(s,x)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})\,ds,\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
u_{1,\varrho}(t,x)
is the solution of (
1.4
) . Assume first that
\varrho\in(0,1)
. It follows from
[
16
, Chapter 3]
that there is a convolution semigroup
(\mu^{\varrho}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that

u_{1,\varrho}(t,x)=e^{t}\mu^{\varrho}_{t}(x).

It is well known that
\mu_{t}^{\varrho}(x)
is non-negative. Therefore, we have that

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\geq t^{-\alpha}\int_{t}^{2t}e^{s}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})\mu^{\varrho}_{s}(x)\,ds.

Despite the absence of explicit formulas for
\mu_{t}^{\varrho}(x)
, the asymptotic behavior is well understood, see e.g.,
[
6
, Section 2]
. More precisely, there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

C_{1}\frac{t}{(\|x\|^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{\varrho}})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}\leq\mu^{\varrho}_{t}(x)\leq C_{2}\frac{t}{(\|x\|^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{\varrho}})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}},\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This implies that

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\geq C_{1}t^{-\alpha}\int_{t}^{2t}e^{s}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})\,\frac{s}{(\|x\|^{2}+s^{\frac{1}{\varrho}})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}\,ds.

Since the mapping
s\mapsto se^{s}
is increasing and
s\mapsto\frac{1}{(\|x\|^{2}+s^{\frac{1}{\varrho}})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}
is decreasing, it follows that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}t^{-\alpha}\frac{t\,e^{t}}{((2t)^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}+\|x\|^{2})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}\int_{t}^{2t}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})\,ds\geq C_{1}\frac{t\,e^{t}}{((2t)^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}+\|x\|^{2})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha}),

where in the last inequality we have applied (
2.4
).
By considering
x=mt^{\beta}\vec{v}
with
m>0
,
\beta>0
and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
one gets

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\geq C_{1}\frac{t\,e^{t}}{((2t)^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}+m^{2}t^{2\beta})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})\qquad t>0.

Since
\gamma_{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and

W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})\sim(Ct)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\gamma_{\alpha}t},\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

with
C
a positive constant, we have that
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, which proves the assertion
(i)
.

In order to prove the assertion
(ii)
, assume that both
\varrho,\beta>1
and we decompose
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
as follows

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}u_{1,\varrho}(s,x)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds+t^{-\alpha}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}u_{1,\varrho}(s,x)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds,

where
\delta=\frac{\beta+1}{2}
. Therefore, by considering
x=mt^{\beta}\vec{v}\,
with
m>0
and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})|\leq I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t),

where

I_{1}(t)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})|u_{1,\varrho}(s,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})|\,ds,

and

I_{2}(t)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})|u_{1,\varrho}(s,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})|\,ds.

We analyze both terms separately. We start estimating
I_{1}(t)
. It is a well known fact that

u_{1,\varrho}(t,x)=e^{t}\nu^{\varrho}_{t}(x),

where
\nu^{\varrho}_{t}
is the unique function whose Fourier transform is given by
\widehat{\nu^{\varrho}_{t}}(\xi)=\exp(-t|\xi|^{2\varrho})
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
14
, Section 2]
) that

\nu^{\varrho}_{t}(x)=t^{-\frac{d}{2\varrho}}F(t^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho}}x),\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\displaystyle F(y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s^{2\varrho}}(s\|y\|)^{d/2}J_{(d-2)/2}(s\|y\|)ds
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
J_{(d-2)/2}
denotes the Bessel functions of first kind.

We point out that there is not closed formulas for the function
F
. However, it has been proved in
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
that for any dimension
d
there are constants
K>1
and
\omega>0
(depending on
d
and
\varrho
) such that

|F(y)|\leq Ke^{-\omega\|y\|^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where,
K=D\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-q(1+\|y\|^{2})^{\frac{\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}}\,dy
, for a some constants
D>1
and
q>0
. Hence, the previous considerations together Proposition
2.12
implies that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}s^{-\frac{d}{2\varrho}}|F(mt^{\beta}s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho}}\vec{v})|\,ds,

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}s^{-\frac{d}{2\varrho}}e^{-\omega(mt^{\beta}s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho}})^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}}ds,

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}s^{-\frac{d}{2\varrho}}e^{-\gamma s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}}\,ds,\quad t\to\infty,

where
\gamma=\gamma(m,t,\varrho,\beta):=\omega(mt^{\beta})^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}
and
C
denotes a positive constant different from line to line and independent on
t.

By making the following change of variables
u=\gamma s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}
, we note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{\delta}}s^{-\frac{d}{2\varrho}}e^{-\gamma s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}}\,ds

\displaystyle=(2\varrho-1)\gamma^{(2\varrho-1)(1-\frac{d}{2\varrho})}\int_{\gamma t^{-\frac{\delta}{2\varrho-1}}}^{\infty}u^{d\left(\frac{2\varrho-1}{2\varrho}\right)-2\varrho}e^{-u}\,du

\displaystyle=(2\varrho-1)\gamma^{(2\varrho-1)(1-\frac{d}{2\varrho})}\Gamma\left(d\left(\frac{2\varrho-1}{2\varrho}\right)-2\varrho+1,\gamma t^{-\frac{\delta}{2\varrho-1}}\right)

In consequence we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)\leq Ct^{-\alpha}e^{t^{\delta}}\gamma^{(2\varrho-1)(1-\frac{d}{2\varrho})}\Gamma\left(d\left(\frac{2\varrho-1}{2\varrho}\right)-2\varrho+1,\,\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}\right),\quad t>0,

with
C>0
independent on
t.
Note that
t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}\to\infty
as
t\to\infty,
since
\beta>\delta
and
\varrho>1,
so by using the asymptotic behavior of the incomplete Gamma function (see for example
[
8
, Section 2]
) we have that

\Gamma\left(d\left(\frac{2\varrho-1}{2\varrho}\right)-2\varrho+1,\,\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}\right)\sim(\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}})^{\tau-1}e^{-\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}},\quad t\to\infty,

where
\tau:=d\left(\frac{2\varrho-1}{2\varrho}\right)-2\varrho+1
. Hence,

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)\lesssim t^{-\alpha}(\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}})^{\tau-1}e^{-\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}+t^{\delta}},\quad t\to\infty.

Since
\delta<\beta
, it follows that

t^{\delta}-\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}\to-\infty\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

which implies that
I_{1}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{-\alpha}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})e^{s}|\nu^{\varrho}_{s}(mt^{\beta}\vec{v})|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\alpha}\int_{t^{\delta}}^{\infty}e^{s}e^{-\tilde{m}s^{-\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}t^{\frac{2\beta\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-st^{-\alpha})\,ds

for all
t>1,
with
C
a positive constant independent on
t
and
\tilde{m}:=\omega m^{\frac{2\varrho}{2\varrho-1}}
. Proceeding analogously to the proof of Theorem
1.2
(ii), it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-n\Big(\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)t^{\delta}+\frac{\tilde{m}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}}{n(n+1)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}}\Big)\right).

Since
\beta>1
, we have that
\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}>\delta,
so there is
t_{0}>1
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)t^{\delta}+\frac{\tilde{m}t^{\frac{2\varrho\beta-\delta}{2\varrho-1}}}{n(n+1)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho-1}}}>\gamma_{\alpha}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{\delta-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)t^{\delta}>0

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the same way as in Theorem
1.2
(ii)

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Proof of Theorem
1.7

We point out that when
\varrho>1
the fundamental solution
u_{1,\varrho}
of

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\varrho}u(t,x)=u(t,x),

is sign changing. This is a big obstacle to apply in a sharp way the techniques developed above to study the speed of invasion of (
1.4
), for example in the case of
u_{\alpha,\rho}(t,mt^{\beta}\vec{v})
with
\varrho>1
and
0<\beta<1
.

To overcome partially such a difficulty, we introduce several auxiliary results to face this problem. In a first place, by the inverse Fourier transform formula of a radial function, we note that
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
has the following representation

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}(1-\xi^{2\varrho}))\cos(x\xi)\,d\xi,\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
C
is a non-negative constant.

The oscillatory behavior of
\xi\mapsto\cos(x\xi)
in the preceding representation complicates the analysis of the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
. To address this, we state a generalization of
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
, which, in some way, separates the negative part of the solution from its positive part. We omit the proof since it is anologous to the case
\rho=1
(see
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\varrho\geq 1
. Then
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
has the following representation

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2x}}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}(1-\xi^{2\varrho}))\cos(x\xi)\,d\xi,

\displaystyle a_{k}(t,x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2x}(2k-1)}^{\frac{\pi}{2x}(2k+1)}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}(1-\xi^{\varrho}))\cos(x\xi)\,d\xi,\quad k\geq 1.

Further, the sequence
\{a_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{k\geq 1}
satisfies the conditions of the Leibniz Criteria for alternating series, that is, for every
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 0
, the following statements hold:

(a)

a_{k}(t,x)>0
,

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}(t,x)=0
,

(c)

a_{k+1}(t,x)<a_{k}(t,x)
,  for all
k\geq 1
,
and

2a_{0}(t,x)>a_{1}(t,x)
.

As a simple consequence of Lemma
3.4
we obtain the following result (we left the proof to the reader).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\varrho\geq 1
. For all
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)

\displaystyle>\sum_{k=0}^{2m+1}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(t,x),

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=0}^{2m}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(t,x).

In particular,

(3.5)

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)>a_{0}(t,x)-a_{1}(t,x).

Now, note that by means of the change of variable
r:=1-\xi^{2\varrho}
in the expressions given in Lemma
3.4
, the coefficients
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
can be rewritten as follows,

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varrho}\int_{1-(\frac{\pi}{2x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}r)\cos(x\sqrt[2\varrho]{1-r}\,)(1-r)^{\frac{1-2\varrho}{2\varrho}}\,dr,

\displaystyle a_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\varrho}\int_{1-(\frac{3\pi}{2x})^{2\varrho}}^{1-(\frac{\pi}{2x})^{2\rho}}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}r)\cos(x\sqrt[2\varrho]{1-r}\,)(1-r)^{\frac{1-2\varrho}{2\varrho}}\,dr.

In what follows, we look for a lower estimate for
a_{0}
and an upper estimate for
a_{1}
, in such a manner that
a_{0}(t,mt^{\beta})-a_{1}(t,mt^{\beta})\to\infty
as
t\to\infty,
for
\varrho\geq 1
and
0<\beta<1
. Since
u_{\alpha,\varrho}
satisfies (
3.5
), this will imply that

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta})\to\infty,\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty,

so the proof of Theorem
1.7
will be finished. For this purpose we prove the following auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
n\in\{2,3,\cdots\},

\rho\geq 1
and
\alpha\in\big[\frac{1}{n},1)
. If
0<\ell<\frac{\pi}{2}<x
, then there is a function
c_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

a_{0}(t,x)\geq\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha n}}\left[E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell}{x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)+c_{0}(t,x)\right],\quad t>0,

with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{c_{0}(t,mt^{\beta})}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=0,

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
m>0
.

Proof.
Let
x>\frac{\pi}{2}
and consider the function
h\colon[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

h(r):=\cos(x\sqrt[2\rho]{1-r}\,)(1-r)^{\frac{1-2\varrho}{2\varrho}},\quad r\in[0,1).

Since
\varrho\geq 1
, we have that
h
is increasing in the interval
\Big(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho},1\Big),
with
\ell<\pi/2.
Indeed, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}h(r)=\sin(x(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}})\frac{x}{2\varrho}(1-r)^{\frac{1}{\varrho}-2}-\cos(x(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}})\frac{1-2\varrho}{2\varrho}(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}-2}.

Since
0<x(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, we have that,

\displaystyle 0<\cos(x(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,0<\sin(x(1-r)^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}}),

which implies that
h^{\prime}(r)>0
provided that
r\in\Big(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho},1\Big)
. Also, by the choice of
\ell
, we have that
(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho},1)\subset(1-(\frac{\pi}{2x})^{2\varrho},1)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,x)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varrho}\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}r)\cos(x\sqrt[2\varrho]{1-r}\,)(1-r)^{\frac{1-2\varrho}{2\varrho}}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}r)dr.

It follows from Lemma
2.6
that

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,x)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{(t^{\alpha}r)^{n-1}}E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t^{\alpha}r)-\frac{1}{(t^{\alpha}r)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}(t^{\alpha}r)^{k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{(t^{\alpha}r)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}(t^{\alpha}r)^{k}\right)dr,

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha(n-1)}}E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t^{\alpha}r)-\frac{1}{(t^{\alpha}r)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}(t^{\alpha}r)^{k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{(t^{\alpha}r)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}(t^{\alpha}r)^{k}\right)dr.

By Fundamental Theorem of Calculus note that

\displaystyle\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha(n-1)}}E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(t^{\alpha}r)\,dr=\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha n}}\left[E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell}{x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)\right].

The other integral terms in the above lower estimate of
a_{0}(t,x)
only produce terms of polynomial or logarithmic order, and they are dominated asymptotically by the function
E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})
. Indeed, for
k\in\big\{0,1,\cdots,[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]\big\}
we have that

b_{k}(x):=\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}r^{k+1-n}dr=\begin{cases}\frac{1-(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}))^{k+2-n}}{k+2-n},&k+1-n\neq-1,\\
\\
-\ln\big|1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}\big|,&k+1-n=-1.\end{cases}

On the other hand, for
n-1\leq k\leq[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]
we have that

\int_{1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho}}^{1}r^{k+1-n}dr=\frac{1-(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho})^{k+2-n}}{k+2-n}.

Thus,

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,x)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha n}}\left(E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell}{x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)-\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}t^{\alpha k}b_{k}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}t^{\alpha k}\frac{1-(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{2\varrho})^{k+2-n}}{k+2-n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos(\ell)}{2\varrho}\left(\frac{x}{\ell}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha n}}\left[E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell}{x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)+c_{0}(t,x)\right],

where

\displaystyle c_{0}(t,x):=-\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}t^{\alpha k}b_{k}(x)+\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}t^{\alpha k}\frac{1-(1-(\frac{\ell}{x})^{s})^{k+2-{2\varrho}}}{k+2-n},

and the constants
\beta_{k}
and
\lambda_{k}
have been defined in Lemma
2.6
.
By the asymptotic behavior of Mittag-Leffler function (see Lemma
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}(t,mt^{\beta})}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=\frac{\displaystyle-\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\lambda_{k}t^{\alpha k}b_{k}(mt^{\beta})+\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\displaystyle\sum_{k=n-1}^{[n-1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}]}\beta_{k}t^{\alpha k}\frac{1-(1-(\frac{\ell}{mt^{\beta}})^{s})^{k+2-n}}{k+2-n}}{\frac{1}{\alpha}e^{t}+o(1/t^{\alpha})}=o(1),\ \text{as}\ t\to\infty,

which proves the result.

Let

\rho\geq 1

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
d=1
,
n\in\{2,3,\cdots,\}

\varrho\geq 1
. For all
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{n},1)
,
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2\varrho})
and

, we have

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta})>Cm^{2\varrho-1}t^{\beta(2\varrho-1)-\alpha n}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})[1-o(1)],

where
C_{1}=\frac{\alpha\ell^{2-2\varrho}}{2\varrho}
and
\ell\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
is the unique solution of
\cos(\ell)=\ell
. In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta})=\infty.

Proof.
Let
\ell\in\big(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\big)
be the unique solution of
\cos(\ell)=\ell
, and
x>\frac{\pi}{2}
. It follows from Lemma
3.5
, Lemma
3.6
, and Lemma
3.7
that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\geq{}

\displaystyle\ \ell^{2-2\varrho}\frac{x^{2\varrho-1}\alpha}{t^{\alpha n}2\varrho}\Biggl[E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})-E_{\alpha}\biggl(t^{\alpha}\bigl(1-\bigl(\tfrac{\ell}{x}\bigr)^{2\varrho}\bigr)\biggr)+c_{0}(t,x)\Biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{t^{\alpha}2\varrho}\left(\frac{2x}{\pi}\right)^{2\varrho-1}\Biggl[E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{3\pi}{2x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)-E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\pi}{2x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)-c_{1}(t,x)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\ \frac{x^{2\varrho-1}}{t^{\alpha n}}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})\Biggl[C_{1}-C_{1}\frac{E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell}{x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}+C_{1}\frac{c_{0}(t,x)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}

\displaystyle+C_{2}\frac{t^{\alpha(n-1)}E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{3\pi}{2x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}-C_{2}\frac{t^{\alpha(n-1)}E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\left(\frac{\pi}{2x}\right)^{2\varrho}\right)\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}

\displaystyle-C_{2}\frac{t^{\alpha(n-1)}c_{1}(t,x)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}\Biggr],

where

for some large enough

3.6

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}(t,mt^{\beta})}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=o(1),\quad\frac{t^{\alpha(n-1)}c_{1}(t,mt^{\beta})}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=o(1),\quad t\to\infty.

\displaystyle\frac{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\ell^{2\varrho}m^{-2\varrho}t^{-2\varrho\beta})\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{t((1-\ell^{2\varrho}m^{-2\varrho}t^{-2\varrho\beta})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})}+o(1)}{e^{t}+o(1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{t(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\ell^{2\varrho}m^{-2\varrho}t^{-2\varrho\beta})}+o(1)}{e^{t}+o(1)}

\displaystyle=e^{-(\frac{1}{\alpha}\ell^{2\varrho}m^{-\varrho}t^{-2\varrho\beta+1})}+o(e^{-t}),

as

0<\beta<\frac{1}{2\varrho}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\ell^{2\varrho}m^{-2\varrho}t^{-2\varrho\beta})\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=o(1),\quad t\to\infty.

Analogously, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2\varrho}
,

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\alpha(n-1)}E_{\alpha}\left(t^{\alpha}\left(1-\varepsilon t^{-2\varrho\beta}\right)\right)}{E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})}=t^{\alpha(n-1)}e^{-\varepsilon t^{-2\varrho\beta+1}}=o(1),\quad t\to\infty.

Therefore, for all
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2\varrho}
one gets

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta})\geq C_{1}m^{2\varrho-1}t^{\beta(2\varrho-1)-\alpha n}E_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha})[1-o(1)],\quad t\to\infty.

In particular, by the asymptotic behavior of Mittag-Leffler function (Lemma
2.2
),
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,mt^{\beta})\to\infty
as
t
goes to infinity.

Remark 3.9
.

As we have mentioned before, in the particular case
n=1
and
\varrho=1
, Theorem
3.8
has been obtained by Dipierro, Pellacci, Valdinoci and Verzini
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
.

4.
Case
\varrho\in(0,1)
revisited

As we have mentioned in the first section, the statement
i)
in Theorem
1.4
suggests that a power-type function does not provide enough information for determining the optimal speed that distinguishes between the diverging and vanishing behavior of the fundamental solution of equation (
1.1
) in the case
\varrho\in(0,1)
. To overcome this difficulty, we consider the function
\theta_{m,\beta}(t)=(e^{mt^{\beta}}-1)
for some
m>0
and
\beta>0
. The subtraction of
1
allows to the function
\theta
to start from
0
at
t=0
. By choosing appropriate values for
m
and
\beta
, we can potentially control the speed and manner in which
\theta_{m,\beta}
grows, allowing for a more refined understanding of the behavior of solutions to the equation in question. This approach may lead to insights that cannot be easily obtained by using power-type functions alone, particularly when dealing with equations involving fractional powers (
\varrho\in(0,1)
in this case).

Proof of Theorem
1.5

According to Lemma
3.1
, the fundamental solution of (
1.1
) is given by

u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{s}\ \mu^{\varrho}_{s}(x)W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)ds,\quad t>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
(\mu^{\varrho}_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is the convolution semigroup associated to the symbol
\xi\mapsto\|\xi\|^{\varrho}
. It is well known, see, e.g.
[
16
, Section 3]
, that the Fourier transform of
\mu^{\varrho}_{s}
is given by

\widetilde{\mu}^{\varrho}_{s}(\xi)=e^{-s\|\xi\|^{2\varrho}},\quad s>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

As we have mentioned before, there is not closed formulas for
\mu_{s}^{\varrho}
except in the cases
\varrho=1
and
\varrho=\frac{1}{2}
. However, in any case there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(4.1)

C_{1}\frac{t}{\Big(\|x\|^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}\Big)^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}\leq\mu_{t}^{\varrho}(x)\leq C_{2}\frac{t}{\Big(\|x\|^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}\Big)^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad t>0.

By a similar argument to the proof of Theorem
1.2

(i)
, the positivity and the lower bounds of
\mu_{t}^{\varrho}
given in (
4.1
) imply that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\gtrsim\frac{te^{t}}{((2t)^{1/\varrho}+\|x\|^{2})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}W_{-\alpha,1}(-t^{1-\alpha})(1-o(1)),\qquad t\to\infty.

Considering
x=\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v}
and the asymptotic behavior of the Wright functions, we have that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\gtrsim\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{t-\gamma_{\alpha}t}}{((2t)^{1/\varrho}+(e^{mt^{\beta}}-1)^{2})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}(1-o(1)),\qquad t\to\infty.

Moreover, taking
t>0
sufficiently large such that
e^{mt^{\beta}}>e^{mt^{\beta}}-1>(2t)^{1/\varrho}
, the last inequality implies that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)\gtrsim t^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{t(1-t^{\beta-1}m(d+2\varrho)-\gamma_{\alpha})}(1-o(1)),\qquad t\to\infty.

Therefore, it follows that if
\beta<1
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=\infty.

Furthermore, in the case
\beta=1
, we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,\theta_{m,\beta}(t)\vec{v})=\infty
, provided that

1-m(d+2\varrho)-\gamma_{\alpha}\geq 0,

which is equivalent to the condition
0<m\leq\frac{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}{d+2\varrho},
and the statement
(iii)
follows.

For parts
(ii)
and
(iv)
, we consider the upper bound of the inequalities (
4.1
) and the Taylor expansion of the exponential function, to get

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{s}\frac{s}{(s^{1/\varrho}+\|x\|^{2})^{\frac{d+2\varrho}{2}}}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{k+1}}{k!}W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-t^{-\alpha}s)\,ds.

We denote
\Phi(r):=W_{-\alpha,1-\alpha}(-r)
. Using Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.9
, we have that

\displaystyle u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\|x\|^{n+2\varrho}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{k+1}}{k!}\Phi(st^{-\alpha})\,ds=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{k+1}\Phi(st^{-\alpha})\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{\alpha})^{k+1}}{k!}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k+1}\Phi(u)\,du=\frac{1}{\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{\alpha})^{k+1}}{k!}\frac{\Gamma(k+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha(k+1)+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{\alpha})^{k+1}}{k!}\frac{(k+1)!}{\alpha(k+1)\Gamma(\alpha(k+1))}=\frac{1}{\alpha\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{\alpha})^{k+1}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(t^{\alpha})=\frac{t^{\alpha}}{\alpha\|x\|^{d+2\varrho}}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{1-\alpha}e^{t}+O(t^{2\alpha})\right).

If
x=(e^{mt^{\beta}}-1)\vec{v}
, for
\beta>1
and some unitary vector
\vec{v}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,(e^{mt^{\beta}}-1)\vec{v})=0.

For the case
\beta=1,
the statement
(iv)
follows providing that
m(d+2\varrho)>1
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that, similar to Theorem
1.2
, in the case
\beta=1
, there is a gap where the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,(e^{mt}-1)\vec{v})
remains unknown. Unlike the previous scenario, this gap depends not only on the parameter
\alpha
but also on the dimension
d
and on the order
\varrho
, where the gap decreases as
d
increases, as well as,
\alpha\to 1^{-}
. In such a case, we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,(e^{mt}-1)\vec{v})=\begin{cases}\infty,&m\leq\frac{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}{d+2\varrho},\\
0,&m>\frac{1}{d+2\varrho}.\end{cases}

While our method does not provide information about the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\alpha,\varrho}(t,(e^{mt}-1)\vec{v})
for
m
within the range

\frac{1-\gamma_{\alpha}}{d+2\varrho}<m\leq\frac{1}{d+2\varrho},

this gap closes as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
.
Thus, in the limite case, we recover the classical result

\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{1,\varrho}(t,(e^{mt}-1)\vec{v})=\begin{cases}\infty,&m\leq\frac{1}{d+2\varrho},\\
0,&m>\frac{1}{d+2\varrho}.\end{cases}

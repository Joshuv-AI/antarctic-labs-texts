Degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials and degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with a random variable
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. This paper has two primary contributions. First, we explore degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials, a hybrid of higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials, and derive their properties. Second, assuming that the moment generating function of Y Y exists in a neighborhood of the origin, we introduce the degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with Y Y . We then investigate their properties in general and for the specific cases of uniform and Bernoulli random variables. We also present new results for the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials.

11B68; 11B83; 60-08

1.
Introduction

This paper explores two main areas. First, we delve into degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials, a hybrid of higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials. Second, we examine degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with a random variable
Y
, which are a degenerate form of the Appell polynomials associated with
Y
, as previously investigated in
[
2
,
20
,
22
]
. It’s worth noting that the study of various degenerate versions of special polynomials and numbers, originated by Carlitz’s work in
[
4
,
5
]
, has seen a renewed surge of interest among mathematicians in recent years (see
[
3
,
7
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
23
]
). We assume that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin (see (
24
)).

The paper is structured as follows:

\bullet
Section 1 reviews degenerate exponentials, degenerate Bernoulli polynomials, and degenerate Euler polynomials. We also briefly remind the reader about uniform and Bernoulli random variables.

\bullet
Section 2 investigates higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials, along with Sheffer-type polynomials. After presenting several properties of the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials (see Proposition 2.1, Theorems 2.3, 2.4, 2.7), we introduce the degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials
T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)
(see (
18
)). These polynomials represent a hybrid form of the aforementioned higher-order polynomials (see Theorems 2.5, 2.6, 2.8).

\bullet
Section 3 introduces the degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with
Y
, denoted as
S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
(see (
25
)). We derive several properties for these polynomials:

\bullet
In a general context (see Theorems 3.1, 3.2).

\bullet
For a uniform random variable on
[0,1]
(see Theorems 3.3, 3.5, 3.6).

\bullet
For a Bernoulli random variable with the success probability
\frac{1}{2}
(see Theorems 3.4, 3.7).

Furthermore, Theorem 3.8 establishes an identity for
T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)
by utilizing the Bernoulli random variable with the success probability of
\frac{1}{2}

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)
in Theorem 3.9, and for the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials in Theorems 3.10 and 3.11. For general references pertaining to this paper, please consult
[

For any nonzero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by (see
[
7
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
23
]
)

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k,\lambda}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),

where

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\ (n\geq 1).

Note from (
1
) that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}.

We also observe that

(2)

(x+y)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(x)_{k,\lambda}(y)_{n-k,\lambda}.

The degenerate Bernoulli polynomials are defined by (see
[
13
,
15
]
)

(3)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0,\ \beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(0)
are called the degenerate Bernoulli numbers.

From (
3
), we note that

(4)

\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

The degenerate Euler polynomials are given by (see
[
13
,
15
]
)

(5)

\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0,\ \mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(0),\ (n\geq 0)
, are called the degenerate Euler numbers.

By (
5
), we get

(6)

\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}(x)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note from (
3
) and (
5
) that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=B_{n}(x),\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)=E_{n}(x),

where
B_{n}(x)
and
E_{n}(x)
are respectively Bernoulli polynomials and Euler polynomials, respectively given by (see
[
6
,
12
,
18
]
)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We recall that
Y
is the uniform random variable on
[a,b]
, denoted by
Y\sim U(a,b)
, if the probability density function of
Y
is given by (see
[
14
,
19
]
)

f_{Y}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{b-a},&\textrm{if $x\in[a,b]$},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Note that

E\big[g(Y)\big]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)f_{Y}(y)dy=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}g(y)dy,

where
g
is a real-valued function.

Also,
Y
is the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
, denoted by
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)
, if the probability mass function of
Y
is given by (see
[
14
,
19
]
)

p(0)=p\{Y=0\}=1-p,\quad p(1)=p\{Y=1\}=p.

Note that

E\big[g(Y)\big]=(1-p)g(0)+pg(1).

2.
Degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials

By (
2
) and (
4
), we have

(7)

\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)_{n-k,\lambda}=(\beta+x)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),

with the understanding of replacing
(\beta)_{n,\lambda}
by
\beta_{n,\lambda}
.
The degenerate Bernoulli numbers satisfy the recurrence relation (see (
3
), (
7
)) as follows:

(8)

(\beta+1)_{n,\lambda}-\beta_{n,\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\textrm{if $n=1$}\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,\quad\beta_{0,\lambda}=1.

From the recurrence relation (
8
), we derive

\displaystyle\beta_{0,\lambda}=1,\ \beta_{1,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda,\ \beta_{2,\lambda}=\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{6}\lambda^{2},\ \beta_{3,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{4}\lambda+\frac{1}{4}\lambda^{3},

\displaystyle\beta_{4,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{30}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda^{2}-\frac{19}{30}\lambda^{4},\ \beta_{5,\lambda}=\frac{1}{4}\lambda-\frac{5}{2}\lambda^{3}+\frac{9}{4}\lambda^{5},\dots.

From (
2
) and (
6
), we note that

(9)

\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}(x)_{n-k,\lambda}=\big(\mathcal{E}+x\big)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),

with the understanding of replacing
(\mathcal{E})_{n,\lambda}
by
\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}
.
The degenerate Euler numbers satisfy the recurrence relation (see (
5
), (
9
)) as follows:

(10)

\big(\mathcal{E}+1\big)_{n,\lambda}+\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2,&\textrm{if $n=0$}\\
0,&\textrm{if $n\neq 0$}\end{array}\right.,\quad\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}=1.

By using the recurrence relation (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}=1,\ \mathcal{E}_{1,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{2},\ \mathcal{E}_{2,\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda,\ \mathcal{E}_{3,\lambda}=\frac{1}{4}-\lambda^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{4,\lambda}=-\frac{3}{2}\lambda+3\lambda^{3},\ \mathcal{E}_{5,\lambda}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{35}{4}\lambda^{2}-12\lambda^{4},\dots.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli polynomials are defined by

(11)

\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0,\ \beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the higher-order degenerate Bernoulli numbers.

The higher-order degenerate Euler polynomials are given by

(12)

\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0,\ \mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the higher-order degenerate Euler numbers.

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}B_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x),\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x),

where
B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
and
E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
are the higher-order Bernoulli and higher-order Euler polynomial, respectively given by (see
[
18
]
)

\bigg(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\bigg)^{\alpha}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\bigg(\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}\bigg)^{\alpha}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
11
) and (
12
), we note that

(13)

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(0)}(x)=(x)_{n,\lambda}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(0)}(x)=(x)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

The following proposition is immediate from the definitions (
11
) and (
12
).

Proposition 2.1
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a+b)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\beta_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y),

and

\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(a+b)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y).

From Proposition 2.1 and (
13
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.2
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)(y)_{n-k,\lambda},

and

\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)(y)_{n-k,\lambda}.

Now, we observe that

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x+1)-\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=t\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{\alpha-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x+1)+\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=2\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{\alpha-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
14
) and (
15
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x+1)-\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=n\beta_{n-1,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x),

and

\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x+1)+\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=2\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha-1)}(x).

From (
3
) and (
5
), we note that

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)+1\big)e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big(\beta_{k,\lambda}(1)+\beta_{k,\lambda}\Big)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Observe that
\beta_{k,\lambda}(1)=\beta_{k,\lambda}+\delta_{k,1}
(see (
8
)), where
\delta_{k,1}
is the Kronecker’s delta. By (
16
), we get

(17)

\displaystyle\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big(2\beta_{k,\lambda}+\delta_{k,1}\Big)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x).

Therefore, by (
17
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)=\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x).

Now, we consider the
degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials
given by

(18)

\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

The following proposition follows immediately from the definition (
18
).

Proposition 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y),

and

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(0)(x)_{n-k,\lambda}.

From (
18
) and (
3
), we have

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a-1}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(0)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\beta_{l,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(0)\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Similarly to (
19
), by (
18
) and (
5
), we get

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b-1}\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(0)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{l,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(0)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
19
) and (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(0)\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(0)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x).

Now, we observe that

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2t}{e_{\lambda}^{2}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{2t}{e_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}(2t)-1}e_{\frac{\lambda}{2}}^{\frac{x}{2}}(2t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\frac{\lambda}{2}}\bigg(\frac{x}{2}\bigg)2^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
21
),we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

2^{n}\beta_{n,\frac{\lambda}{2}}\bigg(\frac{x}{2}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(x).

From (
18
), we note that

(22)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x+1}(t)-\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=t\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)T_{n,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(x)\frac{t^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nT_{n-1,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(23)

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+1)-T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)=nT_{n-1,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(x).

3.
Degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with a random variable

Assume that
Y
is a random variable whose moment generating function exists in some neighborhood of the origin (see
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
21
,
23
]
), that is,

(24)

E\Big[e^{Yt}\Big]<\infty,\quad(|t|<r),\quad\textrm{for some $r>0$}.

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

Y^{(k)}=Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad Y^{(0)}=0.

Now, we define the
degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with
Y
,

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
, by

(25)

\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\Big[S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+Y)\Big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=E\bigg[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+Y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg]

\displaystyle=E\bigg[\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{Y+x}(t)\bigg]=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]}E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

E\big[S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+Y)\big]=(x)_{n,\lambda}.

Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be independent random variables. Then we have

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}(x+y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}(t)\big]}e^{(x+y)t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(t)\big]E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{2}}(t)\big]}e^{xt}e^{yt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}S_{j,\lambda}^{Y_{2}}(y)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(x)S_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y_{2}}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In particular, for
y=0
and
Y_{2}=0
, we get

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(0)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(x)_{l,\lambda}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(0)(x)_{n-k,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
27
) and (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, let
Y_{1},Y_{2}
be independent random variables. Then we have

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(x)S_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y_{2}}(y).

In particular, for
y=0
and
Y_{2}=0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{k,\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(0)(x)_{n-k,\lambda}.

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
, we have

(29)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]=\int_{0}^{1}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)dy=\frac{\lambda t}{\log(1+\lambda t)}\frac{1}{t}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big).

Thus, by (
29
), we have

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\lambda t}\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{l}l!}{l+1}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-k}(n-k)!}{n-k+1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
30
), we obtain the following theorem,

Theorem 3.3
.

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
, we have

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\frac{(-\lambda)^{n-k}(n-k)!}{n-k+1},\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
. Then we have

(31)

E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}e_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}{2}.

By (
31
), we get

(32)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
, and
n\geq 0
, we have

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x).

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
29
), we have

(33)

\displaystyle E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(t)\Big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{m}}(t)\Big]=\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{m}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\log(1+\lambda t)}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}{t}\bigg)^{m}\bigg(\frac{\lambda t}{\log(1+\lambda t)}\bigg)^{m}.

By (
33
), we get

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(t)\big]}=\bigg(\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\lambda t}\bigg)^{m}\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{m}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{m!}{\lambda^{m}}\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(j,m)\lambda^{j}\frac{t^{j-m}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(m)}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{S_{1}(j+m,m)\lambda^{j}}{\binom{j+m}{m}}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(m)}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n-k+m,m)\lambda^{n-k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{n-k+m}{m}}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(m)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
S_{1}(j,m)
are the Stirling number of the first kind defined by

\frac{1}{k!}\log^{k}(1+t)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

Therefore, by (
34
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

For
Y\sim U[0,1]
, and
m,n\geq 0
, we have

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\cfrac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{n-k+m}{m}}\lambda^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k+m,m)\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(m)}(x).

Let
m,l
be integers with
m\geq l\geq 0
. Now, by (
25
) and (
26
), we observe that

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\Big[S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}\big(x+Y^{(l)}\big)\Big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(t)\big]}E\Big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(l)}}(t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}-Y^{(l)}}(t)\big]}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}-Y^{(l)}}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
35
), we get

(36)

E\Big[S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}\big(x+Y^{(l)}\big)\Big]=S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}-Y^{(l)}}(x),\quad(n\geq 0,\ m\geq l\geq 0).

Therefore, by (
36
) and Theorem 3.5, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, and
Y\sim U[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{n-k+m}{m}}\lambda^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k+m,m)E\bigg[\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(m)}\bigg(x+Y^{(l)}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{n-k+m-l}{m-l}}\lambda^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k+m-l,m-l)\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(m-l)}(x),

where
m,l
are integers with
m\geq l\geq 0
.

For
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
, we have

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(t)\big]}=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{m}}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{1}}(t)\big]\cdots E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y_{m}}(t)\big]}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{m}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(m)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(m\geq 0).

By (
37
), we get

(38)

S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(m)}(x).

Therefore, by (
36
) and (
38
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

For
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
, and
n\geq 0
, we have

E\bigg[S_{n,\lambda}^{Y^{(m)}}\bigg(x+Y^{(l)}\bigg)\bigg]=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(m-l)}(x),

where
m,l
are integers with
m\geq l\geq 0
.

For
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
, we have

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\Big[T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+Y)\Big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=E\bigg[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+Y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)\frac{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}\bigg)^{a}\bigg(\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}\bigg)^{b-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by Theorem 2.5, we get

(40)

\displaystyle E\big[T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+Y)\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(m,l)}(0)E\big[(x+Y)_{n-k,\lambda}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}T_{k,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(0)\Big((x+1)_{n-k,\lambda}+(x)_{n-k,\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+1)+T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)\Big).

From (
39
) and (
40
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(41)

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+1)+T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)\Big),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

From (
23
) and (
41
), we derive the following theorem.

Theorem 3.9
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(42)

T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x)=T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(x)-\frac{n}{2}T_{n-1,\lambda}^{(a-1,b)}(x),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

By Theorem 2.5 and (
42
), we get

(43)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)=T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b)}(x+y)=T_{n,\lambda}^{(a,b-1)}(x+y)-\frac{n}{2}T_{n-1}^{(a-1,b)}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y).

From (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\bigg[\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)+\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\bigg]\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
b=1
in Theorem 3.10. Then we have

(44)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)(y)_{n-k,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\bigg[\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)+\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\bigg]\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(y).

Thus, by (
44
) and Corollary 2.2, we get

(45)

\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\bigg[\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)+\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\bigg]\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(y).

Now, we observe that

(46)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)=\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(y).

Let
a=1
in (
46
). Then, by Corollary 2.2, we have

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(0)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}(x)_{k,\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{n-1-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(x+y).

By Theorem 3.10 with
a=1
and (
47
), we get

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(0)}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(x+y).

From (
48
), we note that

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big),

where
n\geq 0
.

Thus, for
n\geq 1
, from (
49
) we have

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n-1,\lambda}^{(b)}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{2}{k}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{k,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{2}{k+1}\binom{n-1}{k}\beta_{n-1-k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k+1,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{k+1,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big).

Therefore, by (
45
) and (
50
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.11
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{n,\lambda}^{(a)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\bigg[\beta_{k,\lambda}^{(a)}(x)+\frac{k}{2}\beta_{k-1,\lambda}^{(a-1)}(x)\bigg]\mathcal{E}_{n-k,\lambda}(y),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}^{(b)}(x+y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{2}{k+1}\beta_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\Big(\mathcal{E}_{k+1,\lambda}^{(b-1)}(y)-\mathcal{E}_{k+1,\lambda}^{(b)}(y)\Big).

4.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied a hybrid of higher-order degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials, namely the degenerate Sheffer-type polynomials. Then, we examined a degenerate form of the Appell polynomials associated with a random variable
Y
, namely the degenerate Sheffer polynomials associated with
Y
.

We notice from (
25
) and (
31
) that, for
Y\sim\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)
, we have

\frac{2}{e_{\lambda}(t)+1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

so that
S_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{Ber}(1/2)}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n,\lambda}(x)
, the degenerate Euler polynomials in (
5
).

While, for
Y\sim U[0,1]
, we observe from (
25
) and (
29
) that

(51)

\frac{\log(1+\lambda t)}{\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n,\lambda}^{U[0,1]}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

so that
S_{n,\lambda}^{U[0,1]}(x)
are the ‘degenerate Bernoulli polynomials arising from Volkenborn integral’ (see
[
9
]
). These polynomials are different from the Carlitz degenerate Bernoulli polynomials
\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)
in (
3
) and (
4
). However, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, both
\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)
and
S_{n,\lambda}^{U[0,1]}(x)
tend to the Bernoulli polynomial
B_{n}(x)
. For details on this, the interested reader may refer to
[
9
]
.

It is one of our future research projects to continue to explore various aspects and applications of degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers.

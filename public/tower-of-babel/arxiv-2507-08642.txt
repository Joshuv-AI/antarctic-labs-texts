Abstract
. We prove a root stack valuative criterion for good moduli space maps and for gerbes for reductive groups under some mild assumptions on the residue characteristic. We give several applications to parahoric extension for torsors, rational points on stacks, gerbes and homogeneous spaces, and the geometry of fibrations.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to prove the following root stack valuative criterion for good moduli spaces.

.

Let

be a good moduli space where

be a DVR with fraction field

and residue field

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

and dotted arrows filling in the diagram. Moreover, if

maps to the closed point of

, then one can choose an extension such that also the closed point of

maps to the closed point of

Good moduli spaces for algebraic stacks were introduced by Alper in his thesis
[
Alp13
]
, and are a generalization both of coarse moduli spaces for Deligne-Mumford stacks in characteristic
0
, and of morphisms, appearing in the context of GIT, of the form
[W(\mathcal{L})^{ss}/G]\to W/\!\!/_{\mathcal{L}}G
, where
W
is a projective variety with an action of a linearly reductive group
G
,
\mathcal{L}
is a
G
-linearized ample line bundle on
W
and

is its semistable locus. On a first approximation, these are morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to X
which are of the form
[\operatorname{Spec}(A)/\GL_{N}]\to\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\GL_{N}})
étale locally on
X
rather than Zariski locally, as in classical GIT
[
AHR25
, Theorem 6.1]
. Good moduli space maps if they exist are universal for maps to algebraic spaces and have very strong topological and cohomological properties. They are a fundamental tool in the study of algebraic stacks with positive dimensional stabilizers.

The key difference between the root stack valuative criterion
Theorem

1.1
and the usual existence part of the valuative criterion for a good moduli space
[
AHLH23
, Theorem A.8]
is that both the fraction field and the residue field of

are preserved by the extension
\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
. This allows for many arithmetic applications (Section
1.1
) and for globalizing the valuative criterion from a DVR to a global curve
C
(
Proposition

1.5
below). For Deligne-Mumford stacks over
\mathbb{C}
, the analogous valuative criterion has been used extensively in the theory of twisted stable maps
[
AV02
,
ACV03
]
and fibered surfaces
[
AB19
]
. Recently it was generalized by Bresciani and Vistoli
[
BV24a
]
to tame stacks in any characteristic and appears in numerous applications including to heights of rational points on tame stacks
[
ESZB23
,
BPS24
,
DY22
]
, resolution of indeterminacy for rational maps to stacks
[
Jeo25
]
, and classification of singular fibers in fibrations
[
Rey25
]
.

Beyond the realm of algebraic stacks with finite inertia, partial progress was made in
[
DLI22
, Theorem 3.9]
and
[
DLI24
, Theorem 1.2]
. For the stack of
K
-semistable Fano varieties and of boundary polarized Calabi-Yau pairs, a proof of properness that shows one only needs to adjoin a root of the uniformizer of

was given in
[

]

respectively, though not necessarily that this can be done

for related results in the context of motivic integration.

The fact that

has a good moduli space is used to reduce to the case that

is a gerbe for a smooth connected linearly reductive group scheme but in that case we prove the following result which in positive and mixed characteristic is more general than the setting of a good moduli space. Recall that a group scheme

Remark 1.2

We can factor the resulting morphism

through its relative coarse moduli space

Theorem 1.3

Let

1.1.1.
Torsors and parahoric extension

Consider the special case of
Theorem

1.3
where
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{B}G
is the classifying stack of a reductive group over a root stack of
R
. We say that a reductive group has tame Weyl group if its Weyl group has order coprime to the residue characteristics (Definition
4.1
). Note that some condition on the group is necessary as illustrated by Example
4.3
. Then Theorem
1.3
implies that every
G
-torsor over
K
is pulled back from some
G
-torsor over some root stack of
\operatorname{Spec}R
. In fact the order of the root stack necessary is also uniformly bounded in terms of
G
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
G\to\sqrt[m]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
be a group over a root stack, with
G
reductive and with tame Weyl group. Then there exists an integer
n
depending only on
G
such that the natural map

\underline{\Hom}(\sqrt[nm]{\operatorname{Spec}R},\mathcal{B}G)\to\underline{\operatorname{H}}^{1}(K,G)

is essentially surjective where
\underline{\Hom}
denotes the groupoid of maps and
\underline{\operatorname{H}}^{1}
denotes the groupoid of torsors.

Note that if
R
is strictly henselian, the groupoid of
G
-torsors on
\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
is equivalent to that of
G
-torsors over
\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k}
. Thus we get an essentially surjective functor

\underline{\Hom}(\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k},\mathcal{B}G)=\left[\Hom(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n,k},G)/G\right]\to\underline{\operatorname{H}}^{1}(K,G)

where the action of
G
is by conjugation. By
[
MT15
, Lemma 7.1]
, all such homomorphisms come from co-characters of
G
over the residue field
k
.

Remark 1.10
.

Compare
Corollary

1.9
with
[
RS22
, Lemma 3.5(d)]
.

Remark 1.11
.

The kernel of the natural map in
Corollary

1.9
should be related to the
\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}
-points of the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
over
K
(see
[
GWZ25
, Theorem 3.17]
).

One of the main interests in
Proposition

4.10
is that it can be interpreted as a parahoric extension theorem for
G
-bundles on
K
. Indeed by work of many authors
[
BS15
,
PR24
,
Dam24
,
BČE+25
]

[
SSW24
, Section 5]
, a reductive group over a root stack
\mathcal{R}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
is equivalent data to a parahoric model
\mathcal{G}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
of
G_{K}
and the category of parahoric
\mathcal{G}
-torsors over
R
is equivalent to the category of
G
-torsors on
\mathcal{R}
. Thus combining
Proposition

2.10
with

and

, we get the following parahoric exension result.

Proposition 1.12
.

Suppose

is as in

extends to a parahoric bundle over

. The same holds if

1.5
and its function field respectively.

Example 1.13
.

Suppose
R
has residue characteristic
\neq 2
and let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
be a smooth conic which corresponds to a map
\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathcal{B}PGL_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be the closure of
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{R}
. Then the central fiber is either a smooth conic, a doubled line, or a union of two lines. In the first case, the
PGL_{2}
-torsor extends to
R
. In the second case, the normalization of the pullback of
\mathcal{C}
to
\sqrt[2]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
has good reduction and thus the torsor extends to the second root stack. In the third case, the total space has an
A_{n}
singularity at the node of the central fiber for some
n\geq 0
(where by convention we an
A_{0}
singularity is a regular point). If
n=0
, blowing up the node and contracting the strict transform of the central fiber yields a model with good reduction so we are in the first case. If
n=1
, blowing up the node and contracting the strict transform of the central fiber yields a model with a double line so we are in the second case. For
n\geq 2
, blowing up the node and contracting the strict transform of the central fiber yields a new central fiber which is still a union of two lines but now has an
A_{n-2}
singularity. Repeating this operation puts us back in either the first or the second case depending on the parity of

. Thus for
\mathcal{B}\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
in odd characteristic, any torsor over
K
extends up to a degree
2
root stack.

1.1.2.
The Lang-Nishimura Theorem for Artin stacks

Following
[
BV24a
]
, we give a generalization of Lang-Nishimura to Artin stacks with a good moduli space. We expect that this will have applications to questions on essential dimension, fields of definition and fields of moduli for Artin stacks as in e.g.
[
BV24b
,
BRV11
]
.

Proposition 1.14

(Lang–Nishimura theorem)
.

Let
S
be a scheme and
\mathcal{X}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
a rational map of algebraic stacks
over
S
, with
\mathcal{X}
locally noetherian and integral and
\mathcal{Y}
admitting a good moduli space
\mathcal{Y}\to Y
which is proper over
S
. Let

k
be a field,
s:\operatorname{Spec}k\to S
a morphism. Assume that
s
lifts to a regular point

\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{X}
; then it also lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{Y}
.

The following is a straightforward corollary.

Corollary 1.15
.

Suppose that a linearly reductive and special group
G
acts on an affine scheme
\operatorname{Spec}A
and that the ring of invariants
A^{G}
is an
R
-algebra. Assume also that
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes_{R}K
has a
K
-point,
and the composition
\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A^{G}
extends to
\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}A^{G}
. Then
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes_{R}k
has a
k
-point.

1.1.3.
Gerbes and homogeneous spaces

Next we give some applications of Theorem
1.3
to gerbes and homogeneous spaces over DVRs which may be viewed as a version of Grothendieck-Serre for gerbes and non-principal homogeneous spaces.

Proposition 1.16
.

Let
G\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be a reductive group which is either special or has tame Weyl group. Then any class
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(R,G)
which is trivial over
\Spec K
becomes trivial after passing to a root stack. In particular, if
\mathcal{G}|_{K}=0
then
\mathcal{G}|_{k}=0
.

As a special case, we can consider a homogeneous space
V
for a group
H
which yields a gerbe
[V/H]
for the stabilizer group.

Corollary 1.17
.

Let
H\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be a special affine group scheme. Let
V\to\operatorname{Spec}R
be an affine homogeneous space for
H
and suppose that the geometric stabilizer groups are reductive groups with tame Weyl group. If
V(K)\neq\emptyset
then
V(k)\neq\emptyset
.

As unirationality for homogeneous spaces is equivalent to admitting a rational point, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.18
.

Unirationality specializes along DVRs for homogeneous spaces for special reductive groups with reductive geometric stabilizer with tame Weyl group.

1.1.4.
Applications to fibrations

Suppose

is a stack with a proper good moduli space which parametrizes a class of projective varieties, for example

Let

]

.

Let

1.2.
Conventions.

By reductive group scheme
G\to S
we mean, as in
[
Con14
, Definition 3.1.1]
, a smooth
S
-affine group scheme, such that the geometric fibers are connected reductive groups. When we don’t specify, the cohomology will be the fppf cohomology.
We will pass between an algebraic stack over a base scheme
B
to the corresponding category fibered in groupoids over
\mathcal{S}ch(B)
without mentioning it specifically; we expect that this will not cause any confusion. Whenever we mention the site of an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
, it will be the flat-fppf site: its object are flat morphisms
B\to\mathcal{X}
from a scheme, and a covering of an object is an fppf-covering. All algebraic stacks will be noetherian, unless mentioned otherwise. All algebraic stacks in the body of the paper will have affine diagonal. The
n^{th}
root stack of a DVR
R
, denoted
\sqrt[n]{\operatorname{Spec}R}
or
\mathcal{R}_{n}
, is the algebraic stack
[\operatorname{Spec}R[t^{1/n}]/\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}]
where
t
is a uniformizer of
R
.

2.
Groups, torsors and gerbes on algebraic stacks

In this section we develop some preliminary results on reductive groups and their torsors and gerbes over an algebraic stack. We also refer the reader to
[
BIMS25
]
where this material will be developed in more depth.

2.1.
Reductive groups over a stack

We begin with the following

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack. A group
G
over
\mathcal{X}
is a sheaf on
\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{fppf}}
which is a sheaf in groups, and such that there is a fppf-cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
such that the sheaf
G|_{U}
is represented by a scheme over
U
. We say that
G
is a smooth (resp. separated, of finite type, fppf) group over
\mathcal{X}
, if there is an fppf-cover as above such that
G|_{U}
is represented by a smooth (resp. separated, of finite type, fppf) group scheme over
U
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G\to\mathcal{X}
be a smooth separated group over an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
. We say that
G
is reductive (resp. linearly reductive, a torus) if for every fppf-cover
B\to\mathcal{X}
from a scheme
B
, the pull-back
G\times_{\mathcal{X}}B\to B
is a reductive (resp. linearly reductive, a torus)
B
-group.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G\to\mathcal{X}
a reductive group over a tame algebraic stack as above, with connected fibers. Then there is an algebraic stack
\mathcal{T}
, with a smooth affine and representable morphism
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{X}
such that for every morphism
f:B\to\mathcal{X}
, there is an equivalence

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{X}}(B,\mathcal{T})\longleftrightarrow\{\text{maximal tori of }G\times_{\mathcal{X}}B\}.

Proof.

Note that
B\mapsto\{\text{maximal tori of }G\times_{\mathcal{X}}B\}
is an fppf sheaf. Indeed, the restriction of this functor to
B_{\operatorname{fppf}}
is represented by the scheme
\Tor_{G_{B}/B}\to B
, as proven in
[
Con14
, Theorem 3.2.6]
. Thus,
\mathcal{T}
is a sheaf over
\mathcal{X}
. Then to prove representability, it is enough to show
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{X}
is affine. For this, we may look fppf locally, where we can assume
\mathcal{X}
is a scheme; we then apply
[
Mil13
, XII, 5.4]
.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack, and

T\to\mathcal{X}
a torus over
\mathcal{X}
which fppf-locally
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
. Then the sheaf

\underline{\Isom}_{\operatorname{grp}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\times\mathcal{X},T)
over
\mathcal{X}_{\operatorname{fppf}}
, that sends
U\to\Isom_{\operatorname{grp}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}(U),T(U))
, is represented by an algebraic stack, which is étale over
\mathcal{X}
but not noetherian for
n>1
.

Proof.

We see that
\underline{\Isom}_{\operatorname{grp}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\times\mathcal{X},T)
is a
\GL_{n,\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathcal{X}}^{n})
-torsor. Since
\GL_{n,\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Z})
is étale over
\mathcal{X}
, it follows that

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a reductive group over a field

acts on

. Then there exists a
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}
-invariant maximal torus of

Proof.

If

is a torus there is nothing to prove, so we will assume

is not a torus. Suppose

has characteristic exponent

and write

where

is coprime to

[

, Theorem

]

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{a}}

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}\cong\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{a}}\times\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}

2.2.
Gerbes and torsors over stacks

We recall some background on torsors, gerbes and bands. Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack and
G/\mathcal{X}
be a group stack as in the previous section.

Definition 2.8
.

A
G
-
torsor
over
\mathcal{X}
is an fppf sheaf
P\to\mathcal{X}
with an action
G\times_{\mathcal{X}}P\to P
for which there exists an fppf cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
such that
P_{U}\cong G_{U}
with the left action of
G_{U}
. A
gerbe
over
\mathcal{X}
is an fppf stack
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
for which there exists an fppf cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
and a group
G_{U}\to U
such that
\mathcal{Y}_{U}\cong\mathcal{B}G_{U}
where
\mathcal{B}G_{U}
is the classifying stack of
G_{U}\to U
.

We refer to
[
Gir65
, Chapter IV]
for background on bands which we recall here; see also
[
EHKV01
, Section 3.1]
. Let
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{X}
be a gerbe which is trivialized after an fppf cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
and suppose that the group
G_{U}
is pulled back from a group stack
G\to\mathcal{X}
. Over
U\times_{\mathcal{X}}U
we have descent data for
BG_{U}
which is a section of the stack
\Isom_{U}(\mathcal{B}G_{U},\mathcal{B}G_{U})=[\Aut(G_{U})/G_{U}]
where
G_{U}
acts by conjugation. Passing to
\pi_{0}
yields a section of
\Out(G_{U})
over
U\times_{\mathcal{X}}U
satisfying the cocyle condition, i.e. an element of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
. Note that there is a natural map
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Aut(G))\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
and that elements of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Aut(G))
classify
\mathcal{X}
-forms of
G
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{X}
be a gerbe
as above. The
band
of
\mathcal{G}
is the element
\Band(\mathcal{G})\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
described above. Similarly, if
G^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
is a form of
G
, we define the band of
G^{\prime}
as the image
\Band(G^{\prime})\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
of the class of
G^{\prime}
under the natural map
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Aut(G))\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
. We say
\mathcal{G}
is banded by
G^{\prime}
if
\Band(\mathcal{G})=\Band(G^{\prime})
and call
\mathcal{G}
a
G^{\prime}
-gerbe.

When
G
is abelian,
G
-torsors and
G
-gerbes are classified by fppf cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},G)
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}(\mathcal{X},G)
respectively (e.g.
[
Ols16
, Theorems 12.1.5 & 12.2.8]
). When
G
is non-abelian, we follow
[
Gir65
]
and define
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},G)
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}(\mathcal{X},G)
respectively as the set of isomorphism classes of
G
-torsors and
G
-gerbes on
X
. The following is a generalization of
[
Dou75
, Ch. V, sect. 3.2, p. 75]
to stacks.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{X}
be a gerbe over an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
. Suppose that there exists an fppf cover
U\to\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{G}|_{U}\cong\mathcal{B}G_{U}
where
G_{U}\to U
is reductive and
U
is connected. Then there exists a reductive group
G\to\mathcal{X}
and a form
G^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
of
G
such that
\Band(G^{\prime})=\Band(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Since
U
is connected, the type of
G_{U}\to U
is constant
[
DGA+11
, XXII Section 2]
. Let
G_{0}\to\Spec\mathbb{Z}
be the Chevalley group of this type, that is, the unique split reductive group with the given root data
[
Con14
, Theorem 6.1.17]
. After possibly refining
U
, we may assume that
G_{U}\cong G_{0}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}U
. Let
G:=G_{0}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{X}
. Then
\Band(\mathcal{G})\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Out(G))
. On the other hand, for the Chevalley model,
\Aut(G_{0})\to\Out(G_{0})
splits
[
Con14
, Theorem 7.1.9 (3)]
. Fix such a splitting. This induces a splitting
\Aut(G)\to\Out(G)
over
\mathcal{X}
and thus a lift of
\Band(G)
to an element of
H^{1}(\mathcal{X},\Aut(G))
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the corresponding
\mathcal{X}
-form of
G
. This is our desired group scheme.
∎

3.1.
Passing to the polystable locus

We first show that it suffices to prove Theorem

Lemma 3.1

Let

For perfect fields this follows from

In characteristic

Thus it suffices to check that every cocharacter closed orbit of the

is contained in the unique closed orbit of

. We refer the reader to
[

]
for the definition of cocharacter closed and cocharacter closure. Toward this end, let

of

is linearly reductive so its connected component is a torus

and under the isomorphism with

•

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
is an isomorphism over

•

the dimension of the stabilizers of the geometric points of

•

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
admits a good moduli space which is obtained by a sequence of blow-ups of

Since

so without loss of generality we may assume

is reduced. By
[

, Proposition B.2]
and its proof (especially
[

, Proposition B.6]

) we can factor

and

, Proposition 3.13]

Note that by construction the

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}

over

As for the moreover part, observe that at each saturated blow-up in the algorithm of Edidin and Rydh

4.
Gerbe case

The goal of this section is to prove the root stack valuative criterion for gerbes banded by a reductive group. When the residue characteristic is finite, we need a tameness assumption on the reductive group.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be a reductive group over a stack

. We say

has tame Weyl group
if for all geometric points

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a henselian DVR with fraction field

be a root stack of
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Let
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{R}
be a gerbe banded by a reductive group

which is either special or has tame Weyl group. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
admits a

-point
\phi:\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathcal{G}
. Then
\phi
extends to a section
\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{G}
up to replacing
\mathcal{R}
with a further root stack.

The following example illustrates that some assumption on

is necessary.

Example 4.3

Let

be a mixed characteristic

henselian DVR with imperfect residue field

The proof will proceed as follows. First we will prove that, up to replacing

with a further root stack, the gerbe

4.1.

Quantising Chiral Bosons On Riemann Surfaces Preprint: Imperial-TP-2025-CH-5
Chris Hull, and Neil Lambert

Abstract
Sen’s action in two dimensions governs a chiral boson coupled to a two-dimensional metric together with a second chiral boson that couples to a flat two-dimensional metric. This second scalar decouples from the physical degrees of freedom. The generalisation of this action to one in which the second chiral scalar couples to an arbitrary second metric is used to formulate the theory on an arbitrary two-dimensional manifold. We use this action with both metrics Riemannian (or complex) to formulate the path integral on any Riemann surface. We calculate the partition function in this way and check the result with that calculated using canonical quantisation, and then extend this to multiple chiral bosons. The partition function for chiral scalars taking values on a rational torus is a sum of terms, each of which is the product of two holomorphic functions, one a function of the modulus of the first metric and the other a function of the modulus of the second metric. In particular, for the case of chiral bosons moving on a torus defined by an even self-dual lattice, the partition function is a single product of two such holomorphic functions, not a sum of such terms. This is applied to the heterotic string to give a world-sheet action whose quantisation is modular invariant and free from anomalies. We discuss modular invariance for the moduli of both metrics and the extension to higher genus Riemann surfaces.

for some constants

This arises in an interesting way. A central role in our analysis is played by an effective action for a non-chiral scalar coupled to a complex metric

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}

\displaystyle=e^{2\sigma^{\prime}}(dx+\rho dy)\left(dx+\tau dy\right),

(6)

with moduli
\rho,\tau
.
We quantise the scalar coupled to this complex metric by doing the partition function in regions of the
\rho,\tau

Chiral bosons arise in
a number of different situations.
On 2-dimensional Minkowski space we can have chiral bosons which are non-compact (taking values on a line) or compact (taking values on a circle). For fields on a 2-dimensional cylinder, as arise in closed string theory, a chiral boson with non-trivial momentum can only be achieved for compact bosons, and moreover the radius
R
of the target space circle must satisfy

(in units in which the string tension is

, i.e.
\alpha^{\prime}=1
). For multiple chiral bosons, they must take values in a rational torus. This will be discussed in detail later.
However,
the bi-metric chiral boson action is defined for any 2-dimensional space and any flat target space, either Euclidean space, or a torus which does not need to be rational, and the resulting partition function can have complicated dependence on both
\tau
and
\rho
. However, for the case of a rational torus
the theory simplifies and the partition function is a sum of a finite number of terms, each of which factorises into a function of
\tau
times a function of
\rho
.

One of our main motivations is in the application to string theory where one would want to study chiral bosons on an arbitrary Riemann surface. The bi-metric action is well-suited for this and we study both the torus case and higher-genus Riemann surfaces here. We will discuss the applications to heterotic string theory correlators elsewhere.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In section 2 we discuss the action in detail and evaluate it in the special case in which the metrics

and

are given by (
1
) and (
2
) respectively. In section 3 we turn to the quantum theory. We first discuss the canonical quantization, generalised to a complex metric, and obtain the partition function. We then compute in detail the path integral over a torus to find the same expression. In section 4 we discuss modular invariance and what the ranges of the parameters
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau,\tilde{\tau}
must be to ensure convergence of the integrals in the path integral. In section 5 we extend our results to multiple chiral scalars and show how to obtain a partition function that factorises in to a simple product as in (
4

2
The Action

2.1
The Action For Chiral Gauge Fields

The Sen action
Sen:2015nph
;
Sen:2019qit
is defined in
4k+2
dimensions. It’s generalisation in the presence of a source
J
and coupled to a general background metric is
Hull:2023dgp

\displaystyle S=-\int\Big(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}dP\wedge\bar{\star}dP-2Q\wedge dP-(Q+J)\wedge{\cal M}(Q+J)

\displaystyle+2Q\wedge J-\frac{1}{2}J\wedge\bar{\star}J\Big).

(7)

Here
P
is a
2k
-form and
Q
is a
(2k+1)
-form which is self-dual with respect to a Lorentzian-signature metric
\bar{g}
;
Q=\bar{\star}Q
. Furthermore,
{\cal M}
is linear map constructed to take
\bar{\star}
-self-dual forms to
\bar{\star}
-anti-self-dual forms and in particular satisfies:

\displaystyle{\cal M}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}(1-\bar{\star}){\cal M}\left(\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{\star})Q\right).

(8)

Its role is to ensure that

\displaystyle Q+{\cal M}(Q)=\star\left(Q+{\cal M}(Q)\right),

(9)

where
\star
is the Hodge dual associated to a second, physical metric
g
that is independent of
\bar{g}
. For the general construction of
{\cal M}
see
Sen:2019qit
;
Andriolo:2020ykk
;
Hull:2023dgp
.

The equations of motion of this action take the form

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=d\left(\bar{\star}dP+2Q\right)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-dP+\bar{\star}dP-2{\cal M}(Q+J)+(1-\bar{\star})J.

(10)

We define

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=Q+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\bar{\star})J+{\cal M}(Q+J)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=Q+\frac{1}{2}(1+\bar{\star})dP,

(11)

so that
F
is self-dual with respect to
g
while
G
is self-dual with respect to
\bar{g}
:

F=\star F,\qquad G=\bar{\star}G.

(12)

The equations of motion can be rewritten as

\displaystyle dG

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle dF

\displaystyle=dJ.

(13)

In this way, we find two dynamical form fields,
one self-dual with respect to the one of the metrics and the other self-dual with respect to the other metric.
However, only
F
couples to the source and to the physical metric
g
. While the source
J
is kept arbitrary here,
in other examples it takes on an explicit form in terms of various matter fields, for example in terms of other form fields and fermions in type IIB supergravity.

2.2
The action For Chiral Scalars

In this paper we will focus on the simplest example of two-dimensions, where
P
is a scalar and
Q
a 1-form.
It is helpful to use a zweibein frame where (see the appendix for our conventions and notation)

\displaystyle{\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle={\bar{e}}_{\mu}{}^{a}{\bar{e}}_{\nu}{}^{b}\eta_{ab}

\displaystyle={\bar{e}}_{\mu}{}^{+}{\bar{e}}_{\nu}{}^{-}+{\bar{e}}_{\mu}{}^{-}{\bar{e}}_{\nu}{}^{+}.

(14)

In this case one sees that the self-duality condition on
Q
simply becomes

\displaystyle Q=Q_{+}\bar{e}^{+},

(15)

so the 1-form
Q
has only one non-vanishing component

Q_{+}(x)
.
Furthermore, in such a basis we must have that

\displaystyle{\cal M}(Q_{+}\bar{e}^{+})=M_{--}Q_{+}\bar{e}^{-},

(16)

so the symmetric tensor
M_{\mu\nu}
has only one non-vanishing component,

M_{--}(x)
.

Rescaling the action (
2.1
) by the string tension we find

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\rm det}(\bar{e})\Big(-\partial_{+}P\partial_{-}P-2Q_{+}\partial_{-}P-(Q_{+}+J_{+})^{2}M_{--}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+2Q_{+}J_{-}+J_{+}J_{-}\Big)d^{2}x,

(17)

where
\partial_{\pm}=\bar{e}_{\pm}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
.
The field strengths with
J=0
are

G_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{+}P+Q_{+},\qquad F_{+}=Q_{+},\qquad F_{-}=M_{--}Q_{+},

(18)

and
G_{-}=0
,
F=\ast F
,
G_{+}=\partial_{+}C
,
F_{\pm}=\partial_{\pm}A
.

As discussed in the introduction, for a given metric
g
we will choose
\bar{g}
so that
M_{--}(x)
is finite and non-zero for all
x
.
We then find that the action (
14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\rm det}(\bar{e})\Big(-\partial_{+}P\partial_{-}P+M_{--}^{-1}\partial_{-}P\partial_{-}P

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73918pt-M_{--}(Q_{+}+J_{+}+M_{11}^{-1}(\partial_{-}P-J_{-}))^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73918pt+2(J_{+}-M_{11}^{-1}J_{-})\partial_{-}P-J_{+}J_{-}+M_{--}^{-1}J_{-}^{2}\Big)d^{2}x,

(19)

with the notation
\partial_{\pm}P=\bar{e}_{\pm}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}P
.
Shifting

and
J
to define

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}_{+}=Q_{+}+J_{+}+M_{--}^{-1}(\partial_{-}P-J_{-}),

(20)

and

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{+}=J_{+}-M_{--}^{-1}J_{-},

(21)

the action becomes

S=S_{eff}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\rm det}(\bar{e})M_{--}{Q^{\prime}_{+}}^{2}d^{2}x,

(22)

where

\displaystyle S_{eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\rm det}(\bar{e})\Big(\partial_{+}P\partial_{-}P-M_{--}^{-1}\partial_{-}P\partial_{-}P-2{\cal J}_{+}\partial_{-}P+{\cal J}_{+}J_{-}\Big)d^{2}x.

(23)

Thus we see that the action splits into an effective action involving the scalar
P
alone, coupled to sources, together with a simple quadratic action in the field
Q^{\prime}_{+}
. The action for
Q^{\prime}_{+}
involves no derivatives, so that
Q^{\prime}_{+}
is an auxiliary field.

2.3
Evaluating
M_{--}

Following
Hull:2023dgp
, to construct
{\cal M}
it is helpful to introduce
G_{ab}

such that

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{e}_{\mu}{}^{a}\bar{e}_{\nu}{}^{b}G_{ab}.

(24)

We see that
{\det G}=-{\det g}/\det\bar{g}
and this is non-vanishing so we can introduce the inverse
G^{ab}
. This allows us to write

\displaystyle(\star F)_{a}

\displaystyle=\bar{e}_{a}{}^{\mu}(\star F)_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-{\det G}}\epsilon_{ab}G^{bc}F_{c}.

(25)

From here we find

\displaystyle\star F_{+}=\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{-+}F_{+}+\sqrt{-G}G^{--}F_{-}

\displaystyle\star F_{-}=-\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{+-}F_{-}-\sqrt{-G}G^{++}F_{+}.

(26)

Thus if
\star F=F
then

\displaystyle F_{+}=\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{-+}F_{+}+\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{--}F_{-}

\displaystyle F_{-}=-\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{+-}F_{-}-\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{++}F_{+},

(27)

from which we find

\displaystyle F_{-}=\frac{1-\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{-+}}{\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{--}}F_{+},

(28)

and

\displaystyle F_{-}=-\frac{\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{++}}{1+\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{+-}}F_{+}.

(29)

These two expressions are equal since
G^{-+}=G^{+-}
and
G^{++}G^{--}-G^{-+}G^{+-}={\det G}^{-1}
.

In our case we have
F=Q+{\cal M}(Q)
so that
F_{+}=Q_{+}
and
F_{-}=M_{--}Q_{+}
. Therefore demanding that
F
is self-dual with respect to
\star
we read off that

\displaystyle M_{--}=\frac{1-\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{-+}}{\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{--}}=-\frac{\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{++}}{1+\sqrt{-{\det G}}G^{+-}}.

(30)

Note that for a two-by-two matrix we have

\displaystyle G^{ab}=\frac{1}{{\det G}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G_{--}&-G_{-+}\\
-G_{+-}&G_{++}\end{array}\right),

so

\displaystyle M_{--}=-\frac{\sqrt{-{\det G}}-G_{+-}}{G_{++}}=\frac{G_{--}}{\sqrt{-{\det G}}+G_{-+}}.

(33)

To continue, we
choose a parameterisation of the two-dimensional metric
\bar{g}
and
give express
M_{--}
in terms of this parameterisation. Without loss of generality, we can parameterise the metric in terms of three functions
\sigma(x),\rho(x),\tilde{\rho}(x)
as

\displaystyle\bar{g}=e^{2\sigma}(dx+\rho dy)(dx+\tilde{\rho}dy).

(34)

A choice of zweibein is

\displaystyle\bar{e}^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma}(dx+\rho dy),\qquad\bar{e}^{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma}(dx+\tilde{\rho}dy).

(35)

Then
\det\bar{g}=-(\det\bar{e})^{2}
with

\displaystyle\det\bar{e}=\frac{1}{2}e^{2\sigma}(\tilde{\rho}-\rho).

(36)

Similarly for the physical metric we write

\displaystyle g=e^{2\omega}(dx+\tau dy)(dx+\tilde{\tau}dy),

(37)

for other functions
\omega(x),\tau(x),\tilde{\tau}(x)
.
In this case we take

\displaystyle\bar{e}^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\omega}(dx+\tau dy),\qquad\bar{e}^{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\omega}(dx+\tilde{\tau}dy),

(38)

and find
\det g=-(\det e)^{2}
with

\displaystyle\det e=\frac{1}{2}e^{2\omega}(\tilde{\tau}-\tau).

(39)

A short computation shows that

\displaystyle G_{ab}=2\frac{e^{2(\omega-\sigma)}}{(\rho-\tilde{\rho})^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(\tilde{\rho}-\tau)(\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{\tau})&-\tau\tilde{\tau}-\rho\tilde{\rho}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\tau+\tilde{\tau})(\rho+\tilde{\rho})\\
-\tau\tilde{\tau}-\rho\tilde{\rho}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\tau+\tilde{\tau})(\rho+\tilde{\rho})&(\rho-\tau)(\rho-\tilde{\tau})\\
\end{array}\right),

and hence we obtain

\displaystyle M_{--}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\rho-\tau)(\rho-\tilde{\tau})}{\tfrac{1}{2}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})(\tau-\tilde{\tau})-\tau\tilde{\tau}-\rho\tilde{\rho}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\tau+\tilde{\tau})(\rho+\tilde{\rho})}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho-\tau}{\tilde{\rho}-\tau}.

(42)

In the special case
\rho=-\tilde{\rho}=1
we have

\displaystyle M_{--}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\tau)}{(1+\tau)}.

(43)

This agrees with a similar computation in
Andriolo:2021gen
(up to
\tau\to-1/\tau
and a change in sign arising from a redefinition of
{\cal M}
).

Let us make some comments about
M_{--}
in special cases. First we note that when
\rho=\tau
we find
M_{--}=0
. This arises as the one form
dx+\rho dy=dx+\tau dy
is then self-dual with respect to both
g
and
\bar{g}
. If we also impose
\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\tau}
,
then
g
is conformal to
\bar{g}
. Since the self-duality condition is conformally invariant we expect to find
M_{--}=0
in this case. However, we also find

is conformal to
\bar{g}
if we take
\tilde{\rho}=\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}=\rho
but this corresponds to

. The reason for this
is
that while

is always taken to be self-dual with respect to
\bar{g}
,

is anti-self-dual with respect to

. Thus although the two metrics are conformal to each other the notion of self-duality has been flipped.
As discussed in the introduction, we choose

so that

is finite and non-zero everywhere, so for given

we choose

to exclude the above cases.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma}(dx+\rho dy)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma}\frac{\rho-\tilde{\rho}}{\rho-\tau}\left(dx+\tau dy\right).

\Lambda=\sqrt{(\rho-\tau)/(\rho-\tilde{\rho})}
,
we can also find something more symmetric:

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{\prime+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma^{\prime}}(dx+\rho dy)

(54)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{\prime-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{\sigma^{\prime}}\left(dx+\tau dy\right).

(55)

For either choice of the zweibein the metric
h
can be taken to be

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}

\displaystyle=e^{2\sigma^{\prime}}(dx+\rho dy)\left(dx+\tau dy\right),

(56)

where we have introduced
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln((\rho-\tilde{\rho})/(\rho-\tau))
. However, we recall that the metric
h
is only defined up to a conformal factor so we can choose
\sigma^{\prime}
to be anything we like.

3
The Quantum Partition Function

3.1
The Path Integral And A Complex Metric

Let us now turn our attention to the quantum theory. We start with the path integral

\displaystyle Z[J]

\displaystyle=\int[dP][dQ_{+}]e^{iS[P,Q_{+},J]}

\displaystyle=\int[dP][dQ^{\prime}_{+}]e^{iS_{eff}[P,J]+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\det}(\bar{e})M_{--}(Q^{\prime}_{+})^{2}}.

(57)

Our first task is to perform the path integral over
Q^{\prime}_{+}
, which is a simple Gaussian
giving formally an infinite factor
\prod_{x}({\det}(\bar{e})M_{--})^{-1/2}(x)
. In
Andriolo:2021gen
it was argued that
this can be regularised to give a finite number that does not depend on
{\det}(\bar{e})M_{--}
.
Dropping this irrelevant multiplicative factor, we find

\displaystyle Z[J]\sim\int[dP]e^{iS_{eff}[P,J]}.

(58)

In
Andriolo:2021gen
the path integral was made convergent by taking the metric
g
to have Euclidean signature.
Here, we wish to consider the path integral for the case in which both metrics
\bar{g}
and
g
have Euclidean signature.
As discussed in the introduction, we actually consider the more general case in which
\bar{g}
and
g
are complex metrics, with

\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
independent complex variables.
We then find Riemannian metrics
\bar{g}
and
g
in the case that
\tilde{\rho}=\rho^{*}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau^{*}
respectively, but we can also be more general.
In what follows, we assume that spacetime is a compact Riemann surface; additional subtleties can arise on non-compact spaces.

To continue, we write
\sqrt{-h}=i\sqrt{h}
and
S_{eff}=i\tilde{S}_{eff}
with

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{eff}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\sqrt{h}\Big(\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P-2{\cal J}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}P+{\cal J}^{\mu}J_{\mu}\Big)d^{2}x.

(59)

so that now the path integral takes the form

\displaystyle Z[\rho,\tau,J]

\displaystyle\sim\int[dP]e^{-\tilde{S}_{eff}[P,J]},

(60)

which depends holomorphically on each of the two complex structures,
\rho
and
\tau
.
This is the same as a path integral for a non-chiral real scalar field but with a complex metric:

\displaystyle h=e^{2\sigma^{\prime}}(dx+\rho dy)(dx+\tau dy).

(61)

Here we see a complex metric combining the holomorphic sectors of two distinct Riemannian metrics
arising naturally from the Sen action.

3.2
A Canonical Approach

It is instructive to construct the partition function from canonical quantization on a 2-torus. As usual, for real Riemannian metrics,
the diffeomorphism symmetry can be used to bring the real metric
g
on a 2-torus to standard form with
\tau
constant, the complex structure modulus.
As explained in
Hull:2023dgp
, there is a second diffeomorphism-like symmetry that can be used to bring the real metric
\bar{g}
to standard form with
\rho
a constant modulus.
Here we set the sources to zero and take the functions
\rho
and
\tau
to be constants.
As already mentioned we will extend our calculations to allow for
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau
and
\tilde{\tau}
to be general independent complex variables.

The metrics are then (using the symmetries to set the conformal factors to 1)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dx+\tau dy)(dx+\tilde{\tau}dy),

(62)

\displaystyle\bar{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dx+\rho dy)(dx+\tilde{\rho}dy),

(63)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dx+\tau dy)(dx+\rho dy).

(64)

It is helpful to introduce the coordinates

\displaystyle z=x+\tau y,\qquad\tilde{z}=x+\rho y.

(65)

so that the metric is simply

\displaystyle h=dzd\tilde{z}.

(66)

Note that for
\rho=-\tau=1
we find that
h
is the Minkowski metric with
y
the time variable. Here we will be more general and allow for complex values of
\rho
and
\tau
. The
associated derivatives are

\displaystyle\partial=-\frac{1}{\rho-\tau}\left(\partial_{y}-\rho\partial_{x}\right),\qquad\tilde{\partial}=\frac{1}{\rho-\tau}\left(\partial_{y}-\tau\partial_{x}\right),

(67)

and these satisfy

\displaystyle\partial z=\tilde{\partial}\tilde{z}=1,\qquad\partial\tilde{z}=\tilde{\partial}z=0.

(68)

The equation of motion for
P
becomes simply

\displaystyle\partial\tilde{\partial}P=0,

(69)

Assuming the coordinate
x
is periodic:
x\cong x+2\pi l
, the general solution can be parameterised as

\displaystyle P=P_{0}+\frac{1}{2l}a_{0}z-\frac{1}{2l}\tilde{a}_{0}\tilde{z}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}e^{-inz/l}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\tilde{a}_{n}}{n}e^{in\tilde{z}/l}.

(70)

Note that the linear terms imply that
P(x+2\pi l,y)=P(x,y)+\pi(a_{0}-\tilde{a}_{0})
.

There are two cases to consider. First, if
P
is a non-compact boson
P\in\mathbb{R}
, then we must have
a_{0}=\tilde{a}_{0}
so setting

\displaystyle a_{0}=p_{0},\qquad\tilde{a}_{0}=p_{0}

(71)

we have

\displaystyle P=P_{0}+\frac{1}{2l}p_{0}(\tau-\rho)y+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}e^{-inz/l}-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\tilde{a}_{n}}{n}e^{in\tilde{z}/l}

(72)

with centre of mass position
P_{0}
and continuous momentum proportional to
p_{0}
.

Secondly, if
P
is periodic with
P\cong P+2\pi R
, then we must have
a_{0}-\tilde{a}_{0}=2mR
for some constant
R
and integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. To this end, we write

\displaystyle a_{0}=p_{0}+mR,\qquad\tilde{a}_{0}=p_{0}-mR.

(73)

Moreover, the momentum
p_{0}
is discrete as the eigenvalues of
p_{0}
are quantised:
p_{0}=n/R
for integers
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the states are labelled by two integers
m,n
, with
n/R
the discrete momenta and
m
being the winding number.

For this to be non-zero we must also introduce a periodicity on
P
;
P\cong P+2\pi R
. In this case we must have
a_{0}-\tilde{a}_{0}=2mR
for some constant
R
and integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. To this end, we write

\displaystyle a_{0}=p_{0}+mR,\qquad\tilde{a}_{0}=p_{0}-mR.

(74)

To quantize this system we treat
y
as time and obtain the conjugate momentum

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\sqrt{h}h^{y\nu}\partial_{\nu}P

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\partial-\tilde{\partial})P

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi l}\left(p_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}a_{n}e^{-inz/l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}{\tilde{a}_{n}}e^{in\tilde{z}/l}\right).

(75)

To reproduce the quantization condition

\displaystyle[P(x,y),\Pi(x^{\prime},y)]=i\delta(x-x^{\prime}),

(76)

we find the non-zero commutation relations

\displaystyle[a_{n},a_{m}]=n\delta_{m,-n},\qquad[\tilde{a}_{n},\tilde{a}_{m}]=n\delta_{m,-n},\qquad[P_{0},p_{0}]=i.

(77)

The Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\int\Pi\partial_{y}P-{\cal L}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\left(\rho(\tilde{\partial}P)^{2}-\tau(\partial P)^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{l}H_{\rho}-\frac{\tau}{l}H_{\tau},

(78)

where

\displaystyle H_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(p_{0}+mR)^{2}+\sum_{n>0}a_{-n}a_{n}-\frac{1}{24}

\displaystyle H_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(p_{0}-mR)^{2}+\sum_{n>0}\tilde{a}_{-n}\tilde{a}_{n}-\frac{1}{24},

(79)

and as usual the
-1/24
arises from normal ordering.

As is familiar in two dimensions, the left and right sectors decouple so that the total Hilbert space is a direct product

{\cal H}={\cal H}_{\tau}\otimes{\cal H}_{\rho}
.
Here this corresponds to the decoupling of the two chiral bosons, as first observed in
Sen:2019qit
.
Since the Hilbert space factorises the partition function takes the form

\displaystyle Z(\beta)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{{\cal H}}\left(e^{-\beta H}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{{\cal H}_{\tau}\otimes{\cal H}_{\rho}}\left(e^{\beta l^{-1}\tau H_{\tau}}e^{-\beta l^{-1}\rho H_{\rho}}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{{\cal H}_{\tau}\otimes{\cal H}_{\rho}}\left(q^{H_{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{H_{\rho}}\right),

(80)

where
q=e^{\beta l^{-1}\tau}
and
\tilde{q}=e^{-\beta l^{-1}\rho}
.
This can be evaluated in the usual way. Summing over the oscillators gives

\displaystyle Z(\beta)=\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}{\rm Tr}_{zero-modes}\left(q^{H_{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{H_{\rho}}\right)

(81)

and it remains to take the trace of
q^{H_{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{H_{\rho}}
over the zero-modes.
If
P
is a non-compact boson, then there are no winding numbers (
m=0
) and the momentum
p_{0}
is a continuous variable that we integrate over to find

\displaystyle Z(\beta)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho-\tau}}\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}.

(82)

On the other hand, if
P
takes values in a circle of radius
S
,
then the eigenvalues of
p_{0}
are quantised:
p_{0}=n/R
. Summing over
n
and
m
leads to

\displaystyle Z(\beta)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}\sum_{m,n}q^{\frac{1}{4}(n/R+mR)^{2}}\tilde{q}^{\frac{1}{4}(n/R-mR)^{2}}.

(83)

We will find agreement with the path integral calculation below by taking
\beta=2\pi il
, as expected.

3.3
Path Integral On The Torus

Our next task is to provide an explicit calculation of the partition function by evaluating the path integral on a torus. As above we take

\displaystyle h=(dx+\rho dy)(dx+\tau dy),

(84)

with
\rho
and
\tau
constant but complex and unrelated. We assume that
x,y\in[0,2\pi l]
with periodic boundary conditions (except for the zero-modes). In this section we will closely follow the calculations in sections 10.2 and 10.4 of
DiFrancesco:1997nk
. The set-up discussed in
DiFrancesco:1997nk
arises in our analysis on setting

\rho=\tau^{*}
, but here we will keep
\rho
and
\tau
unrelated. A related calculation for the Sen action with
\rho=1
was performed in
Andriolo:2021gen
and also
Lambert:2023qgs
.

First we split
P=P_{z.m.}+P_{o}
where
P_{o}
represents the non-zero oscillator modes which can be expanded in a basis of eigenmodes

\displaystyle P_{o}=\sum_{n,m}c_{n,m}\phi_{n,m},

(85)

where

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{h}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{n,m}\right)=\lambda_{n,m}\phi_{n,m},

(86)

where

\sqrt{h}=\sqrt{det(h_{\mu\nu})}

and we normalise

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x\sqrt{h}\phi_{m,n}\phi_{-m^{\prime},-n^{\prime}}=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}.

(87)

Taking a Fourier expansion we find

\displaystyle\phi_{m,n}=Ne^{imx/l+iny/l},

(88)

with
N^{-2}=4\pi^{2}l^{2}\sqrt{h}
a normalization constant and

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,n}=-\frac{4}{l^{2}}\frac{1}{(\rho-\tau)^{2}}(n-m\rho)(n-m\tau).

(89)

The reality of
P
is imposed by taking
c^{*}_{m,n}=c_{-m,-n}
.

The path integral leads to an infinite number of Gaussian integrals

\displaystyle Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m,n}\int dc_{m,n}e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}|c_{m,n}|^{2}\lambda_{m,n}+ic_{m,n}j_{-m,-n}},

(90)

over the complex coefficients
c_{n,m}
with
(m,n)\neq(0,0)
. Here
j_{m,n}
arises from the sources:

\displaystyle{J}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}{J}_{\mu m,n}e^{imx/l+iny/l}

\displaystyle j_{m,n}

\displaystyle=2\pi l\frac{\rho m-n}{\rho-\tau}(\tau{J}_{xm,n}-{J}_{ym,n}).

(91)

The reality condition
c^{*}_{n,m}=c_{-n,-m}
allows us to write this as

\displaystyle Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=\prod_{(m,n)>0}\int dc_{m,n}dc^{*}_{m,n}e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}c_{n,m}c^{*}_{n,m}\lambda_{m,n}+ic_{m,n}j_{-m,-n}+ic^{*}_{m,n}j_{m,n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{(m,n)>0}\int dc^{\prime}_{m,n}d{c^{\prime}}^{*}_{m,n}e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}c^{\prime}_{n,m}{c^{\prime}}^{*}_{n,m}\lambda_{n,m}+4\pi\lambda_{m,n}^{-1}j_{m,n}j_{-m,-n}},

(92)

where we have restricted the product to be only over positive modes
(for example only those pairs
(m,n)
whose first non-zero entry is positive) and in the second line we have shifted the integration variables to remove the linear term.
Thus we find

\displaystyle Z_{o}=e^{-S_{n.l.}}\prod_{(m,n)>0}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda_{m,n}},

(93)

where

\displaystyle e^{-S_{n.l.}}=e^{4\pi\sum_{(m,n)>0}\lambda_{m,n}^{-1}j_{m,n}j_{-m,-n}}.

(94)

However, since
\lambda_{n,m}
is even in
(n,m)\to(-n,-m)
we will find it easier to write this as

\displaystyle Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)=e^{-S_{n.l.}}{\prod_{m,n}}^{\prime}\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda_{m,n}}},

(95)

where the product is over all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, not both zero.
We use the notation
{\prod_{m,n}}^{\prime}
or
{\sum_{m,n}}^{\prime}

to indicate a product or summation over all integers
m,n
where not both are zero.
As usual, to regulate this expression we define

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=(4\pi^{2})^{s}{\sum_{m,n}}^{\prime}\lambda_{m,n}^{-s}

(96)

\displaystyle={(4\pi^{2}l^{2}\sqrt{h})^{s}}{\sum}^{\prime}_{m,n}\frac{1}{(n-m\rho)^{s}(n-m\tau)^{s}},

(97)

and identify

\displaystyle Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)=e^{-S_{n.l.}}e^{\frac{1}{2}G^{\prime}(0)}.

(98)

Next, we write

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle={(4\pi^{2}l^{2}\sqrt{h})^{s}}\tilde{G}(s),

(99)

so that we then have

\displaystyle Z_{o}=(4\pi^{2}l^{2}\sqrt{h})^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{G}(0)}e^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{G}^{\prime}(0)}e^{-S_{n.l.}}.

(100)

In other words, the coefficient
4\pi^{2}l^{2}\sqrt{h}
simply provides an overall factor that multiplies the regulated infinite product.

Our next task is to compute
\tilde{G}(s)
, which exists for suitably large
s
, and with certain restrictions on
\rho
and
\tau
, and then analytically continue it to
s=0
.
In particular we find

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)

\displaystyle={\sum_{n,m}}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(n-m\rho)^{s}(n-m\tau)^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n^{2s}}+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{(n-m\rho)^{s}(n-m\tau)^{s}}

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{(n-m\rho)^{s}(n-m\tau)^{s}}.

(101)

To continue, we write

\displaystyle\chi=\frac{1}{2}(\rho+\tau),\qquad\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(\rho-\tau),

(102)

so that

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{(n-m\chi-m\varphi)^{s}(n-m\chi+m\varphi)^{s}}.

(103)

Here we notice that for fixed
m
the sum over
n
is periodic under
m\chi\to m\chi+1
so we can make a Fourier expansion

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n}\sum_{p}e^{2\pi imp\chi}\int_{0}^{1}dy\frac{e^{-2\pi ipy}}{(n-y-m\varphi)^{s}(n-y+m\varphi)^{s}}.

(104)

Next, we note that the sum over
n
converts the integral over
y
from
[0,1]
to
(-\infty,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p}e^{2\pi imp\chi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\frac{e^{-2\pi ipy}}{(-y-m\varphi)^{s}(-y+m\varphi)^{s}}

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p}e^{2\pi imp\chi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\frac{e^{2\pi ipy}}{(y-m\varphi)^{s}(y+m\varphi)^{s}}

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p}e^{2\pi imp\chi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\frac{e^{2\pi ipy}}{(y^{2}-m^{2}\varphi^{2})^{s}}.

(105)

We now introduce a new integral:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1-s}}e^{2\pi ip(m\chi+y)}e^{-t(y^{2}-m^{2}\varphi^{2})}.

(106)

Note that convergence of the
t
integral requires that
{\rm Re}(\varphi^{2})<0
.
The integral over
y
is Gaussian and can now be computed

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2-s}}e^{2\pi ipm\chi}e^{m^{2}t\varphi^{2}-\pi^{2}p^{2}/t}.

(107)

We need to treat the
p=0
term separately:

\displaystyle\sum_{m\neq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2-s}}e^{m^{2}t\varphi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(s)}(-m^{2}\varphi^{2})^{1/2-s}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(s)}(\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}})^{1-2s}\zeta(s-1/2),

(108)

which again is valid if
{\rm Re}(\varphi^{2})<0
. Now we invoke the
\zeta
-function identity

\displaystyle\pi^{-s^{\prime}/2}\Gamma(s^{\prime}/2)\zeta(s^{\prime}/2)=\pi^{(s^{\prime}-1)/2}\Gamma((1-s^{\prime})/2)\zeta(1-2s^{\prime}),

(109)

to find something valid near
s=0
:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(s)

\displaystyle=(1+(-1)^{2s})\zeta(2s)+2\frac{\pi^{2s-1}}{\Gamma(s)}\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(2-2s)(\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}})^{1-2s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p\neq 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2-s}}e^{2\pi ipm\chi}e^{m^{2}t\varphi^{2}-\pi^{2}p^{2}/t}.

(110)

To continue, we note that for small
s
,
\Gamma(s)\sim 1/s
and so we can read off that

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(0)=2\zeta(0)=-1.

(111)

Furthermore, (noting that
\zeta^{\prime}(0)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln 2\pi
)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=(-\pi i-2\ln 2\pi)+2{\pi^{-1}}\Gamma(1)\zeta(2)\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p\neq 0}e^{2\pi ipm\chi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2}}e^{m^{2}t\varphi^{2}-\pi^{2}p^{2}/t}.

(112)

This final integral can be computed (and exists for
{\rm Re}(\varphi^{2})<0
):

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=(-\pi i-2\ln 2\pi)+\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{1}{|p|}e^{2\pi ipm\chi-2\pi|p||m|\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}+2\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\sum^{\infty}_{p=1}\frac{1}{p}e^{2\pi ipm\chi-2\pi pm\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}}+\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\sum^{\infty}_{p=1}\frac{1}{p}e^{-2\pi ipm\chi-2\pi pm\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}}.

(113)

The first term just gives a constant multiplicative factor and we have dropped it in the second line. If we introduce

\displaystyle q=e^{2\pi i\chi-2\pi\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}},\qquad\tilde{q}=e^{-2\pi i\chi-2\pi\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}},

(114)

then

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}+2\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\sum^{\infty}_{p=1}\frac{1}{p}q^{pm}+\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\sum^{\infty}_{p=1}\frac{1}{p}\tilde{q}^{pm}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\pi}{3}\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}-2\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\ln(1-q^{m})-2\sum^{\infty}_{m=1}\ln(1-\tilde{q}^{m}).

(115)

Let us recall that
\chi=(\rho+\tau)/2
and
\varphi=(\rho-\tau)/2
. If we take

\displaystyle\sqrt{-\varphi^{2}}=i(\rho-\tau)/2,

(116)

then we find

\displaystyle q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\qquad\tilde{q}=e^{-2\pi i\rho}.

(117)

Putting all the pieces together, we find

\displaystyle Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho-\tau}}\frac{1}{\eta(q)}\frac{1}{\eta(\tilde{q})}e^{-S_{n.l.}},

(118)

where

\displaystyle\eta(q)=q^{1/24}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{m}),

(119)

and
(\rho-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
comes from the
(4\pi l^{2}\sqrt{h})^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{G}(0)}=(4\pi l^{2}\sqrt{h})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
prefactor in (
100
).

Next we look at the zero-mode contribution. Here we allow for
P
not to be single valued but rather
to take values on a circle with
P\cong P+2\pi R
for some fixed
R

so that
P
can have winding modes. Then with winding numbers
m,n
on the
x
and
y
directions we have

\displaystyle P_{z.m.}=p_{0}+\frac{mRx}{l}+\frac{nRy}{l}.

(120)

Such field configurations satisfy
-h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P_{z.m.}=0
. Evaluating
the action on them gives

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{z.m.}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\sqrt{h}\left(\frac{1}{2}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}P_{z.m.}\partial_{\nu}P_{z.m.}-2{\cal J}^{\mu}_{0,0}\partial_{\mu}P_{z.m.}\right)d^{2}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi R^{2}}{\sqrt{h}}(n-\rho m)(n-m\tau)-\frac{4\pi lR}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})(n-\rho m).

(121)

Noting that
\sqrt{h}=i(\rho-\tau)/2
, this leads to

\displaystyle Z_{z.m.}(\tau,\rho)=\sum_{m,n}e^{-\frac{2\pi iR^{2}}{{\rho-\tau}}(n-\rho m)(n-m\tau)-\frac{4\pi lR}{{\rho-\tau}}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})(n-\rho m)}.

(122)

Next, we perform a Poisson re-summation over
n
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n}e^{-\pi An^{2}+Bn}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\sum_{k}e^{-\frac{\pi}{A}\left(k+\frac{B}{2\pi i}\right)^{2}}.

(123)

This requires that
{\rm Re}A>0
.
Using
A=2iR^{2}/(\rho-\tau)
and
B=\pi R^{2}(\rho+\tau)m/\sqrt{h}-4\pi lR(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})/(\rho-\tau)
, we get

\displaystyle Z_{z.m.}(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho-\tau}}{\sqrt{2i}R}\sum_{m,k}e^{\frac{2\pi iR^{2}}{{\rho-\tau}}\rho\tau m^{2}-\frac{\pi i({\rho-\tau})}{2R^{2}}k^{2}+\pi i(\rho+\tau)mk-\frac{\pi iR^{2}m^{2}}{2(\rho-\tau)}(\rho+\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times e^{2\pi l\left(ik/R-2i(\rho+\tau)mR/(\rho-\tau)+4imR\rho/(\rho-\tau)\right)(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})-\frac{2\pi il^{2}}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho-\tau}}{\sqrt{2i}R}\sum_{m,k}e^{-\frac{\pi iR^{2}}{2}(\rho-\tau)m^{2}-\frac{\pi i(\rho-\tau)}{R^{2}}k^{2}+\pi i(\rho+\tau)mk}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times e^{2\pi il(mR+k/R)(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})-\frac{2\pi il^{2}}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho-\tau}}{\sqrt{2i}R}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\sum_{m,k}e^{-2\pi il(mR+k/R)J_{y0,0}}q^{\frac{1}{4}(mR+k/R)^{2}+(mR+k/R)lJ_{x0,0}}\tilde{q}^{\frac{1}{4}(mR-k/R)^{2}}.

(124)

Putting everything together gives:

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}e^{-S_{n.l.}}e^{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\sum_{m,k}e^{-2\pi i(mR+k/R)J_{y0,0}}q^{\frac{1}{4}(mR+k/R)^{2}+(mR+k/R)lJ_{x0,0}}\tilde{q}^{\frac{1}{4}(mR-k/R)^{2}}.

(125)

However, we can further simply this by separating out the zero modes in
{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J}=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}_{o}\wedge\tilde{\star}J_{o}}e^{\frac{2\pi il^{2}}{\rho-\tau}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})(\rho J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})},

(126)

and hence we find

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}e^{-S_{n.l.}}e^{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}_{o}\wedge\tilde{\star}J_{o}}e^{2\pi il^{2}(\tau J_{x0,0}-J_{y0,0})J_{x0,0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\sum_{m,k}e^{-2\pi il(mR+k/R)J_{y0,0}}q^{\frac{1}{4}(mR+k/R)^{2}+(mR+k/R)lJ_{x0,0}}\tilde{q}^{\frac{1}{4}(mR-k/R)^{2}}.

(127)

Note that the non-zero modes of the sources only appear in the first two exponential terms.
It is interesting to observe that the source’s zero-modes only appear coupled to
\tau
and not
\rho
. In
the absence of sources we find more simply

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}\sum_{m,k}q^{\frac{1}{4}(mR+k/R)^{2}}\tilde{q}^{\frac{1}{4}(mR-k/R)^{2}},

(128)

which is of the form given in (
4
) that we discussed in the introduction and also agrees with the
partition function (
83
) calculated by canonical quantisation (taking
\beta=2\pi il
).

3.4
Convergence

In the analysis above we have proceeded formally and assumed that the integrals we discussed are convergent where necessary. Let us now consider more carefully the criteria for their convergence. In full generality, we have a four-complex dimensional space of parameters
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau,\tilde{\tau}
. To simplify our analysis in this subsection, let us consider the case in which
\tilde{\rho}=\rho^{*}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau^{*}
which reduces our parameters to a two-dimensional complex subspace corresponding to the case where both
g
and
\bar{g}
are Riemannian.

We recall that our calculation consisted of two path integrals: one over
Q_{+}^{\prime}
, which leads to an effective action involving
P
, and then we must perform a path integral over
P
.
We start with looking at when the integration over
Q^{\prime}_{+}
is valid. In particular, the condition that
Q^{\prime}_{+}
integral is a standard Gaussian is that
{\rm Im}({\det}(\bar{e})M_{--})>0
.
As discussed earlier, the integral remains divergent even in this case, but can be regularised.
If we were to consider the case of Lorentzian-signature metrics
\bar{g}
and
g
, where all metric components are real, then we would have simply
{\rm Im}({\det}(\bar{e})M_{--})=0
and hence the integral would not be Gaussian. Turning to the Euclidean context where
\rho
and
\tau
are complex we find

\displaystyle{\rm Im}(\det\bar{e}M_{--})=e^{2\sigma}\frac{\rho_{2}}{|\rho-\tau^{*}|^{2}}\left((\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}+\tau_{2}^{2}-\rho_{2}^{2}\right),

(129)

where
\rho=\rho_{1}+i\rho_{2}
and
\tau=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}
.
We take the conformal factor

e^{2\sigma}
to be real and positive. If
\rho_{2}<0
then we must have

\displaystyle\rho_{2}<-\sqrt{\tau_{2}^{2}+(\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}}.

(130)

Alternatively for
\rho_{2}>0
we find

\displaystyle\rho_{2}<\sqrt{\tau_{2}^{2}+(\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}}.

(131)

Either way, there is a open set of values of
\rho
and
\tau
for which the
Q_{+}^{\prime}
integral is convergent.

Next, let us examine the convergence of the path integral arising from the integration over
P
. Looking at
S_{eff}
, we see that we expect this requires that
\sqrt{h}h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P
be positive definite. Note that this is a slightly weaker than the so-called KSW condition
Kontsevich:2021dmb
;
Witten:2021nzp
which requires that, in addition to the 0-form
P
, the standard action for a 1-form also converges. A computation shows that the eigenvalues of
{\rm Re}(\sqrt{h}h^{\mu\nu})
are

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{|\rho-\tau|^{2}}\left(X\pm\sqrt{X^{2}-Y}\right),

(132)

where

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=(1+|\rho|^{2})\tau_{2}-(1+|\tau|^{2})\rho_{2}

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=2\tau_{2}^{2}\rho_{2}^{2}-(\rho_{2}^{2}+\tau_{2}^{2})\rho_{2}\tau_{2}-(\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}\rho_{2}\tau_{2}.

(133)

Thus positivity of
{\rm Re}(\sqrt{h}h^{\mu\nu})
requires that both
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
are positive and hence
X>0
and
Y>0
. In full generality, this is a slightly complicated condition. However, we can restrict to the case
\rho_{2}<0
and
\tau_{2}>0
so that
X,Y>0
and, from (
130
), we also require

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{2}-\tau_{2}^{2}>(\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}.

(134)

On the other hand, we can also consider the explicit path-integral calculation over
P
that we performed above for the case of a torus. There we found that the integrals converge if
{\rm Re}(\varphi^{2})<0
(recall
\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(\rho-\tau)
) which is equivalent to

\displaystyle(\rho_{2}-\tau_{2})^{2}>(\rho_{1}-\tau_{1})^{2}.

(135)

By writing
\rho_{2}=-(1+\alpha)\tau_{2}
one sees that (
134
) implies (
135
).
There was also a condition
{\rm Re}A>0
in the Poisson re-summation of the zero-modes which becomes simply
\tau_{2}-\rho_{2}>0
.
Lastly, we find that convergence of the sum over
m
in the
\eta
-functions requires that
\tau_{2}>0
and
\rho_{2}<0
. Thus the explicit torus calculation requires
\tau_{2}>0
,
\rho_{2}<0
in addition to (
134
). These were referred to as the “microscopic” conditions in
BenettiGenolini:2025jwe
and we see that, as found there, they are stronger than the condition
X,Y>0
and (
129
), which allow for more general solutions.

Finally, let us comment on the relation of our calculation to the calculation in
Andriolo:2021gen
which took
\bar{g}=\eta
, corresponding to the choice
\rho=-\tilde{\rho}=1
and hence
\tilde{q}=1
here. This case is not included in our discussion as we require
\rho_{2}<0
for convergence. However, for the non-zero modes the problem is simply an over-all physically irrelevant constant multiplicative factor. The associated zeta-function regularisation is not sensitive to this and the analysis of
Andriolo:2021gen
found a single factor of
1/\eta(q)
rather than
1/\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})
. When looking at the zero-modes, the double sum we found here factorises into two single sums, one of which leads to a delta-function implying that
R^{2}=r_{1}/r_{2}
is rational and the remaining sum becomes a single-sum theta-function. The divergent coefficient arising from this delta-function was interpreted in
Andriolo:2021gen
as arising from the unphysical modes that couple to
\bar{g}=\eta
and not
g
. In this way, the analysis of
Andriolo:2021gen
led to a partition function that is a single factor of
1/\eta(q)
times a theta-function of
\tau
. This is broadly consistent with our result (
128
) if we simply drop
1/\eta(\tilde{q})=1/\eta(1)
and
\sum\tilde{q}^{\tfrac{1}{4}(mR-k/R)^{2}}=\sum 1
as constant but irrelevant divergent prefactors.

4
Modular Properties And Anomalies

4.1
Modular Properties

Geometrically modular invariance arises from requiring invariance under large diffeomorphisms of the torus. However, it is well-known that the partition function for a single chiral boson is not modular invariant. This is sometimes used as an argument why a single chiral boson cannot have a satisfactory action formulation as one would expect the path integral to give a modular invariant expression on the torus. Therefore it is of interest to examine what happens for Sen’s action and the bi-metric action.

Before discussing our result and its generalisations, let us review the partition function obtained from the textbook canonical quantisation analysis on a spatial circle
x\cong x+2\pi
. First, we consider a non-chiral, non-compact boson where the mode expansion is
P=p_{0}+Ex^{0}+oscillators
. The partition function is

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})

\displaystyle=\int dE\sum_{oscillators}q^{L_{0}}(q^{*})^{\tilde{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-\tau^{*}}}\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}.

(136)

Here the factor of
({\tau-\tau^{*}})^{-1/2}
arises from the integral over the continuous
E
variable. The
\eta
-functions arise from sums over the left and right moving oscillators. The zero-mode
p_{0}
does not contribute to the energy – this follows from time translational invariance – and only contributes an irrelevant divergent factor to the partition function. This is well-known to be modular invariant.

In the case of a periodic scalar
P\cong P+2\pi R
, the mode expansion is
P=p_{0}+nx^{0}/Rl+mRx^{1}/l+oscillators
, and both the winding and
E
variables become discrete:
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here find

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}\sum_{oscillators}q^{L_{0}}(q^{*})^{\tilde{L}_{0}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{m,n}q^{\frac{1}{4}(mR+n/R)^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{4}(mR-n/R)^{2}}.

(137)

Again this is modular invariant.
This partition function can be written as

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{(p_{L},p_{R})\in\Gamma}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}p_{R}^{2}}.

(138)

where

\displaystyle p_{L}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(n/R+mR),\qquad p_{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(n/R-mR),

(139)

and the sum is over all points
(p_{L},p_{R})
in the Narain lattice
\Gamma
.
The modular invariance can be seen to follow from the fact that the Narain lattice is even and self-dual.

For rational values of
R^{2}
, the Narain lattice decomposes into sublattices
\Gamma=\Gamma_{L}\oplus\Gamma_{R}
with
p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}
and

p_{R}\in\Gamma_{R}
. Then

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}}\sum_{p_{R}\in\Gamma_{R}}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}p_{R}^{2}}.

(140)

This then takes the form of a sum of holomorphically factorised terms:

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{a}|f_{a}(q)|^{2},

(141)

for a finite set of generalised theta functions
f_{a}(q)
(see e.g.
Blumenhagen:2013fgp
).
In general the functions
f_{a}(q)
transform into each other under modular transformations, so that all are needed for modular invariance in general.

For example, for the self-dual radius
R=1
we find

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{m,n}q^{\frac{1}{4}(m+n)^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{4}(m-n)^{2}}.

(142)

This can then be rewritten as a sum of holomorphically factorised terms:

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}(|\theta_{3}(\tau)|^{2}+|\theta_{2}(\tau)|^{2}),

(143)

where the theta-functions are

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(\tau)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{m^{2}},\qquad\theta_{2}(\tau)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}q^{m^{2}}.

(144)

and these transform into each other under modular transformations.

For rational
R^{2}
there are states with
p_{R}=0
,
i.e. there are values of momentum and winding numbers
m,n
for which
mR-n/R=0
. Restricting to these states defines a chiral boson theory with

p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}

and the partition function is

\displaystyle Z(\tau)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)}\sum_{p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}.

(145)

Here the self-duality condition implies that there is just one set of left-moving oscillators and hence a single
\eta
-function and the partition function is not modular invariant. For example, for the self-dual radius
R=1
the states with
p_{R}=0
have
m=n
and
p_{L}=2m
so that the partition function becomes

\displaystyle Z(\tau)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)}\theta_{3}(q),

(146)

and this is not modular invariant.

Note that if one tries to define a chiral boson theory when
R^{2}
is not rational, the chiral constraint
mR-n/R=0
implies
m,n=0
so that the only states are oscillators with no momentum or winding
with
Z(\tau)\sim 1/\eta(q)
. This is not modular invariant either. Furthermore, in both these cases we have had to impose self-duality by hand, as an additional constraint that does not follow from the action.

We now look at the action of modular transformations on the partition function
Z(\tau,\rho)
we have obtained above using the Sen formulation, which does arise directly from a Lagrangian formulation with no additional constraints.
In the case
\rho=\tau^{*}
then
Z(\tau,\tau^{*})
agrees with the modular invariant partition function given in (
137
).
This suggests that
Z(\tau,\rho)
should be invariant under modular transformations in which both
\tau
and
\rho
transform. Indeed, it was shown in
Hull:2023dgp
the action (
2.1
) inherits two types of diffeomorphism-like symmetries that act separately on
g
and
\bar{g}
. However, the ‘true’ diffeomorphisms, i.e. those that arise from coordinate transformations, correspond to the diagonal subgroup. Thus, looking at the global diffeomorphisms of the torus that generate modular transformations, this implies that
Z(\tau,\rho)
should only be invariant under modular transformations where which both
\tau
and
\rho
transform.
That is, the full modular group is
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau}\times SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\rho}
and
Z(\tau,\rho)
should be invariant under the diagonal subgroup.
Let us now confirm this.

First let us consider the case where
P
is not periodic and so does not admit any winding modes. Here we find, setting the sources to zero for simplicity,

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)\sim Z_{o}(\tau,\rho)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau-\rho}}\frac{1}{\eta(q)}\frac{1}{\eta(\tilde{q})}.

(147)

To examine its modular invariance we first note that

\displaystyle\eta(\tau+1)=e^{\pi i/12}\eta(\tau),\qquad\eta(-1/\tau)=\sqrt{-i\tau}\eta(\tau).

(148)

On the other hand we have defined
\eta(\rho)
using
\tilde{q}=e^{-2\pi i\rho}
which is valid in the lower half plane and so we have

\displaystyle\eta(\rho+1)=e^{-\pi i/12}\eta(\rho),\qquad\eta(-1/\rho)=\sqrt{i\rho}\eta(\rho).

(149)

As a result, for a non-periodic scalar the partition function does not factorise nor does it have any nice properties under general modular transformations of
\tau
and
\rho
. However, it is invariant under diagonal modular transformations where
\tau
and
\rho
both transform in the same way.

Next we look at the case where
P
is periodic:
P\cong P+2\pi R
. In addition to the non-zero mode contribution, we find the zero-mode contribution as given in (
128
).
For rational values of
R
, the partition function takes the form

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}\sum_{a}f_{a}(q)f_{a}(\tilde{q}).

(150)

For example, at the self-dual radius
R=1
(
143
) is replaced by

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\rho)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(\tilde{q})}(\theta_{3}(\tau)\theta_{3}(\rho)+\theta_{2}(\tau)\theta_{2}(\rho)).

(151)

Again it is easy to see that the partition function will not have any simple transformations properties under
\tau\to\tau+1
unless we also take
\rho\to\rho+1
where it is invariant. Next taking both
\tau\to-1/\tau
and
\rho\to-1/\rho
we find that

\displaystyle\frac{(n-\rho m)(n-m\tau)}{\rho-\tau}

\displaystyle\to\frac{(n+m/\rho)(n+m/\tau)}{1/\tau-1/\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n\rho+m)(n\tau+m)}{\rho-\tau}.

(152)

This simply corresponds to swapping the sum
(m,n)\to(n,-m)
in (
122
) and hence the zero-mode contribution is invariant under diagonal modular transformations.

Lastly we note that if we include the source zero-modes the diagonal modular invariance is preserved they transform under
\tau\to\tau+1,\rho\to\rho+1
as

\displaystyle J_{x,0,0}\to J_{x,0,0}\qquad J_{y0,0}\to J_{y0,0}+J_{x0,0},

(153)

and under
\tau\to-1/\tau
,
\rho\to-1/\rho
as

\displaystyle J_{x,0,0}\to-J_{y,0,0}\qquad J_{y,0,0}\to J_{x,0,0}.

(154)

4.2
Gravitational Anomalies

A single left-moving chiral scalar also has a 2-dimensional gravitational anomaly. A consistent quantum theory can then only be found by combining it with other degrees of freedom. For example, the gravitational anomaly of a left-moving chiral scalar can be cancelled by adding a right-moving chiral scalar or two right-moving real chiral Fermions.
However, such a theory of a right-moving chiral scalar plus left-moving degrees of freedom is not modular invariant in general.

The modular invariant partition function for a non-chiral scalar given above was

\displaystyle Z(q,q^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}\sum_{(p_{L},p_{R})\in\Gamma}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}p_{R}^{2}},

(155)

and is a sum the sum over all points
(p_{L},p_{R})
in the Narain lattice
\Gamma
.
For rational values of
R^{2}
, points in the Narain lattice with
p_{L}=0
correspond to a right-moving chiral scalar and the ones with
p_{R}=0
correspond to a left-moving chiral scalar. The partition function for a left and right chiral scalar given by restricting the sum to these states is not modular invariant.
For example, for the self-dual radius
R=1
the restriction to chiral scalars is

\displaystyle Z(q,q^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)\eta(q^{*})}|\theta_{3}(\tau)|^{2},

(156)

which is not modular invariant. For the unrestricted scalar, the modular invariant partition function (
143
) has another term involving

\theta_{2}
coming from the points in the Narain lattice where both
p_{L},p_{R}
are non-zero.

As mentioned above, in the bi-metric chiral boson action, there are two gauge symmetries, one for which
g
is the gauge field and one for which
\bar{g}
is the gauge field, with the ‘true’ diffeomorphisms arising as a diagonal subgroup of these
Hull:2023dgp
. Anomalies arise for both these symmetries and these will be discussed more fully elsewhere.

The action for a left-moving chiral scalar (
2.1
) is invariant under both these symmetries, but the quantum theory has an anomaly in both.
This can be viewed as the physical scalar
A
coupling to
g
giving rise to an anomaly in the
g
-symmetry and the shadow sector
C
coupling to
\bar{g}
giving rise to an anomaly in the
\bar{g}
-symmetry.
However, if one adds a similar action for a right-moving chiral scalar the anomalies in both symmetries are cancelled, as this introduces a right-moving physical scalar
\tilde{A}
coupling to
g
and a right-moving shadow scalar
\tilde{C}
coupling to
\bar{g}
.
However, this theory is not invariant under either the
\tau
or
\rho
modular transformations for reasons similar to those described above.
Such theories must then be coupled to other fields to achieve modular invariance. This will be explored in the following subsection.

Suppose now that two right-moving chiral fermions
\psi^{a}
coupling to
g
are added to the left-moving chiral boson action (
2.1
). This will cancel the
g
-symmetry anomaly but not the
\bar{g}
-symmetry anomaly. To do this a further two right-moving chiral fermions
\tilde{\psi}^{a}
coupling to
\bar{g}
are also needed. Thus the right-moving fermion theory also requires a shadow sector, if the anomaly in the b
\bar{g}
symmetry is to be avoided. This too will be explored further in the next section.

5
Chiral Bosons On A Torus, Anomalies And Modular invariance

5.1
Scalars On A Torus

Consider first the standard theory of
n
scalars taking values on an
n
-torus
T=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
for some lattice
\Gamma
, coupled to a world-sheet metric
g
. A constant
B
-field is incorporated by adding a topological term to the action that does not affect the equations of motion but which changes the definition of the canonical momentum.
Then the momentum and winding vectors combine into a
2n
-vector
(p_{L}^{i},p_{R}^{i})
taking values in the
2n
-dimensional Narain lattice
\Lambda
, where
p_{L}^{i}
is the momentum for left-movers and
p_{R}^{i}
is the momentum for right-movers (
i=1,\dots,n
). The partition function is then a straightforward generalisation of (
169
):

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)^{n}\eta(q^{*})^{n}}\sum_{(p_{L},p_{R})\in\Lambda}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}p_{R}^{2}}.

(157)

This is modular invariant as the
2n
-dimensional Narain lattice
\Lambda
is even and self-dual.

For special choices of the lattice and B-field (i.e. at special points in the Narain moduli space)
the Narain lattice decomposes into sublattices
\Lambda=\Gamma_{L}\oplus\Gamma_{R}
with
p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}
and

p_{R}\in\Gamma_{R}
. These special lattices are the rational lattices. For example, for
n=1
the 2-dimensional lattice is rational when
R^{2}
is rational.
For such a rational torus, the partition function can be written as

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)^{n}\eta(q^{*})^{n}}\sum_{p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}}\sum_{p_{R}\in\Gamma_{R}}q^{\frac{1}{2}_{L}^{2}}(q^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}p_{R}^{2}}.

(158)

Scalars on a rational torus define a rational conformal field theory, so that the partition function can be written in the form of a finite sum

Z(\tau,\tau^{*})\sim\sum_{a,\bar{b}}C_{a\bar{b}}\chi_{a}(\tau)\chi_{\bar{b}}(\tau^{*})

(159)

Here
C_{a\bar{b}}
are constants,
a
labels the left-moving primary fields and
\chi_{a}(\tau)
is the character of the Verma module generated by the
a
’th primary field, while

\bar{b}
labels the right-moving primary fields and
\chi_{\bar{b}}(\tau^{*})
is the character of the Verma module generated by the
\bar{b}
’th primary field.

On a rational torus, one can restrict to

n
chiral bosons with
p_{R}^{i}=0
and
p_{L}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
and the partition function is

\displaystyle Z(\tau)\sim\frac{1}{\eta(q)^{n}}\sum_{p_{L}\in\Gamma_{L}}q^{\frac{1}{2}p_{L}^{2}}.

(160)

This is not modular invariant in general.

5.2
The Action for Chiral Bosons on a Torus Target Space

The action (
17
) can be generalised to
n
scalars
P^{i}
and
n
1-forms
Q_{i+}
(with
i=1,\dots n
) with a constant metric
G_{ij}

and constant B-field
B_{ij}
. The action is (including sources
J_{i\pm}
)

\displaystyle S

Path Integral On A General Riemann Surface

In this section we extend the path integral discussion to higher genus Riemann surfaces. To this end, we write the effective action as

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{eff}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\Big(\frac{1}{2}dP\wedge\tilde{\star}dP-2{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}dP+{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\Big(-\frac{1}{2}P\wedge\tilde{\star}\tilde{\Delta}P-2P\wedge d\tilde{\star}{\cal J}+{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J\Big).

(182)

Here
\tilde{\star}
is defined in analogy with the Hodge dual:

\displaystyle\tilde{\star}V_{\mu}=\sqrt{h}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}h^{\nu\lambda}V_{\lambda},

(183)

with
\tilde{\star}^{2}=-1
when acting on odd-forms,
\tilde{\star}^{2}=1
on even-forms, and

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}P=\tilde{\star}d\tilde{\star}dP,

(184)

is the Laplacian associated to the complex metric
h
.
We can see here that the path integral takes the form

\displaystyle Z[J]=Z_{z.m.}Z_{o}e^{\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}\tilde{J}},

(185)

where
Z_{z.m.}
and
Z_{o}
are the contributions arising from the zero-modes and non-zero-modes respectively.

To continue, let us first consider the non-zero modes. Here we can start from the second form of the expression in (
6
). We can shift the integral over
P
to
P^{\prime}=P+B
with
B
determined by

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}B=-2\tilde{\star}d\tilde{\star}{\cal J}.

(186)

This is possible since, for the non-zero modes,
\tilde{\Delta}
is invertible allowing us to obtain
B
. This removes the linear term in
P
from the action. However, the price of doing this is to introduce a non-local term in
S_{eff}
of the form

\displaystyle S_{n.l.}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dB\wedge\tilde{\star}{\cal J}.

(187)

Thus we find

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=Z_{z.m}\int[dP^{\prime}]_{o}e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int P^{\prime}\wedge\tilde{\star}\tilde{\Delta}P^{\prime}}e^{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J}e^{-S_{n.l}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm det}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Delta})}}Z_{z.m}e^{-S_{n.l}}e^{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J},

(188)

where the determinant only includes the non-zero modes of
\tilde{\Delta}
and
Z_{z.m}
represents the contributions from the zero-modes.

Choosing coordinates where
\rho
and
\tau
constant the operator
\tilde{\Delta}
factorizes:
\tilde{\Delta}P=4\partial\tilde{\partial}P
where, as above,

\displaystyle\partial=-\frac{1}{\rho-\tau}\left(\partial_{y}-\rho\partial_{x}\right)\qquad\tilde{\partial}=\frac{1}{\rho-\tau}\left(\partial_{y}-\tau\partial_{x}\right).

(189)

This means that the eigenvalues of the Laplacian take the following form:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\vec{n}}=-\frac{4}{(\rho-\tau)^{2}}\lambda^{(\rho)}_{\vec{n}}\lambda^{(\tau)}_{\vec{n}},

(190)

where
\vec{n}
is a generic label for the eigenvalues and
\lambda_{\vec{n}}^{(\rho)}
and
\lambda_{\vec{n}}^{(\tau)}
only depend on
\rho
and
\tau
respectively.
Formally the determinant is given by

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Delta})=\prod_{{\vec{n}}}\left(\frac{-2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)^{2}\lambda^{(\rho)}_{\vec{n}}\lambda^{(\tau)}_{\vec{n}}.

(191)

As before there will be a reality constraint on
P
that relates the associated eigenfunctions and thereby restricts the product.
Using a zeta-function regularization this product is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Delta})=e^{G^{\prime}(0)},

(192)

where

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)^{2s}\sum_{\vec{n}}(\lambda^{(\rho)}_{\vec{n}})^{s}(\lambda^{(\tau)}_{\vec{n}})^{s}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)^{2s}\tilde{G}(s).

(193)

It follows that

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=2\ln\left(\frac{-2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)\tilde{G}(0)+\sum_{\vec{n}}\ln\lambda^{(\rho)}_{\vec{n}}+\sum_{\vec{n}}\ln\lambda^{(\tau)}_{\vec{n}},

(194)

and hence the determinant takes the form

\displaystyle{\rm det}^{\prime}(\tilde{\Delta})=\left(\frac{2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)^{2\tilde{G}(0)}D_{+}(\rho)D_{-}(\tau).

(195)

In this way the path integral on a generic Riemann surface can be written as

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\sim\left(\frac{-2i}{\rho-\tau}\right)^{-2\tilde{G}(0)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{+}(\rho)D_{-}(\tau)}}Z_{z.m}e^{-S_{n.l}}e^{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{\cal J}\wedge\tilde{\star}J}.

(196)

To evaluate
Z_{z.m.}
we recall that the zero-modes satisfy
\tilde{\Delta}P=0
. To obtain these we note that

is a one-form and hence can be written as

where
\tilde{\star}\alpha_{\pm}=\pm i\alpha_{\pm}
and

\tilde{\Delta}P=0
now imposes that

. Thus we find that

and
d\tilde{\star}\alpha_{+}=d\tilde{\star}\alpha_{-}=0

Furthermore, these can have non-zero periods over the 1-cycles. This in turn means that

is not globally defined but is allowed to be multi-valued although the periods are restricted, for example to be integer. This means that the integration by parts that we used above is not valid.

To continue, we write

and

where the

\displaystyle Z_{z.m.}

\displaystyle={\rm exp}\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{\hat{m}^{i},\hat{n}^{j}}\int\Big(i(\hat{m}_{i}\alpha^{i}_{+}+\hat{n}_{i}\alpha^{i}_{-})\wedge(\hat{m}_{j}\alpha^{j}_{+}-\hat{n}_{j}\alpha^{j}_{-})-2i{\cal J}\wedge(\hat{m}_{i}\alpha^{i}_{+}-\hat{n}_{i}\alpha^{i}_{-})\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_{\hat{m}_{i}}\hat{m}_{i}\sum_{k}\Big(\oint_{A^{k}}{\cal J}\oint_{B_{k}}\alpha_{+}^{i}-\oint_{B_{k}}{\cal J}\oint_{A^{k}}\alpha_{+}^{i}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_{\hat{n}_{i}}\hat{n}_{i}\sum_{k}\Big(\oint_{A^{k}}{\cal J}\oint_{B_{k}}\alpha_{-}^{i}-\oint_{B_{k}}{\cal J}\oint_{A^{k}}\alpha_{-}^{i}\Big)\Bigg).

To clean this up, we choose a basis where

\displaystyle\oint_{A^{k}}\alpha_{+}^{i}

\displaystyle=\delta^{i}_{k}

\displaystyle\oint_{B_{k}}\alpha_{+}^{i}

\displaystyle={\cal T}^{ik},

(198)

and

\displaystyle\oint_{A^{k}}\alpha_{-}^{i}

\displaystyle=\delta^{i}_{k}

\displaystyle\oint_{B_{k}}\alpha_{-}^{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\cal T}^{ik},

(199)

\displaystyle Z_{z.m.}={\rm exp}\Bigg(-\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_{\hat{\bf n},\hat{\bf m}}\hat{\bf m}\cdot(\tilde{\cal T}^{T}-{\cal T})\hat{\bf n}-

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_{\hat{\bf m}}\hat{\bf m}\cdot\left({\cal T}\oint_{\bf A}{\cal J}-\oint_{\bf B}{\cal J}\right)

7
Discussion and Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the bi-metric generalisation
Hull:2023dgp
of the Sen formulation
Sen:2015nph
;
Sen:2019qit
applied to chiral bosons on a Riemann surface. Analytically continuing these two metrics to Riemannian ones, we showed that the bi-metric action reduces to that of a non-chiral boson coupled to a complex metric. We were then able to compute the partition function and gave an explicit expression for the case in which the 2d-space is a torus.

Let us review our calculation. Formally, the Sen action is defined in terms of two Lorentzian metrics
\bar{g}
and
g
which we have parameterised by four independent real functions
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau,\tilde{\tau}
with
\rho\neq\tilde{\rho}
and
\tau\neq\tilde{\tau}
(as well as conformal factors that drop out of our calculations). As such, the action is real and the partition function, as defined by the path-integral in (
3.1
), is purely oscillatory and ill-defined. However, we can analytically continue
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau,\tilde{\tau}
to the complex plane where the action becomes complex and, at least for a certain range of
\rho,\tilde{\rho},\tau,\tilde{\tau}
, it is damped allowing us to evaluate the partition function. In particular, this includes the regime
\tilde{\rho}=\rho^{*}
and
\tilde{\tau}=\tau^{*}
, where
\bar{g}
and
g
are Riemannian metrics, provided we take
\tau_{2}>0
and
\rho_{2}<0
and sufficiently large in magnitude.

We also discussed modular properties of our partition function and showed that in general it is only invariant under transformations where the two complex structures transform in the same way. This is expected since only these transformations arise from coordinate transformations acting on the torus. In particular the zero mode contributions to the partition function do not factorise. However, for applications to the heterotic string theory we are interested in partition functions where
\tau
and
\rho
can be independently transformed. We showed that by considering
n
chiral scalars, with
n
a multiple of 8, the corresponding partition function arising from a Narain Lattice can factorise into the simple form

Z(\tau,\rho)=F(\tau)\tilde{F}(\rho)\ ,

(204)

and hence one can construct theories where the modular transformations of
\tau
and
\rho
can be made independent and moreover such that the theory is invariant under two sets of modular transformations. We showed how to construct a consistent worldsheet heterotic string theory by including anomaly cancelling matter fields in both the physical and shadow sectors.

We have also seen that in order to have a well-defined partition function we needed to have
M\neq 0
to provide the damping in the integrals.
Specifically, for a given physical metric
g
, we chose the metric
\bar{g}
so that
M\neq 0
.
If we were to try to directly consider the case
M=0
, then the action would be simply

\displaystyle S=\int Q^{\prime}\wedge dP,

(205)

where
Q^{\prime}=2Q+\tfrac{1}{2}P+\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{\star}dP
. In two dimensions, this is a well-known conformal field theory; the holomorphic
\beta\gamma
system, with
c=2
. However, it generalises to higher dimensions where it resembles a topological
B\wedge F
theory except that there is now a metric dependent constraint
Q^{\prime}=\bar{\star}Q^{\prime}
. We have examined this theory separately in
Hull:2025bqo
.

In this paper we use two independent metrics
\bar{g}
and

\bar{\star}
and

.

We adopt the following convention: the zwiebein indices are labelled as

. In particular we never use

or

for world indices. For example

\displaystyle V_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bar{e}_{\mu}{}^{a}V_{a}

(206)

\displaystyle=\bar{e}_{\mu}{}^{+}V_{+}+\bar{e}_{\mu}{}^{-}V_{-},

(207)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{+}=\bar{e}_{+}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\qquad\partial_{-}=\bar{e}_{-}{}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu},

(208)

which in general are not derivatives with respect to a pair of coordinates
x^{+}
and
x^{-}
.

Next we choose a convention where

is defined to be anti-symmetric with
\epsilon_{01}=1
. Similarly
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}
is anti-symmetric with
\epsilon^{01}=-1
. This implies that
\epsilon_{+-}=-\epsilon^{+-}=1
.

Given a metric, say
\bar{g}
, and one-form

we define

\displaystyle\bar{\star}V=\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\bar{g}^{\nu\lambda}V_{\lambda}dx^{\mu}.

(209)

It follows that
\bar{\star}^{2}V=V
for any one-form
V
and for any metric
\bar{g}
, including complex ones.

The benefit of the zwiebein basis is that

\displaystyle\bar{\star}Q_{a}=\epsilon_{ab}\eta^{bc}Q_{c},

(210)

i.e.

\displaystyle\bar{\star}Q_{\pm}=\pm Q_{\pm},

(211)

and hence a condition such as
Q=\bar{\star}Q
simply implies that
Q_{-}=0
.

Lastly for a two-form
\omega^{(2)}=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}
we define

\displaystyle\int\omega^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int\omega^{(2)}dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\omega^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}d^{2}x.

(212)

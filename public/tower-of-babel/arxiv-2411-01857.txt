On ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips complexes
Sergei O. Ivanov, Xiaomeng Xu

Abstract
. We study the concepts of the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips simplicial set and the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips complex of a metric space, where 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ . 1\leq p\leq\infty. This theory unifies two established theories: for p = ∞ , p=\infty, this is the classical theory of Vietoris-Rips complexes, and for p = 1 , p=1, this corresponds to the blurred magnitude homology theory. We prove several results that are known for the Vietoris-Rips complex in the general case: (1) we prove a stability theorem for the corresponding version of the persistent homology; (2) we show that, for a compact Riemannian manifold and a sufficiently small scale parameter, all the “ ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips spaces” are homotopy equivalent to the manifold; (3) we demonstrate that the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips spaces are invariant (up to homotopy) under taking the metric completion. Additionally, we show that the limit of the homology groups of the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips spaces, as the scale parameter tends to zero, does not depend on p p ; and that the homology groups of the ℓ p \ell_{p} -Vietoris-Rips spaces commute with filtered colimits of metric spaces.



1.
Introduction

The Vietoris-Rips complex of a metric space was introduced by Vietoris to define the homology theory of a metric space
[
30
]
. Later, Rips used this complex to study hyperbolic groups. The work of Rips was popularized by Gromov in
[
13
]
. Hausmann proved that for a compact
Riemannian manifold
M
and a sufficiently small scale parameter
r,
the geometric realization of the Vietoris-Rips complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}M
is homotopy equivalent to
M

[
15
]
. He also showed that the Vietoris-Rips complex is invariant up to homotopy under taking the metric completion
|\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}X|\simeq|\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}\hat{X}|.

Adamaszek and Adams identified the homotopy type of the Vietoris-Rips complex of the circle for any scale parameter
r

[
1
]
. They proved that all odd spheres
S^{1},S^{3},S^{5},\dots
appear as homotopy types of the Vietoris-Rips complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}S^{1}
for some
r.

Carlsson-Carlsson-De Silva applied the Vietoris-Rips complex in topological data analysis
[
3
]
, and Chazal with his coauthors proved the stability theorem for the Vietoris-Rips complex

[
5
]
(see also
[
4
]
).

The magnitude function of a compact metric space was introduced by Leinster
[
24
]
. It was treated as an analog of the Euler characteristic of a topological space. The corresponding (co)homology theory was developed by Hepworth and Willerton for the case of graphs
[
17
]
, and extended to all metric spaces by Leinster, Shulman
[
25
]
and Hepworth
[
16
]
under the name of magnitude (co)homology theory. Otter introduced a persistent version of the magnitude homology under the name of blurred magnitude homology
[
28
]
. She proved that the homology groups of the Vietoris-Rips complex and the blurred magnitude homology have the same limit, as the scale parameter tends to zero.
Govc and Hepworth
[
12
]
showed that the magnitude function of a finite metric space can be also recovered from the blurred magnitude homology.

In an unpublished preprint
[
6
]
, Cho used the language of enriched categories over quantales to develop a theory that includes the Vietoris-Rips complex and the blurred homology theory as special cases. For each
1\leq p\leq\infty,
a metric space
X
and a real number
r>0,
he defines a simplicial set which we call the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set
.
For
p=\infty,
the geometric realization of the simplicial set is homotopy equivalent to the geometric realization of the classical Vietoris-Rips complex, and for
p=1,
its homology is the blurred magnitude homology.

We provide an alternative definition of the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets in terms of the
\ell_{p}
-weight
of a tuple of points in a metric space. Our definition is shown to be equivalent to the definition of Cho (Proposition
2.6
).
Since simplicial complexes are more convenient for data analysis, we also define
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes in terms of
\ell_{p}
-weights of finite subsets. We develop a theory that works in both of these two settings
— the setting of simplicial sets and the setting of simplicial complexes.
In particular, we prove the stability theorem, the result about Riemannian manifolds, the result about metric completions, the result about limit homology and commutativity with filtered colimits for both of these two settings.
More precisely, we introduce a general framework that includes both of these two settings and prove the results in this general framework.

The general framework is based on a general concept we introduce called a
distance matrix norm
. Roughly speaking, a distance matrix norm
\nu
is a norm defined on the “space” of all distance matrices of all tuples. A distance matrix norm
\nu
defines a
\nu
-weight of a tuple of points in a metric space, which is used to define the
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set.
We show that the theory of both
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets and
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes can be reduced to the theory of
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets for appropriate distance matrix norms
\nu
, and prove all relevant statements in the general context of any distance matrix norm.

1.1.
Definitions of the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips spaces

Let us define the main characters of the paper: the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{<r}X
and
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X
and the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{<r}X
and
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X
of a metric space
X
.
The definition is based on the concept of the
\ell_{p}
-weight of a tuple of points in a metric space. It is defined by the formula

(1.1)

\mathpzc{w}_{p}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\underset{0\leq i_{0}<\dots<i_{m}\leq n}{\max}\|d(x_{i_{0}},x_{i_{1}}),\dots,d(x_{i_{m-1}},x_{i_{m}})\|_{p},

where
\|-\|_{p}
is the
\ell_{p}
-norm,
d
is the distance and the maximum is taken over all strictly increasing subsequences
i_{0}<\dots<i_{m}
of
0,\dots,n
.
For
p=1,
using the triangle inequality, we obtain

(1.2)

\mathpzc{w}_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=d(x_{0},x_{1})+\dots+d(x_{n-1},x_{n}).

For
p=\infty,
the
\ell_{p}
-norm is equal to the diameter of the set of points in the tuple

(1.3)

\mathpzc{w}_{\infty}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\mathsf{diam}(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}).

For any
r>0,
the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{<r}X
is defined so that its
n
-simplices are tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
\mathpzc{w}_{p}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})<r,
and the face and degeneracy maps are defined by deletions and doublings. Similarly we define
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X
by replacing
<
with
\leq.

The definition of the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{<r}X
and
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X
is based on the concept of the
\ell_{p}
-weight of a finite non-empty subset of a metric space. It is defined by the formula

(1.4)

\mathtt{w}_{p}(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\})=\min_{\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}}\mathpzc{w}_{p}(x_{\pi(0)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)}),

where
\Sigma_{[n]}
denotes the group of permutations of the set
\{0,\dots,n\}.

Thus the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{<r}X
consists of finite non-empty subsets of
X
whose
\ell_{p}
-weight is less than
r.
The non-strict version of the simplicial complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X
is defined similarly.

The simplicial complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{\infty}_{<r}X
is the classical Vietoris-Rips complex and the geometric realization of the simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{\infty}_{<r}X
is homotopy equivalent to the geometric realization of
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{\infty}_{<r}X.
Moreover, it is easy to see that the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complex forms a sub-complex in the classical Vietoris-Rips complex

\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{<r}X\subseteq\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}X
and
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X\subseteq\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{\leq r}X.

The blurred magnitude homology are the homology of the
\ell_{1}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set.

1.2.
Distance matrix norms

In order to unify the cases of simplicial sets and simplicial complexes, we define a general notion of distance matrix norm.
For
n\geq 0,
we denote by
\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
the subset of the set of real square matrices of order
n+1
consisting of all distance matrices of an
(n+1)
-tuple in a metric space. A distance matrix norm
\nu
consists of maps
\nu:\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
for each
n\geq 0,
satisfying a list of axioms (Definition
2.1
).
The
\nu
-weight of a tuple of points is defined as

(1.5)

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\nu(D(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})),

where
D(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=(d(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j}
is the distance matrix.
Then the
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
is the simplicial set consisting of tuples whose
\nu
-weight is less than
r.

We construct two families of distance matrix norms
\nu_{p}
and
\nu_{p}^{\sf sym}
and show that the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set is the
\nu_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set; and the geometric realization of the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complex is homotopy equivalent to the geometric realization of the
\nu_{p}^{\sf sym}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set. Thus, both of the theories can be reduced to the theory of
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets. We also give some other interesting examples of distance matrix norms (Subsection
2.5
).

1.3.
Stability theorem

For any distance matrix norm
\nu,
we prove a version of the stability theorem for the “
\nu
-persistent homology”.
Namely, we show that the interleaving distance between persistent modules
H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{\nu}X)
and
H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{\nu}Y)
is not greater than
2C_{n+2}(\nu)\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y),
where
d_{GH}
is the Gromov-Hausdorff distance and
C_{n}(\nu)
denotes some constant (Theorem
4.2
)

(1.6)

d_{\sf int}(H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{\nu}X),H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{\nu}Y))\leq 2C_{n+2}(\nu)\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y).

As corollaries, we obtain the inequality for
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets

(1.7)

d_{\sf int}(H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{p}X),H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}_{<*}^{p}Y))\leq 2(n+2)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y),

for
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes

(1.8)

d_{\sf int}(H_{n}(\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<*}^{p}X),H_{n}(\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<*}^{p}Y))\leq 2(n+2)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y),

and for the blurred magnitude homology

(1.9)

d_{\sf int}(\mathsf{MH}_{n,<*}(X),\mathsf{MH}_{n,<*}(Y))\leq 2(n+2)\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y).

1.4.
Riemannian manifolds

Hausmann showed that for a compact Riemannian manifold
M
and a sufficiently small scale parameter
r>0
the geometric realization of the Vietoris-Rips complex
\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}M
is homotopy equivalent to
M

[
15
]
. We generalize this theorem by showing that the geometric realization of
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}M
is homotopy equivalent to
M
for a sufficiently small
r>0
and any
\nu.
In particular, we obtain that the blurred magnitude homology of
M
is isomorphic to the ordinary homology of
M
for a sufficiently small
r.

Moreover, we prove a more general version of this theorem for geodesic spaces (Theorem
7.1
).

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty,
we define
r_{p}(M)
as the infimum of
r>0
such that the geometric realization of
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{<r}M
is not homotopy equivalent to
M.
We compute this number for the circle of perimeter one
S^{1}
:

(1.10)

r_{p}(S^{1})=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2+2^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

In particular, we obtain
r_{\infty}(S^{1})=\frac{1}{3},
which coincides with the result of
Adamaszek and Adams
[
1
]
.

1.5.
Metric completions and shortly contractible spaces

We prove a general theorem (Theorem
5.4
) which states that under certain conditions an embedding of metric spaces
A\hookrightarrow X
induces a weak equivalence of simplicial sets

(1.11)

\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}A\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X

for any distance matrix norm
\nu
and any
r>0.
As a corollary we show that the homotopy type of the strict
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set does not change after taking the metric completion:

(1.12)

|\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X|\simeq|\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}\hat{X}|.

In particular, we obtain

\mathsf{MH}_{n,<r}(X)\cong\mathsf{MH}_{n,<r}(\hat{X}).

We say that a metric space
X
is
shortly contractible
if there exists a contracting homotopy to the point
h:X\times[0,c]\to X,
which is a short map (Definition
5.9
). For example, bounded convex subsets, bounded star domains and the semicircle are shortly contractible. As a corollary of the general theorem, we obtain that for a shortly contractible metric space
X
the simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
is weakly contractible. In particular, we have

\mathsf{MH}_{n,<r}(X)=0
for
n\geq 1.

Note that these results hold only for the strict versions of the simplicial sets
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
and do not hold for the non-strict versions
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{\leq r}X.
There is an example of a metric space
X
such that

\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq r}(X)\not\cong\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq r}(\hat{X})
(Remark
5.8
), and there is also an example of a shortly contractible metric space
X
such that
\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq r}(X)\neq 0

(Remark
5.15
).

1.6.
Pro-simplicial sets and limit homology

Despite the fact that for different distance matrix norms
\nu
the simplicial sets
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
are usually different, all of them are similar for small
r.
This observation can be formalized in terms of pro-simplicial sets, which can be understood as “formal cofiltered limits” of simplicial sets.

We denote by
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu,n}_{<r}X
the
n
-skeleton of the
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set.
Then the functor

(0,\infty)\to{\sf sSet}

sending
r
to
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu,n}_{<r}X
defines a pro-simplicial set that we denote by
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu,n}_{\sf pro}X.
We prove that this pro-simplicial set does not depend on
\nu

(1.13)

\mathscr{VR}^{\nu,n}_{\sf pro}X\cong\mathscr{VR}^{\infty,n}_{\sf pro}X.

In particular, we obtain that the limits of homology groups coincide

(1.14)

\lim_{r\to 0}H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X)\cong\lim_{r\to 0}H_{n}(\scalebox{0.8}[1.0]{\tt VR}_{<r}X).

1.7.
Filtered colimits of metric spaces

A disadvantage of the ordinary magnitude homology is that it does not commute with filtered colimits of metric spaces and short maps. However, we show that the strict versions of the
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
commute with filtered colimits of metric spaces up to homotopy. More precisely, for any filtered category
I
and any
I
-diagram
X_{i}
in the category of metric spaces and short maps the map

(1.15)

\colim(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X_{i})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}(\colim X_{i})

is a weak equivalence (Theorem
10.3
). In particular, the strictly blurred magnitude homology commutes with filtered colimits of metric spaces and short maps

(1.16)

\colim\mathsf{MH}_{n,<r}(X_{i})\cong\mathsf{MH}_{n,<r}(\colim X_{i}).

2.
Vietoris-Rips spaces

This section is devoted to unifying the notion of “Vietoris-Rips spaces” through the concept of distance matrix norms. We define a distance matrix norm, and for any distance matrix norm
\nu,
left infinite interval
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and metric space
X,
we associate a simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X.

We will show that the study of both
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets and
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips complexes can be reduced to the study of the simplicial set
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X
for a general
\nu,
and the blurred magnitude homology theory can be thought of as the study of the
\ell_{1}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set.
Throughout the article, we denote by
X,Y
some metric spaces, by
p
an element of
[1,\infty],
and by
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}
a left infinite interval, which is one of the sets
(-\infty,r),(-\infty,r]
or
\mathbb{R}.

2.1.
Distance matrix norms

For
n\geq 0,
we denote by
\mathsf{Mat}_{[n]}(\mathbb{R})
the set of real square matrices
A=(A_{i,j})_{i,j\in[n]}
of order
n+1
, whose entries are indexed by the set
[n]=\{0,\dots,n\}.

Consider a simplicial set
\mathsf{Mat}_{[\bullet]}(\mathbb{R}),
whose
n
-th component is

\mathsf{Mat}_{[n]}(\mathbb{R}),

the face map
\partial_{i}
is defined by deleting the
i
-th row and the
i
-th column of a matrix, and the degeneracy map
s_{i}
is defined by doubling the
i
-th row and the
i
-th column of a matrix. In other words, we can say that the simplicial set
\mathsf{Mat}_{[\bullet]}(\mathbb{R}):\Delta^{op}\to{\sf Set}
is defined so that, its
n
-simplices are maps
A:[n]\times[n]\to\mathbb{R}
and for a monotone map
f:[m]\to[n],
the map
f^{*}:\mathsf{Mat}_{[n]}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathsf{Mat}_{[m]}(\mathbb{R})
defined by
f^{*}A=A(f\times f).

We consider a simplicial subset of “distance matrices”

(2.1)

\mathsf{Dist}_{[\bullet]}\subseteq\mathsf{Mat}_{[\bullet]}(\mathbb{R})

consisting of matrices
D
such that
D_{i,j}\geq 0,

D_{i,i}=0,

D_{i,j}=D_{j,i}
and
D_{i,j}+D_{j,k}\geq D_{i,k}
for
i,j,k\in[n].
Here we allow
D_{i,j}=0
for
i\neq j.
In other words
\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
consists of all pseudo-metrics on the set
\{0,\dots,n\}.
It is easy to see that
\mathsf{Dist}_{[\bullet]}
is a simplicial subset of
\mathsf{Mat}_{[\bullet]}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, for each
n
the set
\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
is a convex cone in
\mathsf{Mat}_{[n]}(\mathbb{R})
i.e. for any
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
we have
\alpha D+\alpha^{\prime}D^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}.

Definition 2.1
.

By a
distance matrix norm
, we mean a collection of maps

(2.2)

\nu:\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\hskip 28.45274ptn\geq 0

such that for any
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
and any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
we have

(1)

\nu(\alpha\cdot D)=\alpha\cdot\nu(D);

(2)

\nu(D+D^{\prime})\leq\nu(D)+\nu(D^{\prime});

(3)

If
D_{i,j}\leq D^{\prime}_{i,j}
for all
i,j\in[n],
then
\nu(D)\leq\nu(D^{\prime});

(4)

\nu(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix})=1;

(5)

\nu(\partial_{i}D)\leq\nu(D)
and
\nu(s_{i}D)=\nu(D).

The property (3) is called monotone; the property (4) is called normalization; the property (5) is called simpliciality.

We denote by
E_{n}\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
the matrix such that
(E_{n})_{i,j}=1,
for any
i\neq j,
and
(E_{n})_{i,i}=0,
for any
i.
For a distance matrix norm
\nu,
we consider an increasing sequence of constants
C_{n}(\nu)

(2.3)

1=C_{1}(\nu)\leq C_{2}(\nu)\leq C_{3}(\nu)\leq\dots

defined by the formula

(2.4)

C_{n}(\nu)=\nu(E_{n}).

The sequence is increasing because
\partial_{0}E_{n+1}=E_{n}
and
\nu(\partial_{0}D)\leq\nu(D).

Let
\Sigma_{[n]}
be the group of permutations of
[n].
For a matrix
D\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
and
\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]},
we denote by
D^{\pi}
a matrix such that
D^{\pi}_{i,j}=D_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}.
A distance matrix norm
\nu
is called
symmetric
if

(2.5)

\nu(D)=\nu(D^{\pi})

for all
D\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}
and
\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}
.
For any distance matrix norm
\nu,
we define a collection of maps
\nu^{\sf sym}:\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.6)

\nu^{\sf sym}(D)=\min_{\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}}\nu(D^{\pi}).

Lemma 2.2
.

For any distance matrix norm
\nu,
the collection of maps

\nu^{\sf sym}
is a symmetric distance matrix norm.

Proof.

The properties (1)-(4) are obvious. The property
\nu^{\sf sym}(\partial_{i}D)\leq\nu^{\sf sym}(D)
is also obvious. Let us prove that for any matrix
D\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]},
we have
\nu^{\sf sym}(s_{i}D)=\nu^{\sf sym}(D).
Since
\partial_{i}s_{i}D=D,
we have
\nu^{\sf sym}(s_{i}D)\geq\nu^{\sf sym}(D).

Let us prove that
\nu^{\sf sym}(s_{i}D)\leq\nu^{\sf sym}(D).
Here we will treat the matrix
D
as a map
D:[n]\times[n]\to\mathbb{R}
and any map
f:[m]\to[n]
we set
D^{f}=D(f\times f).
Then
\partial_{k}D=D^{\partial^{k}}
and
s_{k}D=D^{s^{k}},
where
\partial^{k}:[n-1]\to[n]
and
s^{k}:[n+1]\to[n]
are the coface and codegeneracy maps. Take
\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}
such that
\nu^{\sf sym}(D)=\nu(D^{\pi}).
Then there exists
\pi^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{[n+1]}
such that

s^{i}\pi^{\prime}=\pi s^{\pi^{-1}(i)}.

Indeed, it can be defined by the formula

(2.7)

\pi^{\prime}(k)=\begin{cases}\partial^{i+1}\pi s^{\pi^{-1}(i)}(k),&k\neq\pi^{-1}(i)+1,\\
i+1,&k=\pi^{-1}(i)+1.\end{cases}

Then we obtain

\nu^{\sf sym}(D^{s^{i}})\leq\nu(D^{s^{i}\pi^{\prime}})=\nu(D^{\pi s^{\pi^{-1}(i)}})\ =\nu(D^{\pi}),

and hence
\nu^{\sf sym}(s_{i}D)\leq\nu^{\sf sym}(D).

∎

Two main examples of distance matrix norms for us are the following. For
p\in[1,\infty],
we set

(2.8)

\nu_{p}(D)=\max_{0\leq i_{0}<\dots<i_{m}\leq n}\|D_{i_{0},i_{1}},D_{i_{1},i_{2}},\dots,D_{i_{m-1},i_{m}}\|_{p},

where
\|-\|_{p}
denotes the
\ell_{p}
-norm. Since the diagonal of a distance matrix
D
is trivial, we have
(s_{i}D)_{i,i+1}=0.
Using this we can prove that
\nu_{p}(s_{i}D)=\nu_{p}(D).
All other conditions of a distance matrix norm are obvious. The second example that we need is the symmetric version of this distance matrix norm

(2.9)

\nu_{p}^{\sf sym}(D)=\min_{\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}}\nu_{p}(D^{\pi}).

The sequences of constants
C_{n}
for these examples are

(2.10)

C_{n}(\nu_{p})=C_{n}(\nu_{p}^{\sf sym})=n^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

2.2.
Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets

Let

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\nu
be a distance matrix norm,
\sigma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n+1}
be a tuple and
0\leq i\leq n
. Set
\tilde{\sigma}=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}.
Then the following holds.

(1)

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\partial_{i}\sigma)\leq\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma)
and

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(s_{i}\sigma)=\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma).

(2)

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{0},x_{1})=d(x_{0},x_{1}).

(3)

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})+\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

(4)

\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq\sum_{j}d(x_{j},x_{j+1}).

(5)

\mathsf{diam}(\tilde{\sigma})\leq\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq C_{n}(\nu)\cdot\mathsf{diam}(\tilde{\sigma}).

Proof.

(1). Follows from the simpliciality of
\nu.

(2). Follows from
\nu(D(x_{0},x_{1}))=\nu(d(x_{0},x_{1})\cdot E_{1})=d(x_{0},x_{1})\cdot\nu(E_{1})=d(x_{0},x_{1}).

(3). Consider a matrix
A
defined by

(2.12)

A_{k,l}=\begin{cases}d(x_{k},x_{l}),&k,l\leq i,\text{ or }i\leq k,l,\\
d(x_{k},x_{i})+d(x_{i},x_{l}),&k<i<l,\text{ or }l<i<k.\end{cases}

Then
D(\sigma)_{k,l}\leq A_{k,l}
and

(2.13)

A=s_{n}\dots s_{i+1}s_{i}D(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})+s_{0}\dots s_{0}D(x_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

In particular, we obtain
A\in\mathsf{Dist}_{[n]}.

Then
\nu(D(\sigma))\leq\nu(A)\leq\nu(D(x_{0},\dots,x_{i}))+\nu(D(x_{i},\dots,x_{n})).

(4). Follows from (2) and (3).

(5). (1) and (2) imply that
d(x_{k},x_{l})=\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{k},x_{l})\leq\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma).
Then
\mathsf{diam}(\tilde{\sigma})\leq\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma).
On the other hand
D(\sigma)_{i,j}\leq\mathsf{diam}(\tilde{\sigma})\cdot(E_{n})_{i,j}.
Then
\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq C_{n}(\nu)\cdot\mathsf{diam}(\tilde{\sigma}).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any sequence
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in X
(with possible repetitions), we have

(2.29)

\mathtt{w}_{\nu}(\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\})=\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n}).

Proof.

Set
\sigma=\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}.

If all points
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
are distinct, then it is obvious. If they are not distinct, there exists a permutation
\pi\in\Sigma_{[n]}
and a subsequence

\sigma=\{x_{\pi(i_{0})},\dots,x_{\pi(i_{m})}\}
and for

we have
x_{\pi(i_{k})}=x_{\pi(j)}.
Then
(x_{\pi(0)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})

can be obtained from
(x_{\pi(i_{0})},\dots,x_{\pi(i_{m})})

It follows that
\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{\pi(0)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})=\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}(x_{\pi(i_{0})},\dots,x_{\pi(i_{m})})=\mathtt{w}_{\nu}(\sigma).

∎

2.6.
Cho’s definition

We fix some

and consider the binary operation for non-negative real numbers

(2.45)

If

we have

and for

we have

This binary operation defines a structure of a monoid on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

(2.46)

a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n}=\|a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\|_{p}.

For a metric space
X,
Cho defines the
\ell_{p}
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial set
\widetilde{\mathscr{VR}}^{p}_{\leq r}X
of a metric space
X
as a simplicial set, whose
n
-simplices are tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that there exists a sequence of non-negative real numbers
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
satisfying the following conditions:
r_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{n}\leq r
and
d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq r_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{j}
for
i\leq j
(see

[
6
, Prop. 13]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

The definition of Cho is equivalent to our definition

(2.47)

\widetilde{\mathscr{VR}}^{p}_{\leq r}X=\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X.

Proof.

First we prove that
\widetilde{\mathscr{VR}}^{p}_{\leq r}X\subseteq\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X.
Take
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in(\widetilde{\mathscr{VR}}^{p}_{\leq r}X)_{n}.
Then there exist
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
such that
r_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{n}\leq r
and

d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq r_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{j}
for
i\leq j.
Then for any subsequence
0\leq i_{0}<\dots<i_{m}\leq n,
we have
d(x_{i_{0}},x_{i_{1}})\otimes\dots\otimes d(x_{i_{m-1}},x_{i_{m}})\leq r_{i_{0}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{i_{m}}\leq r.
Therefore
\mathpzc{w}_{p}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\leq r.

Now we prove that
\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X\subseteq\widetilde{\mathscr{VR}}^{p}_{\leq r}X.
For any
n
-simplex
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in(\mathscr{VR}^{p}_{\leq r}X)_{n}
, we define
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
inductively. First we set
r_{1}=d(x_{0},x_{1})
. Suppose we have defined
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k-1}
. We define
r_{k}
to be the least number such that the following inequalities are satisfied:

(2.48)

d(x_{i},x_{k})\leq r_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes r_{k}\hskip 14.22636pt0\leq i\leq k-1.

Since it is the least number, there exists an index
f(k)<k
such that the equality holds

(2.49)

Now we verify that such

satisfy the desired properties. Since
0\leq f(k)<k,
there exists
m
such that
f^{m}(n)=0.
We consider the subsequence
0\leq i_{0}<\dots<i_{m}\leq n
defined by
i_{j}=f^{m-j}(n).
Then

(2.50)

d(x_{i_{j}},x_{i_{j+1}})=r_{i_{j}+1}\otimes\dots\otimes r_{i_{j+1}}.

It follows that

(2.51)

r_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes r_{n}=d(x_{i_{0}},x_{i_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes d(x_{i_{m-1}},x_{i_{m}})\leq r,

and for any

, we know from the construction of

that

(2.52)

d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq r_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes r_{j}.

Therefore we complete the proof.
∎

3.
Lipschitz and short maps

In this section we introduce the notion of an
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz map
f:X\to Y,
and an
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz pair of maps
f,g:X\to Y.
We show that an
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz map
f
induces a morphism of
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets with appropriate scale parameters. Moreover, if

is an
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz pair, then these morphisms are simplicially homotopic.

Throughout this section
X,Y
denote two metric spaces;
L
denotes a left infinite interval;
\nu
denotes a distance matrix norm. We also set
C_{n}=C_{n}(\nu),

\mathpzc{w}=\mathpzc{w}_{\nu}
and assume that
a,b\geq 0
are two non-negative real numbers.

A map between metric spaces
f:X\to Y
is called
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz
, if for any

we have

(3.1)

d(x,x^{\prime})\in L\hskip 14.22636pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.22636ptd(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq a\cdot d(x,x^{\prime})+b.

If
a=1,
then
f
is called a
L
-locally
b
-short map. Note that if
b\neq 0,
the maps are not necessarily continuous.

A pair of maps
f,g:X\to Y
is called
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz pair
, if both of them are
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz and, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we have

(3.2)

d(x,x^{\prime})\in L\hskip 14.22636pt\Rightarrow\hskip 14.22636ptd(f(x),g(x^{\prime}))\leq a\cdot d(x,x^{\prime})+b.

If
a=1,
then
f,g
is called an
L
-locally
b
-short pair. If
L=\mathbb{R}
, we use terms “
(a,b)
-Lipschitz map”, “

-short map”, “
(a,b)
-Lipschitz pair” and “

-short pair”.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n+1}
and
(y_{0},\dots,y_{n})\in Y^{n+1}
be tuples of points, and
a,b\geq 0
be real numbers such that

(3.3)

d(y_{i},y_{j})\leq a\cdot d(x_{i},x_{j})+b,\hskip 28.45274pt0\leq i,j\leq n.

Then

(3.4)

\mathpzc{w}(y_{0},\dots,y_{n})\leq a\cdot\mathpzc{w}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})+b\cdot C_{n}.

Proof.

The conditions imply that
D(y_{0},\dots,y_{n})_{i,j}\leq a\cdot D(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})_{i,j}+b\cdot(E_{n})_{i,j}.
Therefore, using the properties of a distance matrix norm, we obtain

(3.5)

\nu(D(y_{0},\dots,y_{n}))\leq a\cdot\nu(D(x_{0},\dots,x_{n}))+b\cdot\nu(E_{n}).

The statement follows.
∎

For a simplicial complex
K,
we denote by
\mathsf{SS}(K)
the simplicial set consisting of tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
such that
\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}\in K.

Note that

(3.6)

\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X\subseteq\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(X)).

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
n\geq 0,
an
L
-locally
(a,b)
-Lipschitz map

induces a morphism of simplicial sets

Proof.

For

the statement is obvious, so let us assume that

For a tuple
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X)_{n},
we have
(x_{i},x_{j})\in(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X)_{1}
for

4.
Stability theorem

In this section, we will prove a stability theorem for homology of
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets. We will show that the interleaving distance between the persistent modules
H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<*}X)
and
H_{n}(\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<*}Y)
is bounded above by
2C_{n+2}(\nu)\cdot d_{GH}(X,Y),
where
d_{GH}
is the Gromov-Hausdorff distance. Throughout this section we denote by
X
and
Y
some metric spaces, by
\nu
a distance matrix norm, and set
C_{n}=C_{n}(\nu).

Let us recall the definitions of the Hausdorff distance and the Gromov-Hausdorff distance. If
Z
is a metric space, the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}(X,Y)
between subsets
X,Y\subseteq Z
is the infimum of constants
c>0
such that for any
x\in X
there exists
y\in Y
such that
d(x,y)\leq c,
and for any
y\in Y
there exists
x\in X
such that
d(y,x)\leq c.

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance
d_{GH}(X,Y)
between any metric spaces
X
and
Y
is defined as the infimum of
d_{H}(i(X),j(Y))
taken by all isometric embeddings of
X
and
Y
to some metric space
i:X\hookrightarrow Z\hookleftarrow Y:j.

Here we do not assume that
X
and
Y
are compact, so the distance
d_{GH}(X,Y)
can be zero for non-isometric spaces, and can also be infinite.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\delta>2d_{GH}(X,Y).
Then there exist
\delta
-short maps
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to X
such that
({\sf id}_{X},gf)
and
({\sf id}_{Y},fg)
are
2\delta
-short pairs.

Proof.

By the definition of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, we can choose some embeddings to an ambient space
X,Y\hookrightarrow Z
such that
\delta>2d_{H}(X,Y).
Then for any
x\in X,
there exists an element
y\in Y
such that
d(x,y)\leq\delta/2.
Choosing one
y=f(x)
for each
x,
we define a function
f:X\to Y
such that

d(x,f(x))\leq\delta/2.

Similarly, we define a function
g:Y\to X
such that
d(y,g(y))\leq\delta/2
for any
y\in Y.
These inequalities imply the following inequalities

(4.1)

d(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d(x,x^{\prime})+\delta,\hskip 28.45274ptd(g(y),g(y^{\prime}))\leq d(y,y^{\prime})+\delta.

(4.2)

d(x,gf(x^{\prime}))\leq d(x,x^{\prime})+\delta,\hskip 28.45274ptd(y,fg(y^{\prime}))\leq d(y,y^{\prime})+\delta.

for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y.

Since
f
and
g
are
\delta
-short, their compositions
gf
and
fg
are
2\delta
-short. The statement follows.

6.
Nerve theorem and Mayer-Vietoris sequence

This section is devoted to results that allow us to reduce the computation of the homotopy type of
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{L}X
of a metric space
X
covered by a family of subsets

to the homotopy types of

Let

be a set (resp. simplicial set) and

(Nerve theorem)
.

be a left infinite interval,

be a metric space and

It is easy to see that for

Then the statement follows from the nerve theorem for simplicial sets that can be found in Appendix (Theorem

10.
Filtered colimits of metric spaces

The content of this section is the result of a personal discussion with Emily Roff, to whom we express our gratitude. Here we prove that
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}
commutes (up to homotopy) with filtered colimits of metric spaces and short maps. As a corollary, we obtain that strictly blurred magnitude homology commutes with filtered colimits.

A disadvantage of the ordinary magnitude homology theory is that it does not commute with filtered colimits of metric spaces in the category of metric spaces and short maps. Indeed, if we consider two-element metric spaces
X_{n}=\{x_{n},y_{n}\},
where the distance is defined by
d(x_{n},y_{n})=1+\frac{1}{n},
and take the obvious short maps
X_{n}\to X_{n+1},
then
\colim X_{n}=X=\{x,y\},
where
d(x,y)=1.
However,
\mathsf{MH}_{1,1}(X_{n})=0
for
n<\infty,
and
\mathsf{MH}_{1,1}(X)=\langle(x,y),(y,x)\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
Therefore

(10.1)

\colim\mathsf{MH}_{1,1}(X_{n})\ncong\mathsf{MH}_{1,1}(X).

It is also easy to see that
\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq 1}(X_{n})=0
and
\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq 1}(X)=\langle(x,y)+(y,x)\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}.
Therefore

(10.2)

\colim\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq 1}(X_{n})\ncong\mathsf{MH}_{1,\leq 1}(X).

However, further we prove that strictly blurred magnitude homology commute with filtered colimits.

In this section it will be convenient to slightly extend the category of metric spaces to the category of “pseudometric” spaces. By a pseudometric space we mean a set
X
equipped by a map
d:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
d(x,x)=0,

d(x,y)=d(x,y)
and
d(x,y)+d(y,z)\geq d(x,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X.
So the distance between different points can be equal to zero. Short maps between pseudometric spaces is defined in the same way as for metric spaces. The category of pseudometric spaces and short maps is denoted by
\mathsf{PMet}.
Then the category of metric spaces and short maps
\mathsf{Met}
is its full subcategory

(10.3)

\mathsf{Met}\subset\mathsf{PMet}.

The Vietoris-Rips complex
\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X
of a pseudometric space
X
is defined in the way similar to the case of metric spaces.

For any pseudometric space
X,
there is a universal map to a metric space called Kolmogorov quotient of
X

(10.4)

X\longrightarrow\mathsf{Kol}(X).

The metric space
\mathsf{Kol}(X)
is defined as the quotient
X/\sim
by the equivalence relation
\sim
such that
x\sim y
if and only if
d(x,y)=0.
The distance in
\mathsf{Kol}(X)
is defined by
d([x],[y])=d(x,y).
Therefore, the category of metric spaces is a reflective subcategory of the category of pseudometric spaces.

Later we show that filtered colimits in
\mathsf{PMet}
are defined in the straightforward way, but filtered colimits in
\mathsf{Met}
are Kolmogorov quotients of the filtered colimits in the category of pseudometric spaces. We will need the following lemma to compare Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets of colimits in the category of metric spaces and in the category of pseudometric spaces.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
\nu
be a distance matrix norm and

For any pseudometric space

the map

induces a trivial fibration of the
\nu
-Vietoris-Rips simplicial sets

(10.5)

\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}X\overset{\sim}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}(\mathsf{Kol}(X)).

Proof.

By
[
11

, we have to check that any lifting problem of the form

(10.6)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{VR}^{\nu}_{<r}(\mathsf{Kol}(X))}

\leftarrow

In general, since

11.
Appendix. Simplicial sets vs. simplicial complexes

We show that there is an adjunction between the category of simplicial sets and simplicial complexes, which defines an equivalence between the category of simplicial complexes and some full subcategory of simplicial sets. We also show that this equivalence agrees with the geometric realization up to homotopy. The results of this section were inspired by the post of John Baez
“Simplicial Sets vs. Simplicial Complexes”
in
“The
n
-category Café ”
, its comments and the discussion in the proof of
[
28
, Prop. 9]
in the paper of Nina Otter. These results are well known to experts, but we decided to add them here for completeness.

By a simplicial complex
K,
we mean a set of finite non-empty sets such that
\emptyset\neq\tau\subseteq\sigma\in K
implies
\tau\in K.
The set of vertices of
K
is defined by
V(K)=\bigcup_{\sigma\in K}\sigma.
A morphism of simplicial complexes
f:K\to L
is a map
f:V(K)\to V(L)
such that for any
\sigma\in K
we have
f(\sigma)\in L.
The category of simplicial complexes is denoted by
\mathsf{SCpx}.

For a simplicial complex
K,
we denote by
\mathsf{SS}(K)
a simplicial set, whose components are defined by

(11.1)

\mathsf{SS}(K)_{n}=\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mid\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}\in K\},

the
i
-th face map is defined by deletion of the
i
-th vertex, and the
i
-th degeneracy map is defined by doubling of the
i
-th vertex.

For a simplicial set
X,
we consider a
map of the
i
-th vertex

(11.2)

v_{i}:X_{n}\longrightarrow X_{0},\hskip 28.45274pt0\leq i\leq n,

which is induced by the map
[0]\to[n]
sending
0
to
i.
For a simplex
x\in X_{n},
we set

(11.3)

v_{*}(x)=(v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{n}(x)).

It is easy to see that there are the following relations with face maps and degeneracy maps

(11.4)

\begin{split}v_{*}(\partial_{i}x)&=(v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{i-1}(x),v_{i+1}(x),\dots,v_{n}(x)),\\
v_{*}(s_{i}x)&=(v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{i}(x),v_{i}(x),\dots,v_{n}(x)).\end{split}

Therefore we can consider a simplicial complex

(11.5)

\mathsf{SC}(X)=\{\{v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{n}(x)\}\mid n\geq 0,x\in X_{n}\}

with a morphism of simplicial sets

(11.6)

v_{*}:X\longrightarrow\mathsf{SS}(\mathsf{SC}(X)).

It is easy to see that for any simplicial complex
K
, there is an isomorphism

(11.7)

\mathsf{SC}(\mathsf{SS}(K))\cong K.

These two constructions define functors between the category of simplicial sets and the category of simplicial complexes

(11.8)

\mathsf{SC}:\mathsf{sSet}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{SCpx}:\mathsf{SS}.

Proposition 11.1
.

The functors (
11.8
) are adjoint

(11.9)

\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{SCpx}}(\mathsf{SC}(X),K)\cong\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{sSet}}(X,\mathsf{SS}(K)),

where
v_{*}:X\to\mathsf{SS}(\mathsf{SC}(X))
is the unit of the adjunction, and the isomorphism
\mathsf{SC}(\mathsf{SS}(K))\cong K
is the counit of the adjunction.

Proof.

Let
X
be a simplicial set and
K
be a simplicial complex. Then a morphism
f:\mathsf{SC}(X)\to K
is a map of sets of vertices
f:X_{0}\to V(K)
such that
f(\{v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{n}(x)\})\in K
for any
x\in X_{n}
. Then the relations (
11.4
) imply that we can construct a morphism of simplicial sets
\tilde{f}:X\to\mathsf{SS}(K)
defined by
\tilde{f}(x)=(f(v_{0}(x)),\dots,f(v_{n}(x))).
We need to show that the correspondence
f\mapsto\tilde{f}
defines a bijection (
11.9
).
The fact that the map is injective is obvious.

Let us prove that it is surjective. Assume that we have a morphism of simplicial sets
g:X\to\mathsf{SS}(K).
For
x\in X_{0}
we set
f(x)=g(x)\in V(K).
This defines a map
f:X_{0}\to V(K).
For
x\in X_{n}
we have
f(v_{i}(x))=v_{i}(g(x)).
Therefore

(11.10)

g(x)=(f(v_{0}(x)),\dots,f(v_{n}(x))).

It follows that
\{f(v_{0}(x)),\dots,f(v_{n}(x))\}\in K.
Hence
f
defines a morphism of simplicial complexes
f:\mathsf{SC}(X)\to K
such that
g=\tilde{f}.

∎

Corollary 11.2
.

The category of simplicial complexes
\mathsf{SCpx}
is equivalent to the full subcategory of the category of simplicial sets
\mathsf{sSet}
consisting of simplicial sets
X
such that the map
v_{*}:X\to\mathsf{SS}(\mathsf{SC}(X))
is an isomorphism.

Further we show that this equivalence of categories agrees with geometric realizations up to homotopy (Theorem
11.5
).

For a set
S,
we denote by
\Delta(S)
the simplicial complex consisting of all non-empty finite subsets of
S.

Lemma 11.3
.

For a non-empty set
S,
the simplicial set
\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(S))
is contractible.

Proof.

Consider the category
\tilde{S},
whose objects are elements of
S
and each hom set consists of one morphism. Then
\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(S))
is isomorphic to the nerve of this category
N(\tilde{S}).
Moreover, the category
\tilde{S}
is equivalent to the one-point category
*.
Therefore
\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(S))\simeq N(\tilde{S})\simeq N(*)=*.

∎

For a simplicial complex
K,
we denote by
P(K)
the poset of its simplices.

Lemma 11.4
.

For a simplicial complex
K,
the inclusions
\Delta(s)\to K,s\in K,
induce an isomorphism

(11.11)

\underset{s\in P(K)}{\colim}\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\cong\mathsf{SS}(K).

Proof.

Let us first formulate a general statement about colimits of sets.
Let
S
be a set and
F:\mathcal{C}\to{\sf Sets}
be a functor from a small category to the category of sets such that: (1)
F(c)\subseteq S;
(2) for any morphism
f:c\to c^{\prime}
the map
F(f):F(c)\to F(c^{\prime})
is the inclusion
F(c)\subseteq F(c^{\prime})
; (3) for any element from an intersection
s\in F(c)\cap F(c^{\prime})
there is an object
c^{\prime\prime}
with morphisms
f:c^{\prime\prime}\to c
and
g:c^{\prime\prime}\to c^{\prime}
such that
s\in F(c^{\prime\prime}).
Then
\colim F=\bigcup_{c\in C}F(c).
Indeed, any natural transformation to a constant functor

\varphi:F\to\Delta T
uniquely defines a well defined map
\tilde{\varphi}:\bigcup_{c\in C}F(c)\to T
such that
\tilde{\varphi}(s)=\varphi_{c}(s)
for
s\in F(c).
Since colimits of simplicial sets are defined component-wise, we can apply this statement to simplicial sets.

Therefore, the statement of the lemma follows from the following facts. For
s\in K
the embedding
\Delta(s)\subseteq K
induces an embedding
\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\subseteq\mathsf{SS}(K).
The simplicial set
\mathsf{SS}(K)
can be presented as the union
\mathsf{SS}(K)=\bigcup_{s\in P(K)}\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s)).
For any
s,t\in K
such that
s\cap t\neq\emptyset
we have
\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\cap\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(t))=\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s\cap t))
and
s\cap t\in K.

∎

Theorem 11.5
.

For a simplicial complex
K,
there is a natural homotopy equivalence of geometric realizations

(11.12)

|K|\simeq|\mathsf{SS}(K)|.

Proof.

Let us remind the notion of a free diagram of simplicial sets
[
8
, §2.4]
,
[
10
, §2.4]
,
[
9
, Appendix I]
. A functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{sSet}
is called free, if it is possible to choose a collection of subsets (called basis of the functor)
B_{n,c}\subseteq F(c)_{n}
for
n\geq 0,c\in\mathcal{C},
which are closed with respect to degeneracy maps
\sigma_{i}(B_{n,c})\subseteq B_{n+1,c},
and such that for any simplex
x\in F(c)_{n}
there exists a unique
f:c^{\prime}\to c
and a unique element of the basis
b\in B_{n,c^{\prime}}
such that
x=F(f)(b).
For a free functor
F
the comparison map
\hocolim F\to\colim F
is a weak equivalence
[
8
, §4.2]
.

We claim that the functor
P(K)\to\mathsf{sSet},s\mapsto\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))
is free. Indeed the sets

(11.13)

B_{n,s}=\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\mid\{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\}=s\}

form its basis. Combining this with Lemma
11.4
we obtain that there is a natural weak equivalence

(11.14)

\underset{s\in P(K)}{\hocolim}\ \mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathsf{SS}(K).

On the other hand, it is well known that for any category
C
the homotopy colimit of the trivial functor is the nerve
\hocolim_{C}*\simeq N(C).
Therefore, using Lemma
11.3
, we obtain a weak equivalence

(11.15)

\underset{s\in P(K)}{\hocolim}\ \mathsf{SS}(\Delta(s))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}N(P(K)).

Applying the geometric realisation, we obtain that there is a natural homotopy equivalence

(11.16)

|N(P(K))|\simeq|\mathsf{SS}(K)|.

Then the result follows from the homotopy equivalences

(11.17)

|N(P(K))|\simeq|{\sf sd}(K)|\simeq|K|,

where
{\sf sd}(K)
is the barycentric subdivision of
K.

∎

Lemma 12.2
.

For a surjective map of sets
f:X\to Y,
the morphism of simplicial sets
\hat{f}:\text{\rm\v{C}}(f)\to Y
is a weak equivalence to a constant simplicial set.

Proof.

First assume that
Y=\{y\}
is a one-point set. Then
\text{\rm\v{C}}(f)\cong\mathsf{SS}(\Delta(X))
is contractible by Lemma
11.3
. Now consider the general case of a surjective map
f:X\to Y.
Since
\text{\rm\v{C}}(f)_{n}\cong\coprod_{y\in Y}f^{-1}(y)^{n+1},
the map to the discrete simplicial set
\hat{f}:\text{\rm\v{C}}(f)\to Y
is a disjoint union of maps
\hat{f}_{y}:\text{\rm\v{C}}(\hat{f}_{y})\to\{y\}
for
y\in Y,
where
f_{y}:f^{-1}(y)\to\{y\}
is the restriction of
f.
Using that each of the maps
\hat{f}_{y}
is a homotopy equivalence, we obtain that
\hat{f}
is a homotopy equivalence.
∎

(Čech nerve of a cover)
.

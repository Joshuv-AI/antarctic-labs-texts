A. Aberqi, Z.. Ech-chaffani, T. Karite, Fez Morocco. echzoubida@gmail.com

Abstract
In this manuscript, we are concerned with the approximate controllability of fractional nonlinear differential equations with nonlocal conditions of order 1 < q < 2 \displaystyle 1<q<2 in Banach spaces. As far as we know, few articles have investigated this issue. The idea is to see under which sufficient conditions the proposed control problem is approximately controllable. The discussion is based on the theory of resolvent operator, fractional calculus techniques and Krasnoselskii’s fixed point theorem under the assumption that the associated linear system is approximately controllable. The obtained results improve some existing analogous ones on this topic. Finally, an example is provided to illustrate the applications of the obtained results

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|u_{\beta}(\theta,z)\|&\leq\displaystyle\frac{M_{B}Ma^{q-1}}{\beta\Gamma(q)}\Bigg[\|z_{d}\|+M[\|z_{0}\|+\|\phi(z)\|]+M[\|z_{1}\|+\|\psi(z)\|]+\displaystyle\frac{Ma^{q-1}}{\Gamma(q)}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}\Bigg(C_{1}\|z(\nu)\|\\

(3.3)

Then

\|u_{\beta}(\theta,z)\|\leq\displaystyle\frac{M_{B}}{\beta}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{4}+\mathcal{L}_{5}\|z\|_{C}\Big),

where

∎

Under the above conditions, we prove the following existence theorem.

Theorem 2

Assume that
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{1})-(\mathcal{H}_{4})
are satisfied, Further suppose that
\displaystyle\{P_{q}(\theta)\}(\theta\geq 0)\}

) has at least one mild solution on
\displaystyle[0,a]
provided that
\displaystyle M(d_{1}+d_{2})<1.

We base the argument on Theorem
1

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle B_{k}
is a bounded, closed, and convex subset in
\displaystyle C([0,a],Z)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{G}_{1}z)(\theta)=C_{q}(\theta)[z_{0}-\phi(z)]+S_{q}(\theta)[z_{1}-\psi(z)],\\

(3.5)

Clearly, the fixed point of the operator equation
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{2}
is a mild solution of (
1.1
). We will use Theorem
1
to prove that
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}
has a fixed point. Our proof will be divided into three steps.

Step I:

Hence, for sufficiently large
\displaystyle k>0

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{1}z+\mathcal{G}_{2}w\|\leq k
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}z+\mathcal{G}_{2}w\in B_{k}
for any
\displaystyle z,w\in B_{k}
.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{1})
is a contraction on
\displaystyle B_{k}
. For any
\displaystyle\theta\in J
and
\displaystyle z,w\in B_{k}

.
Since
\displaystyle M(d_{1}+d_{2})<1

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}
is a contraction.

Step III:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}
is a completely continuous operator.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}
on
\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle(z_{n})

\displaystyle B_{k}

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z_{n})(\theta)-(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta)\|&\leq\displaystyle\frac{Ma^{q-1}}{\Gamma(q)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\|f(\nu,z_{n},\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z_{n})d\tau)-f(\nu,z,\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z)d\tau)\|d\nu\\

&\displaystyle z_{n})d\tau)-f(\sigma,z,\int_{0}^{\sigma}g(\sigma,\tau,z)d\tau)\|d\sigma\Bigg].\end{array}

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z_{n})-(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)\|\to 0
as
\displaystyle n\to+\infty

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}
in
\displaystyle B_{k}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}
. To prove this, we first show that
\displaystyle\{(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta),~z\in B_{k}\}
is relatively compact in
\displaystyle Z
. Subsequently, we prove that
\displaystyle\{(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta),~z\in B_{k}\}
is uniformly bounded. For any

and
\displaystyle z\in B_{k}

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta)\|&\leq\displaystyle\frac{Ma^{q-1}}{\Gamma(q)}\Bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\|f(\nu,z(\nu),\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z(\tau))d\tau)-f(\nu,0,\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,0)d\tau)\|d\nu\\

Therefore, we have
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{k}}\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta)\|<\infty
,
which means that
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{2}z,~~z\in B_{k}\}

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{2}z,~~z\in B_{k}\}
is an equicontinuous family of functions on
\displaystyle[0,a]
. For any fixed
\displaystyle z\in B_{k}
and
\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leq a
, we get

\begin{array}[]{llll}\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta_{2})-(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta_{1})\|&\leq\Bigg\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta_{2}}P_{q}(\theta_{2}-\nu)f(\nu,z(\nu),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z(\tau))d\tau)d\nu-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}P_{q}(\theta_{1}-\nu)f(\nu,z(\nu),\\

1

3.2
),
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{2})-(ii)
and
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{3})-(ii)
we get

\begin{array}[]{llll}|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta_{2})-(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta_{1})\|&\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta_{1}}\|P_{q}(\theta_{2}-\nu)-P_{q}(\theta_{1}-\nu)\|\Big[C_{1}k+C_{2}C_{3}k+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(J,\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\displaystyle\frac{M_{B}}{\beta}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{4}+\mathcal{L}_{5}\|z\|_{C}\Big)\Big]d\nu\\

&+\frac{Ma^{q-1}}{\Gamma(q)}\Big[C_{1}k+C_{2}C_{3}k+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(J,\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\displaystyle\frac{M_{B}}{\beta}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{4}+\mathcal{L}_{5}k\Big)\Big]|\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}|.\end{array}

\displaystyle P_{q}(\theta)

\displaystyle P_{q}(\theta)
is uniformly continuous. Then
\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)(\theta_{2})-(\mathcal{G}_{2}z)\|

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}_{2}z,~z\in B_{k}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}

, we conclude that there exists a fixed point
\displaystyle z(\cdot)
for

\displaystyle[0,a]
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Approximate controllability

In this section, we present our main result on approximate controllability of the control system (
1.1
). For this purpose, we add the following hypothesis:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{5})

The linear control system (
2.8
) is approximately controllable.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{6})

The function
\displaystyle f
is uniformly bounded on her domain.

Remark 1
.

In view of
[
35
]

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{5})
is equivalent to
\displaystyle\beta\mathbf{R}(\beta,\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})=\beta(\beta I+\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})^{-1}\to 0\quad as\quad\beta\to 0^{+}
in the strong operator topology.

Now we are in a position to prove the approximate controllability of system (
1.1
). That is

Theorem 3
.

Assume that the hypothesis
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{1})-(\mathcal{H}_{6})
are satisfied and
\displaystyle C_{q}(\theta)
,
\displaystyle S_{q}(\theta)
are compact. Then the system (
1.1
) is approximately controllable on
\displaystyle[0,a]
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle z_{\beta}(\cdot)=z(\cdot,z_{0},z_{1},u_{\beta})
be a fixed point of
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{2}
in
\displaystyle B_{k}
. By Theorem
2
,
\displaystyle z_{\beta}(\cdot)
is a mild solution of (
1.1
) associated to the control

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\beta}(\theta,z)&=B^{\star}P_{q}^{\star}(a-\theta)(\beta I+\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})^{-1}\bigg(z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z))\bigg)\\
&-B^{\star}P_{q}^{\star}(a-\theta)\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}(\beta I+\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})^{-1}P_{q}(a-\nu)f(\nu,z(\nu),\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z(\tau))d\tau)d\nu.\end{array}

Then, when
\displaystyle\theta=a
,

\begin{array}[]{llll}z_{\beta}(a)&=C_{q}(a)[z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta})]+S_{q}(a)[z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta})]+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\nu)f(\nu,z_{\beta}\nu),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\nu\\
&+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\nu)\Big[BB^{\star}P_{q}^{\star}(\theta-\nu)R(\beta,\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})\Big(z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta}))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta}))\\
&-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\sigma)f(\sigma,z_{\beta}(\sigma),\int_{0}^{\sigma}g(\sigma,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\sigma\Big)\Big]d\nu\\
&=C_{q}(a)[z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta})]+S_{q}(a)[z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta})]+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\nu)f(\nu,z(\nu),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\nu\\
&+(-\beta I+\beta I+\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})R(\beta,\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})\Big(z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta}))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta}))-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\sigma)f(\sigma,z_{\beta}(\sigma),\\
&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sigma}g(\sigma,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\sigma\\
&=z_{d}-\beta R(\beta,\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})\Big(z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta}))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta}))-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\sigma)f(\sigma,z_{\beta}(\sigma),\int_{0}^{\sigma}\\
&g(\sigma,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\sigma\Big)\\
&=z_{d}-\beta R(\beta,\mathbf{K}_{0}^{a})p(z_{\beta}),\end{array}

(3.6)

where
\displaystyle p(z_{\beta})=z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta}))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta}))\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\sigma)f(\sigma,z_{\beta}(\sigma),\int_{0}^{\sigma}g(\sigma,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)d\sigma

By the assumption
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{6})
, we get that the sequence
\displaystyle\{f(\nu,z_{\beta}(\nu),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)\}
is uniformly bounded on
\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\{f(\nu,z_{\beta}(\nu),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\nu}g(\nu,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)\}

\displaystyle f^{\prime}
in
\displaystyle L^{2}(J\times Z\times Z,Z)

\displaystyle\omega=z_{d}-C_{q}(a)(z_{0}-\phi(z_{\beta}))-S_{q}(a)(z_{1}-\psi(z_{\beta}))\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}P_{q}(a-\sigma)f^{\prime}d\sigma

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|p(z_{\beta})-\omega\|&=\Big\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{q}(a-\sigma)\Big[f(\sigma,z_{\beta}(\sigma),\int_{0}^{\sigma}g(\sigma,\tau,z_{\beta}(\tau))d\tau)-f^{\prime}\Big]d\sigma\Big\|\\

\displaystyle\beta\to 0

\displaystyle\theta\in[0,a]

Example

Let
\displaystyle Z=L^{2}([0,\pi])
. Consider the following fractional control system

\begin{cases}{}^{c}D^{q}\vartheta(\theta,y)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\vartheta(\theta,y)+\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\theta}|\vartheta(\theta,y)|}{(3+e^{a})(1+|\vartheta(\theta,y)|)}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\displaystyle\frac{e^{\nu}}{\sqrt{2}+|\vartheta(\nu,y)|}d\nu+Bu(\theta,y),&\theta\in J,\\
\displaystyle\vartheta(\theta,0)=\vartheta(\theta,\pi)=0,\quad\quad\vartheta^{\prime}(\theta,0)=\vartheta^{\prime}(\theta,\pi)=0,\\
\vartheta(\theta,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{i=p}a_{i}\vartheta(\theta_{i},y)=\vartheta_{0}(y)\quad\quad\vartheta^{\prime}(\theta,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{i=p}\gamma_{i}\vartheta(\theta_{i},y)=\vartheta_{1}(y),\end{cases}

(3.8)

Where
{}^{c}D^{q}
is the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\displaystyle q\in(1,2)
,
\displaystyle\theta\in J=[0,1]
,
\displaystyle a_{i},\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle p\in\mathbb{N}
. We assume that there exists
\displaystyle L_{1},~L_{2}>0
such that
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}|a_{i}|<L_{1}
and
\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}|\gamma_{i}|<L_{2}
. The operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset Z\to Z
is defined by
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\vartheta)=-\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}(\vartheta)}{\partial y^{2}}
, with the domain
\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A})=\{\vartheta(\cdot)\in Z:\vartheta,\vartheta^{\prime}\,\text{are absolutely continuous},\vartheta^{\prime\prime}\in Z,\vartheta(0)=\vartheta(\pi)=0\}
.
It is well known that the operator
\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}
is densely defined in
\displaystyle Z
and
\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}
is the infinitesimal generator of a uniformly bounded strongly continuous cosine family
\displaystyle\{C(\theta),\,\theta\geq 0\}
, see
[
37
,
38
]
.

Furthermore,
\displaystyle-\mathcal{A}
has a discrete spectrum, the eigenvalues are
\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=-n^{2}\pi^{2},\,n\in\mathbb{N}
, with corresponding normalized eigenvectors
\displaystyle e_{n}(y)=\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}}sin(n\pi y),\,y\in[0,\pi],n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is clear that
\displaystyle\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
form an orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle X
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\vartheta=-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\langle\vartheta,e_{n}\rangle e_{n},\,\vartheta\in D(\mathcal{A}),

and the cosine function is given by

\displaystyle C(\theta)\vartheta=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}cos(\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\theta)\langle\vartheta,e_{n}\rangle e_{n},\,\vartheta\in Z,

and the associated sine function is given by

\displaystyle S(\theta)\vartheta=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}sin(\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}{\theta)}\langle\vartheta,e_{n}\rangle e_{n},\,\vartheta\in Z.

It is easy to check that for all
\displaystyle\theta\geq 0
,
\displaystyle S(\theta)
is compact,
\displaystyle C(\theta)
is strongly continuous and
\displaystyle\|C(\theta)\|\leq 1
.

The system (
3.8
) can be formulated as the following nonlocal problem in
\displaystyle Z
.

\begin{cases}{}^{c}D^{q}z(\theta)=-\mathcal{A}z(\theta)+f(\theta,z(\theta),\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}g(\theta,\nu,z(\nu))d\nu)+Bu(\theta),&\theta\in J=[0,1],\\
\displaystyle z(0)+\phi(z)=z_{0},\\
z^{\prime}(0)+\psi(z)=z_{1},\end{cases}

(3.9)

where
\displaystyle[z(\theta)](y)=\vartheta(\theta,y)
, the function
\displaystyle g:J\times J\times Z\to Z
is defined by

g(\theta,\nu,\varphi)=\displaystyle\frac{e^{\nu}}{\sqrt{2}+|\varphi(\nu,y)|},\quad y\in[0,\pi],

the function

Conclusion

In this work, fractional nonlinear evolution equations have been considered in Banach spaces. We obtain the approximate controllability for the system (
1.1
) under different conditions. More precisely, conditions are formulated and proved under which approximate controllability of the nonlinear control system is implied by the approximate controllability of its corresponding linear part. In particular, the controllability problem is transformed into a fixed point problem for an appropraite nonlinear operator in a function space. Krasnoselskii’s fixed point theorem and fractional calculations are used to demonstrate the existence of a fixed point of this operator and established the approximate controllability of the proposed system. This work opens to other questions, this is the case of the approximate controllability for impulsive neutral semilinear and nonlinear evolution equations with nonlocal conditions and with delay. This will be the purpose of future research.

Rivers under Additive Noise
Michael Scheutzow, Michael Grinfeld

Abstract
We consider the deterministic and stochastic versions of a first order non-autonomous differential equation which allows us to discuss the persistence of rivers (“fleuves”) under additive noise.

1
Introduction

This paper uses a simple example in the pursuit of understanding the
stochastic counterpart of the river (“fleuve”) phenomenon. We
believe that this example contains all the important ingredients of the
general theory.

Rivers, a remarkable organising feature of phase portraits of
polynomial non-autonomous first order ODEs were discovered in the 1980s
by nonstandard analysts (see
Blais (1991)
for the definitions and
additional references, and
Diener (1985)
for connections between
rivers and centre manifolds). Clearly, an interesting question is to
try to extend the notions introduced in the context of rivers to the
case of stochastic differential equations (SDEs).

The present paper is devoted to an analysis of a simple example of the
stochastic counterpart of a polynomial non-autonomous ordinary
differential equation which exhibits rivers in which a trichotomy of
asymptotic fates of solutions occurs. The structure of the paper is as
follows. In Section
2
we discuss the structure of solutions of
the deterministic version of our equation (
2.1
), and exhibit
the asymptotics of the critical solution; this material is standard
and is helped by the existence of an explicit formula for the general
solution. In Section 3, we briefly study the much simpler
(deterministic and random) unstable
linear
case in which there
is a repelling river of sub-exponential growth. This case is easy to
analyze since again there is an explicit formula for the general
solution and for the repelling river. In Section 4, we state our main
result: the non-autonomous quadratic stochastic differential equation
(
4.1
), the additive noise counterpart of (
2.1
) also
admits a trichotomy: there is a random repelling river with the
property that trajectories starting above it blow up in finite time
while trajectories starting below the repelling river converge to 0
and the river which separates the two regimes has linear growth as

t\rightarrow\infty
(as in the deterministic case). Note that,
contrary to the linear case, there is an attracting solution (any
solution which converges to 0 attracts all trajectories starting below
the repelling river). We formulate our results as four theorems which
we prove in the subsequent sections. Our main tools are well-known
estimates of exit probabilities of diffusions from intervals of the
real line which we quote in the appendix for the reader’s convenience.
Finally, in Section
8
, we discuss extending other tools
available in the deterministic setting (Ważewski principle,
asymptotic expansions) to the stochastic context.

2
The deterministic situation

The deterministic equation we start with is

x^{\prime}=x^{2}-tx,\quad t>0,\quad x(0)=x_{0}.

(2.1)

It is easy to analyse as the general solution of the initial value
problem can be found explicitly,

x(t)=\frac{2x_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)}{2-x_{0}\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hbox{erf}\,\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}.

(2.2)

Here
\hbox{erf}(\cdot)
is the error function,

\hbox{erf}\,(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}t,

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\hbox{erf}\,(z)=1
, from which we
immediately obtain the following trichotomy of fates of solutions in
positive time : If
x_{0}>\sqrt{2/\pi}
, solutions blow up in finite
time; if
x_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}
,
x(t)\rightarrow\infty
as

t\rightarrow\infty
, and finally if
x_{0}<\sqrt{2/\pi}
,

x(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. We call the solution
(
2.2
) with
x_{0}=\sqrt{2/\pi}
the
critical solution
and
denote it by
x_{c}(t)
.

As there is symmetry
x\rightarrow-x
,
t\rightarrow-t
, similar
statements can be made as
t\rightarrow-\infty
; below we restrict
ourselves to positive times.

Given the explicit form (
2.2
) of solutions of (
2.1
),
the asymptotics of the critical solution as
t\rightarrow\infty
is
easily computed; it is given by

x_{c}(t)\sim t+\frac{1}{t}-\frac{2}{t^{3}}+O\left(\frac{1}{t^{5}}\right).

(2.3)

Note that in terms of rivers,
x_{c}(t)
provides a repelling river,
while
x(t)\equiv 0
and all the positive semi-orbits that converge
to it, constitute attracting rivers.

The asymptotic expansion of the repelling river in (
2.3
) can
be constructed directly from the equation (
2.1
) without using
(
2.2
) by following the procedure explained in
Blais (1991)
.

3
The linear case

Before investigating the behaviour of equation (
2.1
) with
additive white noise we study the much easier linear stochastic
differential equation

\text{\rm{d}}X(t)=\big(cX(t)+f(t)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\;t\geq 0,

(3.1)

where
W(t),\,t\geq 0
is a standard (one-dimensional) Wiener process (also known as Brownian motion),
c>0
,
\sigma\geq 0
and
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

The SDE (
3.1
) with initial condition
X(0)=x
has the explicit solution

X(t,x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}\Big(x+\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}f(s)\,\text{\rm{d}}s+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(s)\Big).

It follows that all trajectories are exponentially unstable with

\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}~X(t,x)~=~\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}
,
t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}
.
We now assume that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}|f(s)|\,\text{\rm{d}}s<\infty.

Below we will be using improper Itô integrals. For a rigorous
definition of this mathematical object see, e.g.,
(
Klenke, 2020
, Definition
25.10)
. Denote the unique solution
X(t),\,t\geq 0
of
(
3.1
) with the initial condition

X(0)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}f(s)\,\text{\rm{d}}s-\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(s)

by
\mathscr{R}(t),\,t\geq 0
(here and below
\mathscr{R}
stands for
river
).
Then

\displaystyle X(t,X(0))

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{R}(t)=-\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}\int_{t}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}f(s)\,\text{\rm{d}}s-\sigma e^{ct}\int_{t}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(s)

\displaystyle=-\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}\int_{t}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}f(s)\,\text{\rm{d}}s-\sigma A(t),

where

A(t)=\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}\int_{t}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-cs}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(s),\;t\geq 0

is a stationary Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. It follows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}X(t,x)\text{\rm{e}}^{-ct}=x-\mathscr{R}(0),

so solutions go to
+\infty
(respectively,
-\infty
) exponentially
fast with rate
c
when
x
is above (respectively below)
\mathscr{R}(0)
while
if
x=\mathscr{R}(0)
,

\big|X(t,\mathscr{R}(0))\big|=\big|\mathscr{R}(t)\big|=o\big(\text{\rm{e}}^{ct}\big).

In particular, if in (
3.1
) we set
f\equiv 0
, then just like in
the deterministic case (
\sigma\equiv 0
) where
the repelling river given
X(t,x)\equiv 0
, we have a trichotomy
with random repelling river given by
\mathscr{R}(t)
.

4
Main results

We now study the additive noise stochastic equivalent of (
2.1
),
namely, the stochastic differential equation

\text{\rm{d}}X(t)=X(t)\big(X(t)-t\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\,X(s)=x,\,t\geq s,

(4.1)

where
W
is a one-dimensional Wiener process defined on a probability
space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
,
s\geq 0
,
\sigma>0
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We
will study the long-time behaviour of the unique local solution of
(
4.1
) which we denote by
X_{s,t}(x)
, and we will see that
(
4.1
) possesses both a random repelling river
\mathscr{R}
(which
satisfies
\mathscr{R}(t)-t\to 0
as in the deterministic case (
2.1
))
and an attracting river of trajectories which converge to 0 as

t\to\infty
(again as in (
2.1
)). Just like in the linear
case,
\mathscr{R}(0)
is random. All trajectories starting above
\mathscr{R}(s)
at
time
s\geq 0
blow up to
\infty
in finite time while all
trajectories starting below
\mathscr{R}(s)
converge to 0 as
t\to\infty
. It will turn out that
\mathscr{R}(0)
will be
-\infty
with
positive probability. In this case,
\mathscr{R}(t)
will start becoming
larger than
-\infty
at some positive random time and then stay
finite for all future times and just like in the nonlinear
deterministic and the linear random cases it will solve the underlying
equation. All these properties (and more) will follow from four
theorems stated below. Further, Proposition
4.10
shows that,
unlike the repelling river in the deterministic case in (
2.1
),

\mathscr{R}(t)
oscillates around
t
as
t\to\infty
almost surely.

Before stating our first main result, we collect some
properties of the following more general model:

X(t)=x+\int_{s}^{t}X(u)\big(X(u)-u\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u+H(t)-H(s),\,t\geq s,

(4.2)

where
H:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a given (random or deterministic) continuous function satisfying
H(0)=0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and

s\geq 0
. Again we denote the unique local solution of (
4.2
)
by
X_{s,t}(x)
.
We define the blow-up time by

\beta_{s}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf\{t>s:|X_{s,t}(x)|\geq n\}\in(s,\infty],\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that the local solution
X_{s,t}(x)
depends continuously on
(t,x)
.
Arguing from the contrary from (
4.2
), we have the
fundamental monotonicity property:

Lemma 4.1
.

x<y
implies
X_{s,t}(x)\leq X_{s,t}(y)
for
t\geq s
;
moreover,
X_{s,t}(x)<X_{s,t}(y)
when
\beta_{s}(x)>t
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\geq s\geq 0
, we
have

\inf_{s\leq t\leq v}X_{s,t}(x)\geq x\wedge 0+\inf_{s\leq t\leq u\leq v}\big(H(u)-H(t)\big).

Proof.

Assume that
x\leq 0
and that
u\in[s,v]
is such that
X_{s,u}(x)\leq x
. Let
t=t(u)\in[s,u]
be such that
X_{s,t}(x)=x
and
X_{s,r}(x)\leq x
for all
r\in[t,u]

(such a
t
exists by continuity of
r\mapsto X_{s,r}(x)
and the fact that
X_{s,s}(x)=x
). Then

\displaystyle X_{s,u}(x)

\displaystyle=X_{s,t}(x)+\int_{t}^{u}X_{s,r}(x)\big(X_{s,r}(x)-r\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}r+H(u)-H(t)\geq x+H(u)-H(t)

\displaystyle\geq x+\inf_{s\leq\overline{t}\leq\overline{u}\leq v}\big(H(\overline{u})-H(\overline{t})\big),

since the integrand is at least 0 since
X_{s,r}(x)\leq x\leq 0
for
r\in[t,u]
. This holds true for all
u
as above and therefore the assertion holds for
x\leq 0
.

The case of
x>0
follows by monotonicity from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Proposition
4.2
implies that the function

t\mapsto X_{s,t}(x)
cannot reach
-\infty
in finite time. Further,
if
X_{s,t}(x)\geq A
for some
t\in[s,v]
and

A\geq v+\inf_{s\leq\kappa\leq u\leq v}\big(H(u)-H(\kappa)\big)
, then

\inf_{t\leq u\leq v}X_{s,u}(x)\geq A+\inf_{s\leq\kappa\leq u\leq v}\big(H(u)-H(\kappa)\big)

by the same argument as was used in Proposition
4.2
, since

y(y-r)\geq 0
for
y\geq v
and
r\in[s,v]
. This result implies that

\lim_{t\uparrow\beta_{s}(x)}X_{s,t}(x)=\infty
whenever

\beta_{s}(x)<\infty
. The function
x\mapsto\beta_{s}(x)
is
non-decreasing for each
s\geq 0
.

We will write
X_{s,t}(x)=\infty
whenever
t\geq\beta_{s}(x)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
s\geq 0
, we have
\beta_{s}(x)<\infty
for sufficiently
large
x
.

Proof.

To see this, let
H_{*}
be a lower bound of
H(t)-H(s)
for

t\in[s,s+1]
and let
x
be so large that the solution of

Y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}Y^{2}
with initial condition
x+H_{*}-1
at time
s
blows
up before time
s+1
. In addition, assume that
x\geq 3-H_{*}
. Let

X(t):=X_{s,t}(x)
,
t\geq s
. Then
X(s)>Y(s)
since

H_{*}\leq 0
. Let
\tau
be the infimum of all
t\geq s
such that

X(t)=Y(t)
and assume that
\tau\leq s+1
. For
u\leq\tau
we
have
X(u)\geq Y(u)\geq x+H_{*}-1
since
Y
is increasing. Then, for

u\in[s,\tau]
,

\displaystyle X(u)

\displaystyle\big(X(u)-1\big)=\frac{1}{2}X^{2}(u)+\frac{1}{2}X(u)\big(X(u)-2\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}Y^{2}(u)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}X(u)\big(X(u)-2\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}Y^{2}(u),

since
X(u)-2\geq Y(u)-2\geq 0
. Hence

\displaystyle X(\tau)

\displaystyle=x+\int_{s}^{\tau}X(u)\big(X(u)-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u+H(\tau)-H(s)

\displaystyle>x+H_{*}-1+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{\tau}Y^{2}(u)\text{\rm{d}}u=Y(\tau),

which is a contradiction.
∎

From Proposition
4.3
it follows by monotonicity that the set

I_{1}(s):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\beta_{s}(x)<\infty\}

(4.3)

is an interval which is unbounded above and non-empty.

Now we define

x_{\inf}(s):=\inf\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\,\beta_{s}(x)<\infty\}\in[-\infty,\infty).

Proposition 4.4
.

x_{\inf}(t)=X_{s,t}(x_{\inf}(s))

whenever
s>t
and
x_{\inf}(s)>-\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that for some
t>s
we have that

X_{s,t}(x_{\inf}(s))<x_{\inf}(t)
. Consider

x\in(X_{s,t}(x_{\inf}(s)),x_{\inf}(t))
. Then by
monotonicity there exists
y
such that
x=X_{s,t}(y)
and

y>x_{\inf}(s)
. Hence
X_{s,t}(y)
blows up in finite time but
since
x<x_{\inf}(t)
, this is impossible. The argument for the
case
X_{s,t}(x_{\inf}(s))>x_{\inf}(t)
is similar.
∎

It is obvious from Proposition
4.4
that

X_{s,t}(x_{\inf}(s))
is defined for all
t>s
.

In particular, the set
I_{1}(s)
of (
4.3
) is open; depending on

H
it may be all of
\mathbb{R}
or a proper (non-empty) subset of
\mathbb{R}
.

Now we return to our initial model (
4.1
). A Wiener process
W

has continuous paths and satisfies
W(0)=0
, so that our general
statements above can be applied for (almost) every

\omega\in\Omega
.

Theorem 4.5
.

For every
s\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
the probability that either

\beta_{s}(x)<\infty
or
\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{s,t}(x)=0
equals one,
i.e., almost surely, either the solution blows up in finite time or
it converges to 0.

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem
4.5
implies that for each
s\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

the set
\Omega_{s,x}\subset\Omega
consisting of those

\omega\in\Omega
for which the solution starting at
(s,x)

neither blows up nor converges to zero has measure 0. For
s\geq 0

denote the union of all
\Omega_{s,x},\,x\in\mathbb{R}
by
\Omega_{s}
.
Since this is an uncountable union it does not follow that

\Omega_{s}
has measure 0. Indeed we will see in the following that

\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{s})>0
for every
s\geq 0
. In order to obtain a better
understanding of the set
\Omega_{s}
we will use the notation

I_{1}(s,\omega):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\beta_{s}(x)<\infty\}

corresponding to a realisation
\omega
, consistent with
(
4.3
). We saw that
I_{1}(s,\omega)
is a non-empty open interval
which is unbounded from above. Similarly, we set

I_{2}(s,\omega):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,X_{s,t}(x)\rightarrow 0\hbox{ as }t\rightarrow\infty\}.

(4.4)

Again, by monotonicity of solutions,
I_{2}(s,\omega)
is a (possibly
empty) interval lying below
I_{1}(s,\omega)
. Using Theorem

4.5
and a Fubini-type argument we will show that

I_{2}(s,\omega)
is unbounded below whenever
I_{2}(s,\omega)
is
nonempty, that
I_{1}(s,\omega)
is unbounded below whenever

I_{2}(s,\omega)
is empty and that the complement of the (disjoint)
union of these intervals can contain at most one point, almost
surely.

Let

\zeta(s,\omega):=\inf I_{2}(s,\omega)\mbox{ if }I_{2}(s,\omega)\neq\emptyset

and
\zeta(s,\omega):=-\infty
otherwise. If

P\big(\zeta(s,\omega)>-\infty\big)>0
, then, by monotone convergence,
there exists some
x\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathbb{P}\big(\zeta(s,\omega)>x\big)>0
. On the set

\{\omega:\zeta(s,\omega)>x\}
the number
x
lies below the set

I_{2}(s,\omega)
and, a fortiori, also below
I_{1}(s,\omega)
, so
x
is
not in the union of the two intervals which contradicts Theorem

4.5
, so
\zeta(s,\omega)=-\infty
almost surely, so

I_{2}(s,\omega)
is unbounded below whenever
I_{2}(s,\omega)
is
nonempty.

Almost exactly the same argument shows that
\inf I_{1}(s,\omega)=-\infty
on the set where
I_{2}(s,\omega)
is empty. The fact that, almost surely, the complement of the union of
I_{1}(s,\omega)
and
I_{2}(s,\omega)

contains at most one point is clear when
I_{2}(s,\omega)
is empty (and hence
I_{1}(s,\omega)=\mathbb{R}
). Otherwise, assuming that
\sup I_{2}(s,\omega)<\inf I_{1}(s,\omega)
with strictly positive probability, by Fubini’s theorem, there must
exist some (deterministic)
x\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\mathbb{P}\big(x\in\big(\sup I_{2}(s,\omega),\inf I_{1}(s,\omega)\big)>0
so, in particular,
x
is not contained in the union of
I_{1}(s,\omega)
and
I_{2}(s,\omega)
which again contradicts Theorem
4.5
.

We set

\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)=x_{\inf}(s)

for a realisation
\omega\in\Omega
, i.e. it is the
infimum of
I_{1}(s,\omega)
(which necessarily coincides with the
supremum of
I_{2}(s,\omega)
). Note that
\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)=-\infty
iff

I_{2}(s,\omega)
is empty iff
I_{1}(s,\omega)=\mathbb{R}
. We know that

\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)
does not belong to
I_{1}(s,\omega)
but at the
moment, it is unclear if
\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)
belongs to

I_{2}(s,\omega)
. We will see later that it almost surely
doesn’t. Therefore (almost surely),
\omega\in\Omega_{s}
iff

\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)>-\infty
and
\omega\in\Omega_{s,x}
iff

\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)=x
. In particular,

\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{s})=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{R}(s,\omega)>-\infty)
and we will see in Theorem

4.9
that this quantity converges to 1 as
s\to\infty
.

We will denote the blow-up probability of the solution starting at
(s,x)
by
B(s,x)
, i.e.

B(s,x):=\mathbb{P}\big(\beta_{s}(x)<\infty\big).

Clearly, the function
x\mapsto B(s,x)
is non-decreasing and converges to 1 as
x\to\infty
for every
s\geq 0
(by the general properties stated above).
We have the following result concerning the asymptotics of
B(s,x)
as
s\to\infty
.

Theorem 4.7
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\lim_{s\to\infty}B\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)=\Phi(z):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{z}\exp\Big\{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y.

Note that the convergence is even uniform in
z
since both sides are non-decreasing, take values in
[0,1]
, and
\Phi
is continuous. In what follows, we will call the subset

. Theorem

(together with Theorem

solutions starting slightly above the diagonal will blow-up with high probability while solutions starting slightly below the diagonal will converge to 0 with high probability.

The theorem above tells us, in particular, that
B(s,0)
converges
to 0 as
s\to\infty
. This does not mean that
B(s,x)
converges to
0 as
x\to-\infty
for fixed

. In fact it doesn’t. Let

B(s,-\infty)
be the limit of
B(s,x)
as
x\to-\infty
which exists since
x\mapsto B(s,x)
is non-decreasing.

Theorem 4.8
.

B(s,-\infty)>0

for every
s\geq 0
.

Finally, we investigate the random borderline
\mathscr{R}
between blow-up and convergence to 0.

Recall that
\mathscr{R}(s)\in[-\infty,\infty)
for all
s\geq 0
and
that
\mathscr{R}(s)=-\infty
implies
\mathscr{R}(r)=-\infty
for every

r<s
. Further,
X_{s,t}(\mathscr{R}(s))=\mathscr{R}(t)
whenever

and
\mathscr{R}(s)>-\infty
, so
\mathscr{R}(t)
is a solution of (
4.1
)
on the interval where it is larger than
-\infty
. Theorem

4.7
implies that, for each
\varepsilon>0
and

\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

\lim_{s\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(s^{\alpha}|\mathscr{R}(s)-s|\geq\varepsilon\big)=0
but the following result is stronger.

Theorem 4.9
.

For each
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{\alpha}|\mathscr{R}(s)-s|=0\big)=1.

We will prove the four theorems in the next three sections.

In the deterministic case, it follows from the fact that the initial
value of the repelling river at time 0 is positive, together with
the fact that the derivative
x^{\prime}
in (
2.1
) is zero on the
diagonal
x=t
that the function
\mathscr{R}
lies above the diagonal,
i.e.
\mathscr{R}(s)>s
for all
s\geq 0
. This is not true in the

Proposition 4.10
.

For each
c\geq 0
there exist increasing sequences of random times
\rho_{n}
and
\kappa_{n}
such that, almost surely,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_{n}=\infty
and

\mathscr{R}(\rho_{n})>\rho_{n}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{\rho_{n}}}
and

\mathscr{R}(\kappa_{n})<\kappa_{n}-\frac{c}{\sqrt{\kappa_{n}}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The events

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\{\omega:\exists s_{0}\geq 0,\,\forall s\geq s_{0}:\,\mathscr{R}(s)\geq s-\frac{c}{\sqrt{s}}\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\{\omega:\exists s_{0}\geq 0,\,\forall s\geq s_{0}:\,\mathscr{R}(s)\leq s+\frac{c}{\sqrt{s}}\},

are tail events of the driving Brownian motion
W
, i.e.
A
and
B
are contained in the tail-
\sigma
algebra
\mathscr{T}:=\bigcap_{s\geq 0}\sigma\big(W(t)-W(s),\,t\geq s\big)
.
Kolmogorov’s 0-1 law (or Blumenthal’s 0-1 law
(
Karatzas and Shreve, 1991
, Theorem 2.7.17)
and time inversion of Brownian motion
(
Karatzas and Shreve, 1991
, Lemma 2.9.4(ii))
) state that
\mathscr{T}
is trivial, so
A
and
B
have either probability 0 or 1. Further,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s_{0}\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathscr{R}(s)\geq s-\frac{c}{\sqrt{s}}\,\forall s\geq s_{0}\Big)\leq\liminf_{s_{0}\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathscr{R}(s_{0})\geq s_{0}-\frac{c}{\sqrt{s_{0}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{s_{0}\to\infty}\Big(1-B\Big(s_{0},s_{0}-\frac{c}{\sqrt{s_{0}}}\Big)\Big)<1,

where the final inequality follows from Theorem
4.7
. Analogously,
P(B)<1
, so
both events have probability 0 and the statement of the proposition follows.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Proposition
4.10
complements the statement of Theorem
4.9
: we have, almost surely,

\limsup_{s\to\infty}s^{1/2}\big(\mathscr{R}(s)-s\big)=\infty\mbox{ and }\liminf_{s\to\infty}s^{1/2}\big(\mathscr{R}(s)-s\big)=-\infty.

Finally, we provide an explicit asymptotic representation of the unstable river
\mathscr{R}
by linearizing around the diagonal.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
Z_{1}
be the unique solution of the SDE

\text{\rm{d}}z=\big(tz-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\;t\geq 0,

with random initial condition
Z_{1}(0)
chosen such that the second moment of
Z_{1}(t)
converges to 0 as
t\to\infty
,
i.e.

Z_{1}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u-\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(u),

so

Z_{1}(t)=\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u-\sigma\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(u)\Big).

Define
Z_{0}(t)\equiv 0
and, recursively,

Z_{n+1}(t):=Z_{n}(t)-\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\big(Z_{n}^{2}(u)-Z_{n-1}^{2}(u)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\Big),\;n\geq 1,

and
\mathscr{R}_{n}(t):=t+Z_{n}(t)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
t\geq 0
. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\big|\mathscr{R}_{n}(t)-\mathscr{R}(t)\big|=o(t^{-\frac{3}{2}n-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}),\;t\to\infty,

(4.5)

and there exists some
T=T(\omega)\in(0,\infty)
such that
\mathscr{R}_{n}
converges to
\mathscr{R}
uniformly on
[T,\infty)
.

Proof.

Put
Z(t)=\mathscr{R}(t)-t
. Then, using Theorem
4.9
,
Z
is the unique solution of the equation

\text{\rm{d}}z=\big(tz+z^{2}-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\;t\geq 0

such that
Z(t)
converges to 0 as
t\to\infty
(
Z
may be
-\infty
up to some random time
\zeta
). Then, for
t>\zeta
,

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(-\sigma\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(u)-\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\big(Z^{2}(u)-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\Big)

\displaystyle=Z_{1}(t)-\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}Z^{2}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u,

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{n}(t)-\mathscr{R}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{R}_{1}(t)-\mathscr{R}(t)+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\big(\mathscr{R}_{k}(t)-\mathscr{R}_{k-1}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=Z_{1}(t)-Z(t)-\sum_{k=2}^{n}\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\big(Z_{k-1}^{2}(u)-Z_{k-2}^{2}(u)\big)\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(Z^{2}(u)-Z_{n-1}^{2}(u)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\int_{t}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(Z(u)-Z_{n-1}(u)\Big)\Big(Z(u)+Z_{n-1}(u)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}u.

Define
\Gamma_{n}(t):=\sup_{u\geq t}\big|\mathscr{R}_{n}(u)-\mathscr{R}(u)\big|
. Choose
\varepsilon<1/2
. By Theorem
4.9
, there
exists some
t_{0}(\varepsilon,\omega)<\infty
such that

|Z(u)|\leq u^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
for all

u\geq t_{0}(\varepsilon,\omega)
. Hence, for

t\geq t_{0}(\varepsilon,\omega)
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Gamma_{n-1}(t)\frac{1}{t}\int_{t}^{\infty}u\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}u\,\Big(\Gamma_{n-1}(t)+2t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{n-1}(t)\frac{1}{t}\Big(\Gamma_{n-1}(t)+2t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\Big).

Let
t\geq t_{1}(\varepsilon,\omega):=3\vee t_{0}(\varepsilon,\omega)
.
Since
\varepsilon<1/2
, by induction,

\Gamma_{n}(t)\leq t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
(this is true for

n=1

\Gamma_{n}(t)\leq 3t^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}\Gamma_{n-1}(t)\leq\Gamma_{n-1}(t),\,t\geq t_{1}(\varepsilon,\omega).

Hence, for

5
Proofs of Theorem
4.5
and Theorem
4.7

Let us explain the strategy of the proofs. Let

C(s,x):=\mathbb{P}\Big(\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{s,t}(x)=0\Big),\,x\in\mathbb{R},\,s\geq 0,

so
C(s,x)
is the probability that
X_{s,t}(x)
converges to 0 as

t\to\infty
. Clearly,
C(s,x)+B(s,x)\leq 1
since a trajectory
which blows up cannot converge to 0 at the same time. Therefore, the
statement in Theorem
4.5
is equivalent to the statement that

C(s,x)+B(s,x)\geq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\geq 0
. We will say
that a statement concerning
s
is
very likely
if its
probability converges to 1 as
s\to\infty
. We will show the
following:

•

The solution with initial condition
(s,x)
with
x\geq s+1

will either blow-up or hit the diagonal for some
t\geq s
. It is
very likely that blow up happens before hitting the diagonal. In
particular,
\lim_{s\to\infty}B(s,s+1)=1
(Lemma
5.1
). It
seems plausible that the map
s\mapsto B(s,s+1)
is non-decreasing
but we will not prove this and instead only use that

\overline{B}(s):=\inf_{u\geq s}B(u,u+1)
converges to 1 as

s\to\infty
.

•

The solution with initial condition
(s,x)
with
|x-s|<1
will
hit one of the two lines parallel to the diagonal at distance 1 for
some
t\geq s
. We will provide rather precise asymptotic results
for the probability
p^{+}(s,x)
of hitting the upper line before the
lower line in Lemma
5.4
. Together with Lemma
5.1
, we
thus obtain a lower bound for the blow-up probability for these
initial conditions namely
B(s,x)\geq p^{+}(s,x)\overline{B}(s)
. These
bounds will later turn out also to be asymptotic upper bounds but so
far, we can not exclude the possibility that solutions starting at

|x-s|\leq 1
and hitting the lower set
\{(t,t-1):\,t\geq s\}

will still return to a neighborhood of the diagonal many times and
eventually blow up with large probability (or even almost surely).

•

The solution with initial condition
(s,x)
with
x\in(0,s-1]

reaches the set
\{0,s\}
in finite time. Let
\rho(s,x)
be the
probability that it hits level 0 before level
s
. Lemma

5.7
says that

\lim_{s\to\infty}\inf_{x\leq s-1}\rho(s,x)=1
which implies that

\overline{\rho}(s):=\inf_{t\geq s}\inf_{x\in(0,s-1]}\rho(t,x)

converges to 1 as well.

•

The solution with initial condition
(s,x)
,
x<0
will hit
0

after finite time (irrespective of the starting time
s\geq 0
). This is Lemma
5.6
.

•

Let
\chi(s)
be the probability that the solution
X_{s,t}(0)
,
t\geq s
will remain in
[-1,1]
for all
t\geq s
and that

\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{s,t}(0)=0
. Lemma
5.8
states that
\lim_{s\to\infty}\chi(s)=1
. Hence
\overline{\chi}(s):=\inf_{u\geq s}\chi(u)
converges to 1 as well.

These statements together imply the claims in Theorems
4.5
and
4.7
as follows:

Let

\widetilde{p}(s,x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{+}(s,x),&|x-s|\leq 1\\
1,&x>s+1\\
0,&x<s-1.\end{array}\right.

Then
B(s,x)\geq\widetilde{p}(s,x)\overline{B}(s)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\geq 0
.

Further,

C(s,x)\geq\big(1-\widetilde{p}(s,x)\big)\overline{\rho}(s)\overline{\chi}(s).

Hence,

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Big(B(s,x)+C(s,x)\Big)\geq\overline{B}(s)\wedge\big(\overline{\rho}(s)\overline{\chi}(s)\big)

(5.1)

which converges to 1 as
s\to\infty
.

Let
\kappa(s,x)
be the probability that the solution starting at
(s,x)
neither blows up nor converges to 0. Equation (
5.1
) shows that
\lim_{s\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\kappa(s,x)=0
.
Therefore, for
t\geq s
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\kappa(s,x)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa(t,y)\,\mathbb{P}\big(X_{s,t}(x)\in\text{\rm{d}}y\big)

(5.2)

(the “
\leq
” is due to the fact that there may be solutions which reach
y
at time
t
and which are
-\infty
at time
s
) converges to 0 as
t\to\infty
, so
\kappa(s,x)=0
for all
s\geq 0
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}
showing that
B(s,x)+C(s,x)\geq 1
as desired and Theorem
4.5
follows.

Theorem
4.7
is now an easy consequence of these considerations and Lemma
5.4
. Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}
. We saw above that

B\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)\geq\widetilde{p}\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)\cdot\overline{B}(s)

and

B\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)=1-C\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)\leq 1-\Big(1-\widetilde{p}\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)\Big)\overline{\rho}(s)\overline{\chi}(s).

Inserting the limit
\Phi(z)
of
\widetilde{p}\Big(s,s+z\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\Big)
established in Lemma
5.4
the statement of Theorem
4.7
follows.

We will now formulate and prove the lemmas mentioned above. Since the proof of Theorem
4.9
will require finer estimates of
B(s,x)
than those of Theorems
4.5
and
4.7
we will
formulate the lemmas accordingly. The first
lemma provides a lower bound for the blow-up probability when a trajectory starts slightly above the diagonal. The stated result is in fact more precise than needed to prove the two theorems. It will be convenient to work with the process

Y_{s,t}(x):=X_{s,t}(x+s)-t,\;0\leq s\leq t.

Note that
Y_{s,.}(x)
solves the equation

\text{\rm{d}}Y(t)=\big(Y(t)(Y(t)+t)-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\,t\geq s,\,Y(s)=x.

(5.3)

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
, we have

B\big(s,s+s^{-\alpha}\big)\geq 1-\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}s^{1-2\alpha}\big(1+o(1)\big)\Big\},\,s\to\infty.

In particular,
\lim_{s\to\infty}\inf_{x\geq s+s^{-\alpha}}B(s,x)=1
.

Proof.

We want to bound the probability
P(\gamma,s):=B(s,s+\gamma)
of blow-up of
X
starting from
X_{s,s}(s+\gamma)=s+\gamma
or, equivalently, of
Y
starting from
Y_{s,s}(\gamma)=\gamma
for a given
\gamma>0
and
s>0
. We compare
Y_{s,t}(\gamma)
with the solution
\overline{Y}
of the equation

\text{\rm{d}}\,\overline{Y}(t)=\big(\overline{Y}(t)(\overline{Y}(t)+s)-1\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\,t\geq s,\overline{Y}(s)=\gamma.

(5.4)

\overline{Y}
solves an equation with coefficients independent of
t
and is therefore easier to analyze than
Y
.
Note that
\overline{Y}(t)\leq Y(t)
as long as
Y
is non-negative. Further,
\overline{Y}
blows up to
\infty
with probability 1 by Feller’s test of explosion, see Lemma
9.1
c), applied to
(l,r)=\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,

Q(\gamma,s):=\mathbb{P}\big(\inf_{t\geq s}\overline{Y}(t)>0\big)\leq P(\gamma,s).

Let
b(u):=u(u+s)-1
, and denote by
p
the
scale function
of
\overline{Y}
, i.e.

p(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{y}b(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y=\int_{0}^{x}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{y^{3}}{3}+s\frac{y^{2}}{2}-y\Big)\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, by Lemma
9.1
a) with
r=\infty
and
l=0
,

Q(\gamma,s)=\frac{p(\gamma)}{p(\infty)}.

Note that
p(\infty)<\infty
. We have

p(\infty)=\int_{0}^{\gamma}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{y^{3}}{3}+s\frac{y^{2}}{2}-y\Big)\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y+\int_{\gamma}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{y^{3}}{3}+s\frac{y^{2}}{2}-y\Big)\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y=:A+B,

so
Q(\gamma,s)=\frac{A}{A+B}
. To obtain a lower bound, we estimate
B
from above and
A
from below.
Now we assume that
s\geq 1
. Then

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(s\frac{y^{2}}{2}-y\Big)\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\exp\Big\{\frac{1}{s\,\sigma^{2}}\Big\}\int^{\infty}_{\frac{\sqrt{2s}\gamma}{\sigma}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{s}}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}z,

and

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\geq\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\gamma^{3}}{3}\Big\}\int_{0}^{\gamma}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(s\frac{y^{2}}{2}-y\Big)\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{-\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\gamma^{3}}{3}\Big\}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2s}}\exp\Big\{\frac{1}{s\,\sigma^{2}}\Big\}\int_{-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{s}}}^{\frac{\sqrt{2s}\gamma}{\sigma}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{s}}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}z.

Inserting
\gamma=s^{-\alpha}\big(\leq 1\big)
, setting
u:=\frac{\sqrt{2s}\gamma}{\sigma}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{s}}
, and using the estimate

\int_{R}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}z\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}R^{2}\Big\}

for any
R\geq 0
, we get

\displaystyle 1-Q\big(s^{-\alpha},s\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{B}{A}\leq\exp\Big\{\frac{2}{3}\sigma^{-2}\Big\}\int_{u}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}z\Bigg(\int_{0}^{u}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}z\Bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{\frac{2}{3}\sigma^{-2}\Big\}\int_{u}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}z\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}-\int_{u}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}z\Bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{\frac{2}{3}\sigma^{-2}\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big(1-\exp\Big\{-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Big).

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{\frac{2}{3}\sigma^{-2}\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\sigma^{-2}\big(s^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}-s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\Big\}\big(1+o(1)\big),\;s\to\infty.

Therefore, using the writing
\kappa_{1}(s)\lesssim\kappa_{2}(s),\;s\to\infty
whenever
\limsup_{s\to\infty}\Big(\kappa_{1}(s)/\kappa_{2}(s)\Big)\leq 1
,

\log\big(1-P\big(s^{-\alpha},s\big)\big)\leq\log\big(1-Q\big(s^{-\alpha},s\big)\big)\lesssim-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}s^{1-2\alpha},\;s\to\infty,

so the statement of the lemma follows as
\alpha<1/2
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

It should not come as a surprise that the upper bound for the expected exit time computed in the previous proof blows up as
\sigma\to 0
. After all, in the deterministic case, for
s
sufficiently large, there exists an initial condition
(s,x)
with
x\in[s-1,s+1]
for which the solution stays in the interval
(s-1,s+1)
forever.

The two previous lemmas together provide an asymptotic lower bound for the blow-up probability for initial conditions of distance at most 1 from the diagonal:
Lemma
5.4
provides an asymptotic bound for the probability of reaching

before reaching
s-1
and Lemma
5.1
states that once we hit
s+1
the probability of blow-up converges to 1 as
s\to\infty
.

Our next aim is to show that all solutions starting below 0 will almost surely hit 0 later (Lemma
5.6
) and that with probability converging to 1 as
s\to\infty
, trajectories starting from
(s,s-s^{-\alpha})
will hit 0 before hitting level
s
whenever

(Lemma

).

Lemma 5.6
.

For every

and

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

. If

and
X_{s,t}(x)<0
, then, for

and

, we have

\displaystyle 0\geq X_{s,u}(x)

\displaystyle=X_{s,t}(x)+\int_{s}^{u}X_{s,v}(x)\big(X_{s,v}(x)-v\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}v+\sigma\big(W(u)-W(s)\big)

Lemma 5.7

Lemma 5.8
.

For
s\geq 0
, let

\chi(s):=\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{s,t}(0)=0\big\}\cap\big\{\sup_{t\geq s}\big|X_{s,t}(0)\big|<1\big\}\big).

Fix

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X(t)

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}X_{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\big(A-tX_{A}(t)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\quad X_{A}(s)=0.

\overline{\tau}:=\inf\big\{t\geq s:\,X(t)\geq 1\big\}

(here,

and

\lim_{s\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\overline{\tau}_{1}=\infty\big)=1
.

ii)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}X_{1}(t)\leq 0
almost surely for each
s\geq 2
.

iii)

\lim_{s\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\underline{\tau}_{0}=\infty\big)=1

\liminf_{t\to\infty}X_{0}(t)\geq 0

s\geq 2
.

The process
X_{A}
solves an affine SDE. Its solution is given by

X_{A}(t)=A\int_{s}^{t}\exp\Big\{\frac{u^{2}}{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u+\sigma\exp\Big\{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\int_{s}^{t}\exp\Big\{\frac{u^{2}}{2}\Big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}W(u),\;t\geq s.

s\geq 2
. Observe that, for
\kappa>0

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\exp\big\{\kappa u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-1}^{t}\exp\big\{\kappa u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u+\int_{s}^{(t-1)\vee s}\exp\big\{\kappa u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-1}^{t}\frac{u}{t-1}\exp\big\{\kappa u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u+\int_{s}^{(t-1)\vee s}\frac{u}{s}\exp\big\{\kappa u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa(t-1)}\exp\big\{\kappa t^{2}\big\}+\frac{1}{2\kappa s}\exp\big\{\kappa(t-1)^{2}\big\}.

Therefore, the first term in (

A\Big(\frac{1}{t-1}+\frac{1}{s}\exp\big\{-t+\frac{1}{2}\big\}\Big)

. The stochastic integral in (

The law of the iterated logarithm for Brownian motion (
(

, Theorem 2.9.23)
) implies that there exists a random variable

such that, for every

,

\overline{W}(r)\leq\overline{C}(\omega)+2\sqrt{r}\log^{+}\log^{+}r,

X_{A}(t)\leq A\Big(\frac{1}{t-1}+\frac{1}{s}\exp\big\{-t+\frac{1}{2}\big\}\Big)+\sigma\exp\Big\{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Big\}\Bigg(\overline{C}(\omega)+2\sqrt{\int_{s}^{t}\exp\big\{u^{2}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}u}\,2\log t\Bigg).

\displaystyle X_{A}(t)\leq

\displaystyle A\Big(\frac{1}{t-1}+\frac{1}{s}\exp\Big\{-t+\frac{1}{2}\Big\}\Big)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[\tau_{\overline{X}}\big]=\lim_{a\to-\infty}M_{a,0}(x)

\sup_{x\in(-\infty,0)}\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[\tau_{\overline{X}}\big]=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}\big[\tau_{\overline{X}}\big]=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\big(p(0)-p(y)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}m(y)=:D<\infty.

In particular, the probability that
X
starting at
(s,x)
with
x<0
reaches the interval
[0,\infty)
before or at time
s+2D
is at least
1/2
(by Markov’s inequality).

Next, we show that
u\mapsto B(u,0)
is bounded away from 0 on compact intervals. Together with the previous result, this implies the statement in Theorem
4.8
.

Fix
s\geq 0
. Then, for
t\in[s,s+1]
,

X_{s,t}(0)\geq b(t-s)+\sigma\big(W(t)-W(s)\big),

where
b:=b(s):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R},\,u\in[s,s+1]}\{x(x-u)\}=-\frac{1}{4}(s+1)^{2}
is a lower bound for the drift on
[s,s+1]
. Hence,

\mathbb{P}\big(X_{s,s+1}(0)\geq s+1\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma W(1)\geq s+1-b\big)

is bounded away from 0 uniformly for
s
in a compact interval and, using Theorem
4.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle B(s,0)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\big(X_{s,s+1}(0)\geq s+1\big)B(s+1,s+1)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma W(1)\geq s+1-b\big)B(s+1,s+1)\sim\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma W(1)\geq s+1-b\big),\,s\to\infty.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.8
.
∎

Remark 6.1

b=b(s)

Proof of Theorem

For every

for almost every

) and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma.

Then,

Y_{n}(t)\geq Z_{n}(t)

for all

\displaystyle Y_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=n^{-\alpha_{0}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-nt}+2\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-n(t-s)}\,\text{\rm{d}}s+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-n(t-s)}\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{n}(s)

(7.4)

n\in\mathbb{N}

\widetilde{Z}_{n}

\widetilde{Y}_{n}(t)=-n^{-\alpha_{0}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-nt}+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-n(t-s)}\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{n}(s).

.

) and Markov’s inequality,

8
Remarks and Conclusions

In this paper, using as an example the SDE (
4.1
), we have identified
and investigated the stochastic equivalent of a repelling river. Several
open questions remain, e.g. generalizations to other SDEs possibly driven by more general noise and other drift functions. One open question (reminiscent of the law of the iterated logarithm for Brownian motion) is to find decreasing positive deterministic functions
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
such that

\limsup_{s\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr{R}(s)-s}{l_{1}(s)}=1
and
\liminf_{s\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr{R}(s)-s}{l_{2}(s)}=-1
almost surely. If such functions exist then Remark
4.11
shows that they decrease (slightly) more slowly than

s^{-1/2}
. Using Corollary
4.12
it should not be too hard to find such functions by investigating the question for
\overline{\mathscr{R}}
instead of
\mathscr{R}
.

In the deterministic context, rivers can be
located in the absence of an exact solution by asymptotics

Blais (1991)
or by using the Ważewski principle
Srzednicki (2004)
;
Ważewski (1947)
. We
leave it to future work to determine whether similar methods could be
tailored to work in the stochastic case.

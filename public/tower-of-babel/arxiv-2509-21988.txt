Properties of computational entanglement measures
Ilia Ryzov, Faedi Loulidi, David Elkouss

Abstract
. Quantum entanglement is a useful resource for implementing communication tasks. However, for the resource to be useful in practice, it needs to be accessible by parties with bounded computational resources. Computational entanglement measures quantify the usefulness of entanglement in the presence of limited computational resources. In this paper, we analyze systematically some basic properties of two recently introduced computational entanglement measures, the computational distillable entanglement and entanglement cost. To do so, we introduce lower bound and upper bound extensions of basic properties to address the case when entanglement measures are not defined by a scalar value but when only lower or upper function bounds are available. In particular, we investigate the lower bound convexity and upper bound concavity properties of such measures, and the upper and lower bound additivity with respect to the tensor product. We also observe that these measures are not invariant with local unitaries, although invariance is recovered for efficient unitaries. As a consequence, we obtain that these measures are only LOCC monotones under efficient families of LOCC channels. Our analysis covers both the one-shot scenario and the uniform setting, with properties established for the former naturally extending to the latter.



1.
Introduction

Entanglement is a distinctive quantum correlation that empowers tasks impossible in the classical world. It serves as the key resource in various tasks, including quantum teleportation
[
BBC+93
]
, super-dense coding
[
BW92
]
, and quantum cryptography
[
PAB+20
]
. Traditional entanglement theory idealizes parties furnishing them with unbounded local computational power: so called local operations and classical communication (LOCC) protocols may call on arbitrarily large coherent unitaries and classical post-processing. Yet every real device is constrained by circuit depth, qubit count and classical computing time.

To bridge this gap Arnon-Friedman et al.
[
AFBV23
]
proposed a computational resource theory of entanglement restricting LOCC protocols to polynomial-size quantum circuits. Subsequent works showed how computational constraints radically reshape entanglement manipulations—revealing qualitative gaps in accessible entanglement, inspiring pseudo-entangled state constructions
[
LREJ25
]
, and even providing efficient-entanglement tools for probing AdS/CFT complexity
[
ABC+24
]
. Despite this growing relevance, the structure of computational entanglement measures remains largely unexplored.

We take first steps toward clarifying how classical entanglement-measure axioms translate to the two computational quantities introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
: the computational distillable entanglement and the computational entanglement cost. Because these measures are specified by function bounds rather than scalar values, the familiar notions of convexity, additivity and LOCC or Local Unitary (LU) invariance must be reformulated. In particular, we

•

introduce lower-bound convexity and upper-bound concavity to capture how efficient distillation or dilution behaves under mixing;

•

establish lower-bound superadditivity for distillable entanglement and upper-bound subadditivity for entanglement cost under tensor products;

•

show that full LU/LOCC monotonicity can fail, yet holds whenever the operations themselves are computationally efficient;

•

and lift all one-shot statements to uniform families of instances, matching the keyed settings common in cryptography.

These results supply a first structural toolkit for reasoning about entanglement when local computation is bounded. The remainder is organized as follows. In Section
2
we summarize our main theorems. In Section
3
we review quantum information theory preliminaries. In Section
4
,
we recall the computational measures introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
and we introduce extended notions of some entanglement measures properties to their lower bound/upper bound counterparts. In Section,
5
, we analyze the lower bound convexity and the upper bound concavity property for each of the computational distillable entanglement and cost. In Section
6
, we analyze the upper bound subadditivity and lower bound superadditivity properties. In Section
7
, analyze the invariance under local unitaries and LOCC monotonicity for each of the computational distillable entanglement and cost, respectively.

2.
Main Results

In this work, we analyze the mathematical properties of the
computational distillable entanglement
and the
computational entanglement cost

[
AFBV23
]
, which quantify, under computational constraints, the number of EPR pairs one can extract from a given state and the number of EPR pairs needed to prepare it, respectively. We begin by presenting properties that entanglement measures may satisfy. We then recall informal definitions of the computational entanglement measures and summarize the main results.

Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
denote the set of quantum states shared between Alice and Bob, where
\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
are their respective Hilbert spaces. Let
E
be a generic entanglement measure:

\displaystyle E:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle\mapsto E(\rho_{AB})\geq 0.

Entanglement measures are typically evaluated under the paradigm of
local operations and classical communication
(LOCC). An LOCC protocol consists of an arbitrary sequence of local quantum operations performed independently by each party followed by classical communication.

An entanglement measure
E
may satisfy some of the following properties:

•

Convexity/Concavity
:
E
is convex (concave), if for any finite set of bipartite states
\{\rho^{x}_{AB}\}_{x\in X}
and for any probability distribution
p
over
X
the following holds:

E\left(\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\rho^{x}_{AB}\right)\underset{\displaystyle(\geq)}{\leq}\sum_{x\in X}p(x)E(\rho^{x}_{AB}).

•

Superadditivity/Subadditivity
:
E
is superadditive (subadditive) if for any given states shared between Alice and Bob
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
the following holds:

E(\rho_{AB}\otimes\sigma_{AB})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}E(\rho_{AB})+E(\sigma_{AB}).

•

LOCC monotonicity
:
E
is an LOCC monotone if for any LOCC protocol
\Gamma
and state
\rho_{AB}
,

E(\rho_{AB})\geq E(\Gamma(\rho_{AB})).

•

Invariance under local unitaries
:
E
is invariant under local unitaries if for any shared state
\rho_{AB}
and for any unitary
U_{AB}:=U_{A}\otimes U_{B}
we have

E(U_{AB}\,\rho_{AB}\,U^{\dagger}_{AB})=E(\rho_{AB}).

While these properties are well-studied for entanglement measures, many open problems remain (see
[
BCY11
, Table 1]
).

Computational entanglement theory
[
AFBV23
]
introduces entanglement measures under computational assumptions. Specifically, it restricts LOCC operations to those implementable by circuits with a polynomial number of gates.

Definition 2.1
.

(Informal of Definition
4.7
) A family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
is efficient if every LOCC channel in the family admits a circuit representation and the total number of gates in these circuit representations grows at most polynomially with
\lambda
.

Figure

illustrates such circuit representations. The definitions of computational distillable entanglement and entanglement cost introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
(see also Definitions

and
4.9

(Informal)
.

Let
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}
be a family of bipartite quantum states.
A function

The main goal of this work is to analyze different properties of both the computational distillable entanglement
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{D}
and the computational entanglement cost

However, the conventional desired properties of entanglement measures (such as convexity (concavity), superadditivity (subadditivity) and so on) are defined assuming that entanglement measures have a scalar value. The computational distillable entanglement and cost, however, are given by a valid function lower and upper bounds, respectively. Therefore, to analyze different mathematical properties we need to extend classical properties to accommodate for such measures. For this we define
lower and upper bound
counterparts of some selected properties of entanglement measures. We refer to Section
4
for more details.

Our first results establish that, in both the one-shot and uniform settings,
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{D}
is
lower bound convex
, and
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}
is
upper bound concave
. See Section
5
.

3.1.
Quantum Information Theory

In this subsection, we fix the notation and recall the elements of quantum information theory needed.

First, we recall the Landau notation; we use the Big-Omega notation
f(n)=\Omega(g(n))
if
f(n)
is bounded below by
g(n)
asymptotically, more precisely if there exists
c>0,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f(n)\geq c\cdot g(n)
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. We denote by
\operatorname{Poly}(\lambda)
the set of real-valued polynomials with
\lambda
as dependent variable. We also use the convention of
[N]:=\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

In the following, we assume the dimension of Hilbert spaces is finite
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the space of
n\times n
matrices. One can introduce metrics on this space using (Schatten)
p
norms
\|\cdot\|_{p}
defined for
1\leq p\leq\infty
:

\forall A\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\|A\|_{p}:=\Tr\left(|A|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{where}\quad|A|:=\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})\cong\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the set of linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. In quantum information theory, a quantum state is described by density matrices
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
where:

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}):=\{\rho\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\,\rho\geq 0,\,\Tr\rho=1\},

and denote by
\mathbb{U}(n)
the space of unitary matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

A quantum state
\rho
is pure if
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
where
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}
. We use the notation
\psi
to denote pure states of the form
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
. Moreover, we denote by
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
the set of
bipartite quantum states
. We use the shorthand notation
\mathcal{H}_{AB}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. A state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
is
separable
if it can be expressed as a convex combination of product states
\{\sigma^{x}_{A}\otimes\tilde{\sigma}^{x}_{B}\}_{x\in X}
:

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\sigma^{x}_{A}\otimes\tilde{\sigma}^{x}_{B}.

If a state is not separable, we say it is
entangled
.
Recall that a
partial trace
is a map from a bipartite system to one of the subsystems. Formally we denote by
\Tr_{B}
the partial trace on the subsystem
B
given by:

\displaystyle\Tr_{B}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})

\displaystyle\Tr_{B}:\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle\to\sigma_{A}:=\Tr_{B}\rho_{AB},

where
\Tr_{B}(\cdot):=I_{A}\otimes\Tr(\cdot)
.

Another relevant notion that we make use of in this work is the
LOCC channel
. LOCC channels represent transformations of a state in the scenario where two physically separated parties, Alice and Bob, each perform some
local operations
and exchange
classical information
based on a measurement of their part of resulting state.

Definition 3.1
.

An LOCC channel
\Gamma_{AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
, is a quantum channel where:

\Gamma_{AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\quad\text{where}\quad\Gamma_{AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}:=\sum_{y\in Y}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{W}^{y}_{B},

for some finite alphabet
Y
and
\{\mathcal{S}_{A}^{y}\}
and
\{\mathcal{W}_{B}^{y}\}
are complete positive trace-non-increasing maps, i.e.
\Tr S^{y}_{A}(\cdot)\leq\Tr(\cdot)
and the same for
W^{y}_{B}
, such that
\sum_{y\in Y}\mathcal{S}^{y}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{W}^{y}_{B}
is trace preserving.

From now on, to simplify the notation, we denote by
\Gamma
any LOCC channels instead of
\Gamma_{AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
.

Another important quantity is the
fidelity

[
KW24
]
. Intuitively, the fidelity measures the similarity between two given quantum states.

Definition 3.2
.

Let the quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, the fidelity is defined as:

F(\rho,\sigma):=\left\|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\right\|_{1}^{2}

It is easy to check if one of the quantum states is pure, the fidelity reduces to:

F(\rho,|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=\bra{\psi}\rho\ket{\psi}.

We recall some of the properties of fidelity.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let a set of states
\{\rho_{x}\}_{x\in X}
with
X
a finite register and let
\sigma
quantum states in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, the fidelity is a concave function in one of its argument:

F\Big(\sum_{x\in X}\,p(x)\,\rho_{x},\sigma\Big)\geq\sum_{x\in X}\,p(x)\,F(\rho_{x},\sigma).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,2\}}
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in\{1,2\}}
quantum states in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{i})
with
i\in\{1,2\}
, the fidelity function factorizes as:

F(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2},\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})=F(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1})F(\rho_{2},\sigma_{2}).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be quantum states in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
U
be a unitary operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the fidelity is unitary invariant

F(U\rho U^{\dagger},U\sigma U^{\dagger})=F(\rho,\sigma).

Another interesting property of the fidelity is relation between the fidelity and the Schatten
1
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, which is also known as Fuchs & van de Graaf inequality:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be quantum states in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
. Then the following inequalities hold

1-\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)}.

Finally, we recall the data-processing inequality:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
, and let
\tilde{\Phi}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
be a quantum channel. Then the following holds

F(\rho,\sigma)\leq F(\tilde{\Phi}(\rho),\tilde{\Phi}(\sigma)).

3.2.
Entanglement Measures

In this subsection, we recall different entanglement measures that are relevant for the rest of this work.

The first two entanglement measures we recall, are the
distillable entanglement
and
entanglement cost
denoted respectively by
E_{D}
and
E_{C}
. The distillable entanglement and entanglement cost represent, respectively, the asymptotic rate at which one can extract EPR pairs from a generic bipartite state and the asymptotic rate at which one can fully reconstruct a given bipartite state from EPR pairs. We recall that EPR pair state is given by
|\varphi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)
. We also denote corresponding density matrix of EPR pair as
\Phi=|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|
.

We first start with distillable entanglement
[
BDSW96
,
BBP+96
]
. The
one-shot distillable entanglement
denoted by
E^{\varepsilon}_{D}
and the
asymptotic distillable entanglement
denoted by
E_{D}
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathcal{H}_{B}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}}=\mathcal{H}_{\bar{B}}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m}
. Define the LOCC channel
\Gamma:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{B}})
. The
one-shot distillable
entanglement of a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
is given by:

\forall\varepsilon\in[0,1],\quad E^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\rho_{AB}):=\sup_{m,\Gamma}\{\,m\,|\,p_{\text{err}}(\Gamma,\rho_{AB})\leq\varepsilon\},

where

p_{\emph{err}}(\Gamma,\rho_{AB})=1-\bra{\varphi^{\otimes m}}\Gamma(\rho_{AB})\ket{\varphi^{\otimes m}}.

From the definition above, the one-shot distillable entanglement
E^{\varepsilon}_{D}
represents the number of EPR pairs
\ket{\varphi}
that can be extracted from a given state
\rho_{AB}
with a given precision
\varepsilon
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
a bipartite state on
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
, the
asymptotic distillable entanglement
is given by:

E_{D}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}E^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes t}).

The distillable entanglement is LOCC monotone
[
BDSW96
]
and superadditive on tensor product
[
BR05
]
. At the same time, it is known that distillable entanglement, if one assumes validity of necessary conjectures, is not convex
[
SST01
]
.

Similar to the distillable entanglement, we first introduce the
one-shot entanglement cost
and then the
asymptotic entanglement cost
denoted respectively by
E_{C}^{\varepsilon}
and
E_{C}
.

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{A}=\mathcal{H}_{B}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}}=\mathcal{H}_{\bar{B}}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
. Define the LOCC channel
\Gamma:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{\bar{A}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\bar{B}})
. The
one-shot entanglement cost
of a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
is given by:

\forall\varepsilon\in[0,1],\quad E^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{n,\Gamma}\{\,n\,|\,p_{\text{err}}(\Gamma,\rho_{AB})\leq\varepsilon\},

where

p_{\emph{err}}(\Gamma,\rho_{AB}):=1-F(\Gamma(\Phi^{\otimes n}),\rho_{AB}).

The one-shot entanglement cost
E^{\varepsilon}_{C}
represents the “minimal” number of copies of EPR pairs needed to recover a bipartite state with a given precision
\varepsilon
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
a bipartite quantum state, the asymptotic entanglement cost is given by:

E_{C}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}E^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes t}).

The entanglement cost is LOCC monotone due to connection to regularized entanglement of formation
[
HHT01
]
and LOCC monotonicity of the latter
[
BDSW96
]
. Entanglement cost is known to be convex
[
DHR02
]
.

Another major entanglement measure is
squashed entanglement
. We recall its definition and relation to distillable entanglement and entanglement cost
[
CW04a
]
.

We first recall the notion of conditional mutual information.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\rho_{ABE}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{ABE})
be a tripartite state. The
conditional mutual information
of the state
\rho_{AB}
is defined as follows:

I(A;B|E)_{\rho}:=H(AE)_{\rho}+H(BE)_{\rho}-H(ABE)_{\rho}-H(E)_{\rho},

where
H(\cdot)_{\rho}
is the von Neumann entropy.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
be a bipartite state. The
squashed entanglement
of the state
\rho_{AB}
is defined as follows:

E_{sq}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2}I(A;B|E)_{\rho_{ABE}}\,|\,\rho_{ABE}\right\},

where
\rho_{ABE}
is an extension of
\rho_{AB}:\,\operatorname{Tr}_{E}(\rho_{ABE})=\rho_{AB}
.

The squashed entanglement measure is LOCC monotone, convex and additive on tensor product
[
CW04b
]
.

In the following we recall relation of squashed entanglement with entanglement cost and distillable entanglement. First of all, it is lower bounded by the one-shot distillable entanglement.

Proposition 3.14
.

[
KW24
, Theorem 13.9]

Let
\rho_{AB}
a bipartite state in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
. Then the following holds:

E^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\rho_{AB})\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}}\left(E_{sq}(\rho_{AB})+g_{2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right)\right),

where
g_{2}(\delta):=(\delta+1)\operatorname{log}_{2}(\delta+1)-\delta\operatorname{log}_{2}\delta
.

Moreover, the squashed entanglement is upper bounded by entanglement cost and lower bounded by distillable entanglement
[
CW04a
]
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})
, the following relations hold:

E_{D}(\rho_{AB})\leq E_{\text{sq}}(\rho_{AB})\leq E_{C}(\rho_{AB}).

4.
Computational Entanglement

In this section we introduce extensions of some entanglement measures properties. These extensions address entanglement measures that admit function lower/upper bounds. We then recall key notions from computational entanglement theory as introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
.

In Subsection
4.1
we introduce extensions of some desirable entanglement measures properties. In Subsection
4.2
, we recall the notion of computational distillable entanglement and entanglement cost in the one-shot setting — computational analogues of the one-shot distillable entanglement and one-shot entanglement cost in Section
3
. Finally, in Subsection
4.3
, we recall the computational distillable entanglement and entanglement cost in the uniform setting.

4.1.
Computational Entanglement Measures Properties

In this subsection, we introduce refined formulations of well-known entanglement measure properties. These generalized definitions capture the essential features of properties such as convexity, concavity, subadditivity, superadditivity, invariance under local unitaries, and LOCC monotonicity. In particular, computational entanglement theory provides only function upper or lower bounds rather than scalar values. This motivates the definitions below, which adapt the properties to measures that admit only such bounds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E
be an entanglement measure and let
\{\rho_{x}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+},x\in X}
be a family of bipartite states indexed by a growth parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and finite alphabet
X
. We say that
E
is
lower bound convex
(
upper bound concave
) if

E(\{\sigma^{\lambda}\})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}n(\lambda)\implies\sum_{x\in X}p(x)E(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}n(\lambda)\quad\text{with}\quad\sigma^{\lambda}:=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\rho^{\lambda}_{x}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{1}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
and
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{2}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be two families of bipartite states with entanglement measure
E
admitting two lower bounds (upper bound)
n_{1}(\lambda)
and
n_{2}(\lambda)
. We say the entanglement measure
E
is a
lower bound subadditive
(
upper bound superraditive
) if the following holds

E(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\rho^{\lambda}_{2}\})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}n_{1}(\lambda)+n_{2}(\lambda).

Definition 4.3
.

An entanglement measure
E
with a lower bound (upper bound)
m(\lambda)
for a family of states
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is said to be invariant under family of local unitaries if the following holds

E(\{U^{\lambda}_{AB}\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}U^{\lambda\dagger}_{AB}\})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}m(\lambda)\iff E(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\underset{\displaystyle(\leq)}{\geq}m(\lambda),

for all families of local unitaries
\{U^{\lambda}_{AB}:=U^{\lambda}_{A}\otimes U^{\lambda}_{B}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let an entanglement measure
E
with a valid upper bound
n(\lambda)
(or lower bound
m(\lambda)
) for a family of states
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
. We say that
E
is LOCC monotone if for any family of LOCC maps
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
the following holds:

\displaystyle E(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})

\displaystyle\leq n(\lambda)\implies E(\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\})\leq n(\lambda)

\displaystyle E(\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\})

\displaystyle\geq m(\lambda)\implies E(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\geq m(\lambda).

4.2.
Computational One-Shot Entanglement

In this subsection, we recall the definitions of computational one-shot distillable entanglement and computational one-shot entanglement cost.

We begin by recalling the definition of a circuit representation for LOCC channels, followed by the notion of an efficient family of such channels. An efficient family consists of LOCC maps whose circuit descriptions scale polynomially with the parameter
\lambda
. This formalizes the idea of
efficient scaling
in a computational setting; see
[
AFBV23
]
for details.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be an arbitrary input state on
n_{A}+n_{B}
qubits:

\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{A}}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{B}}.

A LOCC channel
\Gamma
with
r
rounds admits a circuit description if it can be represented by two families of circuits
\{\mathcal{C}_{A,i}\}_{i\in[r]}
and
\{\mathcal{C}_{B,i}\}_{i\in[r]}
. The family
\{\mathcal{C}_{A,i}\}
acts on registers
A
,
A^{\prime}
, and
C
, consisting of
n_{A}+t_{A}+q
qubits:
A
is Alice’s input register,
A^{\prime}
is her ancilla register, and
C
is a shared classical communication register. The circuits
\{\mathcal{C}_{B,i}\}
are defined analogously for Bob.

Initially,
A
and
B
hold the input state
\rho_{AB}
;
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are initialized in
|0\rangle^{\otimes t_{A}}
and
|0\rangle^{\otimes t_{B}}
, respectively, and
C
is initialized in
|0\rangle^{\otimes q}
. At each round
i\in[r]
, Alice applies
\mathcal{C}_{A,i}
to
(A,A^{\prime},C)
and measures
C
in the computational basis. Bob then applies
\mathcal{C}_{B,i}
to
(B,B^{\prime},C)
and measures
C
again. The output state is stored in the first
m_{A}
qubits of
(A,A^{\prime})
and the first
m_{B}
qubits of
(B,B^{\prime})
.

Figure 1.
A circuit representation of a LOCC channel
\hat{\Gamma}
according to Definition
4.7
.

Definition 4.6
.

A LOCC channel
\Gamma
is said to have
circuit size

c
if it can be implemented by a circuit with at most
c
total gates, including all unitaries, ancilla creation, and measurements.

From a computational viewpoint, it is natural to restrict to circuit representations whose size grows at most polynomially. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 4.7
.

A family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
is called
efficient
if each
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
has a circuit description of size at most
c(\lambda)
for some
c(\lambda)\in\operatorname{Poly}(\lambda)
.

We now recall the definitions of the
computational one-shot distillable entanglement
and
computational one-shot entanglement cost
. Each quantifies the number of EPR pairs that can be distilled from—or required to reproduce—a given state family, under the assumption that only efficient families of LOCC protocols are permitted. These were introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to[0,1]
and let
n_{A},n_{B}:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
be polynomial functions. Consider a family of bipartite states
\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
with

\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{A}(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{B}(\lambda)}.

Then the
computational one-shot distillable entanglement with error
\varepsilon
of the state family
\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}
is denoted as

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\right\}\right).

A function
m:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
is a
valid lower bound
on
\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}\left(\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}\right)
if there exists an efficient family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
producing
2m(\lambda)
-qubit states such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\quad p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}):=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\right)\leq\varepsilon(\lambda).

In this case, each channel
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
maps the state
\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}
to a state on
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m(\lambda)}
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to[0,1]
and let
n_{A},n_{B}:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
be polynomial functions. Consider a family of states
\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
with

\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{A}(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{B}(\lambda)}.

Then the
computational one-shot entanglement cost with error
\varepsilon
is denoted as

\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\right\}\right).

A function
n:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
is a
valid upper bound
on
\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}\left(\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}\right)
if there exists an efficient family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\quad p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}):=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\right)\leq\varepsilon(\lambda).

Here, each channel
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
maps the EPR input state on
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n(\lambda)}
to an approximation of the target state
\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}
.

Finally, since the set of valid LOCCs in the computational quantities is restricted, we trivially have the following relations:

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\{\rho^{\lambda}\})\geq m(\lambda)

\displaystyle\implies E^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\rho^{\lambda})\geq m(\lambda),\,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho^{\lambda}\})\leq n(\lambda)

\displaystyle\implies E^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\rho^{\lambda})\leq n(\lambda),\,\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

4.3.
Uniform Computational Entanglement

In this subsection, we recall the
uniform
description for each computational distillable entanglement and cost.

The concept of computational one-shot distillable entanglement/cost can be generalized to the case when one wants to distill/dilute multiple states corresponding to one value of the parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. The requirement for this quantity is that the maximal error of operation should remain the same for all states corresponding to the same
\lambda
. This requirement corresponds to the notion of
uniformity
.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to[0,1]
and let
n_{A},n_{B},\kappa:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
be any polynomials. Let the encoded family of quantum states be
\{\rho^{k}_{AB}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
with

\rho_{AB}^{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{A}(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{B}(\lambda)}.

Then the
computational uniform distillable entanglement with error
\varepsilon
is denoted as

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{k,\rho_{AB}^{k}\right\}\right).

A function
m(\lambda)
is a valid lower bound on the computational distillable entanglement of the family
\{k,\rho^{k}_{AB}\}

if there exist an efficient family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\,\forall k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)},\quad p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},k,\rho^{k}_{AB})\leq\varepsilon(\lambda),

where

p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},k,\rho^{k}_{AB})=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(k,\rho^{k}_{AB}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\right)=1-\bra{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(k,\rho^{k}_{AB})\ket{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}.

It is clear from the definition above that the member of efficient family of LOCC channels is the channel
\hat{\Gamma}(k,\rho^{k}_{AB})
that acts on the bipartition
A^{\prime}A:BB^{\prime}
. More precisely

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{D}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{AB}):=\mathcal{D}\left((\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}:|k_{A^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{A^{\prime}}|\otimes\rho^{k}_{AB}\otimes|k_{B^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{B^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\to\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(|k_{A^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{A^{\prime}}|\otimes\rho^{k}_{AB}\otimes|k_{B^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{B^{\prime}}|),

where
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}})
represent respectively extra registers for Alice and Bob encoding the different keys used in the distillation protocol via the circuit representation of LOCC channel.

As for the uniform computational distillable entanglement, we recall the definition of uniform computational entanglement cost.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to[0,1]
and let
n_{A},n_{B},\kappa:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
be any polynomials. Let the encoded family of quantum states be
\{\rho^{k}_{AB}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
with

\rho_{AB}^{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{H}_{AB}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{A}(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n_{B}(\lambda)}.

Then the
computational uniform entanglement cost with error
\varepsilon
is denoted as

\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{k,\rho_{AB}^{k}\right\}\right).

A function
n(\lambda)
is a valid upper bound on the uniform computational cost entanglement of the family
\{k,\rho^{k}_{AB}\}

if there exist an efficient family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
such that

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\,\forall k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)},\quad p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},k,\rho^{k}_{AB})\leq\varepsilon(\lambda),

where

p_{\emph{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},k,\rho^{k}_{AB})=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(k,\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{k}_{AB}\right).

Here the member of efficient family of LOCC channels is the channel
\hat{\Gamma}(k,\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)})
that acts on the bipartition
A^{\prime}\tilde{A}:\tilde{B}B^{\prime}
. More precisely

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}:|k_{A^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{A^{\prime}}|\otimes\Phi^{\otimes n}\otimes|k_{B^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{B^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\to\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(|k_{A^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{A^{\prime}}|\otimes\Phi^{\otimes n}\otimes|k_{B^{\prime}}\rangle\langle k_{B^{\prime}}|),

where

\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}\tilde{B}}:=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n(\lambda)}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n(\lambda)}.

Moreover, the spaces
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{B^{\prime}})
represent, respectively, extra registers for Alice and Bob encoding the different keys used in the dilution protocol via the efficient circuit representation of LOCC channels.

Abusing notation, in the following, we write
\{\rho^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
and
\{\rho^{k}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
instead of
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
and
\{\rho^{k}_{AB}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
.

5.
Convexity and Concavity

In this section we analyze the upper bound concavity and lower bound convexity of computational entanglement measures in the sense of definitions given in the Subsection
4.1
.

In Subsection
5.1
, we establish that the computational one-shot distillable entanglement is lower bound convex and that the computational one-shot entanglement cost is upper bound concave. These properties extend naturally to the uniform setting (Subsection
5.2
).

5.1.
Computational One-Shot Case

We begin by establishing structural properties of the computational one-shot entanglement measures. Specifically, we show that the computational one-shot distillable entanglement is
lower bound convex
, while the computational one-shot entanglement cost is
upper bound concave
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\}_{x\in X,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a family of bipartite quantum states, where
X
is a finite set. Let
\sigma^{\lambda}:=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\rho^{\lambda}_{x}
for each
\lambda
, where
p(x)
is a probability distribution. Then,

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}(\{\sigma^{\lambda}\})\geq m(\lambda)\implies\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\geq m(\lambda),

where
\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda)=\varepsilon(\lambda)
and
\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda):=1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{x}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)})
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
be an efficient LOCC channel such that
m(\lambda)
is a valid lower bound on
\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}(\{\sigma^{\lambda}\})
. Since
\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}
is pure, by linearity of quantum operations and fidelity in Remark
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{x\in X}p(x)F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{x}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\left(1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{x}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)})\right).

Let
\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda):=1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{x}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)})
. Since
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
is also a valid efficient map for each
\rho^{\lambda}_{x}
, we conclude that

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\geq m(\lambda),

for all
x\in X
, and therefore

\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\geq m(\lambda).

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\}_{x\in X,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a family of bipartite quantum states, where
X
is a finite set, and let
\sigma^{\lambda}:=\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\rho^{\lambda}_{x}
for each
\lambda
, with
p(x)
a probability distribution. Then,

\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}(\{\sigma^{\lambda}\})\leq n(\lambda)\implies\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\leq n(\lambda),

where
\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda)\geq\varepsilon(\lambda)
and
\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda):=1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{x})
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
be an efficient LOCC channel such that
n(\lambda)
is a valid upper bound on
\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}(\{\sigma^{\lambda}\})
. By concavity of fidelity (Proposition
3.3
),

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\left(1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{x})\right).

Let
\varepsilon_{x}(\lambda):=1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{x})
. Since
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
is a valid efficient LOCC channel for all
\rho^{\lambda}_{x}
, it follows that

\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\leq n(\lambda),

for all
x\in X
, and therefore

\sum_{x\in X}p(x)\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon_{x}}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{x}\})\leq n(\lambda).

∎

6.
Superadditivity and Subadditivity

In this section, we show the behavior of computational one-shot distillable entanglement and entanglement cost with respect to the tensor product. In Subsection
6.1
, we prove that each computational distillable entanglement and entanglement cost are respectively lower bound superadditive and upper bound subadditive in the one-shot setting. In Subsection
6.2
, as a direct consequence, we show that the same properties hold in the uniform scenario.

6.1.
Computational One-Shot Case

In this subsection, we analyze the behavior of the computational one-shot distillable entanglement and entanglement cost with respect to the tensor product.

In the following theorem, we prove that the one-shot computational distillable entanglement is lower bound superadditive.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\{\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
and
\{\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be families of bipartite quantum states. Suppose

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon_{i}}(\{\rho_{i}^{\lambda}\})\geq m_{i}(\lambda)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then:

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}(\{\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\})\geq m_{1}(\lambda)+m_{2}(\lambda),

where
\varepsilon(\lambda)\leq\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda)+\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
\{\hat{\Gamma}_{i}^{\lambda}\}_{i\in\{1,2\}}
be the efficient family of LOCC channels achieving
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon_{i}}_{D}(\{\rho_{i}^{\lambda}\})\geq m_{i}(\lambda)
. Now consider the efficient family of LOCC channels

\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}\}
defined as:

\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}:=\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2},

allowing to extract a certain amount of EPR pairs
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda})
with probability error given by:

p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{12}^{\lambda},\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda})=1-\bra{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}\hat{\Gamma}_{12}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\rho^{\lambda}_{2})\ket{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}=\varepsilon(\lambda),

where
m(\lambda)\equiv m_{1}(\lambda)+m_{2}(\lambda)
.

By linearity, we obtain that:

(1)

\varepsilon(\lambda)=1-\bra{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}\hat{\Gamma}_{12}^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\rho^{\lambda}_{2})\ket{\varphi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}}\leq 1-(1-\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda))(1-\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda))\leq\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda)+\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda).

where the upper bound holds from the assumption and the particular choice of the efficient LOCC channels
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}=\hat{\Gamma}_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2}
.
∎

In the following, we show the upper bound subadditivity property of the computational one-shot entanglement cost.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\left\{\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
and
\left\{\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be families of bipartite quantum states. Suppose that
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon_{i}}_{C}(\rho^{\lambda}_{i})\leq n_{i}(\lambda)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Then:

\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}(\{\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\})\leq n_{1}(\lambda)+n_{2}(\lambda),

where
\varepsilon(\lambda)\leq\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda)+\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Consider the efficient family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}_{12}^{\lambda}\}
defined as:

\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\quad\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}:=\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2},

allowing to achieve the one shot computational cost
\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}(\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda})
with probability error:

p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{12}^{\lambda},\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{1}^{\lambda})=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}(\Phi_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}(\lambda)}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes n_{2}(\lambda)}),\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\right)=\varepsilon(\lambda).

By the tensor product property of the fidelity (Proposition
3.4
), the following holds:

\varepsilon(\lambda)=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}(\Phi_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}(\lambda)}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes n_{2}(\lambda)}),\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\right)=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{1}(\Phi_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}(\lambda)}),\rho_{1}^{\lambda}\right)\,F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2}(\Phi_{2}^{\otimes n_{2}(\lambda)}),\rho_{2}^{\lambda}\right),

for the particular choice of family of LOCC channels
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}=\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
. Therefore from the assumption that
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon_{i}}_{C}(\rho^{\lambda}_{i})\leq n_{i}(\lambda)
achieved with probability error:

p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}_{i}^{\lambda},\rho_{i}^{\lambda})=1-F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{i}(\Phi_{i}^{\otimes n_{i}(\lambda)}),\rho_{i}^{\lambda}\right)\leq\varepsilon_{i}(\lambda),

we have:

(2)

\varepsilon(\lambda)\leq 1-(1-\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda))(1-\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda))\leq\varepsilon_{1}(\lambda)+\varepsilon_{2}(\lambda),

obtained for the particular choice of LOCC channels
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{12}=\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{1}\otimes\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}_{2}
.

∎

7.
Unitary Invariance and LOCC Monotonicity

In this section we examine how computational constraints affect two classical desiderata for entanglement measures: LU invariance and LOCC monotonicity. We proceed in two steps:

In Subsection
7.1
, we formalize efficient families of local unitaries and efficient LOCC channels, and the corresponding notions of LU invariance and LOCC monotonicity for computational measures. In Subsection
7.2
, we show that, in both one-shot and uniform settings, the computational distillable entanglement and the computational entanglement cost retain LU invariance only when the unitary family is efficient. In Subsection
7.3
, we show, as corollary of LU invariance, that these computational entanglement measures are LOCC-monotone only under efficient LOCC protocols in both one-shot and uniform setting. Thus, unconstrained local operations can increase or decrease computational entanglement, while their efficient counterparts preserve the familiar monotonic behavior.

7.2.
Invariance Under Local Unitaries

In this subsection, we analyze the invariance under local unitaries of each of the computational entanglement measures.

The discussion unfolds in four stages.
First, we recall the
\eta
–net machinery on the unitary group together with the iterative family of states

introduced by Arnon-Friedman, Brakerski and Vidick
[
AFBV23
]

\bigl|S^{\lambda}\bigr|\geq\bigl(1/\eta\bigr)^{\Omega(2^{2m(\lambda)})}

Second, we compare the cardinality of

with any
efficient
family of LOCC channels or local unitaries and derive two counting lemmas that will be invoked repeatedly.
Third, we prove that the computational one-shot

is LU-invariant whenever the LU family is efficient, and we show—using the counting lemmas and the set

Fourth, we treat the computational one-shot

specifying precisely when invariance holds and supply counter-examples when it does not.
Corollaries
7.11
and
7.13
extend all results to the uniform (keyed) scenario in a straightforward component-wise manner.

We now recall the iterative construction and the necessary combinatorial tools.

First, we introduce an
\varepsilon

Definition 7.7

Proposition 7.8

We first show, in Theorem

, that the computational one-shot entanglement cost is invariant under efficient families of local unitaries while for generic families of unitaries it is not invariant for all choices of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. We then show, in Theorem

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a family of bipartite states, and let
\{U^{\lambda}_{AB}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be an efficient family of local unitaries. Then the computational one-shot entanglement cost is invariant under such unitary transformations:

\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\leq n(\lambda)\iff\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{U^{\lambda}_{AB}\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}U^{\lambda\dagger}_{AB}\})\leq n(\lambda).

In contrast, for general (not necessarily efficient) families of local unitaries, this invariance does not hold.

Proof.

We first prove the positive direction: invariance under efficient local unitaries. Assume that
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\leq n(\lambda)
. Consider the transformed family
\{\rho^{\prime\lambda}_{AB}:=U^{\lambda}_{AB}\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}U^{\lambda\dagger}_{AB}\}
. Using the unitary invariance of fidelity (Proposition
3.5
) and the efficient implementability of
U^{\lambda}_{AB}
, one can define an efficient LOCC family
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
by conjugation:

\Gamma^{\prime\lambda}(\cdot):=U^{\lambda}_{AB}\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\cdot)U^{\lambda\dagger}_{AB}.

Then, for each
\lambda
we have:

F\left(\Gamma^{\prime\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\prime\lambda}_{AB}\right)=F\left(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon(\lambda),

showing that the transformed family is also dilutable at cost
n(\lambda)
via an efficient LOCC. The converse direction follows by symmetry, proving the equivalence.

We now show by contradiction that this invariance fails for generic (non-efficient) families of local unitaries. Denote by
\{\psi^{\lambda}_{a}\}_{a}
the finite set of all possible states in
S^{\lambda}
and assume that
S^{\lambda}\equiv\{\psi^{\lambda}_{a}\}_{a}
is
n(\lambda)
dilutable, i.e
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\psi^{\lambda}_{a}\})\leq n(\lambda)
. From the construction of
S^{\lambda}
we have that for two distinct states from
S^{\lambda}
we have
|\langle\psi^{\lambda}_{a}|\psi^{\lambda}_{b}\rangle|\leq 1-\eta
with
a\neq b
.

Define the set of states around a given state
\psi_{i}\in S^{\lambda}
by
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon(\lambda)}(\psi^{\lambda}_{i})
where
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{AB})
is given by:

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{AB}):=\left\{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{AB})\,|\,F\left(\sigma_{AB},\rho_{AB}\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon^{2}\right\}.

From the Lemma
7.9
, the total number of states is
S^{\lambda}
is exponentially larger than the total number of efficient LOCC channels:

\frac{|\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}|}{|S^{\lambda}|}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{-\Omega(2^{2n(\lambda)})}.

Therefore, there exists an efficient LOCC channel
\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
which dilutes two distinct pure states
\psi_{a}^{\lambda}
and
\psi_{b}^{\lambda}

a\neq b,\quad\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi_{AB}^{\otimes n(\lambda)})=:\sigma^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon(\lambda)}(\psi^{\lambda}_{a})\cap\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon(\lambda)}(\psi^{\lambda}_{b}).

Applying the triangle and the Fuchs–van de Graaf inequality in Proposition

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi^{\lambda}_{a}-\psi^{\lambda}_{b}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi^{\lambda}_{a}-\sigma^{\lambda}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi^{\lambda}_{b}-\sigma^{\lambda}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\varepsilon(\lambda),

where we have used that for

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi_{i}^{\lambda}-\sigma^{\lambda}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{1-F(\sigma^{\lambda},\psi_{i}^{\lambda})}\leq\varepsilon(\lambda).

On the other hand, the distance between
\psi^{\lambda}_{a}
and
\psi^{\lambda}_{b}

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi^{\lambda}_{a}-\psi^{\lambda}_{b}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-\sqrt{F\left(\psi^{\lambda}_{a},\psi^{\lambda}_{b}\right)}\geq\eta.

By combining the upper and the lower bound

\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\psi_{a}^{\lambda}-\psi_{b}^{\lambda}\right\|_{1}\leq 2\varepsilon(\lambda),

which should hold for all
\eta
and
\varepsilon

Choosing

to be greater than

yields a contradiction with the assumption that
S^{\lambda}
is dilutable with precision
\varepsilon(\lambda)
, hence the proof of the theorem.

We have the following chain of inequalities:

(3)

\displaystyle m(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq E^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\psi^{\lambda})

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}}\left(E_{sq}(\psi^{\lambda})+g_{2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right)\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}}\left(m(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H(AB)_{\psi^{\lambda}}+g_{2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right)\right),

where the second inequality follows from Proposition

.

Computation shows that
H(AB)_{\psi^{\lambda}}\equiv H(AB)_{\psi^{\lambda}}[x]
is given by:

H(AB)_{\psi^{\lambda}}[x]:=1-\frac{1}{2}(1-x)\operatorname{log}_{2}(1-x)-\frac{1}{2}(1+x)\operatorname{log}_{2}(1+x),

which is strictly decreasing on the interval
x\in(0,1)
with
x:=|\langle\psi_{1}^{\lambda}|\psi_{2}^{\lambda}\rangle|\in[0,1)
.

Recall that the overlap between two states in
S^{\lambda}
is upper bounded by
1-\eta
by construction. Hence we have the following lower bound

H(AB)_{\psi^{\lambda}}[x]\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\eta\operatorname{log}_{2}\eta-\frac{1}{2}(2-\eta)\operatorname{log}_{2}(2-\eta)\equiv H^{*}(AB)[\eta].

Therefore we can further bound from above
m(\lambda)

m(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}}\left(m(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}H^{*}(AB)[\eta]+g_{2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right)\right),

where with the right choice of

Explicitly, it suffices to choose

H^{*}(AB)[\eta]>2\left(g_{2}\left(\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right)+m(\lambda)\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\right).

Corollary 7.13
.

Let the family of encoded states
\{k,\rho^{k}_{AB}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}

7.3.
LOCC Monotonicity

In this subsection, we analyze the monotonicity properties of computational entanglement measures under LOCC channels in the sense of Definition
7.5
.

Theorem 7.14
.

Let
\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a family of states and
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
– an efficient family of LOCC channels, the following holds

\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\})\leq n(\lambda)\implies\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\})\leq n(\lambda).

However, the one-shot computational cost is not LOCC monotonous under the generic families of LOCC maps.

Proof.

For efficient families of LOCC channels, consider the efficient family of LOCC maps given by
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\}
, and define the family of LOCC maps
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}:=\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\circ\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
, where
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is the family that achieves
\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\})\leq n(\lambda)
. The family
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
is efficient since
\Lambda^{\lambda}\circ\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}
admits efficient circuit representation for all values of
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

Using the family
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}
we obtain the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\quad p_{\text{err}}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=1-F(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=1-F(\Lambda^{\lambda}\circ\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\leq 1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})=:p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}),

where the last inequality is obtained by using the data-processing inequality from Proposition
3.7

F(\Lambda^{\lambda}\circ\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}))\geq F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}(\Phi^{\otimes n(\lambda)}),\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}).

From the assumptions where one needs
n(\lambda)
EPR pairs to recover the family
\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\}
, we get:

p_{\text{err}}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}))\leq p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\leq\varepsilon(\lambda),

therefore by Definition
4.9
we have

\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\leq n(\lambda)\implies\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\})\leq n(\lambda).

The result for the non-efficient families of LOCC follows as a simple corollary of Theorem
7.10
.

∎

Corollary 7.15
.

Let the family of encoded states be
\{k,\rho^{k}_{AB}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
and
\{k,\Lambda^{k}\}_{k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa(\lambda)}}
– an efficient family of encoded LOCC channels.
Then,

\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{C}(\{k,\rho^{k}_{AB}\})\leq n(\lambda)\implies\hat{E}_{C}^{\varepsilon}(\{k,\Lambda^{k}(\rho^{k}_{AB})\})\leq n(\lambda).

However, the same relation does not hold for non-efficient families of encoded LOCC channels.

We conclude this section by discussing LOCC monotonicity property for the computational distillable entanglement.

Theorem 7.16
.

Let
\{\rho_{AB}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a family of bipartite states and
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
– an efficient family of LOCC channels. Then,

\hat{E}_{D}^{\varepsilon}(\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\})\geq m(\lambda)\implies\hat{E}^{\varepsilon}_{D}(\{\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}\})\geq m(\lambda).

However, if
\varepsilon:\mathbb{N}_{+}\rightarrow[0,1)
is such that
m(\lambda)\sqrt{\varepsilon(\lambda)}\rightarrow 0,\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
, this does not hold for generic families of LOCC channels.

Proof.

For the efficient family of LOCC channels, we use arguments similar to the ones used in Theorem
7.14
. Let
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be an efficient family of LOCC maps and assume we can distill
m(\lambda)
EPR pairs from
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\}
, it is obvious for
\varepsilon(\lambda)\in[0,1]
that

\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\quad p_{\text{err}}(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}_{AB}))=1-F(\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\circ\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda}),\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)})=:p_{\text{err}}(\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda},\rho^{\lambda}_{AB})\leq\varepsilon(\lambda).

Therefore, one can distill
m(\lambda)
EPR pairs from
\{\rho^{\lambda}\}
with the efficient family of LOCC maps
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\}:=\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\lambda}\circ\Lambda^{\lambda}\}
, hence the proof of the implication.

For the non-efficient family of LOCC, we construct a counterexample.
Consider states
\rho^{\lambda}\in S^{\lambda}
and the LOCC maps
\Lambda^{\lambda}
of the form

\rho^{\lambda}:=I\otimes U\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}I\otimes U^{\dagger},\quad\Lambda^{\lambda}(\cdot):=I\otimes U^{\dagger}(\cdot)I\otimes U,

for any
U\in\mathcal{U}^{\lambda}
and for every value of
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
One can distill
\{\Lambda^{\lambda}(\rho^{\lambda})\}=\{\Phi^{\otimes m(\lambda)}\}
with the use of the trivial LOCC
\{\Gamma^{\lambda}\}=\{\mathbb{I}^{\lambda}\}
, implying that also the family of states
\{\rho^{\lambda}\}
is distillable to
m(\lambda)
EPR pairs with precision
\varepsilon(\lambda)
. This contradicts Theorem
7.12
, hence the contradiction.

∎

8.
Conclusion

Here, we studied the mathematical structure of the computational entanglement measures introduced in
[
AFBV23
]
. To this end, we developed a general framework to analyze entanglement measures given by function lower or upper bounds. This required extending notions such as convexity/concavity and superadditivity/subadditivity to their asymmetric counterparts: lower bound convexity and superadditivity, and upper bound concavity and subadditivity. We also formulated suitable extensions of local unitary invariance and LOCC monotonicity in this setting.

Applying this framework, we showed that both the computational distillable entanglement and the entanglement cost are lower bound convex and superadditive, and upper bound concave and subadditive, in the one-shot regime. These properties also extend to the uniform setting. Furthermore, we established that these computational measures are generally not invariant under arbitrary families of local unitaries, but are invariant under efficient families. Similarly, LOCC monotonicity holds only when restricted to efficient families of LOCC channels.

We believe that these properties may be useful in different areas of quantum information theory where computational constraints are relevant. As we have shown in Section
7
, the computational entanglement measures are LOCC monotones, in the sense of Definition
7.6
, and remain invariant only under efficient families of local unitaries.

Several natural questions remain. In particular, it would be interesting to investigate other properties such as monogamy. Another direction is to generalize from the one-shot to the
k
-shot regime, in the spirit of the pure-state framework introduced in
[
LREJ25
]
, and to characterize the properties of such measures.

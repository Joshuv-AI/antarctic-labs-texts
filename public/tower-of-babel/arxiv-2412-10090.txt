Codegree and regularity of stable set polytopes
Koji Matsushita, Akiyoshi Tsuchiya

Abstract
. The codegree codeg ⁡ ( 𝒫 ) {\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}) of a lattice polytope 𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} is a fundamental invariant in discrete geometry. In the present paper, we investigate the codegree of the stable set polytope 𝒫 G {\mathcal{P}}_{G} associated with a simple graph G G . Specifically, we establish the inequalities ω ⁡ ( G ) + 1 ≤ codeg ⁡ ( 𝒫 G ) ≤ χ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 , \omega(G)+1\leq{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\leq\chi(G)+1, where ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) and χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) denote the clique number and the chromatic number of G G , respectively. Furthermore, an explicit formula for codeg ⁡ ( 𝒫 G ) {\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G}) is given when G G is either a line graph or an h h -perfect graph. Finally, as an application of these results, we provide upper and lower bounds on the regularity of the toric ring associated with 𝒫 G {\mathcal{P}}_{G} .

1.
Introduction

A
lattice polytope

is a convex polytope all of whose vertices have integer coordinates.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope. Then the
codegree
of
{\mathcal{P}}
, denoted by
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}})
, is the smallest positive integer
k
such that the
k
th dilated polytope
k{\mathcal{P}}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
has an interior lattice point.
On the other hand, the
degree
of
{\mathcal{P}}
, denoted by
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
, is defined by
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})=n+1-{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}})
.
Then
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
coincides with the degree of the
h^{*}
-polynomial of
{\mathcal{P}}
, which encodes the number of lattice points in the dilatations of
{\mathcal{P}}
.
The codegree and the degree are fundamental invariants of lattice polytopes, playing significant roles in various fields, particularly in discrete geometry and commutative algebra.
See, e.g.,
[
1
]
for detailed information for the degree and codegree of lattice polytopes.

Throughout the present paper, we only treat simple graphs, which we simply call graphs instead of simple graphs.
The
stable set polytope

{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
associated with a graph
G
is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of all stable sets of
G
, where a set of vertices of
G
is called
stable
if no two of its elements are adjacent.
In the present paper, we investigate the codegree of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
.
The following is the first main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Then one has

\omega(G)+1\leq{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\leq\chi(G)+1,

where
\omega(G)
and
\chi(G)
denote the clique number and the chromatic number of G, respectively.
In particular, if
G
is perfect, then we obtain

\omega(G)+1={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\chi(G)+1.

It is well known that
\omega(G)\leq\chi(G)
for any graph
G
. From the above theorem, we can regard
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})-1
as an invariant lying between these two important invariants in graph theory. Furthermore, it is an important fact that there exists a graph
G
satisfying
\omega(G)<{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})-1<\chi(G)
(see Example
5.1
).

Also, Theorem
1.1
implies that
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
can be computed by using invariants of the underlying graph
G
when
G
is a perfect graph.
Next, for several classes of graphs, we give an explicit formula of
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
in terms of
G
.
The
matching polytope

{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
of
G
is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of all matchings of
G
, where a set of edges of
G
is called a
matching
if no two of its elements share a common endpoint.
We can regard
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
as the stable set polytope of the line graph of
G
. Namely,
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}={\mathcal{P}}_{L(G)}
, where
L(G)
denotes the line graph of
G
. Then an explicit formula for
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})
is given as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with connected components
G_{1},\ldots,G_{r}
.
Then one has

{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\begin{cases}\Delta(G)+2&\left(\mbox{if $\Delta(G)$ is even and each $G_{i}$ with $\Delta(G_{i})=\Delta(G)$ is a complete graph}\right),\\
\Delta(G)+1&(\mbox{otherwise}),\end{cases}

where

\Delta(G)
denotes the maximal degree of
G
.

Note that
\omega(L(G))=\Delta(G)
unless
G
is a triangle, and if
G
is a complete graph with an odd number of vertices
\geq 5
, then one has

\Delta(G)+1=\omega(L(G))+1<{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{L(G)})=\chi(L(G))+1=\Delta(G)+2.

A graph is said to be
h
-perfect
if
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is defined by the constraints corresponding to cliques and odd holes, and the nonnegativity constraints. Note that every perfect graph is
h
-perfect.
Then for an
h
-perfect graph
G
, an explicit formula for
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
is given as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be an
h
-perfect graph.
Then one has

\displaystyle{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

\displaystyle=\omega(G)+1.

Note that if
G
is an odd cycle of length
\geq 5
, then
G
is
h
-perfect and one has

3=\omega(G)+1={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})<\chi(G)+1=4.

Finally, we discuss the regularity of the toric ring of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
.
In combinatorial commutative algebra, finding methods to evaluate algebraic invariants using combinatorial invariants is an important problem. For example, in
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
, the regularity of edge ideals is evaluated using invariants of the underlying graphs. Similarly, in this paper, we establish upper and lower bounds for the regularity of the toric rings of stable set polytopes in terms of invariants of the underlying graphs.
For a lattice polytope
{\mathcal{P}}
, let
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
be the toric ring associated with
{\mathcal{P}}
over a field
K
and
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}])
the regularity of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices.
Then one has

n-\chi(G)\leq{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]).

Moreover, if
K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]
is normal, then we obtain

{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])\leq n-\omega(G).

In particular, if
G
is perfect, then one has

n-\chi(G)={\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])=n-\omega(G).

The present paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, after recalling the definitions and properties of stable set polytopes and perfect graphs, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
. Section
3
gives explicit formulas for
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
in the cases where
G
is a line graph or an
h
-perfect graph. In particular, Section
3.1
proves Theorem
1.2
, and Section
3.2
proves Theorem
1.3
. Section
4
discusses the regularity of
K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]
, with a focus on proving Theorem
1.4
. Finally, Section
5
considers examples related to the inequalities in Theorem
1.1
.

2.
bounds on the codegree of stable set polytopes

In this section, a proof of Theorem
1.1
is given.
First, we recall the definition of stable set polytopes.
Let
G
be a graph on
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with edge set
E(G)
.
A subset
S\subset[n]
is called a
stable set
(or an
independent set
) of
G
if
\{i,j\}\notin E(G)
for all
i,j\in S
with
i\neq j
. In particular, the empty set
\emptyset
and any singleton
\{i\}
with
i\in[n]
are stable sets of
G
.
Let
S(G)
be the set of all stable sets of
G
. For a subset
A\subset[n]
, we denote
{\mathbf{e}}_{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the indicator vector of
A
, namely,
{\mathbf{e}}_{A}=\sum_{i\in A}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
, where
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
is the
i
th unit coordinate vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Remark that
{\mathbf{e}}_{\emptyset}=\mathbf{0}:=(0,\ldots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then the
stable set polytope
of
G
is defined as

{\mathcal{P}}_{G}=\operatorname{conv}\{{\mathbf{e}}_{S}:S\in S(G)\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Note that
\dim{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is equal to the number of vertices of
G
.

Next, we define a perfect graph. A subset
C\subset[n]
is said to be
clique
of
G
if for any
i,j\in C
with
i\neq j
,
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. We denote
\omega(G)
the maximal cardinality of cliques of
G
, which is called the
clique number
of
G
.
A map
f:[n]\to[k]
with a positive integer
k
is said to be a
coloring
of
G
, if for any
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
,
f(i)\neq f(j)
.
The minimal integer
k
such that there exists a coloring
f:[n]\to[k]
of
G
is called a
chromatic number
of
G
.
In general, one has

\omega(G)\leq\chi(G).

However, the equality does not always hold (e.g., an odd cycle of length
\geq 5
).
A graph
G
is called
perfect
if for any induced subgraph
H
of
G
,
\omega(H)=\chi(H)
.
Perfect graphs were introduced by Berge in
[
2
]
. A
hole
is an induced cycle of length
\geq 5
and an
antihole
is the complement graph of a hole. In 2006, Chudnovsky, Robertson, Seymour, and Thomas solved a famous conjecture in graph theory, which was conjectured by Berge and is now known as the strong perfect graph theorem.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

A graph is perfect if and only if it has no odd holes and no odd antiholes.

Also, there is a polyhedral characterization of perfect graphs.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
. Then
G
is perfect if and only if

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{G}=\left\{{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{rlc}x_{i}&\geq 0,&\forall i\in[n]\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in Q}x_{i}&\leq 1,&\text{ for any maximal clique $Q$}\end{array}\right\}.

Note that for a (not necessarily perfect) graph
G
, each inequality appearing in (
2.2
) defines a facet of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
(
[
16
]
).

Before proving Theorem
1.1
, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
.
For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, one has
{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})
. In particular, one has

{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\min(k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})).

Proof.

Since subsets of stable sets are also stable, for any inequality
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\leq b
defining a facet of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
, one has

(i)

b=0
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
for some
i\in[n]
(called a
trivial facet
), or

(ii)

b>0
and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.

Suppose that there is a lattice point
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
in
{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})
.
To see that
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})
, it is enough to show that
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}
satisfies any inequality of the form (ii) strictly, that is,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}<kb
.

Since
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})
, one has
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{n}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}c_{i}<kb
.
Therefore, we get
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}c_{i}<kb
, as desired.

The converse is trivial.
∎

Next, we prove the lower bound of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
.
Then one has
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\geq\omega(G)+1
. Moreover, one has
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\omega(G)+1
if
G
is perfect.

Proof.

For a clique
Q
of
G
with
|Q|=\omega(G)
, the inequality
\sum_{i\in Q}x_{i}\leq 1
defines a facet of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
(
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
).
Thus, if
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{P}}_{G})
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, then
k
must be greater than
\omega(G)
, which implies that
\omega(G)+1\leq{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
from Lemma
2.3
.

Suppose that
G
is perfect. Then any non-trivial facet of
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is defined by the inequality
\sum_{i\in Q}x_{i}\leq 1
for some maximal clique of
G
(Proposition
2.2
).
Thus, we have
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\omega(G)+1
.
∎

Note that, for a perfect graph
G
, the equality
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\omega(G)+1
is also shown in the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 2.1 (b)]
.

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we recall the notion of the join of graphs.
Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs on disjoint vertex sets.
The
join
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

by adding an edge between every vertex of
G_{1}
and every vertex of
G_{2}
.
The join of more than two graphs is defined analogously by repeatedly applying this construction.

Now, we turn to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We have already shown that
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\geq\omega(G)+1
in Proposition
2.4
, so we prove that
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\leq\chi(G)+1
.
By Lemma
2.3
, it is enough to show that
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}((\chi(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G})
.

We take a proper coloring
f:[n]\to[\chi(G)]
of
G
.
By the minimality of
\chi(G)
, we have
f^{-1}(i)\neq\emptyset
for any
1\leq i\leq\chi(G)
.
For any
1\leq i\leq\chi(G)
, let
G_{i}
be the graph on
f^{-1}(i)
with no edges, and let
G^{\prime}
be the join of
G_{1},\ldots,G_{\chi(G)}
.
Then since
G^{\prime}
is a complete multipartite graph,
G^{\prime}
is a perfect graph on
[n]
with
\chi(G^{\prime})=\chi(G)
.
Hence
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G^{\prime}})=\omega(G^{\prime})+1=\chi(G^{\prime})+1=\chi(G)+1
from Proposition
2.4
.
By Lemma
2.3
, this implies that
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}((\chi(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G^{\prime}}
.
On the other hand, for each
i\in[\chi(G)]
,
f^{-1}(i)
is a stable set of
G
. Moreover, the set
\{f^{-1}(1),\ldots,f^{-1}(\chi(G))\}
coincides with the set of all maximal stable sets of
G^{\prime}
.
Hence
{\mathcal{P}}_{G^{\prime}}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
. It then follows from
\dim{\mathcal{P}}_{G}=\dim{\mathcal{P}}_{G^{\prime}}
that
{\rm int}((\chi(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G^{\prime}}\subset{\rm int}((\chi(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G}
. In particular, one has
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}((\chi(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G}
.
∎

3.
Explicit formula for the codegree of a stable set polytope

In this section, for several classes of graphs, we give an explicit formula of
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
in terms of
G
.
In particular, proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
are given.

3.1.
Matching polytopes

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
with edge set
E(G)
.
A
matching

M
of a graph
G
is a subset of
E(G)
such that every vertex of
G
is incident to at most one edge in
M
.
The
matching polytope
of
G
is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of the matchings on
G
, that is,

{\mathcal{M}}_{G}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{{\mathbf{e}}_{M}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E(G)}:M\subset E(G)\text{ is a matching of }G\right\}.

The matching polytope
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
is a stable set polytope of some graph.
Indeed, the
line graph

L(G)
of
G
is a graph whose vertex set is
E(G)
and whose edge set is

\{\{e,e^{\prime}\}\subset\ E(G):e\neq e^{\prime}\mbox{ and }e\cap e^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}.

Then one has
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}={\mathcal{P}}_{L(G)}
by changing coordinates.

The matching polytope has the following description:

Proposition 3.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
with edge set
E(G)
. Then one has

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{G}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{E(G)}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{rlc}x(e)&\geq 0,&\forall e\in E(G)\\
\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\iota_{G}(v)}x(e)&\leq 1,&\forall v\in[n]\\
\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(G_{U})}x(e)&\leq\frac{|U|-1}{2},&\forall U\subset[n]:\text{$|U|$ is odd}\end{array}\right\},

where
\iota_{G}(v)
and
G_{U}
denote the set of all edges incident to a vertex
v
in
G
and the induced subgraph of
G
with vertex set
U
, respectively.

Now, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}={\mathcal{M}}_{G_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{M}}_{G_{r}}
, one has

{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\max\{{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G_{1}}),\ldots,{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G_{r}})\}

by Lemma
2.3
, where
G_{1},\ldots,G_{r}
are the connected components of
G
.
Thus, we may assume that
G
is connected and it suffices to prove that
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\Delta(G)+2
if
G
is a complete graph
K_{m}
with
m
vertices, where
m
is odd, otherwise
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\Delta(G)+1
.

By Proposition
3.1
, if
{\mathbf{e}}_{E(G)}\in{\rm int}(k{\mathcal{M}}_{G})
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, then the inequalities
|\iota_{G}(v)|<k
and
|E(G_{U})|<k\frac{|U|-1}{2}
hold for any
v\in G
and
U\subset[n]
such that
|U|
is odd.
In particular,
k
must be greater than
\Delta(G)
for the first inequality to hold.

On the other hand, it follows from the handshaking lemma and
|U|-\Delta(G_{U})-1\geq 0
that

|E(G_{U})|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}|\iota_{G_{U}}(v)|\underset{(a)}{\leq}\frac{\Delta(G_{U})\cdot|U|}{2}\underset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{(\Delta(G_{U})+1)\cdot(|U|-1)}{2}\underset{(c)}{\leq}(\Delta(G)+1)\frac{|U|-1}{2}.

The equalities of (a) and (b) hold if and only if
G_{U}=K_{|U|}
.
In addition, the equality of (c) holds as well, that is, we have
|E(G_{U})|=(\Delta(G)+1)\frac{|U|-1}{2}
if and only if
G_{U}=K_{|U|}=G
since
G
is connected.
Therefore, if
G=K_{m}
for some odd integer
m
, then
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\Delta(G)+2
, otherwise
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{M}}_{G})=\Delta(G)+1
.
∎

3.2.
The stable set polytopes of
h
-perfect graphs

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
. We say that
G
is
h
-perfect

[
17
]
if the stable set polytope has the following description:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{G}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{rlc}x_{i}&\geq 0,&\forall i\in[n]\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in Q}x_{i}&\leq 1,&\text{ for any clique $Q$}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i\in C}x_{i}&\leq\frac{|C|-1}{2},&\text{ for any odd cycle $C$}\end{array}\right\}.

From Proposition
2.2
every perfect graph is
h
-perfect.
However, an
h
-perfect graph is not necessarily perfect. For example, it is known that every odd cycle of length
\geq 5
is
h
-perfect, but not perfect.

Now, we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If
\omega(G)=1
, that is,
G
has no edges, then our assertion clearly holds, thus we may assume that
\omega(G)\geq 2
.

By Proposition
2.4
, we have
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\geq\omega(G)+1
, so it suffices to show that
{\mathbf{e}}_{[n]}\in{\rm int}((\omega(G)+1){\mathcal{P}}_{G})
.
To this end, we show that
|C|<(\omega(G)+1)\frac{|C|-1}{2}
, equivalently
2<(\omega(G)-1)(|C|-1)
for any odd cycle
C
of
G
.
This is true if
|C|\geq 5
, and even if
|C|=3
,
\omega(G)\geq 3
in this case, so it is true either way.
∎

4.
bounds on the regularities of the toric ring associated with
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
with each
{\rm deg}(x_{i})=1
. Let
0\neq I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal of
S
and

0\to\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}S(-j)^{\beta_{h,j}}\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{j\geq 1}S(-j)^{\beta_{1,j}}\to S\to S/I\to 0

a (unique) graded minimal free
S
-resolution of
S/I
. The (
Castelnuovo-Mumford
)
regularity
of
S/I
is defined by

{\rm reg}(S/I)=\max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}\neq 0\}.

See, e.g.,
[
4
]
for detailed information about regularity.

In this section, we discuss the regularity of the toric rings associated with stable set polytopes.
In particular, a proof of Theorem
1.4
is given.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a full-dimensional lattice polytope with
{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
, and
let
K[{\mathbf{t}},s]:=K[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},s]
be the polynomial ring in
n+1
variables over a field
K
.
Given a nonnegative integer vector

{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d}
, we write

{\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{a}}}:=t_{1}^{a_{1}}t_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots t_{n}^{a_{n}}\in K[{\mathbf{t}},s]
.
The
toric ring
of
{\mathcal{P}}
is

K[{\mathcal{P}}]:=K[{\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}s,\ldots,{\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}s]\subset K[{\mathbf{t}},s].

We regard
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
as a homogeneous algebra by setting each
{\rm deg}({\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}s)=1
.
Let
R[{\mathcal{P}}]=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
denote the polynomial ring in
d
variables over
K
with each
{\rm deg}(x_{i})=1
.
The
toric ideal
of
{\mathcal{P}}
is the kernel of the surjective homomorphism

\pi:R[{\mathcal{P}}]\to K[{\mathcal{P}}]
defined by
\pi(x_{i})={\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}s
for
1\leq i\leq d
.
Note that
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is a prime ideal generated by homogeneous binomials.
See, e.g.,
[
10
]
for details on toric rings and toric ideals.

We recall some properties for
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
.
A full-dimensional lattice polytope
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called
spanning
if every lattice point in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is affine integer combination of the lattice points in
{\mathcal{P}}
.
Moreover, we say that
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
has the
integer decomposition property
if for every integer
k\geq 1
, every lattice point in
k{\mathcal{P}}
is a sum of
k
lattice points from
{\mathcal{P}}
.
A lattice polytope which has the integer decomposition property is called
IDP
.
It is easy to see that an IDP polytope is spanning.
Furthermore, a spanning polytope
{\mathcal{P}}
is IDP if and only if the toric ring
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is normal. In this case,
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is Cohen–Macaulay.

The regularity
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}]):={\rm deg}(R[{\mathcal{P}}]/I_{{\mathcal{P}}})
of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
does not have a direct combinatorial interpretation. However, if
{\mathcal{P}}
is spanning, then
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
gives a lower bound on
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}])
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
11
, p. 5952]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a spanning lattice polytope.
Then one has
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}])\geq{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
.

On the other hand, it is known that if
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is Cohen–Macaulay, then
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}])
is equal to the degree of the
h
-polynomial of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
(
[
10
, Corollary 2.18]
).
Moreover, if
{\mathcal{P}}
is IDP, then
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
coincides with the degree of the
h
-polynomial of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
(
[
10
, Theorem 4.5 and Lemma 4.22 (b)]
). Hence we know the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an IDP lattice polytope.
Then one has
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}])={\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}})
.

Now, we prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The origin
{\bf 0}
and each unit coordinate vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
are vertices of the stable set polytope
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
. This implies that
{\mathcal{P}}
is spanning.
Since
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=n+1-{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})
, it then follows from Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
4.1

that

n+1-\chi(G)-1=n-\chi(G)\leq{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\leq{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]).

On the other hand, if
{\mathcal{P}}
is IDP, then from Theorem
1.1

and Proposition
4.2
one has

{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])={\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=n+1-{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})\leq n+1-\omega(G)-1=n-\omega(G).

If
G
is perfect, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is compressed, in particular, it is IDP (
[
14
]
).
Hence we obtain

n-\chi(G)={\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])=n-\omega(G),

as desired.
∎

In general, stable set polytope are not always IDP. For example, let
G
be the graph whose complement graph is the disjoint union of two
5
-cycles. Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is not IDP
[
13
, Proposition 8]
, in particular,
K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]
is not normal. Hence we cannot apply Theorem
1.4
to obtain an upper bound on
K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]
. However, in this case, by using Macaulay2
[
8
]
, one has
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])=6
. Hence we obtain
{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])=10-\omega(G)
.
Therefore, the following question naturally occurs.

Question 4.3
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
. Then does the following inequality hold?

{\rm reg}(K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}])\leq n-\omega(G).

5.
Examples related with Theorem
1.1

We return the inequalities in Theorem
1.1
. Then there are the following 4 cases:

(i)

\omega(G)+1={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\chi(G)+1
;

(ii)

\omega(G)+1<{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=\chi(G)+1
;

(iii)

\omega(G)+1={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})<\chi(G)+1
;

(iv)

\omega(G)+1<{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})<\chi(G)+1
.

For each case, is there a graph
G
satisfying the condition?
We can give an example for each case.

Example 5.1
.

(1) Every perfect graph satisfies the condition (i).

(2) Let
n\geq 5
be an odd integer and set
G=L(K_{n})
.Then one has
\omega(G)=n-1,\chi(G)=n
and
\Delta(G)=n-1
. Hence it follows from Theorem
1.2
that
G
satisfies the condition (ii).

(3) Let
n\geq 5
be an odd integer and let
G=C_{n}
be an odd cycle of length
n
.
Then one has
\omega(G)=2
and
\chi(G)=3
. Since it is known that
C_{n}
is
h
-perfect, from Theorem
1.3

G
satisfies the condition (iii).

(4) Assume that
C_{5}
and
L(K_{5})
have no common vertices and let
G
be the join of
C_{5}
and
L(K_{5})
.
Then one has
\omega(G)=\omega(C_{5})+\omega(L(K_{5}))=2+4=6
and
\chi(G)=\chi(C_{5})+\chi(L(K_{5}))=3+5=8
. Moreover, by using Normaliz
[
3
]
, we obtain
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=8
. In particular,
(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{15}
is an interior lattice point in
8{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
from Lemma
2.3
.
Hence
G
satisfies the condition (iv).

As a preliminary question, one may ask whether the differences

{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})-(\omega(G)+1)
and
(\chi(G)+1)-{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

can be made arbitrarily large, either simultaneously or individually while keeping the other bounded or zero.
More general, we can consider the following problem.

Problem 5.2
.

Let
a,b,c
be integers satisfying

0<a\leq b\leq c.

Determine when there exists a graph
G
satisfying

\displaystyle\omega(G)+1=a,\ \ \ \ \ {\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=b,\ \ \ \ \ \chi(G)+1=c.

In other words,
determine all the possible sequences

(\omega(G)+1,{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G}),\chi(G)+1).

Note that the case
a=b=c
in this problem can be solved by considering perfect graphs.
In investigating this problem, it seems useful to consider the join of graphs.
Indeed, for graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
and their join
G
, one has

\omega(G)=\omega(G_{1})+\omega(G_{2}),\chi(G)=\chi(G_{1})+\chi(G_{2}).

Moreover, in Example
5.1
(4), we have

\displaystyle{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

\displaystyle=8=3+5={\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})+{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{L(K_{5})}),

\displaystyle{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

\displaystyle=3+6-1={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})+{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{L(K_{5})})-1.

However, in general these equalities for the degree and the codegree do not hold.
We present an example where these equalities fail.

Example 5.3
.

Let
G
be the join of two
5
-cycles.
Then one has
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=5
and
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})=6
.
In particular, this example also satisfies the condition (iv) since
\omega(G)=4
and
\chi(G)=6
.
On the other hand,
{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})=3
and
{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})=3
. Hence we know that

\displaystyle{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})+{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})

\displaystyle=6\neq 5={\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G}),

\displaystyle{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})+{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{C_{5}})-1

\displaystyle=5\neq 6={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G}).

Thus, for graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
and their join
G
, it is natural to ask
when the equalities

\displaystyle{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

\displaystyle={\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G_{1}})+{\rm deg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G_{2}}),

\displaystyle{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G})

\displaystyle={\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G_{1}})+{\rm codeg}({\mathcal{P}}_{G_{2}})-1

hold.
A complete characterization of such pairs
(G_{1},G_{2})
seems to be an interesting problem.

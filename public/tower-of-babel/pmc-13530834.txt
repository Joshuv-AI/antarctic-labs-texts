Poster 113. Stressors and Achilles Tendon Rupture Risk in American Professional Basketball
Authors: Nate Kattady, Rohith Ryali, Mani Eftekhari, Matteo L Burnett, Gregory M Waryasz, Eric M Berkson, Soheil Ashkani-Esfahani, Gergo L Merkely
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Achilles tendon ruptures (ATRs) in American professional basketball have risen markedly and peaked with seven cases during the 2025 season, far higher than the average of 1.3 ATRs per season over the previous three decades. Despite advancements in recovery, ATRs remain career-altering, with one of the lowest return-to-play rates and steepest declines in performance among major orthopedic injuries. Recent trends suggest increases in maximum vertical leap heights and league-wide three-point attempts per game, both of which may contribute to in-game ankle load. Furthermore, there has been limited research exploring the role of external motivators, which may increase injury risk by driving players to push beyond their typical physical limits. In this study, we aim to assess whether in-game ankle load and player incentives contribute to ATR risk.Methods:We retrospectively identified 20 American professional basketball players who sustained full ATRs between 2014 and 2025 using publicly available injury reports. Injured players were matched 1:2 with controls (n=40) by age, position, height, and role (all-star, starter, or bench player). Controls were selected from players who did not sustain any significant injuries in the corresponding season. Player demographics and contextual factors pertaining to fatigue, incentives, and recent lower-extremity injury were collected. Advanced load-related statistics, including rebounding, speed/distance, and effort-based metrics, were measured for the 30 games preceding the ATR. League-wide three-point attempts per game and average maximum vertical leap heights at the draft combine were also collected annually for the timeframe of the study. Univariate analyses were conducted as appropriate, and variables with a P-value less than 0.20 on univariate tests were entered into a multivariable logistic regression (LR) model used to assess associations between demographic factors, in-game mechanical load, and situational context. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05.Results:In the univariate analyses, players who had an ATR were more likely to have been playing on an expiring contract compared to controls (55% vs. 23%, P=0.01). No other significant differences were observed in the univariate analysis between the ATR and control groups even after stratifying for minutes played in the season. In the multivariate LR model, only playing on an expiring contract (OR=5.93, 95% CI 1.40-25.17, P=0.02) was independently associated with increased odds of ATR, with an area under the receiver operating curve of 0.71.Conclusions:Contract year status was independently associated with increased odds of ATR. This suggests that psychological and incentive-driven factors, such as heightened motivation to perform during an expiring contract year, may elevate physical stress and exertion, raising injury risk. Validating our findings through biomechanical video analysis may allow for the tracking and quantification of ankle load, thus providing insights into the targeted load management of athletes at high risk of ATR.

General M-estimators of location on Riemannian manifolds: existence and uniqueness
Jongmin Lee, Sungkyu Jung

Abstract
We study general M M -estimators of location on Riemannian manifolds, extending classical notions such as the Fréchet mean by replacing the squared loss with a broad class of loss functions. Under minimal regularity conditions on the loss function and the underlying probability distribution, we establish theoretical guarantees for the existence and uniqueness of such estimators. In particular, we provide sufficient conditions under which the population and sample M M -estimators exist and are uniquely defined. Our results offer a general framework for robust location estimation in non-Euclidean geometric spaces and unify prior uniqueness results under a broad class of convex losses.

Introduction

In statistical data analysis, locating the mean of a dataset plays a fundamental role, with applications in data summarization, hypothesis testing, confidence region construction, and analysis of variance.
Emerging developments in non-Euclidean data analysis have led to the extension of the classical notion of the Fréchet mean via
M
-estimators, enabling it to be a robust measure of central tendency in geometric spaces (see, e.g.,
Lee and Jung 2024
).

Replacing the
L_{2}
-loss function used in the Fréchet mean by a generic loss function
\rho(\cdot)
, we define the general
M
-estimators of location as follows.

Definition 1
.

The population
M
-estimator set with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is

E:=\text{argmin}_{m\in M}F(m),\penalty\ F(m)=\int\rho\{d(x,m)\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x).

For a given
n
-tuple of observations
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
, the sample
M
-estimator set is given by

E_{n}:=\text{argmin}_{m\in M}F_{n}(m),\penalty\ F_{n}(m)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho\{d(x_{i},m)\}.

Theoretical properties of
M
-estimators on abstract metric spaces, such as consistency and asymptotic normality, have been the subject of active research in recent years
(
Huckemann 2011
;
Ginestet, Simmons and Kolaczyk 2012
;
Sinova, González-Rodríguez and Van Aelst 2018
;
Schötz 2019
;
Brunel 2023
;
Park and Jung 2023
;
Ghosal, Meiring and Petersen 2023
;
Evans and Jaffe 2024
)
. However, most of these studies rely on the assumption that the estimators exist and are unique. To address this gap, this article establishes theoretical guarantees for the existence and uniqueness of general
M
-estimators for location, with a particular focus on Riemannian manifolds.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the article, we assume that
M
is a connected, complete, and separable
k
-dimensional smooth Riemannian manifold (equipped with a Riemannian metric) without boundary. The Riemannian metric naturally induces the distance
d:M\times M\to[0,\infty)
, enabling
(M,d)
to be a metric space. The least upper bound of sectional curvature of
M
is denoted by
\Delta
and assumed to be finite (i.e.,
\Delta<\infty
). For details on the Riemannian geometry, see
Chavel 2006
;
Do Carmo 1992
.

The following notations are used in the remainder of the article: For an
M
-valued random variable
X
defined on a probability space equipped with a probability
\mathbb{P}
, the pushforward measure of
\mathbb{P}
under
X
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{X}
; For a point
p\in M
and
r>0
, we denote
B_{r}(p):=\{x\in M:d(x,p)<r\}
; For an
n
-tuple of observations
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in M^{n}
, we denote the empirical measure
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}}
by
\mathbb{P}_{n}
, where
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac probability measure concentrated at
x
.

3
Main results

We require a minimal condition on
\rho
in its role as a generic loss function.

(C1)

\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is non-decreasing, continuous,
\rho(0)=0
, but
\rho\not\equiv 0
.

Examples of loss functions satisfying Condition (C1) include the Huber loss
\rho(t)=t^{2}\penalty\ \mbox{if}\penalty\ t\leq c,\penalty\ c(2t-c)\penalty\ \mbox{if}\penalty\ t>c
, Tukey’s biweight loss
\rho(t)=\frac{c^{2}}{6}\{1-(1-(\frac{t}{c})^{2})^{3}1_{t\leq c}\}
, Welsch’s loss
\rho(t)=1-\exp(-(\frac{t}{c})^{2})
, Andrew’s sine loss
\rho(t)=c^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{t}{c}))\penalty\ \mbox{if}\penalty\ t\leq\pi c,\penalty\ 2c^{2}\penalty\ \mbox{if}\penalty\ t>\pi c
for some cutoff
c>0
, logarithm of the hyperbolic cosine loss
\rho(t)=\log\{\cosh(t)\}
, and so many others (see, e.g., Table C.5 of
De Menezes et al. 2021
).

To guarantee the existence of
M
-estimators, an integrability condition is assumed:

(A1)
For any
m\in M
,
F(m)=\int\rho\{d(x,m)\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)<\infty
.

To ensure the existence of
M
-estimators, we begin with verifying a
coercive
property of the objective function
F
with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, meaning that
F
attains a minimum in a compact set.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that the loss function
\rho
satisfies Condition (C1), and that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfies Assumption (A1). Then, there exists a compact set
K\subseteq M
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{m\in K}F(m)=\inf_{m\in M}F(m),\penalty\ \mbox{and}

\displaystyle\mbox{if}\penalty\ K\subsetneq M,\penalty\ \inf_{m\in K}F(m)<\inf_{m\in M\setminus K}F(m)

Accordingly,
E\subset K
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

The proof is divided into two cases depending on whether
M
is bounded or not.

Case I (
M
is bounded).
Put
K=M
. Note that
M
is connected and complete. Since
K
is closed and bounded, an application of the Hopf-Rinow theorem implies that
K
is compact and
\inf_{m\in K}F(m)=\inf_{m\in M}F(m)
.

Case II (
M
is unbounded).
We shall show that
\{F(m):m\in M\}
is not singleton. For this, suppose for contradiction there exists
C\in[0,\infty)
such that
F(m)=C
for any
m\in M
. Let
\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\rho(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)=:U\in(0,\infty]
and pick a point
p\in M
. Since
M
is unbounded, there exists an escaping geodesic ray
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M
starting at
p=\gamma(0)
(see, e.g.,
(
Do Carmo 1992
, Exercise 7.6)
). The term,
escaping geodesic
, means that
d(p,\gamma(t))\underset{t\to\infty}{\to}\infty
; that is, the escaping geodesic extends infinitely in one direction, and does not oscillate wildly.
An application of Fatou’s lemma leads that

\mathbb{E}[\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho\{d(X,\gamma(t))\}]\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\rho\{d(X,\gamma(t))\}}_{=C}\leq U.

(1)

Furthermore, by the triangle inequality, we have for any
x\in M
,

U\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho\{d(p,\gamma(t))-d(x,p)\}=U.

(2)

If
U=\infty
, then
\mathbb{E}[\liminf_{t\to\infty}\rho\{d(X,\gamma(t))\}]=\infty
, which contradicts to the hypothesis of finite
C
. Thus, if
U=\infty
, then
\{F(m):m\in M\}
is not singleton.

For the case
U<\infty
, (
1
) and (
2
) together lead that
F(m)=C=U\in(0,\infty)
. Owing to the continuity of
t\mapsto\rho(t)
at
t=0
with
\rho(0)=0
(inherent in Condition (C1)), there exists
\delta>0
such that
0\leq t<\delta
implies
\rho(t)<U/2
. Since
M
is separable, there is a countable dense subset of
M
,
\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots\}
. Note that
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{\delta}(p_{i})=M
and thus there exists at least one point
p_{\ell}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{X}(B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))=\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))>0
(since if not, it holds that
1=\mathbb{P}_{X}(M)=\mathbb{P}_{X}(\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{\delta}(p_{i}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}_{X}(B_{\delta}(p_{i}))=0
due to the countable sub-additivity of probability measure, which is a contradiction). Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle F(p_{\ell})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\rho\{d(X,p_{\ell})\}1_{X\notin B_{\delta}(p_{\ell})}]+\mathbb{E}[\rho\{d(X,p_{\ell})\}1_{X\in B_{\delta}(p_{\ell})}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle U\cdot\mathbb{P}(X\notin B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))+U/2\cdot\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U\{\mathbb{P}(X\notin B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))\}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle U\{\mathbb{P}(X\notin B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))+\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{\delta}(p_{\ell}))\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U,

which contradicts to the hypothesis that
F(m)=U
for any
m\in M
. Therefore,
\{F(m):m\in M\}
is not a singleton set.

The remainder of the proof is divided into two cases depending on whether
U
is infinite or not.

(i)
U=\infty
.
Because
\{F(m):m\in M\}
is not a singleton set (thus, it has at least two elements), there exists
m_{1}\in M
such that
F(m_{1})>\inf_{m\in M}F(m)\geq 0
. Because
M=\cup_{r\geq 0}B_{r}(m_{1})
and
\mathbb{P}(X\in M)=1
, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{r_{0}}(m_{1}))>0
. Now put
K_{0}=\overline{B_{r_{0}}(m_{1})}
.
Recall that
\rho
is non-decreasing as assumed in (C1) and satisfies
\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)=\infty
. Thus, for any finite
u
, there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that, for all
t\geq t_{0}
,
\rho(t)>u
.
Accordingly, there exists
r_{1}\penalty\ (>r_{0})
such that for any
r\geq r_{1}

\rho(r-r_{0})>\frac{\inf_{m\in K_{0}}F(m)}{\mathbb{P}(X\in K_{0})}.

We set
K:=\overline{B_{r_{1}}(m_{1})}
, which contains
K_{0}
and is compact by the Hopf-Rinow theorem.
For each
m\in M\setminus K
,

F(m)\geq\int_{K_{0}}\rho\{d(x,m)\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)\geq\rho(r_{1}-r_{0})\mathbb{P}(X\in K_{0})>\inf_{m\in K_{0}}F(m).

(3)

Here, the second inequality holds by
d(x,m)\geq d(m_{1},m)-d(m_{1},x)\geq r_{1}-r_{0}
for any
x\in K_{0}
. From (
3
), we get

\inf_{m\in M\setminus K}F(m)\geq\rho(r_{1}-r_{0})\mathbb{P}(X\in K_{0})>\inf_{m\in K_{0}}F(m)\geq\inf_{m\in K}F(m)=\inf_{m\in M}F(m),

thereby meaning that
E\subset K
.

(ii)
U<\infty
.

Since
\{F(m):m\in M\}
is not singleton, there is a point
m_{0}\in M
such that
F(m_{0})<U
. Note that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
r(\epsilon)>0
such that
r\geq r(\epsilon)
implies
\mathbb{P}(X\in B_{r}(m_{0}))\geq 1-\epsilon
and there also exists
t(\epsilon)>0
such that
t>t(\epsilon)
implies
\rho(t)\geq U-\epsilon
. For any
m\in M\setminus B_{r(\epsilon)+t(\epsilon)}(m_{0})
,

F(m)\geq\int_{B_{r(\epsilon)}(m_{0})}\rho\{d(x,m)\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)\geq(1-\epsilon)(U-\epsilon),

(4)

where the last inequality holds because for any
x\in B_{r(\epsilon)}(m_{0})
,
d(x,m)\geq d(m,m_{0})-d(x,m_{0})>r(\epsilon)+t(\epsilon)-r(\epsilon)=t(\epsilon)
; thus,
\rho\{d(x,m)\}\geq U-\epsilon
. Notably, the inequality (
4
) holds for any
\epsilon>0
. Since the quantity in (
4
),
(1-\epsilon)(U-\epsilon)
, can be arbitrarily close to

U
for infinitesimal
\epsilon
, and since
U>F(m_{0})
, we have,
for sufficiently small
\epsilon
,

U>(1-\epsilon)(U-\epsilon)>(U+F(m_{0}))/2>F(m_{0}).

According to this fact and (
4
), there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
m\in M\setminus B_{r(\epsilon_{0})+t(\epsilon_{0})}(m_{0})
,

F(m)\geq\frac{U+F(m_{0})}{2}>F(m_{0}).

Letting
K=\overline{B_{r(\epsilon_{0})+t(\epsilon_{0})}(m_{0})}
gives

\inf_{m\in M\setminus K}F(m)\geq\frac{U+F(m_{0})}{2}>F(m_{0})\geq\inf_{m\in K}F(m)=\inf_{m\in M}F(m).

Since
K
is closed and bounded, it is compact by the Hopf-Rinow theorem, and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 1

(Existence of population
M
-estimators)
.

Suppose that Condition (C1) holds and that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfies Assumption (A1). Then, the population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
exists; that is,
E\neq\phi
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For any
m\in M
and for any sequence of points
(m_{n})_{n\geq 1}
converging to
m
, an application of Fatou’s lemma leads that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}F(m_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\rho\{d(X,m_{n})\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho\{d(X,m_{n})\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\rho\{d(X,m)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(m),

where the penultimate equality holds by the continuity of
\rho
. Accordingly, the map
m\mapsto F(m)
is lower semi-continuous. Let
K
be as specified in Proposition
1
. The minimum of
F
is attained in the compact set
K
since
F
is lower semi-continuous, thereby implying that the population
M
-estimator set
E
is non-empty.
∎

By replacing
\mathbb{P}_{X}
with the empirical measure
\mathbb{P}_{n}
,
the existence of the sample
M
-estimator is established below.

Corollary 1

(Existence of the sample M-estimators)
.

Given an
n
-tuple of observations
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
, the sample
M
-estimator set exists; that is,
E_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n})
is non-empty.

An important question prompted by the existence of
M
-estimator concerns the conditions under which the estimator is unique. To ensure the uniqueness of
M
-estimator, we utilize either of the following convexity conditions on
\rho
.

(C2)

\rho^{\prime}(\cdot)
is positive and non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
. Moreover,
\rho^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)
is well-defined and is non-negative on
[0,\infty)\setminus S
, where
S
is a countable set.

(C3)
The right derivative at zero satisfies
\rho^{\prime}(0):=\lim_{t\to+0}\frac{\rho(t)-\rho(0)}{t}=0
. In addition,
\rho(\cdot)
is twice continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
, and
\rho^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)
is positive and non-decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.

Notably, Condition (C3) implies (C2). Examples of loss functions satisfying (C3) include the
L_{p}
-loss
\rho(t)=t^{p}
for any
p\in[2,\infty)
and softplus square loss
\rho(t)=\{\log(1+e^{t})\}^{2}\penalty\ (t\geq 0)
, which are
strictly
convex. While the absolute loss and the (pseudo-) Huber losses do not satisfy (C3), they are convex and satisfy (C2).

Recall that
\Delta
stands for the least upper bound of the sectional curvature of
M
. By convention, we regard
{1}/{\sqrt{\Delta}}
as
\infty
when
\Delta\leq 0
, and denote the injectivity radius of
M
by
r_{\mbox{\tiny inj}}(M)
. We assume the following on
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
, the support of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
.

(A2)
There exists
p_{0}\in M
such that

\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\subseteq B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
,
and

r_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\Delta}},r_{\mbox{\tiny inj}}(M)\}\quad\mbox{if $\rho$ satisfies (C2)},\\
\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\Delta}},r_{\mbox{\tiny inj}}(M)\}\quad\mbox{if $\rho$ satisfies (C3)}.\end{cases}

(5)

When
M
is Hadamard,
r_{0}
specified in (
5
) is infinite; thus, in this case, Assumption (A2) is not a restriction on
\mathbb{P}_{X}
at all.

The next technical lemma is necessary to verify the uniqueness of
M
-estimators.

Lemma 1
.

Under Condition (C3),
\rho^{\prime}
is strictly increasing. Moreover, for any
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\rho^{\prime}(t)\leq t\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)

Proof of Lemma
1
.

\rho^{\prime\prime}>0
implies that
\rho^{\prime}
is strictly increasing. The fundamental theorem of calculus implies that for any
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(t)=\rho^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\rho^{\prime\prime}(u)du\leq\int_{0}^{t}\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)du=t\rho^{\prime\prime}(t),

where above inequality holds since
\rho^{\prime\prime}
is non-decreasing.
∎

The following lemma is required to ensure that the population
M
-estimator set with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
lies in a strongly convex ball.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that Conditions (C1) and either (C2) or (C3) hold, and that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfies Assumptions (A1) and (A2). Then, there exist
p_{0}\in M
and a finite

(in fact, if

Proof of Lemma
2
.

The proof is divided into two situations relying on the value of
r_{0}
: Case I for
r_{0}=\infty
and Case II for
r_{0}<\infty
.

Case I (
r_{0}=\infty
).
In this case, Assumption (A2) is of limited utility, as it allows for the unbounded support for
\mathbb{P}_{X}
. On the other hand, since the objective function is integrable, there exists a compact set
K\subset M
such that
E\subset K=\overline{B_{r^{\prime}}(p_{0})}
for some

Case II (

).
Assumption (A2) leads that
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\subseteq B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
, where
r_{0},p_{0}
are specified in (

). Let
r=r_{0}
.

The rest of the proof closely follows the argument used in
(

Firstly, we show that
E\subset B_{2r}(p_{0})

m_{0}\in E

. By the triangle inequality, for any

.

Secondly, we show that

Theorem 2

(Uniqueness of population
M
-estimators)
.

Suppose Condition (C1) holds and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfies Assumption (A1). The population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is unique if either of the following conditions holds:

(a)

\rho
satisfies Condition (C2),
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfies Assumption (A2), and
\mathbb{P}_{X}

(b)

\rho

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We begin by verifying case (a).

(a) Suppose that the population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not unique, and that
m_{0}
and
m^{\prime}_{0}
are two distinct population
M
-estimators with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
; that is,
m_{0}
and
m^{\prime}_{0}
are both minimizers of
F(\cdot)

Since

there exists a unique minimal geodesic

m^{\prime}_{0}\penalty\ (=\gamma(t_{0}))
.
(We abuse the notation for the geodesic
\gamma
, which either refers to a function from an interval to
M
or the image of the function
\gamma
in
M
.)

First of all, for any
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
,
\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))
is defined for
x\neq\gamma(t)
, and is nonnegative. Furthermore, for
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
,

\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))>0

(6)

This can be seen from the Hessian comparison theorem,
which states that
for any
t\in(0,t_{0})
,

\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))\geq\frac{\textsf{sn}_{\Delta}^{\prime}(d(x,\gamma(t))}{\textsf{sn}_{\Delta}(d(x,\gamma(t))}\sin^{2}(\alpha),

(7)

where
\alpha
is the angle formed at
\gamma(t)
by the geodesic
\gamma
and the unique length-minimizing geodesic from
x
to
\gamma(t)
, and

\textsf{sn}_{\Delta}(s)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\sin(s\sqrt{\Delta})
for
\Delta>0
,

s
for
\Delta=0
, and
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{|\Delta|}}\sinh(s\sqrt{|\Delta|})
for
\Delta<0

(
Afsari 2011
)
.
It can be checked that for any
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
, we have
d(x,\gamma(t))<\tfrac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\Delta}}
, which in turn leads that

\frac{\textsf{sn}_{\Delta}^{\prime}(d(x,\gamma(t))}{\textsf{sn}_{\Delta}(d(x,\gamma(t))}>0,

unless
d(x,\gamma(t))=0
.
The above result and the fact that for
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
, the angle is non-zero (i.e.,
\sin(\alpha)\neq 0
), together with (
7
), lead to (
6
).
On the other hand, if
x\in\gamma\cap B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
, but
x\neq\gamma(t)
, then

\sin(\alpha)=0
, leading that

\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))=0
.

The following holds for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
satisfying that there is no point mass at
\gamma(t)
or at any
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
such that
d(x,\gamma(t))\in S
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}F(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\big[\rho^{\prime\prime}\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d(x,\gamma(t))\}^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))\big]d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)>0.

Note that (
8
) is positive wherever it is defined because
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not supported entirely on
\gamma
by assumption and for
x\in B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
, the second integrand of (
8
) is positive. Since there are at most countable point masses along
\gamma
and even in
B_{r_{0}}(p_{0})
(together with the fact that
S
is countable), (
8
) is well-defined for
t\in(0,t_{0})
possibly excluding a countable set. Hence,

t\mapsto\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(\gamma(t))
is strictly increasing by an application of Goldowsky-Tonelli’s theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 1 of
Casey and Holzsager 2005
). It implies that
t\mapsto F(\gamma(t))
is strictly convex, which is a contradiction to the hypothesis of two distinct population
M
-estimators with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
.

We next show that if condition (b) holds, then the population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is unique.

(b) The proof is divided into two cases depending on whether
\Delta
is non-positive or not.

Case I (
\Delta\leq 0
).

Suppose that the population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not unique, and that
m_{0}
and
m^{\prime}_{0}
are two distinct population
M
-estimators with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
; that is,
m_{0}
and
m^{\prime}_{0}
are both minimizers of
F(\cdot)
. Since
m_{0},m^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{r}(p_{0})
,
there exists a unique geodesic
\gamma
with unit speed connecting
m_{0}\penalty\ (=\gamma(0))
and
m^{\prime}_{0}\penalty\ (=\gamma(t_{0}))
. Since
B_{r}(p_{0})
is strongly convex, there exist
-\infty<t_{1}<t_{2}<\infty
such that
\gamma((t_{1},t_{2}))=\gamma\cap B_{r}(p_{0})
. For
x\in B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
, the angle
\alpha
, as defined in (
7
), but for
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, is non-zero (i.e.,
\sin(\alpha)\neq 0
). Hence, for
x\in B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
, it holds that
\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))>0
due to (
7
).
On the other hand,
if
x\in\gamma\cap B_{r}(p_{0})
, but
x\neq\gamma(t)
, then

\sin(\alpha)=0
, leading that

\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))=0
. Thus, for any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}F(\gamma(t))
is non-negative.

We now verify that the function
t\mapsto F(\gamma(t))
is strictly convex on
(t_{1},t_{2})
. We divide the proof into two cases:

(i) (
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\not\subset\gamma
).
The probability distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
has a positive mass on
B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
. For any
x\in B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\gamma
, it holds that
d(x,\gamma(t))>0
; thus,
\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}>0
(due to Lemma
1
with
\rho^{\prime}(0)=0
) and
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}d(x,\gamma(t))>0
. Since the second integrand of (
8
) is positive in a non-zero probability, it follows that
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}F(\gamma(t))>0
for any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
possibly excluding a countable set.
Accordingly, the map
t\mapsto\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(\gamma(t))
is strictly increasing, and thus
t\mapsto F(\gamma(t))
is strictly convex on
(t_{1},t_{2})
.

(ii) (
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})\subset\gamma
).

There are at most countable point mass of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
along
\gamma
; in such a scenario, (
8
) reduces to

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}F(\gamma(t))=\int\rho^{\prime\prime}\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d(x,\gamma(t))\}^{2}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x),

which is well-defined and positive for any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
except for a countable set.
Goldowsky-Tonelli’s theorem implies that
t\mapsto\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(\gamma(t))
is strictly increasing, which implies that
t\mapsto F(\gamma(t))
is strictly convex.

To sum up, for all cases (i) and (ii),
the function
t\mapsto F(\gamma(t))
is strictly convex on
(t_{1},t_{2})
, which contradicts the assumption of two distinct
M
-estimators. Therefore, the population
M
-estimator with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is unique.

Case II (
\Delta>0
).

Since it is assumed that
\Delta>0
,
r_{0}
specified in Assumption (A2) is finite. Let
B_{r}(p_{0})
with
r=r_{0}
be as in the statement of Lemma
2
, so that both
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
and
E
are contained in the strongly convex ball
B_{r}(p_{0})
. If
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is supported entirely on a single geodesic contained in the strongly convex ball, an argument similar to
Case I
for (ii) completes the proof. Hence, suppose that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not supported entirely on any single geodesic contained in
B_{r}(p_{0})
.

Since
E\subset B_{r}(p_{0})
is non-empty by Theorem
1
, there is at least one critical point of
F
in
B_{r}(p_{0})
, which is in fact a minimizer. While the existence of other critical points that are not minimizers can not be ruled out a priori, we claim that any critical point of
F
, restricted to
B_{r}(p_{0})
, is in fact a local minimizer. If this claim holds, then the Poincaré-Hopf index theorem
(
Guillemin and Pollack 2010
, Chapter 3.5)
applied to the compact submanifold
\overline{B_{r}(p_{0})}
of
M
leads that
F
has exactly one critical point in
\overline{B_{r}(p_{0})}
, which must be the global minimizer. Thus,
E
is a singleton, completing the proof.

It is left to verify the claim that
any critical point of
F
, restricted to
B_{r}(p_{0})
, is in fact a local minimizer. Let
m_{0}\in B_{r}(p_{0})
be such a critical point, and let
\gamma
be any unit-speed geodesic with
\gamma(0)=m_{0}
.

By applying the Hessian comparison theorem (
7
), we obtain for
x\in B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\{m_{0}\}
,

\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mid_{t=0}d(x,\gamma(t))\geq\sqrt{\Delta}\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\cdot\sin^{2}(\alpha_{x}),

where
\alpha_{x}
denotes
the angle formed at
m_{0}
between the geodesic segment from
m_{0}
to
x
and
\gamma
. Furthermore, since
\gamma
has a unit speed, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mid_{t=0}d(x,\gamma(t))=\cos(\alpha_{x})
for
x\neq m_{0}
.

For
x=m_{0}
, since
\gamma
is a unit speed geodesic,
d(m_{0},\gamma(t))=|t|
, and
\rho\{d(m_{0},\gamma(t))\}=\rho(|t|)
. By Condition (C3), the function
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\rho_{0}(t):=\rho(|t|)
, is twice continuously differentiable at 0, and
\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mid_{t=0}\rho\{d(m_{0},\gamma(t))\}=\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\rho^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
.
These facts, together with (
8
), lead that

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mid_{t=0}F(\gamma(t))\geq I_{1}(m_{0},\gamma)+I_{2}(m_{0},\gamma),

(9)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(m_{0},\gamma)=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(p_{0})\setminus\{m_{0}\}}\big[\rho^{\prime\prime}\{d(x,m_{0})\}\cos^{2}(\alpha_{x})

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,m_{0})\}\cdot\sqrt{\Delta}\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\cdot\sin^{2}(\alpha_{x})\big]d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x),

(10)

and

I_{2}(m_{0},\gamma)=\int_{\{m_{0}\}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\mid_{t=0}\rho\{d(x,\gamma(t))\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)=\rho^{\prime\prime}(0)\mathbb{P}_{X}(\{m_{0}\})\geq 0
.

Suppose that
m_{0}=p_{0}
. Then, the integrand of (
10
) is positive for any
x\in B_{r}(m_{0})\setminus\{m_{0}\}
. This can be seen from the fact that
d(x,m_{0})<r\leq{\pi}/({2\sqrt{\Delta}})
, thus
\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}>0
, and by Condition (C3), in particular, that
\rho^{\prime\prime}(\cdot),\rho^{\prime}(\cdot)>0
on
(0,\infty)
with
\rho^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
. Therefore, (
9
) is positive unless
\textsf{supp}(\mathbb{P}_{X})=\{m_{0}\}
, which is precluded by the assumption that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not supported entirely on any single geodesic. Since
\gamma
was arbitrarily chosen, the Hessian of
F
at
m_{0}
is positive definite; hence,
m_{0}
is a local minimizer of

, as desired.

Hereafter, suppose
m_{0}\neq p_{0}
.
Plugging-in the inequality
\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\leq 1
into the integrand of (

\rho^{\prime}(t)\leq t\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle I_{1}(m_{0},\gamma)\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B}\big[\rho^{\prime\prime}\{d(x,m_{0})\}d(x,m_{0})\cdot\sqrt{\Delta}\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\cos^{2}(\alpha_{x})

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,m_{0}\}\sqrt{\Delta}\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\sin^{2}(\alpha_{x})\big]d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B}\big[\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,m_{0})\}\sqrt{\Delta}\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\cos^{2}(\alpha_{x})

\displaystyle+\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,m_{0}\}\sqrt{\Delta}\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}\sin^{2}(\alpha_{x})\big]d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\rho^{\prime}\{d(x,m_{0})\}\sqrt{\Delta}\cdot\cot\{\sqrt{\Delta}d(x,m_{0})\}d\mathbb{P}_{X}(x),

where

For

Corollary 2

(Uniqueness of sample
M
-estimators)
.

Suppose Condition (C1) holds and that either of the following holds:

(a)

\rho
satisfies (C2), and the sample points
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}
do not lie in the same geodesic, and reside in an open ball of radius
r_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\Delta}},r_{\mbox{\tiny inj}}(M)\}
.

(b)

\rho
satisfies (C3), and the sample points
\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}\}
lie in an open ball of radius
r_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\Delta}},r_{\mbox{\tiny inj}}(M)\}
.

Then, the sample
M
-estimator is unique.

Remark 1
.

(a)

We emphasize that Theorem
2
(a) is a generalization of Theorem 2(a) for (i) in
Lee and Jung 2024
, and that Theorem
2
(b) is a generalization of Theorem 2.1 for
L_{p}
-loss function
\rho(t)=t^{p}\penalty\ (2\leq p<\infty)
in
Afsari 2011
.

(b)

In Theorem
2
and Corollary
2
, the (strict) convexity of
\rho
is imposed on the statements. In general, establishing the uniqueness of
M
-estimators becomes substantially more difficult when
\rho
is non-convex. In fact, it is well-known that
M
-estimators with non-convex losses (e.g., redescending
M
-estimators) can fail to be unique even in Euclidean spaces; see, for example,
(
Huber and Ronchetti 2009
, Chapter 4.8)
.

4
Conclusion

We have established the existence and uniqueness of generic
M
-estimators on Riemannian manifolds under regularity conditions on the loss function
\rho
and assumptions on probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{X}
. We believe these results provide a foundational basis for the development of methodologies and theories for
M
-estimators on geometric spaces.

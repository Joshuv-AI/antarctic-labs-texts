Multifractal formalism of Lyapunov exponents for fiber-bunched linear cocycles
Reza Mohammadpour ID and Paulo Varandas ID

Abstract
. We develop a higher-dimensional extension of multifractal analysis for typical fiber-bunched linear cocycles. Our main result is a relative variational principle, which shows that the topological entropy of the level sets of Lyapunov exponents can be approximated by the metric entropy of ergodic measures fully concentrated on those level sets, addressing a question posed by Breuillard and Sert [ 17 ] . We also establish a variational principle for the generalized singular value function. As an application to dynamically defined linear cocycles, we obtain a multifractal formalism for open sets of C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} repellers and Anosov diffeomorphisms.

Introduction

The multifractal formalism in dynamical systems aims to describe the fine structures of level sets, usually obtained by limit theorems, and has attracted significant interest from the mathematics and physics communities. The most classic example is the level sets defined by the convergence of Birkhoff averages, which we now detail.
Given a continuous map
T
on a compact metric space
X
and a continuous observable
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem ensures that sequence

\big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))\big)_{n\geqslant 1}

is almost everywhere convergent, with respect to any
T
-invariant probability measure.
This motivates us to consider the multifractal decomposition

(1.1)

X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}E_{f}(\alpha)\;\cup\;I_{f}

where

E_{f}(\alpha)=\Big\{x\in X\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^{j}(x))=\alpha\Big\}

and the set
I_{f}
denotes the irregular set, constituted by points whose Birkhoff averages
do not converge. The complexity of the level sets
E_{f}(\alpha)
can be measured in terms of entropy (see e.g.
[
15
]
) or Hausdorff dimension (see e.g.
[
8
]
), just to mention a few. Early contributions on the multifractal analysis can be traced back to Besicovitch and the global picture is nowadays quite well understood especially in the case of hyperbolic and nonuniformly hyperbolic dynamical system and Hölder continuous observables (cf.
[
7
,
26
,
33
,
35
,
34
,
42
,
43
,
46
]
and references therein). Moreover, even though the set
I_{f}
is negligible from the ergodic viewpoint, it can be a Baire generic set with full topological entropy or full Hausdorff dimension (see e.g.
[
8
,
11
,
21
]
, just to mention a few).

The multifractal formalism for Birkhoff averages of vector valued maps over the shift was initiated in
[
7
,
26
]
, where the authors consider a higher-dimensional version of the multifractal decomposition (
1.1
) by studying level sets
E_{f}(\vec{\alpha})
,
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
determined by the Birkhoff averages of Hölder continuous observables
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\geqslant 1
. A Legendre-type formula for the topological entropy of level sets
A first key distinction arises between the one-dimensional and higher-dimensional settings as, when
k>1
the higher-dimensional spectra may be a non-convex domain and may even have empty interior (see
[
7
]
for more details).

In recent years, motivated by several applications to thermodynamic formalism, dimension theory, and large deviations, the classical notion of potential in dynamical systems has been extended to more general frameworks that include sequences of continuous potentials
\mathcal{F}=(f_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
, under mild additivity assumptions. Some contributions on the description of the complexity of level sets

(1.2)

E_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha)=\Big\{x\in X\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}f_{n}(x)=\alpha\Big\}

and irregular set
I_{\mathcal{F}}

include
[
12
,
18
,
19
,
21
,
25
,

]
, with important applications.
Among the more general results, we emphasize that Feng and Huang
[
28
]
computed the topological entropy of level sets for sequences of vector valued asymptotically additive potentials (we refer the reader to
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
for the precise statements).
Nevertheless, Cuneo
[
22
]
revealed that asymptotically additive sequences of real-valued potentials do not fundamentally extend the scope of the theory beyond the standard additive case for continuous observables.

Lyapunov exponents can be used to quantify the exponential rates of divergence or convergence of nearby trajectories in smooth dynamical systems. They characterize chaos and stability through the sensitivity of orbits to initial conditions and are used to study the statistical properties of dynamical systems in ergodic theory.
These exponents arise from the exponential growth rates in derivative cocycles, which are our primary focus and belong to the broader class of linear cocycles.
For that reason, in this paper we address on the multifractal analysis of Lyapunov exponents for cocycles taking values on the non-abelian Lie group
\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and applications to smooth dynamical systems. Given a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
over a two-sided subshift
of finite type
(\Sigma,T)
,
x\in X
and
n\geqslant 1
consider

\mathcal{A}^{n}(x):=\mathcal{A}\left(T^{n-1}(x)\right)\ldots\mathcal{A}(x)

and

\mathcal{A}^{-n}(x):=\mathcal{A}(T^{-n}(x))^{-1}\mathcal{A}(T^{-n+1}(x))^{-1}\dots\,\mathcal{A}(T^{-1}(x))^{-1}.

For each

T
-invariant probability measure
\mu
such that
\log\|\mathcal{A}^{\pm 1}\|\in L^{1}(\mu)
, Oseledets’ theorem ensures that for
\mu
-almost every
x
there exist
k(x)\geqslant 1
and an
\mathcal{A}
-invariant splitting
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E^{1}_{x}\oplus E^{2}_{x}\oplus\dots\oplus E_{x}^{k(x)}
so that the
Lyapunov exponents
are well defined by the limits

\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{A},x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)v\|,\quad\text{for every $v\in E^{i}_{x}\setminus\{0\}$ }.

We denote by

\chi_{1}(\mathcal{A},x)\geqslant\chi_{2}(\mathcal{A},x)\geqslant\ldots\geqslant\chi_{d}(\mathcal{A},x)

the Lyapunov exponents, counted with multiplicity, of the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
at
x\in\Sigma
. Set also

\chi_{i}(\mu,\mathcal{A}):=\int\chi_{i}(\mathcal{A},\cdot)d\mu
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d.

We note that the Lyapunov exponents
\chi_{i}(\mathcal{A},x)
can be computed as

\chi_{i}(\mathcal{A},x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))

where
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d}
are the singular values, listed in decreasing order according to multiplicity
[
32
]
.
The
Lyapunov spectrum
of the linear cocycle

\mathcal{A}
is the set

(1.3)

\vec{L}=\bigg\{\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:E(\vec{\alpha})\neq\emptyset\bigg\}

where, for each

\vec{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
\vec{\alpha}
-
level set
is
defined by

(1.4)

E(\vec{\alpha})=\bigg\{x\in\Sigma:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))=\alpha_{i}\text{ for every }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\bigg\}.

Due to the non-abelian nature of the
\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
group of matrices, the level sets in (
1.4
) are substantially harder to describe
than level sets for the top Lyapunov exponent of linear cocycles. Indeed, level sets for the top Lyapunov exponent are of the form (
1.2
) with
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}=(\log\|\mathcal{A}^{n}(\cdot)\|)_{n\geqslant 1}
, and have been studied for restrictive classes of linear cocycles
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies some mild additivity conditions (see
[
6
,
3
,
23
]
for precise statements and the relation between mild forms of additivity and existence of dominated splittings for the cocycle). Later,
[
27
,
38
]
extended their result for the generic linear cocycles. Irregular sets for the top Lyapunov exponent of linear cocycles have also been discussed and the interested reader may find a geometric approach in
[
29
]
.

First results on the multifractal formalism of the
\vec{\alpha}
-level sets for Lyapunov exponents in (
1.4
) for generic matrix cocycles have been obtained by the first named author
in the special case of locally constant linear cocycles
[
40
,
39
]
, where the analysis boils down to understanding products of matrices. However, these results do not contribute to the broader goal of describing the multifractal formalism of Lyapunov exponents in smooth dynamics, as derivative cocycles are rarely locally constant.

In this paper we address the problem of computing the topological entropy of Lyapunov
\vec{\alpha}
-level sets for typical Hölder continuous, fiber-bunched matrix cocycles. This provides a natural framework for extending the study of multifractal formalism beyond locally constant linear cocycles.
The fiber-bunching assumption (see Subsection
4.2
for a precise definition and further details) is an open condition in the space of Hölder continuous and ensures the existence of stable and unstable holonomies, analogous to center submanifolds in the setting of partial hyperbolicity in smooth dynamical systems
[
14
]
.
The notion of typicality for linear cocycles is closely related to the notion introduced by Bonatti and Viana
[
14
]
and Avila and Viana
[
1
]
. While typical cocycles form an open and dense subset of infinite codimension within the set of fiber-bunched
cocycles
[
1
,
14
]
, these may fail to have a dominated splitting.

In order to overcome the lack of domination, one of the key novelties of this paper is the construction of special induced subsystems that resemble the construction of horseshoes for nonuniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems due to Katok
[
37
]
. Indeed, over the induced subshift of finite type, the induced cocycle is locally constant and is dominated. This construction, of independent interest, combined with a relation between the entropy and Lyapunov exponents of the original cocycle and induced cocycle, allowed us to give a full characterization of the topological entropy of Lyapunov level sets. Indeed, we establish a relative variational principle for topological entropy of level sets and for the topological pressure of the singular valued function and a relative variational principle for the topological entropy of Lyapunov exponents level sets
in case of typical cocycles (we refer the reader to Theorems
A
and
B
for the precise statements).

Considering this class of fiber-bunched linear cocycles offers a significant advantage in terms of applications.
Indeed, if

f:M\rightarrow M
is a smooth map or diffeomorphism of a closed Riemannian manifold
M

and
E\subset TM
is a
Df
-invariant sub-bundle, the derivative map restricted to
E
is a cocycle generated by the map
\mathcal{A}(x)=

D_{x}f:E_{x}\rightarrow E_{f(x)}
.
In the special case that

f
is uniformly expanding or uniformly hyperbolic such a cocycle can be modeled by a Hölder continuous cocycle over a subshift of finite type (see Section
8
for details). Hence, building over the results for more general linear cocycles, we derive a multidimensional multifractal formalism for certain classes of Anosov diffeomorphisms and repellers (see Theorems
C
and
D
for the precise statements).

2.
Statement of the main results

2.1.
Setting

Given a transition matrix
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{k\times k}(\{0,1\})
the one-sided
subshift of finite type determined by
Q
is a left shift map

T:\Sigma_{Q}^{+}\to\Sigma_{Q}^{+}
defined as
T(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
=
(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, acting on the space of sequences

\Sigma_{Q}^{+}:=\Big\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}:x_{i}\in\{1,...,k\}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{and}\hskip 2.84544ptQ_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}=1\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for all}\hskip 2.84544pti\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Similarly, we define a two-sided subshift of finite type
(\Sigma_{Q},T)
on the space

\Sigma_{Q}:=\Big\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}:x_{i}\in\{1,...,k\}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{and}\hskip 2.84544ptQ_{x_{i},x_{i+1}}=1\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for all}\hskip 2.84544pti\in\mathbb{Z}\Big\}.

For notational simplicity, we shall omit the dependence of
Q
on the space
\Sigma_{Q}
and
\Sigma_{Q}^{+}
and always write
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{+}
, respectively.
We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{n}
the set of all admissible words of length
n
of
\Sigma_{Q}
and write
\mathcal{L}=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 1}\mathcal{L}_{n}
. For each
I\in\mathcal{L}
let
|I|
denote the length of the word
I
and let

[I]=\bigg\{x\in\Sigma_{Q}\colon x_{j}=i_{j}\;\text{for every }\,0\leqslant j\leqslant|I|-1\bigg\}

be the cyclinder set in
\Sigma_{Q}
determined by
I
.
The case where all entries of the matrix
Q
are equal to 1 corresponds to the
full shift
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)
the space of
T
-invariant probability measures.

For any linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\rightarrow\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{I}\in\mathcal{L}
, we define

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{I})\|:=\max_{x\in[I]}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{[I]}(x)\right\|.

In order to describe the level sets of Lyapunov exponents for linear cocycles we shall need a generalization of Falconer’s singular value function.
Given
A\in\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
,
Falconer’s
singular value function

\varphi^{s}({A})

(s\geqslant 0)

is defined by

\varphi^{s}({A})=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{1}({A})\cdots\sigma_{k}({A})\sigma_{k+1}({A})^{s-k},&\text{if $0\leqslant k\leqslant d-1$ and $s\in[k,k+1]$}\\
\det({A})^{\,\frac{s}{d}}&\text{if $s\geqslant d$}\end{array}\end{cases}.

Given a vector
q:=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the
generalized singular value function

\psi^{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}}:\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as

(2.2)

\psi^{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}}({A}):=\sigma_{1}({A})^{q_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{d}({A})^{q_{d}}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\left\|{A}^{\wedge i}\right\|^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}\right)\left\|{A}^{\wedge d}\right\|^{q_{d}}.

When
s\in[0,d]
, the singular value function
\varphi^{s}(A)
coincides with the generalized singular value function

\psi^{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}}({A})
where

\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}\right)=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{m\text{ times }},s-m,0,\ldots,0)\quad\text{and}\quad m=\lfloor s\rfloor.

For notational simplicity we shall write
\psi^{q}({A}):=\psi^{q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}}({A})

for each
A\in\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.1
.

We should notice that even though some similarity between the previous expressions, Falconer’s singular value function
\varphi^{s}(A)
is sub-multiplicative whereas the generalized
singular value function
\psi^{q}(A)
is neither sub-multiplicative nor supermultiplicative.

The main goal of this paper is to contribute to the
multifractal
formalism of a wide class of
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
-valued and Hölder continuous matrix cocycles over
\Sigma.

In
[
14
]
, Bonatti and Viana introduced the notion of
typical cocycles

and showed that the set of typical cocycles forms an open and dense subset of the space of fiber-bunched Hölder continuous linear cocycles (see Section
3
for the definitions).
Given a typical cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
and
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we will consider
the family of
potentials

(2.3)

\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}):=\Big(\,\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(\cdot))\Big)_{n\geqslant 1}.

Note that
\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))\geqslant 0
for every
x\in\Sigma
and that,
despite not being neither sub-multiplicative nor super-multiplicative any of the coordinates of the parameter
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function
\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(\cdot))

satisfies a bounded distortion property
(cf. Remark
5.2
).

The
topological pressure
of the generalized singular value potential (
2.3
) is defined by

(2.4)

P^{*}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(q),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall q\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(2.5)

Z_{n}(q):=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(I))\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(I)):=\max_{x\in[I]}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)).

The function
Z_{n}(q)
is often referred to as
the
partition function
.
In case
\mathcal{A}
is a typical cocycle one can replace
\limsup
by a limit in (
2.4
) and we denote it by
P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))
(cf. Section
5
).

2.2.
General statements

The first main result guarantees that the topological entropy of vector-valued Lyapunov exponent spectra
(recall that
\vec{L}
stands for the Lyapunov spectrum of the linear cocycle (cf. (
1.3
)), which is closed and convex (cf.
[
44
]
))
can be computed using a Legendre-type transform
of the topological pressure
of the generalized singular value potential.
More precisely:

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a typical cocycle. Then,
for each
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathring{\vec{L}}
,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,E(\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{\;P\left(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\right)-\langle q,\vec{\alpha}\rangle\;\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\,h_{\nu}(T)\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(T),\;\chi_{i}(\nu,{\mathcal{A}})=\alpha_{i},\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\Big\}.

The relative variational principle in Theorem
A
provides an affirmative answer to a problem raised by Breuillard and Sert
[
17
, Problem (7)]
.

A different problem would be to obtain a variational principle for the topological pressure function
\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})
involving the space of
T
-invariant probability measures.
Variational principles for
sub-additive and for super-additive potentials have been obtained in
[
19
,
20
]
. However,
\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})
is neither sub-additive nor super-additive. In case
\mathcal{A}
is a typical cocycle and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))d\mu(x)
exists (cf. Remark (
6.9
)). In the following theorem, we overcome the lack of such additive properties for
\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})
and establish the following variational principle.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a typical cocycle. Then, the following variational principle holds: for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))\,d\mu(x):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\}.

2.3.
Applications to smooth dynamical systems

The previous results have a wide range of applications. In what follows we shall focus on the multifractal analysis for Anosov diffeomorphisms. Recall that
f
is a
C^{1}
Anosov diffeomorphism of a closed manifold
M
if there exist a
Df
-invariant splitting
TM=E^{s}\oplus E^{u}
and constants
C>0,\nu\in(0,1)
such that

\left\|\left.D_{x}f^{n}\right|_{E_{x}^{s}}\right\|\leqslant C\nu^{n}\text{ and }\left\|\left.D_{x}f^{-n}\right|_{E^{u}_{x}}\right\|\leqslant C\nu^{n}

for every
x\in M
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The restrictions of the derivative cocycle of a
C^{1+\alpha}
Anosov diffeomorphism to the stable and unstable subbundles can be modeled by a Hölder continuous cocycle over a subshift of finite type. Indeed,
for a
C^{1+\alpha}
Anosov diffeomorphism
f
, the unstable and stable bundles
E^{s}
and
E^{u}
are
\beta
-Hölder continuous, for some
\beta\in(0,\alpha]
(cf.
[
30
]
).
Denoting the dimension of the unstable bundle
E^{u}
by
d
, one can realize
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
as a

\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
-cocycle over a suitable subshift of finite type
\left(\Sigma,\sigma\right)
. Indeed, the existence of a Markov partition for
f

[
16
]
results in a Hölder continuous surjection
\pi:\Sigma\rightarrow M
such that
f\circ\pi=\pi\circ\sigma
. By choosing a Markov partition of sufficiently small diameter, one may assume that the image of each cylinder
[j]
of
\Sigma,1\leqslant j\leqslant q
, is contained in an open set on which
E^{u}
is trivializable. For
x\in[j]
, we let
L_{j}(x)
:
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow E_{\pi(x)}^{u}
be a fixed trivialization of
E^{u}
over
\pi([j])
. We define the
\alpha
-Hölder
\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
-cocycle

\mathcal{A}
over the subshift
\left(\Sigma,\sigma\right)
by

(2.6)

\mathcal{A}(x):=\left.L_{k}(\sigma(x))^{-1}\circ D_{\pi(x)}f\right|_{E^{u}}\circ L_{j}(x),\text{whenever $\sigma(x)\in[k]$.}

We will say the derivative cocycle
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
is
fiber-bunched
if there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2.7)

\left\|\left.D_{x}f^{N}\right|_{E_{x}^{u}}\right\|\cdot\left\|\left(\left.D_{x}f^{N}\right|_{E_{x}^{u}}\right)^{-1}\right\|\cdot\max\left\{\left\|\left.D_{x}f^{N}\right|_{E_{x}^{s}}\right\|^{\beta},\left\|\left(\left.D_{x}f^{N}\right|_{E_{x}^{u}}\right)^{-1}\right\|^{\beta}\right\}<1.

When
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
is fiber-bunched, the canonical stable and unstable holonomies for the cocycle
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
converge and are
\beta
-Hölder continuous (see Subsection
4.2
for the definition of holonomy maps).

Theorem C
.

Let
f
be a transitive
C^{1+\alpha}
Anosov diffeomorphism of a closed manifold
M
such that
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
is fiber-bunched. If
\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}
is a typical cocycle then

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(E(\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\{P(f,\log\Psi^{q}(\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}}))-\langle\vec{\alpha},q\rangle\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(f):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(f),\;\chi_{i}(\mu,\left.Df\right|_{E^{u}})=\alpha_{i}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\bigg\}.

for every
\vec{\alpha}\in ri(\Omega)
.

Some comments are in order. Both the bunching and typicality requirements in Theorem
C
are
C^{1}
-open conditions. Moreover, as the bunching condition (

) is satisfied by a
C^{1}
-open set of

-diffeomorphisms sufficiently

-close to a

Our main results also find applications to the
Lyapunov multifractal formalism of expanding repellers.
Given a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
M
and a
C^{1}
map
h
on
M
, a compact

-invariant subset
\Lambda\subset M
is a

if

(1)

there exists an adapted norm
\|\cdot\|
and
\lambda>1
such that

\left\|D_{x}h(v)\right\|\geqslant\lambda\|v\|\qquad\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$ and $v\in T_{x}M$,}

(2)

there exists a bounded open neighborhood

of
\Lambda
such that

\Lambda=\Big\{x\in V:h^{n}x\in V\text{ for all }n\geqslant 0\Big\}.

Given such a repeller

, we say that
\left.h\right|_{\Lambda}

-bunched

\left\|\left(D_{x}h\right)^{-1}\right\|^{1+\alpha}\cdot\left\|D_{x}h\right\|<1\qquad\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$.}

Remark 2.2
.

A natural class of
\alpha
-bunched repellers are small perturbations of conformal repellers.
The 1-bunching assumption for repellers was studied first in

]
.

The next theorem provides a variational principle for the entropy of the Lyapunov spectrum level sets of most
\alpha
-bunched repellers. More precisely:

Theorem D
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
r>1
and
h:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}
map. Suppose
\Lambda\subset M
is a
\alpha
-bunched repeller defined by
h
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
with
r-1>\alpha
. Then there exist a
C^{1}
-neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{1}
of
h
in
C^{r}(M,M)
and a
C^{1}
-open and
C^{r}
-dense subset
\mathcal{V}_{2}
of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
such that for every
g\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
and
\vec{\alpha}\in ri(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(E(\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\{P(g,\log\Psi^{q}(\left.Dg\right|_{\Lambda_{g}}))-\langle\vec{\alpha},q\rangle\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(g):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(g),\chi_{i}(\mu,\left.Dg\right|_{\Lambda_{g}})=\alpha_{i}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\bigg\}.

We observe that the open set
\mathcal{V}_{2}
in the previous theorem is formed by maps that generate typical repellers, whose definition will be given in Section
9
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we shall introduce some terminology and recall some basic concepts concerning subshifts of finite type, exterior powers and topological pressure and capacity of sets. The reader familiar with these topics may choose to skip this section, returning to it if necessary.

3.1.
Subshifts of finite type

Assume that
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{k\times k}(\{0,1\})

is a transition matrix and
(\Sigma_{Q},T)
is the corresponding subshift of finite type.
It is well known that the primitivity of
Q
(i.e. the existence of an integer
n\geqslant 1
such that all the entries of
Q^{n}
are positive) is equivalent to
the property of the subshift of finite type
(\Sigma_{Q},T)
to be
topologically mixing
.
Endow the space
\Sigma=\Sigma_{Q}
with the following metric
d
: for
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},y=(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\Sigma

(3.1)

d(x,y)=2^{-\inf{\{k\geqslant 0}\colon\text{$x_{i}\neq y_{i}$ for some $|i|\leqslant k$}\}},

The
local stable set
at
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the set

W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(x)=\{(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}:y_{n}=x_{n}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptn\geqslant 0\}

while the
local unstable set

at
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is defined by

W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(x)=\{(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}:y_{n}=x_{n}\hskip 5.69046pt\textrm{for all}\hskip 5.69046ptn\leqslant 0\}.

The global stable (resp. global unstable) manifold of
x\in\Sigma
is

W^{s}(x):=\left\{y\in\Sigma:T^{n}y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(T^{n}(x))\text{ for some }n\geqslant 0\right\},

(\text{resp.}\quad W^{u}(x):=\left\{y\in\Sigma:T^{n}y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(T^{n}(x))\text{ for some }n\leqslant 0\right\}).

The two-side subshift of finite type
(\Sigma,T)

equipped with the metric
d
in (
3.1
)
is a hyperbolic homeomorphism (see
[
2
, Subsection 2.3]
for more details) and, in particular, it has a local product structure defined by

(3.2)

[x,y]:=W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(x)\cap W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(y)

for any
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},y=(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\Sigma
so that
x_{0}=y_{0}
.

3.2.
Multilinear algebra

Given
A\in\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and an integer
1\leqslant l\leqslant d

recall that the
l^{th}
exterior power of
A
is the invertible linear map
A^{\wedge l}:\land^{l}\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\land^{l}\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

A^{\wedge l}(e_{i_{1}}\wedge e_{i_{2}}\wedge...\wedge e_{i_{l}})=Ae_{i_{1}}\wedge Ae_{i_{2}}\wedge...\wedge Ae_{i_{l}},

where
e_{i}
’s are the standard vectors in the classical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The linear map
A^{\wedge l}
can be represented by a
\binom{d}{l}\times\binom{d}{l}

real valued matrix whose entries are the
l\times l
minors of
A
(see e.g.
[
31
]
).
In particular, for each
1\leqslant l\leqslant d
, the matrix
A^{\wedge l}
has

(3.3)

d_{\ell}:=\binom{d}{l}\times\binom{d}{l}

generalized eigenvalues.
It can also be shown that

(3.4)

(AB)^{\wedge l}=A^{\wedge l}B^{\wedge l}\quad\text{and }\quad\|A^{\wedge l}\|=\sigma_{1}(A)\dots\sigma_{l}(A),

where
\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{d}
are the singular values of the matrix
A
.
The right hand-side in (
3.4
)
is extremely useful and allows to relate the potential
\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(\cdot))
with singular values and products of norms of exterior powers.

3.3.
Sequences of potentials

Let
T:X\to X
be a continuous map an let
\Phi=\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of continuous potentials
\phi_{n}:X\to\mathbb{R}
. The sequence
\Phi
is
additive
(with respect to
T
) if

\phi_{n+m}(x)=\phi_{n}(x)+\phi_{m}(T^{n}(x))
for every
m,n\geqslant 1
and every
x\in X
. If this is the case, a simple computation shows that

\phi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{1}\circ T^{j}

for every
n\geqslant 1
.
The sequence of potentials
\Phi=\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is
sub-additive
(with respect to
T
) if

\phi_{n+m}(x)\leqslant\phi_{n}(x)+\phi_{m}(T^{n}(x))\quad\forall x\in X,\,\forall m,n\geqslant 1,

and it is called

super-additive
if
-\Phi=\{-\phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is sub-additive.

Finally, one says that the sequence
\Phi=\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is said to be an
almost additive
(with respect to
T
) if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
m,n\geqslant 1
,
x\in X
, we have

\phi_{n}(x)+\phi_{m}(T^{n})(x)-C\leqslant\phi_{n+m}(x)\leqslant\phi_{n}(x)+\phi_{m}(T^{n}(x))+C.

3.4.
Topological pressure and entropy

In the context of matrix cocycles it appears naturally some sequences of sub-additive real valued cocycles. In what follows we recall some notions from the thermodynamic formalism of non-additive sequences of potentials, including notions of entropy and pressure using Caratheodory structures.

3.4.1.
Topological pressure

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
be a continuous map. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a metric
d_{n}
on
X
by

(3.5)

d_{n}(x,y)=\max\Big\{\,d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y)):0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\,\Big\}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
we say that
E\subset X

is an
(n,\varepsilon)
-
separated subset
if
d_{n}(x,y)>\varepsilon

for any two points
x\neq y\in E
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sub-additive potential over
(X,T)
. Given
\varepsilon>0
and
n\geqslant 1
define

P_{n}(T,\Phi,\varepsilon)=\sup\bigg\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\phi_{n}(x)}:E\hskip 2.84544pt\textrm{is}\hskip 2.84544pt(n,\varepsilon)\textrm{-separated subset of }X\bigg\}.

The
topological pressure
of
\Phi
is defined by

(3.6)

P(T,\Phi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(T,\Phi,\varepsilon),

where the limit in
\varepsilon
exists by monotonicity.
In
[
19
]
, Cao, Feng and Huang established a variational principle for the topological pressure of sub-additive families of continuous potentials:

(3.7)

P(T,\Phi)=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(T)+\chi(\mu,\Phi):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\},

where
h_{\mu}(T)
is the measure-theoretic entropy and

(3.8)

\chi(\mu,\Phi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\phi_{n}(x)d\mu(x).

Any invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)
achieving the
supremum in (
3.7
) is called an
equilibrium state
of
\Phi
.

Remark 3.1
.

By upper semi-continuity of the functional (
3.8
) with respect to
\mu
, in the weak
+
topology, one concludes that if the entropy map
\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(T)

is upper semi-continuous (e.g. in case
T
is a subshift of finite type) then there exists at least one equilibrium state for each sub-additive family of continuous potentials
\Phi
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
(\Sigma,T)
is a subshift of finite type
endowed with the metric
d
defined in (
3.1
) then
T
is an expansive map with expansivity constant 1, meaning that for any
x\neq y\in\Sigma
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
so
d(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y))\geqslant 1
. In particular the pressure
P(T,\Phi)
coincides with

P^{*}(\Phi)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\phi_{n}(x)}:E\hskip 2.84544pt\textrm{is}\hskip 2.84544pt(n,1)\textrm{-separated subset of }\Sigma\right\},

meaning that
one can drop the limit in
\varepsilon
from the definition of the pressure (
3.6
) (see e.g.
[
47
]
).

3.4.2.
Topological entropy of subsets

Let
T
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
X
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
we define
Bowen ball

B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)
as follows:

B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)=\Big\{y\in X:d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon\Big\}.

Fix a subset
Y\subset X
and
\varepsilon>0
. We define a covering of
Y
as a countable collection of dynamic balls
\mathcal{Y}:=\left\{B_{n_{i}}\left(y_{i},\varepsilon\right)\right\}_{i}
such that

Y\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{n_{i}}\left(y_{i},\varepsilon\right)
. Given a collection
\mathcal{Y}=\left\{B_{n_{i}}\left(y_{i},\varepsilon\right)\right\}_{i}
, we define
n(\mathcal{Y})
as the minimum value of
n_{i}
among all indices
i
. Let
s\geqslant 0
and define

S(Y,s,N,\varepsilon)=\inf\sum_{i}e^{-sn_{i}},

where the infimum is taken over all collections
\mathcal{Y}=\{B_{n_{i}}(x_{i},\varepsilon)\}_{i}
that cover
Y
and satisfy
n(\mathcal{Y})\geqslant N
. As
S(Y,s,N,\varepsilon)
is non-decreasing with respect to
N
, the limit
S(Y,s,N,\varepsilon)
exists

S(Y,s,\varepsilon):=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}S(Y,s,N,\varepsilon).

There is a critical value of the parameter
s
, which we denote by
h_{\text{top}}(T,Y,\varepsilon)
such that

S(Y,s,\varepsilon)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mbox{$s>h_{\text{top}}(T,Y,\varepsilon)$},\\
\infty,&\mbox{$s<h_{\text{top}}(T,Y,\varepsilon)$}.\end{array}\right.

Since
h_{\text{top}}(T,Y,\varepsilon)
does not decrease with
\varepsilon
, the following limit exists,

(3.9)

h_{\text{top}}(T,Y)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}h_{\text{top}}(T,Y,\varepsilon).

We say that
h_{\text{top}}(T,Y)
is the
topological entropy
of
T
at
Y
(we shall denote it simply by
h_{\text{top}}(Y)
for notational simplicity). We denote
h_{\text{top}}(X,T)=h_{\text{top}}(T).
Again, in case
T
is subshift of finite type it is enough to take
\varepsilon=1
above.

4.
Bunching and typicality for linear cocycles

Through this section, we will set some notation, collect some necessary results on the existence of holonomies for fiber-bunched cocycles, and define a notion of typicality for linear cocycles.
We will always assume that

T:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is a topologically mixing subshift of finite type,
where
\Sigma\subset\{1,2,\dots,k\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the symbolic space, endowed with the metric
d
defined in (
3.1
), and
T(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
for any
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\Sigma
.

4.1.
One-step cocycles

The simplest example of a linear cocycles
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a Locally constant one, meaning that for each
x\in\Sigma
there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{x}\subset\Sigma
of
x
such that
\mathcal{A}(y)=\mathcal{A}(x)
for every
y\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
.

A simple class of such linear cocycles are the so-called
one-step cocycles

defined as follows. Given a
k
-tuple of matrices
\textbf{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})\in\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})^{k}
, we associate with it the locally constant map
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\rightarrow\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
given by
\mathcal{A}(x)=A_{x_{0}}
. This means the matrix cocycle
\mathcal{A}
depends only on the zero-th symbol
x_{0}
of
(x_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}
. It is clear that

\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)=A_{x_{n-1}}\cdot\dots\cdot A_{x_{1}}\cdot A_{x_{0}}

for any
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\Sigma
, and it models a random product of the finite collection of matrices.

4.2.
Fiber bunching and holonomies

Given
\alpha>0
, a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\rightarrow GL(d,\mathbb{R})
over a topologically mixing subshift of finite type
(\Sigma,T)
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function if there exists
C>0
such that

(4.1)

\|\mathcal{A}(x)-\mathcal{A}(y)\|\leqslant Cd(x,y)^{\alpha}\hskip 5.69046pt\quad\forall x,y\in\Sigma.

We denote by
C^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
the vector space of
\alpha
-Hölder continuous cocycles over the topologically mixing subshift of finite type
(\Sigma,T)
.

The action of a cocycle
\mathcal{A}
can be observed by the skew-product
F:X\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to X\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
given by

(4.2)

F(x,v)=(T(x),\mathcal{A}(x)v),\qquad(x,v)\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{k},

as the n-th iterate of
F
is
F^{n}(x,v)=(T^{n}(x),\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)v)
, for each
n\geqslant 1
.

We say that the cocycle
\mathcal{A}\in C^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
over
T
is
fiber bunched
if

(4.3)

\|\mathcal{A}(x)\|\|\mathcal{A}(x)^{-1}\|<2^{\alpha}\quad\text{for every $x\in\Sigma$}

(note that the constant
2
appearing above is a hyperbolicity constant for the shift map
T
). In rough terms, the fiber-bunching condition ensures the skew-product
F
in (
4.2
) is partially hyperbolic (see
[
14
]
for more details).
Let
C_{b}^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
denote the space of
\alpha
-Holder continuous and fiber-bunched cocycles over
T
,
and note that
C_{b}^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))

is a
C^{0}
-open subset of
C^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given
\mathcal{A}\in C^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
,

x\in\Sigma
and
y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(x)

the
local stable holonomy

H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}\in GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is defined
by the limit (in case the limit exists)

H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{A}^{n}(y)^{-1}\mathcal{A}^{n}(x).

Similarly, the
local unstable holonomy

H_{y\leftarrow x}^{u}
is likewise defined as (in case the limit exists)

H_{y\leftarrow x}^{u}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow-\infty}\mathcal{A}^{n}(y)^{-1}\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)

for any
x\in\Sigma
and
y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(x)
.

Some comments are in order. First, even though the stable and unstable holonomies depend on the linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}
, we shall omit its dependence on
\mathcal{A}
whenever possible, for notational simplicity. Second, it follows from
[
14
]
that stable and unstable holonomies exist for fiber-bunched linear cocycles, that stable holonomies satisfy

a)

H_{x\leftarrow x}^{s}=Id
and
H_{z\leftarrow y}^{s}\circ H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}=H_{z\leftarrow x}^{s}
for any
z,y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(x)
.

b)

\mathcal{A}(y)\circ H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}=H_{T(y)\leftarrow T(x)}^{s}\circ\mathcal{A}(x).

c)

(x,y,v)\mapsto H^{s}_{y\leftarrow x}(v)
is continuous.

and similar properties hold for unstable holonomies (with
s
and
T
replaced by
u
and
T^{-1}
, respectively).
Third, one can use item
b)
above to one can extend the definition to the global stable holonomy
H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}
for
y\in W^{s}(x)
not necessarily in
W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(x)
:

(4.4)

H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}=\mathcal{A}^{n}(y)^{-1}\circ H_{T^{n}(y)\leftarrow T^{n}(x)}^{s}\circ\mathcal{A}^{n}(x),

where

n\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough such that
T^{n}(y)\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(T^{n}(x))
(the global unstable holonomy can be defined similarly).
Finally, the holonomies vary
\alpha
-Hölder continuously (see
[
36
]
), which means that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

d)

\|H_{y\leftarrow x}^{s}-I\|\leqslant Cd(x,y)^{\alpha}
for every
y\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(x)
(and an analogous statement for unstable holonomies).

Remark 4.2
.

It is worth mentioning that stable and unstable holonomies always exist for one-step cocycles (see
[
14
, Proposition 1.2]
and
[
38
, Remark 1]
).

In the remainder of this subsection, we will introduce one of the key objects to be used in this paper.
Assume that
x,y\in\Sigma
,
x^{\prime}\in W_{\text{loc }}^{u}(x)
, and
y^{\prime}:=T^{n}(x^{\prime})\in W_{\text{loc }}^{s}(y)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We can describe these points as forming a path from
x
to
y
via
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
, which can be represented as:

(4.5)

x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W_{\text{loc }}^{u}(x)}}{{\longrightarrow}}x^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}y^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W_{\text{loc }}^{s}(y)}}{{\longrightarrow}}y

(see Figure
4.1
below).

Figure 4.1.
Path from
x
to
y
as in (
4.5
).

To such a path and a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\rightarrow GL(d,\mathbb{R})
that admits canonical holonomies, one associates the matrix

(4.6)

B_{x,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},y}:=H_{y\leftarrow y^{\prime}}^{s}\mathcal{A}^{n}\left(x^{\prime}\right)H_{x^{\prime}\leftarrow x}^{u}.

When the context is clear, we will simply use the terminology “path from
x
to
y
” while referring to
B_{x,y}
.

4.3.
Typical cocycles

Let
T:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be a topologically mixing subshift of finite type.
Suppose that
p\in\Sigma
is a periodic point of
T
. We say
z\in\Sigma\setminus\{p\}
is a
homoclinic point
associated to
p
if
z\in W^{s}(p)\cap W^{u}(p)
. We denote the set of all homoclinic points of
p
by

\mathcal{H}(p)
. For each

z\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(p)\cap W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(p)

consider the matrix

(4.7)

{\widetilde{H}}_{p}^{z}:=H_{p\leftarrow z}^{s}\circ H_{z\leftarrow p}^{u}

associated to the homoclinic loop. Such matrices have been used in a crucial way to prove simplicity of the Lyapunov spectrum for typical fiber-bunched linear cocycles (cf.
[
14
]
).
In general, given
z\in\mathcal{H}(p)
, up to replacing
z
by some backward iterate, we may suppose that
z\in W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(p)
and
T^{n}(z)\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(p)
for
some
n\geqslant 1
.
Then, using (
4.4
), one defines

(4.8)

{\widetilde{H}}_{p}^{z}=\mathcal{A}^{-n}(p)\circ H_{p\leftarrow T^{n}(z)}^{s}\circ\mathcal{A}^{n}(z)\circ H_{z\leftarrow p}^{u}.

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}\in C_{b}^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
1-typical
with respect to a pair
(p,z)
, where
p
is a periodic point for
T
and
z\in\mathcal{H}(p)
if:

(i)

(pinching)
the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}^{per(p)}(p)
have multiplicity
1
and distinct absolute value,

(ii)

(twisting) the eigenvectors
\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\right\}
of
\mathcal{A}^{per(p)}(p)
are such that, for any
I,J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
|I|+

|J|\leqslant d
, the set of vectors

\left\{{\widetilde{H}}_{p}^{z}\left(v_{i}\right):i\in I\right\}\cup\left\{v_{j}:j\in J\right\}

is linearly independent.

Definition 4.4
.

We say
\mathcal{A}\in C_{b}^{\alpha}(\Sigma,GL(d,\mathbb{R}))
is
typical
if
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge t}
is 1-typical with respect to the same pair
(p,z)
for all
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
.

Remark 4.5
.

For simplicity, we will always assume that
p
in Definition
4.3
is a fixed point by considering the map
T^{\text{per}(p)}
and the cocycle
\mathcal{A}^{\text{per}(p)}
if necessary (this is possible because powers of typical cocycles are typical). Moreover, for any homoclinic point
z\in\mathcal{H}(p)
,
T^{n}(z)
is a homoclinic point of
p
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.6
.

The definition of typical cocycles described above is slightly stronger than the notion of typical cocycles introduced by Bonatti and Viana
[
14
]
. In their definition, they only require 1-typicality of
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge t}
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant d/2
, and they do not require the pair
(p,z)
to be
independent of
t
.
Despite our version of typicality being slightly stronger, it seems likely to modify their techniques to prove that the set of typical cocycles is open and dense.

4.4.
Bounded distortion

We say that a family of continuous sub-additive real-valued potentials
\Phi:=\{\phi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
over
(\Sigma,T)
has
bounded distortion
if there exists
C\geqslant 1
such that

(4.9)

C^{-1}\leqslant\frac{\exp(\phi_{n}(x))}{\exp(\phi_{n}(y))}\leqslant C

for any
x,y\in[I]
, every
I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Simple examples of sub-additive potentials with bounded distortion are given by singular value potentials
\varphi^{s}(\mathcal{A})

of one-step
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
-cocycles
\mathcal{A}
, where
\varphi^{s}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))=\varphi^{s}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(y))
for any
x,y\in[I].

5.
Upper bound on the entropy of level sets

This section is devoted to the proof of the upper bound in the variational principle stated in Theorem
A
. More precisely, in this section we shall prove the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

If
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a typical cocycle then,
for each
\vec{\alpha}\in\mathring{\vec{L}}
,

(5.1)

h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,E(\vec{\alpha}))\leqslant\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\Big\{\;P\left(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\right)-\langle q,\vec{\alpha}\rangle\;\Big\}.

The proof of this theorem will occupy the remainder of this section. To this end,
we will start by proving that the pressure function
P^{*}\left(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\right)
, defined by a
\limsup
in (
2.4
), can be expressed as a limit in case of typical cocycles.
Let us first recall some
necessary concepts. For any
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\rightarrow GL(d,\mathbb{R})
and
I\in\mathcal{L}
, recall that

\|\mathcal{A}(I)\|=\max_{x\in[I]}\|\mathcal{A}^{|I|}(x)\|\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(I))=\max_{x\in[I]}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{|I|}(x)).

Similarly, if
\mathbb{V}_{t}:=\mathbb{R}^{{\bf d}_{t}}
, where
{\bf d}_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}d\\
t\end{array}\right)
, and
\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\mathcal{A}^{\wedge t}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(\mathbb{V}_{t})
is the
t^{th}
-exterior power, for each
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
,
one defines

(5.2)

\left\|\mathcal{A}_{t}(\mathrm{I})\right\|:=\max_{x\in[\mathrm{I}]}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{t}^{|\mathrm{I}|}(x)\right\|\quad\text{and}\quad\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{t}(I)):=\max_{x\in[I]}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{t}^{|I|}(x)).

We say that a fiber bunched linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is
simultaneously quasi-multiplicative
if there exist
c>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
I,J\in\mathcal{L}
, there is
K=K(I,J)\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
such that
IKJ\in\mathcal{L}
and

(5.3)

\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|\geqslant c\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|,\quad\text{for every $1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1$}.

Remark 5.2
.

If
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a Hölder continuous and fiber-bunched over a topologically mixing subshift of finite type
(\Sigma,T)
then the sub-additive sequence of potentials
\{\log\|\mathcal{A}_{t}^{n}\|\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the bounded distortion property
(
4.9
)
for every
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
as a consequence of
[
44
, Lemma 3.10]
.

The next lemma ensures that the simultaneous quasi-multiplicativity condition is itself sufficient to guarantee that the pressure function is computed as a limit.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that a fiber bunched cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is simultaneously quasi-multiplicative. Then the sequence in the definition of
P^{*}(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))
is convergent, for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

By the simultaneous quasi-multiplicativity property of
\mathcal{A}
, there exist
c>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m,n>k
,
I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
and
J\in\mathcal{L}_{m}
, there is
K\in\mathcal{L}_{k}
such that
IKJ\in\mathcal{L}
and

\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|\geqslant c\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|,
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
.
Now, by definition of the generalized
singular value function
(
2.2
)
and the bounded distortion property (see Remark
5.2
),
for any
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

(5.4)

c_{0}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|^{t_{i}}\geqslant\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(IKJ))\geqslant c_{0}\underbrace{\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|^{t_{i}}}_{\text{($\star$)}},

where
t_{i}=q_{i}-q_{i+1},
and
q_{d+1}=0
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d.

If
t_{i}<0
, then by sub-multiplicativity of the norm of the cocycle
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}
, there is
C_{0}>0

(depending only on
\mathcal{A}
)
such that

(5.5)

\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|^{t_{i}}\geqslant C_{0}^{t_{i}}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|^{t_{i}}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|^{t_{i}}.

If
t_{i}\geqslant 0
, the simultaneous quasi-multiplicativity of
\mathcal{A}
, ensures that

(5.6)

\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|^{t_{i}}\geqslant c^{t_{i}}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|^{t_{i}}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|^{t_{i}}.

Altogether, Remark
5.2
and equations (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) imply that

(\star)\geqslant C_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|^{t_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|^{t_{i}},

where
C_{1}:=C_{1}(c_{0},C^{t_{i}},c^{t_{i}},C_{0}^{t_{i}})>0.

The latter, combined with (
2.5
) and (
5.4
), guarantees that

\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(IKJ))\geqslant C_{2}\;\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(I))\;\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(J))

where
C_{2}:=c_{0}\,C_{1}>0
and, consequently,

Z_{n+k+m}(q)\geqslant C_{2}Z_{n}(q)Z_{m}(q).

Hence the sequence
a_{n}:=\log Z_{n}(q)+\log C_{2}
satisfies

a_{n+m-k}\geqslant a_{n}+a_{m}
for all
m,n>k
.
The convergence of the sequence

(\frac{a_{n}}{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
follows as in the proof
of Fekete’s lemma (cf.
[
16
]
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Now, consider the potential
\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}:=\left(\log\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{A}),\ldots,\log\sigma_{d}(\mathcal{A})\right),

and note that, denoting by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the usual inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
one has that

\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle=\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\qquad\text{for every $q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

We denote

(5.7)

P_{n}(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle,1):=\sup\left\{\sum_{x\in E}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x)):E\hskip 2.84544pt\textrm{is}\hskip 2.84544pt(n,1)\textrm{-separated subset of }\Sigma\right\}.

According to Lemma
5.3
,

P\left(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\right)=P(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle,1).

In
[
44
, Theorem 4.1]
, Park proved that
typical cocycles satisfy a weaker notion of simultaneous quasi-multiplicativity, defined in a
way that the connecting word
K
appearing in (
5.3
) is bounded. We need the following strengthened version.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
be a typical cocycle. Then
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is simultaneously quasi-multiplicative.

Proof.

This statement can be obtained from the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
44
]
, where it is shown that
if
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is a typical cocycle then there exist
c>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
I,J\in\mathcal{L}
, there is
K=K(I,J)\in\mathcal{L}
with
|K|\leqslant k
such that
IKJ\in\mathcal{L}
and

(5.8)

\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(IKJ)\|\geqslant c\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(I)\|\|\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}(J)\|,\quad\text{for every $1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1$}.

To prove the theorem one needs to show that
(
5.8
) holds with words
K
of constant length
k
.
We start by noticing that,
if
\ell_{0}\geqslant 1
is the integer given by

[
44
, Lemma 4.13]
, for any
\ell\geqslant\ell_{0}

the transition word
K=K(I,J)
can be chosen such that

(5.9)

|K(I,J)|=2m+2\bar{\tau}+n+\hat{n}+2\ell

where
m=m(I,J)
,
n=n(I,J)
and

\hat{n}=\hat{n}(I,J)
are constants determined by invariance of cones
(see Lemmas 4.22 and 4.12 in
[
44
]
) and

\bar{\tau}
is a constant given by primitivity of the subshift of finite type, all of them independent of
\ell
.
In particular, there exists
C\geqslant 1
so that
|K(I,J)|\leqslant C+2\ell

(cf.
[
44
, page 1983]
).

We now show that the length of the connecting words
K\in\mathcal{L}
above can be chosen uniform
we use the same notations as in
[
44
]
whenever possible, for the reader’s convenience.
Fix
k_{0}=C+3\ell_{0}
. Given
I,J\in\mathcal{L}
choose

\ell(I,J)=\frac{1}{2}[C+3\ell_{0}-2m(I,J)-2\bar{\tau}-n(I,J)-\hat{n}(I,J)]

which, by construction, satisfies

\ell(I,J)\geqslant\frac{3}{2}\ell_{0}
.
The argument described above guarantees that
there exists
K=K(I,J)\in\mathcal{L}

satisfying equations (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) with
\ell=\ell(I,J)
, hence
|K(I,J)|=k_{0}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Remark 5.5
.

The lack of control over the length of the connecting word
K
in quasi-multiplicativity is a limitation when studying important applications (cf.
[
4
,
41
]
). Lemma
5.4
is the first result extending it beyond one-step cocycles. Indeed,
Bárány and Troscheit
[
4
, Proposition 2.5]
proved that if a one-step cocycle
\mathcal{A}
is fully strongly irreducible and proximal, then
\mathcal{A}
is simultaneously quasi-multiplicative. Recently, Mohammadpour and Park
[
41
]
generalized their result for the norm of linear cocycles under the ireducibility assumption (see
[
41
, Corollary 1.2]
).

Given a fiber bunched linear cocycle

\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
,
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathring{\vec{L}}
and
r>0
, consider the set

(5.10)

G(\vec{\alpha},n,r):=\bigg\{x\in\Sigma:\hskip 2.84544pt\bigg|\frac{1}{m}\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{m}(x))-\alpha_{i}\bigg|<\frac{1}{r}\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\text{ and }m\geqslant n\bigg\}.

It is clear from the definition that, for any
r>0
,

E(\vec{\alpha})\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G(\vec{\alpha},n,r).

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is
fiber bunched and simultaneously quasi-multiplicative, and that
G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\neq\emptyset
for some
\alpha\in\mathring{\vec{L}}
. Then, for any
q=\left(q_{1},\cdots,q_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

h_{\mathrm{top}}\big(\,T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\,\big)\leqslant P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{|q_{i}|}{r}\right).

Proof.

Since
T
is a subshift of finite type,

h_{\mathrm{top}}\big(\,T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\,\big)=h_{\mathrm{top}}\big(\,T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r),\,1\big)

(recall Subsection
3.4.2
).
Thus, if

s<h_{\mathrm{top}}\big(\,T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\,\big)

then

\infty=S(G(\vec{\alpha},n,r),s,1)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}S(G(\vec{\alpha},n,r),s,N,1)

and there exists
N_{0}\geqslant 1
such that

S(G(\vec{\alpha},n,r),s,N,1)\geqslant 1,\quad\forall N\geqslant N_{0}.

Now take
N\geqslant\max\left\{n,N_{0}\right\}
and let
E\subset G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)
be a
(N,1)
-separated subset of maximal cardinality. As
G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\subset\bigcup_{x\in E}B_{N}(x,1)
, it follows that

(5.11)

\#E\cdot\exp(-sN)\geqslant S(G(\vec{\alpha},n,r),s,N,1)\geqslant 1.

Moreover, recalling (
5.10
), if
x\in G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)
then

\sum_{i=1}^{d}q_{i}\log\sigma_{i}\left(\mathcal{A}^{N}(x)\right)\geqslant N\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{\left|q_{i}\right|}{r}\right)

for each
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Therefore, recalling (
5.7
),

\displaystyle P_{N}(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle,1)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{x\in E}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}q_{i}\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{N}(x))\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\#E\cdot\exp\left(N\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{|q_{i}|}{r}\right)\right).

By (
5.11
) and Lemma
5.3

P_{N}(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle,1)\geqslant\exp\left(N\left[s+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{|q_{i}|}{r}\right)\right]\right)

In consequence,

P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))=P(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle)\geqslant s+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{|q_{i}|}{r}\right).

Since
s<h_{\mathrm{top}}\big(\,T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\,\big)
was chosen arbitrary, this finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the following:

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is a typical cocycle so that
\bigcup_{|\vec{\beta}-\vec{\alpha}|<\varepsilon}E(\vec{\beta})\neq\emptyset
,

for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Then, for any
q=\left(q_{1},\cdots,q_{d}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
r>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,

h_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\bigcup_{|\vec{\beta}-\vec{\alpha}|<\varepsilon}E(\vec{\beta})\right)\leqslant P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{|q_{i}|}{r}\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
, the cocycle

\mathcal{A}
is simultaneously quasi-multiplicative. Moreover,
for each
r>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,
one has
\bigcup_{|\vec{\beta}-\vec{\alpha}|<\varepsilon}E(\vec{\alpha})\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)

and, consequently,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\bigcup_{|\vec{\beta}-\vec{\alpha}|<\varepsilon}E(\vec{\alpha})\right)\leqslant h_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)\right)=\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\;h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,G(\vec{\alpha},n,r)).

The corollary follows directly from the above and the statement of Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

)

6.
Dominated subsystems

The idea to obtain a lower bound for the entropy of Lyapunov level sets involves the obtainance of dominated subsystems from the original cocycle, as defined below.

6.1.
Construction of induced dominated subsystems

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space. We say that a linear cocycle
\mathcal{A}:X\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
is

dominated with index
i
if there exist constants
C>1
,
0<\tau<1
such that

\frac{\sigma_{i+1}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))}{\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))}\leqslant C\tau^{n},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N},x\in X.

Moreover, the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
is
dominated
if it is dominated with index
i
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1.

In the case that
X
is compact,
Bochi and Gourmelon
[
9
, Theorem A]
proved that the latter notion of the domination of index
i
is equivalent to the original definition of a dominated splitting requiring an
\mathcal{A}
-invariant splitting
X\times\mathbb{R}^{d}=V\oplus W
with
\dim W=i
and such that there exists
k\geqslant 1
for which

\|\mathcal{A}^{k}(x)\mid_{V_{x}}\|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{(}A^{k}(x)\mid_{W_{x}})^{-1}\|^{-1}

for every
x\in X
(cf.
[
30
]
).

Remark 6.2
.

According to the multilinear algebra properties,
\mathcal{A}
is dominated with index
i
if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}
is dominated with index 1. Therefore, the cocycle
\mathcal{A}
is dominated if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{\wedge i}
is dominated with index
i
for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1.

We recall that
\mathbb{V}_{t}=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}_{t}},\text{ where }\mathbf{d}_{t}=\binom{d}{t}
and denote by
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{V}_{t})
its projective space. We define the
angular metric

\rho
on
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
by

\rho(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{v}):=\min\{\measuredangle(u,v),\measuredangle(u,-v)\},

for every
u,v\in\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
.
Given any set
\mathrm{S}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
and
\delta>0
, we denote the
\delta
-neighborhood of
S
by

\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{S},\delta):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{P}^{d-1}:\rho(v,\mathrm{~S})\leqslant\delta\right\}.

Let
\mathring{\mathcal{C}}(v,\varepsilon)
denote the interior
of the cone
\mathcal{C}(v,\varepsilon)
.
For any
A\in\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
, we define

\|A\|_{\rho}:=\sup_{\mathrm{u}\neq\mathrm{v}}\frac{\rho(Au,Av)}{\rho(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{v})}.

Given a typical cocycle
\mathcal{A}
and
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
, for notational simplicity we shall write

H^{s,t}:=\left(H^{s}\right)^{\wedge t}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{u,t}:=\left(H^{u}\right)^{\wedge t}

for the stable and unstable holonomies of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, respectively (here
H^{s}
and
H^{u}
are stable and unstable canonical holonomies of
\mathcal{A}
, respectively).

In what follows we say that
\mathfrak{p}=(p,x,\sigma^{n}(x),y)
is a
path

(of length
n
) starting at a periodic point
p
provided that
x\in W_{\text{loc}}^{u}(p)
and that
\sigma^{n}(x)\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s}(y)
. In case
y=p
we say that
\mathfrak{p}
is a
loop
starting at
p
.

A fiber-bunched cocycle
\mathcal{A}
is called
transverse
if for any
x,y\in\Sigma
, any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
and any hyperplane
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a path
B_{x,y}
from
x
to
y
such that
B_{x,y}v\notin W
. By the compactness of
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
, every transverse fiber-bunched cocycle is
uniformly transverse
: there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that the
path
B_{x,y}
can be chosen to have its length at most
N
and that

B_{x,y}v\notin\mathcal{C}(W,\varepsilon).

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
A\in C_{b}^{\alpha}(\Sigma,\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R}))
be typical with respect to a pair
(p,z)
, where
p

is a fixed point for
T
and
z
is an homoclinic point.
There exists
\tau^{\prime}>0
so that, for any
\tau_{1}\leqslant\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2}>0
, the following holds: there exists
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
x\in\Sigma
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists

\omega:=\omega_{x}\in\Sigma

and integers

m_{1}:=m_{1}(\omega)\geqslant 1
and

m_{2}:=m_{2}(\omega)\geqslant 1
satisfying

(1)

\omega:=\omega_{x}\in W^{u}_{loc}(p)
,

T^{m_{1}}(\omega)\in[x]_{n},

and

T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega)\in W^{s}_{loc}(p)
;

(2)

the cocycle over the loop
\mathfrak{p}=(p,\omega,T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega),p)
defined by

(6.1)

B_{p,\omega,T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega),p}^{t}:=H_{p\longleftarrow T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega)}^{s,t}\mathcal{A}_{t}^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega)H_{\omega\longleftarrow p}^{u,t}

satisfies

(6.2)

B_{p,\omega,T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega),p}^{t}\mathcal{C}(v_{1}^{t},\tau_{1})\subset\mathring{\mathcal{C}}(v_{1}^{t},\tau_{2})

for all
t\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
, where
v_{1}^{t}
is the eigendirection
\mathcal{A}_{t}(p)
corresponding to the eigenvalue of the largest modulus; and

(3)

1\leqslant m_{i}\leqslant K_{0}
, for
i=1,2.

Proof.

By assumption, the cocycle
\mathcal{A}_{t}:\Sigma\to GL(\mathbb{V}_{t})
is 1-typical cocycle with respect to the distinguished fixed point
p
and its homoclinic point
z
, for all
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
.
Since the argument resembles the construction in the proof of
[
45
, Theorem 4.6]
, we just sketch the proof.
Let
\mathbb{V}_{t}
be a real vector space of
dimension
d_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
equipped with an inner product.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
p\in\Sigma
is a fixed point and that the homoclinic point
z\in\mathcal{H}(p)
lies in
W_{\text{loc }}^{u}(p)
. Let
x\in\Sigma
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be given. We denote the eigenvectors of
P_{t}:=\mathcal{A}_{t}(p)
by
\big\{v_{1}^{(t)},\ldots,v_{d_{t}}^{(t)}\big\}
, listed in the order of decreasing absolute values for their corresponding eigenvalues and consider the hyperplanes

\mathbb{W}_{i}^{(t)}:=\text{span}\left\{v_{1}^{(t)},\ldots,v_{i-1}^{(t)},v_{i+1}^{(t)},\ldots,v_{d_{t}}^{(t)}\right\}\subset\mathbb{V}_{t}.

The twisting assumption on the holonomy loop
\tilde{H}_{t}:=\widetilde{H^{\wedge t}}_{p}^{z}
(recall Definition
4.3
) ensures that all coefficients
c_{i,j}^{(t)}
in the linear combination

\tilde{H}_{t}v_{i}^{(t)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{d_{t}}c_{i,j}^{(t)}v_{j}^{(t)}

are nonzero for any
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant d_{t}
. In particular, by compactness of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{V}_{t})
we can choose
\delta_{t}>0
depending only on
\mathcal{A}_{t}
such that the projectivized matrix

\mathcal{H}_{t}:=\mathbb{P}(\tilde{H}_{t})
satisfies

\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d_{t}}\mathcal{C}\left(v_{i}^{(t)},\delta_{t}\right)\right)\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d_{t}}\mathcal{C}\left(W_{i}^{(t)},\delta_{t}\right)\right)^{c}

where
W_{i}^{(t)}:=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{W}_{i}^{(t)}\right)
. Set
\delta:=\min_{1\leqslant t\leqslant\kappa}\delta_{t}
. The pinching assumption on
P_{t}
(cf. Definition
4.3
) ensures that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\ell_{t}(\varepsilon)\geqslant 1
such that

(6.3)

\mathrm{P}_{t}^{\ell}\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d_{t}}\mathcal{C}\left(v_{i}^{(t)},\delta\right)\right)\subseteq\mathcal{C}\left(v_{1}^{(t)},\varepsilon\right)

holds for every
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
and every
\ell\geqslant\ell_{t}(\varepsilon)
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
take
\ell(\varepsilon):=\max_{1\leqslant t\leqslant\kappa}\ell_{t}(\varepsilon)
.

For each
q\in[p]_{1}
, we define the
rectangle
through
p
and

6.2.
Approximation of pressure and metric entropy

From the previous construction, to each typical cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
and integer
n\geqslant 1
one can associate a dominated cocycle

\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
over a compact full shift
(\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}},f)
as in Corollary
6.5
. The
topological pressure on the dominated sub-system

\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{n}
is defined by
the expression

(6.8)

P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B})):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\in\mathbb{A}_{n}}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}(I_{1}\ldots I_{k})).

Remark 6.7
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the expression (
6.8
) coincides with the topological pressure of the locally constant cocycle
\mathcal{B}
over the full shift
f:\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
(recall that the alphabet
\mathbb{A}
depends on
n
) and the potential
\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B})
. Moreover,
one can write the potential

\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B})
using the potential
\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined by

\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}:=\left(\log\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{B}),\ldots,\log\sigma_{d}(\mathcal{B})\right),

observing that

\left\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}\right\rangle=\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B})
for every

q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following result guarantees that the topological pressure of a typical cocycle is well aproximated by the topological pressure of dominated sub-systems.

Theorem 6.8
.

If
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
is a typical cocycle then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}))=P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})),

for each
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(uniformly on compact subsets).

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the finite alphabet
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{n}
determined by (
6.6
).
Applying Proposition
6.6
to the 1-step cocycle
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
, one concludes that there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa_{n}>0
such that

(6.9)

\|\mathcal{B}^{\wedge t}(IJ)\|\geqslant\kappa\,\|\mathcal{B}^{\wedge t}(I)\|\|\mathcal{B}^{\wedge t}(J)\|

for every
I,J\in\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{A}^{k}
and
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
.
We claim that the constant
\kappa
can be chosen uniform for all
n
. Indeed,
this follows from the fact that the constant
\kappa
in
Proposition
6.6
is guaranteed by the existence of a constant invariant cone-field (recall equation (
6.2
)), and that
if
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are one-dimensional cones such that
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\subset\text{int}(\overline{\mathcal{C}})
then there exists
\kappa_{0}>0
so that

\|Dv\|\geqslant\kappa_{0}\,\|D\|\,\|v\|
for every
v\in C_{0}
and every matrix
D\in\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
so that
D(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}_{0}

(cf.
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
).
Therefore, there exists
C(\kappa)>0
so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{nk}\log\sum_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\in\mathbb{A}}e^{\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{1}\ldots I_{k})\rangle}

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{nk}\log\sum_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\in\mathbb{A}}e^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(I_{\ell})\rangle-C(\kappa)k}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{I\in\mathbb{A}}e^{\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B}}(I)\rangle}-\frac{C(\kappa)}{n}}_{\text{(1)}}.

Recall that we denote
\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\mathcal{A}^{\wedge t}
for each
1\leqslant t\leqslant d-1
.
By the definition of the one-step cocycle
\mathcal{B}
and Remark
6.4
, for any
2\leqslant i\leqslant d
, given
I\in\mathbb{A}

in such a way that
[\omega_{x}]_{m_{1}(\omega_{x})+n+m_{2}(\omega_{x})}^{w}=I
for some
x\in\Sigma
,
and given
a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
so that
a_{0}=I
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{B}(I))

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\mathcal{B}^{\wedge i}(I)\|}{\|\mathcal{B}^{\wedge(i-1)}(I)\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|B_{p,\omega^{a_{0}},T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega^{a_{0}}),p}^{\wedge i}\|}{\|B_{p,\omega^{a_{0}},T^{m_{1}+n+m_{2}}(\omega^{a_{0}}),p}^{\wedge i}\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|P_{1,i}H_{1}^{i}H_{2}^{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)P_{2,i}\|}{\|P_{1,i-1}H_{1}^{i-1}H_{2}^{i-1}\mathcal{A}_{i-1}^{n}(x)P_{2,i-1}\|},

where
m_{1}=m_{1}(\omega^{a_{0}})
and
m_{2}=m_{2}(\omega^{a_{0}})
are the integers given by
Theorem
6.3
.

From the uniform continuity of the canonical holonomies

, with

for the norm of such holonomies whenever

. Therefore, for any

,
x\in\Sigma,
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant d

(6.10)

C_{0}^{-2}\|\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)\|\leqslant\|H_{1}^{i}H_{2}^{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)\|.

Therefore, combining (
6.5
) and
(
6.10
),

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{0}^{2}\;\|H_{1}^{i}H_{2}^{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)\|=C_{0}^{2}\;\|P_{1,i}^{-1}P_{1,i}H_{1}^{i}H_{2}^{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{n}(x)P_{2,i}P_{2,i}^{-1}\|

6.5

for any

\{\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{B}^{n}_{\mathbb{A}})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost additive. By
[

\mathring{\vec{L}}_{\mathbb{A}}\subset\Omega
.
Thus, since such cocycle is one-step and dominated, combining
[
40
, Theorem 5.4]

and

[
28
, Theorem 5.2]

one concludes that

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{P\left(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}})\right)-\langle q,\vec{\alpha}\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{h_{\mu}(f_{\mathbb{A}})\colon\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(f_{\mathbb{A}}),\;\chi_{i}(\mu,\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}})=\alpha_{i},\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\big\}

for every
\alpha\in\mathring{\vec{L}}_{\mathbb{A}}
, where

E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(\vec{\alpha}):=\bigg\{x\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sigma_{i}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}^{n}(x))=\alpha_{i}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\bigg\}.

The idea in the proof of Theorem
A
is to estimate the topological entropy of level sets for the original typical cocycle by the one for one-step dominated systems, using the known variational principle for induced subsystems.
Fix
\alpha\in\mathring{\vec{L}}
.
On the one hand, using
Proposition
6.10

and the previous variational principles,

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(n\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{h_{\mu}(f_{\mathbb{A}})\colon\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(f_{\mathbb{A}}),\;\chi_{i}(\mu,\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}})=n\alpha_{i},\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup\Big\{(n+2K_{0})\,h_{\nu}(T)+\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\log(2K_{0}+1)\colon

\displaystyle\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(T),\;\chi_{i}(\nu,{\mathcal{A}})\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in\Big[\frac{n}{n+2K_{0}}\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\Big],\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\Big\}.

On the other hand, using
Proposition
6.10
once more,

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(n\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\geqslant n\cdot\sup\Big\{\,h_{\nu}(T)\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(T),

\displaystyle\chi_{i}(\nu,{\mathcal{A}})\in\Big[\frac{n}{n+2K_{0}}\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\Big],\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\Big\}.

Therefore, there exists
C>0
so that, if
n\geqslant 1
is large,

(7.1)

h_{\text{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(n\vec{\alpha}))\leqslant(n+2K_{0})\;h_{\text{top}}\Big(T,\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})\Big)+C

where the set
\Theta_{n}
is formed by all vectors
\vec{\beta}
so that
\beta_{i}\in\Big[\frac{n}{n+2K_{0}}\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\Big]
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
.
Notice that
|\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}|\leqslant\frac{2K_{0}\|\vec{\alpha}\|}{n+2K_{0}}
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
.
Then, using Corollary
5.7
, we obtain

(7.2)

h_{\text{top}}\Big(T,\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})\Big)\leqslant P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\alpha_{i}q_{i}-\frac{2|q_{i}|K_{0}\|\vec{\alpha}\|}{n+2K_{0}}\right)

for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

In this way, combining the variational principle for the dominated cocycle
\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}
with inequalities (
7.1
) and (
7.2
),

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{P_{n,\mathcal{D}}\left(\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B_{\mathbb{A}}}}-n\vec{\alpha})\rangle\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=h_{\text{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(n\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\,n\,h_{\text{top}}\Big(T,\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})\Big)+{C}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\Big[n\,P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left({\alpha_{i}}q_{i}-\frac{2|q_{i}|K_{0}\|\vec{\alpha}\|}{n+2K_{0}}\right)\Big]+C.

Dividing all terms by
n
, taking the limit as

tends to infinity, and noticing that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}P_{n,\mathcal{D}}\left(\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B_{\mathbb{A}}}}-n\vec{\alpha}\rangle\right)=P\left(T,\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{{\mathcal{A}}}}-\vec{\alpha}\rangle\right)

(cf. Theorem
6.8
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{\text{top}}\Big(T,\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})\Big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}h_{\text{top}}(f_{\mathbb{A}},E^{n,\mathcal{D}}(n\vec{\alpha}))\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{\;P\left(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})\right)-\langle q,\vec{\alpha}\rangle\;\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\,h_{\nu}(T)\colon\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{inv}(T),\;\chi_{i}(\nu,{\mathcal{A}})=\alpha_{i},\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant d\Big\}

\displaystyle\leqslant h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,E(\vec{\alpha})).

As
E(\vec{\alpha})\subset\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})
for each

, one concludes that

h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,E(\vec{\alpha}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{\text{top}}\Big(T,\bigcup_{\vec{\beta}\in\Theta_{n}}E(\vec{\beta})\Big),

and the proof of the theorem is now complete.

8.
Variational principle for the pressure

In this section, we proceed to prove Theorem
B
. Let
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a fiber-bunched cocycle over a topologically mixing subshift of finite type
(\Sigma,T)
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)
. By Remark
6.9
, the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))d\mu(x)

is well-defined. We will prove the two inequalities separately.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a typical cocycle. Then,
for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))\,d\mu(x):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\}\leqslant P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})).

Proof.

We recall the following inequality (see e.g.
[
16
]
): given
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},p_{i}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}=1
one has that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\left(c_{i}-\log p_{i}\right)\leqslant\log\sum_{i=1}^{m}e^{c_{i}}.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T).
Since
\sum_{I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}}\mu([I])=1
, the inequality above implies that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}}\mu([I])\big(-\log\mu([I])+\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}(I)))\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{I\in\mathcal{L}_{n}}\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{I}),

for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By Lemma
5.3
and the fact that the limit that defines
P(\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))
does exist for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one ccncludes that

h_{\mu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int{\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))}\,d\mu(x)\leqslant P(\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})).

Since
\mu
was chosen arbitrary, we obtain the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
B
we are left to prove the converse inequality

\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))\,d\mu(x):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\}\geqslant P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A})).

We will use the existence of dominated cocycles.
For each
n\geqslant 1
let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{n}
be the
finite alphabet and let

\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}:\mathbb{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\text{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be a dominated cocycle as in Corollary
6.5
.
Since
\{\langle q,\Phi_{\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}}\rangle\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is almost additive for each
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(recall Proposition
6.6
) and the measure theoretical entropy function is upper semi-continuous we one has the varitional principle

P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}))=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(f)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}^{k}(x))d\mu(x):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(f)\bigg\},

and, for each
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the supremum is attained by a unique ergodic equilibrium state
\mu_{n}^{q}\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(f)
which has the Gibbs property
(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 10.1.9]
for more details). Therefore,

(8.1)

P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}))=h_{\mu_{n}^{q}}\left(f\right)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}^{k}(x))d\mu_{n}^{q}(x)

By Proposition
6.10
, to each
\mu_{n}^{q}\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(f)
one can associate a
T
-invariant probability measure
\nu_{n}^{q}
on
\Sigma
such that

(8.2)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}^{k}(x))d\mu_{n}^{q}(x)\leqslant(n+2K_{0})\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{k}(x))d\nu_{n}^{q}(x)

and

(8.3)

h_{\mu_{n}^{q}}(f)\leqslant(n+2K_{0})h_{\nu_{n}^{q}}(T)+\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\log(2K_{0}+1).

In consequence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}P_{n,\mathcal{D}}(\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{A}}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{var-prin-for-almost addititve1}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{n}\left(h_{\mu_{n}^{q}}\left(f\right)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi(\mathcal{B}^{k}(x))d\mu_{n}^{q}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{relation between entropies}\text{ and }\eqref{Lyapunov-exponent-relation}}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\left(h_{\nu_{n}^{q}}(T)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{k}(x))d\nu_{n}^{q}(x)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n^{2}}\log(2K_{0}+1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n}\sup\bigg\{h_{\nu}(T)+\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{k}(x))d\nu(x):\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\frac{n+2K_{0}}{n^{2}}\log(2K_{0}+1).

Taking the limit as
n\to+\infty
and recalling Theorem
6.8
we deduce

P(T,\log\Psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}))\leqslant\sup\bigg\{h_{\nu}(T)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log\psi^{q}(\mathcal{A}^{n}(x))\,d\nu(x):\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(T)\bigg\}.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
B
.

9.
Multifractal formalism of Anosov diffeomorphisms and repellers

The goal of this section is to describe a multifractal formalism for Anosov diffeomorphisms and repellers whose derivative cocycles fit our assumptions.

9.1.
Proof of Theorem
C

Assume that
f
is an Anosov diffeomorphism and that there exists a periodic point
p\in M
of period
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left.D_{p}f^{n}\right|_{E^{u}}
has simple eigenvalues of distinct norms and there exist gives homoclinic points
z_{\pm}\in M
of
p
for
f^{n}
whose holonomy loops twist the eigendirections of
\left.D_{p}f^{n}\right|_{E^{u}}
.

By existence of a Markov partition for
f

[
16
]
there exists a Hölder continuous surjection
\pi:\Sigma\rightarrow M
such that
f\circ\pi=\pi\circ\sigma
, where
\sigma:\Sigma\to\Sigma
is a topologically mixing subshift of finite type. The assumption on the periodic point
p
guarantees that, defining
the cocycle
\mathcal{A}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
by (
2.6
),
the cocycle
\mathcal{A}^{n}:\Sigma\to GL(d,\mathbb{R})
over the shift
(\Sigma,\sigma^{n})
is typical.
Then the proof follows from Theorem
A
.

9.2.
Proof of Theorem
D

The strategy in the proof of the theorem is to introduce a notion of typicality on repellers (similar to Definition
4.3
), to show that this is satisfied by a large set of repelllers and that it is a sufficient condition to obtain the variational principles.

Definition 9.1
.

We say that a
\alpha
-bunched repeller
\Lambda
associated to a
C^{1+\alpha}
-map
h
is
typical
if the following conditions are satisfied:

1)

There exists such a periodic point
p_{0}\in\Lambda
such that the eigenvalues of the matrix
A(p_{0}):=D_{p_{0}}h^{\operatorname{per}\left(p_{0}\right)}
and all its exterior powers
A(p_{0})^{\wedge t}

(1\leqslant t\leqslant d)

have multiplicity
1
and distinct absolute value;

2)

There exists a sequence of points
\left\{z_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset\Lambda
such that

z_{0}=p_{0},\;h(z_{n})=z_{n-1}\text{, and }z_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{0}

and so that, for each
1\leqslant t\leqslant d
,
the eigenvectors
\left\{v_{1}^{(t)},\ldots,v_{d_{t}}^{(t)}\right\}
of
A(p_{0})^{\wedge t}

are such that, for any
I,J\subset\{1,\ldots,d_{t}\}
with
|I|+

|J|\leqslant d_{t}
, the set of vectors

\left\{\tilde{H}_{p_{0},t}^{\left\{z_{n}\right\},-}\left(v_{i}^{(t)}\right):i\in I\right\}\cup\left\{v_{j,t}:j\in J\right\}

is linearly independent, where
d_{t}
is defined as in (
3.3
) and

(9.1)

\tilde{H}_{p_{0},t}^{\left\{z_{n}\right\},-}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left((D_{p_{0}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{n}\left((D_{z_{n}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{-1}\ldots\left((D_{z_{1}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{-1}.

As in the invertible setting, in case the periodic point
p_{0}
given by the previous definition is not fixed, we can consider a power of the
C^{1+\alpha}
-map. In this way we will always assume that
p_{0}
is a fixed point of
h
(see Remark
4.5
).

Remark 9.2
.

It is worth noticing that the choice of a pre-orbit of
p_{0}
in item (2) replaces the homoclinic loop in case of invertible maps and that the existence of the limit in (
9.1
)
is guaranteed by
C^{\alpha}
-regularity of
Dh
and the
\alpha
-bunching assumption on
\Lambda
. Indeed, given
p\in\Sigma
so that
\pi(p)=p_{0}
, an homoclinic point
z
, the identification
L(\pi p):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow T_{p_{0}}M
and the cocycle

\mathcal{C}
over
\left(\Sigma,f^{-1}\right)

(cf. (
9.4
) below), the canonical holonomy
H_{p\leftarrow z}^{s,-}
is given by

\displaystyle H_{z,p}^{s,-}=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{C}^{n}(p)^{-1}\mathcal{C}^{n}(z)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}L(\pi p)^{-1}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left[\left((D_{p_{0}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{n}\left((D_{z_{n}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{-1}\ldots\left((D_{z_{1}}h)^{\wedge t}\right)^{-1}\right]L(\pi z)}

and, since
L(\pi z)=L(\pi p)
,
H_{z\leftarrow p}^{u,-}=\text{Id}
. In particular
the holonomy loop for
\mathcal{C}
, defined by
\tilde{H}_{p\leftarrow z}^{s,-}:=H_{z,p}^{s,-}\circ H_{p,z}^{u,-}
, relates to

\tilde{H}_{p_{0},t}^{\left\{z_{n}\right\},-}

in (
9.1
) by the conjugacy relation

(9.2)

\tilde{H}_{p,t}^{\left\{z\right\},-}=L(\pi p)^{-1}\circ\tilde{H}_{p_{0},t}^{\left\{z_{n}\right\},-}\circ L(\pi p)

Remark 9.3
.

The typicality of
\Lambda
implies the typicality of the cocycle
\mathcal{C}
in the sense of Definition
4.3
. Indeed,
\mathcal{C}(p)
is equal to the inverse of
D_{p_{0}}h
up to the identification
L(\pi p):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow T_{p_{0}}M
. This implies that
\mathcal{C}(p)
satisfies the pinching condition if
D_{p_{0}}h
does. Moreover, since the eigendirections of
\mathcal{C}(p)
are equal to the eigendirections of its inverse

\tilde{H}_{p,t}^{\left\{z\right\},-}
satisfies the twisting condition if
\tilde{H}_{p_{0},t}^{\left\{z_{n}\right\},-}
does.

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
h:M\rightarrow M
be a
C^{r}
map with
r>1
. Suppose
\Lambda\subset M
is a
\alpha
-bunched repeller defined by
h
for some

satisfying

. Then there exist a
C^{1}
-open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{1}
of
h
in
C^{r}(M,M)
and a
C^{1}
-open and
C^{r}
-dense subset
\mathcal{V}_{2}
of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
such that
\Lambda_{g}
is typical for every
g\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
and

\displaystyle h_{\text{top}}(E(\vec{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\{P(g,\log\Psi^{q}(\left.Dg\right|_{\Lambda_{g}}))-\langle\vec{\alpha},q\rangle\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigg\{h_{\mu}(g):\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{inv}}(g),\chi_{i}(\mu,\left.Dg\right|_{\Lambda_{g}})=\alpha_{i}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant d\bigg\}.

for
\vec{\alpha}\in ri(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
h\in C^{r}(M,M)
be as above.
By
[
44
, Lemma 5.10]
, there exists a
C^{1}
-open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{1}
of
h
in
C^{r}(M,M)
and a
C^{1}
-open and
C^{r}
-dense subset
\mathcal{V}_{2}
of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
such that
\Lambda_{g}
is typical for every

.
Hence we are left to prove the variational principles.

Let us first recall some general facts. As
\Lambda
is a repeller, there exists a finite Markov partition
\mathcal{R}
for
\Lambda
, a one-sided subshift of finite type
(\Sigma^{+},f^{+})
,
\Sigma^{+}\subset\{1,2,\dots,q\}^{\mathbb{N}}

(9.3)

\chi:\Sigma^{+}\rightarrow\Lambda

By fixing a Markov partition of sufficiently small diameter, we may ensure that the
\chi

Consider the natural extension

\left(\Sigma,f^{-1}\right.)

Let

For each

over
\left(\Sigma,f\right)
defined in the oBvious way.

\varphi^{s}\left(\mathcal{C}^{n}\left(f^{n}(x)\right)\right)=\varphi_{\Lambda,n}^{s}(\chi(\pi(x))),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By the proof of
[

∎

We are now in a position to finish the proof of Theorem

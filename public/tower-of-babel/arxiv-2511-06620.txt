Fault-Tolerant Encoding of Logical Qudits in Spin Systems
Sumin Lim

Abstract
The universal quantum computer will enable the simulation of arbitrary quantum states governed by arbitrary Hamiltonians. In this context, it is essential to equip future quantum processors with fault-tolerant logical qudits, since qudits naturally align with the simulation of multi-level physical systems. In this study, we present a general framework and working examples of fault-tolerant logical qudit encoding using spin systems, which are among the most coherent and robust finite multi-level physical platforms. The d d -dimensional logical qudit encoding with distance- 3 3 (or 5 5 ) codewords can be designed within a 12 ​ d 12d (or 40 ​ d 40d )-dimensional Hilbert space, and the design can be further generalized to 2 ​ t + 1 2t+1 -distance codes and to encodings exploiting multiple physical qudits. A quantitative comparison shows that the logical qudit encoding proposed here offers an exponential resource advantage over multi-level mappings from logical qubits, and therefore we believe this strategy can pave the way for realizing logical qudit encodings in finite multi-level physical systems.

As shown in both comparisons, encoding of logical qudits in this work requires orders of magnitudes smaller Hilbert space volume, and implies that fault-tolerant encoding can be performed in a resource-efficient manner compared to the conventional approach. The total Hilbert space dimension also provides an upper bound for maximum decoding cases, since it indicates total error subspace considered during the error correction process. The scaling of qubit-based QEC schemes nowadays faces exponentially increasing decoding tasks, and while there are efforts from communities to establish compact and efficient decoding scheme
deMarti iOlius et al. 2024
, there is a trade-off between decoding scaling and error threshold. Thus, encoding scheme with smaller total Hilbert space can give advantage in this viewpoint. Additionally, it also reduces the required physical resources, since fewer number of spin qudits – or even a single spin qudit – can provide the entire encoding space.

Another overhead arising from qudit-to-qubit mapping is the increased gate complexity in both error correction scheme and practical information processing scheme. Regarding gate complexity of error correction process, although it is a bit subtle to directly compare the circuit depth for both cases – there is no multi-qubit/qudit gate in single qudit encoding –, the above comparsions also confirm that logical qudit mapping requires orders of magnitudes fewer quantum gates, as it remains within the polynomial range of computation space. Regarding gate complexity for the information processing, while there is vast ongoing research in efficient decomposition of qudit-based gates into qubit-based gates
Kiktenko et al. 2025
, recent reports claim that qudit to qubit mapping still requires extra resources, such as additional entangling gates in Hamiltonian exponentian
Fischer et al. 2023
;
Chizzini et al. 2024
, or increased circuit depth in Grover’s algorithm implementations
Rambow and Tian
, which also support direct use of logical qudits offers advantages in practical algorithm implementation steps.

We also tested model that account for single qudit gate fidelity and decoherence during encoding/decoding process, to check practical conditions to obtain advantage from the error correction protocol. As shown in the comparison between several gate fidelities and single gate duration parameters, such as single gate fidelity of

Appendix B
The coefficients for logical qudit encoding code words

In this section, we provide a brief formalism for constructing logical qudit with a general qudit dimension
d
and code-word distance
2t+1
. As a working example, for the first-order (i.e., distance-3) Z-error correction code, each logical qudit state can be written as,

\begin{split}\lvert 0_{L}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}(\left\lvert-(S/2+1/4)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(S/2+1/4)\right\rangle)\\
\lvert i_{L}\rangle=a_{i}(\left\lvert-(S/2+1/4-i)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(S/2+1/4-i)\right\rangle)+\\
b_{i}(\left\lvert-(S/2+1/4+i)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(S/2+1/4i+i)\right\rangle),\\
\end{split}

(14)

where
S=2d-3/2
, and
i=1,2,\cdots,d-1
. Each
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
coefficients are must be carefully chosen to satisfy the KL criteria (in Eq.
2
). For the first-order Z-error case, the nontrivial conditions in KL criteria can be summaarized as following three sets: 1)
\langle i_{L}\rvert\lvert i_{L}\rangle=1
(Normalization), 2)
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
, 3)
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
, for
i,j=0,1,2
. It follows straightforwardly that the coefficients
b_{i}
are directly determind from
a_{i}
using normalization conditions, the first-order projection condtions are satisfied by symmetry, and the second-order projection conditions constrains
a_{i}
coefficients thorugh the relation of
\langle 0_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert 0_{L}\rangle=\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle
. Therefore, total angular momentum
S=2d-3/2
yields a valid solution, as shown in Eq.
16
.

To construct the distance-5 code, we doubled the number of
S_{Z}
-basis states by duplicating the distribution used in the distance-3 code and placing it in the higher
S_{Z}
basis space. For example, the distance-5 code for a qutrit retains the code word distribution from
\lvert 1/2\rangle\cdots\lvert 9/2\rangle
and adds an similar set to
\lvert 11/2\rangle\cdots\lvert 19/2\rangle
.

In this distance-5 case, the codewords must satisfy KL conditions of
\langle i_{L}\rvert\lvert i_{L}\rangle=1
, and
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{n}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{n}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
, for
n=1,2,3,4
. The general form of distance-5 code for
d
-dimesional qudit can be written as,

\begin{split}\lvert 0_{L}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{5}{16}}(\left\lvert-(S/4+1/8)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(S/4+1/8)\right\rangle)\\
+\sqrt{\frac{3}{16}}(\left\lvert-(3S/4+3/8)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(3S/4+3/8)\right\rangle)\\
\lvert i_{L}\rangle=a_{i}(\left\lvert-(2d-1/2-i)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(2d-1/2-i)\right\rangle)\\
+b_{i}(\left\lvert-(2d-3/2+i)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(2d-3/2+i)\right\rangle)\\
+c_{i}(\left\lvert-(i-1/2)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(i-1/2)\right\rangle),\\
+d_{i}(\left\lvert-(4d-3/2-i)\right\rangle+\left\lvert+(4d-3/2-i)\right\rangle),\\
\end{split}

(15)

where
S=4d-5/2
, and
i=1,2,\cdots,d-1
. The cofficients that satisfy the above KL criteria are obtained as,

\begin{split}a_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{8d+2i-5}{32d-16}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{66d^{3}+2d^{2}i-100d^{2}-14di^{2}+12di+47d+2i^{3}+4i^{2}-5i-7}{96d^{3}+32d^{2}i-160d^{2}-16di^{2}-16di+84d+8i^{2}-14}}\\
b_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{8d-2i-3}{32d-16}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{66d^{3}+2d^{2}i-100d^{2}-14di^{2}+12di+47d+2i^{3}+4i^{2}-5i-7}{96d^{3}+32d^{2}i-160d^{2}-16di^{2}-16di+84d+8i^{2}-14}}\\
c_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{12d-2i-5}{32d-16}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{36d^{3}+30d^{2}i-60d^{2}-2di^{2}-28di+37d-2i^{3}+4i^{2}+5i-7}{96d^{3}+32d^{2}i-160d^{2}-16di^{2}-16di+84d+8i^{2}-14}}\\
d_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{4d+2i-3}{32d-16}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{36d^{3}+30d^{2}i-60d^{2}-2di^{2}-28di+37d-2i^{3}+4i^{2}+5i-7}{96d^{3}+32d^{2}i-160d^{2}-16di^{2}-16di+84d+8i^{2}-14}}.\\
\end{split}

(16)

Although there are five sets of conditions in the KL criteria, the odd-order of error projection terms (
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{3}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{3}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
) are satisfied from symmetry. Therefore, it is sufficient to check only the remaining even-order terms,
\langle i_{L}\rvert\lvert i_{L}\rangle=1
,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
, and
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{4}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{4}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
. As a result, the code-word design with the four free parameters described above satisfies all KL criteria.

It is worth pointing out that the distance-5 code for qutrit, as well as for general
d
-dimensional qudits, is not unique. As a simple example, we provide an alternative set of coefficients, as shown below,

\begin{split}\lvert 0_{L}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{3}{10}}(\left\lvert-\frac{5}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{5}{2}\right\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{1}{5}}(\left\lvert-\frac{15}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{15}{2}\right\rangle)\\
\lvert 1_{L}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1152}{9225}}(\left\lvert-\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{133}{1025}}(\left\lvert-\frac{9}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{9}{2}\right\rangle)\\
\sqrt{\frac{399}{2050}}(\left\lvert-\frac{11}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{11}{2}\right\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{468}{9225}}(\left\lvert-\frac{19}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{19}{2}\right\rangle)\\
\lvert 2_{L}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1081}{7700}}(\left\lvert-\frac{3}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{3}{2}\right\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{252}{1650}}(\left\lvert-\frac{7}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{7}{2}\right\rangle)\\
\sqrt{\frac{441}{3300}}(\left\lvert-\frac{13}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{13}{2}\right\rangle)+\sqrt{\frac{282}{3850}}(\left\lvert-\frac{17}{2}\right\rangle+\left\lvert+\frac{17}{2}\right\rangle).\end{split}

(17)

Similar to the logical qubit construction using qudits in Ref.
Lim et al. 2023
, this non-uniqueness indicates that further investigation is required to identify the mathematically minimal (i.e., most compact) dimension that enables fault-tolerant encoding.

For general code-word distance of
2t+1
with
t\geq 3
, similar expansion – which doubles the encoding
S_{Z}
-basis in the same manner as the distance-3 to distance-5 construction – provides sufficient Hilbert space and degrees of freeedom to satisfy KL criteria, i.e.,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{n}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{n}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
up to
n=0,1,2\cdots 2t
, by appropriately tailoring the coefficients. Here we do not offer a proof, or a general analytical solution demonstrating that this approach works for arbitrary
2t+1
distance and arbitray qudit dimension
d
, though we find it works for wide range of cases that we have explored.

We presented a general formalism for extending the
Z
-error-correcting code to full
X/Y/Z
error correction. This was achieved by incresing basis dimension by a factor of
2t+1
times (i.e., a threefold increase from 9/2 code to 29/2 code and a fivefold increase from 19/2 code to 99/2), or by increasing number of qudits
2t+1
times (i.e., three-9/2 qudits code or five-19/2 qudits code). In both approaches, the same set of coefficients used for the
Z
-error correction remained valid for constructing the corresponding
2t+1
-distance
X/Y/Z
error correcting code.

As a working example, we briefly desrcribe why the code-words in Eq.
1
can also be extended to correct X/Y/Z errors. The Eq.
1
satisfies the conditions
\langle i_{L}\rvert\lvert i_{L}\rangle=1
,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
, for
i,j=0,1,2
. Therefore, the threefold expanded code-words in Eq.
8
already satisfies above three conditions, since the expansion corresponds to a linear increase of the basis in the
S_{Z}
-representation. The orthogonality conditions in the KL criteria (
\langle i_{L}\rvert E_{a}E_{B}\lvert j_{L}\rangle=0
) are also fulfilled due to the
2t+1
-fold spacing between the basis, for any
E_{a},E_{b}
corresponding to X, Y, or Z operator. Thus, the remaining nontrivial terms that must be verified are
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{X}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{X}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle
and
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Y}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{Y}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle

In the case of
S_{X}^{2}
projection, as
S_{X}
can be expressed in
S_{X}=\frac{1}{2}(S_{+}+S_{-})
, equation can be rewritten as
\langle i_{L}\rvert(S_{+}+S_{-})(S_{+}+S_{-})\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert(S_{+}+S_{-})(S_{+}+S_{-})\lvert j_{L}\rangle

Due to the spacing between the
S_{Z}
-basis, the only remaining nontrivial terms are
\langle i_{L}\rvert(S_{+}S_{-}+S_{-}S_{+})\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert(S_{+}S_{-}+S_{-}S_{+})\lvert j_{L}\rangle

Using the relations for the spin ladder operators,

\begin{split}S_{+}\lvert S,m\rangle=\sqrt{S(S+1)-m(m+1)}\lvert S,m+1\rangle\\
S_{-}\lvert S,m\rangle=\sqrt{S(S+1)-m(m-1)}\lvert S,m-1\rangle,\end{split}

(18)

if we decompose
\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\sum{c_{m}\lvert S,m\rangle}

\begin{split}\langle i_{L}\rvert(S_{+}S_{-}+S_{-}S_{+})\lvert i_{L}\rangle=2S(S+1)-2\sum{\langle S,m\rvert m^{2}\lvert c_{m}\rvert^{2}\lvert S,m\rangle}=2S(S+1)-2\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{Z}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle.\end{split}

(19)

Therefore,
\langle i_{L}\rvert S_{X}^{2}\lvert i_{L}\rangle=\langle j_{L}\rvert S_{X}^{2}\lvert j_{L}\rangle

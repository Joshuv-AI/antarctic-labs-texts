Modulus of continuity for solutions to complex Monge-Ampère equations on Hermitian manifolds
Junbang Liu

Abstract
. In this note, we give a proof of the uniform log-continuity of the solution to complex Monge-Ampère equations on compact Hermitian manifolds, which is a generalization of the result of [ 13 ] in the Kähler case.



1.
introduction

Let
(M^{n},\omega_{M})
be a
n
-dim compact Hermitian manifold with a fixed Hermitian metric
\omega_{M}
. Let
F\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a smooth real function. Then it’s proved by Tosatti and Weinkove in
[
21
]
that the following volume conjecture holds: there exists an unique
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
and unique constant
c
such that

(1.1)

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=e^{F+c}\omega_{M}^{n},\quad\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u>0,\quad\sup_{M}u=0.

The theorem we proved in this note is

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
p>n
, for any
0<\alpha<\frac{p}{n}-1
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
M,\omega_{M},p,\alpha
, and upper bound of
\int_{M}(|F|^{p}+1)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}
, positive lower bound of
\int_{M}e^{\frac{1}{n}F}\omega_{M}^{n}
, such that solutions to (
1.1
) satisfies

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\log dist_{\omega_{M}}(x,y)|^{\alpha}}.

It’s a generalization of the theorem in
[
13
,
10
]
to the Hermitian manifolds.
We remark that in the Kähler case, the exponent
\alpha
in the
\log
-continuity can be
\frac{p}{n}-1
. The Hölder regularity of the solutions to complex Monge-Ampère equation on compact Hermitian manifolds have been studied by many authors, see
[
19
,
15
]
and the reference therein, after the internsively developed pluripotential theorey on Hermitian manifolds(see
[
8
,
20
,
16
]
). Here we use a purely PDE method developed by Guo-Phong-Tong in
[
12
]
.

In the following, we say a constant is uniform if it depends on the parameters stated in the above theorem.

2.
stability estimate

The following stability estimate is crucial to our proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
v\in Psh(\omega_{M})\cap C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}^{+})
, there exists a uniform constant
C
additionally depending on
||v||_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\sup(v-u)\leq\frac{C}{|\log(||(v-u)_{+}||_{L^{1}})|^{\alpha}}.

To begin with, we need a lemma to estimate the constant
c
under the assumption of a weaker bound on the volume density.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(u,c)
be a smooth solution to equation (
1.1
) and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a positive increasing function with
\int_{1}^{\infty}\Phi^{-\frac{1}{n}}(t)dt<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}
depending on
M,\omega_{M},\Phi
and upper bounds for
\int_{M}\Phi(F)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n},
and positive lower bound of
\int_{M}e^{\frac{F}{n}}\omega_{M}^{n}
, such that

e^{c}+e^{-c}+||u||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}.

Proof.

The lower bound
c
is proved in
[
18
]
by the complex ABP estimate. The upper bound of
c
is proved in
[
16
]
lemma 5.9, and it depends on the positive lower bound of
\int_{M}e^{\frac{F}{n}}\omega_{M}^{n},M,\omega_{M}
. We only need to prove the
L^{\infty}
-estimate. Here, we introduce another method to construct a good comparison metric. We cover the manifold
(M,\omega_{M})
by coordinates balls
\{B(x_{i},2r_{i})|i=1,\cdots,n\}
such that

B_{i}:=B(x_{i},r_{i})
still covers
M
. Let
\rho_{i}
be smooth functions on
M
, such that
2B_{i}=\{\rho_{i}\leq 0\}
and
\rho_{i}=|z|^{2}-4r_{i}^{2}
on
2B_{i}
. On each
2B_{i}
, by the work of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
2
]
, one can solve the following equation

(2.1)

(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{i})^{n}=\Phi(F)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n},\quad\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{i}>0,\quad\psi_{i}|_{\partial 2B_{i}}=-1.

By the complex ABP estimate in
[
18
]
,
||\psi_{i}||_{L^{\infty}}
is uniformly bounded by a constant
C_{1}
. Then by choosing
C_{2}
large enough with
3C_{2}r_{i}^{2}>C_{1}+1
and
\epsilon
small, let
\tilde{\psi}_{i}:=\max_{\epsilon}\{C_{2}\rho_{i},\psi_{i}\}
, where
\max_{\epsilon}
is the regularized max function, c.f. lemma 5.18 in Demailly’s book
[
5
]
. Then the function
\tilde{\psi}_{i}
satisfies:

•

\tilde{\psi}_{i}=\psi_{i}
on
B_{i}
(since
C_{2}\rho_{i}<-C_{1}-1<\psi_{i}
on
B_{i}
with our choice of
C_{2}
);

•

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\tilde{\psi}_{i}\geq-C_{3}\omega_{M}
on
M
, for some uniform constant
C_{3}
depending on
M,\omega_{M}
(since
\rho_{i}
is smooth on
M
, and
\tilde{\psi}_{i}=\rho_{i}
in a neighborhood of
M\setminus 2B_{i}
).

Let
\psi:=\frac{1}{C_{3}N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\psi}_{i}
.
Therefore, one has on each
B_{i}
,

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}\geq\frac{1}{(NC_{3})^{n}}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{i})^{n}\geq\epsilon_{0}\Phi(F)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}

for some uniform constant
\epsilon_{0}
. Once one has such a comparison metric, the proof of
L^{\infty}
-estimate follows from
[
17
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

It’s interesting to know whether the constant c can be estimated from below when we remove the condition
\int_{1}^{\infty}\Phi^{-\frac{1}{n}}<\infty
? If so, it can be applied to get Trudinger type estimates for solutions to Monge-Ampère equations on Hermitian manifolds like in the Kähler case.

With this lemma, we can construct appropriate comparison Hermitian metrics to reduce the proof to
L^{p}

Let
\Phi(x)=x^{n\alpha}
for

\mathbb{R}

\int_{M}(|\log\Phi(F)+F|^{p-n\alpha}+1)\Phi(F)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}\leq C\int_{M}(|F|+1)^{p}e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}.

Let
\psi_{1}

(2.2)

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1})^{n}=e^{c_{1}}\Phi(F)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n},\quad\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}>0,\quad\sup\psi_{1}=0.

Then by lemma
2.2

and

is uniformly bounded from above by a uniform constant
C_{0}
. Let
\alpha_{M}

.
For any
q,\Lambda_{1}>0,a\in(0,1)

, and
\psi_{2}=-h(-\frac{\alpha_{M}}{q}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1})
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\sup(-\frac{\alpha_{M}}{q}\psi)\leq C_{0}
, so in this case, we get that
\psi_{2}
is a nonnegative function. We have

Lemma 2.4
.

The inequality holds

(2.3)

\omega_{u}^{n}\leq a^{n}\omega_{\psi_{2}}^{n}+f\omega_{M}^{n},

with
f=\exp{(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1}+c)}
and
\Lambda_{1}
is chosen so that
\Phi(\Lambda_{1})^{\frac{1}{n}}=\frac{1}{a}e^{\frac{c-c_{1}}{n}}.

Proof.

From our choice of
\Lambda_{1}
, we have
\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(\Lambda_{1})=1
. Note that
h
is an increasing concave function, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{2}=

\displaystyle\omega_{M}+\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{q^{2}}h^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{-1}\partial\psi_{1}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi_{1}\geq\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{1})

In the above inequality we have used that
\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1})\leq\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(\Lambda_{1})=1.

Hence

\omega_{u}^{n}=e^{F+c}\omega_{M}^{n}\leq\frac{q^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}}\frac{e^{c}}{e^{c_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Phi(F)}\omega_{\psi_{2}}^{n}.

Next, for any
a\in(0,1)
,
if
\frac{q^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}}\frac{e^{c}}{e^{c_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Phi(F)}\leq a^{n}
, we get

\omega_{u}^{n}\leq a^{n}\omega_{\psi_{2}}.

Otherwise, we have
\frac{q^{n}}{\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}}\frac{e^{c}}{e^{c_{1}}}\frac{1}{\Phi(F)}>a^{n}
, which is equivalent to
\Phi(F)<\frac{q^{n}}{a^{n}\alpha^{n}}\frac{1}{h^{\prime n}(-\alpha q^{-1}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1})}e^{c-c_{1}}=\Phi(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1}),
where we use the definition of
h
in the last equality. From the monotonicity of
\Phi
, we get
F\leq-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}+\Lambda_{1}
. So, in this case, we have
e^{F+c}\leq f
with
f
stated in the lemma.
∎

For

and
{p_{1}}>1

Lemma 2.5
.

The inequality holds:

\tilde{u}-s\leq\epsilon_{3}(\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}

\epsilon_{3}=\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}e^{-c_{s,j,{p_{1}}}}A_{s,j,{p_{1}}}^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\quad\Lambda_{3}=\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{A_{s,j,{p_{1}}}}{a^{n+1}}e^{-(n+1)c_{s,j,{p_{1}}}}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume the maximum of
\tilde{u}-s-\epsilon_{3}(-\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\tilde{u}+\epsilon_{3}\frac{n}{n+1}(-\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{3}+\epsilon\frac{n^{2}}{n+1}\sqrt{-1}\partial\psi_{3}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi_{3}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\omega_{u}+(1-2a)\omega_{v}+a\omega_{\psi_{2}}+\epsilon_{3}\frac{n}{n+1}(-\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\omega_{\psi_{3}}+(a-\epsilon_{3}\frac{n}{n+1}(\Lambda_{3})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}})\omega_{M}

From our choice of

\displaystyle\omega_{u}^{n}\geq

\displaystyle a^{n}\omega_{\psi}^{n}+\left(\epsilon_{3}\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n}(-\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}\omega_{\psi_{3}}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{n}\omega_{\psi}^{n}+\left(\epsilon_{3}\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n}\frac{\eta_{j}(\tilde{u}-s)}{(-\psi_{3}+\Lambda_{3})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}}\frac{e^{c_{s,j,{p_{1}}}}f\omega_{M}^{n}}{A_{s,j,{p_{1}}}}

.

(1)

As this note was being written, we note that Fang also proved similar stability results in

F
with
\int_{M}e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}=1
. In some cases, one has a positive lower bound of
v_{-}(\omega_{M})
, for instance, when the manifold admits a nef and big closed real
(1,1)
-form
\beta
, see Lemma 9 in
[
12
]
.

3.
log-continuity

Now we introduce the approximation of plurisubharmonic functions we need. Let
\rho:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth kernel such that
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\rho(|w|)dV(w)=1
and
\rho(t)=1
when
t\leq\frac{3}{4}
and has compact support in the unit ball. One defines

\rho_{\delta}u(x):=\frac{1}{\delta^{2n}}\int_{T_{x}M}u(\mathrm{exph}_{x}(\zeta))\rho(\frac{|\zeta|_{\omega_{x}}^{2}}{\delta^{2}})dV_{\omega_{x}}(\zeta),

where
\mathrm{exph}
is the holomorphic part of the exponential map with respect to the Chern connection associated with Hermitian metric
\omega
. For details about this holomorphic part of the exponential map, see
[
6
]
proposition 2.9.

We list some properties of
\rho_{\delta}u
we need in the proof as follows:

Proposition 3.1

(Berman-Demailly
[
1
]
, lemma 1.2 in see also
[
10
]
)
.

Let
u\in psh(M,\omega)
with normalization
1\leq u\leq C
. We have

(1)

There exists a constant
K>0
depends on the curvature of
\omega
, such that
\rho_{t}u+Kt
is monotone increasing in
t
.

(2)

For any
c_{1}>0
, define
U_{c_{1},\delta}=\inf_{t\in(0,\delta)}(\rho_{t}u(x)+Kt-c_{1}\log(\frac{t}{\delta})-K\delta)
, then

\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}U_{c_{1},\delta}\geq-(Ac_{1}+K\delta)\omega,

where
-A
is the lower bound of the bidectional curvature of
\omega
.

(3)

\int_{M}|\rho_{\delta}u-u|\omega^{n}\leq C(n,\omega)\delta^{2},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1].

With this approximation lemma, one can prove that

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a uniform constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
\delta<\delta_{1}
, one has

(3.1)

|\rho_{\delta}u(x)-u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}},

Proof.

One side of the inequality
\rho_{\delta}u-u\geq-K\delta
follows from proposition
3.1
that
\rho_{t}u+Kt
is increasing in
t
. We prove the other side as follows:

We choose a small
c_{1}=|\log\delta|^{-\alpha}
, then
c_{1}
is large compared with
\delta
. So
\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}U_{c_{1},\delta}\geq-A^{\prime}c_{1}\omega_{M}
. Hence we can define a
\omega_{M}
-psh function
u_{c_{1},\delta}:=(1-A^{\prime}c_{1})U_{c_{1},\delta}
. It follows that
\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{c_{1},\delta}\geq(A^{\prime}c_{1})^{2}\omega_{M}\geq 0.
And from the normalization of
u
we have
u_{c_{1},\delta}>0
. From our definition of
u_{c_{1},\delta}
, we have
u_{c_{1},\delta}\leq U_{c_{1},\delta}\leq\rho_{\delta}u
, and hence

(3.2)

||(u_{c_{1},\delta}-u)_{+}||_{L^{1}}\leq||(\rho_{\delta}u-u)_{+}||_{L^{1}}\leq C\delta^{2}.

By proposition
2.1
, we have

u_{c_{1},\delta}(x)-u(x)\leq\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}}.

From the definition of
u_{c_{1},\delta}=(1-Ac_{1})U_{c_{1},\delta}
and

U_{c_{1},\delta}=\inf_{t\in(0,\delta]}\{\rho_{t}u+Kt-c_{1}\log(\frac{t}{\delta})-K\delta\},

one gets there exists
t_{x}\in(0,\delta]
such that

\rho_{t_{x}}u(x)+Kt_{x}-c_{1}\log(\frac{t_{x}}{\delta})-K\delta=U_{c_{1},\delta}(x)\leq u(x)+A^{\prime}c_{1}U_{c_{1},\delta}(x)+2ru_{c_{1},\delta}+\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}}.

Since
\rho_{t}u+Kt
is increasing in
t
, we have
\rho_{t_{x}}u(x)+Kt_{x}\geq u(x)
.
So

-\log(\frac{t_{x}}{\delta})\geq K\frac{\delta}{c}+A^{\prime}U_{c_{1},\delta}+C.

From the choice of
c_{1}
we have

-\log\frac{t_{x}}{\delta}\geq-C.

In other words, there exists a uniform constant
\theta
such that
t_{x}\geq\theta\delta
. So using the fact that
\rho_{t}u+KT
is increasing in
t
again, and the observation
\delta\leq c_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta\delta}+K\theta t-u\leq

\displaystyle\rho_{t_{x}}+Kt_{x}-u

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\log(\frac{t_{x}}{\delta})+K\delta+A^{\prime}c_{1}U_{c_{1},\delta}+\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}}.

It implies that there is a universal constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\forall\delta\leq\delta_{1}
, we have

\rho_{\delta}u(x)-u(x)\leq\frac{C}{|\log\delta|^{\alpha}}.

∎

Next, one can improve the integral estimate above to the modulus of continuity in a standard way as in
[
7
,
19
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we include it here.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
|\rho_{\delta}u(x)-u(x)|\leq C|\log\delta|^{-\alpha}
for all
x\in M
, and for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{1}
, then there exists uniform constants
\delta_{2},C>0
such that

(3.3)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\log\mathrm{dist}_{\omega_{M}}(x,y)|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\delta\leq\delta_{2}.

Proof.

Set
\omega(\delta):=\sup_{\mathrm{dist}_{\omega_{M}}(x,y)\leq\delta}(u(x)-u(y)).
We argue by contradiction.
If the lemma is false, there exists a sequence of points
x_{i},y_{i}\in M
and
\delta_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that

(3.4)

\mathrm{dist}_{\omega_{M}}(x_{i},y_{i})\leq\delta_{i},\quad\omega(\delta_{i})=u(x_{i})-u(y_{i}),\quad\omega(\delta_{i})|\log\delta_{i}|^{\alpha}\rightarrow\infty.

Since
M
is compact, we can assume
x_{i},y_{i}\rightarrow x_{0}
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Let
B
be a small neighborhood of
x_{0}
which can identified as a unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and in
B
one has
(1-\epsilon_{0})\mathrm{dist}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(x,y)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{\omega_{M}}(x,y)\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})\mathrm{dist}_{g_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}}(x,y)
. By adding a smooth function, one can assume that
u
is a plurisubharmonic function in
B
. Fix
b>1
, which will be specified later,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{i},(b+1)a)}u(x)dV(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(y_{i},b\delta_{i})}u(x)dV(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|B(x_{i},b+1)\delta_{i})}\int_{B(x_{i},(b+1)\delta_{i})\setminus B(y_{i},b\delta_{i})}u(x)dV(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}}u(y_{i})+(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})(u(y_{i})-\omega((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(y_{i})-(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\omega((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}).

Here we have used the mean value inequality for
u
and the fact
dist_{\omega_{M}}(x,y_{i})\leq(1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}
for
x\in B(x_{i},(b+1)\delta_{i})\setminus B(y_{i},b\delta_{i}),
in the second line.
On the other hand

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{i},2(b+1)\delta_{i})}u(x)\rho(\frac{|x-x_{i}|^{2}}{(2(b+1)\delta_{i})^{2}})dV(x)-u(x_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\partial B(0,1)|\int_{0}^{1}(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\partial B(x_{i},2(b+1)t)}ud\sigma(x)-u(x_{i}))t^{2n-1}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\partial B(0,1)|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{i},(b+1)a)}u(x)dV(x)-u(x_{i}))t^{2n-1}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(u(y_{i})-u(x_{i})-(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\omega((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\omega(\delta_{i})-(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\omega((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i})).

From the expansion of
\mathrm{exph}
(Remark 4.6 in
[
6
]
), we have

(3.7)

\rho_{\delta}u(x)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{i},\delta)}u(x)\rho(\frac{|x-x_{i}|^{2}}{\delta_{i}^{2}})dV(x)+O(\delta^{2}).

So the assumption in the lemma implies

(3.8)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{i},2(b+1)\delta_{i})}u(x)\rho(\frac{|x-x_{i}|^{2}}{(2(b+1)\delta_{i})^{2}})dV(x)-u(x)\leq C|\log(2(b+1)\delta_{i})|^{-\alpha}.

Combine (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), we get

(3.9)

\omega(\delta_{i})-(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\omega((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}))\leq C|\log(2(b+1)\delta_{i})|^{-\alpha}.

Set
\tau(\delta):=\omega(\delta)|\log(\delta)|^{\alpha}
. Then

(3.10)

\tau(\delta_{i})\leq(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\frac{|\log(\delta_{i})|^{\alpha}}{|\log((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i})|^{\alpha}}\tau((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}))+C

Choose
b
large such that
(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\leq\frac{1}{2}
and choose
\delta_{1}
small enough, then

(1-\frac{b^{2n}}{(b+1)^{2n}})\frac{|\log(\delta_{i})|^{\alpha}}{|\log((1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2)\delta_{i}}<\frac{3}{4}.

Then

(3.11)

\tau(\delta_{i})\leq\frac{3}{4}\tau(\gamma\delta_{i})+C,\quad\gamma=(1+\epsilon_{0})(b+2).

Iterate this inequality, we get a contradiction with
\tau(\delta_{i})\rightarrow\infty
.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

We remark that when one assumes a uniform bound of
\int_{M}\log^{p}(|F|+1)(|F|+1)^{n}e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}
, for some
p>n
, then with a similar argument, one can prove a uniform modulus of continuity of
\log\log
-type:

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\frac{C}{(\log|\log(\mathrm{dist}_{\omega_{M}}(x,y))|)^{\alpha}}.

A corollary of the
\log
-continuity is the diameter bound of the metric. The proof of lemma 5 in
[
13
]
shows:

Corollary 3.5
.

Fix
p>3n
, let
u
be a smooth solution to equation (
1.1
), then the diameter of metric
\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u
is bounded uniformly in terms of
M,\omega_{M},n,p
, an upper bound of
\int_{M}(|F|^{p}+1)e^{F}
and a positive lower bound of
\int_{M}e^{\frac{1}{n}F}\omega_{M}^{n}.

4.
Extending to bounded solutions

In this section, we want to prove that any bounded solution to the equation (
1.1
) in the sense of Bedford-Taylor also satisfies the log-continuity stated in the main theorem. We follow the trick of Lu-Phung-Tô
[
19
]
to reduce it to the following stability estimate:

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
e^{F},e^{G}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Assume
u,v
are
\omega
-psh functions on
M
satisfying

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=e^{u+F}\omega_{M}^{n}\quad\text{and }\quad(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}v)^{n}=e^{v+G}\omega_{M}^{n}.

Then there exists a constant
C
depending on
M,\omega_{M}
,
n,p
, an upper bounds for
\int_{M}(|F|^{p}+1)e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}
,
\int_{M}(|G|^{p}+1)e^{G}\omega_{M}^{n}
, and a positive lower bound for
\int_{M}e^{\frac{F}{n}}\omega_{M}^{n}
,
\int_{M}e^{\frac{G}{n}}\omega_{M}^{n}
, such that

||u-v||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}^{\frac{1}{n}}

Here, we will use the notation that

||e^{F}||_{\Phi}:=\inf\{t>0|\int_{M}\Phi(F-\log t)e^{F-\log t}\omega_{M}^{n}\leq 1\}.

The norm
||e^{F}||_{\Phi}
is equivalent to the Orlicz norm of
e^{F}
with the weight grows like
x\Phi(\log x).
For simplicity, we fix a increasing function
\Phi(x):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\Phi(x)=x^{p}
for some
p>n
when
x>1
.
Such form of stability estimate is easier in the Kähler case for equation
(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{M}^{n}
because the comparison principle holds. On Hermitian manifolds, a modified comparison principle was proved by Kolodziej-Cuong and they used it to prove a stability estimate with an additional non-degeneracy assumption that
e^{F}>c_{0}>0
. But for the equation
(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u)^{n}=e^{u+F}\omega_{M}^{n}
, one can remove the nondegeneracy conditions on
e^{F}
(
[
19
]
).

Proof.

First, we note that the solution
u,v
are uniformly bounded: We consider the following equation:

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi)^{n}=e^{F+c_{F}}\omega_{M}^{n},\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi>0,\sup\psi=0.

By lemma
2.2
, we know that
e^{c_{F}}+e^{-c_{F}}+||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}
for some uniform constant
C_{0}
. Then we can apply the maximum principle to get the uniform estimate for
u
as follows: let
x_{0}
be the point where
u-\varphi
attains its maximum, then at
x_{0}
, we have

0\geq\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(u-\psi)=\omega_{u}-\omega_{\psi}.

Combining with the equation we get
e^{u+F}\leq e^{c_{F}+F}
, which implies
u(x_{0})\leq e^{c_{F}}
. It follows that

\max u\leq\max(u-\psi)+\max\psi\leq u(x_{0})-\min\psi\leq 2C_{0}.

Similary one can prove that
u\geq-2C_{0}
. So let
C_{1}
be a uniform upper bound for
||u||_{L^{\infty}},||v||_{L^{\infty}}
.

We consider the following auxiliary equation

(\omega_{M}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{c_{F,G}}\left(\frac{|e^{F}-e^{G}|}{||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}}+1\right)\omega_{M}^{n}

Then it is clear that the
L^{1}\Phi(\log L)
-integral of
\frac{|e^{F}-e^{G}|}{||e^{F}-e^{F}||_{\Phi}}
is bounded by
1
from our definition of
||\cdot||_{\Phi}
. Therefore from lemma
2.2
, we have
e^{c_{F,G}}+e^{-c_{F,G}}+||\varphi||_{L^{\infty}}
is uniformly bounded, say by
C_{2}
.

Set
\epsilon=e^{(C_{1}-C_{2})/n}||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}.
If
\epsilon\leq\frac{1}{2},
we’d like to apply maximum principle to the function

w=(1-\epsilon)u+\epsilon\varphi-C_{1}\epsilon+n\log(1-\epsilon),

By our choice of
\epsilon
, we have
\frac{\epsilon^{n}e^{c_{F,G}}}{||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}}=e^{C_{1}+c_{F,G}-C_{2}}\geq e^{u+n\log(1-\epsilon)}
, and for
w
we also have
w\leq u+n\log(1-\epsilon)
. Therefore we can compute

\omega_{w}^{n}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{n}\omega_{u}^{n}+\epsilon^{n}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}\geq e^{u+n\log(1-\epsilon)}e^{G}\omega_{M}^{n}\geq e^{w}\omega_{M}^{n}.

Apply the maximum principle to
w-v
: at the maximum point of
w-v
, say
x_{0}
, we have
\omega_{w}\leq\omega_{v}
, which imples

e^{w+G}\leq e^{v+G},

so
w\leq v.
From the expression of
w
, we get

u-v\leq C_{3}\epsilon

for a uniform constant
C_{3}
. Similarly, one can prove the reverse
v-u\leq C_{3}\epsilon
.
In summary, we get in the case of
\epsilon=e^{(C_{1}-C_{2})/n}||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have
|u-v|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{4}||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}^{\frac{1}{n}}
, for a uniform constant
C_{4}
.
When,
\epsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it’s much easier since we have a uniform lower positive lower bound of
||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}
, and enlarge the constant
C_{4}
if necessary, we still get

|u-v|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{4}||e^{F}-e^{G}||_{\Phi}^{\frac{1}{n}}

∎

With such a stability lemma, one can pass the previous log-continuity in theorem
1.1
to the bounded solution
u
. Assume the integral bounds for
F
as in theorem
1.1
, and let
u
be a bounded solution (in the Bedford-Taylor sense) to equation

\omega_{u}^{n}=e^{F+c}\omega_{M}^{n},\omega_{u}\geq 0,\sup u=0.

Let
G_{j}
be a sequence of smooth function such that
G_{j}\leq G_{j+1}\to-u+F+c
pointwisely. Then we have

\int_{M}\Phi(G_{j})e^{G_{j}}\omega_{M}^{n}\leq\int_{M}\Phi(-u+F+c)e^{-u+F+c}\omega_{M}^{n}\leq C,

for a uniform constant
C
. And by dominant convergence theorem,
\int_{M}e^{G_{j}/n}\omega^{n}\to\int_{M}e^{-u+F+c}\omega_{M}^{n}
. So we have a uniform positive lower bound of
\int_{M}e^{G_{j}/n}\omega^{n}
. Moreover, we claim that
||e^{G_{j}}-e^{-u+F+c}||_{\Phi}\rightarrow 0
. For simplicity, we let
G:=-u+F+c
. Assume this is not true, then there exist
\delta>0
such that

\int_{M}\Phi(\log(e^{G}-e^{G_{j}})-\log\delta)(e^{G}-e^{G_{j}})/\delta\geq 1.

But by the dominant convergence theorem, the above integral converges to
0
, we get a contradiction.

Now consider the following family of equations

\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}=e^{u_{j}+G_{j}}\omega_{M}^{n},\omega_{u_{j}}>0.

The by theorem
1.1
, the solution
u_{j}
has the stated uniform log-continuity. And by passing to a subsequence, with proposition
4.1
,
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}
. Note that
u_{\infty}
solves

\omega_{u_{\infty}}^{n}=e^{u_{\infty}-u+F+c}\omega_{M}^{n}

Note that
u
is a solution to the limit equation, and by uniqueness(theorem 0.1 in
[
20
]
) we get
u_{\infty}=u
. It follows that
u
satisfies the stated log-continuity.

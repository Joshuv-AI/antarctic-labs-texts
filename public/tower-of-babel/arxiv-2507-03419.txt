News from the strong interaction program of NA61/SHINE
Antoni Marcinek for the NA61/SHINE Collaboration

Abstract
The NA61/SHINE experiment at the CERN SPS is a multipurpose fixed-target spectrometer for charged and neutral hadron measurements. Its research program includes studies of strong interactions as well as reference measurements for neutrino and cosmic-ray physics. This contribution summarizes a selected set of new results from our strong interaction program, including system-size and energy dependence of charged kaon to pion production, ϕ \phi (1020) spectra in Ar + Sc collisions and enhancement over p p + p p reactions, femtoscopy of like-sign pions in Ar + Sc and Be + Be collisions, D 0 + D ¯ 0 D^{0}+\overline{D}^{0} (open charm) yield in Xe + La interactions, and finally an unexpected excess of charged over neutral kaon production indicating a strong violation of isospin symmetry in Ar + Sc collisions.



1
Introduction

The NA61/SHINE experiment has a physics program consisting of
studies of strong interactions, as well as precision measurements for neutrino
and cosmic-ray physics. This contribution focuses on the strong interaction
program which includes the study of the onset of
deconfinement
[
1
]
, search for the critical point of strongly
interacting matter,
measurements of open charm and, unexpectedly, isospin-symmetry violation in
multiparticle production.

The NA61/SHINE detector is a multipurpose fixed-target spectrometer at the CERN
SPS. Its main components
are large-volume Time Projection Chambers, two of them immersed in a magnetic
field perpendicular to the beam. This gives NA61/SHINE a significant advantage
over collider experiments — acceptance covering nearly entire forward
hemisphere, down to
p_{T}=0
for charged hadrons.
The detector underwent a major
upgrade during CERN LS2 increasing the data taking rate ten-fold to the order of

1\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
, enlarging acceptance of the Vertex Detector, exchanging Time of
Flight walls and adding new forward calorimeter.

The data were measured with small acceptance, prototype Vertex

constraints and differentiate between the existing models of hadron production.

For exact isospin symmetry,
i
.
e
. equality of
u
and
d
quarks and collisions of
charge-symmetric (
Z=N
) nuclei, one expects for the yields of hadrons:

\expectationvalue*{\Kp}(u\bar{s})=\expectationvalue*{K^0}(d\bar{s})

and

\expectationvalue*{\Km}(\bar{u}s)=\expectationvalue*{\overline{K}^0}(\bar{d}s)
.
Neglecting small CP violation we also have

\expectationvalue*{K_S^0}=\frac{1}{2}\expectationvalue*{K^0}+\frac{1}{2}\expectationvalue*{\overline{K}^0}=\expectationvalue*{K_L^0}
.
Therefore in this case, the expected charged to neutral kaon ratio:

R_{K}=\frac{\expectationvalue*{\Kp}+\expectationvalue*{\Km}}{\expectationvalue*{K^0}+\expectationvalue*{\overline{K}^0}}=\frac{\expectationvalue*{\Kp}+\expectationvalue*{\Km}}{2\expectationvalue*{K_S^0}}=1\,.

(1)

Surprisingly, NA61/SHINE observed large deviations from unity for this ratio,
first in the
\pi^{-}
+
C
collisions
[
9
]
, then in
Ar
+
Sc
reactions
[
8
]
, see
Fig.

5
,
left
. While

\pi^{-}
+
C
is not charge-symmetric, so
Eq.

1
is not expected to hold,
the measured value of
R_{K}
for
\pi^{-}
+
C
collisions still differs significantly
from that predicted by models as well as quark
counting
[
9
]
.

Absence of real resonances of Dirac operators
Henry DUMANT

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to introduce the resonances of tridimensional Dirac operators by continuing meromorphically the resolvent and to establish a result about their localization : a kind of Rellich Theorem. We first consider the case of the Dirac operator in an external field which is essentially bounded and compactly supported. Secondly, we consider the case of the MIT bag model outside a smooth and bounded obstacle.



1.
Introduction

Scattering resonances are the replacement of discrete spectral data for problems on non-compact domains. Several approaches have been developped for their study in the case of Schrödinger operators. For Dirac operators, most of the contributions are based on the method of
complex scaling
that use mainly analytic microlocal analysis (see
[
1
,
26
]
). With this method, a semi-classical limit of the resonances is studied in
[
23
,
24
]
and a relativistic limit of the resonances is studied in
[
2
]
. A
limiting absorption principle
, which can be related to resonances, has also been shown in
[
5
]
. The first purpose of this work is to adapt the
potential and obstacle scattering
, formalized for Schrödinger operators in
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
27
,
13
]
), to Dirac operators. We mention that, for Dirac operators, the potential scattering has been introduced for unidimensional and radial cases in
[
17
,
18
]
or for the study of Poisson wave formulas in
[
21
,
9
]
. The principle starts with the fact that if
L
is an unbounded self-adjoint operator in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
then the resolvent function
z\mapsto(L-z)^{-1}
is meromorphic from
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ess}}(L)
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H})
and that the set of its poles is
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{disc}}(L)
. One idea is to encode the resonances as poles of the meromorphic continuation to an approriate Riemann surface of the resolvent, seen in suitable functional spaces. Such an idea first appeared in
[
12
]
. Then a natural question is the localization of the resonances in the surface. One of the classic results for Schrödinger operators is that a resonance may not live in the essential spectrum, result that sometimes wear the name of Rellich uniqueness Theorem (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.33, 3.35]
). In the semi-classical regime, much precise results mention that there are no resonances in a neighbourhood of the essential spectrum (see
[
8
,
30
,
20
]
as well as the works cited therein). The second purpose of this work is to prove the Rellich uniqueness Theorem for some Dirac operators.

1.1.
Preliminaries

1.1.1.
Tridimensionnal massive Dirac operator

The three
2\times 2
hermitian matrices

\sigma_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\mbox{, }\sigma_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\imath\\
\imath&0\end{pmatrix}\mbox{ and }\sigma_{3}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

are called the Pauli matrices. The four
4\times 4
hermitian matrices

\beta:=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2}&0_{2}\\
0_{2}&-I_{2}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{ and }\alpha_{j}:=\begin{pmatrix}0_{2}&\sigma_{j}\\
\sigma_{j}&0_{2}\end{pmatrix}\mbox{ for }j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket

are called the Dirac matrices.
Formally, if
X:=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})

\alpha\cdot X:=\alpha_{1}X_{1}+\alpha_{2}X_{2}+\alpha_{3}X_{3}.

The Dirac operator associated with the energy of a particle living in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, of mass
m\in\mathbb{R}
and of spin
1/2
is the first order differential operator
m\beta+\alpha\cdot D
where we recall that
D_{j}=-\imath\partial_{j}
for
j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket
. It acts on four components distributions. In this paper, we will only discuss the case
m=1
since a correct transformation permits to obtain all our results when
m\not=0
. A study of the case
m=0
may also be interesting (see
[
17
]
for this direction). To allege the reading, we set

\mathbf{D}:=\beta+\alpha\cdot D.

1.1.2.
Fundamental solution

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Using standard anti-commutation relations between the Dirac matrices (see
[
29
, Appendix 1.B]
), one computes

(1.1)

(\mathbf{D}-zI_{4})(\mathbf{D}+zI_{4})=-\Delta-(z^{2}-1)I_{4}.

Consider
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\omega^{2}=z^{2}-1
. It is well known that a fundamental solution
G_{\omega}
of the Helmoltz operator
-\Delta-\omega^{2}
can be defined by

G_{\omega}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{e^{\imath\omega\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}}{4\pi\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\mbox{ for }\mathbf{x}\not=\mathbf{0}.

Considering (
1.1
), the distribution

F_{z,\omega}:=(\mathbf{D}+z)(G_{\omega}I_{4})

is a fundamental solution of
\mathbf{D}-z
. For
\mathbf{x}\not=\mathbf{0}
, one has

(1.2)

F_{z,\omega}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{e^{\imath\omega\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}}{4\pi\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}\left(zI_{4}+\beta+(\imath+\omega\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)\alpha\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}\right).

1.1.3.
Dirac operator in an external field

We will first perturb
\mathbf{D}
by an external field
V
which may be think as an electric or a magnetic potential.

The operator
H_{0}:=\mathbf{D}
on the domain
\mathfrak{D}:=\mathsf{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
is self-adjoint in the ambient Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}:=\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
, its spectrum is purely continuous and

\mathrm{Sp}(H_{0})=]-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty[

(see
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
).
For
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}
and
z\not\in\mathrm{Sp}(H_{0})
one has

(1.3)

\left(H_{0}-z\right)^{-1}\phi=F_{z,(z^{2}-1)^{1/2}}*\phi

where the power
1/2
denotes the branch of the square root defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\left[0,+\infty\right[
and valued in
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\tq\Im z>0\right\}
.

The potential
V
is a
4\times 4
matrix which entries are compactly supported functions of
\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. One shows that the operator
H:=H_{0}+V
on the domain
\mathfrak{D}
is closed and thanks to Weyl’s Theorem

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=]-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty[.

Rellich-Kato Theorem ensures that
H
is self-adjoint when
V^{*}=V
.

1.1.4.
Dirac operator with MIT boundary condition

In a second time, we will perturb
\mathbf{D}
by an obstacle. We impose the so-called MIT boundary condition at the boundary of the obstacle. Such a model is used to study confined particles of spin
1/2
(see
[
10
,
11
]
) or is considered in general relativity (see
[
4
]
).

\Omega
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
\partial\Omega
\mathbf{n}

Figure 1.

The obstacle is
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is open,
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-smooth and such that
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
is bounded. The outward pointing normal to the boundary of
\Omega
is denoted
\mathbf{n}
(see Figure
1
). If
\psi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}
then
\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{4}
denotes its trace. The operator
H:=\mathbf{D}
on its domain

\mathfrak{D}:=\left\{\psi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}\tq(-\imath\beta\alpha\cdot\mathbf{n})\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega}=\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega}\right\}

is self-adjoint in the ambient Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}:=\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}
(see
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
). Moreover

\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=]-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty[

(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
). For all
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}

(1.4)

\frac{1}{C}\left\|H\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}\leq\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}}\leq C\left\|H\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}

where
C>0
does not depend on
\psi
. Since
H
is closed on its domain we deduce that
\mathfrak{D}
is a Banach space when equipped with
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathfrak{D}}:=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}}
. For completeness, (
1.4
) is proven in the appendix.

1.2.
Statement of the results

The first aim of the paper is to extend meromorphically the resolvent of
H
across
\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)
. Consider the easiest case which is the one of the free Dirac operator. A natural way to continue the resolvent of
H_{0}
is to use its convolution representation given by (
1.3
). The question reduces to the continuation of
z\mapsto(z^{2}-1)^{1/2}
across
(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty)
. Therefore we need the Riemann surface associated with this function. Specificly, we introduce

\pazocal{M}:=\left\{(z,\omega)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\tq z^{2}-\omega^{2}=1\right\}

which we equip with an appropriate holomorphic atlas (the details are given in Section
2
). If
(z,\omega)\in\pazocal{M}
with
\Im\omega>0
then
z\not\in\mathrm{Sp}(H_{0})
and
(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
can be considered as a function of
(z,\omega)
. Specificly for
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}

(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\phi=F_{z,\omega}*\phi.

When
(z,\omega)\in\pazocal{M}
with
\Im\omega\leq 0
we would like to define the continuation of
(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\phi
as the convolution between
F_{z,\omega}
and
\phi
. However,
F_{z,\omega}
being only locally square integrable in that case, we need
\phi
to be compactly supported.
The example of
H_{0}
makes it clear that one needs to suit approriate functional spaces in order to continue the resolvent. For the operators
H
, we will consider

\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}=\left\{\phi\in\mathfrak{H}\tq\Supp\phi\mbox{ is bounded}\right\}\mbox{ and }\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}=\left\{\psi\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\tq\chi\psi\in\mathfrak{D}\mbox{ for all }\chi\in\mathscr{C}\right\}

where
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
,
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in the context
1.1.3
and
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{4}
,
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\Omega})
in then context
1.1.4
. One defines
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
as the vector space of the linear functions
T:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}
such that
\chi T\tilde{\chi}
is bounded from
\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{D}
for all
\chi,\tilde{\chi}\in\mathscr{C}
.

We are able to state the first result of this work.

Theorem 1

(Meromorphic continuations, resonances)
.

Consider both contexts
1.1.3
and
1.1.4
. There exists a finite meromorphic function from
\pazocal{M}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
denoted
\pazocal{R}
such that if
(z,\omega)\in\pazocal{M}
is not a pole of
\pazocal{R}
and
\Im\omega>0
then
z\not\in\mathrm{Sp}(H)
and

\pazocal{R}(z,\omega)=(H-z)^{-1}\mbox{ on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}.

A pole of
\pazocal{R}
is called a resonance of
H
.

The notion of finite meromorphic function from
\pazocal{M}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
is precised in Section
2
. Although we focus ourselves on tridimensional Dirac operators, it should be possible to adapt our results in other dimensions once the Riemann surface involved is well understood (in even dimension, logarithmic singularities should appeared at the ramification points). Unlike the case of Schrödinger operators where one omits the abstract setting of the Riemann surface by parametrizing it with
\omega\mapsto\omega^{2}
, this setting is needed for Dirac operators as
z
and
\omega
appear simultaneously in (
1.2
). Moreover, it permits to work with a global definition that avoids treating cases according to the localization on the surface as in
[
21
,
9
]
.

We are now ready to state the major result of our paper. For Schrödinger operators, such a result is known as Rellich uniqueness Theorem (see
[
27
,
13
]
).

Theorem 2

(Absence of real resonances)
.

Let
(\lambda,\kappa)\in\pazocal{M}
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
.

(1)

In the context
1.1.3
, if
V^{*}=V
and
\kappa\not=0
then
(\lambda,\kappa)
is not a resonance of
H
.

(2)

In the context
1.1.4
,

•

if
\kappa=0
then
(\lambda,\kappa)
is not a resonance of
H
.

•

if
\Omega
is connected and
\kappa\not=0
then
(\lambda,\kappa)
is not a resonance of
H
.

Remark 1.1
.

In the context
1.1.3
,
H
can have an eigenvalue at
\pm 1
(see
[
14
]
). This explains the difference between the two assertions of Theorem
2
.

Structure of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.
The analytical setup

In this section, we introduce the appropriate notions of analyticity needed. We prefer to be specific since our functions will be valued in
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
which is not a Banach space. Nevertheless we come back to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{D})
that has the Banach structure.

We start with the functions defined in an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
and valued in
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\pazocal{T}:\pazocal{W}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})

\pazocal{W}

Now we discuss about the complex structure on

there exists an open subset
\pazocal{V}_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}
of
\pazocal{M}
containing

and an open subset
\pazocal{W}_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}
of
\mathbb{C}
such that

\varphi_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}:(z,\omega)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z\mbox{ if }\omega_{0}\not=0\\

\pazocal{V}_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}
to
\pazocal{W}_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}
with
\varphi_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}^{-1}
being holomorphic from
\pazocal{W}_{(z_{0},\omega_{0})}
to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. One checks that
\left\{(\pazocal{V}_{\mathfrak{m}},\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}})\tq\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}\right\}
is an holomorphic atlas on
\pazocal{M}
.
We equip
\pazocal{M}
with the maximal holomorphic atlas
\mathscr{A}
associated with
\left\{(\pazocal{V}_{\mathfrak{m}},\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}})\tq\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}\right\}
. Specificly
\mathscr{A}
is the set of the local chart
(\pazocal{V},\varphi)
on
\pazocal{M}
such that
\varphi\circ\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-1}
is holomorphic on

for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}
such that
\pazocal{V}\cap\pazocal{V}_{\mathfrak{m}}\not=\emptyset
.

We can now define what is a holomorphic function defined in an open subset of
\pazocal{M}
and valued in
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\pazocal{T}:\pazocal{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
with
\pazocal{U}\subset\pazocal{M}
open. One says that
\pazocal{T}
is holomorphic if
\pazocal{T}\circ\varphi^{-1}
is holomorphic from
\varphi(\pazocal{U}\cap\pazocal{V})
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
for all
(\pazocal{V},\varphi)\in\mathscr{A}
.

We finally define a meromorphic function defined in
\pazocal{M}
and valued in
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
.

Definition 2.3
.

One calls finite meromorphic function from
\pazocal{M}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
any data
(\pazocal{Z},\pazocal{T})
such that

(1)

\pazocal{Z}
is closed and discrete in
\pazocal{M}

(2)

\pazocal{T}
is holomorphic from
\pazocal{M}\setminus\pazocal{Z}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})

(3)

for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{Z}
there exists
(\pazocal{V},\varphi)\in\mathscr{A}
with
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{V}
and

\varphi(\mathfrak{m})
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
.

One calls

the residue at
\mathfrak{m}
of

in

Remark 2.1
.

The set

does not benefit of a natural algebra structure. We shall check by hand that compositions are allowed and that the analytical properties are preserved. Since we want a weak dependance in the cut-offs, it will be crucial to follow them precisely in our proofs.

Let

\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow\zeta_{0}}\frac{R_{0}(\zeta)-R_{0}(\zeta_{0})}{\zeta-\zeta_{0}}\psi=(\chi A_{1}\tilde{\chi})\psi\mbox{ and }\lim_{\zeta\rightarrow\zeta_{0}}H_{0}\left(\frac{R_{0}(\zeta)-R_{0}(\zeta_{0})}{\zeta-\zeta_{0}}\psi\right)=B_{1}\psi

but
H_{0}
being closed this yields
(\chi A_{1}\tilde{\chi})\psi\in\mathfrak{D}
and

H_{0}\left((\chi A_{1}\tilde{\chi})\psi)\right)=B_{1}\psi.

One deduces of Lemma
3.1
that
\chi A_{1}\tilde{\chi}
is bounded from
\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{D}
. Iterating the argument, one obtains that
\chi A_{\ell}\tilde{\chi}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{D})
and
H_{0}\chi A_{\ell}\tilde{\chi}=B_{\ell}
for all

. Again by Lemma

one has

\left\|R_{0}(\zeta)-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(\zeta-\zeta_{0})^{\ell}\chi A_{\ell}\tilde{\chi}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{D})}=C\left\|H_{0}R_{0}(\zeta)-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(\zeta-\zeta_{0})^{\ell}B_{\ell}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H})}

for all

. Taking

gives the result.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}
and
(z,\omega)\in\pazocal{M}
. The function
\pazocal{R}_{0}(z,\omega)\phi
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Supp\phi
. Moreover as
r\rightarrow+\infty

\pazocal{R}_{0}(z,\omega)\phi(r\mathbf{s})=\frac{e^{\imath\omega r}}{4\pi r}\left(\left(zI_{4}+\beta+\omega\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\imath\omega\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)

D_{j}\pazocal{R}_{0}(\pm 1,0)\phi(r\mathbf{s})=\frac{\imath s_{j}}{4\pi r^{2}}(\pm I_{4}+\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right)\mbox{ , }j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket

uniformly in
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1
.

Proof.

The regularity result is a straight consequence of the dominated convergence Theorem. We move on the asymptotics. One easily computes, as
\left|\mathbf{y}\right|\rightarrow+\infty

F_{z,\omega}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{e^{\imath\omega\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}}{4\pi\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}\left(zI_{4}+\beta+\omega\alpha\cdot\frac{\mathbf{y}}{\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}+O\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}\right)\right)

and

D_{j}F_{\pm 1,0}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{4\pi\left|\mathbf{y}\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{\imath y_{j}}{\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}(\pm I_{4}+\beta)+O\left(\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{y}\right|}\right)\right).

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\Supp\phi
. As
r\rightarrow+\infty
, one has

\left|r\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{x}\right|=r-\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{x}+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

and hence

\frac{1}{\left|r\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{x}\right|}=\frac{1}{r}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right)\mbox{ and }e^{\imath\omega\left|r\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{x}\right|}=e^{\imath\omega r}\left(e^{-\imath\omega\mathbf{s}\cdot\mathbf{x}}+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right).

Since
\left|\mathbf{s}\right|=1
and
\mathbf{x}\in\Supp\phi
, all those asymptotics are uniform in
(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{x})
. Therefore the asymptotics follow by integrating against
\phi
.
∎

\pazocal{Z}=\left\{(\lambda,\kappa)\in\pazocal{M}\tq\exists\psi\in\mathrm{Ran}(\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa))\mbox{ s.t. }\psi\not=0\mbox{ and }(\mathbf{D}+V)\psi=\lambda\psi\right\}.

Definition 4.1
.

\pazocal{R}
. Their set is denoted
\mathrm{Res}(H)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Associated with a resonance

\pazocal{Z}
. In general, the corresponding function
\psi
can not be square integrable. The integrability is replaced by the condition
\psi\in\mathrm{Ran}(\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa))
. In particular, Proposition
3.1
provides a behaviour of

\infty
. One says that

Let us give the proof of Theorem

Proof.

Let
z\not\in\mathrm{Sp}(H)
. We start with

(4.1)

H-z=\left(1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\right)(H_{0}-z).

Take
\rho\in\mathscr{C}
such that
V\prec\rho
. Then

(4.2)

1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}=\left(1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}(1-\rho)\right)\left(1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\rho\right).

But
1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}(1-\rho)
and
1-V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}(1-\rho)
are inverse of one another therefore

(4.3)

(H-z)^{-1}=(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\left(1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\rho\right)^{-1}\left(1-V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}(1-\rho)\right).

Then it is natural to define
\pazocal{R}(\mathfrak{m})
by replacing
(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
by
\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})
in the r.h.s from (

). Precisely, we set

\pazocal{Z}:=\left\{\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}\tq 1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\mbox{ is not invertible}\right\}

and for
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}\setminus\pazocal{Z}

(4.4)

\pazocal{R}(\mathfrak{m}):=\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)^{-1}\left(1-V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})(1-\rho)\right).

This makes sense since both
1-V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})(1-\rho)
and
\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)^{-1}

map
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}
into itself. Indeed, for all
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\rho\prec\tilde{\rho}
we have

(4.5)

\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)^{-1}\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\rho}\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)^{-1}.

since

\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\rho}\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right).

If
(z,\omega)\not\in\pazocal{Z}
and
\Im\omega>0
then
z\not\in\mathrm{Sp}(H_{0})
and

1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\rho=1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(z,\omega)\rho

is invertible. Then
1+V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
is invertible by (
4.2
) so

by (
4.1
). Hence (
4.3
) yield

\pazocal{R}(z,\omega)=(H-z)^{-1}\mbox{ on }\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}.

We must now show the meromorphy of
\pazocal{R}
. The game is to apply the Fredholm analytic Theorem
[
25

to the holomorphic function

\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}\mapsto 1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\in\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}).

For this, we must show that
V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho
is a compact operator for every

and prove that
\pazocal{Z}\not=\pazocal{M}
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{M}
and
\tilde{\rho}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\rho\prec\tilde{\rho}
. Thanks to Rellich-Kondrachov Theorem, the multiplication by

defines a compact operator from

to
\mathfrak{H}
. Consequently
\rho\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho=\tilde{\rho}\rho\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho

\mathfrak{H}
since
\rho\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho

\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{D}
. Since the multiplication by

\mathfrak{H}
.

Let

V\pazocal{R}\left(z,(z^{2}-1)^{1/2}\right)\rho=V(H_{0}-z)^{-1}\rho

\left\|V\pazocal{R}\left(z,(z^{2}-1)^{1/2}\right)\rho\right\|_{\mathscr{L}\left(\mathfrak{H}\right)}\leq\frac{\left\|V\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\rho\right\|_{\infty}}{\left|\Im z\right|}<1

so that

by the Neumann invertibility criterion.

Thanks to the Fredholm analytic Theorem

•

\pazocal{Z}
is closed and discrete

•

\mathfrak{m}\mapsto\left(1+V\pazocal{R}_{0}(\mathfrak{m})\rho\right)^{-1}
is holomorphic from
\pazocal{M}\setminus\pazocal{Z}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H})

•

for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\pazocal{Z}
and all

with

there exists a sequence

where the convergence of the series hold in

Let

Now let

4.2.
Absence of resonances of
H

This part of the section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
2
in the context
1.1.3
, that is the following Theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that
V^{*}=V
. If
(\lambda,\kappa)\in\pazocal{M}
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
then
(\lambda,\kappa)
is not a resonance of
H
.

We denote by
\mathbb{B}_{r}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}_{r}
) the ball (resp. sphere) of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose center is
\mathbf{0}
and radius is

. The surface measure on
\mathbb{S}_{r}
is denoted
\sigma_{r}
.

Proof.

One proceeds by contradiction : assume that
(\lambda,\kappa)\in\pazocal{M}
is a resonance of
H
with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Thanks to Theorem
4
one can pick
\phi\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}
such that
\psi:=\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa)\phi
is not identically zero and solves the equation

(\mathbf{D}+V)\psi=\lambda\psi.

Step 1. We prove that
(\lambda I_{4}+\beta+\alpha\cdot\xi)\mathscr{F}\phi(\xi)=0
when
\left|\xi\right|^{2}=\kappa^{2}
.

Let
r_{0}>0
such that
\Supp\phi\subset\mathbb{B}_{r_{0}}
and let

. Then
\psi
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{r_{0}}
(see Proposition
3.1
). Using

, the fact that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and an integration by parts, one gets

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}}\left|\psi(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}}\psi(\mathbf{x})^{*}(\mathbf{D}+V)\psi(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}}\left((\mathbf{D}+V)\psi(\mathbf{x})\right)^{*}\psi(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}}\psi(\mathbf{s})^{*}\left(-\imath\alpha\cdot\frac{\mathbf{s}}{r}\right)\psi(\mathbf{s})d\sigma_{r}(\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{B}_{r}}\left|\psi(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}-\imath r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}\psi(r\mathbf{s})^{*}(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s})\psi(r\mathbf{s})d\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{s})

and hence

(4.12)

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}\psi(r\mathbf{s})^{*}(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s})\psi(r\mathbf{s})d\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{s})=0.

For
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}

\psi(r\mathbf{s})^{*}(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s})\psi(r\mathbf{s})=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\lambda}\left|\left(\lambda+\beta+\kappa\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s}\right)\mathscr{F}\phi\left(\kappa\mathbf{s}\right)\right|^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right).

Combining (
4.12
) and (

) and taking the limit

, one obtains

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{1}}\left|(\lambda I_{4}+\beta+\kappa\alpha\cdot\mathbf{s})\mathscr{F}\phi(\kappa\mathbf{s})\right|^{2}d\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{s})=0

and the conclusion of step 1 follows.

Since Step

[
13
, Theorem 3.33]
).

Step 2. We check that
\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa)\phi
is compactly supported.

One has
(\mathbf{D}-\lambda I_{4})(\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa)\phi)=\phi
and then in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
(as

Let

\mathscr{F}\left((\mathbf{D}+\lambda)\phi\right)

Consider the analytic surface

\pazocal{A}:=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\tq\xi^{2}=\kappa^{2}\right\}.

Thanks to
[

, if we prove that

Let
\xi_{0}\in\pazocal{A}

\theta_{0},\varphi_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\xi_{0}=\left(\left|\kappa\right|\cos\theta_{0}\cos\varphi_{0},\left|\kappa\right|\sin\theta_{0}\cos\varphi_{0},\left|\kappa\right|\sin\varphi_{0}\right).

Consider the holomorphic function

\Gamma:(r,\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\mapsto\left(r\cos\theta\cos\varphi,r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,r\sin\varphi\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}.

Then
\Gamma(\left|\kappa\right|,\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})=\xi_{0}
and the jacobian of
\Gamma
is equal to
\left|\kappa\right|
at
(\left|\kappa\right|,\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})
. The holomorphic local inversion Theorem implies that there is an open set
O
of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
centered in
\xi_{0}
such that

(\left|\kappa\right|,\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\in O\subset\left\{(r,\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\tq\Re r>0\right\}

and
\Gamma
is biholomorphic from
O
to
\Gamma(O)
. We denote its inverse by

\xi\in\Gamma(O)\mapsto\left(R(\xi),\Theta(\xi),\Phi(\xi)\right)\in O.

For
\xi\in\Gamma(O)
we have
\xi^{2}=R(\xi)^{2}
so
\xi^{2}\not\in]-\infty,0]
since
\Re R(\xi)>0
. We can finally write

E(\xi)=h(\xi^{2}-\kappa^{2},\Theta(\xi),\Phi(\xi))\mbox{ for }\xi\in\Gamma(O)

where

h(r,\theta,\varphi)=E\left(\Gamma\left(\sqrt{r+\kappa^{2}},\theta,\varphi\right)\right)\mbox{ for }(r,\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\mbox{ with }r\not\in]-\infty,-\kappa^{2}]

and where
\sqrt{\cdot}
is the holomorphic branch of the square root defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus]-\infty,0]
. We see that
h(0,\theta,\varphi)=0
for all
\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{R}
by Step 1 and then for all
\theta,\varphi\in\mathbb{C}
by the isolated zeros principle applied separately in
\theta
and
\varphi
.

For
\xi\in\Gamma(O)
we get

\displaystyle E(\xi)

\displaystyle=h\left(\xi^{2}-\kappa^{2},\Theta(\xi),\Phi(\xi)\right)-h\left(0,\Theta(\xi),\Phi(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi^{2}-\kappa^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}\left(t\left(\xi^{2}-\kappa^{2}\right),\Theta(\xi),\Phi(\xi)\right)dt.

Since the integral term is clearly holomorphic in
\Gamma(O)
, this provides a holomorphic continuation near
\xi_{0}
of

\xi\mapsto\frac{E(\xi)}{\xi^{2}-\kappa^{2}}.

Eventually, we deduce that
\pazocal{R}_{0}(\lambda,\kappa)\phi
is compactly supported.

Step 3. Contradiction.

We have just shown in Step 2 that
\psi
is equal to zero in a neighbourhood of
\infty
. We will now show that
\psi
is identically zero, which will be our contradiction. For this, we use a kind of Carleman estimate for the Dirac operator : thanks to
[
7
, Lemma 1]
, for all real valued function
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(]0,+\infty[)
and all
u\in\mathfrak{D}
which support is a compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}

\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}e^{2\varphi(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}\left|(\alpha\cdot D)u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}e^{2\varphi(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}-\frac{3}{2\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}\right)\left|u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}.

We set

\varphi_{h}(r):=\frac{1}{h}\left(\frac{3}{4}(\log r)^{2}+\log r\right)\mbox{ for }r,h>0

and apply the Carleman estimate with
u:=\psi(\cdot+\mathbf{x}_{0})
where
\mathbf{x}_{0}\not\in\Supp\psi
is fixed. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}e^{2\varphi_{h}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}\left|(\lambda I_{4}-\beta-V(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{x}_{0}))u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\geq\left(\frac{1}{h}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}\frac{e^{2\varphi_{h}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}\left|u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}.

Since
\Supp u
is a compact of
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}
one deduces that

C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}\left|e^{\varphi_{h}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}\geq\left(\frac{1}{h}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}\left|e^{\varphi_{h}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}

where
C>0
does not depend on
h
. By taking

small enough we get

0\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}}\left|e^{\varphi_{h}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)}u(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}.

Eventually,
u
is identically zero and so does
\psi
: this is a contradiction.
∎

As explained in Remark

\pazocal{R}
in

Definition 4.2
.

We call

•

multiplicity of

Proposition 4.1

The space

Remark 4.2
.

This result is very similar to the description of the Laurent expansion of the resolvent near an eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity of a general closed operator (see
[
19

Proof.

Let
T_{1}=\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}},T_{2},...,T_{L}\in\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})
of finite rank with

such that

\mathscr{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}},\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}})

Let
\psi\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}\cap\mathfrak{D}
. Pick

such that
\psi\prec\chi
.
For all

(4.14)

\chi\pazocal{R}(z,\omega)\chi(H-z)\psi=\psi.

Indeed, (
4.14
) is obviously true if

and remains true if

by analytic continuation. Replacing
(z,\omega)
by
\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-1}(\zeta)
in (

) and using the uniqueness of the Laurent expansion at

, one gets if
\ell<L

-\chi T_{\ell}\chi(H-\lambda)\psi+\chi T_{\ell+1}\chi\psi=0

\chi T_{\ell}(H-\lambda)\psi=\chi T_{\ell+1}\psi.

Again,

T_{\ell}(H-\lambda)\psi=T_{\ell+1}\psi.

We deduce that if

then

T_{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathrm{comp}}\cap\mathfrak{D})\subset\mathrm{Ran}(T_{\ell}).

For the Fréchet topology of
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{loc}}

In a similar fashion one can show that

(\mathbf{D}+V-\lambda)T_{\ell}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{\ell+1}&\mbox{ if }\ell<L\\
0&\mbox{ if }\ell=L\end{array}.\right.

Thanks to (

) and (

is left invariant by
\mathbf{D}+V
,

and

∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let

Proof.

and

A.2.

In this section, we compute the quadratic form of the square of

\mathbf{s}\in\partial\Omega

Theorem 8

(Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz)
.

For all
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}

(A.1)

\left\|H\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}=\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}+\left\langle\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega},h\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{4}}.

Proof.

Let

. Using that
\beta
and the
\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\left|(\beta+\alpha\cdot D)f\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|f\right|^{2}+\left|D_{1}f\right|^{2}+\left|D_{2}f\right|^{2}+\left|D_{3}f\right|^{2}+\partial_{1}(f^{*}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\partial_{2}f+f^{*}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\partial_{3}f)

\displaystyle+\partial_{2}(f^{*}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\partial_{3}f-f^{*}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\partial_{1}f)-\partial_{3}(f^{*}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}\partial_{1}f+f^{*}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\partial_{2}f)

so by Stokes Theorem

(A.2)

\int_{\Omega}\left|(\beta+\alpha\cdot D)f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega}\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\left|Df(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\mathbf{s})^{*}Pf(\mathbf{s})d\sigma(\mathbf{s})

where

(A.3)

Pf:=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(n_{1}\partial_{2}f-n_{2}\partial_{1}f)+\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}(n_{1}\partial_{3}f-n_{3}\partial_{1}f)+\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}(n_{2}\partial_{3}f-n_{3}\partial_{2}f)

Now take
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
and a sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of
\mathscr{C}
which is an approximation of identity. Consider the sequence
(f_{n}:=\rho_{n}*f)_{n\geq 0}
of
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}

(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and its first derivatives converge to
f
and its first derivatives uniformly and in
\mathfrak{H}
. In particular, as
\partial\Omega
is compact, (
A.2
) remains true with
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{4}
.

Until the end of the proof, we consider
f\in\mathfrak{D}\cap\mathscr{C}^{1}_{c}(\overline{\Omega})
. We must compute the surface integral from (
A.2
) using the boundary condition. In fact, we will be able to make the simplifications pointwise. Hence, we fix
\mathbf{s}\in\partial\Omega
and we drop the dependance of the functions in
\mathbf{s}
to allege the reading. In (
A.3
), one can recognize the structure of an exterior product by the normale : we are only considering tangential derivatives. Since
W=W(\mathbf{s})
is symmetric we can consider
(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})
an orthonormal basis of

such that

and

(

\partial\Omega
) and such that
(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}^{\prime},\mathbf{n})
is a direct orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(\partial_{\mathbf{v}}f)_{k}:=\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla f_{k}\mbox{ for }k=1,2,3,4.

Since
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t}^{\prime})
is orthonormal, one has

\partial_{j}f=n_{j}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}f+t_{j}\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f+t^{\prime}_{j}\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}f\mbox{ for }j=1,2,3.

so (
A.3

\displaystyle Pf

\displaystyle=\left[\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t})_{3}-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t})_{2}+\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t})_{1}\right]\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f

\displaystyle+\left[\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t}^{\prime})_{3}-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{3}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t}^{\prime})_{2}+\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{t}^{\prime})_{1}\right]\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}f

which, with the choice of the orientation, simplifies as

(A.4)

Pf=\imath\gamma_{5}\left[(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f-(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t})\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}f\right]

where

and

(\mathbf{n}\circ c)^{\prime}(0)=W\mathbf{t}=\lambda\mathbf{t}
one gets

\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f=-\imath\lambda\beta(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t})f+B\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f.

Since
(\alpha\cdot X)(\alpha\cdot Y)=X\cdot Y+\imath\gamma_{5}\alpha\cdot(X\times Y)
and
\beta\gamma_{5}=-\gamma_{5}\beta
one eventually gets by using again the boundary condition

(A.5)

(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f=-\imath\lambda\gamma_{5}f+B(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t}^{\prime})\partial_{\mathbf{t}}f.

Similary

(A.6)

(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t})\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}f=\imath\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{5}f+B(\alpha\cdot\mathbf{t})\partial_{\mathbf{t}^{\prime}}f.

Using
\gamma_{5}^{2}=-I_{4}
,
\gamma_{5}(\alpha\cdot X)=(\alpha\cdot X)\gamma_{5}
,
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}=2h
and combining (
A.4
),(
A.5
) and (
A.6
) we end up with

Pf=2hf-BPf.

Using a last time the boundary condition, we get

f(\mathbf{s})^{*}Pf(\mathbf{s})=h(\mathbf{s})\left|f(\mathbf{s})\right|^{2}.

Hence, Theorem
8
is proven for smooth enough functions. Lemma
A.1
finishes the job.
∎

A.3.
Conclusion

Corollary A.1
.

For all
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}

\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}}\leq C\left\|H\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}

where
C>0
does not depend on
\psi
.

Proof.

Pick
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
C_{0}>0
such that for all
\psi\in\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}

\left\|\psi_{\tq\partial\Omega}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{4}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}+C_{0}\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}.

Take
\psi\in\mathfrak{D}
. Thanks to Theorem
8
and the fact that
h\in\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
one obtains

(A.7)

\left\|H\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}\geq\left(1-\varepsilon\left\|h\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}-C_{0}\left\|h\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{4}}^{2}.

Taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough in (
A.7
) and using (
A.8
), we get the result expected.
∎

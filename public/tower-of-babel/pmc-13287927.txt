Consensus on the Management of Inavolisib Side Effects. A Survey on Defined Side Effects, the Assumed Pathomechanism, Preventative and/or Therapeutic Measures Including the Integration of Additional Health Care Professionals – a Consensus Paper
Authors: Carlo Fremd, Michaela Müller-Rösel, Tjoung-Won Park-Simon, Mattea Reinisch, Iris Scheffen, Manfred Welslau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This consensus paper depicts prevention, identification, treatment, and the involvement of relevant medical specialists and other health care professionals in the management of defined side effects of the new selective oral PI3K inhibitor inavolisib. All topics were discussed and agreed upon between experts in oncology, gynecology, and diabetology from both clinical and private practice. In addition to a brief summary of the assumed pathomechanism of each selected side effect, the management of the defined adverse effects is described, based on respective severity and according to the summary of product characteristics (SmPC). These details are supplemented by the expertise and experience of the participating experts regarding possible preventative and supportive therapeutic measures, special precautions, and practical recommendations for daily clinical practice and patient management.

Introduction

On 23 July 2025, the EU Commission approved the oral PI3K inhibitor inavolisib (Itovebi) 1 . Inavolisib is used in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant for the treatment of adult patients with PIK3CA-mutated , oestrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer if a recurrence occurs during adjuvant endocrine treatment or within 12 months after completion of adjuvant endocrine treatment 2 .

Inavolisib is an alpha-selective PI3K inhibitor, which shows fewer “on-target” side effects (unintended effects at the specific target site) than non-selective PI3K inhibitors as well as “off-target” effects (at sites other than the specific target site).

Despite improved tolerability compared to non-selective PI3K inhibitors, inavolisib therapy can be associated with side effects that can be attenuated in their intensity with preventive therapies and can be addressed with appropriate countermeasures. Relevant side effects are hyperglycaemia, stomatitis, diarrhoea, rash and dry eyes 2 . In principle, these side effects occur early 3 in the course of therapy ( Fig. 1 ).

Given the variety of organ systems involved, multidisciplinary approaches for affected patients is generally recommended, particularly in case of treatment-resistent symptoms. The background and suitable measures for the management of the presented side effects, especially hyperglycaemia and dry eye management, which may not yet be routinely known, were discussed by the authors and summarised here as a consensus.

Inavo 120 study inavolisib + palbociclib + fulvestrant 3 : Time to first occurrence of the most important selected adverse events.

In principle, side effects are countered with temporary treatment interruptions, dose reductions and pharmacological or other therapeutic measures, depending on their severity. The severity of a specific side effect is determined by the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE criteria, version 5 4 ). The respective management for the defined side effects is illustrated as an overview and supplemented with suggestions for prevention or experiences with the management of side effects by the authors of this article.

Hyperglycaemia

Incidence per the Summary of Product Characteristics 2 based on the data from the INAVO120 approval study 3 :

very common ; all grades 59.9 %, grade 3: 5.6 %, grade 4: not yet reported

Also: Increased insulin in the blood: common; with 6.2 % all grades, to date, there have been no grade 3 or 4 reports for inavolisib.

The median time to the occurrence of the first hyperglycaemia in the pivotal study was 7.0 days after treatment initiation with inavolisib.

Why does this side effect occur?

By binding to the insulin receptor, the body’s own insulin causes activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway and thereby mediates the physiological insulin effect in the context of glucose homeostasis. This PI3K/AKT signalling pathway is inhibited by PI3K inhibitor treatment ( Fig. 2 ). This reduces glucose transport, glycogen synthesis and glycolysis by around 40 %. The resulting hyperglycaemia is therefore regarded as an “on target” effect of PI3K inhibition 5 6 7 .

PI3K mechanism of action and influence on glucose regulation (in accordance with 5 6 ).

1 Under physiological conditions, insulin signalling controls glucose uptake via the PI3K/AKT pathway

2 PI3K inhibition blocks the AKT-mediated translocation of the glucose transporter GLUT4 to the cell membrane

3 Glucose uptake from the blood is reduced, hepatic glycogenolysis increases, releasing more glucose. → Hyperglycaemia occurs with increased insulin secretion in the pancreas (hyperinsulinaemia)

Cave

When managing hyperglycaemia, the patient’s hydration status must be monitored, as the high blood glucose levels can lead to significantly increased urine excretion.

In this clinical setting, insulin should be considered as the last resort. Insulin can reactivate the PI3K signalling pathway – at least partially – despite the administration of the PI3K inhibitor 6 . If insulin is used as a countermeasure in hyperglycaemia management, therapy with inavolisib should be interrupted. There is an increased risk of hypoglycaemia in this treatment context.

It is also important to point out to patients and, if applicable, their relatives that insulin is not an appropriate therapy for hyperglycaemia occurring in this therapy situation , so that the patient and/or relatives can also notify the treating physicians in the event of a possible hyperglycaemia situation.

Stomatitis

Stomatitis – reported as: aphthous ulcer, glossitis, glossodynia, lip ulceration, mouth ulceration, mucosal inflammation and stomatitis. (Patients report pain, burning, difficulty in eating and ulcerations in the oral cavity.)

Incidence per the Summary of Product Characteristics 2 based on the data from the INAVO120 approval study 3 :

very common ; all grades 51.2 %, grade 3: 5.6 %, grade 4: not yet reported.

In the approval study, the median time to the first occurrence of stomatitis was 13 days 3 .

Why does this side effect occur?

While PI3K inhibitors are designed to attack cancer cells and block the signalling pathway, healthy cells can also be affected and may lead to stomatitis. This is particularly true because the oral mucosa reacts especially sensitive to disturbances in cellular processes, which can lead to inflammation and/or ulceration 8 .

Diarrhoea

Diarrhoea is one of the known side effects in breast cancer management.

Incidence per Summary of Product Characteristics 2 , based on the data of the INAVO120 approval study 3 :

very common ; all grades 48.1 %, grade 3: 3.7 %, grade 4: not yet reported for inavolisib.

In the pivotal study, the median time to the first occurrence of diarrhoea was 15 days 3 .

Why does this side effect occur?

The mechanism of diarrhoea induction by PI3K inhibitors has not yet been conclusively demonstrated 10 .

In the context of side effect management, the possibility of overlapping side effects in patients treated with metformin who experience diarrhoea should be considered as a superimposition of side effect aspects.

Rash

Reported as rash, erythematous rash, maculo-papular rash, papular rash, rash with itching, pustular rash.

Incidence per Summary of Product Characteristics 2 , based on the data of the INAVO120 approval study 3 :

very frequent ; all grades 22.8 %, to date, there have been no grade 3 or 4 reports for inavolisib.

The median time to the first appearance of skin symptoms in the pivotal study was 29 days 3 .

Why does this side effect occur?

The maculo-papular rash is considered an “on target effect” of inavolisib. Inhibition of the PI3Kα signalling pathway can lead to structural changes in the epidermis and dermis. The PI3Kα signalling pathway coordinates the cell cycle of keratinocytes 11 .

Preventive measures

Maintenance of a well-moisturized skin with urea ointment 5 % (10 % of the foot area) can be recommended prophylactically.

Patients should minimise the risk of sunburn in this therapy situation and use sun protection.

Cave

In general, skin symptoms have been described for PI3K inhibitors. These range from a mild maculo-papular rash to life-threatening toxic epidermal necrolysis (Lyell-, Stephens-Johnson Syndrome). Exfoliative and multiforme exanthema of varying severity have been described. So far, none of the severe skin symptoms such as Lyell-Syndrome, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome etc. have been described for inavolisib.

In case of blistering skin rash, an immediate dermatological consultation is required!

Other relevant side effects:

Eye disorders

The most common eye disorders reported to date with inavolisib therapy are dry eye or blurred vision.

Incidence per the Summary of Product Characteristics 2 based on the data from the INAVO120 approval study 3 :

common ; all grades 8.6 %, no grade 3 or 4 reports to date.

Why does this side effect occur?

The corneal epithelial cells occupy the outer layer of the cornea and play an important role in maintaining corneal epithelial homeostasis, barrier and optical functions. The PI3K/AKT signalling pathway is involved in proliferation, apoptosis, migration and other functions of corneal epithelial cells and can be affected accordingly by inhibition 15 .

Preventive and therapeutic measures

Prophylactic moisturising of dry eyes with preparations for tear fluid replacement without the addition of cortisone (NaCl eye drops, eye drops containing euphrasia or hyaluronic acid) is recommended.

Summary

In principle, it is recommended to monitor and follow-up patients at least once a week during the first treatment cycle . This allows for an early and targeted identification of emerging symptoms and enables timely counteracting intervention, thus promoting a better treatment adherence. In principle interrruptions of treatment, dose reductions (from 9 mg to 6 mg and in a further step from 6 mg to 3 mg as the lowest dosage) , additional pharmacologic or other therapeutic measures may be employed, up to and including discontinuation of inavolisib therapy, depending on the severity and manifestation of the adverse effects 13 . In the pivotal study, treatment interruptions due to any side effect were documented in 72.7 %, dose reductions in 14.9 % and treatment discontinuations in 6.8 % of study participants 3 . In any case, the Specifications of the Summary of Product Characteristics 2 must be followed.

Einleitung

Am 23.07.2025 ließ die EU-Kommission den oralen PI3K-Inhibitor Inavolisib (Itovebi) zu 1 . Inavolisib wird in Kombination mit Palbociclib und Fulvestrant zur Behandlung von erwachsenen Patienten mit PIK3CA -mutiertem, Östrogenrezeptor-(ER-)positivem, HER2-negativem, lokal fortgeschrittenem oder metastasiertem Brustkrebs angewendet, wenn während einer adjuvanten endokrinen Behandlung oder innerhalb von 12 Monaten nach Abschluss einer adjuvanten endokrinen Behandlung ein Rezidiv auftritt 2 .

Inavolisib ist ein alpha-selektiver PI3K-Inhibitor, der geringere „On-Target“-Nebenwirkungen (unbeabsichtigte Wirkungen am spezifischen Zielort) als nicht-selektive PI3K-Inhibitoren sowie „Off-Target“-Wirkungen (an anderen Stellen als dem spezifischen Zielort) zeigt.

Trotz gegenüber nicht-selektiven PI3K-Inhibitoren verbesserter Verträglichkeit kann die Inavolisib-Therapie mit Nebenwirkungen einhergehen, die mit präventiven Therapien in ihrer Intensität abgeschwächt und mit entsprechenden Gegenmaßnahmen gut behandelt werden können. Relevante Nebenwirkungen sind Hyperglykämie, Stomatitis, Diarrhö, Rash (Hautausschlag) und trockene Augen 2 . Prinzipiell treten diese Nebenwirkungen früh 3 im Therapieverlauf auf ( Abb. 1 ).

Eine multidisziplinäre Betreuung betroffener Patientinnen ist aufgrund der verschiedenen betroffenen Organsysteme prinzipiell empfehlenswert, insbesondere bei therapieresistenten Beschwerden. Hintergründe und geeignete Maßnahmen für das Management der aufgeführten Nebenwirkungen, vor allem auch des bislang ggf. noch nicht routinemäßig bekannten Hyperglykämie- und Trockene-Augen-Managements wurden von den Autoren diskutiert und hier als Konsensus zusammengefasst.

Hyperglykämie

Inzidenz per Fachinformation 2 , basierend auf den Daten der Zulassungsstudie INAVO120 3 :

sehr häufig ; alle Grade 59,9 %, Grad 3: 5,6 %, Grad 4: bisher nicht berichtet

Dazu auch: Insulin im Blut erhöht: häufig; mit 6,2 % alle Grade, bisher keine Grad 3- oder 4- Berichte.

Die mediane Zeit bis zum Auftreten einer ersten Hyperglykämie betrug in der Zulassungsstudie 7,0 Tage nach Start der Therapie mit Inavolisib.

Wieso kommt es zu dieser Nebenwirkung?

Das körpereigene Insulin verursacht durch seine Bindung an den Insulinrezeptor eine Aktivierung des PI3K/AKT-Signalweges und vermittelt dadurch die physiologische Insulinwirkung im Kontext der Glukose-Homöostase. Dieser PI3K/AKT-Signalweg wird durch die PI3K-Inhibitor-Behandlung inhibiert ( Abb. 2 ). Dadurch reduzieren sich Glukosetransport, Glykogensynthese und Glykolyse um etwa 40 %. Die daraus resultierende Hyperglykämie wird daher als ein „On-Target“-Effekt der PI3K-Inhibition betrachtet 5 6 7 .

PI3K-Wirkmechanismus und Einfluss auf die Glukoseregulierung (nach 5 6 ).

1 Unter physiologischen Bedingungen steuert die Insulin-Signalübertragung die Glukoseaufnahme über den PI3K/AKT-Signalweg.

2 Die PI3K-Inhibition blockiert die AKT-vermittelte Translokation des Glukosetransporters GLUT4 an die Zellmembran.

3 Die Glukoseaufnahme aus dem Blut ist reduziert, die hepatische Glykogenolyse nimmt zu, wodurch mehr Glukose freigesetzt wird. → Es kommt zu einer Hyperglykämie mit einer erhöhten Insulinausschüttung in der Bauchspeicheldrüse (Hyperinsulinämie).

Wieso kommt es zu dieser Nebenwirkung?

Die kornealen Epithelialzellen belegen die äußere Schicht der Kornea und spielen eine wichtige Rolle bei der Aufrechterhaltung der kornealen epithelialen Homöostase, Barriere und optischer Funktionen. Der PI3K/AKT-Signalweg ist in die Proliferation, Apoptose, Migration und andere Funktionen der kornealen Epithelialzellen involviert und kann durch die Inhibition entsprechend beeinträchtigt werden 15 .

Empfohlen wird eine prophylaktische Befeuchtung bei trockenen Augen mit Präparaten zum Tränenflüssigkeitsersatz ohne Kortison-Zusatz (NaCl-Augentropfen, euphrasia- oder hyaluronhaltige Augentropfen).

Zusammenfassung

Prinzipiell empfiehlt sich eine mindestens wöchentlich erfolgende Interaktion und Überwachungsbegleitung der Patientin für den ersten Therapiezyklus . So können beginnende Symptome frühzeitig gezielt erkannt und ihnen kann entgegen gewirkt werden, wodurch eine bessere Therapieadhärenz gefördert wird. Prinzipiell kommen Therapiepausen, Dosisreduktionen (von 9 mg auf 6 mg und in einem weiteren Schritt von 6 mg auf 3 mg als niedrigste Dosierung) , weitere medikamentöse sowie andere therapeutische Maßnahmen zum Einsatz bis hin zum Absetzen der Inavolisib-Therapie je nach Schweregrad und Manifestation der unerwünschten Effekte 13 . In der Zulassungsstudie wurden unter Inavolisib-Therapie Therapie-Pausen aufgrund jeglicher Nebenwirkung bei 72,7 %, Dosisreduktionen bei 14,9 % und Therapieabbrüche bei 6,8 % der Studienteilnehmenden dokumentiert 3 . In jedem Fall sind die Vorgaben der Fachinformation 2 zu beachten.

Inverse Hamiltonian reduction in type A and generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian
Dylan Butson and Sujay Nair

Abstract
. We give a geometric proof of inverse Hamiltonian reduction for all finite W-algebras in type A A , a certain embedding of the finite W-algebra corresponding to an arbitrary nilpotent in 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 N \mathfrak{gl}_{N} into that corresponding to a larger nilpotent with respect to the closure order on orbits, tensored with an auxiliary algebra of differential operators. We first prove a classical analogue for equivariant Slodowy slices using multiplication maps on generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian, then deduce the result for equivariant finite W-algebras by Fedosov quantization. This implies the statement for finite W-algebras, as well as Kostant-Whittaker reductions of arbitrary algebras in the category of Harish Chandra bimodules, including quantizations of Moore–Tachikawa varieties.



1.
Introduction

The finite W-algebra
{\mathcal{W}}_{f}({{\mathfrak{g}}})
associated to a nilpotent element
f
in a semisimple Lie algebra
{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is an associative algebra quantizing the transverse slice
S_{f}\subset{{\mathfrak{g}}}
to the nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}_{f}=G\cdot f\subset{{\mathfrak{g}}}
. In type
A_{N-1}
, such orbits are parameterized by partitions of
N
, or equivalently tuples of positive integers
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geq...\geq\mu_{n-1}\geq 0)
such that the total sum
|\mu|=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\mu_{k}=N
; we call the positive integer
n-1
the length of such a partition, and identify the set of partitions of length at most
n-1
with the set of dominant, integral coweights
\Lambda^{+}({{\textup{PGL}}}_{n})
for the projective linear group
{{\textup{PGL}}}_{n}
.

In the case
\mu=[1^{N}]=(1,...,1)
for which
n-1=N
, the corresponding nilpotent
f_{\mu}=0\in{{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N}
, so that the slice
S_{0}={{\mathfrak{g}}}
and the finite W-algebra
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}({{\mathfrak{g}}})=U({{\mathfrak{g}}})
is simply the universal enveloping algebra of
{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, which naturally controls the representation theory of
{{\mathfrak{g}}}
. The algebras
{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{g}}})
are a natural family of generalizations thereof, which analogously control the categories of so-called Whittaker modules for
{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, with respect to a unipotent group
N_{\mu}
and character
\chi_{\mu}=\langle f_{\mu},\cdot\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\mu}^{\vee}
determined by
f_{\mu}
(up to some relatively insignificant auxiliary choices).

The primary goal of this paper is to provide a geometric paradigm for proving a family of results we call
inverse Hamiltonian reduction
, which can be understood most elementarily as the existence of certain highly structured embeddings of algebras. In particular, we establish the following theorem, which is essentially the statement of quantum inverse Hamiltonian reduction for finite W-algebras:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}({{\textup{PGL}}}_{n})
and
\alpha
a positive coroot of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n}
for which
\mu+\alpha\in\Lambda^{+}({{\textup{PGL}}}_{n})
. Then there exists an embedding of associative algebras with quantum Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-action

(1.0.1)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\hookrightarrow D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\ ,

where
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\times\ell(\alpha)}
denotes a rank
\ell(\alpha)
additive group scheme acting on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
,
D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}
denotes a localization of the algebra of differential operators on
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, and
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
acts trivially on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
.

We now recall a semi-classical analogue of Theorem
1.1
, and explain the geometric interpretation and significance of these statements in the context of (quantum) Hamiltonian reduction:

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a isomorphism of Poisson algebras with Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-action

\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})[E_{\alpha}^{-1}]\cong\mathcal{O}(T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})[\tilde{E}_{\alpha}^{-1}]\otimes\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}})\ ,

where
\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})[E_{\alpha}^{-1}]
denotes a localization of
\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})
,
\mathcal{O}(T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})[\tilde{E}_{\alpha}^{-1}]
a localization of
\mathcal{O}(T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})
, and
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
acts on
\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})
and
\mathcal{O}(T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})
preserving the localization ideals and trivially on
\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}})
.

In particular, composing with the natural map to the localization, we obtain

\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})[\tilde{E}_{\alpha}^{-1}]\otimes\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}})

a strongly
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant embedding of Poisson algebras analogous to Equation
1.0.1
. Geometrically, the localization
\mathcal{O}(S_{f_{\mu}})[E_{\alpha}^{-1}]
corresponds to a
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-invariant basic affine open
U_{\mu,\alpha}\subset S_{f_{\mu}}
, on which the Hamiltonian action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is appropriately trivialized. This property is abstracted by the following notion of inverse Hamiltonian reduction: Let
H
be an abelian group with
\mathfrak{h}={\textup{Lie}}(H)
,
\chi\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, and
Y
a Poisson variety with Hamiltonian
H
-action and moment map
\Phi:Y\to{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}
.

Definition 1.3
.

A (classical, finite) inverse Hamiltonian reduction of
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a pair
(U,\varphi)
where

•

U\subset{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}
is an open, Poisson, subvariety containing
\chi
, and

•

\varphi:H\times U\times Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\xrightarrow{\cong}Y\times_{\Phi,{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}}U
a strongly
H
-equivariant isomorphism of Poisson varieties,

with respect to the canonical symplectic structure on
H\times U\subset H\times{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}=T^{*}H
, as well as the natural Hamiltonian
H
-action on
H\times U
and the trivial
H
action on
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
.

Note that this gives an embedding
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\to Y
which can be thought of as inverting the Hamiltonian reduction procedure. More precisely, an inverse Hamiltonian reduction can be understood as a relative variant of the Gan–Ginzburg isomorphism, Lemma 2.1 of
[
GG02
]
; it requires that the moment map level set
\Phi^{-1}(\chi)\cong H\times Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a trivial H-torsor over the Hamiltonian reduction, as in
loc. cit.
, and moreover that such isomorphisms can be constructed compatibly as we vary
\chi\in U
. In particular, this implies that the inclusion of the open set
Y\times_{\Phi,{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}}U\to Y
induces an isomorphism

(Y\times_{\Phi,{{\mathfrak{h}}}^{*}}U)/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\xrightarrow{\cong}Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\ .

In the example of Theorem
1.2
, we take
H=\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and
{{\mathfrak{h}}}=\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}:={\textup{Lie}}(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})
, and this reduces to the statement that there is an open subset
U\subset\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}^{*}
such that the action map
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U\to(\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}\cap S_{f_{\mu}})
is an open immersion over which the family of transverse slices
S_{f}
for
f\in{\textup{im}}(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U)\subset(\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}\cap S_{f_{\mu}})
is trivialized, and thus we obtain the desired open immersion

(1.0.2)

\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U\times S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}\to S_{f_{\mu}}\ .

Another perspective on this result is that it provides a Darboux chart on
S_{f_{\mu}}
relative to the space
S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}
, in the sense that choosing a Darboux chart on the latter induces such on the former, noting
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U
is isomorphic to an open subset of
T^{*}\mathbb{A}^{\ell(\alpha)}
with the canonical symplectic form. This is the classical, finite-type analogue of the fact that our main theorem in the affine setting, described in Theorem
1.6
below, can be understood as a free field realization of
{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
relative to
{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
.

In fact, the proof of Theorem
1.1
follows from an analogous inverse quantum Hamiltonian reduction principle, as we now explain: the algebra
{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})=\Gamma(S_{f_{\mu}},\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}})
is the global sections of a sheaf of algebras
\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}}
quantizing the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{S_{f_{\mu}}}
, and we prove that its restriction to the image of the open immersion of Equation
1.0.2
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U\times S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{D}^{\rm loc}_{\alpha}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}

where
\mathcal{D}^{\rm loc}_{\alpha}
is the sheaf of differential operators on
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, extended to a sheaf on
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and restricted to
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U
. Thus, the restriction map on sections induces the desired embedding

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})=\Gamma(S_{f_{\mu}},\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}})\xrightarrow{\text{res}}\Gamma(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U\times S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}},\mathcal{D}^{\rm loc}_{\alpha}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}})=D_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\ .

Similarly, we generalize this approach to establish an abstract inverse quantum Hamiltonian reduction theorem in finite type
A
for Kostant–Whittaker reductions of arbitrary associative algebra objects in the category of Harish Chandra modules:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra in
\rm HC_{\hbar}
. There exists an embedding of associative algebras with quantum Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-action

A/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\hookrightarrow A/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}}N_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}}\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}\ .

In particular, we deduce the following corollary for the quantized coordinate rings of Moore–Tachikawa varieties:

Corollary 1.5
.

There is an embedding of associative algebras with quantum Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-action

\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}_{\hbar}[W_{\mu^{1},...,\mu^{b-1},\mu}({{\textup{SL}}}_{N})]{\ \hookrightarrow\ }\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}_{\hbar}[W_{\mu^{1},...,\mu^{b-1},\mu+\alpha}({{\textup{SL}}}_{N})]\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}\ ,

where
W_{\mu^{1},...,\mu^{b-1},\mu^{b}}({{\textup{SL}}}_{N})
denotes the non-principal Moore–Tachikawa variety of Definition
3.8
.

Finally, we mention the generalization of the preceding results to the setting of affine W-algebras, which is proved in the companion paper
[
BN25a
]
and was our primary motivation for this work:

Theorem 1.6
.

For generic
\kappa
, there exists an embedding of vertex algebras

{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\hookrightarrow D^{\textup{ch}}_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\ ,

compatible with the chiral Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-action, where
{D}^{\textup{ch}}_{\alpha}
denotes a localization of the vertex algebra
D^{\textup{ch}}(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})
of chiral differential operators on
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, and
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
acts trivially on
{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
.

Our proof of this theorem follows the same pattern: there exists a sheaf of vertex algebras
\mathcal{A}^{\kappa}_{f_{\mu}}
on
S_{f_{\mu}}
satisfying
\Gamma(S_{f_{\mu}},\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}}^{\kappa})={\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}^{\kappa}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
and we establish an isomorphism

\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}}^{\kappa}|_{\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U\times S_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{\textup{ch}}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu+\alpha}}^{\kappa}\ ,

where
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{\textup{ch}}
denotes the sheaf of chiral differential operators on
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, analogously extended to
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and restricted to
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times U
, so that the restriction map on sections of
\mathcal{A}_{f_{\mu}}^{\kappa}
induces the desired embedding.

1.1.
Relation with previous results

We now discuss the relation of these results to the existing literature. Although the contemporary context and motivation for inverse Hamiltonian reduction comes from the setting of vertex algebras, as we explain below, we begin by discussing some previous results in the finite-type setting.

Though perhaps less obviously than the other results we discuss below, Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
can be understood as generalizations of the main results of
[
KPW22
]
, and moreover our proof of the former follows from first proving a direct generalization of
loc. cit
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, dominant coweight
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{{{\textup{GL}}}_{n}}
, and arbitrary coweight
\mu\in\Lambda_{{{\textup{PGL}}}_{n}}
, we let
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
denote the
generalized slice
between orbits in the affine Grassmannian of
{{\textup{PGL}}}_{n}
, and we prove:

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists an open immersion of Poisson, Hamiltonian
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-varieties

\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}^{\lambda}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}^{\lambda}\ ,

where
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
acts trivially on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}^{\lambda}
, and by a certain canonical Hamiltonian action on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
.

We then use certain isomorphisms relating equivariant Slodowy slices in type
A
with generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian of
{\rm PGL}_{2N}
. Theorem
1.2
is therefore a special case of this more general result on affine Grassmannian slices.

Next we discuss the relation of our results with those of
[
Los07
]
. The embeddings of Equation
1.0.1
can be iteratively composed to yield

{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\hookrightarrow\bigotimes_{k=1}^{m}D_{\alpha_{k}}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\nu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})

for any
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda^{+}({{\textup{PGL}}}_{n})
such that
|\mu|=|\nu|=N
and
\mu\leq\nu
respect to the closure order on nilpotent orbits in
{{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N}
, as well as any choice of positive coroots
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n}
such that
\nu=\mu+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{k}
. Passing to formal completions with respect to the left ideal in
{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
corresponding to
f_{\nu}
, we obtain an isomorphism of complete, topological associative algebras

(1.1.1)

{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}}^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\cong}(A(V_{\mu,\nu})\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\nu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N}))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\nu}}^{\wedge}

where
A(V_{\mu,\nu})
denotes the Weyl algebra associated to the symplectic vector space

V_{\mu,\nu}=T_{f_{\nu}}(\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{f_{\nu}}\cap S_{f_{\mu}})\ ,

the tangent space to the intersection
\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{f_{\nu}}\cap S_{f_{\mu}}
, which is symplectic by construction.

In the special case that
\mu=[1^{N}]
so that
\nu
may be arbitrary, and moreover
f_{\mu}=0
and thus
{\mathcal{W}}_{f_{\mu}}({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})\cong U({{\mathfrak{gl}}}_{N})
, the isomorphism of Equation
1.1.1
is precisely Theorem 1.2.1 from
[
Los07
]
. Thus, Theorem
1.1
can be considered as a generalization (under the more restrictive hypothesis that
{{\mathfrak{g}}}
be of type
A
) of
loc. cit.
to general pairs of W-algebras associated to nilpotents
\mu\leq\nu
, as well as a strengthening of the result in the sense that we lift the isomorphism of formal completions to a Zariski neighborhood. Of course, one would also like to analogously generalize the other main results of
loc. cit.
, especially Theorem 1.2.2. We hope to pursue this question in future work, as well as some related results about module categories in the affine setting, as we discuss below.

The results of this paper can also be understood as a generalization of the main theorem of
[
KPW22
]
to the case of higher rank additive group schemes
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, translated under the so-called Mirković–Vybornov isomorphism
[
MVK22
]
to the setting of slices to nilpotent orbits and in turn finite W-algebras. Indeed, our proof of Theorem
1.2
, on which the remainder of our results are built, follows the proof in
[
KPW22
]
very closely. Moreover, the results of the companion papers
[
BN25a
]
and
[
BN25b
]
should be understood as part of a broader program to extend the results of
[
FKP+18
]
and
[
WWY20
]
, and in turn
[
BK04b
]
, to the affine setting in a compatible way.

The original context of inverse Hamiltonian reduction was in the case of affine W-algebras, though a precise geometric definition did not appear prior to the present work. The first result of this kind was due to Semikhatov
[
Sem94
]
, who constructed an embedding

V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}){\ \hookrightarrow\ }D_{\alpha}^{\textup{ch}}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

by comparing the FMS bosonization
[
FMS86
]
of the Wakimoto resolution
[
Wak86
]
of
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
with the Feigin–Frenkel resolution
[
FF96
]
of
{\mathcal{W}}^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

This result was recently re-popularized by Dražen Adamović, who showed in
[
Ada04
,
Ada19
]
that the result above in type
A_{1}
descends to an embedding of the simple quotient vertex algebras, and used this to relate their categories of modules in order to express that category of modules over the affine algebra in terms of the more simple category of modules over the principal affine W-algebra for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Following this, many papers were written studying this phenomena in increasingly general families of examples: see
[
ACG24
]
,
[
Feh23b
]
,
[
Feh23a
]
,
[
CFLN24
]
,
[
FFFN24
]
, and
[
FKN25
]
. The proof of the analogous embeddings in all of these papers rely on analogous ad hoc combinatorial arguments relating the explicit descriptions of the vertex algebras in terms of so-called screening operators. The goal of this series of papers is to establish the general result in type
A
at generic level
\kappa
and for arbitrary nilpotents, as well as to give a geometric proof providing a more conceptual explanation for the results.

Finally, both this work and its companion should be viewed as part of a series of recent works in the vertex algebra literature where free field realizations are constructed geometrically—via localization. In particular, the viewpoint that (sheaves of) vertex algebras should be viewed as geometric objects over a space and that free field realizations should be viewed as arising from restricting these sheaves to open subsets. This has been used to great effect in
[
ACM11
]
,
[
BMR19
]
,
[
BN23
]
,
[
But23
]
,
[
BR23
]
,
[
Fur23
]
,
[
AKM23
]
,
[
BDM+24
]
.

2.
Generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian

2.1.
The affine Grassmannian

Let
G
be an affine algebraic group,
X
a smooth algebraic curve over
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}
, and for a closed point
x\in X
let
\mathbb{D}_{x}=X^{\wedge}_{\{x\}}={\textup{Spf}\ }\mathcal{O}_{x}
be the formal disk at
x
and
\mathbb{D}_{x}^{\times}=X^{\wedge}_{\{x\}}-\{x\}={\textup{Spf}\ }\mathcal{K}_{x}
, the punctured formal disk, where
\mathcal{O}_{x}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\wedge}
denotes the complete local ring at
x\in X
and
\mathcal{K}_{x}
denotes its field of fractions. We also write simply
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![t]\!]}}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{(\!(t)\!)}}
as well as
\mathbb{D}=(\mathbb{A}^{1})^{\wedge}_{\{0\}}={\textup{Spf}\ }\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\times}=(\mathbb{A}^{1})^{\wedge}_{\{0\}}\setminus\{0\}={\textup{Spf}\ }\mathcal{K}
for the model examples on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
at
0
, which are (non-canonically) isomorphic to those at any smooth point
x
on any curve
X
, by the Cohen structure theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

The (thin) affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
is the stack that parameterizes pairs
(\mathcal{P},\sigma)
where

•

\mathcal{P}\in\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a principal
G
-bundle on
\mathbb{D}
; and

•

\sigma:\mathcal{P}|_{\mathbb{D}^{\times}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{P}^{\textup{triv}}
is a trivialization of the restriction
\mathcal{P}|_{\mathbb{D}^{\times}}\in\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{D}^{\times})
of
\mathcal{P}
to
\mathbb{D}^{\times}
.

As we will explain, the space
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
is in fact an indscheme. It is modeled concretely by the following quotient stack description, induced by the identifications

(2.1.1)

\mathrm{Gr}_{G}={\textup{pt}}\underset{\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{K})\right]}{\times}\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{O})\right]\cong\left[G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})\right]~,

where
G(\mathcal{O})
and
G(\mathcal{K})
denote the
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{K}
points of the algebraic group
G
. Concretely, one can choose a trivialization of
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathbb{D}
, after which the data of
\sigma
determines an element of
G(\mathcal{K})
that is well-defined up to a change of the choice of trivialization, or equivalently precomposition with an automorphism of the trivial bundle on
\mathbb{D}
, which is given by the action of
G(\mathcal{O})
on the right.

Note that
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}=\left[G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})\right]
admits a left action of
G(\mathcal{O})
, induced by that on
G(\mathcal{K})
, and for
G
reductive we have the stratifications

G(\mathcal{K})=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{G}}G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda}G(\mathcal{O})\quad\quad\text{and thus}\quad\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{G}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+}}\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\lambda}\quad\quad\text{where}\quad\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\lambda}=G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda}

for
\Lambda_{G}^{+}
is the set of dominant coweights of
G
and
t^{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
defined by the restriction of the corresponding cocharacter
\lambda:\mathbb{\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}}^{\times}\to G
to the formal neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\supset\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.

Further, each
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\lambda}
is a finite type scheme, and the
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
action on
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
by ‘loop rotation’ extends to a contraction
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\lambda}{\ \twoheadrightarrow\ }G\cdot t^{\lambda}=G/P_{\mu}

where
P_{\mu}\subset G
is the corresponding parabolic subgroup, with Levi quotient
\textup{Stab}_{G}(\lambda)
. Thus,
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\lambda}
is a finite dimensional vector bundle over
G/P^{\lambda}
, as the attracting set fibers are equidimensional affine spaces. Finally, we let

\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\leq\lambda}:=\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G}^{\lambda}=\bigsqcup_{\mu\leq\lambda}\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\mu}\quad\quad\text{so that}\quad\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{G}={\textup{colim}}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{G}^{+}}\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\leq\lambda}\ ,

and note that each
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{\leq\lambda}
is a projective scheme of dimension
\langle 2\rho^{\vee},\lambda\rangle
, so that
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
is an ind-projective, ind-finite type indscheme.

More generally, for a smooth, projective curve
X
and closed point
x\in X
, we can define the (non-canonically) equivalent space
\mathrm{Gr}_{G,x}=\left[G(\mathcal{K}_{x})/G(\mathcal{O}_{x})\right]
and we have a natural isomorphism

(2.1.2)

\Bun_{G}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\left[G(\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus\{x\}})\backslash G(\mathcal{K}_{x})/G(\mathcal{O}_{x})\right]=\left[G(\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus\{x\}})\backslash\mathrm{Gr}_{G,x}\right]

by Beauville–Laszlo descent
[
BL95
]
, so that we obtain the following equivalent modular description:

Corollary 2.2
.

The stack
\textup{Gr}_{G,x}
is equivalent to that parameterizing pairs
(\mathcal{P},\sigma)
where

•

\mathcal{P}\in\Bun_{G}(X)
is a principal
G
-bundle on
X
; and

•

\sigma:\mathcal{P}|_{X\setminus\{x\}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{P}^{\textup{triv}}
is a trivialization of the restriction
\mathcal{P}|_{X\setminus\{x\}}\in\Bun_{G}(X\setminus\{x\})
.

In particular, we identify
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
with
\mathrm{Gr}_{G,0}
for the point
0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}=X
, and we have

(2.1.3)

\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\xrightarrow{\cong}\left[G[t^{-1}]\backslash G{{(\!(t)\!)}}/G{{[\![t]\!]}}\right]\quad\quad\text{and thus}\quad\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{G}\cong\left[G{{(\!(t)\!)}}/G{{[\![t]\!]}}\right]\ ,

as above. Note that we also have the alternative, equivalent description by Čech descent

(2.1.4)

\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\xrightarrow{\cong}\left[G[t^{-1}]\backslash G[t^{\pm 1}]/G[t]\right]\quad\quad\text{and thus}\quad\quad\mathrm{Gr}_{G}\cong\left[G[t^{\pm 1}]/G[t]\right]\ .

Definition 2.3
.

•

Note that restriction of the trivialization

2.2.
Generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian

Recall that for a subgroup

is a principal

-bundle

. For example, in the case that

Let

-bundles on

with a reduction of structure to

at the point

where we define the first congruence and Iwahori subgroups of

as the fiber products

with respect to the evaluation homomorphism

\textbf{Gr}_{G}\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{\infty}\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{(B,\infty)}\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\ ,

to

to a choice of

-structure, and forgetting the

-structure entirely, respectively.

The map

\Bun_{B}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\bigsqcup_{\mu\in\Lambda}\Bun_{B}^{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\quad\quad\text{where}\quad\quad\Bun_{B}^{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\Bun_{B}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\times_{\Bun_{T}(\mathbb{P}^{1})}\Bun_{T}^{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{1})

denotes the substack of

parameterizing

-bundles of degree
\mu\in\Lambda
. Further, note that the stack of
B
-bundles is evidently equivalent to the stack of
G
-bundles equipped with a global

-structure, so that restriction of the

-structure to

defines a map

\Bun_{B}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{(B,\infty)}\quad\quad\text{modeled by}\quad\quad\left[B{{[\![t^{-1}]\!]}}\backslash B{{(\!(t^{-1})\!)}}/B[t]\right]\to\left[I_{\infty}\backslash G{{(\!(t^{-1})\!)}}/G[t]\right]\ ,

,

We now give the definition of generalized slices for

.

As for the preceding spaces, we again have the natural quotient stack description

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}_{G}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\cong{\textup{pt}}\times_{\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{K})\right]}\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{O})\right]\times_{\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{K})\right]}\ldots\times_{\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{K})\right]}\left[{\textup{pt}}/G(\mathcal{O})\right]

-actions on the right on the first factor and on the left on the second, and similarly the further iterated products are defined inductively. Note there is a natural convolution map

, or equivalently in terms of the quotient stack description

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\underline{\lambda}}_{G}\cong\left[\overline{G(\mathcal{K})}^{\lambda_{1}}\times_{G(\mathcal{O})}\overline{G(\mathcal{K})}^{\lambda_{2}}\times_{G(\mathcal{O})}\ldots\times_{G(\mathcal{O})}\overline{G(\mathcal{K})}^{\lambda_{n}}/G(\mathcal{O})\right]\ ,

where

denotes the closure of the

-orbit of

in

. Evidently the convolution map restricts to a map

.

2.3.
Beilinson–Drinfeld generalized slices

Next, we define the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, a factorization analogue of the usual affine Grassmannian, which will provide a natural deformation of the spaces
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic curve and
I
be a finite set.

Definition 2.7
.

The Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}^{\textup{BD}}_{G,X^{I}}
over

is the stack parameterizing tuples
((x_{i})_{i\in I},\mathcal{P},\sigma)
where

•

(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in X^{I}
is a closed point of the
I
-fold product

;

•

\mathcal{P}\in\Bun_{G}(X)
is a principal
G
-bundle on
X
; and

•

is a trivialization of the restriction
\mathcal{P}|_{X\setminus\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}}\in\Bun_{G}(X\setminus\{x\})
.

Evidently there is a natural map
\mathrm{Gr}^{\textup{BD}}_{G,X^{I}}\to X^{I}
, and for
I=\{1\}
so that
X^{I}=X
we have

\mathrm{Gr}^{\textup{BD}}_{G,X}\times_{X}\{x\}=\mathrm{Gr}_{G,x}\ ,

for each closed point
x\in X
, where
\mathrm{Gr}_{G,x}
is as in Corollary
2.2
above. More generally, for each
(x_{i})\in X^{I}
, the fiber of
\mathrm{Gr}_{G,X^{I}}^{\textup{BD}}
is given by

\mathrm{Gr}^{\textup{BD}}_{G,X^{I}}\times_{X^{I}}\{(x_{i})\}\cong\prod_{y\in\{x_{i}\}}\mathrm{Gr}_{G,y}\ ,

where the product is over the distinct points

. Note that this space depends on the tuple

only via

, and in particular ignores any repetitions

for

\mathrm{Gr}_{G,X^{I}}\times_{X^{I}}X^{J}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{Gr}_{G,X^{J}}

for any partial diagonal embedding
\Delta(\pi):X^{J}{\ \hookrightarrow\ }X^{I}
corresponding to a surjection
\pi:I{\ \twoheadrightarrow\ }J
.

We consider the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and for each finite set

define the space
\underline{\textup{Gr}}^{I}_{G}\to\mathbb{A}^{I}

(2.3.1)

Note that the support of the trivialization
\sigma

always contains

\underline{\textup{Gr}}^{I}_{G}\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{\infty}\quad\quad\text{and thus}\quad\quad\underline{\textup{Gr}}^{I}_{G}\to\Bun_{G}(\mathbb{P}^{1})_{(B,\infty)}

given by restriction of the trivialization
\sigma
to
\infty
, and further forgetting the trivialization to a choice of
B
structure at
\infty
, respectively.

Given a finite set
I
and an
I
-tuple
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
of dominant coweights, there is a natural substack

\overline{\underline{\textup{Gr}}}^{\underline{\lambda}}_{G}\subset\underline{\textup{Gr}}_{G}^{I}
defined by the condition that the trivialization

has a pole at each point

(2.3.2)

where the product is again over the distinct points

Equivariant Slodowy slices

The transversal slices

, such that for any

, its image under the differential of the action,

agrees with the derivation

A
H
-equivariant quantization,
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)
, of

is strongly

-equivariant if its global sections are a strongly
H
-equivariant quantization.

, of strongly equivariant algebras (Poisson or associative) is strongly

-equivariant if it is
H
-equivariant and intertwines the comoment maps,
i.e.
the following diagram commutes

(4.2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm id}

Such strongly equivariant quantizations have been classified, in the

[

. Recently, a deformation theory argument has appeared in

Proposition 4.3
.

The set of isomorphism classes of strongly equivariant graded quantizations is in bijection with the second
H
-equivariant cohomology

that is, any two quantizations

4.3.
Quantum Hamiltonian reduction

Let
\chi\in(\mathfrak{h}^{*})^{H}
be a character of
\mathfrak{h}
and suppose that
H
acts on
\Phi^{-1}(\chi)
freely, then we may define the symplectic reduction

(4.3.1)

Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\coloneqq\Phi^{-1}(\chi)/H~.

Thanks to the freeness hypothesis, the reduced space is smooth, symplectic and we denote its structure sheaf as
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
, which is a sheaf of Poisson algebras.

We need a quantized version of this reduction that produces a quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H}
from a quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
. We follow the exposition of
[
Los12
]
.

At the level of global sections, we have the following construction. Let
(A,\varphi)
be a strongly equivariant quantization with comoment map
\Phi^{\#})
. Consider the subspace,
\mathfrak{h}_{\chi}\subset U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{h})
, of elements of the form
x-\chi(x)\hbar
for
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and write
\langle\Phi^{\#}(\mathfrak{h}_{\chi})\rangle
for the two sided ideal generated by its image under
\Phi^{\#}
.

The quantum Hamiltonian reduction of
A
by
H
at
\chi
is

(4.3.2)

A/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\coloneqq\big(A/\langle\Phi^{\#}(\mathfrak{h}_{\chi})\rangle\big)^{H}~.

It is not true in general that
A_{\rm KW}
is a quantization of
Y_{\rm KW}
but for a nice enough
H
-action this is the case—we shall give sufficient conditions shortly.

Now let us consider the sheaf theoretic version of this construction. Let
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi,\Phi^{\#})
be a strongly
H
-equivariant quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
. The ideal
\langle\Phi^{\#}(\mathfrak{h})\rangle
gives rise to a sheaf,
{\mathcal{I}}_{\chi}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules. The quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{\chi}
is a sheaf of
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules supported, by construction, on
\Phi^{-1}(\chi)
. Let
p_{H}:\Phi^{-1}(\chi)\rightarrow\Phi^{-1}(\chi)/H
be the natural projection, then

(4.3.3)

{\mathcal{A}}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\coloneqq p_{H,*}\big({\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{\chi}\big)

is a sheaf on
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
, called the quantum Hamiltonian reduction of
{\mathcal{A}}
. The reduction,
{\mathcal{A}}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a sheaf of equivariant associative algebras, flat over
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
and complete in the
\hbar
-adic topology. However, it is not clear that its classical limit agrees with
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
H
acts on
Y
freely and the moment map
\Phi:Y\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is flat in a neighborhood around
\chi
. Then, if
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi,\Phi^{\#})
is any strongly
H
-equivariant quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
,
{\mathcal{A}}\underset{\chi}{/\!\!/\!\!/}H
is a quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{\rm KW}}
. Moreover, if
A
is any strongly
H
-equivariant quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}(Y)
, then
A/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}(Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H)
.

Therefore, when the
H
-action is sufficiently nice, quantization commutes with reduction.

4.4.
Equivariant W-algebras

Consider the cotangent bundle
T^{*}G
, with its natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
that scales the fibers with weight
1
. Moreover it has an obvious graded quantization given by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar}(G)
, the sheaf of homogeneous differential operators on
G
.

Let
\mu
be some partition of
n
and
\mathbb{O}_{\mu}
the corresponding nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Pick a representative
\chi\in\mathbb{O}_{\mu}
and construct
N_{\chi}
as in Section
3.1
. Since the action of
N_{\chi}
is everywhere free and the moment map is flat, Proposition
4.4
implies that,

(4.4.1)

{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}\coloneqq{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,G}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}N_{\chi}~,

is a quantization of the equivariant Slodowy slice
S_{G,\mu}
. In fact,
{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}
is a graded quantization where the
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-action comes from the Kazhdan grading of (
3.1.5
).

Its global sections are

(4.4.2)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\equiv\Gamma(S_{G,\mu},{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu})~,

the finite equivariant,
\hbar
-adic W-algebra of
[
Los07
]
. This is a graded quantization of
S_{G,\mu}
which is weakly
G
-equivariant for the right action of
G
on
S_{G,\mu}
.

We recover the usual (
\hbar
-adic) finite W-algebra by taking invariants with respect to the right
G
action:

(4.4.3)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}^{G}~,

and we may recover the non
\hbar
-adic finite W-algebra by

(4.4.4)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mu}\cong\big({\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}\underset{\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}}{\otimes}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}(\!(\hbar)\!)\big)^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}~,

since the Kazhdan grading is bounded below.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any partition
\mu
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}
is the unique graded quantization of
S_{G,\mu}
.

Proof.

The space of graded quantizations of
S_{G,\mu}
is given by
H^{2}_{\rm dR}(S_{G,\mu})
. But
S_{G,\mu}
contracts to
G
and so
H^{2}_{\rm dR}(S_{G,\mu})=H^{2}_{\rm dR}(G)=0
. Thus, there is a unique isomorphism class of quantizations.
∎

4.5.
Quantizing Zastava

We shall also require quantizations of the Zastava spaces from Section
2.5
. Once again, let
\alpha
be a positive coroot in
{\rm PGL}_{n}
of height
k
.

From Lemma
2.14
, we note that
{\rm Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
is an open symplectic subvariety of
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and so has a natural quantization given by localizing the sheaf of differential operators on
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
.

Write
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,\alpha}
for the sheaf of (homogenized) differential operators on
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
. We denote its restriction to
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
by
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}
. Its global sections,
D_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}=\Gamma(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha},{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc})
is the free, associative
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
-algebra on the generators
(p,e^{\pm},b_{i},g_{i})
subject to the relations

(4.5.1)

\begin{split}e^{+}e^{-}&=e^{-}e^{+}=1\\
[p,e^{\pm}]=\pm\hbar e^{\pm}~&,\qquad[b_{i},g_{i}]=\hbar~.\end{split}

The Zastava has a natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
by loop rotation, which scales
p,b,e^{+}
with weight
1
and leaves the
g_{i}
invariant. This corresponds to the restriction of the natural
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-action that scales the cotangent fibers of
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
with weight
1
. Clearly, this action preserves the algebra structure on the global sections.

Lemma 4.6
.

Up to isomorphism,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}
is the unique, graded quantization of
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
.

Proof.

The space of graded quantizations of
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
is isomorphic to
H^{2}_{\rm dR}(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha})
by Proposition
4.1
. But,
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
contracts to
S^{1}
and so its second cohomology vanishes and there is only one quantization up to isomorphism.
∎

5.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction

Now let us specialize so that
H
is abelian. The
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}
-scheme
Y
continues to be affine, symplectic, and equipped with a Hamiltonian action of
H
, with moment map
\Phi:Y\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Moreover, we continue to assume that
Y
is equipped with an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
, that scales the symplectic form with weight
-1
.

Remark 5.1
.

Consider
T^{*}H
with its canonical symplectic structure. The left moment map
T^{*}H\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, is
H
-equivariant and Poisson. Suppose
U
is some open Poisson subset of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, then

has a natural Poisson structure arising from viewing it as the preimage of

under the left moment map of
T^{*}H
. It is equipped with a strong action of
H
, inheriting the moment map of
T^{*}H
. Moreover, if
U
is
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-stable, for the action that scales
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with unit weight, then

inherits an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
. Henceforth,
H\times U
is assumed to have this symplectic structure in the rest of this section.

Definition 5.2
.

An inverse Hamiltonian reduction of
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a pair
(U,f)
, where

(i)

U\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is an open,
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-stable Poisson subvariety, such that
\chi\in U
.

(ii)

f:H\times U\times Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\xrightarrow{\sim}Y\underset{\Phi,\mathfrak{h}^{*}}{\times}U
is a strongly
H
-equivariant, isomorphism of Poisson schemes.

We say that
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
admits an inverse Hamiltonian reduction (IHR), if there exists some IHR
(U,f)
satisfying the conditions of Definition
5.2
.

The map
\Phi:Y\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is
H
-equivariant but
Y
is not necessarily a
H
-torsor over
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. However, if
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
admits an IHR, there is some dense open set
U\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
over which
Y
is a
H
-torsor and moreover is trivial over
U
, with
f
an explicit trivialization.

The pullback of functions along
H\times U\times Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\xrightarrow{f}Y\underset{\mu,\mathfrak{h}^{*}}{\times}U\rightarrow Y
gives a strongly
H
-equivariant embedding
{\mathcal{O}}(Y)\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(H\times U)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H)
of Poisson algebras. Hence, the nomenclature inverse Hamiltonian reduction.

It follows from definition that

(5.0.1)

\big(Y\underset{\mu,\mathfrak{h}^{*}}{\times}U\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H\cong Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H~.

This is the characterizing property of IHR tuple and it lifts to the quantum setting.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose the reduction
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
admits some IHR
(U,f)
, and
{\mathcal{A}}
is a strongly
H
-equivariant graded quantization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
, then

(i)

{\mathcal{A}}|_{\mu^{-1}(U)}\underset{\chi}{/\!\!/\!\!/}H\cong{\mathcal{A}}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H

{\mathcal{A}}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
is a quantization of

.

Proof.

Note that
(i)
follows immediately from the fact that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a sheaf and
\mu^{-1}(\chi)
is contained in
\mu^{-1}(U)
.

For

, we note that
\mu^{-1}(U)
admits, by construction, a flat moment map to
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and is a
H
-torsor over it, thus by Lemma 3.3.1 of
[
Los12
]
, the quantum Hamiltonian reduction of

is a quantization of the reduction of
\mu^{-1}(U)
, which is
Y/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi}H
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
U
be an open,
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-stable Poisson subvariety of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
containing
\chi
, and consider

Since

whose fibers are

]
applies; thus,

∎

Definition 5.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a strongly
H
-equivariant graded quantization of

. A quantum inverse Hamiltonian reduction for
{\mathcal{A}}
is a tuple

(i)

(ii)

.

Since

and

are
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-equivariant,

n
dimensional representation of
{\rm GL}_{n}
. Our choice of
\alpha
gives rise to a splitting
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
where
V_{2}
is a
k+1
dimensional subspace stabilized by the image of
\tau_{\alpha}
.

A matrix
u\in N^{\alpha}
is, schematically, of the form

(7.0.4)

u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{11}&u_{12}&u_{13}\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{V_{2}}&u_{23}\\
0&0&u_{33}\end{pmatrix}~,

where
u_{ij}\in{\rm Hom}(V_{j},V_{i})
with
u_{ii}
unipotent. Now if
g\in{\rm GL}_{k+1}
, then conjugation by
\tau_{\alpha}(g)
is

(7.0.5)

\begin{split}\tau_{\alpha}(g)u\tau_{\alpha}(g)^{-1}&=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{1}}&0&0\\
0&g&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{V_{3}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}u_{11}&u_{12}&u_{13}\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{V_{2}}&u_{23}\\
0&0&u_{33}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{V_{1}}&0&0\\
0&g^{-1}&0\\
0&0&\mathbbm{1}_{V_{3}}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}u_{11}&u_{12}\circ g^{-1}&u_{13}\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{V_{2}}&g\circ u_{23}\\
0&0&u_{33}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

which is in
N^{\alpha}
, as desired.
∎

7.1.
An action of the translation group

Let
\mathbb{G}_{a}
be the additive group, we write
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\equiv\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k}
for the group of translations on
\mathbb{A}^{k}
.

Let
\underline{v}\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, thought of as being embedded in
{\rm GL}_{k+1}
via

(7.1.1)

\underline{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k})\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v_{k}&v_{k-1}&\dots&v_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}

To that end, we abuse notation to write
x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})
for the composition

(7.1.2)

\underline{v}\mapsto x_{-\alpha}(\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v_{k}&v_{k-1}&\dots&v_{1}&1\end{pmatrix})~.

Proposition 7.2

(
[
KP21
, Remark 2.8]
)
.

For any coweight
\mu
of
\rm PGL_{n}
, the transversal slice,
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
has an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
given by

(7.1.3)

\underline{v}\cdot g=\pi(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})g)~,

for
\underline{v}\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
,
g\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
and where
\pi:N(\!(t^{-1})\!)t^{\mu}T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]N_{-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
is the map from
6.5.2
.

Indeed, this action will later be shown to be Hamiltonian, for now we construct a candidate for its moment map.

Let
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}\coloneqq{\rm Lie}\,\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
thought of as a commutative, Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{n}
generated by the roots
(\alpha_{r_{1}},\alpha_{r_{1}}+\alpha_{r_{1}+1},\dots,\sum_{i=r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\alpha_{i})
. Define
\psi_{\alpha}:N\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
by

(7.1.4)

a\mapsto(a_{r_{2},r_{2}+1},a_{r_{2}-1,r_{2}+1},\dots,a_{r_{1},r_{2}+1})~,

i.e.
, we extract the matrix entries in the rightmost column of the image of
\tau_{\alpha}
.

This lifts, naturally to a map on the loop spaces
\psi_{\alpha}:N(\!(t^{-1)})\!)\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
. Moreover, we can extend
\psi_{\alpha}
to a map
\psi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}{{(\!(t)\!)}}
by

(7.1.5)

\psi_{\alpha}(uht^{\mu}u_{-})=\psi_{\alpha}(u)~,

where
u\in N_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
,
h\in T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
and
u_{-}\in N_{-,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
parameterize elements of the slice by their triangular decomposition.

We shall be interested in the Fourier modes of
\psi_{\alpha}
and its components, so we write

(7.1.6)

\psi_{\alpha}(u)=\bigg(\sum_{j}\psi_{\alpha,1}^{(j)}(u)t^{-j},\sum_{j}\psi_{\alpha,2}^{(j)}(u)t^{-j},\dots,\sum_{j}\psi_{\alpha,k}^{(j)}(u)t^{-j}\bigg)~.

In particular, we use
\Phi_{\alpha}:N(\!(t^{-1})\!)\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
for the restriction to the first Fourier modes,
i.e.
,

(7.1.7)

\Phi_{\alpha}(u)=\bigg(\psi_{\alpha,1}^{(1)}(u),\psi_{\alpha_{2}}^{(1)}(u),\dots,\psi_{\alpha,k}^{(1)}(u)\bigg)

Similarly, we introduce
\zeta_{\alpha}:N(\!(t^{-1})\!)\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}^{k}
defined on an element
n\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)
by

(7.1.8)

\zeta_{\alpha}(n)=(n_{r_{1},r_{1}+1}^{(1)},n_{r_{1},r_{1}+2}^{(1)},\dots,n_{r_{1},r_{2}-1}^{(1)},n_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{(2)})~,

where
n_{ij}^{(l)}
is the
l
th Fourier mode of the entry at position
(i,j)
Like
\psi_{\alpha}
, these maps extend naturally to the slice
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
by

(7.1.9)

\Phi_{\alpha}(uht^{\mu}u_{-})\equiv\Phi_{\alpha}(u),\text{ and }\zeta_{\alpha}(uht^{\mu}u_{-})\equiv\zeta_{\alpha}(u)~.

7.2.
Action on Zastava

Now we focus on the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
on the slice
\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
. Recall the co-ordinates
(p,e,b_{i},g_{i})
from (
2.5.2
).

Lemma 7.3
.

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
defined by Proposition
7.2
simplifies to

(7.2.1)

\underline{v}\cdot g=\pi(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})g)=x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})g~.

and corresponds to translating the co-ordinates as

(7.2.2)

g_{i}\mapsto g_{i}+v_{i}~,\qquad p\mapsto p+v_{k}e~,

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
y\in\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
be a matrix representative, then
\Phi_{\alpha}(y)
and
\zeta_{\alpha}(y)
are given by

(7.2.3)

\begin{split}\Phi_{\alpha}(y)&=\bigg(b_{1}(y),b_{2}(y),\dots,b_{k-1}(y),e(y)\bigg)~,\\
\zeta_{\alpha}(y)&=\bigg(g_{k-1}(y)e(y),g_{k-2}(y)e(y),\dots,g_{1}(y)e(y),p(y)e(y)+e(y)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}(y)g_{i}(y)\bigg)~,\end{split}

where
(b_{i},g_{i},e,p)
are the co-ordinates introduced in (
2.5.2
).

Proof.

A proof essentially amounts to having a good understanding of the Gaussian decomposition of the matrix representative
y\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
.

Write
y=UDL
, we want to compute a number of the matrix entries of
U\in N_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. To do so we can proceed recursively. Let
y_{m}
be the lower
m\times m
block of
y
.

Suppose
y_{m}=U_{m}D_{m}L_{m}
is a Gaussian decomposition of the lower block. Then we can compute the decomposition of
y_{m+1}=U_{m+1}D_{m+1}L_{m+1}
as follows. Write,

(7.2.4)

y_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{n-m-1,n-m-1}&\underline{y}_{n-m,\bullet}\\
\underline{y}_{\bullet,n-m}&y_{m}\end{pmatrix}

with

(7.2.5)

U_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\underline{u}_{m+1}\\
0&U_{m}\end{pmatrix}~,\qquad D_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{m+1}&0\\
0&D_{m}\end{pmatrix}~,\qquad L_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\underline{l}_{m+1}&L_{m}\end{pmatrix}~,

Then, one can show that
d_{m+1}={\rm det}(y_{m+1})/{\rm det}(y_{m})
, and

(7.2.6)

\underline{u}_{m+1}=\underline{y}_{n-m,\bullet}\cdot L_{m}^{-1}\cdot D_{m}^{-1}~,\qquad\underline{l}_{m+1}=D_{m}^{-1}\cdot U_{m}^{-1}\cdot\underline{y}_{\bullet,n-m}

Suppose,
m<n-1
, then
\underline{y}_{n-m,\bullet}=(0,0,\dots,b_{m-1})
and
\underline{y}_{\bullet,n-m}=(0,0,\dots,g_{m-1})^{\rm T}
. So from (
7.2.6
), we see that

(7.2.7)

\underline{u}_{m+1}=(\ast,\ast,\dots,\ast,b_{m-1}/D_{1})~,\qquad\underline{l}_{m+1}=(\ast,\ast,\dots,\ast,-g_{m-1}/D_{1})~,

where
\ast
represent nontrivial terms yet to be determined.

Looking at the realization
y
, we see that
{\rm det}\,y_{m}=t-p-\sum_{i=m}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}
, and so

(7.2.8)

D={\rm diag}\bigg(\frac{1}{t-p},\frac{t-p}{t-p-b_{k-1}g_{k-1}},\frac{t-p-b_{k-1}g_{k-1}}{t-p+b_{k_{2}}g_{k-1}-b_{k-1}g_{k-1}},\dots,\frac{t-p-\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}},t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}\bigg)~,

which of course factorizes as
D=t^{-\alpha}D^{\prime}
with
D^{\prime}\in T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
, as expected. Moreover, we see that the terms
D_{ii}\sim O(t^{0})
for
1<i<k
, with
D_{kk}\sim O(t)
and
D_{11}\sim O(t^{-1})
.

We know that
y_{1}=t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}
has a Gaussian decomposition with

(7.2.9)

U_{1}=L_{1}=1~,\qquad D_{1}=t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}~.

We see that

(7.2.10)

\underline{u}_{j}=(\ast,\ast,\dots,\ast,\frac{b_{j-1}}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}})~,\qquad\underline{l}_{j}=(\ast,\ast,\dots,\ast,\frac{-g_{j-1}}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}})~,

for
j<k
. From this, we see that

(7.2.11)

\Phi_{\alpha}(y)=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},\dots,b_{k-1},\ast)~,

so we have yet to determine the final component of
\Phi_{\alpha}
and the components of
\zeta_{\alpha}
. These are extracted from the top row of
U
, which is computed by

(7.2.12)

\underline{u}_{k}=y_{1,\bullet}\cdot L_{k-1}^{-1}D_{k-1}^{-1}=(0,0,\dots,e)\cdot L_{k-1}^{-1}D_{k-1}^{-1}~,

which just picks out the bottom row of
L_{k-1}^{-1}D_{k-1}^{-1}
. We see immediately that

(7.2.13)

\underline{u}_{k}=(\ast,\ast,\dots,\ast,\frac{e}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}})~,

which fixes
\Phi_{\alpha}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k-1},e)
. Now we need to fix the rest of the entries. First note that
L_{k-1}\in N_{-,k,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
, since
L_{k}\in N_{-,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. So its inverse is of the form

(7.2.14)

L_{k-1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
g_{k-1}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})&g_{k-2}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})&\dots&g_{1}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})&1\end{pmatrix}

Moreover from (
7.2.8
), we see that

(7.2.15)

D_{k}^{-1}=\bigg(1+O(t{{}^{-}1}),1+O(t^{-1}),\dots,1+O(t^{-1}),\frac{1}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}}\bigg)~.

Putting this together, we see that

(7.2.16)

\begin{split}\underline{u}_{k}&=(0,0,\dots,e)\cdot L_{k-1}^{-1}D_{k-1}^{-1}~,\\
&=\bigg(eg_{k-1}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2}),eg_{k-2}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2}),\dots,eg_{1}t^{-1}+O(t^{-2}),\frac{e}{t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}})~,\end{split}

which fixes
\zeta_{\alpha}(y)
as desired.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we have the following result.

Proposition 7.5
.

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
is Hamiltonian with moment map
\Phi_{\alpha}
, which corresponds to projection to the co-ordinates
(b_{i},e)
.

Proof.

From
7.3
we see that the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
preserves the symplectic form and that the moment map is the projection to the
(b_{i},e)
co-ordinates. What remains is to show that the map
\Phi_{\alpha}
agrees with this projection but this is precisely the content of Lemma
7.4
.
∎

We see, therefore, that the image of
\Phi_{\alpha}
defines an open set

(7.2.17)

\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}}\equiv\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha})\subset\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}~.

We write
\chi_{\alpha}
for the character in
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}
corresponding to
b_{i}=0,e=1
.

For convenience we write,
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}
for the preimage of
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}
under
\Phi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
.

Define a map
\xi_{\alpha}:(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
on the usual co-ordinates by

(7.2.18)

\begin{split}b_{i}(\xi_{\alpha}(y))&=\Phi_{\alpha,i}(y)~,\text{ for }i<k\\
e(\xi_{\alpha}(y))&=\Phi_{\alpha,k}(y)~,\\
g_{i}(\xi_{\alpha}(y))&=\zeta_{\alpha,{k-i}}(y)/\Phi_{\alpha,k}(y)~,\text{ for }i<k~,\\
p(\xi_{\alpha}(y))&=\bigg(\zeta_{\alpha,k}(y)-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\Phi_{\alpha,i}(y)\zeta_{\alpha,i}(y)\bigg)/\Phi_{\alpha,k}(y)^{-1}~.\end{split}

Note that in
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}
,
\Phi_{\alpha,k}(y)
is nonzero, hence this map is well-defined.

Proposition 7.6
.

Write
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{-\alpha}^{0}
for the preimage of
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}
under the moment map
\Phi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
. Then the map

(7.2.19)

(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}})_{-\alpha}^{0}\xrightarrow{\xi_{\alpha}}\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{\alpha}

is the identity morphism.

Proof.

Proposition
7.5
implies that
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{-\alpha}^{0}=\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
and
\xi_{\alpha}(y)=y
follows from Lemma
7.4
and the definition of
\xi_{\alpha}
.
∎

Essentially, the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
is the natural left action coming from identifying it with an open subset of
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
as in Lemma
2.14
.

7.3.
Equivariance of the multiplication map

To prove our result on inverse Hamiltonian reduction we will need to examine how the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
interacts with the multiplication map.

First, let us glean what we can from the multiplication map
{\bf m}:\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\mu
be a coweight of
\rm PGL_{n}
,
y_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and
y_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
, then

(i)

y_{1}y_{2}\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}t^{\mu}T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]N_{-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
.

(ii)

There exist
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
and
n_{-}\in N_{-}[t]
such that
\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})=\pi(y_{1}y_{2})=ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}
.

(iii)

\Phi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2}))=\Phi_{\alpha}(y_{1})
and
\zeta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})=\zeta_{\alpha}(y_{1})
.

Proof.

Let
y_{1}=u_{1}t^{-\alpha}h_{1}u_{-,1}
and
y_{2}=u_{2}t^{\mu+\alpha}h_{2}u_{-,2}
be Gaussian decompositions, with
u_{i}\in N_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
,
h_{i}\in T_{1}[\![T^{-1}]\!]
, and
u_{-,i}\in N_{-,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Moreover, note that
u_{1}\in{\rm im}\,x_{\alpha}
.

So,

(7.3.1)

y_{1}y_{2}=u_{1}t^{-\alpha}h_{1}u_{-,1}u_{2}h_{2}t^{\mu+\alpha}u_{-,2}~.

Since
h_{1}u_{-,1}u_{2}h_{2}\in G_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
, it has a Gaussian decomposition
h_{1}u_{-,1}u_{2}h_{2}=u_{3}h_{3}u_{-,3}
where
u_{3}\in N_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
,
h_{3}\in T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
, and
u_{-,3}\in U_{-,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Now

(7.3.2)

\begin{split}y_{1}y_{2}&=u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}h_{3}u_{-,3}t^{\mu+\alpha}u_{-,2}~,\\
&=u_{1}\big(t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha}\big)h_{3}t^{\mu}\underbrace{\big(t^{-\mu-\alpha}u_{-,3}t^{\mu+\alpha}\big)u_{-,2}}_{\in N_{-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}~.\end{split}

So to establish
(i)
we need to show that
u_{1}(t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}
. To do so, let us focus in on the lower
(k+1)\times(k+1)
block. Write
u_{1}=x_{\alpha}(u_{1}^{\prime})
and
u_{3}=x_{\alpha}(u_{3}^{\prime})\cdot u_{3}^{\prime\prime}
, with
u_{3}^{\prime\prime}\in N^{\alpha}_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
for

(7.3.3)

\begin{split}u_{1}^{\prime}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&\dots&a_{1,k-1}&a_{1,k}\\
0&1&a_{2,2}&\dots&a_{2,k-1}&a_{2,k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&a_{k,k}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~,\\
u_{3}^{\prime}&=\begin{pmatrix}1&c_{1,1}&c_{1,2}&\dots&c_{1,k-1}&c_{1,k}\\
0&1&c_{2,2}&\dots&c_{2,k-1}&c_{2,k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&c_{k,k}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~,\end{split}

with
a_{i,j},c_{i,j}\in t^{-1}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Then,

(7.3.4)

t^{-\alpha}u_{3}^{\prime}t^{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}1&t^{-1}c_{1,1}&t^{-1}c_{1,2}&\dots&t^{-1}c_{1,k-1}&t^{-2}c_{1,k}\\
0&1&c_{2,2}&\dots&c_{2,k-1}&t^{-1}c_{2,k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&t^{-1}c_{k,k}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~,

i.e.
, the outer shell gets scaled by
t^{-1}
and the top right corner gets scaled by
t^{-2}
while all other entries are unchanged. So

(7.3.5)

u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha}=x_{\alpha}(u_{1}^{\prime})t^{-\alpha}x_{\alpha}(u_{3}^{\prime})t^{\alpha}\big(t^{-}\alpha u_{3}^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}\big)=x_{\alpha}(u^{\prime})\big(t^{-\alpha}u_{3}^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}\big)~,

where
u^{\prime}\in N_{k,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
is in the first congruent subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{k}
. Noting that
\big(t^{-\alpha}u_{3}^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}\big)\in N^{\alpha}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
we see that
u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha}\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}
, establishing
(i)
.

Now we move on to
(ii)
. We know that there are unique
n\in N[t]
and
n_{-}\in N-[t]
such that
ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
, so it suffices to show that
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
. But this has to be the case, since
u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha}\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}
already has the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
block in
{\rm im}\,x_{\alpha}
in the appropriate congruent subgroup. Thus, if
n
had any component in the image of
x_{\alpha}
, it would cause this block to not be in the correct form.

So all that remains is to establish
(iii)
. Note that
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
\zeta_{\alpha}
only see the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
block and so

(7.3.6)

\begin{split}\Phi_{\alpha}(nu_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})&=\Phi_{\alpha}(u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})~,\\
\zeta_{\alpha}(nu_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})&=\zeta_{\alpha}(u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})~.\\
\end{split}

Zooming into the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
block and using (
7.3.4
), one sees that

(7.3.7)

u^{\prime}_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}^{\prime}t^{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a_{1,1}+O(t^{-2})&a_{1,2}+O(t^{-2})&\dots&a_{1,k-1}+O(t^{-2})&a_{1,k}+O(t^{-3})\\
0&1&\ast&\dots&\ast&a_{2,k}+O(t^{-2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&a_{k,k}+O(t^{-2})\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~,

where
\ast
are undetermined terms. So, from the definitions of
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
\zeta_{\alpha}
, we see that

(7.3.8)

\begin{split}\Phi_{\alpha}(u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})&=\big(a_{k,k}^{(1)},a_{k-1,k}^{(1)},\dots,a_{1,k}^{(1)}\big)=\Phi_{\alpha}(y_{1})~,\\
\zeta_{\alpha}(u_{1}t^{-\alpha}u_{3}t^{\alpha})&=\big(a_{11}^{(1)},a_{1,2}^{(1)},\dots,a_{1,k-1}^{(1)},a_{1,k}^{(2)}\big)=\zeta_{\alpha}(y_{1})~,\end{split}

as desired.
∎

This lemma is enough to establish one of our main results

Theorem 7.8
.

The map
\mathbf{m}:\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
is
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
equivariant, where the action on the domain is the product of the natural action on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and the trivial action on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
\underline{v}\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
,
y_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
, and
y_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
. On the left hand side,

(7.3.9)

\underline{v}\cdot(y_{1},y_{2})=(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})y_{1},y_{2})~,

by Lemma
7.3
. The action on the right hand side is given by

(7.3.10)

\underline{v}\cdot\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})=\pi(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v}){\bf m}(y_{1},y_{2}))~.

By Lemma
7.7

(ii)
,
\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})=ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}
for some
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
and
n_{-}\in N_{-}[t]
. Therefore,

(7.3.11)

\begin{split}x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v}){\bf m}(y_{1},y_{2})&=x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}~,\\
&=\big(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})nx_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})^{-1}\big)x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})y_{1}y_{2}n_{-}~.\end{split}

But, from Proposition
7.1

(iii)
,
x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})nx_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})^{-1}=n^{\prime}\in N^{\alpha}[t]
. Putting things together,

(7.3.12)

\underline{v}\cdot{\bf m}(y_{1},y_{2})=\pi(n^{\prime}x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})y_{1}y_{2}n_{-})=\pi(x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})y_{1}y_{2})=x_{-\alpha}(\underline{v})y_{1}y_{2}

which agrees with the action on the left hand side in (
7.3.10
).
∎

7.4.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction for the translation group

From Lemma
7.7
\,(iii)
, and Proposition
7.5
, we see that the composition
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\xrightarrow{\bf m}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}\xrightarrow{\Phi_{\alpha}}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
factors through the inclusion
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}
. So, the image of the multiplication map lies inside the preimage of
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}}
,
i.e.
,

(7.4.1)

{\bf m}:\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\hookrightarrow(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}~.

We wish to show that this inclusion is actually an isomorphism and so we proceed to define an inverse to
{\bf m}
. First, we prove a small but useful result.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
y_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and
y_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
, then

(7.4.2)

\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2}))=y_{1}~,

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
7.7

(iii)
and Proposition
7.6
, since
\xi_{\alpha}
is fully determined by the values of
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
\zeta_{\alpha}
.
∎

So we see that
\xi_{\alpha}
gives a putative section of
{\bf m}:\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\hookrightarrow\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}_{k}^{a}})_{\mu}
to the first factor
\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
. We want to extend this to a full section.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
y\in(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}})_{\mu}
, then

(7.4.3)

\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}(y)\in N(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}t^{\mu+\alpha}T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]N_{-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)~.

In particular,
\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
and there exist
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
and
n_{-}\in N_{-}[t]
such that

(7.4.4)

\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y)=n\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1})yn_{-}~.

Proof.

Write
\xi_{\alpha}(y)=\tau_{\alpha}(y^{\prime})
, where

(7.4.5)

y^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\dots&0&e\\
0&1&0&\dots&0&b_{k-1}\\
0&0&1&\dots&0&b_{k-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&b_{1}\\
-1/e&-g_{k-1}&-g_{k-2}&\dots&-g_{1}&t-p-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}\end{pmatrix}~.

in the usual co-ordinates on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
. Since
y\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
, it has a Gaussian decomposition of the form,

(7.4.6)

y=x_{\alpha}(u)u^{\prime}ht^{\mu}u_{-}~,

where
u\in N_{1,k}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
,
u^{\prime}\in N^{\alpha}_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
,
h\in T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
, and
u_{-}\in N_{-}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Then,

(7.4.7)

\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y=\tau_{\alpha}(y^{\prime})^{-1}x_{\alpha}(u)u^{\prime}t^{\mu}u_{-}~,

Our strategy is the following. We claim that
(y^{\prime})^{-1}u
is in the
{\rm GL}_{k+1,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
orbit of
t^{\alpha}
,
i.e.
,

(7.4.8)

(y^{\prime})^{-1}u=y^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}~,

for some
y^{\prime\prime}
in the first congruent subgroup
{\rm GL}_{k+1,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Therefore,
y^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}
has a Gaussian decomposition

(7.4.9)

y^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}=u^{\prime\prime}t^{\alpha}l_{-}~,

where
u^{\prime\prime}\in N_{k+1,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
and
l_{-}\in B_{k+1,-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
. Thus,

(7.4.10)

\begin{split}\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y&=x_{\alpha}(u^{\prime\prime})\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})u^{\prime}ht^{\mu}u_{-}~,\\
&=x_{\alpha}(u^{\prime\prime})t^{\alpha}\big(\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})u^{\prime}\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})^{-1}\big)\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})t^{\mu}hu_{-}~,\end{split}

Now Proposition
7.1
implies that
\big(\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})u^{\prime}\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})^{-1}\big)\in N^{\alpha}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
and so
x_{\alpha}(u^{\prime\prime})\big(\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})u^{\prime}\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})^{-1}\big)\in N^{\alpha}(\!(t^{-1})\!)^{\alpha}_{1}
by definition. The term
\tau_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}l_{-})t^{\mu}hu_{-}
has a Gaussian decomposition of the form
t^{\mu+\alpha}T_{1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]N_{-}(\!(t^{-1})\!)
as desired.

Thus, it is sufficient to show that the matrix
y^{\prime\prime}
is in the first congruent subgroup,
{\rm GL}_{k+1,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. To do so we have to massage the expression
(y^{\prime})^{-1}u
and, crucially, use the fact that the first few Fourier modes appearing in the entries of
u
actually determine the entries of
y^{\prime}
, by way of the map
\xi_{\alpha}
.

One computes the inverse of
y^{\prime}
to be

(7.4.11)

(y^{\prime})^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}t-p&-eg_{k-1}&-g_{k-2}&\dots&-eg_{1}&-e\\
-b_{k-1}/e&1&0&\dots&0&0\\
-b_{k-2}/e&0&1&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-b_{1}/e&0&0&\dots&1&0\\
\frac{1}{e}&0&0&\dots&0&0\end{pmatrix}~.

Write

(7.4.12)

u=\begin{pmatrix}1&u_{1,1}&u_{1,2}&\dots&u_{1,k-1}&u_{1,k}\\
0&1&u_{2,2}&\dots&u_{2,k-1}&u_{2,k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&u_{k,k}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}~,

where
u_{i,j}\in t^{-1}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Performing the matrix multiplication we have, schematically,

(7.4.13)

(y^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot u=\begin{pmatrix}t-p&\underline{q}&s\\
\underline{b}^{T}&Q&\underline{c}^{T}\\
1/e&\underline{g}&u_{1,k}/e\end{pmatrix}~,

where
\underline{b}=(-b_{k-1}/e,-b_{k-2}/e,\dots,-b_{1}/e)
,
\underline{g}=(u_{1,1}/e,u_{1,2}/e,\dots,u_{1,k-1}/e)
. Moreover
\underline{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{k-1})
and
\underline{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k-1})
, with

(7.4.14)

\begin{split}q_{i}&=(t-p)u_{1,i}-\sum_{j=2}^{i}eg_{k-j+1}u_{j,i}-eg_{k-i}~,\\
c_{i}&=u_{i+1,k}-b_{k-i}u_{1,k}/e~,\end{split}

and

(7.4.15)

s=(t-p)u_{1,k}-\sum_{j=2}^{k}eg_{k-j+1}u_{j,k}-e~.

How about the
(k-1)\times(k-1)
matrix
Q
? Its entries are given by

(7.4.16)

Q_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\big(1-b_{k-i}u_{1,j}/e\big)+(\delta_{ij}-1)b_{k-i}/eu_{1,j}+\Theta_{ij}u_{i+1,j}~,

where
\Theta_{ij}
is the discrete Heaviside function,
i.e.
,
\Theta_{ij}=1
if
j>i
and
\Theta_{ij}=0
otherwise. Really, all that matters is the observation that the off diagonal terms of
Q
are homogeneous in the
u
variables and so
Q_{ij}\sim O(t^{-1})
and the diagonal terms satisfy,
Q_{ii}\sim 1+O(t^{-1})
.

Now we set
(y^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot u=y^{\prime\prime}\cdot t^{\alpha}
, so

(7.4.17)

y^{\prime\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1-p/t&\underline{q}&ts\\
t^{-1}\underline{b}^{T}&Q&t\underline{c}^{T}\\
t^{-1}/e&\underline{g}&tu_{1,k}/e\end{pmatrix}~,

and recall that we wish to show that
y^{\prime\prime}
is in the first congruent subgroup of
{\rm GL}_{k+1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
. Note that

(7.4.18)

tu_{1,k}/e=t\big(et^{-1}+O(t^{-2})\big)/e=1+O(t^{-1})~,

since
e=u_{1,k}^{(1)}
from the definition of
\xi_{\alpha}
. We have also shown that
Q
is in the appropriate form and, from their definitions,
\underline{g}
,
t^{-1}\underline{b}
and
t^{-1}/e
are all
O(t^{-1})
. So, now we must show that
\underline{q},ts,t\underline{c}
are all
O(t^{-1})
.

Let us start with
t\underline{c}
,

(7.4.19)

tc_{i}=t(u_{i+1,k}-b_{k-i}u_{1,k}/e)=t\big(b_{k-i}t^{-1}-b_{k-i}(et^{-1})/e+O(t^{-2})\big)=O(t^{-1})~,

where we used the fact that
u_{i+1,k}^{(1)}=b_{k-i}
, and
u_{1,k}^{(1)}=e
, again coming from the definition of
\xi_{\alpha}
. Now we move on to
\underline{q}
,

(7.4.20)

q_{i}=(t-p)u_{1,i}-\sum_{j=2}^{i}eg_{k-j+1}u_{j,i}-eg_{k-i}=u_{1,i}^{(1)}-g_{k-i}+O(t^{-1})=O(t^{-1})

where in the final equality we use the fact that
u_{1,i}^{(1)}=+g_{k-i}
by the definition of
\xi_{\alpha}
. Now, all that remains is to check
ts
or, equivalently, to show that
s\sim O(t^{-2})
.

(7.4.21)

\begin{split}s&=(t-p)u_{1,k}-\sum_{j=2}^{k}eg_{k-j+1},u_{j,k}-e~,\\
&=(t-p)\big(u_{1,k}^{(1)}t^{-1}+u_{1,k}^{(2)}t^{-2}+O(t^{-3})\big)-e\sum_{j=2}^{k}g_{k-j+1}u_{j,k}^{(1)}t^{-1}-e+O(t^{-2})~,\\
&=(u_{1,k}^{(1)}-e)+\big(u_{1,k}^{(2)}-pu_{1,k}^{(1)}+e\sum_{j=2}^{k}g_{k-j+1}u_{j,k}^{(1)}\big)t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})~.\end{split}

Unwinding the definition of
\xi_{\alpha}
, we see that

(7.4.22)

u_{1,k}^{(1)}=e~,\qquad\sum_{j=2}^{k}g_{k-j+1}u_{j,k}^{(1)}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{j}g_{j}~,\qquad u_{1,k}^{(2)}=pe+e\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}~,

Substituting this in, we see that

(7.4.23)

s=(e-e)+\big(pe+e\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}-pe-e\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{i}g_{i}\big)t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})=O(t^{-2})~,

as desired.

Thus,
y^{\prime\prime}
is in the first congruent subgroup,
{\rm GL}_{k+1,1}[\![t^{-1}]\!]
and we are done.
∎

With this lemma in hand, we are able to define a putative inverse to
{\bf m}:\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
. Define

(7.4.24)

\begin{split}F_{\alpha}:(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}&\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\\
y&\mapsto\big(\xi_{\alpha}(y),\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y)\big)\end{split}

From Lemma
7.10
we see that
F_{\alpha}
is indeed well-defined.

Proposition 7.11
.

Let
\mu
be a coweight of
\rm PGL_{n}
, then
\mathbf{m}
and
F_{\alpha}
define an isomorphism

(7.4.25)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha})_{\mu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\alpha}}

Proof.

We proceed by directly showing that
\mathbf{m}\circ F_{\alpha}={\rm id}_{\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mu}}
and
F_{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{m}={\rm id}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}}
.

First, we check
\mathbf{m}\circ F_{\alpha}={\rm id}_{\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mu}}
. Let
y\in\overset{\circ}{\mathrm{Gr}}_{\mu}
, then

(7.4.26)

F_{\alpha}(y)=\big(\xi_{\alpha}(y),\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y)\big)~,

From Lemma
7.7
, we know that there exist
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
and
n_{-}\in N_{-}[t]
such that
\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y)=n\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}yn_{-}
. So,

(7.4.27)

\mathbf{m}(F_{\alpha}(y)=\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y),\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}y))=\pi(\xi_{\alpha}(y)n\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}yn_{-})~.

But, from Proposition
7.1

(iii)
we see that
\xi_{\alpha}(y)n\xi_{\alpha}(y)^{-1}=n^{\prime}\in N^{\alpha}[t]
. Thus,

(7.4.28)

{\bf m}(F_{\alpha}(y))=\pi(n^{\prime}yn_{-})=y~,

as desired.

Now, we check
F_{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{m}={\rm id}_{\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}}
. Let
y_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and
y_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}
. From Lemma
7.7
, we know that there exist some
n\in N^{\alpha}[t]
and
n_{-}\in N_{-}[t]
(not to be confused with the ones from the previous paragraph) such that

(7.4.29)

\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})=ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}~.

By Lemma
7.9
, we know that
\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2}))=y_{1}
, and so

(7.4.30)

F_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2}))=(y_{1},\pi(y_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2})))=(y_{1},\pi(y_{1}^{-1}ny_{1}y_{2}n_{-}))~.

Note that
y_{1}\in{\rm im}\,\tau_{\alpha}
; so by Proposition
7.1

(iii)
, we once again have that
y_{1}^{-1}ny_{1}=n^{\prime}\in N^{\alpha}[t]
. Therefore,

(7.4.31)

F_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m}(y_{1},y_{2}))=(y_{1},\pi(n^{\prime}y_{2}n_{-}))=(y_{1},y_{2})~.

∎

Combining Theorem
7.8
and Proposition
7.11
, we have an immediate corollary:

Corollary 7.12
.

The action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
is Hamiltonian with moment map
\Phi_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
\mu
be a coweight of
{{\textup{PGL}}}_{n}
, then we have an isomorphism of Poisson varieties

(7.4.32)

\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}

The character
\chi_{\alpha}
is contained in
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}

7.11
, we can identify its preimage under the moment map with

7.8
this identification is
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant. Thus,

(7.4.33)

\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}\cong(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha})/\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu+\alpha}~.

∎

Theorem 7.14
.

Let
\mu
be a coweight of
{{\textup{PGL}}}_{n}
and

a positive coroot, then the tuple

.

Looking at Definition

, we see that conditions
(i)
and

are immediately satisfied, and Proposition
7.11
tells us that
\mathbf{m}

.

Intersecting with

8.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction in type
A

In this section we apply the results of the previous section on inverse Hamiltonian reduction for generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian, via the identifications of the latter with Slodowy slices and their equivariant analogues recalled in Section
3
, to produce inverse Hamiltonian reductions of finite W-algebras and their equivariant analogues, and thereby prove Theorems
1.2
,
1.1
, and
1.4
from the introduction.

Throughout this section, we fix
\mu
to be a partition of
N
of length
n-1
, thought of as a dominant coweight of
\rm PGL_{n}
as in Section
3
, and let
\alpha
be a positive coroot of
\rm PGL_{n}
such that
\mu+\alpha
is again a dominant coweight, in keeping with the hypotheses of the preceding Section
7
. Similarly, we return to the notational conventions of Section
3
, for example letting
G={{\textup{GL}}}_{N}
throughout.

8.1.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction for finite W-algebras

Recall that the equivariant Slodowy slices
S_{G,\mu}
were defined in Section
3.3
as Hamiltonian reductions of
T^{*}{{\textup{GL}}}_{N}
with respect to the left moment map, so that the right moment map
\mu_{r}^{\#}
descends to each
S_{G,\mu}
. Similarly, the equivariant slices retain a residual group action on the left after Hamiltonian reduction with respect to the left moment map restricted to
N_{\chi_{\mu}}
, and the group
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
which acts on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mu}
is identified with some subgroup of this residual action, so that in particular it commutes with the right
G
action. To see this, note that the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is always contained within the upper
n\times n
block of a matrix realization of
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha_{\mu}}
, and so the leaves the lower
n\times n
block invariant.

The above discussion leads us to the following key result:

Lemma 8.1
.

The maps induced by multiplication of generalized slices in the affine Grassmannian,

\mathbf{m}:T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}^{\textup{loc}}\times S_{G,\mu+\alpha}\hookrightarrow S_{G,\mu}~,

are strongly
G
-equivariant with respect to the right
G
-actions on
S_{G,\mu+\alpha}
and
S_{G,\mu}
, respectively, where we let
T^{*}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}^{\textup{loc}}=\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{-\alpha}^{0}
.

Proof.

Note that the moment maps for the right
G
-action are (coefficients of) matrix elements in the lower
(n-1)\times(n-1)
block of a matrix realization
x\in\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha_{\mu}}\cong S_{G,\mu}
. The action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and the multiplication map only sees the top
(\ell(\alpha)+1)\times(\ell(\alpha)+1)
block of
x
, which does not touch the lower
(n-1)\times(n-1)
block. So the moment map for the right
G
-action is unchanged under multiplication and the multiplication map is strongly
G
-equivariant.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

The multiplication maps can be made to be
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-equivariant where
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
acts on the equivariant Slodowy slices by some Kazhdan action,
i.e.
, when restricted to functions on
S_{\mu}
, the grading is positive. The induced grading on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
is not the usual
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-action that scales the cotangent fibre with unit weight. This follows from Lemma
6.12
. We can untwist by modifying the action of the maximal torus.

Applying the results of the previous section, we obtain the following corollary, which is essentially the statement of Hamiltonian reduction by stages for equivariant Slodowy slices
S_{G,\mu}
and in turn the usual Slodowy slices
S_{\mu}
in type
A
; the latter was established previously in general type in
[
GJ24
]
.

Corollary 8.3
.

The equivariant Slodowy slice,
S_{G,\mu}
, has a Hamiltonian action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and

(8.1.1)

S_{G,\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}=S_{G,\mu+\alpha}~,

as symplectic varieties. Moreover,
S_{\mu}
has a Hamiltonian action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
such that

(8.1.2)

S_{\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}=S_{\mu+\alpha}~,

as Poisson varieties.

Proof.

The first result is an immediate application of Corollary
7.15
and thus Theorem
7.13
and the second follows by passing to the quotient by the right
G
-action, which—by Lemma
8.1
— the moment map is equivariant with respect to, and commutes with the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

The tuple,
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha},\mathbf{m})
is an inverse Hamiltonian reduction for
(S_{G,\mu},\mathbb{G}_{\alpha},\Phi_{\alpha},\chi_{\alpha})
.

This in turn implies that we have an isomorphism of Poisson algebras

(8.1.3)

{\mathcal{O}}(S_{G,\mu})[E_{\alpha}^{-1}]\cong{\mathcal{O}}(S_{G,\mu+\alpha})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha})

where
E_{\alpha}^{-1}
denotes the localization at the image of
\bf m
. By taking
G
-invariants, we obtain

(8.1.4)

{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\mu})[E_{\alpha}^{-1}]\cong{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\mu+\alpha})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha})~,

the semiclassical limit of our desired embedding of W-algebras.

Next, let us upgrade Corollary
8.3
to the quantum setting.

Lemma 8.5
.

Up to isomorphism, there exists a unique strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
equivariant, graded quantization of the equivariant Slodowy slice
S_{G,\mu}
.

Proof.

The space of such quantizations are parameterized by the second
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times G
-equivariant cohomology of
S_{G,\mu}
, by Proposition
4.3
. Further,
H_{\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\times G}^{2}(S_{G,\mu})=0
, noting
G
acts freely with contractible quotient
S_{\mu}
, and
IG_{\alpha}
is unipotent. Thus, there is a unique strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant graded quantization, up to isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 8.6
.

The sheaf of algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}
is strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemmas
8.5
and
4.5
, as
{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}
is the unique graded quantization of
S_{G,\mu}
, up to isomorphism.
∎

Combining both results, we have a novel proof of reduction by stages for
\hbar
-adic finite W-algebras in type
A
. This result was initially conjectured (in general type) by Morgan in
[
Mor15
]
and proven (likewise in general type) by Genra and Julliard in
[
GJ24
]
, via homological methods.

Theorem 8.7
.

Let
\mu
be a partition of
N
, thought of as a dominant coweight of
\rm PGL_{n}
, and
\alpha
a positive root in such that
\mu+\alpha
is dominant. Then, we have isomorphisms of graded quantizations.

(8.1.5)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}

(8.1.6)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
, since
S_{G,\mu}
admits an IHR, the Hamiltonian reduction of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}
is a quantization of
S_{G,\mu+\alpha}
. However, from Lemma
4.5
, there is a unique quantization of
S_{G,\mu+\alpha}
, up to isomorphism, and thus
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}
. Taking invariants with respect to the right
G
-action, we have the desired result for the ordinary (non-equivariant) finite W-algebras.
∎

We already have IHR data for the equivariant Slodowy slices, all that remains is to find an isomorphism between
\mathbf{m}^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu})
and a split quantization of
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}
and the reduced slice.

Instead of explicitly constructing such a splitting, we will appeal to the rigidity of quantizations of
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times S_{G,\mu+\alpha}
.

Proposition 8.8
.

There exists a strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant isomorphism of quantizations

\psi_{\mu,\alpha}:\mathbf{m}^{*}({\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathcal{D}}_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}\boxtimes{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu+\alpha}\ .

Proof.

Note
H^{2}_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}}(\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times S_{G,\mu+\alpha})=0
, as
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
acts freely on
\mathrm{Gr}^{0}_{-\alpha}\times S_{G,\mu+\alpha}
with quotient
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}\times S_{\mu}
, the latter of which is homotopy equivalent to
S^{1}
, so that there is a unique quantization up to strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant isomorphism.

∎

Theorem 8.9
.

The reduction
{\mathcal{A}}_{G,\mu}/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\alpha}}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
admits a quantum inverse Hamiltonian reduction given by
(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha},{\bf m},{\mathcal{D}}^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha},\psi_{\mu,\alpha})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
8.4
we see that
((\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha},{\bf m})
is inverse Hamiltonian reduction data for the equivariant Slodowy slices and by Proposition
8.8
,
({\mathcal{D}}^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha},\psi_{\mu,\alpha})
completes this to a quantum inverse Hamiltonian reduction.
∎

Corollary 8.10
.

There is a strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant embedding of
\hbar
-adic associative algebras

(8.1.7)

\psi_{\mu,\alpha}:{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\hookrightarrow D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}\otimes W_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}\ .

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
8.9
by taking global sections. The embedding of ordinary W-algebras follows from taking
G
-invariants.
∎

Corollary 8.11
.

There is a strongly
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant embedding of
\hbar
-adic associative algebras

(8.1.8)

\psi_{\mu,\alpha}:{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}\hookrightarrow D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}\otimes W_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}\ .

Proof.

This follows from the preceding corollary by taking invariants with respect to the right
G

∎

To recover the usual non
\hbar
-adic W-algebras, we use a trick of Losev’s from
[
Los07
]
.

Definition 8.12
.

Given a graded quantization
A
that is weakly equivariant for some reductive group
H
, we write

(8.1.9)

A_{H\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}\coloneqq\{a\in A\,|\,a\text{ is in a finite dimensional }H\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}\text{ representation }\}~,

Recalling Section 3.1 of
[
Los07
]
, we note that we can recover the filtered equivariant W-algebra as

(8.1.10)

{\mathcal{W}}_{G,\mu}=({\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}/(\hbar-1)({\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}

and, moreover, we can recover the usual finite W-algebras by taking
G
-invariants:

(8.1.11)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\mu}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{G,\mu}^{G}~.

Note that the modified
\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-grading on
D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}
is not bounded below and the
G
-action on it is trivial, thus
(D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}/(\hbar-1)(D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}
will contain infinite linear combinations. More specifically, we write
D^{\rm loc}_{\alpha}
for the associative
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra generated by
(p,e^{\pm},b_{i},g_{i})
, subject to the relations

(8.1.12)

\begin{split}e^{+}e^{-}&=e^{-}e^{+}=1\\
[p,e^{\pm}]=\pm e^{\pm}~&,\qquad[b_{i},g_{i}]=1~.\end{split}

Then,
(D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}/(\hbar-1)(D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha})_{G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}-{\rm fin}}
is isomorphic to some completion of
D_{\alpha}^{\rm loc}
, call it
\hat{D}_{\alpha}^{\rm loc}
.

Theorem 8.13
.

We have a strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
equivariant embedding of associative algebras over
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}
,

(8.1.13)

{\mathcal{W}}_{G,\mu}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{G,\mu+\alpha}\otimes\hat{D}^{\rm loc}_{\alpha}~.

Proof.

Since the embedding of Corollary
8.10
is strongly
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
-equivariant and graded, we may restrict to the
G\times\mathbb{G}_{\hbar}
-finite subalgebras on both sides. We obtain the desired result by setting
\hbar=1
.
∎

8.2.
Inverse Hamiltonian reduction for Harish Chandra bimodules

Let
\rm HC_{\hbar}
denote the category of
\hbar
-adic Harish Chandra bimodules for
G={{\textup{GL}}}_{N}
. An object
M\in{\rm HC}_{\hbar}
is a graded
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
-module, equipped with an algebraic action
\rho
of
G
, such that the action map
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes M\rightarrow M
is
G
-equivariant. The category
\rm HC_{\hbar}
is symmetric monoidal with the tensor product over
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}
and the diagonal coproduct on
G
and
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
.

We have a monoidal functor
\rm HC_{\hbar}\rightarrow HC_{0}={\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})-{\rm Mod}\big({\rm Rep}\,G\big)\cong{\rm QCoh}_{G}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
by setting
\hbar=0
, called the classical limit.

Note that the equivariant W-algebras
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}
are objects in
\rm HC_{\hbar}
for any partition
\mu
, with the right
G
-action and the action of
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
coming from the right comoment map.

Remark 8.14
.

The
\hbar
-adic differential operators,
D_{\hbar}(G)
, has two commuting actions of
G
and two commuting comoment maps
\Phi^{\#}_{L,R}:U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow D_{\hbar}(G)
and thus is an object in
\rm HC_{\hbar}
in two
a priori
non-equivalent ways. However both actions are equivalent and so both
{\rm HC_{\hbar}}
structures on
D_{\hbar}(G)
are isomorphic. Thus for any
M\in\rm HC_{\hbar}
,
M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G)
has two equivalent
\rm HC_{\hbar}
structures, where
U(\mathfrak{g})
and
G
act diagonally on
M
and
D_{\alpha}(G)
via either the left or right comoment maps on
D_{\alpha}(G)
.

Let
\chi\in\mathbb{O}_{\mu}
be some representative in the nilpotent orbit defined by
\mu
. Recall the grading on
\mathfrak{g}
from Section
3.1
and write
\mathfrak{g}_{\chi,<0}
for the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
that is strictly negatively graded.

If
M\in\rm HC_{\hbar}
, then we have a natural action of
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{\chi_{\mu},<0})
coming from restriction. We write
M_{\chi_{\mu}}
for the maximal
U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{\chi,<0})
submodule generated by
\{(x-\chi(x)\hbar)m\in M\,|\,x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\chi_{\mu},<0},m\in M\}
. We define the reduction of
M
at
\chi
as

(8.2.1)

M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi}\coloneqq\big(M\big/M_{\chi_{\mu}}\big)^{G}\cong(M\underset{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{\chi_{\mu},<0})}{\otimes}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}_{\chi})^{N_{\chi_{\mu}}}~,

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\chi,<0}
acts on
\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}_{\chi}
via
\chi_{\mu}
. The reduction
M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}
is naturally a module for the W-algebra
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}
.
Thus for every partition
\mu
, we have a functor.

(8.2.2)

\begin{split}{\rm KW}_{\mu}:{\rm HC}_{\hbar}&\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}-{\rm Mod}\\
M&\mapsto M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\end{split}

In the classical limit, we note that

(8.2.3)

M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}|_{\hbar=0}\cong M|_{\hbar=0}\underset{{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\mu})

The associated restriction functor induced by the embedding
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}\otimes D_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}
of Corollary
8.10
is the following

Corollary 8.15
.

There exists a natural functor

(8.2.4)

\begin{split}\rm{IHR_{\alpha}}:{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}-{\rm Mod}&\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}-{\rm Mod}\\
M&\mapsto{\rm Res}^{{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu+\alpha}\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}}(M\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha})\end{split}

If
M\in{\rm HC}_{\hbar}
, then we may also compute its reduction with respect to the full
\mathfrak{g}
action:

(8.2.5)

M/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\coloneqq\big(M\big/U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})M\big)^{G}\cong\bigg(M\underset{U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})}{\otimes}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}{{[\![\hbar]\!]}}\bigg)^{G}~,

In the classical limit
M/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G|_{\hbar=0}\cong(M|_{\hbar=0}\underset{{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{\otimes}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}})^{G}
.

Proposition 8.16
.

For any
M\in{\rm HC}
,

(8.2.6)

\big(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\cong M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}~.

as
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}
-modules, where on the left
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}
acts solely on
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}
.

Proof.

This Proposition is essentially an associative algebra version of Theorem 6.5 in
[
Ara18
]
and in some sense follows from it. Therefore, we paraphrase the proof of
loc. cit.
, suppressing some homological details for brevity.

By taking the classical limit, we have the following identifications

(8.2.7)

\big(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G|_{\hbar=0}\cong\big((M|_{\hbar=0}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(S_{G,\mu}))\underset{{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{\otimes}\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}\big)^{G}\cong(M|_{\hbar=0}\underset{{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})}{\otimes}{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\mu}))\cong(M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}})|_{\hbar=0}~,

where we have used the fact that
{\mathcal{O}}(S_{G,\mu}
is free as a
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
module.

The equivariant W-algebras are defined by reducing
D_{\hbar}(G)
and so
M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\cong(M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G))/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}
as objects in
\rm HC_{\hbar}
, where the tensor product is using the right action on
D_{\hbar}(G)
and the reduction is performed with respect to the left action.

Therefore,
(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu})/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\cong\big((M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G))/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G
, where we remark again that the reduction along the diagonal
G
action uses the right comoment map of
D_{\hbar}(G)
. By a standard spectral sequence argument we note that

(8.2.8)

\big((M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G))/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\cong\big(M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G)\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{(\chi_{\mu},0)}(N_{\chi_{\mu}}\times G)~.

In other words, the two step reduction is isomorphic to reducing at once. But, by Remark
8.14
we can swap to the other
\rm HC_{\hbar}
structure on
M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G)
,
i.e.
, the diagonal action uses the left comoment map on
D_{\hbar}(G)
.

Consider the morphism,
M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\rightarrow\big(M\otimes D_{\hbar(G)}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{(\chi_{\mu},0)}(N_{\chi_{\mu}}\times G)
, that sends a class
[m]\in(M/M_{\chi_{\mu}})^{N_{\chi}}
to
[m\otimes 1]\in((M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G))/U_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})(M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G))_{\chi_{\mu}})^{N_{\chi_{\mu}}\times G}
.

In the classical limit, this induces a morphism

(M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}})|_{\hbar=0}\rightarrow\big(M\otimes D_{\hbar}(G)\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{(\chi_{\mu},0)}(N_{\chi_{\mu}}\times G)|_{\hbar=0}
. However, by (
8.2.7
), this is an isomorphism and so the morphism
M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\rightarrow\big(M\otimes D_{\hbar(G)}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{(\chi_{\mu},0)}(N_{\chi_{\mu}}\times G)
must be too. Thus, we may conclude that

M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\cong(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu})/\!\!/\!\!/_{G}~.

∎

Theorem 8.17
.

We have natural embeddings of
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,\mu}
modules

(8.2.9)

M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}\hookrightarrow{\rm IHR}_{\alpha}(M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}}N_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}})=M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}}N_{\chi_{\mu+\alpha}}\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}

Proof.

By Proposition
8.16
,
M/\!\!/\!\!/_{\chi_{\mu}}N_{\chi_{\mu}}
is isomorphic to
\big(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G
. From Corollary
8.10
, we have a strongly
G
-equivariant embedding
{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}\otimes D^{\rm loc}_{\hbar,\alpha}
which induces a map between the reductions

(8.2.10)

\big(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\hookrightarrow\big(M\otimes{\mathcal{W}}_{\hbar,G,\mu+\alpha}\big)/\!\!/\!\!/_{0}G\otimes D_{\hbar,\alpha}^{\rm loc}~,

which by Proposition
8.16
gives the desired result.
∎

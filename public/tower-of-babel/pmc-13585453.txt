Bleeding-related conditions, procedural safety, and mortality after left atrial appendage closure
Authors: Grégoire Massoullié, Come Fleury, Emeline Tourret, Simon Berger, Catherine Basthier-Denis, Guillaume Clerfond, Pierre-Antoine Catalan, Aurélie Chalard, Clément Riocreux, Aurélien Lebreton, Bruno Pereira, Romain Eschalier
Published: 2026

Left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) is increasingly used for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation patients unsuitable for long-term oral anticoagulation.1,2 In daily practice, referral is often driven by bleeding-prone conditions—thrombocytopenia, advanced renal dysfunction, severe anaemia, or advanced age—that may be intuitively perceived as procedural risk. We hypothesized that these conditions mainly reflect long-term clinical-biological vulnerability rather than acute procedural hazard.

We retrospectively analysed 747 consecutive patients undergoing LAAC between 2016 and 2023 in a tertiary centre. All indications were validated by a multidisciplinary Heart Team, with geriatric and specialist assessment when appropriate. This cohort therefore reflects outcomes after structured multidisciplinary selection rather than an unfiltered population of high-risk patients.

Procedures were performed under general anaesthesia or conscious sedation with transoesophageal or micro-transoesophageal echocardiography guidance, as previously described.3 Clinical follow-up was scheduled at 3 months. Device surveillance imaging was performed according to local practice using cardiac computed tomography (CT) or transoesophageal echocardiography when clinically appropriate. Device-related thrombus was defined as thrombus on the atrial surface of the device detected by CT or transoesophageal echocardiography.

The primary procedural outcome was any peri-procedural complication, including access-site complications. Major peri-procedural adverse events were analysed separately and included peri-procedural death, tamponade, stroke or transient ischaemic attack, intracranial haemorrhage, device embolization, anaphylactic shock, or clinically significant airway bleeding. Access-site complications were reported separately because of their heterogeneous clinical severity.

The primary long-term outcome was all-cause mortality. A parsimonious exploratory score was derived from independent baseline mortality predictors, assigning one point for age ≥ 85 years, male sex, estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, haemoglobin < 10 g/dL, fibrinogen ≥ 4 g/L, and platelet count < 150 × 109/L. The score was grouped as low (0–1), intermediate (2–3), and high risk (≥4).

Successful implantation was achieved in 736/747 patients (98.5%; three aborted before device deployment). Any peri-procedural complication occurred in 38 patients (5.1%), including access-site complications in 16 (2.1%). Major peri-procedural adverse events occurred in 13 patients (1.7%). Individual major events included cardiac tamponade in six patients (0.8%), device embolization in four (0.5%), stroke or transient ischaemic attack in one (0.1%), intracranial haemorrhage in two (0.3%), and in-hospital death in three (0.4%), including one procedure-related death. Among attempted implantations, devices were Amulet in 338 (45.4%), Watchman in 116 (15.6%), and Watchman FLX in 290 (39.0%). Device type was not associated with all-cause mortality in unadjusted analysis (log-rank P = 0.925) or after adjustment for score components.

Bleeding-prone or clinically vulnerable conditions were not associated with excess overall peri-procedural complications: 4.8% vs. 5.2% in patients aged ≥85 years vs. younger patients (P = 1.000), 8.6% vs. 5.0% in patients with platelet count < 150 × 109/L vs. ≥150 × 109/L (P = 0.187), 4.6% vs. 5.0% in patients with estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 vs. ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m2 (P = 1.000), and 5.6% vs. 5.3% in patients with haemoglobin < 10 vs. ≥10 g/dL (P = 0.804). No tamponade occurred in patients with thrombocytopenia or advanced renal dysfunction.

Post-procedural therapy was predominantly single antiplatelet therapy (94.0%), reflecting contraindication to anticoagulation and multidisciplinary assessment of bleeding risk. Dual antiplatelet therapy or therapeutic-dose anticoagulation was reserved for selected patients after Heart Team assessment. Three-month CT was available in 400 patients (53.5%). Device-related thrombus was identified in eight CT-evaluated patients (2.0%) and did not significantly differ across device types. At 3 years, major bleeding occurred in 9 patients (1.3%), ischaemic stroke in 10 (1.4%), and all-cause death in 227 (31.7%).

Long-term mortality was associated with baseline clinical-biological markers rather than procedure-related events. As summarized in Figure 1, the exploratory LAAC score stratified 3-year survival: 85.8%, 68.5%, and 52.6% in low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups, respectively. Compared with low-risk patients, mortality was higher in intermediate-risk (hazard ratio 2.58, 95% confidence interval 1.59–4.20; P < 0.001) and high-risk patients (hazard ratio 4.39, 95% confidence interval 2.67–7.21; P < 0.001).

These results are consistent with previous LAAC cohorts showing that mortality after implantation is largely driven by baseline comorbidity rather than device-related complications.4,5 Bleeding indication categories provided limited incremental prognostic information beyond biological markers, suggesting that transversal markers of vulnerability may capture prognosis more effectively than disease-specific labels. This matters because bleeding causes often drive LAAC referral, whereas biological markers may better reflect residual life expectancy and capacity to benefit. This study addresses a practical distinction in contemporary LAAC: bleeding-related conditions should not automatically be equated with procedural risk. In this selected high-risk cohort, procedural safety was preserved across subgroups traditionally perceived as challenging, whereas long-term mortality reflected baseline clinical-biological vulnerability. The key clinical issue is therefore not only whether LAAC can be performed safely in a high-volume centre, but whether procedural success translates into meaningful benefit within the patient’s broader clinical trajectory. The score should therefore be viewed as a tool for prognostic framing and follow-up planning, not as a gatekeeping instrument to deny LAAC.

This distinction is increasingly relevant after OPTION and CHAMPION-AF, which evaluated LAAC in selected anticoagulation-eligible populations, and CLOSURE-AF, which challenged the net benefit of LAAC in older patients at high thromboembolic and bleeding risk.1,2,6 In this context, the neutral CLOSURE-AF results reinforce the need to identify patients in whom technical success may not translate into durable clinical benefit. Real-world registry and subgroup data have also documented LAAC in heterogeneous high-risk populations, including patients with thrombocytopenia, end-stage renal disease, and advanced age.7–10 Accordingly, risk stratification may be most useful in the historical contraindication-to-anticoagulation population, whereas its relevance in younger or anticoagulation-eligible candidates remains uncertain.

Several caveats are essential. First, these findings should not be extrapolated to unselected patients with severe thrombocytopenia, advanced renal dysfunction, or very advanced age. Second, cause-specific mortality was unavailable, limiting distinction between cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular death. Therefore, the interpretation that mortality reflects non-procedural vulnerability is supported indirectly by the low rates of stroke, major bleeding, device-related thrombus, and procedural events, but cannot be confirmed by adjudicated cause-specific mortality. Third, the score is exploratory and hypothesis-generating; it should not determine LAAC eligibility before external validation against independent cohorts and established frailty or mortality tools.

In conclusion, among high-risk patients undergoing LAAC for contraindication to anticoagulation, bleeding-prone conditions were not associated with excess procedural complications, whereas baseline clinical-biological markers stratified long-term mortality. A simple exploratory score may help multidisciplinary atrial fibrillation teams contextualize procedural success and plan follow-up, without replacing individualized Heart Team judgement.

Building Equitable Linkages with Interprofessional Education Valuing Everyone (BELIEVE): research protocol for a multisite step-wedge cluster randomized trial
Authors: Deysia Oakcrum, Precious Ikejiaku, Jasmine L Garland McKinney, Amelia N Gibson, Li Jiang, Feng-Chang Lin, Kristin P Tully, Janiya M Williams, Kimberly Harper, Alison Stuebe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere maternal morbidity (SMM) and maternal mortality (MM) rates are higher in the U.S. than in other high-income countries, with Black birthing individuals disproportionately impacted. Research highlights the need for collaborative, team-based approaches to tackle these disparities. Interprofessional education and practice (IPEP) competencies provide a promising framework to foster interdisciplinary relationships and improve maternal health outcomes. However, there is still insufficient integration of these competencies in educational curricula and clinical practice. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of an interprofessional education (IPE) maternity care training to reduce disparities in maternity care.Methods and designThe study is a multisite step-wedge cluster randomized trial to evaluate the extent to which IPE training reduces racial disparities in maternity care, indexed by postpartum pain experience, assessment, and treatment. IPE training will be implemented at four maternity hospitals in central North Carolina that provide intrapartum care to about 15,000 people each year. The IPE training will be delivered to healthcare team members who provide postpartum maternity care at each facility. The training design includes a 30-minute pre-work module, a 90-minute interprofessional collaboration session utilizing virtual reality (VR) headsets, and a 30-minute post-work module. The pre- and post-work modules will be delivered through an online learning platform. Outcomes will be measured over six study periods, each lasting four months. In the first study period, hospital maternity staff will continue to receive standard continuing education. During study periods 2 to 5, hospitals will sequentially invite their staff to participate in the BELIEVE IPE training. During study period 6, all hospitals will have completed the staff training intervention. Outcomes will be assessed before, during, and after implementation of the IPE training at all four hospitals over 24 months. Our primary outcome is prevalence of severe postpartum pain among Black and white birthing people. The protocol version at time of writing is 6.1.DiscussionThis project will evaluate the effectiveness of a virtual reality interprofessional training to reduce disparities in clinical care. Background

In 2021, the maternal mortality rate among Black birthing people was 69.9 deaths per 100,000 live births, compared with 26.6 per 100,000 lives births among white birthing people [1]; Black women are three times more likely to experience pregnancy-related mortality than their white counterparts [1]. There are large, unacceptable disparities in health outcomes for Black birthing people, perpetuated by multiple factors, which include but are not limited to unequal treatment in maternity care [2]. For example, despite having higher pain scores, Black birthing people’s pain is assessed less frequently, and they receive less pain medication than white birthing people in studies at multiple maternity care facilities [3–5]. Severe pain and inadequate support can significantly increase the risk of postpartum complications, including the development of chronic pain that extends beyond the postpartum period [6]. Healthcare teams play a critical role in assessment and management of pain during the postpartum period to mitigate adverse outcomes that may occur.

To reduce disparities and improve care, healthcare team members must work collaboratively to center the birthing person. Working collaboratively requires interprofessional education (IPE), which facilitates learning among different professions to improve teamwork and reduce barriers that hinder effective healthcare delivery [7]. Focusing on person-centered care allows the patient to come first, rather than the provider, creating a healthy relationship based on respect, trust, dignity, support, autonomy, and communication, reflecting key components of high-quality care in healthcare [8].

To foster collaboration among healthcare teams and improve health outcomes for Black birthing people, The Building Equitable Linkages with Interprofessional Education Valuing Everyone (BELIEVE) project set out to create an IPE curriculum delivered through VR technology. BELIEVE intends to create trust among birthing people and health team members through respectful, equitable, and person-centered care and communication. We aim to test the effectiveness of this intervention in four hospitals in North Carolina.

Methods and design

Study design and setting

This is a multisite step-wedge cluster randomized trial to test the extent to which IPE training reduces racial disparities in postpartum pain (primary outcome) and pain assessment and treatment (secondary, healthcare team processes), improves birthing people’s experience of respectful care (secondary outcomes), and fosters a positive shift in healthcare team interprofessional collaboration attitudes and teamwork (secondary process outcomes). The IPE training will be administered at four maternity hospitals in central North Carolina that provide intrapartum care for about 15,000 people each year: Cone Health Women’s and Children’s Center at Cone Hospital, Cone Health Alamance Regional Medical Center, North Carolina Women’s Hospital (NCWH), and Rex Women’s Center. This study adheres to the CONSORT guidelines for randomized controlled trials (see Additional file 1).

During the two-year trial, outcomes from the four sites will be quantified over six study periods, each lasting four months, as presented in Fig. 1. In the pre-intervention periods, birthing people at participating hospitals will experience treatment-as-usual. In the post-intervention periods, birthing people at participating hospitals will experience treatment by health care teams that have undergone the BELIEVE IPE training.

BELIEVE intervention multisite step-wedge timeline

In periods two through five, the BELIEVE IPE training will be offered to staff who provide postpartum maternity care at each facility at one of the four participating hospitals, so that by the sixth period, staff at all four hospitals will have had the opportunity to complete the training. The planned training included a 30-minute pre-session module, a 90-minute interprofessional collaboration session utilizing VR headsets, and a 30-minute post-session module. During implementation at the first clinical site, the IPE session was modified to last 60 min in response to feedback from health team participants and their supervisors.

Participants

Healthcare team

The study will enroll healthcare team members who provide maternity care at each participating site. Health team members will complete a prework assessment, the study intervention, intervention evaluation survey, and a final post-intervention assessment. A random sample of Healthcare Team Participants who have completed the Curriculum consent process, regardless how much of the curriculum they complete, will be invited to complete a semi-structured interview about their perceptions of the study intervention including feasibility and acceptability of the intervention.

The IPE training will be delivered to healthcare team members who provide intrapartum and/or postpartum maternity care at each facility. Individuals who provide care as a maternity care provider, nurse, lactation consultant or doula and whose primary inpatient clinical assignment is maternity care will be invited to complete the training. To participate, per diem healthcare team members must be employed for a minimum of 4 shifts/month at a participating site. Participants must be age ≥ 18. All healthcare team members who meet eligibility criteria will be invited to participate.

Birthing parents

Our primary outcome, severe postpartum pain, will be ascertained from hospital electronic medical records. We will extract data for all birthing parents with a live birth at ≥ 24 weeks at one of the participating sites from the start of the study through the last data retrieval time point.

We will also conduct qualitative interviews. We will recruit individuals with a live birth at a participating site from the start of the study through the last data retrieval time point who identify as Black race or have Limited English Proficiency with a preferred language of Spanish, with age ≥ 18 years. We will exclude birthing parents with a birth gestational age of < 24 weeks, if the baby is no longer alive at the time of data retrieval (regardless of gestational age), and those who are currently incarcerated, previously completed a birthing parent interview for the BELIEVE study, or previously enrolled as a Healthcare Team participant. The interview questionnaire was developed specifically for this study (see Additional file 2).

Recruitment

Email addresses of potentially eligible healthcare team members will be requested from the hospitals participating in the study. An IRB-approved informational message summarizing the study will be sent via email. In addition, an information sheet will be provided to unit managers at each participating site for distribution to their staff. The frequency and method of distribution will be determined at the discretion of the unit managers. All healthcare team members who meet eligibility criteria will be invited to participate. They will then have the option to provide consent or decline participation.

To promote healthcare team retention, study activities will be designed to respect participants’ time and minimize disruptions to clinical workflows. Reminders about upcoming study procedures will be sent, and a $50 gift card incentive will be offered for those who complete the pre-work and in-person IPE activity. A subset of healthcare team participants who complete the curriculum and post-work will be invited to participate in a one-time interview to assess the acceptability of the intervention.

To recruit birthing parents for interviews, the medical record system of each participating site will be queried for a list of eligible patients by data managers with appropriate access. A limited HIPAA waiver will be requested so that the study team may receive contact information for patients eligible for an interview. Study personnel at each site will be provided with a list of individuals who are eligible for birthing parent interviews. Recruitment methods include email, mail, phone call, or text. A maximum of 3 recruitment attempts will be made.

Intervention

After reviewing and signing an informed consent document, participants will complete a demographic survey and a baseline assessment of interprofessional collaboration and teamwork.

The BELIEVE Curriculum is designed to enhance interprofessional collaboration among healthcare teams. This curriculum focuses on patient-centered care for Black birthing people, who disproportionately experience disrespectful treatment and care [9]. The curriculum builds upon TeamSTEPPS, an evidence-based, multifaceted instructional toolkit that was developed to promote teamwork and collaboration in healthcare teams to improve patient safety and care quality [10]. The TeamSTEPPS framework emphasizes essential components of effective interprofessional collaboration and patient-centered care. The key competencies for this curriculum are respectful care, pain management in the postpartum period, communication, and documentation.

BELIEVE Curriculum phases

The BELIEVE Curriculum is delivered in three phases, including prework, the Interprofessional Problem Solving (IPS) session, and postwork. The participants will complete a 30-minute online prework module that highlights educational gaps and aids in the assignment of curriculum content and is comprised of surveys, reflection scenarios, open-response sections, and optional podcast excerpts. After completing the prework, participants will engage in an IPS session using VR headsets to simulate patient care scenarios. These sessions include group discussions and self-reflection to enhance collaboration and address pain management and respectful care. Following the IPS Session, participants will complete a 30-minute post work module to reinforce concepts from the prework and IPS session.

iBELIEVE booster

To reinforce desired behavior changes from the curriculum, during the post-implementation period, project champions will lead a series of iBELIEVE Boosters. The iBELIEVE boosters include 10–15-minute huddles that are integrated into clinical workflows, with the goal of engaging interprofessional teams in conversations about key behaviors that were addressed in the IPE curriculum.

Measures

Primary and secondary outcome measures

The primary outcome of this study is prevalence of severe postpartum pain, defined as the proportion of individuals with a pain score > 4 on postpartum day 1 following vaginal birth, or pain score > 7 on postpartum day 2 following cesarean birth, as documented in the electronic health record using the Numerical Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) of patient-reported pain from 0 to 10 [11]. The NPRS is not a proprietary instrument and does not require a license for use. The NPRS is in the public domain and has been widely used in clinical practice and research.

Our secondary outcomes include both quantitative and qualitative measures. Using EHR data, we will measure processes of pain management, including number of pain assessments, defined as instances of pain score assessments on a 0–10 scale that are documented in the birthing parent’s medical record; and pain treatment, measured as number of doses of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) medication and of acetaminophen, as well as Morphine Milligram Equivalents (MMEs) of opioids, administered to the birthing parent, as documented in the medication administration record (MAR). We will complement these quantitative data with qualitative interviews with birthing parents before and after implementation of the BELIEVE curriculum at each of the four participating hospitals.

We will also measure the effect of the BELIEVE curriculum on the healthcare team. Before and after completing the curriculum, we will quantify health care team members’ perceptions of interprofessional collaboration and quality of interprofessional teamwork using validated instruments. We will measure interprofessional collaboration using the modified Interprofessional Attitudes Scale (IPAS) [12]. The IPAS is a 5-point Likert scale with scores ranging from 27 to 135, with higher scores reflecting more positive attitudes toward interprofessional collaboration. We will measure quality of interprofessional teamwork using the modified Assessment for Collaborative Environments (ACE) Scale [13, 14]. The modified ACE is a 4-point Likert scale with scores ranging from 6 to 24, with higher scores reflecting better quality interprofessional teamwork. We will complement these quantitative assessments with semi-structured interviews with health team members to assess the feasibility of deploying the IPE training in birthing hospitals.

Data collection

Data extracted from electronic health records will be stored on the CDWH server, which is password-protected and available only via UNC Onyen secure sign-on. For outcomes extracted from the Electronic Health Record, eligible births will be identified from data entered in the Delivery Summary in the Epic Electronic Health Record. Following methods used in prior studies of postpartum pain disparities [3, 4], we will extract clinical data from the EHR including time and date of delivery, maternal body mass index (BMI), parity, history of prior cesarean delivery, classical cesarean delivery, gestational age at delivery, and whether an infant was admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU). We will also extract pain scores from delivery through 48 h postpartum, as well as doses administered of opioid medications, NSAIDs, and acetaminophen.

We will index pain scores to 0 to < 24 h or 24 to 48 h. We will tabulate opioid medications administered in 5 mg oxycodone tablet equivalents (OTEs) for all opioid medications, including morphine, hydromorphone, codeine, and oxymorphone. Doses of acetaminophen and NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and ketorolac will also be tabulated by 0 to < 24 h and 24 to 48 h postpartum. We will then calculate the number of pain scores > 7 (severe pain), and total OTEs, NSAID, and acetaminophen doses administered, in the 0 to < 24 and 24 to 48 h postpartum periods.

For survey data, responses will be collected in digital or physical format. Digital responses will be collected using REDCap. Responses provided in a physical format will be transformed into a digital format to aid data analysis. Data entry will be cross checked to ensure data quality.

Interview responses will be audio recorded, transcribed, and coded. Audio recordings will be downloaded from the audio recording device onto a secure UNC server. Transcriptions will also be stored on a secure UNC server.

Access to digital or physical data is restricted to study team personnel who have completed appropriate training and have been granted access, which will be determined as needed according to their delegated tasks.

Data management

The data management protocol has been created and approved. Data is accessible to study team personnel who have completed appropriate training and have been granted access. A DMC is not required as oversight is provided by the DSMB within the P3 Equate Network.

Sample size

Given the variation in patient numbers across the sites, we employ a power calculation method that accommodates varying cluster sizes within each site. With four clusters and four sequences, there are 24 (4!) possible permutations of the randomization order. Power calculations are performed for each permutation using the Wald test for intervention effect, as described by Hemming et al. The computations were implemented in R based on the Shiny CRT Calculator source code [15], which was modified to allow direct specification of cluster sizes. A normal approximation for binary outcomes is also utilized.

Based on electronic health record (EHR) data, with an expected 100% enrollment rate, we calculate the annual number of Black births at each site. Over a single four-month period, these figures are 98, 668, 316, and 241 for the four sites, respectively. Preliminary results from Rex and North Carolina Women’s Hospital (NCWH) indicate a day 2 severe pain rate (pain score > 7) of 21.3% among Black patients compared to 11.7% among White patients and a day 1 severe pain rate (pain score > 4) of 31.9% compared to 20.0%. To detect a reduction in day 2 pain scores from 21.3% to the baseline rate of 11.7% among Whites (effect size = 0.259) and in day 1 pain scores from 31.9% to 20.0% (effect size = 0.271), the trial has an estimated median power of 98.1% and 98.8%, respectively. The median refers to the middle power estimate across the 24 possible randomization orders of the four clusters. When the power estimate accounts for variations in cluster size, the 24 trial power estimates range from 93.6% to 99.7% for day 2 outcomes and from 95.4% to 99.8% for day 1 outcomes.

Adjustments in cluster autocorrelation (CAC) to 0.9 resulted in slightly decreased median power, at 94.3% and 95.9%, respectively. Assuming independent site effects (ICC = 0) increases the median power to 99.8% and 99.9%. If 80% of patient EHR data are available, the power drops to 95.2% and 96.6%; with 50% data availability, the power decreases to 82.6% and 85.8%.

Statistical analysis

In this study, demographic and baseline characteristics will be summarized to provide a clear understanding of the study population. Continuous variables such as age will be presented using means and standard deviations. Categorical variables, including race and type of delivery, will be expressed as frequencies and percentages. Statistical tests for inferential analysis will be conducted using a two-sided significance level, with a p-value threshold of 0.05 to determine statistical significance. Results yielding p-values greater than 0.05 will be considered inconclusive, indicating insufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

The primary endpoint of this study is defined as severe pain, quantified as a pain score > 4 on postpartum day 1 following vaginal birth or > 7 on postpartum day 2 following cesarean birth among Black birthing individuals. To analyze this primary endpoint, we will employ a logistic mixed model that accounts for the hierarchical structure of the data, with sites treated as random effects to accommodate intra-cluster correlations and period as another random effect to accommodate the autocorrelation between periods. Demographic variables such as age will serve as covariates, selected based on clinical relevance and insights from similar studies. These analyses will be conducted using the lme4 package in R, which leverages maximum likelihood estimation for the robust estimation of both fixed and random effects. The mixed-effects model can be complicated when accounting for correlations. We will use information criteria to select the best model that strikes a balance between model complexity and fitness.

Results will be presented as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals, providing a clear measure of the effect of treatment on severe pain occurrences. The model assumptions, such as the appropriateness of logistic regression and the handling of potential outliers or multicollinearity among covariates, will be rigorously checked and adjusted for, if necessary, to ensure the validity of the analysis. Since the dataset comprises comprehensive EHR data with minimal missing values, extensive imputation methods are deemed unnecessary.

The secondary endpoint, which examines parents’ experiences of inpatient postpartum care, is associated with the implementation of the BELIEVE intervention but is not dependent on the findings of the primary endpoint. Semi-structured interviews will be audio-recorded and professionally transcribed. Once the transcriptions are de-identified and accuracy-checked, study team members will review the files to familiarize themselves with the data. The researchers will then conduct thematic content-coding of responses. They will group keywords and phrases into categories and collaboratively develop themes. The analytic process will entail repeated review of quotes, accompanied by memos and discussions, to construct and refine the categories and themes. Consensus in coding will be reached through discussions among team members.

The secondary endpoint of the healthcare team’s experiences with BELIEVE depends on the implementation of the intervention, but not on the findings of the primary endpoint. The qualitative data will be collected and analyzed as described with the birthing parent data. Additionally, descriptive statistics will be used to describe the characteristics of the samples.

Risk assessment

Data indicates that virtual headset use is tolerable, as protocol-related satisfaction surveys show [16]. Upon evaluation, the benefits of virtual reality-based education in this study will outweigh any minimal short-term risks. Participants can pause the intervention at any point if any side effects occur, and they will have the option to transition to a laptop if they do not tolerate the VR headset.

Discussion

Rates of maternal mortality and severe maternal morbidity in the United States are more than twice as high as in other high-income countries, and unacceptable disparities persist [17]. Moreover, during intrapartum care, 20% of all birthing people report mistreatment [18]. Among individuals who identify as Black, Hispanic, or multiracial, 30% report mistreatment, and 45% report holding back from discussing concerns with their provider. For members of the maternity team, power dynamics and communication challenges are barriers to high quality care [19]. To address these issues, the BELIEVE interprofessional education intervention is designed to foster a culture within birthing hospitals where every patient and healthcare team member feels seen, heard and valued. The immersive and realistic environments created in virtual reality allow learners to enhance their knowledge and practice skills, patient interactions, and communication skills [20]. Studies have shown a significant increase in knowledge when utilizing VR compared to traditional teaching methods [21].

There is a need to enhance the type of care Black birthing people receive in maternity care in hopes of reducing the adverse outcomes and unacceptable disparities seen in the United States. This study will provide evidence regarding the effectiveness of virtual reality-based IPE training in maternity health providers to reduce disparities and improve outcomes for birthing people.
